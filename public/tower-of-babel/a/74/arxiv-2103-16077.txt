Parameterized discrete uniformization theorems and curvature flows for polyhedral surfaces, II
Xu Xu, Chao Zheng

Abstract
This paper investigates the combinatorial α \alpha -curvature for vertex scaling of piecewise hyperbolic metrics on polyhedral surfaces, which is a parameterized generalization of the classical combinatorial curvature. A discrete uniformization theorem for combinatorial α \alpha -curvature is established, which generalizes Gu-Guo-Luo-Sun-Wu’s discrete uniformization theorem for classical combinatorial curvature [ 16 ] . We further introduce combinatorial α \alpha -Yamabe flow and combinatorial α \alpha -Calabi flow for vertex scaling to find piecewise hyperbolic metrics with prescribed combinatorial α \alpha -curvatures. To handle the potential singularities along the combinatorial curvature flows, we do surgery along the flows by edge flipping. Using the discrete conformal theory established by Gu-Guo-Luo-Sun-Wu [ 16 ] , we prove the longtime existence and convergence of combinatorial α \alpha -Yamabe flow and combinatorial α \alpha -Calabi flow with surgery, which provide effective algorithms for finding piecewise hyperbolic metrics with prescribed combinatorial α \alpha -curvatures.



Keywords
: Discrete uniformization theorem; Piecewise hyperbolic metric;
Combinatorial curvature; Combinatorial Yamabe flow; Combinatorial Calabi flow

w=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(x)dx^{i}
in an open subset
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be continuous if each
a_{i}(x)
is continuous on
U
. A continuous differential 1-form
w
is called closed if
\int_{\partial\tau}w=0
for each triangle
\tau\subset U
.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
20
]
, Corollary 2.6)
.

Suppose
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open convex set and
A\subset X
is an open subset of
X
bounded by a real analytic codimension-1 submanifold in
X
. If
w=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(x)dx_{i}
is a continuous closed 1-form on
A
so that
P(x)=\int_{a}^{x}w
is locally convex on
A
and each
a_{i}
can be extended continuous to
X
by constant functions to a function
\widetilde{a}_{i}
on
X
, then
\widetilde{P}(x)=\int_{a}^{x}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{a}_{i}(x)dx_{i}
is a
C^{1}
-smooth
convex function on
X
extending
P
.

Combining Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
2.4
,
F_{ijk}(u_{i},u_{j},u_{k})
defined on
\mathcal{U}^{H,\mathcal{T}}_{ijk}(d)
could be extended to be

\widetilde{F}_{ijk}(u_{i},u_{j},u_{k})=\int^{(u_{i},u_{j},u_{k})}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}^{jk}du_{i}+\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}^{ik}du_{j}+\widetilde{\alpha}_{k}^{ij}du_{k},

(13)

which is a
C^{1}
-smooth concave function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Using the extension
\widetilde{F}_{ijk}
of the Ricci energy function
F_{ijk}
,
we can prove the following rigidity for the extended combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature

\widetilde{R}_{\alpha}=\frac{\widetilde{K}_{i}}{w_{i}^{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{w_{i}^{\alpha}}(2\pi-\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}}\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}^{jk})
,
which is a generalization of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose
(S,V,\mathcal{T})
is a connected closed triangulated surface with a PH metric
d_{0}
,

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant and
\overline{R}
is a function defined on the vertices with
\alpha\overline{R}\leq 0
.
If there exist
u_{A}\in\mathcal{U}^{H,\mathcal{T}}(d_{0})
and
u_{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the same extended combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{R}
,
then
u_{A}=u_{B}
.

Proof.

Define the following Ricci energy
P(u)
for
\overline{R}
by

P(u)=-\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}F_{ijk}(u_{i},u_{j},u_{k})+\int^{u}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(2\pi-\overline{R}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i})du_{i}.

By direct calculations, we have

\nabla_{u_{i}}P(u)=-\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\alpha_{i}^{jk}+2\pi-\overline{R}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i}=K_{i}-\overline{R}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i},\\

and

\mathrm{Hess}_{u}P=L-\alpha\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\overline{R}_{1}w^{\alpha}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\overline{R}_{N}w^{\alpha}_{N}\\
\end{array}\right).

Combining Lemma
2.1
and the condition
\alpha\overline{R}\leq 0
,

\mathrm{Hess}_{u}P
is positive definite and
P
is locally strictly convex on
\mathcal{U}^{H,\mathcal{T}}(d_{0})
.

By the extension
\widetilde{F}_{ijk}(u_{i},u_{j},u_{k})
of
F_{ijk}(u_{i},u_{j},u_{k})
in (
13
),
the Ricci energy function
P(u)
defined on
\mathcal{U}^{H,\mathcal{T}}(d_{0})
could be extended to be

\widetilde{P}(u)=-\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\widetilde{F}_{ijk}(u_{i},u_{j},u_{k})+\int^{u}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(2\pi-\overline{R}_{i}\omega^{\alpha}_{i})du_{i},

which is a
C^{1}
-smooth convex function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and locally strictly convex on
\mathcal{U}^{H,\mathcal{T}}(d_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

with
\nabla_{u_{i}}\widetilde{P}=\widetilde{K}_{i}-\overline{R}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i}
.

Set

f(t)=\widetilde{P}((1-t)u_{A}+tu_{B}),\ t\in[0,1].

Then
f(t)
is a
C^{1}
smooth convex function of
t\in[0,1]
with

f^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_{u_{i}}\widetilde{P}|_{(1-t)u_{A}+tu_{B}}\cdot(u_{B,i}-u_{A,i})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\widetilde{K}_{i}-\overline{R}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i})|_{(1-t)u_{A}+tu_{B}}\cdot(u_{B,i}-u_{A,i}).

By the assumption that
\widetilde{R}_{\alpha}(u_{A})=\widetilde{R}_{\alpha}(u_{B})=\overline{R}
, we have
f^{\prime}(0)=f^{\prime}(1)=0
,
which implies
f^{\prime}(t)\equiv 0
by the convexity of
f(t)
.
Note that
u_{A}
is in the open subset
\mathcal{U}^{H,\mathcal{T}}(d_{0})
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
P(u)
is smooth and

\mathrm{Hess}_{u}P
is positive definite,
there exists
0<\epsilon<1
such that
(1-t)u_{A}+tu_{B}\in\mathcal{U}^{H,\mathcal{T}}(d_{0})

and
f(t)
is smooth and strictly convex for
t\in[0,\epsilon)
.
By
f^{\prime}(t)\equiv 0
for
t\in[0,1]
, we have
f^{\prime\prime}(t)\equiv 0
for
t\in[0,\epsilon)
.
Combining with

f^{\prime\prime}(t)=(u_{B}-u_{A})^{T}\cdot\mathrm{Hess}_{u}P|_{(1-t)u_{A}+tu_{B}}\cdot(u_{B}-u_{A})

for
t\in[0,\epsilon)
and
\mathrm{Hess}_{u}P
is positive definite for
u\in\mathcal{U}^{H,\mathcal{T}}(d_{0})
,
this implies
u_{A}=u_{B}
.
∎

2.2
Combinatorial
\alpha
-flows on triangulated surfaces

The combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow (
5
)
and the combinatorial
\alpha
-Calabi flow (
7
) are ODE systems
with smooth coefficients.
Therefore, the solutions always exist locally around the initial time
t=0
.
We further have the following result on the longtime existence and convergence for the solutions of combinatorial
\alpha
-flows.

Theorem 2.6
.

Suppose
(S,V,\mathcal{T})
is a connected closed triangulated surface with a PH metric
d_{0}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant and

\overline{R}
is a function defined on
V
.
If the solutions of combinatorial
\alpha
-flows converge, there exist PH metrics with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{R}
.
Furthermore, if there is a PH metric
d^{\ast}=u^{\ast}\ast d_{0}
with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{R}
and
\alpha\overline{R}\leq 0
, there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that if
||R_{\alpha}(u(0))-R_{\alpha}(u^{\ast})||<\delta
, then the solutions of combinatorial
\alpha
-flows exist for all time and converge exponentially fast to
u^{\ast}
.

Proof.

Suppose
w(t)
is a solution of the combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow (
5
),
then
u(t)=\ln w(t)
is a solution of the flow

\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=\overline{R}_{i}-R_{\alpha,i}.

If
w^{\ast}:=w(+\infty)=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}w(t)
exists,
then
u^{\ast}:=u(+\infty)=\ln w(+\infty)
exists in
\mathcal{U}^{H,\mathcal{T}}(d_{0})
and
R_{\alpha}(u^{\ast})=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}R_{\alpha}(u(t))
exists.
Furthermore, there exists a sequence
\xi_{n}\in(n,n+1)
such that

u_{i}(n+1)-u_{i}(n)=u^{\prime}_{i}(\xi_{n})=\overline{R}_{i}-R_{\alpha,i}(u(\xi_{n}))\rightarrow 0\ \text{as}\ n\rightarrow+\infty,

which implies
R_{\alpha,i}(u^{\ast})=\overline{R}_{i}
for all
i\in V

and
u^{\ast}\ast d_{0}
is a PH metric with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{R}
.
Similarly, if the combinatorial
\alpha
-Calabi flow (
7
) converges, there exists a PH metric with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{R}
.

Suppose there exists a PH metric
d^{\ast}=u^{\ast}\ast d_{0}
with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{R}
.
For the combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow (
5
),
set
\Gamma(u)=\overline{R}-R_{\alpha}
. By direct calculations, we have

D\Gamma|_{u=u^{*}}=-\Sigma^{-\alpha}L+\alpha\Lambda=-\Sigma^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\Sigma^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}L\Sigma^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}-\alpha\Lambda)\Sigma^{\frac{\alpha}{2}},

where
\Sigma=\text{diag}\{w_{1},w_{2},...,w_{N}\}
,
\Lambda=\text{diag}\{\overline{R}_{1},\overline{R}_{2},...,\overline{R}_{N}\}
.
By the condition
\alpha\overline{R}\leq 0
,
D\Gamma|_{u=u^{*}}
has
N
negative eigenvalues,
which implies
u^{*}
is a local attractor of (
5
).
Then the conclusion follows from Lyapunov Stability Theorem (
[
21
]
, Chapter 5).

Similarly, for the combinatorial
\alpha
-Calabi flow (
7
), set
\Gamma(u)=\Delta^{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}(R_{\alpha}-\overline{R})
. By direct calculations, we have

\displaystyle D\Gamma|_{u=u^{*}}

\displaystyle=-\Sigma^{-\alpha}L\Sigma^{-\alpha}L+\alpha\Sigma^{-\alpha}L\Lambda

\displaystyle=-\Sigma^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left(\Sigma^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}L\Sigma^{-\alpha}L\Sigma^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}-\alpha\Sigma^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}L\Sigma^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Lambda\right)\Sigma^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle=-\Sigma^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left(Q^{2}-\alpha Q\Lambda\right)\Sigma^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle=-\Sigma^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(Q^{2}+Q^{\frac{1}{2}}(-\alpha\Lambda)Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Sigma^{\frac{\alpha}{2}},

where
Q=\Sigma^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}L\Sigma^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
is a symmetric and positive definite matrix.
By the condition
\alpha\overline{R}\leq 0
,
D\Gamma|_{u=u^{*}}
has
N
negative eigenvalues, which implies
u^{\ast}
is a local attractor of (
5
). Then the conclusion follows from Lyapunov Stability Theorem (
[
21
]
, Chapter 5).
∎

Theorem
2.6
gives the longtime existence and convergence for
the solutions of combinatorial
\alpha
-flows for initial PH metrics with small initial energy.
But for general initial PH metrics, the combinatorial
\alpha
-flows may develop singularities, including that some triangles degenerate and the conformal factors tend to infinity along the corresponding combinatorial
\alpha
-flows.
To handle the possible singularities along the combinatorial
\alpha
-flows, we can do surgery by flipping on the
\alpha
-flows.

3
Combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature and combinatorial
\alpha
-flows on discrete Riemannian surfaces

3.1
Gu-Guo-Luo-Sun-Wu’s work on discrete uniformization theorem

To analyze the behavior of the combinatorial
\alpha
-flows with surgery,
we need the discrete conformal theory for PH metrics established by Gu-Guo-Luo-Sun-Wu
[
16
]
.
In this subsection, we briefly recall the theory. Please refer to
[
16
]
for more details.

Definition 3.1
.

(
[
16
]
, Definition 1)
Two PH metrics
d,d^{\prime}
on a connected closed marked surface
(S,V)
are discrete conformal
if there exist a sequence of PH metrics
d_{1}=d,d_{2},...,d_{m}=d^{\prime}
on
(S,V)
and triangulations
\mathcal{T}_{1},...,\mathcal{T}_{m}
of
(S,V)
satisfying

(a)

each
\mathcal{T}_{i}
is Delaunay in
d_{i}
,

(b)

if
\mathcal{T}_{i}=\mathcal{T}_{i+1}
, there exists a function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, called a conformal factor, so that if
e
is an edge in
\mathcal{T}_{i}
with end points
v
and
v^{\prime}
, then the lengths
x_{d_{i}}(e)
and
x_{d_{i+1}}(e)
of
e
in metrics
d_{i}
and
d_{i+1}
are related by

\sinh\frac{x_{d_{i+1}}(e)}{2}=\sinh\frac{x_{d_{i}}(e)}{2}e^{u(v)+u(v^{\prime})},

(c)

if
\mathcal{T}_{i}\neq\mathcal{T}_{i+1}
, then
(S,d_{i})
is isometric to
(S,d_{i+1})
by an isometry homotopic to the identity in
(S,V)
.

The discrete conformal class of a PH metric
d
on
(S,V)
is called a discrete Riemannian surface, which is denoted by
\mathcal{D}(d)

[
16
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(
[
16
]
, Proposition 16)
If
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
are Delaunay triangulations of a PH metric
d
on a closed marked surface
(S,V)
, there exist a sequence of Delaunay triangulations
\mathcal{T}_{1}=\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}_{2},...,\mathcal{T}_{k}=\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
of
d
so that
\mathcal{T}_{i+1}
is obtained from
\mathcal{T}_{i}
by a diagonal switch.

The diagonal switch in Lemma
3.2
is the surgery by flipping described in the introduction.
Using the new definition of discrete conformality, Gu-Guo-Luo-Sun-Wu
[
16
]
proved the following
discrete conformal uniformization theorem for vertex scaling of PH metrics.

Theorem 3.3
.

(
[
16
]
, Theorem 3)
Suppose
(S,V)
is a connected closed marked surface and
d
is a PH metric on
(S,V)
.
Then for any
K^{\ast}:V\rightarrow(-\infty,2\pi)
with
\sum_{v\in V}K^{\ast}(v)>2\pi\chi(S)
, there exists a unique PH metric
d^{\prime}
on
(S,V)
so that
d^{\prime}
is discrete conformal to
d
and the discrete curvature of
d^{\prime}
is
K^{\ast}
. Furthermore, the discrete Yamabe flow with surgery associated to curvature
K^{\ast}
having initial value
d
converges to
d^{\prime}
linearly fast.

Denote the Teichimüller space of all PH metrics on
(S,V)
by
T_{hp}(S,V)
and decorated Teichimüller space of all equivalence class of decorated hyperbolic metrics on
S-V
by
T_{D}(S-V)
. To prove Theorem
3.3
, Gu-Guo-Luo-Sun-Wu
proved the following result.

Theorem 3.4
.

(
[
16
]
, Theorem 22, Corollary 24)
There is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism
\mathbf{A}:T_{hp}(S,V)\rightarrow T_{D}(S,V)
between
T_{hp}(S,V)
and
T_{D}(S,V)
. Furthermore, the space
\mathcal{D}(d)\subset T_{hp}(S,V)
of all equivalence classes of PH metrics discrete conformal to
d
is
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism to
\{p\}\times\mathbb{R}^{V}_{>0}
under the diffeomorphism
\mathbf{A}
, where
p
is the unique hyperbolic metric on
S-V
determined by the PH metric
d
on
(S,V)
.

Theorem
3.4
implies that the union of the admissible spaces
\mathcal{U}^{H,\mathcal{T}}_{D}(d^{\prime})
of conformal factors such that
\mathcal{T}
is Delaunay for
d^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}(d)
is
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Set
u_{i}=\ln w_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq n
, for
w=(w_{1},w_{2},...,w_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
and
\mathbf{P}=\{x\in(-\infty,2\pi)^{n}|\sum^{n}_{i=1}x_{i}>2\pi\chi(S)\}
. Using the map
\mathbf{A}
, Gu-Guo-Luo-Sun-Wu
[
16
]
defined the curvature map

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}:

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}

(14)

\displaystyle u\mapsto K_{\mathbf{A}^{-1}(p,w(-2u))}

and proved the following property of
\mathbf{F}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

(
[
16
]
)
There exists a
C^{2}
-smooth strictly convex function
W:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

defined by
W(u)=\int^{u}_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{F}_{i}du_{i}
so that its
gradient
\nabla W
is
\mathbf{F}
.

Remark 5
.

Proposition
3.5
implies that
\mathbf{F}
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
C^{1}
-extension of the combinatorial
curvature
K
defined on the space of conformal factors
\mathcal{U}^{H,\mathcal{T}}_{D}(d^{\prime})
for
d^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}(d)
.
Furthermore, the discrete conformal factor
u
is uniquely determined by the curvature map
\mathbf{F}
and then determined
by the PH metric in the discrete conformal class
\mathcal{D}(d)

[
16
]
.

Note that the discrete
\alpha
-Laplace operator
\Delta^{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}
is independent of the Delaunay triangulations of a PH metric, then the discrete
\alpha
-Laplace operator
\Delta^{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}
could be extend to the following operator
\Delta_{\alpha}
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is the space of discrete conformal factors for the discrete conformal class
\mathcal{D}(d)
.

Definition 3.6
.

Suppose
(S,V)
is a connected closed marked surface with a PH metric
d_{0}
.
For a function
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
on the vertices, the hyperbolic discrete
\alpha
-Laplace operator of
d\in\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
on

is defined to be the map

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha}:

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{V}

\displaystyle f\mapsto\Delta_{\alpha}f,

where the value of
\Delta_{\alpha}f
at vertex

is

\Delta_{\alpha}f_{i}=-\frac{1}{w^{\alpha}_{i}}\sum_{j\in V}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}f_{j}=-\frac{1}{w^{\alpha}_{i}}(\widetilde{L}f)_{i}

(15)

with
\widetilde{L}_{ij}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}
being an extension of

.

Remark 6
.

By Remark
5
, the discrete conformal factor
u\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is uniquely
determined by the PH metric

and the discrete
\alpha
-Laplace operator in Definition

By Proposition
3.5
,
\Delta_{\alpha}
is continuous and piecewise smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as a matrix-valued function of

. By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\Delta_{\alpha}f_{i}=\sum_{j\sim i}\frac{B_{ij}}{w^{\alpha}_{i}}(f_{j}-f_{i})-\frac{A_{i}}{w^{\alpha}_{i}}f_{i}

(16)

with

and

.

3.2
Rigidity of combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature on discrete Riemannian surfaces

Motivated by the relationship of
\mathbf{F}
defined on a discrete Riemannian surface and the combinatorial
curvature
K
defined on a triangulated surface,
we introduce the following definition of
combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
for PH metrics on a discrete Riemannian surface,
which is an extension of the combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
R_{\alpha}
defined on a triangulated surface.

Definition 3.7
.

Suppose
(S,V)
is a connected closed marked surface with a PH metric
d_{0}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant and

\mathbf{F}
is the curvature map defined by (
14
). The combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature for
d\in\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
is defined to be

\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}=\frac{\mathbf{F}_{i}}{w^{\alpha}_{i}},

(17)

where
w_{i}=e^{u_{i}}
and
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the unique discrete conformal factor determined by
the PH metric
d\in\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
.

By Remark
5
,
the combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
in Definition
3.7
is well-defined for
d\in\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
.
Denote the space of conformal factors by
\mathcal{U}(d)
for a PH metric
d
on the marked surface
(S,V)
,
which is
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by Theorem
3.4
.
Analogous to Theorem
1.3
, we have the following global rigidity for the combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}

with respect to discrete conformal factors on discrete Riemannian surfaces.

Theorem 3.8
.

Suppose
(S,V)
is a closed marked surface with a PH metric
d_{0}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant and

\overline{F}
is a function defined on
V
.
If
\alpha\overline{F}\leq 0
, there exists at most one discrete conformal factor
u^{*}\in\mathcal{U}(d_{0})
such that
\mathbf{A}^{-1}(p,w(-2u^{\ast}))\in\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
has the combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{F}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.5
, define the following Ricci energy function

W_{\alpha}(u)=W(u)-\int_{0}^{u}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\overline{F}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i}du_{i}=\int_{0}^{u}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{F}_{i}du_{i}-\int_{0}^{u}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\overline{F}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i}du_{i}.

Then
W_{\alpha}(u)
is a
C^{2}
-smooth function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

\nabla_{u_{i}}W_{\alpha}=\nabla_{u_{i}}W-\overline{F}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i}=\mathbf{F}_{i}-\overline{F}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i}.

Therefore, for
u^{*}\in\mathcal{U}(d)
,
\mathbf{A}^{-1}(p,w(-2u^{\ast}))\in\mathcal{D}(d)
has the combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{F}
if and only if
\nabla_{u_{i}}W_{\alpha}(u^{\ast})=0
. By direct calculations, we have

\mathrm{Hess}W_{\alpha}=\widetilde{L}-\alpha\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\overline{F}_{1}w^{\alpha}_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\overline{F}_{N}w^{\alpha}_{N}\\
\end{array}\right).

Due to
\alpha\overline{F}\leq 0
,
\mathrm{Hess}W_{\alpha}
is positive definite and
W_{\alpha}
is strictly convex on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
by Proposition
3.5
. Then the rigidity follows from the following well-known result from analysis.

Lemma 3.9
.

If
W:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{1}
-smooth strictly convex function on an open convex set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then its gradient
\nabla W:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is an embedding.

∎

3.3
Combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow with surgery

Using the discrete conformal theory established by Gu-Guo-Luo-Sun-Wu
[

]
,
the combinatorial

Definition 3.10
.

Suppose

is a connected closed marked surface with a PH metric

and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant. The combinatorial

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=-\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i},\\

(18)

One can modify the combinatorial
\alpha

\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}-\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}

(19)

to study prescribed curvature problems, where
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
is a function defined on
V
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Suppose
(S,V)
is a connected closed marked surface with a PH metric
d_{0}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant
and
\overline{\mathbf{F}}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a function defined on

with
\alpha\overline{\mathbf{F}}\leq 0
.
Then there exists a PH metric in the discrete conformal class
\mathcal{D}(d_{0})

with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
if and only if
the solution of modified combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow with surgery (
19
) exists for all time and converges exponentially fast to a PH metric with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
.

Proof.

Suppose the solution
u(t)
of the modified combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow with surgery (
19
) converges to
u^{\ast}\in\mathcal{U}(d_{0})
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, then
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(u^{*})=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(u(t))
by the
C^{1}
-smoothness of
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
.
Furthermore, there exists a sequence
\xi_{n}\in(n,n+1)
such that

u_{i}(n+1)-u_{i}(n)=u^{\prime}_{i}(\xi_{n})=\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}-\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u(\xi_{n}))\rightarrow 0,\ \text{as}\ n\rightarrow+\infty,

which implies
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u^{*})=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u(\xi_{n}))=\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}
for all
v_{i}\in V

and
u^{\ast}\in\mathcal{U}(d_{0})
is a discrete conformal factor with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
.

Conversely, suppose there exists a PH metric
u^{\ast}
in the discrete conformal class
\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
. Set

W_{\alpha}(u)=W(u)-\int_{u^{*}}^{u}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i}du_{i}=\int^{u}_{u^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{F}_{i}du_{i}-\int_{u^{*}}^{u}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i}du_{i}.

Then
W_{\alpha}(u)
is a
C^{2}
-smooth strictly convex function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by Proposition
3.5
and the condition
\alpha\overline{\mathbf{F}}\leq 0
.
Furthermore,
W_{\alpha}(u^{*})=0,\ \nabla W_{\alpha}(u^{*})=0
,
which implies
W_{\alpha}(u)\geq W_{\alpha}(u^{\ast})=0
and
\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}W_{\alpha}(u)=+\infty
by the convexity of
W_{\alpha}(u)
.
On can also refer to Lemma 4.6 in
[
13
]
for a proof for this fact.
By direct calculations, we have

\frac{dW_{\alpha}(u(t))}{dt}=\sum^{N}_{i=1}\frac{\partial W_{\alpha}}{\partial u_{i}}\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=\sum^{N}_{i=1}(\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}-\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i})w^{\alpha}_{i}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}-\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i})=-\sum^{N}_{i=1}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}-\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i})^{2}w^{\alpha}_{i}\leq 0,

which implies
0\leq W_{\alpha}(u(t))\leq W_{\alpha}(u(0))
.
Therefore,
\{u(t)\}\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
by
\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}W_{\alpha}(u)=+\infty
,
which implies the solution of modified combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow with surgery (
19
) exists for all time and
W_{\alpha}(u(t))
converges.
Moreover, there exists a sequence
\xi_{n}\in(n,n+1)
such that as
n\rightarrow+\infty
,

W_{\alpha}(u(n+1))-W_{\alpha}(u(n))=(W_{\alpha}(u(t))^{\prime}|_{\xi_{n}}=\nabla W_{\alpha}\cdot\frac{du_{i}}{dt}|_{\xi_{n}}=-\sum^{N}_{i=1}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}-\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i})^{2}w^{\alpha}_{i}|_{\xi_{n}}\rightarrow 0.

Then
\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u(\xi_{n}))=\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u^{*})
for all
v_{i}\in V
.
By
\{u(t)\}\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exists
\overline{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
a subsequence of
\{u(\xi_{n})\}
, still denoted as
\{u(\xi_{n})\}
for simplicity,
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}u(\xi_{n})=\overline{u}
, which implies

\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(\overline{u})=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u(\xi_{n}))=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u^{*})
.
This further implies
\overline{u}=u^{*}
by Theorem
3.8
.
Therefore,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}u(\xi_{n})=u^{*}
.

Set
\Gamma(u)=\overline{\mathbf{F}}-\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
, then
D\Gamma|_{u=u^{\ast}}
has
N
negative eigenvalues, which implies that
u^{\ast}
is a local attractor of (
19
). Then the conclusion follows from Lyapunov Stability Theorem (
[
21
]
, Chapter 5).
∎

3.4
Existence of PH metrics with the prescribed
\alpha
-curvature

Set
M_{\alpha,i}=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}-\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}
.
Then the modified combinatorial Yamabe flow with surgery (
19
) could be written as

\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=-M_{\alpha,i}.

We have the following evolution equation for
M_{\alpha,i}
along the modified combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow with surgery (
19
).

Lemma 3.12
.

Along the modified combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow with surgery (
19
),
M_{\alpha,i}
evolves according to

\frac{dM_{\alpha,i}}{dt}=\sum_{j\sim i}\frac{B_{ij}}{w^{\alpha}_{i}}(M_{\alpha,j}-M_{\alpha,i})-\frac{A_{i}}{w^{\alpha}_{i}}M_{\alpha,i}+\alpha M_{\alpha,i}(M_{\alpha,i}+\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}),

(20)

where
A_{i}>0
,
B_{ij}\geq 0.

Proof.

By the chain rules,

\displaystyle\frac{dM_{\alpha,i}}{dt}=

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{j=1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}}{\partial u_{j}}\frac{du_{j}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{j=1}(\frac{1}{w^{\alpha}_{i}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}-\frac{\mathbf{F}_{i}}{w^{\alpha}_{i}}\alpha\delta_{ij})\cdot(-M_{\alpha,j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\alpha}M_{\alpha})_{i}+\alpha M_{\alpha,i}(M_{\alpha,i}+\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\sim i}\frac{B_{ij}}{w^{\alpha}_{i}}(M_{\alpha,j}-M_{\alpha,i})-\frac{A_{i}}{w^{\alpha}_{i}}M_{\alpha,i}+\alpha M_{\alpha,i}(M_{\alpha,i}+\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}),

where (
16
) is used in the last line.

A_{i}>0
and
B_{ij}\geq 0
follows from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

To analyze the longtime behavior of the combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow with surgery, we need the following
discrete maximum principle obtained in
[
14
]
.

Theorem 3.13
.

(Maximum Principle)
Let
f:V\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{1}
function such that

\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}\geq\sum_{j\sim i}a_{ij}(f_{j}-f_{i})+\Phi_{i}(f_{i}),\ \forall(v_{i},t)\in V\times[0,T),

where
a_{ij}\geq 0
and
\Phi_{i}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a local Lipschitz function. Suppose there exists
C_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
f_{i}(0)\geq C_{1}
for all
v_{i}\in V
. Let
\varphi
be the solution to the associated ODE

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{d\varphi}{dt}=\Phi_{i}(\varphi)\\
\varphi(0)=C_{1},\end{cases}

then
f_{i}(t)\geq\varphi(t)
for all
(v_{i},t)\in V\times[0,T)
such that
\varphi(t)
exists.

Similarly, suppose
f:V\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{1}
function such that

\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}\leq\sum_{j\sim i}a_{ij}(f_{j}-f_{i})+\Phi_{i}(f_{i}),\ \forall(v_{i},t)\in V\times[0,T).

Suppose there exists
C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
f_{i}(0)\leq C_{2}
for all
v_{i}\in V
. Let
\psi
be the solution to the associated ODE

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{d\psi}{dt}=\Phi_{i}(\psi)\\
\psi(0)=C_{2},\end{cases}

then
f_{i}(t)\leq\psi(t)
for all
(v_{i},t)\in V\times[0,T)
such that
\psi(t)
exists.

3.13

used in

\alpha
-Laplace operator

,
here we use the notation

rather than

.

3.13
to (

).

As another application of Theorem

, we have the following existence of PH metrics with
prescribed combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature.

Lemma 3.15
.

Suppose
(S,V)
is a connected closed marked surface with a PH metric
d_{0}
,
\alpha>0
is a constant and
\overline{\mathbf{F}}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a negative constant function defined on
V
.
If there exists
u^{\ast}\in\mathcal{U}(d_{0})
such that
\overline{\mathbf{F}}<\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u^{\ast})<0
for all
v_{i}\in V
,
then there exists a PH metric in the discrete conformal class
\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
.

Proof.

Taking
u^{*}
as the initial value of
the modified combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow with surgery (
19
).
We shall prove that the solution of (
19
) with initial value
u(0)=u^{*}

exists for all time and converges to a discrete conformal factor with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
, which implies
the existence of a PH metric in the discrete conformal class
\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
.

Set

\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,\max}(u(0))=\max_{i\in V}\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u^{\ast}),\ M_{\alpha,\max}=\max_{i\in V}M_{\alpha,i}.

\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,\min}(u(0))=\min_{i\in V}\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u^{\ast}),\ M_{\alpha,\min}=\min_{i\in V}M_{\alpha,i}.

Due to
\overline{\mathbf{F}}<\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,\min}(u(0))
, we have
M_{\alpha,i}(0)>0
for all
v_{i}\in V
, which implies
M_{\alpha,i}(t)\geq 0
for all
v_{i}\in V
by Corollary
3.14
.
Combining
A_{i}>0
,
M_{\alpha,i}(t)\geq 0
and (
20
), we have

\frac{dM_{\alpha,i}}{dt}\leq\sum_{j\sim i}\frac{B_{ij}}{w^{\alpha}_{i}}(M_{\alpha,j}-M_{\alpha,i})+\alpha M_{\alpha,i}(M_{\alpha,i}+\overline{\mathbf{F}}),

which implies

0\leq M_{\alpha,i}(t)\leq\frac{\mathbf{\overline{F}}}{-1+(\frac{\mathbf{\overline{F}}}{M_{\alpha,\max}(0)}+1)e^{-\alpha\mathbf{\overline{F}}t}}\leq\frac{\overline{\mathbf{F}}M_{\alpha,max}(u(0))}{\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,max}(u(0))}e^{\alpha\mathbf{\overline{F}}t}

(21)

by Theorem
3.13
.
Combining
\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=-M_{\alpha,i}
,
\alpha>0
and
\overline{\mathbf{F}}<0
,
the estimation (
21
) further implies
\{u(t)\}\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Therefore, the solution of modified combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow with surgery (
19
) exists for all time and converges, which implies the existence of a PH metric in the conformal class of
\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
.
∎

Remark 8
.

Lemma
3.15
still holds under the condition that
\alpha<0
,
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
is a positive constant and
0<\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u^{\ast})<\overline{\mathbf{F}}
for all
v_{i}\in V
,
the proof of which is the same as that for Lemma
3.15
.

To handle more general cases, we need the following technique lemma.

Lemma 3.16
.

Suppose
\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F
is a hyperbolic triangle with discrete hyperbolic metrics
d_{ij},d_{jk},d_{ki}
.
If the discrete conformal factors
u_{i},u_{j},u_{k}
for the hyperbolic triangle are uniformly bounded from below, i.e.
\exists C
such that

u_{i},u_{j},u_{k}\in[C,+\infty)
.
Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a number
\widetilde{C}=\widetilde{C}(d_{ij},d_{jk},d_{ki},C)
such that if
u_{i}>\widetilde{C}
,
the inner angle
\alpha^{jk}_{i}
is smaller than
\epsilon
.

Proof.

We use the trick from
[
12
]
to prove the lemma.
Due to

\sinh\frac{l_{ij}}{2}=\sinh\frac{d_{ij}}{2}e^{u_{i}+u_{j}}\geq\sinh\frac{d_{ij}}{2}e^{u_{i}+C},

we have
\sinh\frac{l_{ij}}{2}\rightarrow+\infty,\ \sinh\frac{l_{ik}}{2}\rightarrow+\infty
uniformly as
u_{i}\rightarrow+\infty
, where
l_{rs}=(u*d)_{rs}
,
\{r,s,t\}=\{i,j,k\}
.
By the hyperbolic cosine law,

\displaystyle\cos\alpha^{jk}_{i}=

\displaystyle\frac{\cosh l_{ij}\cosh l_{ik}-\cosh l_{jk}}{\sinh l_{ij}\sinh l_{ik}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\cosh(l_{ij}+l_{ik})+\cosh(l_{ij}-l_{ik})-2\cosh l_{jk}}{\cosh(l_{ij}+l_{ik})-\cosh(l_{ij}-l_{ik})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\lambda-2\mu}{1-\lambda},

where
\lambda=\frac{\cosh(l_{ij}-l_{ik})}{\cosh(l_{ij}+l_{ik})}
and
\mu=\frac{\cosh l_{jk}}{\cosh(l_{ij}+l_{ik})}
.
To prove
\alpha^{jk}_{i}\rightarrow 0
uniformly,
we just need to prove
\lambda,\mu\rightarrow 0
uniformly as
u_{i}\rightarrow+\infty
.
Note that

\displaystyle 0<

\displaystyle\lambda<\frac{\cosh l_{ij}}{\cosh(l_{ij}+l_{ik})}<\frac{1}{\cosh l_{ik}}\leq\frac{1}{\sinh\frac{d_{ik}}{2}e^{u_{i}+C}},

\displaystyle 0<

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{1}{(1+2\sinh^{2}\frac{l_{ij}}{2})(1+2\sinh^{2}\frac{l_{ik}}{2})}+\frac{\sinh^{2}\frac{d_{jk}}{2}}{\sinh^{2}\frac{d_{ij}}{2}\sinh^{2}\frac{d_{ik}}{2}e^{4u_{i}}},

we have
\lambda,\mu\rightarrow 0
uniformly as
u_{i}\rightarrow+\infty
.
Therefore,
\alpha^{jk}_{i}\rightarrow 0
uniformly as
u_{i}\rightarrow+\infty
.
∎

As an application of Lemma
3.16
, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.17
.

Suppose
(S,V)
is a connected closed marked surface with a PH metric
d_{0}
,
\alpha>0
is a constant and
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
is a non-positive constant.

\mathcal{D}(d_{0})

\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}

Because
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(u^{*})
and

In the case that
\overline{\mathbf{F}}=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(u^{*})<0
,
\mathbf{A}^{-1}(p,w(-2u^{\ast}))\in\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
is a PH metric
with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
.

In the case that
\overline{\mathbf{F}}<\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(u^{*})<0
, the conclusion follows from Lemma
3.15
.

In the case that
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(u^{*})<\overline{\mathbf{F}}\leq 0
, we
take
u^{*}
as the initial value of the modified combinatorial
\alpha
-Yamabe flow with surgery (
19
).
Then
M_{\alpha}(0)<0
is a constant.
By Corollary
3.14
,
M_{\alpha,i}(t)\leq 0
for all
v_{i}\in V
, which implies
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u(t))\leq\overline{\mathbf{F}}\leq 0
for all
v_{i}\in V
.
Combining
A_{i}>0
,
M_{\alpha,i}(t)\leq 0
and (
20
), we have

\frac{dM_{\alpha,i}}{dt}\geq\sum_{j\sim i}\frac{B_{ij}}{w^{\alpha}_{i}}(M_{\alpha,j}-M_{\alpha,i})+\alpha M_{\alpha,i}(M_{\alpha,i}+\overline{\mathbf{F}}).

Therefore, by Theorem
3.13
,

\frac{\mathbf{\overline{F}}}{-1+(\frac{\mathbf{\overline{F}}}{M_{\alpha}(0)}+1)e^{-\alpha\mathbf{\overline{F}}t}}\leq M_{\alpha,i}(t)\leq 0,\ \text{if}\ \mathbf{\overline{F}}<0,

(22)

\frac{M_{\alpha}(0)}{-\alpha M_{\alpha}(0)t+1}\leq M_{\alpha,i}(t)\leq 0,\ \text{if}\ \mathbf{\overline{F}}=0.

In the case that
\mathbf{\overline{F}}<0
, by the the estimation (
22
) and
\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=-M_{\alpha,i}(t)
,
we have
\{u(t)\}\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

\alpha

Proof of Theorem

\overline{u}\in\mathcal{U}(d_{0})
such that
\sum^{N}_{i}K_{i}^{\ast}(\overline{u})>2\pi\chi(S)
and
K_{i}^{\ast}(\overline{u})<0
for all
v_{i}\in V
. Then
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u^{\ast})<0
for all
v_{i}\in V
.
By Theorem
3.19
, there exists a PH metric in the discrete conformal class
\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
with
non-positive combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
.

In the case of
\alpha<0,\overline{\mathbf{F}}>0
,
by Theorem
3.3
,
there exists
u^{*}\in\mathcal{U}(d_{0})
such that
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u^{\ast})>0
for all
v_{i}\in V
.
By Theorem
3.19
, there exists a PH metric in the discrete conformal class
\mathcal{D}(d_{0})

with positive combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
.

The case of
\alpha=0
follows from Theorem
3.3
.
∎

Definition 3.21
.

Suppose
(S,V)
is a connected closed marked surface with a PH metric
d_{0}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant. The combinatorial
\alpha
-Calabi flow with surgery is defined to be

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=\Delta_{\alpha}\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i},\\
u_{i}(0)=0,\end{cases}

(23)

where
\Delta_{\alpha}
is the
\alpha
-Laplace operator defined by (
15
).

One can use the following modified combinatorial
\alpha
-Calabi flow with surgery
to study prescribed combinatorial
\alpha

\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=\Delta_{\alpha}(\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}-\overline{\mathbf{F}})_{i},

(24)

where
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
is a function defined on
V
.

Theorem 3.22
.

Suppose
(S,V)
is a connected closed marked surface with a PH metric
d_{0}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant
and
\overline{\mathbf{F}}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a function defined on
V
with
\alpha\overline{\mathbf{F}}\leq 0
.
Then there exists a PH metric in the discrete conformal class
\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
if and only if the solution of modified combinatorial
\alpha
-Calabi flow with surgery (
24
)
exists for all time and converges exponentially fast to a PH metric with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
.

Proof.

Suppose the solution
u(t)
of the modified combinatorial
\alpha
-Calabi flow with surgery (
24
)
converges to
u^{\ast}\in\mathcal{U}(d_{0})
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, then

\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(u^{*})=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(u(t))
by the
C^{1}
-smoothness of
\mathbf{F}
.
Furthermore, there exists a sequence
\xi_{n}\in(n,n+1)
such that

u_{i}(n+1)-u_{i}(n)=u^{\prime}_{i}(\xi_{n})=\Delta_{\alpha}(\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(u(\xi_{n}))-\mathbf{\overline{F}})_{i}\rightarrow 0,\ \text{as}\ n\rightarrow+\infty,

which implies
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u^{*})=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u(\xi_{n}))=\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}
for all
v_{i}\in V
and
u^{*}\in\mathcal{U}(d_{0})
corresponds to a PH metric with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}
.

Conversely, suppose there exists a PH metric in the conformal class
\mathcal{D}(d_{0})
with combinatorial
\alpha
-curvature
\overline{\mathbf{F}}

and
u^{*}
is the corresponding discrete conformal factor in
\mathcal{U}(d_{0})
. Set

W_{\alpha}(u)=W(u)-\int_{u^{*}}^{u}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i}du_{i}=\int^{u}_{u^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{F}_{i}du_{i}-\int_{u^{*}}^{u}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i}du_{i}.

Then
W_{\alpha}(u)
is a
C^{2}
-smooth strictly convex function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by Proposition
3.5
and
\alpha\overline{\mathbf{F}}\leq 0
.
Furthermore,
W_{\alpha}(u^{*})=0,\ \nabla W_{\alpha}(u^{*})=0
and
\mathrm{Hess}W_{\alpha}>0
, which implies
W_{\alpha}(u)\geq W_{\alpha}(u^{\ast})=0
and
\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}W_{\alpha}(u)=+\infty
. By direct calculations, we have

\frac{dW_{\alpha}(u(t))}{dt}=\sum^{N}_{i=1}\frac{\partial W_{\alpha}}{\partial u_{i}}\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=\sum^{N}_{i=1}(\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}-\overline{\mathbf{F}})_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i}\Delta_{\alpha}(\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}-\overline{\mathbf{F}})_{i}=-(\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}-\overline{\mathbf{F}})^{T}\cdot\widetilde{L}\cdot(\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}-\overline{\mathbf{F}})\leq 0

by Proposition
3.5
,
which implies
0\leq W_{\alpha}(u(t))\leq W_{\alpha}(u(0))
. Thus
\{u(t)\}\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
by
\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}W_{\alpha}(u)=+\infty
,
which implies the solution of the modified combinatorial
\alpha
-Calabi flow with surgery (
24
) exists for all time and
W_{\alpha}(u(t))
converges.
Moreover, there exists a sequence
\xi_{n}\in(n,n+1)
such that as
n\rightarrow+\infty
,

\displaystyle W_{\alpha}(u(n+1))-W_{\alpha}(u(n))=(W_{\alpha}(u(t))^{\prime}|_{\xi_{n}}=\nabla W_{\alpha}\cdot\frac{du_{i}}{dt}|_{\xi_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}(\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}-\overline{\mathbf{F}})_{i}w^{\alpha}_{i}\Delta_{\alpha}(\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}-\overline{\mathbf{F}})_{i}|_{\xi_{n}}=-(\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}-\overline{\mathbf{F}})^{T}\cdot\widetilde{L}\cdot(\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}-\overline{\mathbf{F}})|_{\xi_{n}}\rightarrow 0.

Then
\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u(\xi_{n}))=\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{i}=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha,i}(u^{*})
for all
v_{i}\in V
, which implies
\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}u(\xi_{n})=u^{*}
by Theorem
3.8
.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
2.6
, set
\Gamma(u)=\Delta_{\alpha}(\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}-\overline{\mathbf{F}})
,
then
D\Gamma|_{u=u^{\ast}}
has
N
negative eigenvalues, which implies that
u^{*}
is a local attractor of (
24
). Then the conclusion follows by Lyapunov Stability Theorem (
[
21
]
, Chapter 5).
∎

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
2.2

We use
j\sim i
to denote that the vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
are adjacent.
By Lemma
2.1
,
\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}=\frac{\partial\alpha^{ik}_{j}}{\partial u_{i}}
and
\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}=\frac{\partial K_{j}}{\partial u_{i}}
.

(1)

If
j\nsim i
and
j\neq i
, then
\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}=0.

(2)

If
j\sim i
, then

\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}=\frac{\partial(2\pi-\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\alpha^{jk}_{i})}{\partial u_{j}}=-\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}=-(\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}+\frac{\partial\alpha^{jl}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}),

where
\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}
and
\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{l}
are adjacent triangles sharing a common edge
\{v_{i}v_{j}\}
.
Set
B_{ij}=\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}+\frac{\partial\alpha^{jl}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}
, then

\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}=-B_{ij}.

(3)

If
j=i
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\frac{\partial\left(\alpha^{ik}_{j}+\alpha^{ij}_{k}+\text{Area}(\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k})\right)}{\partial u_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\left(\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}+\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{k}}\right)+\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\frac{\partial\text{Area}(\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k})}{\partial u_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\sim i}\left(\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}+\frac{\partial\alpha^{jl}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}\right)+\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\left(\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\text{Area}(\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k})\right).

Set
A_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\left(\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\text{Area}(\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k})\right)
, then

\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}=A_{i}+\sum_{j\sim i}B_{ij}.

(25)

Set
L_{A}=\text{diag}\{A_{1},...,A_{N}\}
and
L_{B}=((L_{B})_{ij})_{N\times N}
, where

\displaystyle(L_{B})_{ij}=\begin{cases}\sum_{k\sim i}B_{ik},&{j=i},\\
-B_{ij},&{j\sim i},\\
0,&{j\nsim i,j\neq i}.\end{cases}

Then
L=L_{A}+L_{B}
.

To get the expression of
B_{ij}
in (
12
), we need the following lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose
\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}
is a hyperbolic triangle with a PH metric
d_{0}
and
u
is a discrete conformal factor for the
triangle. Denote
d_{st}:=(u*d_{0})_{st}
as the edge length of
\{st\}
in the triangle
\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}

and
\alpha^{jk}_{i},\alpha^{ik}_{j},\alpha^{ij}_{k}
as the inner angles facing
\{jk\},\{ik\},\{ij\}
respectively. Then

\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}=\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}\frac{d_{ij}}{2}}\tan\frac{\alpha^{jk}_{i}+\alpha^{ik}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{k}}{2}.

Proof.

By the derivative cosine law (see Lemma A1 in
[
2
]
for example),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial d_{jk}}=\frac{\sinh d_{jk}}{A},\ \frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial d_{ik}}=\frac{-\sinh d_{jk}\cos\alpha^{ij}_{k}}{A},\ \frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial d_{ij}}=\frac{-\sinh d_{jk}\cos\alpha^{ik}_{j}}{A},

where
A=\sinh d_{ik}\sinh d_{ij}\sin\alpha^{jk}_{i}
.
By (
2
), we have

\frac{\partial d_{jk}}{\partial u_{i}}=0,\ \frac{\partial d_{jk}}{\partial u_{j}}=\frac{\partial d_{jk}}{\partial u_{k}}=2\tanh\frac{d_{jk}}{2}.

According to the chain rules,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial d_{jk}}\frac{\partial d_{jk}}{\partial u_{j}}+\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial d_{ik}}\frac{\partial d_{ik}}{\partial u_{j}}+\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial d_{ij}}\frac{\partial d_{ij}}{\partial u_{j}}

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\sinh d_{jk}}{A}\tanh\frac{d_{jk}}{2}-\frac{2\sinh d_{jk}\cos\alpha^{ik}_{j}}{A}\tanh\frac{d_{ij}}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2(\cosh d_{jk}+\cosh d_{ik}-\cosh d_{ij}-1)}{A(\cosh d_{ij}+1)}.

By the hyperbolic cosine law expressing
d_{ik}
in terms of
\alpha^{jk}_{i},\alpha^{ik}_{j},\alpha^{ij}_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\cosh^{2}\frac{d_{ik}}{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\cosh d_{ik}+1)

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\cos\alpha^{ik}_{j}+\cos\alpha^{ij}_{k}\cos\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\sin\alpha^{ij}_{k}\sin\alpha^{jk}_{i}}+1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\cos\frac{\alpha^{ik}_{j}+\alpha^{ij}_{k}-\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{2}\cos\frac{\alpha^{ik}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{k}+\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{2}}{\sin\alpha^{ij}_{k}\sin\alpha^{jk}_{i}},

which implies

\displaystyle\cosh\frac{d_{jk}}{2}\cosh\frac{d_{ik}}{2}\sin\alpha^{ij}_{k}

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\cos\frac{\alpha^{jk}_{i}+\alpha^{ik}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{k}}{2}\cos\frac{\alpha^{jk}_{i}-\alpha^{ik}_{j}+\alpha^{ij}_{k}}{2}}{\sin\alpha^{ik}_{j}\sin\alpha^{ij}_{k}}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\cos\frac{\alpha^{ik}_{j}+\alpha^{ij}_{k}-\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{2}\cos\frac{\alpha^{ik}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{k}+\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{2}}{\sin\alpha^{ij}_{k}\sin\alpha^{jk}_{i}}}\sin\alpha^{ij}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cosh\frac{d_{ij}}{2}\cos\frac{\alpha^{jk}_{i}+\alpha^{ik}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{k}}{2},

where (
27
) is used in the last line.
Recall the following formula obtained by Gu-Guo-Luo-Sun-Wu (
[
16
]
Lemma 11)

2\sin\frac{\alpha^{jk}_{i}+\alpha^{ik}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{k}}{2}\cdot\cosh\frac{d_{ij}}{2}=\frac{\sinh^{2}\frac{d_{jk}}{2}+\sinh^{2}\frac{d_{ik}}{2}-\sinh^{2}\frac{d_{ij}}{2}}{\sinh\frac{d_{jk}}{2}\sinh\frac{d_{ik}}{2}}.

(29)

Then (
26
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}=

\displaystyle\frac{2(\cosh d_{jk}+\cosh d_{ik}-\cosh d_{ij}-1)}{\sinh d_{ik}\sinh d_{jk}\sin\alpha^{ij}_{k}(\cosh d_{ij}+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4(\sinh^{2}\frac{d_{jk}}{2}+\sinh^{2}\frac{d_{ik}}{2}-\sinh^{2}\frac{d_{ij}}{2})}{\sinh d_{ik}\sinh d_{jk}\sin\alpha^{ij}_{k}(\cosh d_{ij}+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{8\sinh\frac{d_{jk}}{2}\sinh\frac{d_{ik}}{2}\cosh\frac{d_{ij}}{2}}{\sinh d_{ik}\sinh d_{jk}\sin\alpha^{ij}_{k}(\cosh d_{ij}+1)}\sin\frac{\alpha^{jk}_{i}+\alpha^{ik}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{k}}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh\frac{d_{ij}}{2}\cosh\frac{d_{jk}}{2}\cosh\frac{d_{ik}}{2}\sin\alpha^{ij}_{k}}\sin\frac{\alpha^{jk}_{i}+\alpha^{ik}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{k}}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}\frac{d_{ij}}{2}}\tan\frac{\alpha^{jk}_{i}+\alpha^{ik}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{k}}{2},

where (
29
) is used in the third line and (
28
) is used in the last line.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose
(S,V,\mathcal{T})
is a connected closed triangulated surface with a PH metric
d_{0}
.

d:=u*d_{0}
is a PH metric defined by a function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Then the triangulation
\mathcal{T}
is Delaunay in
d
if and only if
B_{ij}\geq 0
for any
\{v_{i}v_{j}\}\in E
,
which implies
L_{B}
is positive semi-definite.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle A_{i}=\sum_{j\sim i}B_{ij}(\cosh d_{ij}-1),

(30)

which is nonnegative if the triangulation
\mathcal{T}
is Delaunay in
d
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.1
, for
j\sim i
,

\displaystyle B_{ij}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}+\frac{\partial\alpha^{jl}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}\frac{d_{ij}}{2}}\left(\tan\frac{\alpha^{jk}_{i}+\alpha^{ik}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{k}}{2}+\tan\frac{\alpha^{jl}_{i}+\alpha^{il}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{l}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}\frac{d_{ij}}{2}}\cdot\frac{\sin\frac{\alpha^{jk}_{i}+\alpha^{ik}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{k}+\alpha^{jl}_{i}+\alpha^{il}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{l}}{2}}{\sin\frac{\pi-\alpha^{jk}_{i}-\alpha^{ik}_{j}+\alpha^{ij}_{k}}{2}\sin\frac{\pi-\alpha^{jl}_{i}-\alpha^{il}_{j}+\alpha^{ij}_{l}}{2}}.

Note that the triangulation
\mathcal{T}
is Delaunay if and only if
\alpha^{jk}_{i}+\alpha^{ik}_{j}+\alpha^{jl}_{i}+\alpha^{il}_{j}\geq\alpha^{ij}_{l}+\alpha^{ij}_{k}

[
16
,
18
]
, which is equivalent to
B_{ij}\geq 0
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have

x^{T}L_{B}x=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(L_{B})_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}=\sum_{i\sim j}(-B_{ij}x_{i}x_{j})+\sum_{i\sim j}x_{i}^{2}B_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\sim j}B_{ij}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\geq 0,

which implies
L_{B}
is positive semi-definite.
One can also refer to Lemma 3.10 in
[
2
]
for the positive semi-definiteness of
L_{B}
.

Recall the following formula obtained by Glickenstein-Thomas (
[
15
]
Proposition 9)

\frac{\partial\text{Area}(\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k})}{\partial u_{k}}=\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{k}}(\cosh d_{ik}-1)+\frac{\partial\alpha^{ik}_{j}}{\partial u_{k}}(\cosh d_{jk}-1).

Then

\displaystyle A_{i}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\frac{\partial\text{Area}(\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k})}{\partial u_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}(\cosh d_{ij}-1)+\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{k}}(\cosh d_{ik}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\sim i}\left(\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}+\frac{\partial\alpha^{jl}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}\right)(\cosh d_{ij}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\sim i}B_{ij}(\cosh d_{ij}-1).

Therefore,
B_{ij}\geq 0
implies
A_{i}\geq 0
.
∎

Remark 10
.

Submitting (
12
) into (
30
) gives

\displaystyle A_{i}=2\sum_{j\sim i}\tanh^{2}\frac{d_{ij}}{2}\cdot\left(\tan\frac{\alpha^{jk}_{i}+\alpha^{ik}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{k}}{2}+\tan\frac{\alpha^{jl}_{i}+\alpha^{il}_{j}-\alpha^{ij}_{l}}{2}\right).

(32)

Then the decomposition
L=L_{A}+L_{B}
with
A_{i}
given by (
32
) and
B_{ij}
given by (
12
) is equivalent to the formula obtained by
Bobenko-Pinkall-Springborn (
[
1
]
, Proposition 6.1.7).

We further have the following result on the sign of
A_{i}
under the Delaunay condition, which completes the proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma A.3
.

Suppose
(S,V,\mathcal{T})
is a connected closed triangulated surface with a PH metric
d_{0}
.

d:=u*d_{0}
is a PH metric defined by a function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
If the triangulation
\mathcal{T}
is Delaunay in
d
, then

A_{i}>0
for all
v_{i}\in V
, which implies
L_{A}
is positive definite.

Proof.

Combining (
25
) and (
30
) gives

\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}=A_{i}+\sum_{j\sim i}B_{ij}=\sum_{j\sim i}B_{ij}\cosh d_{ij}.

(33)

For a single triangle
\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}
, by direct calculations, we have

\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}=\frac{\cosh^{2}d_{ik}+\cosh^{2}d_{ij}-2\cosh d_{jk}\cosh d_{ik}\cosh d_{ij}+(1-\cosh d_{jk})(\cosh d_{ik}+\cosh d_{ij})}{A(1+\cosh d_{ik})(1+\cosh d_{ij})},

(34)

where
A=\sinh d_{ik}\sinh d_{ij}\sin\alpha^{jk}_{i}
. Note that

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\alpha^{jk}_{i}=

\displaystyle 1-\left(\frac{\cosh d_{ik}\cosh d_{ij}-\cosh d_{jk}}{\sinh d_{ik}\sinh d_{ij}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}d_{ik}\sinh^{2}d_{ij}}(-\cosh^{2}d_{jk}-\cosh^{2}d_{ik}-\cosh^{2}d_{ij}+1+2\cosh d_{jk}\cosh d_{ik}\cosh d_{ij}),

which implies

\displaystyle-\cosh^{2}d_{ik}-\cosh^{2}d_{ij}+2\cosh d_{jk}\cosh d_{ik}\cosh d_{ij}+(\cosh d_{jk}-1)(\cosh d_{ik}+\cosh d_{ij})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sinh^{2}d_{ik}\sinh^{2}d_{ij}\sin^{2}\alpha^{jk}_{i}+\sinh^{2}d_{jk}+(\cosh d_{jk}-1)(\cosh d_{ik}+\cosh d_{ij})

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0,

we have
\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}<0
by (
34
).
Then

\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}=\frac{\partial(2\pi-\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\alpha^{jk}_{i})}{\partial u_{i}}=-\sum_{\triangle v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in F}\frac{\partial\alpha^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}>0,

which implies
\sum_{j\sim i}B_{ij}>0
by (
33
) and
B_{ij}\geq 0
.
Therefore,
A_{i}=\sum_{j\sim i}B_{ij}(\cosh d_{ij}-1)>0
by (
30
).

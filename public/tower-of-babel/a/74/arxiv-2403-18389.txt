Abstract
. We investigate the rationality problem for ℚ \mathbb{Q} -Fano threefolds of Fano index ≥ 2 \geq 2 .



1.
Introduction

A three-dimensional algebraic projective variety
X

is called
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold
if it has only terminal

\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities,
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\simeq\mathbb{Z}
,
and its anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
is ample.
The class of these varieties is important in birational geometry because it is one of the possible outputs
of the Minimal Model Program in dimension
3
.
It is known that
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds are bounded, i.e. they lie in a finite number of algebraic
families. Moreover, the methods of
[
Kaw92
]
allow to obtain a (huge) list
of numerical invariants of
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds
[
B+
]
.
At the moment there is no classification, but there are a lot of partial results.

This work is a sequel to our previous papers
[
Pro22b
]
,
[
Pro22a
]
.
We are interested in the birational geometry of
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds rather than
biregular one.
Mainly, we will discuss the rationality question.

The
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index
of a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold
X
is the maximal integer
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)
that divides the
canonical class
K_{X}
in the Weil divisor class group modulo torsion (see (
2.2.1
)).
A Weil divisor
A
such that
-K_{X}\mathbin{\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)A
we call the
fundamental divisor

and denote it by
A_{X}
. It turns out that the classification of
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds of large index
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)

is much simpler (see
[
Suz04
]
and
[
Pro10
]
). Moreover,
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds of large index
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)

are expected to be rational:

1.1 Theorem

(
[
Pro22b
]
)
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold.
If
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}\geq 8
, then
X
is rational.

On the other hand, there are nonrational
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds of large
index.
For example, T. Okada
[
Oka19
]
showed that there are
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano
threefold hypersurfaces of index

2
,
3
,
5
, and
7
that
are not rational.

The following invariant will be very important in the sequel:

\operatorname{p}_{n}(X):=\max\left\{\operatorname{h}^{0}(X,{\mathscr{O}}_{X}(D))\mid D\mathbin{\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}nA_{X}\right\}.

If the Weil divisor class group
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
is torsion free, then the above definition becomes simpler:

\operatorname{p}_{n}(X)=\operatorname{h}^{0}(X,{\mathscr{O}}_{X}(nA_{X})).

Our main result is as follows:

1.2 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold with
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)\geq 2
.
If one of the following conditions hold, then
X
is rational

(i)

\operatorname{p}_{1}(X)\geq 4
,

(ii)

\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)\geq 3
and
\operatorname{p}_{1}(X)\geq 3
,

(iii)

\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)\geq 4
and
\operatorname{p}_{1}(X)\geq 2
,

(iv)

\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)\geq 5
and
\operatorname{p}_{2}(X)\geq 2
and
X
is not of type
[
B+
, # 41422]

(
see Proposition

7.4
)
,

(v)

\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)\geq 6
and
\operatorname{p}_{3}(X)\geq 2
.

We also study birational geometry of
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds with

\operatorname{p}_{1}(X)\geq 2
and
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)=2
or
3

(see Propositions
6.4
and

8.1
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation.

We employ the following notation.

•

\sim
and
\mathbin{\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}
denote the linear and
\mathbb{Q}
-linear equivalences of divisors, respectively;

•

\operatorname{Cl}(X)
denotes the Weil divisor class group of a normal variety
X
;

•

\operatorname{Cl}(X)_{\mathrm{t}}
is the torsion subgroup of
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
;

•

\operatorname{g}(X)
is the genus of the
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold
X
, that is,
\operatorname{g}(X):=\operatorname{h}^{0}(X,{\mathscr{O}}_{X}(-K_{X}))-2
;

•

\mathbb{P}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
is the weighted projective space with weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
;
coinciding weights we group together as follows
\mathbb{P}(\underbrace{w_{1},\dots,w_{1}}_{k},w_{2},\dots)=\mathbb{P}(w_{1}^{k},w_{2},\dots)
;

•

{\mathbf{B}}(X)
is the basket of singularities of a terminal threefold
X
(see
[
Rei87
]
);
this is a collection of virtual cyclic quotient singularities
\frac{1}{r_{P}}(1,-1,b_{P})

associated to each actual singular point of
X
. For short, in
{\mathbf{B}}(X)
we list only indices of
these virtual points, i.e.
{\mathbf{B}}(X)=(\{r_{P}\})
.

•

{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{N}
denotes the multiplicative cyclic group of order
N
. If
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{N}
acts on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
by

(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\longmapsto(\zeta_{n}^{w_{1}}x_{1},\dots,\zeta_{n}^{w_{n}}x_{n}),

where
\zeta_{n}
is a primitive
N
-th root of
1
, then we say that
(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
are the weights of the action an we write

{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{N}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
to specify the action.

2.2.
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds

For a Fano variety with at worst log terminal singularities we define
Fano-Weil
and
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano indices

as follows:

(2.2.1)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\operatorname{q}_{\operatorname{W}}(X)&:=&\max\{q\in\mathbb{Z}\mid-K_{X}\sim qA,\ \text{$A$ is a Weil divisor}\},\\[5.0pt]
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)&:=&\max\{q\in\mathbb{Z}\mid-K_{X}\mathbin{\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}qA,\ \text{$A$ is a Weil divisor}\}.\end{array}

The
fundamental divisor
of
X
is a Weil divisor
A_{X}
such that

(2.2.2)

-K_{X}\mathbin{\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)A_{X}.

Note that if
\operatorname{Cl}(X)_{\mathrm{t}}\neq 0
, then the class of
A_{X}

is not uniquely defined modulo linear equivalence.
However, in the case
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)=\operatorname{q}_{\operatorname{W}}(X)
we always accept the following.

2.2.3 Convention
.

If
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)=\operatorname{q}_{\operatorname{W}}(X)
we take
A_{X}

so that

-K_{X}\sim\operatorname{q}_{\operatorname{W}}(X)A_{X}.

The
Hilbert series
of a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold
X
is the formal power series
[
ABR02
]

\operatorname{h}_{X}(t)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\operatorname{h}^{0}(X,mA_{X})\cdot t^{m}.

It is computed by using the orbifold Riemann-Roch formula
[
Rei87
]
. If the group
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
contains an element
T
of
N
-torsion, we define
T
-Hilbert series

\operatorname{h}_{X}(t,\sigma)\in\mathbb{Z}[[t,\sigma]]/(\sigma^{N}-1)

as follows:

\operatorname{h}_{X}(t,\sigma)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\operatorname{h}^{0}(X,mA_{X}+jT)\cdot t^{m}\sigma^{j}.

Obviously, the above definition depends on the choice the class of
A_{X}
in

\operatorname{Cl}(X)
. Typically calculating
\operatorname{h}_{X}(t)
or
\operatorname{h}_{X}(t,\sigma)
for our purposes we need only
a few initial terms of the series.

Recall that the
Gorenstein index
of a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein variety
X
is a minimal positive integer
r

such that the Weil divisor
rK_{X}
is Cartier.

2.3.1 Definition
.

Let
X
be a threefold with terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities.
An
extremal blowup
of
X
is a birational morphism
f:\tilde{X}\to X

such that

\tilde{X}
has only terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities and
\uprho(\tilde{X}/X)=1
.

Note that in this situation the divisor
-K_{\tilde{X}}
is
f
-ample.

2.3.2 Theorem

(
[
Kaw96
]
)
.

Let
X\ni P
a cyclic quotient terminal threefold singularity of type
\frac{1}{r}(a,r-a,1)
,
r\geq 2

and let
f:\tilde{X}\to X
be
an extremal blowup
f:\tilde{X}\to X\ni P
whose center contains
P
.
Then
f
is the weighted
blowup with the system of weights
\frac{1}{r}(a,r-a,1)
, in particular, the
discrepancy of the
f
-exceptional divisor equals
1/r
.

2.3.3 Theorem

(
[
Kaw93
]
)
.

Let
X\ni P
a terminal threefold singularity of index
r
.
Then there exists an exceptional divisor
E
over
P
whose discrepancy equals
1/r
.

The following useful fact is a consequence of the classification of
extremal blowups
[
Kaw05
]
(see
[
Pro13
, Lemma 2.6]
for explanations).

2.3.4 Lemma
.

Let
X\ni P
be a threefold terminal point of index
r>1

with basket
{\mathbf{B}}(X,P)
,
let
f:\tilde{X}\to X
be an extremal blowup with
f(E)=P
,
where
E
is the exceptional divisor, and let
\alpha
be the discrepancy of
E
.

(i)

If
X\ni P
is a point of type other than
\mathrm{cA}/r
and
r>2
, then

\alpha=1/r
.

(ii)

If
X\ni P
is of type
\mathrm{cA}/r

and
{\mathbf{B}}(X,P)
consists of
n
points of index
r
, then
\alpha=a/r
, where
n\equiv 0\mod a
.

2.4.
Du Val del Pezzo surfaces

Let
S
be a del Pezzo surface with
only Du Val singularities. We assume that
\uprho(S)=1

The definitions of Fano indices and the fundamental divisor

(see (

) and (

Recall also our Convention
2.2.3
.
Let
\mu:\tilde{S}\to S
be the minimal resolution.
We say that a curve

(-1)
-curve

2.4.1 Lemma
.

In the above notation assume that
d:=K_{S}^{2}<8
.
One has:

(i)

The set of lines on
S
is finite and non-empty.

(ii)

The group
\operatorname{Cl}(S)
is generated by the classes of lines.

(iii)

For every effective Weil divisor
D
on
S
there is a presentation

D\sim a_{0}(-K_{S})+\sum a_{i}L_{i},

where the
L_{i}
are lines in
S
, the

if

.

(iv)

For any line

\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(S)=d
.

(v)

If

3.3 Proposition
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold with
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)\geq 3
,
\operatorname{q}_{\operatorname{W}}(X)\neq\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)
, and
\operatorname{p}_{1}(X)\geq 2
.
Then one of the following holds

(i)

X
is of type

3.2
of Proposition

3.2
and then
X
is the quotient
X^{\prime}/{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
,
where
X^{\prime}
is a hypersurface of degree
3
in
\mathbb{P}(1^{4},2)
:

\left\{x_{2}x_{1}^{(1)}+\phi_{3}\big(x_{1}^{(1)},\dots,x_{1}^{(4)}\big)=0\right\}/{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}(0,0,1,1,-1).

(ii)

X
is of type

3.2
of Proposition

3.2
and then
X
is the quotient
X^{\prime}/{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}
,
where
X^{\prime}
is a hypersurface of degree
4
in
\mathbb{P}(1^{3},2,3)

\left\{x_{3}x_{1}+x_{2}^{2}+\phi_{4}(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{1}^{\prime\prime})+\phi_{2}(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{1}^{\prime\prime})x_{1}^{2}=0\right\}/{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}(0,1,1,1,0).

Here the subscript is the degree of the corresponding variable or polynomial.

In both cases
X
is rational.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
we have either
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)=3
or
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)=4
.
First, consider the case
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)=3
. Then the variety

X
is of type
3.2
of Proposition
3.2
.
The group
\operatorname{Cl}(X)_{\mathrm{t}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
defines a triple cyclic cover
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
which is étale outside
\operatorname{Sing}(X)
. Thus
X=X^{\prime}/{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
, where

X^{\prime}
is a Fano threefold with terminal singularities (not necessarily
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano) such that

\operatorname{q}_{\operatorname{W}}(X^{\prime})
is divisible by
3
,
A^{3}_{X^{\prime}}=3/2
, and
{\mathbf{B}}(X^{\prime})=(2)
.
By Theorem
2.2.5
the variety
X^{\prime}
is a hypersurface of degree
3
in
\mathbb{P}(1^{4},2)
.
In these settings,
the unique point
P^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
of index
2
has coordinates
(0,0,0,0,1)
.
Since
P^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
is a terminal cyclic quotient singularity, the variable
x_{2}
of degree
2
appears in the equation of
X^{\prime}
.

The embedding
X^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{P}(1^{4},2)
is canonical,
so it is
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
-equivariant and the action of
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
on
X^{\prime}
is induced by a liner action on the ambient space
\mathbb{P}(1^{4},2)
.
Moreover, the homogeneous coordinates
x_{1}^{(k)}
and
x_{2}
can be taken to be semi-invariant.
Modulo a linear coordinate change we may assume that the equation of
X^{\prime}
is as follows:

x_{2}x_{1}^{(1)}+\phi(x_{1}^{(1)},\dots,x_{1}^{(4)})=0.

Consider the affine chart
U_{2}:=\{x_{2}\neq 0\}\subset X^{\prime}
.
Then

U_{2}=\{y^{(1)}+\phi^{\prime}(y^{(1)},\dots,y^{(4)})=0\}/{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}(1,1,1,1).

The quotient
U_{2}/{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
is a terminal cyclic quotient singularity.
Hence
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
-action on
U_{2}
has weights
(a,1,1,-1)
for some

modulo permutations
of
y^{(2)},y^{(3)},y^{(4)}
and changing the generator of
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
.
Thus
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
-action on
\mathbb{P}(1^{4},2)
has weights
(0,a,1,1,-1)
.
If
a=1
, then the fixed point locus contains the curve
\{x_{2}=x_{1}^{(4)}=0\}\cap X^{\prime}
, a contradiction.
If
a=-1
, then, by the same reason, the line
x_{2}=x_{1}^{(2)}=x_{1}^{(3)}=0

and so

. But in this case

and
x_{2}x_{1}^{(1)}

-invariant, a contradiction.

. This proves
(i)
.

Now consider the case
\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)=4
. Then again by Proposition
3.2
the variety

X
is of type
3.2
or
3.2
.
The group
\operatorname{Cl}(X)_{\mathrm{t}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
defines a double cover
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
which is étale outside
\operatorname{Sing}(X)

X^{\prime}
is a Fano threefold with terminal singularities. Moreover,
in our two cases we have

3.2
.

\operatorname{q}_{\operatorname{W}}(X^{\prime})=4

A^{3}_{X^{\prime}}=2/3

{\mathbf{B}}(X^{\prime})=(3)

\dim|A_{X^{\prime}}|=2

\operatorname{g}(X^{\prime})=22
;

3.2
.

\operatorname{q}_{\operatorname{W}}(X^{\prime})=4
,

,
{\mathbf{B}}(X^{\prime})=(5)
,
\dim|A_{X^{\prime}}|=2
,
\operatorname{g}(X^{\prime})=26
.

By Theorem
2.2.5
in the case

3.2

the variety
X^{\prime}
is a hypersurface of degree
4
in
\mathbb{P}(1^{3},2,3)
and
by Proposition
3.3.1
below the case
3.2
does not occur.
As above

{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}
-equivariant, where

\mathbb{P}(1^{3},2,3)
.

is a semi-invariant of degree

. Then we see that

3.3.1 Proposition
.

Let
Y
be a weak Fano threefold with terminal singularities with
\operatorname{g}(Y)>22
.
Assume that there exists a Weil divisor
B
on
Y
such that
-K_{Y}\sim 4B
and
\dim|B|\geq 2
.
Then
Y\simeq\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

Proof.

If
Y
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold, then the assertion follows from
[
Pro13
, Theorem 1.2]
.
Thus we assume that
Y
is not
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano.
Replacing
Y
with its
\mathbb{Q}
-factorialization, we may assume that
Y
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.
Then
\uprho(Y)>1
.
Run the MMP on
Y
:

(3.3.2)

Y=Y^{(0)}\dashrightarrow Y^{(1)}\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow Y^{(n-1)}\dashrightarrow Y^{(n)}.

Let
{\mathscr{B}}_{k}
be the proper transform of the linear system
{\mathscr{B}}_{0}:=|B|
on
Y^{(k)}
.
On each step the relation
-K_{Y^{(k)}}\sim 4{\mathscr{B}}_{k}
is kept.
Moreover,
\dim{\mathscr{B}}_{k}=\dim|B|\geq 2
. Thus
Y^{(n)}
has a structure of Mori-Fano fiber space
\varphi:Y^{(n)}\to Z
such that
for a general fiber
F
we have
-K_{F}\sim 4{\mathscr{B}}_{n}|_{F}
. This is not possible if
\varphi
is a del Pezzo or
a rational curve fibration. Hence
Z
is a point and
Y^{(n)}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold such that

\operatorname{q}_{\operatorname{W}}(Y^{(n)})
is divisible by
4
and
\operatorname{g}(Y^{(n)})\geq\operatorname{g}(Y)>22
.
By
[
Pro13
, Theorem 1.2]
we have
Y^{(n)}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
Let us consider the last step
\psi:Y^{(n-1)}\dashrightarrow Y^{(n)}
of the MMP.
Since
\uprho(Y^{(n)})=1
, the map
\psi:Y^{(n-1)}\dashrightarrow Y^{(n)}
must be a divisorial contraction and
since
-K_{Y^{(n-1)}}
is divisible,
\psi
cannot be a contraction of a divisor to a curve.
Thus
\psi
contracts a divisor
E\subset Y^{(n-1)}
to a point
P\in Y^{(n)}
.
Since
-K_{Y^{(n-1)}}\sim 4{\mathscr{B}}_{n-1}
is
\psi
-ample, we see that
P\in\operatorname{Bs}{\mathscr{B}}_{n}
.
Then
{\mathscr{B}}_{n}
is subsystem in
|{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1)|
of codimension
1
and
so
{\mathscr{B}}_{n}
has a single base point, say
P
.
Therefore,
{\mathscr{B}}_{n-1}
has no base points outside
E
. Since
-K_{Y^{(n-1)}}
is ample on
E
, we see that

-K_{Y^{(n-1)}}
is nef. Since
-K_{Y^{(n-1)}}
is the proper transform of
-K_{Y}
, it is also big.
Thus
Y^{(n-1)}
is a weak Fano threefold with terminal singularities and
-K_{Y^{(n-1)}}\sim 4{\mathscr{B}}_{n-1}
.
According to
[
Kaw01
]
the contraction
\psi
is the weighted blowup with weights
(1,w_{1},w_{2})
,
where
\gcd(w_{1},w_{2})=1
. Then we have

{\mathbf{B}}(Y^{(n-1)})=(w_{1},w_{2}),\qquad K_{Y^{(n-1)}}=\psi^{*}K_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}+(w_{1}+w_{2})E.

Here
w_{1}+w_{2}
is divisible by
4
because so
K_{Y^{(n-1)}}
is.
Then

0<(-K_{Y^{(n-1)}})^{3}=64-\frac{(w_{1}+w_{2})^{3}}{w_{1}w_{2}}.

Up to permutation of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
we obtain the following possibilities
for
\left(w_{1},w_{2},A_{Y^{(n-1)}}^{3}\right)
:

(1,3,2/3),\qquad(3,5,7/15),\qquad(5,7,8/35).

Then from the Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing and the orbifold Riemann-Roch formula (see (
A.1.2
)) we obtain

\operatorname{g}(Y^{(n-1)})=22,15,7

in these cases, respectively.
This contradicts our assumption
\operatorname{g}(Y)>22
.
∎

Consider the case

-Fano threefold.

Consider the first step

Assume that

On the other hand, by

Since

Assume that

Since

Write

.

In particular,

. On the other hand, by Theorem

there exists an exceptional over

.
Then the discrepancy of

Since

Therefore,

.
The anticanonical model

of

a Fano threefold whose singularities are also of index
\leq 2
.
By Theorem

we have

and

is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.
But in this case

Therefore,

, i.e.

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold. Then

in

As in the case

Finally in the case

.
As above, we may assume that the coordinates

4.

The following construction will be systematically used throughout the paper.
From now on we adopt the following notation.

4.1.

Let

According to

By our construction

and

As in

]

(see e.g.

We obtain the following Sarkisov link:

\textstyle{\tilde{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\chi}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{\bar{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\bar{f}}
\textstyle{X}
\textstyle{\hat{X}}

,

-factorial singularities,

-negative Mori contraction

4.1.2 Remark
.

The proof of
[

, Proposition 2.10]
shows that for any zero-dimensional
canonical center

of the pair

there exists an extremal blowup

as in

with center

.
Thus in general the link (

by the choice of

.

4.2.

In what follows, for a divisor (or a linear system)

on

and

we denote
proper transforms of

on
\tilde{X}
and

, respectively.
By

-exceptional divisor.
For

As in the case

We can write

\begin{array}[]{lll}K_{\tilde{X}}&\mathbin{\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}&f^{*}K_{X}+\alpha E,\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{Q},\ \alpha>0,\\[2.0pt]

(

Assume that the contraction

is birational. Then

-Fano threefold.
In this case, denote by

4.3.1 Lemma
.

If

4.4.

Assume that

is a fibration. Then we denote by

4.4.1 Lemma
.

Assume that

. Then

\hat{X}
is a del Pezzo surface with Du Val singularities of type

Regardless of whether

Appendix A

In this section we present a computer algorithm (see
[
Pro22b
, § 3]
or
[
Car08
, § 3]
) that alow to list
all the numerical possibilities for
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefolds of index at least
3
.
Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold
with
q:=\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)\geq 3
and let
T\in\operatorname{Cl}(X)_{\mathrm{t}}
be an element of order
N
.

Step 1
.

By
[
Kaw92
]
we have the inequality

(A.1.1)

0<-K_{X}\cdot c_{2}(X)=24-\sum_{P\in{\mathbf{B}}}\frac{r_{P}-1}{r_{P}}.

This produces a
finite (but huge) number of possibilities for the basket
{\mathbf{B}}(X)
and the number

-K_{X}\cdot c_{2}(X)
.

Step 2
.

Theorem
2.2.4
implies that
q\in\{3,\dots,11,13,17,19\}
.
In each case we compute
A_{X}^{3}
by the formula

A_{X}^{3}=\frac{12}{(q-1)(q-2)}\Bigl(1-\frac{A_{X}\cdot c_{2}(X)}{12}+\sum_{P\in B}c_{P}(-A_{X})\Bigr)

(see
[
Suz04
]
), where
c_{P}
is the correction term in the
orbifold Riemann-Roch formula
[
Rei87
]
. The number
rA_{X}^{3}

must be a positive integer by Theorem
2.2.4
(iii)
.

Step 3
.

On this step we can use an improved version of Bogomolov–Miyaoka inequality

[
LL23
]

instead of the one used in
[
Kaw92
]
and
[
Suz04
]
. Thus we have

(-K_{X})^{3}\ \begin{cases}<3(-K_{X})\cdot c_{2}(X)&\text{if $\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)\neq 4,\,5$},\\[10.00002pt]
\leq\frac{25}{8}(-K_{X})\cdot c_{2}(X)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This removes a lot of possibilities.

Step 4
.

In a neighborhood of each point
P\in X
we can write

A_{X}\sim l_{P}K_{X}
, where
0\leq l_{P}<r_{P}
.
There is a finite number of possibilities for the collection
\{(l_{P})\}
.
If
\operatorname{q}_{\operatorname{W}}(X)=\operatorname{q_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)
, then
\gcd(q,r)=1
by Theorem
2.2.4
.
In this case the numbers
l_{P}
are uniquely determined by
1+ql_{P}\equiv 0\mod r_{P}
because
K_{X}+qA_{X}\sim 0
.

Step 5
.

Similarly, a neighborhood of each point
P\in X
we can write

T\sim l_{P}^{\prime}K_{X}
, where
0\leq l_{P}^{\prime}<r_{P}
.
The collection
\{(l_{P}^{\prime})\}
and the number
N
satisfy the following properties:

\chi(X,\,{\mathscr{O}}_{X}(NT))=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\chi(X,\,{\mathscr{O}}_{X}(jT))=0\quad\text{for $j=1,\dots,N-1$}

(by the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing). Thus we obtain a finite number of possibilities for
\{(l_{P}^{\prime})\}
and
N
.

Step 6
.

Finally, applying Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing we obtain

\chi(X,\,{\mathscr{O}}_{X}(mA_{X}+jT))=\operatorname{h}^{0}(X,\,{\mathscr{O}}_{X}(mA_{X}+jT))=0.

for
-q<m<0
and
0\leq j<n
. Again, we check this condition using
orbifold Riemann-Roch and remove a lot of possibilities.

Step 7
.

We obtain a list of collections
\left(q,{\mathbf{B}}(X),A_{X}^{3},\{(l_{P})\},n\{(l_{P}^{\prime})\}\right)
.
In each case we compute
\operatorname{g}(X)
and
\operatorname{h}_{X}(t,\sigma)
by using the orbifold Riemann-Roch theorem.
For example,

(A.1.2)

\operatorname{g}(X)=-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}^{3}+1-\sum_{P\in{\mathbf{B}}}\frac{b_{P}(r_{P}-b_{P})}{2r_{P}}.

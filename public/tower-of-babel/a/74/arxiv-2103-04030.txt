1.2.
The construction of the distinguished metric

One of the main difficulties for the

-representation over a general complex analytic singularity, compared to isolated singularities, is the problem of equisingularity. The first step towards the

-representation of the intersection complex is to find a stratification along which

is constructible. In order to achieve this, we require the metrics

and

to be quasi-isotrivial along each stratum. In
[

,
20
,
5
]

and

are quasi-isotrivial along each stratum in a rather nice way. More precisely, for every stratum

in

in

\displaystyle\alpha_{x}:U_{S,x}\times C_{x}\simeq U_{x}

such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\alpha_{x}^{\ast}ds^{2}\sim ds^{2}_{S}+ds^{2}|_{C_{x}},\quad\alpha_{x}^{\ast}h\sim q^{\ast}(h|_{C_{x}})

for some hermitian metric

on

is a simple normal crossing divisor. Moreover, along each stratum the fibers
\pi^{-1}(x)
are smoothly stratified isotrivial. Such a pair
(\{X_{i}\},\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X)
is called a Whitney stratified desingularization (Definition
4.6
). It exists when
X
is compact or is a germ of complex space (Theorem
4.7
). For this construction, along each stratum the
C^{\infty}
fiberation (
1.1
) exists on the smooth model
\widetilde{X}
(Proposition
4.9
), rather than on
X
. A general phenomenon is that
every motivic hermitian metric (a metric defined by holomorphic functions) is quasi-isotrivial along the
C^{\infty}
fibration associated to some Whitney stratified desingularization, as long as its quasi-isometric local expression is independent of the choice of
C^{\infty}
complex coordinates.

This is useful to produce many interesting constructible
L^{2}
-de Rham complexes (Proposition
4.5
).

The distinguished metric could be described as follows (Definition
4.12
). Let
X
be a complex space and
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
a dense Zariski open subset. A hermitian metric on
X^{o}
is called distinguished associated to
(X,X^{o})
if for every point
x\in X
there is a neighborhood
U
and a Whitney stratified desingularization
\pi:\widetilde{U}\to U
of
(U,X^{o}\cap U)
such that
ds^{2}
is quasi-isometric to

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{dz_{i}d\overline{z}_{i}}{|z_{i}|^{2}\log^{2}|z_{1}\cdots z_{r}|^{2}\log^{2}(-\log|z_{1}\cdots z_{r}|^{2})}+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}dz_{i}d\overline{z}_{i}}{-\log|z_{1}\cdots z_{r}|^{2}\log^{2}(-\log|z_{1}\cdots z_{r}|^{2})}+\pi^{\ast}\omega_{0}.

Here
\omega_{0}
is a hermitian metric on
X
and
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
is a holomorphic coordinate on
\widetilde{U}
such that
\pi^{-1}(U\backslash X^{o})=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
. The distinguished metric witnesses the stratification
\{X_{i}\}
, which is necessary to construct an
L^{2}
-representation of the intersection complex.

1.3.
Strategy of the proof of the main theorem

One of the consequences of Theorem
1.4
is the Verdier duality

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h}[\dim X])\simeq\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V}^{\ast};ds^{2},h^{\ast}}[\dim X]\in D(X).

Here
\mathbb{D}
is the Verdier dual operator on
D(X)
.
Locally at every point
x\in X
this implies the local duality

Theorem 1.7

(Locally Duality, Theorem
8.1
)
.

Notations as in Theorem
1.4
. Let
x\in X
be a point. Then for every
0\leq k\leq 2\dim X
there is a duality

\displaystyle\varinjlim_{x\in U}\mathbb{H}^{2\dim X-k}(U,\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h})\simeq\varinjlim_{x\in U}\left(\mathbb{H}^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U,\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V}^{\ast};ds^{2},h^{\ast}})\right)^{\ast},

where
U
ranges over all open neighborhoods of
x
. Moreover both sides of the isomorphism are finite dimensional.

Denote the statements

•

{\bf IC}_{n}
: Theorem
1.4
holds when
\dim X\leq n
.

•

{\bf VD}_{n}
: Theorem
1.7
holds for
\dim X\leq n
and
k\leq\dim X
, under the additional assumptions that

\varinjlim_{x\in U}\left(\mathbb{H}^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U,\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V}^{\ast};ds^{2},h^{\ast}})\right)^{\ast}

is finite dimensional for every
k\leq\dim X
and

\varinjlim_{x\in U}\left(\mathbb{H}^{\dim X}_{\rm cpt}(U,\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h})\right)^{\ast}

is finite dimensional.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
follows from a bi-induction procedure:

Initial Step:

{\bf IC}_{0}
. This case is trivial.

Bi-induction Step:

{\bf IC}_{n-1}\Rightarrow{\bf VD}_{n}
,
{\bf VD}_{n}\Rightarrow{\bf IC}_{n}
.

Notice that in the induction procedure, non-irreducible complex space appears even
X
is irreducible. Therefore in Theorem
1.4
we allow the space to have many components.

Now let us explain the ideas in the proof of the bi-induction steps.

{\bf IC}_{n-1}\Rightarrow{\bf VD}_{n}
follows from an abstract
L^{2}
-Poincaré duality (Proposition
3.1
). The main difficulty is to show that
H^{\dim X}_{(2),\rm max}(U,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h)
is finite dimensional for a certain neighborhood
U
of
x
. Our strategy is to use an
L^{2}
-Mayer-Vietoris sequence to cut this cohomology into two parts: one is
\mathbb{H}^{\dim X}_{\rm cpt}(U,\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{\pi},h})
which is finite dimensional by the assumption. The other is the
L^{2}
-cohomology over the boundary
\partial U
. Since
\partial U
is a compact stratified space which intersects with
X
on the strata of positive dimensions,
{\bf IC}_{n-1}
ensures that the
L^{2}
-cohomology over
\partial U
is finite dimensional.

{\bf VD}_{n}\Rightarrow{\bf IC}_{n}
is divided into six steps.

(1)

Reduce the problem
{\bf IC}_{n}
to the case of a germ of complex space
0\in X\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
(Corollary
2.4
). This is a consequence of the Goresky-MacPherson-Deligne criterion (Proposition
2.2
).

(2)

Show that the distinguished metric and the harmonic metric are bimeromorphically stratified (Definition
4.4
) along the fibering stratification of
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
with respect to some Whitney stratified desingularization of
X
. This implies that the cohomology sheaves of
\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h}
are weakly constructible.

(3)

According to Step (2), the
L^{2}
-Poincaré lemma (Lemma
4.1
) and the
L^{2}
-Thom isomorphism (Lemma
4.2
) reduce the problem (via Proposition
4.5
) to two types of vanishings: Notations as in Theorem
1.4
. Assume that
0\in Z:=X
is a germ of complex space. Then there are vanishings

(1.3)

\displaystyle\varinjlim_{0\in U}\mathbb{H}^{k}(U,\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{Z,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h})=0,\quad\forall k\geq\dim Z

and

(1.4)

\displaystyle\varprojlim_{0\in U}\mathbb{H}^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U,\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{Z,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h})=0,\quad\forall k\leq\dim Z.

Here
U
ranges over the open neighborhoods of
0
.

(4)

Assuming
{\bf VD}_{n}
, it suffices to prove the vanishing (
1.4
) for both
\mathbb{V}
and
\mathbb{V}^{\ast}
. Since
(\mathbb{V}^{\ast},h^{\ast})
is also a
0
-tame harmonic bundle, it is enough to show (
1.4
) for an arbitrary
(\mathbb{V},h)
. This is the only place where we assume
{\bf VD}_{n}
.

(5)

(
1.4
) is proved via calculating the
L^{2}
-cohomologies of
U_{\delta}:=\{z\in Z|\|z\|<\delta\}
,
0<\delta\ll 1
. The key feature of
U_{\delta}
is that its
L^{2}
-cohomologies are independent of
\delta>0
. I.e. the ’extension by zero’ morphism

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U_{\delta^{\prime}},\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{Z,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h})\to\mathbb{H}^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U_{\delta},\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{Z,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h})

is an isomorphism for every
0<\delta^{\prime}<\delta\ll 1
(Proposition
5.6
). Hence (
1.4
) is equivalent to the vanishing

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U_{\delta},\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{Z,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h})=0,\quad\forall k\leq\dim Z.

We prove this vanishing by solving the equation
\nabla\beta=-\alpha
for
\nabla\alpha=0
in the space of
L^{2}
differential forms whose support is compact in
U_{\delta}
.
In order to do this, we introduce another cofinal system of neighborhoods
V_{\delta}
,
0<\delta\ll 1
. The main feature of
V_{\delta}
is the existence of a fiberation

(1.7)

\displaystyle V_{\delta}\cap Z^{o}\simeq(0,\delta)\times L

for some real submanifold
L\subset Z^{o}
, such that the distinguished metric satisfies the estimate (Proposition
5.4
)

(1.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho^{2}}{\rho^{2}\log^{2}\rho\log^{2}(-\log\rho)}+\frac{ds^{2}_{L}}{\log^{2}\rho\log^{2}(-\log\rho)}\lesssim ds^{2}\lesssim\frac{d\rho^{2}}{\rho^{2}\log^{2}\rho\log^{2}(-\log\rho)}+ds^{2}_{L}.

Here
ds^{2}_{L}
is a Riemannian metric on
L
which can be extended smoothly to the boundary.

To get rid of the complicated boundary condition (
[
15
, Proposition 2.1.1]
) caused by the incompleteness on
\partial U_{\delta}
, we make a modification on
ds^{2}
along
\partial U_{\delta}
, denoted by
ds^{2}_{\delta}
, so that
ds^{2}_{\delta}
is complete and
\|-\|_{ds^{2},h}\lesssim\|-\|_{ds^{2}_{\delta},w_{\delta}h}
for some weight function
w_{\delta}
. If
{\rm supp}\alpha
is compact in
U_{\delta}
, the modification on
\partial U_{\delta}
does not affect the analytical behavior of
\alpha
. This is one of the reasons why we choose to prove (
1.4
) instead of (
1.3
).

Due to the completeness of
ds^{2}_{\delta}
, the equation
\nabla\beta=-\alpha
is solvable in the Hilbert space of
L^{2}
differential forms with respect to
ds^{2}_{\delta}
and
w_{\delta}h
if one shows the estimate (
[
30
, §2.1.2 Lemma 2.1]
)

(1.9)

\displaystyle|(\alpha,u)|_{ds^{2}_{\delta},w_{\delta}h}\leq C\|\nabla^{\ast}_{\delta}u\|_{ds^{2}_{\delta},w_{\delta}h},\quad\forall u\in A^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U_{\delta}\cap Z^{o},\mathbb{V}),\forall k\leq\dim Z.

Here
\nabla^{\ast}_{\delta}
is the Hilbert adjoint of
\nabla
with respect to
(ds^{2}_{\delta},w_{\delta}h)
. Thanks to (
1.5
), we may assume that
{\rm supp}\alpha
lies in some
V_{\delta^{\prime\prime}}\subset U_{\delta}
.
Let
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+d\rho\wedge\alpha_{0}
be the decomposition according to (
1.7
).
There is a simple observation that the integration of
\alpha_{0}
along
(0,\delta^{\prime\prime})
in (
1.7
) gives a formal solution to the equation
\nabla\beta=-\alpha
. However this solution fails to be square integrable with respect to
(ds^{2}_{\delta},w_{\delta}h)
.
Our strategy is to deform
(ds^{2}_{\delta},w_{\delta}h)
to
(ds^{2}_{\delta,\epsilon},w_{\delta,\epsilon}h)
,
\epsilon\in(0,1)
so that

\|\alpha\|_{ds^{2}_{\delta,\epsilon},w_{\delta,\epsilon}h}\lesssim\|\alpha\|_{ds^{2}_{\delta},w_{\delta}h}.

The asymptotic behavior (
1.8
) and the
0
-tameness of the harmonic metric

ensure that

\beta\in L_{(2)}^{k-1}(U_{\delta}\cap Z^{o},\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{\delta,\epsilon},w_{\delta,\epsilon}h)

for every
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. Hence there is an estimate

(1.10)

\displaystyle|(\alpha,u)|_{ds^{2}_{\delta,\epsilon},w_{\delta,\epsilon}h}\leq C_{\epsilon}\|\nabla^{\ast}_{\delta,\epsilon}u\|_{ds^{2}_{\delta,\epsilon},w_{\delta,\epsilon}h},\quad\forall u\in A^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U_{\delta}\cap Z^{o},\mathbb{V}),\forall k\leq\dim Z.

Here
\nabla^{\ast}_{\delta,\epsilon}
is the Hilbert adjoint of
\nabla
with respect to
(ds^{2}_{\delta,\epsilon},w_{\delta,\epsilon}h)
and

C_{\epsilon}\leq\|\beta\|_{ds^{2}_{\delta,\epsilon},w_{\delta,\epsilon}h}.

Although
\|\beta\|_{ds^{2}_{\delta,\epsilon},w_{\delta,\epsilon}h}
may tend to infinity when
\epsilon\to 0
, a twisted Donelly-Fefferman type estimate (Theorem
6.2
) for
(ds^{2}_{\delta,\epsilon},w_{\delta,\epsilon}h)
shows that the constants
C_{\epsilon}
could be chosen in a way independent of
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
Hence (
1.9
) is the limit of (
1.10
). This solves the equation
\nabla\beta=-\alpha
.

To get a solution of
\nabla\beta=-\alpha
with compact supports, one may shrink
U_{\delta}
first and use the zero extension of the solution. This is valid due to the introducing of the weight
w_{\delta}
(Lemma
6.1
). As a consequence of the local vanishings there is a quasi-isomorphism
IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})\simeq_{\rm qis}\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h}
.

(6)

Make the quasi-isomorphism
IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})\simeq_{\rm qis}\mathscr{D}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h}
canonical. This can be done by Proposition
2.2
.

.

Recall that in

Remark 1.9
.

The choice of the deformation
(ds^{2}_{\delta,\epsilon},w_{\delta})
is deeply inspired by Ohsawa’s work
[
29
]
on isolated singularities. To perform the trick by Ohsawa to non-isolated singularities, one faces the difficulty that the intersection
(X\backslash X^{o})\cap\partial U_{\delta}
is always non-empty when

is non-isolated. The metric

on

is a complicated mixture of

and the modifier

. Hence it is difficult to study the local

-cohomology at

) instead of (

) and introduce the trick of two neighborhoods

,

is far away from

in the equation

.

The paper is organized as follows.

Section 2 and 3 are the review of some basic knowledge of the intersection complex, the
L^{2}
-cohomology and the
L^{2}
-de Rham complex, in order to fix notations. Some preliminary results are proved.

In Section 4 we establish the basic theory of Whitney stratified desingularization of a pair of complex spaces. Distinguished metrics and the notion of
0
-tame harmonic bundles are introduced. We also prove that they are stratified along the bimeromorphic fibering neighborhood associated to a given Whitney stratified desingularization. The weakly constructibility of the
L^{2}
-de Rham complex is then a consequence.

Section 5 is devoted to the geometry and the asymptotic behavior of the distinguished metric at an arbitrary complex singularity. The Whitney stratified desingularization is the basic tool. We fix the basic setting and constructions in §
5.1
and §
5.2
which will be used in the rest of the paper.

We prove the key vanishing theorem of this paper in Section 6.

Everything is combined together in Section 7 to give a bi-induction proof of the main theorem.

Section 8 contains some applications of the main theorem which have been mentioned in the introduction.

2.
Intersection Complex on Complex Spaces

Due to the nature of the
L^{2}
-cohomology, we need to consider constructible sheaves that may not be locally finitely generated. In this section we review the basic knowledge of such sheaves and prove some technical results for later usage.

Let
X
be a complex space of pure dimension
n
and
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
a dense Zariski open subset.
Throughout this paper, a stratification of the pair
(X,X^{o})
is a topological stratification

\displaystyle\emptyset=X_{-1}\subset X_{0}\subset\cdots\subset X_{n}=X

in the sense of
[
3
, §I-1.1]
, in addition that
X_{n-1}=X\backslash X^{o}
and
X_{i}
is a Zariski closed analytic subset for every
i=0,\dots,n
. Roughly speaking,
X_{i}\backslash X_{i-1}
is an
i
-dimensional complex submanifold of
X
for every
i
and the links along each
X_{i}\backslash X_{i-1}
are topologically isotrivial. A typical example of a stratification is the Whitney stratification (c.f.
[
23
]
).

A connected component of
\coprod_{i}X_{i}\backslash X_{i-1}
is called a stratum of
\{X_{i}\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a complex space and
A
a sheaf of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces on
X
.
A
is called a weakly local system on
X
if for every point
x\in X
there is a neighborhood
x\in U
such that
A|_{U}\simeq\mathbb{C}_{U}^{I}
, where
I
is an (possibly infinite) index set. In this case we say that
A
is a local system if the
I
s are finite sets.

A
is called (weakly) constructible if there is a stratification
\{X_{i}\}
such that
A|_{X_{i}\backslash X_{i-1}}
is a (weakly) local system for every
i
. In this situation we also say that
A
is (weakly) constructible with respect to
\{X_{i}\}
.

The category of weakly constructible sheaves on
X
is an abelian category.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a local system on
X^{o}
. Denote
U_{i}:=X\backslash X_{i}
and denote by
j_{i}:U_{i}\to U_{i-1}
(
U_{-1}:=X
) the inclusion. The intersection complex
IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})
is defined, in the derived category
D(X)
of sheaves of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces on
X
, by

(2.1)

\displaystyle IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}):=R^{<n}j_{0\ast}R^{<n-1}j_{1\ast}\cdots R^{<1}j_{n-1\ast}\mathbb{V}.

The right handside is independent of the stratification
\{X_{i}\}
up to quasi-isomorphisms and (
2.1
) gives a well defined bounded complex in
D(X)
.

In order to make the intersection complex a perverse sheaf (
[
2
]
), one usually uses a Tate twist
IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})[n]
instead of
IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})
. In this paper the difference between twisting causes no problem.

The following proposition is a mild generalization of Goresky-MacPherson-Deligne criterion for intersection complexes to weakly constructible complexes. It also shows that in the set of isomorphisms
{\rm Iso}_{D(X)}(\mathscr{A}^{\bullet},IC_{X})
, there is a canonical one, represented by a natural zigzag of quasi-isomorphisms between complexes, as long as a quasi-isomorphism
\iota_{0}:\mathbb{V}\to\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{X^{o}}
is fixed. A canonical isomorphism is necessary if we want to compare the Hodge structures on both sides of Theorem
1.4
(Conjecture
8.5
).

Proposition 2.2

(Goresky-MacPherson-Deligne criterion)
.

Let
X
be a complex space of pure dimension
n
and
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
a dense Zariski open subset. Let
\{X_{i}\}
be a stratification of
(X,X^{o})
and
\mathbb{V}
a local system on
X^{o}
.
A bounded complex of sheaves of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces
\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}
is isomorphic to the intersection complex
IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})
in
D(X)
if and only if the following conditions hold.

(1)

\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{X^{o}}
is quasi-isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}
.

(2)

For every
j
,
h^{j}(\mathscr{A}^{\bullet})
is weakly constructible with respect to
\{X_{i}\}
.

(3)

For each stratum
S
of
\{X_{i}\}
and every point
x\in S
, denote by
i_{x}:\{x\}\to X
the inclusion. Then

(2.2)

\displaystyle h^{k}(i_{x}^{\ast}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet})=0,\quad\forall k\geq n-\dim S

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle h^{k}(i_{x}^{!}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet})=0,\quad\forall k\leq n+\dim S.

Moreover, given a quasi-isomorphism of complexes

\iota_{0}:\mathbb{V}\to\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{X^{o}}
,
there is a canonical zigzag of quasi-isomorphisms between complexes (rather than morphisms in the derived category)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}\leftarrow\tau^{<n}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}\to\tau^{<n}Rj_{0\ast}\left(\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{0}}\right)\leftarrow\tau^{<n}Rj_{0\ast}\left(\tau^{<n-1}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{0}}\right)\to\tau^{<n}Rj_{0\ast}\tau^{<n-1}Rj_{1\ast}\left(\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{1}}\right)\leftarrow

\displaystyle\cdots\leftarrow\tau^{<n}Rj_{0\ast}\cdots\tau^{<{2}}Rj_{n-2\ast}\left(\tau^{<{1}}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{n-2}}\right)\to\tau^{<n}Rj_{0\ast}\cdots\tau^{<1}Rj_{n-1\ast}\left(\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{X^{o}}\right)\leftarrow IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})

Proof.

The first part is well known (c.f.
[
3
, chap. 5]
or
[
16
, Prop. 8.2.11]
). To prove the second part, it suffices to show that the natural diagram

\textstyle{\tau^{<n-d}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau^{<n-d}Rj_{d\ast}\left(\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d}}\right)}
\textstyle{\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}}

is a diagram of quasi-isomorphisms for every
d
. Consider the distinguished triangle

\displaystyle\tau^{<n-d}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}\to\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}\to\tau^{\geq n-d}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}\to\tau^{<n-d}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}[1].

By (
2.2
),
\tau^{\geq n-d}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}=0
. Hence the canonical map

\tau^{<n-d}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}\to\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}

is a quasi-isomorphism. Denote by
i:U_{d-1}\backslash U_{d}\to U_{d-1}
and
i_{x}:\{x\}\to U_{d-1}\backslash U_{d}
the closed immersions. Since
U_{d-1}\backslash U_{d}
is smooth with real dimension
2d
, we have
i_{x}^{!}\simeq i_{x}^{\ast}[-2d]
. Hence by (
2.3
), we see that

\displaystyle h^{k}((i_{x}i)^{!}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}})\simeq i^{\ast}_{x}h^{k-2d}(i^{!}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}})=0,\quad\forall k\leq n+d.

Equivalently
\tau^{\leq n-d}i_{!}i^{!}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}=0
.

This, together with the distinguished triangle

\displaystyle i_{!}i^{!}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}\to\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}\to Rj_{d\ast}\left(\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d}}\right)\to i_{!}i^{!}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}[1]

shows that the canonical map

\tau^{<n-d}\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d-1}}\to\tau^{<n-d}Rj_{d\ast}\left(\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}|_{U_{d}}\right)

is a quasi-isomorphism.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Condition (3) in Proposition
2.2
is equivalent to the following statement. For every
x\in X_{i}\backslash X_{i-1}
,

\displaystyle\varinjlim_{x\in U_{x}}\mathbb{H}^{k}(U_{x},\mathscr{A}^{\bullet})=0,\quad\forall k\geq n-i

\displaystyle\varprojlim_{x\in U_{x}}\mathbb{H}^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U_{x},\mathscr{A}^{\bullet})=0,\quad\forall k\leq n+i.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let

be a complex space and

a dense Zariski open subset. Let

be a local system on

and

an open covering. Then a complex of sheaves of

is quasi-isomorphic to

if and only if

-de Rham Complex and

3.1.
L^{2}
-cohomology

Let
(M,ds^{2}_{M})
be an orientable Riemannian stratified space with boundary and
\mathbb{V}
a complex local system on
M
. For this notion we always assume that
ds^{2}_{M}
can be extended smoothly to the boundary. Denote by
(\mathcal{V}:=\mathbb{V}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathscr{O}_{M},\nabla)
the associated flat bundle. Let
h
be a hermitian metric on
\mathcal{V}
which is not necessarily compatible with
\nabla
. Such a pair
(\mathbb{V},h)
is called a
hermitian local system
. Denote by
L_{(2)}^{k}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)
the space of
\mathbb{V}
-valued measurable
k
-forms which are square integrable with respect to
(ds^{2}_{M},h)
. Let

D^{k}_{\rm max}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h):=\left\{\alpha\in L_{(2)}^{k}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)\big|\nabla\alpha\in L_{(2)}^{k+1}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)\right\}

and

D^{\bullet}_{\rm max}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h):=D^{0}_{\rm max}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\to}}D^{1}_{\rm max}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\to}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\to}}D^{\dim_{\mathbb{R}}M}_{\rm max}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h).

Here
\nabla
are operators in the sense of distribution.

The
L^{2}
-cohomology of
(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)
with the maximal ideal boundary condition is defined by

H^{\ast}_{(2),\rm max}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h):=H^{\ast}\left(D^{\bullet}_{\rm max}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)\right).

Subscripts such as
ds^{2}_{M}
and
h
would be omitted when they are clear from the context.
\mathbb{V}
would be omitted when
\mathbb{V}\simeq\mathbb{C}
is the trivial local system.

There is another ideal boundary condition for the operator
\nabla
. Let

D^{k}_{\rm min}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h):=\left\{\alpha\in D^{k}_{\rm max}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)\big|\exists\alpha_{n}\in A^{k}_{\rm cpt}(M,\mathbb{V}),\alpha_{n}\to\alpha,\nabla\alpha_{n}\to\nabla\alpha\right\}

and

D_{\rm min}^{\bullet}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h):=D^{0}_{\rm min}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\to}}D^{1}_{\rm min}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\to}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\to}}D^{\dim_{\mathbb{R}}M}_{\rm min}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h).

The
L^{2}
-cohomology of
(M,ds^{2}_{M})
with minimal ideal boundary condition is defined by

H^{\ast}_{(2),\rm min}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h):=H^{\ast}\left(D^{\bullet}_{\rm min}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)\right).

To distinguish the ideal boundary conditions we denote by
\nabla_{\rm max}
and
\nabla_{\rm min}
the unbounded operators with domains
{\rm Dom}\nabla_{\rm max}:=\bigoplus_{i}D^{i}_{\rm max}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)
and
{\rm Dom}\nabla_{\rm min}:=\bigoplus_{i}D^{i}_{\rm min}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)
respectively.
These two ideal boundary conditions coincide when
M
is a manifold without boundary and
ds^{2}_{M}
is complete (
[
44
, Theorem 7.2]
). Hence there is a canonical isomorphism

\displaystyle H^{k}_{(2),\rm min}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)\simeq H^{k}_{(2),\rm max}(M,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{M},h)

whenever
(M,ds^{2}_{M})
is a complete Riemannian manifold (without boundary). In this case we denote the
L^{2}
-cohomology by
H^{\ast}_{(2)}(-)
for short.

Generally these two kinds of
L^{2}
-cohomologies are related by the
L^{2}
-Poincaré duality.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(M,ds^{2}_{M})
and
(N,ds^{2}_{N})
be Riemannian manifolds with boundaries. Let
(\mathbb{V}_{1},h_{1})
and
(\mathbb{V}_{2},h_{2})

and

\displaystyle H^{k}_{(2),\rm min}(X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{X},h)\simeq\bigoplus_{i+j=k}H^{i}_{(2),\rm min}(M,\mathbb{V}_{1};ds^{2}_{M},h_{1})\otimes H^{j}_{(2),\rm min}(N,\mathbb{V}_{2};ds^{2}_{N},h_{2}).

The proof is originated by
[
45
, Theorem 2.29]
with a mild modification. For the convenience of readers we give a sketched proof.

Since
\bigoplus_{i+j=k}A_{\rm cpt}^{i}(M,\mathbb{V}_{1})\otimes A_{\rm cpt}^{j}(N,\mathbb{V}_{2})
is dense in

under the graph norm

\displaystyle D^{k}_{\rm min}(X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{X},h)\simeq\bigoplus_{i+j=k}D^{i}_{\rm min}(M,\mathbb{V}_{1};ds^{2}_{M},h_{1})\widehat{\otimes}D^{j}_{\rm min}(N,\mathbb{V}_{2};ds^{2}_{N},h_{2}).

Definition 4.10
.

4.4.

Let

be a complex space of pure dimension

a dense Zariski open subset.
Let

be a good desingularization of

in the sense that

•

\widetilde{X}
is a complex manifold,
\pi
is a projective holomorphic map and
\pi|_{\pi^{-1}X^{o}}:\pi^{-1}X^{o}\to X^{o}

•

The (reduced) exceptional loci

is a simple normal crossing divisor of

with finite components.

Let
E=\bigcup_{i\in I}E_{i}
be the irreducible decomposition. Since
\pi
is projective, there are constants
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
i\in I
such that
\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}E_{i})
is
\pi
-positive, i.e. locally there is a hermitian metric

such that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}(\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}E_{i}))+\pi^{\ast}\omega_{0}

is positive definite on
\widetilde{X}
for some hermitian metric
\omega_{0}
on
X
.

For each
i\in I
, let
h_{i}
be a hermitian metric on
\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(E_{i})
such that

. Let

be the defining section of

and denote

for short.

By perturbing the vector
(a_{i})_{i\in I}
slightly in general directions and multiplying the common denominators we get integers

,
i\in I
,
j=1,\dots,m
so that

(1)

After a possible shrinking of

(\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-\sum_{i\in I}a_{ij}E_{i}),\prod_{i\in I}h^{a_{ij}\ast}_{i})

(2)

{\rm rank}(a_{ij})_{i\in I,j\in J}=m

.

The distinguished potential is defined by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\pi}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\log\left(-\log\prod_{i\in I}|s_{i}|^{2a_{ij}}\right)}.

Denote

[-,-]

[T_{1},T_{2}]=T_{1}T_{2}-(-1)^{\deg T_{1}\deg T_{2}}T_{2}T_{1}.

\displaystyle[\Lambda_{\omega},\nabla]=\sqrt{-1}(\nabla^{c}_{h})^{\ast},\quad[\Lambda_{\omega},\nabla^{c}_{h}]=-\sqrt{-1}\nabla^{\ast}_{h}.

Here
\omega

and

\displaystyle\Theta_{h}(\nabla):=[\nabla,\nabla^{c}_{h}]=\nabla\nabla^{c}_{h}+\nabla^{c}_{h}\nabla

\displaystyle\Delta_{\nabla}=\nabla\nabla^{\ast}_{h}+\nabla^{\ast}_{h}\nabla,\quad\Delta_{\nabla^{c}_{h}}={\nabla^{c}_{h}}{\nabla^{c}_{h}}^{\ast}+{\nabla^{c}_{h}}^{\ast}{\nabla^{c}_{h}}.

We have

Lemma 4.17

(Kodaira-Bochner formula for

\displaystyle\Delta_{\nabla}=\Delta_{\nabla^{c}_{h}}+[\Lambda_{\omega},\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}(\nabla)].

Proof.

\displaystyle\Delta_{\nabla}-\Delta_{\nabla^{c}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}\nabla[\Lambda_{\omega},\nabla^{c}_{h}]+\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\omega},\nabla^{c}_{h}]\nabla+\sqrt{-1}\nabla^{c}_{h}[\Lambda_{\omega},\nabla]+\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\omega},\nabla]\nabla^{c}_{h}\quad(\ref{align_Kahler_id2})

\displaystyle=[\Lambda_{\omega},\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}(\nabla)].

Lemma 4.18

\Theta_{e^{\varphi}h}(\nabla)=\Theta_{h}(\nabla)+dd^{c}\varphi

Proof.

For every

4.5.2.

The

Definition 4.20
.

Let
X
be a complex space and

(4.23)

\displaystyle(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\|f_{i}\|^{2})^{\epsilon}\lesssim|v|_{h}\lesssim(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\|f_{i}\|^{2})^{-\epsilon},\quad\forall\epsilon>0

for every (multivalued) flat section

is a local generator of the ideal sheaf defining

The
0
-tameness is independent of the choice of the local generators

\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a desingularization of

so that the preimage of

is supported on a simple normal crossing divisor

4.23
) is equivalent to the estimate

(4.24)

\displaystyle\|z_{1}\cdots z_{r}\|^{\epsilon}\lesssim|v|_{h}\lesssim\|z_{1}\cdots z_{r}\|^{-\epsilon},\quad\forall\epsilon>0

where
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
is an arbitrary holomorphic local coordinate of
\widetilde{X}
such that
E=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
.

One has a more general notion of tame harmonic bundles, with (
4.23

\displaystyle|v|_{h}\lesssim(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\|f_{i}\|^{2})^{-b}

for some
b>0
. Or equivalently, the eigenvalues of the higgs field are multivalued holomorphic one-forms with poles along (some resolution of)

with order at most 1. For a tame harmonic bundle
(V,h)
on
(X,X^{o})
and a holomorphic disc

which satisfies the estimate
|v|_{\gamma^{\ast}h}\lesssim\|w\|^{-b}

for some

\Delta
. According to

there is a canonical filtration of bundles

(the associated vector bundle of

,

. The

is the only object that matters in the filtration for both
(\gamma^{\ast}\mathbb{V},\gamma^{\ast}h)
and

8.
Applications: Kähler Package on the Intersection cohomology

The
L^{2}
-representation of Hodge modules provides a way to study Hodge modules by using differential geometrical methods as well as
L^{2}
-techniques. In this section we use Theorem
1.4
to give a differential geometrical proof of the Hodge-Lefschetz package of a pure Hodge module, or more generally a
0
-tame harmonic bundle. This is one of the motivations why Cheeger-Goresky-MacPherson post Conjecture
1.3
.

8.1.
Poincaré duality and Verdier duality

A typical property of
IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})
is its Verdier duality, i.e.

(8.1)

\displaystyle IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})\simeq\mathbb{D}(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}^{\ast})).

This induces the global duality (Poincaré duality)

\displaystyle IH^{2\dim X-k}(X,\mathbb{V}^{\ast})\simeq\left(IH^{k}(X,\mathbb{V})\right)^{\ast},\quad\forall k

in the case when
X
is compact.
Here
\mathbb{D}
is the Verdier dual operator on
D(X)
. Theorem
1.4
shows that these dualities could be realized by integration as in the classical case.

Locally at every point
x\in X
, (
8.1
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\varinjlim_{x\in U}\mathbb{H}^{2\dim X-k}(U,IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})))\simeq\varinjlim_{x\in U}\left(\mathbb{H}^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U,IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}^{\ast}))\right)^{\ast},\quad\forall k

where
U
ranges over all open neighborhoods of
x
. The following theorems could be viewed as differential geometrical proofs of the local and global dualities of intersection complexes.

Theorem 8.1

(Locally Duality)
.

Notations as in Theorem
1.4
. Let
x\in X
be a point. Then there is a duality

\displaystyle\varinjlim_{x\in U}\mathbb{H}^{2\dim X-k}(U,\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h})\simeq\varinjlim_{x\in U}\left(\mathbb{H}^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U,\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V}^{\ast};ds^{2},h^{\ast}})\right)^{\ast},\quad\forall k

where
U
ranges over all open neighborhoods of
x
. Both sides of the isomorphism are finite dimensional. Moreover, the duality is induced by the limits of the pairings

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{2\dim X-k}(U,\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h})\times\mathbb{H}^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U,\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V}^{\ast};ds^{2},h^{\ast}})\to\mathbb{C},\quad\forall k,

([\alpha],[\beta])\mapsto\int_{U\cap X^{o}}\alpha\wedge\beta.

Proof.

Since the problem is local, we assume that
x=0\in X\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Let
U_{\delta}=\left\{z\in X\big|\|z\|<\delta\right\}
and
U_{\delta}^{o}=X^{o}\cap U_{\delta}
. By Proposition
5.6
the natural maps

\displaystyle\left(H^{k}_{(2),\rm min}(U^{o}_{\delta},\mathbb{V}^{\ast};ds^{2},h^{\ast})\right)^{\ast}\to\left(\mathbb{H}^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U_{\delta},\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V}^{\ast};ds^{2},h^{\ast}})\right)^{\ast}\to\varinjlim_{x\in U}\left(\mathbb{H}^{k}_{\rm cpt}(U,\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V}^{\ast};ds^{2},h^{\ast}})\right)^{\ast}

are isomorphisms for every
k
and every
0<\delta\ll 1
. Hence by Theorem
1.4
,
H^{k}_{(2),\rm min}(U^{o}_{\delta},\mathbb{V}^{\ast};ds^{2},h^{\ast})
is finite dimensional for every
k
. Now
the theorem is a consequence of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Theorem 8.2

(Poincaré Duality)
.

Notations as in Theorem
1.4
. Assume that
X
is a compact complex space. Then for every
k
there is a perfect pairing

\displaystyle H^{2\dim X-k}_{(2)}(X^{o},\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h)\times H^{k}_{(2)}(X^{o},\mathbb{V}^{\ast};ds^{2},h^{\ast})\to\mathbb{C},\quad([\alpha],[\beta])\mapsto\int_{X^{o}}\alpha\wedge\beta.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.4
, both
L^{2}
-cohomologies
H^{\ast}_{(2)}(X^{o},\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h)
and
H^{\ast}_{(2)}(X^{o},\mathbb{V}^{\ast};ds^{2},h^{\ast})
are of finite dimension. Now the theorem follows from Proposition
3.1
.
∎

8.2.
Lefschetz package

By the Lefschetz package we mean the Hard Lefschetz and the Lefschetz decomposition. Due to the classical Hodge theory this package holds for the de Rham cohomology of every compact Kähler manifold. The Lefschetz package for semisimple local systems is established by Simpson
[
41
]
. More generally, Kashiwara
[
21
]
conjectures that the Lefschetz package should hold for any semisimple perverse sheaf over a projective algebraic variety
3
3

3

The conjecture of Kashiwara is even more general. It asserts that the Lefschetz package should be true in the relative case, for semisimple holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules with possibly irregular singularities. For
\mathscr{D}
-modules with regular singularities over a quasi-projective algebraic variety, Kashiwara’s conjecture is proved by C. Sabbah
[
31
]
in lisse case and by T. Mochizuki
[
25
,
26
]
and V. Drinfeld
[
12
]
in general.
. In this subsection we prove this conjecture over a compact Kähler base by using the
L^{2}
-representation of the intersection complex.

Let
X
be a complex space. Define
\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}_{X}
to be the complex of piecewise
C^{\infty}
forms which can be extended smoothly over some neighborhood of a local embedding
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
. I.e. locally
\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}_{X}:=\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C}^{N}}|_{X}
. Since the restriction of sheaves is an exact functor,
\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}_{X}
is a complex of fine sheaves and the canonical inclusion

\iota:\mathbb{C}_{X}\to\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}_{X}
is a quasi-isomorphism. We say a closed
(1,1)
-form
\omega\in\mathscr{A}^{2}_{X}
is positive if
\omega
is the Kähler form of some hermitian metric on
X
, i.e. locally
\omega
is the restriction of a Kähler form through a local embedding
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
. A cohomology class
\xi\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
is a Kähler class if it is represented by some closed positive
(1,1)
-form. A typical example of positive forms is
\omega_{FS}|_{X}
where
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a projective variety and
\omega_{FS}
is the Kähler class associated to the Fubini-Study metric.

Let
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
be a dense Zariski open subset and
(\mathbb{V},h)
a
0
-tame harmonic bundle on
(X,X^{o})
.
Let
ds^{2}
be a distinguished metric associated to
(X,X^{o})
. Since every form in
\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}_{X}
has bounded length with respect to
ds^{2}
(Lemma
3.5
), there is a well defined wedge product

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}_{X}\otimes\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h}\to\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h}.

This makes
\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h}
a differential graded
\mathscr{A}^{\bullet}_{X}
-module.

Let
\tau:IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})\to\mathscr{D}^{\bullet}_{X,\mathbb{V};ds^{2},h}
be the quasi-isomorphism in Theorem
1.4
. Since
\tau
is
\mathbb{C}_{X}
-linear, the diagram

\textstyle{\mathbb{C}_{X}\otimes IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota\otimes\tau}
\textstyle{IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Theorem 8.3
.

Let

a dense Zariski open subset. Let
\mathbb{V}
be a local system admitting a

Hard Lefschetz:

The morphism

Lefschetz Decomposition:

Define the primitive cohomology

\displaystyle IP^{n-k}(X,\mathbb{V}):={\rm Ker}\left(L_{\omega}^{k+1}:IH^{n-k}(X,\mathbb{V})\to IH^{n+k+2}(X,\mathbb{V})\right),\quad\forall k\geq 0.

\displaystyle IH^{k}(X,\mathbb{V})=\bigoplus_{i}L_{\omega}^{i}IP^{k-2i}(X,\mathbb{V}).

Let

associated to some Whitney stratified desingularization

(Lemma

\displaystyle IH^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{V})\simeq H^{\ast}_{(2)}(X^{o},\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{\pi},h).

\displaystyle L_{\omega_{\pi}}:H^{\ast}_{(2)}(X^{o},\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{\pi},h)\to H^{\ast+2}_{(2)}(X^{o},\mathbb{V};ds^{2}_{\pi},h),\quad[\alpha]\mapsto[\omega_{\pi}\wedge\alpha].

Since the Lefschetz operator

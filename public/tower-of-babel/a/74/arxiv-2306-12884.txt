Decays of 1 − ⁣ + 1^{-+} charmoniumlike hybrid
Chunjiang Shi, Ying Chen, Ming Gong, Xiangyu Jiang, Zhaofeng Liu, Wei Sun

Abstract
By extracting the transition amplitudes, we give the first lattice QCD prediction of the two-body decay partial widths of the 1 − ⁣ + 1^{-+} charmoniumlike hybrid η c ​ 1 \eta_{c1} . Given the calculated mass value m η c ​ 1 = 4.329 ​ ( 36 ) m_{\eta_{c1}}=4.329(36) GeV, the η c ​ 1 \eta_{c1} decay is dominated by the open charm modes D 1 ​ D ¯ D_{1}\bar{D} , D ∗ ​ D ¯ D^{*}\bar{D} and D ∗ ​ D ¯ ∗ D^{*}\bar{D}^{*} with partial widths of 258 ​ ( 133 ) 258(133) MeV, 88 ​ ( 18 ) 88(18) MeV and 150 ​ ( 118 ) 150(118) MeV, respectively. The coupling of η c ​ 1 \eta_{c1} to χ c ​ 1 \chi_{c1} plus a flavor singlet pseudoscalar is not small, but χ c ​ 1 ​ η \chi_{c1}\eta decay is suppressed by the small η − η ′ \eta-\eta^{\prime} mixing angle. The partial width of η c ​ 1 → η c ​ η ′ \eta_{c1}\to\eta_{c}\eta^{\prime} is estimated to be around 1 MeV. We suggest experiments to search for η c ​ 1 \eta_{c1} in the P P -wave D ∗ ​ D ¯ D^{*}\bar{D} and D ∗ ​ D ¯ ∗ D^{*}\bar{D}^{*} systems. Especially, the polarization of D ∗ ​ D ¯ ∗ D^{*}\bar{D}^{*} can be used to distinguish the 1 − ⁣ + 1^{-+} product (total spin S = 1 S=1 ) from 1 − ⁣ − 1^{--} products ( S = 0 S=0 ).



I
Introduction

Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) permits the existence of hybrid mesons that are composed of both quarks and gluons, among which the
J^{PC}=1^{-+}
states are most intriguing since this quantum number is prohibited for
q\bar{q}
states. Up to now there are three experimental candidates for
I^{G}J^{PC}=1^{-}1^{-+}
light hybrid mesons, namely,
\pi_{1}(1400)

Alde et al. 1988
,
\pi_{1}(1600)

Adams et al. 1998
;
Aghasyan et al. 2018
;
Rodas et al. 2019
and
\pi_{1}(2105)

Adams et al. 1998
(details can be found in the latest review
Chen et al. 2023
and the references therein). Recently, the BESIII collaboration reported the first observation of a
I^{G}J^{PC}=0^{+}1^{-+}
structure
\eta_{1}(1855)
in the
\eta\eta^{\prime}
system through the partial wave analysis of the
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta\eta^{\prime}
process
Ablikim et al. 2022a
;
Ablikim et al. 2022b
, which is a candidate for light isoscalar
1^{-+}
hybrid
Chen et al. 2022
;
Qiu and Zhao 2022
;
Shastry et al. 2022
. It is natural to anticipate the existence of the
1^{-+}
charmoniumlike hybrid (named
\eta_{c1}
, whose mass is predicted to be
m_{\eta_{c1}}\sim 4.2-4.4
GeV by lattice QCD studies
Liao and Manke 2002
;
Mei and Luo 2003
;
Dudek and Rrapaj 2008
;
Dudek et al. 2009a
;
Liu et al. 2012
;
Yang et al. 2012
;
Ma et al. 2021
;
Brambilla et al. 2020
. In order to identify
\eta_{c1}
experimentally, its decay properties is also very demanding. Although the flux-tube model gives a selection rule that a hybrid meson decays preferably into final states of an
S
-wave meson and a
P
-wave meson
Isgur and Paton 1985
;
Close and Page 1995
;
Page 1997
;
Page et al. 1999
, we would like to perform an
ab initio
investigation of hybrid decays through the lattice QCD approach.

The state-of-art lattice QCD approach to study strong decays of hadrons is the Lüscher method
Lüscher 1986
;
Lüscher 1991
;
Luscher 1991
and its generalization that takes the coupled channel effects into account (see the review articles Ref.
Briceño et al. 2018
;
Mai et al. 2023
and the references therein).
For
\eta_{c1}
with a mass as high as roughly 4.3 GeV, many open-charm and closed-charm decay channels are opened.
In order to tackle the complicated coupled channel effects, the related study using the (generalized) Lüscher method requires quite a lot of finite volume energy levels to be determined as precisely as possible. The calculation should be carried out on multiple lattice volumes and in different moving frames. This is numerically and computationally very challenging. The computational complication of this sort of studies can be learnt by the recent lattice QCD studies by the Hadron Spectrum Collaboration on the strong decays of the light hybrid
\pi_{1}

Woss et al. 2021
and on the charmonium-like systems
Wilson et al. 2023a
;
Wilson et al. 2023b
.

In order to take a quick look at the decay properties of
\eta_{c1}
, we adopt the method proposed by Michael and McNeile (M&M)
McNeile et al. 2002
;
McNeile and Michael 2003
to calculate the tree-level transition amplitudes for
\eta_{c1}
decays, from which the effective couplings can be estimated. Previously lattice studies using the M&M method gave reasonable results for the decays of some mesons
McNeile et al. 2004
;
Michael 2006
;
McNeile and Michael 2006a
;
McNeile and Michael 2006b
;
Hart et al. 2006
;
Michael 2007
. The M&M method is also applied to the study of the decay process
\Delta\to N\pi

Alexandrou et al. 2013
;
Alexandrou et al. 2016
and the results are consistent with the those from the Lüscher method
Andersen et al. 2018
;
Silvi et al. 2021
;
Morningstar et al. 2022
and physical values
Pascalutsa and Vanderhaeghen 2006
;
Hemmert et al. 1995
.
In Ref.
Bali et al. 2016
, the M&M method is applied to the decay process
\rho\to\pi\pi
and obtains the effective coupling constants
g_{\rho\pi\pi}
ranging from 5.2 to 8.4 (from different lattice volumes and different
\pi\pi
kinetic configurations), which is compatible with the value
g_{\rho\pi\pi}\sim 6.0
that is derived by the width of
\rho
meson
Workman et al. 2022
and is determined by lattice QCD studies using the Lüscher method
Aoki et al. 2007
;
Frison et al. 2010
;
Lang et al. 2011
;
Aoki et al. 2011
;
Pelissier and Alexandru 2013
;
Dudek et al. 2013
;
Fahy et al. 2015
;
Wilson et al. 2015
;
Bali et al. 2016
;
Bulava et al. 2016
;
Guo and Alexandru 2016
;
Fu and Wang 2016
;
Hu et al. 2016
;
Alexandrou et al. 2017
;
Hu et al. 2017
;
Andersen et al. 2019
;
Erben et al. 2020
;
Werner et al. 2020
;
Fischer et al. 2021
;
Akahoshi et al. 2021
;
Mai et al. 2023
. Obviously, the discrepancy of
g_{\rho\pi\pi}
obtained by the M&M method from the physical value can be as large as 40-50%, and serves as an estimate of the systematic uncertainty of the M&M method.
With this sizeable uncertainty in mind, we think the results obtained from the M&M method are helpful and informative for us to learn qualitatively the decay properties of
\eta_{c1}
from first principles.

This article is organized as follows. We introduce the theoretical formalism of this study in Sect.
II
, which includes the explicit expressions of the effective Lagrangian for the
\eta_{c1}
decays and the basic logic of the M&M method. The numerical procedures and results are presented in Sec.
III
(for the convenience of the readers who are interested only in the physical results, we leave the very details of the lattice treatments, such as the operator construction, the derivation of the decay amplitudes and the data analysis, to the Appendix part), Section
IV
is devoted to the discussions and the physical implications of our results. Sect.
V
is the summary of this work.

II
Formalism

The two-body decay
\eta_{c1}\to AB
includes closed-charm decays
\chi_{c1}\eta(\eta^{\prime})
,
\eta_{c}\eta(\eta^{\prime})
,
J/\psi\omega(\phi)
, and open-charm modes
D_{(s)}^{*}\bar{D}_{(s)}
,
D_{(s)}^{*}\bar{D}_{(s)}^{*}
,
D\bar{D}_{1}(2420)
, etc. The effective Lagrangian for closed-charm decays can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{cc}}\sim

\displaystyle-g_{\chi\eta}m_{\eta_{c1}}H_{\mu}A^{\mu}\eta-ig_{\eta_{c}\eta}H_{\mu}\eta_{c}\overleftrightarrow{\partial}^{\mu}\eta

(1)

\displaystyle+iH_{\mu}\left(g\psi_{\nu}\partial^{\nu}\omega^{\mu}+g^{\prime}\omega_{\nu}\partial^{\nu}\psi^{\mu}+g_{0}\psi_{\nu}\overleftrightarrow{\partial}^{\mu}\omega^{\nu}\right),

where
\overleftrightarrow{\partial}
represents
\overleftarrow{\partial}-\overrightarrow{\partial}
, and the fields
H_{\mu}
,
\chi_{\mu}
,
\eta_{c}
,
\eta
,
\psi_{\mu}
and
\omega_{\mu}
are for
\eta_{c1}
,
\chi_{c1}
,
\eta_{c}
,
\eta(\eta^{\prime})
,
J/\psi
and
\omega
mesons, respectively.

For open-charm decays, the quantum numbers
I^{G}J^{PC}=0^{+}1^{-+}
of
\eta_{c1}
constrain the flavor structure of the
D\bar{D^{\prime}}
(
D^{\prime}
here refers to either
D^{*}
and
D_{1}
) final state completely. We take the conventions
\mathcal{C}|D\rangle=+|\bar{D}\rangle
,
\mathcal{C}|D^{*}\rangle=-|\bar{D}^{*}\rangle
,
\mathcal{C}|D_{1}\rangle=+|\bar{D}_{1}\rangle
for the charge conjugate transformation (
\mathcal{C}
) of charm mesons.
When the isospin doublet fields
D_{1\mu}
,
D_{\mu}
and
D
are introduced for
D_{1}
,
D^{*}
and
D
mesons of the flavor wave functions
(|c\bar{d}\rangle,-|c\bar{u}\rangle)^{T}
, the effective Lagrangian for open-charm decays reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{oc}}

\displaystyle\sim g_{D_{1}D}m_{\eta_{c1}}H_{\mu}\frac{1}{2}\left(D_{1}^{\mu,\dagger}D+D^{\dagger}D_{1}^{\mu}\right)

(2)

\displaystyle+g_{D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}}H^{\mu}\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\left(D^{\nu,\dagger}\partial_{\nu}D_{\mu}+\partial_{\nu}D^{\dagger}_{\mu}D^{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{g_{D^{*}\bar{D}}}{m_{\eta_{c1}}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(\partial_{\mu}H_{\nu})\frac{1}{2}\left[(\partial_{\rho}D_{\sigma}^{\dagger})D-D^{\dagger}(\partial_{\rho}D_{\sigma})\right].

So the prediction of partial decay widths depends on a reliable determination of the effective couplings in the effective Lagrangian.

According to the effective Lagrangian Eq. (
1
) and Eq. (
2
), the tree-level transition amplitudes

x_{AB}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})\lambda}\approx\langle AB;(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}),\vec{k}|\hat{H}|\eta_{c1},\lambda\rangle

in the rest frame of
\eta_{c1}
are expressed explicitly as

\displaystyle x_{AP}^{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle g}_{AP}m_{\eta_{c1}}\vec{\epsilon}_{\lambda}(\vec{0})\cdot\vec{{\epsilon}}^{~*}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\vec{k}),

(3)

\displaystyle x_{PP}^{\lambda}=

\displaystyle 2{g}_{PP}\vec{{\epsilon}}_{\lambda}(\vec{0})\cdot\vec{k},

\displaystyle x_{D^{*}\bar{D}}^{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle g}_{D^{*}\bar{D}}\vec{\epsilon}_{\lambda}(\vec{0})\cdot(\vec{\epsilon}^{~*}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\vec{k})\times\vec{k}),

\displaystyle x_{D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}}^{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}\lambda}=

\displaystyle 2{g}_{D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}}\vec{\epsilon}_{\lambda}(\vec{0})\cdot\left(\vec{k}\times\left[\vec{\epsilon}^{~*}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\vec{k})\times\vec{\epsilon}^{~*}_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}(-\vec{k})\right]\right),

where
\epsilon^{\mu}_{\lambda}(\vec{0})
,
\epsilon_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\vec{k})
and
\epsilon_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}(-\vec{k})
are the polarization vectors
of
\eta_{c1}
,
A
(if a (an axial) vector) and
B
(if a (an axial) vector). The
J/\psi\omega
decay is strongly suppressed due to the OZI rule and will not be discussed in depth in this Letter, so the explicit expression of
x_{J/\psi\omega}
is omitted here.

On the lattice, the transition amplitude
x_{AB}
for
\eta_{c1}\to AB
appears in the temporal transfer matrix
\hat{T}=e^{-a_{t}\hat{H}}
, where
a_{t}
is the lattice spacing in the time direction. If the coupled channel effect is tentatively ignored,
\hat{T}
is parameterized as

\hat{T}=e^{-a_{t}\hat{H}}=e^{-a_{t}\bar{E}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{-a_{t}\Delta/2}&a_{t}x_{AB}\\
a_{t}x_{AB}&e^{+a_{t}\Delta/2}\end{array}\right)

(4)

in the Hilbert space spanned by
|\eta_{c1}\rangle
and
|AB\rangle
(normalized as
\langle\eta_{c1}|\eta_{c1}\rangle=1
and
\langle{AB}|{AB}\rangle=1
), where
\bar{E}=(m_{\eta_{c1}}+E_{AB})/2
and
\Delta=m_{\eta_{c1}}-E_{AB}
have been defined with
E_{AB}=E_{A}+E_{B}
. It is easy to show that, when each single particle state
|X\rangle
is normalized as
\langle X|X\rangle=2E_{X}L^{3}
with
L^{3}
being the spatial volume of the lattice and
E_{X}
being the energy of
|X\rangle
, then
x_{AB}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})\lambda}
is encoded in the following ratio function
McNeile et al. 2004
;
Michael 2006
;
Alexandrou et al. 2016
,

\displaystyle R_{AB}(\vec{k},t)=

\displaystyle\frac{C^{33}_{AB,\eta_{c1}}(\vec{k},t)}{\sqrt{[C_{AA}(\vec{k},t)C_{BB}(-\vec{k},t)]^{33}C^{33}_{\eta_{c1},\eta_{c1}}(\vec{0},t)}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle t\left(1+\frac{1}{24}(a_{t}\Delta t)^{2}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{8L^{3}m_{\eta_{c1}}E_{A}E_{B}}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}\frac{a_{t}x^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})\lambda}_{AB}[\epsilon_{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}^{3}(\vec{k},AB)\epsilon^{3,*}_{\lambda}(\vec{0})]}{\sqrt{\mathcal{P}_{A}(\vec{k})\mathcal{P}_{B}(-\vec{k})\mathcal{P}_{\eta_{c1}}^{33}(\vec{0})}},

(5)

for
a_{t}\Delta\ll 1
, where
C^{ii}_{AB,\eta_{c1}}(\vec{k},t)
is the correlation function of operators
\mathcal{O}^{i}_{\eta_{c1}}
and
\mathcal{O}^{i}_{AB}(\vec{k})
that create (or annihilate) the states
\eta_{c1}
and
A(\vec{k})B(-\vec{k})
respectively, and
C_{XX}(\vec{p},t)
is the correlation function for
X
moving with a momentum
\vec{p}
. Here
X
refers to
\eta_{c1}
,
A
or
B
. The polarization of the
AB
state,
\epsilon_{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}^{\mu}(\vec{k},AB)
, depends on the wave functions of
A
(either 1 or
\epsilon_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\vec{k})
) and
B
(either 1 or
\epsilon_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}(-\vec{k})
), as well as the relative momentum
\vec{k}
(see Appendix.
.2
for the explicit expressions). The quantity
\mathcal{P}_{X}(\vec{k})
comes from the completeness of polarization vectors of
X
and takes the value of unity for a pseudoscalar and
\mathcal{P}_{X}^{ij}=\delta^{ij}+\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{m_{X}^{2}}
for a vector. Note that the major assumptions in the derivation of Eq. (
II
) are

\langle 0|\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c1}}|AB\rangle\approx 0~~\langle 0|\mathcal{O}_{AB}|\eta_{c1}\rangle\approx 0.

(6)

This can be checked by looking at the deviation of
R_{AB}(\vec{0},t=0)
from zero (see Appdenix D).

Obviously,
R_{AB}(t)
in Eq. (
II
) is linear in the time range where excited state contamination is negligible and
a_{t}\Delta t
is sufficiently small. This allows the transition matrix
a_{t}x_{AB}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})\lambda}
, and thereby the effective coupling
g_{AB}
, to be extracted from the slope of
R_{AB}(t)
.

III
Numerical details

The calculations are performed on two
N_{f}=2
gauge ensembles at the same pion mass
m_{\pi}\approx 350
MeV, the same temporal lattice spacing
a_{t}
, but with different lattice sizes, namely,
N_{s}^{3}\times N_{t}=16^{3}\times 128
and
24^{3}\times 192
(labeled as L16 and L24, respectively).
Both ensembles are generated on anisotropic lattice with the same anisotropic parameter
\xi=a_{s}/a_{t}=5.3
(
a_{s}
is the lattice spacing in spatial directions)
Jiang et al. 2022
.
The parameters of the gauge ensembles are collected in Table
1
.
We adopt the tadpole improved gauge action
Morningstar and Peardon 1997
;
Chen et al. 2006
for gluons and the tadpole improved anisotropic clover fermion action in Ref.
Zhang and Liu 2001
;
Su et al. 2006
;
Meng et al. 2009
for light quarks.
The valence charm quark mass parameters are set by
(m_{\eta_{c}}+3m_{J/\psi})/4=3069
MeV.
Within the framework of the distillation method, the perambulators of light
u,d
quarks and charm quark are calculated with the time sources running over all the time slices and the number of eigenvectors spanning the Laplacian subspace are chosen to be
N_{V}=70
for L16 and
N_{V}=160
for L24. Since all the mesons involved in
\eta_{c1}
decays are the ground states in specific
J^{PC}
channels, and the distillation method provides an inherent smearing scheme for quark fields, we use only the simplest generic quark bilinear operators for all the mesons.
Table
2
lists the masses of mesons involved in this calculation. Since we work in the
N_{f}=2
QCD, we label the flavor singlet (isoscalar) pseudoscalar
meson by
\eta_{(2)}
to differentiate it from the physical states
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
when we present the corresponding lattice results. We keep the name
\omega
for the isoscalar vector meson in our lattice setup by considering that the physical
\omega
meson is nearly a pure
(u\bar{u}+d\bar{d})
meson.

The key task is to calculate the correlation function
C^{ii}_{AB,\eta_{c1}}(\vec{k},t)
, where both operators
\mathcal{O}^{i}_{\eta_{c1}}
and
\mathcal{O}^{i}_{AB}(\vec{k})
belong to the
T_{1}
representation of the octahedral group
O
on the lattice.
We use the partial-wave method
Feng et al. 2011
;
Wallace 2015
;
Prelovsek et al. 2017
to construct
\mathcal{O}^{i}_{AB}(\vec{k})
.

\mathcal{O}^{i}_{\eta_{c1}}
takes the generic quark bilinear form
\epsilon_{ijk}\bar{c}\gamma_{j}B_{k}c
with
B_{k}
being the chromomagnetic field strength, following the operator construction in Ref.
Basak et al. 2005
;
Dudek et al. 2008
;
Dudek et al. 2009b
;
Dudek et al. 2010
;
Thomas et al. 2012
.
The operator
\mathcal{O}^{i}_{AB}(\vec{k})
is built from the single particle operators
\mathcal{O}_{A}(\vec{k})
and
\mathcal{O}_{B}(-\vec{k})
through a specific combination
\left[\mathcal{O}_{A}(\vec{k})\circ\mathcal{O}_{B}(-\vec{k})\right]^{i}
(the flavor structure is also considered for open-charm modes).
See the Appendix.
.1
for the details.
The schematic quark diagrams for the two-body
\eta_{c1}
decays are shown in Fig.
1
where the left panel is for the open-charm decays, in which the original gluon in
\eta_{c1}
splits into a light quark anti-quark pair

, which reorganizes with the
c\bar{c}
pair to form an open-charm final state.
The right panel depicts the process of the closed-charm decay. Along with the
c\bar{c}
pair coupling to a final state charmonium, the original gluon and the gluon(s) emitted by the charm quark pair generate a flavor singlet meson (note that the mesons
\eta(\eta^{\prime}),\omega(\phi)
have flavor singlet components).
Obviously, both diagrams have annihilation diagrams of light quarks which can be readily tackled by the distillation method.

In practice, the relative momentum

is as close as possible to

Figure

shows

In the data analysis, we use the following function form to fit the data

\displaystyle R(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{0}+r_{1}t+r_{3}t^{3}.

(7)

).

.

For each decay mode, the connection between

and the transition amplitude

can be established through Eq. (

) and Eq. (

). Finally, on the L16 and L24 ensembles, we obtain the effective coupling
g_{AB}
for each decay mode

.
For each mode, the values of

obtained on the two ensembles L16 and L24 are very close to each other and provide a self-consistent check except for

. The differences among the values of
g_{D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}}
from L16 and L24 with different momentum configurations of

IV
Discussion

Although theoretically they are functions of meson masses, the dimensionless
g_{AB}
are usually assumed to be insensitive to the meson masses in the initial and final states in suitable mass ranges. The physics behind this can be understood as follows. For two-body strong decays of a hadron,
the dictating dynamics is the gluon-quark coupling in QCD.
Although a rigorous proof is lacking in the low energy regime, it is expected the light quark mass effects on the dimensionless coupling
g_{AB}
is
\mathcal{O}(m_{q}/\Lambda_{QCD})
.
So for
m_{q}\ll\Lambda_{QCD}
, the quark mass dependence can be neglected when a raw estimate is made.
Consequently, the averaged
g_{AB}
is applied to predict the partial decay width
\Gamma_{AB}
of
\eta_{c1}\to AB
by

\Gamma_{AB}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\frac{k_{\text{ex}}}{m_{\eta_{c1}}^{2}}\overline{|\mathcal{M}(\eta_{c1}\to AB)|^{2}},

(8)

where
k_{\text{ex}}
is the decay momentum determined by the experimental mass values of
A
and
B
along with a given
m_{\eta_{c1}}
,
\mathcal{M}
is the tree-level decay amplitude
x_{AB}
in Eq. (
3
) with
\vec{k}
being replaced by
\vec{k}_{\text{ex}}
.
Using the mass
m_{\eta_{c1}}=4.329(36)
GeV determined by averaging the values on two ensembles, the partial widths
\Gamma_{AB}
of all the decay modes concerned are predicted and are shown in Table
3
. Specifically, the partial widths of open charm decays at
m_{\eta_{c1}}=4.329(36)
GeV are

\displaystyle\Gamma_{D_{1}\bar{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 258(133)~\text{MeV},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 150(118)~\text{MeV},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{D^{*}\bar{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 88(18)~\text{MeV},

(9)

which indicate
\eta_{c1}
is a very wide resonance with a total width
\Gamma_{\eta_{c1}}\gtrsim 400\text{MeV}
.
The
D_{1}\bar{D}
dominance of the
\eta_{c1}
decay is in agreement with the expectation of the flux tube model qualitatively, which favors the final state of a
S
-wave meson and a
P
-wave meson a hybrid state.
However, the partial width in this study is much larger than
\sim 25~\text{MeV}
predicted by the flux tube model
Page et al. 1999
.
On the other hand, the
D_{1}\bar{D}
mass threshold
E_{\text{th}}(D_{1}\bar{D})\approx 4290~\text{MeV}
is very close to the expected
\eta_{c1}
mass, so the partial width
\Gamma_{D_{1}\bar{D}}
is very sensitive to the
m_{\eta_{c1}}
. There is a possibility that this decay mode is kinetically prohibited if
m_{\eta_{c1}}<E_{\text{th}}(D_{1}\bar{D})
.

It is most striking that the
{D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}}
and
D^{*}\bar{D}
decays have large branching fractions and even dominate the
\eta_{c1}
decay when
m_{\eta_{c1}}<E_{\text{th}}(D\bar{D}_{1})
.
The large
\Gamma_{D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}}
and
\Gamma_{D^{*}\bar{D}}
refute the expectation of the flux tube model that the decay modes of two
S
-wave mesons are disfavored and that decay modes with two spatially identical particles such as
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
is prohibited
Isgur and Paton 1985
;
Close and Page 1995
;
Page 1997
;
Page et al. 1999
. We notice that a recent phenomenological study in the Born-Oppenheimer approximation also indicates the non-suppression of the
D^{(*)}\bar{D}^{(*)}
decay modes of the
1^{-+}
heavy quarkonium-like hybrids
Bruschini 2024
.
Our observation suggests
D^{*}\bar{D}
and
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
are good channels for
\eta_{c1}
to be searched experimentally.
First, the
D^{*}\bar{D}
and
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
decay modes are kinetically favored since their thresholds are much lower than the expected
m_{\eta_{c1}}
.
Secondly,
D
and
D^{*}
are easier to be reconstructed than
D_{1}
in experiments.
One feature of the
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
channel is that, its
C
-parity can be determined by measuring the polarizations of
D^{*}
and
\bar{D}^{*}
, since the
C
-parity of a
P
-wave
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
system is determined by its total spin
S
through
C=(-)^{L+S}
.
This feature can be used to distinguish the possible
1^{-+}
state from the
1^{--}
states in the
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
system.

For the closed charm decay modes, the tiny partial width of the
J/\psi\omega
decay is understood due to the OZI rule.
However, the effective couplings for the
\chi_{c1}\eta_{(2)}
and
\eta_{c}\eta_{(2)}
decays appear not to be particularly small.
This can be attributed to the enhancement by the QCD
U_{A}(1)
anomaly
Jiang et al. 2023
;
Bali et al. 2015
, which introduces an anomalous coupling between gluons and the flavor singlet pseudoscalar meson (the isoscalar
\eta_{(2)}
in the
N_{f}=2
case).
Since this coupling is proportional to
\sqrt{N_{f}}
, the coupling
g_{AB}
for
\chi_{c1}\eta_{(2)}
and
\eta_{c}\eta_{(2)}
in Table
3
should be proportionally increased by a factor of
\sqrt{3/2}
for the flavor SU(3) case.
Considering that the
\chi_{c1}\eta^{\prime}
is likely kinetically prohibited and the
\eta_{c}\eta
decay is suppressed by
\sin^{2}\theta
with
\theta=-24.5^{\circ}
(linear mass relation) or
-11.3^{\circ}
(squared mass relation)
Workman et al. 2022
, where
\theta
is the small
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mixing angle,
here we restrict our predictions on the partial widths of
\chi_{c1}\eta
and
\eta_{c}\eta^{\prime}
decays as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\chi_{c1}\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta\cdot 44(29)~\text{MeV},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\eta_{c}\eta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta\cdot 0.93(77)~\text{MeV}.

(10)

Although many systematic uncertainties are not well controlled yet, the results in this Letter have already provided very important physical information for
\eta_{c1}
decay properties.
Since the open-charm partial widths are sensitive to
m_{\eta_{c1}}
, we plot the
m_{\eta_{c1}}
dependence of the partial widths as curves with colored error bands in Fig.
3
, where the vertical grey band illustrates
m_{\eta_{c1}}
obtained in this work.
This figure serves as a further guidance for experiments to search for
\eta_{c1}
. In comparison with
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders that favor the production of
1^{--}
mesons, the
B
meson decay might be a good place to search for the
\eta_{c1}
search.
The LHCb and Belle II experiments may take the mission.

V
Summary

Based on two gauge ensembles of
N_{f}=2
dynamical quarks at
m_{\pi}\approx 350
MeV, we perform the first lattice QCD calculation of the two-body partial decay widths of the
1^{-+}
charmonium-like hybrids
\eta_{c1}
. We extract the decay amplitudes for open charm and closed charm decays directly from hadronic correlation functions, and then predict the partial widths
\Gamma_{D_{1}\bar{D}}=258(133)~\text{MeV}
,
\Gamma_{D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}}=150(118)~\text{MeV}
and
\Gamma_{D^{*}\bar{D}}=88(18)~\text{MeV}
for
m_{\eta_{c1}}=4329(36)~\text{MeV}
, which indicate
that
\eta_{c1}
is a wide resonance. The strikingly large partial widths of
D^{*}\bar{D}
and
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
decays suggest these system to be good places for the
\eta_{c1}
search. Especially, the polarization of the
P
-wave
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
is an ideal probe to distinguish the
1^{-+}
products from
1^{--}
ones. LHCb, Belle II and BESIII experiments may take the mission to search for
\eta_{c1}
in
D^{*}\bar{D}
and
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
systems.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{M_{L},M_{S},M_{A},M_{B}}\langle L,M_{L};S,M_{S}|JM\rangle\langle S_{A}M_{A};S_{B}M_{B}|S,M_{S}\rangle

(A1)

\displaystyle\times\sum\limits_{R\in O_{h}}Y^{*}_{LM_{L}}(R\circ\vec{k})\mathcal{O}_{A}^{M_{A}}(R\circ\vec{k})\mathcal{O}_{B}^{M_{B}}(-R\circ\vec{k}),

The Parametrization of correlation functions

As for the decay process
\eta_{c1}\to AB
, we are interested in the following correlation function

C^{ij}_{AB,\eta_{c1}}(\hat{k},t)\equiv\langle 0|\mathcal{O}^{i}_{AB}(\hat{k},t)\mathcal{O}^{j,\dagger}_{\eta_{c1}}(0)|0\rangle,

(A15)

where
\mathcal{O}^{i}_{\eta_{c1}}
is the interpolating operator that annihilates a
\eta_{c1}
state,
\mathcal{O}^{i}_{AB}(\hat{k})(t)
is any one of the two-particle operators defined in Eqs. (
A4
,
A5
,
A7
,
A12
,
A13
,
A8
,
A9
,

A10
) that annihilates a
AB
state with the same quantum number as that of
\eta_{c1}
. Actually, each term
\mathcal{O}_{AB}^{i}(\vec{k})
of the operator
\mathcal{O}^{i}_{AB}(\hat{k})
with a specifically oriented momentum
\vec{k}
and a specific index combination of
\mathcal{O}_{A}
and
\mathcal{O}_{B}
(if available),

\mathcal{O}_{AB}^{i}(\vec{k})=\left[\mathcal{O}_{A}(\vec{k})\circ\mathcal{O}_{B}(-\vec{k})\right]^{i},

(A16)

contributes uniformly to
C^{ij}_{AB,\eta_{c1}}(\hat{k},t)
by a quantity

C^{ij}_{AB,\eta_{c1}}(\vec{k},t)\equiv\langle 0|\mathcal{O}^{i}_{AB}(\vec{k},t)\mathcal{O}^{j,\dagger}_{\eta_{c1}}(0)|0\rangle,

(A17)

with
\vec{k}
being the spatial momentum of
A
in the rest frame of
\eta_{c1}
. In Eq. (
A16
) the symbol ‘
\circ
’ stands for the specific combination in the explicit expression of
\mathcal{O}^{i}_{AB}(\hat{k})
that results from Eq. (
A1
).

Let
\ket{A(\vec{k})B(-\vec{k})}
be the state generated by
\mathcal{O}^{i,\dagger}_{AB}(\hat{k})
, then in the space of
\ket{\eta_{c1}}
and
\ket{A(\vec{k})B(-\vec{k})}
, the temporal transfer matrix is expressed as

\hat{T}=e^{-a_{t}\hat{H}}=e^{-a_{t}\bar{E}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{-a_{t}\Delta/2}&a_{t}x_{AB}\\
a_{t}x_{AB}&e^{+a_{t}\Delta/2}\end{array}\right).

(A18)

where
\bar{E}=(m_{\eta_{c1}}+E_{AB})/2
and
\Delta=m_{\eta_{c1}}-E_{AB}
have been defined with
E_{AB}=E_{A}+E_{B}
. Note that this expression assumes the state normalizations
\langle\eta_{c1}|\eta_{c1}\rangle=1
and
\langle AB|AB\rangle=1
. Without losing the generality, in the following discussion we use the state normalization conditions

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\eta_{c1}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{c1},\lambda|\eta_{c1},\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{AB}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\langle AB;(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}),\vec{k}|AB;(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}),\vec{k}\rangle,

(A19)

where
\lambda
is the polarization index of
\eta_{c1}
, while
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
represent the the polarization indices (if available) for the final state mesons
A
and
B
, respectively. If we assume
\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c1}}^{i}
does not couple to
|AB\rangle
states and
\mathcal{O}_{AB}^{i}
does not couple to the
|\eta_{c1}\rangle
state, then the
\eta_{c1}-AB
transition takes place at any time slice
t^{\prime}\in[0,t]
, such that

\displaystyle C^{ij}_{AB,\eta_{c1}}(t)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\eta_{c1}}\mathcal{N}_{AB}}Z_{AB,i}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}Z_{\eta_{c1,j}}^{\lambda,*}\langle AB;(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}),\vec{k}|e^{-a_{t}\hat{H}t}|\eta_{c1},\lambda\rangle

(A20)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}a_{t}x_{AB}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})\lambda}\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\eta_{c1}}\mathcal{N}_{AB}}Z_{AB,i}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}Z_{\eta_{c1},j}^{\lambda,*}(\lambda)\sum\limits_{t_{1}=0}^{t}e^{-a_{t}E_{AB}(t-t_{1}-1)}e^{-a_{t}m_{\eta_{c1}}t_{1}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle-\frac{\sinh(a_{t}\Delta t/2)}{\sinh(a_{t}\Delta/2)}e^{-a_{t}\bar{E}t}\sum\limits_{\lambda(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}a_{t}x_{AB}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})\lambda}\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\eta_{c1}}\mathcal{N}_{AB}}Z_{AB,i}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}Z_{\eta_{c1},j}^{\lambda,*}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle-t\left(1+\frac{1}{24}(a_{t}\Delta t)^{2}\right)e^{-a_{t}\bar{E}t}\sum\limits_{\lambda(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}a_{t}x_{AB}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})\lambda}\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\eta_{c1}}\mathcal{N}_{AB}}Z_{AB,i}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}Z_{\eta_{c1,j}}^{\lambda,*},

where
x_{AB}
is now replaced by the transition amplitude

x_{AB}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})\lambda}(\vec{k})\approx\langle AB;(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}),\vec{k}|\hat{H}|\eta_{c1},\lambda\rangle,

(A21)

with proper state normalizations,
Z_{\eta_{c1,j}}^{\lambda}
and
Z_{AB,i}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}
are the overlapping matrix elements

\displaystyle Z_{\eta_{c1},i}^{\lambda}=

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c1}}^{i}|\eta_{c1},\lambda\rangle\equiv Z_{\eta_{c1}}\epsilon^{i}_{\lambda}(\vec{0})

(A22)

\displaystyle Z_{AB,i}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}=

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathcal{O}^{i}_{AB}|AB;(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}),\vec{k}\rangle.

is not so straightforward as that of

. To the first order of the Bonn approximation, the two-particle state can be expressed as the direct product of the state vector of
A
and

(A23)

such that

and

are approximated by

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{AB}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{A}\mathcal{N}_{B}

\displaystyle Z_{AB,i}^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle Z_{A}Z_{B}\epsilon^{i}_{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}(\vec{k},AB),

(A24)

where
Z_{A}
and
Z_{B}
are defined through

\langle 0|\mathcal{O}_{X}^{(i)}(\vec{k})|X;\lambda^{\prime},\vec{k}\rangle=Z_{X}\epsilon_{(\lambda^{\prime})}^{(i)}(\vec{k})

(A25)

with
X
referring to
A
or
B

(A26)

A16
). The brackets of the superscripts and subscripts mean

The factors

,
Z_{A}
and
Z_{B}

,
\mathcal{O}_{A}^{(i)}(\vec{k})

\mathcal{O}_{B}^{(i)}(\vec{k})

(A27)

where the normalization condition

is used for single particle states

, and

(A28)

comes from the completeness of polarization vectors of (axial) vector mesons. Consequently, we have the ratio function

\displaystyle R_{AB}(\vec{k},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C^{33}_{AB,\eta_{c1}}(\vec{k},t)}{\sqrt{[C_{AA}(\vec{k},t)C_{BB}(-\vec{k},t)]^{33}C^{33}_{\eta_{c1},\eta_{c1}}(\vec{0},t)}}

\displaystyle t\left(1+\frac{1}{24}(a_{t}\Delta)^{2}t^{2}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{8L^{3}m_{\eta_{c1}}E_{A}E_{B}}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}\frac{a_{t}x^{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})\lambda}_{AB}[\epsilon_{(\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime})}^{3}(\vec{k},AB)\epsilon^{3}_{\lambda}(\vec{0})]}{\sqrt{\mathcal{P}_{A}(\vec{k})\mathcal{P}_{B}(-\vec{k})\mathcal{P}_{\eta_{c1}}^{33}(\vec{0})}}~~~(t\gg 1~~\text{and}~~(a_{t}\Delta)t\ll 1),

which is free of the factors

,

and

It is easily seen that the transition amplitude

(A30)

with respect to

In practice, we average contribution of Eq. (
A17
) over

and

.4
Fitting details

As addressed in the main text, we use simple generic
\bar{q}\Gamma q
operators for all the single mesons, all of which are ground states of specific quantum numbers. In the framework of the distillation methods, quark fields are Laplacian Heaviside smeared such that the coupling of these operators to excited states are suppressed. In order to determine the energy of each meson
X
at a given momentum
k=|\vec{k}|
, we perform a two-mass fit to the correlation function
C_{X}(k,t)
through the function form

\displaystyle C_{X}(k,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{1}\left(e^{-E_{1}(k)t}+e^{-E_{1}(k)(T-t)}\right)

(A48)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle W_{2}\left(e^{-E_{2}(k)t}+e^{-E_{2}(k)(T-t)}\right),

with the second term accounting for the contribution from higher states. Indeed, this function form presents accurately for all the single mesons. In Fig.
A2
, the data points are the effective masses of
\eta_{c1}
(upper panel) and
D_{1}
(lower panel) on L16 ensemble. The shaded band in each panel illustrates the fit time range and the fit quality. The correlations of other single mesons (mainly pseudoscalars and vectors) have much better signal-to-noise ratios (except for
\eta_{(2)}
).

The small but nonzero constant
r_{0}
can be understood as follows. Assuming the correlation function
C_{AB,\eta_{c1}}^{33}(\vec{k},t)
in Eq. (
A17
) is contributed only from two orthogonal states
|\eta_{c1}\rangle
and
|AB\rangle
which are normalized as
\langle\eta_{c1}|\eta_{c1}\rangle=1
and
\langle AB|AB\rangle=1
, one has

\displaystyle C^{33}_{AB,\eta_{c1}}(\vec{k},t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathcal{O}^{i}_{AB}(\vec{k},t)\mathcal{O}^{j,\dagger}_{\eta_{c1}}(0)|0\rangle

(A49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathcal{O}^{3}_{AB}(\vec{k},0)\hat{T}^{t}\mathcal{O}^{3,\dagger}_{\eta_{c1}}(0)|0\rangle,

where
\hat{T}
is the transfer matrix defined in Eq. (
A18
) in the Hilbert space spanned by
|\eta_{c1}\rangle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\end{array}\right)
and
|AB\rangle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
1\end{array}\right)
. For
(a_{t}\Delta)\ll 1
and
(a_{t}x_{AB})\ll 1
, one has

\hat{T}^{t}=e^{-(a_{t}\hat{H})t}=e^{-(a_{t}\bar{E})t}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{-(a_{t}\Delta)t/2}&(a_{t}x_{AB})t\\
(a_{t}x_{AB})t&e^{+(a_{t}\Delta)t/2}\end{array}\right)+\mathcal{O}(a_{t}^{2}t^{2}\Delta x_{AB}).

(A50)

Now considering

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c1}}^{3,\dagger}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\eta_{c1}\rangle\langle\eta_{c1}|\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c1}}^{3,\dagger}|0\rangle+|AB\rangle\langle AB|\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c1}}^{3,\dagger}|0\rangle\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{c}Z_{\eta_{c1}}^{\eta_{c1}}\\
Z_{\eta_{c1}}^{AB}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{AB}^{3,\dagger}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\eta_{c1}\rangle\langle\eta_{c1}|\mathcal{O}_{AB}^{3,\dagger}|0\rangle+|AB\rangle\langle AB|\mathcal{O}_{AB}^{3,\dagger}|0\rangle\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{c}Z_{AB}^{\eta_{c1}}\\
Z_{AB}^{AB}\end{array}\right),

the correlation function
C_{AB,\eta_{c1}}^{33}(\vec{k},t)
can be expressed as

C_{AB,\eta_{c1}}^{33}(\vec{k},t)\approx Z_{AB}^{\eta_{c1}}\left(Z_{\eta_{c1}}^{\eta_{c1}}-Z_{\eta_{c1}}^{AB}(a_{t}x_{AB})t\right)e^{-m_{\eta_{c1}}t}+Z_{AB}^{AB}\left(Z^{AB}_{\eta_{c1}}-Z_{\eta_{c1}}^{\eta_{c1}}(a_{t}x_{AB})t\right)e^{-E_{AB}t}.

(A57)

If
\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c1}}
(
\mathcal{O}_{AB}
)does not couple to
|AB\rangle
(
|\eta_{c1}\rangle
), namely,
Z_{\eta_{c1}}^{AB}=0
and
Z_{AB}^{\eta_{c1}}=0
, then one can get the ratio function
R_{AB}(\vec{k},t)
in Eq. (
A29
). However, if
Z_{\eta_{c1}}^{AB}
and
Z_{AB}^{\eta_{c1}}
are nonzero but small, one can easily derive that

\displaystyle R_{AB}(\vec{k},t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Z_{AB}^{\eta_{c1}}}{Z_{AB}^{AB}}+\frac{Z^{AB}_{\eta_{c1}}}{Z_{\eta_{c1}}^{\eta_{c1}}}\right)-(a_{t}x_{AB})t+\ldots

(A58)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle r_{0}+r_{1}t+\ldots.

Thus the constant term
r_{0}\sim\left(\frac{Z_{AB}^{\eta_{c1}}}{Z_{AB}^{AB}}+\frac{Z^{AB}_{\eta_{c1}}}{Z_{\eta_{c1}}^{\eta_{c1}}}\right)
serves as
a measure of the validity of the assumption

\langle 0|\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c1}}|AB\rangle\approx 0,~~\langle 0|\mathcal{O}_{AB}|\eta_{c1}\rangle\approx 0.

(A59)

We list
r_{0}
of all the
AB
modes in Table
A1
, where one can see that all the values of
r_{0}
are less than 0.02 and justifies assumptions in Eq. (
A59
).

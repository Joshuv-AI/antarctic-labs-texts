Simon Blatt, Christopher Hopper, Nicole Vorderobermeier

Abstract
. We prove long-time existence for the negative L 2 L^{2} -gradient flow of the p p -elastic energy, p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 , with an additive positive multiple of the length of the curve. To achieve this result we regularize the energy by adding a small multiple of a higher order energy, namely the square of the L 2 L^{2} -norm of the normal gradient of the curvature κ \kappa . Long-time existence is proved for the gradient flow of these new energies together with the smooth sub-convergence of the evolution equation’s solutions to critical points of the regularized energy in W 2 , p W^{2,p} . We then show that the solutions to the regularized evolution equations converge to a weak solution of the negative gradient flow of the p p -elastic energies. These latter weak solutions also sub-converge to critical points of the p p -elastic energy.

Apart from the invention of the curvature of a curve by James Bernoulli, this led to the remarkable classification of planar elastica by Leonhard Euler

2.
The regularized equations

To construct solutions to (
2.2
), we approximate the energy
\mathcal{E}
by regularized energies. We get rid of the degeneracy of
\mathcal{E}
by introducing the regularization

\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}(f)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}(|\kappa|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}ds

for any
\delta>0
. We will furthermore add a small positive multiple of the energy

\mathcal{F}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}ds,

where
\nabla_{s}\phi=\partial_{s}\phi-\langle\partial_{s}\phi,\tau\rangle\tau
indicates the normal part of
\partial_{s}\phi
. For any
p\geq 2
,
\varepsilon>0
,
\delta>0
, and
\lambda>0
let

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f)=\varepsilon\mathcal{F}(f)+\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}(f)+\lambda\mathcal{L}(f).

(2.1)

The negative
L^{2}
-gradient flow for the regularized energy
\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}
is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=-\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f)

(2.2)

where
\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f)=\varepsilon\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{F}(f)+\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}(f)+\lambda\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{L}(f)
.
A straightforward calculation yields following
[
5
]

\displaystyle\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{F}(f)

\displaystyle=-\left(\nabla_{s}^{4}\kappa+|\kappa|^{2}\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\langle\nabla_{s}\kappa,\kappa\rangle\nabla_{s}\kappa-\tfrac{3}{2}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}\kappa\right),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}(f)

\displaystyle=(\kappa^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+|\kappa|^{2}\kappa\right)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\quad+(p-2)(\kappa^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-4}{2}}\left(\langle\kappa,\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa\rangle\kappa+|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}\kappa+2\langle\kappa,\nabla_{s}\kappa\rangle\nabla_{s}\kappa\right)

\displaystyle\quad+(p-4)(p-2)(\kappa^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-6}{2}}\langle\kappa,\nabla_{s}\kappa\rangle^{2}\kappa

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{1}{p}(\kappa^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\kappa

and
\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{L}(f)=-\kappa
. (For the convenience of the reader we will give the details of this calculation in the next section.)

, it is therefore sufficient to focus on
\nabla_{s}^{i_{1}}\kappa\ast\cdots\ast\nabla_{s}^{i_{\nu}}\kappa
with
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{\nu}=\mu
and
i_{l}\leq k-1
instead of
P_{\nu}^{\mu,k-1}(k)
subsequently. We then observe by Hölder’s inequality, the notion of scale invariant norms in (
2.15
), and Lemma

that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}^{i_{1}}\kappa\ast\cdots\ast\nabla_{s}^{i_{\nu}}\kappa|ds

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{l=1}^{\nu}\|\nabla_{s}^{i_{l}}\kappa\|_{L^{\nu}}=\mathcal{L}^{1-\mu-\nu}(f)\prod_{l=1}^{\nu}\|\nabla_{s}^{i_{l}}\kappa\|_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq c\mathcal{L}^{1-\mu-\nu}(f)\prod_{l=1}^{\nu}\|\kappa\|_{2}^{1-\alpha_{l}}\|\kappa\|^{\alpha_{l}}_{k,2}

for
\alpha_{l}=(i_{l}+\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{\nu})/k
and some positive constant
c=c(n,k,\nu)
. As
\sum_{l=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{l}=\gamma
, the first inequality (
2.16
) follows. For the second claim (
2.8
) we recall the following standard interpolation result

\displaystyle\|\kappa\|_{k,2}^{2}\leq c(k)(\|\nabla_{s}^{k}\kappa\|_{2}^{2}+\|\kappa\|_{2}^{2}),

(2.18)

from which we deduce together with
\gamma<2
and the equivalence of
p
-norms on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

\displaystyle\|\kappa\|_{2}^{\nu-\gamma}\|\kappa\|^{\gamma}_{k,2}\leq c(k)(\|\kappa\|_{2}^{\nu-\gamma}\|\nabla_{s}^{k}\kappa\|_{2}^{\gamma}+\|\kappa\|_{2}^{\nu}).

Now taking account of the scaling and applying Young’s inequality for
\eta>0
, we observe

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f)^{1-\mu-\nu}\|\kappa\|_{2}^{\nu-\gamma}\|\kappa\|^{\gamma}_{k,2}

\displaystyle\leq c\mathcal{L}(f)^{1-\mu-\nu}(\|\kappa\|_{2}^{\nu-\gamma}\|\nabla_{s}^{k}\kappa\|_{2}^{\gamma}+\|\kappa\|_{2}^{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq c\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{\nu-\gamma}\|\nabla_{s}^{k}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{\gamma}+c\mathcal{L}(f)^{1-\mu-\frac{\nu}{2}}\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|\nabla_{s}^{k}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{\gamma}+c\eta^{-\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma}}\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{2\frac{\nu-\gamma}{2-\gamma}}+c\mathcal{L}(f)^{1-\mu-\frac{\nu}{2}}\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{\nu}

(2.19)

for some constant
c=c(k)>0
.

∎

The interpolation inequality (
2.8
) can be transfered to any term
P^{\mu}_{\nu}(\nabla_{s}\kappa)
that only involves derivatives of
\nabla_{s}\kappa
. The aim is to interpolate the
L^{1}
-norm of

between the
L^{2}
-norms of
\nabla_{s}\kappa
and
\nabla_{s}^{k+1}\kappa

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
f
as in Lemma

. For any term

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|P^{\mu,k-1}_{\nu}(\nabla_{s}\kappa)|ds

\displaystyle\leq c\|\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}}\Bigg(\eta\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}^{k+1}\kappa|^{2}ds+\eta^{-\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{\nu-\gamma}{2-\gamma}}

(2.20)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{L}(f)^{1-\mu-\frac{\nu}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\Bigg),

(2.21)

where

Proof.

also applies to derivatives of

and

.
Therefore, the argument in the proof of Lemma

∎

.
Here

(

We have the identities

(

Assume the bounds

2.2.
Short-time existence

For the flow considered in this paper, short-time existence is a standard matter. Therefore, we only shortly sketch the prove for smooth initial data.

Using
\nabla_{s}\phi=\partial_{s}\phi+\langle\kappa,\phi\rangle\tau
, we obtain

\nabla_{s}^{4}\kappa=|f^{\prime}|^{-6}(\partial_{x}^{6}f-\langle\partial_{x}^{6}f,\tau\rangle\tau)+\text{ lower order terms}.

So using standard theory, we get a smooth solution
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the quasilinear parabolic equation

\partial_{t}\tilde{f}=\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(\tilde{f})+|\tilde{f}^{\prime}|^{-6}\langle\partial_{x}^{6}\tilde{f},\tilde{\tau}\rangle\tilde{\tau}

with initial data
f_{0}
, i.e. a smooth family of smooth curves with the right normal velocity. We set
\sigma(t,\cdot)=|\tilde{f}_{t}^{\prime}|^{-7}\langle\partial_{x}^{6}\tilde{f}_{t},\tilde{\tau}_{t}\rangle
and solve the initial value problem
\partial_{t}\phi=\sigma(t,\phi)
with
\phi(0,\cdot)=id
. Then one easily calculates that
f(t,x)=f(t,\phi(t,x))
solves the initial value problem (
2.2
).

2.3.
Long-time existence and asymptotic behavior

As mentioned before, we can use the
\ast
-factor notation and its linear combinations to write the evolution equation (
2.2
) in the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=\varepsilon(\nabla_{s}^{4}\kappa+P^{2}(\kappa))+P^{2}(\kappa)+\lambda\kappa.

(2.22)

Let us first deduce some obvious uniform bounds on
\mathcal{L}(f_{t})
,
\|\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and
\|\nabla_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}
for solutions of (
2.2
).
Note that
\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{t})
is monotonically decreasing in time
t
as

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{t})=-\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{t})|^{2}ds=-\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\partial_{t}f_{t}|^{2}ds,

(2.23)

we therefore obtain from the non-negativity of each term in the energy (
2.1
)

\displaystyle\lambda\mathcal{L}(f_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{t})\leq\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{0}),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{2}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{0}),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\kappa|^{p}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}(\kappa^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}ds\leq p\,\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{0})

(2.24)

for all
T>t>0
. Fenchel’s and Hölder’s inequality imply that

2\pi\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\kappa|ds\leq\mathcal{L}(f)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\kappa|^{p}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f_{t})\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\kappa|^{p}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}{(p\,\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{0}))^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}

(2.25)

From the inequalities in (
2.3
) and (
2.25
) we thereby gain the following bounds for both the length und the
L^{2}
norm of
\nabla_{s}\kappa
:

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}{(p\,\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{0}))^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\leq\mathcal{L}(f_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{0})}{\lambda},

(2.26)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{2}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{0}).

(2.27)

Also the
L^{\infty}
norm of
\kappa
is bounded, which can be seen as follows. Hölder’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}(f_{t})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\|\kappa\|_{L^{p}}\leq\mathcal{L}(f_{t})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}(\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\kappa^{2}+\delta^{2}|^{\frac{p}{2}}ds)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq(p\lambda)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}},

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}(f_{t})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\leq(\tfrac{\lambda\varepsilon}{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{0}).

Hence, Lemma
2.11
tells us that

\displaystyle\|\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}}<c(p,\varepsilon,\lambda,\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{0})).

(2.28)

To establish the long-time existence of a solution, let us assume that the maximal time of smooth existence for the flow (
2.2
)
T
satisfies
T<\infty
, i.e. let us assume that the flow does not exist for all times. Considering Lemma
2.4
, Lemma
2.5
, and the flow equation (
2.22
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa|^{2}ds+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}^{m+3}\kappa|^{2}ds

(2.29)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle P^{m+4}(\kappa)-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\nabla_{s}^{4}\kappa+P^{2}(\kappa),\kappa\rangle\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa,\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa\rangle ds

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle P^{m+4}(\kappa)-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle P^{2}(\kappa),\kappa\rangle\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa,\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa\rangle ds

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\nabla_{s}^{m+2}\kappa+P^{m}(\kappa)-\tfrac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa,\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle P^{m+4}(\kappa),\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa\rangle ds+\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle P^{m+4}(\kappa),\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa\rangle ds

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle P^{m+2}(\kappa),\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa\rangle ds.

In order to apply interpolation estimates on the right-hand side, we need to integrate by parts once or twice, in case that terms like
\nabla_{s}^{m+3}\kappa
or
\nabla_{s}^{m+4}\kappa
appear in
P^{m+4}(\kappa)
, to achieve from (
2.29
)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa|^{2}ds+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}^{m+3}\kappa|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}P^{2m+4,m+2}(\kappa)ds+\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}P^{2m+4,m+2}(\kappa)ds+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}P^{2m+2,m+2}(\kappa)ds.

(2.30)

It suffices to estimate, as
P^{2m+2,m+2}(\kappa)
is included in the notation of
P^{2m+4,m+2}(\kappa)
, by using Lemma
2.6
and Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle P^{2m+4,m+2}(\kappa)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|P^{2m+4,m+2}(\kappa)|ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2m+4}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|P_{i}^{2m+4-i,m+1}(\nabla_{s}\kappa)|\cdot|P^{0}(\kappa)|ds+\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|P^{0}(\kappa)|ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2m+4}c_{0}\|\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|P_{i}^{2m+4-i,m+1}(\nabla_{s}\kappa)|ds+c_{1}\|\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}}

for some constants

depending on
m
and
\delta
. Note that the case

can be ruled out thanks to the underlying precise gradient flow equation, including the gradients (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), and integration by parts if needed.

Now let es assume the initial bound
\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{t})\leq\Lambda
for
0\leq t<T
. Then the previous inequality reduces by the uniform bound on
\kappa
in (
2.28
) to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}P^{2m+4,m+2}(\kappa)ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=2}^{2m+4}c_{0}(m,p,\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda,\Lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|P_{i}^{2m+4-i,m+1}(\nabla_{s}\kappa)|ds+c_{1}(m,p,\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda,\Lambda).

(2.31)

Finally, we interpolate the remaining integral between
\|\nabla_{s}^{m+3}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\nabla_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}
by Lemma
2.9
with
k=m+2
,

,
\nu=i
, and

. Note that Lemma

, and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|P_{i}^{2m+4-i,m-1}(\nabla_{s}\kappa)|ds

(2.32)

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\Bigg(\eta\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}^{m+3}\kappa|^{2}ds+\eta^{-\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{i-\gamma}{2-\gamma}}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{L}(f)^{-2m-3+\frac{i}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{i}{2}}\Bigg)

for a constant

Hence, the estimates (

2.3

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa|^{2}ds+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}^{m+3}\kappa|^{2}ds\leq c(m,n,p,\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda,\Lambda),

where we absorbed

, that appears on the right-hand side in (

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(t)\leq\|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(0)+c(m,n,p,\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda,\Lambda)T

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c(m,n,p,\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda,\Lambda,f_{0},T)

3.
A priori estimates for the regularized energies

In this section we prove the essential a priori estimates that will allow to obtain a solution to (
2.2
) sending
\varepsilon,\delta\downarrow 0
in Section
4
. To this end, we first have to bring the evolution equations in a suitable form.

3.1.
The first variation of the energy

We will once more calculate the first variation of the energy
\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}
. This time we will not decompose the direction into a normal and tangential part as later on we test the equation with a finite difference
f(\cdot+h)-f
which need not be pointing in normal direction.
We consider a smooth time dependent family of curves

f(t,x)=f:[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}

and set
V=\partial_{t}f.

We obtain the following variant of
[
5
, Lemma 9]
.

Lemma 3.1

(First variation of geometric quantities)
.

We have

(i)

\partial_{t}(ds)=\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle ds
,

(ii)

\partial_{t}\partial_{s}-\partial_{s}\partial_{t}=-\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\partial_{s}
,

(iii)

\partial_{t}\tau=\nabla_{s}V
,

(iv)

\nabla_{t}\kappa=(\partial_{s}^{2}V)^{\bot}-2\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\kappa-\langle\partial_{s}V,\kappa\rangle\tau
,

(v)

\nabla_{t}\nabla_{s}\kappa=(\partial_{s}^{3}V)^{\bot}-3\langle\partial_{s}^{2}V,\tau\rangle\kappa-3\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle(\partial_{s}\kappa)^{\bot}-\langle\nabla_{s}V,\kappa\rangle\kappa+|\kappa|^{2}\nabla_{s}V
.

Proof.

Firstly, we observe that

\partial_{t}|f^{\prime}|=\left\langle\frac{f^{\prime}}{|f^{\prime}|},\partial_{x}V\right\rangle=\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle|f^{\prime}|.

Hence, from
ds=|f^{\prime}|dx
we get

\partial_{t}(ds)=(\partial_{t}|f^{\prime}|)dx=\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle ds

and

\partial_{t}\tau=\frac{\partial_{t}f^{\prime}}{|f^{\prime}|}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{|f^{\prime}|}\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle=\partial_{s}V-\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle\tau=\nabla_{s}V.

This proves parts (i) and (iii). Similarly, differentiating
\partial_{s}=\frac{\partial_{x}}{|f^{\prime}|}
yields

\partial_{t}\partial_{s}=\frac{1}{|f^{\prime}]}\partial_{t}\partial_{x}-\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle\frac{1}{|f^{\prime}|}\partial_{x}=\partial_{s}\partial_{t}-\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle\partial_{s}.

To get (iv), we calculate using the formulas (ii) and (iii)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\kappa

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\partial_{s}\tau=\partial_{s}\partial_{t}\tau-\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle\partial_{s}\tau=\partial_{s}(\partial_{s}V-\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\tau)-\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle\kappa

\displaystyle=(\partial_{s}^{2}V)^{\bot}-\langle\partial_{s}V,\kappa\rangle\tau-\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\kappa-\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle\kappa

\displaystyle=(\partial_{s}^{2}V)^{\bot}-2\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\kappa-\langle\partial_{s}V,\kappa\rangle\tau.

Finally from

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}\partial_{s}\kappa

\displaystyle=(\partial_{s}\partial_{t}\kappa-\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\partial_{s}\kappa)^{\bot}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{s}(\partial_{s}^{2}V-\langle\partial_{s}^{2}V,\tau\rangle\tau-2\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\kappa+\langle\partial_{s}V,\kappa\rangle\tau)-\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\partial_{s}\kappa)^{\bot}

\displaystyle=((\partial_{s}^{3}V)^{\bot}-\langle\partial_{s}^{2}V,\tau\rangle\kappa-2\langle\partial_{s}^{2}V,\tau\rangle\kappa-2\langle\partial_{s}V,\kappa\rangle\kappa-2\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\partial_{s}\kappa

\displaystyle\quad+\langle\partial_{s}V,\kappa\rangle\kappa-\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\partial_{s}\kappa)^{\bot}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{s}^{3}V)^{\bot}-3\langle\partial_{s}^{2}V,\tau\rangle\kappa-3\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\nabla_{s}\kappa-\langle\partial_{s}V,\kappa\rangle\kappa

together with

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}(\langle\partial_{s}\kappa,\tau\rangle\tau)=\langle\partial_{s}\kappa,\tau\rangle\nabla_{t}\tau=\langle\partial_{s}\kappa,\tau\rangle\nabla_{s}V=-|\kappa|^{2}\nabla_{s}V

we get (v).
∎

Using this lemma with
f_{t}=f+tV
, we obtain for the first variation of
\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\delta_{V}\mathcal{F}(f)

\displaystyle:=\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}(f+tV)|_{t=0}

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\nabla_{s}\kappa,\delta_{V}(\nabla_{s}\kappa)\rangle ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}\delta_{V}(ds)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\nabla_{s}\kappa,(\delta_{V}(\nabla_{s}\kappa))^{\bot}\rangle ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle ds,

where

(\delta_{V}(\nabla_{s}\kappa))^{\bot}=(\partial_{s}^{3}V)^{\bot}-3\langle\partial_{s}^{2}V,\tau\rangle\kappa-3\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\nabla_{s}\kappa-\langle\nabla_{s}V,\kappa\rangle\kappa+|\kappa|^{2}\nabla_{s}V.

Similarly,

\delta_{V}\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\delta}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}(|\kappa|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{p-2}\langle\kappa,\delta_{V}\kappa\rangle+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}(|\kappa|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle ds

(3.2)

and

\delta_{V}\kappa=(\partial_{s}^{2}V)^{\bot}-2\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle\kappa-\langle\partial_{s}V,\kappa\rangle\tau.

The first variation of length of course is

\delta_{V}\mathcal{L}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle ds.

(3.3)

Let us bring the first variation of
\nabla_{s}\kappa
in a more suitable form for our task, using again a slight variant of the
\ast
-notation introduced in
[
5
]
.
Together with
\nabla_{s}\kappa=\partial_{s}\kappa-\langle\partial_{s}\kappa,\tau\rangle\tau=\partial_{s}\kappa+|\kappa|^{2}\tau
, we get

(\delta_{V}(\nabla_{s}\kappa))^{\bot}=(\partial_{s}^{3}V)^{\bot}+\partial_{s}^{2}V\ast\tau\ast\kappa+\partial_{s}V\ast(\tau\ast\partial_{s}\kappa+\kappa\ast\kappa+\kappa\ast\kappa\ast\tau).

3.2.
Higher regularity

We will now prove that solutions of the negative gradient flow for the regularized energies
\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}
are more regular, using a finite difference method as in
[
2
]
. We measure this additional regularity using the Besov-Nikolski space
B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
for
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, which consists of all functions
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
for which the semi-norm

|u|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}:=\sup_{h}\frac{\|u_{h}-u\|_{L^{p}}}{|h|^{s}}

is finite. Here,
u_{h}(x):=u(x+h)
denotes the by the value
h
shifted function. This space is equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}=\|u\|_{L^{p}}+|u|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}.

Theorem 3.2

(Higher regularity)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth curve parametrized by arc-length which satisfies
the equation

\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f)=g

(3.4)

for a
g\in L^{2}(ds)
and an
0<\varepsilon<1
. Then

\varepsilon\|\partial_{s}^{3}f\|_{B^{\frac{1}{4}}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+\|\partial_{s}^{2}f\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2p}}_{p,\infty}}^{p}\leq C(1+\|g\|_{L^{2}})

for some constant
C=C(p,\lambda,\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f))
.

We note that Fenchel’s together with Hölder’s inequality give

2\pi\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\kappa|ds\leq\mathcal{L}(f)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\kappa|^{p}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\mathcal{L}(f)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}(p\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f))^{\frac{1}{p}},

which implies

\mathcal{L}(f)\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}{(p\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f))^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}.

Since trivially

\mathcal{L}(f)\leq\frac{\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f)}{\lambda},

we see that the length of the curve is bounded from above and from below away from zero if the regularized energy is controlled.

We note that using Hölder’s inequality we get

\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}ds+\frac{1}{p}\mathcal{L}(f)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}(\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\kappa|^{2}ds)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{F}(f)+\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}(f)\leq\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f)

(3.5)

as
p\geq 2.

From
\nabla_{s}\kappa=\partial_{s}\kappa+|\kappa|^{2}\tau
together with Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s interpolation inequality (cf. Lemma
2.7
) and Young’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}+\|\kappa\|^{2}_{L^{4}})\leq C(\|\nabla_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla_{s}\kappa\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}}\|\kappa\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}+\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{3})\leq C(\|\nabla_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}+1).

Hence, (
3.5
) implies

\varepsilon\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C,

where the constant
C>0
only depends on the energy
\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f).

From Equation (
3.4
) we get

\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{h})=g_{h}\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f)=g,

where for a function
u
we set
u_{h}(x):=u(x+h)
. We test these equations with
V=f_{h}-f
, subtract the results and integrate over
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
to get

\displaystyle\varepsilon\delta_{V}(\mathcal{F}(f_{h})-\mathcal{F}(f))

\displaystyle+\delta_{V}(\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}(f_{h})-\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}(f))+\lambda(\delta_{V}(\mathcal{L}(f_{h})-\mathcal{L}(f))

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}(g_{h}-g)(f_{h}-f)ds.

We will estimate this further using that

\hol_{\frac{1}{2}}\tau\leq C\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\qquad\hol_{\frac{1}{2}}\kappa\leq C\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}

(3.7)

which can easily seen to be true observing that the fundamental theorem of calculus together with Hölder’s inequality gives for a differentiable function
u:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
x,h\in\mathbb{R}
with
|h|<1
,

|u(x+h)-u(x)|=\Big|\int_{0}^{h}u^{\prime}(x+t)dt\Big|\leq\int_{0}^{h}|u^{\prime}(x+t)|dt\leq|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence, we also have

\|\partial_{s}V\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\qquad\|\partial_{s}^{2}V\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.8)

We start discussing the terms coming from the first variation of
\mathcal{F}
in (
3.6
).

Lemma 3.3
.

We have

\varepsilon\delta_{V}(\mathcal{F}(f_{h})-\mathcal{F}(f))=\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\partial_{s}^{3}f_{h}-\partial_{s}^{3}f|^{2}ds+R_{\mathcal{F}}

with
|R_{\mathcal{F}}|\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

To prove this claim, we decompose

\displaystyle\varepsilon\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\nabla_{s}f_{h},\delta_{V}(\nabla_{s}f_{h})\rangle ds-\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\nabla_{s}f,\delta_{V}(\nabla_{s}f)\rangle ds\Big)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\partial_{s}^{3}f_{h}+|\kappa_{h}|^{2}\tau_{h},\partial_{s}^{3}V+\partial_{s}^{2}V\ast\tau_{h}\ast\kappa_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\partial_{s}V\ast(\tau_{h}\ast\partial_{s}\kappa_{h}+\kappa_{h}\ast\kappa_{h}+\kappa_{h}\ast\kappa_{h}\ast\tau_{h})\rangle ds

\displaystyle\quad-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\partial_{s}^{3}f+|\kappa|^{2}\tau,\partial_{s}^{3}V+\partial_{s}^{2}V\ast\tau\ast\kappa+\partial_{s}V\ast(\tau\ast\partial_{s}\kappa+\kappa\ast\kappa+\kappa\ast\kappa\ast\tau)\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\partial^{3}_{s}f_{h}-\partial^{3}_{s}f|^{2}ds+\varepsilon(I+II+III),

where

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle|\kappa_{h}|^{2}\tau_{h}-|\kappa|^{2}\tau,\partial_{s}^{3}V\rangle ds,

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\partial_{s}^{3}f_{h},\partial_{s}^{2}V\ast\kappa_{h}\ast\tau_{h}+\partial_{s}V\ast(\tau_{h}\ast\partial_{s}\kappa_{h}+\kappa_{h}\ast\kappa_{h}+\kappa_{h}\ast\kappa_{h}\ast\tau_{h})\rangle ds

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\partial_{s}^{3}f,\partial_{s}^{2}V\ast\kappa\ast\tau+\partial_{s}V\ast(\tau\ast\partial_{s}\kappa+\kappa\ast\kappa+\kappa\ast\kappa\ast\tau)\rangle ds,

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle|\kappa_{h}|^{2}\tau_{h},\partial_{s}^{2}V\ast\kappa_{h}\ast\tau_{h}+\partial_{s}V\ast(\tau_{h}\ast\partial_{s}\kappa_{h}+\kappa_{h}\ast\kappa_{h}+\kappa_{h}\ast\kappa_{h}\ast\tau_{h})\rangle ds

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle|\kappa|^{2}\tau,\partial_{s}^{2}V\ast\kappa\ast\tau+\partial_{s}V\ast(\tau\ast\partial_{s}\kappa+\kappa\ast\kappa+\kappa\ast\kappa\ast\tau)\rangle ds.

For the term I we see

\displaystyle|I|\leq\|\partial_{s}^{3}V\|_{L^{2}}\||\kappa_{h}|^{2}\tau_{h}-|\kappa|^{2}\tau\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\||\kappa_{h}|^{2}\tau_{h}-|\kappa|^{2}\tau\|_{L^{2}}.

The equality

|\kappa_{h}|^{2}\tau_{h}-|\kappa|^{2}\tau=(|\kappa_{h}|-|\kappa|)|\kappa_{h}|\tau_{h}+|\kappa|(|\kappa_{h}|-|\kappa|)\tau_{h}+|\kappa|^{2}(\tau_{h}-\tau)

together with (
3.7
) and Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation estimates (cf. Lemma
2.7
) imply that

\displaystyle\||\kappa_{h}|^{2}\tau_{h}-|\kappa|^{2}\tau\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}(2(\hol_{\frac{1}{2}}\kappa)\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}+(\hol_{\frac{1}{2}}\tau)\|\kappa\|_{L^{4}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}+\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\|\kappa\|_{L^{4}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\kappa\|^{1+\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}+1)

as
\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded.
Hence,

\varepsilon|I|\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon(\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}})\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon(\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we applied (
3.5
).
To see that the same estimate holds for the terms in II, we observe by (
3.8
)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\partial_{s}^{3}f,\partial^{2}V\ast\kappa\ast\tau\rangle ds\leq\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial^{2}V\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\partial_{s}^{3}f,\partial_{s}V\ast

\displaystyle(\tau\ast\partial_{s}\kappa+\kappa\ast\kappa+\kappa\ast\kappa\ast\tau)\rangle ds

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{s}V\|_{\infty}(\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}+\|\kappa\|^{2}_{L^{4}})\

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\frac{1}{2}}\|\kappa\|^{1+\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1),

where again we used (
3.5
) and the interpolation estimates stated in Lemma
2.7
.
As the other terms in
II
envolving
f_{h}
instead of
f
can be estimated in precisely the same way, we obtain

\varepsilon|II|\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon(\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle|III|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\kappa\|^{3}_{L^{3}}\|\partial_{s}^{2}V\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\|\kappa\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\partial_{s}V\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\kappa\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\|\partial_{s}V\|_{L^{\infty}})

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\kappa\|_{L^{3}}^{3}\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\frac{1}{2}}\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\frac{3}{2}}+\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{4})

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\partial_{s}\kappa\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+1)

Hence, also

\varepsilon|III|\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

As finally

\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle ds\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla_{s}\kappa\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

this gives

\varepsilon\delta_{V}(\mathcal{F}(f_{h})-\mathcal{F}(g))=\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\partial_{s}^{3}f_{h}-\partial_{s}^{3}f|^{2}ds+R_{\mathcal{F}}

with

|R_{\mathcal{F}}|\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

The terms in (

Lemma 3.4
.

We have

\delta_{V}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}(f_{h})-\delta_{V}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle(|\kappa_{h}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\kappa_{h}-(|\kappa|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\kappa,\partial^{2}_{s}V\rangle ds+\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}}

with
|\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}}|\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

We decompose

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}(|\kappa_{h}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}

\displaystyle\langle\kappa_{h},\delta_{V}(\kappa_{h})\rangle-(|\kappa|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\langle\kappa,\delta_{V}(\kappa)\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}(|\kappa_{h}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\langle\kappa_{h},\partial_{s}^{2}V+\partial_{s}V\ast\kappa_{h}\ast\tau_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-(|\kappa|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\langle\kappa,\partial_{s}^{2}V+\partial_{s}V\ast\kappa\ast\tau\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle(|\kappa_{h}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\kappa_{h}-(|\kappa|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\kappa,\partial^{2}_{s}V\rangle ds+I

where this time

Observe that

|I|\leq C\|\partial_{s}V\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}(|\kappa|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\kappa|^{2}ds\leq C\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}(f)\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Furthermore, we have

|\tfrac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}(|\kappa|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\langle\partial_{s}V,\tau\rangle ds|\leq\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}(f)\|\partial_{s}V\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This proves the claim.

Lemma 3.5
.

The following lemma tells us, that the main part in Lemma

Lemma 3.6
.

We have

\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle(|\kappa_{h}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\kappa_{h}-(|\kappa|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\kappa,\partial^{2}_{s}V\rangle ds\geq\frac{1}{4^{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\kappa_{h}-\kappa|^{p}ds.

Proof.

For vectors
w,v
we set
w_{t}=v+(w-v)t
. Using the fundamental theorem of calculus, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle(|w|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}w

\displaystyle-(|v|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}v,w-v\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(p-2)(|w_{t}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-4}{2}}\langle w_{t},(w-v)\rangle^{2}dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{1}(|w_{t}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|w-v|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}(|w_{t}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|w-v|^{2}dt.

If we now assume that
|v|\geq|w|
, we get
for
t\in[0,\tfrac{1}{4}]

|w_{t}|\geq|v|-t(|w|+|v|)\geq|v|-\frac{1}{2}|v|=\frac{|v|}{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}|w-v|.

So,

\displaystyle\langle(|w|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}w-(|v|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}v,w-v\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}(|w_{t}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|w-v|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4^{p-1}}|w-v|^{p}

and hence

\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle(|\kappa_{h}|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\kappa_{h}-(|\kappa|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\kappa,\partial^{2}_{s}V\rangle ds\geq\frac{1}{4^{p-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\kappa_{h}-\kappa|^{p}ds.

∎

4.1.
The case of smooth initial data

We now show the following version of Theorem

Theorem 4.1
.

Given any regular closed curve
f_{0}:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
of class
C^{\infty}
parametrized with constant speed, there is a family of regular curves
f:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f\in H^{1}([0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{\infty}([0,\infty),W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap C^{\frac{1}{2}}([0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}^{n}))
solving the initial value problem

\begin{cases}\partial^{\bot}_{t}f&=-\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}(f)\\
f(0,\cdot)&=f_{0}\end{cases}

in the weak sense, i.e. for all
V\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}^{n})

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\partial_{t}^{\bot}f,V\rangle dsdt=-\int_{0}^{\infty}(\delta_{V}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}(f))dt.

Furthermore, this solution satisfies

\|\partial_{t}f\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})}\leq C,\qquad\|\kappa\|_{L^{p}((0,T),B^{\frac{1}{2p}}_{p,\infty})}\leq C(T+1),

and

\|f_{t_{1}}-f_{t_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C|t_{1}-t_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
t_{0},t_{1}\in[0,\infty)
, where
C>0
only depends on
\mathcal{E}(f_{0})
,
p
, and
\lambda
.

To construct this solution, we take solutions
f^{\varepsilon,\delta}
to the gradient flow of the regularized energies (
2.2
) with
0<\varepsilon,\delta\leq 1
. We will now carefully reparametrize this family such that each curve is parametrized by constant speed.
Since
f^{\varepsilon,\delta}
is long-time solution for the gradient flow of
\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}
, we get

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\partial_{t}f^{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}dsdt\leq\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{0}).

We now reparametrize the solution setting by constant speed setting

\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,x)=f^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,\sigma_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(x))

where
\sigma_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta}
is the inverse of the function
\phi_{t}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(f^{\varepsilon,\delta})}\int_{x^{\varepsilon,\delta}}^{x}|(f^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t}(y))^{\prime}|dy
.
Of course these reparametrized solutions solve the equation

\partial_{t}^{\bot}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}=-\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}),

but they also satisfy

\displaystyle|(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta})^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}),

(4.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\bot}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,0)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,0),

(4.2)

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,0)dt\leq C.

(4.3)

We will now show that these furthermore have the following properties.

Lemma 4.2
.

The reparametrized solutions
\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}
are parametrized by constant speed, solve the equation
\partial_{t}^{\bot}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}=-\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}),

and satisfy the following estimates

(1)

\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}dsdt\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{5}}(T+1),

(2)

\int_{0}^{T}\|\kappa\|^{p}_{B^{\frac{1}{2p}}_{p,\infty}}dt\leq C(T+1)
,

(3)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\partial_{t}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}dxdt\leq C,

(4)

\|\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t_{1}}-\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C|t_{1}-t_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}
for all
t_{1},t_{0}\in[0,\infty)
with
|t_{1}-t_{0}|\leq 1,

where
C>0
is a constant only depending on the initial energy
\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}(f_{0})
.

Proof.

The estimate (1) and (2) follow directly from Corollary
3.7
and Corollary
3.8
. Differentiating Equation (
4.1
) we get

\mathcal{L}(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta})\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta})=\langle(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta})^{\prime},\partial_{x}\partial_{t}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\rangle=-\mathcal{L}(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta})^{2}\langle\kappa,\partial_{t}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\rangle+\partial_{x}\langle(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta})^{\prime},\partial_{t}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\rangle

and hence, using that the length of the curves is bounded from below and above,

|\partial_{x}\langle(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta})^{\prime},\partial_{t}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\rangle|\leq C(|\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta})|+|\langle\kappa,\partial^{\bot}_{t}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\rangle|).

Integrating over this estimate, we get by applying the fundamental theorem of calculus, Hölder’s inequality and (
4.2
)

\displaystyle|\langle(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta})^{\prime},\partial_{t}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C(|\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta})|+\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{t}^{\bot}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}})+|\langle(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta})^{\prime}(t,0),\partial_{t}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,0)\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C(|\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta})|+\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{t}^{\bot}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}}).

Combined with

|\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\kappa,\partial_{t}^{\bot}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\rangle ds\right|\leq\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{t}^{\bot}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\partial_{t}^{\bot}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}},

this gives

|\partial_{t}^{T}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}|\leq C(\|\partial_{t}^{\bot}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}}+|\kappa||\partial_{t}^{\bot}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}|).

Integrating over space and time and using Hölder’s inequality, we hence obtain

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\partial_{t}^{T}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}dsdt\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\partial_{t}^{\bot}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}dsdt\leq C.

As
ds=\mathcal{L}dx
and the length is bounded from below, this proves property (3).

We can derive the Hölder estimate (4) using a standard estimate for
L^{2}
gradient flows. Differentiating the quantity
\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t}-\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t_{0}}|^{2}dx
for a fixed time
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, we get that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t}-\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t}-\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t_{0}},\partial_{t}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\rangle dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t}-\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{t}\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence, by the fundamental theorem of calculus

\|\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t}-\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C|t-t_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

So also (4) is proven.
∎

It is now straightforward to prove convergence of the solutions
\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon,\delta}
to a weak solution that has all the properties mentioned in Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
\varepsilon_{n},\delta_{n}\rightarrow 0
and let us set
f^{(n)}=\tilde{f}^{\varepsilon_{n},\delta_{n}}
. After chosing an appropriate subsequence, we can assume that
\partial_{t}f^{(n)}
converges to
\partial_{t}f
weakly in
L^{2}
. Using that the solutions
f^{(n)}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}W^{2,p}
, the compact embedding
W^{2,p}\hookrightarrow L^{2}
and a standard diagonal sequence argument, we can furthermore assume after going to a subsequence that
f^{(n)}
converges in
L^{2}
for all times
t\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,\infty)
. Due to the uniform control of the Hölder constant (4) in Lemma
4.2
, this subsequence then also converges locally in
L^{\infty}L^{2}
to
f:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Interpolating once more, using that the
W^{2,p}
-norm of the curves is uniformly bounded, we also get convergence in
L^{\infty}W^{1,\infty}
locally in time.
If we integrate over the interpolation estimate

\|\partial_{s}^{2}(f^{(n)}-f^{(m)})\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(\|\partial_{s}^{2}f^{(n)}-\partial_{s}^{2}f^{(m)}\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2p}}}^{\theta}\|\tau_{n}-\tau_{m}\|^{1-\theta}_{L^{p}}+\|\tau_{n}-\tau_{m}\|_{L^{p}})

and use property (2) of Lemma
4.2
, we see that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\kappa_{n}-\kappa_{m}|^{p}dsdt\leq C(T+1)\|\tau_{n}-\tau_{m}\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{p})}\rightarrow 0

as
n
and
m
go to
\infty
.
This implies that the
f^{(n)}
even converge in
L^{p}W^{2,p}
to
f
locally in time. Furthermore, we know from property (1) of Lemma
4.2
that

\varepsilon_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}dsdt\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{5}}_{n}\rightarrow 0.

From the evolution equation we see that for all test functions
V\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\partial_{t}^{\bot}f^{(n)},V\rangle dxdt=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\varepsilon\delta_{V}\mathcal{F}(f^{(n)})+\delta_{V}\mathcal{E}^{(p)}_{\delta}(f^{(n)})+\lambda\delta_{V}\mathcal{L}(f^{(n)}))dt.

(4.4)

As
\partial_{t}f^{(n)}
converges weakly to
\partial_{t}f
in
L^{2}
,
\tau_{n}
converges strongly to
\tau
in
L^{\infty}
, and
\partial_{t}^{\bot}f^{(n)}=\partial_{t}f^{(n)}-\langle\partial_{t}f^{(n)},\tau_{n}\rangle\tau_{n}

\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle\partial_{t}^{\bot}f^{(n)},V\rangle dxdt\rightarrow\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle\partial_{t}^{\bot}f,V\rangle dxdt

as
n
goes to
\infty.
The first variation of
\varepsilon\mathcal{F}
is given by

\varepsilon\delta_{V}\mathcal{F}(f^{(n)})=\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\langle\nabla_{s}\kappa_{n},\delta_{V}(\nabla_{s}\kappa_{n})\rangle ds+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa_{n}|^{2}\langle\tau,\partial_{s}V\rangle ds

\delta_{V}(\nabla_{s}\kappa)=(\delta_{V}(\nabla_{s}\kappa))^{\bot}=(\partial_{s}^{3}V)^{\bot}+\partial_{s}^{2}V\ast\tau\ast\kappa+\partial_{s}V\ast(\tau\ast\partial_{s}\kappa+\kappa\ast\kappa+\kappa\ast\kappa\ast\tau).

Hence,

\displaystyle\varepsilon|\delta_{V}\mathcal{F}(f^{(n)})|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon C(\|\partial^{3}_{s}f^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial^{3}_{s}f^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}(\|\kappa_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{4}}+\|\kappa_{n}\|_{L^{2}}))

\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta_{V}\mathcal{F}(f^{(n)})dt\rightarrow 0

(4.5)

as

goes to
\infty
.

Standard monomial theory and toric degenerations of Richardson varieties in the Grassmannian
Narasimha Chary Bonala, Oliver Clarke and Fatemeh Mohammadi

Abstract
Richardson varieties are obtained as intersections of Schubert and opposite Schubert varieties. We provide a new family of toric degenerations of Richardson varieties inside Grassmannians by studying Gröbner degenerations of their corresponding ideals. These degenerations are parametrised by block diagonal matching fields in the sense of Sturmfels-Zelevinsky [ 33 ] . We associate a weight vector to each block diagonal matching field and study its corresponding initial ideal. In particular, we characterise when such ideals are toric, hence providing a family of toric degenerations for Richardson varieties. Given a Richardson variety X w v X_{w}^{v} and a weight vector 𝐰 ℓ {\bf w}_{\ell} arising from a matching field, we consider two ideals: an ideal G k , n , ℓ | w v G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v} obtained by restricting the initial of the Plücker ideal to a smaller polynomial ring, and a toric ideal defined as the kernel of a monomial map ϕ ℓ | w v \phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v} . We first characterise the monomial-free ideals of form G k , n , ℓ | w v G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v} . Then we construct a family of tableaux in bijection with semi-standard Young tableaux which leads to a monomial basis for the corresponding quotient ring. Finally, we prove that when G k , n , ℓ | w v G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v} is monomial-free and the initial ideal in ( I ( X w v ) ) 𝐰 ℓ {}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v})) is quadratically generated, then all three ideals in ( I ( X w v ) ) 𝐰 ℓ {}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v})) , G k , n , ℓ | w v G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v} and ker ( ϕ ℓ | w v ) (\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v}) coincide, and provide a toric degeneration of X w v X_{w}^{v} .

Introduction

Toric degenerations are a particularly useful tool for describing algebraic properties of varieties in terms of combinatorics of polytopes and polyhedral fans, see
[
2
]
.
The goal of this paper is to construct a family of toric degenerations for Richardson varieties inside the Grassmannian. To do this, we will consider a family of matching fields, which were originally introduced by Sturmfels and Zelevinsky for studying certain Newton polytopes
[
33
]
. We study Gröbner degenerations of the Plücker ideals associated to matching fields.

Toric degenerations of Grassmannians and Schubert varieties have been extensively studied in the literature, see e.g.
[
18
,
6
,
16
,
32
,
30
,
3
]
. In particular, most of these degenerations can be realised as Gröbner degenerations, even though, this is not true in general, see e.g.
[
22
]
.
More recently, Kaveh and Manon
[
23
]
used tools from tropical geometry to study toric degenerations of ideals. The main idea is that the initial ideals associated to the points in the top-dimensional facets of tropicalizations of ideals are good candidates for toric degenerations. A similar approach has been taken in studying toric degenerations of Grassmannians and Schubert varieties in
[
28
,
11
,
24
,
5
,
31
]
.

Let
k\leq n
be positive integers and let
{\bf I}_{k,n}=\{I\subseteq[n]:|I|=k\}
be the set of
k

[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
. The Grassmannian
\Gr(k,n)
is the collection of

\mathbb{K}^{n}

\Gr(k,n)

The intersection of Schubert and opposite Schubert varieties in
\Gr(k,n)
give rise to Richardson varieties which are indexed by pairs of subsets
v,w\in{\bf I}_{k,n}
where
v\leq w
.
An explicit description of the ideal of a Richardson variety is given by Kreiman and Lakshmibai
[
25
]
. We view this ideal as the
restriction
of
G_{k,n}
to the variables
P_{I}
where
I
is in the set

T^{v}_{w}=\{I\in{\bf I}_{k,n}:v\leq I\leq w\}
. More specifically, the ideal of the Richardson variety

is defined as

I(X_{w}^{v}):=G_{k,n}|_{T_{w}^{v}}=\left(G_{k,n}+\langle P_{I}:I\in{\bf I}_{k,n}\backslash T_{w}^{v}\rangle\right)\cap\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}.

Our goal is to find toric degenerations of the Richardson variety
X_{w}^{v}
by studying its Gröbner degenerations. We consider a collection of weight vectors
{\bf w}_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{n}{k}}
associated to so-called
block diagonal matching fields
, see Definition
2.4
and Remark
2.10
. In
[
11
]
, it is shown that each ideal
\init_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(G_{k,n})
is toric and equals to the kernel of a monomial map
\phi_{\ell}
,
see Equation (

) and Theorem

.
In particular, each vector
{\bf w}_{\ell}
gives rise to a flat family over

whose fiber over

\init_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(G_{k,n})
, see e.g.
[

.
Here, for the Richardson variety we project the weight vector
{\bf w}_{\ell}
to the coordinates corresponding to the variables in the polynomial ring

\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}

and study the
following question. Note that this is related to
Degeneration Problem

[
6
]
for Schubert varieties.

2.1 Grassmannian varieties.

The Grassmannian
\Gr(k,n)
is the collection of all
k
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
, which is embedded into a projective space
as follows. Any point in the Grassmannian is the rowspan of some
k\times n
matrix and two
k\times n
matrices and have the same rowspan if and only if they have the same sequence of maximal minors up to a scalar. And so we obtain an embedding of
\Gr(k,n)
into a projective space

\displaystyle\Gr(k,n)\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm with}\hskip 10.00002ptX=(x_{i,j})\mapsto(\det(X_{I})),

(2.1)

where
X_{I}
is the submatrix of
X
with columns indexed by
I
. The Plücker embedding gives rise to a defining ideal for the Grassmannian. Consider the map

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}:\ \mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in{\bf I}_{k,n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}[x_{i,j}]\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm with}\hskip 10.00002ptP_{I}\mapsto\det(X_{I})

(2.2)

where
X_{I}
is the submatrix of
X=(x_{i,j})
with columns indexed by
I
. The kernel of the map
\varphi_{n}
is called the
Plücker ideal of
\Gr(k,n)
and we denote it by
G_{k,n}
.

2.2 Schubert and opposite Schubert varieties.

Schubert and opposite Schubert varieties are families of closed subvarieties of the Grassmannian
\Gr(k,n)
which are indexed by
{\bf I}_{k,n}
. Note that there are a few equivalent ways to define Schubert varieties, see e.g.
[
26
]
. We consider the classical construction for these varieties as follows. Fix a basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}
and for each
i\in[k]
define the subspaces
W_{i}=\langle e_{1},\dots,e_{i}\rangle
and

W^{i}=\langle e_{n},\dots,e_{n-i+1}\rangle
. For sets
v=\{v_{1}<\dots<v_{k}\}
and
w=\{w_{1}<\dots<w_{k}\}\in{\bf I}_{k,n}
we define the corresponding Schubert variety
X_{w}
and opposite Schubert variety
X^{v}
to be

X_{w}=\left\{V\in\Gr(k,n):\dim(V\cap W_{w_{j}})\geq j\textrm{ for all }j\in[k]\right\},\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm and}

X^{v}=\left\{V\in\Gr(k,n):\dim(V\cap W^{n-v_{(k-j+1)}+1})\geq j\textrm{ for all }j\in[k]\right\}.

Note that the sets
X_{w}
and
X^{v}
are closed subvarieties of
\Gr(k,n)
.
There is a natural partial order on
{\bf I}_{k,n}
given by
\{v_{1}<\dots<v_{k}\}\leq\{w_{1}<\dots<w_{k}\}
if and only if
v_{1}\leq w_{1},\dots,v_{k}\leq w_{k}
. This partial order allows us to see a number of properties of Schubert varieties.

. Then the following hold:

1.

\langle e_{v_{1}},\dots,e_{v_{k}}\rangle\in X_{w}
if and only if
v\leq w
.

2.

X_{v}\subseteq X_{w}
if and only if
v\leq w
.

3.

For all
I\in{\bf I}_{k,n}
, the function
P_{I}|_{X_{w}}
is non-zero if and only if
I\leq w
.

Moreover, the Schubert variety
X_{w}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{\binom{n}{k}-1}
is precisely the zero set of the polynomials in the ideal
G_{k,n}+\langle P_{I}:I\nleq w\rangle
.
It is often convenient to think of the ideal of the Schubert variety as a subset of the ring
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\leq w}
.

Analogous statements hold for the opposite Schubert variety
X^{v}
whose ideal
is given by
G_{k,n}\cup\langle P_{I}:I\ngeq v\rangle
. Similarly,
we think of the ideal of
X^{v}
as a subset of the ring
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\geq v}
.

2.3 Richardson varieties.

Fix
k\leq n
positive integers and let
v,w\in{\bf I}_{k,n}
. The Richardson variety
X_{w}^{v}
associated to
v,w

is defined as
X_{w}\cap X^{v}
. We recall that
X_{w}^{v}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
v\leq w
, see
[
25
, Corollary 2.1.2]
.
To fix our notation we define the set

T^{v}_{w}=\{I\in{\bf I}_{k,n}:v\leq I\leq w\}.

The associated ideal of
X_{w}^{v}
is the restriction of
G_{k,n}
to the ring
K[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}
or equivalently it is the sum of the ideals of the corresponding Schubert and opposite Schubert varieties:

\displaystyle I(X_{w}^{v})

\displaystyle=G_{k,n}|_{T^{v}_{w}}:=(G_{k,n}+\langle P_{I}:I\in{\bf I}_{k,n}\backslash T^{v}_{w}\rangle)\cap\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T^{v}_{w}}

\displaystyle=(I(X_{w})+I(X^{v}))\cap\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T^{v}_{w}}.

Schubert and opposite Schubert varieties are special examples of Richardson varieties, namely

X_{w}=X_{w}^{id}
and

X^{v}=X_{w_{0}}^{v}
where
w_{0}=\{n-k+1,\dots,n-1,n\}
is the largest element of
{\bf I}_{k,n}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Richardson varieties are defined more generally for quotients of semi-simple algebraic groups
G
by parabolic subgroups
P\subseteq G
in
[
27
]
. When
G
is of type
A_{n}
, they are indexed by permutations
v,w\in S_{n}
. In the classical formulation above for the Grassmannian, the parabolic subgroups are maximal. In this case, the permutations
v,w
giving rise to distinct Richardson varieties can be taken to be pairs of
Grassmann permutations
, which are left coset representatives of
S_{n}/(S_{k}\times S_{n-k})
. These coset representatives can be identified with subsets
I\in{\bf I}_{k,n}
. We can think of
I
as a permutation which sends
[k]
to
I
.

2.4 Standard monomial basis.

The Plücker algebra of the Grassmannian
\Gr(k,n)
is the quotient of the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in{\bf I}_{k,n}}
by the Plücker ideal
G_{k,n}
.
In
[
21
]
, Hodge provided a combinatorial rule to choose a monomial basis for the Plücker algebra in terms of
semi-standard Young tableaux
. A monomial
P=P_{I_{1}}P_{I_{2}}\dots P_{I_{d}}
is called
standard
if
I_{1}\leq\dots\leq I_{d}
. The monomial
P
is called
standard for
X_{w}^{v}
if
P
is standard and
v\leq I_{i}\leq w
for all

.
It is convenient to write monomials as rectangular tableaux whose columns correspond to factors of the monomial. For example, the corresponding tableau of the monomial
P_{I}P_{J}
with
I=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\}
and
J=\{j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\}
is the following:

I

J

i_{1}

j_{1}

i_{2}

j_{2}

\vdots

\vdots

i_{k}

j_{k}

The tableaux corresponding to standard monomials are known as
semi-standard Young tableaux
. These tableaux are defined by the property that the entries in each column are strictly increasing and the entries in each row are weakly increasing.
We recall that:

Theorem 2.3

(Theorem 3.3.2
[

]
)
.

The standard monomials for

give a monomial basis for the associated algebra
of the Richardson variety
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T^{v}_{w}}/I(X_{w}^{v})
.

2.5 Matching fields.

A matching field is a combinatorial object that encodes a weight vector for the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[P_{J}]_{I\in{\bf I}_{k,n}}
which is
induced
from a weight vector for the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[x_{i,j}]
. Here, we recall
block diagonal
matching fields from
[
28
,
11
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Given integers
k,n
and

0\leq\ell\leq n
, we fix the
k\times n
matrix
M_{\ell}
with entries:

\displaystyle M_{\ell}(i,j)=\begin{cases}(i-1)(n-j+1)&\textrm{if }i\neq 2,\\
\ell-j+1&\textrm{if }i=2,1\leq j\leq\ell,\\
n-j+\ell+1&\textrm{if }i=2,\ell<j\leq n.\\
\end{cases}

(2.3)

Recall that
X=(x_{i,j})
is a
k\times n
matrix of indeterminates. For each
k
-subset
J
of
[n]
, the initial term of the Plücker form
\varphi_{n}(P_{J})\in\mathbb{K}[x_{i,j}]
denoted by
\operatorname{in}_{M_{\ell}}(P_{J})
is the sum of all terms in
\varphi_{n}(P_{J})
of the lowest weight, where the weight of a monomial
\bf m
is the sum of entries in
M_{\ell}
corresponding to the variables in
\bf m
. We write
M_{\ell}(\textbf{m})
for the weight of
m
. We prove below that
\operatorname{in}_{M_{\ell}}(P_{J})
is a monomial for each subset
J\subseteq[n]
.
The weight of each variable
P_{J}
is defined as the weight of each term of
\textrm{in}_{M_{\ell}}(P_{J})
with respect to
M_{\ell}
, and it is called
the weight induced by
M_{\ell}
. We write
{\mathbf{w}}_{\ell}
for the weight vector induced by

.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
M=(m_{i,j})
and
M^{\prime}=(m^{\prime}_{i,j})
be
k\times n
weight matrices. Suppose there exists
p\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
m_{i,j}=m^{\prime}_{i,j}
for all
i\in[k]
and
j\in[n]\backslash p
. Suppose that there exists
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
m^{\prime}_{i,p}=m_{i,p}+c
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Then the initial terms of the Plücker forms are the same with respect to
M
and
M^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
J
be a
k
-subset of
[n]
. If
J
does not contain
p
then the submatrices of
M
and
M^{\prime}
with columns indexed by
J
coincide, hence the initial terms of the Plücker form
\varphi_{n}(P_{J})
with respect to
M
and
M^{\prime}
are the same. On the other hand, if
J
contains
p
then consider each monomial
x
in the Plücker form
\varphi_{n}(P_{J})
. The monomial is squarefree and contains a unique variable of the form
x_{i,p}
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Therefore,
M^{\prime}(\textbf{x})=M(\textbf{x})+c
. Therefore, the initial term of
\varphi_{n}(P_{J})
is the same with respect to
M
and
M^{\prime}
.

∎

By the same method, one can prove an analogous result for weight matrices which differ by a constant in a particular row.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
M=M_{0}
be the
k\times n
weight matrix and
J

be a
k
-subset. Then the initial term
{\rm in}_{M}(P_{J})

is the leading diagonal term, in particular, it is a monomial.

Proof.

We show that the leading diagonal term of the Plücker form
\varphi_{n}(P_{J})
i.e.
x_{1,j_{1}}x_{2,j_{2}}\cdots x_{k,j_{k}}
where
j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{k}
equals to
\textrm{in}_{M}(P_{J})
. We proceed by induction on
k
. For
k=1
the result holds trivially. So assume
k>1
. We have

\varphi_{n}(P_{J})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\cdots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}}.

For each
\sigma\in S_{k}
such that
x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\cdots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}}
has minimum weight with respect to
M
, consider the value
\sigma(k)\in[k]
.
Suppose
\sigma(k)=p
for some
p\in[k]
. Then, by induction, we have that the leading term of the
\varphi_{n}(P_{J\backslash j_{p}})
is the leading diagonal term. So
\sigma(1)=1,\dots,\sigma(p-1)={p-1},\sigma(p)={p+1},\dots,\sigma(k-1)=k
and
\sigma(k)=p
, therefore, the weight of the monomial is

\displaystyle M(x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\cdots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}(i-1)(n-j_{i}+1)+\sum_{i=p+1}^{k}(i-2)(n-j_{i}+1)+(k-1)(n-j_{p}+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(i-1)(n-j_{i}+1)-\sum_{i=p+1}^{k}(n-j_{i}+1)+(k-p)(n-j_{p}+1)

\displaystyle=M(x_{1,j_{1}}\cdots x_{k,j_{k}})+\sum_{i=p+1}^{k}j_{i}-(k-p)j_{p}.

Note that
j_{p}<j_{p+1}<\dots<j_{k}
. So
\sum_{i=p+1}^{k}j_{i}-(k-p)j_{p}>0
. If
\sigma(k)<k
then the weight
M(x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\cdots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}})
is not minimum. So
\sigma(k)=k
and we are done by induction.

∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
,
k\geq 2
,
M=M_{\ell}
, the
k\times n
weight matrix, and
J=\{j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\}\subset[n]
.
Then the initial term of the Plücker form
\varphi_{n}(P_{J})
is given by

{\rm in}_{M}(P_{J})=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{@{}ll}$x_{1,j_{1}}x_{2,j_{2}}x_{3,j_{3}}\dots x_{k,j_{k}}$&if $j_{1}>\ell$ or $j_{2}\leq\ell$,\\
$x_{1,j_{2}}x_{2,j_{1}}x_{3,j_{3}}\dots x_{k,j_{k}}$&otherwise.\end{tabular}\right.

In particular, the leading term
\textrm{in}_{M}(P_{J})
is a monomial.

Proof.

Suppose that
j_{1}>\ell
. By definition, the weight matrices
M_{\ell}
and
M_{0}
differ only in the second row. The entries of the second row are

\displaystyle(M_{0}):

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt[n\ n-1\ \dots\ 1],

\displaystyle(M_{\ell}):

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt[\ell\ \ell-1\ \dots\ 1\ n\ n-1\ \dots\ \ell+1].

Consider the submatrices of
M_{0}
and
M_{\ell}
consisting of the columns indexed by
J
. Since
j_{1}>\ell
the second row entries differ by exactly
\ell
in each respective position. And so by the row-version of Lemma
2.5
, the leading term of the Plücker form
\varphi_{n}(P_{J})
is the same with respect to
M_{0}
and
M_{\ell}
. By Proposition
2.6
, the initial term is
\textrm{in}_{M}(P_{J})=x_{1,j_{1}}x_{2,j_{2}}x_{3,j_{3}}\dots x_{k,j_{k}}
.

Suppose that
j_{1}\leq\ell
.
We will prove the result by induction on
k
. For the case
k=2
,

M_{\ell}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\dots&0&0&0&\dots&0\\
\ell&\ell-1&\dots&1&n&n-1&\dots&\ell+1\end{bmatrix}.

If
j_{1}>\ell
or
j_{2}\leq\ell
then the leading term of the Plücker form
\varphi_{n}(P_{J})
is the leading diagonal term, i.e.
\textrm{in}_{M}(P_{J})=x_{1,j_{1}}x_{2,j_{2}}
. Otherwise we have
j_{1}\leq\ell
and
j_{2}>\ell
, and so the leading term of the Plücker form is the antidiagonal term, i.e.
\textrm{in}_{M}(P_{J})=x_{1,j_{2}}x_{2,j_{1}}
.

Suppose
k>2
. For each
\sigma\in S_{k}
such that
x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\dots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}}
has minimum weight with respect to
M_{\ell}
, consider the value
p=\sigma(k)\in[k]
. Let
J^{\prime}=J\backslash j_{p}=\{j_{1}^{\prime}<j_{2}^{\prime}<\dots<j_{k-1}^{\prime}\}
. There are two cases for
J^{\prime}
, either
j_{2}^{\prime}\leq\ell
or
j_{2}^{\prime}>\ell
.

Case 1.
Assume
j_{2}^{\prime}\leq\ell
. By induction we have
\textrm{in}_{M}(P_{J^{\prime}})=x_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}}x_{2,j_{2}^{\prime}}\dots x_{k-1,j_{k-1}^{\prime}}
. And so we have
\sigma(1)=1,\dots,\sigma(p-1)=p-1,\sigma(p)=p+1,\dots,\sigma(k-1)=k,\sigma(k)=p
. Suppose by contradiction that
p\leq k-1
, then we have

M_{\ell}(x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\dots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}})-M_{\ell}(x_{1,j_{1}}\dots x_{k,j_{k}})=\sum_{i=p}^{k}\left(M_{\ell}(x_{i,j_{\sigma(i)}})-M_{\ell}(x_{i,j_{i}})\right)=\sum_{i=p}^{k}(i-1)(j_{i}-j_{\sigma(i)})

=\left(\sum_{i=p}^{k-1}(i-1)(j_{i}-j_{i+1})\right)+(k-1)(j_{k}-j_{p})=\sum_{i=p+1}^{k}(j_{i}-j_{p})>0.

But by assumption
x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\dots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}}
has minimum weight, a contradiction. And so we have
p=k
hence
\textrm{in}_{M}(P_{J})=x_{1,j_{1}}x_{2,j_{2}}\dots x_{k,j_{k}}
.

Case 2.
Assume
j_{2}^{\prime}>\ell
. Either we have
j_{1}^{\prime}\leq\ell
or
j_{1}^{\prime}>\ell
. In this case assume further that
j_{1}^{\prime}\leq\ell
, we will show that
j_{1}^{\prime}>\ell
is impossible in Case 3. By induction we have
\textrm{in}_{M}(P_{J^{\prime}})=x_{1,j_{2}^{\prime}}x_{2,j_{1}^{\prime}}x_{3,j_{3}^{\prime}}\dots x_{k-1,j_{k-1}^{\prime}}
. Assume by contradiction that
k\neq p
. We proceed by taking cases on
p
, either
p=1
,
p=2
or
3\leq p\leq k-1
.

Case 2.1
Assume
p=1
. So we have
\sigma(1)=3,\sigma(2)=2,\sigma(3)=4,\dots,\sigma(k-1)=k,\sigma(k)=1
. Since
j_{p}<j_{1}^{\prime}\leq\ell
, we have

M_{\ell}(x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\dots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}})-M_{\ell}(x_{1,j_{1}}\dots x_{k,j_{k}})=\left(\sum_{i=4}^{k}(j_{i}-j_{1})\right)+2(j_{3}-j_{1})>0.

But by assumption
x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\dots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}}
has minimum weight, a contradiction.

Case 2.2.
Assume
p=2
. So we have
\sigma(1)=3,\sigma(2)=1,\sigma(3)=4,\dots,\sigma(k-1)=k,\sigma(k)=2
. Since
j_{p}<j_{1}^{\prime}\leq\ell
, we have

M_{\ell}(x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\dots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}})-M_{\ell}(x_{1,j_{1}}\dots x_{k,j_{k}})=\left(\sum_{i=4}^{k}(j_{i}-j_{2})\right)+(j_{3}-j_{2})+(j_{3}-j_{1})>0.

But by assumption
x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\dots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}}
has minimum weight, a contradiction.

Case 2.3.
Assume
3\leq p\leq k-1
. And so we have
\sigma(1)=2,\sigma(2)=1,\sigma(3)=3,\dots,\sigma(p-1)=p-1,\sigma(p)=p+1,\dots,\sigma(k-1)=k,\sigma(k)=p
. Therefore,

M_{\ell}(x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\dots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}})-M_{\ell}(x_{1,j_{2}}x_{2,j_{1}}x_{3,j_{3}}\dots x_{k,j_{k}})=\sum_{i=p+1}^{k}(j_{i}-j_{p})>0.

But by assumption
x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\dots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}}
has minimum weight, a contradiction.

Case 3.
Assume
j_{1}^{\prime},j_{2}^{\prime}>\ell
. By induction, we have
\textrm{in}_{M}(P_{J^{\prime}})=x_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}}x_{2,j_{2}^{\prime}}\dots x_{k-1,j_{k-1}^{\prime}}
. Since
j_{1}\leq\ell
we must have
j_{1}^{\prime}=j_{2},\dots j_{k-1}^{\prime}=j_{k}
and so
\sigma(1)=2
,
\sigma(2)=3,\dots,

\sigma(k-1)=k
and
\sigma(k)=1
. Therefore,

M_{\ell}(x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\dots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}})-M_{\ell}(x_{1,j_{2}}x_{2,j_{1}}x_{3,j_{3}}\dots x_{k,j_{k}})=\left(\sum_{i=3}^{k}(j_{i}-j_{1})\right)+j_{3}+n>0.

But by assumption
x_{1,j_{\sigma(1)}}\dots x_{k,j_{\sigma(k)}}
has minimum weight, a contradiction.

∎

2.6 Matching field ideals.

A matching field also admits the data of a monomial map

Lemma 3.5
.

We have the following:

(i)

The ideal

(ii)

The ideal
G_{k,n,0}|^{v}
is zero if and only if
w_{0}v\in Z_{k,n}
,

(iii)

If
\ell=0

\ell>n-k+1

G_{k,n,\ell}|^{v}
is monomial-free.

Proof.

(i)
This statement follows directly from
[
11

.

(ii)
For the first statement, note that
G_{k,n,0}|^{v}
is non-zero if and only if there exists a binomial
P_{I}P_{J}-P_{I^{\prime}}P_{J^{\prime}}

and all binomials can be written in this form since

. Therefore,
G_{k,n,0}|^{v}
is non-zero if and only if
G_{k,n,0}|_{w_{0}v}
is non-zero.

(iii)
This statement is a consequence of part (i) and the bijection between binomials described above. If
G_{k,n,\ell}|^{v}
contains a monomial

then there exists a binomial
P_{I}P_{J}-P_{I^{\prime}}P_{J^{\prime}}
in

such that

and

. By Lemma

,
w_{0}I,w_{0}J\leq w_{0}v
and
w_{0}I^{\prime},w_{0}J^{\prime}>w_{0}v
. Since
P_{w_{0}I}P_{w_{0}J}-P_{w_{0}I^{\prime}}P_{w_{0}J^{\prime}}
is also a binomial in
\init_{{\bf w}_{0}}(G_{k,n})
, therefore,
P_{w_{0}I}P_{w_{0}J}
is a monomial in
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w_{0}v}
, which contradicts part (i).

∎

4
Standard monomials for Richardson varieties

In this section, we
provide a bijection between the semi-standard Young tableaux with two columns and the set of standard monomials for
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
of degree two.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
T
be a semi-standard Young tableau with two columns and
k
rows whose entries lie in
[n]
, see §
2.4
. For each
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
we define the map
\Gamma_{\ell}:T\mapsto T^{\prime}
where
T^{\prime}
is a tableau whose columns are ordered according to the matching field
B_{\ell}
. Suppose that the entries of the columns of
T
are
I=\{i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{k}\}
and
J=\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k}\}
. Since
T
is in a semi-standard form, we assume that
i_{s}\leq j_{s}
for each
s\in[k]
. We define
T^{\prime}
as the tableau whose columns are
I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
as sets and are ordered by the matching field
B_{\ell}
. The sets
I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
are defined as follows.

1.

If
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1}\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
,
j_{2}\in\{\ell+1,\dots,n\}
and
i_{1}<j_{1}<i_{2}
then we define
I^{\prime}=\{j_{1}<i_{2}<i_{3}<\dots<i_{k}\}
and
J^{\prime}=\{i_{1}<j_{2}<j_{3}<\dots<j_{k}\}
.

2.

If
i_{1}\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
,
i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\in\{\ell+1,\dots,n\}
and
j_{1}<i_{2}<j_{2}
then we define
I^{\prime}=\{j_{1}<i_{2}<i_{3}<\dots<i_{k}\}
and
J^{\prime}=\{i_{1}<j_{2}<j_{3}<\dots<j_{k}\}

3.

Otherwise we define
I^{\prime}=I
and
J^{\prime}=J
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
be semi-standard Young tableaux. If
\Gamma_{\ell}(T_{1})
and
\Gamma_{\ell}(T_{2})
are row-wise equal then
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are equal.

Proof.

We begin by noting that all rows except possibly the first two rows of a tableau are fixed by
\Gamma_{\ell}
. So it remains to show that if the first two rows of
\Gamma_{\ell}(T_{1})
and
\Gamma_{\ell}(T_{2})
are row-wise equal then so are the first two rows of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
. We also note that
\Gamma_{\ell}
preserves the entries of a tableau, thought of as a multi-set. Let us assume by contradiction that
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are not row-wise equal. By the above facts we may assume without loss of generality that

T_{1}=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$j_{1}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptT_{2}=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$i_{2}$\\
\hline\cr$j_{1}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}

and
j_{1}<i_{2}
. We proceed by taking cases on
s=|\{i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\}\cap\{1,\dots,\ell\}|
.

Case 1.
Assume
s=0
or
4
. It follows that
\Gamma_{\ell}
fixes
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
. By row-wise equality of the second row of
\Gamma_{\ell}(T_{1})
and
\Gamma_{\ell}(T_{2})
we have that
j_{1}=i_{2}
, a contradiction.

Case 2.
Assume
s=1
. It follows that
i_{1}\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
and
i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\in\{\ell+1,\dots,n\}
. Since
j_{1}<i_{2}
we have

\Gamma_{\ell}(T_{1})=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$j_{1}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$i_{1}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\Gamma_{\ell}(T_{2})=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$j_{1}$&$i_{2}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,.

By row-wise equality of the second row, we have that
j_{2}=i_{2}
. However, in the tableau
T_{2}
, we have that
i_{2}<j_{2}
, a contradiction.

Case 3.
Assume
s=2
. Since
j_{1}<i_{2}
, it follows that
i_{1},j_{1}\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
and
i_{2},j_{2}\in\{\ell+1,\dots,n\}
. And so we have

\Gamma_{\ell}(T_{1})=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$j_{1}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\Gamma_{\ell}(T_{2})=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$i_{2}$\\
\hline\cr$j_{1}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,.

By row-wise equality of the second row the tableau we have that
i_{1}=j_{2}
, a contradiction since
i_{1}\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
and
j_{2}\in\{\ell+1,\dots,n\}
.

Case 4.
Assume that
s=3
. It follows that
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1}\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
and
j_{2}\in\{\ell+1,\dots,n\}
. And so we have

\Gamma_{\ell}(T_{1})=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$j_{1}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$i_{1}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\Gamma_{\ell}(T_{2})=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr$j_{1}$&$i_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,.

By row-wise equality of the second row, we have that
i_{1}=j_{1}
. However, in
T_{2}
, we have that
i_{1}<j_{1}
, a contradiction.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
T
be any tableau whose columns are valid for the block diagonal matching field

. Then there exists a semi-standard Young tableau

such that
\Gamma_{\ell}(T^{\prime})
and

are row-wise equal.

Proof.

Let

be the tableau with entries
\{i_{1},i_{2}<i_{3}<\dots<i_{k}\}
and
\{j_{1},j_{2}<j_{3}<\dots<j_{k}\}
,

\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr$i_{k}$&$j_{k}$\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
i_{s}\leq j_{s}
for all
s\geq 3
. We proceed by taking cases on
s=|\{i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\}\cap\{1,\dots,\ell\}|
.

Case 1.
Assume
s=0
or
4
. We have that
i_{1}<i_{2}
and
j_{1}<j_{2}
. So we may order the entries in row to obtain
T^{\prime}
. Note that in this case
\Gamma_{\ell}
fixes

.

Case 2.
Assume
s=1
. Without loss of generality we assume
j_{2}\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
.

1.

If

then

\Gamma_{\ell}\left(\,\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$j_{2}$&$i_{1}$\\

\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,\right)=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$j_{1}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,.

2.

If
j_{1}\leq i_{2}
then

\Gamma_{\ell}\left(\,\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$j_{2}$&$i_{1}$\\
\hline\cr$j_{1}$&$i_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,\right)=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$j_{1}$&$i_{1}$\\
\hline\cr$j_{2}$&$i_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,.

The tableau on the right is row-wise equal to
T
.

Case 3.
Assume
s=2
.

1.

If
i_{1},i_{2}\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
then
\Gamma_{\ell}
fixes each column of

, which is a semi-standard Young tableau.

2.

If
i_{2},j_{2}\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
then without loss of generality assume
i_{2}\leq j_{2}
and
i_{1}\leq j_{1}
. We have

\Gamma_{\ell}\left(\,\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$j_{1}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,\right)=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$j_{1}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,.

Case 4.
Assume
s=3
. Without loss of generality we may assume
j_{1}\in\{\ell+1,\dots,n\}
.

1.

If
j_{2}<i_{1}
then

\Gamma_{\ell}\left(\,\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$j_{2}$&$i_{1}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$j_{1}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,\right)=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$j_{1}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,.

Note that in this case we have

and so the tableau on the left is a semi-standard Young tableau.

2.

If
j_{2}\geq i_{1}
then

\Gamma_{\ell}\left(\,\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$j_{1}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,\right)=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$j_{1}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,.

This completes the proof.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
v,w\in{\bf I}_{k,n}
with
v\leq w
. A semi-standard Young tableau
T
vanishes in
G_{k,n,0}|_{w}^{v}
if and only if
\Gamma_{\ell}(T)
vanishes in
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
.

Proof.

Let
I,J
be the columns of
T
and
I^{\prime},J^{\prime}
be the columns of
\Gamma_{\ell}(T)
. The result follows from the fact that
\{\min(I),\min(J)\}=\{\min(I^{\prime}),\min(J^{\prime})\}
and similarly for the second smallest of elements of
I,J,I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
.

∎

By the results of Kreiman and Lakshmibai in
[
25
]
, the semi-standard Young tableaux whose columns
I
satisfy
v\leq I\leq w
are a monomial basis for the Richardson variety
X_{w}^{v}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

If
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
is monomial-free then the set

{\rm Im}(\Gamma_{\ell})|_{w}^{v}=\{\Gamma_{\ell}(T):T\textrm{ a two column semi-standard Young tableau for }X_{w}^{v}\}

is a monomial basis for
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
in degree two.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive, i.e. if
\textrm{Im}(\Gamma_{\ell})|_{w}^{v}
is not a monomial basis for
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
then
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
contains a monomial. Let
T
be a matching field tableau for
B_{\ell}
representing a monomial in
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
which does not lie in the span of
\textrm{Im}(\Gamma_{\ell})|_{w}^{v}
. Since
\textrm{Im}(\Gamma_{\ell})
is a basis for
G_{k,n,\ell}
, it follows that
T
is row-wise equal to
\Gamma_{\ell}(T^{\prime})
for some semi-standard Young tableau
T^{\prime}
which vanishes in
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
. We write
I,J
for the columns of
T
and
I^{\prime},J^{\prime}
for the columns of
\Gamma_{\ell}(T^{\prime})
. Since

T
and
\Gamma_{\ell}(T^{\prime})
are row-wise equal we may assume that all their entries below the second row are in semi-standard form. So we write

T=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\lx@intercol\hfil$I$\hfil\lx@intercol &\lx@intercol\hfil$J$\hfil\lx@intercol \\
\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$j_{1}$\\
\hline\cr$i_{2}$&$j_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\Gamma_{\ell}(T^{\prime})=\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\lx@intercol\hfil$I^{\prime}$\hfil\lx@intercol &\lx@intercol\hfil$J^{\prime}$\hfil\lx@intercol \\
\hline\cr$i_{1}$&$j_{1}$\\
\hline\cr$j_{2}$&$i_{2}$\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,.

Throughout the proof we write
v=\{v_{1}<\dots<v_{k}\}
and
w=\{w_{1}<\dots<w_{k}\}
for the Grassmannian permutations. We now take cases on
s=|\{i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\}\cap\{1,\dots,\ell\}|
.

Case 1.
Assume
s=0
or
4
. It follows that
\Gamma_{\ell}(T^{\prime})
is a semi-standard Young tableau and so
\Gamma_{\ell}(T^{\prime})
does not vanish, a contradiction.

Assume
s=1
. Without loss of generality assume that
j_{2}\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
and note that in this case we may possibly have that
I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
are swapped in

. Since

does not vanish we have

. So by ordering the entries of

and

, we have

Since

vanishes we must have that either

or

vanishes. Let us take cases.

Case 2.1.
Assume

vanishes. We have

and so

because

3.3

we have that
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}

vanishes. We have

Hence

.

And so by Theorem
3.3

we have that
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
contains a monomial.

Case 3.
Assume
s=2
.
If
i_{1},i_{2}\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
then

is not a valid tableau with respect to the matching field

does not vanish in

, a contradiction.

Case 4.

s=3
. Without loss of generality assume that

Theorem 4.6
.

If
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
is monomial-free, then the size of
{\rm Im}(\Gamma_{\ell})|_{w}^{v}

is equal to the number of semi-standard Young tableaux with two columns
I,J
such that
v\leq I,J\leq w
.

Proof.

First note that a collection of standard monomials for
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/G_{k,n,0}|_{w}^{v}
in degree two is given by semi-standard Young tableaux with two columns such that each column
I
satisfies
v\leq I\leq w
. The map
\Gamma_{\ell}
from Definition
4.1
takes each semi-standard Young tableau with two columns to a degree two monomial in
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in{\bf I}_{k,n}}
. By Lemma
4.4
we have that the restriction of
\Gamma_{\ell}
to the standard monomials for
X_{w}^{v}
gives a well-defined map to monomials in the quotient ring
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
. By Lemma
4.2
and Proposition
4.5
we have that the restriction of
\Gamma_{\ell}
to the standard monomials of
X_{w}^{v}
is a bijection between standard monomials for
X_{w}^{v}
and a monomial basis for
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
.

∎

5
Toric degenerations of Richardson varieties

We are now ready to answer Question
1.1
and prove our main results,
given the complete characterisation of monomial-free ideals
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
in §
3
and the description of a monomial basis for
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
in §
4
.
In particular, we will show that when
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
is monomial-free and
\init_{\bf w_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
is quadratically generated, then
\init_{\bf w_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
provides a toric degeneration of the Richardson variety
X_{w}^{v}
. We expect that
\init_{\bf w_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
is always quadratically generated, see Conjecture
1.2
, Theorem
5.3
and
[
8
]
. Hence, assuming that Conjecture
1.2
holds, all the pairs
(v,w)
classified in Theorem
3.3
lead to toric degenerations of
X_{w}^{v}
.

The first step is to prove our main results is to show that the inclusions in (
1.2
) hold.

Lemma 5.1
.

We have the following:

(i)

The ideals
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
and
\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
coincide if and only if
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
is monomial-free.

(ii)

G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}\subseteq{\rm in}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
.

Proof.

We first note that by Theorem
3.1
the ideal
\init_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(G_{k,n})
is quadratically generated. We let
G
be a quadratic binomial generating set for
\init_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(G_{k,n})
.

(i) Note that
\phi_{n}|_{w}^{v}
is a monomial map, hence its kernel does not contain any monomials. So, if the ideal
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
contains a monomial then it is not equal to
\ker{(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})}
.
Now assume that the ideal
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
does not contain any monomials. By definition, we have
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}=\langle G|_{T_{w}^{v}}\rangle=\langle G\cup\{P_{J}:J\in{\bf I}_{k,n}\backslash T_{w}^{v}\}\rangle\cap\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}
. Since the ideal
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
is monomial-free, the set
G|_{T_{w}^{v}}
does not contain any monomials.
Moreover, since all binomials
m_{1}-m_{2}\in G|_{T_{w}^{v}}
lie in the ideal
\init_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(G_{k,n})
and contain only the non-vanishing Plücker variables
P_{J}
for
J\in T_{w}^{v}
, therefore,
m_{1}-m_{2}\in\ker{(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})}
. Thus
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}\subseteq\ker{(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})}
which complete the proof of (i).

(ii) Since
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}=\langle G|_{T_{w}^{v}}\rangle
, we take
\hat{g}\in G|_{T_{w}^{v}}
. We have that
g\in G\subseteq\init_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(G_{k,n})
and so
there exists
f\in G_{k,n}
such that
\init_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(f)=g
.
The terms of
\hat{g}
are precisely the non-vanishing terms of the initial terms of
f
. Thus
\hat{g}=\init_{\bf w}(\hat{f})\in{\rm in}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
, as desired.

∎

We now use the description of a monomial basis for the algebra
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
to show that the containment in Lemma
5.1
(ii) is indeed an equality.

Theorem 5.2
.

If
G_{k,n,\ell}|^{v}_{w}
is monomial-free and
\init_{\bf{w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
is quadratically generated, then the ideals
\init_{\bf{w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
,
G_{k,n,\ell}|^{v}_{w}
and
\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
are all equal.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
we have that
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}=\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})\subseteq{\rm in}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
. In particular, the inclusion
\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})\subseteq{\rm in}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
implies that for any collection of monomials
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}
, if
\mathcal{M}
is linearly independent in
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/{\rm in}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
then
\mathcal{M}
is linearly independent in
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
. So any standard monomial basis of degree
d
for
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/{\rm in}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
is linearly independent in
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
. Note that Gröbner degeneration gives rise to a flat family, and so the Hilbert polynomials of all fiber are identical. So by Theorem
2.3
, the dimension of degree
d
part of
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/{\rm in}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
is equal to the number of standard monomials for
X_{w}^{v}
of degree
d
. By Proposition
4.5

we have that

\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/{\rm in}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
and
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
have the same number of standard monomials in degree two. Since
\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})\subseteq{\rm in}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
it follows that
\textrm{Im}(\Gamma_{\ell})|_{w}^{v}
is a collection of standard monomials for
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/{\rm in}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
and
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v}
.
Since
G_{k,n,\ell}|_{w}^{v},{\rm in}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
and
\ker(\phi_{\ell}|_{w}^{v})
are all generated in degree two, it follows that they are equal.

∎

Theorem 5.3
.

For
\ell=0
, the ideals
G_{k,n,0}|_{w}^{v}
,
\init_{{\bf w}_{0}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
and
\ker(\phi_{0}|_{w}^{v})
are all equal. In particular, they are all quadratically generated toric ideals.

Proof.

First note that by Theorem
3.3
each ideal
G_{k,n,0}|_{w}^{v}
is monomial-free. By Theorem
2.3
the standard monomials for the Plücker algebra of the Richardson variety are in bijection with the rectangular semi-standard Young tableaux with columns
J
such that
v\leq J\leq w
.
To see that these tableaux form a monomial basis for
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\ker(\phi_{0}|_{w}^{v})
, observe that any two monomials in this algebra are equal if and only if their corresponding tableaux are row-wise equal. Also, for any tableau, there is a unique semi-standard Young tableau which is row-wise equal to it.
It follows that the dimension of the degree
d
part of
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\ker(\phi_{0}|_{w}^{v})
and
\mathbb{K}[P_{I}]_{I\in T_{w}^{v}}/\init_{{\bf w}_{0}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
are equal for all
d
. Moreover, by Lemma
5.1
we have that
\ker(\phi_{0}|_{w}^{v})=G_{k,n,0}|_{w}^{v}\subseteq\init_{{\bf w}_{0}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
. Hence, these ideals are all equal and, in particular, they are quadratically generated.

∎

Remark 5.4
.

In
[
8
]
, using
Macaulay2
we have calculated the initial ideals
{\rm in}_{{\bf w}_{\ell}}(I(X_{w}^{v}))
for all Richardson varieties inside
G_{k,n}
where
n\leq 7
and
k\leq n-2
.
We have observed that they are all quadratically generated. This
confirms that Conjecture
1.2
holds for
n\leq 7
.

We proceed by comparing our results to previous results in the literature. We highlight possible connections to other areas and future research directions.

Remark 5.5
.

In
[
4
]
, the authors study the degenerations of Schubert varieties inside the full flag variety built upon on the flat degeneration given by Feigin
[
17
]
. They give a number of sufficient conditions on the permutation
w\in S_{n}
such that the restriction of the degeneration to the Schubert variety
X_{w}
is reducible. Similarly to our methods, this is done by showing that the corresponding initial ideals contain monomials.
In
[
7
]
, we use the results of
[
12
]
to study the degenerations of Richardson varieties inside the flag variety.

Remark 5.6
.

In
[
9
]
, the authors showed that the polytopes associated to toric degenerations of the Grassmannian arising from matching fields, are related to each other by sequences of combinatorial mutations in the sense of
[
1
]
. We expect that the polytopes of toric degenerations of Richardson varieties provided here to have similar properties.

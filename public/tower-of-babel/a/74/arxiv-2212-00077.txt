An Identity relating Eisenstein series on general linear groups
Zahi Hazan

Abstract
We give a general identity relating Eisenstein series on general linear groups. We do it by constructing an Eisenstein series, attached to a maximal parabolic subgroup and a pair of representations, one cuspidal and the other a character, and express it in terms of a degenerate Eisenstein series. In the local fields analogue, we prove the convergence in a half plane of the local integrals, and their meromorphic continuation. In addition, we find that the unramified calculation gives the Godement-Jacquet zeta function. This realizes and generalizes the construction proposed by Ginzburg and Soudry in Section 3 in [ GS19 ] .

1.2
Background and motivation

Eisenstein series are key objects in the theory of automorphic forms. They are an important tool in the study of automorphic
L
-functions, and they figure out in the spectral decomposition of the
L^{2}
-space of automorphic forms. In recent years, new constructions of global integrals generating identities relating Eisenstein series were discovered.

In
[
GS18
]
Ginzburg and Soudry introduced two general identities relating Eisenstein series on split classical groups, as well as double covers of symplectic groups.
Basically, the idea of their theorem, for example, in case of symplectic group, is as follows.
Let
\tau
be an irreducible, automorphic, cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A})
. Denote by
\Delta(\tau,i)
the Speh representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{ni}(\mathbb{A})
of
“length”

i
corresponding to
\tau
. Let
\sigma
be an irreducible, automorphic, cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{Sp}_{m}(\mathbb{A})
, and
\sigma^{\iota}
a certain outer conjugation of
\sigma
by an element of order 2.
Their theorem shows that Eisenstein series parabolically induced from
\Delta(\tau,i)\left|\det\cdot\right|^{s}\times\sigma^{\iota}
can be expressed in terms of
“more degenerate”
Eisenstein series, namely Eisenstein series parabolically induced from the Speh representation
\Delta(\tau,m+i)\left|\det\right|^{s}
.
In particular they get that any Eisenstein series attached to an irreducible, cuspidal representation on a maximal parabolic subgroup can be expressed in terms of an Eisenstein series, attached to a Speh representation on a Siegel parabolic subgroup.
They generalized Mœglin’s work
[
Mœg97
]
which is an extension of
[
GPSR97
]
. The identity of Ginzburg and Soudry can also be viewed as an extension of the doubling construction introduced in
[
CFGK16
]
.
The second identity in
[
GS18
]
generalizes Ikeda’s work
[
Ike94
]
and can be viewed as a generalization of the descent construction studied in
[
GRS11
]
.

In this work we demonstrate the principle above by proving an identity relating Eisenstein series on general linear groups. Namely, we express an Eisenstein series, attached to a maximal parabolic subgroup and a pair of representations, one cuspidal and the other a character, in terms of a degenerate Eisenstein series.

The identity (
1.4
) has further applications. In an ongoing project, we explore several of these. For example, we use it to provide another proof that the Eisenstein series
\mathrm{E}_{m}\left(\xi\left(f_{\omega_{\pi},s},\varphi_{\pi}\right)\right)
is holomorphic, and also apply the aforementioned realization (see
1.1
) to certain Rankin-Selberg integrals.

&B\end{pmatrix}\right)\tilde{f}_{\tau,s}\left(g;aA,bB\right).

For fixed

We realize the space of

&B\end{pmatrix}g\right)=\delta_{P_{\ell,r}}^{s+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}A&X\\
&B\end{pmatrix}\right)\tilde{f}_{\tau,s}\left(g;A,B\right).

In particular,

f_{\tau,s}:\mathrm{S}_{\ell,r}(k)U_{\ell,r}(\mathbb{A})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{\ell+r}(\mathbb{A})\to\mathbb{C}.

There is a bijection between
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho_{\tau,s}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{K_{\ell+r}}}

\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{\ell,r}(\mathbb{A})\cap K_{\ell+r}}^{K_{\ell+r}}{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(1\otimes\tau\right)\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{P_{\ell,r}(\mathbb{A})\cap K_{\ell+r}}}

Kronecker product

Let

where

By

(1.16)

\displaystyle t(h,g)=t(h,I_{r})t(I_{\ell},g)=t(I_{\ell},g)t(h,I_{r}),

(1.17)

\displaystyle(t(h,g))^{T}=t(h^{T},g^{T}),\ \left|t(h,g)\right|=\left|{h}\right|^{r}\left|{g}\right|^{\ell},\ \mathrm{and}\ (t(h,g))^{*}=t(h^{*},g^{*}).

2.1
The double cosets
P_{mn-1,1}(k)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{mn}(k)/T_{m,n}(k)

As mentioned above, in this section we show that the set
P_{mn-1,1}(k)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{mn}(k)/T_{m,n}(k)
is finite and find an explicit set of representatives. Namely, we prove

Theorem 5
.

There are exactly
n
double cosets in
P_{mn-1,1}(k)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{mn}(k)/T_{m,n}(k)
. We list the following set of representatives: for
0\leq r\leq n-1

(2.3)

\varepsilon_{r}:=\begin{pmatrix}I_{(m-r)n-1}&&\\
&&I_{rn}\\
&1&\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I_{(m-r)n-1}&0&0\\
&1&\underline{b_{r}}\\
&&I_{rn}\end{pmatrix},

where
\underline{b_{r}}:=\left(e_{n-1}^{T},e_{n-2}^{T},\ldots,e_{n-r}^{T}\right)
.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of
5
.

We begin as follows. Using the Bruhat decomposition in
\mathrm{GL}_{mn}(k)
, we have

(2.4)

\mathrm{GL}_{mn}(k)=\bigcup\limits_{1\leq j\leq mn}\bigcup\limits_{u\in U^{(j)}_{mn}(k)}P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{j}uT_{m,n}(k),

where
U^{(j)}_{mn}(k):=w_{j}^{-1}P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{j}\cap U_{mn}(k)\backslash U_{mn}(k)
, and for
1\leq j\leq mn
,
w_{j}
are the representatives of
\begin{pmatrix}W_{mn-1}&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\backslash W_{mn}
, i.e.

(2.5)

w_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{j-1}&&\\
&&I_{mn-j}\\
&1&\end{pmatrix}.

The following lemma gives an explicit form of the representatives in
eq.

2.4
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq j\leq mn
. The elements

u_{j}(\underline{v_{j}}):=\begin{pmatrix}I_{j-1}&0&0\\
&1&\underline{v_{j}}\\
&&I_{mn-j}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\underline{v_{j}}\in k^{mn-j}.

form a set of representatives of
U^{(j)}_{mn}(k)
.

Proof.

Let

u=\begin{pmatrix}u_{j-1}&X_{1}&X_{2}\\
&1&\underline{v_{j}}\\
&&u_{mn-j}\end{pmatrix}\in U_{mn}(k).

Then,

w_{j}\begin{pmatrix}u_{j-1}&X_{1}&X_{2}\\
&1&\underline{v_{j}}\\
&&u_{mn-j}\end{pmatrix}w_{j}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{j-1}&X_{2}&X_{1}\\
&u_{mn-j}&0\\
&\underline{v_{j}}&1\end{pmatrix}.

Thus,
w_{j}uw_{j}^{-1}\in P_{mn-1,1}(k)
iff
\underline{v_{j}}=\underline{0}
. This gives

U_{mn}(k)\cap w_{j}^{-1}P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{j}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}u_{j-1}&X_{1}&X_{2}\\
&1&0\\
&&u_{mn-j}\end{pmatrix}\in U_{mn}(k)\right\}

and the lemma follows.
∎

We denote

C_{j}(\underline{v_{j}}):=P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{j}u_{j}(\underline{v_{j}})T_{m,n}(k).

In this notation we can rewrite the decomposition in
eq.

2.4
as follows.

(2.6)

\mathrm{GL}_{mn}(k)=\bigcup\limits_{1\leq j\leq mn}\bigcup\limits_{\underline{v_{j}}\in k^{mn-j}}C_{j}(\underline{v_{j}}).

We now note that in particular, for
r=0
we have
\varepsilon_{0}=I_{mn}
and
C_{mn}(\underline{b_{0}})=P_{mn-1,1}(k)T_{m,n}(k)
. For
0\leq r\leq n-1
the representative
\varepsilon_{r}
corresponds to the double coset
C_{(m-r)n}(\underline{b_{r}})
. Therefore,
5
reduces the decomposition in
eq.

2.6
to the disjoint union

(2.7)

\mathrm{GL}_{mn}(k)=\bigcup\limits_{0\leq r\leq n-1}C_{(m-r)n}(\underline{b_{r}}).

We continue by viewing the first row of
u_{1}(\underline{v_{1}})
as
m
vectors in
k^{n}
,
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in k^{mn}
, where
a_{j}\in k^{n}
for all
1\leq j\leq m
and
a_{1}=(1,a^{\prime}_{1})
with
a^{\prime}_{1}\in k^{n-1}
.
Denote

R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{m}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{m,n}(k).

Denote also
u_{1}(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})):=u_{1}(\underline{v_{1}})
.
The following lemma describes the orbit of the right action of
\mathrm{H}_{1,m-1}(k)\otimes\mathrm{H}_{1,n-1}(k)
on the coset
P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{1}u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
h\in\mathrm{H}_{1,m-1}(k)
and
g\in\mathrm{H}_{1,n-1}(k)
. Then,

(2.8)

P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{1}u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)t(h,g)=P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{1}u_{1}\left(h^{T}R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})g\right).

Proof.

By the Levi decomposition, we can write
h=h_{U}h_{\mathrm{S}}
and
g=g_{U}g_{\mathrm{S}}
. We denote

h_{U}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&I_{m-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad g_{U}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\
0&I_{n-1}\end{pmatrix},

where
x=(x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}),\ y=(y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})
, and

h_{\mathrm{S}}:=\mathrm{diag}(1,h^{\prime})
,
g_{\mathrm{S}}:=\mathrm{diag}(1,g^{\prime})
, where
h^{\prime}=\left(h_{i,j}\right)_{2\leq i,j\leq m}\in\mathrm{GL}_{m-1}(k)
,
g^{\prime}=\left(g_{i,j}\right)_{2\leq i,j\leq n}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(k)
.
We have

(2.9)

t(h,g)=t\left(h_{U},I_{n}\right)t\left(I_{m},g_{U}\right)t\left(h_{\mathrm{S}},I_{n}\right)t\left(I_{m},g_{\mathrm{S}}\right).

We first prove
eq.

2.8
for each one of the matrices on the right hand side of
eq.

2.9
.

Case
t(h_{U},I_{n})
:

u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)t(h_{U},I_{n})=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&A_{2}&\ldots&A_{m}\\
&I_{n}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&I_{n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}&x_{2}I_{n}&\ldots&x_{m}I_{n}\\
&I_{n}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&I_{n}\end{pmatrix},

where

A_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&a^{\prime}_{1}\\
0&I_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\forall 2\leq i\leq m,\ A_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{i}\\
0\end{pmatrix}\in M_{n}(k).

Thus,

u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)t(h_{U},I_{n})=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&x_{2}A_{1}+A_{2}&\ldots&x_{m}A_{1}+A_{m}\\
&I_{n}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&I_{n}\end{pmatrix}=u_{1}\left(h_{U}^{T}R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right).

Case
t((I_{m},g_{U})
:

u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)t(I_{m},g_{U})=\mathrm{diag}(I_{n},g_{U},\ldots,g_{U})\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}g_{U}&A_{2}g_{U}&\ldots&A_{m}g_{U}\\
&I_{n}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&I_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

By
2.1

\mathrm{diag}(I_{n},g_{U},\ldots,g_{U})\in w_{1}^{-1}P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{1}\cap U_{mn}(k)
. Therefore,

P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{1}u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)t(I_{m},g_{U})=P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{1}u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})g_{U}\right).

Case
t(h_{\mathrm{S}},I_{n})
:

u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)t(h_{\mathrm{S}},I_{n})=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&A_{2}&\ldots&A_{m}\\
&I_{n}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&I_{n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}&0&\ldots&0\\
0&h_{2,2}I_{n}&\ldots&h_{2,m}I_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&h_{m,2}I_{n}&&h_{m,m}I_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

We get that,

u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)t(h_{\mathrm{S}},I_{n})=t(h_{\mathrm{S}},I_{n})\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&\sum_{j=2}^{m}h_{j,2}A_{j}&\ldots&\sum_{j=2}^{m}h_{j,m}A_{j}\\
&I_{n}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&I_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore,

u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)t(h_{\mathrm{S}},I_{n})=t(h_{\mathrm{S}},I_{n})u_{1}\left(h_{\mathrm{S}}^{T}R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right).

Case
t(I_{m},g_{\mathrm{S}})
:

u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)t(I_{m},g_{\mathrm{S}})=t(I_{m},g_{\mathrm{S}})\begin{pmatrix}g_{\mathrm{S}}^{-1}A_{1}g_{\mathrm{S}}&g_{\mathrm{S}}^{-1}A_{2}g_{\mathrm{S}}&\ldots&g_{\mathrm{S}}^{-1}A_{m}g_{\mathrm{S}}\\
&I_{n}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&I_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Again, by the fact that the first column of
g_{\mathrm{S}}
is
(1,0,\ldots,0)\in k^{n}
and for
1\leq i\leq m
, only the first row of
A_{i}
is nonzero, we have
g_{\mathrm{S}}^{-1}A_{i}g_{\mathrm{S}}=A_{i}g_{\mathrm{S}}
.
Thus,

u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)t(I_{m},g_{\mathrm{S}})=t(I_{m},g_{\mathrm{S}})u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})g_{\mathrm{S}}\right).

Let
D
be one of the matrices
t(h_{\mathrm{S}},I_{n})
or
t(I_{m},g_{\mathrm{S}})
. Then, by the fact that the first column of
D
is
(1,0,\ldots,0)\in k^{mn}
, we have
w_{1}Dw_{1}^{-1}\in P_{mn-1,1}(k)
. Hence,
eq.

2.8
is true for the last two cases as well.

All in all, together with
eq.

2.9
, we get

P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{1}u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)t(h,g)=P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{1}u_{1}\left(h_{\mathrm{S}}^{T}h_{U}^{T}R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})g_{U}g_{\mathrm{S}}\right),

and the lemma follows.

∎

Next, we use
2.2
to show that for each
\underline{v_{1}}\in k^{mn-1}
, the double coset
C_{1}(\underline{v_{1}})
equals to one of the double cosets in
eq.

2.7
.

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists
0\leq r\leq n-1
such that
C_{1}(\underline{v_{1}})=C_{(m-r)n}(\underline{b_{r}})
.

Proof.

Let
r:=\mathrm{rk}(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}}))-1
, i.e. the dimension of the space spanned by
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
minus
1
. There exist
h_{0}\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}(k)
and
g_{0}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
such that
h_{0}^{T}R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})g_{0}=R_{m,n}(\underline{b^{\prime}_{r}})
, where
\underline{b^{\prime}_{r}}
corresponds to

R_{m,n}(\underline{b^{\prime}_{r}})=\begin{pmatrix}I_{r+1}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

In fact, in order to preserve the
1
at the top left corner of
R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})
, we must have
h_{0}\in\mathrm{H}_{1,m-1}(k)
and
g_{0}\in\mathrm{H}_{1,n-1}(k)
. Hence, by
2.2
, we have

C_{1}(\underline{v_{1}})=P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{1}u_{1}\left(R_{m,n}(\underline{v_{1}})\right)t(h_{0},g_{0})T_{m,n}(k)=C_{1}(\underline{b^{\prime}_{r}}).

Now,

C_{1}(\underline{b^{\prime}_{r}})=P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{1}E\left(E^{-1}u_{1}\left(\underline{b^{\prime}_{r}}\right)E\right)T_{m,n}(k),

where

E:=t\left(\begin{pmatrix}&&1&\\
&I_{m-r-2}&&\\
1&&&\\
&&&I_{r}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}&&&1\\
&&1&\\
&\reflectbox{$\ddots$}&&\\
1&&&\end{pmatrix}\right).

On the one hand,
E^{-1}u_{1}\left(\underline{b^{\prime}_{r}}\right)E=u_{(m-r)n}\left(\underline{b_{r}}\right)
, and on the other,
P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{1}E=P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{(m-r)n}
.
Thus,
C_{1}(\underline{b^{\prime}_{r}})=C_{(m-r)n}(\underline{b_{r}})
as requested. ∎

Now, we conclude that for each
1\leq j\leq mn
and
\underline{v_{j}}\in k^{mn-j}
, the double coset
C_{j}(\underline{v_{j}})
equals to one of the double cosets in
eq.

2.7
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1\leq j\leq mn
and
\underline{v_{j}}\in k^{mn-j}
. There exists
0\leq r\leq n-1
such that
C_{j}(\underline{v_{j}})=C_{(m-r)n}(\underline{b_{r}})
.

Proof.

If
j=1
then by
2.3
there exists
0\leq r\leq n-1
such that
C_{1}(\underline{v_{1}})=C_{(m-r)n}(\underline{b_{r}})
as requested. For all other cases
1<j\leq mn
, we denote
j=\alpha n+\beta
, where
0\leq\alpha\leq m-1
and
1\leq\beta\leq n
.
We have

C_{j}(\underline{v_{j}})=P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{j}E\left(E^{-1}u_{j}\left(\underline{v_{j}}\right)E\right)T_{m,n}(k),

where

E:=t\left(\begin{pmatrix}&I_{\alpha}&\\
1&&\\
&&I_{m-\alpha-1}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}&I_{\beta-1}&\\
1&&\\
&&I_{n-\beta}\end{pmatrix}\right).

On the one hand,
E^{-1}u_{j}\left(\underline{v_{j}}\right)E=u_{1}\left(\underline{v_{1}}\right)
, where
\underline{v_{1}}=(0,\underline{v_{j}})\in k^{mn-1}
, and on the other,
P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{j}E=P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{1}
.
Thus,
C_{j}(\underline{v_{j}})=C_{1}(\underline{v_{1}})
, and the proof follows from the
j=1
case.
∎

2.4
covers all the possibilities for the double cosets that appear in
eq.

2.6
. i.e.

\bigcup\limits_{1\leq j\leq mn}\bigcup\limits_{\underline{v_{j}}\in k^{mn-j}}C_{j}(\underline{v_{j}})=\bigcup\limits_{0\leq r\leq n-1}C_{(m-r)n}(\underline{b_{r}}).

It is left to show that the double cosets in
eq.

2.7
are pairwise disjoint. This is done in following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
0\leq r\neq\ell\leq n-1
be two integers. Then,
C_{(m-r)n}(\underline{b_{r}})\neq C_{(m-\ell)n}(\underline{b_{\ell}})
.

Proof.

Assume that
C_{(m-r)n}(\underline{b_{r}})=C_{(m-\ell)n}(\underline{b_{\ell}})
. i.e.,

(2.10)

P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{(m-r)n}u_{(m-r)n}\left(\underline{b_{r}}\right)T_{m,n}(k)=P_{mn-1,1}(k)w_{(m-\ell)n}u_{(m-\ell)n}\left(\underline{b_{\ell}}\right)T_{m,n}(k).

We show that
r=\ell
.
Equation

2.10
gives that there exist
p\in P_{mn-1,1}(k)
,
h\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}(k)
, and
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
such that

(2.11)

w_{(m-\ell)n}^{-1}pw_{(m-r)n}u_{(m-r)n}\left(\underline{b_{r}}\right)=u_{(m-\ell)n}\left(\underline{b_{\ell}}\right)t(h,g).

Denote

p:=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&A_{2}&\underline{y_{1}}\\
A_{3}&A_{4}&\underline{y_{2}}\\
0&0&d\end{pmatrix},

where
A_{4}
is a
\ell n
by
rn
matrix and
d\in k^{\times}
.
Then, the left hand side of
eq.

2.11
equals

(2.12)

w_{(m-\ell)n}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&A_{2}&\underline{y_{1}}\\
A_{3}&A_{4}&\underline{y_{2}}\\
0&0&d\end{pmatrix}w_{(m-r)n}u_{(m-r)n}\left(\underline{b_{r}}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&\underline{y_{1}}&\underline{y_{1}}\cdot\underline{b_{r}}+A_{2}\\
0&d&d\underline{b_{r}}\\
A_{3}&\underline{y_{2}}&\underline{y_{2}}\cdot\underline{b_{r}}+A_{4}\end{pmatrix}.

Denote
h=\left(h_{i,j}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq m}
and
g=\left(g_{i,j}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
. Then, the right hand side of
eq.

2.11
equals

(2.13)

u_{(m-\ell)n}\left(\underline{b_{\ell}}\right)t(h,g)=\begin{pmatrix}I_{(m-\ell)n-1}&0&0\\
&1&\underline{b_{\ell}}\\
&&I_{\ell n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}h_{1,1}g&\cdots&h_{1,\ell}g\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
h_{\ell,1}g&\cdots&h_{\ell,\ell}g\end{pmatrix}.

We see that for
1\leq i\leq(m-r)n-1
the coordinates
\left((m-r)n,i\right)
of the matrix in the right hand side of
eq.

2.12
are all zero. Hence, by comparing
eqs.

2.12
and
2.13
, we get the following system of equations. For all
1\leq j\leq n
and all
1\leq j^{\prime}\leq m-r-1

\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}h_{m-\ell+i,j^{\prime}}g_{n-i,j}=0.

The rows of
g
are linearly independent, so we conclude that for
m-\ell\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq m-r-1
we have
h_{i,j}=0
.
This gives

h=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
0&D\end{pmatrix},

where
D\in M_{\ell+1,r+1}(k)
. i.e.
D
has
\ell+1
linearly independent rows in
k^{r+1}
. This implies
\ell\leq r
. From symmetry of
r
and
\ell
in
eq.

2.10
we get that
r\leq\ell
as well. Thus,
r=\ell
as requested.
∎

2.2
The stabilizers

In this section we compute
Q^{\varepsilon_{r}}=P_{mn-1,1}^{\varepsilon_{r}}\cap T_{m,n}
for all
0\leq r<n
, where
\varepsilon_{r}
are the different representatives in
eq.

2.3
. We denote the following maximal parabolic subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}(k)
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
by
P_{r+1}^{m}:=P_{m-r-1,r+1}(k)
and
P_{r+1}^{n}:=P_{n-r-1,r+1}(k)
, respectively.
Consider the following subgroup of
t\left(P_{r+1}^{m}(k),P_{r+1}^{n}(k)\right)\leq T_{m,n}(k)
,

t_{\Delta}\left(P_{r+1}^{m}(k),P_{r+1}^{n}(k)\right):=\left\{t\left(\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
0&\lambda d^{\ast}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\right)\in T_{m,n}(k)\bigg|d\in\mathrm{GL}_{r+1}(k),\ \lambda\in k^{\times}\right\},

such that
d^{\ast}:=\tilde{w}_{r}^{-1}(d^{T})^{-1}\tilde{w}_{r}
, where

\tilde{w}_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}&&1\\
&\reflectbox{$\ddots$}&\\
1&&\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}(k).

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
0\leq r\leq n-1
. Then,
Q^{\varepsilon_{r}}(k)=t_{\Delta}\left(P_{r+1}^{m}(k),P_{r+1}^{n}(k)\right)
.

Before proving
2.6
, we need the following two lemmas. Generally, we have,

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
V,W
be vector spaces of dimension
\ell
over
k
, with bases
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}
and
\{w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{\ell}\}
, respectively.
Let
B:V\times W\to k
be a non-degenerate bilinear form, such that
B(v_{i},w_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq\ell
.
Let
S:V\to V
and
R:W\to W
be linear transformations. Then, for
\alpha\in k
,

(2.14)

\left(S\otimes R\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{j}\otimes w_{j}\right)=\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{j}\otimes w_{j}

iff
S\circ R^{T}=\alpha\cdot\mathrm{id}_{V}
. Therefore, in case
\alpha\neq 0
,
eq.

2.14
holds iff
S,R
are invertible and
S=\alpha\left(R^{T}\right)^{-1}
, where
R^{T}:V\to V
is the transformation adjoint to
R
via the non-degenerate bilinear form
B
.

Proof.

The bilinear form
B
defines an isomorphism
\iota:V\otimes W\to\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(V,V)
by sending
v\otimes w
to the linear map
\iota(v\otimes w)=i_{v\otimes w}:V\to V
defined by
i_{v\otimes w}(x)=B(x,w)v
. Under this isomorphism, the inverse image of
\mathrm{id}_{V}
is
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{j}\otimes w_{j}
. We have
(S\circ i_{v\otimes w})(x)=B(x,w)S(v)=i_{S(v)\otimes w}
, and
(i_{v\otimes w}\circ S)(x)=B(S(x),w)v=B(x,S^{T}(w))v=i_{v\otimes S^{T}(w)}
. Thus, we get that
S\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W}
corresponds to postcomposition by
S
, and that
\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes R
corresponds to precomposition by
R^{T}
.
Hence,
eq.

2.14
is equivalent under the isomorphism
\iota
to
S\circ R^{T}=\alpha\cdot\mathrm{id}_{V}
, as requested.
∎

We now make use of
2.7
to prove the following lemma,

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
h\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}(k)
,
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
, and

(2.15)

\underline{v}:=(0_{n},\ldots,0_{n},e_{n}^{T},e_{n-1}^{T},\ldots,e_{n-r}^{T})

where
0_{n}
is the row vector of
n
zeros (it appears
m-r-1
times in
\underline{v}
).

Then, there is
\lambda\in k^{\times}
such that
\underline{v}t\left(h,g\right)=\lambda\underline{v}
iff
t\left(h,g\right)\in t_{\Delta}\left(P_{r+1}^{m}(k),P_{r+1}^{n}(k)\right)
.

Proof.

Let
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{m}\}
and
\{w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n}\}
be the bases of standard row vectors of
k^{m}
and
k^{n}
, respectively.
We take the basis of
k^{m}\otimes k^{n}
in lexicographic order, i.e.

(2.16)

\begin{split}&\sum_{j=0}^{r}T_{C}\left(v_{m-j}\right)\otimes T_{c}\left(w_{n-r+j}\right)\in E_{1}\otimes E^{\prime}_{1},\\
&\sum_{j=0}^{r}T_{C}\left(v_{m-j}\right)\otimes T_{d}\left(w_{n-r+j}\right)\in E_{1}\otimes E^{\prime}_{0},\\
&\sum_{j=0}^{r}T_{D}\left(v_{m-j}\right)\otimes T_{c}\left(w_{n-r+j}\right)\in E_{0}\otimes E^{\prime}_{1},\\

The integral
I\left(f_{\omega_{\pi_{\nu}},s},v_{\pi_{\nu}}\right)(I_{m})
has meromorphic continuation for
\Re(s)>>0
.

Proof.

By
3.11
,
I\left(f_{\omega_{\pi_{\nu}},s},v_{\pi_{\nu}}\right)(I_{m})
is a finite sum of integrals of the form

\alpha\left(\tau,\phi,\phi^{\prime}\right)\beta\int\limits_{Z_{n}(k_{\nu})\backslash T_{\Theta}^{-}(\varepsilon)}P(q_{\nu}^{-s},q_{\nu}^{s})\mu(\underline{t})c(X(\tilde{\varepsilon},\underline{t}))\left|\det\underline{t}\right|^{ms+\frac{m}{2}}\sum_{s\in S,c\in\mathcal{M}}h_{s,c}\underline{t}^{s}\log^{c}\underline{t}d\underline{t}\cdot\phi^{\prime}.

where,
P(q_{\nu}^{-s},q_{\nu}^{s})
is a holomorphic function,
c(X(\tilde{\varepsilon},\underline{t}))
does not depend on
s
,
\mu(\underline{t})=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{+}}\left|\sinh\alpha(H)\right|^{\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}}
(by
3.3
).
We have
||\underline{t}||=|t_{1}|^{-1}
. Therefore, each of the integrals above equals

\begin{split}\int\limits_{|t_{1}|\leq|t_{2}|\leq\ldots|t_{n-1}|\leq 1}&\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}e^{c_{i}t_{i}}|t_{1}|^{1-n+ms+m/2}|t_{2}|^{3-n+ms+m/2}\cdots|t_{n-1}|^{n-3+ms+m/2}\\
&\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r_{\Theta}}\chi_{i}(t_{i})\left|t_{i}\right|^{p_{i}}\log^{p^{\prime}_{i}}\left|t_{i}\right|d^{\times}t_{1}d^{\times}t_{2}\cdots d^{\times}t_{n-1}.\end{split}

By separating variables we find that the last integral equals

(3.70)

\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\int\limits_{|t_{j}|\leq 1}e^{c_{j}t_{j}}\chi_{j}(t_{j})|t_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}+ms}\log^{p^{\prime}_{j}}\left|t_{j}\right|d^{\times}t_{j}.

Each of the classic integrals in
eq.

3.70
has meromorphic continuation and so does
I\left(f_{\omega_{\pi_{\nu}},s},v_{\pi_{\nu}}\right)(I_{m})
.

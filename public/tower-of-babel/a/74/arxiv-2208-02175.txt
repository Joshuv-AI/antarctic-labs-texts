CLASSIFICATION OF COHEN–MACAULAY t t –SPREAD LEXSEGMENT IDEALS VIA SIMPLICIAL COMPLEXES
Marilena Crupi, Antonino Ficarra

Abstract
We study the minimal primary decomposition of completely t t –spread lexsegment ideals via simplicial complexes. We determine some algebraic invariants of such a class of t t –spread ideals. Hence, we classify all t t --spread lexsegment ideals which are Cohen--Macaulay. † † Keywords: Betti numbers, t t –spread ideals, primary decomposition, simplicial complexes, Cohen–Macaulay rings. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05E40, 13B25, 13D02, 16W50.

Introduction

In combinatorial commutative algebra, one often associates to a combinatorial object
X
a suitable monomial ideal
I_{X}
in a polynomial ring
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
in finitely many variables with coefficients in a field
K
. Distinguished combinatorial properties of the object
X
are reflected by algebraic properties of the ideal
I_{X}
, and conversely. One says that
X
is Cohen–Macaulay if the ring
S/I_{X}
is Cohen–Macaulay. A typical task is to classify all Cohen–Macaulay objects
X
of a given class. For instance, in combinatorics, to a simplicial complex
\Delta
on the vertex set
[n]
one associates the Stanley–Reisner ideal
I_{\Delta}
,
i.e.
, the ideal generated by all squarefree monomials
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{r}}
,
r\leq n
, such that
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}\notin\Delta
.
A simplicial complex
\Delta
is said Cohen–Macaulay if the ideal
I_{\Delta}
is Cohen–Macaulay,
i.e.
,
S/I_{\Delta}
is a Cohen–Macaulay ring.
To classify all Cohen–Macaulay simplicial complexes is an hopeless task. However, many results can be found, for instance, in
[
18
]
.

The aim of this article is to classify all Cohen–Macaulay
t
–spread lexsegment ideals for
t\geq 1
.

S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring in

Let

If

A monomial ideal is

Let

–spread ideal

–spread monomials

–spread monomials

1. Preliminaries

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring in
n
indeterminates with coefficients in a field
K
.
S
is a
{\mathbb{N}}
–graded
K
–algebra with
\deg(x_{i})=1
for all

If
I
is a monomial ideal of
S
, we denote by

the unique minimal set of monomial generators of
I
. Moreover, we set
G(I)_{j}=\{u\in G(I):\deg(u)=j\}
.

Given a non empty subset

, we set
{\bf x}_{A}=\prod_{i\in A}x_{i}
.
Moreover, we set
{\bf x}_{\varnothing}=1
. Every monomial

can be written as
w=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}
, with sorted indexes
1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\dots\leq i_{d}\leq n
. We define the
support
of

{\operatorname{supp}}(w)=\{j:x_{j}\,\mbox{divides $w$}\}=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{d}\}.

With the notation above,
\min(w)=\min\big\{i:i\in{\operatorname{supp}}(u)\big\}=i_{1}
,
\max(w)=\max\big\{i:i\in{\operatorname{supp}}(u)\big\}=i_{d}
. Moreover, we set
\max(1)=\min(1)=0
.
If
i_{1}\neq i_{2}\neq\dots\neq i_{d}
we say that
u
is
squarefree
. In such a case,
u
can be written as
u={\bf x}_{A}
with
A={\operatorname{supp}}(u)
.

We quote next definition from
[
13

Definition 1.1

Given

From now on we consider

In

Definition 1.2

Let

A

\beta_{i,i+j}(I)\ =\ \sum_{u\in G(I)_{j}}\binom{\max(u)-t(j-1)-1}{i}.

(1)

A

(2)

We close the section recalling some notions about simplicial complexes.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. We recall that
a
simplicial complex
on the
vertex set

[n]
is a family of subsets of

such that

-

\{i\}\in\Delta
for all
i\in[n]
, and

-

if
F\subseteq[n]
,
G\subseteq F
, we have
G\in\Delta
.

The dimension of
\Delta
is the number

. Any
F\in\Delta
is called a
face
and
|F|-1
is the
dimension
of
F
. A
facet
of
\Delta
is a maximal face with respect to the inclusion. The set of facets of
\Delta
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
.

It is well known that for any squarefree ideal (
1
–spread ideal)
I
of
S
there exists a unique simplicial complex
\Delta
on
[n]
such that
I=I_{\Delta}
, where

I_{\Delta}=({\bf x}_{F}:F\subseteq[n],F\notin\Delta)

is the Stanley–Reisner ideal of
\Delta

[
18
]
. In the sequel when we say that a simplicial complex
\Delta
is associated to a squarefree ideal
I
of
S
, we mean that
I_{\Delta}=I
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
on
[n]
is called
pure
if its facets have the same dimension. We say that
\Delta
is
Cohen–Macaulay
if
K[\Delta]=S/I_{\Delta}=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/I_{\Delta}
is a Cohen–Macaulay ring. If
\Delta
is Cohen–Macaulay then

is pure (see, for instance,
[

]

It is well known that any monomial ideal (not necessarily squarefree) has a unique minimal primary decomposition. We refer to it as the
standard primary decomposition
. Moreover, for a squarefree monomial ideal, its standard primary decomposition is
I=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Min}(I)}\mathfrak{p}
,
[

, where

is the set of minimal primes of

. Every minimal prime is a monomial prime ideal,
i.e.
,
\mathfrak{p}=(x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\dots,x_{i_{s}})
, for some
A=\{i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{s}\}\subseteq[n]

Proposition 1.5

[
18
, Lemma 1.5.4]
The standard primary decomposition of
I_{\Delta}
is

I_{\Delta}=\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)}\mathfrak{p}_{[n]\setminus F}.

Given two positive integers
j\geq k
, we set
[j,k]=\{\ell\in\mathbb{N}:j\leq\ell\leq k\}
and we associate to a

Let

of degree

If

(3)

whereas, if

Moreover, we set

.

Remark 1.6

Let

. The case

We start the section with some comments and remarks.

Let
n,d,t\geq 1
,
u=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}
and

v=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{d}}
monomials of
M_{n,d,t}
, with
u\geq_{{\operatorname{slex}}}v
.
Set
L=\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v)
,
I=(L)
. We assume that
I
is a completely
t
–spread lexsegment ideal,
i.e.
,
I\ =\ J\cap T
,
with
J=(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{i}(v))
and
T=(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u))
.

-

If
u=v
,
I=(L)=(u)
is a principal ideal, and its standard primary decomposition is
I=(x_{i_{1}})\cap\dots\cap(x_{i_{d}})
. Therefore, we may assume
u>_{{\operatorname{slex}}}v
.

-

If
\deg(u)=\deg(v)=d=1
, then
I=(L)=(x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{1}+1},\dots,x_{{j_{1}}-1},x_{j_{1}})=\mathfrak{p}_{[i_{1},j_{1}]}
is the standard primary decomposition of
I
. Thus, we may assume

.

-

If
I
is initial,
i.e.
,
I=J=(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{i}(v))
, we may assume
\min(v)=j_{1}\geq 2
, otherwise

J=(x_{1})\cap(\mathcal{L}_{t}(x_{1+t}\cdots x_{1+(d-1)t},v/x_{1})),

and to determine the standard primary decomposition of

, it suffices to determine that of
(\mathcal{L}_{t}(x_{1+t}\cdots x_{1+(d-1)t},v/x_{j_{1}}))
which is an initial
t
–spread lexsegment ideal in the polynomial ring
K[x_{1+t},\dots,x_{n}]
with fewer indeterminates than
S
.

-

If
I
is final,
i.e.
,
I=T=(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u))
, we can assume
\min(u)=i_{1}=1
. Indeed, if
i_{1}>1
, none of the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{i_{1}-1}
divides any minimal monomial generator of
T
. So computing the standard primary decomposition of
T
is equivalent to computing that of
T\cap K[x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{n}]
in the polynomial ring
K[x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{n}]
with fewer indeterminates than
S
.

Hence, from now on, we assume that
I=(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v))=J\cap T
, with
u>_{{\operatorname{slex}}}v
,
\min(u)=1
,
\min(v)\geq 2
and
\deg(u)=\deg(v)=d\geq 2
.

To compute the standard primary decomposition of
I=J\cap T
, we can proceed as follows.
Firstly, we determine the decomposition of
J
, and if
I=J
we are done. Secondly, we determine that of
T
, and if
I=T
we are done. Finally, knowing the primary decompositions of
J
and
T
we take into account the intersection

, and deleting the non minimal primes, we obtain the standard primary decomposition of
I
in the general case.

2.1. The initial
t
–spread lexsegment case

In this subsection we analyze the case of the standard primary decomposition of an initial
t
–spread lexsegment ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

Theorem 2.1

Let
v=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\dots x_{j_{d}}
be a
t
–spread monomial of degree
d\geq 2
of
S
with
j_{1}\geq 2

and let
J=(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{i}(v))
be an initial
t
–spread lexsegment ideal of
S
. Then, the standard primary decomposition of
J
is

J\ =\ \bigcap_{p=1}^{d}\mathfrak{p}_{F_{p}}\cap\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{p}_{[n]\setminus F},

where

\displaystyle F_{p}\

\displaystyle=[j_{p}]\setminus{\operatorname{supp}}_{t}(x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{p-1}}),\ p=1,\dots,d,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\

\displaystyle=\ \big\{{\operatorname{supp}}_{t}(w):w\in M_{n+1-t,d-1,t},\ w>_{{\operatorname{slex}}}v/x_{\max(v)}\big\}.

Proof.
Let
\Delta
be the simplicial complex on the vertex set
[n]
associated to
J
.
From Proposition
1.5
, we have to prove that the facets of
\Delta
are exactly the sets
[n]\setminus F_{p}
,
p=1,\dots,d
, together with the sets of
\mathcal{F}
,
i.e.
,

\mathcal{F}(\Delta)=\big\{[n]\setminus F_{p}:p=1,\dots,d\big\}\cup\mathcal{F}.

(7)

Observe that if
G\in\Delta
,
G\neq\varnothing
, then
G
is a facet of
\Delta
if and only if
G\cup\{i\}\notin\Delta
, for all
i\in[n]\setminus G
,
i.e.
, if and only if
{\bf x}_{G\cup\{i\}}\in J
, for all
i\in[n]\setminus G
.

We show that each
F\in\mathcal{F}
is a facet of
\Delta
. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}
, then

\displaystyle F\

\displaystyle=\ {\operatorname{supp}}_{t}(x_{\ell_{1}}\cdots x_{\ell_{d-1}})\ =\ \bigcup_{r=1}^{d-1}[\ell_{r},\ell_{r}+(t-1)]

\displaystyle=\ \big\{\ell_{1},\ell_{1}+1,\dots,\ell_{1}+(t-1),\ \dots,\ \ell_{d-1},\ell_{d-1}+1,\dots,\ell_{d-1}+(t-1)\big\},

with
x_{\ell_{1}}\cdots x_{\ell_{d-1}}\in M_{n+1-t,d-1,t}
and
x_{\ell_{1}}\cdots x_{\ell_{d-1}}>_{{\operatorname{slex}}}v/x_{\max(v)}=x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{d-1}}
.

Clearly,
{\bf x}_{F}\notin J
, since

{\bf x}_{F}
is not a multiple of any
t
–spread monomial of degree
d
of
S
. To prove that
F
is a facet, it suffices to show that
{\bf x}_{F\cup\{i\}}\in J
, for all
i\in[n]\setminus F
.

Let
i\in[n]\setminus F
. We have:

-

if
i<\ell_{1}
, then
{\bf x}_{F\cup\{i\}}
is divided by the
t
–spread monomial
x_{i}x_{\ell_{1}+(t-1)}\cdots x_{\ell_{d-1}+(t-1)}
;

-

if
\ell_{j}+(t-1)<i<\ell_{j+1}
, for some
1\leq j\leq d-2
, then
{\bf x}_{F\cup\{i\}}=

{\bf x}_{F}x_{i}
is divided by the
t
–spread monomial
\big(\prod\limits_{r=1}^{j}x_{\ell_{r}}\big)x_{i}\big(\prod\limits_{r=j+1}^{d-1}x_{\ell_{r}+(t-1)}\big)
;

-

if
\ell_{d-1}+(t-1)<i
, then
{\bf x}_{F\cup\{i\}}
is is divided by the monomial
x_{\ell_{1}}\cdots x_{\ell_{d-1}}x_{i}
.

More in detail, in every case,
{\bf x}_{F\cup\{i\}}
is a multiple of a
t
–spread monomial of degree
d
strictly greater than
v
, with respect to
>_{{\operatorname{slex}}}
. Therefore
{\bf x}_{F\cup\{i\}}\in J
and
F
is a facet.

Now, we determine all the facets of
\Delta
. Let
G\in\Delta
and define the following integers:

\ell_{1}=\min(G)
, and

\ell_{i}=\min\big\{r\in G:r\geq\ell_{i-1}+t\big\}
, for
i\geq 2
.

Let
\ell_{k}
be the last element of the sequence
\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\cdots
.

If
\ell_{i}\leq j_{i}
for all
i
, then
k\leq d-1
, otherwise
x_{\ell_{1}}x_{\ell_{2}}\cdots x_{\ell_{d}}\in J
. Against the fact that
G\in\Delta
. Then,

\displaystyle G\

\displaystyle\subseteq\ \bigcup_{i=1}^{k}[\ell_{i},\ell_{i}+(t-1)]\cup\bigcup_{i=k+1}^{d-1}[j_{i},j_{i}+(t-1)]

\displaystyle=\ {\operatorname{supp}}_{t}(x_{\ell_{1}}\cdots x_{\ell_{k}}x_{j_{k+1}}\cdots x_{j_{d-1}})=F\in\mathcal{F}.

Assume there exists an integer
1\leq p\leq d
such that
\ell_{p}>j_{p}
and let
p
be minimal for such a property. By the meaning of
p
, for
q<p
,
\ell_{q}\leq j_{q}
. We need to distinguish two cases.

Case 1.
There exists an integer
q<p
such that
\ell_{q}<j_{q}
.

In such a case,
x_{\ell_{1}}x_{\ell_{2}}\cdots x_{\ell_{p-1}}>_{{\operatorname{slex}}}x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{p-1}}
. Moreover,
k\leq d-1
, otherwise
x_{\ell_{1}}x_{\ell_{2}}\cdots x_{\ell_{p-1}}x_{\ell_{p}}\cdots x_{\ell_{d}}>_{{\operatorname{slex}}}v

Hence,

and we can quickly find a facet

which contains

and consequently

.

Case 2.
For all integers
q<p
,
\ell_{q}=j_{q}
.

In such a case, since
\ell_{p}>j_{p}
, we have
x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{p}}>_{{\operatorname{slex}}}x_{\ell_{1}}\cdots x_{\ell_{p}}
, and

G\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{p-1}[j_{i},j_{i}+(t-1)]\cup[j_{p}+1,n]=[n]\setminus F_{p}

and

is clearly a facet.
Finally, all the facets of
\Delta
are those described in (
7
).

\Box

Remark 2.2

We note that for an initial
t
–spread lexsegment ideal
J=I_{\Delta}
, all the facets
F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
have cardinality
|F|\geq(d-1)t
. Indeed, from Theorem
2.1
,
\mathcal{F}(\Delta)=\big\{[n]\setminus F_{p}:p=1,\dots,d\big\}\cup\mathcal{F}
. If
F\in\mathcal{F}
, then
|F|=(d-1)t
. Otherwise, if
F=[n]\setminus F_{p}
for some

, then
|F|=|[n]\setminus F_{p}|=n-|F_{p}|=n-(j_{p}-(p-1)t)
. We observe that
j_{p}\leq n-(d-p)t
. Thus

|F|=n+(p-1)t-j_{p}\geq n+(p-1)t-n+(d-p)t=(d-1)t,

as desired.

We illustrate the previous result with an example.

,

The next result will be crucial.

Proposition 2.6

Let
u
be a
t
–spread monomial of degree
d
of
S
such that
\min(u)=1
and let

be a final

. Let
\Delta
be the simplicial complex on
[n]
associated to

.
Then
|F|\in\{(d-1)t,1+(d-1)t\}
, for all
F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
.

Proof.
Since we may see
T
as an initial
t
–spread lexsegment ideal with the order on the variables reversed
x_{n}>x_{n-1}>\cdots>x_{1}
, then each facet of
\Delta
has cardinality
\geq(d-1)t
(Remark
2.2
). To prove that
|F|\leq 1+(d-1)t
for all

with cardinality

does not belong to
\Delta
,

,
{\bf x}_{G}\in T
.

Let
G\subseteq[n]
with

, and

\ell_{j}=\min\big\{k\in G:k\geq\ell_{j-1}+t\big\}
, for

\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\cdots
has at least
d
terms. Therefore,

and

\Box

In the next theorem, we assume that

, as the case of the Veronese ideal has been covered in Theorem

Theorem 2.7

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\

\displaystyle=\ \big\{{\operatorname{cosupp}}_{t}(w)\cup\{1\}:x_{1}w\in M_{n,d,t},\ w>_{{\operatorname{slex}}}u/x_{1}\big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\

\displaystyle=\ \big\{{\operatorname{cosupp}}_{t}(w):x_{1}w\in M_{n,d,t},\ w\leq_{{\operatorname{slex}}}u/x_{1}\big\}.

Proof.
Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on the vertex set
[n]
associated to
T
. By Proposition
1.5
, we have to prove that

\mathcal{F}(\Delta)=\mathcal{G}\cup\mathcal{H}.

(8)

By Proposition
2.6
,
the facets of
\Delta
have cardinality
(d-1)t
or

.

Let

Finally,

\displaystyle G\subseteq F\

\mathfrak{p}_{[i_{2},n-(d-2)t]}
must be a minimal prime of height two. Hence,

, as desired.

By what shown so far, if

is Cohen–Macaulay, then
I=(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u_{\ell},v_{k}))
, for some
\ell\in\{0,\dots,d-1\}
and

.
On the other hand,

is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
\dim(S/I)=\textup{depth}(S/I)
. We note that
\dim(S/I)=n-2
. Indeed, we have shown previously that
\textup{height}(I)=2
.
By the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula,
\textup{depth}(S/I)=n-{\operatorname{pd}}(S/I)=n-1-{\operatorname{pd}}(I)
. Hence,

is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
\textup{height}(I)=2
and
{\operatorname{pd}}(I)=1
.

Hence, in order to establish the theorem we need to prove the next facts.

Claim 1.

0pt(I)=2
if and only if
\gcd(I)=\gcd(w:w\in G(I))=1
.

Proof of Claim 1.
Indeed,
\mathfrak{p}_{[2]}=(x_{1},x_{2})
is a minimal prime of
I
having height two, so
0pt(I)\leq 0pt(\mathfrak{p}_{[2]})=2
. Moreover,
0pt(I)=1
if and only if there is a minimal prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
I
whose height is
1
,
i.e.
, there exists a variable
x_{s}
dividing all generators of
I
. The claimed statement follows.

Claim 2.

{\operatorname{pd}}(I)=1
if and only if the ideal
(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,u_{d-1}))\cap(\mathcal{L}_{t}(v_{1},v))=P\cap Q
is a principal ideal.

Proof of Claim 2.
We can observe that

P=(w\in G(I):\min(w)=1),\ \ \ Q=(w\in G(I):\min(w)=2).

Clearly
I=P+Q
. We claim that
I=P+Q
is a Betti splitting of
I
. Indeed using Lemma
3.1
, it sufficies to note that
Q=(w\in G(I):x_{2}\ \textup{divides}\ w)
and that
Q
has a linear resolution. For all monomials

with
\min(w)=1
,
\min(w/x_{1})\geq n-(d-2)t-1>2
, as by hypothesis
n\geq 3+(d-1)t>3+(d-2)t
. Moreover
Q
is an equigenerated initial
t
–spread lexsegment ideal in
K[x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
, so it has a linear resolution. Hence, by (
11
),

{\operatorname{pd}}(I)\ =\ \max\{{\operatorname{pd}}(P),{\operatorname{pd}}(Q),{\operatorname{pd}}(P\cap Q)+1\}.

We have
{\operatorname{pd}}(P)\leq 1
. Indeed,
P
is isomorphic to the final
t
–spread lexsegment ideal
(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u/x_{1},x_{n-(d-2)t}\cdots x_{n-t}x_{n}))
generated in degree
d-1
, and formula (
2

{\operatorname{pd}}(I)=\begin{cases}n-(n-(d-2)t-1)-(d-2)t=1&\textup{if}\ u>u_{d-1},\\
n-(n-(d-2)t)-(d-2)t=0&\textup{if}\ u=u_{d-1}.\end{cases}

Analogously, as

is an initial
t
–spread lexsegment ideal in

if

, or
{\operatorname{pd}}(Q)=1
if

. Thus, (

) implies that
{\operatorname{pd}}(I)=1
if and only if
{\operatorname{pd}}(P\cap Q)=0
,
i.e.
, if and only if

is a principal ideal.

\Box

Conversely, let

\Box

For the proof of the last case (c), we need some more preparations.

First, recall that if

Therefore

Lemma 3.6

Let
I,J
equigenerated monomial ideals of
S
having initial degree

, then

\beta_{i,i+d}(J)\leq\beta_{i,i+d}(I),

. Moreover, if

Proof.
The first assertion follows from

\beta_{s}(J)=\beta_{s,s+d}(J)\leq\beta_{s,s+d}(I)

{\operatorname{pd}}(I)\geq s={\operatorname{pd}}(J)

\Box

(c) Let

4. Conclusions and Perspectives

In this article, we have investigated the Cohen–Macaulayness of
t
–spread lexsegment ideals using the theory of simplicial complexes. The completely
t
–spread lexsegment ideals have played an essential role. Non–completely
t
–spread lexsegment ideals are much less understood. Here are some possible questions to investigate.

Question 4.1

Determine the standard primary decomposition of non–completely
t
–spread lexsegment ideals.

Question 4.2

Classify the pure simplicial complexes associated to
t
–spread lexsegment ideals.

In
[
19
]
, Olteanu classified all
sequentially Cohen–Macaulay
squarefree completely lexsegment ideals. To the best of our knowledge, a classification for squarefree non–completely lexsegment ideals is unknown. More generally, we ask the following

Question 4.3

Classify all sequentially Cohen–Macaulay
t
–spread lexsegment ideals.

Finally, due to many examples that we performed and our results on completely
t
–spread lexsegment ideals, we are led to conjecture the following:

Conjecture 4.4

Let
I
be a
t
–spread lexsegment ideal generated in degree
d\geq 2
. Then
\dim(S/I)\geq(d-1)t
.

Note that Conjecture
4.4
is trivially true when

. Indeed, if
\Delta
is the simplicial complex associated to

, then each set

, with

, is in

. Thus

4.4
is also true for completely

–spread lexsegment ideals by virtue of Theorems

,

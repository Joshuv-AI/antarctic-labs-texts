Rigidity Result for Four Dimensional Willmore Submanifolds with Boundary
Peter Olamide Olanipekun

Abstract
We establish a rigidity result for the critical points, with boundary, of a four dimensional Willmore energy. These critical points satisfy a 4-Willmore equation which is a sixth order nonlinear elliptic partial differential equation. We establish several curvature estimates and prove that four dimensional Willmore submanifold with totally geodesic boundary condition are umbilic.

Introduction

The goal of this present paper is to establish a rigidity result for the critical points of the four dimensional Willmore energy. This four dimensional energy is relatively new in literature and has only been studied from analytical perspective in

2
Preliminaries

Let
\vec{\Phi}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a smooth immersion of a manifold
\Sigma
into
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The induced metric
g
on
\Sigma
is given by

g_{ij}:=\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

where
\nabla_{(.)}
is a covariant derivative associate with
g
and
g^{ij}
is the inverse metric. Note that
g^{ij}g_{ij}=4=\textnormal{dim}(\Sigma)
. We append arrows on vector quantities and use a dot to denote the scalar product of vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The volume element on
\Sigma
will be denoted by

d\mu:=dvol_{g}=|g|^{1/2}dx

and on the boundary
\partial\Sigma
by
d\sigma
. The components of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma

are given by

\vec{h}_{ij}:=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}

from which we find the mean curvature vector
4\vec{H}=g^{ij}\vec{h}_{ij}=\vec{h}_{j}^{j}
.
We denote by
\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{X}
and
\pi_{T}\vec{X}
the normal and tangential projections of a vector
\vec{X}
respectively and we have
\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{X}+\pi_{T}\vec{X}=\vec{X}
. We will often decompose vectors into normal and tangential components.
For a vector
\vec{X}
, we denote by

\Delta_{\perp}\vec{X}:=g^{ij}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{X}

the negative covariant Laplacian for the Levi-Civita connection in the normal bundle. We also denote

\Delta_{\perp}^{2}\vec{X}:=g^{kl}g^{ij}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{k}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{l}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{X}.

The tracefree second fundamental form of
\Sigma
is the symmetric tensor
\vec{h}_{0}
whose components are given by

(\vec{h}_{0})_{ij}=\vec{h}_{ij}-g_{ij}\vec{H}.

The tracefree second fundamental form satisfies

\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}(\vec{h}_{0})_{ij}=\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{h}_{ij}-\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H}=3\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H}

where we have used the Codazzi-Mainardi equations

\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{h}_{jk}=\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{h}_{ik}=\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{k}\vec{h}_{ij}.

We now introduce the cut-off function that will be needed whenever we are integrating by parts. Let
\vec{\Phi}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be our immersion and let
\tilde{\gamma}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow[0,1]
be a
C^{1}
function with compact support, we take the composition

\displaystyle\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}\circ\vec{\Phi}:\Sigma\rightarrow[0,1]

(4)

with the assumption that
\|\nabla\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{\gamma}
. We set
c_{\gamma}=\frac{c}{\rho}
where
c
is an absolute constant and
\rho
will be specified later.

3
The 4-Willmore equation

We derive the normal variation of the energy
\mathcal{E}(\Sigma)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\vec{\Phi}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an immersion of a 4-dimensional manifold
\Sigma
. Let
\vec{B}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a smooth normal variation of
\vec{\Phi}
. The first variation of the energy is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}\mathcal{E}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}\vec{B}\cdot\vec{\mathcal{W}}\,\,d\mu+\int_{\partial\Sigma}V\,d\sigma

(5)

where

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{2}_{\perp}\vec{H}-\frac{1}{2}(\vec{h}_{ik}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H})\vec{h}^{ik}-4|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}+2\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}((\vec{h}^{j}_{i}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{H})\vec{H})

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt-2\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{j}_{i})\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H})+2(\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H})\vec{h}^{ij}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\perp}((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\vec{h}_{ij})-2\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ik})\vec{H})-28|\vec{H}|^{4}\vec{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt-\frac{1}{2}(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})(\vec{h}_{ij}\cdot\vec{h}_{pq})\vec{h}^{pq}-4(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{ij})(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{i}_{k})\vec{h}^{jk}+4|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}\vec{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+7\Delta_{\perp}(|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H})+7|\vec{H}|^{2}(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{ij})\vec{h}^{ij}

and

\displaystyle V^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ik})(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}_{ik})+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{B}-2(\nabla^{i}\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{i}^{j})(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{B})

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+2(\nabla^{i}\vec{H}\cdot\vec{B})(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{i}^{j})-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\cdot\nabla^{j}\vec{B}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{j}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\cdot\vec{B}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ik})(\vec{h}_{ik}\cdot\nabla^{j}\vec{B})+\frac{1}{2}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ik})\vec{h}_{ik})\cdot\vec{B}-2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})(\vec{H}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{B})

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+2\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\vec{H})\cdot\vec{B}+7|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\cdot\nabla^{j}\vec{B}-7\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}(|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H})\cdot\vec{B}.

(6)

\displaystyle\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\tilde{\delta}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H}

Similar computation gives

\displaystyle\textnormal{II}+\textnormal{III}

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\vec{\Phi}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an immersion of a 4-dimensional manifold
\Sigma
whose boundaries satisfy the condition (
3
) and let
\gamma
be the cut-off function in (
4
) then

\displaystyle cc_{\gamma}^{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{h}_{0}|^{2}\gamma^{p-2}d\mu+2p\int_{\Sigma}U^{i}\nabla_{i}\gamma\gamma^{p-1}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\delta\int_{\Sigma}|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{(2)}\nabla\vec{h}|^{2}\gamma^{p}d\mu+\delta\int_{\Sigma}|\vec{h}|^{2}|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{h}|^{2}\gamma^{p}d\mu+c(\delta)c_{\gamma}^{4}\int_{\Sigma}|\vec{h}|^{2}\gamma^{p-4}d\mu.

Proof.

We handle the
c_{\gamma}^{2}
term by observing that
|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{h}_{0}|^{2}\leq c|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{h}|^{2}
and using inequality (
43
). We have

\displaystyle cc_{\gamma}^{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{h}_{0}|^{2}\gamma^{p-2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\delta\int_{\Sigma}|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{(2)}\vec{h}|^{2}\gamma^{p}d\mu+c(\delta)c_{\gamma}^{4}\int_{\Sigma}|\vec{h}|^{2}\gamma^{p-4}d\mu.

(44)

Next, we recall the expression for

\displaystyle U^{i}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{H}+\frac{1}{2}\vec{H}\cdot\nabla^{i}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}+J^{i}

where, for convenience, we have set

\displaystyle J^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{i}\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{jk})(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{jk})-2(\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{i}_{j})|\vec{H}|^{2}+\nabla^{j}|\vec{H}|^{2}(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{i}_{j})

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{i}((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{kj})\vec{h}^{kj})\cdot\vec{H}+2\nabla_{j}((\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})\vec{H})\cdot\vec{H}-7\nabla^{i}(|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H})\cdot\vec{H}.

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{H}\gamma^{p-1}\nabla_{i}\gamma d\mu\leq\delta\int_{\Sigma}|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{(2)}\vec{h}|^{2}\gamma^{p}d\mu+c(\delta)c_{\gamma}^{4}\int_{\Sigma}|\vec{h}|^{2}\gamma^{p-4}d\mu.

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\gamma^{p-1}\nabla\gamma d\mu\leq cc_{\gamma}\int_{\Sigma}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}\gamma^{p-1}d\mu

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{jk18}}{\leq}\delta\int_{\Sigma}|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{(2)}\vec{h}|^{2}\gamma^{p}d\mu+c(\delta)c_{\gamma}^{4}\int_{\Sigma}|\vec{h}|^{2}\gamma^{p-4}d\mu.

With the help of the Peter-Paul inequality we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}J^{i}\nabla_{i}\gamma\gamma^{p-1}d\mu\leq\delta\int_{\Sigma}|\vec{h}|^{2}|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{h}|^{2}\gamma^{p}d\mu+\delta\int_{\Sigma}|\vec{h}|^{4}\gamma^{p}d\mu+c(\delta)c_{\gamma}^{4}\int_{\Sigma}|\vec{h}|^{4}\gamma^{p-4}d\mu.

\Sigma

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+2\int_{\Sigma}(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{k}_{j})(\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\nabla_{k}\vec{H})\gamma^{p}d\mu+\int_{\Sigma}\left(2(\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\vec{H})\vec{h}^{ij}-4|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\right)\cdot\vec{H}\gamma^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\left.-2(\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{i}_{j})|\vec{H}|^{2}+2(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{i}_{j})(\vec{H}\cdot\nabla^{j}\vec{H})\right)\gamma^{p-1}\nabla_{i}\gamma d\mu

We integrate by parts and use the boundary condition (
3

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\Delta_{\perp}^{2}\vec{H}\cdot\vec{H}\gamma^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{k}[(\nabla^{j}\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{k}_{j})\vec{H}]\cdot\vec{H}\gamma^{p}d\mu

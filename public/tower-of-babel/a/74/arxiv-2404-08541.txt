Abstract
. Given a smooth asymptotically conical self-expander that is strictly unstable we construct a (singular) Morse flow line of the expander functional that connects it to a stable self-expander. This flow is monotone in a suitable sense and has small singular set.

A difficulty in the construction of the flow in Theorem

comes from the lack of a general avoidance principle for non-compact weak flows – see for instance
[

, Example 7.3]
. In particular, while the PDE methods used in
[

]
allow one to show that the initial expander mean convexity condition is preserved as long as the flow remains smooth, it is not immediately clear that an appropriate weak notion of expander mean convexity is preserved for the biggest flow, which is the “hull” of all weak flows of the given initial set. To handle this, we construct the flow as an exhaustion limit of compact expander mean convex flows. We then show that the flow is what we call
ample
, a localized notion of being a biggest flow – see Section
4
. This allows one to adapt the strategy of White
[
Whi00

Whi03
]
for compact mean convex flows to address the regularity of the noncompact flow constructed above. Recently, White
[
Whi24
]
has established a quite general avoidance principle for non-compact hypersurfaces that are a positive distance apart. This does not seem to suffice for our application as the flows we consider are all asymptotic to a fixed cone and so different time slices have distance zero.

Finally, we note a related construction of a (singular) monotone Morse flow line of the shrinker functional in
[
CCMS20
]
coming out of an asymptotically conical self-shrinker. This is a crucial ingredient used there to study generic mean curvature flows. As in this paper, the initial construction is based off a compact exhaustion and the asymptotic regularity of the flow, though there are several differences related to the different geometric properties of the shrinker and expander functionals. Most notably, unlike the situation considered here, the geometry of the shrinker equation does not allow one to take these compact flows to be themselves monotone. Our approach stresses the preservation of expander mean convexity, and the corresponding regularity, under appropriate limiting process.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

-mean-convex compact set. There is an

(2)

\Gamma=W\cap C_{\partial U,T_{1}}=(\bar{B}_{\varepsilon}(p)\cap\partial U)\times[T_{1},T_{1}+\varepsilon]

(3)

is a weak

-flow generated by
\Gamma
with starting time

.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may take
T_{1}=0
. Pick
\varepsilon_{0}>0
small enough so that for any
p\in\partial U
, the injectivity radius of
N
at
p
is bigger than
\varepsilon_{0}
and
\bar{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}(p)\cap\partial U
is a small graph over

(within normal coordinates around

). Moreover, as
U
is strictly
X
-mean-convex, there is a uniform lower bound

on the scalar
X
-mean-curvature of
U
(with respect to the outward unit normal), and we choose
\varepsilon_{0}\leq 100^{-1}h_{0}
.

Fix
p\in\partial U
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and let
\mathbf{n}
be the inward unit normal to
\partial U
defined on
B^{\prime}=\bar{B}_{\varepsilon}(p)\cap\partial U
. Pick a smooth non-negative and compactly supported function,
\phi
, on

with the property that
\|\phi\|_{C^{2}(B^{\prime})}\leq 10^{-1}\varepsilon
and on
B_{100^{-1}\varepsilon}(p)\cap B^{\prime}

\phi(q^{\prime})\geq 100^{-1}\varepsilon
.
For

, set

W(t)=\left\{\exp_{N}(s\phi(q^{\prime})\mathbf{n}(q^{\prime}))\mid q^{\prime}\in B^{\prime},s\in[0,t]\right\}

The construction immediately ensures that

satisfies (1)-(3) with
T_{1}=0
.

The construction also implies that, for any point
q^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
with
\phi(q^{\prime})>0

\displaystyle q=\exp_{N}(t\phi(q^{\prime})\mathbf{n}(q^{\prime}))\in\partial W(t)\cap\mathrm{int}(U).

Thus, up to shrinking
\varepsilon_{0}
, the unit normal at
q
pointing into
W(t)
is compatible with the outward normal to
U
at
q^{\prime}
,
-\mathbf{n}(q^{\prime}),
in the sense that, after parallel transporting along the minimizing geodesic connecting
q
and
q^{\prime}
, the two differ by less than

. In particular, the
X
-mean-curvature of
\partial W(t)
with respect to this unit normal,

H_{W}^{X}(q,t)\geq h_{0}-c_{1}\varepsilon t\geq(100-c_{1}\varepsilon)\varepsilon

where

depends on
\partial U
and

. Hence, up to shrinking
\varepsilon_{0}
,
H_{W}^{X}(q,t)\geq 2\varepsilon
.

To check that

is a weak
X
-flow generated by
\Gamma
, it suffices to consider a smooth compact barrier
K\colon[a,b]\to N
with

for

so that there is a

where

. Clearly,

for

satisfying

. One readily checks that the normal velocity of

Combining this with the lower bound on

This is what is required in the definition of weak

We prove the following semigroup property for biggest flows. Let

, it remains only to show that if
K\colon t\in[a,b]\mapsto K(t)
is a strong barrier with
a\geq T_{0}
such that
K(a)\cap Y(a)=\emptyset
and
K(t)\cap\Upsilon(t)=\emptyset
for all
t\in[a,b]
, then
K(t)\cap Y(t)=\emptyset
for all
t\in[a,b]
. We argue by contradiction. Without loss of generality we may assume
b
is the first time that
Y
intersects
K
. We claim
\partial K(b)\cap Y(b)\neq\emptyset
. Indeed, the hypotheses and
Proposition

2.10
ensure
Y(b)\setminus\Upsilon(b)\subseteq Z(b)\setminus\Gamma(b)\subseteq\mathrm{int}(U)
. Thus, by
Proposition

A.1
applied to
u(\cdot,t)=\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,Z(t))
and
Proposition

2.1
, any point in
Y(b)\setminus\Upsilon(b)
is a subsequential limit of points in
Y(t_{i})
with
t_{i}\uparrow b
. As
K(t)\cap Y(b)=\emptyset
for
t\in[a,b)
, one has
\mathrm{int}(K(b))\cap Y(b)=\emptyset
and so
\partial K(b)\cap Y(b)\neq\emptyset
.

Let
p\in\partial K(b)\cap Y(b)
. As observed before
p\in\mathrm{int}(U)
. By the adaption of Theorem 8 in
[
HW23
]
to
X
-flow (see Section 11 of
[
HW23
]
) there exists a strong barrier
\hat{K}\colon t\in[\hat{a},b]\mapsto\hat{K}(t)\subseteq\mathrm{int}(U)
with
\hat{a}\in(a,b)
such that
\hat{K}(t)\subseteq\mathrm{int}(K(t))
for
t\in[\hat{a},b)
and such that
\hat{K}(b)\cap\partial K(b)=\{p\}
. Furthermore, we may assume
\hat{K}
is connected as otherwise we can replace
\hat{K}
by its connected component containing
(p,b)
. As
\hat{K}(t)\cap Y(t)=\emptyset
for
t\in[\hat{a},b)
, either (i)
\hat{K}|_{[\hat{a},b)}\cap Z=\emptyset
or (ii)
\hat{K}|_{[\hat{a},b)}\subseteq\mathrm{int}(Z)
. In Case (i), by
Proposition

2.10
one has
\hat{K}(t)\cap\Gamma(t)=\emptyset
for all
t\in[\hat{a},b]
and so
\hat{K}(b)\cap Y(b)\subseteq\hat{K}(b)\cap Z(b)=\emptyset
, a contradiction. In Case (ii), let
\tilde{K}
be a prolongation in time of
\hat{K}
and fattening in space of
\hat{K}
so that
\tilde{K}
is also a strong barrier with
\tilde{K}(\hat{a})\subseteq Z(\hat{a})
. By
Theorem

2.8
, as strong barriers are automatically weak flows,
\tilde{K}\subseteq Z
and so
\hat{K}\subseteq\mathrm{int}(Z)
. In particular,
\hat{K}(b)\cap Y(b)=\emptyset
. This is a contradiction and completes the proof.
∎

3.
Monotone
X
-flows and
X
-mean-convexity

3.1.
Monotone
X
-flows generated by spacetime sets

The boundary of a smooth and strictly
X
-mean-convex compact set moves inwards under the smooth
X
-flow. In this subsection, we define a notion in terms of weak
X
-flows that captures the phenomenon. First of all, we formalize what it means for a set to “move inwards”.

Definition 3.1
.

Fix a set
U\subseteq N
and
T_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. A set
Z\subseteq C_{U,T_{0}}
is
monotone (in
C_{U,T_{0}}
)
if, for
T_{0}\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}
,

Z(t_{2})\subseteq Z(t_{1}).

It is
strictly monotone (in
C_{U,T_{0}}
)
if, for
T_{0}\leq t_{1}<t_{2}
,

Z(t_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{U}(Z(t_{1})).

Here we use the convention that the interior of the empty set is the empty set and omit the cylinder
C_{U,T_{0}}
when it is clear from context.

We record some basic topological properties of strictly monotone sets that will be used in the next section.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix a closed set
U\subseteq N
and
T_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose
Z\subseteq C_{U,T_{0}}
is closed and strictly monotone in
C_{U,T_{0}}
. The following hold:

(1)

Z|_{(T_{0},\infty)}=\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z))|_{(T_{0},\infty)}
;

(2)

Suppose
V\subseteq U
and
\Gamma\subseteq Z
are closed. If
\Gamma\subseteq C_{\partial V,T_{0}}
is strictly monotone in
C_{\partial V,T_{0}}
, then
\mathrm{int}_{C_{\partial V,T_{0}}}(\Gamma)|_{(T_{0},\infty)}\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z)
.

Proof.

As
Z
is closed and
\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z)\subseteq Z
we have

\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z))\subseteq Z.

Hence,

\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z))|_{(T_{0},\infty)}\subseteq Z|_{(T_{0},\infty)}.

For the reverse inclusion, suppose
P=(p,T_{1})\in\partial_{C_{U,T_{0}}}Z
with
T_{1}>T_{0}
. By the strict monotonicity of
Z
, for each
T_{0}<t<T_{1}
, there is a subset
W_{t}
with
p\in W_{t}\subseteq Z(t)
so that
W_{t}
is open in
U
. The monotonicity ensures that

\hat{W}_{t}=[T_{0},t)\times W_{t}\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z)

is an open subset of
C_{U,T_{0}}
. Hence,

\hat{W}=\bigcup_{T_{0}<t<T_{1}}\hat{W}_{t}\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z)

is an open subset of
C_{U,T_{0}}
and
P\in\mathrm{cl}(\hat{W})
. It follows that
P\in\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z))
and so

Z|_{(T_{0},\infty)}\subseteq\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z))|_{(T_{0},\infty)},

which completes the proof of Item (1).

Similarly, suppose
(p,T_{1})\in\mathrm{int}_{C_{\partial V,T_{0}}}(\Gamma)
with
T_{1}>T_{0}
. There is an
\varepsilon>0
so that
p\in\Gamma(T_{1}+2\varepsilon)\subseteq Z(T_{1}+2\varepsilon)
. Strict monotonicity of
Z
implies
p\in\mathrm{int}_{U}(Z(T_{1}+\varepsilon))
and so there is an open subset
W
of
U
such that
p\in W\subseteq Z(T_{1}+\varepsilon)
. Monotonicity ensures

(p,T_{1})\in[T_{0},T_{1}+\varepsilon)\times W\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z).

As
(p,T_{1})
is arbitrary, this proves Item (2).
∎

Definition 3.3
.

Fix a closed set
U\subseteq N
, a
T_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and a closed set
\Gamma\subseteq\partial C_{U,T_{0}}
. A weak
X
-flow,
Z\subseteq C_{U,T_{0}}
, generated by
\Gamma
with starting time
T_{0}
is
(strictly) monotone
if
\Gamma\cap C_{\partial U,T_{0}}
is (strictly) monotone in
C_{\partial U,T_{0}}
and
Z
is a (strictly) monotone in
C_{U,T_{0}}
.

We record the following topological property for strictly monotone biggest flows.

Lemma 3.4
.

Fix a smooth closed set
U\subseteq N
and
T_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose
\Gamma\subseteq\partial C_{U,T_{0}}
is closed and
Z=F^{X}(\Gamma;T_{0})\subseteq C_{U,T_{0}}
is strictly monotone. If
\Gamma(T_{0})=\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{int}_{U}(\Gamma(T_{0}))
, then

Z=\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z)).

Proof.

For
p\in Z(T_{0})=\Gamma(T_{0})=\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{int}_{U}(\Gamma(T_{0})))
, there is a sequence of points
p_{i}\in\mathrm{int}(U)\cap\Gamma(T_{0})
converging to
p
. Using the sphere barriers of
Proposition

2.1
as in the proof of
Proposition

2.10
, one sees that
(p_{i},T_{0})\in\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z)
and so
(p,T_{0})\in\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z)
.
Combined with Item (1) of
Lemma

3.2
, it follows that
Z\subseteq\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(Z))
. The reverse inclusion follows as
Z
is closed and proves the claim.
∎

3.2.
X
-mean-convex spacetime sets and their flows

We introduce a notion of
X
-mean-convexity for, not necessarily regular, subsets of spacetime and prove some basic properties of the biggest flows generated by such sets. This should be thought of as a spacetime and low regularity analogue of strict
X
-mean-convexity of smooth sets. For related results on
X
-mean-convexity of spatial and spacetime sets, see
[
Whi03
]
,
[
HW23
]
and
[
Whi15
]
.

Definition 3.5
.

Fix a closed set
U\subseteq N
and suppose
\Gamma\subseteq\partial C_{U,T_{0}}
is closed. The set
\Gamma
is
spacetime
X
-mean-convex
if

(1)

\Gamma\cap C_{\partial U,T_{0}}
is a strictly monotone set in
C_{\partial U,T_{0}}
;

(2)

There is an
\varepsilon>0
so that
F_{t}^{X}(\Gamma;T_{0})\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{U}(\Gamma(T_{0}))
for all
T_{0}<t<T_{0}+\varepsilon
;

(3)

\mathrm{int}_{\partial C_{U,T_{0}}}(\Gamma)|_{(T_{0},\infty)}\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(F^{X}(\Gamma;T_{0}))
.

When
U
is a smooth and strictly
X
-mean-convex compact set, Proposition
2.10
implies Item (3) holds automatically. For such a
U
,
\Gamma=U\times\left\{T_{0}\right\}
is spacetime
X
-mean-convex.

The main result in this subsection is the following:

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose
(N,X)
is tame.
Let
U\subseteq N
be a smooth and strictly
X
-mean-convex compact set and suppose that
\Gamma\subseteq\partial C_{U,T_{0}}
is spacetime
X
-mean-convex. The following hold:

(1)

F_{t+h}^{X}(\Gamma;T_{0})\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{U}(F_{t}^{X}(\Gamma;T_{0}))
for all
t\geq T_{0}
and
h>0
;

(2)

\partial_{C_{U,T_{0}}}F^{X}(\Gamma;T_{0})=F^{X}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,T_{0}}}\Gamma;T_{0})
.

In particular, the following
partition property
holds

(3)

In
\mathrm{int}(U)
the family
\{\partial_{U}F^{X}_{t}(\Gamma;T_{0})\}_{t\in[T_{1},T_{2})}
forms a partition of
F^{X}_{T_{1}}(\Gamma;T_{0})\setminus F^{X}_{T_{2}}(\Gamma;T_{0})
for each
T_{0}<T_{1}<T_{2}
.

Remark 3.7
.

The biggest flow
F^{X}(\Gamma;T_{0})
of a spacetime
X
-mean-convex set
\Gamma\subseteq\partial C_{U,T_{0}}
is called an
X
-mean-convex flow
and is a strictly monotone flow.

The proof below is in part inspired by the proof of
[
HW23
, Theorem 32]
.

Proof of
Proposition

3.6
.

In this proof we assume, without loss of generality, that
T_{0}=0
and write
F^{X}(\Gamma)=F^{X}(\Gamma;0)
. For the first item, let
h\in(0,\varepsilon)
, where
\varepsilon
is from Item (2) of
Definition

3.5
. Since
\Gamma
is spacetime
X
-mean-convex, the inclusion holds trivially by
Definition

3.5
for
t=0
. By
Proposition

2.9
we have, for
t\geq h
,

F_{t}^{X}(\Gamma)=F^{X}_{t}(\Gamma^{h};h)

where

\Gamma^{h}=(F^{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\times\left\{h\right\})\cup\Gamma|_{[h,\infty)}.

Thus, if
\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}
is the translation in time of
\Gamma^{h}
given by

\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}=(F^{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\times\left\{0\right\})\cup\left(\bigcup_{t\geq h}(\Gamma(t)\times\left\{t-h\right\})\right)

then

F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})=\bigcup_{t\geq h}(F^{X}_{t}(\Gamma^{h};h)\times\left\{t-h\right\})=\bigcup_{t\geq h}(F_{t}^{X}(\Gamma)\times\left\{t-h\right\}).

As
\Gamma
is strictly monotone,
\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}\subseteq\Gamma\subseteq F^{X}(\Gamma)
. Therefore, by
Theorem

2.8
,

(3.1)

F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\subseteq F^{X}(\Gamma).

By
Proposition

2.10
and definition of
\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}
, one has for all
t>0

F^{X}_{t}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\cap\partial U=\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}(t)=\Gamma(t+h).

Combined with the strict monotonicity of
\Gamma
and of
F^{X}(\Gamma)
at
t=0
, it follows that

(3.2)

\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{\partial C_{U,0}}(\Gamma)

which implies

F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\cap\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma=\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}\cap\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{\partial C_{U,0}}(\Gamma)\cap\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma=\emptyset.

Likewise, by
Proposition

2.10
and (
3.2
),

\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}\cap F^{X}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)\subseteq\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}\cap\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{\partial C_{U,0}}(\Gamma)\cap\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma=\emptyset.

Thus, by
Theorem

2.3
,

(3.3)

F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\cap F^{X}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)=\emptyset.

As
U
is a smooth and strictly
X
-mean-convex compact set,
Proposition

2.11
ensures
\partial_{C_{U,0}}F^{X}(\Gamma)
is a weak
X
-flow generated by
\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma
and so, by
Theorem

2.8
,

(3.4)

\partial_{C_{U,0}}F^{X}(\Gamma)\subseteq F^{X}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma).

Thus, combined with (
3.3
), it follows that

F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\cap\partial_{C_{U,0}}F^{X}(\Gamma)=\emptyset

and so, by (
3.1
),

F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,0}}(F^{X}(\Gamma)).

It is a basic topological fact that
(p,t)\in\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,0}}(F^{X}(\Gamma))
implies
p\in\mathrm{int}_{U}(F_{t}^{X}(\Gamma))
and so we conclude that, for all
t\geq 0
and
0<h<\varepsilon
,

F_{t+h}^{X}(\Gamma)=F^{X}_{t}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{U}(F_{t}^{X}(\Gamma)).

Iterating this inclusion gives the first conclusion.

For the second item, first observe by (
3.3
) and definition of
F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})

F^{X}_{t+h}(\Gamma)\cap F^{X}_{t}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)=F^{X}_{t}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\cap F^{X}_{t}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)=\emptyset

for all
t\geq 0
and
0<h<\varepsilon
. By the first item

F^{X}_{T}(\Gamma)\subseteq F^{X}_{t+h}(\Gamma)

for all
T\geq t+h
, and thus

(3.5)

F^{X}_{T}(\Gamma)\cap F^{X}_{t}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)=\emptyset

for all
T>t
. By
Theorem

2.8
, as
\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma
one has
F^{X}_{T}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)\subseteq F^{X}_{T}(\Gamma)
and so

(3.6)

F^{X}_{T}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)\cap F_{t}^{X}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)=\emptyset

for all
T>t
.

Now define the arrival-time function
u\colon\Gamma(0)\to[0,\infty]
by

u(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}t&\mbox{if $x\in F^{X}_{t}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)$}\\
\infty&\mbox{if }x\in\Gamma(0)\setminus\bigcup_{t\geq 0}F^{X}_{t}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma).\end{array}\right.

By (
3.6
), the
F^{X}_{t}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)
are disjoint for distinct
t
, so
u
is well-defined. If
x\in\Gamma(0)\setminus F_{t}^{X}(\Gamma)
, then
(x,0)\in F^{X}(\Gamma)
, but
(x,t)\not\in F^{X}(\Gamma)
. This means there is a
\tau\in[0,t)
so
(x,\tau)\in\partial_{C_{U,0}}F^{X}(\Gamma)
. Combined with (
3.4
), it follows that
x\in F^{X}_{\tau}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)
and so
u(x)<t
.
Conversely, by (
3.5
), if
u(x)<t
then
F^{X}_{u(x)}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)
is disjoint from
F_{t}^{X}(\Gamma)
and so
x\not\in F_{t}^{X}(\Gamma)
. Thus, we have proven that
F_{t}^{X}(\Gamma)=\{u\geq t\}
.
Hence,

\Gamma(\infty)=\bigcap_{t\geq 0}F_{t}^{X}(\Gamma)=\bigcap_{t\geq 0}\left\{u\geq t\right\}=\left\{u=\infty\right\}

is closed. As
F^{X}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)
is also a closed subset of spacetime,
u
is continuous.

By Item (
1
) of
Proposition

A.1
applied to
F^{X}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)
, every point in
\{u=t\}\setminus(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)(t)
is a limit of points in
\left\{u<t\right\}
when
t>0
. Hence, for
t>0
,

\left\{u=t\right\}\setminus(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)(t)=\partial_{U}\left\{u\geq t\right\}\setminus(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)(t)=\partial_{U}F^{X}_{t}(\Gamma)\setminus(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)(t).

By general topology, if
p\in\partial_{U}F^{X}_{t}(\Gamma)
then
(p,t)\in\partial_{C_{U,0}}F^{X}(\Gamma)
, and thus

F^{X}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)\subseteq\partial_{C_{U,0}}F^{X}(\Gamma).

Combined with (
3.4
), this proves the second claim.
∎

We have the following closedness property of
X
-mean-convex flows:

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose
(N,X)
is tame. Let
U\subseteq N
be a smooth and strictly
X
-mean-convex set and suppose
\Gamma,\Gamma_{i}\subseteq\partial C_{U,T_{0}}
, for
i\in\mathbb{N}
, are spacetime
X
-mean-convex. If

(1)

\Gamma_{i}\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{\partial C_{U,T_{0}}}(\Gamma_{i+1})
;

(2)

\mathrm{int}_{\partial C_{U,T_{0}}}(\Gamma)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{i}
,

then
\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,T_{0}}}(F^{X}(\Gamma;T_{0}))=Z_{\infty}
where
Z_{\infty}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}F^{X}(\Gamma_{i};T_{0}).

If, in addition,

(3)

\Gamma(T_{0})=\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{int}_{U}(\Gamma(T_{0})))
,

then
F^{X}(\Gamma;T_{0})=\mathrm{cl}(Z_{\infty})
.

Remark 3.9
.

Hypothesis (3) is needed as can be seen by considering two disjoint balls connected by a line segment – the line segment disappears immediately under the flow. Likewise, the result fails if instead of
Item

2
, one assumed that

\Gamma=\mathrm{cl}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{i}\right).

Indeed, let
X=\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the closed unit disk. Consider
\Gamma_{i}=\Gamma^{+}_{i}\cup\Gamma_{i}^{-}\subseteq\mathbb{D}
, where
\Gamma_{i}^{\pm}
are the sets

\Gamma^{\pm}_{i}=\left\{(x,y):\pm y\geq i^{-1}\right\}\cap\mathbb{D},\mbox{ so }\Gamma=\mathrm{cl}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{i}\right)=\mathbb{D}.

However, as
\mathbf{0}\not\in\Gamma^{\pm}_{i}
and the
\Gamma^{\pm}_{i}
are mean convex, by Proposition
B.1
, for every
t>0
, there is a radius
r>0
so that
B_{r}(\mathbf{0})\cap F_{t}^{X}(\Gamma_{i};0)=\emptyset
for all
i
. Hence, for
t>0
small,

(0,t)\in F^{X}(\Gamma;0)\setminus\mathrm{cl}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}F^{X}(\Gamma_{i};0)\right).

Proof of
Proposition

3.8
.

Without loss of generality we take
T_{0}=0
and write
F^{X}(\Gamma_{i})=F^{X}(\Gamma_{i};0)
and
F^{X}(\Gamma)=F^{X}(\Gamma;0)
, omitting the starting time when it is
0
. For
h>0
, let

\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}=(F^{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\times\left\{0\right\})\cup\left(\bigcup_{t\geq h}\Gamma(t)\times\left\{t-h\right\}\right).

First we show that

(3.7)

\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,0}}(F^{X}(\Gamma))=\bigcup_{h>0}F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}).

To see this, observe that by
Proposition

2.9

F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})=\bigcup_{t\geq h}F^{X}_{t}(\Gamma)\times\left\{t-h\right\}.

Thus, if
(p,t)\in\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,0}}(F^{X}(\Gamma))
, there is an
h>0
so that

p\in F^{X}_{t+h}(\Gamma)=F^{X}_{t}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}).

That is,
(p,t)\in F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})
and so

\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,0}}(F^{X}(\Gamma))\subseteq\bigcup_{h>0}F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}).

We next establish the reverse inclusion. Fix
h>0
. As
\Gamma
is spacetime
X
-mean-convex, it is strictly monotone and, moreover, by
Proposition

3.6
so is
F^{X}(\Gamma)
. Hence,
\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{\partial C_{U,0}}(\Gamma)
and so, by
Theorem

2.8
,
F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\subseteq F^{X}(\Gamma)
, and by
Proposition

2.10

F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\cap\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma=\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}\cap F^{X}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)=\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}\cap\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma=\emptyset.

As
\Gamma
is spacetime
X
-mean-convex, it follows from
Proposition

3.6
and
Theorem

2.3
that

F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\cap\partial_{C_{U,0}}F^{X}(\Gamma)=F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\cap F^{X}(\partial_{\partial C_{U,0}}\Gamma)=\emptyset.

Hence,
F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,0}}(F^{X}(\Gamma))
and so

\bigcup_{h>0}F^{X}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{h})\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{C_{U,0}}(F^{X}(\Gamma))

since
h>0
is arbitrary. This proves the claim.

We now establish, for any
T_{1}>0
and
h>0
, the existence of
i_{0}=i_{0}(T_{1},h)
so that

\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}|_{[0,T_{1}]}\subseteq\Gamma_{i_{0}}.

Indeed, strict monotonicity of
\Gamma
and Hypothesis (2) ensure

\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}|_{[0,T_{1}]}\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{\partial C_{U,0}}(\Gamma)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{i}.

That is,
\{\mathrm{int}_{\partial C_{U,0}}(\Gamma_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is an open cover of the compact set
\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}|_{[0,T_{1}]}
and so there is a finite subcover. Thus, one may choose
i_{0}
sufficiently large so that

\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}|_{[0,T_{1}]}\subseteq\mathrm{int}_{\partial C_{U,0}}(\Gamma_{i_{0}})\subseteq\Gamma_{i_{0}}.

Now fix
T_{1}>0
and
h>0
, and assume in the following that
i\geq i_{0}
. Set
\hat{\Gamma}_{i}^{h}=\Gamma_{i}\cap\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}
. By
Theorem

2.8
, as
\hat{\Gamma}^{h}_{i}\subseteq\Gamma_{i}\subseteq F^{X}(\Gamma_{i})
,

F^{X}(\hat{\Gamma}^{h}_{i})\subseteq F^{X}(\Gamma_{i}).

Moreover, the choice of
i
ensures

\hat{\Gamma}^{h}_{i}|_{[0,T_{1}]}=\tilde{\Gamma}^{h}|_{[0,T_{1}]}.

Hence, by uniqueness,
Theorem

(3)

For

(4)

In particular, for large

Proof.

, each contained in one of the open sets.

To see this, we prove the Bernstein-type theorem

for limit flows to

. With this and

[

gives the claim. Indeed, any limit flow to

consists of a monotone weak flow and an integral Brakke flow as limit flows to a mean-convex mean curvature flow. The proof of

5.4

.

By

,

into two parts,
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{i}^{\pm}

Hence, the

).
∎

We are now ready to prove the regularity and long-time behavior for the flow

.

Theorem 5.9
.

The boundary

(2)

If, in addition,

Proof.

First, using Propositions
5.3
and

in place of
[

, Theorems 5.3 and 3.9]
, the proof of
[

, Theorem 5.4]

,

(1)

6.
Existence of monotone Morse flow line

We show the existence of the monotone Morse flow line – i.e.,
Theorem

1.3
– using Theorem
1.5
and by constructing an appropriate ancient expander mean convex flow. This will be done by showing we can take limits of solutions constructed in
[
BW22c
]
. Throughout, we will fix
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, a smooth self-expander asymptotic to a
C^{3}
-regular cone
\mathcal{C}
. As remarked in
Section

1
,
\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\rho\Sigma=\mathcal{C}
in
C^{2,\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\})
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Before proceeding, we introduce some conventions and notations we use in this section. The time variable
s
is used for MCFs, while the time variable
t=\log s
is used for EMCFs. The hypersurfaces along a MCF will be denoted with tildes. We use the following notations for norms on functions
g\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
.
For an integer
k\geq 0
, let

\displaystyle\|g\|_{W,k}^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}^{i}g|^{2}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}

be a weighted integral norm. Denote the
C^{k}
and, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the Hölder norm by

\|g\|_{C^{k}(\Sigma)}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sup_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}^{i}g|\mbox{ and }\left\|g\right\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\Sigma)}=\left\|g\right\|_{C^{k}(\Sigma)}+[\nabla_{\Sigma}^{k}g]_{\alpha;\Sigma}.

Here
[g]_{\alpha;\Sigma}
is the (unweighted) Hölder seminorm

\displaystyle[g]_{\alpha;\Sigma}=\sup_{{p\neq q\in\Sigma,q\in B_{\delta}^{\Sigma}(p)}}\frac{\left|g(p)-g(q)\right|}{d_{\Sigma}(p,q)^{\alpha}}

where
d_{\Sigma}
denotes the geodesic distance on
\Sigma
and
B^{\Sigma}_{\delta}(p)
is the geodesic ball in
\Sigma
centered at
p
with radius
\delta
.
For simplicity, we also write
C^{k,\alpha}=C^{k,\alpha}(\Sigma)
and
[\cdot]_{\alpha;\Sigma}=[\cdot]_{\alpha}
. The parabolic Hölder space
C^{k,\alpha}_{P}(I\times\Sigma)
consists of functions
f\colon I\times\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{P}(I\times\Sigma)}=\sum_{i+2j\leq k}\sup_{I\times\Sigma}\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}^{i}\partial_{t}^{j}f\right|+\sum_{i+2j=k}[\nabla_{\Sigma}^{i}\partial_{t}^{j}f]_{\alpha;I\times\Sigma}<\infty,

where
[f]_{\alpha;I\times\Sigma}
is the Hölder seminorm

\displaystyle[f]_{\alpha;I\times\Sigma}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t_{1},p)\neq(t_{2},q)\in I\times\Sigma\\
|t_{1}-t_{2}|<\delta^{2},q\in B_{\delta}^{\Sigma}(p)\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|f(p)-f(q)\right|}{d_{\Sigma}(p,q)^{\alpha}+\left|t_{1}-t_{2}\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}.

We write
C^{k,\alpha}_{P}(I\times\Sigma)=C^{k,\alpha}_{P}
if
I
is understood. It is straightforward to extend the above notations to tensor fields.
In what follows, we will fix
\delta
and
\alpha
.

Given a function
u\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
, let
\Gamma_{u}
denote the normal graph of
u
over
\Sigma
, i.e.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{u}=\{\mathbf{x}(p)+u(p)\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}(p)\mid p\in\Sigma\}.

We need the following result ensuring the expander mean curvature of
\Gamma_{u}
can be computed from the function
u
in a controlled manner.

Lemma 6.1
.

There is an
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(\Sigma)>0
so that if
\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\leq\eta_{0}
, then

\mathbf{F}_{u}\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1},p\mapsto\mathbf{x}(p)+u(p)\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}(p)

is a
C^{2,\alpha}
embedding. Moreover, the expander mean curvature of
\Gamma_{u}
satisfies

H_{\mathbf{F}_{u}}+\frac{\mathbf{F}_{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\mathbf{F}_{u}}}{2}=-L_{\Sigma}u+Q(u,\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}u,\nabla_{\Sigma}u,\nabla_{\Sigma}^{2}u)

where

L_{\Sigma}=\Delta_{\Sigma}+\frac{\mathbf{x}}{2}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}

and
Q
is a homogeneous degree-two polynomial of the form

Q(\tau,\rho,\mathbf{d},\mathbf{T})=\mathbf{a}(\tau,\rho,\mathbf{d},\mathbf{T})\cdot\mathbf{d}+b(\tau,\mathbf{d},\mathbf{T})\tau.

Moreover, the
\mathbf{a}
and
b
are homogeneous degree-one polynomials whose coefficients are functions on
\Sigma
that are bounded in
C^{0,\alpha}(\Sigma)
by
\bar{M}=\bar{M}(\Sigma)>0
.

Proof.

First observe that, as
\Sigma
is
C^{2,\alpha}
-asymptotically conical, it has the property that, in a uniform sized ball around each point
p\in\Sigma
, the unit normal has uniform
C^{2,\alpha}
estimates. For any fixed
R_{1}\geq 1
, this is true for
p\in B_{R_{1}}\cap\Sigma
by standard interior elliptic estimates. For
p\in\Sigma\setminus B_{R_{1}}
, as
\Sigma
is
C^{2,\alpha}
-asymptotically conical, it follows essentially from the definition – see
[
BW21a
, Proposition 4.3]
– that there are constants
\gamma>0
and
C_{1}>0
so that, with
R=|\mathbf{x}(p)|\geq R_{1}\geq 1
, on
B_{\gamma R}(p)\cap\Sigma
one has the estimates

\|\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}\|_{C^{0}}+R\|\nabla_{\Sigma}\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}\|_{C^{0}}+R^{1+\alpha}[\nabla_{\Sigma}\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}]_{\alpha}+R\|\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}\|_{C^{0}}+R^{1+\alpha}[\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}]_{\alpha}\leq C_{1}

A consequence is that, on
B_{\gamma R}(p)\cap\Sigma
,

\|\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}\|_{C^{0}}+R^{\alpha}[\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}]_{\alpha}\leq 2C_{1}.

Using,
L_{\Sigma}\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}
– see
[
BW21b
, Lemma 5.9]
–
it follows that on
B_{\gamma R}(p)\cap\Sigma

\|\Delta_{\Sigma}\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}\|_{C^{0}}+[\Delta_{\Sigma}\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}]_{\alpha}\leq C_{1}^{\prime}.

Hence, applying standard interior Schauder estimates
[
GT01
, 15, Theorem
6.2]
on a fixed scale around
p
one obtains the desired bounds on the unit normal.
Hence, for
\eta_{0}
sufficiently small depending only on
\Sigma
, the map
\mathbf{F}_{u}

is a
C^{2,\alpha}
embedding of
\Gamma_{u}=\mathbf{F}_{u}(\Sigma)
.

Finally, the hypotheses ensure that
[
BW22b
, Lemma A.2]
gives the desired form of the
Q
,
\mathbf{a}
and
b
and the boundedness of the coefficients of
\mathbf{a}
and
b
. However, by using the established uniform Hölder estimates on
\mathbf{x}|_{\Sigma},\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma},\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}
one readily checks that the proof of
[
BW22b
, Lemma A.2]
also gives the required Hölder norm estimates.
∎

Next we establish a lemma that shows a self-expander cannot be written as a one-sided normal graph with sufficiently small
C^{2}
norm over a strictly unstable expander – i.e., each connected component of the expander is unstable.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose
\Sigma
is strictly unstable.
There is an
\eta_{1}=\eta_{1}(\Sigma)>0
depending on
\Sigma
so that if
u\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
is positive and
\|u\|_{C^{2}}\leq\eta_{1}
, then
\Sigma^{\prime}=\Gamma_{u}
, is not a self-expander.

Proof.

Suppose the graph of
u>0
is a self-expander, by
[
BW22b
, Lemma A.2]
, there is a constant
\bar{M}_{0}>0
depending on
\Sigma
so that if
\varepsilon<(8\bar{M}_{0})^{-1}
and
\|u\|_{C^{2}}\leq\varepsilon
, then
u
satisfies

|L_{\Sigma}u|\leq C_{1}\varepsilon(|\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}u|+|\nabla_{\Sigma}u|+|u|).

where
C_{1}
depends only on
\Sigma
.

For
\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}=\Delta_{\Sigma}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}
, the drift Laplacian, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies,

-\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}\log u\geq(1-C_{1}\varepsilon)|\nabla_{\Sigma}\log u|^{2}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2}C_{1}\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}C_{1}\varepsilon|\mathbf{x}|^{2},

Using the Poincaré inequality of
[
BW23
, Lemma A.1]
one has

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\psi^{2}

\displaystyle(-\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}\log u)e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\geq(1-C_{1}\varepsilon)\int_{\Sigma}\psi^{2}|\nabla_{\Sigma}\log u|^{2}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\left(|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2}C_{1}\varepsilon\right)\psi^{2}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}-8C_{1}\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

If
\psi
is a compactly supported smooth function on
\Sigma
it follows from an integration by parts and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that when
C_{1}\varepsilon<1
,

\displaystyle(1-C_{1}\varepsilon)^{-1}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle+(1-C_{1}\varepsilon)\int_{\Sigma}\psi^{2}|\nabla_{\Sigma}\log u|^{2}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Sigma}\psi^{2}(-\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}\log u)e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

Combining these two inequalities yields

\displaystyle(1+8C_{1}\varepsilon)\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\geq(1-C_{1}\varepsilon)\int_{\Sigma}\left(|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2}C_{1}\varepsilon\right)\psi^{2}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

Hence, the boundedness of the second fundamental form, implies that, up to shrinking
\varepsilon
,

\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{\Sigma}\psi|^{2}-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\psi^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\psi^{2})e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\geq-K\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma}\psi^{2}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}

for some
K>0
that depends only on
\Sigma.

By
[
BW23
, Lemma 4.1]
,
L_{\Sigma}
has a discrete spectrum and it follows from the above that
the lowest eigenvalue satisfies
\lambda_{0}(L_{\Sigma})\geq-K\varepsilon
.
However, as
\Sigma
is strictly unstable,
\lambda_{0}(L_{\Sigma})<0
. Hence, when
\eta_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{-K^{-1}\lambda_{0}(L_{\Sigma}),8(\bar{M}_{0})^{-1}\right\}>0
there can be no such
u
satisfying
\|u\|_{C^{2}}\leq\eta_{1}
.
∎

We now begin the construction of the flow line. It will be convenient to have some notion for EMCFs that are near
\Sigma
in a controlled way and that have certain good properties.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
\eta_{0}
be the constant given by
Lemma

6.1
.
For

and

,

(2)

\Sigma_{t}=\Gamma_{v(t,\cdot)}

;

(3)

t\mapsto\Sigma_{t}
is a EMCF for
t\in[0,T]
.

We say such a

satisfies a
reverse Poincaré inequality with constant

(4)

For all
t\in[0,T]
,
\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{W,1}\leq\kappa\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{W,0}<\infty
.

We say such a

if, in addition,

(5)

For all
0\leq t\leq t^{\prime}\leq T
,
0\leq v(t,\cdot)\leq v(t^{\prime},\cdot)
.

By

6.1
,

, an

-uniform EMCF on

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=L_{\Sigma}v+Q(v,\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}v,\nabla_{\Sigma}v,\nabla^{2}_{\Sigma}v).

Here

is the homogeneous degree-two polynomial given by

which we will sometimes shorten to

We may treat the equation

Standard existence results for MCF establish a short time existence and uniqueness in the class of

Proposition 6.4

Given

\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\Sigma,\eta)>0

∎

We now establish estimates that will allow us to iterate Proposition

.
First, we recall an interior

Proposition 6.5

Suppose

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=a^{ij}\nabla_{ij}v+b^{j}\nabla_{j}v+cv.

on

\|a^{ij}\|_{C_{P}^{0,\alpha}}+R\|b^{j}\|_{C_{P}^{0,\alpha}}+R^{2}\|c\|_{C_{P}^{0,\alpha}}\leq C_{0}

.
Then there is a constant

-mean-curvature of
\partial K
at

is non-vanishing and points into

H^{X}_{K}(p)=-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}_{\partial K}\mathbf{n}_{K}(p)+X(p)\cdot\mathbf{n}_{K}(p)<0

\mathbf{n}_{K}
is the unit outward normal on
\partial K
.

We have the following approximation result:

Theorem B.3
.

Suppose
(N,X)
is tame. Let
V\subseteq N
be a strictly viscosity
X
-mean-convex compact set with
C^{1,1}
-regular boundary. For every sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a compact subset,
V_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathrm{int}(V)
, so that:

(1)

V_{\varepsilon}
is strictly
X
-mean-convex with smooth boundary;

(2)

\partial V_{\varepsilon}
is a
C^{1}
exponential graph over
\partial V
with
C^{1}
norm bounded above by
\varepsilon
.

In fact, there is
\varepsilon_{1}
small such that, for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{1}
, the sets
V_{\varepsilon}
can be constructed so that
V_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathrm{int}(V_{\varepsilon})
for
\varepsilon<\varepsilon^{\prime}
, and

\mathrm{int}(V)\setminus V_{\varepsilon_{1}}=\bigcup_{0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{1}}V_{\varepsilon}.

In order to prove this we will need the following uniqueness result that seems to be not quite standard; see
[
MS08
,
Her17
]
for related results.

Proposition B.4
.

Suppose
(N,X)
is tame. If
\Sigma\subseteq N
is a (two-sided)
C^{1,1}
-regular closed hypersurface, then there is a unique solution,
t\in[0,T)\mapsto\Sigma(t)
, of the
X
-mean-curvature flow which is smooth in maximal existence interval
(0,T)
and so
\Sigma(t)\to\Sigma(0)=\Sigma
in
C^{1}
as
t\to 0^{+}
.

Proof.

The existence follows readily from the local regularity of White
[
Whi05
]
. Hence, it remains only to show that it is the unique smooth flow.

To that end, pick a small
\delta>0
and use a convolution argument to approximate
\Sigma
by a smooth hypersurface,
\Sigma^{\prime}
, so that
\Sigma
can be written as a normal exponential graph over
\Sigma^{\prime}
by a function whose
C^{1}
norm is bounded above by
\delta
. Moreover, there is a constant
C>0
independent of
\delta
so that the
C^{1,1}
norm of the function is bounded above by
C
.

For two
X
-mean-curvature flows,
t\in[0,T)\mapsto\Sigma_{i}(t)
with
\Sigma_{i}(0)=\Sigma
for
i=1,2
, there is
T^{\prime}<T
so that, for all
t\in(0,T^{\prime})
, one can express both of these as normal graphs,
u_{i}(t,\cdot)
,
i=1,2
, over
\Gamma
with
C^{1}
norm bounded by
2\delta
and
C^{1,1}
norm bounded by
2C
. In fact, for
t\in(0,T^{\prime})
both functions are smooth and will have
C^{2}
norm bounded by
2C
. If
v=u_{1}-u_{2}
, then
v
solves a uniformly parabolic equation in divergence form with coefficients that are
C^{1}
. Moreover, at time
t=0
,
v
identically vanishes. It follows from the strong maximum principle that
v
identically vanishes and so
\Sigma_{1}(t)=\Sigma_{2}(t)
for all
t\in[0,T^{\prime})
. As the flows remain compact (by
[
HW23
, Theorem 29]
), uniqueness up to
T
follows by iteration.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

.

By standard convolution arguments and the hypothesis that
\partial V
is
C^{1,1}
-regular, there is
C>0
so that, for every sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0

K_{\varepsilon}\subseteq N

(1)

K_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathrm{int}(V)
;

(2)

\sup_{\partial K_{\varepsilon}}|A_{\partial K_{\varepsilon}}|\leq C

(3)

\partial K_{\varepsilon}
can be written as a

-graph over
\partial V
with

\varepsilon

Let
t\in[0,T_{\varepsilon})\mapsto K_{\varepsilon}(t)
be a continuous family of compact sets with

and whose boundary is a smooth
X
-mean-curvature flow. By Items (2) and (3) and
[

]
, there is a

so that the maximum time of existence

for all small
\varepsilon
.

We claim that
K_{\varepsilon}(t)\subseteq\mathrm{int}(V)
for all
t\in(0,T_{\varepsilon})
. To see this, we observe that
K_{\varepsilon}(0)=K_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathrm{int}(V)
by construction. The curvature bound implies that there is a
T_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\in(0,T_{\varepsilon})
so that for
t\in[0,T_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}]
,

. If the claim is not true, then there is a first time

for which the inclusion fails. Clearly,
t^{\prime}>T_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
,

and there is a

V
is strictly viscosity

-mean-convex, this implies that the
X
-mean-curvature of
\partial K_{\varepsilon}(t^{\prime})
at

K_{\varepsilon}(t^{\prime})
which is impossible, as it would mean at some previous time

was not contained in
V
.

Let
t\in[0,T)\mapsto K(t)
be a subsequential limit of the
t\in[0,T)\mapsto K_{\varepsilon}(t)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Thus,
t\in[0,T)\mapsto\partial K(t)
is a solution of the
X
-mean-curvature flow which is smooth on
(0,T)
and

in

as

. As

and small

, the nature of the convergence ensures that

for all

with equality at time

is strictly viscosity

-mean-convex and
\partial K(t)

for all
t\in(0,T)
.

By the uniqueness result of Proposition
B.4
, one appeals to
[

, Theorem 31]
to see that the biggest

-flow of

agrees with the domain bounded by the smooth

-mean-curvature flow as long as the latter exists, i.e.,

. As

, it follows that

To conclude the proof we observe, for sufficiently small

Appendix C
A property of the biggest flow of an asymptotically conical hypersurface

Throughout this section, we take
X=-\frac{\mathbf{x}}{2}
. We show that the biggest
X
-flow of a asymptotic conical hypersurface agrees with the smooth EMCF for a short time.

Proposition C.1

(cf.
[
HW23
, Theorem 31]
)
.

Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth hypersurface that is
C^{2}
-asymptotic to a
C^{2}
-regular cone
\mathcal{C}
. There is an
\varepsilon>0
so that the smooth
X
-mean-curvature flow starting from
\Sigma
,
t\mapsto\Sigma(t)
exists for
t\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
and agrees with the biggest
X
-flow
F^{X}(\Sigma\times\{0\};0)
. Moreover, if
\Sigma=\partial U
for a closed subset
U
, then for
t\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
,
\Sigma(t)=\partial F^{X}_{t}(U\times\{0\};0)
.

Proof.

As
\Sigma
is an embedded hypersurface, there are disjoint open sets,
U^{\pm}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\Sigma
so
\Sigma=\partial U^{\pm}
.
As
\Sigma
is smooth and
C^{2}
-asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
, there is a uniform upper bound on the norm of the second fundamental form, and so there are sequences of smooth compact sets
V_{i}^{\pm}\subseteq U^{\pm}
so that
U^{\pm}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}V_{i}^{\pm}
and
\Sigma_{i}^{\pm}=\partial V_{i}^{\pm}
converge with multiplicity one to
\Sigma
in
C^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
as
i\to\infty
. As
X=-\frac{\mathbf{x}}{2}
, by the change of variable
5.1
, we may directly apply Ilmanen’s elliptic regularization scheme
[
Ilm94
]
for the usual MCF. In particular, there are small perturbations of
V_{i}^{\pm}
so that the biggest flows of the
\Sigma_{i}^{\pm}
do not fatten, but all other properties are preserved. Thus, there are unit-regular integral Brakke
X
-flows,
\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\pm}
from
\Sigma_{i}^{\pm}
with

\Sigma_{i}^{\pm}(t)=\mathop{\mathrm{spt}}(\mathcal{M}_{i}(t))\subseteq F_{t}^{X}(\Sigma_{i}^{\pm}\times\left\{0\right\};0)\subseteq F_{t}^{X}(V_{i}^{\pm}\times\left\{0\right\};0).

By
[
Whi05
]
and standard compactness results, up to passing to a subsequence, the
\Sigma_{i}^{\pm}
converge in
C^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times[0,\varepsilon_{0}])
to smooth
X
-mean-curvature flows
t\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]\mapsto\Sigma^{\pm}(t)
that satisfy
\Sigma^{\pm}(0)=\Sigma
. As this convergence is locally smooth
when
t\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, there are open subsets
U^{\pm}(t)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
so that, for such
t
,
\Sigma^{\pm}=\partial U^{\pm}(t)
and

U^{\pm}(t)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}F_{t}(V_{i}^{\pm}\times\left\{0\right\};0).

Moreover, as
\Sigma
is
C^{2}
-asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
, the uniqueness result of
[
CY07
]
applies, and so there is only one smooth
X
-mean-curvature flow out of
\Sigma
, i.e.,
\Sigma(t)=\Sigma^{\pm}(t)
for all
t\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
.
By
[
HW23
, Theorem 30]
, for
t\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
,
F^{X}_{t}(\Sigma\times\{0\};0)
is disjoint from
F^{X}_{t}(V_{i}^{\pm}\times\left\{0\right\};0)
, and so
F_{t}^{X}(\Sigma\times\{0\};0)\subseteq\Sigma(t)
. As the set
\left\{(x,t)\mid x\in\Sigma(t),t\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]\right\}
is a weak
X
-flow, it follows that
F^{X}_{t}(\Sigma\times\{0\};0)=\Sigma(t)
for
t\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]
.

Finally, set
U^{+}=\mathrm{int}(U)
. By
[
HW23
, Theorems 30]
,
F^{X}(U\times\{0\};0)
is disjoint from the biggest
X
-flow of the
V_{i}^{-}
and , likewise, contains the biggest flow of
V_{i}^{+}
. Hence,
F^{X}_{t}(U\times\{0\};0)\cap U^{-}=\emptyset
and
U^{+}\subseteq F^{X}_{t}(U\times\{0\};0)
. As
F^{X}(\Sigma\times\{0\};0)\subseteq F^{X}(U\times\{0\};0)
by the definition of the biggest flow and
\Sigma(t)=\partial U^{\pm}(t)
, the last claim follows.
∎

Discovery of the cyclic C 5 H radical in TMC-1 Thanks: Based on observations carried out with the Yebes 40m telescope (projects 19A003, 20A014, 20D023, and 21A011). The 40m radio telescope at Yebes Observatory is operated by the Spanish Geographic Institute (IGN; Ministerio de Transportes, Movilidad y Agenda Urbana).
C. Cabezas, M. Agúndez, R. Fuentetaja, Y. Endo, N. Marcelino, B. Tercero, J. R. Pardo, P. de Vicente and J. Cernicharo

Abstract
Cyclic C 5 H ( c c -C 5 H), the radical formed by substituting an ethynyl group CCH for the hydrogen atom in the c c -C 3 H radical, has been detected for the first time in the space. The c c -C 5 H species is an isomer of the well-known linear radical l l -C 5 H and is ∼ \sim 6 kcal/mol less stable. A total of 17 rotational transitions were detected for the c c -C 5 H species in TMC-1 within the 31.0–50.3 GHz range using the Yebes 40m radio telescope. We derive a column density of (9.0 ± \pm 0.9) × \times 10 10 cm -2 for c c -C 5 H. Additionally, we observed 12 lines for l l -C 5 H and derive a column density for it of (1.3 ± \pm 0.3) × \times 10 12 cm -2 , which results in an abundance ratio c c -C 5 H/ l l -C 5 H of 0.069. This is in sharp contrast with the value found for the analogue system c c -C 3 H/ l l -C 3 H, whose ratio is 5.5 in TMC-1. We discuss the formation of c c -C 5 H and conclude that this radical is probably formed in the reaction of atomic carbon with diacetylene.

The observational data used in this article consist of spectra of

Receiver temperatures in 2019 and 2020 varied from 22 K at 32 GHz to 42 K at 50 GHz. The backends are 16 Fourier transform spectrometers, which provide a bandwidth of 8

The rotational spectrum of the
c
-C
5
H radical was observed using a Balle-Flygare narrow-band-type FTMW spectrometer operating in the frequency region 4-40 GHz
(

;

-C
5
H was produced in a supersonic expansion by a pulsed electric discharge of a gas mixture of C
2
H
2
(0.3%) diluted in neon or argon. The gas mixture was flowed through a pulsed-solenoid valve that is accommodated in the backside of one of the cavity mirrors and aligned parallel to the optical axis of the resonator. A pulse voltage of 1000 V with a duration of 450
\mu

The resulting products generated in the discharge were then probed by FTMW spectroscopy, which allowed small hyperfine splittings to be resolved.

Previous studies have shown that heavier inert gases have an enhanced cooling efficiency in supersonic expansions due to the larger collision energies they provide
(
Cabezas et al. 2018

1
shows the spectra recorded using argon and neon gases. When argon is used, only the

4
Chemistry

In order to shed some light on the formation of
c
-C
5
H
in
TMC-1
, we carried out gas-phase chemical modelling calculations similar to those presented in

. Briefly, we adopted physical conditions typical of cold dark clouds: a volume density of H nuclei of 2
\times

-3
, a gas kinetic temperature of 10 K, a visual extinction of 30 mag, a cosmic-ray ionization rate of H
2
of 1.3
\times

-17

, and the so-called set of low-metal elemental abundances (e.g.,

)
, which has been updated with results from

and expanded with the subset of gas-phase chemical reactions involving C

\rm c-C_{3}H+C_{2}H\rightarrow c-C_{5}H+H.

We thus included this reaction with a rate coefficient of 3

\displaystyle\rm C+C_{4}H_{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\rm l-C_{5}H+H,

(2a)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\rm c-C_{5}H+H,

(2b)

where both the linear and cyclic isomers of C

5
Conclusions

We have reported the first identification of the
c
-C
5
H radical towards TMC-1. We observed 17 rotational transitions within the 31.0–50.3 GHz range using the Yebes 40m radio telescope. The derived
c
-C
5
H/
l
-C
5
H abundance ratio of 0.069 is very different from that found for
c
-C
3
H/
l
-C
3
H, whose ratio is 5.5 in TMC-1. A state-of-the-art chemical model reproduces the observed abundance of
c
-C
5
H and indicates that this radical can probably be formed in the reaction of atomic carbon with diacetylene.

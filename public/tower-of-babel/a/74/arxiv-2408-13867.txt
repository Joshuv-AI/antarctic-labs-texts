Partitions into triples with equal products and Families of Elliptic Curves
Ahmed El Amine Youmbai, Arman Shamsi Zargar, Maksym Voznyy

Abstract
. Let 𝒮 ℓ ​ ( M , N ) {\mathcal{S}_{\ell}}(M,N) denote a set of ℓ \ell triples of positive integers having the same sum M M and the same product N N . For each 2 ≤ ℓ ≤ 4 2\leq\ell\leq 4 we establish a connection between a subset of 𝒮 ℓ ​ ( M , N ) {\mathcal{S}_{\ell}}(M,N) with (integral) parametric elements and a family of elliptic curves. When ℓ = 2 \ell=2 and 3 3 , we use certain known subsets of 𝒮 ℓ ​ ( M , N ) {\mathcal{S}_{\ell}}(M,N) with parametric elements and respectively find families of elliptic curves of generic rank ≥ 5 \geq 5 and ≥ 6 \geq 6 , while for ℓ = 4 \ell=4 we first obtain a subset of 𝒮 ℓ ​ ( M , N ) {\mathcal{S}_{\ell}}(M,N) with parametric elements, then construct a family of elliptic curves of generic rank ≥ 8 \geq 8 . Finally, we perform a computer search within these families to find specific curves with rank ≥ 11 \geq 11 and in particular we found two curves of rank 14 14 .

Introduction

For any positive integers
\ell
and
n

{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}^{(n)}(M,N)
denote a set of
\ell

(x_{j1}^{n},x_{j2}^{n},x_{j3}^{n})
of positive integers having the same sum
M
and the same product
N
. For the
j

\displaystyle M_{j}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}x_{ji}^{n},

\displaystyle N_{j}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{3}x_{ji}^{n},

\displaystyle T_{j}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<k\leq 3}x_{ji}^{n}x_{jk}^{n},

\displaystyle(1\leq j\leq\ell).

We drop the notation “
(n)
” from the terminology when
n=1
.

In this context, we are interested in the following partitioning problem, which from the geometric point of view, is equivalent to examining the existence of
\ell

Problem 1.1
.

For given positive integers
\ell\geq 2
and
n

{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}^{(n)}(M,N)
with (integral) parametric elements.

Note that solving this problem is equivalent to finding a parametric solution to the symmetric system of diophantine equations

(1.1)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=M_{j}^{(n)},

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=N_{j}^{(n)},

for all
j=1,\ldots,\ell

On the other direction, making any connection between algebraic or geometric notions and elliptic curves has been of interest to researchers. Specifically, symmetric diophantine equations of certain forms have led to families of elliptic curves with higher ranks, see, for example,
[
1

17

[

illustrated such a connection between positive rank elliptic curves of the form

and triples satisfying (

. In 2015, Sadek and El-Sissi
[

extended this result by studying the same family assigned to triples satisfying (

\geq 2
and
\geq 3

[

constructed several families of elliptic curves whose generic ranks range from
\geq 8
to
\geq 12
coming from the system
M=M_{j}
and

for some
j

In this work, we establish a new connection between each of certain solutions of (

and a family of elliptic curves, whose torsion subgroups are trivial in general. When
(\ell,n)=(2,1)
and

\geq 5

\geq 6

Families of elliptic curves related to (

First we recall the following specialisation theorem of Néron
[
34
, Theorem 20.1]
that we need for our next results:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
K
be a number field, and let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over the function field
K({\mathbb{P}^{n}})
. Then there are infinitely many points
t\in{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(K)
such that the specialisation homomorphism

\sigma_{t}:E(K({\mathbb{P}^{n}}))\rightarrow E_{t}(K)

is injective. The set of
t
for which
\sigma_{t}
is noninjective forms a thin set.

2.1.
A family with rank
\geq 5

According to
[
11
, Subsection 3.1]
, Choudhry has fully determined the set
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}(M,N)
by finding two complete solutions to the related equations (
1.1
). Without loss of generality, we consider his first solution, then

{\mathcal{A}}:={\mathcal{A}}(M,N):={\mathcal{S}}_{2}(M,N)=\{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}),(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\}

where

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=p(s+rt),

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=q(s+pt),

\displaystyle a_{3}

\displaystyle=r(s+qt),

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=q(s+rt),

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=r(s+pt),

\displaystyle b_{3}

\displaystyle=p(s+qt).

To this set, we assign an elliptic curve described by the equation

y^{2}=(x+a_{1}a_{2})(x+a_{2}a_{3})(x+a_{1}a_{3})+e^{2}_{1}x^{2},

where
e_{1}
is a nonzero rational number which will be defined later.

The above elliptic curve can be rewritten as:

(2.1)

y^{2}=x^{3}+(T_{1}+e_{1}^{2})x^{2}+MNx+N^{2},

where
T_{1}=a_{1}a_{2}+a_{1}a_{3}+a_{2}a_{3}
.

By imposing

(2.2)

T_{1}+e_{1}^{2}=T_{2}+e_{2}^{2},

for some rational
e_{2}
, the curve (
2.1
) will contain the seven rational points
P_{1}=(0,N)
,
P_{ij}=(-a_{i}a_{j},a_{i}a_{j}e_{1})
and
Q_{ij}=(-b_{i}b_{j},b_{i}b_{j}e_{2})
,
1\leq i<j\leq 3
. Here,
T_{2}=b_{1}b_{2}+b_{1}b_{3}+b_{2}b_{3}
.

The quadratic (
2.2
) is easily accomplished by parametrising

e_{1}=\frac{k^{2}+T_{2}-T_{1}}{2k},\ e_{2}=\frac{k^{2}+T_{1}-T_{2}}{2k},

for any nonzero rational number
k
.

By this description, we have thus constructed a family of elliptic curves, defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}(p,q,r,s,t,k)
, coming from the elements of
{\mathcal{A}}
, that has the above rational points. We denote this family by
{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Notice that the points
P_{ij}
are co-linear (because their
(x,y)
-coordinates satisfy the linear equation
y+e_{1}x=0
), showing that at most two of them can be linearly independent. The same result holds for the points
Q_{ij}
. Therefore, at most five points of the seven points of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{A}}
can be linearly independent.

In the next theorem we show that five of seven points of
E_{\mathcal{A}}
are linearly independent for infinitely many tuples
(p,q,r,s,t,k)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For the set of all tuples
(p,q,r,s,t,k)
except for a thin subset, the rank of the family
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{A}}
is at least five with the five linearly independent points
P_{1}
,
P_{12}
,
P_{13}
,
Q_{12}
,
Q_{13}
.

Proof.

By Néron’s specialisation theorem (Theorem
2.1
), in order to prove that the family
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{A}}
has rank
\geq 5
over
{\mathbb{Q}}(p,q,r,s,t,k)
, it suffices to find a specialisation
(p,q,r,s,t,k)=(p_{0},q_{0},r_{0},s_{0},t_{0},k_{0})
such that the above points in the statement are linearly independent on the specialised curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. We take
(p,q,r,s,t,k)=(1,4,2,4,1,1)
for which we have

{\mathcal{A}}(42,1920)=\{(6,20,16),(24,10,8)\}

and

T_{1}=536,\ T_{2}=512,\ e_{1}=-\frac{23}{2},\ e_{2}=\frac{25}{2}.

The five points are linearly independent and of infinite order on the specialised rank

{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{A}(42,1920)}:y^{2}=x^{3}+\frac{2673}{4}x^{2}+80640x+3686400.

{\mathbb{Q}}(p,q,r,s,t,k)
with independent points

P_{12}
,
P_{13}
,

∎

T_{1}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}a_{i}a_{j},\ T_{2}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}b_{i}b_{j},\ T_{3}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}c_{i}c_{j},\ T_{4}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}d_{i}d_{j},

and
T=T_{1}+T_{2}-T_{3}-T_{4}
.

One can rewrite (
2.8
) as
\phi^{2}_{1}(x)-\phi^{2}_{2}(x)
where

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{3}+Mx^{2}+\frac{4(T_{1}T_{2}-T_{3}T_{4})+T^{2}}{4T}x+N,

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{3}+Mx^{2}+\frac{4(T_{1}T_{2}-T_{3}T_{4})-T^{2}}{4T}x+N.

It follows that

\phi^{2}_{i}(x)-f_{i}(x)=Kx^{4}+MKx^{3}+Hx^{2}+NKx,\ i=1,2,

where

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=-\frac{T^{2}+2(T_{3}+T_{4})T-4(T_{1}T_{2}-T_{3}T_{4})}{2T},

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(T^{2}+4(T_{1}T_{2}-T_{3}T_{4})\right)^{2}}{16T^{2}}-T_{1}T_{2}.

We now introduce the quartic elliptic curve

(2.9)

y^{2}=Kx^{4}+MKx^{3}+Hx^{2}+NKx,

which contains the rational points
P_{a_{i}}
,
P_{b_{i}}
,
P_{c_{i}}
,
P_{d_{i}}
with abscissas
-a_{i}

-b_{i}

The quartic curve (
2.9

by the rational transformation

{\mathcal{P}}_{b_{i}}

{\mathcal{P}}_{d_{i}}

-\frac{NK}{a_{i}},\ -\frac{NK}{b_{i}},\ -\frac{NK}{c_{i}},\ \text{and}\ -\frac{NK}{d_{i}}\quad\text{for}\ i=1,2,3.

We have thus constructed a family of elliptic curves, defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}(q,s)
, coming from the elements of
\mathcal{C}

3.
Heuristics

As all curves in the families
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{A}}
,
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{B}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}
have trivial torsion subgroups in general, to identify promising high-rank candidates we used heuristic arguments (motivated by the BSD conjecture) given by Mestre
[
27
]
and Nagao
[
30
]
. They suggest that for curves of high rank, certain sums should assume the largest values in the observed families. The first of these sums is

S_{1}(X)=\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{N_{p}+1-p}{N_{p}}\log p

where
X
is a prime bound for primes
p
, and
N_{p}=|E(\mathbb{F}_{p})|
is the number of points on elliptic curve
E
under the reduction modulo
p
. We followed the sieve phase of the general method for finding high-rank elliptic curves described in
[
21
, pp. 64–68]
.

The Mestre–Nagao sum
S_{1}(X)
with the prime bound
X=10^{6}
was calculated in Magma
[
3
]
for all curves with the absolute values of parameters
p
,

,

,

,

,

,

up to
40
. For curves with

function in Magma was used to uncover the independent generators of the Mordell–Weil group of the curve.

4.
A concluding remark

In this work, we considered the system of diophantine equations

\sum_{i=1}^{3}x_{1i}=\cdots=\sum_{i=1}^{3}x_{\ell i},\quad\prod_{i=1}^{3}x_{1i}=\cdots=\prod_{i=1}^{3}x_{\ell i}

for
\ell
ranging from
2
to
4
, which from the geometric
point of view is equivalent to
\ell
rectangular boxes with integer sides having the same perimeter and the same volume, and for each system we introduced a family of elliptic curves and then studied them closely. Here arises this natural question that whether there exists more than four rectangular boxes with integer sides that have the same perimeter and the same volume.

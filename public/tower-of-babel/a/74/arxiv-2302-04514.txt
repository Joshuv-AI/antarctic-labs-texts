1.
Introduction

In a Banach lattice
E
we can consider two kinds of closed subspaces: those generated by a disjoint sequence of non-zero vectors, and those that are at a positive distance of every normalized disjoint sequence. The later ones are called
dispersed subspaces
in
[
12
]
.
In the study of operators acting on
E
it is useful to consider their action on these kinds of subspaces (see
[
7
]
).
The
disjointly strictly singular
operators (
DSS
operators, for short) were introduced in
[
18
]
as those operators
T:E\to Y
from a Banach lattice
E
into a Banach space
Y
such that
T
is an isomorphism on no closed subspace of
E
generated by a disjoint sequence of non-zero vectors. These operators have been applied to the study of the structure of Banach lattices (see
[
6
]
and references therein). More recently, the disjointly non-singular operators (
DN-S
operators, for short) where introduced in
[
12
]
as those operators
T:E\to Y
from a Banach lattice
E
to a Banach space
Y
that are strictly singular in no closed subspace of
E
generated by a disjoint sequence of non-zero vectors.
The
DN-S
operators have also been studied in
[
1
]
and
[
13
]
.
Note that the kernel of a
DN-S
operator is a dispersed subspace.

By
[
10
, Theorem 2]
, an operator
T:L_{1}\to Y
is
DN-S
if and only it is tauberian in the sense of Kalton and Wilansky
[
20
]
. In this case the second conjugate
T^{**}:L_{1}^{**}\to Y^{**}
and the ultrapowers
T_{\mathcal{U}}:(L_{1})_{\mathcal{U}}\to Y_{\mathcal{U}}
are also
DN-S
, and the operator
T^{co}:L_{1}^{**}/L_{1}\to Y^{**}/Y
induced by
T^{**}
is an (into) isomorphism; see
[
10
,
11
]
.

In this paper we extend these results for
E=L_{1}
to the operators in
\textrm{DN-S}(E,Y)
when
E
is order continuous with a weak unit. Our main tool is the fact that in this case
E
admits a representation and a dense sublattice of some
L_{1}(\mu)
space with
\mu
a probability measure.
We characterize the operators in
\textrm{DN-S}(E,Y)
in terms of their action over the normalized sequences
(x_{n})
in
E
satisfying
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(\textrm{supp\,}x_{n})=0
. As a consequence,
T
is an isomorphism on the closed band
E_{A}
of
E
generated by a measurable set
A
when
\mu(A)
is small enough.
Moreover, using the push-out construction, we show that every operator
T\in\textrm{DN-S}(E,Y)
admits an extension
\overline{T}\in\textrm{DN-S}(L_{1}(\mu),\operatorname{PO})
, where
\operatorname{PO}
is the push-out Banach space. From this result, we derive that each
T\in\textrm{DN-S}(E,Y)
is a tauberian operator such that
T^{**}
is tauberian and
T^{co}
is an (into) isomorphism.
Also, using a local variation of the notion of
DN-S
operator, we prove that the class of
DN-S
operators is preserved by ultrapowers, we give an example showing that it is not preserved by ultraproducts, and we introduce and study the
n,r
-dispersed subpaces, a local variation of the notion of dispersed subspace. Moreover, when
E
contains no copies of
c_{0}
and admits a weak unit, we show that
T\in\textrm{DN-S}(E,Y)
implies
T^{**}\in\textrm{DN-S}
.

2.
Preliminaries

An operator
T\in\textrm{L}(E,Y)
is
disjointly strictly singular
, and we write
T\in\textrm{DSS}(E,Y)
, if there is no
(x_{n})\in\textrm{d}(E)
such that
T_{[x_{n}]}
is an isomorphism. The class
DSS
was introduced by Hernández and Rodríguez-Salinas in
[
18
]
and
[
17
]
.

An operator
T\in\textrm{L}(E,Y)
is
disjointly non-singular
, and we write
T\in\textrm{DN-S}(E,Y)
, if there is no
(x_{n})\in\textrm{d}(E)
such that
T_{[x_{n}]}
is strictly singular.
These operators were introduced in
[
12
]
, and studied in
[
1
]
and
[
13
]
. Note that
\textrm{DN-S}(L_{1},Y)
is the set of tauberian operators from
L_{1}
into
Y
(see
[
10
,
12
]
). We refer to
[
14
]
and
[
11
]
for information on tauberian operators.
A closed subspace
M
of
E
is
dispersed
if there is no
(x_{n})\in\textrm{dn}(E)
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\textrm{dist}(x_{n},M)=0
.

A sequence
(x_{n})
in
E
is
unbounded norm convergent (or un-convergent)
to
x\in E
if
(|x_{n}-x|\wedge u)
converges in norm to
0
for each
u\in E_{+}

[
23
]
. In this case we write
x_{n}\overset{un}{\longrightarrow}x
.

The disjointly non-singular operators can be characterized as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
12
, Theorems 2.8 and 2.10]

For an operator
T\in\textrm{L}(E,Y)
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

T
is disjointly non-singular.

(2)

For every
(x_{n})\in\textrm{d}(E)
, the restriction
T_{[x_{n}]}
is an upper semi-Fredholm operator.

(3)

For every
(x_{n})\in\textrm{dn}(E)
,
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|Tx_{n}\|>0
.

(4)

For every compact operator
S\in\textrm{L}(E,Y)
,
N(T+S)
is dispersed.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
1
, Theorem 5.3]

Suppose that
E
is order continuous. For
T\in\textrm{L}(E,Y)
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

T
is disjointly non-singular.

(2)

For no normalized
un
-null sequence
(x_{n})
we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|Tx_{n}\|=0
.

(3)

There exists
r>0
such that for every
(x_{n})\in\textrm{dn}(E)
,
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|Tx_{n}\|>r
.

A Banach lattice
E
is
order continuous
if every net in
E
decreasing in order to
0
converges in norm to
0
; and
a
weak unit
in
E
is a vector
e\in E_{+}
such that
\||x|\wedge e\|=0
implies

. We refer to
[
21
,
24
]
for information on order continuous Banach lattices.

Representation of order continuous Banach lattices

In
[
21
, Theorem 1.b.14]
it is shown that every order continuous Banach lattice
E
with a weak unit admits a representation as a Köthe function space, in the sense that there exists a probability space
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
so that

•

L_{\infty}(\mu)\subset E\subset L_{1}(\mu)
with
E
dense in
L_{1}(\mu)
and
L_{\infty}(\mu)
dense in
E
,

•

\|f\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{E}\leq 2\|f\|_{\infty}
when
f\in L_{\infty}(\mu)
,

•

the order in
E
coincides with the one induced by
L_{1}(\mu)
.

In the paper we will use this representation without further comments. For vectors in
L_{1}(\mu)
, we denote by
x_{n}\overset{\mu}{\longrightarrow}x
the
convergence in measure.

Among the order continuous Banach lattices with a weak unit we have some rearrangement invariant (r.i., for short) function spaces on
(0,1)
. Besides
L_{p}(0,1)
(
1\leq p<\infty
), the most commonly used r.i. function spaces on
(0,1)
are the Orlicz spaces and the Lorentz spaces (see
[
21
, Section 2a]
). Below we give a brief description of the second ones.

Example 2.3
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
W
be a positive non-increasing continuous function on
(0,1]
so that
\lim_{t\to 0}W(t)=\infty
,
W(1)=1
and
\int_{0}^{1}W(t)=1
. The
Lorentz function space
L_{W,p}(0,1)
is the space of all measurable functions
f
on
(0,1)
such that

\|f\|_{W,p}=\left(\int_{0}^{1}f^{*}(t)^{p}W(t)dt\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

where
f^{*}
is the decreasing rearrangement of
|f|
.

The space
L_{W,p}(0,1)
is a r.i. function space on
(0,1)
different from
L_{1}(0,1)
.

The following result will be useful.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
3
, Corollary 2.12, Theorem 4.6]

Let
E
be an order continuous Banach lattice with a weak unit
e
, and let
(x_{n})\subset E
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

x_{n}\overset{un}{\longrightarrow}0
.

(2)

(|x_{n}|\wedge e)
converges in norm to

.

(3)

x_{n}\overset{\mu}{\longrightarrow}0
.

For an order continuous Banach lattice
E
with a weak unit

, we define the

of

as
\textrm{supp\,}x=\{t\in\Omega:x(t)\neq 0\}
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Suppose that

is order continuous with a weak unit. Then each sequence
(x_{k})
in
E
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\left(\textrm{supp\,}x_{n}\right)=0

.

Note that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\left(\textrm{supp\,}x_{n}\right)=0

.

Ultraproducts of spaces and operators

Let
I
be a set admitting a non-trivial ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
and let
(X_{i})_{i\in I}
and
(Y_{i})_{i\in I}
be families of Banach spaces. The ultraproduct
(X_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}
of
(X_{i})_{i\in I}
is defined as the quotient of
\ell_{\infty}(I,X_{i})
by the closed subspace

N_{\mathcal{U}}(X_{i})=\{(x_{i})\in\ell_{\infty}(I,X_{i}):\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\|x_{i}\|=0\}.

The element of
(X_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}
which has
(x_{i})\in\ell_{\infty}(I,X_{i})
as a representative is denoted
[(x_{i})]
.

When
X_{i}=X
for each
i\in I
, we denote the ultraproduct by
X_{\mathcal{U}}
, and we call it an ultrapower of
X
.

If each
X_{i}
is a Banach lattice then
(X_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}
has a natural structure of Banach lattice:
[(x_{i})]\leq[(y_{i})]
if there exists
(z_{i})\in N_{\mathcal{U}}(X_{i})
such that
x_{i}+z_{i}\leq y_{i}
for each
i\in I
.

If
(T_{i})_{i\in I}
is a bounded family of operators with
T_{i}\in\textrm{L}(X_{i},Y_{i})
for each
i\in I
, the ultraproduct
(T_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}\in\textrm{L}((X_{i})_{\mathcal{U}},(Y_{i})_{\mathcal{U}})
is defined by
(T_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}[(x_{i})]=[(T_{i}x_{i})]
. When
T_{i}=T
for each
i\in I
, we write
T_{\mathcal{U}}
which is called an ultrapower of
T
. We refer to
[
4
, Chapter 8]
or
[
16
]
for additional information on ultraproducts of spaces and operators.

We begin with a complement to

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E
be an order continuous Banach lattice with a weak unit. For

(1)

T
is disjointly non-singular.

(2)

There exists

in

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\left(\textrm{supp\,}x_{n}\right)=0

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|Tx_{n}\|>r

(3)

For every
(x_{n})
in
S_{E}
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\left(\textrm{supp\,}x_{n}\right)=0
,
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|Tx_{n}\|>0

(4)

There is

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) Suppose that

, there is

such that for every disjoint sequence

First, we choose

Suppose that

Given an operator

for the pair

\begin{CD}E@>{T}>{}>Y\\
@V{\textrm{\char 17\relax}}V{}V@V{\overline{\textrm{\char 17\relax}}}V{}V\\
L_{1}(\mu)@>{\overline{T}}>{}>\operatorname{PO}\end{CD}

is the quotient

\overline{\textrm{\char 17\relax}}

are defined by
\overline{\textrm{\char 17\relax}}y=(y,0)+\overline{\Delta}

Note that

(1)

or there exists a subsequence

.

By Proposition

become dichotomies if and only if

is

.

We consider the injective operator

defined by

.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
E
is an order continuous Banach lattice with a weak unit, and let

(1)

If

, then
\Delta
is a closed subspace of

and
\overline{\textrm{\char 17\relax}}
is injective.

(2)

If

and
\Delta
is closed in

then

.

Proof.

(1) Suppose that

is not closed. Then there is a sequence
(x_{n})
in
S_{E}
such that
\|Dx_{n}\|=\|Tx_{n}\|+\|x_{n}\|_{1}\to 0
as

. By part (2) in Lemma

, there is a subsequence
(x_{n_{k}})
and a disjoint sequence

in
E
such that

. Hence
\|Tz_{k}\|\to 0
, and Theorem

implies that

.

Also,
\overline{\textrm{\char 17\relax}}y=0

(2) Suppose that

. Since

is non-closed.
∎

When both

and
\overline{\textrm{\char 17\relax}}
are injective, we can see

as an extension of

.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
E
is an order continuous Banach lattice with a weak unit, and let

.

(1)

\overline{T}\in\textrm{DN-S}(L_{1}(\mu),\operatorname{PO})
; equivalently,

\overline{T}
is tauberian.

(2)

T
is a tauberian operator.

(3)

T^{**}
is tauberian and
T^{co}
is an (into) isomorphism.

Proof.

(1) Let
(f_{n})
be a disjoint sequence in
S_{L_{1}(\mu)}
. Then
\overline{T}f_{n}=(0,f_{n})+\Delta
and

\|\overline{T}f_{n}\|=\inf_{x\in E}\|Tx\|+\|f_{n}-\textrm{\char 17\relax}x\|_{L_{1}}.

Since
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-\textrm{\char 17\relax}x\|_{L_{1}}\geq 1
for each

, we get
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\overline{T}f_{n}\|_{L_{1}}\geq 1

by Theorem

.

(2) Note that
\overline{T}f=0
iff

iff

for some

and

. Hence
N(\overline{T})=\textrm{\char 17\relax}\left(N(T)\right)
and
ȷ
is an isomorphism on

. Since

is tauberian,

is reflexive, hence so is

.

Now, if

and

is compact, then

[

is reflexive for each compact

[

.

(3) The argument we gave in the proof of (2) shows that each

is supertauberian in the sense of
[

, because each reflexive subspace of

is superreflexive and supertauberian operators admit a perturbative characterization:

is supertauberian if and only if

∎

Proposition 4.5

Suppose that

5.
Ultraproducts of operators

Here we prove the stability of the class of
DN-S
operators under ultrapowers when
E
is order continuous with a weak unit. The following local variation of the notion of
DN-S
operator will be useful.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
r>0
. An operator
T\in\textrm{L}(E,Y)
is in the class
\textrm{DN-S}_{n,r}
if for each normalized disjoint
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
in
E
we have
\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|Tx_{i}\|\geq r
.

Next result was proved in
[
19
]
for operators acting on a
L_{1}
space using Kakutani’s representation theorem. Our proof here uses the properties of un-convergence.

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that
E
is order continuous with a weak unit. An operator
T\in\textrm{L}(E,Y)
is in
DN-S
if and only if
T\in\textrm{DN-S}_{n,r}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
r>0
.

Proof.

If
T\in\textrm{DN-S}_{n,r}
, then for every
(x_{n})\in\textrm{dn}(E)
,
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|Tx_{n}\|\geq r
. Thus, by Theorem
2.1
,
T\in\textrm{DN-S}
.

Conversely, suppose that
T\in\textrm{DN-S}_{n,r}
for no pair
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
r>0
. Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can find a normalized disjoint
(x^{n}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
with
\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|Tx^{n}_{i}\|\leq 1/n
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we select
i(n)\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
so that
\mu\left(\textrm{supp\,}x^{n}_{i(n)}\right)\leq 1/n
, and we denote
y_{n}=x^{n}_{i(n)}
. By Corollary
2.5
,
y_{n}\overset{un}{\longrightarrow}0
. Since
\|Ty_{n}\|\to 0
, Theorem
2.2
implies that
T\notin\textrm{DN-S}
.
∎

Next we state a characterization of the class
\textrm{DN-S}_{n,r}
that was given in the proof of
[
19
, Lemma 2.2]
for
E=Y
a
L_{1}
space. Note that for
0\neq x\in E\subset L_{1}(\mu)
, we have
x/|x|\in L_{\infty}(\mu)
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that
E
is order continuous with a weak unit. Then
T\in\textrm{L}(E,Y)
is in
\textrm{DN-S}_{n,r}
if and only if for every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that if
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in S_{E}
and
\||x_{i}|\wedge|x_{j}|\|<\delta
for
1\leq i<j\leq n
then
\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|Tx_{i}\|>r-\varepsilon
.

Proof.

For the direct implication, if
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in S_{E}
satisfy
\||x_{i}|\wedge|x_{j}|\|<\delta
for
i\neq j
and we define

z_{i}=\big(|x_{i}|-(|x_{i}|\wedge(\vee_{j\neq i}|x_{j}|)\big)\frac{x_{i}}{|x_{i}|},

then the vectors
z_{i}
are disjoint and
1-n\delta\leq\|z_{i}\|\leq 1
. Applying the
\textrm{DN-S}_{n,r}
condition to
(z_{i}/\|z_{i}\|)_{i=1}^{n}
we get
\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|Tx_{i}\|>r-\varepsilon
if
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,n,\|T\|)
is small enough.

The converse implication is immediate.
∎

As a consequence,
\textrm{DN-S}_{n,r}
is stable under ultraproducts:

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose that
E_{i}
is order continuous with a weak unit for each
i\in I
. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a non-trivial ultrafilter on
I
. If
(T_{i})_{i\in I}
is a bounded family with
T_{i}\in\textrm{DN-S}_{n,r}(E_{i},Y_{i})
for each
i\in I
then
(T_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}\in\textrm{DN-S}_{n,r}
.

Proof.

Two vectors
[(x_{i})],[(z_{i})]
in
(E_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}
are disjoint if and only if
\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\||x_{i}|\wedge|y_{i}|\|=0
. In this case, for each
\delta>0
we can choose the representatives
(x_{i}),(y_{i})
so that
\||x_{i}|\wedge|y_{i}|\|<\delta
for every
i\in I
. Since
(T_{i})_{i\in I}
is bounded, for each
\varepsilon>0
we can choose
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,n)
in Proposition
5.3
which is valid for all
T_{i}
, and conclude that
(T_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}\in\textrm{DN-S}_{n,r}
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Suppose that
E
is order continuous with a weak unit, and let
\mathcal{U}
be a non-trivial ultrafilter. If
T\in\textrm{DN-S}(E,Y)
then the ultrapower
T_{\mathcal{U}}\in\textrm{DN-S}
.

As a consequence of the following observation, we shall show that the class of

Remark 5.6

It follows from

Example 5.7

Let

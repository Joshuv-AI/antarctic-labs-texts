The bounds on the rate of decay of

in Theorem

are simple to derive from (

) once we have established the finiteness of the derivatives at

of the right-hand side of (

). The latter yields finiteness of the derivatives of the integral to the left-hand side.

2.4
Background and previous results

We discuss the available results in the domain of the asymptotic behaviour of exponential functionals of Lévy processes on deterministic horizon.

When
\mathbb{E}\left[\xi_{1}\right]=0
, the Lévy process oscillates and
I_{\xi}\left(\infty\right)=\infty
. In this case, under the assumptions that
\mathbb{E}\left[\xi^{2}_{1}\right]<\infty
and the existence of some positive exponential moments for
\xi
, the authors of
[
21
]
show that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\sqrt{t}\mathbb{E}\left[I^{-a}_{\xi}(t)\right]=C\in\left(0,\infty\right)
. Furthermore, under the additional assumption for the existence of some negative exponential moments, they have obtained a result of the type
\lim_{t\to\infty}\sqrt{t}\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(I_{\xi}(t)\right)\right]=C_{F}
for a limited class of functions
F
. Similar results in the same setting are obtained in
[
16
]
. The requirements for the existence of exponential moments, for the finiteness of the variance, and for the limited form of
F
have been completely removed in
[
28
, Theorem 2.20]
. Therein even oscillating Lévy processes
\xi
with no first moment and with Spitzer’s condition are studied in full generality.

When
\xi
is symmetric with infinite activity, it is shown in
[
22
, Corollary 2.3.]
that
\mathbb{E}\left[I^{-1/2}_{\xi}(t)\right]=1/\sqrt{t}
. This is one of the few cases where a precise evaluation in a closed form of a fractional moment of exponential functional of Lévy processes on deterministic horizon is available. It fits the asymptotics mentioned in the previous paragraph.

The case when
\mathbb{E}\left[\xi_{1}\right]<0
and
\xi
drifts to minus infinity is considered in
[
21
]
.
Under an assumption for the existence of negative exponential moments of some order, they show that the quantities considered in the paragraph above have explicit rate of decay. The case
\mathbb{E}\left[\xi_{1}\right]<0
and
\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{1}>t\right)
being regularly varying at infinity of index
\alpha>1
, which is precisely our setting, is considered in
[
36
]
.
However, there the author assumes additionally that
\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{1}\in\left[t,t+\delta\right)\right)
is regularly varying at infinity for any
\delta>0
, that is
\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{1}>t\right)
is additionally
locally
regularly varying. Then upon further assuming that
F
is bounded, positive, non-increasing, locally-Lipschitz, and
\sup_{x>0}x^{a}F(x)<\infty
for some
a\in\left(0,1\right)
, see
[
36
, Conditions 4.1 and 4.2]
, then (
3.4
) holds true. We note that in our Corollary
3.1
we only assume
\sup_{x>0}x^{a}F(x)<\infty
for some
a\in\left(0,1\right)
and
F
being continuous. Since the result in
[
36
]
is proved in a probabilistic way, the constant has a probabilistic representation, see
[
36
, (4.3)]
. We offer a characterization of the limiting measure and the related constants in terms of special functions which in many cases are easier to evaluate. Moreover, the Mellin inversion representation of the limiting measure yields analytical properties of this measure that cannot be captured by probabilistic means and offers in turn even a neat new probabilistic representation of the normed limiting measure as the law of the product of two independent well-known in the literature and easier to study random variables. We do not discard lattice Lévy processes either which would be necessitated by
local
regular variation. We wish to highlight that
[
37
]
obtains similar results to the results in
[
36
]
for exponential functionals of random walks. The respective conditions in
[
37
]
match those in
[
36
]
.

In all the aforementioned works that deal with asymptotic behaviour of
\mathbb{E}\left[F(I_{\xi}(t))\right]
there are results that explicitly or implicitly yield some bounds on the rate of decay of
\mathbb{E}\left[F(I_{\xi}(t))\right]
. However, they require as a minimum the existence of exponential moments or fine structure of the tail
\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{1}>t\right)
as in
[
36
]
. Here, we offer such estimates on the rate of decay based on the finiteness of an explicit general integral criterion.

We are not aware of any results linking the derivatives of bivariate Bernstein-gamma functions and convolutions and derivatives of the
q-
potential measures of the underlying Lévy process. We expect that this connection will prove useful in other contexts.

2.5
Open problems

A close inspection of our proof, see Section
6
, shows that
\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{1}>t\right)
being regularly varying at infinity with index
\alpha>1
implies that
\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{t}>0\right)
is regularly varying with index
\alpha-1
, see (
6.5
), which in turn yields that the derivative of the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of the ascending ladder time is regularly varying at zero, that is
\phi^{(n)}_{+}(q,0)
is regularly varying of order
\left\{\alpha\right\}-1
, see (
6.8
). A natural question is whether we can start only with assumption of the behaviour of the latter derivative. In fact, the only point where we need the regular variation of
\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{1}>t\right)
is when we apply the de Haan theory via the probabilistic estimate, see the proof of Lemma
6.3
, which relies on the one large jump principle. Should the latter be avoided,
then one might prove results like ours only upon requirements on some derivative of
\phi_{+}(q,0)
. Due to the link to bivariate Bernstein-gamma functions, we believe this is a worthy line for future research which could further expand our knowledge not only on exponential functionals but on bivariate Bernstein-gamma functions themselves too.

The curious generalisation of the classical product factorisation of exponential functionals, see Theorem
3.4
, leads us to the question whether such a factorisation is more general in the limit as now it depends on the assumption of regular tails. However, classical factorisations are universal. Unfortunately, our approach does not offer a route to answer this.

Theorem 3.1

Proof of Theorem
4.6
.

We prove by induction that for
k=1,2,\dots,n+1

u_{q}^{*k}(x)\leq kCU^{*(k-1)}_{q}(\min\{0,x\},\infty).

(5.58)

The result in the base case
k=1
holds because

U_{q}(\mathrm{d}y)\leq U(\mathrm{d}y)
implies that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u_{q}(x)\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u(x)=:C
a.e. which leads to inequality everywhere when working with the canonical
u_{q}
, see
[
6
, Proposition I.11.(ii)]
.
Let (
5.58
) hold for some

. Then,

\begin{split}u^{*(l+1)}_{q}(x)&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u_{q}(y)u^{*l}_{q}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq C\int_{-\infty}^{x}u_{q}^{*l}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y+lC\int_{x}^{\infty}u_{q}(y)U^{*(l-1)}_{q}\left(\min\left\{0,x-y\right\},\infty\right)\mathrm{d}y\\
&=CU_{q}^{*l}\left(0,\infty\right)+lC\int_{x}^{\infty}U_{q}^{*(l-1)}\left(x-y,\infty\right)U_{q}\left(\mathrm{d}y\right)\\
&\leq CU_{q}^{*l}\left(0,\infty\right)+lC\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}U_{q}^{*(l-1)}\left(x-y,\infty\right)U_{q}\left(\mathrm{d}y\right)\\
&=CU_{q}^{*l}\left(0,\infty\right)+lCU_{q}^{*l}\left(x,\infty\right)\leq(l+1)CU^{*l}_{q}\left(\min\left\{0,x\right\},\infty\right),\end{split}

as needed,
which settles (
4.11
). Next, under the integral condition in the theorem’s statement, by taking a limit

in (
5.58

for any

and any

∎

\widetilde{\nu}_{a,t}(\mathrm{d}y):=\nu_{a,t}(\mathrm{d}y)/\nu_{a,t}(0,\infty)
. From
(

\lim_{t\to\infty}\widetilde{\nu}_{a,t}(\mathrm{d}y)\left((0,x]\right)=\frac{h_{a}^{x}(0)}{M_{\Psi}\left({0},{1-a}\right)},

hence we only need to verify that the last

probability measure, for which it
would be enough to check, substituting the definition of

, (

\lim_{x\to\infty}h_{a}^{x}(0)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathtt{Re}\left(z\right)=b}\frac{x^{-z}}{z}M_{\Psi}\left({0},{z+1-a}\right)\mathrm{d}z=M_{\Psi}\left({0},{1-a}\right).

(6.29)

We do this by the residue theorem: by
[
28
, (2.10)]
,
the function

is meromorphic on
\mathbb{C}_{(a-1,a)}
, with a single simple pole at
0. Its residue there is exactly
M_{\Psi}\left({0},{1-a}\right)
, so integrating by the
negatively oriented contour of the rectangle
\gamma_{R}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}|-b_{1}\leq\mathtt{Re}\left(z\right)\leq b_{1},-R\leq\mathtt{Im}z\leq R\}

with the choice
-b=b_{1}\in(0,\min\{a,1-a\})
and
R>0
,
gives us

-2\pi iM_{\Psi}\left({0},{1-a}\right)=\lim_{R\to\infty}\oint_{\gamma_{R}}\frac{x^{-z}}{z}M_{\Psi}\left({0},{z+1-a}\right)\mathrm{d}z.

(6.30)

Along the horizontal
strips of the contour,
i.e.
\mathtt{Im}z=\pm R
, we use the bound

|M_{\Psi}\left({q},{z}\right)|\leq M_{\Psi}\left({q},{\mathtt{Re}\left(z\right)}\right),

which holds when the quantities
are well-defined, and is based on the results

[

, Theorem 6.1 (3), Proposition 6.8]
,
which state,
respectively,
that

W_{\phi_{q,-}}(z)

and

\Gamma(z)/W_{\phi_{q,+}}(z)

are Mellin transforms of
positive random variables.
Indeed, if we call these
random variables

V_{\phi_{q,-}}
and

I_{\phi_{q,+}}
, we can
see that

\begin{split}\left|M_{\Psi}\left({q},{z}\right)\right|&=\left|\frac{\Gamma(z)}{W_{\phi_{q,+}}(z)}W_{\phi_{q,-}}(1-z)\right|=\left|\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\phi_{q,+}}^{z-1}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[V_{\phi_{q,-}}^{-z}\right]\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|I_{\phi_{q,+}}^{z-1}\right|\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\left|V_{\phi_{q,-}}^{-z}\right|\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\phi_{q,+}}^{\mathtt{Re}\left(z\right)-1}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[V_{\phi_{q,-}}^{-\mathtt{Re}\left(z\right)}\right]=M_{\Psi}\left({q},{\mathtt{Re}\left(z\right)}\right).\end{split}

(6.31)

This gives us

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{-b_{1}}^{b_{1}}\frac{x^{-y-iR}}{y+iR}M_{\Psi}\left({0},{y+1-a+iR}\right)-\frac{x^{-y+iR}}{y-iR}M_{\Psi}\left({0},{y+1-a-iR}\right)\mathrm{d}y\right|\\
&\hphantom{{}=\int_{-b_{1}}^{b_{1}}\frac{x^{y+iR}}{y+iR}M_{\Psi}\left({0},{y+1-a+iR}\right)+\frac{x^{y-iR}}{y-iR}}\leq\frac{2}{R}\int_{-b_{1}}^{b_{1}}x^{-y}M_{\Psi}\left({0},{y+1-a}\right)\mathrm{d}y=\mathrm{O}\left(\frac{1}{R}\right),\end{split}

because
on
[-b_{1},b_{1}]
,

y\mapsto x^{-y}
is obviously continuous, and
also
y\mapsto

M_{\Psi}\left({0},{y+1-a}\right)
is continuous
there by
[
28
, (2.10)]
.
Therefore, by dominated convergence along the horizontal strips of
\gamma_{R}
,
by (
6.30
),

\begin{split}-2\pi iM_{\Psi}\left({0},{1-a}\right)&=\lim_{R\to\infty}\oint_{\gamma_{R}}\frac{x^{-z}}{z}M_{\Psi}\left({0},{z+1-a}\right)\mathrm{d}z\\
&=\int_{\mathtt{Re}\left(z\right)=-b_{1}}\frac{x^{-z}}{z}M_{\Psi}\left({0},{z+1-a}\right)\mathrm{d}z-\int_{\mathtt{Re}\left(z\right)=b_{1}}\frac{x^{-z}}{z}M_{\Psi}\left({0},{z+1-a}\right)\mathrm{d}z.\end{split}

It remains to prove
that the integral along
\mathtt{Re}\left(z\right)=b_{1}
goes to zero as

.
Using once again the polynomial decay of
M_{\Psi}\left({0},{\cdot}\right)
along complex lines, see (
6.21
) and (

),
and the fact that

, we have that

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\mathtt{Re}\left(z\right)=b_{1}}\frac{x^{-z}}{z}M_{\Psi}\left({0},{z+1-a}\right)\mathrm{d}z\right|&=\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-b_{1}-iy}}{-b_{1}+iy}M_{\Psi}\left({0},{-b_{1}+1-a+iy}\right)\mathrm{d}y\right|\\
&\leq x^{-b_{1}}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\max\left\{b_{1},|y|\right\}}\left|M_{\Psi}\left({0},{-b_{1}+1-a+iy}\right)\right|\mathrm{d}y}<\infty,\end{split}

so by dominated convergence, the last tends to
0

when

as desired, i.e.
(
6.29
) holds. Therefore, by the Portmanteau
theorem, we have the weak convergence

\widetilde{\nu}_{a,t}(\mathrm{d}y)=\frac{\nu_{a,t}(\mathrm{d}y)}{\nu_{a,t}\left((0,\infty]\right)}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{w}\widetilde{\nu}_{a}(\mathrm{d}y)

with

a

we obtain the statement

(6.32)

with

In this appendix, we provide

A.1
Classical Karamata theory

Definition A.1

Let
\ell

[X,\infty)

of infinity, and satisfying for all

\lambda>0

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\ell(\lambda x)}{\ell(x)}=1,

\ell

at
0+
/
0-
. We note the class of these
functions as

.

Basic properties, such as
comparison with polynomials and
linear properties, can be found in

[
8
, Proposition 1.3.6]
.

Theorem A.2

(Uniform Convergence Theorem,

[
8
, Theorem 1.2.1]
)
.

If

, then for all

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\ell(\lambda x)}{\ell(x)}=1,\text{ uniformly on each compact set in $(0,\infty)$}.

(A.1)

As we will often use the fact that slowly varying functions grow slower than polynomials, we state the following easy

[

, Theorem 1.5.6]
.

Proposition A.3

If

, then for
any chosen constants

,

,
there exist

such that

\ell(x)\leq Ax^{\delta}\quad\text{and}\quad\ell(x)\geq Ax^{-\delta}.

(A.2)

Remark A.4

If

(Karamata’s Theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-sx}\mathrm{d}U(x)=:\widehat{U}(s)

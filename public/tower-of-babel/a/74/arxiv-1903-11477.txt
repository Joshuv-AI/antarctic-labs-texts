The Tanaka-Thomas’s Vafa-Witten invariants via surface Deligne-Mumford stacks
Yunfeng Jiang and Promit Kundu

Abstract
. We provide a definition of Vafa-Witten invariants for projective surface Deligne-Mumford stacks, generalizing the construction of Tanaka-Thomas on the Vafa-Witten invariants for projective surfaces inspired by the S-duality conjecture. We give calculations for a root stack over a general type quintic surface, and quintic surfaces with ADE singularities. The relationship between the Vafa-Witten invariants of quintic surfaces with ADE singularities and the Vafa-Witten invariants of their crepant resolutions is also discussed.

1.1.
Background

The motivation from physics is the S-duality conjecture, where by physical duality theory Vafa and Witten
[
60
]
predict that the generating function of the Euler characteristic of the moduli space of stable coherent sheaves on projective surfaces should be modular form. In history the S-duality is a rich conjecture. For instance, the reduction of the S-duality conjecture from projective surfaces to product of curves is given by Kapustin-Witten
[
35
]
, which is related to the Langland duality in number theory.
In the mathematics side, the moduli space of solutions of the Vafa-Witten equation on a projective surface
S
has a partial compactification by Gieseker semistable Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
on
S
, where
E
is a coherent sheaf with rank
\mathop{\rm rk}>0
, and
\phi\in\mbox{Hom}_{S}(E,E\otimes K_{S})
is a section called a Higgs field.

The formula in
[
60
]
(for instance Formula (5.38) of
[
60
]
) and some mathematical calculations as in
[
16
]
imply that the invariants in
[
60
]
may have other contributions except purely from the projective surfaces. In
[
54
]
,
[
55
]
Tanaka and Thomas have developed the Vafa-Witten theory using the moduli space
\mathcal{N}
of Gieseker semi-stable Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
on
S
with topological data
(\mathop{\rm rk}=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits,c_{1}(E),c_{2}(E))
. By spectral theory, the moduli space
\mathcal{N}
of Gieseker semi-stable Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
on
S
is isomorphic to the moduli space of Gieseker semi-stable torsion sheaves

{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}
on the total space
X:=\mbox{Tot}(K_{S})
. In the case that the semistability and stability coincide, the moduli space
\mathcal{N}
admits a symmetric obstruction theory in
[
4
]
. Therefore there exists a dimension zero virtual fundamental cycle

[\mathcal{N}]^{\vir}\in H_{0}(\mathcal{N})
. The moduli space
\mathcal{N}
is not compact, but it admits a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action induced by the

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
X
by scaling the fibers of
X\to S
. The
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-fixed locus
\mathcal{N}^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
is compact, then from
[
12
]
,
\mathcal{N}^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
inherits a perfect obstruction theory from
\mathcal{N}
and Tanaka-Thomas
[
54
]
define the virtual localized invariant

(1.1.1)

\widetilde{\VW}(S):=\widetilde{\VW}_{\mathop{\rm rk},c_{1},c_{2}}(S)=\int_{[\mathcal{N}^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}]^{\vir}}\frac{1}{e(N^{\vir})}

as the
U(\mathop{\rm rk})
Vafa-Witten invariants, where
N^{\vir}
is the virtual normal bundle.

By the property of the obstruction sheaf this invariant
\widetilde{\VW}(S)
is zero unless

H^{0,1}(S)=H^{0,2}(S)=0
. As mentioned in
[
54
]
, most of the surfaces satisfying the condition admit a vanishing theorem such that the invariants are just the signed Euler characteristic of the moduli space of stable sheaves on
S
.
The right invariants are defined by the moduli space
\mathcal{N}_{L}^{\perp}
of Higgs pairs with fixed determinant
L\in\Pic(S)
and trace-free
\phi
.
Tanaka-Thomas have carefully studied the deformation and obstruction theory of the Higgs pairs instead of using the ones for the two dimensional sheaves, and constructed a
symmetric obstruction theory
on
\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L}
.
The space
\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L}
still admits a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action, therefore inherits a perfect obstruction theory on the fixed locus.
Then the Vafa-Witten invariants are defined as:

(1.1.2)

\VW(S):=\VW_{\mathop{\rm rk},c_{1},c_{2}}(S)=\int_{[(\mathcal{N}_{L}^{\perp})^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}]^{\vir}}\frac{1}{e(N^{\vir})}.

This corresponds to the
SU(\mathop{\rm rk})
gauge group in Gauge theory. They did explicit calculations for some surfaces of general type in
[
54
, §8]
and verified some parts of Formula (5.38) in
[
60
]
. Since for such general type surfaces, the

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-fixed loci contain components such that the Higgs fields are non-zero, there are really contributions from the threefolds to the Vafa-Witten invariants. This is the first time that the threefold contributions are made for the Vafa-Witten invariants.
Some calculations and the refined version of the Vafa-Witten invariants have been studied in
[
57
]
,
[
42
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
38
]
.

1.2.
Invariants using the Behrend function

There is another way to do the localization on the moduli space
\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L}
. In
[
4

\nu_{\mathcal{N}}:\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L}\to{\mathbb{Z}}

(1.2.1)

\vw(S):=\vw_{\mathop{\rm rk},c_{1},c_{2}}(S)=\chi(\mathcal{N}_{L}^{\perp},\nu_{\mathcal{N}})

where
\chi(\mathcal{N}_{L}^{\perp},\nu_{\mathcal{N}})
is the weighted Euler characteristic by the Behrend function
\nu_{\mathcal{N}}

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L}

by taking the dual of the associated vector field

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action. The degenerate locus is the fixed locus

, therefore there exists a cosection localized virtual cycle

[\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L}]^{\vir}_{\loc}\in H_{0}((\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L})^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}})
, and

\int_{[\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L}]^{\vir}_{\loc}}1=\chi(\mathcal{N}_{L}^{\perp},\nu_{\mathcal{N}})

which is proved in
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
.
Tanaka-Thomas prove that in the case
\deg K_{S}<0
and the case that

is a K3 surface,

\VW(S)=\vw(S)
. They also prove their corresponding generalized Vafa-Witten invariants in
[
30
]
agree, see
[
55
]
for the Fano case and
[
42
]
for the K3 surface case.

1.3.
Motivation for surface DM stacks

The S-duality conjecture is interesting for surfaces with orbifold singularities. In
[
60
]
, Vafa-Witten discussed the ALE spaces, which are hyperkahler four manifolds and crepant resolutions of the orbifold
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}/G
with
G\subset SU(2)
a finite subgroup in
SU(2)
corresponding to ADE Dynkin diagrams. It is interesting to directly study the Vafa-Witten invariants for such two dimensional DM stacks.

On the other hand, the crepant resolution conjecture in both Gromov-Witten theory
[
50
]
,
[
6
]
and Donaldson-Thomas theory
[
9
]
has been attracting a lot of interests in the past ten years. Given a local orbifold
{\mathcal{X}}=[{\mathbb{C}}^{3}/G]
for
G\subset SU(2)
, Bryan-Young formulated the crepant resolution conjecture for the generating function of the Donaldson-Thomas invariants for
{\mathcal{X}}
and their crepant resolutions which are
G
-Hilbert schemes. The conjecture can be explained as wall crossing formula of the counting invariants in the derived category of coherent sheaves corresponding to different Bridgeland stability conditions, see
[
11
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
59
]
,
[
26
]
.
It is interesting to study the Vafa-Witten invariants and the generalized Vafa-Witten invariants for orbifolds via wall crossing techniques of Joyce-Song and Bridgeland. In such a situation, the invariants
\vw
seem to be more suitable to be put into the wall crossing formula, and we leave it as a future work.

Finally our study of the Vafa-Witten invariants for surface DM stacks is motivated by the S-duality conjecture and Langlands duality in
[
35
]
. In Tanaka-Thomas
[
54
]
,
[
55
]
, the gauge group is

SU(\mathop{\rm rk})
. Since the Langlands dual group of
SU(\mathop{\rm rk})
is
SU(\mathop{\rm rk})/{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathop{\rm rk}}
, one hopes that the study of the Vafa-Witten invariants for orbifold surfaces will be related to the
SU(\mathop{\rm rk})/{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathop{\rm rk}}
-Vafa-Witten invariants. In particular, one has
SU(2)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\cong SO(3)
, so the
SU(2)
-Vafa-Witten invariants for a global quotient surface DM stack

[S/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}]
is related to the
SO(3)
-Vafa-Witten invariants for
S
. This should be related to the
SO(3)
-Donaldson invariants for the surface
S
, see
[

,
[

1.4.
The moduli space of Higgs pairs on surface DM stacks

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a smooth two dimensional DM stack with projective coarse moduli space
S
, which we call it “a surface DM stack”. The moduli space of stable coherent sheaves with a fixed Hilbert polynomial
H\in{\mathbb{Q}}[m]
has been constructed by F. Nironi
[
45
]
. In
[
45
]
, in order to define suitable Hilbert polynomials the author chooses a generating sheaf
\Xi
for
{\mathcal{S}}
and defines the
modified
Hilbert polynomial associated with this generating sheaf. Let
p:{\mathcal{S}}\to S
be the morphism to its coarse moduli space. A locally free sheaf
\Xi
on
{\mathcal{S}}
is
p
-very ample if for every geometric point of
{\mathcal{S}}
the representation of the stabilizer group at that point contains every irreducible representation. We define

\Xi
as a
generating sheaf
of
{\mathcal{S}}
.
The readers may understand that the generating sheaf is introduced to deal with some stacky issues and in order not to loose stacky information like finite group gerbes over schemes.

Let us fix a polarization
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)
on the coarse moduli space
S
.
Choose a generating sheaf
\Xi
, and for a coherent sheaf
E
on
{\mathcal{S}}
, the modified Hilbert polynomial is defined by:

H_{\Xi}(E,m)=\chi({\mathcal{S}},E\otimes\Xi^{\vee}\otimes p^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(m)).

Then we can write down

H_{\Xi}(E,m)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{\Xi,i}\frac{m^{i}}{i!},

where
d=\dim(E)
is the dimension of the sheaf
E
.
The reduced Hilbert polynomial for pure sheaves, which
we will denote by
h_{\Xi}(E)
; is the monic polynomial
\frac{H_{\Xi}(E)}{\alpha_{\Xi,d}}
with rational coefficients.
Then let
E
be a pure coherent sheaf, it is semistable if for every proper subsheaf

F\subset E
we have
h_{\Xi}(F)\leq h_{\Xi}(E)
and it is stable if the same is true with a strict inequality.
Fixing a modified Hilbert polynomial
H
, the moduli stack of semistable coherent sheaves
on
{\mathcal{S}}
is constructed in
[
45
]
. If the stability and semistability coincide, the coarse moduli space

\mathscr{M}:=\mathscr{M}^{\Xi}_{H}({\mathcal{S}})
is a projective scheme.

Our goal is to study the moduli stack of Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
on
{\mathcal{S}}
, where
E
is a torsion free coherent sheaf with
rank
\mathop{\rm rk}>0
and
\phi\in\mbox{Hom}(E,E\otimes K_{\mathcal{S}})
is a Higgs field. The Hilbert polynomial
h_{\Xi}(E)
can be similarly defined by choosing a generating sheaf
\Xi
for
{\mathcal{S}}
. Then
(E,\phi)
is semistable if for every proper
\phi
-invariant subsheaf

F\subset E
we have
h_{\Xi}(F)\leq h_{\Xi}(E)
. The moduli stack of stable Higgs pairs on
{\mathcal{S}}
with modified Hilbert polynomial
H
has a coarse moduli space

\mathcal{N}:=\mathcal{N}_{H}
which is a quasi-projective scheme.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}:=\mbox{Tot}(K_{{\mathcal{S}}})
be the total space of the canonical line bundle of
{\mathcal{S}}
, then
{\mathcal{X}}
is a smooth Calabi-Yau threefold DM stack.
By spectral theory, the category of Higgs pairs on
{\mathcal{S}}
is equivalent to the category of torsion sheaves
{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}
on
{\mathcal{X}}
supporting on
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}
.
Let
\pi:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{S}}
be the projection, then the bullback
\pi^{*}\Xi
is a generating sheaf for
{\mathcal{X}}
. One can take a projectivization
\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits(K_{{\mathcal{S}}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{S}}})
, and consider the moduli space of stable torsion sheaves
on
\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
with modified Hilbert polynomial
H
. The part that is supported on
{\mathcal{X}}
is isomorphic to the moduli space of stable Higgs pairs
\mathcal{N}
on
{\mathcal{S}}
with modified Hilbert polynomial
H
.

1.5.
Perfect obstruction theory and the Vafa-Witten invariants

To construct the perfect obstruction theory, we also take the moduli space
\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L}
of stable Higgs pairs

(E,\phi)
with fixed determinant
L
and trace-free
\phi
. For a Higgs pair
(E,\phi)
on
{\mathcal{S}}
, and the associated torsion sheaf

{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}
on
{\mathcal{X}}
, the deformation and obstruction of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}
are controlled by

\mbox{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathcal{X}}}({\mathcal{E}}_{\phi},{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}),\quad\mbox{Ext}^{2}_{{\mathcal{X}}}({\mathcal{E}}_{\phi},{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi})

respectively; and also we have an exact triangle

R\mathscr{H}om({\mathcal{E}}_{\phi},{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi})\to R\mathscr{H}om_{{\mathcal{S}}}(E,E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ\phi-\phi\circ}}{{\longrightarrow}}R\mathscr{H}om_{{\mathcal{S}}}(E\otimes K_{{\mathcal{S}}}^{-1},E)

relating the deformation and obstruction theory of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\phi}
to the Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
. We found that all the arguments as in

[
54
, §5]
work for smooth DM stacks
{\mathcal{S}}
. We write down some parts in the appendix for
{\mathcal{S}}
, and for the more precise details we refer to
[
54
, §5]
.
Therefore
\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L}
admits a symmetric obstruction theory and the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
acts on
\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L}
with compact fixed loci, we define

(1.5.1)

\VW^{L}_{H}({\mathcal{S}})=\int_{[(\mathcal{N}_{L}^{\perp})^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}]^{\vir}}\frac{1}{e(N^{\vir})}

as the
SU(\mathop{\rm rk})
-Vafa-Witten invariants.
We have the Behrend function in this case
and define

(1.5.2)

\vw^{L}_{H}({\mathcal{S}})=\chi(\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L},\nu_{\mathcal{N}^{\perp}_{L}})

as the weighted Euler characteristic.

1.6.
Calculations

For surface DM stacks, the essential part is to calculate the Vafa-Witten invariants for some examples to see if one can get different phenomenon comparing with the case of smooth projective surfaces. First the moduli space
\mathcal{N}_{L}^{\perp}
admits a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action induced by the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

-fixed loci on

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

-fixed Higgs pairs

such that the Higgs fields

We include a short calculation for

In this case the vanishing theorem as in Proposition

1.7.
Related and future research

As we have already pointed out, our study for the Vafa-Witten invariants for surface DM stacks is definitely motivated by the beautiful work of Tanaka-Thomas
[
54
]
,
[
55
]
. Our original motivation is to expect that surface DM stacks can give different phenomenon for the invariants.
As mentioned earlier, it is interesting to define generalized invariants
\vw
as in
[
55
]
and study the wall-crossing formula of Joyce-Song, and we leave this as a future research.
Special examples of surface DM stacks are cyclic gerbes over smooth surfaces, and in
[
28
]
Jiang has developed a theory of twisted Vafa-Witten invariants using gerbe twisted sheaves and proved the S-duality conjecture of Vafa-Witten for K3 surfaces.

Let
S
be a projective surface with isolated ADE type singularities. It is interesting to calculate the Vafa-Witten invariants using the techniques developed in
[
14
]
, and compare with the Vafa-Witten invariants with its crepant resolution
\widetilde{S}
.
In particular, if
S
is a smooth projective K3 surface, and let
[S/\mu_{N}]
be the quotient stack, where
\mu_{N}
is a cyclic group of order
N
acting as symplectic automorphisms on
S
. From
[
20
]
, the action only has finite isolated rational double points singularities and its crepant resolution is a smooth K3 surface. It is interesting to study the Vafa-Witten invariants of the quotient stack
[S/\mu_{N}]
and compare with the Vafa-Witten invariants for
S
calculated in
[
55
]

2.1.
Surface DM stacks, examples

The basic knowledge of stacks can be found in the book
[
39
]
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be a smooth DM stack. Roughly speaking the stack
{\mathcal{X}}
is a fibre category by groupoids. An interesting DM stack is the global quotient stack
[M/G]
, where
M
is a smooth scheme and
G
is a finite group acting on
M
. The quotient stack
[M/G]
classifies principal
G
-bundles over the category of schemes, together with a
G
-equivariant morphism to
M
. Not every stack is a global quotient, but most interesting DM stacks are locally global quotient, i.e., for any point
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
, there exists an open étale neighborhood
x\in{\mathcal{U}}
such that
{\mathcal{U}}\cong[M/G]
is a global quotient. We mainly work on locally quotient DM stacks.

Let
f:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
be a morphism between two DM stacks.
f
is called “representable” if every morphism
g:S\to{\mathcal{Y}}
from a scheme
S
, the fibre product
S\times_{g,{\mathcal{Y}},f}{\mathcal{Y}}
is a scheme. In particular, any morphism from a scheme to
{\mathcal{Y}}
is representable.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be a smooth DM stack. The inertia stack
I{\mathcal{X}}
associated to
{\mathcal{X}}
is defined to be the fibre product:

I{\mathcal{X}}:={\mathcal{X}}\times_{\Delta,{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{X}}}{\mathcal{X}}

where
\Delta:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{X}}
is the diagonal morphism.

The objects in the category underlying
I{\mathcal{X}}
is:

\Ob(I{\mathcal{X}})=\{(x,g)|x\in\Ob({\mathcal{X}}),g\in\Aut_{{\mathcal{X}}}(x)\}.

Remark 2.2
.

(1)

There exists a morphism
q:I{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
given by
(x,g)\mapsto x
;

(2)

There exists a decomposition

I{\mathcal{X}}=\bigsqcup_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mbox{Hom}(\mbox{Rep}(B\mu_{r},{\mathcal{X}})),

hence
I{\mathcal{X}}
can be decomposed into connected components, and

I{\mathcal{X}}=\bigsqcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}{\mathcal{X}}_{i}

Here
\mathcal{I}
is the index set. We denote the component
\{(x,\id)|x\in\Ob({\mathcal{X}}),\id\in\Aut_{{\mathcal{X}}}({\mathcal{X}})\}
by
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}={\mathcal{X}}
.

For instance let
{\mathcal{X}}=[M/G]
be the global quotient DM stack. Then
\mathcal{I}=\{(g)|g\in G\}
, where
(g)
is the conjugacy class. The inertia stack
I{\mathcal{X}}=\bigsqcup_{(g)}{\mathcal{X}}_{(g)}
, and
{\mathcal{X}}_{(g)}=[M^{g}/C(g)]
, where
M^{g}
is the
g
fixed locus of
M
and
C(g)
is the centralizer of
g
.

2.1.1.
Orbifold K3 surfaces

An example of projective smooth K3 surface

isolated orbifold points. After taking resolution

(-2)
-curves on the smooth K3 surface

.
Of course the finite group

2.1.2.
Automorphism of K3 surfaces

Let
S
be a smooth projective K3 surface with a holomorphic symplectic form

\sigma\in H^{0}(S,\Omega_{S}^{2})
. An automorphism

of

is called a

if
\sigma
preserves the symplectic form of

. Our reference is Daniel Huybrechts’s book
[
20

. The classification of finite order symplectic automorphisms of

gives abelian group actions on

. Let

be such a finite abelian group, then the quotient stack
{\mathcal{S}}:=[S/G]
is an orbifold K3 surface, and from
[
20
]

{\mathcal{S}}
, which are rational double points.
A list of the number of fixed points and the corresponding finite abelian groups can be found in

[
20
, §15.1]
.
For instance, in the case of cyclic group
\mu_{2}
of order two, the orbifold K3 surface
[S/\mu_{2}]
is called the

Nikulin involution
. There are eight isolated
A_{1}
-singular points.

The global Torelli theorem tells us that the symplectic automorphism
g
is uniquely determined by its induced action on
H^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
. By
[
20
]
the action of
g
on the abstract lattice

depends up to an orthogonal transformation of the lattice only on the order

.
Let

1&0\end{array}\right)
denote by the hyperbolic lattice. Recall that

The invariant lattice with respect to

is

\Lambda^{g}=\{v\in\Lambda|g\cdot v=v\}.

The coinvariant lattice of
g
is the orthogonal complement of the invariant lattice:

\Lambda_{g}=(\Lambda^{g})^{\perp}\subset H^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}}).

In the case of Nikulin involution
{\mathcal{S}}:=[S/\mu_{2}]
. The action of the generator

on

is trivial on

and interchanges the two copies of

where

represents for the diagonal and the anti-diagonal in

respectively.
These invariants of lattices play an important role for the study of Donaldson-Thomas invariants of

in
[

is an elliptic curve with trivial

action, and the authors call it a CHL Calabi-Yau threefold.
We hope that the invariant lattice and co-invariant lattice play a role in the calculation of Vafa-Witten invariants.

2.2.1.
Generating sheaf and the modified Hilbert polynomial

Let
S
be a smooth projective scheme. Recall that to define the Hilbert polynomial
H
on
S
we need a polarization
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)
. In the case of a smooth DM stack
{\mathcal{S}}
, one can choose the polarization
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)
on its coarse moduli space
p:{\mathcal{S}}\to S
. However, there are no very ample invertible sheaves on a stack. We need to loose the condition to choose some locally free sheaves on
{\mathcal{S}}
so that they behave like very ample sheaves. This is the notion of
generating sheaves
in
[
46
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

A locally free sheaf
\Xi
on
{\mathcal{S}}
is
p
-very ample if for every geometric point of
{\mathcal{S}}
the representation of the stabilizer group at that point contains every irreducible representation of the stabilizer group.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\Xi
be a locally free sheaf on
{\mathcal{S}}
. We define a functor

F_{\Xi}:D\mbox{Coh}_{{\mathcal{S}}}\to D\mbox{Coh}_{S}

by

F\mapsto p_{*}\mathscr{H}om_{{\mathcal{O}}_{S}}(\Xi,F)

and a functor

G_{\Xi}:D\mbox{Coh}_{S}\to D\mbox{Coh}_{{\mathcal{S}}}

by

F\mapsto p^{*}F\otimes\Xi.

Remark 2.7
.

As in
[
45
]
,
[
46
]
, the functor
F_{\Xi}
is exact since the dual
\Xi^{\vee}
is locally free and the pushforward
p_{*}
is exact. The functor
G_{\Xi}
is not exact unless
p
is flat. For instance, if
p
is a flat gerbe or a root stack, it is flat.

Definition 2.8
.

A locally free sheaf
\Xi
is said to be a generator for the quasi-coherent sheaf
F
if the left adjunction morphism of the identity
p_{*}F\otimes\Xi^{\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\id}}{{\longrightarrow}}p_{*}F\otimes\Xi^{\vee}

\theta_{\Xi}(F):p^{*}p_{*}\mathscr{H}om_{{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}}(\Xi,F)\otimes\Xi^{\vee}\to F

is surjective. The sheaf
\Xi
is a generating sheaf of
{\mathcal{S}}
if it is a generator for every quasi-coherent sheaf on
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proposition 2.9
.

(
[
46
, §5.2]
)
A locally sheaf
\Xi
on a DM stack
{\mathcal{S}}
is a generating sheaf if and only if it is
p
-very ample.

So later we will use the
p
-very ampleness to define generating sheaves.

2.2.2.
Gieseker stability and the moduli space by F. Nironi

Let us fix again
p:{\mathcal{S}}\to S
a smooth DM stack and the map to its coarse moduli space
S
. Let

{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)
be the very ample invertible sheaf on
S
, and
\Xi
a generating sheaf on
{\mathcal{S}}
. We call the pair
(\Xi,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))
a polarization of
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
F
be a coherent sheaf on
{\mathcal{S}}
, we define the support of
F
to be the closed substack associated with the ideal

0\to{\mathcal{I}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}\to\mathscr{E}nd_{{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}}(F).

So
\dim(\Supp F)
is the dimension of the substack associated with the ideal
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}
since
{\mathcal{S}}
is a DM stack.

Definition 2.11
.

A pure sheaf of dimension
d
is a coherent sheaf
F
such that for every non-zero subsheaf
F^{\prime}\subset F
the support of
F^{\prime}
is of pure dimension
d
.

So for any coherent sheaf
F
, we have the torsion filtration:

0\subset T_{0}(F)\subset\cdots\subset T_{d}(F)=F

where every
T_{i}(F)/T_{i-1}(F)
is pure of dimension
i
or zero, see
[
21
, §1.1.4]
.

Let us define the Gieseker stability condition:

Definition 2.12
.

The modified Hilbert polynomial of a coherent sheaf
F
on
{\mathcal{S}}
is defined as:

H_{\Xi}(F,m)=\chi({\mathcal{S}},F\otimes\Xi^{\vee}\otimes p^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}(m))=H(F_{\Xi}(F)(m))=\chi(S,F_{\Xi}(F)(m)).

Remark 2.13
.

(1)

Let
F
be of dimension
d
, then we can write:

H_{\Xi}(F,m)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{\Xi,i}(F)\frac{m^{i}}{i!}

which is induced by the case of schemes.

(2)

Also the modified Hilbert polynomial is additive on short exact sequences since the functor
F_{\Xi}
is exact.

(3)

If we don’t choose the generating sheaf
\Xi
, the Hilbert polynomial
H
on
{\mathcal{S}}
will be the same as the Hilbert polynomial on the coarse moduli space
S
. In order to get interesting information on the DM stack
{\mathcal{S}}
, the sheaf
\Xi
is necessary. For example, in
[
45
, §7]
,
[
26
]
the modified Hilbert polynomial on a root stack
{\mathcal{S}}
will corresponds to the parabolic Hilbert polynomial on the pair
(S,D)
with

a smooth divisor.

Definition 2.14
.

The reduced modified Hilbert polynomial for the pure sheaf
F
is defined as

h_{\Xi}(F)=\frac{H_{\Xi}(F)}{\alpha_{\Xi,d}(F)}.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
F
be a pure coherent sheaf. We call
F
semistable if for every proper subsheaf
F^{\prime}\subset F
,

h_{\Xi}(F^{\prime})\leq h_{\Xi}(F).

We call
F
stable if
\leq
is replaced by
<
in the above inequality.

Definition 2.16
.

We define the slope of
F
by

\mu_{\Xi}(F)=\frac{\alpha_{\Xi,d-1}(F)}{\alpha_{\Xi,d}(F)}.

Then
F
is
semistable if for every proper subsheaf
F^{\prime}\subset F
,

\mu_{\Xi}(F^{\prime})\leq\mu_{\Xi}(F).

We call
F
stable if
\leq
is replaced by
<
in the above inequality.

Remark 2.17
.

(1)

The notion of
\mu
-stability and semistability is related to the Gieseker stability and semistability in the same way as schemes, i.e.,

\mu-\text{stable}\Rightarrow\text{Gieseker stable}\Rightarrow\text{Gieseker semistable}\Rightarrow\mu-\text{semistable}

(2)

The stability really depends on the generating sheaf
\Xi
. This stability is not necessarily the same as the ordinary Gieseker stability even when
{\mathcal{S}}
is a scheme.

(3)

One can define the rank

\mathop{\rm rk}F_{\Xi}(F)=\frac{\alpha_{\Xi,d}(F)}{\alpha_{d}({\mathcal{O}}_{S})}.

Let us fix a polarization
(\Xi,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))
on
{\mathcal{S}}
, and a modified Hilbert polynomial

{\mathcal{M}}:={\mathcal{M}}^{H}_{\Xi}({\mathcal{S}}):(\Sch_{\kappa})\to(\mbox{Groupoids})

\text{on $S_{T}/T$ with modified Hilbert polynomial $H$.}\end{array}\right\}\right/\cong.

In

We briefly review it here.
Fix an integer

{\mathcal{S}}

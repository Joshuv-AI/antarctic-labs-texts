Comments on finite termination of the generalized Newton method for absolute value equations
Chun-Hua Guo

Abstract
. We consider the generalized Newton method (GNM) for the absolute value equation (AVE) A ​ x − | x | = b Ax-|x|=b . The method has finite termination property whenever it is convergent, no matter whether the AVE has a unique solution. We prove that GNM is convergent whenever ρ ⁡ ( | A − 1 | ) < 1 / 3 \rho(|A^{-1}|)<1/3 . We also present new results for the case where A − I A-I is a nonsingular M M -matrix or an irreducible singular M M -matrix. When A − I A-I is an irreducible singular M M -matrix, the AVE may have infinitely many solutions. In this case, we show that GNM always terminates with a uniquely identifiable solution, as long as the initial guess has at least one nonpositive component.

3.
Convergence and finite termination

We start with an improved statement about finite termination of GNM, as compared to
[
11
, Proposition 4]
.

Proposition 7
.

Suppose that
x^{1},\ldots,x^{i},x^{i+1}
are defined by (
3
) for a given
x^{0}
. If

(D(x^{i+1}))_{j}=(D(x^{i}))_{j}
for each
j
with

(D(x^{i+1}))_{j}\neq 0
, then
x^{i+1}
is a solution of the AVE (
1
).

Proof.

The proof is the same as that of
[
11
, Proposition 4]
. That is,

0=(A-D(x^{i}))x^{i+1}-b=Ax^{i+1}-D(x^{i+1})x^{i+1}-b=Ax^{i+1}-|x^{i+1}|-b.

Note that
D(x^{i})x^{i+1}=D(x^{i+1})x^{i+1}
holds without the stronger assumption
D(x^{i+1})=D(x^{i})
in
[
11
, Proposition 4]
.
∎

Example 1
.

Consider the AVE (
1
) with

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}3&-1\\
-1&3\end{array}\right],\quad b=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
-4\end{array}\right].

With

is a solution of (
1
) by Proposition
7

[
11
, Proposition 4]
not yet applies.

We will see later in this paper that Proposition
7
can be used to improve a recent result in
[
15
]
.
If the sequence
\{x^{i}\}
is defined by (
3
) and converges to some vector

, then

is clearly a solution of (

). If

has no zero components, then

for all

sufficiently large. In other words, GNM has the finite termination property.
If

has some zero components, then Proposition

by itself does not guarantee
that
x^{i}=x^{*}
for all
i
sufficiently large. In fact, it does not rule out the situation where

The next result shows that the above situation would never happen. It is a more general statement than
[

, Lemma 2]
, where

is regular.

Proposition 8
.

Suppose that
x^{1},\ldots,x^{i},x^{i+1}
are defined by (

) for a given

and

is a solution of (

If
(D(x^{i}))_{j}=(D(x^{*}))_{j}

Proof.

For later use, we present a simple proof as follows.

\displaystyle x^{i+1}-x^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A-D(x^{i}))^{-1}b-x^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A-D(x^{i}))^{-1}(b-(A-D(x^{i}))x^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A-D(x^{i}))^{-1}(D(x^{i})x^{*}-|x^{*}|).

If
(D(x^{i}))_{j}=(D(x^{*}))_{j}
for each
j
with

(D(x^{*}))_{j}\neq 0
, then
D(x^{i})x^{*}-|x^{*}|=0
and thus

x^{i+1}=x^{*}
.
∎

Although the sign pattern of

is usually not known beforehand, Proposition
8
guarantees finite termination of GNM whenever it is convergent, as
already mentioned in
[
19
]
for the case where

is regular.

Corollary 9

If the sequence
\{x^{i}\}
is defined by (

is a solution of (

Sometimes, it is more convenient to show convergence and then know the finite termination. Sometimes, it is more convenient to show finite termination and then know the convergence.

Theorem 10

Remark 1

This result is a special case of

The same conclusion is reached in

We now prove a new convergence result.

Theorem 11
.

Suppose that
\rho(|A^{-1}|)<1/3
. Then for any
b
and any starting point
x^{0}

3
) generates a sequence

Proof.

For each

. Therefore, by Lemma

\rho(A^{-1}D(x^{i}))\leq\rho(|A^{-1}D(x^{i})|)\leq\rho(|A^{-1}|)<1/3
, and

(I-A^{-1}D(x^{i}))^{-1}

\left|(I-A^{-1}D(x^{i}))^{-1}\right|=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(A^{-1}D(x^{i}))^{k}\right|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|A^{-1}|^{k}=(I-|A^{-1}|)^{-1}.

Thus
A-D(x^{i})=A(I-A^{-1}D(x^{i}))
is nonsingular. Since

whenever

is defined, we have

|(A-D(x^{i}))^{-1}|\leq|(I-A^{-1}D(x^{i}))^{-1}||A^{-1}|\leq(I-|A^{-1}|)^{-1}|A^{-1}|.

Let
B=(I-|A^{-1}|)^{-1}|A^{-1}|\geq 0
.

We continue with the expression for

in the proof of Proposition

\displaystyle x^{i+1}-x^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A-D(x^{i}))^{-1}(D(x^{i})x^{*}-|x^{*}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A-D(x^{i}))^{-1}(D(x^{i})(x^{*}-x^{i}+x^{i})-|x^{*}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A-D(x^{i}))^{-1}(D(x^{i})(x^{*}-x^{i})+|x^{i}|-|x^{*}|).

Taking absolute value on both sides, we have for each

\displaystyle|x^{i+1}-x^{*}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(A-D(x^{i}))^{-1}|(||x^{i}|-|x^{*}||+|D(x^{i})||x^{i}-x^{*}|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(A-D(x^{i}))^{-1}|(2|x^{i}-x^{*}|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2B|x^{i}-x^{*}|.

Then for each

|x^{i}-x^{*}|\leq(2B)^{i}|x^{0}-x^{*}|.

So
\{x^{i}\}
converges to

as long as
\rho(2B)=2\rho(B)<1
.
However, by Lemma

\rho(B)=(1-\rho(|A^{-1}|))^{-1}\rho(|A^{-1}|)<\frac{\frac{1}{3}}{1-\frac{1}{3}}=\frac{1}{2}.

So
\rho(2B)<1
, as required.
∎

So

Two issues have been raised in

The first issue is how to verify assumption (A2). This issue is resolved by the following result.

Proposition 12

Proof.

Suppose

Now suppose assumption (A2) holds. By letting

) is well defined and converges to the unique solution with one or more zero components
in at most
n+1
iterations.

Proof.

Let
x
be any solution of (
1
). Then
b=(A-D(x))x=(A-I+I-D(x))x
and

(I-D(x))x=b-(A-I)x
. Thus
v^{T}(I-D(x))x=0
, which means that
x
cannot have any negative components.

For any
x^{0}
with
D(x^{0})\neq I
,
x^{1}
is defined since
A-D(x^{0})=A-I+I-D(x^{0})
is a nosingular
M
-matrix. We have

b=(A-D(x^{0}))x^{1}=(A-I+I-D(x_{0}))x^{1}
and then
v^{T}(I-D(x^{0}))x^{1}=0
. So
x^{1}
has at least one nonpositive component.
Thus
D(x^{1})\neq I
and iteration (
3
) continues.
The sequence
\{x^{i}\}
is then well defined. Moreover,
D(x^{i+1})\geq D(x^{i})
for all
i\geq 1
exactly as for the case
v^{T}b<0
in
[
15
]
.
It follows that
x^{i}=\hat{x}
is a solution of (
1
) with at least one zero component, for some
i\leq n+1
, by Proposition
7
and the explanation in the proof of Theorem
13
.

We now show that (
1
) has exactly one solution that has one or more zero components.
Let
\bar{x}
be any such solution. Applying GNM (
3
) with
x^{0}=\bar{x}
gives
x^{n+1}=\bar{x}
.
We now change the first component of
b
from
b_{1}
to
b_{1}-\epsilon
(with
\epsilon>0
), and obtain a new vector
b(\epsilon)
.
Since
v^{T}b(\epsilon)<0
, the equation
Ax-|x|=b(\epsilon)
has a unique solution
x(\epsilon)
.
Applying GNM (
3
) with
x^{0}=\bar{x}
to the equation
Ax-|x|=b(\epsilon)

gives
x^{n+1}(\epsilon)=x(\epsilon)
(see Theorem
13
). Since
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}x^{n+1}(\epsilon)=x^{n+1}=\bar{x}
,
we have
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}x(\epsilon)=\bar{x}
. Therefore,
\bar{x}
is uniquely determined.
∎

5.
Conclusion

In this paper, we have explained that GNM has finite termination property whenever it has convergence, regardless whether or not the AVE (
1
) has a unique solution.
We have proved a new convergence result for GNM.
We have also obtained some new results for the case where
A-I
is a nonsingular
M
-matrix or an irreducible singular
M
-matrix.
In particular, when
A-I
is an irreducible singular
M
-matrix and the AVE has more than one solutions,
we have shown that the AVE then has infinitely many solutions, all of them are nonnegative. Moreover, exactly one of them has some zero components, and this uniquely identified solution can be found by GNM in at most
n+1
iterations, starting with any
x^{0}
with at least one nonpositive component.

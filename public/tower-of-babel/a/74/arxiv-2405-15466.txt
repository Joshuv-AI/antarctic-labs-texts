Skew-group A ∞ A_{\infty} -categories as Fukaya categories of orbifolds
Claire Amiot, Pierre-Guy Plamondon

Abstract
. We study the partially wrapped Fukaya category of a surface with boundary with an action of a group of order two. We let the notion of a skew-group A ∞ A_{\infty} -category play the role of the partially wrapped Fukaya category of an orbifold surface. We classify indecomposable objects in terms of graded curves with signs, or taggings, at orbifold points. We compute morphisms between a class of objects, and we use this to describe tilting objects and find algebras derived-equivalent to skew-gentle algebras.



1.
Introduction

Our main interest in this paper is the partially wrapped (or topological) Fukaya category of a surface with boundary
[
HKK17
]
with an action of a group of order two. We use the notion of a skew-group
A_{\infty}
-category
[
OZ22
]
, which is an enhancement of the notion of a skew-group algebra as studied in
[
RR85
]
, as a way to construct the partially wrapped Fukaya category of an orbifold surface. We use this to describe algebras derived-equivalent to skew-gentle algebras.

The partially wrapped Fukaya category of a graded surface with marked boundary is an
A_{\infty}
-category defined in
[
HKK17
]
, where it was seen that dissections of the surface gave rise to formal generators. The endomorphism algebras of these generators, in cohomology, are isomorphic to graded gentle algebras, and any such algebra arises from a dissection
[
OPS18
,
BCSo21
,
PPP19
]
. This point of view has been fruitful in providing new results on derived equivalences of gentle algebras
[
LP20
,
APS23
,
Opp
,
CJS
,
JSW
]
. Related geometric models have also been used to study gentle algebras
[
DRS12
,
cPS19
,
LZ21
,
BS21
,
BL22
,
CS23
]
, Brauer graph algebras
[
OZ22
]
and skew-gentle algebras
[
AB22
,
LFSV22
,
HZZ23
,
Ami23
]
.

Skew-gentle algebras were introduced by Geiss and de la Peña in
[
GdlPn99
]
and are a special case of clannish algebras
[
CB89
]
; however, when working over a field of characteristic different from
2
, skew-gentle algebras are conveniently described (up to Morita-equivalence) as skew-group algebras of gentle algebras with an action of a group of order two on their Gabriel quiver. The group action lifts to an action on the graded surface with dissection giving rise to the gentle algebra, and thus to the partially wrapped Fukaya category.

For an
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{A}
with a strict action of a finite group
G
, we define the skew-group
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{A}*G
as in
[
OZ22
]
, see also
[
RR85
]
for usual categories (Definition
2.11
).

The philosophy put forward in this paper is that, if
\mathcal{A}
comes from a
G
-invariant arc system on the graded surface
\mathcal{S}
, then the skew-group
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{A}*G
, or rather the split-closure of the category
\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal{A}*G)
of twisted complexes, plays the role of the partially wrapped Fukaya category of the graded orbifold surface
\mathcal{S}/G
.

This necessitates an understanding of the split-closure
\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal{A}*G)_{+}
(Section
2.6
). In the case where
\mathcal{A}
is a finite-dimensional algebra
\Lambda
, we obtain in this way an equivalence of triangulated categories between
\big((\operatorname{per}\Lambda)*G\big)_{+}
and
\operatorname{per}(\Lambda*G)
(Corollary
2.25
).

We apply this to the partially wrapped Fukaya category of a graded surface
\mathcal{S}
with marked boundary endowed with an action of a group
G
of order two. In this case,
\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal{A}*G)_{+}
is equivalent (in cohomology) to the perfect derived category of a graded skew-gentle algebra. The natural functor

\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A}\to\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal{A}*G)_{+}

sends indecomposable objects either to indecomposable objects or to direct sums of two distinct indecomposable objects; this allows us to give a description of all indecomposable objects in this category in terms of curves on
\mathcal{S}
together with
taggings
(Section
4
). In Section
5
, we turn this into a description in terms of
tagged graded arcs
on the orbifold surface
\mathcal{S}/G
. By the above functor, objects corresponding to arcs are sent to objects corresponding to tagged arcs, but interestingly, objects corresponding to a closed curve with paramater are sent either to closed curves or to tagged arcs joining two orbifold points, depending on the parameter. This was already noted in
[
Ami23
]
, and is similar to a phenomenon observed in
[
AP21
]
in the context of cluster categories from triangulated surfaces.

One interesting and useful facet of the construction of the Fukaya category developped in
[
HKK17
]
is the fact that given two full systems of graded arcs on the surface
\mathcal{S}
, there is a canonical Morita equivalence linking the corresponding Fukaya categories. This permits in particular when studying an object to choose a suitable full system of arcs in which the description of this object as twisted complexes becomes simple. One central point studied in Section
4
is to understand how the tagging behaves when changing the system. This careful checking is of importance when computing the morphisms spaces between tagged arcs interescting in an orbifold point.

This allows us to describe tilting objects in the derived category of a graded skew-gentle algebra in terms of dissections of the orbifold surface
\mathcal{S}/G
(Thoerem
5.10
). This gives a class of algebras (Definition
5.15
) which are derived-equivalent to skew-gentle algebras. In the case of a disk with one orbifold point, this yields in Section
6
a complete list of the algebras piecewise hereditary of Dynkin type
\mathbf{D}_{n}
(see also
[
Kel91
]
).

As stated above, the philosophy of this paper is that the skew-group
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{A}*G
, or rather the category
\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal{A}*G)_{+}
, plays the role of the partially wrapped Fukaya category of the graded orbifold surface
\mathcal{S}/G
. Recently, another approach has appeared in
[
CK
]
, in which the authors define an
A_{\infty}
-structure directly on a tagged arc system on
\mathcal{S}/G
, and relate it to the
G
-invariant portion of
\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A}
. Our approach differs from theirs in that we define the skew-group
A_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}*G
, which should be seen as an
A_{\infty}
-enhancement of the algebra
\Lambda*G
, with
\Lambda
a graded gentle algebra. It should be noted that
\Lambda*G
is not
basic
, and so is not given by a quiver with relations; however, it has the advantage of being canonical – no choice of idempotent is involved in its description. It would be of great interest to compare more carefully the two points of view.

Related work of Barmeier, Schroll and Wang on Fukaya categories of orbifold surfaces can be found in
[
BSW24
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the main defnition of
A_{\infty}
structures and their split closures, and introduce skew-group
A_{\infty}
-categories. In Section
3
, we recall the main results on topological Fukaya categories of graded surfaces, and in particular the description of their indecomposable objects in terms of graded curves. In Section
4
, we describe some indecomposable objects in
\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal{A}*G)_{+}
and the morphisms between them. This allows us to obtain tilting objects in Section
5
in terms of dissection of the orbifold
\mathcal{S}/G
. Finally, in Section
6
, we apply this to provide a complete list of piecewise hereditary algebras of Dynkin type
\mathbf{D}
.

2.1.
A_{\infty}

The aim of this subsection is mainly to fix the notations we will be using throughout the paper. Our main references are

We fix an algebraically closed field

Definition 2.1
.

An
A_{\infty}
-category

\mathcal{A}
is given by the following data.

•

A set of objects
\operatorname{Obj}(\mathcal{A})
.

•

For each pair of objects
X
and
Y
of
\mathcal{A}
, a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space of morphisms
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}^{m}(X,Y)
. The degree of a homogeneous morphism
f
is denoted by
|f|
and its
reduced degree
is
\|f\|:=|f|-1
.

•

For each integer
n\geq 1
and each
(n+1)
-tuple of objects
X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
, a morphism of degree
2-n

\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X_{n-1},X_{n})\otimes\cdots\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X_{0},X_{1})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X_{0},X_{n})

such that the
Stasheff relations
are satisfied for all
n
-tuples of homogeneous morphisms
(a_{n},\ldots,a_{1})
:

\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k\geq 0,j\geq 1}}{{k+j\leq n}}}(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|a_{i}\|}\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{n-j+1}\big(a_{n},\ldots,a_{k+j+1},\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{j}(a_{k+j},\ldots,a_{k+1}),a_{k},\ldots,a_{1}\big).

The
\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}
are called the
higher multiplications
of
\mathcal{A}
.

An
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{A}
is
strictly unital
if for every object
X
of
\mathcal{A}
, there is a morphism
1_{X}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}^{0}(X,X)
such that

•

\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(1_{X})=0
;

•

\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(a,1_{X})=f
and
\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(1_{Y},a)=(-1)^{|a|}f
for all homogeneous morphism
a
in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)
;

•

\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}(\ldots,1_{X},\ldots)=0
if
n\geq 3
.

We will often write
\mu^{n}
instead of
\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}
. We will also sometimes denote
\mu^{n}
simply by
\mu
, since the
n
in
\mu^{n}
is implied when writing
\mu(a_{n},\ldots,a_{1})
.

2.2.
Twisted complexes

Our main interest in
A_{\infty}
-categories lies in their use as enhancement of
K
-linear or triangulated categories.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a strictly unital
A_{\infty}
-category. Its
cohomological category

H^{*}\mathcal{A}
is the (graded) category whose objects are those of
\mathcal{A}
and whose morphism graded spaces are defined as

\operatorname{Hom}_{H^{*}\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)=H^{*}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y),

where
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)
is viewed as a complex with differential
\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}
, and where composition of homogeneous morphisms is defined by

[a_{2}][a_{1}]:=(-1)^{|a_{1}|}\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(a_{2},a_{1}).

We denote by
H^{0}\mathcal{A}
the subcategory obtained by keeping only morphisms of degree
0
.

The main way triangulated categories will arise in this paper is by taking twisted complexes over an
A_{\infty}
category. We define them in several steps.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an
A_{\infty}
-category. The
A_{\infty}
-category
\operatorname{add}\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{A}
is defined as follows.

•

Its objects are formal direct sums of the form
\bigoplus_{X\in\operatorname{Obj}(\mathcal{A})}V_{X}\otimes X
, where
V_{X}
is a finite-dimensional graded vector space which is zero for all but finitely many
X
.

•

Morphism spaces are defined by

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{add}\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{A}}(V_{X}\otimes X,V_{Y}\otimes Y):=\operatorname{Hom}(V_{X},V_{Y})\otimes_{K}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y),

extended to direct sums by additivity.

•

The higher multiplications are defined on homogeneous morphisms by

\mu_{\operatorname{add}\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{A}}^{n}(\phi_{n}\otimes a_{n},\ldots,\phi_{1}\otimes a_{1})=(-1)^{\sum_{i<j}|\phi_{i}|\|a_{j}\|}\phi_{n}\cdots\phi_{1}\otimes\mu_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}(a_{n},\ldots,a_{1}).

Notation 2.4
.

(1)

If
\bigoplus_{X\in\operatorname{Obj}(\mathcal{A})}V_{X}\otimes X
and
\bigoplus_{X\in\operatorname{Obj}(\mathcal{A})}W_{X}\otimes X
are two objects of

\left(\bigoplus_{X\in\operatorname{Obj}(\mathcal{A})}V_{X}\otimes X\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{X\in\operatorname{Obj}(\mathcal{A})}W_{X}\otimes X\right):=\bigoplus_{X\in\operatorname{Obj}(\mathcal{A})}(V_{X}\oplus W_{X})\otimes X.

(2)

When writing an object of
\operatorname{add}\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{A}
, we usually omit the summands for which

is the zero space.

(3)

If we view the field

as a graded vector space over itself, then for any integer

, the identity map from

to itself induces a morphism of degree

from
K

(4)

If

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an
A_{\infty}

over

is a pair

\displaystyle\mu_{\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A}}^{n}(a_{n},\ldots,a_{1})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\hskip-71.13188pt\sum\mu_{\operatorname{add}\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{A}}(\delta_{n},\ldots,\delta_{n},a_{n},\delta_{n-1},\ldots,\delta_{n-1},a_{n-1},\delta_{n-2},\ldots,\delta_{n-2},a_{n-2}\ldots,\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{1},a_{1},\delta_{0},\ldots,\delta_{0}),

where the sum is taken over all possible ways of inserting a finite number of copies of the

2.3.

The only

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be two

-categories. A

-functor

F:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is given by

•

a map
F:\operatorname{Obj}\mathcal{A}\to\operatorname{Obj}\mathcal{B}
;

•

for each pair of objects
X,Y
of
\mathcal{A}
, a morphism of
K

and all morphisms

in

If
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are strictly unital, then
F
is

if for each object
X
of

, we have that

.

For a strict

-functor

A strict

-functor

.

A strictly unital strict

-functor

is

•

if the induced functor

•

A

Given a strict

If

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{A}

For any morphism

Proposition 2.14
.

The following is a commutative diagram of strict
A_{\infty}
-functors which are all Morita equivalences.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}*G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A})*G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal{A}*G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Tw}\big((\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A})*G\big)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{\mathcal{A}}*G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{\mathcal{A}*G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Tw}\big(I_{\mathcal{A}}*G\big)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{(\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A})*G}}

In particular, the bottom arrow is a quasi-equivalence.

Proof.

Commutativity of the diagram follows from the fact that for any strict
A_{\infty}
-functor
\Phi:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
, the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{\mathcal{B}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Tw}(\Phi)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{\mathcal{C}}}

is commutative.
Moreover,
I_{\mathcal{B}}
is a Morita equivalence for any
\mathcal{B}
, so the two vertical arrows are Morita equivalences.

Let us prove that the bottom arrow is a Morita equivalence. Since the two bottom
A_{\infty}
-categories are triangulated, the bottom functor is a Morita equivalence if and only if it is a quasi-equivalence. Moreover,

H^{0}\operatorname{Tw}\big(I_{\mathcal{A}}*G\big):H^{0}\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal{A}*G)\to H^{0}\operatorname{Tw}\big((\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A})*G\big)

is a triangulated functor; we need to prove that it is an equivalence. It is sufficient to prove that its image contains a generator of the target category. Such a generator is given by (the
H^{0}
of) the image of
I_{(\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A})*G}
.

An object of
(\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A})*G
is a direct sum of objects of the form
\{(X,\delta)\}
, with
(X,\delta)
in
\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A}
. The image of this object by
I_{(\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A})*G}
is
\big(\{(X,\delta)\},0\big)
.

Now, the image of the object
(\{X\},\delta\otimes 1)
by the bottom functor is the object
\big(\{(X,0)\},\delta\otimes 1\big)
, where
\delta
in the right hand side is viewed as an endomorphism of
(X,0)
. If we prove that there is an isomorphism in cohomology between
(\{(X,0)\},\delta\otimes 1)
and
\big(\{(X,\delta)\},0\big)
, then we are done proving that the bottom arrow is a quasi-equivalence. The morphism
1_{X}\otimes 1_{G}
provides this isomorphism.

Lastly, the top arrow is a Morita equivalence because the three other arrows are.
∎

Remark 2.15
.

In
proposition

, it is unclear whether the right arrow should be a quasi-equivalence. In the next section, we will see that it will become one if we pass to split-closures.

4.1.
G
-invariant graded marked surfaces

In the rest of the paper, the group
G
will be assumed to be

, written multiplicatively with generator

. Moreover, we assume that
\mathcal{S}
has no completely marked boundary components (since we are mainly interested in finite-dimensional algebra).

Definition 4.1
.

A
G
-graded marked surface

(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{M},\eta,g)
is a graded marked surface
(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{M},\eta)
together with an orientation-preserving automorphism
g
of
\mathcal{S}
of order
2
such that

and
g^{*}(\eta)=\eta
. We assume that

has only finitely many fixed points.

The group
G=\langle g\rangle
acts on the set of graded arcs and curves. More precisely, if
(\gamma,\mathfrak{f})
is a graded arc on
(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{M},\eta,g)
, then so is
(g_{*}(\gamma),g^{*}(\mathfrak{f}))
. A
G
-invariant graded arc
is an arc
(\gamma,\mathfrak{f})
such that
(\gamma^{-1},\mathfrak{f}^{-1})=(g_{*}(\gamma),g^{*}(\mathfrak{f}))
. Note that there are no arcs satisfying
g_{*}(\gamma)=\gamma
, otherwise
g
would act as the identity.

A
full
G
-invariant arc system

\mathbb{S}
is a full arc system
\mathbb{S}
which is globally invariant by the action of
G
. It consists of pairs of graded arcs
\{(\gamma,\mathfrak{f}),(g_{*}(\gamma),g^{*}(\mathfrak{f})\}
and of
G
-invariant graded arcs.
The action of
G
on
\mathbb{S}
induces a strict action of
G
on the
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{S}}
. Our aim is now to understand the indecomposable objects in the category
H^{0}(\operatorname{Tw}((\mathcal{A}*G)_{+}))
. By Theorem
2.24
they are in natural bijection with the indecomposable objects in the category
H^{0}((\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A})*G)_{+}
which is the split-closure of the category
H^{0}((\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A})*G)
.

Therefore, from now on, in order to make the notations less heavy, we will write
H^{0}(\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A}*G)_{+}
instead of
H^{0}((\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A})*G)_{+}
or
H^{0}(\operatorname{Tw}((\mathcal{A}*G)_{+}))
when discussing indecomposable objects.

The results of Section
2
can be summarized as follows for
X
an object in
\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A}
.

•

If
gX
is not isomorphic to
X
in
H^{0}(\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A})
, then the object
\{X\}:=(\{X\},1_{X}\otimes 1_{G})
is indecomposable in
H^{0}(\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A}*G)_{+}
.

•

If
gX
is isomorphic to
X
in
H^{0}(\operatorname{Tw}\mathcal{A})
, and if there is a morphism

then

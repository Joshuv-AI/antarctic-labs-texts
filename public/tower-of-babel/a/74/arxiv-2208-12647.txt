Deformations, cohomologies and abelian extensions of compatible 3 3 -Lie algebras
Shuai Hou, Yunhe Sheng, Yanqiu Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper, first we give the notion of a compatible 3 3 -Lie algebra and construct a bidifferential graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are compatible 3 3 -Lie algebras. We also obtain the bidifferential graded Lie algebra that governs deformations of a compatible 3 3 -Lie algebra. Then we introduce a cohomology theory of a compatible 3 3 -Lie algebra with coefficients in itself and show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between equivalent classes of infinitesimal deformations of a compatible 3 3 -Lie algebra and the second cohomology group. We further study 2-order 1-parameter deformations of a compatible 3 3 -Lie algebra and introduce the notion of a Nijenhuis operator on a compatible 3 3 -Lie algebra, which could give rise to a trivial deformation. At last, we introduce a cohomology theory of a compatible 3 3 -Lie algebra with coefficients in arbitrary representation and classify abelian extensions of a compatible 3 3 -Lie algebra using the second cohomology group.

1.
Introduction

Compatible algebraic structures refer to two algebraic structures of the same kind in a linear category such that any linear combination of multiplications corresponding to these two algebraic structures still defines the same kind of algebraic structure. Compatible algebraic structures have been widely studied in mathematics and mathematical physics. Golubchik and Sokolov studied compatible Lie algebras with the background of integrable equations
[
11
]
, classical Yang-Baxter equations
[
12
]
, loop algebras over
Lie algebras
[
13
]
. Compatible Lie algebras are also related to elliptic theta functions
[
22
]
. See
[
23
,
25
,
27
]
for more details about classification, operads and bialgebra theory of compatible Lie algebras. Recently, the theory of bidifferential graded Lie algebras was developed in
[
17
]
, by which the deformation
theory and the cohomology theory of compatible Lie algebras were established. Then compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras
and compatible associative algebras were studied in
[
4
,
5
]
using similar ideas.

The notion of
3
-Lie algebras and more generally,
n
-Lie algebras was introduced in
[
10
]
, and have been
widely studied from different aspects. The
n
-Lie algebra is the algebraic structure corresponding to Nambu mechanics
[
21
]
.

3
-Lie algebras play important roles in the study of the Bagger-Lambert-Gustavsson theory of multiple
M2-branes
[
3
,
20
]
. Cohomology and
deformation theories of
3
-Lie algebras have been deeply studied in
[
1
,
6
,
9
,
24
,
26
,
28
]
. See the review article
[
8
,
19
]
for more details.

The purpose of the paper is to study compatible
3
-Lie algebras. We construct a bidifferential graded Lie algebra such that
compatible
3
-Lie algebras can be characterized by its Maurer-Cartan elements. We introduce a cohomology
theory of a compatible
3
-Lie algebra with coefficients in itself with the help of the aforementioned bidifferential graded Lie algebra.
As applications, we study infinitesimal deformations and
2
-order
1
-parameter deformations of a compatible
3
-Lie algebra respectively. We show that infinitesimal deformations of a compatible
3
-Lie algebra are classified by the second cohomology group. We introduce the notion of a Nijenhuis operator on a compatible 3-Lie algebra and show that a Nijenhuis operator gives rise to a trivial
2
-order
1
-parameter deformation of the compatible
3
-Lie algebra. Moreover, we introduce the cohomology of a compatible
3
-Lie algebra with coefficients in arbitrary
representation, and classify abelian
extensions of a compatible
3
-Lie algebra by the second cohomology group.

As aforementioned, compatible Lie algebras and 3-Lie algebras both have various applications in mathematical physics, so compatible 3-Lie algebras also have potential applications in mathematical physics, which will be studied in the future. Note that the theory of compatible 3-Lie algebras is not totally parallel to that of compatible Lie algebras, e.g. a Nijenhuis operator on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
gives rise to a deformed Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{N})
such that
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],[\cdot,\cdot]_{N})
is naturally a compatible Lie algebra, while this is not true for 3-Lie algebras. A Nijenhuis operator on a 3-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
also gives rise to a deformed 3-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{N})
. But
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{N})
is in general not a compatible 3-Lie algebra unless extra conditions are satisfied (see Proposition
3.9
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

3
-Lie algebra, and construct a bidifferential graded Lie algebra
whose Maurer-Cartan elements are compatible 3-Lie algebra structures. We further construct the bidifferential graded Lie algebra

In Section

In Section

3
-Lie algebra.

In this paper, all the vector spaces are over algebraically closed field

3.
Cohomologies, infinitesimal deformations and Nijenhuis operators

In this section, we introduce a cohomology theory of a compatible 3-Lie algebra with coefficients in itself, and show that infinitesimal deformations can be characterized by the second cohomology group. We also introduce the notion of a Nijenhuis operator on a compatible 3-Lie algebra, which gives rise to a trivial 2-order 1-parameter deformation.

3.1.
Cohomologies of compatible
3
-Lie algebras

First, we recall the cohomology theory of
3
-Lie algebras.
Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a
3
-Lie algebra with
\pi(x,y,z)=[x,y,z]
. Denote by

{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}\wedge\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~(n\geq 1),

which is the space of
n
-cochains.
Define the coboundary operator
\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi}:{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
by

\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi}\omega=(-1)^{n-1}[\pi,\omega]_{\mathsf{3Lie}},\quad\forall~\omega\in{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

More precisely, for all
~\mathfrak{X}_{i}=x_{i}\wedge y_{i}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},~i=1,2,\cdots,n
and
x_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi}\omega(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(-1)^{j}\omega\Big(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathfrak{X}_{j}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{k-1},\pi(x_{j},y_{j},x_{k})\wedge y_{k}+x_{k}\wedge\pi(x_{j},y_{j},y_{k}),\mathfrak{X}_{k+1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}\omega\Big(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathfrak{X}_{j}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},\pi(x_{j},y_{j},x_{n+1})\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\pi\Big(x_{j},y_{j},\omega(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathfrak{X}_{j}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})\Big)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}\Big(\pi(y_{n},x_{n+1},\omega(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n-1},x_{n}))+\pi(x_{n+1},x_{n},\omega(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n-1},y_{n}))\Big).

Because of the graded Jacobi
identity, we can deduce that
\mathrm{d}^{n+1}_{\pi}\circ\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi}=0.
Thus,
(\oplus_{n=1}^{+\infty}{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g}),{\mathrm{d}}_{\pi}^{*})
is a cochain complex, whose cohomology is defined to be the cohomology of 3-Lie algebras. See
[
6
,
8
]
for more details.

Next we introduce a cohomology

3
-Lie algebras.
Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a compatible
3
-Lie algebra with
\pi_{1}(x,y,z)=[x,y,z]
and
\pi_{2}(x,y,z)=\{x,y,z\}

is a Maurer-Cartan element of the bidifferential graded Lie algebra

Define the space of
n
-cochains
{\mathfrak{C}}_{{\rm c}\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),(n\geq 1)
by

{\mathfrak{C}}_{{\rm c}\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\underbrace{{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\cdots\oplus{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})}_{n~{copies}}.

Define
\mathbbm{d}^{1}:{\mathfrak{C}}_{{\rm c}\mathsf{3Lie}}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{C}}_{{\rm c}\mathsf{3Lie}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
by

3.2.

In this subsection, we study infinitesimal deformations of compatible
3
-Lie algebras using the cohomology theory of compatible

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a compatible
3
-Lie algebra,

\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
.
Define

(9)

[x,y,z]_{t}=[x,y,z]+t\omega_{1}(x,y,z),\;\;\{x,y,z\}_{t}=\{x,y,z\}+t\omega_{2}(x,y,z),\;\;\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

is still a

3
-Lie algebra structure modulo

, then we say that
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

generates an

of

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
.

It is straightforward to verify that
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

generates an infinitesimal deformation of a
compatible
3
-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
if and only if for any

generates an infinitesimal
deformation of the
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket=k_{1}[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]+k_{2}\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})

We set

\pi_{1}(x,y,z)=[x,y,z],\quad\pi_{2}(x,y,z)=\{x,y,z\}.

By Theorem
2.10
,
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{t},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{t})
is an infinitesimal deformation of
(\mathfrak{g},\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
if and only if

{}[{\pi}_{1},{\omega}_{1}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}=0,\quad[{\pi}_{1},{\omega}_{2}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}+[{\pi}_{2},{\omega}_{1}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}=0,\quad\hfill[{\pi}_{2},{\omega}_{2}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}=0,

i.e.

\mathbbm{d}^{2}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=0
, which means that

(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in{\mathfrak{C}}_{{\rm c}\mathsf{3Lie}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is a
2
-cocycle for the
compatible
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
.

Definition 3.4
.

Two infinitesimal deformations
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{t},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{t})
and

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{t}^{\prime},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{t}^{\prime})
of a compatible
3
-Lie algebra

generated by
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
and

(\omega^{\prime}_{1},\omega^{\prime}_{2})
respectively are said to be

if there exists

such that

is a compatible
3
-Lie algebra homomorphism modulo

.

Two infinitesimal deformations
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{t},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{t})
and

generated by
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
and

respectively are equivalent
if and only if

(10)

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(x,y,z)=

\displaystyle\omega_{1}^{\prime}(x,y,z)+[Nx,y,z]+[x,Ny,z]+[x,y,Nz]-N([x,y,z]),

(11)

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(x,y,z)=

\displaystyle\omega_{2}^{\prime}(x,y,z)+\{Nx,y,z\}+\{x,Ny,z\}+\{x,y,Nz\}-N(\{x,y,z\}).

It is easy to see that

and

mean that

.

We summarize the above discussion by the following

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{t},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{t})
be an infinitesimal deformation of a compatible
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})

. Then
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
is

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{t},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{t})

3.3.

In this subsection, we study 2-order 1-parameter deformations of compatible
3
-Lie algebras, and introduce the notion of a Nijenhuis operator on a compatible
3
-Lie algebra, which could generate a trivial deformation.

First we recall Nijenhuis operators on 3-Lie algebras.
Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a
3
-Lie algebra. We also use
\pi:\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
to indicate the
3
-Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]

\pi(x,y,z)=[x,y,z].

We use

to denote the Nijenhuis torsion of

defined by

\displaystyle T_{\pi}N:=\frac{1}{2}[[[\pi,N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}},N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}},N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}-\frac{1}{2}[[\pi,N^{2}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}},N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}-[[\pi,N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}},N^{2}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}+[\pi,N^{3}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}.

More precisely,

\displaystyle T_{\pi}N(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3[Nx,Ny,Nz]-3N\Big([Nx,Ny,z]+[x,Ny,Nz]+[Nx,y,Nz]

\displaystyle-N[Nx,y,z]-N[x,Ny,z]-N[x,y,Nz]+N^{2}[x,y,z]\Big).

Definition 3.6
.

(
[
18
]
)
Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a

-Lie algebra. A linear map

on

if

, i.e.

(12)

\displaystyle[Nx,Ny,Nz]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\Big([Nx,Ny,z]+[x,Ny,Nz]+[Nx,y,Nz]

\displaystyle-N[Nx,y,z]-N[x,Ny,z]-N[x,y,Nz]+N^{2}[x,y,z]\Big),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proposition 3.7
.

(
[
18

)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Nx,Ny,z]+[x,Ny,Nz]+[Nx,y,Nz]

Lemma 3.8

Denote the deformed bracket

\displaystyle\pi_{N}:=\frac{1}{2}\Big([[\pi,N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}},N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}-[\pi,N^{2}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}\Big).

It follows from straightforward computations. We omit details.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let

-Lie algebra

Let

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]).
By (

\displaystyle[\pi,\pi_{N}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\pi,[[\pi,N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}},N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}-\frac{1}{2}[\pi,[\pi,N^{2}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}},]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}([[\pi,[\pi,N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}},N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}-[[\pi,N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}},[\pi,N]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}})

Remark 3.10
.

The above result is different from the case of Lie algebras.

is naturally a compatible Lie algebra. See

17

for more details.

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Nx,Ny,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,Ny,Nz\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Nx,y,Nz\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle-N\left\llbracket Nx,y,z\right\rrbracket-N\left\llbracket x,Ny,z\right\rrbracket-N\left\llbracket x,y,Nz\right\rrbracket+N^{2}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{1}[x,y,z]_{N}+k_{2}\{x,y,z\}_{N},

which implies that

-Lie algebra

is a Nijenhuis operator on the compatible

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})

Definition 4.1
.

(
[
16
]
)

A
representation
of a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle~\rho(x_{1},x_{2})\rho(x_{3},x_{4})

\displaystyle\rho([x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{4})+\rho(x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\rho(x_{3},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{2}),

\displaystyle~\rho(x_{1},[x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x_{3},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{2})-\rho(x_{2},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{3})+\rho(x_{2},x_{3})\rho(x_{1},x_{4}).

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be vector spaces. Denote by
\mathfrak{g}^{l,k}
the subspace of
\wedge^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})\otimes
\overset{(n)}{\cdots}
\otimes\wedge^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})\wedge(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})

which contains the number of

(resp.

l
(resp.

Then the vector space
\wedge^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})\otimes
\overset{(n)}{\cdots}

is isomorphic to the direct sum of
\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},~l+k=2n+1
. For instance,

\wedge^{3}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})=\mathfrak{g}^{3,0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{2,1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{1,2}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{0,3}.

An element

(resp.
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
) naturally gives an element
\hat{f}\in C^{n}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
, which is called its
lift
.
For example, the lifts of linear maps
\pi:\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{1},~\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are defined by

(32)

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}\big((x,u),(y,v),(z,w)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi(x,y,z),0),

(33)

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}\big((x,u),(y,v),(z,w)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0,\rho(x,y)w+\rho(y,z)u+\rho(z,x)v),

respectively.
It is straightforward to see that we have the following natural isomorphism

(34)

\displaystyle C^{n}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})\cong\sum_{l+k=2n+1}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g}_{1})\oplus\sum_{l+k=2n+1}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g}_{2}).

Definition 4.2
.

[
15
]

A linear map

l|k

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

All the other case,
f(X)=0.

A linear map
f
is said to be homogeneous if
f
has a bidegree.
Linear maps
\hat{\pi},~\hat{\rho}\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
given in (

[

.

\displaystyle[\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho},\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho}]_{\mathsf{3Lie}}=0.

Proposition 4.9

Theorem 4.14
.

Two abelian extensions of

-Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}\oplus_{\rho,\mu,\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}V

\mathfrak{g}\oplus_{\rho,\mu,\omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{2}^{\prime}}V

(\omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{2}^{\prime})
are in the same cohomology class.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{g}\oplus_{\rho,\mu,\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}V
and
\mathfrak{g}\oplus_{\rho,\mu,\omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{2}^{\prime}}V
be isomorphic abelian extensions,
and
\theta:\mathfrak{g}\oplus_{\rho,\mu,\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\oplus_{\rho,\mu,\omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{2}^{\prime}}V
be the corresponding isomorphism.
Then there exist
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
such that

\theta(x+u)=x+\tau(x)+u,\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V.

For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},
we have

\displaystyle\theta[x,y,z]_{\rho,\omega_{1}}=\theta([x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\omega_{1}(x,y,z))=[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\tau([x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\omega_{1}(x,y,z),

and

\displaystyle[\theta(x),\theta(y),\theta(z)]_{\rho,\omega_{1}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x+\tau(x),y+\tau(y),z+\tau(z)]_{\rho,\omega_{1}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\omega_{1}^{\prime}(x,y,z)+\rho(x,y)\tau(z)+\rho(y,z)\tau(x)+\rho(z,x)\tau(y).

Therefore, by
\theta[x,y,z]_{\rho,\omega_{1}}=[\theta(x),\theta(y),\theta(z)]_{\rho,\omega_{1}^{\prime}}
, we get

\displaystyle(\omega_{1}-\omega_{1}^{\prime})(x,y,z)=\rho(x,y)\tau(z)+\rho(y,z)\tau(x)+\rho(z,x)\tau(y)-\tau([x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\mathrm{d}^{1}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}\tau(x,y,z),

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle(\omega_{2}-\omega_{2}^{\prime})(x,y,z)=\mu(x,y)\tau(z)+\mu(y,z)\tau(x)+\mu(z,x)\tau(y)-\tau(\{x,y,z\}_{\mathfrak{g}})=\mathrm{d}^{1}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}\tau(x,y,z),

which means that
(\omega_{1}-\omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{2}-\omega_{2}^{\prime})=\delta^{1}\tau

Therefore,
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
and
(\omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{2}^{\prime})
are in the same cohomology class.

The converse part can be proved similarly, and we omit details.

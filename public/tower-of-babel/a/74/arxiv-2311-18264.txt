Cm 15+ and Bk 16+ ion clocks with enhanced sensitivity to new physics
V. A. Dzuba and V. V. Flambaum

Abstract
We perform calculations of electronic structure of Cm 15+ and Bk 16+ ions and demonstrate that they have transitions which combine the features of atomic optical clocks with the enhanced sensitivity to the variations of the fine structure constant α \alpha . The high sensitivity is due to large nuclear charge Z Z , high ionisation degree Z i Z_{i} and the effect of level crossing , which enables optical transitions between states of different configurations. These are the 6 ​ p 1 / 2 − 5 ​ f 5 / 2 6p_{1/2}-5f_{5/2} and 6 ​ p 1 / 2 − 5 ​ f 7 / 2 6p_{1/2}-5f_{7/2} transitions in the single valence electron approximation. Variation of α \alpha may be due to the interaction with scalar and pseudoscalar (axion) dark matter fields. Therefore, Cm 15+ and Bk 16+ clocks are promising candidates to search for these fields.

Ion-based optical clocks are susceptible to thermal motion due
to the finite ion temperature. This motion can be reduced by applying sympathetic cooling of the clock ion via the
cotrapped logic ion. The most efficient sympathetic cooling occurs when the charge-to-mass ratio of the clock ion is equal
to that of the logic ion

Method of calculations.

We treat the Cm

[1s^{2}\dots 5d^{10}]

Calculations start from the relativistic Hartree-Fock (RHF) procedure for the closed-shell core.
The RHF Hamiltonian has the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\rm RHF}=c\mathbf{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{p}+(\beta-1)mc^{2}+

(6)

\displaystyle V_{\rm nuc}+V_{\rm Breit}+V_{\rm QED}+V_{\rm core},

where

\mathbf{\alpha}
and
\beta

\mathbf{p}
is the electron momentum,

is the potential due to the Breit interaction
Breit

V_{\rm QED}
is the potential describing quantum electrodynamic corrections
QED
,
V_{\rm core}
is the self-consistent RHF potential created by all core electrons.
The single-electron basis states for valence electrons are calculated in the field of the frozen core using the B-spline technique
B-spline
.
These basis states are used in all consequent calculations.
This corresponds to the so-called

The single-double coupled-cluster method (SD) is used (we use the version described in Ref.

\hat{\Sigma}_{1}

\hat{H}^{\rm CI}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left(\hat{H}^{\rm RHF}+\hat{\Sigma}_{1}\right)_{i}+\sum_{i<j}^{M}\left(\frac{e^{2}}{r_{ij}}+\hat{\Sigma}_{2ij}\right).

(7)

\left(\hat{H}^{\rm CI}-E_{a}\right)X_{a}=0,

(8)

where

The RPA equations have the form (see e.g.

(9)

where

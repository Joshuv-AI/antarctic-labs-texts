Holographic RG from ERG: Locality and General Coordinate Invariance in the Bulk
Pavan Dharanipragada, B. Sathiapalan

Abstract
In earlier papers it was shown that the correct kinetic term for scalar, vector gauge field and the spin two field in A ​ d ​ S D + 1 AdS_{D+1} space is obtained starting from the ERG equation for a C ​ F ​ T D CFT_{D} perturbed by scalar composite, conserved vector current and conserved traceless energy momentum tensor respectively. In this paper interactions are studied and it is shown that a flipped version of Polchinski ERG equation that evolves towards the UV can be written down and is useful for making contact with the usual AdS/CFT prescriptions for correlation function calculations. The scalar-scalar-spin-2 interaction in the bulk is derived from the ERG equation in the large N N semiclasical approximation. It is also shown that after mapping to AdS the interaction is local on a scale of the bare cutoff rather than the moving cutoff (which would have corresponded to the AdS scale). The map to A ​ d ​ S D + 1 AdS_{D+1} plays a crucial role in this locality. The local nature of the coupling ensures that this interaction term in the bulk action is obtained by gauge fixing a general coordinate invariant scalar kinetic term in the bulk action. A wave function renormalization of the scalar field is found to be required for a mutually consistent map of the two fields to A ​ d ​ S D + 1 AdS_{D+1} .

By the phrase “from first principles” we mean the following:

We start with the boundary field theory with

We then write down an evolution operator for this ERG equation in the form of a functional integral over a field theory in one higher dimension i.e.

It is in this sense that we use the phrase “from first principles”.

The result of the calculation is that the interaction terms are almost local. It turns out that with some minor modifications they can be made exactly local. This is one of the important results of this paper. It is not clear whether this procedure can be consistently extended for quartic and higher interactions or indeed for all other cubic interactions.

Outline:

To start with, the fixed point theory is a conformal field theory. We consider the Gaussian fixed point of the O(N) model, free field theory with the action

The free O(N) model has an infinite number of conserved currents that are O(N) singlets, one for each even spin. They could be scalars or in the form of higher spin currents. towards the UV

3.1
Gauge fixing a General Coordinate Invariant Action

3.1.1
Free Theory

In
[
28
]
it was shown that starting from the ERG equation for a free O(N) scalar field theory
perturbed by the term
h^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}
, (where
T_{\mu\nu}
is the improved energy momentum tensor), in the boundary of AdS, one can construct a bulk dual action for a free massless spin 2 field in the AdS bulk. In the boundary theory this spin 2 tensor is an auxiliary field standing for the traceless conserved energy momentum tensor composite field. In the bulk a massless spin 2 field must be a graviton. This is confirmed by comparing this action with a bulk calculation. To be more precise, one starts with the Einstein action and takes the metric in the form
(g_{AdS})_{MN}+h_{MN}
, where
(g_{AdS})_{MN}
(
M=1,...,D+1
)is the background
AdS_{D+1}
metric given by

ds^{2}=\frac{dz^{2}+\delta_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}}{z^{2}}.

and
h_{MN}
is a small deviation. Extract the quadratic action for
h_{MN}
from the Einstein action. One can set (
x^{M}=(z,x^{\mu})
) (in the gauge
h_{zM}=0
)

h_{zz}=0=h_{\mu z}=\partial^{\mu}h_{\mu\nu}=h^{\mu}_{\mu}.

(3.1)

This is a transverse-traceless gauge. The quadratic action so obtained, i.e. by gauge fixing a general coordinate invariant action is
[

50

51

S=\frac{1}{2}\int dz\frac{d^{D}p}{(2\pi)^{D}}~z^{1-D}[z^{4}\partial_{z}h_{\mu\nu}\partial^{z}h^{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\rho}h_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\rho}h^{\mu\nu}+4z^{3}h_{\mu\nu}\partial_{z}h^{\mu\nu}+4z^{2}h_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu}].

(3.2)

Indices are raised in the above action using
\delta^{MN}

Setting
h_{\mu\nu}z^{2}=y_{\mu\nu}
,

S=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{D+1}x~z^{-D+1}~[\partial_{z}y_{\mu\nu}\partial^{z}y^{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\rho}y_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\rho}y^{\mu\nu}].

(3.3)

This is compared with what is obtained from ERG and one finds that there is agreement
[
28
]
. Thus one can say that at the free level, a dynamical gravity consistent with general coordinate invariance is obtained starting from the ERG equation of a boundary CFT in flat space. That dynamical gravity emerges from ERG is conceptually remarkable.

3.1.2
Interactions

One can further test general coordinate invariance at the interacting level as follows. The ERG procedure applied to a scalar composite of the boundary theory generates an action
[
26
]

S=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\sqrt{g_{AdS}}~g_{AdS}^{MN}\partial_{M}\phi\partial_{N}\phi.

(3.4)

Here
\int_{x}\equiv\int\mathrm{d}^{D+1}x
.

Let us analyse this from the bulk viewpoint. Our bulk starting point is a general coordinate invariant action:

S=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\sqrt{g}~g^{MN}\partial_{M}\phi\partial_{N}\phi.

(3.5)

If we expand about
AdS
background
g_{MN}=(g_{AdS})_{MN}+h_{MN}
one expects a graviton-scalar-scalar coupling of the form

S=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\sqrt{g_{AdS}}~[g_{AdS}^{MN}\partial_{M}\phi\partial_{N}\phi+~h^{MN}\partial_{M}\phi\partial_{N}\phi].

(3.6)

(The
\sqrt{g}
does not contribute a term linear in
h
if it is traceless.) In the gauge (
3.1
) the interaction term becomes

S=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\sqrt{g_{AdS}}~h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi.

(3.7)

In this paper we will verify that such a coupling does indeed exist. This shows that the action obtained from ERG is general coordinate invariant to this order.

Since the action obtained in the bulk using the ERG procedure can be obtained by gauge fixing a general coordinate invariant theory, one concludes that the bulk theory obtained by
the ERG procedure is indeed a general coordinate invariant theory of gravity. One can then ask whether it is possible to change gauge from (
3.1
) to some other gauge. This should be equivalent to a coordinate transformation. Thus
we are interested in studying the change in form due to a coordinate transformation (change of variables).

We start with

S=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\sqrt{G}~G^{MN}\partial_{M}\phi\partial_{N}\phi.

Here
G_{MN}
has the functional form of an
AdS
metric but is not a dynamical field. Thus to begin with
G_{MN}=(g_{AdS})_{MN}
.
Under a change of coordinates

G^{MN}(x)=G^{MN}(x^{\prime}+\epsilon(x^{\prime}))=G^{MN}(x^{\prime})+\epsilon^{P}(x^{\prime})\partial_{P}G^{MN}(x^{\prime}),

\frac{\partial\phi(x)}{\partial x^{M}}=\bigg(\delta^{Q}_{M}-\frac{\partial\epsilon^{Q}}{\partial x^{M}}\bigg)\frac{\partial\phi(x^{\prime}+\epsilon)}{\partial x^{\prime Q}},

\sqrt{G}G^{MN}(x)\partial_{M}\phi(x)\partial_{N}\phi(x)=\sqrt{G}G^{MN}\frac{\partial\phi(x^{\prime})}{\partial x^{\prime M}}\frac{\partial\phi(x^{\prime})}{\partial x^{\prime N}}+\epsilon^{R}\partial_{R}\bigg(\sqrt{G}G^{MN}\frac{\partial\phi(x^{\prime})}{\partial x^{\prime M}}\frac{\partial\phi(x^{\prime})}{\partial x^{\prime N}}\bigg)

-\sqrt{G}\bigg(\frac{\partial\epsilon^{M}}{\partial x^{Q}}G^{QN}+\frac{\partial\epsilon^{N}}{\partial x^{P}}G^{MP}\bigg)\frac{\partial\phi(x^{\prime})}{\partial x^{\prime M}}\frac{\partial\phi(x^{\prime})}{\partial x^{\prime N}}.

Similarly,

\int d^{D+1}x=\int d^{D+1}x^{\prime}(1+\partial_{R}\epsilon^{R}).

This modified action is physically equivalent to the original action because all we have done is a change of variables. But it is not manifestly invariant. Because the metric is not a dynamical variable. If we drop the boundary term, (assuming that
\epsilon^{R}
vanishes at the boundary—small gauge transformations), then we are left with a change (
x

x^{\prime}
to this order in

-\int_{x^{\prime}}\sqrt{G}\bigg(\frac{\partial\epsilon^{M}}{\partial x^{\prime Q}}G^{QN}+\frac{\partial\epsilon^{N}}{\partial x^{\prime P}}G^{MP}\bigg)\frac{\partial\phi(x^{\prime})}{\partial x^{\prime M}}\frac{\partial\phi(x^{\prime})}{\partial x^{\prime N}}.

If we find a coupling to a dynamical spin two field of the form

\delta S=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\sqrt{G}~h^{MN}\partial_{M}\phi\partial_{N}\phi

(3.8)

then this change can be absorbed into a change of field variable

h^{MN}-\frac{\partial\epsilon^{M}}{\partial x^{\prime Q}}G^{QN}-\frac{\partial\epsilon^{N}}{\partial x^{\prime P}}G^{MP}=h^{\prime MN}.

(3.9)

This gives
Polchinski’s ERG equation:

\frac{\partial\Psi_{l}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{p}\dot{\Delta}_{h}(p)\frac{\delta^{2}\Psi_{l}}{\delta\phi^{I}_{l}(p)\delta\phi^{I}_{l}(-p)}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{p}\dot{\Delta}_{l}(p)\frac{\delta^{2}\Psi_{l}}{\delta\phi^{I}_{l}(p)\delta\phi^{I}_{l}(-p)}.

(4.5)

As
\Lambda
is lowered the
\phi_{l}
modes get integrated over.

Notice that mathematically there is complete symmetry between
\phi_{l},\Delta_{l}
and
\phi_{h},\Delta_{h}
in the way they appear in the starting action, i.e. there is a symmetry:

\Delta_{h}\leftrightarrow\Delta_{l}~~~;~~~\phi_{h}\leftrightarrow\phi_{l}.

(4.6)

Flipped Polchinski ERG Equation:

Thus let us repeat all the steps above but this time we integrate out
\phi_{l}
first and obtain a Wilson action for
\phi_{h}
. We can then derive an equation describing the evolution of the Wilson action as
\Lambda
is increased and the
\phi_{h}
modes are integrated out. The resulting equation can be obtained simply by interchanging
l,h
in the previous equation (
4.5
) to obtain

\frac{\partial\Psi_{h}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{p}\dot{\Delta}_{l}(p)\frac{\delta^{2}\Psi_{h}}{\delta\phi^{I}_{h}(p)\delta\phi^{I}_{h}(-p)}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{p}\dot{\Delta}_{h}(p)\frac{\delta^{2}\Psi_{h}}{\delta\phi^{I}_{h}(p)\delta\phi^{I}_{h}(-p)},

(4.7)

where

\Psi_{h}=e^{-S_{I,\Lambda}[\phi_{h},J]}=\int{\cal D}\phi_{l}~e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{N}\int\phi^{I}_{l}\Delta_{l}^{-1}\phi^{I}_{l}-S_{B,I}[\phi_{l}+\phi_{h}]+\int J(\phi_{l}+\phi_{h})}.

(4.8)

This is an action obtained by integrating out
\phi_{l}
rather than
\phi_{h}
. We will refer to (
4.7
) as the flipped ERG equation.

We hasten to add that this exchange given in (
4.6
) does not imply any physical symmetry between the low energy dynamics and the high energy dynamics. All that is being done is that the order of integration has been reversed -
\phi_{l}
is integrated out first and then
\phi_{h}
.

So far, since
\Delta_{l},\Delta_{h}
have not been specified the physical significance of these equations have not become apparent. Let us now make a choice of propagators:

\Delta_{l}=\frac{e^{-p^{2}/\Lambda^{2}}}{p^{2}},~~~\Delta_{h}=\frac{e^{-p^{2}/\Lambda_{0}^{2}}-e^{-p^{2}/\Lambda^{2}}}{p^{2}}.

(4.9)

Here
\Lambda_{0}
(with
\Lambda\leq\Lambda_{0}
) refers to a bare cutoff and for the continuum theory one can take
\Lambda_{0}\to\infty
. So for our purposes

\Delta_{l}=\frac{e^{-p^{2}/\Lambda^{2}}}{p^{2}},~~~\Delta_{h}=\frac{1-e^{-p^{2}/\Lambda^{2}}}{p^{2}}.

(4.10)

Now it becomes clear that in
\Psi_{l}
the high energy modes

\phi_{h}
have been integrated out and thus
S_{I,\Lambda}[\phi_{l},J]
is the Wilson action and (
4.5
) is Polchinski’s ERG equation. (
4.7
) is a mirror image of Polchinski’s equation where the low energy modes have been integrated out. In (
4.7
), when
\Lambda\to\infty

the full functional integral has been done and we have the final answer for
Z[J]
.

Integrating
\phi_{l}
may seem unphysical from a Wilsonian point of view, because one obtains non local terms, but in fact this is what a standard perturbative Feynman diagram calculation does. Consider the typical loop integral in a field theory

\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{1}{p^{2}(p+k)^{2}}.

It is clear here that the low energy modes with
0<|p|<\Lambda
are the ones being integrated. Eventually
\Lambda
is taken to infinity to get continuum results. Thus while the interpretation of a “low energy” effective action is lost, mathematically this is a valid procedure.

In practice the low energy theory may be complicated and one may not know how to do the integral over the low energy fields due to infrared problems for instance. One may also imagine that the low energy integral is done using the bulk theory. In any case the evolution equation continues to be valid.

Mapping Evolution Operator to AdS

The evolution operator for the flipped ERG equation can be written as

\int{\cal D}\sigma{\cal D}\sigma_{\mu\nu}~\exp\bigg\{-\int dt\int_{p}~\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\frac{\dot{\sigma}\dot{\sigma}}{\dot{G}_{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\dot{\sigma}_{\mu\nu}\dot{\sigma}^{\mu\nu}}{\dot{G}_{t}}+~V(\sigma,\sigma_{\mu\nu})\bigg]\bigg\}

-4\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\int_{p}(\Delta_{l}(p+k_{1}+k_{2})\Delta_{l}(p+k_{1})p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\Delta_{l}(p)g(k_{1})g(k_{2}))\Big)\Big(\frac{\sigma(k_{1},t)}{G_{s}(k_{1},t)}\frac{\sigma(k_{2},t)}{G_{s}(k_{2},t)}\frac{\sigma_{\mu\nu}(k_{3},t)}{G_{t}(k_{3},t)}\Big)\bigg\}.

(7.1)

The scalar part of this was done in Section 3, (without the factor
g
). As shown in Section 3.1, substituting the leading order solution, one sees that the factors
\frac{\sigma(k,t)}{G_{s}(k,t)}
are all time independent. Then the integrand becomes a total derivative and we recover the expected amplitudes. The important point is that the final amplitude thus depends only on the value of
g\Delta_{l}
at the limits of the
t

z=\infty

\Delta
at the other
(z=0)

8
Summary and Conclusions

The most interesting aspect of the AdS-CFT correspondence is that the bulk dual of a field theory in flat space is a theory with dynamical gravity. The approach outlined in this and earlier papers demonstrates that the functional integral describing an evolution operator of the Exact RG equation of a flat space field theory involves a field theory in a background AdS space and further that the metric fluctuations about this background are also dynamical. That dynamical gravity comes out of ERG is quite remarkable. This demonstration did not assume the AdS-CFT conjecture.

The calculation described in this paper also explains why AdS is special. While the functional integral description of the ERG evolution operator started off with a non standard action, a very special field redefinition was required to map this to a field theory in AdS space. What we see is that when this is done the action is local on a much smaller scale than the AdS scale. The scale of locality is set by the bare cutoff of the field theory rather than the moving cutoff. In the bulk this should correspond to the Planck scale or the string scale. This was already shown for the kinetic terms in earlier papers
[
24
,
28
]
. In this paper this was demonstrated for the scalar-scalar-spin 2 coupling (
7.6
). This also happens for the cubic scalar coupling(
7.3
).

The locality of the scalar gravity coupling also ensures that this interaction term can be obtained by gauge fixing a general coordinate invariant scalar kinetic term. The spin 2 kinetic term is also obtainable by gauge fixing the kinetic term for a metric perturbation obtained from the Einstein action in an AdS background
[
28
]
. Thus both these calculations provide evidence for a general coordinate invariant theory in the bulk. If this persists to higher orders, that would provide some further insight into the AdS-CFT correspondence.

In deriving this cubic interaction it turned out to be useful to work with a flipped ERG equation (
4.7
), that evolved the theory towards the UV rather than the towards the IR as in the usual Wilsonian RG. It remains to be seen whether this equation has other applications.

Another ingredient in making all this work is the necessity of performing a wave function renormalization of the scalar field standing for the composite scalar. One expects that extending the ERG approach to higher spins will involve similar wave function renomrmalizations for all the fields.

Extending all this to higher spins is certainly an interesting open problem. Some aspects of RG for higher spins has been discussed in
[
48
,
54
,
55
]
. It would be interesting to apply our techniques to understand the higher spin action and symmetries along those lines. That would supplement holographic reconstruction of the bulk higher spin interactions from the boundary
O(N)
model
[
56
]
. A consistent application of the prescription in this paper could be applied to obtain bulk higher spin interactions and strengthen the higher spin-
O(N)
model duality
[
57
]
.

All our calculations have been done for Euclidean field theories. The issue of analytically continuation to Minkowski space also needs to be investigated.

These are pressing questions and we hope to address these soon.

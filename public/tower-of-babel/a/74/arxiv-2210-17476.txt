Powersum Bases in Quasisymmetric Functions and Quasisymmetric Functions in Non-commuting Variables
Anthony Lazzeroni

Abstract
We introduce a new P P basis for the Hopf algebra of quasisymmetric functions that refine the symmetric powersum basis. Unlike the quasisymmetric power sums of types 1 and 2, our basis is defined combinatorially: its expansion in quasisymmetric monomial functions is given by fillings of matrices. This basis has a shuffle product, a deconcatenate coproduct, and has a change of basis rule to the quasisymmetric fundamental basis by using tuples of ribbons. We lift our quasisymmetric powersum P P basis to the Hopf algebra of quasisymmetric functions in non-commuting variables by introducing fillings with disjoint sets. This new basis has a shifted shuffle product and a standard deconcatenate coproduct, and certain basis elements agree with the fundamental basis of the Malvenuto-Reutenauer Hopf algebra of permutations. Finally we discuss how to generalize these bases and their properties by using total orders on indices.

2
Preliminaries for QSym

Given an integer composition of
n
of length
k
,
\alpha=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
, let
m_{i}(\alpha)
denote the multiplicities of the part size
i
within
\alpha
, and denote the weak composition of size multiplicities by
m(\alpha)=(m_{1}(\alpha),\dotsc,m_{n}(\alpha))
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a poset on compositions with the cover relation
\alpha\lessdot\beta
if
\beta
is obtained from
\alpha
by summing two adjacent parts, i.e.
\beta=(a_{1},\dotsc,a_{i}+a_{i+1},\dotsc,a_{k})
.

For the purpose of several proofs in Section
10
, the standard bijection between compositions of
n
and subsets of
[n-1]
will be useful:

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\longleftrightarrow\{a_{1},a_{1}+a_{2},\ldots,a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k-1}\}.

Using this identification, we see
\alpha\lessdot\beta
if and only if
\alpha=\beta\cup\{k\}
for some
k\in[n-1]
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},x_{2}\ldots\}
be a set of variables and
\alpha=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
be a composition of
n
. Then a generating function is quasisymmetric if, for every
k
and
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}
, the coefficient of
x_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}^{\alpha_{k}}
is equal to the coefficient of
x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
. When
|X|=\infty
, the set of all quasisymmetric functions is a graded Hopf algebra denoted as
\mathrm{QSym}
. One of the more natural bases of
\mathrm{QSym}
is the
quasisymmetric monomial basis

M_{\alpha}
, defined as

M_{\alpha}=\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}}x_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}^{\alpha_{k}}.

\mathrm{QSym}
is most notable for the
quasisymmetric fundamental basis

F_{\alpha}
which proves useful for countless enumeration problems and also corresponds to the characters of the 0-Hecke algebra. It is defined as

F_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}M_{\beta}.

3.1
Fillings Interpretation

A

. The

. The following two fillings are important to the sequel.

Remark 1
.

Fillings can be defined more generally to generate other bases in

]
, but for the subject of this paper the definition above suffices.

Definition 1
.

A

filling is a filling such that:

1.

entry

is in the upper leftmost corner of the matrix.

2.

entry
a_{i}
is in row
i
.

3.

(a)

a_{i}
is directly below
a_{i-1}
or in the southeast position of
a_{i-1}

(b)

if

a_{i}
is in the southeast position of

4.

row

3.2
Subposet Interpretation

Notice that we can define these quasipowersums by subposets as all fillings only coarsen according to the row reading. Given compositions

Theorem 2
.

Let
\gamma
be a composition of
n
, then a quasisymmetric powersum has a deconcatenate coproduct:

\Delta(P_{\gamma})=\sum_{\alpha|\beta=\gamma}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{m_{i}(\gamma)!}{m_{i}(\alpha)!m_{i}(\beta)!}P_{\alpha}\otimes P_{\beta}.

(8)

Proof.

By (
3
) and (
7
)

\Delta(P_{\gamma})=\Delta\left(\sum_{\mathsf{F}\in\mathcal{SD}(\gamma)}|\mathfrak{S}_{\mathsf{F}}|M_{\mathsf{col}(\mathsf{F})}\right)=\sum_{\mathsf{F}\in\mathcal{SD}(\gamma)}\sum_{\mathsf{F}_{1}|\mathsf{F}_{2}=\mathsf{F}}|\mathfrak{S}_{\mathsf{F}}|M_{\mathsf{col}(\mathsf{F}_{1})}\otimes M_{\mathsf{col}(\mathsf{F}_{2})}.

Let
m
be the degree of
\mathsf{col}(\mathsf{F}_{1})
, then fix
m
. Let
\mathsf{F}_{1}^{\prime}
be the diagonal filling with
\mathsf{row}(\mathsf{F}_{1})=\mathsf{row}(\mathsf{F}_{1}^{\prime})
, say
\mathsf{row}(\mathsf{F}_{1}^{\prime})=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{j})
. Then every filling of degree
m
comes from moving entry
\gamma_{i+1}
to the column of
\gamma_{i}
when
\gamma_{i}\geq\gamma_{i+1}
for certain choices of

m
is precisely
\mathcal{SD}(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{j})
. The same is said for the right side, and thus

\sum_{\mathsf{F}\in\mathcal{SD}(\gamma)}\sum_{\mathsf{F}_{1}|\mathsf{F}_{2}=\mathsf{F}}|\mathfrak{S}_{\mathsf{F}}|M_{\mathsf{col}(\mathsf{F}_{1})}\otimes M_{\mathsf{col}(\mathsf{F}_{2})}=\sum_{\alpha|\beta=\gamma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{F}_{1}\in\mathcal{SD}(\alpha)\\

\frac{|\mathfrak{S}_{\mathsf{F}_{1}|\mathsf{F}_{2}}|}{|\mathfrak{S}_{\mathsf{F}_{1}}||\mathfrak{S}_{\mathsf{F}_{2}}|}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\frac{m_{i}(\gamma)!}{c_{i}(\gamma,\mathsf{col}(\mathsf{F}_{1}|\mathsf{F}_{2}))!}}{\frac{m_{i}(\alpha)!}{c_{i}(\alpha,\mathsf{col}(\mathsf{F}_{1}))!}\frac{m_{i}(\beta)!}{c_{i}(\beta,\mathsf{col}(\mathsf{F}_{2}))!}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{m_{i}(\gamma)!}{m_{i}(\alpha)!m_{i}(\beta)!}

\mathsf{F}_{1}

Theorem 3
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be compositions of
n
, then the quasipowersums have a shuffle product, in other words

P_{\alpha}P_{\beta}=\sum_{\gamma\in\alpha\shuffle\beta}\prod_{i}\frac{m_{i}(\alpha)!m_{i}(\beta)!}{m_{i}(\gamma)!}P_{\gamma}.

Proof.

From (
3
)

\displaystyle P_{\alpha}P_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\mathsf{F}_{1}\in\mathcal{SD}(\alpha)}|\mathfrak{S}_{\mathsf{F}_{1}}|M_{\mathsf{col}(\mathsf{F}_{1})}\right)\left(\sum_{\mathsf{F}_{2}\in\mathcal{SD}(\beta)}|\mathfrak{S}_{\mathsf{F}_{2}}|M_{\mathsf{col}(\mathsf{F}_{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{F}_{1}\in\mathcal{SD}(\alpha),\mathsf{F}_{2}\in\mathcal{SD}(\beta)\\
\mathsf{F}\in\mathsf{F}_{1}\widetilde{\shuffle}\mathsf{F}_{2}\end{subarray}}|\mathfrak{S}_{\mathsf{F}_{1}}||\mathfrak{S}_{\mathsf{F}_{2}}|M_{\mathsf{col}(F)}.

In order to express the above in the powersum basis, we have to switch the sums, i.e. use
\mathcal{SD}(\alpha\shuffle\beta)=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\alpha\shuffle\beta}\mathcal{SD}(\gamma)
instead of
\mathcal{SD}(\alpha)\widetilde{\shuffle}\mathcal{SD}(\beta)
. We will first show that these two are equivalent as sets, then show that multiplying by
O_{\mathsf{F}}
(to be defined) will result in them being equal as multisets. Finally, we will reduce the coefficient.

Notice that any filling in
\mathcal{SD}(\alpha)\widetilde{\shuffle}\mathcal{SD}(\beta)
is also in
\mathcal{SD}(\alpha\shuffle\beta)
due to the fact that every filling
\mathsf{F}\in\mathsf{F}_{1}\widetilde{\shuffle}\mathsf{F}_{2}
has the property that
\mathsf{row}(\mathsf{F})\in\mathsf{row}(\mathsf{F}_{1})\shuffle\mathsf{row}(\mathsf{F}_{2})
. Conversely, we can also construct any filling
\mathsf{F}\in\mathcal{SD}(\alpha\shuffle\beta)
as a quasishuffle of two fillings
\mathsf{F}_{1}
and
\mathsf{F}_{2}
of
\mathcal{SD}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{SD}(\beta)
. Fix a filling
\mathsf{F}
, then if the first row is from
\alpha
, make a new filling
\mathsf{F}_{1}^{\prime}
where the first row is
\mathsf{F}_{1}^{\prime}
is the first row of
\mathsf{F}
(and likewise for
\beta
) and remove the first row from
\mathsf{F}
. Continue to do this for all row to get two fillings
\mathsf{F}_{1}
and
\mathsf{F}_{2}
from
\mathcal{SD}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{SD}(\beta)
. Thus
\mathcal{SD}(\alpha)\widetilde{\shuffle}\mathcal{SD}(\beta)
and
\mathcal{SD}(\alpha\shuffle\beta)
are equivalent as sets, but not as multisets.

In order to switch the sum from
\mathcal{SD}(\alpha)\widetilde{\shuffle}\mathcal{SD}(\beta)
to
\mathcal{SD}(\alpha\shuffle\beta)
, we must consider the double count that comes from the quasishuffle, which will be denoted as
O_{\mathsf{F}}
. We illustrate this with an example

\mathsf{F}
is a quasishuffle of
\mathsf{F}_{1}
and
\mathsf{F}_{2}
there are three ways a column of
\mathsf{F}
is made.

First consider the case when a column

isn’t combined. Fix

, then

doesn’t contribute to the coefficient which leaves us with

\frac{c_{i}((a_{i_{j-1}+1},\ldots,a_{j}),|\alpha_{j}|)!}{c_{i}((a_{i_{j-1}+1},\ldots,a_{j}),|\alpha_{j}|)!}

which is obviously 1. The same argument is made when
\beta_{j^{\prime}}
isn’t combined with any other column.

The third case is when two columns
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{j}^{\prime}
combine. Fix
i
, and we get the expression

\frac{1}{\binom{m_{i}(\alpha_{j})+m_{i}(\beta_{j^{\prime}})}{m_{i}(\beta_{j^{\prime}})}}\frac{c_{i}((a_{i_{j-1}+1},\ldots,a_{j})\shuffle(b_{i_{{j^{\prime}}-1}+1},\ldots,b_{j^{\prime}}),|\alpha_{j}|+|\beta_{j^{\prime}}|)!}{c_{i}((a_{i_{j-1}+1},\ldots,a_{j}),|\alpha_{j}|)!c_{i}((b_{i_{{j^{\prime}}-1}+1},\ldots,b_{j^{\prime}}),|\beta_{j^{\prime}}|)!}

Recall that the formula of
c_{i}
counts the number of
i
’s in the composition
(a_{i_{j-1}+1},\ldots,a_{j})|(b_{i_{{j^{\prime}}-1}+1},\ldots,b_{j^{\prime}})

m_{i}
. Thus using this information and writing out the choose function yields

\frac{m_{i}(\alpha_{j})!(m_{i}(\alpha_{j})+m_{i}(\beta_{j^{\prime}})-m_{i}(\alpha_{j}))!}{(m_{i}(\alpha_{j})+m_{i}(\beta_{j^{\prime}}))!}\frac{m_{i}((a_{i_{j-1}+1},\ldots,a_{j})\shuffle(b_{i_{{j^{\prime}}-1}+1},\ldots,b_{j^{\prime}}))!}{m_{i}((a_{i_{j-1}+1},\ldots,a_{j}))!m_{i}((b_{i_{{j^{\prime}}-1}+1},\ldots,b_{j^{\prime}})))!}.

Recall that
\alpha_{j}
are the entries of column
j
and
(a_{i_{j-1}+1},\ldots,a_{j})
are also the entries of column
j
, thus the expression is 1, which completes the proof.
∎

We with hold a formula for antipode for now.

The Scaled Quasi-Powersum Basis and its Dual

4.1
Scaled Quasi-Powersum Basis

We will now look at a scaled version of this basis that will end up being the dual of the Zassenhauss basis and will have a nice product and coproduct. Recall the scalar

z_{\lambda}=\prod_{i}i^{m_{i}(\lambda)}m_{i}(\lambda)!.

We extend this definition to compositions by
z_{\alpha}=z_{\alpha)}
. Symmetric powersums have the property that they form an orthogonal basis for
\mathrm{Sym}
, to be more exact,
\langle p_{\lambda},p_{\mu}\rangle=z_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda\mu}
where
\langle,\rangle
is the Hall inner product. Thus the
scaled symmetric powersums
is defined as
\widetilde{p}_{\lambda}=z_{\lambda}^{-1}p_{\lambda}
, so that
\langle\widetilde{p}_{\lambda},p_{\mu}\rangle=\delta_{\lambda\mu}
. Though
\mathrm{QSym}
is not self-dual, we define scaled quasipowersums in the same way.

Definition 5
.

A Scaled Quasi-Powersum functions are defined as

\tilde{P}_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{z_{\alpha}}P_{\alpha}.

(10)

By multiplying both sides of (
4
) by
z_{\lambda}^{-1}
it is evident that the scaled quasipowersum functions refines
\widetilde{p}_{\lambda}
. However with this new scaling there is a nicer product and coproduct.

Corollary 1
.

The scaled quasipowersum functions has a deconcatenate coproduct and a shuffle product, in other words

\Delta(\widetilde{P}_{\gamma})=\sum_{\alpha|\beta=\gamma}\widetilde{P}_{\alpha}\otimes\widetilde{P}_{\beta}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{P}_{\alpha}\otimes\widetilde{P}_{\beta}=\sum_{\gamma\in\alpha\shuffle\beta}\widetilde{P}_{\gamma}.

Proof.

Substituting (
10

\Delta(z_{\gamma}\widetilde{P}_{\gamma})=\sum_{\alpha|\beta=\gamma}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{m_{i}(\gamma)!}{m_{i}(\alpha)!m_{i}(\beta)!}z_{\alpha}\widetilde{P}_{\alpha}\otimes z_{\beta}\widetilde{P}_{\beta}.

The coefficient reduces to

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{m_{i}(\gamma)!}{m_{i}(\alpha)!m_{i}(\beta)!}\frac{z_{\alpha}z_{\beta}}{z_{\gamma}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{i^{m_{i}(\alpha)+m_{i}(\beta)}}{i^{m_{i}(\gamma)}}=1.

Likewise we do the same for the coefficient of the shuffle product.

7.1
NCSym

The Hopf algebra of symmetric functions in non-commuting variables, denoted
\mathrm{NCSym}
, was first defined by Wolf in
[
19
]
. This space should not be confused with
\mathrm{NSym}
from Subsection
4.2
. Function in NCSym are indexed by set partitions. A
set partition

\phi=\{\mathrm{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{B}_{k}\}
of
[n]
is a partition of
[n]
into nonempty disjoint subsets. Of course being a partition means that order doesn’t matter, but we write our set partition (for convenience) as
\mathrm{B}_{1}/\cdots/\mathrm{B}_{k}
where
|\mathrm{B}_{1}|\geq\cdots\geq|\mathrm{B}_{k}|
and
\min(\mathrm{B}_{i})>\min(\mathrm{B}_{i+1})
whenever
|\mathrm{B}_{i}|=|\mathrm{B}_{i+1}|
. The poset,
\bar{\mathcal{P}}
, on set partitions,
\phi
and
\psi
, has the covering relation
\phi\lessdot\psi
if
\psi=\mathrm{B}_{1}/\cdots/\mathrm{B}_{i}\cap\mathrm{B}_{j}/\cdots/\mathrm{B}_{k}
.

In
[
17
]
, Rosas and Sagan defined many bases analogous to those in Sym, here we review two. The
symmetric monomial basis in non-commuting variables
is defined as

m_{\phi}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{j}=i_{j^{\prime}}\text{ iff}\\
j,j^{\prime}\in B_{l}\end{subarray}}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}

Then the change of basis formulae of a
symmetric powersum function in noncommuting variables
to a symmetric monomial function in noncommuting variables is

p_{\phi}=\sum_{\phi\leq\psi}m_{\psi}.

(26)

7.2
NCQSym and Morphisms

Functions in
\mathrm{NCQSym}
are indexed by set compositions of
[n]
, or ordered set partitions: a list
\Phi=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{k})
of disjoint nonempty sets satisfying
\bigcup_{i}B_{i}=[n]
. When there is no danger of confusion, we compress the notation for set compositions as usual, e.g.,
(\{5\},\{1,3\},\{2\},\{4\})
is written as
5|13|2|4
. The refinement poset
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}
on set compositions has the cover relation
\Phi\lessdot\Psi
if
\Psi=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{i}\cup B_{i+1},\ldots,B_{j})
.

Denote by
\rho
the map from set compositions of
[n]
to compositions of
n
that records the cardinality of each block. For example
\rho(5|13|2|4)=(1,2,1,1)
. Let
\alpha=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
be a composition, then
\varrho(\alpha)
is the set composition
\Phi=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{k})
where
k
is the number of distinct parts in
\alpha
and
i\in B_{|\{a_{l}:a_{l}<a_{i}\}|+1}
for every
i
in
[n]
. For example
\varrho(1,6,4,3,6)=1|4|3|25
.

The Quasisymmetric functions in non-commuting variables
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
, denoted
\mathrm{NCQSym}
, is the space spanned by the formal series

M_{\Phi}=\sum_{\alpha:\varrho(\alpha)=\Phi}x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\cdots x_{a_{n}}.

The map
\rho
from set compositions to compositions induces a Hopf algebra surjection that we will also denote as
\rho
with
\rho:\mathrm{NCQSym}\to\mathrm{QSym}
given by
\rho(M_{\Phi})=M_{\rho(\Phi)}
.

Analogous to the commutative story, there is a Hopf inclusion of NCSym to NCQSym given by

m_{\phi}=\sum_{\Phi:\Phi)=\phi}M_{\Phi}.

(27)

In

\hat{P}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{F}\in\hat{\mathcal{SD}}(\alpha)\\
\sigma\in\hat{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathsf{F}}\end{subarray}}M_{\mathsf{col}(\sigma(\mathsf{F}))}.

\hat{P}_{\alpha}=\sum_{i\in I}P_{\varsigma^{i}(\alpha)}.

where

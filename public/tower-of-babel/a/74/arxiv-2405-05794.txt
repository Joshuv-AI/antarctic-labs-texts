Quantum vs. classical P P -divisibility
Fabio Benatti, Dariusz Chruściński, Giovanni Nichele

Abstract
P P -divisibility is a central concept in both classical and quantum non-Markovian processes; in particular, it is strictly related to the notion of information backflow. When restricted to a fixed commutative algebra generated by a complete set of orthogonal projections, any quantum dynamics naturally provides a classical stochastic process. It is indeed well known that a quantum generator gives rise to a P P -divisible quantum dynamics if and only if all its possible classical reductions give rise to divisible classical stochastic processes. Yet, this property does not hold if one operates a classical reduction of the quantum dynamical maps instead of their generators: as an example, for a unitary dynamics, P P -divisibility of its classical reduction is inevitably lost and the latter thus exhibits information backflow. Instead, for some important classes of purely dissipative qubit evolutions, quantum P P -divisibility always implies classical P P -divisibility and therefore excludes information backflow both in the quantum and classical scenarios. On the contrary, for a wide class of orthogonally covariant qubit dynamics, we show that loss of classical P P -divisibility originates from the classical reduction of a purely dissipative P P -divisible quantum dynamics as in the unitary case. Moreover, such an effect can be interpreted in terms of information backflow due to the coherences developed by the quantumly evolving classical state.



I
Introduction

In the following, we shall be concerned with
n
-level open quantum systems interacting with their environment in a way that cannot be approximated by
means of dynamical semigroups
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. The presence of memory effects is embodied
by one-parameter families of completely positive and trace-preserving (
CPTP
) maps

\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
that act on the convex space
\mathcal{S}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
of their positive and unit trace density matrices
\rho\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that
\Lambda_{t+s}\neq\Lambda_{t}\Lambda_{s}
.
We shall assume the dynamics
\rho\mapsto\rho(t)=\Lambda_{t}[\rho]
to consist of algebraically invertible maps generated by time-dependent generators
\mathcal{L}_{t}
on
\mathcal{S}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
, namely by master equations of the form

\dot{\Lambda}_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{t}\,\Lambda_{t}\ ,\quad\mathcal{L}_{t}:=\dot{\Lambda}_{t}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}\ ,

(1)

which are solved by the time-ordered exponentials

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{\leftarrow}e^{\int_{0}^{t}\differential{s}\,\mathcal{L}_{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\differential s_{1}\int_{0}^{s_{2}}\hskip-2.84544pt\differential s_{2}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{k-1}}\hskip-14.22636pt\differential s_{k}\,\mathcal{L}_{s_{1}}\cdots\mathcal{L}_{s_{k}}\ .

(2)

Because of the invertibility of the dynamics, the intertwining maps
\Lambda_{t,s}
connecting time
s
to time
t\geq s\geq 0
,

\Lambda_{t}=\Lambda_{t,s}\Lambda_{s}
, are given by

\Lambda_{t,s}:=\Lambda_{t}^{\phantom{.}}\,\Lambda_{s}^{-1}\ ,\quad t\geq s\geq 0\ .

(3)

Given
n^{2}
Hilbert-Schmidt orthonormal matrices
F_{k}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that
{\rm Tr}(F^{\dagger}_{j}F_{k})=\delta_{jk}
,
with
F_{0}=\mathds{1}/\sqrt{n}
, the generator can always be written as

\mathcal{L}_{t}[\rho]=-i[H(t),\rho]+\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho]\ ,

(4)

with
H(t)
being a time-dependent Hamiltonian and dissipator

\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho]=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n^{2}-1}K_{ij}(t)\left(F_{i}\rho F^{\dagger}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\{F^{\dagger}_{j}F_{i},\rho\}\right)\,,

(5)

with Hermitian, time-dependent
(n^{2}-1)\times(n^{2}-1)
Kossakowski matrix
K(t)=[K_{ij}(t)]=K^{\dagger}(t)
. According to the GKSL-theorem
[
4
,
5
]
, for time-independent generators, such that
\dot{\Lambda}_{t}=\mathcal{L}\Lambda_{t}
, the dynamics becomes a semi-group,

\Lambda_{t}={\rm e}^{t\mathcal{L}}\ ,\quad\Lambda_{t+s}=\Lambda_{t}\Lambda_{s},\qquad\Lambda_{t=0}=\mathrm{id}\quad\forall t,s\geq 0\ ,

(6)

and the complete positivity of the maps
\Lambda_{t}
is equivalent to the positive semi-definiteness of the time-independent Kossakowski matrix:

K(t)=K=[K_{jk}]\geq 0
.
Instead, in the time-dependent case there are no general constraints on
K(t)
characterizing the complete positivity of the generated
\Lambda_{t}
.
What certainly holds is that
K(t)\geq 0
is equivalent to the complete positivity of all the intertwiners
\Lambda_{t,s}

[
3
,
6
]
(and thus implies the complete positivity of
\Lambda_{t}
). In this case, the dynamics is called
CP
-divisible.
However, the complete positivity of
\Lambda_{t}
does not require
K(t)\geq 0
; indeed, completely positive
\Lambda_{t}
can very well be only
P
-divisible, namely with intertwiners
\Lambda_{t,s}
that are only positive and not completely positive.
Even when not semi-groups,
P
-divisible families of
CPTP
maps may be considered to be Markovian as they do not give rise to the phenomenon of backflow of information, which we will be referring to as
BFI
in the following, from the environment to the open quantum system immersed in it
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.
Indeed, Markovianity is nowadays identified with information being lost from an open system to its environment, leading to increasing lack of distinguishability of generic pairs of open system states in the course of time.

The classical analogue of a one-parameter family of quantum dynamical maps
is a one-parameter family of
n\times n
stochastic matrices
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, with entries
T_{ij}(t)
such that
T_{ij}(t)\geq 0
,
\sum_{i}T_{ij}(t)=1
.
These matrices transform probability vectors
\boldsymbol{p}=\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,
p_{i}\geq 0
,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}=1
, into themselves,
\boldsymbol{p}(t)=T(t)\boldsymbol{p}
.

In the following we shall study the properties inherited by the classical dynamics that naturally emerges from
P
-divisible
quantum dynamics. The classical dynamics will be obtained by restricting both the quantum dynamics and its tomographic reconstruction to a same (maximally) Abelian sub-algebra of
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
generated by any given choice of
n
orthogonal rank-
1
projections
P_{j}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that

P_{j}P_{k}=\delta_{jk}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{j}=\mathds{1}
. In the following, for sake of simplicity, we shall identify the commutative sub-algebra
\mathcal{P}
with the orthonormal, complete set
\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
that generates it and refer to the quantum dynamics restricted to it as its
classical reduction
.

In order to extract a classical dynamics from a quantum one, one can follow two prescriptions; one can either reduce the generator of the quantum evolution, or reduce the quantum dynamics itself. The latter reduction procedure is somewhat dual to the so-called embeddability
of a given classical stochastic matrix into a Markovian quantum evolution, that may give rise to some quantum advantage
[
10
,
11
]
.
The two different points of view yield different classical dynamics that are discussed in Section
II
.
In particular, regarding the property of
P
-divisibility, the following question naturally emerges:
Given a
P
-divisible dynamics
\{{\Lambda_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
, are their classical reductions
P
-divisible?

As we shall see, by reducing the generator, the answer to the previous question

is somewhat trivial, in that quantum
P
-divisibility is equivalent to classical
P
-divisibility. Conversely,
by reducing the dynamical map one
has the peculiar effect that a
P
-divisible quantum dynamics need not give rise to classically reduced divisible dynamics.

Such an issue is not only mathematically interesting; indeed, as we shall see, the loss of
P
-divisibility by the classical reduction (
7
) can be interpreted from a physical perspective, in terms of backflow of information.

In the same Section
II
, after presenting the general framework of classical reductions, we will illustrate the main results of the work which will be proved in Sections
III
-
VI
.

II
Classical reductions and results

As explained in the Introduction,
upon choosing a maximally Abelian subalgebra
\mathcal{P}
of
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
one may proceed along two different paths.
Namely, given the generator
\mathcal{L}_{t}
of the quantum dynamics
\Lambda_{t}
, one can consider the
n\times n
matrix
L(t)
with entries

L_{ij}(t)=\Tr(P_i\mathcal{L}_t[P_j])

(7)

and study the one-parameter family of
n\times n
stochastic matrices
D(t)
solving the classical master equation

\dot{D}(t)=L(t)\,D(t)\,.

(8)

Another possibility is to focus upon the classical reduction of the generated quantum dynamics
\Lambda_{t}
itself; namely, to restrict to the stochastic matrix
T(t)
with entries

T_{ij}(t)=\Tr(P_i\Lambda_t[P_j])]\ .

(9)

The physical interpretation of
T(t)
becomes clear by introducing the decohering map

\mathbb{P}[\rho]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}\rho P_{i}

(10)

which diagonalizes every state
\rho
along the orthonormal basis associated with the chosen complete set of orthonormal projections

\mathcal{P}=\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.
Then, the map
\Lambda^{\mathbb{P}}_{t}:=\mathbb{P}\,\Lambda_{t}\,\mathbb{P}
acts initially on diagonal matrices, turns them into non-diagonal ones at time
t
and the developed coherences are then suppressed by the left-most
\mathbb{P}
.
Explicitly, for all states
\rho
, one gets:

\Lambda^{\mathbb{P}}_{t}[\rho]=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}T_{jk}(t)\,\Tr(P_k\rho)\,P_{j}\ .

(11)

Practically speaking, in this approach, at each instant of time, the quantum dynamics is preceded and followed by two projective measurements specified by

\mathcal{P}
.

To compare the latter classical dynamics with the one obtained from (
7
), for sake of simplicity we restrict to the case when
the quantum dynamics
\Lambda_{t}
is a semigroup with time-independent generator
\mathcal{L}
:
\Lambda_{t}=e^{t\mathcal{L}}
.
It thus follows that
L(t)=L
in (
8
) and
D(t)=e^{t\,L}
so that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{P}}[\rho]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\,\mathcal{L}\,\mathbb{P}[\rho]=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}L_{jk}\,\Tr(P_k\,\rho)\,P_{j}\ ,

(12)

\displaystyle e^{t\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{P}}}\mathbb{P}[\rho]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}D_{jk}(t)\,\Tr(P_k\,\rho)\,P_{j}\ .

(13)

On the other hand, fix
t\geq 0
and consider the large
N
behaviour

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{P}\,\Lambda_{t/N}\,\mathbb{P}\right)^{N}\simeq\left(\mathds{1}+\frac{t}{N}\,\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{P}}\right)^{N}\mathbb{P}\simeq e^{t\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{P}}}\mathbb{P}\ .

(14)

Therefore, unlike when reducing the quantum evolution up to time
t
by an initial and a final projective measurements, the classical reduction of the quantum generator physically amounts to letting the quantum dynamics to act in between iterated projective measurements
separated by smaller and smaller time intervals.

Remark 1
.

Notice that

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{P}\,\Lambda_{\epsilon}\,\mathbb{P}\right)^{N}[\rho]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j_{N},\ldots,j_{1}}T_{ij_{N}}(\epsilon)\cdots T_{j_{2}j_{1}}(\epsilon)\,\Tr(\rho P_{j_1})\,P_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\left[\big(T(\epsilon)\big)^{N}\right]_{ij}\,\Tr(\rho P_j)\,P_{i}\ ,

where
\epsilon=t/N
. On the other hand, in the semi-group case, from (
7
) and (
9
), with
\mathcal{L}_{t}=\mathcal{L}
and
L(t)=L
, it follows that
T(\epsilon)\simeq\mathds{1}+\epsilon\,L\simeq D(\epsilon)
; therefore,

\lim_{N\to+\infty}T(t/N)^{N}=D(t)\neq T(t)\ .

The origin of the discrepancy is the fact that the family of matrices
T(t)
is not a semigroup; indeed, if
T(t)
is forced to be a semigroup, so that

T(t+\epsilon)=T(\epsilon)T(t)
, then one has

T(t)=D(t)
.

II.1
Results

We will mainly focus not upon classical reductions of generators, rather upon those of the generated quantum dynamics and illustrate
a number of results in this context. They can be summarized as follows.

First results, some of them already present in the literature, regard the connections between
P
-divisibility and classical and quantum stochastic processes. They are fundamental tools for what follows and are reported in Section
III
.

Other results concern an interesting physical scenario that involves a
P
-divisible quantum dynamics whose classical reduction is not
P
-divisible.
Indeed, the quantum dynamics does not exhibit
BFI
, while the classical one does.
In Section
IV
, by working in the so-called
BLP
framework
[
7
,
8
]
,
we show that classical
BFI
at a given instant of time is due to information being stored in the coherences of the quantum states at previous times.
These coherences are built up by the quantum dynamics as it generically maps out of the chosen commutative algebra used for the reduction.

Further results regard unital qubit dynamics, for which
P
-divisibility of both the quantum dynamics and its classical reduction are to some extent under control. In Section
V
, we show that loss of
P
-divisibility for the classical reduction
is typical of unitary evolutions, but not of purely dissipative (namely, whose generator is such that
\mathcal{L}_{t}=\mathcal{D}_{t}
, see (
4
)) and self-dual qubit dynamics. Indeed, for them we show that
P
-divisibility of the reduced classical stochastic processes is equivalent to that of the quantum map that originates it.

The last results concern a wider class of of orthogonally covariant qubit dynamics; in Section
VI
, using necessary and sufficient conditions for
P
and
CP
-divisibility in terms of the generators, we provide a fairly general construction of purely dissipative quantum dynamics whose classical reductions are not
P
-divisible.

III
Classical reductions and
P
-divisibility

In this Section, we discuss some fundamental preliminary results about classical reductions, first considering the case of time-independent positive maps and time independent-generators of positive semigroups before moving to the time-inhomogeneous case of
P
-divisible families.

III.1
Classical Reduction of
PTP
maps

As much as for one parameter families of dynamical maps in (
9
), given an orthonormal complete set of orthogonal rank-1 projections
\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
spanning a maximally Abelian algebra
\mathcal{P}
, any
PTP
quantum map
\Phi
on
\mathcal{S}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
defines a stochastic matrix through the entries

T_{ij}:=\Tr(P_i\Phi[P_j])\,,\qquad P_{i}P_{j}=\delta_{ij}P_{i}\,.

(15)

Indeed, the positivity of
\Phi
together with the completeness of the projections and trace-preservation yields
T_{ij}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{ij}=1
. The converse, though, is not true: even if the matrices
T
defined by
\Phi
through (
15
) are stochastic for
all maximally Abelian
\mathcal{P}
,
\Phi
need not be positive.
The reason is that the positivity of
\Phi
amounts to

\Tr(P\Phi[Q])\geq 0

(16)

for all one-dimensional, not necessarily orthogonal projections
P
and
Q
, while (
15
) restricts to orthogonal ones.

Example 1
.

Let
\Phi
be a Hermiticity and trace-preserving, unital qubit map on
\mathcal{S}(M_{2}(\mathbb{C}))
, namely
\Phi[\sigma_{0}]=\sigma_{0}
. Here
\sigma_{0}=\mathds{1}
denotes the identity
2\times 2
matrix which, together with the other Pauli matrices,
\sigma_{1,2,3}
, after normalization, constitutes a Hilbert-Schmidt basis:

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left(\frac{\sigma_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2}}\,\frac{\sigma_{\beta}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)=\delta_{\alpha\beta}
.
They provide a matrix representation of
\Phi
through the entries

\Phi_{\alpha\beta}:=\frac{1}{2}\Tr\left({\sigma_{\alpha}}\,\Phi\left[{\sigma_{\beta}}\right]\right)\ ,\quad\alpha=0,\dots,3\ .

(17)

where
\Phi_{00}=1
and
\Phi_{0i}=0
for
i=1,2,3
follows from trace preservation. Also, from unitality it follows that
\Phi_{i0}=0
; therefore, one concentrates upon the real matrix
\widetilde{\Phi}
with entries

\widetilde{\Phi}_{ij}:=\frac{1}{2}\Tr(\sigma_i\Phi[\sigma_j]),\quad i=1,2,3\,,

(18)

so that
\Phi[\sigma_{i}]=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\widetilde{\Phi}_{ji}\,\sigma_{j},
.
Let
P_{0}
,
P_{1}=\mathds{1}-P_{0}
arbitrary orthonormal projectors in
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
define the
2\times 2
matrix with entries
T_{ij}=\Tr(P_i\Phi[P_j])
. Since
\Phi
is unital,

T=\begin{pmatrix}T_{00}&1-T_{00}\\
1-T_{00}&T_{00}\end{pmatrix},

(19)

is a (bi-)stochastic matrix iff
0\leq T_{00}\leq 1
. In the Bloch representation,
P_{0}
is identified by means of a unit real vector
\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
P_{\boldsymbol{n}}=(\mathds{1}+\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma})/2
, where
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})
.
Then, the bistochasticity conditions read

\displaystyle 1\geq T_{00}=\Tr(P_0\Phi[P_0])=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\Phi}}{\boldsymbol{n}}\geq 0,

for all unit
\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Hence,
T
is stochastic for every choice of orthogonal projectors
P_{0}
and
\mathds{1}-P_{0}
iff

\ell(\widetilde{\Phi}):=\sup_{\norm{\boldsymbol{n}}=1}|\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\Phi}}{\boldsymbol{n}}|\leq 1.

(20)

Notice that, if
\widetilde{\Phi}
is non-symmetric,
\widetilde{\Phi}\neq\widetilde{\Phi}^{T}
, where
T
denotes transposition,
\ell(\widetilde{\Phi})
is in general smaller than the norm

\|\widetilde{\Phi}\|=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{m}\\
\norm{\boldsymbol{n}}=\norm{\boldsymbol{m}}=1\end{subarray}}|\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\Phi}}{\boldsymbol{m}}|\ .

(21)

On the other hand, the map is positive iff

\Tr(P_{\pm\boldsymbol{n}}\Phi[P_{\vb{m}}])=\frac{1}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\Phi}}{\boldsymbol{m}}\geq 0\,,

(22)

for all unit vectors
\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
; namely, iff
|\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\Phi}}{\boldsymbol{m}}|\leq 1
. Then,
\|\widetilde{\Phi}\|\leq 1
, since
\widetilde{\Phi}
is real.
If
\widetilde{\Phi}=\widetilde{\Phi}^{T}
, then
\|{\widetilde{\Phi}}\|=\ell(\widetilde{\Phi})
and
T
is stochastic for all
{P}_{0}
iff the map
\Phi
is positive.

Instead, let
\alpha\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
,
\lambda>\eta\geq\lambda\cos(\alpha)>0
; then,

\widetilde{\Phi}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\cos(\alpha)&-\lambda\sin(\alpha)&0\\
\lambda\sin(\alpha)&\lambda\cos(\alpha)&0\\
0&0&\eta\\
\end{pmatrix}\neq\widetilde{\Phi}^{T}

(23)

has singular values
\lambda
and
\eta
. Therefore,
\|{\widetilde{\Phi}}\|=\lambda
, while
|\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\Phi}}{\boldsymbol{n}}|=\lambda\cos(\alpha)+n_{3}^{2}\,(\eta-\lambda\cos(\alpha))
.
Then, choosing
\lambda=1+\varepsilon
and
\eta=\lambda\cos(\alpha)
, one has

\displaystyle\ell(\widetilde{\Phi})=\sup_{\norm{\boldsymbol{n}}=1}|\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\Phi}}{\boldsymbol{n}}|=(1+\varepsilon)\cos(\alpha)\leq 1<\|\widetilde{\Phi}\|.

for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. With such choice of parameters, the map
{\Phi}
is not positive, while
T_{ij}=\Tr(P_i\Phi[P_j])
are the entries of a well defined stochastic matrix, for all choices of orthonormal projectors
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
.

III.2
Classical from quantum semi-groups

For semigroups of quantum maps as in (
6
), trace preservation imposes
\Tr(\mathcal{L}[\rho])=0
for all
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
. Kossakowski
[
12
]
found necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mathcal{L}
to be the generator of a positive trace-preserving (
PTP
) semigroup
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

Theorem 1
.

A linear map
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{S}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
generates a semigroup of PTP maps
\{{\Lambda_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
iff

\Tr(Q\mathcal{L}[P])\geq 0\ ,\quad\Tr(\mathcal{L}[\mathds{\rho}])=0

(24)

for all pairs of projectors
P\perp Q
and all
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
.

A semigroup of
n\times n
stochastic matrices
T(t)=e^{t\,L}
with generating matrix
L=[L_{ij}]
naturally gives rise to a semigroup of operators on every commutative
algebra
\mathcal{P}
spanned by
\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
orthogonal projectors such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}=\mathds{1}
. Indeed, using the decohering map introduced in (
10
), one sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{P}}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}L_{ij}\Tr(\rho P_j)\,P_{i}\,,

and exponentiates obtaining a semigroup of maps
\Lambda^{\mathbb{P}}_{t}={\rm e}^{t\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{P}}}
on the algebra of
n\times n
matrices.
It turns out that

\Lambda_{t}^{\mathbb{P}}[\rho]=\sum_{ij}T_{ij}(t)\Tr(\rho P_j)P_{i}\ .

Then, reducing
\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{P}}
or
\Lambda_{t}^{\mathbb{P}}
to the algebra
\mathcal{P}
, one ends up with the same classical process

T(t)=e^{tL}
.
Thus, from Theorem
1

\displaystyle L_{ij}=\Tr(P_i\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{P}}[P_j])\geq 0\quad i\neq j

(25)

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{i}\Tr(P_i\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{P}}[P_j])=\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{ij}=0\ .

which are the so-called Kolmogorov conditions
[
6
,
13
]
, that are necessary and sufficient for an
n\times n
matrix
L
to generate a semigroup of stochastic matrices
{T(t)={e}^{tL}}
. This can be seen by expanding the semigroup
T(\epsilon)
for
\epsilon\ll 1
,
yielding

T_{ij}(\epsilon)=\delta_{ij}+\epsilon\,L_{ij}+O(\epsilon^{2})\,,

(26)

Necessity of (
25
) is straightforwardly deduced from (
26
), while sufficiency follows by invoking the semigroup property.

In conjunction with the previous observation, one derives the following natural consequence from Theorem
1
.

Corollary 1
.

A linear map
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{S}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
such that
\Tr(\mathcal{L}[\mathds{\rho}])=0
, generates a semigroup of
PTP

maps
\{{\Lambda_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
iff the
n\times n
matrix
L
with entries

L_{ij}=\Tr(P_i\mathcal{L}[P_j])

(27)

generates a semigroup of stochastic
n\times n
matrices, for any orthonormal, complete set
\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of rank-1 projectors.

Proof.

The “only if” part is trivial. For the “if” part, suppose that
L
in (
27
) generates a positive stochastic semigroup for all choices of
\{P_{i}\}_{i}
. This is equivalent to ask that conditions (
25
) are satisfied. Trace preservation readily follows since for any
\rho=\sum_{i}r_{i}R_{i}
,
R_{i}
being its eigenprojectors,
\Tr(\mathcal{L}[\rho])=\sum_{j}r_{j}\sum_{i}\Tr(R_i\mathcal{L}[R_j])=0
. On the other hand, given any pair of orthogonal projectors
P
and
Q
,
P\,Q=0
, let their spectral representation be
Q=\sum_{i\in I}q_{i}P_{i},P=\sum_{i\in J}p_{j}P_{i}
, with
I\cap J=0
, so that

\Tr(P\mathcal{L}[Q])=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}p_{j}\,q_{i}\Tr({P}_j\mathcal{L}[P_i])\geq 0\,,

which completes the proof.
∎

III.3
Classical from quantum P-divisible families

We now consider the case of time-dependent generators as in (
1
) and positive intertwiners as in (
3
), namely the case of
P
-divisible families of
CPTP
maps.
Theorem
1
generalizes as follows
[
8
,
6
]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{t}
be the generator of a
CPTP
dynamical map
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.
Then
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is
P
-divisible iff
\Tr(Q\mathcal{L}_t[P])\geq 0
, for all
t\geq 0
, and for any pair of mutually orthogonal projectors
P\perp Q
.

Proof.

The “only if” part follows straightforwardly from the positivity of
\Lambda_{t,s}
, since for all
s\geq 0

\Tr(Q\mathcal{L}_s[P])=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}{\Tr(Q\Lambda_{s+\epsilon,s}[P])}\geq 0.

(28)

For the “if” part, rewrite
\mathcal{T}_{\leftarrow}e^{\int_{s}^{t}\differential{s}\mathcal{L}_{s}}
by means of the time-splitting formula
[
3
]

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t,s}=\lim_{\max\limits_{k}\delta t_{k}\to 0}\prod_{k=n-1}^{0}e^{\delta t_{k}\,\mathcal{L}_{t_{k}}}\,,

(29)

with
t\equiv t_{n}\geq t_{n-1}\geq\dots\geq t_{0}\equiv s
and
\delta t_{k}\equiv t_{k+1}-t_{k}
.
Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq k\leq n-1
. Since
\Tr(Q\mathcal{L}_{t_k}[P])\geq 0
for all
P\perp Q
,
t\mapsto e^{({t-t_{k}})\mathcal{L}_{t_{k}}}
is a positive map for all
t\geq t_{k}
, by Theorem
1
.
Positivity of
\Lambda_{t,s}
follows from noting that the composition of positive maps is positive and that the set of positive maps is closed (for finite level systems, the closure is with respect to the norm topology on the space of linear maps of
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
onto itself)
[
14
]
.
∎

The classical analogue of the family of
P
-divisible
CPTP
maps is a one-parameter family of stochastic matrices such that

T(t)=T(t,s)T(s),

(30)

for all
t\geq s\geq 0
, where
T(t,t)=\mathds{1}
and
T(t,s)
is a stochastic matrix,
T_{ij}(t,s)\geq 0
,
\sum_{i}T_{ij}(t,s)=1
. The classical time-local master equation has the form

\dot{T}(t)=L(t)T(t).

(31)

The quantum rendering of classical processes within a quantum setting outlined in Section
III.2
leads to the following generalizations of the Kolmogorov
conditions (
25
) and of Corollary
1
.

Proposition 1
.

A matrix
L(t)
generates a classical
P
-divisible dynamics
T(t)
iff

L_{ij}(t)\geq 0\quad\forall\,i\neq j,\qquad\sum_{i}L_{ij}(t)=0.

(32)

If
T(t)
is an invertible matrix, writing
L(t)=\dot{T}(t)T(t)^{-1}
and
T(t,s)=T(t)T(s)^{-1}
provides a time dependent version of Corollary
1
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{t}
be the generator of a
CPTP
dynamical map
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.
Then
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is
P
-divisible iff the
n\times n
matrix defined by
L_{ij}(t)=\Tr(P_i\mathcal{L}_t[P_j])
generates a classical
P
-divisible dynamics for any orthonormal set of rank-1 projectors
\{P_{i}\}_{i}
.

Therefore, any quantum dynamics
\{{\Lambda_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
is
P
-divisible iff its generator defines classically
P
-divisible processes for any choice of orthonormal basis.

However, as discussed in Section
II
, one may derive a classical continuous stochastic process not from the quantum generator, but directly from the generated quantum maps, as in (
9
).
When this reduction procedure is adopted, the relation between
P
-divisibility of
\Lambda_{t}
and that of its classical reduction
T(t)
is non-trivial and, in particular, classical
P
-divisibility may be lost. The following Section
IV
is devoted to a general interpretation of such effect in terms of
BFI
. In the remainder of the paper, we will focus exclusively on classical processes of the type (
9
), analysing in detail instances when
P
-divisibility is either preserved or lost through the reduction procedure.

Remark 2
.

Notice that, from (
9
), when the matrices
T(t)
are invertible, the classical intertwiners are given by
T(t,s)=T(t)T(s)^{-1}
. In general, they have no simple connection to the quantum intertwiners
\Lambda_{t,s}=\Lambda_{t}\,\Lambda_{s}^{-1}
. A particular case is when the dynamics
\Lambda_{t}
is of the kind

\Lambda_{t,s}\,\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}\,\Lambda_{t,s}\,\mathbb{P},
with
\mathbb{P}
as in (
10
),
so that
one can identify a stochastic propagator as
T_{ik}(t,s)=\Tr(P_i\Lambda_{t,s}[P_k])
. Such maps are a subset of the so-called non-coherence-generating-and-detecting dynamics considered in
[
15
]
to characterize the classicality of a Markovian process.

IV
Coherence-assisted backflow of information

In the following, we consider a
P
-divisible quantum evolution
\Lambda_{t}
and its classical reduction obtained through (
9
) in order to connect the property of
P
-divisibility of the so-obtained classical process with the notion of information flows.

In the so-called
BLP
approach to quantum non-Markovianity
[
7
]
, the Holevo-Helstrom distinguishability is interpreted as “internal information” of a quantum system as embodied by two states
\rho
and
\sigma
under the dynamics
\Lambda_{t}
:

\mathcal{I}^{q}_{t}(\rho,\sigma;\mu)=\norm{\Lambda_t[\Delta_\mu(\rho,\sigma)]}_{1}\,,

(33)

where

\Delta_{\mu}(\rho,\sigma)=\mu\rho-(1-\mu)\sigma

(34)

is the so-called Helstrom matrix, with
\mu\in[0,1]
being the prior probability in preparing
\rho
and
\sigma
, so that (
33
) reduces for
\mu=1/2
to the standard trace distance

D(\rho,\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\norm{\rho-\sigma}_{1}\ .

(35)

In the classical setting as in Section
III.2
, the Helstrom matrix becomes
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})=\mu\boldsymbol{p}-(1-\mu)\boldsymbol{q}
, with
\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q}
probability vectors, and its trace norm reduces to the so-called Kolmogorov distance

\norm{\bs{\delta}_\mu(\bs{p},\bs{q})}_{\ell_{1}}=\sum_{i}|\mu p_{i}-(1-\mu)\,q_{i}|\,,

(36)

with respect to the
\ell_{1}
-norm
\norm{\bs{x}}_{\ell_{1}}=\sum_{i}\absolutevalue{x_i}
. Therefore, the internal information

\mathcal{I}^{cl}_{t}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)
of the classical reduction is given by

\mathcal{I}^{cl}_{t}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)=\norm{T(t) \bs{\delta}_\mu(\bs{p},\bs{q})}_{\ell_{1}}\ .

(37)

For invertible quantum dynamics,
P
-divisibility is known to be equivalent
[
16
]
to the monotonicity in time of
\mathcal{I}^{q}_{t}(\rho,\sigma;\mu)
,

\partial_{t}\norm{\Lambda_t[\Delta_\mu(\rho, \sigma)]}_{1}\leq 0\,,

(38)

for any choice of the initial Helstrom matrix (not true in the non invertible case
[
17
]
).
The typical interpretation of (
38
) is that information about the initial distinguishability of any pair of the system states is lost by the dissipative effect of the environment and never retrieved.
Similarly, for classical invertible dynamics,
P
-divisibility is equivalent to

\partial_{t}\norm{T(t)\boldsymbol{\delta}_\mu}_{\ell_{1}}\leq 0\,,

(39)

for all choices of
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We stress that lack of
P
-divisibility straightforwardly implies a violation of (
39
) only if
T(t)
is invertible.
Furthermore, in the usual open party scenario, a system is immersed in an environment and the compound state then evolves unitarily,
\rho_{SE}(t)=\mathcal{U}^{SE}_{t}[\rho\otimes\rho_{E}]
, so that the distinguishability of two system-environment states is constant and one defines accordingly an “external information” relative to two states
\rho_{S}
and
\sigma_{S}
of a quantum system
S
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{q}_{t}(\rho_{S},\sigma_{S};\mu)

\displaystyle=\norm{\mathcal{U}_t^{SE}[\Delta_{\mu}(\rho_S,\sigma_S)\otimes\rho_E]}_{1}-\mathcal{I}^{q}_{t}(\rho_{S},\sigma_{S};\mu)

\displaystyle=\norm{\Delta_{\mu}(\rho_S,\sigma_S)\otimes\rho_E}_{1}-\mathcal{I}^{q}_{t}(\rho_{S},\sigma_{S};\mu)\ .

Indeed, the trace-norm is invariant under unitary transformations, yielding

\dot{\mathcal{E}^{q}_{t}}(\rho_{S},\sigma_{S};\mu)=-\dot{\mathcal{I}^{q}_{t}}(\rho_{S},\sigma_{S};\mu)
. Thus,
an increase of the internal information leads necessarily to a decrease of the external one. In the
BLP
approach, such a recovery of distinguishability is interpreted as backflow of information from the environment to the system. Such an interpretation, which involves the degrees of freedom of the environment, is also supported by the observation
[
8
,
18
,
19
]
that the difference of internal quantum informations at different times,

\Delta\mathcal{I}^{q}_{t,s}(\rho_{S},\sigma_{S};\mu):=\mathcal{I}^{q}_{t}(\rho_{S},\sigma_{S};\mu)-\mathcal{I}^{q}_{s}(\rho_{S},\sigma_{S};\mu)\ ,

can be upper-bounded by means of trace distances (
35
) involving the marginal density matrices

\rho_{S}(t)=\Tr_{E}\rho_{SE}(t)
,
\rho_{E}(t)=\Tr_{S}\rho_{SE}(t)
. Namely,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{I}^{q}_{t,s}\leq

\displaystyle\,\,2\mu\,D(\rho_{SE}(s),\rho_{S}(s)\otimes\rho_{E}(s))

\displaystyle+2(1-\mu)D(\sigma_{SE}(s),\sigma_{S}(s)\otimes\sigma_{E}(s))\,

(40)

\displaystyle+2\min\{\mu,1-\mu\}D(\rho_{E}(s),\sigma_{E}(s))\ .

It follows that
\Delta\mathcal{I}^{q}_{t,s}(\rho_{S},\sigma_{S};\mu)>0
can only occur if system environment-correlations are present at time
s
or if the environment marginals differ at time
s
.

Let us now consider the decohering map
\mathbb{P}
in (
10
) and a Helstrom matrix
\Delta_{\mu}(\rho_{\boldsymbol{p}},\sigma_{\boldsymbol{q}})\in\mathcal{P}
with

\rho_{\boldsymbol{p}}=\sum_{i}p_{i}P_{i}
and
\sigma_{\boldsymbol{q}}=\sum_{i}q_{i}P_{i}
.

Although the chosen density matrices commute and are incoherent classical states with respect to
\mathcal{P}
, the dynamics
\Lambda_{t}
takes them out of the
commutative algebra
\mathcal{P}
and generates non-vanishing coherences, while the classical reduction to
\mathcal{P}
eliminates them. Notice that the classical internal information of the classical reduction to
\mathcal{P}
of the quantum dynamics reads

\mathcal{I}_{t}^{cl}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)=\norm{T(t)\boldsymbol{\delta}_\mu(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})}_{\ell_{1}}=\norm{\mathbb{P}\Lambda_t[\Delta_\mu(\rho_{\boldsymbol{p}},\sigma_{\boldsymbol{q}})]}_{1}\ .

(41)

Let us then introduce the difference between the quantum and classical internal information relative to two classical states:

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\mathcal{C}_{t}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)=\mathcal{I}^{q}_{t}(\rho_{\boldsymbol{p}},\rho_{\boldsymbol{q}};\mu)-\mathcal{I}^{cl}_{t}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)

\displaystyle=\norm{\Lambda_t[\Delta_\mu(\rho_{\boldsymbol{p}},\sigma_{\boldsymbol{q}})]}_{1}-\norm{\mathbb{P}\Lambda_t[\Delta_\mu(\rho_{\boldsymbol{p}},\sigma_{\boldsymbol{q}})]}_{1}\ .

(42)

Since
\mathbb{P}
is a contraction, the quantity
\mathcal{C}_{t}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)
cannot be negative; moreover, it allows to write the quantum internal information of two quantumly evolving classical states as

\mathcal{I}^{q}_{t}(\rho_{\boldsymbol{p}},\rho_{\boldsymbol{q}};\mu)=\mathcal{I}_{t}^{cl}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)+\mathcal{C}_{t}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)\ .

(43)

It is thus appropriate to name
\mathcal{C}_{t}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)

coherent internal information
. Then, the
P
-divisibility of
\Lambda_{t}
implies that
\mathcal{I}^{q}_{t}(\rho_{\boldsymbol{p}},\rho_{\boldsymbol{q}};\mu)

monotonically decreases in time, namely the information contained in the system leaks towards the environment and never comes back. Hence, for
t\geq s\geq 0
,

\mathcal{I}_{t}^{cl}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)+\mathcal{C}_{t}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)\leq\mathcal{I}_{s}^{cl}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)+\mathcal{C}_{s}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)\ .

(44)

Moreover, one can upper-bound the variation of the classical internal information between times
s
and
t\geq s
as (see Appendix
B
)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{I}_{t,s}^{cl}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu):=\mathcal{I}_{t}^{cl}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)-\mathcal{I}_{s}^{cl}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)

\displaystyle\hskip 22.76228pt\leq\,\mathcal{C}_{s}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)

\displaystyle\hskip 22.76228pt\leq\,\mu\,C_{\ell_{1}}(\Lambda_{s}[\rho_{\boldsymbol{p}}])\,+\,(1-\mu)\,C_{\ell_{1}}(\Lambda_{s}[\rho_{\boldsymbol{q}}])\,,

(45)

where

C_{\ell_{1}}
denotes the so-called
\ell_{1}
norm of coherence of a state
[
20
,
21
]
,

C_{\ell_{1}}(\rho):=\sum_{i\neq j}\absolutevalue{\rho_{ij}}\,,

(46)

with respect to the classical subalgebra
\mathcal{P}
. Therefore,
C_{\ell_{1}}(\Lambda_{s}[\rho_{\boldsymbol{p}}])
and
C_{\ell_{1}}(\Lambda_{s}[\rho_{\boldsymbol{q}}])
measure the amount of coherence produced by the dynamics at time
s
acting on the diagonal states
\rho_{\boldsymbol{p}}
and
\rho_{\boldsymbol{q}}
.

In analogy with (
40
), from (
45
), we can thus interpret the revival of the classical internal information,
\Delta\mathcal{I}_{t,s}^{cl}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q};\mu)>0
, between times
s
and
t\geq s
as a classical backflow of information, which can occur only if a certain degree of quantum coherence has been built up to time
s
in the quantumly evolving classical states. Hence, in the classical reduction of the quantum evolution of pairs of commuting quantum states, quantum coherences play an information storing role as the environment does in the quantum scenario, as emerges by comparing the r.h.s of (
45
) and (
40
).

V
Unital qubit dynamics

In this Section, we investigate the
P
-divisibility of classical reductions of the dynamical map obtained by (
9
). In particular, we will argue that the question of whether
P
-divisibility of the quantum process is inherited by its reduction becomes particularly relevant when one considers purely dissipative dynamics with generators not containing commutators with Hamiltonians (see (
4
)), which would provide non-divisible classical processes.
Moreover, we shall mostly focus on qubit unital dynamics for which the
P
-divisibility of both
\Lambda_{t}
and its classical reduction
T(t)
can be controlled.

Let
\Lambda_{t}
be a unital qubit dynamics,
\Lambda_{t}[\mathds{1}]=\mathds{1}
, and
P_{0}
,
P_{1}
two orthogonal projectors generating a maximally Abelian sub-algebra
\mathcal{P}\subset M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. As in Example
1
, the structure of the classical process
T(t)

obtained from
\Lambda_{t}
by means of (
9
) is determined only by
T_{00}(t)
:

T(t)=\begin{pmatrix}T_{00}(t)&1-T_{00}(t)\\
1-T_{00}(t)&T_{00}(t)\end{pmatrix},

(47)

where
T_{00}(t)=\Tr(P_0\Lambda_t[P_0])
. If
T_{00}(t)\neq\frac{1}{2}
,
T(t)
is invertible and one computes the classical generator as

L(t)=\dot{T}(t)T(t)^{-1}=\frac{\dot{T}_{00}(t)}{2T_{00}(t)-1}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{pmatrix},

(48)

Thus, from Proposition
1
,
L(t)
generates of a divisible classical process iff

f_{t}:=\frac{\dot{T}_{00}(t)}{2T_{00}(t)-1}\leq 0\qquad\forall t\geq 0\ .

(49)

As in Example
1
, we thus introduce the
3\times 3
matrix representation of
\Lambda_{t}

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{ij}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\Tr(\sigma_i\Lambda_t[\sigma_j]),

(50)

and analogously
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)
for
\mathcal{L}_{t}
, satisfying the master equation

\dot{\widetilde{\Lambda}}(t)=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)
,
whose formal solution is given by a time-ordered exponentiation (see (
2
)):

\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)=\mathcal{T}_{\leftarrow}e^{\int_{0}^{t}\differential{s}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(s)}.

(51)

Using the Bloch representation of Example
1
, one rewrites

T_{00}(t)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)}{\boldsymbol{n}},\ \dot{T}_{00}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)}{\boldsymbol{n}}\ .

Then, (
49
) reads

f_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)}{\boldsymbol{n}}}{\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)}{\boldsymbol{n}}}\leq 0\,,\qquad\forall t\geq 0\ .

(52)

Similarly,
\Lambda_{t}
is
P
-divisible iff

\Tr(P_0\mathcal{L}_t[P_1])=-\frac{1}{2}\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)}{\boldsymbol{n}}\geq 0\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\ ,

(53)

namely iff the symmetric part of the generator in the Bloch representation has negative eigenvalues

-(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{T}(t))\geq 0\,.

(54)

In the following, we consider the behaviour of
f_{t}
under two oppositely behaving dynamics.
The first one is a purely unitary qubit rotation which cannot give rise to a classically divisible process, while the second is that of a purely dissipative Pauli dynamics for which
P
-divisibility of the dynamics is equivalent to that of its classical reduction.

Example 2
.

Consider the qubit Hamiltonian
H=1/2\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}
,
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and the evolution
\mathcal{U}_{t}[\rho]=U_{t}\,\rho\,{U}^{\dagger}_{t}
,
U_{t}=e^{-itH}
.
The classical reduction of its generator
\mathcal{L}[\cdot]=-i[H\,,\cdot\,]=0
to any commutative sub-algebra
\mathcal{P}\subset M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
vanishes. Indeed,
\Tr(PHQ)=\Tr(PQH)=0
. However, the Bloch representation of
\mathcal{U}_{t}
acts as
3\times 3
rotation matrix

\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}(t)=e^{t\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}}
with generator

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\omega\,\begin{pmatrix}0&-r_{3}&r_{2}\\
r_{3}&0&-r_{1}\\
-r_{2}&r_{1}&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\ ,

(55)

where
\omega=\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|
and
\boldsymbol{r}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})/\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a unit vector.
Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{3}=-\omega^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\omega^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{T}
, one rewrites

\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}(t)=e^{t\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}}=\mathds{1}+\frac{\sin(\omega t)}{\omega}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}+\frac{1-\cos(\omega t)}{\omega^{2}}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{2}\ .

(56)

Also,
\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\wt{\mathcal{L}}}{\boldsymbol{n}}=0
,

\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\wt{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}{\boldsymbol{n}}=-\|\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\ket{\boldsymbol{n}}\|^{2}
.
Given a projector with Bloch vector
\boldsymbol{n}
, with
\theta
the angle between the latter and
\boldsymbol{r}
, one has
\|\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\ket{\boldsymbol{n}}\|=\omega\sin(\theta)
and

\displaystyle T_{00}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr\Big(P_0\,\mathcal{U}_t[P_0]\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\wt{\mathcal{U}_t}}{\boldsymbol{n}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1+\cos^{2}(\theta)+\cos(\omega t)\sin^{2}(\theta))\ ,

so that (
52
) is rewritten as follows,

f_{t}=-\frac{\omega}{2}\frac{\sin(\omega t)\sin^{2}(\theta)}{\cos^{2}(\theta)+\cos(\omega t)\sin^{2}(\theta)}\ .

(57)

Thus, the sign of
f_{t}
changes for all
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, unless
\theta=0,\pi
(when
\ket{\boldsymbol{n}}
is an eigenstate of
H
) yielding
f_{t}=0
.
Notice that the denominator in
f_{t}
is the determinant of the classical stochastic matrix
T(t)
which is thus
invertible if

\cos(2\theta)>0
, namely, if
\theta\in(0,\pi/4)\cup(3\pi/4,\pi)
. In this case, classical
P
-divisibility breaks when
\dot{T}_{00}(t)
becomes positive.
As described in Section
IV
, such loss of
P
-divisibility for an invertible classical reduction
T(t)
can be interpreted in terms of
BFI
.

Example 3
.

Let
\Lambda_{t}
be the Pauli dynamics generated by

\mathcal{L}_{t}[\rho]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\gamma_{k}(t)(\sigma_{k}\rho\sigma_{k}-\rho).

(58)

Notice that
{[\mathcal{L}_{t},\mathcal{L}_{s}]=0}
, yielding the exponential solution
\Lambda_{t}=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\differential{s}\mathcal{L}_{s}}
. Moreover, the representation in the Pauli representation of the generator is diagonal,

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}(t)[\rho]=-\Gamma_{i}(t)\,\delta_{ij}\,,\qquad\Gamma_{i}(t)=\sum_{k\neq i}\gamma_{k}(t)\,,

(59)

and necessary and sufficient for
P
-divisibility follow from (
54
),

\Gamma_{k}(t)\geq 0\,,\quad k=1,2,3\,.

(60)

\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)
is also diagonal, with strictly positive eigenvalues

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{ij}(t)=\lambda_{i}(t)\delta_{ij}\,,\qquad\lambda_{i}(t)=e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\differential{s}\Gamma_{i}(t)}\ .

so that

\displaystyle f_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)}{\boldsymbol{n}}}{\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)}{\boldsymbol{n}}}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sum_{i}\Gamma_{i}(t)\lambda_{i}(t)n_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}(t)n_{j}^{2}}\leq 0

(61)

for all
\boldsymbol{n}
.
Thus,
T(t)
is
P
-divisible for all choices of the reference basis iff
\Lambda_{t}
is
P
-divisible.

Notice that, for
\gamma_{i}(t)=const
, the Pauli generator includes the case of a unital purely dissipative GKSL evolution (the time-independent version of (
66
)), which thus yields
P
-divisible classically reduced dynamics.

Example 4
.

Adding a Hamiltonian to a Pauli generator
\mathcal{L}_{t}^{P}
as in (
58
) may generally lead to a positive
f_{t}
, so that the process
T(t)
is not divisible. Indeed, consider

\mathcal{L}_{t}[\rho]=-i[\sigma_{z},\rho]+\mathcal{L}_{t}^{P}[\rho],

(62)

and take, for the sake of simplicity,
\Gamma_{1}(t)=\Gamma_{2}(t)\equiv\Gamma(t)
, so that the Hamiltonian generator commutes with the Pauli one. Hence, the evolution will have the form

\Lambda_{t}=\mathcal{U}_{t}\,\Lambda_{t}^{P}\implies\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)=\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}(t)\,\widetilde{\Lambda}^{P}(t)\,.

(63)

Using (
52
) and (
56
), it follows that

f_{t}=f_{t}^{P}+f_{t}^{H}
,
where
f_{t}^{P}
is as in (
61
) and always negative, while
f_{t}^{H}
is as in (
57
) and generally oscillates between positive and negative values. In particular,
P
-divisibility is lost due to the divergence of the
f_{t}^{H}
for some
\boldsymbol{n}
. On the other hand, for those
\boldsymbol{n}
that lead to an invertible
T(t)
,
f_{t}^{H}
is bounded, so that
f_{t}
can stay negative for all times for large enough Pauli rates
\Gamma_{i}(t)
.

The previous examples suggest that the presence of the commutator in the generator is in general responsible for the loss of
P
-divisibility for the classically reduced process defined by (
9
), while the classical reduction of
Pauli dynamics preserves
P
-divisibility.
Therefore, in order to study which
P
-divisible quantum dynamics
\Lambda_{t}
give certainly rise to classically divisible processes defined by (
9
), one better focus upon purely dissipative generators.

From (
4
), it follows that the time-local generator of a trace and Hermiticity preserving, qubit dynamics can be always expressed in the form
[
4
]

\mathcal{L}_{t}[\rho]=-i[H(t),\rho]+\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho],

(64)

with dissipative part

\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}K_{ij}(t)\left(\sigma_{i}\rho\sigma_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\anticommutator{\sigma_j\,\sigma_i}{\rho}\right)\ .

(65)

For a unital qubit dynamics
the following then holds.

Lemma 1
.

A qubit dynamics generated by
\mathcal{L}_{t}
is unital iff the Kossakowski matrix
K(t)
is real and symmetric, so that
\mathcal{D}_{t}
can be written in the diagonal form

\mathcal{D}_{t}[\rho]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}(t)\left(\sigma_{i}(t)\rho\sigma_{i}(t)-\rho\right),

(66)

with respect to a set of matrices (generally time dependent)
\{\sigma_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{3}
that fulfil the Pauli algebra. Moreover, the unital dynamics is purely dissipative, namely
\mathcal{L}_{t}=\mathcal{D}_{t}
, iff the time-independent Bloch-representation of the generator

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\Tr(\sigma_i\mathcal{L}_t[\sigma_j]),\quad i,j=1,2,3\ ,

(67)

consists of a symmetric matrix.

Proof.

Let us recall that
\Lambda_{t}
is unital if and only if
\mathcal{L}_{t}[\mathds{1}]=0
for all
t\geq 0
. Indeed, if
\Lambda_{t}
is unital,
0=\dot{\Lambda}_{t}[\mathds{1}]=\mathcal{L}_{t}\Lambda_{t}[\mathds{1}]=\mathcal{L}_{t}[\mathds{1}]
; conversely, if the generator kills the identity at all times, unitality follows from the Dyson series (
2
). Hence, in the qubit case, plugging the identity into (
65
)

\Lambda_{t}
is unital iff

\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}K_{ij}(t)[\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j}]=0,

(68)

which is satisfied iff
K_{ij}(t)=K_{ji}(t)={K_{ij}^{*}}(t)
. Then, the real symmetric Kossakowski matrix
K(t)
can be diagonalized by means of an orthogonal transformation,

K(t)=O(t)\gamma(t)O(t)^{T}\ ,\quad\gamma(t)=\textrm{diag}\{\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t),\gamma_{3}(t)\}\ .

Therefore,
O(t)O^{T}(t)=O^{T}(t)O(t)=\mathds{1}
guarantees that the matrices
\sigma_{i}(t)=\sum_{j}O_{ji}(t)\sigma_{j}
satisfy the Pauli algebra.
Moreover, the Bloch representation (
67
) of the dissipative part
\mathcal{D}_{t}
of the generator reads

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{ij}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}(t)\left[\Tr\left(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{k}(t)\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}(t)\right)-2\delta_{ij}\right]

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{ji}(t)\ ,

(69)

where the last equality follows from the cyclicity property of the trace. Thus, an anti-symmetric contribution to the matrix representation of the full generator
\mathcal{L}(t)
can only come from the commutator with the Hamiltonian. It follows that the unital dynamics is purely dissipative, namely that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)=\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}(t)
, iff
{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{T}(t)}
.
∎

Remark 3
.

The dual,
\Lambda^{\ddagger}
, of a state-transformation
\Lambda
is defined as

\Tr(X\Lambda[\rho])=\Tr(\Lambda^\ddagger[X]\rho),

for all system states
\rho
and system operators
X
. Since
\Lambda
is trace-preserving
\Lambda^{\ddagger}
is unital

\Lambda^{\ddagger}[\mathds{1}]=\mathds{1}
. A state-transformation
\Lambda
is self-dual iff
\Lambda=\Lambda^{\ddagger}
so that
it is necessarily unital, while the reverse is obviously not true.
When
\Lambda_{t}
is a qubit dynamical map, going to the Bloch representation, one then has that self-duality is equivalent to

\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)=\widetilde{\Lambda}^{T}(t)
. On the other hand, if the generator
\mathcal{L}_{t}
of the qubit dynamics
\Lambda_{t}

is self-dual,
\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\phantom{\ddagger}}=\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\ddagger}
, it must be purely dissipative,
\mathcal{L}_{t}=\mathcal{D}_{t}
, and
\Lambda_{t}
unital.
Indeed,
\mathcal{L}_{t}[\mathds{1}]=\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\ddagger}[\mathds{1}]=0
yields a symmetric Kossakowski matrix which in turn implies
the absence of the commutator with a Hamiltonian. The unitality of
\Lambda_{t}
then follows from its Dyson expansion.
Moreover, in the qubit case, Lemma
1
implies that a purely dissipative generator of a unital dynamics is necessarily self-dual. In the following Section
VI
, we shall provide a concrete qubit construction for a non self-dual unital dynamics arising from a self-dual generator. In particular, this cannot occur if the solution is of the form
\Lambda_{t}=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\differential{s}\mathcal{L}_{s}}
.

It follows that if
\Lambda_{t}
is unital and purely dissipative,
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)
is real symmetric and then, from (
54
), the
P
-divisibility of
\Lambda_{t}
becomes equivalent to

-\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)\geq 0
. The result for Pauli dynamics can be then generalized to self-dual qubit dynamics with self-dual generators, for which
P
-divisibility is preserved by their classical reduction.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\Lambda_{t}^{\phantom{\ddagger}}=\Lambda_{t}^{\ddagger}
be a self-dual, purely dissipative, invertible qubit dynamics.
Then, the associated classical stochastic process
T(t)
is divisible if and only if
\Lambda_{t}
is
P
-divisible.

Proof.

One has to check when (
52
) holds. Since

\Lambda_{t}^{\phantom{\ddagger}}=\Lambda_{t}^{\ddagger}
, this implies that their Bloch representations satisfy

\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)=\widetilde{\Lambda}^{T}(t)\,.

(70)

Also, the assumed pure dissipativeness of
\Lambda_{t}
entails that the generator itself is self-dual,
\mathcal{L}_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\ddagger}
, so that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{T}(t)=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)
(see Lemma
1
).
Then, taking the time derivative of both members of (
70
),
one gets

\dot{\widetilde{\Lambda}}(t)=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)=\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)\ ,

(71)

or, equivalently,
[{\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)},{\dot{\widetilde{\Lambda}}(t)}]=0
. The invertibility of
\Lambda_{t}
means that none of the real eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)=\widetilde{\Lambda}^{T}(t)
can change sign with varying
t
. Since, at
t=0
,
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{t}=\mathds{1}
all of them must remain positive. Therefore, one can consistently express the generator as a logarithmic derivative:

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)=\dot{\widetilde{\Lambda}}(t)\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)^{-1}=\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)^{-1}\dot{\widetilde{\Lambda}}(t)=\derivative{t}\log\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)\ .

(72)

Thus,
\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\differential{s}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(s)}
without time-ordering; furthermore,

f_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\sqrt{\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)}(-\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t))\sqrt{\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)}}{\boldsymbol{n}}}{\matrixelement{\boldsymbol{n}}{\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)}{\boldsymbol{n}}}\leq 0\,.

(73)

The proof is completed by observing that, because of (
54
), the dynamics
\Lambda_{t}
is
P
-divisible iff
-\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)\geq 0
.
∎

Remark 4
.

The assumptions in Proposition
2
lead to a proper exponential solution
\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\differential{s}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(s)}
without asking for commuting generators at different times as in Example
3
. Indeed, a sufficient condition for the exponential to solve

\dot{\widetilde{\Lambda}}(t)=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)
is that
\Big[\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)\,,\,\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\,\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(s)\Big]=0
for all
t\geq 0
(notice that, from the power series of the exponential, (
71
) also follows).
As an example, consider a generator of a unital dynamics of the form

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)=\left\{\begin{matrix}\begin{pmatrix}-1&\cos t&0\cr\cos t&-1&0\cr 0&0&-2\cr\end{pmatrix}&\quad 0\leq t\leq\pi\cr-\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\cr 0&2&0\cr 0&0&1\end{pmatrix}&\quad\pi\leq t\end{matrix}\right.\ .

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(s)
do not commute when
0\leq s\leq\pi
and
t>\pi
, while they do when either
0\leq s,t\leq\pi
or
s,t>\pi
; on the other hand, since

\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\,\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}-t&\sin t&0\cr\sin t&-t&0\cr 0&0&-2t\end{pmatrix}

when
0\leq t\leq\pi
and, when
t\geq\pi
,

\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\,\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(s)=-\pi\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\cr 0&1&0\cr 0&0&2\end{pmatrix}-(t-\pi)\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\cr 0&2&0\cr 0&0&1\end{pmatrix}

the condition
\Big[\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t),\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\,\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(s)\Big]=0
, for all
t\geq 0
, that avoids time-ordering is fulfilled. Moreover,
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)
is symmetric (self-dual generator), so that the dynamics is purely dissipative. The exponential solution then entails the self-duality of the map.
-\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(t)\geq 0
finally assures
P
-divisibility of the generated dynamics, so that all the hypothesis of Proposition
2
are matched.

VI
A class of orthogonally covariant dynamics

In the following, we study whether the
P
-divisibility of a quantum qubit dynamics
\Lambda_{t}
is inherited by its classical reductions
T(t)
for non self-dual, purely dissipative dynamics.
We focus upon the following family of maps,

\displaystyle\Phi^{({A,\lambda,\mu})}[\rho]=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{1}A_{ij}E_{ij}\rho E_{ji}+\lambda E_{00}\rho E_{11}+\overline{\lambda}\,E_{11}\rho E_{00}

\displaystyle+\mu\,E_{11}\rho^{T}E_{00}+\overline{\mu}\,E_{00}\rho^{T}E_{11}\,,

(74)

where
E_{ij}=\outerproduct{i}{j}
are the matrix units associated with the basis of eigenvectors of
\sigma_{3}
,
\sigma_{3}\ket{0}=\ket{0},\sigma_{3}\ket{1}=-\ket{1}
. The maps are not in general of Pauli type and
depend parametrically on a triplet
(A,\lambda,\mu)
, given by a
2\times 2
matrix
{A=[A_{ij}]}
and coefficients
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C}
.

They were studied in the
d
-dimensional case in
[
22
]
. In particular, they satisfy the following group composition law,

\Phi^{({A,\lambda,\mu})}\Phi^{({A^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}})}=\Phi^{({AA^{\prime},\,\lambda\lambda^{\prime}+\mu\overline{\mu^{\prime}},\,\lambda\mu+\mu\overline{\lambda^{\prime}}})}\ .

(75)

Moreover, if
\mu=0
, the maps
\Phi^{(A,\lambda,0)}
satisfy the diagonal unitary-covariance property

\Phi^{(A,\lambda,0)}[UXU^{\dagger}]=U\Phi^{(A,\lambda,0)}[X]U^{\dagger}\ ,\quad U=\sum_{i}e^{i\theta_{i}}E_{ii}\ .

Conversely, if
\lambda=0
, the maps
\Phi^{(A,0,\mu)}
are conjugate diagonal unitary-covariant, namely

\Phi^{(A,0,\mu)}[UXU^{\dagger}]=\overline{U}\Phi^{(A,0,\mu)}[X]U^{T}\ .

Lastly, if both
\lambda,\mu
are different from zero, the only symmetry left is with respect to rotations around the
z
axis, corresponding to the diagonal orthogonal covariance

\Phi^{(A,\lambda,\mu)}[OXO^{T}]=O\Phi^{(A,\lambda,\mu)}[X]O^{T}\ ,

where
O=\sum_{i}o_{i}E_{ii}
,
o_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
.

Consider a time-dependent family of hermiticity and trace-preserving maps within the above class,

\Lambda_{t}=\Phi^{(A(t),\lambda_{t},\mu_{t})}
. Then,
A(t)
is to be taken real and of the form

A_{ij}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sum_{i}A_{ij}(t)=1
, so that

A(t)=\begin{pmatrix}a_{t}&1-b_{t}\\
1-a_{t}&b_{t}\end{pmatrix}\ ,\quad a_{t},b_{t}\in\mathbb{R}\ .

(76)

Asking that
\Lambda_{t=0}=\mathrm{id}_{2}
yields
\lambda_{0}=1,\mu_{0}=0
and
a_{0}=1
; whereas positivity can be proved to be equivalent to
[
22
]

\displaystyle A_{ij}(t)\geq 0\,,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\lambda_t}+\absolutevalue{\mu_t}\leq\sqrt{a_{t}b_{t}}+\sqrt{(1-a_{t})(1-b_{t})}\,.

(77)

so that
A(t)
has to be a stochastic matrix. On the other hand, the conditions for complete positivity can be obtained by imposing positivity of the associated Choi matrix,

\Lambda_{t}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{2}\bigg[\sum_{ij}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ij}\bigg]\geq 0\,,

(78)

leading to the stronger necessary and sufficient conditions

\absolutevalue{\lambda_t}\leq\sqrt{a_{t}b_{t}}\,,\quad\absolutevalue{\mu_t}\leq\sqrt{(1-a_{t})(1-b_{t})}\,.

(79)

Recently, Schwartz-positivity for maps within this class has also been fully characterized
[
23
]
. Sufficient conditions for complete positivity in terms of the generator for this class of maps were also investigated in
[
24
]
.
We shall now characterize the divisibility properties of the dynamics within class (
74
). Due to the composition property (
75
), the generator
{\mathcal{L}_{t}=\dot{\Lambda}^{\phantom{.}}_{t}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}}
will itself belong to the class (
74
),
{\mathcal{L}_{t}=\Phi^{(B(t),\ell_{t},m_{t})}}
, with
B(t)
of the form

B(t)=\dot{A}(t)A(t)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}-\gamma_{-}(t)&\gamma_{+}(t)\\
\gamma_{-}(t)&-\gamma_{+}(t)\end{pmatrix}

(80)

where
\gamma_{-}(t)
,
\gamma_{+}(t)
are related to
a_{t}
,
b_{t}
via

\gamma_{-}(t)=-\frac{\dot{a}_{t}(1-b_{t})+\dot{b}_{t}a_{t}}{a_{t}+b_{t}-1},\ \ \gamma_{+}(t)=-\frac{\dot{a}_{t}b_{t}+\dot{b}_{t}(1-a_{t})}{a_{t}+b_{t}-1},

and
l_{t}
,
m_{t}
are related to
\lambda_{t}
,
\mu_{t}
via

\displaystyle l_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\dot{\lambda}_{t}\overline{\lambda_{t}}-\dot{\mu}_{t}\overline{\mu_{t}}}{\absolutevalue{\lambda_t}^{2}-\absolutevalue{\mu_t}^{2}}\,,\qquad m_{t}=\frac{\dot{\mu}_{t}\lambda_{t}-\dot{\lambda}_{t}{\mu}_{t}}{\absolutevalue{\lambda_t}^{2}-\absolutevalue{\mu_t}^{2}}\,.

(81)

It is convenient to introduce time-dependent “transversal” and “longitudinal” rates,

\Gamma_{T}(t):=-\real(l_{t})\,,\qquad\Gamma_{L}(t):={\gamma_{+}(t)+\gamma_{-}(t)}\,.

(82)

and let also
\omega(t):=-\imaginary(l_{t})
.
Notice that
\Gamma_{T}(t)
only depends on the absolute values of
\lambda_{t}
and
\mu_{t}
,

\Gamma_{T}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial_{t}\absolutevalue{\lambda_t}^{2}-\partial_{t}\absolutevalue{\mu_t}^{2}}{|\lambda_{t}|^{2}-|\mu_{t}|^{2}}\,.

(83)

Necessary and sufficient conditions for
P
and
CP
-divisibility of the dynamics can be then determined from the generator.

Proposition 3
.

Let
{\mathcal{L}_{t}=\Phi^{(B(t),l_{t},m_{t})}}
be the generator of an evolution
\Lambda_{t}=\Phi^{(A(t),\lambda_{t},\mu_{t})}
. Then,

•

\Lambda_{t}
is
P
-divisible iff

\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}(t)\geq 0\,,

(84)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{T}(t)-\frac{\Gamma_{L}(t)}{2}+\sqrt{\gamma_{+}(t)\gamma_{-}(t)}\geq\absolutevalue{m_t}\,;

(85)

•

\Lambda_{t}
is
CP
-divisible iff

\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}(t)\geq 0\,,\,\qquad{\gamma_{+}(t)\gamma_{-}(t)}\geq\absolutevalue{m_t}^{2}\,,

(86)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{T}(t)\geq\frac{\Gamma_{L}(t)}{2}\ .

(87)

Though in a different form, this result has already been obtained in
[
25
]
; in Appendix
A
we report the proof since it is somewhat simpler that in the quoted reference. Notice that condition (
87
) is the time-dependent version of the celebrated constraint between relaxation rates already discussed in seminal paper
[
4
]
, which has been proved to be universal for qubit completely positive semigroups
[
26
]
, and recently conjectured to be universal for all completely positive dynamical semigroups
[
27
]
.

Remark 5
.

The Bloch representation of
\Lambda_{t}
as in (

\boldsymbol{n}_{t}=\widetilde{\Lambda}(t)\boldsymbol{n}+\boldsymbol{u}_{t}\,,\qquad\boldsymbol{u}_{t}=(0,0,a_{t}-b_{t})\,,

\end{pmatrix}.

The block diagonal structure of

(89)

Furthermore, letting

,

, with Kossakowski matrix

Notice that

-divisibility conditions can be then read off asking for

.
If
\Lambda_{t}
is also unital,

has to be bistochastic, thus

and

so that the

(90)

Indeed, the generator

will also be Bloch diagonal, and the conditions (

) follow from

.
On the other hand, the conditions for

(91)

VI.1
Non-self dual dynamics from self-dual generator.

As already stressed, generators of dynamical maps in the class (
74
) are also in the same class; moreover, we shall focus upon purely dissipative generator; for qubit unital dynamics, this amounts to requiring that
\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\phantom{\ddagger}}=\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\ddagger}
(see Lemma
1
). From the general form (
74
), the dual of
\Phi^{(A,\lambda,\mu)}
acts on a matrix

as

\displaystyle(\Phi^{(A,\lambda,\mu)})^{\ddagger}[X]=

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}A_{ji}E_{ij}XE_{ji}+\overline{\lambda}E_{00}XE_{11}

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt+\lambda E_{11}XE_{00}+\mu E_{00}X^{T}E_{11}+\overline{\mu}E_{11}X^{T}E_{00}\ .

Then, self-duality corresponds to
A=A^{T}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

Therefore, a generator

(92)

Let

Notice that this fixes

We now provide a construction for a

-divisible, non self-dual dynamics arising from a self-dual, purely dissipative generator. Let us define

g_{t}:=\absolutevalue{\lambda_t}+\absolutevalue{\mu_t}\,,\qquad h_{t}:=\absolutevalue{\lambda_t}-\absolutevalue{\mu_t}\,,

with

\Gamma_{T}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial_{t}(g_{t}h_{t})}{g_{t}h_{t}}=-\frac{\dot{g}_{t}}{2g_{t}}-\frac{\dot{h}_{t}}{2h_{t}}\,,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{m_t}=\absolutevalue{\frac{\dot g_t}{2g_t}-\frac{\dot h_t}{2h_t}+ i \,\dot\theta_t\, \frac{g_t-h_t}{g_t+h_t}}\,.

VII
Conclusions

Generic quantum dynamics restricted to commutative algebras of orthogonal projections generate quantum coherences among them.
With respect to that commutative algebra, by restricting to the diagonal components of its time-evolving orthogonal projections, one obtains a so-called classical reduction of the quantum dynamics, of course depending on the chosen set of orthogonal projections.
In the manuscript, we have studied whether and how the divisibility properties of time-dependent, non-Markovian qubit quantum dynamics are inherited by their classical reductions. We have done it by classically reducing the dynamics and by then studying the generator of the classical reduction.

In Section
IV
, we have argued that the lack of
P
-divisibility in classical reductions can be ascribed to information created by the quantum dynamics which is stored in the quantum coherences and then released back into the classical component of the dynamics.
Furthermore, for
P
-divisible and unital qubit dynamics we showed that 1) the Hamiltonian contributions to the time-dependent quantum generators generally give rise to qubit quantum dynamics with non
P
-divisible classical reductions; 2) purely dissipative, self-dual dynamics
always have
P
-divisible classical reductions while 3) purely dissipative, non self-dual dynamics may give rise to non
P
-divisible classical reductions.

Though this behaviour is somewhat typical of unitary quantum dynamics, yet it can also emerge from purely dissipative quantum evolutions due to the presence of a non-trivially time-ordered Dyson expansion.

The Dirichlet problem for Monge-Ampère type equations on Hermitian manifolds
Weisong Dong

Abstract
. In this paper, we solve the Dirichlet problem for Monge-Ampère type equations for ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -plurisubharmonic functions on Hermitian manifolds. Keywords: Monge-Ampère equations; Second order estimates; Hermitian manifolds.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact complex manifold of complex dimension
n\geq 2
with Hermitian metric
\omega
.
Let
\chi(z)
be a smooth real (1,1) form on
M

and
\psi(z)\in C^{\infty}(M)
be a positive function.
Given any smooth function
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
the operator
\mathcal{M}_{p}^{n}[u]
is defined as below

\mathcal{M}_{p}^{n}(u)=\Pi_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq n}(\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p}}),

where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
are the eigenvalues of
g:=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u
with respect to

\omega
.
We also simply write
\mathcal{M}(u)
when there is no ambiguity.
In this paper, we consider the following Monge-Ampère type equation

(1.1)

\mathcal{M}_{p}^{n}(u)=\psi\;\mbox{in}\;M,

with Dirichlet boundary data

(1.2)

u=\varphi\;\mbox{on}\;\partial M,

where
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial M,\mathbb{R})
is a given function.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
p\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
. The cone
\mathcal{P}_{p}
is a subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with element

(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
such that
\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p}}>0

for all
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq n
.
The cone of
n\times n
Hermitian matrices
P_{p}
is defined as:

A\in P_{p}
if the
n
-tuple of it’s eigenvalues is in
\mathcal{P}_{p}
.
We call
A
is
p
-positive if
A\in P_{p}
.

For any smooth real
(1,1)
-forms
h
on
(M,\omega)
,
written in local coordinates as
h=\sqrt{-1}h_{i\bar{j}}dz^{i}\wedge dz^{\bar{j}}
,
we say that

h\in P_{p}(M)

if the vector of eigenvalues of the Hermitian endomorphism
{h^{i}}_{j}=\omega^{i\bar{k}}h_{j\bar{k}}
lies in the

\mathcal{P}_{p}
cone at each point of
M
.
A function
u\in C^{2}(M)
is called admissible if
\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u\in P_{p}(M)
.
In this paper, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold with boundary.
Let
\chi\in P_{n-1}(M)
be a smooth
(1,1)
form.
Assume that there exists an admissible subsolution
\underline{u}\in C^{\infty}(M)
, that is

\mathcal{M}_{n-1}^{n}(\underline{u})\geq\psi\;\mbox{in}\;M\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\underline{u}=\varphi\;\mbox{on}\;\partial M.

Then there exists a unique admissible solution
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) for
p=n-1
.

The equation (
1.1
) with
p=n-1
is of special interest, which is closely related to the Gaudochon conjecture,
the form-type Calabi-Yau equation and the

n-1
plurisubharmonic functions studied by Harvey-Lawson
[
29
,
30
]
.
Since Yau in
[
49
]
proved the existence of a solution to the complex Monge-Ampère equation
on Kähler manifolds, that corresponds to the end point
p=1
in (
1.1
),
there have been many extensions and generalizations of Yau’s theorem.
Tian-Yau was concerned with the existence of complete Ricci-flat Kähler metrics on quasiprojective varieties in
[
44
,
45
]
.
Gaudochon conjecture
[
21
]
is a natural generalization of the Calabi-Yau theorem from Käkler to Hermitian manifolds
and was solved by Székelyhidi-Tosatti-Weinkove
[
43
]
recently. See also Guan-Nie
[
25
]
.
The form-type Calaby-Yau equation was first introduced by Fu-Wang-Wu
[
18
]
and they solved the
equation with the assumption that the metric has non-negative orthogonal bisectional curvature in
[
19
]
.
This assumption was removed in the work of Tosatti-Weinkove
[
48
]
.
Fino-Li-Salamon-Vezzoni
[
17
]
studied the Calabi-Yau equation on certain symplectic non-Kähler 4-manifolds,
which is equivalent to solve the Monge-Ampère equation on 2-torus. Recently, Y. Li and the author
[
16
]
solved the Monge-Ampère
equation on flat
n
-torus involving gradient terms in the determinant.
For the study of Calabi-Yau metric on non-compact singular varieties, we refer the reader to Collins-Guo-Tong
[
10
]

and references therein.
The complex Monge-Ampère equation was solved on Hermitian manifolds by Tosatti-Weinkove
[
47
]
,
and on almost Hermitian manifolds by Chu-Tosatti-Weinkove
[
9
]
.

The Dirichlet problem for complex Monge-Ampère equation in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
was solved by
Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
4
]
when
\Omega
is a strongly pseudoconvex domain.
B. Guan explored the problem on general domains with the existence of a subsolution in
[
22
]
.
The technique in
[
22
]
of deriving second order estimates was used by P. F. Guan in
[
28
]

to resolve the conjecture of Chern-Levine-Nirenberg.
Li in
[
36
]
treated the problem for more Hessian equations.
On complex manifolds with boundary, the Dirichlet problem for complex Monge-Ampère equation was widely concerned.
Cheng-Yau
[
7
]
proved the existence of a solution
u
to the problem on Kähler manifolds with
u=+\infty
on the boundary.
Cherrier-Hanani
[
8
]
investigated the problem on strongly pseudoconvex Hermitian manifolds.
Assuming that there exists a subsolution, Guan-Li
[
24
]
considered the problem on general Hermitian manifolds.
We refer the reader to the survey of Phong-Song-Sturm
[
39
]
for the vast field of complex Monge-Ampère equation.
For a type of Hessian quotient equations, see Guan-Sun
[
27
]
and Song-Weinkove
[
40
]
.
Weak solutions to complex Hessian equations have been discussed by many authors.
We refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
13
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
38
]
.
For the real Hessian equation, see
[
6
,
5
,
37
,

,
26
,
32
]
and references therein.

The other end point case
p=n
is also well known since it is a linear PDE.
The two end point case
p=1
and
p=n
respectively correspond to the end point case
k=n
and
k=1
of the following
counterpart to equation (
1.1
), i.e. the complex
k
-Hessian equations,

\sigma_{k}(\lambda)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}=\psi,\;\mbox{where}\;1\leq k\leq n.

The above equation arose from the study of Hull-Strominger system by Fu-Yau
[
20
]
.
The complex
k
-Hessian equations for
1<k<n
on closed Kähler manifolds was solved by Dinew-Kołodziej
[
14
]

in 2012 by first establishing a Liouville theorem, and then combining it with the second order estimate derived by Hou-Ma-Wu
[
31
]
.
Sun in
[
41
]
deduced the existence of a solution to the Hessian quotient equation.
The complex
k
-Hessian equations on Hermitian manifolds was solved independently by Zhang
[
50
]
and Székelyhidi
[
42
]

by virtue of the Liouville theorem.
The Liouville theorem was employed in a blow-up argument to conclude the missing part of the a priori estimate, the gradient estimate.
Zhang in
[
51
]
proved the gradient estimate with an extra assumption that
\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u>0
.
It is still open to derive the gradient estimate by the maximum principle.
The Dirichlet problem for complex
k
-Hessian equations was solved by Collins-Picard
[
11
]
on Hermitian manifolds recently.

Now we discuss the proof of our main theorem.
The approach to tackle the equation is the well-known and standard continuity method,
which reduced the solvability to the a priori estimates.
By the envelope trick in
[
46
]
, and the Evans-Krylov theorem,
one can derive the
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate as long as one have
C^{0}
,
C^{1}
and
C^{2}
estimate.
The
C^{0}
estimate and the boundary
C^{1}
estimate are easy to obtain since we assumed a subsolution.
Therefore, our main goal is to derive the following second order estimate

\sup_{M}|\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u|\leq C(1+\sup_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}),

where
C
only depends on the background data, but not on
u
.
This was achieved by following closely the argument in Collins-Picard
[
11
]
.
Then, by virtue of the Liouville theorem for maximal
(n-1)
-plurisubharmonic functions
established by Tosatti-Weinkove
[
48
]
we can apply a similar blow up argument as in Collins-Picard
[
11
]

to get the desired gradient estimate, and thereby obtain the second order estimate.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we introduce some useful notations and recall some preliminary estimates.
In Section 3, we prove the boundary mixed normal-tangential estimates.
Finally, we establish the boundary double normal estimate in Section 4.

2.
Preliminaries and main results

For an admissible solution
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
, in local coordinates,
denote
{g^{i}}_{j}=\omega^{i\bar{k}}(\chi_{\overline{k}j}+u_{\overline{k}j})
.
Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
be the eigenvalues of the endomorphism
{g^{i}}_{j}
.
Then equation (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

(2.1)

F({g^{i}}_{j}):=F(\lambda):=\Pi_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq n}(\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p}})=\psi,

Denote
\tilde{F}=F^{1/C_{n}^{p}}
, and for convenience, we use the notation
f(\lambda)=\tilde{F}(\lambda)
.
We remark that
f
satisfies
the following structure conditions:

(f1)

\displaystyle f>0\;\mbox{in}\;\mathcal{P}_{p}\;\mbox{and}\;f=0\;\mbox{on}\;\partial\mathcal{P}_{p};

(f2)

\displaystyle f_{i}:=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0,\;\forall\;1\leq i\leq n;

(f3)

\displaystyle f\;\mbox{is concave in}\;\mathcal{P}_{p};

(f4)

\displaystyle f\;\mbox{is homogeneous of degree one, i.e. }\;f(t\lambda)=tf(\lambda),\;\forall\;t>0.

We refer the reader to
[
12
]
for the properties of the operator, or to the appendix in
[
15
]
.
By (f3) and (f4), we can derive that

(f5)

\displaystyle\sum f_{i}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)+\sum f_{i}(\lambda)(1-\lambda_{i})\geq f(1,\ldots,1)>0\;\mathrm{in}\;\mathcal{P}_{p}.

Note that
\tilde{F}(A)
is concave for
A\in P_{p}
.
As in Collins-Picard
[
11
]
, we define the tensor

F^{p\bar{q}}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial{g^{r}}_{p}}\omega^{r\bar{q}}\;\mbox{and}\;F^{p\bar{q},r\bar{s}}=\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial{g^{a}}_{p}\partial{g^{b}}_{r}}\omega^{a\bar{q}}\omega^{b\bar{s}}.

At a point
p_{0}\in M
, where
\omega=\sqrt{-1}\delta_{k\ell}dz^{k}\wedge d\overline{z}^{\ell}

and
{g^{i}}_{j}=\lambda_{j}{\delta^{i}}_{j}
is diagonal,
we have

\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}=f_{p}\delta_{pq}\;\mbox{and}\;\tilde{F}^{k\bar{k}}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{k}}=\sum_{k\in\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{p}\}}\frac{\frac{1}{C_{n}^{p}}f(\lambda)}{\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p}}}.

Now we introduce the following notations that

K=\sup_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+1\;\mbox{and}\;\mathcal{F}=\sum_{i}f_{i}.

We recall some known estimates in the literature.
We first recall the maximum principle.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold with boundary. Suppose that
v:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function such that
the vector of eigenvalues of
\omega^{-1}(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}v)
lies outside the set

\mathcal{P}_{p}^{\psi}:=\{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{p}:f(\lambda)\geq\tilde{\psi}\}
for all
z\in M
. If
u\leq v
on
\partial M
,
then

u\leq v\;\mbox{on}\;M.

Proof.

Suppose
u>v
at some interior point
x
in
M
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
u-v
attains a maximum at
x
.
Then
D^{2}u(x)\leq D^{2}v(x)
.
Let
\lambda(z)=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
be the eigenvalues of
\omega^{-1}(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u)(z)

and
\mu(z)=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n})
be the eigenvalues of
\omega^{-1}(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}v)(z)
,
both arranged in decreasing order.
By Weyl inequality, we have
\lambda_{i}\leq\mu_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
Since
\mathcal{P}_{p}+\overline{\mathcal{P}_{1}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{p}
and
\lambda(z)\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{\psi}
,
we can derive a contradiction.
∎

Next, we prove that

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
u\in C^{3}(M)
is an admissible solution to the equation (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).
We have the following estimates

\sup_{M}|u|\leq C\;\mbox{and}\;\sup_{\partial M}|\nabla u|\leq C,

where
C
depends on
(M,\omega)
,
\chi
and
\underline{u}
.

Proof.

Define
\tilde{\psi}=\psi^{1/C_{n}^{p}}
.
By the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means, we have

\frac{p\omega^{j\bar{k}}(\chi_{\overline{k}j}+u_{\overline{k}j})}{n}\geq f(\lambda)\geq\inf\tilde{\psi}>0.

Let
\overline{u}
be the solution to the following equation

\omega^{j\bar{k}}(\chi_{\bar{k}j}+\partial_{j}\partial_{\bar{k}}\overline{u})=0\;\mbox{in}\;M\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\overline{u}=\varphi\;\mbox{on}\;\partial M.

Then, by the maximum principle we can derive that
\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}
on
M
.
Since
\underline{u}=u=\overline{u}
on
\partial M
, we get the desired gradient estimate on
\partial M
.
∎

We now recall second order estimates for admissible solutions from
[
42
]
and
[
31
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
u\in C^{4}(M)
is an admissible solution to the equation (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).
Then, we have

(2.2)

\sup_{M}|\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u|\leq C(K+\sup_{\partial M}|\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u|),

where
C
depends on
(M,\omega)
,
|\tilde{\psi}|_{C^{2}}
,
\underline{u}
and
\chi
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that our operator satisfies the assumptions in
[
42
]
.
Define the following two functions,

\varphi(s)=-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-\frac{s}{2K})\;\mbox{and}\;\phi(t)=-2At+\frac{A\tau}{2}t^{2},

where
\tau
is a small constant to be chosen depending on
|u|_{C^{0}}
and
A
is a large constant to be chosen.
Apply the maximum principle to the following test function:

G=\log\lambda_{1}+\varphi(|\nabla u|^{2})+\phi(u),

where
\lambda_{1}:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the largest eigenvalue of the
Hermitian endomorphism
{g^{i}}_{j}
.
By the argument in
[
31
]
and
[
42
]
, we can
conclude the estimate (
2.2
).
∎

The main purpose of this paper is to establish the following second order estimate for admissible solutions on the boundary
\partial M
,
which will be completed in the next two sections.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose
u\in C^{4}(M)
is an admissible solution to the equation (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) with
p=n-1
.
Then, we have

(2.3)

\sup_{\partial M}|\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u|\leq CK,

where
C
depends on
(M,\omega)
,
\underline{u}
,
\varphi
,
|\tilde{\psi}|_{C^{2}}
,
\inf\psi

.

We remark that the assumption
p=n-1
is only used in deriving the double normal estimate.

3.
Boundary Mixed Normal-Tangential Estimates

Following the setup in
[
11
]
, let
\Omega
be a boundary chart of
p\in\partial M
.
Choose coordinates
z=(z^{1},\cdots,z^{n})
such that
p
corresponds to the origin and
\alpha_{\bar{k}j}(0)=\delta_{kj}
,
where
z^{i}=x^{i}+\sqrt{-1}y^{i}
.
We may rotate the coordinates such that
x^{n}
is in the direction of the inner normal vector to
\Omega
at the origin.
Let
\rho
be the defining function of the boundary
\partial M
, that is

\partial M\cap\Omega=\{\rho=0\},\;\;\Omega\subset\{\rho\leq 0\},\;\;d\rho\neq 0\;\mbox{on}\;\partial M.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that

\rho=-x^{n}+O(|z|^{2}).

Denote

t^{\alpha}=y^{\alpha},\alpha\in\{1,\cdots,n\},t^{n+\alpha}=x^{\alpha},\alpha\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}.

We can find a function
\zeta(t)
such that
\rho(t,\zeta(t))=0
on
\partial M
.
By the boundary condition
u=\underline{u}
on
\partial M
, we get the estimate

(3.1)

|\partial_{t^{\alpha}}\partial_{t^{\beta}}u(0)|\leq C,\;\mbox{for}\;1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq 2n-1,

where
C
depends on
(M,\omega)
,
\chi
and
\underline{u}
by the gradient estimate in Lemma
2.2
,
as

(3.2)

\partial_{t^{\alpha}}\partial_{t^{\beta}}(u-\underline{u})(0)=-\partial_{x^{n}}(u-\underline{u})(0)\rho_{t^{\alpha}t^{\beta}}(0).

The goal of this section is to prove

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
u\in C^{4}(\overline{M})
is an admissible solution to the equation (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).
Then, we have

(3.3)

|g_{\bar{n}i}|\leq CK^{1/2}\;\mbox{for}\;i=1,\cdots,n-1,

where
C
depends on
(M,\omega)
,
\underline{u}
,
\varphi
,
|\tilde{\psi}|_{C^{2}}
,
\inf_{M}\psi
and
\chi
.

Before proving the above result, we need a few lemmas.
First is a Lemma due to B. Guan. Note that
f
satisfies the assumptions as in
[
23
]
.
Since
\sum f_{i}\lambda_{i}\geq 0
, from Lemma 2.20 and Corollary 2.21 in Guan
[
23
]
, we have

Lemma 3.2

(
[
23
]
)
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{p}
, index
r
and
\epsilon>0
, we have

\sum f_{i}|\lambda_{i}|\leq\epsilon\sum_{i\neq r}f_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}+\frac{C}{\epsilon}\sum f_{i}+C,

where
C
depends on
n
,
p
and
f(\lambda)
.

Proof.

By (f1)-(f4) and following the proof of Lemma 3.4 in
[
11
]
, one can
prove the inequality.
∎

The second lemma is on a barrier function due to B. Guan
[
22
]
, which
is used frequently in literatures to establish the normal-tangential estimate on the boundary.
For small
\delta>0
to be determined, define

\Omega_{\delta}=\Omega\cap\{|z|<\delta\}.

Let
d
denote the distance function to
\partial M
.
Consider the following barrier function

v=(u-\underline{u})+\tau d-Nd^{2},

where
\tau\ll 1
and
N\gg 1
are two positive constant.
Then, we can prove

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
u
is an admissible solution to the equation (
1.1
).
There exist
\tau,N,\varepsilon,\delta>0
depending on
(M,\omega)
,
\underline{u}
,
\sup_{M}\psi
,
\inf_{M}\psi
and
\chi
,
such that
v\geq 0
and

\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\partial_{p}\partial_{\bar{q}}v\leq-\varepsilon(1+\mathcal{F})\;\mbox{in}\;\Omega_{\delta}.

Proof.

We compute at a point with coordinates such that
\omega_{\bar{k}j}=\delta_{kj}
and
{g^{i}}_{j}=\lambda_{j}{\delta^{i}}_{j}

with the eigenvalues arranged in the descending order
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
.

Note that
u\geq\underline{u}
in
\Omega
, which implies
v\geq d(\tau-Nd)>0
in
\Omega_{\delta}
for sufficiently small
\delta>0
.
By direct calculations, we see that

\displaystyle\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\partial_{p}\partial_{\bar{q}}v=

\displaystyle\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}((u-\underline{u})_{\bar{q}p}+(\tau-2Nd)d_{\bar{q}p}-2Nd_{\bar{q}}d_{p}).

Since
\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\underline{u}\in P_{p}(M)
, we can find a positive constant
\epsilon_{0}
such that

\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\underline{u}-\epsilon_{0}\omega\in P_{p}(M).

By the concavity of
\tilde{F}
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(\omega^{i\bar{k}}(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\underline{u}-\epsilon_{0}\omega)_{\bar{k}j})-\tilde{F}({g^{i}}_{j})\leq\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}(\underline{u}_{\bar{q}p}-\epsilon_{0}\omega_{\bar{q}p}-u_{\bar{q}p}).

Hence, we derive that

\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}(u_{\bar{q}p}-\underline{u}_{\bar{q}p}+\epsilon_{0}\omega_{\bar{q}p})\leq C,

where
C
depends on
\tilde{\psi}
and
\underline{u}
. Therefore, we obtain that

\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\partial_{p}\partial_{\bar{q}}v\leq C-\epsilon_{0}\mathcal{F}-2N\tilde{F}^{\bar{q}p}d_{\bar{q}}d_{p}+C(\tau+2Nd)\mathcal{F}.

Choosing
\tau\ll 1
and
\delta\ll 1
, we have

(3.4)

\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\partial_{p}\partial_{\bar{q}}v\leq C-\frac{3\epsilon_{0}}{4}\mathcal{F}-\frac{N}{2}\tilde{F}^{\bar{1}1},

since
|\partial d|=\frac{1}{2}
.

Note that
\tilde{F}^{\bar{i}i}=f_{i}
and
f_{1}\leq\cdots\leq f_{n}
. Then, we have

N\tilde{F}^{\bar{1}1}+\epsilon_{0}\sum_{i}\tilde{F}^{\bar{i}i}=(N+\epsilon_{0})f_{1}+\epsilon_{0}f_{2}+\cdots+\epsilon_{0}f_{n}.

Since

\displaystyle f_{k}=\sum_{k\in\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{p}\}}\frac{\frac{1}{C_{n}^{p}}f(\lambda)}{\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p}}},

it follows that

\displaystyle N\tilde{F}^{\bar{1}1}+\epsilon_{0}\sum_{i}\tilde{F}^{\bar{i}i}\geq

\displaystyle N\sum_{1\notin\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{p-1}\}}\frac{\frac{1}{C_{n}^{p}}f(\lambda)}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p-1}}}

\displaystyle+\epsilon_{0}\sum_{1,2\notin\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{p-1}\}}\frac{\frac{1}{C_{n}^{p}}f(\lambda)}{\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{p-1}}}

\displaystyle+\cdots+\epsilon_{0}\;\frac{\frac{1}{C_{n}^{p}}f(\lambda)}{\lambda_{n-p+1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}}.

By the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means, we obtain

\displaystyle N\tilde{F}^{\bar{1}1}+\epsilon_{0}\sum_{i}\tilde{F}^{\bar{i}i}\geq N^{p/n}\epsilon_{0}^{(n-p)/n}.

Choosing
N
sufficiently large, we derive from (
3.4

as

In

We introduce the notation

,
\chi
, and

. As in Collins-Picard

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists

(3.6)

Proof.

The proof of the above lemma is the same as that of Lemma 4.3 in
[
11
]
.
So we only sketch it here.
Note that

\displaystyle\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\partial_{p}\partial_{\bar{q}}T_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u})=

\displaystyle\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\partial_{p}\partial_{\bar{q}}\partial_{t^{\alpha}}(u-\underline{u})-\frac{\rho_{t^{\alpha}}}{\rho_{x^{n}}}\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\partial_{p}\partial_{\bar{q}}\partial_{x^{n}}(u-\underline{u})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\text{Re}\Big(\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\Big(\partial_{p}\frac{\rho_{t^{\alpha}}}{\rho_{x^{n}}}\Big)\partial_{\bar{q}}\partial_{x^{n}}(u-\underline{u})\Big)-\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\Big(\partial_{p}\partial_{\bar{q}}\frac{\rho_{t^{\alpha}}}{\rho_{x^{n}}}\Big)\partial_{x^{n}}(u-\underline{u}).

The third order terms and the last term on the right hand side can be controlled by

\displaystyle\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\Big(\partial_{p}\frac{\rho_{t^{\alpha}}}{\rho_{x^{n}}}\Big)\partial_{\bar{q}}\partial_{x^{n}}(u-\underline{u})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K^{1/2}}\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\partial_{p}\partial_{y^{n}}(u-\underline{u})\partial_{\bar{q}}\partial_{y^{n}}(u-\underline{u})+C\sum f_{i}|\lambda_{i}|+CK^{1/2}\mathcal{F}

Therefore, the lemma follows.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{K^{1/2}}\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\partial_{p}\partial_{y^{i}}(u-\underline{u})\partial_{\bar{q}}\partial_{y^{i}}(u-\underline{u})+\frac{2}{K^{1/2}}\partial_{y^{i}}(u-\underline{u})\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\partial_{p}\partial_{\bar{q}}\partial_{y^{i}}(u-\underline{u})

e_{a}={e^{i}}_{a}\partial_{i}

Proof of Proposition

.

Consider the following barrier function due to Collins-Picard,

\Psi=AK^{1/2}v+BK^{1/2}|z|^{2}-\frac{1}{K^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{y^{i}}(u-\underline{u}))^{2}-\frac{1}{K^{1/2}}\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}|\nabla_{a}(u-\underline{u})|^{2}.

Denote

.
By Lemma
3.3
, Lemma
3.4
, Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\Psi+T_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-A\varepsilon K^{1/2}(1+\mathcal{F})+BK^{1/2}\sum_{i}f_{i}-\frac{1}{2nK^{1/2}}\sum_{i\neq r}f_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{K^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\tilde{F}^{p\bar{q}}\partial_{p}\partial_{y^{i}}(u-\underline{u})\partial_{\bar{q}}\partial_{y^{i}}(u-\underline{u})+|\mathcal{E}|.

Choosing

, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\Psi+T_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{A\varepsilon}{2}K^{1/2}(1+\mathcal{F})-\frac{1}{2nK^{1/2}}\sum_{i\neq r}f_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}+C\sum_{i}f_{i}|\lambda_{i}|.

By Lemma
3.2
with
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2nCK^{1/2}}
, we see that

\mathcal{L}(\Psi+T_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u}))\leq-\frac{A\varepsilon}{2}K^{1/2}(1+\mathcal{F})+\frac{C}{\epsilon}\sum_{i}f_{i}+C\leq 0

for
A\gg 1
.

On the boundary
\partial M\cap\Omega_{\delta}
,
we have
\partial_{y^{i}}(u-\underline{u})=-\partial_{x^{n}}(u-\underline{u})\partial_{y^{i}}\zeta

and by Lemma
2.2
we obtain

(\partial_{y^{i}}(u-\underline{u}))^{2}\leq C|z|^{2}.

On
\partial M
, since
\zeta_{t^{\alpha}}=-\frac{\rho_{t^{\alpha}}}{\rho_{x^{n}}}
, we have

T_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u})=\partial_{t^{\alpha}}(u-\underline{u})+\zeta_{t^{\alpha}}\partial_{x^{n}}(u-\underline{u})=0

and
\nabla_{a}(u-\underline{u})=0
. Recall that
v\geq 0
. Hence, we see that

\Psi+T_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u})\geq BK^{1/2}|z|^{2}-\frac{C}{K^{1/2}}|z|^{2}\geq 0

on
\partial M\cap\Omega_{\delta}
for

.
On the piece
\partial B_{\delta}\cap\Omega_{\delta}
, for sufficiently large

\Psi+T_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u})\geq BK^{1/2}\delta^{2}-CK^{1/2}\geq 0.

It follows that
\Psi+T_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u})\geq 0
on
\Omega_{\delta}
by the maximum principle.
Since

[\Psi+T_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u})](0)=0,

we therefore derive that

AK^{1/2}\partial_{x^{n}}v(0)+\partial_{x^{n}}\partial_{t^{\alpha}}(u-\underline{u})(0)-\partial_{x^{n}}(\frac{\rho_{t^{\alpha}}}{\rho_{x^{n}}})(0)\partial_{x^{n}}(u-\underline{u})(0)\geq 0.

By the boundary gradient estimate Lemma
2.2

\partial_{x^{n}}\partial_{t^{\alpha}}(u-\underline{u})(0)\geq-CK^{1/2}.

Similarly, consider

to conclude that

\partial_{x^{n}}\partial_{t^{\alpha}}(u-\underline{u})(0)\leq CK^{1/2}.

It follows that

|g_{\bar{n}i}|(0)\leq CK^{1/2}
,

Note that for

\displaystyle(\mbox{tr}_{\omega}g-g_{\bar{1}1})\cdots(\mbox{tr}_{\omega}g-g_{\bar{n}n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|g_{\bar{n}i}|^{2}(\mbox{tr}_{\omega}g-g_{\bar{1}1})\cdots\widehat{(\mbox{tr}_{\omega}g-g_{\bar{i}i})}\cdots(\mbox{tr}_{\omega}g-g_{\overline{n-1}n-1}),

Without loss of generality, we also assume

.
By (

,

4.1

Second, if

.

\underline{\lambda^{\prime}}
lives in a compact set of
\mathcal{P}_{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.
Therefore,

\sum_{\alpha}\rho_{\bar{\alpha}\alpha}(0)\geq\frac{t_{0}}{\eta_{0}}\sum_{\alpha}\underline{\lambda^{\prime}_{\alpha}}(0)\geq\frac{1}{2\eta_{0}}\inf_{p\in\partial M}\sum_{\alpha}\underline{\lambda^{\prime}_{\alpha}}(p)\geq\frac{\theta}{2\eta_{0}},

where
\theta>0
is a constant only depending on
(M,\omega)
,
\chi
,
\underline{u}
. Since
|\nabla u|\leq C
on
\partial M

as proved in Lemma
2.2
, we know
|\eta_{0}|=|(u-\underline{u})_{x^{n}}(0)|\leq C
, which implies that

(4.7)

\sum_{\alpha}\rho_{\bar{\alpha}\alpha}(0)\geq\theta_{0}

for
\theta_{0}>0
is a constant only depending on
(M,\omega)
,
\chi
,
\underline{u}
.

Now, we compute the the quantity in the lemma.
We have

\Lambda(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi)=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4},

where

\displaystyle T_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}{\xi^{j}}_{a}\overline{{\xi^{k}}_{a}}[(\chi_{\bar{k}j}+\underline{u}_{\bar{k}j})-\frac{\eta_{0}}{t_{0}}\rho_{\bar{k}j}]

\displaystyle T_{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}{\xi^{j}}_{a}\overline{{\xi^{k}}_{a}}(l_{\bar{k}}\rho_{j}+l_{j}\rho_{\bar{k}})

\displaystyle T_{3}=

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}{\xi^{j}}_{a}\overline{{\xi^{k}}_{a}}(l_{i}z^{i}+l_{\bar{i}}\bar{z}^{i})\rho_{\bar{k}j}

\displaystyle T_{4}=

\displaystyle 2L\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}{\xi^{j}}_{a}\overline{{\xi^{k}}_{a}}\partial_{j}D\partial_{\bar{k}}D+2LD\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}{\xi^{j}}_{a}\overline{{\xi^{k}}_{a}}\partial_{j}\partial_{\bar{k}}D.

At the origin, we have
T_{1}(0)=0
. Therefore, we see

T_{1}=m_{i}z^{i}+m_{\bar{i}}\bar{z}^{i}+O(|z|^{2}),

for some bounded constants
m_{i}
depending only on
(M,\omega),\chi,\underline{u}
.
Since the vector fields
\xi_{a}
are tangential to the level sets of
D(z)
,
direct computation gives that

0={\xi^{j}}_{a}\partial_{j}D=-{\xi^{j}}_{a}\rho_{j}+\tau{\xi^{j}}_{a}\bar{z}^{j}.

Substituting the above equality into
T_{2}
, we get

\displaystyle T_{2}=

\displaystyle\tau\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}{\xi^{j}}_{a}\overline{{\xi^{k}}_{a}}(l_{\bar{k}}\bar{z}^{j}+l_{j}z^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\big({\xi^{j}}_{a}(0)\overline{{\xi^{k}}_{a}}(0)+O(|z|)\big)(l_{\bar{k}}\bar{z}^{j}+l_{j}z^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}(l_{a}z^{a}+l_{\bar{a}}\bar{z}^{a})+O(|z|^{2}),

where in the third equality we used
\xi_{a}(0)=\partial_{a}
.
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle T_{3}=

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}{\xi^{j}}_{a}\overline{{\xi^{k}}_{a}}(l_{i}z^{i}+l_{\bar{i}}\bar{z}^{i})\rho_{\bar{k}j}(0)+O(|z|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}(l_{i}z^{i}+l_{\bar{i}}\bar{z}^{i}){\xi^{j}}_{a}(0)\overline{{\xi^{k}}_{a}}(0)\rho_{\bar{k}j}(0)+O(|z|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(l_{i}z^{i}+l_{\bar{i}}\bar{z}^{i})\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\rho_{\bar{a}a}(0)+O(|z|^{2}).

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}=

\displaystyle 2\text{Re}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\{m_{i}+\tau l_{i}+l_{i}\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\rho_{\bar{a}a}(0)\}z^{i}

\displaystyle+2\text{Re}\{m_{n}+l_{n}\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\rho_{\bar{a}a}(0)\}z^{n}+O(|z|^{2}).

By (
4.7
), for any
\tau>0
, we can choose
l_{i}
such that

l_{i}\Big(\tau+\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\rho_{\bar{a}a}(0)\Big)=-m_{i}\;\mbox{for}\;1\leq i\leq n-1\;\mbox{and}\;l_{n}\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\rho_{\bar{a}a}(0)=-m_{n}.

Note that
|l_{i}|\leq\frac{|m_{i}|}{\theta_{0}}
. We then arrive at

T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}\leq C|z|^{2}.

Recall that
\xi_{a}
is tangential to level sets of
D
. So,

T_{4}=2LD\Lambda\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}D.

At the origin,

\Lambda\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}D(0)=\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}(-\rho_{\bar{a}a}(0)+\tau)\leq-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}

as long as we choose
0<\tau<\frac{\theta_{0}}{2(n-1)}
.
We can choose
\delta>0
small enough to ensure that

\Lambda\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}D\leq-\frac{\theta_{0}}{4}\;\mbox{in}\;\Omega_{\delta},

and, therefore,

T_{4}\leq-\frac{\tau\theta_{0}L}{2}|z|^{2}\;\mbox{in}\;\Omega_{\delta}.

It follows that

T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4}\leq C|z|^{2}-\frac{\tau\theta_{0}L}{2}|z|^{2}\leq 0

for
L\geq C(\tau\theta_{0})^{-1}
,
which completes the proof of Lemma
4.3
.
∎

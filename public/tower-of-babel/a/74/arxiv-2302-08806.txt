Periodic Normal Forms for Bifurcations of Limit Cycles in DDEs
Bram Lentjes, Len Spek, Maikel M. Bosschaert, Yuri A. Kuznetsov

Abstract
A recent work [ 39 ] by the authors on the existence of a periodic smooth finite-dimensional center manifold near a nonhyperbolic cycle in delay differential equations motivates the derivation of periodic normal forms. In this paper, we prove the existence of a special coordinate system on the center manifold that will allow us to describe the local dynamics on the center manifold near the cycle in terms of these periodic normal forms. To construct the linear part of this coordinate system, we prove the existence of time periodic smooth Jordan chains for the original and adjoint system. Moreover, we establish duality and spectral relations between both systems by using tools from the theory of delay equations and Volterra integral equations, dual perturbation theory, duality theory and evolution semigroups.



{keywords}

delay differential equations, dual perturbation theory, sun-star calculus, center manifold, normal forms, Jordan chains, nonhyperbolic cycles, duality theory, evolution semigroups

1
Introduction

Bifurcation theory allows one to analyze the behavior of complicated high dimensional nonlinear dynamical systems near bifurcations by reducing the system to a low dimensional invariant manifold, called the center manifold. Using normal form theory, the dynamics on the center manifold can be described by a simple canonical equation called the normal form. These bifurcations and normal forms can be categorized, and their properties can be understood in terms of certain coefficients of the normal form, see
[
36
]
for more details. Methods to compute these normal form coefficients have been implemented in software like
MatCont

[
13
]
and
DDE-BifTool

[
22
,
46
]
to study various classes of dynamical systems.

For bifurcations of limit cycles in continuous-time dynamical systems, there are three generic codimension one bifurcations: fold (or limit point), period-doubling (or flip) and Neimark-Sacker (or torus) bifurcation. These bifurcations are well understood for ordinary differentials equations (ODEs)
[
32
,
33
,
36
,
37
,
53
,
54
]
, but for delay differential equations (DDEs) the theory is still lacking. To understand these bifurcations, one should first prove the existence of a center manifold near a nonhyperbolic cycle. The authors proved in
[
39
]
that indeed such a center manifold near a nonhyperbolic cycle exists and is sufficiently smooth. The next step is to study the local dynamics on the center manifold near a nonhyperbolic cycle in terms of normal forms.

The aim of this paper is to show for classical DDEs that the local dynamics on the center manifold near a nonhyperbolic cycle can be studied in terms of periodic normal forms. We generalize the results from Iooss (and Adelmeyer)
[
32
,
33
]
on periodic normal forms from finite-dimensional ODEs towards infinite-dimensional DDEs. This task will be accomplished by using the rigorous perturbation framework of dual semigroups (sun-star calculus). In an upcoming paper, we present explicit computational formulas for the critical normal form coefficients, along the lines of the periodic normalization method
[
37
,
53
,
54
]
, for all codimension one bifurcations of limit cycles in classical DDEs. Finally, we plan to implement the obtained computational formulas into a public software package.

Consider a

Using the perturbation framework of dual semigroups, called sun-star calculus, developed in

To study the local dynamics generated by (

1) To parametrize

2) Once the existence of the periodic smooth Jordan chains for

Regarding normal form theory for periodic DDEs, we mention the work by Faria

where normal forms were derived for periodic DDEs possessing an autonomous linear part. However, when studying (

Periodic center manifolds for classical DDEs

In this section, we primarily summarize the results from

]
and secondly recall and extend some results from (time-dependent) dual perturbation theory, for which the book

]

In the setting of classical DDEs, we work with the real Banach space
X:=C([-h,0],\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the state space for some (maximal) finite
delay

h>0
equipped with the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. Consider for an integer

a
C^{k+1}
-smooth (nonlinear) operator

together with the initial value problem

\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)=F(x_{t}),\quad&t\geq 0,\\
x_{0}=\varphi,\quad&\varphi\in X,\end{cases}

(DDE)

where
x_{t}\in X
denotes the history of
x
at time
t\geq 0
, as introduced in (
2
).
By a
solution
of
(
DDE
)
we mean a continuous function
x:[-h,t_{\varphi})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
t_{\varphi}\in(0,\infty]
that is continuously differentiable on
[0,t_{\varphi})
and satisfies (
DDE
). We say that a function
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a

of (
DDE
) if
\gamma|_{[-h,\infty)}
is a solution of (
DDE
) and there exists a minimal
T>0
, called the
period
of
\gamma
, such that
\gamma_{T}=\gamma_{0}
. We call
\Gamma:=\{\gamma_{t}\in X:t\in\mathbb{R}\}

or (limit)

We want to study (
DDE
) near the periodic solution
\gamma
, and it is therefore convenient to translate
\gamma
towards the origin. More specifically, if
x
is a solution of (
DDE
), then

defined by

satisfies the periodic (nonlinear) DDE

(3)

where the
C^{k}
-smooth bounded linear operator

denotes the Fréchet derivative of

evaluated at
\gamma_{t}
, and the

-smooth operator
G(t,\cdot):=F(\gamma_{t}+\cdot)-F(\gamma_{t})-L(t)
consists solely of nonlinear terms. Regarding the linear part in (
3
), it is traditional to apply a vector-valued version of the Riesz representation theorem
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
and write

L(t)\varphi=\int_{0}^{h}d_{2}\zeta(t,\theta)\varphi(-\theta)=:\langle\zeta(t,\cdot),\varphi\rangle,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\ \varphi\in X.

(4)

The

is a matrix-valued function,

is of bounded variation, right-continuous on the open interval

,

The starting point of applying the sun-star calculus construction towards the setting of classical DDEs is by studying the trivial DDE

\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)=0,\quad&t\geq 0,\\
x_{0}=\varphi,\quad&\varphi\in X,\end{cases}

(5)

which has the unique solution

We define the

{proposition}

The unbounded linear operators
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star}
are not closed but closable.

Proof.

Let us first prove that
\mathcal{A}
is closable. Let
(\varphi_{m})_{m}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
converging in norm to zero, and assume that the sequence
(\mathcal{A}\varphi_{m})_{m}
converges in norm to some
\psi\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X)
. We have to show that
\psi=0
. Set
\psi_{m}=\mathcal{A}\varphi_{m}
and notice that this is equivalent to

\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\bigg)\varphi_{m}(\tau)(\theta)=\psi_{m}(\tau)(\theta),\quad\forall\tau\in\mathbb{R},\ \theta\in[-h,0],

where
\varphi_{m}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}

\varphi_{m}(\tau)(\theta)=\varphi_{m}(\tau+\theta)(0)+\int_{0}^{\theta}\psi_{m}(\tau+\theta-s)(s)ds,\quad\varphi_{m}(\tau)^{\prime}(0)=L(\tau)\varphi_{m}(\tau).

Since the convergence of
\varphi_{m}\to 0
and
\psi_{m}\to\psi
is uniform in the first and second variable as

, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\theta}\psi(\tau+\theta-s)(s)ds&=\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\theta}\psi_{m}(\tau+\theta-s)(s)ds\\
&=\lim_{m\to\infty}(\varphi_{m}(\tau)(\theta)-\varphi_{m}(\tau+\theta)(0))=0,\quad\forall\tau\in\mathbb{R},\ \theta\in[-h,0].\end{split}

(33)

Let
\psi_{i}
denote the
i
th component of
\psi
for
i=1,\dots,n
. To prove the claim, let
\tau_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[-h,0]
with
\theta_{1}\neq\theta_{2}
be given. If
\tau_{2}=\tau_{1}+\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}
, then according to (
33
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}\psi_{i}(\tau_{1}+\theta_{1}-s)(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\theta_{1}}\psi_{i}(\tau_{1}+\theta_{1}-s)(s)ds+\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}\psi_{i}(\tau_{1}+\theta_{1}-s)(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\theta_{2}}\psi_{i}(\tau_{1}+\theta_{1}-s)(s)ds=\int_{0}^{\theta_{2}}\psi_{i}(\tau_{2}+\theta_{2}-s)(s)ds=0

since the second and last integral vanish due to (
33
). Assume now without loss of generality that
\theta_{2}>\theta_{1}
. Since
\psi
is continuous, the real-valued function

is continuous on the compact set
[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]
. Hence, the mean value theorem for integrals applies and there exists a

such that
\operatorname{Re}\psi_{i}(\tau_{1}+\theta_{1}-c_{i})(c_{i})=0

when

and we obtain
\operatorname{Re}\psi_{i}(\tau_{1})(\theta_{1})=0

\operatorname{Im}\psi_{i}(\tau_{1})(\theta_{1})=0

\psi_{i}(\tau_{1})(\theta_{1})=0

\tau_{1}
and
\theta_{1}

{lemma}

The evolution semigroup
\mathcal{U}
is a well-defined
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-semigroup.

Proof.

Let us first observe that

is well-defined for any

since

is a strongly continuous forward evolutionary system, and there holds

\displaystyle(\mathcal{U}(s)f)(\tau+T)

\displaystyle=U(\tau+T,\tau+T-s)f(\tau+T-s)

\displaystyle=U(\tau,\tau-s)f(\tau-s)=(\mathcal{U}(s)f)(\tau),\quad\forall f\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X),

where the second equality follows from (
13
). Since the bounded linear perturbation
B
from (
11

T
-periodic, there exist a

and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that

for all
\tau\geq s

[
19

is a family of bounded linear operators forming a semigroup that satisfies

for all

It remains to show that
\mathcal{U}
is strongly continuous. To do this, let us reformulate (
41

C(\mathbb{T},X)
. Let

and

be given. Since

for all

for some

such that

is covered by the union of

and satisfy in addition

\sup_{\tau\in[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\|f(\tau)-f(\tau_{i})\|_{\infty}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4(Me^{\omega s}+1)},\quad\forall i\in\{1,\dots,l-1\}.

Due to the strong continuity of the forward evolutionary system

, there exists a
\delta_{2}>0
such that

for all

\displaystyle\sup_{\hat{\tau}\in\mathbb{T}}\|U(\tau+s,\tau)f(\tau)-f(\tau)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1,\dots,l-1}\sup_{\tau\in[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}\bigg(\|U(\tau+s,\tau)[f(\tau)-f(\tau_{i})]\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\|U(\tau+s,\tau)f(\tau_{i})-f(\tau_{i})\|_{\infty}+\|f(\tau)-f(\tau_{i})\|_{\infty}\bigg)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

If we let

which proves the claim.

8

Proof.

3.3
Duality and spectral relations

Let us now take the time to study the duality and spectral relations between the non-closed but closable unbounded linear operators
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\star},\mathcal{A}^{\odot}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star}
from
Section

3.1
and
Section

3.2
. Recall from the definition of the (complexified) natural dual pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:X^{\star}\times X\to\mathbb{C}
that

\displaystyle\langle U^{\star}(s-T,s)\varphi^{\star},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi^{\star},U(s+T,s)\varphi\rangle,\quad

\displaystyle\forall(\varphi^{\star},\varphi)\in X^{\star}\times X,

\displaystyle\langle A^{\star}(s)\varphi^{\star},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi^{\star},A(s)\varphi\rangle,\quad

\displaystyle\forall(\varphi^{\star},\varphi)\in\mathcal{D}(A^{\star}(s))\times\mathcal{D}(A(s)).

By construction,
U^{\star}(s-T,s)
and
A^{\star}(s)
are the unique linear operators satisfying these relations since
\mathcal{D}(A(s))
is norm dense in
X
, recall
Section

2
. Since
X^{\odot}
is again a Banach space, one can play this game once more, and obtain

\displaystyle\langle U^{\odot\star}(s+T,s)\varphi^{\odot\star},\varphi^{\star}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi^{\odot\star},U^{\odot}(s-T,s)\varphi^{\odot}\rangle,\quad

\displaystyle\forall(\varphi^{\odot\star},\varphi^{\odot})\in X^{\odot\star}\times X^{\odot},

\displaystyle\langle A^{\odot\star}(s)\varphi^{\odot\star},\varphi^{\odot}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi^{\odot\star},A^{\odot}(s)\varphi^{\odot}\rangle,\quad

\displaystyle\forall(\varphi^{\odot\star},\varphi^{\odot})\in\mathcal{D}(A^{\odot\star}(s))\times\mathcal{D}(A^{\odot}(s)),

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:X^{\odot\star}\times X^{\odot}\to\mathbb{C}
is now the (complexified) natural dual pairing between the (complexified) Banach spaces
X^{\odot}
and
X^{\odot\star}
. Again,
U^{\odot\star}(s+T,s)
and
A^{\odot\star}(s)
are the unique linear operators satisfying these relations since
\mathcal{D}(A^{\odot}(s))
is norm dense in
X^{\odot}
, recall
Section

2
.

Let us now turn our attention to the study of the (point) spectrum of the four mentioned operators of interest. As sufficiently large iterates of
U(s+T,s)
are compact, see
[
19
, Corollary XII.3.4]
and (
13
),
\sigma(U(s+T,s))
is a countable set in
\mathbb{C}
, independent of
s\in\mathbb{R}
, consisting of
0
and isolated eigenvalues of finite type (Floquet multipliers) that can possibly accumulate to
0
, recall
Section

2
. Since
U(s+T,s)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
, it follows from Schrauder’s theorem that sufficiently large iterates of
U^{\star}(s-T,s)\in\mathcal{L}(X^{\star})
are also compact and thus the spectrum of
U^{\star}(s-T,s)
is a countable set in
\mathbb{C}
, independent of
s\in\mathbb{R}
, consisting of
0
and isolated eigenvalues of finite type that can possibly accumulate to
0
. Recalling (
50
), we conclude

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{p}(U(s+T,s))&=\sigma_{p}(U^{\star}(s-T,s))\\
&=\sigma_{p}(U^{\odot}(s-T,s))\\
&=\sigma_{p}(U^{\odot\star}(s+T,s))=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\mbox{$\lambda$ is a Floquet multiplier}\},\end{split}

(80)

where
\sigma_{p}(M)
denotes the point spectrum of a bounded linear operator
M
defined on a complex Banach space
E
. The aim of this subsection is to show that a similar duality relation, spectral equality (
80
) and decomposition (
53
) hold for the operators
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\star},\mathcal{A^{\odot}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star}
. The results in this subsection will be helpful for our upcoming article on the study of bifurcations of limit cycles in classical DDEs, but are interesting on its own.

To construct a duality relation for the unbounded linear operators
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\star},\mathcal{A}^{\odot}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star}
, it is clear that we first need bilinear maps defined on the (complexified) spaces
C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\star})\times C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X)
and
C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot\star})\times C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot})
. To do this, let us introduce the (complexified) bilinear maps
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T}:C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\star})\times C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X)\to\mathbb{C}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T}:C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot\star})\times C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot})\to\mathbb{C}
, also called
pairings
, by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\varphi^{\star},\varphi\rangle_{T}&:=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\varphi^{\star}(\tau),\varphi(\tau)\rangle d\tau,\quad\forall(\varphi^{\star},\varphi)\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\star})\times C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X),\\
\langle\varphi^{\odot\star},\varphi^{\odot}\rangle_{T}&:=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\varphi^{\odot\star}(\tau),\varphi^{\odot}(\tau)\rangle d\tau,\quad\forall(\varphi^{\odot\star},\varphi^{\odot})\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot\star})\times C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot}),\end{split}

(81)

where the natural dual pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is between
X^{\star}
and
X
in the first integral, and between
X^{\odot\star}
and
X^{\odot}
in the second integral, recall
Section

2
. An important preliminary result, that we need in several upcoming results, is the following.

{lemma}

The bilinear maps
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T}
from (
81
) are nondegenerate.

Proof.

The main idea of the proof is to extend the nondegeneracy of the natural duality pairings, appearing in the integrands of (
81
), towards the pairings
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T}
. Recall from the definition of nondegeneracy of bilinear maps that one must prove that the linear maps
\varphi^{\star}\mapsto\langle\varphi^{\star},\cdot\rangle_{T}
and
\varphi\mapsto\langle\cdot,\varphi\rangle_{T}
are injective. We first prove the claim for the first pairing appearing in (
81
).

Let us start by proving that the map
\varphi^{\star}\mapsto\langle\varphi^{\star},\cdot\rangle_{T}
is injective. Suppose that
\varphi^{\star}\neq 0
, then there exists a
\tau_{0}\in(0,T)
such that
\varphi^{\star}(\tau_{0})\neq 0
. Hence, there exists by linearity a
x\in X
such that
\operatorname{Re}\langle\varphi^{\star}(\tau_{0}),x\rangle_{T}=2>0
. Because the map
\tau\mapsto\operatorname{Re}\langle\varphi^{\star}(\tau),x\rangle
is continuous, there exists a
\delta\in(0,\min\{\tau_{0},T-\tau_{0}\})
such that
\operatorname{Re}\langle\varphi^{\star}(\tau),x\rangle\geq 1
for all
\tau\in[\tau_{0}-\delta,\tau_{0}+\delta]
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
be given and consider the
X
-valued map
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
defined on
[0,T]
by

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\tau):=\begin{cases}\bigg(1-\frac{|\tau-\tau_{0}|}{\varepsilon}\bigg)x,\quad&\tau\in(\tau_{0}-\varepsilon,\tau_{0}+\varepsilon),\\
0,\quad&\tau\in[0,\tau_{0}-\varepsilon]\cup[\tau_{0}+\varepsilon,T].\end{cases}

Extending this function
T
-periodically on
\mathbb{R}
shows that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X)
. Hence, a straightforward calculation proves that
\operatorname{Re}\langle\varphi^{\star},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{T}\geq\varepsilon>0
and thus
\langle\varphi^{\star},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{T}\neq 0
. We conclude that the map
\varphi^{\star}\mapsto\langle\varphi^{\star},\cdot\rangle_{T}
is injective.

To prove that the map
\varphi\mapsto\langle\cdot,\varphi\rangle_{T}
is injective, we assume that
\varphi(\tau_{0})\neq 0
for some
\tau_{0}\in(0,T)
. By a corollary of the (geometric) Hahn-Banach theorem, there exists a
x^{\star}\in X^{\star}
such that
\operatorname{Re}\langle x^{\star},\varphi(\tau_{0})\rangle_{T}>0
. The same strategy as above can be used now to prove that
\langle\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\star},\varphi\rangle_{T}\neq 0
for some appropriate
\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\star}\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\star})
with sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.

The proof on the nondegeneracy of the second pairing in (
81
) is analogous because
X
and
X^{\odot}
are both Banach spaces.
∎

{proposition}

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}),\varphi^{\star}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\star}),\varphi^{\odot}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\odot})
and
\varphi^{\odot\star}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star})
, there holds

\langle\mathcal{A}^{\star}\varphi^{\star},\varphi\rangle_{T}=\langle\varphi^{\star},\mathcal{A}\varphi\rangle_{T},\quad\langle\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star}\varphi^{\odot\star},\varphi^{\odot}\rangle_{T}=\langle\varphi^{\odot\star},\mathcal{A}^{\odot}\varphi^{\odot}\rangle_{T}.

(82)

Moreover
\mathcal{A}^{\star}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star}
are the unique linear operators satisfying (
82
).

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
and
\varphi^{\star}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\star})
be given. Then,

\displaystyle\langle\varphi^{\star},\mathcal{A}\varphi\rangle_{T}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\varphi^{\star}(\tau),A(\tau)\varphi(\tau)-\dot{\varphi}(\tau)\rangle d\tau=-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\varphi^{\star}(\tau),\dot{\varphi}(\tau)\rangle d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}\langle\varphi^{\star}(\tau),A(\tau)\varphi(\tau)\rangle d\tau.

Performing integration by parts on the first integral shows that
\langle\varphi^{\star},\dot{\varphi}\rangle_{T}=-\langle\dot{\varphi}^{\star},\varphi\rangle_{T}
as
\varphi^{\star}
and
\varphi
are
T
-periodic. Hence,

\langle\varphi^{\star},\mathcal{A}\varphi\rangle_{T}=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{\varphi}^{\star}(\tau),\varphi(\tau)\rangle d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}\langle A^{\star}(\tau)\varphi^{\star}(\tau),\varphi(\tau)\rangle d\tau=\langle\mathcal{A}^{\star}\varphi^{\star},\varphi\rangle_{T},

which proves the claim. Let us now prove that
\mathcal{A}^{\star}
is the unique operator satisfying the first equation in (
82
). Let
\mathcal{E}^{\star}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\star})\to C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\star})
be a linear operator satisfying
\langle\mathcal{E}^{\star}\varphi^{\star},\varphi\rangle_{T}=\langle\varphi^{\star},\mathcal{A}\varphi\rangle_{T}
, or equivalently
\langle(\mathcal{E}^{\star}-\mathcal{A}^{\star})\varphi^{\star},\varphi\rangle=0
, for all
(\varphi^{\star},\varphi)\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\star})\times\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
. We claim that
\langle(\mathcal{E}^{\star}-\mathcal{A}^{\star})\varphi^{\star},\varphi\rangle=0
for all
\varphi\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X)
. Recall from
Section

3.1
that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
is norm dense in
C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X)
and so there exists for every
\varphi\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X)
a sequence
(\varphi_{m})_{m}
in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
such that
\varphi_{m}\to\varphi
in norm as
m\to\infty
. Since
(\mathcal{E}^{\star}-\mathcal{A}^{\star})\varphi^{\star}
takes values in
C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\star})
, there exists a
\psi^{\star}\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\star})
such that
(\mathcal{E}^{\star}-\mathcal{A}^{\star})\varphi^{\star}=\psi^{\star}
. As
\langle(\mathcal{E}^{\star}-\mathcal{A}^{\star})\varphi^{\star},\varphi_{m}\rangle=0
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

|\langle(\mathcal{E}^{\star}-\mathcal{A}^{\star})\varphi^{\star},\varphi\rangle_{T}|=|\langle\psi^{\star},\varphi_{m}-\varphi\rangle_{T}|\leq T\|\psi^{\star}\|_{\infty}\|\varphi-\varphi_{m}\|_{\infty},\quad m\to\infty,

which proves that
\langle(\mathcal{E}^{\star}-\mathcal{A}^{\star})\varphi^{\star},\varphi\rangle=0
for all
\varphi\in C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X)
. As
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T}
is nondegenerate by
Section

3.3
, we obtain
(\mathcal{E}^{\star}-\mathcal{A}^{\star})\varphi^{\star}=0
for all
\varphi^{\star}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\star})
and so
\mathcal{E}^{\star}=\mathcal{A}^{\star}
on
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\star})
, i.e.
\mathcal{A}^{\star}
is uniquely determined. The proof for the second identity in (
82
) and uniqueness of
\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star}
is analogous, but one uses
Section

3.2
to guarantee that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{\odot})
is norm dense in
C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot})
.
∎

The previous result shows that there exists a duality relation between, on the one hand
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\star}
, and on the other hand
\mathcal{A^{\odot}}
and

Section

3.3
and in particular
[

for more information.

Let us now study the spectra of the non-closed but closable unbounded linear operators
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\star},\mathcal{A}^{\odot}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star}

[
21
,
29
,
25
,
24
,

]

E

\mathcal{D}(A)

{proposition}

The point spectra of
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\star},\mathcal{A}^{\odot}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star}
satisfy

\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{A})=\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{A}^{\star})=\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{A}^{\odot})=\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star})=\{\sigma\in\mathbb{C}:\sigma\mbox{ is a Floquet exponent}\}.

Proof.

It follows from
Section

3.1
and

3.2
that

\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{A}),\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{A}^{\star}),\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{A}^{\odot})

\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star})

, then
\mathcal{A}\varphi=z\varphi
for some nonzero

. This equation is of the form (
27
) and so
\varphi(\tau)=e^{-z(\tau-s)}U(\tau,s)\phi(s)

for some nonzero initial condition

, defined at starting time

. As

must be

Analogous proofs to compute the point spectrum of

{proposition}

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{-}
be a Floquet multiplier with associated Floquet exponent
\sigma
of algebraic multiplicity
m_{\sigma}
and let

be the order of of the pole

where the annihilator

is defined in terms of the pairings
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T}
from (

C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X)=E_{\sigma}\oplus E_{\sigma}^{\odot\perp},\quad C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot})=E_{\sigma}^{\odot}\oplus\iota(E_{\sigma})^{\perp}.

(86)

Proof.

Let us prove the first equality of (

\varphi^{\star}\in E_{\sigma}^{\star}

\langle\varphi^{\star},(\sigma I-\mathcal{A})^{k_{\sigma}}\varphi\rangle_{T}=\langle(\sigma I-\mathcal{A}^{\star})^{k_{\sigma}}\varphi^{\star},\varphi\rangle_{T}=0

82
)
m_{\lambda}
times. The proof for the other inclusion is analogous. Taking the annihilator of the first equality in (

[

in the first inclusion and noticed from the same reference that

To prove the other inclusion, assume by contraction that there exists a

\varphi_{0}(\tau_{0})\notin R_{\lambda}(\tau_{0})

5
Conclusion and outlook

We have proven that the periodic normal forms from Iooss (and Adelmeyer)
[
32
,
33
]
for bifurcations of limit cycles in finite-dimensional ODEs suit naturally in the framework of infinite-dimensional classical DDEs. This task has been accomplished by proving two principal results: the existence of a dual periodic smooth basis for the center eigenspace (
Section

3.1
and
Section

3.2
) and the existence of a special coordinate system on the periodic center manifold (
Section

4.2
,
Section

4.2
and
Section

4.2
) in which the local dynamics near the nonhyperbolic cycle can be described. A paper providing explicit computational formulas for the critical normal form coefficients for all codimension one bifurcations of limit cycles in classical DDEs, along the lines of the periodic normalization method
[
37
,
53
,
54
]
, is in preparation.

In the present paper, we restricted ourselves to the setting of classical DDEs. However, our proof on the existence of a periodic smooth finite-dimensional center manifold near a nonhyperbolic cycle in
[
39
, Theorem 4]
is given in the general context of dual perturbation theory (sun-star calculus). As a consequence, the periodic center manifold theorem also holds for a much broader class of delay equations, as for example renewal equations
[
4
,
14
,
19
]
. The natural question arises if the results from this paper can also be generalized towards the general context of sun-star calculus. Regarding the linear part, one can not expect in general that the (adjoint) (generalized) eigenfunctions are
C^{k+1}
-smooth as this was only a consequence of applying the method of steps to periodic linear DDEs (
Section

3.1
and
Section

3.2
). This loss of smoothness of the (generalized) eigenfunctions also causes a problem in the proof of the normal forms theorems (
Section

4.2
) as the parametrization of the periodic center manifold (
93
) is then not
C^{k}
-smooth anymore. However, if one assumes that the strongly continuous evolutionary systems
U
and
U^{\odot}
are
eventually domain invariant
(there exists a
h\geq 0
such that
U(t,s)
maps
X
into
\mathcal{D}(A(s))
for
t\geq s+h
, and
U^{\odot}(s,t)
maps
X^{\odot}
into
\mathcal{D}(A^{\odot}(s))
for
s\leq t-h
) and one still allows differentiability in the strong sense, then we believe that the generators of the strongly continuous evolution semigroups
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{U}^{\odot}
are still given by the closure of the densely defined linear operators
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\odot}
, respectively. It would then be interesting to see how the theory of evolution semigroups fits into the sun-star calculus framework. This would enable us to characterize a lot of relations between
\mathcal{U}^{\star}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\star}
defined on (a subspace of)
C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\star})
, and
\mathcal{U}^{\odot\star}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\odot\star}
defined on (a subspace of)
C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X^{\odot\star})
. Afterwards, it would be interesting to study the duality and spectral relations, as performed in
Section

3.3
for classical DDEs, but in the general context of dual perturbation theory. When one does not allow differentiability in the strong sense, we can not introduce
\mathcal{A}
on a (dense) subspace of
C_{T}(\mathbb{R},X)
. Therefore, we must study this operator on a (dense) subspace of, for example, the Bochner space
L_{T}^{p}(\mathbb{R},X)
for some
1\leq p\leq\infty
. This has been done recently in
[
2
]
since evolution semigroups and their (restricted) generators are related with
L^{p}
-maximal regularity of periodic boundary value problems, see
[
42
,
1
,
20
,
5
]
for more information. There are some advantages regarding duality when one would work in the
L_{T}^{2}(\mathbb{R},X)
setting, since then the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T}
from (
81
) is precisely the (canonical) dual pairing. However, it is not clear to the authors if the normal forms from
Section

4.2
in this setting would hold, as one loses the smoothness of the Jordan chains and the center manifold parametrization (
93
).

If one is interested in bifurcations of limit cycles for systems consisting of infinite delay
[
16
,
15
,
30
,
41
]
or abstract DDEs
[
34
,
35
,
48
,
47
,
51
,
49
]
that describe for example neural fields, it is known that
\odot
-reflexivity is in general lost
[
48
, Theorem 12]
, and therefore the center manifold theorem for nonhyperbolic cycles from
[
39
]
does not directly apply. However, we believe that this technical difficulty can be resolved by employing similar techniques as in
[
35
]
. We are convinced that these techniques can also be used to prove the existence of the periodic normal forms in the setting of abstract DDEs and systems consisting of infinite delay because the proof on the existence of the periodic normal forms are written in a rather general setting of sun-star calculus.

. This yields the initial value problem

\begin{cases}d^{\star}u(s)=-A^{\star}(s)u(s)+f(s),\quad&s\leq t,\\
u(t)=\psi,\quad&\psi\in X^{\odot},\end{cases}

(113)

which suggests the variation-of-constants formula

u(s)=U^{\odot}(s,t)\psi+\int_{t}^{s}U^{\star}(s,\tau)f(\tau)d\tau,\quad\psi\in X^{\odot},

(114)

for
t\leq s
, where the integral must be interpreted as a weak
⋆
integral. This suggestion, with the additional assumptions on
f
, will be verified in this section. Let us first turn our attention towards the weak
⋆
integral appearing in (
114
).

{lemma}

Let
g:J\to X^{\star}
be a continuous function and denote the set
\{(s,r,t)\in J^{3}:s\leq r\leq t\}
by
\Theta_{J}^{\star}
. Then the map
v(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,g):\Theta_{J}^{\star}\to X^{\star}
defined as the weak
⋆
integral

v(s,r,t,g):=\int_{t}^{s}U^{\star}(r,\tau)g(\tau)d\tau,\quad\forall(s,r,t)\in\Theta_{J}^{\star},

is continuous and takes values in
X^{\odot}
.

Proof.

The statement of the theorem is a dual version of the first part of
[
39
, Lemma 1]
. The proof is along the same lines as the proof in
[
39
, Lemma 1]
, where one just has to work with the backward evolutionary system
U^{\star}
. See also
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
for a semigroup analogue of this lemma.
∎

As we have proven that the weak
⋆
integral, appearing in (
114
) is well-defined, we can turn our attention towards the verification of the variation-of-constants formula. The proof is inspired from
[
39
, Proposition 13]
and
[
35
, Proposition 21]
.

{proposition}

Let
f:J\to X^{\star}
be a continuous function. If
u
is a solution of (
113
) on
J
then
u
is given by (
114
).

Proof.

Let
(s,t)\in\Omega_{J}^{\star}
with
t>s
be arbitrary. Define the function
w:[s,t]\to X^{\star}
as
w(\tau):=U^{\star}(s,\tau)u(\tau)
for all
\tau\in[s,t]
. We claim that
w
is weak
⋆
differentiable with weak
⋆
derivative

d^{\star}w(\tau)=U^{\star}(s,\tau)d^{\star}u(\tau)+U^{\star}(s,\tau)A^{\star}(\tau)u(\tau).

(115)

To show this claim, let
\tau\in[s,t]
and
x\in X
be given. For any
h\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\tau+h\in[s,t]
we have that

\displaystyle\langle w(\tau+h)-w(\tau),x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle U^{\star}(s,\tau+h)u(\tau+h)-U^{\star}(s,\tau)u(\tau),x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle U^{\star}(s,\tau+h)[u(\tau+h)-u(\tau)],x\rangle+\langle[U^{\star}(s,\tau+h)-U^{\star}(s,\tau)]u(\tau),x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u(\tau+h)-u(\tau),U(\tau+h,s)x\rangle+\langle[U^{\star}(s,\tau+h)-U^{\star}(s,\tau)]u(\tau),x\rangle

Because
U
is a strongly continuous forward evolutionary system, we have that
U(\tau+h,s)x\to U(\tau,s)x
in norm as
h\downarrow 0
. The definition of the weak
⋆
derivative implies

\frac{1}{h}(u(\tau+h)-u(\tau))\to d^{\star}u(\tau),\quad\mbox{ weakly}^{\star}\ h\downarrow 0,

if we can show that the difference quotients remains bounded in the limit. Since
u
is a solution to (
113
), we know that
u
is weak
⋆
continuously differentiable and so locally Lipschitz continuous by
[
35
, Remark 16]
. Because
[s,t]
is compact,
u
is Lipschitz continuous on
[s,t]
and so the difference quotient remains bounded in the limit. Combining these two facts, we get

\frac{1}{h}\langle u(\tau+h)-u(\tau),U(\tau+h,s)x\rangle\to\langle d^{\star}u(\tau),U(s,\tau)x\rangle,\quad h\downarrow 0.

Furthermore, since
u(\tau)\in\mathcal{D}(A^{\star}(\tau))=\mathcal{D}(A_{0}^{\star})
(recall
Section

3.2
), it follows from
[
8
, Theorem 5.7]
that

\frac{1}{h}\langle[U^{\star}(s,\tau+h)-U^{\star}(s,\tau)]u(\tau),x\rangle\to\langle U^{\star}(s,\tau)A^{\star}(\tau)u(\tau),x\rangle,\quad h\downarrow 0.

Hence, we get

\frac{1}{h}\langle w(\tau+h)-w(\tau),x\rangle\to\langle U^{\star}(s,\tau)d^{\star}u(\tau)+U^{\star}(s,\tau)A^{\star}(\tau)u(\tau),x\rangle,\quad h\downarrow 0,

which proves (
115
). Substituting the differential equation from (
113
) into (
115
) yields

d^{\star}w(\tau)=U^{\star}(s,\tau)f(\tau)

and so
d^{\star}w
is weak
⋆
continuous since
f
was assumed to be norm continuous. For every
x\in X
we have

\displaystyle\langle u(s)-U^{\star}(s,t)u(t),x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle w(s),x\rangle-\langle w(s),x\rangle=\int_{t}^{s}\langle d^{\star}w(\tau),x\rangle d\tau=\langle\int_{t}^{s}U^{\star}(s,\tau)f(\tau)d\tau,x\rangle.

Since
x
and
(s,t)\in\Omega_{J}^{\star}
were arbitrary, we conclude that

u(s)-U^{\star}(s,t)u(t)=\int_{t}^{s}U^{\star}(s,\tau)f(\tau)d\tau,

or equivalently

u(s)=U^{\star}(s,t)\psi+\int_{t}^{s}U^{\star}(s,\tau)f(\tau)d\tau,

since
u(t)=\psi
by assumption. The continuity and range of
f
ensures from
Appendix

A
that the weak
⋆
integral takes values in
X^{\odot}
. Since
\psi\in X^{\odot}
, we have that

u(s)=U^{\odot}(s,t)\psi+\int_{t}^{s}U^{\star}(s,\tau)f(\tau)d\tau,

which completes the proof.
∎

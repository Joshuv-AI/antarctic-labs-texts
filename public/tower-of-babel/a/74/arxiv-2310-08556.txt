Linear-in-temperature conductance in two-dimensional electron fluids
Serhii Kryhin, Qiantan Hong and Leonid Levitov

Abstract
Linear temperature dependence of transport coefficients in metals is often ascribed to non-Fermi-liquid physics. Here we demonstrate the T T -linear behavior of nonlocal conductivity in a clean 2D electron fluid, where carrier collisions assist conduction and lead to hydrodynamic transport with conductance rather than resistance growing with temperature. The key aspect is the occurrence of multiple hydrodynamic modes representing odd-parity modulations of the Fermi surface evolving in space and time. A cascade of such modes results in a linear T T dependence that extends to lowest temperatures, as well as a Kolmogorov-like fractional power − 5 / 3 -5/3 scaling of conductivity vs. wavenumber. These dependences provide a smoking gun for nonclassical hydrodynamics driven by such modes, expected to be generic for 2D electron fluids with simple near-circular Fermi surfaces.

Geometry-dependent conductance

Here we detail properties of the geometry-dependent conductance referred to in the main text. Namely, for a realistic system geometry studied in Refs.

;
Kryhin2023

Specifically, we demonstrate the implications of the nonlocal conductivity scaling with

and

The geometry frequently used in transport measurements to probe hydrodynamic behavior is that of a constriction. We model it as a narrow channel of width

\ell

tomogrph
;
Kryhin2023
,
\ell\gg w

1.

The system conductance is a sum of contributions

with wavenumbers

, where the dominant contribution originates from

.

2.

Therefore, the conductance dependence on the system width

\sigma(k,T)

for

The behavior of the channel conductance can be predicted by combining these properties with the power-law dependence

for the nonlocal conductivity. This scaling relation, derived in the main text for the super FL phase for wavenumbers

As discussed in the main text, the

and

describe crossovers to the FL hydrodynamic and ballistic phases, respectively. Since at low temperatures

the crossover positions behave as

and

-linear scaling is characteristic of both the super-FL phase and the ballistic phase. It is a dominant contribution for the former and represents a residual

The inequalities

(23)

The crossover between super-FL regime and ordinary hydrodynamics happens at

(24)

The same pair of conditions on

Moving on to the analysis of the transport problem, we first describe a framework

;
Kryhin2023

\ell\gg w

The most general current-field linear response due to a space- and time-dependent external electric field

can be written as

j_{\alpha}({\boldsymbol{x}},t)=\int d^{3}{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\prime}\,\sigma_{\alpha\beta}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\prime})E_{\beta}({\boldsymbol{x}}^{\prime},t),

(25)

where indices
\alpha
and

is the non-local conductivity in real space. In the case of an infinite system, the response form is constrained by space translation and rotation symmetries as
\sigma_{\alpha\beta}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\prime})=\sigma_{ij}({\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\prime})
.

In a system of a finite size, finding the current-field response requires solving a boundary-value problem posed for the transport equations for carrier distribution with suitable boundary conditions. In general, this yields conductance that may have a nontrivial dependence on system geometry. Since this problem is well known to be mathematically challenging even when the constituting current-field response relations are local (ohms’ law), and can become considerably more cumbersome for the general nonlocal response as in Eq.(
25

Here, we employ the phenomenological method developed in

;
Kryhin2023

for a general sample geometry. The unique advantage of this method is that it utilizes the infinite-space translation-invariant conductivity to describe the geometry-dependent currents for a given system geometry.

Specifically, we focus on the conductance of a long strip of width

\delta E(x)=-\alpha\sum_{p=-\infty}^{\infty}j(x)\delta(x-wp).

(26)

The coefficient

With this

The self-consistent solution of the problem in Eqs. (

The cascade of long-lived modes and conductivity scaling

Here we detail the derivation of the scaling behavior of the nonlocal conductivity given in the main text, Eqs. (1) and (10). Our analysis relies on a continued fraction representation of conductivity.
We introduced level-
m

continued fractions
\Gamma_{m}
which obey recurrence relations, and used them to calculate
\Gamma_{m=2}

To analyze the recurrence relations obeyed by
\Gamma_{m}

\Gamma_{m}-\Gamma_{m+2}=\gamma_{m}-\frac{\gamma_{m+1}\Gamma_{m+2}^{2}}{\gamma_{m+1}\Gamma_{m+2}+z}.

\displaystyle\frac{z}{\gamma_{m+1}}u_{m+4}+\frac{z}{\gamma_{m-1}}u_{m}=\left(\gamma_{m}+\frac{z}{\gamma_{m-1}}+\frac{z}{\gamma_{m+1}}\right)u_{m+2}.

By regrouping terms, the relation above can be cast into the form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{z}{\gamma_{m-1}}+\frac{z}{\gamma_{m+1}}\right)(u_{m+4}-2u_{m+2}+u_{m})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{z}{\gamma_{m-1}}-\frac{z}{\gamma_{m+1}}\right)(u_{m+4}-u_{m})=\gamma_{m}u_{m+2}.

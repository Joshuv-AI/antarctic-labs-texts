Collective modes of nanoparticles levitated in a cavity: mechanical mode combs
M. Rademacher, T.S. Monteiro

Abstract
Levitated nanoparticles are being investigated as ultrasensitive sensors of forces and accelerations, with applications ranging from fundamental physics phenomena such as dark matter or quantum gravity to real world applications. Quantum cooling in up to 2 motional modes was achieved recently, and attention is now turning to multimode and multiparticle regimes. We investigate here the spectral characteristics of collective motion of N^{tot} = 2 − 20 \Ntot=2-20 nanoparticle modes interacting via the optical light of a cavity. We find output spectra of collective motion typically exhibit two dominant generic features: a broad spectral feature, the collective bright mode (CBM) as expected; but we introduce also the mechanical mode comb (MC) a - not previously studied- spectral feature. We investigate numerically, and can also precisely reproduce all the intricate details of the MC spectra, including ponderomotive squeezing, with a simple closed-form expression. We show the mechanical comb can autonomously repair loss of teeth due to nanoparticle loss, a feature that may offer robustness to a levitated sensor of external forces.

Theoretical model:
The linearised Hamiltonian gives rise to a set of
\Ntot+2
Langevin equations. For the modes of interest:

\displaystyle\hat{a}

\displaystyle=\textrm{i}\chi_{c}(\omega)[\sum_{j}g_{j}\hat{q}_{j}(\omega)+\sqrt{\kappa}\tilde{a}_{\textrm{in}}]

(1)

\displaystyle\hat{q}

{}_{j}=\textrm{i}g_{j}[\chi_{j}(\omega)-\chi^{*}_{j}(-\omega)](\hat{a}(\omega)+\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\omega))+\sqrt{\Gamma}\hat{X}^{th}

where
X^{th}
are thermal noise operators (see SuppInfo) corresponding to the
T=300
K Gaussian bath due to Brownian motion of background gas
molecules. We can take the corresponding mechanical damping
\Gamma_{j}\equiv\Gamma
, related to gas pressure

\Gamma\sim 15PR^{2}/M{\bar{v}}
with
R,M
the nanoparticle radius, mass and
{\bar{v}}
the mean gas velocity. The Brownian bath has phonon occupancies of
n_{B,j}=kT_{B}/(\hbar\omega_{j})

The linear amplifier model (LAM) is nothing more than the full numerical solution of the above Langevin equations by matrix inversion.
Above we have introduced the optical (cavity mode) susceptibility
\chi_{c}(\omega)=\left[-i(\omega+\Delta)+\frac{\kappa}{2}\right]^{-1}

and mechanical susceptibilities
\chi_{j}(\omega)=\left[-i(\omega-\omega_{j})+\frac{\Gamma}{2}\right]^{-1}
.

For the effective 1D model, we take
\hat{Q}\equiv\sum_{j}\hat{q}_{j}
. We can then show that the power spectral density (PSD)
for the collective motion is written:

S_{\hat{Q}\hat{Q}}(\omega)=|M_{tot}(\omega)|^{-2}\left(\Gamma S_{tot}^{\text{th}}+\kappa g_{j}^{2}|\eta_{tot}(\omega)|^{-2}|\chi_{c}(\omega)|^{2}\right)

(2)

where
S_{tot}^{\text{th}}=\sum_{j}\left[|\chi_{j}(\omega)|^{2}(n_{B,j}+1)+|\chi^{*}_{j}(-\omega)|^{2}n_{B,j}\right]

and where
M_{tot}=1+g^{2}\eta_{tot}(\omega)\eta_{c}(\omega)
, with
\eta_{tot}(\omega)=\sum_{j}\chi_{j}(\omega)-\chi^{*}_{j}(-\omega)
.
We can readily write the exact corresponding expression for a heterodyne PSD. The experimentally measured displacement PSD
of the collective coordinate will be given by
S^{het}(\omega)\approx g^{2}|\chi_{c}(\omega)|^{2}S^{meas}_{\hat{Q}\hat{Q}}(\omega)
.

By inspection, we can see that both the CBM feature as well as all the complexity and orders of magnitude variability of the widths of the Mechanical comb (MC) plotted in Fig.
1
are fully determined the rather simple analytical prefactors
|M_{tot}(\omega)|^{-2}|\chi_{j}(\omega)|^{2}

One caveat is that we assume that the relative phase of the different tweezer beams is controllable and hence neglect this effect.
While the geometric modes of a single nanoparticle are unaffected, arbitrary dephasing between different tweezers has not been investigated.

Sensing external forces with the comb:
it is of interest to consider the differences between using the MC, for detection of an external force centered on an unknown frequency
\omega\approx\omega_{ext}
and (say) an array of independent nanoparticles in
\Ntot
tweezers, in free space, also in an interval of frequencies
\omega_{j}\in[\omega_{m}\pm\Delta\omega/2]
containing
\omega\approx\omega_{ext}
; and with similar damping from feedback cooling. One notable difference is illustrated in Fig.
2
, which is the remarkable tendency of the MC to autonomously repair the loss of some of its teeth; particle loss is a perennial problem in these set-ups and the problem of efficient reloading remains to be fully addressed. In this respect, the mechanical mode comb, in the
g\sim\Delta\omega
regime, may add some robustness to particle loss.

A full study of the relative sensitivity remains for the future, but here we discuss potential sensitivity enhancements.
A key point to consider will be whether the external unknown force acts acts mainly or exclusively on a single particle, or all particles equally.
The former case might arise because the particles are naturally charged, so unless this is controlled experimentally, the most highly charged nanoparticles would be most sensitive to an external electromagnetic force. The latter case might arise from, say, a gravitational force, as all nanoparticles have comparable masses. We note there is, in general, no one to one mapping between a single frequency and a single particle or mode of the MC. In
Gosling2024
the case where the force acts on more than one mode of a single particle was investigated experimentally, including for the case of a stochastic force, in order to measure the orientation of an external force. For now, here we focus on the
\Ntot=N_{p}
case.

For the independent particle array, it is usual to consider the relation
S_{q_{j}q_{j}}(\omega)=|\chi^{(j)}(\omega)|^{2}S^{ext}_{FF,j}(\omega)
, the relation between the displacement PSD and the force spectrum, in terms of the appropriate single-particle susceptibility. The latter is broadened by a damping
\Gamma_{j}
that includes the effect of feedback cooling, for instance, but remains peaked at
\omega=\omega_{j}
.
For the MC we can write:

|\chi^{(j)}_{eff}(\omega)|^{2}\equiv|M_{tot}(\omega)|^{-2}|\chi_{j}(\omega)|^{2}

(3)

where
\chi^{(j)}_{eff}(\omega)|^{2}
includes the dampings and frequency shifts due to the optical back action function
M_{tot}
.
In both cases the effect of many particles on the detected heterodyne signal detecting single-particle forces is still additive and

S^{het}(\omega)/(g^{2}|\chi_{c}(\omega)|^{2})\approx\sum_{j}|\chi^{(j)}_{eff}(\omega)|^{2}S^{ext}_{FF,j}(\omega)
.
A harmonic force restricted to a single particle selects a single component in the sum. A harmonic force acting on several
j
will select mode components that have a significant amplitude near
\omega\approx\omega_{ext}
.

A force acting on all particles equally is a different matter. It would have
S^{het}(\omega)/(g^{2}|\chi_{c}(\omega)|^{2})\approx S^{ext}_{FF}(\omega)|\left(\sum_{j}\chi^{(j)}_{eff}(\omega)\right)|^{2}
so there is interference between the
\Ntot
components, and a resulting sensitivity enhancement relative to the
\Ntot
independent particle case is expected, for overlapping components, with constructive interference, in the collective mode case.

In conclusion, we presented an analysis of collective effects and spectral features of arrays of nanoparticles levitated in a cavity.
We introduce a not previously investigated spectral feature, the mechanical mode comb (MC) and explore its potential for sensing external forces over a band of frequencies rather than a single resonant frequency.

Vanishing of cohomology and parameter rigidity of actions of solvable Lie groups, II
Hirokazu Maruhashi

Abstract
Let M ↶ ρ 0 S M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{0}}}{{\curvearrowleft}}S be a C ∞ C^{\infty} locally free action of a connected simply connected solvable Lie group S S on a closed manifold M M . Roughly speaking, ρ 0 \rho_{0} is parameter rigid if any C ∞ C^{\infty} locally free action of S S on M M having the same orbits as ρ 0 \rho_{0} is C ∞ C^{\infty} conjugate to ρ 0 \rho_{0} . In this paper we prove two types of result on parameter rigidity. First let G G be a connected semisimple Lie group with finite center of real rank at least 2 2 without compact factors nor simple factors locally isomorphic to SO 0 ( n , 1 ) \mathop{\mathrm{SO}}\nolimits_{0}(n,1) ( n ≥ 2 ) (n\geq 2) or SU ( n , 1 ) \mathop{\mathrm{SU}}\nolimits(n,1) ( n ≥ 2 ) (n\geq 2) , and let Γ \Gamma be an irreducible cocompact lattice in G G . Let G = K ​ A ​ N G=KAN be an Iwasawa decomposition. We prove that the action Γ \ G ↶ A ​ N \Gamma\backslash G\curvearrowleft AN by right multiplication is parameter rigid. One of the three main ingredients of the proof is the rigidity theorems of Pansu and Kleiner–Leeb on the quasiisometries of Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type. Secondly we show, if M ↶ ρ 0 S M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{0}}}{{\curvearrowleft}}S is parameter rigid, then the zeroth and first cohomology of the orbit foliation of ρ 0 \rho_{0} with certain coefficients must vanish. This is a partial converse to the results in the author’s [ 17 ] , where we saw sufficient conditions for parameter rigidity in terms of vanishing of the first cohomology with various coefficients.

2.1
Leafwise cohomology

Let
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{0}}}{{\curvearrowleft}}S
be a
C^{\infty}
locally free action of a connected simply connected solvable Lie group
S
on a closed manifold
M
with the orbit foliation
\mathcal{F}
. Let
\omega_{0}\in\Gamma\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(T\mathcal{F},\mathfrak{s})\right)
denote
the canonical
1
-form of
\rho_{0}
, ie
(\omega_{0})_{x}\colon T_{x}\mathcal{F}\to\mathfrak{s}
for
x\in M
is defined as the inverse of the derivative at the identity of the map
S\to M
,
s\mapsto\rho_{0}(x,s)
. Let

d_{\mathcal{F}}\colon\Gamma\left(\bigwedge^{p}T^{*}\mathcal{F}\right)\to\Gamma\left(\bigwedge^{p+1}T^{*}\mathcal{F}\right)

be the leafwise exterior derivative of
\mathcal{F}
, defined by the same formula as the usual exterior derivative. Then
\omega_{0}
satisfies the Maurer–Cartan equation
d_{\mathcal{F}}\omega_{0}+[\omega_{0},\omega_{0}]=0
. Here
d_{\mathcal{F}}\omega_{0}
and
[\omega_{0},\omega_{0}]
are defined by

d_{\mathcal{F}}\omega_{0}(X,Y)=X\omega_{0}(Y)-Y\omega_{0}(X)-\omega_{0}\left([X,Y]\right)

and

[\omega_{0},\omega_{0}](X,Y)=\left[\omega_{0}(X),\omega_{0}(Y)\right]

for
X
,
Y\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
. Let
\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\curvearrowright}}V
be a representation of
\mathfrak{s}
on a finite dimensional real vector space
V
. Then
\pi\omega_{0}\in\Gamma\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits\left(T\mathcal{F},\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(V)\right)\right)
satisfies

d_{\mathcal{F}}\pi\omega_{0}+\left[\pi\omega_{0},\pi\omega_{0}\right]=0.

We regard
\pi\omega_{0}
as the connection form of a flat
\mathcal{F}
-partial connection
\nabla
of the trivial vector bundle
M\times V\to M
relative to any global frame of the bundle which has constant
V
components, ie
\nabla_{X}v=\pi\left(\omega_{0}(X)\right)v
for
X\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
and
v\in V
, where
v
is regarded as a section of

. Hence
\nabla\xi=d_{\mathcal{F}}\xi+\pi\omega_{0}\xi
for general
\xi\in\Gamma(V)
. The exterior derivative of
\nabla
is

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(\bigwedge^{p}T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes V\right)

\displaystyle\to\Gamma\left(\bigwedge^{p+1}T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes V\right)

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle\mapsto d_{\mathcal{F}}\omega+\pi\omega_{0}\wedge\omega,

where our definition of exterior product is

\left(\pi\omega_{0}\wedge\omega\right)\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{p+1}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{i+1}\pi\omega_{0}(X_{i})\omega\left(X_{1},\ldots,\widehat{X_{i}},\ldots,X_{p+1}\right).

The square of this operator is zero by the flatness. The cohomology
H^{*}\left(\mathcal{F};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right)
of this complex is
the leafwise cohomology of
\mathcal{F}
with coefficient
\pi
. Recall that the cohomology
H^{*}\left(\mathfrak{s};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right)
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
with coefficient
\pi
is obtained from the complex
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits\left(\bigwedge^{*}\mathfrak{s},V\right)
. We have an injective cochain map

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits\left(\bigwedge^{*}\mathfrak{s},V\right)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\Gamma\left(\bigwedge^{*}T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes V\right)

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\mapsto\omega_{0}^{*}\varphi,

where
\omega_{0}^{*}
is the pullback by
\omega_{0}
. Then by Lemma 2.1.3 of
[
17
]
, the induced map

H^{*}\left(\mathfrak{s};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right)\to H^{*}\left(\mathcal{F};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right)

is injective and we see
H^{*}\left(\mathfrak{s};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right)
as a subspace of
H^{*}\left(\mathcal{F};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right)
.

5
Necessary conditions for parameter rigidity

From this section we consider necessary conditions for parameter rigidity. (For the definition of parameter rigidity, see the beginning of Section
1
.) These necessary conditions are given by certain vanishing of zeroth and first cohomology of the orbit foliation. The main results are Theorem
30
and Theorem
35
.

Let
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{0}}}{{\curvearrowleft}}S
denote a
C^{\infty}
locally free action of a connected simply connected solvable Lie group
S
on a closed
C^{\infty}
manifold
M
, with the orbit foliation
\mathcal{F}
.

Recall that a connected simply connected solvable Lie group
S
is called
of exponential type
if the exponential map
\exp\colon\mathfrak{s}\to S
is a diffeomorphism, or equivalently, every eigenvalue of
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits X
either is
0
or has nonzero real part for each
X\in\mathfrak{s}
. For a proof of this equivalence, see Dixmier
[
4
, Théorème 3]
or Saito
[
20
]
. A derivation of a Lie algebra is called
an outer derivation
if it is not an inner derivation.

The first necessary condition is the following.

Theorem 30

(Vanishing of
H^{0}
)
.

Assume that
S
is of exponential type and there is an outer derivation of
\mathfrak{s}
. If
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{0}}}{{\curvearrowleft}}S
is parameter rigid, then
M
is connected and
H^{0}(\mathcal{F})=H^{0}(\mathfrak{s})
.

We will prove Theorem
30
in Section
6
.

Corollary 31
.

Let
N\neq 1
be a connected simply connected nilpotent Lie group and let
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{0}}}{{\curvearrowleft}}N
be a parameter rigid action. Then
M
is connected and
H^{0}(\mathcal{F})=H^{0}(\mathfrak{n})
.

Proof.

Every nonzero nilpotent Lie algebra over any field has an outer derivation. See Jacobson
[
9
]
.
∎

Note that

consists of real valued leafwise constant

functions on

and

(as a subspace of

if and only if leafwise constant

H^{0}(\mathcal{F})=H^{0}(\mathfrak{n})
.

•

H^{1}(\mathcal{F})=H^{1}(\mathfrak{n})
.

Hence we have the following.

Corollary 34
.

Let

be a connected simply connected nilpotent Lie group, and let

be a

locally free action. Then the following are equivalent:

•

The action
\rho_{0}
is parameter rigid.

•

H^{1}(\mathcal{F})=H^{1}(\mathfrak{n})
.

Proof.

This is true even if

.
∎

Theorem 35

(Vanishing of
H^{1}
)
.

Let

be an
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits
-invariant subspace (ie an ideal of
\mathfrak{s}
) for which
\mathfrak{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}}{{\curvearrowright}}V
is trivial. Assume that any eigenvalue of

on

either is

or has nonzero real part for any

. If

is parameter rigid, then we have

H^{1}\left(\mathcal{F};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right)=H^{0}(\mathcal{F})\otimes H^{1}\left(\mathfrak{s};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right).

Note that the assumption is weaker than the assumption that

is of exponential type, as it allows

to have purely imaginary nonzero eigenvalues.

Here an element
[\omega]\in H^{1}\left(\mathcal{F};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right)
is in
H^{0}(\mathcal{F})\otimes H^{1}\left(\mathfrak{s};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right)
if and only if
[\omega]
is represented by
a leafwise constant form
, that is, represented by a form
\phi\circ\omega_{0}
for some
C^{\infty}
leafwise constant map
\phi\colon M\to\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(\mathfrak{s},V)
. If we assume also that
\mathfrak{s}
has an outer derivation, then by Theorem
30
, the conclusion simplifies to

.

Let us consider the coefficients appearing Theorem
35
. We have
V\subset\mathfrak{n}
, thus
V
is contained in the center of
\mathfrak{n}
, and is an abelian ideal of
\mathfrak{s}
. (For the first part, if not, take

, then
\mathfrak{n}+\mathbb{R}X
would be a nilpotent ideal of
\mathfrak{s}
which is larger than the nilradical
\mathfrak{n}
.)

As an example of a coefficient

satisfying the property, we can take

, where
\mathfrak{n}\supset\mathfrak{n}^{2}\supset\cdots\supset\mathfrak{n}^{s}\supset 0
is the lower central series of

.

As a more concrete example, we consider the

-dimensional solvable Lie algebra

defined by

. Then the

-dimensional representation

6
Vanishing of
H^{0}
—proof of Theorem
30

The proof of Theorem
30
is immediate after proving Lemma
40
, whose proof is the main part of this section. Several lemmas before Lemma
40
prepare an “integration” map
\mu
, which will be used in the proof of Lemma
40
. Sublemma
Sublemma
inside Lemma
40
is similar to Lemma
45
in the next section and the same kind of argument already appeared in Maruhashi
[
16
]
when the vanishing of
H^{1}
was proved under the assumption of parameter rigidity together with the vanishing of
H^{0}
for actions of nilpotent Lie groups.

Let
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{0}}}{{\curvearrowleft}}S
be a
C^{\infty}
locally free action of a connected simply connected solvable Lie group
S
on a closed
C^{\infty}
manifold
M
, with the orbit foliation
\mathcal{F}
and the canonical
1
-form
\omega_{0}
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\curvearrowright}}V
be a finite dimensional real representation, and let
S\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Pi}}{{\curvearrowright}}V
denote the representation whose differentiation is
\pi
. Then the trivial bundle
M\times V\to M
is an
S
-equivariant vector bundle with the action defined by

(x,v)s=\left(\rho_{0}(x,s),\Pi\left(s^{-1}\right)v\right).

Let
\Gamma_{blc}(V)
be the space of all bounded sections of
M\times V\to M
which are continuous on each leaf. (An element
\xi\in\Gamma_{blc}(V)
can be discontinuous on
M
.) We have a representation
S\curvearrowright\Gamma_{blc}(V)
by

\left(s\xi\right)(x)=\Pi(s)\xi\left(\rho_{0}(x,s)\right)

for
s\in S
,
\xi\in\Gamma_{blc}(V)
and
x\in M
. We equip
V
with a norm coming from an inner product. Then
\Gamma_{blc}(V)
is a Banach space with the supremum norm. Let
\Gamma_{lc}(V)
be the closed subspace of
\Gamma_{blc}(V)
which consists of bounded leafwise constant sections.

Lemma 36
.

There is an
S
-equivariant continuous linear map

\mu\colon\Gamma_{blc}(V)\to\Gamma_{lc}(V)

which is the identity on
\Gamma_{lc}(V)
.

Proof.

Since
S
is amenable, by one of the characterizations of amenability, we have a bi-invariant mean
\mu_{0}\colon C_{b}(S)\to\mathbb{R}
on the space
C_{b}(S)
of all bounded continuous real valued functions on
S
. See Page 26–29 of Greenleaf
[
6
]
. Recall that
\mu_{0}(1)=1
and its operator norm is
1
. Take a basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
of
V
. For
\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}v_{i}\in\Gamma_{blc}(V)
and
x\in M
, we define

\mu(\xi)(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{0}\left(f_{i}\left(\rho_{0}(x,\cdotp)\right)\right)v_{i}.

Then this is independent of a choice of a basis of
V
. We have

\mu(\xi)\left(\rho_{0}(x,s)\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{0}\left(f_{i}\left(\rho_{0}(x,s\ \cdot\ )\right)\right)v_{i}=\mu(\xi)(x)

by left invariance, and

\mu(s\xi)(x)=\Pi(s)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{0}\left(f_{i}\left(\rho_{0}(x,\cdot\ s)\right)\right)v_{i}=s\mu(\xi)(x)

by right invariance. We also have
\mu(\xi)=\xi
for
\xi\in\Gamma_{lc}(V)
since
\mu_{0}(1)=1
. By taking
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
to be an orthonormal basis and using
\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|=1
, we see

\left\lVert\mu(\xi)(x)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert f_{i}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\leq n\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}.

∎

Let
\nabla
denote the flat leafwise connection of
M\times V\to M
defined by
\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\curvearrowright}}V
.

Lemma 37
.

For
v\in V
,
x_{0}\in M
and sufficiently small
s\in S
, the locally defined section

\xi_{0}\left(\rho_{0}(x_{0},s)\right)=\left(\rho_{0}(x_{0},s),\Pi\left(s^{-1}\right)v\right)

of
M\times V\to M
on the leaf containing
x_{0}
, is a parallel section for
\nabla
, that is,
\nabla\xi_{0}=0
.

Proof.

For any
y=\rho_{0}(x_{0},s_{0})
with small
s_{0}\in S
and any
X\in\mathfrak{s}
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\left.\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{0}\left(y,e^{tX}\right)\right|_{t=0}}\xi_{0}

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{F}}\xi_{0}\left(\left.\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{0}\left(y,e^{tX}\right)\right|_{t=0}\right)+\pi(X)\xi_{0}(y)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\Pi\left(e^{-tX}s_{0}^{-1}\right)v\right|_{t=0}+\pi(X)\Pi\left(s_{0}^{-1}\right)v

\displaystyle=0.

∎

Therefore, the directions of orbits of the action
M\times V\curvearrowleft S
are horizontal for the leafwise connection
\nabla
. By the expression of covariant derivative by parallel transport, we have

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{X}\xi\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\Pi\left(e^{tX}\right)\xi\left(\rho_{0}\left(x,e^{tX}\right)\right)-\xi(x)}{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\left(e^{tX}\xi\right)(x)-\xi(x)}{t}

for any
\xi\in\Gamma(V)
,
X\in\mathfrak{s}
and
x\in M
. Note that
X\in\mathfrak{s}
is regarded as
X\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
using the locally free action
\rho_{0}
.

Lemma 38
.

For any
\xi\in\Gamma(V)
and
X\in\mathfrak{s}
,
\frac{e^{tX}\xi-\xi}{t}
converges uniformly to
\nabla_{X}\xi
as
t\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

Take a basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
of
V
and write
\left(e^{tX}\xi\right)(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(t,x)v_{i}
for some
C^{\infty}
functions
f_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}\times M\to\mathbb{R}
. Then we have
\left(\nabla_{X}\xi\right)(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}^{\prime}(0,x)v_{i}
. The function
f_{i}(t,x)
has the Taylor expansion

f_{i}(t,x)=f_{i}(0,x)+tf_{i}^{\prime}(0,x)+\frac{t^{2}}{2}f_{i}^{\prime\prime}\left(\theta_{i,x,t},x\right),

where
\theta_{i,x,t}
is a number between
0
and
t
. Since

\frac{\left(e^{tX}\xi\right)(x)-\xi(x)}{t}-\left(\nabla_{X}\xi\right)(x)=\frac{t}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}^{\prime\prime}\left(\theta_{i,x,t},x\right)v_{i}

and
f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(\theta,x)
is bounded for
-1\leq\theta\leq 1
and

, we get the conclusion.
∎

Lemma 39
.

Let
\mu\colon\Gamma_{blc}(V)\to\Gamma_{lc}(V)
be the map in Lemma
36
. Then

\mu\left(\nabla_{X}\xi\right)=\nabla_{X}\mu(\xi)

for all
\xi\in\Gamma(V)
and
X\in\mathfrak{s}
. (Note that
\mu(\xi)
might be discontinuous on
M
.)

Proof.

By Lemma
38
,
\frac{e^{tX}\xi-\xi}{t}
converges uniformly to
\nabla_{X}\xi
as
t\rightarrow 0
. By continuity and equivariance, we have

\mu\left(\nabla_{X}\xi\right)=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{e^{tX}\mu(\xi)-\mu(\xi)}{t}=\nabla_{X}\mu(\xi).

∎

Lemma 40
.

Assume that
S
is of exponential type. Let
\Psi\colon M\to\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(S)
be a

map which is constant on each leaf of

. If

is parameter rigid, then
\overline{\Psi}\colon M\to\mathop{\mathrm{Out}}\nolimits(S)
is constant on

, where bar denotes the projection
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(S)\to\mathop{\mathrm{Out}}\nolimits(S)
. In particular, if
\mathop{\mathrm{Out}}\nolimits(S)\neq 1
,

must be connected.

Proof.

Define
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\curvearrowleft}}S
by
\rho(x,s)=\rho_{0}\left(x,\Psi_{x}^{-1}(s)\right)
. This defines an action because

is leafwise constant:

\displaystyle\rho\left(x,ss^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\rho_{0}\left(\rho_{0}\left(x,\Psi_{x}^{-1}(s)\right),\Psi_{x}^{-1}\left(s^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\rho_{0}\left(\rho(x,s),\Psi_{\rho(x,s)}^{-1}\left(s^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\rho(x,s),s^{\prime}\right).

Since
\rho

locally free action with the same orbit foliation as

,

by parameter rigidity. Note that

is the canonical

. By Proposition 1.4.4 of Asaoka
[
2
]
, there exist

and a

map

such that

\Psi_{x*}\omega_{0}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(P^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}+P^{*}\Theta,

(13)

where

denotes the left Maurer–Cartan form of

. (In
[

]
,

is referred to as an endomorphism, but it is the same

appearing in the definition of parameter equivalence which we saw in Section

in
[

Let

denote both projections

and

, where
\mathfrak{n}
is the nilradical of
\mathfrak{s}
and

is the Lie subgroup corresponding to

. By projecting (

(14)

since

is abelian. For any

,

and

, we integrate (
14
) over the curve

for

. Then noting

being leafwise constant,

Since

is bounded due to the compactness of

, we must have

and

is leafwise constant. Hence there exists a leafwise constant

map

such that

. Since

is leafwise constant, we have

and (

\Psi_{x*}\omega_{0}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(Q^{-1}\right)\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(R^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}+Q^{*}\Theta.

(15)

Let
\mathfrak{n}\supset\mathfrak{n}^{2}\supset\cdots\supset\mathfrak{n}^{s}\supset 0
be the lower central series of
\mathfrak{n}
. Recall that
\exp\colon\mathfrak{n}\to N
is a diffeomorphism and

is defined.

Sublemma
.

Assume that there exist a
C^{\infty}
map
Q\colon M\to N
and a leafwise constant
C^{\infty}
map
R\colon M\to S
such that:

•

\Psi_{x*}\omega_{0}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(Q^{-1}\right)\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(R^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}+Q^{*}\Theta

•

\log Q\in\mathfrak{n}^{k}
for some

.

Then we can find a
C^{\infty}
map

and a leafwise constant
C^{\infty}
map
R^{\prime}\colon M\to S
such that:

•

\Psi_{x*}\omega_{0}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(\left(Q^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\right)\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(\left(R^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}+\left(Q^{\prime}\right)^{*}\Theta

•

\log Q^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}
.

Proof.

Take subspaces
V_{0},\ldots,V_{s}
such that
\mathfrak{s}=V_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{i}=V_{i}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{i+1}
for

. We can write
Q=\exp\left(\sum_{i=k}^{s}Q_{i}\right)
for some
C^{\infty}
maps
Q_{i}\colon M\to V_{i}
. We will calculate the
V_{k}
component of

\Psi_{x*}\omega_{0}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(Q^{-1}\right)\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(R^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}+Q^{*}\Theta.

(16)

First note that

Q^{*}\Theta\equiv d_{\mathcal{F}}Q_{k}\ \ \ \ \text{mod $\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}$}.

In fact, for all
X=\left.\frac{d}{dt}x(t)\right|_{t=0}\in T_{x}\mathcal{F}
,

\displaystyle Q^{*}\Theta(X)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}Q(x)^{-1}Q\left(x(t)\right)\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\exp\left(-\sum_{i=k}^{s}Q_{i}(x)\right)\exp\left(\sum_{i=k}^{s}Q_{i}\left(x(t)\right)\right)\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\exp\left(\sum_{i=k}^{s}\left(Q_{i}\left(x(t)\right)-Q_{i}(x)\right)+\text{an element of $\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}$}\right)\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\exp\left(Q_{k}\left(x(t)\right)-Q_{k}(x)+\text{an element of $\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}$}\right)\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle\equiv d_{\mathcal{F}}Q_{k}(X)\ \ \ \ \text{mod $\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}$}.

Let
\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{k}^{0}}}{{\curvearrowright}}V_{k}
be the representation obtained from
\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}}{{\curvearrowright}}\mathfrak{n}^{k}/\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}
by the identification
V_{k}\simeq\mathfrak{n}^{k}/\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}
. Put
\pi_{k}=\pi_{k}^{0}\circ\Phi_{*}
. We take
\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{k}}}{{\curvearrowright}}V_{k}
as
\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\curvearrowright}}V
considered in the beginning of this section; we let
\nabla
be the leafwise connection defined by
\pi_{k}
, and we let
\mu\colon\Gamma_{blc}(V_{k})\to\Gamma_{lc}(V_{k})
be the map in Lemma
36
.

Write
\Psi_{x*}\omega_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\alpha_{i}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(R^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\beta_{i}
according to the decomposition
\mathfrak{s}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{s}V_{i}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(Q^{-1}\right)\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(R^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits\left(-\sum_{i=k}^{s}Q_{i}\right)\right)\sum_{i=0}^{s}\beta_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta_{i}+\left[\beta_{0},Q_{k}\right]\ \ \ \ \text{mod $\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}$}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta_{i}+\pi_{k}^{0}\beta_{0}Q_{k}\ \ \ \ \text{mod $\mathfrak{n}^{k+1}$}.

Take the
V_{k}
components of (

) to get

\alpha_{k}=\beta_{k}+\pi_{k}^{0}\beta_{0}Q_{k}+d_{\mathcal{F}}Q_{k}.

Since

\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(R^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}\equiv\beta_{0}\ \ \ \ \text{mod $\mathfrak{n}$}

and

vanishes on
\mathfrak{n}
, we have
\pi_{k}\omega_{0}=\pi_{k}^{0}\beta_{0}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\nabla Q_{k}

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{F}}Q_{k}+\pi_{k}\omega_{0}Q_{k}

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{F}}Q_{k}+\pi_{k}^{0}\beta_{0}Q_{k},

and we get

\alpha_{k}=\beta_{k}+\nabla Q_{k}.

(17)

Hence

\alpha_{k}(X)=\beta_{k}(X)+\nabla_{X}Q_{k}

for any

. Note that
\alpha_{k}(X)
and
\beta_{k}(X)
are leafwise constant because

is leafwise constant. Applying

and using Lemma

\alpha_{k}(X)=\beta_{k}(X)+\nabla_{X}\mu(Q_{k}).

Therefore,

\nabla_{X}\left(Q_{k}-\mu(Q_{k})\right)=0.

Put
Q_{k}^{\prime}=Q_{k}-\mu(Q_{k})
. We shall see

is leafwise constant. Let

be the representation with the derivative
\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{k}}}{{\curvearrowright}}V_{k}
. Then for any

and

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}\left(e^{sX}Q_{k}^{\prime}\right)(x)

which turns

&\ddots&\\

\begin{pmatrix}e^{ta}R_{t}&&*\\
&\ddots&\\

where

if the eigenvalue

is not real. Since this must be bounded for all

,

, which implies the corresponding components of

On the other hand, for a Jordan block with the eigenvalue
0
, the corresponding components in
e^{-t\pi_{k}(X)}Q_{k}^{\prime}(x)
is

\begin{pmatrix}1&&*\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}\\
\vdots\\

where the entries in the

So

\Psi_{x*}\omega_{0}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(\left(Q^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\right)\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(\left(R^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}+\left(Q^{\prime}\right)^{*}\Theta,

where

∎

Applying Sublemma

\Psi_{x*}\omega_{0}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(R^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}

is equal to

Theorem 41
.

Assume that
S
is of exponential type and there is an outer derivation of
\mathfrak{s}
. If
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{0}}}{{\curvearrowleft}}S
is parameter rigid, then
M
is connected and
H^{0}(\mathcal{F})=H^{0}(\mathfrak{s})
.

Proof.

Since there is an outer derivation of
\mathfrak{s}
, the outer automorphism group
\mathop{\mathrm{Out}}\nolimits(S)
of
S
is nontrivial, hence
M
is connected. Take an outer derivation
\varphi
of
\mathfrak{s}
and set
\Phi_{t}=e^{t\varphi}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(S)
. For any
f\in H^{0}(\mathcal{F})
, consider a map
M\to\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(S)
defined by
x\mapsto\Phi_{f(x)}
. Since this is leafwise constant,
x\mapsto\overline{\Phi_{f(x)}}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Out}}\nolimits(S)
is constant by Lemma
40
. Let
\mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(S)
denote the inner automorphism group of
S
. This is a connected normal Lie subgroup of
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(S)
. We must be a bit careful because
\mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(S)
might not be closed in
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(S)
in general. See Hochschild
[
8
]
. But the cosets of
\mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(S)
defines a foliation on
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(S)
and
\Phi_{t}
is a curve transverse to the foliation. Since the automorphisms
\Phi_{f(x)}
for all

are contained in a single leaf of
\mathcal{F}
and
M
is connected,
\Phi_{f(x)}
must be constant with respect to
x
. This implies
f
is constant over
M
.
∎

Lemma 42
.

Assume
H^{0}(\mathcal{F})=H^{0}(\mathfrak{s})
. Let
\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\curvearrowright}}V
be a representation for which
\pi(X)
has no nonzero purely imaginary eigenvalues for each
X\in\mathfrak{s}
. Then
H^{0}(\mathcal{F};\pi)=H^{0}(\mathfrak{s};\pi)
.

Proof.

Take
\xi\in H^{0}(\mathcal{F};\pi)
. The function
\xi
satisfies
d_{\mathcal{F}}\xi+\pi\omega_{0}\xi=0
. This means
X\xi+\pi(X)\xi=0
for all
X\in\mathfrak{s}
. For each
x\in M
this is solved as
\xi\left(\rho_{0}\left(x,e^{tX}\right)\right)=e^{-t\pi(X)}\xi(x)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. As in the proof of Sublemma
Sublemma
we transform
\pi(X)
into a real Jordan normal form and
\xi
being bounded implies
\xi\left(\rho_{0}\left(x,e^{tX}\right)\right)
must be constant. Therefore,
\xi
is leafwise constant. By the assumption
H^{0}(\mathcal{F})=H^{0}(\mathfrak{s})
,
\xi
is constant on
M
. Hence
\xi\in V
and
\pi(X)\xi=0
for all

, which shows
\xi\in H^{0}(\mathfrak{s};\pi)
.
∎

Theorem 43
.

Let
V\subset\mathfrak{s}
be an
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits
-invariant subspace (ie an ideal of
\mathfrak{s}
) for which
\mathfrak{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}}{{\curvearrowright}}V
is trivial. Assume that any eigenvalue of
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits X
on
\mathfrak{s}/V
either is
0
or has nonzero real part for any
X\in\mathfrak{s}
. If
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{0}}}{{\curvearrowleft}}S
is parameter rigid, then we have

H^{1}\left(\mathcal{F};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right)=H^{0}(\mathcal{F})\otimes H^{1}\left(\mathfrak{s};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right).

Proof.

Take any
[\omega]\in H^{1}\left(\mathcal{F};\mathfrak{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits}}{{\curvearrowright}}V\right)
. Let
\omega_{0}
be the canonical
1
-form of
\rho_{0}
. Fix an
\epsilon>0
and put
\eta:=\omega_{0}+\epsilon\omega\in\Gamma\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits\left(T\mathcal{F},\mathfrak{s}\right)\right)
. Let us see
\eta
satisfies the Maurer–Cartan equation. As we saw in Section
5
,
V
is abelian and then

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{F}}\eta+[\eta,\eta]

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{F}}\omega_{0}+\epsilon d_{\mathcal{F}}\omega+[\omega_{0},\omega_{0}]+\epsilon\left([\omega_{0},\omega]+[\omega,\omega_{0}]\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon\left(d_{\mathcal{F}}\omega+[\omega_{0},\omega]+[\omega,\omega_{0}]\right).

But this is zero because
\omega
satisfies
d_{\mathcal{F}}\omega+(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits\omega_{0})\wedge\omega=0
and
(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits\omega_{0})\wedge\omega=[\omega_{0},\omega]+[\omega,\omega_{0}]
.

Since
M
is compact, we can assume
\eta_{x}\colon T_{x}\mathcal{F}\to\mathfrak{s}
is bijective for all
x\in M
by taking
\epsilon>0
small enough. Then there exists a unique action
\rho
of
S
on
M
whose orbit foliation is
\mathcal{F}
and whose canonical
1
-form is
\eta
. See Asaoka
[
2
, Proposition 1.4.3]
. By parameter rigidity,
\rho
is parameter equivalent to
\rho_{0}
. Thus by Proposition 1.4.4 of
[
2
]
, there exist a
C^{\infty}
map
P\colon M\to S
and an automorphism
\Phi
of
S
satisfying

\omega_{0}+\epsilon\omega=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(P^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}+P^{*}\Theta,

(18)

where
\Theta
is the left Maurer–Cartan form of
S
. By seeing this equation modulo
\mathfrak{n}
, we get

\omega_{0}\equiv\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}+d_{\mathcal{F}}\overline{P}\ \ \ \ \text{mod $\mathfrak{n}$},

where bar denotes the projection
S\to S/N
. The same argument as in the proof of vanishing of
H^{0}
yields

\omega_{0}\equiv\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}\ \ \ \ \text{mod $\mathfrak{n}$},

d_{\mathcal{F}}\overline{P}\equiv 0\ \ \ \ \text{mod $\mathfrak{n}$}.

So we can take a leafwise constant
C^{\infty}
map
R\colon M\to S
such that
Q:=R^{-1}P\in N
. Then Equation (
18
) becomes

\displaystyle\omega_{0}+\epsilon\omega

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(Q^{-1}R^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}\omega_{0}+\left(RQ\right)^{*}\Theta

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(Q^{-1}\right)\Psi_{*}\omega_{0}+Q^{*}\Theta,

where
\Psi_{*}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(R^{-1}\right)\Phi_{*}
is leafwise constant.

Lemma 44
.

There exists a filtration

\mathfrak{s}\supset\mathfrak{n}=W_{1}\supset W_{2}\supset\cdots\supset W_{s}=V\supset W_{s+1}=0,

where
W_{i}
’s are ideals of
\mathfrak{s}
such that
[\mathfrak{n},W_{i}]\subset W_{i+1}
.

Proof.

If

\mathfrak{n}\supset\mathfrak{n}^{2}\supset\cdots\supset\mathfrak{n}^{s-1}\supset 0

denotes the lower central series of
\mathfrak{n}
, then the filtration

\mathfrak{s}\supset\mathfrak{n}\supset\mathfrak{n}^{2}+V\supset\mathfrak{n}^{3}+V\supset\cdots\supset\mathfrak{n}^{s-1}+V\supset V\supset 0

gives the desired filtration.
∎

Note that we have
\omega_{0}\equiv\Psi_{*}\omega_{0}
modulo
W_{1}
.

Lemma 45
.

Assume there exist a
C^{\infty}
map
Q\colon M\to N
and a leafwise constant
C^{\infty}
map
\Psi\colon M\to\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(S)
such that

\omega_{0}+\epsilon\omega=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(Q^{-1}\right)\Psi_{*}\omega_{0}+Q^{*}\Theta,

(19)

\log Q\in W_{k}

and

\omega_{0}\equiv\Psi_{*}\omega_{0}\ \ \ \ \text{mod $W_{k}$: }

1.

If
k<s
, then there exist a
C^{\infty}
map
Q^{\prime}\colon M\to N
and a leafwise constant
C^{\infty}
map
\Psi^{\prime}\colon M\to\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(S)
such that

\omega_{0}+\epsilon\omega=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(\left(Q^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\right)\Psi^{\prime}_{*}\omega_{0}+\left(Q^{\prime}\right)^{*}\Theta,

\log Q^{\prime}\in W_{k+1}

and

\omega_{0}\equiv\Psi^{\prime}_{*}\omega_{0}\ \ \ \ \text{mod $W_{k+1}$}.

2.

If
k=s
, then
\omega
is cohomologous to a leafwise constant cocycle.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Sublemma
Sublemma
.
Take complementary subspaces
V_{i}
’s so that
\mathfrak{s}=V_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
and

\displaystyle\omega_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\alpha_{i},\quad\Psi_{*}\omega_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\beta_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad Q=\exp\left(\sum_{i=k}^{s}Q_{i}\right)

according to the decomposition
\mathfrak{s}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{s}V_{i}
.

The same calculation as in Sublemma
Sublemma
gives

Q^{*}\Theta\equiv d_{\mathcal{F}}Q_{k}\ \ \ \ \text{mod $W_{k+1}$}.

We have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits\left(Q^{-1}\right)\Psi_{*}\omega_{0}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits\left(-\sum_{i=k}^{s}Q_{i}\right)\right)\sum_{i=0}^{s}\beta_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta_{i}+[\beta_{0},Q_{k}]\ \ \ \ \text{mod $W_{k+1}$}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}+\beta_{k}+[\alpha_{0},Q_{k}]\ \ \ \ \text{mod $W_{k+1}$}.

Thus

If

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\epsilon^{-1}Q_{s}\right)

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\epsilon^{-1}Q_{s}\right)+\left[\omega_{0},\epsilon^{-1}Q_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\epsilon^{-1}Q_{s}\right)+\left[\alpha_{0},\epsilon^{-1}Q_{s}\right].

\omega
is cohomologous to
\epsilon^{-1}\left(\beta_{s}-\alpha_{s}\right)

If

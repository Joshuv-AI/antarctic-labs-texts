The relativistic quantum Boltzmann equation near equilibrium
Gi-Chan Bae, Jin Woo Jang, Seok-Bae Yun

Abstract
. The relativistic quantum Boltzmann equation (or the relativistic Uehling-Uhlenbeck equation) describes the dynamics of single-species fast-moving quantum particles. With the recent development of the relativistic quantum mechanics, the relativistic quantum Boltzmann equation has been widely used in physics and engineering such as in the quantum collision experiments and the simulations of electrons in graphene. In spite of such importance, there has been no mathematical theory on the existence of solutions for the relativistic quantum Boltzmann equation to the best of authors’ knowledge. In this paper, we prove the global existence of a unique classical solution to the relativistic Boltzmann equation for both bosons and fermions when the initial distribution is nearby a global equilibrium.



1.
Introduction

The dynamics of single-species fast-moving quantum particles is described by the relativistic quantum Boltzmann equation:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}p^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}F=p^{0}\partial_{t}F+p\cdot\nabla_{x}F=C(F,F,F,F),\end{split}

where
F(x,p,t)
is a momentum distribution function on the phase point
(x,p)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
at time
t\in[0,\infty)
.
The relativistic quantum collision operator is given by

(1.2)

C(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq}{q^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dp^{\prime}}{p^{\prime 0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{q^{\prime 0}}W(p,q|p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\cr\times\big[F_{1}(p^{\prime})F_{2}(q^{\prime})(1+\tau F_{3}(p))(1+\tau F_{4}(q))-(1+\tau F_{1}(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau F_{2}(q^{\prime}))F_{3}(p)F_{4}(q)\big],

where
\tau=+1
for bosons, and
\tau=-1
for fermions.
When the functions
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
,
F_{3}
and
F_{4}
are identical, we denote
C(F,F,F,F)
as
C(F)
. The transition rate
W(p,q|p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
is defined as

(1.3)

\displaystyle W(p,q|p^{\prime},q^{\prime})=\frac{c}{2}s\sigma(g,\theta)\delta^{(4)}(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}-q^{\prime\mu}),

where
\sigma(g,\theta)
denotes the differential cross-section which describes the collisions between particles. The
4
-dimensional Dirac-delta function implies the conservation laws of momentum and energy. The precise definitions of
s
and
\sigma(g,\theta)
are given in Section
1.2
.

1.2.
Notations

Before introducing our main results, we define several notations on the relativistic quantities. First of all, we remark that we normalize all physical constants to
1
throughout the paper including the speed of light
c
. We denote the energy-momentum
4
-vector as
p^{\mu}
and usually write it as
p^{\mu}=(p^{0},p^{1},p^{2},p^{3})
. The energy-momentum
4
-vector with the lower index is written as a product in the Minkowski metric
p_{\mu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\nu}
, where the Minkowski metric is given by
\eta_{\mu\nu}=diag(-1,1,1,1)
.
The inner product of energy-momentum
4
-vectors
p^{\mu}
and
q_{\mu}
is defined via the Minkowski metric:

\displaystyle p^{\mu}q_{\mu}=p^{\mu}\eta_{\mu\mu}q^{\mu}=-p^{0}q^{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}p^{i}q^{i}.

The energy of a relativistic particle is given by
p^{0}=\sqrt{1+|p|^{2}}
. Then we can see that the inner product of an energy-momentum
4
-vector with itself is
p^{\mu}p_{\mu}=-1
. We note that the inner product of energy-momentum
4
-vectors is Lorentz invariant
p^{\mu}q_{\mu}=\Lambda p^{\mu}\Lambda q_{\mu}
, where
\Lambda
is a Lorentz transform which will be defined below.

We define the relative energy
s
between two energy-momentum 4-vectors
p^{\mu}
and
q^{\mu}
as

(1.4)

\displaystyle s(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})=-(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu})(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})=-2p^{\mu}q_{\mu}+2,

and the relative momentum
g
between them as

(1.5)

\displaystyle g(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})=\sqrt{(p^{\mu}-q^{\mu})(p_{\mu}-q_{\mu})}=\sqrt{2(-p^{\mu}q_{\mu}-1)}.

Note that
s=g^{2}+4
. In the same sense, we define the relative momentum between
p^{\mu}
and another momentum
p^{\prime\mu}
and
q^{\prime\mu}
as
\bar{g}=g(p^{\mu},p^{\prime\mu})
and
\tilde{g}=g(p^{\mu},q^{\prime\mu})
, respectively. Also, we define
\bar{s}=s(p^{\mu},p^{\prime\mu})
and
\tilde{s}=s(p^{\mu},q^{\prime\mu})
, so that

\bar{s}=\bar{g}^{2}+4
and
\tilde{s}=\tilde{g}^{2}+4
.

1.3.
Conservation laws and the Boltzmann
H
-theorem

The conservation law of the pre-post collisional energy-momentum
4
-vectors is given by

(1.6)

\displaystyle p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}=p^{\prime\mu}+q^{\prime\mu}.

The inner product with itself gives

\displaystyle(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu})(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})=(p^{\prime\mu}+q^{\prime\mu})(p^{\prime}_{\mu}+q^{\prime}_{\mu}).

Using
p^{\mu}p_{\mu}=p^{\prime\mu}p^{\prime}_{\mu}=q^{\mu}q_{\mu}=q^{\prime\mu}q^{\prime}_{\mu}=-1
, we have
p^{\mu}q_{\mu}=p^{\prime\mu}q^{\prime}_{\mu}
. Similarly, we can have
p^{\mu}p^{\prime}_{\mu}=q^{\mu}q^{\prime}_{\mu}
and
p^{\mu}q^{\prime}_{\mu}=p^{\prime\mu}q_{\mu}
, which implies

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}g&=g(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})=g(p^{\prime\mu},q^{\prime\mu}),\cr\bar{g}&=g(p^{\mu},p^{\prime\mu})=g(q^{\mu},q^{\prime\mu}),\cr\tilde{g}&=g(p^{\mu},q^{\prime\mu})=g(p^{\prime\mu},q^{\mu}).\end{split}

Then it turns out
[
47
, Proposition 2.7]
that (
1.6
) can further imply the
Pythagorean
theorem as

(1.8)

\displaystyle g^{2}=\bar{g}^{2}+\tilde{g}^{2}.

The angle of the scattering kernel
\sigma(g,\theta)
in (
1.3
) is given by

(1.9)

\displaystyle\cos\theta=\frac{(p^{\mu}-q^{\mu})(p^{\prime}_{\mu}-q^{\prime}_{\mu})}{g^{2}}.

By (
1.7
) and (
1.8
),
\cos\theta
can also be written in the following form
[
18
, page 277, (A.11)]
:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\cos\theta=1-2\frac{\bar{g}^{2}}{g^{2}}.

Now we introduce the
center-of-momentum
framework. The relativistic pre-post collisional momenta
p
,
q
and
p^{\prime}
,
q^{\prime}
satisfying the conservation law (
1.6
) can be expressed as

(1.11)

\begin{split}p^{\prime}&=\frac{p+q}{2}+\frac{g}{2}\left(w-(\gamma-1)(p+q)\frac{(p+q)\cdot w}{|p+q|^{2}}\right),\cr q^{\prime}&=\frac{p+q}{2}-\frac{g}{2}\left(w-(\gamma-1)(p+q)\frac{(p+q)\cdot w}{|p+q|^{2}}\right),\end{split}

where
\gamma=(p^{0}+q^{0})/\sqrt{s}
, and
w
denotes
\mathbb{S}^{2}
component of the unit sphere:

(1.12)

\displaystyle w=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta),

on
\theta\in[0,\pi]
and
\phi\in[0,2\pi]
. The pre-post collisional energy
p^{\prime 0}
and
q^{\prime 0}
is written by

\displaystyle p^{\prime 0}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{0}+q^{0}}{2}+\frac{g}{2\sqrt{s}}(p+q)\cdot w,

\displaystyle q^{\prime 0}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{0}+q^{0}}{2}-\frac{g}{2\sqrt{s}}(p+q)\cdot w.

Once we divide each side of (
1.1
) by
p^{0}
, then we can rewrite the relativistic quantum Boltzmann equation as follows:

(1.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}F+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}F&=Q(F,F,F,F),\cr F(x,p,0)&=F_{0}(x,p).\end{split}

Note that
Q(F,F,F,F)=\frac{1}{p^{0}}C(F)
. We generally denote the normalized momentum as
\hat{p}
where

\displaystyle\hat{p}=\frac{p}{p^{0}}=\frac{p}{\sqrt{1+|p|^{2}}}.

In this framework, the relativistic quantum collision operator is reduced to the following form
[
72
]
:

(1.14)

Q(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})~\sigma(g,\theta)\\
\times\big[F_{1}(p^{\prime})F_{2}(q^{\prime})(1+\tau F_{3}(p))(1+\tau F_{4}(q))-(1+\tau F_{1}(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau F_{2}(q^{\prime}))F_{3}(p)F_{4}(q)\big],

where the M
\o
ller velocity
v_{\o}
is given by

\displaystyle v_{\o}(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})=\sqrt{\bigg|\frac{p}{p^{0}}-\frac{q}{q^{0}}\bigg|^{2}-\bigg|\frac{p}{p^{0}}\times\frac{q}{q^{0}}\bigg|^{2}}=\frac{g\sqrt{s}}{p^{0}q^{0}}.

One of the properties that the relativistic quantum collision operator satisfies is the following identity

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~Q(F,F,F,F)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\cr p^{\mu}\end{array}\right)=0,

which implies the conservation laws of the total mass, momentum and energy:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~F\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\cr p^{\mu}\end{array}\right)=0.

The
H
-theorem for the relativistic quantum Boltzmann equation is established in
[
26
]
as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~F\ln F-\tau^{-1}(1+\tau F)\ln(1+\tau F)\leq 0.

1.4.
Global equilibria

It has been shown in
[
25
]
that the global equilibria for the relativistic quantum Boltzmann equation (
1.1
) has the form of

\mathcal{F}(p)=\frac{1}{e^{\nu(p)}-\tau},\text{ for }\nu(p)=ap^{0}+b\cdot p+c,

for some constant
a
,
b,
and
c.
In this paper, we rescale the problem and consider the situation that the macroscopic mean velocity
b=0
is zero. Then we can define the global equilibrium
m(p)
in the form of

(1.17)

\displaystyle m(p)=\frac{1}{e^{ap^{0}+c}-\tau},

where
a>0
and
c\geq-a
in the case of bosons and
a>0
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
in the case of fermions. In this paper, we only consider
a>0
and
c>-a
in the case of bosons in order to exclude a possible blow-up of the equilibrium. We choose and fix the constant
a
and
c
and consider the initial distribution
F_{0}
nearby the equilibrium
m(p)
. Then we will prove that the particle distribution
F
whose initial distribution
F_{0}
is sufficiently close to the relativistic quantum global equilibrium
m(p)
will converge to
m(p)
in
H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}
sense for
N\geq 3
.

We also denote the non-quantum relativistic equilibrium
J(p)
as

(1.18)

\displaystyle J(p^{0})=e^{-ap^{0}}.

1.5.
Spaces

Now we define some notations on norms and inner products which are frequently used throughout this paper. The constant
C
is generically used, whose value can be changed from line to line. Especially, when we want to indicate the dependency of
a
, we indicate it as
C_{a}
.
We define the standard
L^{2}
norm as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}_{p}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~|f(p)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~|f(x,p)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and we define the weighted
L^{2}
norm as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\nu}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~\nu(p)|f(p)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\|f\|_{x,\nu}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~\nu(p)|f(x,p)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The standard
L^{2}
inner product is given by

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~f(p)g(p),\quad\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~f(x,p)g(x,p).

We use the multi-index notation

\displaystyle\alpha=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}),

to simplify the differential operator:

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}=\partial_{t}^{\alpha_{0}}\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x_{3}}^{\alpha_{3}}.

For a brevity of discussion on the frequently used function
m+\tau m^{2}
, we often write the variable only at the end of the function as

\displaystyle(m+\tau m^{2})(p)=m(p)+\tau(m(p))^{2}.

We define the higher-order energy norm as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f(t))=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,p}}+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f(s)\|^{2}_{x,\nu}ds.

1.6.
Hypothesis on the collision kernel

We assume that the differential cross section
\sigma(g,\theta)
satisfies the hard potential assumption with an angular cut-off as in
[
23
,
24
]
:

(1.19)

\displaystyle\sigma(g,\theta)=g\sin\theta.

This assumption is analogous to the standard hard-sphere assumption for the Newtonian Boltzmann equation; if
|p+q|
or
|p-q|
is sufficiently small or if
|p-q|
is much larger than
|p^{0}-q^{0}|
, then it behaves as the Newtonian hard sphere kernel.

1.7.
Main results

In this paper, we prove the existence of a unique global-in-time classical solution of the relativistic quantum Boltzmann equation nearby the global equilibrium. Before we state our main theorem, we would like to introduce the reformulation of the relativistic quantum Boltzmann equation via a special linearization that is relevant to the relativistic and quantum case.

In the non-quantum case, the standard decomposition of the perturbed solution
f
near the global equilibrium
\mu
is
F=\mu+\sqrt{\mu}f
for
\mu=e^{-|p|^{2}/2}
or
\mu=e^{-p^{0}}
in the Newtonian and the relativistic cases, respectively.
But in the quantum case, the previous decomposition
F=\mu+\sqrt{\mu}f
does not guarantee the non-negativity of
\langle Lf,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}
as in Lemma
2.2
. Thus inspired by the previous work related to the quantum kinetic models in
[
5
,
80
,
52
,
57
]
, we choose the following decomposition of
F
:

F(x,p,t)=m(p)+\sqrt{m(p)+\tau m^{2}(p)}f(x,p,t),

where the global equilibrium
m(p)
is defined as in (
1.17
):

\displaystyle m(p)=\frac{1}{e^{ap^{0}+c}-\tau}.

We plug the decomposition into (
1.13
) and divide each side of the equation by
\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}
to have

(1.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}f+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}f+Lf&=\Gamma(f)+T(f),\cr f(x,p,0)&=f_{0}(x,p).\end{split}

The linear term
Lf
is given by

Lf=\nu(p)f+K_{1}f-K_{2}f,

where
\nu(p)
is the collision frequency of a relativistic quantum particle, and
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are compact operators. The right-hand side of (
1.20
) consists of nonlinear terms
\Gamma(f)
and
T(f)
where
\Gamma(f)
consists of all the second-order nonlinear terms and
T(f)
consists of all the third-order nonlinear terms. We can easily check that the fourth-order nonlinear terms disappear by cancellation. In the linearization process, we observe that the collision operator does not satisfy the quad-linearity
Q(k+h,f,f,f)=Q(k,f,f,f)+Q(h,f,f,f)
. For more of the detailed linearization process, see Section
2
. Now we are ready to state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
. Suppose that the initial data
F_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0\leq F_{0}(x,p)\leq 1\quad\mbox{for fermions,}\\
0\leq F_{0}(x,p)\qquad\hskip 8.53581pt\mbox{for bosons,}\end{array}\right.

and the global equilibrium
m(p)
shares the same total mass, momentum and energy with the initial data:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}dxdp~F_{0}(x,p)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\cr p^{\mu}\end{array}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}dxdp~m(p)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\cr p^{\mu}\end{array}\right).

Then there exist
\delta>0
and
C>0
such that if
\mathcal{E}(f_{0})\leq\delta
then there exists a unique global-in-time solution of (
1.20
) such that

(1)

The distribution function
F(x,p,t)
has the following bounds:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0\leq F(x,p,t)\leq 1\quad\mbox{for fermions,}\\
0\leq F(x,p,t)\qquad\hskip 8.53581pt\mbox{for bosons.}\end{array}\right.

(2)

The energy norm is bounded globally in time:

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}\mathcal{E}(f(t))\leq C\mathcal{E}(f_{0}).

(3)

There exists a uniform constant
\epsilon>0
such that the perturbation decays exponentially:

\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,p}}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}.

(4)

Let
f
and
\bar{f}
be the solutions with the initial data
f_{0}
and
\bar{f}_{0}
, respectively. Then there exists a positive constant
\delta>0
such that

\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}}\leq e^{-\delta t}\|f_{0}-\bar{f}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}}.

To prove the main theorem, we need a coercivity estimate of the linear operator
L
. The linear operator
L
satisfies the following dissipation property:

\displaystyle\langle Lf,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle\geq\delta\|(I-P)f\|_{\nu}^{2},

for some positive
\delta>0
. The macroscopic projection
Pf
denotes the orthonormal projection onto
L^{2}_{p}
space with respect to the following
5
-basis:

(1.25)

\displaystyle\left\{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},p_{1}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},p_{2}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},p_{3}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},p^{0}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}\right\}.

The
5
-dimensional basis above constitutes the kernel of
L
.
Then it is crucial to obtain some upper-bound estimates of the nonlinear terms
\Gamma
and
T
in order to construct the solution. Some parts of the nonlinear terms can be estimated by a simple change of variables and the Hölder inequality. However, regarding some second-order nonlinear terms whose integrands consist of the product of
f(p^{\prime})
(or
f(q^{\prime})
) and
f(p)
, we need to take a change of variables of either
p\mapsto p^{\prime}
(or
p\mapsto q^{\prime}
) and there occur some non-trivial difficulties. Different from the non-relativistic case, a uniform positive lower bound for the Jacobian for the change of variables
|\partial p^{\prime}/\partial p|
(or
|\partial q^{\prime}/\partial p|
) does not exist as shown in
[
16
]
, and hence we need another way to deal with this difficulty. One way is to proceed the estimates of the second-order nonlinear terms by lifting the
dq
integral to energy-momentum four vector integral
dq^{\mu}
imposing additional Dirac-delta function. This technique has been introduced in
[
18
,
44
,
45
]
.
Then we reduce the integral by computing the Dirac-delta function. In this process, there appears a singularity of
1/\bar{g}
and the exponential growth with respect to the
p
variable from
\exp(p^{0}-p^{\prime 0})
. We will explain this more in detail in Section
1.8.2
.

Once we obtain the estimates of the linear terms and the nonlinear terms, we define an iteration scheme to construct the local-in-time solution as

(1.26)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x})F^{n+1}=Q(F^{n},F^{n},F^{n+1},F^{n}).

Regarding the case of fermions, the solution
F
has to be bounded by
1
from above. Therefore, we also need to prove that the function
F^{n+1}
is bounded in the closed interval
[0,1]
in each iteration scheme based on the induction hypothesis that
F^{n}
is in
[0,1]
. Then we obtain that the collision operator
Q(F^{n},F^{n},F^{n+1},F^{n})
is well-defined.
On the right-hand side of (
1.26
), note that we place
F^{n+1}
in the
p
variable position of the collision operator
Q
(i.e., at the third input of
Q(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
). This is by the specific structure of the nonlinear operator
Q
from (
1.14
). Once we place
F^{n+1}
as a third input of
Q
then we can rewrite (
1.26
) in a more clear structure

(1.27)

\displaystyle\{\partial_{t}+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}-\tau G(F^{n})+R(F^{n})\}F^{n+1}=G(F^{n}),

where
G
and
R
is now defined as

\displaystyle G(F_{1},F_{2},F_{4})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)F_{1}(p^{\prime})F_{2}(q^{\prime})(1+\tau F_{4}(q)),

\displaystyle R(F_{1},F_{2},F_{4})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)(1+\tau F_{1}(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau F_{2}(q^{\prime}))F_{4}(q).

Also, we can show that the boundedness
0\leq F^{n}\leq 1
implies that
0\leq G(F^{n})
and
0\leq R(F^{n})
, which guarantees the boundedness of
F^{n+1}
.
Then the additional linearization of
F^{n+1}=m+\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}f^{n+1}
results in creating the dissipation term
\nu(p)f^{n+1}
in the left-hand side of (
1.27
), which would then result in the exponential decay of the perturbation
f^{n+1}
in the
L^{2}
sense. By the induction argument, we obtain the uniform boundedness of the energy locally in time.

Then the standard way of extending the local existence to the global one is to eliminate the dissipation of the linear part
L
. Similarly to the Newtonian case, we substitute
f=(I-P)f+Pf
on each side of (
1.20
) where
Pf
is defined by the orthonormal projection of (
1.25
).
Then the expansion of the linear term
(\partial_{t}+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x})Pf
yields a linear combination with respect to the
14
-basis. Thus we can achieve the following coercivity estimate:

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\langle L\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}

\displaystyle\geq\delta\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}^{2},

for some positive constant
\delta>0
. This full coercivity estimate enables the extension of the local-in-time solution to a global-in-time solution.

1.8.
Main difficulties and our strategy

In this subsection, we present the difficulties that arise from the estimates of the nonlinear terms. In the relativistic quantum case, there appear new types of nonlinear terms involving both pre- and post-collisional momenta at the same time as
f(p)f(p^{\prime})
and
f(p)f(q^{\prime})
. In general, this kind of terms has been expected to appear only in the non-cutoff Boltzmann theory in the non-quantum case. In the non-cutoff Boltzmann theory, it has been considered very crucial to understand the change of variables
q\mapsto p^{\prime}
or
q^{\prime}
and the cancellation lemma to understand the fractional diffusive behavior
[
36
,
44
]
.
In the non-relativistic case, these new nonlinear terms can be handled because
|\partial p^{\prime}/\partial p|
and
|\partial q^{\prime}/\partial q|
are bounded from below, as in
[
2
,
19
]
. However, in the relativistic case under the
center-of-momentum
frame, such a positive uniform lower-bound of the Jacobian of the collision map does not exist
[
16
]
.

The remedy of the issue has been introduced in
[
18
,
31
,
71
]
in the
center-of-momentum
frame (
1.11
) where the linear term
K_{2}
of the difficulty has been calculated. Namely, the authors lift the
dq
integral to the energy-momentum four vector integral
dq^{\mu}
by imposing an additional Dirac-delta function, and they take a change of variables via a suitable Lorentz transform. In this paper, we also follow a similar technique to deal with the nonlinearity that occurs in dealing with the terms
\Gamma(f)
and
T(f)
. In this direction, we however still encounter several other additional difficulties on the nonlinear term involving
f(p)f(p^{\prime})
as below. Let us first denote the nonlinear term
\Gamma_{2,1}
involving
f(p)f(p^{\prime})
as

(1.28)

\big|\langle\Gamma_{2,1}(f,h),\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}\big|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dp}{p^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq}{q^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dp^{\prime}}{p^{\prime 0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{q^{\prime 0}}s\sigma(g,\theta)\cr\times\delta^{(4)}(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}-q^{\prime\mu})J(q^{0})J(p^{\prime 0}/2)|f(p)||h(p^{\prime})||\eta(p)|,

and the integral part with respect to the measure
dqdq^{\prime}
as

(1.29)

\displaystyle B=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq}{q^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{q^{\prime 0}}s\sigma(g,\theta)\delta^{(4)}(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}-q^{\prime\mu})J(q^{0}).

Then we present the three main difficulties that arise regarding the nonlinear terms.

1.8.1.
The Lorentz-invariant measure
\frac{dp}{p^{0}}
and the nonlinear structure of
s\sigma(g,\theta)

The first difficulty arises from the Lorentz-invaraint measure
\frac{dp}{p^{0}}
including the fraction of the energy and the nonlinear structure of the differential cross section
s\sigma(g,\theta)
with respect to the collision variables
p
and
q
. A good way to remove the fraction of the energy is to lift
dq
and
dq^{\prime}
integrals to the energy momentum
4
-vector
dq^{\mu}
and
dq^{\prime\mu}
integrals by considering extra Dirac-delta and unit step functions as

B=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}dq^{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}dq^{\prime\mu}s\sigma(g,\theta)\delta^{(4)}(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}-q^{\prime\mu})J(q^{0})u(q^{0})u(q^{\prime 0})\delta(q^{\mu}q_{\mu}+1)\delta(q^{\prime\mu}q^{\prime}_{\mu}+1).

If we simply eliminate the
dq^{\prime\mu}
integral by computing the Dirac-delta function of
\delta^{(4)}(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}-q^{\prime\mu})
, the rest of the integral becomes highly complicated to deal with. Thus, motivated by de Groot et al. in
[
18
]
, we instead apply a symmetric change of variables
\bar{q}^{\mu}=q^{\mu}+q^{\prime\mu}
and
\bar{q}^{\prime\mu}=q^{\mu}-q^{\prime\mu}
.
Despite the nonlinear structure of the
s
,
g
and
\cos\theta
in (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (
1.9
), respectively, we can also represent them as the terms that depend only on the variables
p^{\mu}
,
p^{\prime\mu}
,
\bar{q}^{\mu}
and
\bar{q}^{\prime\mu}
(See Lemma
3.5
) as follows:

\displaystyle g_{c}^{2}=\bar{g}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(p^{\mu}+p^{\prime\mu})(\bar{q}_{\mu}-p_{\mu}-p^{\prime}_{\mu}),\quad s_{c}=g_{c}^{2}+4,\quad\cos\theta_{c}=1-2\frac{\bar{g}^{2}}{g_{c}^{2}}.

Then we can have
B
in the following form

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}}d\Theta(\bar{q}^{\mu},\bar{q}^{\prime\mu})s_{c}\sigma(g_{c},\theta_{c})\delta^{(4)}(p^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}+\bar{q}^{\prime\mu})J\left(\frac{\bar{q}^{0}+\bar{q}^{\prime 0}}{2}\right),

where

\displaystyle d\Theta(\bar{q}^{\mu},\bar{q}^{\prime\mu})

\displaystyle=d\bar{q}^{\mu}d\bar{q}^{\prime\mu}u(\bar{q}^{0})u(\bar{s}-4)\delta((\bar{q}^{\mu}\bar{q}_{\mu}+\bar{q}^{\prime\mu}\bar{q}^{\prime}_{\mu})+4)\delta(\bar{q}^{\mu}\bar{q}^{\prime}_{\mu}).

By substituting
\bar{q}^{\prime\mu}=p^{\prime\mu}-p^{\mu}
, we now reduce the
4
-dimensional Dirac-delta function with the energy-momentum
4
-vector
\delta^{(4)}(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}-q^{\prime\mu})
and obtain a more intuitive form of
B
as

(1.30)

B=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}d\bar{q}^{\mu}u(\bar{q}^{0})u(\bar{s}-4)\delta(\bar{q}^{\mu}\bar{q}_{\mu}+(p^{\prime\mu}-p^{\mu})(p^{\prime}_{\mu}-p_{\mu})+4)\cr\times\delta(\bar{q}^{\mu}(p^{\prime}_{\mu}-p_{\mu}))s_{c}\sigma(g_{c},\theta_{c})J\left(\frac{\bar{q}^{0}+p^{\prime 0}-p^{0}}{2}\right).

1.8.2.
Singularity with respect to
\bar{g}

Then another difficulty arises from the singularity in the relative momentum
\bar{g}
, which occurs from the reduction of the second Dirac-delta function. We first remark that the second Dirac-delta function of (
1.30
) consists of an inner product between energy momentum
4
-vectors. Motivated by the explicit form of the Lorentz transform by Strain
[
71
]
, we apply the Lorentz trasnform which converts
p^{\prime}_{\mu}-p_{\mu}
to
(0,0,0,\bar{g})
(i.e.
\Lambda(p^{\prime}_{\mu}-p_{\mu})=(0,0,0,\bar{g})
). Then we obtain

\displaystyle\delta(\bar{q}^{\mu}(p^{\prime}_{\mu}-p_{\mu}))=\delta(\Lambda\bar{q}^{\mu}\Lambda(p^{\prime}_{\mu}-p_{\mu}))=\delta(\bar{q}^{3}\bar{g})=\frac{1}{\bar{g}}\delta(\bar{q}^{3}),

where we used that the Lorentz transform is invariant under the inner-product and that
\delta(ax)=\frac{1}{a}\delta(x)
. We can now see that one
\bar{g}
in the Dirac-delta function comes out as
1/\bar{g}
and creates an additional singularity. However, motivated by (
1.10
), we use the half angle formula
[
18
, page 277, (A.11)]
of the
\cos\theta
and observe that

\sin^{2}(\theta/2)=\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2}=\frac{\bar{g}^{2}}{g^{2}}.

Combining with the assumption of the differential cross section, we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma(g,\theta)=g\sin\theta=2g\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}=2g\sqrt{\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2}}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}=2\bar{g}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}.

This allows us to eliminate the singularity of
\bar{g}
.

1.8.3.
Exponential growth

The last difficulty is regarding the last multiplier on the right-hand side of the equation (
1.30
). Since the function
J(\bar{q}^{0}/2)
is contained in
d\bar{q}^{0}
integral, we have an exponential decay in
p^{\prime 0}
and an exponential growth in
p^{0}
from
J\left((p^{\prime 0}-p^{0})/2\right)
at the same time. The decay for
p^{\prime 0}
is beneficial for the upper-bound estimate, but there is a problematic term of the exponential growth of
p^{0}
even in (
1.28
).
However, we prove that the remaining part of the right-hand side of (
1.30
) includes the following exponential decaying factor

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\sqrt{\frac{(p^{0}+p^{\prime 0})^{2}}{4}-\frac{|p\times p^{\prime}|^{2}}{\bar{g}^{2}}}\right).

Then, by the estimates in
[
30
, Lemma 3.1, (iii) and (iv)]
of

\displaystyle\frac{(p^{0}+p^{\prime 0})^{2}}{4}-\frac{|p\times p^{\prime}|^{2}}{\bar{g}^{2}}=|p-p^{\prime}|^{2}\frac{\bar{g}^{2}+4}{4\bar{g}^{2}}\geq\max\left\{\frac{\bar{g}^{2}}{4}+1,\frac{1}{4}|p-p^{\prime}|^{2}\right\},

we can have an exponential decay of
\exp\left(-|p-p^{\prime}|/2\right)
. Since the difference of the energy
|p^{\prime 0}-p^{0}|
can further be bounded by the difference of the momentum
|p^{\prime}-p|
, the exponential growth can be absorbed by the exponential decay as

\displaystyle J\left(\frac{p^{\prime 0}-p^{0}}{2}\right)\exp\left(-\sqrt{\frac{(p^{0}+p^{\prime 0})^{2}}{4}-\frac{|p\times p^{\prime}|^{2}}{\bar{g}^{2}}}\right)\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}(p^{\prime 0}-p^{0})}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|p-p^{\prime}|}\leq 1.

Then we can have properly weighted
L^{2}
bounds for the nonlinear terms.

1.9.
Outline of the paper

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we linearize the collision operator of the relativistic quantum Boltzmann equation nearby a global equilibrium. In Section
3
, we establish several estimates on the linear and the nonlinear terms. Section
4
is devoted to constructing the unique local-in-time classical solution. In the last section, we prove the coercivity estimate and establish the global-in-time classical solution.

Linearization of the relativistic quantum Boltzmann equation

\displaystyle m(p)=\frac{1}{e^{ap^{0}+c}-\tau}.

Proposition 2.1

\displaystyle=\nu(p)f+K_{1}f-K_{2}f,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\tau m(p)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)m(q)(1+\tau m(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau m(q^{\prime})),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}f

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}}Q(m+\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}f).

To decompose the right-hand side into linear and nonlinear terms, we first define the zeroth-order-in-

\displaystyle Q_{0}=Q(m,m,m,m).

As we can see in (

\displaystyle Q_{4}=Q(m,m,m,m+\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}f)-Q_{0}.

In view of this notation, the collection of the zeroth and the first-order terms in

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{4}Q_{i}+Q_{0}\right).

Thus we divide the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}}Q(m+\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}f)=-Lf+\Gamma(f),

We observe from the conservation of energy

), we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle\frac{m(p)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}\frac{1+\tau m(p^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}}=\frac{1+\tau m(q)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}}\frac{m(q^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}}.

Substituting it in (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle K_{2}f

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}f(p^{\prime}).

This completes the derivation of the linear term
\nu f
,
K_{1}f
and
K_{2}f
.

Now we consider the nonlinear terms. Since the quantum collision operator is not quad-linear (Recall that
Q(k+h,f,f,f)\neq Q(k,f,f,f)+Q(h,f,f,f)
), the second-order nonlinear term is highly complicated to be represented. Thus, we first observe one of the second-order nonlinear terms involving
f(p^{\prime})
and
f(q^{\prime})
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~\frac{v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}\\
\times\big[\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}f(p^{\prime})\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}f(q^{\prime})(1+\tau m(p))(1+\tau m(q))\cr-\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}f(p^{\prime})\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}f(q^{\prime})m(p)m(q)\big].

There are 6 second-order nonlinear terms of the same kind similar to the term above where the number 6 is coming from the number of choices for choosing 2 variables among the four variables
p
,
q
,
p^{\prime}
, and
q^{\prime}
. We represent all of the second-order nonlinear terms (
6
nonlinear terms) as follows:

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Gamma(f,h)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw\frac{v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}\cr&\times\bigg[(m(p^{\prime})m(q^{\prime})-(1+\tau m(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau m(q^{\prime})))\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}f(p)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}h(q)\cr&\quad+\tau(m(q^{\prime})(1+\tau m(q))-m(q)(1+\tau m(q^{\prime})))\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}f(p)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}h(p^{\prime})\cr&\quad+\tau(m(q^{\prime})(1+\tau m(p))-m(p)(1+\tau m(q^{\prime})))\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}f(q)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}h(p^{\prime})\cr&\quad+\tau(m(p^{\prime})(1+\tau m(q))-m(q)(1+\tau m(p^{\prime})))\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}f(p)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}h(q^{\prime})\cr&\quad+\tau(m(p^{\prime})(1+\tau m(p))-m(p)(1+\tau m(p^{\prime})))\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}f(q)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}h(q^{\prime})\cr&\quad+(1+\tau m(p))((1+\tau m(q))-m(p)m(q))\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}f(p^{\prime})\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}h(q^{\prime})\bigg]\cr&=\Gamma_{1}(f,h)+\Gamma_{2}(f,h)+\Gamma_{3}(f,h)+\Gamma_{4}(f,h)+\Gamma_{5}(f,h)+\Gamma_{6}(f,h).\end{split}

We also denote as

\displaystyle\Gamma(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq 6}\Gamma_{i}(f,f),

when
f=h
.
Lastly, we consider the following third-order nonlinear terms involving
f(p^{\prime})
,
f(q^{\prime})
and
f(p)
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)\big[\tau\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}f(p^{\prime})\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}f(q^{\prime})f(p)(1+\tau m(q))\cr-\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}f(p^{\prime})\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}f(q^{\prime})f(p)m(q)\big].

Similarly, we represent all of the third-order nonlinear terms (
4
nonlinear terms) as

(2.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{1}(f,h,\eta)&=-\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)f(p)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}h(q)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}\eta(p^{\prime}),\cr T_{2}(f,h,\eta)&=-\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)f(p)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}h(q)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}\eta(q^{\prime}),\cr T_{3}(f,h,\eta)&=\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)f(p)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}h(p^{\prime})\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}\eta(q^{\prime}),\cr T_{4}(f,h,\eta)&=\tau\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}f(q)\cr&\quad\times\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}h(p^{\prime})\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}\eta(q^{\prime}).\end{split}

Similarly, we define

\displaystyle T(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq 4}T_{i}(f,f,f).

We can easily check that the fourth-order nonlinear term is cancelled.
∎

By the linearization proposition above and substituting
F=m+\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}f
in (
1.13
), we obtain the linearized equation for the relativistic quantum Boltzmann model (
1.13
) as follows:

(2.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}f+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}f+Lf&=\Gamma(f)+T(f),\cr f(x,p,0)&=f_{0}(x,p).\end{split}

where
f_{0}(x,p)=(F_{0}(x,p)-m)/\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}
. Then the conservation laws (
1.3
) can be written as following form:

(2.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~f(x,p,t)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~f_{0}(x,p)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},\cr\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~f(x,p,t)p\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~f_{0}(x,p)p\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},\cr\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~f(x,p,t)p^{0}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~f_{0}(x,p)p^{0}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}.\end{split}

Now we state several useful properties for the linear term
Lf
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any smooth function
\phi
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)m(p)m(q)(1+\tau m(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau m(q^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\frac{f(p)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}+\frac{f(q)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}}-\frac{f(p^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}}-\frac{f(q^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}}\bigg)\phi(p)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)m(p)m(q)(1+\tau m(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau m(q^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\frac{f(p)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}+\frac{f(q)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}}-\frac{f(p^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}}-\frac{f(q^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}}\bigg)\phi(q)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)m(p)m(q)(1+\tau m(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau m(q^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\frac{f(p)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}+\frac{f(q)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}}-\frac{f(p^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}}-\frac{f(q^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}}\bigg)(-\phi(p^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)m(p)m(q)(1+\tau m(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau m(q^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\frac{f(p)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}+\frac{f(q)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}}-\frac{f(p^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}}-\frac{f(q^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}}\bigg)(-\phi(q^{\prime})).

Proof.

Note that
m(p)m(q)(1+\tau m(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau m(q^{\prime}))
is invariant under the change of variables
(p,q)\leftrightarrow(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
from (
2.4
). Thus, by the change of variables
p\leftrightarrow q
and
(p,q)\leftrightarrow(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
introduced in
[
29
]
, we have the desired results.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

We have the following properties for the linear operator
L
:

(1)

L
is a symmetric operator:
\langle Lf,g\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}=\langle f,Lg\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}
.

(2)

Lf=0
if and only if
f=Pf
.

where
Pf
is defined as the orthonormal projection to the
L^{2}_{p}
space which is spanned by following
5
-dimensional basis:

\displaystyle\left\{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},p_{1}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},p_{2}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},p_{3}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},p^{0}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}\right\}.

Proof.

(1) We take an inner product of
g
with
Lf
of (
2.5
) to have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~gLf

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\frac{g(p)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)m(p)m(q)(1+\tau m(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau m(q^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\frac{f(p)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}+\frac{f(q)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}}-\frac{f(p^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}}-\frac{f(q^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}}\bigg).

By Lemma
2.1
, we substitute

\phi(p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}g(p)

and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~gLf

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)m(p)m(q)(1+\tau m(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau m(q^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{f(p)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}+\frac{f(q)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}}-\frac{f(p^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}}-\frac{f(q^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{g(p)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}+\frac{g(q)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}}-\frac{g(p^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}}-\frac{g(q^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}}\right).

This proves the symmetricity of
L
.

(2) Once we substitute
g=f
in the equation above, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~fLf

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)m(p)m(q)(1+\tau m(p^{\prime}))(1+\tau m(q^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\frac{f(p)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}+\frac{f(q)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}}-\frac{f(p^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}}-\frac{f(q^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Therefore
Lf=0
implies

\displaystyle\frac{f(p)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}+\frac{f(q)}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}}=\frac{f(p^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}}+\frac{f(q^{\prime})}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}},

which is satisfied if and only if
f=Pf
. Moreover,
L(Pf)=0
gives the desired results.
∎

3.
Estimates of the linear and the nonlinear terms

This section is devoted to proving several estimates of the linear and the nonlinear terms. Especially, we emphasize that the estimates of the nonlinear term
\Gamma_{2}
involve the main difficulties as we mentioned in Section
1.8
. Before we move onto it, we present some useful properties that are commonly used throughout this paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exist positive constants
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}J(p^{0})\leq m(p)\leq C_{2}J(p^{0}).

Proof.

In the fermionic case,
C_{1}=1/(1+e^{c})
and
C_{2}=e^{-c}
give the desired estimates:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e^{c}+1}\frac{1}{e^{ap^{0}}}\leq\frac{1}{e^{ap^{0}+c}+1}\leq\frac{1}{e^{ap^{0}+c}}.

In the case of bosons, we choose
C_{1}=e^{-c}
and
C_{2}=1/(e^{c}-e^{-a})
. Since
c>-a
, we have
C_{2}<\infty
, and we get

\frac{1}{e^{ap^{0}+c}}\leq\frac{1}{e^{ap^{0}+c}-1}\leq\frac{1}{e^{c}-e^{-a}}\frac{1}{e^{ap^{0}}}.

∎

By this Lemma, the relativistic quantum equilibrium
m(p)
can be treated as the non-quantum relativistic equilibrium
J(p^{0})
. Now we present the estimates for collision frequency
\nu(p)
and operator
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a positive constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}(p^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\nu(p)\leq C(p^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The integral operator
K_{i}(f)
is a compact operator in
L^{2}_{p}
for
i=1,2
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle 1+\tau m(p)=\frac{e^{ap^{0}+c}}{e^{ap^{0}+c}-\tau}=\frac{e^{c}m(p)}{J(p^{0})}\leq C.

Then by (
3.1
), we have the upper bound of
\nu
as

\displaystyle\nu(p)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)m(q).

Similarly we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}=\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}(ap^{0}+c)}}{e^{ap^{0}+c}-\tau}\leq Ce^{\frac{1}{2}ap^{0}}=CJ(p^{0}/2),

which yields

\displaystyle K_{1}f(p)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)J(p^{\prime 0}/2)J(q^{\prime 0}/2)f(q),\text{and}

\displaystyle K_{2}f(p)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)J(q^{0}/2)J(q^{\prime 0}/2)f(p^{\prime}).

We note that the upper bounds of
\nu
and
K_{i}
are identical to those of non-quantum relativistic linear terms in
[
23
]
. Therefore we obtain the desired results.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

The linear operator
L
satisfies following dissipation property for some positive constant
\delta
:

\langle Lf,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\geq\delta\|(I-P)f\|_{\nu}^{2}.

Proof.

Since
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are compact and
\nu
satisfies Lemma
3.1
, we have the desired results by following the same proof of Lemma 3.4 of
[
71
]
.
∎

3.1.
Estimates of the second-order nonlinear terms

In this subsection, we establish the estimates on the second-order nonlinear terms.

Lemma 3.4
.

We have

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Gamma(f,h),\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}\big|\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|h\|_{\nu}+\|f\|_{\nu}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\right)\|\eta\|_{\nu}.

Proof.

As we can see in (
2.13
), there are six second-order nonlinear terms. By the change of variables
p^{\prime}\leftrightarrow q^{\prime}
, we can see that
\Gamma_{2}(f,h)=\Gamma_{4}(f,h)
and
\Gamma_{3}(f,h)=\Gamma_{5}(f,h)
. Thus we write the proof in three parts. Firstly, we prove the estimates of
\Gamma_{1}(f,h)
and
\Gamma_{6}(f,h)
, since they are similar to the non-quantum relativistic case. Secondly, we present the estimate of
\Gamma_{3}(f,h)
. Lastly, we prove the most difficult part
\Gamma_{2}(f,f)
. For this we need several techniques to deal with the difficulties that arise from the relativistic integrals.

(1) Estimates of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{6}
:
We first consider
\Gamma_{1}(f,h)
term. By (
3.1
), (
3.2
), and Lemma
3.1
, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Gamma_{1}(f,h),\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)J(q^{0}/2)|f(p)||h(q)||\eta(p)|.

By the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Gamma_{1}(f,h),\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq~v_{\o}^{2}\sigma^{2}(g,\theta)J(q^{0})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq|h(q)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}|f(p)||\eta(p)|.

For the first
dq
integral, we recall the definition of
v_{\o}
and
\sigma(g,\theta)
:

\displaystyle v_{\o}=\frac{g\sqrt{s}}{p^{0}q^{0}},\qquad\sigma(g,\theta)=g\sin\theta.

By an explicit computation, we have

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=2+2p^{0}q^{0}-2p\cdot q~\leq~2p^{0}q^{0}+2(1-\cos\theta)|p||q|~\leq~4p^{0}q^{0},

and

\displaystyle g=\sqrt{s-4}\leq\sqrt{s}\leq 2\sqrt{p^{0}q^{0}}.

Applying above inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq~v_{\o}^{2}\sigma^{2}(g,\theta)J(q^{0})\leq Cp^{0}.

We apply the Hölder inequality again to obtain the following estimate:

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Gamma_{1}(f,h),\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\|h\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~(p^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}|f(p)||\eta(p)|\leq C\|f\|_{\nu}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|\eta\|_{\nu}.

Now we consider
\Gamma_{6}(f,h)
term. We first use the energy conservation law
p^{0}+q^{0}=p^{\prime 0}+q^{\prime 0}
to have

\displaystyle\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q^{\prime})}\leq CJ(p^{\prime 0}/2)J(q^{\prime 0}/2)=CJ(p^{0}/2)J(q^{0}/2),

which gives

(3.4)

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Gamma_{6}(f,h),\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)J(q^{0}/2)|f(p^{\prime})||h(q^{\prime})||\eta(p)|.

Applying the Hölder inequality yields

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Gamma_{6}(f,h),\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq~v_{\o}\sigma^{2}(g,\theta)J(q^{0})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq~v_{\o}|f(p^{\prime})|^{2}|h(q^{\prime})|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}|\eta(p)|.

Similarly we use
s\leq 4p^{0}q^{0}
and
g\leq 2(p^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}(q^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}
to have

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Gamma_{6}(f,h),\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~(p^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq~v_{\o}|f(p^{\prime})|^{2}|h(q^{\prime})|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}|\eta(p)|.

By the Hölder inequality for the
dpdw
integral, we have

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Gamma_{6}(f,h),\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\eta\|_{\nu}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw\ (p^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dqv_{\o}|f(p^{\prime})|^{2}|h(q^{\prime})|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The energy conservation law implies

\displaystyle(p^{0})^{1/2}\leq(p^{\prime 0}+q^{\prime 0})^{1/2}\leq(p^{\prime 0})^{1/2}+(q^{\prime 0})^{1/2},

which yields

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Gamma_{6}(f,h),\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta\|_{\nu}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq~v_{\o}\left((p^{\prime 0})^{\frac{1}{2}}+(q^{\prime 0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)|f(p^{\prime})|^{2}|h(q^{\prime})|^{2}.

Then we consider the pre-post change of variables
(p,q)\leftrightarrow(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
with the Jacobian in
[
32
]
:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{dpdq}{p^{0}q^{0}}=\frac{dp^{\prime}dq^{\prime}}{p^{\prime 0}q^{\prime 0}},

and recall from (
1.7
) that we already have

\displaystyle s(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})=s(p^{\prime\mu},q^{\prime\mu}),\qquad g(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})=g(p^{\prime\mu},q^{\prime\mu}).

Thus we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq~v_{\o}\left((p^{\prime 0})^{\frac{1}{2}}+(q^{\prime 0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)|f(p^{\prime})|^{2}|h(q^{\prime})|^{2}.\cr=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq^{\prime}~\frac{g(p^{\prime\mu},q^{\prime\mu})\sqrt{s(p^{\prime\mu},q^{\prime\mu})}}{p^{\prime 0}q^{\prime 0}}\left((p^{\prime 0})^{\frac{1}{2}}+(q^{\prime 0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)|f(p^{\prime})|^{2}|h(q^{\prime})|^{2}.

Finally by
v_{\o}(p^{\prime\mu},q^{\prime\mu})\leq C
and the Hölder inequality, we obtain the desired results:

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Gamma_{6}(f,h),\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|h\|_{\nu}+\|f\|_{\nu}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\right)\|\eta\|_{\nu}.

(2) Estimates of
\Gamma_{3}
:
We split the
\Gamma_{3}
term into two parts:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3}(f,h)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau}{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)\{m(q^{\prime})(1+\tau m(p))-m(p)(1+\tau m(q^{\prime}))\}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(q)}f(q)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}(p^{\prime})}h(p^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{3,1}(f,h)+\Gamma_{3,2}(f,h).

By
1+\tau m(p)\leq C
and
1+\tau m(q^{\prime})\leq C
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{3,1}(f,h)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)\frac{J(q^{\prime 0})J(q^{0}/2)J(p^{\prime 0}/2)}{J(p^{0}/2)}|f(q)||h(p^{\prime})|

\displaystyle R=\frac{p^{0}+p^{\prime 0}}{2},\qquad r=\frac{|p\times p^{\prime}|}{\bar{g}},

\displaystyle B\leq\frac{\bar{s}^{3/2}\pi}{2}J\left(\frac{p^{\prime 0}-p^{0}}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{y^{2}+1}}+y\right)\exp\left(-R\sqrt{y^{2}+1}\right)I_{0}(ry),

4.
Local existence

In this section, we construct the local-in-time classical solution. Here we briefly introduce a main difference of the quantum Boltzmann theory from the Newtonian one.
From the statistical description of quantum Boltzmann equation in
[
17
]
, we denote that the term
(1-F(p))
in the collision operator for fermions is the probability that a fermion is being placed in the
p
momentum place after a collision. Thus the ratio
(1-F(p))
has to be non-negative. Then, in each iteration scheme in the proof of the local existence, the collision operator depending on
F^{n+1}
is well-defined only when
F^{n+1}
is in the interval
[0,1]
in the case of fermions. Thus we need to prove the boundedness of the solution
F^{n+1}
in each iteration step as well.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
. Suppose that the initial data
F_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0\leq F_{0}(x,p)=m(p)+\sqrt{m(p)-m^{2}(p)}f_{0}(x,p)\leq 1,\quad\mbox{for fermion}\\
0\leq F_{0}(x,p)=m(p)+\sqrt{m(p)+m^{2}(p)}f_{0}(x,p),\quad\hskip 17.07164pt\mbox{for boson},\end{array}\right.

Then there exist
M_{0}>0
and
T_{*}>0
such that if
\mathcal{E}(f_{0})\leq\frac{M_{0}}{2}
then there exists a unique local-in-time solution f(x,p,t) of (
2.15
) satisfying

(1)

The higher order energy is uniformly bounded:

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{*}}\mathcal{E}(f(t))\leq M_{0}.

(2)

The distribution function is bounded in
t\in[0,T_{*}]
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0\leq F(x,p,t)=m(p)+\sqrt{m(p)-m^{2}(p)}f(x,p,t)\leq 1,\quad\mbox{for fermions}\\
0\leq F(x,p,t)=m(p)+\sqrt{m(p)+m^{2}(p)}f(x,p,t),\quad\hskip 17.07164pt\mbox{for bosons},\end{array}\right.

(3)

The higher order energy norm
\mathcal{E}(f(t))
is continuous in
t\in[0,T_{*}]
.

Proof.

We first take an iteration scheme as follows:

(4.1)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x})F^{n+1}=Q(F^{n},F^{n},F^{n+1},F^{n}),

with
F^{0}(x,p,t)=F_{0}(x,p)
. Note that
F^{n+1}
in the operator
Q
is placed in the
p
variable location. We proceed the proof by applying the induction argument. Let us assume that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0\leq F^{n}(x,p,t)\leq 1\quad\mbox{for fermions,}\\
0\leq F^{n}(x,p,t)\qquad\hskip 8.53581pt\mbox{for bosons,}\end{array}\ \text{and}\ \sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{*}}\mathcal{E}(f^{n}(t))\leq M_{0}.\right.

Note that the collision operator
Q(F^{n},F^{n},F^{n+1},F^{n})
is well-defined when
F^{n+1}
is in the interval
[0,1]
. We define

\displaystyle G(F)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}dw~v_{\o}\sigma(g,\theta)F(p^{\prime})F(q^{\prime})(1+\tau F(q)),

\displaystyle R(F)

5.1.
Coercivity estimate

Recall the definition of
Pf
in Lemma

. Since

is the orthonormal projection to the

\displaystyle\left\{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},p_{1}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},p_{2}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},p_{3}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},p^{0}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}\right\},

Pf
can be written as follows by the Gram-Schmidt process:

\displaystyle Pf=\mathcal{A}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}+\mathcal{B}\cdot p\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}+\mathcal{C}p^{0}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},

where
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~f\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda}\frac{1}{\lambda_{00}-\frac{\lambda_{0}^{2}}{\lambda}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~fp^{0}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~f\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~fp^{i}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{00}-\frac{\lambda_{0}^{2}}{\lambda}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~fp^{0}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~f\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}\right),

and

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~m(p)+\tau m^{2}(p),\qquad\hskip 17.07164pt\lambda_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~(p^{i})^{2}(m(p)+\tau m^{2}(p)),

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~p^{0}(m(p)+\tau m^{2}(p)),\qquad\lambda_{00}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~(p^{0})^{2}(m(p)+\tau m^{2}(p)),

for
i=1,2,3
. Since
Pf
has the exponential decay
\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}
, we can have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}Pf\|_{x,\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{B}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{C}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right).

Now we substitute

in the perturbation equation (

) to have

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x})(Pf)=-(\partial_{t}+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x})((I-P)f)-L(I-P)f+\Gamma(f)+T(f),

where we used
L(Pf)=0
by Lemma
2.2
. We expand the left-hand side as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{A}\frac{p_{i}}{p^{0}}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\partial_{t}\mathcal{B}_{i}+\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{C})p_{i}+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{B}_{j}\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p^{0}}+\partial_{t}\mathcal{C}p^{0}\right)\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},

which is a linear combination of following
14
-basis:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},\quad\frac{p_{i}}{p^{0}}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},\quad p_{i}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},\quad\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p^{0}}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}},\quad p^{0}\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}\right\},

for
1\leq i,j\leq 3
. To denote the right-hand side, we define

(5.3)

\displaystyle l

\displaystyle=-(\partial_{t}+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}+L)((I-P)f),\qquad h=\Gamma(f)+T(f).

By expanding
l
and
h
with respect to the
14
-basis elements in (

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}&=l_{a}+h_{a},\cr\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{A}&=l_{i}+h_{i},\cr\partial_{t}\mathcal{B}_{i}+\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{C}&=l_{bci}+h_{bci},\cr\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{B}_{j}+\partial_{x_{j}}\mathcal{B}_{i}&=l_{ij}+h_{ij},\cr\partial_{t}\mathcal{C}&=l_{c}+h_{c},\end{split}

for

. On the right-hand side,

and

are the coefficients of expansion of
l
and
h
with respect to the basis in (

\displaystyle\tilde{l}

\displaystyle=l_{a}+l_{c}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}\left(l_{i}+l_{bci}\right)+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}l_{ij},

\displaystyle\tilde{h}

\displaystyle=h_{a}+h_{c}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}\left(h_{i}+h_{bci}\right)+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}h_{ij}.

Note that the macro-micro system of (

75
]
when the electromagnetic field is zero

We first observe following property of
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
.
The conservation laws of
f
in (

Lemma 5.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mathcal{A}(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mathcal{B}(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mathcal{C}(x,t)dx=0.

Proof.

The conservation laws in (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}dxdp~(F-F_{0})\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\cr p^{\mu}\end{array}\right)

Combining with the assumption (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}dxdp~f\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\cr p^{\mu}\end{array}\right)=0.

We derived desired results.
∎

We now establish the estimates of
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}
. The proof of the following lemma is motivated by the proof of the estimate of (108) in
[

]

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\left\{\|\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{B}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{C}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right\}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right).

Proof.

We first prove the estimate of
\mathcal{A}
. Taking
\partial^{\alpha}
on the first equation of (
5.4
) gives

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\partial^{\alpha}l_{a}+\partial^{\alpha}h_{a}.

we take the innerproduct with
\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}
and apply the Hölder inequality to have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}l_{a}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}h_{a}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\right).

Dividing each side by
\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq C(\|\partial^{\alpha}l_{a}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}h_{a}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}).

Similarly we take
\partial^{\alpha}
on the second equation of (
5.4
) to have

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\partial^{\alpha}l_{i}+\partial^{\alpha}h_{i}.

Multiplying
\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{A}
on each side and applying the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{A}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq C(\|\partial^{\alpha}l_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}h_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}).

We apply the Poincaré inequality on
\mathcal{A}
to have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}-\left\|\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{3}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mathcal{A}(x)dx\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{A}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Then the conservation law for
\mathcal{A}
in Lemma
5.1

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{A}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

This completes the estimate of
\mathcal{A}
.
Since
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are connected by the third equation of (
5.4
), we consider the fourth and the fifth equations of (
5.4
) first. For the notational brevity, we use
\partial_{j}
to denote
\partial_{x_{j}}
. Using the fourth equation of (
5.4
), we calculate

\displaystyle\triangle\mathcal{B}_{i}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq 3}\partial_{jj}\mathcal{B}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq i}\partial_{jj}\mathcal{B}_{i}+\partial_{ii}\mathcal{B}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\partial_{j}l_{ij}+\partial_{j}h_{ij}-\partial_{ji}\mathcal{B}_{j}\right)+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}l_{ii}+\partial_{i}h_{ii}).

We substitute the fourth equation for
i=j

\displaystyle\triangle\mathcal{B}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\partial_{j}l_{ij}+\partial_{j}h_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}l_{jj}+\partial_{i}h_{jj})\right)+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}l_{ii}+\partial_{i}h_{ii}).

Taking
\partial^{\alpha}
and multiplying
\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{B}_{i}
on each side yield

(5.5)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}l_{ij}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}h_{ij}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right).

We take
\partial^{\alpha}
on the fifth equation of (
5.4
) to have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\mathcal{C}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq C\|\partial^{\alpha}l_{c}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}h_{c}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Now we use the third equation of (
5.4
). We consider the estimate of
\mathcal{B}
when there are more than one temporal derivatives. For this, we take the temporal derivative
\partial_{t}^{n}
for
1\leq n\leq N-1
on the third equation of (

) to have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{n}\partial_{t}\mathcal{B}_{i}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}^{n}l_{bci}+\partial_{t}^{n}h_{bci}-\partial_{t}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{C}.

Then the estimate (
5.6
) gives

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{n+1}\mathcal{B}_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{t}^{n}l_{bci}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial_{t}^{n}h_{bci}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+C\left(\|\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}^{n-1}l_{c}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}^{n-1}h_{c}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right).

For the estimate of
\mathcal{B}
when there are less than two temporal derivatives, we apply the Poincaré inequality on
\mathcal{B}
and
\partial_{t}\mathcal{B}
to have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{n}\mathcal{B}_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}-\left\|\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}^{3}|}\partial_{t}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mathcal{B}_{i}dx\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}^{n}\mathcal{B}_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

for

5.1
yields

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{n}\mathcal{B}_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}^{n}\mathcal{B}_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Combining with (

(5.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{B}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right).

For the estimate of
\mathcal{C}
, we take
\partial^{\alpha}
on third equation of (

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\partial^{\alpha}l_{bci}+\partial^{\alpha}h_{bci}-\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\mathcal{B}_{i}.

Applying (

(5.9)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{C}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\beta|=|\alpha|}\left(\|\partial^{\beta}\tilde{l}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\beta}\tilde{h}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right).

Then the Poincaré inequality with Lemma

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{C}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
N\geq 3
. Suppose that

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}\leq M_{0}.

Then we have

\displaystyle(1)\ \sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|(I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu},

\displaystyle(2)\ \sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C(\sqrt{M_{0}}+M_{0})\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}.

Proof.

For the Newtonian Boltzmann equation and the relativistic Landau-Maxwell system, analogous proofs can be found in
[
38
]
and
[
75
]
, respectively. The difference now is that the estimate of the nonlinear term
h
includes the third-order nonlinear terms.

(1) We denote the
14
-basis of (
5.2
) as
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq 14}
, and let
\{e_{i}^{*}\}_{1\leq i\leq 14}
be the corresponding orthonormal basis. Then the orthonormal basis can be written by a linear combination of the original basis

as follows:

\displaystyle e_{i}^{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{14}C_{ij}e_{j},

for

. We consider the orthonormal expansion of

as follows:

\displaystyle l=\sum_{i=1}^{14}\langle l,e_{i}^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}e_{i}^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{14}\left\langle l,\sum_{j=1}^{14}C_{ij}e_{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}\sum_{k=1}^{14}C_{ik}e_{k}.

Then the coefficient of

can be read as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 14}C_{ij}C_{ik}\langle l,e_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}},

which correspond to

, and

.
By the definition of

in (
5.3
) and the linear operator

in Proposition

, we can write

as

\displaystyle l

\displaystyle=-(\partial_{t}+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}+\nu+K_{1}-K_{2})((I-P)f).

For
|\alpha|=N-1
, we have

\bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~\partial^{\alpha}l\cdot e_{i}(p)\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\cr\leq\bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~\bigg(\sum_{|\beta|=N}(1+\hat{p})(I-P)\partial^{\beta}f+(\nu+K_{1}-K_{2})(I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f\bigg)\cdot e_{i}(p)\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

We use the Hölder inequality on the

\nu(I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp~\bigg(\sum_{|\beta|=N}(1+\hat{p})(I-P)\partial^{\beta}f+\nu(I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f\bigg)\cdot e_{i}(p)\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|(I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}},

where we used the exponential decay
\sqrt{m+\tau m^{2}}
of the basis

in (

) that implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left((1+\hat{p})^{2}+\nu(p)\right)e_{i}^{2}(p)dp\leq C.

From the estimate of the compact operator

from Lemma
3.6
, we also have

\displaystyle\langle K_{j}f,e_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\nu},

for

and
i=1,\cdots,14
. The estimates above together yield

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial^{\alpha}l\cdot e_{i}(p)dp\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\|(I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}}+\|(I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|(I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}.

(2) In the same manner, the coefficient of
e_{k}
of expansion of

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 14}C_{ij}C_{ik}\langle h,e_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}.

\displaystyle\big|\langle\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma(f,f),e_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}\big|\leq C\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|\leq|\alpha|}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f\|_{x,\nu}\leq C\sqrt{M_{0}}\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|\leq|\alpha|}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{x,\nu}.

For the third-order nonlinear term, Lemma

\displaystyle\big|\langle\partial^{\alpha}T(f,f,f),e_{i}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial^{\alpha}T(f,f,f)\cdot e_{i}(p)dp\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}Pf\|_{x,\nu}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\left(\|(I-P)\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}+C\sqrt{M_{0}}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}\right).

Thus, for a sufficiently small

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\langle L\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}

\displaystyle\geq\delta\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}^{2},

by Lemma

.

Global existence

1.13
) yields the following linearized equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}f+Lf

\displaystyle=\Gamma(f)+T(f),

\displaystyle f(x,p,0)

\displaystyle=f_{0}(x,p).

We take
\partial^{\alpha}
on each side to have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}f+\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}f+L\partial^{\alpha}f

\displaystyle=\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma(f)+\partial^{\alpha}T(f).

Taking the inner product with
\partial^{\alpha}f
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}+\langle L\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}

\displaystyle=\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}+\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}T(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}.

Applying the full coercivity estimate (
5.10
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}+\delta\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}+\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}T(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}.

The estimate of the second-order nonlinear terms in Lemma
3.4
gives

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|\leq N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx~\left(\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f\|_{\nu}+\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{\nu}\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\right)\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\nu}.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\alpha_{1}
is less than or equal to
\alpha_{2}
. Since
N\geq 3
, we have
|\alpha_{1}|+2\leq N
. Thus the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\subset\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
implies

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}^{2}.

For the estimate of the third-order nonlinear terms, we apply Lemma
3.7
to have

\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}T(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|+|\alpha_{3}|\leq N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}dx~\big(\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{3}}f\|_{\nu}\cr+\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f\|_{\nu}\|\partial^{\alpha_{3}}f\|_{L^{2}_{p}}+\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{\nu}\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{3}}f\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\big)\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\nu}.

Similarly we assume that
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are less than or equal to
\alpha_{3}
.
Since
N\geq 3
, we have
|\alpha_{1}|+2\leq N
and
|\alpha_{2}|+2\leq N
. By the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\subset\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}T(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}^{2}.

Combining these estimates, we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}+\delta\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}+\mathcal{E}(t)\right)\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{x,\nu}^{2}.

The remaining proof can be established by the standard continuity argument as in
[
38
,
37
]
. This completes the proof.

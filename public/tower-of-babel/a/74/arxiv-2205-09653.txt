Self-Consistent Dynamical Field Theory of Kernel Evolution in Wide Neural Networks
Blake Bordelon & Cengiz Pehlevan

Abstract
We analyze feature learning in infinite-width neural networks trained with gradient flow through a self-consistent dynamical field theory. We construct a collection of deterministic dynamical order parameters which are inner-product kernels for hidden unit activations and gradients in each layer at pairs of time points, providing a reduced description of network activity through training. These kernel order parameters collectively define the hidden layer activation distribution, the evolution of the neural tangent kernel, and consequently output predictions. We show that the field theory derivation recovers the recursive stochastic process of infinite-width feature learning networks obtained from Yang & Hu with Tensor Programs [ 1 ] . For deep linear networks, these kernels satisfy a set of algebraic matrix equations. For nonlinear networks, we provide an alternating sampling procedure to self-consistently solve for the kernel order parameters. We provide comparisons of the self-consistent solution to various approximation schemes including the static NTK approximation, gradient independence assumption, and leading order perturbation theory, showing that each of these approximations can break down in regimes where general self-consistent solutions still provide an accurate description. Lastly, we provide experiments in more realistic settings which demonstrate that the loss and kernel dynamics of CNNs at fixed feature learning strength is preserved across different widths on a CIFAR classification task.

Recently developed alternative parameterizations such as the mean field

Our novel contributions in this paper are the following:

1.

We develop a path integral formulation of gradient flow dynamics in infinite-width networks in the feature learning regime. Our parameterization includes a scalar parameter
\gamma_{0}
to allow interpolation between rich and lazy regimes and comparison to perturbative methods.

2.

Using a stationary action argument, we identify a set of saddle point equations that the kernels satisfy at infinite-width, relating the stochastic processes that define hidden activation evolution to the kernels and vice versa. We show that our saddle point equations recover at
\gamma_{0}=1

[

3.

We develop a polynomial-time numerical procedure to solve the saddle point equations for deep networks. In numerical experiments, we demonstrate that solutions to these self-consistency equations are predictive of network training at a variety of feature learning strengths, widths and depths. We provide comparisons of our theory to various approximate methods, such as perturbation theory.

Problem Setup and Definitions

Our theory applies to infinite-width networks, both fully-connected and convolutional. For notational ease we will relegate convolutional results to later sections. For input
\bm{x}_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
, we define the hidden
pre-activation
vectors
\bm{h}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
for layers
\ell\in\{1,...,L\}
as

\displaystyle f_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\gamma\sqrt{N}}\bm{w}^{L}\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{L}_{\mu})\ ,\quad\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu})\ ,\quad\bm{h}^{1}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{W}^{0}\bm{x}_{\mu},

(1)

where
\bm{\theta}=\text{Vec}\{\bm{W}^{0},...,\bm{w}^{L}\}
are the trainable parameters of the network and
\phi
is a twice differentiable activation function. Inspired by previous works on the mechanisms of lazy gradient based training, the parameter
\gamma
will control the laziness or richness of the training dynamics
[
18
,
19
,
1
,
42
]
. Each of the trainable parameters are initialized as Gaussian random variables with unit variance
W^{\ell}_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. They evolve under gradient flow
\frac{d}{dt}\bm{\theta}=-\gamma^{2}\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\mathcal{L}
. The choice of learning rate
\gamma^{2}
causes
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}|_{t=0}
to be independent of
\gamma
. To characterize the evolution of weights, we introduce backpropagation variables
\bm{g}_{\mu}^{\ell}=\gamma\sqrt{N}\frac{\partial f_{\mu}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}}=\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu})\odot\bm{z}^{\ell}_{\mu}
, where
\bm{z}^{\ell}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}
is the
pre-gradient
signal.

The relevant dynamical objects to characterize feature learning are feature and gradient kernels for each hidden layer
\ell\in\{1,...,L\}
, defined as

\displaystyle\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\frac{1}{N}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))\ ,\quad G_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s).

(2)

From the kernels
\{\Phi^{\ell},G^{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}
, we can compute the
Neural Tangent Kernel

K^{NTK}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\nabla_{\theta}f_{\mu}(t)\cdot\nabla_{\theta}f_{\alpha}(s)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s),

[
7
]
and the dynamics of the network function
f_{\mu}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}f_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}K^{NTK}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)\Delta_{\alpha}(t)\ ,\quad\Delta_{\mu}(t)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial f_{\mu}}\mathcal{L}|_{f_{\mu}(t)},

(3)

where we define base cases
G_{\mu\alpha}^{L+1}(t,s)=1,\Phi^{0}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}=\frac{1}{D}\bm{x}_{\mu}\cdot\bm{x}_{\alpha}
. We note that the above formula holds for any data point
\mu
which may or may not be in the set of
P
training examples. The above expressions demonstrate that knowledge of the temporal trajectory of the NTK on the
t=s
diagonal gives the temporal trajectory of the network predictions
f_{\mu}(t)
.

Following prior works on infinite-width networks
[
22
,
1
,
40
,
19
]
, we study the mean field limit

\displaystyle N,\gamma\to\infty\ ,\quad\gamma_{0}=\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{N}}=\mathcal{O}_{N}(1)

(4)

As we demonstrate in the Appendix
D
and
N
, this is the only
N
-scaling which allows feature learning as
N\to\infty
. The
\gamma_{0}=0
limit recovers the static NTK limit
[
7
]
. We discuss other scalings and parameterizations in Appendix
N
, relating our work to the
\mu P
-parameterization and TP analysis of
[

, showing they have identical feature dynamics in the infinite-width limit. We also analyze the effect of different hidden layer widths and initialization variances in the Appendix
D.8
. We focus on equal widths and NTK parameterization (as in eq. (
1
)) in the main text to reduce complexity.

3
Self-consistent DMFT

Next, we derive our self-consistent DMFT in a limit where
t,P=\mathcal{O}_{N}(1)
. Our goal is to build a description of training dynamics purely based on representations, and independent of weights. Studying feature learning at infinite-width enjoys several analytical properties:

•

The kernel order parameters
\Phi^{\ell},G^{\ell}
concentrate over random initializations but are dynamical, allowing flexible adaptation of features to the task structure.

•

In each layer
\ell
, each neuron’s preactivation
h_{i}^{\ell}
and pregradient
z^{\ell}_{i}
become i.i.d. draws from a distribution characterized by a set of order parameters
\{\Phi^{\ell},G^{\ell},A^{\ell},B^{\ell}\}
.

•

The kernels are defined as self-consistent averages (denoted by
\left<\right>
) over this distribution of neurons in each layer
\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\left<\phi(h_{\mu}^{\ell}(t))\phi(h_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s))\right>
and
G_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)=\left<g_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)g_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s)\right>
.

The next section derives these facts from a path-integral formulation of gradient flow dynamics.

3.1
Path Integral Construction

Gradient flow after a random initialization of weights defines a high dimensional stochastic process over initalizations for variables
\{\bm{h},\bm{g}\}
. Therefore, we will utilize DMFT formalism to obtain a reduced description of network activity during training. For a simplified derivation of the DMFT for the two-layer (
L=1
) case, see
D.2
. Generally, we separate the contribution on each forward/backward pass between the initial condition and gradient updates to weight matrix
\bm{W}^{\ell}
, defining new stochastic variables
\bm{\chi}^{\ell},\bm{\xi}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
as

\displaystyle\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\ ,\quad\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t).

(5)

We let
Z
represent the moment generating functional (MGF) for these stochastic fields

\displaystyle Z[\{\bm{j}^{\ell},\bm{v}^{\ell}\}]=\left<\exp\left(\sum_{\ell,\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\left[\bm{j}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\right]\right)\right>_{\{\bm{W}^{0}(0),...\bm{w}^{L}(0)\}},

which requires, by construction the normalization condition
Z[\{\bm{0},\bm{0}\}]=1
. We enforce our definition of
\bm{\chi},\bm{\xi}
using an integral representation of the delta-function. Thus for each sample
\mu\in[P]
and each time
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we multiply
Z
by

\displaystyle 1=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{d\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)d\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)}{(2\pi)^{N}}\exp\left(i\hat{\bm{\chi}}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)\cdot\left[\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\right]\right),

(6)

for
\bm{\chi}
and the respective expression for
\bm{\xi}
. After making such substitutions, we perform integration over initial Gaussian weight matrices to arrive at an integral expression for
Z
, which we derive in the appendix
D.4
. We show that
Z
can be described by set of order-parameters
\{\Phi^{\ell},\hat{\Phi}^{\ell},G^{\ell},\hat{G}^{\ell},A^{\ell},B^{\ell}\}

\displaystyle Z[\{\bm{j}^{\ell},\bm{v}^{\ell}\}]\propto\int\prod_{\ell\mu\alpha ts}

\displaystyle d\Phi_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)d\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)dG^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)d\hat{G}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)dA^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)dB^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

(7)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left({NS[\{\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G},A,B,j,v\}]}\right),

\displaystyle S=\sum_{\ell\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds

\displaystyle\left[\Phi_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)\hat{\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)+G^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\hat{G}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-A^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]

\displaystyle+\ln\mathcal{Z}[\{\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G},A,B,j,v\}],

(8)

where
S
is the DMFT action and
\mathcal{Z}
is a single-site MGF, which defines the distribution of fields
\{\chi^{\ell},\xi^{\ell}\}
over the neural population in each layer. The kernels
A
and
B
are related to the correlations between feedforward and feedback signals in the network. We provide a detailed formula for
\mathcal{Z}
in the Appendix
D.4
and show that it factorizes over different layers
\mathcal{Z}=\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}
.

3.2
Deriving the DMFT Equations from the Path Integral Saddle Point

As
N\to\infty
, the moment-generating function
Z
is exponentially dominated by the saddle point of
S
. The equations that define this saddle point also define our DMFT. We thus identify the kernels that render
S
locally stationary (
\delta S=0
). The most important equations are those which define
\{\Phi^{\ell},G^{\ell}\}

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}

\displaystyle={\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{Z}}{\delta\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}={\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\left<\phi(h_{\mu}^{\ell}(t))\phi(h_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s))\right>=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta\hat{G}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=G_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{Z}}{\delta\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)}=G^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\left<g_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)g_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s)\right>=0,

(9)

where
\left<\right>
denotes an average over the stochastic process induced by
\mathcal{Z}
, which is defined below

\displaystyle\{u_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\}_{\mu\in[P],t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1})\ ,\ \{r_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\}_{\mu\in[P],t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{G}^{\ell+1}),

\displaystyle h_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\left[A_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell-1}(t,s)+\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]z_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s)\dot{\phi}(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)),

\displaystyle z_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=r_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\left[B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\Delta_{\alpha}(s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\phi(h_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s)),

(10)

where we define base cases
\Phi^{0}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}
and
G^{L+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=1
,
A^{0}=B^{L}=0
. We see that the fields
\{h^{\ell},z^{\ell}\}
, which represent the single site preactivations and pre-gradients, are implicit functionals of the mean-zero Gaussian processes
\{u^{\ell},r^{\ell}\}
which have covariances
\left<u^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)u^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\right>=\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)
and
\left<r^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)r^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\right>=G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)
. The other saddle point equations give
A_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)=\gamma_{0}^{-1}\left<\frac{\delta\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))}{\delta r^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)}\right>,B_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)=\gamma_{0}^{-1}\left<\frac{\delta g^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)}{\delta u^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(s)}\right>
which arise due to coupling between the feedforward and feedback signals. We note that, in the lazy limit
\gamma_{0}\to 0
, the fields approach Gaussian processes
h^{\ell}\to u^{\ell}
,
z^{\ell}\to r^{\ell}
. Lastly, the final saddle point equations
\frac{\delta S}{\delta\Phi^{\ell}}=0,\frac{\delta S}{\delta G^{\ell}}=0
imply that
\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}=\hat{G}^{\ell}=0
. The full set of equations that define the DMFT are given in
D.7
.

This theory is easily extended to more general architectures such as networks with varying widths by layer (App.
D.8

To describe the result, we first introduce a vectorization notation
\bm{h}^{\ell}=\text{Vec}\{h_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\}_{\mu\in[P],t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
. Likewise we convert kernels
\bm{H}^{\ell}=\text{Mat}\{H^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\}_{\mu,\alpha\in[P],t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}

\bm{a}\cdot\bm{b}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\sum_{\mu=1}^{P}a_{\mu}(t)b_{\mu}(t)
. In a practical computational implementation, the theory would be evaluated on a grid of
T

\bm{H}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{PT\times PT}
would indeed be matrices of the appropriate size. The fields
\bm{h}^{\ell},\bm{g}^{\ell}
are linear functionals of independent Gaussian processes
\bm{u}^{\ell},\bm{r}^{\ell}
, giving
(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{D}^{\ell})\bm{h}^{\ell}=\bm{u}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{r}^{\ell}\ ,\ (\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{\ell})\bm{g}^{\ell}=\bm{r}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{u}^{\ell}
.
The matrices
\bm{C}^{\ell}
and
\bm{D}^{\ell}
are causal integral operators which depend on
\{\bm{A}^{\ell-1},\bm{H}^{\ell-1}\}
and
\{\bm{B}^{\ell},\bm{G}^{\ell+1}\}
respectively which we define in Appendix
F
. The saddle point equations which define the kernels are

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left<\bm{h}^{\ell}\bm{h}^{\ell\top}\right>=(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{D}^{\ell})^{-1}[\bm{H}^{\ell-1}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{G}^{\ell+1}\bm{C}^{\ell\top}]\left[(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{D}^{\ell})^{-1}\right]^{\top}

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left<\bm{g}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell\top}\right>=\left(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{\ell}\right)^{-1}\left[\bm{G}^{\ell+1}+\gamma^{2}_{0}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{H}^{\ell-1}\bm{D}^{\ell\top}\right]\left[\left(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{\ell}\right)^{-1}\right]^{\top}.

(11)

Examples of the predictions obtained by solving these systems of equations are provided in Figure

. We see that these DMFT equations describe kernel evolution for networks of a variety of depths and that the change in each layer’s kernel increases with the depth of the network.

Unlike many prior results
[

,

,
70
,
71
]
, our DMFT does not require any restrictions on the structure of the input data but hold for any
\bm{K}^{x},\bm{y}
. However, for whitened data
\bm{K}^{x}=\mathbf{I}
we show in Appendix
F.1.1
,
F.2
that our DMFT learning curves interpolate between NTK dynamics and the sigmoidal trajectories of prior works
[
68
,
69
]
as
\gamma_{0}
is increased. For example, in the two layer (
L=1
) linear network with
\bm{K}^{x}=\mathbf{I}
, the dynamics of the error norm
\Delta(t)=||\bm{\Delta}(t)||
takes the form
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Delta(t)=-2\sqrt{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}(y-\Delta(t))^{2}}\Delta(t)
where
y=||\bm{y}||
. These dynamics give the linear convergence rate of the NTK if
\gamma_{0}\to 0
but approaches logistic dynamics of
[
69
]
as
\gamma_{0}\to\infty
. Further,
\bm{H}(t)=\left<\bm{h}^{1}(t)\bm{h}^{1}(t)^{\top}\right>\in\mathbb{R}^{P\times P}
only grows in the
\bm{y}\bm{y}^{\top}
direction with
H_{y}(t)=\frac{1}{y^{2}}\bm{y}^{\top}\bm{H}(t)\bm{y}=\sqrt{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}(y-\Delta(t))^{2}}
. At the end of training
\bm{H}(t)\to\mathbf{I}+\frac{1}{y^{2}}[\sqrt{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}y^{2}}-1]\bm{y}\bm{y}^{\top}
, recovering the rank one spike which was recently obtained in the small initialization limit
[

]
. We show this one dimensional system in Figure

4.2
Feature Learning with L2 Regularization

As we show in Appendix
J
, the DMFT can be extended to networks trained with weight decay
\frac{d\bm{\theta}}{dt}=-\gamma^{2}\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\mathcal{L}-\lambda\bm{\theta}
. If neural network is homogenous in its parameters so that
f(c\bm{\theta})=c^{\kappa}f(\bm{\theta})
(examples include networks with linear, ReLU, quadratic activations), then the final network predictor is a kernel regressor with the final NTK
\lim_{t\to\infty}f(\bm{x},t)=\bm{k}(\bm{x})^{\top}[\bm{K}+\lambda\kappa\mathbf{I}]^{-1}\bm{y}
where
K(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})
is the
final
-NTK,
[\bm{k}(\bm{x})]_{\mu}=K(\bm{x},\bm{x}_{\mu})
and
[\bm{K}]_{\mu\alpha}=K(\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\alpha})
. We note that the effective regularization
\lambda\kappa
increases with depth
L
. In NTK parameterization, weight decay in infinite width homogenous networks gives a trivial fixed point

and consequently a zero predictor

[

]

can prevent convergence to the trivial fixed point, allowing a non-zero fixed point for

even at infinite width. The kernel and function dynamics can be predicted with DMFT. The fixed point is a nontrivial function of the hyperparameters
\lambda,\kappa,L,\gamma_{0}

5
Approximation Schemes

We now compare our exact DMFT with approximations of prior works, providing an explanation of when these approximations give accurate predictions and when they break down.

5.1
Gradient Independence Ansatz

We can study the accuracy of the ansatz
\bm{A}^{\ell}=\bm{B}^{\ell}=0
, which is equivalent to treating the weight matrices
\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)
and
\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)^{\top}
which appear in forward and backward passes respectively as independent Gaussian matrices. This assumption was utilized in prior works on signal propagation in deep networks in the lazy regime
[
75
,
76
,
77
,
78
,
79
]
. A consequence of this approximation is the Gaussianity and statistical independence of
\chi^{\ell}
and
\xi^{\ell}
(conditional on
\{\bm{\Phi}^{\ell},\bm{G}^{\ell}\}
) in each layer as we show in Appendix
O
. This ansatz works very well near
\gamma_{0}\approx 0
(the static kernel regime) since
\frac{d\bm{h}}{d\bm{r}},\frac{d\bm{z}}{d\bm{u}}\sim\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0})
or around initialization
t\approx 0
but begins to fail at larger values of
\gamma_{0},t
(Figure
4
).

5.2
Perturbation theory in
\gamma_{0}
at infinite-width

In the
\gamma_{0}\to 0
limit, we recover static kernels, giving linear dynamics identical to the NTK limit
[
7
]
. Corrections to this lazy limit can be extracted at small but finite
\gamma_{0}
. This is conceptually similar to recent works which consider perturbation series for the NTK in powers of
1/N

[
35
,
27
,
28
]
(though not identical, see Appendix
P.7
for finite
N
effects). We expand all observables
q(\gamma_{0})
in a power series in
\gamma_{0}
, giving
q(\gamma_{0})=q^{(0)}+\gamma_{0}q^{(1)}+\gamma_{0}^{2}q^{(2)}+...
and compute corrections up to
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{2})
. We show that the
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0})
and
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{3})
corrections to kernels vanish, giving leading order expansions of the form
\bm{\Phi}=\bm{\Phi}^{0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{\Phi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})
and
\bm{G}=\bm{G}^{0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{G}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})
(see Appendix
P.2
). Further, we show that the NTK has relative change at leading order which scales linearly with depth
|\Delta K^{NTK}|/|K^{NTK,0}|\sim\mathcal{O}_{\gamma_{0},L}(L\gamma_{0}^{2})=\mathcal{O}_{N,\gamma,L}(\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{N})
, which is consistent with finite width effective field theory at
\gamma=\mathcal{O}_{N}(1)

[
26
,
27
,
28
]
(Appendix
P.6
). Further, at the leading order correction, all temporal dependencies are controlled by
P(P+1)
functions
v_{\alpha}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}ds\Delta^{0}_{\alpha}(s)
and
v_{\alpha\beta}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}ds\Delta^{0}_{\alpha}(s)\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}\Delta^{0}_{\beta}(s^{\prime})
, which is consistent with those derived for finite width NNs using a truncation of the Neural Tangent Hierarchy
[
34
,
35
,
27
]
. To lighten notation, we focus our main text comparison of our non-perturbative DMFT to perturbation theory in the deep linear case. Full perturbation theory is in Appendix
P.2
.

Using the timescales derived in the previous section, we find that the leading order correction to the kernels in infinite-width deep linear network have the form

\displaystyle K^{NTK}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)=(L+1)K_{\mu\nu}^{x}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\frac{L(L+1)}{2}K^{x}_{\mu\nu}\sum_{\alpha\beta}K^{x}_{\alpha\beta}[v_{\alpha\beta}(t)+v_{\beta\alpha}(s)+v_{\alpha}(t)v_{\beta}(s)]

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma_{0}^{2}\frac{L(L+1)}{2}\left[\sum_{\alpha\beta}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}K^{x}_{\nu\beta}[v_{\alpha\beta}(t)+v_{\beta\alpha}(s)]+\sum_{\alpha\beta}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}K^{x}_{\nu\beta}v_{\alpha}(t)v_{\beta}(s)\right]+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4}).

(12)

We see that the relative change in the NTK
|\bm{K}^{NTK}-\bm{K}^{NTK}(0)|/|\bm{K}^{NTK}(0)|\sim\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{2}L)=\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{2}L/N)
, so that large depth
L
networks exhibit more significant kernel evolution, which agrees with other perturbative studies
[
35
,
27
,
25
]
as well as the non-perturbative results in Figure
2
. However at large
\gamma_{0}
and large
L
, this theory begins to break down as we show in Figure

The DMFT formalism can also be used to extract leading corrections to observables at large but finite width
N
as we explore in
P.7
. When deviating from infinite width, the kernels are no longer deterministic over network initializations. The key observation is that the DMFT action
S
defines a Gibbs measure over the space of kernel order parameters
\bm{k}=\text{Vec}\{\bm{\Phi}^{\ell},\bm{G}^{\ell},\bm{A}^{\ell},\bm{B}^{\ell}\}
with probability density
\frac{1}{Z}\exp\left(NS[\bm{k}]\right)
where
Z
is a normalization constant. Near infinite width, any observable average
\left<O(\bm{k})\right>=\frac{1}{Z}\int d\bm{k}\exp\left(NS[\bm{k}]\right)O(\bm{k})
is dominated by order parameters within a
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}
neighborhood of
\bm{k}^{*}
. As a consequence, a perturbative series for
\left<O(\bm{k})\right>
can be obtained from simple averages over Gaussian fluctuations in the kernels
\bm{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{k}^{*},-\frac{1}{N}[\nabla^{2}S[\bm{k}^{*}]]^{-1})

[
29
]
. The components for
\nabla^{2}S[\bm{k}^{*}]
include four point correlations of fields computed over the DMFT distribution.

7
Discussion

We provided a unifying DMFT derivation of feature dynamics in infinite networks trained with gradient based optimization. Our theory interpolates between lazy infinite-width behavior of a static NTK in
\gamma_{0}\to 0
and rich feature learning. At
\gamma_{0}=1
, our DMFT construction agrees with the stochastic process derived previously with the Tensor Programs framework
[
1
]
.
Our saddle point equations give self-consistency conditions which relate the stochastic fields to the kernels. These equations are exactly solveable in deep linear networks and can be efficiently solved with a numerical method in the nonlinear case. Comparisons with other approximation schemes show that DMFT can be accurate at a much wider range of
\gamma_{0}
. We believe our framework could be a useful perspective for future theoretical analyses of feature learning and generalization in wide networks.

Though our DMFT is quite general in regards to the data and architecture, the technique is not entirely rigorous and relies on heuristic physics techniques. Our theory holds in the
T,P=\mathcal{O}_{N}(1)
and may break down otherwise; other asymptotic regimes (such as
P/N,T/\log(N)=\mathcal{O}_{N}(1)
, etc) may exhibit phenomena relevant to deep learning practice
[
32
,
82
]
. The computational requirements of our method, while smaller than the exponential time complexity for exact solution
[
1
]
, are still significant for large
PT
. In Table
1
, we compare the time taken for various theories to compute the feature kernels throughout
T
steps of gradient descent. For a width
N
network, computation of each forward pass on all
P
data points takes
\mathcal{O}(PN^{2})
computations. The static NTK requires computation of
\mathcal{O}(P^{2})
entries in the kernel which do not need to be recomputed. However, the DMFT requires matrix multiplications on
PT\times PT
matrices giving a
\mathcal{O}(P^{3}T^{3})
time scaling. Future work could aim to improve the computational overhead of the algorithm, by considering data averaged theories
[
64
]
or one pass SGD
[
1
]
. Alternative projected versions of gradient descent have also enabled much better computational scaling in evaluation of the theoretical predictions
[
46
]
, allowing evaluation on full CIFAR-10.

Appendix D
Derivation of Self-Consistent Dynamical Field Theory

In this section, we introduce the dynamical field theory setup and saddle point equations. The path integral theory we develop is based on the Martin-Siggia-Rose-De Dominicis-Janssen (MSRDJ) framework
[
47
]
, of which a useful review for random recurent networks can be found here
[
54
]
. Similar computations can be found in recent works which consider typical behavior in high dimensional classification on random data
[
63
,
64
]
.

D.1
Deep Network Field Definitions and Scaling

As discussed in the main text, we consider the following wide network architecture parameterzied by trainable weights
\bm{\theta}=\text{Vec}\{\bm{W}^{0},\bm{W}^{1},...\bm{w}^{L}\}
, giving network output
f_{\mu}
defined as

\displaystyle f_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma}h^{L+1}_{\mu}\ ,\ h^{L+1}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{w}_{L}\cdot\phi(\bm{h}_{\mu}^{L})

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}\phi(\bm{h}_{\mu}^{\ell})\ ,\ \bm{h}^{1}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{W}^{0}\bm{x}_{\mu}

(13)

Using gradient flow with learning rate
\eta
on cost
\mathcal{L}=\sum_{\mu}\ell(f_{\mu},y_{\mu})
for loss function, we introduce functions
\Delta_{\mu}=-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial f_{\mu}}
and
\eta
for learning rate, gradient flow induces the following dynamics

\displaystyle\frac{d\bm{\theta}}{dt}=\frac{\eta}{\gamma}\sum_{\mu}\Delta_{\mu}\frac{\partial h_{\mu}^{L+1}}{\partial\bm{\theta}}\ ,\ \frac{\partial f_{\mu}}{\partial t}=\frac{\eta}{\gamma^{2}}\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}K^{NTK}_{\mu\alpha}\ ,\ K^{NTK}_{\mu\alpha}=\frac{\partial h^{L+1}_{\mu}}{\partial\bm{\theta}}\cdot\frac{\partial h_{\alpha}^{L+1}}{\partial\bm{\theta}}

(14)

Since
K_{NTK}
is
O_{\gamma}(1)
at initialization, it is clear that to have
O_{\gamma}(1)
evolution of the network output at initialization we need
\eta=\gamma^{2}
. With this scaling, we have the following

\displaystyle\frac{d\bm{\theta}}{dt}=\gamma\sum_{\mu}\Delta_{\mu}\frac{\partial h_{\mu}^{L+1}}{\partial\bm{\theta}}\ ,\ \frac{\partial f_{\mu}}{\partial t}=\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}K^{NTK}_{\mu\alpha}

(15)

Now, to build a valid field theory, we want to express everything in terms of features
\bm{h}_{\mu}^{\ell}
rather than parameters
\bm{\theta}
and we will define the following gradient features
\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}=\sqrt{N}\frac{\partial h^{L+1}_{\mu}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}}
which admit the recursion and base case

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{N}\frac{\partial h^{L+1}_{\mu}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}}=\left(\frac{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}}\right)^{\top}\left(\sqrt{N}\frac{\partial h^{L+1}_{\mu}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}}\right)=\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu})\odot\bm{z}_{\mu}^{\ell}\ ,\ \bm{z}_{\mu}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{L}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}^{L}_{\mu})\odot\bm{w}^{L}

(16)

We define the
pre-gradient
field
\bm{z}^{\ell}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}
so that
\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}=\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu})\odot\bm{z}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)
. From these quantities, we can derive the gradients with respect to parameters

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{\mu}^{L+1}}{\partial\bm{W}^{\ell}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial h_{\mu}^{L+1}}{\partial h^{\ell+1}_{\mu,i}}\frac{\partial h_{\mu,i}^{\ell+1}}{\partial\bm{W}^{\ell}}=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}\phi(\bm{h}_{\mu}^{\ell})^{\top}

(17)

which allows us to compute the NTK in terms of these features

\displaystyle K^{NTK}_{\mu\alpha}=\frac{1}{N}\phi(\bm{h}^{L}_{\mu})\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{L}_{\alpha})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\left(\frac{\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}}{N}\right)\left(\frac{\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu})\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\alpha})}{N}\right)+\frac{\bm{g}^{1}_{\mu}\cdot\bm{g}_{\alpha}^{1}}{N}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}

(18)

where
K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}=\frac{\bm{x}_{\mu}\cdot\bm{x}_{\alpha}}{D}
is the input Grammian. We see that the NTK can be built out of the following primitive kernels

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mu\nu}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{N}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu})\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\nu})\ ,\ G_{\mu\nu}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\nu}

(19)

We utilize the parameter space dynamics to express
\bm{W}^{\ell}
in terms of the
\{\bm{g},\bm{h}\}
fields

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)=\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)+\frac{\gamma}{N}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\mu}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\bm{g}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(s)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(s))^{\top}

(20)

Using the field recurrences
\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))
we can derive the following recursive dynamics for the features

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\nu}(t)\Phi_{\nu\mu}^{\ell}(s,t)

\displaystyle\bm{z}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\xi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}(s)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\nu}(s))G^{\ell+1}_{\nu\mu}(s,t)\ ,\ \bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)=\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\odot\bm{z}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{\mu}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(t)\left[\Phi_{\mu\alpha}^{L}(t,t)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)+G^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)K_{\mu\alpha}^{x}\right]

(21)

where we introduced the following random fields
\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t),\bm{\xi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)
which involve the random initial conditions

\displaystyle\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\phi(\bm{h}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t))\ ,\ \bm{\xi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)

(22)

We observe that the dynamics of the hidden features is controlled by the factor
\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{N}}
. If
\gamma=O_{N}(1)
then we recover static NTK in the limit as
N\to\infty
. However, if
\gamma=O_{N}(\sqrt{N})
then we obtain
O_{N}(1)
evolution of our features and we reach a new rich regime. We choose the scaling
\gamma=\gamma_{0}\sqrt{N}
for our field theory so that
\gamma_{0}>0
will give a feature learning network.

D.2
Warmup: DMFT for One Hidden Layer NN

In this section, we provide a warmup problem of a
L=1
hidden layer network which allows us to illustrate the mechanics of the MSRDJ formalism. A more detailed computation can be found in the next section. Though many of the interesting dynamical aspects of the deep network case are missing in the two layer case, our aim is to show a simple application of the ideas. The fields of interest are
\bm{\chi}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{W}^{0}(0)\bm{x}_{\mu}
and
\bm{\xi}=\bm{w}^{1}(0)
. Unlike the deeper
L\geq 2
case, both of these fields are time invariant since
\bm{x}_{\mu}
does not vary in time. These random fields provide initial conditions for the preactivation and pre-gradient fields
\bm{h}_{\mu}(t),\bm{z}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which evolve according to

\displaystyle\bm{h}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\chi}_{\mu}+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha}[\bm{z}(s)\odot\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}_{\alpha}(s))]K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)

\displaystyle\bm{z}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\xi}+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha}\phi(\bm{h}_{\alpha}(s))\Delta_{\alpha}(s).

(23)

where the network predictions evolve as
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f_{\mu}(t)=\sum_{\alpha}[\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)+G_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}]\Delta_{\alpha}(t)
for kernels
\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)=\frac{1}{N}\phi(\bm{h}_{\mu}(t))\cdot\phi(\bm{h}_{\alpha}(t))
and
G_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{g}_{\alpha}(t)
. At finite
N
, the kernels
\Phi,G
will depend on the random initial conditions
\bm{\chi},\bm{\xi}
, leading to a predictor
f_{\mu}
which varies over initializations. If we can establish that the kernels
\Phi,G
concentrate at infinite-width
N\to\infty
, then
\Delta_{\mu}
are deterministic. We now study the moment generating function for the fields

\displaystyle Z[\{\bm{\bm{j}}_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in[P]},\bm{v}]

\displaystyle=\left<\exp\left(\sum_{\mu}\bm{j}_{\mu}\cdot\bm{\chi}_{\mu}+\bm{\xi}\cdot\bm{v}\right)\right>_{\bm{\theta}_{0}}.

(24)

To perform the average over
\bm{\theta}_{0}=\{\bm{W}^{0}(0),\bm{w}^{1}(0)\}
, we enforce the definition of
\bm{\chi}_{\mu},\bm{\xi}
with delta functions

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{\chi}_{\mu}\delta\left(\bm{\chi}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{0}(0)\bm{x}_{\mu}\right)=\int\frac{d\bm{\chi}_{\mu}d\bm{\hat{\chi}}_{\mu}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\exp\left(i\bm{\hat{\chi}}_{\mu}\cdot\left(\bm{\chi}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{W}^{0}(0)\bm{x}_{\mu}\right)\right)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{\xi}\ \delta\left(\bm{\xi}-\bm{w}^{1}(0)\right)=\int\frac{d\bm{\xi}d\bm{\hat{\xi}}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\exp\left(i\bm{\hat{\xi}}\cdot\left(\bm{\xi}-\bm{w}^{1}(0)\right)\right).

(25)

Though this step may seem redundant in this example, it will be very helpful in the deep network case, so we pursue it for illustration. After mulitplying by these factors of unity and performing the Gaussian integrals, we obtain

\displaystyle Z=\int\prod_{\mu}\frac{d\bm{\chi}d\bm{\hat{\chi}}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\frac{d\bm{\xi}d\bm{\hat{\xi}}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\bm{\hat{\chi}}_{\mu}\cdot\bm{\hat{\chi}}_{\alpha}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}+\sum_{\mu}\bm{\chi}_{\mu}\cdot(i\bm{\hat{\chi}}_{\mu}+\bm{\bm{j}}_{\mu})-\frac{1}{2}|\bm{\hat{\xi}}|^{2}+\bm{\xi}\cdot(i\bm{\hat{\xi}}+\bm{v})\right)

(26)

We now aim enforce the definitions of the kernel order parameters with delta functions

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=N\int d\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\ \delta\left(N\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\phi(\bm{h}_{\mu}(t))\cdot\phi(\bm{h}_{\alpha}(s))\right)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)d\hat{\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}{2\pi iN^{-1}}\exp\left(N\hat{\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\left(N\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\phi(\bm{h}_{\mu}(t))\cdot\phi(\bm{h}_{\alpha}(s))\right)\right)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=N\int dG_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\ \delta\left(NG_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\bm{g}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{g}_{\alpha}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{dG_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)d\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}{2\pi iN^{-1}}\exp\left(N\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\left(NG_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\bm{g}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{g}_{\alpha}(s)\right)\right),

(27)

where the fields
\bm{h}_{\mu}(t),\bm{g}_{\mu}(t)
are regarded as functions of
\{\bm{\chi}_{\mu}\}_{\mu},\bm{\xi}
(see Equation (
D.2
)) and the
\hat{\Phi},\hat{G}
integrals run over the imaginary axis
(-i\infty,i\infty)
. After this step, we can write

\displaystyle Z\propto\int\prod_{\mu\alpha ts}d\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)d\hat{\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)dG_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)d\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\exp\left(NS[\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G}]\right)

(28)

where the DMFT action
S[\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G}]
is
\mathcal{O}_{N}(1)
and has the form

\displaystyle S[\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G}]=\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int dtds[\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\hat{\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+G_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)]+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ln\mathcal{Z}[j_{i},v_{i}].

(29)

The single site moment generating function
\mathcal{Z}[j,v]
arises from the factorization of the integrals over
N
different fields in the hidden layer and takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[j,v]=

\displaystyle\int\prod_{\mu}\frac{d\chi_{\mu}d\hat{\chi}_{\mu}}{2\pi}\frac{d\xi d\hat{\xi}}{2\pi}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\hat{\chi}_{\mu}\hat{\chi}_{\alpha}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}+(j_{\mu}+i\hat{\chi}_{\mu})\chi_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\xi}^{2}+(v+i\hat{\xi})\xi\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\sum_{\mu\alpha}[\hat{\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\phi(h_{\mu}(t))\phi(h_{\alpha}(s))+\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)g_{\mu}(t)g_{\alpha}(s)]\right)

(30)

where, again we must regard
h_{\mu}(t),g_{\mu}(t)
as functions of
\chi,\xi
. The variables in the above are no longer vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
but rather are scalars. We can write
\mathcal{Z}[j,v]=\int\prod_{\mu}d\chi_{\mu}d\hat{\chi}_{\mu}d\xi d\hat{\xi}\exp\left(-\mathcal{H}[\{\chi_{\mu},\hat{\chi}_{\mu}\},\xi,\hat{\xi},j,v]\right)
where
\mathcal{H}
is the logarithm of the integrand above. Since the full MGF takes the form
Z\propto\int d\Phi d\hat{\Phi}dGd\hat{G}\exp\left(NS[\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G}]\right)
, characterization of the
N\to\infty
limit requires one to identify the saddle point of
S
, where
\delta S=0
for any variation of these 4 order parameters.

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=\hat{\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=0\ ,\ \frac{\delta S}{\delta\hat{\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}=\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left<\phi(h_{\mu}(t))\phi(h_{\alpha}(s))\right>_{i}=0

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta G_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=0\ ,\ \frac{\delta S}{\delta\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}=G_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left<g_{\mu}(t)g_{\alpha}(s)\right>_{i}=0

(31)

where the
i
-th single site average
\left<\right>_{i}
of an observable
O(\chi,\hat{\chi},\xi,\hat{\xi})
is defined as

\displaystyle\left<O(\chi,\hat{\chi},\xi,\hat{\xi})\right>_{i}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}[j_{i},v_{i}]}\int\prod_{\mu}d\chi_{\mu}d\hat{\chi}_{\mu}d\xi d\hat{\xi}\exp\left(-\mathcal{H}[\{\chi_{\mu},\hat{\chi}_{\mu}\},\xi,\hat{\xi},j_{i},v_{i}]\right)O(\chi,\hat{\chi},\xi,\hat{\xi})

(32)

Since
\hat{\Phi}=\hat{G}=0
the single site MGF reveals that the initial fields are independent Gaussians
\{\chi_{\mu}\}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{K}^{x})
and
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. At zero source
\bm{j},\bm{v}\to 0
, all single site averages
\left<\right>_{i}
are equivalent and we may merely write
\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\left<\phi(h_{\mu}(t))\phi(h_{\alpha}(s))\right>\ ,\ G_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\left<g_{\mu}(t)g_{\alpha}(s)\right>
, where
\left<\right>
is the average over the single site distributions for
\bm{j},\bm{v}\to 0
.

D.2.1
Final
L=1
DMFT equations

Putting all of the saddle point equations together, we arrive at the following DMFT

\displaystyle\{\chi_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in[P]}

\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{K}^{x})\ ,\ \xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\chi_{\mu}+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha}[z(s)\dot{\phi}(h_{\alpha}(s))]K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\ ,\ z(t)=\xi+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha}\phi(h_{\alpha}(s))\Delta_{\alpha}(s)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\left<\phi(h_{\mu}(t))\phi(h_{\alpha}(s))\right>\ ,\ G_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\left<g_{\mu}(t)g_{\alpha}(s)\right>=\left<z(t)z(s)\dot{\phi}(h_{\mu}(t))\dot{\phi}(h_{\alpha}(s))\right>

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{\mu}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}[\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)+G_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}]\Delta_{\alpha}(t)

(33)

We see that for
L=1
networks, it suffices to solve for the kernels on the time-time diagonal. Further in this two layer case
\chi,\xi
are independent and do not vary in time. These facts will not hold in general for
L\geq 2
networks, which requires a more intricate analysis as we show in the next section.

D.3
Path Integral Formulation for Deep Networks

As discussed in the main text, we study the distribution over fields by computing the moment generating functional for the stochastic processes
\{\bm{\chi}^{\ell},\bm{\xi}^{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}

\displaystyle Z[\{\bm{j}^{\ell},\bm{v}^{\ell}\}]=\left<\exp\left(\sum_{\ell,\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\left[\bm{j}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\right]\right)\right>_{\bm{\theta}_{0}=\text{Vec}\{\bm{W}^{0}(0),...\bm{w}^{L}(0)\}}

(34)

Moments of these stochastic fields can be computed through differentiation of
Z
near zero-source

\displaystyle\left<\chi_{\mu_{1}}^{\ell_{1}}(t_{1})...\chi_{\mu_{n}}^{\ell_{n}}(t_{n})\xi_{\mu_{1}}^{\ell_{1}}(t_{1})...\xi_{\mu_{m}}^{\ell_{m}}(t_{m})\right>=\frac{\delta}{\delta j^{\ell_{1}}_{\mu_{1}}(t_{1})}...\frac{\delta}{\delta j^{\ell_{n}}_{\mu_{n}}(t_{n})}\frac{\delta}{\delta v^{\ell_{1}}_{\mu_{1}}(t_{1})}...\frac{\delta}{\delta v^{\ell_{m}}_{\mu_{m}}(t_{m})}Z[\{\bm{j}^{\ell},\bm{v}^{\ell}\}]|_{\bm{j}=\bm{v}=0}.

(35)

To perform the average over the initial parameters, we enforce the definition of the fields
\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))
,
\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)
, by inserting the following terms in the definition of
Z[\{\bm{j},\bm{v}\}]
so we may more easily perform the average over weights
\bm{\theta}_{0}
. We enforce these definitions with an integral representation of the Dirac-Delta function
1=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\ \delta(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\hat{x}\exp\left(ix\hat{x}\right)
. We note that we are implicitly working in the Ito scheme, where factors of Jacobian determinants are equal to one
[
54
,
84
,
85
]
(we note that
\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)
does not causally depend on
\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)
and
\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)
does not causally depend on
\bm{\xi}^{\ell}(t)
). Applying this to fields
\bm{\chi},\bm{\xi}
, we have

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{d\bm{\chi}^{1}_{\mu}(t)d\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{1}_{\mu}(t)}{(2\pi)^{N}}\exp\left(i\hat{\bm{\chi}}_{\mu}^{1}(t)\cdot\left[\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{1}(t)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\bm{x}_{\mu}\right]\right)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{d\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)d\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)}{(2\pi)^{N}}\exp\left(i\hat{\bm{\chi}}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)\cdot\left[\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\right]\right)\ ,\ \ell\in\{1,...,L-1\}

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{d\bm{\xi}^{L}_{\mu}(t)d\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{L}_{\mu}(t)}{(2\pi)^{N}}\exp\left(i\hat{\bm{\xi}}_{\mu}^{L}(t)\cdot\left[\bm{\xi}_{\mu}^{L}(t)-\bm{w}^{L}(0)\right]\right)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{d\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)d\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)}{(2\pi)^{N}}\exp\left(i\hat{\bm{\xi}}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\left[\bm{\xi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\right]\right)\ ,\ \ell\in\{1,...,L-1\}

(36)

where
\{h^{\ell},g^{\ell}\}
are understood to be stochastic processes which are causally determined by the
\{\chi^{\ell},\xi^{\ell}\}
fields, in the sense that
h^{\ell}(t)
only depends on
\chi^{\ell}(s)
for
s<t
. We thus have an expression of the form

\displaystyle Z[\{\bm{j}^{\ell},\bm{v}^{\ell}\}]=

\displaystyle\int\prod_{\ell\mu t}\frac{d\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)d\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)}{(2\pi)^{N}}\prod_{\ell\mu t}\frac{d\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)d\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)}{(2\pi)^{N}}\exp\left(\sum_{\ell,\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\left[\bm{j}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\left<\exp\left(-\frac{i}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\left[\hat{\bm{\chi}}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))+\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\hat{\bm{\xi}}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\right]\right)\right>_{\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)}

\displaystyle\times\left<\exp\left(-\frac{i}{\sqrt{D}}\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ \hat{\bm{\chi}}^{1}_{\mu}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}^{0}(0)\bm{x}_{\mu}\right)\right>_{\bm{W}^{0}(0)}

\displaystyle\times\left<\exp\left(-i\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{\bm{\xi}}_{\mu}^{L}(t)\cdot\bm{w}^{L}(0)\right)\right>_{\bm{w}^{L}(0)}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}\exp\left(i\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ \left[\hat{\bm{\chi}}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\right]\right)

(37)

Since
\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)
are all Gaussian random variables, these averages can be performed quite easily yielding

\displaystyle\left<\exp\left(-\frac{i}{\sqrt{D}}\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{1}_{\mu}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}^{0}(0)\bm{x}_{\mu}\right)\right>_{\bm{W}^{0}(0)}=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}dtds\sum_{\mu\alpha}\bm{\hat{\chi}}_{\mu}^{1}(t)\cdot\bm{\hat{\chi}}_{\alpha}^{1}(s)K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\left<\exp\left(-i\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{\bm{\xi}}_{\mu}^{L}(t)\cdot\bm{w}^{L}(0)\right)\right>_{\bm{w}^{L}(0)}=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}dtds\ \bm{\hat{\xi}}_{\mu}^{L}(t)\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}_{\alpha}^{L}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\left<\exp\left(-\frac{i}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\left[\hat{\bm{\chi}}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))+\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\hat{\bm{\xi}}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\right]\right)\right>_{\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}dtds\left[\bm{\hat{\chi}}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)\cdot\bm{\hat{\chi}}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))+\bm{\hat{\xi}}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s)\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(s)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}dtds\ \bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(s)\ \phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s)\right)

(38)

D.4
Order Parameters and Action Definition

We define the following order parameters which we will show concentrate in the
N\to\infty
limit

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mu,\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)=\frac{1}{N}\phi(\bm{h}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t))\cdot\phi(\bm{h}_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s))\ ,\ G^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\ ,\ A_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)=-\frac{i}{N}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s).

(39)

The NTK only depends on
\{\Phi^{\ell},G^{\ell}\}
so from these order parameters, we can compute the function evolution. The parameter
\bm{A}^{\ell}
arises from the coupling of the fields across a single layer’s initial weight matrix
\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)
. We can again enforce these definitions with integral representations of the Dirac-delta function. For each pair of samples
\mu,\alpha
and each pair of times
t,s
, we multiply by

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int\int\frac{d\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)d\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}{2\pi iN^{-1}}\exp\left(N\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\phi(\bm{h}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t))\cdot\phi(\bm{h}_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s))\right)\ell\in\{1,...,L\}

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int\int\frac{dG_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)d\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}{2\pi iN^{-1}}\exp\left(NG^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\hat{G}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\right)\ ,\ \ell\in\{1,...,L\}

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int\int\frac{dA^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)dB^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}{2\pi iN^{-1}}\exp\left(-NA^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-iB_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))\right)\ ,\ \ell\in\{1,...,L-1\}

(40)

After introducing these order parameters into the definition of the partition function, we have a factorization of the integrals over each of the
N
sites in each hidden layer. This gives the following partition function

\displaystyle Z=\int\prod_{\ell,\mu\alpha,ts}\frac{d\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)d\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}{2\pi iN^{-1}}

\displaystyle\frac{dG_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)d\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}{2\pi iN^{-1}}\frac{dA^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)dB^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}{2\pi iN^{-1}}\exp\left(NS[\{\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G},A,B\}]\right)

\displaystyle S[\{\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G},A,B\}]=\sum_{\ell\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle dtds\left[\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+G^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\hat{G}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-A^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]

\displaystyle+\ln\mathcal{Z}[\{\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G},A,B,j,v\}]

(41)

We thus see that the action
S
consists of inner-products between order parameters
\{\Phi,G,A\}
and their duals
\{\hat{\Phi},\hat{G},B\}
as well as a single site MGF
\mathcal{Z}[\{\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G},A,B,j,v\}]
, which is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=

\displaystyle\int\prod_{\ell\mu t}\frac{d\hat{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)d\chi_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)}{2\pi}\frac{d\hat{\xi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)d\xi_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)}{2\pi}\exp\left(\sum_{\ell\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\left[\left(j^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+i\hat{\chi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\right)\chi_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\left(v^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+i\hat{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\right)\xi_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\hat{\chi}_{\mu}^{1}(t)\hat{\chi}_{\alpha}^{1}(s)K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\hat{\xi}^{L}_{\mu}(t)\hat{\xi}^{L}_{\alpha}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\left[\hat{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)\hat{\chi}_{\alpha}^{\ell+1}(s)\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\hat{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\hat{\xi}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\left[\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\phi(h_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s))\hat{\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)+g^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)g^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\hat{G}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-i\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\left[\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\hat{\xi}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\hat{\chi}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)g^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(s)A^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\right)

(42)

D.5
Saddle Point Equations

Since the integrand in the moment generating function
Z
takes the form
e^{NS[\{\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G},A,B\}]}
, the
N\to\infty
limit can be obtained from saddle point integration, also known as the method of steepest descent
[
86
]
. This consists in finding order parameters
\{\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G},A,B\}
which render the action
S
locally stationary. Concretely, this leads to the following saddle point equations.

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}

\displaystyle={\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{Z}}{\delta\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}={\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)-\left<\phi(h_{\mu}^{\ell}(t))\phi(h_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s))\right>=0

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{Z}}{\delta\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}=\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\frac{1}{2}\left<\hat{\chi}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\hat{\chi}^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(s)\right>=0

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=G_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{Z}}{\delta\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)}=G^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\left<g_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)g_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s)\right>=0

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta G_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{Z}}{\delta{G}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)}=\hat{G}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\frac{1}{2}\left<\hat{g}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\hat{g}_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s)\right>=0

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta A^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=-B_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{Z}}{\delta A^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}=-B_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)-i\left<\hat{\chi}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)g_{\alpha}^{\ell+1}(s)\right>=0

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=-A_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{Z}}{\delta B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}=-A_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)-i\left<\phi(h_{\mu}^{\ell}(t))\hat{\xi}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\right>=0

(43)

We use the notation
\left<\right>
to denote an average over the self-consistent distribution on fields induced by the single-site moment generating function
\mathcal{Z}
at the saddle point. Concretely if
\mathcal{Z}=\int d\chi d\xi d\hat{\chi}d\hat{\xi}\exp\left(-\mathcal{H}[\chi,\xi,\hat{\chi},\hat{\xi}]\right)
then the single-site self-consistent average of observable
O([\chi,\xi,\hat{\chi},\hat{\xi}])
is defined as

\displaystyle\left<O([\chi,\xi,\hat{\chi},\hat{\xi}])\right>=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\int d\chi d\xi d\hat{\chi}d\hat{\xi}\ O([\chi,\xi,\hat{\chi},\hat{\xi}])\exp\left(-\mathcal{H}[\chi,\xi,\hat{\chi},\hat{\xi}]\right)

(44)

To calculate the averages of the dual variables such as
\left<\hat{\chi}^{\ell+1}\hat{\chi}^{\ell+1}\right>
, it will be convenient to work with vector and matrix notation. We let
\bm{\chi}^{\ell}=\text{Vec}\{\chi_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\}_{\mu\in[P],t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
represent the vectorization of the stochastic process over different samples and times and define the dot product between two of these vectors as
\bm{a}\cdot\bm{b}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{P}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ a_{\mu}(t)b_{\mu}(t)
. We also apply this procedure on the kernels so that
\bm{\Phi}=\text{Mat}\{\Phi_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\}_{\mu\alpha\in[P],t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
. Matrix vector products take the form
[\bm{A}\bm{b}]_{\mu,t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\sum_{\alpha}A_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)b_{\alpha}(s)
. We can obtain the behavior of
\left<\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1\top}_{\mu}\right>
in terms of primal fields
\{\chi,\xi,h,z\}
by insertion of a dummy source
\bm{u}
into the effective partition function.

\displaystyle\left<\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1}\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1}\right>

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{u}\partial\bm{u}^{\top}}\left<\exp\left(i\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1}\right)\right>|_{\bm{u}=\bm{0}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{u}\partial\bm{u}^{\top}}\int d\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}...\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}+\bm{u}-\bm{A}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\right)^{\top}[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}]^{-1}\left(\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}+\bm{u}-\bm{A}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\right)-...\right)

\displaystyle=[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}]^{-1}-[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}]^{-1}\left<\left(\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}-\bm{A}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\right)\left(\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}-\bm{A}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\right)^{\top}\right>\left[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}\right]^{-1}

(45)

Similarly, we can obtain the equation for
\left<\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell\top}\right>
by inserting a dummy source
\bm{r}
and differentiating near zero source

\displaystyle\left<\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}\right>

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{r}\partial\bm{r}^{\top}}\left<\exp\left(i\hat{\bm{r}}\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}\right)\right>|_{\bm{r}=\bm{0}}

\displaystyle=[\bm{G}^{\ell+1}]^{-1}-[\bm{G}^{\ell+1}]^{-1}\left<(\bm{\xi}^{\ell}-\bm{B}^{\ell\top}\bm{\phi}^{\ell})(\bm{\xi}^{\ell}-\bm{B}^{\ell\top}\bm{\phi}^{\ell})^{\top}\right>[\bm{G}^{\ell+1}]^{-1}

(46)

As we will demonstrate in the next subsection, these correlators must vanish. Lastly, we can calculate the remaining correlators in terms of primal variables

\displaystyle-i\left<\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}\bm{g}^{\ell+1,\top}\right>

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{u}}\left<\exp\left(-i\hat{\bm{u}}\cdot\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1}\right)\bm{g}^{\ell+1\top}\right>=[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}]^{-1}\left<(\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}-\bm{A}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell+1})\bm{g}^{\ell+1\top}\right>

\displaystyle-i\left<\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell\top}\right>

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{r}^{\top}}\left<\phi(\bm{h})\exp\left(-i\bm{r}\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}\right)\right>=\left<\bm{\phi}(\bm{h}^{\ell})(\bm{\xi}^{\ell}-\bm{B}^{\ell\top}\bm{\phi}(\bm{h}^{\ell}))\right>[\bm{G}^{\ell+1}]^{-1}

(47)

D.6
Single Site Stochastic Process: Hubbard Trick

To get a better sense of this distribution, we can now simplify the quadratic forms appearing in
\mathcal{Z}
using the Hubbard trick
[
87
]
, which merely relates a Gaussian function to its Fourier transform.

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{x}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{d\bm{u}}{(2\pi)^{d/2}\sqrt{\det\bm{A}}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{u}-i\bm{u}\cdot\bm{x}\right)=\left<\exp\left(-i\bm{u}\cdot\bm{x}\right)\right>_{\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{A})}

(48)

Applying this to the quadratic forms in the single-site MGF
\mathcal{Z}
, we get

\displaystyle\exp

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\ \hat{\chi}_{\mu}^{1}(t)\hat{\chi}_{\alpha}^{1}(s)K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}\right)=\left<\exp\left(-i\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ u_{\mu}^{1}(t)\hat{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)\right)\right>_{\{u^{1}\}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{K}^{x}\otimes\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top})}

\displaystyle\exp

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\ \hat{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)\hat{\chi}_{\alpha}^{\ell+1}(s)\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right)=\left<\exp\left(-i\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ u_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)\hat{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)\right)\right>_{\{u^{\ell}\}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell})}

\displaystyle\exp

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\ \hat{\xi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\hat{\xi}_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right)=\left<\exp\left(-i\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ r_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\hat{\xi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\right)\right>_{\{r^{\ell}\}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{G}^{\ell+1})}

\displaystyle\exp

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\ \hat{\xi}_{\mu}^{L}(t)\hat{\xi}_{\alpha}^{L}(s)\right)=\left<\exp\left(-i\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ r_{\mu}^{L}(t)\hat{\xi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\right)\right>_{\{r^{L}\}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top})}

(49)

Next, we integrate over all
\hat{\chi}^{\ell},\hat{\xi}^{\ell}
variables which yield Dirac-delta functions

\displaystyle\int\prod_{\mu t}\frac{d\hat{\chi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)}{2\pi}\exp\left(i\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell}\cdot\left[\bm{\chi}^{\ell}-\bm{u}^{\ell}-\bm{A}^{\ell-1}\bm{g}^{\ell}\right]\right)=\delta\left(\bm{\chi}^{\ell}-\bm{u}^{\ell}-\bm{A}^{\ell-1}\bm{g}^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\int\prod_{\mu t}\frac{d\hat{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)}{2\pi}\exp\left(i\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}\cdot\left[\bm{\xi}^{\ell}-\bm{r}^{\ell}-\bm{B}^{\ell\top}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})\right]\right)=\delta\left(\bm{\xi}^{\ell}-\bm{r}^{\ell}-\bm{B}^{\ell\top}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})\right)

(50)

To remedy the notational asymmetry, we redefine
\bm{B}^{\ell}
as its transpose
\bm{B}^{\ell}\to\bm{B}^{\ell\top}
. The presence of these delta-functions in the MGF
\mathcal{Z}
indicate the constraints
\bm{u}^{\ell}=\bm{\chi}^{\ell}-\bm{A}^{\ell-1}\bm{g}^{\ell}
and
\bm{r}^{\ell}=\bm{\xi}^{\ell}-\bm{B}^{\ell}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})
. We can thus return to the
\hat{\Phi}
and
\hat{G}
saddle point equations and verify that these order parameters vanish

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\Phi}}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left<\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1}\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1\top}\right>=\frac{1}{2}[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}]^{-1}\left<\left(\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}-\bm{A}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\right)\left(\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}-\bm{A}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\right)^{\top}\right>\left[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}\right]^{-1}-\frac{1}{2}[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}]^{-1}\left<\bm{u}^{\ell+1}\bm{u}^{\ell+1\top}\right>\left[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}\right]^{-1}-\frac{1}{2}[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}]^{-1}=0,

(51)

since
\left<\bm{u}^{\ell+1}\bm{u}^{\ell+1\top}\right>=\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}
. Following an identical argument,
\hat{\bm{G}}^{\ell}=0
. After this simplification, the single site MGF takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[\{\bm{j}^{\ell},\bm{v}^{\ell}\}]=\left<\int\prod_{\ell}d\bm{\chi}^{\ell}d\bm{\xi}^{\ell}\delta\left(\bm{\chi}^{\ell}-\bm{u}^{\ell}-\bm{A}^{\ell-1}\bm{g}^{\ell}\right)\delta\left(\bm{\xi}^{\ell}-\bm{r}^{\ell}-\bm{B}^{\ell}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})\right)\exp\left(i\bm{j}^{\ell}\cdot\bm{\chi}^{\ell}+i\bm{v}^{\ell}\cdot\bm{\xi}^{\ell}\right)\right>_{\{\bm{u}^{\ell},\bm{r}^{\ell}\}}

(52)

The interpretation is thus that
\bm{u}^{\ell},\bm{r}^{\ell}
are sampled independently from their respective Gaussian processes and the fields
\bm{\chi}^{\ell}
and
\bm{\xi}^{\ell}
are determined in terms of
\bm{u}^{\ell},\bm{r}^{\ell},\bm{h}^{\ell},\bm{g}^{\ell}
. This means that we can apply Stein’s Lemma (integration by parts)
[
88
]
to simplify the last two saddle point equations

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\ell}=\left<\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})\bm{r}^{\ell\top}\right>[\bm{G}^{\ell+1}]^{-1}=\left<\frac{\partial\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})}{\partial\bm{r}^{\ell\top}}\right>\ ,\ \bm{B}^{\ell}=\left<\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\bm{u}^{\ell+1}\right>[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}]^{-1}=\left<\frac{\partial\bm{g}^{\ell+1}}{\partial\bm{u}^{\ell+1\top}}\right>

(53)

D.7
Final DMFT Equations

We can now close this stochastic process in terms of preactivations
h^{\ell}
and pre-gradients
z^{\ell}
. To match the formulas provided in the main text, we rescale
A^{\ell}\to A^{\ell}/\gamma_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{\gamma_{0}}(1)
and
B^{\ell}\to B^{\ell}/\gamma_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{\gamma_{0}}(1)
, which makes it clear that the non-Gaussian corrections to the
h_{\mu}^{\ell}(t),z_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)
fields are
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0})
. After this rescaling, we have the following complete DMFT equations.

\displaystyle h_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\chi_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)z_{\alpha}(s)\dot{\phi}(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))

\displaystyle=u^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha}\left[A^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\dot{\phi}(h_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s))z_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s)

\displaystyle z_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\xi^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))

\displaystyle=r_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{\alpha}\left[B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\Delta_{\alpha}(s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\phi(h_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s))

\displaystyle\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\left<\phi(h_{\mu}^{\ell}(t))\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))\right>\ ,\ G^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\left<g^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)g^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\right>

\displaystyle A^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}^{-1}\left<\frac{\delta\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))}{\delta r^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)}\right>\ ,\ B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\gamma_{0}^{-1}\left<\frac{\delta g^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)}{\delta u^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(s)}\right>

The base cases in the above equations are that
A^{0}=B^{L}=0
and
\Phi^{0}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}
and
G^{L+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=1
. From the above self-consistent equations, one obtains the NTK dynamics and consequently the output predictions of the network with
\frac{\partial f_{\mu}}{\partial t}=\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(t)\left[\sum_{\ell}G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)\right]
.

D.8
Varying Network Widths and Initialization Scales

In this section, we relax the assumption of network widths being equal while taking all widths to infinity at a fixed ratio. This will allow us to analyze the influence of bottlenecks on the dynamics. We let
N^{\ell}=a_{\ell}N
represent the width of layer
\ell
. Without loss of generality, we can choose that
N^{L}=N
and proceed by defining order parameters in the usual way

\displaystyle\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\frac{1}{N^{\ell}}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))\ ,\ G^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\frac{1}{N^{\ell}}\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)

(55)

Since
N^{L}=N
, the variable
\bm{g}^{L}=\sqrt{N^{L}}\frac{\partial h^{L+1}}{\partial\bm{h}^{L}}=\bm{w}^{L}\odot\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}^{L})=\mathcal{O}_{N,\gamma}(1)
as desired. We extend this definition to each layer as before
\bm{g}^{\ell}=\sqrt{N^{\ell}}\frac{\partial h^{L+1}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}}
which again satisfies the recursion

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)=\bm{z}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\odot\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\ ,\ \bm{z}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N^{\ell+1}}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)

(56)

Now, we need to calculate the dynamics on weights
\bm{W}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bm{W}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\sum_{\mu}\Delta_{\mu}\frac{\partial f_{\mu}}{\partial\bm{W}^{\ell}}=\gamma^{2}\sum_{\mu}\Delta_{\mu}\frac{\partial f_{\mu}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}}\cdot\frac{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}}{\partial\bm{W}^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{N^{\ell}}\sqrt{N^{\ell+1}}}\sum_{\mu}\Delta_{\mu}\bm{g}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}\phi(\bm{h}_{\mu}^{\ell})^{\top}

(57)

Using our definition of the kernels and the
\bm{h},\bm{z}
fields

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\chi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{N^{\ell}}}\sum_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}ds\ \Delta_{\alpha}(s)\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle\bm{z}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{N^{\ell}}}\sum_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}ds\ \Delta_{\alpha}(s)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

(58)

We also find the usual formula for the NTK

\displaystyle K^{NTK}_{\mu\alpha}=\gamma^{2}\sum_{\ell}\text{Tr}\left[\frac{\partial f_{\mu}}{\partial\bm{W}^{\ell}}\right]^{\top}\frac{\partial f_{\alpha}}{\partial\bm{W}^{\ell}}=\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}+G^{1}_{\mu\alpha}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}

(59)

Now, as before, we need to consider the distribution of
\bm{\chi},\bm{\xi}
fields. We assume
W^{\ell}_{ij}(0)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{\ell})
. This requires computing integrals like

\displaystyle\left<\exp\left(i\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\left[\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))/\sqrt{N^{\ell}}+\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)^{\top}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)/\sqrt{N^{\ell+1}}\right]\right)\right>_{\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{\sigma^{2}_{\ell}}{2}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\left[\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-i\sigma^{2}_{\ell}\sqrt{\frac{a_{\ell}}{a_{\ell+1}}}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}dsA^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(s)\right)

(60)

where
\bm{A}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=-\frac{i}{N^{\ell}}\bm{\phi}(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)
.
The action thus takes the form

\displaystyle S=\sum_{\ell}a_{\ell}\text{Tr}\left[\bm{\hat{\Phi}}^{\ell\top}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}+\bm{G}^{\ell\top}\hat{\bm{G}}^{\ell}-\bm{A}^{\ell\top}\bm{B}^{\ell}\right]+\sum_{\ell}a_{\ell}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}

(61)

where the zero-source MGF for layer
\ell
has the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}=\int\prod_{\mu t}\frac{d\chi_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)d\hat{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)}{2\pi}\frac{d\xi_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)d\hat{\xi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})^{\top}\bm{\hat{\Phi}}^{\ell}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})-\bm{g}^{\ell\top}\hat{\bm{G}}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell}+i\bm{\chi}^{\ell}\cdot\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell}+i\bm{\xi}^{\ell}\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\sigma^{2}_{\ell-1}}{2}\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1}\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell}-\frac{\sigma^{2}_{\ell}}{2}\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}\bm{G}^{\ell+1}\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-i\sigma^{2}_{\ell-1}\sqrt{\frac{a_{\ell-1}}{a_{\ell}}}\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell}\bm{A}^{\ell-1}\bm{g}^{\ell}-i\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})^{\top}\bm{B}^{\ell}\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell}\right)

(62)

The saddle point equations give

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left<\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})^{\top}\right>\ ,\ \bm{G}^{\ell}=\left<\bm{g}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell\top}\right>

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=-i\left<\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\ell\top}\right>=\left<\frac{\partial\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})}{\partial\bm{r}^{\ell\top}}\right>

\displaystyle a_{\ell}\bm{B}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=-ia_{\ell+1}\sigma^{2}_{\ell}\sqrt{\frac{a_{\ell}}{a_{\ell+1}}}\left<\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\ell+1}\bm{g}^{\ell+1,\top}\right>\implies\bm{B}^{\ell}=\sigma^{2}_{\ell}\sqrt{\frac{a_{\ell+1}}{a_{\ell}}}\left<\frac{\partial\bm{g}^{\ell+1\top}}{\partial\bm{u}^{\ell+1}}\right>

(63)

where
\bm{u}^{\ell}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\sigma^{2}_{\ell-1}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1}),\bm{r}^{\ell}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\sigma^{2}_{\ell}\bm{G}^{\ell+1})
. We redefine
\bm{B}^{\ell}\to\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}_{\ell}}\sqrt{\frac{a_{\ell}}{a_{\ell+1}}}\bm{B}^{\ell}
. To take the
N\to\infty
limit of the field dynamics, again use
\gamma_{0}=\gamma/\sqrt{N}=O_{N}(1)
. The field equations take the form

\displaystyle h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\left[\sigma^{2}_{\ell-1}\sqrt{\frac{a_{\ell-1}}{a_{\ell}}}A_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell-1}(t,s)+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{a_{\ell}}}\Theta(t-s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\dot{\phi}(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))z^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)

\displaystyle z^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=r_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\left[\sigma^{2}_{\ell}\sqrt{\frac{a_{\ell+1}}{a_{\ell}}}B_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{a_{\ell}}}\Theta(t-s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))

(64)

We thus find that the evolution of the scalar fields in a given layer is set by the parameter
\gamma_{0}/\sqrt{a_{\ell}}
, indicating that relatively wider layers evolve less and contribute less of a change to the overall NTK. This definition for
\bm{A}^{\ell},\bm{B}^{\ell}
is non-ideal to extract intuition about bottlenecks since
\bm{A}^{\ell-1}\sim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{a_{\ell-1}}}\right)
and
\bm{B}^{\ell}\sim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{a_{\ell+1}}}\right)
. To remedy this, we redefine
\tilde{\bm{A}}^{\ell}=\frac{\sqrt{a_{\ell}}}{\gamma_{0}}\bm{A}^{\ell},\tilde{\bm{B}}^{\ell}=\frac{\sqrt{a_{\ell+1}}}{\gamma_{0}}\bm{B}^{\ell}
. With this choice, we have

\displaystyle h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{a_{\ell}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\left[\sigma^{2}_{\ell-1}\tilde{A}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell-1}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\dot{\phi}(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))z^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)

\displaystyle z^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=r_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{a_{\ell}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\left[\sigma^{2}_{\ell}\tilde{B}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))

(65)

where
\tilde{A}^{\ell-1},\tilde{B}^{\ell}
do not have a leading order scaling with
a_{\ell-1}
or
a_{\ell+1}
respectively. Under this change of variables, it is now apparent that a very wide layer
\ell
, where
\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{a_{\ell}}}\ll 1
is small, the fields
h^{\ell},z^{\ell}
become well approximated by the Gaussian processes
u^{\ell},r^{\ell}
, albeit with evolving covariances
\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1},\bm{G}^{\ell+1}
respectively. In a realistic CNN architecture where the number of channels increases across layers, this result would predict that more feature learning and deviations from Gaussianity to occur in the early layers and the later layers to be well approximated as Gaussian fields
u^{\ell},r^{\ell}
with temporally evolving covariances for
\ell\sim L
. We leave evaluation of this prediction to future work.

Appendix E
Two Layer Networks

In a two layer network, there are no
\bm{A}
or
\bm{B}
order parameters, so the fields
\chi^{1}
and
\xi^{1}
are always independent. Further,
\chi^{1}
and
\xi^{1}
are both constant throughout training dynamics. Thus we can obtain differential rather than integral equations for the stochastic fields
h^{1},z^{1}
which are

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h^{1}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\Delta_{\alpha}(t)K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}\dot{\phi}(h_{\alpha}^{1}(t))z^{1}(t)\ ,\ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}z^{1}(t)=\gamma_{0}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\Delta_{\alpha}(t)\phi(h_{\alpha}^{1}(t))

\displaystyle\Phi^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=\left<\phi(h^{1}_{\mu}(t))\phi(h^{1}_{\alpha}(t))\right>\ ,\ G^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(t)=\left<z(t)^{2}\dot{\phi}(h^{1}_{\mu}(t))\dot{\phi}(h^{1}_{\alpha}(t))\right>

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Delta_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\left[G^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(t)K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}+\Phi^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(t)\right]\Delta_{\alpha}(t)

(66)

where the average is taken over the random initial conditions
\bm{h}^{1}(0)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{K}^{x})
and
\bm{z}^{1}(0)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top})
. An example of the two layer theory for a ReLU network can be found in Appendix Figure
6
. In this two layer setting, a drift PDE can be obtained for the joint density of preactivations and feedback fields
p(\bm{h},z;t)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p(\bm{h},z,t)

\displaystyle=-p(\bm{h},z,t)z(t)\sum_{\mu}\Delta_{\mu}(t)K^{x}_{\mu\mu}\ddot{\phi}(h_{\mu}(t))

\displaystyle-\gamma_{0}\sum_{\mu\alpha}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}\dot{\phi}(h_{\alpha}(t))z(t)\frac{\partial p(\bm{h},\bm{z},t)}{\partial h_{\mu}}-\gamma_{0}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}\phi(h_{\alpha})\frac{\partial p(\bm{h},\bm{z},t)}{\partial z_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Delta_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\left[G^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(t)K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}+\Phi^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(t)\right]\Delta_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle\Phi^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=\left<\phi(h^{1}_{\mu}(t))\phi(h^{1}_{\alpha}(t))\right>\ ,\ G^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(t)=\left<z^{1}(t)^{2}\dot{\phi}(h^{1}_{\mu}(t))\dot{\phi}(h^{1}_{\alpha}(t))\right>,

(67)

which is a zero-diffusion feature space version of the PDE derived in the original two layer mean field limit of neural networks
[
22
,
42
,
43
]
.

Appendix F
Deep Linear Networks

In the deep linear case, the
g^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)
fields are independent of sample index
\mu
. We introduce the kernel
H^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\left<h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\right>
. The field equations are

\displaystyle h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=u^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\left[A^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)H^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\Delta_{\alpha}(s)g^{\ell}(s)

\displaystyle g^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=r^{\ell}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}[B^{\ell}_{\alpha}(t,s)+\gamma_{0}\Theta(t-s)G^{\ell+1}(t,s)]\Delta_{\alpha}(s)h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)

(68)

Or in vector notation
\bm{h}^{\ell}=\bm{u}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell}
and
\bm{g}^{\ell}=\bm{r}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{h}^{\ell}
where

\displaystyle C^{\ell}_{\mu}(t,s)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}[A_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell-1}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)H^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)]\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\ ,\ D^{\ell}_{\mu}(t,s)=[B^{\ell}_{\mu}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)G^{\ell+1}(t,s)]\Delta_{\mu}(s)

(69)

Using the formulas which define the fields, we have

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\bm{u}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{r}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{h}^{\ell}\implies\bm{h}^{\ell}=(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{D}^{\ell})^{-1}[\bm{u}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{r}^{\ell}]

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\bm{r}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{u}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell}\implies\bm{g}^{\ell}=(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{\ell})^{-1}[\bm{r}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{u}^{\ell}]

(70)

The saddle point equations can thus be written as

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left<\bm{h}^{\ell}\bm{h}^{\ell\top}\right>=(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{D}^{\ell})^{-1}[\bm{H}^{\ell-1}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{G}^{\ell+1}\bm{C}^{\ell\top}]\left[(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{D}^{\ell})^{\top}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left<\bm{g}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell\top}\right>=\left(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{\ell}\right)^{-1}\left[\bm{G}^{\ell+1}+\gamma^{2}_{0}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{H}^{\ell-1}\bm{D}^{\ell\top}\right]\left[\left(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{\ell}\right)^{\top}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{D}^{\ell})^{-1}\bm{C}^{\ell}\ ,\ \bm{B}^{\ell-1}=(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{\ell})^{-1}\bm{D}^{\ell}

(71)

We solve these equations by repeatedly updating
\bm{H}^{\ell},\bm{G}^{\ell}
, using Equation (
F
) and the current estimate of
\bm{C}^{\ell},\bm{D}^{\ell}
. We then use the new
\bm{H}^{\ell},\bm{G}^{\ell}
to recompute
\bm{K}^{NTK}
and
\bm{\Delta}(t)
, calculating
\bm{C}^{\ell},\bm{D}^{\ell}
and then recomputing
\bm{H}^{\ell},\bm{G}^{\ell}
. This procedure usually converges in
\sim 5-10
steps.

F.1
Two Layer Linear Network

As we saw in Appendix
E
, the field dynamics simplify considerably in the two layer case, allowing description of all fields in terms of differential equations. In a two layer linear network, we let
\bm{h}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{P}
represent the hidden activation field and
g(t)\in\mathbb{R}
represent the gradient

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bm{h}(t)=\gamma_{0}g(t)\bm{K}^{x}\bm{\Delta}(t)\ ,\ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(t)=\gamma_{0}\bm{\Delta}(t)\cdot\bm{h}(t)

(72)

The kernels
\bm{H}(t)=\left<\bm{h}(t)\bm{h}(t)^{\top}\right>
and
G(t)=\left<g(t)^{2}\right>
thus evolve as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bm{H}(t)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\bm{K}^{x}\bm{\Delta}\left<g(t)\bm{h}(t)^{\top}\right>+\gamma_{0}\left<g(t)\bm{h}(t)\right>\bm{\Delta}^{\top}\bm{K}^{x}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(t)

\displaystyle=2\gamma_{0}\left<g(t)\bm{h}(t)\right>\cdot\bm{\Delta}(t)

(73)

It is easy to verify that the network predictions on the
P
training points are
\bm{f}(t)=\bm{y}-\bm{\Delta}(t)=\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}\left<g(t)\bm{h}(t)\right>\in\mathbb{R}^{P}
. Thus the dynamics of
\bm{H}(t),G(t)
and
\bm{\Delta}(t)
close

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bm{H}(t)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{K}^{x}\bm{\Delta}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Delta})^{\top}+\gamma_{0}^{2}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Delta})\bm{\Delta}^{\top}\bm{K}^{x}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(t)

\displaystyle=2\gamma_{0}^{2}(\bm{y}-\bm{\Delta})\cdot\bm{\Delta}(t)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bm{\Delta}(t)

\displaystyle=-[\bm{H}(t)+G(t)\bm{K}^{x}]\bm{\Delta}(t)

(74)

where the initial conditions are
\bm{H}(0)=\bm{I}
,
G(0)=1
and
\bm{\Delta}(0)=\bm{y}
. These equations hold for any choice of data
\bm{K}^{x},\bm{y}
.

F.1.1
Whitened Data in Two Layer Linear

For input data which is whitened where
\bm{K}^{x}=\mathbf{I}
, then the dynamics can be simplified even further, recovering the sigmoidal curves very similar to those obtained under a special initialization
[
68
,
69
,
71
,
73
]
. In this case we note that the error signal always evolves in the
\bm{y}
direction,
\bm{\Delta}(t)=\Delta(t)\frac{\bm{y}}{|\bm{y}|}
, and that
\bm{H}
only evolves in a rank one direction
\bm{y}\bm{y}^{\top}
direction as well. Let
\frac{1}{|\bm{y}|^{2}}\bm{y}^{\top}\bm{H}(t)\bm{y}=H_{y}(t)
. Let
y=|\bm{y}|
represent the norm of the target vector, then the relevant scalar dynamics are

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{y}(t)

\displaystyle=2\gamma_{0}^{2}\Delta(t)(y-\Delta(t))\ ,\ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(t)=2\gamma_{0}^{2}\Delta(t)(y-\Delta(t))

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Delta(t)

\displaystyle=-[H_{y}(t)+G(t)]\Delta(t)

(75)

Now note that, at initialization
H_{y}(0)=G(0)=1
and that
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{y}(t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(t)
. Thus, we have an automatic balancing condition
H_{y}(t)=G(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and the dynamics reduce to two variables

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{y}(t)

\displaystyle=2\gamma_{0}^{2}\Delta(t)(y-\Delta(t))\ ,\ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Delta(t)=-2H_{y}(t)\Delta(t)

(76)

We note that this system obeys a conservation law which constrains
(H_{y},y-\Delta)
to a hyperbola

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[H_{y}^{2}-\gamma_{0}^{2}(y-\Delta(t))^{2}\right]=2\gamma_{0}^{2}H_{y}\Delta(y-\Delta)-2\gamma_{0}^{2}H_{y}\Delta(y-\Delta)=0

(77)

This conservation law implies that
H_{y}(0)^{2}=1=\lim_{t\to\infty}H_{y}(t)^{2}-\gamma_{0}^{2}y^{2}
or that the final kernel has the form
\lim_{t\to\infty}\bm{H}(t)=\frac{1}{y^{2}}[\sqrt{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}y^{2}}-1]\bm{y}\bm{y}^{\top}+\mathbf{I}
. The result that the final kernel becomes a rank one spike in the direction of the target function was also obtained in finite width networks in the limit of small initialization
[
73
]
and also from a normative toy model of feature learning
[
81
]
. We can use the conservation law above
1=H_{y}(t)^{2}-\gamma_{0}^{2}(\Delta(t)-y)^{2}
to simplify the dynamics to a one dimensional system

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Delta(t)=-2\sqrt{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}(\Delta(t)-y)^{2}}\ \Delta(t)\implies\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f=2\sqrt{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}f^{2}}(y-f)

(78)

where
f=y-\Delta
. We see that increasing
\gamma_{0}
provides strict acceleration in the learning dynamics, illustrating the training benefits of feature evolution. Since this system is separable, we can solve for the time it takes for the network output norm to reach output level
f

\displaystyle 2t=\int_{0}^{f}\frac{ds}{(y-s)\sqrt{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}s^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}y^{2}}}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}yf}{\sqrt{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}y^{2}}\sqrt{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}f^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}y^{2}}}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\gamma_{0}^{2}y^{2}}}\right)

(79)

The NTK limit can be obtained by taking
\gamma_{0}\to 0
which gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Delta(t)\sim-2\Delta(t)\implies\Delta(t)\sim e^{-2t}

(80)

which recovers the usual convergence rate of a linear model. The right hand side of Equation (
F.1.1
) has a perturbation series in
\gamma_{0}^{2}
which converges in the disk
\gamma_{0}<\frac{1}{y}
. The other limit of interest is the
\gamma_{0}\to\infty
limit where

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Delta(t)\sim-2\gamma_{0}(y-\Delta(t))\Delta(t)

(81)

which recovers the logistic growth observed in the initialization scheme of prior works
[
68
,
69
]
. The timescale
\tau
required to learn is only
\tau\sim\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}\ll 1
, which is much smaller than the
O_{\gamma_{0}}(1)
time to learn predicted from the small
\gamma_{0}
expansion. We note that the above leading order asymptotic behavior at large
\gamma_{0}
considers the DMFT initial condition
\Delta(0)=y
as an unstable fixed point. For realistic learning curves, one would need to stipulate some alternative initial condition such as
\Delta=y-\epsilon
for some small
\epsilon>0
in order to have nontrivial leading order dynamics.

F.2
Deep Linear Whitened Data

In this section, we examine the role of depth when linear networks are trained on whitened data. As in the two layer case, all hidden kernels
\bm{H}^{\ell}(t,s)
need only be tracked in the one dimensional task relevant subspace along the vector
\bm{y}
. We let
\Delta(t)=\frac{1}{y}\bm{y}\cdot\bm{\Delta}(t)
and let
h_{y}(t)=\frac{1}{y}\bm{h}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{y}
. We have

\displaystyle h^{\ell}_{y}(t)

\displaystyle=u_{y}^{\ell}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\ C^{\ell}(t,s)g^{\ell}(s)\ ,\ C^{\ell}(t,s)=A_{y}^{\ell-1}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)H^{\ell-1}_{y}(t,s)\Delta(s)

\displaystyle g^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=r^{\ell}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\ D^{\ell}(t,s)h_{y}^{\ell}(s)\ ,\ D^{\ell}(t,s)=B_{y}^{\ell-1}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)G^{\ell+1}(t,s)\Delta(s)

(82)

Lastly we have the simple evolution equation for the scalar error
\Delta(t)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Delta(t)}{\partial t}=-\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}G^{\ell+1}(t,t)H_{y}^{\ell}(t,t)\Delta(t)\implies\Delta(t)=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}G^{\ell+1}(s,s)H^{\ell}_{y}(s,s)\right)y

(83)

Vectorizing we find the following equations for the time
\times
time matrix order parameters
\bm{h}^{\ell}=\bm{u}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell}\ ,\ \bm{g}^{\ell}=\bm{r}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{h}^{\ell}
, we can solve for the response functions
\bm{A}^{\ell}=\left(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{D}^{\ell}\right)^{-1}\bm{C}^{\ell}
and
\bm{B}^{\ell}=\left(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{D}^{\ell}\bm{C}^{\ell}\right)^{-1}\bm{D}^{\ell}
. This formulation has the advantage that it no longer has any sample-size dependence: arbitrary sample sizes can be considered with no computational cost.

Appendix G
Convolutional Networks with Infinite Channels

The DMFT described in this work can be extended to CNNs with infinitely many channels, much in the same way that infinite CNNs have a well defined kernel limit
[
89
,
90
]
. We let
W^{\ell}_{ij,\mathfrak{a}}
represent the value of the filter at spatial displacement
\mathfrak{a}
from the center of the filter, which maps relates activity at channel
j
of layer
\ell
to channel
i
of layer
\ell+1
. The fields
h_{\mu,i,\mathfrak{a}}^{\ell}
are defined recursively as

\displaystyle h^{\ell+1}_{\mu,i,\mathfrak{a}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{S}^{\ell}}W^{\ell}_{ij,\mathfrak{b}}\phi(h_{\mu,j,\mathfrak{a+b}}^{\ell})\ ,\ i\in\{1,...,N\}

(84)

where
\mathcal{S}^{\ell}
is the spatial receptive field at layer
\ell
. For example, a
(2k+1)\times(2k+1)
convolution will have
\mathcal{S}^{\ell}=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:-k\leq i\leq k,-k\leq j\leq k\}
. The output function is obtained from the last layer is defined as
f_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i,\mathfrak{a}}^{L}\phi(h^{L}_{\mu,i,\mathfrak{a}})
. The gradient fields have the same definition as before
\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu,\mathfrak{a}}=\gamma_{0}N\frac{\partial f_{\mu}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu,\mathfrak{a}}}
, which as before enjoy the following recursion from the chain rule

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu,\mathfrak{a}}=\gamma_{0}N\sum_{\mathfrak{b}}\frac{\partial f_{\mu}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu,\mathfrak{b}}}\cdot\frac{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu,\mathfrak{b}}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu,\mathfrak{a}}}=\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu,\mathfrak{a}})\odot\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{S}^{\ell}}\bm{W}^{\ell\top}_{\mathfrak{b}}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu,\mathfrak{a-b}}\right]

(85)

The dynamics of each set of filters
\{\bm{W}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{b}}\}
can therefore be written in terms of the features
\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{a}},\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{a}}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bm{W}^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\mu,\mathfrak{a}}\Delta_{\mu}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu,\mathfrak{a}}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu,\mathfrak{a+b}})^{\top}.

(86)

The feature space description of the forward and backward pass relations is

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu,\mathfrak{a}}(t)=\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}_{\mu,\mathfrak{a}}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha\mathfrak{b,c}}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha,\mathfrak{a+b},\mathfrak{c+b}}(t,s)\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\alpha,\mathfrak{c}}(s)

\displaystyle\bm{z}^{\ell}_{\mu,\mathfrak{a}}(t)=\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha\mathfrak{b,c}}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha,\mathfrak{a-b},\mathfrak{c-b}}(t,s)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\alpha,\mathfrak{c}})

(87)

where
\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}_{\mu,\mathfrak{a}}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t))
. The order parameters for this network architecture are

\displaystyle\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha,\mathfrak{ab}}(t,s)=\frac{1}{N}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t))\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\alpha\mathfrak{b}}(s))\ ,\ G^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha,\mathfrak{ab}}(t,s)=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\alpha\mathfrak{b}}(s)

(88)

These two order parameters per layer collectively define the neural tangent kernel. Following the computation in
D
, we obtain the following field theory in the
N\to\infty
limit:

\displaystyle\{u^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t)\}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1})\ ,\ \{r^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t)\}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{G}^{\ell+1})

\displaystyle h^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t)

\displaystyle=u^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha,\mathfrak{b}}A^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha,\mathfrak{ab}}(t,s)\dot{\phi}(h^{\ell}_{\alpha\mathfrak{b}}(s))z^{\ell}_{\alpha\mathfrak{b}}(s)

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha\mathfrak{b,c}}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha,\mathfrak{a+b},\mathfrak{c+b}}\dot{\phi}(h^{\ell}_{\alpha\mathfrak{c}}(s))z^{\ell}_{\alpha\mathfrak{c}}(s)

\displaystyle z^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t)

\displaystyle=r^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\sum_{\alpha,\mathfrak{b}}B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha,\mathfrak{ab}}(t,s)\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha\mathfrak{b}}(s))

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha\mathfrak{b,c}}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha,\mathfrak{a-b},\mathfrak{c-b}}\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha\mathfrak{c}}(s))

\displaystyle\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha,\mathfrak{ab}}(t,s)=\left<\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t))\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha\mathfrak{b}}(s))\right>\ ,\ G^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha,\mathfrak{ab}}(t,s)=\left<g^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t)g^{\ell}_{\alpha\mathfrak{b}}(s)\right>

\displaystyle A^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha,\mathfrak{ab}}(t,s)=\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}\left<\frac{\delta\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t))}{\delta r^{\ell}_{\alpha\mathfrak{b}}(s)}\right>\ ,\ B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha,\mathfrak{ab}}(t,s)=\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}\left<\frac{\delta g^{\ell+1}_{\mu\mathfrak{a}}(t)}{\delta u^{\ell+1}_{\alpha\mathfrak{b}}(s)}\right>

(89)

We see that this field theory essentially multiples the number of sample indices by the number of spatial indices
P\to P|\mathcal{S}|
. Thus the time complexity of evaluation of this theory scales very poorly as
\mathcal{O}(P^{3}|\mathcal{S}|^{3}T^{3})
, rendering DMFT solutions very computationally intensive.

If we include a bias
\bm{b}^{\ell}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
in our trainable model, so that

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))+\bm{b}^{\ell}(t)

(90)

then the dynamics on
\bm{b}^{\ell}(t)
induced by gradient flow is

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bm{b}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(t)\frac{\partial f_{\alpha}}{\partial b^{\ell}}=\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(t)\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(t)=\gamma_{0}\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(t)\bm{g}_{\alpha}^{\ell}(t)

(91)

b_{i}^{\ell}(0)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)

\displaystyle\{u^{\ell}\}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1}+\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top})\ ,\ \{r^{\ell}\}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{G}^{\ell+1})

\displaystyle h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=u^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\sum_{\alpha}[A^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)]g_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)g^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)

\displaystyle z^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=r^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\sum_{\alpha}[B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)\Delta_{\alpha}(s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)]\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))

(92)

Appendix I
Multiple Output Channels

We now consider network outputs on
C=\mathcal{O}_{N}(1)
classes. The prediction for a data point
\mu\in[P]
at time
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is
\bm{f}_{\mu}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{C}
.
As before, we define the error signal as
\bm{\Delta}_{\mu}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{f}_{\mu}}\ell(\bm{f}_{\mu},\bm{y}_{\mu})\in\mathbb{R}^{C}
. For any pair of data points
\mu,\alpha
the NTK is a
C\times C
matrix
\bm{K}^{NTK}_{\mu\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times C}
with entries
K_{\mu\alpha,cc^{\prime}}^{NTK}=\frac{\partial f_{c}(\bm{x}_{\mu})}{\partial\bm{\theta}}\cdot\frac{\partial f_{c^{\prime}}(\bm{x}_{\alpha})}{\partial\bm{\theta}}
. From these matrices, we can compute the evolution of the predictions in the network.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bm{f}_{\mu}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\bm{K}^{NTK}_{\mu\alpha}\bm{\Delta}_{\alpha}

(93)

In this case, we have matrices for the backprop features
\bm{g}^{\ell}=\gamma\sqrt{N}\frac{\partial\bm{f}^{\top}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times C}
. These satisfy the usual recursion

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}=\gamma\sqrt{N}\frac{\partial\bm{f}^{\top}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}}=\gamma\sqrt{N}\left(\frac{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell+1}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell}}\right)^{\top}\frac{\partial\bm{f}^{\top}}{\partial\bm{h}^{\ell+1}}=\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}^{\ell})\bm{1}^{\top}\right]\odot\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\right]

(94)

We can now compute the NTK for samples
\mu,\alpha

\displaystyle\bm{K}^{NTK}_{\mu\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\frac{\partial\bm{f}(\bm{x}_{\mu})}{\partial\bm{W}^{\ell}}\cdot\frac{\partial\bm{f}(\bm{x}_{\alpha})}{\partial\bm{W}^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}\ \mathbf{I}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\bm{G}^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}+\bm{G}^{1}_{\mu\alpha}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}

(95)

where
\bm{G}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{N}\bm{g}_{\mu}^{\ell\top}\bm{g}_{\alpha}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times C}
and
\Phi_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}=\frac{1}{N}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu})\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\alpha})\in\mathbb{R}
. Next we introduce kernels
\bm{A}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{C}
and
\bm{B}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{C}
which are defined in the usual way. The corresponding field theory has the form

\displaystyle h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\chi^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\left[\bm{A}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell-1}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)\bm{\Delta}_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\bm{z}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\left[\bm{B}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)\bm{G}^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}\bm{\Delta}_{\alpha}(s)\right]\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{C}

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\dot{\phi}(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\bm{z}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{C}

(96)

From these fields, the saddle point equations define the kernels as

\displaystyle\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\left<\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))\right>\in\mathbb{R}\ ,\ \bm{G}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\left<\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)^{\top}\right>\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times C}

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}\left<\frac{\delta\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))}{\delta\bm{r}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)}\right>\in\mathbb{R}^{C}\ ,\ \bm{B}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}\left<\frac{\delta\bm{g}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)}{\delta u^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)}\right>\in\mathbb{R}^{C}.

(97)

This allows studying the multi-class structure of learned representations.

Appendix J
Weight Decay in Deep Homogenous Networks

If we train with weight decay,
\frac{d}{dt}\bm{\theta}=-\gamma^{2}\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\mathcal{L}-\lambda\bm{\theta}
, in a
\kappa
-degree homogenous network (
f(c\bm{\theta})=c^{\kappa}f(\bm{\theta})
), then the prediction dynamics satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}f(\bm{x},t)=\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(t)K^{NTK}_{\mu\alpha}(\bm{x},\bm{x}_{\alpha},t)-\lambda\kappa f(\bm{x},t)\ ,\

This holds by the following identity
\frac{\partial}{\partial c}f(c\bm{\theta})=\frac{\partial}{\partial c}c^{\kappa}f(\bm{\theta})
, which when evaluated at
c=1
gives
\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{\theta}}f(\bm{\theta})\cdot\bm{\theta}=\kappa f(\bm{\theta})
. This identity was utilized in a prior work which studied L2 regularization in the lazy regime
[
74
]
. For a
L
-hidden layer ReLU network
\phi(h)=\max(0,h)
, the degree is
\kappa=L+1
, while rectified power law nonlinearities
\phi(h)=\max(0,h)^{q}
give degrees
\kappa=\frac{q^{L+1}-1}{q-1}
. We note that the fixed point of the function dynamics above gives a representer theorem with the final NTK

\displaystyle f(\bm{x})=\bm{k}(\bm{x})^{\top}\left[\bm{K}+\lambda\kappa\bm{I}\right]^{-1}\bm{y}

(98)

where
[\bm{k}(x)]_{\mu}=\lim_{t\to\infty}K(\bm{x},\bm{x}_{\mu},t)
and
K_{\mu\alpha}=\lim_{t\to\infty}K(\bm{x}_{\mu},\bm{x}_{\alpha},t)
. The prior work of Lewkowycz et al
[
74
]
considered NTK parameterization
\gamma_{0}=0
. In this limit, the kernel (and consequently output function) decay to zero at large time, but if
\gamma_{0}>0
, then the network converges to a nontrivial fixed point as
t\to\infty
. In the DMFT limit we can determine the final kernel by solving the following field dynamics

\displaystyle h_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda t}\chi_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\ e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)g_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle z_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda t}\xi_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\ e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{P}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\phi(h_{\alpha}^{\ell}(s))G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s).

(99)

We see that the contribution from initial conditions is exponentially suppressed at large time
t
while the second term contributes most when the system has equilibrated. We provide an example of the weight decay DMFT showing its validity in a two layer ReLU network in Figure
3
.

Appendix K
Bayesian/Langevin Trained Mean Field Networks

Rather than studying exact gradient flow, many works have considered Langevin dynamics (gradient flow with white noise process on the weights) of neural network training
[
25
,
32
,
91
,
30
,
31
]
. This setting is of special theoretical interest since the distribution of parameters converges at long times to a Gibbs equilibrium distribution which has a Bayesian interpretation
[
4
,
5
,
91
]
. The relevant Langevin equation for our mean field gradient flow is

\displaystyle d\bm{\theta}(t)=-\gamma^{2}\nabla L(\bm{\theta}(t))dt-\lambda\beta^{-1}\bm{\theta}(t)dt+\sqrt{2\beta^{-1}}d\bm{\epsilon}(t),

(100)

where
\lambda
is a ridge penalty which controls the scale of parameters, and
d\bm{\epsilon}(t)
is a Brownian motion term which has covariance structure
\left<d\bm{\epsilon}(t)d\bm{\epsilon}(t^{\prime})^{\top}\right>=\delta(t-t^{\prime})\mathbf{I}
. The parameter
\beta
, known as the inverse temperature controls the scale of the random Gaussian noise injected into this stochastic process. The dynamical treatment of the
\beta\to\infty
limit will coincide with our usual DMFT while the
\beta\ll\infty
will exhibit a nontrivial balance between the usual DMFT feature updates and the random Langevin noise. At late times, such a system will equilibrate to its Gibbs distribution.

K.1
Dynamical Analysis

In this section we analyze the dynamical mean field theory for these Langevin dynamics. First we note that the effect of regularization can be handled with a simple integrating factor

\displaystyle d[\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)e^{\frac{\lambda t}{\beta}}]

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}t}\left[\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\mu}\Delta_{\mu}(t)\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))^{\top}\right]dt+\sqrt{2\beta^{-1}}e^{\frac{\lambda t}{\beta}}d\bm{\epsilon}^{\ell}(t).

(101)

where
d\bm{\epsilon}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is the Gaussian noise for layer
\ell
at time
t
. It is straightforward to verify by Ito’s lemma that, under mean field parameterization, the fluctuations in
f^{\prime}s
dynamics due to Brownian motion are
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bm{\theta}}\cdot d\bm{\epsilon}(t)\sim\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})
and are thus negligible in the
N\to\infty
limit. Thus the evolution of the network function takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{\mu}(t)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(t)K_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)-\lambda\beta^{-1}\bm{\theta}(t)\cdot\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}f_{\mu}(t)+\frac{1}{\beta}\text{Tr}\nabla^{2}_{\bm{\theta}}f_{\mu}(t)

We can express both of these parameter contractions in feature space provided we introduce the new features
r_{i,\mu}^{\ell}(t)=\frac{\partial g_{i,\mu}^{\ell}}{\partial h_{i,\mu}^{\ell}}
which are necessary to compute Hessian terms like
\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial W_{ij}^{\ell}\partial W^{\ell}_{ij}}=N^{-3/2}\frac{\partial}{\partial W^{\ell}_{ij}}[g_{i}^{\ell+1}\phi(h_{j}^{\ell})]=N^{-2}\ r_{i}\ \phi(h_{j}^{\ell})^{2}
in each layer. This gives the following evolution

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{\mu}(t)}{\partial t}=\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(t)K_{\mu\alpha}(t,t)-\lambda\beta^{-1}\sum_{\ell}\left<z_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\phi(h_{\mu}^{\ell}(t))\right>+\beta^{-1}\sum_{\ell}\left<r^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\right>\left<\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))^{2}\right>

(102)

As before, we compute the next layer field
\bm{h}^{\ell+1}
in terms of
\bm{\chi}^{\ell+1}
and
\bm{z}^{\ell}
in terms of
\bm{\xi}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}t}\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\ e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}(t-s)}\left[ds\frac{\gamma_{0}}{N}\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(s)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))^{\top}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta N}}d\bm{\epsilon}^{\ell}(s)\right]\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))

\displaystyle\bm{z}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}t}\bm{\xi}_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\ e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}(t-s)}\left[ds\frac{\gamma_{0}}{N}\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(s)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))^{\top}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta N}}d\bm{\epsilon}^{\ell}(s)\right]^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)

The dependence on the initial condition through
\bm{\chi},\bm{\xi}
is suppressed at long times due the regularization factor
e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}t}
, while the Brownian motion and gradient updates will survive in the
t\to\infty
limit. In addition to the usual
\{\bm{\chi}^{\ell},\bm{\xi}^{\ell}\}
fields which arise from the initial condition, we see that
\bm{h}^{\ell}(t),\bm{z}^{\ell}(t)
also depend on the following fields which arise from the integrated Brownian motion

\displaystyle\bm{\chi}^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\ e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}(t-s)}\Theta(t-s)d\bm{\epsilon}^{\ell}(s)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))

\displaystyle\bm{\xi}^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\ e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}(t-s)}\Theta(t-s)d\bm{\epsilon}^{\ell}(s)^{\top}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)

(103)

Our aim is now to compute the moment generating function for the
\{\bm{\chi},\bm{\xi},\bm{\chi}^{\epsilon},\bm{\xi}^{\epsilon}\}
fields which causally determine
\{\bm{h},\bm{z}\}
. This MGF has the form

\displaystyle Z=\left<\exp\left(\sum_{\ell\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\bm{j}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\ell}(t)+\bm{v}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\bm{j}_{\mu}^{\epsilon,\ell}(t)\cdot\bm{\chi}_{\mu}^{\epsilon,\ell}(t)+\bm{v}^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{\xi}^{\epsilon\ell}_{\mu}(t)\right]\right)\right>_{\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{\epsilon}(t)}

(104)

We insert Dirac-delta functions in the usual way to enforce the definitions of
\bm{\chi},\bm{\xi},\bm{\chi}^{\epsilon},\bm{\xi}^{\epsilon}
and then average over
\bm{\theta}_{0},\bm{\epsilon}(t)
. These averages can be performed separately with the
\bm{\theta}_{0}
average giving the identical terms as derived in previous sections. We focus on the average over Brownian disorder

\displaystyle\ln\left<\exp\left(i\sqrt{2}\beta^{-1/2}N^{-1/2}\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ \text{Tr}\left[\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))^{\top}+\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)^{\top}\right]\int e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}(t-s)}\Theta(t-s)d\bm{\epsilon}(s)\right)\right>_{\bm{\epsilon}(t)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\beta N}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\left|\int dt\ \Theta(t-s)e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}(t-s)}\sum_{\mu}\left[\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))^{\top}+\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)^{\top}\right]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}dt^{\prime}\Theta(t-s)\Theta(t^{\prime}-s)e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}(t-s+t^{\prime}-s)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\mu\alpha}\left[\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})+\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})+2i\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})A^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}dt^{\prime}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}(t+t^{\prime})\right)\left[e^{2\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\min\{t,t^{\prime}\}}-1\right]

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\mu\alpha}\left[\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})+\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})+2i\bm{\hat{\chi}}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})A^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})\right]

(105)

where we introduced the order parameter
iA^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\cdot\bm{\hat{\xi}}^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\alpha}(s)
. We will use the shorthand for the temporal prefactor in the above

C_{\lambda,\beta}(t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\lambda}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}(t+t^{\prime})\right)\left[e^{2\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\min\{t,t^{\prime}\}}-1\right]\sim_{t,t^{\prime}\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}|t-t^{\prime}|\right)
. We insert a Lagrange multiplier
B^{\epsilon,\ell}
to enforce the definition of
A^{\epsilon,\ell}
. After

\displaystyle Z\propto

\displaystyle\int d\Phi_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)d\hat{\Phi}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)dG_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)d\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)dA_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)dB_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}(t,s)dA_{\mu\alpha}^{\epsilon\ell}(t,s)dB_{\mu\alpha}^{\epsilon\ell}(t,s)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(NS[\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G},A,B,A^{\epsilon},B^{\epsilon}]\right)

(106)

The order parameters can be determined by the saddle point equations. These equations for
\Phi,\hat{\Phi},G,\hat{G},A,B
are the same as before. The new equations are

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta A^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=-B^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-iC_{\lambda,\beta}(t,s)\left<{\hat{\chi}}_{\mu}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}(t)g^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(s)\right>=0

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta B^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=-A^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-iC_{\lambda,\beta}(t,s)\left<\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\hat{\xi}^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\right>=0

(107)

Using the fact that
\bm{\Phi}^{\ell},G^{\ell}
concentrate, we can use the Hubbard trick to linearize the quadratic terms in
\hat{\chi}^{\epsilon}
and
\hat{\xi}^{\epsilon}
.

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\ C_{\lambda,\beta}(t,s)\sum_{\mu\alpha}\hat{\chi}_{\mu}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}(t)\hat{\chi}_{\alpha}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}(s)\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right)

\displaystyle=\left<\exp\left(-i\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ u^{\epsilon,\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\hat{\chi}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\right)\right>_{u^{\epsilon,\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,C\odot\Phi^{\ell})}

(108)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\ C_{\lambda,\beta}(t,s)\sum_{\mu\alpha}\hat{\xi}_{\mu}^{\epsilon,\ell}(t)\hat{\xi}_{\alpha}^{\epsilon,\ell}(s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right)

\displaystyle=\left<\exp\left(-i\sum_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ r^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)\hat{\xi}^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)\right)\right>_{r^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,C\odot G^{\ell+1})}

(109)

Using the vectorization notation, we find the interpretation that
\bm{\chi}^{\epsilon,\ell}
and
\bm{\xi}^{\epsilon,\ell}
decouple as

\displaystyle\bm{\chi}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{u}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}+\bm{A}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}\bm{g}^{\ell+1}\ ,\ \bm{\xi}^{\epsilon,\ell}=\bm{r}^{\epsilon,\ell}+\bm{B}^{\epsilon,\ell\top}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})

(110)

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\epsilon,\ell+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{C}_{\lambda,\beta}\odot\left<\frac{\partial\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})}{\partial\bm{r}^{\epsilon,\ell}}\right>\ ,\ \bm{B}^{\epsilon\ell}=\bm{C}_{\lambda,\beta}\odot\left<\frac{\partial\bm{g}^{\ell+1}}{\partial\bm{u}^{\ell+1}}\right>^{\top}

(111)

As before, we make the substitutions
\bm{B}\to\gamma_{0}^{-1}{\bm{B}}^{\top}
and
\bm{A}\to\gamma_{0}^{-1}\bm{A}
and arrive at the final DMFT equations

\displaystyle\{u^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\Phi^{\ell-1})\ ,\ \{r^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,G^{\ell+1})

\displaystyle\{u^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)\}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,C_{\lambda,\beta}\odot\Phi^{\ell-1})\ ,\ \{r^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)\}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,C_{\lambda,\beta}\odot G^{\ell+1})

\displaystyle h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}t}\left[u^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha}A^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)g^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\right]

\displaystyle+u^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\ \sum_{\alpha}\left[A^{\epsilon,\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}(t-s)}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]g^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)

\displaystyle z^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}t}\left[r^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\sum_{\alpha}B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)g^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\right]

\displaystyle+r^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ds\ \sum_{\alpha}\left[B^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+e^{-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}(t-s)}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))

where the kernels are defined in the usual way. As expected, the contributions from the initial conditions
\chi^{\ell},\xi^{\ell}
are exponentially suppressed at late time whereas the contributions from the Brownian disorder
\chi^{\epsilon,\ell},\xi^{\epsilon,\ell}
persist at late time.

K.2
Weak Feature Learning, Long Time Limit

In the weak feature learning
\gamma_{0}\to 0
and long time
t\to\infty
limit, the preactivation fields equilibrate to Gaussian processes
h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\sim u^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\mu}(t),z^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\sim r^{\epsilon,\ell}_{\alpha}(t)
, which have respective covariances
H^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\left<h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\right>=C_{\lambda,\beta}(t,s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s),Z^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\left<z^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)z^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\right>=C_{\lambda,\beta}(t,s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)
. In this long time limit, the feature kernels will be time translation invariant eg
\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(|t-s|)
. Letting
\tau=|t-s|
and
C_{\lambda,\beta}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\tau\right)
, we have the following recurrence for
H^{\ell},\Phi^{\ell}

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)

\displaystyle=C_{\lambda,\beta}(\tau)K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}\ ,\ \Phi^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)=\left<\phi(h)\phi(h^{\prime})\right>_{h,h^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\bm{H}^{1}\right)}\ ,\ \bm{H}^{1}=\begin{bmatrix}H^{1}_{\mu\mu}(0)&H^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)\\
H^{1}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)&H^{1}_{\alpha\alpha}(0)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle H^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)

\displaystyle=C_{\lambda,\beta}(\tau)\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)\ ,\ \Phi^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\left<\phi(h)\phi(h^{\prime})\right>_{h,h^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\bm{H}^{\ell+1}\right)}

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}H^{\ell+1}_{\mu\mu}(0)&H^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)\\
H^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)&H^{\ell+1}_{\alpha\alpha}(0)\end{bmatrix}

(113)

Similarly, we can obtain
Z^{\ell}
and
G^{\ell}
in a backward pass recursion

\displaystyle Z^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)

\displaystyle=C_{\lambda,\beta}(\tau)\ ,\ G^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)=\dot{\Phi}^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)Z^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)\ ,\ \dot{\Phi}^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)=\left<\dot{\phi}(h)\dot{\phi}(h^{\prime})\right>_{h,h^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{H}^{L})}

\displaystyle Z^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)

\displaystyle=C_{\lambda,\beta}(\tau)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)\ ,\ G^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)=\dot{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)Z^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)\ ,\ \dot{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(\tau)=\left<\dot{\phi}(h)\dot{\phi}(h^{\prime})\right>_{h,h^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{H}^{\ell})}

(114)

On the temporal diagonal
\tau=0
, these equations give the usual recursions used to compute the NNGP kernels at initialization
[
5
]
, though with initialization variance
C_{\lambda,\beta}(0)=\lambda^{-1}
, set by the weight decay term in the Langevin dynamics. This indicates that the long time Langevin dynamics at
\gamma_{0}\to 0
simply rescales the Gaussian weight variance based on
\lambda
. It would be interesting to explore fluctuation dissipation relationships at finite
\gamma_{0}
within this framework which we leave to future work.

K.3
Equilibrium Analysis

The Langevin dynamics at finite
N
converges (possibly in a time extensive in
N
) to an equilibrium distribution with several interesting properties, as was recently studied by Aitchison et al
[
91
]
and implicitly by Seroussi et al
[
31
]
in a large sample size limit. This setting differs from the previous section where first
N\to\infty
limit is taken, followed by a
t\to\infty
limit in the DMFT. This, section, on the other hand studies for any
N
, the
t\to\infty
limiting equilibrium distribution. This equilibrated distribution is then analyzed in the
N\to\infty
limit. The relationship between these two orders of limits remains an open problem.
The equilibrium distribution over parameters
p(\bm{\theta}|\mathcal{D})\propto\exp\left(-\beta\gamma^{2}L(\bm{\theta})-\frac{\lambda}{2}|\bm{\theta}|^{2}\right)
can be viewed as a Bayes posterior with log-likelihood
-\beta\gamma^{2}L(\bm{\theta})
and a Gaussian prior with scale
\lambda^{-1/2}
. In the mean field limit with
\gamma=\sqrt{N}\gamma_{0}
, we can express the density over pre-activations
\bm{h}^{\ell}
and the output predictions
f
. This gives

\displaystyle p(\bm{f}|\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle\propto\exp\left(-N\gamma_{0}^{2}\beta\sum_{\mu}\ell(f_{\mu},y_{\mu})\right)

\displaystyle\times\int d\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}\left<\prod_{\mu}\delta\left(f_{\mu}-\frac{1}{N\gamma_{0}}\bm{w}^{L}\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{L}_{\mu})\right)\prod_{\mu\ell}\delta\left(\bm{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{W}^{\ell}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu})\right)\right>_{\bm{\theta}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\lambda^{-1}\mathbf{I})}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\int\prod_{\mu}d\hat{f}_{\mu}\prod_{\ell\mu\alpha}d\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}d\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}\exp\left(-N\gamma_{0}^{2}\beta\sum_{\mu}\ell(f_{\mu},y_{\mu})-N\gamma_{0}\sum_{\mu}\hat{f}_{\mu}f_{\mu}+\frac{N}{2\lambda}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\hat{f}_{\mu}\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}\hat{f}_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{N}{2}\sum_{\ell\mu\alpha}\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}+N\sum_{\ell}\ln\mathcal{Z}[\Phi^{\ell-1},\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}]\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[\Phi^{\ell-1},\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}]=\int\prod_{\mu}\frac{dh_{\mu}d\hat{h}_{\mu}}{2\pi}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\hat{h}_{\mu}\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}\hat{h}_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\phi(h_{\mu})\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}\phi(h_{\alpha})+i\sum_{\mu}\hat{h}_{\mu}h_{\mu}\right)

(115)

We see that
p(\bm{f}|\mathcal{D})\propto\int d\Phi d\hat{\Phi}\exp\left(NS[\Phi,\hat{\Phi}]\right)
where

\displaystyle S=-\gamma_{0}^{2}\beta\sum_{\mu}\ell(f_{\mu},y_{\mu})-\gamma_{0}\sum_{\mu}\hat{f}_{\mu}f_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\hat{f}_{\mu}\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}\hat{f}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell\mu\alpha}\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}+\sum_{\ell}\ln\mathcal{Z}[\Phi^{\ell-1},\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}]

(116)

Thus the predictions
f_{\mu}
become non-random in this
N\to\infty
limit and can be determined from the saddle point equations as in
[
91
]
. Again, letting
\Delta_{\mu}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial f_{\mu}}\ell(f_{\mu},y_{\mu})
, we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S}{\partial f_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\hat{f}_{\mu}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\beta\Delta_{\mu}=0\ ,\ \frac{\partial S}{\partial\hat{f}_{\mu}}=-\gamma_{0}f_{\mu}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{\alpha}\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}\hat{f}_{\alpha}=0

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S}{\partial\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\lambda}\hat{f}_{\mu}\hat{f}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\Phi}^{L}_{\mu\alpha}=0\ ,\ \frac{\partial S}{\partial\hat{\Phi}^{L}_{\mu\alpha}}=\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\left<\phi(h^{L}_{\mu})\phi(h^{L}_{\alpha})\right>=0

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S}{\partial\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left<\hat{h}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}\hat{h}_{\alpha}^{\ell+1}\right>+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}=0\ ,\ \frac{\partial S}{\partial\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}}=\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\left<\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu})\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha})\right>=0

(117)

which implies that
f_{\mu}
at the fixed point satisfies the following equations

\displaystyle f_{\mu}=\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\sum_{\alpha}\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}\ ,\ \Delta_{\alpha}=-\frac{\partial\ell(f_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})}{\partial f_{\alpha}}.

(118)

The last layer’s dual kernel has the form
\hat{\Phi}^{L}_{\mu\alpha}=-\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}\beta^{2}}{2\lambda}\Delta_{\mu}\Delta_{\alpha}
, which we see vanishes as feature learning strength is taken to zero
\gamma_{0}\to 0
, while for non-negligible
\gamma_{0}
, we see that the last layer features are non-Gaussian. We thus see that the moment generating function for the last layer field has the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[\Phi^{L-1},\hat{\Phi}^{L}]=\int\prod_{\mu}\frac{dh_{\mu}d\hat{h}_{\mu}}{2\pi}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\hat{h}_{\mu}\hat{h}_{\alpha}\Phi^{L-1}_{\mu\alpha}+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}\beta^{2}}{2\lambda}\left[\sum_{\mu}\Delta_{\mu}\phi(h_{\mu})\right]^{2}+i\sum_{\mu}\hat{h}_{\mu}h_{\mu}\right)

(119)

In the
\gamma_{0}\to 0
limit, the non-Gaussian component of this density vanishes. Now that we have this form, we can compute
\Phi^{L}
conditional on
\Phi^{L-1}
. Next, we calculate
\hat{\Phi}^{L-1}_{\mu\alpha}=\left<\hat{h}^{L}_{\mu}\hat{h}^{L}_{\alpha}\right>
, giving

\displaystyle\bm{\hat{\Phi}}^{L-1}=\lambda[\bm{\Phi}^{L-1}]^{-1}-\lambda^{2}[\bm{\Phi}^{L-1}]^{-1}\left<\bm{h}^{L}\bm{h}^{L\top}\right>[\bm{\Phi}^{L-1}]^{-1}

(120)

Again, we note that in the
\gamma_{0}\to 0
limit, since
\left<\bm{h}^{L}\bm{h}^{L}\right>\sim\lambda^{-1}\bm{\Phi}^{L-1}
, so that
\hat{\bm{\Phi}}^{L-1}=0
, implying that the
h^{L-1}
fields are also Gaussian in this
\gamma_{0}\to 0
limit.
For arbitrary
\gamma_{0}
, this recursive argument can be completed going backwards using

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}=\left<\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})^{\top}\right>\ ,\ \bm{\hat{\Phi}}^{\ell-1}=\lambda[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1}]^{-1}-\lambda^{2}[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1}]^{-1}\left<\bm{h}^{\ell}\bm{h}^{\ell\top}\right>[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1}]^{-1}

(121)

For deep linear networks, the distributions are all Gaussian, allowing one to close algebraically, the saddle point equations for
\Phi,\hat{\Phi}

]

Appendix L
Momentum Dynamics

Standard gradient descent often converges slowly and requires careful tuning of learning rate. Momentum, in contrast can, be stable under a wider range of learning rates and can benefit from acceleration on certain problems
[
92
,
93
,
94
,
95
]
. In this section we show that our field theory is still valid when training with momentum; simply altering the field definitions appropriately gives the infinite-width feature learning behavior.

Momentum uses a low-pass filtered version of the gradients to update the weights. A continuous limt of momentum dynamics on the trainable parameters
\{\bm{W}^{\ell}\}
would give the following differential equations.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{Q}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle\tau\frac{d}{dt}\bm{Q}^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=-\bm{Q}^{\ell}+\frac{\gamma}{N}\sum_{\mu}\Delta_{\mu}(t)\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))^{\top}

(122)

We write the expression this way so that the small time constant
\tau\to 0
limit corresponds to classic gradient descent. Integrating out the
\bm{Q}^{\ell}(t)
variable, this gives the following weight dynamics

\displaystyle\bm{W}^{\ell}(t)=\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)+\frac{\gamma}{N\tau}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime\prime}e^{-(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})/\tau}\sum_{\mu}\Delta_{\mu}(t^{\prime\prime})\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t^{\prime\prime})\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t^{\prime\prime}))^{\top}

(123)

which implies the following field evolution

\displaystyle h^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\chi^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime\prime}e^{-(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})/\tau}\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(t^{\prime\prime})g^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime\prime})\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle z^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\xi^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime\prime}e^{-(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime})/\tau}\sum_{\alpha}dt^{\prime\prime}\Delta_{\alpha}(t^{\prime\prime})\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime\prime}))G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,t^{\prime\prime})

(124)

We see that in the
\tau\to 0
limit, the
t^{\prime\prime}
integral is dominated by the contribution at
t^{\prime\prime}\sim t^{\prime}
recovering usual gradient descent dynamics. For
\tau\gg 0
, we see that the integral accumulates additional contributions from the past values of fields and kernels.

Appendix M
Discrete Time

Our model can also be accommodated in discrete time, though we lose the NTK as a key player in the theory (note that
\frac{d}{dt}f_{\mu}=\frac{df_{\mu}}{d\theta}\cdot\frac{d\theta}{dt}=\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}K_{\mu\alpha}^{NTK}
requires a continuous time limit of the gradient descent dynamics). For a discrete time analysis we let
t\in\mathbb{N}
and define our network function as

\displaystyle f_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N\gamma_{0}}\bm{w}^{L}(t)\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{L}_{\mu}(t))=\frac{1}{N\gamma_{0}}\left[\bm{w}^{L}(0)+\gamma_{0}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\phi(\bm{h}^{L}_{\alpha}(s))\right]\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{L}_{\mu}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N\gamma_{0}}\bm{w}^{L}(0)\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{L}_{\mu}(t))+\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{s<t}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

(125)

We treat
f_{\mu}(t)
as a potentially random variable and insert

\displaystyle 1=\int\frac{d\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)df_{\mu}(t)}{2\pi N^{-1}}\exp\left(i\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)\left[Nf_{\mu}(t)-\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}\bm{w}^{L}(0)\cdot\phi(\bm{h}^{L}(t))-N\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{s<t}\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right]\right)

(126)

Noting that
\bm{w}^{L}(0)
is involved in the definition of both
f_{\mu}(t)
and
\bm{\xi}_{\mu}^{L}(t)
, we see that the average over
\bm{w}^{L}(0)
now takes the form

\displaystyle\left<\exp\left(i\sum_{\mu t}[\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{L}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}^{-1}\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)\phi(\bm{h}^{L}_{\mu}(t))]\cdot\bm{w}^{L}(0)\right)\right>_{\bm{w}^{L}(0)}=

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu t\alpha s}\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{L}_{\mu}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{L}_{\alpha}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{N}{2\gamma_{0}^{2}}\sum_{\mu\alpha ts}\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\right)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}\sum_{\mu\alpha ts}\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)\phi(\bm{h}_{\mu}^{L}(t))\cdot\hat{\bm{\xi}}^{L}_{\alpha}(s)\right)

(127)

We extend our definition as before
iA^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\frac{1}{N\gamma_{0}}\phi(\bm{h}^{L}_{\mu}(t))\cdot\bm{\xi}^{L}_{\alpha}(s)
. Proceeding with the calculation as usual, we find that

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\propto\int df_{\mu}(t)d\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)d\Phi^{\ell}...dB^{\ell}\exp\left(NS[\{f,\hat{f},\Phi^{\ell},\hat{\Phi}^{\ell},...,A^{\ell},B^{\ell}\}]\right)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=i\sum_{\mu t}\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)f_{\mu}(t)-\frac{1}{2\gamma_{0}^{2}}\sum_{\mu\alpha ts}\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)\hat{f}_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-i\sum_{\mu\alpha ts}\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)A^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-i\sum_{\mu t\alpha s}\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)[\Theta(t-s)\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)]

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell\mu\alpha ts}[\Phi_{\mu\alpha}^{\ell}\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+G^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\hat{G}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-A^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)]

\displaystyle+\ln\mathcal{Z}[\{\Phi^{\ell},\hat{\Phi}^{\ell},...,A^{\ell},B^{\ell}\}]

(128)

The saddle point equations can now be analyzed. In addition to the usual order parameters, we note that
f,\hat{f}
also generate saddle point equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S}{\partial f_{\mu}(t)}

\displaystyle=i\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)=0

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S}{\partial i\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)}

\displaystyle=f_{\mu}(t)+\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}^{2}}\sum_{\alpha s}\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)(i\hat{f}_{\alpha}(s))-\sum_{\alpha s}A^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-\sum_{\alpha s}\Theta(t-s)\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

(129)

We also obtain saddle point equations for the new
A^{L},B^{L}
order parameters.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S}{\partial A^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=-B^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)-i\hat{f}_{\mu}(t)=0

(130)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S}{\partial B^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)}

\displaystyle=-A^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+i\gamma_{0}^{-1}\left<\phi(h^{L}_{\mu}(t))\hat{\xi}^{L}_{\alpha}(s)\right>=0

(131)

which implies
B^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=0
and
A^{L}=\gamma_{0}^{-1}\left<\frac{\phi(h^{L}_{\mu}(t))}{\partial r^{L}_{\alpha}(s)}\right>
. This gives the following DMFT

\displaystyle f_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s<t}\sum_{\alpha}\Phi^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)\Delta_{\alpha}(s)+\sum_{\alpha s}A^{L}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1})\ ,\ \bm{r}^{\ell}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{G}^{\ell+1})

\displaystyle h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=u^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\sum_{\alpha s}[A^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)\Delta_{\alpha}(s)\Phi^{\ell-1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)]g^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)

\displaystyle z^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=r^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)+\gamma_{0}\sum_{\alpha s}[B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)\Delta_{\alpha}(s)G^{\ell+1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)]\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))

\displaystyle\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\left<\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s))\right>\ ,\ G^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\left<g^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)g^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)\right>

\displaystyle A^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}^{-1}\left<\frac{\partial\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))}{\partial u^{\ell}_{\alpha}(s)}\right>\ ,\ B^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)=\gamma_{0}^{-1}\left<\frac{\partial g_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(t)}{\partial r^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(s)}\right>.

(132)

We leave it to future work to verify that a continuous time limit of the above DMFT recovers function evolution governed by the NTK.

as a
independently sampled Gaussian matrix

\tilde{\bm{W}}^{\ell}(0)

\displaystyle\left<\exp\left(\frac{i}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\left[\sum_{\mu}\hat{\bm{\chi}}^{\ell+1}(t)\bm{W}^{\ell}(0)\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))+\bm{g}^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)^{\top}\tilde{\bm{W}}^{\ell}(0)\bm{\xi}^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)\right]\right)\right>

Appendix P
Perturbation Theory

P.1
Small
\gamma_{0}

In this section we analyze the leading corrections in a small
\gamma_{0}
expansion of our DMFT theory. All fields are expanded in power series in
\gamma_{0}
.

\displaystyle h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)-u^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{0}^{n}h^{\ell,(n)}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle z^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)-r^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{0}^{n}z^{\ell,(n)}_{\mu}(t)

(143)

Our goal is to calculate all corrections to the kernels up to
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{3})
to show that the leading correction is
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{2})
and the subleading correction is
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})
. It will again be convenient to utilize the vector notation defined in
D
.

We note that unlike other works on perturbation theory in wide networks, we do not attempt to characterize fluctuation effects in the kernels due to finite width, but rather operate in a regime where the kernels are concentrating and their variance is negligible. For a more thorough discussion of perturbative field theory in finite width networks, see
[
27
,
28
,
35
]
.

P.1.1
Linear Network

The kernels in deep linear networks can be expanded in powers of
\gamma_{0}^{2}
giving a leading order correction of size
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{2})
and can be computed explicitly from the closed saddle point equations.
We use the symmetrizer
\{\bm{X},\bm{Y}\}_{sym}=\bm{X}\bm{Y}+\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X}^{\top}
as shorthand. The leading order behavior of
\bm{C}^{\ell}\sim\bm{C}^{(0)}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{2})\ ,\ \bm{D}^{\ell}\sim\bm{D}^{(0)}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{2}),\bm{H}^{\ell,0}=\bm{H}^{(0)}=\bm{K}^{x}\otimes\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top},\bm{G}^{\ell,(0)}=\bm{G}^{(0)}=\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}
is independent of layer index so we find the following leading order corrections

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\sim\bm{H}^{(0)}+\ell\gamma_{0}^{2}\left(\{\bm{C}^{(0)}\bm{D}^{(0)},\bm{H}^{(0)}\}_{sym}+\bm{C}^{(0)}\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}\bm{C}^{(0)\top}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\sim\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}+(L+1-\ell)\gamma_{0}^{2}(\{\bm{D}^{(0)}\bm{C}^{(0)},\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}\}_{sym}+\bm{D}^{(0)}\bm{H}^{(0)}[\bm{D}^{(0)}]^{\top})+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})

\displaystyle\bm{K}^{NTK}

\displaystyle\sim L\bm{H}^{0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\frac{L(L+1)}{2}\left(\{\bm{C}^{(0)}\bm{D}^{(0)},\bm{K}^{x}\}_{sym}+\bm{C}^{(0)}\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}\bm{C}^{(0)\top}\right)

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{2}\frac{L(L+1)}{2}\bm{K}^{x}\otimes(\{\bm{D}^{(0)}\bm{C}^{(0)},\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}\}_{sym}+\bm{D}^{(0)}\bm{H}^{(0)}[\bm{D}^{(0)}]^{\top})+O(\gamma_{0}^{4})

(144)

Note that
[\bm{C}^{0}\bm{g}]_{\mu t}=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\sum_{\beta}H^{0}_{\mu\beta}(t,t^{\prime})\Delta_{\beta}(t^{\prime})g(t^{\prime})=\sum_{\beta}K^{x}_{\mu\beta}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\Delta_{\beta}(t^{\prime})g(t^{\prime})
and note that
[\bm{D}\bm{h}]_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}G^{0}(t,t^{\prime})\sum_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})h_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})=\sum_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\Delta_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})h_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})
.

\displaystyle H^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)

\displaystyle=K^{x}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\ell\gamma_{0}^{2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}K^{x}_{\nu\beta}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\Delta_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime\prime}\Delta_{\beta}(t^{\prime\prime})+((\mu,t)\leftrightarrow(\nu,s))

\displaystyle+\ell\gamma_{0}^{2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}K^{x}_{\nu\beta}\left[\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\Delta_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})\right]\left[\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}\Delta_{\beta}(s^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle G^{\ell}(t,s)

\displaystyle=1+\gamma_{0}^{2}(L+1-\ell)\sum_{\alpha\beta}K^{x}_{\alpha\beta}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\Delta_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime\prime}\Delta_{\beta}(t^{\prime\prime})+(t\leftrightarrow s)

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{2}(L+1-\ell)\sum_{\alpha\beta}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}\left[\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\Delta_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})\right]\left[\int_{0}^{s}ds^{\prime}\Delta_{\alpha}(s^{\prime})\right]

(145)

We can simplify the notation by introducing functions
v_{\alpha}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\Delta_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})
and
v_{\alpha\beta}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\Delta_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime\prime}\Delta_{\beta}(t^{\prime\prime})
.

\displaystyle H^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)

\displaystyle=K^{x}_{\mu\nu}+\ell\gamma_{0}^{2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}K^{x}_{\nu\beta}[v_{\alpha\beta}(t)+v_{\beta\alpha}(s)]+\ell\gamma_{0}^{2}\sum_{\alpha\beta}K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}K^{x}_{\nu\beta}v_{\alpha}(t)v_{\beta}(s)

\displaystyle G^{\ell}(t,s)

\displaystyle=1+\gamma_{0}^{2}(L+1-\ell)\sum_{\alpha\beta}K^{x}_{\alpha\beta}[v_{\alpha\beta}(t)+v_{\beta\alpha}(s)+v_{\alpha}(t)v_{\beta}(s)]

(146)

Using the fact that

\displaystyle K^{NTK}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}G^{\ell+1}(t,s)H^{\ell}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle\sim(L+1)K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}H^{\ell,2}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\gamma_{0}^{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}G^{\ell,2}(t,s)K^{x}_{\mu\alpha}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})

(147)

and utilizing the identity
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\ell=\frac{1}{2}L(L+1)
, we recover the result provided in the main text.

P.2
Nonlinear Perturbation Theory

We start with the formula which implicitly defines
\bm{h},\bm{z}

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\bm{u}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{\ell}[\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}^{\ell})\odot\bm{z}^{\ell}]\ ,\ \bm{z}^{\ell}=\bm{r}^{\ell}+\gamma_{0}\bm{D}^{\ell}\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell})

(148)

We proceed under the assumption of a power series in
\gamma_{0}

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell}-\bm{u}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\bm{h}^{\ell,1}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{h}^{\ell,2}+...

\displaystyle\bm{z}^{\ell}-\bm{r}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\bm{z}^{\ell,1}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{z}^{\ell,2}+...

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}-\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,0}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,1}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,2}+...

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{\ell}-\bm{G}^{\ell,0}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\bm{G}^{\ell,1}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{G}^{\ell,2}+...

\displaystyle\bm{C}^{\ell}-\bm{C}^{\ell,0}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{\ell,1}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{C}^{\ell,2}+...

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{\ell}-\bm{D}^{\ell,0}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}\bm{D}^{\ell,1}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{D}^{\ell,2}+...

(149)

Expanding both sides of the implicit equation for
\bm{z}^{\ell}
we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\bm{z}^{\ell,1}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{z}^{\ell,2}+...=

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\phi(\bm{u}^{\ell})

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{2}\left[\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{h}^{\ell,1}+\bm{D}^{\ell,1}\phi(\bm{u})\right]

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{3}\left[\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{h}^{\ell,2}+\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{h}^{\ell,1}]^{2}+\bm{D}^{\ell,1}\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{h}^{\ell,1}+\bm{D}^{\ell,2}\phi(\bm{u})\right]

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})

(150)

Performing a similar exercise for
\bm{h}^{\ell}
, we get the following first three leading terms for
\bm{z}^{\ell},\bm{h}^{\ell}
, we find

\displaystyle\bm{z}^{\ell,1}=\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\phi(\bm{u})

\displaystyle\bm{z}^{\ell,2}=\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{h}^{\ell,1}+\bm{D}^{\ell,1}\phi(\bm{u})

\displaystyle\bm{z}^{\ell,3}=\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\left[\frac{1}{2}\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{h}^{\ell,1}]^{2}+\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{h}^{\ell,2}\right]+\bm{D}^{\ell,1}[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{h}^{\ell,1}]+\bm{D}^{\ell,2}\phi(\bm{u})

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell,1}=\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0}=\bm{C}^{\ell,0}[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{r}]

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell,2}=\bm{C}^{\ell,1}\bm{g}^{\ell,1}+\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,2}

\displaystyle=\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{z}^{\ell,1}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{\ell,1}\bm{r}\right]+\bm{C}^{\ell,1}\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{z}^{\ell,2}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{\ell,1}\bm{z}^{\ell,1}+\frac{1}{2}\dddot{\phi}(\bm{u})[\bm{h}^{\ell,1}]^{2}\bm{r}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{\ell,2}\bm{r}\right]

\displaystyle\bm{h}^{\ell,3}=\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,2}+\bm{C}^{\ell,1}\bm{g}^{\ell,1}+\bm{C}^{\ell,2}\bm{g}^{\ell,0}

\displaystyle=\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{z}^{\ell,2}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{\ell,1}\bm{z}^{\ell,1}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{\ell,2}\bm{r}+\frac{1}{2}\dddot{\phi}(\bm{u})[\bm{h}^{\ell,1}]^{2}\bm{r}\right]

\displaystyle+\bm{C}^{\ell,1}\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{z}^{\ell,1}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{\ell,1}\bm{r}\right]+\bm{C}^{\ell,2}\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{r}\right]

(151)

As will become apparent soon, it is crucially important to identify the dependence of each of these terms on
\bm{r}
. We note that
z^{\ell,1}
does not depend on
r
and
h^{\ell,1}
is linear in
r
. In the next section, we use this fact to show that
\Phi^{\ell,1}=0
and
G^{\ell,1}=0
. These conditions imply that
C^{\ell,0}
and
D^{\ell,1}=0
. As a consequence,
z^{\ell,2}
is linear in
r
and
h^{\ell,2}
only contains even powers of
r
. Lastly, this implies that
z^{\ell,3}
only contains even powers of
r
and
h^{\ell,3}
contains only odd powers of
r
.

P.2.1
Leading Corrections to
\Phi^{1}
Kernel is
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{2})

We start in the first layer where
\bm{u}^{1}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{K}^{x}\otimes\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top})
(note that this is
\mathcal{O}_{\gamma_{0}}(1)
) and compute the expansion of
\Phi^{1}
in
\gamma_{0}

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}^{1}=

\displaystyle\left<\phi(\bm{h}^{1})\phi(\bm{h}^{1})^{\top}\right>=\left<\phi(\bm{u}^{1})\phi(\bm{u}^{1})^{\top}\right>

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}\left<\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{1})\bm{h}^{1,1}\right]\phi(\bm{u}^{1})^{\top}\right>+\gamma_{0}\left<\phi(\bm{u}^{1})\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{1})\bm{h}^{1,1}\right]^{\top}\right>

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{2}\left<\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{1})\bm{h}^{1,1}\right]\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{1})\bm{h}^{1,1}\right]^{\top}\right>

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\left<\left[\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{1})\bm{h}^{1,2}\right]\phi(\bm{u}^{1})\right>+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\left<\left[\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{1})\bm{h}^{1,2}\right]\phi(\bm{u}^{1})\right>

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{3}\left<\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{1})\bm{h}^{1,3}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{1,1}\bm{h}^{1,2}+\frac{1}{6}\dddot{\phi}(\bm{u})(\bm{h}^{1,1})^{3}\right]\phi(\bm{u})^{\top}\right>

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{3}\left<\phi(\bm{u})\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{1})\bm{h}^{1,3}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{1,1}\bm{h}^{1,2}+\frac{1}{6}\dddot{\phi}(\bm{u})(\bm{h}^{1,1})^{3}\right]^{\top}\right>

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{3}\left<\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{1,2}+\frac{1}{2}\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})(\bm{h}^{1,1})^{2}\right]\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{1,1}\right]^{\top}\right>

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{3}\left<\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{1,1}\right]\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{1,2}+\frac{1}{2}\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})(\bm{h}^{1,1})^{2}\right]^{\top}\right>

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})

(152)

where powers and multiplications of vectors are taken elementwise. Now, note that, as promised, the terms linear in
\gamma_{0}
vanish since
\bm{h}^{1,1}
is linear the Gaussian random variable
\bm{r}^{1}
, which is a mean zero and independent of
\bm{u}^{1}
so an average like
\left<\bm{r}^{1}F(\bm{u}^{1})\right>=\left<\bm{r}^{1,0}\right>\left<F(\bm{u}^{1})\right>=0
must vanish for any function
F
. Thus we see that
\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}
’s leading correction is
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{2})
.

We also obtain, by a similar argument, that the cubic
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{3})
term vanishes. To see this, note that
\bm{h}^{1,3}
only contains odd powers of
\bm{r}^{1}
. Next,
\bm{h}^{1,1}\bm{h}^{1,2}
contains only odd powers of
\bm{r}
, and
(\bm{h}^{1,1})^{3}
is cubic in
\bm{r}
. Since all odd moments of a mean-zero Gaussian vanish, all averages of these terms over
\bm{r}
annihilate, causing the
\gamma_{0}^{3}
terms to vanish. Thus
\bm{\Phi}^{1}=\bm{\Phi}^{1,0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{\Phi}^{1,2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})
.

P.3
Forward Pass Induction for
\Phi^{\ell}

We now assume the inductive hypothesis that for some
\ell\in\{1,...,L-1\}
that

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}=\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})

(153)

and we will show that this will imply that the next layer must have a similar expansion
\bm{\Phi}^{\ell+1}=\bm{\Phi}^{\ell+1,0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell+1,2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})
. First, we note that
\bm{u}^{\ell+1}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,2}+...)
. As before, we compute the leading terms in the expansion of
\bm{\Phi}^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}^{\ell+1}=

\displaystyle\left<\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell+1})\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell+1})^{\top}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<\phi(\bm{u}^{\ell+1})\phi(\bm{u}^{\ell+1})\right>+\gamma_{0}^{2}\left<\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{\ell+1})\bm{h}^{\ell+1,1}\right]\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{\ell+1})\bm{h}^{\ell+1,1}\right]^{\top}\right>

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\left<\left[\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{\ell+1})\bm{h}^{\ell+1,2}\right]\phi(\bm{u}^{\ell+1})^{\top}\right>+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\left<\phi(\bm{u}^{\ell+1})\left[\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{\ell+1})\bm{h}^{\ell+1,2}\right]^{\top}\right>+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})

(154)

where, as before the
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{0}^{3}
terms vanish by the fact that odd moments of
\bm{r}^{\ell+1}
vanish. Now, note that all averages are performed over
\bm{u}^{\ell+1}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,2}+...)
, which depends on the perturbed kernel of the previous layer. How can we calculate the contribution of the correction which is due to the previous layer’s kernel movement? This can be obtained easily from the following identity. Let
F(\bm{u},\bm{r})
be an arbitrary observable which depends on Gaussian fields
\bm{u}
and
\bm{r}
which have covariances
\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})
and
\bm{G}^{\ell+2,0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{G}^{\ell+2,2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{3})
(note this only requires that the linear in
\gamma_{0}
terms of
G
vanish which is easy to verify). Then

\displaystyle\left<F(\bm{u},\bm{r})\right>_{\bm{u},\bm{r}}

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{k}d\bm{u}d\bm{v}d\bm{r}F(\bm{u},\bm{r})\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bm{k}^{\top}[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,2}+...]\bm{k}+i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{u}\right)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bm{v}^{\top}[\bm{G}^{\ell+2,0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{G}^{\ell+2,2}+...]\bm{v}+i\bm{v}\cdot\bm{r}\right)

(155)

\displaystyle\sim\left<F(\bm{u},\bm{r})\right>_{\bm{u}_{0}\bm{r}_{0}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\text{Tr}\left[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1,2}\left<\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{u}\partial\bm{u}^{\top}}f(\bm{u},\bm{r})\right>_{\bm{u}_{0}\bm{r}_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\text{Tr}\left[\bm{G}^{\ell+1,2}\left<\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{r}\partial\bm{r}^{\top}}f(\bm{u},\bm{r})\right>_{\bm{u}_{0}\bm{r}_{0}}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{3})

(156)

where
\bm{u}_{0}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,0}),\bm{r}_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{G}^{\ell+2,0})
. Thus, the leading order behavior of
\bm{\Phi}^{\ell+1}
can easily be obtained in terms of averages over the original unperturbed covariances

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle=\left<\phi(\bm{u}_{0})\phi(\bm{u}_{0})^{\top}\right>_{\bm{u}_{0}}+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\text{Tr}\left[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,2}\left<\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{u}_{0}\partial\bm{u}_{0}^{\top}}\phi(\bm{u}_{0})\phi(\bm{u}_{0})^{\top}\right>_{\bm{u}_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\gamma_{0}^{2}}|_{\gamma_{0}=0}\left<\phi(\bm{h}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{r}_{0},\gamma_{0}))\phi(\bm{h}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{r}_{0},\gamma_{0}))\right>_{\bm{u}_{0},\bm{r}_{0}}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4}),

(157)

where the trace is taken against the Hessian indices and the indices on
\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,2}
. This gives us the desired result by induction that for all
\ell\in\{1,...,L\}
, we have
\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}=\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})
. We see that
\Phi^{\ell}
accumulates corrections from the previous layers’ corrections through the forward pass recursion.

P.4
Leading Corrections to
G^{L}
Kernel is
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{2})

The analogous argument for
\bm{G}^{L}
now can be provided. First note that
\bm{r}^{L}
is independent of
\bm{u}^{L}
and of
\gamma_{0}
. Thus we can find that
\bm{G}^{L}
has no linear-in-
\gamma_{0}
term in its expansion since

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{L,1}

\displaystyle=\left<[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{L})\bm{r}^{L}]\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{L})\bm{z}^{L,1}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{L})\bm{h}^{L,1}\bm{r}^{L}\right]\right>+\left<[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{L})\bm{r}^{L}]\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{L})\bm{z}^{L,1}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u}^{L})\bm{h}^{L,1}\bm{r}^{L}\right]\right>=0

each term contains only odd powers of
\bm{r}^{L}
and odd moments of Gaussian variables vanish. After much more work, one can verify that
\bm{G}^{L,3}
also must vanish since all terms contain odd powers of
\bm{r}
.

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{L,3}=

\displaystyle\left<\bm{g}^{L,3}\bm{g}^{L,0\top}\right>+\left<\bm{g}^{L,0}\bm{g}^{L,3\top}\right>+\left<\bm{g}^{L,2}\bm{g}^{L,1\top}\right>+\left<\bm{g}^{L,1}\bm{g}^{L,2\top}\right>

(158)

First, note that
\bm{g}^{L,0}
is linear in
\bm{r}
. Next, note that
\bm{g}^{L,1}
only depends on even powers of
\bm{r}
since
\bm{g}^{L,1}=\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{z}^{L,1}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{L,1}\bm{r}
. Next, we have

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{L,2}=\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{z}^{L,2}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})[\bm{h}^{L,2}\bm{r}+\bm{h}^{L,1}\bm{z}^{L,1}]+\frac{1}{2}\dddot{\phi}(\bm{u})[\bm{h}^{L,1}]^{2}

(159)

which only depends on odd powers of
\bm{r}
. Lastly, we have
\bm{g}^{L,3}

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{L,3}

\displaystyle=\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{z}^{L,3}+\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})[\bm{h}^{L,3}\bm{r}+\bm{h}^{L,2}\bm{z}^{L,1}+\bm{h}^{L,1}\bm{z}^{L,2}]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\dddot{\phi}(\bm{u})[2\bm{h}^{L,1}\bm{h}^{L,2}\bm{r}+[\bm{h}^{L,1}]^{2}\bm{z}^{L,1}]+\frac{1}{6}\phi^{(4)}(\bm{u})[\bm{h}^{L,1}]^{3}\bm{r}

(160)

which we see only contains even powers of
\bm{r}
. Thus
\bm{g}^{L,3}\bm{g}^{L,0}
will be odd in
\bm{r}
. Looking at the expansion for
\bm{G}^{L,3}
, we see that all terms are odd in
\bm{r}
and so the averages vanish under the Gaussian integrals.

P.5
Backward Pass Recursion for
G^{\ell}

We can derive a similar recursion on the backward pass for
\bm{G}^{\ell}
’s leading order corrections. Using the same idea from the previous section, we find the following expressions

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{\ell}=

\displaystyle\left<\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}_{0})\bm{r}_{0}\right]\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}_{0})\bm{r}_{0}\right]^{\top}\right>_{\bm{u}_{0},\bm{r}_{0}}+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\left<\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}_{0})\dot{\phi}(\bm{u}_{0})\right>_{\bm{u}_{0}}\odot\bm{G}^{\ell+1,2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\gamma_{0}^{2}}|_{\gamma_{0}=0}\left<\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{r}_{0},\gamma_{0}))\bm{r}_{0}\right]\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{r}_{0},\gamma_{0}))\bm{r}_{0}\right]^{\top}\right>_{\bm{u}_{0},\bm{r}_{0}}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})

This time, we see that
\bm{G}^{\ell}
accumulates corrections from succeeding layers through the backward pass recursion.

P.6
Form of the Leading Corrections

We can expand the
\bm{h}^{\ell}
and
\bm{z}^{\ell}
fields around
\bm{u}^{\ell,0},\bm{r}^{\ell,0}
to find the leading order corrections to each feature kernel

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\gamma_{0}^{2}}|_{\gamma_{0}=0}\left<\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{r}_{0},\gamma_{0}))\phi(\bm{h}^{\ell}(\bm{u}_{0},\bm{r}_{0},\gamma_{0}))^{\top}\right>_{\bm{u}_{0},\bm{r}_{0}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\left[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1,2}\left<\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{u}_{0}\partial\bm{u}_{0}^{\top}}\left[\phi(\bm{u}_{0})\phi(\bm{u}_{0})^{\top}\right]\right>_{\bm{u}_{0}}\right]

(161)

The first term requires additional expansion to extract the corrections in
\gamma_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle\phi(\bm{u}+\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell})

\displaystyle\sim\phi(\bm{u})+\gamma_{0}\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell}]+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{C}^{\ell}\bm{g}^{\ell}]^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\phi(\bm{u})+\gamma_{0}\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0}]+\gamma_{0}^{2}\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,1}]+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0}]^{2}

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}(\bm{h}^{\ell})\odot\bm{z}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\sim\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{r}+\gamma_{0}\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0}]\odot\bm{r}+\gamma_{0}\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\phi(\bm{u})]+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{2})

\displaystyle C^{\ell,0}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle=A^{\ell-1,1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)\Delta_{\alpha}^{0}(s)\Phi^{\ell-1,0}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle D^{\ell,0}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

\displaystyle=B^{\ell,1}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)+\Theta(t-s)\Delta_{\alpha}^{0}(s)\Phi^{\ell-1,0}_{\mu\alpha}(t,s)

(162)

where we used the fact that
\bm{C}^{\ell,1}=0
which follows from the fact that
\Phi^{\ell-1,1}=0
, and
\Delta^{\ell,1}=0
. Now, expanding out term by term

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}^{\ell}=

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\left<[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot(\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0})][\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot(\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0})]^{\top}\right>

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{2}\left<\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot(\bm{C}^{\ell,0}[\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0}]\odot\bm{r}])\right]\phi(\bm{u})^{\top}\right>+\text{transpose}

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{2}\left<\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot(\bm{C}^{\ell,0}[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\phi(\bm{u})]])\right]\phi(\bm{u})^{\top}\right>+\text{transpose}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\left<\left[\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0}]^{2}\right]\phi(\bm{u})^{\top}\right>+\text{transpose}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\text{Tr}\left[\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1,2}\left<\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{u}\partial\bm{u}^{\top}}\left[\phi(\bm{u})\phi(\bm{u})^{\top}\right]\right>_{\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1,0})}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})

(163)

We see that the corrections for the
\Phi^{\ell}
kernels accumulate on the forward pass through the final term so
\Phi^{\ell,2}\sim\mathcal{O}(\ell)
. Now we will perform the same analysis for
\bm{G}^{\ell}
.

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{\ell}=

\displaystyle\left<\bm{g}^{\ell}(\bm{u},\bm{r})\bm{g}^{\ell}(\bm{u},\bm{r})^{\top}\right>_{\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1,0})\bm{r}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\bm{G}}^{\ell+1,0})}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\text{Tr}\left[\bm{G}^{\ell+1,2}\left<\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{r}\partial\bm{r}^{\top}}\left[(\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{r})(\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{r})^{\top}\right]\right>_{\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1,0})\bm{r}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\bm{G}}^{\ell+1,0})}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<\bm{g}^{\ell}(\bm{u},\bm{r})\bm{g}^{\ell}(\bm{u},\bm{r})^{\top}\right>_{\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1,0})\bm{r}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\bm{G}}^{\ell+1,0})}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\bm{G}^{\ell+1,2}\odot\left<\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\right>_{\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1,0})}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})

(164)

We see that, through the second term, the
\bm{G}^{\ell}
kernels accumulate on the backward pass so that
\bm{G}^{\ell,2}\sim\mathcal{O}(L+1-\ell)
.
As before the difficult term is the first expression which requires a full expansion of
\bm{g}^{\ell}
to second order

\displaystyle\bm{g}^{\ell}\sim

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{r}+\gamma_{0}\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot[\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\phi(\bm{u})+\gamma_{0}\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0}]

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})[\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0}+\gamma_{0}\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,1}]\odot\bm{r}

(165)

From these terms we find

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{\ell}=

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{\ell,0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}\left<[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot(\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\phi(\bm{u}))][\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot(\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\phi(\bm{u}))]^{\top}\right>

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{2}\left<[\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})(\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0})][\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})(\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0})]^{\top}\right>

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{2}\left<\left[\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\left(\bm{D}^{\ell,0}\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0}\right)\right]\bm{g}^{\ell,0}\right>+\text{transpose}

\displaystyle+\gamma_{0}^{2}\left<[\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{C}^{\ell,0}(\ddot{\phi}(\bm{u})\odot\bm{C}^{\ell,0}\bm{g}^{\ell,0})]\bm{g}^{\ell,0}\right>+\text{transpose}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\bm{G}^{\ell+1,2}\odot\left<\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\dot{\phi}(\bm{u})\right>_{\bm{u}\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,\bm{\Phi}^{\ell-1,0})}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{4})

(166)

Now the correction to the NTK has the form

\displaystyle\bm{K}^{NTK,2}=\bm{\Phi}^{L,2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\bm{G}^{\ell,0}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\bm{G}^{\ell,2}\bm{\Phi}^{\ell,0}+\bm{G}^{1,2}\odot(\bm{K}^{x}\otimes\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top})

(167)

Since each
\Phi^{\ell,2},G^{L+1-\ell,2}\sim\mathcal{O}(\ell)
, each of the two sums from
\ell\in\{1,...,L-1\}
gives a depth scaling of the form
\sim\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\ell=\frac{L(L-1)}{2}
. Since the original NTK has scale
\bm{K}^{NTK,0}\sim\mathcal{O}(L)
, the relative change in the kernel is
\frac{|\bm{K}^{2}|}{|\bm{K}^{0}|}=\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{2}L)
. In a finite width
N
, network, our definition
\gamma=\gamma_{0}\sqrt{N}
would indicate that a width
N
network would have corrections of scale
\gamma_{0}^{2}L=\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{N}
in the NTK regime where
\gamma=\mathcal{O}_{N}(1)
provided the network is sufficiently wide to disregard initialization dependent fluctuations in the kernels.

P.7
Perturbation Theory in Width
N
(Finite Size Corrections)

Finite size corrections to the DMFT can also be obtained within our field theoretic framework. Let
\bm{k}=\text{Vec}\{\bm{\Phi}^{\ell},\hat{\bm{\Phi}}^{\ell},\bm{G}^{\ell},\hat{\bm{G}}^{\ell},\bm{A}^{\ell},\bm{B}^{\ell}\}
denote the collection of kernel order parameters of the DMFT. To simplify the subsequent discussion, we redefine the DMFT action to be its negation
S\to-S
. The DMFT action
S[\bm{k}]
defines a Gibbs measure over order parameters
\bm{k}
, where observables
O(\bm{k})
have averages which can be computed as

\displaystyle\left<O(\bm{k})\right>=\frac{\int d\bm{k}\exp\left(-NS[\bm{k}]\right)O(\bm{k})}{\int d\bm{k}\exp\left(-NS[\bm{k}]\right)}

(168)

The infinite-width DMFT is characterized by the set of saddle point equations which are
\nabla_{\bm{k}}S[\bm{k}]|_{\bm{k}=\bm{k}^{*}}=0
. Let the saddle point be
\bm{k}^{*}
. To identify corrections to the observable average
\left<O(\bm{k})\right>
due to finite size, we now Taylor expand
S
around
\bm{k}^{*}

\displaystyle S[\bm{k}]=

\displaystyle S[\bm{k}^{*}]+\frac{1}{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{*})\nabla^{2}_{\bm{k}}S[\bm{k}]|_{\bm{k}=\bm{k}^{*}}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{*})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{ijl}(k_{i}-k_{i}^{*})(k_{j}-k_{j}^{*})(k_{l}-k_{l}^{*})\frac{\partial^{3}S}{\partial k_{i}\partial k_{j}\partial k_{l}}+...

(169)

The linear component vanishes at the saddle point since
\nabla_{\bm{k}}S[\bm{k}]|_{\bm{k}=\bm{k}^{*}}=0
. Our observable average is thus

\displaystyle\left<O(\bm{k})\right>=\frac{\int d\bm{k}\exp\left(-\frac{N}{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{*})\nabla^{2}S[\bm{k}^{*}](\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{*})+...\right)O(\bm{k})}{\int d\bm{k}\exp\left(-\frac{N}{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{*})\nabla^{2}S[\bm{k}^{*}](\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{*})+...\right)}

(170)

\displaystyle=\frac{\int d\bm{\delta}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\delta}\nabla^{2}S[\bm{k}^{*}]\bm{\delta}-U(\bm{\delta})\right)O(\bm{k}^{*}+N^{-1/2}\bm{\delta})}{\int d\bm{\delta}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\delta}\nabla^{2}S[\bm{k}^{*}]\bm{\delta}-U(\bm{\delta})\right)}

(171)

where we made the change of variables
\bm{\delta}=\sqrt{N}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{*})
. The function
U(\bm{\delta})
contains all higher order terms (cubic and higher) in the Taylor expansion of
NS[\bm{k}]
. Since the leading power in
U
is cubic in
(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{*})=N^{-1/2}\bm{\delta}
, the leading behavior of this remainder is
U=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})
so it can be regarded as a perturbation to the Gibbs distribution. Taylor expanding the exponential
\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\delta}\nabla^{2}S[\bm{k}^{*}]\bm{\delta}-U(\bm{\delta})\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\delta}\nabla^{2}S[\bm{k}^{*}]\bm{\delta}\right)[1-U+\frac{1}{2}U^{2}+...]
in both numerator and denominator, we eliminate the presence of the higher order terms in the Gibbs measure. Lastly, we let
\left<\cdot\right>_{0}
represent an average over the unperturbed Gaussian potential
\bm{\delta}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,[\nabla^{2}S[\bm{k}^{*}]]^{-1})
. For notational simplicity, we let
\epsilon=N^{-1/2}
and obtain

\displaystyle\left<O(\bm{k})\right>

\displaystyle=\frac{\left<O(\bm{k}^{*}+\epsilon\bm{\delta})\right>_{0}-\left<O(\bm{k}^{*}+\epsilon\bm{\delta})U(\bm{\delta})\right>+\frac{1}{2}\left<O(\bm{k}^{*}+\epsilon\bm{\delta})U(\bm{\delta})^{2}\right>_{0}+...}{1-\left<U(\bm{\delta})\right>_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\left<U(\bm{\delta})^{2}\right>_{0}+...}

\displaystyle=\left<O(\bm{k}^{*}+\epsilon\bm{\delta})\right>_{0}-\left[\left<O(\bm{k}^{*}+\epsilon\bm{\delta})U(\bm{\delta})\right>-\left<O(\bm{k}^{*}+\epsilon\bm{\delta})\right>\left<U(\bm{\delta})\right>\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left[\left<O(\bm{k}^{*}+\epsilon\bm{\delta})U(\bm{\delta})^{2}\right>-\left<O(\bm{k}^{*}+\epsilon\bm{\delta})\right>\left<U(\bm{\delta})^{2}\right>\right]

\displaystyle-\left[\left<O(\bm{k}^{*}+\epsilon\bm{\delta})U(\bm{\delta})\right>\left<U(\bm{\delta})\right>_{0}-\left<O(\bm{k}^{*}+\epsilon\bm{\delta})\right>\left<U(\bm{\delta})\right>_{0}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+...

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\left<O(\bm{k}^{*}+\epsilon\bm{\delta})U(\bm{\delta})^{n}\right>_{0}^{c}

(172)

where
\left<\right>_{0}^{c}
represents a
connected cumulant

[
96
]
. The first two connected correlations have the form

\displaystyle\left<OU\right>_{0}^{c}

\displaystyle=\left<OU\right>_{0}-\left<O\right>_{0}\left<U\right>_{0}

\displaystyle\left<OU^{2}\right>_{0}^{c}

\displaystyle=\left<OU^{2}\right>_{0}-2\left<OU\right>_{0}\left<U\right>_{0}-\left<O\right>_{0}\left<U^{2}\right>_{0}+2\left<O\right>_{0}\left<U\right>_{0}^{2}

(173)

If one is interested only in the leading order correction to the observable
\left<O(\bm{k})\right>
, this can be obtained with the following correction

\displaystyle\left<O(\bm{k})\right>=O(\bm{k}^{*})+\frac{1}{2N}\text{Tr}\left[\nabla^{2}S[\bm{k}^{*}]\right]^{-1}\nabla_{\bm{k}}^{2}O(\bm{k}^{*})+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\nabla_{\bm{k}}O(\bm{k}^{*})\cdot\left<\bm{\delta}U(\bm{\delta})\right>_{0}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})

(174)

Since
U=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})
both corrections are of order
1/N
. This analysis shows that the leading order correction of the kernel distributions is
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
and can be approximated by performing averages over a Gaussian distribution for
\bm{k}
determined by the saddle point solution
\bm{k}^{*}
and covariance given by
\frac{1}{N}\left[\nabla^{2}_{\bm{k}}S[\bm{k}]|_{\bm{k}=\bm{k}^{*}}\right]^{-1}
. We derive expressions for the components of this Hessian in
P.7.1
. These fluctuations have standard deviation
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})
. This technique is a common approach to identifying finite size effects
[
56
]
and was recently employed in Bayesian inference setting for networks in the lazy regime
[
29
]
.

Before computing the Hessian terms, we can compare finite size effects under NTK scaling
\gamma=\mathcal{O}_{N}(1)
, and the mean field scaling
\gamma=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})
. Concretely, we are interested in the feature learning component of the kernel change which is
\mathcal{O}(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{N})
. Let
\left<\Delta\bm{k}\right>
represent the change in the kernel through training, which we showed in
P.2
is of size
\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{0}^{2})=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{N}\right)
. We will now define the signal to noise ratio of feature learning as

\displaystyle\text{SNR}=\frac{\left<\Delta\bm{k}\right>}{\sqrt{\text{Var}(\bm{k})}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\sqrt{N}}\right)

(175)

For NTK regime, this is vanishing as
N\to\infty
, while for the DMFT regime, this is goes as
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})
since kernel evolution is always
\mathcal{O}(1)
but variance is
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
.

P.7.1
DMFT Action Hessian

We now compute the various blocks of the Hessian of the DMFT action
-\nabla_{\bm{k}}^{2}S[\bm{k}^{*}]
. The various necessary derivatives are given below. We will utilize the the factorization of the single site MGF
\sum_{\ell}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{\ell}
to simplify many of the expressions. Below we provide exhaustive expressions for each of the relevant terms. These expressions are included for completeness but we did not yet attempt computing all of them as we did the saddle point equations which define
\bm{k}^{*}
. First, we list the collection of Hessian terms which do not involve
A,B
below.

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\hat{\Phi}^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\left[\left<\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\phi(h^{\ell}_{\nu}(s))\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime}))\phi(h^{\ell}_{\beta}(s^{\prime}))\right>-\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\Phi_{\alpha\beta}^{\ell}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\Phi^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\Phi^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=-\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{\mu\alpha}\delta_{\nu\beta}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\delta(s-s^{\prime})+\frac{\partial}{\partial\Phi^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial\hat{G}^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\hat{G}^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\left[\left<g^{\ell}_{\mu}(t)g^{\ell}_{\nu}(s)g^{\ell}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})g^{\ell}_{\beta}(s^{\prime})\right>-G^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)G_{\alpha\beta}^{\ell}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial{G}^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial G^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial\hat{G}^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial G^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=-\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{\mu\alpha}\delta_{\nu\beta}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\delta(s-s^{\prime})+\frac{\partial}{\partial G^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}G^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\hat{G}^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\left[\left<\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\phi(h^{\ell}_{\nu}(s))g^{\ell}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})g^{\ell}_{\beta}(s^{\prime})\right>-\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)G^{\ell}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial\hat{\Phi}^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial G^{\ell}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial G^{\ell}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}\Phi^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial\hat{G}^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\Phi^{\ell}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\Phi^{\ell}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}G^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial G^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\Phi^{\ell}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=0

Now for the terms involving
A^{\ell},B^{\ell}
, we find the following expressions.

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial A^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial A^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\left<\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u^{\ell+1}_{\nu}(s)\partial u^{\ell^{\prime}+1}_{\beta}(s^{\prime})}[g^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)g^{\ell^{\prime}+1}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})]\right>-B^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)B^{\ell}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial B^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial B^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\left<\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{\ell}_{\nu}(s)\partial r^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\beta}(s^{\prime})}[\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\phi(h^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime}))]\right>

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial A^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial B^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{\mu\alpha}\delta_{\nu\beta}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\delta(s-s^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\left<\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u_{\nu}^{\ell+1}(s)\partial r^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\beta}(s^{\prime})}[g^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\phi(h^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha}(s))]\right>-B^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)A^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial A^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\Phi^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\left<\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial u^{\ell+1}_{\nu}(t)\partial u^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})\partial u^{\ell+1}_{\beta}(s^{\prime})}g^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\right>

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial A^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\hat{\Phi}^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell+1,\ell^{\prime}}\left[\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\ell+1}_{\nu}(t)}[g^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\phi(h^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime}))\phi(h^{\ell+1}_{\beta}(s^{\prime}))]\right>-B^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\Phi^{\ell+1}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial A^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial G^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell+2,\ell^{\prime}}\left<\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial u^{\ell+1}_{\nu}(t)\partial r^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})\partial r^{\ell+1}_{\beta}(s^{\prime})}g^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)\right>

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial A^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\hat{G}^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell+1,\ell^{\prime}}\left[\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\ell+1}_{\nu}(t)}[g^{\ell+1}_{\mu}(t)g^{\ell+1}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})g^{\ell+1}_{\beta}(s^{\prime})]\right>-B^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)G^{\ell+1}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial B^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\Phi^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell-1,\ell^{\prime}}\left<\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial r^{\ell}_{\nu}(t)\partial u^{\ell}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})\partial u^{\ell}_{\beta}(s^{\prime})}\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\right>

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial B^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\hat{\Phi}^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\left[\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial r^{\ell}_{\nu}(t)}[\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\phi(h^{\ell}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime}))\phi(h^{\ell}_{\beta}(s^{\prime}))]\right>-A^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\Phi^{\ell}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial B^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial G^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell+1,\ell^{\prime}}\left<\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial r^{\ell}_{\nu}(t)\partial r^{\ell}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})\partial r^{\ell}_{\beta}(s^{\prime})}\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))\right>

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial B^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)\partial\hat{G}^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\left[\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial r^{\ell}_{\nu}(t)}[\phi(h^{\ell}_{\mu}(t))g^{\ell}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime})g^{\ell}_{\beta}(s^{\prime})]\right>-A^{\ell}_{\mu\nu}(t,s)G_{\alpha\beta}^{\ell}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\right]

From these block matrices which comprise the Hessian, we can obtain the finite width covariance structure in our order parameters
\bm{k}=\text{Vec}\{\Phi^{\ell},G^{\ell},A^{\ell},B^{\ell}\}
by computing the inverse
\bm{C}=\left(-\nabla^{2}_{\bm{k}}S[\bm{k}^{*}]\right)^{-1}
. In this approximation scheme, we have
\bm{\bm{k}}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{k}^{*},\frac{1}{N}\left(-\nabla^{2}_{\bm{k}}S[\bm{k}^{*}]\right)^{-1})
. Many questions about this expansion remain including: can it be proven that these
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})
fluctuations always lead to higher expected test loss?

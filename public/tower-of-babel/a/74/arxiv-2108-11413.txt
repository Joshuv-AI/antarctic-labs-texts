From the lineshape of the X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) to its structure
Angelo Esposito, Luciano Maiani, Alessandro Pilloni, Antonio D. Polosa, Verónica Riquer

Abstract
From a study of the lineshape of the X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) , the LHCb collaboration measures a sizeable negative effective range. This cannot be reconciled with a shallow D ​ D ¯ ∗ D\bar{D}^{*} bound state hypothesis. Based on Weinberg’s compositeness criterion, together with a theorem by Smorodinsky, it follows that the X X has to have a compact hidden charm structure interacting with unbound D ​ D ¯ ∗ D\bar{D}^{*} pairs via short-distance color forces. This conclusion is strengthened by the general pattern recently emerging from exotic mesons.

II
Scattering amplitude and compositeness

Analogously to
Weinberg 1965
, call
H_{0}
the Hamiltonian representing the QCD quark interaction generating compact color singlets. In the open charm sector, the spectrum of

Consider the low-energy

\displaystyle f=\frac{1}{k\cot\delta(k)-ik}=\frac{1}{-\kappa_{0}+\frac{1}{2}r_{0}k^{2}+\dots-ik}\,,

(2)

In presence of a state
X
below threshold by an energy

\displaystyle\kappa=\kappa_{0}+\frac{1}{2}r_{0}\kappa^{2}\,,

(3)

A discrimination between different structures of the

III
The effective range of a molecule

As anticipated, Landau and Smorodinsky add a specific consideration regarding the value of
r_{0}
for a molecule, constraining the terms of order
1/m_{\pi}
, left unspecified in Eq. (
5b
)
Landau and Lifshitz 2003
;
Blatt and Weisskopf 1952
, when
Z=0
. Their conclusion extends what happens for the deuteron to a general theorem:
shallow bound states with purely attractive binding force give always
r_{0}>0
. For convenience of the reader, we report a proof of the theorem, following Bethe’s derivation
Bethe 1949
.

Consider the Schrödinger’s equation for the radial wave function of the molecular constituents,

\displaystyle u_{k}^{\prime\prime}(r)+\big[k^{2}-U(r)\big]u_{k}(r)=0\,,

(10)

with
U(r)\equiv 2\mu V(r)
,
V(r)<0
being the potential, which is assumed to be attractive everywhere.
We consider the wave function for two values of the momentum:
u_{k_{1,2}}\equiv u_{1,2}
. A simple manipulation leads to the identity

\displaystyle u_{2}u_{1}^{\prime}-u_{2}^{\prime}u_{1}\Big|_{0}^{R}=(k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2})\int_{0}^{R}dr\,u_{2}u_{1}\,,

(11)

with
R
fixed and much larger than the range of the potential,
R\gg 1/m_{\pi}
.

Consider now the free equation,
\psi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(r)+k^{2}\psi_{k}(r)=0
, from which we also obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{2}\psi_{1}^{\prime}-\psi_{2}^{\prime}\psi_{1}\Big|_{0}^{R}=(k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2})\int_{0}^{R}dr\,\psi_{2}\psi_{1}\,.

(12)

Normalizing to unity at
r=0
, the general expression for
\psi_{k}
is

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(r)=\frac{\sin(kr+\delta(k))}{\sin\delta(k)}\,,

(13)

so that
\psi^{\prime}_{k}(0)=k\cot\delta(k)
.
The radial wave function
u_{k}
vanishes at
r=0
, and we normalize it so that it tends exactly to the corresponding
\psi_{k}
for large enough radii.

With this proviso, we subtract (
11
) from (
12
), to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&k_{2}\cot\delta(k_{2})-k_{1}\cot\delta(k_{1})\\
&\qquad=(k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,(\psi_{2}\psi_{1}-u_{2}u_{1})\,.\end{split}

(14)

Here we used the fact that, for
R
large enough
u_{k}\to\psi_{k}
, as well as the fact that
u_{k}(0)=0
. Moreover, we have extended the integral to infinity, given that it is now convergent due to the same asymptotic behavior of
\psi_{k}
and
u_{k}
.

We are now ready to compare the result with the parameters of the scattering amplitude (
2
). First we set
k_{1}=0
which, recalling that
\lim_{k_{1}\to 0}k_{1}\cot\delta(k_{1})=-\kappa_{0}
, gives

\displaystyle k_{2}\cot\delta(k_{2})=-\kappa_{0}+k_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,\left(\psi_{2}\psi_{0}-u_{2}u_{0}\right)\,.

(15)

Finally, for a shallow bound state, one can further expand for small momenta,
k_{2}\cot\delta(k_{2})=-\kappa_{0}+\frac{1}{2}r_{0}k_{2}^{2}+\dots
, thus finding

\displaystyle r_{0}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,(\psi_{0}^{2}-u_{0}^{2})\,.

(16)

Since
u_{0}(0)=0
and
\psi_{0}(0)=1
and both go to the same limit at infinity, calling
\Delta(r)=\psi_{0}(r)-u_{0}(r)
, we have
\Delta(0)=+1
and
\Delta(\infty)=0
. Moreover, the equations of motion imply
\Delta^{\prime\prime}(r)=-U(r)u_{0}(r)>0
for an attractive potential. In presence of a single bound state, where
u_{0}(r)
does not have nodes, we get
\Delta^{\prime\prime}(r)>0
everywhere, hence proving that
\psi_{0}(r)>u_{0}(r)
.
Given that
u_{0}<\psi_{0}
everywhere, this proves that
r_{0}>0
for a shallow bound state.

This is the result quoted in
Landau and Lifshitz 2003
where the further approximation
\psi_{0}=1
is made, consistently with the fact that
\psi_{0}\simeq 1-r/a_{0}\simeq 1
for a shallow bound state and
r
within the range of the potential.

V
Conclusions

Since the first observation of the
X(3872)
, the debate regarding its nature (and consequently that of other exotic states too) has been intense. To discriminate between a compact tetraquark nature and a loosely-bound molecular one, it is crucial to find quantities that are qualitatively different in the two instances.

In this work we show that, combining the arguments by Weinberg
Weinberg 1965
and by Landau and Smorodinsky
Landau and Lifshitz 2003
;
Smorodinsky 1948
;
Blatt and Weisskopf 1952
;
Bethe 1949
, one can identify one such quantity in the effective range. If the dynamics of the
X
is that of a purely shallow molecule, the effective range must be strictly positive and of the order of the inverse pion mass, as it happens for the deuteron. If, instead, the dynamics is that of an elementary object, the effective range is negative and in magnitude much larger that the inverse pion mass.

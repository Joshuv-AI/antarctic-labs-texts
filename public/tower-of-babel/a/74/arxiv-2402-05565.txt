Duality for condensed cohomology of the Weil group of a p p -adic field
Marco Artusa

Abstract
We use the theory of Condensed Mathematics to build a condensed cohomology theory for the Weil group of a p p -adic field. The cohomology groups are proved to be locally compact abelian groups of finite ranks in some special cases. This allows us to enlarge the local Tate Duality to a more general category of non-necessarily discrete coefficients, where it takes the form of a Pontryagin duality between locally compact abelian groups.

1.2
Relation to previous work

As we already remarked, in
[
29
]
Tate proves that for any finite continuous
G_{F}
-module
M
, the cup-product pairing (
1
) is perfect. This means that the induced maps

\psi^{q}(M):\mathrm{H}^{q}(G_{F},\Hom(M,\overline{F}^{\times}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2-q}(G_{F},M)^{*}

and

\psi^{q}(M^{D}):\mathrm{H}^{q}(G_{F},M)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2-q}(G_{F},\Hom(M,\overline{F}^{\times}))^{*}

are isomorphisms for all
q
, where
(-)^{*}
denotes the Pontryagin dual. In the attempt of generalising this result to finitely generated continuous
G_{F}
-modules, Milne proves the following (see
[
21
, Theorem 2.1]
)

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
G_{F}
-module, and consider the map

\psi^{q}(M^{D}):\mathrm{H}^{q}(G_{F},\Hom(M,\overline{F}^{\times}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2-q}(G_{F},M)^{*}.

Then
\psi^{q}(M)
is an isomorphism for all
q\geq 1
, and
\psi^{0}(M^{D})
defines an isomorphism (of profinite groups)

\mathrm{H}^{0}(G_{F},\Hom(M,\overline{F}^{\times}))^{\wedge}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}(G_{F},M)^{*}.

Hence
\psi^{0}(M)
is an isomorphism only up to profinite completion. However, the profinite completion loses a lot of information on the abelian groups. For example, let
M=\mathbbm{Z}
with the trivial action of
\mathbbm{Z}
. Then we have

\mathrm{H}^{0}(G_{F},\Hom(M,\overline{F}^{\times}))=F^{\times},\qquad\mathrm{H}^{2}(G_{F},M)^{*}=G_{F}^{ab},

and
\psi^{0}(M)
induces an isomorphism of abelian groups

(F^{\times})^{\wedge}\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}G_{F}^{ab},

(3)

which is the reciprocity isomorphism of Local Class Field Theory. We have
F^{\times}\cong\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}\oplus\mathbbm{Z}\cong\mathbbm{Z}_{p}^{n}\oplus H\oplus\mathbbm{Z}
, for some finite abelian group
H
and some
n\in\mathbbm{N}
. Taking the profinite completion, we get

(F^{\times})^{\wedge}\cong\mathbbm{Z}_{p}^{n+1}\oplus H\oplus\prod_{l\neq p}\mathbbm{Z}_{l}.

Hence the information coming from
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
and
\mathbbm{Z}
is mixed. In order to resolve this problem, we should replace the Galois group
G_{F}
with the Weil group
W_{F}
, as suggested by Lichtenbaum (see
[
17
]
).

In
[
16
]
, Karpuk follows this intuition and studies the cohomology of
W_{F}
with discrete coefficients. The role of
\overline{F}^{\times}
is taken by
\overline{L}^{\times}
. The cohomology of
W_{F}
with coefficients in
\overline{L}^{\times}
is given by

\mathrm{H}^{q}(W_{F},\overline{L}^{\times})=\begin{array}[]{ll}F^{\times}&q=0\\
\mathbbm{Z}&q=1\\
0&q\geq 2.\end{array}

If
M
is a finitely generated continuous
W_{F}
-module, we define
M^{D}\coloneqq\Hom(M,\overline{L}^{\times})
and we obtain a cup-product pairing in
\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{Ab})

R\Gamma(W_{F},M)\otimes^{L}R\Gamma(W_{F},M^{D})\rightarrow\tau^{\geq 1}R\Gamma(W_{F},\overline{L}^{\times})=\mathbbm{Z}[-1].

(4)

Karpuk proves the following (see
[
16
, Theorem 3.3.1]
)

Theorem B
.

Suppose that
M
is a finitely generated continuous
W_{F}
-module. Then the map

\psi(M):R\Gamma(W_{F},M^{D})\rightarrow R\Hom(R\Gamma(W_{F},M),\mathbbm{Z}[-1])

induced by (
4
) is a equivalence in
\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{Ab})
. In particular, there are, for all
q
, short exact sequences

0\rightarrow\Ext(\mathrm{H}^{2-q}(W_{F},M),\mathbbm{Z})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{q}(W_{F},M^{D})\rightarrow\Hom(\mathrm{H}^{1-q}(W_{F},M^{D}),\mathbbm{Z})\rightarrow 0.

(5)

Hence we now have a duality for finitely generated modules which holds in all degrees, without needing the profinite completion. However, since
\Ext(\mathrm{H}^{2-q}(W_{F},M),\mathbbm{Z})
does not vanish in general, (
5
) can’t express this duality as a duality between the cohomology groups.

Another aspect to consider is that topology is not taken into account neither by Milne nor by Karpuk. This is the reason why both in Theorem
A
and in Theorem
B
we don’t have perfect pairings, but we only have results about the map
\psi(M)
. We solve this problem by putting a topology both on coefficients and on cohomology groups, as suggested by Geisser and Morin in
[
11
]
. The role played by the discrete
G_{F}
-module
\overline{F}^{\times}
for Milne and by the discrete
W_{F}
-module
\overline{L}^{\times}
for Karpuk is now played by the complex of condensed
\hat{W}_{F}
-modules

\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}(1)\coloneqq[\overline{L}^{\times}\overset{val}{\rightarrow}\mathbbm{R}].

If
M
is a locally compact abelian groups of finite ranks with a structure of a
\hat{W}_{F}
-module, we define
M^{D}\coloneqq R\underline{\Hom}(M,\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}(1))
. In
4.27
we show that if
M
has an action of a finite quotient
G
of
G_{F}
and if
R\underline{\Hom}_{\mathbf{LCA}}(\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z},M)\in\mathbf{D}^{perf}(\mathbbm{Z})
, the cup product-pairing

\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)\otimes\mathbbm{H}^{2-q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\rightarrow\mathbbm{H}^{2}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}(1))=\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}

is a perfect pairing of locally compact abelian groups of finite ranks.

This theorem enlarges the result of Tate to more general non-necessarily discrete coefficients. The “enlarged” local Tate duality takes the form of a Pontryagin duality between locally compact abelian groups, and in this sense it is richer than Theorems
A
and
B
. The proof relies on the topological structure of the cohomology groups. In order to determine it, we must suppose that the action of the Weil group is induced by the action of a finite quotient
G
of
G_{F}
. Hence, even if Theorem
B
only considers discrete finitely generated coefficients, the hypothesis on the action of
W_{F}
is less restrictive in that case. However, if we consider finitely generated abelian groups with a continuous action of
G_{F}
, as in Theorem
A
, this “finitary” property on the action is always satisified.

Finally, as a particular case of
4.27
, if
M=\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}
and
q=1
, the perfect cup-product pairing yields the isomorphism of topological abelian groups

F^{\times}\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}W_{F}^{ab},

which is the reciprocity morphism of Local Class Field Theory “à la Weil” (compare it with (
3
)), and does not need profinite completion.

Remark 2.1
.

We recall some properties of extremally disconnected topological spaces, and their role among condensed sets.

1)

[
26

A condensed set is a functor
X:(\mathbf{Top}^{ed})^{op}\to\mathbf{Set}
such that

X(S_{1}\sqcup S_{2})=X(S_{1})\times X(S_{2})

3)

is an isomorphism if and only if

is an isomorphism for all

Remark 2.32

.

We call

. In particular, we have

.

Let

is naturally isomorphic to

A condensed set

2.3
The classifying topos of a pro-condensed group

We would like to generalize a well known fact from the cohomology of profinite groups with discrete coefficients. Let
(G_{i})_{i\in I}
be a projective system of topological groups and
(A_{i}\in\mathbf{Ab}(B_{\underline{G_{i}}}))_{i\in I}
a compatible system. Let
\pi_{i}:G\to G_{i}
be the projections. We set
G\coloneqq\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\leftarrow\\
i\end{subarray}}{\lim}\,G_{i}
and
A_{\infty}\coloneqq\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\rightarrow\\
i\end{subarray}}{\lim}\,\pi_{i}^{*}A_{i}
. We would like the isomorphism

\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\underline{G}},A_{\infty})\cong\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rightarrow\\
i\end{subarray}}\,\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\underline{G_{i}}},A_{i})

to hold. Unfortunately, this is not always the case even if
G_{i}
is finite for all
i
, see
3.4
and
3.10
. In order to recover this property, we introduce pro-condensed groups and their cohomology.

We are adapting
[
23
, Section 2.6]
to the condensed setting.
A pro-object of a category
C
is a functor
\hat{X}:I\to C
, where
I
is a cofiltered category.

Definition 2.43
.

A pro-condensed group
\hat{G}
is a pro-object in the category
\mathbf{Grp}(\mathscr{C})
of condensed groups. A pro-condensed group
\hat{G}
is
strict
if all transition maps
G_{i}\to G_{j}
are epimorphisms of condensed groups.

Let
\hat{G}:I\to\mathbf{Grp}(\mathscr{C})
be a pro-condensed group. For every
i\in I
we have a condensed group
G_{i}
and a classifying topos
B_{G_{i}}
, which is a topos over
\mathscr{C}
. We get
(B_{G_{i}},f_{ji})_{i,j\in I}
, a projective system of topoi over
\mathscr{C}
.

Definition 2.44
.

The classifying topos of a pro-condensed group
\hat{G}:I\to\mathbf{Grp}(\mathscr{C})
is defined as

B_{\hat{G}}\coloneqq\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\leftarrow\\
i\in I\end{subarray}}{\lim}\,B_{G_{i}},

where the limit is taken in the 2-category of topoi.

For all
i\in I
, we call
\pi_{i}:B_{\hat{G}}\to B_{G_{i}}
the projection morphism, and
f_{\hat{G}}:B_{\hat{G}}\to\mathscr{C}
the structure morphism.

Definition 2.45
.

The
q
th group cohomology of the pro-condensed group
\hat{G}:I\to\mathbf{Grp}(\mathscr{C})
is

\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{G}},-)\coloneqq R^{q}f_{\hat{G},*}:\mathbf{Ab}(B_{\hat{G}})\rightarrow\mathbf{Ab}(\mathscr{C}).

Remark 2.46
.

Let
\hat{G}
be a constant pro-condensed group with value
G\in\mathbf{Grp}(\mathscr{C})
. Then
B_{\hat{G}}
is equivalent to
B_{G}
via any projection
\pi_{i}
, and
\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{G}},-)
coincides with
\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{G},-)
for all
q
. Hence the condensed cohomology of pro-condensed groups generalises the cohomology of condensed groups.

We now prove the existence of a Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence.

Construction 2.47
.

Let

R\underline{\Hom}(R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},V),\mathbbm{R})=R\underline{\Hom}(R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},V),\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z})

and the same for
V^{*}
. Moreover, we have

R\underline{\Hom}(R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},V),\mathbbm{R}[-1])=R\underline{\Hom}_{\mathbbm{R}}(R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},V),\mathbbm{R}[-1])

and the same for
V^{*}
. Consequently, it is enough to prove that the cup-product pairing of condensed
\mathbbm{R}
-modules

R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},V)\otimes^{L}_{\mathbbm{R}}R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},V^{*})\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}[-1]

is perfect. This coincides with the cup-product pairing

R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{W_{k}},V^{I/U})\otimes_{\mathbbm{R}}^{L}R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{W_{k}},(V^{*})^{I/U})\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}[-1].

We conclude as in
4.16
.
∎

Proposition 4.25

(Condensed Weil-Tate Local Duality)
.

Let
M
be a finite abelian group with a continuous action of
W_{F}
. Then the trace map

R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}(1))\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}[-2]

induces a perfect cup-product pairing

R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)\otimes^{L}R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\longrightarrow\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}.

Proof.

Let
\mu\subset\overline{L}^{\times}
denote the discrete continuous
W_{F}
-module of roots of unity. We set
M^{\prime}\coloneqq\Hom(M,\mu)
. By
3.25
and
3.22
, the cup-product pairing becomes

\underline{R\Gamma(B_{W_{F}}(\mathbf{Set}),M)}\otimes^{L}\underline{R\Gamma(B_{W_{F}}(\mathbf{Set}),M^{\prime})}\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}.

By
3.26
, we have
R\underline{\Hom}(\underline{R\Gamma(B_{W_{F}}(\mathbf{Set}),M)},\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z})=\underline{R\Hom(R\Gamma(B_{W_{F}}(\mathbf{Set}),M),\mathbbm{Q}/\mathbbm{Z})}
and the same for
M^{\prime}
. Hence it is enough to prove that the cup-product pairing in
\mathbf{D}^{perf}(\mathbbm{Z})

R\Gamma(B_{W_{F}}(\mathbf{Set}),M)\otimes^{L}R\Gamma(B_{W_{F}}(\mathbf{Set}),M^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathbbm{Q}/\mathbbm{Z}

is perfect. This follows from

[
16
, Proposition 4.1.1]
and
[
21
, Theorem 2.1]
.
∎

Proposition 4.26
.

Let
M\in\mathbf{D}^{perf}(\mathbbm{Z})
with a continuous action of a finite quotient
G
of
G_{F}
. Then one has
R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M),R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mathbf{FLCA})
. Moreover, the trace map

R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}(1))\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}[-2]

induces a perfect cup-product pairing

R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)\otimes^{L}R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}[-2]

in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mathbf{Ab}(\mathscr{C}))
.

Proof.

By induction on the length of the complex and by
4.25
, we can suppose that
M
is a free abelian group of finite
\mathbbm{Z}
-type. By
3.30
and
3.31
, we have
R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M),R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mathbf{FLCA})
.

Let
\psi(M^{D})
and
\psi(M)
be defined as in (
22
). Let us consider
\psi(M^{D})^{\wedge}=R\lim_{m}\psi((M\otimes^{L}\mathbbm{Z}/m)^{D})
and
\psi(M)^{\wedge}=R\lim_{m}\psi(M\otimes^{L}\mathbbm{Z}/m).
Localising at
EG
, we have
M\otimes^{L}\mathbbm{Z}/m\cong M/m
in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}})
. Hence
M\otimes^{L}\mathbbm{Z}/m
is a finite abelian group with a continuous action of
G
. By
4.25
,
\psi((M/m)^{D})
and
\psi(M/m)
are equivalences. Hence so are
\psi(M^{D})^{\wedge}
and
\psi(M)^{\wedge}
.
By
3.30
(resp.
3.31
),
\psi(M^{D})
(resp.
\psi(M)
) satisfies the hypothesis (i) (resp. (ii)) of
4.21
. Thus
\psi(M^{D})
(resp.
\psi(M)
) is an equivalence. This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.27
.

Let
M\in\mathbf{D}^{perf}_{\mathbbm{Z},\mathbbm{R}}
with a continuous action of a finite quotient
G
of
G_{F}
. Then one has
R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M),R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mathbf{FLCA})
. Moreover, the trace map

R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}(1))\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}[-2]

induces a perfect cup-product pairing

R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)\otimes^{L}R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}[-2]

in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mathbf{Ab}(\mathscr{C}))
.

If
\mathrm{H}^{q}(M)
is a locally compact abelian group for all
q
, the induced cup-product pairing

\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)\otimes\mathbbm{H}^{2-q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\rightarrow\mathbbm{H}^{2}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}(1))=\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}

is a perfect pairing of locally compact abelian groups of finite ranks.

Proof.

We have a distinguished triangle in
\mathbf{D}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}})

R\underline{\Hom}(\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z},M)\rightarrow R\underline{\Hom}(\mathbbm{R},M)\rightarrow M.

Since
M\in\mathbf{D}^{perf}_{\mathbbm{Z},\mathbbm{R}}
, we have
R\underline{\Hom}(\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z},M)\in\mathbf{D}^{perf}(\mathbbm{Z})
and
R\underline{\Hom}(\mathbbm{R},M)\in\mathbf{D}^{perf}(\mathbbm{R})
. By
4.26
and
4.23
, the fist two statements of the theorem hold for
R\underline{\Hom}(\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z},M)
and
R\underline{\Hom}(\mathbbm{R},M)
instead of
M
.
Hence the same holds for
M
.

Let us suppose that
\mathrm{H}^{q}(M)
is locally compact for all
q
. We have the spectral sequence

E_{2}^{i,j}=\mathbbm{H}^{i}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},\mathrm{H}^{j}(M))\Longrightarrow\mathbbm{H}^{i+j}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M).

By
4.11
, we have
\mathrm{H}^{j}(M)\in\mathbf{D}^{perf}_{\mathbbm{R},\mathbbm{Z}}\cap\mathbf{FLCA}
for all
j
. By
4.10
,
E_{2}^{i,j}
is locally compact of finite ranks for all
i,j
, and vanishes for all
i\geq 3
. Moreover,
E_{2}^{2,j}
is discrete for all
j
. Thus the morphism
E_{2}^{0,j+1}\rightarrow E_{2}^{2,j}
is strict for all
j
. These are the only morphisms of the second page. Moreover, for all
n\geq 3
and for all
i,j
, we have
d_{n}^{i,j}=0
. Consequently, the condensed abelian group
E_{\infty}^{i,j}
is a locally compact abelian group of finite ranks for all
i,j
. By
4.5
, we have
\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)\in\mathbf{FLCA}
for all
q
. Consequently, we have
\underline{\Ext}^{i}(\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M),\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z})=0
for all
i\geq 1
and for all
q
. Hence the equivalence

R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\cong R\underline{\Hom}(R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M),\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}[-2])

yields

\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\cong\mathrm{H}^{q}(R\underline{\Hom}(R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M),\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}[-2]))\cong\mathbbm{H}^{2-q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)^{\vee},

which is locally compact of finite ranks. The result follows.
∎

Corollary 4.29
.

Let
M
be a discrete countable abelian group. Then for all
q
the condensed abelian group
\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)
is represented by a
\kappa
-small discrete abelian group and
\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})
is represented by a
\kappa
-small compact Hausdorff topological abelian group. Moreover, the trace map

R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}(1))\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}[-2]

induces a perfect cup-product pairing

\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)\otimes\mathbbm{H}^{2-q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\rightarrow\mathbbm{H}^{2}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}(1))=\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}

of
\kappa
-small locally compact abelian groups.

Proof.

Firstly, we observe that we can write

M=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\rightarrow\\
n\end{subarray}}{\lim}\,M_{n},

where
M_{n}
is a
G
-equivariant finitely generated abelian group. In order to show this, we observe that since
M
is countable, we can write
M=\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbbm{N}}
. Hence, for all
n\in\mathbbm{N}
, we consider
M_{n}
as the subgroup of
M
generated by
\{g\cdot x_{i}\}_{g\in G,0\leq i\leq n}
. Then
M_{n}
is
G
-equivariant and finitely generated for all
n
, and if
n_{1}<n_{2}
, we have
M_{n_{1}}\subset M_{n_{2}}
.

Consequently, we have

\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\rightarrow\\
n\end{subarray}}{\lim}\,\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M_{n}),

which is
\kappa
-small and discrete for all
q
. Moreover, since we have
M^{D}=R\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\leftarrow\\
n\end{subarray}}{\lim}\,M_{n}^{D}
, there is an exact sequence in
\mathbf{Ab}(\mathscr{C})

0\rightarrow\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\leftarrow\\
n\end{subarray}}{\lim}^{1}\,\mathbbm{H}^{q-1}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M_{n}^{D})\rightarrow\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\rightarrow\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\leftarrow\\
n\end{subarray}}{\lim}\,\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M_{n}^{D})\rightarrow 0

for all
q
. By
3.31
, the condensed abelian groups
\mathbbm{H}^{q-1}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M_{n}^{D})
and
\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M_{n}^{D})
are compact Hausdorff of finite ranks for all
n
. Hence
\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)
is
\kappa
-small compact Hausdorff for all
q
by
[
1
, Proposition 3.2]
. In particular, since
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mathbf{LCA}_{\kappa})\rightarrow\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mathbf{Ab}(\mathscr{C}))
is a stable
\infty
-subcategory, we have
R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M),R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mathbf{LCA}_{\kappa})
. Moreover, the cup-product pairing

R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)\otimes^{L}R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}[-2]

induces the morphisms

\psi(M^{D}):R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)\rightarrow R\underline{\Hom}(R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D}),\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}[-2])

and

\psi(M):R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\rightarrow R\underline{\Hom}(R\mathbb{\Gamma}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M),\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}[-2]).

It is easy to see that we have
\psi(M)=R\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\leftarrow\\
n\end{subarray}}{\lim}\,\psi(M_{n})
. Moreover, since
R\underline{\Hom}(-,\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z}[-2]):\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mathbf{LCA}_{\kappa})^{op}\rightarrow\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mathbf{LCA}_{\kappa})
is an equivalence, we also have
\psi(M^{D})=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\rightarrow\\
n\end{subarray}}{\lim}\,\psi(M_{n}^{D})
. Consequently, both
\psi(M)
and
\psi(M^{D})
are equivalences by
4.27
. Moreover, since
\underline{\Ext}^{i}(\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M),\mathbbm{R}/\mathbbm{Z})=0
for all
i\geq 1
and for all
q
, taking
q
th cohomology on
\psi(M)
yields

\mathbbm{H}^{q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M^{D})\cong\mathbbm{H}^{2-q}(B_{\hat{W}_{F}},M)^{\vee}

and similarly for
\psi(M^{D})
. The result follows.
∎

Example 4.30
.

Let us consider

. Then we have

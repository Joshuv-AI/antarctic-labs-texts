1.
Introduction

Let
f:Y\rightarrow X
be a finite map of complex algebraic varieties. The
essential dimension

{\text{\rm ed}}(Y/X)
of
f
is the smallest integer
e
such that, over some dense open of
X,
the map
f
arises as the pullback of a map of varieties
of dimension
e.
The motivation for studying this invariant goes back to classical questions about reducing the number of
parameters in a solution to a general
n^{\text{th}}
degree polynomial. It first appeared in work of Kronecker
[
Kronecker
]
and Klein
[
Klein
]
on the quintic, and was formally defined by Tschebotarow
[
Tschebotarow
]
, and in a modern context by Buhler-Reichstein
[
BR
]
,
[
BR2
]
.

An idea which goes back to Arnol’d
[
Arnold
]
,
[
Arnold2
]
, is to use characteristic classes to obtain a lower bound for essential dimension: if
f
has covering group
G
then the cohomology of
G
sometimes contributes to the cohomology of
X
- such classes on
X
are called
characteristic
. If they contribute to the cohomology of
X
in some degree
i,
and
X
is affine,
then
f
cannot arise as a pullback of a
G
-cover of dimension
<i.

The main difficulty with this approach is that to get a lower bound for
{\text{\rm ed}}(Y/X)
one needs to know that (some of) the classes coming from
G
continue to be nontrivial on any Zariski open in
X.
Indeed in
loc. cit.
Arnol’d was able to address the question
only without restricting to open subsets.
For the universal
S_{n}
-cover, this problem was solved by Buhler-Reichstein
[
BR2
]
. Following a suggestion of
Serre, they showed the Stiefel-Whitney classes which arise in that situation are nonzero at the generic point.

In this paper we introduce a new method, which solves this restriction problem in many cases. In particular, it allows us to give lower bounds on
{\text{\rm ed}}(Y/X)
in many cases when
X
is
proper
.
Previously, lower bounds on the essential dimension of coverings
of proper varieties were known only in very special cases
[
CT
,
FKW
,
FS
]
. In fact our results apply to the so called

p
-essential dimension

{\text{\rm ed}}(Y/X;p)
(
p
a prime), where one is allowed to pull back the covering not just to Zariski opens, but to auxiliary coverings of degree prime to
p

[
ReiYo
]
.
A first example of our results is the following, which appears as Cor.
2.3.12
below.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper complex variety of maximal Albanese dimension,
and
Y\rightarrow X
its mod
p
homology cover.
Then for
p\gg 0,

Y\rightarrow X
is p-incompressible, i.e.

{\text{\rm ed}}(Y/X;p)=\text{\rm dim}X.

Recall that the mod
p
homology cover is the covering corresponding to the maximal elementary abelian
p
-group quotient of the fundamental group of
X,
and that
X
has maximal Albanese dimension if the image of its Albanese map has dimension equal to
\text{\rm dim}X.

The condition
p\gg 0
can be made effective in terms of the behavior of
X
and its Albanese map under reduction mod
p.

There are clearly a plethora of varieties to which Theorem
1
applies. This includes the following:

•

X
an abelian variety.

•

X=C_{1}\times\cdots\times C_{r}
for curves

with
{\rm genus}(C_{i})\geq 1.

•

Certain cocompact,
n
-dimensional ball quotients for

; see
[

, p. 167, Cor. 5.9]
.

When
X
is an abelian variety, this confirms (almost all of) a conjecture of Brosnan
[

, Conj. 6.1]
,
which was previously known only for either a very general abelian variety, or in dimension at most 3 for a positive density set of primes
[
FS
]
,
compare also
[
Bogomolov
, Cor. p14]
. The result for a product of curves was known only for a generic product of elliptic curves, by a result of Gabber
[

]
. If

is a generic abelian variety, or a product of generic curves, then the effective version of Theorem

actually implies that the conclusion of the theorem holds for all

We leave it to the reader to find further examples.

-covers. One can apply them to

The study of the (

We remark that when

In this case, one does not need our results on characteristic classes (this is why there is no restriction on

As mentioned above, our lower bounds on essential dimension rely on results
that assert that cohomology classes do
not vanish on restriction to any Zariski open. An example of such a statement is the following, which appears as Cor.
2.2.13
below:

Theorem 4
.

Let
X
be a smooth, proper, complex variety, with unramified good reduction at
p.

Let
i<p-2,
and
W\subset X
a Zariski open. Then the image of the map

H^{i}(X,\mathbb F_{p})\rightarrow H^{i}(W,\mathbb F_{p})

has dimension at least
h^{0,i}_{X}=\text{\rm dim}H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{i}).

To get a feel for what such a statement entails, suppose that the integral cohomology
H^{\bullet}(X,\mathbb Z)
is torsion free.
Then Theorem
4
asserts that certain classes
in the image of
H^{i}(X,\mathbb Z)\rightarrow H^{i}(W,\mathbb Z)
are not divisible by
p
. Although this is a statement about the topology of complex algebraic varieties, it appears to be out of reach of classical methods. The analogue with
\mathbb Q
-coefficients can be proved using Hodge theory, but this does not suffice for applications to essential dimension. We remark that the theorem could also be formulated in terms of the
stable cohomology
introduced by Bogomolov
[
Bogomolov
, p.2]
, which is the image of the cohomology of
X
in the cohomology of its generic point.

For
X
ordinary, Theorem
4
was established by Bloch-Esnault
[
BlochEsnault
, Theorem 1.2]
using more classical
p
-adic Hodge theory. Our proof of Theorem
4
makes use of
prismatic cohomology
, recently introduced by Bhatt-Scholze
[
BhattScholze
]
.
This is a cohomology theory that takes as an input a smooth formal scheme
\mathcal{X}
over a
p
-adic base and, in some sense,
interpolates between the mod
p
(or more generally
p
-adic) étale cohomology of its generic fiber, and the de Rham cohomology of
its special fiber
\mathcal{X}_{k}.
Using it, we translate the statement of Theorem
4
into
the analogous statement for de Rham cohomology of
\mathcal{X}_{k},
and then into a statement about differentials using the Cartier isomorphism. In light of
[
BlochEsnault
, Theorem 1.2]
, this proof shows the strength of the prismatic theory.

The theorem of Bloch-Esnault
[
BlochEsnault
, Theorem 1.2]
has played a role in a number of applications, including the study of torsion and divisibility in Chow groups as in
[
Schoen
,
Totaro
,
Diaz
]
, Lefschetz type theorems
[
PR
]
and Galois actions on fundamental groups of curves
[
HM
]
. We expect that Theorem
4
should allow for strengthenings of many of these results. For example, Scavia
[
Sca
]
has recently applied Theorem
4
to extend the main result of
[
Schoen
]
from

p\equiv 1\text{\rm mod\,}3
to all primes
p>5.

To deduce Theorem
2
from Theorem
4
one considers the composite

H^{i}(G,\mathbb F_{p})\rightarrow H^{i}(X,\mathbb F_{p})\rightarrow H^{i}(W,\mathbb F_{p}).

Theorem
4
and the assumptions of Theorem
2
guarantee this map is nonzero.
However if
Y|_{W}\rightarrow W

arises from a covering of varieties of dimension
<i,
then we may assume that these varieties are affine, and it follows
that the above map must vanish since the cohomological dimension of affine varieties is at most their dimension.

We also prove a variant of Theorem
4
where we consider the image of the map

\wedge^{i}H^{1}(X,\mathbb F_{p})\rightarrow H^{i}(W,\mathbb F_{p})
given by the cup product and restriction. This leads to
Theorem
1
. Using the trace map, it is easy to deduce from Theorem
4
a version where one allows restriction to a prime-to-
p
covering of
W,
so we obtain lower bounds on
p
-essential dimension.

The paper is organized as follows. In §2.1 we review the results we need from prismatic and crystalline cohomology.
In §2.2 we prove Theorem
4
and its variants, and in §2.3 we apply this to characteristic classes to deduce lower bounds on
p
-essential dimension. Throughout §2 we work with schemes or formal schemes equipped with a normal
crossings divisor, and we consider coverings of the complement. This greater level of generality is needed for the applications in §3.
In §3.1, we carry out an analysis of the mod
p
cohomology of (generalized) finite Heisenberg groups. This is used in §3.2 to apply our
results on characteristic classes to torus embeddings over abelian varieties, by verifying the
surjectivity assumption in Theorem
2
(or more precisely its logarithmic analogue) in this case.
Most of §3.2 is actually concerned with a variant of Theorem
4
for torus embeddings over an abelian scheme, where we restrict not just to Zariski opens, but to analytic neighborhoods of the boundary. This is then applied in §3.3 to obtain our results on the
p
-essential dimension of congruence covers: the analytic neighborhoods map to our locally symmetric varieties and we can restrict the congruence covers to them.

2.2.

p

The goal of this subsection is to prove a result on restriction of
étale cohomology classes to open neighborhoods. We begin with a technical lemma. As above,

Lemma 2.2.1
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
k[\![u]\!]
-module, equipped with a Frobenius semi-linear map

\varphi:M\rightarrow M,
and a linear map
V:M\rightarrow\varphi^{*}M
such that
\varphi\circ V=V\circ\varphi=u^{d}
for some integer
d.

If
d+1<p,
then
M
is torsion free.

Proof.

Suppose that
M
contains nontrivial torsion. Since
M
is finitely generated over
k[\![u]\!],
there is a minimal integer

m\geq 1
such that
u^{m}\cdot x=0
for any torsion element
x\in M.
Choose a torsion element
x
so that
u^{m-1}\cdot x\neq 0.

Then in
\varphi^{*}M
we have

u^{d+m}(1\otimes x)=V\circ(1\otimes\varphi)(u^{m}\otimes x)=V(u^{m}\varphi(x))=0,

since
u^{pm}\varphi(x)=0,
so that
\varphi(x)
is torsion in
M.

On the other hand, as
\varphi
is finite flat over
k[\![u]\!],
the smallest power of
u
which kills

1\otimes x\in\varphi^{*}M
is
pm.

Thus
d+m\geq pm,
which implies
p\leq 1+d/m\leq 1+d.

∎

Proposition 3.1.9
.

Let
E
be as in (
3.1.4
), and let

be a mod
p
reduction of

If
p>\frac{1}{2}(\text{\rm rk\,}_{\mathbb Z_{p}}H+3\,\text{\rm rk\,}_{\mathbb Z_{p}}N+1)
, then the map of cohomology rings
H^{\bullet}(\bar{E},\mathbb F_{p})\rightarrow H^{\bullet}(E,\mathbb F_{p})
is surjective.

Proof.

Recall the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence, which converges to the mod
p
cohomology of
E:

E^{i,j}_{2}=\wedge^{i}\bar{H}^{*}\otimes\wedge^{j}\bar{N}^{*}=H^{i}(H,H^{j}(N,\mathbb F_{p}))\Rightarrow H^{i+j}(E,\mathbb F_{p}).

By Lemma
3.1.6
and our assumptions on
p,
the spectral sequence
E_{r}

degenerates on the
E_{3}
page.
There is also the analogous spectral sequence
\bar{E}_{r}
converging to
H^{i+j}(\bar{E},\mathbb F_{p})
:

\bar{E}^{i,j}_{2}=H^{i}(\bar{H},\mathbb F_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb F_{p}}H^{j}(\bar{N},\mathbb F_{p})=H^{i}(\bar{H},H^{j}(\bar{N},\mathbb F_{p}))\Rightarrow H^{i+j}(\bar{E},\mathbb F_{p}).

As observed above (by
[
BensonCarlson
, §10]
or
[
AdemMilgram
, Ch. II, Cor. 4.3]
), the cohomology ring
H^{\bullet}(\bar{H},\mathbb F_{p})
is a tensor product of an exterior and a symmetric algebra:

H^{\bullet}(\bar{H},\mathbb F_{p})=\wedge^{\bullet}\bar{H}^{*}(1)\otimes\text{\rm Sym}^{\bullet}\bar{H}^{*}(2),

where

\bar{H}^{*}(1),\bar{H}^{*}(2),
denote the
\mathbb F_{p}
-dual of
\bar{H}
considered in degree
1
and
2
respectively.
One also has
the analogous description of
H^{\bullet}(\bar{N},\mathbb F_{p}).

Let
\tilde{E}^{i,j}_{2}\subset\bar{E}^{i,j}_{2}
be the subgroup generated by the image of

\wedge^{i}\bar{E}_{2}^{1,0}\otimes\wedge^{j}\bar{E}_{2}^{0,1}\simeq\wedge^{i}H^{1}(\bar{H},\mathbb F_{p})\otimes\wedge^{j}H^{1}(\bar{N},\mathbb F_{p})\simeq\wedge^{i}H^{1}(H,\mathbb F_{p})\otimes\wedge^{j}H^{1}(N,\mathbb F_{p})

under the cup product. The inclusion
\tilde{E}^{i,j}_{2}\subset\bar{E}^{i,j}_{2}
induces a splitting of the projection

\bar{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}_{2}\rightarrow E^{\bullet,\bullet}_{2}
as graded groups.

We now make use of the
\mathbb F_{p}^{\times}
-action on
\bar{E}
given by Lemma
3.1.2
.
This induces an
\mathbb F_{p}^{\times}
-action on the spectral sequence
\bar{E}_{r}.

In particular, the differential

d_{2}:\bar{N}^{*}\simeq\bar{E}^{0,1}_{2}\rightarrow\bar{E}^{2,0}_{2}\simeq\wedge^{2}\bar{H}^{*}\oplus\bar{H}^{*}

respects weights, and so satisfies
d_{2}(\bar{E}^{0,1})\subset\tilde{E}^{2,0}_{2}.

Since
\bar{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}_{2}
is a differential graded algebra,
this implies that
\tilde{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}_{2}
is stable under
d_{2}.
Denote the cohomology
of
(\tilde{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}_{2},d_{2})
by
\tilde{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}_{3}.
Then

\tilde{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}_{3}\subset\bar{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}_{3}
induces a splitting of the projection

\bar{E}^{\bullet,\bullet}_{3}\rightarrow E^{\bullet,\bullet}_{3},
as graded groups, so again the latter map is surjective.

To prove the proposition it suffices to show that for
r\geq 2,
and
0\leq i\leq\text{\rm rk\,}_{\mathbb Z_{p}}H,

0\leq j\leq\text{\rm rk\,}_{\mathbb Z_{p}}N,

the map
\bar{E}^{i,j}_{r}\rightarrow E^{i,j}_{r}
is surjective, as the target of this map is trivial for
i,j
outside this range.
Thus we assume from now on that
i,j
satisfy these inequalities.

The above description of the cohomology rings

H^{\bullet}(\bar{H},\mathbb F_{p}),

H^{\bullet}(\bar{N},\mathbb F_{p})
shows that the weights of
\bar{E}^{i,j}_{2}
are represented by integers in the
interval
[\frac{1}{2}(i+2j),i+2j].
Our assumptions on
i,j
and
p
imply that this interval has length less than
p-1
,
i.e. the set of integers in it maps injectively to
\mathbb Z/(p-1)\mathbb Z.

In particular, the weight
i+2j
piece
(\bar{E}^{i,j}_{2})_{i+2j}\subset\bar{E}^{i,j}_{2}
is precisely
\tilde{E}^{i,j}_{2},

and so
\tilde{E}^{i,j}_{3}=(E^{i,j}_{3})_{i+2j}.

Now for
r\geq 3,
consider the differential
d_{r}:\bar{E}^{i,j}_{r}\rightarrow\bar{E}^{i+r,j-r+1}_{r}.

The weights appearing in
\bar{E}^{i+r,j-r+1}_{r}
are represented by integers in

[\frac{1}{2}(i+2j-r+2),i+2j-r+2].
Our assumptions on
i
,
j
, and
p
imply that the interval
[\frac{1}{2}(i+2j-r+2),i+2j]
has length less than
p-1
for
r=3,\dots,j+1.
This implies that
(\bar{E}^{i+r,j-r+1}_{r})_{i+2j}=0,

and so
d_{r}|_{(\bar{E}^{i,j}_{r})_{i+2j}}=0.
On the other hand for
r>j+1

as the target of this map is trivial.

We now show by induction on
r
that
(\bar{E}^{i,j}_{r})_{i+2j}
maps isomorphically to
E^{i,j}_{r},
which in particular implies that

\bar{E}^{i,j}_{r}\rightarrow E^{i,j}_{r}
is surjective, finishing the proof of the proposition.
We have already seen this for
r=3.
Assume the result for some
r\geq 3.
As
d_{r}|_{(\bar{E}^{i,j}_{r})_{i+2j}}=0
it follows that

(\bar{E}^{i,j}_{r})_{i+2j}\twoheadrightarrow H(\bar{E}_{r})^{i,j}_{i+2j}\simeq(\bar{E}^{i,j}_{r+1})_{i+2j}\rightarrow E^{i,j}_{r+1}\simeq E^{i,j}_{r},

where the final isomorphism follows from Lemma
3.1.6
.
The composite is the natural map, which we are assuming is an isomorphism. Hence all the maps above are isomorphisms, which
completes the induction.
∎

3.2.10
.

Now suppose that

Then

Suppose that

Lemma 3.2.12
.

Suppose that
S
and
X/S
are smooth, with
D\subset X
a relative normal crossings divisor.
Let
Z\subset\widehat{X}
be a formal subscheme of codimension
1,
and
Z^{i}\subset\widehat{X}^{i}
its proper transform.
Then there exists a dense open subset
W\subset S
and an integer
i\geq 1
such that
D_{0}^{i}|_{W}\nsubseteq Z^{i}.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{i}:\widehat{X}^{i}\rightarrow\widehat{X}.
Recall that the proper transform
Z^{i}
is defined to be the union of those
components of
\pi_{i}^{-1}(Z)
which are not contained in
\pi_{i}^{-1}(D_{0}).
It suffices to show the lemma with
S
replaced by one of its
generic points, so we may assume that
S={\operatorname{Spec\,}}\kappa
is a field.
By induction on the number of irreducible components of
Z,
we may assume that
Z
is an irreducible Cartier divisor in
X.

Let
x\in D_{0},
and let
z_{1},\dots z_{n}
be a system of local co-ordinates at
x,
such that the ideal of
D
at
x
is given by

z_{1}z_{2}\dots z_{n}.
Then in a formal neighborhood of
x
, the ideal of
Z
is generated by a nonzero power series
f=\sum_{I}a_{I}z^{I},

where
I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
runs over
n
-tuples of non-negative integers, and
a_{I}\in\kappa.
Choose such an
n
-tuple

J=(j_{1},\dots,j_{n})
such that
a_{J}\neq 0
and
|J|=j_{1}+\dots+j_{n}
is as small as possible. If
|J|=0,
then
x\notin Z,

and the lemma holds without blowing up
X.
In general we proceed by induction on
|J|.

We may assume without loss of generality that
j_{1}\neq 0.
There is a point
x^{1}\in D^{1}_{0}\subset\widehat{X}^{1}
such that
the functions
z_{1},u_{2},u_{3},\dots,u_{n},
with
u_{i}=\frac{z_{i}}{z_{1}}
are a system of local co-ordinates at
x^{1},
and the
ideal of
D^{1}
is generated by
z_{1}u_{2}\dots u_{n}.
The ideal of the proper transform
Z^{1}\subset\widehat{X}^{1}
is generated by the function

z_{1}^{-|J|}f,
whose expansion contains the term
a_{I}u_{2}^{j_{2}}\dots u_{n}^{j_{n}}.
Thus the result follows by induction on
|J|.

∎

3.2.13
.

We now assume that
C=\mathbb C,
the complex numbers, and we denote by
X^{{\text{\rm an}}}
the complex analytic space associated to
X.

Recall that a
Zariski closed
subset of a complex analytic space

is a closed subset
Z\subset Y,
which is locally defined by
(local) analytic functions on
Y.
We call the complement of a Zariski closed analytic subset a
Zariski open
subset of
Y.
Note that if
Y\to W
is an open embedding of complex analytic spaces, it is not in general the case that a Zariski open
U\subset Y
is a Zariski open of
W
; this is only the case if the closed analytic subset
Z\subset Y
is of the form
Z=Y\cap Z^{\prime}
for some closed analytic subset
Z^{\prime}\subset W
.

Proposition 3.2.14
.

Let
S
be an abelian scheme and let
X/S
be a proper torus embedding with boundary
D\subset X,

and dense open
T
-torsor
U\subset X.
Let
d=\text{\rm dim}X,
and suppose that
U/S
has unramified good reduction at a prime

Let

be an analytic open subset containing
D^{{\text{\rm an}}},
and
V^{\prime}\subset V
a non-empty Zariski open subset of
V
. Then the map

H^{d}(U,\mathbb F_{p})\rightarrow H^{d}(U\cap V^{\prime},\mathbb F_{p})

is nonzero.

Proof.

After replacing
X
by a blow up, we may assume that
X/S
is smooth and
D\subset

X
is a normal crossings divisor.
Such blow ups are obtained by subdividing the rational cone decomposition of
X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb Q}
defining

see
[
Fultontoric
, p 48]
.
Then
(X,D)
has unramified good reduction at

by Lemma
3.2.11
.

Next the same proof as in Proposition
3.2.7
shows that
h^{0,d}_{(X,D)}\neq 0.
In fact the differential
\omega_{r}\otimes\omega_{s}

defined there is in
H^{0}(X,\Omega^{d}_{X}(\text{\rm log}D))
for any torus embedding
(X,D)
with open dense
T
-torsor
U\subset X.

Shrinking
V^{\prime}
as necessary, we may assume that
Z=V-V^{\prime}
has codimension
1
in
V.
Let
\widehat{Z}\subset\widehat{X}
denote the formal completion of

Z
along
D.
We apply Lemma
3.2.12
to
\widehat{Z}.
Then, after replacing
X
by a blow up and
Z
by its proper transform,
we may assume there
is a non-empty Zariski open
W\subset S
such that
D_{0}|_{W}\nsubseteq Z.
Shrinking
W
if necessary, since
D_{0}
is étale over
S,

we may assume that there is an irreducible component
D^{\prime}_{0}
of
D_{0}
which does not meet
Z,
and that
X|_{W}=W\times X_{0}

for a toric variety
X_{0}
over
\mathbb C.
Then
D_{0}^{\prime}
has the form
W\times x_{0}
for a
T
-fixed point
x_{0}\in X_{0}.

We write
U_{0}\subset X_{0}
for the open
T
-orbit.

By
[
Loj
, Thm. 4]
, there exists a compact subset
W^{-}\subset W
such that the inclusion induces a homotopy equivalence.
Denote by
D_{X_{0}}
the boundary of
X_{0}.
Let
Y_{0}\subset X_{0}
be a ball around

which is small enough that

Y_{0}\backslash D_{X_{0}}\rightarrow U_{0}
is a homotopy equivalence,

W^{-}\times Y_{0}\subset V,
and
W^{-}\times Y_{0}
does not meet

Here we are using the compactness of

for the second and third properties.
Let

Then

Now consider the composite

H^{d}(U,\mathbb F_{p})\rightarrow H^{d}(U|_{W},\mathbb F_{p})\rightarrow H^{d}(U|_{W^{-}},\mathbb F_{p})\rightarrow H^{d}(Y^{*},\mathbb F_{p}).

Since

and

has unramified good reduction at

∎

3.2.15
.

We are nearly ready to show the variant of Proposition
3.2.7
that will be used in the next subsection.
To formulate it we need a notion of essential dimension at
p
for complex analytic spaces. This is defined in a similar way as for algebraic varieties, but there is an important difference in that we do not insist the auxiliary coverings of order prime to
p
are étale.
Unlike the algebraic situation, one cannot reduce ramified coverings
to the unramified case, because the notion of Zariski open subsets of complex analytic spaces is not transitive.

Let
V_{1}\rightarrow V
be a finite map of (reduced) complex analytic spaces. The
essential dimension

{\text{\rm ed}}(V_{1}/V)
is the smallest integer
e

such that for some dense Zariski open
V^{\prime}\subset V,
there exists a finite map of analytic spaces
Y_{1}\rightarrow Y,
and a
map
V^{\prime}\rightarrow Y

such that
V_{1}|_{V^{\prime}}\rightarrow V^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the normalization of
V^{\prime}\times_{Y}Y_{1}.
The
p
-
essential dimension

{\text{\rm ed}}(V_{1}/V_{2};p)

is defined as the minimum value of
{\text{\rm ed}}(\tilde{V}_{1}/\tilde{V}),
where
\tilde{V}\rightarrow V
runs over finite maps of degree prime to
p,

and
\tilde{V}_{1}
is the normalization of
\tilde{V}\times_{V}V_{1}.
Note that the map
\tilde{V}\rightarrow V
is finite flat over some Zariski open;
its degree is defined as the degree over any such Zariski open.

3.2.16
.

Keep the notation of Proposition
3.2.14
, and consider a surjection onto a finite Heisenberg group

the normalization

Corollary 3.2.17
.

With the assumptions of Proposition

, suppose that

and write

Then

{\text{\rm ed}}(V(\bar{E})/V;p)=d.

Proof.

Let
\pi:\tilde{V}\rightarrow V
be a finite covering of degree prime to
p,
and
\tilde{V}^{\prime}\subset\tilde{V}
a dense Zariski open.
We claim that the map
H^{d}(U,\mathbb F_{p})\rightarrow H^{d}(\tilde{V}^{\prime}|_{U},\mathbb F_{p})

The complement
\tilde{Z}=\tilde{V}\backslash\tilde{V}^{\prime},

We may replace
\tilde{V}^{\prime}
by the preimage of

and assume that

Now consider the composite

The first map is nonzero by Proposition
3.2.14
, and the second map is injective, as in the proof of Proposition

,
hence the claim. As in the proof of Proposition

Write

for the normalization of

If

3.3.
Shimura Varieties

3.3.1
.

Let
(G,X)
be a Shimura datum
[
Deligne
, §1]
. Recall that this consists of a reductive group
G
over
\mathbb Q,
together
with a
G(\mathbb R)
-conjugacy class,
X,
of homomorphisms
h:\mathbb S=\text{\rm Res}_{\mathbb C/\mathbb R}\mathbb G_{m}\rightarrow G
satisfying certain conditions.
These imply, in particular, that
X
is a Hermitian domain, and that for any neat compact open
K\subset G(\mathbb A_{f}),

the quotient

\text{\rm Sh}_{K}(G,X)=G(\mathbb Q)\backslash X\times G(\mathbb A_{f})/K

has the structure of a complex algebraic variety. Here
\mathbb A_{f}
denote the finite adeles over
\mathbb Q.

For
h\in X,
define a
G
-valued cocharacter
\mu_{h}
over
\mathbb C,
as follows.
For a
\mathbb C
-algebra
R,
we have
R\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C=R\oplus c^{*}(R),
where
c
denotes complex conjugation.
The first factor gives an inclusion
R^{\times}\subset(R\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C)^{\times},
which gives a map
\mathbb G_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb S

over
\mathbb C,
and
\mu_{h}
is the composite of this map and
h.

The cocharacter
\mu_{h}
is miniscule
[
Deligne
, 1.2.2]
, and the axioms for a Shimura variety imply that
it is nontrivial.

3.3.2
.

Now consider the Dynkin diagram
\Delta(G)
of
G,
which is equipped with an action of
\text{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb}Q/\mathbb Q).

To simplify the discussion, we assume from now on that
G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}
is
\mathbb Q
-simple, so that the
\text{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb}Q/\mathbb Q)
-action
on
\Delta(G)
is
transitive
. The subgroup of
\text{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb}Q/\mathbb Q)
that acts on
\Delta(G)
trivially corresponds to a field
K_{\Delta}
that is either CM or a totally real field. The action of complex conjugation
c\in\text{\rm Gal}(K_{\Delta}/\mathbb Q)
is given by the

opposition involution
of the root system of
G.

Recall that this is given by
(-1)\circ w_{0},
where
w_{0}
is the longest element of the Weyl group
W_{G}.

Thus
K_{D}
is a totally real field exactly when
-1\in W_{G}.

Fix a maximal torus
T\subset G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}
and a set of positive roots
\Delta^{+}\subset X^{*}(T)
for
G.

The vertices of
\Delta(G)
correspond to the simple roots in
\Delta^{+}.

For
\alpha\in\Delta(G)
, let
\mu_{\alpha}\in X_{*}(T)
be the cocharacter
which takes the value
1
on
\alpha
and vanishes otherwise.
For a subset
R\subset\Delta(G)
, define
\mu_{R}\in X_{*}(T)
by
\mu_{R}:=\prod_{\alpha\in R}\mu_{\alpha}.

If we do not specify
\Delta^{+},
then the conjugacy class of
\mu_{R}
is still well defined, and we will denote it
by
[\mu_{R}].

Let
P_{R}\subset G
be the parabolic associated to
\mu_{R}.

Recall that
P_{R}
is characterized by the condition that its Lie algebra
\text{\rm Lie}P_{R}
is the sum of root spaces on which
\mu_{R}
is
non-negative
[
SGA3
, XXVI, 1.4]
. As for
\mu_{R},
if we do not specify
\Delta^{+}
then the conjugacy class of
P_{R}

is still well defined, and we denote it by
[P_{R}].

Since
\mu_{h}
is miniscule, it corresponds to a collection of vertices
\varSigma\subset\Delta(G),

with each component of
\Delta(G)
containing at most one element of
\varSigma.

Let
\tilde{\varSigma}=\varSigma\cup c(\varSigma).

Lemma 3.3.3
.

Let
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}
be in
[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}],

P_{\tilde{\varSigma}}
the associated
parabolic and
U_{\tilde{\varSigma}}\subset P_{\tilde{\varSigma}}
the unipotent radical of
P_{\tilde{\varSigma}}.

Then
U_{\tilde{\varSigma}}
is a central extension of additive groups

0\rightarrow Z(U_{\tilde{\varSigma}})\rightarrow U_{\tilde{\varSigma}}\rightarrow U_{\tilde{\varSigma}}/Z(U_{\tilde{\varSigma}})\rightarrow 0,

and
U_{\tilde{\varSigma}}=Z(U_{\tilde{\varSigma}})
if
c
fixes
\varSigma.

The cocharacter
w_{\tilde{\varSigma}}=\mu_{\varSigma}\mu_{c(\varSigma)}
acts with weight
2
on

Z(U_{\tilde{\varSigma}})
and weight
1
on
U_{\tilde{\varSigma}}/Z(U_{\tilde{\varSigma}}).

Proof.

Write
G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}_{\mathbb R}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}_{\mathbb R,i},
where each factor
G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}_{\mathbb R,i}
is absolutely simple.
For each
i
the Dynkin diagram of
G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}_{\mathbb R,i}
corresponds to a component
\Delta(G)_{i}
of
\Delta(G),
and it suffices to prove
the statement of the Lemma with
U_{\tilde{\varSigma},i}=U_{\tilde{\varSigma}}\cap G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}_{\mathbb C,i}
in place of
U_{\tilde{\varSigma}}.

We set
\varSigma_{i}=\varSigma\cap\Delta(G)_{i}
and
\tilde{\varSigma}_{i}=\tilde{\varSigma}\cap\Delta(G)_{i}.

The root spaces which appear in
\text{\rm Lie}U_{\tilde{\varSigma}}
are exactly those which correspond to roots whose expressions as a sum of simple roots in
\Delta^{+}
contain an element of
\tilde{\varSigma}.

If
\varSigma_{i}
is empty, then
U_{\tilde{\varSigma},i}=0,
and there is nothing to prove. Suppose that
\varSigma_{i}
is non-empty.

If
c
fixes
\varSigma_{i}
then
\tilde{\varSigma}_{i}=\varSigma_{i}
and
\mu_{\varSigma_{i}}
is nonzero on exactly one simple root in
\Delta(G)_{i}.

As
\mu_{h}
is miniscule, so is
\mu_{\varSigma_{i}},
so the root in
\varSigma_{i}
can appear in the expression for a root in
\Delta^{+}
with multiplicity at most
1.
This implies that
U_{\tilde{\varSigma},i}
is abelian, and
\mu_{\varSigma_{i}}
acts with weight
1
on
U_{\tilde{\varSigma},i},

so
\mu_{\varSigma_{i}}\mu_{c(\varSigma_{i})}=\mu_{\varSigma_{i}}^{2}
acts with weight
2.

Now suppose that
c
does not fix
\varSigma_{i}.
Then
\tilde{\varSigma}_{i}
has exactly two elements
and
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}
is nonzero on the two corresponding simple roots, which are exchanged by
c.

If
\alpha\in\varSigma_{i},
and
e_{\alpha}
is the corresponding root, then
e_{\alpha}
and
e_{c(\alpha)}
both appear in the
expression for the longest root in
\Delta^{+}
as a sum of simple roots. Using this, one sees that the root spaces appearing in

\text{\rm Lie}Z(U_{\tilde{\varSigma},i})

correspond to
\beta\in\Delta^{+}
whose expression as a sum of simple roots contains both
\alpha
and
c(\alpha),

and that
[U_{\tilde{\varSigma}_{i}},U_{\tilde{\varSigma}_{i}}]\subset Z(U_{\tilde{\varSigma}_{i}}).

Moreover the description of the root spaces appearing in the Lie algebras of
U_{\tilde{\varSigma},i}
and
Z(U_{\tilde{\varSigma},i})

implies the claim about the weights of
\mu_{\varSigma_{i}}\mu_{c(\varSigma_{i})}=\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}_{i}}.

∎

3.3.4
.

We will be interested in the following condition

(3.3.5)

The conjugacy class
[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}]
contains a cocharacter defined over
\mathbb Q.

Note that this condition implies that
[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}]
is fixed by
\text{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb}Q/\mathbb Q).

We can rephrase this condition in terms of the parabolic
P_{R}\subset G_{\mathbb C}
associated to
\mu_{R}.

Lemma 3.3.6
.

The conjugacy class
[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}]
contains a cocharacter defined over
\mathbb Q

if and only if
[P_{\tilde{\varSigma}}]
contains a parabolic defined over
\mathbb Q.

Proof.

If
[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}]
contains a cocharacter
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}
defined over
\mathbb Q,
then
the subspace of
\text{\rm Lie}G
on which
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}
is non-negative is the Lie algebra of a parabolic
in
[P_{\tilde{\varSigma}}],
which is defined over
\mathbb Q.

Conversely if
[P_{\tilde{\varSigma}}]
contains a parabolic
P_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}
defined over
\mathbb Q,
then

P_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}
is associated (as above) to some cocharacter
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}
(not necessarily defined over
\mathbb Q
)
in
[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}].
Let
U_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}\subset P_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}
denote the unipotent radical.
As
P_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}
is its own normalizer,
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}
is determined up to conjugation by points of
P_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}.

Hence the conjugacy class of
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}
as a
P_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}/Z_{G}
-valued cocharacter is defined over
\mathbb Q,

where
Z_{G}
denotes the center of
G.
On the other hand, the composite

\mathbb G_{m}\overset{\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}}{\rightarrow}P_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}/Z_{G}\rightarrow P_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}/U_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}Z_{G}

is central, hence defined over
\mathbb Q.
Now this composite can be lifted to a
P_{\tilde{\varSigma},\mathbb Q}/Z_{G}
-valued cocharacter defined over
\mathbb Q,

and any such lift is in
[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}]
, by
[
SGA3
, IX, Thm. 3.6]
.
∎

3.3.7
.

Somewhat more explicit conditions which guarantee that
3.3.5
holds are given by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3.8
.

Suppose that
G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}
is
\mathbb Q
-simple. Then
G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}=\text{\rm Res}_{F/\mathbb Q}G_{0}
where
F
is a totally real field, and
G_{0}
is an absolutely simple group over
F.

If

(1)

\tilde{\varSigma}
is
\text{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb}Q/\mathbb Q)
-stable, and

(2)

for some (and hence any, by (1)) embedding
F\rightarrow\mathbb R,
the
F
-rank of
G_{0}
is equal to its
\mathbb R
-rank,

then condition
3.3.5
holds.

Proof.

For the fact that
G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}
has the form
\text{\rm Res}_{F/\mathbb Q}G_{0}
see
[
Deligne
, 2.3.4(a)]
.
Now suppose that the conditions (1) and (2) are satisfied.
Fix an embedding
F\rightarrow\mathbb R,
so that
G_{0,\mathbb R}
is a factor of
G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}_{\mathbb R},

and corresponds to a connected component
\Delta(G)_{0}
of the Dynkin diagram of
G.

Let
\tilde{\varSigma}_{0}=\tilde{\varSigma}\cap\Delta(G)_{0},
and let
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}_{0}}
be
the corresponding
G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}_{\mathbb R,0}
-valued cocharacter.

By
[
AMRT
, §3.2]
, the conjugacy class of the parabolic subgroup of
G_{0,\mathbb R}
corresponding to
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}_{0}}

contains a parabolic defined over
\mathbb R,
and hence the conjugacy class

[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}_{0}}]
contains a cocharacter defined over
\mathbb R,
as in the proof of

3.3.6
. By (2), after conjugation by an element of
G_{0}(\mathbb R),
this cocharacter, which we again denote by

\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}_{0}},
factors through an
\mathbb R
-split torus which is defined over
F,
and
F
-split.
Thus we have a map of
F
-groups
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}_{0}}:\mathbb G_{m}\rightarrow G_{0}.

By definition of the restriction of scalars,
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}_{0}}
induces
a map
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}^{\prime}:\mathbb G_{m}\rightarrow G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}
over
\mathbb Q.

To see that
\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}^{\prime}\in[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}],
note that, by (1),
[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}]
is
\text{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb}Q/\mathbb Q)
-stable.
Thus it suffices to check that the projections of
[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}]
and
[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}^{\prime}]
onto
G_{0}
are equal.
But these are both equal to
[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}_{0}}].

∎

3.3.9
.

Let us explain how to apply Lemma
3.3.8
in examples.
First, the condition (1) implies that

X
has the form
X_{0}^{m}
for some irreducible Hermitian symmetric domain
X_{0}.
When
X_{0}
is not of type
D_{n}^{\mathbb H},

then (1) is actually equivalent to this condition. In the case of type
D_{n}^{\mathbb H},
there are two conjugacy classes of
cocharacters of
G_{0,\mathbb R},
which are exchanged by an outer isomorphism of
G_{0},
and give rise to isomorphic (via the outer isomorphism)
Hermitian symmetric domains. If the outer isomorphism is induced by complex conjugation, which happens when
n
is odd, then
X

having the form
X_{0}^{m}
still implies (1), but if
n
is even, then the condition is stronger.

The condition (2) can also be made more explicit in many cases: If
G
is of type
B
or
C
or
D_{n}
with
n
even, then
G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}_{\mathbb R}
is split,
and (2) means that
G_{0}
is a split group, or (in this context) that
G
is quasi-split. If
G
is of type
A_{n}
then (2) means that
G_{0}

is the adjoint group of a unitary group over
F
- that is one associated to a Hermitian form on a vector space over a quadratic CM extension of
F

- and not just an inner form of such a group. In particular, when
n=1
this covers the case of Hilbert modular varieties.

The following lemma gives a way of constructing many examples when
X
is irreducible.

Corollary 3.3.10
.

For any irreducible Hermitian domain
X,
and any quadratic imaginary extension
L/\mathbb Q,

there exists a Shimura datum
(G,X)
such that
G
is an absolutely simple group which splits over
L,
and

3.3.5
holds.

Proof.

Let
G_{\mathbb R}
be the absolutely simple reductive
\mathbb R
-group associated to
X,
and
G_{0,\mathbb R}
its quasi-split inner form.
Consider the Dynkin diagram of
G_{0,\mathbb R}
with its action of
\text{\rm Gal}(\mathbb C/\mathbb R)\simeq\text{\rm Gal}(L/\mathbb Q).
This corresponds to a quasi-split group

G_{0}
over
\mathbb Q,
which splits over
L.
Now by
[
PrasadRapinchuk
, Thm. 1]
, there exists an inner form
G
of
G_{0},
which
becomes isomorphic to
G_{\mathbb R}
over
\mathbb R
(as an inner form of
G_{0,\mathbb R}
), and whose
\mathbb Q
-rank is equal to the
\mathbb R
-rank of

G_{0,\mathbb R}.
(Note that in
loc. cit.
the number of
i
satisfying the condition (ii) with
v=\infty
is precisely the split rank of
G_{\mathbb R}.
).
The corollary now follows from Lemma
3.3.8
.
∎

3.3.11
.

We will now apply the theory of toroidal compactifications of Shimura varieties to our situation. We refer the reader
to the book of Ash-Mumford-Rapoport-Tai
[
AMRT
]
, and Pink’s thesis
[
Pinkthesis
]
; see also Madapusi-Pera’s paper

[
Keerthi
, §2]
for a convenient summary.
We suppose from now on that the condition
3.3.5
holds.

Let
P\in[P_{\tilde{\varSigma}}]
be defined over
\mathbb Q,
and let
U\subset P
be its unipotent radical,
and
Z\subset U
the center of of
U.
Abusing notation slightly, write
U(\mathbb Z)=U(\mathbb Q)\cap K,

Z(\mathbb Z)=Z(\mathbb Q)\cap K.

Let
\xi_{K}=U(\mathbb Z)\backslash U(\mathbb R)Z(\mathbb C),
and
C_{K}=(Z(\mathbb C)/Z(\mathbb Z))\backslash\xi_{K}.

Then
C_{K}
is an abelian scheme and
\xi_{K}\rightarrow C_{K}

is a torus bundle. Fix a fan
\Sigma,
in
X^{*}(Z(\mathbb C)/Z(\mathbb Z))_{\mathbb Q}
so that the resulting torus embedding is smooth with a normal crossings
divisor at the boundary. As in Lemma
3.2.9
, we obtain a smooth torus embedding over
C_{K},

\xi_{K}(\Sigma)\rightarrow C_{K}

whose boundary
D_{K}(\Sigma)
is a normal crossings divisor, see
[
Pinkthesis
, §3]
,
[
Keerthi
, 2.1.7]
.

Note that in the constructions of
loc. cit.
one gets a smooth torus embedding over a base which is itself an abelian scheme over a Shimura variety.
That is because those constructions start with the preimage under
P\rightarrow P/U
of a factor of the reductive group
P/U.
However, for
our specific choice of parabolic
P_{\tilde{\varSigma}}
the relevant factor of
P/U
is a torus, and the Shimura variety is
0
-dimensional.
In the notation of
[
Pinkthesis
, §4.7]
, our parabolic
P
is what Pink denotes by
Q,
and the preimage mentioned above
is denoted by
P_{1}.
These groups are denoted by
P
and
Q_{P}
in
[
Keerthi
, §2]
.

3.3.12
.

By an
analytic neighborhood of
D_{K}(\Sigma)
we mean an analytic open subset

V_{K}\subset\xi_{K}(\Sigma)(\mathbb C),
containing
D_{K}(\Sigma).
We write
V_{K}^{*}=V_{K}\backslash D_{K}(\Sigma).

Write
\text{\rm Sh}_{K}=\text{\rm Sh}_{K}(G,X).
Fix a connected component
X^{+}\subset X,
and let
\text{\rm Sh}_{K}^{+}\subset\text{\rm Sh}_{K}
be the corresponding connected
component of
\text{\rm Sh}_{K}^{+}.
Then
\text{\rm Sh}^{+}_{K}=\Gamma\backslash X^{+},
where
\Gamma=K\cap G(\mathbb Q)_{+},
and
G(\mathbb Q)_{+}\subset G(\mathbb Q)

is the subgroup mapping to the connected component of the identity in
G^{{\text{\rm ad}}}(\mathbb R)

[
Deligne
, 2.1.2]
. In particular, for any choice of base point,
this allows us to identify the fundamental group of
\text{\rm Sh}_{K}^{+}
with
\Gamma.

For any smooth
\mathbb C
-scheme
Y,
by an
ncd compactification
of
Y
we mean a dense embedding
Y\subset\bar{Y}

into a proper smooth
\mathbb C
-scheme
\bar{Y},
such that
\bar{Y}\backslash Y
is a normal crossings divisor.
We collect the results we need in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3.13
.

There exists an ncd compactification
\text{\rm Sh}_{K}(\Sigma)^{+}
of
\text{\rm Sh}_{K}
and
an analytic neighborhood
V_{K}
of
D_{K}(\Sigma)
such that

(1)

The inclusion
V_{K}^{*}\rightarrow\xi_{K}(\mathbb C)
induces an isomorphism of topological fundamental groups.

(2)

There is an étale map of complex analytic spaces

\pi_{K}:V_{K}\rightarrow\text{\rm Sh}_{K}(\Sigma)^{+}

such that
\pi_{K}^{-1}(\text{\rm Sh}_{K}(\Sigma)^{+}\backslash\text{\rm Sh}_{K}^{+})=D_{K}(\Sigma),

and
\pi_{K}:V_{K}^{*}\rightarrow\text{\rm Sh}_{K}^{+}
induces the natural map
U(\mathbb Z)\hookrightarrow\Gamma
on fundamental groups.

Proof.

By
[
Pinkthesis
, 4.11]
,
[
Keerthi
, 2.1.6]
there is an open immersion of complex analytic spaces

X^{+}\rightarrow U(\mathbb R)Z(\mathbb C).
Now set
V_{K}^{*}=U(\mathbb Z)\backslash X^{+},
then there is an induced open immersion
V_{K}^{*}\rightarrow\xi_{K}(\mathbb C)
(see
[
Pinkthesis
, 6.10]
,

[
Keerthi
, 2.1.13]
) which induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups, by construction.
By
[
Pinkthesis
, 6.13]
,
[
Keerthi
, 2.1.22]
, the latter open immersion extends to an analytic neighborhood
V_{K}
of
D_{K}(\Sigma).

The existence of
\text{\rm Sh}_{K}(\Sigma)^{+}
and the map
\pi_{K}
with the properties in (2) is the main result of
[
Pinkthesis
, §6]
, see also

[
Keerthi
, 2.1.26]
. We remark that the ncd compactification
\text{\rm Sh}_{K}(\Sigma)^{+},
depends on more choices than just
\Sigma,

but as these will play no role for us, we omit them from the notation.
∎

3.3.14
.

We continue to assume that condition
3.3.5
holds. Suppose that
G
admits a reductive model
G_{\mathbb Z_{p}}
over
\mathbb Z_{p}.

As the scheme of parabolic subgroups is projective

[
SGA3
, XXVI, Cor. 3.5]
,
P
extends to a parabolic subgroup
P_{\mathbb Z_{p}}\subset G_{\mathbb Z_{p}}.
Denote by
U_{\mathbb Z_{p}}\subset P_{\mathbb Z_{p}}
the unipotent
radical. We will sometimes write
G,

P
and
U
for
G_{\mathbb Z_{p}},

P_{\mathbb Z_{p}}
and
U_{\mathbb Z_{p}}
if this causes no confusion.

Lemma 3.3.15
.

The group
U_{\mathbb Z_{p}}(\mathbb Z_{p})
is a central extension of finitely generated, free abelian pro-
p
groups.
The map
U(\mathbb Z_{p})\rightarrow U(\mathbb F_{p})
is surjective, and
U(\mathbb F_{p})
is a reduction of
U(\mathbb Z_{p})
mod
p.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.3.6
, one sees that
P_{\mathbb Z_{p}}\subset G_{\mathbb Z_{p}}
corresponds to a cocharacter

\mu_{\mathbb Z_{p}}
in
[\mu_{\tilde{\varSigma}}],
defined over
\mathbb Z_{p}.
As
U_{\mathbb Z_{p}}
is unipotent, it is an iterated extension of additive groups.
It then follows by Lemma
3.3.3
that
U_{\mathbb Z_{p}}
is a central extension of
additive groups, so that
U(\mathbb Z_{p})
is a central extension of finitely generated, free, abelian pro-
p
groups.
As
U_{\mathbb Z_{p}}
is smooth,
U(\mathbb Z_{p})\rightarrow U(\mathbb F_{p})
is surjective.

To check that
U(\mathbb F_{p})
is a reduction of
U(\mathbb Z_{p})
mod
p,
we have to check that
U(\mathbb F_{p})
is a Heisenberg group.
This condition is vacuous if
U
is abelian. If
U
is not abelian, then
by Lemma
3.3.3
,
\mu_{\mathbb Z_{p}}
has weight
2
on
Z(U_{\mathbb Z_{p}})
and weight
1
on
U_{\mathbb Z_{p}}/Z(U_{\mathbb Z_{P}}).

Hence
\mathbb F_{p}^{\times}
acting via
\mu_{\mathbb Z_{p}}
has weight
2
on
Z(U_{\mathbb Z_{p}})(\mathbb F_{p})
and weight
1
on
U_{\mathbb Z_{p}}/Z(U_{\mathbb Z_{p}})(\mathbb F_{p}).

Thus
U(\mathbb F_{p})
is a Heisenberg group by Lemma
3.1.2
.
∎

3.3.16
.

Now suppose that
K=K_{p}K^{p},
with
K_{p}=G(\mathbb Z_{p}),
and
K^{p}\subset G(\mathbb A_{f}^{p})
compact open.
Let
K^{1}_{p}=\text{\rm ker}\,(G(\mathbb Z_{p})\rightarrow G(\mathbb F_{p})),
and
K^{1}=K^{1}_{p}K^{p}.
As above, let
\Gamma=K\cap G(\mathbb Q)_{+},

and set
\Gamma_{1}=K^{1}\cap G(\mathbb Q)_{+}.
A covering,
\Gamma_{1}\backslash X^{+}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash X^{+},

with
\Gamma,\Gamma_{1}
of the above form is called a
principal
p
-covering
.

Theorem 3.3.17
.

Assume that
3.3.5
holds, and that
G
extends to a reductive group scheme over

\mathbb Z_{p}.

If
\varSigma\neq c(\varSigma)
then we assume that
p>\text{\rm dim}X+\frac{1}{2}(\text{\rm dim}Z+1),
and that
p
is a prime of unramified
good reduction for
\xi_{K}.

Then

{\text{\rm ed}}(\Gamma_{1}\backslash X^{+}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash X^{+};p)=\text{\rm dim}X.

Proof.

Let
V_{K}
be an analytic neighborhood as in Prop.
3.3.13
, and fix a base point
\bar{s}\in V_{K}.

By Proposition
3.3.13
(1), we may identify
\pi_{1}(V^{*}_{K},\bar{s})
with
\pi_{1}(\xi_{K}(\mathbb C),\bar{s})\simeq U(\mathbb Z).

By the strong approximation for unipotent groups, the pro-
p
completion of
U(\mathbb Z)
is
U(\mathbb Z_{p}).

The pullback of
\Gamma_{1}\backslash X^{+}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash X^{+}
by the map
\pi_{K}
in Proposition
3.3.13
(2)
is a covering, one of whose components
V^{*}_{K^{1}}\rightarrow V_{K}^{*},
corresponds to the kernel of the composite

U(\mathbb Z)\rightarrow\Gamma\rightarrow K\rightarrow G(\mathbb Z_{p})\rightarrow G(\mathbb F_{p}).

That is, it is the kernel of
U(\mathbb Z)\rightarrow U(\mathbb F_{p}).

Using the strong approximation for unipotent groups, as above, it follows that
V^{*}_{K^{1}}\rightarrow V^{*}_{K}
is a covering with group
U(\mathbb F_{p}),

and is the restriction of a covering of
schemes

\xi_{K^{1}}\rightarrow\xi_{K}
with group
U(\mathbb F_{p}).

Let
\xi_{K^{1}}(\Sigma)
be the normalization of
\xi_{K}(\Sigma)
in
\xi_{K^{1}},
and set
V_{K^{1}}=\xi_{K^{1}}(\Sigma)^{{\text{\rm an}}}|_{V_{K}}.

Next suppose
B\subset\text{\rm Sh}^{+}_{K}=\Gamma\backslash X^{+}
is a Zariski closed subset. Let
\bar{B}
be its Zariski closure in

\text{\rm Sh}^{+}_{K}(\Sigma).
Then
\pi_{K}^{-1}(\bar{B})
is Zariski closed in
V_{K}.
It follows that

{\text{\rm ed}}(\Gamma_{1}\backslash X^{+}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash X^{+};p)\geq{\text{\rm ed}}(V_{K^{1}}\rightarrow V_{K};p).

Here we are using the notion of
p
-essential dimension for analytic spaces introduced in
3.2.15
.

As
U(\mathbb F_{p})
is a mod
p
reduction of
U(\mathbb Z_{p})
by Lemma
3.3.15
,
the theorem follows from Corollary
3.2.17
if
c(\varSigma)\neq\varSigma
and from Corollary
3.2.18

if
c(\varSigma)=\varSigma.

∎

Corollary 3.3.18
.

With the assumptions of Theorem
3.3.17
, suppose that
(G,X)
is of Hodge type.
Then
p
is a prime of unramified good reduction for
\xi_{K},
and the conclusion of the theorem holds without this assumption.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the main result of Madapusi-Pera
[
Keerthi
]
.
∎

Corollary 3.3.19
.

Let
X
be an irreducible symmetric domain, and
let
L/\mathbb Q
be a quadratic extension.
Then there exists a Shimura datum
(G,X)
with
G
an absolutely simple group which splits over
L,
such that
for any principal
p
-covering
\Gamma_{1}\backslash X^{+}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash X^{+},
we have

{\text{\rm ed}}(\Gamma_{1}\backslash X^{+}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash X^{+};p)=\text{\rm dim}X,

provided
p
satisfies the following conditions if
X
is not a tube domain:

•

If
X
is of classical type, then
p>\frac{3}{2}\text{\rm dim}X.

•

If
X
is of type
E_{6}
, then
p
is sufficiently large.

Proof.

Apply Corollary
3.3.10
, to obtain a Shimura datum
(G,X)
such that
G
is an absolutely simple group which splits over
L,
and such that
3.3.5
holds. Our definition of principal
p
-coverings already assumes that
G
admits a reductive model over
\mathbb Z_{p},
so we assume this from now on.

If
X
is of tube type (that is
c(\varSigma)=\varSigma
), or
X
is of type
E_{6},
then the corollary follows from Theorem
3.3.17
.

Suppose
X
is not a tube domain and is of classical type. Then
(G,X)
is of abelian type
[
Deligne
, 2.3.10]
. Recall that this means
(since
G
is adjoint), that there is a morphism of Shimura data
(G^{\prime},X^{\prime})\rightarrow(G,X),
induced by a
central isogeny
G^{\prime}\rightarrow G,
with
(G^{\prime},X^{\prime})
of Hodge type. As in
[
Kisin
, 3.4.13]
, we can assume that
G^{\prime}
again has a reductive
model over
\mathbb Z_{p}.

Now we apply
3.3.18
to a principal
p
-covering

\Gamma_{1}^{\prime}\backslash X^{+}\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash X^{+},

coming from the group
G^{\prime}.

We obtain the
p
-incompressibility of this covering for
p>\text{\rm dim}X+\frac{1}{2}(\text{\rm dim}Z+1).

As

\text{\rm dim}X=\frac{1}{2}(\text{\rm dim}U+\text{\rm dim}Z)=\frac{1}{2}\text{\rm dim}(U/Z)+\text{\rm dim}Z,

we have

\text{\rm dim}X+\frac{1}{2}(\text{\rm dim}Z+1)=\text{\rm dim}X+\frac{1}{2}(\text{\rm dim}X-\frac{1}{2}(\text{\rm dim}U/Z)+1)\leq\frac{3}{2}\text{\rm dim}X.

Now a calculation as in
[
FKW
, 4.3.12]
shows that the kernel and cokernel of

\Gamma_{1}^{\prime}\backslash\Gamma^{\prime}\rightarrow\Gamma_{1}\backslash\Gamma
are finite groups of order prime to
p.

The result now follows by
[
FKW
, 2.2.7]
.
∎

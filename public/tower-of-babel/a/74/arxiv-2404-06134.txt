The turnpike property for high-dimensional interacting agent systems in discrete time
Martin Gugat, Michael Herty, Jiehong Liu, Chiara Segala †

Abstract
We investigate the interior turnpike phenomenon for discrete-time multi-agent optimal control problems. While for continuous systems the turnpike property has been established, we focus here on first-order discretizations of such systems. It is shown that the resulting time-discrete system inherits the turnpike property with estimates of the same type as in the continuous case. In particular, we prove that the discrete time optimal control problem is strictly dissipative and the cheap control assumption holds.



Keywords:

turnpike property, time-discrete systems, multi-agent systems, optimal control

The collective behavior of possibly actively interacting agents, also called active particle systems,

Most of

Those models have been applied to several fields, such as biology, engineering, economy, and sociology, see e.g.
[

]
[

]
[

]

]

Typically, the action of the control is used as an exterior influence to enforce the emergence of the desired asymptotic target behavior of the agents. The control of high-dimensional, interacting agents can be achieved in several ways, e.g. using Riccati-based approaches
[
27
,
1
]
, moment-driven control
[
2
]
, or model predictive control approaches
[
5
,
4
,
31
]
. Those approaches have been followed to reduce the possible high computational costs.

Here, as in
[
25
]
, we consider turnpike controls for such agent systems as an effective strategy to devise controls for high-dimensional systems.
The turnpike phenomenon describes the fact that,
in an interior subinterval of the time interval,
the dynamic control can be
approximated to arbitrarily precision
by the control of the (cheaper) static problem for a sufficiently large time horizon, and this reduces the computational effort as shown e.g. in
[
6
,
30
,
35
]
. The turnpike property has also been used to show that model-predictive control strategy converges, see e.g.
[
20
,
22
,
21
]
.
While in
[
25
]
the turnpike property of continuous agent systems has been investigated, we consider now the discrete-time dynamics. For discrete-time control problems, the turnpike property has been established in
[
36
,
34
,
14
,
18
,
23
]
.
Contrary to those approaches, we exploit the particular structure of interacting agent systems to derive the turnpike property with interior decay
[
24
,
26
]
, or the interval turnpike property
[
19
]
.

The general continuous trajectories
\psi_{k}=\psi_{k}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
of
k=1,\dots,N
interacting agents in the time interval
[t_{0},T]
are given by the following system

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\psi_{k}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{N}P(\psi_{k},\psi_{l})(\psi_{l}-\psi_{k})\right)+u_{k},\quad\psi_{k}(t_{0})=\psi_{k}^{0}.

(1)

The data
\psi_{k}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the initial state of agent
k
and
P(\cdot,\cdot)
is a nonlinear interaction kernel representing attraction and/or repulsive forces, see e.g.
[
8
,
15
]
for examples. The control
u_{k}=u_{k}(t)
acts on each agent individually in order to minimize e.g. a tracking-type cost towards a desired state
\bar{\psi}
and Tykhonov regularization for some
\gamma>0:

J_{N}(u)=\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\psi_{k}-\bar{\psi}\|^{2}+\gamma\|u_{k}\|^{2}\right)dt.

(2)

Here and in the following we denote by
\|\cdot\|
the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}.
The turnpike properties of the problem (
1
)–(
2
) have been studied in
[
25
]
showing that they are independent of the number of agents
N.
In this paper, we consider the corresponding time-discrete problem, see Section
2
for a detailed description. In particular, we show that the turnpike property is independent of the discretization time-step
h
. Further, the time discrete system allows for a turnpike with interior decay independent of the number of agents
N
. The proof of the interior turnpike property requires a dynamic programming principle, a strict dissipativity inequality, and the existence of a cheap control. Those results are established for the aforementioned system in Section
3.1
and Section
3.2
, respectively. Furthermore, the consistency in the limit for the vanishing discretization time-step is discussed in Section
4
. Finally, numerical examples are presented in Section
5
.

2
The discrete-time optimal control problem

Consider a discretization
t_{0}<t_{1}<...<t_{M}=T
of the time interval
[t_{0},T]
, where
t_{i+1}-t_{i}=h
for
i=0,..,M-1
and for the stepsize
we have
h=\frac{T-t_{0}}{M}.
The system of ordinary differential equations (
1
) is discretized using an explicit Euler method. The control of agent
k
is piecewise constant on
t_{i+1}-t_{i}
and denoted by
u^{i}_{k}=u_{k}
. Furthermore, let

\psi^{i}_{k}=\psi_{k}(t_{i})
and let the initial data be given by
\psi_{k}(t_{0})
. The discrete system is then given by

\displaystyle\psi_{k}^{i+1}=\psi_{k}^{i}+hf(\psi_{k}^{i},u_{k}^{i}),\qquad i=0,\dots,M-1,\;k=1,\dots,N,

(3)

\displaystyle\psi_{k}^{0}=\psi_{k}(t_{0}).

for

f(\psi_{k}^{i},u_{k}^{i})=\frac{1}{N}{\sum_{l=1}^{N}P(\psi_{k}^{i},\psi_{l}^{i})(\psi_{l}^{i}-\psi_{k}^{i})}+u_{k}^{i}.

(4)

For the interaction kernel
P:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
we assume

\displaystyle P\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),\;P(0,0)=0,\;\|P\|_{\infty}\leq 0.

(5)

Furthermore, we denote by
\psi^{i}=(\psi^{i}_{1},\psi^{i}_{2},\dots,\psi^{i}_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}

the state of all agents at time
t_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,M-1
, by
\psi=\left(\psi^{i}_{k}\right)_{i=0,\dots,M-1,k=1,\dots,N}
and similarly for
u.
We define the following norm of the state
\psi^{i}
at the discrete time
t_{i}
as

\displaystyle\|\psi^{i}\|_{N}^{2}:=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\|\psi_{k}^{i}\|^{2}.

(6)

The cost functional (
2
) is discretized using a first-order integration method leading to the time-discrete optimal control problem

\displaystyle Q(t_{0},T,h,N,\psi^{0})

\displaystyle:\,\min_{u}J_{N}^{(h,t_{0},T)}(\psi,u),

(7)

\displaystyle\text{ for}\kern 5.0ptJ_{N}^{(h,t_{0},T)}(\psi,u)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\,\frac{h}{N}\left(\|\psi^{i}-\bar{\psi}\|^{2}_{N}+\gamma\|u^{i}\|^{2}_{N}\right).

The problem (
7
) is a parametric optimization problem with parameters
t_{0},T
and discretization size
h.
In the following we assume that those parameters are chosen s.t. there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
with

M=\frac{T-t_{0}}{h}.

(8)

The optimal value of the time-discrete optimal control problem is denoted by

\displaystyle v_{N}^{(h,t_{0},T)}(\psi^{0}):=\min_{u}J_{N}^{h,t_{0},T}(\psi,u).

(9)

and the optimal state and control are denoted by
\hat{\psi}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N\times d}
and
\hat{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N\times d},
respectively. Further, we introduce the running cost

\displaystyle g(\psi^{i},u^{i}):=\frac{1}{N}\left(\|\psi^{i}-\bar{\psi}\|^{2}_{N}+\gamma\|u^{i}\|_{N}^{2}\right).

(10)

Next, we establish the dynamic programming principle for the discrete in-time
optimal control problem.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(\hat{\psi},\hat{u})
be the optimal state-control pair for the optimal control problem
Q(t_{0},t_{L},h,N,\psi^{0})
for some
t_{0}<t_{L}<T.

Then,
\hat{\psi}\mid_{(a,L)}=(\hat{\psi}^{a},\hat{\psi}^{a+1},\dots,\hat{\psi}^{L})
and
\hat{u}\mid_{(a,L)}=(\hat{u}^{a},\hat{u}^{a+1},\dots,\hat{u}^{L})
is the optimal solution to the problem
Q(t_{a},t_{L},h,N,\hat{\psi}^{a})
for any
t_{0}<t_{a}<t_{L}
.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction. Note that
\psi^{i+1}_{k}
is uniquely determined for given state
\psi^{i}_{k}
and control
u^{i}_{k}
by equation (
3
). Assume that
\hat{\psi}\mid_{(a,L)}
and
\hat{u}\mid_{(a,L)}
is not the optimal solution to the problem
Q(t_{a},t_{L},h,N,\hat{\psi}^{a})
and denote the optimal state and control by

(\overline{\psi},\overline{u})
where
\overline{\psi}=(\overline{\psi}^{0},\overline{\psi}^{1},\dots,\overline{\psi}^{L-a})
and
\overline{u}=(\overline{u}^{0},\overline{u}^{1},\dots,\overline{u}^{L-a})
for
\overline{\psi}^{0}=\hat{\psi}^{a}
. By assumption,
(\overline{\psi},\overline{u})\not=(\hat{\psi}\mid_{(a,L)},\hat{u}\mid_{(a,L)})
. Consider the control
(\hat{u}\mid_{(0,a-1)},\overline{u})
and the corresponding state
(\hat{\psi}\mid_{(0,a-1)},\overline{\psi})
that has a smaller objective value for the problem
Q(t_{0},t_{L},h,N,\psi^{0})
. This contradicts the optimality of
(\hat{\psi},\hat{u})
.
∎

We introduce now the

static control problem
. The static state of agent
k=1,\dots,N
is denoted by
\psi^{(\sigma)}_{k}
and the control by
u_{k}^{(\sigma)}
. The problem reads

\min\limits_{\left(u_{1}^{(\sigma)},\dots,u_{N}^{(\sigma)}\right)}\frac{1}{N}\left({\|\psi^{(\sigma)}-\bar{\psi}\|^{2}_{N}+\gamma\|u^{(\sigma)}\|_{N}^{2}}\right),

(11)

subject to the stationary dynamics

\frac{1}{N}\,\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left(P(\psi_{k}^{(\sigma)},\psi_{l}^{(\sigma)})(\psi_{l}^{(\sigma)}-\psi_{k}^{(\sigma)})+u_{k}^{(\sigma)}\right)=0.

(12)

Due to the particular structure of the agent-based dynamics, the optimal
solution
(\psi_{k}^{*,(\sigma)},u^{*,(\sigma)}_{k})_{k=1}^{N}
to (
11
)–(
12
) is given by

\displaystyle\psi_{k}^{*,(\sigma)}=\bar{\psi}\ \mbox{ and }\ u^{*,(\sigma)}_{k}=0.

(13)

Furthermore, if we consider the dynamics (
3
), the initial condition
\psi_{k}^{0}=\psi_{k}^{*,(\sigma)}
and the control
u_{k}^{i}=u^{*,(\sigma)}_{k}
for
k=1,\dots,N
, then we obtain
\psi_{k}^{i}=\psi_{k}^{*,(\sigma)}
for
k=1,\dots,N
and
i\geq 1
.

In the following, we establish the turnpike property with interior decay in the sense of the Definition
2.1
. We refer to
[
25
]
for the continuous-time formulation. The turnpike property formalizes that an optimal solution of the
dynamic
optimal control problem is close to the optimal solution of the corresponding
static
problem.

Definition 2.1
.

The optimal control problem
Q(t_{0},T,h,N,\psi^{0})

defined in (
7
) has the turnpike property with interior decay, if there exist constants
\tilde{C}_{1}>0
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
and a monotone increasing non-negative function
\alpha
with
\alpha(0)=0
, such that for all
T>t_{0}
and
M
given by equation (
8
) the following
inequality holds true:

\sum_{i=\left\lfloor(1-\lambda)M\right\rfloor}^{M-1}\;h\;\alpha\left(\|{\psi}^{i}-\psi^{(\sigma)}\|_{N}+\|{u}^{i}-u^{(\sigma)}\|_{N}\right)\leq\tilde{C}_{1}

(14)

Note that the constant
\tilde{C_{1}}
can possibly depend on
h.
This dependence will be studied in Section
4
.

Our main result is the following theorem. It will be proven in the subsequent sections establishing first a dissipativity inequality and then showing that the problem is cheaply controllable. The final proof of the following theorem is deferred to Section
3.3
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Under assumption (
5
), the optimal solution to
Q(t_{0},T,h,N,\psi^{0})
has the turnpike property with interior decay in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

3
The turnpike property with interior decay

3.1
The strict dissipativity property

Dissipativity of the cost is crucial to obtain the turnpike property. The dissipativity has been introduced in
[
32
]
for the continuous problem (
1
)–(
2
). Applied to the time discrete agent system
and optimal control problem
Q(t_{0},T,h,N,\psi^{0})
, it reads as follows:

Definition 3.1
.

The optimal control problem
Q(t_{0},T,h,N,\psi^{0})
is called strictly dissipative with respect to the supply rate function
\omega(\psi^{i},u^{i})
, if there exists a bounded storage function
S:\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\to\mathbb{R}
and a monotone increasing continuous function
\alpha:\left[0,\infty\right)\to\left[0,\infty\right)
with
\alpha(0)=0
such that for all
(\psi,u)
fulfilling (
3
) holds

S(\psi^{i})+h\,\omega(\psi^{i},u^{i})\geq S(\psi^{i+1})+h\,\alpha\left(\|\psi^{i}-\psi^{(\sigma)}\|_{N}+\|u^{i}-u^{(\sigma)}\|_{N}\right).

(15)

Lemma 3.1
.

The discrete time optimal control problem
Q(t_{0},T,h,N,\psi^{0})
for
\gamma
is strictly dissipative with
\omega(\psi^{i},u^{i})=g(\psi^{i},u^{i})-g(\psi^{(\sigma)},u^{(\sigma)})
where
g
is as defined in (
10
),
S=0
and
\alpha(x)=\frac{\gamma}{2\;N}x^{2}.

Proof.

Note that the optimal stationary state and control
(\psi^{(\sigma)},u^{(\sigma)})
fulfils
g(\psi^{(\sigma)},u^{(\sigma)})=0
due to equation (
13
). By the definition of
\alpha
and norm (
6
) we have

\displaystyle\alpha\left(\|\psi^{i}-\psi^{(\sigma)}\|_{N}+\|u^{i}-u^{(\sigma)}\|_{N}\right)\leq\frac{\gamma}{N}\left(\|\psi^{i}-\psi^{(\sigma)}\|^{2}_{N}+\|u^{i}-u^{(\sigma)}\|^{2}_{N}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\left(\|\psi^{i}_{k}-\psi_{k}^{(\sigma)}\|^{2}_{N}+\|u^{i}_{k}-u_{k}^{(\sigma)}\|^{2}\right)\leq\frac{\gamma}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\left(\|\psi^{i}_{k}-\bar{\psi}\|^{2}_{N}+\|u^{i}_{k}\|^{2}\right)=g(\psi^{i},u^{i})-g(\psi^{(\sigma)},u^{(\sigma)}).

∎

3.2
The cheap control condition

The turnpike property
2.1
asserts that the solution to the dynamic optimal control problem is closed to the stationary optimal control problem for a large time interval. The cheap control assumption defined below (
3.2
), introduces a bound depending on the distance between the initial state and the steady state for the discrete in time problem.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that the optimal control problem (
7
) satisfies
the cheap control condition
if there exist constants
C_{0}>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}\geq 0
such that for all initial times
t_{0}
and all initial states
\psi^{0}
and for all terminal times
T>t_{0}
the inequality

v_{N}^{h,t_{0},T}(\psi^{0})\leq C_{0}\,\alpha(\|\psi^{0}-\psi^{(\sigma)}\|_{N})+\varepsilon_{0}+S(\psi^{M})-S(\psi^{0}).

(16)

where
v_{N}^{h,t_{0},T}(\psi^{0})
is the optimal value of the control problem (
9
) and
\psi^{M}
is the optimal state at time
T
given by equation (
3
) for
M
given by equation (
8
).

Also, here the constant
C_{0}
can depend on
h
and its dependence is discussed in Section
4
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
P
fulfils (
5
). The optimal control problem
Q(t_{0},T,h,N,\psi^{0})
is cheaply controllable in the sense of Definition
3.2
for
\varepsilon_{0}=0
,
\alpha(x)=\frac{\gamma}{2N}x^{2}
and
S=0
as in Lemma (
3.1
).

Proof.

Due to the choice of
\alpha,S
and
\varepsilon_{0}
we only need to show, that there exists a constant
\tilde{C_{0}}
such that for all initial times
t_{0}
and for all terminal times
T>t_{0}
and all initial states
\psi^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
we have the inequality

v_{N}^{h,t_{0},T}(\psi^{0})\leq\tilde{C_{0}}\,\frac{\gamma}{2}\frac{1}{N}\left(\|\psi^{0}-\psi^{(\sigma)}\|_{N}\right)^{2}.

(17)

To prove inequality (
17
) we consider the same stabilizing feedback law for controls in
[
25
]
that leads to exponential decay of
g(\psi^{i},u^{i})
given by equation (
10
). Let
h=\frac{T-t_{0}}{M}
and let
\frac{1}{h}\geq\beta>0
be given and define for all

k=1,..,N,
and
i=0,...,M

u_{k}^{i}=\beta(\bar{\psi}-\psi_{k}^{i})-\frac{1}{N}{\sum_{l=1}^{N}P(\psi_{k}^{i},\psi_{l}^{i})(\psi_{l}^{i}-\psi_{k}^{i})}.

(18)

The corresponding state
\psi^{i}
for any initial state
\psi^{0}
at time
t_{0}
is then given by the dynamics (
3
), i.e.,

\psi_{k}^{i+1}=\psi_{k}^{i}+h\,\beta\;\left(\bar{\psi}-\psi_{k}^{i}\right).

(19)

This implies

\psi_{k}^{i}=(1-h\beta)^{i}\psi_{k}^{0}+\sum_{l=0}^{i-1}(1-h\beta)^{l}h\beta\;\bar{\psi}.

Since by definition of
\beta
we have
h\;\beta<1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\psi_{k}^{i}-\bar{\psi}\|=\|(1-h\beta)^{i}\psi_{k}^{0}+[\sum_{l=0}^{i-1}(1-h\beta)^{l}h\beta-1]\bar{\psi}\|=(1-h\beta)^{i}\|\psi_{k}^{0}-\bar{\psi}\|.

The control
u^{i}_{k}
is estimated by

\displaystyle\|u_{k}^{i}\|

\displaystyle\leq\beta\|\bar{\psi}-\psi_{k}^{i}\|+\frac{\|P\|}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}{\|\psi_{k}^{i}-\bar{\psi}\|+\|\psi_{\ell}^{i}-\bar{\psi}\|}=(\beta+\|P\|)\|\bar{\psi}-\psi_{k}^{i}\|+\frac{\|P\|}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}{\|\psi_{\ell}^{i}-\bar{\psi}\|}

\displaystyle\leq(1-h\beta)^{i}\left(\left(\beta+\|P\|\right)\|\psi_{k}^{0}-\bar{\psi}\|+\frac{\|P\|}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}{\|\psi_{\ell}^{0}-\bar{\psi}\|}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|u^{i}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1-h\beta)^{2i}\left(\left(\beta+\|P\|\right)^{2}\|\psi_{k}^{0}-\bar{\psi}\|^{2}+\frac{\|P\|^{2}}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}{\|\psi_{\ell}^{0}-\bar{\psi}\|^{2}}\right),

and

\displaystyle\|u^{i}\|_{N}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1-h\beta)^{2i}\left(\left(\beta+\|P\|\right)^{2}+\|P\|^{2}\right)\|\psi_{\ell}^{0}-\bar{\psi}\|^{2}_{N}.

Also, the state at time
i
are bounded by the initial state

\frac{1}{N}\|\psi^{i}-\bar{\psi}\|_{N}^{2}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\psi_{k}^{i}-\bar{\psi}\|^{2}=\frac{(1-h\beta)^{2i}}{N}\|\psi^{0}-\bar{\psi}\|^{2}_{N}.

Combining the previous estimates yields for the cost
J_{N}^{h,t_{0},T}(\psi,u)
the inequality

\displaystyle J_{N}^{h,t_{0},T}(\psi,u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{h}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}(1-h\beta)^{2i}\left(1+2\gamma((\beta+\|P\|)^{2}+\|P\|^{2})\right)\|\psi^{0}-\bar{\psi}\|_{N}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{h-h(1-h\beta)^{2M}}{1-(1-h\beta)^{2}}\left(1+2\gamma(\beta^{2}+2\beta\|P\|+2\|P\|^{2})\right)\frac{1}{N}\|\psi^{0}-\bar{\psi}\|_{N}^{2}.

Therefore, the optimal value (
9
) of problem (
7
) fulfils

\displaystyle v_{N}^{h,t_{0},T}(\psi^{0})\leq\frac{h}{1-(1-h\beta)^{2}}\left(1+2\gamma(\beta^{2}+2\beta\|P\|+2\|P\|^{2})\right)\frac{1}{N}\|\psi^{0}-\bar{\psi}\|_{N}^{2}.

Hence, equations (
16
) and (
17
) hold true with

\tilde{C}_{0}:=\frac{h}{1-(1-h\beta)^{2}}\left(\frac{2}{\gamma}+4(\beta^{2}+2\beta\|P\|+2\|P\|^{2})\right).

(20)

∎

Proof of Theorem (
2.2
)

Next, we turn to the proof of Theorem

Assume

For the cheap control (

(29)

Due to the relation (

\displaystyle L^{i}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}(1-h\beta)^{2i}\|\psi^{0}-\bar{\psi}\|^{2}_{N}=(1-h\beta)^{2i}L^{0}_{N}.

(30)

for
h\rightarrow 0^{+}

In this section, we show that the turnpike property
for each discretization level
h>0
,
also holds uniformly for
h\rightarrow 0^{+}
.
This completes the picture about the turnpike property
that we have established already for the continuous in time problem in
[

]
.
The turnpike property possesses a continuous transition between the time-discrete level and the continuous description.

Due to (

and for a given stepsize
h
, the constant in the
inequality
(

)
in the cheap control condition
is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{0}(h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\,\beta-h\,\beta^{2}}\left[\frac{2}{\gamma}+4(\beta^{2}+2\beta\|P\|+2\|P\|^{2})\right].

This implies

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\tilde{C}_{0}(h)=\frac{1}{\beta}\left[\frac{1}{\gamma}+2(\beta^{2}+2\beta\|P\|+2\|P\|^{2})\right]=:\tilde{D}_{0}

and

is strictly decreasing with respect to

.
Hence, for all

\tilde{D}_{0}<\tilde{C}_{0}(h)\leq 2\tilde{D}_{0}.

Thus inequality
(

in the cheap control condition
with the constant

6
Conclusion

The continuous-time multi-agent optimal control problem has been discretized and the turnpike property has been established for the discrete-time case.
Without additional assumptions on the problem, the turnpike property is independent of the step size
h
of the discretization and therefore holds uniformly in
h.
This shows that the turnpike property is inherited by suitable numerical discretization schemes. The proof relies on an extension of the strict dissipativity and the cheap control inequality for the discrete system. Numerical tests illustrate the turnpike phenomenon and support the theoretical findings. Future research includes the treatment of other integrators in time, in particular, symplectic integrators that are suitable for long time horizons. Another interesting aspect is the time-discrete case for the large agent limit, since we expect that the turnpike property still holds in the limit of infinitely many agents as in
[
25
]
.
Also the study of second order models is of interest, see
[
29
]
.

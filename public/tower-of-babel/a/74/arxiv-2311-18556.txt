Null controllability for stochastic coupled systems of fourth order parabolic equations
Yu Wang

Abstract
This paper aims to establish null controllability for systems coupled by two backward fourth order stochastic parabolic equations. The main goal is to control both equations with only one control act on the drift term. To achieve this, we develop a new global Carleman estimate for fourth order stochastic parabolic equations, which allows us to deduce a suitable observability inequality for the adjoint systems.



AMS Mathematics Subject Classification.

93B05, 93B07.

Keywords
. Null controllability, coupled systems, backward fourth order stochastic parabolic equation, observability, Carleman estimates.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{G}(z_{1}^{2}(T)+z_{2}^{2}(T))dx\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{G}(z_{1}^{2}(t)+z_{2}^{2}(t))dx.

Combining it with
Section

4
, we complete the proof.
∎

Proof of the null controllability

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

We define the linear subspace
\mathcal{Y}
of

to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}=\big\{\chi_{G_{0}}z_{2}\mid(z_{1},z_{2})~\text{solves the system \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eqEquationsAdj} with}(z_{1}^{0},z_{2}^{0})\in L^{2}(G)\times L^{2}(G)\big\},

and define a linear functional

on

as follows

\displaystyle F(\chi_{G_{0}}z_{2})=\mathbb{E}\int_{G}(y_{1}^{T}z_{1}(T)+y_{2}^{T}z_{2}(T))dx.

Thanks to

1.2
, it holds that

\displaystyle|F(\chi_{G_{0}}z_{2})|

\displaystyle=\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\int_{G}(y_{1}^{T}z_{1}(T)+y_{2}^{T}z_{2}(T))dx\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\int_{G}(|y_{1}^{T}|^{2}+|y_{2}^{T}|^{2})dx\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\int_{G}(|z_{1}(T)|^{2}+|z_{2}(T)|^{2})dx\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(5.1)

Hence,
F
is a bounded linear functional on
\mathcal{Y}
.
By Hahn-Banach theorem,
F
can be extended to a bounded linear functional on
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))
.
For simplicity, we also denote
F
for this extension.
From Riesz representation theorem, there exists
u\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{G}(y_{1}^{T}z_{1}(T)+y_{2}^{T}z_{2}(T))dx=F(\chi_{G_{0}}z_{2})=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{G_{0}}z_{2}udxdt.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{G}y_{1}^{T}z_{1}(T)dx-\int_{G}y_{1}(0)z_{1}^{0}dx=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{G}d(y_{1}z_{1})dx

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{G}(z_{1}dy_{1}+y_{1}dz_{1}+dy_{1}dz_{1})dx

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{G}[(\Delta z_{1}\Delta y_{1}+a_{1}z_{1}y_{1}+a_{2}z_{1}y_{2}+a_{3}Y_{1}z_{1})+(-\Delta z_{1}\Delta y_{1}-a_{1}z_{1}y_{1}-b_{1}z_{2}y_{1})-a_{3}z_{1}Y_{1}]dxdt

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{G}(b_{1}z_{2}y_{2}-a_{2}z_{2}y_{2})dxdt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{G_{0}}uz_{2}dxdt.

(5.4)

Combining

5.2
,
5
and
5
, for any

\displaystyle\int_{G}y_{1}(0)z_{1}^{0}dx+\int_{G}y_{2}(0)z_{2}^{0}dx=0.

Hence, this implies

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma 2.4

We need the following regularity estimate.

Lemma A.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, such that for all
h\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;H^{4}(G))
,
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))
and
g\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;H_{0}^{2}(G))
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &dh+\Delta^{2}hdt=fdt+gdW(t)&&\quad\text{ in }Q,\\
&h=\frac{\partial h}{\partial\nu}=0&&\quad\text{ on }\Sigma,\\
&h(0,\cdot)=0&&\quad\text{ in }G,\end{aligned}\right.

we have

\displaystyle|h|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;H^{4}(G))}\leq C(|f|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}+|g|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;H^{2}(G))}).

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be an unbounded operator on
L^{2}(G)
as follows

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{A})=\bigg\{\varphi\in H^{4}(G)\mid\varphi=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}=0\text{ on }\Gamma\bigg\},\\
&\mathsf{A}\varphi=\Delta^{2}\varphi,\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{A}).\end{aligned}\right.

Denote by
0<\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}\leq\cdots
the eigenvalues of
\mathsf{A}
and
by
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{A})
the corresponding eigenfunctions, which serves as a standard orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(G)
.
By means of the regularity for solutions to the elliptic equations (see
[
11
, Chapter 2, Theorem 5.1]
), we know that
\phi_{i}\in H^{8}(G)
, and

\displaystyle\phi_{i}=|\nabla\phi_{i}|=|\Delta^{2}\phi_{i}|=|\nabla\Delta^{2}\phi_{i}|=0

on
\Gamma
for
i=1,2,3,\cdots

Consider the following stochastic differential equations

\begin{cases}dc_{i}^{m}=\mu_{i}c_{i}^{m}dt+(f,\phi_{i})_{L^{2}(G)}dt+(g,\phi_{i})_{L^{2}(G)}dW(t),\\
c_{i}^{m}(0)=0,\end{cases}

(A.1)

where
i=1,\cdots,m
.
Clearly, we have
(f,\phi_{i})_{L^{2}(G)},~(g,\phi_{i})_{L^{2}(G)}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
.
Thanks to the classical theory of stochastic differential equations (see
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
), equation
Eq.

A.1
exists a unique solution
c_{i}^{m}(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{R}))
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Let
h^{m}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}^{m}(t)\phi_{i}
.
From
Eq.

A.1
, we have

\displaystyle dh^{m}=-\Delta^{2}h^{m}dt+f^{m}dt+g^{m}dW(t),

(A.2)

where
f^{m}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}(f,\phi_{i})_{L^{2}(G)}\phi_{i}
and
g^{m}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}(g,\phi_{i})_{L^{2}(G)}\phi_{i}
. Note that
h^{m}(0)\equiv 0
.
Thanks to Itô’s formula, it holds that

d(h^{m}\Delta^{2}h^{m})=dh^{m}\Delta^{2}h^{m}+h^{m}\Delta^{2}(dh^{m})+dh^{m}d(\Delta^{2}h^{m}).

(A.3)

Integrating it on
Q
, taking expectation on both sides, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{G}h^{m}(T)\Delta^{2}h^{m}(T)dx=\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}[dh^{m}\Delta^{2}h^{m}+h^{m}\Delta^{2}(dh^{m})+dh^{m}d(\Delta^{2}h^{m})]dx.

(A.4)

From the definition of
h^{m}
and
\phi_{i}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\int_{G}h^{m}(T)\Delta^{2}h^{m}(T)dx=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}\mathbb{E}(c_{i}^{m}(T))^{2}\geq 0.

(A.5)

From
Eq.

A.2
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}dh^{m}\Delta^{2}h^{m}dx

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}|\Delta^{2}h^{m}|^{2}dxdt+2\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}|f|^{2}dxdt,

(A.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}h^{m}\Delta^{2}(dh^{m})dx

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}h^{m}(-\Delta^{4}h^{m})dxdt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}h^{m}(\Delta^{2}f^{m})dxdt

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}|\Delta^{2}h^{m}|^{2}dxdt+2\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}|f|^{2}dxdt,

(A.7)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}dh^{m}d(\Delta^{2}h^{m})dx=\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}g^{m}\Delta^{2}g^{m}dxdt=\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}|\Delta g^{m}|^{2}dxdt.

(A.8)

Combining
Eqs.

A.4
,
A.5
,
A.6
,
A
and
A.8
, we have

|\Delta^{2}h^{m}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}\leq 4|f^{m}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}+|\Delta g^{m}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}.

(A.9)

From
Eq.

A.9
, thanks to elliptic regularity theory (see
[
12
, Chapter 4, Theorem 4.1]
), we get

\displaystyle|h^{m}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;H^{4}(G))}\leq C|f^{m}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}+C|\Delta g^{m}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}.

Therefore
\{h^{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
exists a subsequence, which is still denoted by
\{h^{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
,
weakly convergence to
\tilde{h}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;H^{4}(G))
as
m
tends to
\infty
.
On the other hand, thanks to the well-posedness of the stochastic evolution equations (see

[
18
, Theorem 3.13]
), we know that
\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}|h^{m}-h|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}=0
.
Hence,
\tilde{h}=h
for a.e.
(t,x,\omega)\in[0,T]\times G\times\Omega
and

|h|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;H^{4}(G))}\leq\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}|h^{m}|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;H^{4}(G))}\leq C(|f|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}+|g|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;H^{2}(G))}).

∎

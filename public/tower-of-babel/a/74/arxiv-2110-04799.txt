Abstract
. A number of the form x ⁡ ( x + 1 ) / 2 x(x+1)/2 where x x is an integer is called a triangular number. 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of integers, the set of positive integers, and the set of non-negative integers, respectively. The numbers of the form
x(x+1)/2
where
x\in\mathbb{Z}
are called triangular numbers. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
(
k\geq 2
), we set the following notations.

\displaystyle N(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k};n):=

\displaystyle\#\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:n=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}x_{i}^{2}\right\},

\displaystyle T(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k};n):=

\displaystyle\#\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}:n=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\frac{x_{i}(x_{i}+1)}{2}\right\}.

Also, define

\displaystyle C(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k}):=\binom{i_{1}}{4}+\binom{i_{1}}{2}+i_{1}i_{3},

where
i_{j}
denotes the number of times
j
appears in
(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k})
. Adiga, Cooper, and Han
[
1
]
showed that for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for
a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}\leq 7
,

\displaystyle 2^{k}T(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k};n)=\frac{2}{2+C(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k})}N(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k};8n+a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}).

Baruah, Cooper, and Hirchhorn
[
3
]
proved that for
a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}=8
,

\displaystyle 2^{k}T(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k};n)=\frac{2}{2+C(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k})}\big(

\displaystyle N(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k};8n+8)

\displaystyle-N(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k};2n+2)\big).

2.
Preliminaries

Ramanujan’s theta functions
\varphi(q)
and
\psi(q)
are defined by

\displaystyle\varphi(q)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\psi(q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n(n+1)/2}.

It is easy to see that the generating functions for
N(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k};n)
and
T(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k};n)
are given by

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}N(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k};n)q^{n}=\varphi(q^{a_{1}})\varphi(q^{a_{2}})\cdots\varphi(q^{a_{k}}),

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k};n)q^{n}=\psi(q^{a_{1}})\psi(q^{a_{2}})\cdots\psi(q^{a_{k}}).

Let’s list the following identities involving
\varphi(q)
and
\psi(q)
, which will be used in the proofs.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following identities hold:

(3)

\displaystyle\varphi(q)\varphi(q^{15})

\displaystyle=\varphi(-q^{6})\varphi(-q^{10})+2q\psi(q^{3})\psi(q^{5}),

(4)

\displaystyle\varphi(q^{3})\varphi(q^{5})

\displaystyle=\varphi(-q^{2})\varphi(-q^{30})+2q^{2}\psi(q)\psi(q^{15}),

(5)

\displaystyle 2\psi(q^{6})\psi(q^{10})

\displaystyle=\psi(q)\psi(q^{15})+\psi(-q)\psi(-q^{15}),

(6)

\displaystyle 2q^{3}\psi(q^{2})\psi(q^{30})

\displaystyle=\psi(q^{3})\psi(q^{5})-\psi(-q^{3})\psi(-q^{5}).

These identities from Ramanujan’s notebooks appear on page no. 377 in

]
with proof.

\displaystyle=\varphi(q^{4})+2q\psi(q^{8}),

(8)

\displaystyle\varphi(q)^{2}

\displaystyle=\varphi(q^{2})^{2}+4q\psi(q^{4})^{2},

\displaystyle\psi(q)^{2}

\displaystyle=\psi(q^{2})\varphi(q^{4})+2q\psi(q^{2})\psi(q^{8}),

Pseudomode description of general open quantum system dynamics: non-perturbative master equation for the spin-boson model
Graeme Pleasance, Francesco Petruccione

Abstract
We outline a non-perturbative approach for simulating the behavior of open quantum systems interacting with a bosonic environment defined by a generalized spectral density function. The method is based on replacing the environment by a set of damped harmonic oscillators—the pseudomodes—thereby forming an enlarged open system whose dynamics is governed by a Markovian master equation. Each pseudomode is connected to one of the poles of the spectral density when analytically continued to the lower-half complex frequency plane. Here, we extend previous results to a completely generic class of open system models, and discuss how our framework can be used as a powerful and versatile tool for analyzing non-Markovian open system dynamics. The effectiveness of the method is demonstrated on the spin-boson model by accurately benchmarking its predictions against numerically exact results.

In this Letter, we go beyond all previous limitations to extend the validity of the pseudomode mapping to a completely generic class of open system models. We achieve this by adapting a general proof from

To demonstrate the method, we apply it to a paradigmatic open system model—the spin-boson model—finding it able to capture the exact non-Markovian dynamics at strong system-bath coupling, whilst simultaneously being implemented at low computational costs.

We consider a microscopic model of a generic open system

Application to arbitrary SDs.—
Following on from this proof, we will now discuss some details relevant to the application of the result. One key consideration is that because the correspondence in Eq. (
10
) is entirely determined by the equivalence between the two-time correlation functions
C(\tau)
and
C^{\prime}(\tau)
, the mapping will only be exact when
C(\tau)
can be written as a finite sum of complex exponentials. This is equivalent to saying
\gamma(\omega)
is constrained to have the same frequency dependence as the effective spectral density
\gamma^{\prime}(\omega)=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}d\tau\,C^{\prime}(\tau)e^{i\omega\tau}
:

\gamma^{\prime}(\omega)=2\sum^{N}_{l=1}\frac{r^{R}_{l}(\omega-\xi_{l})+r^{I}_{l}\lambda_{l}}{(\omega-\xi_{l})^{2}+\lambda^{2}_{l}},

(11)

where
r_{l}\equiv r^{R}_{l}+ir^{I}_{l}
. For other forms of SD, including those related to thermal baths (see below), the correspondence between
\gamma(\omega)
and
\gamma^{\prime}(\omega)
cannot be made exact
Tamascelli et al. 2018
;
Mascherpa et al. 2020
, and so for these cases we will instead rely on optimizing the parameters of Eq. (
11
) as those most accurately representing the original SD function to obtain

C(\tau)\approx C^{\prime}(\tau),\quad\tau\geq 0.

(12)

In practice this can be achieved by fitting the SD of the physical problem with a set of
N
basis functions
\gamma^{\prime}_{l}(\omega)=2[r^{R}_{l}(\omega-\xi_{l})+r^{I}_{l}\lambda_{l}]/[(\omega-\xi_{l})^{2}+\lambda^{2}_{l}]
—or with
N
complex exponentials
C^{\prime}_{l}(\tau)=-ir_{l}\exp(-iz_{l}\tau)
in the time domain—where each pole of
\gamma^{\prime}_{l}(\omega)
in the lower-half plane is associated to one of the
N
pseudomodes in the auxiliary model. The residues of these poles are again used to parameterize the couplings
g^{\prime}_{l}
within the enlarged system Hamiltonian. In fact, since the non-Hermitian terms of
H_{0}
are generated through the imaginary part of these couplings
g^{\prime}_{l}
, their role in determining
\gamma^{\prime}(\omega)
may be conveniently illustrated by imposing a Hermitian form of interaction, i.e., by setting
r^{R}_{l}=0
and
r^{I}_{l}>0
, so that
g^{\prime}_{l}
are constrained to be real. From Eq. (
11

\gamma^{\prime}(\omega)
being written as a sum of positively weighted Lorentzians, implying the effect of the non-Hermitian terms is to generalize the form of
\gamma^{\prime}(\omega)
from a simple sum of Lorentzians to more complicated functions which are either (i) non-Lorentzian, or (ii) Lorentzian but with negative weights. As one might expect, these functions are capable of representing a much greater variety of environments compared to when
g^{\prime}_{l}
are real
Imamoglu 1994
;
Stenius and Imamoglu 1996
, thus enabling a more efficient simulation of the problem in general.

We finish this section by briefly commenting on the connection of our approach with that of Mascherpa
et. al.
in Ref.
Mascherpa et al. 2020
. There, the authors have introduced an analogous treatment based on using network of damped harmonic oscillators to replicate the effect of a bosonic environment on an open system, assuming the bath correlation function can be approximated as a sum of complex exponentials (
3
). Our approach differs from
Mascherpa et al. 2020
primarily in the ansatz used to construct the effective environment as well as the master equation used to model the dynamics of the enlarged system; more precisely, the auxiliary modes used to replace the environment in
Mascherpa et al. 2020
are interacting, and their dynamics when coupled to the system is described by a Lindblad master equation. The main consequence of these differences is that the couplings and decay rates of the auxiliary modes cannot be directly related to those of the correlation function (
3
), and so these parameters must be extracted by performing an additional step of inverting a system of non-linear equations depending on the weights and exponents of
C^{\prime}(\tau)
. In this regard, our method may offer certain numerical advantages for situations where it is difficult to perform such an inversion, since apart from the fitting of the two-time correlation function
C(\tau)
, no further steps are necessary in order to simulate the physical problem.

Summary & discussion.—
In conclusion, we have presented a novel framework for simulating the non-equilibrium behavior of open quantum systems within structured environments. Our method relies on replicating the effect of a bosonic environment on a general open system with a collection of pseudomodes, i.e., discrete bosonic modes, where each pseudomode is connected to one of the poles of the SD in the lower-half complex frequency plane. Since the mapping is non-perturbative in the original system-bath coupling, our formalism enables the full inclusion of memory effects within the reduced dynamics of the simulated problem. This has been demonstrated for the spin-boson model by benchmarking the results obtained from the pseudomode master equation against those from the numerically exact HEOM. Besides having numerous theoretical applications, our findings open up ways to possibly analyze the complex dynamics occurring within experimental setups relevant to e.g., quantum thermodynamics
Strasberg et al. 2016
;
Newman et al. 2017
, trapped ions
Lemmer et al. 2018
and quantum many-body physics
Daley 2014
. Future developments of the method could look at extensions to fermionic environments
Arrigoni et al. 2013
;
Dorda et al. 2014
;
Chen et al. 2019
, as well as making improvements to its performance based on the accuracy of the fitting algorithm.

First, we are going to prove that the reduced dynamics stemming from the master equation (
7
) in the main text is equivalent to the reduced system dynamics of an extended auxiliary model
S+M+R
, where each of the pseudomodes
M
is coupled to a local Markovian reservoir. The total Hamiltonian of this extended model reads

H^{\prime}=H_{S}+H_{E^{\prime}}+H^{\prime}_{I}

(17)

where
H_{E^{\prime}}=H_{M}+H_{R}+V_{MR}
,

\displaystyle H_{R}=\sum_{l}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}d\omega\,\omega a^{\dagger}_{Rl}(\omega)a_{Rl}(\omega),

(18)

\displaystyle V_{MR}=\sum_{l}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{l}}{\pi}}d\omega\bigg(b^{\dagger}_{l}a_{Rl}(\omega)+\text{h.c.}\bigg),

(19)

with
a_{Rl}(\omega)
(
a^{\dagger}_{Rl}(\omega)
) the annihilation (creation) operator for excitation of frequency
\omega
in the
l
-reservoir of
R
, and
[a_{Rl}(\omega),a^{\dagger}_{Rl^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})]=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})
. Here,
H_{R}
is the free Hamiltonian of the reservoirs, while
V_{MR}
describes the total
M+R
interaction. Note that the frequency independence of the coupling constants in Eq. (
19
) ensures the reservoirs each have a vanishing correlation time in accordance with
Gardiner and Zoller 2005
.

For a non-unitary evolution
S+M
in the auxiliary model,

\rho(t)=e^{\mathcal{L}t}\Big[\rho_{S}(0)\otimes\rho_{M}(0)\Big],

(20)

we now proceed to show the density matrix

\rho_{SMR}(t)=e^{-iH^{\prime}t}\big[\rho(0)\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|_{R}\big]e^{iH^{\prime}t}

(21)

yields a valid purification of
\rho(t)
on the extended state space
S+M+R
, such that
\rho(t)=\text{Tr}_{R}[\rho_{SMR}(t)]
(here,
|0\rangle_{R}=\bigotimes^{N}_{l=1}|0_{l}\rangle
denotes the collective vacuum state of the reservoirs). In particular, it is worth noting that since the Hamiltonian
H^{\prime}
is in principle non-Hermitian, Eq. (
21
) defines a pseudo-unitary evolution which neither preserves Hermiticity or positivity of the initial state
\rho_{SMR}(0)
. The ‘pseudo’ prefix refers to the fact that the righthand side of (
21
) is acted on by the inverse of the time evolution operator
\exp(-iH^{\prime}t)
, rather than its Hermitian conjugate, as might otherwise be employed for a time evolution involving a non-Hermitian generator.

Using Eq. (
21
), the Heisenberg equation of motion for an arbitrary operator
O=O^{\dagger}
of the enlarged system is given as (
O(t)=e^{iH^{\prime}t}Oe^{-iH^{\prime}t}
)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}O(t)=-i[O(t),H_{0}(t)]

\displaystyle\quad-i\sum_{l}\int d\omega\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{l}}{\pi}}\Big\{[O(t),b^{\dagger}_{l}(t)]a_{Rl}(\omega,t)+a^{\dagger}_{Rl}(\omega,t)[O(t),b_{l}(t)]\Big\}.

(22)

Eliminating the dependence on the reservoir operators
a_{Rl}(\omega,t)
and
a^{\dagger}_{Rl}(\omega,t)
subsequently yields the quantum Langevin equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}O(t)

\displaystyle=-i[O(t),H_{0}(t)]+\sum_{l}\lambda_{l}\left\{b^{\dagger}_{l}(t)[O(t),b_{l}(t)]-[O(t),b^{\dagger}_{l}(t)]b_{l}(t)\right\}

\displaystyle-i\sum_{l}\sqrt{2\lambda_{l}}\left\{[O(t),b^{\dagger}_{l}(t)]a^{(in)}_{Rl}(t)+a^{(in)\dagger}_{Rl}(t)[O(t),b_{l}(t)]\right\},

(23)

where

a^{(in)}_{Rl}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}d\omega\,e^{-i\omega t}a_{Rl}(\omega).

(24)

obeys the quantum white noise relation
Gardiner and Zoller 2005

[a^{(in)}_{Rl}(t),a^{(in)\dagger}_{Rl^{\prime}}(s)]=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta(t-s).

(25)

Next we can write down the expectation value
\langle O(t)\rangle=\text{Tr}\big[O(t)\rho_{SMR}(0)\big]
of Eq. (
.1
) for an initially factorized density matrix
\rho_{SMR}(0)=\rho(0)\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|_{R}
. In this case, the second line of Eq. (
.1
) vanishes from

a^{(in)}_{Rl}(t)\rho_{R}(0)=0=\rho_{R}(0)a^{(in)\dagger}_{Rl}(t),

(26)

and so

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle O(t)\rangle=-i\text{Tr}\Big[\mathinner{[{O(t)},{H_{0}(t)}]}\rho_{SMR}(0)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{l}\lambda_{l}\,\text{Tr}\left[\left\{b^{\dagger}_{l}(t)[O(t),b_{l}(t)]-[O(t),b^{\dagger}_{l}(t)]b_{l}(t)\right\}\rho_{SMR}(0)\right].

(27)

Because Eq. (
.1
) only contains operators acting on the enlarged system Hilbert space, we are then able to use the cyclic property of the trace

\langle O(t)\rangle\equiv\text{Tr}[O(t)\rho_{SMR}(0)]=\text{Tr}[O\rho^{\prime}(t)]

(28)

to obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\rho^{\prime}(t)=-i[H_{0},\rho^{\prime}(t)]+2\sum_{l}\lambda_{l}\left(b_{l}\rho^{\prime}(t)b^{\dagger}_{l}-\frac{1}{2}\big\{b^{\dagger}_{l}b_{l},\rho^{\prime}(t)\big\}\right),

(29)

which, from being identical to the master equation (
7
) in the main text, and with
\rho^{\prime}(t)
subject to the same initial condition as
\rho(t)
[Eq. (
20
)], implies
\rho(t)=\rho^{\prime}(t)
. Finally, tracing out the pseudomode degrees of freedom from both sides of this expression leads to

\rho^{\prime}_{S}(t)=\text{Tr}_{MR}[\rho_{SMR}(t)].

(30)

.2
Physical and extended auxiliary models

We now move on to prove that the reduced system evolution in the physical model
S+E
is equivalent to reduced dynamics of
S
in the extended auxiliary model. As we may recall from Ref.
Tamascelli et al. 2018
(and as a direct consequence of Wick’s theorem), a sufficient condition to guarantee the equivalence of the open system dynamics evolving under two physically different Gaussian environments is for the moments of their free bath coupling operators to match to second-order. More recently this condition was shown to hold even if coupling between the system and one of the environments is non-Hermitian
Lambert et al. 2019
;
Pleasance et al. 2020
, so long as the total density matrix of the system-plus-auxiliary environment obeys a pseudo-unitary evolution as per Eq. (
21
). As such, we are able to employ the same arguments here to prove the reduced system density matrices
\rho_{S}(t)
and
\text{Tr}_{MR}[\rho_{SMR}(t)]
share an equal time dependence. To this end, let us assume the auxiliary environment
M+R
is initially in the vacuum state

\rho_{MR}(0)=|0\rangle\langle 0|_{M}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|_{R},

(31)

such that

[H_{E^{\prime}},\rho_{MR}(0)]=0.

(32)

The reduced system dynamics of the extended auxiliary model will then be fully determined by the second-order correlation function (assuming vanishing first order moments
\langle B^{\prime}(t)\rangle=0
)

C^{\prime}(\tau)=\text{Tr}\Big[B^{\prime}(\tau)B^{\prime}(0)\rho_{MR}(0)\Big],\qquad\tau\geq 0,

(33)

where
B^{\prime}(\tau)=e^{iH_{E^{\prime}}\tau}B^{\prime}e^{-iH_{E^{\prime}}\tau}
.

By now inserting the explicit expression for
B^{\prime}(\tau)
into the above, we obtain

C^{\prime}(\tau)=-i\sum^{N}_{l,m=1}\sqrt{r_{l}r_{m}}\langle b_{l}(\tau)b^{\dagger}_{m}(0)\rangle,

(34)

given that all second-order moments involving normal ordered products of operators
b_{l}
and
b^{\dagger}_{l}
(e.g.,
\langle b^{\dagger}_{l}(\tau)b_{m}(0)\rangle
) are zero for the initial state (
31
). The time dependence of the correlation functions
\langle b_{l}(\tau)b^{\dagger}_{m}(0)\rangle
is contained in free evolution of the pseudomodes, whose Heisenberg equation of motion reads

\frac{d}{d\tau}b_{l}(\tau)=-i[b_{l}(\tau),H_{E^{\prime}}]=-i\xi_{l}b_{l}(\tau)-i\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{l}}{\pi}}a_{Rl}(\omega,t).

(35)

After eliminating the reservoir variables
a_{Rl}(\omega,\tau)
this equation becomes

\frac{d}{d\tau}b_{l}(\tau)=-iz_{l}b_{l}(\tau)-i\sqrt{2\lambda_{l}}a^{(in)}_{Rl}(t),

(36)

which can be formally solved to yield

b_{l}(\tau)=e^{-iz_{l}\tau}b_{l}(0)-i\sqrt{2\lambda_{l}}\int^{\tau}_{0}ds\,e^{-iz_{l}(\tau-s)}a^{(in)}_{Rl}(s).

(37)

In turn, we may substitute
b_{l}(\tau)
into the above and use the initial condition
\langle b_{l}(0)b^{\dagger}_{m}(0)\rangle=\delta_{lm}
, together with
\Big\langle b_{m}(0)a^{(in)}_{Rl}(s)\Big\rangle=0
, to obtain

C^{\prime}(\tau)=-i\sum^{N}_{l=1}r_{l}e^{-iz_{l}\tau},\quad\tau\geq 0.

(38)

Since the correlation function
C^{\prime}(\tau)
is therefore identical to its counterpart
C(\tau)
in the physical model, we have from the Gaussian property of
\rho_{E}(0)
and
\rho_{MR}(0)
that

\rho_{S}(t)=\text{Tr}_{MR}[\rho_{SMR}(t)].

(39)

Finally, by direct comparison of this result with Eq. (
30
), it is possible to establish the exact equivalence of the reduced dynamics occurring in both the physical and auxiliary models,

\rho_{S}(t)=\rho^{\prime}_{S}(t),

(40)

as stated in Eq. (
10
) of the main text.

As an additional remark, it is worth noting that the same result may equally be derived by utilizing an alternative definition of the two-time correlation function (
33
) that is consistent with the quantum regression theorem
Gardiner and Zoller 2005
;
Carmichael 1999
. Since the master equation (
29
) is Markovian, by setting
H_{S}=0
and
H^{\prime}_{I}=0
, it is possible to show that
C^{\prime}(\tau)
can be re-written in the form (see the Supplemental Material of
Tamascelli et al. 2018
),

C^{\prime}(\tau)=\text{Tr}\Big\{B^{\prime}e^{\mathcal{L}_{M}\tau}\Big[B^{\prime}\rho_{M}(0)\Big]\Big\},\qquad\tau\geq 0,

(41)

where
\mathcal{L}_{M}\rho=-i[H_{M},\rho]+\mathcal{D}\rho
. From here the proof follows the same steps outlined above: using that
\rho_{M}(0)=|0\rangle\langle 0|_{M}
, the correlation function
C^{\prime}(\tau)
can again be reduced to the same form as in Eq. (
34
) where the time dependence of
b_{l}(\tau)
is determined by
b_{l}(\tau)=\exp\big(\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}_{M}\tau\big)[b_{l}(0)]
(here,
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}_{M}
is defined via the duality relation
\text{Tr}\big[A\mathcal{L}_{M}(B)\big]=\text{Tr}\big[\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}_{M}(A)B\big]
). Since
b_{l}
obeys the eigenoperator relation
\mathcal{L}_{M}b_{l}=-iz_{l}b_{l}
, it is then straightforward to demonstrate that the above definition recovers Eq. (
38
), implying
\rho^{\prime}_{S}(t)=\rho_{S}(t)
.

𝒑 \bm{p} -Adic Properties of Hauptmoduln with Applications to Moonshine This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Moonshine and String Theory. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/moonshine.html
Ryan C. CHEN, Samuel MARKS and Matthew TYLER

Abstract
The theory of monstrous moonshine asserts that the coefficients of Hauptmoduln, including the j j -function, coincide precisely with the graded characters of the monster module, an infinite-dimensional graded representation of the monster group. On the other hand, Lehner and Atkin proved that the coefficients of the j j -function satisfy congruences modulo p n p^{n} for p ∈ { 2 , 3 , 5 , 7 , 11 } p\in\{2,3,5,7,11\} , which led to the theory of p p -adic modular forms. We combine these two aspects of the j j -function to give a general theory of congruences modulo powers of primes satisfied by the Hauptmoduln appearing in monstrous moonshine. We prove that many of these Hauptmoduln satisfy such congruences, and we exhibit a relationship between these congruences and the group structure of the monster. We also find a distinguished class of subgroups of the monster with graded characters satisfying such congruences.

Introduction and statements of results

The theory of monstrous moonshine arose from the remarkable observation of McKay and Thompson
[

\displaystyle 196884=1+196883

\displaystyle 21493760=1+196883+21296876,

\displaystyle J(\tau)=j(\tau)-744=q^{-1}+196884q+21493760q^{2}+\cdots,\qquad\text{where}\quad q={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}\tau},

\displaystyle 1,\;196883,\;21296876,\;842609326,\;19360062527,\;\dots.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect technical details and definitions that will be used later. We first describe the types of groups
\Gamma
whose Hauptmoduln will be studied. We then discuss various properties of operators on spaces of modular forms, most importantly the
U_{p}
operator and the Atkin–Lehner involutions. We also give descriptions of which cusps a Hauptmodul may have poles at once
U_{p}
is applied to it, and we give a modular form
g
with zeros at all such cusps. Finally, we discuss the trace of a modular form, which transforms modular forms on some
\Gamma
into modular forms on some
\Gamma^{\prime}\geq\Gamma
. These facts will ultimately be used to interpret Hauptmoduln as
p
-adic modular forms in Section
3
.

2.1
\bm{n|h}
-type groups

Monstrous moonshine associates to each
g\in\mathbb{M}
a Hauptmodul
\mathcal{T}_{g}
for some genus zero group
\Gamma_{g}\leq\GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
. This means that
\Gamma_{g}\backslash\mathbb{H}^{*}
is a genus zero curve and that
\mathcal{T}_{g}
is a generator for the function field such that
\mathcal{T}_{g}
is bounded away from the cusp
\infty
; moreover the
q
-expansion of
\mathcal{T}_{g}
at infinity begins
\mathcal{T}_{g}=q^{-1}+O(q)
. Conway and Norton described the groups
\Gamma_{g}
in
[
10
]
, all of which take on a particular form which we reproduce here.

First we describe the normalizer of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
in
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
h
be the largest integer such that
h^{2}|N
and
h|24
, and set
n=N/h
. The normalizer of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
is given by
\bigcup_{e\|n/h}w_{e}
where
w_{e}
is the set of all matrices
A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}ae&b/h\\
cn&de\end{smallmatrix}\right)
such that
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\det A=e
. Here the notation
x\|y
means that
x

exactly divides

y
, i.e., that
x|y
and
\gcd(x,y/x)=1
. Given integers
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
we set
e_{1}*e_{2}=\frac{e_{1}e_{2}}{\gcd(e_{1},e_{2})^{2}}
, and under
*
the set of exact divisors of any integer
N
forms the abelian group
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{n}
where
n
is the number of primes dividing
N
.

More generally, a class of subgroups called
n|h
-type groups
is defined as follows. Let
n
be any positive integer and let
h|\gcd(n,24)
. Set
N=nh
and
w_{e}
as above, for
e\|n/h
. We define the group
\Gamma_{0}(n|h)=w_{1}
. We will often abuse notation and write
w_{e}
for any element of
w_{e}
, and we see that
w_{e_{1}}w_{e_{2}}=w_{e_{1}*e_{2}}
. Since
h|24
we have that
m^{2}\equiv 1\ \left({\rm mod}\ h\right)
for all
m
coprime to
h
. For a subgroup
\{1,e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
of the group of exact divisors of
n/h
, we then define

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(n|h){+}e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}=\langle\Gamma_{0}(n|h),w_{e_{1}},w_{e_{2}},\dots,w_{e_{n}}\rangle=w_{1}\cup w_{e_{1}}\cup w_{e_{2}}\cup\dots\cup w_{e_{n}}.

A group of this form is called an
n|h
-type group.

Setting
N=nh
, the group
\Gamma_{0}(n|h){+}e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
normalizes both
\Gamma_{0}(n|h)
and
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, and the
w_{e_{i}}
are cosets of
\Gamma_{0}(n|h)
. When
h=1
we have
\Gamma_{0}(n|1)=\Gamma_{0}(N)
and we denote the matrix

\displaystyle w_{e}=\begin{pmatrix}ae&b\\
cN&de\end{pmatrix}

by
W_{e}
. The matrices
W_{e}
for
e\|N
are called
Atkin–Lehner involutions
. Given an Atkin–Lehner involution
W_{E}
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, we can interpret this as an element of
\Gamma_{0}(n|h)
via

\displaystyle W_{E}=\begin{pmatrix}aE&b\\
cN&dE\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}aE/h_{E}&b/h_{E}\\
cN/h_{E}&dE/h_{E}\end{pmatrix}=w_{e},

where
h_{E}
is the largest divisor of
h
with
h_{E}^{2}|E
and
e=E/h_{E}^{2}
. In fact, setting

\displaystyle\AL(\Gamma)=\{e\|nh\colon W_{e}\in\Gamma\text{ and every prime dividing }e\text{ also divides }n/h\}

we have that this association gives a bijection

\displaystyle\AL(\Gamma)\longleftrightarrow\{e\colon w_{e}\subset\Gamma\}.

For example, letting
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}(8|2){+}4
we have

\displaystyle\AL(\Gamma)=\{1,16\}\longleftrightarrow\{1,4\}=\{e\colon w_{e}\subset\Gamma\}.

When dealing with
n|h
-type groups, it is standard to simplify notation in the following ways. When
h=1
, we simply omit the
|h
, so that
\Gamma_{0}(n|1)=\Gamma_{0}(n)
, and when all
e\|n/h
are included in a group, we simply write
\Gamma_{0}(n|h){+}
so that
\Gamma_{0}(8|2){+}=\Gamma_{0}(8|2){+}4
. We will also sometimes use the symbol
n|h{+}e,f,\dots
to represent the group
\Gamma_{0}(n|h){+}e,f,\dots
in order to save space, particularly in tables, so we might write
8|2{+}
instead of
\Gamma_{0}(8|2){+}
.

The
n|h
-type groups appear in monstrous moonshine as
eigengroups
of the Hauptmoduln. That is, the Hauptmodul
\mathcal{T}
has an associated group
\Gamma_{0}(n|h){+}e,\dots
such that for all
A
in this group,
\mathcal{T}(A\tau)=\mu\mathcal{T}(\tau)
for some
h^{\text{th}}
root of unity
\mu
. Conway–Norton
[
10
]
conjectured the following rule for computing the eigenvalue
\lambda
corresponding to an element of
\Gamma_{0}(n|h){+}e,\dots
:

(i)

\lambda=1
for any element of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
,

(ii)

\lambda=1
for all
W_{e}
with
e\in\operatorname{AL}(\Gamma),

(iii)

\lambda={\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}/h}
for the element
x=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1/h\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
,

(iv)

\lambda={\rm e}^{\pm 2\pi{\rm i}/h}
for the element
y=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
n&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
where the sign is
+
if and only if
\tau\mapsto\frac{-1}{N\tau}
is in
\Gamma_{0}(n|h){+}e,\dots
.

This rule’s well-definedness and correctness follow from
[
17
]
and the correctness of the monstrous moonshine conjectures, respectively.

Since the cosets

and
y
generate
\Gamma_{0}(n|h)
, we have
\Gamma=\langle x,y,W_{e}\colon e\in\operatorname{AL}(\Gamma)\rangle
for any
n|h
-type group
\Gamma
. Hence Conway–Norton’s rule uniquely determines a map
\lambda\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\bm{\mu}_{h}
where
\bm{\mu}_{h}
denotes the group of
h^{\text{th}}
roots of unity. We always use
\lambda
to denote this map.

More generally, let
\Gamma
be an
n|h
-type group. An
eigenvalue map
is a homomorphism
\eta\colon\!\Gamma\!\rightarrow\!\bm{\mu}_{2h}
such that
\Gamma_{0}(nh)\subset\ker\eta
. Then we define

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\eta}=\ker\eta.

When
\lambda
is the map given by Conway–Norton’s rule, we have that
\Gamma_{\lambda}
is an index

subgroup of
\Gamma
called the
fixing group
of
\Gamma
. However, Conway–Norton’s rule does not always give a well-defined map, so
\Gamma_{\lambda}
does not exist for every
n|h
-type group
\Gamma
; for example
\Gamma_{\lambda}
doesn’t exist when
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}(21|3)
. Ferenbaugh
[
17
, Theorem 2.8]
classified the groups
\Gamma
for which Conway–Norton’s rule is consistent, and we call such
\Gamma

admissible
. There are
213
admissible

-type groups giving genus zero groups, including all
171
groups appearing in monstrous moonshine. Ferenbaugh also determined the structure of the quotient
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}(nh)
, and therefore also the structure of
\Gamma_{\lambda}/\Gamma_{0}(nh)
. In
174
cases, including the groups of monstrous moonshine, the latter quotient group has exponent
2
; the remaining
3
groups are known as the “ghosts”. For further discussion of which
n|h
-type groups appear in monstrous moonshine, see
[
11
]
.

2.2
Action of
\bm{U_{p}}
on Hauptmoduln

Given an
n|h
-type group
\Gamma
with eigenvalue map
\eta\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\bm{\mu}_{2h}
, we say a weight
k
weakly holomorphic modular form on
\Gamma_{0}(nh)
is
on
\Gamma

\eta

on
(\Gamma,\eta)
if

\displaystyle f|_{k}\gamma=\eta(\gamma)f\qquad\text{for all}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma,

where the weight
k
slash operator is defined by (
2.1
) below. By a
weakly holomorphic modular form
we mean a meromorphic modular form whose poles are supported on the cusps; on the other hand a
modular form
is assumed to be holomorphic everywhere. We write
M_{k}(\Gamma,\eta)
for the space of weight
k
modular forms on
\Gamma
with eigenvalue map
\eta
. Similarly, we denote the space of weight
k
modular forms on
\Gamma_{0}(nh)
invariant under all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\eta}
by

. Throughout, all our weights will be integers.

Fix a prime
p
. In this section, we study

applied to Hautpmoduln
\mathcal{T}
, and more generally weakly holomorphic modular forms on
\Gamma_{\eta}
or on
\Gamma
with eigenvalue map
\eta
. For our results to extend to
n|h
-type groups, the results of this section will be stated in the necessary general language. However the reader looking to use these results for modular forms on
\Gamma_{0}(N){+}e,\dots
should remember that this corresponds to taking
h=1
and ignoring eigenvalue maps in the following results.

Recall that the weight
k
slash operator
|_{k}\gamma
for
\gamma\in\GL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

\displaystyle(f|_{k}\gamma)(\tau)=(\det\gamma)^{k/2}(c\tau+d)^{-k}f(\gamma\tau).

(2.1)

If
f
is on
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
then for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
f(N\tau)
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, and for
d|N
and
e\|N
,

\displaystyle f(d\tau)|_{k}W_{e}=\left(\frac{d*e}{d}\right)^{k/2}f((d*e)\tau).

(2.2)

In terms of the slash operator,
U_{p}
is defined on weight
k
modular forms by

\displaystyle f|U_{p}=p^{k/2-1}\sum_{\mu=0}^{p-1}f|_{k}S_{\mu}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\mu=0}^{p-1}f(S_{\mu}\tau),

(2.3)

where
S_{\mu}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\mu\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. This operator is independent of
k
and acts on Fourier expansions by

\displaystyle\left(\sum a(n)q^{n}\right)|U_{p}=\sum a(pn)q^{n}.

We first recall some basic facts about the

[
3

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f
be a weight
k
meromorphic modular form on
\Gamma_{0}(p^{\alpha}N)
where
p\nmid N
.

(a)

If

2.3

Following Serre’s idea
[

]
, we will apply the trace to view classical modular forms as

-adic modular forms of lower level. In this section we discuss a few properties of trace maps.

Suppose
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are subgroups of
\GL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
with

a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma^{\prime}

\displaystyle\tr_{\Gamma\backslash\Gamma^{\prime}}f=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f|_{k}\gamma_{i},

is modular on

and

First consider

and

Lemma 2.10
.

Let

be prime.

(a)

A set of representatives for

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\

is the corresponding Atkin–Lehner involution on

Since

, part (a) follows upon checking that the representatives are inequivalent modulo

. One can also check that for any

∎

The remainder of this section will extend Lemma

to the more general context we need, for example tracing from

to
\Gamma_{0}(N){+}e,\dots
for

, suppose that
\Gamma
is a
pn|h
-type group with eigenvalue map

with

for all

be the

, and can take

to be the eigenvalue map on

\displaystyle x=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/h\\

The correct extension of the mod

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be an
n|h

a prime. If
\eta
is an eigenvalue map on

, then the

is the map

defined by

\displaystyle\eta_{t}(\gamma)=\eta(\gamma)\qquad\text{for}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(n|h)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta_{t}(W_{e})=\left(\frac{e}{p}\right)\eta(W_{e})\qquad\text{for}\quad e\in\operatorname{AL}(\Gamma),

where
\left(\frac{\cdot}{p}\right)
denotes the Legendre symbol.

Remark 3.2
.

If
\bm{1}
denotes the trivial eigenvalue map
\bm{1}(\gamma)=1
for all
\gamma
, then
\eta_{t}=\eta\bm{1}_{t}
for all
\eta
. Also, if
\left(\frac{e}{p}\right)=1
for all

then
\eta=\eta_{t}
.

Below, we collect some useful facts and begin to see the relationship between eigenvalue map twists and the weight mod
2(p-1)
. Let
E_{k}(\tau)
denote the weight
k

Eisenstein series
with constant term
1
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be an
n|h
-type group with eigenvalue map
\eta
, and let
p\geq 5
be prime with
p\nmid nh
.

(a)

Let
F(\tau)=E_{p-1}(h\tau)
. Then

\displaystyle\hat{F}=\sum_{e\in\operatorname{AL}(\Gamma)}\left(\frac{e}{p}\right)F|_{p-1}W_{e}\in M_{p-1}(\Gamma,\bm{1}_{t})

is congruent to
\#\AL(\Gamma)
mod
p
.

(b)

For all
k
, we have the inclusions

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\eta)\subseteq\tilde{M}_{k+p-1}(\Gamma,\eta_{t})\subseteq\tilde{M}_{k+2(p-1)}(\Gamma,\eta)\subseteq M_{k+3(p-1)}(\Gamma,\eta_{t})\subseteq\cdots.

(c)

Suppose
\eta\neq\eta_{t}
. Then for
f\in M_{k}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\Gamma,\eta)
and
f^{\prime}\in M_{k^{\prime}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\Gamma,\eta)
we have that

\displaystyle 0\not\equiv f\equiv f^{\prime}\pmod{p^{n}}\qquad\text{implies}\qquad k\equiv k^{\prime}\pmod{2p^{n-1}(p-1)}.

(d)

If
\eta\neq\eta_{t}
then the weight of a
p
-adic modular form on
(\Gamma,\eta)
is well-defined as an element of
\hat{\mathfrak{X}}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}/(2p-2)\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Since
F
is invariant under
\Gamma_{0}(n|h)
, the statement that
\hat{F}\in M_{p-1}(\Gamma,\bm{1}_{t})
becomes

\displaystyle\hat{F}|W_{e}=\left(\frac{e}{p}\right)F,

which follows from multiplicativity of the Legendre symbol. We also have

\displaystyle F|_{p-1}W_{e}(\tau)=\left(\frac{h*eh^{2}}{h}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}E_{p-1}\big(\big(h*eh^{2}\big)\tau\big)\equiv\left(\frac{e}{p}\right)\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right)

since
E_{p-1}\equiv 1\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right)
(see
[
37
, Section 1]
). Thus
\hat{F}\equiv\sum\limits_{e\in\AL(\Gamma)}1\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right)
, giving part (a). Since
p\neq 2
, this implies
1\in\tilde{M}_{p-1}(\Gamma,\bm{1}_{t})
, giving part (b) since
\eta\bm{1}_{t}=\eta_{t}
and

\displaystyle M_{k}(\Gamma,\eta)\cdot M_{k^{\prime}}(\Gamma,\eta^{\prime})=M_{k+k^{\prime}}(\Gamma,\eta\eta^{\prime}).

For part (c), we already know
k\equiv k^{\prime}\pmod{p^{n-1}(p-1)}
. Assume without loss of generality that
k\leq k^{\prime}
. If
k\not\equiv k^{\prime}\ \left({\rm mod}\ 2p-2\right)
then by part (b) there exists
g\in M_{k^{\prime}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\Gamma,\eta_{t})
with
g\equiv f

\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right)
. Since
\eta\neq\eta_{t}
, let
e\in\operatorname{AL}(\Gamma)
be a quadratic nonresidue so that

\displaystyle\eta(W_{e})f^{\prime}=f^{\prime}|_{k^{\prime}}W_{e}\equiv g|_{k^{\prime}}W_{e}=-\eta(W_{e})g\equiv-\eta(W_{e})f^{\prime}\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right),

which implies
f^{\prime}\equiv f\equiv 0\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right)
. Part (d) then follows.
∎

This motivates the following
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-spaces, which incorporate these twisted inclusions. Set

\displaystyle\tilde{M}(\Gamma,\eta)^{\alpha}=\bigcup_{k\equiv\alpha\ \left({\rm mod}\ 2p-2\right)}\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\eta)\quad\cup\quad\bigcup_{k\equiv\alpha+p-1\ \left({\rm mod}\ 2p-2\right)}\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\eta_{t})

for

. If every

is a quadratic residue mod

, we have
\eta=\eta_{t}
, and
\tilde{M}(\Gamma,\eta)^{\alpha}
only depends on
\alpha
mod
p-1
.

3.1
Producing
\bm{p}
-adic modular forms from Hautpmoduln

Let
\Gamma
be a

-type group with eigenvalue map
\eta
where
p\nmid nh
is prime. Suppose
\Gamma_{\eta}
is genus zero, and its Hautpmodul
\mathcal{T}
is on
\Gamma
with eigenvalue map
\eta
. We show that for some
\beta
,
\mathcal{T}|U_{p}^{\beta}
is a
p
-adic modular form on
(\Gamma^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})
for some specified
n|h
-type group
\Gamma^{\prime}
and eigenvalue map
\eta^{\prime}
. We can take

whenever

.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
p^{\alpha}n|h
-type group for
p\nmid nh
prime, and
\eta
be an eigenvalue map on
\Gamma
. Let
f
be a weight
0
weakly holomorphic modular form on
(\Gamma,\eta)
that is holomorphic away from
\infty
. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be the
n|h
-type group with
e\in\operatorname{AL}(\Gamma^{\prime})
if and only if
e\in\operatorname{AL}(\Gamma)
and
p\nmid e
. Let
\beta=\max\{1,\alpha-1\}
, and let
\eta^{\prime}
be the eigenvalue map on
\Gamma^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}(x)=\eta\big(x^{p^{\beta}}\big),\qquad\eta^{\prime}(y^{\prime})=\eta\big(y^{p^{\alpha+\beta}}\big),\qquad\eta^{\prime}(W_{e})=\eta(W_{e})\qquad\text{for}\quad e\in\operatorname{AL}(\Gamma^{\prime}),

where
x
,
y
,
y^{\prime}
are the generators of
\Gamma_{0}(p^{\alpha}n|h)
and
\Gamma_{0}(n|h)
given by

\displaystyle x=\begin{pmatrix}1&h\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad y=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
p^{\alpha}n&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad y^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
n&1\end{pmatrix}.

Then,
f|U_{p}^{\beta}
is
p
-adic modular form of weight
0
on
(\Gamma^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
,
f|U_{p}^{\beta}
is a weakly holomorphic modular form on
(\Gamma,\nu)
where
\Gamma
is the
pn|h
-type group
\Gamma
where
e\in\operatorname{AL}(G)
if and only if
e\in\operatorname{AL}(\Gamma)
and
p\nmid e
, and
\nu
satisfies

\displaystyle\nu(x)=\eta\big(x^{p^{\beta}}\big),\qquad\nu(Y)=\eta\big(y^{p^{\alpha+\beta-1}}\big),\qquad\nu(W_{e})=\eta(W_{e}),\qquad\text{where}\quad Y=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
pn&1\end{pmatrix}.

The remainder of the proof follows
[
37
, Theorem 10]
. To show that
f|U_{p}^{\beta}
is a
p
-adic modular form on
(\Gamma^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})
, we set for
m\geq 0

\displaystyle f_{m}=\tr_{G_{\nu}\backslash\Gamma^{\prime}_{\eta^{\prime}}}\big(f|U_{p}^{\beta}g^{p^{m}}\big)=\tr_{pnh\backslash nh}\big(f|U_{p}^{\beta}g^{p^{m}}\big),

where
g
is the modular form on
\Gamma
given by Lemma
2.6
. Since

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}(x)=\nu(x)=\eta\big(x^{p^{\beta}}\big)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta^{\prime}(y^{\prime})=\nu(Y^{p})=\eta\big(y^{p^{\alpha+\beta}}\big)\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}(W_{e})=\nu(W_{e})=\eta(W_{e})\qquad\text{for}\quad e\in\operatorname{AL}(\Gamma^{\prime})

we know
f_{m}
is a weakly holomorphic modular form on
(\Gamma^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})
by Lemma
2.11
. Lemma
2.4
shows that
f|U_{p}^{\beta}
has poles only at the cusps equivalent to
0
on
\Gamma_{0}(p)
, and since
g
has zeros at all such cusps, we know
f|U_{p}^{\beta}g^{m}
is holomorphic for
m
sufficiently large. If
a
is the weight of
g
, the weight of
f_{m}
is
ap^{m}
, which
p
-adically converges to
0
. Hence it suffices to show that
f_{m}\rightarrow f|U_{p}^{\beta}
in the
p
-adic limit. We compute that

\displaystyle f_{m}-f|U_{p}^{\beta}=\big(f_{m}-f|U_{p}^{\beta}g^{p^{m}}\big)+f|U_{p}^{\beta}\big(g^{p^{m}}-1\big),

\displaystyle v_{p}\big(f_{m}-f|U_{p}^{\beta}\big)\geq\min\big\{v_{p}\big(f_{m}-f|U_{p}^{\beta}g^{p^{m}}\big),v_{p}\big(f|U_{p}^{\beta}\big)+v_{p}\big(g^{p^{m}}-1\big)\big\}.

Since
g^{p^{m}}\equiv 1\ \left({\rm mod}\ p^{m+1}\right)
, we have
v_{p}\big(f|U_{p}^{\beta}\big)+v_{p}\big(g^{p^{m}}-1\big)\geq m+1
. Lemma
2.11
implies

\displaystyle f_{m}-f|U_{p}^{\beta}g^{p^{m}}=p^{1-ap^{m}/2}\big(f|U_{p}^{\beta}g^{p^{m}}|_{ap^{m}}W_{p}\big)|U_{p},

and since applying
U_{p}
does not decrease the power of
p
dividing a
q
-expansion, we have

\displaystyle v_{p}\big(f_{m}-f|U_{p}^{\beta}g^{p^{m}}\big)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{ap^{m}}{2}+v_{p}\big(f|U_{p}^{\beta}|_{0}W_{p}\big)+p^{m}v_{p}(g|_{a}W_{p})

\displaystyle\geq 1+v_{p}\big(f|U_{p}^{\beta}|_{0}W_{p}\big)+p^{m}\left(v_{p}(g|_{a}W_{p})-\frac{a}{2}\right).

Lemma
2.6
gives
v_{p}(g|_{a}W_{p})>\frac{a}{2}
. Hence,
v_{p}\big(f_{m}-f|U_{p}^{\beta}\big)\rightarrow\infty
as
m\rightarrow\infty
, as desired.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

If
\Gamma
is a

-type group with eigenvalue map

is genus zero, and the Hauptmodul

on

is on

, then Lemma

applies. Moreover, since

is on

, the lemma also applies to powers of the Hauptmodul. In particular, polynomials in

after enough applications of

.

3.2
Ordinary spaces

Having produced
p
-adic modular forms from Hauptmoduln on certain
n|h
-type groups, we now study the action of
U_{p}
. The key idea, developed by Serre on level
1
in
[
37
]
, is that
U_{p}
contracts mod
p
modular forms onto a finite-dimensional space. These structural results will allow us to verify
p
-adic annihilation of certain Hauptmoduln in Section
4.2
.

We will take
p\geq 5
prime with
p\nmid nh
. For the Hecke operator
T_{p}
, we have

\displaystyle f|_{k}T_{p}=f|U_{p}+p^{k/2-1}f|_{k}A=f|U_{p}+p^{k-1}f(p\tau),\qquad\text{where}\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},

so
f|U_{p}\equiv f|_{k}T_{p}\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right)
for
k\geq 2
. Since
T_{p}
acts on
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(nh))
, we know
U_{p}
acts on
\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(nh))
. Furthermore, let
\Gamma
be an
n|h
-type group and
\eta
be an eigenvalue map. Since

\displaystyle Ax=x^{p}A,\qquad Ay^{p}=yA,\qquad AW_{e}=W_{e}A\qquad\text{for}\quad p\nmid e,

Lemma
2.2
implies that
T_{p}\colon M_{k}(\Gamma,\eta)\rightarrow M_{k}(\Gamma,\eta^{\sigma_{p}})
. Hence
U_{p}\colon\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\eta)\rightarrow\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\eta^{\sigma_{p}})
, so we consider the space

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p})=\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\eta)+\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\eta^{\sigma_{p}}),

which is stabilized by
U_{p}
. This sum is direct if and only if
\eta\neq\eta^{\sigma_{p}}
. We thus set

\displaystyle\tilde{M}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p})^{\alpha}=\tilde{M}(\Gamma,\eta)^{\alpha}+\tilde{M}(\Gamma,\eta^{\sigma_{p}})^{\alpha}

and remind the reader that
\tilde{M}(\Gamma,\eta)^{\alpha}
already encodes spaces with twisted eigenvalue map. We next show how
U_{p}
contracts
\tilde{M}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p})^{\alpha}
onto a finite-dimensional space called the
ordinary space
. Ordinary spaces of
p
-adic modular forms were extensively studied by Hida
[
25
]
. We describe our ordinary spaces in the language of Serre’s
p
-adic modular forms.

Proposition 3.6

(ordinary decomposition)
.

Let
\Gamma
be an
n|h
-type group with eigenvalue map
\eta
.

(a)

We can write

\displaystyle\tilde{M}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p})^{\alpha}=\mathfrak{S}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p})^{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{N}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p})^{\alpha}

so that
U_{p}
is bijective on the ordinary space
\mathfrak{S}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p})^{\alpha}
and locally nilpotent on
\mathfrak{N}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p})^{\alpha}
; that is, for any
\mathfrak{N}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p})^{\alpha}
, we have
f|U_{p}^{n}=0
for
n
sufficiently large.

(b)

Let
4\leq k\leq p+1
be such that
k\equiv\alpha\ \left({\rm mod}\ p-1\right)
. If
k\equiv\alpha\ \left({\rm mod}\ 2p-2\right)
then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p})^{\alpha}\subseteq\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p}).

Otherwise,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p})^{\alpha}\subseteq\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma,[\eta_{t}]_{p}).

Proposition
3.6
can be interpreted to mean that repeated application of
U_{p}
reduces the weight of a modular form mod
p
to either
0
or
p-1
. To accomplish this, we need to incorporate the twisted eigenvalue maps. More precisely, the
filtration
of

with respect to

is

\displaystyle w_{\Gamma,\eta}(f)=\min\big\{k\colon f\in\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma,[\eta]_{p})\text{ or }\tilde{M}_{k}(\Gamma,[\eta_{t}]_{p})\big\}.

When
(\Gamma,\eta)
is clear from context, we will simply write
w
for

. Similarly, the filtration

of a modular form mod
p
with respect to
\Gamma
is the filtration with respect to
(\Gamma,\bm{1})
.

To prove Proposition
3.6
we generalize the following fact from
[
27
]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}(N)
. Then for modular forms
f
mod
p
on
\Gamma
we have

\displaystyle w_{\Gamma}(f|U_{p})\leq p+\frac{w_{\Gamma}(f)-1}{p}.

In particular,
w_{\Gamma}(f|U_{p})<w_{\Gamma}(f)
if
w_{\Gamma}(f)>p+1
.

We give a suitable modification of Lemma
3.7
for
(\Gamma,\eta)
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be an
n|h
-type group with eigenvalue map
\eta
. For

,

\displaystyle w_{\Gamma,\eta}(f|U_{p})\leq p+\frac{w_{\Gamma,\eta}(f)-1}{p}.

Proof.

We first consider the special case
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}(n|h)
. We proceed by induction on the finite index
[\Gamma:\Gamma_{0}(nh)]
. Suppose that for some
\Gamma_{0}(nh)\leq\Gamma^{\prime}\lneq\Gamma_{0}(n|h)
we have

\displaystyle w_{\Gamma^{\prime},\eta|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}}(f|U_{p})\leq p+\frac{w_{\Gamma^{\prime},\eta|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}}(f)-1}{p}.

(3.1)

Let

be a representative for a nontrivial coset. Setting
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}=\langle\Gamma^{\prime},T\rangle
and
\eta^{\prime\prime}=\eta|\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
, we will prove that (

) also holds for
(\Gamma^{\prime\prime},\eta^{\prime\prime})
.

Let
f\in M_{k}^{(p)}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime},[\eta^{\prime\prime}]_{p})\subseteq M_{k}^{(p)}(\Gamma^{\prime},[\eta|\Gamma^{\prime}]_{p})
be a modular form of filtration

. By assumption there is some
g\in M_{k^{\prime}}^{(p)}(\Gamma^{\prime},[\eta|\Gamma^{\prime}]_{p})
with

and of filtration

\displaystyle k^{\prime}\leq p+\frac{k-1}{p}.

Let
t
be the order of
T
in
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}/\Gamma_{0}(nh)
, and let
\pi_{m}
be the projection

\displaystyle\pi_{m}(g)=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{\ell=0}^{t-1}{\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}\ell/t}g|_{k^{\prime}}T^{\ell}

onto the
{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}m/t}
eigenspace of
T
. Since
p\geq 5
, we know

. Thus, if
\varphi
,
\varphi^{\prime}
have the same weight and
\varphi\equiv\varphi^{\prime}\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right)
, then
\pi_{m}(\varphi)\equiv\pi_{m}(\varphi^{\prime})\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right)
.

By Proposition
3.3
, multiplying by some power of

gives
f^{\prime}\in M_{k}^{(p)}(\Gamma^{\prime},[\eta|\Gamma^{\prime}]_{p})
with
f^{\prime}\equiv g

\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right)
. Since
\hat{F}
is invariant under
\Gamma_{0}(n|h)
, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{m}(g)\equiv\pi_{m}(f^{\prime})\equiv\pi_{m}(f)\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right).

Let

be such that
\eta(T)={\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}m/t}
and set

\displaystyle\pi=\begin{cases}\pi_{m}&\text{if }\pi_{pm}=\pi_{m},\\
\pi_{m}\oplus\pi_{pm}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

which is projection onto the span of the eigenspaces of

. Thus, we have
\pi(g)\equiv\pi(f)=f\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right)
. Then
\pi(g)\in M_{k^{\prime}}^{(p)}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime},[\eta^{\prime\prime}]_{p})
with
\pi(g)\equiv f\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right)
as desired.

Extending by Atkin–Lehner involutions

is a similar computation. Define the projections as before, replacing

with the order

action

. The function

is not necessarily fixed by

, but rather lies in the
\big(\frac{e}{p}\big)
-eigenspace of

. Thus

\displaystyle\pi_{m+\varepsilon}(g)=\pi_{m}(f^{\prime})\equiv\pi_{m}(f)\ \left({\rm mod}\ p\right)

where

, and we have
\pi(g)\in M_{k^{\prime}}^{(p)}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime},[\eta^{\prime\prime}]_{p})
or
\pi(g)\in M_{k^{\prime}}^{(p)}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime},[\eta^{\prime\prime}_{t}]_{p})
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.6
.

Let
k
be as in the statement of Proposition

p_{j}\in p^{e+1+\alpha+v_{p}(b_{j})-\alpha}p^{\alpha}Z\cdot\mathbb{Z}[p^{\alpha}Z,p^{\alpha}]
and applying the inductive hypothesis gives
p_{j}S_{\ell-j}\in p^{e+1+\alpha+v_{p}(b_{j})-\alpha+(e-\alpha)(\ell-j)+1+\alpha}p^{\alpha}Z\cdot\mathbb{Z}[p^{\alpha}Z]
. We have the inequality
e+1+\alpha+v_{p}(b_{j})-\alpha\geq(e-\alpha)j
, which one checks explicitly using the values of
b_{j}
. In particular,
p_{j}S_{\ell-j}\in p^{(e-\alpha)\ell+1+\alpha}p^{\alpha}Z\cdot\mathbb{Z}[p^{\alpha}Z,p^{\alpha}]
. We also check that
\ell p_{\ell}\in p^{(e-\alpha)\ell+1+\alpha}p^{\alpha}Z\cdot\mathbb{Z}[p^{\alpha}Z,p^{\alpha}]
from the explicit values of
b_{j}
. Thus
S_{\ell}\in p^{(e-\alpha)\ell+1+\alpha}p^{\alpha}Z\cdot\mathbb{Z}[p^{\alpha}Z,p^{\alpha}]
for
\ell\leq p
.
When
l\geq p+1
we have

\displaystyle S_{\ell}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{j+1}p_{j}S_{\ell-j}

and we check that
p_{j}S_{\ell-j}\in p^{(e-\alpha)\ell+1+\alpha}p^{\alpha}Z\cdot\mathbb{Z}[p^{\alpha}Z,p^{\alpha}]
as above, which completes the induction.
Thus, we have shown that

\displaystyle\big(p^{\alpha(\ell-1)}Z^{\ell}|U_{p}\big)\in p^{\alpha}Z\cdot\mathbb{Z}[p^{\alpha}Z,p^{\alpha}]

so that if
g\in Z\cdot\mathbb{Z}[p^{\alpha}Z,p^{\alpha}]
, then
g|U_{p}\in p^{\alpha}Z\cdot\mathbb{Z}[p^{\alpha}Z,p^{\alpha}]
. Note that
\mathcal{T}|U_{p}=-p^{e-1}Z\in Z\cdot\mathbb{Z}[p^{\alpha}Z,p^{\alpha}]
. Repeatedly applying the result of the induction thus gives the rates of annihilation claimed in Proposition
4.11
.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

These explicit techniques also apply when
p\geq 5
, as long as
\mathcal{T}|U_{p}
is modular on a genus zero group. For example, if
\mathcal{T}
is the Hauptmodul for
2{+}
, then
\mathcal{T}|U_{5}
is modular on

\mathcal{T}
the Hauptmodul for

4.4
Preservation of annihilation under power maps

We discuss the relationship between Hauptmoduln
p
-adic annihilation and power maps in the monster. We have already seen hints of this in Section
4.1
. Unlike previous sections, this section will not be used elsewhere, except as motivation for Section
5
.

Definition 4.13
.

For a fixed Hauptmodul
\mathcal{T}_{g}
and an integer

, we say that
p
-adic annihilation is
preserved under the
d
-th power map
if
\mathcal{T}_{g}

p
-adically annihilated implies
\mathcal{T}_{g^{d}}
is
p
-adically annihilated.

These relationships are depicted in Appendix
B
for primes

, which we expect are the only primes with annihilation. Note that if

is relatively prime to the order of

, then
\mathcal{T}_{g^{d}}=\mathcal{T}_{g}
, so

-adic annihilation is preserved under the
d
-th power map. We will therefore restrict our attention to those

that divide the order of

. We first give conceptual explanations for this preservation of
p
-adic annihilation, when it holds. We then characterize exactly when
p
-adic annihilation is not preserved, and offer a notion of
p
-adic annihilation that seems to always be preserved under power maps. The average numerical rates of annihilation from Appendix

often do not decrease under power maps – we remark upon this briefly.

In certain situations, the compression formulas show that Hauptmodul

-adic annihilation is preserved under power maps, e.g. powering from the group
\ell+
to level

, when
\ell
is prime. When

Example 4.14
.

When
p=5
, the groups

,
10+2
,
10+
, and
2+
all have the same ordinary space
\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathfrak{S}(\Gamma_{0}(2)+,\bm{1})^{0}\subseteq\tilde{M}_{p-1}(\Gamma_{0}(2)+,\bm{1}_{t})
, and their Hauptmoduln share the same annihilation behavior. This explains preservation of
p
-adic annihilation under
p
-th power maps. The compression formulas of Proposition
4.5
capture these same relations.

Furthermore, in light of Lemma
4.8
and Section

, we seek inclusions of the form
\mathfrak{S}(\Gamma^{d},[\lambda]_{p})^{0}

\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{S}(\Gamma,[\lambda]_{p})^{0}
for

-type with

. This would explain

-th power map preservation of

-adic annihilation from

to

, and similarly for groups with the same ordinary spaces. The following proposition accomplishes this in certain situations.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let

be prime,
(d,nh)=1
,
\Gamma
be an
n|h
-type group with eigenvalue map
\eta
, and
\Gamma^{\prime}
be a

-type group with eigenvalue map

(a)

There is an inclusion

.

(b)

For any

, there is an inclusion

.

Proof.

Part (b) follows from part (a) using the description of

Remark 4.16
.

Inclusions of the form

may have further implications for preserving average rates of

-adic annihilation via Remark

.

We now discuss when power maps do not preserve Hauptmodul

.

If

for

Definition 4.19
.

Let

\mathfrak{S}_{p-1}(\Gamma,[(\lambda^{\sigma_{r}})_{t}]_{p})^{0}=0
otherwise.

We can check that strong

-adic annihilation is now preserved for the groups of Example

be the Hauptmodul on

for

Proposition 4.20
.

Let

5
Moonshine

In this section, we will investigate groups with
p
-adic moonshine. Recall from the introduction that a
moonshine module
for a finite group
G
is a graded
G
-module
V=\bigoplus_{n=-1}^{\infty}V_{n}
such that

(i)

For each
g\in G
, the McKay–Thompson series

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{g}(\tau)=\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}\tr(g\big|V_{n})q^{n}

is the Hauptmodul of an order

conjugacy class of the monster.

(ii)

For any
g\in G
and any

, if
\mathcal{T}_{g}
is the Hauptmodul for
\Gamma
, then
\mathcal{T}_{g^{n}}
is the Hauptmodul for
\Gamma^{n}
.

If for some prime
p
,

also satisfies the following property, then we call

a
p
-adic moonshine module
.

(iii)

For each irreducible character
\chi
of
\Gamma
, if
m_{\chi}(n)
is the multiplicity of
\chi
in the character of
V_{n}
, then the multiplicity generating function

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\chi}(\tau)=\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}m_{\chi}(n)q^{n}

associated to
\chi
is
p
-adically annihilated.

Throughout this section, we assume that Conjecture
4.2
holds. That is, we assume that Theorem
4.1
exactly characterizes which of the
171
Hauptmoduln appearing in monstrous moonshine are
p
-adically annihilated for each prime
p
.

We begin in Section
5.1
by stating basic facts about groups with
p
-adic moonshine, including the fact that for each prime

, only finitely many such groups exist. In Section
5.2
, we illustrate

-adic moonshine with several examples of groups having

-adic moonshine modules. In Section

-adic moonshine in a slightly more general sense.

We begin by presenting an alternative formulation of

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

is a

is

Proof.

By the Schur orthogonality relations, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{g}=\sum_{\chi\in\Irrep(G)}\chi(g)\mathcal{M}_{\chi}\qquad\text{for each}\quad g\in G

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\overline{\chi(g)}\mathcal{T}_{g}\qquad\text{for each}\quad\chi\in\Irrep(G).

If each
\mathcal{M}_{\chi}
is
p
-adically annihilated, then for all

, there exists

such that
\mathcal{M}_{\chi}|U_{p}^{N}\equiv 0

\ \left({\rm mod}\ p^{n}\right)
. Since each

is an algebraic integer, it follows that
\mathcal{T}_{g}|U_{p}^{N}\equiv 0\ \left({\rm mod}\ p^{n}\right)
, so each
\mathcal{T}_{g}
is
p
-adically annihilated. Similarly, if each
\mathcal{T}_{g}
is
p
-adically annihilated, then each

-adically annihilated.

\epsilon

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\mathcal{T}_{g}=\frac{1}{|G|}(J-\mathcal{T})+\mathcal{T},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\overline{\chi(g)}\mathcal{T}_{g}=\frac{1}{|G|}\left(\overline{\chi(e)}J+\sum_{g\in G-\{e\}}\overline{\chi(g)}\mathcal{T}\right)=\frac{\dim\chi}{|G|}(J-\mathcal{T}).

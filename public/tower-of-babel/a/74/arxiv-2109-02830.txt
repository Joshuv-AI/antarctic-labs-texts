Abstract




Abstract

Let
\Gamma=(G,\sigma)
be a signed graph and
A(G,\sigma)
be its adjacency matrix. Denote by
gr(G)
the girth of
G
, which is the length of the shortest cycle in
G
. Let
r(G,\sigma)
be the rank of
(G,\sigma)
.
In this paper, we will prove that
r(G,\sigma)\geq gr(G)-2
for a signed graph
(G,\sigma)
. Moreover, we characterize all extremal graphs which satisfy the equalities
r(G,\sigma)=gr(G)-2
and
r(G,\sigma)=gr(G)
.

Key Words
: Signed graph; Rank; Girth.

AMS Subject Classification (2010)
: 05C35; 05C50.

Preliminaries

For a signed graph

Lemma 2.1
.

[

]

Let
(P_{n},\sigma)
be a signed path. Then

if

is odd and

Lemma 2.2
.

[
4

Let
(C_{n},\sigma)
be a signed cycle, then

(a)

if
(C_{n},\sigma)
is balanced, then
r(C_{n},\sigma)=n-2
if

and
r(C_{n},\sigma)=n

(b)

if
(C_{n},\sigma)
is unbalanced, then
r(C_{n},\sigma)=n-2
if
n\equiv 2\;(mod\;4)
and
r(C_{n},\sigma)=n
otherwise.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
7
]

Let
(G,\sigma)
be a signed graph, if
u
is a pendant vertex of
G

is its unique neighbor, then

.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
4
]

Let

3
Bounds for the rank of
(G,\sigma)
in terms of girth

In this section, we will obtain some bounds for the rank of a signed graph in terms of its girth.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(G,\sigma)
be a connected signed graph and
(H,\sigma)
be an induced subgraph of
(G,\sigma)
. If
r(G,\sigma)=r(H,\sigma)
or
r(H,\sigma)+1
, then every vertex of
V(\bar{H})
has a neighbor in
V(H)
.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that there are two vertices
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
in
G
such that
d(u_{1},H)=1
and
d(u_{2},H)=2
, where
u_{1}
is adjacent
u_{2}
, respectively. By Lemma
2.3
, one has that

r((H,\sigma)+u_{1}+u_{2})=r(H,\sigma)+2\geq r(G,\sigma)-1+2=r(G,\sigma)+1,

since
(H,\sigma)+u_{1}+u_{2}
is an induced subgraph of
(G,\sigma)
, we obtain a contradiction to Lemma
2.7
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(G,\sigma)
be a connected signed graph with girth
g
. Then

r(G,\sigma)\geq g-2
, where the equality holds if and only if
(G,\sigma)
is a signed graph of one of the followings.

(a)

a balanced complete bipartite graph with
g=4
;

(b)

a balanced cycle with
g\equiv 0\;(mod\;4)
;

(c)

an unbalanced cycle with
g\equiv 2\;(mod\;4)
.

Proof.

Let
(C,\sigma)
be a shortest cycle in
(G,\sigma)
. Then by Lemmas
2.2
and
2.7
, we have that
r(G,\sigma)\geq r(C,\sigma)\geq g-2
. Now we prove the equality case.

Sufficiency:
If
(G,\sigma)
is a balanced complete bipartite graph with
g=4
, then by Lemma
2.4
(a), we have that

r(G,\sigma)=2=g-2.

If
(G,\sigma)
is a balanced cycle with
g\equiv 0\;(mod\;4)
or an unbalanced cycle with
g\equiv 2\;(mod\;4)
, then by Lemma
2.2
,

r(G,\sigma)=g-2.

Necessity:
Because
r(G,\sigma)=g-2
, one has that
(C,\sigma)
is a balanced cycle with
g\equiv 0\;(mod\;4)
or an unbalanced cycle with
g\equiv 2\;(mod\;4)
; otherwise, by Lemmas
2.2
and
2.7
, we have that

r(G,\sigma)\geq r(C,\sigma)=g,

a contradiction.
Now, we discuss two cases.

Case 1.
Suppose
(C,\sigma)
is a balanced cycle with
g\equiv 0\;(mod\;4)
.

Subcase 1.1.

When
(G,\sigma)
is a cycle, (b) of this theorem holds.

Subcase 1.2.

If
(G,\sigma)
is not a cycle. Then by Lemma
2.2
(a),

r(G,\sigma)=g-2=r(C,\sigma).

So by Lemma
3.1
, any vertex of
V(\bar{C})
has a neighbor in
V(C)
. If for every vertex
u
of
V(\bar{C})
, we have
|N_{C}(u)|\geq 2
, then by Lemma
2.9
,
g=4
. So

r(G,\sigma)=g-2=2,

by Lemma
2.4
(a),
(G,\sigma)
is a balanced complete bipartite graph. If there exists a vertex
u
of
V(\bar{C})
such that
|N_{C}(u)|=1
, then by Lemmas
2.1
and
2.3
,

r((C,\sigma)+u)=r(P_{g-1},\sigma)+2=g>g-2=r(G,\sigma),

a contradiction to Lemma
2.7
. Combining with above, we can obtain that
(G,\sigma)
is a balanced complete bipartite graph with
g=4
or a balanced cycle with
g\equiv 0\;(mod\;4)
.

Case 2.
Suppose
(C,\sigma)
is an unbalanced cycle with
g\equiv 2\;(mod\;4)
, we have
g\geq 6
. Similarly to above, any vertex
u
of
V(\bar{C})
has a neighbor in
C
, by Lemma
2.9
,
|N_{C}(u)|=1
. Using the same method as in Case 1, we obtain
(G,\sigma)
is an unbalanced cycle with
g\equiv 2\;(mod\;4)
.

This completes the proof.
\square

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(G,\sigma)
be a connected signed graph with girth
g
. If
(G,\sigma)
is not a balanced complete bipartite graph with
g=4
,
a balanced cycle with
g\equiv 0\;(mod\;4)
or an unbalanced cycle with
g\equiv 2\;(mod\;4)
, then
r(G,\sigma)\geq g
.

Proof.
By Theorem
3.2
, we have that

r(G,\sigma)\geq g-1.

If
r(G,\sigma)=g-1
, let
(C,\sigma)
be a shortest cycle in
(G,\sigma)
, assume that
(C,\sigma)
is neither an unbalanced cycle with
g\equiv 2\;(mod\;4)
nor a balanced cycle with
g\equiv 0\;(mod\;4)
, then by Lemmas
2.2
and
2.7
,

r(G,\sigma)\geq r(C,\sigma)=g>g-1=r(G,\sigma),

a contradiction. Now, we discuss two cases.

Case 1.
Suppose
(C,\sigma)
is an unbalanced cycle with
g\equiv 2\;(mod\;4)
, so by Lemma
2.2
(b),

r(G,\sigma)=g-1=r(C,\sigma)+1,

then by Lemma
3.1
, every vertex
u\in V(\bar{C})
has a neighbor in
C
. Since
g\geq 6
, by Lemma
2.9
, we have that for any vertex
u
of
V(\bar{C})
,
|N_{C}(u)|=1
. For some vertex
u_{1}
of
V(\bar{C})
, by Lemmas
2.1
,
2.3
and
2.7
, one has that

g-1=r(G,\sigma)\geq r((C,\sigma)+u_{1})=r(P_{g-1},\sigma)+2=g,

a contradiction. So
(G,\sigma)
is an unbalanced cycle with
g\equiv 2\;(mod\;4)
, a contradiction.

Case 2.
Suppose
(C,\sigma)
is a balanced cycle with
g\equiv 0\;(mod\;4)
. If
g=4
,
r(G,\sigma)=3
, then by Lemma
2.4
(b),
(G,\sigma)
is a complete tripartite graph, so
g=3
, a contradiction. If
g\geq 8
, using the same method as in Case 1, we can obtain that

(G,\sigma)
is a balanced cycle with
g\equiv 0\;(mod\;4)
, a contradiction.

Thus
r(G,\sigma)\neq g-1
, then
r(G,\sigma)\geq g
.

This complete the proof.
\square

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(G,\sigma)
be a connected signed graph with girth
g
. If
r(G,\sigma)=g
and
g
is odd, then one of the following results is established:

(a)

(G,\sigma)
is an odd cycle;

(b)

(G,\sigma)
is a complete tripartite graph (
g=3
) with partition
(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3})
, which satisfies that for each
i=1,2,3
, there exists a vertex
u_{i}\in V_{i}
such that for every other vertex
v\in V_{i}
, either
N_{+}(v)=N_{+}(u_{i}),N_{-}(v)=N_{-}(u_{i})
or
N_{+}(v)=N_{-}(u_{i}),N_{-}(v)=N_{+}(u_{i})
.

Proof.

Because
g
is odd,
g\geq 3
. Assume that
g=3
, that is
r(G,\sigma)=3
, then by Lemma
2.4
(b), we have that
(G,\sigma)
is a complete tripartite graph with partition
(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3})
, which satisfies that for each
i=1,2,3
, there exists a vertex
u_{i}\in V_{i}
such that for every other vertex
v\in V_{i}
, either
N_{+}(v)=N_{+}(u_{i}),N_{-}(v)=N_{-}(u_{i})
or
N_{+}(v)=N_{-}(u_{i}),N_{-}(v)=N_{+}(u_{i})
.

If
g\geq 5
, let
(C,\sigma)
be a shortest cycle of
(G,\sigma)
, then by Lemma
2.2
,

r(C,\sigma)=g=r(G,\sigma).

So by Lemmas
2.9
and
3.1
, for every vertex
u\in V(\bar{C})
, we have that
|N_{C}(u)|=1
. Let
u_{1}
be a vertex in
V(\bar{C})
,
then by Lemmas
2.1
and
2.3
,

r((C,\sigma)+u_{1})=r(P_{g-1},\sigma)+2=g+1>r(G,\sigma),

a contradiction to Lemma
2.7
.
Thus
(G,\sigma)
is an odd cycle.

This completes the proof.
\square

.

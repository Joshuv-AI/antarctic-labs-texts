The Cauchy dual subnormality problem via de Branges-Rovnyak spaces Thanks: The second author is supported by the SERB through the NPDF post-doctoral fellowship (Ref.No. PDF/2019/002724).
Sameer Chavan, Soumitra Ghara and Md. Ramiz Reza

Abstract
. The Cauchy dual subnormality problem (for short, CDSP) asks whether the Cauchy dual of a 2 2 -isometry is subnormal. In this paper, we address this problem for cyclic 2 2 -isometries. In view of some recent developments in operator theory on function spaces (see [ 4 , 23 ] ), one may recast CDSP as the problem of subnormality of the Cauchy dual ℳ z ′ \mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z} of the multiplication operator ℳ z \mathscr{M}_{z} acting on a de Branges-Rovnyak space ℋ ⁡ ( B ) , \mathcal{H}(B), where B B is a vector-valued rational function. The main result of this paper characterizes the subnormality of ℳ z ′ \mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z} on ℋ ⁡ ( B ) \mathcal{H}(B) provided B B is a vector-valued rational function with simple poles. As an application, we provide affirmative solution to CDSP for the Dirichlet-type spaces 𝒟 ⁡ ( μ ) \mathscr{D}(\mu) associated with measures μ \mu supported on two antipodal points of the unit circle.

1.
Cauchy dual subnormality problem for
2
-isometries

The Cauchy dual subnormality problem (for short, CDSP) for
2
-isometries can be seen as a manifestation of the rich interplay between positive definite and negative definite functions on abelian semigroups. Indeed, CDSP can be considered as the non-commutative variant of the fact from the harmonic analysis on semigroups that the reciprocal of a Bernstein function
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is completely monotone (see
[
32
, Theorem 3.6]
). This fact turns out to be somewhat equivalent to the solution of CDSP for completely hyperexpansive weighted shifts (see
[
7
, Proposition 6]
for a generalization). Another early result
towards the solution of CDSP asserts that the Cauchy dual of any concave operator is a hyponormal contraction (see
[
34
, Equation (26)]
). Later this fact was generalized in
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
by deducing power hyponormality of the Cauchy dual of any concave operator. Around the same time CDSP was settled affirmatively
for
\Delta_{T}
-regular
2
-isometries in
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
and for
2
-isometric operator-valued weighted shifts in
[
5
, Theorems 2.5 and 3.3]
(see also
[
14
, Corollary 6.2]
for the solution for yet another subclass of
2
-isometries). Further, it was shown in
[
5
, Examples 6.6 and 7.10]
that there exist
2
-isometric weighted shifts on directed trees (that include adjacency operators) whose Cauchy dual is not necessarily subnormal. Recently, a class of cyclic
2
-isometric composition operators without subnormal Cauchy dual has been exhibited in
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
.

For a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H},
let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denote the
C^{*}
-algebra of bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}.
A bounded linear operator
T
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is
cyclic
if there exists a vector
f\in\mathcal{H},
called a
cyclic vector
of
T,
such that

\bigvee\{T^{n}f:n\geqslant 0\}=\mathcal{H}.

We say that
T
is
analytic
if
\cap_{n\geqslant 0}T^{n}\mathcal{H}=\{0\}.

Following
[
33
]
, the
Cauchy dual

T^{\prime}
of a left-invertible
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is defined by
T^{\prime}=T(T^{*}T)^{-1}.

An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a
2
-isometry
if

\displaystyle I-2T^{*}T+T^{*2}T^{2}=0.

An operator
T
is said to be a
concave operator
if
I-2T^{*}T+T^{*2}T^{2}\leqslant 0.

Clearly, any
2
-isometry is a concave operator.
By
[
27
, Lemma 1]
, a concave operator
T
is
norm increasing
(that is,
T^{*}T\geqslant I
), and hence the Cauchy dual of
T
is well-defined (see
[
2
,
27
]
for the basic properties of
2
-isometries and concave operators). An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be
subnormal
if there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
containing
\mathcal{H}
and a normal operator
N\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
such that
Nx=Tx
for every
x\in\mathcal{H}
(the reader is referred to
[
16
]
for an excellent exposition on the theory of subnormal operators).
With these notions, one can now state
the Cauchy dual subnormality problem. This problem asks
when is the Cauchy dual
T^{\prime}
of a cyclic
2
-isometry
T
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
subnormal?

For a finite positive Borel measure
\mu
on the unit circle
\mathbb{T},
the
Dirichlet-type space

\mathcal{D}(\mu)
is defined by

D(\mu):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D}):\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}P_{\mu}(z)\,dA(z)<\infty\right\},

where

P_{\mu}(z)
denotes the
Poisson integral

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|z-\zeta|^{2}}d\mu(\zeta)
of the measure
\mu,

dA(z)
denotes the
normalized Lebesgue area measure
on the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D})
denotes the space of complex valued holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}.

It is well-known that any cyclic analytic
2
-isometry is unitarily equivalent to the operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
of multiplication by the coordinate function
z
on a Dirichlet-type space
D(\mu)
for some finite positive Borel measure
\mu
on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
(see
[
27
, Theorems 3.7 and 5.1]
).
In view of this model theorem and the fact that any
2
-isometry decomposes into a direct sum of an analytic
2
-isometry and a unitary (see
[
33
, Proposition 3.4]
), the forgoing problem is equivalent to the following variant of CDSP.

Problem

(CDSP)
.

Classify all finite positive Borel measures
\mu
on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
for which the Cauchy dual
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
of the multiplication operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on the Dirichlet-type space
\mathscr{D}(\mu)
is subnormal.

The counter-example to CDSP, as constructed in
[
6
, Section 4]
, is a cyclic
2
-isometry
T
for which the range of
T^{*}T-I
is of infinite dimension. This construction poses the following question: Whether or not CDSP has affirmative answer for all cyclic
2
-isometries for which
T^{*}T-I
is of finite rank? It turns out that all such
2
-isometries arise as the multiplication operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
acting on a Dirichlet-type space associated with a positive finitely supported measure (see Theorem
6.1
). Since every analytic norm increasing operator is unitarily equivalent to the multiplication operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
acting on a de Branges-Rovnyak space (see
[
23
, Theorem 4.6]
and Lemma
3.4
),
the above problem for Dirichlet-type spaces associated with finitely supported measures can be addressed by examining de Branges-Rovnyak spaces of finite rank. This is precisely the purpose of the present paper.

2.
Main Results

For complex separable Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{E},
let

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})
denote the Banach space of bounded linear transformations from
\mathcal{D}
into
\mathcal{E}.

The
Schur class

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})=\{B:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})\,|\,B\mbox{~is~holomorphic~with~}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|B(z)\|_{{}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})}}\leqslant 1\}.

In case
\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{C}=\mathcal{E},
the Schur class is equal to the closed unit ball of
the space
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
of bounded holomorphic functions on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
.
For any
B\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}),
the
de Branges-Rovnyak space

\mathcal{H}(B)
is the reproducing kernel Hilbert space associated with the
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
-valued positive semi-definite kernel

\displaystyle\kappa_{B}(z,w)=\frac{I_{\mathcal{E}}-B(z)B(w)^{*}}{1-z\overline{w}},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

The kernel
\kappa_{B}
is
normalized
if
\kappa_{B}(z,0)=I_{\mathcal{E}}
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
. This is equivalent to
B(0)=0.

If
\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{C}=\mathcal{E},
then we refer to
\mathcal{H}(B),
to be denoted by
\mathcal{H}(b),
as the
classical
de Branges-Rovnyak space (refer to
[
30
]
and
[
20
]
for the basic theory of classical de Branges-Rovnyak spaces).
Following
[
4
, Definition 1.1]
, the spaces
\mathcal{H}(B)
are referred to as
finite rank de Branges-Rovnyak spaces
provided
\mathcal{D}
is finite dimensional and
\mathcal{E}
is of dimension
1
. More generally, for a positive integer
k
and
B\in\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{D}},\mathcal{E}),

the de Branges-Rovnyak space
\mathcal{H}(B)
is said to be of of

rank

k
if

\displaystyle k~=

\displaystyle\inf\big\{\dim\,\tilde{\mathcal{D}}:\mbox{there exist a Hilbert space}~\tilde{\mathcal{D}}~\mbox{and~}\tilde{B}\in\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}},\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\mbox{such that~}\mathcal{H}(B)=\mathcal{H}(\tilde{B})~\mbox{with equality of norms}\big\},

where
\dim
denotes the Hilbert space dimension.
Assume that
\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{C}^{k}
and
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{C}.
Then, for
B=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})
,
z
is a
multiplier
, that is,
zf\in\mathcal{H}(B)
whenever
f\in\mathcal{H}(B)
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\big(1-\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\tilde{b}_{j}|^{2}\big)d\theta>-\infty,

where
d\theta
denotes the normalized Lebesgue measure on the unit circle and
\tilde{b}_{j}(\zeta)
denotes the non-tangential boundary value of
b_{j}
at
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}

(see
[
4
, Theorem 5.2]
and
[
20
, Corollary 20.20]
). In case of
k=1,
by de Leeuw–Rudin theorem,
z
is a multiplier if and only if
b
is a non-extreme point of the unit ball of
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
(see
[
19
, Theorem 12]
).

Let
B\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}).

If
z
is a multiplier, then by the closed graph theorem, the operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
of multiplication by
z
on
\mathcal{H}(B)
is bounded. In this case,
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is always a norm increasing operator, that is,

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{M}_{z}f\|\geqslant\|f\|,\quad f\in\mathcal{H}(B)

(2.1)

(see
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
and

[
23
, Pg 17]
). Conversely, every analytic norm increasing operator is unitarily equivalent to the operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
of multiplication by
z
acting on a de Branges-Rovnyak space
\mathcal{H}(B)
for some Schur-class function
B
(see
[
23
, Theorem 4.6]
and Lemma
3.4
).

Here is the main result of this paper concerning the CDSP for a family of finite rank de Branges-Rovnyak spaces.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
B=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C}^{k},\mathbb{C})
be such that
B(0)=0,
where

b_{j}(z)=\frac{p_{j}(z)}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}(z-\alpha_{j})}

for polynomials
p_{j}
of degree at most
k
and distinct numbers
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k}
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}.

For
r=1,\ldots,k,

let
a_{r}=\prod_{1\leq t\neq r\leq k}(\alpha_{r}-\alpha_{t}).

Assume that the operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
of multiplication by
z
on the de Branges-Rovnyak space
\mathcal{H}(B)
is bounded.
Then the Cauchy dual
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
of
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is subnormal if and only if the matrix

\displaystyle\sum_{r,t=1}^{k}\Big(\frac{1}{a_{r}\overline{a_{t}}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(\alpha_{r})\overline{p_{j}(\alpha_{t})}\Big)(1-\frac{1}{\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha}_{t}})^{l}\Big(\!\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha^{m+2}_{r}\overline{\alpha}^{n+2}_{t}}\Big)\!\Big)_{m,n\geqslant 0}~\mbox{~}

is formally positive semi-definite for every
l\geqslant 1.

Remark 2.2
.

One of the essential ingredients in the proof of Theorem
2.1
is Theorem
3.5
. The latter theorem can be used to extend Theorem
2.1
to the case in which the rational symbol
B
admits a pole of multiplicity bigger than
1.
However, the characterization in this case is not as elegant as we get when
B
admits simple poles.

As the first application of Theorem
2.1
, we obtain a handy criterion for the subnormality of the Cauchy dual of the multiplication operator under consideration.

Corollary 2.3
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.1
hold. If

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(\alpha_{r})\overline{p_{j}(\alpha_{t})}=0,\quad 1\leqslant r\neq t\leqslant k,

(2.2)

then
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is subnormal.

The following theorem provides an affirmative solution to CDSP for the Dirichlet-type spaces
\mathscr{D}(\mu)
associated with measures
\mu
supported on two antipodal points of the unit circle.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
and nonnegative numbers
c_{1},

c_{2},
let
\mathscr{D}(\mu)
be the Dirichlet-type space associated with the measure
\mu=c_{1}\delta_{\zeta}+c_{2}\delta_{-\zeta}
and let
\mathscr{M}_{z}
be the multiplication operator on
\mathscr{D}(\mu).
Then the Cauchy dual
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
of
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is a subnormal contraction.

3.
The Cauchy dual subnormality problem in de Branges-Rovnyak spaces

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an auxiliary complex separable Hilbert space.
Let
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of
\mathcal{E}
-valued holomorphic functions defined on the unit disc and let
\kappa:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\rightarrow B(\mathcal{E})
be the reproducing kernel for
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},
that is,
\kappa(\cdot,w)x\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
and

\displaystyle\langle{f},\,{\kappa(\cdot,w)x}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}}=\langle{f(w)},\,{x}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}},\quad f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},~w\in\mathbb{D},~x\in\mathcal{E}.

In the remaining part of this section, we assume the following:

(A1)

z
is a multiplier for
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},
that is,
zf\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
for every
f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},

(A2)

\kappa
is normalized at the origin, that is,
\kappa(z,0)=I_{\mathcal{E}}
for every
z\in\mathbb{D},

(A3)

Under the assumptions (A1) and (A2), the orthogonal complement of
\{zf:f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}\}
is spanned by the space of
\mathcal{E}
-valued constant functions.

(A4)

Under the assumption (A1),
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is left-invertible.

We refer to the pair
(\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},\mathcal{E})
satisfying (A1)-(A4) as the
functional Hilbert space
. The reader is referred to
[
18
,
10
,
4
,
32
,
36
]
for the basics of vector-valued reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, positive semi-definite kernels, Hausdorff moment problem, complex moment problem, completely monotone functions and related notions.

Remark 3.1
.

By (A1) and the closed graph theorem, the operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
of multiplication by
z
on
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
is bounded. Further, since
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
consists of
\mathcal{E}
-valued holomorphic functions,
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is analytic.
It is also clear from (A2) and (A3) that

\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}
is spanned by the space of
\mathcal{E}
-valued constant functions and
\|x\|_{\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}}=\|x\|_{\mathcal{E}}
for every
x\in\mathcal{E}.
The
backward shift operator

\mathscr{L}_{z}
given by

\displaystyle(\mathscr{L}_{z}f)(w)=\frac{f(w)-f(0)}{w},\quad f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},~w\in\mathbb{D}

is a well-defined bounded linear operator
on
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
that satisfies

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{\prime*}_{z}=\mathscr{L}_{z}.

(3.1)

Indeed, in view of the closed graph theorem, it suffices to check that
\mathscr{L}_{z}f
belongs to
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
for every
f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.
Since
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is left-invertible, the range of
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is closed. By (A2) and (A3),

\{zf:f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}\}=\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}\ominus\{\kappa(\cdot,0)x:x\in\mathcal{E}\}=\{g\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}:g(0)=0\}.

This shows that
\mathscr{L}_{z}
is bounded.
Moreover,
\mathscr{M}^{\prime*}_{z}\mathscr{M}_{z}=I=\mathscr{L}_{z}\mathscr{M}_{z}.

This together with
\mathscr{M}^{\prime*}_{z}x=0=\mathscr{L}_{z}x,

x\in\mathcal{E},
yields (
3.1
).

For a nonnegative integer
n,
let
e_{n}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow B(\mathcal{E})
be given by

\displaystyle e_{n}(z)x=\frac{\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa(z,w)x}{n!}\Big|_{w=0},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},~x\in\mathcal{E},

where
\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa(z,w)
denotes the
n
th partial derivative of
\kappa(z,w)
with respect to
\overline{w}.
After interchanging the roles of analytic and coanalytic functions, it is easy to deduce from
[
18
, Lemma 4.1]
that

\displaystyle e_{n}(\cdot)x\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}~\mbox{and~}\langle{f},\,{e_{n}(\cdot)x}\rangle=\Big\langle\frac{({\partial}^{n}f)(0)}{n!},\,x\Big\rangle,\quad f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},~x\in\mathcal{E}.

(3.2)

For integers
m,n\geqslant 0,
we use the short notation
{\partial^{m}\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa(0,0)}
to denote
{\partial^{m}\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa(w,w)}|_{w=0}.

Although the following is well-known (see
[
18
, Lemma 4.1]
and
[
12
, Remark 2]
),
we include a proof for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\kappa:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\rightarrow B(\mathcal{E})
be a kernel function given by

\displaystyle\kappa(z,w)=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}A_{m,n}z^{m}\overline{w}^{n},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D},

where
A_{m,n}\in B(\mathcal{E}).
Then
\kappa
is a positive semi-definite kernel if and only if the matrix
A=\Big(\!\!\Big(A_{m,n}\Big)\!\!\Big)_{m,n\geqslant 0}
is formally positive semi-definite, that is,
\Big(\!\!\Big(A_{m,n}\Big)\!\!\Big)_{0\leqslant m,n\leqslant k}
is positive semi-definite for every nonnegative integer
k.

Proof.

If
\kappa
is positive semi-definite, then
by
[
18
, Lemma 4.1(c)]
, the matrix
B=\Big(\!\!\Big({\partial^{m}\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa(0,0)}\Big)\!\!\Big)_{m,n\geqslant 0}
is formally positive semi-definite. If
C=\Big(\!\!\Big(\frac{\delta_{m,n}}{n!}\Big)\!\!\Big)_{m,n\geqslant 0},
then
A=C^{*}BC,
and hence

A
is also formally positive semi-definite. This
yields the necessity part. To see the sufficiency part, note that

\displaystyle\kappa_{l}(z,w):=\sum_{m,n=0}^{l}A_{m,n}z^{m}\overline{w}^{n}=X_{l}(z)AX_{l}(w)^{*},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D},~l\geqslant 0,

where
X_{l}(z)=(I_{\mathcal{E}},zI_{\mathcal{E}},\ldots,z^{l}I_{\mathcal{E}},0,\ldots):\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}.
Thus if
A
is formally positive semi-definite, then
\kappa_{l}
is a positive semi-definite kernel for every integer
l\geqslant 0.
Since the pointwise limit of positive semi-definite kernels is again positive semi-definite, the proof is complete.
∎

We need the following characterization of the subnormality of the Cauchy dual of multiplication operator
\mathcal{M}_{z}
acting on functional Hilbert spaces in the proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},\mathcal{E})
be a functional Hilbert space and let
\mathscr{M}_{z}
be the operator of multiplication by
z
on
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.
Assume that
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is norm-increasing.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The Cauchy dual
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
of
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is subnormal.

(ii)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}{k\choose j}\Big(\!\!\Big(\frac{\partial^{m+j}\overline{\partial}^{n+j}\kappa(0,0)}{(m+j)!(n+j)!}\Big)\!\!\Big)_{m,n\geqslant 0}
is formally positive semi-definite for every
k\geqslant 0
.

If, in addition,
\mathcal{E}
is one dimensional, then (1) and (2) are equivalent to

(iii)

\big\{\frac{\partial^{m}\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa(0,0)}{m!n!}\big\}_{m,n\geqslant 0}
is a complex moment sequence.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is norm-increasing.
To see the equivalence of (i) and (ii), note that
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is a contraction, and hence by Agler’s criterion
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
,
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is subnormal if and only if for every integer
k\geqslant 0,

\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}{k\choose j}\mathscr{M}^{\prime*j}_{z}\mathscr{M}^{\prime j}_{z}\geqslant 0.

One may now infer from
[
33
, Theorem 2.15]
that
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is subnormal if and only if for every integer
k\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}{k\choose j}\sigma^{*j}\kappa(z,w)

is formally positive semi-definite, where
\sigma^{*j}
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma^{*j}\kappa(z,w)=\sum_{m,n\geqslant 0}\frac{\partial^{m+j}\overline{\partial}^{n+j}\kappa(0,0)}{(m+j)!(n+j)!}z^{m}\overline{w}^{n},\quad j\geqslant 0.

The equivalence of (i) and (ii) is now immediate from Proposition
3.2
.

To see the remaining implications, let
\mathscr{L}_{z}
be the backward shift operator (see Remark
3.1
). We claim that

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}(e_{n}(\cdot)x)=e_{n+1}(\cdot)x~\mbox{ for every}~n\geqslant 0~\mbox{and}~x\in\mathcal{E}.

(3.3)

Note that for any
f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},
by (
3.2
) (applied twice),

\displaystyle\langle{f},\,{\mathscr{L}^{*}_{z}e_{n}(\cdot)x}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathscr{L}_{z}(f),\,{e_{n}(\cdot)x}\big\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}}=\Big\langle\frac{({\partial}^{n}\mathscr{L}_{z}(f))(0)}{n!},\,x\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle\frac{({\partial}^{n+1}f))(0)}{(n+1)!},\,x\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}=\langle{f},\,{e_{n+1}(\cdot)x}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}}.

Since
f
is arbitrary, an application of (
3.1
) yields (
3.3
).

To see the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii), note that by (
3.3
) and (
3.2
), for all integers
m,n\geqslant 0
and
x,y\in\mathcal{E},

\displaystyle\langle{\mathscr{M}^{\prime n}_{z}x},\,{\mathscr{M}^{\prime m}_{z}y}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{e_{n}(\cdot)x},\,{e_{m}(\cdot)y}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle\frac{({\partial}^{m}e_{n}(\cdot)x)(0)}{m!},\,y\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle\frac{\partial^{m}{\overline{\partial}}^{n}\kappa(0,0)}{m!n!}x,\,y\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}.

This shows that

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{E}}\mathscr{M}^{\prime*m}_{z}\mathscr{M}^{\prime n}_{z}|_{\mathcal{E}}=\frac{\partial^{m}{\overline{\partial}}^{n}\kappa(0,0)}{m!n!},\quad m,n\geqslant 0,

(3.4)

where
P_{\mathcal{E}}
denotes the orthogonal projection of
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
onto the space
\mathcal{E}
of constant functions in
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.

Assume now that
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is subnormal. By Bram’s characterization of subnormality
[
16
, 1.9 Theorem(a)(i)]
,
there exists a semispectral measure
Q
compactly supported in
\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{\prime*m}_{z}\mathscr{M}^{\prime n}_{z}=\int z^{n}\overline{z}^{m}dQ(z),\quad m,n\geqslant 0.

It now follows from (
3.4
) that
\big\{\frac{\partial^{m}\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa(0,0)}{m!n!}\big\}_{m,n\geqslant 0}
is a complex moment sequence with the representing measure
P_{\mathcal{E}}Q(\cdot)|_{\mathcal{E}}.
This gives the implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(3) (here we do not need the assumption that
\mathcal{E}
is one dimensional).

To see the implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i), assume that the dimension of
\mathcal{E}=\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}
is
1.
By
[
33
, Corollary 2.8]
, the Cauchy dual of
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is cyclic with cyclic vector in
\mathcal{E},
and hence
one may apply
[
35
, Theorem 35]
to complete the verification of (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i).
∎

We already noted that every analytic norm increasing operator can be modeled as the operator of multiplication by
z
on a de Branges-Rovnyak space (see
[
4
, Proposition 2.1]
and
[
23
, Theorem 4.6]
for generalizations). In view of the Shimorin’s model theorem (see
[
33
]
), the forgoing fact can be obtained using the reproducing kernel space techniques (see
[
4
]
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},\mathcal{E})
be a functional Hilbert space and let
\mathscr{M}_{z}
be the operator of multiplication by
z
on

\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.
If
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is norm increasing, then there exist a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{D}
and
B\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})
such that

\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
coincides with
\mathcal{H}(B)
with equality of norms.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}\mathscr{M}_{z}\geqslant I
and let

P_{\mbox{ran}\,\mathscr{M}_{z}}
denote the orthogonal projection of
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
onto the
\mbox{ran}\,\mathscr{M}_{z}.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{z}\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}-P_{\mbox{ran}\,\mathscr{M}_{z}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{z}\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}-\mathscr{M}_{z}\mathscr{M}^{\prime*}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{z}(I-(\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}\mathscr{M}_{z})^{-1})\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z},

which is positive.
It follows that for any
f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}f\|^{2}\geqslant\|P_{\mbox{ran}\,\mathscr{M}_{z}}f\|^{2}=\|f-f(0)\|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}-\|f(0)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}},

where we used the fact that
f-f(0)
and
f(0)
are orthogonal in
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
.
Letting
f=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\kappa(\cdot,\omega_{j})c_{j}
for
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in\mathcal{E}
and
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}\in\mathbb{D},
note further that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{\omega}_{i}\omega_{j}\langle{\kappa(\omega_{j},\omega_{i})c_{i}},\,{{c}_{j}}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle{\kappa(\omega_{j},\omega_{i})c_{i}},\,{{c}_{j}}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle{c_{i}},\,{c_{j}}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle{\big(\kappa(\omega_{j},\omega_{i})-I_{\mathcal{E}}\big)c_{i}},\,{{c}_{j}}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}.

Thus
\eta(z,w):=z\overline{w}\kappa(z,w)-(\kappa(z,w)-I_{\mathcal{E}}),

z,w\in\mathbb{D},
is a positive semi-definite kernel. The existence of
B:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow B(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})
such that
\eta(z,w)=B(z)B(w)^{*},

z,w\in\mathbb{D},
now follows from the factorization theorem for positive semi-definite kernels (see
[
3
, Theorem 2.62]
).
Moreover, since
\eta(z,z)\leqslant I_{\mathcal{E}}

for every

z\in\mathbb{D},

B\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}).
Finally, note that

\kappa(z,w)=\frac{I_{\mathcal{E}}-B(z)B(w)^{*}}{1-z\overline{w}}
for every
z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

∎

In view of Lemma
3.4
, the Cauchy dual subnormality problem for analytic norm increasing operators reduces to the same problem for the operator of multiplication by
z
on de Branges-Rovnyak spaces.
The main result of this section characterizes subnormality of the Cauchy dual of the multiplication operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on de Branges-Rovnyak spaces.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
B(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}B_{j}z^{j}
belong to the Schur class
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}).
Assume that the operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
of multiplication by
z
on
\mathcal{H}(B)
is bounded and the orthogonal complement of
\{zf:f\in\mathcal{H}(B)\}
is spanned by the space of
\mathcal{E}
-valued constant functions.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The Cauchy dual
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
of
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is subnormal.

(ii)

The matrix
\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}{k\choose j}\big(\!\!\big(B_{m+1+j}\,B^{*}_{n+1+j}\big)\!\!\big)_{m,n\geqslant 0}
is formally positive semi-definite for every
k\geqslant 1.

(iii)

There exists a
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E}))
-valued semi-spectral measure
F
supported in
[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\big(\!\!\big(B_{m+1+j}\,B^{*}_{n+1+j}\big)\!\!\big)_{m,n\geqslant 0}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{j}F(dt),\quad j\geqslant 0.

Proof.

Let
\kappa_{B}
denote the reproducing kernel for
\mathcal{H}(B).

Note that

\displaystyle\kappa_{B}(z,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\overline{w}^{n}I_{\mathcal{E}}-B(z)B(w)^{*}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\overline{w}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\overline{w}^{n}I_{\mathcal{E}}-\Big(\sum_{j,k=1}^{\infty}B_{j}B^{*}_{k}\,z^{j}\overline{w}^{k}\Big)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\overline{w}^{n}.

Comparing the coefficients of
z^{m}\overline{w}^{n},

m,n\geqslant 0,
on the both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{m}\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa_{B}(0,0)}{m!n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{m,n}I_{\mathcal{E}}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}B_{j}{B^{*}_{k}}\delta_{m-j,n-k}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\delta_{m,n}I_{\mathcal{E}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}B_{m-n+k}{B^{*}_{k}}&\mbox{if~}m\geqslant n,\\
\delta_{m,n}I_{\mathcal{E}}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}B_{j}{B^{*}_{n-m+j}}&\mbox{if~}m<n,\end{cases}

where
\delta_{m,n}
denotes the Kronecker delta and the convention that sum over the empty set is
0
is used. Set

\displaystyle f_{k}(m,n)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}{k\choose j}\frac{\partial^{m+j}\overline{\partial}^{n+j}\kappa_{B}(0,0)}{(m+j)!(n+j)!},\quad k\geqslant 1,~m,n\geqslant 0.

A routine verification using
{n\choose k}+{n\choose k-1}={n+1\choose k},

n\geqslant k
, shows that

\displaystyle f_{k+1}(m,n)=f_{k}(m,n)-f_{k}(m+1,n+1),\quad k\geqslant 1,~m,n\geqslant 0.

(3.6)

We verify by induction on
k\geqslant 1
that

\displaystyle f_{k}(m,n)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}{k-1\choose j}B_{m+1+j}B^{*}_{n+1+j},\quad m,n\geqslant 0.\quad

(3.7)

By (
3.5
), for integers
m,n\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle f_{1}(m,n)=\frac{\partial^{m}\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa_{B}(0,0)}{m!n!}-\frac{\partial^{m+1}\overline{\partial}^{n+1}\kappa_{B}(0,0)}{(m+1)!(n+1)!}=B_{m+1}B^{*}_{n+1}.

Thus (
3.7
) holds for
k=1.
If (
3.7
) holds for
k\geqslant 1,
then by (
3.6
),

\displaystyle f_{k+1}(m,n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}{k-1\choose j}B_{m+1+j}B^{*}_{n+1+j}-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}{k-1\choose j}B_{m+2+j}B^{*}_{n+2+j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}{k\choose j}B_{m+1+j}B^{*}_{n+1+j}.

This completes the verification of (
3.7
).

To complete the proof, recall that

\mathscr{M}_{z}
is norm increasing (see (
2.1
)) or equivalently
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is a contraction. One may now apply Theorem
3.3
together with (
3.7
) to obtain the equivalence of (i) and (ii). In view of the polarization technique (see
[
21
, Proof of Theorem 4.2]
), the equivalence of (ii) and (iii) follows from the solution of the Hausdorff moment problem (cf
[
10
, Proposition 6.11, Chapter 4]
) provided we show that
\big(\!\!\big(B_{m+1+j}B^{*}_{n+1+j}\big)\!\!\big)_{m,n\geqslant 0}
defines a bounded linear operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E})
for every integer
j\geqslant 0.
Since
B
belongs to
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}),

the function
B(\cdot)x
belongs to
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}).
Thus the linear map
C:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E})
given by
C(x)=(B_{k}x)_{k\geqslant 1}
is well-defined.
The boundedness of
C
now follows either from the assumption that
B\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})
or from the closed graph theorem. Since
\big(\!\!\big(B_{m}B^{*}_{n}\big)\!\!\big)_{m,n\geqslant 1}=CC^{*},
the desired equivalence is immediate.
∎

As an application, we characterize the subnormality of the Cauchy dual of the multiplication operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on finite rank de Branges-Rovnyak spaces.

.

For a positive integer

let

belong to the Schur class

Assume that the operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
of multiplication by

on

is bounded.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The Cauchy dual
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
of
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is subnormal.

(ii)

The matrix
\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}{k\choose j}\big(\!\!\big(B_{m+1+j}\,B^{*}_{n+1+j}\big)\!\!\big)_{m,n\geqslant 0}
is formally positive semi-definite for every

(iii)

There exists a
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}))
-valued semi-spectral measure
F
supported in
[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\big(\!\!\big(B_{m+1+j}\,B^{*}_{n+1+j}\big)\!\!\big)_{m,n\geqslant 0}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{j}F(dt),\quad j\geqslant 0.

In view of Theorem

, it suffices to check that the orthogonal complement of

4.
The proof of the main theorem and its consequences

We now complete the proof of the main result stated in Section 2.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Write
B(z)=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}B_{l}z^{l},

|z|<1.
Since
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}
are distinct, by
[
25
, Proposition 2.1]
, for any polynomial
p
of degree less than
k,

\displaystyle\frac{p(z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(z-\alpha_{i})}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{p(\alpha_{i})}{a_{i}}\frac{1}{z-\alpha_{i}}.

Applying this to
\frac{p_{j}(z)}{z}
for every
j=1,\ldots,k,
we obtain

\displaystyle b_{j}(z)=\frac{p_{j}(z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(z-\alpha_{i})}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{p_{j}(\alpha_{i})}{\alpha_{i}a_{i}}\frac{z}{z-\alpha_{i}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{p_{j}(\alpha_{i})}{\alpha_{i}a_{i}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{l+1}}{\alpha^{l+1}_{i}}.

(4.1)

For
j=1,\ldots,k,
let
b_{j}(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}b_{j,m}z^{m},

z\in\mathbb{D}
and note that
B_{m}=(b_{1,m},\ldots,b_{k,m}),

m\geqslant 1.

By (
4.1
), for any
s\geqslant 0
and
m\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle b_{j,m+1+s}=-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{p_{j}(\alpha_{i})}{a_{i}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{m+s+2}_{i}},

and hence

\displaystyle B_{m+1+s}\,B^{*}_{n+1+s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j,m+1+s}\,\overline{b_{j,n+1+s}}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big(\sum_{r=1}^{k}\frac{p_{j}(\alpha_{r})}{a_{r}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{m+s+2}_{r}}\Big)\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{k}\frac{\overline{p_{j}(\alpha_{t})}}{\overline{a_{t}}}\frac{1}{\overline{\alpha}^{n+s+2}_{t}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r,t=1}^{k}\Big(\frac{1}{a_{r}\overline{a_{t}}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(\alpha_{r})\overline{p_{j}(\alpha_{t})}\Big)\frac{1}{\alpha^{m+2+s}_{r}\overline{\alpha}^{n+2+s}_{t}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{l}(-1)^{s}{l\choose s}\frac{1}{(\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha}_{t})^{s}}=(1-\frac{1}{\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha}_{t}})^{l},\quad l\geqslant 1,

and hence by (
4.2
), for
m,n\geqslant 0,
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{l}(-1)^{s}{l\choose s}B_{m+1+s}\,B^{*}_{n+1+s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r,t=1}^{k}\frac{\Big(\frac{1}{a_{r}\overline{a_{t}}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(\alpha_{r})\overline{p_{j}(\alpha_{t})}\Big)}{\alpha^{m+2}_{r}\overline{\alpha}^{n+2}_{t}}(1-\frac{1}{\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha}_{t}})^{l}.

This together with (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) of Theorem
3.5
completes the proof.
∎

In the remaining part of this section, we present some applications of Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Corollary
2.3
.

By (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\sum_{r,t=1}^{k}\Big(\frac{1}{a_{r}\overline{a_{t}}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(\alpha_{r})\overline{p_{j}(\alpha_{t})}\Big)(1-\frac{1}{\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha}_{t}})^{l}\Big(\!\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha^{m+2}_{r}\overline{\alpha}^{n+2}_{t}}\Big)\!\Big)_{m,n\geqslant 0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\frac{(1-\frac{1}{|\alpha_{r}|^{2}})^{l}}{|a_{r}|^{2}|\alpha_{r}|^{4}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}|p_{j}(\alpha_{r})|^{2}\Big(\!\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha^{m}_{r}\overline{\alpha}^{n}_{r}}\Big)\!\Big)_{m,n\geqslant 0}.

Since
|\alpha_{r}|>1,
in view of Theorem
2.1
, it suffices to check that
\Big(\!\!\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha^{m}_{r}\overline{\alpha}^{n}_{r}}\Big)\!\!\Big)_{m,n\geqslant 0}
is formally positive semi-definite for every
r=1,\ldots,k
.
Since this matrix is equal to
V_{r}V^{*}_{r}
with
V_{r}
denoting the column vector
\Big(\!\!\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha^{m}_{r}}\Big)\!\!\Big)_{m\geqslant 0},
the desired conclusion is immediate.
∎

We provide below a necessary condition for the subnormality of the Cauchy dual of the multiplication operator on a de Branges-Rovnyak space.

Corollary 4.1
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.1
hold. If
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is subnormal, then

\sum_{r,t=1}^{k}\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}_{r}\overline{\alpha}^{2}_{t}a_{r}\overline{a_{t}}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(\alpha_{r})\overline{p_{j}(\alpha_{t})}\Big)\delta_{\!\frac{1}{\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha}_{t}}}

is a positive measure supported in
[0,1].

Proof.

Assume that
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is subnormal and let

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}=\sum_{r,t=1}^{k}\Big(\frac{1}{a_{r}\overline{a_{t}}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(\alpha_{r})\overline{p_{j}(\alpha_{t})}\Big)\frac{1}{\alpha^{m+2}_{r}\overline{\alpha}^{m+2}_{t}},\quad m\geqslant 0.

By Theorem
2.1
and (
4.2
), the sequence
\{\gamma_{m}\}_{m\geqslant 0}
is completely monotone. Hence, by the solution of the Hausdorff moment problem (see
[
10
]
), there exists a finite positive Borel measure
\nu
on
[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{m}d\nu(t),\quad m\geqslant 0.

On the other hand, if
K=[0,1]\cup\{\frac{1}{\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha}_{t}}:1\leqslant r,t\leqslant k\}
and

\mu:=\sum_{r,t=1}^{k}\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}_{r}\overline{\alpha}^{2}_{t}a_{r}\overline{a_{t}}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(\alpha_{r})\overline{p_{j}(\alpha_{t})}\Big)\delta_{\!\frac{1}{\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha}_{t}}},

then we get

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}=\int_{K}z^{m}d\mu,\quad m\geqslant 0.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{m}d\nu(t)=\int_{K}z^{m}d\mu,\quad m\geqslant 0.

Since
K
is a compact set with connected complement in
\mathbb{C},
by Mergelyan’s Theorem (see
[
29
, 20.5 Theorem]
), any continuous function on
K
can be approximated uniformly by polynomials in
z.
By the Riesz representation theorem (see
[
29
, 6.19 Theorem]
),
\mu
is necessarily supported in
[0,1]
and it coincides with
\nu.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

For
1\leqslant r,t\leqslant k,
let
A_{r,t}=\{(u,v):\alpha_{u}\overline{\alpha}_{v}=\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha}_{t}\}
and

c_{r,t}:=\sum_{(u,v)\in A_{r,t}}\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}_{u}\overline{\alpha}^{2}_{v}a_{u}\overline{a_{v}}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(\alpha_{u})\overline{p_{j}(\alpha_{v})}\Big).

If the hypotheses of Theorem
2.1
hold, then the following possibilities occur:

(i)

If
\frac{1}{\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha}_{t}}\notin[0,1],
then
c_{r,t}=0.

(ii)

If
\frac{1}{\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha}_{t}}\in[0,1],
then
c_{r,t}\geqslant 0.

Combining Corollary
2.3
with Remark
4.2
(i) yields the following.

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
2.1
. If
{\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha}_{t}}
’s are distinct complex numbers belonging to
\mathbb{C}\setminus[1,\infty)
for every
1\leqslant r\neq t\leqslant k,
then
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is subnormal if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(\alpha_{r})\overline{p_{j}(\alpha_{t})}=0,\quad 1\leqslant r\neq t\leqslant k.

5.
Classical de Branges-Rovnyak spaces

Let
b
be a non-extreme point of the closed unit ball of
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}).

It is well-known that there exists a unique outer function
a\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
(that is,
\bigvee\{z^{n}a:n\geqslant 0\}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
) such that
|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}=1
almost everywhere on unit circle and
a(0)>0
(see
[
20
, Chapter 23, Section 1]
). We refer to
a
as the
mate
of
b.
The following lemma provides a formula for the reproducing kernels of the so-called the Cauchy dual of classical de Branges-Rovnyak spaces.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
b
be a nonextreme point of the closed unit ball of
H^{\infty}
such that
b(0)=0
and let
a
be the mate of
b.
Let
\phi=\frac{b}{a}.

Then the Cauchy dual
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
of
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is unitarily equivalent to the operator of multiplication by
z
on the reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa^{\prime}_{b}},
where
\kappa^{\prime}_{b}
is given by

\displaystyle\kappa^{\prime}_{b}(z,w)=\frac{1+\phi(z)\overline{\phi(w)}}{1-z\overline{w}},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

Proof.

Let
\phi(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{j}z^{j}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}.
By
[
15
, Lemma 3.2]
,

\displaystyle\langle{z^{m}},\,{z^{n}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}(b)}=\begin{cases}\delta_{m,n}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\overline{c_{m-n+k}}{c_{k}}&\mbox{if~}m\geqslant n,\\
\sum_{j=0}^{m}\overline{c_{j}}{c_{n-m+j}}&\mbox{if~}m<n,\end{cases}

(5.1)

Since
\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}
is spanned by
1
and
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is cyclic with cyclic vector
1
(see
[
20
]
and
[
4
, Theorems 1.3 and 5.5]
), by
[
33
, Corollary 2.8]
,
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is analytic.
One may now apply
[
33
, Corollary 2.14]
to
T=\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
to conclude that
\kappa^{\prime}_{b}
is given by

\displaystyle\kappa^{\prime}_{b}(z,w)=\sum_{m,n\geqslant 0}\langle{z^{m}},\,{z^{n}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}(b)}z^{n}\overline{w}^{m},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

It is now easy to see using (
5.1
) that
\kappa^{\prime}_{b}
has the desired formula (see the proof of Theorem
3.5
for a similar argument).
∎

The main result of this section provides an affirmative solution of the Cauchy dual subnormality problem for classical de Branges-Rovnyak spaces (cf.
[
8
, Corollary 3.6]
and
[
5
, Theorem 3.3]
).

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
b
be a nonextreme point of the closed unit ball of
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
such that
b(0)=0.
If
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathcal{H}(b)
is concave, then the Cauchy dual
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
of
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is a subnormal contraction. Moreover, the following hold:

(i)

The sequence
\big\{\frac{\partial^{m}\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa_{b}(0,0)}{m!n!}\big\}_{m,n\geqslant 0}
is a complex moment sequence with the representing measure
\mu
given by

\displaystyle\Big(1-\nu\Big(2\,\Re\Big(\frac{1}{1-e^{-i\theta}\beta}\Big)-1\Big)\Big)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}+\nu\,\delta_{\beta},

where
\nu=\frac{|\gamma|^{2}}{1-|\beta|^{2}}
for some scalars
\gamma,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\beta|<1.

(ii)

\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is unitarily equivalent to the operator of multiplication by
z
on the reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa^{\prime}_{b}},
where
\kappa^{\prime}_{b}
is given by

\displaystyle\kappa^{\prime}_{b}(z,w)=\frac{1+\frac{|\gamma|^{2}z\overline{w}}{(\rho-\sigma z)(\overline{\rho}-\overline{\sigma w})}}{1-z\overline{w}},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}

for some scalars
\sigma,\rho\in\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathcal{H}(b)
is concave. By
[
22
, Theorem 1]
and the assumption
b(0)=0,
there exist scalars
\gamma,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|\beta|<1
and

\displaystyle{b}(z)=\frac{\gamma z}{1-\beta z},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

It now follows from Corollary
2.3
that
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is a subnormal contraction.

Note that

b(z)=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}b_{n}z^{n},
where
b_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle b_{n}=\gamma\beta^{n-1},\quad n\geqslant 1.

(5.2)

To see (i), note that
by (
3.5
) and (
5.2
),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{m}\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa_{b}(0,0)}{m!n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\delta_{m,n}-|\gamma|^{2}\beta^{m-n}\frac{1-|\beta|^{2n}}{1-|\beta|^{2}}&\mbox{if~}m\geqslant n,\\
\delta_{m,n}-|\gamma|^{2}\overline{\beta}^{{}^{n-m}}\frac{1-|\beta|^{2m}}{1-|\beta|^{2}}&\mbox{if~}m<n.\end{cases}

Since for any integer
\ell\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{i(m-n)\theta}\Re\Big(\frac{e^{-i\ell\theta}}{1-e^{-i\theta}\beta}\Big)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}=\begin{cases}\beta^{m-n-\ell}&\mbox{if~}m>n,\\
2\delta_{0,\ell}&\mbox{if~}m=n,\\
\overline{\beta}^{n-m-\ell}&\mbox{if~}m<n.\end{cases}

(5.3)

it can be easily seen that
\mu,
as given in part (i), is the representing measure of
\big\{\frac{\partial^{m}\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa_{b}(0,0)}{m!n!}\big\}_{m,n\geqslant 0}.

To see part (ii), note that by
[
22
, Lemma 6]
, the mate
a
of
b
is given by

\displaystyle a(z)=\frac{\rho-\sigma z}{1-\beta z},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

for some
\rho,\sigma\in\mathbb{C}.
The desired formula now
follows from Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The conclusion of Theorem
5.2
can be extended to Schur functions which do not necessarily vanish at the origin. Indeed, by
[
23
, Lemma 4.7]
,
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathcal{H}(b)
is unitarily equivalent to the operator
{\mathscr{M}}_{z}
of multiplication by
z
on
\mathcal{H}(\tilde{b}),
where
\tilde{b}(0)=0.
Thus the Cauchy dual
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
of any concave multiplication operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathcal{H}(b)
is a subnormal contraction. Moreover, by
[
22
, Theorem 1]
,

\displaystyle{b}(z)=\frac{c+\gamma z}{1-\beta z},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}

for some
c,\gamma,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\beta|<1.
One may now argue as above using (
5.3
) to see that the sequence
\big\{\frac{\partial^{m}\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa_{b}(0,0)}{m!n!}\big\}_{m,n\geqslant 0}
is a complex moment sequence with the representing measure
\mu
given by

\displaystyle\Big(1-(\nu+|c|^{2})\Big(2\,\Re\Big(\frac{1}{1-e^{-i\theta}\beta}\Big)-1\Big)-2\gamma\,\overline{c}\,\Re\Big(\frac{e^{-i\theta}}{1-e^{-i\theta}\beta}\Big)\Big)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}+\nu\,\delta_{\beta},

where
\nu=\frac{|c\beta+\gamma|^{2}}{1-|\beta|^{2}}
for some scalars
c,\gamma,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\beta|<1.

We conclude this section with an application to Dirichlet-type spaces associated with measures supported at a point.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\lambda
be a point in the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
and let
\tau
be a positive number. Then the Cauchy dual
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
of
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on the
Dirichlet-type space
\mathscr{D}(\tau\delta_{\lambda})
is a subnormal contraction.

Proof.

By
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
, there exist
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with

and
\eta\in(0,1)
satisfying

such that

\mathscr{D}(\tau\delta_{\lambda})
coincides with the de Branges-Rovnyak space
\mathcal{H}(b_{\alpha,\eta}),
where
b_{\alpha,\eta}
is given by

\displaystyle b_{\alpha,\eta}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{\eta}\alpha\overline{\lambda}z}{1-\eta\overline{\lambda}z},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Since
\mathscr{M}_{z}
acting on
\mathscr{D}(\tau\delta_{\lambda})
is a

-isometry (see
[

),
by Theorem

, the Cauchy dual of
\mathscr{M}_{z}

∎

6.
Dirichlet-type spaces associated with finitely supported measures

In this section, we discuss cyclic analytic
2
-isometries
T
with finite rank defect operators
\Delta_{T}:=T^{*}T-I
and their relationship with Dirichlet-type spaces associated with finitely supported measures.
In particular, we classify cyclic analytic
2
-isometries with finite rank defect operators
\Delta_{T}
and also
\Delta_{T}
-regular cyclic analytic
2
-isometries.
Recall that for any
2
-isometry,
\Delta_{T}
is a positive operator (see
[
26
, Lemma 1]
).
Following
[
24
]
, we say that a norm increasing operator
T
is
\Delta_{T}
-regular
if
\Delta_{T}T=\Delta^{1/2}_{T}T\Delta^{1/2}_{T}.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a cyclic analytic
2
-isometry and let
\Delta_{T}:=T^{*}T-I
. Then the following statements are true:

(i)

Let
k
be a positive integer. The rank of
\Delta_{T}
is
k
if and only if there exist distinct points
\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{k}
on the unit circle and positive numbers
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}
such that
T
is unitarily equivalent to the multiplication operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathscr{D}(\mu),
where
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\delta_{\zeta_{j}}.

(ii)

The operator
T
is
\Delta_{T}
-regular
if and only if
T
is unitarily equivalent to the multiplication operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathscr{D}(c\,\delta_{\zeta})
for some scalar
c\geqslant 0
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}.

Proof.

In view of the model theorem of Richter for cyclic analytic
2
-isometries (see
[
27
, Theorem 5.1]
), we may assume that
T=\mathscr{M}_{z}
acting on a Dirichlet-type space
\mathscr{D}(\mu)
for some finite positive Borel measure
\mu
on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. One may argue as in the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 1.26]
to see that with respect to the decomposition
\mathscr{D}(\mu)=\ker\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}\oplus\overline{\mbox{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}},

\mathscr{M}_{z}
and
\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}
decompose as follows:

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{z}=\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}S&E\\
0&W\end{smallmatrix}\Big),\quad\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}=\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&D\end{smallmatrix}\Big),

(6.1)

where
S
is an isometry,
S^{*}E=0
and
D
is an injective positive operator such that
W^{*}DW=D.

(i) Assume that
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\delta_{\zeta_{j}}.

It is well known that the point evaluation at
\zeta_{j},

j=1,\ldots,k,
is bounded on
\mathscr{D}(\mu)
(see
[
28
, Corollary 2.3]
). Thus there exist linearly independent vectors

\kappa_{\zeta_{1}},\ldots,\kappa_{\zeta_{k}}\in\mathscr{D}(\mu)
such that

\displaystyle\langle{f},\,{\kappa_{\zeta_{j}}}\rangle=f(\zeta_{j}),\quad f\in\mathscr{D}(\mu),~j=1,\ldots,k.

By
[
28
, Corollary 2.3]
, for any
f\in\mathscr{D}(\mu),

\displaystyle\langle{\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}f},\,{f}\rangle=\|zf\|^{2}-\|f\|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(\zeta)|^{2}d\mu(\zeta)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}|\langle{f},\,{\kappa_{\zeta_{j}}}\rangle|^{2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mbox{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}=\bigvee\{\kappa_{\zeta_{j}}:j=1,\ldots,k\}.

(6.2)

To see the converse, assume that the range of
\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}
is
k
-dimensional. Note that
D
is an invertible operator on the finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mbox{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}.
Hence,
D^{1/2}WD^{-1/2}
is a unitary operator on
\mbox{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}.
Consider the orthonormal basis of
\mbox{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}
consisting of eigenvectors
v_{j}
of
D^{1/2}WD^{-1/2}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\zeta_{j}\in\mathbb{T},

j=1,\ldots,k.

By
[
28
, Corollary 2.3]
and the polarization identity,

\displaystyle\langle{\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}\mathscr{M}^{n}_{z}1},\,{1}\rangle=\langle{z^{n+1}},\,{z}\rangle-\langle{z^{n}},\,{1}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\zeta^{n}d\mu(\zeta),\quad n\geqslant 0.

(6.3)

Let
P
denote the orthogonal projection of
\mathscr{D}(\mu)
onto

\mbox{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}.
Since the set
\{D^{-1/2}v_{j}:j=1,\ldots,k\}
forms a basis for
\mbox{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}},
there exist scalars
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

P1=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}D^{-1/2}v_{j}.

It now follows from (
6.3
) and (
6.1
) that for any integer
n\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\zeta^{n}d\mu(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{DW^{n}P1},\,{P1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\overline{\lambda}_{j}\langle{D^{1/2}W^{n}D^{-1/2}v_{i}},\,{v_{j}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\overline{\lambda}_{j}\zeta^{n}_{i}\langle{v_{i}},\,{v_{j}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}\zeta^{n}_{i}.

By the uniqueness of the trigonometric moment problem (which in turn follows from the density of trigonometric polynomials and the Riesz representation theorem
[
29
]
),
\mu
is equal to
\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}\delta_{\zeta_{i}}.
Since the range of
\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}
is
k
-dimensional, by the first half,
\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{k}
are all distinct. This completes the proof of (i).

(ii) If
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is an isometry, then the equivalence holds with
c=0.

Suppose that
\mu=c\,\delta_{\zeta}
for some scalar
c>0
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}.
By (i),

\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}
is one-dimensional, and hence by
[
8
, Corollary 3.6]
,

\mathscr{M}_{z}
is
\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}
-regular. This yields the sufficiency part.
To see the converse, assume now that
\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}
is nonzero and
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is
\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}
-regular. Hence, by

[
24
, Proposition 5.1]
,
E
as appearing in (
6.1
) is a one-to-one map from
\overline{\mbox{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}}
into
\ker\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}

such that
S^{*}E=0.
However, by
[
28
, Theorem 3.2]
,
\ker S^{*}
is of dimension
1.
It follows that the range of
E
is at most of dimension
1.
Since
E
is injective,
\mbox{ran}\,\Delta_{\mathscr{M}_{z}}
is one dimensional. The conclusion now follows from (i).
∎

B_{\sigma,e^{i\theta}}

The second result of this section describes the de Branges-Rovnyak model space of the Dirichlet-type spaces associated with finitely supported measures. We capitalize below on the algorithm for computing the reproducing kernel for Dirichlet-type spaces associated with finitely supported measures, as presented in
[
17
]
. A variant of this fact may also be deduced from
[
23
, Theorems 4.6
\&
6.2 and Lemma 8.3]
.

Theorem 6.4
.

For positive scalars
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}
and distinct points
\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{k}
on the unit circle
\mathbb{T},
consider the positive Borel measure
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\delta_{\zeta_{j}},
where
\delta_{\zeta_{j}}
denotes the Dirac delta measure supported at
\zeta_{j}.

Let
X(z)=(z,\ldots,z^{k})^{T}
and
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k}
denote the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{k}.
Then there exist
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}},
unique up to permutation, and a
k\times k
upper triangular matrix
P
such that
the Dirichlet-type space

D(\mu)
coincides with the de Branges-Rovnyak space
\mathcal{H}(B)
with equality of norms, where
B=(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\cdots,\frac{p_{k}}{q})
and

p_{j}(z)=\langle{PX(z)},\,{e_{j}}\rangle\quad j=1,\ldots,k,\quad q(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(z-\alpha_{j}),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Moreover,
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}
are governed by

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}|z-\zeta_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1,\ldots,k\\
l\neq j\end{subarray}}|z-\zeta_{l}|^{2}=\gamma\prod_{j=1}^{k}|z-\alpha_{j}|^{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{T}

(6.4)

for some
\gamma>0.

Proof.

It has been observed in
[
17
]
that there exist
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and
\gamma>0
such that (
6.4
) holds. Two applications of maximum modulus theorem shows that
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
are unique up to permutation.
Let
p(z)=\frac{e^{i\theta}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(z-\zeta_{j}),
where
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
is chosen such that
\frac{p(0)}{q(0)}>0.

Since the multiplication operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathscr{D}(\mu)
is an analytic norm increasing operator (see
[
27
, Theorem 3.6]
) and the reproducing kernel for
\mathscr{D}(\mu)
is normalized, by Lemma
3.4
, there exists a positive semi-definite kernel
\eta:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\eta(z,0)=0,\quad\kappa(z,w)=\frac{1-\eta(z,w)}{1-z\overline{w}},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

(6.5)

By
[
17
, Theorems 5.1 and 4.4]
, the reproducing kernel
\kappa(z,w)
for
\mathscr{D}(\mu)
is given by

\displaystyle\kappa(z,w)=\frac{O(z)\overline{O(w)}}{1-z\overline{w}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}f_{j}(z)\beta_{j}(w),\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D},

(6.6)

where
O,f_{j},\beta_{j}
are given by

\displaystyle O(z)=\frac{p(z)}{q(z)},\quad f_{j}(z)=\frac{O(z)}{O^{\prime}(\zeta_{j})(z-\zeta_{j})},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\overline{\beta_{1}(w)}\\
\vdots\\
\overline{\beta_{k}(w)}\end{pmatrix}=\Big((\!(\langle{f_{i}},\,{f_{j}}\rangle)\!)_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant k}\Big)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}(w)\\
\vdots\\
f_{k}(w)\end{pmatrix}.

Letting
(\!(b_{ij})\!)_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant k}
denote the inverse of
\!(\langle{f_{i}},\,{f_{j}}\rangle)\!)_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant k},
we obtain

\displaystyle\beta_{j}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\overline{b}_{ji}\,\overline{f_{i}(w)},\quad j=1,\ldots,k.

This combined with (
6.5
) and (
6.6
)

\displaystyle\frac{1-\eta(z,w)}{1-z\overline{w}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-z\overline{w}}\frac{p(z)\overline{p(w)}}{q(z)\overline{q(w)}}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\overline{b}_{ji}f_{j}(z)\overline{f_{i}(w)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p(z)\overline{p(w)}}{q(z)\overline{q(w)}}\Big(\frac{1}{1-z\overline{w}}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\frac{\overline{b}_{ji}}{O^{\prime}(\zeta_{j})\overline{O^{\prime}(\zeta_{i})}}\frac{1}{(z-\zeta_{j})(\overline{w}-\overline{\zeta_{i}})}\Big).

This yields

\displaystyle q(z)\,\eta(z,w)\,\overline{q(w)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!q(z)\overline{q(w)}-p(z)\overline{p(w)}\Big(1+(1-z\overline{w})\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\frac{\overline{b}_{ji}}{O^{\prime}(\zeta_{j})\overline{O^{\prime}(\zeta_{i})}}\frac{1}{(z-\zeta_{j})(\overline{w}-\overline{\zeta_{i}})}\Big).

Since the expression on the right hand side is a polynomial in
z
and
\overline{w},

there exists a matrix
\tilde{A}=(\!(a_{ij})\!)_{0\leqslant i,j\leqslant k}
such that

\displaystyle q(z)\,\eta(z,w)\,\overline{q(w)}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{k}a_{ij}z^{i}\overline{w}^{j},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

As
\eta(z,0)=0
for
z\in\mathbb{D},

\displaystyle q(z)\,\eta(z,w)\,\overline{q(w)}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}a_{ij}z^{i}\overline{w}^{j}=\langle{AX(z)},\,{X(w)}\rangle,\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D},

where
A
is the
k\times k
matrix obtained from
\tilde{A}
by removing first row and first column.
Further, since
q(z)\,\eta(z,w)\,\overline{q(w)}
is a positive semi-definite kernel, the matrix
A
is positive semi-definite (see Proposition
3.2
). By Cholesky’s decomposition (see
[
11
, Pg 2]
), there exists a
k\times k
upper triangular matrix
P
such that
A=P^{*}P.

It follows that

\displaystyle q(z)\,\eta(z,w)\,\overline{q(w)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{AX(z)},\,{X(w)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{PX(z)},\,{PX(w)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle{PX(z)},\,{e_{j}}\rangle\overline{\langle{PX(w)},\,{e_{j}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(z)\overline{p_{j}(w)}.

This shows that

\displaystyle\eta(z,w)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{p_{j}(z)}{q(z)}\frac{\overline{p_{j}(w)}}{\overline{q(w)}},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

As noticed in the above proof,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(z)\overline{p_{j}(w)}

(6.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(z)\overline{q(w)}-p(z)\overline{p(w)}\Big(1+\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\frac{\overline{b}_{ji}}{O^{\prime}(\zeta_{j})\overline{O^{\prime}(\zeta_{i})}}\frac{1-z\overline{w}}{(z-\zeta_{j})(\overline{w}-\overline{\zeta_{i}})}\Big),

where the matrix
(\!(b_{ij})\!)_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant k}
is the inverse of
(\!(\langle{f_{i}},\,{f_{j}}\rangle_{\mathscr{D}(\mu)})\!)_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant k}
and
f_{j}(z)=\frac{O(z)}{O^{\prime}(\zeta_{j})(z-\zeta_{j})},

j=1,\ldots,k.
Thus, for
1\leqslant r\neq t\leqslant k,
we have

\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}(\alpha_{r})\overline{p_{j}(\alpha_{t})}=0
if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\frac{\overline{b}_{ji}}{O^{\prime}(\zeta_{j})\overline{O^{\prime}(\zeta_{i})}}\frac{1}{(\alpha_{r}-\zeta_{j})(\overline{\alpha_{t}}-\overline{\zeta_{i}})}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{r}\overline{\alpha_{t}}-1}.

Note further that
by
[
17
, Lemmas 4.2 and 4.3]
,
\langle{f_{i}},\,{f_{j}}\rangle
is given by

\displaystyle\langle{f_{i}},\,{f_{j}}\rangle=\begin{cases}c_{i}\zeta_{i}f^{\prime}_{i}(\zeta_{i})&\mbox{if~}i=j,\\
\frac{1}{O^{\prime}(\zeta_{i})\overline{O^{\prime}(\zeta_{j})}(1-\zeta_{i}\overline{\zeta_{j}})}&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

7.
Dirichlet-type spaces associated with measures supported at antipodal points

In this section, we apply the results obtained in the previous sections to solve affirmatively the Cauchy dual subnormality problem for
Dirichlet-type spaces associated with measures supported at antipodal points.
We begin with a fact which allows us to reduce the above problem to the case in which the antipodal points are
1
and
-1.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\mu
be a finite positive Borel measure on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}.
For
\zeta\in\mathbb{T},
let
\mu_{\zeta}
be the finite positive Borel measure defined by
\mu_{\zeta}(\Delta)=\mu(\zeta\Delta)
for every Borel subset
\Delta
of
\mathbb{T}.
Let
\mathscr{M}_{z,\zeta}
denote the operator of multiplication by
z
on the Dirichlet-type space
\mathscr{D}(\mu_{\zeta}).
Then
\mathscr{M}_{z,\zeta}
is unitarily equivalent to
\overline{\zeta}\mathscr{M}_{z,1}.
In particular, we have the following:

(i)

The Cauchy dual operator
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z,\zeta}
of
\mathscr{M}_{z,\zeta}
is subnormal if and only if
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z,1}
is subnormal.

(ii)

If
\kappa
is the reproducing kernel for
\mathscr{D}(\mu),
then the reproducing kernel
\kappa_{\zeta}
for
\mathscr{D}(\mu_{\zeta})
is given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{\zeta}(z,w)=\kappa(\zeta z,\zeta w),\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

Proof.

Note that for any
z\in\mathbb{D},
by the
\mathbb{T}
-invariance of the Poisson kernel
P(z,\lambda)=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|z-\lambda|^{2}}
,
z\in\mathbb{D},

\lambda\in\mathbb{T},
we get

\displaystyle P_{\mu_{\zeta}}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}P(z,\lambda)d\mu_{\zeta}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}P(\zeta z,\zeta\lambda)d\mu(\zeta\lambda)=P_{\mu}(\zeta z).

It now follows from the rotation invariance of the area measure that for any
f\in\mathscr{D}(\mu_{\zeta}),

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{\mathscr{D}(\mu_{\zeta})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}P_{\mu}(\zeta z)dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(\overline{\zeta}z)|^{2}P_{\mu}(z)dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f_{\overline{\zeta}}\|^{2}_{\mathscr{D}(\mu)},

where
f_{\overline{\zeta}}(z)=f(\overline{\zeta}z),

z\in\mathbb{D}.
This yields the unitary
U_{\zeta}:f\mapsto f_{\overline{\zeta}}
from
\mathscr{D}(\mu_{\zeta})
onto
\mathscr{D}(\mu).
It is easy to verify that
U_{\zeta}\mathscr{M}_{z,\zeta}=\overline{\zeta}\mathscr{M}_{z,1}U_{\zeta}.
Part (i) now follows from the definition of the Cauchy dual operator and the fact that a scalar multiple of a subnormal operator is again subnormal. Since

U_{\zeta}
is a unitary,
\kappa(\zeta z,\zeta w),

z,w\in\mathbb{D},
is easily seen to be a reproducing kernel for
\mathscr{D}(\mu_{\zeta}),
and hence
by the uniqueness of the reproducing kernel, part (ii) follows.
∎

To complete the proof of Theorem
2.4
, in view of Proposition
7.1
, one may focus on the antipodal points
1
and
-1.

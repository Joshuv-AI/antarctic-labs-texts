Mean Field Games in a Stackelberg problem with an informed major player
Philippe Bergault, Pierre Cardaliaguet, Catherine Rainer

Abstract
We investigate a stochastic differential game in which a major player has a private information (the knowledge of a random variable), which she discloses through her control to a population of small players playing in a Nash Mean Field Game equilibrium. The major player’s cost depends on the distribution of the population, while the cost of the population depends on the random variable known by the major player. We show that the game has a relaxed solution and that the optimal control of the major player is approximatively optimal in games with a large but finite number of small players.

Introduction

We are interested in a model with a major player and infinitely many small players. In this model, the major player has a private information, which is disclosed to her at the beginning of the game. The game is then played in a Stackelberg equilibrium: the major player announces her strategy and, given this strategy, the small players play a mean field game (MFG) Nash equilibrium. The whole point is that, as the strategy of the major player might depend on her private information, the realization of her control gives a hint on this private information to the small players along the time.

In order to present our contribution, let us describe a little further our model. We consider a MFG problem with an informed player in a Stackelberg problem.

•

At time
0
(before the game starts), nature chooses at random an index
{\bf i}\in\{1,\dots,I\}
with law
p^{0}=(p^{0}_{i})_{i=1,\dots,I}
and announces the result to the major player only. The index
{\bf i}
is the private information of the major player.

•

The goal of the major player is to minimize over her (random) control
({\bf u}^{0}=({\bf u}^{0}_{i})_{i=1,\dots,I}))
a cost of the form

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}L^{0}_{\bf i}(t,{\bf u}^{0}_{{\bf i},t},{\bf m}_{t})dt\ |\ {\bf i}=i\right]

where
{\bf m}_{t}
is the (random) distribution of the small players.

•

Once the major player has announced her strategy, the small players, observing the realization
{\bf u}^{0}_{\bf i}
of the control of the informed player and their own state, minimize (in a decentralized way through a mean field game) their cost

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}L_{\bf i}(X_{t},\alpha_{t})+F_{{\bf i}}(X_{t},{\bf m}_{t})dt+G_{{\bf i}}(X_{T},{\bf m}_{T})\right],

where
(\alpha_{t})
is the control of a typical small player and
(X_{t})
is the process

X_{t}=Z+\int_{0}^{t}\alpha_{s}ds+\sqrt{2}B_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T].

Above,
Z
is a random variable on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with law
m_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
B
is a standard
d-
dimensional Brownian motion, with
Z
,
B
and
({\bf i},{\bf u}^{0})
independent. For any
i\in\{1,\dots,I\}
,
L_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the cost for a small player to play a control,
F_{i},G_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are the interaction costs between the small players,
L^{0}_{i}:[0,T]\times U^{0}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the running cost for the major player. The maps
L_{i}
,
F_{i}
,
G_{i}
and
L^{0}_{i}
are at least continuous and locally bounded.

The fact that the small player’s cost is given by a mean field game equilibrium means that the distribution of players
{\bf m}_{t}
is the conditional law of the optimal path
X^{*}
given
{\bf u}^{0}
.

Note that, in contrast with the major player, the minor players do not know the index
{\bf i}
. However they can obtain some information on
{\bf i}
by observing the major player’s actions.

In this very elementary game, interactions between major and minor players are minimal: the small players appears in the major player’s cost only through the distribution of their state, while the major player influences the minor player’s actions only by revealing (or not) information on
{\bf i}
. This information structure is inspired by Aumann-Maschler theory of repeated games with incomplete information: see
[
2
]
for a presentation of the theory and
[
9
]
for its extension to two player zero-sum stochastic differential games.

Our main results are the following: first we rewrite the problem in a relaxed form and show that the major player has an optimal strategy for the game (Theorem
3.5
). This relaxed formulation involves the theory of MFG with a common noise (common noise being here the information disclosed by the major player) as developed in
[
11
]
. Second, we show that such an optimal strategy is still approximatively optimal in Stackelberg games with finite many small players (Theorem
4.1
). The fact that the optimal strategy in a mean field game provides an approximated Nash equilibria for the associated game with finitely many players is very classical: see
[
12
,
25
]
for instance. The extension of such a property to Stackelberg equilibria in MFG with a large player has only been handled very recently in
[
20
, Theorem 2.20]
in a very general framework, but quite different from our: indeed in
[
20
]
, the major player can choose among the Nash equilibria of the
N-
player game, while in our set-up it is more natural that she has to handle any such Nash equilibrium. This difference leads us to show the approximate optimality of the control for the major player when the small players play
any
(approximate) Nash equilibria. To prove such a stability, we use in a very strong way the characterization of the limit of (approximate and open loop) Nash equilibria in differential games with a large number of players as discussed by Lacker
[
29
]
and Fischer
[
23
]
, as well as the standard Lasry-Lions monotonicity condition which ensures the uniqueness of the MFG Nash equilibrium. The main difference with Lacker (besides the framework, which is much more general in
[
29
]
) is the fact that, in our case, the common noise is the revelation by the major player of her private knowledge (and thus does not have a fixed law), while in
[
29
]
it is a fixed Brownian motion. Let us finally underline that, in the game with finitely many players, we allow the small players to play only “open loop strategies”: following Lacker and Flem
[
30
]
and Djete
[
19
]
, an extension to problems in which the small players are allowed to play closed loop strategies is probably doable, but the proof would be much more technical.

Let us finally comment upon the choice of Stackelberg versus Nash equilibria. As explained above, the analysis of Nash equilibria for MFG problems with a major player is well understood
[
7
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
. However the analysis of Nash equilibria with incomplete information seems much more challenging. Indeed, in order to study this question, one should understand how the small players could play in such a game without knowing a priori the major player’s strategy: this question is understood in a two player zero-sum game framework
[
9
]
, but remains completely open for nonzero-sum differential games.

Notation, assumption and definitions

2.1
Notation and assumptions

We work within the set
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The set
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the subset of
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of measures with finite second order moment; it is endowed with the Wasserstein distance
{\bf d}_{2}
.

We fix
I\in\mathbb{N}
, with
I\geq 2
. We denote by
\Delta(I)
the simplex
\Delta(I)=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{I}_{+},\;\sum_{i}p_{i}=1\}
interpreted as the set of probability measures over
\{1,\dots,I\}
. We also fix
p^{0}\in\Delta(I)
, which is the initial belief of the small players on the random variable
{\bf i}
(which has law
p^{0}
).

Let
{\bf D}
be the set of càdlàg functions from
\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\Delta(I)
, endowed with the Meyer-Zheng topology. Let us recall that, if
{\bf D}
is not a Polish space, it is continuously embedded into the Polish space
\mathcal{P}
of Borel probability measures on
[0,T]\times\Delta(I)
with first marginal equal to the Lebesgue measure. Hence any Borel probability measure on
{\bf D}
may be viewed as a Borel probability measure on the Polish space
\mathcal{P}
. We use this property throughout the paper.

Recall that, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,I\}
,
L_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the cost for a small player to play a control,
F_{i},G_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are the interaction costs between the small players,
L^{0}_{i}:[0,T]\times U^{0}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the running cost for the major player. Given
p\in\Delta(I)
,
x,u,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
m\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we set

L(x,u,p)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}p_{i}L_{i}(x,u),\;F(x,m,p)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}p_{i}F_{i}(x,m),\;G(x,m,p)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}p_{i}G_{i}(x,m),

(2.1)

H(x,\xi,p)=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}-\xi\cdot u-L(x,u,p),

L^{0}(s,u^{0},p,m)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}p_{i}L^{0}_{i}(s,u^{0},p,m),\qquad\bar{L}^{0}(s,p,m)=\inf_{u^{0}\in U^{0}}L^{0}(s,u^{0},p,m).

Let us recall that a continuous, bounded map
K:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is monotone if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(K(x,m_{1})-K(x,m_{2}))(m_{1}-m_{2})(dx)\geq 0\qquad\forall m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We say that the map
K
is strongly monotone if there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(K(x,m_{1})-K(x,m_{2}))(m_{1}-m_{2})(dx)\geq\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(K(x,m_{1})-K(x,m_{2}))^{2}dx.

(2.2)

It is called strictly monotone if

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(K(x,m_{1})-K(x,m_{2}))(m_{1}-m_{2})(dx)\leq 0\ \ \text{implies}\ \ m_{1}=m_{2}.

(2.3)

A classical example of a map
K
satisfying the strict and strong monotonicity conditions, which goes back to
[
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
, is

K(x,m)=f(\cdot,m\ast\rho(\cdot))\ast\rho.

(2.4)

where
\rho
is a smooth, non negative and radially symmetric kernel with Fourier transform
\hat{\rho}
vanishing almost nowhere, and
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is strictly increasing and Lipschitz continuous in the second variable, that is, there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\alpha\leq\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}(x,s)\leq\alpha^{-1},

with
f(x,0)
bounded.

Assumption:
The following conditions are in force throughout the paper:

(H1)

Cost functions of the major player:

\begin{array}[]{c}\text{The control set $(U^{0},d^{0})$ for the major player is a compact convex subset}\\
\text{of a finite dimensional space, not reduced to a singleton, and }\\
\text{for $i=1,\dots,I$,}\qquad L^{0}_{i}:[0,T]\times U^{0}\times\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\text{is continuous and bounded.}\end{array}

(2.5)

(H2)

Regularity and monotonicity of the cost functions of the small players:

\begin{cases}\text{ $F_{i},G_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ are Lipschitz continuous and bounded,}\\
\text{ $\sup_{m\in{\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\|F_{i}(\cdot,m)\|_{C^{2+\alpha}}+\|G_{i}(\cdot,m)\|_{C^{2+\alpha}}\leq C$, for $C,\alpha>0$,}\\
\text{ $F_{i}$ and $G_{i}$ \ are strongly monotone, and $F_{i}$ is strictly monotone.}\end{cases}

(2.6)

(H3)

Regularity of the Hamiltonians of the small players: for each
i\in\{1,\dots,I\}
, the map

H_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies:

\text{there exists $C>0$ such that, for all $x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $C^{-1}|\xi|^{2}-C\leq H_{i}(x,\xi)\leq C(|x|^{2}+1)$},

(2.7)

\left\{\begin{split}&\text{for all $R>0$, }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times B_{R}\ni(x,\xi)\mapsto H_{i}(x,\xi)\quad\text{is uniformly bounded and Lipschitz;}\\
&\xi\mapsto H_{i}(x,\xi)\quad\text{is uniformly convex},\;\text{for all $R>0$,}\;\|H(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{C^{2+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times B_{R})}\leq C_{R},\end{split}\right.

(2.8)

and

\left\{\begin{split}&\text{for some $\lambda_{0},C_{0}>0$ and all $t\in[0,T]$, $\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, and $|z|=1$,}\\
&|D_{x}H(x,\xi)|\leq C_{0}+\lambda_{0}(\xi\cdot D_{\xi}H(x,\xi)-H(x,\xi))\text{ and}\\
&\lambda_{0}\left(D_{\xi}H(x,\xi)\cdot\xi-H(x,\xi)\right)+D^{2}_{\xi\xi}H(x,\xi)\xi^{\prime}\cdot\xi^{\prime}+2D^{2}_{\xi x}H(x,\xi)z\cdot\xi^{\prime}+D^{2}_{xx}H(x,\xi)z\cdot z\geq-C_{0}.\end{split}\right.

(2.9)

A typical Hamiltonian satisfying the properties above is
H(x,\xi)=a(x)|\xi|^{2}
, where
a:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is smooth and bounded above and below by positive constants.

Note that the (strong/strict) monotonicity assumption of the
F_{i}
implies the (strong/strict) monotonicity of the map
F(\cdot,\cdot,p)
for any
p\in\Delta(I)
. Assumption (
2.9
), introduced in
[
11
]
, ensures the solution to the Hamilton-Jacobi equation in the MFG system to be wellposed and plays a key role in the construction of a solution to this MFG system.

2.2
The mean field game system

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle\leq\|D^{2}G\|_{\infty}+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{2}\Gamma(x-y,s-t)||h_{s}(y)-h_{s}(x)|dyds\ |\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|D^{2}G\|_{\infty}+\sup_{(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega}\|h_{t}(\cdot)\|_{C^{\alpha}}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{2}\Gamma(x-y,s-t)||y-x|^{\alpha}dyds

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}(1+\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha/2-1}ds)\leq C_{\alpha}.

Recalling (
2.12
) and the fact that
M_{0}=0
also gives the

bound on

.

The uniqueness in law can be proved as in
[
11
, Theorem 4.2]
.

We now show that, given a random distribution of players, the solution to the backward Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) equation can be interpreted as a value function:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]})
be a filtered probability space and
p=(p_{t})
be a càdlàg process taking values in
\Delta(I)
and adapted to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
. Let also
(m_{t})
be a continuous random process with values in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, adapted to the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
. Then the HJ equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}d\phi_{t}(x)=\left\{-\Delta\phi_{t}(x)+H(x,D\phi_{t}(x),p_{t})-F(x,m_{t},p_{t})\right\}dt+dM_{t}(x)\qquad\text{in}\;(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\phi_{T}(x)=G(x,m_{T},p_{T})\qquad\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{array}\right.

(2.15)

has a unique solution in the sense described above. Let also
(\Omega^{1},\mathbb{F}^{1},\mathbb{P}^{1},(\mathcal{F}^{1}_{t}))
be another filtered probability space supporting a Brownian motion
B
and a random variable
Z
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of law
\bar{m}_{0}
(
B
and
Z
being independent) and
\alpha^{*}
and
X^{*}
be given by

X^{*}_{t}=Z-\int_{0}^{t}H_{\xi}(X^{*}_{s},D\phi_{s}(X^{*}_{s}),p_{s})ds+\sqrt{2}B_{t},\qquad\alpha^{*}_{t}=-H_{\xi}(X^{*}_{t},D\phi_{t}(X^{*}_{t}),m_{t},p_{t}),\qquad t\in[0,T].

Then, for any control
\alpha\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\Omega_{1})
adapted to the filtration generated by
(p,m,B)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\phi_{0}(Z)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(L(X^{*}_{s},\alpha^{*}_{s},p_{s})+F(X^{*}_{s},m_{s},p_{s}))ds+G(X^{*}_{T},m_{T},p_{T})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(L(X_{s},\alpha_{s},p_{s})+F(X_{s},m_{s},p_{s}))ds+G(X_{T},m_{T},p_{T})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-C^{-1}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\alpha_{s}+H_{\xi}(X_{s},D\phi_{s}(X_{s}),p_{s})|^{2}ds\right],

where
X_{t}=Z+\int_{0}^{t}\alpha_{s}ds+\sqrt{2}B_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

The proof is standard and we only sketch it. As the probability space is fixed in the proof, we set
\mathbb{E}[\cdots]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}[\cdots]
. We discretize time, setting
t^{n}_{k}=kT/2^{n}
for
k\in\{0,\dots,2^{n}\}
and

(m^{n}_{t},p^{n}_{t})=(m^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}},p^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}})\qquad\text{on}\;[t^{n}_{k},t^{n}_{k+1})

Then we consider the solution
(\phi^{n},M^{n})
to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}d\phi^{n}_{t}(x)=\left\{-\Delta\phi^{n}_{t}(x)+H(x,D\phi^{n}_{t}(x),p^{n}_{t})-F(x,m^{n}_{t},p^{n}_{t})\right\}dt+dM^{n}_{t}(x)\qquad\text{in}\;(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\phi^{n}_{T}(x)=G(x,m^{n}_{T},p_{T})\qquad\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

adapted to the complete filtration
(\mathcal{F}^{n}_{t})
generated by
(m^{n},p^{n})
. Then
M^{n}
is constant on each interval
(t^{n}_{k},t^{n}_{k+1})
and thus
(\phi^{n},M^{n})
is a classical solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\phi^{n}_{t}(x)=\left\{-\Delta\phi^{n}_{t}(x)+H(x,D\phi^{n}_{t}(x),p^{n}_{t})-F(x,m^{n}_{t},p^{n}_{t})\right\}dt\qquad\text{in}\;(t^{n}_{k},t^{n}_{k+1})\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\phi^{n}_{(t^{n}_{k+1})^{-}}(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{n}_{(t^{n}_{k+1})}(x)\ |\ \mathcal{F}^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}}\right]\qquad\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{d},\;k<2^{n},\\
\phi^{n}_{T}(x)=G(x,m_{T},p_{T})\qquad\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{array}\right.

Given a control
\alpha
adapted to the filtration generated by
(p,m,B)
and
X_{t}=Z+\int_{0}^{t}\alpha_{s}ds+\sqrt{2}B_{t}
, we can compute on each time interval

\displaystyle\phi^{n}_{(t^{n}_{k+1})^{-}}(X_{t^{n}_{k+1}})=\phi^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}}(X_{t^{n}_{k}})+\int_{t^{n}_{k}}^{t^{n}_{k+1}}(\partial_{t}\phi^{n}_{t}(X_{t})+\Delta\phi^{n}(X_{t})+\alpha_{t}\cdot D\phi^{n}_{t}(X_{t}))dt+\int_{t^{n}_{k}}^{t^{n}_{k+1}}D\phi^{n}_{t}(X_{t})\cdot dB_{t}

\displaystyle=\phi^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}}(X_{t^{n}_{k}})+\int_{t^{n}_{k}}^{t^{n}_{k+1}}(H(X_{t},D\phi^{n}_{t}(X_{t}),p^{n}_{t})-F(X_{t},m^{n}_{t},p^{n}_{t})+\alpha_{t}\cdot D\phi^{n}_{t}(X_{t}))dt+\int_{t^{n}_{k}}^{t^{n}_{k+1}}D\phi^{n}_{t}(X_{t})\cdot dB_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\phi^{n}_{t^{n}_{k}}(X_{t^{n}_{k}})+\int_{t^{n}_{k}}^{t^{n}_{k+1}}(-L(X_{t},\alpha_{t},p^{n}_{t})-F(X_{t},m^{n}_{t},p^{n}_{t})+C^{-1}|\alpha_{t}+H_{\xi}(X_{t},D\phi_{t}^{n}(X_{t}),p_{t}^{n})|^{2})dt+\int_{t^{n}_{k}}^{t^{n}_{k+1}}D\phi^{n}_{t}(X_{t})\cdot dB_{t},

with an equality only if
\alpha_{t}=-H_{\xi}(X_{t},D\phi^{n}_{t}(X_{t}),p^{n}_{t})
for a.e.
t
and a.s.. Taking expectation and summing over
k
gives

\mathbb{E}\left[G(X_{T},m_{T},p_{T})\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{0}(Z)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(L(X_{t},\alpha_{t},p^{n}_{t})+F(X_{t},m^{n}_{t},p^{n}_{t})+C^{-1}|\alpha_{t}+H_{\xi}(X_{t},D\phi_{t}^{n}(X_{t}),p_{t}^{n})|^{2})dt\right],

with an equality only if
X=X^{n}
where
X^{n}_{t}=Z-\int_{0}^{t}H_{\xi}(X^{n}_{s},D\phi^{n}_{s}(X^{n}_{s}),p^{n}_{s})ds+B_{t}
and
\alpha=\alpha^{n}=-H_{\xi}(X^{n},D\phi^{n}(X^{n}),p^{n})
. Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
gives the result.

2.3
Formulation of the Stackelberg problem

We now come back to our original Stackelberg problem. Let us recall that
p^{0}\in\Delta(I)
is the initial belief on the random variable
{\bf i}
shared by the small players:
{\bf i}=i
with probability
p^{0}_{i}
.

Admissible control of the major player.
We denote by
\mathcal{U}^{0}([t_{0},T])
the set of measurable maps
u^{0}:[t_{0},T]\rightarrow U^{0}
, endowed with the
L^{1}
-distance:

d(u^{0},v^{0})=\int_{t_{0}}^{T}d^{0}(u^{0}_{s},v^{0}_{s})ds\qquad\forall u^{0},v^{0}\in\mathcal{U}^{0}([t_{0},T]).

We abbreviate
\mathcal{U}^{0}([t_{0},T])
into
\mathcal{U}^{0}
when
t_{0}=0
and endow
\mathcal{U}^{0}
with its Borel
\sigma-
algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{U}^{0})
. We denote by
u^{0}
its canonical process and by
(\mathcal{F}^{0}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
the filtration generated by the canonical process
t\rightarrow u^{0}_{t}
. Let
\Delta(\mathcal{U}^{0})
be the set of random controls for the major player, that is the set of Borel probability measures on
\mathcal{U}^{0}
. An admissible strategy for the major player is an element
{\bf u}^{0}=({\bf u}_{i}^{0})_{i=1,\dots,I}
of
(\Delta(\mathcal{U}^{0}))^{I}
. The interpretation of such a strategy is that the major players can choose her control in function of the index
{\bf i}
chosen by nature. However, in order to use her information, she also needs to hide it by randomizing it. Letting
\Omega^{0}=\{1,\dots,I\}
, the probability
{\bf u}^{0}
generates the probability
P^{{\bf u}^{0}}
on
(\Omega^{0}\times\mathcal{U}^{0},\mathcal{B}(\Omega^{0}\times\mathcal{U}^{0}))
defined by

P^{{\bf u}^{0}}(\{i\}\times A^{0}\}=p^{0}_{i}{\bf u}_{i}^{0}(A)\qquad\forall i\in\{1,\dots,I\},\;A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{U}).

The mean field game equilibria
. Given an admissible control
{\bf u}^{0}=({\bf u}_{i}^{0})_{i=1,\dots,I}\in(\Delta(\mathcal{U}^{0}))^{I}
of the major player, the small players observe along the time a realization
s\rightarrow u^{0}_{s}
of this strategy and deduce information on the random variable
{\bf i}
from this observation. Throughout this section we fix a filtered probability space
(\Omega^{1},\mathbb{F}^{1},\mathbb{P}^{1},(\mathcal{F}^{1}_{t}))
supporting a Brownian motion
B
and a random variable
Z
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of law
\bar{m}_{0}
. We denote by
(\mathcal{F}^{{\bf u^{0}},1}_{t})
the completion of the filtration generated by Z and by the process
t\rightarrow(u^{0}_{t},B_{t})
with respect to the probability
P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}
on
(\Omega^{0}\times\mathcal{U}\times\Omega^{1},\mathcal{B}(\Omega^{0}\times\mathcal{U})\otimes\mathbb{F}^{1})
.

Definition 2.4
.

Given an admissible control
{\bf u}^{0}=({\bf u}_{i}^{0})_{i=1,\dots,I}\in(\Delta(\mathcal{U}^{0}))^{I}
, an MFG equilibrium associated to
{\bf u}^{0}
is a pair
(\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}},m^{{\bf u}^{0}})
of processes on
(\Omega^{0}\times\mathcal{U}^{0}\times\Omega^{1},\mathcal{B}(\Omega^{0}\times U^{0})\otimes\mathcal{F}^{1},P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1},(\mathcal{F}^{{\bf u^{0}},1}_{t}))
, where
\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}}
takes its values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
m^{{\bf u}^{0}}
in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and

1.

\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}}
is optimal in the control problem

\inf_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(L_{\bf i}(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t})+F_{{\bf i}}(X^{\alpha}_{t},m^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t}))dt+G_{\bf i}(X^{\alpha}_{T},m^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T})\right]

where the infimum is taken over all
\mathbb{R}^{d}-
valued,
(\mathcal{F}^{{\bf u^{0}},1}_{t})-
adapted controls
\alpha
and

X^{\alpha}_{t}=Z+\int_{0}^{t}\alpha_{s}ds+\sqrt{2}B_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T],

2.

for any
t\in[0,T]
,
m^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t}
is the conditional law of
X^{\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}}}_{t}
given
\sigma(\{s\rightarrow{\bf u}^{0}_{s},\;s\leq t\})
.

Definition 2.5

(The major player’s problem)
.

The problem of the major player then consists in minimizing over her admissible controls
{\bf u}^{0}\in(\Delta(\mathcal{U}^{0}))^{I}
the cost

J^{0}({\bf u}^{0})=\sup_{(\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}},m^{{\bf u}^{0}})}\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{I}p_{i}^{0}L^{0}_{i}(t,u^{0}_{s},m_{s}^{{\bf u}^{0}})ds\Bigr],

(2.16)

where the sup is taken over all MFG equilibria
(\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}},m^{{\bf u}^{0}})
associated with
{\bf u}^{0}
.

We take the supremum with respect to the MFG Nash equilibria in order to handle the worst case for the informed player; however we will see that the MFG Nash equilibrium is unique (Corollary
3.2
), so that this supremum is actually useless.

3
The relaxed problem

In this section we rewrite the problem for the major player as an optimal control problem over a suitable set of martingales with values in
\Delta(I)
.

3.1
Definition of the relaxed problem

Fix
{\bf u}^{0}\in(\Delta(\mathcal{U}^{0}))^{I}
and denote by
(\mathcal{F}^{{\bf u^{0}}}_{t})
the completion with respect to
P^{{\bf u}^{0}}
of the filtration generated by the canonical process
t\rightarrow u^{0}_{t}
over
(\Omega^{0}\times\mathcal{U},\mathcal{B}(\Omega^{0}\times\mathcal{U}))
. Knowing
{\bf u}^{0}
and observing the realization
{\bf u}_{\bf i}^{0}
, the small players have access to the martingale process (with values in the simplex
\Delta(I)
)

{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t}=\mathbb{E}^{{\bf u^{0}}}\left[e_{{\bf i}}\ |\ \mathcal{F}^{{\bf u^{0}}}_{t}\right],

(3.17)

where
(e_{i})
is the canonical basis on
\mathbb{R}^{I}
. Note that
({\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})
is a
(\mathcal{F}^{{\bf u^{0}}}_{t})-
martingale; we always consider its càdlàg version. The next lemma shows how to rewrite the cost of the small players in terms of
({\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix
{\bf u}^{0}\in(\Delta(\mathcal{U}^{0}))^{I}
and let
(\Omega^{1},\mathcal{F}^{1},\mathbb{P}^{1},(\mathcal{F}^{1}_{t}))
a filtered probability space supporting a Brownian motion
B
and a random variable
Z
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of law
\bar{m}_{0}
(
B
and
Z
being independent). Let
(m_{t})
be a random distribution of the players, i.e., a
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})-
valued and
(\mathcal{F}^{{\bf u^{0}}}_{t})-
adapted process. Then, for any
\mathbb{R}^{d}-
valued,
(\mathcal{F}^{{\bf u^{0}},1}_{t})-
adapted control
\alpha
, and if

X^{\alpha}_{t}=Z+\int_{0}^{t}\alpha_{s}ds+\sqrt{2}B_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T],

then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(L_{{\bf i}}(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t})+F_{{\bf i}}(X^{\alpha}_{t},{\bf m}_{t}))dt+G_{{\bf i}}(X^{\alpha}_{T},{\bf m}_{T})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}L(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})+F(X^{\alpha}_{t},{\bf m}_{t},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})dt+G(X^{\alpha}_{T},{\bf m}_{T},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T})\right],

where the maps
H
,
F
and
G
are defined in (
2.1
).

Proof.

Indeed, as
(\mathcal{F}^{1}_{t})
is independent of
(\mathcal{F}^{\bf u^{0}}_{t})
,

{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left[e_{\bf i}|\mathcal{F}^{\bf u^{0}}_{t}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[e_{\bf i}|\mathcal{F}^{\bf u^{0},1}_{t}\right],

thus we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(L_{{\bf i}}(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t})+F_{{\bf i}}(X^{\alpha}_{t},{\bf m}_{t}))dt+G_{{\bf i}}(X^{\alpha}_{T},{\bf m}_{T})\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[e_{\bf i}\cdot((L_{i}(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t})+F_{i}(X^{\alpha}_{t},{\bf m}_{t})))_{i=1,\dots,I}\ |\ \mathcal{F}^{\bf u^{0},1}_{t}\right]\right]dt+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[e_{\bf i}\cdot(G_{i}(X^{\alpha}_{T},{\bf m}_{T}))_{i=1,\dots,I}\ |\ \mathcal{F}^{\bf u^{0},1}_{T}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t}\cdot((L_{i}(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t})+F_{i}(X^{\alpha}_{t},{\bf m}_{t})))_{i=1,\dots,I}\right]dt+\mathbb{E}\left[{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T}\cdot(G_{i}(X^{\alpha}_{T},{\bf m}_{T}))_{i=1,\dots,I}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[L(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})+F(X^{\alpha}_{t},{\bf m}_{t},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})\right]dt+\mathbb{E}\left[G(X^{\alpha}_{T},{\bf m}_{T},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T})\right].

Corollary 3.2
.

Under the notation and assumption of Lemma
3.1
, there is a unique MFG equilibrium
(\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}},m^{{\bf u}^{0}})
associated to
{\bf u}^{0}
which is given by

\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t}=-H_{\xi}(X^{*}_{t},D\phi_{t}(X^{*}_{t}),{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})

(3.18)

with
X^{*}_{t}=Z-\int_{0}^{t}H_{\xi}(X^{*}_{s},D\phi_{s}(X^{*}_{s}),{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{s})ds+\sqrt{2}B_{t}
and
m^{{\bf u}^{0}}=m
, where
(\phi,m,M)
is the unique solution to the MFG system (
2.10
) associated to
({\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}})
on
(\Omega^{0}\times\mathcal{U}^{0},\mathcal{B}(\Omega^{0}\times\mathcal{U}^{0}),P^{{\bf u}^{0}},(\mathcal{F}^{{\bf u^{0}}}_{t}))
.

A consequence of the corollary is that the “sup” in Definition
2.5
is not needed.

Proof.

Let
(\phi,m,M)
,
m^{{\bf u}^{0}}=m
,
X^{*}
and
\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}}
be as in the lemma. In view of Lemma

\alpha

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(L_{{\bf i}}(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t})+F_{{\bf i}}(X^{\alpha}_{t},{\bf m}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t}))dt+G_{{\bf i}}(X^{\alpha}_{T},{\bf m}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}L(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})+F(X^{\alpha}_{t},{\bf m}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})dt+G(X^{\alpha}_{T},{\bf m}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T})\right].

Recalling Proposition
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\tilde{\phi}_{0}(Z)\right]=\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}L(X^{*}_{t},\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})+F(X^{*}_{t},m^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})dt+G(X^{*}_{T},m^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}L(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})+F(X^{\alpha}_{t},m^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})dt+G(X^{\alpha}_{T},m^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-C^{-1}\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\alpha_{t}-\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t}|^{2}dt\right].

Therefore
\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}}
is optimal in the control problem

\inf_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(L_{\bf i}(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t})+F_{{\bf i}}(X^{\alpha}_{t},m^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t}))dt+G_{\bf i}(X^{\alpha}_{T},m^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T})\right].

By Itô’s formula and the definition of

, the conditional law of
X^{\alpha^{{\bf u}^{0}}}_{t}
given

solves the Fokker-Planck equation in (
2.10

.

Conversely, let
(\alpha^{*},m^{*})
be an MFG equilibrium associated to
{\bf u}^{0}
. Let
(\phi^{*},M^{*})
be the unique solution to the HJ equation on
(\Omega^{0}\times\mathcal{U}^{0},\mathcal{B}(\Omega^{0}\times\mathcal{U}^{0}),P^{{\bf u}^{0}},(\mathcal{F}^{{\bf u^{0}}}_{t}))
(see
[

]
)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}d\phi^{*}_{t}(x)=\left\{\Delta\phi^{*}_{t}(x)-H(x,D\phi^{*}_{t}(x),{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})+F(x,m^{*}_{t},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})\right\}dt+dM^{*}_{t}(x)\qquad\text{in}\;(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\phi^{*}_{T}(x)=G(x,m^{*}_{T},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T})\qquad\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{array}\right.

Proposition
2.3
states that
\bar{\alpha}_{t}=-H_{\xi}(\bar{X}_{t},D\phi^{*}_{t}(\bar{X}_{t}),{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})
, where
\bar{X}
solves
\bar{X}_{t}=Z-\int_{0}^{t}H_{\xi}(\bar{X}_{s},D\phi^{*}_{s}(\bar{X}_{s}),{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{s})ds+\sqrt{2}B_{t}
, is the unique minimizer of the problem

\displaystyle\inf_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(L(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})+F(X^{\alpha}_{t},m^{*}_{t}),{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})dt+G(X^{\alpha}_{T},m^{*}_{T},{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{T})\right]

\displaystyle=\inf_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{P^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{1}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}(L_{\bf i}(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t})+F_{{\bf i}}(X^{\alpha}_{t},m^{*}_{t}))dt+G_{\bf i}(X^{\alpha}_{T},m^{*}_{T})\right].

As
(\alpha^{*},m^{*})
is an MFG equilibrium associated to
{\bf u}^{0}
, this implies that

a.s. and for a.e.

, and therefore that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dm^{*}_{t}(x)=\left\{\Delta m^{*}_{t}(x)+{\rm div}(H_{\xi}(x,D\phi^{*}_{t}(x),{\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}}_{t})m^{*}_{t}(x))\right\}dt\qquad\text{in}\;(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\

Therefore the triple
(\phi^{*},m^{*},M^{*})
is a solution to the MFG system associated to
({\bf p}^{{\bf u}^{0}})
. By uniqueness we conclude that
(\phi^{*},m^{*},M^{*})=(\phi,m,M)
. This completes the proof.

Recall that
{\bf D}
is the set of càdlàg functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{D}(I)
, endowed with the Meyer-Zheng topology. Let

be the coordinate mapping on
{\bf D}
,
\mathcal{G}
be the Borel
\sigma-
algebra on
{\bf D}
and
({\cal G}_{t})
be the filtration generated by

. Given
p_{0}\in\Delta(I)
, we denote by
{\bf M}(p_{0})
the set of probability measures

4
Application to problems with finitely many players

4.1
Statement of the main result

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
N
being the large number of small players. We consider Stackelberg equilibria of a differential game in which the
N
small players interact with a major player. The major player announces a (random) strategy and the small players answer through a family of (“decentralized”) controls based on their observation of the Brownian motions of all players and of the control of the major player. For simplicity we assume as before that a strategy of the informed player is a probability measure
{\bf u}^{0}=({\bf u}^{0}_{i})_{i=1,\dots,I}\in(\Delta(\mathcal{U}^{0}))^{I}
on the set of controls. In particular it is independent of the Brownian motions of the players, of their strategies and of the initial positions.

Let us fix a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),{\bf P})
supporting, for any
N\geq 1
, a family of i.i.d.
\mathcal{F}_{0}-
measurable initial conditions
(x^{N,k}_{0})_{k=1,\dots,N}
of law
\bar{m}_{0}
and a family of independent Brownian motions
(B^{k})
, independent of the
(x^{N,k}_{0})_{k=1,\dots,N}

For
\delta>0

J^{N,0}({\bf u}^{0},\delta)=\sup_{(\alpha^{N,j})\ \delta-{\text{N}ash}}\mathbb{E}^{{\bf P}^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes{\bf P}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}L^{0}_{\bf i}(t,u^{0}_{s},m^{N}_{{\bf X}^{N}_{s}})ds\right]

(4.21)

where the supremum is taken over all the open-loop
\delta-
Nash equilibria
(\alpha^{N,j})
associated to the costs

J^{N,j}({\bf u}^{0},(\alpha^{N,k}))=\mathbb{E}^{{\bf P}^{{\bf u}^{0}}\otimes{\bf P}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}L_{\bf i}(X^{N,j}_{t},\alpha^{N,j}_{t})+F_{\bf i}(X^{N,j}_{t},m^{N,j}_{{\bf X}^{N}_{t}})dt+G_{{\bf i}}(X_{T}^{N,j},m^{N,j}_{{\bf X}^{N}_{t}})\right],

each control
(\alpha^{N,j})
being adapted to the filtration generated by the initial conditions
(x^{N,k}_{0})
, the control
u^{0}
and the Brownian motions
(B^{k})
, and
X^{N,j}

X^{N,j}_{t}=x^{N,j}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\alpha^{N,j}_{s}ds+\sqrt{2}B^{j}_{t}\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

(4.22)

We have set
{\bf X}^{N}_{t}=(X^{N,1}_{t},\dots,X^{N,N}_{t})
and
m^{N,j}_{{\bf X}^{N}_{t}}=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq j}\delta_{X^{N,k}_{t}}
.
The fact that
(\alpha^{N,j})
is a
\delta-
Nash equilibrium means that, for any

\delta-
Nash equilibrium for

Nash equilibria, so choosing a supremum or and infimum in the definition (

gives a good approximation of the

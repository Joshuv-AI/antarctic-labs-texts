Unraveling Gluon TMDs in J / ψ J/\psi and Pion production at the EIC
Khatiza Banu, Asmita Mukherjee, Amol Pawar, Sangem Rajesh

Abstract
We investigate the azimuthal asymmetries such as cos ⁡ 2 ​ ϕ T \cos 2{\phi_{T}} and Sivers symmetry for J / ψ J/\psi and π ± \pi^{\pm} production in electron-proton scattering, focusing on scenarios where the J / ψ J/\psi and the pion are produced in an almost back-to-back configuration. The electron is unpolarized, while the proton can be unpolarized or transversely polarized. For the J / ψ J/\psi formation, we use non-relativistic QCD (NRQCD), while π ± \pi^{\pm} is formed due to parton fragmentation. In this kinematics, we utilize the transverse momentum-dependent factorization framework to calculate the cross sections and asymmetries. We consider both quark and gluon-initiated processes and show that the gluon contribution dominates. In this work, we used the generalized parton model (GPM) for the TMD parametrizations and did not consider the effect of TMD evolution. We provide numerical estimates of the upper bounds on the azimuthal asymmetries, as well as employ a Gaussian parametrization for the gluon transverse momentum distributions (TMDs), within the kinematical region accessible by the upcoming Electron-Ion Collider (EIC).

Introduction

Unraveling the three-dimensional structure of a hadron is a fundamental quest in hadron physics, which has attracted a lot of interest both theoretically and experimentally. The 3D tomography of a hadron is encoded in the transverse momentum-dependent parton distribution functions (TMD PDFs), in short TMDs
[
1
]
. Generally, TMDs are non-perturbative functions, hence they have to be extracted from the experimental data which come from processes like semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering (SIDIS) and Drell-Yan (DY)
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5

Unlike collinear PDFs, TMDs depend both on the longitudinal momentum fraction of partons (
x_{a}
) and on their intrinsic transverse momentum (
k_{\perp a}
).
Moreover, TMDs are process-dependent because their operator definition contains Wilson lines
[
6
]
. The quark TMD operator requires a single Wilson line to ensure local gauge invariance, while two Wilson lines are needed for the gluon TMD operator. These Wilson lines encapsulate information about the processes under consideration, whether future-pointing (representing final-state interaction) or past-pointing (indicating initial-state interaction). Final-state interactions arise from the interaction between the proton remnant and final-state particles, whereas initial-state interactions involve the interaction between the proton remnant and initial-state partons.

At the leading twist, there are eight quark and gluon TMDs. In recent years, significant attention has been drawn to certain TMDs, for instance, the Boer-Mulders function,
h_{1}^{\perp q}

[
6
]
, and the Sivers function,

[
7
,

The linearly polarized gluon distribution was introduced in

[

The gluon Sivers TMD is also in the spotlight, as it correlates the hadron spin with the intrinsic transverse momentum of the gluon. It is interpreted as the probability of finding an unpolarized gluon within a transversely polarized hadron

The

[

In the NRQCD framework, a heavy-quark pair is produced initially either in color singlet (CS) or color octet (CO) configuration with a definite quantum number. Later, the heavy-quark pair hadronizes into physical

[
37

and the average velocity

In this article, we present a calculation of azimuthal asymmetries in the almost back-to-back electroproduction of
J/\psi
and

J/\psi

[
48

J/\psi

-pion kinematical configuration, the quarkonium is produced with large transverse momentum. We use NRQCD factorization in terms of LDMEs, and did not include the effect of initial and final state radiations
[
44
]

Our aim here is to provide numerical estimates of the asymmetries at leading order, in the kinematics of the upcoming EIC. We primarily focus on the calculation of azimuthal asymmetries like
\cos 2\phi_{T}

II
Kinematics and Formalism

We investigate
J/\psi
and pion production in the electron-proton scattering process, represented as:

\displaystyle e(l)+p(P)\rightarrow e(l^{\prime})+J/\psi(P_{\psi})+\pi^{\pm}(P_{\pi})+X\,,

(1)

where the momenta of the particles are denoted within the brackets. Our analysis encompasses both unpolarized and transversely polarized protons. Our study, considers the photon-proton center-of-mass (cm) frame, where the photon and proton travel along the positive and negative
z
axes, respectively. In this work, we consider electro-production, where the virtuality of the photon
Q^{2}
is large. In photoproduction, when
Q^{2}
is close to zero, one has to consider the contribution from the resolved photon as well, where the photon acts as a bunch of quarks or gluons that contribute to the hard process
[
62
]
.

The 4-momenta of the colliding proton
P
, virtual photon
q
and the incoming lepton
l
can be written as,

\displaystyle P^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{\mu}_{-}+\frac{M_{p}^{2}}{2}n^{\mu}_{+}\approx n^{\mu}_{-}\,,

\displaystyle q^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-x_{\scriptscriptstyle B}n^{\mu}_{-}+\frac{Q^{2}}{2x_{\scriptscriptstyle B}}n^{\mu}_{+}\approx-x_{\scriptscriptstyle B}P^{\mu}+(P\cdot q)n^{\mu}_{+}\,,

\displaystyle l^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-y}{y}x_{\scriptscriptstyle B}n^{\mu}_{-}+\frac{1}{y}\frac{Q^{2}}{2x_{\scriptscriptstyle B}}n^{\mu}_{+}+\frac{\sqrt{1-y}}{y}Q\hat{l}^{\mu}_{\perp}\,,

(2)

where
M_{p}
denotes the mass of the proton, and it has been neglected throughout the study. The Bjorken variable
x_{\scriptscriptstyle B}
is an important kinematic quantity defined as
x_{\scriptscriptstyle B}=\frac{Q^{2}}{2P\cdot q}
, where
Q^{2}
is the squared invariant mass of the virtual photon. Additionally, the inelasticity (

W_{\gamma p}
through the following relations:

\displaystyle Q^{2}\approx x_{\scriptscriptstyle B}yS~~~~~~~~W^{2}_{\gamma p}=\frac{Q^{2}(1-x_{\scriptscriptstyle B})}{x_{\scriptscriptstyle B}}\approx yS-Q^{2}.

(3)

The dominant partonic subprocess contributing to our investigated process is
\gamma^{*}(q)+a(k)\rightarrow J/\psi(P_{\psi})+a(P_{a})
, where

J/\psi
meson and a final-state parton. This parton further fragments to form a pion. In this work, we take into account the contributions that come from both the gluon and quark channels and estimate their relative contribution in the relevant kinematics. The 4-momenta of the

[

\displaystyle k^{\mu}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle x_{a}P^{\mu}+k^{\mu}_{\perp a},\,

\displaystyle P_{\psi}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{\psi}(P\cdot q)n_{+}^{\mu}+\frac{M_{\psi}^{2}+\bm{P}_{{\psi}\perp}^{2}}{2z_{\psi}P\cdot q}P^{\mu}+P_{{\psi}\perp}^{\mu}\,

\displaystyle P_{a}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{a}(P\cdot q)n_{+}^{\mu}+\frac{\bm{P}_{a\perp}^{2}}{2z_{a}P\cdot q}P^{\mu}+P_{a\perp}^{\mu}\,,

(4)

where
a=q,g
refers to both quark and gluon.
The variables

and

denote the light-cone momentum fraction and the intrinsic transverse momentum of the incoming parton relative to the direction of the parent proton, respectively. Furthermore, we consider the momentum fractions

J/\psi

The four-momentum of the pion, denoted as
P_{\pi}

\displaystyle P_{\pi}^{\mu}=z_{\pi}(P\cdot q)n_{+}^{\mu}+\frac{M_{\pi}^{2}+\bm{P}_{{\pi}\perp}^{2}}{2z_{\pi}P\cdot q}P^{\mu}+P_{{\pi}\perp}^{\mu}\,,

\displaystyle P_{a\perp}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{z}P_{\pi}^{\mu}.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}{\bm{P}}_{a}}{E_{a}}=J(z)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}{\bm{P}}_{\pi}}{E_{\pi}}\,~~~~~~~~{\rm with}~~~~~~~~J=\frac{1}{z^{3}}\frac{E_{\pi}}{E_{g}}.

\displaystyle\delta^{4}\bigl(q+k-P_{\psi}-P_{a}\bigr)

\displaystyle\delta^{4}\bigl(q+k-P_{\psi}-P_{a}\bigr)

The phase space of the outgoing particles is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}{\bm{l}^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{l^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}y\,,\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}{\bm{P}}_{\pi}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{\pi}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{P}}_{\pi\perp}\mathrm{d}z_{\pi}}{(2\pi)^{3}2z_{\pi}}\,,\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}{\bm{P}}_{\psi}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{\psi}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{P}}_{\psi\perp}\mathrm{d}z_{\psi}}{(2\pi)^{3}2z_{\psi}}.

In our analysis, we consider a scenario in which the final parton and the
J/\psi
are oriented back-to-back in the transverse plane, which means the sum of their transverse momenta is significantly smaller than the individual transverse momenta of the
J/\psi
and final parton. This condition allows us to use the TMD factorization for the scattering cross-section. We define the sum and difference of their transverse momentum as follows,

\displaystyle\bm{q}_{T}={\bm{P}}_{\psi\perp}+\frac{{\bm{P}}_{\pi\perp}}{z},\quad\bm{K}_{\perp}=\frac{{\bm{P}}_{\psi\perp}-\frac{{\bm{P}}_{\pi\perp}}{z}}{2}\,.

(23)

The Eq.(

J/\psi
are almost equal in magnitude but point in approximately opposite directions
i.e.
,
|\bm{q}_{T}|\ll|\bm{K}_{\perp}|
.
From Eq.(
21

z_{\psi}=1-\frac{z_{\pi}}{z}
and
\bm{q}_{T}=\bm{k}_{\perp a}
. Further, the transformation given in Eq.(

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{P}}_{\psi\perp}\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{P}}_{\pi\perp}=z\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{q}}_{T}\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{K}}_{\perp}.

(24)

After performing the integration over
z_{\pi}
,
x_{a}
and
{\bm{k}}_{\perp a}
in Eq.(

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{ep\to e+J/\psi+\pi^{\pm}+X}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z_{\psi}\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{q}}_{T}\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{K}}_{\perp}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{yS^{2}}\frac{1}{16(2\pi)^{4}}\int\mathrm{d}z\,D(z)\,

(25)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{Q^{4}}L^{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(l,q)\,\Phi_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{a}(x_{a},{\bm{q}}_{T})\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu\alpha}^{\gamma^{\ast}a\to c\bar{c}+a}{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}^{\dagger;\gamma^{\ast}a\to c\bar{c}+a}\,\,\frac{zJ(z)}{z_{\psi}(1-z_{\psi})}\,.

Azimuthal asymmetries

The cross-section for
J/\psi

\pi^{\pm}

J/\psi

\pi^{\pm}

\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z_{\psi}\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{q}}_{T}\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{K}}_{\perp}}\equiv\mathrm{d}\sigma(\phi_{S},\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T})=\mathrm{d}\sigma^{U}(\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T},\phi_{\perp})+\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T}(\phi_{S},\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T})\,.

(26)

For an unpolarized proton, the differential cross-section can be written in terms of different azimuthal modulations associated with the

h_{1}^{\perp\,g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T}}^{2})

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{U}

(27)

(28)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma^{T}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}{|\bm{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}|}\,\int\mathrm{d}z\bigg[\sin(\phi_{S}-\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T})\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{0}+\mathcal{A}_{1}\cos\phi_{\perp}+\mathcal{A}_{2}\cos 2\phi_{\perp}\bigr)\frac{|\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}|}{M_{p}}f_{1T}^{\perp\,g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T}}^{2})

\displaystyle+\cos(\phi_{S}-\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T})\bigl(\mathcal{B}_{0}\sin 2\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}+\mathcal{B}_{1}\sin(2\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}-\phi_{\perp})+\mathcal{B}_{2}\sin 2(\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}-\phi_{\perp})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{B}_{3}\sin(2\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}-3\phi_{\perp})+\mathcal{B}_{4}\sin(2\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}-4\phi_{\perp})\bigr)\frac{|\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}|^{3}}{M_{p}^{3}}\,h_{1T}^{\perp\,g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T}}^{2})

\displaystyle+\bigl(\mathcal{B}_{0}\sin(\phi_{S}+\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T})+\mathcal{B}_{1}\sin(\phi_{S}+\phi_{T}-\phi_{\perp})+\mathcal{B}_{2}\sin(\phi_{S}+\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}-2\phi_{\perp})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{B}_{3}\sin(\phi_{S}+\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}-3\phi_{\perp})+\mathcal{B}_{4}\sin(\phi_{S}+\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}-4\phi_{\perp})\bigr)\frac{|\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}|}{M_{p}}h_{1T}^{g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{{\scriptscriptstyle T}}^{2})\bigg]\,D(z)\,,

III.1
Upper bounds

The upper bounds of the azimuthal asymmetries as defined in Eqs.(
35
)-(
39
), can be determined by saturating the positivity bounds on the gluon TMDs, which are model-independent constraints
[
68
,
6
]
; given below :

\displaystyle\frac{|\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}|}{M_{p}}\,|f_{1T}^{\perp\,g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2})|

\displaystyle\leq f_{1}^{g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{q}^{2}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}}{2M_{p}^{2}}\,|h_{1}^{\perp\,g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2})|

\displaystyle\leq f_{1}^{g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\frac{|\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}|}{M_{p}}\,|h_{1}^{g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2})|

\displaystyle\leq f_{1}^{g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\frac{|\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}|^{3}}{2M_{p}^{3}}\,|h_{1T}^{\perp\,g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2})|

\displaystyle\leq f_{1}^{g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2})\,.

(40)

The bounds, as stated in Eq. (
40
), essentially ensure that the Sivers functions as well as the linearly polarized gluon distribution do not dominate the unpolarized gluon distribution
f_{1}^{g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{T}^{2})
. Upper bounds on the asymmetries are also model-independent. By incorporating these positivity bounds into our analysis, we can derive upper limits on azimuthal asymmetries such as
A^{\cos 2\phi_{T}}
and
A^{\cos 2(\phi_{T}-\phi_{\perp})}
as follows:

\displaystyle|A^{\cos 2\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}}|=2\frac{|{\cal B}_{0}|}{{\cal A}_{0}}\,,\quad|A^{\cos 2(\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}-\phi_{\perp})}|=2\frac{|{\cal B}_{2}|}{{\cal A}_{0}}\,,

(41)

The gluon TMDs influence these asymmetries, and the bounds provide a ceiling on how large these asymmetries can be for a given kinematic condition. Additionally, the upper bound for the Sivers asymmetry,
A^{\sin(\phi_{S}-\phi_{T})}
, reaches unity. While the upper bounds for the other asymmetries are related as below

\displaystyle|A^{\sin(\phi_{S}+\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T})}|=\frac{1}{2}\,|A^{\cos 2\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}}|\,,\quad|A^{\sin(\phi_{S}-3\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T})}|=\frac{1}{2}\,|A^{\cos 2(\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle T}-\phi_{\perp})}|\,.

(42)

III.2
Gaussian Parametrization of the TMDs

The asymmetries are dependent on the parametrizations of the gluon TMDs. In our analysis, we employ a widely used Gaussian parameterization for the TMDs. This parametrization involves factorizing the TMDs into collinear PDFs and an exponential factor that depends on the transverse momentum of the parton. For the unpolarized TMD
f_{1}^{a}(x_{a},\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2})
, the parametrization is given by :

\displaystyle f_{1}^{a}(x_{a},\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2})=f_{1}^{a}(x_{a},\mu)\frac{e^{-\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2}/\langle q_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2}\rangle}}{\pi\langle q_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2}\rangle}\,.

(43)

Here,
f_{1}^{a}(x_{a},\mu)
represents the collinear gluon and quark PDFs at the probing scale
\mu=\sqrt{M^{2}_{\psi}+Q^{2}}

[
69
]
. In this work, we have used the Gaussian width
\langle q_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2}\rangle=1~\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
for gluons
[
70
]
and
\langle q_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2}\rangle=0.25~\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
for quarks.
For the linearly polarized TMD
h_{1}^{\perp g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2})
, we adopted the Gaussian parameterization proposed in Ref.
[
71
,
72
]
:

\displaystyle h_{1}^{\perp g}(x_{g},\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2})=\frac{M_{p}^{2}f_{1}^{g}(x_{g},\mu)}{\pi\langle q_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2}\rangle^{2}}\frac{2(1-r)}{r}e^{1-\frac{\bm{q}_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2}}{r\langle q_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2}\rangle}},

(44)

where
M_{p}
is the proton mass,
r
(with
0<r<1
), and the average intrinsic transverse momentum width of the incoming gluon,
\langle q_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2}\rangle
, are parameters of this model. In our numerical estimation, we take

and

\langle q_{\scriptscriptstyle T}^{2}\rangle=1~\text{GeV}^{2}
for gluons in line with Ref.
[
72

.

For the numerical estimates of Sivers asymmetry, we utilized Gaussian parameterization for both the gluon and quark Sivers functions, denoted as
f_{1T}^{\perp g}
and
f_{1T}^{\perp q}

Starting with the gluon Sivers function

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{g}\left(x_{a}\right)=N_{g}x_{a}^{\alpha}\left(1-x_{a}\right)^{\beta}\frac{(\alpha+\beta)^{(\alpha+\beta)}}{\alpha^{\alpha}\beta^{\beta}}.\,

(46)

q=u,d

IV
Numerical Results

In the section, we present numerical results for
J/\psi
and
\pi^{\pm}
production in electron-proton collisions. The process involves the interaction of a virtual photon with the partonic content of the proton, resulting in the creation of a pair of charm and anti-charm quarks along with a final-state parton. The charm and anti-charm quark pair subsequently hadronize into a
J/\psi
meson through the NRQCD mechanism, while the produced parton fragments to form a pion in the final state. The process considered is
\gamma^{\ast}+a\rightarrow J/\psi+a\rightarrow J/\psi+\pi^{\pm}
. The heavy quark pair can be produced in different color and spin configurations, depending on which, contribution to
J/\psi
production comes from the states
{}^{3}{S}_{1}^{(1,8)}
,
{}^{1}{S}_{0}^{8}
and
{}^{3}{P}_{J}^{8}
, with
J=0,1,2
. The hadronization of heavy-quark pair into the quarkonium is encoded in the LDMEs. Even though LDMEs are assumed to be universal, there are several sets of LDMEs in the literature that are distinct from each other. This discrepancy among the LDMEs is related to the fact that the order of perturbative QCD considered and the second one, the transverse momentum cut imposed in the fitting. Although the choice of the LDME set strongly influences the asymmetries, we considered two LDME sets, labeled with CMSWZ
[
79
]
and SV
[
80
]
, to obtain maximum asymmetries. As discussed earlier, for this process,
an analytic form of the smearing effect coming from the TMD shape functions is still not available, as matching the TMD factorized result with the collinear framework at the intermediate transverse momentum region is still to be done. So in this work, we did not include any smearing effect. The effect of the shape function on the asymmetry is expected to be small, as the
J/\psi
is produced with relatively large transverse momentum. However, a careful consideration of these effects is needed, possibly in a future publication. For the pion production, we consider a collinear fragmentation process, that is we assume that the transverse momentum of the pion with respect to the fragmenting quark is small compared to the transverse momentum of the pion itself. For the pion fragmentation, we use the neural networks fragmentation function (NNFF)
[
81
]
. The CT18NLO
[
82
]
is employed for collinear PDFs. The PDFs and fragmentation function are probed at the scale
\mu=\sqrt{M^{2}_{\psi}+Q^{2}}
. The mass of the
J/\psi
is taken as
M_{\psi}=3.1
GeV. We consider kinematics where the
J/\psi
and
\pi^{\pm}
are produced almost back-to-back in the transverse plane, wherein the relative transverse momentum,
q_{T}
, is significantly smaller than the transverse momentum,
K_{\perp}
, of the
J/\psi
, i.e.,
|\bm{q}_{T}|\ll|\bm{K}_{\perp}|
. To ensure this, we consider the following kinematical cuts: the transverse momentum imbalance
q_{T}
and
z

V
Conclusion

We have presented a calculation of the azimuthal asymmetries, particularly
\cos 2\phi_{T}
,
\cos 2(\phi_{T}-\phi_{\perp})
, and Sivers asymmetries, in almost back-to-back production of
J/\psi
and
\pi^{\pm}
in the process
e+p\rightarrow e+J/\psi+\pi^{\pm}+X
. We considered the kinematics of the upcoming EIC. In this kinematical region, TMD factorization is expected to be valid. We calculated the
J/\psi
production using NRQCD factorization in terms of LDMEs and ignoring the effects of TMD shape functions and soft functions, at leading order. We considered the formation of the pion through fragmentation of the final state parton. We estimated the contribution to the cross-section and asymmetries both from quark and gluon channels and showed that in the kinematics to be accessed by EIC, the contribution from virtual photon-gluon fusion is dominant. We presented numerical estimates of the upper bounds on the asymmetries by saturating the positivity bound.
We also calculated them using a Gaussian parametrization of the gluon TMDs and a more recent spectator model-based parametrization. The asymmetries in the Gaussian parameterization satisfy the positivity bound, but the spectator model saturates the bound. The asymmetries depend on the LDME sets used in NRQCD. For an unpolarized proton, the estimated weighted
\cos 2\phi_{T}
and
\cos 2(\phi_{T}-\phi_{\perp})
asymmetries can reach a maximum of about
40\%
within the considered kinematical region. The estimated Sivers asymmetry is about
14\%
and
5\%
for
\sqrt{S}=45~
GeV and
\sqrt{S}=140~
GeV respectively when the proton is transversely polarized. Thus, an almost back-to-back
J/\psi
and
\pi^{\pm}
production at EIC will be a potential channel to probe the gluon TMDs

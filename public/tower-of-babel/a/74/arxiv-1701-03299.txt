Extraction of the bare form factors for the semi-leptonic B s {\text{B}_{\text{s}}} decays
F. Bahr, D. Banerjee, H. Simma, R. Sommer

Abstract
The computation of the form factors for the B s → K ​ ℓ ​ ν {\text{B}_{\text{s}}}\to\text{K}\ell\nu decay is presented. The b quark is treated by means of Heavy Quark Effective Theory, currently in the static approximation. In these proceedings we discuss the extraction of the bare matrix elements from lattice data through a combined fit to two- and three-point correlation functions, as well as by considering suitable ratios. The different methods agree concerning the extracted form factors and approximately 2% accuracy is reached. The non-perturbative renormalization and matching to QCD is described in accompanying proceedings [ 1 ] .

Introduction: definitions, ensembles, measurements

The QCD matrix elements for the semi-leptonic
{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}\to\text{K}\ell\nu
decay in the
rest frame of the
{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}
meson are

\displaystyle(2m_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}})^{-1/2}\langle\text{K}(\vec{p}_{\text{K}})|V^{0}(0)|{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=h_{\parallel}(E_{\text{K}}),

(1)

\displaystyle(2m_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}})^{-1/2}\langle\text{K}(\vec{p}_{\text{K}})|V^{k}(0)|{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=p^{k}_{\text{K}}h_{\perp}(E_{\text{K}}),

(2)

where
V^{\mu}(x)=\bar{\psi}_{\rm u}(x)\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{\rm b}(x)
and
\vec{p}_{\text{K}}
is the Kaon momentum.

The b quark is treated in the framework of Heavy Quark Effective Theory (HQET)
where a full non-perturbative renormalization program exists
[
2
]
. Here
we focus on the extraction of the bare HQET matrix elements from the lattice
data, while the procedure to obtain the QCD form factors from these bare matrix
elements is described in
[
1
]
.

For the heavy quark we use HYP1 and HYP2 discretizations
[
3
]
. We first
restrict ourselves to the static approximation. The two- and three-point
functions of interest are

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\text{K}}({t_{\text{K}}};\vec{p}_{\text{K}})=

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{i}}\sum_{\vec{x}_{f},\vec{x}_{i}}e^{-i\vec{p}_{\text{K}}\cdot(\vec{x}_{f}-\vec{x}_{i})}\langle P_{\rm su}(\vec{x}_{f},t_{i}+t_{\text{K}})P_{\rm us}(\vec{x}_{i},t_{i})\rangle,

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}({t_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}};\vec{0})=

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{i}}\sum_{\vec{x}_{f},\vec{x}_{i}}\langle P_{\rm sb}(\vec{x}_{f},t_{i}+t_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}})P_{\rm bs}(\vec{x}_{i},t_{i})\rangle,

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{C}^{{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}\to\text{K}}_{\mu}({t_{\text{K}}},{t_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}};\vec{p}_{\text{K}})=&\sum_{t_{i}}\sum_{\vec{x}_{f},\vec{x}_{v},\vec{x}_{i}}e^{-i\vec{p}_{\text{K}}\cdot(\vec{x}_{f}-\vec{x}_{v})}\langle P_{\rm su}(\vec{x}_{f},t_{i}+t_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}\!+t_{\text{K}})V_{\mu}(\vec{x}_{v},t_{i}+t_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}})P_{\rm bs}(\vec{x}_{i},t_{i})\rangle,\end{split}

(5)

with
P_{\rm q_{1}q_{2}}(\vec{x},t)=\bar{\psi}_{\rm q_{1}}(\vec{x},t)\gamma_{5}\psi_{\rm q_{2}}(\vec{x},t)
. For the light quarks we use Wuppertal smearing

[
4
,
5
]
. The Kaon correlator is calculated with only one level of
smearing, while for the
{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}
meson we apply three levels of smearing (in both
the two- and three-point functions). We can decompose the Euclidean correlation
functions as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\text{K}}({t_{\text{K}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}(\kappa^{(m)})^{2}e^{-E_{\text{K}}^{(m)}t_{\text{K}}},

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}_{ij}({t_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\beta_{i}^{(n)}\beta_{j}^{(n)}e^{-E_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}^{(n)}t_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}},

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}\to\text{K}}_{\mu,i}({t_{\text{K}}},{t_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m}\kappa^{(m)}\varphi^{(m,n)}_{\mu}\beta_{i}^{(n)}e^{-E_{\text{K}}^{(m)}t_{\text{K}}}e^{-E_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}^{(n)}t_{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}},

(8)

where the indices
m,n
label the Kaon and
{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}
meson energy levels
respectively, while the indices
i,j
label the smearing levels used for the

{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}
meson. In our current setup
\vec{p}_{K}
has a non-zero component only in
the
x
-direction, therefore we only extract the form factors for
\mu=0,1
.
Thus, the desired static bare matrix elements are given by

h^{\rm stat,bare}_{\parallel}=\varphi^{(0,0)}_{0}\sqrt{2E^{(0)}_{\text{K}}},\quad p^{1}_{\text{K}}h^{\rm stat,bare}_{\perp}=\varphi^{(0,0)}_{1}\sqrt{2E^{(0)}_{\text{K}}}.

(9)

We use three
N_{f}=2
CLS ensembles
[
6
]
: A5, F6, and N6, which have
similar pion mass (
m_{\pi}=
310–340 MeV) but different lattice spacings
(
a\approx 0.075
, 0.065 and
0.048\;\text{fm}
respectively), allowing us to
take the continuum limit. For further details on the ensembles, see Table 2 of
Ref.
[
7
]
.

We choose
|\vec{p}_{\text{K}}|=0.535
GeV which corresponds to
2\pi/L
on the N6
lattice. We keep the same value of
\vec{p}_{\text{K}}
on the other lattices by
introducing flavour-twisted boundary conditions
[
8
]
for the strange
quark (cf. Ref.
[
7
]
).

Computing all-to-all propagators with a random source on every timeslice (“full
time dilution”) allows us to access all time separations in the two-point and
three-point functions. For more details on the measurements and analysis we
refer to the upcoming Ref.
[
9
]
.

2
Bare form factor extraction by means of a combined fit

Our goal is to extract the form factors by fitting to the three-point
correlation function, Eq. (
8
). We first determine the parameters of
the two-point correlation functions, Eqs. (
6
), (
7
), and
use these as fixed input to estimate the form factors
\varphi_{\mu}
from a
linear fit to Eq. (
8
). We then use all these parameters as initial values
to a “global” combined fit to Eqs. (
6
)–(
8
). We find
that in this way one obtains superior stability of the fit results with respect
to small changes of the initial values and fit ranges.

Our interest is mostly limited to the ground-state form factors

\varphi_{\mu}^{(0,0)}
, however we find that for the safe extraction, free of
contamination by the excited states, we need to include more terms in the sums
of Eqs. (
6
)–(
8
). In these proceedings we keep only the
Kaon ground state but include two excited states for the
{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}
meson.

Clearly, a good choice for the initial values and a careful choice of fit ranges
is required to obtain stable combined fits. Let us briefly describe how this is
done in the following subsections.

2.1
Two-point function fits

Eq. (
6
) for the two-point light-light correlator is taken in the limit of
infinite
T
. In practice, our lattices have finite
T
and one has to take into
account the wrap-around state, giving

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\text{K}}(t)\cong

\displaystyle(\kappa^{(0)})^{2}(e^{-E_{\rm K}^{(0)}t}+e^{-E_{\rm K}^{(0)}(T-t)}),

(10)

when
t
and
T-t
are large enough that we can neglect the contribution of the
excited states.

We select the time
t^{\rm K2}_{\rm min}
at which we start the fit by choosing the
smallest value of
t
at which the (fitted) excited-state contribution is
smaller than 1/4 of the statistical uncertainty at that value of
t
. There is
no severe signal-to-noise problem in the Kaon sector, so we always use

t^{\rm K2}_{\rm max}=T/2
.

For the two-point heavy-light (
{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}
) correlator, we have three different
smearings. Including the off-diagonal terms yields six independent correlators
in the symmetric
\mathcal{C}^{{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}}_{ij}
matrix. We first obtain the energies using
GEVP, with
t_{0}=\lceil t/2\rceil
(cf. Refs.
[
10
,
5
]
). Then we
determine the amplitudes in Eq. (
7
) by first doing a linear fit to the
diagonal elements of
\mathcal{C}^{{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}}
to find the squares of the amplitudes and then
using these values as input to the non-linear fit for
\beta^{(n)}_{i}
to all
elements of
\mathcal{C}^{{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}}_{ij}
, including the off-diagonal ones.

2.3
Combined fit, stability

Having determined the parameters in Eqs. (
6
), (
7
) and
the estimates for the form factors
\varphi_{\mu}
from linear fits to
Eq. (
8
), we use them as initial values for the combined non-linear
fit, i.e. we simultaneously fit the three equations for all values of
\mu
and
all light-quark smearings for the
{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}
meson.

The temporal fit ranges are determined by suitable criteria described above,
except for three minimum times:
t^{\rm B2}_{\rm min}
,
t^{\rm B3}_{\rm min}
and

t^{\rm K3}_{\rm min}
, which in our setup are independent of
\mu
and the smearing
level. They are chosen such that the contributions from the excited states are
negligible.

We check that the change of the fit results with respect to variations of the
fit ranges is negligible within the statistical errors, plotting the results on
the “stability plots”. An example stability plot for the ground-state matrix
elements is presented in Fig.

To cross-check the results obtained by the combined fit, we use the ratio

4
Conclusions and outlook

We extract the bare matrix elements of the semi-leptonic
{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}
decay in the
static order of HQET. In this framework, renormalization
and matching to QCD can be performed non-perturbatively, such that the
continuum limit can be taken, as described in Refs.
[
1
,
7
]
.

Our numerical setup allows us to access all time separations in the two-point
and three-point correlation functions of interest, therefore giving us a very
good handle on the excited-state and finite-
T
contributions.

To extract the matrix elements from the lattice data, we use two different
extraction methods: the combined fit and the ratio method. They give consistent
results which makes us confident in the robustness of the analysis.
The precision of our results for the bare matrix elements is approx. 2%. The
final resulting precision of the static
continuum-extrapolated
RGI
form factors is better than 5%
[
7
]
.

We estimate the systematic error from neglecting the subleading terms in

1/m_{\rm b}
to be of order 15%. Therefore, the next step is to include the

1/m_{\rm b}
terms, which will reduce this systematic error to 1–2%. The
required set of HQET parameters is being calculated in the parallel effort by
the ALPHA collaboration
[
13
]
.

We also plan to include ensembles with a smaller pion mass, although we expect
the effects of the quark mass to be below our uncertainties in the case of
the
{\text{B}_{\text{s}}}\to\text{K}\ell\nu
decay.

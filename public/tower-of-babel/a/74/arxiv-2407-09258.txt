More on 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -closed sets
Javier Camargo, Sergio Macías

Abstract
We consider properties of the diagonal of a continuum that are used later in the paper. We continue the study of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -closed subsets of a continuum X X . We prove that for a continuum X X , the statements: Δ X \Delta_{X} is a nonblock subcontinuum of X 2 X^{2} , Δ X \Delta_{X} is a shore subcontinuum of X 2 X^{2} and Δ X \Delta_{X} is not a strong centre of X 2 X^{2} are equivalent, this result answers in the negative Questions 35 and 36 and Question 38 ( i ∈ { 4 , 5 } i\in\{4,5\} ) of the paper “ Diagonals on the edge of the square of a continuum , by A. Illanes, V. Martínez-de-la-Vega, J. M. Martínez-Montejano and D. Michalik”. We also include an example, giving a negative answer to Question 1.2 of the paper “ Concerning when ℱ 1 ​ ( X ) \mathcal{F}_{1}(X) is a continuum of colocal connectedness in hyperspaces and symmetric products , Colloquium Math., 160 (2020), 297-307”, by V. Martínez-de-la-Vega, J. M. Martínez-Montejano. We characterised the 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -closed subcontinua of the square of the pseudo-arc. We prove that the 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -closed sets of the product of two continua is compact if and only if such product is locally connected. We show that for a chainable continuum X X , Δ X \Delta_{X} is a 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -closed subcontinuum of X 2 X^{2} if and only if X X is an arc. We prove that if X X is a continuum with the property of Kelley, then the following are equivalent: Δ X \Delta_{X} is a 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -closed subcontinuum of X 2 X^{2} , X 2 ∖ Δ X X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X} is strongly continuumwise connected, Δ X \Delta_{X} is a subcontinuum of colocal connectedness, and X 2 ∖ Δ X X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X} is continuumwise connected. We give models for the families of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -closed sets and 𝒯 \mathcal{T} -closed subcontinua of various families of continua.

2.9 Theorem
.

Let
X
and
Y
be chainable continua. Let
A
and
B
be subcontinua
of
X\times Y
.
If
\pi_{X}(A)=X
and
\pi_{Y}(B)=Y
, then
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.
Let
A
and
B
be subcontinua of
X\times Y

such that
\pi_{X}(A)=X

and
\pi_{Y}(B)=Y
. Suppose that
A\cap B=\emptyset
.

Let
d_{X}
and
d_{Y}
be metrics on
X
and
Y
, respectively.
We consider a metric

\rho
on
X\times Y
defined by

\rho((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}))=\max\{d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2}),d_{Y}(y_{1},y_{2})\}.

Since
A
and
B
are disjoint, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\min\{\rho(a,b)\ |\ a\in A\text{ and }b\in B\}>\varepsilon.

(2.1)

Since
X
and
Y
are chainable, there exist surjective
\varepsilon
-maps

f\colon X\ \hbox{$\to\!\!\!\!\!\to$}\ [0,1]

and
g\colon Y\ \hbox{$\to\!\!\!\!\!\to$}\ [0,1]
. Let
G\colon X\times Y\ \hbox{$\to\!\!\!\!\!\to$}\ [0,1]^{2}

be defined for each
(x,y)\in X\times Y
by

G((x,y))=(f(x),g(y)).

Note that, since
\pi_{X}(A)=X
,
\pi_{1}(G(A))=[0,1]
. Hence,

G(A)\cap\{0\}\times[0,1]\neq\emptyset
and

G(A)\cap\{1\}\times[0,1]\neq\emptyset
.
Similarly, since
\pi_{Y}(B)=Y
, we have that

G(B)\cap([0,1]\times\{0\})\neq\emptyset
and

G(B)\cap([0,1]\times\{1\})\neq\emptyset
. Therefore, by

[
40
, Theorem 130, p. 158]
,

G(A)\cap G(B)\neq\emptyset
. Let
(a_{1},a_{2})\in A
and
(b_{1},b_{2})\in B

be such that

G((a_{1},a_{2}))=G((b_{1},b_{2}))
. Thus,
f(a_{1})=f(b_{1})
and
g(a_{2})=g(b_{2})
.
Since
f
and
g

are
\varepsilon
-maps,
d_{X}(a_{1},b_{1})<\varepsilon
and

d_{Y}(a_{2},b_{2})<\varepsilon
.
Thus,
\rho((a_{1},a_{2}),(b_{1},b_{2}))<\varepsilon
. We contradict (
2.1
).
Therefore,
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
.

K
of
X
, then
\Delta_{X}
is not a
\mathcal{T}
-closed subcontinuum
of
X^{2}
.

Proof.
By Theorem
4.4
,
\mathcal{T}(\Delta_{K})=K\times K
.
Since
\Delta_{K}\subseteq\Delta_{X}
,
K\times K\subseteq\mathcal{T}(\Delta_{X})
.
Since
K
is nondegenerate,
\mathcal{T}(\Delta_{X})\neq\Delta_{X}
.
Therefore,
\Delta_{X}
is not a
\mathcal{T}
-closed subset of
X^{2}
.

Q.E.D.

By Theorem

,

,

5.5 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a chainable continuum. If
X
is indecomposable,
then
\mathcal{T}(\Delta_{X})=X^{2}
. Hence,

\Delta_{X}
is not a
\mathcal{T}
-closed subset of
X^{2}
.

Proof.
Note that if
W
is a subcontinuum of
X^{2}

such that
Int(W)\neq\emptyset
,
then both
\pi_{1}(W)
and
\pi_{2}(W)
are subcontinua of
X
with
nonempty interior.
Since
X
is indecomposable,
\pi_{1}(W)=\pi_{2}(W))=X

[
33
, Corollary 1.4.35]
.
Thus,
W\cap\Delta_{X}\neq\emptyset
(Theorem
2.9
).
Therefore,
\mathcal{T}(\Delta_{X})=X^{2}
.

Q.E.D.

Next result follows from Theorems
5.2
and
5.5
.

5.6 Corollary
.

Let
X
be a chainable continuum. Then
\mathcal{T}(\Delta_{X})=X^{2}
if and only if
X
is indecomposable.

The following theorem tells us that the arc is the only chainable continuum
for which
\Delta_{X}
is
\mathcal{T}
-closed subcontinuum of
X^{2}
.

5.7 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a chainable continuum. Then
\Delta_{X}
is
\mathcal{T}
-closed subcontinuum
of
X^{2}
if and only if
X
is an arc.

Proof.
Suppose that
\Delta_{X}
is
\mathcal{T}
-closed. Since
X
is chainable,
there exist two points
p
and
q
in
X
such that
X
is irreducible
between
p
and
q

[
42
, Theorem 12.5]
. Note that
X=A\cup B
, where
A
and
B
are proper
subcontinua of
X
(Theorem
5.5
). Without loss of
generality, we may assume that
p\in A\setminus B
and
q\in B\setminus A
.
We consider two cases:

Case (1).

\{p\}=X\setminus\kappa(q)
and

\{q\}=X\setminus\kappa(p)
.

Let

M=\{(x,y)\in X^{2}\ |\ \text{ there exists }K\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X})\text{ such that }\{(p,q),(x,y)\}\subseteq K\}

and

N=\{(x,y)\in X^{2}\ |\ \text{ there exists }K\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X})\text{ such that }\{(q,p),(x,y)\}\subseteq K\}.

By
[
19
, Lemma 2.1]
,
M\cap N=\emptyset
. Also,
M
and
N

are continuumwise connected.

We prove that
Cl(M\cup N)=X^{2}
. Let
\alpha,\beta\colon[0,1]\to\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)
be
order arcs such that
\alpha(0)=\{p\}
,
\beta(0)=\{q\}
and

\alpha(1)=\beta(1)=X

[
41
, Theorem (1.8)]
.
Let
(z,w)\in X^{2}
and let
U
and
V
be open subsets of
X
such
that
(z,w)\in U\times V
.
We consider two more subcases:

Subcase (1.1).
There exist
t<s
such that

\alpha(t)\cap U\neq\emptyset
and

(\alpha(s)\setminus\alpha(t))\cap V\neq\emptyset
.

Let
a\in\alpha(t)\cap U
and let

b\in(\alpha(s)\setminus\alpha(t))\cap V
. Let
I=\alpha(t)\times\{b\}
.
Observe that

(p,b)\in I
and
I\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X})
. Since

\{p\}=X\setminus\kappa(q)
,
there exists a proper subcontinuum
Z
of
X
such that
\{b,q\}\subseteq Z
.
Note
that
p\in X\setminus Z
and
J=\{p\}\times Z
is a subcontinuum
of
X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
.
Since
(p,b)\in I\cap J
,
I\cup J
is a subcontinuum of

X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
such that

(p,q)\in I\cup J
. Thus,
I\cup J\subseteq M
. Since

(I\cup J)\cap(U\times V)\neq\emptyset
,
we have that
(z,w)\in Cl(M)
.

Subcase (1.2).

V\subseteq\alpha(t)
, for each
t

such that
\alpha(t)\cap U\neq\emptyset
.

Note that there exist
t^{\prime}<s^{\prime}
such that
\beta(t^{\prime})\cap U\neq\emptyset
and

(\beta(s^{\prime})\setminus\beta(t^{\prime}))\cap V\neq\emptyset
; because, if

V\subseteq\beta(t^{\prime})
,
for each
t
such that
\beta(t^{\prime})\cap U\neq\emptyset
, then

\alpha(t)\cup\beta(t^{\prime})=X

and
U\nsubseteq\alpha(t)\cup\beta(t^{\prime})
, a contradiction. Now,
with a similar argument
to the one given in Subcase
(1.1)
, we have that
(z,w)\in Cl(N)
.

Therefore, we have that
Cl(M\cup N)=X^{2}
. Now, suppose that there
exists
(r,s)\in Cl(M)\cap N
.
Since
\Delta_{X}
is
\mathcal{T}
-closed,
(r,s)\in N
and
N\cap\Delta_{X}=\emptyset
, we
have that there exists a subcontinuum
W
such that
(r,s)\in Int(W)
and

W\cap\Delta_{X}=\emptyset
. Observe that
W\cap M\neq\emptyset
and

W\cap N\neq\emptyset
. Thus, we have both
W\subseteq M
and
W\subseteq N
,
a contradiction to the fact that
M\cap N=\emptyset
. Therefore,

Cl(M)\cap N=\emptyset
.
Similarly, we have that
M\cap Cl(N)=\emptyset
. Hence,

X^{2}=M\cup N\cup\Delta_{X}
, where
M
and
N
are disjoint open
subsets of
X^{2}
.
By
[
22
, Theorem 3]
,
X
is an arc.

Case (2).
Either
X\setminus\kappa(p)
is nondegenerate
or
X\setminus\kappa(q)
is nondegenerate.

Assume that
X\setminus\kappa(p)
is nondegenerate. Let

K=Cl(X\setminus\kappa(p))
.
Observe that
K
is a nondegenerate proper subcontinuum of
X
. Note that if
K

has empty interior, by Lemma
2.8
,
K
is a terminal
subcontinuum of
X
. Hence,
by Corollary
5.3
,
\Delta_{X}
is not

\mathcal{T}
-closed. Thus, suppose that
Int(K)\neq\emptyset
.
By Lemma
2.8
,
K
is indecomposable. Let

(a,b)\in X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
be such that

a
and
b
are in
K
. Since
\Delta_{X}
is
\mathcal{T}
-closed, there
exists a subcontinuum
W
of
X^{2}
such
that
(a,b)\in Int(W)\subseteq W\subseteq X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
. Note that

K\subseteq\pi_{1}(W)\cap\pi_{2}(W)
and,
\pi_{1}(W)\subseteq\pi_{2}(W)
or

\pi_{2}(W)\subseteq\pi_{1}(W)
. Suppose that
\pi_{1}(W)\subseteq\pi_{2}(W)
and let

L=\pi_{2}(W)
. Since
X
is chainable,
L
is chainable
[
10
, Theorem (9.C.4)]
.
Moreover,
\Delta_{L}

and
W
are subcontinua of
L^{2}
such that
\pi_{1}(\Delta_{L})=L
and
\pi_{2}(W)=L
.
Thus, by Theorem
2.9
,
W\cap\Delta_{L}\neq\emptyset
. Since

\Delta_{L}\subseteq\Delta_{X}
,
W\cap\Delta_{X}\neq\emptyset
. A contradiction.
Therefore, by Cases
(1)
and
(2)
, we have that
X
is an arc.

Conversely, if
X
is an arc, then
X^{2}
is locally connected. Hence, by

[
33
, Theorem 2.1.37]
,
\Delta_{X}
is
\mathcal{T}
-closed.

Q.E.D.

5.8 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a continuum that is not an arc, and consider the following;

(1)

\Delta_{X}
is a
\mathcal{T}
-closed subcontinuum of
X^{2}
.

(2)

X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
is strongly continuumwise connected.

(3)

\Delta_{X}
is a subcontinuum of colocal connectedness of
X^{2}
.

(4)

X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
is continuumwise connected.

Then
(1)
,
(2)
and
(3)
are equivalent,
(1)
implies
(4)
.
Moreover, if
X
has the property of Kelley, then
(4)
is equivalent
(1)
.

Proof.
Suppose
\Delta_{X}
is a
\mathcal{T}
-closed subcontinuum
of
X^{2}
. Then, since
X
is not an arc, by

[
22
, Theorem 3]
,
X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
is connected.
Hence, by

[
33
, Theorem 4.2.9]
,
X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
is an
exhausted
\sigma
-continuum.
Now, by
[
33
, Theorem 1.4.75
(2)
]
,
X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}

is strongly continuumwise connected.
Assume that
X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
is strongly continuumwise
connected.
Since
X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
is open, we have that

X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}

is open and strongly continuumwise connected. By

[
33
, Theorem 1.4.75
(2)
]
,

X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
is an exhausted
\sigma
-continuum.
Therefore,
by
[
33
, Theorem 4.2.9]
,
\Delta_{X}
is a
\mathcal{T}
-closed
subcontinuum of
X^{2}
. Hence,
(1)
and
(2)
are equivalent.

By Theorem
2.3
, we have that
(2)
and
(3)
are equivalent.

If
\Delta_{X}
is a
\mathcal{T}
-closed subcontinuum of
X^{2}
, by
Theorem
4.15
,
X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
is continuumwise connected.
Thus,
(1)
implies
(4)
. Suppose
X
has the property of Kelley and

X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
is continuumwise connected. Since
X
has the
property of Kelley, by Theorem
2.4
,

X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
is strongly continuumwise connected.
Therefore,

\Delta_{X}
is a
\mathcal{T}
-closed subcontinuum of
X^{2}
.

Q.E.D.

In this section, we present partial answers to
[
9
, Problem 6.6]
.
Note that, since atomic maps are monotone
[
32
, Theorem 8.1.24]
,
we can extend
[
9
, Theorem 6.3]
to continua; i.e.,

6.1 Theorem
.

Let
X
and
Y
be continua and let
f\colon X\ \hbox{$\to\!\!\!\!\!\to$}\ Y
be an atomic open map.
Then
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
is homeomorphic to
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y)
.

6.2 Theorem
.

Let
X
and
Y
be continua and let
f\colon X\ \hbox{$\to\!\!\!\!\!\to$}\ Y
be a monotone open map.
Then:

(1)

\mathfrak{T}(Y)
may be embedded in
\mathfrak{T}(X)

and

(2)

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y)
may be embedded in

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
.

Proof.
Suppose
f
is an open map. Hence, by

[
33
, Theorem 1.6.16]
,

\Im(f)\colon 2^{Y}\ \hbox{$\to\!\!\!\!\!\to$}\ 2^{X}
given by
\Im(f)(B)=f^{-1}(B)

is a map. Hence, since

\Im(f)
is a one-to-one map between compacta,
\Im(f)

is an embedding.
Since
f
is monotone too, by
[
33
, Theorem 4.1.25]
,
we have that

\Im(f)(\mathfrak{T}(Y))\subset\mathfrak{T}(X)
, and

\Im(f)(\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y))\subset\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
. Thus,

\mathfrak{T}(Y)
may be embedded in
\mathfrak{T}(X)
, and

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y)
may be embedded in

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)

Q.E.D.

6.3 Remark
.

Note that, by
[
3
, Theorem 4.12]
, if
X
is a type
\lambda
continuum,
then there exists a bijection between
\mathfrak{T}(X)
and
2^{[0,1]}
, whose
restriction to
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
gives a bijection between

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)

and
\mathcal{C}_{1}([0,1])
.

6.4 Theorem
.

Suppose that a continuum
X
belongs to the following classes of continua:

(1)
Continuously irreducible continua.

(2)
Continuously type
A^{\prime}

\theta
-continuum.

(3)
Continuously type
A^{\prime}

\theta_{n}
-continuum,
n\in{\hbox{{\rm I$\!$\rm N}}}
.

Then we have that:

(1^{\prime})

\mathfrak{T}(X)
is homeomorphic to the Hilbert cube and

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
is homeomorphic to a
2
-cell.

(2^{\prime})

\mathfrak{T}(X)
is homeomorphic to the Hilbert cube and

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
is homeomorphic to a polyhedron.

(3^{\prime})

\mathfrak{T}(X)
is homeomorphic to the Hilbert cube and

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
is homeomorphic to a polyhedron.

Proof.
Suppose
(1)
, we show that
(1^{\prime})
is true. Since
X

is a a continuously irreducible continuum, by
[
29
, Lemma 3.3]
,
the quotient map
q\colon X\ \hbox{$\to\!\!\!\!\!\to$}\ [0,1]
is an open atomic map.
Then, by
[
9
, Theorem 6.3]
, we have that
\mathfrak{T}(X)

is homeomorphic to
2^{[0,1]}
; i.e.,
\mathfrak{T}(X)
is homeomorphic
to the Hilbert cube
[
44
, Theorem 3.6]
.
By Theorem
6.1
, we also obtain that

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{1}([0,1])
; i.e.,
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)

is homeomorphic to a
2
-cell
[
17
, 1, p. 267]
.

Assume
(2)
, we prove
(2^{\prime})
holds. Since
X
is
a continuously type
A^{\prime}

\theta
-continuum, by
[
31
, Theorem 3.6]
,
the quotient map
q\colon X\ \hbox{$\to\!\!\!\!\!\to$}\ G
, where
G
is a graph, is an atomic open map.
Then, by
[
9
, Theorem 6.3]
, we have that
\mathfrak{T}(X)

is homeomorphic
to
2^{D}
; i.e.,
\mathfrak{T}(X)
is homeomorphic to the Hilbert cube

[
45
, Theorem 4.4]
.
By Theorem
6.1
, we also obtain that

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{1}(G)
; i.e.,
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)

is homeomorphic to a polyhedron
[
17
, 6.4, p. 276]
.

By
[
31
, Corollary 3.9]
, if
X
is a continuously type
A^{\prime}

\theta
-continuum, then there exists
n\in{\hbox{{\rm I$\!$\rm N}}}
such that
X
is a

\theta_{n}
-continuum. Hence, the argument of the previous paragraph holds
for continuously type
A^{\prime}

\theta_{n}
-continua. Thus, assuming
(3)
, we
obtain
(3^{\prime})
.

Q.E.D.

6.5 Theorem
.

Suppose
X
is:

(1)
The Menger universal curve and
\widehat{X}
is the
Menger universal curve of pseudo-arcs.

(2)
The Sierpiński universal plane curve and
\widehat{X}

is the Sierpiński universal plane curve of pseudo-arcs.

(3)
The circle and
\widehat{X}
is the circle of pseudo-arcs.

(4)
The arc and
\widehat{X}
is the arc of pseudo-arcs.

Then we obtain that

(1^{\prime})
Both
\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{X})
and
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(\widehat{X})

are homeomorphic
to the Hilbert cube.

(2^{\prime})
Both
\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{X})
and
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(\widehat{X})

are homeomorphic
to the Hilbert cube.

(3^{\prime})

\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{X})
is homeomorphic to the Hilbert cube and

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(\widehat{X})
is homeomorphic to a
2
-cell.

(4^{\prime})

\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{X})
is homeomorphic to the Hilbert cube and

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(\widehat{X})
is homeomorphic to a
2
-cell.

Proof.
If
X
is the Menger universal curve and
\widehat{X}
is the Menger
universal curve of pseudo-arcs, by
[
9
, Corollary 6.5]
,

\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{X})

is homeomorphic to
2^{X}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(\widehat{X})
is
homeomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)
.
Thus,
\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{X})
is homeomorphic to the Hilbert cube

[
11
, Theorem 3.2]
, and since
X
does not have frHilbertee arcs, also

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(\widehat{X})
is homeomorphic to the Hilbert cube

[

, Theorem 4.1]
.

A similar argument proves the case of the Sierpiński universal plane curve.

X
is the circle and
\widehat{X}
is the circle of pseudo-arcs.

, Corollary 6.5]
,
\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{X})
is

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(\widehat{X})

. In this case, by

, Theorem 3.2]

\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{X})

\mathfrak{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(\widehat{X})

is homeomorphic to a

-cell
[

, 2, p. 267]

Regarding paper
[
43
]
, we have the following observations:

(1)
In part
(3)
of Proposition 4.3, the authors imply that if
\mathcal{T}
is
continuous on continua, then each subcontinuum of
X
is of
\mathcal{T}
-finite type.
We do not know if this is true or not. By the word “similarly”, we think that
they believe that the following is true:
The continuity of
\mathcal{T}
on continua implies that
\mathcal{T}
is idempotent on
continua. This is still an open question.

(2)
Part
(5)
of Proposition 4.3 is not accurate. If
X
is a homogeneous
continuum, then
\mathcal{T}
is idempotent on closed sets
[
32
, Theorem 4.2.32]
.
Hence, for each nonempty closed subset
A
of
X
, we know that
\mathcal{T}^{2}(A)=\mathcal{T}(A)
.

(3)
Part
(6)
of Proposition 4.3 is not accurate. Since the authors are only
considering nonempty closed subsets of a type
\lambda
continuum
X
, by
the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 7.5]
,
\mathcal{T}^{3}(A)=\mathcal{T}^{2}(A)
, for all nonempty closed
subsets

A
of
X
.

(4)
Regarding Corollary 4.8 and Theorem 4.9, it is well known that
atomic maps are monotone
[
32
, Theorem 8.1.24]
. Hence, it not
necessary to ask the map on those results to be monotone.

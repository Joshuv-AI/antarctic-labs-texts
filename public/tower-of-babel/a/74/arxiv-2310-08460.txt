Schrödinger-Poisson systems with zero mass in the Sobolev limiting case
Giulio Romani

Abstract
We study the existence of positive solutions for a class of systems which strongly couple a quasilinear Schrödinger equation driven by a weighted N N -Laplace operator and without the mass term, and a higher-order fractional Poisson equation. Since the system is considered in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , the limiting case for the Sobolev embedding, we consider nonlinearities with exponential growth. Existence is proved relying on the study of a corresponding Choquard equation in which the Riesz kernel is a sign-changing logarithm. This is in turn solved by means of a variational approximating procedure for an auxiliary Choquard equation where the logarithm is uniformly approximated by polynomial kernels.

Introduction

In this paper we investigate existence of solutions for the quasilinear Schrödinger-Poisson system in the whole space given by

\begin{cases}-{\rm div}\left(A(|x|)|\nabla u|^{N-2}\nabla u\right)=Q(|x|)\phi\,f(u)\ \ &\mbox{in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
(-\Delta)^{\frac{N}{2}}\phi=Q(|x|)F(u)&\mbox{in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\end{cases}

(SP
0
)

where
N\geq 2
, the positive weight functions
A
and
Q
enjoy a suitable behaviour at
0
and infinity,
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a positive nonlinearity, and
F(s):=\int_{0}^{s}f(t)\,{\rm d}t
its primitive function. The first evident peculiarity of this system is that the mass term is missing in the left-hand side of the Schrödinger equation, namely we are in the so-called "zero-mass case". Moreover, the quasilinear operator in the same equation is a weighted
p
-Laplacian with
p=N
, and since we are studying (
SP0
) in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we are lead to a functional setting which is critical with respect to the Sobolev embeddings. This implies that one can consider nonlinearities
f
with exponential growth, and that the kernel of the (possibly high-order, possibly fractional) operator in the Poisson equation is logarithmic. It is then of interest the study of the interplay of all these phenomena.

Systems posed in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
which couple a Schrödinger-type equation with a Poisson equation, emerge in several fields of Physics: in electrostatics, they model the interaction of two identically charged particles; in quantum mechanics, the self-interaction of the wave function with its own gravitational field; they also emerge in the Hartree theory for crystals and in astrophysics about selfgravitating boson stars; see
[
BF
,
LRZ
]
and references therein. In the higher-dimensional case
N\geq 3
, and when
f
has a polynomial growth, there is a extensive literature about Schrödinger-Poisson system, see the survey
[
MV2
]
and references therein. The usual first step to deal with such systems is to transform them into equivalent Choquard equations, which are nonlocal in their nonlinear part, by solving the Poisson equation by means of the Riesz kernel, and inserting it into the Schrödinger equation. Besides the effect of variables reduction, the advantage of this approach is that Choquard equations can be studied by variational techniques. This reduction principle is well-known and widely used in case the Riesz kernel is polynomial. For instance, if we consider the system with positive potential
V
given by

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+V(x)u=\phi\,f(u)\ \ &\mbox{in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
-\Delta\phi=F(u)&\mbox{in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\end{cases}

(SP)

when
N\geq 3
, the (positive) Riesz kernel of the Poisson equation is
c_{N}|x|^{2-N}
, where
c_{N}
is an explicit positive constant depending on the dimension
N
, and the corresponding Choquard equation is then

-\Delta u+V(x)u=c_{N}\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{\mu}}\ast F(u)\right)f(u)\quad\ \mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}

(1.1)

with
\mu=N-2
. Here, new interesting phenomena arise, such as the appearance of a lower-critical exponent in addition to the usual upper-critical exponent, as in the Sobolev case, see
[
MV1
,
MV2
,
CZ
,
CVZ
]
.

In the Sobolev limiting case the Riesz kernel is logarithmic and therefore unbounded both from below and from above. This, together with the fact that it is sign-changing, introduces a major difficulty with respect to the higher dimensional setting. Indeed, the Choquard equation which is formally related to (
SP
) when
N=2
is

-\Delta u+V(x)u=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\log\frac{1}{|x|}\ast F(u)\right)f(u)\quad\ \mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

(Ch)

However, due to the convolution term, the functional associated to (

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

In this setting, if
f(u)=u
, an approach originating from the unpublished work of Stubbe
[
St
]
was proposed in
[
CW
,
DW
,
BCV
,
CW2
]
, according to which (

, where the logarithmic convolution term is well-defined.

However, in dimension two it is well-known that the maximal degree of summability for functions belonging to
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is exponential. The more general case of
f
with exponential growth, was considered in
[
CT
]
, where the authors establish a proper functional setting by means of a log-weighted version of the Pohožaev-Trudinger inequality, so that the functional associated to (
Ch
) turns out to be well-defined. An extension of these techniques to a quasilinear extension of (
Ch
) has been recently established in
[
BCT
]
, where also the relationship between the Choquard equation and the corresponding Schrödinger-Poisson system has been carefully analysed; see also
[
CMR
]
for the study of a weighted version of (
Ch
) with similar techniques. We also refer to
[
ACTY
]
in which exponential nonlinearities in (
1.1
), still mantaining the polynomial Riesz kernel and so loosing the connection to the Schrödinger-Poisson system, have been first investigated, see also
[
dACS
,
AFS
]
in the case of weights; on the other hand, Schrödinger-Poisson systems with logaritmic kernel and exponential nonlinearity, but not in gradient form as (

[

BM1
]

[

]
.

The method recently proposed by
[

to study (

[

and extended to the nonlinear fractional setting in

, exploits instead an approximation approach: the logarithmic Riesz kernel in (

The main advantage of this method is that one can work in the natural Sobolev space associated to the equation and exploit simpler variational tools, while the price to pay is of course a more careful analysis, since at the end one needs to be able to pass to the limit.

The special case of an identically zero potential in Schrödinger equations and in systems such as (
SP
), is in the literature referred to as "zero-mass case", and emerges in physical context, e.g. in the nonabelian gauge theory of particle physics, such as the study of the Yang-Mills equation, see
[

D^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
defined as the completion of

C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\|u\|_{D^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

[

[

,

A possible way to overcome the drawback of working with the spaces

has been noticed in the recent paper
[

]
by means of weight functions. Here, the authors study the (possibly quasilinear) Schrödinger equation with zero mass in the limiting Sobolev case

)

Schrödinger-Poisson systems with zero mass in the limiting Sobolev case, namely (

1.2
The approximating method

As we mentioned in the Introduction, the applicability of variational methods to the logarithmic Choquard equation (

However,

[

]
, see also
[

,

]
, we will use an approximation technique as follows. Observing that

\log\frac{1}{t}=\lim_{\mu\to 0^{+}}\frac{t^{-\mu}-1}{\mu},

(1.7)

set

G_{\mu}(x):=\frac{|x|^{-\mu}-1}{\mu},\qquad\mu\in(0,1]\,,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

and consider the approximating problem

-{\rm div}\big(A(x)|\nabla u|^{N-2}\nabla u\big)=C_{N}\left(G_{\mu}(\cdot)\ast Q(\cdot)F(u)\right)Q(x)f(u)\quad\ \mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.8)

with corresponding functional

\begin{split}J_{\mu}(u):&=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\!A(x)|\nabla u|^{N}+\frac{C_{N}}{2\mu}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}QF(u)\,{\rm d}x\bigg]^{2}\\
&\quad-\frac{C_{N}}{2\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\mu}}Q(x)F(u(x))Q(y)F(u(y))\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\\
&=\frac{1}{N}\|u\|^{N}-\frac{C_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(G_{\mu}(\cdot)\ast QF(u)\right)QF(u)\,{\rm d}x\,.\end{split}

Unlike the logarithmic term in the original functional
J
, the power-type singularity in
G_{\mu}
can be handled by the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (Lemma
2.3
), and it is possible to prove under conditions (
f_{0}
)-(
f_{2}
) that
J_{\mu}
is well-defined and
C^{1}
on
E
with

\begin{split}J^{\prime}_{\mu}(u)v=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\!A(x)|\nabla u|^{N-2}\nabla u\nabla v\,{\rm d}x-C_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(G_{\mu}(\cdot)\ast QF(u))Qf(u)v\,{\rm d}x\,,\end{split}

(1.9)

provided conditions (A) and (Q) are fulfilled, as we will see next (Lemma
3.1
). The overall strategy consists then in producing a critical point
u_{\mu}\in E
for
J_{\mu}
for all
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
for some
\mu_{0}>0
, and then pass to the limit as
\mu\to 0
in order to obtain a critical point
u_{0}
for the original functional
J
in
E
, which a posteriori satisfies
\left(\log\frac{1}{|\cdot|}\ast QF(u)\right)QF(u)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In this way, on the one hand we do not need to restrict to a constraint or weighted subspace of the natural space
E
, but of course, on the other hand, we have to be careful in our estimates, namely they must be independent of
\mu
so that we are allowed to pass to the limit at the end.

Note also that, since the functions
A
and
Q
are radially symmetric, and in order to retrieve compactness in light of Theorem
A
, we will work in the radial subspace
E_{\text{rad}}
. This is not a restriction since, by means of a suitable version of Palais’ principle of symmetric criticality in
[

, Lemma 4.1]
, any critical point

for

in

is also a critical point in the whole

, namely a weak solution of (
Ch0
).

3.1
The approximating problem

We start by showing that the functional

Lemma 3.1
.

Under assumptions (A), (Q), and (

)-(
f_{2}
) the exists
\mu_{0}
such that for all

Proof.

We need to check the finiteness the two terms which originate from the nonlinearity

. First, by (

) and the Hölder inequality with

, one has

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}QF(u)&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q|u|^{\widetilde{p}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q|u|^{pq^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q\,\Phi_{\alpha r,j_{0}}(u)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\end{split}

with

by Lemma
2.2
. Since assumption (Q) is fulfilled, the last term is finite for all
q>0
in view of Theorem
B
, while the continuous embedding given by Theorem

ensures that the first two terms are finite too, provided one chooses

sufficiently small so that

.
On the other hand, by means of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (Lemma

\begin{split}\bigg|&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{|\cdot|^{\mu}}\ast QF(u)\right)QF(u)\bigg|\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|QF(u)|^{\frac{2N}{2N-\mu}}\right)^{\frac{2N-\mu}{N}}\\
&\lesssim\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q^{\frac{2N}{2N-\mu}}|u|^{\frac{2N{\widetilde{p}}}{2N-\mu}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q^{\frac{2N}{2N-\mu}}|u|^{\frac{2Npq^{\prime}}{2N-\mu}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q^{\frac{2N}{2N-\mu}}\Phi_{r\alpha,j_{0}}(u)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]^{\frac{2N-\mu}{N}}\!\!\!,\end{split}

(3.1)

with

again by Lemma
2.2
. We claim that there exists
\mu_{0}>0
such that for all

possibly depending on

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{Q^{\frac{2N}{2N-\mu}}(r)}{r^{\nu_{0}}}<+\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{Q^{\frac{2N}{2N-\mu}}(r)}{r^{\nu}}<+\infty\,.

Indeed, since

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{Q(r)}{r^{\nu_{0}\left(1-\frac{\mu}{2N}\right)}}<+\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{Q(r)}{r^{\nu}\left(1-\frac{\mu}{2N}\right)}<+\infty\,.

b_{0}\left(1+\tfrac{\mu}{2N-\mu}\right)\geq\nu_{0}\quad\mbox{and}\quad b\left(1+\tfrac{\mu}{2N-\mu}\right)\leq\nu.

\end{cases}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\nu:=\begin{cases}b\left(1+\frac{\mu}{2N-\mu}\right)\ \ &\mbox{if}\ \,b\geq 0,\\
b&\mbox{if}\ \,b<0,\\
\end{cases}

it is easy to verify that (

) holds true, that

Lemma 3.2

Let

(i) By Lemma

\begin{split}J_{\mu}(u)&\geq\frac{\|u\|^{N}}{N}-\frac{C_{N}}{2}\iint_{\{|x-y|\leq 1\}}\frac{|x-y|^{-\mu}-1}{\mu}\,Q(x)F(u(x))Q(y)F(u(y))\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\\

(3.4)

(ii) Take

Integrating this on

By the compact support of

\begin{split}J_{\mu}(t\varphi_{0})&=\frac{t^{N}}{N}\|\varphi_{0}\|^{N}-\frac{C_{N}}{2}\iint_{\{|x-y|\leq\frac{1}{2}\}}\frac{|x-y|^{-\mu}-1}{\mu}\,Q(x)F(t\varphi_{0}(x))Q(y)F(t\varphi_{0}(y))\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\\

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

be a Cerami sequence of

\nu_{*}>1
, and
\kappa_{0}>1

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q^{\nu}f(u_{n})u_{n}\,\,{\rm d}x\leq C\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q^{\nu}F(u_{n})^{\kappa}\,{\rm d}x\leq C\,.

for any
\nu\in[1,\nu_{*})
and
\kappa\in[1,\kappa_{0})

Proof.

First we note that, for a weight
Q
satisfying (Q), then
Q^{\nu}
fulfills (Q) for small values of
\nu>1
. Indeed,
Q^{\nu}
is readily continuous and positive, and moreover it is easy to see that

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{Q^{\nu}(r)}{r^{\epsilon_{0}}}=\left(\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{Q(r)}{r^{b_{0}}}r^{b_{0}-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{\nu}}\right)^{\nu}<+\infty,

provided
b_{0}-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{\nu}\geq 0
, and similarly

\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{Q^{\nu}(r)}{r^{\epsilon}}=\left(\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{Q(r)}{r^{b}}r^{b-\frac{\epsilon}{\nu}}\right)^{\nu}<+\infty,

provided
b-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{\nu}\geq 0
. Taking
\epsilon_{0}:=b_{0}\nu
and
\epsilon:=b\nu
, then
\epsilon_{0},\epsilon>-N
provided
\nu<\nu_{*}:=\min\{\tfrac{N}{b},\tfrac{N}{b_{0}}\}

We show next the uniform boundedness of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q^{\nu}F(u_{n})^{\kappa}\,{\rm d}x
. Inspired by
[
BCT
]

and define

\begin{split}\|v_{n}\|^{N}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\!A(x)|H^{\prime}(u_{n})\nabla u_{n}|^{N}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\!A(x)\left(\frac{N}{2}\frac{F(u_{n})f^{\prime}(u_{n})}{(f(u_{n}))^{2}}-\frac{N}{2}+1\right)|\nabla u_{n}|^{N}.\\
&\end{split}

Recalling (
3.11
) and that one can choose

\|v_{n}\|^{N}=Nc_{\mu}+o_{n}(1)\,.

(3.30)

\begin{split}v_{n}&=H(u_{n})=\int_{0}^{s_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{N}{2}\frac{F(s)f^{\prime}(s)}{(f(s))^{2}}-\frac{N}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\!\,{\rm d}s+\int_{s_{\varepsilon}}^{u_{n}}\left(\frac{N}{2}\frac{F(s)f^{\prime}(s)}{(f(s))^{2}}-\frac{N}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}\!\,{\rm d}s\\

u_{n}
is uniformly bounded in the first term, we can estimate

\int_{\{u_{n}\leq s_{\varepsilon}\}}Q^{\nu}F(u_{n})^{\kappa}\,{\rm d}x\leq C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q^{\nu}|u_{n}|^{{\widetilde{p}}\kappa}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|u_{n}\|^{{\widetilde{p}}\kappa}\leq C

, and then Lemma

&\leq C_{\varepsilon,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q^{\nu}\Phi_{\kappa\alpha_{\varepsilon}\|v_{n}\|^{\frac{N}{N-1}},j_{0}}\left(\frac{v_{n}}{\|v_{n}\|}\right)\end{split}

&\quad\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\!\left(\int_{\{|x-y|>1\}}\log\frac{1}{|x-y|}Q(y)F(u_{0}(y))\,{\rm d}y\right)Q(x)f(u_{0}(x))\varphi(x)\,{\rm d}x\,.\end{split}

(3.54)

Combining (

Next, we show (

\begin{split}&\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\!\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\log|x-y|Q(y)F(u_{0}(y))\,{\rm d}y\right)Q(x)F(u_{0}(x))\,{\rm d}x\bigg|\\
&\leq\liminf_{\mu\to 0}\bigg(\int\int_{\{|x-y|\leq 1\}}G_{\mu}(x-y)Q(y)F(u_{\mu}(y))\,{\rm d}y\,Q(x)F(u_{\mu}(x))\,{\rm d}x\\
&-\int\int_{\{|x-y|\geq 1\}}G_{\mu}(x-y)Q(y)F(u_{\mu}(y))\,{\rm d}y\,Q(x)F(u_{\mu}(x))\,{\rm d}x\bigg).\end{split}

(3.55)

Using (
3.49

3.8
, and (
3.48

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle\int_{\{|x-y|\leq 1\}}G_{\mu}(x-y)Q(y)F(u_{\mu}(y))\,{\rm d}y\,Q(x)F(u_{\mu}(x))\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\int_{\{|x-y|\leq 1\}}\frac{Q(y)F(u_{\mu}(y))}{|x-y|^{\frac{4(\omega-1)}{3\omega}}}\,{\rm d}y\right)Q(x)F(u_{\mu}(x))\,{\rm d}x\leq C

uniformly for

\displaystyle\frac{C_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\int_{\{|x-y|>1\}}G_{\mu}(x-y)Q(y)F(u_{\mu}(y))\,{\rm d}y\right)Q(x)F(u_{\mu}(x))\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}+\frac{C_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\int_{\{|x-y|\leq 1\}}G_{\mu}(x-y)Q(y)F(u_{\mu}(y))\,{\rm d}y\right)Q(x)F(u_{\mu}(x))\,{\rm d}x-\frac{\|u_{\mu}\|^{N}}{N}\leq C

This in particular implies that

is well-defined, and hence, by Palais’ principle of symmetric criticality, we can conclude that

is a critical point of

in

To conclude the proof, we need to show that

, hence

\begin{split}0&=J_{\mu}^{\prime}(u_{\mu})[u_{\mu}]=\|u_{\mu}\|^{N}-C_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(G_{\mu}(\cdot)\ast QF(u_{\mu})\right)Qf(u_{\mu})u_{\mu}\,{\rm d}x\\

(3.57)

By (

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}QF(u_{\mu})\,{\rm d}x\right|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q|u_{\mu}|^{\widetilde{p}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q|u_{\mu}|^{p\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q\,\Phi_{\alpha\widetilde{\theta},j_{0}}(u_{\mu})\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}=o_{\mu}(1),

4
Existence for the Schrödinger-Poisson system (

): Proof of Theorem

Let
u\in E
be the weak solution of the Choquard equation (
Ch0
) given by Theorem
1.1
and define

\phi_{u}(x):=C_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\log\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right)Q(y)F(u(y))\,{\rm d}y\,.

Note that, buying the lines of Lemma
3.6
, one can show that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}QF(u_{\mu})\,{\rm d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}QF(u)\,{\rm d}x\,,

as
\mu\to 0
, where
(u_{\mu})_{\mu}\subset E
is the sequence of solutions of the family of approximating problems (
1.8
) with

. This in particular implies that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}QF(u)\,{\rm d}x<+\infty\,.

(4.1)

By the radial Lemma
2.4
we can actually improve it.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u\in E_{\text{rad}}
be the weak solution of (
Ch0
) given by Theorem
1.1
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\log(1+|x|)Q(x)F(u(x))\,{\rm d}x<+\infty\,.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
there exists
r_{0}>0
such that
|u(x)|\lesssim\|u\||x|^{-\frac{\ell}{N-1}}
, hence by (
f_{2}
) and (Q), up to a bigger

, we have

Q(x)F(u(x))\lesssim|x|^{b-\frac{\ell{\widetilde{p}}}{N-1}}\quad\ \mbox{for}\ \,|x|>r_{0}\,.

Therefore, by (
4.1
) and the elementary estimate
\log(1+|x|)\lesssim|x|^{d}
for
|x|>r_{0}
,
d>0
, we infer

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\log(1+|x|)Q(x)F(u(x))\,{\rm d}x&\lesssim C\log(1+r_{0})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(x)F(u(x))\,{\rm d}x\\
&\quad+\int_{|x|\geq r_{0}}\log(1+|x|)|x|^{b-\frac{\ell{\widetilde{p}}}{N-1}}\,{\rm d}x\\
&\lesssim 1+\int_{r_{0}}^{+\infty}\rho^{d+b-\frac{\ell{\widetilde{p}}}{N-1}+N-1}\,{\rm d}\rho<+\infty\,,\end{split}

for small values of

, since the integrability condition (
3.53
) is fulfilled.
∎

We are now in the position to prove Theorem
1.2
following the approach of
[

,
CLR
]
. First, we need to show that
\phi_{u}\in L_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

:

\begin{split}\frac{1}{C_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}&\frac{|\phi_{u}(x)|}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(y)F(u(y))\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\log\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right|\frac{1}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\right){\rm d}y\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(y)F(u(y))\left(\int_{\{|x-y|>1\}}\frac{\log|x-y|}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\{|x-y|\leq 1\}}\log\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,{\rm d}x\right){\rm d}y\\
&\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(y)F(u(y))\,\,{\rm d}y\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\log(1+|x|)}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}\,\,{\rm d}x\right)\\
&\quad+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\log(1+|y|)Q(y)F(u(y))\,\,{\rm d}y\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\,{\rm d}x}{1+|x|^{N+2s}}\right)\\
&\quad+\|\log(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(B_{1}(0))}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(y)F(u(y))\,{\rm d}y<+\infty\end{split}

for all

Define now the function

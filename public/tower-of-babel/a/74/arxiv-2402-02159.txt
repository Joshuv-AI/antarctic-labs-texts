Abstract
Fluid Antenna System (FAS) is recognized as a promising technology for enhancing communication performance. In this context, we explored the potential of FAS-assisted wireless powered communication systems. Specifically, the transmitter, equipped with FAS, harvests the radio frequency (RF) signal from a power beacon and utilizes the harvested energy for data transmission to the receiver. To evaluate the performance of the considered systems, we derive both the analytical and asymptotic expressions of the outage probability. Simulation results indicate that the diversity of the considered network closely aligns with the number of ports. Besides, it is also revealed that the port selection criteria based solely on single-hop configurations yield a diversity order of only one.

II
System Model

In this study, we examine a wireless powered system wherein the transmitter, receiving wireless power from a power beacon, utilizes this energy to transmit information to the receiver at a data rate
\displaystyle R
. To enhance the performance, the transmitter is equipped with a fluid antenna, which can flexibly adjust the antenna fluid to the optimal port, out of the total
\displaystyle M
switchable ports. In the following, we will detail the procedure of communications and introduce the FAS channel model, respectively.

II-A

Communications Model

To clarify, we define the channel parameters between the power beacon and the
\displaystyle m
-th port of the transmitter as
\displaystyle h_{1,m}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\psi_{1})
, and those between the
\displaystyle m
-th port of the transmitter and the receiver as
\displaystyle h_{2,m}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\psi_{2})
. Here,
\displaystyle m\in\mathcal{M}=\{1,2,\cdots,M\}
,
\displaystyle\psi_{1}
represents the average channel gain from the power beacon to the transmitter, and
\displaystyle\psi_{2}
denotes the average channel gain from the transmitter to the receiver. Assuming that the
\displaystyle m
-th port is selected for both wireless power harvesting and subsequent data transmission, the power signal received at the transmitter is given as

\displaystyle\displaystyle g_{1}=h_{1,m}\sqrt{P_{S}}b+v_{1},

(1)

where
\displaystyle P_{S}
denotes the transmit power of the power beacon,
\displaystyle b\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
represents the wireless power signal, and
\displaystyle v_{1}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2})
denotes the zero-mean complex Gaussian noise. Therefore, the total harvested energy at the transmitter is
\displaystyle|h_{1,m}|^{2}P_{S}T_{1}
, where
\displaystyle T_{1}
is the time duration of the first hop.

Then, the transmitter utilizes the harvested wireless energy to transmit data
\displaystyle s\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
. Consequently, the signal received at the receiver is written as

\displaystyle\displaystyle g_{2}=h_{2,m}|h_{1,m}|\sqrt{P_{S}\rho\theta}s+v_{2},

(2)

where
\displaystyle v_{2}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2})
represents the zero-mean complex Gaussian noise,
\displaystyle\rho
denotes the energy conversion efficiency,
\displaystyle\theta=T_{1}/T_{2}
, and
\displaystyle T_{2}
is the time duration of the second hop.

Drawing on the analysis from Eq. (
1
) and Eq. (
2
), the received SNR at the receiver utilizing the
\displaystyle m
-th port can be formulated as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Gamma_{m}=\frac{P_{S}\rho\theta}{\sigma^{2}}\gamma_{1,m}\gamma_{2,m},

(3)

where
\displaystyle\gamma_{i,m}=|h_{i,m}|^{2}
for
\displaystyle i\in\{1,2\}
represent the channel gains of the first and second hops, respectively.

II-B

FAS Channel Model

In this subsection, we present the details of the channel model of FAS under the Rayleigh fading environments
[
9
]
, which accounts for the combined impact of port correlation. It is important to note that while some more intricate models are available, such as those described in
[
17
]
, they often yield results that are impractical for widespread applications due to their complexity. Building on the Rayleigh fading channel model, the expression for
\displaystyle h_{i,m}
is expressed as

\displaystyle\displaystyle h_{i,m}=\sqrt{\mu}h_{i,0}+\sqrt{1-\mu}e_{i,m},

(4)

where
\displaystyle e_{i,m}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\psi_{i})
for
\displaystyle m\in\mathcal{M}
are independently and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables (RVs). Here,
\displaystyle\psi_{i}
denotes the average channel gain both from the power beacon to the transmitter and from the transmitter to the receiver.
Moreover,
\displaystyle h_{1,0}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\psi_{1})
and
\displaystyle h_{2,0}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\psi_{2})
serve as virtual reference channels for these respective links. Consequently, the PDFs of
\displaystyle\gamma_{i,0}=|h_{i,0}|^{2}
for
\displaystyle i\in\{1,2\}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\displaystyle f_{\gamma_{i,0}}(x)=\frac{1}{\psi_{i}}e^{-\frac{x}{\psi_{i}}}.

(5)

Additionally,
\displaystyle\mu
represnts the correlation factor, which is given by
[
21
]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\sqrt{{}_{1}F_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2};1;\frac{3}{2};-\pi^{2}W^{2}\Big)-\frac{J_{1}(2\pi W)}{2\pi W}},

(6)

where

\displaystyle{}_{a}F_{b}
denotes the generalized hypergeometric function and
\displaystyle J_{1}(\cdot)
is the first kind Bessel function with order 1.

Given a fixed channel parameter
\displaystyle h_{i,0}
, and in accordance with
\displaystyle\gamma_{i,0}
, the associated channel gain for
\displaystyle h_{i,m}
, expressed as
\displaystyle\gamma_{i,m}=|h_{i,m}|^{2}
, follows a non-central chi-square distribution. Consequently, the conditional PDF can be expressed as

\displaystyle\displaystyle f_{\gamma_{1,m}|\gamma_{1,0}=x_{0}}(x)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\omega_{1}e^{-\omega_{1}(x+\mu x_{0})}I_{0}\big(2\omega_{1}\sqrt{\mu x_{0}x}\big),

(7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle f_{\gamma_{2,m}|\gamma_{2,0}=y_{0}}(y)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\omega_{2}e^{-\omega_{2}(y+\mu y_{0})}I_{0}\big(2\omega_{2}\sqrt{\mu y_{0}y}\big),

(8)

where
\displaystyle\omega_{i}=\big(\psi_{i}(1-\mu)\big)^{-1}
. Besides,
\displaystyle I_{0}(u)
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind with order
\displaystyle 0
, which can be expressed in series representation as
[
22
]

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{0}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2k}}{2^{2k}k!\Gamma(k+1)}.

(9)

Combining (
7
)–(
9
), we further derive
\displaystyle f_{\gamma_{1,m}|\gamma_{1,0}=x_{0}}(x)
and
\displaystyle f_{\gamma_{2,m}|\gamma_{2,0}=y_{0}}(y)
as

\displaystyle\displaystyle f_{\gamma_{1,m}|\gamma_{1,0}=x_{0}}(x)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}x_{0}^{k}e^{-\omega_{1}\mu x_{0}}x^{k}e^{-\omega_{1}x},

(10)

\displaystyle\displaystyle f_{\gamma_{2,m}|\gamma_{2,0}=y_{0}}(y)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}y_{0}^{n}e^{-\omega_{2}\mu y_{0}}y^{n}e^{-\omega_{2}y},

(11)

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle a_{k}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{1}^{2k+1}\mu^{k}}{(k!)^{2}},

\displaystyle\displaystyle b_{n}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{2}^{2n+1}\mu^{n}}{(n!)^{2}}.

(12)

In communication networks equipped with FAS, the transmitter possesses the capability to switch the antenna fluid to the optimal port, thereby having additional degree-of-freedom to improve the system performance. To maximize the received SNR at the receiver, the selection of the port for transmission is governed by the following criterion:

\displaystyle\displaystyle m^{*}=\arg\max_{m\in\mathcal{M}}(\gamma_{1,m}\gamma_{2,m}).

(13)

Hence, the received SNR at the receiver is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Gamma_{m^{*}}=\frac{P_{S}\rho\theta}{\sigma^{2}}\max_{m\in\mathcal{M}}(\gamma_{1,m}\gamma_{2,m}).

(14)

III
Outage Analysis

In this section, we derive the closed-form expression of the outage probability. Subsequently, the asymptotic expression of the outage probability is derived. These derivations offer valuable insights for the practical application of the FAS-enabled WPC system.

III-A

Closed-Form Outage Probability

The outage of communications happens when the received SNR falls below the given threshold
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm{th}}
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm{th}}=2^{R}-1
. Consequently, the outage probability is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{out}}=\Pr\left(\max_{m\in\mathcal{M}}(\gamma_{1,m}\gamma_{2,m})\leq z\right),

(15)

where
\displaystyle z=\frac{\Gamma_{\rm{th}}\sigma^{2}}{P_{S}\rho\theta}
.

To derive the analytical expression for the outage probability
\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{out}}
, we reformulate
\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{out}}
as

\displaystyle\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{\gamma_{1,0},\gamma_{2,0}}\left[F(z\big|\gamma_{1,0},\gamma_{2,0})\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(z\big|x_{0},y_{0})f_{\gamma_{1,0}}(x_{0})f_{\gamma_{2,0}}(y_{0})dx_{0}dy_{0},

(16)

where
\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{X}\left[\cdot\right]
denotes the expectation over RV
\displaystyle X
. Besides,

\displaystyle F(z\big|x_{0},y_{0})
represents the conditional CDF with given
\displaystyle\gamma_{1,0}=x_{0}
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{2,0}=y_{0}
, which is defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle F(z\big|x_{0},y_{0})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\Pr\big(\max_{m\in\mathcal{M}}(\gamma_{1,m}\gamma_{2,m})\leq z\big|\gamma_{1,0}=x_{0},\gamma_{2,0}=y_{0}\big).

(17)

Expanding on this, and considering that
\displaystyle\gamma_{1,m}\gamma_{2,m}
for
\displaystyle m\in\cal{M}
are independent with each other, the conditional CDF
\displaystyle F(z\big|x_{0},y_{0})
, when conditioned on
\displaystyle\gamma_{1,0}=x_{0},\gamma_{2,0}=y_{0}
, can be further rewritten as

\displaystyle\displaystyle F(z\big|x_{0},y_{0})=\big(\Phi(z\big|x_{0},y_{0})\big)^{M},

(18)

where
\displaystyle\Phi(z\big|x_{0},y_{0})
is defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(z\big|x_{0},y_{0})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\Pr\left(\gamma_{1,m}\gamma_{2,m}\leq z\big|\gamma_{1,0}=x_{0},\gamma_{2,0}=y_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{z/y}f_{\gamma_{1,m}|\gamma_{1,0}=x_{0}}(x)f_{\gamma_{2,m}|\gamma_{2,0}=y_{0}}(y)dxdy

(19)

Building upon this, further mathematical manipulations are applied to calculate this function. The detailed derivation leads to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(z\big|x_{0},y_{0})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{k}b_{n}x_{0}^{k}e^{-\omega_{1}\mu x_{0}}y_{0}^{n}e^{-\omega_{2}\mu y_{0}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\frac{z\omega_{1}}{y}}x^{k}e^{-\omega_{1}x}y^{n}e^{-\omega_{2}y}dxdy

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}b_{n}x_{0}^{k}e^{-\omega_{1}\mu x_{0}}y_{0}^{n}e^{-\omega_{2}\mu y_{0}}}{\omega_{1}^{k+1}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma\Big(k+1,\frac{z\omega_{1}}{y}\Big)y^{n}e^{-\omega_{2}y}dy

(20)

where
\displaystyle\gamma(\alpha,x)
is the upper Gamma function, which can be expressed as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\gamma(\alpha,x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}e^{-t}t^{\alpha-1}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(\alpha-1)!\bigg(1-e^{x}\sum_{d=0}^{\alpha-1}\frac{x^{d}}{d!}\bigg).

(21)

Applying (
III-A
) in (
III-A
),
\displaystyle\Phi(z\big|x_{0},y_{0})
can be expressed in an series representation as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(z\big|x_{0},y_{0})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}b_{n}x_{0}^{k}e^{-\omega_{1}\mu x_{0}}k!y_{0}^{n}e^{-\omega_{2}\mu y_{0}}}{\omega_{1}^{k+1}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\times\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{n}e^{-\omega_{2}y}dy-\sum_{l=0}^{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{z\omega_{1}}{y}-\omega_{2}y}\frac{(z\omega_{1})^{l}}{l!y^{l}}dy\bigg)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}b_{n}x_{0}^{k}e^{-\omega_{1}\mu x_{0}}k!y_{0}^{n}e^{-\omega_{2}\mu y_{0}}}{\omega_{1}^{k+1}\omega_{2}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\times\big(n!-\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{2(z\omega_{1}\omega_{2})^{\frac{n+l+1}{2}}}{l!}K_{n-l+1}(2\sqrt{z\omega_{1}\omega_{2}})\big),

(22)

where
\displaystyle K_{\nu}(\cdot)
denotes the second kind modified Bessel function with order
\displaystyle\nu
.
The final expression in (

[

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{o}^{\infty}x^{a-1}e^{-\frac{b}{x}-cx}dx=2\Big(\frac{b}{c}\Big)^{\frac{a}{2}}K_{a}(2\sqrt{bc}).

(23)

Then, by substituting (
III-A

\displaystyle\displaystyle F(z\big|x_{0},y_{0})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{m},m\in\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{m},n\in\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}D_{k,n}x_{0}^{\epsilon_{k}}y_{0}^{\lambda_{n}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\times e^{-M\omega_{1}\mu x_{0}}e^{-M\omega_{2}\mu y_{0}},

(24)

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle D_{k,n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\prod_{m=1}^{M}\big(n_{m}!-\sum_{l=0}^{k_{m}}\frac{2(z\omega_{1}\omega_{2})^{\frac{n_{m}+l+1}{2}}}{l!}K_{n_{m}-l+1}(2\sqrt{z\omega_{1}\omega_{2}})\big)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\omega_{1}^{k_{m}}\omega_{2}^{n_{m}}\mu^{k_{m}+n_{m}}}{k_{m}!(n_{m}!)^{2}},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\epsilon_{k}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}k_{m},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}n_{m}.

(25)

To derive the analytical expression of the outage probability

\displaystyle f_{\gamma_{i,0}}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle P_{\rm{out}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{m},m\in\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{m},n\in\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}\frac{D_{k,n}}{\psi_{1}\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}x_{0}^{\epsilon_{k}}e^{-M\omega_{1}\mu\frac{x_{0}}{\psi_{1}}}dx_{0}\times\int_{0}^{\infty}y_{0}^{\lambda_{n}}e^{-M\omega_{2}\mu\frac{y_{0}}{\psi_{2}}}dy_{0}

III-B

Asymptotic Analysis

To more effectively assess the outage performance of the system under consideration, a theorem is proposed as following.

Theorem 1

In the high SNR region, particularly for small values of
\displaystyle z
, the asymptotic expression of outage probability is

\displaystyle\displaystyle P{\rm{out}}\approx\chi\big(\beta\ln(\beta)\big)^{M},

(28)

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\chi=\Big(\frac{1-\mu}{M\mu+1-\mu}\Big)^{2},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\beta=\frac{\Gamma_{\rm{th}}\sigma^{2}}{P_{S}\rho\phi_{1}\phi_{2}(1-\mu)^{2}}.

(29)

Proof 1:
By setting
\displaystyle U_{1}
and
\displaystyle U_{2}
to 0, and then applying the approximations
\displaystyle e^{-x}=1-x
and
\displaystyle K_{0}(x)\approx-\ln(x)
for tiny value of
\displaystyle|x|

[
23
]
, we can easily obtain the asymptotic expression.

\displaystyle\hfill\blacksquare

Accordingly, the following remark can be made concerning the proposed FAS-assisted WPC systems.

Remark 1

It is demonstrated that the diversity order of the system under consideration is approximately
\displaystyle M
. Consequently, larger values of
\displaystyle M
can significantly enhance the performance of the FAS-assisted WPC systems. Furthermore, as evident from equation (
III-A
), the outage probability
\displaystyle P{\rm{out}}
inversely correlates with the parameter
\displaystyle\mu
. Therefore, with an increased value of
\displaystyle W
, the efficiency of the FAS-assisted WPC systems can be further improved.

V
Conclusions

In this work, we proposed a performance analysis of the FAS-assisted WPC systems. We derived both exact and asymptotic expressions for the outage probability of the proposed system with Rayleigh fading channels. Our analysis revealed that the diversity order of the system is equal to the number of ports. Numerical results were provided to demonstrate the correctness of our analysis.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we present a heat semigroup approach to some intertwining formulas which arise in conformal CR geometry. The prototypical example of the questions we are interested in can be described as follows. In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
, for
0<s<1
consider the pseudodifferential operator which in Fourier transform is given by
\widehat{(-\Delta)^{s}u}(\xi)=(2\pi|\xi|)^{2s}\hat{u}(\xi)
,
see
[
32
,
35
]
. Then, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and each fixed
y>0
, one has the following intertwining relation

(1.1)

\left(-\Delta\right)^{s}\left((|x|^{2}+y^{2})^{-\frac{n-2s}{2}}\right)=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+s\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}-s\right)}\left(2y\right)^{2s}(|x|^{2}+y^{2})^{-\frac{n+2s}{2}}.

The objective of this note is to use the heat equation to establish a suitable variant of (
1.1
) in which
(-\Delta)^{s}
is replaced by the nonlocal conformal horizontal Laplacian
\mathscr{L}_{s}
on a Lie group of Heisenberg type
\mathbb{G}
. It is worth mentioning here that our approach is new even in the classical case of (
1.1
).

To provide the reader with some historical perspective we recall that (
1.1
) implicitly appeared in the celebrated 1983 work of Lieb concerning the best constants in the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequalities, see (3.12), (3.13) in the proof of
[
31
, Theorem 3.1]
. In this connection we observe that a notable consequence of (
1.1
) is that it provides a family of positive solutions to the nonlocal Yamabe equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(1.2)

\left(-\Delta\right)^{s}u=u^{\frac{n+2s}{n-2s}}.

The uniqueness of such positive solutions was subsequently proved by Chen, Li, and Ou in
[
8
]
. A different way of looking at (
1.1
), which also underscores its geometric invariance, goes back to the ideas in
[
3
,
4
]
. For a function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote by
f^{\star}
its push forward to
\mathbb{S}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
through the stereographic projection. Branson characterized the conformal pseudodifferential operators
A_{s}
of order
2s
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
satisfying the intertwining formula

(1.3)

A_{s}\left(\left(\left(\frac{1+|\cdot|^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-s}f\right)^{\star}\right)=\left(\left(\frac{1+|\cdot|^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}+s}(-\Delta)^{s}f\right)^{\star},

and proved that they have the following spectral representation

(1.4)

A_{s}=\frac{\Gamma(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}}+s+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}}-s+\frac{1}{2})}.

The number
A_{s}(1)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-s)}
is called, up to a renormalising constant, the fractional
Q_{s}
-curvature of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. For more insights on these aspects we refer the reader to
[
2
,
7
,
23
]
.
We note that from (
1.3
) it is immediate to recognise the validity of (
1.1
). It suffices to take
f(x)=\big(\frac{1+|x|^{2}}{2}\big)^{s-\frac{n}{2}}
, and observe that

(-\Delta)^{s}\big(\frac{2}{1+|x|^{2}}\big)^{\frac{n}{2}-s}=\big(\frac{2}{1+|x|^{2}}\big)^{\frac{n}{2}+s}\big(A_{s}(1^{\star})\big)^{\star}=\big(\frac{2}{1+|x|^{2}}\big)^{\frac{n}{2}+s}A_{s}(1)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-s)}\big(\frac{2}{1+|x|^{2}}\big)^{\frac{n}{2}+s}.

In our main result, Theorem
1.1
below, we present a new approach to intertwining formulas such as (
1.1
) which is exclusively based on the heat equation.
To explain the main ideas without delving into technical aspects we mention that, instead of looking at
(-\Delta)^{s}
, we consider the nonlocal heat operator
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}
and its
extension problem
: given a function
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})
, find
U\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{y})
such that

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\mathfrak{P}^{(s)}U\overset{def}{=}\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{1-2s}{y}\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}+\Delta_{x}U-\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}=0,\\
U(x,t,0)=u(x,t).\end{cases}

We claim that the fundamental solution
q^{(s)}(x,y,t)
of the operator
\mathfrak{P}^{(s)}
in (
1.5
) can be used to provide a simple proof of (
1.1
).
To see this we note that, if
w\in\mathbb{R}^{2(1-s)}
and
y=|w|
, then
\mathfrak{P}^{(s)}
represents the action on functions
U(x,w,t)=\overline{U}(x,y,t)
of the heat operator
\Delta_{x}+\Delta_{w}-\partial_{t}
in the space with fractal dimension
\mathbb{R}^{n+2(1-s)}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{t}
whose fundamental solution (with pole at the origin) is given by

(1.6)

q^{(s)}(x,y,t)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{n}{2}+1-s}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}+y^{2}}{4t}}.

If we denote by
q^{(-s)}(x,y,t)
the heat kernel obtained by replacing
s
into
-s
in (
1.6
), then by Bochner’s subordination the two functions

(1.7)

E^{(\pm s)}(x,y)\overset{def}{=}\int_{0}^{\infty}q^{(\pm s)}(x,y,t)dt

are the fundamental solutions of the time-independent differential operators
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{1\mp 2s}{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\Delta_{x}
. If we now use the elementary (but very important) consequence of (
1.6
) that

(1.8)

\int_{0}^{\infty}q^{(\pm s)}(x,y,t)dt=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n\mp 2s}{2})}{4\pi^{\frac{n}{2}+1\mp s}}(|x|^{2}+y^{2})^{-\frac{n\mp 2s}{2}},

then in view of (
1.7
) the problem of proving (
1.1
) is reduced to that of establishing the equivalent dimension-free relation

(1.9)

\left(-\Delta\right)^{s}\left(E^{(s)}(\cdot,y)\right)(x)=\left(2\pi y\right)^{2s}E^{(-s)}(x,y).

To prove (
1.9
) we once again bring to the center stage the heat semigroup
P_{t}=e^{-t\Delta}
. If, in fact, instead of the Fourier transform definition of
(-\Delta)^{s}
we use the (equivalent) one given by the following formula of Balakrishnan
[
1
]
,

(1.10)

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=-\frac{s}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{1+s}}(P_{t}u(x)-u(x))dt,

then the proof of (
1.9
) hinges on the computation of
P_{t}(E^{(s)}(\cdot,y))(x)
, with
E^{(s)}
defined by (
1.7
). As we show in (
2.7
) below and subsequent considerations, the Chapman-Kolmogorov identity and an elementary change of variable allow to easily complete this computation and establish (
1.9
).
By comparison, a direct proof of (
1.1
) via Fourier transform rests on elaborate computations involving special functions which tend to overshadow the geometric content of the formula itself (for complete details see
[
20
, Lemma 8.6]
).

The pseudodifferential operators
(-\Delta)^{s}
and (
1.4
) have a counterpart in CR geometry. In this context the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with coordinates
(z,\sigma)

, with

, the CR conformal nonlocal operator

was first introduced in the work by Branson et al.

via the spectral formula

(1.11)

\mathscr{L}_{s}=2^{s}|T|^{s}\frac{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{L}|T|^{-1}+\frac{1+s}{2})}{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{L}|T|^{-1}+\frac{1-s}{2})},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0<s<1,

where we have denoted by
\mathscr{L}
the Kohn-Spencer horizontal Laplacian in
\mathbb{H}^{n}

We note that, when
0<s<1

\mathscr{L}_{s}
is dramatically different from
\mathscr{L}^{s}=(-\mathscr{L})^{s}
(for the definition of this operator see (
2.2

s=1
. In fact, by formally letting
s\nearrow 1
in (

\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)

\mathscr{L}_{1}=-\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{L}^{1}

In their work
[
16
]
Frank et al. introduced a new extension problem for the nonlocal operator (
1.11

\mathscr{L}^{s}
in
[
13

(based on the work of Caffarelli and Silvestre

), and used scattering theory to solve it. Subsequently, Roncal and Thangavelu employed a parabolic version of the extension problem in
[
16

to establish some optimal Hardy inequalities in
\mathbb{H}^{n}

[
33
]
, or more in general in groups of Heisenberg type
[
34

. Such time-dependent extension problem plays an important role in our recent work
[
21

To introduce the relevant geometric framework we recall that Lie groups of Heisenberg type were introduced by Kaplan
[
28
]
in connection with hypoellipticity questions. They are geometrically interesting since on one hand they retain most of the important symmetries of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, on the other they naturally arise as the nilpotent component
N
in the Iwasawa decomposition
\mathbb{G}=KAN
of a simple group of rank one, see
[
11
]
.
In a group of Heisenberg type
\mathbb{G}
(see Section
3
for the relevant definitions) the following generalisation of (
1.11
) was introduced in
[
34
]
. Let
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
be the Lie algebra of
\mathbb{G}
, and denote
m=\operatorname{dim}V_{1}
,

(we note that the complex structure of
\mathbb{G}
forces
m=2n
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
). We routinely identify
\mathfrak{g}
with
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and the generic point
g\in\mathbb{G}
with its logarithmic coordinates
(z,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Let
\mathscr{L}
be a given horizontal Laplacian in
\mathbb{G}
associated with an orthonormal basis of the horizontal layer
V_{1}
. Consider the pseudo-differential operator of order
2s
defined by

(1.12)

\mathscr{L}_{s}=2^{s}(-\Delta_{\sigma})^{s/2}\frac{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{L}(-\Delta_{\sigma})^{-1/2}+\frac{1+s}{2})}{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{L}(-\Delta_{\sigma})^{-1/2}+\frac{1-s}{2})},\ \ \ \ \ \ 0<s<1.

We note that for
\mathbb{H}^{n}
the dimension of the vertical layer is
k=1
and
(-\Delta_{\sigma})^{s/2}
in (
1.12

|T|^{s}
, thus giving back (

In a group of Heisenberg type
\mathbb{G}
the counterpart of the intertwining formula (
1.1
) is given by the following

(1.13)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{s}\left(((|z|^{2}+y^{2})^{2}+16|\sigma|^{2})^{-\frac{m+2k-2s}{4}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2+2s}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2k+2s}{4}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2-2s}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2k-2s}{4}\right)}(4y)^{2s}((|z|^{2}+y^{2})^{2}+16|\sigma|^{2})^{-\frac{m+2k+2s}{4}},

for
(z,\sigma)\in\mathbb{G}
, and
y>0
. We stress that in the particular case of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
(which corresponds to the case
m=2n
and
k=1
) the function appearing in the left-hand side of (
1.13
) defines, up to group translations, the unique extremal of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequalities obtained by Frank and Lieb in
[
15
]
. We also note that, similarly to (
1.2
) above, a remarkable by-product of (
1.13
) is that up to a renormalising factor the function
u(z,\sigma)=((|z|^{2}+y^{2})^{2}+16|\sigma|^{2})^{-\frac{Q-2s}{4}}
(where
Q=m+2k
denotes the so-called homogeneous dimension of the group
\mathbb{G}
) provides a positive solution to the following nonlocal Yamabe equation

(1.14)

\mathscr{L}_{s}u=u^{\frac{Q+2s}{Q-2s}}.

Following
[
16

, one can consider semilinear equations as (
1.14

\mathbb{H}^{n}
as (flat-)model case: proving existence of positive solutions in this framework would in fact resolve the fractional CR Yamabe problem posed in
[

[

\begin{cases}\mathfrak{P}_{(s)}U\overset{def}{=}\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{1-2s}{y}\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}+\frac{y^{2}}{4}\Delta_{\sigma}U+\mathscr{L}U-\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \text{in}\ \mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{y},\\

\displaystyle q_{(s)}((z,\sigma),t,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{k}}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{m}{2}+k+1-s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}e^{-\frac{i}{t}\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{\sinh|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+1-s}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}+y^{2}}{4t}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh|\lambda|}}d\lambda.

Similarly to the case of

is the fundamental solution with pole at the origin of the time-independent part of

\mathfrak{L}_{(s)}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{1-2s}{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\frac{y^{2}}{4}\Delta_{\sigma}+\mathscr{L}.

The reader should bear in mind that
\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}((z,\sigma,y)

Theorem 1.1

(Geometric intertwining)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a group of Heisenberg type and let
s\in(0,1)
. For every
g\in\mathbb{G}
and
y>0
one has

(1.18)

\mathscr{L}_{s}(\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)=(2\pi y)^{2s}\mathfrak{e}_{(-s)}(g,y).

Theorem
1.1
is the CR counterpart of the dimension-free identity (
1.9
). It is worth mentioning here that in our proof of (
1.18
) we do not use the definition
(
1.12
) of
\mathscr{L}_{s}
since the latter would immediately lead into the elaborate machinery of non-commutative Fourier analysis and group representation theory. Instead, we base our analysis on the equivalent representation (
3.8
) below which is only formally similar to (
1.10
).

With Theorem
1.1
in hands, we combine it with the following result, which is
[
21
, Theorem 1.4]
, that further underscores the geometric relevance of the functions
\mathfrak{e}_{(\pm s)}((z,\sigma),y)
.

Theorem A.

Let
0<s\leq 1
. In any group of Heisenberg type
\mathbb{G}
the distribution in the thick space
\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{y}
defined by (
1.17
) is given by

(1.19)

\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}((z,\sigma),y)=\frac{\Gamma(s)}{(4\pi)^{1-s}}C_{(s)}(m,k)\ ((|z|^{2}+y^{2})^{2}+16|\sigma|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{m}{2}+k-s)},

where we have let

(1.20)

C_{(s)}(m,k)=\frac{2^{\frac{m}{2}+2k-3s-1}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{m}{2}+1-s))\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{m}{2}+k-s))}{\pi^{\frac{m+k+1}{2}}\Gamma(s)}.

An equation similar to (
1.19
) holds if we replace
s
with
-s
, provided that
\Gamma(s)
is replaced by
|\Gamma(-s)|
.

It should now be clear to the reader that the combination of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem

immediately implies the intertwining formula (

), see Corollary

below.

In closing, we provide a brief description of the paper. In Section
2
we present a fairly general non-geometric version of the intertwining phenomenon that goes well beyond the setting of Lie groups of Heisenberg type. We warn the reader that, although on a formal level the statement of Theorem
2.1
seems similar that of Theorem
1.1
, the geometric content of the latter result is lost in the former. Our intent is to illustrate the flexibility of the heat equation approach which allows to treat situations in which the arsenal of Fourier analysis is not readily available. For this we have chosen the framework of stratified nilpotent Lie groups (aka Carnot groups) of arbitrary step, but the proof of Theorem
2.1
holds with no changes for the fractional powers of the infinitesimal generator of a Dirichlet form under by now standard assumptions which guarantee Gaussian estimates for the relevant heat semigroup. Section
3
is devoted to proving Theorem
1.1
. We begin by explaining in some detail the geometric extension problem (
1.15
), and we introduce the two operators
P_{(\pm s),t}
. We highlight the new difficulties that one encounters with respect to the non-geometric case and we establish two preliminary results of an elementary character, Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, that serve to overcome such obstructions. Finally, we prove Theorem
1.1
. In Corollary
3.3
, which closes the paper, we combine this result with Theorem
A
to establish (
1.13
).

2.
Non-conformal intertwining formula

In this section we establish in the setting of stratified nilpotent Lie groups of arbitrary steps (aka Carnot groups) a general version of the intertwining formula (
1.9
), see Theorem
2.1
below. On one hand, our purpose is to show that the heat equation approach outlined in the case of flat
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the introduction works in great generality. On the other hand, with the present section we intend to highlight the dramatic differences between the geometric and the non-geometric operators
\mathscr{L}_{s}
and
\mathscr{L}^{s}
. To introduce our discussion we recall that a Carnot group of step
r
is a simply-connected Lie group
\mathbb{G}
whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is stratified and
r
-nilpotent. This means that
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus...\oplus V_{r}
, with
[V_{1},V_{i}]=V_{i+1}
,
i=1,...,r-1
, and
[V_{1},V_{r}]=\{0\}
. The bracket generating layer
V_{1}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is called the horizontal layer. If we denote by
L_{g}(g^{\prime})=g\circ g^{\prime}
the operator of left-translation on
\mathbb{G}
, and define left-invariant vector fields
X_{j}(g)=dL_{g}(e_{j})
, where
\{e_{1},...,e_{m}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
V_{1}
, then a horizontal Laplacian in
\mathbb{G}
(associated with the basis
\{e_{1},...,e_{m}\}
) is defined by
\mathscr{L}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}^{2}
. Such differential operator fails to be elliptic at every point
g\in\mathbb{G}
, but according to Hörmander’s theorem in
[
25
]
it is hypoelliptic in
\mathbb{G}
. In
[
14
]
Folland proved the existence of a strictly positive kernel
p(g,g^{\prime},t)
,
C^{\infty}
off the diagonal, such that the heat semigroup
P_{t}=e^{-t\mathscr{L}}
is represented by
P_{t}f(g)=\int_{\mathbb{G}}p(g,g^{\prime},t)f(g^{\prime})dg^{\prime}
. Such semigroup is stochastically complete (
P_{1}1=1
) and a contraction on
L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Furthermore, the kernel
p(g,g^{\prime},t)
satisfies the Gaussian estimates

(2.1)

Ct^{-Q/2}e^{-\alpha\frac{d(g,g^{\prime})^{2}}{t}}\leq p(g,g^{\prime},t)\leq C^{-1}t^{-Q/2}e^{-\beta\frac{d(g,g^{\prime})^{2}}{t}},

for appropriate universal constants
C,\alpha,\beta>0
. In (
2.1
) we have indicated with
Q=\sum_{j=1}^{r}j\operatorname{dim}V_{j}
the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}
with respect to the anisotropic dilations associated with the grading of the Lie algebra, whereas
d(g,g^{\prime})
denotes the control distance, see
[
14
,
36
]
.
The semigroup
P_{t}
is all that is needed to introduce the fractional powers
\mathscr{L}^{s}=(-\mathscr{L})^{s}
by means of the following formula due to Balakrishnan

(2.2)

\mathscr{L}^{s}u(g)=-\frac{s}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{1+s}}(P_{t}u(g)-u(g))dt.

Next, we consider the parabolic extension problem for the pseudodifferential operator
(\partial_{t}-\mathscr{L})^{s}
, see
[
19
]
: given a function
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})
, find
U\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{y})
such that

\begin{cases}\mathfrak{P}^{(s)}U\overset{def}{=}\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{1-2s}{y}\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}+\mathscr{L}U-\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}=0,\\
U(g,t,0)=u(g,t).\end{cases}

Since we can think of the differential operator
\mathfrak{P}^{(s)}
as a heat operator on the group
\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{R}^{2(1-s)}_{w}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{t}
acting on functions
U(g,w,t)=\overline{U}(g,y,t)
, with
y=|w|
, its fundamental solution with pole at the identity is given by

(2.3)

q^{(s)}(g,t,y)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{1-s}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}p(g,e,t),

where we have denote by
e\in\mathbb{G}
the identity element. We stress that the function
q^{(s)}(g,t,y)
in (
2.3
) is dramatically different from its geometric counterpart

q_{(s)}((z,\sigma),t,y)
in (
1.16
) above which is not a product.
Hereafter, we indicate with the
q^{(-s)}(g,t,y)
the function obtained by changing
s
into
-s
in (
2.3
) (see (
2.9
) below), and we define

(2.4)

\mathfrak{e}^{(\pm s)}(g,y)\overset{def}{=}\int_{0}^{\infty}q^{(\pm s)}(g,t,y)dt.

It should be clear to the reader that the function
\mathfrak{e}^{(s)}
in (
2.4
) is the fundamental solution (with pole at the identity) of the time-independent extension operator
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{1-2s}{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\mathscr{L}
.
We have the following result.

Theorem 2.1

(Non-geometric intertwining)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group and
s\in(0,1)
. For any
g\in\mathbb{G}
and
y>0
one has

(2.5)

\mathscr{L}^{s}(\mathfrak{e}^{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)=(2\pi y)^{2s}\mathfrak{e}^{(-s)}(g,y).

Proof.

First, we observe that if
u
is a
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
function which suitably decays at infinity, then
the estimates (
2.1
) allow to integrate by parts in (
2.2
) obtaining

(2.6)

\mathscr{L}^{s}u(g)=-\frac{s}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\tau^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\partial_{t}P_{t}u(g)dtd\tau=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\partial_{t}P_{t}u(g)dt.

If we apply
P_{t}
to
u(g)=\mathfrak{e}^{(s)}(g,y)
, and we use (
2.4
), we find

(2.7)

\displaystyle P_{t}\mathfrak{e}^{(s)}(g,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{G}}p(g,g^{\prime},t)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{1-s}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4\tau}}p(g^{\prime},0,\tau)d\tau dg^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{1-s}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4\tau}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}p(g,g^{\prime},t)p(g^{\prime},e,\tau)dg^{\prime}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{1-s}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4\tau}}p(g,e,t+\tau)d\tau,

where in the last equality we have used the Chapman-Kolmogorov equation (semigroup property for
P_{t}
)

p(g,g^{\prime\prime},t+\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{G}}p(g,g^{\prime},t)p(g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime},\tau)dg^{\prime}\quad\mbox{ for }g^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{G}.

Substituting this formula in (
2.6
), we find

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{s}(\mathfrak{e}^{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(4\pi)^{(s-1)}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\tau}{t}\right)^{s}\partial_{t}\left(e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4\tau}}p(g,e,t+\tau)\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau}dt.

The change of variable

(2.8)

(v,\rho)=\left(t+\tau,\frac{\tau}{t}\right),\qquad\qquad\text{or}\qquad\qquad(t,\tau)=\left(\frac{v}{1+\rho},\frac{v\rho}{1+\rho}\right),

for which
dtd\tau=\frac{v}{(1+\rho)^{2}}dvd\rho
and
\partial_{t}=\partial_{v}-\frac{\rho(1+\rho)}{v}\partial_{\rho}
, now gives

\mathscr{L}^{s}(\mathfrak{e}^{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)=\frac{-(4\pi)^{s-1}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{s-1}}{1+\rho}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v}-\frac{\rho(1+\rho)}{v}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\right)\left(e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4v}\frac{1+\rho}{\rho}}p(g,0,v)\right)dvd\rho.

Observing that, as a function of
v
,
e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4v}\frac{1+\rho}{\rho}}p(g,0,v)
tends to
0
both as
v\to 0^{+}
and
v\to\infty
thanks to (
2.1
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{s}(\mathfrak{e}^{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)=\frac{(4\pi)^{s-1}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{s}}{v}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left(e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4\rho v}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4v}}p(g,0,v)\right)dvd\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{(4\pi)^{s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4\pi v}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4v}}p(g,0,v)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left(e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4\rho v}}\right)d\rho\right)dv

\displaystyle=\frac{(4\pi)^{s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4\pi v}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4v}}p(g,0,v)\left(\frac{y^{2}}{4v}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{s-1}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4\rho v}}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}\right)dv.

Finally, keeping in mind that

\frac{y^{2}}{4v}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{s-1}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4\rho v}}\frac{d\rho}{\rho}=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{(4v)^{s}}y^{2s},

and recalling

(2.9)

q^{(-s)}(g,v,y)=\frac{1}{(4\pi v)^{1+s}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4v}}p(g,e,v),

we reach the desired conclusion (
2.5
).
∎

In the Abelian case when
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the functions
\mathfrak{e}^{(\pm s)}(g,y)
coincide with
E^{(\pm s)}(x,y)
in (
1.7
). However, we warn the reader that for a general non-Abelian group
\mathbb{G}
the conformal significance of formulas (
1.6
) and (
1.8
) is lost. We close this section by recording that, as an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.1
and formulas (
1.6
)-(
1.8
), we have the following.

Corollary 2.2
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
n\geq 2
, we have for
0<s<1
,

\left(-\Delta\right)^{s}\left((|x|^{2}+y^{2})^{-\frac{n-2s}{2}}\right)=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+s\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}-s\right)}\left(2y\right)^{2s}(|x|^{2}+y^{2})^{-\frac{n+2s}{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ y>0.

Proof.

We observe that (
2.3
) and its counterpart obtained by changing
s
into
-s
presently give

(2.10)

q^{(\pm s)}(g,t,y)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{1\mp s}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{n}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{n}{2}+1\mp s}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}+y^{2}}{4t}}.

From (
2.10
) and the definition (
2.4
) we find

(2.11)

\mathfrak{e}^{(\pm s)}(g,y)\overset{def}{=}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{n}{2}+1\mp s}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}+y^{2}}{4t}}dt=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n\mp 2s}{2})}{4\pi^{\frac{n}{2}+1\mp s}}(|x|^{2}+y^{2})^{-\frac{n\mp 2s}{2}}.

Substituting the right-hand side of (
2.11
) in (
2.5
) of Theorem
2.1
we reach the desired conclusion.

∎

3.
Conformal intertwining formula

The objective of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
following the ideas outlined in Sections
1
and
2
. We begin by introducing the relevant geometric framework. Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group of step
r=2
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
, which we assume endowed with an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and induced norm
|\cdot|
. We fix orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},...,e_{m}\}
and
\{\varepsilon_{1},...,\varepsilon_{k}\}
for
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
respectively. Points
z\in V_{1}
and
\sigma\in V_{2}
will be identified with either one of the representations
z=\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}e_{j}
,
\sigma=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\sigma_{\ell}\varepsilon_{\ell}
, or also
z=(z_{1},...,z_{m})
,
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{k})
. Accordingly, whenever convenient we will identify the point
g=\exp(z+\sigma)\in\mathbb{G}
with its logarithmic coordinates
(z,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. The Kaplan mapping
J:V_{2}\to\operatorname{End}(V_{1})
is defined by

(3.1)

\langle J(\sigma)z,\zeta\rangle=\langle[z,\zeta],\sigma\rangle=-\langle J(\sigma)\zeta,z\rangle,

for
z,\zeta\in V_{1}
and
\sigma\in V_{2}
.
Clearly,
J(\sigma)^{\star}=-J(\sigma)
, and one has
<J(\sigma)z,z>=0
. By (
3.1
) and the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula, see e.g. p. 12 of
[
9
]
,

\exp(z+\sigma)\exp(\zeta+\tau)=\exp{\left(z+\zeta+\sigma+\tau+\frac{1}{2}[z,\zeta]\right)},

we obtain the non-Abelian multiplication in
\mathbb{G}

(3.2)

g\circ g^{\prime}=(z,\sigma)\circ(\zeta,\tau)=\big(z+\zeta,\sigma+\tau+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}<J(\varepsilon_{\ell})z,\zeta>\varepsilon_{\ell}\big).

From (
3.2
) it is easy to recognise that
e=(0,0)
and
(z,\sigma)^{-1}=(-z,-\sigma)
.

Henceforth in this section we assume that
\mathbb{G}
is of Heisenberg type. By this we mean that for every
\sigma\in V_{2}
one has

J(\sigma)^{2}=-|\sigma|^{2}\ {\mathbb{I}}_{V_{1}}.

We now denote by
\mathscr{L}
the horizontal Laplacian associated with the orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
of the horizontal layer
V_{1}
, see the opening of Section
2
.

We consider the extension problem (
1.15
) above: given a function
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})
, find a function
U\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{y})
such that

(3.3)

\begin{cases}\mathfrak{P}_{(s)}U\overset{def}{=}\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial y^{2}}+\frac{1-2s}{y}\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}+\frac{y^{2}}{4}\Delta_{\sigma}U+\mathscr{L}U-\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \text{in}\ \mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{y},\\
U(g,t,0)=u(g,t).\end{cases}

For the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
the time-independent version of (
3.3
) was first introduced in the cited work of Frank et al.
[
16
]
. Subsequently, Roncal and Thangavelu considered the time-dependent problem (
3.3
) in their cited papers
[
33
,
34
]
. Our approach is quite different from that in these works since, as we have mentioned above, these authors do not rely on (
3.3
) to establish (
1.13
), but instead use non-commutative Fourier analysis and group representation theory.

We continue to denote by
q_{(\pm s)}((z,\sigma),t,y)
respectively the fundamental solution of
\mathfrak{P}_{(s)}
introduced in (
1.16
), and the function obtained from it by changing
s
into
-s
. We now consider the kernels in the thin space
\mathbb{G}\times(0,\infty)
defined by

(3.4)

\mathscr{K}_{(\pm s)}((z,\sigma),t)=(4\pi t)^{1\mp s}q_{(\pm s)}((z,\sigma),t,0).

From (
1.16
) and the definition (
3.4
) it is easy to see that the kernels
\mathscr{K}_{(\pm s)}
have the following explicit expression

(3.5)

\mathscr{K}_{(\pm s)}((z,\sigma),t)=\frac{2^{k}}{\left(4\pi t\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}e^{-\frac{i}{t}\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{\sinh|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+1\mp s}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4t}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh|\lambda|}}d\lambda,\ \ \ \ 0<s\leq 1.

It is worth noting here that if we take
s=1
in (
3.5
) we obtain the Gaveau-Hulanicki-Cygan heat kernel in
\mathbb{G}
(we refer the reader to
[
22
]
for a recent pde-based derivation of such kernel). With (
3.5
) in hands, and by slightly abusing the notation, we define

\mathscr{K}_{(\pm s)}(g,g^{\prime},t)=\mathscr{K}_{(\pm s)}(g^{-1}\circ g^{\prime},t)=\mathscr{K}_{(\pm s)}((g^{\prime})^{-1}\circ g,t).

With such functions in hands, we next introduce two operators on
L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
by the formula

(3.6)

P_{(\pm s),t}u(g)=\int_{\mathbb{G}}\mathscr{K}_{(\pm s)}(g,g^{\prime},t)u(g^{\prime})dg^{\prime}.

As for
P_{(s),t}
, we mention that its
raison d’être
is in the fact, which is one of the main results in
[
21
]
, that the operator

(3.7)

\mathscr{I}_{(2s)}u(g)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}P_{(s),t}u(g)dt

provides the inverse of
\mathscr{L}_{s}
. The relevance of the operator
P_{(-s),t}
, instead, is underscored by the following formula which was proved in
[
34
]

(3.8)

\mathscr{L}_{s}u(g)=-\frac{s}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{1+s}}\left[P_{(-s),t}u(g)-u(g)\right]dt.

This preliminary discussion brings us to the heart of the present section, the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Our plan is to proceed as closely as possible to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, but we immediately encounter some difficulties. To explain this point we mention that in the non-geometric setting of Section
2
there are two aspects that play a crucial role: (i) the same heat kernel
p(\cdot,t)
occurs both in the expression (
2.6
) of
\mathscr{L}^{s}
and in that of the function
\mathfrak{e}^{(s)}
; (ii) the Chapman-Kolmogorov identity enters crucially in the final equality of the key identity (
2.7
). Both facts fail to hold in the present conformal setting. A third more pervasive complication is represented by the very different nature of the fundamental solutions
q^{(\pm s)}
and
q_{(\pm s)}
of the parabolic extension problems for
\mathscr{L}^{s}
and
\mathscr{L}_{s}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
rests on two preliminary lemmas in which we circumvent the difficulties listed above. In the first one we establish a replacement of the semigroup property for the group convolution of the different kernels
q_{(-s)}(\cdot,t,0)
and
q_{(s)}(\cdot,\tau,y)
which respectively appear in
P_{(-s),t}
and
\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}(\cdot,y)
). We mention that the case
y=0
of the following lemma was proved in
[
21
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix
s\in(0,1)
,
g\in\mathbb{G}
,
t,\tau>0
, and
y\geq 0
. Then, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}

\displaystyle q_{(-s)}((g^{\prime})^{-1}\circ g,t,0)q_{(s)}(g^{\prime},\tau,y)\,dg^{\prime}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}\right)^{1-s}\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi t|\lambda|}\right)^{1+s}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi(t+\tau)|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi(t+\tau)|\lambda|}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}}d\lambda.

Proof.

To establish (
3.9
), we fix
t,\tau>0
,
y\geq 0
, and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. By partial Fourier transform with respect to the vertical variable
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we observe that the desired conclusion (
3.9
) will be true if we can prove that for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
the following holds

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}e^{-2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{G}}q_{(-s)}((g^{\prime})^{-1}\circ g,t,0)q_{(s)}(g^{\prime},\tau,y)\,dg^{\prime}d\sigma=\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}\right)^{1-s}\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi t|\lambda|}\right)^{1+s}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi(t+\tau)|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi(t+\tau)|\lambda|}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}}.

The rest of the proof of the lemma will thus be devoted to establishing (
3.10
). Recalling the definition of
q_{(\pm s)}
in (
1.16
), by a simple change of variable we see that

(3.11)

q_{(\pm s)}(g^{\prime},\tau,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma^{\prime},\lambda\rangle}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+1\mp s}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}(|z^{\prime}|^{2}+y^{2})\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}}d\lambda.

From the expression (
3.2
) of the group law, we also have

(3.12)

\displaystyle q_{(\pm s)}((g^{\prime})^{-1}\circ g,t,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}e^{2\pi i\left(\langle\sigma-\sigma^{\prime},\lambda\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle J(\lambda)z,z^{\prime}\right\rangle\right)}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi t|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+1\mp s}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi t|\lambda|}}d\lambda.

Next, we note that by (
3.11
), (
3.12
), and applying twice the Fourier inversion formula, for every fixed
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
we can rewrite the left-hand side of (
3.10
) in the following way

(3.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}e^{-2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{G}}q_{(-s)}((g^{\prime})^{-1}\circ g,t,0)q_{(s)}(g^{\prime},\tau,y)\,dg^{\prime}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}e^{-2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma-\sigma^{\prime},\mu\rangle}e^{\pi i\langle J(\mu)z,z^{\prime}\rangle}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma^{\prime},\omega\rangle}\left(\frac{|\mu|}{2\sinh 2\pi t|\mu|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+1+s}\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{|\omega|}{2\sinh 2\pi\tau|\omega|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+1-s}\times e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}\frac{|\mu|}{\tanh 2\pi t|\mu|}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}(|z^{\prime}|^{2}+y^{2})\frac{|\omega|}{\tanh 2\pi\tau|\omega|}}dg^{\prime}d\omega d\mu d\sigma

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi t|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+1+s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}e^{-2\pi i\langle\sigma^{\prime},\lambda\rangle}e^{\pi i\langle J(\lambda)z,z^{\prime}\rangle}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma^{\prime},\omega\rangle}\left(\frac{|\omega|}{2\sinh 2\pi\tau|\omega|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+1-s}\times

\displaystyle\times e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi t|\lambda|}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}(|z^{\prime}|^{2}+y^{2})\frac{|\omega|}{\tanh 2\pi\tau|\omega|}}dg^{\prime}d\omega

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi t|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+1+s}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+1-s}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}}\times

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{\pi i\langle J(\lambda)z,z^{\prime}\rangle}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi t|\lambda|}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z^{\prime}|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}}dz^{\prime}.

We now notice that the last integral is the same appearing in proof of
[
21
, Lemma 4.2]
: in fact, exploiting the properties of the
J
map (
3.1
), and elementary manipulations of hyperbolic functions, in the final equality of formula (4.6) in
[
21
]
we showed that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{\pi i\langle J(\lambda)z,z^{\prime}\rangle}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi t|\lambda|}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z^{\prime}|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}}dz^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi(t+\tau)|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi(t+\tau)|\lambda|}}.

Inserting (
3.14
) in (
3.13
) we obtain the desired conclusion (
3.10
). This completes the proof of the lemma.

∎

The next lemma is purely technical and its significance will be clear in the subsequent discussion.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0<s<1
,
B,\mu>0
. We have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu}{(1+\rho)\sinh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\sinh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}{\sinh\frac{\mu}{1+\rho}}\right)^{s}e^{-\frac{B\mu}{\tanh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}}d\rho

\displaystyle=B^{s-1}\left(\frac{\mu}{\sinh\mu}\right)^{s}\Gamma(1-s)e^{-\frac{B\mu}{\tanh\mu}}.

Proof.

If we keep in mind the following identity

\frac{1}{\tanh\mu}-\frac{1}{\tanh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}=-\frac{1}{\sinh\mu}\frac{\sinh\frac{\mu}{1+\rho}}{\sinh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu},

we have

\displaystyle e^{\frac{B\mu}{\tanh\mu}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu}{(1+\rho)\sinh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\sinh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}{\sinh\frac{\mu}{1+\rho}}\right)^{s}e^{-\frac{B\mu}{\tanh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}}d\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu}{(1+\rho)\sinh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\sinh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}{\sinh\frac{\mu}{1+\rho}}\right)^{s}e^{-\frac{B\mu}{\sinh\mu}\frac{\sinh\frac{\mu}{1+\rho}}{\sinh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}}d\rho.

In the last integral we now make the change of variable
\rho\to\tau=\frac{B\mu}{\sinh\mu}\frac{\sinh\frac{\mu}{1+\rho}}{\sinh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}
, which yields
d\tau=-\frac{B\mu^{2}}{(1+\rho)^{2}\sinh^{2}\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}d\rho
. We thus have

\displaystyle e^{\frac{B\mu}{\tanh\mu}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu}{(1+\rho)\sinh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\sinh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}{\sinh\frac{\mu}{1+\rho}}\right)^{s}e^{-\frac{B\mu}{\tanh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}}d\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{B\mu}{\tau\sinh\mu}\right)^{s}e^{-\tau}d\tau=B^{s-1}\left(\frac{\mu}{\sinh\mu}\right)^{s}\Gamma(1-s).

This proves (
3.15
).

∎

We are now ready to present the proof of the main result in this note.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
and suitably decay at infinity, and
g\in\mathbb{G}
, one has

(3.16)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{s}u(g)=-\frac{s}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{-s-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(P_{(-s),t}u(g)\right)dtd\tau

\displaystyle=-\frac{s}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{\infty}\tau^{-s-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(P_{(-s),t}u(g)\right)d\tau dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(P_{(-s),t}u(g)\right)dt,

which is the conformal counterpart of (
2.6
). For
y>0
we want to apply (
3.16
) with the choice

u(\cdot)=\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}(\cdot,y).

By (
3.4
), (
3.6
) and (
1.17
), we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{s}(\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(P_{(-s),t}(\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)\right)dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}(4\pi t)^{1+s}q_{(-s)}((g^{\prime})^{-1}\circ g,t,0)q_{(s)}(g^{\prime},\tau,y)dg^{\prime}\right)d\tau dt.

In view of Lemma
3.1
we thus infer

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{s}(\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}t^{-s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left((4\pi t)^{1+s}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}\right)^{1-s}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi t|\lambda|}\right)^{1+s}\times\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi(t+\tau)|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi(t+\tau)|\lambda|}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}}\right)d\lambda d\tau dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{(4\pi)^{s-1}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\frac{e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}}{\tau}\left(\frac{\tau}{t}\right)^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\left(\frac{2\pi\tau|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}\right)^{1-s}\left(\frac{2\pi t|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi t|\lambda|}\right)^{1+s}\times\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi(t+\tau)|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi(t+\tau)|\lambda|}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\tau|\lambda|}}\right)d\lambda d\tau dt.

Hence, the same change of variable as in (
2.8
) leads to the following identity

(3.17)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{s}(\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{-(4\pi)^{s-1}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\frac{\rho^{s-1}}{1+\rho}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v}-\frac{\rho(1+\rho)}{v}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\right)\left(\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}\right)^{1-s}\times\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}\right)^{1+s}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi v|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi v|\lambda|}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}}\right)d\rho dvd\lambda.

We notice that, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
\rho>0
, the function

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}\right)^{1-s}\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}\right)^{1+s}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi v|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi v|\lambda|}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}}

converges to
0
both as
v\to 0^{+}
and
v\to\infty
(this holds for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
y>0
). Inserting this information in (
3.17
) we deduce

(3.18)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{s}(\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{(4\pi)^{s-1}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\frac{\rho^{s}}{v}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left(\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}\right)^{1-s}\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}\right)^{1+s}\times\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{2\sinh 2\pi v|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi v|\lambda|}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}}\right)d\rho dvd\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{(4\pi)^{s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\frac{1}{(4\pi v)^{\frac{m}{2}+1}}\left(\frac{2\pi v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi v|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi v|\lambda|}}\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left(\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}\right)^{1-s}\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}\right)^{1+s}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}}\right)d\rho\right)dvd\lambda.

To deal with the integral in the last equality in (
3.18
), for
0<s<1
and
\mu>0
we now consider the function
h_{s,\mu}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

(3.19)

h_{s,\mu}(\rho)=\frac{\mu}{\sinh\mu}\left(\frac{\sinh\frac{\rho\mu}{1+\rho}}{\sinh\frac{\mu}{1+\rho}}\right)^{s}\left[\frac{\mu}{\sinh\mu}\frac{\rho}{(1+\rho)^{2}}\left(\frac{\sinh\frac{\rho\mu}{1+\rho}}{\sinh\frac{\mu}{1+\rho}}+2\cosh\mu+\frac{\sinh\frac{\mu}{1+\rho}}{\sinh\frac{\rho\mu}{1+\rho}}\right)-1\right].

This function was introduced in
[
21
, Section 4]
, and the main motivation behind it is in the formula

(3.20)

h^{\prime}_{s,\mu}(\rho)=\rho^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left(\left(\frac{\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}{\sinh\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\mu}\right)^{1-s}\left(\frac{\frac{\mu}{1+\rho}}{\sinh\frac{\mu}{1+\rho}}\right)^{1+s}\right),

see
[
21
, equation (4.19)]
. This property was critical in showing that the operator
\mathscr{I}_{(2s)}
in (
3.7
) inverts
\mathscr{L}_{s}
. If in (
3.19
) we now take

\mu=2\pi v|\lambda|
, we obtain from (
3.18
)

(3.21)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{s}(\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)=\frac{(4\pi)^{s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\frac{1}{(4\pi v)^{\frac{m}{2}+1}}\left(\frac{2\pi v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi v|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}\times

\displaystyle\times e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi v|\lambda|}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(h^{\prime}_{s,2\pi v|\lambda|}(\rho)e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}}+\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\rho^{s}\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}\right)^{1-s}\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}\right)^{1+s}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left(e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}}\right)\right)d\rho dvd\lambda,

where in the last identity we have used (
3.20
). We also observe that from (
3.19
) we have
h_{s,\mu}(\rho)\to 0
as
\rho\to 0
and
\rho\to\infty
(see
[
21
, (4.18)]
). From (
3.21
) we thus find

(3.22)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{s}(\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{(4\pi)^{s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\frac{1}{(4\pi v)^{\frac{m}{2}+1}}\left(\frac{2\pi v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi v|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi v|\lambda|}}\times

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left(e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}}\right)\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\rho^{s}\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}\right)^{1-s}\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}\right)^{1+s}-h_{s,2\pi v|\lambda|}(\rho)\right)d\rho dvd\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{4^{s}\pi^{s+1}y^{2}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\frac{1}{(4\pi v)^{\frac{m}{2}+2}}\left(\frac{2\pi v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi v|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi v|\lambda|}}\times

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}}\left(\frac{2\pi v|\lambda|}{(1+\rho)\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}\right)^{2}\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\rho^{s}\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}\right)^{1-s}\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}\right)^{1+s}-h_{s,2\pi v|\lambda|}(\rho)\right)d\rho dvd\lambda.

From the identity

\displaystyle\sinh^{2}2\pi v|\lambda|=

\displaystyle\sinh^{2}2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|+\sinh^{2}2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|+

\displaystyle+2(\cosh 2\pi v|\lambda|)(\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|)(\sinh 2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|)

and the definition (
3.19
), a straightforward computation shows that

\rho^{s}\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}\right)^{1-s}\left(\frac{2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}\right)^{1+s}-h_{s,2\pi v|\lambda|}(\rho)=\frac{2\pi v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi v|\lambda|}\left(\frac{\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}\right)^{s}.

From (
3.22
) we thus infer

(3.23)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{s}(\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{4^{s}\pi^{s+1}y^{2}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\frac{1}{(4\pi v)^{\frac{m}{2}+2}}\left(\frac{2\pi v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi v|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+1}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi v|\lambda|}}\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2\pi v|\lambda|}{(1+\rho)\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}\right)^{s}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}}d\rho\right)d\lambda dv.

For any
\lambda\neq 0
and
v>0
we now apply Lemma
3.2
with the choices
\mu=2\pi v|\lambda|
and
B=\frac{y^{2}}{4v}
, and find

(3.24)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2\pi v|\lambda|}{(1+\rho)\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\sinh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi\frac{v}{1+\rho}|\lambda|}\right)^{s}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}v|\lambda|}}d\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{y^{2s-2}}{(4v)^{s-1}}\left(\frac{2\pi v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi v|\lambda|}\right)^{s}\Gamma(1-s)e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}y^{2}\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi v|\lambda|}}.

Inserting (
3.24
) in (
3.23
), we finally obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{s}(\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}(\cdot,y))(g)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{2s}y^{2s}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma,\lambda\rangle}\frac{1}{(4\pi v)^{\frac{m}{2}+1+s}}\left(\frac{2\pi v|\lambda|}{\sinh 2\pi v|\lambda|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}+1+s}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}(|z|^{2}+y^{2})\frac{|\lambda|}{\tanh 2\pi v|\lambda|}}d\lambda dv

\displaystyle=(2\pi y)^{2s}\int_{0}^{\infty}q_{(-s)}((z,\sigma),v,y)dv=(2\pi y)^{2s}\mathfrak{e}_{(-s)}((z,\sigma),y),

which gives the desired conclusion (
1.18
).

∎

Although it should at this point be self-evident, for the sake of completeness we show how (
1.13
) can be derived from Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
A
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be of Heisenberg type, and let
0<s<1
. For every
(z,\sigma)\in\mathbb{G}
, and
y>0
one has

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{s}\left(((|z|^{2}+y^{2})^{2}+16|\sigma|^{2})^{-\frac{m+2k-2s}{4}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2+2s}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2k+2s}{4}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2-2s}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2k-2s}{4}\right)}(4y)^{2s}((|z|^{2}+y^{2})^{2}+16|\sigma|^{2})^{-\frac{m+2k+2s}{4}}.

Proof.

Combining (
1.19
) and (
1.18
), we obtain

(3.25)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{s}\left(((|z|^{2}+y^{2})^{2}+16|\sigma|^{2})^{-\frac{m+2k-2s}{4}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(4\pi)^{1-s}}{\Gamma(s)C_{(s)}(m,k)}\mathscr{L}_{s}\left(\mathfrak{e}_{(s)}((z,\sigma),y)\right)=\frac{(4\pi)^{1-s}(2\pi y)^{2s}}{\Gamma(s)C_{(s)}(m,k)}\mathfrak{e}_{(-s)}((z,\sigma),y)

\displaystyle=\frac{y^{2s}}{4^{s}}\frac{|\Gamma(-s)|C_{(-s)}(m,k)}{\Gamma(s)C_{(s)}(m,k)}((|z|^{2}+y^{2})^{2}+16|\sigma|^{2})^{-\frac{m+2k+2s}{4}}.

On the other hand, by (
1.20
) we have

(3.26)

\frac{|\Gamma(-s)|C_{(-s)}(m,k)}{\Gamma(s)C_{(s)}(m,k)}=4^{3s}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2+2s}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2k+2s}{4}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2-2s}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2k-2s}{4}\right)}.

Substituting (
3.26
) in (
3.25
) we reach the sought for conclusion.

∎

In closing, we observe that it is clear from Corollary
3.3
that, for any
y>0
, the function

(3.27)

u_{y}(z,\sigma)=\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2+2s}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2k+2s}{4}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2-2s}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{m+2k-2s}{4}\right)}\right)^{\frac{m+2k-2s}{4s}}\left(\frac{16y^{2}}{(|z|^{2}+y^{2})^{2}+16|\sigma|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{m+2k-2s}{4}}

satisfies
\mathscr{L}_{s}u_{y}=u_{y}^{\frac{m+2k+2s}{m+2k-2s}}
. It is worth emphasising here that Corollary
3.3
is stable under the convergence of
s\nearrow 1
. In particular, we recover from (
3.27
) the functions that, in the local case
s=1
, were found in
[
26
]
in the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, see also
[
17
, Theor. 1.1]
and
[
18
]
for partial results in groups of Heisenberg type.

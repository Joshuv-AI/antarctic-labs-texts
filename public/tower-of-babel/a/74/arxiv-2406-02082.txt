On the Computation of 2-Dimensional Recurrence Equations
Giuseppe Natale

Abstract




Introduction

A generic 2-dimensional recurrence problem can be defined as:

\displaystyle w_{i,j}

\displaystyle=a_{i,j}w_{i-1,j}+b_{i,j}w_{i,j-1}+c_{i,j}

\displaystyle\forall(i,j)

\displaystyle\in\{1,..,n\}\times\{1,..,n\}

(1)

\displaystyle w_{0,j}

\displaystyle=c_{0,j}

\displaystyle\forall j

\displaystyle\in\{1,..,n\}

\displaystyle w_{i,0}

\displaystyle=c_{i,0}

\displaystyle\forall i

\displaystyle\in\{1,..,n\}

It is quite easy to notice that the computational pattern induced by the dependence relations is a wavefront pattern. A visualization of such pattern is also shown in Figure
1
. Indeed, wavefront parallelization is what is nowadays used to efficiently compute such recurrence problems, as it is able to reduce the computation time from being proportional to
n^{2}
to being proportional to
2n-1
.

Figure 1:
Wavefront computation pattern induced by the dependence relation in (
1
).

I here show how it is possible to systematically reduce a 2-dimensional recurrence problem of the above form into a mono-dimensional recurrence problem of the form:

\displaystyle x_{d}

\displaystyle=h_{d}x_{d-1}+k_{d}

\displaystyle\forall d

\displaystyle\in\{2,..,2n-1\}

(2)

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle=k_{1}

with
x_{d}
and
k_{d}
column vectors of length
n
, and
h_{d}
a matrix of dimension
n\times n
.
This will prove that Kogge and Stone algorithm proposed in
[
1
]
is applicable even to 2-dimensional recurrence relations (and probably also to 3+ dimensions following a similar approach).

Discussion

As previously said, the wavefront computational pattern enforced by the data dependencies can be exploited to perform parallelization and make the computation time proportional to
2n-1
for an
n\times n
grid. This is achieved by computing the grid points along the wavefront in parallel, in a way “clustering” the computation along the diagonal. I use the same “clustering” intuition to perform the proposed reduction, going from a 2-dimensional system
(i,j)\in\{1,..,n\}\times\{1,..,n\}
to a mono-dimensional system
d\in\{1,..,2n-1\}
, where each
d
corresponds to a diagonal – i.e. a set of grid points of the same color as in Figure
1
– of the original 2-dimensional grid.

A 2-dimensional recurrence problem of the form of (
1
) for an arbitrary
n
can always be reduced to a mono-dimensional equivalent of the form of (
2
), where
x_{d}
and
k_{d}
are column vectors of length
n
, and
h_{d}
is a matrix of dimension
n\times n
. If the reduction provides less non-zero elements than
n
for
x_{d}
and
k_{d}
(and less non-zero rows than
n
for
h_{d}
), then a number of 0s (or rows of 0s) is inserted after the last non-zero element until the vectors/matrices reach the required cardinality. This is just for consistency purposes and could be avoided in practical implementations.

Proof.

The reduction is performed by relating the different
(i,j),\,\,\forall(i,j)\in\{1,..,n\}\times\{1,..,n\}
of the 2-dimensional space of (
1
) to the different
d,\,\,\forall d\in\{1,..,2n-1\}
of the mono-dimensional space of (
2
). In particular, each
(i,j)
belonging to a diagonal – i.e. a set of grid points of the same color as in Figure
1
– is grouped within the same
d
.

Indeed, we can define the different
x_{d},\,\,\forall d\in\{1,..,2n-1\}
from the
w_{i,j}\,\,\forall(i,j)\in\{1,..,n\}\times\{1,..,n\}
:

\displaystyle x_{d}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\begin{bmatrix}w_{1,d}\\
w_{2,d-1}\\
\vdots\\
w_{1+i,d-i}\\
\vdots\\
w_{d-1,2}\\
w_{d,1}\end{bmatrix}&\text{if }1\leq d\leq n,\quad\forall i\in\{0,..,d-1\}\\
\\
\begin{bmatrix}w_{d+1-n,n}\\
w_{d+2-n,n-1}\\
\vdots\\
w_{(d+1-n)+i,n-i}\\
\vdots\\
w_{n-1,d+2-n}\\
w_{n,d+1-n}\end{bmatrix}&\text{if }n+1\leq d\leq 2n-1,\quad\forall i\in\{0,..,2n-d-1\}\\
\end{cases}

(3)

In this way, each
x_{d}
represent a diagonal (a collection of grid points) of the original 2-dimensional space. The
x_{d}
column vector has a fixed cardinality of
n
, because of how I defined (
2
) (a number of 0s will be inserted after the last non-zero element given by the relation defined in (
3
) until the vector
x_{d}
doesn’t reach a cardinality of
n
). That said, the number of non-zero values inside each
x_{d}
column-vector is:

\displaystyle|x_{d}|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}d&\text{if }1\leq d\leq n\\
2n-d&\text{if }n+1\leq d\leq 2n-1\\
\end{cases}

Now that the relation between the unknowns of (
1
) and its mono-dimensional equivalent (
2
) is defined, we can define the
h_{d}
and
k_{d},\,\,\forall d\in\{1,..,2n-1\}
of (
2
) from the
a_{i,j},b_{i,j},c_{i,j}
and the
c_{0,j},c_{i,0},\,\,\forall(i,j)\in\{1,..,n\}\times\{1,..,n\}
of (
1
).

\displaystyle k_{d}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\begin{bmatrix}c_{1,d}+a_{1,d}c_{0,d}\\
c_{2,d-1}\\
\vdots\\
c_{1+i,d-i}\\
\vdots\\
c_{d-1,2}\\
c_{d,1}+b_{d,1}c_{d,0}\end{bmatrix}&\text{if }1\leq d\leq n,\quad\forall i\in\{0,..,d-1\}\\
\\
\begin{bmatrix}c_{d+1-n,n}\\
c_{d+2-n,n-1}\\
\vdots\\
c_{(d+1-n)+i,n-i}\\
\vdots\\
c_{n-1,d+2-n}\\
c_{n,d+1-n}\end{bmatrix}&\text{if }n+1\leq d\leq 2n-1,\quad\forall i\in\{0,..,2n-d-1\}\\
\end{cases}

(4)

From the definition of
k_{d}
in (
4
), it is evident that
x_{1}=[w_{1,1},0,..,0]^{T}
is implicitly defined as
x_{1}=k_{1}
since
w_{1,1}=a_{1,1}c_{0,1}+b_{1,1}c_{1,0}+c_{1,1}
. This is indeed expected, as also defined in (
2
), since
x_{1}
is the known initial value of the recurrence relation. We can therefore define
h_{d}
only
\forall d\in\{2,..,2n-1\}
, and we can leave
h_{1}
as undefined (or
=0
for simplicity), since it is not needed.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}h_{1}=0\end{cases}

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{if}\,\,2\leq d\leq n,\quad\forall i\in\{0,..,d-1\}\\
h_{d}=\begin{bmatrix}b_{1,d}&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
a_{2,d-1}&b_{2,d-1}&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&a_{3,d-2}&b_{3,d-2}&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&&&\vdots\\
0&\dots&a_{1+i,d-i}&b_{1+i,d-i}&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&&&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&a_{d-1,2}&b_{d-1,2}&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&a_{d,1}&0\\
\end{bmatrix}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{if}\,\,n+1\leq d\leq 2n-1,\quad\forall i\in\{0,..,2n-d-1\}\\
h_{d}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{d+1-n,n}&b_{d+1-n,n}&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&a_{d+2-n,n-1}&b_{d+2-n,n-1}&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&&&\vdots\\
0&\dots&a_{(d+1-n)+i,n-i}&b_{(d+1-n)+i,n-i}&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&&&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&a_{n-1,d+2-n}&b_{n-1,d+2-n}&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&a_{n,d+1-n}&b_{n,d+1-n}\\
\end{bmatrix}\end{cases}

∎

The ability to always reduce a 2-dimensional recurrence problem of the form of (
1
) to a mono-dimensional equivalent of the form of (
2
), as per Theorem 1, imply that Kogge and Stone algorithm presented in
[
1
]
is indeed applicable. Moreover, each row of a generic
x_{d}=h_{d}x_{d-1}+k_{d}\,\,\forall d\in\{2,..,2n-1\}
is independent from the others and can indeed be computed in parallel. This proves that – at least theoretically – by applying Kogge and Stone algorithm and parallelizing the computation of each row of the different
x_{d}\,\,\forall d\in\{1,..,2n-1\}
, the recurrence relations can be computed in time proportional to
\lceil log_{2}(2n-1)\rceil
.

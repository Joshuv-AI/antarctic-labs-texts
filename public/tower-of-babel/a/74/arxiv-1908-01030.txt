1.
Introduction and main results

This paper is motivated by the study of boundary value problems for elliptic operators having a BMO anti-symmetric part. These operators arise in the study of equations with divergence-free drift, e.g.
-\Delta u+\mathbf{c}\cdot\nabla u=0
and
\partial_{t}u+\mathbf{c}\cdot\nabla u-\Delta u=0
, where
c
is a divergence-free vector field in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Seregin, Silvestre, Šverák, and Zlatoš (
[
23
]
) discovered that the condition
\divg\mathbf{c}=0
can be used to relax the regularity assumptions on
\mathbf{c}
under which one can prove the Harnack inequality and other regularity results for solutions. It turns out that
\mathbf{c}\in BMO^{-1}
in the elliptic case, and
\mathbf{c}\in L^{\infty}(BMO^{-1})
in the parabolic case are the right conditions, in the sense that the interior regularity theory of De Giorgi, Nash, and Moser carries over to these operators. Generalizing to elliptic or parabolic equations in divergence form, this condition is equivalent to assuming that the matrix
A
can be decomposed into an
L^{\infty}
elliptic symmetric part and an unbounded anti-symmetric part in a certain function space. In the elliptic case, the anti-symmetric part should belong to the John-Nirenberg space BMO (bounded mean oscillation) and, in the parabolic case, to
L^{\infty}(BMO)
. The space BMO plays an important role in two ways. First, this space has the appropriate scaling properties which appear naturally in the iterative arguments of De Giorgi-Nash-Moser. Secondly, the BMO condition on the anti-symmetric part of the matrix allows one to define suitable weak solutions. This latter fact is essentially due to an application of the div-curl lemma appearing in the theory of compensated compactness, and the details can be found in
[
23
]
or
[
17
]
.

These operators have gained much attention since
[
23
]
. In
[
22
]
, the authors showed the existence of the fundamental solution of the parabolic operator
L-\partial_{t}
, and derived Gaussian estimate for the fundamental solution. Later, H. Dong and S. Kim
[
4
]
have generalized the result for fundamental solutions to second-order parabolic systems, under the assumption that weak solutions of the system satisfy a certain local boundedness estimate. The investigation into boundary value problems for elliptic operators having a BMO anti-symmetric part was launched by the work
[
17
]
. There, the second and the fourth authors of this paper studied the boundary behavior of weak solutions as well as the Dirichlet problem for elliptic operators in divergence form with BMO anti-symmetric part.

In another direction, Escauriaza and the first author of this paper proved the Kato conjecture for elliptic operators having a BMO anti-symmetric part in
[
5
]
. To be precise, they showed that the domain of the square root
\sqrt{L}
contains
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and that

\left\|\sqrt{L}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(1.1)

holds over
\dot{W}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Their proof does not rely on the Gaussian estimates obtained in
[
22
]
.
The Kato conjecture dates back to the 60’s, when T. Kato conjectured (
[
14
]
,
[
13
]
) that an abstract version of (
1.1
) might hold, for “regularly accretive operators”. The conjecture was disproved by McIntosh (
[
18
]
), who then reformulated the conjecture for divergence form elliptic operators with complex,
L^{\infty}
,
n\times n
matrix. The validity of the conjecture was established when the heat kernel of the operator
L
satisfies the “Gaussian property”, first in 2 dimensions (
[
11
]
) and then in all dimensions
[
9
]
.
We say
L
satisfies the Gaussian property if the kernel
K_{t}(x,y)
of the operator
e^{-tL}
satisfies the following:
for all
t>0
, for some constants
0<\beta,\mu\leq 1
and
C
,

\displaystyle\left|K_{t}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{\beta\left|x-y\right|^{2}}{t}},

\displaystyle\left|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(x+h,y)\right|

\displaystyle+\left|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(x,y+h)\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{\left|h\right|}{t^{1/2}+\left|x-y\right|}\right)^{\mu}e^{{-\frac{\beta\left|x-y\right|^{2}}{t}}}

when
2\left|h\right|\leq t^{1/2}+\left|x-y\right|
.
The conjecture was solved for elliptic operators in divergence form with complex, bounded coefficients in
[
2
]
, and, as we mentioned above, for operators considered in the present paper in
[
5
]
.

The
L^{2}
boundeness (
1.1
) naturally leads to the question about
L^{p}
boundeness,
p\neq 2
. Namely, if
L
is such that the domain of
L^{1/2}
agrees with
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, how do
\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{p}}
and
\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}}
compare? It turns out that the ranges of
p
for
\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}}
and
\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{p}}
can be different. In
[
3
]
, it is shown that for divergence form differential operators
L=-\divg(A\nabla)
, where
A
is a matrix with complex-valued bounded entries and satisfying a uniform ellipticity condition, if
L
has the Gaussian property, and that (
1.1
) and its corresponding inequality for
L^{*}
hold, then

\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}}\qquad\forall\,1<p<\infty,

(1.2)

\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq c_{p}^{\prime}\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{p}}\qquad\forall\,1<p<2+\varepsilon,

(1.3)

for some
\varepsilon>0
depends only on
L
. The proof relies on a non-standard factorization of
L^{1/2}
which makes Calderón-Zygmund theory fully available. We remark that although the Gaussian property is available for elliptic operators with a BMO anti-symmetric part (
[
22
]
), the results in
[
3
]
do not apply to this setting, mainly because the decomposition used in
[
3
]
requires the coefficients being bounded. In
[
1
]
, Auscher presents the
L^{p}
boundedness results without a direct appeal to kernels of the operators. The main observation in
[
1
]
is that the limits of the interval of exponents
p\in[1,\infty]
for which the semigroup is
L^{p}
bounded, and the limits of the interval of exponents
p\in[1,\infty]
for which
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
is
L^{p}
bounded, fully describe the
L^{p}
behavior of the square root operator, as well as some Littlewood-Paley-Stein type functionals (we simply call them square functions in this paper). We record here that the two vertical square functions studied in
[
1
]
are

g_{L}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|(L^{1/2}e^{-tL}f)(x)\right|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}

and

G_{L}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|(\nabla e^{-tL}f)(x)\right|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}.

For
L
being an elliptic operator having a BMO anti-symmetric part, the
L^{p}
boundedness for
L^{1/2}
and square functions was unknown. While this question is interesting by itself, we are motivated also by the study of boundary value problems for these operators. Indeed, for divergence form operators with matrix in the “block form”, that is,
L=\divg_{x,t}(A(x)\nabla_{x,t})
with
A=\begin{bmatrix}\vbox{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\vbox{\vbox{}\hrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\hbox{}}}\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vbox{}\hss}\vbox{}}}\end{bmatrix}
, inequalities (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) can be thought of as a “Rellich identity”
\left\|\partial_{t}u\right\|_{L^{p}}\approx\left\|\nabla_{x}u\right\|_{L^{p}}
. The latter plays an important role in the solvability of Neumann and regularity problem with
L^{p}
data. See e.g.
[
12
]
,
[
24
]
and
[
16
]
. Even more generally, for operators having a full
(n+1)\times(n+1)
elliptic coefficients matrix
A(x)
, tools related to the Kato problem have been successfully used to tackle boundary value problems. In
[
8
]
, for instance, the
L^{p}
estimates for some square functions similar to
g_{L}
and
G_{L}
form parts of the proof of the
L^{p}
solvability for elliptic operators with real,
L^{\infty}
, t-independent coefficients in the upper-half space for
p
sufficiently large. However, details regarding these
L^{p}
estimates are missing, and one did not know whether these estimates are valid for elliptic operators having a BMO anti-symmetric part.

In this paper, we create a complete narrative of the
L^{p}
theory for the square root operator (Section
4
), and derive the
L^{p}
estimates for the vertical square functions (Section
5
). Let
L=-\divg(A\nabla)
be an operator with real coefficients defined in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Assume that the symmetric part of the
n\times n
matrix
A
is elliptic and
L^{\infty}
, and the anti-symmetric part is in
BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Our main results are the following:

(1)

\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}}
for
1<p<\infty
, and
\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{p}}
for
1<p<2+\epsilon_{1}
. (Theorem
4.77
)

(2)

we have the
L^{p}
square functions estimates

\left\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|tLe^{-t^{2}L}F\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{p}\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\forall\,1<p<\infty,

(Proposition
5.7
)

\left\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|t^{2}\nabla Le^{-t^{2}L}F\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{p}\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\forall\,1<p<2+\epsilon_{1},

(Proposition
5.12
)

\left\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|t^{2}\partial_{t}Le^{-t^{2}L}F\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{p}\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\forall\,1<p<\infty.

(Proposition
5.26
)

In these results,
\epsilon_{1}>0
depends only on the ellipticity constant and the BMO semi-norm of the coefficients of the operator, and on dimension (Proposition
4.38
).

To deal with the BMO coefficients, we need estimates on the Hardy norm of some functions of particular form. These are presented in Section
2
. We give a precise definition of the operator
L
in Section
3
, starting from a sesquilinear form.
The
L^{p}
estimates for the square root and square functions rely on the off-diagonal estimates for the semigroup and
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
, which are derived in Section

. Another key ingredient in proving the

estimates for the square root is the representation formula for the Riesz transform, which we carefully justify in Proposition
4.62
. To prove the
L^{p}
estimates for the square functions (Proposition
5.7
–
5.26
), we exploit the
L^{p}
estimates for the square root operator and borrow some ideas from
[
1
]
.

While the paper can be viewed independently as a part of an extensive theory of functional calculus of elliptic operators and associated Hardy spaces, for us it was mainly motivated by the demands coming from the theory of boundary value problems. In
[
10
]
, to continue the work
[
17
]
, we study of

Dirichlet problem for elliptic operators having a BMO anti-symmetric part. There, we are able to prove the Dirichlet problem with

boundary data in the upper half-space
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
,
n\geq 2
, is uniquely solvable for
p
sufficiently large, for these operators under some natural structural assumptions on the matrix, namely,
t
-independent. In
[
10
]
, we use the Gaussian estimate for the

estimates for some non-tangential maximal functions. The

2.
Hardy Norms

Definition 2.1
.

We say
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is in the real Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left\|\sup_{t>0}\left|h_{t}*f\right|\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty,

where
h_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{t^{n}}h\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)
, and
h
is any smooth non-negative function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
\supp h\subset B_{1}(0)
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)dx=1
.

The following estimates shall be used frequently in the rest of the paper.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Let
u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
v\in\dot{W}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then for any
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
\partial_{j}u\partial_{i}v-\partial_{i}u\partial_{j}v\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\left\|\partial_{j}u\partial_{i}v-\partial_{i}u\partial_{j}v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}},

(2.3)

where the implicit constant depends only on
p
and dimension.

We refer to
[
17
]
and
[
23
]
for its proof.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Let
u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
v\in\dot{W}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then for any
1\leq i\leq n
,
\partial_{i}(uv)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\left\|\partial_{i}(uv)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}},

(2.5)

where the implicit constant depends only on
p
and dimension.

Proof.

Let
h
be a smooth nonnegative compactly supported mollifier with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)dx=1
,
\supp h\subset B_{1}(0)
. And let
h_{t}(x)=t^{-n}h(\frac{x}{t})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\quad h_{t}*\partial_{i}(uv)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{t}(x-y)\partial_{i}(uv)(y)dy=-\int_{B_{t}(x)}\partial_{i}h_{t}(x-y)u(y)v(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{t}(x)}\frac{1}{t^{n+1}}\partial_{i}h\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)u(y)\left(v(y)-(v)_{B_{t}(x)}\right)dy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{t}(x)}\frac{1}{t^{n+1}}\partial_{i}h\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)u(y)(v)_{B_{t}(x)}dy=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n}}\int_{B_{t}(x)}\left|u(y)\right|\left|\frac{v(y)-(v)_{B_{t}(x)}}{t}\right|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{B_{t}(x)}\left|u\right|^{\alpha}dy\right)^{1/{\alpha}}\left(\fint_{B_{t}(x)}\left|\frac{v(y)-(v)_{B_{t}(x)}}{t}\right|^{\alpha^{\prime}}dy\right)^{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{B_{t}(x)}\left|u\right|^{\alpha}dy\right)^{1/{\alpha}}\left(\fint_{B_{t}(x)}\left|\nabla v\right|^{\beta}dy\right)^{1/{\beta}}\lesssim\left(M\left|u\right|^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x)\left(M\left|\nabla v\right|^{\beta}\right)^{1/{\beta}}(x),

where
\alpha\in[1,p)
,
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=1+\frac{1}{n}
, and
M(f)
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function of
f
. For
I_{2}
, note that
I_{2}=\int_{B_{t}(x)}h_{t}(x-y)\partial_{i}u(y)(v)_{B_{t}(x)}dy
. So

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n}}\int_{B_{t}(x)}\left|\partial_{i}u\right|\left|(v)_{B_{t}(x)}\right|dy\lesssim\fint_{B_{t}(x)}\left|\nabla u\right|dy\fint_{B_{t}(x)}\left|v\right|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim M(\left|\nabla u\right|)(x)M(v)(x).

Combining the estimates for
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, and using Hölder inequality, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{t>0}\left|h_{t}*\partial_{i}(uv)(x)\right|dx\\
\lesssim\left\|\left(M\left|u\right|^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|\left(M\left|\nabla v\right|^{\beta}\right)^{1/{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\left\|M(\left|\nabla u\right|)\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|M(v)\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\\
\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}},

where in the last inequality we have used that
1\leq\alpha<p
and
1<\beta<p^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
u
,
v\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
\varphi
be a Lipschitz function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for any
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
\partial_{j}(uv)\partial_{i}\varphi-\partial_{i}(uv)\partial_{j}\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\left\|\partial_{j}(uv)\partial_{i}\varphi-\partial_{i}(uv)\partial_{j}\varphi\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|u\left|\nabla\varphi\right|\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\left|\nabla u\right|\left|\nabla\varphi\right|\right\|_{L^{2}},

or

\left\|\partial_{j}(uv)\partial_{i}\varphi-\partial_{i}(uv)\partial_{j}\varphi\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}\Big),

(2.7)

where the implicit constant depends only on dimension.

Proof.

We can assume
\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Set
\vec{\Phi}=(0,\dots,0,\partial_{j}\varphi,0,\dots,0,-\partial_{i}\varphi,0,\dots,0)
. Then
\divg\vec{\Phi}=0
and

\partial_{i}(uv)\partial_{j}\varphi-\partial_{j}(uv)\partial_{i}\varphi=\vec{\Phi}\cdot\nabla(uv)=\divg(\vec{\Phi}uv).

(2.8)

Let
h
be a smooth nonnegative compactly supported mollifier with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)dx=1
,
\supp h\subset B_{1}(0)
. And let
h_{t}(x)=t^{-n}h(\frac{x}{t})
. We compute

\displaystyle h_{t}*\divg(\vec{\Phi}uv)(x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{t}(x)}\nabla_{y}h_{t}(x-y)\cdot\vec{\Phi}(y)u(y)v(y)dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{t}(x)}\nabla_{y}h_{t}(x-y)\cdot\vec{\Phi}(y)u(y)(v(y)-(v)_{B_{t}(x)})dy

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{B_{t}(x)}\divg_{y}(h_{t}(x-y)\vec{\Phi}(y))u(y)(v)_{B_{t}(x)}dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{t}(x)}\nabla_{y}h_{t}(x-y)\cdot\vec{\Phi}(y)u(y)(v(y)-(v)_{B_{t}(x)})dy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{t}(x)}h_{t}(x-y)\vec{\Phi}\cdot\nabla u(y)(v)_{B_{t}(x)}dy

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n+1}}\int_{B_{t}(x)}\left|\nabla\varphi\right|\left|u\right|\left|v-(v)_{B_{t}(x)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\fint_{B_{t}(x)}\left|u\nabla\varphi\right|^{\alpha}\Big)^{1/{\alpha}}\Big(\fint_{B_{t}(x)}(\frac{\left|v-(v)_{B_{t}(x)}\right|}{t})^{\alpha^{\prime}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\fint_{B_{t}(x)}\left|u\nabla\varphi\right|^{\alpha}\Big)^{1/{\alpha}}\Big(\fint_{B_{t}(x)}\left|\nabla v\right|^{\beta}\Big)^{1/{\beta}}\lesssim M^{1/{\alpha}}(\left|u\nabla\varphi\right|^{\alpha})(x)M^{1/{\beta}}(\left|\nabla v\right|^{\beta})(x),

where
1<\alpha,\beta<2
,
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}=1
,
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=1+\frac{1}{n}
. And

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}\right|\lesssim\Big(\fint_{B_{t}(x)}\left|\nabla\varphi\right|\left|\nabla u\right|\Big)\left|(v)_{B_{t}(x)}\right|\lesssim M(\left|\nabla\varphi\right|\left|\nabla u\right|)(x)M(v)(x).

So

\left|h_{t}*\divg(\vec{\Phi}uv)(x)\right|\lesssim M^{1/{\alpha}}(\left|u\nabla\varphi\right|^{\alpha})(x)M^{1/{\beta}}(\left|\nabla v\right|^{\beta})(x)+M(\left|\nabla\varphi\right|\left|\nabla u\right|)(x)M(v)(x),

and thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{t>0}\left|h_{t}*\divg(\vec{\Phi}uv)(x)\right|dx\lesssim\left\|u\left|\nabla\varphi\right|\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\left|\nabla u\right|\left|\nabla\varphi\right|\right\|_{L^{2}}.

By (
2.8
) and the definition of Hardy norm, we complete the proof.
∎

3.
Sectorial operators and resolvent estimates

We give a precise definition for the operator
L
.

Let
\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the space of the bounded semilinear functionals on
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We say that
f\in\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}
is semilinear if

\langle f,\alpha u+\beta v\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}=\bar{\alpha}\langle f,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}+\bar{\beta}\langle f,v\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}},

whenever
\alpha
,
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and
u
,
v\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Define
\mathscr{L}:W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as follows

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}u,v\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla\bar{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{s}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\bar{v}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{a}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\bar{v},

where
A=(a_{ij}(x))
is
n\times n
, real,
A^{s}=\frac{1}{2}(A+A^{\intercal})=(a^{s}_{ij}(x))
is of coefficients in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and elliptic, i.e. there exists
0<\lambda_{0}\leq 1
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\lambda_{0}\left|\xi\right|^{2}\leq a^{s}_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\left\|A^{s}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{0}^{-1},

and the coefficients of
A^{a}=\frac{1}{2}(A-A^{\intercal})=(a^{a}_{ij}(x))
are in BMO
(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with

\left\|a^{a}_{ij}\right\|_{BMO}:=\sup_{Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\fint_{Q}\left|a^{a}_{ij}-(a^{a}_{ij})_{Q}\right|dx\leq\Lambda_{0}

for some
\Lambda_{0}>0
, where
Q
is any cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then by Proposition
2.2
,

\left|\langle\mathscr{L}u,v\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\right|\leq C\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}},

with
C
depending on
\lambda_{0}
,
\Lambda_{0}
and dimension.

Now define a sesquilinear form on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
: for any
u
,
v\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, let

t[u,v]=\langle\mathscr{L}u,v\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}.

The numerical range
\Theta(t)
of
t
is defined as

\Theta(t):=\{t[u,u]:u\in D(t)\text{ with }\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}=1\}.

Proposition 3.1
.

t
is a densely defined, closed, sectorial sesqulinear form in
L^{2}
, and there exists
0<\theta_{0}<\frac{\pi}{2}
such that for any
\xi\in\Theta(t)
,
\left|\arg\xi\right|\leq\theta_{0}
.

Proof.

The domain
D(t)
of
t
is
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is dense in
L^{2}
. So
t
is densely defined. To see that it is closed, let
u_{n}\in D(t)
,
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{2}
and
t[u_{n}-u_{m},u_{n}-u_{m}]\to 0
. We want to show that
u\in D(t)
and
t[u_{n}-u,u_{n}-u]\to 0
. Since
t[u_{n}-u_{m},u_{n}-u_{m}]\to 0
,

\lambda_{0}\left|\nabla(u_{n}-u_{m})\right|\leq\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla(u_{n}-u_{m})\cdot\nabla\overline{(u_{n}-u_{m})}\to 0.

So
\{u_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which implies that
u\in W^{1,2}=D(t)
and

\left|t[u_{n}-u,u_{n}-u]\right|\leq\Lambda_{0}\left\|\left|\nabla(u_{n}-u)\right|\right\|_{L^{2}}\to 0.

Now we show that
t
is sectorial, i.e., its numerical range
\Theta(t)
is a subset of a sector of the form

\left|\arg(\xi-\gamma)\right|\leq\theta,\quad\text{for some }0\leq\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}\text{ and }\gamma\in\mathbb{R}.

For
u\in D(t)
with
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}=1
, write
u=u_{1}+iu_{2}
. Then

\Re\langle\mathscr{L}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a^{s}_{ij}(\partial_{j}u_{1}\partial_{i}u_{1}+\partial_{j}u_{2}\partial_{i}u_{2})\geq\lambda_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2},

\Im\langle\mathscr{L}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a^{s}_{ij}(\partial_{j}u_{2}\partial_{i}u_{1}-\partial_{j}u_{1}\partial_{i}u_{2})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a^{a}_{ij}(\partial_{j}u_{2}\partial_{i}u_{1}-\partial_{j}u_{1}\partial_{i}u_{2}).

By Proposition
2.2
,
\left|\Im\langle\mathscr{L}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\right|\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}
, with
C
depending on
\lambda_{0}
,
\Lambda_{0}
and
n
. This implies that

\frac{\left|\Im\langle\mathscr{L}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\right|}{\Re\langle\mathscr{L}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}}\leq C,\text{ with }C=C(\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},n).

Therefore, there exists
0<\theta_{0}=\theta_{0}(\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},n)<\frac{\pi}{2}
such that for any
\xi\in\Theta(t)
,
\left|\xi\right|\leq\theta_{0}
.
∎

Then by
[
15
]
Chapter VI Theorem 2.1 and its proof, we obtain

Lemma 3.2
.

There is a unique m-accretive, sectorial operator
L:D(L)\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(1)

D(L)\subset D(t)=W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
D(L)
is dense in
D(t)
with respect to the
W^{1,2}
norm.

(2)

(Lu,v)=t[u,v]
for all
u\in D(L)
,
v\in D(t)
. Here
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the inner product on complex
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(3)

If
u\in D(t)
,
w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
t[u,v]=(w,v)
for any
v\in D(L)
, then
u\in D(L)
and
Lu=w
.

By m-accretive we mean that
(L+\lambda I)^{-1}
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}
for any
\Re\,\lambda>0
, and
\left\|(L+\lambda I)^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq(\Re\,\lambda)^{-1}
.

This lemma implies that there is a unique
L:D(L)\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with its domain dense in
W^{1,2}
corresponding to
\mathscr{L}:W^{1,2}\to\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}
, and
(Lu,v)=\langle\mathscr{L}u,v\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}
for any
u\in D(L)
,
v\in W^{1,2}
.

We denote by
\Theta(L)
the numerical range of
L
:

\Theta(L)=\{(Lu,u):u\in D(L)\text{ with }\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}=1.\}.

Let
T=-L
. Denote the resolvent set of
T
by
\rho(T)
. Note that since
L
is m-accretive,
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\Re\,\lambda<0\}\subset\rho(L)
. So
\lambda\in\rho(T)
whenever
\Re\,\lambda>0
. Let
\Sigma_{0}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Theta(T)}
, and denote by
\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
the component of
\Sigma_{0}
that contains
\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

\hat{\Sigma}_{0}\subset\rho(T)
. And for any
\lambda\in\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
,

\left\|(\lambda I-T)^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}=\left\|(\lambda I+L)^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\dist(\lambda;\overline{\Theta(T)})}.

Proof.

For any fixed
\lambda\in\Sigma_{0}
, for any
u\in D(T)
with
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}=1
, we have

0<\dist(\lambda,\overline{\Theta(T)})\leq\left|\lambda-(Tu,u)\right|=\left|((\lambda I-T)u,u)\right|\leq\left\|(\lambda I-T)u\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence, if
\lambda\in\rho(T)
, then

\left\|(\lambda I-T)^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\dist(\lambda;\overline{\Theta(T)})}.

(3.4)

Now we show
\hat{\Sigma}_{0}\subset\rho(T)
. Consider
\rho(T)\cap\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
. It is nonempty since
\mathbb{R}^{+}\subset\rho(T)\cap\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
. The fact that
\rho(T)
is open implies that
\rho(T)\cap\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
is open in
\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
. But it is also closed in
\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
since
\lambda_{n}\in\rho(T)\cap\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
and
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda\in\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
imply for
n
large enough,
\dist(\lambda_{n},\overline{\Theta(T)})>\frac{1}{2}\dist(\lambda,\overline{\Theta(T)})
, and consequently for
n
large enough

\left|\lambda_{n}-\lambda\right|<\dist(\lambda_{n},\overline{\Theta(T)})
. Write
\lambda I-T=(\lambda_{n}I-T)(I+(\lambda-\lambda_{n})(\lambda_{n}I-T)^{-1})
. From (
3.4
),

\left\|(\lambda-\lambda_{n})(\lambda_{n}I-T)^{-1}\right\|\leq\left|\lambda-\lambda_{n}\right|\left\|(\lambda_{n}I-T)^{-1}\right\|\leq\frac{\left|\lambda-\lambda_{n}\right|}{\dist(\lambda_{n};\overline{\Theta(T)})}<\frac{1}{2},

which implies that
(I+(\lambda-\lambda_{n})(\lambda_{n}I-T)^{-1})^{-1}
is bounded in
L^{2}
, and consequently so is
(\lambda I-T)^{-1}
, i.e.
\lambda\in\rho(T)
. This implies that
\rho(T)\cap\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
is closed in
\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
. By the connectedness of
\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
,
\rho(T)\cap\hat{\Sigma}_{0}=\hat{\Sigma}_{0}
, or
\hat{\Sigma}_{0}\subset\rho(T)
.
∎

Fix a
\theta_{1}\in(\theta_{0},\frac{\pi}{2})
. Let

\Gamma_{\pi-\theta_{1}}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\lambda\neq 0,\left|\arg{\lambda}\right|\leq\pi-\theta_{1}\}
. Then
\Gamma_{\pi-\theta_{1}}\subset\rho(T)
and there exists a
c_{0}=c_{0}(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})\geq 1
such that for any
\lambda\in\Gamma_{\pi-\theta_{1}}
,

\dist(\lambda;\overline{\Theta(T)})\geq\frac{\left|\lambda\right|}{c_{0}}.

(3.5)

Corollary 3.6
.

There exists a
C=C(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\lambda_{0})>0
such that
for any
\lambda\in\Gamma_{\pi-\theta_{1}}
,

\left\|(\lambda I+L)^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}+\frac{1}{\left|\lambda\right|^{1/2}}\left\|\nabla(\lambda I+L)^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\left|\lambda\right|}.

Proof.

It follows immediately from Lemma
3.3
and (
3.5
) that

\left\|(\lambda I+L)^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq\frac{c_{0}}{\left|\lambda\right|}.

Since
\Gamma_{\pi-\theta_{1}}\subset\rho(T)
, for any
\lambda\in\Gamma_{\pi-\theta_{1}}
, the range of
\lambda I+L
is
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Also, for any
u\in D(L)
,
((\lambda I+L)u,u)=\langle(\mathscr{L}+\lambda)u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}
, which gives that

\Re((\lambda I+L)u,u)\leq\left\|(\lambda I+L)u\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}.

On the other hand,

\Re((\lambda I+L)u,u)=\Re(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla\bar{u}+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u\right|^{2})\geq\lambda_{0}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\Re\lambda\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore,

\lambda_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\leq\left|\lambda\right|\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|(\lambda I+L)u\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Since

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|(\lambda I+L)^{-1}(\lambda I+L)u\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{c_{0}}{\left|\lambda\right|}\left\|(\lambda I+L)u\right\|_{L^{2}},

one obtains
\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(c_{0},\lambda_{0})\left|\lambda\right|^{-1/2}\left\|(\lambda I+L)u\right\|_{L^{2}}
, and consequently

\left\|\nabla(\lambda I+L)^{-1}\right\|\leq C(c_{0},\lambda_{0})\left|\lambda\right|^{-1/2}.

∎

4.
L^{p}
theory for the semigroup and square roots

The resolvent estimates we derived in Corollary
3.6
imply that there exists an analytic contraction semigroup
e^{-tL}
on
L^{2}
generated by
-L
. The semigroup can be expressed as the contour integral (see e.g.
[
21
]
Chapter 1, Theorem 7.7)

e^{-tL}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}e^{t\lambda}(L+\lambda I)^{-1}d\lambda,

where the path
\Gamma
consists of two half-rays
\Gamma_{\pm}=\left\{\lambda=re^{\pm i(\pi-\theta_{1})},r\geq R\right\}
and of the arc
\Gamma_{0}=\left\{\lambda=Re^{i\theta},\left|\theta\right|\leq\pi-\theta_{1}\right\}
, for any fixed
R>0
,
\theta_{1}\in(\theta_{0},\frac{\pi}{2})
, where
\theta_{0}
is as in Proposition
3.1
. It follows that
e^{-tL}
is holomorphic in
t
in the open sector
\left|\arg t\right|<\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{0}
.

Let
K_{t}(x,y)
be the kernel of
e^{-tL}
.
The results by Qian and Xi
[
22
]
show that the operator
L
satisfies the “Gaussian Property”, that is,

K_{t}(x,y)
satisfies the following bounds: there are some constants
C=C(\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},n)
,
\beta=\beta(\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},n)\in(0,1)
and
\mu_{0}=\mu_{0}(\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},n)\in(0,1)
, such that for all
t>0
,
0<\mu<\mu_{0}

\displaystyle\left|K_{t}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{\beta\left|x-y\right|^{2}}{t}},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(x+h,y)\right|

\displaystyle+\left|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(x,y+h)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{\left|h\right|}{t^{1/2}+\left|x-y\right|}\right)^{\mu}e^{{-\frac{\beta\left|x-y\right|^{2}}{t}}}

(4.2)

when
2\left|h\right|\leq t^{1/2}+\left|x-y\right|
.

We remark that the Gaussian estimates for the
t
-derivatives of the kernel
K_{t}(x,t)
can be derived from the Gaussian estimates (
[
20
]
Theorem 6.17):

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
\partial_{t}^{l}K_{t}(x,y)
satisfies the following estimates: there are some constants
C=C(\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},n)
,
\beta=\beta(\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},n)\in(0,1)
, such that for all
t>0
,

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{t}^{l}K_{t}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{l}t^{-\frac{n}{2}-l}e^{-\frac{\beta\left|x-y\right|^{2}}{t}}

(4.4)

The
L^{2}
(actually,
L^{p}
for all
1\leq p<\infty
) estimate for
\partial_{t}^{l}e^{-tL}
follows from (
4.4
) immediately: for all
t>0
,

\left\|\partial_{t}^{l}e^{-tL}\right\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq c_{l}t^{-l}\qquad\text{for all }l\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.5)

Moreover, we have for all
t>0
,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla e^{-tL}\right\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.6)

(
4.6
) can be derived from (
4.5
) as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla e^{-tL}\cdot\overline{\nabla e^{-tL}f}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\left\|\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,t^{-1}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

4.1.
L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates for the semigroup

Definition 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t>0}
be a family of operators. We say that
\mathcal{T}
satisfies
L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates if for some constants
C\geq 0
and
\alpha>0
for all closed sets
E
and
F
, all
h\in L^{2}
with support in
E
and all
t>0
we have

\left\|T_{t}h\right\|_{L^{2}(F)}\leq Ce^{-\frac{\alpha d(E,F)^{2}}{t}}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(4.8)

Here and subsequntly,
d(E,F)
is the semi-distance induced on sets by the Euclidean distance.

If
\mathcal{T}=(T_{z})_{z\in\Sigma_{\mu}}
is a family defined on a complex sector
\Sigma_{\mu}
with
0\leq\mu<\frac{\pi}{2}
, then we adopt the same definition and replace
t
by
\left|z\right|
in the right hand side of (
4.8
). In this case, the constants
C
and
\alpha
may depend on the angle
\mu
.

Proposition 4.9
.

There exists
\omega_{0}=\omega_{0}(n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0})\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, such that for all
\mu\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega_{0})
, the families
(e^{-zL})_{z\in\Sigma_{\mu}}
,
(zLe^{-zL})_{z\in\Sigma_{\mu}}
and
(\sqrt{z}\nabla e^{-zL})_{z\in\Sigma_{\mu}}
satisfy
L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates.

Proof.

We begin with the case of real times
t>0
. Let
\varphi
be a bounded Lipschitz function with Lipschitz constant 1 and
\rho>0
. Define

\mathscr{L}_{\rho}=e^{\rho\varphi}\mathscr{L}e^{-\rho\varphi}
as follows: for any
u,v\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\langle\mathscr{L}_{\rho}u,v\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}=\langle\mathscr{L}(e^{\rho\varphi}u),e^{-\rho\varphi}v\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}.

Note that since
\left\|\nabla\rho\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
is bounded, Proposition
2.6
implies that
\mathscr{L}_{\rho}:W^{1,2}\to\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}
is bounded.
Define
\mathscr{L}_{\rho}^{\prime}=\mathscr{L}_{\rho}+c_{0}\rho^{2}
, with
c_{0}
to be determined. Using Proposition
2.6
, we estimate

\Re\langle\mathscr{L}_{\rho}^{\prime}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\\
\geq\Re\langle\mathscr{L}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}-C_{n,\Lambda_{0}}\left\|\rho\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
+\left(c_{0}\rho^{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\left\|\rho\nabla\varphi\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left(c_{0}\rho^{2}-C_{n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0}}\rho^{2}\right)\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Here we have used
\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
. Now by choosing
c_{0}=c_{0}(n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0})
sufficiently large, we have

\Re\langle\mathscr{L}_{\rho}^{\prime}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{c_{0}}{2}\rho^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

For
\Im\langle\mathscr{L}_{\rho}^{\prime}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Im\langle\mathscr{L}_{\rho}^{\prime}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\Im\langle\mathscr{L}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\right|+C_{\lambda_{0}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\lambda_{0}}\rho^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\lambda_{0}}\rho^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, there exists
C_{0}=C_{0}(n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0})>0
, such that

\frac{\left|\Im\langle\mathscr{L}_{\rho}^{\prime}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\right|}{\Re\langle\mathscr{L}_{\rho}^{\prime}u,u\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}}\leq C_{0},

which implies that there exists
0<\omega_{0}=\omega_{0}(n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0})<\frac{\pi}{2}
such that

\forall\,\xi\in\Theta(\mathscr{L}_{\rho}^{\prime}),\quad\left|\xi\right|\leq\omega_{0}.

(4.10)

As we argue in Section
3
, we can find unique sectorial operators
L_{\rho}^{\prime}
and
L_{\rho}
corresponds to
\mathscr{L}_{\rho}^{\prime}
and
\mathscr{L}_{\rho}
, respectively. Then we can prove estimates (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) for the operator
L_{\rho}^{\prime}
. That is, there is
C=C(n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0})
, such that

\left\|e^{-tL_{\rho}^{\prime}}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|t\partial_{t}e^{-tL_{\rho}^{\prime}}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL_{\rho}^{\prime}}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}

for all
t>0
. Then by
L_{\rho}^{\prime}=L_{\rho}+c_{0}\rho^{2}I
and
\partial_{t}e^{-tL_{\rho}^{\prime}}=-L_{\rho}^{\prime}e^{-tL_{\rho}^{\prime}}
, a direct computation shows

\left\|e^{-tL_{\rho}}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|t(L_{\rho}+c_{0}\rho^{2}I)e^{-tL_{\rho}}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL_{\rho}}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{c_{0}\rho^{2}t}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}.

This implies

\left\|e^{-tL_{\rho}}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|t\partial_{t}e^{-tL_{\rho}}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL_{\rho}}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{2c_{0}\rho^{2}t}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}\quad\forall\,t>0.

(4.11)

Let
E
and
F
be two closed sets and
f\in L^{2}
, with compact support contained in
E
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, choose
\varphi(x)=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{d(x,E)}{1+\varepsilon d(x,E)}
. With this choice of

, the operator
\mathscr{L}_{\rho}
has bounded form.
Observe that there exists

sufficiently large, such that for any

Also, for any fixed

, such that for any

,
\varepsilon d(x,E)\leq\frac{1}{2}
for all

, and thus

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{d(x,E)}{1+\varepsilon d(x,E)}\geq\frac{2}{3}d(x,E),\quad\forall\,x\in F\cap B_{R}(0).

(4.12)

With these observations and (
4.11
), the argument of the

off-diagonal estimates for

,
(t\partial_{t}e^{-tL})_{t>0}
and

follows from

Proposition 3.1.

To extend to complex times, consider the operator

Remark 4.13

The same argument applies to the adjoint operator

Using the

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\underset{R\to\infty}{\lim}e^{-tL}(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}(x_{0})})
exists in
L_{\loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the limit does not depend on
x_{0}
. We define the limit to be
e^{-tL}f
.

Proof.

We first fix any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Fix any
R_{0}>1
, and let
R_{2}>R_{1}>8R_{0}
. Then there exists
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{l}R_{1}<R_{2}\leq 2^{l+1}R_{1}
. We write

\displaystyle\quad\left|e^{-tL}(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})})-e^{-tL}(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{0})})\right|=\left|e^{-tL}\left(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})}-f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{0})}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|e^{-tL}f\left(\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})}-\mathbbm{1}_{B_{2^{l}R_{1}}(x_{0})}\right)\right|+\sum_{k=1}^{l}\left|e^{-tL}f\left(\mathbbm{1}_{B_{2^{k}R_{1}}(x_{0})}-\mathbbm{1}_{B_{2^{k-1}R_{1}}(x_{0})}\right)\right|.

Observe that
d(B_{R_{0}}(x_{0}),B_{2^{k}R_{1}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{2^{k-1}R_{1}}(x_{0}))\gtrsim 2^{k-1}R_{1}
for
k=1,2,\dots,l
. Then by
L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates for
\left(e^{-tL}\right)_{t>0}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\Big|e^{-tL}

\displaystyle(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})})-e^{-tL}(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{0})})\Big|\Big|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{0}}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{c(2^{l}R_{1})^{2}}{t}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{2^{l}R_{1}}(x_{0}))}+\sum_{k=1}^{l}e^{-\frac{c(2^{k-1}R_{1})^{2}}{t}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{2^{k}R_{1}}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{l+1}e^{-\frac{c(2^{k-1}R_{1})^{2}}{t}}(2^{k}R_{1})^{n/2}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{n/2}R_{1}^{-n/2}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}},

where the implicit constant depends only on
\lambda_{0}
,
\Lambda_{0}
and
n
. This shows that
e^{-tL}(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}(x_{0})})
is a Cauchy sequence in
L_{\loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, when
f\in L^{\infty}
.

We now show the limit is independent of the choice of
x_{0}
. Let
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a different point than
x_{0}
. Then for
R
sufficiently large, the symmetric difference
B_{R}(x_{0})\Delta B_{R}(x_{1})
is contained in
B_{2R}(0)\setminus B_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)
. So by the
L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates for
e^{-tL}
, and that
d(B_{R}(x_{0})\Delta B_{R}(x_{1}),B_{\frac{R}{4}}(0))\gtrsim R
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-tL}f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}(x_{0})}-e^{-tL}f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}(x_{1})}\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{R}{4}}(0))}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{cR^{2}}{t}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x_{0})\Delta B_{R}(x_{1}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{cR^{2}}{t}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

This implies that
\lim_{R\to\infty}e^{-tL}f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}(x_{0})}=\lim_{R\to\infty}e^{-tL}f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}(x_{1})}
in the
L_{\loc}^{2}
sense.
∎

Remark 4.15
.

The limit
\lim_{R\to\infty}e^{-tL}(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}(x_{0})})
actually exists in

instead of the

Similarly, we can define
e^{-tL}f
for

Lipschitz.

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
f
be a Lipschitz function. Then for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the limit
\lim_{R\to\infty}e^{-tL}(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}(x_{0})})
exists in
L_{\loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and not depend on
x_{0}
. We define the limit to be
e^{-tL}f
.

Proof.

Fix any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, any
R_{0}>1
, and let
R_{2}>R_{1}>8R_{0}
. Then there exists
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{l}R_{1}<R_{2}\leq 2^{l+1}R_{1}
. We write

\left|e^{-tL}(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})})-e^{-tL}(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{0})})\right|\\
\leq\left|e^{-tL}\left((f-f(x_{0}))(\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})}-\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{0})})\right)\right|\\
+\left|e^{-tL}\left(f(x_{0})(\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})}-\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{1}}(x_{0})})\right)\right|=:I_{1}+I_{2}

Since
f(x_{0})
is a bounded constant function, the proof of Lemma
4.14
applies to
I_{2}
, and we have

\left\|I_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{0}}(x_{0}))}\lesssim t^{n/2}R_{1}^{-n/2}\left|f(x_{0})\right|.

(4.17)

For
I_{1}
, we have

\left\|I_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{0}}(x_{0}))}\lesssim e^{-c\frac{(2^{l}R_{1})^{2}}{t}}\left\|(f-f(x_{0}))\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{2^{l}R_{1}}(x_{0})}\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
+\sum_{k=1}^{l}e^{-c\frac{(2^{k-1}R_{1})^{2}}{t}}\left\|(f-f(x_{0}))\mathbbm{1}_{B_{2^{k}R_{1}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{2^{k-1}R_{1}}(x_{0})}\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{l+1}e^{-c\frac{(2^{k-1}R_{1})^{2}}{t}}(2^{k}R_{1})^{1+\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{(2^{k}R_{1})^{2}}{t}\right)^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}(2^{k}R_{1})^{1+\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
\lesssim t^{\frac{n+1}{2}}R_{1}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

This and (
4.17
) show that
e^{-tL}(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}(x_{0})})
is a Cauchy sequence in
L_{\loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, when
f
is Lipschitz. By a similar argument as in the proof of Lemma
4.14
, one can show the limit is independent of
x_{0}
.
∎

By applying the

Proposition 4.19
.

The conservation property
e^{-tL}1=1
, for any
t>0
, holds in the sense of
L_{\loc}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi
be an
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function with compact support, and suppose the support of
\Phi
is contained in a cube
Q
with
l(Q)=r_{0}
. We first show
e^{-tL}\Phi\in L^{1}
.

Let
Q_{0}=2Q
, and decompose
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into a union of nonoverlapping cubes with same size. That is,
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\bigsqcup_{k=0}^{\infty}Q_{k}
, with
\left|Q_{k}\right|=\left|Q_{0}\right|
. Let
x_{Q}
denote the center of
Q
and
Q_{0}
, and let
x_{k}
denote the center of
Q_{k}
, for
k=1,2,\dots
. Define

\mathcal{F}_{l}=\left\{Q_{k}:2lr_{0}\leq\left|x_{k}-x_{Q}\right|<2(l+1)r_{0}\right\},\quad\text{for }l=1,2,\dots.

By the
L^{2}
boundedness of the semigroup, we have

\int_{Q_{0}}\left|e^{-tL}\Phi\right|\leq\left|Q_{0}\right|^{1/2}\left\|e^{-tL}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left|Q_{0}\right|^{1/2}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(4.20)

For any
Q_{k}\in\mathcal{F}_{l}
, we use the
L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates for
(e^{-tL})_{t>0}
to obtain

\int_{Q_{k}}\left|e^{-tL}\Phi\right|\leq\left|Q_{k}\right|^{1/2}\left\|e^{-tL}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(Q_{k})}\lesssim\left|Q_{0}\right|^{1/2}e^{-\frac{c(lr_{0})^{2}}{t}}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
\left|\mathcal{F}_{l}\right|\approx l^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{Q_{k}\in\mathcal{F}_{l}}\int_{Q_{k}}\left|e^{-tL}\Phi\right|\lesssim l^{n}\left|Q_{0}\right|^{1/2}e^{-\frac{c(lr_{0})^{2}}{t}}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim l^{-2}r_{0}^{-\frac{n}{2}-2}t^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Summing in
l
yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Q_{0}}\left|e^{-tL}\Phi\right|\lesssim\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l^{-2}r_{0}^{-\frac{n}{2}-2}t^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim r_{0}^{-\frac{n}{2}-2}t^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(4.21)

Then
e^{-tL}\Phi\in L^{1}
follows from (
4.20
) and (
4.21
). The argument also applies to
e^{-tL^{*}}
and so
e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi\in L^{1}
. Therefore, by Lemma
4.14
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-tL}1\overline{\Phi}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-tL}(\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}})\overline{\Phi}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}\overline{e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\overline{e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}.

(4.22)

Here and subsequently,
B_{R}
is the ball centered at the origin with radius
R
. We shall first show
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-tL}1\overline{\Phi}
does not depend on
t>0
by proving

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-tL}1\overline{\Phi}=0,

(4.23)

and then show

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-tL}1\overline{\Phi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\overline{\Phi}.

(4.24)

This implies that
e^{-tL}1=1
in the sense of
L^{2}_{\loc}
.

Observe that we can define
\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f=\lim_{R\to\infty}\partial_{t}e^{-tL}(f\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}(x_{0})})
in
L_{\loc}^{2}
, for
f\in L^{\infty}
, for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This is because the argument in the proof of Lemma
4.14
applies to
\partial_{t}e^{-tL}
if one uses the
L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates for
\left(tLe^{-tL}\right)_{t>0}
instead of that for
\left(e^{-tL}\right)_{t>0}
. In particular, we have
\partial_{t}e^{-tL}1=\lim_{R\to\infty}\partial_{t}e^{-tL}\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}
. Also, using the
L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates for
\left(tL^{*}e^{-tL^{*}}\right)_{t>0}
, one can show
\partial_{t}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi\in L^{1}
. Therefore,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}e^{-tL}1\overline{\Phi}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int\partial_{t}e^{-tL}\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}\overline{\Phi}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int\mathbbm{1}_{B_{R}}\overline{\partial_{t}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}=\int\overline{\partial_{t}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}.

Let
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\eta=1
in
B_{1}
, and
\supp\eta\subset B_{2}
. Let
\eta_{R}(x)=\eta\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)
for
R>0
.
Then

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-tL}1\overline{\Phi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}e^{-tL}1\overline{\Phi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta_{R}\overline{\partial_{t}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1-\eta_{R})\overline{\partial_{t}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}.

(4.25)

Since
\partial_{t}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi\in L^{1}
, the last term goes to 0 as
R\to\infty
. We write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta_{R}\overline{\partial_{t}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta_{R}\overline{L^{*}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla\eta_{R}\cdot\overline{\nabla e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{s}\nabla\eta_{R}\cdot\overline{\nabla e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{a}\nabla\eta_{R}\cdot\overline{\nabla\left(\zeta_{R}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi\right)}.

Here,
\zeta_{R}(x)=\zeta\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)
, where
\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\zeta=1
in
B_{2}\setminus B_{1}
and
\supp\zeta\subset B_{5/2}\setminus B_{1/2}
. Note that
\zeta_{R}=1
in the support of
\nabla\eta_{R}
.

Choose
R
to be sufficiently large so that
Q\subset B_{\frac{R}{8}}
.
We estimate

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{s}\nabla\eta_{R}\cdot\overline{\nabla e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\left\|\nabla\eta_{R}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R}\setminus B_{R})}\\
\lesssim R^{\frac{n}{2}-1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{cR^{2}}{t}}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}},

where the last inequality follows from the
L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates for
\left(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL^{*}}\right)_{t>0}
. By Proposition
2.2
, we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{a}\nabla\eta_{R}\cdot\overline{\nabla\left(\zeta_{R}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi\right)}\right|\leq C\Lambda_{0}\left\|\nabla\eta_{R}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\left(\zeta_{R}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Using the support property of
\zeta_{R}
and
\nabla\zeta_{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\left(\zeta_{R}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\nabla(e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi)\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{5R}{2}}\setminus B_{\frac{R}{2}})}+R^{-1}\left\|e^{-tL^{*}}\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{5R}{2}}\setminus B_{\frac{R}{2}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{cR^{2}}{t}}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}}+R^{-1}e^{-\frac{cR^{2}}{t}}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}},

where the last inequality follows from the

off-diagonal estimates for
\left(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL^{*}}\right)_{t>0}
and
\left(e^{-tL^{*}}\right)_{t>0}
. Combining these estimates, we obtain
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta_{R}\overline{\partial_{t}e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}\to 0
as

. So from (

), the desired result (
4.23
) follows.

To prove (
4.24
), we fix
R>0
sufficiently large so that

. Write

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-tL}1\overline{\Phi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta_{R}\overline{e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1-\eta_{R})\overline{e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}.

Since

is strongly continuous in

We have

, where

is constructed in the beginning. Since we can also obtain (

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1-\eta_{R})\overline{e^{-tL^{*}}\Phi}\right|\lesssim r_{0}^{-\frac{n}{2}-2}t^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}},

which goes to

∎

4.2.
L^{p}
theory for the semigroup

We now study the uniform boundedness of the semigroup
(e^{-tL})_{t>0}
and of the family
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
on
L^{p}
spaces. We begin with a few definitions.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t>0}
be a family of uniformly bounded operators on
L^{2}
.

Definition 4.26
.

We say that
\mathcal{T}
is
L^{p}-L^{q}
bounded for some
p,q\in[1,\infty]
with
p\leq q
if for some constant
C
, for all
t>0
and all
h\in L^{p}\cap L^{2}

\left\|T_{t}h\right\|_{L^{q}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{\gamma_{pq}}{2}}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{p}},

where
\gamma_{pq}=\left|\frac{n}{q}-\frac{n}{p}\right|
. We shall use
\gamma_{p}
to denote
\gamma_{p2}=\left|\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n}{p}\right|
.

Definition 4.27
.

We say that
\mathcal{T}
satisfies
L^{p}-L^{q}
off-diagonal estimates for some
p,q\in[1,\infty]
with
p\leq q
if for some constants
C
,
c>0
, for all closed sets
E
and
F
, all
h\in L^{p}\cap L^{2}
with support in
E
and all
t>0
we have

\left\|T_{t}h\right\|_{L^{q}(F)}\leq Ct^{-\frac{\gamma_{pq}}{2}}e^{-\frac{cd(E,F)^{2}}{t}}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{p}}.

Note that the uniform
L^{p}
boundedness of
(e^{-tL})_{t>0}
and of
(t\partial_{t}e^{-tL})_{t>0}
follows from the kernel estimates (
4.4
).

Lemma 4.28
.

Let
p\geq 1
. There is some constant
C=C(n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},p)
such that for all
t>0
and all
f\in L^{p}
,

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}},

(4.29)

\displaystyle\left\|t\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}}.

(4.30)

Proof.

By (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|K_{t}(x,y)\right|dx\leq C

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|K_{t}(x,y)\right|dy\leq C,

(4.31)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\partial_{t}K_{t}(x,y)\right|dx\leq Ct^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\partial_{t}K_{t}(x,y)\right|dy\leq Ct^{-1}.

(4.32)

Then

\left|e^{-tL}f(x)\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{t}(x,y)f(y)dy\right|\\
\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|K_{t}(x,y)\right|dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|K_{t}(x,y)\right|\left|f(y)\right|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

So (
4.29
) follows from (
4.31
). And (
4.30
) follows from (
4.32
) by the same argument.
∎

Proposition 4.33
.

(1)

(e^{-tL})_{t>0}
is
L^{p}-L^{2}
bounded for any
1\leq p<2
.

(2)

(e^{-tL})_{t>0}
satisfies the

.

(3)

The next results for

Proposition 4.34
.

(1)

(2)

bounded for
2<p\leq\infty
, and satisfies the
L^{2}-L^{p}
off-diagonal estimates for
2<p<\infty
.

Proof.

(1). Let
p\in[1,2)
. Since
\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
we can apply the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and get,

\left\|\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2\alpha}\left\|\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{p}}^{2\beta}

(4.35)

for all
t>0
and
f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}
, where

\alpha+\beta=1\text{ and }(1+\gamma_{p})\alpha=\gamma_{p}.

Using
\partial_{t}^{2}e^{-tL}f\in L^{2}
and Lemma
A.2
, we have

\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\cdot\overline{\nabla\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f}=\Re\left(L\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f,\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\right)\\
=-\Re\langle\partial_{t}^{2}e^{-tL}f,e^{-tL}f\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By ellipticity,

\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{2\lambda_{0}}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.36)

Assume
f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}
with
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}}=1
. Let
\varphi(t)=\left\|\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\right\|
. By the
L^{p}
boundedness of
(\partial_{t}e^{-tL})_{t>0}
,
\left\|\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{p}t^{-1}
. Then by (
4.35
) and (
4.36
), one obtains

t^{\frac{2\beta}{\alpha}}\varphi(t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq-C\varphi^{\prime}(t)
.
Integrating in
t
,

\int_{t}^{2t}t^{\frac{2\beta}{\alpha}}dt\leq-C\int_{t}^{2t}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(t)}{\varphi(t)^{1/\alpha}}dt,

and thus
\varphi(t)\leq Ct^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}
.
Here we assumed that
\varphi(t)\neq 0
. Otherwise, considering
\varphi(t)+\varepsilon
and then letting
\varepsilon\to 0
would give the same result. Thus

\left\|t\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Ct^{-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}=Ct^{-\gamma_{p}}
, which proves (1).

(2). As in the proof of Proposition
4.33
, it follows from the
L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates of
(t\partial_{t}e^{-tL})_{t>0}
, the
L^{1}-L^{2}
boundedness of
(t\partial_{t}e^{-tL})_{t>0}
, and the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem.

(3). Since
e^{-tL}f\in D(L)
for any
f\in L^{2}
,
t\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f=-tLe^{-tL}f=-te^{-tL}Lf
. So we have

(t\partial_{t}e^{-tL})^{*}=-tL^{*}e^{-tL^{*}}=t\partial_{t}e^{-tL^{*}}.

Since (2) and (3) also hold for
t\partial_{t}e^{-tL^{*}}
, (3) follows from duality and a limiting argument.
∎

For
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
, when
p<2
, we immediately obtain the
L^{p}-L^{2}
boundedness and the
L^{p}-L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates. We include the short proofs here for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 4.37
.

(1)

(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
is
L^{p}-L^{2}
bounded for any
1\leq p<2
.

(2)

(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
satisfies
L^{p}-L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates for any
1<p<2
.

Proof.

Let
p\in[1,2)
and
f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}
. Write
\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL}f=\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-\frac{tL}{2}}e^{-\frac{tL}{2}}f
. Then by the
L^{2}
boundedness of
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
and the
L^{p}-L^{2}
boundedness of
(e^{-tL})_{t>0}
, one has

\left\|\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\|e^{-\frac{tL}{2}}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{p}t^{-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}},

which proves (1).

(2) follows from the
L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates of
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
, the
L^{1}-L^{2}
boundedness of
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
, and the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem.
∎

When
p>2
, a duality argument would not give us the desired results as in Proposition
4.33
(3) and Proposition
4.34
(3). However, we are able to derive a reverse Hölder type inequality for
\nabla e^{-tL}f
, and then use the
L^{2}-L^{p}
boundedness of
(t\partial_{t}e^{-tL})_{t>0}
to obtain the
L^{2}-L^{p}
boundedness of
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
. Note that this approach is entirely different from the existing proof (see e.g.
[
1
]
chapter 4), as the latter relies on the boundedness of the coefficients and does not work for BMO coefficients.

Proposition 4.38
.

(1)

(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
is
L^{2}-L^{p}
bounded for any
2\leq p\leq 2+\epsilon_{1}
for some
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{1}(\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},n)>0
.

(2)

(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
satisfies the
L^{2}-L^{p}
off-diagonal estimates for any
2\leq p<2+\epsilon_{1}
, where
\epsilon_{1}
is as in (1).

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and let
u(x,t)=e^{-tL}f(x)
. Then
u
satisfies the equation
\partial_{t}u+Lu=0
in
L^{2}
. That is, for any
w\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(x)\nabla u(x,t)\cdot\overline{\nabla w(x)}dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}u(x,t)\overline{w(x)}dx,\quad\forall\,t>0.

Fix
t>0
and fix a cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
l(Q)=\rho_{0}
, where
\rho_{0}
is to be determined. Let
x_{0}\in 3Q
and let

0<\rho<\min\left\{\frac{1}{2}\dist(x_{0},\partial(3Q)),\rho_{0}\right\}
. Let
Q_{s}(x)
denote the cube centered at
x
with side length
s
. Choose
\varphi\in C_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
,
\varphi=1
in
Q_{\rho}(x_{0})
,
\supp\varphi\subset Q_{\frac{3}{2}\rho}(x_{0})
, and
\left|\nabla\varphi\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho}
.

Let
w(x)=\left(u(x,t)-c\right)\varphi^{2}(x)
, where
c=\fint_{Q_{2\rho}(x_{0})}u(x,t)dx
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(x)\nabla u(x,t)\cdot\nabla\left((u(x,t)-c)\varphi^{2}(x)\right)dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}u(x,t)\left(u(x,t)-c\right)\varphi^{2}(x)dx.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{s}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left((u-c)\varphi^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{s}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\varphi^{2}dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{s}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\left((u-c)\varphi\right)dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\varphi^{2}-C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u-c\right|^{2}\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\int_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}-C\rho^{-2}\int_{Q_{\frac{3}{2}\rho}(x_{0})}\left|u-c\right|^{2}.

To deal with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{a}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left((u-c)\varphi^{2}\right)dx
, we introduce another bump function
\eta\in C_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta=1
on
Q_{\frac{3}{2}\rho}(x_{0})
,
\supp\eta\subset Q_{2\rho}(x_{0})
, and
\left|\nabla\eta\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{a}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left((u-c)\varphi^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{a}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(\varphi^{2})(u-c)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{a}\nabla\left((u-c)^{2}\eta^{2}\right)\cdot\nabla(\varphi^{2}).

By Proposition
2.6
,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{a}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left((u-c)\varphi^{2}\right)dx\right|\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|(u-c)\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\left((u-c)\eta\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\eta^{2}dx+C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u-c\right|^{2}\eta^{2}dx\\
+C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u-c\right|^{2}\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{2}dx\\
\leq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{4}\int_{Q_{2\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx+C\rho^{-2}\int_{Q_{2\rho}(x_{0})}\left|u-c\right|^{2}dx.

For
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}u(x,t)\left(u(x,t)-c\right)\varphi^{2}(x)dx
, we use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to get

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}u(x,t)\left(u(x,t)-c\right)\varphi^{2}(x)dx\right|\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\varphi^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u-c\right|^{2}\varphi^{2}dx\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}^{2}\int_{Q_{2\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{-2}\int_{Q_{2\rho}(x_{0})}\left|u-c\right|^{2}dx.

Combining these estimates, we obtain

\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\int_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx\\
\leq\frac{C}{\rho^{2}}\int_{Q_{2\rho}(x_{0})}\left|u-c\right|^{2}dx+\frac{\lambda_{0}}{4}\int_{Q_{2\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx+\frac{\rho_{0}^{2}}{2}\int_{Q_{2\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}dx.

The Sobolev-Poincaré inequality gives

\fint_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx\\
\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{2\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u\right|^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}+\frac{1}{2}\fint_{Q_{2\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx+C\rho_{0}^{2}\fint_{Q_{2\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}dx.

Then by Lemma
A.1
, there is some
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{1}(\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},n)>0
, such that for all
p\in[2,2+\epsilon_{1}]
,

\left(\fint_{Q}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\leq C\Big\{\left(\fint_{2Q}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\fint_{2Q}\left|\rho_{0}\partial_{t}u\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\Big\},

that is,

\int_{Q}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}dx\leq C\rho_{0}^{-p\gamma_{p}}\left(\int_{2Q}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\right)^{p/2}+\int_{2Q}\left|\rho_{0}\partial_{t}u\right|^{p}dx.

(4.39)

Decompose
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into a union of disjoint cubes
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\sqcup_{j=1}^{\infty}Q_{j}
with each
Q_{j}
having side length
\rho_{0}
. For each
Q_{j}
, applying (
4.39
) and then summing in
j
, one has

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\rho_{0}^{-\gamma_{p}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C\left\|\rho_{0}\partial_{t}u\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad\forall\,p\in[2,2+\epsilon_{1}].

Choosing
\rho_{0}=\sqrt{t}
gives

\left\|\sqrt{t}\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}}\left\|\sqrt{t}\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}+C\left\|t\partial_{t}u\right\|_{L^{p}},\quad\forall\,p\in[2,2+\epsilon_{1}].

Then by the
L^{2}
boundedness of
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
and the
L^{2}-L^{p}
boundedness of
(t\partial_{t}e^{-tL})_{t>0}
, we obtain

\left\|\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\forall\,p\in[2,2+\epsilon_{1}],\quad f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Thus (1) follows from a standard limiting argument.

(2) can be proved using the
L^{2}
off-diagonal estimates and the
L^{2}-L^{2+\epsilon_{1}}
boundedness of
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
, and the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem.
∎

4.3.
L^{p}
Theory for the square root

Since
L
is an m-accretive operator, there is a unique m-accretive square root
L^{1/2}
such that

L^{1/2}L^{1/2}=L\qquad\text{in}\quad D(L).

(4.40)

Also,
L^{1/2}
is m-sectorial with the numerical range contained in the sector
\left|\arg\xi\right|\leq\frac{\pi}{4}
. And
D(L)
is a
core
of
L^{1/2}
, i.e.
\left\{(u,L^{1/2}u):u\in D(L)\right\}
is dense in the graph
\left\{(u,L^{1/2}u):u\in D(L^{1/2})\right\}
(see
[
15
]
p.281 for a proof for these facts).

Our goal in this section is to prove the
L^{p}
bounds for the square root.

Many formulas can be used to compute
L^{1/2}
. The one we are going to use is

L^{1/2}f=\pi^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tL}Lf\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}.

(4.41)

Observe that the integral converges in
L^{2}
when
f\in D(L)
. Since for
f\in D(L)
,
Lf\in L^{2}
, then by the
L^{2}
boundedness of the semigroup,
\int_{0}^{1}e^{-tL}Lf\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}
converges. And the
L^{2}
bound of
(t\partial_{t}e^{-tL})_{t>0}
implies that
\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-tL}Lf\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}
converges in
L^{2}
.

The determination of the domain of the square root of
L
has become known as the Kato square root problem. It has been shown by Auscher, Hofmann, Lacey, McIntosh, and Tchamitchian
[
2
]
that for a uniformly complex elliptic operator
L=-\divg(A\nabla)
with bounded measurable coefficients, one has in all dimensions

\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\approx\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}},

(4.42)

and the domain of
L^{1/2}
is
W^{1,2}
, which was known as the Kato’s conjecture. Recently, Escauriaza and Hofmann (
[
5
]
) extended the result to the same kind of operators that we are interested in, that is, operators with a BMO anti-symmetric part. Note that although they only showed one side of (
4.42
), that is

\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}},

(4.43)

the other direction follows from a duality argument.
In fact, note that the same argument applies to
L^{*}
so one has

\left\|(L^{*})^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(4.44)

It turns out that (
4.43
) and (
4.44
) are enough:

Lemma 4.45
.

If (
4.43
) holds for all
f\in D(L)
, and (
4.44
) holds for all
f\in D(L^{*})
. Then

\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\forall\,f\in W^{1,2}.

(4.46)

And the domain of
L^{1/2}
is
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

We first show that
W^{1,2}\subset D(L^{1/2})
. Since
D(L)
is dense in
W^{1,2}
, for any
u\in W^{1,2}
, there are
\left\{u_{k}\right\}\subset D(L)
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
W^{1,2}
. Then by (
4.43
),
\left\|L^{1/2}(u_{k}-u_{j})\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|\nabla(u_{k}-u_{j})\right\|_{L^{2}}
. This shows that
\left\{L^{1/2}u_{k}\right\}
is Cauchy in
L^{2}
. Suppose
L^{1/2}u_{k}\to v\in L^{2}
. Since
L^{1/2}
is closed, we have
L^{1/2}u=v
, and
u\in D(L^{1/2})
.

Now we show (
4.46
) holds. Let
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
\mathbf{g}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\left\|\mathbf{g}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
. For any
\delta>0
, define

h_{\delta}:=(L^{*}+\delta I)^{-1}(-\divg\mathbf{g})\in D(L^{*})
. That is,

\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{\delta}\,\overline{w}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{*}\nabla h_{\delta}\cdot\overline{\nabla w}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\divg\mathbf{g}\,\overline{w}\,dx\qquad\forall\,w\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(4.47)

Letting
w=h_{\delta}
and taking real parts of (
4.47
), then ellipticity and Young’s inequality give

\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|h_{\delta}\right|^{2}dx+\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla h_{\delta}\right|^{2}dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathbf{g}\right|^{2}dx\leq C.

(4.48)

By writing

\displaystyle\left(\nabla f,\mathbf{g}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(f,\divg\mathbf{g}\right)=\left(f,(L^{*}+\delta I)h_{\delta}\right)=(f,(L^{*})^{1/2}(L^{*})^{1/2}h_{\delta})+\delta(f,h_{\delta})

\displaystyle=(L^{1/2}f,(L^{*})^{1/2}h_{\delta})+\delta(f,h_{\delta}),

we get that

\displaystyle\left|(\nabla f,\mathbf{g})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|(L^{*})^{1/2}h_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\delta\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|h_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla h_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\delta\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|h_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{2}}+\delta^{1/2}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}},

where we have used (
4.44
) in the second inequality, and (
4.48
) in the last inequality.
Therefore,

\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{g}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\\
\left\|\mathbf{g}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left|\left(\nabla f,\mathbf{g}\right)\right|\lesssim\left\|L^{1/2}f\right\|_{L^{2}}+\delta^{1/2}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Letting
\delta\to 0
, we obtain (
4.46
) holds for all
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, (
4.46
) holds for all
f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which contains the domain of
L
.

Finally, we show
D(L^{1/2})\subset W^{1,2}
, and thus proves
D(L^{1/2})=W^{1,2}
. To this end, let
u\in D(L^{1/2})
. Since
D(L)
is a core of
L^{1/2}
, there exist
\left\{u_{n}\right\}\subset D(L)
such that
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{2}
, and
L^{1/2}(u_{n})\to L^{1/2}u
in
L^{2}
. Since

\left\|\nabla(u_{n}-u_{m})\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|L^{1/2}(u_{n}-u_{m})\right\|_{L^{2}},

\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,2}
. This implies
u\in W^{1,2}
.
∎

From the Kato’s estimate (
4.43
) one can see that
L^{1/2}
can be extended to the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{1,2}
.
In particular,
L^{1/2}
extends to an isomorphism from
\dot{W}^{1,2}
to
L^{2}
and

g=L^{1/2}L^{-1/2}g\qquad\forall\,g\in L^{2}.

(4.49)

In fact, by (
4.46
),
L^{1/2}
is one-to-one. So it suffices to justify that the range of
L^{1/2}
is the whole
L^{2}
. To this end, we first show that the range of
L
is dense in
L^{2}
.

Lemma 4.50
.

The range of
L
is dense in
L^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
g\in L^{2}
. For any
\delta>0
, let
g_{\delta}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\left\|g_{\delta}-g\right\|_{L^{2}}<\delta
. Define

f^{(\delta)}_{\epsilon}:=\left(L+\epsilon I\right)^{-1}g_{\delta}\in D(L).

We claim that
\left\|Lf^{(\delta)}_{\epsilon}-g\right\|_{L^{2}}<C\delta
when
\epsilon
is sufficiently small. And this would complete the proof of the lemma.

We write

Lf^{(\delta)}_{\epsilon}-g=L(L+\epsilon I)^{-1}g_{\delta}-g=g_{\delta}-\epsilon(L+\epsilon I)^{-1}g_{\delta}-g,

and then

\displaystyle\left\|Lf^{(\delta)}_{\epsilon}-g\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|g_{\delta}-g\right\|_{L^{2}}+\epsilon\left\|(L+\epsilon I)^{-1}g_{\delta}\right\|<\delta+\epsilon\left\|(L+\epsilon I)^{-1}g_{\delta}\right\|.

(4.51)

We have

(L+\epsilon I)^{-1}g_{\delta}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t(L+\epsilon I)}(g_{\delta})dt,

and thus

\left\|(L+\epsilon I)^{-1}g_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t\epsilon}\left\|e^{-tL}g_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}}dt.

Fix any
1<p<2
, then by the
L^{p}-L^{2}
bound of the semigroup, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|(L+\epsilon I)^{-1}g_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t\epsilon}t^{-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}}\left\|g_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{p}}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\tau}\tau^{-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}}d\tau\left\|g_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}-1}.

By choosing
\epsilon
sufficiently small, this and (
4.51
) imply that
\left\|Lf^{(\delta)}_{\epsilon}-g\right\|_{L^{2}}<2\delta
. Since
\delta>0
is arbitrary, it proves that the range of
L
is dense in
L^{2}
.
∎

Remark 4.52

Lemma 4.56
.

Let
n\geq 3
, and let
2^{*}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
. Let
1<p<\infty
and
p\neq 2
. Then for all
g\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}
,

L^{-1/2}g=\pi^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tL}g\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}

(4.57)

is valid and converges in
L^{p}+L^{2^{*}}
if
p\neq 2^{*}
, and in
L^{2^{*}}+L^{p-\epsilon}
if
p=2^{*}
, where
\epsilon>0
is arbitrarily small.

Proof.

Let
g\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}
, and write

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tL}g\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-tL}g\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-tL}g\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}=:I+II.

We first consider
1<p<2
. By the
L^{p}
boundedness of the semigroup,
I
converges in
L^{p}
norm.
For
II
, note that we have

\left\|e^{-tL}g\right\|_{L^{2^{*}}}\lesssim\left\|\nabla e^{-tL}g\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1+\gamma_{p}}{2}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p}},

(4.58)

which is a consequence of the
L^{p}-L^{2}
bound of
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
and Sobolev embedding.
So the integral converges in
L^{p}+L^{2^{*}}
norm.

For
p>2
, we consider the following cases.

(1)

p>2^{*}
. By the
L^{2^{*}}
bound of
(e^{-tL})_{t>0}
,
\left\|I\right\|_{L^{2^{*}}}\lesssim\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2^{*}}}\leq\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}\cap L^{p}}
, and thus
I
converges in
L^{2^{*}}
.
II
converges in
L^{p}
because of the
L^{2}-L^{p}
boundedness of
(e^{-tL})_{t>0}
. Note that
p>2^{*}
gives
\gamma_{p}>1
, and thus
\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{1+\gamma_{p}}{2}}dt<\infty
.

(2)

2<p<2^{*}
. For
I
, we use the
L^{p}-L^{2^{*}}
boundedness of the semigroup to get

\left\|I\right\|_{L^{2^{*}}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}t^{-\frac{\gamma_{p2^{*}}+1}{2}}dt\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p}},

where we have used
\gamma_{p2^{*}}=\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n-2}{n}<1
. That is,
I
converges in
L^{2^{*}}
.
Let
p_{*}=\frac{np}{n+p}
be the reverse Sobolev exponent of
p
. Then by Sobolev embedding and
L^{p_{*}}-L^{2}
boundedness of
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
(note that
p_{*}<2
), one gets

\left\|e^{-tL}g\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\|\nabla e^{-tL}g\right\|_{L^{p_{*}}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1+\gamma_{p_{*}2}}{2}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}},

which yields
II
converges in
L^{p}
.

(3)

p=2^{*}
. One can see
I
converges in
L^{2^{*}}
from the
L^{2^{*}}
boundedness of
(e^{-tL})_{t>0}
. For
II
, the Sobolev embedding and
L^{(p-\epsilon)_{*}}-L^{2}
boundedness of
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
imply that
II
converges in
L^{p-\epsilon}
, for arbitrary small
\epsilon>0
.

Now it remains to show that the equality (
4.57
) holds for any
g\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}
.
By Lemma
4.50
and the remark after it,
R(L)
contains a dense subset of
L^{2}\cap L^{p}
. Therefore, there exists
\left\{g_{n}\right\}\subset R(L)\cap L^{p}
such that
g_{n}\to g\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}
. Then

L^{-1/2}g_{n}=\pi^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tL}g_{n}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}.

So from the convergence argument above, one can see that
\left\{L^{-1/2}g_{n}\right\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}+L^{2^{*}}
if
p\neq 2^{*}
. Suppose
L^{-1/2}g_{n}\to f\in L^{p}+L^{2^{*}}
. Since
L^{1/2}
is an isomorphism from
\dot{W}^{1,2}
to
L^{2}
,
L^{-1/2}g
is well-defined. We compute

\displaystyle\left\|L^{-1/2}g-f\right\|_{L^{p}+L^{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|L^{-1/2}g-L^{-1/2}g_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}+L^{2^{*}}}+\left\|L^{-1/2}g_{n}-f\right\|_{L^{p}+L^{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|L^{-1/2}g-L^{-1/2}g_{n}\right\|_{L^{2^{*}}}+\left\|L^{-1/2}g_{n}-f\right\|_{L^{p}+L^{2^{*}}}.

And by Sobolev embedding and (
4.55
),

\left\|L^{-1/2}g-L^{-1/2}g_{n}\right\|_{L^{2^{*}}}\lesssim\left\|\nabla L^{-1/2}(g_{n}-g)\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|g_{n}-g\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Thus, we have proved that
L^{-1/2}g=f
. When
p=2^{*}
,
\left\{L^{-1/2}g_{n}\right\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p-\epsilon}+L^{2^{*}}
, and a similar argument gives the same result.
∎

Lemma 4.59
.

Let

Corollary 4.60
.

We have the following representation for the Riesz transform:

\left(\nabla L^{-1/2}f,\mathbf{v}\right)=\pi^{-1/2}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\nabla e^{-tL}f\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,\,\overline{\mathbf{v}}\,dx

for all
f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}
, and for all
\mathbb{C}^{n}
- valued
\mathbf{v}\in C_{0}^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Since we have observed in Lemma
4.56
and
4.59
that the improper integral defining
L^{-1/2}f
converges in

L^{p}+L^{2^{*}}
or in
L^{p-\epsilon}+L^{2^{*}}
if
p=2^{*}
, or in
L^{p}+BMO
if
n=2
, and since
\divg{\mathbf{v}}
is compactly supported and belongs to every

L^{p}
, and to the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{1}
, we can write

\displaystyle\pi^{1/2}

\displaystyle\left(\nabla L^{-1/2}f,\mathbf{v}\right)=\pi^{1/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla L^{-1/2}f\,\,\overline{\mathbf{v}}\,dx=-\pi^{1/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}L^{-1/2}f\,\,\overline{\divg\mathbf{v}}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}e^{-tL}f\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,\,\overline{\divg\mathbf{v}}\,dx=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\epsilon}^{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla e^{-tL}f\,\,\overline{\mathbf{v}}\,dx\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\epsilon}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla e^{-tL}f\,\,\overline{\mathbf{v}}\,dx\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla e^{-tL}f\,\,\overline{\mathbf{v}}\,dx\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}

(4.61)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\epsilon}^{1}\nabla e^{-tL}f\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,\,\overline{\mathbf{v}}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\nabla e^{-tL}f\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,\,\overline{\mathbf{v}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\nabla e^{-tL}f\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,\,\overline{\mathbf{v}}\,dx

where we have used the
L^{p}-L^{2}
bound (or the
L^{2}-L^{p}
bound if
p>2
) of
(\sqrt{t}\nabla e^{-tL})_{t>0}
, so the second improper integral converges, and we can then exchange the order of integration.
∎

We now show that the limit can be taken inside the integral in Corollary
4.60
and thus we have the following formula for the Riesz transform associated to
L
:

Proposition 4.62
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
p\neq 2
, then

\nabla L^{-1/2}(f)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\nabla e^{-tL}f}{\sqrt{t}}dt,\qquad\forall\,f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.60
, it suffices to show that for
f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}
,

F_{k}(f):=\int_{\frac{1}{k}}^{\infty}\frac{\nabla e^{-tL}f}{\sqrt{t}}dt\quad\text{is a Cauchy sequence in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(4.63)

By the McIntosh-Yagi theorem (
[
19
]
Theorem 1), we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|(tL)^{1/4}e^{-tL}f\right|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\qquad\forall\,f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

which implies that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta_{0}=\delta(f,\epsilon)>0
such that

\int_{0}^{\delta_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|(tL)^{1/4}e^{-tL}f\right|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\leq\epsilon^{2}.

(4.64)

Set
N_{0}>1
so that
\frac{1}{N_{0}}<\delta_{0}
, and choose
m>k>N_{0}
. We write

F_{m}(f)-F_{k}(f)=\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{\nabla e^{-tL}f}{\sqrt{t}}dt=\nabla L^{-1/2}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{L^{1/2}e^{-tL}f}{\sqrt{t}}dt.

Since
\nabla L^{-1/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}
,

\displaystyle\left\|F_{m}(f)-F_{k}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{L^{1/2}e^{-tL}f}{\sqrt{t}}dt\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=C\sup_{g\in L^{2},\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1}\left|\left(\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{L^{1/2}e^{-tL}f}{\sqrt{t}}dt,g\right)\right|.

We compute

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{L^{1/2}e^{-tL}f}{\sqrt{t}}dt,g\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\left(L^{1/2}e^{-tL/2}f,e^{-tL^{*}/2}g\right)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\left(L^{1/4}e^{-tL/2}f,(L^{*})^{1/4}e^{-tL^{*}/2}g\right)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{1}{t}\left((tL)^{1/4}e^{-tL/2}f,(tL^{*})^{1/4}e^{-tL^{*}/2}g\right)dt.

Then by Cauchy-Schwartz, the McIntosh-Yagi theorem and (
4.64
), we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\left|\left(\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{L^{1/2}e^{-tL}f}{\sqrt{t}}dt,g\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|(tL)^{1/4}e^{-tL/2}f\right|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|(tL^{*})^{1/4}e^{-tL^{*}/2}g\right|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{\delta_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|(tL)^{1/4}e^{-tL/2}f\right|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right)^{1/2}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon.

This implies that for any
m\geq k>N_{0}
,
\left\|F_{m}(f)-F_{k}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon
, that is,
\left\{F_{k}(f)\right\}
is Cauchy in
L^{2}
.
∎

Proposition 5.1

Proof.

We first prove

(5.4)

Actually, the converse of (

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|e^{-tL}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

We postpone its proof to the end.
Using Lemma

\displaystyle\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla e^{-tL}f\cdot\overline{\nabla e^{-tL}f}

\displaystyle=\Re\left(Le^{-tL}f,e^{-tL}f\right)=-\Re\langle\partial_{t}e^{-tL}f,e^{-tL}f\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}

Therefore,

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla e^{-tL}f\cdot\overline{\nabla e^{-tL}f}dxdt,

and (

Using

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|(L^{1/2}e^{-tL}f)(x)\right|^{2}dxdt\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla e^{-tL}f\right|^{2}dxdt\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

Proof of (

.
The only thing that needs clarification is

Fix any

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose
u,v\in L^{2}\left((0,T),W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
with
\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v\in L^{2}\left((0,T),\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
. Then

(1)

u\in C\left([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
;

(2)

The mapping
t\mapsto\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is absolutely continuous, with

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=2\Re\langle\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t),u(\cdot,t)\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in[0,T].

As a consequence,

\frac{d}{dt}\left(u(\cdot,t),v(\cdot,t)\right)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\langle\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t),v(\cdot,t)\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}+\overline{\langle{\partial_{t}v(\cdot,t),u(\cdot,t)\rangle}}_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\quad\text{a.e.}.

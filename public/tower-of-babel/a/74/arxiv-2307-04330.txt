Abstract
This paper presents a pressure-robust enriched Galerkin (EG) method for the Brinkman equations with minimal degrees of freedom based on EG velocity and pressure spaces. The velocity space consists of linear Lagrange polynomials enriched by a discontinuous, piecewise linear, and mean-zero vector function per element, while piecewise constant functions approximate the pressure. We derive, analyze, and compare two EG methods in this paper: standard and robust methods. The standard method requires a mesh size to be less than a viscous parameter to produce stable and accurate velocity solutions, which is impractical in the Darcy regime. Therefore, we propose the pressure-robust method by utilizing a velocity reconstruction operator and replacing EG velocity functions with a reconstructed velocity. The robust method yields error estimates independent of a pressure term and shows uniform performance from the Stokes to Darcy regimes, preserving minimal degrees of freedom. We prove well-posedness and error estimates for both the standard and robust EG methods. We finally confirm theoretical results through numerical experiments with two- and three-dimensional examples and compare the methods’ performance to support the need for the robust method. Keywords: enriched Galerkin finite element methods, Brinkman equations, pressure-robust, velocity reconstruction, uniform performance

Introduction

We consider the stationary Brinkman equations in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d=2,3
with simply connected Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
: Find fluid velocity
{\bf u}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and pressure
p:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle-\mu\Delta\mathbf{u}+\frac{\mu}{K}\mathbf{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle{\bf u}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(1.1c)

where
\mu
is fluid viscosity,
K
is media permeability, and
{\bf f}
is a given body force.
The Brinkman equations describe fluid flow in porous media characterized by interconnected pores that allow for the flow of fluids, considering both the viscous forces within the fluid and the resistance from the porous media. The Brinkman equations provide a mathematical framework for studying and modeling complex phenomena such as groundwater flow, multiphase flow in oil reservoirs, blood flow in biological tissues, and pollutant transport in porous media.
In this paper, for simplicity, we consider the scaled Brinkman equations

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

(1.2a)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

(1.2b)

\displaystyle{\bf u}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(1.2c)

where
\nu\in[0,1]
is a viscous parameter.
Mathematically, the Brinkman equations can be seen as a combination of the Stokes and Darcy equations.
When
\nu\rightarrow 1
, the Brinkman equations approach a Stokes regime affected by the viscous forces, so standard mixed formulations require the
H^{1}
-conformity for velocity.
On the other hand, since the Darcy model becomes more prominent as
\nu\rightarrow 0
, finite-dimensional spaces for velocity are forced to satisfy the
H(\text{div})
-conformity.
This compatibility in velocity spaces makes it challenging to construct robust numerical solvers for the Brinkman equations in both the Stokes and Darcy regimes.
The numerical tests in
[
8
,
16
]
show that standard mixed methods with well-known inf-sup stables Stokes elements, such as MINI and Taylor-Hood elements, produce suboptimal orders of convergence in the Darcy regime.
Moreover, with piecewise constant approximations for pressure, the standard methods’ velocity errors do not converge in the Darcy regime, while mesh size decreases.
On the other hand, Darcy elements such as Raviart-Thomas and Brezzi-Douglas-Marini do not work for the Stokes domain because they do not satisfy the
H^{1}
-conformity.
Therefore, the development of robust numerical solvers for the Brinkman equations has had considerable attention.

There have been three major categories in developing robust numerical methods for the Brinkman equations. The first category considers Stokes/Darcy elements and adds stabilization (or penalty) terms or degrees of freedom to impose normal/tangential continuity, respectively. This approach allows Stokes elements to cover the Darcy regime
[
3
,
19
]
or
H(\text{div})
-conforming finite elements to be extended to the Stokes regime
[
13
,
14
,
19
,
12
]
. Also, the stabilized method in
[
2
]
coarsens a pressure space and applies a stabilization term on pressure, while the robust method in
[
16
]
uses an enlarged velocity space. The second approach is to introduce another meaningful unknown and define its suitable formulation and finite-dimensional space, such as velocity gradient
[
22
,
6
,
7
,
9
]
, vorticity
[
5
,
1
,
18
]
, and Lagrange multipliers at elements’ boundaries
[
11
]
. The third direction is the development of a velocity reconstruction operator, first introduced in
[
15
]
, mapping Stokes elements into an
H(\text{div})
-conforming space. In a discrete problem for the Brinkman equations, reconstructed velocity functions replace Stokes elements in the Darcy term and the test function on the right-hand side. This idea has been adopted for a uniformly robust weak Galerkin method for the Brinkman equations
[
17
]
, which inspires our work because of its simplicity in modification.

Our research focuses on developing a robust numerical method for the Brinkman equations with minimal degrees of freedom. The enriched Galerkin (EG) velocity and pressure spaces have been proposed by
[
20
]
for solving the Stokes equations with minimal degrees of freedom. The velocity space consists of linear Lagrange polynomials enriched by a discontinuous, piecewise linear, and mean-zero vector function per element, while piecewise constant functions approximate the pressure. More precisely, a velocity function
{\bf v}={\bf v}^{C}+{\bf v}^{D}
consists of a continuous linear Lagrange polynomial
{\bf v}^{C}
and a discontinuous piecewise linear enrichment function
{\bf v}^{D}
, so interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (IPDG) formulations are adopted to remedy the discontinuity of
{\bf v}^{D}
. These velocity and pressure spaces satisfy the inf-sup condition for the Stokes equations, so they are stable Stokes elements.
We first observe a standard EG method derived from adding the Darcy term
({\bf u},{\bf v})_{\Omega}
to the Stokes discrete problem in
[
20
]
.
Our numerical analysis and experiments show that the standard EG method provides stable solutions and convergent errors for the Brinkman equations if a mesh size satisfies the condition
h<\sqrt{\nu}
that is impractical in the Darcy regime (
\nu\rightarrow 0
). Hence, inspired by
[
17
]
, we use the velocity reconstruction operator
[
10
]
mapping the EG velocity to the first-order Brezzi-Douglas-Marini space, whose consequent action is preserving the continuous component
{\bf v}^{C}
and mapping only the discontinuous component
{\bf v}^{D}
to the lowest-order Raviart-Thomas space. Then, we replace the EG velocity in the Darcy term and the test function on the right-hand side with the reconstructed linear
H(\text{div})
-conforming velocity.
Therefore, with this simple modification, our resulting EG method yields pressure-robust error estimates and shows uniform performance from the Stokes to Darcy regime without any restriction in a mesh size, which is verified by our numerical analysis and experiments. Through two- and three-dimensional examples, we compare the numerical performance of our robust EG and the standard EG methods with the viscous parameter
\nu
and mesh size
h
. The numerical results demonstrate why the standard EG method is not suitable for the Brinkman equations in the Darcy regime and show that the robust EG method has uniform performance in solving the Brinkman equations.

Enriched Galerkin Methods for the Brinkman equations

We first introduce the enriched Galerkin (EG) finite-dimensional velocity and pressure spaces
[
20
]
.
The space of continuous components for velocity is

{\bf C}_{h}=\{{\bf v}^{C}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{d}:{\bf v}^{C}|_{T}\in[P_{1}(T)]^{d},\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

The space of discontinuous components for velocity is defined as

{\bf D}_{h}=\{{\bf v}^{D}\in L^{2}(\Omega):{\bf v}^{D}|_{T}=c({\bf x}-{\bf x}_{T}),\ c\in\mathbb{R},\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

where
{\bf x}_{T}
is the barycenter of
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Thus, the EG finite-dimensional velocity space is defined as

{\bf V}_{h}:={\bf C}_{h}\oplus{\bf D}_{h}.

We note that any function
{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}
consists of unique continuous and discontinuous components,
{\bf v}={\bf v}^{C}+{\bf v}^{D}
for
{\bf v}^{C}\in{\bf C}_{h}
and
{\bf v}^{D}\in{\bf D}_{h}
.
At the same time, the EG pressure space is

Q_{h}:=\{q\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega):q|_{T}\in P_{0}(T),\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

Therefore, we formulate a standard EG method for the Brinkman equations with the pair of the EG spaces
{\bf V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
by adding the Darcy term to the Stokes formulation
[
20
]
.

) method

\displaystyle=({\bf f},{\bf v})

\displaystyle\quad\forall{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h},

\displaystyle{\bf b}({\bf u}_{h},q)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\forall q\in Q_{h},

\displaystyle{\bf a}({\bf v},{\bf w})

\displaystyle:=(\nabla{\bf v},\nabla{\bf w})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\{\nabla{\bf v}\}{\bf n}_{e},[{\bf w}]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}

\displaystyle:=({\bf v},{\bf w})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\rho_{2}\langle h_{e}[{\bf w}],[{\bf v}]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}},

Remark 3.1
.

This algorithm employs interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (IPDG) formulations because any EG velocity function in

has a discontinuity.
IPDG formulations include two penalty terms scaled by

with the penalty parameters
\rho_{1}

\rho_{2}

The

method provides reliable numerical solutions in the Stokes regime.
However, this approach may not be effective in solving the Brinkman equations in the Darcy regime because it requires

-conforming discrete velocity functions. Moreover, the

For this reason, we develop a pressure-robust EG method that produces stable and accurate solutions to Brinkman problems with any value of

First, the velocity reconstruction operator

\mathcal{R}:{\bf V}_{h}\to\mathcal{B}DM_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\subset H(\text{div},\Omega)

\displaystyle\int_{e}(\mathcal{R}{\bf v})\cdot{\bf n}_{e}p_{1}\ ds

\displaystyle=\int_{e}\{{\bf v}\}\cdot{\bf n}_{e}p_{1}\ ds,

\displaystyle\quad\forall p_{1}\in P_{1}(e),\ \forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o},

(3.3a)

\displaystyle\int_{e}(\mathcal{R}{\bf v})\cdot{\bf n}_{e}p_{1}\ ds

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\quad\forall p_{1}\in P_{1}(e),\ \forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b},

(3.3b)

where
\mathcal{B}DM_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
is the Brezzi-Douglas-Marini space of index 1 on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Then, we propose the pressure-robust EG method as follows.

such that

\displaystyle\nu{\bf a}({\bf u}_{h},{\bf v})+\tilde{\mathbf{c}}({\bf u}_{h},{\bf v})-{\bf b}({\bf v},p_{h})

\displaystyle=({\bf f},\mathcal{R}{\bf v})

\displaystyle\quad\forall{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h},

(3.4a)

\displaystyle{\bf b}({\bf u}_{h},q)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\forall q\in Q_{h},

(3.4b)

where
{\bf a}({\bf v},{\bf w})
and
{\bf b}({\bf v},{\bf w})
are defined in (
3.2a
) and (

), respectively, and

\tilde{\mathbf{c}}({\bf v},{\bf w}):=(\mathcal{R}{\bf v},\mathcal{R}{\bf w})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\rho_{2}\langle h_{e}[{\bf w}],[{\bf v}]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}.

(3.5)

Using the velocity reconstruction operator

We replace the velocity functions in the bilinear form

Thus, the term

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq C\left[(\sqrt{\nu}+1)h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}+\left(h+\frac{h}{\sqrt{\nu+c_{1}h^{2}}}\right)\lVert p\rVert_{1}\right].

(4.17)

On the other hand, we observe the intermediate estimate (
4.14
) and omit high-order terms (
h^{2}
or
h^{3}
) to show the pressure error estimate,

\lVert\epsilon_{h}\rVert_{0}\leq C\left[(\sqrt{\nu}+h){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+\nu h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}+h\lVert p\rVert_{1}\right].

Thus, we bound
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
with the velocity error estimate (
4.17
), so we finally obtain

\lVert\epsilon_{h}\rVert_{0}\leq C\left[(\nu+\sqrt{\nu})h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}+(\sqrt{\nu}+1)h\lVert p\rVert_{1}\right],

when omitting
h^{2}
-terms.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

Theorem
4.9
explains that the errors converge in the first order with
h
under the condition
h<\sqrt{\nu}
easily satisfied in the Stokes regime.
However, the velocity error in the Darcy regime may not decrease with
h
due to the pressure term in the velocity error bound, that is, when
\nu\rightarrow 0
,

\frac{h}{\sqrt{\nu+c_{1}h^{2}}}\lVert p\rVert_{1}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{1}}}\lVert p\rVert_{1}.

We will confirm these theoretical results through numerical experiments.
For this reason, the
ST-EG
method in Algorithm
1
may not be effective in solving the Brinkman equations with small
\nu
, which motivates us to develop and analyze the
PR-EG
method in Algorithm
2
.

We first define another energy norm by replacing

with

We also introduce the interpolation error estimate of the operator
\mathcal{R}
in
[
10
]

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}
, there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
\nu
and
h
such that

\lVert{\bf v}-\mathcal{R}{\bf v}\rVert_{0}\leq Ch\lVert h_{e}^{-1/2}[{\bf v}]\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}\leq Ch\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}.

(5.1)

This interpolation error estimate allows to have the norm equivalence between
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf v}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}
and
\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}
scaled by
\nu
and

, similar to Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}
, it holds

\sqrt{\nu}\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\sqrt{\nu+c_{2}h^{2}}\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf v}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}\leq C_{\textsc{ne}}\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}},

(5.2)

where
C_{\textsc{ne}}
is the constant defined in Lemma
4.1
and

is a small constant.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
\lVert\mathcal{R}{\bf v}\rVert_{0}\leq Ch\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}
for the upper bound because
\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{0}
is replaced by
\lVert\mathcal{R}{\bf v}\rVert_{0}
in the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf v}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}
.
Indeed, it follows from the triangle inequality, the error estimate (
5.1
), and the argument in the proof of Lemma
4.1
that

\lVert\mathcal{R}{\bf v}\rVert_{0}\leq\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{0}+\lVert\mathcal{R}{\bf v}-{\bf v}\rVert_{0}\leq\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{0}+Ch\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}\leq Ch\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}.

Hence, we obtain

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf v}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}=\nu\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}+\lVert\mathcal{R}{\bf v}\rVert_{0}^{2}+\rho_{2}\lVert h_{e}^{1/2}[{\bf v}]\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2}\leq C\left(\nu+h^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}+1\right)\right)\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}.

For the lower bound, we recall the result in Lemma
4.1
and apply (
5.1
) to it,

\displaystyle\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-2}\left(\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{0}^{2}+\rho_{2}\lVert h_{e}^{1/2}[{\bf v}]\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-2}\left(\lVert\mathcal{R}{\bf v}\rVert_{0}^{2}+\lVert\mathcal{R}{\bf v}-{\bf v}\rVert_{0}^{2}+\rho_{2}\lVert h_{e}^{1/2}[{\bf v}]\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=Ch^{-2}\left(\lVert\mathcal{R}{\bf v}\rVert_{0}^{2}+\rho_{2}\lVert h_{e}^{1/2}[{\bf v}]\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2}\right)+C_{0}\lVert h_{e}^{-1/2}[{\bf v}]\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2},

where

\nu
and

Therefore, we set

∎

In addition, we prove the norm equivalence between
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf v}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf v}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}
using the results in Lemma

5.11
), we have

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}\leq C\left(h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}\lVert\epsilon_{h}\rVert_{0}+\sqrt{\nu}h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}+h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}\right),

while omitting
h^{2}
-terms.
We also replace
\lVert\epsilon_{h}\rVert_{0}
by its upper bound in (
5.10
) omitting high-order terms,

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\mathcal{R}}\leq C\left(\sqrt{\nu}h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}+h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}\right).

In this case, the Young’s inequality gives

\sqrt{\nu}h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\frac{\nu h^{2}}{2\alpha}\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\mathcal{R}},\quad h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\frac{h^{2}}{2\alpha}\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\mathcal{R}}.

Therefore, it follows from choosing a proper
\alpha
that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\mathcal{R}}\leq Ch^{2}(\nu+1)\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}^{2},

which implies that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf e}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}\leq Ch(\sqrt{\nu}+1)\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}.

If we apply this estimate to (
5.10
), then we obtain

\lVert\epsilon_{h}\rVert_{0}\leq Ch(\nu+\sqrt{\nu})\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}+Ch^{2}\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}.

∎

Remark 5.11
.

We emphasize that the error bounds in Theorem
5.10
are pressure-robust and have no detrimental effect from small
\nu
.
With
\nu\rightarrow 0
, the
PR-EG
method’s velocity errors decrease in the optimal order, and pressure errors do in the second order (superconvergence is expected).
This result implies that the
PR-EG
method produces stable and accurate solutions to the Brinkman equations in the Darcy regime.

In addition, we prove total error estimates showing the optimal orders of convergence in velocity and pressure.

Theorem 5.12
.

Under the same assumption of Theorem
5.10
, we have the following error estimates

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}\leq Ch(\sqrt{\nu}+1)\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2},

\displaystyle\lVert p-p_{h}\rVert_{0}\leq Ch\left((\nu+\sqrt{\nu})\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}+\lVert p\rVert_{1}\right).

Proof.

For the velocity error estimate, we show

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}\leq C\sqrt{\nu}h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}.

More precisely, we recall
\bm{\chi}_{h}={\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}
and observe the energy norm,

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\chi}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\mathcal{R}}=\nu\lVert\bm{\chi}_{h}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}+\lVert\mathcal{R}\bm{\chi}_{h}\rVert_{0}^{2}+\rho_{2}\lVert h_{e}^{1/2}[\bm{\chi}_{h}]\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2}.

Then, it follows from (
5.1
), (
4.5b
), and (
4.5a
) that

\lVert\mathcal{R}\bm{\chi}_{h}\rVert_{0}\leq\lVert\mathcal{R}\bm{\chi}_{h}-\bm{\chi}_{h}\rVert_{0}+\lVert\bm{\chi}_{h}\rVert_{0}\leq Ch\lVert\bm{\chi}_{h}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}+\lVert\bm{\chi}_{h}\rVert_{0}\leq Ch^{2}\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}.

Also, from (
2.1
) and (
4.5a
), we obtain

\lVert h_{e}^{1/2}[\bm{\chi}_{h}]\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}\leq C\left(\lVert\bm{\chi}_{h}\rVert_{0}^{2}+h^{2}\lVert\nabla\bm{\chi}_{h}\rVert_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq Ch^{2}\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}.

Hence, since
\lVert\bm{\chi}_{h}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}\leq Ch\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}
, the error bound is

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\chi}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}}\leq C\left(\sqrt{\nu}h+h^{2}\right)\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}.

Furthermore, the pressure error estimate is readily proved by the triangle inequality and interpolation error estimate (
4.6
).
∎

In conclusion, the proposed
PR-EG
method solves the Brinkman equations in both the Stokes and Darcy regimes, having the optimal order of convergence for both velocity and pressure.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a pressure-robust numerical method for the Brinkman equations with minimal degrees of freedom based on the EG piecewise linear velocity and constant pressure spaces
[
20
]
.
To derive the robust method, we used the velocity reconstruction operator
[
10
]
mapping the EG velocity to the first-order Brezzi-Douglas-Marini space.
Then, we replaced the EG velocity in the Darcy term and the test function on the right-hand side with the reconstructed velocity. With this simple modification, the robust EG method showed uniform performance in both the Stokes and Darcy regimes compared to the standard EG method requiring the
mesh restriction
h<\sqrt{\nu}
that is impractical in the Darcy regime.
We also validated the error estimates and performance of the standard and robust EG methods through several numerical tests with two- and three-dimensional examples.

Our efficient and robust EG method for the Brinkman equations can be extended to various Stokes-Darcy modeling problems, such as coupled models with an interface and time-dependent models. Also,
the proposed EG method can be extended for nonlinear models, such as nonlinear Brinkman models for non-Newtonian fluid and unsteady Brinkman-Forchheimer models.

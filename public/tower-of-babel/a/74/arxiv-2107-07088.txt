Aubry-Mather theory for contact Hamiltonian systems II
Kaizhi Wang and Lin Wang and Jun Yan

Abstract
. In this paper, we continue to develop Aubry-Mather and weak KAM theories for contact Hamiltonian systems H ⁡ ( x , u , p ) H(x,u,p) with certain dependence on the contact variable u u . For the Lipschitz dependence case, we obtain some properties of the Mañé set. For the non-decreasing case, we provide some information on the Aubry set, such as the comparison property, graph property and a partially ordered relation for the collection of all projected Aubry sets with respect to backward weak KAM solutions. Moreover, we find a new flow-invariant set 𝒮 ~ s \tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{s} consists of strongly static orbits, which coincides with the Aubry set 𝒜 ~ \tilde{\mathcal{A}} in classical Hamiltonian systems. Nevertheless, a class of examples are constructed to show 𝒮 ~ s ⫋ 𝒜 ~ \tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{s}\subsetneqq\tilde{\mathcal{A}} in the contact case. As their applications, we find some new phenomena appear even if the strictly increasing dependence of H H on u u fails at only one point, and we show that there is a difference for the vanishing discount problem from the negative direction between the minimal viscosity solution and non-minimal ones.

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Motivation and assumptions

In the early 1990s, based on Tonelli variational principle in Lagrangian systems, Mather
[
33
,
34
]
founded a seminal work on global action-minimizing orbits generated by Hamilton equations.
The method has a profound impact on many fields, such as Hamiltonian dynamics, Hamilton-Jacobi equations and symplectic geometry (see
[
2
,
3
,
22
]
for instance).
In the mid-1990’s, Fathi developed weak KAM theory to study the dynamics of Hamiltonian systems and Hamilton-Jacobi equations. Weak KAM theory provides an explanation for Mather theory from the PDE point of view and thus builds a bridge between Mather theory and viscosity solutions of Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\displaystyle H(x,Du)=c(H),\quad x\in M,

(1.1)

where
c(H)
is the critical value of
H
. Under the assumption that
H
is a Tonelli Hamiltonian, it was shown that backward weak KAM solutions and viscosity solutions of equation (
1.1
) are the same (see
[
14
,
16
,
30
]
for related issues on viscosity solutions). The fundamental reference on weak KAM theory is
[
21
]
.

Correspondingly, one has a natural generalization of
Hamiltonian systems, the so-called contact Hamiltonian systems
[
1
]
. In recent years, several applications of contact Hamiltonian dynamics have been found, ranging from thermodynamics to classical and statistical mechanics. For more details on contact Hamiltonian systems, we refer the reader to
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
17
,
18
,
40
]
for a series of nice introduction and geometrical description of these systems.

Recently, the Aubry-Mather theory for conformally symplectic systems was established by Marò and Sorrentino in
[
32
]
. The conformally symplectic systems are closely related to discounted Hamilton-Jacobi equations. The discounted Hamilton-Jacobi equation is a specific example of contact cases, for which the weak KAM theory was developed by Mitake and Soga in
[
36
]
. Both cases above were proceeded under Tonelli assumption and
\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}\equiv\lambda>0
.

In
[
43
]
,
the authors generalized some fundamental results of Aubry-Mather theory and weak KAM theory from Hamiltonian systems to contact Hamiltonian systems under Tonelli assumption and
0<\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}\leq\lambda
, where the analysis was owing to a new implicit variational principle and a dynamical approach developed in
[
39
,
41
,
42
]
. It is worth mentioning that an alternative variational formulation was provided in
[
8
,
9
,
31
]
in light of G. Herglotz’s work
[
25
]
, which is of explicit form with nonholonomic constraints.
See
[
45
]
for recent work on the implicit variational principle for contact Hamiltonian systems in the time-dependent and non-compact case.

In this paper, we continue to study the global dynamics of the contact Hamiltonian system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}(x,u,p),\\
\dot{p}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(x,u,p)-\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}(x,u,p)p,\qquad(x,u,p)\in T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R},\\
\dot{u}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}(x,u,p)\cdot p-H(x,u,p).\end{array}\right.

under assumptions as follows. Let
H:T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{3}
function satisfying

(H1)

Strict convexity
: the Hessian
\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial p^{2}}(x,u,p)
is positive definite for all
(x,u,p)\in T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}
;

(H2)

Superlinearity
: for every
(x,u)\in M\times\mathbb{R}
,
H(x,u,p)
is superlinear in
p
;

(H3)

Non-decreasing
: there is a constant
\lambda>0
such that for every
(x,u,p)\in T^{\ast}M\times\mathbb{R}
,

0\leq\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}(x,u,p)\leq\lambda,

where
M
is a connected, closed and smooth Riemannian manifold, and
T^{*}M
denotes the cotangent bundle of
M
. In order to deal with global dynamics of (
1.1
), we assume additionally

(A)

Admissibility
: the stationary Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\displaystyle H(x,u,Du)=0,\quad x\in M,

(HJ)

has a viscosity solution.

From the dynamical point of view (
[
43
]
), under the assumptions (H1)-(H3),
H
is admissible if and only if there exists
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\inf_{u\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})}\sup_{x\in M}H(x,a,Du)=0.

We are devoted to extend some fundamental results of Aubry-Mather theory and weak KAM theory from Hamiltonian systems
H(x,p)
to contact Hamiltonian systems
H(x,u,p)
under (H1)-(H3) and (A). Different from
[
43
]
, this generalization is compatible with classical Hamiltonian systems (
[
21
,
33
,
34
]
).
In order to deal with general cases, we have the following new challenges:

•

In classical Hamiltonian systems, due to its conservativity, the forward and backward objects can be transferred from each other. One can obtain certain “information” by considering
either
forward objects
or
backward objects, where “information” can be provided by some tools like Lax-Oleinik semigroup, weak KAM solution, action function and semi-static orbits
etc.
In contact cases, the correspondence between the forward and backward “information” does not hold any more. The assumption (H3) admits both conservative and dissipative cases. Therefore, we need to consider
both
forward
and
backward objects. The difficulties are closely related to the dependence of
H(x,u,p)
on
u
. If
0<\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}\leq\lambda
, the backward Lax-Oleinik semigroup contracts along the evolution in time, while the forward Lax-Oleinik semigroup dissipates. If
0\leq\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}\leq\lambda
, one has the
coexistence
of contraction, conservation and dissipation, which increases certain difficulties to the analysis on the dynamics generated by (
1.1
).

•

In view of the coupling issue of (
1.1
), we have to consider the action minimizing orbits and the flow invariant sets in
T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}
instead of
T^{*}M
. Moreover, it is not natural to expect the compactness of action minimizing sets, since (backward/forward) weak KAM solutions are not unique under the assumption (H3). Due to the non-uniqueness of (backward/forward) weak KAM solutions, the action minimizing sets with respect to the solutions are also necessary to be involved.

In this paper, we prove the existence of Mañé sets
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}
, Aubry sets
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
and Mather sets
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
. Moreover, we provide more information on the Aubry set, such as the comparison property, graph property and
a partially ordered relation for the collection of all projected Aubry sets with respect to backward weak KAM solutions. Compared to the definition of the static curve, we find a new flow-invariant set
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{s}
consists of so-called
strongly
static orbits, which coincides with the Aubry set
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
in classical Hamiltonian systems. Nevertheless, a class of examples are constructed to show

\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{s}\subsetneqq\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
. Besides,
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is not chain-recurrent in these examples.
As applications of these results, we show

•

some new phenomena appear from both dynamical and PDE aspects, even if the strictly increasing dependence on the contact variable
u
fails at only one point;

•

there is a difference for the vanishing discount problem from the negative direction between the
minimal
viscosity solution and
non-minimal
ones.

1.2.
Mañé sets

The authors
[
43
]
introduced the Aubry set and the Mather set for strictly increasing contact Hamiltonian systems. Due to the non-uniqueness of backward weak KAM solutions, the notion of the Mañé set is needed for non-decreasing cases. Following Mañé (
[
13
,
15
,
35
]
), we have

Definition 1.1

(Semi-static curves)
.

•

A curve
(x(\cdot),u(\cdot)):[0,+\infty)\to M\times\mathbb{R}
is called positively semi-static, if it is positively minimizing and for each
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\in[0,+\infty)
, there holds

u(t_{2})=\inf_{s>0}h_{x(t_{1}),u(t_{1})}(x(t_{2}),s).

(1.5)

•

A curve
(x(\cdot),u(\cdot)):(-\infty,0]\to M\times\mathbb{R}
is called negatively semi-static, if it is negatively minimizing and (
1.5
) holds for any
t_{1}
,
t_{2}\in(-\infty,0]
with
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
. A curve
(x(\cdot),u(\cdot)):\mathbb{R}\to M\times\mathbb{R}
is called semi-static, if it is both positively and negatively semi-static.

In the case with
0<\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}\leq\lambda
, static curves are the same as semi-static ones (see Remark
C.8
below). Let
p(t):=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}}(x(t),u(t),\dot{x}(t))
. Then
(x(t),u(t),p(t))
is a solution of equations (
1.1
). We denote

•

\Phi_{t}:T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}
, the local flow generated by (
1.1
);

•

\pi:T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M
, the standard projection once and for all.

Moreover, one can define
(positively, negatively) semi-static orbit of
\Phi_{t}
from semi-static curves by adding the
p(t)
-component.

Definition 1.2

(Mañé set)
.

We call the set of all semi-static orbits the Mañé set of
H
, denoted by
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}
.
We call
\mathcal{N}:=\pi\tilde{\mathcal{N}}
the projected Mañé set.
We define
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{+}
(resp.
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{-}
) as the set of all positively (resp. negatively) semi-static orbits.

By the definition of Mañé set,
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}
is an invariant subset of
T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}
by
\Phi_{t}
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{-}
and
\mathcal{S}_{+}
be the sets of all backward weak KAM solutions and forward weak KAM solutions of
H(x,u,Du)=0
respectively. Let
v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}
,
v_{+}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}
. Define

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v_{-}}:=\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\cap G_{v_{-}},\quad\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v_{+}}:=\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\cap G_{v_{+}},\quad\mathcal{N}_{v_{-}}:=\pi\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v_{-}},\quad\mathcal{N}_{v_{+}}:=\pi\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v_{+}},

where
G_{v_{\pm}}
denote the Legendrian pseudographs of
v_{\pm}
, namely

G_{v_{\pm}}:=\overline{\big\{(x,u,p):Dv_{\pm}(x)\ \text{exists},\ u=v_{\pm}(x),\ p=Dv_{\pm}(x)\big\}}.

(1.6)

By
[
43
, Theorem 1.1]
,
the contact vector field generates a semi-flow
\Phi_{t}

(t\leq 0)
on
G_{v_{-}}
and a semi-flow
\Phi_{t}

(t\geq 0)
on
G_{v_{+}}
. Define

\tilde{\Sigma}_{v_{-}}:=\bigcap_{t\geq 0}\Phi_{-t}(G_{v_{-}}),\quad\tilde{\Sigma}_{v_{+}}:=\bigcap_{t\geq 0}\Phi_{t}(G_{v_{+}}),\quad\Sigma_{v_{+}}:=\pi\tilde{\Sigma}_{v_{+}},\quad\Sigma_{v_{-}}:=\pi\tilde{\Sigma}_{v_{-}}.

(1.7)

Given
v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}
, let
v_{+}(x):=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}T_{t}^{+}v_{-}(x)
. Then
v_{+}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}
. Similarly, given
v_{+}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}
, let
v_{-}(x):=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}T_{t}^{-}v_{+}(x)
. Then
v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}
.
Let

\mathcal{I}_{v_{-}}:=\{x\in M\ |\ v_{-}(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}T_{t}^{+}v_{-}(x)\},\quad\mathcal{I}_{v_{+}}:=\{x\in M\ |\ v_{+}(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}T_{t}^{-}v_{+}(x)\},

for each
v_{\pm}\in\mathcal{S}_{\pm}
. It follows
[
43

that both
v_{\pm}
are of class
C^{1,1}
on
\mathcal{I}_{v_{\pm}}
. Define

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{v_{\pm}}:=\{(x,u,p):\ x\in\mathcal{I}_{v_{\pm}},\ u=v_{\pm}(x),\ p=Dv_{\pm}(x)\}.

(1.8)

Given
v_{\pm}\in\mathcal{S}_{\pm}
, if
v_{+}(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}T_{t}^{+}v_{-}(x)
and
v_{-}(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}T_{t}^{-}v_{+}(x)
, then
(v_{-},v_{+})
is called a
conjugate pair
.
v_{-}
(resp.
v_{+}

principal
backward (resp. forward) weak KAM solution.

Theorem 1.3
.

There hold

(1)

Covering property
:

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{-}=\cup_{v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}}G_{v_{-}},\quad\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{+}=\cup_{v_{+}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}}G_{v_{+}}.

Moreover,

(2)

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v_{-}}=\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{v_{-}}=\tilde{\Sigma}_{v_{-}},\quad\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v_{+}}=\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{v_{+}}=\tilde{\Sigma}_{v_{+}},

where
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{v_{\pm}}

) and (

) below. Moreover, if

Remark 1.4
.

Theorem
1.3
still holds if the assumption
(H3)
is replaced by
|\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}|\leq\lambda

|\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}|\leq\lambda

To prove Theorem

1.3.
Aubry sets and Mather sets

By definition, for each
v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v_{-}}
is an invariant subset of
T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}
by
\Phi_{t}
.
By Theorem
1.3
,
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v_{-}}
is non-empty and compact. Consequently,
there exist Borel
\Phi_{t}
-invariant probability measures supported in
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v_{-}}
, called
Mather measures
. Denote by
\mathfrak{M}
the set of Mather measures for all
v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}
.
Mather set
of contact Hamiltonian systems (
1.1
) is defined by

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}=\mathrm{cl}\left(\bigcup_{\mu\in\mathfrak{M}}\text{supp}(\mu)\right).

By the definitions of Aubry set and Mather set, both of them are invariant subsets of
T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}
by
\Phi_{t}
. Define

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{v_{-}}:=\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\cap G_{v_{-}},\quad\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{v_{+}}:=\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\cap G_{v_{+}},\quad\mathcal{A}_{v_{-}}:=\pi\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{v_{-}},\quad\mathcal{A}_{v_{+}}:=\pi\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{v_{+}},

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{v_{-}}:=\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\cap G_{v_{-}},\quad\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{v_{+}}:=\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\cap G_{v_{+}},\quad\mathcal{M}_{v_{-}}:=\pi\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{v_{-}},\quad\mathcal{M}_{v_{+}}:=\pi\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{v_{+}}.

By definitions,
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{\pm}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{\pm}
. Moreover, we obtain the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

(1)

Characterizations
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\cup_{v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{v_{-}}=\cup_{v_{+}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{v_{+}},\quad\tilde{\mathcal{M}}=\cup_{v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}}\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{v_{-}}=\cup_{v_{+}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}}\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{v_{+}}.

(2)

Asymptotic behavior
:
For each
v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}
and each

(x_{1},v_{1},p_{1})\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v_{-}}^{-},\quad(x_{2},v_{2},p_{2})\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v_{-}}^{+},

the
\alpha
-limit set of
(x_{1},v_{1},p_{1})
and
\omega
-limit set of
(x_{2},v_{2},p_{2})
are contained in
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{v_{-}}
. Moreover, for each
(x_{1},v_{1},p_{1})\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{-}
,
(x_{2},v_{2},p_{2})\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{+}
, the
\alpha
-limit set of
(x_{1},v_{1},p_{1})
and
\omega
-limit set of
(x_{2},v_{2},p_{2})
are contained in
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
.

(3)

Inclusion relation
: Aubry set
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
and Mather set
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
are closed. Moreover,

\emptyset\neq\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{N}}.

1.4.
More on Aubry sets

In this part, we provide more information on Aubry sets. The first one is concerned with the comparison property of
u_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}
.

Theorem 1.6

(
Comparison property).

Given
u_{-},v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}
, if
u_{-}\leq v_{-}
on
\mathcal{A}_{v_{-}}
, then
u_{-}\leq v_{-}
on
M
. Moreover, if
u_{-}=v_{-}
on
\mathcal{A}_{u_{-}}\cup\mathcal{A}_{v_{-}}
, then
u_{-}=v_{-}
on
M
.

In fact, one has a corresponding result in terms of the Mather set (see Remark
4.1
below). By a completely different approach, Jing, Mitake and Tran
[
29
]
introduced adjoint measures and they proved that the closure of the union of all supports of those measures plays a role as the uniqueness set of the HJ equations. It is also remarked that those measures turn out to be projected Mather measures if
H
satisfies appropriate conditions.

Let
\bar{\mathcal{A}}
be the dual Aubry set of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
in
TM\times\mathbb{R}
by the Legendre transformation
p=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}}(x,u,\dot{x})
. Let

\rho:TM\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow TM,\quad\rho^{*}:T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow T^{*}M,\quad\Pi:TM\rightarrow M,\quad\Pi^{*}:T^{*}M\rightarrow M

be the standard projections. The second one is about the graph property of
\Pi
restricting on
\rho(\bar{\mathcal{A}})
.

Theorem 1.7

(
Graph property).

\Pi:\rho(\bar{\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow M
is injective.

In order to prove it, we need to show that for each static orbit
(x(\cdot),u(\cdot),\dot{x}(\cdot)):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow TM\times\mathbb{R}
,
\dot{x}(t)
is uniquely determined by
x(t)
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In view of the complicated structure of
\mathcal{S}_{-}
under the assumption (H3), the injectivity of
\Pi
is subtle to be confirmed. In addition, due to the appearance of
u
-component in the Legendre transformation
p=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}}(x,u,\dot{x})
, one can not expect the injectivity of the standard projection
\Pi^{*}
from
\rho^{*}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
to
M
, except for the separated case with
H(x,u,p):=f(x,u)+h(x,p)
.

The third one is about the structure of the projected Aubry set
\mathcal{A}
. Note that
\mathcal{A}=\cup_{u_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}}\mathcal{A}_{u_{-}}
. We obtain a partially ordered relation for the collection
\{\mathcal{A}_{u_{-}}\}_{u_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}}
.

Theorem 1.8

(
Partially ordered relation).

Given
u_{-},v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}
, if
u_{-}\leq v_{-}
on
\mathcal{A}_{u_{-}}
, then
{\mathcal{A}}_{u_{-}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{v_{-}}
.

Comparably,

(1)

for classical Hamiltonians
H(x,p)
, the Aubry set
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is independent of viscosity solutions, in this case, there always hold
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}_{u_{-}}={\mathcal{A}}_{v_{-}}
for any
u_{-},v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}
;

(2)

for the case with discounted Hamiltonians
\lambda u+H(x,p)
,
\lambda>0
or more generally, strictly increasing Hamiltonians
0<\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}\leq\lambda
, we have
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}_{u_{-}}
;

In general cases, a new phenomenon with
{\mathcal{A}}_{u_{-}}\subsetneqq{\mathcal{A}}_{v_{-}}
for certain
u_{-},v_{-}\in\mathcal{S}_{-}
could emerge (see Proposition
1.13
below).

1.5.
Strongly static set

Compared to the definition of the static curve, it is natural to consider another kind of action minimizing curves.

Definition 1.9

(Strongly static curves)
.

A curve
(x(\cdot),u(\cdot)):\mathbb{R}\to M\times\mathbb{R}
is called strongly static, if it is globally minimizing and for each
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, there holds

u(t_{2})=\sup_{s>0}h^{x(t_{1}),u(t_{1})}(x(t_{2}),s).

(1.9)

In the same way as Definition
1.2
, one can define
strongly static orbits

(x(\cdot),p(\cdot),u(\cdot)):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, one has a new flow-invariant set defined as follow.

Definition 1.10

(Strongly static set)
.

We call the closure of the set of all strongly static orbits the strongly static set of
H
, denoted by
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{s}
. We call
\mathcal{S}_{s}:=\pi\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{s}
the projected strongly static set.

Theorem 1.11

(
Strong staticity and staticity).

There holds

\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{s}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{A}}.

In particular,
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{s}=\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
if
H
is independent of
u
.

Generally,
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{s}
may be a proper subset of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
. For example, we have

Proposition 1.12

(
Difference between
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{s}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
).

Let

H(x,u,p):=\lambda u+\frac{1}{2}|p|^{2}+p\cdot V(x),\quad(x,u,p)\in T^{*}\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R},

(E1)

where
\mathbb{T}
denotes a flat circle and
V:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{3}
function which has exactly two vanishing points
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
with
V^{\prime}(x_{1})>0
,
V^{\prime}(x_{2})<0
.
Then

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\left\{(x,0,0)\ |\ x\in\mathbb{T}\right\},\quad\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{s}=\left\{(x_{1},0,0),\ (x_{2},0,0)\right\}.

From the conditions on
V
, it is clear that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is not chain-recurrent. Nevertheless,
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{s}
is non-wandering.

1.6.
Applications

First of all, we give an example to show certain new phenomena appearing in contact Hamiltonian systems with non-decreasing dependence on
u
.

H(x,u,p):=\frac{1}{2}|p|^{2}+f(x)u,\ \quad(x,u,p)\in T^{*}\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R},

(E2)

where
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
denotes a flat circle,
f:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function with
f(0)=0
and
f(x)>0
for all
x\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\backslash\{0\}
, where
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
is a fundamental domain of
\mathbb{T}

For a classical Hamiltonian

2.4.

By Theorem
1.3
(1) and
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{\pm}

For each
t_{1}
,
t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
with
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
, take
K>0
such that
[t_{1},t_{2}]\in[-K,K]

Note that the sequence of
(x_{n}(t),u_{n}(t),p_{n}(t))
converges uniformly to

as

on

. Thus, we get
(x_{n}(t_{i}),u_{n}(t_{i}),p_{n}(t_{i}))\to(x_{0}(t_{i}),u_{0}(t_{i}),p_{0}(t_{i}))

for

Fix

and by the Lipschitz continuity of

w.r.t.

, for each

there is

such that

is semi-static, then we have

is uniformly Lipschitz on

with some Lipschitz constant
\kappa
, Namely

\left|h_{x_{0},u_{0}}(x,t)-h_{x_{0},u_{0}}(y,t)\right|\leq\kappa\ d(x,y),\quad\forall t>2\delta,

which means

\left|\inf_{s>0}h_{x_{0},u_{0}}(x,s)-\inf_{s>0}h_{x_{0},u_{0}}(y,s)\right|\leq\sup_{s>0}|h_{x_{0},u_{0}}(x,s)-h_{x_{0},u_{0}}(y,s)|\leq\kappa\ d(x,y).

Finally, we prove that
x_{0}\mapsto\inf_{s>0}h_{x_{0},u_{0}}(x,s)
is Lipschitz continuous. Given
x_{1},x_{2}\in M
,
let
\Delta t:=d(x_{1},x_{2})
. By the proof of Proposition
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle h_{x_{1},u_{0}}(x,t+\Delta t)\leq h_{x_{2},u_{0}}(x,t)+C_{2}\Delta t.

It follows that

\inf_{s>0}h_{x_{1},u_{0}}(x,s)\leq\inf_{s>0}h_{x_{2},u_{0}}(x,s)+C_{2}d(x_{1},x_{2}).

By exchanging the roles of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
, we know that
x_{0}\mapsto\inf_{s>0}h_{x_{0},u_{0}}(x,s)
is also Lipschitz continuous.
∎

Let
(x(\cdot),u(\cdot)):\mathbb{R}\to M\times\mathbb{R}
be a static curve. By Proposition
3.1
,

h_{x(s),u(s)}(x(t),+\infty):=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow+\infty}h_{x(s),u(s)}(x(t),\tau)

is well defined for each
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
. By
[
43
, Proposition 3.2]
, there holds

Proposition 3.4
.

If
(x(\cdot),u(\cdot)):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M\times\mathbb{R}
is static, then

u(t)=h_{x(s),u(s)}(x(t),+\infty),\quad\forall s,\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

3.1.
Asymptotic behavior

We only need to prove that for each
(x_{0},v_{0},p_{0})\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{+}
, the
\omega
-limit set of
(x_{0},v_{0},p_{0})
is contained in
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
. Based on the invariance (resp. forward invariance) of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{v_{-}}
(resp.
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{v_{-}}^{+}

,
\omega
-limit set of
(x_{0},v_{0},p_{0})
is contained in
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{v_{-}}
.

Let
z:=(x_{0},v_{0},p_{0})\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{+}
. Denote
(x(t),u(t),p(t))=\Phi_{t}(z)
for each

. Then
(x(\cdot),u(\cdot)):[0,+\infty)\rightarrow M\times\mathbb{R}
is positively semi-static. Let
z_{\omega}
be an
\omega
-limit point of the orbit passing through

. Denote
(x_{\omega}(t),u_{\omega}(t),p_{\omega}(t))=\Phi_{t}(z_{\omega})
. By Theorem
1.3
,
(x_{\omega}(t),u_{\omega}(t))
is semi-static, namely for each
t_{1}\geq t_{2}
,

u_{\omega}(t_{1})=\inf_{s>0}h_{x_{\omega}(t_{2}),u_{\omega}(t_{2})}(x_{\omega}(t_{1}),s).

(3.2)

It remains to show that for each
t_{1}<t_{2}
, (
3.2
) still holds. One can find

and
(x(t_{n}),u(t_{n}))

Since

Take a limiting passage as

This completes the proof of Theorem

3.2.
Closedness of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}

Let
\{(x_{n},u_{n},p_{n})\}_{n}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
with

as

. It is sufficient to show that

Note that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{N}}
. It follows from Proposition
C.5
and the continuity and differentiability of solutions of ordinary differential equations with respect to initial values,

. Thus, we only need to prove

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(x_{n}(t),u_{n}(t),p_{n}(t))
denote the solution of equations (
1.1
) with initial value
(x_{n},u_{n},p_{n})
. For each
t_{1}
,
t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, take
K>0
such that
t_{1},t_{2}\in[-K,K]
. By a similar argument as the global characterizations of Mañé sets,

\inf_{s>0}h_{x_{0}(t_{1}),u_{0}(t_{1})}(x_{0}(t_{2}),s)\geq u_{0}(t_{2}).

On the other hand, by Proposition
3.4

.

Since

3.3.
Inclusion relations

The
Mather set is defined by

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}=\mathrm{cl}\left(\bigcup_{\mu\in\mathfrak{M}}\text{supp}(\mu)\right),

where
\mathfrak{M}
denotes the set of Mather measures.
By definition,
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{N}}
. It remains to show

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{A}}.

By definition,
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is closed. Note that Mather measures are invariant Borel probabilities measures. Given
\mu\in\mathfrak{M}
. By the Poincaré recurrence theorem, one can find a set
A\subseteq T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}
of total
\mu
-measure such that if
(x_{0},u_{0},p_{0})\in A
, then there exist
\{t_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

d\left((x_{0},u_{0},p_{0}),\Phi_{t_{m}}(x_{0},u_{0},p_{0})\right)\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\ \ t_{m}\rightarrow+\infty,

where
d(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the distance induced by the Riemannian metric on

.
Let

(x(t),u(t),p(t)):=\Phi_{t}(x_{0},u_{0},p_{0}),\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

Since
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is closed and
A
is dense in supp
(\mu)
, then we only need to show that
(x(\cdot),u(\cdot)):\mathbb{R}\to M\times\mathbb{R}
is a static curve.
By the definition of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
,
(x(\cdot),u(\cdot)):\mathbb{R}\to M\times\mathbb{R}
is semi-static. Let
p(t):=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}}(x(t),u(t),\dot{x}(t))
. By assumption,

d\left((x(t_{2}),u(t_{2}),p(t_{2})),\Phi_{t_{m}}(x(t_{2}),u(t_{2}),p(t_{2}))\right)\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\ \ t_{m}\rightarrow+\infty.

We only need to prove

u(t_{2})=\inf_{s>0}h_{x(t_{1}),u(t_{1})}(x(t_{2}),s).

(3.3)

Without loss of generality, we assume
t_{1}\geq t_{2}
. Since
(x(\cdot),u(\cdot)):\mathbb{R}\to M\times\mathbb{R}
is semi-static, we have

u(t_{1})=\inf_{s>0}h_{x(t_{2}),u(t_{2})}(x(t_{1}),s).

(3.4)

Let
\Delta:=t_{1}-t_{2}
. By (
3.4
), if
t_{m}>\Delta
, we have

u(t_{2}+t_{m})=\inf_{s>0}h_{x(t_{1}),u(t_{1})}(x(t_{2}+t_{m}),s).

Let

. It follows from Proposition
3.3
that

u(t_{2})=\inf_{s>0}h_{x(t_{1}),u(t_{1})}(x(t_{2}),s),

which together with (

) implies

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
(3).

4.1.
Comparison property

By contradiction, we assume that there exists
x_{0}\in M
such that
v_{-}(x_{0})<u_{-}(x_{0})
. Let
\gamma:(-\infty,0]\rightarrow M
be a
(v_{-},L,0)
-calibrated curve with
\gamma(0)=x_{0}
. Let

F(s):=u_{-}(\gamma(s))-v_{-}(\gamma(s)).

By assumption, we have
F(0)>0
. Note that
F(s)
is continuous, we have a dichotomy as follows.

(1)

There exists
s_{0}<0
such that
F(s_{0})=0
and
F(s)>0
for any
s\in(s_{0},0]
;

(2)

F(s)>0
for all
s\in(-\infty,0]
.

For Item (1), we have

v_{-}(\gamma(s))-v_{-}(\gamma(s_{0}))=\int_{s_{0}}^{s}L(\gamma(\tau),v_{-}(\gamma(\tau)),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))d\tau,

u_{-}(\gamma(s))-u_{-}(\gamma(s_{0}))\leq\int_{s_{0}}^{s}L(\gamma(\tau),u_{-}(\gamma(\tau)),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))d\tau.

It follows that

F(s)\leq\lambda\int_{s_{0}}^{s}F(\tau)d\tau,

which implies
F(s)=0
for any
s\in[s_{0},0]
. This contradicts
F(0)>0
. It means
u_{-}\leq v_{-}
on
M
.

It remains to prove this theorem for Item (2). Let
u_{0}:=v_{-}(x_{0})
,
p_{0}:=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}}(x_{0},u_{0},\dot{\gamma}(0)_{-})
, where
\dot{\gamma}(0)_{-}
denotes the left derivative of
\gamma(t)
at
t=0
. According to Proposition
C.2
, let
x(t):=\gamma(t)
,
u(t):=v_{-}(\gamma(t)))
, then
(x(\cdot),u(\cdot)):(-\infty,0]\rightarrow M\times\mathbb{R}
is negatively semi-static. Let
\alpha(x_{0},u_{0},p_{0})
be the
\alpha
-limit set of
(x_{0},u_{0},p_{0})
. By Theorem
1.5
(2), we have

\alpha(x_{0},u_{0},p_{0})\subset\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{v_{-}}.

By definition, one can find a subsequence
\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\gamma(s_{n})\rightarrow\bar{x}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{v_{-}}
as
s_{n}\rightarrow-\infty
. Since
u_{-}\leq v_{-}
on
\mathcal{A}_{v_{-}}
, then
u_{-}(\bar{x})\leq v_{-}(\bar{x})
, which yields
\lim_{s_{n}\rightarrow-\infty}F(s_{n})\leq 0
.
Note that

v_{-}(x_{0})-v_{-}(\gamma(s))=\int_{s}^{0}L(\gamma(\tau),v_{-}(\gamma(\tau)),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))d\tau,

u_{-}(x_{0})-u_{-}(\gamma(s))\leq\int_{s}^{0}L(\gamma(\tau),u_{-}(\gamma(\tau)),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))d\tau.

By (H3), there holds for each
s\in(-\infty,0]
,

u_{-}(x_{0})-v_{-}(x_{0})\leq u_{-}(\gamma(s))-v_{-}(\gamma(s)).

(4.1)

In particular, (
4.1
) still holds for
\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Let
s_{n}\rightarrow-\infty
, we have
u_{-}(x_{0})-v_{-}(x_{0})\leq 0
, which contradicts the assumption
v_{-}(x_{0})<u_{-}(x_{0})
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Remark 4.1
.

By the definition of the Mather set, the
\alpha
-limit set of
(x_{0},u_{0},p_{0})
carries Mather measures. Consequently, there exists a sequence

such that
\gamma(t_{n})\to\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{M}_{v_{-}}
, where

\mathcal{M}_{v_{-}}=\pi\big(\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\cap G_{v_{-}}\big).

. Following from the same argument as the proof of Comparison property, if

on

, then
u_{-}\leq v_{-}
on

. Moreover, if

on

, then

on

4.2.
Static curves and positively semi-static curves

As a preparation to prove the injectivity of
\Pi:\rho(\bar{\mathcal{A}})\to M
, we show that for certain minimizing orbits
(x(\cdot),p(\cdot),u(\cdot)):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}
,
p(t)
is uniquely determined by
(x(t),u(t))
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
(x,u,p_{1})\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
,
(x,u,p_{2})\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{+}
, then
p_{1}=p_{2}
.

Proof.

For each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let

(x_{1}(t),u_{1}(t),p_{1}(t)):=\Phi_{t}(x,u,p_{1}).

For each
t\geq 0
, let

(x_{2}(t),u_{2}(t),p_{2}(t)):=\Phi_{t}(x,u,p_{2}).

Since
(x,u,p_{2})\in\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{+}
, then
(x_{2}(t),u_{2}(t))
is positively semi-static.
Fix
\delta>0
, by the Markov property, we have

h_{x_{1}(-\delta),u_{1}(-\delta)}(x_{2}(\delta),2\delta)=\inf_{y\in M}h_{y,h_{x_{1}(-\delta),u_{1}(-\delta)}(y,\delta)}(x_{2}(\delta),\delta).

Note that

h_{x_{1}(-\delta),u_{1}(-\delta)}(x,\delta)=u.

It follows that

h_{x_{1}(-\delta),u_{1}(-\delta)}(x_{2}(\delta),2\delta)\leq h_{x,u}(x_{2}(\delta),\delta).

We assert that the equality holds. If the assertion is true, then by Proposition
C.1
, the curve defined by

\gamma(\sigma):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x_{1}(\sigma-\delta),\quad\sigma\in[0,\delta],\\
x_{2}(\sigma-\delta),\,\quad\sigma\in[\delta,2\delta],\end{array}\right.

is a minimizer of
h_{x_{1}(-\delta),u_{1}(-\delta)}(x_{2}(\delta),2\delta)
and thus is of class
C^{1}
. Thus,

p_{1}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}}(x,u,\dot{\gamma}(0))=p_{2}.

It remains to verify the assertion. By contradiction, we assume that there exists
\Delta>0
such that

h_{x_{1}(-\delta),u_{1}(-\delta)}(x_{2}(\delta),2\delta)=h_{x,u}(x_{2}(\delta),\delta)-\Delta.

By assumption, for each
\varepsilon>0
, one can find
s_{0}>0
such that

|h_{x,u}(x_{1}(-\delta),s_{0})-u_{1}(-\delta)|\leq\varepsilon.

From Proposition
A.3
,

|h_{x_{1}(-\delta),h_{x,u}(x_{1}(-\delta),s_{0})}(x_{2}(\delta),2\delta)-h_{x_{1}(-\delta),u_{1}(-\delta)}(x_{2}(\delta),2\delta)|\leq\varepsilon.

It follows that

\displaystyle u_{2}(\delta)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tau>0}h_{x,u}(x_{2}(\delta),\tau)

\displaystyle\leq h_{x,u}(x_{2}(\delta),s_{0}+2\delta)

\displaystyle\leq h_{x_{1}(-\delta),h_{x,u}(x_{1}(-\delta),s_{0})}(x_{2}(\delta),2\delta)

\displaystyle\leq h_{x_{1}(-\delta),u_{1}(-\delta)}(x_{2}(\delta),2\delta)+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=h_{x,u}(x_{2}(\delta),\delta)-\Delta+\varepsilon.

Note that
\Delta
is independent of
\varepsilon
, we have

u_{2}(\delta)\leq h_{x,u}(x_{2}(\delta),\delta)-\frac{\Delta}{2}=u_{2}(\delta)-\frac{\Delta}{2},

which is a contradiction.
∎

4.3.

Lemma

implies that

.

Lemma

4.4.
Partially ordered relation

Given
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{A}_{u_{-}}

which gives rise to

h_{x(t_{0}),v(t_{0})}(x(t_{0}),s)-h_{x(t_{0}),u(t_{0})}(x(t_{0}),s)\leq v(t_{0})-u(t_{0}).

(4.5)

Since
h_{x(t_{0}),u(t_{0})}(x(t_{0}),+\infty)=u(t_{0})

h_{x(t_{0}),v(t_{0})}(x(t_{0}),+\infty)\leq v(t_{0})

v(t_{0})=v_{-}(x(t_{0}))=T_{t}^{-}v_{-}(x(t_{0}))\leq h_{x(t_{0}),v(t_{0})}(x(t_{0}),t),

(4.6)

which gives rise to
h_{x(t_{0}),v(t_{0})}(x(t_{0}),+\infty)=v(t_{0})

(6.2)

where the infimum is taken among the Lipschitz continuous curves
\xi:[0,\delta]\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
with
\xi(0)=\gamma(0)=z_{0}
and
\xi(\delta)=\gamma(\delta)
. We assume

without loss of generality, since the argument is similar for

. Similar to
[

,
\gamma(t)\rightarrow x_{2}
and

. It gives rise to

(6.3)

Note that
u(0)=v^{+}_{\lambda}(z_{0})<0
, then we have a contradiction. Therefore, we complete the proof of the assertion
x_{2}\notin\mathcal{I}_{v^{+}_{\lambda}}
, which means
\mathcal{I}_{v^{+}_{\lambda}}
has only one possibility, namely,
\mathcal{I}_{v^{+}_{\lambda}}=\{x_{1}\}
.

. Similar to

is the unique forward weak KAM solution except

\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}

(6.4)

Generalities

The contact Lagrangian
L(x,u,v)
associated to
H(x,u,p)
is defined by

L(x,u,v):=\sup_{p\in T^{*}_{x}M}\left\{\langle v,p\rangle-H(x,u,p)\right\}.

Since

satisfies (H1), (H2) and (H3), we have:

(L1)

Strict convexity
: the Hessian
\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial{v}^{2}}(x,u,v)
is positive definite for all
(x,u,v)\in TM\times\mathbb{R}

(L2)

Superlinearity
: for every
(x,u)\in M\times\mathbb{R}
,
L(x,u,v)
is superlinear in

;

(L3)

Non-increasing
: there is a constant
\lambda>0
such that for every
(x,u,v)\in TM\times\mathbb{R}

Remark C.7
.

In classical Hamiltonian systems, the Mañé potential (see
[
15
]
for instance) is defined by

\Phi(x_{0},x):=\inf_{t>0}h^{t}(x_{0},x)=\inf_{t>0}\inf_{\gamma}A_{L}(\gamma)=\inf_{t>0}\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}L(\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))d\tau,

where the infimums are taken among the absolutely continuous curves
\gamma:[0,t]\rightarrow M
with
\gamma(0)=x_{0}
and
\gamma(t)=x
.
h^{t}(x_{0},x):=\inf_{\gamma}A_{L}(\gamma)
is called Mather’s action function.
Moreover, an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M
is called semi-static if for each

A_{L}(\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]})=\Phi(\gamma(t_{1}),\gamma(t_{2})).

(C.4)

An absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M
is called static if it is semi-static and for each
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
,

\Phi(\gamma(t_{1}),\gamma(t_{2}))+\Phi(\gamma(t_{1}),\gamma(t_{2}))=0.

(C.5)

In our considerations, if

is independent of
u
, then

h_{x_{0},u_{0}}(x,t)=u_{0}+h^{t}(x_{0},x),\quad h^{x_{0},u_{0}}(x,t)=u_{0}-h^{t}(x,x_{0}).

By Definition
1.1
, we have

u(t_{2})=u(t_{1})+\Phi(x(t_{1}),x(t_{2})),

which together with
\dot{u}(t)=L(x(t),u(t),\dot{x}(t))
implies
(
C.4
)
holds. By Definition

, we have

u(t_{2})=u(t_{1})+\Phi(x(t_{1}),x(t_{2})),\quad u(t_{1})=u(t_{2})+\Phi(x(t_{2}),x(t_{1})),

which implies
(
C.5
)
holds.

Therefore, the Aubry-Mather theory developed in present paper is compatible with the classical case.

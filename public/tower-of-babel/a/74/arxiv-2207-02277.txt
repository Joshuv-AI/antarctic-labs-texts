Abstract
We provide a unified framework to study hierarchies of relaxations for Constraint Satisfaction Problems and their Promise variant. The idea is to split the description of a hierarchy into an algebraic part, depending on a minion capturing the “base level”, and a geometric part – which we call tensorisation – inspired by multilinear algebra. We exploit the geometry of the tensor spaces arising from our construction to prove general properties of hierarchies. We identify certain classes of minions, which we call linear and conic , whose corresponding hierarchies have particularly fine features. We establish that the (combinatorial) bounded width, Sherali-Adams LP, affine IP, Sum-of-Squares SDP, and combined “LP + affine IP” hierarchies are all captured by this framework. In particular, in order to analyse the Sum-of-Squares SDP hierarchy, we also characterise the solvability of the standard SDP relaxation through a new minion.

Like all decision problems,
\PCSP
s can be solved by designing
tests
. If
a test, applied to a given instance of the problem, is positive then the answer
is
Yes
; if it is negative then the answer is
No
.
The challenge is then to find tests that are able to guarantee a low number – ideally, zero – of false positives and false negatives.
Clearly, a test is itself a decision problem. However, its nature may be substantially different, and less complicated, than the nature of the original problem.

Given a
\PCSP
template
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
, we may use any (potentially infinite)
structure
\mathbf{T}
to make a test for
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
: We simply let the outcome of
the test on an instance structure
\mathbf{X}
be
Yes
if
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{T}
, and
No
if

\mathbf{X}\not\to\mathbf{T}
. In other words,
\CSP(\mathbf{T})
is a test for
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
. Let
\mathbf{X}

be an instance of
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
. If
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{T}
whenever
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{A}
, the test is
guaranteed not to generate false negatives, and we call it
complete
. Since homomorphisms compose, if
\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{T}
the test is automatically complete.
If
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{B}
whenever
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{T}
, the test is guaranteed not to generate false
positives, and we call it
sound
. If both of these conditions hold,
we say that the test
solves

\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
. Notice that, in this case, one obtains a reduction from
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
to
\CSP(\mathbf{T})
.
These two types of computational problems – the original problem and the test – have different algebraic natures.
The complexity of both
\CSP
s and
\PCSP

higher-order symmetries of the solution sets of the problems, known as

polymorphisms

\Pol(\mathbf{A})

\CSP(\mathbf{A})

[
35

and by
\Pol(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
for

\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})

[
12
]
. For
\CSP
s, polymorphisms form
clones

particular, they are closed under composition. This means that some symmetries
may be obtainable through compositions of other symmetries, so that one can hope
to capture properties of entire families of
\CSP
s (e.g., bounded width,
tractability, etc.) through the presence of a certain polymorphism and, more
generally, to describe their complexity through universal-algebraic tools. A
chief example of this approach is the positive resolution of the
dichotomy conjecture for
\CSP
s by Bulatov
[
37
]
and

[

For
\PCSP

algebraic structure they are endowed with – known as

Contributions

The main contribution of this work is the introduction of
a general
framework for refining algorithmic relaxations of
\parPCSPs
. Given a minion

{\mathscr{M}}
, we present a technique to systematically turn
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}

into the corresponding
hierarchy of minion tests
: a sequence of
increasingly tighter relaxations
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

The technique we adopt to build hierarchies of minion tests is inspired by multilinear algebra. We describe a
tensorisation
construction that turns a given structure
\mathbf{X}
into a structure
\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}
on a different signature, where both the domain and the relations are multidimensional objects living in tensor spaces.
Essentially,
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{k}
works by applying
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}
to
tensorised
versions of the structures
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{A}
rather than to
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{A}
themselves.
This allows us to study the functioning of the algorithms in the hierarchy by describing the geometry of a space of tensors – which can be accomplished by using multilinear algebra.
As far as we know, this approach has not appeared in the literature on
Sherali-Adams, bounded width, Sum-of-Squares, hierarchies of integer
programming, and related algorithmic techniques such as the high-dimensional
Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm
[
6
,
39
]
.
2
2

2

Butti and
Dalmau
[
39
]
recently characterised for
\CSP
s when the
k
-th
level of the Sherali-Adams LP programming hierarchy accepts in terms of a
construction different from the one introduced in this work. Unlike the
tensorisation, the construction considered in
[
39
]
yields a
relational structure whose domain includes the set of constraints of the
original structure.

One key feature of our framework is that it is
modular
, in that
it allows splitting the description of a hierarchy of minion tests into an

algebraic
part, corresponding to the minion
{\mathscr{M}}
, and a

geometric
part, entirely dependent on the tensorisation construction and
hence common to all hierarchies. By considering certain well-behaved families of
minions, which we call
linear
and
conic
, we can then deduce
general properties of the corresponding hierarchies by only focussing on the
geometry of spaces of tensors.

Letting
the minion
{\mathscr{M}}
be
\mathscr{H}
(resp.,
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}
,
{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
), we shall retrieve in this
way the bounded width
hierarchy (resp., the Sherali-Adams LP hierarchy, the affine integer
programming hierarchy). Additionally, we describe
a new minion
{\mathscr{S}}

capturing the power of the basic semidefinite programming relaxation
(
\SDP
),
3
3

3

In the recent paper
[
29
]
, Brakensiek, Guruswami, and Sandeep independently provided a characterisation for the power of
\SDP
that is similar to the one we obtain in the current work.
and we show that
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{S}}}^{k}
coincides with the
Sum-of-Squares hierarchy. As a consequence, our framework is
able to provide a unified description of all these four well-known
hierarchies of algorithmic relaxations.
In addition to
casting
known
hierarchies of relaxations as hierarchies of minion tests,
this approach can be used to design
new
hierarchies. In particular, we describe an operation that we call
semi-direct product
of minions, which consists in combining multiple minions to form a new minion associated with a stronger relaxation. In practice, this method can be used to design an algorithm that combines the features of different known algorithmic techniques. We show that the minion
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{BA}}

is the semi-direct product of

The scope of this framework is potentially not limited to

appears to be transversal to the constraint satisfaction setting and, instead, inherently
connected to the algorithmic techniques themselves, which can be applied to

Promise CSPs

A signature
\sigma

R

\ar(R)\in\mathbb{N}
.
A
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{A}
consists of a domain (universe)
A
and, for each
R\in\sigma

R^{\mathbf{A}}\subseteq A^{\ar(R)}
. A
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{A}
is finite if the size
|A|
of its domain
A

is finite. In this case, we often assume that the domain of
\mathbf{A}
is
A=[n]
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
be
\sigma
-structures. A

from
\mathbf{A}
to
\mathbf{B}
is a map

\mathbf{a}\in R^{\mathbf{A}}

. We denote the existence of a homomorphism from
\mathbf{A}
to
\mathbf{B}
by
\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}
.
A pair of
\sigma
-structures
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
with
\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}
is called a
promise constraint satisfaction problem
(
\PCSP
)
template
. The
\PCSP
problem parameterised by the template
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
, denoted by
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
, is the following computational problem:
The input is a
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{X}
and the goal is to answer
Yes
if
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{A}
and
No
if
\mathbf{X}\not\to\mathbf{B}
. The promise is that it is not the case that
\mathbf{X}\not\to\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{B}
.
We write
\CSP(\mathbf{A})
for
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A})
, the classic (non-promise) constraint satisfaction problem.

Relaxations and hierarchies

The following relaxations of
\parPCSPs
shall be mentioned in this paper: Arc
Consistency (
\ArcC
) is a propagation algorithm that checks for the existence
of assignments satisfying the local constraints of the given
\parPCSP

instance
[
87
]
; the basic linear programming (
\BLP
)
relaxation looks for compatible probability distributions on
assignments
[
81
]
; the affine integer programming (
\AIP
)
relaxation turns the constraints into linear equations, that can be solved over
the integers using (a variant of) Gaussian elimination
[
25
]
; the basic
semidefinite programming (
\SDP
) relaxation is essentially a strengthening of

\BLP
, where probabilities are replaced by vectors satisfying orthogonality
requirements
[
91
]
; the combined basic linear programming and affine integer programming (
\BA
) relaxation is a hybrid algorithm blending
\BLP
and
\AIP

[
30
]
.

In this work, we shall mainly focus on algorithmic
hierarchies
.
The bounded width (
\BW^{k}
) hierarchy (also known as local consistency checking algorithm) refines
\ArcC
by propagating local solutions over bigger and bigger portions of the instance, while the Sherali-Adams LP (
\SA^{k}
), affine integer programming (
\AIP^{k}
), Sum-of-Squares (
\SoS^{k}
), and combined basic linear programming and affine integer programming (
\BA^{k}
) hierarchies strengthen the
\BLP
,
\AIP
,
\SDP
, and
\BA
relaxations, respectively, by looking for compatible distributions over bigger and bigger assignments.

The five hierarchies mentioned above, as well as the
\SDP
relaxation, are described in Section
4.2
(see also the discussion in Appendix
A
).
We refer to
[
12
]
for
\ArcC
,
\BLP
, and
\AIP
, and to
[
30
]
for
\BA
.

Algebraic approach to PCSPs

The algebraic theory of
\PCSP
s developed in
[
12
]
relies on the notions of polymorphism and minion.
Let
\mathbf{A}
be a
\sigma
-structure. For
L\in\mathbb{N}
, the
L
-th power
of
\mathbf{A}
is the
\sigma
-structure
\mathbf{A}^{L}
with domain
A^{L}
whose relations are defined as follows: Given
R\in\sigma
and an
L\times\ar(R)
matrix
M
such that all rows of
M
are tuples in
R^{\mathbf{A}}
, the columns of
M
form a tuple in
R^{\mathbf{A}^{L}}
. An
L
-ary
polymorphism
of a
\PCSP
template
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is a homomorphism from
\mathbf{A}^{L}
to
\mathbf{B}
.
Minions were defined in
[
12
]
as sets of functions with certain properties. We shall use here the abstract definition of minions, as first done in
[
30
]
, cf. also
[
44
]
.
A
minion

\mathscr{M}
consists in the disjoint union of nonempty sets
\mathscr{M}^{(L)}
for
L\in\mathbb{N}
equipped with (so-called
minor
) operations
(\cdot)_{/\pi}:\mathscr{M}^{(L)}\rightarrow\mathscr{M}^{(L^{\prime})}
for all functions
\pi:[L]\rightarrow[L^{\prime}]
, which satisfy

M_{/\operatorname{id}}=M
and, for

\pi:[L]\rightarrow[L^{\prime}]
and
\tilde{\pi}:[L^{\prime}]\rightarrow[L^{\prime\prime}]
,

(M_{/\pi})_{/\tilde{\pi}}=M_{/\tilde{\pi}\circ\pi}

for all
M\in\mathscr{M}^{(L)}
.

Example 1
.

The set
\Pol(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
of all polymorphisms of a
\PCSP
template
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is a minion with the minor operations defined by

f_{/\pi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{L^{\prime}})=f(a_{\pi(1)},\dots,a_{\pi(L)})

for
f:\mathbf{A}^{L}\to\mathbf{B}
and
\pi:[L]\to[L^{\prime}]
.
In this minion, the minor operations correspond to identifying coordinates, permuting coordinates, and introducing dummy coordinates (of polymorphisms).

Example 2
.

Other examples of minions that shall appear frequently in this work are
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}
,
{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
, and
\mathscr{H}
, capturing the power of the algorithms
\BLP
,
\AIP
, and
\ArcC
, respectively.
The
L
-ary elements of
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}
are rational vectors of size
L
that are stochastic (i.e., whose entries are nonnegative and sum up to
1
), with the minor
operations defined as follows: For
\mathbf{q}\in{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}^{(L)}

and
\pi:[L]\to[L^{\prime}]
,
\mathbf{q}_{/\pi}=P\mathbf{q}
, where
P
is the
L^{\prime}\times L
matrix whose
(i,j)
-th entry is
1
if
\pi(j)=i
, and
0
otherwise.

{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
is defined similarly to
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}
, the only difference being that its
L
-ary elements are affine integer vectors (i.e., their entries are integer – possibly negative – numbers and sum up to
1
).

\mathscr{H}
is the minion of polymorphisms of the
\CSP
template
Horn-3-Sat
, i.e., the Boolean structure whose four relations are “
x\wedge y\Rightarrow z
”, “
x\wedge y\Rightarrow\neg z
”,
\{0\}
, and
\{1\}
. Equivalently (cf.
[
12
]
),
\mathscr{H}
can be described as follows: For any
L\in\mathbb{N}
, the
L
-ary elements of
\mathscr{H}
are Boolean functions of the form
f_{Z}(x_{1},\dots,x_{L})=\bigwedge_{z\in Z}x_{z}
for any
Z\subseteq[L]
,
Z\neq\emptyset
; the minor operations are defined as in Example
1
.
We shall also mention the minion
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{BA}}
capturing the algorithm
\BA
. Its
L
-ary elements are
L\times 2
matrices whose first column
\mathbf{u}
belongs to
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}^{(L)}
and whose second column
\mathbf{v}
belongs to
{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}^{(L)}
, and such that if the
i
-th entry of
\mathbf{u}
is zero then the
i
-th entry of
\mathbf{v}
is also zero, for each
i\in[L]
. The minor operation is defined on each column individually; i.e.,
[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{u}&\mathbf{v}\end{array}]_{/\pi}=[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{u}_{/\pi}&\mathbf{v}_{/\pi}\end{array}]
.

For two minions
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{N}
, a
minion homomorphism

\xi:\mathscr{M}\rightarrow\mathscr{N}
is a map that preserves arities and minors: Given
M\in\mathscr{M}^{(L)}
and
\pi:[L]\rightarrow[L^{\prime}]
,
\xi(M)\in\mathscr{N}^{(L)}
and
\xi(M_{/\pi})=\xi(M)_{/\pi}
. We denote the existence of a minion homomorphism from
\mathscr{M}
to
\mathscr{N}
by
\mathscr{M}\to\mathscr{N}
.

We will also need the concept of free structure from
[
12
]
.
Let
\mathscr{M}
be a minion and let
\mathbf{A}
be a (finite)
\sigma
-structure. The

free structure
of
\mathscr{M}
generated by
\mathbf{A}
is a
\sigma
-structure

\mathbb{F}_{\mathscr{M}}(\mathbf{A})
with domain
\mathscr{M}^{(|A|)}
(potentially infinite). Given a
relation symbol
R\in\sigma
of arity
r
, a tuple
(M_{1},\dots,M_{r})
of elements
of
\mathscr{M}^{(|A|)}
belongs to
R^{\mathbb{F}_{\mathscr{M}}(\mathbf{A})}
if and only if there is some

Q\in\mathscr{M}^{(|R^{\mathbf{A}}|)}
such that
M_{i}=Q_{/\pi_{i}}
for each
i\in[r]
, where

\pi_{i}:R^{\mathbf{A}}\to A
maps
\mathbf{a}\in R^{\mathbf{A}}
to its
i
-th coordinate
a_{i}
. The
definition of free structure may at this point strike the reader as rather
abstract. We shall see that if we consider certain quite general classes of
minions then this object unveils an interesting geometric description of linear and multilinear nature.

3
Overview of results and techniques

Let
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
be a
\PCSP
template. As discussed in Section
1
,
any (potentially infinite) structure
\mathbf{T}
on the same signature as
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}

can be viewed as a test for the computational problem
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
: Given an
instance
\mathbf{X}
, the test returns
Yes
if
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{T}
, and
No
otherwise.
As the next definition illustrates, minions provide a systematic method to build tests for
\PCSPs
.

Definition 3
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a minion. The
minion test

\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}
is the computational problem defined as follows: Given two
\sigma
-structures
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{A}
, return
Yes
if
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A})
, and
No
otherwise.

If
\mathbf{X}
is an instance of
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
for some template
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
, we write
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})=\textsc{Yes}
if
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}
applied to
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{A}
returns
Yes
(i.e., if
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A})
), and we write
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})=\textsc{No}
otherwise.
Note that, in the expression “
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})
”,
\mathbf{X}
is the input structure of the
\PCSP
, while
\mathbf{A}
is the fixed structure from the
\PCSP
template.

Leaving
\SDP
aside for the moment, it turns out that the algebraic structure lying at the core of all relaxations mentioned in Section
2
, of seemingly different nature, is the same, as all of them are minion tests for specific minions.

Theorem 4

(
[
12
,
30
]
)
.

\ArcC=\operatorname{Test}_{\mathscr{H}}
,

\BLP=\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}}
,

\AIP=\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}}
,

\BA=\operatorname{Test}_{\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{BA}}}
.

One reason why minion tests are an interesting type of tests is that they are always complete.

Proposition 5
.

\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}
is complete for any minion
{\mathscr{M}}
; i.e., for any
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{A}
with
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{A}
, we have
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A})
.

A second feature of minion tests is that their soundness can be characterised algebraically, as stated in the next proposition and shown easily using a compactness argument from
[
88
]
, cf.
[
12
]
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a minion and let
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
be a
\PCSP
template. Then,
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}
solves
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
if and only if
{\mathscr{M}}\to\Pol(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
.

3.1
A minion for SDP

The first contribution of this work is to design a minion
{\mathscr{S}}
capturing the power of
\SDP
, thus showing that, similarly to
\ArcC
,
\BLP
,
\AIP
, and
\BLP+\AIP
, also
\SDP
is a minion test.

Definition 7
.

For
L\in\mathbb{N}
, let
{\mathscr{S}}^{(L)}
be the set of real
L\times{\aleph_{0}}
matrices
{M}
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{(C1)}\;\csupp(M)\mbox{ is finite}

\displaystyle\mbox{(C2)}\;{M}{M}^{T}\mbox{ is a diagonal matrix}

\displaystyle\mbox{(C3)}\;\tr({M}{M}^{T})=1.

(1)

Given a function
\pi:[L]\rightarrow[L^{\prime}]
and a matrix
{M}\in{\mathscr{S}}^{(L)}
,
we let
{M}_{/\pi}=P{M}
, where
P
is the
L^{\prime}\times L
matrix whose

(i,j)
-th entry is
1
if
\pi(j)=i
, and
0
otherwise. We set

{\mathscr{S}}=\bigsqcup_{L\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathscr{S}}^{(L)}
.

In Section
6
, we shall prove that the object defined above is indeed a minion and that it captures the power of the
\SDP
relaxation, as stated below.

Proposition 8
.

\SDP=\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{S}}}
. In other words, given two
\sigma
-structures
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{A}
,
\SDP(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})=\textsc{Yes}
if and only if
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{S}}}(\mathbf{A})
.

Using Proposition
6
,
we obtain a characterisation of the power of the SDP relaxation.

Theorem 9
.

Let
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
be a
\PCSP
template. Then,
\SDP
solves
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
if and only if
{\mathscr{S}}\to\Pol(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
.

3.2
Tensorisation

As discussed earlier, minions give a systematic method for designing tests for
\parPCSPs
. We now describe a construction, which we call

, that provides a technique to systematically refine minion tests, thus creating hierarchies of progressively stronger algorithms.

Let

be a set and let

denotes the set of all functions from

For

and a signature

\sigma
such that each symbol

of arity

The

.

For a minion
{\mathscr{M}}
and an integer

\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}
, denoted by
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{k}

\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{k}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})=\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}},\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})
. In other words, the
k
-th level of a minion test is just the minion test applied to the tensor power of the structures.
We have seen (cf. Proposition
5
) that a minion test is always complete. It turns out that this property keeps holding for any level of a minion test.

Proposition 13
.

\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{k}
is complete for any minion
{\mathscr{M}}
and any integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

The proof of Proposition
13
relies on the fact that homomorphisms between structures are in some sense invariant under the tensorisation construction, as formally stated in Proposition
28
.

It is well known that each of the hierarchies of relaxations mentioned in Section
2
has the property that higher levels are at least as powerful as lower levels. As the next result shows, this is in fact a property of all hierarchies of minion tests.

Proposition 14
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a minion, let
k,p\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
k>p
, and let
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A}
be two
k
- and
p
-enhanced
\sigma
-structures. If
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{k}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})=\textsc{Yes}
then
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{p}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})=\textsc{Yes}
.

It follows from Proposition
14
that, if some level of a minion test is sound for a template
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
(equivalently, if it solves
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
), then any higher level is sound for
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
(equivalently, it solves
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
).

The next theorem is the second main result of this paper. It shows that the framework defined above is general enough to capture each of the five hierarchies for
\parPCSPs
mentioned in Section
2
.

Theorem 15

(Informal)
.

If
k\in\mathbb{N}
is at least the maximum arity of the template,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\bullet\;\BW^{k}=\operatorname{Test}_{\mathscr{H}}^{k}\\[4.0pt]
\bullet\;\SA^{k}=\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}}^{k}\\[4.0pt]
\bullet\;\AIP^{k}=\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}}^{k}\\[4.0pt]
\bullet\;\SoS^{k}=\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{S}}}^{k}\\[4.0pt]
\bullet\;\BA^{k}=\operatorname{Test}_{\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{BA}}}^{k}.\end{array}

3.3
Linear minions

Certain features of the hierarchies of minion tests from
Definition
12
– in particular, the fact that they
are complete (Proposition
13
) and progressively
stronger (Proposition
14
) – hold
true for any minion, as they only depend on basic properties of the
tensorisation construction. In order to prove
Theorem
15
, however, it is necessary to dig
deeper by investigating how the tensorisation construction interacts with the free structure. In other words, we need to understand the object
\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})
. To that end, we isolate a property shared by all minions mentioned in this work: Their objects can be interpreted as matrices, and their minor operations can be expressed as matrix multiplications. We call such minions

linear
.

Definition 16
.

A minion
{\mathscr{M}}
is
linear
if there exists a semiring

\mathcal{S}

with additive identity

3.5
Conic minions

A primary message of this work is that the tensorisation construction establishes a correspondence between the algebraic properties of a minion and the algorithmic properties of the hierarchy of tests built on the minion. For example, we have seen that if the minion is linear some general properties of the solutions of the hierarchy can be deduced by studying the geometry of
\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})

\BA^{k}

4.1
Terminology for tensors

Tuples

Given a set

\{\mathbf{s}\}=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\}

Given two sets
S,\tilde{S}

Semirings

A

•

(S,+)

is a monoid with an identity element “

(r\cdot s)\cdot t=r\cdot(s\cdot t)

•

“
\cdot
” distributes over “
+

(r+s)\cdot t=(r\cdot t)+(s\cdot t)

•

“
0_{\mathcal{S}}
” is a multiplicative absorbing element (i.e.,
0_{\mathcal{S}}\cdot r=r\cdot 0_{\mathcal{S}}=0_{\mathcal{S}}
).

Examples of semirings are
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{Q}
, and
\mathbb{R}
with the usual addition and multiplication operations, or the Boolean semiring
(\{0,1\},\vee,\wedge)
.

Let
V
be a finite set, and choose an element

for each

if

To increase the readability, we shall usually write

and

and

\mathcal{S}

As anticipated in Section
3

Take a semiring

The

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A}\|{\bm{\lambda}}_{x,a}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A}\mathbf{e}_{a}^{T}\xi(x){Z}{Z}^{T}\xi(x)^{T}\mathbf{e}_{a}=\sum_{a\in A}\mathbf{e}_{a}^{T}\xi(x)\xi(x)^{T}\mathbf{e}_{a}=\tr(\xi(x)\xi(x)^{T})=1,

where the second equality follows from the fact that
\xi(x)^{T}\mathbf{e}_{a}\in S_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
and the fourth from C3.
In a similar way, using C2, we obtain

\displaystyle{\bm{\lambda}}_{x,a}\cdot{\bm{\lambda}}_{x,a^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{e}_{a}^{T}\xi(x){Z}{Z}^{T}\xi(x)^{T}\mathbf{e}_{a^{\prime}}=\mathbf{e}_{a}^{T}\xi(x)\xi(x)^{T}\mathbf{e}_{a^{\prime}}=0,

\displaystyle{\bm{\lambda}}_{R,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{a}}\cdot{\bm{\lambda}}_{R,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{a}}^{T}Q_{R,\mathbf{x}}{Z}{Z}^{T}Q_{R,\mathbf{x}}^{T}\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}=\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{a}}^{T}Q_{R,\mathbf{x}}Q_{R,\mathbf{x}}^{T}\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}=0

if
a\neq a^{\prime}\in A
and
\mathbf{a}\neq\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\in R^{\mathbf{A}}
. This shows that
\vardiamond 2
and
\vardiamond 3
hold. Finally, to prove
\vardiamond 4
, we observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{a}\in R^{\mathbf{A}}\\
a_{i}=a\end{subarray}}{\bm{\lambda}}_{R,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{a}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{a}\in R^{\mathbf{A}}\\
a_{i}=a\end{subarray}}{Z}^{T}Q_{R,\mathbf{x}}^{T}\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{a}}=\big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{a}\in R^{\mathbf{A}}\\
a_{i}=a\end{subarray}}\mathbf{e}_{\mathbf{a}}^{T}Q_{R,\mathbf{x}}{Z}\big)^{T}=\big(\mathbf{e}_{a}^{T}P_{i}Q_{R,\mathbf{x}}{Z}\big)^{T}=\big(\mathbf{e}_{a}^{T}\xi(x_{i}){Z}\big)^{T}

\displaystyle={Z}^{T}\xi(x_{i})^{T}\mathbf{e}_{a}={\bm{\lambda}}_{x_{i},a},

where the third equality follows from Lemma
27
. Therefore, the claim is true and the proof is complete.
∎

and
6
, we immediately have the following algebraic characterisation of the power of
\SDP
.

Theorem

(Theorem
9
restated)
.

Let
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
be a
\PCSP
template. Then,
\SDP
solves

if and only if
{\mathscr{S}}\to\Pol(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
.

7
Hierarchies of minion tests

In this section, we start studying the
hierarchies of minion tests
introduced in Definition
12
, by describing two properties that are common to all such hierarchies: They are complete, and they become tighter as the level increases. A finer description will be available in the next sections, where we will focus on hierarchies built on minions having a
linear
or
conic
form.

The next proposition shows that the tensorisation construction described in Definition
10
does not alter whether two structures are homomorphic or not. We let
\Hom(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
denote the set of homomorphisms from
\mathbf{A}
to
\mathbf{B}
.

Proposition 28
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
be two
\sigma
-structures. Then

(i)

\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}
if and only if
\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\to\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}
;

(ii)

if
\mathbf{A}
is
k
-enhanced, there is a bijection
\rho:\Hom(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\to\Hom(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}},\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})
.

Proof.

Let
{f}:\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}
be a homomorphism, and consider the function
f^{\ast}:A^{k}\to B^{k}
defined by
f^{\ast}((a_{1},\dots,a_{k}))=({f}(a_{1}),\dots,{f}(a_{k}))
. Take
R\in\sigma
of arity
r
, and consider
\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\in R^{\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}}
, where
\mathbf{a}\in R^{\mathbf{A}}
. Let
\mathbf{b}=f(\mathbf{a})
.
Since
{f}
is a homomorphism,
\mathbf{b}\in R^{\mathbf{B}}
, so
\mathbf{b}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\in R^{\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}}
. For any
\mathbf{i}\in[r]^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{i}}\ast f^{\ast}\left(\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\right)

\displaystyle=f^{\ast}\left(E_{\mathbf{i}}\ast\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\right)=f^{\ast}(\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{i}})={f}(\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{i}})=({f}(\mathbf{a}))_{\mathbf{i}}=\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{i}}=E_{\mathbf{i}}\ast\mathbf{b}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}},

which yields
f^{\ast}(\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})=\mathbf{b}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\in R^{\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}}
. Hence,
f^{\ast}:\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\to\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}
is a homomorphism.

Conversely, let
g:\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\to\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}
be a homomorphism. We define the function
g_{\ast}:A\to B
by setting
g_{\ast}(a)=\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}g((a,\dots,a))
for each
a\in A
. Take
R\in\sigma
of arity
r
, and consider a tuple
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\in R^{\mathbf{A}}
. Since
\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\in R^{\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}}
and
g
is a homomorphism, we have that
g(\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})\in R^{\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}}
. Therefore,
g(\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})=\mathbf{b}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}
for some
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{r})\in R^{\mathbf{B}}
. For each
j\in[r]
, consider the tuple
\mathbf{i}=(j,\dots,j)\in[r]^{k}
and observe that

\displaystyle g((a_{j},\dots,a_{j}))=g(\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{i}})=g\left(E_{\mathbf{i}}\ast\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\right)=E_{\mathbf{i}}\ast g(\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})=E_{\mathbf{i}}\ast\mathbf{b}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}=\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{i}}=(b_{j},\dots,b_{j}).

Hence, we find

\displaystyle g_{\ast}(\mathbf{a})=\left(\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}g((a_{1},\dots,a_{1})),\dots,\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}g((a_{r},\dots,a_{r}))\right)=\left(b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\right)=\mathbf{b}\in R^{\mathbf{B}}.

Therefore,
g_{\ast}:\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}
is a homomorphism. This concludes the proof of
(i)
.

To prove
(ii)
, observe first that, if
\mathbf{A}\not\to\mathbf{B}
, then
\Hom(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})=\Hom(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}},\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})=\emptyset
, so there is a trivial bijection in this case. If
\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}
, consider the map
\rho:\Hom(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\to\Hom(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}},\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})
defined by
f\mapsto f^{\ast}
and the map
\rho^{\prime}:\Hom(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}},\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})\to\Hom(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
defined by
g\mapsto g_{\ast}
. For
f:\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}
and
a\in A
, we have

\displaystyle(f^{\ast})_{\ast}(a)=\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}f^{\ast}((a,\dots,a))=\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}(f(a),\dots,f(a))=f(a)

so that
\rho^{\prime}\circ\rho=\id_{\Hom(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})}
. Consider now
g:\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\to\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}
, and take
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in A^{k}
. Using the assumption that
\mathbf{A}
is
k
-enhanced, we have
\mathbf{a}\in R_{k}^{\mathbf{A}}
, which implies
\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\in R_{k}^{\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}}
. Hence,
g(\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})\in R_{k}^{\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}}
, so
g(\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})=\mathbf{b}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}
for some
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{k})\in R_{k}^{\mathbf{B}}\subseteq B^{k}
. For
j\in[k]
and
\mathbf{i}=(j,\dots,j)\in[k]^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle g((a_{j},\dots,a_{j}))=g(\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{i}})=g\left(E_{\mathbf{i}}\ast\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\right)=E_{\mathbf{i}}\ast g\left(\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\right)=E_{\mathbf{i}}\ast\mathbf{b}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}=\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{i}}=(b_{j},\dots,b_{j}).

Letting
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}=(1,\dots,k)\in[k]^{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(g_{\ast})^{\ast}(\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=(g_{\ast}(a_{1}),\dots,g_{\ast}(a_{k}))=\left(\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}g((a_{1},\dots,a_{1})),\dots,\mathbf{e}_{1}^{T}g((a_{k},\dots,a_{k}))\right)=(b_{1},\dots,b_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}=E_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}\ast\mathbf{b}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}=E_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}\ast g\left(\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\right)=g\left(E_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}\ast\mathbf{a}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\right)=g(\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}})=g(\mathbf{a}),

so that
\rho\circ\rho^{\prime}=\id_{\Hom(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}},\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})}
, which concludes the proof of
(ii)
.
∎

Remark 29
.

Part
(ii)
of Proposition
28
does not hold in general if we relax the requirement that
\mathbf{A}
be
k
-enhanced. More precisely, in this case, the function
\rho:\Hom(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\to\Hom(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}},\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})
defined in the proof of Proposition
28
still needs to be injective, but may not be surjective. Therefore, we have
\left|\Hom(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\right|\leq\left|\Hom(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}},\mathbf{B}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})\right|
, and the inequality may be strict.

For example, consider the Boolean structure
\mathbf{A}
having a unique unary relation
R_{1}^{\mathbf{A}}=A=\{0,1\}
. So,
\mathbf{A}
is
1
-enhanced but not
2
-enhanced. Observe that
|\Hom(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A})|=4
. The tensorised structure
\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny 2}}}}}
has domain
\{0,1\}^{2}=\{(0,0),(0,1),\allowbreak(1,0),(1,1)\}
, and its (unary) relation is
R_{1}^{\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny 2}}}}}}=\{0^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny 2}}}}},1^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny 2}}}}}\}=\{(0,0),(1,1)\}
. Therefore, each map
f:\{0,1\}^{2}\to\{0,1\}^{2}
such that
f((0,0))\in\{(0,0),(1,1)\}
and
f((1,1))\in\{(0,0),\allowbreak(1,1)\}
yields a proper homomorphism
\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny 2}}}}}\to\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny 2}}}}}
. It follows that
\left|\Hom(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny 2}}}}},\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny 2}}}}})\right|=64
, so
\Hom(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A})
and
\Hom(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny 2}}}}},\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny 2}}}}})
are not in bijection.

It readily follows from Proposition
28
that hierarchies of minion tests are always complete.

Proposition

(Proposition
13
restated)
.

\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{k}
is complete for any minion
{\mathscr{M}}
and any integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{A}
be two
k
-enhanced
\sigma
-structures and suppose that
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{A}
. Proposition
28
yields
\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\to\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}
, while Lemma
24
yields
\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\to\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})

\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{k}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})=\textsc{Yes}
, as required.
∎

We conclude the section by showing that hierarchies of minion tests become tighter as the level increases.

Proposition

(Proposition
14
restated)
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a minion, let
k,p\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
k>p
, and let
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A}
be two
k
- and
p
-enhanced
\sigma
-structures. If
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{k}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})=\textsc{Yes}
then
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{p}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})=\textsc{Yes}
.

Proof.

Let
\xi:\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\to\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})
be a homomorphism witnessing that

\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{k}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})=\textsc{Yes}
. Since
k>p
, we can choose two tuples

\mathbf{v}\in[k]^{p}
and
\mathbf{w}\in[p]^{k}
such that
\mathbf{w}_{\mathbf{v}}=(1,\dots,p)
. (For instance, we may
take
\mathbf{v}=(1,\ldots,p)
and

\mathbf{w}=(1,\ldots,p,\ldots,p)
.) Consider the map
\tau:A^{k}\to A^{p}
defined by
\mathbf{a}\mapsto\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{v}}
. We claim that the map
\vartheta:X^{p}\to{\mathscr{M}}^{(n^{p})}
defined by
\mathbf{x}\mapsto\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}})_{/\tau}
yields a homomorphism from
\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny p}}}}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny p}}}}})
, thus witnessing that
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{p}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A})=\textsc{Yes}
. To that end, for
R\in\sigma
, take
\mathbf{x}\in R^{\mathbf{X}}
and observe that, since
\xi
is a homomorphism and
\mathbf{x}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\in R^{\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}}
,
\xi(\mathbf{x}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})\in R^{\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})}
. Therefore, there exists
Q\in{\mathscr{M}}^{(|R^{\mathbf{A}}|)}
satisfying
\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}})=Q_{/\pi_{\mathbf{i}}}
for each
\mathbf{i}\in[r]^{k}
. If we manage to show that

\vartheta(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{j}})=Q_{/\pi_{\mathbf{j}}}

for each
\mathbf{j}\in[r]^{p}
,
we would deduce that
\vartheta(\mathbf{x}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny p}}}}})\in R^{\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny p}}}}})}
, thus proving the claim. Observe that

\displaystyle\vartheta(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{j}})

\displaystyle=\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{j}_{\mathbf{w}}})_{/\tau}=(Q_{/\pi_{\mathbf{j}_{\mathbf{w}}}})_{/\tau}=Q_{/\tau\,\circ\,\pi_{\mathbf{j}_{\mathbf{w}}}},

so we are left to show that
\tau\circ\pi_{\mathbf{j}_{\mathbf{w}}}=\pi_{\mathbf{j}}
. Indeed, given any
\mathbf{a}\in R^{\mathbf{A}}
,

\displaystyle(\tau\circ\pi_{\mathbf{j}_{\mathbf{w}}})(\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=\tau(\pi_{\mathbf{j}_{\mathbf{w}}}(\mathbf{a}))=\tau(\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{j}_{\mathbf{w}}})=\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{j}_{\mathbf{w}_{\mathbf{v}}}}=\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{j}}=\pi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{a}),

as required.
∎

As noted in Section
3
, it follows from Proposition
14
that if a
\PCSP
template is solved by some level of a minion test, then it is also solved by any higher level.

8
Hierarchies of linear minion tests

By the Definition
12
of a hierarchy of minion tests,
\operatorname{Test}_{{\mathscr{M}}}^{k}
applied to an instance
\mathbf{X}
of
\PCSP(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
checks for the existence of a homomorphism from
\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})
. Therefore, to describe the hierarchy and get knowledge on its functioning it is necessary to study the structure
\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})
. This is done by investigating how the tensorisation construction interacts with the free structure of the minion
{\mathscr{M}}
on which the hierarchy is built. In this section, we show that, if we consider minions having a “matrix form” – namely, the
linear minions
of Definition
16
–
\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})
is a space of highly structured tensors, which we can describe using the tools of multilinear algebra. Restricting the focus on this class of minions may strike as artificial. The next result argues that the choice is in fact quite natural, as all the minions that have been hitherto utilised in the literature on
\parPCSPs
to capture the power of algorithms (including the minion
{\mathscr{S}}
introduced in this work) are linear. In particular, the machinery we build in this section (and in Section
9
, where we consider an even more specialised minion class) shall be crucial to show that the framework of hierarchies of minion tests captures various well-known hierarchies of relaxations (cf. Theorem
15
).

Proposition

(Proposition
17
restated)
.

The following minions are linear:

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;\mathscr{H},\mbox{ with }\mathcal{S}=(\{0,1\},\vee,\wedge)\mbox{ and }{d}=1

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}},\mbox{ with }\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{Q}\mbox{ and }{d}=1

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}},\mbox{ with }\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{Z}\mbox{ and }{d}=1

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;{\mathscr{S}},\mbox{ with }\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{R}\mbox{ and }{d}={\aleph_{0}}

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{BA}},\mbox{ with }\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{Q}\mbox{ and }{d}=2.

Proof.

The result for
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}
,
{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
, and
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{BA}}
directly follows from their definitions in Example
2
, while the result for
{\mathscr{S}}
is clear from Proposition
26
.

As for
\mathscr{H}
, recall its description given in
Example
2
. Given
L\in\mathbb{N}
and
\emptyset\neq{Z}\subseteq[L]
, we identify the Boolean function
f_{Z}=\bigwedge_{{z}\in{Z}}x_{z}\in\mathscr{H}^{(L)}
with the indicator vector
\mathbf{v}_{Z}\in\{0,1\}^{L}
whose

i
-th entry, for
i\in[L]
, is
1
if
i\in{Z}
, and
0
otherwise. To conclude, we need to show that, under this identification, the minor operations of
\mathscr{H}
correspond to the minor operations given in part 3 of Definition
16
. In other words, we claim that the function
{f_{Z}}_{/\pi}
corresponds to the vector
P\mathbf{v}_{Z}
for any
L^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\pi:[L]\to[L^{\prime}]
, where
P
is the
L^{\prime}\times L
matrix given in Definition
16
. First, observe that

\displaystyle{f_{Z}}_{/\pi}(x_{1},\dots,x_{L^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=f_{Z}(x_{\pi(1)},\dots,x_{\pi(L)})=\bigwedge_{{z}\in{Z}}x_{\pi({z})}=\bigwedge_{t\in\pi({Z})}x_{t}=f_{\pi({Z})}(x_{1},\dots,x_{L^{\prime}}),

so
{f_{Z}}_{/\pi}=f_{\pi({Z})}
. To conclude, we need to show that
P\mathbf{v}_{Z}=\mathbf{v}_{\pi({Z})}
, where
\mathbf{v}_{\pi({Z})}
is the indicator vector of the nonempty set
\pi({Z})\subseteq[L^{\prime}]
. Notice that the matrix multiplication is performed in the semiring
\mathcal{S}=(\{0,1\},\vee,\wedge)
.
For any
i\in[L^{\prime}]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{i}^{T}P\mathbf{v}_{Z}

Lemma 30
.

Let

Lemma 31
.

Let

, let
\mathbf{A}
be a
\sigma
-structure, let

Lemma 32
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a linear minion, let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A}
be two
k
-enhanced
\sigma
-structures, and let

for any
\mathbf{x}\in X^{k}
,
\mathbf{a}\in A^{k}

\mathbf{x}\not\prec\mathbf{a}

Proof.

From

\mathbf{x}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\in R_{k}^{\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}}

is a homomorphism, this yields
\xi(\mathbf{x}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})\in R_{k}^{\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})}

\xi(\mathbf{x}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})

31
, we obtain
E_{\mathbf{a}}\ast\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}})=\mathbf{0}_{d}
for any
\mathbf{i}\in[k]^{k}
such that
\mathbf{i}\not\prec\mathbf{a}
. Write
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
and
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
. Since
\mathbf{x}\not\prec\mathbf{a}
, there exist
\alpha,\beta\in[k]
such that
x_{\alpha}=x_{\beta}
and

. Let
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}\in[k]^{k}
be the tuple obtained from

\beta

\alpha

\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}}=\mathbf{x}

\mathbf{i}^{\prime}\not\prec\mathbf{a}

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}_{d}=E_{\mathbf{a}}\ast\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}^{\prime}})=E_{\mathbf{a}}\ast\xi(\mathbf{x}),

∎

Using Lemma

to

Lemma 33
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a linear minion, let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A}
be two
k
-enhanced
\sigma
-structures such that
k\geq\armax(\sigma)
, and let
\xi:\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\to\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})
be a homomorphism. For
R\in\sigma
of arity
r
, let
\mathbf{x}\in R^{\mathbf{X}}
and
\mathbf{a}\in R^{\mathbf{A}}
be such that
\mathbf{x}\not\prec\mathbf{a}
. Let
Q\in{\mathscr{M}}^{(|R^{\mathbf{A}}|)}
be such that
Q_{/\pi_{\mathbf{i}}}=\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}})
for each
\mathbf{i}\in[r]^{k}

E_{\mathbf{a}}\ast Q=\mathbf{0}_{d}
.

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})
and
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
. Since
\mathbf{x}\not\prec\mathbf{a}
, there exist
\alpha,\beta\in[r]
such that
x_{\alpha}=x_{\beta}
and
a_{\alpha}\neq a_{\beta}
. Using that

, we can take the tuple
\mathbf{i}=(1,2,\dots,r,r,\dots,r)\in[r]^{k}
. Consider
\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}}\in X^{k}
,
\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{i}}\in A^{k}
. Notice that

and

, so
\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}}\not\prec\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{i}}
. Applying Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}_{d}=E_{\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{i}}}\ast\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}})=E_{\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{i}}}\ast Q_{/\pi_{\mathbf{i}}}=E_{\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{i}}}\ast P_{\mathbf{i}}\ast Q.

Using Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}_{d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{b}\in R^{\mathbf{A}}\\

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{i}}=\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{i}}
if and only if

.
∎

If
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{A}
are
k

\sigma
-structures, any homomorphism

from
\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}

. As shown below in Proposition

, these symmetries can be concisely expressed through a tensor equation. Given a tuple

, we let

be the tensor defined by

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{a}}\ast\Pi_{\mathbf{i}}\ast E_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if }\mathbf{a}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{i}}=\mathbf{a}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\hskip 28.45274pt\forall\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\in{A}^{k}.

The tensor defined above satisfies the following simple identity, which should be compared to the one in Lemma

Lemma 34
.

For any
\mathbf{a}\in{A}^{k}
and
\mathbf{i}\in[k]^{k}
,

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{a}}\ast\Pi_{\mathbf{i}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{b}\in{A}^{k}\\
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{i}}=\mathbf{a}\end{subarray}}E_{\mathbf{b}}.

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\in{A}^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{b}\in{A}^{k}\\
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{i}}=\mathbf{a}\end{subarray}}E_{\mathbf{b}}\right)\ast E_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{b}\in{A}^{k}\\
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{i}}=\mathbf{a}\end{subarray}}(E_{\mathbf{b}}\ast E_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{b}\in{A}^{k}\\
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{i}}=\mathbf{a}\\
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}1=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if }\mathbf{a}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{i}}=\mathbf{a}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.=\left(E_{\mathbf{a}}\ast\Pi_{\mathbf{i}}\right)\ast E_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}},

from which the result follows.
∎

Remark 35
.

It is clear from the expressions (
8
) and (
8
) that, for any
\mathbf{i}\in[k]^{k}
,
\Pi_{\mathbf{i}}
coincides with the tensor

associated with the relation symbol

.

Proposition 36
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a linear minion, let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A}
be two
k
-enhanced

\sigma
-structures, and let
\xi:X^{k}\to{\mathscr{M}}^{(n^{k})}
be a map. Then,
\xi
preserves
R_{k}
if and only if

\displaystyle\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}})=\Pi_{\mathbf{i}}\overset{\tiny k}{\ast}\xi(\mathbf{x})\hskip 56.9055pt\mbox{for any }\;\mathbf{x}\in X^{k},\mathbf{i}\in[k]^{k}.

(19)

Proof.

Suppose that
\xi
preserves
R_{k}
, and take
\mathbf{x}\in X^{k}=R_{k}^{\mathbf{X}}
. It follows that
\mathbf{x}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\in R_{k}^{\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}}
, so
\xi(\mathbf{x}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})\in R_{k}^{\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})}

such that
\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}})=Q_{/\pi_{\mathbf{i}}}=\Pi_{\mathbf{i}}\overset{\tiny k}{\ast}Q
for each
\mathbf{i}\in[k]^{k}

(where we have used Remark
35
).
Consider now the tuple
\mathbf{j}=(1,\dots,k)\in[k]^{k}
, and observe that
\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{j}}=\mathbf{x}
. Noticing that the contraction by
\Pi_{\mathbf{j}}
acts as the identity, we conclude that

\displaystyle\xi(\mathbf{x})=\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{j}})=\Pi_{\mathbf{j}}\overset{\tiny k}{\ast}{Q}={Q},

which concludes the proof of (
19
).

Conversely, suppose (
19
) holds and take
\mathbf{x}\in R_{k}^{\mathbf{X}}=X^{k}
. We need to show that
\xi(\mathbf{x}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})\in R_{k}^{\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})}
. Take

. Using again Remark

, we observe that, for any
\mathbf{i}\in[k]^{k}
,

\displaystyle\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}})

\displaystyle=\Pi_{\mathbf{i}}\overset{\tiny k}{\ast}\xi(\mathbf{x})=\xi(\mathbf{x})_{/\pi_{\mathbf{i}}}=Q_{/\pi_{\mathbf{i}}},

whence the result follows.
∎

9
Hierarchies of conic minion tests

The machinery developed in Section
8

applies to hierarchies of tests built on
any
linear minion. It turns out that
certain finer features of hierarchies of minion tests can be deduced if we
assume that the minion is
conic
; i.e., that, in addition to being linear, it enjoys a sort of nonnegativity requirement described in Definition
20
. As we establish next, all minions considered in this work are conic, with the notable exception of
{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
.

Proposition

(Proposition
21
restated)
.

\mathscr{H}
,
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}
,
{\mathscr{S}}
, and
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{BA}}
are conic minions, while
{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
is not.

Proof.

The fact that
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}
is conic trivially follows by noting that its elements are nonnegative vectors whose entries sum up to
1
. Similarly, using the description of
\mathscr{H}
as a linear minion on the semiring
(\{0,1\},\vee,\wedge)
(cf. the proof of Proposition
17
), the fact that
\mathscr{H}
is conic follows from the fact that
\bigvee_{i\in V}x_{i}=0
means that
x_{i}=0
for each
i\in V
. (Observe also that the vectors in
\mathscr{H}
are nonzero, as they are the indicator vectors of nonempty sets.) To show that
{\mathscr{S}}
is conic, take
L\in\mathbb{N}
and
M\in{\mathscr{S}}^{(L)}
, and notice first that
M\neq O_{L,{\aleph_{0}}}
by C3 in Definition
7
.
Take now
V\subseteq[L]
. If
\sum_{i\in V}M^{T}\mathbf{e}_{i}=\mathbf{0}_{\aleph_{0}}
, using that
MM^{T}
is a diagonal matrix by C2, we find

\displaystyle 0=(\sum_{i\in V}M^{T}\mathbf{e}_{i})^{T}(\sum_{j\in V}M^{T}\mathbf{e}_{j})=\sum_{i,j\in V}\mathbf{e}_{i}^{T}MM^{T}\mathbf{e}_{j}=\sum_{i\in V}\mathbf{e}_{i}^{T}MM^{T}\mathbf{e}_{i}=\sum_{i\in V}\|M^{T}\mathbf{e}_{i}\|^{2},

which means that
M^{T}\mathbf{e}_{i}=\mathbf{0}_{\aleph_{0}}
for any
i\in V
, as required.
As for
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{BA}}
, we shall see in Section
10
(cf. Example
43
) that this minion can be obtained as the semi-direct product of
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}
and
{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
. Then, the fact that
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{BA}}
is conic is a direct consequence of the fact that semi-direct products of minions are always conic (cf. Proposition
40
).
Finally, the element
(1,-1,1)\in{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
witnesses that
{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
is not conic.
∎

It turns out that Lemma
33
can be slightly strengthened if we are dealing with conic minions, in that the level

for which it holds can be decreased down to
2
. As a consequence, the algebraic description of the Sherali-Adams and Sum-of-Squares hierarchies in terms of the tensorisation construction can be extended to lower levels, cf. Remark
51
. As in Section
8
, the letter

shall denote the depth of the relevant minion in all results of this section. (Recall that a conic minion is linear, by definition).

Lemma 37
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a conic minion, let
2\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A}
be two

-enhanced
\sigma
-structures, and let
\xi:\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\to\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})
be a homomorphism. For

of arity

, let
\mathbf{x}\in R^{\mathbf{X}}
and
\mathbf{a}\in R^{\mathbf{A}}
be such that
\mathbf{x}\not\prec\mathbf{a}
. Let

be such that
Q_{/\pi_{\mathbf{i}}}=\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}})
for each
\mathbf{i}\in[r]^{k}
. Then
E_{\mathbf{a}}\ast Q=\mathbf{0}_{d}
.

Proof.

Take
\alpha,\beta\in[r]
such that

and

, and consider the tuple
\mathbf{j}=(\alpha,\dots,\alpha,\beta)\in[r]^{k}
. Using that

, we have

, since

and

From Lemma

and Lemma

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}_{d}=E_{\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{j}}}\ast\xi(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{j}})=E_{\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{j}}}\ast Q_{/\pi_{\mathbf{j}}}=E_{\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{j}}}\ast P_{\mathbf{j}}\ast Q=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{b}\in R^{\mathbf{A}}\\
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{j}}=\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{j}}\end{subarray}}E_{\mathbf{b}}\ast Q.

Using that

is a conic minion, we deduce that

∎

The following result shows that hierarchies of tests built on conic minions only give a nonzero weight to those assignments that yield partial homomorphisms.

Proposition 38
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a conic minion, let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A}
be two

-enhanced
\sigma

\xi:\mathbf{X}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}}\to\mathbb{F}_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\mathbf{A}^{\textsuperscript{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.1pt}{\tiny k}}}}})
be a homomorphism. Let
R\in\sigma
have arity
r
, and take
\mathbf{x}\in X^{k}
,
\mathbf{a}\in A^{k}
, and
\mathbf{i}\in[k]^{r}
. If
\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{i}}\in R^{\mathbf{X}}
and
\mathbf{a}_{\mathbf{i}}\not\in R^{\mathbf{A}}
, then
E_{\mathbf{a}}\ast\xi(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{0}_{d}
.

Proof.

From

10
The semi-direct product of minions

Is it possible for multiple linear minions to “join forces”, to obtain a new linear minion corresponding to a stronger relaxation? The natural way to do so is to take as the elements of the new linear minion block matrices, whose blocks are the elements of the original minions. Let
{\mathscr{M}}
and
{\mathscr{N}}
be two linear minions that we wish to “merge”. A zero row in a matrix of
{\mathscr{M}}
corresponds to zero weight assigned to the variable associated with the row by the relaxation given by
{\mathscr{M}}
. Ideally, we would like to preserve this information when we run the relaxation given by the second minion
{\mathscr{N}}
. In other words, we require that zero rows in
{\mathscr{M}}
should be associated with zero rows in
{\mathscr{N}}
. For this to make sense (i.e., for the resulting object to be a linear minion), we need to assume that
{\mathscr{M}}
is conic. Under this assumption, it turns out that the new linear minion is conic, too. Therefore, this construction yields a method to transform a linear minion into a conic one, by taking its product with a fixed conic minion (for instance,
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}
). Equivalently, the semi-direct product provides a way to turn a hierarchy of linear tests into a more powerful hierarchy of conic tests – which enjoys the appealing properties described in Section
9
.

Proposition 40
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a conic minion with semiring
\mathcal{S}
and depth
d
, let
{\mathscr{N}}
be a linear minion with semiring
\mathcal{S}
and depth
d^{\prime}
,
and consider, for each
L\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
({\mathscr{M}}\ltimes{\mathscr{N}})^{(L)}=\{[\begin{array}[]{cc}M&N\end{array}]:M\in{\mathscr{M}}^{(L)},N\in{\mathscr{N}}^{(L)},\mbox{ and }N^{T}\mathbf{e}_{i}=\mathbf{0}_{d^{\prime}}\mbox{ for any }i\in[L]\mbox{ such that }M^{T}\mathbf{e}_{i}=\mathbf{0}_{d}\}
. Then
{\mathscr{M}}\ltimes{\mathscr{N}}=\bigcup_{L\in\mathbb{N}}({\mathscr{M}}\ltimes{\mathscr{N}})^{(L)}
is a conic minion with semiring
\mathcal{S}
and depth
d+d^{\prime}
.

Definition 41
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
and
{\mathscr{N}}
be a conic minion and a linear minion, respectively, over the same semiring.
The
semi-direct product

of
{\mathscr{M}}
and
{\mathscr{N}}
is the conic minion
{\mathscr{M}}\ltimes{\mathscr{N}}
described in Proposition
40
.

Remark 42
.

If the minions
{\mathscr{M}}
and
{\mathscr{N}}
in Definition
41
have different semirings
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, we cannot in general use the definition to build their semi-direct product. However, it is immediate to check that a linear minion over
\mathcal{S}
is also a linear minion over any semiring of which
\mathcal{S}
is a sub-semiring. Hence, if
\mathcal{S}
is a sub-semiring of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
(or vice-versa),
{\mathscr{M}}\ltimes{\mathscr{N}}
is well defined (see Example
43
below). In general, however, we might not be able to find a common semiring of which
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
are both sub-semirings. In particular, it is not true in general that the direct sum of semirings admits homomorphic injections from the components, see
[
51
]
. To be able to define
{\mathscr{M}}\ltimes{\mathscr{N}}
also in this case, we would need to redefine linear minions by allowing each of the
d
columns of the matrices in a linear minion of depth
d
to contain entries from a possibly different semiring.
In this way, the requirement in Definition
41
can be circumvented – which, for example, makes it possible to define the minion
\mathscr{H}\ltimes{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
(see Remark
52
).

Example 43
.

Since
\mathbb{Z}
is a sub-semiring of
\mathbb{Q}
, we can view
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}
and
{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
as a conic minion and a linear minion over the same semiring
\mathbb{Q}
, respectively. It is easy to check that their semi-direct product
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}\ltimes{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
is precisely the minion
\mathscr{M}_{\operatorname{BA}}
from
[
30
]
.

Proof of Proposition
40
.

We start by showing that
{\mathscr{M}}\ltimes{\mathscr{N}}
is a linear minion of depth
d+d^{\prime}
. Notice that each set
({\mathscr{M}}\ltimes{\mathscr{N}})^{(L)}
consists of
L\times(d+d^{\prime})
matrices having entries in
\mathcal{S}
. Given
\pi:[L]\to[L^{\prime}]
and
[\begin{array}[]{cc}M&N\end{array}]\in({\mathscr{M}}\ltimes{\mathscr{N}})^{(L)}
, we claim that
P[\begin{array}[]{cc}M&N\end{array}]=[\begin{array}[]{cc}PM&PN\end{array}]
belongs to
({\mathscr{M}}\ltimes{\mathscr{N}})^{(L^{\prime})}
, where
P
is the
L^{\prime}\times L
matrix corresponding to
\pi
as per Definition
16
. First, since
{\mathscr{M}}
and
{\mathscr{N}}
are both linear minions, we have that
PM=M_{/\pi}\in{\mathscr{M}}^{(L^{\prime})}
and
PN=N_{/\pi}\in{\mathscr{N}}^{(L^{\prime})}
. Let

be such that

.
We find

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}_{d}

\displaystyle=M^{T}P^{T}\mathbf{e}_{j}=\sum_{i\in\pi^{-1}(j)}M^{T}\mathbf{e}_{i}.

Using that
{\mathscr{M}}
is conic, we obtain
M^{T}\mathbf{e}_{i}=\mathbf{0}_{d}
for each

. By the definition of

, this means that

for each

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}_{d^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\pi^{-1}(j)}N^{T}\mathbf{e}_{i}=N^{T}P^{T}\mathbf{e}_{j}=(PN)^{T}\mathbf{e}_{j},

which proves the claim. It follows that

is indeed a linear minion.

To show that

is conic, we first note that no element of
{\mathscr{M}}\ltimes{\mathscr{N}}
is the all-zero matrix, since

is conic. If

, this yields

for each

, so

\displaystyle[\begin{array}[]{cc}M&N\end{array}]^{T}\mathbf{e}_{i}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}M^{T}\mathbf{e}_{i}\\

\mathbf{0}_{d^{\prime}}\end{array}\right]=\mathbf{0}_{d+d^{\prime}}

Remark 51
.

The characterisations of
\SA^{k}
and
\SoS^{k}
in Propositions
47
and
49
hold for any higher level than the first, unlike the characterisation of
\AIP^{k}
in Proposition
48
. This is due to the fact that
{\mathscr{Q}_{\operatorname{conv}}}
and
{\mathscr{S}}
are conic minions, so Lemma
37
applies, while
{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
is not. As for
\BW^{k}
, Proposition
46
requires
k\geq\armax(\sigma)
even if
\mathscr{H}
is a conic minion. The reason for this lies in the definition of the bounded width hierarchy. Essentially, any constraint whose scope has more than
k
distinct variables does not appear among the constraints of the partial homomorphisms witnessing acceptance of
\BW^{k}
, while it does appear in the requirements of
\SA^{k}
and
\SoS^{k}
. Finally, assuming
k\geq\armax(\sigma)
is also required in the characterisation of
\BA^{k}
in Proposition
50
, in order to make use of Proposition
44
and of the characterisation of
\AIP^{k}
.

Remark 52
.

As it was shown in Section
9
, hierarchies of relaxations built on conic minions (such as
\BW^{k}
,
\SA^{k}
,
\SoS^{k}
, and
\BA^{k}
) are “sound in the limit”, in that their
k
-th level correctly classifies instances
\mathbf{X}
with
|X|\leq k
(cf. Proposition
39
). This is not the case for the non-conic hierarchy
\AIP^{k}
, as it was established in the follow-up work
[
42
]
.
In
[
19
]
, a stronger affine hierarchy was
proposed, which – contrary to
\AIP^{k}
– requires that the variables in the relaxation should be partial homomorphisms and is thus sound in the limit. By virtue of Proposition
38
, this requirement can be captured by taking the semi-direct product of any conic minion and
{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
. In particular, it follows that the hierarchy in
[
19
]
is not stronger than the hierarchy built on the minion
\mathscr{H}\ltimes{\mathscr{Z}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
(cf. Remark
42
).
In recent work
[
46
]
, a different algorithm for

\parPCSPs
has been proposed. The relationship of
[
46
]
with
our work is an interesting direction for future research.

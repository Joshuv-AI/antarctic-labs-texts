Introduction

Let
k\geq 2
be any fixed integer. Then any integer
n\geq 1
can be uniquely decomposed as
n=q(n)k(n)
,
(q(n),k(n))=1
, where
q(n)
is
k
-free, and
k(n)
is
k
-full (
k(n)
is
k
-full if
p^{k}\mid k(n)
whenever
p\mid k(n)
). A non-negative integer valued function
a(n)
is called
k
-full kernel function if
a(n)=a(k(n))
for all
n\geq 1
and
a(n)\ll n^{\epsilon}
for any
\epsilon>0
by Ivić and Tenenbaum
[
9
]
. It is to be noted that
k
-full kernel functions are not necessarily multiplicative.

In 1987, Erdős and Ivić
[
4
]
studied the 2-full kernel function and obtained the following asymptotic formulas

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}a(n)d(n+1)=C_{1}x\log x+C_{2}x+O\left(x^{\frac{8}{9}+\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}a(n)\omega(n+1)=D_{1}x\log\log x+D_{2}x+O\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right),

where
d(n)
is the divisor function, and
\omega(n)
is the number of different prime factors of
n
, and
C_{1}
,
D_{1}>0
,
C_{2}
,
D_{2}
are constants that can be evaluated explicitly.

For an even integer
\kappa\geq 2
, let
H_{\kappa}
denote the set of all normalized primitive Hecke eigencusp forms of even integral weight
\kappa
for the full modular group
\Gamma=SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
. For
f(z)\in H_{\kappa}
, it has the Fourier expansion at the cusp
\infty

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}e^{2\pi inz},\qquad\Im(z)>0,

where the coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)
are eigenvalues of Hecke operators
T_{n}
with
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
. It’s well known that
\lambda_{f}(n)
is real and satisfies the multiplicative property

\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{d\mid(m,n)}\lambda_{f}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right),

(1.1)

for all
m\geq 1
,
n\geq 1
. In 1974, Deligne
[
3
]
proved the Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture for holomorphic cusp forms

|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq d(n),

(1.2)

where
d(n)
is the divisor function. By (
1.2
), Deligne’s bound is equivalent to the fact that there exist

\alpha_{f}(p),\beta_{f}(p)\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying

\displaystyle\alpha_{f}(p)+\beta_{f}(p)=\lambda_{f}(p),\qquad|\alpha_{f}(p)|=|\beta_{f}(p)|=1=\alpha_{f}(p)\beta_{f}(p).

(1.3)

The Hecke
L
-function
L(s,f)
associated to
f
is defined as

\displaystyle L(s,f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}(1-\lambda_{f}(p)p^{-s}+p^{-2s})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1},\quad\Re(s)>1,

where
\alpha_{f}(p),\beta_{f}(p)
are the local parameters satisfying (
1.3
). The
j
th
sym
metric power
L
-function
associated with
f
is defined as

L(s,\text{sym}^{j}f)=\prod_{p}\prod_{m=0}^{j}(1-\alpha_{f}^{j-m}(p)\beta_{f}^{m}(p)p^{-s})^{-1},\quad\Re(s)>1.

We may expand it into a Dirichlet series

\displaystyle L(s,\text{sym}^{j}f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\text{sym}^{j}f}(n)}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{\lambda_{\text{sym}^{j}f}(p)}{p^{s}}+\cdots+\frac{\lambda_{\text{sym}^{j}f}(p^{k})}{p^{ks}}+\cdots\right),\quad\Re(s)>1.

Obviously
\lambda_{\text{sym}^{j}f}(n)
is a real multiplicative function. For
j=1
, we have
L(s,\text{sym}^{1}f)=L(s,f)
. It is standard that

\lambda_{f}(p^{j})=\lambda_{\text{sym}^{j}f}(p)=\frac{\alpha_{f}^{j+1}(p)-\beta_{f}^{j+1}(p)}{\alpha_{f}(p)-\beta_{f}(p)}=\sum_{m=0}^{j}\alpha_{f}^{j-m}(p)\beta_{f}^{m}(p).

Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
q
. Then we can define the twisted
j
th
sym
metric power
L
-function by the Euler product representation with degree
j+1

\displaystyle L(s,\text{sym}^{j}f\otimes\chi)=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\prod_{m=0}^{j}(1-\alpha_{f}^{j-m}(p)\beta_{f}^{m}(p)\chi(p)p^{-s})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\text{sym}^{j}f}(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}},\quad\Re(s)>1.

Shifted convolution sums with
GL(2)
Fourier coefficients have been investigated extensively by many authors (see
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
14
]

In

Later, Venkatasubbareddy and Sankaranarayanan
[

]
considered the problem to higher moments, and generalized to

-full kernel functions. In this paper, we are interested in the shifted convolution sum

\sum_{n\leq x}a(n)\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(n+1),

where

is
k
-full kernel functions, for any fixed integer
k\geq 2
.

Preliminaries lemmas

In this section, we give some useful lemmas which play important roles in the proofs of the Theorems.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
j\geq 2
be any given integer. Let
f\in H_{\kappa}
be a Hecker eigenform. Then for
\Re s>1
, define

F_{j}(s,\chi):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}.

(2.1)

Then

F_{j}(s,\chi)=G_{j}(s,\chi)H_{j}(s),

where

G_{j}(s,\chi)=L(s,\chi)\prod_{n=1}^{j}L(s,\textup{sym}^{2n}f\otimes\chi).

The function
H_{j}(s)
admits a Dirichlet series which converges uniformly and absolutely in
\Re s>\frac{1}{2}
and
H_{j}(1)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Since
\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}(n)\chi(n)
is a multiplicative function and satisfies the bound
O(n^{\epsilon})
for any
\epsilon>0
, then for
\Re s>1
, we have the Euler product

F_{j}(s,\chi)=\prod_{p}\left(1+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(p^{k})\chi(p^{k})}{p^{ks}}\right).

(2.2)

Taking
m=n=p^{j}
in the Hecke relation (
1.1
), then

\lambda_{f}^{2}(p^{j})=\sum_{d\mid p^{j}}\lambda_{f}\left(\frac{p^{2j}}{d^{2}}\right)=1+\sum_{l=1}^{j}\lambda_{f}(p^{2l}).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(p)\chi(p)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{f}^{2}(p^{j})\chi(p)=\left(1+\sum_{l=1}^{j}\lambda_{f}(p^{2l})\right)\chi(p)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\sum_{l=1}^{j}\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{2l}f}(p)\right)\chi(p)=:b(p).

(2.3)

For
\Re s>1
, the
L
-function

G_{j}(s,\chi):=L(s,\chi)\prod_{n=1}^{j}L(s,\textup{sym}^{2n}f\otimes\chi)

(2.4)

can be represented as

G_{j}(s,\chi)=\prod_{p}\left(1+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{b(p^{k})}{p^{ks}}\right).

(2.5)

By (
2.2
)–(
2.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle F_{j}(s,\chi)

\displaystyle=G_{j}(s,\chi)\times\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(p^{2})\chi(p^{2})-b(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=:L(s,\chi)\prod_{n=1}^{j}L(s,\textup{sym}^{2n}f\otimes\chi)H_{j}(s).

Since the bound
O(n^{\epsilon})
of
\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(n)\chi(n)
for any
\epsilon>0
,
H_{j}(s)
converges uniformly and absolutely in the half-plane
\Re s\geq\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\in H_{\kappa}
be a Hecker eigenform. Then for
\Re s>1
, we define

\mathcal{F}^{(3)}(s,\chi):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{2}f}^{3}(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}.

Then we have

\mathcal{F}^{(3)}(s,\chi)=\mathcal{G}^{(3)}(s,\chi)\mathcal{H}^{(3)}(s),

(2.6)

where

\mathcal{G}^{(3)}(s,\chi)=L(s,\chi)L^{3}(s,\textup{sym}^{2}f\otimes\chi)L^{2}(s,\textup{sym}^{4}f\otimes\chi)L(s,\textup{sym}^{6}f\otimes\chi).

The function
\mathcal{H}^{(3)}(s)
admits a Dirichlet series which converges uniformly and absolutely in
\Re s>\frac{1}{2}
and
\mathcal{H}^{(3)}(1)\neq 0
.

Proof.

By the definition and (
1.3
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{2}f}^{3}(p)=

\displaystyle(\alpha_{f}^{2}(p)+\beta_{f}^{2}(p)+1)^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{f}^{6}(p)+3\alpha_{f}^{4}(p)+6\alpha_{f}^{2}(p)+7+6\beta_{f}^{2}(p)+3\beta_{f}^{4}(p)+\beta_{f}^{6}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\alpha_{f}^{6}(p)+\alpha_{f}^{4}(p)+\alpha_{f}^{2}(p)+1+\beta_{f}^{2}(p)+\beta_{f}^{4}(p)+\beta_{f}^{6}(p)\right)

\displaystyle+2\left(\alpha_{f}^{4}(p)+\alpha_{f}^{2}(p)+1+\beta_{f}^{2}(p)+\beta_{f}^{4}(p)\right)+3\left(\alpha_{f}^{2}(p)+1+\beta_{f}^{2}(p)\right)+1.

Since
\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{2}f}(n)
and
\chi(n)
are multiplicative functions, by standard arguments, the above relation leads us to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(3)}(s,\chi)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{2}f}^{3}(p)\chi(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{2}f}^{3}(p^{2})\chi(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=L(s,\chi)L^{3}(s,\textup{sym}^{2}f\otimes\chi)L^{2}(s,\textup{sym}^{4}f\otimes\chi)L(s,\textup{sym}^{6}f\otimes\chi)\mathcal{H}^{(3)}(s),

where
\mathcal{H}^{(3)}(s)
is some Dirichlet series which converges absolutely and uniformly in
\Re s\geq\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon
and
\mathcal{H}^{(3)}(1)\neq 0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
\chi
be any Dirichlet character modulo
q
. By the orthogonality we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x+1\atop n\equiv 1(q)}\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(n)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\sum_{n\leq x+1}\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(n)\chi(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{n\leq x+1}\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(n)\chi_{0}(n)+\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{n\leq x+1}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}\atop\chi\textup{ is primitvie}}\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(n)\chi(n)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{n\leq x+1}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}\atop\chi\textup{ is non-primitvie}}\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(n)\chi(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\big(\textstyle{\sum_{1}+\sum_{2}+\sum_{3}}\big).

For
\sum_{1}
, by Lemma
2.1
and applying Perron’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x+1}\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(n)\chi_{0}(n)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1+\epsilon-iT}^{1+\epsilon+iT}F_{j}(s,\chi_{0})\frac{(x+1)^{s}}{s}\textup{d}s+O\left(\frac{x^{1+\epsilon}}{T}\right).

Shifting the line of integration to
\Re s=\eta:=\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon
and using Cauchy’s residue theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x+1}\lambda_{\textup{sym}^{j}f}^{2}(n)\chi_{0}(n)=

\displaystyle\Res_{s=1}F_{j}(s,\chi_{0})\frac{(x+1)^{s}}{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left\{\int_{\eta-iT}^{\eta+iT}+\int_{\eta+iT}^{1+iT}+\int_{1-iT}^{\eta-iT}\right\}F_{j}(s,\chi_{0})\frac{(x+1)^{s}}{s}\textup{d}s+O\left(\frac{x^{1+\epsilon}}{T}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:c_{f,j}\frac{\phi(q)}{q}x+I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+O\left(\frac{x^{1+\epsilon}}{T}\right).

Here

c_{f,j}=\prod_{n=1}^{j}L(1,\text{sym}^{2n}\otimes\chi_{0})H_{j}(1).

For
I_{1}
, by (
2.7
), (
2.11
) and (
2.14
), along with Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\eta-iT}^{\eta+iT}\zeta(s)\prod_{p\mid q}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)L(s,\textup{sym}^{2}f\otimes\chi_{0})\prod_{n=2}^{j}L(s,\textup{sym}^{2n}f\otimes\chi_{0})H_{j}(s)\frac{(x+1)^{s}}{s}\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{T}\Big|\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon+it)L(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon+it,\textup{sym}^{2}f\otimes\chi_{0})\prod_{n=2}^{j}L(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon+it,\textup{sym}^{2n}f\otimes\chi_{0})\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}{t}\Big|\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle x^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\log T\max_{1\leq T_{1}\leq T}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{T_{1}}\left(\int_{T_{1}}^{2T_{1}}|\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon+it)|^{12}\textup{d}t\right)^{1/12}\left(\int_{T_{1}}^{2T_{1}}|L(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon+it,\textup{sym}^{2}f)|^{12/5}\textup{d}t\right)^{5/12}

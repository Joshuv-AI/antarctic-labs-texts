Stability of bound states for regularized nonlinear Schrödinger equations
John Albert and Jack Arbunich

Abstract
We consider the stability of bound-state solutions of a family of regularized nonlinear Schrödinger equations which were introduced by Dumas, Lannes and Szeftel as models for the propagation of laser beams. Among these bound-state solutions are ground states, which are defined as solutions of a variational problem. We give a sufficient condition for existence and orbital stability of ground states, and use it to verify that ground states exist and are stable over a wider range of nonlinearities than for the nonregularized nonlinear Schrödinger equation. We also give another sufficient and almost necessary condition for stability of general bound states, and show that some stable bound states exist which are not ground states.



Keywords:
Nonlinear Schrödinger equation, regularization, bound states, ground states, stability

Introduction

In this paper, we consider regularized nonlinear Schrödinger equations recently introduced as models for the propagation of laser beams in
[
11
]
, and further studied in
[
2
,
3
,
23
]
. One of these equations is the fully regularized equation

i(u-\beta\Delta u)_{t}+\Delta u+|u|^{p}u=0,

(1.1)

with
\beta>0
, whose relation to the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation,

iu_{t}+\Delta u+|u|^{p}u=0,

(1.2)

is similar to that between the Korteweg-de Vries equation

u_{t}+u_{x}+u_{xxx}+uu_{x}=0

and its regularized counterpart, the Benjamin-Bona-Mahony equation
[
4
]

(u-\beta u_{xx})_{t}+u_{x}+u_{xxx}+uu_{x}=0.

(1.3)

More generally, we will consider partially regularized equations of the form

i(P_{\beta}u)_{t}+\Delta u+|u|^{p}u=0.

(1.4)

In (
1.4
), the complex-valued function
u(\mathbf{x},t)
is defined for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
. In the physical situations for which (
1.4
) is derived as a model equation in
[
11
]

\beta
is a real parameter with
0<\beta\ll 1
, but for the purposes of the present paper we only need to assume that
\beta>0

The operator
P_{\beta}
is defined as follows. Fix an integer

such that
0\leq k\leq d
.
Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
1\leq k\leq d-1
, we write
\mathbf{x}=(x,y)
, where
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d-k})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-k}
and

, where
y_{j}=x_{d-k+j}

. We define

\displaystyle P_{\beta}u:=u-\beta\Delta_{y}u,

.
In case

, we define

1.4

).
In the case

When

in (

\mathbb{R}^{d}

Our goal here is to study the effect of this partial regularization on the stability of bound-state solutions of (
1.4
), i.e. solutions of the form

u(\mathbf{x},t)=e^{i\omega t}\varphi(\mathbf{x})

where
\omega
is real and

-based Sobolev space

. First, in Theorems
1.8
,
4.11

[
9
]
. Then in Theorem
1.9

\omega
such that

, where

is the function defined below in (

[

Our stability results concern the initial-value problem set for equation (

Definition 1.1
.

For
d\geq 1

, and

When

.
When

Two functionals which play an important role in the evolution of solutions of (

E(u)=\int\left\{\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{(p+2)}|u|^{p+2}\right\}d\mathbf{x}

We can now state the well-posedness result from
[

Theorem 1.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

Suppose

0\leq k\leq d-1

)

Solutions

We define a

of (

,

then

is a solution of (

-\Delta R+\omega R=|R|^{p}R.

(1.7)

(Here, as throughout the paper, we use the convention that the notation

stands for

if

if

Furthermore,

We say that a function

Lemma 1.3
.

Suppose

, and suppose

, where

is as defined in (

). Then equation (

)

must be a translate of

.

Proof.

See Theorems 8.1.4, 8.1.5, and 8.1.6 of
[

]
. (Note that although the assertion that every nontrivial nonnegative solution of (

∎

From the above considerations, we immediately deduce the following.

Lemma 1.4
.

Suppose
d\geq 1
,
0\leq k\leq d
, and
0<p<p_{c}(d)
. For each
\omega>0
and
\beta>0
there exists a nontrivial nonnegative solution
\varphi_{\omega,\beta,d,k,p}
to (
1.6
), given by

\varphi_{\omega,\beta,d,k,p}(x,y)=\omega^{1/p}Q_{d,p}(\omega^{1/2}x,\omega^{1/2}(1+\beta\omega)^{-1/2}y).

(1.9)

Every nontrivial nonnegative solution to (
1.6
) is of the form

\varphi(\mathbf{x})=\varphi_{\omega,\beta,d,k,p}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{x}_{0}),

for some
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

For brevity of notation, in what follows we will drop the subscripts

,

,

Also, in what follows, we will restrict consideration to the cases when

\displaystyle\int\left[|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\beta\omega|\nabla_{y}\varphi|^{2}\right]\ d\mathbf{x}+\omega\int|\varphi|^{2}\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\int|\varphi|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle(d-2)\int\left[|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\beta\omega|\nabla_{y}\varphi|^{2}\right]\ d\mathbf{x}+\omega d\int|\varphi|^{2}\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{2d}{p+2}\int|\varphi|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x},

from which it follows easily that if

An important functional for the study of bound-state solutions is the
action functional

S_{\omega}(u)
defined on
H^{1}

S_{\omega}(u):=E(u)+\omega M(u).

Lemma 1.5
.

Suppose
d\geq 1
,
0\leq k\leq d
,
p>0
,
\beta>0
, and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
.
A function

is a critical point for the functional

if and only if it is a bound-state profile for (

) with phase speed

Proof.

The gradient of

S_{\omega}^{\prime}(u)=E^{\prime}(u)+\omega M^{\prime}(u)=(-\Delta u-|u|^{p}u)+\omega(u-\beta\Delta_{y}u).

Therefore

is a critical point of

if and only if it satisfies equation (

1.4
) with phase speed
\omega
.
∎

Among the bound-state profiles of (
1.4

I_{m}=\inf\left\{E(u):M(u)=m\right\}

Let

We say that a set

), involves an analysis of the spectrum of the second variation
S_{\omega}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{\omega})
of the operator
S_{\omega}
at
\varphi_{\omega}
. A guide to generalizing Weinstein’s argument from the case

to the case

is provided by Theorem 3.3 of
[
15
]
, which suggests the correct form of the key Lemma

used here. It requires some work, however, to verify that the framework of
[
15
,
16
]
applies in our case. Our exposition follows the lines of that given in
[
20
]
for the case
k=0
.

When
k=0
, it is easy to see by a scaling argument that (
1.14
) holds for all
\omega>0
when
p<4/d
, and does not hold for any
\omega>0
when
p\geq 4/d
. For
k\geq 1
we can say the following.

Corollary 1.10
.

Suppose
d\geq 1
and
1\leq k\leq d
.

1.

If
0<p<4/d
, then
\mathcal{P}_{\omega}
is stable for every
\omega>0
.

2.

If
\displaystyle\frac{4}{d}<p<\min\left(\frac{4}{d-k},p_{c}(d)\right)
, then there exists
\omega_{0}
such that

m^{\prime}(\omega)>0
for every
\omega>\omega_{0}
and
m^{\prime}(\omega)<0
for every
\omega\in(0,\omega_{0})
.
Hence
\mathcal{P}_{\omega}
is orbitally stable for every
\omega>\omega_{0}
.

3.

If
\displaystyle\min\left(\frac{4}{d-k},p_{c}(d)\right)<p<p_{c}(d)
, then
m^{\prime}(\omega)<0
for
\omega

sufficiently large and for
\omega
sufficiently near zero.

Proof.

From Lemma
1.4
, we see that, if
k\geq 1
, then
m(\omega)=f(\beta\omega)
, where
f(x)
is defined for
x>0
by

f(x)=x^{a}(1+x)^{b}(A+Bx),

where
a=(4-pd)/2p
,
b=(k-2)/2
,
A=\frac{1}{2}\beta^{-a}\left|Q_{d,p}\right|_{2}^{2}
, and
B=A+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{-a}\left|\nabla_{y}Q_{d,p}\right|_{2}^{2}
. We have

f^{\prime}(x)=x^{a-1}(1+x)^{b-1}(c_{0}+c_{1}x+c_{2}x^{2}),

where
c_{0}=aA
,
c_{1}=a(A+B)+bA+B
, and
c_{2}=(a+b+1)B
.

If
0<p<4/d
, then
c_{0}
,
c_{1}
, and
c_{2}
are all positive, so
f^{\prime}(x)
is positive for all
x>0
, and therefore
m^{\prime}(\omega)>0

for all
\omega>0
, since
m^{\prime}(\omega)=\beta f^{\prime}(\beta\omega)
. If
4/d<p<\min(4/(d-k),p_{c}(d))
, then
c_{0}<0
and
c_{2}>0
,
so
f^{\prime}(x)
has exactly one zero
x_{0}
on
(0,\infty)
, with
f^{\prime}(x)<0
for
x<x_{0}
and
f^{\prime}(x)>0
for
x>x_{0}
, which proves part 2.
Finally, if
\min(4/(d-k),p_{c}(d))<p<p_{c}(d)
, then
c_{0}<0
and
c_{2}<0
, so
f^{\prime}(x)<0
at least for
x
near 0 and for
x
large.
∎

Remark.
In case
\displaystyle\min\left(\frac{4}{d-k},p_{c}(d)\right)<p<p_{c}(d)
, we do not yet know whether
m^{\prime}(\omega)<0
for all
\omega>0
.

Theorem
1.9
gives a sharper criterion for stability than Theorem
1.8
: there exist bound-state
solutions of (
1.4
) which are orbitally stable, but which are not ground states, and whose profiles
\varphi_{\omega}

satisfy
E(\varphi_{\omega})>0
. For example, Lemma
4.6
below shows that when

,

, and

,
and
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are given by (

) and (

), then for all
\omega
such
that
\omega_{1}<\omega<\omega_{2}
, one has that

and

. Therefore the bound
state with profile
\varphi_{\omega}
is not a ground state (by Lemma

), but is orbitally stable

1.9
. Such a bound state is a local minimum for the variational problem of minimizing

.

In the case

. Notice that if

.

with

For

Proof of Lemma
1.6
.
Given
v\in G_{m}
, define
f\in H^{1}
by

. Then
|f|=|v|
, and by Theorem 6.17 of
[
21
]
, we have
|\nabla f|=|\nabla v|
and
|\nabla_{y}f|=|\nabla_{y}v|
. Therefore
f\in G_{m}
also, and so

satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation

E^{\prime}(f)+\omega M^{\prime}(f)=(-\Delta f-|f|^{p}f)+\omega(f-\beta\Delta_{y}f)=0

(2.8)

for some constant
\omega\in\mathbb{C}
.

Multiplying (
2.8
) by the complex conjugate
\bar{f}
of
f
, and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we obtain

\int(|\nabla f|^{2}-|f|^{p+2})\ d\mathbf{x}=-\omega\int(|f|^{2}+\beta|\nabla_{y}f|^{2})\ d\mathbf{x},

and since

0>I_{m}=\int\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla f|^{2}-\frac{1}{p+2}\int|f|^{p+2}\right)\ d\mathbf{x}\geq\frac{1}{2}\int\left(|\nabla f|^{2}-|f|^{p+2}\right)\ d\mathbf{x},

it follows that
\omega
is real and
\omega>0
.

Define
g\in H^{1}
by

f(\mathbf{x})=f(x,y)=g(x,(1+\beta\omega)^{-1/2}y).

(If
k=0
we just define
g=f
.) Then

-\Delta g+\omega g=|g|^{p}g

(2.9)

on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By virtue of satisfying (
2.9
), we know from Theorem 8.1.1 of
[
8
]
that
g
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and that
\displaystyle\lim_{|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty}g(\mathbf{x})=0
.

We now apply Lemma 8.1.12 of
[
8
]
to the functions
h_{1}=\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}g
and
h_{2}=\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}g
, which are related to
v
by

\displaystyle|\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}v(x,y)|

\displaystyle=h_{1}(x,(1+\beta\omega)^{-1/2}y)

\displaystyle|\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}v(x,y)|

\displaystyle=h_{2}(x,(1+\beta\omega)^{-1/2}y),

and which satisfy the equations

\displaystyle-\Delta h_{1}+\omega h_{1}

\displaystyle=|g|^{p}h_{1}

\displaystyle-\Delta h_{2}+\omega h_{2}

\displaystyle=|g|^{p}h_{2}.

Note that the function
a=|g|^{p}
satisfies
\displaystyle\lim_{|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty}a(\mathbf{x})=0
, and multiplying (
2.9
) by

and integrating over

gives

\int(|\nabla g|^{2}-a|g|^{2})\ d\mathbf{x}=-\omega\int|g|^{2}\ d\mathbf{x}<0.

Therefore the hypotheses of Lemma 8.1.12 of
[
8
]
are satisfied, and we can conclude that there exists a positive solution

of the equation

-\Delta u+\omega u=au,

(2.10)

and that
h_{1}=cu
and
h_{2}=du
for some nonnegative constants
c
and
d
.

Since
v\in I_{m}
, then there exists some
\tilde{\omega}\in\mathbb{C}
for which

E^{\prime}(v)+\tilde{\omega}M^{\prime}(v)=0;

(2.11)

and if we define

by

v(\mathbf{x})=v(x,y)=w(x,(1+\beta\tilde{\omega})^{-1/2}y),

then we have that

-\Delta w+\tilde{\omega}w=|w|^{p}w

(2.12)

on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Again using Theorem 8.1.1 of
[
8
]
, we have that
w
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so
q_{1}=\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}w
and
q_{2}=\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}w
are continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as well. But
|q_{1}|=h_{1}
and
|q_{2}|=h_{2}
, and
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are both either everywhere positive on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, or everywhere zero on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so it follows that
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
cannot change sign on

. We thus have that
q_{1}=\tilde{c}u
and

. From (

In particular, comparing (

.

We have now shown that

. Choosing

, and

,

The following lemma is a simple example of what is called an “inverse Sobolev inequality” (cf. Lemma 4.9 of
[
18
]
for a more sophisticated example). Although the lemma is well-known, we are not aware of a convenient reference for it, so we include a proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
0<p<p_{c}(d)
. For every
B>0
and every
\delta>0
there exists
\eta>0
such that if
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|f\|_{1}\leq B
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x}>\delta
, then

\sup_{\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{B_{1}(\mathbf{x}_{0})}|f|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x}\geq\eta.

(3.6)

Proof.

Let
\{Q_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of cubes of side length
2/\sqrt{d}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose union is all of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and whose interiors are disjoint. For
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{Q_{n}}\left(|f|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{2}\right)\ d\mathbf{x}=\|f\|_{1}^{2}\leq B^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{B^{2}}{|f|_{p+2}^{p+2}}\int_{Q_{n}}|f|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x},

where
|f|_{p+2}
denotes
\|f\|_{L^{p+2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. It follows that there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\int_{Q_{n_{0}}}\left(|f|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{2}\right)\ d\mathbf{x}\leq\frac{B^{2}}{|f|_{p+2}^{p+2}}\int_{Q_{n_{0}}}|f|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x}.

On the other hand, by our assumption on
p
and Theorem
2.1
, there exists
C>0
such that

\left(\int_{Q_{n}}|f|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x}\right)^{1/(p+2)}\leq C\left(\int_{Q_{n}}\left(|f|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{2}\right)\ d\mathbf{x}\right)^{1/2}

for all
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore

\left(\int_{Q_{n_{0}}}|f|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x}\right)^{1/(p+2)}\leq\frac{CB}{\delta^{1/2}}\left(\int_{Q_{n_{0}}}|f|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x}\right)^{1/2},

from which it follows that

\int_{Q_{n_{0}}}|f|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x}\geq\eta,

where
\eta=(\frac{\delta^{1/2}}{CB})^{2+(4/p)}
.
This implies (
3.6
), since
Q_{n_{0}}
is contained in a ball of radius 1.
∎

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\theta_{n}=1
and
M(v_{n})=m
for all
n
in
\mathbb{N}
. Since
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}E(v_{n})=I_{m}
, then
\{v_{n}\}
is a minimizing sequence for

, and therefore, by what has already been shown, there exists a subsequence

such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\inf_{v\in G_{m}}\|v_{n_{j}}-v\|_{1}=0.

But from (

) it follows that for

sufficiently large we have

\inf_{v\in G_{m}}\|v_{n_{j}}-v\|_{1}\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2},

a contradiction. ∎

4
Existence of stable ground states

In this section, for each
d\geq 1
and
k\in\{0,1,2,\dots,d\}
we determine ranges of the variables
m
and
p
for which the assumption
-\infty<I_{m}<0
of Theorem
1.8
is satisfied, and therefore stable ground states exist. The results are summarized in Theorems
4.11
and
4.12
at the end of the section.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
d\geq 1
.

1.

If
k=d
and
0<p<p_{c}(d)
, then
I_{m}>-\infty
for all
m>0
.

2.

If
0\leq k\leq d
and
0<p<4/d
, then
I_{m}>-\infty
for all
m>0
.

3.

If
d\geq 2
,
1\leq k\leq d-1
, and
\displaystyle 0<p<\min\left(\frac{4}{d-k},p_{c}(d)\right)
, then
I_{m}>-\infty
for all
m>0
.

Proof.

Under the assumptions of part 1, we have from Theorem
2.1
that

E(u)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+2}|u|_{p+2}^{p+2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{1}^{2}-C\|u\|_{1}^{p+2},

(4.1)

where
C
depends on
p
and
d
but is independent of
u
. If
k=d
and
m>0
, then from (
1.5
) and
M(u)=m
we get that
\|u\|_{1}\leq B=(2m(1+\beta^{-2}))^{1/2}<\infty
. Therefore from (
4.1
) we have

I_{m}\geq-CB^{p+2}>-\infty
.

To prove part 2, we first note that it follows from Theorem
2.1
that

E(u)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{1}^{2}-C|u|_{2}^{p+2-pd/2}\|u\|_{1}^{pd/2}.

Now if
M(u)=m
, then
|u|_{2}^{2}\leq 2M(u)\leq 2m
, so

E(u)\geq f\left(\|u\|_{1}\right),

(4.2)

where
f(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-C(2m)^{b}x^{pd/2}
and
b=\frac{1}{2}(p+2-pd/2)\geq 0
.
If
0<p<4/d
, then
pd/2<2
, and so the function
f(x)
has a finite minimum value on
\{0\leq x<\infty\}
.
From (
4.2
) we have that
I_{m}
is greater than or equal to this minimum value, so
I_{m}>-\infty
.

To prove part 3, we note that under the stated assumptions, by the anisotropic Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in Theorem
2.7
, there exists
C>0
such that (
2.6
) holds for all
u\in H^{1}
, with
\mu_{0}
and
\mu
given by (
2.7
). Suppose
M(u)=m
. Then

\displaystyle|u_{x_{j}}|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{1}\qquad\quad\ \text{(for $j=1,\dots,d-k$),}

\displaystyle|u_{y_{j}}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\beta}M(u)\leq\frac{2m}{\beta}\quad\text{(for $j=1,\dots,k$),}

\displaystyle|u|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2M(u)\leq 2m,

so it follows from (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) that

\int|u|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x}\leq C\|u\|_{1}^{p(d-k)/2},

where
C
depends on
d
,
p
,
\beta
, and
M(u)=m
but is otherwise independent of
u
.

Now we can argue as in the proof of part 2. We write

\displaystyle E(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla u|^{2}\ d\mathbf{x}-\frac{1}{p+2}\int|u|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|u|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+2}\int|u|^{p+2}\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{1}^{2}-m-C\|u\|_{1}^{p(d-k)/2}

\displaystyle=g\left(\|u\|_{1}\right),

where
g(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-m-Cx^{p(d-k)/2}
.
Since
0<p<4/(d-k)
, then
p(d-k)/2<2
, and so the function
g(x)
has a finite minimum value on
[0,\infty)
, allowing us to conclude that
I_{m}>-\infty
.
∎

We pause to note an interesting consequence of Lemma
4.1
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Suppose
d\geq 1
,
0\leq k\leq d
,
\beta>0
,
0<p<\min\left(\frac{4}{d-k},p_{c}(d)\right)
, and
m>0
.
Let
U_{m}=\{\omega>0:M(\varphi_{\omega})=m\}
. If there exists
\omega\in U_{m}
such that

, then
-\infty<I_{m}<0
and
G_{m}
is stable. Conversely, if
-\infty<I_{m}<0
, then there exists
\omega\in U_{m}
such that
E(\varphi_{\omega})<0
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists
\omega\in U_{m}
such that
E(\varphi_{\omega})<0
. Since
\omega\in U_{m}
, then
I_{m}\leq E(\varphi_{\omega})
by definition of
I_{m}
. Hence
I_{m}<0
, and
I_{m}>-\infty
by Lemma
4.1
. So
G_{m}
is stable by Theorem
1.8
.

Conversely, suppose
-\infty<I_{m}<0
. Then by Theorem
1.8
,
G_{m}
is nonempty, and by Theorem
1.6
, there exists some
\omega\in U_{m}
such that
\varphi_{\omega}\in G_{m}
, with

.
∎

We remark that the condition that

for some
\omega\in U_{m}
is not necessary for the stability of

. Indeed, as we see below in the proof of Lemma
4.6
, if
d=1
,
k=1
,
p>4
,
\beta>0
,
\omega_{2}=(p-4)/4\beta
, and
m=M(\varphi_{\omega_{2}})
, then
U_{m}
consists of exactly two numbers
\omega_{2}
and
\omega_{3}
, for which we have
E(\varphi_{\omega_{2}})=0
and
E(\varphi_{\omega_{3}})>0
. Since

, then

. But as shown in the proof of Lemma
4.6
, we have

at

.

Lemma 4.3

Suppose

If

Proof.

Suppose first that
1\leq k\leq d

Fix

such that
u
is not identically zero. For each
\alpha>0
and

(4.3)

For fixed

\alpha^{2}=f(\theta):=\frac{m\theta^{d}}{A+B\theta^{2}},

(4.4)

where

\alpha

.

In case

.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
d\geq 1
,
k=0
, and
p>4/d
. Then

for all

.

Proof.

For any given

such that

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose
d=1
and
k=1
. For each

Proof.

When

for all

where
\displaystyle\theta=\sqrt{\frac{\omega}{1+\beta\omega}}

\displaystyle M(\varphi_{\omega})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\omega(p+2)}{2}\right)^{2/p}\frac{2C_{p}}{p\theta}\left[1+\left(\frac{\beta\omega p}{(1+\beta\omega)(4+p)}\right)\right]

When

Lemma 4.8

Lemma 4.9

Lemma 4.10

Suppose

Proof.

From (

\displaystyle M(\varphi_{\omega})

\displaystyle=\omega^{(4-dp)/(2p)}(1+\beta\omega)^{k/2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int R^{2}(\mathbf{x})\ d\mathbf{x}\right)

In what follows, we assume that

For each

Lemma 5.1
.

There exist
\epsilon>0
and two functions

Furthermore, the function

satisfies

(w,i\varphi)_{2}=0

(5.2)

and

\left(w,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}\right)_{2}=0\quad\text{for all $j\in\{1,\dots,d\}$ }.

(5.3)

Proof.

This is proved in Lemma 6.2 of
[
20
]
; for the reader’s convenience we sketch the proof here. Define
\varphi(\theta,\mathbf{y},v)=|e^{i\theta}v(\cdot-\mathbf{y})-\varphi|_{2}
, and note that for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, if
v\in U_{\epsilon}
then there exists
(\tilde{\theta},\tilde{\mathbf{y}})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\varphi(\tilde{\theta},\tilde{\mathbf{y}},v)=\inf_{(\theta,{\mathbf{y}})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(\theta,\mathbf{y},v).

Indeed, suppose
v\in U_{\epsilon}
, and let
(\theta_{n},\mathbf{y}_{n})
be such that

. If
\epsilon
is sufficiently small, then
\varphi(\theta,\mathbf{y},v)>\epsilon

\left\{(\theta_{n},\mathbf{y}_{n})\right\}
must be bounded in

. Hence some subsequence of

for

, and we must have
F(\tilde{\theta},\tilde{\mathbf{y}},v)=0
, where

is the derivative of

with respect to

.

By the Implicit Function Theorem, there exist
\delta>0
and
\epsilon>0
such that for each

. From the equation

]

) holds as well.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
\omega>0

We follow the lines of the proof of Lemma 4.19 of
[

Let

From equation (

\displaystyle S(v)-S(\varphi)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\langle S^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)z,z\rangle+o(\|w-\varphi\|_{1}^{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{2}\|z\|_{1}^{2}+o\left(\|w-\varphi\|_{1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{4}\|w-\varphi\|_{1}^{2}

for
\|w-\varphi\|_{1}
sufficiently small.

Hence, if
M(v)=M(\varphi)
,
v\in U_{\epsilon}
, and
\epsilon
is sufficiently small, we can conclude that

E(v)-E(\varphi)=S(v)-S(\varphi)\geq\frac{\delta}{4}\|w-\varphi\|_{1}^{2},

from which the statement of the Lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.
Theorem
1.9
follows easily from Lemma
5.5
, by the following (standard) argument, which we paraphrase from
[
22
]
. Suppose the assertion of the theorem is false; then there exist a number
\epsilon>0
and sequences
\left\{u_{0,n}\right\}
in
X_{k,1}
,
\left\{\theta_{n}\right\}
in
\mathbb{R}
,
\left\{\mathbf{y}_{n}\right\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\left\{t_{n}\right\}
in
\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|e^{i\theta_{n}}u_{0,n}(\cdot-\mathbf{y}_{n})-\varphi\|_{H^{1}}=0,

(5.27)

and for all
n\geq 1
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|e^{i\theta}u_{n}(\cdot-\mathbf{y},t_{n})-\varphi\|_{H^{1}}\geq\epsilon,

(5.28)

where
u_{n}(\mathbf{x},t)
is the solution of (
1.4
) with initial data
u_{n}(\mathbf{x},0)=u_{0,n}(\mathbf{x})
.

From (
5.27
) we deduce that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}E(u_{0,n})=E(\varphi)
and
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}M(u_{0,n})=M(\varphi)
, and since
E
and
M
are conserved functionals for the flow of (
1.4
), it follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}E(u_{n}(\cdot,t_{n}))=E(\varphi)\quad\text{and $\lim_{n\to\infty}M(u_{n}(\cdot,t_{n}))=M(\varphi)$}.

For
n\geq 1
, define

v_{n}(\mathbf{x})=\left(\frac{M(\varphi)}{M(u_{n}(\cdot,t_{n}))}\right)^{1/2}u_{n}(\mathbf{x},t_{n});

then we have that
M(v_{n})=M(u_{n}(\cdot,t_{n}))
for all
n
, while
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}E(v_{n})=E(\varphi)
and
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}-u_{n}(\cdot,t_{n})\|_{H^{1}}=0
. Choose
N_{0}
such that
\|v_{n}-u_{n}(\cdot,t_{n})\|_{H^{1}}\leq\epsilon/2
for all
n\geq N_{0}
. For every
n\geq N_{0}
, every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and every
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|e^{i\theta}v_{n}(\cdot-\mathbf{y})-\varphi\right\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|e^{i\theta}u_{n}(\cdot-\mathbf{y},t_{n})-\varphi\right\|_{H^{1}}-\left\|e^{i\theta}\left[u_{n}(\cdot-\mathbf{y},t_{n})-v_{n}(\cdot-\mathbf{y})\right]\right\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon-\|v_{n}-u_{n}(\cdot,t_{n})\|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}

by (
5.28
), which shows that

\inf_{\theta\in\mathbb{R},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|e^{i\theta}v_{n}(\cdot-\mathbf{y})-\varphi\right\|_{H^{1}}\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}

for
n\geq N_{0}
. But on the other hand, from Lemma
5.5
we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\theta\in\mathbb{R},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|e^{i\theta}v_{n}(\cdot-\mathbf{y})-\varphi\right\|_{H^{1}}=0,

a contradiction. ∎

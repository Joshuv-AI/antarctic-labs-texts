Abstract




Neural Radiance Fields
[
3
]
employ simple volume rendering as a way to overcome the challenges of differentiating through ray-triangle intersections by leveraging a probabilistic notion of visibility.
This is achieved by assuming the scene is composed by a cloud of light-emitting particles whose density changes in space (in the terminology of physically-based rendering, this would be described as a volume with absorption and emission but no scattering

In what follows, for the sake of exposition simplicity, and without loss of generality, we assume the emitted light
does not

Transmittance

Let the density field
\sigma(\mathbf{x})
, with
\mathbf{x}{\in}\mathbb{R}^{3}
indicate the differential likelihood of a ray hitting a particle (i.e. the probability of hitting a particle while travelling an infinitesimal distance).
We reparameterize the density along a given ray
\mathbf{r}{=}(\mathbf{o},\mathbf{d})

as a scalar function
\sigma(t)
, since any point
\mathbf{x}
along the ray can be written as
\mathbf{r}(t){=}\mathbf{o}{+}t\mathbf{d}
.
Density is closely tied to the transmittance function
\mathcal{T}(t)
, which indicates the probability of a ray traveling over the interval
[0,t)
without hitting any particles.
Then the probability
\mathcal{T}(t{+}dt)
of
not
hitting a particle when taking a differential step
dt
is equal to
\mathcal{T}(t)
, the likelihood of the ray reaching
t
, times
(1-dt\cdot\sigma(t))
, the probability of not hitting anything during the step:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(t+dt)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(t)\cdot(1-dt\cdot\sigma(t))

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{T}(t+dt)-\mathcal{T}(t)}{dt}\equiv

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\prime}(t)=-\mathcal{T}(t)\cdot\sigma(t)

(2)

This is a classical differential equation that can be solved as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{T}(t)\cdot\sigma(t)

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathcal{T}(t)}

\displaystyle=-\sigma(t)

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathcal{T}(t)}\;dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{a}^{b}\sigma(t)\;dt

(5)

\displaystyle\left.\log\mathcal{T}(t)\right|_{a}^{b}

\displaystyle=-\int_{a}^{b}\sigma(t)\;dt

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(a\rightarrow b)\equiv\frac{\mathcal{T}(b)}{\mathcal{T}(a)}

\displaystyle=\text{exp}\left(-\int_{a}^{b}\sigma(t)\;dt\right)

(7)

where we define
\mathcal{T}(a\rightarrow b)
as the probability that the ray travels from distance
a
to
b
along the ray without hitting a particle, which is related to the previous notation by
\mathcal{T}(t)=\mathcal{T}(0\rightarrow t)
.

Probabilistic interpretation

Note that we can also interpret the function
1-\mathcal{T}(t)
(often called
opacity
) as a cumulative distribution function (CDF) indicating the probability that the ray
does
hit a particle sometime before reaching distance
t
. Then
\mathcal{T}(t)\cdot\sigma(t)
is the corresponding probability density function (PDF), giving the likelihood that the ray stops precisely at distance
t
.

Volume rendering

We can now calculate the expected value of the light emitted by the particles in the volume as the ray travels from
t{=}0
to
D
, composited on top of a background color.
Since the probability density for stopping at
t
is
\mathcal{T}(t)\cdot\sigma(t)
, the expected color is

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{C}=\int_{0}^{D}\mathcal{T}(t)\cdot\sigma(t)\cdot\mathbf{c}(t)\;dt\;+\;\mathcal{T}(D)\cdot\mathbf{c}_{\text{bg}}

(8)

where
\mathbf{c}_{\text{bg}}
is a background color that is composited with the foreground scene according to the residual transmittance
\mathcal{T}(D)
.
Without loss of generality, we omit the background term in what follows.

Homogeneous media

We can calculate the color of some homogeneous volumetric media with constant color
\mathbf{c}_{a}
and density
\sigma_{a}
over a ray segment
[a,b]
by integration:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{C}(a\rightarrow b)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\mathcal{T}(a\rightarrow t)\cdot\sigma(t)\cdot\mathbf{c}(t)\;dt

(9)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}_{a}\int_{a}^{b}\mathcal{T}(a\rightarrow t)\;dt

constant density/radiance

(10)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}_{a}\int_{a}^{b}\text{exp}\left(-\int_{a}^{t}\sigma(u)\;du\right)\;dt

substituting (
7
)

(11)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}_{a}\int_{a}^{b}\text{exp}\left(-\left.\sigma_{a}u\right|_{a}^{t}\right)\;dt

constant density (again)

(12)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}_{a}\int_{a}^{b}\text{exp}\left(-\sigma_{a}(t-a)\right)\;dt

(13)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{a}\cdot\mathbf{c}_{a}\left.\cdot\frac{\text{exp}\left(-\sigma_{a}(t-a)\right)}{-\sigma_{a}}\right|_{a}^{b}

(14)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{c}_{a}\cdot(1-\text{exp}\left(-\sigma_{a}(b-a)\right))

(15)

Transmittance is multiplicative

Note that transmittance factorizes as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(a\rightarrow c)=

\displaystyle=\text{exp}\left(-\left[\int_{a}^{b}\sigma(t)\;dt+\int_{b}^{c}\sigma(t)\;dt\right]\right)

(16)

\displaystyle=\text{exp}\left(-\int_{a}^{b}\sigma(t)\;dt\right)\text{exp}\left(-\int_{b}^{c}\sigma(t)\;dt\right)

(17)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(a\rightarrow b)\cdot\mathcal{T}(b\rightarrow c)

(18)

This also follows from the probabilistic interpretation of
\mathcal{T}
, since the probability that the ray does not hit any particles within
[a,c]
is the product of the probabilities of the two independent events that it does not hit any particles within
[a,b]
or within
[b,c]
.

Transmittance for piecewise constant data

Given a set of intervals
\{[t_{n},t_{n+1}]\}_{n=1}^{N}
with constant density
\sigma_{n}
within the
n
-th segment, and with
t_{1}{=}0
and
\delta_{n}{=}t_{n+1}{-}t_{n}
, transmittance is equal to:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{n}=\mathcal{T}(t_{n})=\mathcal{T}(0\rightarrow t_{n})

\displaystyle=\text{exp}\left(-\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\sigma(t)\;dt\right)=\text{exp}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}-\sigma_{k}\delta_{k}\right)

(19)

Volume rendering of piecewise constant data

Combining the above, we can evaluate the volume rendering integral through a medium with piecewise constant color and density:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{C}(t_{N+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathcal{T}(t)\cdot\sigma_{n}\cdot\mathbf{c}_{n}\;dt

piecewise constant

(20)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathcal{T}(0\rightarrow t_{n})\cdot\mathcal{T}(t_{n}\rightarrow t)\cdot\sigma_{n}\cdot\mathbf{c}_{n}\;dt

from (
18
)

(21)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{T}(0\rightarrow t_{n})\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathcal{T}(t_{n}\rightarrow t)\cdot\sigma_{n}\cdot\mathbf{c}_{n}\;dt

constant

(22)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{T}(0\rightarrow t_{n})\cdot(1-\text{exp}\left(-\sigma_{n}(t_{n+1}-t_{n})\right))\cdot\mathbf{c}_{n}

from (
15
)

(23)

This leads to the volume rendering equations from NeRF
[
3
, Eq.3]
:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{C}(t_{N+1})=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{T}_{n}\cdot(1-\text{exp}\left(-\sigma_{n}\delta_{n}\right))\cdot\mathbf{c}_{n}\text{, \quad where \quad}\mathcal{T}_{n}=\text{exp}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}-\sigma_{k}\delta_{k}\right)

(24)

Finally, rather than writing these expressions in terms of volumetric density, we can re-express them in terms of alpha-compositing weights
\alpha_{n}\equiv 1-\text{exp}\left(-\sigma_{n}\delta_{n}\right)
, and by noting that
\prod_{i}\exp x_{i}=\text{exp}\left(\sum_{i}x_{i}\right)
in (
19
):

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{C}(t_{N+1})=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{T}_{n}\cdot\alpha_{n}\cdot\mathbf{c}_{n}\text{, \quad where \quad}\mathcal{T}_{n}=\prod_{n=1}^{N-1}(1-\alpha_{n})

(25)

Alternate derivation

By making use of the earlier connection between CDF and PDF that
(1-\mathcal{T})^{\prime}=\mathcal{T}\sigma
, and by assuming constant color
\mathbf{c}_{a}
along an interval
[a,b]
:

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\mathcal{T}(t)\cdot\sigma(t)\cdot\mathbf{c}(t)\;dt

\displaystyle=\mathbf{c}_{a}\int_{a}^{b}(1-\mathcal{T})^{\prime}(t)\;dt

(26)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{c}_{a}\cdot(1-\mathcal{T}(t))|_{a}^{b}

(27)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{c}_{a}\cdot(\mathcal{T}(a)-\mathcal{T}(b))

(28)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{c}_{a}\cdot\mathcal{T}(a)\cdot(1-\mathcal{T}(a\rightarrow b))

(29)

Combined with constant per-interval density, this identity yields the same expression for color as (
24
).

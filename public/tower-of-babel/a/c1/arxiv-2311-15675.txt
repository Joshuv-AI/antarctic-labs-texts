Abstract
We determine the complexity of second-order HyperLTL satisfiability, finite-state satisfiability, and model-checking: All three are equivalent to truth in third-order arithmetic. We also consider two fragments of second-order HyperLTL that have been introduced with the aim to facilitate effective model-checking by restricting the sets one can quantify over. The first one restricts second-order quantification to smallest/largest sets that satisfy a guard while the second one restricts second-order quantification further to least fixed points of (first-order) HyperLTL definable functions. All three problems for the first fragment are still equivalent to truth in third-order arithmetic while satisfiability for the second fragment is Σ 1 1 \Sigma_{1}^{1} -complete, i.e., only as hard as for (first-order) HyperLTL and therefore much less complex. Finally, finite-state satisfiability and model-checking are in Σ 2 2 \Sigma_{2}^{2} and are Σ 1 1 \Sigma_{1}^{1} -hard, and thus also less complex than for full second-order HyperLTL.

The introduction of hyperlogics

Logics like

However, while expressive enough to express common information-flow properties, they are unable to express other important hyperproperties, e.g., common knowledge in multi-agent systems and asynchronous hyperproperties (witnessed by a plethora of asynchronous extensions of

In light of this situation, Beutner et al.

They show that the resulting logic,

Consider, e.g., common knowledge in multi-agent systems where each agent

We write

A property

\varphi

\varphi

This infinite closure cannot be expressed using first-order quantification over traces
[
9
]
, like the one used in
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}

The second-order quantification suggested by Beutner et al. allows us to express common knowledge, as demonstrated by the formula
\varphi_{\mathit{ck}}
, which
states that
\varphi
is common knowledge on all traces of the system (we use a simplified syntax for readability):

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathit{ck}}=\forall\pi.\,\exists X.\ \pi\in X\land\Big(\forall\pi^{\prime}\in X.\,\forall\pi^{\prime\prime}.\ \big(\bigvee\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\pi^{\prime}\sim_{i}\pi^{\prime\prime}\big)\rightarrow\pi^{\prime\prime}\in X\Big)\,\land\,\forall\pi^{\prime}\in X.\ \varphi({\pi^{\prime}})

The formula

t^{\prime\prime}
is in

\varphi

However, Beutner et al. also note that this expressiveness comes at a steep price:
model-checking
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
is highly undecidable, i.e.,
\Sigma_{1}^{1}
-hard.
Thus, their main result is a partial model-checking algorithm for a fragment of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
where second-order quantification degenerates to least fixed point computations of
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
definable functions.
Their algorithm over- and underapproximates these fixed points and then invokes a
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
model-checking algorithm on these approximations.
A prototype implementation of the algorithm is able to model-check properties capturing common knowledge, asynchronous hyperproperties, and distributed computing.

However, one question has been left open: Just how complex is
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
verification?

The class

contains the sets of natural numbers of the form

The notation

In this work, the highest order we are concerned with is three, i.e., quantification over sets of sets of natural numbers.

Our Contributions.

In this work, we determine the exact complexity of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
satisfiability, finite-state satisfiability, and model-checking, for the full logic and the two fragments introduced by Beutner et al.
[
5
]
, as well as for two variations of the semantics.

An important stepping stone for us is the investigation of the cardinality of models of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
.
It is known that every satisfiable
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
sentence has a countable model, and that some have no finite models
[
19
]
.
This restricts the order of arithmetic that can be simulated in
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
and explains in particular the
\Sigma_{1}^{1}
-completeness of
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
satisfiability
[
20
]
.
We show that (unsurprisingly) second-order quantification allows to write formulas that only have uncountable models by generalizing the lower bound construction of
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
to
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
.
Note that the cardinality of the continuum is a trivial upper bound on the size of models, as they are sets of traces.

With this tool at hand, we are able to show that
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
satisfiability is equivalent to truth in third-order arithmetic, i.e., much harder than
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
satisfiability.
This increase in complexity is not surprising, as second-order quantification can be expected to increase the complexity considerably.
But what might be surprising at first glance is that the problem is not
\Sigma_{1}^{2}
-complete, i.e., at the same position of the third hierarchy that
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
satisfiability occupies in one full hierarchy below (see Figure
1
). However, arbitrary second-order trace quantification corresponds to arbitrary quantification over type 2 objects, which allows to capture the full third hierarchy.
Furthermore, we also show that
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
finite-state satisfiability is equivalent to truth in third-order arithmetic, and therefore as hard as general satisfiability.
This should be contrasted with the situation for
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
described above, where finite-state satisfiability is
\Sigma_{1}^{0}
-complete (i.e., recursively enumerable) and thus much simpler than general satisfiability, which is
\Sigma_{1}^{1}
-complete.

Finally, our techniques for
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
satisfiability also shed light on the exact complexity of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
model-checking, which we show to be equivalent to truth in third-order arithmetic as well, i.e., all three problems we consider have the same complexity. In particular, this increases the lower bound on
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
model-checking from
\Sigma_{1}^{1}
to truth in third-order arithmetic.
Again, this has be contrasted with the situation for
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
, where model-checking is decidable, albeit
Tower
-complete
[
34
,
32
]
.

So, quantification over arbitrary sets of traces makes verification very hard.
However, Beutner et al.
[
5
]
noticed that many of the applications of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
described above do not require full second-order quantification, but can be expressed with restricted forms of second-order quantification.
To capture this, they first restrict second-order quantification to smallest/largest sets satisfying a guard (obtaining the fragment
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}

As an example, consider again
\varphi_{\mathit{ck}}

\displaystyle\forall\pi^{\prime}\in X.\,\forall\pi^{\prime\prime}.\ \big(\bigvee\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\pi^{\prime}\sim_{i}\pi^{\prime\prime}\big)\rightarrow\pi^{\prime\prime}\in X

defines a condition on what traces have to be in the set

X

X

This minimal set is exactly the least fixed point of the operator induced by the formula above.
Similar behavior is exhibited by many other applications of the logic, which gives the motivation to explore the fragment
\mathrm{lfp\text{-}{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}}
.

Nevertheless, we show that
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}
retains the same complexity as
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
, i.e., all three problems are still equivalent to truth in third-order arithmetic: Just restricting to guarded second-order quantification does not decrease the complexity.

For all results mentioned so far, it is irrelevant whether we allow second-order quantifiers to range over sets of traces that may contain traces that are not in the model (standard semantics) or whether we restrict these quantifiers to subsets of the model (closed-world semantics).
But if we consider
\mathrm{lfp\text{-}{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}}
satisfiability under closed-world semantics, the complexity finally decreases to
\Sigma_{1}^{1}
-completeness.
Stated differently, one can add least fixed points of
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
definable operators to
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
without increasing the complexity of the satisfiability problem.
Finally, for
\mathrm{lfp\text{-}{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}}
finite-state satisfiability and model-checking, we prove
\Sigma_{2}^{2}
-membership and
\Sigma_{1}^{1}
lower bounds for both semantics, thereby confining the complexity to the second level of the third hierarchy.

Preliminaries

We denote the nonnegative integers by
\mathbb{N}
.
An alphabet is a nonempty finite set.
The set of infinite words over an alphabet
\Sigma
is denoted by
\Sigma^{\omega}
.
Let
\mathrm{AP}
be a nonempty finite set of atomic propositions.
A trace over
\mathrm{AP}
is an infinite word over the alphabet
2^{\mathrm{AP}}
.
Given a subset
\mathrm{AP}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathrm{AP}
, the
\mathrm{AP}^{\prime}
-projection of a trace
t(0)t(1)t(2)\cdots
over
\mathrm{AP}
is the trace
(t(0)\cap\mathrm{AP}^{\prime})(t(1)\cap\mathrm{AP}^{\prime})(t(2)\cap\mathrm{AP}^{\prime})\cdots
over
\mathrm{AP}^{\prime}
.

A transition system
\mathfrak{T}=(V,E,I,\lambda)
consists of a finite nonempty set
V
of vertices, a set
E\subseteq V\times V
of (directed) edges, a set
I\subseteq V
of initial vertices, and a labeling
\lambda\colon V\rightarrow 2^{\mathrm{AP}}
of the vertices by sets of atomic propositions.
We assume that every vertex has at least one outgoing edge.
A path
\rho
through
\mathfrak{T}
is an infinite sequence
\rho(0)\rho(1)\rho(2)\cdots
of vertices with
\rho(0)\in I
and
(\rho(n),\rho(n+1))\in E
for every
n\geq 0
.
The trace of
\rho
is defined as
\lambda(\rho)=\lambda(\rho(0))\lambda(\rho(1))\lambda(\rho(2))\cdots
.
The set of traces of
\mathfrak{T}
is
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathfrak{T})=\{\lambda(\rho)\mid\rho\text{ is a path through $\mathfrak{T}$}\}
.

\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
.

Let
\mathcal{V}_{1}
be a set of first-order trace variables (i.e., ranging over traces) and
\mathcal{V}_{2}
be a set of second-order trace variables (i.e., ranging over sets of traces) such that
\mathcal{V}_{1}\cap\mathcal{V}_{2}=\emptyset
.
We typically use
\pi
(possibly with decorations) to denote first-order variables and
X,Y,Z
(possibly with decorations) to denote second-order variables.
Also, we assume the existence of two distinguished second-order variables
X_{a},X_{d}\in\mathcal{V}_{2}
such that
X_{a}
refers to the set
(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}
of all traces, and
X_{d}
refers to the universe of discourse (the set of traces the formula is evaluated over).

The formulas of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
are given by the grammar

\displaystyle\varphi{}\mathop{::=}{}\exists X.\ \varphi\mid\forall X.\ \varphi\mid\exists\pi\in X.\ \varphi\mid\forall\pi\in X.\ \varphi\mid\psi\quad\quad\psi{}\mathop{::=}{}\texttt{p}_{\pi}\mid\neg\psi\mid\psi\vee\psi\mid\mathop{\mathbf{X}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\psi\mid\psi\U\psi

where
p
ranges over
\mathrm{AP}
,
\pi
ranges over
\mathcal{V}_{1}
,
X
ranges over
\mathcal{V}_{2}
, and
\mathop{\mathbf{X}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits
(next) and
\U
(until) are temporal operators. Conjunction (
\wedge
), exclusive disjunction
(\oplus)
, implication (
\rightarrow
), and equivalence
(\leftrightarrow)
are defined as usual, and the temporal operators eventually (
\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits
) and always (
\mathop{\mathbf{G}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits
) are derived as
\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\psi=\neg\psi\U\psi
and
\mathop{\mathbf{G}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\psi=\neg\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\neg\psi
. We measure the size of a formula by its number of distinct subformulas.

The semantics of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
is defined with respect to a variable assignment, i.e., a partial mapping
\Pi\colon\mathcal{V}_{1}\cup\mathcal{V}_{2}\rightarrow(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}\cup 2^{(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}}

such that

•

if
\Pi(\pi)
for
\pi\in\mathcal{V}_{1}
is defined, then
\Pi(\pi)\in(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}
and

•

if
\Pi(X)
for
X\in\mathcal{V}_{2}
is defined, then
\Pi(X)\in 2^{(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}}
.

Given a variable assignment
\Pi
, a variable
\pi\in\mathcal{V}_{1}
, and a trace
t
, we denote by
\Pi[\pi\mapsto t]
the assignment that coincides with
\Pi
on all variables but
\pi
, which is mapped to
t
.
Similarly, for a variable
X\in\mathcal{V}_{2}
, and a set
T
of traces,
\Pi[X\mapsto T]
is the assignment that coincides with
\Pi
everywhere but
X
, which is mapped to
T
.
Furthermore,
\Pi[j,\infty)
denotes the variable assignment mapping every
\pi\in\mathcal{V}_{1}
in
\Pi
’s domain to
\Pi(\pi)(j)\Pi(\pi)(j+1)\Pi(\pi)(j+2)\cdots
, the suffix of
\Pi(\pi)
starting at position
j
(the assignment of variables
X\in\mathcal{V}_{2}
is not updated).

For a variable assignment
\Pi
we define

•

\Pi\models\texttt{p}_{\pi}
if
\texttt{p}\in\Pi(\pi)(0)
,

•

\Pi\models\neg\psi
if
\Pi\not\models\psi
,

•

\Pi\models\psi_{1}\vee\psi_{2}
if
\Pi\models\psi_{1}
or
\Pi\models\psi_{2}
,

•

\Pi\models\mathop{\mathbf{X}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\psi
if
\Pi[1,\infty)\models\psi
,

•

\Pi\models\psi_{1}\U\psi_{2}
if there is a
j\geq 0
such that
\Pi[j,\infty)\models\psi_{2}
and for all
0\leq j^{\prime}<j
we have
\Pi[j^{\prime},\infty)\models\psi_{1}
,

•

\Pi\models\exists\pi\in X.\ \varphi
if there exists a trace
t\in\Pi(X)
such that
\Pi[\pi\mapsto t]\models\varphi
,

•

\Pi\models\forall\pi\in X.\ \varphi
if for all traces
t\in\Pi(X)
we have
\Pi[\pi\mapsto t]\models\varphi
,

•

\Pi\models\exists X.\ \varphi
if there exists a set
T\subseteq(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}
such that
\Pi[X\mapsto T]\models\varphi
, and

•

\Pi\models\forall X.\ \varphi
if for all sets
T\subseteq(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}
we have
\Pi[X\mapsto T]\models\varphi
.

Throughout the paper, we use the following shorthands to simplify our formulas:

•

We write
\pi=_{\mathrm{AP}^{\prime}}\pi^{\prime}
for a set
\mathrm{AP}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathrm{AP}
for the formula
\mathop{\mathbf{G}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\bigwedge_{\texttt{p}\in\mathrm{AP}^{\prime}}(\texttt{p}_{\pi}\leftrightarrow\texttt{p}_{\pi^{\prime}})
expressing that the
\mathrm{AP}^{\prime}
-projection of
\pi
and the
\mathrm{AP}^{\prime}
-projection of
\pi^{\prime}
are equal.

•

We write
\pi\triangleright X
for the formula
\exists\pi^{\prime}\in X.\ \pi=_{\mathrm{AP}}\pi^{\prime}
expressing that the trace
\pi
is in
X
. Note that this shorthand cannot be used under the scope of temporal operators, as we require formulas to be in prenex normal form.

A sentence is a formula in which only the variables
X_{a},X_{d}
can be free.
The variable assignment with empty domain is denoted by
\Pi_{\emptyset}
.
We say that a set
T
of traces satisfies a
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentence
\varphi
, written
T\models\varphi
, if
\Pi_{\emptyset}[X_{a}\mapsto(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega},X_{d}\mapsto T]\models\varphi
, i.e., if we assign the set of all traces to
X_{a}
and the set
T
to the universe of discourse
X_{d}
.
In this case, we say that
T
is a model of
\varphi
.
A transition system
\mathfrak{T}
satisfies
\varphi
, written
\mathfrak{T}\models\varphi
, if
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathfrak{T})\models\varphi
.

Although
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentences are required to be in prenex normal form,
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentences are closed under Boolean combinations, which can easily be seen by transforming such a sentence into an equivalent one in prenex normal form (which might require renaming of variables).
Thus, in examples and proofs we will often use Boolean combinations of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentences.

Remark 1
.

\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
is the fragment of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
obtained by disallowing second-order quantification and only allowing first-order quantification of the form
\exists\pi\in X_{d}
and
\forall\pi\in X_{d}
, i.e., one can only quantify over traces from the universe of discourse.
Hence, we typically simplify our notation to
\exists\pi
and
\forall\pi
in
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
formulas.

Closed-World Semantics.

Second-order quantification in
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
as defined by Beutner et al.
[
5
]
(and introduced above) ranges over arbitrary sets of traces (not necessarily from the universe of discourse) and first-order quantification ranges over elements in such sets, i.e., (possibly) again over arbitrary traces.
To disallow this, we introduce
closed-world
semantics for
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
, only considering formulas that do not use the variable
X_{a}
. We change the semantics of set quantifiers as follows,
where the closed-world semantics of atomic propositions, Boolean connectives, temporal operators, and trace quantifiers is defined as before:

•

\Pi\models_{\mathrm{cw}}\exists X.\ \varphi
if there exists a set
T\subseteq\Pi(X_{d})
such that
\Pi[X\mapsto T]\models\varphi
, and

•

\Pi\models_{\mathrm{cw}}\forall X.\ \varphi
if for all sets
T\subseteq\Pi(X_{d})
we have
\Pi[X\mapsto T]\models\varphi
.

We say that
T\subseteq(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}
satisfies
\varphi
under closed-world semantics, if
\Pi_{\emptyset}[X_{d}\mapsto T]\models_{\mathrm{cw}}\varphi
.
Hence, under closed-world semantics, second-order quantifiers only range over subsets of the universe of discourse.
Consequently, first-order quantifiers also range over traces from the universe of discourse.

Lemma 1
.

Every
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentence
\varphi
can be translated in polynomial time (in
|\varphi|
) into a
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentence
\varphi^{\prime}
such that for all sets
T
of traces we have that
T\models_{\mathrm{cw}}\varphi
if and only if
T\models\varphi^{\prime}
(under standard semantics).

Thus, all complexity upper bounds we derive for standard semantics also hold for closed-world semantics and all lower bounds for closed-world semantics hold for standard semantics.

Remark 2
.

Let
\varphi
be an
X_{a}
-free
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentence over
\mathrm{AP}
.
We have
(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}\models\varphi
(under standard semantics) if and only if
(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}\models_{\mathrm{cw}}\varphi
, as the second-order quantifiers range in both cases over subsets of
(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}
, which implies that the trace quantifiers in both cases range over traces from
(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}
.

Arithmetic.

To capture the complexity of undecidable problems, we consider formulas of arithmetic, i.e., predicate logic with signature
(+,\cdot,<,\in)
, evaluated over the structure
(\mathbb{N},+,\cdot,<,\in)
.
A type
0
object is a natural number in
\mathbb{N}
, a type
1
object is a subset of
\mathbb{N}
, and a type
2
object is a set of subsets of
\mathbb{N}
.

Our benchmark is third-order arithmetic, i.e., predicate logic with quantification over type
0
, type
1
, and type
2
objects.
In the following, we use lower-case roman letters (possibly with decorations) for first-order variables, upper-case roman letters (possibly with decorations) for second-order variables, and upper-case calligraphic roman letters (possibly with decorations) for third-order variables.
Note that every fixed natural number is definable in first-order arithmetic, so we freely use them as syntactic sugar. Similarly, equality can be eliminated if necessary, as it can be expressed using
<
.

Truth of third-order arithmetic is the following problem: given a sentence
\varphi
of third-order arithmetic, does
(\mathbb{N},+,\cdot,<,\in)
satisfy
\varphi
?
Arithmetic formulas with a single free first-order variable define sets of natural numbers. We are interested in the classes

•

\Sigma_{1}^{1}
containing sets of the form
\{x\in\mathbb{N}\mid\exists X_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{N}.\ \cdots\exists X_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{N}.\ \psi(x,X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})\}
, where
\psi
is a formula of arithmetic with arbitrary quantification over type
0
objects (but no second-order quantifiers), and

•

\Sigma_{2}^{2}
containing sets of the following form,
where
\psi
is a formula of arithmetic with arbitrary quantification over type
0
and type
1
objects (but no third-order quantifiers):

\{x\in\mathbb{N}\mid\exists\mathcal{X}_{1}\subseteq 2^{\mathbb{N}}.\ \cdots\exists\mathcal{X}_{k}\subseteq 2^{\mathbb{N}}.\forall\mathcal{Y}_{1}\subseteq 2^{\mathbb{N}}.\ \cdots\forall\mathcal{Y}_{k^{\prime}}\subseteq 2^{\mathbb{N}}.\ \psi(x,\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{k},\mathcal{Y}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{k^{\prime}})\}.

3
The Cardinality of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
Models

In this section, we investigate the cardinality of models of satisfiable
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentences, i.e., the number of traces in the model.

We begin by stating a (trivial) upper bound, which follows from the fact that models are sets of traces.
Here,
\mathfrak{c}
denotes the cardinality of the continuum (equivalently, the cardinality of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
and of
(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}
for any finite nonempty
\mathrm{AP}
).

Proposition 1
.

Every satisfiable
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentence has a model of cardinality at most
\mathfrak{c}
.

In this section, we show that this trivial upper bound is tight.

Remark 3
.

There is a very simple, albeit equally unsatisfactory way to obtain the desired lower bound:
Consider
\forall\pi\in X_{a}.\ \pi\triangleright X_{d}
expressing that every trace in the set of all traces is also in the universe of discourse, i.e.,
(2^{\mathrm{AP}})^{\omega}
is its only model over
\mathrm{AP}
.
However, this crucially relies on the fact that
X_{a}
is, by definition, interpreted as the set of all traces.
In fact, the formula does not even use second-order quantification.

We show how to construct a sentence that has only uncountable models, and which retains that property under closed-world semantics (which in particular means it cannot use
X_{a}
).
This should be compared with
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
, where every satisfiable sentence has a countable model
[
19
]
:
Unsurprisingly, the addition of (even closed-world) second-order quantification increases the cardinality of minimal models, even without cheating.

Example 1
.

We begin by recalling a construction of Finkbeiner and Zimmermann giving a satisfiable
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
sentence
\psi
that has no finite models
[
19
]
.
The sentence intuitively posits the existence of a unique trace for every natural number
n
. Our lower bound for
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
builds upon that construction.

Fix
\mathrm{AP}=\{\texttt{x}\}
and consider the conjunction
\psi=\psi_{1}\wedge\psi_{2}\wedge\psi_{3}
of the following three formulas:

1.

\psi_{1}=\forall\pi.\ \neg\texttt{x}_{\pi}\U(\texttt{x}_{\pi}\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{X}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathbf{G}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\neg\texttt{x}_{\pi})
: every trace in a model is of the form
\emptyset^{n}\{\texttt{x}\}\emptyset^{\omega}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e., every model is a subset of
\{\emptyset^{n}\{\texttt{x}\}\emptyset^{\omega}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

2.

\psi_{2}=\exists\pi.\ \texttt{x}_{\pi}
: the trace
\emptyset^{0}\{\texttt{x}\}\emptyset^{\omega}
is in every model.

3.

\psi_{3}=\forall\pi.\ \exists\pi^{\prime}.\ \mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{x}_{\pi}\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{X}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\texttt{x}_{\pi^{\prime}})
: if
\emptyset^{n}\{\texttt{x}\}\emptyset^{\omega}
is in a model for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then also
\emptyset^{n+1}\{\texttt{x}\}\emptyset^{\omega}
.

Then,
\psi
has exactly one model (over
\mathrm{AP}
), namely
\{\emptyset^{n}\{\texttt{x}\}\emptyset^{\omega}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

A trace of the form
\emptyset^{n}\{\texttt{x}\}\emptyset^{\omega}
encodes the natural number
n
and
\psi
expresses that every model contains the encodings of all natural numbers and nothing else.
But we can of course also encode sets of natural numbers with traces as follows: a trace
t
over a set of atomic propositions containing
x
encodes the set
\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\texttt{x}\in t(n)\}
.
In the following, we show that second-order quantification allows us to express the existence of the encodings of all subsets of natural numbers by requiring that for every subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
(quantified as the set
\{\emptyset^{n}\{\texttt{x}\}\emptyset^{\omega}\mid n\in S\}
of traces) there is a trace
t
encoding
S
, which means
x
is in
t(n)
if and only if
S
contains a trace in which
x
holds at position
n
.
This equivalence can be expressed in
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
.
For technical reasons, we do not capture the equivalence directly but instead use encodings of both the natural numbers that are in
S
and the natural numbers that are not in
S
.

Theorem 1
.

There is a satisfiable
X_{a}
-free
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentence that only has models of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
(both under standard and closed-world semantics).

Proof.

We first prove that there is a satisfiable
X_{a}
-free
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentence
\varphi_{\mathit{allSets}}
whose unique model (under standard semantics) has cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
.
To this end, we fix
\mathrm{AP}=\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-},\texttt{s},\texttt{x}\}
and consider the conjunction
\varphi_{\mathit{allSets}}=\varphi_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge\varphi_{4}
of the following formulas:

•

\varphi_{0}=\forall\pi\in X_{d}.\ \bigvee_{\texttt{p}\in\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-},\texttt{s}\}}\mathop{\mathbf{G}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{p}_{\pi}\wedge\bigwedge_{\texttt{p}^{\prime}\in\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-},\texttt{s}\}\setminus\{\texttt{p}\}}\neg\texttt{p}^{\prime}_{\pi})
: In each trace of a model, one of the propositions in
\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-},\texttt{s}\}
holds at every position and the other two propositions in
\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-},\texttt{s}\}
hold at none of the positions. Consequently, we speak in the following about type
p
traces for
\texttt{p}\in\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-},\texttt{s}\}
.

•

\varphi_{1}=\forall\pi\in X_{d}.\ (\texttt{+}_{\pi}\vee\texttt{-}_{\pi})\rightarrow\neg\texttt{x}_{\pi}\U(\texttt{x}_{\pi}\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{X}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathbf{G}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\neg\texttt{x}_{\pi})
: Type
p
traces for
\texttt{p}\in\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-}\}
in the model have the form
\{\texttt{p}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{p}\}\{\texttt{p}\}^{\omega}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

•

\varphi_{2}=\bigwedge_{\texttt{p}\in\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-}\}}\exists\pi\in X_{d}.\ \texttt{p}_{\pi}\wedge\texttt{x}_{\pi}
: for both
\texttt{p}\in\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-}\}
, the type
p
trace
\{\texttt{p}\}^{0}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{p}\}\{\texttt{p}\}^{\omega}
is in every model.

•

\varphi_{3}=\bigwedge_{\texttt{p}\in\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-}\}}\forall\pi\in X_{d}.\ \exists\pi^{\prime}\in X_{d}.\ \texttt{p}_{\pi}\rightarrow(\texttt{p}_{\pi^{\prime}}\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{x}_{\pi}\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{X}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\texttt{x}_{\pi^{\prime}}))
: for both
\texttt{p}\in\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-}\}
, if the type
p
trace
\{\texttt{p}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{p}\}\{\texttt{p}\}^{\omega}
is in a model for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then also
\{\texttt{p}\}^{n+1}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{p}\}\{\texttt{p}\}^{\omega}
.

The formulas
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3}
are similar to the formulas
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3}
from Example
1
.
So, every model of
\varphi_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge\varphi_{3}
contains
\{\{\texttt{+}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{+}\}\{\texttt{+}\}^{\omega}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
\{\{\texttt{-}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{-}\}\{\texttt{-}\}^{\omega}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
as subsets, and no other type
+
or type
-
traces.

Now, consider a set
T
of traces over
\mathrm{AP}
(recall that second-order quantification ranges over arbitrary sets, not only over subsets of the universe of discourse).
We say that
T
is contradiction-free if there is no
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\{\texttt{+}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{+}\}\{\texttt{+}\}^{\omega}\in T
and
\{\texttt{-}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{-}\}\{\texttt{-}\}^{\omega}\in T
.
Furthermore, a trace
t
over
\mathrm{AP}
is consistent with a contradiction-free
T
if

(C1)

\{\texttt{+}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{+}\}\{\texttt{+}\}^{\omega}\in T
implies
\texttt{x}\in t(n)
and

(C2)

\{\texttt{-}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{-}\}\{\texttt{-}\}^{\omega}\in T
implies
\texttt{x}\notin t(n)
.

Note that
T
does not necessarily specify the truth value of
x
in every position of
t
, i.e., in those positions
n\in\mathbb{N}
where neither
\{\texttt{+}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{+}\}\{\texttt{+}\}^{\omega}
nor
\{\texttt{-}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{-}\}\{\texttt{-}\}^{\omega}
are in
T
.
Nevertheless, for every trace
t
over
\{\texttt{x}\}
there is a contradiction-free
T
such that the
\{\texttt{x}\}
-projection of every trace
t^{\prime}
over
\mathrm{AP}
that is consistent with
T
is equal to
t
.
Thus, each of the uncountably many traces over
\{\texttt{x}\}
is induced by some subset of the model.

•

Hence, we define
\varphi_{4}
as the formula

\displaystyle\forall X.\ {}

\displaystyle{}\overbrace{[\forall\pi\in X.\ \forall\pi^{\prime}\in X.\ (\texttt{+}_{\pi}\wedge\texttt{-}_{\pi^{\prime}})\rightarrow\neg\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{x}_{\pi}\wedge\texttt{x}_{\pi^{\prime}})]}^{\text{$X$ is contradiction-free}}\rightarrow

\displaystyle{}\exists\pi^{\prime\prime}\in X_{d}.\ \forall\pi^{\prime\prime\prime}\in X.\ \texttt{s}_{\pi^{\prime\prime}}\wedge\underbrace{(\texttt{+}_{\pi^{\prime\prime\prime}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{x}_{\pi^{\prime\prime\prime}}\wedge\texttt{x}_{\pi^{\prime\prime}}))}_{\text{(C1)}}\wedge\underbrace{(\texttt{-}_{\pi^{\prime\prime\prime}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{x}_{\pi^{\prime\prime\prime}}\wedge\neg\texttt{x}_{\pi^{\prime\prime}}))}_{\text{(C2)}},

expressing that for every contradiction-free set of traces
T
, there is a type
s
trace
t^{\prime\prime}
in the model (note that
\pi^{\prime\prime}
is required to be in
X_{d}
) that is consistent with
T
.

While
\varphi_{\mathit{allSets}}
is not in prenex normal form, it can easily be turned into an equivalent formula in prenex normal form (at the cost of readability).

Now, the set

\displaystyle T_{\mathit{allSets}}={}

\displaystyle{}\{\{\texttt{+}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{+}\}\{\texttt{+}\}^{\omega}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{\{\texttt{-}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{-}\}\{\texttt{-}\}^{\omega}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup

\displaystyle{}\{(t(0)\cup\{\texttt{s}\})(t(1)\cup\{\texttt{s}\})(t(2)\cup\{\texttt{s}\})\cdots\mid t\in(2^{\{\texttt{x}\}})^{\omega}\}

of traces satisfies
\varphi_{\mathit{allSets}}
.
On the other hand, every model of
\varphi_{\mathit{allSets}}
must indeed contain
T_{\mathit{allSets}}
as a subset, as
\varphi_{\mathit{allSets}}
requires the existence of all of its traces in the model.
Finally, due to
\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi_{1}
, a model (over
\mathrm{AP}
) cannot contain any traces that are not in
T_{\mathit{allSets}}
,
i.e.,
T_{\mathit{allSets}}
is the unique model of
\varphi_{\mathit{allSets}}
.

To conclude, we just remark that

\{(t(0)\cup\{\texttt{s}\})(t(1)\cup\{\texttt{s}\})(t(2)\cup\{\texttt{s}\})\cdots\mid t\in(2^{\{\texttt{x}\}})^{\omega}\}\subseteq T_{\mathit{allSets}}

has indeed cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
, as

(2^{\{\texttt{x}\}})^{\omega}
has cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
.

Finally, let us consider closed-world semantics. We can restrict the second-order quantifier in
\varphi_{4}
(the only one in
\varphi_{\mathit{allSets}}
) to subsets of the universe of discourse, as the set
T=\{\{\texttt{+}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{+}\}\{\texttt{+}\}^{\omega}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{\{\texttt{-}\}^{n}\{\texttt{x},\texttt{-}\}\{\texttt{-}\}^{\omega}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of traces (which is a subset of every model) is already
rich
enough to encode every subset of
\mathbb{N}
by an appropriate contradiction-free subset of
T
.
Thus,
\varphi_{\mathit{allSets}}
has the unique model
T_{\mathit{allSets}}
even under closed-world semantics.
∎

4
The Complexity of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
Satisfiability

A
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentence is satisfiable if it has a model. The
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
satisfiability problem asks, given a
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentence
\varphi
, whether
\varphi
is satisfiable. In this section, we determine tight bounds on the complexity of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
satisfiability and some of its variants.

Recall that in Section
3
, we encoded sets of natural numbers as traces over a set of propositions containing
x
and encoded natural numbers as singleton sets.
The proof of Theorem
1
relies on constructing a sentence that requires each of its models to encode every subset of
\mathbb{N}
by a trace in the model.
Hence, sets of traces can encode sets of sets of natural numbers, i.e., type
2
objects.

Another important ingredient in the following proof is the implementation of addition and multiplication in
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
.
Let
\mathrm{AP}_{\mathit{arith}}=\{\texttt{arg1},\texttt{arg2},\texttt{res},\texttt{add},\texttt{mult}\}
and let
T_{(+,\cdot)}
be the set of all traces
t\in(2^{\mathrm{AP}_{\mathit{arith}}})^{\omega}
such that

•

there are unique
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
with
\texttt{arg1}\in t(n_{1})
,
\texttt{arg2}\in t(n_{2})
, and
\texttt{res}\in t(n_{3})
, and

•

either
\texttt{add}\in t(n)
and
\texttt{mult}\notin t(n)
for all
n
, and
n_{1}+n_{2}=n_{3}
, or
\texttt{mult}\in t(n)
and
\texttt{add}\notin t(n)
for all
n
, and
n_{1}\cdot n_{2}=n_{3}
.

Proposition 2

(Theorem 5.5 of
[
21
]
)
.

There is a satisfiable
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
sentence
\varphi_{(+,\cdot)}
such that the
\mathrm{AP}_{\mathit{arith}}
-projection of every model of
\varphi_{(+,\cdot)}
is
T_{(+,\cdot)}
.

Combining the capability of quantifying over type
0
, type
1
, and type
2
objects and the encoding of addition and multiplication, we show that
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
and truth in third-order arithmetic have the same complexity.

Theorem 2
.

The
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
satisfiability problem is polynomial-time equivalent to truth in third-order arithmetic. The lower bound holds even for
X_{a}
-free sentences.

Proof.

We begin with the lower bound by reducing truth in third-order arithmetic to
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
satisfiability: we present a polynomial-time translation from sentences
\varphi
of third-order arithmetic to
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentences
\varphi^{\prime}
such that
(\mathbb{N},+,\cdot,<,\in)\models\varphi
if and only if
\varphi^{\prime}
is satisfiable.

Given a third-order sentence
\varphi
, we define

\varphi^{\prime}=\exists X_{\mathit{allSets}}.\ \exists X_{\mathit{arith}}.\ (\varphi_{\mathit{allSets}}[X_{d}/X_{\mathit{allSets}}]\wedge\varphi^{\prime}_{(+,\cdot)}\wedge{\mathit{hyp}}(\varphi))

where

•

\varphi_{\mathit{allSets}}[X_{d}/X_{\mathit{allSets}}]
is the
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
sentence from the proof of Theorem
1
where every occurrence of
X_{d}
is replaced by
X_{\mathit{allSets}}
and thus enforces every subset of
\mathbb{N}
to be encoded in the interpretation of
X_{\mathit{allSets}}
(as introduced in the proof of Theorem
1
),

•

\varphi^{\prime}_{(+,\cdot)}
is the
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
formula obtained from the
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
formula
\varphi_{(+,\cdot)}
by replacing each quantifier
\exists\pi
(
\forall\pi
, respectively) by
\exists\pi\in X_{\mathit{arith}}
(
\forall\pi\in X_{\mathit{arith}}
, respectively) and thus enforces that
X_{\mathit{arith}}
is interpreted by a set whose
\mathrm{AP}_{\mathit{arith}}
-projection is
T_{(+,\cdot)}
, and

where
{\mathit{hyp}}(\varphi)
is defined inductively as follows:

•

For third-order variables
\mathcal{Y}
,

{\mathit{hyp}}(\exists\mathcal{Y}.\ \psi)=\exists X_{\mathcal{Y}}.\ (\forall\pi\in X_{\mathcal{Y}}.\ \exists\pi^{\prime}\in X_{\mathit{allSets}}.\ (\pi=_{\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-},\texttt{s},\texttt{x}\}}\pi^{\prime})\wedge\texttt{s}_{\pi})\wedge{\mathit{hyp}}(\psi).

•

For third-order variables
\mathcal{Y}
,

{\mathit{hyp}}(\forall\mathcal{Y}.\ \psi)=\forall X_{\mathcal{Y}}.\ (\forall\pi\in X_{\mathcal{Y}}.\ \exists\pi^{\prime}\in X_{\mathit{allSets}}.\ (\pi=_{\{\texttt{+},\texttt{-},\texttt{s},\texttt{x}\}}\pi^{\prime})\wedge\texttt{s}_{\pi})\rightarrow{\mathit{hyp}}(\psi).

•

For second-order variables
Y
,
{\mathit{hyp}}(\exists Y.\ \psi)=\exists\pi_{Y}\in X_{\mathit{allSets}}.\ \texttt{s}_{\pi_{Y}}\wedge{\mathit{hyp}}(\psi)
.

•

For second-order variables
Y
,
{\mathit{hyp}}(\forall Y.\ \psi)=\forall\pi_{Y}\in X_{\mathit{allSets}}.\ \texttt{s}_{\pi_{Y}}\rightarrow{\mathit{hyp}}(\psi)
.

•

For first-order variables
y
,

{\mathit{hyp}}(\exists y.\ \psi)=\exists\pi_{y}\in X_{\mathit{allSets}}.\ \texttt{s}_{\pi_{y}}\wedge[(\neg\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y}})\U(\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y}}\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{X}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathbf{G}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\neg\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y}})]\wedge{\mathit{hyp}}(\psi).

•

For first-order variables
y
,

{\mathit{hyp}}(\forall y.\ \psi)=\forall\pi_{y}\in X_{\mathit{allSets}}.\ (\texttt{s}_{\pi_{y}}\wedge[(\neg\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y}})\U(\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y}}\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{X}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathbf{G}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\neg\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y}})])\rightarrow{\mathit{hyp}}(\psi).

•

{\mathit{hyp}}(\psi_{1}\vee\psi_{2})={\mathit{hyp}}(\psi_{1})\vee{\mathit{hyp}}(\psi_{2})
.

•

{\mathit{hyp}}(\neg\psi)=\neg{\mathit{hyp}}(\psi)
.

•

For second-order variables
Y
and third-order variables
\mathcal{Y}
,

{\mathit{hyp}}(Y\in\mathcal{Y})=\exists\pi\in X_{\mathcal{Y}}.\ \pi_{Y}=_{\{\texttt{x}\}}\pi.

•

For first-order variables
y
and second-order variables
Y
,
{\mathit{hyp}}(y\in Y)=\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y}}\wedge\texttt{x}_{\pi_{Y}})
.

•

For first-order variables
y,y^{\prime}
,
{\mathit{hyp}}(y<y^{\prime})=\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y}}\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{X}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y^{\prime}}})
.

•

For first-order variables
y_{1},y_{2},y
,

{\mathit{hyp}}(y_{1}+y_{2}=y)=\exists\pi\in X_{\mathit{arith}}.\ \texttt{add}_{\pi}\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{arg1}_{\pi}\wedge\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y_{1}}})\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{arg2}_{\pi}\wedge\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y_{2}}})\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{res}_{\pi}\wedge\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y}}).

•

For first-order variables
y_{1},y_{2},y
,

{\mathit{hyp}}(y_{1}\cdot y_{2}=y)=\exists\pi\in X_{\mathit{arith}}.\ \texttt{mult}_{\pi}\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{arg1}_{\pi}\wedge\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y_{1}}})\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{arg2}_{\pi}\wedge\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y_{2}}})\wedge\mathop{\mathbf{F}\vphantom{a}}\nolimits(\texttt{res}_{\pi}\wedge\texttt{x}_{\pi_{y}}).

While
\varphi^{\prime}
is not in prenex normal form, it can easily be brought into prenex normal form, as there are no quantifiers under the scope of a temporal operator.

As we are evaluating
\varphi^{\prime}
w.r.t. standard semantics and the variable
X_{d}
(interpreted with the model) does not occur in
\varphi^{\prime}
, satisfaction of
\varphi^{\prime}
is independent of the model, i.e., for all sets
T,T^{\prime}
of traces,
T\models\varphi^{\prime}
if and only if
T^{\prime}\models\varphi^{\prime}
.
So, let us fix some set
T
of traces.
An induction shows that
(\mathbb{N},+,\cdot,<,\in)
satisfies
\varphi
if and only if
T

\varphi^{\prime}

Corollary 1
.

The
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
satisfiability problem under closed-world semantics is polynomial-time equivalent to truth in third-order arithmetic.

The
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
finite-state satisfiability problem asks, given a
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}

\varphi

The

\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}

-free sentences.

Proof.

For the lower bound, we reduce truth in third-order arithmetic to the

of third-order arithmetic to pairs

of a finite transition system

and a
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}

such that

if and only if

The same claim is also true for closed-world semantics, using the same proof.

.

Every

Theorem 5
.

There is a satisfiable

Now, let us describe how we settle the complexity of

Recall that
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}

By using a sentence

Theorem 6
.

\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}
satisfiability, finite-state satisfiability, and model-checking are polynomial-time equivalent to truth in third-order arithmetic. The lower bounds hold even for

-free sentences.

Corollary 4

\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}

The Least Fixed Point Fragment of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}

We have seen that even restricting second-order quantification to smallest/largest sets that satisfy a guard formula is essentially as expressive as full
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}

Related Work

As mentioned in Section

, the complexity problems for HyperLTL were thoroughly studied
[

,
20

]
. For
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
, Beutner et al. mainly focused on the algorithmic aspects by providing model checking
[

]
and monitoring
[

]
algorithms, and did not study the respective complexity problems in depth.

Logics related to
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
are asynchronous and epistemic logics. Much research has been done regarding epistemic properties
[

,
16
,
30
,
37
]
and their relations to hyperproperties
[
9
]
. However, most of this work concerns expressiveness and decidability results (e.g.,
[
8
]
), and not complexity analysis for the undecidable fragments. This is similar for asynchronous hyperlogics
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
24
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
, where most work concerns decidability results and expressive power, but not complexity analysis.

Another related logic is
\mathrm{TeamLTL}

[
29
]
, a hyperlogic for the specification of dependence and independence. Lück
[
31
]
studied similar problems to those we study in this paper and showed that, in general, satisfiability and model checking of

[

9
Conclusion

We have investigated and settled the complexity of satisfiability, finite-state satisfiability, and model-checking for
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL}
and
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}
and (almost) settled it for
\mathrm{lfp\text{-}{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}}
.
For the former two, all three problems are equivalent to truth in third-order arithmetic, and therefore (not surprisingly) much harder than the corresponding problems for
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
, which are “only”
\Sigma_{1}^{1}
-complete,
\Sigma_{1}^{0}
-complete, and
Tower
-complete, respectively.
This shows that the addition of second-order quantification increases the already high complexity of
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
significantly.
However, for the fragment
\mathrm{lfp\text{-}{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}}
, in which second-order quantification degenerates to least fixed point computations, the complexity is much lower: satisfiability under closed-world semantics is
\Sigma_{1}^{1}
-complete and finite-state satisfiability as well as model-checking are in
\Sigma_{2}^{2}
.

Recently, Regaud and Zimmermann
[
35
]
have solved several problems left open in this work, e.g., they settled the complexity of
\mathrm{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}
with only minimality constraints or only maximality constraints, the complexity of
\mathrm{lfp\text{-}{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}}
under standard semantics, and closed the gaps in our results for
\mathrm{lfp\text{-}{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}}
finite-state satisfiability and model-checking.
Furthermore, they settled the complexity of all three decision problems we consider here for
\mathrm{Hyper\-QPTL}

[
34
]
.

Proof of Lemma

Recall that we need to show that every

Second-order quantification over subsets of the universe of discourse can easily be mimicked by guarding classical quantifiers ranging over arbitrary sets.

•

each universal second-order quantifier

and then bringing the resulting sentence into prenex normal form, which can be done as no quantifier is under the scope of a temporal operator.

Intuitively, the resulting formula expresses that

Now, a tiling

Let us continue by explaining how to check, using
\mathrm{lfp\text{-}{Hyper^{2}LTL_{\mathrm{mm}}}}

\mathit{Ti}
admits a recurrent wedge tiling.
Consider a word of the form

t=\{\tau_{0,1}\}\,\{\#\}\,\{\tau_{0,2}\}\{\tau_{1,2}\}\,\{\#\}\,\{\tau_{0,3}\}\{\tau_{1,3}\}\{\tau_{2,3}\}\,\{\#\}\,\cdots.

Whether the encoded rows are horizontally compatible can be checked by inspecting adjacent letters of

\mathit{Ti}_{0}
appear in the first column can be checked by looking for infinitely many
\#

\tau\in\mathit{Ti}_{0}

Both checks can easily be implemented in
\mathrm{LTL}
.
It remains to consider vertical compatibility.
Let

be the suffixes of

These two rows are vertically compatible if

for all
i<j

(j+1)
-th row has one more tile than the
j
-th row. Both checks are implementable in quantifier-free
\mathrm{Hyper\-LTL}
with a formula having two free variables for the two suffixes.

Intuitively, we will write a formula that expresses the existence of a trace

(tentatively encoding a tiling), then use a fixed point to compute the set of suffixes of

\mathit{Ti}_{0}
after
\#

of

of

\#

The set of suffixes is obtained by removing the first letter from a trace that is already in the fixed point, starting with
t_{c}
. The minimality constraint takes care of only suffixes of

being in the fixed point.

There is one last detail we need to explain: How to identify

j

\#

\{\tau_{0,j}\}\{\tau_{1,j}\}\cdots\{\tau_{j-1,j}\}\,\{\#\}\,\cdots

\{\tau_{1,j}\}\cdots\{\tau_{j-1,j}\}\,\{\#\}\,\cdots

are both obtained removing

However, ultimately, we are only interested in suffixes that start right after a

Hence, the proposition we use to mark traces comes in two forms:
x

\texttt{{x}}^{\texttt{\#}}
where the later signifies that this trace is obtained by removing a

.
We show that this process of taking and comparing suffixes can proceed indefinitely if and only if the trace we started with encodes a recurrent wedge tiling.

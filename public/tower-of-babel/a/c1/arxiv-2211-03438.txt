The field of moduli of a divisor on a rational curve
Giulio Bresciani

Abstract
. Let k k be a field with algebraic closure k ¯ \bar{k} and D ⊂ ℙ k ¯ 1 D\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}} a reduced, effective divisor of degree n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , write k ⁡ ( D ) k(D) for the field of moduli of D D . A. Marinatto proved that when n n is odd, or n = 4 n=4 , D D descends to a divisor on ℙ k ⁡ ( D ) 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}_{k(D)} . We analyze completely the problem of when D D descends to a divisor on a smooth, projective curve of genus 0 0 on k ⁡ ( D ) k(D) , possibly with no rational points. In particular, we study the remaining cases n ≥ 6 n\geq 6 even, and we obtain conceptual proofs of Marinatto’s results and of a theorem by B. Huggins about the field of moduli of hyperelliptic curves.



We work over a field
k
with algebraic closure
\bar{k}
. A rational curve is a smooth, projective, geometrically connected curve of genus
0
.

The field of moduli

Let
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
be an effective reduced divisor. If
k
is perfect, consider the subgroup
H\subset\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)
of elements
\sigma
such that there exists an automorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}/\bar{k}
mapping
D
to
\sigma(D)
. The
field of moduli

k(D)
of
D
is the subfield of
\bar{k}
of elements fixed by
H
. In our joint paper with A. Vistoli
[
BVb
]
we have generalized the definition of the field of moduli to arbitrary
k
; we work in this generality.

If
\bar{k}/k^{\prime}/k
is a sub-extension and there exists a rational curve
P
over
k^{\prime}
with a divisor
D_{0}\subset P
such that
(P_{\bar{k}},D_{0,\bar{k}})\simeq(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
, we say that
k^{\prime}
is a field of definition for
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
. The field of moduli is contained in every field of definition.

Marinatto’s results

Let
n
be the degree of
D
, assume
n\geq 3
. Assuming
k
perfect of characteristic
\neq 2
, A. Marinatto
[
Mar13
]
showed that, if
n
is odd or
n=4
, then
D
descends to a divisor of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k(D)}
. Hence, if
n
is odd or
n=4
,
k(D)
is a field of definition for
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
.

For every even integer
n\geq 6
, Marinatto constructed examples of divisors
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
of degree
n
which do not descend to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k(D)}
. His examples descend to a non-trivial rational curve over
k(D)
, though, hence
k(D)
is a field of definition for
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
in these cases.

Our results

Because of this, the problem of fields of definition versus fields of moduli for
n
even and
\geq 6
is still open. We solve it, and generalize Marinatto’s results for
n
odd and
n=4
to an arbitrary base field. The following theorem sums up the various results we obtain.

Theorem 1
.

Let
k
be any field,
n\geq 3
an integer and
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
a reduced, effective divisor of degree
n
. If at least one of the following conditions holds, then
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is defined over its field of moduli.

•

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1},D)
is not cyclic of degree even and prime with
\operatorname{char}k
.

•

n=4
.

•

n=6
.

•

n
is odd.

In particular, the first condition implies that
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is always defined over its field of moduli if
\operatorname{char}k=2
.

If
n=4
or
n
is odd, we can say more precisely that
D
descends to a divisor of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
over the field of moduli.

On the other hand, if
\operatorname{char}k\neq 2
,
n\geq 8
is even and the
2
-torsion
\operatorname{Br}(k)[2]
of the Brauer group of
k
is non-trivial, then we may choose
D
such that the field of moduli is
k
and
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is not defined over
k
.

For
n=4
or
n
odd Theorem
1
is a generalization of Marinatto’s theorem to arbitrary fields. For
n
even and
\geq 6
, it is new. In order to prove all the various sub-statements of Theorem
1
, we first characterize the divisors
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
such that the field of moduli is a field of definition for
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
, see Theorem
5
.

Discussion of proofs

Marinatto’s proof for
n
odd is based on a case-by-case analysis of the finite subgroups of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
. Our approach is more conceptual: for
n
odd, we only use general techniques for problems about fields of moduli (not specific to divisors or rational curves) plus a parity counting. For
n
even and
\geq 8
, we also need to make some elementary geometric arguments. If
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1},D)
is not cyclic of degree even and prime with
\operatorname{char}k
, we have a conceptual argument based on the Riemann-Hurwitz formula; from this case we get the cases
n=4,6
, too.

Marinatto also uses a theorem of Huggins
[
Hug07
, Theorem 5.3]
about fields of moduli of hyperelliptic curves. Huggins’ proof is based on a case-by-case analysis of the finite subgroups of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
, too. Our argument with Riemann-Hurwitz directly yields a short, conceptual proof of Huggins’ result; we include it at the end of the paper. Our version is slightly more general than the original one since we do not assume the base field to be perfect.

While we use the general framework constructed in
[
BVb
]
, we mention the fact that, in dimension
1
(which is our case), the main ideas are due to P. Dèbes and M. Emsalem
[
DE99
]
.

1.
The residual gerbe and the compression

For the convenience of the reader, we recall some objects and constructions from our joint paper with A. Vistoli
[
BVb
]
.

Consider the functor
\mathcal{G}:\operatorname{Sch}/k\to\operatorname{Sets}
sending a scheme
S
over
k
to the set of projective bundles
Q\to S
of relative dimension
1
with an effective divisor
E\subset Q
finite étale over
S
such that each geometric fiber is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
, possibly after enlarging the base field. It can be checked that
\mathcal{G}
is an algebraic stack of finite type over
k
with finite, étale inertia.

The algebraic stack
\mathcal{G}
is a gerbe over a field
k(D)
finite over
k
which is, by definition, the field of moduli of
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
, and the base change
\mathcal{G}\times_{k(D)}\bar{k}
is isomorphic to the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}_{\bar{k}}\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)

[
BVb
, Proposition 3.10]
. It is called the
residual gerbe
of
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
. By definition,
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is defined over
k(D)
if and only if the residual gerbe
\mathcal{G}
has a
k(D)
-rational point. If
k
is finite then this is always true
[
DPTZ20
, Theorem 8.1]
.

There is a universal projective bundle
\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{G}
over
\mathcal{G}
with a universal divisor
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{P}
finite étale over
\mathcal{G}
. The coarse moduli space
P
of
\mathcal{P}
is called the
compression
of
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
, it is a rational curve over
k(D)
whose pullback to
\bar{k}
identifies naturally with
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}/\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)

[
BVb
, §5]
. Let us call
compressed divisor
the coarse moduli space
\mathbf{D}\subset\mathbf{P}
of
\mathcal{D}
, since
\mathcal{D}
is finite étale over
\mathcal{G}
then
\mathbf{D}
is finite étale over
\operatorname{Spec}k
.

If
k
is perfect, we can give a more down-to-earth construction of the compression:
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{k}/k(D))
acts naturally on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}/\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
which thus descends to a rational curve
\mathbf{P}
over
k(D)
, and similarly
D/\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
descends to a divisor
\mathbf{D}\subset\mathbf{P}
.

There is a rational map
P\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}
whose pullback
P_{\bar{k}}=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}/\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1},D)\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\bar{k}}=\mathcal{B}_{\bar{k}}\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
to
\bar{k}
is associated with the projection
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}/\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
, see the proof of
[
BVb
, Theorem 5.4]
. In particular, if

then
\mathcal{G}
has a
k(D)
-rational point.

2.
Rational maps from rational curves to gerbes

As we have seen above, rational maps
P\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}
where
P
is a rational curve,
\mathcal{G}
is a finite étale gerbe and such that the geometric fibers are connected of genus
0
play a crucial role in our problem. In this section, we study such maps.

Proposition 2
.

Let
k
be a field,
P
a rational curve with
P(k)=\emptyset
,
\Phi
a finite, étale gerbe with a rational map
P\dashrightarrow\Phi
. Assume that the geometric fibers of
P\dashrightarrow\Phi
are irreducible curves of genus
0
.

The gerbe
\Phi
is abelian with cyclic band of order prime to
\operatorname{char}k
, and there exists a separable extension
k^{\prime}/k
of degree
2
and a point
p\in P(k^{\prime})
such that
P\dashrightarrow\Phi
restricts to a morphism
P\smallsetminus\{p\}\to\Phi
.

Let us first sketch the proof in the particular case in which
k
is perfect. Write
\Phi_{\bar{k}}=\mathcal{B}_{\bar{k}}G
for some finite group
G
, the base change of
P\dashrightarrow\Phi
to
\bar{k}
corresponds to a
G
-covering
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
. The branch locus of the
G
-covering descends to
P
: if
P(k)=\emptyset
, this forces every branch point
p
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
to have at least one Galois conjugate
\bar{p}
such that the ramification data over
p
and
\bar{p}
are equal. Using the Riemann-Hurwitz formula, it can be easily checked that this only happens for
G
cyclic.

Proof.

Let
\bar{k}
be an algebraic closure and
\bar{k}\supseteq k^{s}\supseteq k
be the separable closure, we have
P_{k^{s}}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k^{s}}
, in particular
\Phi_{k^{s}}
is neutral since there is a rational map
P\dashrightarrow\Phi
. Choose a section
\operatorname{Spec}k^{s}\to\Phi
and let
G
be its automorphism group, it is a finite constant group and there is an induced branched
G
-covering
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k^{s}}\to P_{k^{s}}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k^{s}}
.

Let
R
be the ramification divisor of
f
as in
[
Sta21
,
Tag 0C1B
]
, we think of
R
as a non-reduced, finite scheme over
k^{s}
. There is an action of
G
on
R
, in particular on the underlying set. Given a point
r\in R
denote by
d_{r}
the length of
R
at
r
,
e_{r}
the ramification index,
o_{r}
the cardinality of the set-theoretic
G
-orbit of
r
. Since
f
has degree
n
, then
n=o_{r}e_{r}[k^{s}(r):k^{s}(f(r))]
. By Riemann-Hurwitz
[
Sta21
,
Lemma 0C1F
]
we have

2n-2=\sum_{r\in R}d_{r}[k^{s}(r):k^{s}]

with
d_{r}=e_{r}-1
if
f
is tamely ramified at
r
and
d_{r}\geq e_{r}
otherwise.

There exists a largest open subset
U\subset P
such that
U\to\Phi
is defined
[
Bre21
, Corollary A.2]
. Write
Z=P\smallsetminus U
, then
Z_{k^{s}}
is the branch locus of
f

[
Bre21
, Lemma A.1, Corollary A.2.iv]
, in particular
f(R)=Z_{k^{s}}
set-theoretically. Since
P
is a non-trivial Brauer-Severi variety,
Z
has no rational points. This implies that, if
r\in R
is a point such that
k^{s}(f(r))=k^{s}
, there exists a point
r^{\prime}\in R
such that
f(r^{\prime})\neq f(r)
is a Galois conjugate of
f(r)
, and hence
o_{r}=o_{r^{\prime}}
,
d_{r}=d_{r^{\prime}}
,
e_{r}=e_{r^{\prime}}
,
k^{s}(r)\simeq k^{s}(r^{\prime})
.

Let us show first that
f
is tamely ramified at every point. Assume that
f
has wild ramification at some point
r
, in particular
d_{r}\geq e_{r}
. If
k^{s}(f(r))=k^{s}
, by what we have said above we have

2n-2\geq 2o_{r}e_{r}[k^{s}(r):k^{s}]=2n,

which is absurd. If
k^{s}(f(r))\neq k^{s}
, then

2n-2\geq o_{r}e_{r}[k^{s}(r):k^{s}]\geq 2o_{r}e_{r}[k^{s}(r):k^{s}(f(r))]=2n,

hence we get a contradiction in this case, too.

We may thus assume that
f
has tame ramification: in particular,
k^{s}(r)=k^{s}(f(r))
for every
r
,
n=o_{r}e_{r}
and
d_{r}=e_{r}-1
for every
r\in R
. Since
f
is ramified at every
r\in R
, then
e_{r}\geq 2
and
o_{r}\leq n/2
. For every
z\in Z_{k^{s}}
, write
e_{z}
,
o_{z}
for
e_{r}
,
o_{r}
, where
r\in R
is some point with
f(r)=z
. We have

2n-2=\sum_{r\in R}(e_{r}-1)[k^{s}(r):k^{s}]=\sum_{z\in Z_{k^{s}}}o_{z}(e_{z}-1)[k^{s}(z):k^{s}]=

=\sum_{z\in Z_{k^{s}}}(n-o_{z})[k^{s}(z):k^{s}]\geq\deg Z\cdot n/2

which implies
\deg Z\leq 3
. Since
P
is a non-trivial Brauer-Severi variety of dimension
1
, then
\deg Z
is even, hence we may assume
\deg Z=2
(if
\deg Z=0
, then
G
is trivial).

Assume first
Z_{k^{s}}
contains only one point
z
with
[k^{s}(z):k^{s}]=2
, in particular
\operatorname{char}k=2
. Then

2n-2=o_{z}(e_{z}-1)[k^{s}(z):k^{s}]=2n-2o_{z},

hence
o_{z}=1
and
n=e_{z}
is prime with
\operatorname{char}k=2
, i.e. it is odd. Since
n
is odd, the base change
f_{\bar{k}}
of
f
to
\bar{k}
is tamely ramified and since
f
has only one point then the same is true for
f_{\bar{k}}
, and the ramification index must be
n
. By Riemann-Hurwitz applied to
f_{\bar{k}}
, we have
2n-2=n-1
, i.e.
n=1
which is in contradiction with
\deg Z=2
.

If
Z_{k^{s}}
contains two
k^{s}
-rational points, the Galois action swaps them because
Z
has no rational points, hence

2n-2=2o_{z}(e_{z}-1)=2n-2o_{z},

which implies
o_{z}=1
and
n=e_{z}
is prime to
\operatorname{char}k
. If
r\in R
is one of the ramification points, since
|G|=n=e_{z}
then
r
is fixed by
G
. Since
|G|=n
is prime with
\operatorname{char}k
, then
G
is linearly reductive: this implies that
G
acts faithfully on the tangent space of
r
and is thus cyclic.
∎

Corollary 3
.

Let
k
be a field and
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
an effective, reduced divisor of degree
n\geq 3
defined over
\bar{k}
. If the compression of
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
over the field of moduli, then
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is cyclic of order prime to the characteristic.

Lemma 4
.

Let
P\dashrightarrow\Phi
be as in Proposition
2
. Then
\Phi
is neutral if and only if it has odd degree.

Proof.

By Proposition
2
,
\Phi
is abelian with cyclic band
A
of order prime to
\operatorname{char}k
. We have that
\Phi
corresponds to a class
\phi\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(k,A)
and
\Phi
is neutral if and only if
\phi
is trivial. Let
n
be the degree of
\Phi
, which corresponds to the degree of
A
. The group
\operatorname{H}^{2}(k,A)
is abelian and
n
-torsion.

Let
k^{\prime}
be the residue field of the point
p
given by Proposition
2
, then
k^{\prime}/k
is a separable extension of degree
2
and
\phi_{k^{\prime}}=0
since
k^{\prime}
splits
P
. We have
2\phi=\operatorname{cor}_{k^{\prime}/k}(\phi_{k^{\prime}})=0
: if
n
is odd, then
\phi=\frac{n+1}{2}2\phi=0
.

If
\Phi
has even degree, by Proposition
2

\operatorname{char}k
is prime with
n
and hence
\operatorname{char}k\neq 2
. In particular, the
2
-adic étale fundamental group of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
over
k^{s}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Write
U=P\smallsetminus\{p\}
, to prove that
\Phi
is not neutral it is then enough to show that there exists a morphism
U\to\Gamma
where
\Gamma
is a non-neutral gerbe banded by
\mu_{2}
, since the fact the the degree of
\Phi
is even gives us a factorization
U\to\Phi\to\Gamma
. Such a morphism
U\to\Gamma
is constructed in the proof of
[
BV15
, Proposition 13.2]
using parameters
p=r=2
and
X=U
.
∎

3.
Which divisors descend to a rational curve

We characterize which pairs
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
descend to the field of moduli. We’ll then prove Theorem
1
using this characterization.

Theorem 5
.

Let
k
be a field and
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
an effective, reduced divisor of degree
n\geq 3
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
with
n\geq 3
. The following are equivalent.

•

(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is not defined over its field of moduli.

•

The compression has no rational points and
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
has even degree.

•

The compression has no rational points and
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is cyclic of degree even and prime to
\operatorname{char}k
.

Proof.

Thanks to
[
BVb
, Proposition 4.1]
, we may assume that
k
is infinite. Furthermore, by base change we may assume that
k
is the field of moduli. Let
\mathcal{G},\mathbf{P}
be the residual gerbe and the compression respectively, there is a rational map
\mathbf{P}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}
whose geometric fibers are irreducible of genus
0
.

If
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is not defined over
k
, i.e.
\mathcal{G}
is not neutral, then
\mathbf{P}
is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and hence
\mathcal{G}
is abelian with cyclic band of degree even and prime with
\operatorname{char}k
by Proposition
2
and Lemma
4
. Since
\mathcal{G}_{\bar{k}}=\mathcal{B}_{\bar{k}}\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
, we obtain that the first condition implies the third, which in turn clearly implies the second. If
\mathbf{P}(k)=\emptyset
and
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
has even degree, then by Lemma
4
the residual gerbe
\mathcal{G}
is not neutral, hence we conclude.
∎

Corollary 6
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
2
and
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
an effective, reduced divisor with
n\geq 3
. Then
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is defined over its field of moduli.

4.
Divisors of degree
6

Proposition 7
.

Let
k
be a field and
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
an effective, reduced divisor of degree
6
. Then
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is defined over its field of moduli.

Proof.

Thanks to Corollary
6
, we may assume
\operatorname{char}k\neq 2
. Up to base change, we may assume that
k
is the field of moduli, let
\mathbf{P}
be the compression and
\mathbf{D}\subset\mathbf{P}
the compressed divisor, we have that
\mathbf{D}
is finite étale over
k
. Assume by contradiction that
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is not defined over
k
, then by Theorem
5

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is cyclic of degree even and prime to
\operatorname{char}k
, and
\mathbf{P}
is a non-trivial rational curve.

Since
\mathbf{P}
is non-trivial, then
\mathbf{D}
has even degree. Since
\operatorname{char}k\neq 2
, then
\mathbf{D}_{\bar{k}}=D/\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1},D)
has either
2,4
or
6
points. Since
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is cyclic of degree even and prime with
\operatorname{char}k
, then its action on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
has two fixed points, while all the other orbits have even cardinality equal to
|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)|
. It then follows that
D
contains the
2
fixed points,
\mathbf{D}_{\bar{k}}
has
4
points and
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
has order
2
.

Choose coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
such that
0,\infty\in D
are the two fixed points, and
1
is another point of
D
. With respect to these coordinates, we have that the only non-trivial element of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
is the map
x\mapsto-x
, we may thus write
D=\{0,\infty,1,-1,\lambda,-\lambda\}
. It is immediate to check that
x\mapsto\lambda/x
defines another non-trivial automorphism of
(\mathbb{P}^{1},D)
, hence we get a contradiction.
∎

5.
Divisors of even degree
n\geq 8

In order to construct divisors of even degree
n\geq 8
not defined over their field of moduli, we first construct a divisor over a non-trivial rational curve with certain special properties.

Lemma 8
.

Let
k
be an infinite field of characteristic
\neq 2
,
P
a non-trivial rational curve,
n\geq 8
an even integer.

There exists a reduced, effective divisor
E\subset P
, a quadratic extension
k^{\prime}/k
, a point
p\in P(k^{\prime})
with Galois conjugate
\bar{p}\in P(k^{\prime})
and a cyclic cover
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}}\to P_{\bar{k}}
of degree
2
ramified over
p,\bar{p}
such that the divisor
D=f^{-1}(E_{\bar{k}})
with the reduced structure has degree
n
and
\operatorname{Aut}(f)
is its own centralizer in
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{k}},D)
.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be the canonical bundle of
P
. Write either
n=4m
or
n=4m-2
with
m\geq 2
, depending on the class of
n
modulo
4
. Consider the natural map
\mathbb{P}(\operatorname{H}^{0}(\omega^{-1}))\times\mathbb{P}(\operatorname{H}^{0}(\omega^{-m+1}))\to\mathbb{P}(\operatorname{H}^{0}(\omega^{-m}))
corresponding to the sum of divisors, it is dominant.

If
n\geq 10
, i.e.

, a generic rational point of

6.
Generalizations of results of A. Marinatto and B. Huggins

The following lemma connects our results to those of Marinatto.

Lemma 11
.

Let

•

descends to a divisor of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
over the field of moduli.

•

The universal family
\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{G}

By base change, we may assume that

is the field of moduli. If

descends to a divisor of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
over

\mathcal{P}
has a rational point, which in turn implies that the compression

descends to a divisor of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
over

Assume first that

[

,

of

over

[
BVb
, Theorem 5.4]

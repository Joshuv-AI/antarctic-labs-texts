The set of partial isometries as a quotient Finsler space
E. Andruchow

Abstract
A known general program, designed to endow the quotient space 𝒰 𝒜 / 𝒰 ℬ {\cal U}_{\cal A}/{\cal U}_{\cal B} of the unitary groups 𝒰 𝒜 {\cal U}_{\cal A} , 𝒰 ℬ {\cal U}_{\cal B} of the C ∗ algebras ℬ ⊂ 𝒜 {\cal B}\subset{\cal A} with an invariant Finsler metric, is applied to obtain a metric for the space ℐ ⁡ ( ℋ ) {\cal I}({\cal H}) of partial isometries of a Hilbert space ℋ {\cal H} . ℐ ⁡ ( ℋ ) {\cal I}({\cal H}) is a quotient of the unitary group of ℬ ⁡ ( ℋ ) × ℬ ⁡ ( ℋ ) {\cal B}({\cal H})\times{\cal B}({\cal H}) , where ℬ ⁡ ( ℋ ) {\cal B}({\cal H}) is the algebra of bounded linear operators in ℋ {\cal H} . Under this program, the solution of a linear best approximation problem leads to the computation of minimal geodesics in the quotient space. We find solutions of this best approximation problem, and study properties of the minimal geodesics obtained.



2020 MSC: 47A05, 58B20

Keywords: partial isometries, Finsler metric, minimal curves

The purpose of this note is to apply the program by C. Durán, L. Mata-Lorenzo and L. Recht

Remark 2.1

Let

More recently, in

5.

Given
V_{0}

of

{\rm I}_{V_{0}}=\{(G,H)\in{\cal U}({\cal H})\times{\cal U}({\cal H}):GV_{0}=V_{0}H\}.

(3)

It is a
C^{\infty}
Banach-Lie group, whose Banach-Lie algebra is

\iota_{V_{0}}=\{(iX,iY)\in{\cal B}({\cal H})\times{\cal B}({\cal H}):X^{*}=X,Y^{*}=Y\hbox{ and }XV_{0}=V_{0}Y\}.

(4)

V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
is called

. This type of partial isometries comprise full connected components of
{\cal I}({\cal H})
. Note that
V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
is balanced if and only if there exists an orthogonal projection
P
and unitaries

such that

. Indeed, if

, then
V
and
P
lie in the same connected component, and therefore, by Halmos-McLaughlin’s characterization,

. The converse statement is clear.

Non-unitary isometries are examples of non balanced partial isometries.

The purpose of this note, is to introduce a natural invariant Finsler metric in
{\cal I}({\cal H})
. That is, a metric
|\ \ |_{V}
in each tangent space
\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V}
, which is invariant under the action of

|{\cal V}|_{V}=|U{\cal V}W^{*}|_{UVW^{*}}.

Here, note that the action is linear: for fixed

is the restriction of a global bounded linear map

.

It will be useful to recall the form of the tangent spaces of

({\rm T}\pi_{V_{0}})_{(1,1)}:(T{\cal U}({\cal H})\times{\cal U}({\cal H}))_{(1,1)}=\{(iX,iY):X^{*}=X,Y^{*}=Y\}\to\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V_{0}},

({\rm T}\pi_{V_{0}})_{(1,1)}((iX,iY)=iXV_{0}-iV_{0}Y

is surjective, and

\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V_{0}}=\{iXV_{0}-iV_{0}Y:X^{*}=X,Y^{*}=Y\}.

(5)

The metric that will be considered in

is a

, using the homogeneous structure of

(as a quotient of the group

Following this program, one can compute curves of minimal length (

Let us briefly describe the context and main result of
[
13
]
. Let
{\cal B}\subset{\cal A}
be unital C
∗
-algebras (with the same unit). Denote by
{\cal U}_{\cal B}
and
{\cal U}_{\cal A}
the unitary groups of
{\cal B}
and
{\cal A}
, respectively. In
[
13
]
a metric was introduced in the homogeneous (quotient) space

. If

, let
[u]\in M
be the class of
u
in the quotient space. The Banach-Lie algebras of
{\cal U}_{\cal A}
and
{\cal U}_{\cal B}
are, respectively

\mathfrak{u}_{\cal A}=\{ix:x^{*}=x\in{\cal A}\},\ \mathfrak{u}_{\cal B}=\{iy:y^{*}=y\in{\cal A}\}.

For
[u]\in M
, let

\pi_{[u]}:{\cal U}_{\cal A}\to M,\ \pi_{[u]}(w)=[uw].

The tangent space
of
M

naturally identifies with the quotient of the Lie algebras of
{\cal U}_{\cal A}
and
{\cal U}_{\cal B}
:

({\rm}TM)_{[u]}\simeq\mathfrak{u}_{\cal A}\ /\ \mathfrak{u}_{\cal B},

since
({\rm T}\pi_{[u]})_{[1]}:\mathfrak{u}_{\cal A}\to({\rm T}M)_{[u]}
is an epimorphism with nullspace
\mathfrak{u}_{\cal B}
.
In this tangent space they define the natural metric:

Definition 4.1
.

If
{\bf v}=ix+\mathfrak{u}_{\cal B}\in(TM)_{[u]}

|{\bf v}|_{[u]}:=\inf\{\|x+y\|:y^{*}=y\in{\cal B}\},

(6)

i.e., the usual metric in the quotient of (real) Banach spaces
\mathfrak{u}_{\cal A}\ /\ \mathfrak{u}_{\cal B}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
{\bf v}\in(TM)_{[u]}
. An element
x_{0}=x_{0}^{*}\in{\cal A}
is a minimal lifting of
{\bf v}
if

{\bf v}=ix_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{\cal B}

\|x_{0}\|=\inf\{\|x_{0}+y\|:y=y^{*}\in{\cal B}\}=|{\bf v}|_{[u]}.

That is,
x_{0}

In general, minimal liftings may not exist (see for instance the paper
[
7
]
for an interesting example). However, when they do exist, they provide curves of minimal length in

Theorem 4.3
.

(Durán, Mata-Lorenzo, Recht
[
13
]
)

Let
[u]\in M
and
{\bf v}\in(TM)_{[u]}
. Suppose that

has a minimal lifting

. Then the curve

which satisfies the initial conditions

\delta(0)=[u]\ \hbox{ and }\ \dot{\delta}(0)={\bf v},

has minimal length along its path for
|t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2|{\bf v}|_{[u]}}=\frac{\pi}{2\|x_{0}\|}
.

Here, by

at the given interval of

[t_{0},t_{1}]
is a subinterval of
[-\frac{\pi}{2|{\bf v}|_{[u]}},\frac{\pi}{2|{\bf v}|_{[u]}}]

\ell(\delta|_{[t_{0},t_{1}]})=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}|\dot{\delta}(t)|_{\delta(t)}dt\leq\int_{I}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|_{\gamma(t)}dt=\ell(\gamma).

Example 4.4
.

An example where minimal liftings exist at every tangent vector (at every point), occurs when both
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
are von Neumann algebras. For instance, if

5
Finsler metric and minimal curves in
{\cal I}({\cal H})

In this section we show that minimal liftings of the homogeneous space

{\cal O}_{\epsilon_{V}}\simeq{\cal U}({\cal H})\ /{\cal U}({\cal H})\cap\{\epsilon_{V}\}^{\prime}

induce in a simple manner minimal liftings in
{\cal I}({\cal H})

Note that

If

Since clearly

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}^{*}A_{11}+A_{21}^{*}A_{21}&0\\
0&A_{12}^{*}A_{12}+A_{22}^{*}A_{22}\end{array}\right)\geq\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{21}^{*}A_{21}&0\\
0&A_{12}^{*}A_{12}\end{array}\right)

we get

\|A\|^{2}\geq\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{21}^{*}A_{21}&0\\
0&A_{12}^{*}A_{12}\end{array}\right)\Big\|=\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&A_{21}^{*}\\
A_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&0\end{array}\right)\Big\|=\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&0\end{array}\right)\Big\|^{2}

∎

Our next goal is to compare
|{\cal V}|_{V}
with the usual operator norm
\|{\cal V}\|
of
{\cal V}\in\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V}
, for
V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
. In fact, we shall see that for each fixed
V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
,
|\ \ |_{V}
and
\|\ \|
are equivalent in
\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V}
. To do this task, we shall need a classical result by M.C. Krein
[
17
]
, known as the extension problem for symmetric transformations (see also the excellent text
[
23
]
, Section 125, or also
[
12
]
,
[
20
]
for more nuanced developements on this subject).
We state this result in the following remark, adapted to our particular problem:

Remark 5.3
.

Let

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&*\end{array}\right)

be an incomplete operator matrix, with
A_{11}^{*}=A_{11}
. Then there exist (non unique) selfadjoint completions

{\bf A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&A_{22}\end{array}\right)

with
\|{\bf A}\|=\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{11}\\
A_{12}^{*}\end{array}\right)\Big\|=\|\left(\begin{array}[]{lr}A_{11}&A_{12}\end{array}\right)\|
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
and
{\cal V}\in\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V}
. Then

|{\cal V}|_{V}\leq\|{\cal V}\|\leq 2|{\cal V}|_{V}

Proof.

Let us first consider the case of a balanced partial isometry,
n(V)=r^{\perp}(V)
. Let
U,W
be unitaries such that
UP_{0}W^{*}=V
, for an orthogonal projection
P_{0}
. Clearly, pulling back
{\cal V}
with the left action of the pair
(U,W)
, it suffices to reason in the case
V=P_{0}
. To this effect, note that the action of
(U,W)
is isometric both for the Finsler norm
|\ \ |_{V}
and the operator norm
\|\ \|
. Let
(A,B)
be a lifting for
{\cal V}
, i.e.,
A^{*}=A
,
B^{*}=B
and
iAP_{0}-iP_{0}B={\cal V}
. Note that, in matrix form in terms of
P_{0}
,

{\cal V}=i\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&A_{22}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)-i\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{12}^{*}&B_{22}\end{array}\right)=i\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&B_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)

Let us alter the lifting
(A,B)
. Put

B_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&B_{12}\\
B_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)

and

A_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&Z\end{array}\right)

where
Z=Z^{*}
is such that
A_{0}
is a solution of Krein’s extension problem for the symmetric incomplete matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&*\end{array}\right)

Straightforward computations show that
(A_{0},B_{0})
is also a lifting of
{\cal V}
:
A_{0}^{*}=A_{0}
,
B_{0}^{*}=B_{0}
and
iA_{0}P_{0}-iP_{0}B_{0}={\cal V}
.
Then

\|A_{0}\|=\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{11}-B_{11}\\
A_{12}^{*}\end{array}\right)\Big\|=\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&0\\
A_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)\Big\|=\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&B_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)\Big\|

=\|{\cal V}P_{0}\|\leq\|{\cal V}\|.

On the other hand

\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&B_{12}\\
B_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)\Big\|=\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&B_{12}\\
0&0\end{array}\right)\Big\|=\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&B_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\Big\|=\|{\cal V}P_{0}^{\perp}\|\leq\|{\cal V}\|.

Thus, we have found a lifting
(A_{0},B_{0})
of
{\cal V}
such that

\|(A_{0},B_{0})\|=\max\{\|A_{0}\|,\|B_{0}\|\}\leq\|{\cal V}\|.

It follows that
|{\cal V}|_{P_{0}}\leq\|{\cal V}\|
.

Let us now consider the general case. Consider the Hilbert space
{\cal H}\times{\cal H}
.
Note that
V\oplus 0
in
{\cal H}\times{\cal H}
defined as
V(\xi,\eta)=(V\xi,0)
is a partial isometry with
n(V\oplus 0)=r^{\perp}(V\oplus 0)=+\infty
. Similarly, if
{\cal V}\in\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V}
, then
{\cal V}\oplus 0\in\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H}\times{\cal H})\right)_{V\oplus 0}
. Note that

|{\cal V}\oplus 0|_{V\oplus 0}\leq|{\cal V}|_{V}.

Indeed, any lifing
(A,B)
of
{\cal V}
, provides a lifting
(A\oplus 0,B\oplus 0)
of
{\cal V}\oplus 0
. On the other hand, if

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&A_{22}\end{array}\right)\ ,\ \left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{12}^{*}&B_{22}\end{array}\right)

is a lifting of
{\cal V}\oplus 0
. i.e.,

{\cal V}\oplus 0=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\cal V}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)=i\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}V-VB_{11}&-VB_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}V&0\end{array}\right),

then, in particular
(A_{11},B_{11})
is a lifting for
{\cal V}
.
Since

\|A_{11}\|\leq\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&A_{22}\end{array}\right)\Big\|\ \hbox{ and }\ \|B_{11}\|\leq\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{12}^{*}&B_{22}\end{array}\right)\Big\|,

it follows that
|{\cal V}|_{V}\leq|{\cal V}\oplus 0|_{V\oplus 0}
.
Then, by the first case,

\|{\cal V}\|=\|{\cal V}\oplus 0\|\leq|{\cal V}\oplus 0|_{V\oplus 0}=|{\cal V}|_{V}.

The other inequality is trivial:

\|{\cal V}\|=\|AV-VB\|\leq\|AV\|+\|VB\|\leq\|A\|+\|B\|\leq 2\max\{\|A\|,\|B\|\},

for any lifting
(A,B)
of
{\cal V}
.

∎

Remark 5.5
.

The inequality
|{\cal V}|_{V}\leq\|{\cal V}\|
may be strict. The problem of finding completions of
2\times 2
matrices with minimal norm has been studied for non-selfadjoint matrix operators (see for instance
[
12
]
). Namely, applied in our context and following the notations of the above theorem, given the incomplete (non-selfadjoint) matrix operator

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&B_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&*\end{array}\right)

There exists a completion
C=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&B_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&Y\end{array}\right)
with minimal norm, that is

\|C\|=\max\{\|\left(\begin{array}[]{lr}A_{11}-B_{11}&B_{12}\end{array}\right)\|,\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{11}-B_{11}\\
A_{12}^{*}\end{array}\right)\Big\|\}.

The row
\left(\begin{array}[]{lr}A_{11}-B_{11}&B_{12}\end{array}\right)
is the first row of the incomplete matrix
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{12}^{*}&*\end{array}\right)

which can be completed with minimal norm to the selfadjoint operator

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{12}^{*}&Z^{\prime}\end{array}\right),

with

\|\left(\begin{array}[]{lr}A_{11}-B_{11}&B_{12}\end{array}\right)\|=\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{12}^{*}&Z^{\prime}\end{array}\right)\Big\|.

By Lemma
5.2
,

\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{12}^{*}&Z^{\prime}\end{array}\right)\Big\|\geq\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&B_{12}\\
B_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)\Big\|.

Similarly, reasoning with the first column
\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{11}-B_{11}\\
A_{12}^{*}\end{array}\right)
, we get that there is a selfadjoint completion
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&Z\end{array}\right)
such that

\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{11}-B_{11}\\
A_{12}^{*}\end{array}\right)\Big\|=\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&Z\end{array}\right)\Big\|.

It follows that

\|C\|\geq\max\{\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&B_{12}\\
B_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)\Big\|,\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}-B_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{12}^{*}&Z\end{array}\right)\Big\|\},

which is the norm of a lifting
(A_{0},B_{0})
of
{\cal V}
(as in the first part of the proof of the above Theorem). That is,
\|C\|\geq|{\cal V}|_{V}
. Now,
C
and
-i{\cal V}
are both completions of the same incomplete (non-selfadjoint) matrix. Since
C
has minimal norm among these completions, one has
\|C\|\leq\|{\cal V}\|
. Moreover, it is known that, in general, putting
0
in the
2,2
place is not the optimal solution (see
[
12
]
,
[
20
]
): there are examples where
\|C\|<\|{\cal V}\|
. Then, for such
{\cal V}
, we have
|{\cal V}|_{V}<\|{\cal V}\|
.

In order to establish the existence of minimal liftings in
{\cal I}({\cal H})
, we need the next lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
and
{\bf A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{1}&0\\
0&A_{2}\end{array}\right)
be selfadjoint in
{\cal H}\times{\cal H}
. Let

{\bf X}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X_{11}&X_{12}\\
X_{12}^{*}&X_{22}\end{array}\right)\in\{\epsilon_{V}\}^{\prime},\ {\bf X}^{*}={\bf X}^{*},

such that

\|{\bf A}+{\bf X}\|\leq\|{\bf A}+{\bf Y}\|\hbox{ for all }{\bf Y}^{*}={\bf Y}\in\{\epsilon_{V}\}^{\prime}

(which exists, recall Example
4.4
).
Then
{\bf X}_{0}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X_{11}&0\\
0&X_{22}\end{array}\right)
satisfies

•

{\bf X}_{0}
commutes with
\epsilon_{V}
, in particular,
i{\bf X}_{0}\in\iota_{V}
;

•

\|{\bf A}+{\bf X}_{0}\|\leq\|{\bf A}+{\bf Z}\|
for all
i{\bf Z}\in\iota_{V}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
{\bf X}_{0}^{*}={\bf X}_{0}
. The fact that
{\bf X}
commutes with
\epsilon_{V}
, means that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}X_{12}V^{*}=VX_{12}^{*},\\
X_{12}^{*}V=V^{*}X_{12},\\
X_{11}V=VX_{22}\end{array}\right.

and thus also
{\bf X}_{0}
commutes with
\epsilon_{V}
, in particular
X_{11}V=VX_{22}
, which means that
i{\bf X}_{0}\in\iota_{V}
. By the same argument, it is also clear that if

{\bf Z}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Z_{11}&Z_{12}\\
Z_{12}^{*}&Z_{22}\end{array}\right)

commutes with
\epsilon_{V}
, then also
{\bf Z}_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Z_{11}&0\\
0&Z_{22}\end{array}\right)
also commutes with
\epsilon_{V}
. Then

\|{\bf A}+{\bf X}\|\leq\|{\bf A}+{\bf Z}_{0}\|.

On the other hand, the linear map
E:{\cal B}({\cal H}\times{\cal H})\to{\cal B}({\cal H}\times{\cal H})
given by

E\big(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}T_{11}&T_{12}\\
T_{21}&T_{22}\end{array}\right)\big)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}T_{11}&0\\
0&T_{22}\end{array}\right)

is contractive. Then

\|{\bf A}+{\bf X}_{0}\|=\|E({\bf A}+{\bf X})\|\leq\|{\bf A}+{\bf X}\|\leq\|{\bf A}+{\bf Z}_{0}\|,

(10)

which completes the proof.
∎

We shall call
{\bf A}_{0}:={\bf A}+{\bf X}_{0}
a
diagonal minimal lifting
. Note that the above Lemma states that any vector tangent to
\Delta\cdot\epsilon_{V}
has a diagonal minimal lifting
{\bf A}_{0}
.
Applying Theorem
4.3

[

]
we get

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
and

\delta(t)=e^{it{\bf A}_{0}}\ \epsilon_{V}\ e^{-it{\bf A}_{0}}

, has minimal length along its path in

The proof of the above Lemma, in fact shows that

6
Initial and final projections

If
V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
, denote by
\alpha(V)=V^{*}V
and
\omega(V)=VV^{*}
the initial and final projections of
V
. Denote by
{\cal P}({\cal H})
the space of (orthogonal) projections of
{\cal B}({\cal H})
. The space of projections of a C
∗
-algebra has been well studied, as a complemented submanifold of the algebra, and as an homogeneous space of the inner action of the unitary group of the algebra (
u\cdot p=upu^{*}
, if
u
is unitary and
p
is a projection). It has also been studied as a Finsler metric space, where each tangent space is endowed with the usual norm of the algebra (see
[
21
]
and
[
11
]
). In the specific case of the algebra
{\cal B}({\cal H})
, existence of minimal geodesics with given initial conditions or with given endpoints, have been characterized (see the references above, or
[
6
]
for the specific case of the algebra
{\cal B}({\cal H})
).

Clearly, the maps

\alpha:{\cal I}({\cal H})\to{\cal P}({\cal H}),\ \alpha(V)=V^{*}V\ \ \hbox{ and }\ \ \omega:{\cal I}({\cal H})\to{\cal P}({\cal H}),\ \omega(V)=VV^{*}

are
C^{\infty}
. Let us show that, if
{\cal P}({\cal H})
is given the above mentioned Finsler metric, i.e., the usual norm at every tangent space, and
{\cal I}({\cal H})
is considered with the quotient metric studied here, then both maps
\alpha
and
\omega
decrease distances.
This fact is based in Lemma
5.2
.
As said above, we consider
{\cal I}({\cal H})
and
{\cal P}({\cal H})
as metric spaces, with their given Finsler metrics. Recall how a Finsler metric in the tangent spaces induces a metric in the original space: if
M
is a manifold with a Finsler metric
|\ \ |_{m}
at
(TM)_{m}
(for
m\in M
), then

d_{M}(m_{1},m_{2})=\inf\{\ell(\gamma):\gamma(t)\in M,t\in[a,b],\gamma\hbox{ is smooth },\gamma(a)=m_{1},\gamma(b)=m_{2}\},

where

\ell(\gamma)=\int_{a}^{b}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|_{\gamma(t)}dt.

Proposition 6.1
.

The maps

\alpha:{\cal I}({\cal H})\to{\cal P}({\cal H}),\ \alpha(V)=V^{*}V

and

\omega:{\cal I}({\cal H})\to{\cal P}({\cal H}),\ \omega(V)=VV^{*}

are distance decreasing, i.e., if
V_{1},V_{2}\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
,
E_{i}=\alpha(V_{i})
,
F_{i}=\omega(V_{i})
,
i=1,2
, then

d_{{\cal P}({\cal H})}(E_{1},E_{2})\leq d_{{\cal I}({\cal H})}(V_{1},V_{2})\ \ \ \hbox{ and }\ \ \ d_{{\cal P}({\cal H})}(F_{1},F_{2})\leq d_{{\cal I}({\cal H})}(V_{1},V_{2}).

Proof.

We reason with the map
\alpha
(the argument with
\omega
is similar). It suffices to show that the tangent maps
(T\alpha)_{V}:\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V}\to({\rm T}{\cal P}({\cal H}))_{\alpha(V)}
,

(T\alpha)_{V}({\cal V})={\cal V}^{*}V+V^{*}{\cal V}

are contractive. Pick a pair
(iX,iY)
,
X^{*}=X,Y^{*}=Y
which lifts
{\cal V}
, i.e.,
{\cal V}=iXV-iVY
.
Then

(T\alpha)_{V}({\cal V})=(-iV^{*}X+iYV^{*})V+V^{*}(iXV-iVY)=iY\alpha(V)-i\alpha(V)Y=i[Y,\alpha(V)].

Note that the matrix of
[Y,\alpha(V)]
in terms of the projection
\alpha(V)
is

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Y_{11}&Y_{12}\\
Y_{12}^{*}&Y_{22}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)-\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Y_{11}&Y_{12}\\
Y_{12}^{*}&Y_{22}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-Y_{12}\\
Y_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right),

whose norm equals the norm of

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&Y_{12}\\
Y_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-Y_{12}\\
Y_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-Y_{12}\\
Y_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)(2\alpha(V)-1),

bacause
2\alpha(V)-1
is a unitary operator.
By Lemma
5.2

\Big\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-Y_{12}\\
Y_{12}^{*}&0\end{array}\right)\Big\|\leq\|Y\|\leq\max\{\|X\|,\|Y\|\}=\|(X,Y)\|.

Since this holds for any pair
(X,Y)
which lifts
{\cal V}
, we have that

|{\cal V}|_{V}=\inf\{\|(X,Y)\|:iXV-iVY={\cal V}\}\geq\|(T\alpha)_{V}({\cal V})\|,

as claimed.
∎

6.1
Balanced isometries

Recall that
V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
is called
balanced
if
n(V)=r^{\perp}(V)
.

In some special directions, more can be said about minimal liftings and geodesics at balanced isometries.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
be balanced, with inititial space
R(\alpha(V))={\cal S}_{i}
and final space
R(\omega(V))={\cal S}_{f}
. We call a tangent vector
{\cal V}\in\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V}

orthogonal at

V
if
{\cal V}
as an operator in
{\cal H}
, satisfies that
{\cal V}({\cal S}_{i})\perp{\cal S}_{f}
. Equivalently,
\omega(V){\cal V}\alpha(V)=0

Let us construct explicit minimal liftings for tangent vectors which are othogonal to
V
.

1.

Let
{\cal V}\in\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V}
such that
\omega(V)V\alpha(V)=0
. First, using the left action of
{\cal U}({\cal H})\times{\cal U}({\cal H})
, we may suppose without loss of generality that
V=P_{0}
is an orthogonal projection. Indeed, there exist
U,W\in{\cal U}({\cal H})
such that
V=UP_{0}W^{*}
. Then
{\cal V}_{0}:=U^{*}{\cal V}W\in\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{P_{0}}
satisfies

0=\omega(V){\cal V}\alpha(V)=VV^{*}{\cal V}V^{*}V=UP_{0}W^{*}(UP_{0}W^{*})^{*}{\cal V}(UP_{0}W^{*})^{*}UP_{0}W^{*}=UP_{0}U^{*}{\cal V}WP_{0}W^{*}

=UP_{0}{\cal V}_{0}P_{0}W^{*},

and thus,
P_{0}{\cal V}_{0}P_{0}=0
. Suppose that we find
(A,B)
a minimal lifting for
{\cal V}_{0}
at
P_{0}
, i.e.,
iAP_{0}-iP_{0}B={\cal V}_{0}
with

\|(A,B)\|\leq\|(A,B)+(X,Y)\|\ \hbox{ for all }(iX,iY)\in\iota_{P_{0}}.

Then
(UAU^{*},W^{*}BW)
is a lifting for
U{\cal V}_{0}W^{*}={\cal V}
at
V
:

iUAU^{*}V-iVW^{*}BW=U\{iAU^{*}VW^{*}-iU^{*}VWB\}W^{*}=U\{iAP_{0}-iP_{0}B\}W^{*}=U{\cal V}_{0}W^{*}={\cal V},

which is minimal

\|(UAU^{*},W^{*}BW)\|=\|(A,B)\|\leq\|(A,B)+(X,Y)\|=\|(UAU^{*},W^{*}BW)+(UXU^{*},W^{*}YW)\|,

where
(UXU^{*},W^{*}YW)
parametrizes all elements in
Ad(U,W)(\iota_{P_{0}})=\iota_{UP_{0}W^{*}}=\iota_{V}
.

2.

Let us construct a minimal lifting for
{\cal V}_{0}
at
P_{0}
(with
P_{0}{\cal V}_{0}P_{0}=0
). Let
(A,B)
,
A^{*}=A
,
B^{*}=B
such that
iAP_{0}-iP_{0}B={\cal V}_{0}
. Then
P_{0}AP_{0}=P_{0}BP_{0}
. Pick

A_{0}=P_{0}AP_{0}^{\perp}+P_{0}^{\perp}AP_{0}\ \hbox{ and }\ B_{0}=P_{0}BP_{0}^{\perp}+P_{0}^{\perp}BP_{0}.

Clearly
A_{0}^{*}=A_{0}
and
B_{0}^{*}=B_{0}
. Also, since
P_{0}AP_{0}=P_{0}BP_{0}
, after elementary computations,

{\cal V}_{0}=iAP_{0}-iP_{0}B=iA_{0}P_{0}-iP_{0}B_{0}.

Finally, if
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is another lifting of
{\cal V}_{0}
, then

P_{0}{\cal V}_{0}P_{0}^{\perp}=P_{0}(iA^{\prime}P_{0}-P_{0}B^{\prime}P_{0}^{\perp})P_{0}^{\perp}=iP_{0}B^{\prime}P_{0}^{\perp},

i.e.,
P_{0}B^{\prime}P_{0}^{\perp}=P_{0}B_{0}P_{0}^{\perp}
, and therefore also

P_{0}^{\perp}B^{\prime}P_{0}=(P_{0}B^{\prime}P_{0}^{\perp})^{*}=(P_{0}B_{0}P_{0}^{\perp})^{*}=P_{0}^{\perp}B_{0}P_{0}.

That is, in matrices in terms of
P_{0}
,
B^{\prime}
and
B_{0}
have the same off-diagonal entries. Clearly the same happens for
A^{\prime}
and
A_{0}
.
By Lemma
5.2
, since
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
are codiagonal,

\|A_{0}\|\leq\|A^{\prime}\|\ \hbox{ and }\ \|B_{0}\|\leq\|B^{\prime}\|,

i.e.,
(A_{0},B_{0})
is a minimal lifting.

These special (co-diagonal, minimal) liftings just exhibited for these special velocities, have the following property:

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
and
{\cal V}\in\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V}
such that
{\cal V}
is orthogonal to
{\cal V}
(i.e.,
\omega(V){\cal V}\alpha(V)=0
). Pick
(A_{0},B_{0})
a codiagonal minimal lifting of
{\cal V}
as above. Then the curve
\delta(t)=e^{itA_{0}}Ve^{-itB_{0}}
(minimal along its path up to
|t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2|{\cal V}|_{V}}
), verifies that the initial and final projection curves

\alpha(\delta),\omega(\delta)\in{\cal P}({\cal H})

are minimal along their paths in
{\cal P}({\cal H})
, for
|t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2\|B_{0}\|}
and
|t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2\|A_{0}\|}
, respectively.

Proof.

Note that

\alpha(\delta)(t)=\delta^{*}(t)\delta(t)=(e^{itA_{0}}Ve^{-itB_{0}})^{*}e^{itA_{0}}Ve^{-itB_{0}}=e^{-itB_{0}}V^{*}Ve^{-itB_{0}}=e^{-itB_{0}}\alpha(V)e^{-itB_{0}},

with
B_{0}
co-diagonal with respect to
\alpha(V)
. Indeed, with the same argument as above, it suffices to consider
V=P_{0}
, in which case it is evident. Therefore (see
[
21
]
),
\alpha(\delta)
is minimal in
{\cal P}({\cal H})
for
|t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2\|B_{0}\|}
. The argument with
\omega(\delta)
is analogous.
∎

In other words, for balanced isometries, and velocities which are orthogonal to
V
, locally, moving from
V_{0}
to
V_{1}
optimally in
{\cal I}({\cal H})
, involves the optimal paths for the initial and final spaces of
V_{0}
and
V_{1}
.

Recall from Theorem
5.4
the comparison between the Finsler norm of
{\cal V}
at
V
and the ambient norm of
{\cal V}
:
|{\cal V}|_{V}\leq\|{\cal V}\|
. Note that, for velocities which are orthogonal, at balanced partial isometries, both norms coincide:

Remark 6.4
.

Let
V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
and
{\cal V}\in\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V}
such that
{\cal V}
is orthogonal to
V
. Then

|{\cal V}|_{V}=\|(A_{0},B_{0})\|=\max\{\|A_{0}\|,\|B_{0}\|\}=\|{\cal V}\|,

its norm as an element in
{\cal B}({\cal H})
. Indeed, again it suffices to reason in the case
V=P_{0}
.
As seen in the discussion preceding the above proposition,
{\cal V}
has co-diagonal matrix in terms of
P_{0}
:

{\cal V}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&iB_{0}\\
iA_{0}&0\end{array}\right).

Then

\|{\cal V}\|^{2}=\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&iB_{0}\\
iA_{0}&0\end{array}\right)^{*}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&iB_{0}\\
iA_{0}&0\end{array}\right)\|=\|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{0}^{2}&0\\
0&B_{0}^{2}\end{array}\right)\|=\max\{\|A_{0}\|^{2},\|B_{0}\|^{2}\}.

If
\gamma(t)\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
,
t\in I
is smooth, denote by
\ell_{\infty}(\gamma)
the length of
\gamma
with the metric induced by the ambient norm of
{\cal B}({\cal H})
:

\ell_{\infty}(\gamma)=\int_{I}\|\dot{\gamma}(t)\|dt.

(11)

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
V\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
be balanced, and
{\cal V}\in\left({\rm T}{\cal I}({\cal H})\right)_{V}
orthogonal to
V
. Let
(A,B)
be a minimal lifting for
{\cal V}
. Then

\delta(t)=e^{itA}Ve^{-itB}

is minimal along its path, for
|t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2\|{\cal V}\|}
, when the lengths of curves are measured as in (
11
), with the
\ell_{\infty}
functional.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.8
, we know that
\delta
is minimal for the
\ell
functional, for
|t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2|{\cal V}|_{V}}=\frac{\pi}{2\|{\cal V}\|}
, because
|{\cal V}|_{V}=\|{\cal V}\|
. For an arbitrary smooth curve
\gamma
in
{\cal I}({\cal H})
, Theorem
5.4
implies that

\ell_{\infty}(\gamma)=\int_{I}\|\dot{\gamma}(t)\|dt\leq\int_{I}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|_{\gamma(t)}dt=\ell(\gamma).

Note that

\dot{\delta}(t)=e^{itA}\{iAV-iVB\}e^{-itB}=e^{itA}{\cal V}e^{-itB}.

Then, the facts that the metric
|\ \ |_{V}
is invariant under the action of
{\cal U}({\cal H})\times{\cal U}({\cal H})
, and that
{\cal V}
is orthogonal, imply that

|\dot{\delta}(t)|_{\delta(t)}=|{\cal V}|_{V}=\|{\cal V}\|=\|e^{itA}{\cal V}e^{-itB}\|=\|\dot{\delta}(t)\|,

and therefore
\ell(\delta)=\ell_{\infty}(\delta)
.
∎

Clearly
{\cal P}({\cal H})
is a complemented submanifold of
{\cal I}({\cal H})
. We shall prove another consequence of Remark
6.4
: if
P_{0},P_{1}\in{\cal P}({\cal H})
are regarded as points in
{\cal I}({\cal H})
, and they can be joined by a minimal geodesic in
{\cal P}({\cal H})
, then this path is minimal between
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
in
{\cal I}({\cal H})
.

Before, let us recall the necessary and sufficient condition that
P_{0},P_{1}
must satisfy in order that they can be joined by a minimal geodesic of
{\cal P}({\cal H})
(see
[
6
]
):

Remark 6.6
.

Let
P_{0},P_{1}\in{\cal P}({\cal H})
, then there exists a minimal geodesic of
{\cal P}({\cal H})
(or in fact, any geodesic) joining
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
if and only if

\dim\left(R(P_{0})\cap N(P_{1})\right)=\dim\left(R(P_{1})\cap N(P_{0})\right).

(12)

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
P_{0},P_{1}\in{\cal P}({\cal H})
satisfy condition
(
12
)
. Let
\delta(t)\in{\cal P}({\cal H})
be a geodesic joining
\delta(0)=P_{0}
and
\delta(1)=P_{1}
, and
\gamma(t)\in{\cal I}({\cal H})
be any other smooth curve joining
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
. Then

\ell(\delta)\leq\ell(\gamma).

Proof.

First, note that if
P
is a projection and
{\cal V}\in\left({\rm T}{\cal P}({\cal H})\right)_{P}
, then
{\cal V}
is
P
-co-diagonal:
P{\cal V}P=0
(or, in the notation employed here,
{\cal V}
is
orthogonal at

P
, regarded as an element in
{\cal I}({\cal H})
). This basic fact is well known in the geometry of
{\cal P}({\cal H})
(see
[
11
]
): if
P(t)
is a smooth curve in
{\cal P}({\cal H})
with
P(0)=P
and
\dot{P}(0)={\cal V}
, then differentiating
P^{2}(t)=P(t)
yields (at
t=0
)

{\cal V}P+P{\cal V}={\cal V},

which implies
P{\cal V}P=P^{\perp}{\cal V}P^{\perp}=0
. Therefore, by Remark
6.4
,
|{\cal V}|_{P}=\|{\cal V}\|
. It follows that
\ell_{\infty}(\delta)=\ell(\delta)
. On the other hand, by Proposition
6.1
,

\ell_{\infty}\left(\alpha(\gamma)\right)\leq\ell(\gamma);

since
\delta
is minimal in
{\cal P}({\cal H})
,

\ell(\delta)=\ell_{\infty}(\delta)\leq\ell_{\infty}\left(\alpha(\gamma)\right),

and the proof follows.
∎

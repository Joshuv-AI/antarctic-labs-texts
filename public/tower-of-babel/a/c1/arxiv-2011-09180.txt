Integrated density of states of the Anderson Hamiltonian with two-dimensional white noise
Toyomu Matsuda

Abstract
We construct the integrated density of states of the Anderson Hamiltonian with two-dimensional white noise by proving the convergence of the Dirichlet eigenvalue counting measures associated with the Anderson Hamiltonians on the boxes. We also determine the logarithmic asymptotics of the left tail of the integrated density of states. Furthermore, we apply our result to a moment explosion of the parabolic Anderson model in the plane. MSC 2020 - 60H25, 60H20, 35J10, 35P20, 82B44. Keywords - integrated density of states, Anderson Hamiltonian, parabolic Anderson model, white noise, Lifshitz tails, self-intersection local time.

Introduction

Background.
The time evolution of a particle in quantum mechanics is described by a Schrödinger operator,
often given by
-\frac{1}{2}\Delta-V
where
\Delta=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}^{2}
is a Laplacian and
V
is a potential.
We are interested in
random Schrödinger operators
, also called
Anderson Hamiltonians
, where the potentials are random.
Such operators appear in models where disorder need to be taken into consideration.
Mathematical study of random Schrödinger operators has a long history and is still active.
We refer the reader for overview to Carmona and Lacroix
[
7
]
, Kirsch
[
22
]
and Aizenman and Warzel
[
1
]
.

Anderson Hamiltonians can also be used to describe heat flow in disordered media.
The
parabolic Anderson model
(PAM) is the
stochastic partial differential equations
(SPDEs) of the following form:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)+V(x)u(t,x)\quad\mbox{in }(0,\infty)\times X,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},\end{cases}

where
X
is the space for the spatial variable
x
and can be either discrete or continuous.
Then, the solution
u
is described by the semigroup associated with the Anderson Hamiltonian
-\frac{1}{2}\Delta-V
.
The PAM is one of the most fundamental SPDEs and attracted attention from many mathematicians, see the monograph König
[
26
]
. a

The
integrated density of states
(IDS) is a fundamental object in the theory of random Schrödinger operators.
It heuristically measures the number of energy levels per unit volume below a given energy.
Given an Anderson Hamiltonian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the IDS is defined as the limit

N(\lambda)\vcentcolon=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{d}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{(-\infty,\lambda_{n,L}]}(\lambda),

(1.1)

where
\{\lambda_{n,L}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is the eigenvalues of the Anderson Hamiltonian on

[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{d}
.
It is a natural question whether the limit exists and is not trivial.
This question is positively answered for various models, see the references above, Veselic
[
37
]
or Kirsch and Metzger
[
23
]
.
We note that properties of the IDS have been studied more than its construction.
For instance, the tail behavior of the IDS
N(\lambda)
as
\lambda
tends to the bottom of the spectrum, called
Lifshitz tails
,
has been extensively investigated for various models.

In this paper, we are interested in white noise potential, that is, centered
\delta
-correlated Gaussian field, in continuous settings.

Formally
, this can be viewed as a limiting model of discrete Anderson Hamiltonian with i.i.d. potential.
The problem is that white noise has low regularity and
can be realized only as a distribution-valued random variable.
Therefore, it is already not obvious to make sense of the Anderson Hamiltonian, let alone to construct the IDS.
Nevertheless, Fukushima and Nakao
[
14
]

constructed the IDS of the Anderson Hamiltonian with one-dimensional white noise by using the theory of Dirichlet forms.
Moreover, it rigorously proved the result from physicists
that the IDS has an exact form, which enables us to study the Lifshitz tails.
For later development of the Anderson Hamiltonian with one-dimensional white noise,
see Dumaz and Labbé
[
13
]
and the references given in its introduction.

Mathematical treatment of continuous Anderson Hamiltonians with white noise in higher dimensions
had been out of reach until the theory of regularity structures
[
18
]
of Hairer
and the theory of paracontrolled distributions
[
17
]
of Gubinelli, Imkeller and Perkowski emerged.
Both theories provide rigorous approaches to so-called
singular SPDEs
, SPDEs inaccessible by classical approaches
due to the presence of ill-defined nonlinear terms or products.
For examples, they enable us to solve the PAM with white noise in two and three dimensions,
as shown in
[
17
]

and Hairer and Labbé
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
.
There, the solution of the PAM is given by
the limit in probability of the solution of the PAM with the renormalized potential
V_{\varepsilon}-c_{\varepsilon}

as
\varepsilon\to 0+
, where

V_{\varepsilon}
is smoothed white noise and
c_{\varepsilon}
is an appropriately chosen constant diverging to infinity.
It is believed that the PAM with white noise in four and higher dimensions cannot be solved.

Inspired by these theories, Allez and Chouk
[
2
]
constructed continuous Anderson Hamiltonians with white noise on
two-dimensional toruses, followed by Labbé
[
28
]
and Gubinelli, Ugurcan and Zachhuber
[
16
]

to construct continuous Anderson Hamiltonians
with white noise in three dimensions and
by Mouzard
[
30
]
to construct Anderson Hamiltonians on two-dimensional manifolds.
These works showed that the spectrum of the Anderson Hamiltonian consists of the eigenvalues.
Asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalues as the underlying boxes grow is studied in Chouk and van Zuijlen
[
11
]
, which is
crucially used in König, Perkowski and van Zuijlen
[
27
]

to prove the long time asymptotics of the mass of the two-dimensional PAM.
We also remark that
the work
[
16
]
studies nonlinear wave equations and nonlinear Schrödinger equations whose
linear parts are given by the Anderson Hamiltonian with white noise in two and three dimensions.

Main result.

The aim of this paper is to construct the IDS of the Anderson Hamiltonian with white noise in two dimensions and
determine the Lifshitz tail asymptotics.
Let
\{\lambda_{n,L}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be the spectrum of the Anderson Hamiltonian on
Q_{L}\vcentcolon=[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{2}

with Dirichlet boundary conditions. We denote by
N_{L}(\lambda)

the distribution function
L^{-2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\lambda_{n,L}\leq\lambda\}}
.
The main theorem of this paper is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Almost surely, the measures
\{N_{L}(d\lambda)\}_{L}
converge vaguely to some limit
N(d\lambda)
.
The measure
N(d\lambda)
is deterministic and if we set
N(\lambda)\vcentcolon=N((-\infty,\lambda])
,

\lim_{\lambda\to-\infty}\frac{1}{-\lambda}\log N(\lambda)=-\kappa^{-1},

(1.2)

where
\kappa
is the best constant of Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality (a special case of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality):

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{4}dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{2}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\lvert\nabla f(x)\rvert^{2}dx.

(1.3)

The proof is given in Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.5
.
The constant
\kappa
has appeared in many related works. For instance, the work
[
4
]
of Bass and Chen shows that
we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbb{P}(\gamma([0,1]_{\leq}^{2})\geq t)=-\kappa^{-1},

where
\gamma
is the
self-intersection local time
(SILT) of the two-dimensional Brownian motion and

[0,1]_{\leq}^{2}\vcentcolon=\{(s,t)\in[0,1]^{2}\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}s\leq t\}
.
In
[
11
]
, the constant
\kappa
is used to describe the eigenvalue asymptotics of the Anderson Hamiltonian
with two-dimensional white noise.
Both results will be crucially used in this work.

The moment of the PAM and the exponential moment of the SILT are related through the Feynman-Kac formula.
By exploiting this relation, Gu and Wu
[
15
]
proved that the PAM with two-dimensional white noise has
finite moment for small
t
and conjectured that the moment explodes for large
t
.
As an easy application of Theorem
1.1
and ideas from its proof, the following is proved,
whose proof is given in Corollary
4.8
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
u=u(t,x)
be the solution of the PAM in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with white noise potential and with
u(0,\cdot)=\delta_{0}
.
Then,

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,0)]\begin{cases}<\infty\quad&\mbox{if }t\in(0,\kappa^{-1}),\\
=\infty\quad&\mbox{if }t\in(\kappa^{-1},\infty).\end{cases}

We now discuss the strategy of proving Theorem
1.1
.
A standard approach to the proof of (
1.1
) is the path integral approach (
[
32
]
,

[
7
, Section VI.1.2]
), namely the approach to prove
the convergence of the Laplace transform.
An advantage of this approach is to obtain a representation of the Laplace transform.
The convergence of the Laplace transform can be proved as a consequence of the ergodic theorem applied to
the heat kernel of the Anderson Hamiltonian. There, the Feynman-Kac formula plays an important role as a technical tool.
However, in our setting, what the Feynman-Kac formula for the PAM with white noise should be is not obvious, since the white noise is not a function.
Although the work
[
27
]
derived a Feynman-Kac representation for the PAM on a box, this is not useful for our problem.
To overcome the lack of useful Feynman-Kac formula, we view the heat kernel as the solution of the PAM with initial condition
\delta_{0}
and
use moment estimate of the solution as technical input to apply the ergodic theorem.
For the moment estimate, we exploit the SILT in the spirit of
[
15
]
.
However, since we have to work on the PAM with initial condition
\delta_{0}
, we have to use the SILT of Brownian bridges.
It will be revealed that basic properties do not change between the SILT of the Brownian motion and that of Brownian bridges.

The proof of (
1.2
) consists of two steps. We first show

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N(d\lambda)\begin{cases}<\infty,\quad&\mbox{if }t\in(0,\kappa^{-1}),\\
=\infty,\quad&\mbox{if }t\in(\kappa^{-1},\infty),\end{cases}

by applying the estimate of the exponential moment of the SILT of a Brownian bridge.
This readily implies
\limsup_{\lambda\to-\infty}(-\lambda)^{-1}\log N(\lambda)=-\kappa^{-1}
.
We next show

\liminf_{\lambda\to-\infty}(-\lambda)^{-1}\log N(\lambda)\geq-\kappa^{-1}

(1.4)

by using the tail estimate of
the first eigenvalue of the Anderson Hamiltonian obtained in
[
11
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

Let us describe the relation between our result and those of other Gaussian potentials. Fukushima and Nakao

[
14
]
showed that the IDS
N^{\operatorname{1D}}
with one-dimensional white noise satisfies

\log N^{\operatorname{1D}}(\lambda)\sim-c^{\operatorname{1D}}(-\lambda)^{\frac{3}{2}}
as
\lambda\to-\infty

for some constant
c_{\operatorname{1D}}\in(0,\infty)
.
As shown in Pastur
[
34
]
, Nakao
[
32
]
and Kirsch and Martinelli
[
25
]
,
for a continuous centered Gaussain potential
U
, the IDS
N^{U}
satisfies

\log N^{U}(\lambda)\sim-(2\mathbb{E}[U(0)^{2}])^{-1}(-\lambda)^{2}
.
We will prove in Appendix
A
that for a centered Gaussian potential
V
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

\mathbb{E}[V(x)V(y)]=\lvert x-y\rvert^{-\alpha},\quad\alpha<\min\{d,2\},

the IDS
N^{V}
satisfies
\log N^{V}(\lambda)\sim-c^{V}(-\lambda)^{\frac{4-\alpha}{2}}
.
Thus, the decay of the Lifshitz tail is connected with the scaling property of the Gaussian potential.
Our main result, Theorem
1.1
, is distinct in that we establish this connection in the setting where renormalization is necessary.

It turns out that leading coefficients of Lifshitz tails are also connected with covariances of Gaussian potentials. For this regard,
see Remark
A.2
.

For further topics such as restricted IDS and the Wegner estimate on Gaussian potentials, the reader is referred to the survey
[
29
]
by
Leschke, Müller and Warzel.

Lemma 2.7
.

For every

and

u_{\infty}^{x,y}(t,x)=u_{\infty}^{x-z,y+z}(t,x-z)

Proof.

For

\displaystyle u_{L,\varepsilon}^{x,y}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\{T_{y}\xi_{\varepsilon}(x+X^{t}_{s})-c_{\varepsilon}\}ds\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\{x+X^{t}_{[0,t]}\subseteq Q_{L}\}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\{T_{y+z}\xi_{\varepsilon}(x-z+X^{t}_{s})-c_{\varepsilon}\}ds\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\{x-z+X^{t}_{[0,t]}\subseteq Q_{2L}\}}\Big]

\displaystyle=u_{2L,\varepsilon}^{x-z,y+z}(t,x-z).

Hence
u_{\infty}^{x,y}(t,x)\leq u_{\infty}^{x-z,y+z}(t,x-z)

u_{\infty}^{x,y}(t,x)=u_{\infty}^{x-z,y+z}(t,x-z)

We next discuss relation between

Definition 2.8

We denote by

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{u}^{\phi}_{\infty,\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta\tilde{u}^{\phi}_{\infty,\varepsilon}+(\xi_{\varepsilon}-c_{\varepsilon})\tilde{u}^{\phi}_{\infty,\varepsilon},\,\,\mbox{in }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\

Proposition 2.9
.

For each

Proof.

By the Feynman-Kac formula

\tilde{u}^{\delta_{x}}_{\infty,\varepsilon}(t,y)=p_{t}(x-y)\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\{\xi_{\varepsilon}(y+B_{s})-c_{\varepsilon}\}ds\Big)\Big|B_{t}=x-y\Big].

(2.2)

Therefore, by comparing (

If we set

\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}
-a.s.

\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}
-a.s.

3

Self-intersection local time of Brownian bridges

Here we study SILT of two-dimensional Brownian bridges, which is used to
construct the IDS of the Anderson Hamiltonian with white noise in Section
4
.
The SILT
\gamma
of the Brownian motion
B
has been extensively studied, see
[
9
]
and references therein.
It is formally given by

\gamma([0,t]_{\leq}^{2})=\iint_{[0,t]_{\leq}^{2}}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-B_{r})drds.

For rigorous construction, one naturally considers the limit
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\iint_{[0,t]_{\leq}^{2}}p_{\varepsilon}(B_{s}-B_{r})drds
. However, this limit does not converge. In fact, the construction of

\gamma([0,t]_{\leq}^{2})
requires renormalization, and hence we have the following
definition.

Definition 3.1
.

We set

\gamma([0,t]_{\leq}^{2})\vcentcolon=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\iint_{[0,t]_{\leq}^{2}}\{p_{\varepsilon}(B_{s}-B_{r})-\mathbb{E}[p_{\varepsilon}(B_{s}-B_{r})]\}drds,

where the convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
is proved by
[
9
, Theorem 2.3.2 and Theorem 2.4.1]
.
We note that the convergence actually takes place in
L^{p}(\mathbb{P})
for every
p\in(0,\infty)
by (
3.7
).

By the scaling property of
B
, we have
\gamma([0,t]_{\leq}^{2})\overset{\operatorname{d}}{=}t\gamma([0,1]_{\leq}^{2})
.
According to
[
4
]
or
[
9
, Theorem 4.3.1]
, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mathbb{P}(\gamma([0,1]_{\leq}^{2})\geq t)=-\kappa^{-1},

where
\kappa
is the best constant of Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality (
1.3
).

Recall from Notation that we denote by
X^{t}
the Brownian bridge with

X^{t}_{0}=X^{t}_{t}=0
.
The aim of this section is to prove this form of the large deviation where the Brownian motion is replaced by the Brownian bridge
X^{1}
.
The following lemma serves as an important technical tool.
Note that the process
(B_{s}-\frac{s}{t}(B_{t}-x))_{s\in[0,t]}
has the same law as the Brownian bridge from
0
at
s=0
to
x
at
s=t
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
32
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
u\in(0,t)
. We define the probability measure
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
by

d\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\vcentcolon=\Big(\frac{t}{t-u}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}u}{2t(t-u)}-\frac{\lvert B_{u}\rvert^{2}}{2(t-u)}+\frac{\langle x,B_{u}\rangle}{t-u}\Big)d\mathbb{P}.

Then, the law of
(B_{s})_{s\in[0,u]}
under
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
is that of
(B_{s}-\frac{s}{t}(B_{t}-x))_{s\in[0,u]}
under
\mathbb{P}
.

Definition 3.3
.

For a Borel set
A\subseteq[0,t]_{\leq}^{2}
, we set

\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}(A)\vcentcolon=\iint_{A}p_{\varepsilon}(X^{t}_{s}-X^{t}_{r})drds

and

\beta_{\varepsilon}(A)\vcentcolon=\iint_{A}p_{\varepsilon}(B_{s}-B_{r})drds.

In addition, we set

\alpha_{\varepsilon}(A)\vcentcolon=\iint_{A}p_{\varepsilon}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})drds,

where
\tilde{B}
is an independent copy of
B
.

The SILT of
X^{t}
should be given as the limit of
\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([0,t]_{\leq}^{2})-\mathbb{E}[\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([0,t]_{\leq}^{2})]
as
\varepsilon\to 0+
.
We first estimate the renormalization constant
\mathbb{E}[\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([0,t]_{\leq}^{2})]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
t\in(0,\infty)
and
0\leq a\leq b\leq c\leq d\leq t
, we have, as
\varepsilon\to 0+
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([a,b]_{\leq}^{2})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big[(b-a)\log\varepsilon^{-1}+(b-a)\log t

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{0}^{b-a}\{\log s-\log(t-s)\}ds\Big]+o(1),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([a,b]\times[c,d])]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{c-a}^{d-a}\{\log s-\log(t-s)\}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{c-b}^{d-b}\{\log s-\log(t-s)\}ds+o(1).

Proof.

Let
s\geq r
. Since
(X^{t}_{s})_{s\in[0,t]}\overset{\operatorname{d}}{=}(B_{s}-\frac{s}{t}B_{t})_{s\in[0,t]}
,
X^{t}_{s}-X^{t}_{r}
is centered Gaussian with

\mathbb{E}[(X^{t}_{s}-X^{t}_{r})^{2}]=s-r-\frac{(s-r)^{2}}{t}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle 2\pi\varepsilon\mathbb{E}[p_{\varepsilon}(X_{s}^{t}-X_{r}^{t})]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\Big(s-r-\frac{(s-r)^{2}}{t}\Big)\lvert x\rvert^{2}\Big)\frac{e^{-\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{2}}}{2\pi}dx

\displaystyle=\Big(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(s-r-\frac{(s-r)^{2}}{t}\Big)\Big)^{-1}

and

\displaystyle 2\pi\iint_{[a,b]_{\leq}^{2}}\mathbb{E}[p_{\varepsilon}(X_{s}^{t}-X_{r}^{t})]drds

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{s}\frac{dr}{\varepsilon+s-r-\frac{(s-r)^{2}}{t}}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\int_{0}^{s-a}\frac{dr}{\varepsilon+r-\frac{r^{2}}{t}}\,ds.

We compute

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s-a}\frac{dr}{\varepsilon+r-\frac{r^{2}}{t}}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s-a}\frac{dr}{\varepsilon+\frac{t}{4}-\frac{1}{t}(r-\frac{t}{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{\sqrt{4\varepsilon t+t^{2}}}\int_{0}^{s-a}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon t+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}+r-\frac{t}{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon t+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}-r+\frac{t}{2}}\Big)dr

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{\sqrt{4\varepsilon t+t^{2}}}\Big\{\log\Big(\sqrt{\varepsilon t+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}-\frac{t}{2}+s-a\Big)-\log\Big(\sqrt{\varepsilon t+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}-\frac{t}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\log\Big(\sqrt{\varepsilon t+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}+\frac{t}{2}-s+a\Big)+\log\Big(\sqrt{\varepsilon t+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}+\frac{t}{2}\Big)\Big\}.

Thus,
2\pi\iint_{[a,b]_{\leq}^{2}}\mathbb{E}[p_{\varepsilon}(X_{s}^{t}-X_{r}^{t})]drds
equals to

\displaystyle\frac{t}{\sqrt{4\varepsilon t+t^{2}}}\Big[(b-a)\log\Big(\sqrt{\varepsilon t+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}+\frac{t}{2}\Big)-(b-a)\log\Big(\sqrt{\varepsilon t+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}-\frac{t}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{0}^{b-a}\Big\{\log\Big(\sqrt{\varepsilon t+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}-\frac{t}{2}+s\Big)-\log\Big(\sqrt{\varepsilon t+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}+\frac{t}{2}-s\Big)\Big\}ds\Big]

To estimate
\mathbb{E}[\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([a,b]_{\leq}^{2})]
, it remains to compute

\displaystyle\log\Big(\sqrt{\varepsilon t+\frac{t^{2}}{4}}-\frac{t}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\log\frac{t}{2}+\log\Big(\sqrt{1+\frac{4\varepsilon}{t}}-1\Big)

\displaystyle=\log\frac{t}{2}+\log\Big(\frac{2\varepsilon}{t}+o(\varepsilon)\Big)=\log\varepsilon+o(1).

Similarly, we compute

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}2\pi\mathbb{E}[\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([a,b]\times[c,d])]

\displaystyle=\int_{c}^{d}\int_{s-b}^{s-a}\Big\{\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{t-r}\Big\}drds

\displaystyle=\int_{c}^{d}\{\log(s-a)-\log(s-b)-\log(t-s+a)+\log(t-s+b)\}ds.\qed

Remark 3.5
.

Similar calculation yields

\mathbb{E}[\beta_{\varepsilon}([a,b]_{\leq}^{2})]=\frac{b-a}{2\pi}\{\log\varepsilon^{-1}+\log(b-a)-1\}+o(1).

Theorem 3.7
.

Let

(i)

For

(iii)

For every

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big\{\lambda(\chi_{\varepsilon}^{1}([0,1]_{\leq}^{2})-\mathbb{E}[\chi_{\varepsilon}^{1}([0,1]_{\leq}^{2})])\Big\}\Big]<\infty.

Proof.

(i) Suppose
d<t
. Then, by Lemma

\mathbb{E}[\lvert\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([a,b]\times[c,d])-\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}([a,b]\times[c,d])\rvert^{p}]\\
\leq\frac{t}{t-d}\mathbb{E}[\lvert\beta_{\varepsilon}([a,b]\times[c,d])-\beta_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}([a,b]\times[c,d])\rvert^{p}]\to 0\quad(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0+).

It remains to consider the case
a=0
and

Let
\delta\in(0,\min\{b,t-c,\frac{t}{4}\})

The first two terms converges in

The key is to observe that, under the condition that

the processes
(X^{t}_{s})_{s\in[0,\frac{t}{2}]}

(X_{t-s}^{t})_{s\in[0,\frac{t}{2}]}

from

at

Let
\tilde{Y}
and
\tilde{B}

and

respectively.
Then, since

the preceding remark implies

\mathbb{E}[\lvert\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([0,\delta]\times[t-\delta,t])-\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}([0,\delta]\times[t-\delta,t])\rvert^{p}]\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}dxp_{\frac{t}{4}}(x)\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\lvert\iint_{[0,\delta]^{2}}\{p_{\varepsilon}(Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{r})-p_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{r})\}\Big\rvert^{p}\Big]

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\lvert\iint_{[0,\delta]^{2}}\{p_{\varepsilon}(Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{r})-p_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{r})\}\Big\rvert^{p}\Big]\\
=\frac{t}{t-2\delta}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(-\frac{4\delta\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{t(t-2\delta)}-\frac{\lvert B_{\delta}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\tilde{B}_{\delta}\rvert^{2}}{t-2\delta}+\frac{2\langle x,B_{\delta}+\tilde{B}_{\delta}\rangle}{t-2\delta}\Big)\\
\times\lvert\alpha_{\varepsilon}([0,\delta]^{2})-\alpha_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}([0,\delta]^{2})\rvert^{p}\Big].

Therefore, by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\mathbb{E}[\lvert\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([0,\delta]\times[t-\delta,t])-\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}([0,\delta]\times[t-\delta,t])\rvert^{p}]\\
\leq\frac{t}{t-2\delta}\mathbb{E}[\lvert\alpha_{\varepsilon}([0,\delta]^{2})-\alpha_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}([0,\delta]^{2})\rvert^{2p}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}dxp_{\frac{t}{4}}(x)e^{-\frac{4\delta\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{t(t-2\delta)}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(-\frac{2\lvert B_{\delta}\rvert^{2}}{t-2\delta}+\frac{4\langle x,B_{\delta}\rangle}{t-2\delta}\Big)\Big].

We compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(-\frac{2\lvert B_{\delta}\rvert^{2}}{t-2\delta}+\frac{4\langle x,B_{\delta}\rangle}{t-2\delta}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{2\delta\lvert u\rvert^{2}}{t-2\delta}+\frac{4\sqrt{\delta}\langle x,u\rangle}{t-2\delta}\Big)\frac{e^{-\frac{\lvert u\rvert^{2}}{2}}}{2\pi}du

\displaystyle=\frac{t-2\delta}{t}\exp\Big(\frac{8\delta\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{t(t-2\delta)}\Big)

and observe

\lim_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0+}\mathbb{E}[\lvert\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([0,\delta]\times[t-\delta,t])-\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}([0,\delta]\times[t-\delta,t])\rvert^{p}]=0

(ii) Set

3.4
, we have

\mathbb{E}[\chi^{1}_{t^{-1}\varepsilon}([0,1]_{\leq}^{2})]-c_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log t+o(1)
as

\varepsilon\to 0+
.
By Theorem
3.7
-(iii),

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)}\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big\{2t(\chi_{t^{-1}\varepsilon}^{1}([0,1]_{\leq}^{2})-\mathbb{E}[\chi_{t^{-1}\varepsilon}^{1}([0,1]_{\leq}^{2})])\big\}\big]<\infty

for
t\in(0,(2\kappa)^{-1})
.
Similarly, by applying Remark
3.8
, we obtain

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}p_{\varepsilon}(X^{t,1}_{s}-X^{t,2}_{r})drds\Big)\Big]<\infty

for
t\in(0,(2\kappa)^{-1})
.
Therefore,
if
t\in(0,(2\kappa)^{-1})
,

\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)}\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\tilde{u}_{\infty,\varepsilon}^{\delta_{0}}(t,0)^{2}]<\infty

and the first claim follows by Fatou’s lemma.

We move to prove the second claim. The uniform integrability yields

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,0)]=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\frac{1}{2\pi t}\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}\times\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}(\xi_{\varepsilon}(X_{s}^{t})-c_{\varepsilon})ds\Big)\Big].

Since

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\xi_{\varepsilon}(X^{t}_{s})ds\Big)\Big]=e^{\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([0,t]_{\leq}^{2})},

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,0)]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\frac{1}{2\pi t}\mathbb{E}[e^{\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([0,t]_{\leq}^{2})-c_{\varepsilon}t}]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\frac{1}{2\pi t}e^{t(\mathbb{E}[\chi^{1}_{t^{-1}\varepsilon}([0,1]_{\leq}^{2})]-c_{\varepsilon})}\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big\{t(\chi_{t^{-1}\varepsilon}^{1}([0,1]_{\leq}^{2})-\mathbb{E}[\chi_{t^{-1}\varepsilon}^{1}([0,1]_{\leq}^{2})])\big\}\big],

where we applied the scaling property of
X^{t}
.
Thus, the second claim follows from Lemma
3.4
and
Theorem
3.7
-(ii).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

If
t\in(0,(2\kappa)^{-1})
, then
\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}
-almost surely,

\lim_{L\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N_{L}(d\lambda)=\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,0)].

Proof.

We have by (
2.7
)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N_{L}(d\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-t\lambda_{n,L}}=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{L}}u_{L}^{x,0}(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{L}}u_{\infty}^{x,0}(t,x)dx-\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{L}}\{u_{\infty}^{x,0}(t,x)-u_{L}^{x,0}(t,x)\}dx.

By Lemma
2.7
, we have
u_{\infty}^{x,0}(t,x)=u_{\infty}^{0,x}(t,0)
.
Therefore, Proposition
B.1
and the ergodic theorem
[
21
, Theorem 10.14]
yield

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{L}}u_{\infty}^{0,x}(t,0)dx=\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,0)]\quad\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}\mbox{-a.s.}

Therefore, it suffices to show

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{L}}\{u_{\infty}^{x,0}(t,x)-u_{L}^{x,0}(t,x)\}dx=0\quad\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}\mbox{-a.s.}

(4.4)

Take
\delta\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{L}\setminus Q_{(1-\delta)L}}\{u_{\infty}^{x,0}(t,x)-u_{L}^{x,0}(t,x)\}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q_{L}\setminus Q_{(1-\delta)L}}u_{\infty}^{0,x}(t,0)dx

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\delta}L\Big(\int_{Q_{L}}u_{\infty}^{0,x}(t,0)^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus, by the ergodic theorem,

\limsup_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{L}\setminus Q_{(1-\delta)L}}\{u_{\infty}^{x,0}(t,x)-u_{L}^{x,0}(t,x)\}dx\leq\sqrt{2\delta}\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,0)^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}\mbox{-a.s.}

We move to estimate
\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{(1-\delta)L}}\{u_{\infty}^{x,0}(t,x)-u_{L}^{x,0}(t,x)\}dx
.
If
x\in Q_{(1-\delta)L}
, we have
-x+Q_{L}\supseteq Q_{\delta L}
and hence the Feynman-Kac formula implies

u_{L}^{x,0}(t,x)\geq u_{\delta L}^{0,x}(t,0)
.
If
L_{0}\leq L
, then

\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{(1-\delta)L}}\{u_{\infty}^{x,0}(t,x)-u_{L}^{x,0}(t,x)\}dx\leq\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{(1-\delta)L}}\{u_{\infty}^{0,x}(t,0)-u_{\delta L_{0}}^{0,x}(t,0)\}dx.

By the ergodic theorem again, we obtain,
\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}
-a.s.,

\limsup_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{(1-\delta)L}}\{u_{\infty}^{x,0}(t,x)-u_{L}^{x,0}(t,x)\}dx\leq\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u_{\infty}^{0,0}(t,0)-u_{\delta L_{0}}^{0,0}(t,0)].

By the monotone convergence theorem,
\lim_{L_{0}\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u_{\delta L_{0}}^{0,0}(t,0)]=\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u_{\infty}^{0,0}(t,0)]
.
Therefore, by letting
L_{0}\to\infty
and then
\delta\to 0+
, we complete the proof of (
4.4
)
and hence the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By Lemma
4.4
, if we set
T\vcentcolon=(2\kappa)^{-1}
, there exists
a
\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}
-full subset
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}_{1}
of
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}_{0}

such that for every
t\in(0,T)\cap\mathbb{Q}
we have

\lim_{L\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N_{L}(d\lambda)=\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,0)]\quad\mbox{ on }\mathscr{S}^{\prime}_{1}.

In the rest of the proof, we restrict the probability space for
\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}
to
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}_{1}
.
We fix
\tau\in(0,T)\cap\mathbb{Q}
and
\delta\in(0,\min\{\tau,T-\tau\})
and define the probability measure
\nu_{L}
by

\nu_{L}(d\lambda)\vcentcolon=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\tau\mu}N_{L}(d\mu)\Big)^{-1}e^{-\tau\lambda}N_{L}(d\lambda).

By Lemma
C.1
,
\nu_{L}
converges weakly to some limit
\nu
and if we define
N
by

N(d\lambda)\vcentcolon=\Big\{\lim_{L\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\tau\mu}N_{L}(d\mu)\Big\}e^{\tau\lambda}\nu(d\lambda),

then
N_{L}
converges vaguely to
N
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N(d\lambda)=\frac{t^{\frac{t}{2\pi}-1}}{2\pi}\mathbb{E}[e^{t\zeta}]

for
t\in(\tau-\delta,\tau+\delta)
.
But by Lemma
C.2
, this identity continues to hold for
t\in(0,\kappa^{-1})
.
To prove the last claim (
4.2
), suppose
\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N(d\lambda)<\infty

for some
t>\kappa^{-1}
. Then, Lemma
C.2
implies

\mathbb{E}[e^{t\zeta}]<\infty
, which contradicts Theorem
3.10
.
∎

We next study the Lifshitz tails of the IDS
N
constructed in Theorem
4.1
.
The goal is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
N
be the IDS of the Anderson Hamiltonian with two-dimensional white noise as constructed in Theorem
4.1
.
Then, the equation (
1.2
) holds.

From Theorem
4.1
, we easily deduce

A\vcentcolon=\limsup_{\lambda\to-\infty}\frac{1}{-\lambda}\log N(\lambda)=-\kappa^{-1}.

(4.5)

In fact,
A\leq-\kappa^{-1}
follows from Chebyshev’s inequality. To show the opposite inequality, suppose
A<-\kappa^{-1}
. Then,
there exist
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

N(\lambda)\leq Ce^{(1+\varepsilon){\kappa}^{-1}\lambda}
for
\lambda\in(-\infty,0)
.
This implies
\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N(d\lambda)<\infty
for
t\in(\kappa^{-1},(1+\varepsilon){\kappa}^{-1})
,
which contradicts Theorem
4.1
.

To the best of the author’s knowledge, the proof of the Lifshitz tails of the form

\lim_{\lambda\to\lambda_{0}}(-\lambda)^{a}\log N^{V}(\lambda)=b,

where
\lambda_{0}
is the bottom of the spectrum,
usually relies on the asymptotics of the Laplace transform

\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\tilde{a}}\log\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N(d\lambda)=\tilde{b}.

Indeed, the approach to study the asymptotics of the Laplace transform was first implemented in the case where
the random potential
V
is of the form

V(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x-y)\mu(dy),

where
\mu
is the Poisson random measure with respect to the Lebesgue measure and
\phi
is a nonnegative continuous function
which is not identically zero and satisfies
\phi(x)=o(\lvert x\rvert^{-d-2})
as
x\to\infty
.
The work
[
12
]
(see
[
32
]
as well) studied the asymptotics
of the Laplace transform of the IDS
N^{V}
and consequently proved

\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}\log N^{V}(\lambda)=-\gamma_{d}^{\frac{d}{2}},

where
\gamma_{d}
is the smallest eigenvalue of
-\frac{1}{2}\Delta
on the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Dirichlet boundary conditions.
This approach works for stationary, ergodic centered Gaussian potentials
V
which are not too singular.
See Appendix
A
for more detail.

However, this approach does not work in the case of the two-dimensional white noise, since the Laplace transform blows up.
We remark that the work
[
5
]
studied tail behaviors of distribution functions
which have finite exponential moment only for small parameter
t>0
.
In order to apply the result from
[
5
]
to our problem, we need to understand the behavior of

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N(d\lambda)=\frac{t^{\frac{t}{2\pi}-1}}{2\pi}\mathbb{E}[e^{t\zeta}]

as
t\to\kappa^{-1}-
. This seems very difficult, and therefore we take a different approach.

In view of (
4.5
), the proof of Theorem
4.5
comes down to
showing (
1.4
).
To obtain a lower bound of
N(\lambda)
, we use the inequality
N(\lambda)\geq\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[N_{1}(\lambda)]
,
which will be proved in Lemma
4.6
. This inequality is obtained by a well-known technique to use superadditivity of the Dirichlet eigenvalue counting functions
(
[
24
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
23
, Section 3]
).
Then, we prove (
1.4
)
by using the tail estimate of
the first eigenvalue of the Anderson Hamiltonian obtained in
[
11
]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

For every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
L\in(0,\infty)
, we have

N(\lambda)\geq\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[N_{L}(\lambda)]
.

Proof.

We set

N_{L,\varepsilon}(\lambda)\vcentcolon=L^{-2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\lambda_{n,L}^{\bm{\xi}_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}}}\leq\lambda\}}

and denote by
N_{\infty,\varepsilon}
the IDS of the
Anderson Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta-(\xi_{\varepsilon}-c_{\varepsilon})
.
By the continuity of the eigenvalues with respect to the enhanced noise

[
11
, Theorem 5.4]
,
we have
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\lambda_{n,L}^{\bm{\xi}_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}}}=\lambda_{n,L}^{\bm{\xi}_{L}^{\mathfrak{n}}}
for every
n
and hence

N_{L,\varepsilon}(d\lambda)
converges to
N_{L}(d\lambda)
vaguely.
Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N_{\infty,\varepsilon}(d\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi t}\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[e^{\chi^{t}_{\varepsilon}([0,t]_{\leq}^{2})-c_{\varepsilon}t}],

Theorem
3.7
-(ii) implies

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N_{\infty,\varepsilon}(d\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N(d\lambda)

for
t\in(0,\kappa^{-1})
.
Thus, by applying the same trick used in the proof of Theorem
4.1

to prove the vague convergence of
N_{L}
, we conclude
N_{\infty,\varepsilon}(d\lambda)
converges vaguely to
N(d\lambda)
.
We remark that
N_{\infty,\varepsilon}(d\lambda)|_{(-\infty,0)}
converges to

N_{\infty,\varepsilon}(d\lambda)|_{(-\infty,0)}
weakly.

Let
\mu>\lambda
. For each
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
, we have

N_{\infty,\varepsilon}(\mu)\geq\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[N_{L,\varepsilon}(\mu)]
. See
[
7
, Section VI.1.3]
, especially (VI.17).
Therefore, by letting
\varepsilon\to 0+
and applying Fatou’s lemma,
we obtain
N(\mu)\geq\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[N_{L}(\lambda)]
. By letting
\mu\to\lambda+
,
we obtain
N(\lambda)\geq\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[N_{L}(\lambda)]
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
L\in(0,\infty)
and
\mu>\kappa^{-1}
. Then, there exist
c\in(0,\infty)
and

\Lambda\in(-\infty,0)
such that

\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}(\lambda_{1,L}\leq\lambda)\geq 1-e^{c\lambda e^{\mu\lambda}},\quad\mbox{for }\lambda\leq\Lambda.

Proof.

We denote by
\bm{\lambda}(Q_{L},\beta)
the largest eigenvalue of
\Delta+\beta\xi
. Then, one has

\lambda_{1,L}=-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\lambda}(Q_{L},2)\quad\text{in law}.

(4.6)

By
[
11
, Theorem 2.6 and Theorem 2.7]
,
if
\mu^{\prime}>2\kappa^{-1}
, there exist
r\in(0,\infty)
and
M\in(0,\infty)
such that

\mathbb{P}(\bm{\lambda}(Q_{L},2)\geq 2x)\geq 1-\exp\Big(-\frac{2xe^{2\log L-\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{2^{2}}(2x)}}{2r^{2}}\Big)=1-\exp\Big(-\frac{xe^{2\log L-\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{2}x}}{r^{2}}\Big)

(4.7)

for every
x\geq M
. Therefore, (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) prove the claim
(
\mu=\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{2}
,
c=\frac{e^{2\log L}}{r^{2}}
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

By Lemma
4.6
, we have

N(\lambda)\geq\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[N_{1}(\lambda)]\geq\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}(\lambda_{1,1}\leq\lambda)
.
Let
\mu>\kappa^{-1}
. If
-\lambda
is sufficiently large, then Lemma
4.7
yields

N(\lambda)\geq 1-e^{c\lambda e^{\mu\lambda}}\geq\frac{c(-\lambda)}{2}e^{\mu\lambda}.

Therefore,
\liminf_{\lambda\to-\infty}(-\lambda)^{-1}\log N(\lambda)\geq-\mu
.
Since
\mu>\kappa^{-1}
is arbitrary, we complete the proof of (
1.4
)
and hence the proof of Theorem
4.5
.
∎

Finally, we apply Theorem
4.1
to the moment of the PAM in the plane.

Corollary 4.8
.

As before, let
u
be the solution of the PAM in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with initial condition
\delta_{0}
.
Then, we have

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,0)]\begin{cases}<\infty\qquad&\mbox{if }t<\kappa^{-1},\\
=\infty&\mbox{if }t>\kappa^{-1}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, for
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\kappa^{-1}\}
, we have

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,0)]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\lambda t}N(d\lambda)=\frac{t^{\frac{t}{2\pi}-1}}{2\pi}\mathbb{E}[e^{t\zeta}].

Proof.

If
t<\kappa^{-1}
, by Fatou’s lemma, the Feynman-Kac formula and Theorem
3.7
-(iii), we have

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,0)]\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u_{\varepsilon}(t,0)]=\frac{t^{\frac{t}{2\pi}-1}}{2\pi}\mathbb{E}[e^{t\zeta}]<\infty.

(4.8)

Suppose
t>0
.
If
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\lVert\phi\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
,
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}\phi(\lambda)N(d\lambda)=\lim_{L\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}\phi(\lambda)N_{L}(d\lambda)\leq\liminf_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{L}}u_{\infty}^{0,x}(t,0)dx,

where we used the identity (
2.7
).
By the ergodic theorem, as no assumption of integrability is needed, we have

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{L}}u_{\infty}^{0,x}(t,0)dx=\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u_{\infty}^{0,0}(t,0)],\qquad\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}\mbox{-a.s.}

Therefore, recalling Proposition
2.9
, we conclude

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N(d\lambda)\leq\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,0)].

(4.9)

Thus, the claims of Corollary
4.8
follows from
(
4.8
), (
4.9
) and
Theorem
4.1
.

Alternatively, we can prove the blow-up of the moment by applying a result on the first eigenvalue asymptotics.
Indeed, we have

\frac{1}{L^{2}}e^{-t\lambda_{1,L}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N_{L}(d\lambda)\leq\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{Q_{L}}u_{\infty}^{0,x}(t,0)dx.

By Lemma
4.7
, the moment of the left hand side explodes if
t>\kappa^{-1}
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

As the proof above suggests, Corollary
4.8
holds if

is replaced by

.

Remark 4.10
.

For
t\in(0,\kappa^{-1})
, we have
\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,x)]\leq\frac{t^{\frac{t}{2\pi}-1}}{2\pi}\mathbb{E}[e^{t\zeta}]<\infty
.
Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,x)]

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\tilde{u}^{\delta_{0}}_{\infty,\varepsilon}(t,x)]

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0+}p_{t}(x)\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\iint_{[0,t]_{\leq}^{2}}p_{\varepsilon}\Big(X^{t}_{s}-X^{t}_{r}-\frac{(s-r)x}{t}\Big)dsdr-c_{\varepsilon}t\Big)\Big].

As in the proof of (
3.14
), we observe

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\iint_{[0,t]_{\leq}^{2}}p_{\varepsilon}\Big(X^{t}_{s}-X^{t}_{r}-\frac{(s-r)x}{t}\Big)dsdr\Big)^{m}\Big]

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,t]_{\leq}^{2})^{m}}e^{-\varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert x_{j}\rvert^{2}}\mathbb{E}[e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle x_{j},X^{t}_{s_{j}}-X^{t}_{r_{j}}-\frac{s_{j}-r_{j}}{t}x\rangle}]\prod_{j=1}^{m}ds_{j}dr_{j}dx_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,t]_{\leq}^{2})^{m}}e^{-\varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert x_{j}\rvert^{2}}\mathbb{E}[e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle x_{j},X^{t}_{s_{j}}-X^{t}_{r_{j}}\rangle}]\prod_{j=1}^{m}ds_{j}dr_{j}dx_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\iint_{[0,t]_{\leq}^{2}}p_{\varepsilon}(X^{t}_{s}-X^{t}_{r})dsdr\Big)^{m}\Big]

and therefore

\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{[0,t]_{\leq}^{2}}p_{\varepsilon}\Big(X^{t}_{s}-X^{t}_{r}-\frac{(s-r)x}{t}\Big)dsdr-c_{\varepsilon}t\Big)\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}[e^{\chi_{\varepsilon}^{t}([0,t]_{\leq}^{2})-c_{\varepsilon}t}].

Hence,

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,x)]\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\frac{1}{2\pi t}\mathbb{E}[e^{\chi_{\varepsilon}^{t}([0,t]^{2}_{\leq})-c_{\varepsilon}t}]=\frac{t^{\frac{t}{2\pi}-1}}{2\pi}\mathbb{E}[e^{t\zeta}]<\infty.

However, we do not know whether
\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[u(t,x)]=\infty
for
t>\kappa^{-1}
.

Similarly, in view of (
3.7
), we have

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\tilde{u}^{\phi}_{\infty}(t,x)]<\infty
if
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
t\in(0,\kappa^{-1})
.
It is not known whether the moment blows up for
t>\kappa^{-1}
with general nonnegative initial condition,
especially for
\phi=\mathbbm{1}
.
Furthermore, we do not know whether the moment blows up or not at
t=\kappa^{-1}
.

Remark 4.11
.

The work
[
28
]
constructed the Anderson Hamiltonian
with three-dimensional white noise on the box
[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{3}
and showed that the spectrum consists of
the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n,L}^{\operatorname{3D}}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
It is natural to extend our work to this three-dimensional settings, namely
to prove the convergence of

N_{L}^{\operatorname{3D}}(\lambda)\vcentcolon=L^{-3}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\lambda_{n,L}^{\operatorname{3D}}\leq\lambda\}}

and the Lifshitz tail asymptotics.
We remark that the path integral approach is not available in this setting, as we expect

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}N^{\operatorname{3D}}(d\lambda)=\infty
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
In fact, we should expect from
Remark
A.2
the asymptotics

\lim_{\lambda\to-\infty}(-\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log N^{\operatorname{3D}}(\lambda)=-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{3}\kappa(3,3)},

(4.10)

where
\kappa(3,3)
is the best constant of the inequality

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{4}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{3}
.
For the construction of the IDS in this setting, we believe that
the functional analytic approach
[
7
, Section VI.1.3]
should be adopted.
We also believe that the lower bound of (
4.10
) can be obtained as in the proof
of (
1.4
), if we have a corresponding result of Lemma
4.7

for the three-dimensional white noise.

Appendix A
Lifshitz tails of Anderson Hamiltonians with generalized Gaussian potentials

Let
V
be the generalized centered Gaussian field in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose covariance is given by

\mathbb{E}^{V}[V(x)V(y)]=\nu\lvert x-y\rvert^{-\sigma}\qquad\sigma\in(0,\min\{2,d\}).

We denote by
V_{L}^{\mathfrak{n}}
the restriction of
V
on
Q_{L}=[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]^{d}
with Dirichlet boundary conditions.
Since the regularity of
V
is better than
-\frac{\sigma}{2}-\varepsilon
for every
\varepsilon>0
,
V_{L}^{\mathfrak{n}}
can be
enhanced to
\bm{V}_{L}^{\mathfrak{n}}
without renormalization, and hence we can define
\mathcal{H}_{L}^{\bm{V}_{L}^{\mathfrak{n}}}
.
As in Section
4
, we can prove that the eigenvalue counting measure associated with the distribution function

N_{L}^{V}(\lambda)\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\lambda_{n,L}^{\bm{V}_{L}^{\mathfrak{n}}}\leq\lambda\}}

converges vaguely to
N^{V}(d\lambda)
almost surely.
The measure
N^{V}(d\lambda)
is characterized by the identity

\sum_{j=-1}^{\infty}\rho_{j}(y)=1,\quad\mbox{for every }y\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

\lvert j-k\rvert\geq 2\implies\operatorname{supp}(\rho_{j})\cap\operatorname{supp}(\rho_{k})=\varnothing.

We set
\Delta_{i}w\vcentcolon=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\rho_{i}(k/L)\langle w,\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}
and

\rho^{\varodot}(k,l)\vcentcolon=\sum_{\lvert i-j\rvert\leq 1}\rho_{i}(k)\rho_{j}(l)
.
We have for every
\gamma\in(0,\infty)

\rho_{i}(x)\lesssim_{\gamma}\Big(\frac{2^{i}}{1+\lvert x\rvert}\Big)^{\gamma},\qquad i\geq-1,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(B.2)

As shown in the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 11.11]
, we have for
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

L\in[1,\infty)
and
y\in Q_{L}
,

\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}\frac{\rho^{\varodot}(\frac{k}{L},\frac{l}{L})^{2}(\lvert k\rvert^{\gamma}+\lvert l\rvert^{\gamma})}{(1+\pi^{2}L^{-2}\lvert l\rvert^{2})^{2}}\lvert\Delta_{i}(\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\mathfrak{n}_{l,L})(y)\rvert^{2}\lesssim_{L,\gamma}2^{\gamma i}.

(B.3)

We set

T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle],\qquad h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)\vcentcolon=T_{0}h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l).

Lemma B.2
.

There exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
L\in[1,\infty)
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
,

\lvert T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)\rvert\leq 1,\quad\lvert T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)-h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)\rvert\leq C\lvert k\rvert\lvert x\rvert.

Proof.

Since

\lvert T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)\rvert\leq\mathbb{E}[\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}[\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}

and
\mathbb{E}[\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle^{2}]=\lVert p_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\star\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\lVert\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rVert_{L^{2}(Q_{L})}=1
, we have the first claimed inequality. For the second inequality, as we have

T_{x}h_{\varepsilon,L}(k,l)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{\varepsilon}(y)\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}(z-x)\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}(z+y)\mathbbm{1}_{Q_{L}}(z-x)\mathbbm{1}_{Q_{L}}(z+y)dydz,

it suffices to note

\Big\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}(z-x)\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}(z+y)(\mathbbm{1}_{Q_{L}}(z-x)-\mathbbm{1}_{Q_{L}}(z))\mathbbm{1}_{Q_{L}}(z+y)dz\Big\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert,

\Big\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}(z-x)-\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}(z))\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}(z+y)\mathbbm{1}_{Q_{L}}(z)\mathbbm{1}_{Q_{L}}(z+y)dz\Big\rvert\leq C\lvert k\rvert\lvert x\rvert.\qed

Proposition B.3
.

For each
\alpha\in(-\frac{4}{3},-1)
and
L,M\in[1,\infty)
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0+}\sup_{x\in Q_{M}}\lVert T_{x}\bm{\xi}_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}}-T_{x}\bm{\xi}_{L,\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\rVert_{\mathfrak{X}_{\mathfrak{n},L}^{\alpha}}=0\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{P}^{\operatorname{WN}}\mbox{-probability.}

Proof.

Since

\Delta_{i}(T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon}-\xi_{L,\varepsilon})(y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}\rho_{i}(L^{-1}k)(\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle-\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle)\mathfrak{n}_{k,L},

we have

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\lvert\Delta_{i}(T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon}-\xi_{L,\varepsilon})(y)\rvert^{2}]=\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}\rho_{i}(L^{-1}k)\rho_{i}(L^{-1}l)\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}(y)\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}(y)\\
\times\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}\big[(\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle-\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle)(\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle-\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle)].

By Lemma
B.2
, for
\delta\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}\big[(\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle-\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle)(\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle-\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle)]

\displaystyle=\lvert h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)-T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)-T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(l,k)+h_{L,\varepsilon}(l,k)\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim(\lvert k\rvert^{\delta}+\lvert l\rvert^{\delta})\lvert x\rvert^{\delta}.

Therefore, by (
B.2
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\lvert\Delta_{i}(T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon}-\xi_{L,\varepsilon})(y)\rvert^{2}]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{L,\delta}2^{4i(1+\delta)}\lvert x\rvert^{\delta}\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}\frac{\lvert k\rvert^{\delta}+\lvert l\rvert^{\delta}}{(1+\lvert k\rvert)^{2+2\delta}(1+\lvert l\rvert)^{2+2\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\delta}2^{4i(1+\delta)}\lvert x\rvert^{\delta}.

On the other hand, we have

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\lvert\Delta_{i}(\xi_{L,\varepsilon}-\xi_{L,\varepsilon^{\prime}})(y)\rvert^{2}]\leq 2^{2i(1+\delta)}b(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}),

where
b(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime})\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}[\lVert\xi_{L,\varepsilon}-\xi_{L,\varepsilon^{\prime}}\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{-1-\delta}_{\mathfrak{n},L}}^{2}]
.
According to
[
27
, Theorem 6.1, Theorem 6.2 and Lemma 6.14]
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0+}b(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime})=0
.
Therefore, combining these two estimates,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}\big[\lvert\Delta_{i}\{(T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}}-T_{y}\xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}})-(T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{n}}-T_{y}\xi_{L,\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{n}})\}\rvert^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}\big[\lvert\Delta_{i}\{(T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}}-T_{y}\xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}})-(T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{n}}-T_{y}\xi_{L,\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{n}})\}\rvert^{2}\big]^{\delta}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}\big[\lvert\Delta_{i}\{(T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}}-T_{y}\xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}})-(T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{n}}-T_{y}\xi_{L,\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{n}})\}\rvert^{2}\big]^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{L,\delta}2^{2i(1+\delta)^{2}}b(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime})^{1-\delta}\lvert x-y\rvert^{\delta^{2}}.

Then, by Lemma
[
11
, Lemma 6.10]
,

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\lVert(T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}}-T_{y}\xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}})-(T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{n}}-T_{y}\xi_{L,\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{n}})\rVert_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{n},L}^{-(1+\delta)^{2}-\delta-\frac{2}{p}}}^{p}]\lesssim_{L,\delta,p}b(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime})^{\frac{p(1-\delta)}{2}}\lvert x-y\rvert^{\frac{p\delta^{2}}{2}}

for every
p\geq 2
. If
p\delta^{2}>4
, the multidimensional Kolmogorov continuity theorem implies

\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}\big[\sup_{x\in Q_{M}}\lVert T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}}-T_{x}\xi_{L,\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{n}}\rVert_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{n},L}^{-(1+\delta)^{2}-\delta-\frac{2}{p}}}]\lesssim_{L,\delta,p,M}b(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2(1-\delta)}.

Supposing
\delta\in(0,1)
are sufficiently small and
p
is sufficiently large, we have
the convergence of the first component.

The proof of the convergence of the second component is similar but more complicated.
We have

T_{x}\Xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}}-\Xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}\rho^{\varodot}(L^{-1}k,L^{-1}l)(1+\pi^{2}L^{-2}\lvert l\rvert^{2})^{-1}\\
\times(\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle-\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle)\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\lvert\Delta_{i}(T_{x}\Xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}}-\Xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}})(y)\rvert^{2}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},l_{1},l_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}\Big\{\prod_{j=1}^{2}\rho^{\varodot}(L^{-1}k_{j},L^{-1}l_{j})(1+\pi^{2}L^{-2}\lvert l_{j}\rvert^{2})^{-1}\Delta_{i}(\mathfrak{n}_{k_{j},L}\mathfrak{n}_{l_{j},L})(y)\Big\}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{2}(\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k_{j},L}\rangle\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l_{j},L}\rangle-\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k_{j},L}\rangle\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l_{j},L}\rangle)\Big].

By Wick’s Theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle]\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle]\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle]\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)^{2}+T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,k)T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(l,l)+T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(l,k).

By Lemma
B.2
, we obtain for
\delta\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}\big[(\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l,L}\rangle-\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\rangle)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\big\{h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)^{2}+h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,k)h_{L,\varepsilon}(l,l)+h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)h_{L,\varepsilon}(l,k)\big\}

\displaystyle-2\big\{h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)^{2}+T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,k)T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(l,l)+T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(k,l)T_{x}h_{L,\varepsilon}(l,k)\big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\lvert k\rvert^{\delta}+\lvert l\rvert^{\delta})\lvert x\rvert^{\delta}.

Thus, by (
B.3
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}[\lvert\Delta_{i}(T_{x}\Xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}}-\Xi_{L,\varepsilon}^{\mathfrak{n}})(y)\rvert^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},l_{1},l_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}\Big\{\prod_{j=1}^{2}\rho^{\varodot}(L^{-1}k_{j},L^{-1}l_{j})(1+\pi^{2}L^{-2}\lvert l_{j}\rvert^{2})^{-1}\lvert\Delta_{i}(\mathfrak{n}_{k_{j},L}\mathfrak{n}_{l_{j},L})(y)\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{WN}}\Big[(\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k_{j},L}\rangle\langle T_{x}\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l_{j},L}\rangle-\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{k_{j},L}\rangle\langle\xi_{\varepsilon},\mathfrak{n}_{l_{j},L}\rangle)^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert x\rvert^{\delta}\Big\{\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}\frac{\rho^{\varodot}(\frac{k}{L},\frac{l}{L})^{2}(\lvert k\rvert^{\delta}+\lvert l\rvert^{\delta})}{(1+\pi^{2}L^{-2}\lvert l\rvert^{2})^{2}}\lvert\Delta_{i}(\mathfrak{n}_{k,L}\mathfrak{n}_{l,L})(y)\rvert^{2}\Big\}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{L,\delta}\lvert x\rvert^{\delta}2^{2\delta i}

The remaining estimate can be completed as in the first half of the proof.
∎

Appendix C
Two lemmas on Laplace transforms

Lemma C.1
.

If a set of probability measures
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
on
\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\sup_{n\geq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{T\lvert\lambda\rvert}\nu_{n}(d\lambda)<\infty

for some
T\in(0,\infty)

and the Laplace transform
g_{n}(t)\vcentcolon=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}\nu_{n}(d\lambda)
converges in
[0,\infty)
for
t

in a dense subset of
(-T,T)
, then
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges weakly to a probability measure
\nu
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}\nu_{n}(d\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t\lambda}\nu(d\lambda),\quad t\in(-T,T).

Proof.

We regard
g_{n}
as an analytic function in
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\lvert\operatorname{Re}(z)\rvert<T\}
. Since
g_{n}
is uniformly bounded,
Montel’s theorem implies that
\{g_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is normal. Since
g_{n}(t)
converges in a dense subset of
(-T,T)
, uniqueness of
analytic continuation implies that the sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges to
g
uniformly on each compact set.
Then, Bochner’s theorem implies that there exists a probability measure
\nu
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-it\lambda}\nu(d\lambda)=g(it)

and the claim follows.
∎

Lemma C.2
.

Let
0<T_{1}<T_{2}<\infty
,
\nu
be a finite measure on
[0,\infty)
with

\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{t\lambda}\nu(d\lambda)<\infty
for
t<T_{1}
, and
g
be an analytic function
in
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\operatorname{Re}(z)\in(0,T_{2})\}
.
If the identity

\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{t\lambda}\nu(d\lambda)=g(t)

(C.1)

holds for
t\in(0,T_{1})
, then it holds for
t\in(0,T_{2})
.

Proof.

Set
T\vcentcolon=\sup\{t\in(0,T_{2})\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{t\lambda}\nu(d\lambda)<\infty\}
.
By uniqueness of analytic extension, it suffices to show
T=T_{2}
. Assume
T<T_{2}
.
Take
\tau\in(0,T)
and
r\in(0,\tau)
with
\tau+r<T_{2}
.
As (
C.1
) holds for
t\in(0,T)
, we have

\int_{[0,\infty)}\lambda^{k}e^{\tau\lambda}\nu(d\lambda)=g^{(k)}(\tau),

where
g^{(k)}
is the
k
th derivative of
g
. Then, by Fubini-Tonelli theorem,

\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{(\tau+r)\lambda}\nu(d\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{k}}{k!}\int_{[0,\infty)}\lambda^{k}e^{\tau\lambda}\nu(d\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{k}}{k!}g^{(k)}(\tau)=g(\tau+r)<\infty.

Since
T<\tau+r
, this contradicts the definition of
T
. Thus,
T=T_{2}
.
∎

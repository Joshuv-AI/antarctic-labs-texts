Affine representability of quadrics revisited
Aravind Asok

Abstract
The quadric Q 2 ​ n \operatorname{Q}_{2n} is the ℤ {\mathbb{Z}} -scheme defined by the equation ∑ i = 1 n x i ​ y i = z ⁡ ( 1 − z ) \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}=z(1-z) . We show that Q 2 ​ n \operatorname{Q}_{2n} is a homogeneous space for the split reductive group scheme SO 2 ​ n + 1 \operatorname{SO}_{2n+1} over ℤ {\mathbb{Z}} . The quadric Q 2 ​ n \operatorname{Q}_{2n} is known to have the 𝔸 1 {\mathbb{A}}^{1} -homotopy type of a motivic sphere and the identification as a homogeneous space allows us to give a characteristic independent affine representability statement for motivic spheres. This last observation allows us to give characteristic independent comparison results between Chow–Witt groups, motivic stable cohomotopy groups and Euler class groups.

Introduction

Assume
k
is an arbitrary commutative (unital) base ring. Consider the hypersurface
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
in
{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+1}_{k}:=\operatorname{Spec}k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n+1}]
cut out by the equation

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}x_{n+i}=x_{2n+1}(1-x_{2n+1});

\operatorname{Spec}k
. Let

be the standard split quadratic form
\sum_{i}x_{i}x_{n+i}+x_{2n+1}^{2}

2n+1

\operatorname{S}_{2n}
for the quadric hypersurface

{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+1}_{k}

If

\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}

\operatorname{S}_{2n}

\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n}

2
is invertible in

\operatorname{Q}_{2n}

\operatorname{S}_{2n}

\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}

[

The proof of Theorem

[

]
mentioned above, which after various algebro-geometric reductions follows from
[

. At the heart of those reductions is an interpretation of

in terms of conditions on traces and norms of split octonion algebras. This paper can be viewed in a similar vein:
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
acts transitively on vectors in a representation space satisfying suitable “norm” and “trace” conditions. The algebraic structures underlying the notions of “norm” and “trace” we use in this paper are quadratic Jordan algebras, and
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
may be interpreted as a “projective space” for such an algebra, in a sense that we explain momentarily. While the theory of (quadratic) Jordan algebras make no appearance in the proofs, it does suggest various avenues of generalization, so we add a few comments about this point of view here.

Assume
(J,1,U)
is quadratic Jordan algebra over a commutative ring
k
; for us,
J
is a finitely generated, projective
k
-module,
1
is a distinguished element of
J
and
U:J\to\operatorname{End}_{k}(J)
(
x\mapsto U_{x}
) is a quadratic map satisfying various identities (see
[
Jac69
, §1.2 Definition 3]
for the general definition; the cases of interest will even be obtained by base-change from
k={\mathbb{Z}}
). Of particular interest will be a class of quadratic Jordan algebras attached to quadratic spaces over
k
called a (quadratic) spin factors; see
[
Jac69
, Chapter 1.7]
. The special orthogonal group-schemes
\operatorname{SO}_{i}
act by automorphisms on quadratic spin factors, even when
2
is not invertible in
k
.

Given any quadratic Jordan algebra
(J,1,U)
over a commutative ring
k
, the quadratic map
U
allows one to speak of “projection operators”. Indeed, if one defines
x^{2}:=U_{x}1
, then a projection operator is one that satisfies
x^{2}=x
. Also attached to a quadratic spin factor is a suitable trace function, and one may consider “rank 1” projection operators by imposing a suitable trace condition. Granted these identifications, the scheme
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
is precisely the projective space attached to a quadratic spin factor and
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
acts by Jordan algebra automorphisms.

While the above interpretation does not aid in any calculations, it does suggest various natural generalizations of the main result. For example, one can study “octonionic projective space”
\operatorname{OP}^{2}:=\operatorname{F}_{4}/\operatorname{Spin}_{9}
over an arbitrary commutative ring as a suitable “projective space” of the exceptional quadratic Jordan algebra of Hermitian
3\times 3
-matrices over the octonions. Once again, this variety admits a description in terms of explicit “rank 1” projection operators. Because of the applications of Theorem
1
stated above, we have decided to present a proof less encumbered by additional notation and terminology, and we defer possible generalizations to projective spaces of more general quadratic Jordan algebras to future work.

2.1
Quadratic spaces and orthogonal groups

We begin by recalling some facts about orthogonal groups over
k
, in particular, we will not assume
2
is invertible in
k
. Assume
(V,q)
is a quadratic space over
k
; we will always assume that
V
is a projective
k
-module of constant rank, and that either
(V,q)
is regular (a.k.a. non-singular) or, if
V
has odd rank, semi-regular (see
[
Knu91
, I.3.2]
for the former and
[
Knu91
, §IV.3.1]
for the latter).

Special orthogonal groups

We write
\operatorname{O}(V,q)
for the associated orthogonal group of isometries of
(V,q)
. We will mostly be interested in the “standard” split quadratic spaces:

q_{2n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}x_{n+i},\;\;\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\;\;\;q_{2n+1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n+1})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}x_{n+1}+x_{2n+1}^{2}

on the free modules of rank
2n
and
2n+1
over
k
. We write
\operatorname{O}_{i}
for the orthogonal group scheme; functorially, this group scheme assigns to a
k
-algebra
R
the usual orthogonal group
\operatorname{O}(R^{\oplus i},q_{i})
of automorphisms of
R^{\oplus i}
preserving
q_{i}
. We also consider the group-scheme
\operatorname{GO}_{i}
assigning to a
k
-algebra
R
the group of orthogonal similitudes, i.e., the group of linear automorphisms of
R^{\oplus n}
that preserve
q_{i}
up to a unit.

Abusing notation slightly, we write
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
for the (constant) group scheme assigning to a
k
-algebra
R
the additive group of continuous functions
\operatorname{Spec}R\to{\mathbb{Z}}/2
. To discuss special orthgonal group schemes, recall that there is a Dickson invariant homomorphism

D:\operatorname{O}_{i}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}/2;

(see
[
Knu91
, IV.5.1]
) and a morphism
\chi:{\mathbb{Z}}/2\to\mu_{2}
defined by
f\mapsto(-1)^{f}
such that the composite
\chi\circ D
coincides with the determinant homomorphism
\det:\operatorname{O}_{i}\to\mu_{2}
. If
i=2n
, the Dickson invariant is split and surjective and one defines
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
to be the kernel of the Dickson invariant, while if
i=2n+1
, we define
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
to be the kernel of the composite map
\chi\circ D
. The group-scheme
\operatorname{GO}_{i}
has a subgroup scheme
\operatorname{GSO}_{i}
which is the fppf sub-group functor generated by
{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
and
\operatorname{SO}_{i}
. The group schemes
\operatorname{O}_{2n}
and
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
are both smooth
k
-group schemes; the group scheme
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
is a smooth
k
-group scheme, while
\operatorname{O}_{2n+1}
is a smooth
k
-group scheme if and only if
2
is a unit in
k
(see
[
Con14
, C.1.5]
for these assertion). Likewise, the group scheme
\operatorname{GSO}_{i}
is a smooth
k
-group scheme while
\operatorname{GO}_{i}
fails to be smooth if

is odd (see, e.g.,
[
Con14
, Remark C.3.11]
and the discussion just preceding that statement).

We view
(k^{\oplus 2n+1},q_{2n+1})
as a quadratic submodule of
(k^{\oplus 2n+2},q_{2n+2})
by the embedding

(e_{1},\ldots,e_{2n+1})\mapsto(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n},e_{2n+1},e_{n+1},\ldots,e_{2n+1}).

Likewise, we view
(k^{\oplus 2n},q_{2n})
as the subspace of
(k^{\oplus 2n+1},q_{2n+1})
where the coordinate function
x_{2n+1}
vanishes. These embeddings give rise to stabilization homomorphisms

\operatorname{O}_{i}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{O}_{i+1}.\;\;\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\;\;\operatorname{SO}_{i}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{SO}_{i+1}

that we will need in the sequel.

Bilinear forms, pointed quadratic spaces and traces

If
q
is a quadratic form, then we write
B
for the associated bilinear form obtained by polarization, i.e.,
B(x,y)=q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)
. When we consider
q_{i}
on
k^{\oplus i}
with coordinates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}
, the associated bilinear forms are

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{n+i}+x_{n+i}y_{i},\;\;\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\;\;\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{n+i}+x_{n+i}y_{i}+2x_{2n+1}y_{2n+1}

depending on whether
i
is even or odd.

Recall that a
pointed
quadratic space over a commutative ring
k
is a triple
(V,q,1)
where
(V,q)
is a quadratic space and
1\in V
such that
q(1)=1\in k
. We point the split quadratic spaces
(k^{\oplus i},q_{i})
as follows: for
i=2n
, we define
1\in k^{\oplus 2n}
to be
x_{n}=1,x_{2n}=1,x_{i}=x_{n+i}=0,i=1,\ldots,n-1
; for
i=2n+1
, we define
1\in k^{\oplus 2n+1}
by
x_{2n+1}=1
and
x_{i}=0,1\leq i\leq 2n
. With this convention, only the embedding
(k^{\oplus 2n+1},q_{2n+1})\to(k^{\oplus 2n+2},q_{2n+2})
is pointed.

Given a pointed quadratic space
(V,q,1)
, there is an associated trace function
t(x):=B(x,1)
. In the special case
(k^{\oplus 2n+2},q_{2n+2},1)
, this trace function is given by the formula

t_{2n+2}(x)=x_{n+1}+x_{2n+2}.

Note that with this convention,
(k^{\oplus 2n+1},q_{2n+1})
is contained in the orthogonal complement of the
k
-subspace
k\cdot 1
.

2.2
Orthogonal group actions

We now analyze various orthogonal group actions; what we say is well-known over a field, but we review the statements over an arbitrary base ring for the convenience of the reader. In order to streamline the analysis of actions over a general base, we begin by recalling some general facts about actions of reductive group schemes and homogeneous spaces by fiberwise techniques.

Homogeneous spaces for reductive group schemes

We recall the following result that allows us to deduce structural results about homogeneous spaces from “fiberwise” computations over geometric points. Recall that a reductive
k
-group scheme is a smooth affine
k
-group scheme
\operatorname{G}
such that the geometric fibers are
connected
reductive groups (e.g.,
[
Con14
, Definition 3.1.1]
). Unfortunately, we will need to deal with possibly disconnected group schemes and here the notion of geometric reductivity will be more useful for us; we refer the reader to
[
Alp14
, Definition 9.1.1]
for a modern treatment of this notion.

Proposition 2.2.1
.

Assume
\operatorname{G}
is an equidimensional (finitely presented) reductive
k
-group scheme with connected fibers and
(X,x)
is an equidimensional, pointed, finitely presented smooth affine
k
-scheme equipped with an action of
\operatorname{G}
. Consider the orbit morphism:

\tilde{\varphi}:\operatorname{G}\longrightarrow X

sending
g
to
g\cdot x
. Write
\operatorname{G}_{x}
for the stabilizer subgroup scheme of
\operatorname{G}
. If for every geometric point
s
of
\operatorname{Spec}k
the action of
\operatorname{G}_{\kappa(s)}
on
X_{\kappa(s)}
is transitive, then

1.

\tilde{\varphi}
is finitely presented, faithfully flat and factors through a (pointed) isomorphism
\varphi:\operatorname{G}/\operatorname{G}_{x}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptscriptstyle{\sim}}}}{{\;\rightarrow\;}}}X
(in particular,
\operatorname{G}/\operatorname{G}_{x}
exists as a scheme), and

2.

the group scheme
\operatorname{G}_{x}
is a finitely presented flat affine group scheme that is moreover geometrically reductive;

3.

if for every geometric point
s\in\operatorname{Spec}k
, the fiber
(\operatorname{G}_{x})_{s}
is regular, then
\operatorname{G}_{x}
is a finitely presented smooth affine
k
-group scheme,
\operatorname{G}_{x}/\operatorname{G}_{x}^{\circ}
is a finite étale
k
-group scheme, and
\operatorname{G}_{x}^{\circ}
is reductive.

Proof.

The first assertion is the implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) from
[
DG70
, III §3 Proposition 2.1]
. We repeat the proof of the result to clarify and strengthen the conclusions. Since
\operatorname{G}
and
X
are both finitely presented over
k
,
\tilde{\varphi}
is finitely presented
[
Gro64
, 1.6.2(v)]
. In that case, since
\operatorname{G}
is
k
-smooth and affine by assumption as a reductive
k
-group scheme, the fibral flatness criterion of
[
Gro66
, 11.3.10]
states that
\tilde{\varphi}
is flat if and only if the fibers of
\tilde{\varphi}
at points
s
of
\operatorname{Spec}k
are flat. Since flatness can be checked after faithfully flat extension, we reduce to considering the geometric fibers of
\tilde{\varphi}
.

Since
X
is smooth, it is automatically reduced. By generic flatness
[
Sta21
,
Proposition 052B
]
, there is a dense open subscheme
U
of
X
such that
\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(U)\to U
is flat and finitely presented. In that case, the transitivity of the action guarantees that we can cover
X
by translates of
U
and thus conclude that
\tilde{\varphi}
is faithfully flat. In conjunction with the discussion of the preceding paragraph,
\tilde{\varphi}
is fppf. Moreover the geometric fibers of
\tilde{\varphi}
are isomorphic to
(\operatorname{G}_{x})_{s}
, so if they are regular, then
\tilde{\varphi}
is smooth.

Since
\operatorname{G}_{x}\to\operatorname{G}
is a closed subgroup scheme by construction, we see that
\operatorname{G}_{x}
is a finitely presented, flat, affine
k
-group scheme. In that case, the second assertion is “Matsushima’s theorem”. In more detail, by
[
Alp14
, Theorem 9.4.1]
we conclude that
\operatorname{G}_{x}
is geometrically reductive group scheme. In that case, note that
\operatorname{G}_{x}/\operatorname{G}_{x}^{\circ}
is necessarily also a geometrically reductive
k
-group scheme.

If
\operatorname{G}_{x}
happens to be smooth, which as we observed in the previous paragraph happens if and only if its geometric fibers are regular, then
[
Alp14
, Theorem 9.7.6]
guarantees that
\operatorname{G}_{x}
is geometrically reductive if and only if
\operatorname{G}_{x}^{\circ}
is reductive and
\operatorname{G}_{x}/\operatorname{G}_{x}^{\circ}
is finite. In that case, it is automatically also smooth and therefore étale.
∎

Remark 2.2.2
.

In Proposition
2.2.1
, to guarantee smoothness of the fibers, the hypothesis that geometric fibers of
\operatorname{G}_{x}
are smooth is essential. For example, the morphism
{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}\to{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
given by

is transitive at the level of geometric points and makes the source into the total space of a
\mu_{n}
-torsor over the target. Thus, if
k
is a field having characteristic
p

p|n
, the morphism in question is flat but not smooth since it has non-reduced geometric fibers.

Checking transitivity

In order to apply Proposition

Representation spaces

Consider the standard action of

Proposition 2.2.5

The action of
\operatorname{GSO}_{2n+2}

{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+2}_{k}\smallsetminus\{{q_{2n+2}=0}\}
is transitive on geometric points and taking the orbit through
1
, there is an induced isomorphism of schemes

\operatorname{GSO}_{2n+2}/\operatorname{O}_{2n+1}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\;\longrightarrow\;}}}{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+2}_{k}\smallsetminus\{{q_{2n+2}=0}\}.

Proof.

Suppose
x
is a geometric point of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+2}_{k}\smallsetminus\{{q_{2n+2}=0}\}
. Henceforth, we write
q
for
q_{2n+2}
. By construction
q(x)\neq 0
and
q(1)=1\neq 0
. Since
k
is algebraically closed, we can always find
\lambda\in k^{\times}
such that
q(\lambda x)=q(1)=1
. In that case, if
q(\lambda x-1)\neq 0
, then the reflection
r_{\lambda x-1}
moves
\lambda x
to
1
(Lemma
2.2.4
.1), and the composite of scaling by
\lambda
and
r_{\lambda x-1}
lies in
\operatorname{GSO}_{2n+2}
.

Thus, suppose that
q(\lambda x-1)=0
. In that case, since
\lambda x\neq 0
, we know that
\lambda x
has a non-zero coordinate, and taking the standard basis vector
e_{i}
for suitable

we see that
B(\lambda x,e_{i})
is non-zero, i.e., the locus
B(\lambda x,-)\neq 0
is a non-empty open subscheme of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+2}_{k}
. Likewise, the locus
B(1,-)\neq 0
is a non-empty open subscheme of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+2}_{k}
. Since
k
is algebraically closed and thus infinite, it follows that the intersection of
B(\lambda x,-)\neq 0
and
B(1,-)\neq 0
with
q(-)\neq 0
is non-empty. Therefore, by Lemma
2.2.4
.2 there exists a composite of reflections taking
\lambda x
to
1
.

Appealing to the first two points of Proposition
2.2.1
, there is an induced isomorphism

\operatorname{GSO}_{2n+2}/\operatorname{Stab}_{1}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\;\longrightarrow\;}}}{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+2}_{k}\smallsetminus\{{q_{2n+2}=0}\},

and it remains to identify
\operatorname{Stab}_{1}
, which is a geometrically reductive flat affine
k
-group scheme. To this end, we analyze the fibers of
\operatorname{Stab}_{1}
over points of
\operatorname{Spec}k
. Note that any element of
\operatorname{GSO}_{2n+2}
that stabilizes

necessarily fixes the restriction of
q
to the orthogonal complement to
k\cdot 1
. In other words, any element of
\operatorname{GSO}_{2n+2}
that stabilizes
1
preserves the form
q_{2n+1}
and thus lies in
\operatorname{O}_{2n+1}
. Conversely any element of
\operatorname{O}_{2n+1}
stabilizes
1
, so we conclude that
\operatorname{Stab}_{1}=\operatorname{O}_{2n+1}
. Note: we cannot appeal to the third point of Proposition
2.2.1
as
\operatorname{O}_{2n+1}
fails to be a smooth group scheme.
∎

Remark 2.2.6
.

In line with the discussion of the introduction, Proposition
2.2.5
is a special case of a more general statement relating the structure group of quadratic Jordan algebra and the automorphism group. General results of
[
Loo79
, Corollary 6.6]
guarantee that the structure group of a separable, unital, quadratic Jordan algebra is a reductive group scheme. The automorphism group of the Jordan algebra can be identified with the subgroup scheme of the structure group scheme that preserves the identity. The relationship between the structure group and the automorphism group for quadratic Jordan algebras was analyzed in
[
Spr98
, §14]
and we refer the reader to
[
BLMG05
, §3]
for more general results in the spirit of the above proposition.

Proof of Theorem

Consider the pointed quadratic space

t_{2n+2}(x)=x_{n+1}+x_{2n+2}

in
\operatorname{GSO}_{2n+2}

. It follows that there is an action of
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}\subset\operatorname{O}_{2n+1}

{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+2}_{k}
as automorphisms that preserve

and fix

. This
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}

since it fixes

and thus induces an action on the variety defined by
q_{2n+2}(x)=0
and
t_{2n+2}(x)=1
. After renaming variables appropriately, the variety defined by
q_{2n+2}=0,t_{2n+2}=1
is precisely
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
. We summarize these observations in the following result.

Lemma 2.3.1
.

The closed subscheme of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+2}_{k}
defined by
t_{2n+2}=1
and
q_{2n+2}=0
is isomorphic to the scheme
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
; this scheme comes equipped with the action of
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
as a subgroup of
\operatorname{GSO}_{2n+2}
stabilizing the base-point

.

We consider the point
x_{0}\in\operatorname{Q}_{2n}(k)
given by
x_{2n+2}=1
,
x_{1}=\cdots=x_{2n+1}=0
, and we view
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
as pointed by
x_{0}
. Observe that under the inclusion
\operatorname{O}_{2n}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{O}_{2n+1}
, there is an inclusion

\operatorname{O}_{2n}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Stab}_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{O}_{2n+1}).

Our goal will be to use this fact to identify the stabilizer in
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
of the point
x_{0}
. The next result yields Theorem
1
from the introduction.

Theorem 2.3.2
.

Assume
k
is a commutative ring and
n\geq 1
is an integer. Consider the action of
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
on
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
of
Lemma
2.3.1
.

1.

Taking the
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
-orbit through the
k
-point
x_{0}
on
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
yields a surjective smooth morphism

\tilde{\varphi}:\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}\to\operatorname{Q}_{2n}

that factors through an
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
-equivariant isomorphism

\varphi:\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\;\longrightarrow\;}}}\operatorname{Q}_{2n}.

2.

The
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
-torsor
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}\to\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
is Zariski locally trivial.

Proof.

Since all schemes and groups in question are defined over
{\mathbb{Z}}
, it suffices to prove the result in that case and the results in the general case are deduced by base change. Thus, we assume
k={\mathbb{Z}}
in what follows. By appeal to Proposition
2.2.1
, if
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
acts transitively on
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
after base-change to geometric points of
{\mathbb{Z}}
, then there is an induced isomorphism
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}/\operatorname{Stab}_{x_{0}}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\;\longrightarrow\;}}}\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
, where
\operatorname{Stab}_{x_{0}}
is the stabilizer group-scheme of the point
x_{0}
in
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
. Transitivity of the action at geometric points is Proposition
2.3.4
below.

Now, we identify the stabilizer explicitly. Since any element of the stabilizer fixes
x_{0}
, it preserves
k\cdot x_{0}
. An explicit computation shows that one has a sequence of inclusions
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\subset\operatorname{Stab}_{x_{0}}\subset\operatorname{O}_{2n}
. Suppose
\operatorname{Stab}_{x_{0}}
were all of
\operatorname{O}_{2n}
. In that case, the sequence of inclusions
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\subset\operatorname{O}_{2n}\subset\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
would yield an fppf
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-torsor of the form
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\to\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
. Since
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
is connected,
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
must be a connected affine scheme, so this torsor is necessarily non-trivial. On the other hand, this torsor is necessarily étale locally trivial since it is an associated fiber space for an
\operatorname{O}_{2n}
-torsor, and
\operatorname{O}_{2n}
is a smooth
k
-group scheme (see
[
GP11
, XVII.8.1]
or
[
Gro68
, Remarques 11.8.2]
). In that case, since this
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-torsor is trivial upon base-change to the geometric generic point of
\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{Z}}
by appeal to Lemma
2.3.3
below, and since
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
is a constant group scheme, it must have been trivial to begin. It follows that
\operatorname{Stab}_{x_{0}}
must be isomorphic to
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
and thus
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
.

For the third statement, recall that Witt cancellation holds for quadratic spaces over local rings. More precisely, assume
R
is a local ring, and consider a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}R\to\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
. Such a morphism corresponds to an
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
-torsor on
\operatorname{Spec}R
that becomes trivial when viewed as an
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
-torsor, i.e., a stably hyperbolic quadratic form. By
[
Bae78
, Corollary III.4.3]
, if
q_{1}
is a stably hyperbolic quadratic space over
R
, it follows that
q_{1}
is actually hyperbolic, i.e., the morphism
\operatorname{Spec}R
lifts to
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
. It follows that
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}\to\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
is Zariski locally trivial, since it has Zariski local sections.
∎

Lemma 2.3.3
.

If
k
is an algebraically closed field having characteristic not equal to

, then for any integer

, the variety
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
has no non-trivial étale

\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\operatorname{Q}_{2n},{\mathbb{Z}}/2)=0

Under the hypothesis on

\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\operatorname{Q}_{2n},{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
with the

-torsion subgroup of

. A straightforward induction argument using the localization sequence and the fact that
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
has an open subscheme isomorphic to

with closed complement
\operatorname{Q}_{2n-2}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
then identifies
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Q}_{2n})
as
{\mathbb{Z}}
if
n=1
and
0
if
n>1
. In either case, it follows that the
2
-torsion subgroup is trivial.
∎

Proposition 2.3.4
.

Suppose

is an infinite field and

is an integer. The action of
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}(k)

\operatorname{Q}_{2n}(k)
is transitive.

.

The case

\operatorname{Q}_{0}

\operatorname{Spec}k
.

Proof.

If
k
has characteristic not equal to
2
, then this fact is well-known, so let us assume that

has characteristic
2
. In that case, note that the map
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}(k)\to\operatorname{O}_{2n+1}(k)
is a bijection. Suppose

is an arbitrary point not equal to

. To establish the result, it therefore suffices to construct an element of
\operatorname{O}_{2n+1}(k)
that takes
x
to
x_{0}
, which we will accomplish using explicit reflections. For the remainder of this proof, we write
B
for the bilinear form obtained by polarizing

and

for the associated trace function

Case 1

. Since

lies in the orthogonal complement of the linear space

. In that case, the reflection

Case 2.
Suppose

. We claim that we can choose

such that

and such that

,
B(x,a)\neq 0
and
B(x_{0},a)\neq 0
.

The locus of points where

corresponds to imposing the equation
x_{n+1}=x_{2n+2}
, which defines a closed subscheme of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+2}_{k}
isomorphic to
{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+1}_{k}
. The restriction of

to this subspace is

. In particular, the locus where

is a principal open subset of

(the point

,

Since

has trace zero, and since

, we know that

Set

Over fields of positive characteristic there are alternative group-theoretic arguments for transitivity, once the action above has been defined and the stabilizer computed. The following argument for transitivity was suggested by R. Guralnick.

Proposition 2.3.6
.

Over any algebraically closed field
k
of positive characteristic, the action of
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}(k)
on
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}(k)
is transitive.

Proof.

To check transitivity over a given algebraically closed field, it suffices to check transitivity over an algebraically closed subfield by
[
GLMS97
, Proposition 1.1]
. In that case we may assume that
k
is the algebraic closure of a finite field. It then suffices to check transitivity over any finite field, and we do this by counting points. We can compute the number of points of
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
over a finite field inductively. Indeed, for every
n\geq 1
, the scheme
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}
has an open subscheme isomorphic to
{\mathbb{A}}^{2n-1}\times{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
with closed complement
\operatorname{Q}_{2n-2}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
. Since
\operatorname{Q}_{0}=\operatorname{Spec}k\sqcup\operatorname{Spec}k
, one sees immediately that
|\operatorname{Q}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|=q^{2}+q
. The decomposition above gives the recursive formula

|\operatorname{Q}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|=q^{2n-1}(q-1)+q(|\operatorname{Q}_{2n-2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|),

and a straightforward induction argument allows one to conclude that
|\operatorname{Q}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|=q^{2n}+q^{n}
.

On the other hand, the formulas for the order of the special orthogonal group over a finite field
[
Ste16
, Theorem 25 on p. 77]
allow one to compute that
|\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})/\operatorname{SO}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|=q^{2n}+q^{n}
as well. It follows immediately that the action of
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
on
\operatorname{Q}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is transitive for every
q=p^{n}
, and thus transitivity holds after passing to
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
as well; the result follows.
∎

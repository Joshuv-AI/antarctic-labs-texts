On α \alpha -type (equivariant) cohomology of Hom-pre-Lie algebras
Shuangjian Guo, Ripan Saha 1. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Guizhou University of Finance and EconomicsGuiyang 550025, P. R. of China2. Department of Mathematics, Raiganj University Raiganj, 733134, West Bengal, India

Abstract




ABSTRACT

In this paper, we define a new cohomology theory for multiplicative Hom-pre-Lie algebras which controls deformations of Hom-pre-Lie algebra structure. This new cohomology is a natural one by considering the structure map. We develop equivariant cohomology theory for a Hom-pre-Lie algebra equipped with a finite group action by formulating a proper notion of coefficients system for the equivariant cohomology. We also study the associated formal deformation theory for Hom-pre-Lie algebras in the equivariant context.

Key words
: Group action, Hom-pre-Lie algebra, equivariant cohomology, formal deformation, rigidity.

2020 MSC
: 17A30, 17B56, 17B61, 17D99.

Introduction

The notion of pre-Lie algebra (also known as left-symmetric algebra
[
23
]
) was introduced by Gerstenhaber
[
6
]
while studying the algebraic deformation theory of associative algebras. The pre-Lie algebras have a close relationship with Lie algebras. For example, a pre-Lie algebra gives rise to a Lie algebra via the commutator bracket, which is known as the sub-adjacent Lie algebra. Pre-Lie algebras have appeared in many areas of mathematics, for example, vertex algebras, quantum field theories, complex and symplectic structures on Lie groups and Lie algebras, etc., see
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
10
]
, and references therein. A (left) pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space together with a binary operation
\cdot
satisfying the following identity :

(a\cdot b)\cdot c-a\cdot(b\cdot c)=(b\cdot a)\cdot c-b\cdot(a\cdot c),~\text{for all}~a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}.

The notion of Hom-pre-Lie algebras is a twisted analog of pre-Lie algebras, where the pre-Lie algebra identity is twisted by a self linear map, called the structure map. The study of Hom-version of this type of algebras is an interesting and active area of research these days and found some applications in mathematics as well as in physics. For the various type of Hom-algebras, see
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

Gertenhaber
[
6
,
7
]
initiated the study of algebraic deformation theory for associative algebra. Later, following his ideas, deformation theory for various types of algebras has been studied, see
[
2
,
18
,
19
,
12
,
21
,
20
]
. Hurle and Makhlouf studied cohomology and deformation theory for Hom-associative and Hom-Lie algebras in
[
8
,
9
]
. In
[
17
]
, Mukherjee and Saha studied cohomology and deformation theory for Hom-Leibniz algebras.

In this paper, we develop a new cohomology theory for Hom-pre-Lie algebras, we call this new cohomology
\alpha
-type cohomology of Hom-pre-Lie algebras. In
[
12
]
, the authors defined cohomology and a one-parameter formal deformation for Hom-pre-Lie algebras without considering the structure map
\alpha
. In
[
12
]
, they have only deformed the multiplication map, and ignored to deform the structure map. Thus, the study of cohomology and deformation in
[
12
]
is incomplete. This is one of the motivation of writing this paper. In this paper, we deform both the multiplication operation and the structure map. We also develop the corresponding deformation cohomology of Hom-pre-Lie algebras. Recently, we have noticed that a similar cohomology and deformation theory were independently studied in
[
11
]
.
In the next part, we develop equivariant cohomology for Hom-pre-Lie algebras equipped with a finite group action and also introduce a one-parameter formal deformation theory for Hom-pre-Lie algebras in the equivariant contexts. To develop the equivariant cohomology, we closely follow Bredon’s approach
[
3
]
for equivariant cohomology of topological
G
-spaces. Similar to the non-equivariant case we show that this equivariant cohomology is deformation cohomology of the equivariant deformation of Hom-pre-Lie algebras.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section
1
, we have discussed the definition, examples, representation, and a known cohomology of Hom-pre-Lie algebras which we shall use throughout the paper. In Section
2
, we develop a new cohomology for (multiplicative) Hom-pre-Lie algebras, we call this
\alpha
-type cohomology of Hom-pre-Lie algebras. In Section
3
, we introduce a one-parameter formal deformation theory of Hom-pre-Lie algebras considering the structure map
\alpha
. We show that infinitesimal of a deformation is a
2
-cocycle of the
\alpha
-type cohomology. We also discuss obstructions and rigidity of Hom-pre-Lie algebra deformations and show that how they are related to our new cohomology. In Section
4
, we develop the equivariant cohomology of Hom-pre-Lie algebras equipped with finite group action. In Section
5
, we introduce a one-parameter formal deformation theory for Hom-pre-Lie algebras in the equivariant contexts. We show some results for equivariant deformation of Hom-pre-Lie algebras analoguous to the non-equivariant case.

1
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the definition, representation, and cohomology of Hom-pre-Lie algebras following
[
12
,
22
]
. Throughout the paper,
\mathbb{K}
denotes a field of characteristics zero.

Definition 1.1
.

A Hom-Lie algebra is a triple
(g,[~,~],\alpha)
consisting of a linear space
g
, a skew-
symmetric bilinear map
[~,~]:\wedge^{2}g\rightarrow g
and an algebra morphism
\alpha:g\rightarrow g
, satisfying:

\displaystyle[\alpha(x),[y,z]]+[\alpha(y),[z,x]]+[\alpha(z),[x,y]]=0,~\text{for all}~x,y,z\in g.

(1.1)

Definition 1.2
.

A Hom-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
is a vector space
A
equipped with a bilinear product

\cdot:A\times A\rightarrow A,~\text{and a linear map}~\alpha:A\to A
, satisfying

1.

(
a\cdot b)\cdot\alpha(c)-\alpha(a)\cdot(b\cdot c)=(b\cdot a)\cdot\alpha(c)-\alpha(b)\cdot(a\cdot c)
, for all
a,b,c\in A
.

2.

\alpha(x\cdot y)=\alpha(x)\cdot\alpha(y)
, for all
a,b\in A.

A Hom-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
is called regular if
\alpha
is invertible.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
(A_{1},\cdot_{1},\alpha_{1})
and
(A_{2},\cdot_{2},\alpha_{2})
be two Hom-pre-Lie algebras. A morphism from
A_{1}
to
A_{2}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map
\phi:A_{1}\to A_{2}
such that

\phi(a\cdot_{1}b)=\phi(a)\cdot_{2}\phi(b),~~\phi\circ\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}\circ\phi,~\text{for all}~a,b\in A_{1}.

Example 1.4
.

Let
A
be a two dimensional vector space with basis
\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
. Define a multiplication on
A
by

a_{1}\cdot a_{1}=0,~a_{1}\cdot a_{2}=0,~a_{2}\cdot a_{1}=a_{1},~a_{2}\cdot a_{2}=a_{1}+a_{2}.

Let
\alpha
be a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map on
A
defined as
\alpha(a_{1})=a_{1},~\alpha(a_{2})=a_{1}+a_{2}
. Then
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
is a regular Hom-pre-Lie algebra.

Definition 1.5
.

A representation of a Hom-Lie algebra
(g,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
on a
vector space
V
with respect to
\beta\in gl(V)
is a linear map
\rho:g\rightarrow gl(V)
, such that for all
x,y\in g
,
the following equalities are satisfied:

\displaystyle\rho(\alpha(x))\circ\beta=\beta\circ\rho(x),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\rho([x,y])\circ\beta=\rho(\alpha(x))\circ\rho(y)-\rho(\alpha(y))\circ\rho(x).

(1.3)

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
be a Hom-pre-Lie algebra. We always assume that it is regular, i.e.
\alpha
is invertible.
The commutator
[x,y]_{C}=x\cdot y-y\cdot x
gives a Hom-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{C},\alpha)
, which is denoted by

A^{C}
and called the sub-adjacent Hom-Lie algebra of
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
.

Definition 1.6
.

A representation of a Hom-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
on a vector space
V
with
respect to
\beta\in gl(V)
consists of a pair
(\rho,\mu)
, where
\rho:A\rightarrow gl(V)
is a representation of the
sub-adjacent Hom-Lie algebra
A^{C}
on
V
with respect to
\beta\in gl(V)
, and
\mu:A\rightarrow gl(V)
is a linear
map, for all

, satisfying:

\displaystyle\beta\circ\mu(a)=\mu(\alpha(a))\circ\beta,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mu(\alpha(b))\circ\mu(a)-\mu(a\cdot b)\circ\beta=\mu(\alpha(b))\circ\rho(a)-\rho(\alpha(a))\circ\mu(b).

(1.5)

If
V
is a presentation of a Hom-pre-Lie algebra
A
as defined above, then we denote it as
(V,\rho,\mu,\beta).

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
be a Hom-pre-Lie algebra. Then
(A,\alpha)
is a representation of itself via the maps
L,R:A\to\mathfrak{gl}(A)
defined as follows:

L_{a}(b)=a\cdot b,~~~R_{a}(b)=b\cdot a,~\text{for all}~a,b\in A.

It is easy to check that
(A,L,R,\alpha)
is a representation of
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
. This representation of

is called the regular representation. In this paper, we will use regular representation only.

Let
(V,\rho,\mu,\beta)
be a representation of a Hom-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
. The cohomology of
A
with coefficients in
V
is the
cohomology of the cochain complex
\{C^{*}(A,V),\partial\}
, where
C^{n}(A,V)=\{\phi\in\text{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},V)\mid\beta\circ\phi=\phi\circ\alpha^{\otimes n}\},(n\geq 0)
and the
coboundary operator

given by

\displaystyle(\partial f)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})

(1.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(\alpha^{n-1}(x_{i}))f(a_{1},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,a_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}\mu(\alpha^{n-1}(a_{n+1}))f(a_{1},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,a_{n},a_{i})

\displaystyle-\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}f(\alpha(a_{1}),\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,\alpha(a_{n}),a_{i}\cdot a_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(-1)^{i+j}f([a_{i},a_{j}]_{C},\alpha(a_{1}),\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{a_{j}},\ldots,\alpha(a_{n+1})),

for

. The corresponding cohomology groups are denoted by

We will see that this cohomology is not a deformation cohomology for Hom-pre-Lie algebras.

3
Formal deformation theory of Hom-pre-Lie algebras

In this section, we show that the
\alpha
-type cohomology for Hom-pre-Lie algebras controls one-parameter formal deformation of Hom-pre-Lie algebras. Unlike
[
12
]
, our formal deformation takes into account the structure map
\alpha
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(A,\nu,\alpha)
be a Hom-pre-Lie algebra over
\mathbb{K}
. A one-parameter formal deformation of
A
is given by a
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-bilinear map
\nu_{t}:A[[t]]\times A[[t]]\to A[[t]]
, and a
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-linear map
\alpha_{t}:A[[t]]\to A[[t]]
of the forms

\nu_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\nu_{i}t^{i},~~~\alpha_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\alpha_{i}t^{i}

such that

1.

For all
i\geq 0
,
\nu_{i}:A\times A\to A
is a
\mathbb{K}
-bilinear map, and
\alpha_{i}:A\to A
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map.

2.

\nu_{0}(a,b)=\nu(a,b)=a\cdot b
, is the the multiplication of
A
and
\alpha_{0}=\alpha
is the given structure map on
A
.

3.

\nu_{t}(\nu_{t}(a,b),\alpha_{t}(c))-\nu_{t}(\alpha_{t}(a),\nu_{t}(b,c))=\nu_{t}(\nu_{t}(b,a),\alpha_{t}(c))-\nu_{t}(\alpha_{t}(b),\nu_{t}(a,c))
for all
a,b,c\in A.

4.

The map
\alpha_{t}
is multiplicative, that is,
\nu_{t}(\alpha_{t}(a),\alpha_{t}(b))=\alpha_{t}(\nu_{t}(a,b))
, for all
a,b\in A
.

From the Condition
3
, we have the following equations for all
n\geq 0
.

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\
i,j,k\geq 0\end{subarray}}\nu_{i}(\nu_{j}(a,b),\alpha_{k}(c))-\nu_{i}(\alpha_{j}(a),\nu_{k}(b,c))-\nu_{i}(\nu_{j}(b,a),\alpha_{k}(c))+\nu_{i}(\alpha_{j}(b),\nu_{k}(a,c))=0.

(3. 56)

The Condition
4
is equivalent to the following equation:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\
i,j,k\geq 0\end{subarray}}\nu_{i}(\alpha_{j}(a),\alpha_{k}(b))-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}(\nu_{j}(a,b))=0.

(3. 57)

For a Hom-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\nu,\alpha)
, an
\alpha_{k}
-associator is defined as

\displaystyle\nu_{i}\circ_{\alpha_{k}}\nu_{j}(a,b,c)=\nu_{i}(\nu_{j}(a,b),\alpha_{k}(c))-\nu_{i}(\alpha_{k}(a),\nu_{j}(b,c)).

(3. 58)

Thus, Equation
3. 56
may be written in terms of associator
\alpha_{j}
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\
i,j,k\geq 0\end{subarray}}(\nu_{i}\circ_{\alpha_{j}}\nu_{k})(a,b,c)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\
i,j,k\geq 0\end{subarray}}(\nu_{i}\circ_{\alpha_{k}}\nu_{j})(b,a,c)=0.

(3. 59)

We can rewrite Equation
3. 59
as follows:

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\nu}\nu_{n}-\widetilde{\partial}_{\alpha\nu}\alpha_{n})(a,b,c)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\
0\leq i,j,k\leq n-1\end{subarray}}(\nu_{i}\circ_{\alpha_{k}}\nu_{j})(a,b,c)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\
0\leq i,j,k\leq n-1\end{subarray}}(\nu_{i}\circ_{\alpha_{k}}\nu_{j})(b,a,c).

(3. 60)

Observe that

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\nu}\nu_{n})(a,b,c)=

\displaystyle\alpha(a)\cdot\nu_{n}(b,c)-\alpha(b)\cdot\nu_{n}(a,c)+\nu_{n}(b,a)\cdot\alpha(c)-\nu_{n}(a,b)\cdot\alpha(c)-\nu_{n}(\alpha(b),a\cdot c)

\displaystyle+\nu_{n}(\alpha(a),b\cdot c)-\nu_{n}(a\cdot b,\alpha(c))+\nu_{n}(b\cdot a,\alpha(c)),

and

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\partial}_{\alpha\nu}\alpha_{n})(a,b,c)=\alpha_{n}(b)\cdot(a\cdot c)-\alpha_{n}(a)(b\cdot c)+(a\cdot b)\cdot\alpha_{n}(c)-(b\cdot a)\cdot\alpha_{n}(c)

The Equation
3. 57
is same as the following equation involving differentials.

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\alpha}\nu_{n}-\widetilde{\partial}_{\alpha\alpha}\alpha_{n})(a,b)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\

Note that

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\partial}_{\alpha\alpha}\alpha_{n})(a,b)=\alpha(a)\cdot\alpha_{n}(b)+\alpha_{n}(a)\cdot\alpha(b)-\alpha_{n}(a\cdot b).

For

Definition 3.2

The pair

Let

0\leq i,j,k\leq n\end{subarray}}\nu_{i}(\alpha_{j}(a),\alpha_{k}(b))-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n+1\\
0\leq i,j\leq n\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}(\nu_{j}(a,b)).

(3. 63)

Note that
O^{n}(A)\in\widetilde{C}^{3}(A,A)
.

Theorem 3.5
.

A deformation
(\nu_{t},\alpha_{t})
of order
n
is extendable to a deformation of order

if and only if the cohomology class of

vanishes.

Proof.

Suppose a deformation
(\nu_{t},\alpha_{t})
of order

extends a deformation of order

. From the definition of obstruction, we have

O^{n}(A):=(O^{n}_{\nu}(A),O^{n}_{\alpha}(A))=\partial(\nu_{n+1},\alpha_{n+1}).

Thus,
\partial(O^{n}(A))=0
, that is, the cohomology class of the obstruction vanishes.

Conversely, suppose the cohomology class of

vanishes, that is,

O^{n}(A)=\partial(\nu_{n+1},\alpha_{n+1}),

for some
2
-cochain
(\nu_{n+1},\alpha_{n+1})
. We define an

-deformation extending an

-deformation
(\nu_{t},\alpha_{t})
as follows:

\displaystyle\bar{\nu_{t}}=\nu_{t}+\nu_{n+1}t^{n+1},

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha_{t}}=\alpha_{t}+\alpha_{n+1}t^{n+1}.

It is easy to check that

defines an

-deformation.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

The obstruction of extending an infinitesimal deformation lies in
\widetilde{H^{3}}(A,A)
.

3.2
Equivalent deformation and cohomology

Let
(\nu_{t},\alpha_{t})
and
(\nu^{{}^{\prime}}_{t},\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{t})
be two formal deformations of
A
, where

\nu^{{}^{\prime}}_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\nu^{{}^{\prime}}_{i}t^{i},~~~\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{i}t^{i}
.

Definition 3.7
.

Two deformations
(\nu_{t},\alpha_{t})
and
(\nu^{{}^{\prime}}_{t},\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{t})
are said to be equivalent if there exists
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-isomorphism
\Psi_{t}:A[[t]]\to A[[t]]
of the form
\Psi_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\psi_{i}t^{i}
such that
\psi_{0}=\text{id}
, and
\psi_{i}:A\to A
are
\mathbb{K}
-linear maps for all
i\geq 0
, satisfying the following conditions:

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}\circ\nu^{{}^{\prime}}_{t}=\nu_{t}\circ(\Psi_{t}\otimes\Psi_{t}),~\text{and}~\alpha_{t}\circ\Psi_{t}=\Psi_{t}\circ\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{t}.

(3. 64)

Definition 3.8
.

A deformation
(\nu_{t},\alpha_{t})
of
A
is called trivial if
(\nu_{t},\alpha_{t})
is equivalent to
(\nu_{0},\alpha_{0})
. A Hom-pre-Lie algebra
A
is called rigid if it has only trivial deformation upto equivalence.

The Condition
3. 64
is equivalent to the following equations:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}\psi_{i}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\nu^{\prime}_{j}(a,b)t^{j}\bigg)t^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\nu_{i}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\psi_{j}(a)t^{j},\sum_{k\geq 0}\psi_{k}(b)t^{k}\bigg)t^{i},

(3. 65)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}\alpha_{i}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\psi_{j}(a)t^{j}\bigg)t^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\psi_{i}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\alpha^{\prime}_{j}(a)t^{j}\bigg)t^{i}.

(3. 66)

This is same as the following equations:

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\psi_{i}(\nu^{\prime}_{j}(a,b))t^{i+j}=\sum_{i,j,k\geq 0}\nu_{i}(\psi_{j}(a),\psi_{k}(b))t^{i+j+k},

(3. 67)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\alpha_{i}(\psi_{j}(a))t^{i+j}=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\psi_{i}(\alpha^{\prime}_{j}(a))t^{i+j}.

(3. 68)

Comparing constant terms on both sides of the above equations, we have

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}_{0}(a,b)=\nu_{0}(a,b)=a\cdot b,\,\,\,\text{as}\,\,\,\psi_{0}=Id,

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}(a)=\alpha^{\prime}_{0}(a)=\alpha(a).

Now comparing coefficients of
t
, we have

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}_{1}(a,b)+\psi_{1}(\nu^{\prime}_{0}(a,b))=\nu_{1}(a,b)+\nu_{0}(\psi_{1}(a),b)+\nu_{0}(a,\psi_{1}(b)),

(3. 69)

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(a)+\alpha_{0}(\psi_{1}(a))=\alpha^{\prime}_{1}(a)+\psi_{1}(\alpha^{\prime}_{0}(a)).

(3. 70)

Equations (
3. 69
) and (
3. 70
) are same as

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}_{1}(a,b)-\nu_{1}(a,b)=a\cdot\psi_{1}(b)+\psi_{1}(a)\cdot b-\psi_{1}(a\cdot b)=\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\nu}\psi_{1}(a,b).

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}^{\prime}(a)-\alpha_{1}(a)=\alpha(\psi_{1}(a))-\psi_{1}(\alpha(a))=\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\alpha}\psi_{1}(a).

Thus, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.9
.

Two equivalent formal deformations of
A
have cohomologous infinitesimals.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
(\nu_{t},\alpha_{t})
be a non-trivial formal deformation of the Hom-pre-Lie algebra
A
. Then
(\nu_{t},\alpha_{t})
is equivalent to a deformation whose infinitesimal is not a coboundary.

Proof.

Let
(\nu_{t},\alpha_{t})
be a deformation of the Hom-pre-Lie algebra
A
and
(\nu_{n},\alpha_{n})
be the
n
-infinitesimal of the deformation for some
n\geq 1
. Then by Theorem (
3.3
),
(\nu_{n},\alpha_{n})
is a
2
-cocycle, that is,
\widetilde{\partial}(\nu_{n},\alpha_{n})=0
. Suppose
\nu_{n}=-\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\nu}\phi_{n}
and
\alpha_{n}=-\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\alpha}\phi_{n}
for some
\phi_{n}\in\widetilde{C_{\nu}}^{1}(A,A)
, that is,
(\nu_{n},\alpha_{n})
is a coboundary. We define a formal isomorphism
\Psi_{t}
of
A[[t]]
as follows:

\Psi_{t}(a)=a+\phi_{n}(a)t^{n}.

We set

\bar{\nu_{t}}=\Psi^{-1}_{t}\circ\nu_{t}\circ(\Psi_{t}\otimes\Psi_{t})\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\bar{\alpha_{t}}=\Psi^{-1}_{t}\circ\alpha_{t}\circ\Psi_{t}.

Thus, we have a new deformation
(\bar{\nu_{t}},\bar{\alpha_{t}})
which is equivalent to
(\nu_{t},\alpha_{t})
. By expanding the above equations and comparing coefficients of
t^{n}
, we get

\bar{\nu_{n}}-\nu_{n}=\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\nu}\phi_{n},~~~\bar{\alpha_{n}}-\alpha=\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\alpha}\phi_{n}.

Hence,
\bar{\nu_{n}}=0,\bar{\alpha_{n}}=0
. By repeating this argument, we can kill off any infinitesimal which is a coboundary. Thus, the process must stop if the deformation is non-trivial.
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

If
\widetilde{H^{2}}(A,A)=0
then every deformation of
A
is equivalent to a trivial deformation, that is,
A
is rigid.

4
Equivariant cohomology of Hom-pre-Lie algebras

In this section, we develop an equivariant cohomology for Hom-pre-Lie algebras equipped with a group action. To construct such an equivariant cohomology, we will follow Bredon’s approach
[
3
]
for equivarint cohomology of topological
G
-spaces.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
A
be a Hom-pre-Lie algebra. The group
G
is said to act on
A
from left if there exists a map

\phi:G\times A\to A,~~~(g,a)\mapsto ga,

satisfying

1.

For each
g\in G
, the map
\phi(g,-)=\psi_{g}:A\to A,~a\mapsto ga
is linear.

2.

For all
a\in A,~ea=a
, where
e
denotes the identity element of
G
.

3.

(g_{1}g_{2})a=g_{1}(g_{2}a)
for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
and
a\in A
.

4.

g(a\cdot b)=ga\cdot gb
and
\alpha(g\cdot a)=g\alpha(a)
, for all
g\in G,a,b\in A.

Remark 4.2
.

We defined finite group actions on Hom-pre-Lie algebras for simplicity, but one can do it for any discrete groups.

Let
G
is acting on the Hom-pre-Lie algebra
A
. Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. We define the
H
-fixed point set of
A
as follows:

A^{H}=\{a\in A\mid ha=a,\forall h\in H\}.

Lemma 4.3
.

For all subgroup
H
of
G
, the
H
-fixed point set
A^{H}
is a sub Hom-pre-Lie algebra of
A
.

Proof.

It is enough to show that
A^{H}
is closed under multiplication and if
a\in A^{H}
then
\alpha(a)\in A^{H}
.

Let
a,b\in A^{H}
. Observe that

h(a\cdot b)=ha\cdot hb=a\cdot b,~~~h(\alpha(a))=\alpha(h\cdot a)=\alpha(a),~\text{for all}~h\in H.

Thus,
A^{H}
is a sub Hom-pre-Lie algebra of
A
.
∎

An
O_{G}
-module is a contravariant functor

\mathcal{M}:O_{G}\to{\bf{Mod}}.

Note that

\mathbb{K}

Suppose

-Hom-pre-Lie algebra.

(4. 72)

Remark 4.7

It is well-known that any Hom-pre-Lie algebra

We set

We define

\displaystyle\delta^{n}:

\displaystyle c^{H}_{\nu}\circ\mathcal{A}(\hat{g})^{\otimes n}=\mathcal{A}(\hat{g})\circ c^{K}_{\nu},

As

\displaystyle c^{H}_{\alpha}\circ\psi_{g}^{\otimes{n-1}}=\psi_{g}\circ c^{K}_{\alpha}.

Lemma 4.9
.

The set of all invariant

of

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle c^{H}_{\nu}(ga_{1},ga_{2},\ldots,ga_{n})=gc^{K}_{\nu}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}),

\displaystyle c^{H}_{\alpha}(ga_{1},ga_{2},\ldots,ga_{n-1})=gc^{K}_{\alpha}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n-1}).

\displaystyle\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\nu}(c^{H}_{\nu})(\psi_{g}(a_{1}),\ldots,\psi_{g}(a_{n+1}))

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\nu}(c^{H}_{\nu})(ga_{1},\ldots,ga_{n+1})

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(\alpha^{n-1}(ga_{i}))c^{H}_{\nu}(ga_{1},\ldots,\widehat{ga_{i}},\ldots,ga_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}\mu(\alpha^{n-1}(ga_{n+1}))c^{H}_{\nu}(ga_{1},\ldots,\widehat{ga_{i}},\ldots,ga_{n},ga_{i})

\displaystyle-\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}c^{H}_{\nu}(\alpha(ga_{1}),\ldots,\widehat{ga_{i}},\ldots,\alpha(ga_{n}),ga_{i}\cdot ga_{n+1})

\displaystyle=g\big(\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(\alpha^{n-1}(a_{i}))c^{H}_{\nu}(a_{1},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,a_{n+1})\big)

\displaystyle+g\big(\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}\mu(\alpha^{n-1}(a_{n+1}))c^{H}_{\nu}(a_{1},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,a_{n},a_{i})\big)

\displaystyle-g\big(\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}c^{H}_{\nu}(\alpha(a_{1}),\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,\alpha(a_{n}),a_{i}\cdot a_{n+1})\big)

\displaystyle=g\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\nu}(c^{H}_{\nu})(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})

In a similar way, it is a routine work to check that other three components of the differential also preserves the group action. Thus,

-cochain.
∎

is a cochain complex and homology of this complex is called equivariant cohomology of the Hom-pre-Lie algebra

Definition 5.4
.

An equivariant deformation of order
n
of
A
eqipped with action of
G
is given by a
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-bilinear map
\nu^{H}_{t}:A^{H}[[t]]\times A^{H}[[t]]\to A^{H}[[t]]
and a
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-linear map
\alpha^{H}_{t}:A^{H}[[t]]\to A^{H}[[t]]
of the forms

\nu^{H}_{t}=\sum^{n}_{i=0}\nu^{H}_{i}t^{i},~~~\alpha^{H}_{t}=\sum^{n}_{i=0}\alpha^{H}_{i}t^{i},

such that the pair
(\nu^{H}_{t},\alpha^{H}_{t})
satisfies the definition of the formal deformation of
A^{H}
(mod
t^{n+1}
), for all subgroups
H
of
G
.

We say an equivariant deformation
(\mathfrak{v}_{t},\mathfrak{a}_{t})
of order
n
is extendable to a deformation of order
(n+1)
if for all subgroups
H
, there exists
\nu^{H}_{n+1}\in\widetilde{C_{\nu}^{n+1}}(A,A)
and
\alpha^{H}_{n+1}\in\widetilde{C_{\alpha}^{n+1}}(A,A)
such that

\bar{\nu^{H}_{t}}=\nu^{H}_{t}+\nu^{H}_{n+1}t^{n+1},~~~\bar{\alpha^{H}_{t}}=\alpha^{H}_{t}+\alpha^{H}_{n+1}t^{n+1},

and the pair
(\bar{\nu^{H}_{t}},\bar{\alpha^{H}_{t}})
satisfies the Definition
5.1
mod(
t^{n+2}
).
From an equivariant deformation
(\mathfrak{v}_{t},\mathfrak{a}_{t})
of order
(n+1)
, we have the following equations:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\
i,j,k\geq 0\end{subarray}}\nu^{H}_{i}(\nu^{H}_{j}(a,b),\alpha^{H}_{k}(c))-\nu^{H}_{i}(\alpha^{H}_{j}(a),\nu^{H}_{k}(b,c))-\nu^{H}_{i}(\nu^{H}_{j}(b,a),\alpha^{H}_{k}(c))+\nu^{H}_{i}(\alpha^{H}_{j}(b),\nu^{H}_{k}(a,c))=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\
i,j,k\geq 0\end{subarray}}\nu^{H}_{i}(\alpha^{H}_{j}(a),\alpha^{H}_{k}(b))-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n+1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\alpha^{H}_{i}(\nu^{H}_{j}(a,b))=0.

We can re-write the above equations as follows:

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\nu}\nu^{H}_{n}-\widetilde{\partial}_{\alpha\nu}\alpha^{H}_{n})(a,b,c)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\
0\leq i,j,k\leq n\end{subarray}}(\nu^{H}_{i}\circ_{\alpha^{H}_{j}}\nu^{H}_{k})(a,b,c)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\
0\leq i,j,k\leq n\end{subarray}}(\nu^{H}_{i}\circ_{\alpha^{H}_{j}}\nu^{H}_{k})(b,a,c),

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\partial}_{\alpha\alpha}\alpha^{H}_{n}-\widetilde{\partial}_{\nu\alpha}\nu^{H}_{n})(a,b)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\
0\leq i,j,k\leq n\end{subarray}}\nu^{H}_{i}(\alpha^{H}_{j}(a),\alpha^{H}_{k}(b))-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n+1\\
0\leq i,j\leq n\end{subarray}}\alpha^{H}_{i}(\nu^{H}_{j}(a,b)).

We define the
n
th equivariant obstruction to extend an equivariant deformation of order
n
to
n+1
as
O_{G}^{n}(A):=(\oplus_{H\leq G}O^{n,H}_{\nu}(A),\oplus_{H\leq G}O^{n,H}_{\alpha}(A))
, where

\displaystyle O^{n,H}_{\nu}(A)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\
0\leq i,j,k\leq n\end{subarray}}(\nu^{H}_{i}\circ_{\alpha^{H}_{k}}\nu^{H}_{j})(a,b,c)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\
0\leq i,j,k\leq n\end{subarray}}(\nu^{H}_{i}\circ_{\alpha^{H}_{k}}\nu^{H}_{j})(b,a,c),

(5. 78)

\displaystyle O^{n,H}_{\alpha}(A)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n+1\\
0\leq i,j,k\leq n\end{subarray}}\nu^{H}_{i}(\alpha^{H}_{j}(a),\alpha^{H}_{k}(b))-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n+1\\
0\leq i,j\leq n\end{subarray}}\alpha^{H}_{i}(\nu^{H}_{j}(a,b)).

(5. 79)

Theorem 5.5
.

An equivariant deformation
(\mathfrak{v}_{t},\mathfrak{a}_{t})
of order
n
is extendable to a deformation of order
n+1

O_{G}^{n}(A)

Corollary 5.6

The obstruction of extending an equivariant infinitesimal deformation lies in
\widetilde{H^{3}}_{G}(A,A)

Proposition 5.9
.

Two equivalent formal deformations of
A
equipped with an action of
G
have cohomologous infinitesimals.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
(\mathfrak{v}_{t},\mathfrak{a}_{t})
be a non-trivial equivariant formal deformation of the Hom-pre-Lie algebra
A
. Then
(\mathfrak{v}_{t},\mathfrak{a}_{t})
is equivalent to a deformation whose infinitesimal is not a coboundary.

Corollary 5.11
.

If
\widetilde{H^{2}}_{G}(A,A)=0
then every equivariant deformation of
A
is equivalent to a trivial deformation, that is,
A
is equivariantly rigid.

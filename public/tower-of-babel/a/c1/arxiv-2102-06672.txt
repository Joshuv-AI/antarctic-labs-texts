Computations of the Comodule Structures of the Chow rings of Flag Varieties
XIONG Rui

Abstract
Let G G be a connected reductive group, and G / B G/B be its flag variety. Let π : G → G / B \pi:G\to G/B be the natural projection. In this paper, we developed an algorithm to describe the map π ∗ : CH ∗ ⁡ ( G / B ; 𝔽 p ) ⟶ CH ∗ ⁡ ( G ; 𝔽 p ) \pi^{*}:\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{p})\longrightarrow\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G;\mathbb{F}_{p}) in terms of Schubert cells. Taking advantage of the Pieri rule, we give an explicit formula for A A -type, C C -type, G 2 G_{2} , F 4 F_{4} of the cohomology map π ∗ : CH ∗ ⁡ ( G / B ; 𝔽 p ) ⟶ CH ∗ ⁡ ( G ; 𝔽 p ) \pi^{*}:\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{p})\longrightarrow\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G;\mathbb{F}_{p}) , and some partial result of π ∗ \pi^{*} is given for E 6 E_{6} and E 7 E_{7} . Denote the group action map μ : G × G / B → G / B \mu:G\times G/B\to G/B , we also give an explicit formula for A A -type, C C -type, G 2 G_{2} , F 4 F_{4} of the cohomology map μ ∗ : CH ∗ ⁡ ( G / B ; 𝔽 p ) ⟶ CH ∗ ⁡ ( G × G / B ; 𝔽 p ) \mu^{*}:\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{p})\longrightarrow\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G\times G/B;\mathbb{F}_{p}) .

It is known that

2
The Algorithm

Pieri rule.

Let
\{\alpha_{i}:i\in I\}
be the set of simple roots,

\{\omega_{i}:i\in I\}
the corresponding fundamental weights.
Let
\{s_{i}:i\in I\}
be the set of simple reflections.
Assume
K
is a semisimple compact group.
We can identify
H^{2}(BT;\mathbb{Z})
with the character of
T
.

When
K
is simply-connected, it can be further recognized as the weight lattice,
and
[\Sigma_{s_{i}}]\in H^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{Z})
is presented by the image of
\omega_{i}\in H^{*}(BT:\mathbb{Z})
.
In particular,
H^{2}(BT;\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(G/B;\mathbb{Z})
is an isomorphism.
For any
p
, the kernel of
\pi^{*}
is the ideal generated by
H^{2}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{p})
.
On the contrary, when
K
is of adjoint type, it can be further recognized as the root lattice.

For any element
\alpha\in H^{2}(G/B;\mathbb{Z})
and
u\in W
, we have the Pieri formula
[
3
]

\alpha\smile[\Sigma_{u}]=\sum\left<\beta^{\vee},\alpha\right>[\Sigma_{w}]

where the sum is taken over all
w
such that
\ell(w)=\ell(u)+1
and there is a positive root
\beta
with
w=us_{\beta}
,
where
\beta^{\vee}=\beta/\left<\beta,\beta\right>
for the standard inner product
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
over the root space.

The algorithm.

The author used
Sage
to realize the algorithm.

Ct=CartanType("G2"); DynkinDiagram(Ct) # Input

def B(x,y): return x.inner_product(y)
def vee(x): return 2*x/B(x,x)

refl=W.reflections(); Refl=[refl[beta[k]] for k in [0..len(beta)-1]];

p=2; # Input
k=6; k=k/2; # Input

Lengthk=list(W.elements_of_length(k));
Lengthkk=list(W.elements_of_length(k-1));
print(len(Lengthkk),len(Lengthk))

V=VectorSpace(GF(p),len(Lengthk))

Lengthkinv={}

Lengthkinv[Lengthk[b]]=b

Relation = {}
for a in [0..len(Lengthkk)-1]:
for i in I:
Relation[(a,i)]=V(0);

for a in [0..len(Lengthkk)-1]:
for j in [0..len(beta)-1]:
z=Lengthkk[a]* Refl[j];

for i in I:
Relation[(a,i)][Lengthkinv[z]]=B(vee(beta[j]),alpha[i]); #(*)

dim(Q)

for b in [0..len(Lengthk)-1]:
v=V(0); v[b]=1;
Image[b]=pi(v)

for q in Q:

for b in [0..len(Lengthk)-1]:

Computations of
A
-type and
C

A general fact.

Let
X,Y,Z
be three path connected spaces.
Pick an
x_{0}\in X
and a
y_{0}\in Y
arbitrarily.
Let
f:X\times Y\to Z
be a continuous map.
If
H^{1}(Y;\mathbb{F}_{p})=0
, then the map

H^{2}(Z)\to H^{2}(X)\otimes H^{0}(Y)\oplus H^{0}(X)\otimes H^{2}(Y)

is given by
x\mapsto f_{1}^{*}(x)\otimes 1+1\otimes f_{2}^{*}(x)
where

f_{1}:X\to Z
maps
x
to
f(x,y_{0})
and

f_{2}:Y\to Z
maps
y
to
f(x_{0},y)
.

The
A
-type case.

Let
G=\operatorname{PGL}_{n}
.
In type A, it is well-known that

H^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{Z})=\frac{\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]}{\left<f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{W}_{+}\right>}

where
\mathbb{Z}[x]^{W}_{+}
stands the set of symmetric polynomials without constant term.
In particular,
H^{2}(G/B;\mathbb{Z})
is generated by
H^{2}
-elements.

Note that the root system is given by
\{x_{i}-x_{j}:i\neq j\}
, so

\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G;\mathbb{F}_{p})=\frac{\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{p})}{\left<x_{i}-x_{j}\right>}\cong\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]}{\left<f(t,\ldots,t)\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{W}_{+}\right>}.

Actually,
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{W}
is generated by the elementary symmetric polynomials.
Therefore,
\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G;\mathbb{F}_{p})=\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]/\left<\binom{n}{i}t^{i}:i=1,\ldots,n\right>
.
Assume
p^{k}|n
but
p^{k+1}\nmid n
.
Elementary number theory shows that
\binom{n}{i}=0
for
i<p^{k}
,
and
\binom{n}{p^{k}}\neq 0
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. As a result,

\operatorname{CH}(G;\mathbb{F}_{p})=\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]/\big<t^{p^{k}}\big>\qquad\text{$p^{k}|n$ but $p^{k+1}\nmid n$}

with
\deg t=2
. This is also summarized in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1
(
A
-type)

For the group
G=\operatorname{PGL}_{n}
, we have

(1)

The projection
\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G;\mathbb{F}_{p})
is given by

x_{i}\longmapsto t\qquad\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(2)

The comoudle structure
\operatorname{CH}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\operatorname{CH}(G\times G/B;\mathbb{F}_{p})
is given by

x_{i}\longmapsto x_{i}+t\qquad\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

The
C
-type case.

Let
G=\operatorname{Ad}\operatorname{Sp}_{n}
.
In type C, it is also known that

H^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{Z})=\frac{\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]}{\left<f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{W}_{+}\right>}

where
\mathbb{Z}[x]^{W}_{+}
the set of even symmetric polynomials without constant term and actually generated by
the elementary symmetric polynomials in
x_{i}^{2}
.
In particular,
H^{2}(G/B;\mathbb{Z})
is generated by
H^{2}
-degree.

Note that the root system is given by
\{x_{i}-x_{j}:i\neq j\}\cup\{\pm 2x_{i}:i=1,\ldots,b\}
.
The analysis is the same as the
A
-type case. We can conclude that

\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G;\mathbb{F}_{2})=\mathbb{F}_{2}[t]/\big<t^{2^{k}}\big>\qquad\text{$2^{k}|n$ but $2^{k+1}\nmid n$},

with
\deg t=2
.

(
C
-type)

For the group
G=\operatorname{Ad}\operatorname{Sp}_{n}

(1)

The projection
\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{2})\to\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G;\mathbb{F}_{2})

The case
p=2
.

Now
\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G_{2};\mathbb{F}_{2})=\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{6}]/\left<x_{6}^{2}\right>
.

There are only two elements of Weyl group of length
3
, say
sts
and
tst
,
and only two for length
2
, say
st
and
ts
. So there are four relations

\begin{cases}\alpha\smile[\Sigma_{st}]=\left<\alpha^{\vee},\alpha\right>[\Sigma_{sts}]+\left<(3\alpha+2\beta)^{\vee},\alpha\right>[\Sigma_{tst}]&=2[\Sigma_{sts}]+0[\Sigma_{tst}]\\
\beta\smile[\Sigma_{st}]=\left<\alpha^{\vee},\beta\right>[\Sigma_{sts}]+\left<(3\alpha+2\beta)^{\vee},\beta\right>[\Sigma_{tst}]&=-[\Sigma_{sts}]+[\Sigma_{tst}]\\
\alpha\smile[\Sigma_{ts}]=\left<(2\alpha+\beta)^{\vee},\alpha\right>[\Sigma_{sts}]+\left<\beta^{\vee},\alpha\right>[\Sigma_{tst}]&=[\Sigma_{sts}]-[\Sigma_{tst}]\\
\beta\smile[\Sigma_{ts}]=\left<(2\alpha+\beta)^{\vee},\beta\right>[\Sigma_{sts}]+\left<\beta^{\vee},\beta\right>[\Sigma_{tst}]&=0[\Sigma_{sts}]+2[\Sigma_{tst}]\\
\end{cases}

So
\pi^{*}[\Sigma_{tst}]=\pi^{*}[\Sigma_{sts}]=x_{6}
.

Theorem 3
(The case
G_{2}
)

For the Chow ring of the exceptional group
G_{2}
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, we have

(1)

The projection
\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{2})\to\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G_{2};\mathbb{F}_{2})
is given by

[\Sigma_{w}]\longmapsto\begin{cases}1,&w=1\\
x_{6},&w=sts,tst,\\

(2)

The comodule structure
\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{2})\to\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G_{2};\mathbb{F}_{2})\otimes\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{2})

[\Sigma_{w}]\longmapsto 1\otimes[\Sigma_{w}]+\begin{cases}0,&w=1,s,t,st,ts,\\
x_{6}\otimes[\Sigma_{stsw}],&w=sts,stst,ststs,\\
x_{6}\otimes[\Sigma_{tstw}],&w=tst,tsts,tstst,\\
x_{6}\otimes([\Sigma_{tst}]+[\Sigma_{sts}]),&w=ststst.\\
\end{cases}

Theorem 8
(The case
E_{7}
,
p=3
)

For the Chow ring of the exceptional group
\operatorname{Ad}E_{7}
over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
,
we have

(1)

The projection
\pi^{*}:\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{3})\to\operatorname{CH}^{*}(\operatorname{Ad}E_{7};\mathbb{F}_{3})
is given by

[\Sigma_{w}]\longmapsto\begin{cases}1,&w=1\\
x_{8},&w\in\Pi(x_{8}),\\
-x_{8},&w\in\Pi(-x_{8}),\\
x_{8}^{2},&w\in\Pi(x_{8}^{2}),\\
-x_{8}^{2},&w\in\Pi(-x_{8}^{2}),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(2)

The comodule structure
\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{3})\to\operatorname{CH}^{*}(\operatorname{Ad}E_{7};\mathbb{F}_{3})\otimes\operatorname{CH}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{F}_{3})
is given by

[\Sigma_{w}]\longmapsto 1\otimes[\Sigma_{w}]+x_{8}\otimes\sum_{{w=u\odot v,}\atop{u\in\Pi(\pm x_{8})}}\pm[\Sigma_{v}]+x_{8}^{2}\otimes\sum_{{w=u\odot v,}\atop{u\in\Pi(\pm x_{8}^{2})}}\pm[\Sigma_{v}].

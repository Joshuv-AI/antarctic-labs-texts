1.
Introduction

A bijection,
(l,r):M\rightarrow M^{2};x\mapsto(l(x),r(x))
, between a set and its square, is called a pairing function. It is said to have no cycles if any function obtained as a composition of
l
and
r
have no fixed point.

Sets endowed with pairing
functions are also known as Jonsson-Tarski algebras or Cantor
algebras, they have been studied in universal algebra (for instance in

[
JT
,
AS
]
), and admit rich groups of automorphisms
[
H
]
. They have been studied by several authors in a model theoretic perspective. One of the motivation being that they are a simple example of a theory that is stable and cannot be obtained as the limit of superstable theories. As such, they can help understanding super-stable theories by providing counterexamples to their properties.
Pairing function without cycles admit quantifier elimination in the language
\{l,r\}
and is complete (
[
BP
]
).

In this article, we compute the Grothendieck ring of pairing function without cycles.

The Grothendieck ring of a structure is obtained by identifying definable sets that are in definable bijection, with the ring structure being induced by laws reflecting respectively the disjoint union (for the additive law) and the Cartesian product (for the multiplicative law).

More precisely:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
M
be a structure. Let us consider
\operatorname{Def}(M)
the set of subsets in the disjoint union of all finite Cartesian powers of
M
that are definable with parameters. Let
\sim
be the equivalent relation defined on
\operatorname{Def}(M)
by
A\sim B
if and only if there exists a definable bijection between
A
and
B
. For any
A\in\operatorname{Def}(M)
, we denote by
[A]
the class of
A
.

We define two laws on the quotient set
\operatorname{Def}(M)
:

•

An additive law that corresponds to the disjoint union:
[A]+[B]=[A\cap B]+[A\cup B]
.

•

A multiplicative law that corresponds to the Cartesian product:
[A]\times[B]=[A\times B]
.

These two laws are compatible with the equivalence relation and the set
\operatorname{Def}(M)
endowed with these two laws is the Grothendieck semi-ring of
M
.

Beware that the additive law is not cancelative:
a+b=a+c
doesn’t imply
b=c
. To remedy this, let us consider the equivalence relation defined on
\operatorname{Def}(M)
by:
a,b\in\operatorname{Def}(M)
are equivalent if and only if there exists
c\in\operatorname{Def}(M)
such that
a+c=b+c
. The quotient set, denoted
\widetilde{\operatorname{Def}}(M)
, is a cancelative monoid for the additive law. Hence, there exists a unique ring (up to isomorphism over
(\widetilde{\operatorname{Def}}(M),+,\times)
) that embeds this last quotient and that is minimal for this property.

Definition 1.2
.

The minimal ring that embeds
(\widetilde{\operatorname{Def}},+,\times)
is called the
Grothendieck ring of

M
and is denoted
K_{0}(M)
.

This ring has been defined by T. Scanlon and J. Krajicek in
[
KS
]
who built up a dictionary between the combinatorial properties of a structure and the algebraic properties of its Grothendieck ring. For example, a structure admits a zero Grothendieck ring, if and only if, there exists a definable set in definable bijection with itself deprived of a point.

This last property called Onto Pigeon Hole principle and abreged Onto-PHP has been used to prove the triviality of Grothendieck rings in the case of several valued fields (for example
\mathbb{Z}
-valued fields
[
CH
]
).

In general case, two elementarily equivalent structures do not
necessarily have isomorphic Grothendieck ring. Nevertheless, we prove
here that all pairing function without cycles admit
\mathbb{Z}^{2}=Z[X]/(X-X^{2})

as their Grothendieck ring. To prove this, we will highlight special class of definable sets that we will call simple sets and that are definable by positive conjunction of positive atomic formulas. Every definable set being a Boolean combination of simple sets, the Grothendieck ring is generated by their classes. It will prove that they are each either isomorphic to
M
or to a singleton implying, because of the relation
[M]=[M]^{2}
, that the Grothendieck ring is naturally a quotient of
Z[X]/(X-X^{2})
. It will remain to show that there is no further relation which will be done by examining the action of definable injection on definable sets.

The following more general theorem can be proved in exactly the same way:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and let
L:=\{l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}\}
be a language consisting of
n
symbols of functions. Let
M
be a
L
-structure such that
(l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}):M\rightarrow M^{n}
is a bijection. Assume furthermore that for every term
t
in
\{l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}\}
,
M\models t(x)\neq x
.
Then
K_{0}(M)=\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X-X^{n})
.

To make the redaction simple, we restrict ourselves to the case
n=2
.

2.
Representation of formulas and definable sets by binary trees

To any element we can associate an infinite binary tree: the left child of a node corresponds to its image by the function
l
and the right child corresponds to its image by the function
r
.

We can also use binary trees to represent certain definable sets and definable functions. We explain how in this section.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T
be an infinite and complete binary tree.
We define the
depth
of a node recursively:

•

by convention, the root is of depth 0;

•

There is thus one node at depth 0, two at depth

and

at depth

.

Definition 2.2
.

Labelling of the nodes

Let
T
be an infinite binary tree.
We label the nodes of
T
by associating them with a finite sequence
of elements of
\{l,r\}
as follows:

•

the root is labelled by the empty sequence;

•

if a node is labelled by a sequence
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
, then his left child is labelled by
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n},l\}
and his right child by
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n},r\}

The nodes of depth

We will denote

(respectively

In the following, all trees considered will be binary infinite trees.

Let

We associate to

.

The nodes of

Let

Suppose that the property is satisfied for

Since

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\psi(x)
be a primitive formula that defines a finite and non-empty set. Then:

(a)

The tree associated to
\psi
admits a closed subtree.

(b)

The formula
\psi(x)
is equivalent to a primitive positive formula and

(a)

(b)

6.

Sets defined by positive primitive formulas -that we will later call "simple sets"- have two crucial properties that we prove in this section:

\phi
is not a bijection: some elementary sets can be associated with several nodes of the tree. All the nodes corresponding to the same set
B\setminus C
, are roots of the same subtree.

c. In the construction proposed in the previous demonstration,
the order in which trees
T_{1},\ldots,T_{s}
are planted on the root
is arbitrary. Nevertheless, this is the only phenomenon that could contradicts
the uniqueness of a tree associated with a given decomposition: its graphical representation is obviously not unique, but there is a single tree associated with a given decomposition.

Proof.

Let
p
be the length of a maximum chain formed by positive sets.
Let’s prove the result by induction on
p
.

Suppose that
p=1
. Then
A=\sqcup_{j=1}^{h}(B_{j}\setminus C_{j})
where no negative set contains positive set. (Positive sets are the closed sets ones of the irreducible decomposition of
\overline{A}
.)
The associated tree is the tree of root
A
of depth
1
with
h
leaves
f_{1},\ldots,f_{h}
corresponding to elementary sets.
To conclude we just have to set
\phi
as

\phi(r)=A
,
\phi(f_{i})=B_{i}\setminus C_{i}
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.
Suppose the result shown for every
1\leq i\leq h
, show it
for
p+1
.

Up to renumbering the sets
B_{i}
, we can assume that
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}
are the maximum positive sets (in the sense of inclusion) of
A
.

We consider
T_{0}
the tree of root
A
and whose
s
leaves
f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}
, correspond to
B_{1}\setminus C_{1},\ldots,B_{s}\setminus C_{s}
.

We can apply the hypothesis of induction to
\sqcup_{j=s+1}^{h}(B_{j}\setminus C_{j})
to get the tree
T_{1}
.

Each node of level 1 of
T_{1}
corresponds to a set
B_{j}\setminus C_{j}
where
j
ranges between
s+1
and
h
, such that, according to the lemma
7.5
,
B_{j}
is included in one of the sets

.

Up to renumbering the sets, we can assume that the children of the root of

are
B_{s+1},\ldots,B_{s+t}
where

.
We consider the tree
T
of root

whose
s

f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}
, correspond to

and such that for every

and all

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
. One places oneself in
T
, the topology generated by the simple sets of depth
p
.
Let

be a closed sets and
\sqcup_{i}(B_{i}\setminus C_{i})=A
an elementary decomposition of
A
into simple sets of depth

.

be a tree associated with this decomposition.
Then any node of

corresponding to

has as many children as

has irreducible components in

Proof.

10.
Computation of the Grothendieck ring

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
A
be a definable set and
h
a definable injection from
A
to
A
.

Let
A=\sqcup_{i}(B_{i}\setminus C_{i})
a decomposition adapted to
h
, and
p
an integer such that all the elements of this decomposition are of depth
p
. Let
T
be the the topology whose closed sets are generated by the simple sets of depth
p
.
Then
A
admits an elementary decomposition in
T
adapted to
h
.

Proof.

Let
j
be an index and
X
an irreducible component of
\overline{C_{j}\cap A}
.
Let us assume that
X
is not one of the positives sets of the decomposition
A=\sqcup_{i}(B_{i}\setminus C_{i})
.
Let us show that there exists
C
a closed set of
T
such that
A=(\sqcup_{i}(B_{i}\setminus C_{i}))\sqcup(X\setminus C)
is a decomposition adapted to
h
.

Since the set
X
is irreducible, there exists an index
i
such that
X\subseteq B_{i}
and
B_{i}
is minimal for this property. The minimality of
B_{i}
and the fact that
X
is included in the closure of
A
imply that
X
is not included in
C_{i}
. Let
C:=X\cap C_{i}
.
The set
X\setminus C
is then included in
B_{i}\setminus C_{i}
.

It is enough to check that:

•

h
is defined on
X\setminus C
by a normal formula,

•

h
restricted to
X\setminus C
can be extended on
X
by an injection
\tilde{h}
definable by the same normal formula than
h
on
X\setminus C
,

•

X^{\prime}:=\tilde{h}(X)
is a simple set of depth
q
and
C^{\prime}:=\tilde{h}(C)
is an union of simple sets of depth
q
.

Since
X\setminus C
is included in
B_{i}\setminus C_{i}
, the two first properties are obvious. Let
\tilde{h}
be the prolongation of
h
on
X
that satisfies the second property.
This prolongation is defined by a normal formula,
X^{\prime}:=\tilde{h}(X)
is a simple sets of depth
q
and
C^{\prime}:=\tilde{h}(C)
is an union of simple sets of depth
q
.

The decomposition
A=(\sqcup_{i}(B_{i}\setminus C_{i}))\sqcup(X\setminus C)
is thus adapted to
h
. By iterating this construction, we obtain an elementary decomposition adapted to
h
.
∎

Corollary 10.2
.

Let
A
a definable set, and
h
a definable injection from
A
into
A
.
Then
A
admits a decomposition adapted to
h
(in the meaning of the previous demonstration) and elementary in the topology generated by the positive and negative sets of this decomposition.

Theorem 10.3
.

Let
M
be a model of the theory of the pairing function without cycles. The morphism of unitary rings of
\mathbb{Z}[X]
sending
X
to
[M]
factorises as an isomorphism between
\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X-X^{2})
and
K_{0}(M)
. In particular, as
\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X-X^{2})
is itself isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the Grothendieck ring
K_{0}(M)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a model of the theory of the pairing functions without cycles.
Let
X
be the class of
M
in the Grothendieck ring.

It is obvious that
X=X^{2}
.

Let
A
be a non-empty definable subset of
M
. Since
A
is a Boolean combination of simple sets, and since every simple set is in definable bijection with
M
or with a singleton, the class of
A
in the Grothendieck ring of

M
is an element of
\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X-X^{2})
.

Let us now consider
\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X-X^{2})
where
X
is an undetermined variable.

Let us fix a family,
\mathbb{F}
, of finite sets containing for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
exactly one set of cardinality
n
.

To each elements
aX+b\in\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X-X^{2})
, with
a
and
b
positives, we associate the definable set
(M\times F)\sqcup G
where
F
is the element of
\mathbb{F}
of cardinality
a
and
G
is the element of
\mathbb{F}
of cardinality
b
.

Let us check that two distinct elements of this ring are associated with definable sets that do not have the same image in
K_{0}(M)
.

In other words, let us show the following lemma:

Lemma 10.4
.

Let
F,G,F^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}
be finite sets. Let us assume that there exists a definable set
C
disjoint from
(M\times F)\sqcup G
and from
(M\times F^{\prime})\sqcup G^{\prime}
such that
(M\times F)\sqcup G\sqcup C
and
(M\times F^{\prime})\sqcup G^{\prime}\sqcup C
are in definable bijection.
Then
|F|=|F^{\prime}|
and
|G|=|G^{\prime}|
.

Proof.

Let
F,G,F^{\prime},G^{\prime}
and
C
be as in the lemma.
We already know that
C
is in definable bijection
∎

Let us assume that there is such a bijection, that we will denote by
h
, between
(M\times F)\sqcup G
and
(M\times F^{\prime})\sqcup G^{\prime}
.
We consider
\sqcup_{i=1}^{N}(B_{i}\setminus C_{i})
a decomposition in simple sets adapted to
h
. We can furthermore assume that this decomposition is elementary.
We consider the tree
T
associated to this decomposition as explained in
9.1
.

To each node
n_{i}
of
T
(distinct of the root), we associate
d_{i}
the number of its children.
The total number of the nodes of
T
is
N=\sum_{i}d_{i}+I
where
I
is the number of children of the root.

Since the decomposition is adapted to
h
,

h(M)=\sqcup_{i=1}^{N}(B^{\prime}_{i}\setminus C^{\prime}_{i})
where for every
i
,
h(B_{i}\setminus C_{i})=B^{\prime}_{i}\setminus C^{\prime}_{i}
.
Let
T^{\prime}
be a tree associated with this decomposition. To each node
n^{\prime}_{i}
of
T^{\prime}
, we associate
d^{\prime}_{i}
the number of its children.

Let us show that the number of children of the root of
T
is equal to the number of children of the root of
T^{\prime}
.
Since the total number of nodes of
T
is obviously equal to the one of
T^{\prime}
,
\sum_{i}d_{i}+I=\sum_{i}d^{\prime}_{i}+I^{\prime}
where
I^{\prime}
is the number of children of the root of
T^{\prime}
and it is sufficient to verify that
\sum_{i}d^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{i}d_{i}
.

Since
(M\times F)\sqcup G
is closed, and since the decomposition is elementary, according to the lemma
9.5
, every node
n
of
T
corresponding to a set
B_{i}\setminus C_{i}
has as much children as
C_{i}
has irreducible components.

According to the lemma
8.3
and the fact that the decomposition is adapted to
h
, for every
i
, the number of irreducible components of
C^{\prime}_{i}
is equal to the number of irreducible components of
C_{i}
.
As the set
(M\times F^{\prime})\sqcup G^{\prime}
is closed, the lemma
9.5
implies that every node
n
of
T^{\prime}
corresponding to a set
B^{\prime}_{i}\setminus C^{\prime}_{i}
has as much children as
C^{\prime}_{i}
has irreducible components.

The combination of these two points lead to
\sum_{i}d_{i}=\sum_{i}d^{\prime}_{i}
and thus to
I=I^{\prime}
.

But the sets corresponding to the children of the root of
T
(respectively
T^{\prime}
) are the elements of the irreducible decomposition of
(M\times F)\sqcup G
(respectively
h(M)
). As
(M\times F)\sqcup G
has a irreducible decomposition of cardinality
|F|+|G|
(respectively
|F^{\prime}|+|G^{\prime}|
), it thus follows that
|F|+|G|=|F^{\prime}|+|G^{\prime}|
.

Let

•

A_{1}:=\{i|B_{i}\text{ is a singleton }\}
,

•

A^{\prime}_{1}:=\{i|B^{\prime}_{i}\text{ is a singleton }\}
,

•

A_{2}:=\{i|B_{i}\text{ is a singleton and there exists }j\neq i,B_{i}\subsetneq B_{j}\}
,

•

A^{\prime}_{2}=\{i|B^{\prime}_{i}\text{ is a singleton and there exists }j\neq i,B^{\prime}_{i}\subsetneq B^{\prime}_{j}\}
.

Let us notice that
|G|=|A_{2}|-|A_{1}|
(respectively,
|G^{\prime}|=|A^{\prime}_{2}|-|A^{\prime}_{1}|
).
The fact that
|A_{1}|=|A^{\prime}_{1}|
is obvious. Let us show that
|A_{2}|=|A^{\prime}_{2}|
.
Because
(M\times F)\sqcup G
is a closed set, the set
A_{2}
is in bijection with the set
A_{3}:=\{i|C_{i}\text{ admits a singleton in its irreductible components }\}
. Similarly,
A^{\prime}_{2}
is in bijection with the set
A^{\prime}_{3}:=\{i|C^{\prime}_{i}\text{ admits a singleton in its irreductible components }\}
.
By the last point of lemma
8.3
,
A_{3}
and
A^{\prime}_{3}
have the same cardinality.
As a result, so do
A_{2}
and
A^{\prime}_{2}
and hence
|G|=|G^{\prime}|
.

It remains to show that
\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X-X^{2})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. This is a direct application of the Chinese remainder theorem:

\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X-X^{2})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[X]/(X)\oplus\mathbb{Z}[X]/(1-X)\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

(An explicit isomorphism is for instance given by the homomorphism such that
1-X\mapsto(1,0)
and
X\mapsto(0,1)
.)

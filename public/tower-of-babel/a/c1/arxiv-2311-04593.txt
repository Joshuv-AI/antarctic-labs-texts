Quasi Normality and P ​ L PL Approximation of Least Area Surfaces in 3 3 -Manifolds
Eli Appleboim eliap@ee.technion.ac.il

Abstract
This paper presents relations between least area and normal surfaces, embedded in either a Euclidean or hyperbolic 3 3 -manifold. A relaxed version of normal surfaces, termed quasi-normal, is introduced, and it is shown that under appropriate conditions, every embedded least area surface is quasi-normal with respect to a fine enough fat triangulation of the 3 3 -manifold. In addition, it is shown that the intersections of a least area surface with the tetrahedra of such fine enough triangulation, even when not as simple as in the case of normal surfaces, are also well behaved. Finally, it is shown that a least area surface, when considered as a quasi normal surface, gives rise to a sequence of piecewise flat surfaces termed as flat-associated surfaces, and this sequence converges to the given least area surface and approximates its area.



1
Introduction

Let
(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{T})
be a smooth
3
-manifold equipped with a Riemannian metric
\mathfrak{g}
and a triangulation
\mathcal{T}
. For surfaces embedded in
\mathcal{M}
there are two notions of area that can be associated. One is the usual notion of area that is given by the metric
\mathfrak{g}
. The second version is given by the intersection of a surface with
\mathcal{T}
, and can be considered as a topological version of area. For each area function there is a notion of simplicity of surfaces. In the metric case this is the notion of least area surfaces, where a least area surface minimizes the area in some equivalence class, while in the topological view point it is given by least weight normal surfaces for which their intersection pattern with the triangulation admits some minimality criteria.

Given these two versions of area minimizing surfaces it is most natural to look for possible relations between them. It is already known that these two families of surfaces share some common properties. For instance, in
[
MSY
]
it is proved that every incompressible surface can be isotoped to a least area surface, while in
[
JR1
]
it is shown that every incompressible surface can be isotoped to a least weight normal surface. Yet, a complete picture of the relations between the two types of area minimizing surfaces is far from being achieved. For example, in
[
JR2
]
the following, still open, conjecture is made: ”… it should be possible to subdivide a triangulation arbitrarily finely and obtain sequences of
PL
minimal surfaces which converge to classical analytic minimal surfaces. This would be analogous to some of the early work on the Dirichlet principle.” (see,
[
JR2
]
, pp.495-496). A possible rephrase of this conjecture is given by the two following questions

(QI) Is it possible to subdivide a given triangulation of a
3
-manifold arbitrarily fine, so that every least area surface becomes a normal surface with respect to a fine enough triangulation?

(QII)Is every least area surface the limit surface, in some appropriate manner, of a sequence of normal surfaces?

An affirmative answer to the first question gives a topological interpretation of the differential geometric picture, while an affirmative answer to the second question gives an alternative topological proof of many classical results about the existence of least area surfaces in
3
-manifolds, such as those that were obtained in
[
MSY
]
, using partial differential equations. Moreover, the use of existing algorithms for finding normal surfaces (such as
[
JO
]
) makes this topological counterpart potentially computable.

The motivation for answering these two questions comes in two flavors. One is purely theoretical, as answering these questions significantly extends the analogy between minimal and normal surfaces. The second motivation is of an applied nature. For example, in the area of image processing, one of the possible ways of dealing with noisy images is by minimal surface theory(
[
MS
]
,
[
APT
]
). However, all existing techniques in this direction use at least second order derivations of the given noisy image, thus induce numerical instabilities that somehow need to be overcome. In contrast, the topological version of minimal and least area surfaces reflected by normal surfaces as proposed in
[
JR2
]
does not rely on derivatives hence may overcome numeric issues.

This paper addresses the two questions presented above. It is organized as follows: Section
2
is devoted to the presentation of all needed background material, covering basic notations, definitions and necessary known results on
3
-manifold topology, least area surfaces, normal surface theory and fat triangulations. Afterwards, in Sections
3
and
4
question (QI) is concerned. Section
3
begins with the definition of a quasi-normal surface which can be thought of as a relaxed version of a normal surface. Then, it is shown that every least area surface is quasi normal with respect to small enough (in terms of mesh-size), fat triangulation. Moreover, the upper bound on the mesh-size will be shown to depend only on the injectivity radius of
\mathcal{M}
, and on the Schoen curvature bound of least area surfaces, thus making this bound global (Section
3
, Theorem
1
). In Section
4
a tameness theorem will be proved, asserting that the intersection patterns of a quasi-normal surface with a small enough fat triangulation are well behaved and controlled (Section
4
, Theorem
2
). Results in these two sections will be shown first for closed
3
-manifolds, after which necessary modifications will be done for compact
3
-manifolds with boundary (Section
4
, Theorem
3
, and Theorem
4
).

In Sections
5
, and
6
the focus is turned toward question (QII). In
5
the median subdivision of a triangulation is introduced, and it will be shown that the median subdivision has the same fatness as the original triangulation. This yields a procedure under which a given triangulation can be iteratively subdivided, to obtain a sequence of triangulations having decreasing mesh and fixed fatness (Section
5
, Lemmas
5.9
and
5.12
). It is important to note that since it is necessary to keep the fatness fixed, such a subdivision process needs special care. For example, the commonly used barycentric subdivision will not do. Section
6
makes use of all results obtained in its preceding sections in order to give a method by which it is possible to approximate a given least area surface by a sequence of simple surfaces. First, the exact meaning of convergence of a sequence of surfaces is defined. Then, as a least area surface is assumed to be in quasi normal position with respect to a given triangulation, the notion of
flat associate
surface of the least area surface is defined. This will follow by showing that the median subdivision procedure built in Section
5
gives rise to a sequence of flat associated surfaces which converges to the given least area surface (Section
6
, Theorem
5
, and Theorem
6
).

2.2.1
Plateau Problem

The Plateau problem was first set by Lagrange although named after Joseph Plateau who was interested in soap films.
The statement of the problem is as follows:

Given a closed piecewise smooth curve
\Gamma
in a
3

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{F})=\inf\{\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{G})\;;\partial\mathcal{G}=\Gamma,and\textrm{ $\mathcal{G}$ is a surface isotopic to $\mathcal{F}$ }\}

A similar question can also be formulated for closed surfaces. A surface
\mathcal{F}
that admits the above equality is called a

.

2.3
Normal Surface Theory

Many of the classical results in the study of minimal and least area surfaces are based on methods of partial differential equations. These techniques although powerful, sometimes pose difficulties when trying to deduce topological information. Hence, it is desirable to have topological methods and techniques to deal with minimal and least area surfaces. For instance, it may be beneficial to analyse the way least area surfaces intersect with the tetrahedra of some given triangulation of the ambient manifold.

An example of such a topological viewpoint is the theory of
normal surfaces
. Normal surfaces were first introduced in the 1930‘s by Kneser (
[
Kne
]
), and further developed by Haken in the 1960‘s (
[
Hak
]
). This theory studies the ways surfaces intersect with a given triangulation of a
3
-manifold. In this section we will give some preliminary definitions and notations from normal surface theory that will be used later in this work. Most material in this section relies on
[
JR1
]
,
[
JR2
]
.

2.3.1
Normal Surfaces

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a surface properly embedded in a
3
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
, and let
\mathcal{T}
be a given triangulation of
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 2.7
.

An isotopy
H:\mathcal{M}\times I:\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is a normal isotopy with respect to
\mathcal{T}
, if
H_{x}(\sigma)=\sigma
for every
x\in I
and for all simplices
\sigma\in\mathcal{T}
. Two submanifolds of
\mathcal{M}
are normally isotopic if there exists a normal isotopy which carries one to the other.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{t}\in\mathcal{T}
denote a tetrahedron, and let
\mathfrak{f}
denote a face of
\mathfrak{t}
.
A normal arc in
\mathfrak{f}
, is an arc properly embedded in
\mathfrak{f}
, such that its end points are on two different edges of
\mathfrak{f}
. A normal curve in
\mathcal{T}^{(2)}
, is a curve properly embedded in
\mathcal{T}^{(2)}
, transverse to the edges of
\mathcal{T}
, so that its intersection with each
2
-face consists of a possibly empty collection of normal arcs. A normal disk is a disk
\mathcal{D}
, properly embedded in a tetrahedron
\mathfrak{t}
such that
\partial\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}\cap\partial\mathfrak{t}
, is a simple closed normal curve in
\partial\mathfrak{t}
. An elementary disk is a normal disk so that its boundary is either a triangle that separates one vertex on
\partial\mathfrak{t}
, from all other three vertices of
\partial\mathfrak{t}
, or a quadrilateral (quad for short) that partitions the vertices of the tetrahedron into two separated pairs.

Elementary disks are illustrated in Figure
2
, and they come in seven different types corresponding to seven possibilities to partition the set of vertices of a tetrahedron into subsets according to the definition. In the following we will sometimes refer to the boundary of an elementary disk as a

Definition 2.9
.

A surface

properly embedded in

is normal with respect to

if it is transverse to the skeleta of
\mathcal{T}
, and if its intersections with any of the tetrahedra of

Note that since the surface is embedded, we can have only one type of

2.4.1
Fat Triangulations

Definition 2.14
.

1.

2.

In this section we examine the way in which a least area surface meets a triangulation of a
3
-manifold. It will be shown that under some considerations such a surface meets the triangulation in a fairly simple way.

3.1
Notations and Definitions

Throughout the rest of this section
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{T})
will denote a smooth triangulated
3
-manifold. It will be assumed that
\mathcal{M}
admits either Euclidean or hyperbolic Reimannian structure. Recall the following notations: The injectivity radius of
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
inj_{\mathcal{M}}
, the maximal sectional curvature of
\mathcal{M}
by
K_{\mathcal{M}}
, and the mesh of a triangulation
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{M}
, by
\lambda(\mathcal{T})
.

Definition 3.1
.

A fixed surface
\mathcal{F}
embedded in the triangulated
3
-manifold
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{T})
, is quasi-normal with respect to
\mathcal{T}
if
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{T}^{(3)}
is a collection of disks, such that every normal disk in this collection is an elementary disk, and for every
2
-face
\mathfrak{f}
,
\mathcal{F}\cap int(\mathfrak{f})
does not contain any simple closed curve.

Note that the difference between quasi-normal surface and a normal one is that the intersection of a quasi-normal and the
2
-skeleton of the triangulation may contain curves having both their end points on the same edge of
\mathcal{T}^{(1)}
. Following Schleimer,
[
Schl
]
, such a curve will be called a

. It is illustrated in Figure
5
below.

\begin{array}[]{c}\hskip-4.83694pt\raisebox{-8.0pt}{\begin{picture}\includegraphics[]{BentCurve.pstex}\end{picture}\begin{picture}(3439.0,-4423.0)\put(6001.0,-3136.0){\pic@makebox@{\makebox}{}(0.0,0.0)[lb]{\smash{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}$\gamma$}}}}}\end{picture}}\end{array}

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a closed, irreducible, orientable, Euclidean or hyperbolic
3
-manifold. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fixed closed, two-sided, incompressible, least area surface in
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be some geodesic least area triangulation of
\mathcal{M}
so that
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{T}^{(0)}=\emptyset
. Then for every
0<\varphi<\frac{\pi}{2}
there exists a constant
\Psi>0
that depends only on
inj_{\mathcal{M}},K_{\mathcal{M}}
, and
\varphi
, such that if
\mathcal{T}
is
\varphi
-fat, and
\lambda(\mathcal{T})<\Psi
, then
\mathcal{F}
is quasi-normal with respect to
\mathcal{T}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Recall that according to Theorem
2.11
(see
2.4
of
[
JR1
]
), any incompressible surface can be isotoped to a surface that is normal with respect to any given triangulation
\mathcal{T}
. In this context Theorem
1
states that if the surface is also least area and the triangulation is fat with small enough mesh, then the surface is already in a position that, in some sense, is close to normal position.

We will make use of the following claim:

Claim 3.3
.

It is enough to prove Theorem
1
for a scaled manifold
(\mathcal{M}_{t},\mathfrak{g}_{t})
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{t}=\frac{1}{t}\mathfrak{g}
is a scaled version of the metric
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

According to
[
Por
]
the scaled manifold
\mathcal{M}_{t}
is homeomorphic to the original manifold
\mathcal{M}
, and has sectional curvature
K_{t}=t^{2}K
. In addition, since the scaling factor
\frac{1}{t}
is positive we have that a least area surface is mapped isotopically onto a least area surface with respect to the scaled metric
\mathfrak{g}_{t}
inside
\mathcal{M}_{t}
. Moreover, a
\varphi
-fat geodesic least area triangulation
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{M}
is mapped onto a
\varphi
-fat geodesic least area triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{t}
of
\mathcal{M}_{t}
. As a result, if
\mathcal{F}_{t}
is quasi normal with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{t}
then
\mathcal{F}
must be quasi normal with respect to
\mathcal{T}
, as the intersection patterns do not change along the scaling
\setminus
rescaling process. This proves the claim.
∎

Following Claim
3.3
and the fact that
K_{t}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow 0
, we have that the Schoen curvature bound
C_{t}
, given in
2.6
also tends to zero. Hence, without loss of generality we will assume that the curvature bound
C
is smaller than

. This fact will be of major significance for the proof of Theorem
1
.

3.2
Essential Lemmas

Theorem
1
will be deduced from the following three Lemmas.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists
\epsilon>0

is
\varphi
-fat for some positive

Proof.

Suppose conversely that

Case I:
Assume that the disk

4.1
Manifolds with Boundary

This section generalizes Theorems
1
and
2
to compact
3
-manifolds with boundary. Doing so requires some modifications in the proofs of those theorems which will be given herein. Before stating the generalized theorem a care need be taken for the mere existence of least area surfaces in manifolds with boundary. For that, the route taken in
[
HS
]
will be followed.

Definition 4.2
.

A Riemannian three manifold
\mathcal{M}
has
s
ufficiently convex boundary if:

1.

\partial\mathcal{M}
is piecewise smooth.

2.

Each smooth subsurface of
\partial\mathcal{M}
has non-negative mean curvature with respect to the inward normal.

3.

There exists a Riemannian three manifold
\mathcal{N}
such that
\mathcal{M}
is isometric to a submanifold of
\mathcal{N}
, and each smooth subsurface
\mathcal{S}
of
\partial\mathcal{M}
extends to a smooth subsurface
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
embedded in
\mathcal{N}
such that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{S}
.

Further, the following definition given by Morrey in
[
Mor
]
is necessary in order to assure the existence of the curvature and injectivity radius bounds that are used in the proofs of Theorems
1
and
2
.

Definition 4.3
.

A Riemnnian three manifold is
h
omogeneously regular if there exist positive constants
k,K
so that every point of the manifolds lies in the image of a chart
\varphi
with domain the unit ball
B(0,1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that

k||\nu||^{2}\leq g_{ij}(\varphi(x))\nu_{i}\nu_{j}\leq K||\nu||^{2}

for all
x\in B(0,1)
,
where
\nu
is any tangent vector to
x
, and
g_{ij}
are the components of the metric
g
on
\mathcal{M}
.

In
[
MY3
]
existence and uniqueness of least area disks spanned by simple closed curves in the boundary of a three manifold with sufficiently convex boundary are proven, see also
[
HS
]
, Section
6
. In addition, Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
extend to homogeneously regular three manifolds with sufficiently convex boundary, see Section
6
of
[
HS
]
. Note that if
\mathcal{T}
is a triangulation of a three-manifold with boundary then
\mathcal{T}
induces a triangulation of
\partial\mathcal{M}
, and according to
[
HS
]
if
\mathcal{M}
is homogeneously regular with sufficiently convex boundary then the boundary of small enough
2
-face of
\mathcal{T}
that is embedded in
\partial\mathcal{M}
bounds a least area disk in
\mathcal{M}
, that is either properly embedded in
\mathcal{M}
or a subset of
\partial\mathcal{M}
. In the proof of Lemma
3.4
we used the fact that
\mathcal{T}
is a least area triangulation, so every
2
-face is a least area disk. This assumption may not hold for three manifolds with boundary, for example in the case where a least area disk bounded by a simple closed curve included in
\partial\mathcal{M}
is properly embedded in
\mathcal{M}
.
As a result a proper modification of Theorem
1
needs to be stated.
Before stating the appropriate theorem the following definition is required.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulation of a three manifold with boundary. A
2
-face of
\mathcal{T}
is called
p
eripheral if it has non empty intersection with
\partial\mathcal{M}
. A
2

b

\partial\mathcal{M}
. A triangulation is called
i

2
-face that is not a boundary
2
-face is a least area disk spanned by its boundary.

In order to simplify the statement of the following theorem the definition of quasi-normal surface is altered.

Definition 4.5
.

A surface
(\mathcal{F},\partial\mathcal{F})
properly embedded in a triangulated three manifold
(\mathcal{M},\partial\mathcal{M},\mathcal{T})
is
i

\mathcal{T}
are bent curves, and a (possibly empty) collection of simple closed curves, each is contained in a boundary
2
-face of
\mathcal{T}
, and bounds a least area disk properly embedded in a peripheral tetrahedron of
\mathcal{T}
.

With these definitions at hand Theorem
1
is restated as follows:

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an irreducible, orientable, homogeneously regular Euclidean or hyperbolic three manifold with sufficiently convex boundary. Let
\mathcal{F}

Piecewise Linear Approximation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Recalling that the starting point for this work is the Jaco-Rubinstein
p\ell
version of least area and minimal surfaces this subsection is devoted to prove a
p\ell
version of Theorem
5
. This will be achieved by considering an isometric embedding of the manifold
\mathcal{M}
in some
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some large enough
n
, as guaranteed by the Nash embedding theorem, see
[
Na
]
. The
p\ell
approximation that will be obtained is therefore an approximation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
rather than inside the manifold
\mathcal{M}
, as done in the previous subsection. Such a
p\ell
version of Theorem
5
is further desirable since it is the common viewpoint in the variety of potential applications for “real world” problems in areas such as image processing and computer graphics as mentioned in the introduction.

The following
p\ell
version of Theorem
5
will be proved in this section.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a closed, orientable, irreducible, Euclidean or hyperbolic
3
-manifold isometrically embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a closed orientable incompressible least area surface embedded in
\mathcal{M}
. There exists a sequence
\large{\{}\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{n}\large{\}}
of piecewise linear
3
-manifolds embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, each homeomorphic to
\mathcal{M}
, and there exists a sequence
\large{\{}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\large{\}}
of piecewise linear surfaces, so that each
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{j}
is embedded in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{j}
, and such that
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{j}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
as
j\rightarrow\infty
, with respect to the Hausdorff metric on closed surfaces embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

6.3.1
Preliminary Lemmas Again

Let
\mathcal{T}
be some
\varphi
-fat triangulation of the Euclidean or hyperbolic manifold
\mathcal{M}
, and let
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}
be its linearization imposed by the secant map induced by
\mathcal{T}
, as in Definition
2.22
. That is, the vertices of
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}
are the same as the vertices of
\mathcal{T}
and in all other dimensions it is given as the linear span over the set of vertices.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a
2
-face of
\mathcal{T}
and let
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}
be its linearization in
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}
. Let
v
and
w
be two common vertices of both
\mathfrak{f}
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}
and let
e
and
\widehat{e}
denote the edges of
\mathfrak{f}
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}
respectively, between
v
and
w
. Let
\theta_{v}
be the angle between
e
and
\widehat{e}
at
v
. Then for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
that depends only on
K_{\mathcal{M}}
and
\epsilon
, so that if
\lambda(\mathcal{T})<\delta
then
|\theta_{v}|<\epsilon
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathfrak{f}
is a least area disk in
\mathcal{M}
, as
\mathcal{T}
is a least area triangulation of
\mathcal{M}
. Let
C
denote the Schoen bound with respect to the metric of
\mathcal{M}
. In particular, this bound holds for the
2
-face
\mathfrak{f}
. In order to prove the lemma we would like have such a bound on the second fundamental form of
\mathfrak{f}
also with respect to the Euclidean metric of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\mathcal{M}
is Euclidean then
C
is the desired bound. If
\mathcal{M}
is hyperbolic manifold then in Lemma
2.6
of
[
CLR
]
it is proved that the second fundamental form of
\mathfrak{f}
is bounded also with respect to the Euclidean metric of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by
8C
. Using this for example in the hyperbolic case, the following holds:

|\theta_{v}|\leq|\theta_{w}-\theta_{v}|\leq|\int_{e_{vw}}II_{Euc}d\xi|\leq\int_{e_{vw}}|II_{Euc}|d\xi\leq 8C\ell_{Euc}(e_{vw})\;,

(26)

where
II_{Euc},\ell_{Euc}
denote, respectively, the second fundamental form and the length function with respect to the Euclidean metric. In addition, if
\ell_{hyp}
denotes the length with respect to the hyperbolic metric of
\mathcal{M}
then:

\ell_{Euc}(e_{vw})<K_{\mathcal{M}}\ell_{hyp}(e_{vw})\leq K_{\mathcal{M}}\lambda(\mathcal{T})<C\lambda(\mathcal{T})\;.

(27)

In the Euclidean case similar inequalities will hold. From Equations
26
and
27
the lemma is deduced.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{f}
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}
be as in Lemma
6.2
. For every point
p\in\mathfrak{f}
and every point
\widehat{p}
in
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}
let
N_{p}\mathfrak{f}
and
N_{\widehat{p}}\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}
be the unit normal vectors to
\mathfrak{f}
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}
respectively. Similarly to Lemma
6.3
we have the following lemma:

Lemma 6.3
.

For every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
that depends only on
\epsilon
and
K_{\mathcal{M}}
, such that if
\lambda(\mathcal{T})<\delta
then
\measuredangle(N_{p}\mathfrak{f},N_{\widehat{p}}\widehat{\mathfrak{f}})<\epsilon
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
2.23
there exists
\delta>0
that depends only on
\varphi
such that if
\lambda(\mathcal{T})<\delta
then
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}
is contained in the
\epsilon
tubular neighborhood of
\mathfrak{f}
. Let
\Gamma
be a geodesic curve in
\mathfrak{f}
connecting the vertex
v
of
\mathfrak{f}
to
p
. Then there exists
\delta_{1}>0
that depends only on
\epsilon
and
K_{\mathcal{M}}
, such that:

|\measuredangle(N_{p}\mathfrak{f},N_{v}\mathfrak{f})|\leq\int_{\Gamma}|II_{Euc}d\xi|\leq 8C\ell(\Gamma)\leq 8C^{2}d_{\mathcal{M}}(p,v)\leq 8C^{2}\lambda(\mathcal{T})<\frac{\epsilon}{2}\;.

(28)

In addition, since
v
is also a vertex of
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}
, and since
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}
is a planar
2
-face in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the normal vectors to
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}
at
\widehat{p}
and at
v
are the same, hence from Lemma
6.2
there exists
\delta_{2}>0
that depends only on
\epsilon
and
K_{\mathcal{M}}
so that if
\lambda(\mathcal{T})<\delta_{2}
then
|\measuredangle(N_{p}\mathfrak{f},N_{\widehat{p}}\widehat{\mathfrak{f}})|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
. Combining this with Equation
28
proves the lemma.
∎

\mathcal{D}
is a non normal disk then

\widehat{\mathcal{T}}

\mathcal{T}
-flat associate of
\mathcal{D}

\bar{p}
be a point in

\delta_{1}>0

then
\measuredangle(N_{p}\mathcal{D},N_{\bar{p}}\overline{\mathcal{D}})<\frac{\epsilon}{2}

\delta_{2}>0

and on

AGP-based unitary coupled cluster theory for quantum computers
Armin Khamoshi, Guo P. Chen, Francesco A. Evangelista, Gustavo E. Scuseria

Abstract
Electronic structure methods typically benefit from symmetry breaking and restoration, specially in the strong correlation regime. The same goes for Ansätze on a quantum computer. We develop a unitary coupled cluster method based on the antisymmetrized geminal power (AGP)—a state formally equivalent to the number-projected Bardeen–Cooper–Schrieffer wavefunction. We demonstrate our method for the single-band Fermi–Hubbard Hamiltonian in one and two dimensions. We also explore post-selection as a state preparation step to obtain correlated AGP and prove that it scales no worse than 𝒪 ⁡ ( M ) \mathcal{O}(\sqrt{M}) in the number of measurements, thereby making it a less expensive alternative to gauge integration to restore particle number symmetry.

II
Theory

Define
{{{\Gamma}}}^{\dagger}
to be the geminal creation operator,

\displaystyle{{{\Gamma}}}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{pq}\eta_{pq}{c}^{\dagger}_{p}{c}^{\dagger}_{q},

(3)

where
{c}^{\dagger}_{p}
is a fermionic creation operator in a spin-orbital
p
and

\eta
is an anti-symmetric matrix whose elements are known as the

geminal coefficients
.
An AGP with
N
pairs, or
2N
electrons, can then be defined as

coleman_structure_1965
;
surjan_introduction_1999

\displaystyle|\text{AGP}\rangle=\frac{1}{N!}\left({{{\Gamma}}}^{\dagger}\right)^{N}|\text{vac}\rangle.

(4)

Unless otherwise specified, we choose to work in the natural orbital basis of
the geminal wherein the matrix
\eta
is brought to a
quasi-diagonal form
Hua_theory_1944
,

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}=\bigoplus_{p=1}^{M}\begin{pmatrix}0&\eta_{p}\\
-\eta_{p}&0\\
\end{pmatrix}=D^{\dagger}\eta D^{*},

(5)

via the orbital rotation

{a}^{\dagger}_{p}=\sum_{q=1}^{2M}D_{qp}{c}^{\dagger}_{q},

(6)

where
D
is the unitary matrix of natural orbital coefficients and

M
is the number of spatial orbitals. As such, the geminal operator can
be written as

\displaystyle{{{\Gamma}}}^{\dagger}=\sum_{p=1}^{M}\eta_{p}{a}^{\dagger}_{p}{a}^{\dagger}_{\bar{p}},

(7)

where the natural orbitals
p
and
\bar{p}
are “paired”
in the sense of Eq. (
5
).
As shown in Appendix
, a
(p,\,\bar{p})
pair
reduces to a spin
(\uparrow,\,\downarrow)
pair if the spins of the
natural orbitals are collinear. With this pairing scheme,
the relationship between AGP and the BCS wavefunction can be easily seen from

\displaystyle|\text{BCS}\rangle

\displaystyle=\prod_{p=1}^{M}(u_{p}+v_{p}{a}^{\dagger}_{p}{a}^{\dagger}_{\bar{p}})|\text{vac}\rangle

(8a)

\displaystyle\propto\prod_{p=1}^{M}(1+\eta_{p}{a}^{\dagger}_{p}{a}^{\dagger}_{\bar{p}})|\text{vac}\rangle

(8b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=0}^{M}\frac{1}{N!}\left({{{\Gamma}}}^{\dagger}\right)^{N}|\text{vac}\rangle,

(8c)

where the BCS coefficients
\{u_{p},v_{p}\}
relate to
\{\eta_{p}\}
through

\eta_{p}=v_{p}/u_{p}
.
Clearly the BCS wavefunction is a linear combination of AGPs with all possible
number of electron pairs (up to an inconsequential normalization factor).
The AGP of
N
pairs, Eq. (
4
), corresponds to
the projected BCS (PBCS) state with the correct particle number.
The AGP ground state can be optimized
on a classical computer at mean-field cost
(see Sec.
).

Beyond zero seniority

Seniority is defined as the number of unpaired electrons
and is denoted by
\Omega

.
Any two-body

Hamiltonian can be written as

\displaystyle H=H^{\delta{\Omega}=0}+H^{\delta{\Omega}=\pm 2}+H^{\delta{\Omega}=\pm 4},

where the superscript differentiates between sectors

The seniority-zero subspace features a remarkable simplicity

One can define a

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{\dagger}_{p}

\displaystyle={a}^{\dagger}_{p}{a}^{\dagger}_{\bar{p}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{p}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{p}h_{p}{Z}_{p}+\sum_{pq}w_{pq}{Z}_{p}{Z}_{q}

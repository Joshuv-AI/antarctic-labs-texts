Characterizations of Matrix valued asymmetric truncated Hankel operators
Rewayat Khan, Ji Eun Lee

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce the class of matrix valued asymmetric truncated Hankel operators. By using characterizations of matrix valued asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operators, we characterize matrix valued asymmetric truncated Hankel operators in the case when two involved inner matrices are J J -symmetric.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 47B35, Secondary 47B32, 30D20

1.
Introduction

As usual,
\mathbb{D}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\lambda|<1\}
and
\mathbb{T}=\partial\mathbb{D}=\{{z}\in\mathbb{C}:|{z}|=1\}
denote the unit disc and unit circle, respectively. Let
H^{2}
denote the classical Hardy space in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. Truncated Hankel operators (THO’s) (respectively truncated Toeplitz operators (TTO’s)) and asymmetric truncated Hankel operators (ATHO’s)(respectively asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operators (ATTO’s)) are compressions of multiplication operator to the backward shift invariant subspaces of
H^{2}
(with two possibly different underlying subspaces called model spaces in the asymmetric case). Each of these subspaces is of the form
K_{\theta}=(\theta H^{2})^{\perp}=H^{2}\ominus\theta H^{2}
, where
\theta
a complex-valued inner function:
\theta\in H^{\infty}
and
|\theta({z})|=1
a.e. on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. Since D. Sarasons paper
[
19
]
TTO’s, and later on ATTO’s
[
5
,
4
,
2
]
, MTTO’s
[
15
]
and MATTO’s
[
13
,
14
]
, have been intensly studied.

The theory of THO’s and ATHO’s in the scalar case can be extended to vector version, defined on subspaces of a vector valued Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathcal{H})
with a finite dimensional complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. These subspaces are corresponding to
inner functions
: An analytic function
\Theta
having values in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
(the algebra of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
), bounded and such that the boundary values
\Theta(z)
are unitary operators a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. We will consider only
pure inner functions
, that is,
\Theta
such that
\|\Theta(0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}<1
.
The model space

K_{\Theta}=[\Theta H^{2}(\mathcal{H})]^{\perp}

corresponding to an inner function
\Theta
is invariant under the backward shift
S^{*}
. Moreover, by the vector valued version of Beurling’s invariant subspace theorem, each closed (nontrivial)
S^{*}
–invariant subspace of
H^{2}(\mathcal{H})
is a model space (
[
1
, Chapter 5, Theorem 1.10]
). Let
P_{\Theta}
be the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
onto
K_{\Theta}
.

Sections 2 and 3 contain preliminary material on spaces of vector valued functions (Section 2), model spaces and MATTO’s (Section 3). Section 4 contains some results related to matrix valued asymmetric truncated Hankel operators. In section 5, we just remind the characterization of matrix valued asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operators (see
[
14
]
). In section 6, connection between matrix valued asymmetric truncated Toeplitz (MATTO’s) and matrix valued asymmetric truncated Hankel operators (MATHO’s) is presented. Section 7 and section 8 is devoted to characterizes matrix valued asymmetric truncated Hankel operators by using compressed shift and modified compressed shift respectively.

2.
Spaces of vector valued functions and their operators

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a complex separable Hilbert space. In what follows
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
will denote the norm and the inner product in
\mathcal{H}
, respectively. Moreover, we will assume that
\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty
. The space
L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
can be defined as

L^{2}(\mathcal{H})=\{f\colon\mathbb{T}\to\mathcal{H}:f\text{ is measurable and}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\|f({z})\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\,dm({z})<\infty\}

(
m
being the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
). As usual, each
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
is interpreted as a class of functions equal to the representing
f
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
with respect to
m
. The space
L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
is a (separable) Hilbert space with the inner product given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle f({z}),g({z})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,dm({z}),\quad f,g\in L^{2}(\mathcal{H})

and the induced norm is given by

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\|f({z})\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\,dm({z})=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\|a_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

The vector valued Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathcal{H})
is defined as the set of all the elements of
L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
whose Fourier coefficients with negative indices vanish. Each
f\in H^{2}(\mathcal{H})
,
\displaystyle f({z})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}{z}^{n}
, can also be identified with a function

f(\lambda)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\lambda^{n},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{D},

analytic in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.

We can also consider the spaces of all essentially bounded functions on the
\mathbb{T}
. This space is denoted by

L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
and

H^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})=L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})\cap H^{2}(\mathcal{H}).

Here we assume that
\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty
so we can consider
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
as a Hilbert space with the Hilbert–Schmidt norm and we may also define as above the spaces
L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
and
H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
:

H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))=\left\{{\mathbf{F}}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})):{\mathbf{F}}({z})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}{z}^{n}\right\}.

We thus have

L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))=[zH^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))]^{*}\oplus H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})).

For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
the function
{\mathbf{k}}_{\lambda}(z)=(1-\bar{\lambda}z)^{-1}I_{\mathcal{H}}
is bounded and belongs to
H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
, and has the following reproducing property

\langle f,{\mathbf{k}}_{\lambda}x\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}=\langle f(\lambda),x\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\quad f\in H^{2}(\mathcal{H}).

To each
\Phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
there corresponds a multiplication operator
M_{\Phi}:L^{2}(\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
: for
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
,

(M_{\Phi}f)({z})=\Phi({z})f({z})\quad\text{a.e. on }\mathbb{T}.

Let
P_{+}
denote the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
to
H^{2}(\mathcal{H})
. Then
the Toeplitz operator
T_{\Phi}
is the compression of
M_{\Phi}
to the Hardy space:
T_{\Phi}:H^{2}(\mathcal{H})\to H^{2}(\mathcal{H})
,

T_{\Phi}f=P_{+}M_{\Phi}f\quad\text{for }f\in H^{2}(\mathcal{H}).

It is clear that
(M_{\Phi})^{*}=M_{\Phi^{*}}
and
(T_{\Phi})^{*}=T_{\Phi^{*}}
, where
\Phi^{*}({z})=\Phi({z})^{*}
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. The operators
S
(forward shift) and
S^{*}
(backward shift) are examples of Toeplitz operators with symbols
zI_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\overline{z}I_{\mathcal{H}}
, respectively, where
I_{\mathcal{H}}
is the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
.

3.
MATTO’s and MATHO’s

For two operator valued inner functions
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
and
\Phi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
let

(3.1)

A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}f=P_{\Theta_{2}}(\Phi f),\quad f\in K_{\Theta_{1}}\cap H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})).

The operator
A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}
is called a matrix valued asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operator (MATTO). When
\Theta_{1}=\Theta_{2}=\Theta
, then
A_{\Phi}^{\Theta}=A_{\Phi}^{\Theta,\Theta}
is called a matrix valued truncated Toeplitz operator (MTTO’s)
[
15
]
. Both are densely defined. Let
\mathcal{MT}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
be the set of all MATTO’s of the form (
3.1
) which can be extended boundedly to the whole space
K_{\Theta_{1}}
and for
\Theta_{1}=\Theta_{2}=\Theta
let
\mathcal{MT}(\Theta)=\mathcal{MT}(\Theta,\Theta)
.

Two important examples of operators from
\mathcal{MT}(\Theta)
are the model operators

(3.2)

S_{\Theta}=A_{z}^{\Theta}=A_{zI_{\mathcal{H}}}^{\Theta}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\Theta}^{*}=A_{\bar{z}}^{\Theta}=A_{\bar{z}I_{\mathcal{H}}}^{\Theta}.

These operators are models for a class of Hilbert space contractions. For example, each
C_{0}
contraction with finite defect indices is unitarily equivalent to
S_{\Theta}
for some
operator valued inner function
\Theta
(see
[
18
, Chapter IV]
).

Truncated Hankel operators were first defined and studied by C. Gu in
[
9
]
. Their asymmetric version were introduced in
[
16
]
.

An asymmetric matrix valued truncated Hankel operator
B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}
with symbol
\Phi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
is an operator from
K_{\Theta_{1}}
into
K_{\Theta_{2}}
densely defined by

(3.3)

B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}f=P_{\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}(I-P_{+})(\Phi f),\quad f\in K_{\Theta_{1}}^{\infty},

where
{\mathbf{J}}:L^{2}(E)\to L^{2}(E)
is the flip operator given by

{\mathbf{J}}f(z)=\overline{z}f(\overline{z}),\quad|z|=1.

Let
\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
be the set of all MATHO’s of the form (
3.3
) which can be extended boundedly to the whole space
K_{\Theta_{1}}
and
\mathcal{MH}(\Theta)
denote the set of all MTHO’s.
Some algebraic properties of THO’s were studied in
[
9
]
. Here we continue the investigation started in
[
10
,
16
]
.

For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
we can consider

{\mathbf{k}}_{\lambda}^{\Theta}(z)=\tfrac{1}{1-\bar{\lambda}z}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\Theta(z)\Theta(\lambda)^{*})\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})).

Then, for each
x\in\mathcal{H}
it has the following reproducing property

\langle f,{\mathbf{k}}_{\lambda}^{\Theta}x\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}=\langle f(\lambda),x\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\quad\text{for every }f\in K_{\Theta}.

It follows in particular that
K_{\Theta}^{\infty}=K_{\Theta}\cap H^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
is a dense subset of
K_{\Theta}
.

Let

\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}=\{(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\Theta\Theta(0)^{*})x:\ x\in\mathcal{H}\}=\{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}^{\Theta}x:\ x\in\mathcal{H}\}\subset K_{\Theta}.

For each
f\in K_{\Theta}
we have
f\perp\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}
if and only if
f(0)=0
.
It follows that

(S_{\Theta}^{*}f)(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\bar{z}f(z)&\text{for }f\perp\mathcal{D}_{\Theta},\\
-\bar{z}\big(\Theta(z)-\Theta(0)\big)\Theta(0)^{*}x&\text{for }f={\mathbf{k}}_{0}^{\Theta}x\in\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}.\end{array}\right.

The defect operator of
S_{\Theta}
is denoted by

D_{\Theta}:=I_{K_{\Theta}}-S_{\Theta}S_{\Theta}^{*}.

Since for each
f\in H^{2}(\mathcal{H})
we have
(I_{H^{2}(\mathcal{H})}-SS^{*})f=f(0)
(a constant function in
H^{2}(\mathcal{H})
), it follows that for
f\in K_{\Theta}
,

(3.4)

\begin{split}D_{\Theta}f&=(I_{K_{\Theta}}-S_{\Theta}S_{\Theta}^{*})f=P_{\Theta}(I_{H^{2}(\mathcal{H})}-SS^{*})f\\
&=(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\Theta\Theta(0)^{*})f(0)={\mathbf{k}}_{0}^{\Theta}f(0)\in\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}.\end{split}

More precisely,

D_{\Theta}f=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\text{for }f\perp\mathcal{D}_{\Theta},\\
{\mathbf{k}}_{0}^{\Theta}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\Theta(0)\Theta(0)^{*})x&\text{for }f={\mathbf{k}}_{0}^{\Theta}x\in\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}.\end{array}\right.

4.
MATHO’s and Model spaces

In
[
12
]
the author considers the generalized Crofoot transform. For a pure inner function
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
and
W\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
such that
\|W\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}<1
define the generalized Crofoot transform
J_{W}^{\Theta}:L^{2}(\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
by

(4.1)

J_{W}^{\Theta}f=D_{W^{*}}(I_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}-\Theta W^{*})^{-1}f,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{H}).

The adjoint of
J_{W}^{\Theta}
is given by the formula

(4.2)

(J_{W}^{\Theta})^{\ast}g=D_{W^{*}}(I_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}+\Theta^{W}W^{*})^{-1}g,\quad g\in L^{2}(\mathcal{H}).

Then
J_{W}^{\Theta}
is unitary and maps
K_{\Theta}
onto
K_{\Theta^{W}}
where

(4.3)

\Theta^{W}(z)=-W+D_{W^{*}}(I_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}-\Theta(z)W^{*})^{-1}\Theta(z)D_{W}.

The following theorem describes the action of the Crofoot transform on
\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
(see
[
12
]
for the case
\Theta_{1}=\Theta_{2}
and
[
11
]
for the scalar case):

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
be two pure inner functions and let
W_{1},W_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be such that
\|W_{1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}<1
and
\|W_{2}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}<1
. A bounded linear operator
B:K_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
belongs to
\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if
J_{W_{2}}^{\Theta_{2}}B(J_{W_{1}}^{\Theta_{1}})^{*}
belongs to
\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1}^{W_{1}},\Theta_{2}^{W_{2}})
. More precisely,
B=B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if

J_{W_{2}}^{\Theta_{2}}B(J_{W_{1}}^{\Theta_{1}})^{*}=B_{\Psi}^{\Theta_{1}^{W_{1}},\Theta_{2}^{W_{2}}}\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1}^{W_{1}},\Theta_{2}^{W_{2}})

where
\Psi
is given by

\Psi(z)=D_{W_{2}^{*}}(I_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}-\Theta_{2}(z)W_{2}^{*})^{-1}\Phi(z)D_{W_{1}^{*}}(I_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}+\Theta^{W_{1}}_{1}(z)W_{1}^{*})^{-1}.

Proof.

Let
f\in K_{\Theta_{1}^{W_{1}}}
and
x\in\mathcal{H}
. Then

\displaystyle\langle J_{W_{2}}^{\Theta_{2}}B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}(J_{W_{1}}^{\Theta_{1}})^{\ast}f,k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}(J_{W_{1}}^{\Theta_{1}})^{\ast}f,(J_{W_{2}}^{\Theta_{2}})^{\ast}k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P_{\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}(I-P_{+})(\Phi(J_{W_{1}}^{\Theta_{1}})^{\ast}f),(J_{W_{2}}^{\Theta_{2}})^{\ast}k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{J}}(\Phi(J_{W_{1}}^{\Theta_{1}})^{\ast}f),k_{\lambda}^{\Theta_{2}}(I-W_{2}\Theta_{2}^{*})^{-1}D_{W_{2}^{*}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle\bar{z}(\Phi(J_{W_{1}}^{\Theta_{1}})^{\ast}f)(\bar{z}),(J_{W_{2}}^{\Theta_{2}})^{\ast}k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle\bar{z}\Phi(\bar{z})((J_{W_{1}}^{\Theta_{1}})^{\ast}f)(\bar{z}),(J_{W_{2}}^{\Theta_{2}})^{\ast}k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle\bar{z}\Phi(\bar{z})D_{W_{1}^{*}}(I+\Theta^{\prime}_{1}(\bar{z})W_{1}^{*})^{-1}f(\bar{z}),(J_{W_{2}}^{\Theta_{2}})^{\ast}k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle J_{W_{2}}^{\Theta_{2}}(\bar{z}\Phi(\bar{z})D_{W_{1}^{*}}(I+\Theta^{\prime}_{1}(\bar{z})W_{1}^{*})^{-1}f(\bar{z})),k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle D_{W_{2}^{*}}(I-\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})W_{2}^{*})^{-1}\bar{z}\Phi(\bar{z})D_{W_{1}^{*}}(I+\Theta^{\prime}_{1}(\bar{z})W_{1}^{*})^{-1}f(\bar{z}),k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle\bar{z}D_{W_{2}^{*}}(I-\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})W_{2}^{*})^{-1}\Phi(\bar{z})D_{W_{1}^{*}}(I+\Theta^{\prime}_{1}(\bar{z})W_{1}^{*})^{-1}f(\bar{z}),k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle{\mathbf{J}}(D_{W_{2}^{*}}(I-\Theta_{2}(z)W_{2}^{*})^{-1}\Phi(z)D_{W_{1}^{*}}(I+\Theta^{\prime}_{1}(z)W_{1}^{*})^{-1}f(z)),k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle P_{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}(\Psi(z)f(z)),k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P_{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}(\Psi f),k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P_{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}(I-P_{+})(\Psi f),k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle B^{\Theta^{W_{1}}_{1},\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}_{\Psi}f,k_{\lambda}^{\Theta^{W_{2}}_{2}}x\rangle,

where
\Psi
is given by

\Psi(z)=D_{W_{2}^{*}}(I_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}-\Theta_{2}(z)W_{2}^{*})^{-1}\Phi(z)D_{W_{1}^{*}}(I_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}+\Theta^{W_{1}}_{1}(z)W_{1}^{*})^{-1}.

Hence we complete the proof.
∎

Recall that if
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
is an inner function, then so is

\widetilde{\Theta}(z)=\Theta(\bar{z})^{*}.

Let us consider the map
\tau_{\Theta}:L^{2}(\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
defined for
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
by

(4.4)

(\tau_{\Theta}f)(z)=\bar{z}\Theta(\bar{z})^{*}f(\bar{z})=\bar{z}\widetilde{\Theta}(z)f(\bar{z})\quad\text{a.e. on }\mathbb{T}.

The map
\tau_{\Theta}
is an isometry and its adjoint
\tau_{\Theta}^{*}=\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}}
is also its inverse. Hence
\tau_{\Theta}
is unitary. Moreover, it is easy to verify that

\tau_{\Theta}(\Theta H^{2}(\mathcal{H}))\subset H^{2}(\mathcal{H})^{\perp}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{\Theta}(H^{2}(\mathcal{H})^{\perp})\subset\widetilde{\Theta}H^{2}(\mathcal{H}),

which implies that

\tau_{\Theta}(K_{\Theta})=K_{\widetilde{\Theta}}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
be two pure inner functions. A bounded linear operator
B:K_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
belongs to
\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if
\tau_{\Theta_{2}}B\,\tau_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}
belongs to
\mathcal{MH}(\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2})
. More precisely,
B=B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if
\tau_{\Theta_{2}}B\,\tau_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=B_{\Psi}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}\in\mathcal{MH}(\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2})
with

(4.5)

\begin{split}\Psi(z)&=\Theta_{2}(z)^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}(z)^{*}\\
&=\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}(\bar{z})\Phi(\bar{z})\Theta_{1}(\bar{z})\quad\text{a.e. on }\mathbb{T}.\end{split}

Proof.

Let
B:K_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
be a bounded linear operator. Assume that
B=B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}\in\mathcal{MT}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
with some
\Phi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
, and take
f\in K_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}^{\infty}
and
g\in K_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}^{\infty}
. Note that
\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}f\in K_{{\Theta}_{1}}^{\infty}
and
\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}g\in K_{{\Theta}_{2}}^{\infty}
. Then

\displaystyle\langle\tau_{\Theta_{2}}B\,\tau_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\langle B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}f,\tau_{{\Theta}_{2}}^{*}g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}(I-P_{+})({\Phi}\,\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}f),\tau_{{\Theta}_{2}}^{*}g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}({\Phi}\,\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}f),\tau_{{\Theta}_{2}}^{*}g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\langle\tau_{{\Theta}_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}({\Phi}\,\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}f),g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle\tau_{{\Theta}_{2}}(\bar{z}\Phi(\bar{z})(\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}f)(\bar{z})),g(z)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,dm(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle\bar{z}\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})^{*}(z\Phi(z)(\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}f)(z)),g(z)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,dm(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})^{*}\Phi(z)\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}(\bar{z})^{*}\bar{z}f(\bar{z}),g(z)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,dm(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle\bar{z}\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})^{*}\Phi(z)\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}(\bar{z})^{*}f(\bar{z}),g(z)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,dm(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle{\mathbf{J}}(\Theta_{2}(z)^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}(z)^{*}f(z)),g(z)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,dm(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle P_{\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}(I-P_{+})(\Theta_{2}(z)^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}(z)^{*}f(z)),g(z)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,dm(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\langle P_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}(I-P_{+})(\Psi(z)f(z)),g(z)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,dm(z)

\displaystyle=\langle B_{\Psi}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}f,g\rangle,

where
\Psi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
is given by (
4.5
).

Now, if
\tau_{\Theta_{2}}B\,\tau_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=B_{\Psi}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}\in\mathcal{MH}(\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2})
for some
\Psi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
, then
B=\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}B_{\Psi}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}^{*}
by the first part of the proof of
B=B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
with

(4.6)

\begin{split}\Phi(z)=\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}(z)^{*}\Psi(\bar{z})\Theta_{1}(z)^{*}\quad\text{a.e. on }\mathbb{T}.\end{split}

∎

Denote

\widetilde{D}_{\Theta}=I-S_{\Theta}^{*}S_{\Theta}.

Applying Theorem
4.2
to the model operator
S_{\Theta}
(
\Theta_{1}=\Theta_{2}=\Theta
) we obtain that

(4.7)

\tau_{\Theta}S_{\Theta}\tau_{\Theta}^{*}=\tau_{\Theta}S_{\Theta}\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}}=S_{\widetilde{\Theta}}^{*}

(see
[
15
, p. 1001]
). It follows that

(4.8)

\widetilde{D}_{\Theta}=\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}}{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}}\tau_{\Theta}=\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}}{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}}\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}}^{*}

and by (
3.4
),

\widetilde{D}_{\Theta}f=\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}}\big({\mathbf{k}}_{0}^{\widetilde{\Theta}}(\tau_{\Theta}f)(0)\big)\quad\text{for all }f\in K_{\Theta}.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, let
\widetilde{{\mathbf{k}}}_{\lambda}^{\Theta}(z)x=\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}}({\mathbf{k}}_{\bar{\lambda}}^{\widetilde{\Theta}}(z)x)
for
x\in\mathcal{H}
. Then (a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
)

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathbf{k}}}_{\lambda}^{\Theta}(z)x

\displaystyle=\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}}({\mathbf{k}}_{\bar{\lambda}}^{\widetilde{\Theta}}(z)x)=\bar{z}\Theta(z){\mathbf{k}}_{\bar{\lambda}}^{\widetilde{\Theta}}(\bar{z})x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\bar{z}}{1-\lambda\bar{z}}\Theta(z)(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\widetilde{\Theta}(\bar{z})\widetilde{\Theta}(\bar{\lambda})^{*})x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{z-\lambda}\Theta(z)(I_{\mathcal{H}}-{\Theta}(z)^{*}{\Theta}(\lambda))x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{z-\lambda}(\Theta(z)-{\Theta}(\lambda))x\in K_{\Theta}\quad\text{for each }x\in\mathcal{H}.

In particular,

\widetilde{{\mathbf{k}}}_{0}^{\Theta}(z)x=\bar{z}(\Theta(z)-{\Theta}(0))x~\quad\text{for each }x\in\mathcal{H}.

and

\widetilde{D}_{\Theta}f=\widetilde{{\mathbf{k}}}_{0}^{\Theta}(\tau_{\Theta}f)(0)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta},

where

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta}=\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}}\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}}=\{\widetilde{{\mathbf{k}}}_{0}^{{\Theta}}x:x\in\mathcal{H}\}=\{\bar{z}(\Theta(z)-\Theta(0))x:x\in\mathcal{H}\}.

Observe that for
f\in K_{\Theta}
,
x\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\langle f,\widetilde{{\mathbf{k}}}_{\lambda}^{\Theta}x\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\langle f,\tau_{\widetilde{\Theta}}({\mathbf{k}}_{\bar{\lambda}}^{\widetilde{\Theta}}x)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\langle\tau_{\Theta}f,{\mathbf{k}}_{\bar{\lambda}}^{\widetilde{\Theta}}x\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}=\langle(\tau_{\Theta}f)(\bar{\lambda}),x\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

Then it follows that for
f\in K_{\Theta}
we have
M_{z}f\in K_{\Theta}
if and only if
f\perp\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta}
.
Indeed,
M_{z}f\in K_{\Theta}
if and only if
\Theta P_{+}(\Theta^{*}M_{z}f)=0
. Since

(\Theta^{*}M_{z}f)(z)=\Theta(z)^{*}zf(z)=(\tau_{\Theta}f)(\overline{z}),

we have
P_{+}(\Theta^{*}M_{z}f)=(\tau_{\Theta}f)(0)
and so
M_{z}f\in K_{\Theta}
if and only if

0=\langle(\tau_{\Theta}f)(0),x\rangle=\langle f,\widetilde{{\mathbf{k}}}_{0}^{\Theta}x\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}\quad\text{for every}\quad x\in\mathcal{H},

i.e,
f\perp\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta}
. Therefore we get

(S_{\Theta}f)(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}zf(z)&\text{for }f\perp\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta},\\
-\big(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\Theta(z)\Theta(0)^{*}\big)\Theta(0)x&\text{for }f=\widetilde{{\mathbf{k}}}_{0}^{\Theta}x\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta}.\end{array}\right.

Hence

\widetilde{{D}}_{\Theta}f=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\text{for }f\perp\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta},\\
\widetilde{{\mathbf{k}}}_{0}^{\Theta}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\Theta(0)\Theta(0)^{*})x&\text{for }f=\widetilde{{\mathbf{k}}}_{0}^{\Theta}x\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta}.\end{array}\right.

Let us now consider conjugations. A conjugation
J
on a Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}
is an antilinear map
J:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that
J^{2}=I_{\mathcal{H}}
and

\langle Jf,Jg\rangle=\langle g,f\rangle\quad\text{for all}\quad f,g\in\mathcal{H}

Recall that a bounded linear operator
T:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is said to be
J
-symmetric (
J
being a conjugation on
\mathcal{H}
) if
JTJ=T^{*}
. We say that
T
is complex symmetric if it is
J
-symmetric with respect
to some conjugation
J
(see
[
8
]
).

In
[
3
]
the authors consider certain classes of conjugations in
L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
. One such conjugation is
{\mathbf{J}}^{*}:L^{2}(\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
defined for a fixed conjugation
J
on
\mathcal{H}
by

(4.9)

({\mathbf{J}}^{*}f)(z)=J(f(\overline{z}))\quad\text{a.e. on}~~\mathbb{T}.

It is not difficult to verify that for
f(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
, we have

({\mathbf{J}}^{*}f)(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}J(a_{n})z^{n}.

Hence,
{\mathbf{J}}^{*}
is an
M_{z}
-commuting conjugation, i.e,
{\mathbf{J}}^{*}M_{z}=M_{z}{\mathbf{J}}^{*}
,
{\mathbf{J}}^{*}(H^{2}(\mathcal{H}))=H^{2}(\mathcal{H})
, and
{\mathbf{J}}^{*}P_{+}=P_{+}{\mathbf{J}}^{*}
(see
[
3
, Section 4]
).

For
\Phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
and an arbitrary conjugation
J
on
\mathcal{H}
let

(4.10)

\Phi_{J}(z)=J\Phi(z)J\quad\text{a.e on}~~~\mathbb{T}.

Then
\Phi_{J}\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
. As observed in
[
3
]
,
\Phi_{J}\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
if and only if
\Phi\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
, and
\Phi_{J}
is an inner function if and only if
\Phi
is. Clearly,
(\Phi_{J})_{J}=\Phi.
Let us also observe that if
\Phi
is
J
-symmetric, that is,
J\Phi(z)J=\Phi(z)^{*}
a.e on
\mathbb{T}
(or equivalently
\Phi(\lambda)
is
J
-symmetric for
\lambda
in
\mathbb{D}
, see
[
3
]
), then
\Phi_{J}=\widetilde{\Phi}
.

For two conjugations
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
on
\mathcal{H}
, let
{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}
and
{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}
denote the corresponding conjugations on
L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
given by
(
4.9
)
. For each
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
we have

(4.11)

({\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}M_{\Phi}f)(z)=J_{2}(\Phi(\overline{z})f(\overline{z}))=J_{2}\Phi(\overline{z})J_{1}J_{1}f(\overline{z})=(M_{\mathbf{G}}{\mathbf{J}}^{*}_{1}f)(z)

where
{\mathbf{G}}(z)=J_{2}\Phi(\overline{z})J_{1}
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. In particular

(4.12)

{\mathbf{J}}^{*}M_{\Phi}=M_{\Phi}{\mathbf{J}}^{*}

(see
[
3
, Lemma 8.3]
).

Note that
\Phi_{J}
is also defined for
\Phi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
. In that case (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) hold for
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
we have the following equality:

(4.13)

({\mathbf{J}}^{*}{\mathbf{J}}f)(z)={\mathbf{J}}^{*}(\bar{z}f(\bar{z}))=J(zf(z))=\bar{z}J(f(z))

and

(4.14)

({\mathbf{J}}{\mathbf{J}}^{*}f)(z)={\mathbf{J}}(Jf(\bar{z}))=\bar{z}J(f(z))=({\mathbf{J}}^{*}{\mathbf{J}}f)(z).

Proposition 4.3
.

[
3
]

Let
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
be a pure inner function and let
J
be a conjugation on
\mathcal{H}
. Then the following identities hold.

(a)

{\mathbf{J}}^{*}(\Theta H(\mathcal{H}))=\Theta_{J}H^{2}(\mathcal{H})
;

(b)

{\mathbf{J}}^{*}P_{\Theta}=P_{\Theta_{J}}{\mathbf{J}}^{*}
;

(c)

;

(d)

{\mathbf{J}}^{*}({\mathbf{k}}_{\lambda}^{\Theta}x)={\mathbf{k}}_{\overline{\lambda}}^{\Theta_{{\mathbf{J}}}}Jx
.

If
\Theta
is
J
-symmetric, we obtain
[
3
, Proposition 7.7]
:

Proposition 4.4
.

[
3
]

Let
J
be a conjugation on
\mathcal{H}
and let
\Theta
be a
J
-symmetric inner function.
Then the following identities hold.

(a)

{\mathbf{J}}^{*}M_{\Theta}=M_{\widetilde{\Theta}}{\mathbf{J}}^{*}
;

(b)

{\mathbf{J}}^{*}(\Theta H^{2}(\mathcal{H}))=\widetilde{\Theta}H^{2}(\mathcal{H})
;

(c)

{\mathbf{J}}^{*}(K_{\Theta})=K_{\widetilde{\Theta}}

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
be two pure inner functions and let
J_{1},J_{2}
be two conjugations on
\mathcal{H}
. A bounded linear operator
B:K_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
belongs to
\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if
{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}
belongs to
\mathcal{MH}((\Theta_{1})_{J_{1}},(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}})
. More precisely,
B=B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if

{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B_{\Phi}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}=B_{\Psi}^{(\Theta_{1})_{J_{1}},(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}}}\in\mathcal{MH}((\Theta_{1})_{J_{1}},(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}})
with

(4.15)

\Psi(z)=J_{2}\Phi(\overline{z})J_{1}\quad\text{a.e. on }~~\mathbb{T}.

Proof.

Assume that
B=B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
with
\Phi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
. Let
f\in K_{(\Theta_{1})_{J_{1}}}^{\infty}
. Note that
{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f\in K_{\Theta_{1}}^{\infty}
. Therefore, by Proposition
4.3
(
b
), (
4.12
) and (
4.14
),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f

\displaystyle={\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}P_{\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}(I-P)(\Phi{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f)

\displaystyle=P_{(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}}}{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}{\mathbf{J}}(\Phi{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f)

\displaystyle=P_{(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}}}{\mathbf{J}}{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}(\Phi{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f)

\displaystyle=P_{(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}}}{\mathbf{J}}{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}(\Phi J_{1}f(\bar{z}))

\displaystyle=P_{(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}}}{\mathbf{J}}(J_{2}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}f(z))

\displaystyle=P_{(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}}}{\mathbf{J}}(I-P_{+})(J_{2}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}f(z))

\displaystyle=B_{\Psi}^{(\Theta_{1})_{J_{1}},(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}}}f

where
\Psi
is given by
(
4.15
)
. Thus
{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}\in\mathcal{MH}((\Theta_{1})_{J_{1}},(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}})
.

On the other hand, if

B={\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B_{\Psi}^{(\Theta_{1})_{J_{1}},(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}\in\mathcal{MH}((\Theta_{1})_{J_{1}},(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}})
with some
\Psi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
, then
B={\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B_{\Psi}^{(\Theta_{1})_{J_{1}},(\Theta_{2})_{J_{2}}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}
and as above,
B=B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}
with

\Phi(z)=J_{2}\Psi(\bar{z})J_{1}\quad\text{a.e. on}~~\mathbb{T}.

∎

In the scalar case each model space
K_{\theta}
is equipped with a natural conjugation
C_{\theta}
defined in terms of boundary functions by

(C_{\theta}f)(z)=\theta(z)\overline{z}\overline{f(z)}
. If
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
is an inner function and

is a conjugation on

we can similarly define
{\mathbf{C}}_{\Theta}:L^{2}(\mathcal{H})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{H})
by

({\mathbf{C}}_{\Theta}f)(z)=\Theta(z)\overline{z}J(f(z))\quad\text{a.e. on }~~\mathbb{T}.

Although
{\mathbf{C}}_{\Theta}
is obviously an antilinear isometry, it is not in general an involution. A simple computation shows that
{\mathbf{C}}_{\Theta}
is an involution (and so a conjugation) if and only if
\Theta(z)J\Theta(z)J=I_{\mathcal{H}}
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
, i.e., if and only if
\Theta
is
J
-symmetric.

If
\Theta
is
J
-symmetric, then
{\mathbf{C}}_{\Theta}(\Theta H^{2}(\mathcal{H}))=H^{2}(\mathcal{H})^{\perp}
and so

{\mathbf{C}}_{\Theta}(K_{\Theta})=K_{\Theta}.

Note that in that case

{\mathbf{C}}_{\Theta}={\mathbf{J}}^{*}\tau_{\Theta}.

By Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
4.5
we get the following.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
be two pure inner functions and let

be two conjugations on
\mathcal{H}
such that

is

-symmetric and

is

-symmetric. A bounded linear operator

belongs to

with

\Psi(z)=J_{2}\Theta_{2}(z)\Phi(z)\Theta_{1}(\bar{z})^{*}J_{1}\quad\text{a.e. on }\mathbb{T}

Let
\Phi\in L^{2}({\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})})
and

. Consider

\displaystyle=C_{\Theta_{2}}B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}={\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}\tau_{\Theta_{2}}B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}\tau^{*}_{\Theta_{1}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}={\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B_{\chi}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}^{*}_{1}

with the symbol

.

Characterizations by Compressed Shift

Lemma 5.1
.

[
14

If

\widehat{B}_{2}:\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\to K_{\Theta_{1}}
such that

S_{\Theta_{2}}A-AS_{\Theta_{1}}=\widehat{B}_{1}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{1}}+D_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}_{2}^{*}.

5.2.
By Shift invariance

In the scalar case the notion of shift invariance for TTO’s was introduced in
[
19
]
. D. Sarason proved that a bounded linear operators
A:K_{\theta}\to K_{\theta}
is a TTO if and only if it is shift invariant, i.e.,

\langle ASf,Sf\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle Af,f\rangle_{L^{2}}

for each
f\in K_{\theta}
such that
Sf\in K_{\theta}.

In
[
15
]
we prove that the same is true for MATTO’s.

For ATTO’s the notion of shift invariance was introduced in
[
6
]
( see also
[
17
]
). Shift invariance of matrix valued truncated Toeplitz operators is presented in
[
15
]
. Here we consider shift invariance of MATTO’s. As in the scalar case (see
[
10
]
), we characterize MATTO’s in term of four (equivalent) types of shift invariance.

Recall that for an operator valued inner function
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
and for
f\in K_{\Theta}
we have

Sf=M_{z}f\in K_{\Theta}~~\text{if and only if}~~f\perp\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta}~~((\tau_{\Theta}f(0))=0)

and

S^{*}f=M_{\overline{z}}f\in K_{\Theta}~~\text{if and only if}~~f\perp\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}~~(f(0)=0).

Theorem 5.7
.

[
14
]

Let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
be two pure inner functions and let
A:K_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
be a bounded linear operator. Then
A
belongs to
\mathcal{MT}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if it has one (and all) of the following properties:

(a)

\langle AS^{*}f,S^{*}g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}=\langle Af,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}
for all
f\in K_{\Theta_{1}}
,
g\in K_{\Theta_{2}}
such that
f\perp\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{1}}
,
g\perp\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{2}}
;

(b)

\langle AS^{*}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}=\langle Af,Sg\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}
for all
f\in K_{\Theta_{1}}
,
g\in K_{\Theta_{2}}
such that
f\perp\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{1}}
,
g\perp\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta_{2}}
;

(c)

\langle ASf,Sg\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}=\langle Af,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}
for all
f\in K_{\Theta_{1}}
,
g\in K_{\Theta_{2}}
such that
f\perp\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta_{1}}
,
g\perp\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta_{2}}
;

(d)

\langle ASf,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}=\langle Af,S^{*}g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H})}
for all
f\in K_{\Theta_{1}}
,
g\in K_{\Theta_{2}}
such that
f\perp\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta_{1}}
,
g\perp\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{2}}
.

5.3.
Characterization by modified compressed shift

The notion of modified compressed shift was introduced in
[
15
]
. For any nonconstant inner function
\Theta
, suppose that
X_{\Theta}:\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}
, and consider
\widehat{X}_{\Theta}\in\mathcal{L}(K_{\Theta})
defined by
\widehat{X}_{\Theta}f=X_{\Theta}P_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta}}f
.
The operator modified shift is defined by

S_{\Theta,X_{\Theta}}=S_{\Theta}+(\widehat{X}_{\Theta}-S_{\Theta})P_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta}},

or

S_{\Theta,X_{\Theta}}=S_{\Theta}+P_{\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}}Y_{\Theta}P_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta}},

implies that

S_{\Theta}=S_{\Theta,X_{\Theta}}-P_{\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}}Y_{\Theta}P_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta}}

where
Y_{\Theta}=\widehat{X}_{\Theta}-S_{\Theta}
.

From equation (3.8) of
[
15
]
, there is an operator
J_{\Theta_{1}}\in\mathcal{L}(K_{\Theta_{1}})
such that

(5.5)

P_{\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{1}}}=(I-S_{\Theta_{1}}S_{\Theta_{1}}^{*})J_{\Theta_{1}}=D_{\Theta_{1}}J_{\Theta_{1}}=J_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}D_{\Theta_{1}},

and similarly there is
J_{\Theta_{2}}\in\mathcal{L}(K_{\Theta_{2}})
such that

(5.6)

P_{\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{2}}}=(I-S_{\Theta_{2}}S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*})J_{\Theta_{2}}=D_{\Theta_{2}}J_{\Theta_{2}}=J_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}D_{\Theta_{2}}.

Theorem 5.8
.

[
14
]
Let
{\Theta_{1}},{\Theta_{2}}\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
be two pure inner functions and let
A:K_{\Theta_{1}}\rightarrow K_{\Theta_{2}}
be a bounded operator.
Then
A\in\mathcal{MT}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if

\displaystyle A-S_{\Theta_{2},X_{\Theta_{2}}}AS_{\Theta_{1},X_{\Theta_{1}}}^{*}=BD_{\Theta_{1}}+D_{\Theta_{2}}B^{{}^{\prime}*},

with
B_{1}:\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
and
B_{2}^{\prime}:\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\to K_{\Theta_{1}}
.

6.
Connection between MATTO’s and MATHO’s

Let
J_{1},J_{2}
be conjugations on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\Theta_{1}
is
J_{1}
-symmetric and
\Theta_{2}
is
J_{2}
-symmetric. Then we have the following relations between MATTO’s and MATHO’s.

Proposition 6.1
.

For every
\Phi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
, we have

C_{\Theta_{2}}A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{1}}=A^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}_{J_{2}\Theta_{2}^{*}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}}
.

C_{\Theta_{2}}B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{1}}=B^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}_{J_{2}\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}}
.

{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}=A_{J_{2}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}
.

{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}=B_{J_{2}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}
.

C_{\Theta_{2}}A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}=B_{J_{2}\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\Theta_{2}}
.

{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{1}}=A_{J_{2}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}}^{\Theta_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}
.

Proof.

(a) Let
f\in K_{\Theta_{1}}
and
g\in K_{\Theta_{2}}
and let
J_{1}\Theta_{1}J_{1}=\Theta_{1}^{*}
and
J_{2}\Theta_{2}J_{2}=\Theta_{2}^{*}
. Then

\displaystyle\langle C_{\Theta_{2}}A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{1}}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle C_{\Theta_{2}}g,A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{1}}f\rangle=\langle\Theta_{2}\bar{z}J_{2}g,\Phi\Theta_{1}\bar{z}J_{1}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Theta_{2}J_{2}g,\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}f\rangle=\langle J_{2}g,\Theta_{2}^{*}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle J_{2}\Theta_{2}^{*}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}f,g\rangle=\langle A_{J_{2}\Theta_{2}^{*}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}}f,g\rangle.

(b) Let
f\in K_{\Theta_{1}}
and
g\in K_{\Theta_{2}}
. Then

\displaystyle\langle C_{\Theta_{2}}B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{1}}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle C_{\Theta_{2}}g,B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{1}}f\rangle=\langle C_{\Theta_{2}}g,{\mathbf{J}}(I-P)(\Phi C_{\Theta_{1}}f)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}C_{\Theta_{2}}g,\Phi C_{\Theta_{1}}f\rangle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}(\Theta_{2}\bar{z}J_{2}g),\Phi\Theta_{1}\bar{z}J_{1}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{z}\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})zJ_{2}(g(\bar{z})),\bar{z}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle J_{2}{\mathbf{J}}g,\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})^{*}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}f\rangle=\langle J_{2}\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}f,{\mathbf{J}}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle P_{\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}(I-P)(J_{2}\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}f),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle B_{J_{2}\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}f,g\rangle.

(c) For
f\in K_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}
and
g\in K_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}^{*}_{2}g,A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f\rangle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}^{*}_{2}g,\Phi{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle J_{2}g(\bar{z}),\Phi J_{1}f(\bar{z})\rangle=\langle J_{2}\Phi J_{1}f(\bar{z}),g(\bar{z})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle J_{2}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}f,g\rangle=\langle A_{J_{2}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}f,g\rangle.

(d) Let
f\in K_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}
and
g\in K_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}
. Then

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}g,B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f\rangle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}g,{\mathbf{J}}\Phi{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}g,\Phi{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f\rangle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}{\mathbf{J}}g,\Phi{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}\Phi{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f,{\mathbf{J}}g\rangle=\langle J_{2}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}f(z),{\mathbf{J}}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}(I-P_{+})(J_{2}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}f(z)),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle B_{J_{2}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}f,g\rangle.

(e) For
f\in K_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}
and
g\in K_{\Theta_{2}}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\langle C_{\Theta_{2}}A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle C_{\Theta_{2}}g,A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f\rangle=\langle\Theta_{2}\bar{z}J_{2}g,\Phi{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Theta_{2}{\mathbf{J}}^{*}Jg,\Phi{\mathbf{J}}^{*}f\rangle=\langle f,{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}\Phi^{*}\Theta_{2}\bar{z}J_{2}g(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,J_{1}\Phi(\bar{z})^{*}\Theta_{2}(\bar{z}){z}J_{2}g(\bar{z})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,J_{1}\Phi(\bar{z})^{*}\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})J_{2}(\bar{z}g(\bar{z}))\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,J_{1}\Phi(\bar{z})^{*}\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})J_{2}{\mathbf{J}}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle J_{2}\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}f,\bar{z}g(\bar{z})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle zJ_{2}\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}f,g(\bar{z})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{z}J_{2}\Theta_{2}(z)^{*}\Phi(z)J_{1}f(\bar{z}),g(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}J_{2}\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}f(z),g(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}(I-P_{+})(J_{2}\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}f),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle B_{J_{2}\Theta_{2}(\bar{z})^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\Theta_{2}}f,g\rangle.

(f)
Let
f\in K_{\Theta_{1}}
and
g\in K_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}
. Then

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{1}}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}g,B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{1}}f\rangle=\langle J_{2}g(\bar{z}),{\mathbf{J}}\Phi\Theta_{1}\bar{z}J_{1}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle J_{2}g(\bar{z}),\bar{z}\Phi(\bar{z})\Theta_{1}(\bar{z})zJ_{1}f(\bar{z})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle J_{2}\Phi(\bar{z})\Theta_{1}(\bar{z})J_{1}f(\bar{z}),g(\bar{z})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle J_{2}\Phi(z)\Theta_{1}(z)J_{1}f,g\rangle=\langle P_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}(J_{2}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}f),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{J_{2}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}}^{\Theta_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}f,g\rangle.

∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be bounded linear operators from
K_{\Theta_{1}}
into
K_{\Theta_{2}}
for some inner functions
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
. Then

A\in\mathcal{MT}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if
C_{\Theta_{2}}AC_{\Theta_{1}}\in\mathcal{MT}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
.

B\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if
C_{\Theta_{2}}BC_{\Theta_{1}}\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
.

A\in\mathcal{MT}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if
{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}A{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}\in\mathcal{MT}(\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2})
.

B\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if
{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}\in\mathcal{MH}(\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2})
.

A\in\mathcal{MT}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if
C_{\Theta_{2}}A{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}\in\mathcal{MH}(\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\Theta_{2})
.

B\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if
{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}BC_{\Theta_{1}}\in\mathcal{MT}(\Theta_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2})
.

Proof.

The proof follows from Proposition
6.1
.
∎

The connection between MATTO’s and MATHO’s makes it easy to to transform the results from MATTO’s to MATHO’s. The following proposition is a consequence of this connection.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
be two nonconstant inner functions and let
\Phi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))
. Then
B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}=0
if and only if
\Phi\in J_{2}[H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))]^{*}J_{1}+J_{2}\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))\Theta_{1}J_{1}
.

Proof.

Proposition
6.1
(f)
implies that
B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}=0
if and only if
A_{J_{2}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}}^{\Theta_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}=0
. By
[
13
, Theorem 4.2]
,
A_{J_{2}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}}^{\Theta_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}=0
if and only if

\displaystyle J_{2}\Phi\Theta_{1}J_{1}\in[\Theta_{1}H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))]^{*}+\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\Phi\Theta_{1}\in J_{2}[\Theta_{1}H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))]^{*}J_{1}+J_{2}\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))J_{1}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\Phi\in J_{2}[\Theta_{1}H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))]^{*}J_{1}\Theta_{1}^{*}+J_{2}\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))J_{1}\Theta_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\Phi\in J_{2}[H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))]^{*}\Theta_{1}^{*}\Theta_{1}J_{1}+J_{2}\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))\Theta_{1}J_{1}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\Phi\in J_{2}[H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))]^{*}J_{1}+J_{2}\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}))\Theta_{1}J_{1}.

Hence we complete the proof.
∎

7.
Characterization of MATHO’s

We will use the results of matrix matrix valued asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operators to characterize matrix valued asymmetric Hankel operators. Recall that for the inner function
\Theta
we have
S_{\Theta}=C_{\Theta}S_{\Theta}^{*}C_{\Theta}
and
{\mathbf{J}}^{*}S_{\widetilde{\Theta}}{\mathbf{J}}^{*}=S_{\Theta}
. Furthermore, the defect operators are given by
D_{\Theta}=I-S_{\Theta}S_{\Theta}^{*}
and
\widetilde{D}_{\Theta}=I-S_{\Theta}^{*}S_{\Theta}
.

A simple computation shows that the following relation holds

(7.1)

D_{\Theta}C_{\Theta}=C_{\Theta}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbf{J}}^{*}D_{\widetilde{\Theta}}=D_{\Theta}{\mathbf{J}}^{*}.

The following theorems are generalization of
[
9
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}
be two pure inner functions and let
B:K_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
be a bounded linear operator. Then
B\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if there exist
B_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
and
B_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}:\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\to K_{\Theta_{1}}
such that

(7.2)

B-S_{\Theta_{2}}BS_{\Theta_{1}}=B_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{1}}+D_{\Theta_{2}}B^{{}^{\prime}*}_{2}.

Proof.

Let
B=B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
. By Proposition
6.1
(f) we have

{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}BC_{\Theta_{1}}=A_{J_{2}\Phi\Theta J_{1}}^{\Theta_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}\in\mathcal{MT}(\Theta_{1},\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}).

Therefore by Theorem
5.2
we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}BC_{\Theta_{1}}-S_{\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}BC_{\Theta_{1}}S_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=B_{1}D_{\Theta_{1}}+{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}B_{2}^{*}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle~B-{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}S_{\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}BC_{\Theta_{1}}S_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}C_{\Theta_{1}}={\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B_{1}D_{\Theta_{1}}C_{\Theta_{1}}+{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}B_{2}^{*}C_{\Theta_{1}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle~B-S_{\Theta_{2}}BS_{\Theta_{1}}={\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B_{1}C_{\Theta_{1}}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{1}}+D_{\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B_{2}^{*}C_{\Theta_{1}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle~B-S_{\Theta_{2}}BS_{\Theta_{1}}=B_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{1}}+D_{\Theta_{2}}B_{2}^{{}^{\prime}*}

where
B_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}={\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*}B_{1}C_{\Theta_{1}}
and
B_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}=(C_{\Theta_{1}}B_{2}{\mathbf{J}}_{2}^{*})^{*}
.
∎

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}
be two pure inner functions and let
B:K_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
be a bounded linear operator. Then
B\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if there exist
\widetilde{B}_{1}:\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
and
\widetilde{B}_{2}:\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta_{2}}\to K_{\Theta_{1}}
such that

(7.3)

S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B-BS_{\Theta_{1}}=\widetilde{B}_{1}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{1}}+\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\widetilde{B}^{*}_{2}.

Proof.

Let
B=B_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
. By Theorem
7.1
and using the defect operators
I-\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}=S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}S_{\Theta_{2}}
we have

\displaystyle B-S_{\Theta_{2}}BS_{\Theta_{1}}=B_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{1}}+D_{\Theta_{2}}B_{2}^{{}^{\prime}*}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle~S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B-S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}S_{\Theta_{2}}BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{1}}+S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}D_{\Theta_{2}}B_{2}^{{}^{\prime}*}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle~S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B-(I-\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}})BS_{\Theta_{1}}=S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{1}}+\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B_{2}^{{}^{\prime}*}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle~S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B-BS_{\Theta_{1}}=S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{1}}+\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B_{2}^{{}^{\prime}*}-\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}BS_{\Theta_{1}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle~S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B-BS_{\Theta_{1}}=S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{1}}+\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}(S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B_{2}^{{}^{\prime}*}-BS_{\Theta_{1}})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle~S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B-BS_{\Theta_{1}}=\widetilde{B}_{1}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{1}}+\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\widetilde{B}^{*}_{2}

where
\widetilde{B}_{1}=S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
and
\widetilde{B}^{*}_{2}=(P_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta_{2}}}(S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}B_{2}^{{}^{\prime}*}-BS_{\Theta_{1}}))^{*}:\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta_{2}}\to K_{\Theta_{1}}
.
∎

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}
be two pure inner functions and let
B:K_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
be a bounded linear operator. Then
B\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if there exist
\widehat{B}_{1}:\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
and
\widehat{B}_{2}:\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta_{2}}\to K_{\Theta_{1}}
such that

(7.4)

B-S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=\widehat{B}_{1}D_{\Theta_{1}}+\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}^{*}_{2}.

Proof.

Let
A=A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}\in\mathcal{MT}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
. By Proposition
6.1
(e), we have

C_{\Theta_{2}}A_{\Phi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}=B_{J_{2}\Theta(\bar{z})^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})J_{1}}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\Theta_{2}}=B_{\Psi}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\Theta_{2}}

and so

A_{\Phi}^{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\Theta_{2}}=C_{\Theta_{2}}B_{\Psi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}=C_{\Theta_{2}}B{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}\in\mathcal{MT}(\widetilde{\Theta}_{1},\Theta_{2})

where
B=B_{\Psi}^{\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}}.

Therefore by Theorem
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle C_{\Theta_{2}}B{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}-S_{\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{2}}B{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}S_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}^{*}=B_{1}{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}+D_{\Theta_{2}}B_{2}^{*}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle B-C_{\Theta_{2}}S_{\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{2}}B{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}S_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}^{*}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}=C_{\Theta_{2}}B_{1}{D}_{\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}+C_{\Theta_{2}}D_{\Theta_{2}}B_{2}^{*}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle B-S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=C_{\Theta_{2}}B_{1}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}D_{\Theta_{1}}+\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}C_{\Theta_{2}}B_{2}^{*}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle B-S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=\widehat{B}_{1}D_{\Theta_{1}}+\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}_{2}^{*}

where
\widehat{B}_{1}=C_{\Theta_{2}}B_{1}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
and
\widehat{B}_{2}=(C_{\Theta_{2}}B_{2}^{*}{\mathbf{J}}_{1}^{*})^{*}:\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\Theta_{2}}\to K_{\Theta_{1}}
.
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}
be two pure inner functions and let
B:K_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
be a bounded linear operator. Then
B\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if there exist
\widehat{B}_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}:\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
and
\widehat{B}_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}:\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\to K_{\Theta_{1}}
such that

(7.5)

S_{\Theta_{2}}B-BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=\widehat{B}_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}D_{\Theta_{1}}+D_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}^{{}^{\prime}*}_{2}.

Proof.

By Theorem
7.3
,

B\in\mathcal{MH}(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
if and only if

B-S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=\widehat{B}_{1}D_{\Theta_{1}}+\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}^{*}_{2},

which is equivalent to

S_{\Theta_{2}}B-S_{\Theta_{2}}S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=S_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}_{1}D_{\Theta_{1}}+S_{\Theta_{2}}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}^{*}_{2}.

By the defect operator
D_{\Theta_{2}}=I-S_{\Theta_{2}}S_{\Theta_{2}}^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle S_{\Theta_{2}}B-(I-D_{\Theta_{2}})BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=S_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}_{1}D_{\Theta_{1}}+S_{\Theta_{2}}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}^{*}_{2}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle S_{\Theta_{2}}B-BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=S_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}_{1}D_{\Theta_{1}}+S_{\Theta_{2}}\widetilde{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}^{*}_{2}-D_{\Theta_{2}}BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle S_{\Theta_{2}}B-BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=S_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}_{1}D_{\Theta_{1}}+D_{\Theta_{2}}S_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}^{*}_{2}-D_{\Theta_{2}}BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle S_{\Theta_{2}}B-BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=S_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}_{1}D_{\Theta_{1}}+D_{\Theta_{2}}(S_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}^{*}_{2}-BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle S_{\Theta_{2}}B-BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}=\widehat{B}_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}D_{\Theta_{1}}+D_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}^{{}^{\prime}*}_{2}

where
\widehat{B}_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}=S_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}_{1}:\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{1}}\to K_{\Theta_{2}}
and
\widehat{B}_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}=(P_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}(S_{\Theta_{2}}\widehat{B}^{*}_{2}-BS_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}))^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{\Theta_{2}}\to K_{\Theta_{1}}
.
∎

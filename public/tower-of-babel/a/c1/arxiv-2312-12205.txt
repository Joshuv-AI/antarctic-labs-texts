Global Convergence Analysis of the Power Proximal Point and Augmented Lagrangian Method
Konstantinos A. Oikonomidis, Alexander Bodard, Emanuel Laude, Panagiotis Patrinos

Abstract
In this paper we study an unconventional inexact Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM) for convex optimization problems, as first proposed by Bertsekas, wherein the penalty term is a potentially non-Euclidean norm raised to a power between one and two. We analyze the algorithm through the lens of a nonlinear Proximal Point Method (PPM), as originally introduced by Luque, applied to the dual problem. While Luque analyzes the order of local convergence of the scheme with Euclidean norms our focus is on the non-Euclidean case which prevents us from using standard tools for the analysis such as the nonexpansiveness of the proximal mapping. To allow for errors in the primal update, we derive two implementable stopping criteria under which we analyze both the global and the local convergence rates of the algorithm. More specifically, we show that the method enjoys a fast sublinear global rate in general and a local superlinear rate under suitable growth assumptions. We also highlight that the power ALM can be interpreted as classical ALM with an implicitly defined penalty-parameter schedule, reducing its parameter dependence. Our experiments on a number of relevant problems suggest that for certain powers the method performs similarly to a classical ALM with fine-tuned adaptive penalty rule, despite involving fewer parameters.

1
Introduction

The
Augmented Lagrangian Method
(ALM) introduced in 1969 by Hestenes and Powell
[
11
,
28
]
is a popular algorithm that allows one to cast a difficult nonsmooth or constrained problem into a sequence of smooth ones. Rockafellar showed in the seventies that, in the convex regime, the augmented Lagrangian method can be interpreted in terms of the
Proximal Point Method
(PPM) applied to the Lagrangian dual problem
[
29
]
. However, the classical PPM requires careful selection of the step-sizes to attain fast convergence to a feasible solution
[
17
]
.
In this work we consider a variant of the augmented Lagrangian method for convex optimization problems that is more robust towards suboptimal selection of the step-size sequences. For that purpose we consider a different proximal point method that is obtained by replacing the squared Euclidean proximal term (prox-function) with a higher-power of a potentially non-Euclidean norm. We refer to this variant of the PPM as the
power
PPM. Specializing to the Euclidean norm raised to some power this is an instance of Luque’s nonlinear proximal point method
[
19
,
21
,
18
]
. Its dual counterpart, the nonlinear augmented Lagrangian method was analyzed by the same author
[
20
]
expanding upon the work of Bertsekas
[
3
]
, who studied the algorithm without its connection to the proximal point method.
However, both works are focused on the local convergence of the method only.
More recently, Nesterov rediscovered Luque’s nonlinear proximal point method in the context of tensor methods
[
24
]
, analyzing its fast global sublinear convergence rate, see also
[
2
,
1
]
.
However, the results in
[
24
]
are specialized to Euclidean norms and the error tolerances are incorporated in terms of a tailored relative error bound that is impractical in the context of ALM.

Complementing the results of
[
24
,
20
,
18
]
, we analyze the global convergence rate and the order of local convergence of the power proximal point method and its dual counterpart, the power augmented Lagrangian method (power ALM), under two implementable stopping criteria. Particular emphasis is put on the usage of non-Euclidean norms which goes beyond the existing setup in
[
18
]
, and comes with the challenge of a lack of nonexpansiveness
[
13
]
. As a remedy, we leverage the objective function or its anisotropic Moreau envelope
[
12
]
as merit functions that replace the more classical distance to the solution.

The favorable global rates and the higher order of local convergence is traded off with an increased difficulty of the subproblems which are Hölder continuous in general. In our simulations we therefore utilize a recent family of solvers
[
23
,
15
,
27
]
tailored for (locally) Hölder continous minimization problems besides classical off-the-shelf implementations of (L-)BFGS. Our numerical evaluation shows that the combined scheme behaves favorably when compared to the classical ALM with a fixed penalty parameter while it performs on par with classical ALM with a fine-tuned penalty parameter schedule. This suggests to interpret power ALM as a penalty-parameter-schedule-free augmented Lagrangian method.

More precisely, our contributions are summarized as follows:

•

Complementing Nesterov
[
24
]
we derive fast sublinear rates for the suboptimality gap under more suitable (in the context of the ALM) stopping criteria based on either (1)
\varepsilon
-subgradients or (2)
\varepsilon
-proximal points. A complication arises in the latter case (2) whenever the cost is extended-valued due to infeasibility of iterates and renders the suboptimality gap unsuited for the analysis. This is remedied by interpreting the power PPM as an
anisotropic gradient descent method

[
12
]
applied to the anisotropic Moreau envelope which distinguishes our approach from existing ones. In addition, we show that the suboptimality gap converges locally with higher order under a suitable growth condition.

•

Specializing to the augmented Lagrangian setup we show that our stopping criteria are implementable for the power ALM. In addition we show that the ergodic primal-dual gap converges with the same fast rate as the non-ergodic dual suboptimality. We show that for linear equality constraints this translates to fast global ergodic rates for infeasibility and the primal suboptimality gaps.

•

We compare the performance of the classical and power ALM on a collection of relevant problems using various powers
p
and a collection of inner solvers that perform well on Hölder continuous minimization problems.
We show that for certain choices of
p
the power ALM consistently outperforms its classical counterpart with a fixed penalty parameter in terms of the cumulative number of inner iterations. Moreover, it behaves similarly to classical ALM with a fine-tuned adaptive schedule for the penalty parameter despite involving fewer parameters.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows:

In

\ell_{2}
-norm or the
\ell_{p+1}
-norm raised to the power

. In
Section

, we specialize our results to the augmented Lagrangian setup and analyze the ergodic convergence of the power ALM.

5
presents the numerical evaluation of the method.
Section

6
concludes the paper.

2
Power proximal point and augmented Lagrangian method

This section first introduces the class of problems considered in this work, and formulates both the Langrangian primal and dual problems.
Then, the power augmented Lagrangian method is derived as an instance of the power proximal point method applied to the Lagrangian dual problem, generalizing the Euclidean setup
[
30
]
.
In addition, connections to generalized and sharp Lagrangians
[
31
]
are provided.

2.1
Problem class and duality

In this work we consider convex convex-composite problems that take the form

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big\{\varphi(x)\equiv f(x)+g(\mathcal{A}(x))\big\},

(2.1)

where
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a nonlinear mapping.
In the Rockafellian perturbartion framework for convex duality
[
31
]
the primal problem is embedded in a family of parametric minimization problems of the form
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x,u)
where
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by
F(x,u):=f(x)+g(\mathcal{A}(x)+u)
. Then,
Eq.

2.1
is recovered by the choice
u=0
as
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x,0)
. By dualizing the linear equality constraint
u=0
with a Lagrange multiplier
y
we obtain the convex-concave Lagrangian
L:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
of
Eq.

2.1
:

\displaystyle L(x,y)

\displaystyle=f(x)+\langle A(x),y\rangle-g^{*}(y).

(2.2)

The Lagrange dual function is given as

\displaystyle\varrho(y):=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}L(x,y),

(2.3)

and the Lagrange dual problem can be written as

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}~\varrho(y)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}L(x,y).

(2.4)

For the remainder of this paper we assume that strong duality, i.e., the identity
\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varrho(y)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)
,
holds and primal and dual solutions exist. Sufficient conditions for convexity of
\varphi
and strong duality for nonlinear
\mathcal{A}
in a general conic optimization context are explored in
[
5
,
6
]
. A classical example is when
g
is the indicator function of the nonpositive orthant, and the component-functions of
\mathcal{A}
are convex and smooth, or when
g
is the indicator of a closed, convex cone and
\mathcal{A}
is affine. A classical sufficient condition for strong duality in this case is obtained via Slater’s condition.

2.2
Dual power proximal point

The algorithm that is presented in this paper is obtained by applying a nonlinear extension of the proximal point scheme to the Lagrangian dual problem
Eq.

2.4
.
For notational convenience we introduce

\displaystyle\phi:=\tfrac{1}{p+1}\|\cdot\|_{r}^{p+1},

(2.5)

for some
p\geq 1
, where
\|\cdot\|_{r}
is either the Euclidean norm, i.e.,
r=2
or the
(p+1)
-norm, i.e.,
r={p+1}
. For brevity we omit
r
and write
\|\cdot\|_{s}=\|\cdot\|_{*}
, for
s\in\{2,q+1\}
with
\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}=1
, for the dual norm in the remainder of the manuscript. Note the convex conjugacy relations
\phi^{*}=\tfrac{1}{q+1}\|\cdot\|_{*}^{q+1}
and
\nabla\phi\circ\nabla\phi^{*}=\id
.
We highlight that in applications the
(p+1)
-norm can have certain advantages over the
2
-norm because it leads to a separable
\phi
whereas
\tfrac{1}{p+1}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{p+1}
is nonseparable unless
p=1
.
In addition, we introduce the epi-scaling
\lambda\star\phi
of
\phi
, defined as
(\lambda\star\phi)(x)=\lambda\phi(\lambda^{-1}x)
if
\lambda>0
and
(\lambda\star\phi)(x)=\delta_{\{0\}}(x)
otherwise,
whose convex conjugate amounts to
(\lambda\star\phi)^{*}=\lambda\phi^{*}.

For our specific choice of
\phi
we have
(\lambda\star\phi)(x)=\lambda^{-p}\tfrac{1}{p+1}\|x\|^{p+1}
and
(\lambda\star\phi)^{*}=\lambda\tfrac{1}{q+1}\|\cdot\|_{*}^{q+1}
with
1=\tfrac{1}{q+1}+\tfrac{1}{p+1}
.

The algorithm that is studied in this paper amounts to the proximal point method with prox-term
\phi
and step-size
\lambda
applied to the negative dual function
\psi:=-\varrho
:

\displaystyle y^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})=\argmax_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}~\varrho(y)-\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-y).

(2.6)

Expanding the definition of the Lagrange dual function
\varrho
, the exact version of the algorithm can be rewritten:

\displaystyle(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})

\displaystyle=\argminimax_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}~L(x,y)-\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-y),

(2.7)

where
x^{k}
is the primal variable.
We introduce the corresponding augmented Lagrangian with parameter
\lambda

\displaystyle L_{\lambda}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}L(x,\eta)-\lambda\star\phi(y-\eta).

(2.8)

Note that
-L_{\lambda}(x^{k+1},\cdot)
is the infimal convolution of
-L(x^{k+1},\cdot)
and
\lambda\star\phi
and thus, by
[
12
, Lemma 2.7(ii)]
, its gradient w.r.t.
y
amounts to
-\nabla_{y}L_{\lambda}(x^{k+1},y^{k})=\nabla\phi(\lambda^{-1}(y^{k}-y^{k+1}))
for
y^{k+1}:=\argmax_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\;L(x^{k+1},y)-\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-y)
.
Then
Eq.

2.7
separates into a primal and a consecutive dual update:

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;L_{\lambda}(x,y^{k})

(2.9)

\displaystyle y^{k+1}

\displaystyle=y^{k}+\lambda\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla_{y}L_{\lambda}(y^{k+1},y^{k})).

(2.10)

While the dual update
Eq.

2.10
can typically be solved in closed form given
x^{k+1}
, the primal update
Eq.

2.9
is solved inexactly using an iterative algorithm, e.g., a Quasi-Newton method. Since the dual update depends on
x^{k+1}
the error in the primal update propagates to the dual update. This requires us to analyze the algorithm under a certain error tolerance.
We conclude this section with a simple example for the augmented Lagrangian function.

Example 2.1

(linear equality constraints)
.

Let
g=\delta_{\{0\}}
and
\mathcal{A}
be an affine linear mapping
\mathcal{A}(x)=Ax-b
for matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then
g(\mathcal{A}(x))
encodes the equality constraint
\mathcal{A}(x)=b
and the augmented Lagrangian reads, since
(\lambda\star\phi)^{*}=\lambda\phi^{*}
and
\phi(-x)=\phi(x)
:

\displaystyle L_{\lambda}(x,y)

\displaystyle=f(x)+\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\langle Ax-b,\eta\rangle-\lambda\star\phi(y-\eta)

\displaystyle=f(x)+\langle y,A(x)-b\rangle+\lambda\phi^{*}(Ax-b),

(2.11)

where the penalty
\phi^{*}=\tfrac{1}{q+1}\|\cdot\|_{*}^{q+1}
with
1=\tfrac{1}{q+1}+\tfrac{1}{p+1}
.
The gradient of the augmented Lagrangian w.r.t.
y
amounts to
\nabla_{y}L_{\lambda}(x,y)=Ax-b
and hence the dual update comes out as

\displaystyle y^{k+1}=y^{k}+\lambda\nabla\phi^{*}(Ax^{k+1}-b).

(2.12)

Assume that
\lambda\star\phi=\tfrac{\lambda}{p+1}\|\lambda^{-1}(\cdot)\|^{p+1}
. Then for
p\to\infty
,
\lambda\star\phi
converges (in the pointwise sense) to the indicator function
\delta_{\lambda B_{\|\cdot\|}}
of the unit ball corresponding to the norm
\|\cdot\|
scaled with radius
\lambda
. This limit case can be interpreted in terms of a trust-region constraint with radius
\lambda
in the proximal point scheme
Eq.

2.6
.
Dually, since
\phi^{*}=\tfrac{1}{q+1}\|\cdot\|_{*}^{q+1}
with
1=\tfrac{1}{q+1}+\tfrac{1}{p+1}
,
q+1\to 1
and hence
\lambda\phi^{*}
converges pointwisely to the dual norm
\lambda\|\cdot\|_{*}
scaled with
\lambda
.
In this limit case, the function
\phi^{*}
is a so-called exact penalty
[
4
]
, which guarantees that for
\lambda
sufficiently large the constraints are feasible at the minimizer of the augmented Lagrangian for a certain multiplier
y^{0}
. Furthermore, in this case the minimizer of
L_{\lambda}(\cdot,y^{0})
coincides with the minimizer of the original constrained problem
Eq.

2.1
; see
[
31
, Definition 11.60]
. This reflects the dual perspective in which proximal point with a trust-region constraint converges in a single step to the solution if
y^{0}
is close to
y^{*}\in\argmax\varrho
and
\lambda
is taken sufficiently large.

3
Convergence analysis of the power proximal point method under inexactness

In this section we analyze the global and local convergence rates of the power proximal point method under certain error tolerances when applied to the problem of minimizing a convex, proper lsc function
\psi\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\psi(y).

(3.1)

The results provided in this section are independent of the usecase of the augmented Lagrangian method.
In particular we show that the algorithm converges globally with a fast sublinear rate and transitions locally to fast superlinear convergence if
\psi
satisfies a mild growth condition.

Classically, the convergence of the proximal point method is derived using the nonexpansiveness of the proximal mapping which guarantees that the distance to a solution is nonincreasing. This is still valid for the power proximal point method if the prox-function is a higher power of the Euclidean norm
[
19
]
. However, in this work our focus is on non-Euclidean norms which lack any form of nonexpansiveness. Hence, the distance to the solution cannot be used to show convergence. Instead we rely on the cost
\psi
or its anisotropic Moreau envelope
[
12
]
as merit functions which are in fact the quantities of interest in minimization problems. Specifically, the fast rates are derived relative to the suboptimality gaps.

3.1
Fast global convergence under
\varepsilon
-subgradients

In this subsection we analyze the global complexity of the power proximal point method under a certain error tolerance.
Note that by first-order optimality the update
y^{k+1}=\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})
can be expressed in terms of finding a pair
(v^{k},y^{k+1})
that satisfies the following nonlinear system of inclusions,

\displaystyle v^{k}

\displaystyle\in\partial\psi(y^{k+1})

(3.2a)

\displaystyle y^{k+1}

\displaystyle=y^{k}-\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k}).

(3.2b)

To incorporate error tolerances we replace
\partial\psi(y^{k+1})
with the
\varepsilon_{k}
-subdifferential of
\psi
at
y^{k+1}
, which is defined as follows.
For
\varepsilon>0
the vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
\varepsilon
-subgradient of
\psi
at
\bar{y}\in\dom\psi
if the following inequality holds true:

\displaystyle\psi(y)\geq\psi(\bar{y})+\langle v,y-\bar{y}\rangle-\varepsilon,

(3.3)

for all
x\in\dom\psi
. The set of all
\varepsilon
-subgradients at
\bar{y}
is called the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential of
\psi
at
\bar{y}
and is denoted by
\partial_{\varepsilon}\psi(\bar{y})
. The algorithm that is analyzed in this section is listed in
Algorithm

1
, which possibly incorporates an average with an anchor point
y^{0}
.

Algorithm 1

Inexact power proximal point method with averaging

0:

Choose
y^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and a sequence
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with
\theta_{k}\in(0,1]
.

for all

k=0,1,\dots

do

Set
w^{k}=\theta_{k}y^{k}+(1-\theta_{k})y^{0}
.

Choose
\varepsilon_{k}\geq 0
and solve

\displaystyle v^{k}

\displaystyle\in\partial_{\varepsilon_{k}}\psi(y^{k+1})

(3.4a)

\displaystyle y^{k+1}

\displaystyle=w^{k}-\lambda\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k}).

(3.4b)

end

for

Since
\partial\psi(\bar{y})\subseteq\partial_{\varepsilon}\psi(\bar{y})
,
Algorithm

1
is a strict generalization of the exact version.
To facilitate our analysis we need the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

The following result holds for
\phi=\tfrac{1}{p+1}\|\cdot\|^{p+1}
with
p\geq 1
. For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\phi(x)\geq\phi(y)+\langle\nabla\phi(y),x-y\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2^{p-1}}\phi(x-y).

(3.5)

Proof.

In the case
\phi=\tfrac{1}{p+1}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{p+1}
the result follows from
[
25
, Lemma 4.2.3]
. If
\phi=\tfrac{1}{p+1}\|\cdot\|_{p+1}^{p+1}
is chosen, then the result easily follows by considering
\phi
as a separable sum of
\tfrac{1}{p+1}|\cdot|^{p+1}
and then applying
Eq.

3.5
to each summand.
∎

We first prove the following lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
(y^{+},v)
such that
v\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\psi(y^{+})
and
y^{+}=w-\lambda\nabla\phi^{*}(v)
. Then we have for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
:

\displaystyle\psi(y^{+})\leq\psi(y)+\lambda\star\phi(w-y)-\tfrac{1}{2^{p-1}}\lambda\star\phi(y^{+}-y)+\varepsilon

(3.6)

Proof.

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Since
v\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\psi(y^{+})
we have by definition of the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential:

\displaystyle\psi(y)\geq\psi(y^{+})+\langle v,y-y^{+}\rangle-\varepsilon.

Rearranging yields
\psi(y^{+})\leq\psi(y)-\langle v,y-y^{+}\rangle+\varepsilon.

Since
v=\nabla\phi(\lambda^{-1}(w-y^{+}))=\lambda^{-p}\nabla\phi(w-y^{+})
we obtain

\displaystyle\psi(y^{+})\leq\psi(y)+\lambda^{-p}\langle\nabla\phi(w-y^{+}),(w-y)-(w-y^{+})\rangle+\varepsilon.

In light of
Lemma

3.1
we can further bound

\displaystyle\psi(y^{+})\leq\psi(y)+\lambda^{-p}\phi(w-y)-\lambda^{-p}\phi(w-y^{+})-\tfrac{\lambda^{-p}}{2^{p-1}}\phi(y^{+}-y)+\varepsilon.

(3.7)

Neglecting
-\lambda^{-p}\phi(w-y^{+})
we obtain the claimed result.
∎

We define the sequences of coefficients
\{A_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
and
\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
with
A_{0}:=0
,
A_{k}:=k^{p+1}
and
a_{k+1}:=A_{k+1}-A_{k}
for
k\geq 1
and for the remainder of this subsection we assume that there exists a
y^{\star}\in\argmin\psi
. Using
Lemma

3.1
, we can describe the global convergence of
Algorithm

1
for different choices of the sequence
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. On the one hand, by choosing
\theta_{k}:=\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}
, we obtain a scheme in which the update is given in terms of a convex combination of the proximal point and an anchor point. In this case the convergence proof closely follows
[
8
, Theorem 5]
for the inexact tensor method with averaging.

On the other hand, when using
\theta_{k}:=1
the update depends only on the previous variable. In this setup our analysis is similar to that of
[
8
, Theorem 1]
for the Monotone Inexact Tensor Method I,
[
8

. However, a complication arises due to the fact that the evaluation of
\psi
is not necessarily cheap. For example, in the context of constrained optimization the Lagrangian dual function might not have a simple closed form and as such evaluating it at each iteration might become very expensive.
Consequently, a monotonic decrease can no longer be guaranteed and thus the analysis has to be adapted. For this reason we introduce an enlargement of the diameter of the initial level-set
[
8

Assuming that the sequence of errors
\varepsilon_{k}
is summable we define the constant
\beta:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty
and the diameter of the level-set

\displaystyle D_{\beta}:=\sup\{\|y^{\star}-y\|:\psi(y)\leq\psi(y^{0})+\beta\}.

(3.8)

We assume that
D_{\beta}<\infty
is finite, which happens to be the case when
\psi
is level-bounded, which is equivalent to
0\in\intr\dom\psi^{*}

[
31
, Theorem 11.8(c)]
.
The following theorem describes the global convergence of
Algorithm

1
for both choices of
\theta_{k}
. Note that the derived convergence rate for
\theta_{k}=1
is worse than the one for
\theta_{k}=\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}
since it depends on the diameter of the initial level-set, which can be large. In practice, however, we observed the opposite behavior: The variant with
\theta_{k}=1
performs better throughout our experiments even for
p=1
which corresponds to the classical PPM.

Theorem 3.3
.

Choose the sequence of errors
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
such that
\varepsilon_{k}=\tfrac{c}{(k+1)^{p+1}},

for some
c\geq 0
. Let
y^{\star}\in\argmin\psi
. Then, for the sequence
\{y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by
Algorithm

1
the following statements hold true:

(i)

If
\theta_{k}:=\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}
, then

\psi(y^{k})-\psi(y^{\star})\leq\frac{\lambda^{-p}(p+1)^{p}\|y^{0}-y^{\star}\|^{p+1}+c}{k^{p}}.

(ii)

If
\theta_{k}:=1
, then

\psi(y^{k})-\psi(y^{\star})\leq\frac{\lambda^{-p}(p+1)^{p}D_{\beta}^{p+1}+c}{k^{p}}.

Proof.

Regardless of the choice of
\theta_{k}
, by definition of
Algorithm

1
we have that
v^{k}\in\partial_{\varepsilon_{k}}\psi(y^{k+1})
and
y^{k+1}=w^{k}-\lambda\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k})
.
Define
y:=\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}}y^{\star}+\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}y^{k}
. Invoking
Lemma

3.2
and using the convexity of
\psi
we get:

\displaystyle\psi(y^{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\psi(\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}}y^{\star}+\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}y^{k})+\lambda\star\phi(w^{k}-y)+\varepsilon_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}}\psi(y^{\star})+\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}\psi(y^{k})+\lambda\star\phi(w^{k}-y)+\varepsilon_{k}.

(3.9)

“
3.3(i)
”:
For
\theta_{k}:=\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}
, we have by definition of
y
and
w^{k}
:

\displaystyle w^{k}-y

\displaystyle=\theta_{k}y^{k}+(1-\theta_{k})y^{0}-(1-\theta_{k})y^{\star}-\theta_{k}y^{k}=(1-\theta_{k})(y^{0}-y^{\star}).

Substituting this identity in
Section

3.1
we obtain, since
\lambda\star\phi
is positively homogeneous with order
p+1
, i.e.,
\lambda\star\phi(\alpha x)=|\alpha|^{p+1}(\lambda\star\phi)(x)
and
1-\theta_{k}=\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}}
:

\displaystyle\psi(y^{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}}\psi(y^{\star})+\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}\psi(y^{k})+(\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}})^{p+1}(\lambda\star\phi)(y^{0}-y^{\star})+\varepsilon_{k}.

Rearranging and multiplying both sides with
A_{k+1}>0
yields

\displaystyle A_{k+1}\big(\psi(y^{k+1})-\psi(y^{\star})\big)

\displaystyle\leq A_{k}\big(\psi(y^{k})-\psi(y^{\star})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+A_{k+1}(\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}})^{p+1}(\lambda\star\phi)(y^{0}-y^{\star})+A_{k+1}\varepsilon_{k}.

Summing the inequality from
k=0
to
k=K-1
we obtain by telescoping, since
A_{0}=0
,
A_{k}=k^{p+1}
and
\varepsilon_{k}=\tfrac{c}{A_{k+1}}
:

A_{K}\big(\psi(y^{K})-\psi(y^{\star})\big)\leq\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y^{\star})\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tfrac{a_{k}^{p+1}}{A_{k}^{p}}+Kc.

In light of
[
8
, Equation 35]
we have
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tfrac{a_{k}^{p+1}}{A_{k}^{p}}\leq(p+1)^{p+1}K

and hence

\displaystyle A_{K}\big(\psi(y^{K})-\psi(y^{\star})\big)

\displaystyle\leq(p+1)^{p+1}K(\lambda\star\phi)(y^{0}-y^{\star})+Kc.

Dividing the inequality by
A_{K}
yields:

\psi(y^{K})-\psi(y^{\star})\leq\tfrac{(p+1)^{p+1}(\lambda\star\phi)(y^{0}-y^{\star})}{K^{p}}+\tfrac{c}{K^{p}}=\tfrac{(p+1)^{p+1}(\lambda\star\phi)(y^{0}-y^{\star})+c}{K^{p}}.

“
3.3(ii)
”:
For
\theta_{k}:=1
, we have that
w^{k}=y^{k}
and since
y^{k}-y=\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}}(y^{k}-y^{\star})
using the positive homogeneity of
\phi
with order
p+1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\psi(y^{k+1})\leq\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}}\psi(y^{\star})+\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}\psi(y^{k})+(\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}})^{p+1}(\lambda\star\phi)(y^{k}-y^{\star})+\varepsilon_{k}.

Multiplying both sides with
A_{k+1}
we have since
a_{k+1}=A_{k+1}-A_{k}

\displaystyle A_{k+1}\big(\psi(y^{k+1})-\psi(y^{\star})\big)\leq A_{k}\big(\psi(y^{k})-\psi(y^{\star})\big)+\tfrac{a_{k+1}^{p+1}}{A_{k+1}^{p}}(\lambda\star\phi)(y^{k}-y^{\star})+\varepsilon_{k}A_{k+1}.

Summing the inequality from
k=0
to
k=K-1
we obtain since
A_{0}=0
:

\displaystyle A_{K}\big(\psi(y^{K})-\psi(y^{\star})\big)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\tfrac{a_{k+1}^{p+1}}{A_{k+1}^{p}}(\lambda\star\phi)(y^{k}-y^{\star})+\varepsilon_{k}A_{k+1}.

(3.10)

From
Lemma

3.2
for
w=y=y^{k}
we have
\psi(y^{k+1})\leq\psi(y^{k})+\varepsilon_{k}.

Summing the inequality from
k=0
to
k=K-1
we obtain
\psi(y^{K})-\psi(y^{0})\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\varepsilon_{k}\leq\beta,

and hence
\|y^{K}-y^{\star}\|\leq D_{\beta}
for any
K\geq 0
. This implies that
\phi(y^{k}-y^{\star})=\tfrac{1}{p+1}\|y^{\star}-y^{k}\|^{p+1}\leq\tfrac{1}{p+1}D_{\beta}^{p+1}
.
Thus we can further bound
Eq.

3.10
:

\displaystyle A_{K}\big(\psi(y^{K})-\psi(y^{\star})\big)\leq\lambda^{-p}\tfrac{1}{p+1}D_{\beta}^{p+1}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tfrac{a_{k}^{p+1}}{A_{k}^{p}}+\varepsilon_{k}A_{k+1}.

We choose
A_{K}=K^{p+1}
and by using the fact that
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tfrac{a_{k}^{p+1}}{A_{k}^{p}}\leq(p+1)^{p+1}K
we get:

\displaystyle A_{K}\big(\psi(y^{K})-\psi(y^{\star})\big)\leq\lambda^{-p}D_{\beta}^{p+1}(p+1)^{p}K+cK.

Dividing by
A_{K}
we obtain
\psi(y^{K})-\psi(y^{\star})\leq\tfrac{\lambda^{-p}D_{\beta}^{p+1}(p+1)^{p}+c}{K^{p}}.

∎

3.2
Fast global convergence under
\varepsilon
-proximal points

The algorithm studied in the previous section incorporates error tolerances via the notion of
\varepsilon
-subgradients and relies on the cost function
\psi
as a merit function which is potentially extended-valued. As a consequence, the next iterate has to stay within the domain of
\psi
which can be restrictive in practice. In this section we therefore consider a more relaxed error tolerance that requires the next iterate
y^{k+1}
to stay close to the true proximal point
\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})
, referred to as an
\varepsilon
-proximal point of
\psi
at
y^{k}
:

\displaystyle\lambda\star\phi(y^{k+1}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))\leq\varepsilon_{k},

(3.11)

where
\varepsilon_{k}\geq 0
and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty
.
Note that this error criterion generalizes the classical inexactness conditions studied in
[
17
]
.
For that purpose we leverage the real-valued and smooth anisotropic Moreau envelope
[
14
,
12
]
as a merit function and interpret the power proximal point method in terms of the anisotropic gradient descent method
[
12
]
applied to the merit function.

We define the left anisotropic Moreau envelope adapted from
[
12
, Definition 3.7]
which is given as the value function of the power proximal point update
Eq.

2.6
: For
\lambda>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
the
left anisotropic Moreau envelope
or in short anisotropic envelope of
\psi
with parameter
\lambda
at
y
is defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y):=\inf_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\{\psi(\eta)+\lambda\star\phi(y-\eta)\}.

(3.12)

Thanks to
[
12
, Lemma 3.9(ii)]
we have that

\displaystyle\nabla\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})=\nabla\phi(\lambda^{-1}(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))).

(3.13)

Rearranging the identity reveals that the exact proximal point scheme
Eq.

2.6
is equivalent to the anisotropic gradient descent method
[
12
, Algorithm 1]
applied to the anisotropic envelope of
\psi
, since:

\displaystyle y^{k+1}=\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})=y^{k}-\lambda\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})).

(3.14)

To prove the main convergence result of this subsection we exploit
[
12
, Proposition 4.1]
which reveals that the anisotropic Moreau envelope
\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}
has the anisotropic descent property
[
12
, Definition 3.1]
with reference function
\phi
:

Definition 3.4
.

Let
h\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\phi=\tfrac{1}{p+1}\|\cdot\|^{p+1}
. We say that
h
satisfies the anisotropic descent property relative to
\phi
with constant
L
if for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:adescent}h(y)\leq h(\eta)+\tfrac{1}{L}\star\phi(y-\eta+L^{-1}\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla h(\eta)))-\tfrac{1}{L}\star\phi(L^{-1}\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla h(\eta))).

(3.15)

Note that whenever
p=1
, and
\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{2}
we obtain the classical Euclidean descent lemma by expanding the square.
Based on the anisotropic descent inequality above we can prove the following theorem:

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\{y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be any sequence that satisfies
Eq.

3.11
. Then the following statments hold true:

(i)

\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k+1})-\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})\leq-\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))+\varepsilon_{k}.

(ii)

If
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is summable with
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}\leq\alpha
, for
\alpha\geq 0
, and the set of solutions
Y^{\star}=\argmin\psi
is nonempty, then
\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|\to 0.

(iii)

If
Y^{\star}
is bounded, then
\{y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
remains bounded and all of its limit points lie in
Y^{\star}
. Moreover, if
\varepsilon_{k}=\tfrac{c}{(k+1)^{p+1}}
for some
c\geq 0
, then for all
k\geq 1

\displaystyle\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})-\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{\star})\leq\tfrac{\lambda^{-p}D_{\beta}^{p+1}(p^{2}+p)^{p}+c}{k^{p}}.

(3.16)

Proof.

“
3.5(i)
”:
By
[
12
, Proposition 4.1]
,
\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)
is anisotropically smooth relative to
\phi
with constant
\tfrac{1}{\lambda}
. Hence the anisotropic descent inequality
Eq.

3.15
at points
y=y^{k+1}
and
\eta=y^{k}
yields via the relation
Eq.

3.13
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})+\lambda\star\phi\big(y^{k+1}-y^{k}+\lambda\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(\lambda^{-1}(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))))\big)

\displaystyle\qquad-\lambda\star\phi\big(\lambda\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(\lambda^{-1}(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))))\big)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})+\lambda\star\phi(y^{k+1}-y^{k}+y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))-\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})+\varepsilon_{k}-\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})),

(3.17)

where the last inequality follows by the error bound (
3.11
).

“
3.5(ii)
”: Since
\argmin\phi=\{0\}
, we have that
\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{\star})=\psi(y^{\star})
and as such that
\psi^{\star}\leq\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Thus by summing
Section

3.2
form
k=0
to
K
, we get:

\displaystyle-\infty

\displaystyle<\psi(y^{\star})-\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{0})\leq\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{K+1})-\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{0})

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{k=0}^{K}\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))+\sum_{k=0}^{K}\varepsilon_{k}\leq-\sum_{k=0}^{K}\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))+\alpha,

where the last inequality follows by the assumed bound on
\varepsilon_{k}
.
This result readily implies that
\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))
is summable and therefore

\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))\to 0.

Moreover, from
Eq.

3.11
we have that

0\leq\lambda\star\phi(y^{k+1}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))\leq\varepsilon_{k}\to 0.

Recall that
\lambda\star\phi=\lambda^{-p}\frac{1}{p+1}\|\cdot\|^{p+1}
.
Hence, we can combine these two convergence results to show the claimed relationship, using the triangle inequality:

\|y^{k}-y^{k+1}\|\leq\|y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})\|+\|y^{k+1}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})\|\to 0.

“
3.5(iii)
”:
Regarding the boundedness of the iterates we follow the proof of
3.3(ii)
. We have that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k+1})-\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{0})\leq\alpha.

We define the diameter of the enlarged initial level-set of the envelope function:

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}:=\sup\{\|y^{\star}-y\|:\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)\leq\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{0})+\alpha\}.

(3.18)

Since
\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}
is convex and
Y^{\star}
is bounded,
D_{\alpha}
is also bounded. Therefore,
\|y^{k}-y^{\star}\|\leq D_{\alpha}
and this implies that
\{y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
remains bounded. The subsequential convergence to
Y^{\star}
follows from the fact that
\nabla\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})\to 0
.

Now, we can turn our attention to the convergence rate of the anisotropic envelope of
\psi
. Using the convexity inequality, we get:

\displaystyle\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)\geq\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})+\langle\nabla\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}),y-y^{k}\rangle

Rearranging and substituting
Eq.

3.13
, we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)-\lambda^{-p}\langle\nabla\phi(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})),y-y^{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)+\lambda^{-p}(\tfrac{1}{\sigma}\|\nabla\phi(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))\|_{*})(\sigma\|y-y^{k}\|),

for any
\sigma>0
, where the last inequality follows by Hölder’s inequality. We can further bound the r.h.s. of the previous inequality, by using Young’s inequality with exponents
q+1
and
p+1
, the fact that
\|\nabla\phi(y)\|_{*}=\|y\|^{p}
and
p(q+1)=p+1
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})\leq\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)+(\tfrac{1}{\sigma})^{q+1}\tfrac{\lambda^{-p}}{q+1}\|y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})\|^{p+1}+\sigma^{p+1}\tfrac{\lambda^{-p}}{p+1}\|y-y^{k}\|^{p+1}.

Let us choose
\sigma=(\tfrac{p+1}{q+1})^{\tfrac{1}{q+1}}=p^{\tfrac{1}{q+1}}
. Then we have since
(\tfrac{1}{\sigma})^{q+1}\tfrac{1}{q+1}=\frac{1}{p+1}
and
\sigma^{p+1}=p^{p}
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})\leq\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)+\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))+p^{p}\lambda\star\phi(y-y^{k}),

and by putting this result back in
Section

3.2
we obtain:

\displaystyle\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k+1})\leq\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)+\varepsilon_{k}+p^{p}\lambda\star\phi(y-y^{k}).

(3.19)

The rest of the proof is similar to that of
3.3(ii)
. More specifically, if we choose
y:=\tfrac{a_{k+1}y^{\star}+A_{k}y^{k}}{A_{k+1}}
in
Eq.

3.19
and use the convexity inequality as well as the fact that
\phi
is positively homogeneous with order
p+1
, we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k+1})\leq\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}}\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{\star})+\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})+\varepsilon_{k}+\tfrac{a_{k+1}^{p+1}}{A_{k+1}^{p+1}}p^{p}\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-y^{\star})

Multiplying the inequality above with
A_{k+1}>0
, rearranging and summing from
k=0
to
k=K-1
, we get:

\displaystyle A_{K}\big(\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{K})-\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{\star})\big)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\tfrac{a_{k+1}^{p+1}}{A_{k+1}^{p}}p^{p}\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-y^{\star})+\varepsilon_{k}A_{k+1}.

(3.20)

If we choose
A_{K}=K^{p+1}
and use the fact that
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tfrac{a_{k}^{p+1}}{A_{k}^{p}}\leq(p+1)^{p+1}K
we have that

A_{K}\big(\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k})-\operatorname{env}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{\star})\big)\leq p^{p}D_{\alpha}^{p+1}(p+1)^{p}K+cK,

since
\|y^{k}-y^{\star}\|\leq D_{\alpha}
. Dividing now with
A_{K}
, we obtain the claimed result.
∎

3.3
Local convergence with higher order under function growth conditions

In this subsection we establish the higher order of local convergence of the power proximal point scheme under a mild growth condition of the objective
\psi
.

In the following, let
Y^{\star}
denote the set of minimizers of
\psi
, which we assume is nonempty and compact, and
\psi^{\star}
be the minimum value of
\psi
. Let
d(y,Y^{\star}):=\inf_{\eta\in Y^{\star}}\|y-\eta\|_{r}
for
r\in\{2,p+1\}
denote the distance of
y
to
Y^{\star}
. For brevity we again omit
r
.
The following result captures the order and rate of convergence of the algorithm with exact updates. A similar result is provided in
[
3
, Proposition 5.20]
for the Augmented Lagrangian method.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\{y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be the sequence of iterates generated by
Algorithm

1
for
\theta_{k}=1
,
\varepsilon_{k}=0
. Moreover assume that
Y^{\star}
is compact and there exists a
\delta
-neighborhood
\mathcal{N}(Y^{\star},\delta):=\{y:d(y,Y^{\star})<\delta\}
of
Y^{\star}
such that
\psi(y)-\psi^{\star}\geq\mu d^{\nu}(y,Y^{\star})
, with
\nu>1
, for all
y\in\mathcal{N}(Y^{\star},\delta)
. Let
p>\nu-1
. Then,
\{d(y^{k},Y^{\star})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
converges locally with order
\omega:=\tfrac{p}{\nu-1}>1
and rate
\alpha:=(\frac{1}{\lambda\mu^{q}})^{\omega}
, i.e., for
k
sufficiently large we have

\displaystyle\frac{d(y^{k+1},Y^{\star})}{d^{\omega}(y^{k},Y^{\star})}\leq\alpha.

(3.21)

Proof.

For a vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we denote by
y_{\star}
its unique projection on
Y^{\star}
, i.e.
y_{\star}=\argmin_{\eta\in Y^{\star}}\|y-\eta\|
. First, let us remark that since we have assumed the exact execution of the algorithm, the results of
Theorem

3.5
hold. More specifically, since
Y^{\star}
is assumed compact, every subsequence of
\{y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
converges to some point in
Y^{\star}
and thus
d(y^{k},Y^{\star})\to 0
. Therefore, there exists a
K\geq 0
such that for all
k\geq K
,
y^{k+1}\in\mathcal{N}(Y^{\star},\delta)
. Hence we have that

\mu d^{\nu}(y^{k+1},Y^{\star})\leq\psi(y^{k+1})-\psi^{\star}
. By
Eq.

3.2
we have that
-\lambda^{-p}\nabla\phi(y^{k+1}-y^{k})\in\partial\psi(y^{k+1})
and hence using the convexity inequality we obtain that
\psi^{\star}\geq\psi(y^{k+1})-\lambda^{-p}\langle\nabla\phi(y^{k+1}-y^{k}),y^{k+1}_{\star}-y^{k+1}\rangle
. Thus we have:

\displaystyle\mu d^{\nu}(y^{k+1},Y^{\star})

\displaystyle\leq-\psi^{\star}+\psi(y^{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-p}\langle\nabla\phi(y^{k+1}-y^{k}),y^{k+1}_{\star}-y^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-p}\|\nabla\phi(y^{k+1}-y^{k})\|_{*}\|y^{k+1}-y^{k+1}_{\star}\|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-p}\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{p}d(y^{k+1},Y^{\star}),

(3.22)

where the third inequality follows from Hölder’s inequality and the equality follows from the fact that
\|\nabla\phi(x)\|_{*}=\|x\|^{p}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Now, for the update of the algorithm we have that
y^{k+1}=\argmin_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\{\psi(y)+\lambda\star\phi(y-y^{k})\}
and hence:

\displaystyle\lambda\star\phi(y^{k+1}-y^{k})\leq\psi(y)-\psi(y^{k+1})+\lambda\star\phi(y^{k}-y),\qquad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Thus, by choosing
y=y^{k}_{\star}\in Y^{\star}
, since
\psi^{\star}-\psi(y^{k+1})\leq 0
we obtain since
\lambda\star\phi=\lambda^{-p}\|\cdot\|^{p+1}
:

\displaystyle\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|\leq\|y^{k}-y^{k}_{\star}\|=d(y^{k},Y^{\star}).

substituting the inequality above in
Eq.

3.22
, we get:

\displaystyle d^{\nu-1}(y^{k+1},Y^{\star})\leq\tfrac{1}{\lambda^{p}\mu}d^{p}(y^{k},Y^{\star})

and by taking both sides to the power of
\frac{1}{\nu-1}
we obtain the desired result noting that
\tfrac{1}{\lambda^{p}\mu}^{\frac{1}{\nu-1}}=(\tfrac{1}{\lambda\mu^{q}})^{\omega}
and
\frac{1}{p}=q
.
∎

Although
Theorem

3.6
describes the local convergence of the exact version of the algorithm, the extension to the inexact case is straightforward due to the equivalence of the norms in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. More specifically, one could follow the analysis of
[
19
]
and further specify the inexactness condition
Eq.

3.11
as follows:

\|y^{k+1}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y^{k}))\|\leq\varepsilon_{k}\min\{1,\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{t}\},

for some
t>0
and thus obtain an order of convergence of at least
\min\{t,\tfrac{1}{q(\nu-1)}\}
. It is important to note that for
\phi=\tfrac{1}{p+1}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{p+1}
as in
[
19
]
, it is not necessary to assume that
Y^{\star}
is compact. To the best of our knowledge, however, such a condition cannot be avoided when dealing with
(p+1)
-norms.

4
The inexact power augmented Lagrangian method

In this section we specialize the results of the inexact power proximal point method to the augmented Lagrangian setting as described in
Section

2
. In particular, we provide implementable stopping criteria for the primal update
x^{k+1}
that guarantee that the dual update
y^{k+1}
is within the error tolerance of the inexact power proximal point method when applied to the Lagrangian dual function. Furthermore, we show that the ergodic primal-dual gap vanishes at the same rate as the dual suboptimality gap established in the previous section and provide global convergence results for important quantities in the constrained optimization framework.

4.1
Inexactness criteria

Recall that
\psi:=-\varrho
is the negative Lagrangian dual function
\varrho
which is defined in
Eq.

2.3
.
This subsection describes stopping criteria for the primal update

\displaystyle x^{+}\approx\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}L_{\lambda}(x,y),

(4.1)

which guarantee that the corresponding dual update

\displaystyle y^{+}=y+\lambda\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla_{y}L_{\lambda}(x^{+},y)).

(4.2)

is within the error tolerance for the inexact power proximal point scheme applied to
\psi
.
For that purpose we prove the following result:

Proposition 4.1
.

The following properties hold for
x^{+}
and
y^{+}
:

(i)

Let
x^{+}
such that
L(x^{+},y^{+})\leq\inf L(\cdot,y^{+})+\varepsilon
. Then
-\nabla_{y}L_{\lambda}(x^{+},y)\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\psi(y^{+})
.

(ii)

Let
x^{+}
such that
L_{\lambda}(x^{+},y)\leq\inf L_{\lambda}(\cdot,y)+\varepsilon
. Then
y^{+}
is an
2^{p-1}\varepsilon
-proximal point, i.e.,

\lambda\star\phi(y^{+}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\phi}(y))\leq 2^{p-1}\varepsilon.

(iii)

Assume that
\dom f
is bounded. Let
e\in\partial_{x}L_{\lambda}(x^{+},y)
such that
\|e\|\leq\varepsilon/D
, where
D:=\sup_{x,y\in\dom f}\|x-y\|<\infty
is the diameter of
\dom f
. Then the hypotheses of the first and second item are valid.

A proof of this result is included in
Section

A.1
.

4.2
Convergence of the primal-dual gap

In this subsection we refine the analysis from the previous section in the primal-dual setup. Specifically, we show that the primal-dual gap decreases at the same rate as the dual suboptimality-gap; see
Theorem

3.3
. For that purpose we define for any
K\geq 1
the ergodic primal iterate:

\displaystyle\breve{x}^{K}=\tfrac{\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}x^{k}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}}.

(4.4)

In the following theorem we show that the ergodic primal-dual gap decreases at the same rate as the negative dual function.
The proof is
along the lines of the analysis in
[
32
]
, albeit adapted to the higher-order proximal-point setting where in general nonexpansiveness is not present and as such standard techniques are not directly applicable.

Theorem 4.2
.

Choose the sequence of errors
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
such that
\varepsilon_{k}=\tfrac{c}{(k+1)^{p+1}},

for some
c\geq 0
. Let
(x^{k},y^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be the sequence of iterates generated by
Algorithm

2
with
\theta_{k}:=\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}
and termination criterion
L(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})\leq\inf L(\cdot,y^{k+1})+\varepsilon_{k}
.
Then we have for all
K\geq 1
and any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
:

\displaystyle L(\breve{x}^{K},y)-L(x,y^{K})\leq\tfrac{c+\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y)(p+1)^{p+1}}{K^{p}}.

(4.5)

Proof.

Choose any
w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We have by the
y
-update
Eq.

2.10
that
v^{k}=\lambda^{-p}\nabla\phi(w^{k}-y^{k+1})=\nabla\phi(\lambda^{-1}(w^{k}-y^{k+1}))\in\partial_{y}(-L)(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})
and hence via concavity of
L(x^{k+1},\cdot)
:

\displaystyle L(x^{k+1},w)\leq L(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})+\lambda^{-p}\langle\nabla\phi(w^{k}-y^{k+1}),(w^{k}-w)-(w^{k}-y^{k+1})\rangle.

(4.6)

By uniform convexity of
\phi
invoking
Lemma

3.1
we can further bound:

\displaystyle L(x^{k+1},w)

\displaystyle\leq L(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})+\lambda^{-p}\phi(w^{k}-w)-\lambda^{-p}\phi(w^{k}-y^{k+1})-\lambda^{-p}\tfrac{1}{2^{p}}\phi(y^{k+1}-w)

\displaystyle\leq L(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})+\lambda^{-p}\phi(w^{k}-w).

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Plugging
w:=(1-\theta_{k})y+\theta_{k}y^{k}
in the identity above we obtain by concavity of
L(x^{k+1},\cdot)
, using
w^{k}-w=\theta_{k}y^{k}+(1-\theta_{k})y^{0}-(1-\theta_{k})y-\theta_{k}y^{k}=(1-\theta_{k})(y^{0}-y)
and the homgeneity of
\phi
with order
p+1
:

\displaystyle(1-\theta_{k})L(x^{k+1},y)+\theta_{k}L(x^{k+1},y^{k})

\displaystyle\leq L(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})+\lambda^{-p}(1-\theta_{k})^{p+1}\phi(y^{0}-y).

Since
\theta_{k}=\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}
, and
1-\theta_{k}=\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}}
we obtain:

\displaystyle\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}}L(x^{k+1},y)+\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}L(x^{k+1},y^{k})

\displaystyle\leq L(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})+\lambda^{-p}(\tfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}})^{p+1}\phi(y^{0}-y).

Multiplication with
A_{k+1}
yields:

\displaystyle a_{k+1}L(x^{k+1},y)+A_{k}L(x^{k+1},y^{k})

\displaystyle\leq A_{k+1}L(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})+\lambda^{-p}\tfrac{a_{k+1}^{p+1}}{A_{k+1}^{p}}\phi(y^{0}-y).

Using the relation
a_{k+1}=A_{K+1}-A_{k}
the inequality can be rewritten:

\displaystyle A_{k+1}\big(L(x^{k+1},y)-L(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})\big)

\displaystyle\leq A_{k}\big(L(x^{k+1},y)-L(x^{k+1},y^{k})\big)+\lambda^{-p}\tfrac{a_{k+1}^{p+1}}{A_{k+1}^{p}}\phi(y^{0}-y).

(4.7)

Thanks to the error bound for the
x
-update in
Algorithm

2
and using the convention
\varepsilon_{-1}:=L(x^{0},y^{0})-\inf L(\cdot,y^{0})
we have that for all
k\geq 0
,
L(x^{k},y^{k})\leq\inf L(\cdot,y^{k})+\varepsilon_{k-1}\leq L(x^{k+1},y^{k})+\varepsilon_{k-1}.

This yields

\displaystyle L(x^{k+1},y)-L(x^{k},y)+L(x^{k},y)-L(x^{k},y^{k})+\varepsilon_{k-1}\geq L(x^{k+1},y)-L(x^{k+1},y^{k}).

(4.8)

Plugging this inequality in
Eq.

4.7
we obtain:

\displaystyle A_{k+1}\big(L(x^{k+1},y)-L(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})\big)

\displaystyle\leq A_{k}\big(L(x^{k},y)-L(x^{k},y^{k})\big)+A_{k}\big(L(x^{k+1},y)-L(x^{k},y)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+A_{k}\varepsilon_{k-1}+\tfrac{a_{k+1}^{p+1}}{A_{k+1}^{p}}(\lambda\star\phi)(y^{0}-y).

Summing the inequality from
k=0
to
k=K-1
we obtain since
A_{0}=0
using the fact that
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tfrac{a_{k}^{p+1}}{A_{k}^{p}}\leq(p+1)^{p+1}K
:

\displaystyle A_{K}L(x^{K},y)-A_{k}L(x^{K},y^{K})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}A_{k}\big(L(x^{k+1},y)-L(x^{k},y)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k=0}^{K-2}A_{k+1}\varepsilon_{k}+\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y)(p+1)^{p+1}K.

By reordering the sum using
a_{k+1}=A_{k+1}-A_{k}
and
A_{0}=0
we have the identity:

\displaystyle A_{K}L(x^{K},y)-\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}A_{k}\big(L(x^{k+1},y)-L(x^{k},y)\big)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}L(x^{k},y).

This allows us to rewrite the previous inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}L(x^{k},y)-A_{K}L(x^{K},y^{K})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-2}A_{k+1}\varepsilon_{k}+\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y)(p+1)^{p+1}K.

(4.9)

By convexity of
L(\cdot,y)
since
A_{K}=\sum_{j=1}^{K}a_{j}
we have:

\displaystyle A_{K}L(\breve{x}^{K},y)\leq A_{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tfrac{a_{k}}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}a_{j}}L(x^{k},y)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}L(x^{k},y).

(4.10)

Plugging this relation in
Eq.

4.9
we can lower bound:

\displaystyle A_{K}\big(L(\breve{x}^{K},y)-L(x^{K},y^{K})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-2}A_{k+1}\varepsilon_{k}+\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y)(p+1)^{p+1}K.

By the
x
-update we have for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
L(x^{K},y^{K})\leq\inf L(\cdot,y^{K})+\varepsilon_{K-1}\leq L(x,y^{K})+\varepsilon_{K-1}

and hence we can lower bound since
A_{k+1}\varepsilon_{k}=c

\displaystyle A_{K}\big(L(\breve{x}^{K},y)-L(x,y^{K})\big)

\displaystyle\leq cK+\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y)(p+1)^{p+1}K.

Dividing by
A_{k}
we obtain
L(\breve{x}^{K},y)-L(x,y^{K})\leq\tfrac{c+\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y)(p+1)^{p+1}}{K^{p}}
as claimed.
∎

Note that this result is not specific to the Lagrangian function as it holds for any convex-concave function, where the inexact higher-order proximal point method is applied to the concave part.

4.3
Convergence for problems with linear equality constraints

In this subsection we revisit
Example

2.1
specializing to the problem of minimizing a convex function
f
subject to linear equality constraints. First, we provide results for the ergodic iterates, harnessing the analysis of the primal-dual gap that is presented in
Theorem

4.2
.

Theorem 4.3
.

In the situation of
Example

2.1
let
\{\breve{x}^{K}\}_{K\in\mathbb{N}}
be the ergodic primal iterates of
Algorithm

2
as defined in (
4.4
) with
\theta_{k}=\tfrac{A_{k}}{A_{k+1}}
,
\varepsilon_{k}=\tfrac{c}{(k+1)^{p+1}}
for some
c\geq 0
and termination criterion
L(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})\leq\inf L(\cdot,y^{k+1})+\varepsilon_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then, the following inequality holds for
K\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\max\{\|A\breve{x}^{K}-b\|_{*},|f(\breve{x}^{K})-f(x^{\star})|\}\leq\tfrac{c+\max_{y\in\delta\mathcal{B}}\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y)(p+1)^{p+1}}{K^{p}},

(4.11)

where
\delta=2\|y^{\star}\|+1
,
\mathcal{B}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\|y\|\leq 1\}
denotes the closed unit ball, and
(x^{\star},y^{\star})
is a saddle-point of the problem.

Proof.

From
Theorem

4.2
we have that for
K\geq 1
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

\displaystyle L(\breve{x}^{K},y)-L(x,y^{K})\leq\tfrac{c+\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y)(p+1)^{p+1}}{K^{p}}

and since for
x=x^{\star}
,
L(\breve{x}^{K},y)-L(x^{\star},y^{K})=f(\breve{x}^{K})-f(x^{\star})+\langle A\breve{x}^{K}-b,y\rangle
we obtain the following inequality for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
:

\displaystyle f(\breve{x}^{K})-f(x^{\star})+\langle A\breve{x}^{K}-b,y\rangle\leq\tfrac{c+\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y)(p+1)^{p+1}}{K^{p}}.

(4.12)

By taking
y=0
in the inequality above we obtain

\displaystyle f(\breve{x}^{K})-f(x^{\star})\leq\tfrac{c+\lambda\star\phi(y^{0})(p+1)^{p+1}}{K^{p}}.

(4.13)

Now, let
\delta=2\|y^{\star}\|+1
and take the maximum in (
4.12
) w.r.t.
y\in\delta\mathcal{B}
:

\displaystyle f(\breve{x}^{K})-f(x^{\star})+\delta\|A\breve{x}^{K}-b\|_{*}\leq\tfrac{c+\max_{y\in\delta\mathcal{B}}\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y)(p+1)^{p+1}}{K^{p}}.

(4.14)

Since
(x^{\star},y^{\star})
is a saddle point, it holds that
L(x^{\star},y)\leq L(x^{\star},y^{\star})\leq L(x,y^{\star})
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and as such
L(x,y^{\star})-L(x^{\star},y)\geq 0
. Choose
x=\breve{x}^{K}
and
y=0
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq L(\breve{x}^{K},y^{\star})-L(x^{\star},0)

\displaystyle=f(\breve{x}^{K})-f(x^{\star})+\langle A\breve{x}^{K}-b,y^{\star}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq f(\breve{x}^{K})-f(x^{\star})+\|y^{\star}\|\|A\breve{x}^{K}-b\|_{*},

(4.15)

where the last inequality follows by applying Hölder’s inequality. Summing now (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), we get since
\delta>\|y^{\star}\|

\displaystyle\|A\breve{x}^{K}-b\|_{*}\leq(\delta-\|y^{\star}\|)\|A\breve{x}^{K}-b\|_{*}\leq\tfrac{c+\max_{y\in\delta\mathcal{B}}\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y)(p+1)^{p+1}}{K^{p}}.

(4.16)

From the inequality above together with (
4.15
) we obtain:

\displaystyle f(x^{\star})-f(\breve{x}^{K})\leq\|y^{\star}\|\|A\breve{x}^{K}-b\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq(\delta-\|y^{\star}\|)\|A\breve{x}^{K}-b\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{c+\max_{y\in\delta\mathcal{B}}\lambda\star\phi(y^{0}-y)(p+1)^{p+1}}{K^{p}},

(4.17)

which along with (
4.13
) implies the claimed result.
∎

.

In the situation of

let

be the primal iterates of

2
with
\theta_{k}=1
,
\varepsilon_{k}=\tfrac{c}{(k+1)^{p+1}}
and termination criterion
L(x^{k+1},y^{k+1})\leq\inf L(\cdot,y^{k+1})+\varepsilon_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then, the following hold for
k\geq 1
:

(i)

\|Ax^{k}-b\|_{*}=\mathcal{O}\bigg(k^{-\tfrac{p^{2}}{p+1}}\bigg)

4.4
Solution of the subproblems of the power augmented Lagrangian method

Again we specialize to the minimization of a convex cost subject to linear equality constraints as in
Example

2.1
.
Although the convergence results of the power ALM in terms of outer iterations seem quite attractive when using
p>1
, the inner subproblems become harder to solve.
Notice that when choosing
\phi=\tfrac{1}{p+1}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{p+1}
the term
\|Ax-b\|_{2}^{q+1}
in
Example

2.1
is Hölder smooth of order
q
. In general, such a term slows down the convergence of first-order methods as shown in
[
9
]
. More specifically, if
f
is an
L
-smooth function, the augmented Lagrangian
Example

2.1
as a function in
x
is comprised of functions with varying order of Hölder smoothness. This stands in contrast to the classical ALM, where the augmented Lagrangian is Lipschitz smooth and hence classical first-order methods such as the accelerated proximal gradient method can be applied. Nevertheless, again from
[
9
]
we know that the inexact version of this algorithm, specifically the one presented in
[
23
]
, can be used to minimize
Example

2.1
as well. Moreover in a recent line of work, adaptive proximal gradient methods were introduced that can also tackle composite convex locally Hölder smooth problems
[
27
,
15
]
.

4.5
Interpretation as an implicit adaptive penalty scheme

Since power ALM,
Algorithm

2
with
\theta_{k}=1
, can be interpreted as a proximal point method applied to the negative dual function (
2.6
), the optimality conditions of each multiplier update yield, for
\phi=\frac{1}{p+1}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{p+1}
,

\displaystyle 0\in\partial\psi(y^{k+1})+\lambda^{-p}\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{p-1}(y^{k+1}-y^{k})

(4.18)

For classical ALM with
p=1
, this becomes
0\in\partial\psi(y^{k+1})+\lambda^{-1}(y^{k+1}-y^{k})
,
such that power ALM with
2
-norms can be interpreted as classical ALM with an adaptive penalty scheme that is implicitly defined by

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:adaptive_{p}enalty_{2}norm}\lambda_{k}:=\lambda^{p}\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{1-p}.

(4.19)

In the case of
p
-norms, a similar interpretation holds, but the scalar penalty
\lambda_{k}
becomes an adaptive diagonal penalty matrix
\Lambda_{k}
implicitly defined by

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:adaptive_{p}enalty_{p}norm}\left[\Lambda_{k}\right]_{j,j}=\lambda^{p}\lvert y_{j}^{k+1}-y_{j}^{k}\rvert^{1-p},\quad\forall j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}.

(4.20)

6
Conclusion

In this paper we have analyzed the inexact power proximal point method and the corresponding Augmented Lagrangian scheme under two different absolute inexactness conditions. In order to incorporate a more relaxed error tolerance, we have also studied the algorithm as an inexact anisotropic gradient descent method. Under this framework, we have generalized the results of
[
3
]
for the proximal point method which were specialized to the Augmented Lagrangian setting. Moreover, in the Augmented Lagrangian setting we showed ways to enforce the proposed error conditions in practice, while also providing the corresponding global rates for the primal-dual gap and the measure of infeasibility.
We also provide an interpretation of the power Augmented Lagrangian method as a classical ALM equipped with an implicit adaptive penalty rule.
Finally, our experiments illustrate that for certain powers the power ALM performs similarly to a fine-tuned classical ALM with adaptive penalty scheme, despite requiring fewer parameters and less tuning.

Proof.

“
4.1(i)
”:
Since
y^{+}\in\argmin_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\lambda\star\phi(y-\eta)-L(x^{+},\cdot)
we have that

v=\nabla\phi(\lambda^{-1}(y-y^{+}))\in\partial_{y}(-L)(x^{+},y^{+}).

By concavity of
L(x,\cdot)
we have that for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

\langle v,y^{+}-y\rangle\geq L(x^{+},y)-L(x^{+},y^{+})\geq\varrho(y)-L(x^{+},y^{+}),

where the second inequality follows by the definition of the dual function
\varrho
. Since
L(x^{+},y^{+})\leq\inf L(\cdot,y^{+})+\varepsilon
we can further bound

\langle v,y^{+}-y\rangle\geq\varrho(y)-\varrho(y^{+})-\varepsilon.

Rearranging this yields

(-\varrho)(y)\geq(-\varrho)(y^{+})+\langle v,y-y^{+}\rangle-\varepsilon,

for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and hence
v\in\partial_{\varepsilon}(-\varrho)(y^{+})
, i.e.
v\in\partial_{\varepsilon}\psi(y^{+})
.

“
4.1(ii)
”: Using the concavity of the augmented Lagrangian function
L_{\lambda}(x^{k+1},\cdot)
, we get

\displaystyle L_{\lambda}(x^{+},y)+\langle\nabla_{y}L_{\lambda}(x^{+},y),\eta-y\rangle

\displaystyle\geq L_{\lambda}(x^{+},\eta)\geq\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}L_{\lambda}(x,\eta)

\displaystyle=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}~L(x,y)-\lambda\star\phi(\eta-y)

\displaystyle=\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}~L(x,y)-\lambda\star\phi(\eta-y)

\displaystyle=\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varrho(y)-\lambda\star\phi(\eta-y)

\displaystyle\geq\varrho(\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y))-\lambda\star\phi(\eta-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)),

(A.1)

where we have used the definitions of the Lagrangian dual function, the properties of
\inf
and
\max
and strong duality.
The same reasoning also yields:

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}L_{\lambda}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}~L(x,\eta)-\lambda\star\phi(y-\eta)=\max_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varrho(\eta)-\lambda\star\phi(y-\eta)

\displaystyle=\varrho(\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y))-\lambda\star\phi(y-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)).

(A.2)

Now, from the optimality condition in
Eq.

4.2
, we have that
\lambda^{-p}\nabla\phi(y-y^{+})=\nabla_{y}L_{\lambda}(x^{+},y)
. Therefore, by combining
Eq.

A.1
and
Eq.

A.2
, we obtain the following result:

\displaystyle L_{\lambda}(x^{+},y)-\inf_{x}L_{\lambda}(x,y)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\lambda\star\phi(y-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y))-\lambda\star\phi(\eta-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y))+\lambda^{-p}\langle\nabla\phi(y-y^{+}),\eta-y\rangle,

which holds for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
so that we can replace
\eta
with its maximum. This is attained if
\lambda^{-p}\nabla\phi(\eta-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y))=\lambda^{-p}\nabla\phi(y-y^{+})
, i.e.,
\eta=y-y^{+}+\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)
.
Substituting this result in the inequality above, we finally arrive at the claimed inequality

\displaystyle L_{\lambda}(x^{+},y)-\inf_{x}L_{\lambda}(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\star\phi(y-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y))-\lambda\star\phi(y-y^{+})

\displaystyle\qquad-\lambda^{-p}\langle\nabla\phi(y-y^{+}),y-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)-(y-y^{+})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{-p}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{p-1}\tfrac{1}{p+1}\|y^{+}-\operatorname{prox}_{\psi}^{\lambda\star\phi}(y)\|^{p+1},

where the final inequality follows from
Lemma

3.1
.

“
4.1(iii)
”: We have that
e\in\partial_{x}L(x^{+},y^{+})
and
0\in\partial_{y}(-L)(x^{+},y^{+})-\nabla\phi(\lambda^{-1}(y-y^{+}))
if and only if

(x^{+},y^{+})\in\argminimax_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}~L(x,\eta)-\langle e,x\rangle-\lambda\star\phi(y-\eta),

and hence

\displaystyle e\in\partial_{x}L(x^{+},y^{+})\Leftrightarrow x^{+}\in\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}~L_{\lambda}(x,y)-\langle e,x\rangle\Leftrightarrow e\in\partial_{x}L_{\lambda}(x^{+},y).

(A.3)

By convexity of
L(\cdot,y^{+})
we have for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
that

L(x,y^{+})\geq L(x^{+},y^{+})+\langle e,x-x^{+}\rangle,

and hence

\inf L(\cdot,y^{+})\geq L(x^{+},y^{+})-\|e\|\cdot\inf_{x\in\dom f}\|x-x^{+}\|

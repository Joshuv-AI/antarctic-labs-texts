Theory and Application of the Fractional-order Delta Function Associated with the Inverse Laplace Transform of the Mittag-Leffler Function
Anis Allagui, Ahmed S. Elwakil

Abstract
This paper is devoted to the study of the M M -Wright function ( M α ​ ( t ) M_{\alpha}(t) ) which is the inverse Laplace transform of the single-parameter Mittag-Leffler (ML) function ( E α ​ ( − s ) E_{\alpha}(-s) ). Because E α ​ ( − s ) E_{\alpha}(-s) can be viewed as the fractional-order generalization of the exponential function for 0 < α < 1 0<\alpha<1 , to which it reduces for α = 1 \alpha=1 , i.e. E 1 ​ ( − s ) = exp ⁡ ( − s ) E_{1}(-s)=\exp(-s) , its inverse Laplace transform, being M α ​ ( t ) M_{\alpha}(t) , can be viewed as a generalized fractional-order Dirac delta function. At the limiting case of α = 1 \alpha=1 the M M -Wright function reduces to M 1 ​ ( t ) = δ ⁡ ( t − 1 ) M_{1}(t)=\delta(t-1) . We investigate numerically the behavior of this fractional-order delta function as well as it integral, the fractional-order unit-step function. Subsequently, we validate our results with experimental data for the charging of a supercapacitive device. Keywords: Dirac Delta function, Mittag-Leffler function, M M -Wright function, Fractional calculus



I
Introduction

The Dirac delta function or impulse function
\delta(t)
is a generalized function that has a wide range of applications in various fields of sciences and engineering
Zemanian 1987
. Conceptually, it represents a function singular at a point on the real axis and zero elsewhere, and the integral of any continuous function when multiplied by the Dirac delta function gives the value of the function at that specific point.
Its generalization may provide a convenient way to work around its singular nature, which is the main purpose of this work.

Our analysis in this work originates from the fact that the delayed Dirac delta function
\delta(t-1)
is the inverse Laplace transform of the exponential decaying function
e^{-s}
.
From there, a fractional generalization of the delta function associated with the inverse Laplace transform of the Mittag-Leffler (ML) function
E_{\alpha}(-s)
arises in terms of the
M
-Wright function
M_{\alpha}(t)

Mainardi 2022
;
Mainardi and Pagnini 2003
;
Mainardi, Mura, and Pagnini 2010
, given that the ML function can be viewed as a generalization of the exponential function
Haubold, Mathai, and Saxena 2011
.
A schematic illustration of the Laplace transform pair
\delta(t-1)
and
e^{-s}
, and its (fractional) generalization
M_{\alpha}(t)
and
E_{\alpha}(-s)
is depicted in Fig.
1
, and summarizes the principal result of this work.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section
II
we present an overview of the standard delta function and its main properties, the ML function and its inverse Laplace transform, as well the representation of these functions in terms of the Fox
H
-function. We also provide parametric graphical plots showing how the
M
-Wright function tends to the standard delta Dirac function. In section
III
we study theoretically and verify experimentally (on a supercapacitor) a simple example of a fractional-order differential equation involving the
M
-Wright function used to charge this capacitive device in the form of an applied current. This resembles finding the impulse response of the device using a fractional-order delta function.

II
Theory

First, we start by setting the basic definitions and notations to be used throughout the paper. The Laplace transform of a sufficiently well-behaved function
f(t)\;(t\in\mathbb{R}^{+})
is defined by:

\mathcal{L}[f(t);s]=F(s)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}f(t)dt,\;\;s\in\mathbb{C}

(1)

and the inverse Laplace transform of
F(s)
is defined by:

\mathcal{L}^{-1}[F(s);t]=f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\gamma-i\infty}^{\gamma+i\infty}e^{st}F(s)ds,\;\;\mathcal{R}e(s)=\gamma

(2)

The conditions of validity and the main rules are given in any textbook on advanced mathematics
Harris 2014
.
By using the sign
\div
to denote the juxtaposition of the function
f(t)
with its Laplace transform
F(s)
, a Laplace transform pair is represented by:

f(t)\div F(s)

(3)

II.1
The Dirac delta function and the Laplace transform pair
\delta(t-1)\div e^{-s}

If we consider the function
F(s)
in
3
to be

F(s)=e^{-s}
, where

e^{z}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

(4)

then we have the Laplace transform pair (Fig.
1
):

\delta(t-1)\div e^{-s}

(5)

The (one-dimensional) Dirac delta function
\delta(t)
is known to obey several fundamental properties. For
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
being a well-behaved function we have
Hoskins and Hoskins 2009
:

\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\delta(t-a)dt=f(a)

(6)

and if
f(t)=1
for
\forall t\in\mathbb{R}
then we have the normalization condition:

\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(t)dt=1

(7)

Also, if we chose the function
f(t)=f(0)e^{ikt}
we obtain the Fourier transform of
\delta(t)
as:

\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(t)e^{ikt}dt=1

(8)

and by inversion we obtain the following representation of the delta function:

\delta(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-ikt}dk

(9)

The delta function can also be expressed as the derivative of the Heaviside unit step function (denoted as
H(t)
,
\theta(t)
or
u(t)
), i.e.:

\delta(t)=\frac{du(t)}{dt}

(10)

where
Hoskins and Hoskins 2009

u(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{t}{|t|}\right)

(11)

Finally, convolving
\delta(t)
with a continuous function
g(t)
gives:

(\delta*g)(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\delta(\tau)g(t-\tau)d\tau=g(t)

(12)

and by virtue of the Laplace convolution theorem:

\mathcal{L}[\delta*g]=\mathcal{L}[\delta]\mathcal{L}[g]=\mathcal{L}[g]

(13)

The delta function is the ”identity” with respect to the convolution integral.

II.2
The fractional delta function and the Laplace transform pair
M_{\alpha}(t)\div E_{\alpha}(-s)

Now we consider the single-parameter (or standard) Mittag-Leffler (ML) function denoted by
E_{\alpha}(z)
with
z\in\mathbb{C},\;\alpha>0
, which is defined by (i) the power series representation
Mainardi 2022
:

E_{\alpha}(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha n+1)}

(14)

where the Gamma function is given by
\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\xi}\xi^{z-1}d\xi
, or (ii) by the integral representation
Mainardi 2022
:

E_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{Ha}\frac{\xi^{\alpha-1}e^{\xi}}{\xi^{\alpha}-z}d\xi

(15)

where
Ha
indicates the Hankel path of integration.
The ML function is a direct (fractional) generalization of the exponential series (Eq.
4
)
Mainardi, Mura, and Pagnini 2010
; for
0<\alpha<1
it interpolates between
E_{1}(z)=\exp(z)
and
E_{0}(z)=1/(1-z)
.
A detailed account of its basic properties can be found for instance in the third volume of Batemann Manuscript Project editted by Erdélyi et al.
Erdélyi et al. 1955
.
The ML function has been generalized to other forms with two
Wiman 1905
, three
Prabhakar 1971
and four
Shukla and Prajapati 2007
parameters but we focus here on its single-parameter version.
The ML and related functions usually appear naturally in the solution of fractional order integro-differential equations describing anomalous relaxation, kinetics, diffusion and reaction
Mathai and Haubold 2008
;
Haubold, Mathai, and Saxena 2011
.
From Mainardi
Mainardi 2022
[Appendix F, p. 237],
the inverse Laplace transform of
E_{\alpha}(-s)
for
0<\alpha<1
is given in terms of the Wright function
Mainardi 2022
as:

W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t)\div E_{\alpha}(-s)

(16)

The (classical) Wright function
W_{\lambda,\mu}(z)
with
\lambda>-1,\;\mu\in\mathbb{C}
is defined by the series representation
Mainardi 2022
;
Mainardi 1997
:

W_{\lambda,\mu}(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n!\,\Gamma(\lambda n+\mu)}

(17)

or by the integral:

W_{\lambda,\mu}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{Ha}e^{\xi+z\xi^{-\lambda}}\xi^{-\mu}{d\xi}

(18)

We note that for
0<\alpha<1
an auxiliary function of the Wright type is defined as
Mainardi and Tomirotti 1997
:

W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t)=M_{\alpha}(t)

(19)

where
M_{\alpha}(t)
is usually referred to as the
M
-Wright function or the Mainardi function
Balescu 2007
;
Kiryakova 2010
.
Its series and integral representations are
Mainardi, Mura, and Pagnini 2010
:

M_{\alpha}(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-z)^{n}}{n!\,\Gamma(-\alpha n+(1-\alpha))}

(20)

and

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{Ha}e^{\xi-z\xi^{\alpha}}\xi^{\alpha-1}{d\xi}

(21)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha z}\int_{Ha}e^{\xi-z\xi^{\alpha}}{d\xi}

(22)

respectively. The relationship of the
M
-Wright and ML functions through the Laplace transform can be easily followed for instance from their integral representations, i.e.
Mainardi, Mura, and Pagnini 2010
:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}M_{\alpha}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\left[\int_{Ha}e^{\xi-t\xi^{\alpha}}\xi^{\alpha-1}{d\xi}\right]dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{Ha}e^{\xi}\xi^{\alpha-1}\left[\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(s+\xi^{\alpha})t}dt\right]d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{Ha}\frac{e^{\xi}\xi^{\alpha-1}}{\xi^{\alpha}+s}d\xi=E_{\alpha}(-s)

(23)

Like the ML function, the (classical) Wright function has also been extended to the generalized Wright function defining
a series generalizing the hypergeometric series.
Erdélyi et al. 1953
;
Kilbas et al. 2003
;
El-Shahed and Salem 2015
. They are known to play fundamental roles in various applications of fractional calculus
Luchko 2019
. In particular, like the ML function is viewed as the natural (fractional) generalization of the exponential function, the Mainardi
M_{\alpha}(t)
function can be viewed as a natural (fractional) generalization of the Gaussian function and of the Airy function given that
Mainardi, Mura, and Pagnini 2010
;
Mainardi and Pagnini 2003
:

\displaystyle M_{0}(t)=e^{-t}

(24)

\displaystyle M_{1/3}(t)=3^{2/3}\text{Ai}({-t}/{3^{1/3}})

(25)

\displaystyle M_{1/2}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-t^{2}/4}

(26)

It has also been suggested as the generalized hyper-Airy function
Mainardi 2022
;
Mainardi, Mura, and Pagnini 2010
.

1
shows a schematic representation of
\delta(t-1)\div e^{-s}
given in

, and their generalization
M_{\alpha}(t)\div E_{\alpha}(-s)

II.3
Representation in terms of the Fox
H
-function

The exponential function, the ML function and the Wright (or
M
-Wright) function can all be represented in terms of the Fox
H
-function as
Mathai, Saxena, and Haubold 2009
:

e^{-z}=H^{1,0}_{0,1}\left[z|^{\;\;-}_{(0,1)}\right]

(27)

E_{\alpha}(-z)=H^{1,1}_{1,2}\left[z|^{(0,1)}_{(0,1),(0,\alpha)}\right]

(28)

and
Mainardi and Pagnini 2007
;
Luchko 2019

W_{\lambda,\mu}(z)=\begin{cases}H^{1,0}_{0,2}\left[-z\left|\begin{array}[]{c}-,-\\
(0,1),(1-\mu,\lambda)\\
\end{array}\right.\right],&0<\lambda\\
\\
H^{1,0}_{1,1}\left[-z\left|\begin{array}[]{c}-,(\mu,-\lambda)\\
(0,1),-\\
\end{array}\right.\right],&-1<\lambda<0\\
\\
\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mu)}H^{1,0}_{0,1}\left[-z\left|\begin{array}[]{c}-\\
(0,1)\\
\end{array}\right.\right],&\lambda=0\end{cases}

(29)

respectively.
The Fox’s
H
-function
Fox 1961
is defined in terms of Mellin-Barnes type integral as
Mathai, Saxena, and Haubold 2009
:

\displaystyle H^{m,n}_{p,q}(z)

\displaystyle=H^{m,n}_{p,q}\left[z|^{(a_{p},A_{p})}_{(b_{q},B_{q})}\right]

\displaystyle=H^{m,n}_{p,q}\left[z|^{(a_{1},A_{1}),\ldots,(a_{p},A_{p})}_{(b_{1},B_{1}),\ldots,(b_{q},B_{q})}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{L}h(s)z^{-s}ds

(30)

with
h(s)
given by the ratio of products of Gamma functions:

h(s)=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(b_{j}+B_{j}s)\;\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(1-a_{j}-A_{j}s)}{\prod_{j={n+1}}^{p}\Gamma(a_{j}+A_{j}s)\;\prod_{j={m+1}}^{q}\Gamma(1-b_{j}-B_{j}s)}

(31)

m,n,p,q
are integers satisfying (
0\leqslant n\leqslant p
,
1\leqslant m\leqslant q
),

z\neq 0
, and
z^{-s}=\exp\left[-s(\ln|z|+i\arg z)\right]
,
A_{i},B_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
with
(i=1,2,\ldots,p)
,
(j=1,2,\ldots,q)
.
The contour of integration
L
is a suitable contour separating the poles
-(b_{j}+\nu)/B_{j}
, (
j=1,\ldots,m
;
\nu=0,1,2,\ldots
), of the gamma functions
\Gamma(b_{j}+B_{j}s)
from the poles
(1-a_{\lambda}+k)/A_{\lambda}
, (
\lambda=1,\ldots,n
;
k=0,1,2,\ldots
) of the gamma functions
\Gamma(1-a_{\lambda}-A_{\lambda}s)
, that is
A_{\lambda}(b_{j}+\nu)\neq B_{j}(a_{\lambda-k-1})
.
An empty product in
31
, if it occurs, is taken to be one.
The
H
-function contains a vast number of elementary and special functions as special cases.
Detailed and comprehensive accounts of the matter are available in Mathai, Saxena, and Haubold
Mathai, Saxena, and Haubold 2009
, Mathai and Saxena
Mathai et al. 1978
, and Kilbas and Saigo
Saigo, Kilbas et al. 2004

III
Application

III.1
Fractional-order impulse response

In this section we give a simple example for the application of the generalization
\delta(t-1)\div e^{-s}
to
M_{\alpha}(t)\div E_{\alpha}(-s)
.
We consider the case of a first-order differential equation with initial value as follows:

\frac{dx(t)}{dt}=k\,\delta(t-1),\;\;x(0)=0

(34)

which can represent for example the dynamics of an ideal capacitor in response to an impulse current excitation. The solution for
x(t)
(assuming
k=1
) is given in terms of the step function as:

x(t)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[s^{-1}{e^{-s}};t\right]=u(t-1)

(35)

We rewrite Eq.
34
wherein (i) the first-order derivative
dx/dt
is replaced with
{}^{C}_{0}D_{t}^{\beta}x(t)
where
{}^{C}_{0}D_{t}^{\beta}
is the Caputo differential operator defined as:

{}^{C}_{0}D_{t}^{\beta}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(m-\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{m-\beta-1}f^{(m)}(t)dt

(36)

where
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m-1<\beta\leqslant m
, and (ii) the right-hand side is replaced with the
M
-Wright function
M_{\alpha}(t)
. The fractional derivative in the Caputo sense is frequently encountered in certain boundary value problems arising in the theory of viscoelasticity, hereditary solid mechanics, and supercapacitor modeling.
We now have:

{}^{C}_{0}D_{t}^{\beta}x(t)=k\,M_{\alpha}(t),\;\;x(0)=0

(37)

This equation can describe the dynamics of a non-ideal fractional-order capacitor (electric double-layer capacitors or supercapacitors) known to exhibit distributed time constants or frequency dispersion of capacitance
Allagui and Fouda 2021
;
Allagui, Elwakil, and Fouda 2021
;
Allagui, Zhang, and Elwakil 2018
. It can also be used to model the transient photovoltage/photocurrent in solar cells in response to fast and
small perturbation of incident light
Zhang et al. 2019
.
Applying the Laplace transform to both sides of Eq.
37
(with
k=1
), knowing that:

\mathcal{L}\left[{}^{C}_{0}D_{t}^{\beta}f(t);s\right]=s^{\beta}F(s)-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m-1}s^{\beta-k-1}f^{(k)}(0^{+}),

(38)

gives:

X(s)=s^{-\beta}E_{\alpha}(-s)

(39)

Then
x(t)
is found by inverse Laplace transform of Eq.
39
using formula 2.21 in Mathai, Saxena, and Haubold
Mathai, Saxena, and Haubold 2009
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}

\displaystyle\left[s^{-\rho}H_{p,q}^{m,n}\left[as^{\sigma}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}(a_{p},A_{p})\\
(b_{q},B_{q})\\
\end{array}\right.\right];t\right]

\displaystyle=t^{\rho-1}H_{p+1,q}^{m,n}\left[at^{-\sigma}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}(a_{p},A_{p}),(\rho,\sigma)\\
(b_{q},B_{q})\\
\end{array}\right.\right]

which gives:

x(t)=t^{\beta-1}H_{2,2}^{1,1}\left[t^{-1}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}(0,1),(\beta,1)\\
(0,1),(0,\alpha)\\
\end{array}\right.\right]

(44)

For
\beta=1
and
\alpha=1
,
x(t)
reduces to the Heaviside step function

x(t)=u(1-t)
.

Plots of Eq.

for

and different values of

for computational reasons. Otherwise, it is clear from

IV
Conclusion

In this paper we studied the fractional generalization of the Laplace transform pair
\delta(t-1)\div e^{-s}
giving
M_{\alpha}(t)\div E_{\alpha}(-s)
with focus on the behavior and application of (i) the fractional-order delta function
M_{\alpha}(t)
and (ii) its integral giving the fractional-order unit step function.
Both functions are crucial for the evaluation of the impulse response and step response of circuits and systems. We have experimentally demonstrated the current charging of a supercapacitor using the fractional delta function, and hence its fractional impulse response obtained in the form of the measured voltage. The results fully agree with the numerical modeling of the device.
The results may find many other applications in problems associated with differential and integral equations of fractional order arising in different disciplines of physical sciences and engineering.

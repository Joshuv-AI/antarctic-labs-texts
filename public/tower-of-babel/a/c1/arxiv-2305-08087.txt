Abstract
. We describe a N = 2 N=2 supersymmetric Poisson vertex algebra structure of N = 1 N=1 (resp. N = 0 N=0 ) classical W W -algebra associated with 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( n + 1 | n ) \mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n) and the odd (resp. even) principal nilpotent element. This N = 2 N=2 supersymmetric structure is connected to the principal 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 | 1 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2|1) -embedding in 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( n + 1 | n ) \mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n) superalgebras, which are the only basic Lie superalgebras that admit such a principal embedding.



1.
Introduction

Supersymmetric (SUSY) vertex algebras were introduced by Kac
[
28
]
,
generalizing the original idea of Borcherds
[
9
]
for (bosonic) vertex algebras.
Afterwards, SUSY vertex algebras have been studied in various aspects, see e.g.
[
3
,
24
,
5
]
, and
their structure theory was developed by Kac and Heluani
[
25
]
.
SUSY vertex algebras correspond to a mathematical
definition of the chiral part of two-dimensional (super)conformal field theories studied by physicists,
since the years
80’s up to nowadays, see e.g.
[
4
,
42
,
30
,
22
,
10
,
43
,
19
,
35
,
10
]
and references therein for the historical physics literature on the subject.
Their structure of SUSY vertex algebras includes a superfield formalism, commonly used in physics, for instance in
superstrings theories
[
11
,
23
]
or super-Yang-Mills models
[
20
,
6
,
16
,
7
]
.

The affine Lie algebras and the
W
-algebras are at the core of the study of these SUSY theories, see e.g.
[
1
,
14
,
32
,
27
]
. However, although supersymmetric vertex algebras
may have as many supersymmetries as one wishes,
it is mainly the case
N=1
that is studied in the context of affine Lie algebras
[
29
]
and
W
-algebras
[
33
,
40
]
.
Affine Lie algebras, and
W
-algebras are studied both at the quantum level (using vertex algebra and
SUSY vertex algebra formalism) and at the classical level (with Poisson vertex algebras and SUSY Poisson
vertex algebras).

Quantum and classical SUSY W-algebras can be understood as Hamiltonian reductions of
N=1
SUSY affine vertex algebras in the quantum case or SUSY Poisson affine vertex algebras in the classical case.
As a method of
N=1
SUSY Hamiltonian reduction, BRST formalism was introduced
and this formalism gives rise to
N=1
SUSY quantum W-algebras
[
32
,
33
]
.
On the other hand, SUSY classical W-algebras are obtained as a quasi classical limit of SUSY quantum W-algebras
[
40
]
.
A
N=1
SUSY classical (resp. quantum) W-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\bar{\mathfrak{g}},f)
(resp.
W^{k}(\bar{\mathfrak{g}},f)
) of level
k
is defined when a basic Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
and an odd nilpotent element
f
in an
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
are given.
Especially, for classical cases, their Poisson structures are well understood
[
40
]
and when the given odd nilpotent element
f
is principal, the explicit forms of their generators can be found by a column determinant of certain operator valued matrices
[
38
]
.

The
N=2
SUSY
W
-algebras have been used since a long time in physics. They represent a significant extension of the already rich N=2 superconformal symmetry. The algebraic structure of
N=2
superconformal algebra is particularly fitted to the study of strings and superstrings. Indeed, after a well-suited twist of the super conformal generators, it can be shown
[
21
]
that the
N=2
superconformal algebra encode the BRST algebra for the bosonic string. In that approach, the BRST algebra contains, in addition to the stress energy tensor, the BRST operator, together with a ghost and an anti-ghost operators. Extension of this method to the bosonic
W
-strings leads naturally to the
N=2
SUSY
W
-algebras
[
8
]
, which represent a significant extension of the already rich
N=2
superconformal symmetry. These algebras also appear as the Hamiltonian structure of the
N=2
Korteweg–de Vries or of the
N=2

Kadomtsev–Petriashvili hierarchies
[
31
,
13
]
. These various lines of research have ignited a multitude of studies on these algebras, with a particular focus on the physical perspective
[
36
,
17
,
15
]
.

However, from the mathematical side, the (Poisson) vertex algebra counterpart of the
N=2
SUSY
W
-algebras remains elusive, particularly within the framework of
N=2
superfields. The objective of this paper is to bridge this gap concerning
N=2
SUSY structures of classical W-algebras. In particular, we describe the
N=2
SUSY structures of the non-SUSY classical W-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n),F)
and the
N=1
SUSY classical W-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\overline{\mathfrak{sl}}(n+1|n),f)
associated with the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n)
. Here
F
is the even principal nilpotent and
f
is the odd principal nilpotent. The reason we consider
\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n)
is that it is the only basic Lie superalgebra which has principal
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
-embedding
[
14
]
,
[
36
]
. It is however expected that the even (resp. odd) nilpotent element in any subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
yields a
N=2
SUSY structure of
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n),F)
(resp.
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\overline{\mathfrak{sl}}(n+1|n),f)
)
[
36
]
.

In order to find
N=2
SUSY structure of a Poisson vertex algebra, it is enough to find a
N=2
superconformal vector
[
25
]
. Inspired from this fact, we find a
N=2
superconformal vector of
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\overline{\mathfrak{sl}}(n+1|n),f)
and
N=1
and
N=2
superconformal vector of
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n),F)
.
More precisely, as main results of this paper, we prove the following statements (Theorem
5.2
and Theorem
5.5
):

•

\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n),F)
has
N=1,2
SUSY structures.

•

There is a
N=1
(resp.
N=2
) superconformal vector
G
(resp.
J
) of
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n),F)
and a generating set as a
N=1
(resp.
N=1
) SUSY Poisson vertex algebra consisting of
G
-primary (resp.
J
-primary) elements.

•

\mathcal{W}^{k}(\overline{\mathfrak{sl}}(n+1|n),f)
has
N=1,2
SUSY structures.

•

There is a
N=1
(resp.
N=2
) superconformal vector
G
(resp.
J
) of
\mathcal{W}^{k}(\overline{\mathfrak{sl}}(n+1|n),f)
and a generating set as a
N=1
(resp.
N=2
) SUSY Poisson vertex algebra consisting of
G
-primary (resp.
J
-primary) elements.

In the proof of the listed results, we crucially use explicit formulas for
\lambda
-brackets of the classical W-algebras which appear in
[
40
]
. Here the
\lambda
-bracket is a way to express
the OPE relation between two elements of a Poisson vertex algebra.

The plan of the paper is the following. Some general properties of
\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n)
with respect to their

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
and
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
principal embeddings are recalled in section
2
.
In section
3

Then, in section
4
, the structure of
N=0,1
SUSY
W
-algebras is studied.
Section
5
deals with
N=2
SUSY
W
-algebras and contains

6
on open problems. An appendix gathers the technical proofs of the results presented in section
5
.

2.
\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n)
as
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
and
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
-module

It has been observed in
[
36
]
that we should consider basic Lie superalgebras with
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
embedding to possibly get
N_{K}=2
supersymmetry structure in
W
-algebras. Therefore, a Lie superalgebra with principal
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
-embedding would be the first thing to study for
N_{K}=2
supersymmetry.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
14
]
,
[
36
]
)
.

The only basic Lie superalgebras that admit a principal
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
-embedding are
\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We remind that the basic (or contragredient) Lie superalgebras include the
A(m,n)=\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n)
unitary series,
the
B(m,n)=\mathfrak{osp}(2m+1|2n)
,
C(n+1)=\mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)
, and
D(m,n)=\mathfrak{osp}(2m|2n)
orthosymplectic series and the exceptional
superalgebras
F(4)
and
G(3)
as well as
D(2,1;\alpha)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is a basic Lie superalgebra that admits a principal
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
-embedding. Since
\mathfrak{g}
also has a principal
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
-embedding,
\mathfrak{g}
should be isomorphic to one of the followings:

\mathfrak{sl}(n\!\pm\!1|n),\hskip 5.69054pt\mathfrak{osp}(M|2n)\text{ with }M=2n\!\pm\!1,2n\!+\!2,2n,\hskip 5.69054ptD(2,1;\alpha)\text{ with }\alpha\!\in\mathbb{C}\!\setminus\!\{0,\pm 1\}.

Each type of the above Lie superalgebras has an irreducible
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
-module decomposition

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sl}(n\!-\!1|n)

\displaystyle\simeq R_{2n-2}\oplus R_{2n-3}\oplus R_{2n-4}\oplus\cdots R_{2}\oplus R_{1},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sl}(n\!+\!1|n)

\displaystyle\simeq R_{2n}\oplus R_{2n-1}\oplus R_{2n-2}\oplus\cdots R_{2}\oplus R_{1},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{osp}(2n|2n)

\displaystyle\simeq R_{4n-2}\oplus R_{4n-5}\oplus R_{4n-6}\oplus\cdots\oplus R_{3}\oplus R_{2}\oplus R_{2n-1},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{osp}(2n\!-\!1|2n)

\displaystyle\simeq R_{4n-2}\oplus R_{4n-5}\oplus R_{4n-6}\oplus\cdots\oplus R_{3}\oplus R_{2},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{osp}(2n\!+\!1|2n)

\displaystyle\simeq R_{4n-1}\oplus R_{4n-2}\oplus R_{4n-5}\oplus\cdots\oplus R_{3}\oplus R_{2},

\displaystyle D(2,1;\alpha)

\displaystyle\simeq 2R_{2}\oplus R_{3},

where
R_{q}
is the finite irreducible
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
-module of dimension
2q+1
. Since
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)\simeq R_{2}\oplus R_{1}
as an
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
-module,
\mathfrak{g}
should contain
R_{2}\oplus R_{1}
, which is true only for
\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n)
,
\mathfrak{sl}(n|n+1)\simeq\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n)
and
\mathfrak{osp}(2|2)\simeq\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
. In these cases, one can check that the subspace
R_{2}\oplus R_{1}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
as Lie superalgebras.
∎

Considering the above proposition, this paper only deals with
W
-algebras associated with
\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n)
, that we will denote
\mathfrak{g}
throughout
this section.
As a start, we remind the module structure of
\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n)
as an adjoint representation of the principal
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
subalgebra.

2.1.
\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n)
s an
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
module

We first review the structure of
\mathfrak{g}
as an
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
-module given by the adjoint representation of the principal
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
subalgebra.

Recall that
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
has a basis
\{H,E,F,e,f\}
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

(osp-1)
H
is a nontrivial element in the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
,

(2)

(osp-2)
E,F
are even eigenvectors of
\textup{ad}H
with eigenvalues
1,-1
,

(3)

(osp-3)
e,f
are odd eigenvectors of
\textup{ad}H
with eigenvalues
\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}
,

(4)

(osp-4)
[e,e]=2E,\hskip 5.69054pt[f,f]=-2F,\hskip 5.69054pt[e,f]=-2H,\hskip 5.69054pt[F,e]=f,\hskip 5.69054pt[E,f]=e
,

where
[.,.]
is the graded commutator, which is graded skewsymmetric and obeys the graded Jacobi identity.
We denote by
H,E,F,e,f\in\mathfrak{g}
the elements in the principal
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
satisfying (osp-1)–(osp-4).
With respect to the
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
embedding of
\mathfrak{g}
, we define a gradation of
\mathfrak{g}
:

(2.1)

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/2}\mathfrak{g}(i),\text{ where }\mathfrak{g}(i)=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}|[H,x]=i\,x\}.

Recall that an irreducible
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
-module can be identified by its dimension which is odd. Let
R_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
be the irreducible module of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
of dimension
2i+1

[
41

Proposition 2.2
.

Consider
\mathfrak{g}
as an
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
-representation given by the adjoint action of the principal
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
subalgebra. Then
\mathfrak{g}
decomposes into the direct sum of irreducible representations:

(2.2)

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{2n}R_{i}.

The irreducible component
R_{i}
is decomposed into a sum of 1-dimensional subspaces
R_{i}(j):=R_{i}\cap\mathfrak{g}(j)
for
j\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
such that
-\frac{i}{2}\leq j\leq\frac{i}{2}
. Hence, if we take a highest weight vector
v_{i}\in R_{i}
, then

(2.3)

v^{(m)}_{i}:=(\textup{ad}f)^{m}v_{i}

spans
R_{i}(\frac{i-m}{2})
,
0\leq m\leq 2i
, and
v^{(0)}_{i}=v_{i}
is the highest weight vector. In addition, we have bases
\{v_{i}^{(0)}|i=1,2,\cdots,2n\}
and
\{v^{(2i)}_{i}|i=1,2,\cdots,2n\}

of
\mathfrak{g}^{e}:=\text{ker}(\textup{ad}e)
and
\mathfrak{g}^{f}:=\text{ker}(\textup{ad}f)
, respectively.

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{R}_{i},\quad\widetilde{R}_{i}=R_{2i-1}\oplus R_{2i}

gives the decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
into the direct sum of irreducible representations with respect to the adjoint action of the principal
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
.

Proof.

Lemma
2.3
and the diagram (
2.4
) assure that each
\widetilde{R}_{i}
is closed under the action of
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
except for that of
\tilde{e}
. Hence it remains to show that
[\tilde{e},v_{2i}]=0
, that we prove by a downward induction on
i
.

It is clear that
[\tilde{e},v_{2n}]=0
. Considering the grading (
2.1
) of
\mathfrak{g}
, we have

[\tilde{e},v_{2n-2i}]=\sum_{j=0}^{2i-1}k_{j}(\textup{ad}f)^{j}v_{2n-2i+1+j}

for some constants
k_{j}
’s. Apply
\text{ad}e
on the both sides to get

0=\sum_{j=1}^{2i-1}k_{j}(\textup{ad}e)(\textup{ad}f)^{j}v_{2n-2i+1+j}.

Then, since
\{(\text{ad}e)(\text{ad}f)^{j}v_{2n-2i+1+j}|j=1,2,\cdots,2i-1\}
are linearly independent, all
k_{j}
for
j\geq 1
should be zero. In other words,

[\tilde{e},v_{2n-2i}]=k_{0}\,v_{2n-2i+1}.

By the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle 0=k_{0}[\tilde{e},v_{2n-2i+1}]

\displaystyle=k_{0}c_{n-i+1}^{-1}\big[\tilde{e},[\tilde{f},v_{2n-2i+2}]\big]=k_{0}c_{n-i+1}^{-1}\big[[\tilde{e},\tilde{f}],v_{2n-2i+2}\big]

\displaystyle=-k_{0}c_{n-i+1}^{-1}\big[2H,v_{2n-2i+2}\big]=-(2n-2i+2)k_{0}c_{n-i+1}^{-1}v_{2n-2i+2},

where the nonzero constant
c_{j}
is defined by

[\tilde{f},v_{2j}]=c_{j}v_{2j-1}
.
Thus,
k_{0}
is also zero.
∎

3.
Poisson vertex algebras and Supersymmetric Poisson vertex algebras

In this section, we review the definition of supersymmetric Poisson vertex algebras.
For a detailed explanation or further relation between supersymmetric vertex algebras and supersymmetric Poisson vertex algebras, see the paper
[
25
]
of Heluani and Kac.

3.1.
Basic notations

In this section, we establish basic notations used throughout this paper. Let
P=P_{\bar{0}}\oplus P_{\bar{1}}
be any vector superspace and
a\in P
be a homogeneous element. If
a\in P_{\bar{0}}
(resp.
a\in P_{\bar{1}}
), then the parity is defined by
p(a)=0
(resp.
p(a)=1
).

Let us consider endomorphisms on
P
, denoted as
D_{i}
and
\partial
, where
D_{i}
is odd and
\partial
is even. The tuple of endomorphisms
D_{i}
and
\partial
is denoted by

(3.1)

\nabla:=\left(\partial,D_{1},\cdots D_{N}\right),

provided that they satisfy the relations

(3.2)

[D_{i},D_{j}]=2\delta_{i,j}\partial,\quad[\partial,D_{i}]=0.

Additionally, we let
\mathbb{C}[\nabla]
be the unital superassociative algebra generated by
\nabla
.

We also consider unital superassociative algebra
\mathbb{C}[\Lambda]
generated by

(3.3)

\Lambda:=(\lambda,\chi_{1},\cdots,\chi_{N})

subject to the relation

(3.4)

[\lambda,\chi_{i}]=0,\quad[\chi_{i},\chi_{j}]=-2\delta_{i,j}\lambda,

where
\lambda
is even and
\chi_{i}
’s are odd. One can view the vector superspace
\mathbb{C}[\Lambda]\otimes P
as a
\mathbb{C}[\nabla]
-module with

(3.5)

D_{i}(\chi_{j}a)=-\chi_{j}D_{i}(a)+2\delta_{ij}\lambda a

for
a\in P
, where the tensor product notation is omitted.

3.2.
Supersymmetric Poisson vertex algebras

Definition 3.1
.

A
N_{K}=N
supersymmetric (SUSY) Lie conformal algebra is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\nabla]
-module
R
with a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map
\{\cdot\,{}_{\Lambda}\,\cdot\}:R\otimes R\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\Lambda]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}R
of parity
N\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)
satisfying the following three axioms:

(1)

(sesquilinearity)

\left\{D_{i}a{}_{\Lambda}b\right\}=-(-1)^{N}\chi_{i}\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\},\{a_{\Lambda}D_{i}b\}=(-1)^{p(a)+N}\left(D_{i}+\chi_{i}\right)\{a_{\Lambda}b\},

(2)

(skewsymmetry)

\left\{b{}_{\Lambda}a\right\}=-(-1)^{p(a)p(b)+N}\left\{b{}_{-\Lambda-\nabla}a\right\},

(3)

(Jacobi identity)

\left\{a_{\Lambda}\left\{b_{\Gamma}c\right\}\right\}=(-1)^{(p(a)+1)N}\left\{\left\{a_{\Lambda}b\right\}_{\Gamma+\Lambda}c\right\}+(-1)^{(p(a)+N)(p(b)+N)}\left\{b_{\Gamma}\left\{a_{\Lambda}c\right\}\right\}.

for
a,b\in R
,
i=1,2,\cdots,N
and
\Lambda,\Gamma
two different tuples of the form (
3.3
).

To be more precise, the axioms are understood in the following way.

(i)

The RHS of the sesquilinearity and skewsymmetry relations can be computed by the
\mathbb{C}[\nabla]
-module structure of
\mathbb{C}[\Lambda]\otimes R
,

(ii)

The Jacobi identity is an equality in
\mathbb{C}[\Lambda,\Gamma]\otimes R
and an element in
\Lambda
supercommutes with an element in
\Gamma
.

By performing the sesquilinearity twice, we get

(3.6)

\{\partial a{}_{\Lambda}b\}=-\lambda\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\},\quad\{a{}_{\Lambda}\partial b\}=(\partial+\lambda)\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\},

which are known as sesquilinearities of PVA. In particular, when
N=0
, an
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
R
satisfying the skewsymmetry, Jacobi identity and (
3.6
) is simply called a Lie conformal algebra.
In this case, we usually denote the
\Lambda
-bracket by
\{\cdot{}_{\lambda}\cdot\}
.

If the superspace has in addition a unital supercommutative, superassociative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra structure, which is compatible with the
\Lambda
-bracket, then we call it the
N_{K}=N
supersymmetric Poisson vertex algebra.

Definition 3.2
.

A
N_{K}=N
supersymmetric (SUSY) Poisson vertex algebra (PVA) is a tuple
\left(P,1,\nabla,\{\cdot{}_{\Lambda}\cdot\},\cdot\right)
, where

•

\left(P,\nabla,\{\cdot{}_{\Lambda}\cdot\}\right)
is a
N_{K}=N
SUSY Lie conformal algebra,

•

\left(P,1,D_{i},\cdot\right)
is a unital supercommutative superassociative differential algebra with odd derivations
D_{i}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,N
,

•

(Leibniz rule)
\left\{a_{\Lambda}bc\right\}=\left\{a_{\Lambda}b\right\}c+(-1)^{(p(a)+N)p(b)}b\left\{a_{\Lambda}c\right\}
for any
a,b,c\in P
.

In particular, the
N_{K}=0
SUSY PVA is simply called a PVA.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
P
be a
N_{K}=N
SUSY PVA. An element
G\in P
of parity
N

(\text{mod}\ 2)
is called a
N_{K}=N
superconformal vector of central charge
c\in\mathbb{C}
if it satisfies the two following conditions.

(i)

The
\Lambda
-bracket between
G
and itself is

(3.7)

\{G{}_{\Lambda}G\}=\left(2\partial+(4-N)\lambda+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}D_{i}\right)G+\frac{\lambda^{3-N}\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\cdots\chi_{N}}{3}c,

where
c=0
if
N\geq 4
.

(ii)

Denote by
P_{\Delta}
for
\Delta\in\mathbb{R}
the subspace of
P
consisting of elements
a\in P
satisfying

(3.8)

\{G{}_{\Lambda}a\}=\left(2\partial+2\Delta\lambda+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}D_{i}\right)a+O(\Lambda^{2}).

Here
O(\Lambda^{2})
is a polynomial in
\Lambda
, whose constant and linear terms are vanishing.
Then

P=\bigoplus_{\Delta\geq M}P_{\Delta}
for some
M\in\mathbb{R}
.

If
P
has a superconformal vector,
P=\bigoplus P_{\Delta}
is called the conformal weight decomposition of
P
. In addition, the value
\Delta
in (
3.8
) is called the conformal weight of
a
with respect to
G
. In the physics literature, an element
a
satisfying (
3.8
) is called quasi-primary. Moreover, if
O(\Lambda^{2})=0
in (
3.8
), we say
that
a
is (
G-
)primary of conformal weight
\Delta
.
For later use, we note that
a\in P_{\Delta}
is primary if and only if

(3.9)

\{a{}_{\Lambda}G\}=(-1)^{p(a)N+N}\left((2\Delta-2+N)\partial+2\Delta\lambda+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}D_{i}\right)a.

Additionally, when
N=0
,
G
is commonly referred to as a conformal vector.

Remark 3.4
.

In many references (see e.g.
[
28
]
), when
N=0
, a conformal vector
L
of a PVA is defined through the Virasoro relation:

(3.10)

\{L{}_{\lambda}L\}=(\partial+2\lambda)L+\frac{c}{6}\lambda^{3}

for some constant
c
. The RHS of (
3.10
) is the half of our relation (
3.7
) for the conformal vector. Then, an element
L
in a PVA is a conformal vector with respect to the relation (
3.10
) if and only if
2L
is a conformal vector with respect to (
3.7
). However, the two definitions of conformal vectors give rise to the same conformal weight decomposition.

Remark 3.5
.

There is another kind of SUSY PVA called a
N_{W}
SUSY PVA introduced in
[
25
]
.
However, in this paper, we only deal with
N_{K}=0,1,2
SUSY PVAs and simply denote them by
N=0,1,2
SUSY PVAs.

3.3.
Relation between SUSY PVAs

To understand the structure of SUSY PVAs, we observe the relation between them. Especially, we see the relations between
N=0,1
and
2
Poisson structures. In the rest of this paper, we fix

(3.11)

\displaystyle\bm{\nabla}=(\partial,D,\tilde{D}),

\displaystyle\bm{\Lambda}=(\lambda,\chi,\tilde{\chi}),

\displaystyle\nabla=(\partial,D),\quad\Lambda=(\lambda,\chi),

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}=(\partial,\tilde{D}),\quad\tilde{\Lambda}=(\lambda,\tilde{\chi}),

all of which satisfy the relations introduced in section
3.1
.
We also adopt a uniform notation for the coefficients of each
N=0,1,2
Poisson bracket as

(3.12)

\displaystyle\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}a_{(n)}b,\quad\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}\left(a_{(n|0)}b+\chi a_{(n|1)}b\right),

\displaystyle\{a{}_{\bm{\Lambda}}b\}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}\left(a_{(n|00)}b-\chi a_{\left(n|10\right)}b+\tilde{\chi}a_{\left(n|01\right)}b-\chi\tilde{\chi}a_{\left(n|11\right)}b\right).

Proposition-Definition 3.6
.

Suppose
P
is a
N=1
or
2
SUSY PVA. Then
P
is a
N=0
PVA
with the
\lambda
-bracket
\{\cdot{}_{\lambda}\cdot\}:P\otimes P\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\lambda]\otimes P

defined by

(3.13)

\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}:=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}a_{(n|\rm I)}b,\quad\rm I=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }N=1,\\
11&\text{ if }N=2.\end{array}\right.

In the spirit of (
3.12
), the
\lambda
-bracket in (
3.13
) is equivalent to
a_{(n)}:=a_{(n|\rm I)}
.

Moreover, if
P
is a
N=2
SUSY PVA, each of the following
\Lambda
or
\tilde{\Lambda}
-bracket

(3.14)

\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}\left(a_{(n|01)}b+\chi a_{(n|11)}b\right),\quad\{a{}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}b\}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}\left(a_{(n|10)}b+\tilde{\chi}a_{(n|11)}b\right)

makes
P
a
N=1
SUSY PVA.

Proof.

Note that (
3.13
) is obtained by selecting only those terms from the
\Lambda
(resp.
\bm{\Lambda}
)-bracket in (
3.12
) that contain the variable
\chi
(resp.
\chi\tilde{\chi}
). Therefore, one can show that (
3.13
) is indeed a Poisson bracket by comparing the coefficients of
\chi
(resp.
\chi\tilde{\chi}
) in each of (
3.6
) and conditions in Definition
3.1
. The last statement is also true for a similar reason.
∎

In the rest of this paper, even in the context of
N=1
or
2
SUSY PVA, we will use the expression
a_{(n)}
to refer to
a_{(n|\rm I)}
for
\rm I
in (
3.13
). In addition, focusing on the interrelation between
N=0,1,
and
2
SUSY structures, one can check by sesquilinearity that (
3.12
), (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) are correlated as

(3.15)

\displaystyle\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}=

\displaystyle\{Da{}_{\lambda}b\}+\chi\{a{}_{\lambda}b\},\quad\{a{}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}b\}=\{\tilde{D}a{}_{\lambda}b\}+\tilde{\chi}\{a{}_{\lambda}b\},

(3.16)

\displaystyle\{a{}_{\bm{\Lambda}}b\}=

\displaystyle\{\tilde{D}Da{}_{\lambda}b\}-\chi\{\tilde{D}a{}_{\lambda}b\}+\tilde{\chi}\{Da{}_{\lambda}b\}-\chi\tilde{\chi}\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\{\tilde{D}a{}_{\Lambda}b\}+\tilde{\chi}\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}=\{Da{}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}b\}-\chi\{a{}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}b\},

when
P
is a
N=1
or
2
SUSY PVA. For example, when
P
is a
N=1
SUSY PVA, one can obtain
(Da)_{(n|1)}=a_{(n|0)}
by comparing the coefficients of
\chi
in
\{Da{}_{\Lambda}b\}=\chi\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}
. Then this result demonstrates the first equality in (
3.15
).

Now, it is natural to ask whether the existence of an additional odd derivation on
N=0
or
1
SUSY PVA would lead to an additional SUSY structure if we use (
3.15
) or (
3.16
). To investigate this possibility, we define the induced brackets in an obvious way, when a
N=0
or
1
SUSY PVA has an odd derivation
D
or
\tilde{D}
. Each bracket induced by
\Lambda
,
\tilde{\Lambda}
,
\bm{\Lambda}
will be denoted by

\{\cdot{}_{\Lambda}\cdot\}^{-},\quad\{\cdot{}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}\cdot\}^{-},\quad\{\cdot{}_{\bm{\Lambda}}\cdot\}^{-},

respectively. The induced bracket
\{\cdot{}_{\Lambda}\cdot\}^{-}
can be defined on any PVA when it is equipped with any odd derivation
D
. However, these induced brackets may not be Poisson brackets. If the induced brackets are indeed Poisson brackets, then they satisfy the relations (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) again. The following propositions provide criteria for induced brackets to be Poisson brackets.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
P
be a PVA. The following two statements are equivalent:

(1)

(P,1,\nabla,\{\cdot{}_{\Lambda}\cdot\}^{-},\cdot)
is a
N=1
SUSY PVA,

(2)

There exists an odd derivation
D:P\rightarrow P
satisfying

(3.17)

D^{2}=\partial,\quad D\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}=\{Da{}_{\lambda}b\}+(-1)^{p(a)}\{a{}_{\lambda}Db\}

for any
a,b\in P
.

Here, we assume that
\{\cdot{}_{\Lambda}\cdot\}^{-}
is induced by
\{\cdot{}_{\lambda}\cdot\}
as in (
3.15
).

Proof.

Let
P
be a
N=1
SUSY PVA. Comparing the coefficients of
\chi
in the sesquilinearity
D\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}^{-}-(-1)^{p(a)+1}\{a{}_{\Lambda}Db\}^{-}=-\chi\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}^{-}
, one gets

(3.18)

Da_{(n|1)}b+(-1)^{p(a)+1}a_{(n|1)}Db=a_{(n|0)}b.

In terms of the non-SUSY structure of
P
, (
3.18
) is equivalent to (
3.17
).
Now, assume (2) and check if the induced
\Lambda
-bracket
\{\cdot{}_{\Lambda}\cdot\}^{-}
satisfies sesquilinearity, skewsymmetry and Jacobi identity. By computation, we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle\{Da{}_{\Lambda}b\}^{-}

\displaystyle=\{\partial a{}_{\lambda}b\}+\chi\{Da{}_{\lambda}b\}=\chi(\chi\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}+\{Da{}_{\lambda}b\})=\chi\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}^{-}

and

(3.20)

\displaystyle\{a{}_{\lambda}Db\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}a_{(n)}(Db)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}\left([a_{(n)},D]+(-1)^{p(a)}D\,a_{(n)}\right)b

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}\left((-1)^{p(a)+1}(Da)_{(n)}b+(-1)^{p(a)}D(a_{(n)}b)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(a)+1}\{Da{}_{\lambda}b\}+(-1)^{p(a)}D\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}.

By (
3.20
), one can deduce

(3.21)

\displaystyle\{a{}_{\Lambda}Db\}^{-}=\chi\big((-1)^{p(a)+1}\{Da{}_{\lambda}b\}+(-1)^{p(a)}D\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}\big)

\displaystyle+\big((-1)^{p(a)}\{\partial a{}_{\lambda}b\}+(-1)^{p(a)+1}D\{Da{}_{\lambda}b\}\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(a)+1}(\chi+D)\big(\{Da{}_{\lambda}b\}+\chi\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}\big)=(-1)^{p(a)+1}(\chi+D)\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}^{-}.

Hence we get the sesquilinearity from (
3.19
) and (
3.21
). The skewsymmetry of the
\Lambda
-bracket follows from the skewsymmetry of PVA
P
. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\{a{}_{-\Lambda-\nabla}b\}^{-}

\displaystyle=\{Da{}_{-\lambda-\partial}b\}-(\chi+D)\{a{}_{-\lambda-\partial}b\}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(b)(p(a)+1)+1}\{b{}_{\lambda}Da\}-(\chi+D)(-1)^{p(a)p(b)+1}\{b{}_{\lambda}a\}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}\big(\{Db{}_{\lambda}a\}-D\{b{}_{\lambda}a\}+(\chi+D)\{b{}_{\lambda}a\}\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}\{b{}_{\Lambda}a\}^{-}.

Similarly, the Jacobi identity of
\Lambda
-bracket follows from that of
\lambda
-bracket and (
3.20
).

∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
P
be a PVA. The following three statements are equivalent.

(1)

(P,1,\bm{\nabla},\{\cdot{}_{\bm{\Lambda}}\cdot\}^{-},\cdot)
is a
N=2
SUSY PVA,

(2)

(P,1,\nabla,\{\cdot{}_{\Lambda}\cdot\}^{-},\cdot)
is a
N=1
SUSY PVA with an odd derivation
\tilde{D}
on
P
satisfying
\tilde{D}^{2}=\partial
,
[D,\tilde{D}]=0
and
\tilde{D}\{a{}_{\Lambda}b\}^{-}=-\{\tilde{D}a{}_{\Lambda}b\}^{-}+(-1)^{p(a)+1}\{a{}_{\Lambda}\tilde{D}b\}^{-}
for any
a,b\in P
,

(3)

P
has two supercommuting odd derivations
D
and
\tilde{D}
satisfying
D^{2}=\tilde{D}^{2}=\partial
and
D\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}=\{Da{}_{\lambda}b\}+(-1)^{p(a)}\{a{}_{\lambda}Db\}
,
\tilde{D}\{a{}_{\lambda}b\}=\{\tilde{D}a{}_{\lambda}b\}+(-1)^{p(a)}\{a{}_{\lambda}\tilde{D}b\}
for any
a,b\in P
.

Proof.

The equivalence of (2) and (3) is immediate from Proposition
3.7
, so we only need to show that (1) and (2) are equivalent. If
P
is a
N=2
SUSY PVA, then the
N=1
SUSY PVA structure of
P
is guaranteed by Proposition-Definition
3.6
. Additionally, the properties of
\tilde{D}
can be checked using sesquilinearity, as illustrated in the proof of Proposition
3.7
. One can prove the opposite by the computation similar to (
3.21
).
∎

In the following, when induced brackets are Poisson brackets, we shall omit the “
-
” symbol in the induced brackets.

Thus, to find an additional SUSY structure, one has first to identify an odd derivation
D
that satisfies (
3.17
). For any given element
\tau
in a PVA,
\tau_{(0)}
is a derivation that meets the second condition of (
3.17
), as it can be deduced from Jacobi identity and Leibniz rule. Therefore, we can take
D:=\tau_{(0)}
whenever
\tau
is an odd element satisfying
[\tau_{(0)},\tau_{(0)}]=(\tau_{(0)}\tau)_{(0)}=2\partial
.

At this point, conformal vectors come into play. Note that the last condition for
\tau
is achieved when
\tau_{(0)}\tau
is a conformal vector. Hence, we assume that

(1)

\tau_{(0)}\tau
is indeed a conformal vector, allowing us to put
D:=\tau_{(0)}
,

(2)

\tau
is primary of conformal weight
\frac{3}{2}
with respect to
\tau_{(0)}\tau
.

Then, in terms of the induced
\Lambda
-bracket, we get an equation

(3.22)

\{\tau{}_{\Lambda}\tau\}^{-}=(2\partial+3\lambda+\chi D)\tau+O(\Lambda^{2}),

which has to be compared with the Definition
3.3
for a
N=1
superconformal vector. The following theorem shows how to get a superconformal vector along with an additional SUSY structure and determines the conformal weight decomposition of
P
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
P
be a PVA with a conformal vector
L
of central charge
c\in\mathbb{C}
. Suppose that there are primary elements
G
and
a_{i},\,i\in{\mathcal{I}}
, such that
G
is odd,

(3.23)

\{G{}_{\lambda}G\}=L+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{3}c,\quad\{G{}_{\lambda}a_{i}\}=G_{(0)}a_{i},\quad i\in{\mathcal{I}},

and the set
\{G,L\}\cup\{a_{i},G_{(0)}a_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
freely generates
P
as a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-algebra. Then, for
D:=G_{(0)}
,
P
is a
N=1
SUSY PVA with a
N=1
superconformal vector
G
. Moreover,
P
is freely generated by the set
\{G\}\cup\{a_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
as a
\mathbb{C}[\nabla]
-algebra, where
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
consists of
G
-primary elements.

Proof.

Given that the conformal weight of
G_{(0)}G=L
is
2
,
G
has conformal weight
\frac{3}{2}
. Therefore, by the preceding argument of this theorem, one can take
D:=G_{(0)}
to get a
N=1
SUSY structure. From the
L
-primarity of
G
and the first condition in (
3.23
), it follows that

(3.24)

\{DG{}_{\lambda}G\}=(2\partial+3\lambda)G,\quad\{G{}_{\lambda}G\}=DG+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{3}c.

This is equivalent to (
3.7
) if we use (
3.15
). The remaining conditions prove the
G
-primarity of
a_{i}
in a similar way.
∎

Following a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem

, one can also prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let

. Suppose that there are primary elements

and

,

\{J{}_{\Lambda}J\}=-G-\frac{\lambda\chi}{3}c,\quad\{J{}_{\Lambda}a_{i}\}=J_{(0|0)}a_{i},

and the set
\{J,G\}\cup\{a_{i},J_{(0|0)}a_{i}\}
freely generates

as a

-algebra. Then, for
\tilde{D}:=J_{(0|0)}
,

is a
N=2
SUSY PVA with a

superconformal vector

. Moreover,

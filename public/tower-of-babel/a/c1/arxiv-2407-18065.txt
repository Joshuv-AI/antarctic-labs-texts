Abstract
. We build on our recent results on the Lipschitz dependence of the extreme spectral values of one-parameter families of pseudodifferential operators with symbols in a weighted Sjöstrand class. We prove that larger symbol classes lead to Hölder continuity with respect to the parameter. This result is then used to investigate the behavior of frame bounds of families of Gabor systems 𝒢 ⁡ ( g , α ​ Λ ) \mathcal{G}(g,\alpha\Lambda) with respect to the parameter α > 0 \alpha>0 , where Λ \Lambda is a set of non-uniform, relatively separated time-frequency shifts, and g ∈ M s 1 ​ ( ℝ d ) g\in M^{1}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , 0 ≤ s ≤ 2 0\leq s\leq 2 . In particular, we show that the frame bounds depend continuously on α \alpha if g ∈ M 1 ​ ( ℝ d ) g\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , and are Hölder continuous if g ∈ M s 1 ​ ( ℝ d ) g\in M^{1}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , 0 < s ≤ 2 0<s\leq 2 , with the Hölder exponent explicitly given.

1.
Introduction

The
extreme spectral values
of a bounded self-adjoint operator
T
are given by
\sigma_{+}(T)=\max\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\ \lambda\in\sigma(T)\}
and
\sigma_{-}(T)=\min\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\ \lambda\in\sigma(T)\}
, where
\sigma(T)
denotes the spectrum of
T
.
In
[
24
]
, we studied certain one-parameter families of
pseudodifferential operators and the Lipschitz continuity of their
extreme spectral values. While small perturbations in operator norm result in small changes in spectra, the one-parameter families treated in
[
24
]

include non-norm-continuous operations, such as
dilation of Weyl symbols, and are therefore a veritable deformation theory of pseudodifferential operators.

Our work in
[
24
]
was motivated by Bellissard’s seminal results
[
6
]
on the
almost-Mathieu operator in a non-commutative torus, and extended them to families of operators with possibly non-periodic Weyl symbols by systematically exploiting time-frequency methods and the theory of modulations spaces. See also
[
17
]
for closely related results.

While many other contributions in the wake of Bellissard focus on spectral problems in mathematical physics, in particular on magnetic pseudodifferential operators

In this paper, we extend our results and investigate the relation between the smoothness of a symbol class and the Hölder continuity of the extreme spectral values of a pseudodifferential operator in more detail.

To formulate our main results, let us recall the main objects.

Pseudodifferential operators.
The Weyl transform
of a symbol

is the operator

\sigma^{w}f(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sigma\left(\frac{x+y}{2},\omega\right)e^{2\pi i(y-x)\cdot\omega}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\omega

Let

We study spectral properties of one-parameter families of pseudodifferential operators

The objective of this article is to establish conditions that ensure

Gabor frames.
Our main motivation to consider the specific setup of Theorem
1.1
is its application in Gabor analysis. Let
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
be a
discrete set (not necessarily a lattice), and consider the set of
time-frequency shifts
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)=\{\pi(\lambda)g\ :\lambda\in\Lambda\}
for some window function
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. A major problem in
Gabor analysis
is to study when
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a frame, i.e., when there exist constants
A,B>0
such that

(1.4)

A\|f\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|_{2}^{2},\,\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

In this article, we consider the case that the set of time-frequency
shifts is dilated by a parameter
\alpha>0
and study the dependence
of the frame bounds on the parameter. It is easy to see that
the optimal constants
A(\Lambda)
and
B(\Lambda)
in
(
1.4
)
are the smallest and largest spectral value of the frame operator

S_{g,\Lambda}f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle\pi(\lambda)g
, i.e.,

A(\Lambda)=\sigma_{-}(S_{g,\Lambda})\quad\text{and}\quad B(\Lambda)=\sigma_{+}(S_{g,\Lambda})\,.

In the problem of the deformation of Gabor frames one investigates how
the frame bounds of the deformed Gabor family
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha\Lambda)
depend on the dilation parameter
\alpha
. Since the frame
operator
S_{g,\alpha\Lambda}
can be written as a pseudodifferential operator in the
form (
1.3
), the behavior of the frame bounds with respect to

\alpha
amounts to a problem of (Hölder-)continuity of the
extreme spectral values

A(\alpha\Lambda)=\sigma_{-}(S_{g,\alpha\Lambda})
and

B(\alpha\Lambda)=\sigma_{+}(S_{g,\alpha\Lambda})
. This allows us to
apply Theorem
1.1
and derive conditions on
g
that
guarantee certain modes of continuity.

The study of the influence of dilations of time-frequency sets on the
respective frame bounds has a long history. If
\Lambda
is a lattice and
g\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it was shown in

[
19
]
that the frame bounds depend
lower semi-continuously
on

\alpha
, which implies that the set of lattices that generate a
Gabor frame is an open set. For general
non-uniform sets
\Lambda
, the lower semi-continuity of the frame
bounds was proven later in
[
1
]
.

As a first result, we strengthen the results of
[
19
,
1
]

and show that for
g\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the frame bounds actually
depend continuously on
\alpha
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
g\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be relatively separated.
Then the frame bounds of
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha\Lambda)
are continuous at
\alpha=1
, that is

\displaystyle|\sigma_{\pm}(S_{g,\Lambda})-\sigma_{\pm}(S_{g,\alpha\Lambda})|\to 0,\quad\alpha\to 1.

When
\Lambda
is a lattice, the frame operator
S_{g,\Lambda}
has a periodic Weyl symbol, and Theorem
1.2
follows from a classical result of Elliott
[
18
]
; see also
[
6
, Theorem 1]
. To cover general time-frequency nodes
\Lambda
we shall resort to
[
24
]
.

The density theorem for Gabor frames states that every frame
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
must satisfy the necessary density condition

D^{-}(\Lambda)\geq 1
, where
D^{-}(\Lambda)
is the lower Beurling
density of
\Lambda
. If

g\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then the strict inequality
D^{-}(\Lambda)>1

is necessary to obtain a Gabor frame,
see
[
1
,
23
,
25
]
. In particular, if
g\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
D^{-}(\Lambda)=1
, then
A(\Lambda)=0
. This is
the so-called Balian-Low phenomenon
[
7
]
. For
practical purposes, however, it is important to quantify how the
lower frame bound
A(\alpha\Lambda)
degenerates to zero as

\alpha\to 1
since the ratio

B(\Lambda)/A(\Lambda)
governs the quality of reconstruction methods.

This question can be fully answered with the help of
Theorem
1.1
and an approximation argument. We show that
windows from the intermediate spaces
M^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset M^{1}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
0<s<2
, yield
Hölder-continuous dependence of the frame bounds.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
g\in M^{1}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
0<s\leq 2
, and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be
relatively separated. Define the function

\gamma:(0,2]\to(0,1]
by

(1.5)

\gamma(s)=\begin{cases}\frac{s}{2(4-3s)}&0<s<1\\
\frac{s}{2}&1\leq s\leq 2\\
1&s\geq 2\end{cases}.

Then the frame bounds of
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha\Lambda)
are
\gamma(s)
-Hölder continuous at
\alpha=1
. More precisely,

|\sigma_{\pm}(S_{g,\Lambda})-\sigma_{\pm}(S_{g,\alpha\Lambda})|\leq C_{d,s}\cdot\emph{rel}(\Lambda)\cdot|1-\alpha|^{\gamma(s)}\cdot\|g\|_{M^{1}_{s}}^{2},\qquad 3/4<\alpha<2.

Theorem
1.3
describes the quantitative behavior of the lower frame bound near the critical density
D^{-}(\Lambda)=1
for windows in
M_{s}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
s>0
,

A(\alpha\Lambda)\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1-\alpha)^{s/(2(4-3s))}&0<s<1,\\
(1-\alpha)^{s/2},&1\leq s<2,\\
1-\alpha,&s\geq 2,\end{array}\right.

for
\alpha<1
,
\alpha\to 1
.
The nonlinear behavior of the exponent for
0<s<1
is an artefact of our proof. In fact, with some more effort, one can show that the exponent
s/2
holds for a larger range of
s
, though we decided not to include the required technicalities in this article.

Saturation.
One may wonder what happens when the smoothness
parameter
s
in Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
becomes very large. Does
the smoothness of the extreme spectral values increase? The answer is
no and follows from some examples in the theory of Gabor frames.

Specifically, for
the Gaussian window is was shown in
[
9
]
that the lower
frame bound behaves as

A(\alpha\mathbb{Z}^{2})\asymp(1-\alpha),\quad 1/2\leq\alpha\leq 1,\quad\text{ and }\alpha\to 1.

Similar results were obtained for the exponential functions

e^{-t}\chi_{[0,\infty)}
and
e^{-|t|}

[
26
]
.

Since the Gaussian
\varphi(t)=e^{-\pi t^{2}}
is a Schwartz
function and therefore in all
M^{1}_{s},s\geq 0
, Lipschitz continuity
is the strongest possible mode of continuity one can prove in general,
no matter how big the exponent of the weight
s
is chosen.
In addition, for the Gaussian window,
A(\alpha\mathbb{Z}^{2})
is not differentiable at
\alpha=1
, because
A(\alpha\mathbb{Z}^{2})\asymp(1-\alpha)
for
\alpha\leq 1
and

A(\alpha\mathbb{Z}^{2})=0
for
\alpha\geq 1
.

We conclude that the smoothness of the extreme spectral values is
saturated at Lipschitz continuity. While such saturation phenomena are
well known in approximation theory, we are unaware of similar results
in spectral theory.

2.3.
Time-frequency representations

We now collect some basic results on time-frequency analysis, modulation spaces, and pseudo-differential operators that will be needed to prove our main results.
Thorough introductions to the topic can be found in the textbook
[
20
]
and the two
recent monographs
[

12

For a point
z=(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, the
time-frequency shift
of
f
is defined as

\pi(z)f(t)=M_{\omega}T_{x}f(t)=e^{2\pi i\omega\cdot t}f(t-x),

where
T_{x}f(t)=f(t-x)
and
M_{\omega}f(t)=e^{2\pi i\omega\cdot t}f(t)
.
The
short-time Fourier transform
of a function or distribution

f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with respect to a window function
g
is given by

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-2\pi i\omega\cdot t}\,\mathrm{d}t=\langle f,M_{\omega}T_{x}g\rangle=\langle f,\pi(z)g\rangle.

When
g
is normalized by
\left\|g\right\|_{2}=1
, then
V_{g}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is an isometry

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}}|\langle f,\pi(z)g\rangle|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}z=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}}|V_{g}f(z)|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}z=\|f\|_{2}^{2},\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The isometry property of the short-time Fourier transform  yields the following continuous resolution of the identity

(2.3)

f=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}}\langle f,\pi(z)g\rangle\pi(z)g\,\,\mathrm{d}z,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where the integral should be interpreted in the weak sense. Elementary calculations show that for
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}

(2.4)

|\langle\pi(z_{1})f,\pi(z_{2})g\rangle|=|V_{g}(\pi(z_{1})f)(z_{2})|=|V_{g}f(z_{2}-z_{1})|.

The
(cross-)Wigner distribution
of
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(f,g)(x,\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\Big(x+\frac{t}{2}\Big)\overline{g\Big(x-\frac{t}{2}\Big)}e^{-2\pi i\omega\cdot t}\,\,\mathrm{d}t.

If

, we write
\mathcal{W}(f)
.

2.4.
Modulation spaces

Recall the definition of the mixed-norm modulation spaces given in (
1.1
). We will use the following estimate for the convolution of functions and distributions in modulation spaces, taken from
[
10
, Prop. 2.4]
.
If
f\in M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in M^{1}_{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, then
f\ast g\in M^{\infty,1}_{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

(2.6)

\|f\ast g\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{\infty}}\,\|g\|_{M^{1}_{0,s}}\,.

The actual statement in
[
10
]
does not allow for negative exponents
s<0
of the weight on the right hand side above. However, as we only need the estimate for weights in the frequency variable, one can simply follow the proof in
[
10
]
step by step without significant changes.
We will therefore not repeat the argument here.

As we note below, the space
M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
contains atomic measures supported on relatively separated sets.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be relatively
separated. Then the discrete measure
\mu:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\delta_{\lambda}
belongs to
M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, if
\emph{rel}(\Lambda):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\#\{\lambda\in\Lambda\cap x+[0,1]^{d}\}
then

\|\mu\|_{M^{\infty}}\lesssim\emph{rel}(\Lambda).

This can be proved by a direct calculation (which is easily carried out by taking a window function
g\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported on
[0,1]^{d}
).

The following approximation lemma is the main technical ingredient of
our proofs. The estimates are reminiscent of notions in approximation
theory, such as the K-functional, but they seem to be new for modulation spaces.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
,
a\leq b\leq c
.

(i)

Let
g\in M^{\infty,1}_{0,b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For every
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, there exist

, such that

\|g-h\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,a}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{b-a}\,\|g\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,b}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|h\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,c}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-(c-b)}\,\|g\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,b}}.

(ii)

Let
g\in M^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For every
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, there exist
h=h_{\varepsilon,g}\in M^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that

\|g-h\|_{M^{1}_{a}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{b-a}\,\|g\|_{M^{1}_{b}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|h\|_{M^{1}_{c}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-2(c-b)}\,\|g\|_{M^{1}_{b}}.

(Here, the implied constants depend on
|a|
and
|c|

Let
R\geq 0
and define
\Omega_{R}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times B_{R,d}(0)

to be
determined later, we define the frequency truncation of

With a small computation one sees that

is obtained from

by a
Fourier multiplier, but for modulation spaces estimates we will need the
reproducing formula (

) and the covariance formula
(
2.4

\displaystyle\|g

\displaystyle-h\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,a}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\big(V_{\varphi}g(z^{\prime})\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt\chi_{\Omega_{R}}(z^{\prime})V_{\varphi}g(z^{\prime})\big)\langle\pi(z^{\prime})\varphi,\pi(z)\varphi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\right|\hskip-1.0pt(1+|\omega|)^{a}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\chi_{\Omega_{R}^{c}}(z^{\prime})|V_{\varphi}g(z^{\prime})|\,|V_{\varphi}\varphi(z-z^{\prime})|\,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}(1+|\omega|)^{a}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle=\big\|\big(\chi_{\Omega_{R}^{c}}\cdot|V_{\varphi}g|\big)\ast|V_{\varphi}\varphi|\big\|_{L^{\infty,1}_{0,a}}\leq\big\|\chi_{\Omega_{R}^{c}}\cdot V_{\varphi}g\big\|_{L^{\infty,1}_{0,a}}\,\big\|V_{\varphi}\varphi\big\|_{L^{1}_{0,|a|}},

3.1.
Hölder continuity of extreme spectral values

Our proof of Theorem

relies on the

which was already established in
[

, and on a new approximation argument that may be useful in other

G_{\delta}\in M^{\infty,1}_{0,s}
with

by a suitable

in
M^{\infty,1}_{0,2}

and compare the operator family

T_{\delta}=(D_{\sqrt{1+\delta}}G_{\delta})^{w}
with

U_{\delta}=(D_{\sqrt{1+\delta}}H_{\delta})^{w}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For each
|\delta|<\delta_{0}<1
, let
G_{\delta}\in M^{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and
H_{\delta}\in M^{\infty,1}_{0,2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
be such that
\partial_{\delta}H_{\delta}\in M^{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
Then the following holds for
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in(-\delta_{0},\delta_{0})
:

\displaystyle|\sigma_{\pm}(T_{\delta_{1}})

\displaystyle-\sigma_{\pm}(T_{\delta_{2}})|\lesssim(1-\delta_{0})^{-(d+1)}\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\|G_{t}-H_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle+|\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}|(1-\delta_{0})^{-(d+1)}\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\big(\|H_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,2}}+\|\partial_{t}H_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}}\big).

Proof.

Using the triangle inequality, we find

\displaystyle|\sigma_{\pm}(T_{\delta_{1}})

\displaystyle-\sigma_{\pm}(T_{\delta_{2}})|

\displaystyle\leq|\sigma_{\pm}(T_{\delta_{1}})-\sigma_{\pm}(U_{\delta_{1}})|+|\sigma_{\pm}(U_{\delta_{1}})-\sigma_{\pm}(U_{\delta_{2}})|+|\sigma_{\pm}(U_{\delta_{2}})-\sigma_{\pm}(T_{\delta_{2}})|\,.

Since
H_{\delta}\in M^{\infty,1}_{0,2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, Theorem
1.1
for

s=2
(already proved in
[
24
]
) implies that

|\sigma_{\pm}(U_{\delta_{1}})-\sigma_{\pm}(U_{\delta_{2}})|\lesssim|\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}|(1-\delta_{0})^{-(d+1)}\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\big(\|H_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,2}}+\|\partial_{t}H_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}}\big).

For the first and third term we use Lemma
2.1
and
Theorem
2.5
and bound the spectral values first by the
operator norm and then by the modulation space norm to obtain

|\sigma_{\pm}(T_{\delta_{1}})-\sigma_{\pm}(U_{\delta_{1}})|\leq\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\|D_{\sqrt{1+\delta}}(G_{t}-H_{t})\|_{M^{\infty,1}}\lesssim\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\|G_{t}-H_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}}\,,

where we have used the dilation estimate (
2.9
) in the last

The combination of these estimate yields the claimed error bound.
∎

This result suggests that quantifying the trade-off between the error
of approximating
G_{\delta}\in M^{\infty,1}_{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
,
0<s\leq 2
, by a function
H_{\delta}\in M^{\infty,1}_{0,2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
with

-norm and the magnitude of the

-norm of

will lead to an explicit
estimate for the Hölder exponent of the extreme spectral values. The next lemma make this intuition precise.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G_{\delta}\in M^{\infty,1}_{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
,
0<s<2
, be a family of symbols such that
\partial_{\delta}G_{\delta}\in M^{\infty,1}_{0,s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})

0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, there exist
H_{\delta}=H_{\varepsilon,G_{\delta}}\in M^{\infty,1}_{0,2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
such that

(i)

\|G_{\delta}-H_{\delta}\|_{M^{\infty,1}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{s}\,\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\|G_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,s}},

(ii)

\|H_{\delta}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-(2-s)}\,\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\|G_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,s}},
and

(iii)

\|\partial_{\delta}H_{\delta}\|_{M^{\infty,1}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-(2-s)}\,\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\|\partial_{t}G_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,s-2}}.

(Here, the implied constants depend only on
d

Proof.

Define
H_{\delta}
as in (
2.7
) with

\Omega_{R}=\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times B_{R,2d}(0)
. Properties
(i)
and
(ii)

are then a direct consequence of Lemma
2.3
with

a=0,b=s,c=2
.

To prove (iii), we will show that

(3.1)

\partial_{\delta}H_{\delta}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\chi_{\Omega_{R}}(z)V_{\varphi}(\partial_{\delta}G_{\delta})(z)\pi(z)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}z,

i.e., integration and the partial derivative may be interchanged.

Once (
3.1
) is justified, the approximation of

\partial_{\delta}G_{\delta}
is obtained as the partial derivative of
H_{\delta}
and then the third estimate (iii) follows from
(the proof of) Lemma
2.3
.

To do so, we find a majorant via the following inequalities:

\displaystyle|\chi_{\Omega_{R}}(z)\partial_{\delta}V_{\varphi}G_{\delta}(z)

\displaystyle\pi(z)\varphi(t)|=|\chi_{\Omega_{R}}(z)V_{\varphi}(\partial_{\delta}G_{\delta})(z)\pi(z)\varphi(t)|

\displaystyle\leq(1+R)^{2-s}\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\|V_{\varphi}(\partial_{t}G_{t})\|_{L^{\infty}_{0,s-2}}\cdot|\chi_{\Omega_{R}}(z)\varphi(t-x)|

\displaystyle=(1+R)^{2-s}\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\|\partial_{t}G_{t}\|_{M^{\infty}_{0,s-2}}\cdot|\chi_{\Omega_{R}}(z)\varphi(t-x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+R)^{2-s}\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\|\partial_{t}G_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,s-2}}\cdot|\chi_{\Omega_{R}}(z)\varphi(t-x)|,

where we applied the embedding
M^{\infty,1}_{0,s-2}\hookrightarrow M^{\infty}_{0,s-2}

[
20
, Theorem 12.2.2]
in the last step.
The final expression is an integrable majorant as

\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\varphi(t-x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\omega\lesssim R^{2d}\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}.

Equation (
3.1
) therefore follows from an application of Leibniz integral rule.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us choose
\varepsilon=|\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}|^{\gamma}
for some

\gamma>0
to be determined. The combination of Lemma
3.1
and Lemma

\displaystyle|\sigma_{\pm}(T_{\delta_{1}})-\sigma_{\pm}(T_{\delta_{2}})|\lesssim(1-\delta_{0})^{-(d+1)}|\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}|^{\gamma s}\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\|G_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,s}}

\displaystyle\hskip 36.98866pt+|\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}|^{1+\gamma(s-2)}(1-\delta_{0})^{-(d+1)}\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\big(\|G_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,s}}+\|\partial_{t}G_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,s-2}}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 7.11317pt\leq\hskip-1.0pt(1\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt\delta_{0})^{-(d+1)}\big(|\delta_{1}\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt\delta_{2}|^{\gamma s}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt|\delta_{1}\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt\delta_{2}|^{1+\gamma(s-2)}\big)\sup_{|t|<\delta_{0}}\big(\|G_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,s}}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\|\partial_{t}G_{t}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,s-2}}\big).

∎

3.2.
Gabor frame bounds

Let
g,h\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be a relatively
separated set. The mixed Gabor frame operator of the associated set of
time-frequency shifts using
g
as analysis and
h
as synthesis window is given by

S_{g,h,\Lambda}f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle\pi(\lambda)h,\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

If
g=h
, we write
S_{g,\Lambda}=S_{g,g,\Lambda}
to denote the Gabor frame operator.
The Weyl symbol of the rank one operator
\langle\,\cdot\,,\pi(z)g\rangle\pi(z)h
is just the shift
T_{z}\mathcal{W}(h,g)
, where
\mathcal{W}(h,g)
is the cross-Wigner distribution introduced in (
2.5
). Hence, the Weyl symbol of
S_{g,h,\Lambda}
is

\sigma_{g,h,\Lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}T_{\lambda}\mathcal{W}(h,g).

As we already noted before, the extreme spectral values of
S_{g,\Lambda}
and
S_{g,\alpha\Lambda}
are
equal to the optimal frame bounds of
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
and

\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha\Lambda)
respectively. The Weyl symbol
corresponding to
S_{g,h,\alpha\Lambda}
with the dilated set

\alpha\Lambda
is

\sigma_{g,h,\alpha\Lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}T_{\alpha\lambda}\mathcal{W}(h,g)=D_{1/\alpha}\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}T_{\lambda}D_{\alpha}\mathcal{W}(h,g)\Big)\,.

We now set
\sqrt{1+\delta}=1/\alpha
, then the Weyl symbol of the
deformed frame operator
S_{g,h,\alpha\Lambda}
is
\sigma_{g,h,\alpha\Lambda}=D_{\sqrt{1+\delta}}G_{\delta}

with

G_{\delta}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}T_{\lambda}D_{1/\sqrt{1+\delta}}\mathcal{W}(h,g)\,,

and is therefore of the required form (
1.3
).

Let
\mu=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\delta_{\lambda}
. Then

\|\mu\|_{M^{\infty}}\lesssim\text{rel}(\Lambda)
by
Lemma
2.2
and
G_{\delta}=\mu\ast D_{1/\sqrt{1+\delta}}\mathcal{W}(h,g)
.
Our proof of Theorem
1.3
consists of two steps. First, for
1\leq s\leq 2

we apply Theorem
1.1
which requires that we bound the
M^{\infty,1}_{0,s}
-norm of the symbol and the
M^{\infty,1}_{0,s-2}
-norm of its derivative with respect to the parameter. This is done in the lemma below. For
0<s<1
we apply an approximation argument similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be relatively separated,
1\leq s\leq 2
, and
0<\delta_{0}<1
. If
g=h\in M^{1}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(i)

\|G_{\delta}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,s}}\lesssim\emph{rel}(\Lambda)\cdot(1-\delta_{0})^{-d}\cdot\|g\|_{M^{1}_{s}}^{2}
,
\qquad\delta\in(-\delta_{0},\delta_{0})
,

(ii)

\|\partial_{\delta}G_{\delta}\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{0,s-2}}\lesssim\emph{rel}(\Lambda)\cdot(1-\delta_{0})^{-(d+2)}\cdot\|g\|_{M^{1}_{s}}^{2}

\qquad\delta\in(-\delta_{0},\delta_{0})

The

2.6

\displaystyle\partial_{\delta}G_{\delta}(z)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\delta}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{W}(g)\left(\frac{z-\lambda}{\sqrt{1+\delta}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\|S_{g,\alpha\Lambda}-S_{h,\alpha\Lambda}\|

\displaystyle=\|S_{g,(g-h),\alpha\Lambda}+S_{(g-h),h,\alpha\Lambda}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{g,(g-h),\alpha\Lambda}\|+\|S_{(g-h),h,\alpha\Lambda}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\text{rel}(\Lambda)\Big(\big(\|g\|_{M^{1}}+\|h\|_{M^{1}}\big)\|g-h\|_{M^{1}}\,.

The combination of both estimates yields the claimed estimate.
∎

We have now collected all necessary ingredients to prove our main two results on Gabor frame bounds.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Clearly, we may assume that
g\not=0
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
. As
M^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we may
choose
h=h_{\varepsilon}\in M^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
0<\|h\|_{M^{1}}\leq 2\|g\|_{M^{1}}
and

\text{rel}(\Lambda)\big(\|g\|_{M^{1}}+\|h\|_{M^{1}}\big)\|g-h\|_{M^{1}}\leq\varepsilon.

Subsequently, choose

\delta^{1/2}=\min\big\{\tfrac{1}{2},\big(\text{rel}(\Lambda)\|h\|_{M^{1}_{1}}^{2}\big)^{-1}\,\varepsilon\big\}.

For
|1-\alpha|<\delta
we therefore obtain

\alpha\in(3/4,5/4)\subset(3/4,2)
and

\text{rel}(\Lambda)\cdot\|h\|_{M^{1}_{1}}^{2}\cdot|1-\alpha|^{1/2}\leq\varepsilon.

This, together with Lemma
3.7
, implies

|\sigma_{\pm}(S_{g,\Lambda})-\sigma_{\pm}(S_{g,\alpha\Lambda})|\leq C\varepsilon,\quad\text{for }|1-\alpha|<\delta,

for a constant
C
that is independent of
g,h,\alpha
, and
\Lambda
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

For
1\leq s\leq 2
the result is proven in Proposition
3.5
,
while for
s>2
the claim follows from the case
s=2
(as
\|g\|_{M^{1}_{s}}\geq\|g\|_{M^{1}_{2}}
).
Let therefore
0<s<1
and choose
\varepsilon=|1-\alpha|^{\eta}
, for some
\eta>0
to be specified. A combination of Lemma
3.7
and Lemma
2.3
shows that

\big|\sigma_{\pm}(S_{g,\Lambda})-\sigma_{\pm}(S_{g,\alpha\Lambda})\big|\lesssim\text{rel}(\Lambda)\,\big(|1-\alpha|^{s\eta}+|1-\alpha|^{1/2-2(2-2s)\eta}\big)\,\|g\|_{M^{1}_{s}}^{2}.

We choose
\eta
such that the two exponents are equal, i.e., such that

s\eta=1/2-2(2-2s)\eta.

Solving for
\eta
yields
\eta=\tfrac{1}{2(4-3s)}
.
Consequently,

\big|\sigma_{\pm}(S_{g,\Lambda})-\sigma_{\pm}(S_{g,\alpha\Lambda})\big|\lesssim\text{rel}(\Lambda)\,|1-\alpha|^{\tfrac{s}{2(4-3s)}}\,\|g\|_{M^{1}_{s}}^{2},

which concludes the proof.
∎

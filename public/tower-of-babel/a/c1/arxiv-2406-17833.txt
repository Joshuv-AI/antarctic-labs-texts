Classification of Certain Regular Subalgebras of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( n , ℂ ) \mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C}) up to Conjugacy
By - Shreya Dhar

Abstract
In this paper we will be classifying some regular upper-triangular subalgebras of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( n , ℂ ) \mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C}) up to conjugacy by matrices in SL ​ ( n , ℂ ) \text{SL}(n,\mathbb{C}) . We do so for dimension 2, codimension 1, and codimension 2 subalgebras. We prove some general results for codimension k k . The approach we use reduces an abstract classification problem to a combinatorial one, which we solve through a mixture of inductive and computational approaches.

General Theorems, Lemmas, and Definitions

2.1
Lie Bracket

The general Lie bracket is as follows:

[E_{ij},E_{kl}]=E_{ij}E_{kl}-E_{kl}E_{ij}=\delta_{jk}E_{il}-\delta_{li}E_{kj}

(1)

[E_{ij},H_{k}]=E_{ij}E_{kk}-E_{ij}E_{k+1,k+1}-E_{kk}E_{ij}+E_{k+1,k+1}E_{ij}=\delta_{jk}E_{ik}-\delta_{j,k+1}E_{i,k+1}-\delta_{ki}E_{kj}+\delta_{k+1,i}E_{k+1,j}

[H_{i},H_{k}]=0

The last equation follows from the fact that diagonal matrices commute.

Observation 1
.

Let the star matrix
N
represent the maximal nilpotent subalgebra of a regular-algebra, i.e. it is spanned by the set of all
E_{ij}
’s in the sub-algebra. Then

represents the commutator of the subalgebra. Further,
N^{2^{k}}
represents the
k^{th}
element in the derived series.

2.2

is not a basis element in a nilpotent subalgebra,

then

Suppose

where

and

is a subalgebra, one of the elements in these pairs is not in the Lie algebra, otherwise

and

if

is not a basis element in a nilpotent subalgebra,

then

This is the lowest upper bound. If we remove all of the

2.3
Lemmas on Invariance

Let
L
and
M
be regular upper-triangular sub-algebras of
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
spanned by
E
and
H
, which are conjugate by

Lemma 3
.

(Nilpotent Invariance) Nilpotent elements conjugate to nilpotent elements.

Corollary 2
.

For
E_{ij}\in L
then
g
conjugates it to a linear combination of
E_{kl}\in M
.

Corollary 3
.

Two conjugate subalgebras have the same dimension for the maximal nilpotent subalgebra.

Definition 3
.

We define nilpotent dimension as the dimension of the maximal nilpotent subalgebra of a Lie algebra.

Lemma 4
.

(Dimension of Commutator)
[L,L]
is conjugate to
[M,M]
and so the dimension of the commutator is invariant under conjugation.

Corollary 4
.

(Derived Series under Conjugacy)
The derived series of
L
conjugates to the derived series of
M
. Thus, the dimension of the derived series is invariant under conjugation.

Definition 4
.

The max rank of a Lie algebra is the maximum rank (the column/row rank of a matrix) that an element in the algebra can obtain. Similarly, the min rank is the minimum rank that a non-zero element of the algebra can obtain.

Lemma 5
.

The min and max rank are invariant under conjugation.

Lemma 6
.

(Left action on column vectors)
Two conjugate subalgebras
L,M
send
\mathbb{C}^{n}
to subspaces of the same dimension. That is, when a star matrix acts on a column vector with entries in
\mathbb{C}
, the number of non-zero entries in

is the same as the number of non-zero entries in

where
A
and
B
are the star matrices for
L,M
respectively.

Lemma 7
.

(Right action on row vectors)
Two conjugate subalgebras
L,M
have the same right action on row vectors.

Definition 5
.

We define
column rank
as the dimension of subspace that the left action of the Lie algebra sends
\mathbb{C}^{n}
to. Similarly, we define
row rank
as the dimension of subspace that the right action of the Lie algebra sends
\mathbb{C}^{n}
to.

Corollary 5
.

The column/row rank of the derived series are equal, i.e., the column/row ranks of
{L}^{i}
is the same as the column/row ranks of
{m}^{i}
(here

(Conjugacy of

.

(Invariance of max rank) Let
M
be a Lie algebra. Let
x\in M
. Then
\max_{y\in M}(\text{adj}(x)y)
and
\min_{y\in M}(\text{adj}(x)y)
is invariant under conjugation.

9
,

doesn’t conjugate to

(Classification of algebras with nilpotent part

Theorem 4
.

(Classification of
\frac{n(n+1)}{2}-1

regular subalgebras of dimension

\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})

.

There are
n-1

.

L_{i,i+1}=\text{span }{((E\setminus\{E_{i,i+1})\cup H\}}

for

.

n-1
subalgebras without
H_{i}

for

As

, obtaining an
\frac{n(n+1)}{2}-1

and check whether it is still an algebra. We can remove any

. There are
n-1

is a subalgebra. To remove

from

are the only subalgebras obtained by removing an

∎

Theorem 7

(Classification of
\frac{n(n+1)}{2}-2

Without 2
H_{i}
:

Without

and
H_{i}

(n-1)(n-1)

Without 2
E_{ij}

Analyticity and supershift with regular sampling Thanks: The first and second author are supported by MUR grant Dipartimento di Eccellenza 2023-2027.
F. Colombo, I. Sabadini, D. C. Struppa, A. Yger

Abstract
. The notion of supershift (in itself a generalization of the notion of superoscillation arising in quantum mechanics) expresses the fact that the sampling of a function in an interval allows to compute the values of the function far from the interval. In this paper we study the relation between supershift and real analyticity. We use a classical result due to Serge Bernstein to show that real analyticity for a complex valued function implies a strong form of supershift. On the other hand, we use a parametric version of a result by Leonid Kantorovitch to show that the converse is not true. We additionally study the stability of functions that satisfy suitable supershift requirements under multiplication by complex numbers or primitivization.



Keywords
. Real analyticity; supershift; superoscillation; sampling.

42C10, 26E05

1.
Introduction

In their seminal paper
[
1
]
, Aharonov and his collaborators
introduced a new concept in quantum mechanics, the so-called
weak
value
, and as a consequence they obtained some apparently paradoxical
results (for a complete review of the notion of weak value see
[
17
]
). One of the
reasons why such results were possible is the discovery of a family of
functions from
{\mathbb{R}}
to
{\mathbb{C}}
with the surprising
property that they are completely determined, on the real axis, by their
values on a countable subset of a finite interval in
\mathbb{R}
. The
first example of such a function is the exponential function
f(x,a):a\longrightarrow e^{iax}
regarded as a function of the real variable

a
, with
x
as a parameter. Specifically, it is possible to write the
function
a\longmapsto e^{iax}
, for arbitrary large values of
a
, in
terms of the values
e^{i\lambda_{N,\nu}x}
with

\lambda_{N,\nu}=1-2\nu/N
for
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
0\leq\nu\leq N
, and
therefore
|\lambda_{N,\nu}|\leq 1.
To show that this is the case, one
considers the function

(1.1)

F_{N}(x,a):=\big(\cos\frac{x}{N}+ia\sin\frac{x}{N}\big)^{N},

which (by simply using the Euler formulas for sine and cosine, and the
Newton binomial formula) can be rewritten as

F_{N}(x,a)=\sum_{\nu=0}^{N}C_{\nu}(N,a)e^{i(1-2\nu/N)x},

where

(1.2)

C_{\nu}(N,a):={\binom{N}{\nu}\Big(\frac{1+a}{2}\Big)^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{1-a}{2}\Big)^{\nu}}.

Since (
1.1
) immediately implies that
\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\,F_{N}(x,a)=e^{iax}
, we obtain that the value of the function
a\longmapsto f(x,a)=e^{iax}
for
a
arbitrarily large
is retrievable if one knows the values of the function on the
countable set of values
\lambda_{N,\nu}\in[-1,1]
.

Since in this particular example the variable
a
is the frequency of
the oscillating exponential function, now to be considered as a function
of
x
, this phenomenon is usually referred to as
superoscillation

and we call a sequence such as
\{x\longmapsto F_{N}(x,a)\}_{N\geq 1}
a

superoscillating sequence
, and the limit function a
superoscillating function.

The fact that large oscillations can be obtained by superposition of
much smaller oscillations, so that a highly oscillating function could
be seen as a limit of band-limited functions, led to a series of
surprising physical phenomena, some of which are described in

[
9
,
10
]
and were recently the subject of a more popular
discussion in
[
21
]
.

One of the first questions that Aharonov posed was whether this interesting phenomenon would be conserved if one evolves a superoscillating sequence according to the Schrödinger equation for the free particle. As we have shown in
[
5
,
6
]
, the answer is positive, indeed one can prove that
the solution to the Cauchy problem

i\frac{\partial\psi(x,t)}{\partial t}=-\frac{\partial^{2}\psi(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}},\ \ \ \ \psi(x,0)=F_{N}(x,a),

is given by

(1.3)

\psi_{N}(x,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}C_{k}(N,a)e^{i(1-2k/N)x}e^{-i(1-2k/N)^{2}t},

and that
\psi_{N}(x,t)
converges to
e^{iax-ia^{2}t}
uniformly on every compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

This result is of independent interest, but it also shows the existence of a simple case of superoscillations in two variables. This realization led to the development of an independent theory of superoscillations in several variables, see
[
4
,
7
]
.
Once the case of the free particle is settled, the next natural question is to evolve a superoscillatory sequence with different potentials. The authors have obtained a large number of results in this direction but even the next simplest case, namely the harmonic oscillator studied in
[
13
]
, highlights a completely new phenomenon. Indeed we proved that the Cauchy problem for the quantum harmonic oscillator

i\frac{\partial\psi(t,x)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+x^{2}\Big)\psi(t,x),\ \ \ \ \psi(0,x)=F_{N}(x,a)

has the solution

(1.4)

\psi_{N}(t,x)=(\cos t)^{-1/2}e^{-(i/2)x^{2}\tan t}\sum_{k=0}^{N}C_{k}(N,a)e^{ix(1-2k/N)/\cos t-(i/2)(1-2k/N)^{2}\tan t}

and when we take the limit for
N\to\infty
we get

\lim_{N\to\infty}\psi_{N}(t,x)=(\cos t)^{-1/2}e^{-(i/2)x^{2}\tan t}e^{-(i/2)a^{2}\tan t+iax/\cos t}.

As one can see, the function in (
1.4
) is not superoscillating as the function in (
1.3
). The phenomenon that arises is now the fact that a function can be described in points away from the origin if one knows its value in enough points near the origin. This phenomenon (of which superoscillations is the simplest example) is what we called supershift (a precise definition was given in
[
14
]
and will be repeated later on in Section 2). As it turned out, we were able to demonstrate this phenomenon in a very large number of cases, and we noticed that the possibility of doing so seemed to be a direct consequence of the analyticity of the functions for which supershift was to be demonstrated. This raises the very natural question of whether the two notions are indeed connected. In this paper we will show that in fact analyticity implies supershift (this result is already implied in some of our previous works) but that the converse is not true. This result goes full circle because one of Aharonov’s early remarks was that, in some naive sense,
the property of superoscillations can be considered akin to analyticity.
Indeed, an analytic function is completely determined by countably
many data at the origin (the value, and all of its derivatives) and a
superoscillating function is completely determined by countably many
data near the origin (its values in the points
\lambda_{N,\nu}
).

The plan of the paper is the following.
Section 2 contains the various definitions and refinements of the notions of superoscillation and supershift that we shall use later.
In Section
3
we show that the notion of analyticity is
stronger than a suitably formalized notion of supershift.
Specifically, in Theorem
3.4
, using a parametric reformulation of a classical result due to Serge Bernstein we
show that real analyticity for a complex valued function implies a strong form of supershift. On the other hand, we use a
parametric version of a result by
Leonid Kantorovitch to show, see Theorem

3.8
, that the converse is not true.
In Section 4 we additionally study the stability of functions that satisfy suitable
supershift requirements under essential operations such as
complex multiplication or primitivization (Theorems

4.1
and
4.2
).

2.
Preliminary results

In this section we will offer some preliminary definitions and results that will be useful in the sequel. To begin with,
let

(2.1)

H=\begin{bmatrix}&h_{1,0},...,h_{1,\nu(1)}\\
&h_{2,0},h_{2,1},...,h_{2,\nu(2)}\\
&\vdots\\
&h_{N,0},h_{N,1},h_{N,2},...,h_{N,\nu(N)}\\
&\vdots\end{bmatrix}

be a collection of real numbers (interpreted as frequencies) which all belong to the closed interval
[-1,1]
with
h_{N,\nu}\geq h_{N,\nu+1}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
0\leq\nu\leq\nu(N)-1
.
The simplest example of such double index sequence can be obtained by setting
\nu(N)=N
and
h_{N,\nu}=1-2(\nu+1)/N
,
0\leq\nu\leq N-1
.

Definition 2.1
.

A sequence of generalized trigonometric polynomials
(T_{H,N})_{N\geq 1}
of the form

{T_{H,N}}(x)=\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{\nu(N)}C_{H,\nu}(N)\,\exp(ixh_{N,\nu}),\quad({\rm with}\ C_{H,\nu}(N)\in\mathbb{C}\quad{\rm for}\ N\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\ 0\leq\nu\leq\nu(N))

is called an
H
-sequence of generalized trigonometric polynomials
.

Given an open subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, an
(A,H)
-sequence of generalized trigonometric polynomials

\{T_{H,N}[a]:a\in A\}_{N\geq 1}
is by definition an
A
-parametrized
H
-sequence of generalized trigonometric polynomials such that each complex amplitude
C_{H,\nu}(N,a)

depends continuously on

a\in A
, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.

Definition 2.2

(Superoscillating sequence)
.

An
(A,H)
-sequence of generalized trigonometric polynomials is said to be
superoscillating
if there are two continuous functions
g_{H}\penalty\ :A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
C_{H}:A\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that both
V=\{a\in A\,:\,C_{H}(a)\not=0,\ |g_{H}(a)|>1\}
and the open subset
U
of points
x\in\mathbb{R}
about which

(2.2)

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}T_{H,N}[a]\,(x)=C_{H}(a)\,\exp(i\,g_{H}(a)\,x),

locally uniformly with respect to
(a,x)
, are non-empty open subsets respectively of
A
and

\mathbb{R}
. The set
U\cap V
is then called
the superoscillating subset
of the
(A,H)
-superoscillating sequence
\{T_{N}[a]\,:\,a\in A\}_{N\geq 1}
.

Remark 2.3
.

What motivates the terminology here is the fact that, for any
a\in A
, each

T_{H,N}[a]
is a low-band signal, with frequencies in
[-1,1]
, while the signal

x\mapsto C_{H}(a_{0})\,e^{ig_{H}(a_{0})x}
is a true elementary oscillating signal with frequency
g_{H}(a_{0})
outside

[-1,1]
as soon as

a_{0}\in\{a\in A\,:\,C_{H}(a)\not=0,\ |g_{H}(a)|>1\}\not=\emptyset.

For a further insight to superoscillations see also
[
11
]
.
Throughout this paper, we shall consider
(\mathbb{R},H)
-superoscillating sequences subordinate to the choice of
g={\rm Id}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
C\equiv 1
.

Generalizing the original description of Aharonov, it is easy to realize prototypical examples of superoscillating
(\mathbb{R},H)
-sequences of generalized trigonometric polynomials as we describe below.

Definition 2.4

(Regular sampling)
.

We will call regular sampling of the frequency interval
[-1,1]
the sampling defined by
H^{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}=[h_{N,\nu}^{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}]
where

\boldsymbol{\epsilon}=(\epsilon_{N})_{N\geq 1}
is a sequence of elements in
[0,1[
which tends to
0
when
N
tends to infinity and

(2.3)

h_{N,\nu}^{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}=1-2\,\Big(\frac{\nu+\epsilon_{N}(N-\nu)}{N}\Big),\ 0\leq\nu\leq N\penalty\ .

Remark 2.5
.

The
(\mathbb{R},H^{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}})
-sequence
\{T_{N}^{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}[a]\,:\,a\in\mathbb{R}\}_{N\geq 1}
, where

(2.4)

\begin{split}T_{N}^{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}[a]\,(x)&=\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\,\Big(\frac{1+a}{2}\Big)^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{1-a}{2}\Big)^{\nu}\exp\big(ih_{N,\nu}^{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\,x\big)\\
&=\exp(i\,\epsilon_{N}x)\,\Big(\frac{1+a}{2}\,\exp\Big(ix\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{N}}{N}\Big)+\frac{1-a}{2}\,\exp\Big(-ix\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{N}}{N}\Big)\Big)^{N}\\
&=\exp(i\,\epsilon_{N}x)\,\Big(\cos\Big(x\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{N}}{N}\Big)+i\,a\,\sin\Big(x\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{N}}{N}\Big)\Big)^{N}\end{split}

is superoscillating on
U=\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.6
.

When the array
H
in (
2.1
) is such that

\forall\,N\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\ \nu(N)=N
and so
{\rm card}\,\{h_{N,\nu}\,:\,\nu=0,...,\nu(N)\}=N+1
,
the sequence of Lagrange-Hermite interpolators (with respect to the parameter
a
)

(2.5)

\Big\{x\longmapsto T_{H,N}^{\rm Lag}[a](x):=\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\Big(\prod_{\nu^{\prime}\not=\nu}\frac{a-h_{N,\nu^{\prime}}}{h_{N,\nu}-h_{N,\nu^{\prime}}}\Big)\,e^{ih_{N,\nu}x}\,:\,a\in\mathbb{R}\Big\}

is also superoscillating since

(2.6)

\begin{split}\Big|e^{iax}-T_{H,N}^{\rm Lag}[a](x)\Big|=\frac{\prod_{\nu=0}^{N}|a-h_{N,\nu}|}{(N+1)!}\sup_{|\xi|\leq\max(1,|a|)}\Big|\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\Big)^{N+1}[e^{iax}](\xi)\Big|\leq\frac{\big((|a|+1)|x|\big)^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}\end{split}

for any
(a,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
according to the expression of the reminder term in the Lagrange interpolation formula, see
[
3
, Theorem 2.2]
. Such a superoscillating sequence
\{T_{H,N}^{\rm Lag}[a]\,:\,a\in\mathbb{R}\}_{N\geq 1}
could be interpreted as corresponding to an irregular sampling of the low-frequency domain
[-1,1]
, which occurs as soon as an infinitely many rows
H_{N_{\iota}}
of the array

H
are such that the function
\nu\in\{0,...,N_{\iota}-1\}\mapsto h_{N_{\iota},\nu}-h_{N_{\iota},\nu+1}>0
is not constant.

Let us introduce here the important concept of
Supershift Property
({\rm SP})_{\mathscr{F}}
.

Definition 2.7
.

(Supershift Property
({\rm SP})_{\mathscr{F}}
)
Let
A
be an open interval of
\mathbb{R}
, possibly
\mathbb{R}
itself, which contains
[-1,1]
and let
\psi:a\in A\longmapsto\psi_{a}\in\mathscr{F}
be a continuous map from

A
to a topological
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\mathscr{F}
. Let

\left\{{T_{H,N}}[\lambda](x)=\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{\nu(N)}C_{H,\nu}(N,\lambda)\,\exp(ixh_{N,\nu})\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\right\}_{N\geq 1}

be an

(\mathbb{R},H)
-superoscillating sequence. The map

\psi
is said to satisfy the
Supershift Property

({\rm SP})_{\mathscr{F}}
on
A
with respect to
\{T_{H,N}[\lambda]\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}_{N\geq 1}
if the sequence of functions

(2.7)

a\in A\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{\nu(N)}C_{H,\nu}(N,a)\,\psi_{h_{N,\nu}}\in\mathscr{F},\quad N=1,2,...

converges to
\psi_{a}
in the space of continuous functions
\mathcal{C}(A,\mathscr{F})
, with respect to the topology of uniform convergence on any compact subset, i.e.

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{\nu(N)}C_{H,\nu}(N,a)\,\psi_{h_{N,\nu}}=\psi_{a}.

Remark 2.8
.

The reader will notice that this definition reduces,
for
C_{\nu}(N,a)
as in
(
1.2
), to the original example of Aharonov, which can in fact be obtained by taking
A={\mathbb{R}}
, and
H=H^{\boldsymbol{0}}:=\{1-2\nu/N\,:\,N\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\ 0\leq\nu\leq N\}
.

Given a topological
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\mathscr{F}
, we also introduce
for continuous maps from an open interval
A\subset\mathbb{R}
with length strictly larger than
2(={\rm length}\,([-1,1])
) to
\mathscr{F}
, the so-called

Translation-Commuting Supershift Property

({\rm TCSP})_{\mathscr{F}}
with respect to an
(\mathbb{R},H)
-superoscillating sequence.

Definition 2.9
.

(Translation-Commuting Supershift Property
({\rm TCSP})_{\mathscr{F}}
)
Let
A
be as above and for
a\in A
, let
\psi_{a}\in\mathscr{F}
be a continuous map from

A
to
\mathscr{F}
. Let
\{T_{H,N}[\lambda]\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}_{N\geq 1}
be a

(\mathbb{R},H)
-superoscillating sequence. The continuous map

\psi
is said to satisfy the
\mathscr{F}
-Translation-Commuting Supershift Property
({\rm TCSP})_{\mathscr{F}}
on
A
with respect to the superoscillating sequence
\{T_{H,N}[\lambda]\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}_{N\geq 1}
if the sequence of functions

(2.8)

\left(\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{\nu(N)}C_{H,\nu}(N,a)\,\psi_{a^{\prime}+h_{N,\nu}}\right)\subset\mathscr{F},\quad N=1,2,...

defined for

(a,a^{\prime})\in\mathbb{A}:=\big\{(a,a^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times A\,:\,a^{\prime}+[-1,1]\subset A,\ a+a^{\prime}\in A\big\}

converges to
\psi_{a+a^{\prime}}
in
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{A},\mathscr{F})
with respect to the topology of uniform convergence on any compact subset of
\mathbb{A}
.

Observe that
({\rm TCSP})_{\mathscr{F}}
on an open interval
A
of
\mathbb{R}
with respect to
such an
(\mathbb{R},H)
superoscillating sequence
implies
({\rm SP})_{\mathscr{F}}
with respect to the same superoscillating sequence provided that
A
contains
[-1,1]
.

Remark 2.10
.

If
A=[-\alpha,\beta]
with
\alpha,\beta>1
, then the set
\mathbb{A}
can be equivalently described as

\mathbb{A}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ :1-\alpha\leq y\leq-1+\beta,\ -\alpha\leq x+y\leq\beta\}.

Let
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C})
be the space of entire functions and let us consider

(2.9)

{\rm Exp}(\mathbb{C})=\{\Phi\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C})\ :\ \exists\,R>0\quad{\rm such\ that}\quad\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}}e^{-R|z|}|\Phi(z)|=0\}.

For any
R>0
, let also consider the subspace of
{\rm Exp}(\mathbb{C})
defined by:

(2.10)

{\rm Exp}_{<R}(\mathbb{C})=\{\Phi\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C})\ :\ \exists\,\varepsilon\in\,]0,R]\quad{\rm such\ that}\quad\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}}e^{-(R-\epsilon)|z|}|\Phi(z)|<+\infty\}.

Both spaces are topological spaces with their AU-structures, see
for example
[
8
, Definition 4.1.10]
. We recall that the dual space of
{\rm Exp}_{<R}(\mathbb{C})

is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,R))

[
24

or also
[

.

Let
A
be an open interval of
\mathbb{R}
which contains
[-1,1]
. Among the
(\mathbb{R},H)
-superoscillating sequences,
those for which the sequence

(2.11)

\Big(z\in\mathbb{C}\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{\nu(N)}C_{N,\nu}(N,a)e^{ih_{N,\nu}z}\Big)_{N\geq 1}

converges locally uniformly with respect to

in

(or at least in
{\rm Exp}_{<R}(\mathbb{C})
for some

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a topological
\mathbb{C}
-vector space and let
{\boldsymbol{K}}:{\rm Exp}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathscr{F}
be a continuous operator.
Then the continuous map

(2.12)

\psi_{{\boldsymbol{K}}}\penalty\ :a\in A\longmapsto{\boldsymbol{K}}(z\mapsto e^{iaz}),

(or, more briefly,

)
satisfies
({\rm SP})_{\mathscr{F}}
on
A
with respect to the
(\mathbb{R},H)
-superoscillating sequence
which is considered here. The same argument applies to the continuous operator

{\boldsymbol{K}}_{R}:{\rm Exp}_{<R}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathscr{F}

and the continuous map

\psi_{{\boldsymbol{K}}_{R}}\penalty\ :a\in A\longmapsto{\boldsymbol{K}}_{R}(e^{iaz})\ {\rm when}\ A\subset]-R,R[.

Moreover, since the Taylor sequence

(2.13)

\Big(\sum\limits_{\kappa=0}^{M}(ia)^{\kappa}\,\frac{z^{\kappa}}{\kappa!}\Big)_{M\geq 0}

converges in
{\rm Exp}(\mathbb{C})

\begin{split}\psi_{{\boldsymbol{K}}}(a)&=\lim\limits_{M\rightarrow+\infty}\sum\limits_{\kappa=0}^{M}{\boldsymbol{K}}\Big(\frac{(iz)^{\kappa}}{\kappa!}\Big)\,a^{\kappa}\end{split}

Let us now consider a specific Cauchy problem for the Schrödinger equation.
Let
U
be an open interval in
{\mathbb{R}}
, and consider for
a\in{\mathbb{R}}

(2.16)

\Big(i\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}-V(x)\Big)\psi(t,x)=0,\quad\psi(0,x)=e^{iax},

where
x\in U\mapsto V(x)
is a suitable real continuous potential. The most classical examples of open sets
U\subset\mathbb{R}
which we shall consider are
U=\mathbb{R}
or
U=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. When a Green function
K_{V}\penalty\ :(x,y,t)\in U\times U\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\longmapsto K_{V}(x,y,t)
is explicitly known for such Cauchy problem, the evolution of the initial datum

x\mapsto\psi(0,x)=e^{iax}
, when
a\in\mathbb{R}
, is described (at least formally), by

{\psi_{a}(t,x)}=\int_{U}e^{iay}\,K_{V}(x,y,t)\,dy.

The classical potentials
V
we have in mind are
quadratic
. For example

U=\mathbb{R}
,
V(x)=x^{2}/2
(quantum harmonic oscillator), see

[
8
, §6.4]
or also
[
16
, §5.4 & 6]
;
U=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
V(x)=\varpi/x^{2}
with
\varpi>0
an absolute constant (centrifugal potential), see
[
16
, §5.3]
, or possibly the more general case where
x\mapsto V(t,x)
is quadratic in
x
as a polynomial, see
[
26
]
. Under some requirements imposed to the explicit expression of the Green kernel

K_{V}
, among them the essential fact that
it extends as a function of
y
to an holomorphic function of
z
in a single (or double) sector with vertex
0
about the real axis
, see
[
2
, Assumption 3.1]
, the formal expression

\int_{U}e^{iay}\,K_{V}(x,y,t)\,dy

can be realized concretely as a regularized integral in the Fresnel sense, see

[
16

.

We consider the case when

Proposition 3.3

\begin{split}(\Delta_{(1-\epsilon_{N})/N}^{+})^{0}[\Psi]\,(b^{\prime})&=\Psi(b^{\prime})\\

We will also need in this section the following parametric version of another important result due to Serge Bernstein

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

\{G_{\iota}\,:\,\iota\in I\}

\iota^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}

\Big(z\in D_{\mathbb{C}}(c,\rho)\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}z^{\nu}(1-z)^{N-\nu}\,G_{\iota}\Big(\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}

in
\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathbb{C}}(c,\rho))

is uniform with respect to the parameters

\iota
and
\iota^{\prime}

Proof.

We follow
the proof presented in
[
20
, Theorem 4.1.1]
, slightly adapting it in order to incorporate the parametric setting. Let
\rho_{0}<\rho
and, for each
\iota\in I
, let
G_{\iota}(z)=\sum_{\kappa=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{\iota,\kappa}(z-c)^{\kappa}
, with
\gamma_{\iota,\kappa}\in\mathbb{C}
the Taylor coefficients of
G_{\iota}
about
c
. It follows from the fact that the family
G=\{G_{\iota}\,:\,\iota\in I\}
is bounded in

\mathcal{H}(D_{\mathbb{C}}(c,\rho))

\forall\,\iota\in I,\ \sum_{\kappa=0}^{\infty}|\gamma_{\iota,\kappa}|\,\rho_{0}^{\kappa}\leq M.

For each
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\in[0,1[^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
which tends to
0
when
N
tends to infinity,
for each

and
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}

Let us introduce the (for the time being formal) generating function of the sequence of polynomial maps

(B_{N,\kappa}^{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(z))_{\kappa\geq 0}

(3.12)

(u,z)\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\kappa=0}^{\infty}B_{N,\kappa}^{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(z)\,\frac{u^{\kappa}}{\kappa!}=\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}z^{\nu}(1-z)^{N-\nu}\Big(\sum\limits_{\kappa=0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1-\epsilon_{N}}{N}\,\nu-c\Big)^{\kappa}\frac{u^{\kappa}}{\kappa!}\Big)\\

As proved in
[

\big|z(1-c)^{\kappa}+(1-z)(-c)^{\kappa}\big|\leq|z-c|^{\kappa},

and for any

.
Therefore, for

(raised to the power

For any
\iota\in I
,
\iota^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}
,

\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}z^{\nu}(1-z)^{N-\nu}G_{\iota}\Big(\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)=\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}z^{\nu}(1-z)^{N-\nu}\Big(\sum\limits_{\kappa=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{\iota,\kappa}\,\Big(\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}-c\Big)^{\kappa}\Big)\\

is uniformly bounded in

,
the convergence being uniform with respect both to
\pm\eta

\iota^{\prime}
.
Since

according to the definition of

, Theorem

When

Theorem

Remark 3.6

Definition 3.7

(Regular

(1)

with respect to any superoscillating sequence

, as in
(

) and (

), where
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}
is any sequence in

when

(2)

Given a family

of such sequences, such that the convergence of all sequences
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\iota^{\prime}}
towards

to

in

is uniform with respect to the index

.

It follows from from Theorem
3.5
that such theorem that real analytic functions, provided they can be extended as holomorphic functions in sufficiently large complex domains containing

. Such is the case for example for restrictions to

-supershifts inherit some analyticity property. We will deduce from a result of Leonid Kantorovich

.

Theorem 3.8

\Big(z\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}z^{\nu}(1-z)^{N-\nu}g^{\pm}\Big(b^{\prime}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{N}}{N}\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}

\boldsymbol{\epsilon}

\iota^{\prime}

Proof.

For each

, let
\mathscr{L}_{c}
be the lemniscate

\mathscr{L}_{c}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,|z|^{c}|1-z|^{1-c}=c^{c}(1-c)^{1-c}\},

V^{-}(c)
(respectively

(respectively
1

V^{-}_{\eta}=\bigcap_{c\in[1/2-\eta,1/2+\eta]}V^{-}(c),\qquad{\rm and}\qquad V^{+}_{\eta}=\bigcap_{c\in[1/2-\eta,1/2+\eta]}V^{+}(c).

at infinity uniformly with respect to
\iota^{\prime}
.
It follows from Theorem

that the two sequences of polynomial maps

\begin{split}&\Big(z\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}z^{\nu}(1-z)^{N-\nu}G^{-}\Big(b^{\prime}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}\\
&\Big(z\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}z^{\nu}(1-z)^{N-\nu}G^{+}\Big(b^{\prime}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}\end{split}

converge on any compact subset of the complex plane to respectively

G^{-}(b^{\prime}+z)
and
G^{+}(b^{\prime}+z)
, uniformly
both with respect to

and the index
\iota^{\prime}
.
Fix a compact

\begin{split}z\in K^{+}&\Longrightarrow\Big(\frac{|z|}{c}\Big)^{c}\Big(\frac{|1-z|}{1-c}\Big)^{1-c}\leq q_{\eta}(K^{+})<1\\
&\Longrightarrow\frac{c}{|z|}\,\frac{|1-z|}{1-c}\leq\Big(q_{\eta}(K^{+})\,\frac{c}{|z|}\Big)^{1/(1-c)}\leq 1,\end{split}

see the proof of

Then one has for each

and

that

(3.23)

\Big|\sum\limits_{\{\nu\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,\nu\leq(1/2-b^{\prime})N\}}\binom{N}{\nu}z^{\nu}(1-z)^{N-\nu}H\Big(b^{\prime}+\nu\,\Big(\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)\Big|\\
\leq\sup_{[-b^{\prime},1+b^{\prime}]}|H|\,\sum\limits_{\{\nu\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,\nu\leq(1/2-b^{\prime})\,N\}}\binom{N}{\nu}\,(1/2-b^{\prime})^{\nu}(1/2+b^{\prime})^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{|z|}{1/2-b^{\prime}}\Big)^{\nu}\Big(\frac{|1-z|}{1/2+b^{\prime}}\Big)^{N-\nu}\\

Since for any

converges uniformly towards

We now prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
A
be an open interval of
\mathbb{R}
with length strictly larger than
2
and

\psi:A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a regular supershift in
A
. Let
\theta
be a smooth test-function with support in
[0,\epsilon]
, where
\epsilon<R-2
and
A_{\epsilon}=\{a\in A\,;\,a-\epsilon\in A\}
. Then the function

\psi*\theta:a\in A_{\epsilon}\longmapsto\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\theta(\alpha)\,\psi(a-\alpha)\,d\alpha=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta(\alpha)\,\psi(a-\alpha)\,d\alpha

is a smooth regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershift in
A_{\epsilon}
.

Proof.

Let

\mathbb{A}_{\epsilon}:=\{(a,a^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times A_{\epsilon}\,:\,a^{\prime}+[-1,1]\subset A_{\epsilon},\ a+a^{\prime}\in A_{\epsilon}\}

and
\{(\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N})_{N\geq 1}\,:\,\iota^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}\}
be a family of elements in

[0,1[^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
which all tend to
0
at infinity, uniformly with respect to
\iota^{\prime}
.
For any
(a_{0},a^{\prime}_{0})\in\mathbb{A}_{\epsilon}
and any
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, one has for

(a,a^{\prime})
in a sufficiently small neighborhood
U_{a_{0},a^{\prime}_{0}}
of
(a_{0},a^{\prime}_{0})

(3.25)

\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\theta(\alpha)\,\Big(\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\Big(\frac{1+a}{2}\Big)^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{1-a}{2}\Big)^{\nu}\psi\Big(a^{\prime}-\alpha+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)\,d\alpha\\
=\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\Big(\frac{1+a}{2}\Big)^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{1-a}{2}\Big)^{\nu}(\psi*\theta)\Big(a^{\prime}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big).

Since
\psi
is a regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershift on
A
, the sequence of functions

\Big((a,a^{\prime})\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\Big(\frac{1+a}{2}\Big)^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{1-a}{2}\Big)^{\nu}\psi\Big(a^{\prime}-\alpha+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1},

considered as indexed by the parameter
\alpha\in[0,\epsilon]
,
converges uniformly on any compact subset of
U_{a_{0},a^{\prime}_{0}}
towards

(a,a^{\prime})\longmapsto\psi(a+a^{\prime}-\alpha),

the convergence being also uniform both with respect to
\alpha\in[0,\epsilon]
and to the index

\iota^{\prime}
. As a consequence of (
3.25
), the sequence of functions

\Big((a,a^{\prime})\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\Big(\frac{1+a}{2}\Big)^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{1-a}{2}\Big)^{\nu}(\psi*\theta)\Big(a^{\prime}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}

converges uniformly on any such compact subset of
U_{a_{0},a^{\prime}_{0}}
towards

(a,a^{\prime})\longmapsto\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\theta(\epsilon)\psi(a+a^{\prime}-\alpha)\,d\alpha=(\psi*\theta)(a+a^{\prime}).

This shows that
\psi*\theta
is a regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershift in
\mathbb{A}_{\epsilon}
.
It is smooth since it inherits the smoothness of the regularizing test-function
\theta
.
∎

We deduce then from Theorem

the following result.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
g^{\pm}
be the continuous complex valued function with
1/2
as single non-real analyticity point which is defined
by (
3.19
) and (
3.20
).
There exists an open interval
A_{0}=]-1-\rho_{0},1+\rho_{0}[\subset\mathbb{R}
containing
[-1,1]
such that
a\in A_{0}\longmapsto\psi_{0}(a)=g^{\pm}((1+a)/2)
is a regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershift in
A_{0}
.
As a consequence, the fact that
\psi
is a smooth regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershift on some open interval

A\subset\mathbb{R}
with diameter strictly larger than
2
does not imply in general that
\psi
is real analytic on
A
.

Proof.

The fact that the first assertion implies the second one is an immediate consequence of Lemma

3.9
. One can choose two entire functions
G^{-}
and
G^{+}
such that
(
3.19
) is fulfilled and a smooth regularization function
\theta

such that
\psi=\psi_{0}*\theta
shares with
\psi_{0}
the following property, namely that there exists at least one point of non-real analyticity in
]-1,1[
.
It remains then to prove the first assertion. Let
\eta>0
,
V_{\eta}^{-}
and
V_{\eta}^{+}
as in Theorem

3.8
. Since the family of continuous functions

\big\{x\in[0,1]\longmapsto g^{\pm}(b^{\prime}+x)\,:\,b^{\prime}\in[-\eta,\eta]\}

is uniformly Lipschitz, hence admits a uniform modulus of continuity

[0,1]
, classical facts about Bernstein’s approximation of continuous functions on
[0,1]
, see for example
[
20
, §1.6]
, imply that for each
b^{\prime}\in[-\eta,\eta]
, the sequence of polynomial functions

\Big(b\in[0,1]\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}g^{\pm}\Big(b^{\prime}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{N}}{N}\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}

converges uniformly on
[0,1]
towards
b\longmapsto g^{\pm}(b+b^{\prime})
, the convergence being uniform both with respect to
b^{\prime}\in[-\eta,\eta]
and to the index
\iota^{\prime}
. If we choose

and

shows that the same result holds on any compact subset of

.
∎

There exist smooth regular

4.
Operations preserving supershift

Since it is not immediate to establish when a function has the regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershift property,
we conclude this paper by analyzing which operations preserve this property.
The following theorems mention two of them.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given an open interval
A
of
\mathbb{R}
with diameter strictly larger than
2
, the class of regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershifts on
A
is stable by multiplication by the map
a\in A\longmapsto a\in\mathbb{C}
, hence by any complex valued polynomial map

a\in A\longmapsto p(a)\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Let
\{(\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N})_{N\geq 1}\,:\,\iota^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}\}
be a family of elements in

[0,1[^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
which all tend to
0
at infinity, uniformly with respect to
\iota^{\prime}
.
Let
B=\Upsilon^{-1}(A)
and
\Psi=\psi\circ\Upsilon
.
An easy computation shows that if
\Phi
is the function
\Phi:b\in B\longmapsto b\,\Psi(b)

then, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
N\geq 2
and any

(b,b^{\prime})\in\mathbb{B}=\{(b,b^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times B\,:\,b^{\prime}+[0,1]\subset B,\ b+b^{\prime}\in B\},

one has

(4.1)

\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}\Phi\Big(b^{\prime}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\\
=(1-\epsilon_{N})\,b\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N-1}\binom{N-1}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-1-\nu}\Psi\Big(b^{\prime}+\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N-1}^{[1]}}{N-1}\Big)\\
+b^{\prime}\,\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}\Psi\Big(b^{\prime}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big),

where

(4.2)

1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N-1}^{[1]}=\frac{N-1}{N}(1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}).

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{B}
and
\chi_{K}>0
such that

b^{\prime}+b+\tau\in B
for any
(b,b^{\prime})\in K
and
0\leq\tau\leq\chi_{K}
.
Since
\psi
is a regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershift, there exists, for any

\varepsilon>0
, an integer
N_{K,\varepsilon}
such that

N\geq N_{K,\varepsilon}\\
\Longrightarrow\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle((b,b^{\prime}),\tau)\in K\times[0,\chi_{K}]}}{{\iota^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}}}}\Big|\Psi(b^{\prime}+\tau+b)-\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N-1}\binom{N-1}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-1-\nu}\Psi\Big(b^{\prime}+\tau+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N-1}^{[1]}}{N-1}\Big)\Big|\leq\varepsilon.

One has in particular for
N\geq\max(N_{K,\varepsilon},1/\chi_{K})
that

\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b,b^{\prime})\in K}}{{\iota^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}}}}\Big|\Psi\Big(b^{\prime}+\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}+b\Big)-\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N-1}\binom{N-1}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-1-\nu}\Psi\Big(b^{\prime}+\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N-1}^{[1]}}{N-1}\Big)\Big|\leq\varepsilon.

Observe that the sequences of functions

\begin{split}&\Big((b,b^{\prime})\in\mathbb{B}\longmapsto\Psi\Big(b^{\prime}+\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}+b\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}\\
&\Big((b,b^{\prime})\in\mathbb{B}\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}\Psi\Big(b^{\prime}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}\end{split}

converge both locally uniformly on
\mathbb{B}
towards
(b,b^{\prime})\longmapsto\Psi(b+b^{\prime})
on
K
, the convergence being uniform with respect to the index
\iota^{\prime}
. Comparing the limits when
N
tends to infinity of both sides of
(
4.1
), one gets that the sequence of functions

\Big((b,b^{\prime})\in\mathbb{B}\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}\Phi\Big(b^{\prime}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}

converges locally uniformly on
\mathbb{B}
towards
(b,b^{\prime})\longmapsto\Phi(b+b^{\prime})
,
the convergence being uniform with respect to the index
\iota^{\prime}
. After the rescaling

b=(1+a)/2
, one gets that
a\in A\longmapsto(2a-1)\psi(a)
is a regular

\mathbb{C}
-supershift on
a
, so is
a\in A\longmapsto a\,\psi(a)
since
\psi
already is. The proof of Theorem
4.1
is thus completed.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Given an open interval
A
of
\mathbb{R}
with diameter strictly larger than
2
, the class of regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershifts on
A
is stable by primitivization, namely, if
\psi:A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-regular supershift and

a_{0}\in A
, then

a\in A\longmapsto\int_{a_{0}}^{a}\psi(\alpha)\,d\alpha

is a
\mathbb{C}
-regular supershift.

Proof.

Let
\psi
be a regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershift in
A
,
B=\Upsilon^{-1}(A)
and
\Psi=\psi\circ\Upsilon
, where, as usual,

\Upsilon(a)=2a-1\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\{(\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N})_{N\geq 1}\,:\,\iota^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}\}
be a family of elements in

[0,1[^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
which all tend to
0
at infinity, uniformly with respect to
\iota^{\prime}
.
If
\mathbb{B}=\{(b,b^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times B\,:\,b^{\prime}+[0,1]\subset B,\ b+b^{\prime}\in B\}
, the sequence of functions

\Big((b,b^{\prime})\in\mathbb{B}\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}\Psi\Big(b^{\prime}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}

converges locally uniformly on
\mathbb{B}
towards
(b,b^{\prime})\longmapsto\Psi(b+b^{\prime})
, the convergence being uniform with respect to the index
\iota^{\prime}
. Let us call (P) such property. Let
b_{0}^{\prime}\in B
such that
b_{0}^{\prime}+[0,1]\subset B
. Proving that any primitive of
\Psi
in
A
remains a regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershift amounts to prove that the function

b\in B\longmapsto\Phi(b)=\int_{b_{0}^{\prime}}^{b}\Psi(\beta)\,d\beta

inherits from
\Psi
the property (P). Theorem
4.2
will then follows after the rescaling

b=\Upsilon^{-1}(a)=(1+a)/2
. We first observe that the sequence of functions

\Big((b,b^{\prime})\in\mathbb{B}\longmapsto\int_{b_{0}^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}\Big(\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}\Psi\Big(\beta+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)\,d\beta\Big)_{N\geq 1}

converges locally uniformly on
\mathbb{B}
towards

(b,b^{\prime})\longmapsto\int_{b_{0}}^{b^{\prime}}\Psi(\beta+b)\,d\xi=\Phi(b+b^{\prime})-\Phi(b+b^{\prime}_{0}).

Therefore, if one proves that the sequence of functions

(4.3)

\Big(b\in\{b\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,b^{\prime}_{0}+b\in B\}\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}\,\Phi\Big(b^{\prime}_{0}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}

converges locally uniformly on
-b^{\prime}_{0}+B=\{b\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,b^{\prime}_{0}+b\in B\}

towards
b\longmapsto\Phi(b+b^{\prime}_{0})
, the convergence on each compact subset being
uniform with respect to the index
\iota^{\prime}
, the fact that
\Phi
inherits from
\Psi
the property (P)
will follow. One has on
-b^{\prime}_{0}+B
that for any
N\geq 2
,

(4.4)

\frac{d}{db}\Big(\sum_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\,b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}\Phi\Big(b^{\prime}_{0}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)\\
=(1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N})\,\sum_{\nu=0}^{N-1}\binom{N-1}{\nu}\,b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-1-\nu}\,\frac{\Phi\Big(b^{\prime}_{0}+(\nu+1)\,\displaystyle{\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}}\Big)-\Phi\Big(b^{\prime}_{0}+\nu\,\displaystyle{\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}}\Big)}{\displaystyle{\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}}}\\
=(1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N})\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N-1}\binom{N-1}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}\Psi\Big(b^{\prime}_{0}+\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\,\xi+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N-1}^{[1]}}{N-1}\Big)\Big)\,d\xi,

where
\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N-1}^{[1]}
is defined in terms of
\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}
as in (
4.2
). Let
K^{\circ}
be a compact subset of
-b^{\prime}_{0}+B
,

\chi_{K^{\circ}}>0
such that
b+b^{\prime}_{0}+\tau\in B
for any
\tau\in[0,\chi_{K^{\circ}}]
,
and
\varepsilon>0
. It follows from the fact that

\Psi
has the property (P) that there exists
N_{K^{\circ},\varepsilon}
that

N\geq N_{K^{\circ},\varepsilon}\\
\Longrightarrow\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b,\tau)\in K^{\circ}\times[0,\chi_{K^{\circ}}]}}{{\iota\in I^{\prime}}}}\Big|\Psi(b^{\prime}_{0}+\tau+b)-\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N-1}\binom{N-1}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}\Psi\Big(b^{\prime}_{0}+\tau+\nu\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N-1}^{[1]}}{N-1}\Big)\Big|\leq\varepsilon.

One has in particular for
N\geq\max(N_{K^{\circ},\varepsilon},1/\chi_{K^{\circ}})
such that

\Big|\Psi\Big(b^{\prime}_{0}+\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\,\xi+b\Big)-\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N-1}\binom{N-1}{\nu}b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}\Psi\Big(b^{\prime}_{0}+\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\,\xi+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N-1}^{[1]}}{N-1}\Big)\Big|\leq\varepsilon

for any
(b,\xi)\in K^{\circ}\times[0,1]
and any
\iota\in I^{\prime}
.
It then follows from (
4.4
) that the sequence of functions

\Big(b\in K^{\circ}\longmapsto\frac{d}{db}\Big(\sum_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\,b^{\nu}(1-b)^{N-\nu}\Phi\Big(b^{\prime}_{0}+\nu\,\frac{1-\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}}{N}\Big)\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}

converges uniformly on
K
towards
b\longmapsto\Psi(b^{\prime}_{0}+b)
, the convergence being uniform with respect to the index
\iota^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}
. This implies that the sequence of corresponding primitives vanishing at

0
, namely the sequence of functions (
4.3
), converges locally uniformly on

-b^{\prime}_{0}+B
towards
\Phi(b+b^{\prime}_{0})
, which concludes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

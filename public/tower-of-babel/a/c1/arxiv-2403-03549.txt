1.
Introduction

Suppose that
G
is a finite abelian group and
\emptyset\neq A,B\subseteq G
. Consider the sumset

A+B:=\{a+b:\,a\in A,\ b\in B\}.

For a prime
p
, let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denote the cyclic group of order
p
. The classical Cauchy-Davenport theorem asserts that for any
\emptyset\neq A,B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

|A+B|\geq\min\{p,|A|+|B|-1\}.

(1.1)

In fact, according to the well-known Kneser theorem, (
1.1
) can be extended to any finite abelian group
G
with
|G|>1
as follows:

|A+B|\geq\min\{p(G),|A|+|B|-1\}

(1.2)

for any non-empty subsets
A,B
of
G
, where
p(G)
denotes the least prime divisor of
|G|
.
Using an easy induction, we can get

|A_{1}+\cdots+A_{k}|\geq\min\{p(G),|A_{1}|+\cdots+|A_{k}|-k+1\}

(1.3)

for any
\emptyset\neq A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\subseteq G
.

On the other hand, for any
\emptyset\neq A\subseteq G
, consider the restricted sumset

k^{\wedge}A:=\{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}:\,a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in A,\ a_{i}\neq a_{j}\text{ for }i\neq j\}.

In particular,
1^{\wedge}A=A
and
0^{\wedge}A=\{0\}
.

In
[
4
]
, Erdős and Heilbronn conjectured that for any prime
p
and
\emptyset\neq A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

|2^{\wedge}A|\geq\min\{p,2|A|-3\}.

(1.4)

This conjecture was solved by Dias da Silva and Hamidoune
[
2
]
with the help of the exterior algebra. In general they proved that

|k^{\wedge}A|\geq\min\{p,k|A|-k^{2}+1\}

(1.5)

for any
\emptyset\neq A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. See
[
1
]
for an alternative proof based on the polynomial method.

In
[
6
]
, Károlyi considered the generalization of the Erdős-Heilbronn conjecture for finite abelian groups. Combining the polynomial method and a combinatorial induction, Károlyi obtained that

|2^{\wedge}A|\geq\min\{p(G),2|A|-3\}

(1.6)

for any non-empty subset
A
of a finite abelian group
G
with
|G|>1
. An alternative proof of (
1.6
) was given in
[
5
]
. For further
developments on restricted sumsets, see
[
3
]
,
[
7
]
and the references therein.

The purpose of our paper is to generalize (
1.5
) in the same spirit.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite abelian group with
|G|>1
. For any
\emptyset\neq A\subseteq G
,

|k^{\wedge}A|\geq\min\{p(G),k|A|-k^{2}+1\}.

(1.7)

2.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

We prove Theorem
1.1
by induction on
|G|
. According to (
1.5
), it holds whenever
|G|
is a prime,
so we suppose that
|G|
is composite, and the result has been proved for smaller values of
|G|
. The result is trivial for
k=0,1

or if
|A|=k
. It is also covered for
k=2
by (
1.6
), so in the sequel we will assume that
|A|\geq k+1\geq 4
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
with
[G:H]=p(G)
. For each
a\in G
, consider the coset
\bar{a}=a+H.
Let

\bar{A}=\{\bar{a}:\,a\in A\}
,
m=|\bar{A}|
, and write

A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}+A_{i}),

where
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A
and
A_{i}\subseteq H
with
a_{i}-a_{j}\not\in H
for any distinct
i,j
. Without loss of generality, assume that

|A_{1}|\geq|A_{2}|\geq\cdots\geq|A_{m}|.

Note that
|k^{\wedge}A|\geq|k^{\wedge}\bar{A}|
, so below assume that
k^{\wedge}\bar{A}\subsetneq G/H.

Since (
1.7
) follows from the induction hypothesis on
k^{\wedge}\bar{A}
immediately when
|A|=m
, we will also assume that
|A_{1}|\geq 2.

First we consider the relatively easy case when
m\geq k+1
.
For each
1\leq j\leq k+1
, let

b_{j}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k+1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{i}

and let

U_{j}=A_{1}+\cdots+A_{j-1}+A_{j+1}+\cdots+A_{k+1}.

Note that
\bar{b}_{1},\ldots,\bar{b}_{k+1}
are distinct elements of
G/H
. Hence

b_{1}+U_{1},\ldots,b_{k+1}+U_{k+1}

are disjoint subsets of
{k^{\wedge}}A
.
Additionally, for each
1\leq j\leq k+1
, by (
1.3
) we have

|b_{j}+U_{j}|=\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k+1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}A_{i}\bigg|\geq\min\bigg\{p(H),\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k+1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}|A_{i}|-k+1\bigg\}.

(2.1)

Furthermore, when
m\geq k+2
, for any
1\leq s\leq m-k-1
, let

V_{s}=(k^{\wedge}\{\bar{a}_{1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{k+s+1}\})\setminus(k^{\wedge}\{\bar{a}_{1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{k+s}\}).

Obviously,

V_{1}=(k^{\wedge}\{\bar{a}_{1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{k+2}\})\setminus\{\bar{b}_{1},\ldots,\bar{b}_{k+1}\}.

By (
1.5
) and the assumption that
k^{\wedge}\bar{A}\subsetneq G/H
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s}|V_{j}|=

\displaystyle|(k^{\wedge}\{\bar{a}_{1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{k+s+1}\})\setminus\{\bar{b}_{1},\ldots,\bar{b}_{k+1}\}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle k(k+s+1)-k^{2}+1-(k+1)=ks

for each
1\leq s\leq m-k-1
.
On the other hand, if
\bar{c}\in V_{s}
, then
\bar{c}=\bar{a}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\bar{a}_{i_{k}}
where
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq k+s+1
. It follows that

|(k^{\wedge}A)\cap(\bar{c}+H)|\geq|A_{i_{1}}+\cdots+A_{i_{k}}|\geq|A_{i_{1}}|\geq|A_{s+2}|

since
i_{1}\leq s+2
.
Note that if
x_{1}\geq\cdots\geq x_{n}\geq 0
and the real numbers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\geq r
for every
1\leq r\leq n
,
then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}x_{i}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}.

Hence we have

\sum_{j=1}^{m-k-1}|(k^{\wedge}A)\cap(V_{j}+H)|\geq\sum_{j=1}^{m-k-1}|V_{j}|\cdot|A_{j+2}|\geq k\sum_{j=1}^{m-k-1}|A_{j+2}|.

(2.2)

In summary, using (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we obtain that

\displaystyle|k^{\wedge}A|\geq\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}|b_{j}+U_{j}|+\mathbf{1}_{m\geq k+2}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{m-k-1}|(k^{\wedge}A)\cap(V_{j}+H)|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{p(H),\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k+1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}|A_{i}|-k+1\bigg)+\mathbf{1}_{m\geq k+2}\cdot\bigg(k\sum_{j=1}^{m-k-1}|A_{j+2}|\bigg)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{p(H),k\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}|A_{j}|-k^{2}+1+\mathbf{1}_{m\geq k+2}\cdot\bigg(k\sum_{j=k+2}^{m}|A_{j}|\bigg)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\{p(G),k|A|-k^{2}+1\},

where the indicative function
\mathbf{1}_{m\geq k+2}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }m\geq k+2,\\
0,&\text{if }m=k+1.\end{cases}

It remains to study the case
m\leq k
, which is more technical. Without loss of generality, assume that

|A_{1}|=\cdots=|A_{t_{1}}|>|A_{t_{1}+1}|=\cdots=|A_{t_{2}}|>|A_{t_{2}+1}|=\cdots>|A_{t_{l-1}+1}|=\cdots=|A_{t_{l}}|,

where
1\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{l}=m
. In addition, set
t_{0}=0
. We start with constructing some integers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}\geq 1
and
r
,
t
with
1\leq r\leq t\leq m
as follows.

Consider the sequence

\displaystyle a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m},\underbrace{a_{t_{1}},a_{t_{1}-1},\ldots,a_{1},a_{t_{1}},\ldots,a_{1},\ldots,a_{t_{1}},\ldots,a_{1}}_{(|A_{t_{1}}|-|A_{t_{2}}|)\text{ terms }a_{t_{1}},\ldots,a_{1}},

\displaystyle\underbrace{a_{t_{2}},a_{t_{2}-1},\ldots,a_{1},a_{t_{2}},\ldots,a_{1},\ldots,a_{t_{2}},\ldots,a_{1}}_{(|A_{t_{2}}|-|A_{t_{3}}|)\text{ terms }a_{t_{2}},\ldots,a_{1}},

\displaystyle\cdots\cdots,

\displaystyle\underbrace{a_{t_{l}},a_{t_{l}-1},\ldots,a_{1},a_{t_{l}},\ldots,a_{1},\ldots,a_{t_{l}},\ldots,a_{1}}_{(|A_{t_{l}}|-1)\text{ terms }a_{t_{l}},\ldots,a_{1}}.

Note that the length of the above sequence is

m+\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}t_{i}\cdot(|A_{t_{i}}|-|A_{t_{i+1}}|)+t_{l}\cdot(|A_{t_{l}}|-1)=t_{1}|A_{t_{1}}|+\sum_{i=2}^{l}(t_{i}-t_{i-1})\cdot|A_{t_{i}}|=|A|\geq k+1.

Let
a_{1}^{*},\ldots,a_{k+1}^{*}
be the first
k+1
terms of the above sequence,
then
a_{1}^{*},\ldots,a_{k+1}^{*}
must be of the form

\displaystyle a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m},a_{t_{1}},\ldots,a_{1},\ldots,a_{t_{1}},\ldots,a_{1},

\displaystyle\cdots\cdots,

\displaystyle a_{t_{s-1}},\ldots,a_{1},\ldots,a_{t_{s-1}},\ldots,a_{1},

\displaystyle\underbrace{a_{t_{s}},\ldots,a_{1},\ldots,a_{t_{s}},\ldots,a_{1}}_{h\text{ terms }a_{t_{s}},\ldots,a_{1}},a_{t_{s}},\ldots,a_{r},

where
1\leq r\leq t_{s}
for some
1\leq s\leq l
,
0\leq h<|A_{t_{s}}|-|A_{t_{s+1}}|
if
1\leq s\leq l-1
, and
0\leq h<|A_{t_{s}}|-1
if
s=l
.
Now set
t:=t_{s}
, then we have
1\leq r\leq t
and

h\leq|A_{t}|-2.

(2.3)

For each
1\leq i\leq m
, let

n_{i}=|\{1\leq j\leq k+1:\,a_{j}^{*}=a_{i}\}|.

Clearly we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}=k+1,

(2.4)

and when
t<i\leq m
, we have

n_{i}=1.

(2.5)

For any
i\in(t_{u-1},t_{u}]
with
1\leq u\leq s
, if
1\leq i<r
, then

n_{i}=1+h+\sum_{j=u}^{s-1}(|A_{t_{j}}|-|A_{t_{j+1}}|)=1+h+|A_{t_{u}}|-|A_{t_{s}}|=1+h+|A_{i}|-|A_{t}|,

and if
r\leq i\leq t
, then

n_{i}=2+h+|A_{i}|-|A_{t}|.

That is,

|A_{i}|-n_{i}=\begin{cases}|A_{t}|-h-1,&\text{if }1\leq i<r,\\
|A_{t}|-h-2,&\text{if }r\leq i\leq t.\end{cases}

(2.6)

Then by (
2.3
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we have

n_{i}\leq|A_{i}|\text{ for each }r\leq i\leq m,

(2.7)

n_{i}\leq|A_{i}|-1\text{ for each }1\leq i<r,

(2.8)

and

|A_{i}|-n_{i}=|A_{j}|-n_{j}+1\text{ for each }1\leq i<r\text{ and }r\leq j\leq t.

(2.9)

Furthermore, for
1\leq i\leq t
and
t<j\leq m
, we have

|A_{i}|-n_{i}\geq|A_{t}|-h-2=|A_{t_{s}}|-h-2\geq|A_{t_{s+1}}|-1\geq|A_{j}|-n_{j},

(2.10)

since now
s<l
.

For non-negative integers
l_{1},\ldots,l_{m}
, let

S_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{m}}={l_{1}}^{\wedge}A_{1}+{l_{2}}^{\wedge}A_{2}+\cdots+{l_{m}}^{\wedge}A_{m}\subseteq H.

For each
1\leq j\leq m
, let

b_{j}=(n_{j}-1)a_{j}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq m\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}n_{i}a_{i},

and let

U_{j}=S_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{j-1},n_{j}-1,n_{j+1},\ldots,n_{m}}.

Since
n_{i}\leq|A_{i}|
, we know that
U_{j}\neq\emptyset
.
Clearly
b_{1}+U_{1},\ldots,b_{m}+U_{m}
are disjoint subsets of
k^{\wedge}A
. According to the induction hypothesis, we have

|n^{\wedge}A_{i}|\geq\min\{p(H),n|A_{i}|-n^{2}+1\}

for any
n\geq 0
and
1\leq i\leq m
.
Hence by (
1.3
),

\displaystyle|U_{j}|=\bigg|(n_{j}-1)^{\wedge}A_{j}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq m\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}{n_{i}}^{\wedge}A_{i}\bigg|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{p(H),(n_{j}-1)|A_{j}|-((n_{j}-1)^{2}-1)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq m\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}n_{i}|A_{i}|-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq m\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}(n_{i}^{2}-1)-m+1\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{p(H),(n_{j}-1)|A_{j}|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq m\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}n_{i}|A_{i}|-\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}^{2}+2n_{j}\bigg\}.

(2.11)

However, we need to find more elements lying in
k^{\wedge}A
. For any
I\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,t\}
with
|I|=t-r+1
,
we have

b_{j}-\sum_{i=r}^{t}a_{i}+\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}=(n_{j,I}-1)a_{j}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq m\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}n_{i,I}a_{i}

(2.12)

for
1\leq j\leq m
, where

n_{i,I}=\begin{cases}n_{i}-1,&\text{if }i\in[r,t]\setminus I,\\
n_{i}+1,&\text{if }i\in I\setminus[r,t],\\
n_{i},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for each
1\leq i\leq m
.
If
r\leq i\leq t
, then
n_{i}\geq 2
. So we always have
n_{i,I}\geq 1
. Furthermore, by (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we obtain that
n_{i,I}\leq|A_{i}|
for each
1\leq i\leq m
. Then

S_{n_{1,I},\ldots,n_{j-1,I},n_{j,I}-1,n_{j+1,I},\ldots,n_{m,I}}\neq\emptyset

(2.13)

for any
1\leq j\leq m
.

Let

d_{j}=b_{j}-\sum_{i=r}^{t}a_{i}

for
1\leq j\leq m
, and let

R=\{\bar{d}_{1},\ldots,\bar{d}_{m}\}+(t-r+1)^{\wedge}\{\bar{a}_{1},\bar{a}_{2},\ldots,\bar{a}_{t}\}\subseteq G/H.

Then

\displaystyle|R|

\displaystyle\geq\min\big\{p(G),m+((t-r+1)\cdot t-(t-r+1)^{2}+1)-1\big\}

\displaystyle=\min\big\{p(G),m+(t-r+1)\cdot(r-1)\big\}.

First we suppose
m+(t-r+1)(r-1)<p(G)
. Obviously
(t-r+1)(r-1)\geq 0
. If
(t-r+1)(r-1)\geq 1
, then there exist

1\leq j_{1},\ldots,j_{(t-r+1)(r-1)}\leq m

and

I_{1},\ldots,I_{(t-r+1)(r-1)}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,t\}

with
|I_{j}|=t-r+1
for

1\leq j\leq(t-r+1)(r-1)

such that the elements

\bar{c}_{1},\bar{c}_{2},\ldots,\bar{c}_{(t-r+1)(r-1)}\in R\setminus\{\bar{b}_{1},\ldots,\bar{b}_{m}\}

are distinct, where

c_{u}=d_{j_{u}}+\sum_{i\in I_{u}}a_{i}

for each
1\leq u\leq(t-r+1)(r-1)
.

Let

V_{u}=S_{n_{1,I_{u}},\ldots,n_{j_{u}-1,I_{u}},n_{j_{u},I_{u}}-1,n_{j_{u}+1,I_{u}},\ldots,n_{m,I_{u}}}

for
1\leq u\leq(t-r+1)(r-1)
. Thus in view of (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), we know that

b_{1}+U_{1},\ldots,b_{m}+U_{m},c_{1}+V_{1},\ldots,c_{(t-r+1)(r-1)}+V_{(t-r+1)(r-1)}

are disjoint non-empty subsets of
k^{\wedge}A
.

It follows from (
2
) and (
2.13
) that

\displaystyle|k^{\wedge}A|\geq\sum_{j=1}^{m}|U_{j}|+(t-r+1)(r-1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{p(H),\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigg((n_{j}-1)|A_{j}|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq m\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}n_{i}|A_{i}|-\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}^{2}+2n_{j}\bigg)+(t-r+1)(r-1)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{p(H),m\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}|A_{i}|-m\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}-|A|+(t-r+1)(r-1)\bigg\}.

(2.14)

Note that

\displaystyle m\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}|A_{i}|=

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\bigg)\cdot\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|A_{i}|\bigg)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq m\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}n_{i}(|A_{i}|-|A_{j}|).

So by (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle m\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}|A_{i}|-m\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}-|A|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k|A|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq m\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}n_{i}(|A_{i}|-|A_{j}|)-k^{2}+\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}-1\bigg)^{2}-m\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k|A|-k^{2}+1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq m\\
j>i\end{subarray}}(n_{i}-n_{j})\cdot(|A_{i}|-|A_{j}|)+\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\bigg)^{2}-m\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}^{2}.

(2.15)

Furthermore by (
2.5
), (
2.6
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we have

|A_{i}|-|A_{j}|\ \ \begin{cases}\geq n_{i}-n_{j}\geq 0,&\text{if }1\leq i\leq t<j\leq m\text{, or }t<i<j\leq m,\\
=n_{i}-n_{j}\geq 0,&\text{if }1\leq i<j<r\text{, or }r\leq i<j\leq t,\\
=n_{i}-n_{j}+1\geq 0,&\text{if }1\leq i<r\leq j\leq t.\end{cases}

Hence for any
1\leq i<j\leq m
, if
1\leq i<r\leq j\leq t
, we have

(n_{i}-n_{j})\cdot(|A_{i}|-|A_{j}|)=(n_{i}-n_{j})^{2}+(n_{i}-n_{j})

and
n_{i}-n_{j}\geq-1
, otherwise we have

(n_{i}-n_{j})\cdot(|A_{i}|-|A_{j}|)\geq(n_{i}-n_{j})^{2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq m\\
j>i\end{subarray}}(n_{i}-n_{j})(|A_{i}|-|A_{j}|)+\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\bigg)^{2}-m\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq m\\
j>i\end{subarray}}(n_{i}-n_{j})^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<r\\
r\leq j\leq t\end{subarray}}(n_{i}-n_{j})+\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\bigg)^{2}-m\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(m-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}^{2}-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq m\\
j>i\end{subarray}}n_{i}n_{j}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<r\\

j>i\end{subarray}}n_{i}n_{j}-m\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(r-1)\cdot(t-r+1).

(2.16)

Combining (

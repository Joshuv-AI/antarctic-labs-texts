Projectively Invariant Hardy Spaces on Domains with Corners
Benjamin Krakoff

Abstract
. For smoothly bounded, strongly ℂ \mathbb{C} -convex domains, one can use the Fefferman form or its variants to define projectively invariant norms on sections of holomorphic line bundles, producing a Hardy space. In two variables, we construct a projectively invariant measure on the singular part of a piece-wise smooth domain, and show that positivity of this invariant coincides with a notion of strong ℂ \mathbb{C} -convexity that is compatible with Cauchy-Fantappié-Leray kernels, and thus define projectively invariant Hardy spaces as in the smooth case.



1.
Introduction

Given a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}
,
\Omega
is
\mathbb{C}
-linearly convex
if the complement of
\Omega
is a union of complex hyperplanes. If
\Omega
has
C^{2}
-boundary
S
, this is equivalent to the complex tangent hyperplanes lying to one side of
\Omega
, in which case the second fundamental form is positive semi-definite on the complex tangent hyperplane and
\Omega
is also
\mathbb{C}
-convex. If additionally these complex tangent planes intersect
S
precisely at the point of tangency, we say
\Omega
(or
S
) is
strictly

\mathbb{C}
-convex. Finally, if the second fundamental form is strictly positive definite on the maximal complex subspace, then we say
\Omega
(or
S
) is
strongly

\mathbb{C}
-convex.

For strongly
\mathbb{C}
-convex
\Omega
, it is known how to construct projectively invariant Hardy spaces that satisfy many of the properties familiar from the one variable theory. Using the Fefferman form, or Barrett’s preferred measure, one can construct projectively invariant norms on sections of line bundles over
S
, and thus define invariant
L^{2}
and Hardy spaces. For holomorphic sections, one can use Cauchy-Fantappié-Leray integrals to reconstruct values on
\Omega
from the values on the boundary
S
. In this context, these Cauchy-Fantappié-Leray kernels are simply the Leray transform, and one can make sense of the corresponding singular integrals to turn the Hardy spaces into a reproducing kernel Hilbert space.

Our goal in this paper is to define projectively invariant Hardy spaces on a pseudoconvex domain
\Omega
with piece-wise smooth boundary
S
in 2 variables. To achieve this, we first define an appropriate notion of strict
\mathbb{C}
-convexity in
n
variables defined in terms of a generalization of complex tangents, and use Cauchy-Fantappié-Leray integrals to produce a holomorphic reproducing kernel. This reproducing kernel is the standard Leray kernel on the smooth part of
S
and gives positive mass to the singular part of
S
. Next restricting our attention to 2 variables, we define an appropriate bundle-valued invariant which is non-negative when the domain is
\mathbb{C}
-convex, and when this invariant is positive we show
\Omega
satisfies some local notion of strong
\mathbb{C}
-convexity. Lastly, we use this invariant to produce a norm and a corresponding Hardy space
H^{2}(\Omega)
on
S
and show that, via the reproducing kernel, one can recover the values of a holomorphic section
f
via its corresponding element in
H^{2}(\Omega)
.

2.
Projective Transformations and Line Bundles

2.1.
The Bundles
O_{E}(j,k)
and
\mathcal{O}_{E}(j,0)

We closely follow the expositions in
[
Bar15
]
section 2 and
[
APS04
]
section 3.2. Let
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
have projective coordinates
{\bf z}=[z_{0}:\ldots:z_{n}]
.
z=(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{0}},\ldots,\frac{z_{n}}{z_{0}})
be standard affine coordinates, setting
\hat{z}_{j}=\frac{z_{j}}{z_{0}},\ j\neq 0
.

Let
T:\mathbb{CP}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{n}
be a projective transformation. Choose an invertible matrix
(M_{i,j})_{i,j=0}^{n}
with
\det(M)=1
which descends to
T
. Any two choices of
M
differ by a
n+1
root of unity, and for the formulas we care about the ambiguity will wash out. In the standard affine coordinates,

T(\hat{z}_{1},\ldots,\hat{z}_{n})=\frac{(M_{1,0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}M_{1,j}\hat{z}_{j},\ldots,M_{n,0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}M_{n,j}\hat{z}_{j})}{M_{0,0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}M_{0,j}\hat{z}_{j}}.

Given a set
E\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}
and integers
j,k
, let
O_{E}(j,k)
denote the space of continuous functions on the cone over
E
satisfying

F(\lambda z_{0},\ldots,\lambda z_{n})=\lambda^{j}\overline{\lambda^{k}}F(z_{0},\ldots z_{n}).

In the standard affine coordinates, we can identify
F
with the continuous function
f
on
E
, via
f(\hat{z}_{1},\ldots,\hat{z}_{n})=F(1,\hat{z}_{1},\ldots,\hat{z}_{n})
and obtain the following transformation law

(T^{*}f)(z)=(M_{0,0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{0,i}\hat{z}_{i})^{j}\overline{(M_{0,0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{0,i}\hat{z}_{i})^{k}}f(T(z)).

If
E
is open,
\mathcal{O}_{E}(j,0)
will denote the space of holomorphic sections on
E
, and if
E
is closed
\mathcal{O}_{E}(j,0)
will denote the space of sections holomorphic in some neighborhood of
E
. With this convention, expressions like
z_{j}
can be thought of as sections of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(1,0)
. Given
F\in O_{E}(j,k)
, we have well-defined expressions
\overline{F}\in O_{E}(k,j),|F|^{2}\in O_{E}(j+k,j+k)
, etc. More generally, if
G\in O_{E}(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})
then
FG\in O_{E}(j,k)\otimes O_{E}(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})=O_{E}(j+j^{\prime},k+k^{\prime})
. When
j=k
, one can make sense of the section
F
being positive. Observe that a section of
O_{E}(0,0)
is nothing but a continuous function on
E
.

We will need the notion of a projectively invariant, bundle-valued form. For example, the differential form
dz:=d\hat{z}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge d\hat{z}_{n}
satisfies the transformation law

T^{*}dz=(M_{0,0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}M_{0,j}\hat{z}_{j})^{-n-1}dz

Thus, the expression
z_{0}^{n+1}dz
is a projectively invariant
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(n+1,0)
-valued
n
-form. More generally, an
L
-valued
k
-form on
M
is a section of
L\otimes\bigwedge^{k}T^{*}(M)
. Pulling back a section means writing as a product of a section
s
of
L
and
k
-form
\omega
and pulling back each.

2.2.
Duality and The Universal Cauchy-Fantappié-Leray Form

We give a brief overview of duality theory for
\mathbb{C}
-convex domains, since it is required for constructing the desired reproducing kernels in section
4
. See
[
APS04
]
Chapter 3 for more details of the theory.

The dual projective space
(\mathbb{CP}^{n})^{*}
denotes the set of complex hyperplanes in
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
. We may give
(\mathbb{CP}^{n})^{*}
homogeneous coordinates
{\bf w}=[w_{0}:\ldots:w_{n}]
by identifying
{\bf w}
with the hyperplane
\ell_{{\bf w}}:=\{{\bf z}|{\bf w}\cdot{\bf z}=0\}
. We may also use the notation
\langle{\bf w},{\bf z}\rangle
to indicate dot product, depending on which is easiest to read in context. The
incidence locus

I\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times(\mathbb{CP}^{n})^{*}
is the set of points incident to hyperplanes.

I:=\{({\bf z},{\bf w})|{\bf z}\cdot{\bf w}=0\}

Given a projective transformation
T
, there is a unique dual transformation
T^{-t}
preserving
I
. If
T
is given by the matrix
M
, then
T^{-t}
is given by the matrix
M^{-t}:={}^{t}M^{-1}
, explaining the notation. For
\Omega

\mathbb{C}
-convex with (not necessarily smooth) boundary
S
, denote by
S^{*}
the set of hyperplanes
{\bf w}
incident to
S
not intersecting
S^{*}
. When
S
is
C^{2}
and strongly
\mathbb{C}
-convex, this is the dual hypersurface studied in
[
Bar15
]
. Let
I_{S}
be the set of pointed hyperplanes incident to
S
but avoiding
\Omega
,

I_{S}:=I\cap(S\times S^{*})

On
I
, the
Universal Cauchy-Fantappié-Leray form
is the unique (up to constants) projectively invariant
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(n,0)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{(\mathbb{CP}^{n})^{*}}(n,0)
-valued
2n-1
form. On the coordinate patch
U_{j,k}:=\{z_{j}\neq 0,w_{j}\neq 0\}
, it is given by the expression

\omega_{CFL}=\frac{z_{j}^{n}w_{k}^{n}}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\frac{z_{l}}{z_{j}}d\left(\frac{w_{l}}{w_{k}}\right)\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{l=0}^{n}d\left(\frac{z_{l}}{z_{j}}\right)\wedge d\left(\frac{w_{l}}{w_{k}}\right)\right)^{n-1}

Keeping in mind the relation
{\bf z}\cdot{\bf w}=0
, it’s not hard to see that the Universal CFL form has the symmetry property
\omega_{CFL}({\bf z},{\bf w})=(-1)^{n}\omega_{CFL}({\bf w},{\bf z})
. This form, along with the following theorem, gives a recipe for constructing holomorphic reproducing kernels on
\Omega
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
APS04
]
, Thm. 3.1.7,
[
AY83
]
Lemma 3.3 and Corollary 3.6)

Let
\Omega
be
\mathbb{C}
-convex such that
S
and
S^{*}
are smooth cycles. For
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{CP}^{n}
, set
g_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}({\bf w}):=\frac{1}{\langle\boldsymbol{\tau},{\bf w}\rangle^{n}}
. Then for
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\Omega}}(-n,0),\ \boldsymbol{\tau}\in\Omega

f(\boldsymbol{\tau})=\int_{I_{S}}f\ g_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\ \omega_{CFL}

When
S
is
C^{2}
, the only complex hyperplane incident to
{\bf z}\in S
avoiding
\Omega
is the maximal complex subspace. Thus
{\bf w}
is a function of
{\bf z}
, and given a defining function
\rho
we have

{\bf w}=[-\langle\partial\rho,z\rangle:\partial\rho]

Plugging this expression into the integral of Thm.
2.1
and identifying sections of line bundles with functions produces the
Leray kernel

L\{f\}(\tau):=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{S}f(z)\frac{\partial\rho\wedge(\overline{\partial}\partial\rho)^{n-1}}{\langle\partial\rho,z-\tau\rangle^{n}}

and the theorem implies that the Leray kernel reproduces values of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
with controlled boundary values.

2.3.
The Fefferman Form, Barrett’s Preferred Measure

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}
be strongly
\mathbb{C}
-convex. Given a defining function
\rho
for
S
so that
\rho<0
on the pseudoconvex side, the
Fefferman form
(
[
Fef79
]
, p. 259) is a
2n-1
-form on
S
by the identity

\mu_{S,Fef}\wedge d\rho=2^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}(-1)^{n}\begin{vmatrix}0&\rho_{\overline{z_{j}}}\\
\rho_{z_{k}}&\rho_{z_{k},\overline{z_{j}}}\end{vmatrix}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\omega_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}

where
\omega_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
is the Euclidean area form on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Positivity of the Fefferman form follows from the positivity of the Levi form. Given a holomorphic transformation
F:S\rightarrow F(S)
we have

F^{*}(\mu_{F(S),Fef})=|\det(F^{\prime})|^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}\mu_{S,Fef}

In the specific case of
F
a projective transformation, the above identity implies that the expression
|z_{0}|^{2n}\mu_{S,Fef}
is a projectively invariant
O_{S}(n,n)
-valued
2n-1
form. For convenience, we will write just
\mu_{S,Fef}
to mean the bundle-valued form.

The Fefferman form defines an invariant norm on sections of
O_{S}(-n,0)
via

||f||^{2}=\int_{S}|f|^{2}\mu_{S,Fef}

Note that the integrand is an
O_{S}(-n,0)\otimes O_{S}(0,-n)\otimes O_{S}(n,n)
-valued
2n-1
form, in other words an ordinary
2n-1
form.

For studying
S
along with its dual hypersurface
S^{*}
, one should use Barrett’s preferred measure
\mu_{S,Bar}
, which is the Fefferman form multiplied by a projectively invariant scalar. This measure factorizes the Universal CFL form,

|\omega|=\mu_{S,Bar}^{\frac{1}{2}}\ \mu_{S^{*},Bar}^{\frac{1}{2}}

and so connects more optimally with the duality pairing between
\Omega
and its dual. See section 8 of
[
Bar15
]
.

Using a choice of projectively invariant norm
\mu
, one can complete
O_{S}(-n,0)
to the
L^{2}
-space
L^{2}(S,\mu)
, and define the corresponding Hardy space as the
L^{2}
-closure of restrictions of sections holomorphic in a neighborhood of
\Omega

H^{2}(\Omega,\mu):=\overline{\{f|_{S}|f\in\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\Omega}}(-n,0)\}}

For
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\Omega
, the Leray kernel directly recovers the values of functions in
H^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. For
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in S
, one must either take a non-tangential limit, or take the principal value of a singular integral. For proofs in one variable, see
[
Dur70
]
Chapter 1 and
[
Bel92
]
Chapter 5. In fact, more is true. These singular integrals give bounded maps
L^{2}(S,\mu)\rightarrow H^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. In one variable, see the seminal work of
[
Cal77
]
and
[
CMM82
]
. For proofs in several variables, as well as an excellent survey, see
[
LS13
]
.

3.
Piece-wise Smooth Domains

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}
be an open domain. The boundary
S
is
piece-wise smooth
if

(a)

There are finitely many disjoint, connected
C^{2}
hypersurfaces
H_{j}
so that
S\subset\cup H_{j}

(b)

For
k\leq n
, every set of
k
boundary hypersurfaces
H_{j}
meets complex transversely.

(c)

For
k>n
, every set of
k
boundary hypersurfaces meets real transversely.

(d)

For each
{\bf z}\in S
and sufficiently small neighborhood
U\ni{\bf z}
,
\Omega\cap U
is connected.

Item
c)
implies that no more that
2n
hypersurfaces can meet at one point. Given
k

edge

\sigma(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})
is where exactly these
k
hypersurfaces intersect and no others. It follows that
\sigma(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})
is a manifold of real dimension
2n-k
, which is totally real for
k\geq n
.

Definition 3.2
.

Strong and Weak Tangents
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}
be a pseudoconvex, piece-wise smooth domain.
{\bf w}
is a
strong tangent
to
{\bf z}\in\sigma(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})
if
\ell_{{\bf w}}
is the maximal complex subspace to one of the
H_{j_{l}}
at
{\bf z}
.
{\bf w}
is an
interior weak tangent
if

TC_{{\bf z}}(\ell_{{\bf w}}\cap\Omega)\subset\sigma(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})

{\bf w}
is a
weak tangent
if it is in the closure of the interior weak tangents and is not a strong tangent. Denote by
W_{{\bf z}}(\Omega)
the strong and weak tangents of
\Omega
at
{\bf z}
.

In particular, if a hyperplane
\ell_{{\bf w}}
through
{\bf z}
avoids
\Omega
then
{\bf w}
is a weak tangent.

Proposition 3.3
.

Dual Cycles

Let
\Omega
be a pseudoconvex domain with piece-wise smooth boundary,
{\bf z}\in\sigma(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})
and
\Delta^{k}
the standard
k-1
-simplex with
k
vertices. We can parameterize
W_{{\bf z}}(\Omega)
via

\displaystyle\Delta^{k}

\displaystyle\rightarrow W_{{\bf z}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto[-\sum_{l}t_{l}\partial\rho_{j_{l}}\cdot z:\sum_{l}t_{l}\partial\rho_{j_{l}}]

In particular, the vertices of
\Delta^{k}
map to the strong tangents at
{\bf z}
, and the union of the strong and weak tangents give a smooth cycle in
(\mathbb{CP}^{n})^{*}
.

Proof.

First we prove the statement for a convex cone.

Assume that
z=0
(so
{\bf z}=[1:0:\ldots:0]
), and suppose
\Omega=\{Im(\alpha_{l}\cdot z)<0,l=1,\ldots,k\}
. Then the weak tangents through
0
coincide with the positive cone over the
\alpha_{l}
,

C:=\{[0:w]|w=\sum t_{l}\alpha_{l},t_{l}\geq 0\}

Suppose we take a line of the form
\sum t_{l}\alpha_{l}\cdot z=0
,
t_{l}\geq 0
, then we have that
\sum t_{l}Im(\alpha_{l}\cdot z)=0
, so for at least one
l
,
Im(\alpha_{l}\cdot z)\leq 0
and the line avoids
\Omega
.

Now suppose that we take a line
\alpha
which is not in
C
. Since
C
is convex, by Hahn-Banach there is a real hyperplane
\ell_{z}:=\{w|Im(z\cdot w)=0\}
so that
Im(z\cdot C)<0
and
Im(z\cdot\alpha)>0
. The first inequality implies that
z\in\Omega
, and the second implies that
\alpha
cannot be written as a positive linear combination of the
\alpha_{l}
.

Now we prove the statement for general piece-wise smooth domains. Let
{\bf z}\in\sigma(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})
. For each
l=1,\ldots,k
, write

\rho_{j_{l}}=-Im(\alpha_{l}\cdot z)+h.o.t.

For each
l
, let

.

We prove this for a fixed

C
and

. Thinking of

Now suppose that we take

. Suppose there were a sequence

with
z_{s}\cdot w=0
,
z_{s}\in\Omega
and

that there is at least one

Now take
{\bf w}=[0:w],\ w
not in the closure of the positive cone over the

Completing the proof just requires shifting the origin back to the original point

∎

4.
A Leray Kernel

We want to use the notion of weak tangents along with Thm.
2.1
to formulate a holomorphic reproducing kernel (i.e. a Leray kernel) for piece-wise smooth, pseudoconvex domains. To this end, we say a piece-wise smooth
\mathbb{C}
-convex domain is
strictly
\mathbb{C}
-convex
if every weak tangent intersects
\overline{\Omega}
at exactly one point. Let
\pi:I_{S}\rightarrow S
be projection onto the
{\bf z}
-coordinates, keeping in mind that for piece-wise smooth strictly
\mathbb{C}
-convex domains,
\pi^{-1}({\bf z})=\{({\bf z},{\bf w})|{\bf w}\in W_{{\bf z}}(\Omega)\}
. Over each edge
\sigma:=\sigma(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})
, write
\omega_{CFL}=\pi^{*}\eta_{\sigma}\wedge\tau_{\sigma}
, where
\eta_{\sigma}
is a
2n-k
-form on
\sigma
, and
\tau
is a
k-1
-form on
\pi^{-1}(\sigma)
. A generalization of the Leray kernel is the push-forward of the kernel of Thm
2.1
by
\pi
, in other words

\mathscr{L}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\tau})=\left(\int_{W_{{\bf z}}(\Omega)}\frac{1}{\langle\boldsymbol{\tau},{\bf w}\rangle^{n}}\tau_{\sigma}\right)\eta_{\sigma}

That this operation is well-defined is a small adaptation of the argument on page 61 of
[
BT82
]
for bundle-valued forms, involving applying the decomposition
\omega_{CFL}=\pi^{*}\eta_{\sigma}\wedge\tau_{\sigma}
in two different sets of affinizations and checking that they differ by the correct factor.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a strictly
\mathbb{C}
-convex domain. Then on each edge
\sigma
of dimension
2n-k
,
\mathscr{L}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\tau})
defines a projectively invariant
O_{\sigma}(n,0)
-valued
2n-k
-form with the reproducing property

f(\boldsymbol{\tau})=\int_{S}f({\bf z})\mathscr{L}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\tau}),\ f\in\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\Omega}}(-n,0),\ \boldsymbol{\tau}\in\Omega

Note that this integral gives “weight” to edges of dimension
<2n-1
. We show that the edges of dimension
<n
have no weight (that is
\mathscr{L}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\tau})
vanishes) and compute an exact expression for edges of dimension
n
. Set
dz:=d\hat{z}_{1}\wedge\ldots d\hat{z}_{n}
, and set
dz_{[k]}
to be the same with the term
d\hat{z}_{k}
omitted.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
{\bf z}\in\sigma
. If
\dim(\sigma)<n

\mathscr{L}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\tau})=0
. If
\dim(\sigma)=n
, let
{\bf w}^{j},j=1,\ldots,n
denote the strong tangents at
{\bf z}

\det_{k}({\bf w}^{j})

Proof.

Proof of Thm.
4.1

Strict convexity implies that
\pi
is a submersion with fiber
W_{{\bf z}}(\Omega)
, so we may use Fubini to write

\int_{I_{S}}f({\bf z})\frac{1}{\langle\boldsymbol{\tau},{\bf w}\rangle^{n}}\omega_{CFL}=\int_{S}f({\bf z})\left(\int_{W_{{\bf z}}{(\Omega)}}\frac{1}{\langle\boldsymbol{\tau},{\bf w}\rangle^{n}}\tau_{\sigma}\right)\eta_{\sigma}

Projective invariance of
\mathscr{L}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\tau})
follows from projective invariance of
\omega_{CFL}
.
∎

For the proof of Prop.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega

{\bf z}\in\sigma:=\sigma(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})
with strong tangents
{\bf w}^{j}

\frac{\det_{0}({\bf w}^{j})}{\prod_{j}\langle\boldsymbol{\tau},{\bf w}^{j}\rangle}z_{0}^{n}dz

n

Proof.

Since the strong tangents are mapped to each other by projective transformations which preserve
\langle,\rangle

\frac{1}{\prod_{j}\langle\boldsymbol{\tau},{\bf w}^{j}\rangle}

n

Case 1:

\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}\det(N)^{\frac{-1}{n+1}}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\det(N)^{\frac{-1}{n+1}}N\end{array}\right),\ \ N\in GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})

Recalling that the induced transformation on the

\displaystyle T^{*}\left(\frac{\det_{0}({\bf w}^{j})}{\prod_{j}\langle\boldsymbol{\tau},{\bf w}^{j}\rangle}dz\right)

\displaystyle=\det(N)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\frac{\det_{0}({\bf w}^{j})}{\prod_{j}\langle{\bf w}^{j},\boldsymbol{\tau}\rangle}dz.

Case 2:

T^{-t}({\bf w})=[w_{0}:w_{1}-\lambda w_{0}:w_{2}:\ldots:w_{n}]

∎

5.
An Invariant Hardy Space

To construct a Hardy space, we would like a measure on
S
such that a holomorphic function can be constructed by its
L^{2}
-boundary values with respect to this measure. From Thm
4.1
, such a measure must give mass to edges of dimension
n
to
2n-1
. Following
[
Bar15
]
, we would also like the norm on this Hardy space to be projectively invariant. In this section, we produce such a measure when
n=2
.

When
n=2
, we need only consider edges of dimension two and three. For three-dimensional edges, there are several potential choices of measure, say Fefferman’s measure or Barrett’s preferred measure. Both measures are non-negative when
\Omega
is locally strictly
\mathbb{C}
-convex, positive when
\Omega
is strongly
\mathbb{C}
-convex, and satisfy the correct transformation law. We want to construct an analogous measure on edges of dimension
2
. To this end, we will say that a piece-wise smooth pseudoconvex domain is
locally strictly
\mathbb{C}
-convex
if for each
{\bf z}\in S
there is a a neighborhood
U\ni{\bf z}
such that for all
{\bf w}\in W_{{\bf z}}{(\Omega)}
,
\ell_{{\bf w}}\cap U\cap\overline{\Omega}=\{{\bf z}\}
.

A first guess of a projectively invariant measure would be
|z_{0}^{3}dz|
. This is an
O_{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}(3/2,3/2)
-valued 2-form, and so defines a norm on elements of
O_{S}(-3/2,0)
. However, elements of the Hardy space are going to be sections of
O_{S}(-2,0)
, and so we require a projectively invariant
O_{S}(2,2)
-valued 2-form. Fixing this requires finding a projectively invariant section
\eta\in O_{S}(j,j),\ j\neq 0
which is non-negative when
\Omega
is locally strictly
\mathbb{C}
-convex. The invariant measure will be given by
\mu:=\eta^{\frac{1}{2j}}|z_{0}^{3}dz|
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{CP}^{2}
be a piece-wise smooth, pseudoconvex domain with all boundary hypersurfaces strongly
\mathbb{C}
-convex, and let
\sigma
be a 2-dimensional edge. Then there is a projectively invariant section
\eta
of

O_{\sigma}(3/2,3/2)
which is non-negative at points where
\Omega
is locally strictly
\mathbb{C}
-convex. Furthermore, if
\eta>0
at a point
{\bf z}\in\sigma
then
\Omega
is locally strictly
\mathbb{C}
-convex at
{\bf z}
.

Proof.

First we describe how to construct
\eta
. Let
H_{1},H_{2}
be boundary hypersurfaces with
\sigma=H_{1}\cap H_{2}
. Given
{\bf z}\in\sigma
, make a change of coordinates so that
z=0
and the tangent cone of
\Omega
at

coincides with
\{Im(\hat{z}_{1})<0,Im(\hat{z}_{2})<0\}

\hat{z}_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}

we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle y_{1}=\alpha_{1}|\hat{z}_{2}|^{2}+Re(\beta_{1}\hat{z}_{2}^{2})+b_{1}x_{1}x_{2}+\gamma_{1}x_{1}y_{2}+a_{1}^{2}x_{1}^{2}+h.o.t.

and the same on
H_{2}

(5.2)

\displaystyle y_{2}=\alpha_{2}|\hat{z}_{1}|^{2}+Re(\beta_{2}\hat{z}_{1}^{2})+b_{2}x_{1}x_{2}+\gamma_{2}x_{2}y_{1}+a_{2}^{2}x_{2}^{2}+h.o.t.

Strong
\mathbb{C}

\alpha_{j}+|\beta_{j}|<0
. Along
\sigma
, we can expand the
y_{j}
in terms of the
x_{j}

\displaystyle y_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+b_{1}x_{1}x_{2}+c_{1}x_{2}^{2}+h.o.t.

\displaystyle y_{2}

\displaystyle=a_{2}x_{2}^{2}+b_{2}x_{1}x_{2}+c_{2}x_{1}^{2}+h.o.t.

It follows that
c_{j}=\alpha_{j}+Re(\beta_{j})<0

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
\lambda&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{C},\ S_{r}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\

0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\ r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\ W:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\

Using Mathematica, we compute how the normal forms change under application of these matrices. The calculations are included in the Appendix, and we record them here. Identifying the normal form above with the matrix

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}&c_{1}\\
a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}\end{pmatrix},

we compute

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}&c_{1}\\
a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}+Im(\lambda)&b_{1}&c_{1}\\
a_{2}&b_{2}+Im(\lambda)&c_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle S_{r}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}&c_{1}\\
a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}r&b_{1}&\frac{c_{1}}{r}\\
a_{2}&b_{2}r&c_{2}r^{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle W^{*}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}&c_{1}\\
a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}\\
a_{1}&b_{1}&c_{1}\end{pmatrix}

a_{1}=a_{2}=0
and
c_{2}=c_{1}=-1
. Now suppose every weak tangent, which are given by
{\bf w}=[0:t:1-t],t\in[0,1]

\Omega

{\bf z}

\sigma

\displaystyle 0\neq ty_{1}+sy_{2}

Expanding the right hand side in terms of

\displaystyle ty_{1}+sy_{2}=(-t^{2}s^{-1}b_{1}-t^{3}s^{-2}-tb_{2}-s)x_{1}^{2}+o(|x_{1}|^{3})

Note that the coefficients in the higher order terms are bounded as

Multiplying by

The function

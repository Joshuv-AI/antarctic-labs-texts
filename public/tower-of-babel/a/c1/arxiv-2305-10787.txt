A novel unbinned model-independent method to measure the CKM angle 𝜸 \gamma in 𝑩 ± → 𝑫 ​ 𝑲 ± B^{\pm}\to DK^{\pm} decays with optimised precision.
Jake Lane, Evelina Gersabeck, and Jonas Rademacker

Abstract
We present a novel unbinned method to combine B ± → D ​ K ± B^{\pm}\to DK^{\pm} and charm threshold data for the amplitude-model unbiased measurement of the CKM angle γ \gamma in cases where the D D meson decays to a three-body final state. The new unbinned approach avoids any kind of integration over the D D Dalitz plot, to make optimal use the available information. We verify the method with simulated signal data where the D D decays to K S ​ π + ​ π − K_{S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-} . Using realistic sample sizes, we find that the new method reaches the statistical precision on γ \gamma of an unbinned model-dependent fit, i.e. as good as possible and better than the widely used model-independent binned approach, without suffering from biases induced by a mis-modeled D D decay amplitude. Published in JHEP 09 (2023) 007 .

Introduction

The precision measurement of the
\mathit{CP}
violating phase
\gamma

The measurement of
\gamma
in
B^{\pm}\to DK^{\pm}
and related decays

;
Gronau:1990ra
;
Bondar:2002unpublished
;
GGSZ
;
Belle:2004bbr
;
Atwood:1996ci
has negligible theoretical uncertainty

. The current precision on

is dominated by LHCb, which measures
\gamma=\left(63.8^{+3.5}_{-3.7}\right)^{\circ}

LHCb:2022awq
;
LHCb:2021dcr
. The vast, clean datasets expected from the recently commissioned LHCb upgrade
Bediaga:1443882
and Belle II

will allow a sub

LHCbCollaboration:2776420
;
LHCb:2018roe
.
The parameter
\gamma
is therefore set to become the most precisely measured parameter in the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) description of
\mathit{CP}
violation in the quark sector
CofCKM
;
KMofCKM
, giving it a pivotal role in the search for new physics by over-constraining the Standard Model with precision measurements. To fully benefit from this potential, it is critical to control systematic uncertainties.

The measurement of
\gamma
in
B^{-}\to DK^{-}
and its
\mathit{CP}
conjugate is possible because the neutral
D
meson in this decay is a superposition of
D^{0}
and
\overline{D}{}^{0}
that depends on
\gamma

D\propto D^{0}+r_{B}e^{i(\delta_{B}-\gamma)}\overline{D}{}^{0}
, where
r_{B}\sim 0.1
and
\delta_{B}
is a
\mathit{CP}
-conserving phase induced by the strong interaction. The neutral
D

D^{0}
and
\overline{D}{}^{0}
.
For multibody decays of the
D

K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

\mathit{CP}
-violating phase

is obtained from analysing the amplitude structure of the
D
decay in both
B^{-}\to DK^{-}
and
B^{+}\to DK^{+}
transitions; this approach is known as the BPGGSZ method

;
GGSZ
;
Belle:2004bbr
.

In order to measure
\gamma
in this way,
the phase difference between the
D^{0}
and
\overline{D}{}^{0}
decay amplitude across the Dalitz plot needs to be known.
While this information can be obtained from amplitude models, these models have well-known shortcomings that make the phase information they provide unreliable, leading to significant, difficult-to-quantify systematic uncertainties. For this reason, model-independent methods are required to achieve the ultimate precision on
\gamma
. Similar considerations apply to measurements of
D
-mixing
Bondar:2010qs
.

Model-independent methods currently in use rely on integrating over all or parts (bins) of the multibody phase space of the
D
decay

;
Atwood:2003mj
;
Bondar:2005ki
;
Harnew:2013wea
;
Harnew:2014zla
;
Nayak:2014tea
. Two unbinned model-independent methods have been proposed recently. The method described in

and
\overline{D}{}^{0}
amplitudes is parameterised as a Fourier series. The authors of
Backus:2022xhi

In all cases, one can expect some information loss due to the integration or projection process involved. Here we present a new unbinned model-independent method that optimally uses the full information across the two-dimensional Dalitz plot of a three-body decay of the neutral
D
meson. Using the example of
D^{0}\to K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

\gamma
as a model-dependent method, without suffering from the associated model uncertainty.

2.1
Notation and conventions

We use the notation
A_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})
for the decay amplitude of the
D^{0}
meson to a final state
f
, at point
\mathbf{p}
in the
D\to f
Dalitz plot and
\overline{A}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{\overline{p}})
for that of the
\mathit{CP}
-conjugate process.

Our phase convention for the
\mathit{CP}
operator is such that
\mathit{CP}|D^{0}\rangle=|\overline{D}{}^{0}\rangle
, which is the usual practice in the context of beauty decays to charm. An alternative convention where
\mathit{CP}|D^{0}\rangle=-|\overline{D}{}^{0}\rangle
is widely used in the context of charm physics.

With our phase convention, and assuming the absence of
\mathit{CP}
violation in charm decays,
A_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})=\overline{A}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{\overline{p}})
.
For self-conjugate final states such as
K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
, which we will use for the simulation studies presented below,
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{\overline{p}}
are in the same Dalitz plot. We parameterise the
D^{0}\to K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
Dalitz plot with the usual variables
s_{+}=m^{2}(K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+})
and
s_{-}=m^{2}(K_{S}^{0}\pi^{-})
, representing the invariant mass-squared of the
K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}
and
K_{S}^{0}\pi^{-}
pair, respectively. If
\mathbf{p}=(s_{-},s_{+})
, then
\mathbf{\overline{p}}=(s_{+},s_{-})
, and
\overline{A}_{D}^{K_{S}^{0}\pi\pi}(s_{+},s_{-})=A_{D}^{K_{S}^{0}\pi\pi}(s_{-},s_{+})
.

We define the phases
\phi_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})\equiv\arg(A_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})),\overline{\phi}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})\equiv\arg(\overline{A}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p}))
and the phase difference
\delta_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})\equiv\phi_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})-\overline{\phi}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})
.
It is also useful to define
\overline{\delta}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})\equiv\overline{\phi}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})-\phi_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})
, even though it is trivially related to
\delta_{D}
through
\overline{\delta}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})=\delta_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{\overline{p}})=-\delta_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})
. To declutter the notation we will omit the superscripts and/or the
(\mathbf{p})
, where there is no risk of ambiguity.

When applying the method presented below in practice it will frequently be necessary to combine results obtained using different conventions for the phase of the
\mathit{CP}
operator. Switching from our convention with
\mathit{CP}|D^{0}\rangle=|\overline{D}{}^{0}\rangle
to the convention with
\mathit{CP}|D^{0}\rangle=-|\overline{D}{}^{0}\rangle
corresponds to the change
\delta_{D}\to\delta_{D}+\pi
.

2.2
Measuring
\gamma
with
B^{\pm}\to DK^{\pm}
decays

The decay amplitude of a
D
meson resulting from a
B^{-}\to DK
to a final state
f
at phase space point
\mathbf{p}
is given by

\displaystyle A_{B-}(\mathbf{p})\propto A_{D}(\mathbf{p})+r_{B}e^{i(\delta_{B}-\gamma)}\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{p}),

(1)

and the corresponding decay rate is

\displaystyle\Gamma^{-}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=N\left(|A_{D}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}+r_{B}^{2}|\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}+2\mathsf{Re}(A_{D}(\mathbf{p})r_{B}e^{-i(\delta_{B}(\mathbf{p})-\gamma)}\overline{A}_{D}^{*}(\mathbf{p}))\right)

(2)

\displaystyle=N\left(|A_{D}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}+r_{B}^{2}|\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}+2r_{B}|A_{D}(\mathbf{p})||\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{p})|\cos(-\delta_{B}+\gamma+\delta_{D})\right)

(3)

\displaystyle=N\big(|A_{D}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}+r_{B}^{2}|\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;+2r_{B}|A_{D}(\mathbf{p})||\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{p})|\left(\cos(\delta_{B}-\gamma)\cos(\delta_{D}(\mathbf{p}))+\sin(\delta_{B}-\gamma)\sin(\delta_{D}(\mathbf{p}))\right)\big),

(4)

where
N
is a normalisation factor. For the
\mathit{CP}
-conjugate process, with a
D
from a
B^{+}\to DK^{+}
:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{+}(\mathbf{\overline{p}})

\displaystyle=N\big(|\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{\overline{p}})|^{2}+r_{B}^{2}|A_{D}(\mathbf{\overline{p}})|^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;+2r_{B}|A_{D}(\mathbf{\overline{p}})||\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{\overline{p}})|\left(\cos(\delta_{B}+\gamma)\cos(\overline{\delta}_{D})+\sin(\delta_{B}+\gamma)\sin(\overline{\delta}_{D}(\mathbf{\overline{p}}))\right)\big)

(5)

where
\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{\overline{p}})=A_{D}(\mathbf{p})
,
A_{D}(\mathbf{\overline{p}})=\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{p})
, and
\overline{\delta}_{D}(\mathbf{\overline{p}})=\delta_{D}(\mathbf{p})
for
\mathit{CP}
conservation in charm.
For the fits in our validation studies, we follow the widely used practice to re-parameterise the decay rates in terms of the “cartesian” variables

\displaystyle x_{+}

\displaystyle\equiv r_{B}\cos(\delta_{B}+\gamma),

\displaystyle y_{+}

\displaystyle\equiv r_{B}\sin(\delta_{B}+\gamma),

\displaystyle x_{-}

\displaystyle\equiv r_{B}\cos(\delta_{B}-\gamma),

\displaystyle y_{-}

\displaystyle\equiv r_{B}\sin(\delta_{B}-\gamma).

(6)

This is motivated by the observation that fits in terms of
x_{\pm}
and
y_{\pm}
are statistically better behaved than those in terms of
r_{B},\delta_{B},
and
\gamma
, which is related to the fact that there is no sensitivity to
\delta_{B},\gamma
as
r_{B}\to 0
. The decay rates in terms of the new variables are

\displaystyle\Gamma^{-}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=N\left(|A_{D}|^{2}+\left(x_{-}^{2}+y_{-}^{2}\right)|\overline{A}_{D}|^{2}+2|A_{D}||\overline{A}_{D}|\left(x_{-}\cos(\delta_{D})+y_{-}\sin(\delta_{D})\right)\right),

(7)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{+}(\mathbf{\overline{p}})

\displaystyle=N\left(|\overline{A}_{D}|^{2}+\left(x_{+}^{2}+y_{+}^{2}\right)|A_{D}|^{2}+2|A_{D}||\overline{A}_{D}|\left(x_{+}\cos(\overline{\delta}_{D})+y_{+}\sin(\overline{\delta}_{D})\right)\right).

(8)

In a model-dependent approach,

A_{D}
and
\overline{A}_{D}
are derived from a high-statistics amplitude fit to flavour-specific
D^{0}
,
\overline{D}{}^{0}
decays. However, the models used to describe the decay amplitude have theoretical shortcomings that make their phase information, which enters via
\delta_{D}
, unreliable. This in turn translates into a systematic uncertainty on
\gamma
.

For the model-independent approach described in
GGSZ
;
Bondar:2005ki
, one integrates over regions (bins) of phase space. These bins are defined such that they form
\mathit{CP}
-conjugate pairs and we label them such that the
\mathit{CP}
-conjugate of bin
i
is bin
-i
. In what follows, we assume the
D
decays to
K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
, although the approach clearly generalises to other decay modes. We define the following parameters related to
|A_{D}|^{2},|\overline{A}_{D}|^{2}
:

\displaystyle F_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\mathrm{bin}\;i}|A_{D}|^{2}ds_{+}ds_{-},

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\mathrm{bin}\;i}|\overline{A}_{D}|^{2}ds_{+}ds_{-}.

(9)

In the absence of
\mathit{CP}
violation in charm,
\overline{F}_{i}=F_{-i}
. We also define the following parameters related to the phase-difference between
A_{D}
and
\overline{A}_{D}

\displaystyle c_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\int_{\mathrm{bin}\;i}|A_{D}||\overline{A}_{D}|\cos(\delta_{D})ds_{+}ds_{-}}{\sqrt{\int_{\mathrm{bin}\;i}|A_{D}|^{2}ds_{+}ds_{-}\int_{\mathrm{bin}\;i}|\overline{A}_{D}|^{2}ds_{+}ds_{-}}},

(10)

\displaystyle s_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\int_{\mathrm{bin\;i}}|A_{D}||\overline{A}_{D}|\sin(\delta_{D})ds_{+}ds_{-}}{\sqrt{\int_{\mathrm{bin\;i}}|A_{D}|^{2}ds_{+}ds_{-}\int_{\mathrm{bin\;i}}|\overline{A}_{D}|^{2}ds_{+}ds_{-}}},

(11)

which implies
c_{-i}\equiv c_{i}
and
s_{-i}\equiv-s_{i}
.
In terms if these quantities, the decay rate
B^{-}\to DK^{-}
with the
D
decay in phase-space bin
i
, is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{-}_{i}

\displaystyle=F_{i}+r_{B}^{2}{F}_{-i}+2r_{B}\sqrt{F_{i}F_{-i}}\left(\cos(\delta_{B}-\gamma)c_{i}+\sin(\delta_{B}-\gamma)s_{i}\right).

(12)

The
\mathit{CP}
conjugate process, a
B^{+}\to DK^{+}
decay with the
D
decay in phase-space bin
-i
, is

\displaystyle\Gamma^{+}_{-i}

\displaystyle={F}_{i}+r_{B}^{2}{F}_{-i}+2r_{B}\sqrt{F_{i}F_{-i}}\left(\cos(\delta_{B}+\gamma)c_{i}+\sin(\delta_{B}+\gamma)s_{i}\right).

(13)

Equivalently, these decay rates can be expressed in terms of
x_{\pm},y_{\pm}
:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{-}_{i}

\displaystyle=F_{i}+\left(x_{-}^{2}+y_{-}^{2}\right){F}_{-i}+2\sqrt{F_{i}F_{-i}}\left(x_{-}c_{i}+y_{-}s_{i}\right),

(14)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{+}_{-i}

\displaystyle={F}_{i}+\left(x_{+}^{2}+y_{+}^{2}\right){F}_{-i}+2\sqrt{F_{i}F_{-i}}\left(x_{+}c_{i}+y_{+}s_{i}\right).

(15)

All parameters related to the charm decay, i.e.
F_{i},c_{i}
, and
s_{i}
, can be directly obtained from data, where data from the charm threshold are critical to constraining the parameters related to the phase difference between
A_{D}
and
\overline{A}_{D}
, i.e.
c_{i}
and
s_{i}
.
It is worth noting, though, that the
c_{i}
and
s_{i}
can be obtained alongside
\gamma
from (a sufficiently large sample of)
B^{\pm}\to DK^{\pm}
decays

; however, the input from the charm threshold dramatically improves the fit. Measurements of
\gamma
in this way, using the
D\to K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
Dalitz plot we focus on here, have been made by Belle
Belle:2012ftx
;
Belle:2015roy
, Belle & Belle II
Belle:2021efh
, and LHCb
LHCb:2012apu
;
LHCb:2018wag
;
LHCb:2020yot

2.3
Charm threshold data

At the charm threshold,
\psi(3770)
are produced and decay approximately 50% of the time to a pair of neutral
D
mesons that we label
D_{1},D_{2}
. The resulting system of
D

-odd, like the
\psi(3770)

\displaystyle|\psi(3770)\rangle\to|D^{0}\rangle_{1}|\overline{D}{}^{0}\rangle_{2}-|\overline{D}{}^{0}\rangle_{1}|D^{0}\rangle_{2}.

(16)

Let
D_{1}
decay to final state
f
at phase-space point
\mathbf{p}
and
D_{2}
to final state
g
at phase-space point
\mathbf{q}
. Then the decay amplitude for this process is

\displaystyle A(\psi\to D_{1}D_{2};D_{1}\to f(\mathbf{p}),D_{2}\to g(\mathbf{q}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(A_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})\overline{A}_{D}^{g}(\mathbf{q})-\overline{A}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})A_{D}^{g}(\mathbf{q})\right).

(17)

We will call
g
the
tag
and
f
the
signal
(in our feasibility study,
f
will be
K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
). Depending on the tag, we can distinguish a few important special cases:

1.

D_{2}\to g
is a flavour-specific decay such as a semileptonic decay, or a quasi-flavour-specific decay such as
D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}
(in our feasibility studies, we will ignore the small dilution effect due to the suppressed decay
\overline{D}{}^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}
, although this can be taken into account). Then

\displaystyle A(\psi\to D_{1}D^{0};D_{1}\to f(\mathbf{p}),D^{0}\to g)

\displaystyle\propto\overline{A}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p}),

(18)

and similarly

\displaystyle A(\psi\to D_{1}\overline{D}{}^{0};D_{1}\to f(\mathbf{p}),\overline{D}{}^{0}\to g)

We refer to these decays as flavour tagged. They provide the same information as flavour-tagged

and, in the binned approach,

2.

D_{2}\to g
is a
\mathit{CP}
-specific decay, either a
\mathit{CP}
-even decay such as

, implying

-odd such as

. The corresponding decay amplitudes are

\displaystyle A(\psi\to D_{1}D_{\pm};D_{1}\to f(\mathbf{p}),D_{\pm}\to g)

\displaystyle\propto\mp A_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})-\overline{A}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p}),

and the decay rates:

\displaystyle\Gamma(\psi\to D_{1}D_{\pm};D_{1}\to f(\mathbf{p}),D_{2}\to g)

\displaystyle\propto|A_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}+|\overline{A}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}\pm 2|A_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})||\overline{A}_{D}^{f}(\mathbf{p})|\cos(\delta_{D}(\mathbf{p})).

These provide information on

3.

Both

\displaystyle A(\psi\to D_{1}D_{2};D_{1}\to f(\mathbf{p}),D_{2}\to f(\mathbf{q}))

\displaystyle\propto A_{D}(\mathbf{p})\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{q})-\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{p})A_{D}(\mathbf{q}),

\displaystyle\Gamma(\psi\to D_{1}D_{2};D_{1}\to f(\mathbf{p}),D_{2}\to f(\mathbf{q}))

\displaystyle\propto|A_{D}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}|\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{q})|^{2}+|\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}|A_{D}(\mathbf{q})|^{2}

and

3
Quasi Model Independent unbinned method to measure
\gamma

3.1
Basic idea

This section will introduce the quasi model-independent (QMI) method that is the subject of this paper. For concreteness, we will consider the decay
D^{0}\to K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
, although the method generalises to all self-conjugate charm decays, and with some modification also to non-self-conjugate ones.

The statistically most precise way of measuring
\gamma
is the model-dependent method. We observe that the magnitude of the amplitude structure across the Dalitz plot of
D^{0}\to K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
is well known from flavour-specific
D^{0}
and
\overline{D}{}^{0}
decays analysed by BaBar and Belle
BaBar:2018cka
. The
D^{0}
,
\overline{D}{}^{0}
datasets used in these fits are orders of magnitude larger than the
B^{\pm}\to DK^{\pm}
datasets available for
\gamma
measurements.
The fact that the collaborations achieve a decent fit of their models to these datasets implies that we can trust the magnitude of existing amplitude models. However, those models violate unitarity and analyticity, which breaks the connection between magnitude and phase.
Consequently, the phases of these models stand on less firm ground.
Our approach is therefore to correct the model’s phase in a model-independent way, or, more precisely, correct the
phase difference
between the
D^{0}
amplitude
A_{D}(\mathbf{p})
and
\overline{D}{}^{0}
amplitude
\overline{A}_{D}(\mathbf{p})
at each phase space point
\mathbf{p}
. These phase differences are all that matters and in fact it is all we have access to. Measurements of
c_{i}
and
s_{i}
by CLEO-c and BES III suggest that the model’s phase differences are at least approximately correct
BESIII:2020hlg
;
BESIII:2020hpo
;
CLEO:2010iul
;
CLEO:2009syb
. Our approach is therefore to obtain the model-independent phase difference by adding a correcting term
\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{corr}}
to the model’s phase difference
\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{model}}
,

\displaystyle\delta_{D}=\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{model}}+\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{corr}}.

(25)

We parameterise
\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{corr}}
in a generic way, as a power series in the Dalitz plot parameters. We assume
\mathit{CP}
conservation in charm decays, such that

\displaystyle\delta_{D}(s_{+},s_{-})=-\delta_{D}(s_{-},s_{+}),

(26)

which also implies

\displaystyle\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{corr}}(s_{+},s_{-})=-\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{corr}}(s_{-},s_{+}).

(27)

This symmetry reduces the number of parameters needed to parameterise
\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{corr}}
.
We will see below that even if we depart from the assumption that the model’s phase differences are approximately correct, such that
\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{corr}}
becomes quite sizeable, our method still works.

The information that allows us to constrain
\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{corr}}
comes, as for the binned methods, predominantly from the charm threshold, although
B^{\pm}\to DK^{\pm}
decays also contribute.

Constructing the correction to
\delta_{D}

Figure 1
:
The Dalitz Phasespace,
(s_{+},s_{-})

and the stretched rotated coordinates,
(z^{\prime}_{+},z^{\prime\prime}_{-})
.

In order to implement the symmetry relation Eq
27
, we define the variables

\displaystyle z_{+}

\displaystyle\equiv s_{+}+s_{-},

\displaystyle z_{-}

\displaystyle\equiv s_{+}-s_{-}.

(28)

Now the symmetry condition from Eq
27
becomes

\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{corr}}(z_{+},z_{-})=-\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{corr}}(z_{+},-z_{-})
and can be implemented by only allowing terms with odd powers of
z_{-}
in the correcting polynomial.

We found that parameterising the phase in terms of Legendre polynomials works well. These are defined for values
x\in[-1,1]
. We therefore scale
z_{+},z_{-}

\displaystyle z_{+}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{2z_{+}-(z_{+}^{\mathrm{max}}+z_{+}^{\mathrm{min}})}{z_{+}^{\mathrm{max}}-z_{+}^{\mathrm{min}}},

\displaystyle z_{-}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{2z_{-}-(z_{-}^{\mathrm{max}}+z_{-}^{\mathrm{min}})}{z_{-}^{\mathrm{max}}-z_{-}^{\mathrm{min}}},

(29)

where the superscripts max and min indicate the maximum and minimum values of the unprimed parameters.
The kinematically allowed region of the
K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
in terms of the normalised, rotated parameters
z_{+}^{\prime},z_{-}^{\prime}
is shown in Fig
1(b)
.
The figure shows that within the square

z_{+}^{\prime}\in[-1,1],z_{-}^{\prime}\in[-1,1]

where the Legendre polynomial is defined, there is a lot of space where there are no data, with the kinematically-allowed interval in
z_{-}^{\prime}
varying a lot as a function of
z_{+}
. We therefore “stretch” the Dalitz plot by replacing
z_{-}^{\prime}
with

\displaystyle z_{-}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{2z_{-}^{\prime}}{z_{+}^{\prime}+2}.

(30)

The Dalitz plot phase space for the different parametrisations is shown in Fig
1
. Alternatives such as a variation of the square Dalitz plot
Laura++
would have achieved a similar outcome.

We construct the correcting polynomial,
\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{corr}}
, of order
N
with free parameters
\mathbf{C}

\delta_{D}^{\mathrm{corr}}(z_{+}^{\prime},z_{-}^{\prime\prime}|\mathbf{C})=\sum_{i=0}^{i\leq N}\sum_{j=0}^{j\leq\frac{N-i}{2}}C_{i,2j+1}P_{i}(z^{\prime}_{+})P_{2j+1}(z^{\prime\prime}_{-}),

(31)

where
P_{n}
are, in our implementation,
n^{\mathrm{th}}
order Legendre polynomials, although any function with
P_{2j+1}(z_{-}^{\prime\prime})=-P_{2j+1}(-z_{-}^{\prime\prime})
would be equally valid.
The coefficients
C_{ij}
are free parameters, determined together with
x_{\pm},y_{\pm}
in a simultaneous fit to
\psi(3770)
and
B^{\pm}\to DK^{\pm}
data.

to their true value (according to our model), so that the uncertainty on

show that, for the “optimal” binning scheme, taking into account the measurement uncertainties on

. This results in a total uncertainty on

by the new method introduced, here.

5
Conclusion

We have introduced a novel unbinned quasi model-idependent (QMI) method for the measurement of
\gamma
in
B^{\pm}\to DK^{\pm}
decays, with input from quantum-correlated charm threshold data.
The method uses a polynomial to correct the phase of the
D
meson’s decay amplitude model in an unbinned, model-independent way.

We studied the performance of the method using simulated
B^{\pm}\to DK^{\pm},D\to K_{S}^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
\psi(3770)\to D\overline{D}
signal events. The method produces unbiased results for cases where discrepancies between the assumed amplitude model and the true model produce large biases in a model-dependent measurement.
For realistic current and plausible future sample sizes, the method achieves a statistical precision on
\gamma
that is effectively the same as the optimum defined by the model-dependent method, without suffering from the systematic uncertainty associated with the amplitude model. The statistical uncertainty is significantly better than that of the binned model-independent method currently in use.

We expect that the QMI method will also be beneficial in the study of charm mixing and the study of phases of decay amplitudes across the Dalitz plot.

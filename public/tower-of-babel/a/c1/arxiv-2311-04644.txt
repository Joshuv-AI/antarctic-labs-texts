Bounds on the density of smooth lattice coverings
Or Ordentlich, Oded Regev, Barak Weiss

Abstract
. Let 𝒦 {\mathcal{K}} be a convex body in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} , let L L be a lattice with unit covolume, and let η > 0 \eta>0 . We say that 𝒦 {\mathcal{K}} and L L form an η \eta -smooth cover if each point x ∈ ℝ n x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n} is covered by ( 1 ± η ) ​ vol ​ ( 𝒦 ) (1\pm\eta){\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}}) translates of 𝒦 {\mathcal{K}} by L L . We prove that for any positive σ \sigma and η \eta , asymptotically as n → ∞ , n\to\infty, for any 𝒦 {\mathcal{K}} of volume n 3 + σ n^{3+\sigma} , one can find a lattice L L for which 𝒦 , L {\mathcal{K}},L form an η \eta -smooth cover. Moreover, this property is satisfied with high probability for a lattice chosen randomly, according to the Haar-Siegel measure on the space of lattices. Similar results hold for random construction A lattices, albeit with a worse power law, provided that the ratio between the covering and packing radii of ℤ n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n} with respect to 𝒦 {\mathcal{K}} is at most polynomial in n n . Our proofs rely on a recent breakthrough of Dhar and Dvir on the discrete Kakeya problem.



1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}

denote the set of
bounded convex subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with nonempty interior. For a lattice
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, convex set
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
,
and a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote

(1)

N(L,{\mathcal{K}},x){\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\left|L\cap({\mathcal{K}}+x)\right|=\left|\left\{y\in L\ :\ x\in y-{\mathcal{K}}\right\}\right|.

The expectation of
N(L,{\mathcal{K}},x)
when
x
is drawn uniformly from a fundamental domain for
L

is
{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)
; thus if we draw
x
uniformly in a ball
B(0,T)
with respect to some norm, the expectation of
N(L,{\mathcal{K}},x)
approaches
{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)
, in the limit as
T\to\infty
. Furthermore, we have that
\frac{N(L,\alpha{\mathcal{K}},x)}{{\mathrm{vol}}(\alpha{\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)}
tends to
1
as the dilation factor
\alpha
grows, where the convergence is uniform in
x
. It is therefore natural to ask, given
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
and a lattice
L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, whether the fraction
\frac{N(L,{\mathcal{K}},x)}{{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)}

is nearly constant on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. To that end we define the following quantity:

Definition 1.1
.

The
covering smoothness
of a lattice
L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with respect to a convex body
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\eta}({\mathcal{K}},L){\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|\frac{N(L,{\mathcal{K}},x)}{{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)}-1\right|.

Note that
{\eta}({\mathcal{K}},L)<1
immediately implies that
L+{\mathcal{K}}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. In this case the pair
(L,{\mathcal{K}})
is said to be
a covering
. (The reverse statement is not true — there may be points
x
that are covered an exceptionally large number of times by translates of
{\mathcal{K}}
.)
As in the abstract, if
\eta({\mathcal{K}},L)<\eta
we say that the covering given by
{\mathcal{K}}
and
L
is
\eta
-smooth.

Let
\mu_{n}
denote the Haar-Siegel measure; that is, the unique probability measure on the space of lattices in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of unit covolume, which is invariant under volume preserving linear transformations. Our main result is that for every
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
whose volume is polynomial in
n
, and for most lattices in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the covering smoothness is small.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n>25
, and let
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
. Let
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and assume
{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})\geq c_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon\delta}\right)^{6.5}n^{3}
, where
c_{1}=2^{66}
.
Then, for
L\sim\mu_{n}
we have

(2)

\displaystyle\Pr\left({\eta}({\mathcal{K}},L)\geq\varepsilon\right)<\delta.

In particular, for any positive
\varepsilon,\sigma
,

\displaystyle\sup_{{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}},{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})\geq n^{3+\sigma}}\Pr\left(\eta({\mathcal{K}},L)\geq\varepsilon\right)\longrightarrow_{n\to\infty}0.

We did not attempt to optimize the multiplicative constant
c_{1}
in this result, or similar constants
c_{i}
in the sequel.

Theorem
1.2
and the remaining statements below might find applications in computer science. Specifically, in lattice-based cryptography, smoothing a lattice is a key idea used to hide secret information from an adversary
[
10
]
. Typically, one considers smoothing by a Gaussian distribution. However, for some applications it might be advantageous to smooth using a convex body, since sampling from a convex body like a cube can be more efficient.
It should be noted that in many cryptographic applications, closeness in
L_{1}
is sufficient, and such
L_{1}
-smoothness results (in fact, even
L_{2}
-smoothness) are often much easier to prove (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
); yet there are many cases where closeness in
L_{\infty}
(as in our results) leads to tighter results
[
1
]
.

It is instructive to compare Theorem
1.2
with our previous work
[
12
]
on lattice coverings. Recall that for a lattice
L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of covolume one, the
covering density

\Theta({\mathcal{K}},L)
is the minimal volume of a dilate
\alpha{\mathcal{K}}
such that
(L,\alpha{\mathcal{K}})
is a covering. One of the results of
[
12
]
is that

(3)

\sup_{{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}}\inf_{L\text{ of covolume }1}\Theta({\mathcal{K}},L)

grows at most quadratically in
n
(prior to
[
12
]
the best known bound, due to Rogers
[
14
]
, was superpolynomial). In fact, it was shown that for any
\delta>0
, any
\sigma>0
, any large enough
n
and any
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}},
if
{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})\geq n^{2+\sigma}
then the
\mu_{n}
-probability that
(L,{\mathcal{K}})
is a covering is at least
1-\delta.

Fixing

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we deduce easily from Theorem
1.2
the slightly weaker statement, in which
2+\sigma
is replaced with
3+\sigma
. That is, when
{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})>n^{2+\sigma}
, we know from
[
12
]
that a random
L
gives a covering, and from Theorem
1.2
we know that when
{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})>n^{3+\sigma}
a random
L
gives an
\varepsilon
-smooth covering. On the other hand,
[
5
]
shows that for
{\mathcal{K}}
taken as the Euclidean ball and any lattice
L
of covolume
1
,
\eta({\mathcal{K}},L)\geq 1
(and moreover,
(L,{\mathcal{K}})
is not a covering) unless
{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})=\Omega(n)
.

While we intuitively expect covering
to become smoother as we scale up
{\mathcal{K}}
, it turns out that in general
\alpha\mapsto\eta(\alpha{\mathcal{K}},L)
is not monotonically non-increasing. To see this, take
L={\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
{\mathcal{K}}=\left[0,1\right)^{n}
.
Then
\eta({\mathcal{K}},{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})=0
yet for small
\varepsilon>0
,
\eta((1+\varepsilon){\mathcal{K}},{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})=(2/(1+\varepsilon))^{n}-1
. It is therefore natural to further define the following quantity for a lattice
L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
,

\displaystyle{\Phi}_{{\mathcal{K}},L}(\varepsilon){\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\sup\left\{\frac{{\mathrm{vol}}(\alpha{\mathcal{K}})}{\mathrm{covol}(L)}\ :\ \alpha>0\text{ satisfies }{\eta}(\alpha{\mathcal{K}},L)>\varepsilon\right\}.

In particular, for a lattice
L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of unit covolume, we have that
{\eta}(\alpha{\mathcal{K}},L)\leq\varepsilon
for
all
dilates
\alpha{\mathcal{K}}
of volume exceeding

{\Phi}_{{\mathcal{K}},L}(\varepsilon)
. We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n>25
, and let
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
. Let
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and
c_{2}=2^{112}
.
Then, for
L\sim\mu_{n}
we have

(4)

\displaystyle\Pr\left({\Phi}_{{\mathcal{K}},L}(\varepsilon)\geq c_{2}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}\delta}\right)^{6.5}n^{3}\right)<\delta.

1.1.
Construction A lattices

In many applications in electrical engineering and computer science, integer lattices known as
construction A lattices
are of interest
[
4
,
9
]
. For a prime
p

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
denote the field with
p

, let

{\mathrm{Gr}}_{n,r}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
denote the collection of subspaces of
dimension
r
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
, or equivalently, the rank-
r
additive
subgroups of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with the residues

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/p{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

p

\pi_{p}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}

gives rise to a sub-lattice

\pi_{p}^{-1}(S)/p{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

The ensemble of lattices obtained by drawing
S\sim\mathrm{Uniform}({\mathrm{Gr}}_{n,r}({\mathbb{F}}_{p}))

(p,r)
construction A ensemble

Theorem

1.2.
Non-lattice smooth coverings

If one relaxes the requirement that
L
is a lattice, it is slightly more complicated to define smooth covers, but much easier to construct them.

Let
L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a discrete set (not necessarily a lattice). We continue to use the notation
N(L,{\mathcal{K}},x)
defined in (
1
). Let
B(0,T)
denote the ball of radius
T
around the origin with respect to the Euclidean norm, and define the
asymptotic upper density
of
L
by

(10)

D(L){\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{|B(0,T)\cap L|}{{\mathrm{vol}}(B(0,T))}.

If the limit in (
10
) exists we will say that
L
has an asymptotic density
.
Note that lattices have an asymptotic density given by
D(L)=\mathrm{covol}(L)^{-1}
.
Now for
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
, and
L
as above, we set

\displaystyle{\eta}({\mathcal{K}},L){\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|\frac{N(L,{\mathcal{K}},x)}{{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})\,D(L)}-1\right|.

With this notation we have:

Theorem 1.7
.

For any
0<\varepsilon<1
,
n\geq 20
, and any
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}

there is a discrete set
L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
which has an asymptotic density, satisfying

(11)

{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})D(L)\leq\frac{14}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(n\log n+n\log\frac{1280}{\varepsilon}+2\right)\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \eta({\mathcal{K}},L)<\varepsilon.

We remark that the set
L
constructed in the proof of Theorem
1.7
is
periodic
, i.e., consists of finitely many translates of a lattice in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We also remark that Theorem
1.7
can be derived by modifying the proof of Erdős and Rogers
[
8
]
, who proved a closely related statement. For completeness, we include a proof in Section
3.5
that follows the proof structure of our main theorem.

2.
Techniques and Notation

The main results of this paper follow from the somewhat technical Theorem
3.4
. This result is derived in turn from a new result of Dhar and Dvir (Theorem
2.2
), which is a crucial input to this paper.
In this section we introduce notations, give a brief overview of our approach, and state Theorem
2.2
.

For a
lattice
L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
let

{\mathbb{T}}_{L}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}/L
be the quotient torus, let
m_{L}
be the Haar probability
measure on
{\mathbb{T}}_{L}
, and let
\pi_{L}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{T}}_{L}
be the quotient map.
Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
be the generators of
L
given by the columns of
g

so that the parallellepiped

{\mathcal{P}}_{L}=\left\{\sum a_{i}v_{i}:\forall i,\ 0\leq a_{i}<1\right\}

is a fundamental
domain for
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}/L
. Define the discrete ‘net’

(12)

{\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{(disc)}}_{L}}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\left\{\sum a_{i}v_{i}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{L}:a_{i}\in\left\{0,\frac{1}{p},\ldots,1-\frac{1}{p}\right\}\right\}

and set

\frac{L}{p}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\frac{1}{p}\cdot L.

Then the elements of
{\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{(disc)}}_{L}}
are coset representatives for the inclusion
L\subset\frac{L}{p},
and there is an isomorphism (as abelian groups)
{\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{(disc)}}_{L}}\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}.

Next, we introduce a well-studied technique for randomly choosing lattices.
Given
L=g{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, where
g
is an invertible
n\times n
matrix, and given
S\in{\mathrm{Gr}}_{n,r}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, we define the super-lattice
L(S)\supset L
as

(13)

\displaystyle L(S){\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\frac{1}{p}\cdot g\pi_{p}^{-1}(S).

Notice that the scaled-up version
p^{r/n}\cdot L(S)
of
L(S)
is of the same covolume as
L
.
The assignment

(14)

L\ \mapsto\ \left\{p^{r/n}L(S):S\in{\mathrm{Gr}}_{n,r}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})\right\}

is a special case of the so-called
Hecke correspondence.

Note that the individual lattice
L(S)
also depends on the initial choice of
g
for which
L=g{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},
but the collection on the right-hand side of (
14
) does not.
Also note that Construction A lattices are a special case of this construction (up to scaling), starting with
L={\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.

Given a convex body
{\mathcal{K}}
, our goal is to find a lattice for which the covering smoothness is small.
We will choose this lattice to be
L(S)
for a randomly chosen
S\in{\mathrm{Gr}}_{n,r}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
for some
r
and
p
, and where
L
is a lattice for which we have a reasonable bound on
\rho_{{\mathcal{K}}}(L)
.
For instance, in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we will take
L
to be a randomly chosen lattice according to the Haar-Siegel measure
\mu_{n}
, which has a small
\rho_{{\mathcal{K}}}
(Proposition
3.6
);
importantly, by
[
12
]
*Proposition 2.1,
L(S)
is also distributed according to
\mu_{n}
(up to scaling), as needed for the conclusion of Theorem
1.2
.

By rescaling
{\mathcal{K}}
we may assume that
L
forms a packing with respect to
{\mathcal{K}}
and a covering with respect to the dilate
\rho_{{\mathcal{K}}}(L){\mathcal{K}}
. Recall that our goal is to show that the function

(15)

\mathbb{T}_{L(S)}\to{\mathbb{N}},\ \ \ \ x\mapsto|(x+L(S))\cap{\mathcal{K}}|

is uniformly close to a constant function. By a discretization procedure (see Proposition
3.1
), it will be sufficient to show that the restriction of the function in (
15
) to
\mathcal{P}^{(\mathrm{disc})}_{L}
is close to a constant function. This uses the fact that
L
is a covering with respect to
\rho_{{\mathcal{K}}}(L){\mathcal{K}}
and assumes that
p
is chosen sufficiently large with respect to
\rho_{{\mathcal{K}}}(L)
.

The final step in the proof is to reduce the problem to an analogous problem in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
.
Denoting by
A\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
the set
\pi_{L}\big(\frac{L}{p}\cap{\mathcal{K}}\big)
viewed as a subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
, we have that for any
x\in\mathcal{P}^{(\mathrm{disc})}_{L}
,

(16)

\displaystyle|(x+L(S))\cap{\mathcal{K}}|=\Big|\pi_{L}\Big((x+L(S))\cap{\mathcal{K}}\Big)\Big|=|(x+S)\cap A|\;,

where the first equality uses the assumption that
L
forms a packing with respect to
{\mathcal{K}}
(and so
\pi_{L}
is injective on
{\mathcal{K}}
) and on the right-hand side we think of
x
as being in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
.
Thus our problem reduces to showing that a randomly chosen
S
leads to a smooth covering of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
by the
S
-translates of
A
. This is precisely the problem addressed by Dhar and Dvir.

To state their result we need the following discrete analogue of the covering smoothness:

Definition 2.1
.

Let

Proposition 3.1

Let

Let

{\mathrm{Gr}}_{n,r}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, statistically independent of
L^{\prime}
,
the lattice
L=p^{r/n}L^{\prime}(S)
will also be distributed according to
\mu_{n}
. Thus, for
L\sim\mu_{n}
and any
\tau\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\Pr(\eta({\mathcal{K}},L)\geq 2\tau)=\Pr(\eta({\mathcal{K}},p^{r/n}L^{\prime}(S))\geq 2\tau).

We proceed to upper bound the right hand side of the above expression, using Corollary
3.5
.
Let
\bar{\rho}=8\cdot\left(\frac{50}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}
(note that
\bar{\rho}<\left(\frac{2}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}
for
n>25
), and let
E
be the set of all lattices
L^{\prime}
with unit covolume for which
\rho_{{\mathcal{K}}}(L^{\prime})<\bar{\rho}
. Applying Proposition
3.6
with
\alpha=2\left(\frac{50}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}
, we have that
\Pr(L^{\prime}\in E^{c})<0.06\delta
. Now, applying Corollary
3.5
with
\delta^{\prime}=0.47\delta
,
\tau=\varepsilon/2
, we see that for any
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
with

\displaystyle{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{2}{0.47}\right)^{6}c_{5}\bar{\rho}^{6}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon\delta}\right)^{6}n^{3}

(38)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{0.47}\right)^{6}\cdot 8^{6}\cdot 50^{\frac{12}{n}}\cdot c_{5}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{12}{n}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon\delta}\right)^{6}n^{3},

there is a prime number
p
and an integer
3<r<n
for which

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\eta({\mathcal{K}},p^{r/n}L^{\prime}(S))\geq\varepsilon\penalty\ |\penalty\ L^{\prime}\in E\right)<0.94\delta.

In particular, this holds for any
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
with
{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})>c_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon\delta}\right)^{6.5}n^{3}
, since

is greater than the right hand side of (

).
Our claim now follows since

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\eta({\mathcal{K}},L)\geq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\Pr\left(\eta({\mathcal{K}},p^{r/n}L^{\prime}(S))\geq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Pr(L^{\prime}\in E^{c})+\Pr\Big(\eta({\mathcal{K}},p^{r/n}L^{\prime}(S))\geq\varepsilon\penalty\ |\penalty\ L^{\prime}\in E\Big)

\displaystyle<0.06\delta+0.94\delta.

∎

3.3.
High probability bounds on
\rho_{\mathcal{K}}(L)

In this subsection we will give a simple proof of Proposition
3.6
. The first results showing the existence of a global constant
c>0
, independent of the dimension
n
, such that for any
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
there is a lattice
L
such that
\rho_{{\mathcal{K}}}(L)<c
, are due to Butler
[
3
]
and Bourgain
[
2
]
. The probability (with respect to the Siegel-Haar measure) that a randomly chosen lattice satisfies
\rho_{{\mathcal{K}}}(L)<c
was not discussed in these papers. Using

[
12
]
*Cor. 1.6 together with the bound
{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}}-{\mathcal{K}})\leq{\mathrm{vol}}(4{\mathcal{K}})
proved in
[
13
]
, one sees that
\Pr\left(\rho_{{\mathcal{K}}}(L)>4+o(1)\right)
vanishes exponentially fast with
n
. However, we can give a much simpler proof, albeit with a worse constant. Note that the value of the constant has small effect on the bounds we obtain for the covering smoothness.

Proof of Proposition
3.6
.

We will derive a high-probability lower bound on
\mathrm{r}_{\text{pack},{\mathcal{K}}}(L)
and a high-probability upper bound on
\mathrm{r}_{\text{cov},{\mathcal{K}}}(L)
. Assume without loss of generality that
{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})=1
.
Denote

N^{*}(L,{\mathcal{K}},x){\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\left|((L\setminus\{0\})-x)\cap{\mathcal{K}}\right|=\left|\left\{y\in L\setminus\{0\}\ :\ x\in y-{\mathcal{K}}\right\}\right|.

For lower bounding
\mathrm{r}_{\text{pack},{\mathcal{K}}}(L)
, let
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}=\frac{1}{2\alpha}{\mathcal{K}}
and let
N^{*}(L,{\mathcal{K}}_{0}-{\mathcal{K}}_{0},0)
be the number of non-zero points of the lattice
L
in
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}-{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
. By Siegel’s theorem
[
15
]
, we have

\mathbb{E}[N^{*}(L,{\mathcal{K}}_{0}-{\mathcal{K}}_{0},0)]={\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}}_{0}-{\mathcal{K}}_{0}).

Thus, by Markov’s inequality,

\displaystyle\Pr(N^{*}(L,{\mathcal{K}}_{0}-{\mathcal{K}}_{0},0)

\displaystyle\neq 0)=\Pr(N^{*}(L,{\mathcal{K}}_{0}-{\mathcal{K}}_{0},0)\geq 1)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[N^{*}(L,{\mathcal{K}}_{0}-{\mathcal{K}}_{0},0)]

\displaystyle={\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}}_{0}-{\mathcal{K}}_{0})

(39)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathrm{vol}}(4{\mathcal{K}}_{0})=\left(\frac{4}{2\alpha}\right)^{n},

where the last inequality in (
39
) follows from
[
13
]
*Theorem 1.
Now, since
N^{*}(L,{\mathcal{K}}_{0}-{\mathcal{K}}_{0},0)=0
implies that
L
forms a packing with respect to
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
, we see that

(40)

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\mathrm{r}_{\text{pack},{\mathcal{K}}}(L)\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)\leq\left(\frac{2}{\alpha}\right)^{n}.

Recall that
m_{L}
denotes the Haar measure on
\mathbb{T}_{L}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/L
.
For the upper bound on the covering radius recall the basic fact (see, e.g.,
[
12
]
*Lemma 2.5) that

m_{L}\left(\pi_{L}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}{\mathcal{K}}\right)\right)>\frac{1}{2}\ \implies\ \mathrm{r}_{\text{cov},{\mathcal{K}}}(L)\leq\alpha.

It therefore only remains to upper bound
\Pr\big(m_{L}(\pi_{L}({\mathcal{K}}_{1}))\leq 1/2\big)
, where
{\mathcal{K}}_{1}=\frac{\alpha}{2}{\mathcal{K}}
. We do this by showing that
\mathbb{E}[m_{L}(\pi_{L}({\mathcal{K}}_{1}))]
is close to
1
. We begin by noting that

\displaystyle m_{L}(\pi_{L}({\mathcal{K}}_{1}))

\displaystyle=\int_{x\in{\mathcal{K}}_{1}}\frac{1}{|(x+L)\cap{\mathcal{K}}_{1}|}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{x\in{\mathcal{K}}_{1}}\frac{1}{1+N^{*}(L,{\mathcal{K}}_{1},-x)}dx.

Since the function
t\mapsto\frac{1}{1+t}
is convex in the regime
t>0
, we can apply Jensen’s inequality and obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[m_{L}(\pi_{L}({\mathcal{K}}_{1}))]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{x\in{\mathcal{K}}_{1}}\frac{1}{1+N^{*}(L,{\mathcal{K}}_{1},-x)}dx\right]

(41)

\displaystyle=\int_{x\in{\mathcal{K}}_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{1+N^{*}(L,{\mathcal{K}}_{1},-x)}\right]dx

(42)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{x\in{\mathcal{K}}_{1}}\frac{1}{1+\mathbb{E}\left[N^{*}(L,{\mathcal{K}}_{1},-x)\right]}dx

(43)

\displaystyle=\int_{x\in{\mathcal{K}}_{1}}\frac{1}{1+{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}}_{1})}dx

(44)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}}_{1})}{1+{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}}_{1})},

where (
41
) follows from Fubini’s Theorem, (
42
) from Jensen’s inequality and (
43
) from Siegel’s summation formula. Let

P_{e}=\Pr\left(m_{L}(\pi_{L}({\mathcal{K}}_{1}))\leq\frac{1}{2}\right).

Since
m_{L}(\pi_{L}({\mathcal{K}}_{1}))\leq 1
we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[m_{L}(\pi_{L}({\mathcal{K}}_{1}))]\leq\frac{P_{e}}{2}+(1-P_{e})=1-\frac{P_{e}}{2}.

Combining this with (
44
), we obtain

P_{e}\leq 2\left(1-\frac{{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}}_{1})}{1+{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}}_{1})}\right)<\frac{2}{{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}}_{1})}.

Thus,

(45)

\displaystyle\Pr(\mathrm{r}_{\text{cov},{\mathcal{K}}}(L)\geq\alpha)<2\cdot\left(\frac{2}{\alpha}\right)^{n}.

Combining (
40
) and (
45
) we obtain the claimed result.
∎

3.4.
On the monotonicity of
\alpha\mapsto\eta(\alpha{\mathcal{K}},L)

As mentioned above, the mapping
\alpha\mapsto\eta(\alpha{\mathcal{K}},L)
is not monotonically non-increasing in general.
Nevertheless, Lemma
3.8
, stated above, shows that for dilates by positive integers, the covering smoothness can only decrease. We can exploit this fact to establish Theorems
1.3
and
1.6
, which show that for any
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
with sufficiently large (polynomial) volume, a typical lattice has small
\eta(\alpha{\mathcal{K}},L)
for all
\alpha\geq 1
. We first provide the proof of Lemma
3.8
, and then leverage this result and prove Theorems
1.3
and
1.6
.

Proof of Lemma
3.8
.

We can write

N(L,m{\mathcal{K}},x)=N\left(\frac{L}{m},{\mathcal{K}},\frac{x}{m}\right)=\sum_{a}N\left(L,{\mathcal{K}},\frac{x}{m}-a\right)\;,

where the sums runs over all coset representatives
a
for the inclusion
L\subset\frac{L}{m}
.
We therefore have

\displaystyle{\eta}(m{\mathcal{K}},L)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|\frac{N(L,m{\mathcal{K}},x)}{{\mathrm{vol}}(m{\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)}-1\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|m^{-n}\sum_{a}\Big(\frac{N(L,{\mathcal{K}},x/m-a)}{{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)}-1\Big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq m^{-n}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\sum_{a}\left|\frac{N(L,{\mathcal{K}},x/m-a)}{{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)}-1\right|

\displaystyle\leq m^{-n}\sum_{a}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|\frac{N(L,{\mathcal{K}},x/m-a)}{{\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)}-1\right|={\eta}({\mathcal{K}},L)\;.

∎

Using this weak monotonicity property, we now show that if a lattice
L
smoothly covers
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with respect to
\alpha_{i}{\mathcal{K}}
for all
\alpha_{i}
in a dense enough net in
[1,2)
, it must smoothly cover
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with respect to
\alpha{\mathcal{K}}
for all
\alpha\geq 1
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
0<\varepsilon<1
. Let
\beta=\frac{\varepsilon}{8n}
and
I=\left\lceil\frac{\log 2}{\log(1+\beta)}\right\rceil
. Define
\alpha_{i}=(1+\beta)^{i}
for all
i=0,1,\ldots,I
, such that
\alpha_{0}=1
, and
\alpha_{I}\geq 2
. For a lattice
L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{Conv}_{n}}
assume that
{\eta}(\alpha_{i}{\mathcal{K}},L)\leq\varepsilon/2
for all
i=0,1,\ldots,I
. Then
{\eta}(\alpha{\mathcal{K}},L)<\varepsilon
for all
\alpha\geq 1
.

Proof.

Note that for any
\alpha\in[1,2)
there is
i\in\{1,\ldots,I\}
such that
\alpha_{i-1}\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{i}
. We therefore have that for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\frac{N(L,\alpha{\mathcal{K}},x)}{{\mathrm{vol}}(\alpha{\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\mathrm{vol}}(\alpha_{i}{\mathcal{K}})}{{\mathrm{vol}}(\alpha{\mathcal{K}})}\frac{N(L,\alpha_{i}{\mathcal{K}},x)}{{\mathrm{vol}}(\alpha_{i}{\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\beta)^{n}\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)<(1+\varepsilon),

where in the last inequality we used the fact that
(1+\beta)^{n}\leq e^{\beta n}\leq 1+2\beta n=1+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
, which follows since
e^{t}<1+2t
for
t<1/2
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{N(L,\alpha{\mathcal{K}},x)}{{\mathrm{vol}}(\alpha{\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{{\mathrm{vol}}(\alpha_{i-1}{\mathcal{K}})}{{\mathrm{vol}}(\alpha{\mathcal{K}})}\frac{N(L,\alpha_{i-1}{\mathcal{K}},x)}{{\mathrm{vol}}(\alpha_{i-1}{\mathcal{K}})/\mathrm{covol}(L)}

\displaystyle\geq(1+\beta)^{-n}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)>(1-\varepsilon),

where, as above, in the last inequality we used the fact that
(1+\beta)^{-n}\geq\frac{1}{1+2\beta n}\geq 1-2\beta n=1-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
. Thus,
{\eta}(\alpha{\mathcal{K}},L)<\varepsilon
for all
\alpha\in[1,2)
. Finally, for any
\alpha\geq 1
there is a positive integer
m
such
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha/m\in[1,2)
, and thus, by Lemma
3.8
,
{\eta}(\alpha{\mathcal{K}},L)<\varepsilon
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Assume

For all

1.2
with

and

3.5.
Non-lattice smooth coverings

In this subsection we will prove Theorem
1.7
. The proof follows the same outline and notation as the proof of Theorem
3.4
. In the previous sections we started with a lattice
L
with a reasonable
\rho_{{\mathcal{K}}}(L)
and constructed from it a denser lattice
L(S)
by choosing
S\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
to be a subspace.
The work of Dhar and Dvir
[
7
]
was then used to show that for any subset
A\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
, and a randomly uniform subspace
S\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
, if
p
is sufficiently large and
\frac{|S|\cdot|A|}{p^{n}}>p^{3}
, then
\eta_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(A,S)
is small with high probability. This was then leveraged for showing that under suitable conditions a randomly chosen subspace
S
will yield a lattice
L(S)
such that
L(S)+{\mathcal{K}}
smoothly covers
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Note that if
S\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
is not a subspace, the discrete set
L(S)
, as given in (
13
) is not a lattice, but is nevertheless well defined, and has asymptotic density
\frac{|S|}{\mathrm{covol}(L)}
. Furthermore, recall that the definition of
\eta_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(A,S)
does not require
S
to be a subspace. For a random set
S
(rather than a random subspace, as in
[
7
]
), controlling the tail of
\eta_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(A,S)
is a significantly simpler task. The following result easily follows from large deviation theory.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
n,m
be positive integers,
p
be a prime number and let
\delta,\tau\in(0,1)
. Let
S
be a set of
m
points identically distributed independently uniformly over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
. Then, for any set
A\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
, we have that if
\frac{m|A|}{p^{n}}>\frac{3}{\tau^{2}}\left(n\log p-\log\frac{\delta}{2}\right)
, then

\displaystyle\Pr({\eta_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}(A,S)\geq\tau\penalty\ \text{or}\penalty\ |S|\neq m)\leq\delta+m^{2}p^{-n}.

The lemma follows easily from the following well-known large deviations bound, see e.g.,
[
11
]
*Corollary 4.6.

Proposition 3.11

(Chernoff bound)
.

For any
\eta\in(0,1)
and any
m
identically distributed independent Bernoulli random variables
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}
, the sum
Y{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\sum_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}
satisfies

(49)

\Pr\left(\left|Y-\mu\right|\geq\eta\mu\right)\leq 2e^{-\frac{\eta^{2}\mu}{3}},

where
\mu{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\mathbb{E}(Y).

Proof of Lemma
3.10
.

Let
X_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i.i.d}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Uniform}({\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n})
for
i=1,\ldots,m
, and
S=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
. For any
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
let
Y_{i,x}
be the indicator of the event that
x+X_{i}\in A
. We clearly have that the random variables
\{Y_{i,x}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are i.i.d. Bernoulli with
\Pr(Y_{i,x}=1)=\frac{|A|}{p^{n}}
. Thus,
Y_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i,x}
satisfies the conditions of Proposition
3.11
, and applying it with
\mu=\mathbb{E}(Y_{x})=\frac{m|A|}{p^{n}}
and
\eta=\tau
, gives that if
\frac{m|A|}{p^{n}}>\frac{3}{\tau^{2}}\left(n\log p-\log\frac{\delta}{2}\right)
then

\displaystyle\Pr

\displaystyle\left(\left|\frac{|(x+S)\cap A|}{m\cdot|A|p^{-n}}-1\right|\geq\tau\right)

\displaystyle=\Pr(|Y_{x}-\mu|\geq\tau\mu)\leq 2e^{-\frac{\tau^{2}}{3}\frac{3}{\tau^{2}}\left(n\log p-\log\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}=\delta\cdot p^{-n}.

Applying the union bound, this implies that

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\max_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}}\left|\frac{|(x+S)\cap A|}{m\cdot|A|p^{-n}}-1\right|\geq\tau\right)\leq\delta.

Finally, noting that

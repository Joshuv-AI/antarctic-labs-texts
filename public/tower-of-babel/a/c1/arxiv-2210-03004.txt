Probability computation for high–dimensional semilinear SDEs driven by isotropic α − \alpha- stable processes via mild Kolmogorov equations
Alessandro Bondi

Abstract
Semilinear, N − N- dimensional stochastic differential equations (SDEs) driven by additive Lévy noise are investigated. Specifically, given α ∈ ( 1 2 , 1 ) , \alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right), the interest is on SDEs driven by 2 ​ α − 2\alpha- stable, rotation–invariant processes obtained by subordination of a Brownian motion. An original connection between the time–dependent Markov transition semigroup associated with their solutions and Kolmogorov backward equations in mild integral form is established via regularization–by–noise techniques. Such a link is the starting point for an iterative method which allows to approximate probabilities related to the SDEs with a single batch of Monte Carlo simulations as several parameters change, bringing a compelling computational advantage over the standard Monte Carlo approach. This method also pertains to the numerical computation of solutions to high–dimensional integro–differential Kolmogorov backward equations. The scheme, and in particular the first order approximation it provides, is then applied for two nonlinear vector fields and shown to offer satisfactory results in dimension N = 100 N=100 . Keywords: Kolmogorov equations, Semilinear SDEs, Iterative scheme, Isotropic α − \alpha- stable Lévy processes. MSC2020: 60G52, 60H50, 65C20, 45K05, 47D07.

\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)

N

W=\left[\beta^{1},\dots,\beta^{N}\right]^{\top}

\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)

\alpha-

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{iuL_{1}}\right]=\exp\left\{-\bar{\gamma}^{\alpha}\left|u\right|^{\alpha}\left(1-i\tan\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\text{sign}\,u\right)\right\},\quad u\in\mathbb{R},\text{ for some }\bar{\gamma}>0.

(3)

Let us introduce the diagonal matrices
A=-\text{diag}\left[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}\right]
and
Q=\text{diag}\left[\sigma^{2}_{1},\dots,\sigma^{2}_{N}\right]
, with
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{N}

\sigma^{2}_{n}>0,\,n=1,\dots,N

Given

dZ_{t}^{s,x}=\left(AZ_{t}^{s,x}+f\left(t\right)\right)dt+\sqrt{Q}\,dW_{L_{t}},\quad Z^{s,x}_{s}=x.

R_{s,t}\phi=\mathbb{E}\left[\phi\left(Z^{s,\cdot}_{t}\right)\right],\quad 0\leq s\leq t<T,\,\phi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right),

(6)

For every

\left|\mathbf{I}_{n}\right|\leq\left\lVert\nabla^{\top}\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\left(2\left\lVert DB_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X^{s,x}_{r_{n}}-X^{s,x}_{r}\right|\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|B_{0}\left(r_{n},X^{s,x}_{r}\right)-B_{0}\left(r,X^{s,x}_{r}\right)\right|\right]\right)\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0,

where we denote by
\left\lVert DB_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert DB_{0}\left(t,\cdot\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}
.
As for
\mathbf{II}_{n}
, note that
A\left(r\right)\phi
is continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and that for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
(see (

)),

\left|A\left(r\right)\phi\left(y\right)\right|\leq\left\lVert\nabla^{\top}\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\left(\left|A\right|\left|y\right|+\left\lVert B_{0}\right\rVert_{0,T}\right)\\
+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert D^{2}\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}1_{D}\left(z\right)\left|\sqrt{Q}z\right|^{2}\nu\left(dz\right)+2\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}1_{D^{c}}\left(z\right)\nu\left(dz\right).

(22)

Therefore by the continuous mapping and Vitali’s convergence theorem we obtain
\mathbf{II}_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, proving Point

(

⁢

i

)

.

(

⁢

i
i

)

, where it is sufficient to require
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
. Fix
0\leq r\leq t\leq T
; observe that for every
\psi\in C_{b}^{3}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
one has
A\left(r\right)\psi\in C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

\nabla A\left(r\right)\psi\left(x\right)=\nabla\psi\left(x\right)\left(A+DB_{0}\left(r,x\right)\right)+\left(Ax+B_{0}\left(r,x\right)\right)^{\top}D^{2}\psi\left(x\right)\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\nabla\psi\left(x+\sqrt{Q}z\right)-\nabla\psi\left(x\right)-1_{D}\left(z\right)\left(\sqrt{Q}z\right)^{\top}D^{2}\psi\left(x\right)\right]\nu\left(dz\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

More specifically, in the previous computation we are allowed to differentiate under the integral sign because

The hypotheses prescribe

Theorem 7
.

Take
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
.

\left(\emph{i}\right)

Let
0\leq s\leq T
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. If
B_{0}\in C^{0,1}_{b}\left(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and
\phi\in C_{b}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, then the function
t\mapsto P_{s,t}\phi\left(x\right)
is continuously differentiable in
\left[s,T\right]
and satisfies the Kolmogorov forward equation

\partial_{t}P_{s,t}\phi\left(x\right)=P_{s,t}A\left(t\right)\phi\left(x\right);

(23)

\left(\emph{ii}\right)

Let
0\leq t\leq T
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. If
B_{0}\in C^{0,3}_{b}\left(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and
\phi\in C_{b}^{3}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

s\mapsto P_{s,t}\phi\left(x\right)
is continuously differentiable in
\left[0,t\right]
and satisfies the Kolmogorov backward equation

\partial_{s}P_{s,t}\phi\left(x\right)=-A\left(s\right)P_{s,t}\phi\left(x\right).

(24)

Proof.

Recall that by
[
20
, Theorem
14.7
(iii)]
the process
W_{L}
is centered in
0
when
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
. As a consequence, denoting by
N
the Poisson random measure associated with its jumps and by
\widetilde{N}
the compensated measure,
W_{L}=\int_{0}^{\cdot}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}1_{D}\left(z\right)z\widetilde{N}\left(ds,dz\right)+\int_{0}^{\cdot}1_{D^{c}}\left(z\right)zN\left(ds,\,dz\right)
up to indistinguishability by
[
12
, Theorem
2.34
, Chapter II]
.

As for Point

(

⁢

i

)

, take
0\leq s\leq T,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\phi\in C^{2}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
; by (

\phi\left(X^{s,x}_{t}\right)=\phi\left(x\right)+\int_{s}^{t}\left\langle AX^{s,x}_{r}+B_{0}\left(r,X^{s,x}_{r}\right),\nabla^{\top}\phi\left(X_{r}^{s,x}\right)\right\rangle dr+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}1_{D}\left(z\right)\nabla\phi\left(X^{s,x}_{r-}\right)\sqrt{Q}z\,\widetilde{N}\left(dr,dz\right)\\
+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\phi\left(X_{r-}^{s,x}+\sqrt{Q}z\right)-\phi\left(X_{r-}^{s,x}\right)-1_{D}\left(z\right)\nabla\phi\left(X^{s,x}_{r-}\right)\sqrt{Q}z\right){N}\left(dr,dz\right),

which holds true
\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.}
for every
t\in\left[s,T\right]
. Taking expectations in the previous equation and using Fubini’s theorem we obtain

P_{s,t}\phi\left(x\right)=\phi\left(x\right)+\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[A\left(r\right)\phi\left(X^{s,x}_{r}\right)\right]dr=\phi\left(x\right)+\int_{s}^{t}P_{s,r}A\left(r\right)\phi\left(x\right)dr,\quad t\in\left[s,T\right],

which in turn implies (
23
) by Lemma
6

(

⁢

i

)

.

We now focus on Point

(

⁢

i
i

)

. Take
0\leq t\leq T
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
; arguing as in
[

, Proposition

]
we see that
X^{\cdot,x}_{t}
follows the backward dynamics (
\mathbb{P}-

X^{s,x}_{t}=x+\int_{s}^{t}DX^{r,x}_{t}\left(Ax+B_{0}\left(r,x\right)\right)dr+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[X^{r,x+\sqrt{Q}z}_{t}-X^{r,x}_{t}-1_{D}\left(z\right)DX^{r,x}_{t}\sqrt{Q}z\right]\nu\left(dz\right)dr\\

Hence invoking the backward Itô formula (see, e.g.,

\!\!\!\!\phi\left(X^{s,x}_{t}\right)=\phi\left(x\right)+\int_{s}^{t}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\phi\left(X_{t}^{r,x+\sqrt{Q}z}\right)-\phi\left(X^{r,x}_{t}\right)\right)\widetilde{N}\left(dr,dz\right)\!+\int_{s}^{t}\!\nabla\phi\left(X^{r,x}_{t}\right)DX^{r,x}_{t}\left(Ax+B_{0}\left(r,x\right)\right)dr\\
+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\phi\left(X_{t}^{r,x+\sqrt{Q}z}\right)-\phi\left(X^{r,x}_{t}\right)-1_{D}\left(z\right)\nabla\phi\left(X^{r,x}_{t}\right)DX^{r,x}_{t}\sqrt{Q}z\right]\nu\left(dz\right)dr,

\mathbb{P}-

Another step that we need to prove Theorem
3
consists in a regularization result for the time–dependent Markov transition semigroup
P_{s,t}
(see Lemma
10
) which –at the best of our knowledge– is not established in literature with this type of noise. We start by recalling the
Bismut–Elworthy–Li’s type formula
presented in
[
22
, Theorem
1.1
]
(see also
[
21
]
for a related work treating multiplicative Lévy noise); such a formula is adapted to our framework, where we have to account for an initial time
s
not necessarily equal to
0
.

Theorem 8

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
and
B_{0}\in C_{b}^{0,1}\left(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Then for every
0\leq s<t\leq T
and
\phi\in C^{1}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, the function
P_{s,t}\phi
is differentiable at
x
in every direction
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and

\left\langle\nabla^{\top}P_{s,t}\phi\left(x\right),h\right\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{L_{t}-L_{s}}\phi\left({X}_{t}^{s,x}\right)\int_{s}^{t}\left\langle\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}D_{h}{X}^{s,x}_{r},dW_{L_{r}}\right\rangle\right].

(26)

Furthermore, there exists a constant
{C}_{\alpha}>0
such that the next gradient estimate holds true:

\left\lVert\nabla^{\top}P_{s,t}\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq{C}_{\alpha}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\left|\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}\right|e^{\left(\left|A\right|+\left\lVert DB_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}\right)T}\frac{1}{\left(t-s\right)^{1/\left(2\alpha\right)}},\quad 0\leq s<t\leq T.

(27)

We are able to extend the previous claim to functions
\phi\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
with an approximation procedure, effectively making Theorem
8
a regularization–by–noise result. We need the next estimate, which derives from
[
4
, Eq.
(14)
]
:

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{L_{t}^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\,t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}},\quad t>0,\text{ for some $c=c\left({\alpha,p}\right)>0$, for every $p>0$.}

(28)

Corollary 9
.

Let
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
and
B_{0}\in C_{b}^{0,1}\left(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Then, for every
\phi\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and
0\leq s<t\leq T
, the function
P_{s,t}\phi
is differentiable at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
in every direction
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and the expression in (
26
) holds true.

Proof.

Fix
x,h\in\mathbb{R}^{N},0\leq s<t\leq T
, and
\phi\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Since
C_{b}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
is dense in
C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, we can take a sequence
\left(\phi_{n}\right)_{n}\subset C_{b}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
such that
\left\lVert\phi_{n}-\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Denote by
g_{n}\left(u\right)=P_{s,t}\phi_{n}\left(x+uh\right),\,u\in\mathbb{R}
; by dominated convergence, for every
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

g_{n}\left(u\right)\to P_{s,t}\phi\left(x+uh\right)\eqqcolon g\left(u\right),\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Now we invoke (
26
) to write

g_{n}^{\prime}\left({u}\right)=\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{n}\left(X_{t}^{s,x+uh+vh}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{n}\left(X_{t}^{s,x+uh}\right)\right]}{v}=\left\langle\nabla^{\top}P_{s,t}\phi_{n}\left(x+uh\right),h\right\rangle\\
=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{L_{t}-L_{s}}\phi_{n}\left(X_{t}^{s,x+uh}\right)\int_{s}^{t}\left\langle\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}D_{h}X^{s,x+{u}h}_{r},dW_{L_{r}}\right\rangle\right],\quad{u}\in\mathbb{R}.

Since
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right),
an application of
[
22
, Theorem
3.2
]
, (
28
), Hölder’s inequality with
p\in\left(1,2\alpha\right)
and Lemma
4
(see (
19
)) let us compute

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{1}{L_{t}-L_{s}}\left(\phi_{n}\left(X_{t}^{s,x+{u}h}\right)-\phi\left(X_{t}^{s,x+{u}h}\right)\right)\int_{s}^{t}\left\langle\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}D_{h}X^{s,x+{u}h}_{r},dW_{L_{r}}\right\rangle\right|\right]\\
\leq\left|\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}\right|\left|h\right|\frac{c_{1}}{\left(t-s\right)^{1/\alpha}}\left\lVert\phi_{n}-\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\to 0,\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

(29)

where
c_{1}=c_{1}\left(\alpha,p,A,B_{0},T,N\right)>0
. It follows that

g_{n}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{1}{L_{t}-L_{s}}\phi\left(X_{t}^{s,x+\left(\cdot\right)h}\right)\int_{s}^{t}\left\langle\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}D_{h}X^{s,x+\left(\cdot\right)h}_{r},dW_{L_{r}}\right\rangle\right],\quad\text{uniformly in }\mathbb{R}.

This suffices to obtain the desired result, hence the proof is complete.
∎

Note that for every
\phi\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
the expression on the right–hand side of (
26
) is continuous in
x
for every
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Indeed, let us fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and consider
\left(x_{n}\right)_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
x_{n}\to x
as
n\to\infty.
Then, using the same techniques as in the previous proof (cfr. (
29
)), together with Lemma
4
and a dominated convergence argument, we get (for some
p,q>1
determined by a generalized Holder’s inequality, and
c=c\left(\alpha,p,q,A,B_{0},T,N\right)>0
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{L_{t}-L_{s}}\left(\phi\left({X}_{t}^{s,x_{n}}\right)\int_{s}^{t}\left\langle\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}D_{h}{X}^{s,x_{n}}_{r},dW_{L_{r}}\right\rangle-\phi\left({X}_{t}^{s,x}\right)\int_{s}^{t}\left\langle\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}D_{h}{X}^{s,x}_{r},dW_{L_{r}}\right\rangle\right)\right]\right|\\
\leq\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{L_{t}-L_{s}}\left|\int_{s}^{t}\left\langle\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}\left(D_{h}{X}^{s,x_{n}}_{r}-D_{h}{X}^{s,x}_{r}\right),dW_{L_{r}}\right\rangle\right|\right]\\
+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{L_{t}-L_{s}}\left|\int_{s}^{t}\left\langle\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}D_{h}{X}^{s,x}_{r},dW_{L_{r}}\right\rangle\right|\left|\phi\left({X}^{s,x_{n}}_{t}\right)-\phi\left({X}^{s,x}_{t}\right)\right|\right]\leq\frac{c}{\left(t-s\right)^{1/\alpha}}\left|\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}\right|\\
\times\!\left[\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\!\left(\int_{s}^{t}\!\mathbb{E}\left[\left|D_{h}{X}^{s,x_{n}}_{r}-D_{h}{X}^{s,x}_{r}\right|^{2\alpha}\right]\!dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+\left|h\right|\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\phi\left({X}^{s,x_{n}}_{t}\right)-\phi\left({X}^{s,x}_{t}\right)\right|^{q}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.\end{split}

Therefore,
P_{s,t}\phi\in C_{b}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
for every

Proof of Theorem

Fix

and define
B=B_{0}-f
; we first consider

Recalling (
4
), we introduce the family of integro–differential operators
\big(\widetilde{A}\left(s\right)\big)_{0\leq s\leq T}
, defined for every
\psi\in C^{2}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
by

\widetilde{A}\left(s\right)\psi\left(x\right)=\left\langle Ax+f\left(s\right),\nabla^{\top}\psi\left(x\right)\right\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\psi\left(x+\sqrt{Q}z\right)-\psi\left(x\right)-1_{D}\left(z\right)\nabla\psi\left(x\right)\sqrt{Q}z\right]\nu\left(dz\right),

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let us take
0\leq s<t,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},
and observe that by the definition in (
21
) and Corollary
5
there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for every
r_{1},\,r_{2}\in\left[s,t\right]
,

\sup_{u\in\left[s,t\right]}\left|A\left(u\right)P_{u,t}\phi\left(Z^{s,x}_{r_{2}}\right)-A\left(u\right)P_{u,t}\phi\left(Z^{s,x}_{r_{1}}\right)\right|\\
\leq C\left[\left(\left|A\right|\left(1+\left|Z^{s,x}_{r_{1}}\right|\right)+\left\lVert B_{0}\right\rVert_{1,T}\right)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(1_{D}\left(z\right)\left|\sqrt{Q}z\right|^{2}+1_{D^{c}}\left(z\right)\right)\nu\left(dz\right)\right]\left|Z^{s,x}_{r_{2}}-Z^{s,x}_{r_{1}}\right|.

(30)

We study the mapping
\left[s,t\right]\ni r\mapsto R_{s,r}\left(P_{r,t}\phi\right)\left(x\right)
: using (
25
) and (
30
), it is easy to argue that it is continuous in its domain by Theorem
7

(

⁢

i
i

)

coupled with Vitali’s and dominated convergence theorems. It is also differentiable, with

\partial_{r}R_{s,r}\left(P_{r,t}\phi\right)\left(x\right)=R_{s,r}\left(\widetilde{A}\left(r\right)P_{r,t}\phi\right)\left(x\right)-R_{s,r}\left(A\left(r\right)P_{r,t}\phi\right)\left(x\right)\\
=-R_{s,r}\left(\left\langle B\left(r,\cdot\right),\nabla^{\top}P_{r,t}\phi\right\rangle\right)\left(x\right),\quad r\in\left[s,t\right].

(31)

Indeed, take
r\in\left[s,t\right]
and a generic sequence
\left(r_{n}\right)_{n}\subset\left[s,t\right]\setminus\left\{r\right\}
such that
r_{n}\to r
as
n\to\infty
; then

\frac{R_{s,r_{n}}\left(P_{r_{n},t}\phi\right)\left(x\right)-R_{s,r}\left(P_{r,t}\phi\right)\left(x\right)}{r_{n}-r}=R_{s,r_{n}}\left(\frac{P_{r_{n},t}\phi-P_{r,t}\phi}{r_{n}-r}\right)\left(x\right)+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{P_{r,t}\phi\left(Z^{s,x}_{r_{n}}\right)-P_{r,t}\phi\left(Z^{s,x}_{r}\right)}{r_{n}-r}\right]\\
\eqqcolon\mathbf{I}_{n}+\mathbf{II}_{n}.

We immediately notice that
\mathbf{II}_{n}\to R_{s,r}\left(\widetilde{A}\left(r\right)P_{r,t}\phi\right)\left(x\right)
as
n\to\infty
by Theorem
7

(

⁢

i

)

and Corollary
5
. As for
\mathbf{I}_{n}
, we split it again as follows:

\mathbf{I}_{n}=R_{s,r}\left(\frac{P_{r_{n},t}\phi-P_{r,t}\phi}{r_{n}-r}\right)\left(x\right)+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{P_{r_{n},t}\phi-P_{r,t}\phi}{r_{n}-r}\left(Z^{s,x}_{r_{n}}\right)-\frac{P_{r_{n},t}\phi-P_{r,t}\phi}{r_{n}-r}\left(Z^{s,x}_{r}\right)\right]\eqqcolon\mathbf{III}_{n}+\mathbf{IV}_{n}.

By a dominated convergence argument based on (
22
), (
25
), Corollary
5
and Theorem
7

(

⁢

i
i

)

we have
\mathbf{III}_{n}\to-R_{s,r}\left(A\left(r\right)P_{r,t}\phi\right)\left(x\right)
as
n\to\infty
. Finally we focus on
\mathbf{IV}_{n}
, estimating by (
25
)

\left|\mathbf{IV}_{n}\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{u\in\left[s,t\right]}\left|A\left(u\right)P_{u,t}\phi\left(Z^{s,x}_{r_{n}}\right)-A\left(u\right)P_{u,t}\phi\left(Z^{s,x}_{r}\right)\right|\right].

Notice that the random variables inside the expected value in the previous inequality converge to
0
in probability as
n\to\infty
by (
30
). Such a convergence is true also in the
L^{1}-
sense, thanks to the estimates in (
22
) and Vitali’s convergence theorem. Thus,
\mathbf{IV}_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, fact which completely shows (
31
). Observe that
\partial_{r}R_{s,r}\left(P_{r,t}\phi\right)\left(x\right)
is continuous in
\left[s,t\right]
by Vitali’s and dominated convergence theorems, the mean value theorem, Corollary
5
and the continuity of the mapping
r\mapsto\nabla P_{r,t}\phi\left(x\right)
in
\left[s,t\right]
(see (
25
) and the subsequent sentence). Therefore we can integrate it with respect to
r
on the interval
\left[s,t\right]
and infer that

P_{s,t}\phi\left(x\right)=R_{s,t}\phi\left(x\right)+\int_{s}^{t}R_{s,r}\left(\left\langle B\left(r,\cdot\right),\nabla^{\top}P_{r,t}\phi\right\rangle\right)\left(x\right)dr,

(32)

which coincides with (
11
).

Next, we take
\phi\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and consider a sequence
\left(\phi_{n}\right)_{n}\subset C^{3}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
such that
\left\lVert\phi_{n}-\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Since by (
27
) and Lemma
10
(for some constant
{C}_{\alpha}>0
)

\left|\int_{s}^{t}R_{s,r}\left(\left\langle B\left(r,\cdot\right),\nabla^{\top}P_{r,t}\left(\phi_{n}-\phi\right)\right\rangle\right)\left(x\right)dr\right|\\
\leq{C}_{\alpha}\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{0,T}\left\lVert\phi_{n}-\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\left|\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}\right|e^{\left(\left|A\right|+\left\lVert DB_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}\right)T}\left(\int_{s}^{t}\frac{dr}{\left(t-r\right)^{1/\left(2\alpha\right)}}\right)\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0,

by dominated convergence it is immediate to get the validity of (
32
) for
\phi
, as well.

Finally, we tackle the case
\phi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. We consider
\phi
to be the indicator function of an open set to begin with. Then, by Urysohn’s lemma there exists a sequence
\left(\phi_{n}\right)_{n}\subset C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
such that
0\leq\phi_{n}\leq\phi
and
\phi_{n}\to\phi
pointwise as
n\to\infty
. By construction and dominated convergence we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(P_{s,t}\phi_{n}\left(x\right)-R_{s,t}\phi_{n}\left(x\right)\right)=P_{s,t}\phi\left(x\right)-R_{s,t}\phi\left(x\right).

(33)

Now we focus on the integral term in (
32
). Let us fix
y,h\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,r\in\left(s,t\right)
and
u\in\left(r,t\right)
. Then, exploiting the Chapman–Kolmogorov equations and (
26
), we write (
n\in\mathbb{N}
)

\left\langle\nabla^{\top}P_{r,t}\phi_{n}\left(y\right),h\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla^{\top}\left(P_{r,u}\left(P_{u,t}\phi_{n}\right)\right)\left(y\right),h\right\rangle\\
=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{L_{u}-L_{r}}P_{u,t}\phi_{n}\left(X^{r,y}_{u}\right)\int_{r}^{u}\left\langle\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}D_{h}X_{v}^{r,y},dW_{L_{v}}\right\rangle\right].

(34)

Since, with the same argument as in (
33
),
P_{u,t}\phi_{n}\to P_{u,t}\phi
pointwise in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
as
n\to\infty,
and (see, e.g., (
29
))

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\frac{P_{u,t}\phi_{n}\left(X^{r,y}_{u}\right)}{L_{u}-L_{r}}\int_{r}^{u}\left\langle\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}D_{h}X_{v}^{r,y},dW_{L_{v}}\right\rangle\right|\leq\frac{1}{L_{u}-L_{r}}\left|\int_{r}^{u}\left\langle\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}D_{h}X_{v}^{r,y},dW_{L_{v}}\right\rangle\right|\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{P}\right),

we can pass to the limit in (
34
) to obtain, by dominated convergence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\langle\nabla^{\top}P_{r,t}\phi_{n}\left(y\right),h\right\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{L_{u}-L_{r}}P_{u,t}\phi\left(X^{r,y}_{u}\right)\int_{r}^{u}\left\langle\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}D_{h}X_{v}^{r,y},dW_{L_{v}}\right\rangle\right]\\
=\left\langle\nabla^{\top}\left(P_{r,u}\left(P_{u,t}\phi\right)\right)\left(y\right),h\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla^{\top}P_{r,t}\phi\left(y\right),h\right\rangle.

Observe that the second–to–last equality in the previous equation is due to (
26
) and Lemma
10
. As a consequence, for every
r\in\left(s,t\right)
we infer that

\lim_{n\to\infty}R_{s,r}\left(\left\langle B\left(r,\cdot\right),\nabla^{\top}P_{r,t}\phi_{n}\right\rangle\right)\left(x\right)=R_{s,r}\left(\left\langle B\left(r,\cdot\right),\nabla^{\top}P_{r,t}\phi\right\rangle\right)\left(x\right),

where we use once again the dominated convergence theorem, thanks to the next bound that we get using (
27
) and Lemma
10
:

\left\lVert\left\langle B\left(r,\cdot\right),\nabla^{\top}P_{r,t}\phi_{n}\right\rangle\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq{C}_{\alpha}\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{0,T}\left|\left(\sqrt{Q}\right)^{-1}\right|e^{\left(\left|A\right|+\left\lVert DB_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}\right)T}\frac{1}{\left(t-r\right)^{1/\left(2\alpha\right)}}.

Moreover, this inequality also allows to pass the limit under the integral sign, so that we end up with

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{s}^{t}R_{s,r}\left(\left\langle B\left(r,\cdot\right),\nabla^{\top}P_{r,t}\phi_{n}\right\rangle\right)\left(x\right)dr=\int_{s}^{t}R_{s,r}\left(\left\langle B\left(r,\cdot\right),\nabla^{\top}P_{r,t}\phi\right\rangle\right)\left(x\right)dr.

(35)

Combining (
33
)-(
35
) we conclude that (
32
) holds true for
\phi
, i.e., for every indicator function of an open set.

Note that the passages of the previous step do not require the continuity of the approximating functions
\left(\phi_{n}\right)_{n}
, as long as they are equibounded, satisfy (
32
) and converge pointwise to
\phi
. Therefore, we can state that (
32
) holds true for every
\phi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
by the functional monotone class theorem (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Theorem
2.12.9
]
).

We notice that, from (
32
), the continuity of
P_{\cdot,t}\phi\left(x\right),\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, in the interval
\left[0,t\right)
can be argued by dominated convergence (see (
41
) below for an analogous computation). Furthermore, the measurability of
P_{s,t}\phi\left(x\right)
with respect to
\left(s,x\right)
is a consequence of the measurability of the stochastic flow
X^{s,x}_{t}\left(\omega\right)
and Tonelli’s theorem.
These facts, together with Lemma
10
and the gradient estimate in (
27
), entail that
P_{t-\diamond,t}\phi\left(\cdot\right)\in\Lambda^{\gamma}_{1}\left(0,t\right],\,\gamma=1/\left(2\alpha\right).
Recalling Theorem
1
the proof is complete.

\blacksquare

Remark 1
.

where
u_{s}^{\widetilde{\phi}}\left(r,x\right)
is the unique solution of (
11
) such that

4
The iteration scheme

Let
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right),\,t\in\left(0,T\right],\,u_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and consider
B_{0}\in C_{b}^{0,3}\left(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),\,f\in C\left(\left[0,T\right];\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, so that Theorem
3
holds true. The proof of Theorem
1
(see, in particular, (
12
)-(
16
)) suggests to approximate the unique solution
u^{u_{0}}_{s}\left(t,x\right)(=P_{s,t}u_{0}\left(x\right))
of (
11
) such that
u^{u_{0}}_{t-\diamond}\left(t,\cdot\right)\in\Lambda^{\gamma}_{1}\left(0,t\right],\,\gamma=1/\left(2\alpha\right),
with the iterates

\begin{cases}u^{n+1}_{s}\left(t,x\right)=R_{s,t}u_{0}\left(x\right)+\int_{s}^{t}R_{s,r}\left(\left\langle B\left(r,\cdot\right),\nabla^{\top}u_{r}^{n}\left(t,\cdot\right)\right\rangle\right)\left(x\right)dr\\
u^{0}_{s}\left(t,x\right)=R_{s,t}u_{0}\left(x\right)\end{cases},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,s\in\left[0,t\right],\,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}.

Here we recall that
B=B_{0}-f.

If we define
v^{0}_{s}\left(t,x\right)=u^{0}_{s}\left(t,x\right)
and
v^{n+1}_{s}\left(t,x\right)=u^{n+1}_{s}\left(t,x\right)-u^{n}_{s}\left(t,x\right),\,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}
, then these new functions satisfy the iteration scheme

\begin{cases}v_{s}^{n+1}\left(t,x\right)=\int_{s}^{t}R_{s,u}k_{u,t}^{n}\left(x\right)du\\
k^{n}_{u,t}\left(x\right)=\left\langle B\left(u,x\right),\nabla^{\top}v_{u}^{n}\left(t,x\right)\right\rangle\\
v_{s}^{0}\left(t,x\right)=R_{s,t}u_{0}\left(x\right)\end{cases},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,\,s\in\left[0,t\right],\,u\in\left[0,t\right),\,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}.

(36)

In the Brownian case, (
36
) has been investigated in
[
10
]
. In order to study the convergence of
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}v^{n}_{s}\left(t,x\right)
to
u^{u_{0}}_{s}\left(t,x\right)
(in a sense that will be clarified later on), we need the next, preliminary result.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right),\,t\in\left(0,T\right],\,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}
and denote by
\gamma=1/\left(2\alpha\right)
. Then
k^{n}_{u,t}\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and
v^{n}_{s}\left(t,\cdot\right)\in C_{b}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
for every
u,\,s\in\left[0,t\right)
.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C\left(\alpha,A,Q\right)>0
such that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\left[0,t\right)
,

\left\lVert v^{n}_{s}\left(t,\cdot\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C^{n}\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{0,T}^{n}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t-s}ds_{n}\int_{0}^{s_{n}}ds_{n-1}\dots\int_{0}^{s_{2}}ds_{1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\left(s_{i+1}-s_{i}\right)^{\gamma}},

(37)

and

\left\lVert\nabla^{\top}v^{n}_{s}\left(t,\cdot\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C^{n+1}\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{0,T}^{n}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t-s}ds_{n}\int_{0}^{s_{n}}ds_{n-1}\dots\int_{0}^{s_{2}}ds_{1}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\frac{1}{\left(s_{i+1}-s_{i}\right)^{\gamma}},

(38)

where
s_{0}=0
and
s_{n+1}=t-s
.

We notice that the constant
C
in (
37
)-(
38
) is the same as the one appearing in the gradient estimate (
10
).

Proof.

We proceed by induction to prove that, for every
u,s\in\left[0,t\right)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}
, one has
v^{n}_{s}\left(t,\cdot\right)\in C_{b}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),\,k^{n}_{u,t}\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and

\left\lVert k^{n}_{u,t}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C^{n+1}\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{0,T}^{n+1}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}\int_{u}^{t}ds_{1}\int_{s_{1}}^{t}ds_{2}\dots\int_{s_{n-1}}^{t}ds_{n}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\frac{1}{\left(s_{i+1}-s_{i}\right)^{\gamma}},

(39)

where
C=C\left(\alpha,A,Q\right)>0
is the same constant as in (
10
). In (
39
),
s_{0}=u
and
s_{n+1}=t.
The estimates in (
37
)-(
38
) are an immediate consequence of (
39
) upon shifting the domain of integration and applying Tonelli’s theorem.

For
n=0
, the smoothing effect of the time–dependent Markov semigroup
R
guarantees that
v^{0}_{s}\left(t,\cdot\right)\in C_{b}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, which combined with the continuity of
B
yields
k^{0}_{u,t}\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),
with

\left\lVert k^{0}_{u,t}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{0,T}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(t-u\right)^{\gamma}}.

(40)

To fix the ideas, consider the case
n=1
. Since
k^{0}_{u,t}\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
for every
0\leq u<t
, the dominated convergence theorem, (
36
) and (
40
) imply that
v^{1}_{s}\left(t,\cdot\right)\in C^{1}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),
with
\nabla v_{s}^{1}\left(t,x\right)=\int_{s}^{t}\nabla R_{s,u}k_{u,t}^{0}\left(x\right)du,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Hence
k^{1}_{u,t}\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, and by (
10
)-(
40
) we get

\left\lVert k^{1}_{u,t}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C^{2}\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{0,T}^{2}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}\int_{u}^{t}ds_{1}\frac{1}{\left(s_{1}-u\right)^{\gamma}\left(t-s_{1}\right)^{\gamma}}.

Suppose now that our statement holds true at step
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then by the same argument as before and (
39
)
v^{n+1}_{s}\left(t,\cdot\right)\in C_{b}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, with
\nabla v_{s}^{n+1}\left(t,x\right)=\int_{s}^{t}\nabla R_{s,u}k_{u,t}^{n}\left(x\right)du
. Therefore
k^{n+1}_{u,t}\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, with

\left\lVert k^{n+1}_{u,t}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{0,T}\int_{u}^{t}ds_{1}\frac{1}{\left(s_{1}-u\right)^{\gamma}}\left\lVert k^{n}_{s_{1},t}\right\rVert_{\infty}\\
\leq C^{n+2}\left\lVert B\right\rVert^{n+2}_{0,T}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}\int_{u}^{t}ds_{1}\int_{s_{1}}^{t}ds_{2}\dots\int_{s_{n}}^{t}ds_{n+1}\prod_{i=0}^{n+1}\frac{1}{\left(s_{i+1}-s_{i}\right)^{\gamma}},

where in the last inequality we apply the inductive hypothesis and consider
s_{0}=u,\,s_{n+2}=t
.
Thus, the claim is completely proved.
∎

Another important property of the functions
v^{n}_{\cdot}\left(t,x\right),\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, is the continuity in the interval
\left[0,t\right)
. In the case
n=0
, this follows from the property of
R
discussed in Section
2
; for a generic
n\in\mathbb{N},
it can be argued by (
39
) and dominated convergence writing

v^{n}_{s}\left(t,x\right)=\int_{0}^{t}1_{\left\{u>s\right\}}R_{s,u}k^{n-1}_{u,t}\left(x\right)du.

(41)

Thanks to the estimates in (
37
)-(
38
), the convergence of the iteration scheme (
36
) is proved in the same way as in the Brownian case with no time–shift, see
[
9
, Section
2.4
]
. Overall, the next result is true.

Theorem 12
.

For every
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
and
0<t\leq T
, the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}v^{n}_{s}\left(t,x\right)
converges uniformly in
\left[0,t\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\nabla^{\top}v^{n}_{s}\left(t,x\right)
converges uniformly in
\left[0,t_{0}\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for every
t_{0}\in\left(0,t\right)
. In particular,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}v^{n}_{s}\left(t,x\right)=u^{u_{0}}_{s}\left(t,x\right),\quad s\in\left[0,t\right],\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
u^{u_{0}}_{s}\left(t,x\right)
is the unique solution of (
11
) such that
u^{u_{0}}_{t-\diamond}\left(t,\cdot\right)\in\Lambda^{\gamma}_{1}\left(0,t\right],\,\gamma=1/\left(2\alpha\right)
.

5
The first term of the iteration scheme

Let
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
. The goal of this section is to study the first term
v_{s}^{1}\left(t,x\right)=\int_{s}^{t}R_{s,u}k^{0}_{u,t}\left(x\right)du
of (
36
) for every
0\leq s<t\leq T
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In particular, starting from

k^{0}_{u,t}\left(y\right)=\left\langle B\left(u,y\right),\nabla^{\top}R_{u,t}u_{0}\left(y\right)\right\rangle,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,u\in\left(s,t\right),

(42)

we want to find an alternative, explicit expression (see Lemma
14
) for

R_{s,u}k^{0}_{u,t}\left(x\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[k^{0}_{u,t}\left(Z_{u}^{s,x}\right)\right].

(43)

In order to do this, we propose an approach which at first analyzes a deterministic time–shift, and then allows to recover the subordinated Brownian motion case by conditioning with respect to
\mathcal{F}^{L}
. The results of this part represent the base case for the induction argument that we will develop to compute the general term
v^{n+1}_{s}\left(t,x\right),\,n\geq 1
(see Section
6
).

5.1
Deterministic time–shift

Denote by
\mathbb{S}
the set of real–valued, strictly increasing càdlàg functions defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and starting at
0
. Take
\ell\in\mathbb{S}
and note that
W_{\ell}=\left(W_{\ell_{t}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is a càdlàg martingale with respect to the filtration
\left(\mathcal{F}^{W}_{\ell_{t}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
, where
\left(\mathcal{F}^{W}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is the minimal augmented filtration generated by
W
. For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
0\leq s<T
, the OU process
\left(Z_{t}^{\ell}\left(s,x\right)\right)_{t\in\left[s,T\right]}
is the unique, càdlàg solution of the linear SDE

dZ^{\ell}_{t}\left(s,x\right)=\left(AZ^{\ell}_{t}\left(s,x\right)+f\left(t\right)\right)dt+\sqrt{Q}\,dW_{\ell_{t}},\quad Z^{\ell}_{s}\left(s,x\right)=x.

It can be expressed with a variation of constants formula as follows:

Z^{\ell}_{t}\left(s,x\right)=e^{\left(t-s\right)A}x+\int_{s}^{t}e^{\left(t-r\right)A}f\left(r\right)dr+\int_{s}^{t}e^{\left(t-r\right)A}\sqrt{Q}\,dW_{\ell_{r}},\quad t\in\left[s,T\right].

For every
0\leq s<t\leq T
, define

I^{\ell}_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}e^{2\left(t-r\right)A}Q\,d\ell_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
. It is possible to argue as in
[
5
, Equation
\left(12\right)
]
to deduce that

Z^{\ell}_{t}\left(s,x\right)\sim\mathcal{N}\left(e^{\left(t-s\right)A}x+F_{s,t},\,I^{\ell}_{s,t}\right).

Note that, for every
0\leq s<u<t\leq T
,

Z^{\ell}_{t}\left(s,x\right)=e^{\left(t-u\right)A}Z^{\ell}_{u}\left(s,x\right)+F_{u,t}+\int_{u}^{t}e^{\left(t-r\right)A}\sqrt{Q}\,dW_{\ell_{r}},\quad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.,}

therefore
\left(Z^{\ell}\left(s,x\right)\right)_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is a family of
\left(\mathcal{F}_{\ell_{t}}\right)_{t\in\left[s,T\right]}-
Markov processes as
s
varies in
\left[0,T\right)
. In particular, its transition probability kernels
\mu^{\ell}_{u,t}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\to\left[0,1\right]
are

\mu_{u,t}^{\ell}\left(y,\cdot\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(e^{\left(t-u\right)A}y+F_{u,t},I^{\ell}_{u,t}\right),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(44)

In the sequel, we denote by
\phi_{u,t}^{\ell}\left(y,\cdot\right)
the density of
\mu^{\ell}_{u,t}\left(y,\cdot\right).

Straightforward changes to
[
5
, Theorem
4
]
ensure that, for any
0\leq s<t\leq T,
the function
\mathbb{E}\left[u_{0}\left(Z^{\ell}_{t}\left(s,\cdot\right)\right)\right]\in C_{b}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, with derivative at any point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
in every direction
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
given by

\left\langle\nabla^{\top}\mathbb{E}\left[u_{0}\left(Z^{\ell}_{t}\left(s,x\right)\right)\right],h\right\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left[u_{0}\left(Z_{t}^{\ell}\left(s,x\right)\right)\left\langle\left(I_{s,t}^{\ell}\right)^{-1}e^{\left(t-s\right)A}h,Z^{\ell}_{t}\left(s,x\right)-e^{\left(t-s\right)A}x-F_{s,t}\right\rangle\right].

(45)

With all these preliminaries in mind, we fix

Remark 2
.

The function

, defined by

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
4

In this appendix we provide the proof of Lemma
4
, a useful result for the arguments of Section
3
.

Proof of Lemma
4

Let us fix
0\leq s\leq T,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and a direction
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
; note that all the assertions of the statement are true for
\omega\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
by construction of the stochastic flow, hence we only focus on
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
. For every
\epsilon\in\left(0,1\right]
and
t\in\left[s,T\right]
define the incremental ratio function

\displaystyle Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon,t\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-1}\left(X_{t}^{s,x+\epsilon h}\left(\omega\right)-X_{t}^{s,x}\left(\omega\right)\right)

\displaystyle=h+\int_{s}^{t}\left(AY_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon,r\right)+\frac{B_{0}\left(r,X_{r}^{s,x}\left(\omega\right)+\epsilon Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon,r\right)\right)-B_{0}\left(r,X_{r}^{s,x}\left(\omega\right)\right)}{\epsilon}\right)dr

\displaystyle=h+\int_{s}^{t}\left(A+\int_{0}^{1}DB_{0}\left(r,X^{s,x}_{r}\left(\omega\right)+\rho\epsilon Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon,r\right)\right)d\rho\right)Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon,r\right)dr.

(65)

Notice that, for every
\epsilon\in\left(0,1\right]
(omitting
\omega
to keep notation short)

\left|X^{s,x+\epsilon h}_{t}-X^{s,x}_{t}\right|\leq\epsilon\left|h\right|+\left(\left|A\right|+\left\lVert DB_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}\right)\int_{s}^{t}\left|X^{s,x+\epsilon h}_{r}-X^{s,x}_{r}\right|dr,\quad t\in\left[s,T\right],

where we recall that
\left\lVert DB_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert DB_{0}\left(t,\cdot\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}
.
Thus, an application of Gronwall’s lemma shows that
\left|Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon,t\right)\right|\leq\left|h\right|e^{\left(\left|A\right|+\left\lVert DB_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}\right)T}\eqqcolon C_{1}
for all
t\in\left[s,T\right]
and
\epsilon\in\left(0,1\right]
.
Next, taking
\epsilon_{1},\,\epsilon_{2}\in\left(0,1\right]
and
t\in\left[s,T\right]
we compute from (
A
)

\displaystyle\left|Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{2},t\right)-Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{1},t\right)\right|\leq\int_{s}^{t}\left|A\right|\left|Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{2},r\right)-Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{1},r\right)\right|dr

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{s}^{t}\!\bigg(\!\!\int_{0}^{1}DB_{0}\left(r,X^{s,x}_{r}\!+\rho\epsilon_{2}Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{2},r\right)\right)d\rho\,Y_{x,h}^{1}\!\left(\epsilon_{2},r\right)-\!\int_{0}^{1}\!\!DB_{0}\left(r,X^{s,x}_{r}+\rho\epsilon_{1}Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{1},r\right)\right)d\rho\,Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{1},r\right)\!\!\bigg)dr\right|

\displaystyle\leq\!\left(\left|A\right|\!+\!\left\lVert DB_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}\right)\!\!\int_{s}^{t}\left|Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{2},r\right)-Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{1},r\right)\right|dr+\frac{N^{{2}}}{2}C_{1}\left\lVert\partial^{2}B_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}\!\int_{s}^{t}\!\left|\epsilon_{2}Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{2},r\right)-\epsilon_{1}Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{1},r\right)\right|\!dr

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left|A\right|+\left\lVert DB_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}+\frac{N^{2}}{2}C_{1}\left\lVert\partial^{2}B_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}\right)\int_{s}^{t}\left|Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{2},r\right)-Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon_{1},r\right)\right|dr+\frac{{N^{2}}}{2}C_{1}^{2}T\left\lVert\partial^{2}B_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}\left|\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}\right|,

(66)

where
\left\lVert\partial^{2}B_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\partial^{2}B_{0}\left(t,\cdot\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}
.
Therefore another application of Gronwall’s lemma shows that the mapping
\epsilon\mapsto Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon,t\right)
is Lip–continuous in
\left(0,1\right]
uniformly in
t\in\left[s,T\right]
, and by the theorem of extension of uniformly continuous functions we obtain the existence of
D_{h}X^{s,x}_{t}\left(\omega\right)
. Now by dominated convergence we are allowed to pass to the limit in (
A
), which yields

D_{h}X^{s,x}_{t}\left(\omega\right)=h+\int_{s}^{t}\left(A+DB_{0}\left(r,X_{r}^{s,x}\left(\omega\right)\right)\right)D_{h}X^{s,x}_{r}\left(\omega\right)dr,\quad t\in\left[s,T\right].

(67)

Given the arbitrarity of
h,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, this equation shows that the mapping
x\mapsto X^{s,x}_{t}\left(\omega\right)
belongs to
C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),
with
\left\lVert DX^{s,\cdot}_{t}\left(\omega\right)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq N\exp\left\{\left(\left|A\right|+\left\lVert DB_{0}\right\rVert_{T,\infty}\right)T\right\}
.

In order to analyze higher–order derivatives, we work by induction; fix
m=1,\dots,n-1
and suppose as inductive hypothesis that
X^{s,\cdot}_{t}\left(\omega\right)\in C^{m}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),\,t\in\left[s,T\right]
, with the estimate in (
19
) holding true for a sum from
i=1
to
i=m
. Moreover, assume that for every multi–index
\mathbf{h}\in\left(\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}\right)^{N}
with length
1\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{h}\right\rVert_{1}\leq m
one has, for any
t\in\left[s,T\right]
(omitting
\omega
)

D_{\mathbf{h}}X^{s,x}_{t}=\delta_{\mathbf{h}}+\int_{s}^{t}\left(\left(A+DB_{0}\left(r,X^{s,x}_{r}\right)\right)D_{\mathbf{h}}X^{s,x}_{r}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{h}}\left(r,x\right)\right)dr,\quad\delta_{\mathbf{h}}=\begin{cases}e_{j},&\text{if }\left\lVert\mathbf{h}\right\rVert_{1}=1\text{ and }h_{j}=1,\\
0,&\text{elsewhere}.\end{cases}

(68)

Here
\left(e_{j}\right)_{j=1,\dots,N}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{h}}\left(t,x\right)=\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{h},j}\left(t,x\right)\right)_{j=1,\dots,N},
with
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{h},j}\left(t,x\right)\in\mathbb{R}
denoting a sum of products where one factor is a (partial) derivative at
X_{t}^{s,x}
of
B_{0,j}\left(t,\cdot\right)
up to order
\left\lVert\mathbf{h}\right\rVert_{1}
and the others are (partial) derivatives at
x
of
X^{s,\cdot}_{t}
up to order
\left\lVert\mathbf{h}\right\rVert_{1}-1
. In particular,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{h}}\left(t,x\right)=0
when
\left\lVert\mathbf{h}\right\rVert_{1}=1
(cfr. (
67
)). At this point, consider
x,\,h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and fix a multi–index
\mathbf{h}
with length
\left\lVert\mathbf{h}\right\rVert_{1}=m
; by analogy with (
A
), for any
\epsilon\in\left(0,1\right]
and
t\in\left[s,T\right]
define the incremental ratio function

Y^{m+1}_{x,h}\left(\epsilon,t\right)=\epsilon^{-1}\left(D_{\mathbf{h}}X_{t}^{s,x+\epsilon h}-D_{\mathbf{h}}X_{t}^{s,x}\right)\\
=\int_{s}^{t}\bigg(\left(A+DB_{0}\left(r,X^{s,x}_{r}\right)\right)Y^{m+1}_{x,h}\left(\epsilon,r\right)+\frac{DB_{0}\left(r,X^{s,x+\epsilon h}_{r}\right)-DB_{0}\left(r,X^{s,x}_{r}\right)}{\epsilon}D_{\mathbf{h}}X^{s,x+\epsilon h}_{r}\\
+\epsilon^{-1}\left({\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{h}}\left(r,x+\epsilon h\right)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{h}}\left(r,x\right)}\right)\!\bigg)dr.

Note that for any
j=1,\dots,N
we can write (
t\in\left[s,T\right]
,
\epsilon\in\left(0,1\right]
)

\epsilon^{-1}\left({DB_{0}\left(t,X^{s,x+\epsilon h}_{t}\right)-DB_{0}\left(t,X^{s,x}_{t}\right)}\right)_{j,\cdot}=\left(\left(\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}B_{0,j}\left(t,X^{s,x}_{t}+\rho\epsilon Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon,t\right)\right)d\rho\right)Y^{1}_{x,h}\left(\epsilon,t\right)\right)^{\top},

and that, further, the inductive hypothesis of boundedness for the derivatives of
X^{s,\cdot}_{t}
(see (
19
)), together with the structure of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{h}}
and
B_{0}\in C^{m+1}_{b}\left(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
ensures that

\epsilon^{-1}\left|\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{h}}\left(t,x+\epsilon h\right)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{h}}\left(t,x\right)\right|\leq C_{2}\left|h\right|,\quad t\in\left[s,T\right],\,\epsilon\in\left(0,1\right],

for some constant
C_{2}=C_{2}\left(A,B_{0},T,m,N\right)>0
. These facts, the Lip–continuity of the map
\epsilon\mapsto Y_{x,h}^{1}\left(\epsilon,t\right)
in
\left(0,1\right]
uniformly in
t\in\left[s,T\right]
and computations analogous to those in (
A
) entail that there exists
D_{h}D_{\mathbf{h}}X^{s,x}_{t}\left(\omega\right)
. The arbitrarity of
x,\,h
and
\mathbf{h}
coupled with Gronwall’s lemma provides us with the desired bound (
19
) for the derivatives of order
m+1
, and finally by dominated convergence
the validity of (
68
) for a multi–index of length
m+1
is a consequence of the chain rule. In particular,
X^{s,\cdot}_{t}\left(\omega\right)\in C^{m+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. The proof is then complete, considering that the base case is provided by (
67
).

\blacksquare

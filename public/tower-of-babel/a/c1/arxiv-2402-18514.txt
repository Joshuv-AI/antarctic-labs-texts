Abstract
We present two first-order primal-dual algorithms for solving saddle point formulations of linear programs, namely FWLP (Frank-Wolfe Linear Programming) and FWLP-P. The former iteratively applies the Frank-Wolfe algorithm to both the primal and dual of the saddle point formulation of a standard-form LP. The latter is a modification of FWLP in which regularizing perturbations are used in computing the iterates. We show that FWLP-P converges to a primal-dual solution with error 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k ) {\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{k}) after k k iterations, while no convergence guarantees are provided for FWLP. We also discuss the advantages of using FWLP and FWLP-P for solving very large LPs. In particular, we argue that only part of the matrix A A is needed at each iteration, in contrast to other first-order methods.

Introduction

In recent years, data science applications have given birth to problems of very large scale. This poses a problem for mature LP solvers that require solving a system of linear equations at each iteration.
First-order methods (FoMs) for linear programming aim to solve LPs in such a way that their most expensive operation at each iteration is the product of a matrix and a vector.
Their goal is to provide an alternative to the practitioner over LP algorithms such as the simplex method or interior point methods for large-scale problems.

The Frank-Wolfe algorithm
[
7
]
, also referred to as the conditional gradient algorithm, is a FoM for minimizing a smooth convex objective function
over a compact convex set. A major benefit of the Frank-Wolfe algorithm is that each iteration requires only the solution of a linear optimization problem over
a convex constraint set, a problem which can be solved efficiently over many constraint sets used in practice. It is known that the Frank-Wolfe algorithm converges at a rate of

{\mathcal{O}}(1/k)

[
12
]
.

The focus of this paper is on finding optimal solutions to linear programs in standard form, that is, solving the following optimization problem:

(1)

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{}}{\mathrm{min}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{min}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

\displaystyle A{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle={\bm{b}},\hfil\hfil\displaystyle\displaystyle

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle\geq{\bm{0}},

where we assume throughout that (
1

\displaystyle\underset{\displaystyle}{\mathrm{max}}\quad{\bm{b}}^{T}{\bm{y}}\hfil\hfil\displaystyle\displaystyle\hfil\hfil\displaystyle\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{}}{\mathrm{max}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{max}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

FWLP, first introduced in

problems of a modified saddle-point formulation of (

\min_{{\bm{x}}\in\Delta}\max_{{\bm{y}}\in\Gamma}\mathcal{L}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}):={\bm{c}}^{T}{\bm{x}}+{\bm{y}}^{T}({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}),

(3)

FWLP-P is a modification of the original FWLP algorithm in which regularizing perturbations are introduced when computing the iterates.
FWLP-P is of theoretical importance to FWLP since we are able to prove the convergence of FWLP-P but no convergence proof is known yet for FWLP.

Such methods typically apply existing ideas and algorithms from continuous optimization to linear programming.

\displaystyle=\|(\sqrt{k+1}-\sqrt{k})(A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k+1}-{\bm{c}})+\sqrt{k}(A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k+1}-{\bm{c}}-(A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}-{\bm{c}}))\|

\displaystyle=\|(\sqrt{k+1}-\sqrt{k})(A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k+1}-{\bm{c}})+\sqrt{k}A^{T}({\bm{y}}_{k+1}-{\bm{y}}_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq|\sqrt{k+1}-\sqrt{k}|\cdot\|A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k+1}-{\bm{c}}\|+\sqrt{k}\|A\|\cdot\|{\bm{y}}_{k+1}-{\bm{y}}_{k}\|.

Here, the first line follows from (
13
),
the second (that is, (
33
))
from the fact that the Lipschitz constant of

{\mathrm{proj}}_{C}(\cdot)
is 1 for any closed nonempty convex set
C
,
the third line adds and subtracts the same term, and the
last line applies the triangle inequality and
submultiplicativity.

Now we use the facts
that
\sqrt{k+1}-\sqrt{k}\leq 1/\sqrt{k}
,
and, from (
18
),

{\bm{y}}_{k+1}-{\bm{y}}_{k}=\frac{1}{k+1}({\bm{s}}_{k+1}-{\bm{y}}_{k}),

and finally, the bounds
\|{\bm{y}}_{k}\|\leq\sqrt{m}\eta
,

\|{\bm{s}}_{k}\|\leq\sqrt{m}\eta

to conclude that

\|{\bm{y}}_{k+1}-{\bm{y}}_{k}\|\leq 2\sqrt{m}\eta/(k+1)
and thus

\|{\bm{r}}_{k+2}-{\bm{r}}_{k+1}\|\leq(1/\sqrt{k})\cdot(\|A\|\sqrt{m}\eta+\|{\bm{c}}\|)+(2/\sqrt{k})\|A\|\sqrt{m}\eta.

This takes care of the first factor in
\delta_{k+1}^{\prime\prime}
. The middle
factor is bound by
\|A\|
, and the third factor
\|{\bm{y}}_{k+1}-{\bm{y}}_{k}\|

is bounded by
(see the previous paragraph)
2\sqrt{m}\eta/(k+1)
. Thus, overall,
we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{k+1}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\geq-\|{\bm{r}}_{k+2}-{\bm{r}}_{k+1}\|\cdot\|A\|\cdot\|{\bm{y}}_{k}-{\bm{y}}_{k+1}\|

\displaystyle\geq-C/k^{3/2},

where
C
depends on the problem data:

C\leq 2\|A\|\sqrt{m}\eta(3\|A\|\sqrt{m}\eta+\|{\bm{c}}\|).

For
\delta_{k+1}^{\prime\prime\prime}
, by finding a common denominator and then multiplying the
resulting fraction by
\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{k+1}
, we obtain

\delta_{k+1}^{\prime\prime\prime}\geq-1/(4k^{3/2})\cdot\|{\bm{r}}_{k+2}\|^{2}\geq-1/(4k^{3/2})\cdot\xi^{2}.

Finally, one sees that
\delta_{k+1}^{\mathrm{iv}}\geq 0
. Putting all
of these terms together yields:

\delta_{k+1}\geq-D/k^{3/2},

(34)

where

D:=2\|A\|\sqrt{m}\eta(3\|A\|\sqrt{m}\eta+\|{\bm{c}}\|)+\frac{\xi^{2}}{4}.

(35)

∎

Combining (
30
), Lemma
1
, and Lemma
2
, we now have for all
k\geq 2

U_{k+1}\leq\frac{k}{k+1}U_{k}+\frac{D}{k^{3/2}}+\frac{m\eta^{2}}{6k^{2}\sqrt{k-1}}.

In fact, since
\sqrt{k}\geq 1/\sqrt{k-1}
for all
k\geq 2
, we can enlarge
D
to obtain the bound

U_{k+1}\leq\frac{k}{k+1}U_{k}+\frac{\bar{D}}{k^{3/2}},

(36)

where

\bar{D}:=D+\frac{m\eta^{2}}{6}.

With this bound, we prove the following bound on
U_{k}
.

Theorem 1
.

Recall
U_{k}
defined in (
31
). We have the bound

U_{k+1}\leq\frac{F}{\sqrt{k}},

for all
k\geq 2
, where
F:=\max\{\sqrt{2}U_{2},6\bar{D}\}
.

Proof.

The
k=2
case follows immediately from the definition of
F
.
Suppose inductively
that
U_{k}\leq F/\sqrt{k}
. Then we check:

\displaystyle U_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k}{k+1}U_{k}+\bar{D}/k^{3/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{kF}{(k+1)\sqrt{k}}+\bar{D}/k^{3/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{2}F+\bar{D}(k+1)}{(k+1)k^{3/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}\cdot\frac{k^{2}F+\bar{D}(k+1)}{\sqrt{k+1}\cdot k^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}\cdot\frac{k^{2}F+\bar{D}(k+1)}{(\sqrt{k}+1/(3\sqrt{k}))k^{3/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}\cdot\frac{k^{2}F+\bar{D}(k+1)}{k^{2}+k/3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}\cdot\frac{k^{2}F+kF/3}{k^{2}+k/3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}\cdot F.

Here, we used (
36
) for the
first line, the induction hypothesis for the
second line, the inequality

\sqrt{k+1}\geq\sqrt{k}+1/(3\sqrt{k})
for
k\geq 1
on the 5th line (easy
to confirm by squaring)
and the assumption
\bar{D}\leq F/6
on the 7th line, which implies for all
k\geq 1

that
\bar{D}(k+1)\leq Fk/3
.

Thus,
U_{k}\leq F/\sqrt{k}
.
∎

The perturbation terms from (
15
) and (
17
) in FWLP-P
were essential to this analysis. Without them, as in FWLP, there is no useful bound
on how much the primal step
{\bm{r}}_{k+2}
can differ from
{\bm{r}}_{k+1}
when the index of the most-violated
constraint changes from one iteration to the next. With the perturbations, we are able to obtain the inequality
(
33
).

3.3
Proving convergence of the potential function implies convergence of the iterates

We have shown that the potential function
U_{k}
decreases at a rate of
{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{k})
as
k
increases.
It remains to show that the potential function bounds the distance of the iterates from an optimizer.
We first need the following lemmas. Both lemmas have the same flavor: if an infeasible point for an LP improves on the optimal objective value, then the amount of objective improvement is bounded in terms of the amount of infeasibility.

Lemma 3
.

Let
p^{*}
denote the optimal value of (
1
).
Assume
p^{*}
is finite, i.e., (
1
) is feasible and bounded.
Let
({\bm{x}}^{*},{\bm{y}}^{*})
be an arbitrary optimizing primal-dual pair of (
1
) and (
2
).
Then for an arbitrary
\hat{\bm{x}}\geq{\bm{0}}
,

{\bm{c}}^{T}\hat{\bm{x}}\geq p^{*}-\|{\bm{y}}^{*}\|_{\infty}\|{\bm{b}}-A\hat{\bm{x}}\|_{1}.

(37)

Proof.

Select an arbitrary optimizing pair
({\bm{x}}^{*},{\bm{y}}^{*})
for (
1
).
Let
{\bm{k}}:={\bm{b}}-A\hat{\bm{x}}
. Consider the LP,

\begin{array}[]{rl}\min_{{\bm{x}}}&{\bm{c}}^{T}{\bm{x}}\\
\mbox{s.t.}&A{\bm{x}}={\bm{b}}-{\bm{k}},\\
&{\bm{x}}\geq{\bm{0}}.\end{array}

(38)

This LP is clearly feasible since
\hat{\bm{x}}
satisfies the constraints.
It is also
bounded, as argued by contradiction:
If (
38
) were feasible and unbounded, then there would exist
a certificate of unboundedness, which is a vector

{\bm{w}}
such that
{\bm{w}}\geq{\bm{0}}
,
A{\bm{w}}={\bm{0}}
, and
{\bm{c}}^{T}{\bm{w}}<0
. However,
such a
{\bm{w}}
would also certify unboundedness of
(
1
), which we have already assumed to be bounded.

Therefore, the dual of (
38
), which is,

\begin{array}[]{rl}\max_{{\bm{y}}}&({\bm{b}}-{\bm{k}})^{T}{\bm{y}}\\
\mbox{s.t.}&A^{T}{\bm{y}}\leq{\bm{c}},\end{array}

(39)

has an optimal solution, say
\hat{\bm{y}}
. Since
{\bm{y}}^{*}
is also feasible
for (
39
), we have the following chain of inequalities

\displaystyle p^{*}

\displaystyle={\bm{b}}^{T}{\bm{y}}^{*}

(by strong duality of (
1
))

\displaystyle=({\bm{b}}-{\bm{k}})^{T}{\bm{y}}^{*}+{\bm{k}}^{T}{\bm{y}}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq({\bm{b}}-{\bm{k}})^{T}{\bm{y}}^{*}+\|{\bm{k}}\|_{1}\cdot\|{\bm{y}}^{*}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq({\bm{b}}-{\bm{k}})^{T}\hat{\bm{y}}+\|{\bm{k}}\|_{1}\cdot\|{\bm{y}}^{*}\|_{\infty}

(since
\hat{\bm{y}}
maximizes (
39
))

\displaystyle\leq{\bm{c}}^{T}\hat{\bm{x}}+\|{\bm{k}}\|_{1}\cdot\|{\bm{y}}^{*}\|_{\infty}.

(by weak duality between (
38
) and (
39
))

Recalling
{\bm{k}}={\bm{b}}-A\hat{\bm{x}}
, the final line in this chain
establishes (
37
).
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
d^{*}
denote the optimal value of (
2
).
Assume
d^{*}
is finite, i.e., (
2
) is feasible and bounded.
Let
({\bm{x}}^{*},{\bm{y}}^{*})
be an arbitrary optimizing primal-dual pair of (
1
) and (
2
).
Then for an arbitrary
\hat{\bm{y}}
,

{\bm{b}}^{T}\hat{\bm{y}}\leq d^{*}+\|{\bm{x}}^{*}\|_{1}\cdot l,

(40)

where
l
measures the infeasibility of
\hat{\bm{y}}
, that is,

l:=\max(0,\max_{j}\{{\bm{e}}_{j}^{T}(A^{T}\hat{\bm{y}}-{\bm{c}})\}).

(41)

Proof.

This proof is analogous to the previous proof. Let

({\bm{x}}^{*},{\bm{y}}^{*})
be an arbitrary primal-dual optimizer
of (
2
). Consider the dual-form LP given by

\begin{array}[]{rl}\max_{{\bm{y}}}&{\bm{b}}^{T}{\bm{y}}\\
\mbox{s.t.}&A^{T}{\bm{y}}\leq{\bm{c}}+l{\bm{e}},\end{array}

(42)

where
l
is from (
41
) and
{\bm{e}}
denotes
the vector of all 1’s. This LP is clearly feasible since

\hat{\bm{y}}
satisfies the constraint. Furthermore, it is
bounded as argued by contradiction. If
(
42
) were feasible and unbounded, there would
exist a certificate of unboundedness, that is, a vector

{\bm{z}}
such that
A^{T}{\bm{z}}\leq{\bm{0}}
and
{\bm{b}}^{T}{\bm{z}}>0
. However, this
certificate would also certify the unboundedness of
(
2
), which is assumed to be bounded.

Therefore, the dual of (
42
), which is

\begin{array}[]{rl}\min_{{\bm{x}}}&({\bm{c}}+l{\bm{e}})^{T}{\bm{x}}\\
\mbox{s.t.}&A{\bm{x}}={\bm{b}},\\
&{\bm{x}}\geq{\bm{0}},\end{array}

(43)

has an optimal solution which we denote
\hat{\bm{x}}
.
Since
{\bm{x}}^{*}
is also feasible for (
43
),
we have the following chain of inequalities:

\displaystyle d^{*}

\displaystyle={\bm{c}}^{T}{\bm{x}}^{*}

(by strong duality of (
2
))

\displaystyle=({\bm{c}}+l{\bm{e}})^{T}{\bm{x}}^{*}-l{\bm{e}}^{T}{\bm{x}}^{*}

\displaystyle\geq({\bm{c}}+l{\bm{e}})^{T}{\bm{x}}^{*}-l\|{\bm{x}}^{*}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq({\bm{c}}+l{\bm{e}})^{T}\hat{\bm{x}}-l\|{\bm{x}}^{*}\|_{1}

(by the optimality of
\hat{\bm{x}}
in (
43
))

\displaystyle\geq{\bm{b}}^{T}\hat{\bm{y}}-l\|{\bm{x}}^{*}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\mbox{(by weak duality betwen \eqref{eq:dldu} and \eqref{eq:pldu})}.

The final line after rearrangement is (
40
).
∎

We are now ready to prove the penultimate theorem that shows convergence of FWLP-P. We show that, assuming a primal-dual optimal LP solution exists and
\xi,\eta
have been chosen correctly, the
U_{k}
plus terms that tend to 0 bound the distance from optimality. Note that an analog of Theorem
2
can be derived for FWLP using a similar proof. This analog is not presented here since we are not able to show that
U_{k}\to 0
for FWLP.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose

k\geq 2
. Then,

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{k}\geq{\bm{0}},

(44)

\displaystyle\|{\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k}\|_{1}\leq\frac{2U_{k}}{\eta}+\frac{\xi^{2}}{\eta\sqrt{k}}+\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{k-1}},

(45)

\displaystyle\max(0,\max_{j}\{{\bm{e}}_{j}^{T}(A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}-{\bm{c}})\})\leq\frac{2U_{k}}{\xi}+\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{k}}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi\sqrt{k-1}},

(46)

\displaystyle{\bm{c}}^{T}{\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{b}}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}\leq U_{k},

(47)

provided

\xi\geq 2\|{\bm{x}}^{*}\|_{1},\qquad\eta\geq 2\|{\bm{y}}^{*}\|_{\infty}.

(48)

Proof.

It is immediate from the algorithm that (
44
) holds.
Next, notice that
{\bm{s}}_{k}
is a solution to

\max_{{\bm{s}}\in\Gamma}\left\{{\bm{s}}^{T}({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k})-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k-1}}\|{\bm{s}}\|^{2}\right\},

so for arbitrary
{\bm{s}}\in\Gamma
,

\begin{split}{\bm{s}}_{k}^{T}({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k})-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\|{\bm{s}}_{k}\|^{2}&\geq{\bm{s}}_{k}^{T}({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k})-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k-1}}\|{\bm{s}}_{k}\|^{2},\\
&\geq{\bm{s}}^{T}({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k})-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k-1}}\|{\bm{s}}\|^{2}.\end{split}

(49)

Setting
{\bm{s}}={\bm{0}}
gives

{\bm{s}}_{k}^{T}({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k})-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\|{\bm{s}}_{k}\|^{2}\geq 0.

Similarly,
{\bm{r}}_{k+1}
is a solution to

\min_{{\bm{r}}}\left\{{\bm{r}}^{T}({\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k})+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\|{\bm{r}}\|^{2}\right\},

(50)

so for arbitrary
{\bm{r}}\in\Delta
,

{\bm{r}}_{k+1}^{T}({\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k})+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\|{\bm{r}}_{k+1}\|^{2}\leq{\bm{r}}^{T}({\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k})+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\|{\bm{r}}\|^{2}.

(51)

Setting
{\bm{r}}={\bm{0}}
gives

-{\bm{r}}_{k+1}^{T}({\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k})-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\|{\bm{r}}_{k+1}\|^{2}\geq 0.

(52)

The above two results imply with (
31
) that

U_{k}\geq{\bm{c}}^{T}{\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{b}}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}
, thus establishing (
47
).

Setting
{\bm{s}}:=\eta\cdot{\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}}({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k})\in\Gamma
in (
49
) gives the bound

{\bm{s}}_{k}^{T}({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k})-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\lVert{\bm{s}}_{k}\rVert^{2}\geq\eta\lVert{\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k}\rVert_{1}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k-1}}m\eta^{2}.

(53)

Similarly, set
{\bm{r}}:=\xi{\bm{e}}_{j}
where
j={\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}}_{j}\{{\bm{e}}_{j}^{T}(A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}-{\bm{c}})\}
. Noting
{\bm{r}}\in\Delta
, we can use (
51
)
to obtain the bound

-{\bm{r}}_{k+1}^{T}({\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k})-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\|{\bm{r}}_{k+1}\|^{2}\geq\xi\cdot\max_{j}\{{\bm{e}}_{j}^{T}(A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}-{\bm{c}})\}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\xi^{2}.

In fact, from (
52
) the left-hand side above is nonnegative. It follows that we can strengthen the above bound to

-{\bm{r}}_{k+1}^{T}({\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k})-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\|{\bm{r}}_{k+1}\|^{2}\geq\xi\cdot l-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\xi^{2},

(54)

where
l=\max(0,\max_{j}\{{\bm{e}}_{j}^{T}(A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}-{\bm{c}})\})
.
Using Lemma
3
, Lemma
4
, (
31
), (
53
), and (
54
), we may write

\displaystyle U_{k}

\displaystyle=-{\bm{r}}_{k+1}^{T}({\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k})-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\|{\bm{r}}_{k+1}\|^{2}+{\bm{s}}_{k}^{T}({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k})-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\|{\bm{s}}_{k}\|^{2}+{\bm{c}}^{T}{\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{b}}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k},

\displaystyle\geq\xi\cdot l-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\xi^{2}+\eta\lVert{\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k}\rVert_{1}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k-1}}m\eta^{2}+{\bm{c}}^{T}{\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{b}}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k},

\displaystyle\geq\xi\cdot l-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\xi^{2}+\eta\lVert{\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k}\rVert_{1}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k-1}}m\eta^{2}+p^{*}-\lVert{\bm{y}}^{*}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert{\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k}\rVert_{1}-d^{*}-\lVert{\bm{x}}^{*}\rVert_{1}\cdot l,

\displaystyle\geq\xi\cdot l-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\xi^{2}+\eta\lVert{\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k}\rVert_{1}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k-1}}m\eta^{2}+p^{*}-d^{*}-\frac{\eta}{2}\lVert{\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k}\rVert_{1}-\frac{\xi}{2}\cdot l

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi}{2}\cdot l-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}}\xi^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2}\lVert{\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k}\rVert_{1}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k-1}}m\eta^{2},

where the fourth line used the assumption (
48
) and the final line used the fact that
p^{*}=d^{*}
. We are left with the bound

\frac{\xi}{2}\cdot l+\frac{\eta}{2}\lVert{\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k}\rVert_{1}\leq U_{k}+\frac{\xi^{2}}{2\sqrt{k}}+\frac{m\eta^{2}}{2\sqrt{k-1}},

where both terms on the left are nonnegative. It follows that each term must be individually bounded by the right-hand side above.
This establishes (
45
) and (
46
).
∎

Theorem 3
.

The iterates of FWLP-P converge to an
\epsilon
-optimal solution of (
1
) and its dual (
2
) after
{\mathcal{O}}(1/\epsilon^{2})
iterations.

Proof.

This follows immediately by applying the bound from Theorem
1
in Theorem
2
.

4
Relating
\bm{U_{k}}
to the standard primal-dual gap

Consider the general saddle-point problem

\min_{{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}\max_{{\bm{y}}\in\mathcal{Y}}f({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}).

A standard measure of the optimality gap of an iteration
({\bm{x}}_{k},{\bm{y}}_{k})
used in the analysis of many primal-dual algorithms for saddle-point problems (for example,
[
6
,
9
,
15
]
) is the primal-dual gap:

\max\left\{f({\bm{x}}_{k},\hat{{\bm{y}}}_{k})-f(\hat{{\bm{x}}}_{k},{\bm{y}}_{k}):\hat{\bm{x}}_{k}\in\mathcal{X},\hat{\bm{y}}_{k}\in\mathcal{Y}\right\}.

(55)

Obviously, the solution to (
55
) is

\displaystyle\hat{{\bm{x}}}_{k}={\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}}\{f({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}_{k}):{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{X}\},

\displaystyle\hat{{\bm{y}}}_{k}={\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}}\{f({\bm{x}}_{k},{\bm{y}}):{\bm{y}}\in\mathcal{Y}\}.

It is noted in
[
2
]
that for the saddle-point problem associated with LP

\min_{{\bm{x}}\geq{\bm{0}}}\max_{{\bm{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}{\bm{c}}^{T}{\bm{x}}+{\bm{y}}^{T}({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}),

the primal-dual gap
can be infinite, since the feasible set
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is unbounded. This is not an issue for the modified saddle-point formulation (
3
)
used in our analysis, since the feasible set
\Delta\times\Gamma
is bounded by virtue of the redundant constraints.

Recall the definition of
\mathcal{L}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}})
from (
3
). Let

\mathcal{M}_{k}:=\max\{\mathcal{L}({\bm{x}}_{k},{\bm{s}})-\mathcal{L}({\bm{r}},{\bm{y}}_{k}):({\bm{r}},{\bm{s}})\in\Delta\times\Gamma\},

which is the specialization of (
55
)
to our setting. We argue that
\mathcal{M}_{k}
is a perturbation of
U_{k}
via the following bound:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{k}-U_{k}|

\displaystyle=\left|\max_{{\bm{s}}\in\Gamma}[({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k})^{T}{\bm{s}}+{\bm{c}}^{T}{\bm{x}}_{k}]-\min_{{\bm{r}}\in\Delta}[({\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k})^{T}{\bm{r}}+{\bm{b}}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}]-U_{k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\max_{{\bm{s}}\in\Gamma}\left[({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k})^{T}{\bm{s}}+{\bm{c}}^{T}{\bm{x}}_{k}-\frac{\|{\bm{s}}\|^{2}}{2\sqrt{k-1}}\right]-\min_{{\bm{r}}\in\Delta}\left[({\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k})^{T}{\bm{r}}+{\bm{b}}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}+\frac{\|{\bm{r}}\|^{2}}{2\sqrt{k}}\right]-U_{k}\right|

\displaystyle\hphantom{\leq}\quad\mbox{}+\max_{({\bm{r}},{\bm{s}})\in\Delta\times\Gamma}\left|\frac{\|{\bm{s}}\|^{2}}{2\sqrt{k-1}}+\frac{\|{\bm{r}}\|^{2}}{2\sqrt{k}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left[({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{k})^{T}{\bm{s}}_{k}+{\bm{c}}^{T}{\bm{x}}_{k}-\frac{\|{\bm{s}}_{k}\|^{2}}{2\sqrt{k-1}}\right]-\left[({\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k})^{T}{\bm{r}}_{k+1}+{\bm{b}}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}+\frac{\|{\bm{r}}_{k+1}\|^{2}}{2\sqrt{k}}\right]-U_{k}\right|

\displaystyle\hphantom{\leq}\quad\mbox{}+\frac{m\eta^{2}}{2\sqrt{k-1}}+\frac{\xi^{2}}{2\sqrt{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m\eta^{2}+\xi^{2}}{2\sqrt{k-1}}+{\mathcal{O}}(k^{-3/2}).

Here, the second line adds and subtracts the same terms and then applies the triangle inequality for
|\cdot|
. The third line uses the definitions of
{\bm{s}}_{k}
from (
17
) and
{\bm{r}}_{k}
from (
15
). The fourth line uses (
31
), noting that the terms all cancel out except for the difference between a denominator of
2\sqrt{k-1}
versus a denominator of
2\sqrt{k}
. This small remainder is written as
{\mathcal{O}}(k^{-3/2})
on the fourth line.

5
Efficient implementation of FWLP and FWLP-P

A major advantage of FWLP and FWLP-P is their low computational cost per iteration. Naïve implementations of Algorithms
2.1
and
2.2

have iteration cost bounded by the cost of a full matrix-vector product. We discuss below how this can be significantly improved with an efficient implementation.

5.1
FWLP

Consider, e.g., the iteration
k=1
. Suppose we store
A{\bm{x}}_{1}
. In Algorithm
2.1
we can perform an extra step to update

A{\bm{x}}_{2}
by either computing

A{\bm{x}}_{2}=\frac{k}{2}A{\bm{x}}_{1},

or

A{\bm{x}}_{2}=\frac{k}{2}A{\bm{x}}_{1}+\frac{\xi}{2}A{\bm{e}}_{i},

where
i
is the index of the most violated dual constraint, computed as
{\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}}\{c_{i}-{\bm{e}}_{i}^{T}A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{1}\}
. Ignoring the cost of computing
i
,
such an update runs in
{\mathcal{O}}(m+n)
operations per iteration (cost of updating
{\bm{x}}_{k}
and
A{\bm{x}}_{k}
). Note that
{\mathcal{O}}(m+n)
can be reduced to
{\mathcal{O}}(m)
if we keep track of the product of scaling factors
k/(k+1)
of
{\bm{x}}_{k+1}
in a separate variable.
In contrast, the naïve implementation of the primal update in FWLP, where one instead performs matrix-vector products,
runs in
{\mathcal{O}}(mn)
operations per iteration.

The dual update for, e.g.,
k=1
, is given by

{\bm{y}}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}{\bm{y}}_{1}+\frac{\eta}{2}{\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}}({\bm{b}}-A{\bm{x}}_{2}),

which also runs in
{\mathcal{O}}(m)
since
A{\bm{x}}_{2}
has already been computed in the primal step. The naïve implementation again takes

operations.

To improve the cost of computing the index
i
, first note that in Algorithm
2.1
we only need to compute
{\bm{e}}_{i}^{T}A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}
for indices

such that there is a possibility that
i={\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}}_{j}\{c_{j}-{\bm{e}}_{j}^{T}A{\bm{y}}_{k}\}
. This could be implemented by a data structure to store the
indices
[n]
in some order so that only the possibly most infeasible indices need to be considered at each iteration. In more detail, suppose
j_{k}
indexes the most violated dual constraint on iteration
k
, i.e.,
j_{k}={\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}}_{j}[{\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}]_{j}
. Suppose
j\in[n]
is some other index. Based on the value of
[{\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}]_{j}-[{\bm{c}}-A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}]_{j_{k}}
and prior knowledge of the stepsize (which tends to
0
with
k
), one knows in advance that constraint
j
could not be the most violated constraint prior to iteration
k^{\prime}
, where
k^{\prime}
is a computable index satisfying
k^{\prime}>k
. Then constraint
j
does not even have to be considered by the algorithm on all iterations between
k
and
k^{\prime}
.

Thus, the algorithm maintains some subset of constraints
\mathcal{S}_{k}
on iteration
k
that need to be examined for possibly being the most violated.
The computation of
A^{T}{\bm{y}}_{k}
in the primal update thus runs in
{\mathcal{O}}(|\mathcal{S}_{k}|\cdot m)
plus the cost of updating the data
structure to obtain
\mathcal{S}_{k+1}
. Naturally, the estimate
{\mathcal{O}}(|\mathcal{S}_{k}|\cdot m)
is further reduced if
A
is sparse.

The total cost of iteration
k
for this implementation of FWLP is thus

{\mathcal{O}}(n+\lvert\mathcal{S}_{k}\rvert\cdot m),

plus the cost of updating the proposed data structure and precomputation. For large problems, one would expect that
\lvert\mathcal{S}_{k}\rvert\ll n
, allowing significant speedup to be achieved
by using the proposed efficient implementation of FWLP. And, as mentioned earlier, the “
n
” term may be dropped with a careful implementation for updating
{\bm{x}}_{k}
, and the “
\lvert\mathcal{S}_{k}\rvert\cdot m
” term is reduced in the presence of sparsity.

6
Conclusion

We proposed two primal-dual first-order algorithms, namely FWLP and FWLP-P, for solving linear programming problems
and discussed how both algorithms can be implemented in such a way that significantly improves their efficiency, especially for
large-scale problems.
Our convergence analysis of FWLP-P shows that the algorithm converges to a primal-dual solution with error
{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{k})
after

k
iterations. Despite this, no convergence proof is known at this time for the simpler and faster algorithm FWLP, and analysis of this algorithm is a topic for
future research.

Another interesting question is how FWLP and FWLP-P can cope with primal or dual infeasibility. It should be noted that our proof that
U_{k}\rightarrow 0
(Theorem
1
) did not depend on feasibility nor on the correct choice of
\xi
and
\eta
, so monitoring
U_{k}
cannot diagnose these conditions. We remark that other first-order algorithms have a theory for infeasibility detection; see, e.g.,
[
3
]
and
[
13
]
. A related question is how the algorithm can detect whether the two parameters
\xi
and
\eta
of FWLP and FWLP-P have been chosen correctly.

We showed that FWLP-P converges in the sense that the primal and dual infeasibility measures tend to 0, as does the duality gap. However, we did not prove convergence of the iterates. In the case that the LP has multiple optimizers, an open question is whether the algorithms converge to a particular optimizer.

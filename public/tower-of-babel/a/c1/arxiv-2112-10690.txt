Adversarially Robust Stability Certificates can be Sample-Efficient
Thomas T.C.K. Zhang, Stephen Tu, Nicholas M. Boffi, Jean-Jacques E. Slotine, Nikolai Matni

Abstract
Motivated by bridging the simulation to reality gap in the context of safety-critical systems, we consider learning adversarially robust stability certificates for unknown nonlinear dynamical systems. In line with approaches from robust control, we consider additive and Lipschitz bounded adversaries that perturb the system dynamics. We show that under suitable assumptions of incremental stability on the underlying system, the statistical cost of learning an adversarial stability certificate is equivalent, up to constant factors, to that of learning a nominal stability certificate. Our results hinge on novel bounds for the Rademacher complexity of the resulting adversarial loss class, which may be of independent interest. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first characterization of sample-complexity bounds when performing adversarial learning over data generated by a dynamical system. We further provide a practical algorithm for approximating the adversarial training algorithm, and validate our findings on a damped pendulum example.



1
Introduction

A challenge to the deployment of modern robotic systems to real-world settings is the overall lack of formal safety guarantees. While controller design for complex robotic systems has received much attention, comparatively less effort has been devoted to verifying the safety of the resulting closed-loop system. Without broadly applicable tools for certifying
a-priori
guarantees, it is difficult to justify deploying these methods in applications where safety is paramount, regardless of the impressive performance that they achieve in simulation or controlled laboratory settings.

An important component of ensuring real-world safety is verifying the stability of a closed-loop system from trajectory data.
While recent work
[
4
]
proposes and analyzes a learning-based approach to this problem,
a fundamental limitation of the prior art
is that
learning a stability certificate with failure probability of less than e.g.,
1\%
for high-dimensional systems requires on the order of tens of thousands of trajectories. Realistically, such a large amount of trajectory data can only be collected using a simulation environment.
Therefore, in order for a learned certificate to be meaningful for real-world hardware, it is essential for it to be
robust
to modeling errors between simulation and reality, i.e., robust to the so-called sim-to-real gap.

While bridging the sim-to-real gap has traditionally been addressed via domain randomization
[
39
]
, we take inspiration from the robust control literature, and tackle this challenge by developing an approach for
adversarial learning
of stability certificates for dynamical systems.
We show that under suitable conditions on the underlying system, requiring that a learned certificate is robust to adversarial perturbations that
enter the dynamics
carries little additional statistical overhead.
Taking inspiration from
Boffi et al. 2020a
, we prove our results by converting the
robust
stability certification problem into an adversarial learning problem, and subsequently bounding the Rademacher complexity of the resulting adversarial loss class.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first characterization of sample-complexity bounds when performing adversarial learning over data generated by a dynamical system.
Our results build upon and extend a line of work which shows that underlying system-theoretic properties translate into the difficulty (or ease) of learning over data generated by dynamical systems (see e.g.,
Tsiamis et al. 2020
,
Tsiamis and Pappas 2021
,
Lee et al. 2021
,
Tu et al. 2021
and references therein).
We further provide a practical algorithm for approximating the adversarial training algorithm, and show that adversarially trained certificates are robust to various types of model mis-specification on a damped pendulum example.
Our results are presented in continuous time; however, they readily admit discrete time analogues, which are detailed in Appendix
B
.

1.1
Related Work

Our work draws upon and unifies tools from three areas: (i) learning safety certificates from data, (ii) adversarial robustness, and (iii) statistical learning theory.

Learning safety certificates

A wide body of work addresses learning Lyapunov
[

,

,
7
,

,

,

Adversarial robustness

Traditional approaches

,

,

,
16
]

In particular, our adversarial model perturbs the dynamical system which generates the data, a perspective that is more in line with traditional robust control methods. We further show that under suitable stability assumptions on the underlying dynamical system, there is no additional statistical cost to adversarial training, in contrast to results showing that in the traditional setting, adversarial learning algorithms require more data than their nominal counterparts
[
30

.

Most directly relevant to our work are adversarial deep reinforcement learning methods which learn policies that are robust to various classes of disturbances, such as adversarial observations

21

Nevertheless, there remains a paucity of theoretical guarantees on the generalization error, and thus sample-efficiency, of such learned policies.

Statistical learning theory

While such statistical guarantees, to the best of our knowledge, do not exist for adversarial reinforcement learning, the generalization error of an adversarially trained classifier has been studied using uniform convergence

Problem Framework

2.1
Nominal Stability Certificates

We begin by reviewing the problem setting and results from
Boffi et al. 2020a
. We assume that the underlying dynamical system is a continuous-time, autonomous system of the form
\dot{x}=f(x)
, where
f
is continuous and unknown, and that the state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is fully observed. Let
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
be a compact set and
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
be the maximum interval such that a unique solution
\varphi_{t}(\xi)
exists for all times
t\in\mathcal{T}
and initial conditions
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
, where
\varphi_{t}(\xi)
is the map to the state at time
t
given initial condition
\xi
. We assume that we have access to
n
trajectories initialized from randomly sampled initial conditions. That is, we are given
\mathopen{}\left\{\varphi_{t}(\xi_{i})\right\}\mathclose{}_{i\in[n],\;t\in\mathcal{T}}
, where
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}
is a distribution over
\mathcal{X}
. For simplicity, we assume that we can precisely differentiate
\varphi_{t}(\xi)
with respect to time (in practice, we can estimate
\dot{\varphi}_{t}(\xi)
numerically).

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a class of
continuously differentiable candidate Lyapunov functions
V:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
satisfying
V(0)=0
. Fixing a constant
\eta>0
, we define a scalar violation function
h:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{V}
as:

\displaystyle h(\xi,V):=\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\left\langle\nabla V\mathopen{}\left(\varphi_{t}(\xi)\right)\mathclose{},f\mathopen{}\left(\varphi_{t}(\xi)\right)\mathclose{}\right\rangle+\eta V\mathopen{}\left(\varphi_{t}(\xi)\right)\mathclose{}.

(1)

The violation function
h(\xi,V)
scans the Lyapunov decrease condition for exponential stability with rate
\eta
over the trajectory initialized at
\xi
, and returns the maximal value. Observe that if
h(\xi,V)\leqslant 0
, then
V
certifies exponential stability along the trajectory
\varphi_{t}(\xi)
,
t\in\mathcal{T}
. The nominal stability certification problem is therefore equivalent to the following feasibility problem:

\displaystyle\text{Find}_{V\in\mathcal{V}}\text{ s.t. }h(\xi,V)\leqslant 0\quad\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X}.

(2)

In general, various choices of
\mathcal{V}
and
h(\xi,V)
can encode different notions of stability and accompanying certificates (see
[
4
]
for more details). To search for a
V
that satisfies the above optimization problem given finite data, we solve the following feasibility problem:

\displaystyle\text{Find}_{V\in\mathcal{V}}\text{ s.t. }h(\xi_{i},V)\leqslant-\tau,\quad i=1,\dots,n,

(3)

where
\tau>0
is a margin that ensures generalization of the learned stability certificate
V
on unseen trajectories. Let
\hat{V}_{n}
denote a solution to (
3
) and define the nominal generalization error of
\hat{V}_{n}
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}(\hat{V}_{n}):=\mathbb{P}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{D}}\mathopen{}\left[h\mathopen{}\left(\xi,\hat{V}_{n}\right)\mathclose{}>0\right]\mathclose{}.

(4)

The nominal error (
4
) characterizes the probability that
\hat{V}_{n}
fails to certify stability along a new trajectory with initial condition sampled from
\mathcal{D}
. In
[
4
]
, it is shown that for general classes of
\mathcal{V}
,
\mathrm{err}(\hat{V}_{n})
decays at a rate
\tilde{O}(k/n)
, where
k
captures the effective degrees of freedom of the stability function class
\mathcal{V}
and
\tilde{O}
suppresses polylog dependence on
n
and fixed problem parameters.

Adversarially Robust Stability Certificates

We now consider the stability certification problem under the presence of adversarial perturbations. Consider the following two tubes of perturbed trajectories

1

[0,T]

assumptions. As an example, the set (
5

Similarly, the set (

\displaystyle\Delta^{u}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\left\{\tilde{\varphi}:\dot{\tilde{\varphi}}_{t}=f(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})+\delta_{t},\;\tilde{\varphi}_{0}=\xi,\;\left\|\delta_{t}\right\|_{2}\leqslant\varepsilon,\,t\mapsto\delta_{t}\text{ is locally integrable}\right\}\mathclose{},

\displaystyle\Delta^{x}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\left\{\tilde{\varphi}:\dot{\tilde{\varphi}}_{t}=f(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})+\delta(\tilde{\varphi}_{t}),\;\tilde{\varphi}_{0}=\xi,\;\left\|\delta(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})\right\|_{2}\leqslant\varepsilon\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}\right\|_{2}\right\}\mathclose{}.

Intuitively,
\Delta^{u}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)
is the tube of perturbed trajectories initialized at
\xi
for which an additive adversary has an instantaneous norm budget of
\varepsilon
to perturb the dynamics. Analogously,
\Delta^{x}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)

\displaystyle\Delta^{x,u}_{\varepsilon_{x},\varepsilon_{u}}(\xi):=\mathopen{}\left\{\tilde{\varphi}:\tilde{\varphi}_{t}=f(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})+\delta^{x}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})+\delta^{u}_{t},\;\tilde{\varphi}_{0}=\xi,\;\left\|\delta^{x}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})\right\|_{2}\leqslant\varepsilon_{x}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}\right\|_{2},\;\left\|\delta^{u}_{t}\right\|_{2}\leqslant\varepsilon_{u}\right\}\mathclose{}.

(7)

Here, the
\delta^{u}_{t}
are additionally assumed to be locally integrable with respect to
t
.
The tube (

) of perturbed trajectories defines a natural way of capturing the sim-to-real gap through the effects of both unmodeled dynamics (
\delta^{x}

\delta^{u}

In order to accommodate additive disturbances in our stability analysis, we modify the violation function (
1
) to certify
practical stability

[
18
]
, i.e., convergence to a ball about the origin. To that end, for
\nu\geqslant 0
, define the adversarial violation function:

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{\nu}(\xi,V):=\sup_{\tilde{\varphi}\in\Delta_{\varepsilon}}\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\left\langle\nabla V(\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(\xi)),\dot{\tilde{\varphi}}_{t}(\xi)\right\rangle+\eta V(\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(\xi))-\nu.

(8)

With this definition, finding an adversarially robust certificate of practical stability from data can be posed as solving the following feasibility problem analogous to (
3
):

\displaystyle\text{Find}_{V\in\mathcal{V}}\text{ s.t. }\tilde{h}_{\nu}(\xi_{i},V)\leqslant-\tau,\quad i=1,\dots,n.

(9)

Letting
\tilde{V}_{n}
be the solution to (
9
), we consider the analogous generalization error to (
4
):

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}(\tilde{V}_{n}):=\mathbb{P}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{D}}\mathopen{}\left[\tilde{h}_{\nu}\mathopen{}\left(\xi,\tilde{V}_{n}\right)\mathclose{}>0\right]\mathclose{}.

(10)

Our goal is to show that the fast rates
\tilde{O}(k/n)
enjoyed in the nominal setting are
preserved
in the adversarial setting when the underlying system satisfies certain incremental stability conditions.

3
Sample Complexity of Learning Adversarially Robust Stability Certificates

We first introduce our main stability assumption on the system dynamics.

Assumption 1

(Stability in the sense of Lyapunov)
.

Fix a perturbation set
\Delta(\cdot)
.
There exists a compact set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(\xi)\in S
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
,
t\in\mathcal{T}
, and
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(\cdot)\in\Delta(\xi)
.

For norm-bounded adversaries (
5
), this assumption is satisfied if the underlying nominal dynamics are input-to-state stable
[
18
]
. For Lipschitz (
6
) and combined (
7
) adversaries, additional care must be taken to ensure that
f(x)+\delta^{x}(x)
remains input-to-state stable for all admissible
\delta^{x}(x)
.

We further make the following regularity assumptions on the certificate function class
\mathcal{V}
.

Assumption 2

(Regularity of
\mathcal{V}
)
.

There exists constants
L_{V}
,
L_{\nabla V}
such that
for every
V\in\mathcal{V}
, the maps
x\mapsto V(x)
and
x\mapsto\left\langle\nabla V(x),f(x)\right\rangle
over
x\in S
are
L_{V}
and
L_{\nabla V}
-Lipschitz, respectively.

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
and the continuity of the nominal dynamics
f(x)
, there exist constants
B_{V}
,
B_{\nabla V}
, and
B_{\tilde{h}}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{V\in\mathcal{V}}\sup_{x\in S}{\mathopen{}\left|V(x)\right|\mathclose{}}\leqslant B_{V},\ \sup_{V\in\mathcal{V}}\sup_{x\in S}\left\|\nabla V(x)\right\|_{2}\leqslant B_{\nabla V},\ \sup_{V\in\mathcal{V}}\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{X}}{\mathopen{}\left|\tilde{h}(\xi,V)\right|\mathclose{}}\leqslant B_{\tilde{h}}.

Finally let
\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{V}}:=\sup_{x\in S}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}V(x)\\
\nabla V(x)\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2}
denote the supremum norm on the space
\mathcal{V}
.

Borrowing from the key insight in
[
4
]
,
we observe that any feasible solution
\tilde{V}_{n}
to (
9
) achieves zero empirical risk on the loss
\tilde{\ell}_{n}(V):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{1}\mathopen{}\left\{\tilde{h}(\xi_{i},V)>-\tau\right\}\mathclose{}
.
Therefore, results from statistical learning theory regarding zero empirical risk minimizers can be applied to get fast rates for the generalization error. To do so, we define the adversarial loss class

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}:=\mathopen{}\left\{\tilde{h}(\cdot,V),\;V\in\mathcal{V}\right\}\mathclose{}
.
Lemma 4.1 from
[
4
]
, which is in turn adapted from Theorem 5 of
[
35
]
, immediately gives the following bound on the generalization error.

Lemma 1

(Generalization error bound)
.

Fix a
\delta\in(0,1)
. Let us assume Assumptions
1
and
2
. Suppose that the optimization problem (
9
) is feasible and
\tilde{V}_{n}
is a solution. Then the following holds with probability at least
1-\delta
over
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
drawn i.i.d. from
\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}\mathopen{}\left(\tilde{V}_{n}\right)\mathclose{}\leqslant K\mathopen{}\left(\frac{\log^{3}(n)}{\tau^{2}}\mathcal{R}_{n}^{2}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})+\frac{\log\mathopen{}\left(\log\mathopen{}\left(B_{\tilde{h}}/\tau\right)\mathclose{}/\delta\right)\mathclose{}}{n}\right)\mathclose{},

(11)

where
K>0
is a universal constant and

\mathcal{R}_{n}(\tilde{}\mathcal{H}):=\sup_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma\sim\mathrm{Unif}\mathopen{}\left\{\pm 1\right\}\mathclose{}^{n}}\mathopen{}\left[\sup_{\tilde{h}(\cdot,V)\in\tilde{}\mathcal{H}}\frac{1}{n}{\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\tilde{h}(\xi_{i},V)\right|\mathclose{}}\right]\mathclose{}

is the Rademacher complexity of the adversarial loss class
\tilde{}\mathcal{H}
.

Lemma
1
reduces bounding the generalization error of an adversarially robust stability certificate to bounding the Rademacher complexity of the adversarial loss class
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
. We note that the nominal results of
Boffi et al. 2020a
are recovered by setting the perturbation budget
\varepsilon=0

3.1
A Simple Adversary-Agnostic Rademacher Complexity Bound

A standard technique for controlling the Rademacher complexity

\mathcal{R}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})
is appealing to
Dudley’s entropy integral
[
44
, Ch 5.]
.
Specifically, if we show that for some
L_{\tilde{h}}
,

{\mathopen{}\left|\tilde{h}(\xi,V_{1})-\tilde{h}(\xi,V_{2})\right|\mathclose{}}\leqslant L_{\tilde{h}}\left\|V_{1}-V_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{V}}\>\>\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X},\>\>V_{1},V_{2}\in\mathcal{V},

then Dudley’s inequality implies the bound

\mathcal{R}_{n}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})\leqslant\frac{24L_{\tilde{h}}}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\log N(\varepsilon;\mathcal{V},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{V}})}\,d\varepsilon
.
Our first result shows that our main assumptions
are sufficient to ensure that
L_{\tilde{h}}
can be controlled
with a uniform boundedness assumption on the adversary.

Lemma 2

(Uniformly bounded adversaries are sufficient)
.

Suppose that (i) Assumptions
1
and
2
hold, (ii)
B_{\delta}:=\sup_{x\in S}\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\left\|\delta(t,x)\right\|_{2}
is finite, and (iii) the flow
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(\xi)
is unique and complete over
\mathcal{T}
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
and all admissible
\delta(x,t)
. Let
L_{h}
denote any constant such that

{\mathopen{}\left|h(\xi,V_{1})-h(\xi,V_{2})\right|\mathclose{}}\leqslant L_{h}\left\|V_{1}-V_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{V}}

for all
\xi\in X
and
V_{1},V_{2}\in\mathcal{V}
.
Then,
L_{\tilde{h}}\leqslant L_{h}+B_{\delta}
.

Lemma

shows that if the nominal system is input-to-state stable and if the adversary is uniformly bounded over the set

from Assumption
1
, then by Dudley’s inequality, the Rademacher complexity
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})
is on the same order as the nominal complexity

\mathrm{err}(\tilde{V}_{n})
is on the same order as the nominal bound
\mathrm{err}(\hat{V}_{n})

We show next that with stronger assumptions on the stability of the dynamics, we can obtain bounds on
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})
that are additive, rather than muliplicative, with respect to the nominal complexity
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{H})

3.2
Improving the Adversarial Rademacher Complexity via Stability

To improve the bound from Lemma
2
, we first adapt a fundamental fact from the calculus of Rademacher complexities
[
3

\tilde{h}(\cdot,V)=h(\cdot,V)+\mathopen{}\left(\tilde{h}(\cdot,V)-h(\cdot,V)\right)\mathclose{}
to conclude that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})\leqslant\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{H})+\sup_{\xi\in X}\sup_{V\in\mathcal{V}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{\mathopen{}\left|\tilde{h}(\xi,V)-h(\xi,V)\right|\mathclose{}}.

(12)

Therefore, in order to bound
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})
, it suffices to uniformly bound
\tilde{h}(\xi,V)-h(\xi,V)
over
\xi\in X
and
V\in\mathcal{V}
. To do so, we introduce the notion of
(\beta,\rho,\gamma)
-exponential-incrementally-input-to-state stability
[
1

5
]

Definition 1

(
(\beta,\rho,\gamma)
-E-
\delta
ISS)
.

Let
\beta,\rho,\gamma>0
be positive constants. A continuous-time dynamical system
\dot{x}=f(x,t)
is
(\beta,\rho,\gamma)
-exponential-incrementally-input-to-state stable (
(\beta,\rho,\gamma)
-E-
\delta

and signal

\dot{x}(t)=f(x(t))

\displaystyle\left\|x_{t}-y_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\beta\left\|x_{0}-y_{0}\right\|_{2}e^{-\rho t}+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\rho(t-s)}\left\|u_{s}\right\|_{2}\,ds.

In short, the dependence of the distance between two trajectories on the initial conditions shrinks exponentially with time (incremental stability), and is input-to-state stable with respect to the inputs entering

\delta

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,t)\T M(x,t)+M(x,t)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,t)+\dot{M}(x,t)\preccurlyeq-2\lambda M(x,t).

(\sqrt{L/\mu},\lambda,\sqrt{L/\mu}

\delta

\gamma\varepsilon_{x}<\rho

1
holds and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{H})+\bigg[\mathopen{}\left(L_{\nabla V}+\eta L_{V}+B_{\nabla V}\varepsilon_{x}\right)\mathclose{}\frac{\gamma\varepsilon_{u}\rho^{-1}+\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\rho^{-1}e^{-1}B_{X}\beta\varepsilon_{x}}{1-\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\rho^{-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+B_{\nabla V}\beta\varepsilon_{x}B_{X}+B_{\nabla V}\varepsilon_{u}+\nu\bigg]\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

(15)

In particular, Theorem
1
shows that
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})\leqslant\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{H})+O(1)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}

for all the aforementioned adversary classes. Here,
O(1)
suppresses all problem specific constants. This demonstrates that under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, the Rademacher complexity of the resulting adversarial loss class is no more than an additive factor of order
O(1/\sqrt{n})
greater than the Rademacher complexity class of the nominal loss class.
Because a typical scaling of
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{H})\asymp\sqrt{k/n}
where
k
is the effective degrees of freedom of
\mathcal{V}
, the

O(1/\sqrt{n})
term is often negligible compared to
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{H})
.

The bounds in Theorem
1
involving the Lipschitz adversary are only valid when the denominator
1-\gamma\varepsilon\rho^{-1}
is positive, hence the necessary assumption that
\gamma\varepsilon<\rho
. This is a necessary assumption; when the budget for the Lipschitz adversary is too large, then an adversary can cause the system to diverge exponentially. To illustrate this, consider the scalar system
\dot{x}=-\rho x
, which we can verify is
(1,\rho,1)
-E-
\delta
ISS, perturbed by a
\varepsilon
-Lipschitz adversary that adds
\varepsilon x
to the dynamics such that
\dot{y}=-(\rho-\varepsilon)y
. If
\varepsilon>\rho
, then the perturbed trajectory will diverge away from
0
exponentially and we cannot hope to find a uniform bound on
\tilde{h}(\xi,V)-h(\xi,V)
for all
t
.

We conclude this section with an important example of a certificate function class and its associated Rademacher complexities. This example further highlights that the additive
O(1/\sqrt{n})
factor is comparatively negligible for many certificate function classes of interest.

Example 1

(Lipschitz Parametric Function Classes)
.

Consider the parametric function class

\mathcal{V}=\mathopen{}\left\{V_{\theta}(\cdot)=g(\cdot,\theta):\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{k},\;\left\|\theta\right\|\leqslant B_{\theta}\right\}\mathclose{},

(16)

where we assume
g:\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
is twice-continuously differentiable. The description (
16
) is very general; for example, feed-forward neural networks with differentiable activation functions and sum-of-squares polynomials lie in this function class. It is shown in
Boffi et al. 2020a
that
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{H})=O(\sqrt{k/n})
. Combining this with Theorem
1
, we conclude that

\mathcal{R}_{n}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})\leqslant\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{H})+O(1/\sqrt{n})=O(\sqrt{k/n}).

4
Learning Adversarially Robust Certificates in Practice

In this section, we illustrate the practicality and effectiveness
of learning adversarial certificates. We consider the damped pendulum
with dynamics
m\ell^{2}\ddot{\theta}+b\dot{\theta}+mg\ell\sin(\theta)=0
, where we set
m=1
,
\ell=1
,
b=2
, and
g=9.81
. The state space is given by
x=(\theta,\dot{\theta})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with stable equilibrium is at the origin and we wrap
\theta
to the interval
(-\pi,\pi]
. Consider the following certificate function class

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\left\{V_{\theta}(x)=x^{\top}\mathopen{}\left(L_{\theta}(x)^{\top}L_{\theta}(x)+I\right)\mathclose{}x,\;\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times h\times h\times p\cdot(2p)}\right\}\mathclose{},

(17)

where
L_{\theta}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2p\times p}
is the re-shaped output of a fully-connected neural network with 2 hidden layers of width
h=20
and
\tanh
activations.

We first demonstrate the robustness properties of an adversarially trained Lyapunov function versus a nominal one. We collect
n=1000
trajectories with randomly sampled initial conditions
\xi\sim\mathrm{Unif}\mathopen{}\left([-2,2]^{2}\right)\mathclose{}
. Each trajectory is rolled out using
scipy.integrate.solve_ivp
with horizon

and
dt=0.05
, such the size of the total dataset is
1000\times 160\times 2

Boffi et al. 2020a

(18)

\eta=0.4
and regularization parameter

500

[
15

Solving for the adversarially robust Lyapunov function is challenging due to the inner maximization problem over perturbations entering through the dynamics.
As is standard in the adversarial learning literature, we instead approximate the true adversarially robust loss function via an alternating scheme, summarized in Algorithm

5
Conclusion

Motivated by bridging the sim-to-real gap, we proposed and analyzed an approach to learning adversarially robust Lyapunov certificates. We showed that for systems that enjoy exponential incremental input-to-state stability, stability certificate functions that are robust to norm-bounded and Lipschitz adversarial perturbations to the system dynamics can be learned with negligible statistical overhead as compared to the nominal case. Future research directions include exploring the statistical tradeoffs occurring from progressively weaker notions of stability (e.g., incremental gain stability as defined in
Tu et al. 2021
), providing approximation guarantees for the adversarial training algorithm proposed in Section
4
, and extending our results to provide statistical guarantees for policies synthesized from robust certificate functions
[
19
,
38
]
.

\displaystyle d_{M}(x,y):=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(x,y)}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\gamma^{\prime}(s)\T M(\gamma(s))\gamma^{\prime}(s)}ds,

\gamma(0)=x

\gamma(1)=y

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,t)\T M(x,t)+M(x,t)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,t)+\dot{M}(x,t)\preccurlyeq-2\lambda M(x,t).

\displaystyle=f(x,t),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}d_{M(\cdot,t)}(x_{p}(t),x(t))\leqslant-\lambda d_{M(\cdot,t)}(x_{p}(t),x(t))+\left\|\Theta(x_{p}(t),t)d(x_{p}(t),t)\right\|_{2},

\displaystyle\dot{E}(\gamma_{t})

\displaystyle=2\left\langle\gamma^{\prime}_{t}(1),f(x)\right\rangle-2\left\langle\gamma^{\prime}_{t}(0),f(x_{p})+d(x_{p},t)\right\rangle+2\frac{\partial E}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}d_{M(\cdot,t)}(x_{p}(t),x(t))

∎

•

Combined Adversary

\Delta_{\varepsilon_{x},\varepsilon_{u}}^{x,u}
: proof follows similarly to the Lipschitz adversary case. Using E
\delta
ISS, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi_{t}-\tilde{\varphi}_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\beta\left\|\varphi_{0}-\tilde{\varphi}_{0}\right\|e^{-\rho t}+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\rho(t-s)}\left\|\delta(\tilde{\varphi}_{s})+\delta_{t}\right\|_{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\rho(t-s)}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{s}\right\|_{2}\;ds+\gamma\varepsilon_{u}\rho^{-1}

\displaystyle=\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\rho(t-s)}\left\|\varphi_{s}-\tilde{\varphi}_{s}\right\|_{2}\;ds+\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}te^{-\rho t}+\gamma\varepsilon_{u}\rho^{-1}.

Since
\gamma\varepsilon_{x}<\rho
, we take the supremum of both sides and shift terms around to get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\left\|\varphi_{t}-\tilde{\varphi}_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\gamma\varepsilon_{u}\rho^{-1}+\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\rho^{-1}\beta e^{-1}\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}}{1-\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\rho^{-1}},

and thus plugging into (
19
), we get

\displaystyle{\mathopen{}\left|\tilde{h}(\xi,V)-h(\xi,V)\right|\mathclose{}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathopen{}\left(L_{\nabla V}+\eta L_{V}+B_{\nabla V}\varepsilon_{x}\right)\mathclose{}\frac{\gamma\varepsilon_{u}\rho^{-1}+\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\rho^{-1}e^{-1}\beta\varepsilon_{x}\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}}{1-\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\rho^{-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+B_{\nabla V}\beta\varepsilon_{x}\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+B_{\nabla V}\varepsilon_{u}+\nu.

Applying (
12
) yields the desired result.

Appendix B
Adversarially Robust Certificates in Discrete Time

In the discrete time setting, we consider the system
x_{t+1}=f(x_{t})
. Like in the continuous time case,
f
is continuous and unknown, the state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is fully observed, the initial conditions
\xi
are drawn from some compact set
\mathcal{X}
, and
\varphi_{t}(\xi)
denotes the map to the state at time
t
given initial condition
\xi
. Given a candidate Lyapunov function
V
, we define the nominal and adversarial (exponential) Lyapunov decrease conditions as:

\displaystyle h(\xi,V)

\displaystyle=\max_{t\leqslant T}V\mathopen{}\left(\varphi_{t+1}(\xi)\right)\mathclose{}-\eta^{2}V\mathopen{}\left(\varphi_{t}(\xi)\right)\mathclose{}

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{\nu}(\xi,V)

\displaystyle=\max_{\tilde{\varphi}\in\Delta_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\max_{t\leqslant T}V\mathopen{}\left(\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1}(\xi)\right)\mathclose{}-\eta^{2}V\mathopen{}\left(\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(\xi)\right)\mathclose{}-\nu,\quad\nu\geqslant 0,

where
0<\eta<1
, as well as the corresponding loss classes
\mathcal{H}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
. The stability certification problem can be posed as the following feasibility problem

\mathrm{Find}_{V\in\mathcal{V}}\;\mathrm{s.t.}\;\;\tilde{h}_{\nu}(\xi,V)\leqslant-\tau,\quad i=1,\dots,n.

(20)

We make the following assumptions.

Assumption 3

(Discrete-time stability in the sense of Lyapunov)
.

Fix a perturbation set
\Delta(\cdot)
. There exists a compact set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(\xi)\in S
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
,
t\leqslant T
, and
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(\xi)\in\Delta(\xi)
.

Assumption 4

(Regularity of
\mathcal{V}
)
.

There exist a constant
L_{V}
such that for every
V\in\mathcal{V}
, the map
x\to V(x)
over
x\in S
is
L_{V}
-Lipschitz.

Under Assumptions
3
and
4
, and the continuity of the dynamics
f(x)
, there exist constants
B_{V}
and
B_{\tilde{h}}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{V\in\mathcal{V}}\sup_{x\in S}{\mathopen{}\left|V(x)\right|\mathclose{}}\leqslant B_{V},\;\sup_{V\in\mathcal{V}}\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{X}}{\mathopen{}\left|\tilde{h}(\xi,V)\right|\mathclose{}}\leqslant B_{\tilde{h}}.

Finally, let
\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{V}}:=\sup_{x\in S}{\mathopen{}\left|V(x)\right|\mathclose{}}
denote the supremum norm on the space
\mathcal{V}
. Under Assumptions
3
and
4
, and the discrete time definition of
\tilde{h}
, Lemma
1
holds in precisely the same form, such that we once again need only to bound the Rademacher complexity of the adversarial loss class. We now prove the discrete-time variant of the adversary-agnostic Rademacher complexity bound.

Lemma 4

(Discrete-time analogue of Lemma
2
)
.

Suppose that Assumptions
3
and
4
hold. Let
L_{h}
denote any constant such that

{\mathopen{}\left|h(\xi,V_{1})-h(\xi,V_{2})\right|\mathclose{}}\leqslant L_{h}\left\|V_{1}-V_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{V}}

for all
\xi\in X
and
V_{1},V_{2}\in\mathcal{V}
.
Then,
L_{\tilde{h}}\leqslant L_{h}+2
.

Proof.

Writing out
\tilde{h}(\xi,V_{1})-\tilde{h}(\xi,V_{2})
, for arbitrary
\xi
,
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
, we have:

\displaystyle\tilde{h}(\xi,V_{1})-\tilde{h}(\xi,V_{2})

\displaystyle=\max_{\tilde{\varphi}\in\Delta(\xi)}\max_{t\leqslant T}V_{1}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1})-\eta^{2}V_{1}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})-\max_{\tilde{\varphi}\in\Delta(\xi)}\max_{t\leqslant T}\mathopen{}\left(V_{2}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1})-\eta^{2}V_{2}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})\right)\mathclose{}

\displaystyle\leqslant\max_{\tilde{\varphi}\in\Delta(\xi)}\max_{t\leqslant T}V_{1}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1})-\eta^{2}V_{1}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})-\mathopen{}\left(V_{2}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1})-\eta^{2}V_{2}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})\right)\mathclose{}.

For any time
t
, we have

\displaystyle V_{1}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1})-\eta^{2}V_{1}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})-\mathopen{}\left(V_{2}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1})-\eta^{2}V_{2}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})\right)\mathclose{}

\displaystyle=V_{1}(f(\tilde{\varphi}_{t}))-\eta^{2}V_{1}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})-\mathopen{}\left(V_{2}(f(\tilde{\varphi}_{t}))-\eta^{2}V_{2}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})\right)\mathclose{}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathopen{}\left(V_{1}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1})-V_{2}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1})\right)\mathclose{}

\displaystyle\quad-\mathopen{}\left(V_{1}(f(\tilde{\varphi}_{t}))-V_{2}(f(\tilde{\varphi}_{t}))\right)\mathclose{}

\displaystyle\leqslant{\mathopen{}\left|h(\xi,V_{1})-h(\xi,V_{2})\right|\mathclose{}}+2\sup_{x\in S}{\mathopen{}\left|V_{1}(x)-V_{2}(x)\right|\mathclose{}}

\displaystyle\leqslant L_{h}\left\|V_{1}-V_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{V}}+2\left\|V_{1}-V_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{V}},

where added and subtracted
V_{1}(f(\tilde{\varphi}_{t}))
and
V_{2}(f(\tilde{\varphi}_{t}))
, and used Assumption
1
to bound the leftover terms using the definition of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{V}}
. The argument is symmetric for
V_{1},V_{2}
, so we have

{\mathopen{}\left|\tilde{h}(\xi,V_{1})-\tilde{h}(\xi,V_{2})\right|\mathclose{}}\leqslant(L_{h}+2)\left\|V_{1}-V_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{V}},

for all
\xi\in X
and
V_{1},V_{2}\in\mathcal{V}
.
∎

We note this is at first glance a better bound than the continuous-time version. This can be attributed to the fact that Assumption
3
is at face value more restrictive than its continuous-time analogue Assumption
1
, since it implicitly enforces a norm-constraint on
\delta_{t}
such that it cannot push
x_{t}
out of the compact set
S
, which is a property independent of the certificate
V
. On the other hand, Assumption
1
does not immediately enforce a norm-constraint on
\delta_{t}
–the implicit constraint depends on the choice of
V
, where
\delta_{t}
cannot render optimization problem (
9
) infeasible. As Assumption
3
is in a sense more restrictive than Assumption
1
, the bound we get is stronger.

We now re-introduce the norm-bounded, Lipschitz, and combined adversarial trajectory tubes in discrete time:

\displaystyle\Delta^{u}_{\varepsilon}(\xi):=\mathopen{}\left\{\tilde{\varphi}:\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1}=f(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})+\delta_{t},\;\tilde{\varphi}_{0}=\xi,\;\left\|\delta_{t}\right\|_{2}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\mathclose{}

(21)

\displaystyle\Delta^{x}_{\varepsilon}(\xi):=\mathopen{}\left\{\tilde{\varphi}:\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1}=f(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})+\delta(\tilde{\varphi}_{t}),\;\tilde{\varphi}_{0}=\xi,\;\left\|\delta(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})\right\|_{2}\leqslant\varepsilon\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}\right\|_{2}\right\}\mathclose{}

(22)

\displaystyle\Delta^{x,u}_{\varepsilon_{x},\varepsilon_{u}}(\xi):=\mathopen{}\left\{\tilde{\varphi}:\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1}=f(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})+\delta^{x}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})+\delta^{u}_{t},\;\tilde{\varphi}_{0}=\xi,\;\left\|\delta^{x}(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})\right\|_{2}\leqslant\varepsilon_{x}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}\right\|_{2},\;\left\|\delta^{u}_{t}\right\|_{2}\leqslant\varepsilon_{u}\right\}\mathclose{}.

(23)

Accordingly, we introduce
(\beta,\rho,\gamma)
-E
\delta
ISS in discrete time.

Definition 2

(Discrete-time
(\beta,\rho,\gamma)
-E
\delta
ISS)
.

Let
\beta,\gamma>0
be positive constants and
\rho\in(0,1)
. A discrete-time dynamical system
f(x,t)
is
(\beta,\rho,\gamma)
-exponential-incrementally-input-to-state stable if for every pair of initial conditions
(x_{0},y_{0})
and signal
u_{t}
(which can depend causally on
x,y
), the trajectories
x_{t+1}=f(x_{t},t)
and
y_{t+1}=f(y_{t},t)+u_{t}
satisfy for all
t\geqslant 0
:

\left\|x_{t}-y_{t}\right\|_{2}\leqslant\beta\rho^{t}\left\|x_{0}-y_{0}\right\|_{2}+\gamma\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1-k}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}.

(24)

As mentioned earlier in this paper, Proposition 5.3 of
[
5
]
gives that a discrete-time system contracting with respect to some metric is
(\beta,\rho,\gamma)
-E
\delta
ISS. We are now ready to provide the discrete-time analogues to the adversarial Rademacher complexities provided in Theorem

.

Theorem 2

(Discrete-time analogue to Theorem
1
)
.

Put
B_{X}:=\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{X}}\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}
, let Assumption
4
hold, and assume that the nominal discrete-time system
f(x)
is
(\beta,\rho,\gamma)
-E
\delta
ISS. Then for

•

adversarial trajectories drawn from the norm-bounded tube
\Delta^{u}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)
defined in (

), Assumption
3
holds and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{H})+\mathopen{}\left[(1+\eta^{2})L_{V}\frac{\gamma\varepsilon}{1-\rho}+\nu\right]\mathclose{}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}},

(25)

•

adversarial trajectories drawn from the Lipschitz tube
\Delta^{x}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)
defined in (
22
), if
\varepsilon>0
is small enough such that
\rho+\gamma\varepsilon<1
, then Assumption
3
holds and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{H})+\mathopen{}\left[L_{V}\beta B_{X}(\rho+\gamma\varepsilon+\eta^{2})+\nu\right]\mathclose{}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}},

(26)

•

adversarial trajectories drawn from the combined tube
\Delta^{x,u}_{\varepsilon_{x},\varepsilon_{u}}
defined in (

), if
\varepsilon_{x}>0
is small enough such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{H})+\bigg[(1+\eta^{2})L_{V}\mathopen{}\left(\frac{1-\rho}{1-(\rho+\gamma\varepsilon_{x})}\frac{\gamma\beta B_{X}\varepsilon_{x}}{e\rho\log\mathopen{}\left(\rho^{-1}\right)\mathclose{}}+\frac{\gamma\varepsilon_{u}}{1-\rho}\right)\mathclose{}+\nu\bigg]\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

(27)

We note that the necessary condition that
\rho+\gamma\varepsilon<1
for the Lipschitz and combined adversaries is nicely analogous to the continuous-time case where we needed
\gamma\varepsilon<\rho
; in both cases, the adversary cannot be powerful enough to de-stabilize the system. One can consider the scalar system
x_{t+1}=\rho x_{t}
,
0<\rho<1
and the adversary

to see why this condition cannot be loosened in general. We now provide the proof to Theorem
2
.

Proof.

We observe that for an arbitrary perturbation tube
\Delta(\xi)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}(\xi,V)-h(\xi,V)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\tilde{\varphi}\in\Delta(\xi)}\sup_{t\leqslant T}\;V(\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1})-\eta^{2}V(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})-\nu-\sup_{t\leqslant T}\mathopen{}\left(V(\varphi_{t+1})-\eta^{2}V(\varphi_{t})\right)\mathclose{}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{\tilde{\varphi}\in\Delta(\xi)}\sup_{t\leqslant T}\;V(\tilde{\varphi}_{t+1})-V(\varphi_{t+1})-\eta^{2}\mathopen{}\left(V(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})-V(\varphi_{t})\right)\mathclose{}-\nu

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{\tilde{\varphi}\in\Delta(\xi)}\sup_{t\leqslant T}\;(1+\eta^{2})L_{V}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}-\nu,

and thus

{\mathopen{}\left|\tilde{h}(\xi,V)-h(\xi,V)\right|\mathclose{}}\leqslant\sup_{\tilde{\varphi}\in\Delta(\xi)}\sup_{t\leqslant T}\;(1+\eta^{2})L_{V}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}+\nu.

(28)

Thus, it suffices to establish bounds on
\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}
.

•

Norm-bounded Adversary

\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{u}
: from E
\delta
ISS, we know that the deviation can be bounded by:

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\beta\rho^{t}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{0}-\varphi_{0}\right\|_{2}+\gamma\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1-k}\left\|\delta_{k}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1-k}\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\gamma\varepsilon}{1-\rho}.

Plugging this back into (
28
), we get

\displaystyle{\mathopen{}\left|\tilde{h}(\xi,V)-h(\xi,V)\right|\mathclose{}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+\eta^{2})L_{V}\frac{\gamma\varepsilon}{1-\rho}+\nu.

•

Lipschitz Adversary

\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{x}
: using a more careful analysis, we can get a finer bound in the discrete-time setting than the continuous-time setting, where we get an explicit time-dependent upper bound on the deviation
\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}
. From E
\delta
ISS, observe that for any
k\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{k}-\varphi_{k}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\rho^{k-1-i}\left\|\delta(\tilde{\varphi}_{i})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\rho^{k-1-i}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{i}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\rho^{k-1-i}\mathopen{}\left(\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{i}-\varphi_{i}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\varphi_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\mathclose{}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\rho^{k-1-i}\left\|\varphi_{i}\right\|_{2}+\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\rho^{k-1-i}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{i}-\varphi_{i}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\rho^{k-1}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\rho^{k-1-i}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{i}-\varphi_{i}\right\|_{2}.

(29)

We keep the sum in the first term to keep the our later algebraic manipulations clear. We also observe we can move the starting index of the second sum from
0
to
1
, since
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}-\varphi_{0}=0
. Now fixing any timestep
t\leqslant T
for
t\geqslant 2
, we apply (
29
) recursively:

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1-k_{1}}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{k_{1}}-\varphi_{k_{1}}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1-k_{1}}\mathopen{}\left(\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{k_{1}-1}\rho^{k_{1}-1}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{k_{1}-1}\rho^{k_{1}-k_{2}-1}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{k_{2}}-\varphi_{k_{2}}\right\|_{2}\right)\mathclose{}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+\mathopen{}\left(\gamma\varepsilon\right)\mathclose{}^{2}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{k_{1}-1}\rho^{t-2}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathopen{}\left(\gamma\varepsilon\right)\mathclose{}^{2}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{k_{1}-1}\rho^{t-2-k_{2}}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{k_{2}}-\varphi_{k_{2}}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+\mathopen{}\left(\gamma\varepsilon\right)\mathclose{}^{2}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{k_{1}-1}\rho^{t-2}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+(\gamma\varepsilon)^{j}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{j}=0}^{k_{j-1}-1}\rho^{t-j}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(\gamma\varepsilon)^{j}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{j}=1}^{k_{j-1}-1}\rho^{t-j-k_{j}}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{k_{j}}-\varphi_{k_{j}}\right\|_{2}.

This recursive process terminates when there does not exist an assignment of indices
k_{1},\dots,k_{j-1}
such that the summand
\sum_{k_{j}=1}^{k_{j}-1}
is non-empty, i.e.
1=k_{j}>k_{j-1}-1
. The largest
j
such that the aforementioned summand is possibly non-empty is when
k_{i}=k_{i-1}-1
for all
i<j
and
k_{1}=t-1
, which implies
k_{i}=t-i
. In order for
k_{j}\geqslant 1
, we must have
k_{j-1}-1=t-j\geqslant 1
, i.e.
j\leqslant t-1
, and thus our recursive expansion terminates when
j=t-1
. Therefore, continuing our earlier series of inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+\cdots+(\gamma\varepsilon)^{t-1}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{t-1}=0}^{k_{t-2}-1}\rho^{1-k_{t-1}}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(\gamma\varepsilon)^{t-1}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{t-1}=1}^{k_{t-2}-1}\rho^{t-(t-1)-k_{t-1}}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{k_{t-1}}-\varphi_{k_{t-1}}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+(\gamma\varepsilon)^{t-1}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{t-1}=0}^{k_{t-2}-1}\rho^{1-k_{t-1}}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+(\gamma\varepsilon)^{t-1}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{1}-\varphi_{1}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+(\gamma\varepsilon)^{t-1}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{t-1}=0}^{k_{t-2}-1}\rho^{1-k_{t-1}}\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+(\gamma\varepsilon)^{t-1}\gamma\varepsilon\rho\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}(\gamma\varepsilon)^{j}\rho^{t-j}\mathopen{}\left(\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{j-1}=1}^{k_{j-2}-1}\sum_{k_{j}=0}^{k_{j-1}-1}1\right)\mathclose{}+\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}(\gamma\varepsilon)^{t}\rho

\displaystyle\leqslant\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}(\gamma\varepsilon)^{j}\rho^{t-j}\mathopen{}\left(\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{j-1}=1}^{k_{j-2}-1}\sum_{k_{j}=0}^{k_{j-1}-1}1\right)\mathclose{}+\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}(\gamma\varepsilon)^{t}+\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}\rho^{t}.

Now it remains to determine the value of
\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{j-1}=1}^{k_{j-2}-1}\sum_{k_{j}=0}^{k_{j-1}-1}1
. Observe the sum is only non-empty if for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant j
,
k_{i-1}-1-k_{i}\geqslant 0
, where we define
k_{0}=t
. Let us define the variables
c_{i}=k_{i-1}-k_{i}\geqslant 1
for
i=1,\dots,j
, and we define
c_{j+1}:=k_{j}-0=k_{j}\geqslant 0
. The tuple
(c_{1},\dots,c_{j+1})
thus satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{j}c_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{j+1}k_{i-1}-k_{i}=t-0=t
. Therefore, the number of terms in the nested summand is equal to the number of integer tuples
(c_{1},\dots,c_{j+1})
, where
c_{1},\dots,c_{j}
are positive and
c_{j+1}\geqslant 0
, that sum up to
t
, which in turn can be transformed into a balls-and-bins problem where we have
t
total balls, and
j+1
bins, but with the first
j
bins already containing
1
ball. Thus applying the standard balls-and-bins formula for
t-j
balls and
j+1
bins, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{j-1}=1}^{k_{j-2}-1}\sum_{k_{j}=0}^{k_{j-1}-1}1

\displaystyle=\binom{(t-j)+(j+1)-1}{(j+1)-1}=\binom{t}{j}.

Plugging this back into the last line of our series of inequalities, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}(\gamma\varepsilon)^{j}\rho^{t-j}\mathopen{}\left(\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{t-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{j-1}=1}^{k_{j-2}-1}\sum_{k_{j}=0}^{k_{j-1}-1}1\right)\mathclose{}+\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}(\gamma\varepsilon)^{t}+\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}\rho^{t}

\displaystyle=\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}(\gamma\varepsilon)^{j}\rho^{t-j}\binom{t}{j}+\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}(\gamma\varepsilon)^{t}+\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}\rho^{t}

\displaystyle=\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}\sum_{j=0}^{t}\binom{t}{j}(\gamma\varepsilon)^{j}\rho^{t-j}

\displaystyle=\beta\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}\mathopen{}\left(\rho+\gamma\varepsilon\right)\mathclose{}^{t}.

This gives us the bound:

\displaystyle{\mathopen{}\left|\tilde{h}(\xi,V)-h(\xi,V)\right|\mathclose{}}

\displaystyle\leqslant L_{V}\beta B_{X}(\rho+\gamma\varepsilon+\eta^{2})+\nu.

•

Combined Adversary

\Delta_{\varepsilon_{x},\varepsilon_{u}}^{x,u}
: from
(\beta,\rho,\gamma)
-E
\delta
ISS, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1-k}\left\|\delta^{x}(\tilde{\varphi}_{k})+\delta^{u}_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1-k}\left\|\delta^{x}(\tilde{\varphi}_{k})\right\|_{2}+\gamma\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1-k}\left\|\delta^{u}_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1-k}\left\|\delta^{x}(\tilde{\varphi}_{k})\right\|_{2}+\gamma\frac{1}{1-\rho}\varepsilon_{u}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t-1-k}\mathopen{}\left(\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{k}-\varphi_{k}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\varphi_{k}\right\|_{2}\right)\mathclose{}+\gamma\frac{1}{1-\rho}\varepsilon_{u}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\mathopen{}\left(\max_{t}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}\right)\mathclose{}\frac{1}{1-\rho}+\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\beta t\rho^{t-1}\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+\gamma\frac{1}{1-\rho}\varepsilon_{u}.

Similarly taking a maximum with respect to
t
on both sides, we get

\displaystyle\max_{t}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\mathopen{}\left(\max_{t}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}\right)\mathclose{}\frac{1}{1-\rho}+\max_{t}\gamma\varepsilon_{x}\beta t\rho^{t-1}\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}+\gamma\frac{1}{1-\rho}\varepsilon_{u}.

It is straightforward to compute
\max_{t}t\rho^{t-1}=\frac{1}{e\rho\log(\rho^{-1})}
. Thus, rearranging some terms, and assuming that
\rho+\gamma\varepsilon_{x}<1
, we get

\displaystyle\max_{t}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{t}-\varphi_{t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1-\rho}{1-(\rho+\gamma\varepsilon_{x})}\gamma\mathopen{}\left(\frac{\beta}{e\rho\log\mathopen{}\left(\rho^{-1}\right)\mathclose{}}\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}\varepsilon_{x}+\frac{1}{1-\rho}\varepsilon_{u}\right)\mathclose{},

which yields our desired bound.

∎

Hardy and Poincaré inequalities in fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
Kaushik Bal, Kaushik Mohanta, Prosenjit Roy, Firoj Sk

Abstract
. We provide sufficient conditions for boundary Hardy inequality to hold in bounded Lipschitz domains, complement of a point (the so called point Hardy inequality), domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function, complement of a bounded Lipschitz domain in fractional Orlicz-Sobolev setting. As a consequence we get sufficient conditions for regional fractional Orlicz Poincaré inequality in bounded Lipschitz domains. Necessary conditions for fractional Orlicz Hardy and regional fractional Orlicz Poincaré inequalities are also given for bounded Lipschitz domains. Various sufficient conditions on open sets are provided for fractional Orlicz Poincaré inequality and regional fractional Orlicz Poincaré inequality to hold.

Introduction

The aim of this article is to study two very well known inequalities, the Poincaré inequality and the Hardy inequality on fractional Orlicz-Sobolev setting. The classical Poincaré inequality
[
Eva10
, Chapter 5.8.1]
states that for any bounded domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
q\geq 1
there exists
c=c(q,N,D)>0
such that for any
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
,

(1.1)

\|f\|_{L^{q}(D)}\leq c\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(D)}.

For bounded domains it is a standard fact that the best constant is attained.
Although generalized in many different direction as can be seen in
[
Beb03
,
J+86
,
KZ08
,
LSY03
]
, for the purpose of this paper we would like to refer Gossez
[
Gos74
, Lemma 5.7]
, who generalized
eq.

1.1
in Orlicz-Sobolev setting (defined below). The standard boundary Hardy inequality states that if
D
is a
bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
q\geq 1
, then there exists a constant

c=c(q)>0
such that for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
,

(1.2)

\|f/\delta_{D}\|_{L^{q}(D)}\leq c\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(D)},

where
\delta_{D}(x):=\inf\{|x-y|:y\in D^{c}\}
. We shall frequently denote
\delta_{D}(x)
by
\delta_{x}
. This has been further developed in
[
BM97
,
BMS00
,
KMP07
,
MMP98
]
and several other works. For the generalization to Orlicz version of local Hardy inequality we refer to
[
Hei95
,
KaPPu09
,
MMOS14
,
MNOS11
]
.

We shall primarily be concerned with the “Orlicz space”
L^{A}(D)
and the “fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space”
W^{s,A}(D)
. We start by defining these spaces. A continuous, convex function
A:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that

\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\frac{A(t)}{t}=0
and

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{A(t)}{t}=\infty
is called an

N
-function
or an
Young function

[
AF03
, Chapter 8.2]
.

Definition 1

(
[
AF03
, Chapter 8.2]
)
.

We say that
A
satisfies the
\mathbf{\Delta_{2}}

condition globally
or
simply the
\mathbf{\Delta_{2}}

condition
(
A\in\Delta_{2}
) if there exists constant
p>0
such
that

A(2t)\leq pA(t),\ \forall\ t>0.

The set

L^{A}(D):=\left\{f:D\to\mathbb{R}\ \textrm{measurable}\ \Big|\ \exists\ \lambda>0\mbox{ such that }M_{L^{A}(D)}\left(\frac{f}{\lambda}\right)<\infty\right\}

is called the
Orlicz space
and the set

W^{s,A}(D):=\left\{f\in L^{A}(D)\ \Big|\ \exists\ \lambda>0\mbox{ such that }M_{W^{s,A}(D)}\left(\frac{f}{\lambda}\right)<\infty\right\}

is called the
fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space
, where

M_{L^{A}(D)}(f):=\int\limits_{D}A(|f(x)|)dx\ \textrm{ and }M_{W^{s,A}(D)}(f):=\iint\limits_{D\times D}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}.

In the case
A(t)=t^{q}
for some
q>1
,
L^{A}(D)
and
W^{s,A}(D)
are well known Lebesgue space
L^{q}(D)
and the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,q}(D)
respectively
[
DNPV12
, p. 524]
.

For
s\in(0,1)
, we start by defining the following important quotients that will be used frequently throughout this article:

H_{N,s,A}(D):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)\\
f\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{M_{W^{s,A}(D)}(f)}{\int\limits_{D}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx},\ P^{1}_{N,s,A}(D):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)\\
f\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{M_{W^{s,A}(D)}(f)}{\int\limits_{D}A(|f(x)|)dx}

and

P^{2}_{N,s,A}(D):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)\\
f\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{M_{W^{s,A}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}(f)}{\int\limits_{D}A(|f(x)|)dx}.

We shall say that

•

fractional Orlicz Hardy inequality (denoted as
FOHI(s,A)
)
holds if
H_{N,s,A}(D)>0
;

•

regional fractional Orlicz Poincaré inequality (
RFOPI(s,A)
) holds if
P^{1}_{N,s,A}(D)>0
;

•

fractional Orlicz Poincaré inequality (
FOPI(s,A)
) holds if
P^{2}_{N,s,A}(D)>0
.

.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A\in\Delta_{2}
. Then

A(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{p}A(t)\mbox{ for }t\in[0,\infty),\quad\forall\ \lambda\geq 1,

where
p
is the constant in
definition

1
.
The above inequality is equivalent to

A(\lambda t)\geq\lambda^{p}A(t)\mbox{ for }t\in[0,\infty),\quad\forall\ 0\leq\lambda\leq 1.

Proof.

Since
A\in\Delta_{2}
, there exists
p>0
such that
A(2t)\leq pA(t),\quad\forall\ t>0
. Now for
a
as in
lemma

2.1
, using the non decreasing property of
a
,

pA(t)\geq A(2t)=\int\limits_{0}^{2t}a(\tau)d\tau>\int\limits_{t}^{2t}a(\tau)d\tau>ta(t).

This implies for any
\lambda>1
,

\log\left(\frac{A(\lambda t)}{A(t)}\right)=\int\limits_{t}^{\lambda t}\frac{a(\tau)}{A(\tau)}d\tau<\int\limits_{t}^{\lambda t}\frac{p}{\tau}d\tau=p\log\left(\frac{\lambda t}{t}\right)=\log(\lambda^{p}).

The lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain,
A\in\Delta_{2}
. Then for some constant
c=c(D,A)>0

P^{1}_{N,s,A}(D)\geq cH_{N,s,A}(D).

Proof.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
. First assume
\mbox{diam}(D)\leq 1
. By the monotonicity of
A
,

\int\limits_{D}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\geq\int\limits_{D}A(|f(x)|)dx.

In the case

, we exploit the
\Delta_{2}
condition of

\int\limits_{D}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\geq\int\limits_{D}A\left(\mbox{diam}(D)^{-s}|f(x)|\right)dx\geq\mbox{diam}(D)^{-sp}\int\limits_{D}A(|f(x)|)dx.

Hence the lemma follows.
∎

\iint\limits_{D\times D}A\left(\frac{|v_{t}(x)-v_{t}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}\geq\begin{cases}t^{s-N}\iint\limits_{tD\times tD}A\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}},&t\geq 1,\\
t^{sp-N}\iint\limits_{tD\times tD}A\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}},&t<1.\end{cases}

Furthermore,

\frac{P^{1}_{N,s,A}(D)}{t^{s}}\leq P^{1}_{N,s,A}(tD)\leq\frac{P^{1}_{N,s,A}(D)}{t^{sp}}\ \ \mbox{if}\ t<1,

\frac{P^{1}_{N,s,A}(D)}{t^{sp}}\leq P^{1}_{N,s,A}(tD)\leq\frac{P^{1}_{N,s,A}(D)}{t^{s}}\ \ \mbox{if}\ t\geq 1.

(3)

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set and
t>0
. Assume that
A\in\Delta_{2}
. Then

\frac{P^{2}_{N,s,A}(D)}{t^{s}}\leq P^{2}_{N,s,A}(tD)\leq\frac{P^{2}_{N,s,A}(D)}{t^{sp}}\ \ \mbox{if}\ t<1,

\frac{P^{2}_{N,s,A}(D)}{t^{sp}}\leq P^{2}_{N,s,A}(tD)\leq\frac{P^{2}_{N,s,A}(D)}{t^{s}}\ \ \mbox{if}\ t\geq 1.

(4)

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be such that
BC(D)=\infty
, then
P^{1}_{N,s,A}(D)=0=P^{2}_{N,s,A}(D)
.

Proof.

(1)

Directly follows from the definition.

(2)

For the first inequality, take the change of variable
X=tx,Y=ty
and in case of
t<1
, use
lemma

2.2
to infer
\frac{A(t^{s}f(x,y))}{t^{s}f(x,y)}<\frac{A(f(x,y))}{f(x,y)}
. In case of
t\geq 1
use
lemma

2.3
. For the second inequality replace
t
by
\frac{1}{t}
.

(3)

Similar as case
(2)
.

(4)

BC(D)=\infty
implies there exist a positive sequence
\{r_{n}\}_{n}
and a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n}
such that
r_{n}\to\infty
and
B(x_{n},r_{n})\subseteq D
. Note that
P^{1}_{N,s,A}(D)\leq P^{2}_{N,s,A}(D)
. Then by
(1)
and
(3)
we infer that

P^{2}_{N,s,A}(D)\leq P^{2}_{N,s,A}(B(x_{n},r_{n}))=P^{2}_{N,s,A}(B(0,r_{n}))\leq\frac{P^{2}_{N,s,A}(B(0,1))}{r_{n}^{s}}\to 0.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
D\subsetneqq\mathbb{R}
be an open set with
BC(D)<\infty
and
D=\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}I_{k}
, where
I_{k}
’s are disjoint intervals. Then for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
P^{1}_{1,s,A}(I_{k})\geq\min\{BC(D)^{-sp},1\}P^{1}_{1,s,A}((0,1))
, where
p>1
is as in
lemma

2.3
.

Proof.

Using (2) of
proposition

2.1
, we have

P^{1}_{1,s,A}(I_{k})\geq\mbox{diam}(I_{k})^{-\beta}P^{1}_{1,s,A}((0,1)),

where

\beta=\begin{cases}s,&\mbox{if }\ \mbox{diam}(I_{k})<1,\\
sp,&\mbox{if }\ \mbox{diam}(I_{k})\geq 1.\end{cases}

If
\mbox{diam}(I_{k})<1
,
P^{1}_{1,s,A}(I_{k})\geq P^{1}_{1,s,A}(0,1)
. On the other hand if
\mbox{diam}(I_{k})\geq 1
,

P^{1}_{1,s,A}(I_{k})\geq\mbox{diam}(I_{k})^{-sp}P^{1}_{1,s,A}((0,1))\geq BC(D)^{-sp}P^{1}_{1,s,A}((0,1)).

Combining the two cases we finally get the desired result.
∎

In this section we shall prove

We now fix a function

Lemma 3.1

Let

\int\limits_{\Omega\setminus G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\frac{2^{p+1}l_{1}^{N+sp}}{l_{2}}\int\limits_{x\in\Omega\setminus G}\int\limits_{y\in\Omega}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dydx}{|x-y|^{N}}.

Proof.

For

, using

2.2
and
2.3

\int\limits_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N}}\geq\int\limits_{B(x,l_{1}\delta_{x})\cap\Omega}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N}}\\
\geq\int\limits_{B(x,l_{1}\delta_{x})\cap\Omega}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{(l_{1}\delta_{x})^{s}}\right)\frac{dy}{(l_{1}\delta_{x})^{N}}\\
\geq\frac{1}{l_{1}^{N+sp}\delta_{x}^{N}}\int\limits_{B(x,l_{1}\delta_{x})\cap\Omega}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dy\geq\frac{l_{2}}{l_{1}^{N+sp}2^{p+1}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right).

Integrating over

, we get the desired
result.
∎

In the view of the above lemma, we can says that the set
\Omega\setminus G
is a good set for fractional Orlicz Hardy inequality in the sense that the required inequality holds in it.

For any

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
x\in G
and

. If
\mathcal{L}^{N}(E)\geq l_{2}\delta_{x}^{N}
,
then

\frac{\mathcal{L}^{N}(E)}{2}\leq\mathcal{L}^{N}(E)-\frac{l_{2}\delta_{x}^{N}}{2}\leq\mathcal{L}^{N}(E^{*}(x)).

Proof.

Since

,

, by
eq.

3.1

A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)\geq\frac{2^{p+1}}{l_{2}\delta_{x}^{N}}\int\limits_{B(x,l_{1}\delta_{x})\cap\Omega}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dy\geq\frac{2^{p+1}}{l_{2}\delta_{x}^{N}}\int\limits_{E\setminus E^{*}(x)}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dy.

Note that,
y\in E\setminus E^{*}(x)

|f(x)-f(y)|\geq|\ |f(x)|-|f(y)|\ |\geq\frac{|f(x)|}{2}
. Thus by using
lemmas

2.2
and
2.3
we get

A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)\geq\frac{2}{l_{2}\delta_{x}^{N}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)\left\{\mathcal{L}^{N}(E)-\mathcal{L}^{N}(E^{*}(x))\right\}.

Using the hypothesis on measure of
E
, we get
\frac{\mathcal{L}^{N}(E)}{2}\leq\mathcal{L}^{N}(E)-\frac{l_{2}\delta_{x}^{N}}{2}\leq\mathcal{L}^{N}(E^{*}(x)).

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
E_{1}\subseteq\Omega
and
E_{2}\subseteq B(x,l_{1}\delta_{x})\cap\Omega
be such that

\mathcal{L}^{N}(E_{2})\geq l_{2}\delta_{x}^{N}
for all
x\in E_{1}
. Then

\int\limits_{E_{1}\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\frac{2^{p+1}\mathcal{L}^{N}(E_{1})}{\mathcal{L}^{N}(E_{2})}\int\limits_{E_{2}}A\left(\frac{\sup\{\delta_{x}^{s}\ |\ x\in E_{2}\}\;|f(y)|}{\inf\{\delta_{x}^{s}\ |\ x\in E_{1}\}\;\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy.

Proof.

Assume that
E_{1}\cap G
is non empty. First, we fix
\eta>1
and pick
x_{0}\in E_{1}\cap G
such that
\sup\limits_{x\in E_{1}\cap G}|f(x)|\leq\eta|f(x_{0})|
. Also
|f(x_{0})|\leq 2|f(y)|
for any
y\in E_{2}^{*}(x_{0})
. Hence, for any

y\in E_{2}^{*}(x_{0})
, using
lemmas

2.2
and
2.3
,

\int\limits_{E_{1}\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\mathcal{L}^{N}(E_{1}\cap G)A\left(\frac{\sup\limits_{x\in E_{1}\cap G}|f(x)|}{\inf\{\delta_{x}^{s}\ |\ x\in E_{1}\cap G\}}\right)\\
\leq\mathcal{L}^{N}(E_{1}\cap G)A\left(\frac{2\eta|f(y)|}{\inf\{\delta_{x}^{s}\ |\ x\in E_{1}\cap G\}}\right)\leq 2^{p}\eta^{p}\mathcal{L}^{N}(E_{1})A\left(\frac{\sup\{\delta_{y}^{s}\ |\ y\in E_{2}^{*}(x_{0})\}\;|f(y)|}{\inf\{\delta_{x}^{s}\ |\ x\in E_{1}\}\;\delta_{y}^{s}}\right).

Integrating over
y\in E_{2}^{*}(x_{0})
, using
lemma

3.2
and the fact that
E_{2}^{*}(x_{0})\subseteq E_{2}
, we obtain

\int\limits_{E_{1}\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\frac{2^{p+1}\eta^{p}\mathcal{L}^{N}(E_{1})}{\mathcal{L}^{N}(E_{2})}\int\limits_{E_{2}}A\left(\frac{\sup\{\delta_{x}^{s}\ |\ x\in E_{2}\}\;|f(y)|}{\inf\{\delta_{x}^{s}\ |\ x\in E_{1}\}\;\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy.

Letting

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be two open sets,
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
,
l_{1},l_{2}>0
,
G=G(f,\Omega;l_{1},l_{2}),0<\gamma<1
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume
\Omega=\cup_{j=0}^{\infty}A_{j}
,
\mathcal{L}^{N}(A_{i}\cap A_{j})=0\ \forall\ j\neq i
and
\exists\;n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f\equiv 0
on
A_{j}
for
j\geq n_{0}
.

(1)

If
\ \forall\ j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\int\limits_{G\cap A_{j}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\gamma\int\limits_{A_{j+m}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx,

then there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\int\limits_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq c\iint\limits_{\Omega\times\Omega}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}.

(2)

If
\ \forall\ j\geq m
,

\int\limits_{G\cap A_{j}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\gamma\int\limits_{A_{j-m}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx,

then there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\int\limits_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq c\iint\limits_{\Omega\times\Omega}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}+c\int\limits_{A_{0}\cup\cdots\cup A_{m-1}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx.

Proof.

(1)

\int\limits_{\Omega\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{A_{j}\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\gamma\int\limits_{A_{j+m}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\\
=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\gamma\left[\int\limits_{A_{j+m}\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx+\int\limits_{A_{j+m}\setminus G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\right]\\
\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\gamma\left[\gamma\int\limits_{A_{j+2m}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx+\int\limits_{A_{j+m}\setminus G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\right]\\
\leq\cdots\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}\int\limits_{A_{j+km}\setminus G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx.

Here we used the fact that
f\equiv 0
for large

forcing the iterative process to terminate. So we have

\int\limits_{\Omega\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}\int\limits_{\Omega\setminus G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx.

Hence by using above estimate and
lemma

3.1
we get

\int\limits_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx=\int\limits_{\Omega\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx+\int\limits_{\Omega\setminus G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\\
\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}\int\limits_{\Omega\setminus G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq c\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}.

This completes the proof of (1).

(2) Let for each
j\geq 0
,
k_{j}
be the largest nonnegative integer such that
j-k_{j}m\geq 0
, so that
0\leq j-k_{j}m\leq m-1
. Then
k_{j}
-many repeated applications of the inequality in the hypothesis gives

\int\limits_{A_{j}\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\gamma\int\limits_{A_{j-m}}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy\\
=\gamma\left[\int\limits_{A_{j-m}\setminus G}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy+\int\limits_{A_{j-m}\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy\right]\\
\leq\cdots\leq\gamma^{k_{j}}\int\limits_{A_{j-k_{j}m}\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy\ +\ \sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\gamma^{k}\int\limits_{A_{j-km}\setminus G}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy\\
\leq\gamma^{k_{j}}\int\limits_{A_{0}\cup\cdots\cup A_{m-1}}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy\ +\ \sum_{k=1}^{k_{j}}\gamma^{k}\int\limits_{A_{j-km}\setminus G}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy.

Summing over
j
,

\int\limits_{\Omega\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\ \frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\left[\ \int\limits_{A_{0}\cup\cdots\cup A_{m-1}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx+\ \int\limits_{\Omega\setminus G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\right].

Proceeding as in part (1), the lemma follows.

∎

Before proving
theorem

1.2
, we need a geometric decomposition of a bounded Lipschitz domain
D
, given in
[
Dyd04
, p. 581]
. We outline the construction for the sake of completeness.

Let us denote
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{N-1},x_{N})=(\tilde{x},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with

\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
,
x_{N}\in\mathbb{R}
.
D\subsetneqq\mathbb{R}^{N}
shall be assumed to be a bounded Lipschitz domain throughout the rest of the section. For any
z\in\partial D
there are linear isometry
L_{z}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
and Lipschitz
function
\phi_{z}:\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\mbox{Lip}(\phi_{z})\leq\mbox{Lip}(D)
and

L_{z}(D)\cap B(L_{z}(z),r_{0})=\ \{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\ |\ x_{N}>\phi_{z}(\tilde{x})\}\cap B(L_{z}(z),r_{0}),

for some positive
r_{0}
which depends only on
D
(existence of
r_{0}
is guaranteed because
\partial D
is compact). Without loss of generality, we can assume
L_{z}
to be the identity map. Because otherwise we can work with the Lipschitz domain
L_{z}(D)
and the point
L_{z}(z)
, then pull back the construction to the original domain
D
and the point
z
via
L_{z}^{-1}
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
we set

V_{z}(x):=|x_{N}-\phi_{z}(\tilde{x})|.

For
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
and
r>0
define

Q_{z}(E,r):=\{x\in D\ |\ \tilde{x}\in E,0<V_{z}(x)\leq r\}.

Set

K_{r}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\ \big|\ |x_{\ell}-z_{\ell}|\leq\frac{r}{2},\;\ell=1,2,\cdots,N-1\right\}.

That is
K_{r}
is an
N-1
dimensional square of side-length
r
.

Q_{z}(K_{\rho},r)
is referred to as a Lipschitz box and is denoted by
Q_{z}(r)
.
We now choose
\rho>0
small enough such that
Q_{z}(\rho)\subseteq D\cap B(z,r_{0}/2)
.
Let
x\in Q_{z}(\rho)
. Then using the fact that
\phi_{z}(w)=w_{N}
for any
w\in\partial D\cap B(z,r_{0})
,

V_{z}(x)=|x_{N}-\phi_{z}(\tilde{x})|\leq|x_{N}-w_{N}|+|\phi_{z}(\tilde{w})-\phi_{z}(\tilde{x})|\leq|x_{N}-w_{N}|+\mbox{Lip}(D)|\tilde{w}-\tilde{x}|\leq(1+\mbox{Lip}(D))|w-x|.

Since
w
is arbitrary, we get

(3.2)

\frac{V_{z}(x)}{(1+\mbox{Lip}(D))}\leq\delta_{x}\leq V_{z}(x),\quad\forall\ x\in Q_{z}(\rho).

For
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
consider the dyadic decomposition of
K_{\rho}
(by dissecting sides first and then decomposing the cube into smaller cubes and proceeding by induction) into the union of
(N-1)
-dimensional cubes
K^{i}_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq 2^{j(N-1)}
. Now for each
j
,
K^{i}_{j}
’s have
disjoint interiors and have sides-length
\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}
. Set
Q^{i}_{j}:=Q_{z}(K^{i}_{j},\rho)
and define

(3.3)

A_{k}=Q_{z}(K_{\rho},\rho/2^{k})\setminus Q_{z}(K_{\rho},\rho/2^{k+1}),\text{ for
}k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

Then
A_{k}
’s are mutually disjoint sets with
\cup_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}=Q_{z}(\rho)
. Thus the Lipschitz box
Q_{z}(\rho)
is the union of sets
A_{j}\cap Q^{i}_{j}
,
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
whose pairwise intersections are measure zero sets. Moreover using
eqs.

3.3
and
3.2
, for
k\geq j
, we have

(3.4)

\frac{\rho}{2^{k+1}(1+\mbox{Lip}(D))}\leq\delta_{x}\leq\frac{\rho}{2^{k}},\text{ for }x\in A_{k}\cap Q^{i}_{j}

and

(3.5)

\mathcal{L}^{N}(A_{k}\cap Q^{i}_{j})=\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{k}}-\frac{\rho}{2^{k+1}}\right)\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}\right)^{N-1}=\frac{\rho^{N}}{2^{k+1+jN-j}}.

Let
x\in A_{k}\cap Q^{i}_{j}
and
y\in A_{j}\cap Q^{i}_{j}
where
k\geq j
. Then
\frac{\rho}{2^{k+1}}\leq|x_{N}-\phi_{z}(\tilde{x})|\leq\frac{\rho}{2^{k}}
and
\frac{\rho}{2^{j+1}}\leq|y_{N}-\phi_{z}(\tilde{y})|\leq\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}
. Moreover both
x
and
y
lie ‘above’ the same dyadic cube
Q^{i}_{j}
which has side length
\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}
. So for
1\leq\ell\leq N-1
,
|x_{\ell}-y_{\ell}|\leq\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}
. Therefore
|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}|\leq\sqrt{N-1}\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}
. Again, using these

|x_{N}-y_{N}|=|x_{N}-\phi_{z}(\tilde{x})+\phi_{z}(\tilde{x})-\phi_{z}(\tilde{y})+\phi_{z}(\tilde{y})-y_{N}|\leq\frac{\rho}{2^{k}}+\mbox{Lip}(D)|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}|+\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}\\
\leq\frac{\rho}{2^{j-1}}+\mbox{Lip}(D)\sqrt{N-1}\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}.

So we finally get

|x-y|^{2}\leq\left(\sqrt{N-1}\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{j-1}}+\mbox{Lip}(D)\sqrt{N-1}\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}\right)^{2}\\
=\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}\right)^{2}\left(N-1+\left(2+\mbox{Lip}(D)\sqrt{N-1}\right)^{2}\right).

This gives us for
k\geq j
,

(3.6)

|x-y|\leq\frac{\rho}{2^{j}}\sqrt{N-1+(\mbox{Lip}(D)\sqrt{N-1}+2)^{2}}\quad\mbox{ for }x\in A_{k}\cap Q^{i}_{j}\mbox{ and }y\in A_{j}\cap Q^{i}_{j}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\lim\limits_{\lambda\to 0+}\alpha_{s,A}(\lambda)=0

\alpha_{s,A}

is as in
eq.

1.4
. Then there exists a constant
c=c(D,N,A,s)>0
such that for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
we have

\int\limits_{Q_{z}(\rho)}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq c\iint\limits_{Q_{z}(\rho)\times Q_{z}(\rho)}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}.

Proof.

From the hypothesis on
s,A
it follows that there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

2^{2+p}(1+\mbox{Lip}(D))\alpha_{s,A}(2^{s-ms}(1+\mbox{Lip}(D))^{s})<\frac{1}{2}.

Set

l_{1}=2(1+\mbox{Lip}(D))\sqrt{N-1+(\mbox{Lip}(D)\sqrt{N-1}+2)^{2}}\mbox{ and }l_{2}=\frac{1}{2^{m+1}}.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
and
G=G(f,Q_{z}(\rho);l_{1},l_{2})
. Set
E_{1}:=A_{j}\cap Q^{i}_{j}
and
E_{2}:=A_{j+m}\cap Q^{i}_{j}
. For these choices of
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
, from
eqs.

3.5
and
3.4
, we get
\mathcal{L}^{N}(E_{2})=\frac{\rho^{N}}{2^{1+m+jN}}
and
\delta_{x}\leq\frac{\rho}{2^{j+m}}\text{ for }x\in E_{2}
. Also utilizing
eqs.

3.6
and
3.4
,
E_{2}\subseteq B(x,l_{1}\delta_{x})
. Thus the
sets
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
satisfy the hypotheses of
lemma

3.3
. Thus we obtain, using
lemma

3.3
,
eq.

3.4
and the choice of
m
,

\int\limits_{(A_{j}\cap Q^{i}_{j})\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq 2^{1+p+m}\int\limits_{A_{j+m}\cap Q^{i}_{j}}A\left(2^{s-ms}(1+\mbox{Lip}(D))^{s}\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy\\
=2^{2+p}(1+\mbox{Lip}(D))\int\limits_{A_{j+m}\cap Q^{i}_{j}}\frac{A\left(2^{s-ms}(1+\mbox{Lip}(D))^{s}\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)}{2^{1-m}(1+\mbox{Lip}(D))A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy\\
\leq 2^{2+p}(1+\mbox{Lip}(D))\alpha_{s,A}(2^{s-ms}(1+\mbox{Lip}(D))^{s})\int\limits_{A_{j+m}\cap Q^{i}_{j}}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{A_{j+m}\cap Q^{i}_{j}}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy.

Summing over
i=1,\cdots,2^{j(N-1)}
we obtain

(3.7)

\int\limits_{A_{j}\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{A_{j+m}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx.

The lemma follows from
lemma

3.4
.
∎

We can now apply

3.3

to get

(3.9)

\int\limits_{D_{1}\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq c(E_{1},E_{2})\int\limits_{D_{2}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx,

and also from

3.1

.

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
denote
D_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in D\ |\ \mbox{dist}(x,\partial D)<\varepsilon\}
. Let
f_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)

be such that
f_{\varepsilon}\equiv 1
on
D\setminus D_{\varepsilon}
,

0\leq f_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
on
D
and
|\nabla f_{\varepsilon}|<\frac{c_{1}}{\varepsilon}
on
D
for some constant
c_{1}>0
.
Now

\iint\limits_{D\times D}A\left(\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}=\iint\limits_{D_{\varepsilon}\times D_{\varepsilon}}A\left(\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}\\
+2\iint\limits_{D_{\varepsilon}^{c}\times D_{\varepsilon}}A\left(\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}\leq 3\iint\limits_{D\times D_{\varepsilon}}A\left(\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}\\
\leq 3\int\limits_{D_{\varepsilon}}\Bigg\{\int\limits_{y\in D,|x-y|<\varepsilon}A\left(\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N}}\\
+\int\limits_{y\in D,|x-y|>\varepsilon}A\left(\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N}}\Bigg\}dx=3\int\limits_{D_{\varepsilon}}(I_{1}(x)+I_{2}(x))dx.

Using
|\nabla f_{\varepsilon}|<\frac{c_{1}}{\varepsilon}
on
D
, we get

I_{1}(x)=\int\limits_{y\in D,|x-y|<\varepsilon}A\left(\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N}}\leq\int\limits_{|x-y|<\varepsilon}A\left(\frac{c_{1}|x-y|^{1-s}}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N}}\\
=\int\limits_{|y|<\varepsilon}A\left(\frac{c_{1}|y|^{1-s}}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{dy}{|y|^{N}}\leq c\int\limits_{0}^{\varepsilon}A\left(\frac{c_{1}r^{1-s}}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{dr}{r}=c\int\limits_{0}^{c_{1}\varepsilon^{-s}}\frac{A(z)}{z}dz.

The fact that
0\leq f_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq 1
on
D
gives

I_{2}(x)=\int\limits_{y\in D,|x-y|>\varepsilon}A\left(\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N}}\leq\int\limits_{|x-y|>\varepsilon}A(2|x-y|^{-s})\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N}}\\
=c\int\limits_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{A(2r^{-s})}{r}dr\leq c\int\limits_{0}^{2\varepsilon^{-s}}\frac{A(z)}{z}dz.

The hypothesis on
D
that it has bounded Lipschitz boundary implies that
\mathcal{L}^{N}(D_{\varepsilon})
is bounded above as well as bounded bellow by a constant multiple of
\varepsilon
. Let
\frac{1}{c_{2}}:=\max\{c_{1},2\}
. Then we have, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

(3.11)

\iint\limits_{D\times D}A\left(\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}\leq c\mathcal{L}^{N}(D_{\varepsilon})\int\limits_{0}^{(c_{2}\varepsilon)^{-s}}\frac{A(z)}{z}dz\leq c\ (c_{2}\varepsilon)\int\limits_{0}^{(c_{2}\varepsilon)^{-s}}\frac{A(z)}{z}dz\to\beta.

Now
f_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
,
f_{\varepsilon}\to 1
pointwise a.e. in
D
and
A
is continuous. Therefore by Fatou’s lemma,

(3.12)

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int\limits_{D}A\left(\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\geq\int\limits_{D}A\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx>0

and

(3.13)

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int\limits_{D}A(|f_{\varepsilon}(x)|)dx\geq A(1)\mathcal{L}^{N}(D)>0.

Now to prove
(1)
assume
\beta=0
. Then LHS of
eq.

3.11
, which is also the numerator in the definition of both
H_{N,s,A}
and
P^{1}_{N,s,A}
, converges to
0
. We now use
eqs.

3.12
and
3.13
to conclude
H_{N,s,A}(D)=0
and
P^{1}_{N,s,A}(D)=0
respectively. This proves (1).

Now we prove (2). Applying L’hospital rule to
eq.

1.5
, we get
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon A(\varepsilon^{-s})=\beta
. So there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for
n\geq n_{0}
,
A(n^{s})\geq\frac{\beta n}{2}
. Using
eq.

3.12
we have

\lim\limits_{\frac{1}{n}\to 0}\int\limits_{D}A\left(\frac{|f_{\frac{1}{n}}(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\geq\int\limits_{D}A\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\geq\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\int\limits_{D_{\frac{1}{n}}\setminus D_{\frac{1}{n+1}}}A\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\\
\geq\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\int\limits_{D_{\frac{1}{n}}\setminus D_{\frac{1}{n+1}}}A\left(n^{s}\right)dx=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\beta n}{2}\mathcal{L}^{N}\left(D_{\frac{1}{n}}\setminus D_{\frac{1}{n+1}}\right)=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\beta n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{n+1}\right)=\infty.

The proof follows after observing that
\beta>0
in
eq.

3.11
.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
\liminf\limits_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{\frac{1-N}{s}}\alpha_{s,A}(\lambda)=0
. Then there exists a constant
c=c(s,A,N)>0
such that for any
r>0
and any

\int\limits_{B(0,r)^{c}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{|x|^{s}}\right)dx\leq c\iint\limits_{B(0,r)^{c}\times B(0,r)^{c}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dydx}{|x-y|^{N}}.

Proof.

We can assume
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
, because for the sake of proving the above inequality the value of
f
inside
B(0,r)
does not matter. Let
D:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
so that
\delta_{x}=|x|
,
\Omega:=B(0,r)^{c}
,
l_{1}=2^{m+1}
,
l_{2}=\mathcal{L}^{N}(B(0,1))2^{mN}(2^{N}-1)
,
G:=G(f,\Omega;l_{1},l_{2})
and
A_{k}=B(0,2^{k+1}r)\setminus B(0,2^{k}r)

j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, set
E_{1}=A_{j}
and
E_{2}=A_{j+m}
. Then for
x\in E_{1}
,
2^{j}r\leq\delta_{x}\leq 2^{j+1}r
, which gives
\mbox{dist}(x,A_{j+m})\leq 2^{j+m+1}r\leq l_{1}\delta_{x}
. Again
\mathcal{L}^{N}(A_{k})=\mathcal{L}^{N}(B(0,1))2^{kN}r^{N}(2^{N}-1)
. So
E_{1},E_{2}
satisfy the hypotheses of
lemma

3.3
. We can then conclude

\int\limits_{A_{j}\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\frac{2^{p+1}\mathcal{L}^{N}(A_{j})}{\mathcal{L}^{N}(A_{j+m})}\int\limits_{A_{j+m}}A\left(\frac{\sup\{\delta_{x}^{s}\ |\ x\in A_{j+m}\}\;|f(y)|}{\inf\{\delta_{x}^{s}\ |\ x\in A_{j}\}\;\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy\\
=2^{p+1-mN}\int\limits_{A_{j+m}}A\left(\frac{(2^{j+m+1}r)^{s}\;|f(y)|}{(2^{j}r)^{s}\;\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy=2^{p+1-mN}\int\limits_{A_{j+m}}A\left(\frac{2^{(m+1)s}\;|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy\\
=2^{p+1+N}\int\limits_{A_{j+m}}\frac{A\left(\frac{2^{(m+1)s}\;|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)}{2^{(m+1)N}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy\\
=2^{p+1+N}2^{(m+1)(1-N)}\alpha_{s,A}(2^{(m+1)s})\int\limits_{A_{j+m}}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy.

Now we use the hypothesis and choose large enough
m=m(s,A,N)\in\mathbb{N}
, so that

\int\limits_{A_{j}\cap G}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{A_{j+m}}A\left(\frac{|f(y)|}{\delta_{y}^{s}}\right)dy.

Now we can apply
lemma

3.4
to complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

\liminf\limits_{\lambda\to 0+}\lambda^{\frac{1-N}{s}}\alpha_{s,A}(\lambda)=0

c=c(s,A,N)>0

m=m(s,A,N)\in\mathbb{N}

\leq c\left[\ \iint\limits_{B(0,2^{m}r)^{c}\times B(0,2^{m}r)^{c}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dydx}{|x-y|^{N}}\ +\int\limits_{B(0,2^{m}r)\setminus B(0,r)}A\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\delta_{x}^{s}}\right)dx\right].

From the hypothesis on
s
and
A
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}

We fix this
m
. Let

,
\Omega=B(0,r)^{c}
,

,

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an open set and
\lim\limits_{\lambda\to 0}\alpha_{s,A}(\lambda)=0
. Then
P^{1}_{N,s,A}(D)>0

if and only if
BC(D)<\infty
.

Proof.

Suppose
BC(D)<\infty
. There exists a countable number of disjoint open intervals, say
I_{k}
, such that
D=\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}I_{k}
. Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)\setminus\{0\}
.
Using
theorem

1.2
followed by
lemma

2.5
, we get

\iint\limits_{D\times D}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\ \iint\limits_{I_{k}\times I_{k}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|}\\
\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}P^{1}_{1,s,A}(I_{k})\int\limits_{I_{k}}A(|f(x)|)dx\geq\min\{BC(D)^{-sp},1\}P^{1}_{1,s,A}((0,1))\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int\limits_{I_{k}}A(|f(x)|)dx\\
=\min\{BC(D)^{-sp},1\}P^{1}_{1,s,A}((0,1))\int\limits_{D}A(|f(x)|)dx,

which implies
P^{1}_{1,s,A}(D)\geq cP^{1}_{1,s,A}((0,1))>0
.
The other part follows from
proposition

2.1
.
∎

The next lemma is an important change of variable formula for the fractional Orlicz seminorm.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
0<s<1
and
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a measurable set.
Then for any
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
,

2\iint\limits_{D\times D}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}\\
=\int\limits_{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int\limits_{x\in\omega^{\perp}}\int\limits_{\ell\in L_{D}(x,\omega)}\int\limits_{t\in L_{D}(x,\omega)}A\left(\frac{|f(x+\ell\omega)-f(x+t\omega)|}{|\ell-t|^{s}}\right)\frac{dtd\ell d\sigma(x)d\sigma(\omega)}{|\ell-t|},

where
L_{D}(x,\omega):=\{t\ |\ x+t\omega\in D\}
.

Proof.

For a fixed
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
and
x\in D
, consider the change of variable
y=x+z
followed by another change of variable to polar coordinates. We then get

2\iint\limits_{D\times D}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}=2\int\limits_{x\in D}\int\limits_{z\in D-x}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(x+z)|}{|z|^{s}}\right)\frac{dzdx}{|z|^{N}}\\
=2\int\limits_{x\in D}\int\limits_{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int\limits_{r\in\{r>0\ |\ x+r\omega\in D\}}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(x+r\omega)|}{r^{s}}\right)\frac{drd\sigma(\omega)dx}{r}\\
=\int\limits_{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int\limits_{x\in D}\int\limits_{h\in L_{D}(x,\omega)}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(x+h\omega)|}{|h|^{s}}\right)\frac{dhdxd\sigma(\omega)}{|h|}.

Now, for a fixed
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
we can write
x=(x-(x\cdot\omega)\omega)+(x\cdot\omega)\omega
. Using this we can split the
integral over
x\in D
by considering the change of variable

\ell=x\cdot\omega
and
z=x-(x\cdot\omega)\omega
, where
\ell\in L_{D}(z,\omega)
and
z\in\omega^{\perp}
. So we get

2\iint\limits_{D\times D}A\left(\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}\\
=\int\limits_{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int\limits_{z\in\omega^{\perp}}\int\limits_{\ell\in L_{D}(z,\omega)}\int\limits_{h\in L_{D}(x,\omega)}A\left(\frac{|f(z+\ell\omega)-f(z+(\ell+h)\omega)|}{|h|^{s}}\right)\frac{dhd\ell d\sigma(z)d\sigma(\omega)}{|h|}\\
=\int\limits_{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int\limits_{z\in\omega^{\perp}}\int\limits_{\ell\in L_{D}(z,\omega)}\int\limits_{t\in L_{D}(z+\ell\omega,\omega)+\ell}A\left(\frac{|f(z+\ell\omega)-f(z+t\omega)|}{|\ell-t|^{s}}\right)\frac{dhd\ell d\sigma(z)d\sigma(\omega)}{|\ell-t|}.

We conclude the proof of this lemma with the observation that
L_{D}(z,\omega)=L_{D}(z+\ell\omega,\omega)+\ell.

This finishes the proof of the theorem. ∎

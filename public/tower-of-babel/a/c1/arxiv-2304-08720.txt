A conjectural chain model for positive S 1 \displaystyle S^{1} -equivariant symplectic homology of star-shaped toric domains in ℂ 2 \displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{2}
Kei Irie

Abstract
. For any star-shaped toric domain in ℂ 2 \displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{2} , we define a filtered chain complex which conjecturally computes positive S 1 \displaystyle S^{1} -equivariant symplectic homology of the domain. Assuming this conjecture, we show that the limit lim k → ∞ c k GH ​ ( X ) / k \displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}c^{\mathrm{GH}}_{k}(X)/k exists for any star-shaped toric domain X ⊂ ℂ 2 \displaystyle X\subset\mathbb{C}^{2} , where c k GH \displaystyle c^{\mathrm{GH}}_{k} denotes the k \displaystyle k -th Gutt-Hutchings capacity.



1.
Introduction

Let
\displaystyle n
be a positive integer,
and consider
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with a symplectic form
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}dx_{j}dy_{j}
.
A star-shaped domain in
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is a compact subset
\displaystyle X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with a
\displaystyle C^{\infty}
-boundary such that

\displaystyle(0,\ldots,0)
is in the interior of
\displaystyle X
,
and for any
\displaystyle z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}

the half line
\displaystyle\{tz\mid t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\}
intersects
\displaystyle\partial X
transversally at a unique point.

For any such
\displaystyle X
and
\displaystyle-\infty<a<b\leq\infty
, one can define a vector space
\displaystyle\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{*}(X)
called
\displaystyle S^{1}
-equivariant symplectic homology.
It is well-known that
\displaystyle\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[\delta,\infty)}_{*}(X)\cong H^{S^{1}}_{*-(n+1)}(\text{\rm pt})
when
\displaystyle\delta>0
is sufficiently close to
\displaystyle 0
.
On the other hand, this family of vector spaces (with maps between them) has rich quantitative information of
\displaystyle X
.
In particular, one can define the Gutt-Hutchings capacities
\displaystyle(c^{\mathrm{GH}}_{k})_{k\geq 1}

from “positive part” of
\displaystyle S^{1}
-equivariant symplectic homology.
The Gutt-Hutchings capacities were defined in
[
4
]
for Liouville domains.
It is conjectured (
[
4
]
Conjecture 1.9) that
the Gutt-Hutchings capacities coincide with
the
\displaystyle S^{1}
-equivariant Ekeland-Hofer capacities
[
2
]

for compact star-shaped domains in
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.

A star-shaped domain
\displaystyle X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is called a (star-shaped) toric domain if
\displaystyle X
is invariant by the standard
\displaystyle T^{n}
-aciton on
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
When
\displaystyle X
is a so called “convex” or “concave” toric domain,
Gutt-Hutchings
[
4
]
proved explicit formulas to compute capacities
\displaystyle c^{\mathrm{GH}}_{k}(X)
for all
\displaystyle k\geq 1
.
One remarkable consequence of the formulas is that
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{c^{\mathrm{GH}}_{k}(X)}{k}
exists
if
\displaystyle X
is a convex or concave toric domain.
Actually, this existence of the limit holds under a much weaker assumption; see Remark 1.22 of
[
4
]
.
The proof of the formulas in
[
4
]

is “elementary” in the sense that the proof uses only basic properties of the capacities, which are combined in a very clever way.
On the other hand, it is not clear how to generalize the formulas for toric domains which are neither convex nor concave.
Even for convex or concave domains, it is not clear
how to obtain information beyond the capacities, such as barcodes associated to persistent modules defined from
\displaystyle S^{1}
-equivariant symplectic homology.

The aim of this note is to define a filtered chain complex for any
star-shaped toric domain
\displaystyle X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
,
which conjecturally computes
\displaystyle\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{*}(X)
for any
\displaystyle 0<a<b\leq\infty
.
Assuming this conjecture, we show that
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{c^{\mathrm{GH}}_{k}(X)}{k}
exists for any
star-shaped toric domain
\displaystyle X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.

Let us describe the plan of this paper.
In Section 2, we define an
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}
-filtered chain complex
\displaystyle C^{\Omega}_{*}
for any
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}

(see Definition
2.1
below).
For any
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
, we define a star-shaped domain
\displaystyle X_{\Omega}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
,
formulate a conjecture that
\displaystyle C^{\Omega}_{*}
computes
\displaystyle\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{*}(X_{\Omega})
for any
\displaystyle 0<a<b\leq\infty
,
and support this conjecture by some computations.
In Section 3, we define a sequence of capacities
\displaystyle(c_{k}(\Omega))_{k\geq 1}
for any
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
.
Assuming the above conjecture, one has
\displaystyle c_{k}(\Omega)=c^{\mathrm{GH}}_{k}(X_{\Omega})
for any
\displaystyle k\geq 1
and
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
.
We compute the capacities
\displaystyle c_{k}(\Omega)
when
\displaystyle\Omega
is concave or (weakly) convex,
and check that the results are consistent with the formulas in
[
4
]
.
Moreover, we show that
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{c_{k}(\Omega)}{k}
exists for any
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
.

Conventions.

Throughout this paper we consider vector spaces over
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Q}}
unless otherwise specified.
An
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}
-filtration on a vector space
\displaystyle V
is a family of subspaces
\displaystyle(V^{a})_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}
such that
\displaystyle a\leq b\implies V^{a}\subset V^{b}
.
We set
\displaystyle V^{\infty}:=V
and
\displaystyle V^{-\infty}:=0
.
For any
\displaystyle a<b
, we denote
\displaystyle V^{[a,b)}:=V^{b}/V^{a}
.

2.
A chain model

2.1.
Definition of a chain model

Let us start with the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

For any
\displaystyle n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
, let

\displaystyle\Sigma^{n}:=\{v\in({\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})^{n}\mid|v|=1\}
.
Let
\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{n}
denote the set consisting of

\displaystyle\Omega\subset({\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})^{n}
such that
there exists
\displaystyle r_{\Omega}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma^{n},{\mathbb{R}}_{>0})
satisfying

\displaystyle\Omega=\{tz\mid 0\leq t\leq r_{\Omega}(z),\,z\in\Sigma^{n}\}
.
For any
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n}
, let
\displaystyle U_{\Omega}:=({\mathbb{R}}_{>0})^{n}\setminus\Omega
,
and let
\displaystyle\bar{U}_{\Omega}\subset({\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})^{n}
denote the closure of
\displaystyle U_{\Omega}

in
\displaystyle({\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})^{n}
.

In this paper we mostly consider the case
\displaystyle n=2
.
For any
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
, we define a
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space
\displaystyle C^{\Omega}_{*}
by

C^{\Omega}_{*}:=\bigoplus_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus({\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0})^{2}}C^{\text{\rm sing}\,}_{*+1-2(m_{1}+m_{2})}(U_{\Omega})\otimes H_{*}(S^{1}),

where
\displaystyle C^{\text{\rm sing}\,}_{*}
denotes the singular chain complex and
\displaystyle S^{1}:={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Let us define a boundary operator on
\displaystyle C^{\Omega}_{*}
.
Let
\displaystyle e_{0}:=[\text{\rm pt}]\in H_{0}(S^{1})
and
\displaystyle e_{1}:=[\sigma]\in H_{1}(S^{1})
,
where
\displaystyle\sigma:[0,1]\to S^{1};\,t\mapsto[t]
.
For any homogeneous element
\displaystyle x\in C^{\Omega}_{*}
,
let us set

x=\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2},i)\in({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus({\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0})^{2})\times\{0,1\}}x_{m_{1},m_{2},i}\otimes e_{i},

and define
\displaystyle\partial x
by

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\partial x)_{m_{1},m_{2},0}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\partial^{\text{\rm sing}}\,x_{m_{1},m_{2},0},

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\partial x)_{m_{1},m_{2},1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\partial^{\text{\rm sing}}\,x_{m_{1},m_{2},1}+(-1)^{|x|}(m_{2}\cdot x_{m_{1}+1,m_{2},0}-m_{1}\cdot x_{m_{1},m_{2}+1,0}),

where
\displaystyle\partial^{\text{\rm sing}}\,
denotes the boundary operator of the singular chain complex.
One can check
\displaystyle\partial^{2}=0
by direct computations.

Let us define an
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}
-filtration on
\displaystyle C^{\Omega}_{*}
.
For any
\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, define

\displaystyle A_{x_{1},x_{2}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

A_{x_{1},x_{2}}(y_{1},y_{2}):=x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2}.

For any
\displaystyle a\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\displaystyle(m_{1},m_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, let

U_{\Omega}(a:m_{1},m_{2}):=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in U_{\Omega}\mid A_{m_{1},m_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})<a\}.

and

C^{\Omega,a}_{*}:=\bigoplus_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus({\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0})^{2}}C^{\text{\rm sing}\,}_{*+1-2(m_{1}+m_{2})}(U_{\Omega}(a:m_{1},m_{2}))\otimes H_{*}(S^{1}).

Then
\displaystyle(C^{\Omega,a}_{*})_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}
is an
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}
-filtration on
\displaystyle C^{\Omega}_{*}
.
For any
\displaystyle a<b
, we denote
\displaystyle C^{\Omega,[a,b)}_{*}:=C^{\Omega,b}_{*}/C^{\Omega,a}_{*}
.
For any
\displaystyle(m_{1},m_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus({\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0})^{2}
, there holds

(1)

U_{\Omega}(a:m_{1}+1,m_{2}),U_{\Omega}(a:m_{1},m_{2}+1)\subset U_{\Omega}(a:m_{1},m_{2}).

Thus
\displaystyle\partial(C^{\Omega,a}_{*})\subset C^{\Omega,a}_{*-1}
.

If
\displaystyle\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
satisfy
\displaystyle\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}
,
then
\displaystyle U_{\Omega_{2}}\subset U_{\Omega_{1}}
.
Then we obtain a natural chain map

\displaystyle C^{\Omega_{2}}_{*}\to C^{\Omega_{1}}_{*}

which preserves the
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}
-filtrations.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle-\infty<a<b\leq\infty
.
For any
\displaystyle m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, let

F_{m}C^{\Omega,[a,b)}_{*}:=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m_{1},m_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus({\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0})^{2}\\
m_{1}+m_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}C^{\text{\rm sing}\,}_{*+1-2(m_{1}+m_{2})}(U_{\Omega}(b:m_{1},m_{2}),U_{\Omega}(a:m_{1},m_{2}))\otimes H_{*}(S^{1}).

Then
\displaystyle(F_{m}C^{\Omega,[a,b)}_{*})_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a filtration on
\displaystyle C^{\Omega,[a,b)}_{*}
.
Let
\displaystyle(E^{r},\partial_{E^{r}})_{r\geq 1}
be the spectral sequence associated to this filtration.
Then the following holds.

(i):

There exists an isomorphism

E^{1}_{p,q}\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}+m_{2}=p\\
i+j=q-p+1\end{subarray}}H_{i}(U_{\Omega}(b:m_{1},m_{2}),U_{\Omega}(a:m_{1},m_{2}))\otimes H_{j}(S^{1})

such that

\displaystyle\partial_{E^{1}}:E^{1}_{p,q}\to E^{1}_{p-1,q}
is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\partial_{E^{1}}x)_{m_{1},m_{2},0}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\partial_{E^{1}}x)_{m_{1},m_{2},1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(-1)^{|x|}(m_{2}\cdot x_{m_{1}+1,m_{2},0}-m_{1}\cdot x_{m_{1},m_{2}+1,0}).

(ii):

\displaystyle\partial_{E^{r}}=0
if
\displaystyle r\geq 2
.
Moreover
\displaystyle H_{*}(C^{\Omega,[a,b)})\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=*}E^{\infty}_{p,q}
.

Proof
.

(i) is straightforward.
To see (ii), for each
\displaystyle l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
let

\displaystyle\displaystyle C^{l}_{*}:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bigoplus_{m_{1}+m_{2}=l}C_{*+1-2l}^{\text{\rm sing}\,}(U_{\Omega}(b:m_{1},m_{2}),U_{\Omega}(a:m_{1},m_{2}))\otimes{\mathbb{R}}e_{1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\oplus

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bigoplus_{m_{1}+m_{2}=l+1}C_{*-1-2l}^{\text{\rm sing}\,}(U_{\Omega}(b:m_{1},m_{2}),U_{\Omega}(a:m_{1},m_{2}))\otimes{\mathbb{R}}e_{0}.

Then
\displaystyle C^{l}_{*}
is a subcomplex of
\displaystyle C_{*}:=C^{\Omega,[a,b)}_{*}
, and
there holds
\displaystyle C_{*}=\bigoplus_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}}C^{l}_{*}
,
in particular
\displaystyle H_{*}(C)\cong\bigoplus_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}}H_{*}(C^{l})
.
Each
\displaystyle C^{l}
is equipped with a filtration

\displaystyle F^{m}C^{l}:=F^{m}C\cap C^{l}\,(m\in{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Let
\displaystyle(E^{r}(C^{l}))_{r\geq 1}
be the spectral sequence associated to this filtration.
Since
\displaystyle F^{m}C\cong\bigoplus_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}}F^{m}C^{l}
for each
\displaystyle m
,
there holds
\displaystyle E^{r}_{p,q}(C)\cong\bigoplus_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}}E^{r}_{p,q}(C^{l})

for any
\displaystyle r\geq 1
.
Thus it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\partial_{E^{r}(C^{l})}=0\,(r\geq 2)
and

\displaystyle H_{*}(C^{l})\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=*}E^{\infty}_{p,q}(C^{l})

for each
\displaystyle l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
This follows from
\displaystyle F^{l-1}C^{l}=0
and
\displaystyle F^{l+1}C^{l}=C^{l}
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle H_{i}(U_{\Omega}(b:m_{1},m_{2}),U_{\Omega}(a:m_{1},m_{2}))\neq 0
only if
\displaystyle i=0
.
Then
\displaystyle E^{1}_{p,q}\neq 0
only if
\displaystyle q=p
or
\displaystyle q=p-1
. Moreover, for any
\displaystyle j\in{\mathbb{Z}}

E^{1}_{p,p-1+j}\cong\bigoplus_{m_{1}+m_{2}=p}H_{0}(U_{\Omega}(b:m_{1},m_{2}),U_{\Omega}(a:m_{1},m_{2}))\otimes H_{j}(S^{1}).

2.2.
Conjectural relation to
\displaystyle S^{1}
-equivariant symplectic homology

Let
\displaystyle n
be a positive integer, and let

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}dy_{j}-y_{j}dx_{j})\in\Omega^{1}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
.
For any star-shaped domain
\displaystyle X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,

\displaystyle(X,\lambda_{0})
is a Liouville domain.

For any
\displaystyle-\infty<a<b\leq\infty
,
one can define a
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded vector space

\displaystyle\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{*}(X,\lambda_{0})
,
which we abbreviate by
\displaystyle\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{*}(X)
,
called
\displaystyle S^{1}
-equivariant symplectic homology.
The family of vector spaces
\displaystyle(\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{*}(X))_{a,b,X}
is equipped with
the maps (transfer morphisms)

\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{*}(X)\to\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[a^{\prime},b^{\prime})}_{*}(X^{\prime})

for any
\displaystyle(a,b,X)
and
\displaystyle(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},X^{\prime})
such that

\displaystyle a\leq a^{\prime}
,
\displaystyle b\leq b^{\prime}
and
\displaystyle X^{\prime}\subset X
.

Remark 2.4
.

\displaystyle S^{1}
-equivariant symplectic homology was defined by Viterbo
[
7
]
.
Bourgeois-Oancea
[
1
]
gave alternative definitions via family Floer homology
following Seidel
[
6
]
.
Gutt-Hutchings
[
4
]
uses a family Floer homology definition, following the treatment in Gutt
[
3
]
.

For any
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
,

X_{\Omega}:=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\mid(\pi|z_{1}|^{2},\pi|z_{2}|^{2})\in\Omega\}

is a star-shaped domain in
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.
Now we can state the following conjecture.

Conjecture 2.5
.

For any
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
and
\displaystyle 0<a<b\leq\infty
,
one can define an isomorphism of
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded vector spaces

F^{a,b}_{\Omega}:H_{*}(C^{\Omega,[a,b)})\cong\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{*}(X_{\Omega})

so that the diagram

\displaystyle\textstyle{H_{*}(C^{\Omega,[a,b)})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{F^{a,b}_{\Omega}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\cong}
\displaystyle\textstyle{H_{*}(C^{\Omega^{\prime},[a^{\prime},b^{\prime})})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{F^{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}_{\Omega^{\prime}}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\cong}
\displaystyle\textstyle{\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{*}(X_{\Omega})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\displaystyle\textstyle{\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[a^{\prime},b^{\prime})}_{*}(X_{\Omega^{\prime}})}

commutes for any
\displaystyle a,b,\Omega
and
\displaystyle a^{\prime},b^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle a\leq a^{\prime}
,
\displaystyle b\leq b^{\prime}
and
\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
.

Let us briefly explain an idea to obtain the conjectural isomorphism
\displaystyle F^{a,b}_{\Omega}
.
Given
\displaystyle 0<a<b<\infty
such that
\displaystyle a,b\not\in\text{\rm Spec}\,(\Omega)
and a positive integer
\displaystyle N
,
take an autonomous Hamiltonian
\displaystyle H
on
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
so that

\displaystyle\text{\rm SH}\,^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{\leq N}(X_{\Omega})\cong\mathrm{HF}^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{\leq N}(H)

and the following property holds:
every
\displaystyle 1
-periodic orbit
\displaystyle\gamma
of
\displaystyle X_{H}
with

\displaystyle\mathrm{ind}_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\gamma)\leq N

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{H}(\gamma)>0
is contained in
\displaystyle({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\})^{2}
.
Here
\displaystyle\mathrm{ind}_{\mathrm{CZ}}
denotes the Conley-Zehnder index and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{H}
denotes the Hamiltonian action functional.

To compute
\displaystyle\mathrm{HF}^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{\leq N}(H)
we take
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0

and consider an almost complex structure
\displaystyle J^{\varepsilon}
on
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
which satisfies

J^{\varepsilon}(\partial_{\theta_{i}})=-\varepsilon r_{i}\partial_{r_{i}}\qquad(z_{i}=e^{r_{i}+\sqrt{-1}\theta_{i}},\,i=1,2)

on the complement of a neighborhood of
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}\times\{0\}\cup\{0\}\times{\mathbb{C}}
.
Conjecturally,
when
\displaystyle\varepsilon
is sufficiently close to
\displaystyle 0
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{HF}^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{\leq N}(H)

can be computed by counting certain Morse trajectories on
\displaystyle({\mathbb{R}}_{>0})^{2}
,
and one obtains an isomorphism
\displaystyle\mathrm{HF}^{S^{1},[a,b)}_{\leq N}(H)\cong H_{\leq N}(C^{\Omega,[a,b)})
via finite-dimensional Morse theory;
this gives
\displaystyle F^{a,b}_{\Omega}
up to degree
\displaystyle N
.

2.3.
Computations of relative homologies

In this subsection we compute relative homologies
\displaystyle H_{*}(C^{\Omega,[a,b)})
for some special cases,
verifying that Conjecture
2.5
is consistent with known properties of
\displaystyle S^{1}
-equivariant symplectic homology.

We start with some preparations on toric star-shaped domains in
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.
For any
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
, let
us define
\displaystyle\rho_{\Omega}\in C^{\infty}([0,\pi/2],{\mathbb{R}}_{>0})

by
\displaystyle\rho_{\Omega}(\theta):=r_{\Omega}(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
.
In other words,

(2)

\Omega=\{(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\mid 0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2,\,0\leq r\leq\rho_{\Omega}(\theta)\}.

Let us define

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial\Omega

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\{(\rho_{\Omega}(\theta)\cos\theta,\rho_{\Omega}(\theta)\sin\theta)\mid 0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{+}\Omega

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\partial\Omega\cup\{(t,0)\mid t\geq\rho_{\Omega}(0)\}\cup\{(0,t)\mid t\geq\rho_{\Omega}(\pi/2)\}.

For any
\displaystyle c\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\displaystyle(m_{1},m_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus({\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0})^{2}
, let

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{U}_{\Omega}(c:m_{1},m_{2})

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\bar{U}_{\Omega}\mid A_{m_{1},m_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})<c\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{+}\Omega(c:m_{1},m_{2})

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\partial_{+}\Omega\mid A_{m_{1},m_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})<c\}.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
,
\displaystyle c\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
and
\displaystyle(m_{1},m_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus({\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0})^{2}
,

H_{*}(\bar{U}_{\Omega}(c:m_{1},m_{2}),U_{\Omega}(c:m_{1},m_{2}))=H_{*}(\bar{U}_{\Omega}(c:m_{1},m_{2}),\partial_{+}\Omega(c:m_{1},m_{2}))=0.

Proof
.

Let us define

{\mathcal{C}}:=\{\text{critical values of $\displaystyle A_{m_{1},m_{2}}|_{\partial\Omega}$ }\}\cup\{m_{1}\rho(0),m_{2}\rho(\pi/2)\}.

Note that
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}
is a null set.
We may assume
\displaystyle c\not\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
since the case
\displaystyle c\in{\mathcal{C}}
follows from this case by taking limits.
Then
\displaystyle\bar{U}_{\Omega}(c:m_{1},m_{2})
is a manifold with corners,
which implies that

\displaystyle H_{*}(\bar{U}_{\Omega}(c:m_{1},m_{2}),U_{\Omega}(c:m_{1},m_{2}))=0
.
Next we show that

\displaystyle H_{*}(\bar{U}_{\Omega}(c:m_{1},m_{2}),\partial_{+}\Omega(c:m_{1},m_{2}))=0
.
Let

\partial\Omega(c:m_{1},m_{2}):=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\partial\Omega\mid A_{m_{1},m_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})<c\}.

Then it is sufficient to show that the inclusion maps

i:\partial\Omega(c:m_{1},m_{2})\to\partial_{+}\Omega(c:m_{1},m_{2}),\qquad j:\partial\Omega(c:m_{1},m_{2})\to\bar{U}_{\Omega}(c:m_{1},m_{2})

are homotopy equivalent maps.
For any
\displaystyle x\in\bar{U}_{\Omega}(c:m_{1},m_{2})
,
let
\displaystyle\rho(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
be the unique positive real number such that

\displaystyle\rho(x)x\in\partial\Omega
. Then

r:\bar{U}_{\Omega}(c:m_{1},m_{2})\to\partial\Omega(c:m_{1},m_{2});\,x\mapsto\rho(x)\cdot x

is a homotopy inverse of
\displaystyle j
, and
\displaystyle r|_{\partial_{+}\Omega(c:m_{1},m_{2})}
is an inverse of
\displaystyle i
.
∎

For any
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
, let us define

P(\Omega):=\{(\rho_{\Omega}(0),0),(0,\rho_{\Omega}(\pi/2))\}\cup\bigcup_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus({\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0})^{2}}\mathrm{Crit}\,_{+}(A_{m_{1},m_{2}}|_{\partial\Omega}),

where

3.1.

Proposition 3.1

\displaystyle\text{\rm Spec}\,(\Omega)

(iv): Let us define a linear map

\displaystyle\Omega

Proposition 3.4

We may assume that

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\text{\rm nice}}

Let
\displaystyle a:=\max_{1\leq j\leq k}(\min_{p\in\bar{U}_{\Omega}}A_{j,k+1-j}(p))

To prove
\displaystyle c_{k}(\Omega)=a

\displaystyle a-\varepsilon\leq c_{k}(\Omega)\leq a+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\varepsilon>0

Let us prove
\displaystyle c_{k}(\Omega)\leq a+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\min_{p\in\bar{U}_{\Omega}}A_{m_{1},m_{2}}(p)\leq a

\displaystyle q+p\geq 2k+3

\displaystyle H_{2k+1}(C^{\Omega,[\delta,a+\varepsilon)})\to H_{2k+1}(C^{\Omega,[\delta,\infty)})

\displaystyle c_{k}(\Omega)\geq a-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\delta>0

\displaystyle 0
and

E^{1}_{p,p-1+j}\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}+m_{2}=p\\

\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq k

Proposition 3.5
.

If
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}

Proof
.

We may assume that
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\text{\rm nice}}
.
This is because for any weakly convex
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
and for any
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\text{\rm nice}}
which is weakly convex and satisfies

Let
\displaystyle a:=\min_{0\leq j\leq k}(\max_{p\in\Omega}A_{j,k-j}(p))

It is sufficient to show that, for any
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle a-\varepsilon\leq c_{k}(\Omega)\leq a+\varepsilon

Let us prove
\displaystyle c_{k}(\Omega)\leq a+\varepsilon

Take
\displaystyle j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
so that

\displaystyle\max_{p\in\Omega}A_{j,k-j}(p)=a

We fix such
\displaystyle j

There exists
\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
which is concave and

\Omega\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\{p\in({\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})^{2}\mid A_{j,k-j}(p)\leq a+\varepsilon\}.

where the second inequality follows from Proposition

\min_{A_{j,k-j}(p)\geq a+\varepsilon}A_{i,k+1-i}(p)\leq a+\varepsilon

\displaystyle i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}

\displaystyle j=0

\displaystyle\frac{(k+1-i)(a+\varepsilon)}{k}

\displaystyle j=k

\displaystyle\frac{i(a+\varepsilon)}{k}

\displaystyle j=0

\displaystyle j=k

This completes the proof of
\displaystyle c_{k}(\Omega)\leq a+\varepsilon

Let us prove
\displaystyle c_{k}(\Omega)\geq a-\varepsilon

It is sufficient to show that the image of

(4)

H_{2k+1}(C^{\Omega,[\delta,a-\varepsilon)})\to H_{2k+1}(C^{\Omega,[\delta,\infty)})

is zero for any
\displaystyle\delta>0
sufficiently close to
\displaystyle 0
.

Let us first notice that for any
\displaystyle j\in{\mathbb{Z}}

H_{*}(\bar{U}_{\Omega}(a-\varepsilon:j,k+1-j),\bar{U}_{\Omega}(\delta:j,k+1-j))\cong H_{*}(\partial_{+}\Omega(a-\varepsilon:j,k+1-j),\partial_{+}\Omega(\delta:j,k+1-j))

by Lemma
2.6
.
Then we have the following observations:

(a):

\displaystyle H_{*}(\bar{U}_{\Omega}(a-\varepsilon:j,k+1-j),\bar{U}_{\Omega}(\delta:j,k+1-j))=0
unless
\displaystyle*=0
.

(b):

Any element of
\displaystyle H_{0}(\bar{U}_{\Omega}(a-\varepsilon:j,k+1-j),\bar{U}_{\Omega}(\delta:j,k+1-j))

can be written as
\displaystyle[a^{1}p_{1}+a^{2}p_{2}]
with
\displaystyle a^{1},a^{2}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, where

\displaystyle p_{1}:=(\rho(0),0)
and
\displaystyle p_{2}:=(0,\rho(\pi/2))
.

(a) holds since
\displaystyle\Omega
is convex and
\displaystyle a-\varepsilon<a\leq\max_{p\in\Omega}A_{j,k+1-j}(p)
.
(b) holds since
\displaystyle\Omega
is convex.

Now consider the filtration on
\displaystyle C^{\Omega,[\delta,a-\varepsilon)}_{*}
as in Lemma
2.2
.
By (a) and Remark
2.3
,
if

\displaystyle E^{1}_{p,q}\neq 0
and
\displaystyle p+q=2k+1
,
then
\displaystyle p=k+1
,
\displaystyle q=k
.
Moreover there exists a natural isomorphism

E^{1}_{k+1,k}\cong\bigoplus_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}H_{0}(\bar{U}_{\Omega}(a-\varepsilon:j,k+1-j),\bar{U}_{\Omega}(\delta:j,k+1-j))\otimes H_{0}(S^{1}).

Note that we can replace
\displaystyle U_{\Omega}
with
\displaystyle\bar{U}_{\Omega}
due to Lemma
2.6
.

We are going to prove the following claim:

If
\displaystyle x=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}x_{j,k+1-j}\otimes e_{0}\in E^{1}_{k+1,k}
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{E^{1}}(x)=0
, then
\displaystyle x_{j,k+1-j}=0
for any
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq k
.

By (b), for any
\displaystyle j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
there exist
\displaystyle a^{1}_{j},a^{2}_{j}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
such that

x_{j,k+1-j}=[a^{1}_{j}p_{1}+a^{2}_{j}p_{2}]\in H_{0}(\bar{U}_{\Omega}(a-\varepsilon:j,k+1-j),\bar{U}_{\Omega}(\delta:j,k+1-j)).

Let us prove
\displaystyle[a^{1}_{j}p_{1}]=0
for
\displaystyle 0\leq j\leq k
.

\displaystyle[a^{1}_{0}p_{1}]=0
since
\displaystyle[p_{1}]=0
in

\displaystyle H_{0}(\bar{U}_{\Omega}(a-\varepsilon:0,k+1),\bar{U}_{\Omega}(\delta:0,k+1))
.
Then it is sufficient to show that
if
\displaystyle 0\leq j\leq k-1
and
\displaystyle[a^{1}_{j}p_{1}]=0
then
\displaystyle[a^{1}_{j+1}p_{1}]=0
.
This follows from

\displaystyle(\partial_{E^{1}}x)_{j,k-j}=0
,
\displaystyle k-j\neq 0
and the following claim:

\displaystyle[\alpha p_{1}+\beta p_{2}]=0\implies[\alpha p_{1}]=[\beta p_{2}]=0
in

\displaystyle H_{0}(\bar{U}_{\Omega}(a-\varepsilon:j,k-j),\bar{U}_{\Omega}(\delta:j,k-j))
.
This claim holds since for

\displaystyle c\in C^{0}([0,1],\bar{U}_{\Omega})

such that
\displaystyle c(0)=p_{1}
and
\displaystyle c(1)=p_{2}
,
there holds

\max_{t\in[0,1]}A_{j,k-j}(c(t))\geq\max_{p\in\Omega}A_{j,k-j}(p)\geq a>a-\varepsilon.

Now we have proved that
\displaystyle[a^{1}_{j}p_{1}]=0
for any
\displaystyle 0\leq j\leq k
.
By similar arguments, one can prove that
\displaystyle[a^{2}_{j}p_{2}]=0
for any
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq k+1
.
Then, for any
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq k
, we obtain

\displaystyle x_{j,k+1-j}=[a^{1}_{j}p_{1}+a^{2}_{j}p_{2}]=0
.
This finishes the proof of the claim.

Finally, consider the filtration on
\displaystyle C^{\Omega,[\delta,\infty)}_{*}
as in Lemma
2.2
.
As in the proof of Proposition
2.8
(i), there exist natural isomorphisms

E^{1}_{k+1,k}\cong\bigoplus_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}H_{0}(\bar{U}_{\Omega},\bar{U}_{\Omega}(\delta:j,k+1-j))\otimes H_{0}(S^{1}),

and

H_{0}(\bar{U}_{\Omega},\bar{U}_{\Omega}(\delta:j,k+1-j))\cong\begin{cases}H_{0}(\text{\rm pt})&(1\leq j\leq k),\\
0&(\text{otherwise}).\end{cases}

Thus the above claim implies that the image of (
4
) is zero.
This completes the proof of
\displaystyle c_{k}(\Omega)\geq a-\varepsilon
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

For any
\displaystyle\Omega\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{c_{k}(\Omega)}{k}=\max_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega}\min\{x_{1},x_{2}\}.

Proof
.

Let
\displaystyle a:=\max_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega}\min\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
.
It is sufficient to show that, for any
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
there holds

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{c_{k}(\Omega)}{k}\leq a+\varepsilon

and

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{c_{k}(\Omega)}{k}\geq a-\varepsilon
.

There exists
\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
which is concave and

\Omega\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in({\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})^{2}\mid\min\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\leq a+\varepsilon\}.

Then, for any
\displaystyle k\geq 1

c_{k}(\Omega)\leq c_{k}(\Omega^{\prime})\leq(k+1)(a+\varepsilon)

where the second inequality follows from Proposition
3.4
.
Thus
\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{c_{k}(\Omega)}{k}\leq a+\varepsilon
.

There exists

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2})
and
\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}

such that

\displaystyle\min\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\geq a-\varepsilon
,

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega

and
\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}
is weakly convex.
Then

c_{k}(\Omega)\geq c_{k}(\Omega^{\prime})\geq k(a-\varepsilon),

where the second inequality follows from Proposition
3.5
.
Then we obtain
\displaystyle c_{k}(\Omega)\geq k(a-\varepsilon)
for any
\displaystyle k\geq 1
,
which implies

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{c_{k}(\Omega)}{k}\geq a-\varepsilon
.
∎

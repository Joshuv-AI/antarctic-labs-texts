Exact density matrix elements for a driven dissipative system described by a quadratic Hamiltonian
Sh. Saedi, F. Kheirandish*

Abstract
For a prototype quadratic Hamiltonian describing a driven, dissipative system, exact matrix elements of the reduced density matrix are obtained from a generating function in terms of the normal characteristic functions. The approach is based on the Heisenberg equations of motion and operator calculus. The special and limiting cases are discussed.



I
Introduction

Experimental methods in the design of devices used in nanoscale physics and quantum technologies have advanced much in recent years and have led to very high accuracy in measuring instruments. These devices are very sensitive to external potentials and types of noise because their operation is in the domain of quantum mechanics. Therefore, understanding the performance and quantum dynamics of these devices is essential to control them and correct unwanted behaviors. A real quantum device is not an isolated system and interacts with its environment or there may be some external classical sources applied to the system. Our favorite quantum devices belong to a much wider class of quantum systems, nowadays referred to as open quantum systems
F.1
. The subject of open quantum systems (systems that exchange information with their surroundings) covers a vast range of applications in quantum physics and other related subjects. Generally, by an open quantum system, we mean a possibly driven system as the main system interacting with some other systems modeling its environment. In the terminology of open quantum systems, the main system together with its environment under the influence of external classical sources is considered as a closed system. Therefore, the time-evolution of the total system can be described by a total density matrix evolving unitarily. If we are interested in the dynamics of the main system or any other subsystem in the environment, the other degrees of freedom should be traced out to get the favorite reduced density matrix. II
The main definitions

The prototype system that we have considered in this section is a system described by a quadratic Hamiltonian driven by an external classical source
k(t)\,(\bar{k}(t))
interacting with a bosonic bath linearly. The total Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\hat{H}=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\hbar\omega_{0}\,(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+1/2)+\hbar\bar{\phi}\,\hat{a}^{2}+\hbar\phi\,(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{2}}_{\mbox{Quadratic Hamiltonian}\,\hat{H}_{S}(t)}+\underbrace{\hbar\,k(t){\hat{a}^{\dagger}}+\hbar\,\bar{k}(t)\hat{a}}_{\mbox{Interaction with external force}\,k(t)}

(2)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{{\sum_{j}\hbar\omega_{j}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{j}\,{\hat{b}}_{j}}}_{\mbox{Bosonic Bath}\,\hat{H}_{R}}+\underbrace{\sum_{j}\big[\hbar\,f_{j}{\hat{a}^{\dagger}}{\hat{b}}_{j}+\hbar\,\bar{f}_{j}{\hat{b}^{\dagger}}_{j}{\hat{a}}\big]}_{\mbox{Linear interaction}\,\hat{H}_{SR}},

where
f_{j}
are the coupling constants that couple the system to its environment and the parameter
\phi
is an arbitrary complex parameter. Here, the complex conjugate of any quantity such as
c
is denoted by
\bar{c}
and its norm by
|c|
. The Laplace transform of a function is denoted by
\mu(t)
with
\tilde{\mu}(s)=\mathcal{L}[\mu(t)]
with the inverse
\mu(t)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}[\mu(s)]
.

Our goal is to find the exact matrix components of the reduced density matrix corresponding to the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{S}(t)
. To this end, we first need to find the temporal evolution of the ladder operators. By making use of the Heisenberg equations of motion for the subsystem ladder operators we find (appendix
A
)

\hat{a}(t)=\alpha_{1}(t)\hat{a}(0)-2\,i\,\phi\,\alpha_{2}(t)\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}(0)-i\sum_{j}M_{j}(t)\hat{b}_{j}(0)\\
-i\sum_{j}(2\,i\,\phi)\,N_{j}(t)\hat{b}^{{\dagger}}_{j}(0)-i\,\zeta_{1}(t)-i\,(2\,i\,\phi)\zeta_{2}(t),

(3)

\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}(t)=\bar{\alpha}_{1}(t)\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}(0)+2\,i\,\bar{\phi}\,\alpha_{2}(t)\hat{a}(0)+i\sum_{j}\bar{M}_{j}(t)\hat{b}^{{\dagger}}_{j}(0)-i\sum_{j}(2\,i\,\phi)\bar{N}_{j}(t)\hat{b}_{j}(0)+i\,\bar{\zeta}_{1}(t)-i\,(2\,i\,\phi)\bar{\zeta}_{2}(t),

(4)

where for notational simplicity we have defined the following functions

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\Big[\frac{\tilde{\bar{G}}(s)}{\tilde{L}(s)}\Big],

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}(t)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\Big[\frac{1}{\tilde{L}(s)}\Big],

\displaystyle\tilde{L}(s)=

\displaystyle|\tilde{G}(s)|^{2}-4\left|\phi\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(s)=

\displaystyle s+i\,\omega_{0}+\tilde{\chi}(s),

\displaystyle M_{j}(t)=

\displaystyle f_{j}\int_{0}^{t}d\,t^{\prime}e^{i\,\omega_{j}(t-t^{\prime})}\alpha_{1}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle N_{j}(t)=

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{j}\int_{0}^{t}d\,t^{\prime}e^{-i\,\omega_{j}(t-t^{\prime})}\alpha_{2}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}d\,t^{\prime}\,\alpha_{1}(t-t^{\prime})\,k(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}d\,t^{\prime}\,\alpha_{2}(t-t^{\prime})\,\bar{k}(t^{\prime}).

(5)

Similarly, for the environment ladder operators we find (appendix
A
)

\hat{b}_{j}(t)=\sum_{k}\left[\Lambda_{jk}(t)\,\hat{b}_{k}(0)+\Lambda^{\prime}_{jk}(t)\,\hat{b}^{{\dagger}}_{k}(0)-\Gamma_{jk}(t)\,\hat{a}(0)-\Gamma^{\prime}_{jk}(t)\,\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}(0)-\Omega_{jk}(t)\right],

(6)

\hat{b}^{{\dagger}}_{j}(t)=\sum_{k}\left[\bar{\Lambda}_{jk}(t)\,\hat{b}^{{\dagger}}_{k}(0)+\bar{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{jk}(t)\,\hat{b}_{k}(0)-\bar{\Gamma}_{jk}(t)\,\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}(0)-\bar{\Gamma^{\prime}}_{jk}(t)\,\hat{a}(0)-\bar{\Omega}_{jk}(t)\right],

(7)

where we have defined

\displaystyle\Lambda_{jk}(t)=

\displaystyle e^{-i\,\omega_{j}\,t}\delta_{jk}-\bar{f}_{j}\int_{0}^{t}d\,t^{\prime}e^{i\,\omega_{j}(t-t^{\prime})}M_{k}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}_{jk}(t)=

\displaystyle-\bar{f}_{j}\int_{0}^{t}d\,t^{\prime}e^{i\,\omega_{j}(t-t^{\prime})}(2\,i\,\phi)N_{k}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{jk}(t)=

\displaystyle-i\,\bar{f}_{j}\int_{0}^{t}d\,t^{\prime}e^{i\,\omega_{j}(t-t^{\prime})}\alpha_{1k}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\prime}_{jk}(t)=

\displaystyle-2\,\phi\bar{f}_{j}\int_{0}^{t}d\,t^{\prime}e^{i\,\omega_{j}(t-t^{\prime})}\alpha_{2k}(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\Omega_{jk}(t)=

\displaystyle-\bar{f}_{j}\int_{0}^{t}d\,t^{\prime}e^{i\,\omega_{j}(t-t^{\prime})}\left(\zeta_{1}(t^{\prime})-(2\,i\,\phi)\zeta_{2}(t^{\prime})\right).

(8)

III
Reduced density matrix elements

According to the terminology of the open quantum systems theory, the whole system described by the Hamiltonian Eq. (
2
) is a closed system having a unitary time-evolution given by

\hat{\rho}(t)=\hat{U}(t)\,\hat{\rho}(0)\,\hat{U}^{{\dagger}}(t),

(9)

where the initial density matrix of the whole system (
\hat{\rho}(0)
) is usually assumed to be a separable state

\hat{\rho}(0)=\hat{\rho}_{S}(0)\otimes\hat{\rho}_{R}(0).

(10)

The reduced density matrix of the bosonic-mode subsystem can be obtained by tracing out the degrees of freedom of the environment

\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)=\mbox{Tr}_{R}\left\{\hat{\rho}(t)\right\}.

(11)

We are interested in the matrix elements of the reduced density matrix. We have

\displaystyle\langle\,n\,|\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)|\,m\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\,n\,|Tr_{R}\left\{\hat{U}(t)\hat{\rho}(t_{0})\hat{U}^{{\dagger}}(t)\right\}|\,m\,\rangle,

\displaystyle=\left(|\,m\,\rangle\langle\,n\,|Tr_{R}\left\{\hat{U}(t)\hat{\rho}(t_{0})\hat{U}^{{\dagger}}(t)\right\}\right),

\displaystyle=Tr\left\{\left(|\,m\,\rangle\langle\,n\,|\otimes\,\hat{I}_{R}\right)\hat{U}(t)\hat{\rho}(t_{0})\hat{U}^{{\dagger}}(t)\right\},

\displaystyle=Tr\left\{\overbrace{\hat{U}^{{\dagger}}(t)\left(|\,m\,\rangle\langle\,n\,|\otimes\,\hat{I}_{R}\right)\hat{U}(t)}^{\hat{Q}_{nm}}\hat{\rho}(t_{0})\right\},

(12)

therefore,

\displaystyle\langle\,n\,|\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)|\,m\,\rangle

\displaystyle=Tr\left\{\hat{Q}_{nm}\,\hat{\rho}(0)\right\}

\displaystyle=\mbox{Tr}\left\{\hat{Q}_{nm}\,\hat{\rho}_{S}(0)\otimes\hat{\rho}_{R}(0)\right\}.

(13)

In Eq. (
III
), the operator
\hat{I}_{R}
is the identity operator over the environment Hilbert space. The matrix elements
\hat{Q}_{mn}
can be written in terms of the ladder operators in the Heisenberg representation (appendix
B
)

\hat{Q}_{nm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!\,n!}}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{s}}{s!}\left(\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}(t)\right)^{m+s}\left(\hat{a}(t)\right)^{n+s},

(14)

where

\displaystyle\hat{a}(t)=

\displaystyle\hat{C}(t)-i\left(\hat{B}(t)+\zeta(t)\right),

\displaystyle\hat{C}(t)=

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(t)\hat{a}(0)-2\,i\,\phi\,\alpha_{2}(t)\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}(0),

\displaystyle\hat{B}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\left(M_{j}(t)\hat{b}_{j}(0)+2\,i\,\phi\,N_{j}(t)\hat{b}^{{\dagger}}_{j}(0)\right),

\displaystyle\zeta(t)=

\displaystyle\left(\zeta_{1}(t)+2\,i\,\phi\,\zeta_{2}(t)\right),

(15)

and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}(t)
can be obtained by taking the hermitian conjugation of the relations defined in Eq. (
III
). By inserting the expressions for
\hat{a}(t)
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}(t)
into Eq. (
14
), one easily finds

\hat{Q}_{nm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!\,n!}}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{s}}{s!}\mbox{Tr}\left\{\left(\hat{C}^{{\dagger}}(t)+i\left(\hat{B}^{{\dagger}}(t)+\bar{\zeta}(t)\right)\right)^{m+s}\left(\hat{C}(t)-i\left(\hat{B}(t)+\zeta(t)\right)\right)^{n+s}\right\}.

(16)

Now by making use of Eq. (
III
) we have

\displaystyle\langle\,n\,|\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)|\,m\,\rangle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\sqrt{m!\,n!}}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s!}\frac{\partial^{m+s}}{\partial\lambda^{m+s}}\frac{\partial^{n+s}}{\partial(\bar{\lambda})^{n+s}}\mbox{Tr}\left\{e^{\lambda\left(\hat{C}^{{\dagger}}(t)+i\left(\hat{B}^{{\dagger}}(t)+\bar{\zeta}(t)\right)\right)}e^{-\bar{\lambda}\left(\hat{C}(t)-i\left(\hat{B}(t)+\zeta(t)\right)\right)}\hat{\rho}_{S}(0)\otimes\hat{\rho}_{R}(0)\right\}_{\lambda=\bar{\lambda}=0}.

(17)

From the definitions of operators
\hat{B}
and
\hat{C}
, we observe that

[\hat{B},\hat{C}]=[\hat{B},\hat{C}^{{\dagger}}]=0,

(18)

so we can rewrite Eq. (
17
) as

\displaystyle\langle\,n\,|\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)|\,m\,\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\sqrt{m!\,n!}}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s!}\frac{\partial^{m+s}}{\partial\lambda^{m+s}}\frac{\partial^{n+s}}{\partial(\bar{\lambda})^{n+s}}\,\Bigg[e^{i\,\lambda\,\bar{\zeta}(t)+i\,\bar{\lambda}\zeta(t)}\overbrace{\mbox{Tr}_{S}\left\{e^{\lambda\hat{C}^{{\dagger}}(t)}e^{-\bar{\lambda}\hat{C}(t)}\hat{\rho}_{S}(0)\right\}}^{I_{\hat{C}}}\overbrace{\mbox{Tr}_{R}\left\{e^{i\,\lambda\hat{B}^{{\dagger}}(t)}e^{i\,\bar{\lambda}\hat{B}(t)}\hat{\rho}_{R}(0)\right\}}^{I_{\hat{B}}}\Bigg]_{\lambda=\bar{\lambda}=0},

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\sqrt{m!\,n!}}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial\lambda^{m}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial(\bar{\lambda})^{n}}\,e^{\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{\bar{\lambda}}}\Big[e^{i\,\lambda\,\bar{\zeta}(t)+i\,\bar{\lambda}\zeta(t)}I_{\hat{C}}\,I_{\hat{B}}\Big]_{\lambda=\bar{\lambda}=0}.

(19)

Eq. (
III
) is a general result giving the components of the reduced density matrix in terms of a generating function. Note that
I_{\hat{C}}
and
I_{\hat{B}}
are normal characteristic functions in the terminology of quantum optics. To proceed, let us assume that the initial state of the environment is a thermal state with temperature
T

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{R}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{R}}\prod_{j}\,e^{-\beta\hbar\omega_{j}\hat{b}^{{\dagger}}_{j}\hat{b}_{j}},

\displaystyle Z_{R}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j}z_{j},

\displaystyle z_{j}

\displaystyle=\mbox{Tr}_{j}\left\{e^{-\beta\hbar\omega_{j}\hat{b}^{{\dagger}}_{j}\hat{b}_{j}}\right\},

(20)

where
\beta=1/\kappa_{B}T
and
\kappa_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant. Also,
\mbox{Tr}_{j}
denotes the trace over the base vectors corresponding to the
j
th oscillator of the environment. One can obtain
I_{\hat{B}}
easily as (appendix
C
)

\mbox{Tr}_{R}\left\{e^{i\,\lambda\,\hat{B}^{{\dagger}}(t)}e^{i\,\bar{\lambda}\,\hat{B}(t)}{\hat{\rho}}_{R}(0)\right\}=e^{\vartheta[\lambda,\bar{\lambda},t]},

(21)

where

\displaystyle\vartheta[\lambda,\bar{\lambda},t]=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k}\Big[-\lambda\,\bar{\lambda}\Big(\frac{|V_{k}(t)|^{2}}{e^{\beta\hbar\,\omega_{k}}-1}+4|\phi|^{2}\,|N_{k}(t)|^{2}\Big)+i(\lambda^{2}\,\bar{\phi}\bar{N}_{k}(t)\bar{M}_{k}(t)-\bar{\lambda}^{2}\phi N_{k}(t)M_{k}(t))\Big],

(22)

\displaystyle V_{k}(t)=

\displaystyle M_{k}(t)+2\,i\,\bar{\phi}\,\bar{N}_{k}(t),

(23)

therefore,

\displaystyle\langle\,n\,|\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)|\,m\,\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\sqrt{m!\,n!}}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s!}\frac{\partial^{m+s}}{\partial\lambda^{m+s}}\frac{\partial^{n+s}}{\partial(\bar{\lambda})^{n+s}}\bigg[e^{i\,\lambda\,\bar{\zeta}(t)+i\,\bar{\lambda}\zeta(t)}e^{\vartheta[\lambda,\bar{\lambda},t]}\,\mbox{Tr}_{S}\left\{e^{\lambda\hat{C}^{{\dagger}}(t)}e^{-\bar{\lambda}\hat{C}(t)}\hat{\rho}_{S}(0)\right\}\bigg]_{\lambda=\bar{\lambda}=0},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\sqrt{m!\,n!}}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial\lambda^{m}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial(\bar{\lambda})^{n}}\,e^{\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{\bar{\lambda}}}\bigg[e^{i\,\lambda\,\bar{\zeta}(t)+i\,\bar{\lambda}\zeta(t)}e^{\vartheta[\lambda,\bar{\lambda},t]}\,\mbox{Tr}_{S}\left\{e^{\lambda\hat{C}^{{\dagger}}(t)}e^{-\bar{\lambda}\hat{C}(t)}\hat{\rho}_{S}(0)\right\}\bigg]_{\lambda=\bar{\lambda}=0}.

(24)

Eq. (
III
) is our main result, giving the reduced density matrix elements using a generating function. In the next section, as an application of the main result, we assume that the bosonic mode is initially prepared in a coherent state.

IV
The system is initially prepared in a coherent state

As an application of the general formula Eq. (
III
), let us assume that the initial state of the main system is a coherent state

\hat{\rho}_{S}(0)=|\gamma\rangle\langle\gamma|,

(25)

in this case, the normal characteristic function
I_{\hat{C}}
can be obtained as (appendix
D
)

\displaystyle\mbox{Tr}_{S}\left\{e^{\lambda\hat{C}^{{\dagger}}(t)}e^{-\bar{\lambda}\hat{C}(t)}\hat{\rho}_{S}(0)\right\}=e^{\alpha_{2}(t)\left(i\,\lambda^{2}\bar{\phi}\bar{\alpha}_{1}(t)-i\,\bar{\lambda}^{2}\phi\alpha_{1}(t)-4\lambda\bar{\lambda}|\phi|^{2}\alpha_{2}(t)\right)}e^{\bar{\gamma}\left(\lambda\bar{\alpha}_{1}(t)+2\,i\,\bar{\lambda}\phi\alpha_{2}(t)\right)}e^{-\gamma\left(\bar{\lambda}\alpha_{1}(t)-2\,i\,\lambda\bar{\phi}\alpha_{2}(t)\right)},

(26)

and Eq. (
III
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\langle\,n\,|\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)|\,m\,\rangle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\sqrt{m!\,n!}}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial\lambda^{m}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial(\bar{\lambda})^{n}}\,e^{\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{\bar{\lambda}}}

\displaystyle\left[e^{i\left(\lambda\bar{\zeta}(t)+\bar{\lambda}\zeta(t)\right)}e^{-4\lambda\bar{\lambda}|\phi|^{2}\alpha_{2}^{2}(t)+i\alpha_{2}(t)\left(\lambda^{2}\bar{\phi}\bar{\alpha}_{1}(t)-\bar{\lambda}^{2}\phi\alpha_{1}(t)\right)}e^{\vartheta[\lambda,\bar{\lambda},t]}e^{\left(\lambda\bar{\gamma}\bar{\alpha}_{1}(t)-\bar{\lambda}\gamma\alpha_{1}(t)\right)+2\,i\,\alpha_{2}(t)\left(\bar{\lambda}\bar{\gamma}\phi+\lambda\,\gamma\,\bar{\phi}\right)}\right]_{\lambda=\bar{\lambda}=0}.

(27)

Therefore, if the initial state of the main system is a coherent state and the initial state of the environment is a Maxwell Boltzmann thermal state then the elements of the reduced density matrix can be obtained from a generating function given by Eq. (
IV
). If we set (
\phi=0
), the diagonal elements of the reduced density matrix
P_{n}(t)=\langle\,n\,|\hat{\rho}_{S}(t)|\,n\,\rangle
are

P_{n}(t)|_{\phi=0}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\lambda}}\Big)^{n}\overbrace{\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s!}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\lambda}}\Big)^{s}}^{e^{\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{\bar{\lambda}}}}I\Bigg|_{\lambda=\bar{\lambda}=0},

(28)

where

\displaystyle I=

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda\bar{\lambda}\eta(t)+\lambda\,\bar{Z}-\bar{\lambda}\,Z},

\displaystyle\eta(t)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k}\frac{|M_{k}(t)|^{2}}{e^{\beta\hbar\,\omega_{k}}-1},

\displaystyle Z=

\displaystyle-i\zeta(t)+\gamma\alpha_{1}(t),

\displaystyle\bar{Z}=

\displaystyle i\bar{\zeta}(t)+\bar{\gamma}\bar{\alpha}_{1}(t).

(29)

Therefore, (appendix
E
)

P_{n}(t)|_{\phi=0}=\frac{e^{-\frac{\left|Z\right|^{2}}{1+\eta(t)}}}{1+\eta(t)}\left(\frac{\eta(t)}{\eta(t)+1}\right)^{n}L_{n}\left(\frac{-|Z|^{2}}{\eta(t)(1+\eta(t))}\right),

(30)

where
L_{n}[x]
is a Laguerre polynomial of degree
n
. From Eq. (
30
) the mean excitation number
\bar{n}
at temperature
T
and time
t
is

\bar{n}_{T}(t)=\langle n\rangle_{T}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n\,P_{n}(t)=|Z(t)|^{2}+\eta(t),

(31)

at zero temperature we have
\eta(t)\rightarrow 0
, so
|Z(t)|^{2}=\bar{n}_{0}(t)
, therefore,
\bar{n}_{T}(t)-\bar{n}_{0}(t)=\eta(t)
.
If we set (
\phi=0
) then in low temperature regime (
T\rightarrow 0
), we have

\displaystyle\eta(t)\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle V_{k}(t)=M_{k}(t),

\displaystyle\tilde{L}(s)=\left|\tilde{G}(s)\right|^{2}\Longrightarrow\alpha_{1}(t)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{\tilde{G}(s)}\right],

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(s)=s+i\,\omega_{0}+\tilde{\chi}(s).

(32)

and by making use of the identity

\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\,\left(\frac{\eta(t)}{\eta(t)+1}\right)^{n}L_{n}\left(\frac{-|Z|^{2}}{\eta(t)(\eta(t)+1)}\right)=\frac{|Z|^{2n}}{n!},

(33)

we deduce

P_{n}(t)|_{\phi=0}=\frac{e^{-|Z|^{2}}|Z|^{2n}}{n!}=\frac{|\gamma\alpha_{1}(t)-i\zeta_{1}(t)|^{2n}e^{-|\gamma\alpha_{1}(t)-i\zeta_{1}(t)|^{2}}}{n!},

(34)

which is a Poisson distribution with the mean number parameter
\langle\,n\,\rangle
given by

\left\langle\,n\,\right\rangle=|\gamma\alpha_{1}(t)-i\zeta_{1}(t)|^{2}.

(35)

IV.1
Example

For the choice
\phi_{I}=0
,
k(t)=k_{0}\sin(\nu t)
and the memory-less response function
\chi(t)=\chi_{0}\,\delta(t)
, we find in the large-time limit (
t\gg\chi_{0}^{-1}
) the following time-independent values

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(t)\mapsto 0,

\displaystyle|Z(t)|^{2}_{\phi=0}=|\zeta_{1}(t)|^{2}\mapsto\frac{k_{0}^{2}[8\nu^{2}+2(\chi_{0}^{2}+4\omega_{0}^{2})]}{(4\nu^{2}+\chi_{0}^{2}-4\omega_{0}^{2})^{2}+16\chi_{0}^{2}\,\omega_{0}^{2}},

\displaystyle\eta(t)\mapsto\sum_{k}\frac{4|f_{k}|^{2}}{\chi_{0}^{2}+4(\omega_{0}+\omega_{k})^{2}}\,\frac{1}{e^{\frac{\hbar\omega_{k}}{k_{B}T}-1}}.

Note that
\eta
is a temperature-dependent parameter. The probability
P_{n}(\eta)

VI
Conclusion

We have considered a driven, dissipative quantum system described by a time-dependent quadratic Hamiltonian and found a generating function Eq. (
III
) to find the exact matrix elements of the reduced density matrix. The generating function is given in terms of the well-known normal characteristic functions in the terminology of quantum Optics. For the case of a thermal environment with a Maxwell-Boltzmann equilibrium state, an exact expression for the components of the reduced density matrix is obtained given by Eq. (
III
). Explicit expressions for reduced density matrix components are obtained when the subsystem is initially prepared in a coherent state. Despite the simplicity of the method, while deriving the main result Eq. (
III
), assumptions like weak or strong coupling and/or Markovian approximation have not been applied.

(84)

\displaystyle e^{(\frac{-\eta(t)u^{2}+(\bar{Z}-Z)u}{1+\eta(t)})}=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}H_{p}\Big(\frac{\bar{Z}-Z}{2\sqrt{\eta(t)(\eta(t)+1)}}\Big)\frac{\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{\eta(t)}{\eta(t)+1}}\,u\bigg)^{p}}{p!},

\displaystyle e^{(\frac{\eta(t)v^{2}-i(\bar{Z}+Z)v}{1-\eta(t)})}=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}H_{q}\Big(\frac{-i(Z+\bar{Z})}{2\sqrt{\eta(t)(\eta(t)+1)}}\Big)\frac{\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{\eta(t)}{\eta(t)+1}}\,v\bigg)^{q}}{q!},

(85)

so

\displaystyle\nabla^{2\,n}\left(e^{\frac{-\eta(t)u^{2}+\left(\bar{Z}-Z\right)u}{1+\eta(t)}}e^{\frac{-\eta(t)v^{2}-i\left(\bar{Z}+Z\right)v}{1+\eta(t)}}\right)\Big|_{u=v=0}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\left[\partial_{u}^{2(n-k)}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}H_{p}\left(\frac{\bar{Z}-Z}{2\sqrt{\eta(t)(\eta(t)+1)}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{\eta(t)}{\eta(t)+1}}\right)^{p}\frac{u^{p}}{p!}\right]_{u=0}

\displaystyle P_{n}(t)|_{\phi=0}

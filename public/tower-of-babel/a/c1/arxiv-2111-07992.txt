Query and Depth Upper Bounds for Quantum Unitaries via Grover Search
Gregory Rosenthal

Abstract
We prove that any n n -qubit unitary can be implemented (i) approximately in time OPEN O ~ ​ ( 2 n / 2 CLOSE ) \tilde{O}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2^{n/2}}}\right) with query access to an appropriate classical oracle, and also (ii) exactly by a circuit of depth OPEN O ~ ​ ( 2 n / 2 CLOSE ) \tilde{O}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2^{n/2}}}\right) with one- and two-qubit gates and 2 O ⁡ ( n ) 2^{O(n)} ancillae. The proofs involve similar reductions to Grover search. The proof of (ii) also involves a linear-depth construction of arbitrary quantum states using one- and two-qubit gates (in fact, this can be improved to constant depth with the addition of fanout and generalized Toffoli gates) which may be of independent interest. We also prove a matching OPEN Ω ​ ( 2 n / 2 CLOSE ) \Omega\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2^{n/2}}}\right) lower bound for (i) and (ii) for a certain class of implementations.

Introduction

This paper addresses two seemingly disparate questions in quantum circuit complexity via a common proof technique. The first of these questions is as follows:

Question 1.1

(The unitary synthesis problem
[
2
,
3
]
)
.

Is there a polynomial-time quantum algorithm
A
such that for every unitary
U
, there exists a classical oracle
f
such that
A^{f}
approximately implements
U
?

By

we mean

with query access to the boolean function
f
. Note that

[
3

and named the “unitary synthesis problem” by Aaronson

2

, which is interesting because more is known about boolean function complexity than is known about quantum circuit complexity. Aaronson
[

discusses this motivation in the context of certain physically motivated unitaries.

We also consider the following question:

Question 1.2
.

Given

, what is the minimum circuit depth required to exactly implement a worst-case

-qubit unitary using one- and two-qubit gates?

The depth of a circuit is the number of layers of gates in it. Circuit depth corresponds to parallel computation time, and quantum circuits of higher depth are believed to be more difficult to physically implement.

We prove

Upper bound for the unitary synthesis problem

2.1
Upper bound

We will use a variant of Grover search that finds the marked string with certainty rather than just with high probability:

Lemma 2.1
.

There is a uniform sequence of
\mathsf{QAC_{f}}
circuits
(G_{n})_{n}
—each of depth
O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2^{n/2}}}\right)
, making
O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2^{n/2}}}\right)
queries, and acting on
n+1
qubits—such that for all
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
it holds that
G_{n}^{I-2|x,1\rangle\!\langle x,1|}|0^{n+1}\rangle=|x,0\rangle
.

Imre and Balázs
[
12
]
survey several proofs of the query upper bound from
2.1
in detail, and below we include an alternate proof of
2.1
due to Wiebe
[
22
]
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
denote the marked string that we wish to find, and let

\displaystyle t=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lceil\frac{\pi}{4}2^{n/2}}}\right\rceil,

\displaystyle\theta=\frac{\pi/2}{2t+1},

\displaystyle p=2^{n}\sin^{2}\theta.

Since
p\leq 2^{n}\theta^{2}\leq 2^{n}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\pi/2}{2t}}}\right)^{2}\leq 1
we can define states

\displaystyle|\psi_{0}\rangle=|+^{n}\rangle\otimes\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sqrt{1-p}|0\rangle+\sqrt{p}|1\rangle}}\right),\qquad|\psi_{t}\rangle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((2|\psi_{0}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{0}|-I)(I-2|x,1\rangle\!\langle x,1|)}}\right)^{t}|\psi_{0}\rangle,

and since
\langle x,1|\psi_{0}\rangle=2^{-n/2}\sqrt{p}=\sin\theta
we may write
|\psi_{0}\rangle=\cos\theta|\alpha\rangle+\sin\theta|\beta\rangle
where
|\beta\rangle=|x,1\rangle
and
|\alpha\rangle
is a superposition of standard basis states besides
|x,1\rangle
. By reasoning similar to that in the proof of correctness of Grover’s algorithm
[
5
,
17
]
it follows that

|\psi_{t}\rangle=\cos((2t+1)\theta)|\alpha\rangle+\sin((2t+1)\theta)|\beta\rangle=\cos(\pi/2)|\alpha\rangle+\sin(\pi/2)|\beta\rangle=|x,1\rangle.\qed

Now we reduce the task of implementing a unitary
U
to that of implementing a

Theorem 2.2

(formal version of
1.4
)

There is a uniform family of
\mathsf{QAC_{f}}

(C_{n,m})_{n,m}
for

—each of depth

, making
O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2^{n/2}}}\right)
queries to an

-qubit quantum oracle, and acting on

qubits—such that for all
n
-qubit unitaries

and all
m
-qubit

-qRAMs

C_{n,m}^{A}(I_{n}\otimes|0\dotsc 0\rangle)=U\otimes|0\dotsc 0\rangle
.

Proof.

By linearity we may assume that the input is a string
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
; our goal is to output
U|x\rangle\otimes|0\dotsc 0\rangle
. First apply
A
, yielding
|x\rangle\otimes U|x\rangle\otimes|0\dotsc 0\rangle
. The challenge now is to uncompute
|x\rangle
.

Let

C=(A\otimes I_{1})\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(I_{n}\otimes\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(I_{m-n+1}-2|0^{m-n},1\rangle\!\langle 0^{m-n},1|}}\right)}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A^{\dagger}\otimes I_{1}}}\right),

and observe that
C
can be implemented by a
\mathsf{QAC_{f}^{0}}
circuit making two queries. By the definition of
A
we have that

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=(A\otimes I_{1})\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(I_{m+1}-2\sum_{\mathclap{y\in\{0,1\}^{n}}}|y\rangle\!\langle y|\otimes|0^{m-n}\rangle\!\langle 0^{m-n}|\otimes|1\rangle\!\langle 1|}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A^{\dagger}\otimes I_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=I_{m+1}-2\sum_{\mathclap{y\in\{0,1\}^{n}}}|y\rangle\!\langle y|\otimes U|y\rangle\!\langle y|U^{\dagger}\otimes|0^{m-2n}\rangle\!\langle 0^{m-2n}|\otimes|1\rangle\!\langle 1|

\displaystyle=I_{m+1}-2\sum_{\mathclap{y\in\{0,1\}^{n}}}|y,1\rangle\!\langle y,1|\otimes U|y\rangle\!\langle y|U^{\dagger}\otimes|0^{m-2n}\rangle\!\langle 0^{m-2n}|,

where in the last line we reorder the qubits for future convenience. Therefore

C\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(I_{n+1}\otimes U|x\rangle\otimes|0^{m-2n}\rangle}}\right)=(I_{n+1}-2|x,1\rangle\!\langle x,1|)\otimes U|x\rangle\otimes|0^{m-2n}\rangle,

so using our copy of
U|x\rangle
, the circuit
C
can implement the reflection
I_{n+1}-2|x,1\rangle\!\langle x,1|
in a disjoint register without disturbing the copy of
U|x\rangle
.

We could therefore simulate the circuit
G_{n}
from
2.1
, with queries to
I-2|x,1\rangle\!\langle x,1|
answered in this manner, to construct a copy of
|x\rangle
. Instead perform this simulation
in reverse
, to
uncompute
the existing copy of
|x\rangle
while preserving the copy of
U|x\rangle

bits of
f(x)
, and the disjunction indicates whether the satisfying
t
is such that
t_{j}=1
.) Therefore the unitary
U_{f}
defined by

U_{f}|x,a\rangle=|x,a\oplus f(x)\rangle\qquad\text{for $x\in\{0,1\}^{\{0,1\}^{<n}},a\in\{0,1\}^{n}$}

can be computed by a
\mathsf{QAC_{f}^{0}}
circuit on
\tilde{O}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2^{n}}}\right)
qubits.

Let
(\mathsf{R}_{x})_{x\in\{0,1\}^{<n}}
be one-qubit registers and let
\mathsf{S}
be an
n
-qubit register. The first step toward constructing
|\psi\rangle
is to construct the state

U_{f}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\bigotimes_{\mathrlap{x\in\{0,1\}^{<n}}}|\phi_{x}\rangle_{\mathsf{R}_{x}}\otimes|0^{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{S}}}}\right),

using a layer of one-qubit gates followed by the aforementioned circuit for
U_{f}
. Here, when computing
U_{f}
, the
x
’th input bit to
f
is in
\mathsf{R}_{x}
for all
x
, and the output register of
f
is
\mathsf{S}
. Observe that

\displaystyle U_{f}(I\otimes|0^{n}\rangle)=\sum_{\mathclap{x\in\{0,1\}^{\{0,1\}^{<n}}}}|x\rangle\!\langle x|\otimes|f(x)\rangle=\sum_{\mathclap{t\in\{0,1\}^{n}}}\,\,\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{\mathrlap{x\in f^{-1}(t)}}|x\rangle\!\langle x|}}\right)\otimes|t\rangle=\sum_{\mathclap{t\in\{0,1\}^{n}}}\,\,\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}|t_{i}\rangle\!\langle t_{i}|_{\mathsf{R}_{t_{<i}}}}}\right)\otimes|t\rangle_{\mathsf{S}},

where the
t
’th tensor product above implicitly acts as the identity on all
\mathsf{R}_{x}
for which
x
does not equal
t_{<i}
for any
i
. Therefore

U_{f}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\bigotimes_{\mathrlap{x\in\{0,1\}^{<n}}}|\phi_{x}\rangle_{\mathsf{R}_{x}}\otimes|0^{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{S}}}}\right)=\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\bigotimes_{x\in\{0,1\}^{<n}}\begin{cases}|t_{i}\rangle\langle t_{i}|\phi_{t_{<i}}\rangle_{\mathsf{R}_{x}}&\text{if $x=t_{<i}$ for some $i$}\\
|\phi_{x}\rangle_{\mathsf{R}_{x}}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\otimes|t\rangle_{\mathsf{S}}.

By the definition of
|\phi_{t_{<i}}\rangle
it holds that
\langle t_{i}|\phi_{t_{<i}}\rangle=\beta_{t_{<i}t_{i}}=\beta_{t_{\leq i}}
, so since
\alpha_{t}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{t_{\leq i}}
for all
t\in\{0,1\}^{n}
it follows that

U_{f}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\bigotimes_{\mathrlap{x\in\{0,1\}^{<n}}}|\phi_{x}\rangle_{\mathsf{R}_{x}}\otimes|0^{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{S}}}}\right)=\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\alpha_{t}\bigotimes_{x\in\{0,1\}^{<n}}\begin{cases}|t_{i}\rangle_{\mathsf{R}_{x}}&\text{if $x=t_{<i}$ for some $i$}\\
|\phi_{x}\rangle_{\mathsf{R}_{x}}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\otimes|t\rangle_{\mathsf{S}}.

All that remains to construct the state
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\alpha_{t}|t\rangle
is to uncompute the above content of
(\mathsf{R}_{x})_{x\in\{0,1\}^{<n}}
controlled on the state
|t\rangle
of
\mathsf{S}
. To do so, first make
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\{0,1\}^{<n}}}\right|
copies of
t
using fanout. Then for each
x\in\{0,1\}^{<n}
in parallel, controlled on one of these copies of
t
, if
x=t_{<i}
for some

then perform in
\mathsf{R}_{x}
an operation that maps
|t_{i}\rangle
to
|0\rangle
, and otherwise perform in
\mathsf{R}_{x}
an operation that maps
|\phi_{x}\rangle
to
|0\rangle
. Finally, uncompute the extra copies of
t
using fanout.
∎

First we establish some basic properties of

Lemma 4.3
.

There is a uniform family of
O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(mn\log n}}\right)
-qubit
\mathsf{QAC_{f}^{0}}

(C_{n,m})_{n,m}
, where

(\log n)
-qubit register
\mathsf{K}
and

-qubit registers
\mathsf{A}_{0},\dotsc,\mathsf{A}_{n-1},\mathsf{B}
(and ancillae) and

\mathsf{A}_{k}
and
\mathsf{B}
controlled on the classical state
|k\rangle_{\mathsf{K}}
.

Proof.

We can assume without loss of generality that
m=1
, because then the general case follows by swapping the
i
’th qubits of
\mathsf{A}_{k}
and
\mathsf{B}
for all
i
in parallel. By linearity we may assume that the input is a standard basis state

|k\rangle_{\mathsf{K}}|x_{0}\rangle_{\mathsf{A}_{0}}\dotsb|x_{n-1}\rangle_{\mathsf{A}_{n-1}}|y\rangle_{\mathsf{B}}.

For now assume that
y
is promised to be
0^{n}
. First compute
x_{k}=\bigvee_{j=0}^{n-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbbm{1}_{j=k}\wedge x_{j}}}\right)
in
\mathsf{B}
, using that
\mathsf{QAC_{f}^{0}}
circuits can simulate
\mathsf{AC^{0}}
circuits; note that comparing

and
k
requires

qubits for any given value of

|x_{k}\rangle_{\mathsf{B}}
, for all
j<n
in parallel (using fanout) XOR the bit
\mathbbm{1}_{j=k}\wedge x_{k}
into
\mathsf{A}_{j}
.

For the general case where

might not be
0^{n}
, let
\mathsf{C}
be an
n
-qubit register in the ancillae. First swap
\mathsf{A}_{k}
and
\mathsf{C}
as described above, then swap
\mathsf{B}
and
\mathsf{A}_{k}
as described above, and finally swap
\mathsf{C}
and
\mathsf{B}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

If
C
is an
n
-qubit, size-
s
, depth-
d

\mathsf{QAC_{f}}

C
can be implemented by an
O(n)
-qubit, size-

\mathsf{QAC_{f}}

Proof.

Controlled on a bit

, each gate in a
\mathsf{QAC_{f}}

as follows. A
k

(k+1)
-qubit generalized Toffoli gate, fanning out a bit

n

Appendix A
\mathsf{QNC}
simulation of
\mathsf{QAC_{f}}
circuits

Lemma A.1
.

For all
n
-qubit, depth-
d

\mathsf{QAC_{f}}
circuits
U
, there exists an
O(n)
-qubit depth-
O(d\log n)
, size-
O(dn)

\mathsf{QNC}
circuit
C
such that
C(I_{n}\otimes|0\dotsc 0\rangle)=U\otimes|0\dotsc 0\rangle
.

Proof.

Green et al.
[
9
]
observed that the transformation
|b,0^{n-1}\rangle\mapsto|b^{n}\rangle
for
b\in\{0,1\}
can be implemented by a size-
(n-1)
, depth-
\lceil\log n\rceil
circuit consisting of CNOT gates with no ancillae. Therefore the fanout transformation
|b,x\rangle\mapsto|b,x\oplus b^{n}\rangle
for
b\in\{0,1\},x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
can be implemented by first computing
b^{n}
as described above, then XORing
b^{n}
onto
x
, and finally uncomputing
b^{n}
.

Similarly an
n
-qubit generalized Toffoli gate can be cleanly simulated by a size-
O(n)
, depth-
O(\log n)

\mathsf{QNC}
circuit with
O(n)
ancillae. This follows by simulating a log-depth DeMorgan formula for the AND function (i.e. the circuit whose graph is a balanced binary tree of 2-bit AND gates), with one ancilla qubit allocated to store the value of each gate in the DeMorgan formula, and then uncomputing the garbage.

A general
n
-qubit, depth-1
\mathsf{QAC_{f}}
circuit can be written as
\bigotimes_{j}G_{j}
, where each
G_{j}
is a
k_{j}
-qubit gate such that
\sum_{j}k_{j}\leq n
, and if
k_{j}>1
then
G_{j}
is either a generalized Toffoli or fanout gate. It follows that
\bigotimes_{j}G_{j}
can be cleanly simulated by a
\mathsf{QNC}
circuit where the size and number of ancillae are
O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{j}k_{j}}}\right)\leq O(n)
and the depth is
O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\max_{j}\log k_{j}}}\right)\leq O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\log\sum_{j}k_{j}}}\right)\leq O(\log n)
. The lemma follows by successively implementing each layer of a
\mathsf{QAC_{f}}
circuit in this way, reusing the same ancillae to simulate each layer.
∎

Exponential convergence to equilibrium for coupled systems of nonlinear degenerate drift diffusion equations Thanks: This research was supported by the DFG Collaborative Research Center TRR 109, “Discretization in Geometry and Dynamics”.
Lisa Beck, Daniel Matthes, Martina Zizza

Abstract
. We study the existence and long-time asymptotics of weak solutions to a system of two nonlinear drift-diffusion equations that has a gradient flow structure in the Wasserstein distance. The two equations are coupled through a cross-diffusion term that is scaled by a parameter ε ≥ 0 \varepsilon\geq 0 . The nonlinearities and potentials are chosen such that in the decoupled system for ε = 0 \varepsilon=0 , the evolution is metrically contractive, with a global rate Λ > 0 \Lambda>0 . The coupling is a singular perturbation in the sense that for any ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , contractivity of the system is lost. Our main result is that for all sufficiently small ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , the global attraction to a unique steady state persists, with an exponential rate Λ ε = Λ − K ​ ε \Lambda_{\varepsilon}=\Lambda-K\varepsilon . The proof combines results from the theory of metric gradient flows with further variational methods and functional inequalities.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we analyze existence and long-time asymptotics of non-negative unit-mass solutions
u
and
v

of the following coupled system of two degenerate nonlinear drift-diffusion equations on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(1.1)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u&=\operatorname{div}\big(u\,\nabla[F^{\prime}(u)+\varepsilon\partial_{u}h(u,v)+\Phi]\big),\\
\partial_{t}v&=\operatorname{div}\big(v\,\nabla[G^{\prime}(v)+\varepsilon\partial_{v}h(u,v)+\Psi]\big).\end{split}

Notice that the diffusive contributions
\operatorname{div}(u\nabla F^{\prime}(u))
and
\operatorname{div}(v\nabla G^{\prime}(v))
of the system (
1.1
) can also be expressed as
\Delta f(u)
and
\Delta g(v)
, respectively, by introducing functions
f
and
g
via the relations
f^{\prime}(r)=rF^{\prime\prime}(r)
and
g^{\prime}(r)=rG^{\prime\prime}(r)
for
r>0
.
The precise hypotheses on the various functions are formulated in Section
1.2
below;
in brief: the nonlinearities
F
,
G
for the individual components
are smooth convex functions that degenerate at zero, i.e., with
F^{\prime}(0)=G^{\prime}(0)=0
;
the coupling is moderated by a nonlinear function
h
that is regular and bounded;
the coupling strength
\varepsilon>0
will be assumed small;
and the potentials
\Phi
,
\Psi
are
\Lambda
-convex, with some
\Lambda>0
.
We prove the global existence of transient solutions
(u_{\varepsilon}(t),v_{\varepsilon}(t))_{t\geq 0}
to (
1.1
) for initial data of finite energy;
we show existence and uniqueness of a stationary solution
(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
, and analyze its regularity;
finally, we obtain convergence of
(u_{\varepsilon}(t),v_{\varepsilon}(t))
to
(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
t\to\infty
, at an exponential rate.

Our approach is a variational one:
we consider — at least formally — the system (
1.1
) as a metric gradient flow of the energy functional

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[F(u)+G(v)+u\Phi+v\Psi+\varepsilon h(u,v)\big]\,\mathrm{d}x

on the cross product of two copies of the space
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of probability densities of finite second moment,
endowed with the
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distance;
the definitions are recalled in Section
2
below.

What we consider as our main contribution
— going beyond the analysis of the specific system (
1.1
) —
is a blending of the abstract machinery of uniformly contractive metric gradient flows
with more hands-on variational methods by which we extend the relevant estimates into the totally non-convex regime.
Indeed, under the hypotheses stated below,
the two evolution equations of the
decoupled system
, i.e., (
1.1
) for
\varepsilon=0
,
constitute
\Lambda
-contractive gradient flows in the
L^{2}
-Wasserstein metric.
Stated differently,
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}
is
\Lambda
-uniformly convex along geodesics.
A consequence from the general theory — see e.g.
[
3
, Chapter 11]
— is the convergence of both components
u(t)
and
v(t)

to respective stationary solutions
\bar{u}_{0}
and
\bar{v}_{0}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
at exponential rate
\Lambda
.

It is a remarkable fact that, in the eyes of the Wasserstein metric,
the coupling via
h
in (
1.1
) is a singular perturbation of the two decoupled equations,
no matter how tame
h
is.
As soon as
\varepsilon>0
, geodesic semi-convexity of the energy
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}
is lost entirely, i.e.,

\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}
is
not

\lambda
-uniformly convex along geodesics, not for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
see Proposition
2.4
.
In that case, the gradient flow machinery does not provide any information on the long-time behaviour of solutions to (
1.1
).
Rather, it implies that the coupled systems is
not
globally uniformly semi-contractive anymore,
that is, there is no Lipschitz bound on the instantaneous divergence of trajectories.
Of course, this does not rule out global exponential convergence to equilibrium, but the latter behaviour is inaccessible to that theory.

To prove equilibration at exponential rate, we build on associated functional inequalities, see e.g.
[
14
]
,
which can be obtained as a consequence of metric contractivity, but which are more “structurally robust” than the latter.
Specifically, we obtain in Lemma
4.8
that

(1.3)

\begin{split}2(\Lambda-K_{0}\varepsilon)&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[F(u)-F(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\big(\Phi+\varepsilon\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big)\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\big|\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(u)+\Phi+\varepsilon\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big]\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(and a similar inequality for
v
in place of
u
),
which expresses the relation between energy and dissipation
for the auxiliary scalar gradient flow
\partial_{t}u=\operatorname{div}(u\nabla[F^{\prime}(u)+\Phi+\varepsilon\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})])
.
The constant
\Lambda-K_{0}\varepsilon
in (
1.3
) is a lower bound
on the modulus of convexity of
\Phi+\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
,
which is controlled thanks to our
C^{2}
-estimates on the steady state in Theorem
1.3
.
With (
1.3
) at hand,
the key step in our approach, see Proposition
4.4
, amounts to proving that
the negative time-derivative of the integral on the left-hand side of (
1.3
)
bounds the expression on the right-hand side, up to a factor
1-K_{1}\varepsilon
.
This eventually implies convergence to equilibrium in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

at a global exponential rate of at least
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\Lambda-K\varepsilon
, with
K\coloneqq K_{0}+\Lambda K_{1}
.

We emphasize that the novelty of our result does not lie in the proof of convergence to equilibrium as such
— a
qualitative
result could be obtained at almost no cost e.g. from the LaSalle principle —
but in the
quantitative
estimate on the convergence to equilibrium,
with the exponential rate
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}
that is an
\varepsilon
-perturbation of the decoupled rate
\Lambda
.
It is further significant that (
1.1
) is considered on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and that the steady state
(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
is compactly supported;
hence, there is no standard inequality like Poincaré or log-Sobolev to conclude exponential convergence,
not even at
some
rate smaller than
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}
,
but a more adapted inequality like (
1.3
) appears necessary.

1.1.
Positioning of our results

Coupled systems of nonlinear drift-diffusion equations are ubiquitous.
They are used in the modeling of chemical reactions
[
38
]
,
flows in porous media
[
10
]
, semi-conductor devices
[
34
]

population dynamics
[
8
]
, rival gangs in a city
[
4
]
,
just to name a few of the countless applications.
The literature concerning the very natural question about long-time asymptotics is huge,
albeit mostly focused on such systems
with a particular rigid algebraic structure of the diffusion (being diagonal, or even linear)
but with additional source terms, describing e.g. reactions.

We briefly recall the situation for
scalar
drift-diffusion equations.
The first proof of exponential convergence to equilibrium in a degenerate parabolic equations
of the type
\partial_{t}u=\Delta f(u)+\operatorname{div}(u\nabla\Phi)
has been given
in the case
f(u)=u^{m}
and
\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

by three different methods:
by a nonlinear extension of the Bakry–Emery method
[
13
]
,
by a variational proof of the entropy-dissipation inequality
[
17
]
,
and by virtue of gradient flows in the
L^{2}
-Wasserstein metric
[
35
]
.
These methods have been extended later on to more general
f
’s and
\Phi
’s,
and also to bounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
see e.g.
[
12
]
and
[
14
]
for such generalizations of the first and of the third approach, respectively.
The common fundamental result is that if
f
has certain convexity properties,
and if
\Phi
is uniformly convex of modulus
\Lambda>0
,
then solutions
u
converge to the unique equilibrium in
L^{1}
at exponential rate
\Lambda
.

There appears to be no result of comparable simplicity and generality for
coupled systems
of parabolic equations.
All of the aforementioned methods of proof break down as soon as multi-component densities are considered,
except in some particular systems with a very special algebraic structure, see e.g.
[
29
,
43
]
.
System (
1.1
) is a paradigmatic illustration of this effect on the gradient flow approach,
where geodesic convexity turns out to be highly fragile with respect to the coupling of components:
the decoupled functional
\mathbf{E}_{0}
from (
1.2
) is
\Lambda
-convex along geodesics,
but
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}
completely loses convexity for any
\varepsilon>0
, see Proposition
2.4
below.
For the sake of completeness,
we mention that although the gradient flow approach loses its quantitative estimates,
various qualitative results, e.g.,
on the shape of steady states or on the convergence to equilibrium (without rates),
have been obtained along these lines for specific systems, see for instance
[
28
]
.

Limited generalizations of the scalar theory
have been developed for reaction-diffusion systems, and recently also for cross-diffusion systems (with or without reactions).
Although many of these systems still bear a gradient flow structure
[
32
]
,
the more robust energy method has proven better adapted to study long-time asymptotics.
In reaction-diffusion systems, the substantial challenge is in the control of the growth induced by the reactions,
while the diffusion itself is typically decoupled, and frequently just linear.
Prototypical results on exponential equilibration have been obtained in
[
18
,
21
,
33
,
24
]

for systems with linear diffusion,
and in
[
22
]
for component-wise non-linear diffusion.
In (reaction-)cross-diffusion systems, the diffusion matrix is non-diagonal,
but usually subject to restrictive structural conditions.
Recent results on exponential convergence to equilibrium have been obtained, e.g.,
for systems with volume filling
[
40
]
,
of Maxwell–Stefan type
[
15
]
,
or with SKT-structure
[
16
]
.

None of the above results covers the exponential equilibration presented in Theorem
1.5
below,
i.e., for a system which is fully nonlinear with a general (albeit small) coupling.
Note in particular that one of the main challenges in the analysis of (
1.1
)
is that the steady state is compactly supported,
which rules out the use of the standard log-Sobolev
— that is a key element in the proofs of essentially all of the aforementioned results —
but requires more adapted functional inequalities like (
1.3
).
A recent result that is closer in spirit to our approach to proving Theorem
1.5

has been obtained in
[
2
]
:
the authors treat a system with a small nonlinear coupling like (
1.1
);
however, there, linearity of
F
and
G
, a bounded spatial domain, and an a priori
L^{\infty}
-bound are assumed,
which allows to perform the estimates in a much simpler way, using Poincaré’s inequality.

For completeness, we finally mention two results
[
42
,
41
]
,
where a similar strategy has been applied to the parabolic-parabolic Keller–Segel and the Nernst–Planck system,
respectively.
There, the coupling is between a Wasserstein and an
L^{2}
-gradient flow,
unlike the coupling between two Wasserstein gradient flows here.
A variant of the above has been explored in
[
44
]
.
The paper at hand uses elements from
[
42
,
44
]
.

We briefly comment on the positioning of our existence result, Theorem
1.4
.
We use the celebrated JKO scheme
[
25
]
to obtain solutions by a variational time-discrete approximation.
This scheme has been used for proving existence of various non-linear parabolic equations
like doubly degenerate parabolic PDEs
[
36
]
, including the
p
-Laplace equation
[
1
]
;
in nonlinear diffusion-aggregation equations
[
11
]
, including the parabolic-elliptic Keller–Segel model
[
6
]
,
in fourth order quantum and thin film equations
[
23
,
31
,
30
]
;
and in many further instances.
Applications to coupled systems are numerous as well,
including for instance systems with non-local aggregation
[
20
,
19
]
and cross-diffusion
[
9
,
5
]
,
and also combinations of Wasserstein and
L^{2}
-gradient flows,
like the parabolic-parabolic Keller–Segel
[
42
,
7
]
or the Nernst–Planck system
[
27
]
.
In several of these cases, the existence proof could have been obtained also by more elementary methods.
Also for (
1.1
), the
boundedness-by-entropy method

[
26
]
,
albeit not directly applicable, would have paved an alternative way.
For us, the time-discrete approximation via a minimizing movement with respect to the
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distance
is crucial for making our main result, namely the long-time asymptotics, fully rigorous.
The work most closely related to our proof of Theorem
1.4
below is
[
19
]
:
there, the same variational method has been used to construct time-discrete approximations to a system similar to (
1.1
),
augmented with additional non-local interaction terms.
The hypotheses of
[

are complementary to ours;

1.2.
General hypotheses

The following hypotheses are assumed throughout the paper.
Several of the assumptions are made for convenience of the presentation, and are far from being necessary.

•

Potentials:
For
\Phi,\Psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we assume:

–

there are positive constants
\Lambda
and
M

such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Lambda\mathbf{1}\leq\nabla^{2}\Phi\leq M\mathbf{1},\quad\Lambda\mathbf{1}\leq\nabla^{2}\Psi\leq M\mathbf{1};

–

\Phi
and
\Psi
vanish at their respective minima
\underline{x}_{\Phi},\underline{x}_{\Psi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
i.e.

(1.5)

\displaystyle 0=\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi=\Phi\big(\underline{x}_{\Phi}\big),\quad 0=\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi=\Psi\big(\underline{x}_{\Psi}\big).

•

Nonlinearities:
We assume
F,\,G\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
such that:

–

F^{\prime\prime}(r)>0
and
G^{\prime\prime}(r)>0
for all
r>0
,
and

(1.6)

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to\infty}F^{\prime\prime}(r)>0,\quad\liminf_{r\to\infty}G^{\prime\prime}(r)>0;

–

they degenerate at zero to first order, i.e.,

(1.7)

\displaystyle F(0)=G(0)=0,\quad F^{\prime}(0)=G^{\prime}(0)=0;

–

there are exponents
m,n\geq 2
such that

(1.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\downarrow 0}r^{-(m-2)}F^{\prime\prime}(r)\in(0,\infty),\quad\lim_{r\downarrow 0}r^{-(n-2)}G^{\prime\prime}(r)\in(0,\infty);

–

they satisfy the (infinite dimensional) McCann condition, i.e., for all
r>0
,

(1.9)

\displaystyle rF^{\prime}(r)\leq F(r)+r^{2}F^{\prime\prime}(r),\quad rG^{\prime}(r)\leq G(r)+r^{2}G^{\prime\prime}(r);

–

they satisfy the doubling condition, i.e., there is a constant
D
such that for all
r,s>0
,

(1.10)

\displaystyle F(r+s)\leq D(1+F(r)+F(s)),\quad G(r+s)\leq D(1+G(r)+G(s)).

•

Coupling:
Concerning
h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2})
, we assume that:

–

h
vanishes to first order on
\partial\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
,

(1.11)

\displaystyle h=\partial_{u}h=\partial_{v}h\equiv 0\quad\text{on $\partial\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}$};

–

there is an
\varepsilon^{*}>0
such that

(1.12)

\displaystyle(u,v)\mapsto F(u)+G(v)+2\varepsilon^{*}h(u,v)\quad\text{is convex};

–

with the same
\varepsilon^{*}
, there holds, for all
u,v>0
,

(1.13)

\displaystyle 2\varepsilon^{*}|h(u,v)|\leq F(u)+G(v).

•

Degeneracy, boundedness, and swap condition:

Define
\theta_{u},\theta_{v}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(1.14)

\displaystyle\theta_{u}(\rho,\eta)\coloneqq\partial_{u}h\big((F^{\prime})^{-1}(\rho),(G^{\prime})^{-1}(\eta)\big),\quad\theta_{u}(\rho,\eta)\coloneqq\partial_{u}h\big((F^{\prime})^{-1}(\rho),(G^{\prime})^{-1}(\eta)\big).

We say that the triple
(F,G,h)
…

–

…satisfies the
swap condition
if there is some constant
W

such that, for all
u,v>0
,

(1.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}\big(F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big)\leq W\sqrt{v/u},\quad\partial_{\rho}\theta_{v}\big(F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big)\leq W\sqrt{u/v};

–

…is
k
-bounded
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}

if
\theta_{u},\theta_{v}\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2})

and all partial derivatives of order less or equal to
k
are bounded on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
;

–

…is
k
-degenerate
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}

if
\theta_{u},\theta_{v}\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2})

and all partial derivatives of order less or equal
k
vanish on
\partial\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
.

.

1.3.
Results

Our first result concerns the existence of stationary solutions to (
1.1
),
characterized as minimizers of
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For each
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
, there is a unique minimizer
(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
of
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}
.
The densities
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}
have compact supports that are sublevels of
\Phi
and
\Psi
, respectively.
Further, if
h
degenerates to order
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
then the restrictions of
F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
and
G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
to their respective supports
are bounded in
C^{k}
, uniformly with respect to
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
.

A point of crucial importance is that for degeneracy of order two,
the functions
\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
and
\partial_{v}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
are in
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
with a global bound on second derivatives that is independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
.
This is needed to establish the functional inequalities (
1.3
),
which are essential for our proofs of the following results.
Concerning existence of transient solutions, we have:

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume in addition that
(F,G,h)
is
2
-bounded and
2
-degenerate,
and that the swap condition holds.
There is some
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
, such that for each
\varepsilon\in[0,\bar{\varepsilon}]

and any initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in[\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})]^{2}
of finite energy
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0})<\infty
,
there exists a transient weak solution
(u_{\varepsilon}(t),v_{\varepsilon}(t))_{t\geq 0}
to the initial value problem for (
1.1
).

Here the most significant point is not the mere existence but the way of construction,
namely via the minimizing movement scheme for
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}
in the combined
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distances.

Finally, the main result of this paper is about the long-time asymptotics of transient solutions.

Theorem 1.5
.

Under the same conditions as in Theorem
1.4
above,
there exist constants
K>0
and
B\geq 1
such that the following is true for all
\varepsilon\in[0,\bar{\varepsilon}]
:
the transient solution
(u_{\varepsilon}(t),v_{\varepsilon}(t))_{t\geq 0}
constructed in the proof of Theorem

converges to the unique global minimizer

from Theorem

at exponential rate

.
More precisely, with

being the Lyapunov functional defined in (

),
there holds

(1.21)

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}\big(u_{\varepsilon}(t),v_{\varepsilon}(t)\big)\leq\mathbf{L}(u_{0},v_{0})\,\exp\big(-2\Lambda_{\varepsilon}t\big),

and in particular,

and

converge in

to
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}

, respectively,

(1.22)

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\|v_{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\leq B(\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0})+1)\,\exp\big(-2\Lambda_{\varepsilon}t\big).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Wasserstein distance

\mathbb{B}_{R}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon|x|<R\}
is the ball of radius
R>0
.

{\mathcal{L}^{d}}
denotes the standard Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For a probability measure

\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a measurable map

the

of

under

such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega(y)\,\mathrm{d}\big(T\#\mu\big)(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega\circ T(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x),

for any test function
\omega\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
If both

and

are absolutely continuous, then we write

for brevity.

\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the space of probability densities
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
of finite second moment.
The natural notion of convergence on
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the

By Prokhorov’s and by Alaoglu’s theorem, subsets of densities with uniformly bounded second moment and

-norm (for some

)
are sequentially compact in
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distance
\mathbf{W}_{2}
is a metric on
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Convergence in
\mathbf{W}_{2}
is equivalent to weak convergence and convergence of the second moments.
Among the various possible definitions of
\mathbf{W}_{2}

the following — known as the (pre-)dual Kantorovich formulation —
is the most suitable one for our needs:
for
u,\hat{u}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,\hat{u})^{2}\coloneqq\sup\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(y)\hat{u}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\colon\varphi(x)+\psi(y)\leq\frac{1}{2}|x-y|^{2}\right\}.

(Note the square and the factor
1/2
on the left-hand side.)
A priori, the maximization above is carried out over all pairs
(\varphi,\psi)
for which the integrals are well-defined,
i.e.,
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};u{\mathcal{L}^{d}})
and
\psi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\hat{u}{\mathcal{L}^{d}})
.
However, it suffices to consider pairs
(\varphi,\psi)
from the very restrictive class
of
c
-conjugate
1
1

1

The
c
refers to the cost function, which is the standard one here,
c(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}|x-y|^{2}
.
potentials
.
The latter means that the

given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(x)=\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}-\varphi(x),\quad\tilde{\psi}(y)=\frac{1}{2}|y|^{2}-\psi(y)

are proper, lower semi-continuous, convex, and Legendre-dual to each other,

\tilde{\varphi}^{\ast}=\tilde{\psi}
and
\tilde{\psi}^{\ast}=\tilde{\varphi}
.
Note that knowledge of either

Further note that

is automatically satisfied since

.

The supremum in (

of

-conjugate potentials.
Uniqueness of optimal pairs is delicate in general, even after removing

For us, the following special case is important:

is positive

-a.e. on a ball

-a.e. up to a global constant.

For an optimal pair
(\varphi_{\text{opt}},\psi_{\text{opt}})

the

from

is given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle T(x)\coloneqq x-\nabla\varphi_{\text{opt}}(x),

which is well-defined

-a.e.
It satisfies

(2.4)

\displaystyle\hat{u}=T\#u,

and

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,\hat{u})^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T(x)-x|^{2}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

T
is
u{\mathcal{L}^{d}}
-a.e. unique for a given pair
(u,\hat{u})
, which implies the following converse:
if

is the

-a.e. defined

,
and satisfies (

),
then

also satisfies (

),
and
\varphi_{\text{opt}}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}-\tilde{\varphi}(x)

(\varphi_{\text{opt}},\psi_{\text{opt}})
of

-conjugate potentials.

Finally, we recall a characterization of geodesics:
define the interpolating maps
T_{s}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

T_{s}(x)\coloneqq(1-s)x+sT(x)=x-s\nabla\varphi_{u}(x)

(u_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,u_{s})=s\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,\hat{u}),\quad\mathbf{W}_{2}(u_{s},\hat{u})=(1-s)\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,\hat{u}).

The natural space for solutions

We endow it with a metric

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{2}\big((u,v),(\hat{u},\hat{v})\big)\coloneqq\sqrt{\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,\hat{u})^{2}+\mathbf{W}_{2}(v,\hat{v})^{2}}.

The following is easily seen.

Lemma 2.1
.

A curve
(u_{s},v_{s})_{0\leq s\leq 1}

if and only if
(u_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
and
(v_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
are geodesics
in
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
between

,
u_{1}
, and between
v_{0}
,
v_{1}
, respectively.

2.2.
Displacement convexity

Definition 2.2
.

A functional
\mathbf{F}
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
\lambda
-uniformly displacement convex
with some modulus
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
if the real function

\displaystyle[0,1]\ni s\mapsto\mathbf{F}\big(T_{s}\#u)-\frac{\lambda}{2}s(1-s)\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,\hat{u})^{2}

is convex for any family
(T_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
realizing the geodesic between
u
and
\hat{u}=T_{1}\#u
.

Displacement convex functionals are rare.
An important class of examples is given by the sum of internal and potential energy:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[e(u)+Vu\big]\,\mathrm{d}x.

In this case
\mathbf{F}
is
\lambda
-uniformly displacement convex
provided that the convex function
e\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies McCann’s condition,
and that
V\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\lambda
-convex in the usual sense.
A consequence of that property is the validity of a functional inequality,
see e.g.
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that the functional
\mathbf{F}
of the type (
2.6
) is
such that the convex function
e\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies McCann’s condition,
and such that
V\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\lambda
-convex for
\lambda>0
.
Then
\mathbf{F}
possesses a unique minimizer
u_{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
and for all
u\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there holds:

(2.7)

\displaystyle 2\lambda\big[\mathbf{F}(u)-\mathbf{F}(u_{*})\big]\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\big|\nabla\big[e^{\prime}(u)+V\big]\big|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Functionals of the type (
2.6
) with
\lambda\geq 0
actually even enjoy the stronger property
of being convex along generalized geodesics, which has a variety of consequences.
The only consequence needed below is for the special case
h(u)=u\log u
and
V\equiv 0
,
when
\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{H}
is the entropy functional,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\log u\,\mathrm{d}x.

The metric gradient flow of
\mathbf{H}
is the heat equation
\partial_{s}U_{s}=\Delta U_{s}
,
and (thanks to convexity along generalized geodesics)
it satisfies the so-called
evolution variational inequality
(EVI
0
), see
[
3
, Theorem 4.0.4]
, which is

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{+}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\bigg|_{s=0^{+}}\mathbf{W}_{2}\big(U_{s},w\big)^{2}\leq\mathbf{H}(w)-\mathbf{H}(U_{0})

for all
w\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and all solutions to
\partial_{s}U_{s}=\Delta U_{s}
.

2.3.

We indicate why the aforementioned general theory of
\lambda
-uniformly displacement convex functionals does not apply to the energy functional

on the space

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume that

such that

is
\lambda
-convex along geodesics in

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}s^{2}}\bigg|_{s=0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H_{\varepsilon}(T_{s}\#u^{\omega},v^{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq-C(\omega-1),

where

is the translation by

in

-direction, i.e.,

Note that, by Lemma

Remark 2.5

With a little technical effort,

For the sake of clarity, we only give the construction for one such pair.

Proof.

In view of

on
\partial\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
because of the degeneracy condition (

For the construction below, we assume

Choose

For all sufficiently large

\displaystyle u^{\omega}(x)=u^{0}(x)+\omega^{-1/2}\delta_{r}(x)\sin(\omega x_{1}),\quad v^{\omega}(x)=v^{0}(x)+\omega^{-1/2}\delta_{r}(x)\sin(\omega x_{1}),

For the integral in (

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[\partial_{u}H_{\varepsilon}(u^{\omega},v^{\omega})\partial_{x_{1}x_{1}}u^{\omega}+\partial_{uu}H_{\varepsilon}(u^{\omega},v^{\omega})\big(\partial_{x_{1}}u^{\omega}\big)^{2}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x

In this section, Theorem

For brevity, define

Remark 3.1
.

We notice some important properties of the function

for

First, by hypothesis (

Second, as a consequence of the strict convexity of

In view of the identities

and since

In the following,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime}(\bar{U})\geq F^{\prime}(2)+dM+\varepsilon^{*}\sup_{uv\leq 2}\partial_{u}h(u,v)\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{2}G^{\prime}(\bar{U})\geq G^{\prime}(2)+dM+\varepsilon^{*}\sup_{u,v\leq 2}\partial_{v}h(u,v),

.

Let

is a probability density,
and thus
(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
is an admissible competitor.
On the one hand, on
S
, where
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=\bar{U}\leq\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})-H_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\geq(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})\partial_{u}H_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{1}{2}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-\bar{U})F^{\prime}(\bar{U}),

using the convexity estimate (
3.2
), the degeneracy (
1.7
) of
F
and (
1.11
) of
h
.
Hence, recalling the definition (
3.7
) of
\sigma
and the non-negativity of
\Phi
,

\displaystyle\int_{S}\big(H_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\Phi+\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\Psi\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{S}\big(H_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\Phi+\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\Psi\big)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime}(\bar{U})\sigma.

On the other hand, on
T
, where
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}+\sigma\leq 2
,

\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})-H_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\geq\big(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\big)\partial_{u}H_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\geq-\Big[F^{\prime}(2)+\varepsilon^{*}\sup_{a,b\leq 2}\partial_{u}h(a,b)\Big]\sigma.

With
T
being of unit measure,
and recalling that
0\leq\Phi(x)\leq\frac{M}{2}|x-\underline{x}_{\Phi}|^{2}\leq(3/2)^{2/d}dM/2\leq dM
for all
x\in T
thanks to (
1.16
),
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{T}\big(H_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\Phi+\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\Psi\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{T}\big(H_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\Phi+\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\Psi\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad-\Big[dM+F^{\prime}(2)+\varepsilon^{*}\sup_{a,b\leq 2}\partial_{u}h(a,b)\Big]\sigma.

In summary, recalling the implicit definition (
3.3
) of
\bar{U}
, we find

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\geq\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\sigma.

This contradicts the minimality of
(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
.
Consequently, we have
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\leq\bar{U}
and
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\leq\bar{U}
.

To characterize the minimizer, we perform variations of the form

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{s}=(1-\alpha s)\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}+s\xi,\qquad\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}^{s}=(1-\beta s)\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}+s\eta,

with appropriate functions
\xi,\eta\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of compact support,
and parameters

\displaystyle\alpha=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\beta=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta\,\mathrm{d}x.

We define

\displaystyle U_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[\partial_{u}H_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\Phi\big]\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad V_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[\partial_{v}H_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\Psi\big]\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x.

First, let
\xi,\eta\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be non-negative.
Then
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{s},\,\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}^{s}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
s\geq 0
sufficiently small,
and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{s},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}^{s})-\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})}{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[\partial_{u}H_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\Phi\big]\xi\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[\partial_{v}H_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\Psi\big]\eta\,\mathrm{d}x-\alpha U_{\varepsilon}-\beta V_{\varepsilon}.

This shows that, a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(3.8)

\begin{split}F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\Phi&\geq U_{\varepsilon},\\
G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon\partial_{v}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\Psi&\geq V_{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

Consequently, with
\partial_{u}h(0,v)=0
for all
v\geq 0
by hypothesis (
1.11
) and the fact that
F
is degenerate at zero to first order by hypothesis (
1.7
),
we necessarily have
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}>0
a.e. on
\{U_{\varepsilon}>\Phi\}
,
and similarly
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}>0
a.e. on
\{V_{\varepsilon}>\Psi\}
.

Next let
\xi,\eta\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be arbitrary.
For any
\delta>0
,
we may perform the aforementioned variations with

\displaystyle\xi_{\delta}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\xi&\text{where $\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}>\delta$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},\quad\eta_{\delta}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\eta&\text{where $\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}>\delta$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

even for all
-s\geq 0
sufficiently small,
and can thus conclude the opposite inequalities in (
3.8
).
This means that equality holds in the first inequality in (
3.8
) a.e. on
\{\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}>0\}
,
and in the second inequality a.e. on
\{\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}>0\}
.

We next consider the case that
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}>0
and
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}=0
.
Then
F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})>0
, and since
\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})=0
by hypothesis (
1.11
),
it follows that
\Phi<U_{\varepsilon}
.
Analogously,
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}>0
and
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}=0
implies
\Psi<V_{\varepsilon}
.
Finally, suppose
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}>0
and
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}>0
,
so that (
3.8
) becomes a system of two equations.
Recalling the definition of
H_{\varepsilon}
above,
that system can be written as a single vectorial equation as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}H_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})=\begin{pmatrix}U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi\\
V_{\varepsilon}-\Psi\end{pmatrix}.

By Remark
3.1
,
the positive cone
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2}
is mapped into itself under
\mathrm{D}H_{\varepsilon}
.
Therefore,
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}>0
and
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}>0
implies that
\Phi<U_{\varepsilon}
and
\Psi<U_{\varepsilon}
, respectively.
Consequently, the positivity sets of
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}

are indeed given by the sublevel sets
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
and
\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
from (
3.5
), respectively.

To sum up: a.e. on
\{\Phi<U_{\varepsilon}\}
,
we have
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}>0
and equality in the first inequality of (
3.8
);
and a.e. on the complement
\{\Phi\geq U_{\varepsilon}\}
, we have
u_{\varepsilon}=0
,
which, thanks to
F^{\prime}(0)=0
and
\partial_{u}h(0,\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})=0
,
can be written as

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})=0.

This (and an analogous argument for
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}
) implies (
3.4
).

Next, concerning the uniform boundedness of
U_{\varepsilon}
and
V_{\varepsilon}
:
it suffices to observe that, in view of
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\leq\bar{U}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{u}H_{\varepsilon}\big(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\leq C\coloneqq F^{\prime}(\bar{U})+\varepsilon^{*}\sup_{a,b\leq\bar{U}}h(a,b),

and further that at
x=\underline{x}_{\Phi}
,

\displaystyle U_{\varepsilon}=U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi(\underline{x}_{\Phi})=\partial_{u}H_{\varepsilon}\big(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}(\underline{x}_{\Phi}),\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}(\underline{x}_{\Phi})\big).

In combination, this yields
U_{\varepsilon}\leq C
, and we can argue analogously for
V_{\varepsilon}
.
We further notice that the uniform boundedness of the sets
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
and
\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
is a direct consequence of the estimates in (
1.16
) and the uniform bound on the constants
U_{\varepsilon}
and
V_{\varepsilon}
.

Finally, we verify that the
L^{2}
-representatives of
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}

that are given as pointwise solution of (
3.4
) are continuous.
To that end, we write (
3.4
) as

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})=\begin{pmatrix}(U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi)_{+}\\
(V_{\varepsilon}-\Psi)_{+}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\mathrm{D}H_{\varepsilon}
has a continuous inverse, cp. Remark
3.1
, and since the functions on the right-hand side of (
3.9
) are continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
so are the solutions
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

In addition to the general hypotheses, assume that
(F,G,h)
is
k
-degenerate for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
and
G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
are
k
times continuously differentiable
in
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
and in
\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
, respectively.
Moreover, all partial derivatives
\partial^{\alpha}F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
and
\partial^{\alpha}G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
of order
|\alpha|\leq k

are bounded on
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
and
\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
, respectively, uniformly in
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
.

Remark 3.6
.

The essential point of Proposition
3.5
is that
F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
is
k
times continuously differentiable
across the boundary of the support
\partial\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
of
the other
component
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}
, and vice versa.
Across the boundary of
its own
support
\partial\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
,
the function
F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
is generally Lipschitz but no better,
see Remark
3.7
after the proof.
This translates into mere Hölder continuity for
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
,
and in particular, one cannot expect the derivatives of
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
itself to be bounded on
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

The claim will follow by an application of the inverse function theorem.
To that end, define the map
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\eta)=\mathrm{D}H_{\varepsilon}\big((F^{\prime})^{-1}(\rho),(G^{\prime})^{-1}(\eta)\big)=\begin{pmatrix}\rho+\varepsilon\theta_{u}(\rho,\eta)\\
\eta+\varepsilon\theta_{v}(\rho,\eta)\end{pmatrix},

with
\theta_{u},\,\theta_{v}
from (
1.14
),
and the convention that
\theta_{u}(\rho,\eta)=\theta_{v}(\rho,\eta)=0
if
\rho\leq 0
or
\eta\leq 0
.
By
k
-degeneracy,
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
is
C^{k}
-regular.

Recall from Remark
3.1
that
\mathrm{D}H_{\varepsilon}
is a homeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
. Since
F^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}
are continuous and strictly monotone on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, the restriction of
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
to
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
possesses a continuous inverse as well.
Moreover, on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2}
, the inverse of
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
is simply the identity.
In conclusion,
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
has a global continuous inverse
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

that is the identity on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2}
.

Next, we show that the inverses of the derivative matrices

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon}=\begin{pmatrix}1+\varepsilon\partial_{\rho}\theta_{u}&\varepsilon\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}\\
\varepsilon\partial_{\rho}\theta_{v}&1+\varepsilon\partial_{\eta}\theta_{v}\end{pmatrix}

are locally bounded.
On
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2}
, this is trivial since

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\eta)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{if $\rho\leq 0$ or $\eta\leq 0$}.

On
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{2}
, we use the alternative representation

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\eta)=\mathrm{D}^{2}H_{\varepsilon}(u,v)\,\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{F^{\prime\prime}(u)}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{G^{\prime\prime}(v)}\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{with}\quad u=(F^{\prime})^{-1}(\rho),\,v=(G^{\prime})^{-1}(\eta).

Since
u,v>0
, the Hessian matrix
\mathrm{D}^{2}H_{\varepsilon}(u,v)
is positive definite.
Since
R\geq S
for symmetric, positive definite matrices implies
\det R\geq\det S>0
,
the estimate (
3.2
) shows that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\det\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\rho,\eta)\geq\frac{1}{4}F^{\prime\prime}(u)G^{\prime\prime}(u)\cdot\frac{1}{F^{\prime\prime}(u)G^{\prime\prime}(v)}=\frac{1}{4}.

It follows that the inverses

\displaystyle(\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon})^{-1}=\frac{1}{\det\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}\begin{pmatrix}1+\varepsilon\partial_{\eta}\theta_{v}&-\varepsilon\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}\\
-\varepsilon\partial_{\rho}\theta_{v}&1+\varepsilon\partial_{\rho}\theta_{u}\end{pmatrix}

are bounded on any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
, uniformly with respect to
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
.
The inverse function theorem is applicable and shows that
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
is of class
C^{k}
,
with derivatives up to
k
-th order
\varepsilon
-uniformly bounded on each compact set of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

To conclude regularity of
F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
and
G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
from here,
we perform the following change of variables:

\rho(x)=F^{\prime}(u(x)),\quad\eta(x)=G^{\prime}(v(x)).

Written in terms of
\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
and
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
,
the system (
3.4
) of Euler–Lagrange equations becomes

(3.13)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})=\begin{pmatrix}(U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi)_{+}\\
(V_{\varepsilon}-\Psi)_{+}\end{pmatrix},

and its solution is given by

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}\\
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\end{pmatrix}=(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})^{-1}\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}(U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi)_{+}\\
(V_{\varepsilon}-\Psi)_{+}\end{pmatrix}\bigg).

On
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
, where we have

\displaystyle(U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi)_{+}=U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi,\quad(V_{\varepsilon}-\Psi)_{+}=V_{\varepsilon}-\Psi,

it now follows directly from (
3.14
)
that
\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
inherit the
C^{k}
-regularity of
\Phi
and
\Psi
.
Recalling that
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\leq\bar{U}
,
we conclude from the
\varepsilon
-uniform local boundedness of the derivatives of
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}

that also the partial derivatives of
\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
of order
\leq k
are bounded,
uniformly in
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
on
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
.

Next, recalling that
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\rho,0)=(\rho,0)
,
we observe that on
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\overline{\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}}
,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}=U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi.

Therefore,
\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}
inherits the
C^{k}
-regularity and bounds from
\Phi
.
The analogous statement holds for
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
on
\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\overline{\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}}
.

It remains to verify the existence and continuity of all partial derivatives
\partial^{\alpha}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}
of order
|\alpha|\leq k

across the interfaces
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
.
We will do this by showing that at any point
x^{*}\in\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
,
the limits of
\partial^{\alpha}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}
from the inside and from the outside of
\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
agree.
Since, according to (
3.15
), the outside limit amounts to
-\partial^{\alpha}\Psi
,
which is smooth on
\partial\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
,
this also proves continuity of
\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}
across the boundary
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
.

Thus, let
x^{*}\in\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
be fixed.
At points
x\in\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
, (
3.14
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(x))\\
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}(x))\end{pmatrix}=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi(x)\\
V_{\varepsilon}-\Psi(x)\end{pmatrix}\bigg).

We start by considering first derivatives.
Since

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}\big(\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\big)=(\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon})^{-1}\circ\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=\left[\frac{1}{\det\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}\begin{pmatrix}1+\varepsilon\partial_{\eta}\theta_{v}&-\varepsilon\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}\\
-\varepsilon\partial_{\rho}\theta_{v}&1+\varepsilon\partial_{\rho}\theta_{u}\end{pmatrix}\right]\circ\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{-1},

it follows that

(3.16)

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{k}}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\det\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})}\big[-\big(1+\varepsilon\partial_{\eta}\theta_{v}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})\big)\partial_{x_{k}}\Phi-\varepsilon\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})\big)\partial_{x_{k}}\Psi\big].

Since
\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
are continuous at
x^{*}
with
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}(x^{*})=0
,
it follows thanks to
k
-degeneracy
that also
\partial_{\rho}\theta_{u}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})
and
\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})

converge to zero as
x\to x^{*}
.
Hence, (
3.16
) implies
\partial_{x_{k}}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(x)\to-\partial_{x_{k}}\Phi(x^{*})
,
as desired.
Higher-order partial derivatives can now be obtained by induction on the degree of differentiability.
Assume that for
\ell<k
, uniform boundedness of all partial derivatives of order
\leq\ell
has been shown;
note that the proof for
\ell=1
is above.
Application of
\partial^{\alpha^{\prime}}
with
|\alpha^{\prime}|=\ell
to (
3.16
) yields

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial_{x_{k}}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{\beta+\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}}

\displaystyle C_{\beta,\beta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime}}\partial^{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{\det\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})}\right)

\displaystyle\times\big[-\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}\big(1+\varepsilon\partial_{\eta}\theta_{v}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})\big)\partial^{\beta^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{x_{k}}\Phi-\varepsilon\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})\big)\partial^{\beta^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{x_{k}}\Psi\big],

with certain combinatorial coefficients
C_{\beta,\beta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime}}
.
The partial derivatives of
\Phi
and
\Psi
remain uniformly bounded as
x\to x^{*}
.
The same is true for the partial derivatives of
1/\det\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
;
the entries in
\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
are
C^{k-1}
-regular functions by the
k
-degeneracy condition,
and
\det\mathrm{D}\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
is bounded below by virtue of (
3.12
).
Further, observe that
\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})
can be written
as a finite weighted sum, where each term is
a partial derivative (with respect to
(\rho,\eta)
) of order
\leq\ell+1=k
of
\theta_{u}
at
(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(x),\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}(x))

— and hence continuous —
multiplied by a product of partial derivatives (with respect to
x
) of
\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
of order
\leq\ell

— and hence uniformly bounded by induction hypothesis.
As above, using that
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}(x)\to 0
as
x\to x^{*}
,
it follows
by means
k
-degeneracy
that
\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}\big(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(x),\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)\to 0

and that
\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}\partial_{\eta}\theta_{v}\big(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(x),\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)\to 0

as
x\to x^{*}
.
And so,

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial_{x_{k}}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(x)\to-\partial^{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial_{x_{k}}\Phi(x^{*}),

finishing the proof.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

If
(F,G,h)
is
1
-degenerate, then (
3.11
) implies
that
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
is a differentiable perturbation of the identity near any point
(0,z)
with
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
Thus, the first component
\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.14
)
is of the form
(U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi)_{+}
plus some differentiable perturbation near the boundary
\partial\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
.
This confirms Remark
3.6
that in general only Lipschitz regularity
can be expected from
F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
across the boundary of its own support.

Example 3.8
.

The following example illustrates that without the hypothesis of
2
-degeneracy,
one cannot expect
C^{2}
-regularity of
F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
and
G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
.
We consider

\displaystyle F(u)=\frac{u^{2}}{2},\quad G(v)=\frac{v^{2}}{2},\quad h(u,v)=(uv)^{2},

which is
1
-degenerate, but not
2
-degenerate.
The system (
3.4
) attains the form

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}(1+2\varepsilon\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}^{2})=(U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi)_{+},\quad\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}(1+2\varepsilon\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{2})=(V_{\varepsilon}-\Psi)_{+}\,.

If
x^{*}\in\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
,
then
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\approx(V_{\varepsilon}-\Psi)_{+}
is Lipschitz but not differentiable at
x^{*}
, see Remark
3.7
above.
Thus
\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}^{2}
is once continuously differentiable, but fails to be twice differentiable at
x^{*}
. Consequently,

F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})=\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi}{1+2\varepsilon\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}^{2}}

fails to be twice differentiable at
x^{*}
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Assume that
(F,G,h)
is
2
-degenerate.
Then
\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
and
\partial_{v}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})

are
\varepsilon
-uniformly semi-convex, that is,
there is a
K_{0}\geq 0
such that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\geq-K_{0}\mathbf{1},\quad\nabla^{2}\partial_{v}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\geq-K_{0}\mathbf{1}

on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for all
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.5
above,
it follows that the gradients and the Hessians of
F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
and
G^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})

are
\varepsilon
-uniformly bounded on the respective supports
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
and
\Omega^{v}_{\varepsilon}
.
With

\displaystyle\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})=\theta_{u}\big(F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}),G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big),

and with
\theta_{u}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2})

the first and second order derivatives of

are
\varepsilon

\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
.
In fact, omitting the arguments, we have the representation

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\partial_{u}h

\displaystyle=\partial_{\rho}\theta_{u}\,\nabla^{2}F^{\prime}+\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}\,\nabla^{2}G^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad+\partial_{\rho\rho}\theta_{u}\,\nabla F^{\prime}\otimes\nabla F^{\prime}+\partial_{\eta\eta}\theta_{u}\,\nabla G^{\prime}\otimes\nabla G^{\prime}+\partial_{\rho\eta}\theta_{u}\,\big(\nabla F^{\prime}\otimes\nabla G^{\prime}+\nabla G^{\prime}\otimes\nabla F^{\prime}\big).

Fix some

at the boundary of the support
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
, and consider the expression above for

from inside
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
.
The gradients and Hessians of

and

remain bounded,
whereas the partial derivatives of

converge to zero because of
2
-degeneracy,
and because their argument

converges to zero.
Thus
\nabla^{2}\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})
is continuous across the boundary of the support
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
.
This implies an
\varepsilon
-uniform bound on the second derivatives of
\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})

and in particular the semi-convexity estimate (

).
∎

4.1.
Yosida-regularization and Results

The fundamental object that we use

of
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}

with a time step

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon,\tau}\big((u,v)\big|(\bar{u},\bar{v})\big)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\tau}\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{2}\big((u,v),(\bar{u},\bar{v})\big)^{2}+\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u,v).

A time-discrete approximation of solutions to (

by means of inductive minimization of

The following certifies well-posedness of that induction.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given any pair

of

Moreover, one has

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u^{*},v^{*})+\frac{\tau}{2}\left(\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{2}\big((u^{*},v^{*}),(\hat{u},\hat{v})\big)}{\tau}\right)^{2}\leq\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(\hat{u},\hat{v}).

In particular,
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u^{*},v^{*})\leq\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(\hat{u},\hat{v})
.

Proof.

Existence and uniqueness follows from the direct methods in the calculus of variations, applied to the functional
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon,\tau}
in
[\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})]^{2}
.
Just notice that

is non-negative (as
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]

that

is convex in

that

,
and that

is strictly jointly convex in

Coercivity on

follows thanks to the control on the second moments of

and

by

.
Concerning the inequality (

by

being a minimizer, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u^{*},v^{*})+\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{2}\big((u^{*},v^{*}),(\hat{u},\hat{v})\big)^{2}}{2\tau}=\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon,\tau}\big((u^{*},v^{*})\big|(\hat{u},\hat{v})\big)\leq\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon,\tau}\big((\hat{u},\hat{v})\big|(\hat{u},\hat{v})\big)=\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(\hat{u},\hat{v}),

which proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 4.5

We only consider the

Let

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho u^{*}\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

\Omega
to
\rho

For

-conjugate potentials

\displaystyle|\nabla\tilde{\varphi}_{u}^{\delta}|\leq R\quad\text{a.e. on $\mathbb{R}^{d}$}.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{u}^{\delta}(x)u^{*}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi_{u}^{\delta}(y)\hat{u}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{W}_{2}(v^{*},\hat{v})^{2}\right)+\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u^{*},v^{*})

(4.15)

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\varphi_{u}^{\delta}}{\tau}\frac{u^{\delta}-u^{*}}{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{\delta}\big(\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u^{\delta},v^{*})-\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u^{*},v^{*})\big).

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{u}^{0}(x)=\tilde{\varphi}_{u}(x):=\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}-\varphi_{u}(x)\quad\text{for $u^{*}{\mathcal{L}^{d}}$-a.e. $x$}.

the bound (

Lemma 4.7
.

With a constant
C
independent of
(\hat{u},\hat{v})
, there holds

(4.21)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\big|\nabla[F^{\prime}(u^{*})]_{F^{\prime}(1)}\big|^{2}+\big|\nabla[G^{\prime}(v^{*})]_{G^{\prime}(1)}\big|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\Big(1+\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}(\hat{u},\hat{v})-\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}(u^{*},v^{*})}{\tau}\Big),

where
[z]_{k}\coloneqq\min\{k,z\}
is the cut-off at the value
k
.

Proof.

The proof uses the method of flow interchange,
which estimates the effect of variations
of a Yosida-regularized
non-convex
functional
along the gradient flow of an auxiliary
convex
functional.
The method has been introduced in
[
31
]
,
unifying several similar ideas from the literature, see e.g.
[
25
,
23
]
.

For all
s>0
, define perturbations
(U_{s},V_{s})\in[\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})]^{2}
of
(U_{0},V_{0})\coloneqq(u^{*},v^{*})
as follows:

\displaystyle U_{s}\coloneqq\mathcal{K}_{s}\ast u^{*},\ V_{s}\coloneqq\mathcal{K}_{s}\ast v^{*}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{K}_{s}(z)=(4\pi s)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4s}\right).

Since
\mathcal{K}_{s}(z)
is the fundamental solution of the heat equation, it is well-known that
(s,x)\mapsto U_{s}(x)
and
(s,x)\mapsto V_{s}(x)
are
C^{\infty}
-smooth on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
with

(4.22)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}U_{s}=\Delta U_{s},\quad\partial_{s}V_{s}=\Delta V_{s}

in the classical sense, and that
U_{s}\to u^{*}
and
V_{s}\to v^{*}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Moreover,
(U_{s})_{s>0}
and
(V_{s})_{s>0}
— considered as flows on
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
— satisfy the (EVI
0
), see (
2.9
).

We perform a detailed comparison of the
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon,\tau}
-scores of
(U_{s},V_{s})
and of
(u^{*},v^{*})
.
By minimality,
we know that
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon,\tau}\big((u^{*},v^{*})\big|(\hat{u},\hat{v})\big)\leq\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon,\tau}\big((U_{r},V_{r})\big|(\hat{u},\hat{v})\big)
;
consequently, for each
\sigma>0
,

(4.23)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u^{*},v^{*})-\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(U_{\sigma},V_{\sigma})}{\sigma}\leq\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(\frac{\mathbf{W}_{2}(U_{\sigma},\hat{u})^{2}-\mathbf{W}_{2}(u^{*},\hat{u})^{2}}{\sigma}+\frac{\mathbf{W}_{2}(V_{\sigma},\hat{v})^{2}-\mathbf{W}_{2}(v^{*},\hat{v})^{2}}{\sigma}\right).

We consider the limes superior as
\sigma\downarrow 0
on both sides.
On the right-hand side, the (EVI
0
) from (
2.9
) is applicable and yields

(4.24)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\sigma\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u^{*},v^{*})-\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(U_{\sigma},V_{\sigma})}{\sigma}\leq\frac{\mathbf{H}(\hat{u})-\mathbf{H}(u^{*})}{\tau}+\frac{\mathbf{H}(\hat{v})-\mathbf{H}(v^{*})}{\tau}=\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}(\hat{u},\hat{v})-\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}(u^{*},v^{*})}{\tau}.

For estimation on the left-hand side, we use the heat equation (
4.22
).
By regularity and convexity of
H_{\varepsilon}
, it easily follows that
s\mapsto\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(U_{s},V_{s})
is continuous at
\sigma=0^{+}
.
Thanks to smoothness for
\sigma>0
, we can now write

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u^{*},v^{*})-\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(U_{\sigma},V_{\sigma})}{\sigma}=\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{0}^{\sigma}\partial_{s}\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(U_{s},V_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

by means of the fundamental theorem of calculus.
Next, using also smoothness in
x
and the convexity estimate (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle-\partial_{s}\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(U_{s},V_{s})

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big([\partial_{u}H_{\varepsilon}(U_{s},V_{s})+\Phi]\Delta U_{s}+[\partial_{v}H_{\varepsilon}(U_{s},V_{s})+\Psi]\Delta V_{s}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\nabla\partial_{u}H_{\varepsilon}(U_{s},V_{s})\cdot\nabla U_{s}+\nabla\partial_{v}H_{\varepsilon}(U_{s},V_{s})\cdot\nabla V_{s}-\Delta\Phi\,U_{s}-\Delta\Psi\,V_{s}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x_{j}}U_{s}\\
\partial_{x_{j}}V_{s}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\mathrm{D}^{2}H_{\varepsilon}(U_{s},V_{s})\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x_{j}}U_{s}\\
\partial_{x_{j}}V_{s}\end{pmatrix}-dM\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(U_{s}+V_{s})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x_{j}}U_{s}\\
\partial_{x_{j}}V_{s}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}F^{\prime\prime}(U_{s})&0\\
0&G^{\prime\prime}(V_{s})\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x_{j}}U_{s}\\
\partial_{x_{j}}V_{s}\end{pmatrix}-2dM

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2B}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[\big|\nabla[F^{\prime}(U_{s})]_{F^{\prime}(1)}\big|^{2}+\big|\nabla[G^{\prime}(V_{s})]_{G^{\prime}(1)}\big|^{2}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x-2dM,

where
B=\max_{0\leq r\leq 1}F^{\prime\prime}(r)
,
since by monotonicity of
F^{\prime}
, we have a.e.

\displaystyle\big|\nabla[F^{\prime}(U_{s})]_{F^{\prime}(1)}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}F^{\prime\prime}(U_{s})^{2}|\nabla U_{s}|^{2}&\text{if $0\leq U_{s}<1$},\\
0&\text{if $U_{s}\geq 1$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq BF^{\prime\prime}(U_{s})|\nabla U_{s}|^{2}.

In combination with (
4.24
),
we have

(4.25)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\sigma\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2B\sigma}\int_{0}^{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[\big|\nabla[F^{\prime}(U_{s})]_{F^{\prime}(1)}\big|^{2}+\big|\nabla[G^{\prime}(V_{s})]_{G^{\prime}(1)}\big|^{2}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}(\hat{u},\hat{v})-\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}(u^{*},v^{*})}{\tau}+2dM.

In addition, observe that, thanks to the at most linear growth of
F^{\prime}
near zero,

[F^{\prime}(U_{s})]_{F^{\prime}(1)}
is uniformly controlled in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by the
L^{1}
-norm of
U_{s}
, which is one.
With (
4.25
) it now follows that
[F^{\prime}(U_{\sigma})]_{F^{\prime}(1)}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

at least along some sequence with
\sigma\downarrow 0
.
By Rellich’s lemma, one may assume strong convergence of that sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
and thus identify the limit as
[F^{\prime}(u^{*})]_{F^{\prime}(1)}
, since
U_{s}\to u^{*}
strongly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
s\downarrow 0
.
By Alaoglu’s theorem,
F^{\prime}(U_{\sigma})
converges weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
F^{\prime}(u^{*})
,
and by lower semi-continuity of norms, we finally obtain
from (
4.25
)

\frac{1}{2B}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[\big|\nabla[F^{\prime}(u^{*})]_{F^{\prime}(1)}\big|^{2}+\big|\nabla[G^{\prime}(v^{*})]_{G^{\prime}(1)}\big|^{2}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}(\hat{u},\hat{v})-\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}(u^{*},v^{*})}{\tau}+2dM,

which immediately yields the claim (
4.21
).
∎

Proof of Proposition

Combine (

) and (

4.4.
Definition of auxiliary functionals

In this section and the next, we lay the the basis for the proof of Proposition
4.4
in Section

.

We start with an alternative representation of the functionals
\mathbf{L}_{1}
and
\mathbf{L}_{2}
defined in (
4.8

Recalling (
4.2

(4.26)

\begin{split}\mathbf{L}_{1}(u)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[F(u)+(\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u})u\big]\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[F(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+(\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u})\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x,\\
\mathbf{L}_{2}(v)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[G(v)+(\Psi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{v})v\big]\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[G(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+(\Psi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{v})\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Notice that in both lines, the second integral is simply a normalization depending on
\varepsilon
but not on
u
or
v
.
The equivalence of the first formula above to the definition of
\mathbf{L}_{1}
in (

by using — in that order — the fact that
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\{\Phi\geq U_{\varepsilon}\}

then the definition of

next the first Euler–Lagrange equation from (

in combination with the identity
\Phi-U_{\varepsilon}=(\Phi-U_{\varepsilon})_{+}-(U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi)_{+}

and finally equality of mass of

\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
. In this way, we find

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[d_{F}(u|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})(\Phi-U_{\varepsilon})_{+}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[F(u)-F(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\big((\Phi-U_{\varepsilon})_{+}-F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\big)\big]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[F(u)-F(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\big(\Phi-U_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big)\big]\,\mathrm{d}x

The second formula in (

) is justified analogously. Note that in view of (

),
the relation between

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u,v)-\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})=\mathbf{L}(u,v)+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[h(u,v)-h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})-\big(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\bar{\Theta}_{u}-(v-\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\bar{\Theta}_{v}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x.

and

and

independent of

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi u\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\mathbf{L}_{1}(u)+C,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi v\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\mathbf{L}_{2}(v)+C,

\mathbf{L}(u,v)\leq 2\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u,v)+C,\quad\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u,v)\leq 2\mathbf{L}(u,v)+C.

Next, we introduce dissipation functionals that accompany

(4.30)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{1}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\big|\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(u)+\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\mathbf{D}_{2}(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\big|\nabla\big[G^{\prime}(v)+\Psi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{v}\big]\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

4.5.
An estimate by Bregman distances

The sole purpose of this section is to show Lemma
4.9
below,
which becomes essential for the estimate of “garbage terms” in the proof of Proposition

.
The (technical) proof of the lemma heavily uses
2
-degeneracy and
2
-boundedness of
(F,G,h)
.

Lemma 4.9
.

There is a constant
\kappa
independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]

such that the estimate

(4.32)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u+v)\big[\omega\big(F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big)-\omega\big(F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}),G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big)\big]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\kappa\mathbf{L}(u,v)

with
\mathbf{L}(u,v)<\infty
and for
\omega\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
being any of the following four functions:

\partial_{\rho}\theta_{u}
,
\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}
,
\partial_{\rho}\theta_{v}
, or
\partial_{\eta}\theta_{v}
.

Proof.

We shall derive (
4.32
) as a consequence of a pointwise estimate on the integrand,
namely

(4.33)

\displaystyle(u+v)\big[\omega\big(F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big)-\omega\big(F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}),G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big)\big]^{2}\leq\kappa\big(d_{F}(u|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+d_{G}(v|\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big),

where
d_{F}
and
d_{G}
were introduced in (
4.7
).
In view of the definition of
\mathbf{L}
in (
4.8
)–(
4.9
),
an integration in
x
yields the claim (
4.32
).

We now prove (
4.33
), only with
u
instead of
(u+v)
(as the case with
v
instead of
(u+v)
is analogous). Let
\bar{U}
be such that
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\leq\bar{U}
for all
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
.
We distinguish three cases.

Case 1:

u>3\bar{U}
.
By
2
-boundedness and
2
-degeneracy of
(F,G,h)
, we have

\displaystyle u\big[\omega\big(F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big)-\omega\big(F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}),G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big)\big]^{2}\leq 4A^{2}u,

see Remark
1.1
(3). The multiple of
u
on the right-hand side can be estimated by a multiple of
d_{F}(u|\bar{u})
as follows: thanks to convexity of
F
,

\displaystyle d_{F}(u|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq F(2\bar{U})+(u-2\bar{U})F^{\prime}(2\bar{U})-\big[F(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\big]

\displaystyle\geq(u-2\bar{U})F^{\prime}(2\bar{U})+(2\bar{U}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})-(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})

(4.34)

\displaystyle=(u-2\bar{U})\big[F^{\prime}(2\bar{U})-F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\big]\geq(u-2\bar{U})\big[F^{\prime}(2\bar{U})-F^{\prime}(\bar{U})\big].

By strict convexity,
F^{\prime}(2\bar{U})-F^{\prime}(\bar{U})>0
,
and with
u>3\bar{U}
, we clearly have
4A^{2}u\leq\kappa d_{F}(u|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})

for an appropriate constant
\kappa
independent of
u
and
\varepsilon
.

Case 2:

0\leq u\leq 3\bar{U}
and
v>3\bar{U}
.
Using again the global bound
A
on
\omega
,
we obtain

\displaystyle u\big[\omega\big(F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big)-\omega\big(F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}),G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big)\big]^{2}\leq 4A^{2}\bar{U},

i.e., the left-hand side is bounded by an expression that is independent of
u
,
v
and
\varepsilon
.
Clearly, this expression is estimated by
\kappa d_{G}(v|\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})

with an appropriate
\kappa
independent of
v
;
this follows in analogy to the estimate in the first case above.

Case 3:

0\leq u,v\leq 3\bar{U}
.
As a first step, we show that, for an appropriate constant
L
independent of
\varepsilon
, we have

(4.35)

\displaystyle[F^{\prime}(u)-F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})]^{2}\leq Ld_{F}(u|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})

and correspondingly

[G^{\prime}(v)-G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})]^{2}\leq Ld_{G}(v|\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}).

Considering both sides of (

) as functions in the variable
u
, we obviously have equality in the case
u=\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
. Using the definition of

, we find

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}u}[F^{\prime}(u)-F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})]^{2}=2[F^{\prime}(u)-F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})]F^{\prime\prime}(u)=2F^{\prime\prime}(u)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}u}d_{F}(u|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})

for all

. Notice that this expression, by convexity of

, is negative for

and positive for

. With the help of hypothesis (

) and the convexity of

C

Hence, with the choice

, we conclude that the left-hand side of (

and increases slower on

.

Next, define
\rho_{s}\coloneqq sF^{\prime}(u)+(1-s)F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
and

Then

\displaystyle\big[\omega\big(F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big)-\omega\big(F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}),G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big)\big]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\big[\big(F^{\prime}(u)-F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\big)\partial_{\rho}\omega(\rho_{s},\eta_{s})+\big(G^{\prime}(v)-G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big)\partial_{\eta}\omega(\rho_{s},\eta_{s})\big]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(\sup_{0\leq\mu,\nu\leq 3\bar{U}}\big|\mathrm{D}\omega\big|\bigg)^{2}\big[\big(F^{\prime}(u)-F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\big)^{2}+\big(G^{\prime}(v)-G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big)^{2}\big].

The supremum above is a finite quantity
B
,

.
Therefore, with

from (

\displaystyle u\big[\omega\big(F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big)-\omega\big(F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}),G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big)\big]^{2}\leq 6\bar{U}BL\big(d_{F}(u|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+d_{G}(v|\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big),

proving the pointwise estimate (

) also in the final case.
∎

4.6.
Proof of the core inequality

Finally, we prove Proposition

.
Again, the additional hypothesis (

) enters only indirectly via Lemma

.

Proof of Proposition
4.4
under the additional hypothesis (
4.11
).

Let
P_{F}(r)=rF^{\prime}(r)-F(r)
for

.
Recall that
(\varphi_{u},\psi_{u})
is the optimal pair of

-conjugate potentials for the transport from

to
\hat{u}
.
Since
\mathbf{L}_{1}
is displacement convex, the following “above tangent formula” holds,
see e.g.
[

, Proposition 5.29 & Theorem 5.30]

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{1}(\hat{u})-\mathbf{L}_{1}(u^{*})\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P_{F}(u^{*})\Delta^{\text{ac}}\varphi_{u}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{*}\nabla[\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u}]\cdot\nabla\varphi_{u}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
\Delta^{\text{ac}}\varphi_{u}
is the absolutely continuous part of the signed measure
defined by the distributional Laplacian
\Delta\varphi_{u}
.
Thanks to the regularity of
u^{*}
, we may re-write the first integral on the right-hand side using integration by parts.
Indeed, observe that

\nabla P_{F}(u^{*})\cdot\nabla\varphi_{u}=u^{*}\nabla F^{\prime}(u^{*})\cdot\nabla\varphi_{u}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

since
\nabla F^{\prime}(u^{*})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};u^{*}{\mathcal{L}^{d}})
by Lemma
4.6

and
\nabla\varphi_{u}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};u^{*}{\mathcal{L}^{d}})
in view of (
2.5
).
Now, since
P_{F}\geq 0
by convexity of
F
,
and since
\Delta^{ac}\varphi_{u}\geq\Delta\varphi_{u}
because
\varphi_{u}
is semi-concave,
we have

(4.36)

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{1}(\hat{u})-\mathbf{L}_{1}(u^{*})\geq-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{*}\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(u^{*})+\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]\cdot\nabla\varphi_{u}\,\mathrm{d}x=\tau Z_{1}(u^{*},v^{*}),

where
Z_{1}
can be made more explicit by substitution of the potential
\varphi_{u}
from (

):

\displaystyle Z_{1}(u,v)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\,\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(u)+\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]\cdot\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(u)+\Phi+\varepsilon\partial_{u}h(u,v)\big]\,\mathrm{d}x.

We estimate

using a combination of the previously shown results.
For brevity, we use — only in the calculations below —
in addition to
\bar{\Theta}_{u}
and
\bar{\Theta}_{v}
introduced in (

)
the notations

\displaystyle\Theta_{u}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\theta_{u}\big(F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big)=\partial_{u}h(u,v),

\displaystyle\Theta_{u,\rho}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\partial_{\rho}\theta_{u}\big(F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big)=\frac{\partial_{uu}h(u,v)}{F^{\prime\prime}(u)},

and so on. First, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

\displaystyle Z_{1}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\left|\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(u)+\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\,\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(u)+\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]\cdot\nabla\big[\Theta_{u}-\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\mathbf{D}_{1}(u)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\left|\nabla\big[\Theta_{u}-\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Inside the last integral, we have

\displaystyle\nabla\big[\Theta_{u}-\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]

\displaystyle=\Theta_{u,\rho}\nabla F^{\prime}(u)+\Theta_{u,\eta}\nabla G^{\prime}(v)-\bar{\Theta}_{u,\rho}\nabla F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})-\bar{\Theta}_{u,\eta}\nabla G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\Theta_{u,\rho}\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(u)+\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]+\Theta_{u,\eta}\nabla\big[G^{\prime}(v)+\Psi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{v}\big]

\displaystyle\quad-\Theta_{u,\rho}\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]-\Theta_{u,\eta}\nabla\big[G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\Psi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{v}\big]

\displaystyle\quad+\big(\Theta_{u,\rho}-\bar{\Theta}_{u,\rho}\big)\nabla F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+\big(\Theta_{u,\eta}-\bar{\Theta}_{u,\eta}\big)\nabla G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}).

The third and the fourth term above can be simplified using
that by combination of the Euler–Lagrange system (
3.4
)
with the identity
\Phi-U_{\varepsilon}=(\Phi-U_{\varepsilon})_{+}-(U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi)_{+}
,
one has

\displaystyle\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]=-\nabla(\Phi-U_{\varepsilon})_{+},\quad\nabla\big[G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})+\Psi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{v}\big]=-\nabla(\Psi-V_{\varepsilon})_{+}.

This yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\big|\nabla\big[\Theta_{u}-\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.37)

\displaystyle\leq 6\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\Theta_{u,\rho}^{2}\big|\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(u)+\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+6\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\Theta_{u,\eta}^{2}\big|\nabla\big[G^{\prime}(v)+\Psi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{v}\big]\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.38)

\displaystyle\quad+6\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\Theta_{u,\rho}^{2}\big|\nabla(\Phi-U_{\varepsilon})_{+}\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+6\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\Theta_{u,\eta}^{2}\big|\nabla(\Psi-V_{\varepsilon})_{+}\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.39)

\displaystyle\quad+6\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\big(\Theta_{u,\rho}-\bar{\Theta}_{u,\rho}\big)^{2}|\nabla F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+6\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\big(\Theta_{u,\eta}-\bar{\Theta}_{u,\eta}\big)^{2}|\nabla G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For further estimation, we observe that
2
-boundedness and
2
-degeneracy of
(F,G,h)
imply

(4.40)

\displaystyle|\Theta_{u,\rho}|\leq A\min\big\{1,F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big\},\quad|\Theta_{u,\eta}|\leq A\min\big\{1,F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big\},

see (
1.19
). The first integral in (

) is now easily estimated using that thanks to (

),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\Theta_{u,\rho}^{2}\big|\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(u)+\Phi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{u}\big]\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq A^{2}\mathbf{D}_{1}(u).

For estimation of the second integral in (
4.37
),
we use instead that
(F,G,h)
satisfies the swap condition (
1.15
):
thus
u\Theta_{u,\eta}^{2}\leq W^{2}v

and consequently

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\Theta_{v,\eta}^{2}\big|\nabla\big[G^{\prime}(v)+\Psi+\varepsilon\bar{\Theta}_{v}\big]\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq W^{2}\mathbf{D}_{2}(v).

For estimation of the first integral in (
4.38
), two ingredients are needed.
First, recall that
|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\leq\frac{2M^{2}}{\Lambda}\Phi
by (
1.17
),
and conclude that on
\{\Phi>U_{\varepsilon}\}
:

\displaystyle\big|\nabla(\Phi-U_{\varepsilon})_{+}\big|^{2}=|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\leq\frac{2M^{2}}{\Lambda}\Phi=\frac{2M^{2}}{\Lambda}U_{\varepsilon}+\frac{2M^{2}}{\Lambda}(\Phi-U_{\varepsilon})_{+}\,.

Second, we claim that there is a constant

\displaystyle u\Theta_{u,\rho}^{2}\leq BF(u).

For

.
For

) and (
1.8
),
there are constants

are satisfied. Therefore, employing also (

\displaystyle u\Theta_{u,\rho}^{2}\leq uA^{2}F^{\prime}(u)\leq\frac{C_{0}A^{2}}{c_{0}}F(u).

Now we combine these ingredients, recalling again (

) and
bearing in mind that the integral is actually an integral on

only,
where

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\Theta_{u,\rho}^{2}\big|\nabla(\Phi-U_{\varepsilon})_{+}\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2M^{2}BU_{\varepsilon}}{\Lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d_{F}(u|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2M^{2}A^{2}}{\Lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(\Phi-U_{\varepsilon})_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x

The second integral in (

Finally, the integrals in (

.
We combine this with the boundedness of

and

by Proposition

, we have that

,
and that

\displaystyle|\nabla F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})|\leq\tilde{B},\quad|\nabla G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})|\leq\tilde{B}

a.e. on

We thus obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\big(\Theta_{u,\rho}-\bar{\Theta}_{u,\rho}\big)^{2}\big|\nabla F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\big(\Theta_{u,\eta}-\bar{\Theta}_{u,\eta}\big)^{2}\big|\nabla G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2\tilde{B}^{2}\kappa\mathbf{L}(u,v).

To summarize so far, we have shown that, with a suitable constant

\displaystyle Z_{1}(u,v)\geq\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big[1+A^{2}\big]\right)\mathbf{D}_{1}(u)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}W^{2}\mathbf{D}_{2}(v)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}C\mathbf{L}(u,v).

This finishes our estimate on

.
The pendant of (

\displaystyle Z_{1}(u,v)+Z_{2}(u,v)\geq\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big[1+A^{2}+W^{2}\big]\right)(\mathbf{D}_{1}(u)+\mathbf{D}_{2}(v))-\varepsilon C\mathbf{L}(u,v).

With an application of (

Lemma 4.10
.

As

\Gamma
-converge
to

Proof.

We verify the definition of
\Gamma

first, the “liminf-property” is a direct consequence of

\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}
and that of
\mathbf{W}_{2}

Second, a recovery sequence

(\tilde{u}_{R},\tilde{v}_{R}):=(u,v)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{R}(\tilde{u}_{R},\tilde{v}_{R})-\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon,\tau}\big((u,v)\big|(\hat{u},\hat{v})\big)=\frac{1}{2\tau}\big[\big(\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,\hat{u}_{R})^{2}-\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,\hat{u})^{2}\big)+\big(\mathbf{W}_{2}(v,\hat{v}_{R})^{2}-\mathbf{W}_{2}(v,\hat{v})^{2}\big)\big],

which tends to zero for

since
\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,\hat{u}_{R})\to\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,\hat{u})
and
\mathbf{W}_{2}(v,\hat{v}_{R})\to\mathbf{W}_{2}(v,\hat{v})
follows
by convergence of
\hat{u}_{R}
and
\hat{v}_{R}
in

, and convergence of their second moments, to the respective limits.

Concerning the sublevel set
\mathcal{E}_{R}\leq\overline{\mathcal{E}}
:
By definition of
\mathcal{E}_{R}
, it contains
(\hat{u}_{R},\hat{v}_{R})
and is thus non-empty.
Moreover, any
(u,v)
in that sublevel satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u,v)\leq\overline{\mathcal{E}},\quad\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,\hat{u}_{R})^{2}\leq 2\tau\overline{\mathcal{E}},\quad\mathbf{W}_{2}(v,\hat{v}_{R})^{2}\leq 2\tau\overline{\mathcal{E}}.

and

and

in

.
The second estimate provides an
R
-uniform bound on the second moment of

and

let

\hat{u}_{R}

recall that the second moments of the

-uniformly bounded by construction.
The argument for

∎

As a consequence of Lemma

, and by uniqueness of the minimizer

and

-equation in (

).
The bound on
|R_{u}|+|R_{v}|
is preserved thanks to (
4.41
) and lower semicontinuity of
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}
with respect to narrow convergence.
∎

5.
Existence of weak solutions

The Yosida-regularized energy functional
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon,\tau}
is now used to obtain
a time-discrete approximation
(u_{\tau}^{n},v_{\tau}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of the solution to (
1.1
)
for given initial data
u(0)=u_{0}
,
v(0)=v_{0}
with finite energy
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0})<\infty

by means of the
minimizing movement scheme
.
Inductively, define
(u_{\tau}^{0},v_{\tau}^{0})\coloneqq(u_{0},v_{0})
,
and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
(u_{\tau}^{n},v_{\tau}^{n})
be the minimizer
— which exists and is unique by Lemma
4.1
—
of the functional

\displaystyle[\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})]^{2}\ni(u,v)\mapsto\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon,\tau}\big((u,v)\big|(u_{\tau}^{n-1},v_{\tau}^{n-1})\big).

Further, define the piecewise constant “interpolations”
\tilde{u}_{\tau},\tilde{v}_{\tau}\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(depending of course on
\varepsilon
)
in the usual way:

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)=u_{\tau}^{n},\ \tilde{v}_{\tau}(t)=v_{\tau}^{n}\quad\text{for $(n-1)\tau<t\leq n\tau$}.

The result of this section is the following convergence.

Proposition 5.1
.

For every
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
, the interpolations
\tilde{u}_{\tau}
,
\tilde{v}_{\tau}
converge, for a suitable sequence
\tau\downarrow 0
, to Hölder-continuous limit curves
u_{*},v_{*}\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
weakly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
at every
t\geq 0
:
Moreover,
F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}),G^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{\tau})
converge to the respective limits
F^{\prime}(u_{*}),G^{\prime}(v_{*})
,
weakly in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and strongly in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for any
T>0
.
Furthermore, the limits are weak solutions to (
1.1
) in the following sense:

(5.1)

\begin{split}0&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(u_{*}\partial_{t}\xi-u_{*}\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(u_{*})+\Phi+\varepsilon\partial_{u}h(u_{*},v_{*})\big]\cdot\nabla\xi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,\\
0&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(v_{*}\partial_{t}\xi-v_{*}\nabla\big[G^{\prime}(v_{*})+\Psi+\varepsilon\partial_{v}h(u_{*},v_{*})\big]\cdot\nabla\xi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

holds for arbitrary test functions
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

With the solution
u_{*},v_{*}\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
from Proposition
5.1
we have shown the existence of a transient solution to the initial value problem for (
1.1
), as stated in Theorem
1.4
.

5.1.
Multi-step estimates

We next prove several
\tau
-independent
estimates for
(\tilde{u}_{\tau},\tilde{v}_{\tau})
, which in the subsequent section then allows us to establish convergence for a sequence
\tau\downarrow 0
. We start by recalling the classical estimate that follows directly from the variational construction.

Lemma 5.2
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(5.2)

\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\tau}^{n},v_{\tau}^{n})+\frac{1}{2\tau}\big(\mathbf{W}_{2}(u_{\tau}^{n},u_{\tau}^{n-1})^{2}+\mathbf{W}_{2}(v_{\tau}^{n},v_{\tau}^{n-1})^{2}\big)\leq\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\tau}^{n-1},v_{\tau}^{n-1})

and for each
N\in\mathbb{N}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\tau}^{N},v_{\tau}^{N})+\frac{1}{2\tau}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big(\mathbf{W}_{2}(u_{\tau}^{n},u_{\tau}^{n-1})^{2}+\mathbf{W}_{2}(v_{\tau}^{n},v_{\tau}^{n-1})^{2}\big)\leq\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0}).

Proof.

The first inequality (
5.2
) rephrases (
4.3
).
Summing these inequalities for
n=1,2,\ldots,N
, we then end up with the second inequality (
5.3
).
∎

The following three conclusions of Lemma
5.2
are important in the following:

•

The values of
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\tau}^{n},v_{\tau}^{n})
are monotonically decreasing in
n
,
and in particular bounded by
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0})
.
For
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
, the hypothesis (
1.13
) then implies a uniform bound on
F(u_{\tau}^{n})
and
G(v_{\tau}^{n})
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[F(u_{\tau}^{n})+G(v_{\tau}^{n})\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0}).

•

Another consequence of energy monotonicity: for
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
we obtain,
thanks to non-negativity of
H_{\varepsilon}
,
and to the lower bounds on
\Phi
and
\Psi
by quadratic functions, see (
1.16
), a uniform bound on the second moments of
u_{\tau}^{n}
and
v_{\tau}^{n}
,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}\big(u_{\tau}^{n}+v_{\tau}^{n}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2|\underline{x}_{\Phi}|^{2}+2|\underline{x}_{\Psi}|^{2}+\frac{4}{\Lambda}\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0}).

•

By non-negativity of
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}
,
one can pass for
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
to the limit
N\to\infty
in (
5.3
) to obtain

(5.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big(\mathbf{W}_{2}(u_{\tau}^{n},u_{\tau}^{n-1})^{2}+\mathbf{W}_{2}(v_{\tau}^{n},v_{\tau}^{n-1})^{2}\big)\leq 2\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0}).

This gives rise to the following uniform estimate on the modulus of quasi-continuity.

Lemma 5.3
.

There is a
\tau
-independent constant
C
such that for any
s,t\geq 0
,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{2}\big(\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t),\tilde{u}_{\tau}(s)\big)\leq C\sqrt{|t-s|+\tau},\quad\mathbf{W}_{2}\big(\tilde{v}_{\tau}(t),\tilde{v}_{\tau}(s)\big)\leq C\sqrt{|t-s|+\tau}.

Proof.

Assume
0\leq s<t
, and let
\underline{n},\overline{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
be
such that
(\underline{n}-1)\tau<s\leq\underline{n}\tau
and
(\overline{n}-1)\tau<t\leq\overline{n}\tau
,
i.e.,
\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)=u_{\tau}^{\overline{n}}
and
\tilde{u}_{\tau}(s)=u_{\tau}^{\underline{n}}
,
with
(\overline{n}-\underline{n})\tau\leq(t-s)+\tau
.
If
\overline{n}=\underline{n}
, then (
5.7
) trivially holds.
Otherwise, it follows from (
5.6
)
via the triangle inequality for
\mathbf{W}_{2}
and Hölder’s inequality for sums that

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{2}\big(\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t),\tilde{u}_{\tau}(s)\big)\leq\sum_{n=\underline{n}+1}^{\overline{n}}\mathbf{W}_{2}(u_{\tau}^{n},u_{\tau}^{n-1})

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big(\mathbf{W}_{2}(u_{\tau}^{n},u_{\tau}^{n-1})^{2}\Bigg)^{1/2}\Bigg(\sum_{n=\underline{n}+1}^{\overline{n}}\tau\Bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0})}\sqrt{(t-s)+\tau}.

This proves the first inequality in (
5.7
),
the second follows in the analogous way.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

There is a
\tau
-independent constant
C
such that,
for each
T>0
,

(5.8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(|\nabla F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})|^{2}+|\nabla G^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{\tau})|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(1+T+\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0})).

Proof.

Assume
T=N\tau
for simplicity.
Apply estimate (
4.6
) to
(\hat{u},\hat{v})=(u_{\tau}^{n-1},v_{\tau}^{n-1})
and
((u^{*},v^{*})=(u_{\tau}^{n},v_{\tau}^{n})
,
and sum over
n=1,\ldots,N
.
This yields

\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[|\nabla F^{\prime}(u_{\tau}^{n})|^{2}+|\nabla G^{\prime}(v_{\tau}^{n})|^{2}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq CN\tau\big(1+\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0})\big)+C\big[\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0})-\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\tau}^{N},v_{\tau}^{N})+\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}(u_{0},v_{0})-\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}(u_{\tau}^{N},v_{\tau}^{N})\big].

The left-hand side of this inequality coincides with the left-hand side of (
5.8
).
On the right hand side, first observe that
CN\tau=CT
,
and that
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\tau}^{N},v_{\tau}^{N})
is positive and thus negligible.
To arrive at (
5.8
), it suffices to show that

\displaystyle-C\big(1+\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u,v)\big)\leq\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}(u,v)\leq C\big(1+\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u,v)\big).

The lower bound is easily obtained by combination of Lemma
B.1
from the Appendix
with the following estimate, that is a consequence of (
1.16
) and (
4.28
):

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[|x-\underline{x}_{\Phi}|^{2}u+|x-\underline{x}_{\Psi}|^{2}v\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[\Phi u+\Psi v\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C+\mathbf{H}_{\varepsilon}(u,v).

The control of
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}
from above is a simple consequence
of
u\log u\leq C(1+F(u))
and
v\log v\leq C(1+G(v))
thanks to the at least quadratic growth of
F
and
G
, combined with (
1.13
).
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
p,q>1
be such that

(5.9)

\displaystyle(d-2)p<2d\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}<1+\frac{2}{d}.

For each
T>0
, there is a
\tau
-independent constant
C_{T}
such that

(5.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})\|_{L^{p}}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{T}.

Proof.

Thanks to (
5.9
), we have that

(5.11)

\displaystyle\theta\coloneqq\frac{2d}{d+2}\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}<1\quad\text{and}\quad q\theta<2.

Therefore, by the Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})\|_{L^{p}}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq CT^{1-q\theta/2}\sup_{0<t<T}\|F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t))\|_{L^{1}}^{q(1-\theta)}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{q\theta/2}.

From (
5.8
) the
\tau
-uniform boundedness of the term with
\nabla F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})
follows.
For the other term, we first observe that
F^{\prime}(s)\leq C(s+F(s))
which for
s\geq 1
is a direct consequence of (
1.18
), while for
s\leq 1
, it is obtained from (
1.7
) in combination with the uniform boundedness of
F^{\prime\prime}(t)
for
t\in(0,1]
.
Since
\tilde{u}_{\tau}
is of unit mass, and because of the uniform bound (
5.4
) on
F(\tilde{u}_{\tau})
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
also
F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})
is bounded in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
and in
\tau
.
∎

5.2.
Convergence proofs

The statements of Proposition
5.1
are proven
in the three Lemmas
5.6
,
5.7
, and
5.8
below.

Lemma 5.6
.

For every
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
, there are curves
u_{*},v_{*}\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, Hölder continuous in
\mathbf{W}_{2}
,
such that, along a suitable sequence
\tau\downarrow 0
,
the interpolations
\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)
and
\tilde{v}_{\tau}(t)
converge to
u_{*}(t)
and
v_{*}(t)
, respectively,
weakly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, at every
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

This lemma is a consequence of the generalized Arzelà–Ascoli theorem
[
3
, Proposition 3.3.1]
.
Lemma
5.3
above provides
a uniform modulus of (quasi-)continuity for
\tilde{u}_{\tau}
and
\tilde{v}_{\tau}
in
\mathbf{W}_{2}
;
the topology induced by
\mathbf{W}_{2}
is stronger than the narrow one.
Further, the values of
\tilde{u}_{\tau}
and
\tilde{v}_{\tau}
belong to a narrowly compact set
thanks to the uniform boundedness of second moments (
5.5
).
The aforementioned proposition yields the narrow convergence along a sequence
\tau\downarrow 0

of
\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)
and
\tilde{v}_{\tau}(t)
to respective limits
u_{*}(t)
and
v_{*}(t)
for each
t\geq 0
, and
u_{*}
,
v_{*}
are continuous with respect to
\mathbf{W}_{2}
.
By lower semi-continuity of
\mathbf{W}_{2}
under narrow convergence,
the estimate (
5.7
) is inherited by the limits
u_{*}
,
v_{*}
in the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{2}\big(u_{*}(t),u_{*}(s)\big)\leq C|t-s|^{1/2},\quad\mathbf{W}_{2}\big(v_{*}(t),v_{*}(s)\big)\leq C|t-s|^{1/2},

which is the claimed Hölder continuity of
u_{*}
,
v_{*}
with respect to
\mathbf{W}_{2}
. Finally, the upgrade from narrow convergence of
\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)
and
\tilde{v}_{\tau}(t)
to weak convergence in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
after passage to a suitable subsequence
\tau\downarrow 0

is obtained from the boundedness of
F(\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t))
and
G(\tilde{v}_{\tau}(t))
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
see (
5.4
).
Indeed, since
F
and
G
are super-linear at infinity in view of (
1.6
), the Dunford–Pettis criterion applies.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

For every
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon^{*}]
and every
T>0
, we have along a suitable sequence
\tau\downarrow 0

(5.12)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{\tau}

\displaystyle\to u_{*}\quad\text{strongly in $L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})$},

(5.13)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})

\displaystyle\to F^{\prime}(u_{*})\quad\text{strongly in $L^{2}_{loc}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})$},

(5.14)

\displaystyle\nabla F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\nabla F^{\prime}(u_{*})\quad\text{weakly in $L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

Proof.

For the proof of (
5.12
),
we apply the generalized version
[
37
, Theorem 2]
of the Aubin–Lions Lemma.
On the Banach space
B\coloneqq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define the
normal coercive integrand
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u)=\|F^{\prime}(u)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}u\,\mathrm{d}x,

and the compatible map
g\colon B\times B\to[0,\infty]
by

\displaystyle g(u,u^{\prime})\coloneqq\mathbf{W}_{2}(u,u^{\prime}),

with the conventions
that
\mathcal{F}(u)=\infty
unless
u\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
F^{\prime}(u)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
and that
g(u,u^{\prime})=\infty
unless
u,u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Below we verify lower semi-continuity of
\mathcal{F}
and compactness of sublevel sets;
we further show that

(5.15)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T-h}g\big(\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t+h),\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\to 0\quad\text{uniformly in~$\tau$ as $h\downarrow 0$},

and that

(5.16)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{T}

with some constant
C_{T}
depending only on
T>0
.
In conclusion,
[
37
, Theorem 2]
then yields that
\tilde{u}(t)
converges in
B=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
in measure with respect to
t\in(0,T)
, along a sequence
\tau\downarrow 0
.
That limit necessarily coincides with
u_{*}
obtained in the proof of Lemma
5.6
above.

To prove lower semi-continuity of
\mathcal{F}
,
consider a sequence
(u_{n})
converging to
u_{*}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
(\mathcal{F}(u_{n}))
has a finite limit.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
u_{n}
even converges pointwise a.e..
Fatou’s lemma directly yields

(5.17)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}u\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}u_{n}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Further, by boundedness of the sequence
(F^{\prime}(u_{n}))
in the reflexive space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
a suitable subsequence has a weak
H^{1}
-limit
\nu_{*}
.
By Rellich’s theorem, this convergence is actually strong in
L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
and thus also pointwise a.e..
By a.e. pointwise convergence almost of
(u_{n})
and the continuity of
F^{\prime}
,
we conclude
\nu_{*}=F^{\prime}(u_{*})
.
Weak lower semi-continuity of norms implies

\displaystyle\|F^{\prime}(u_{*})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}=\|\nu_{*}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|F^{\prime}(u_{n})\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

and thus with (
5.17
) we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u_{*})\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{F}(u_{n}).

Concerning compactness, consider the
\bar{\mathcal{F}}
-sublevel set
S
of
\mathcal{F}
.
By boundedness of the
H^{1}
-norm of
F^{\prime}(u)
for all
u\in S
,
there is a sequence
(u_{n})
in
S
for which
F^{\prime}(u_{n})
converges strongly in
L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Since
F^{\prime}
has at least linear growth, also
(u_{n})
itself converges strongly in
L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
To show that this convergence is not just locally, observe that by interpolation,
there is a
p>2
such that
F^{\prime}(u)
is uniformly bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
u
in the sublevel
S
.
Using that
s^{p}\leq C(s+F^{\prime}(s)^{p})
for a suitable constant
C
thanks to the at least linear growth of
F^{\prime}
,
it follows that there is a
L^{p}
-uniform bound for the
u_{n}\in S
as well.
Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2(p-2)/(p-1)}u_{n}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\|u_{n}\|_{L^{p}}^{p/(p-1)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}u_{n}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{(p-2)/(p-1)},

and thus
(u_{n})
is tight and converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
This proves compactness.

The property (
5.15
) follows from (
5.7
).
More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T-h}g\big(\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t+h),\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq CT\big(\sqrt{h}+\sqrt{\tau}\big).

Next, the estimate (
5.16
) is a consequence of the a priori bound (
5.8
) on
\nabla F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})
,
estimate (
5.10
) with
p=q=2
,
and the moment control (
5.5
).
By means of
[
37
, Theorem 2]
, we obtain convergence of
\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)
to
u_{*}(t)
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
in measure with respect to
t\in[0,T]
, along a sequence
\tau\downarrow 0
.
Estimate (
5.10
) with
2=p<q<2+4/d

further yields a
\tau
-uniform control on
\|\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
in
L^{q}(0,T)
, since
F^{\prime}
has at least linear growth,
and thus uniform integrability to exponent two in time.
This finishes the proof of (
5.12
).

Next, we show the convergence (
5.13
) of
(F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}))
to
F^{\prime}(u_{*})
locally in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
By (
5.12
), we may assume without loss of generality that the chosen sequence
(\tilde{u}_{\tau})
converges pointwise to
u_{*}
a.e. on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
By continuity of
F^{\prime}
, the sequence
(F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}))
converges to
F^{\prime}(u_{*})
pointwise almost everywhere.
Moreover, estimate (
5.10
) with
2<p=q<2+2/d
provides

\tau
-uniform integrability of
F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
to exponent two.
Hence, by Vitali’s convergence theorem, we get convergence of
(F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}))
to
F^{\prime}(u_{*})
in
L^{2}_{loc}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

It remains to verify (
5.14
), i.e.,
the weak convergence of
(\nabla F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}))
to
\nabla F^{\prime}(u_{*})
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
In fact, weak

-convergence to
some
limit
\nabla\zeta
follows immediately
from the boundedness (
5.8
) and the local convergence (
5.13
) via Alaoglu’s theorem.
Using once again the local convergence of
(F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}))
to
F^{\prime}(u_{*})
we can identify the limit
\zeta
as
F^{\prime}(u_{*})
.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

The limits
(u_{*},v_{*})
obtained in Lemmas
5.6
&
5.7

satisfy the weak formulations (
5.1
) for every test function
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

By abuse of notation,
\tau
will always denote an element of the sequence
\tau\downarrow 0

along which
(\tilde{u}_{\tau},\tilde{v}_{\tau})
converges to
(u_{*},v_{*})
in the sense of Lemmas
5.6
&
5.7
.

Assume that the support of
\xi
lies in
(0,T)\times\Omega
, for some bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For each
t\in(\tau,T)
, let
n
be such that
(n-1)\tau<t\leq n\tau
,
and use
\zeta\coloneqq\xi(t;\cdot)
as test function in the first equation of (
4.4
) for that

.
Integrate these equations in
t\in(\tau,T)
.
The result can be written as:

(5.18)

\begin{split}&\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)-\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t-\tau)}{\tau}\xi(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t))+\Phi+\varepsilon\partial_{u}h(\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t),\tilde{v}_{\tau}(t))\big]\cdot\nabla\xi(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\tau}^{T}R_{u}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

where, thanks to (
4.5
),

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\tau}^{T}R_{u}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\tau\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sup_{0<t<T}\|\xi(t;\cdot)\|_{C^{2}}\big(\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\tau}^{n-1},v_{\tau}^{n-1})-\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\tau}^{n},v_{\tau}^{n})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\tau\sup_{0<t<T}\|\xi(t;\cdot)\|_{C^{2}}\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0},v_{0}),

and so
\int_{0}^{T}R_{u}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\to 0
as
\tau\downarrow 0
.
For the integral on the left-hand side of (
5.18
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)-\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t-\tau)}{\tau}\xi(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)\frac{\xi(t+\tau)-\xi(t)}{\tau}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for
\tau>0
sufficiently small (recall that the support of
\xi
is contained in
(0,T)\times\Omega
),
which in turn implies

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t)-\tilde{u}_{\tau}(t-\tau)}{\tau}\xi(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{*}(t)\partial_{t}\xi(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

thanks to the convergence of
\tilde{u}_{\tau}
to
u_{*}
in
L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
,
and the uniform convergence of difference quotients of
\xi
.

It remains to verify the convergence of the integral on the right-hand side of (
5.18
).
By the strong
L^{2}
-convergence (
5.12
) of
\tilde{u}_{\tau}
to
u_{*}
,
and since
\nabla\xi
is smooth and has compact support inside
(0,T)\times\Omega
,
it suffices to verify weak convergence of

\displaystyle\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})+\varepsilon\partial_{u}h(\tilde{u}_{\tau},\tilde{v}_{\tau})\big]\rightharpoonup\nabla\big[F^{\prime}(u_{*})+\varepsilon\partial_{u}h(u_{*},v_{*})\big]

in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
.
But this is clear:
on the one hand,
F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})
and
G^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{\tau})
converge weakly in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, see (
5.14
).
On the other hand, recalling the
2
-boundedness of
(F,G,h)
,
the local
L^{2}
-convergence (
5.13
) of
F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})
and
G^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{\tau})

implies convergence in measure of
\partial_{\rho}\theta_{u}\big(F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}),G^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{\tau})\big)
and of
\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}\big(F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}),G^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{\tau})\big)
.
By boundedness and continuity of the derivatives of
\theta_{u}
,
the weak convergence of
\nabla F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})
and
\nabla G^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{\tau})

in
[L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})]^{d}
is inherited by

\nabla\partial_{u}h(\tilde{u}_{\tau},\tilde{v}_{\tau})=\partial_{\rho}\theta_{u}\big(F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}),G^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{\tau})\big)\nabla F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau})+\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}\big(F^{\prime}(\tilde{u}_{\tau}),G^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{\tau})\big)\nabla G^{\prime}(\tilde{v}_{\tau}).\qed

6.
Convergence to equilibrium

In preparation of the proof of Theorem
1.5
,
we provide an adapted version of the Csiszar–Kullback inequality for
\mathbf{L}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

There is a constant
C
, independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,\bar{\varepsilon}]
,
such that for all
u,v\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mathbf{L}_{1}(u)<\infty
and
\mathbf{L}_{2}(v)<\infty
, there hold

(6.1)

\displaystyle\|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\leq C\mathbf{L}_{1}(u),\quad\|v-\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\leq C\mathbf{L}_{2}(v).

Proof.

It suffices to prove the first inequality in (
6.1
).
The point of departure is that both
u
and
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
have unit mass,
and therefore

(6.2)

\displaystyle\|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}=2\int_{\{u<\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\}}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-u)\,\mathrm{d}x.

It is thus sufficient to estimate the integral of
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-u
on
\{u<\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\}
, which is a subset of
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
.
Let
\bar{U}
be an upper bound on
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
, uniformly in
\varepsilon\in[0,\bar{\varepsilon}]
, see Proposition
3.2
.
By hypothesis (
1.8
) there is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that
F^{\prime\prime}(r)\geq c_{0}r^{m-2}
for all
r\leq\bar{U}
,
and thus we have that

\displaystyle d_{F}(u|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\int_{u}^{\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}}(r-u)F^{\prime\prime}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\geq c_{0}\int^{\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}}_{\frac{u+\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}}{2}}(u-r)r^{m-2}\,\mathrm{d}r\geq\frac{c_{0}}{2^{m-2}}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{m-2}\int^{\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}}_{\frac{u+\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}}{2}}(u-r)\,\mathrm{d}r=\frac{3c_{0}}{2^{m+1}}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{m-2}(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})^{2}.

This implies by means of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{\{u<\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\}}(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\{u<\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\}}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{-(m-2)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\{u<\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\}}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{m-2}(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}

(6.3)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2^{m+1}/(3c_{0})}\left(\int_{\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{-(m-2)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d_{F}(u|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)^{1/2}.

It remains to be shown that the integral of
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{-(m-2)}
over
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
is finite.
For the estimation of the integrand, we obtain thanks to (
1.19
)

\displaystyle\partial_{u}h(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})=\theta_{u}\big(F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}),G^{\prime}(\bar{v}_{\varepsilon})\big)\leq AF^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}),

and therefore the first Euler–Lagrange equation in (
3.4
) implies that

\displaystyle(1+A\bar{\varepsilon})F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\geq(U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi)_{+}\,.

Using further that
F^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\leq K\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{m-1}
, again thanks to (
1.7
) and (
1.8
), we conclude that on
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\geq c(U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi)^{1/(m-1)}\quad\text{with}\quad c\coloneqq\big(K(1+A\bar{\varepsilon})\big)^{-1/(m-1)}.

We can now estimate the integral of
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{-(m-2)}
by means of the coarea formula.
Two observations:
first,
|\nabla\sqrt{\Phi}|\geq\sqrt{2\Lambda/M}
by (
1.17
), and second, the diameter of
\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon\in[0,\bar{\varepsilon}]
, see Proposition
3.2
. Hence, the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measures
{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}
of the surfaces of the convex sets
\{\Phi<r^{2}\}

are uniformly bounded by some
S
for every
r
with
r^{2}\leq U_{\varepsilon}
.
Now we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{u}_{\varepsilon}}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{-(m-2)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M^{1/2}}{(2\Lambda)^{1/2}c^{m-2}}\int_{\{\sqrt{\Phi}<\sqrt{U_{\varepsilon}}\}}(U_{\varepsilon}-\Phi)^{-(m-2)/(m-1)}|\nabla\sqrt{\Phi}|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M^{1/2}}{(2\Lambda)^{1/2}c^{m-2}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{U_{\varepsilon}}}(U_{\varepsilon}-r^{2})^{-(m-2)/(m-1)}{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}\big(\partial\big\{\sqrt{\Phi}<r\big\}\big)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M^{1/2}S}{(2\Lambda)^{1/2}c^{m-2}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{U_{\varepsilon}}}(U_{\varepsilon}-r^{2})^{-(m-2)/(m-1)}\,\mathrm{d}r,

and this integral has a finite value since
(m-2)/(m-1)<1
, which is bounded independently of
\varepsilon\in[0,\bar{\varepsilon}]
. Combining this with (
6.3
) and (
6.2
), we obtain

\|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d_{F}(u|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2},

for some constant
C
, which is uniform in
\varepsilon\in[0,\bar{\varepsilon}]
. With the definition of
\mathbf{L}_{1}(u)
from (
4.8
), the proof of the first claim in (
6.1
) is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

We apply Proposition
4.4
with
(u^{*},v^{*})=(u_{\tau}^{n},v_{\tau}^{n})
and

Appendix A
Verification of Example
1.2

The required properties for the nonlinearities
F
and
G
are immediately checked.
Concerning the coupling
h
:
differentiation of
h(u,v)=u^{p}v^{p}e^{-\lambda(u+v)}
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}h(u,v)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}p/u-\lambda\\
q/v-\lambda\end{pmatrix}h(u,v),

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{2}h(u,v)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}(p/u-\lambda)^{2}-p/u^{2}&(p/u-\lambda)(q/v-\lambda)\\
(p/u-\lambda)(q/v-\lambda)&(q/v-\lambda)^{2}-q/v^{2}\end{pmatrix}h(u,v).

For the vanishing of
h
itself on
\partial\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
, it is sufficient that
p>0
and
q>0
.
Further,
\partial_{u}h(u,v)=(p/u-\lambda)h(u,v)
vanishes on
\partial\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}

if
h(u,v)/u
vanishes, i.e.,
p>1
and
q>0
are sufficient.
Similarly,
q>1
and
p>0
is sufficient for the vanishing of
\partial_{v}h
.

For the convexity hypothesis, we need to show that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}(m-1)u^{m-2}&0\\
0&(n-1)v^{n-2}\end{pmatrix}+\varepsilon^{*}\mathrm{D}_{(u,v)}^{2}h(u,v)\geq 0

for some
\varepsilon^{*}>0
.
For that, it is sufficient that

\displaystyle 2\varepsilon^{*}\big|(p/u-\lambda)^{2}-p/u^{2}\big|h(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)u^{m-2},

\displaystyle 2\varepsilon^{*}\big|(q/v-\lambda)^{2}-q/v^{2}\big|h(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)v^{m-2},

\displaystyle 4(\varepsilon^{*})^{2}(p/u-\lambda)^{2}(q/v-\lambda)^{2}h(u,v)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)(n-1)u^{m-2}v^{n-2}.

We make use of the following elementary fact:
for arbitrary
a,b\geq 0
, there is a constant
C
such that

(A.1)

\displaystyle x^{a}y^{b}\leq Ce^{x+y}\quad\text{for all $x,y>0$}.

The first inequality is equivalent to

\displaystyle 2\big|(p-\lambda u)^{2}-p\big|u^{p-m}v^{q}\leq\frac{m-1}{\varepsilon^{*}}e^{\lambda(u+v)},

which is true, uniformly in
u,v>0
for a sufficiently small choice of
\varepsilon^{*}>0

provided that
p\geq m
and
q\geq 0
.
In an analogous manner, it follows that the other two inequalities are satisfiable for some
\varepsilon^{*}>0
provided that
p\geq 0
,
q\geq n
and
p\geq m/2
,
q\geq n/2
, respectively.

Next, we notice that

\displaystyle\theta_{u}(\rho,\eta)

\displaystyle=\big(p\rho^{(p-1)/(m-1)}-\lambda\rho^{p/(m-1)}\big)\eta^{q/(n-1)}\exp\big(-\lambda\big[\rho^{1/(m-1)}+\eta^{1/(n-1)}\big]\big),

\displaystyle\theta_{v}(\rho,\eta)

\displaystyle=\rho^{p/(m-1)}\big(q\eta^{(q-1)/(n-1)}-\lambda\eta^{q/(n-1)}\big)\exp\big(-\lambda\big[\rho^{1/(m-1)}+\eta^{1/(n-1)}\big]\big).

To verify the swap condition, observe that

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}\theta_{u}\big(F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-1}\big(pu^{p-1}-\lambda u^{p}\big)\big(qv^{q-(n-1)}-\lambda v^{(q+1)-(n-1)}\big)e^{-\lambda(u+v)},

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\theta_{u}\big(F^{\prime}(u),G^{\prime}(v)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-1}\big(pu^{p-(m-1)}-\lambda u^{(p+1)-(m-1)}\big)\big(qv^{q-1}-\lambda v^{q}\big)e^{-\lambda(u+v)}.

The estimates in (
1.15
) are obviously equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-1}\big(pu^{p-1/2}-\lambda u^{p+1/2}\big)\big(qv^{q+1/2-n}-\lambda v^{q+3/2-n}\big)

\displaystyle\leq We^{\lambda(u+v)},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m-1}\big(pu^{p+1/2-m}-\lambda u^{p+3/2-m}\big)\big(qv^{q-1/2}-\lambda v^{q+1/2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq We^{\lambda(u+v)},

which are true, thanks to (
A.1
), since
p\geq 1/2
,
q\geq n-1/2
and
p\geq m-1/2
,
q\geq 1/2
, respectively.

For the partial derivatives OF
\theta_{u},\theta_{v}
up to order
k
, we need to study boundedness on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
and vanishing on
\partial\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
.
From the chain and product rule of differentiation, we first observe

\partial_{\rho}^{a}\partial_{\eta}^{b}\big[\rho^{\mu}\eta^{\nu}\exp\big(-\lambda\big[\rho^{1-\gamma}+\eta^{1-\delta}\big]\big)\big]\\
=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime}\geq 0\\
a^{\prime}+a^{\prime\prime}=a,b^{\prime}+b^{\prime\prime}=b\end{subarray}}c_{a^{\prime}a^{\prime\prime}b^{\prime}b^{\prime\prime}}\rho^{\mu-a^{\prime}-a^{\prime\prime}\gamma}\eta^{\nu-b^{\prime}-b^{\prime\prime}\delta}\right)\exp\big(-\lambda\big[\rho^{1-\gamma}+\eta^{1-\delta}\big]\big),

where the
c_{a^{\prime}a^{\prime\prime}b^{\prime}b^{\prime\prime}}
are combinatorial coefficients depending also on the exponents
\mu,\nu,\gamma,\delta
.
According to (
A.1
),
the expression on the right-hand side is globally bounded for
(\rho,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}

if all exponents of
\rho
and
\eta
appearing in the sum are non-negative and
1-\gamma
,
1-\delta
are positive.
Furthermore, these expressions vanish on
\partial\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
if all exponents are positive.
In our case, we have
\gamma=1-1/(m-1)=(m-2)/(m-1)\in[0,1)
and likewise
\delta=(n-2)/(n-1)\in[0,1)
.
Therefore, the smallest possibly occurring exponents for
\rho
and
\eta
are
\mu-a
and
\nu-b
, respectively.
Plugging in the “worst case scenarios”
\mu=(p-1)/(m-1)
with
a=k
, and
\nu=(q-1)/(n-1)
with
b=k
,
we see that the condition (
1.20
) is indeed sufficient.

Appendix B
A lower bound on the entropy

The following has been obtained e.g. in
[
25
]
; we recall the proof for convenience.

Lemma B.1
.

For any
u\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, any
\beta>0
, and any
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(B.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{u}(u)=\int u\log u\geq 1-\big(\pi/\beta)^{d/2}-\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-\underline{x}|^{2}u\,\mathrm{d}x

(
with
z\log z
interpreted as zero for
z=0
)
. In particular,
\mathbf{H}^{u}
is nowhere
-\infty
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

By Legendre duality,
zv\leq z\log z-z+e^{v}
for all
z\geq 0
and
v\in\mathbb{R}
. With the choices
z\coloneqq u(x)
and
v\coloneqq-\beta|x-\underline{x}|^{2}
this gives

\displaystyle-\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-\underline{x}|^{2}u\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\log u\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\beta|x-\underline{x}|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

which is just (
B.1
).
∎

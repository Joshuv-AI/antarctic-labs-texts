Kinked linear response from non-Hermitian cold-atom pumping
Fang Qin, Ruizhe Shen, Linhu Li, Ching Hua Lee

Abstract
It is well known that non-Hermitian, non-reciprocal systems may harbor exponentially localized skin modes. However, in this work, we find that, generically, non-Hermiticity gives rise to abrupt and prominent kinks in the semi-classical wave packet trajectories of quantum gases, despite the absence of sudden physical impulses. This physically stems from a hitherto underappreciated intrinsic non-locality from non-Hermitian pumping, even if all physical couplings are local, thereby resulting in enigmatic singularities in the band structure that lead to discontinuous band geometry and Berry curvature. Specifically, we focus on the realization of the kinked response in an ultracold atomic setup. For a concrete experimental demonstration, we propose an ultracold atomic setup in a two-dimensional optical lattice with laser-induced loss such that response kinks can be observed without fine-tuning in the physical atomic cloud dynamics. Our results showcase unique non-monotonic behavior from non-Hermitian pumping beyond the non-Hermitian skin effect and suggest new avenues for investigating non-Hermitian dynamics on ultracold atomic platforms.

Response kinks are physically interesting because they signal abrupt, unexpected changes in a system’s behavior. II
Non-analytic complex momentum deformation from non-Hermitian pumping

We first review how non-Hermiticity can deform the effective lattice momentum non-analytically into the complex plane. A generic two-dimensional (2D) non-interacting lattice Hamiltonian can be written as

H=\sum_{x,y}\left(\sum_{ji;\alpha\beta}t^{\alpha\beta}_{ji}c^{\alpha\dagger}_{j+x,i+y}c^{\beta}_{x,y}\right),

(1)

where
x,y
and
\alpha,\beta

(x,y)
and sublattice
\beta

(x+j,y+i)
and sublattice
\alpha

L_{x}
and
L_{y}

t^{\alpha\beta}_{ji}=0

(x,y)
or
\hskip-1.42262pt(x+j,y+i)\hskip-1.42262pt

[1,L_{x}]\times[1,L_{y}]

|t^{\alpha\beta}_{ji}|\neq|t^{\beta\alpha}_{ij}|

;
Lee and Thomale 2019
;
Yokomizo and Murakami 2019
;
Yang et al. 2020b
;
Okuma et al. 2020
;
Qin et al. 2023b
;
Jiang and Lee 2023
.

To illustrate how complex-deformed momentum arises, we consider the square lattice in two dimensions for simplicity, such that non-Hermitian pumping in either dimension can be separately treated. For a one-dimensional (1D) subchain
H_{\text{1D}}(y)=\sum_{x}\sum_{j;\alpha\beta}t^{\alpha\beta}_{j}c^{\alpha\dagger}_{j+x,y}c^{\beta}_{x,y}

H_{\text{1D}}(z)

H_{\text{1D}}^{\alpha\beta}(z)=\sum_{j}t^{\alpha\beta}_{j0}z^{j},

(2)

and

,

III
Discontinuous Berry curvature

We next discuss how non-analyticities in the bulk momentum description can give rise to jumps in the band geometry and topology. For a square lattice, the effective 2D deformed momentum is
\bar{\mathbb{k}}=(k_{x}+i\kappa_{x}(\mathbb{k}),k_{y}+i\kappa_{y}(\mathbb{k}))
, with
\kappa_{x}
and
\kappa_{y}
in general dependent on both momentum components
\mathbb{k}=(k_{x},k_{y})
. Consider a two-band 2D Hamiltonian

{\cal H}(\mathbb{k})={\cal H}(k_{x},k_{y})=d_{x}\sigma_{x}+d_{y}\sigma_{y}+d_{z}\sigma_{z}+d_{0}\mathbb{I},

(6)

with the Pauli matrices
\sigma_{x,y,z}
in pseudospin space. Under OBCs in both
x,y
directions, the complex-deformed biorthogonal Berry curvature, computed with the occupied left and right momentum eigenstates
\langle\psi_{L}|
and
|\psi_{R}\rangle
, is

\displaystyle\bar{\Omega}_{xy}(\mathbb{k})

\displaystyle\!=\!

\displaystyle\text{Im}\!\left[\langle\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{L}(\bar{\mathbb{k}})|\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{R}(\bar{\mathbb{k}})\rangle\right]

(7)

\displaystyle\!=\!

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{2}\!\left[(\partial_{k_{x}}D)(\partial_{k_{y}}{\rm In}R)\!-\!(\partial_{k_{y}}D)(\partial_{k_{x}}{\rm In}R)\right]\!,

with
D=d_{z}(\bar{\mathbb{k}})/\sqrt{d_{x}^{2}(\bar{\mathbb{k}})+d_{y}^{2}(\bar{\mathbb{k}})+d_{z}^{2}(\bar{\mathbb{k}})}
and
R=\sqrt{[d_{x}(\bar{\mathbb{k}})-id_{y}(\bar{\mathbb{k}})]/[d_{x}(\bar{\mathbb{k}})+id_{y}(\bar{\mathbb{k}})]}
. Do note that while the eigenstates
\langle\psi_{L}|
and
|\psi_{R}\rangle
are evaluated at complex-deformed momenta
\bar{\mathbb{k}}
, their derivatives
\partial_{k_{x}},\partial_{k_{y}}
are taken with respect to the
physical
momenta
\mathbb{k}
, different from existing definitions

Shen et al. 2018
;
Liang and Huang 2013
;
Yokomizo and Murakami 2019
;
Lieu 2018
. This is because while
\bar{\mathbb{k}}
compensates for the broken translation invariance from boundary-accumulated pumped states, the derivatives
\partial_{k_{x}},\partial_{k_{y}}
must be taken with respect to the
physical
momenta
\mathbb{k}
in order to capture the response from conjugate physical fields.

The kinks from
\kappa_{x}(\mathbb{k})
and
\kappa_{y}(\mathbb{k})
correspond to discontinuities in their gradients, and hence also the Berry curvature. Take, for instance, the contribution

IV
Response kinks from discontinuous Berry curvature

We next demonstrate that the Berry curvature discontinuities [Fig.
1
(c)] are not just mathematical artifacts but are in fact experimentally detectable via semiclassical wave packet dynamics. In Hermitian systems, a wave packet can always be decomposed into real momentum eigenstates obeying well-known semiclassical equations of motion.
Such a picture remains valid in the presence of non-Hermitian pumping if the canonical momentum is replaced by the complex
\kappa
-deformed momentum.
The velocity of a wave packet in a generic bounded 2D non-Hermitian system evolves as
Longhi 2009
;
Longhi 2015
;
Xu et al. 2017

\overline{\mathbf{v}}(t)\!=\!\frac{d\bar{\bf r}}{dt}\!=\!\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial{\rm Re}[E(\bar{\bf k})]}{\partial{\bf k}}\!-\!\frac{1}{\hbar}({\bf F}\times{\bf e}_{z}){\rm Re}[\bar{\Omega}_{xy}(\bar{\bf k})],

(12)

where
\bar{\bf r}
describes its center of mass, and
{\bf F}(t)=(F_{x}(t),F_{y}(t))
is an externally applied force.
The first term
\partial{\rm Re}[E(\bar{\bf k})]/\partial{\bf k}
is the usual canonical velocity, with
E(\bar{\bf k})
being that of the lower occupied band, but evaluated at the effective momentum
\bar{\bf k}
. The second term involving
{\rm Re}[\bar{\Omega}_{xy}(\bar{\bf k})]
is the anomalous velocity contribution.

To isolate the Berry curvature response and extract signatures of its discontinuities, we compare the semiclassical trajectories of two initially stationary wave packets subject to forces

;
Wimmer et al. 2017

VI
Discussion

In this work, we uncovered intriguing non-Hermitian kinked responses beyond the non-Hermitian skin effect, as captured by discontinuities in the Berry curvature, or in fact, any band geometric quantity containing momentum derivatives of the band eigenstates. The advantage of our experimental proposal is the capacity to measure the signature of kinked responses as prominent kinks in short-time dynamics, which is tailored to the state-of-the-art cold atom experimental platforms. Our proposal lays the groundwork for future experimental demonstrations of other non-Hermitian transport phenomena with ultracold atomic platforms.

Our kinks are distinct from collective-excitation-induced kinks observed in harmonically trapped quantum gases
Jin et al. 1996
;
Mewes et al. 1996
;
Chevy et al. 2002
;
Kinoshita et al. 2006
;
Lobser et al. 2015
, or finite-lattice-mismatch-induced kinks associated with the commensurate-incommensurate transition in cold atoms
Büchler et al. 2003
;
Gangloff et al. 2020
.

Curiously, even though the discontinuous Berry curvature is phenomenologically manifested in the semi-classical response of wave packets, it does not affect the Hall response of the
entire
band. Indeed, the Chern number
\bar{{\cal C}}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{\rm Re}(\bar{\Omega}_{xy})dk_{x}dk_{y}
can be shown to surprisingly remain quantized despite the violent deformations of the Brillouin zone.

C.2
Discontinuities of the Berry curvature

To compute the biorthogonal Berry curvature in a non-Hermitian system, with or without non-Hermitian pumping, note that the left (bra) and right (ket) eigenstates are not identical:

\displaystyle\hat{\cal H}_{\rm eff}|\psi_{R}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E|\psi_{R}\rangle,

(63)

\displaystyle\hat{\cal H}_{\rm eff}^{\dagger}|\psi_{L}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E^{*}|\psi_{L}\rangle.

(64)

For any two-band Hamiltonian, such as our Hamiltonian in Eq. (
44
), it can be written as
\hat{\cal H}_{\rm eff}=d_{+}\sigma_{+}+d_{-}\sigma_{-}+d_{z}\sigma_{z}=[(d_{+}+d_{-})/2]\sigma_{x}+[i(d_{+}-d_{-})/2]\sigma_{y}+d_{z}\sigma_{z}
with
d_{\pm}=d_{x}\mp id_{y}
and
\sigma_{\pm}=(\sigma_{x}\pm i\sigma_{y})/2
, such that we can perform the following parametrization on the Bloch sphere
Qin et al. 2024
:

\displaystyle d_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\sin\theta\cos\phi=d\sqrt{1-D^{2}}\cos\phi,

(65)

\displaystyle d_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\sin\theta\sin\phi=d\sqrt{1-D^{2}}\sin\phi,

(66)

\displaystyle d_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\cos\theta=dD,

(67)

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}+d_{z}^{2}},

(68)

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{z}}{\sqrt{d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}+d_{z}^{2}}},

(69)

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{d_{+}}{d_{-}}}=\sqrt{\frac{d_{x}-id_{y}}{d_{x}+id_{y}}}=\sqrt{\frac{e^{-i\phi}}{e^{i\phi}}}=e^{-i\phi},

(70)

where we use
d_{x}+id_{y}=d_{\perp}(\cos\phi+i\sin\phi)=d_{\perp}e^{i\phi}
and
d_{x}-id_{y}=d_{\perp}(\cos\phi-i\sin\phi)=d_{\perp}e^{-i\phi}
with
d_{\perp}=\sqrt{d_{x}^{2}+d_{y}^{2}}
.
Hence, our Hamiltonian can be expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{\cal H}_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{x}\sigma_{x}+d_{y}\sigma_{y}+d_{z}\sigma_{z}=\begin{pmatrix}d_{z}&d_{x}-id_{y}\\
d_{x}+id_{y}&-d_{z}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}dD&d\sqrt{1-D^{2}}(\cos\phi-i\sin\phi)\\
d\sqrt{1-D^{2}}(\cos\phi+i\sin\phi)&-dD\end{pmatrix}

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\begin{pmatrix}D&\sqrt{1-D^{2}}e^{-i\phi}\\
\sqrt{1-D^{2}}e^{i\phi}&-D\end{pmatrix}=d\begin{pmatrix}D&\sqrt{1-D^{2}}R\\
\sqrt{1-D^{2}}R^{-1}&-D\end{pmatrix}.

We have the left and right eigenstates as

\displaystyle|\psi_{R}^{(\pm)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\pm R\sqrt{1\pm D}\\
\sqrt{1\mp D}\end{pmatrix},

(72)

\displaystyle|\psi_{L}^{(\pm)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\pm\sqrt{1\pm D^{*}}/R^{*}\\
\sqrt{1\mp D^{*}}\end{pmatrix},

(73)

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{R}^{(\pm)}|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\pm R^{*}\sqrt{1\pm D^{*}}&\sqrt{1\mp D^{*}}\end{pmatrix},

(74)

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{L}^{(\pm)}|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\pm\sqrt{1\pm D}/R&\sqrt{1\mp D}\end{pmatrix}.

(75)

Here, there are two choices for each bra and ket, and in principle, we can compute four possible inequivalent Berry curvatures, although all four can be shown to integrate to the same Chern number
Shen et al. 2018
. We choose the biorthogonal option with
\langle\psi_{L}^{(\pm)}|
bra and
|\psi_{R}^{(\pm)}\rangle
ket, which is the most physical in terms of the probabilistic interpretation of quantum mechanics
Brody 2014
. Doing so, we obtain the biorthogonal Berry curvature as

\displaystyle\bar{\Omega}_{xy}^{(\pm)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\left(\langle\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{L}^{(\pm)}|\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{R}^{(\pm)}\rangle-\langle\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{L}^{(\pm)}|\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{R}^{(\pm)}\rangle\right)

(76)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}\left[\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{k_{x}}(\pm\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D}/R)&\partial_{k_{x}}\sqrt{1\!\mp\!D}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{k_{y}}(\pm R\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D})\\
\partial_{k_{y}}\sqrt{1\!\mp\!D}\end{pmatrix}\!-\!\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{k_{y}}(\pm\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D}/R)&\partial_{k_{y}}\sqrt{1\!\mp\!D}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{k_{x}}(\pm R\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D})\\
\partial_{k_{x}}\sqrt{1\mp D}\end{pmatrix}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}\left[\partial_{k_{x}}(\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D}/R)\partial_{k_{y}}(R\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D})\!-\!\partial_{k_{y}}(\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D}/R)\partial_{k_{x}}(R\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2R\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D}}\partial_{k_{x}}(\pm D)-\frac{\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D}}{R^{2}}\partial_{k_{x}}R\right)\left(\frac{R}{2\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D}}\partial_{k_{y}}(\pm D)+\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D}\partial_{k_{y}}R\right)\right.

\displaystyle~\left.\!-\!\left(\frac{1}{2R\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D}}\partial_{k_{y}}(\pm D)-\frac{\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D}}{R^{2}}\partial_{k_{y}}R\right)\left(\frac{R}{2\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D}}\partial_{k_{x}}(\pm D)+\sqrt{1\!\pm\!D}\partial_{k_{x}}R\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{i}{2R}\left[(\partial_{k_{x}}D)(\partial_{k_{y}}R)-(\partial_{k_{y}}D)(\partial_{k_{x}}R)\right]=\pm\frac{i}{2}\left[(\partial_{k_{x}}D)(\partial_{k_{y}}\ln R)-(\partial_{k_{y}}D)(\partial_{k_{x}}\ln R)\right],

where

\displaystyle\partial_{k_{y}}D(\bar{k}_{x},k_{y})=\frac{d[D(\bar{k}_{x},k_{y})]}{dk_{y}}=\frac{\partial[D(\bar{k}_{x},k_{y})]}{\partial k_{y}}+i\frac{\partial[D(\bar{k}_{x},k_{y})]}{\partial\bar{k}_{x}}\frac{d\kappa_{x}(k_{y})}{dk_{y}}.

(77)

Finally, we can get the analytical expression for the biorthogonal Berry curvature as

\bar{\Omega}_{xy}^{(\pm)}\!=\!\frac{\mp 2t_{y}\Omega_{0}\Omega_{r}\cos\phi_{0}\cos(k_{x}\!+\!i\kappa_{x})\sin k_{y}}{\{[-i\gamma\!+\!2t_{y}\cos k_{y}\!+\!2t_{x}\cos(k_{x}\!+\!i\kappa_{x})]^{2}\!+\!4\Omega_{0}^{2}\cos^{2}\phi_{0}\sin^{2}(k_{x}\!+\!i\kappa_{x})\!+\![\Omega_{r}\!+\!2\Omega_{0}\sin\phi_{0}\sin(k_{x}\!+\!i\kappa_{x})]^{2}\}^{3/2}}.

(78)

In our cylindrical geometry under
x

contributes to the derivative of

If we define the wave numbers as

(79)

where we use

\displaystyle\partial_{k_{x}}D(\bar{k}_{x}a_{x},k_{y}a_{y})

(81)

\displaystyle\partial_{k_{x}}\ln R(\bar{k}_{x}a_{x},k_{y}a_{y})

\displaystyle=

(82)

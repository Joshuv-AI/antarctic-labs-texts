Octonion algebras over schemes and the equivalence of isotopes and isometric quadratic forms This work was done while the author was a master student at the Department of Mathematics, Uppsala University. Supervisor was S. Alsaody.
V. Hildebrandsson

Abstract
Octonion algebras are certain algebras with a multiplicative quadratic form. In [ 1 ] , Alsaody and Gille show that, for octonion algebras over unital commutative rings, there is an equivalence between isotopes and isometric quadratic forms. The contravariant equivalence from unital commutative rings to affine schemes leads us to a question: can the equivalence of isometry and isotopy be generalized to octonion algebras over a (not necessarily affine) scheme? We present the basic definitions and properties of octonion algebras, both over rings and over schemes. Then we show that an isotope of an octonion algebra 𝒞 \mathcal{C} over a scheme is isomorphic to a twist by an 𝐀𝐮𝐭 ⁡ ( 𝒞 ) \mathbf{Aut}(\mathcal{C}) –torsor. We conclude the paper by giving an affirmative answer to our question.



1
Introduction

The octonions
\mathbb{O}
are a normed division algebra over
\mathbb{R}
of dimension 8, discovered in 1843 by John Graves. Based on these, the definition of a octonion algebras over any field was given by Leonard Dickson in 1927, and this was generalized once more to octonion algebras over rings by Loos–Petersson–Racine in 2008
[
9
]
. An important part of these definitions is the assumption that the algebra has an associated quadratic form. In the case over a field, an octonion algebra is completely determined by its quadratic form
[
2
, Claim 2.3]
. Over rings however, this is not the case, proved by Gille in
[
7
]
. The main result of Alsaody and Gille gives a construction of the non–isomorphic octonion algebras with isometric quadratic forms. By the contravariant equivalence of the category of rings and the category of affine schemes, we expect this construction to generalize to octonion algebras over affine schemes. However, will the construction generalize to octonion algebras over general schemes?

Composition algebras over locally ringed spaces were first defined by Petersson in
[
10
]
. Since a locally ringed space
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
comes with a sheaf of rings
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, the idea is to define the composition algebra as a pair
(\mathscr{C},N)
where
\mathscr{C}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
–algebra that is locally a composition algebra in the ring sense. Composition algebras only exist in ranks 1, 2, 4, 8, and a composition algebra of rank 8 is called an octonion algebra.

The structure of the article is as follows. In Section
2
, we recall background of sheaf theory over general sites, the fppf site, and basic notions of octonion algebras over rings. We also present the main result of Alsaody and Gille. In Section
3
, we give the corresponding definitions for octonion algebras over schemes. In Section
4
, we prove several analogous results of those proved in
[
1
, §4 and §6]
. The main results are (1) twisting an octonion algebra
\mathcal{C}
over a scheme by an
\mathbf{Aut}(\mathcal{C})
–torsor corresponds naturally to an isotope of
\mathcal{C}
, and (2) two octonion algebras over schemes are isotopic if and only if their quadratic forms are isometric.

2.2
Octonion algebras over rings

Let
A=(A,\ast)
be an algebra. It is
alternative
if for each
a,b\in A

a(ab)=(aa)b\text{ and }(ba)a=b(aa).

For each
a\in A
we have the linear maps
L_{a}:x\mapsto a\ast x
and
R_{a}:x\mapsto x\ast a
. If
A
is alternative then
L_{a}R_{a}=R_{a}L_{a}
for all
a\in A
, and we denote this map
B_{a}
.

Two
R
–algebras
(A,\ast_{A})
and
(B,\ast_{B})
are
isotopic
if there exist invertible linear maps
f_{i}:A\to B
,
i=1,2,3
, such that

f_{1}(x\ast_{A}y)=f_{2}(x)\ast_{B}f_{3}(y)

for all
x,y\in A
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
A
and
B
are isotopic, then
A
and
B
are isomorphic as
R
–modules, but not necessarily as
R
–algebras. However,
f_{1}:A\to B
is an isomorphism of algebras
A
and
(B,\ast^{\prime}_{B})
, where

x\ast^{\prime}_{B}y=f_{2}f_{1}^{-1}(x)\ast_{B}f_{3}f_{1}^{-1}(y).

An algebra
(B,\ast_{B}^{\prime})
is a
principal isotope
of
(B,\ast_{B})
if there exist invertible linear maps
g,h:B\to B
such that
x\ast^{\prime}y=g(x)\ast h(y)
for all
x,y\in B
. We denote
(B,\ast_{B}^{\prime})=:B_{g,h}
. The algebras
B
and
B_{g,h}
are obviously isotopic. It follows from remark
2.2
that algebras
A
and
B
are isotopic if and only if
A
is isomorphic to a principal isotope of
B
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be an
R
–algebra and
g,h:A\to A
invertible maps.
A_{g,h}
is unital if there exists
e\in A
such that for all
x\in A

g(x)\ast h(e)=g(e)\ast h(x)=x.

Equivalently,

g^{-1}=R_{h(e)}\text{ and }h^{-1}=L_{g(e)}.

If
A
is a unital, alternative algebra, this implies that
g(e)
and
h(e)
are invertible elements (the inverse of an element is well defined in an alternative algebra). Let
a=g(e)^{-1}
and
b=h(e)^{-1}
. Then (
2.2
) is equivalent to

g=R_{a}\text{ and }h=L_{b}.

Also, for any
a,b\in A^{\ast}
,
A_{R_{a},L_{b}}
is unital with unity
(ab)^{-1}
. Here
A^{\ast}
denotes the invertible elements of
A
.

Proposition 2.3

(Alsaody and Gille, Proposition 2.1)
.

Let
A
be a unital alternative algebra over
R
and let
A^{\prime}
be isotopic to
A
. Then
A^{\prime}
is unital if and only if
A^{\prime}\simeq A_{R_{a},L_{b}}
for some
a,b\in A^{\ast}
.

Remark 2.4
.

1.

We denote
A_{R_{a},L_{b}}=:A^{a,b}
.

2.

If
\phi:R\to S
is a ring morphism, we can pullback an
R
–algebra
A
to an
S
–algebra
A_{S}:=A\otimes_{R}S
. For
a,b\in A^{\ast}
, we have
(A^{a,b})_{S}=(A_{S})^{a_{S},b_{S}}
. We denote this algebra by
A^{a,b}_{S}
.

A quadratic form on an
R
–module
M
is a map
q:M\to R
such that

q(rm)=r^{2}q(m),\quad r\in R,m\in M,

and

b_{q}(m,n):=q(m+n)-q(m)-q(n),\quad m,n\in M

is bilinear. It is multiplicative if

q(mn)=q(m)q(n),\quad m,n\in M,

and regular if the morphism

\displaystyle\alpha:M

\displaystyle\to\text{Hom}(M,R)

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\mapsto b_{q}(m,\_)

is an isomorphism. If
M
is finitely generated and projective, the rank of
M
at
p\in\mathbf{Spec}(R)
is the rank of the free
R_{p}
–module
M_{p}
. It is a locally constant function on
\mathbf{Spec}(R)
.

Now, an
octonion algebra over
R
is an
R
–algebra whose underlying module is projective of constant rank 8, and which is endowed with a regular multiplicative quadratic form. It is unital and alternative.

Example 2.5
.

Over any ring
R
, we have the
Zorn algebra

\mathbf{Zorn}(R)=\begin{pmatrix}R&R^{3}\\
R^{3}&R\end{pmatrix}

with multiplication defined by

\begin{pmatrix}a&u\\
u^{\prime}&a^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}b&v\\
v^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ab+u^{t}v^{\prime}&av+b^{\prime}u-u^{\prime}\times v^{\prime}\\
a^{\prime}v^{\prime}+bu^{\prime}+u\times v&(u^{\prime})^{t}v+a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}

where
u\times v
is the vector cross product and
u^{t}v
is the scalar product. With the quadratic form given by the determinant

\det\begin{pmatrix}a&u\\
u^{\prime}&a^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=aa^{\prime}-u^{t}u^{\prime},

\mathbf{Zorn}(R)
becomes an
R
–octonion algebra.

Remark 2.6
.

1.

If
(C,q)
is an octonion algebra, we have the equality
C^{\ast}=\{x\in C|q(x)\in R^{\ast}\}
.

2.

The trace of a quadratic form
q
is the map
tr:M\to R,m\mapsto b_{q}(1,m)
. Then we define the involution map by
\kappa:x\mapsto\overline{x}:=tr(x)\cdot 1_{C}-x
.

3.

For any octonion algebra
(C,q)
over
R
, we have the
Hamilton–Cayley equation

q(c)1=c\overline{c}
, for any
c\in C

[
10
]
.

For any octonion algebra
C
over
R
we have the octonion sphere
\mathbf{S}_{C}
, which is the
R
–scheme

\mathbf{S}_{C}(S)=\{c\in C_{S}|q_{C_{S}}(c)=1\}.

We can now state the result of Alsaody and Gille.

Theorem 2.7

(Alsaody and Gille, Corollary 6.7)
.

Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be octonion algebras over
R
. The quadratic forms
q_{C}
and
q_{C^{\prime}}
are isometric if and only if there exist
a,b\in\mathbf{S}_{C}(R)
such that
C^{\prime}\simeq C^{a,b}
.

3
Octonion algebras over schemes

In this section, we will present the definition of an octonion algebra over a scheme and some of its constructions. These definitions will be generalizations of the ones defined in subsection
2.2
.

Definition 3.1

(Octonion algebra over a scheme)
.

An
octonion algebra
over
X
is a tuple
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{Q})
consisting of

1.

a sheaf
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras such that for all open affine
U\subset X
, the underlying module of the
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
-algebra
\mathcal{C}(U)
is projective with constant rank 8,

2.

a morphism of sheaves
\mathcal{Q}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
such that for all open affine
U\subset X
,
\mathcal{Q}(U)
is a multiplicative, regular, quadratic form on the
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
–algebra
\mathcal{C}(U)
. We call
\mathcal{Q}
the
quadratic form on
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 3.2
.

In the case where
X=\mathbf{Spec}(R)
is an affine scheme,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)=R
, so for an octonion algebra
\mathcal{C}
over
X
,
\mathcal{C}(X)
is an
R
-octonion algebra (as defined in subsection
2.2
).

Note that defining the stalks and induced stalk maps
(\mathcal{C}_{x},\mathcal{Q}_{x})
to be octonion algebras over
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
, as defined in
[
10
, §1.6 and §1.7]
, is another natural way to define the octonion algebra over a scheme. In the following proposition and corollary, we see that the definitions are equivalent.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
be a natural transformation. Then, for some open affine set
U\subset X
,
\mathcal{Q}(U)
is a regular, multiplicative, and quadratic form if and only if
\mathcal{Q}_{x}
is a regular, multiplicative, and quadratic form for all
x\in U
.

Proof.

We only show the multiplicative part, regularity and being quadratic is similar. The induced stalk map
\mathcal{Q}_{x}
at
x\in U
can be defined in two steps:

\displaystyle 1.

\displaystyle\quad\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{x}:(U,s)\mapsto(U,\mathcal{Q}(U)(s))

\displaystyle 2.

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{Q}_{x}:\overline{(U,s)}\mapsto\overline{\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{x}(U,s)}.

Assume
\mathcal{Q}(U)
is a regular, multiplicative, and quadratic form. Then, by the definition of
\mathcal{Q}_{x}
for
x\in U
, it follows that
\mathcal{Q}_{x}
is aswell.

Now, assume for all
x\in U
that
\mathcal{Q}_{x}
is multiplicative. Then
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{x}
must be multiplicative. For
a,b\in\mathcal{C}(U)
, we have

\displaystyle(U,\mathcal{Q}(U)(ab))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{x}((U,ab))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{x}((U,a)(U,b))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{x}((U,a))\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{x}((U,b))

\displaystyle=(U,\mathcal{Q}(U)(a))(U,\mathcal{Q}(U)(b))

\displaystyle=(U,\mathcal{Q}(U)(a)\mathcal{Q}(U)(b))

so
\mathcal{Q}(U)
must also be multiplicative. The proof of regularity and being quadratic is similar.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

For some open affine set
U\subset X
,
(\mathcal{C}(U),\mathcal{Q}(U))
is an octonion algebra over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
if and only if
(\mathcal{C}_{x},\mathcal{Q}_{x})
is an octonion algebra over
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
for all
x\in U
.

Proof.

The rank of
\mathcal{C}(U)
at
x
is by definition the rank of
\mathcal{C}_{x}
, and
\mathcal{C}(U)
is projective if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{x}
is projective for all
x\in U

[
6
, Exercise 4.11b]
. Then the statement follows from proposition
3.3
.
∎

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{Q})
and
(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{Q}^{\prime})
be two octonion algebras over
X
. A
morphism

\varphi:(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{Q})\to(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{Q}^{\prime})
is a natural transformation
\varphi:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
such that
\varphi(U)
is an algebra morphism for all open sets
U\subset X
.

Remark 3.5
.

If we have an isomorphism
\varphi:(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{Q}^{\prime})\to(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{Q})
of octonion algebras over
X
, we also have an isomorphism of octonion algebras
(\mathcal{C}(U),\mathcal{Q}(U))\simeq(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(U),\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}(U))
over the ring
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
. It follows that
\mathcal{Q}(U)
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}(U)
are isometric, i.e.

\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}(U)=\mathcal{Q}(U)\circ\varphi(U)=(\mathcal{Q}\circ\varphi)(U).

Since this holds for all open affine
U\subset X
, and
X
has an open affine cover, it follows that
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}=\mathcal{Q}\circ\varphi
, i.e.
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
are isometric. We denote that two quadratic forms are isometric by
\mathcal{Q}\sim\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{Q})
be an octonion algebra over
X
. For an octonion algebra over a ring, the form induces a bilinear form, a trace, and a natural involution. The same goes for the quadratic form
\mathcal{Q}
. The bilinear form is the morphism of sheaves
\mathcal{B_{Q}}:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
defined for each open set

\mathcal{B_{Q}}(U):(c,c^{\prime})\mapsto\mathcal{Q}(U)(c+c^{\prime})-\mathcal{Q}(U)(c)-\mathcal{Q}(U)(c^{\prime}).

The trace is then given by the morphism of sheaves
\text{tr}_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
defined for each open set
U\subset X
by

\text{tr}_{\mathcal{C}}(U):c\mapsto\mathcal{B_{Q}}(U)(1,c)

and the natural involution is the morphism of sheaves
\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
defined for each open set
U\subset X
by

\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}(U):c\mapsto\text{tr}_{\mathcal{C}}(U)(c)\cdot 1_{\mathcal{C}(U)}-c=:\overline{c}

Now, let
a\in\mathcal{C}(X)
be a global section. Let
L_{a}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
and
R_{a}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
be the morphisms of sheaves defined for any open
U\subset X
by

\displaystyle L_{a}(U):x\mapsto\text{res}_{X,U}(a)\cdot x,\quad R_{a}(U):x\mapsto x\cdot\text{res}_{X,U}(a).

Also, let
B_{a}:=R_{a}L_{a}
.

The
octonion unit sphere
is the
X
–scheme defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{\mathcal{Q_{C}}}:\mathbf{Sch}/X

\displaystyle\to\mathbf{Set}

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle\mapsto\{c\in\mathcal{C}_{Y}(Y)|\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{C}_{Y}}(Y)(c)=1\}.

Definition 3.6

(Isotope)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an octonion algebra over

. For each pair
a,b\in\mathbf{S}_{\mathcal{Q_{C}}}(X)
, we have an

, denoted

, which is the sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras such that, for any open

,
(\mathcal{C}^{a,b}(U),\ast)
is the
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
-algebra with multiplication

Remark 3.7
.

Using the quadratic form
\mathcal{Q}
of
\mathcal{C}
, the isotope
\mathcal{C}^{a,b}
becomes an octonion algebra over
X
. Also, it follows that
(\mathcal{C}^{a,b}(U),\mathcal{Q}(U))
is an isotope of

as
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
–octonion algebras, for all open affine

. By this and remark

Now, let

over

over

. Consider the presheaf

and let
\mathcal{C}_{Y}

Remark 3.8
.

1.

Let

2.

Let

fV\subset U\subset X

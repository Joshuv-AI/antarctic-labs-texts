Spin Boltzmann equation for non-relativistic spin-1/2 fermions
Wen-Bo Dong, Yi-Liang Yin, Qun Wang

Abstract
We derive the spin Boltzmann equations for spin-1/2 fermions in a non-relativistic model with four-fermion contact interaction which conserves spin degrees of freedom. A great advantage of the model is that the spin matrix elements in collision terms can be completely worked out and be put into such a compact form that one can clearly see how spins are coupled in particle scatterings. A semi-classical expansion in the Planck constant has been made and the on-shell part of the spin Boltzmann equation up to the next-to-leading order is derived. At the leading order the equilibrium spin distribution can be obtained from the vanishing of the collision term for the spin density. The spin chemical potential emerges as a natural consequence of spin conservation. The off-shell part of the spin Boltzmann equation is also discussed. The work can be extended to more sophisticated interaction such as nuclear force in order to apply to spin polarization phenomena in heavy-ion collisions at low energies.

)

(
PhysRevC.104.L061901
)

measurements with the high energy ones, the
\Lambda

is observed to continue the increasing trend with decreasing collision
energy down to 2.4 GeV. At these collision energies of
\mathcal{O}(m_{N})

and non-relativistic theory can be a proper approximation. Experiment

(

;
Deng:2021miw
)
.
These models are based on Boltzmann equations for hadrons which do
not incorporate spin degrees of freedom.

In this paper, we will derive the spin Boltzmann equation for spin-1/2
fermions in a non-relativistic model with four-fermion contact interaction
similar to the Nambu-Jona-Lasinio (NJL) model in relativistic theory

(
PhysRev.122.345
;
Nambu:1961fr
)
. The non-relativistic model
has a feature that the particle’s spin is decoupled from its momentum
and is conserved in the interaction. This is very different from a
relativistic system in which the particle’s spin and momentum are
entangled. The method is based on a previous work by one of us about
the relativistic system of spin-1/2 fermions
(
Sheng:2021kfc
)
.
A great advantage of the current non-relativistic model is that the
spin matrix elements in collision terms can be completely worked out
and be put into such a compact form that one can clearly see how spins
are coupled in two-to-two scatterings of particles. This is not the
case in the relativistic theory
(
Sheng:2021kfc
)
. The current
work can be extended to nucleon-nucleon interaction via nuclear force
and then can be applied to the global polarization in heavy-ion collisions
at low energies.

,

III
, we derive the KB equation from
the Schwinger-Dyson equation in quasi-particle approximation. In Sect.

IV
, we derive the on-shell part of the spin Boltzmann

\hbar
. In
Sect.
V
, the off-shell part of the KB equation
is discussed. A summary of the results is given in the final section.

II
Green functions for fermions in CTP formalism

We consider a non-relativistic system of spin-1/2
fermions. A general form of the Lagrangian with four-fermion interaction
can be written as
(
2003Quantum
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\psi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(t,\mathbf{x})\left(i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}\right)\psi_{\alpha}(t,\mathbf{x})

(1)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}d^{3}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\psi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(t,\mathbf{x})\psi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})V_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime},\beta\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\psi_{\beta^{\prime}}(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\psi_{\alpha^{\prime}}(t,\mathbf{x}),

where
\alpha,\beta=\pm
denote spin states,
V_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime},\beta\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

is the spin-dependent potential, and repeated indices imply a summation
if not explicitly stated. Let us consider the contact interaction
of the NJL type
(
PhysRev.122.345
;
Nambu:1961fr
)
,

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}=-g_{0}\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\left[\psi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(t,\mathbf{x})\psi_{\alpha}(t,\mathbf{x})\right]^{2}-g_{\sigma}\sum_{i}\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\left[\psi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(t,\mathbf{x})\sigma_{\alpha\beta}^{i}\psi_{\beta}(t,\mathbf{x})\right]^{2},

(2)

which correpsonds to the potential in the form

V_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime},\beta\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=2\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\left(g_{0}\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\delta_{\beta\beta^{\prime}}+g_{\sigma}\sum_{i}\sigma_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{i}\sigma_{\beta\beta^{\prime}}^{i}\right).

(3)

The Lagrangian (
1
) with the interaction part
(
2
) is invariant under the global SU(2) transformation
defined as

\displaystyle U(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(-i\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right),

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{\alpha\beta}(\theta)\psi_{\beta},

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}^{\prime\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{\beta}^{\dagger}U_{\beta\alpha}^{\dagger}(\theta),

(4)

which means the spin is conserved. The kinetic term of the Lagrangian
(
1
) and
g_{0}
term of the interaction Lagrangian
(
2
) are obviously invariant under the SU(2) transformation.
Let us look at the
g_{\sigma}
term

\displaystyle\left[\psi_{\alpha}^{\prime\dagger}\sigma_{\alpha\beta}^{i}\psi_{\beta}^{\prime}\right]\left[\psi_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime\dagger}\sigma_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}^{i}\psi_{\beta_{1}}^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\psi_{\gamma}^{\dagger}U_{\gamma\alpha}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\alpha\beta}^{i}U_{\beta\lambda}\psi_{\lambda}\right]\left[\psi_{\gamma_{1}}^{\dagger}U_{\gamma_{1}\alpha_{1}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}^{i}U_{\beta_{1}\lambda_{1}}\psi_{\lambda_{1}}\right]

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{ij}V_{ik}\left[\psi_{\gamma}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\gamma\lambda}^{j}\psi_{\lambda}\right]\left[\psi_{\gamma_{1}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\gamma_{1}\lambda_{1}}^{k}\psi_{\lambda_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\psi_{\gamma}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\gamma\lambda}^{j}\psi_{\lambda}\right]\left[\psi_{\gamma_{1}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\gamma_{1}\lambda_{1}}^{j}\psi_{\lambda_{1}}\right],

where we have used
U^{\dagger}\sigma_{i}U=V_{ij}\sigma_{j}
with

V_{ij}
denoting SO(3) matrices. Corresponding to the SU(2) invariance
of the Lagrangian, the Noether charge and current for spin are given
by

\displaystyle Q_{i}^{\mathrm{spin}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{2}\psi^{\dagger}\sigma_{i}\psi,

\displaystyle J_{ij}^{\mathrm{spin}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i\hbar^{2}}{4m}\left(\nabla_{j}\psi^{\dagger}\sigma_{i}\psi-\psi^{\dagger}\sigma_{i}\nabla_{j}\psi\right),

(6)

which satisfy the conservation equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}Q_{i}^{\mathrm{spin}}+\nabla_{j}J_{ij}^{\mathrm{spin}}=0.

(7)

Note that the Noether charge is a spin vector while the Noether current
is a tensor.

The fermion fields can be quantized as

\displaystyle\psi(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}e^{-ip\cdot x/\hbar}\sum_{s=\pm}a(s,\mathbf{p})\chi(s),

\displaystyle\psi^{\dagger}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}e^{ip\cdot x/\hbar}\sum_{s=\pm}a^{\dagger}(s,\mathbf{p})\chi^{\dagger}(s),

(8)

where
x\equiv(x_{0},\mathbf{x})\equiv(t,\mathbf{x})
,
p\equiv(p_{0},\mathbf{p})\equiv(\omega_{p},\mathbf{p})

with
\omega_{p}=\mathbf{p}^{2}/(2m)
,
p\cdot x\equiv\omega_{p}t-\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{x}
,

a(s,\mathbf{p})
and
a^{\dagger}(s,\mathbf{p})
are annihilation
and creation operators associated with
\mathbf{p}
and the spin
state
s
respectively, and
\chi(s)
is the spin state (Pauli spinor)
which satisfies
(\mathbf{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma})\chi(s)=s\chi(s)

with
\mathbf{n}
being the spin quantum direction
\mathbf{n}=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)
.
The anti-commutators of
a(s,\mathbf{p})
and
a^{\dagger}(s,\mathbf{p})

are given by

\displaystyle\left\{a(s_{1},\mathbf{p}_{1}),a^{\dagger}(s_{2},\mathbf{p}_{2})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi\hbar)^{3}\delta_{s_{1}s_{2}}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{p}_{1}-\mathbf{p}_{2}),

\displaystyle\left\{a(s_{1},\mathbf{p}_{1}),a(s_{2},\mathbf{p}_{2})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{a^{\dagger}(s_{1},\mathbf{p}_{1}),a^{\dagger}(s_{2},\mathbf{p}_{2})\right\}=0,

(9)

which lead to equal-time anti-commutators for fermion fields

\displaystyle\left\{\psi_{\alpha}(t,\mathbf{x}),\psi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\left\{\psi_{\alpha}(t,\mathbf{x}),\psi_{\beta}(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\psi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(t,\mathbf{x}),\psi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\right\}=0.

(10)

Now we define the two-point Green function in the closed-time-path
(CTP) formalism
(
Martin:1959jp
;
Keldysh:1964ud
)
(see, e.g.,
Refs.
(
Chou:1984es
;
Blaizot:2001nr
;
Berges:2004yj
;
Crossley:2015evo
)

for reviews) as

G_{\alpha\beta}^{C}(x_{1},x_{2})=\left\langle T_{C}\left[\psi_{\alpha}(x_{1})\psi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(x_{2})\right]\right\rangle,

(11)

where
T_{C}
denotes the time-ordered product on the CTP, and the
angular brackets denote averages weighted by the density operator
at the initial time
\rho(t_{0})
. Note that our definition for Green’s
function is different from Ref.
(
2003Quantum
)
without the additional
factor
i=\sqrt{-1}
. Depending on whether the two space-time points
are on the positive or negative time branch, there are four types
of two-point functions

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}^{F}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}^{++}(x_{1},x_{2})=\left\langle T\psi_{\alpha}(x_{1})\psi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(x_{2})\right\rangle,

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}^{\overline{F}}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}^{--}(x_{1},x_{2})=\left\langle T_{A}\psi_{\alpha}(x_{1})\psi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(x_{2})\right\rangle,

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}^{<}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}^{+-}(x_{1},x_{2})=-\left\langle\psi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(x_{2})\psi_{\alpha}(x_{1})\right\rangle,

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}^{>}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}^{-+}(x_{1},x_{2})=\left\langle\psi_{\alpha}(x_{1})\psi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(x_{2})\right\rangle,

(12)

where
+/-
stands for the positive/negative time branch respectively,
and
T
and
T_{A}
denote the time-ordered and reverse-time-ordered
product respectively. Note that only three out of four types of two-point
functions in (
12
) are independent due to the identity

G^{F}+G^{\overline{F}}=G^{<}+G^{>}.

(13)

We can choose
(G^{F},G^{<},G^{>})
as three independent two-point
functions. Equivalently we can also choose
(G^{R},G^{A},G^{S})

as independent ones

\displaystyle G^{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{F}-G^{<}=\theta(t_{1}-t_{2})(G^{>}-G^{<}),

\displaystyle G^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{F}-G^{>}=\theta(t_{2}-t_{1})(G^{<}-G^{>}),

\displaystyle G^{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{<}+G^{>},

(14)

where
G^{R}
and
G^{A}
are retarded and advanced two-point functions.

The Wigner function is the building-block of the quantum transport
theory since it is the quantum analogue of the particle distribution
in phase space. The Wigner function can be obtained by taking the
Fourier transformation with respect to the distance of two space-time
points in a two-point function. Then the Wigner functions for
G_{\alpha\beta}^{<}(x_{1},x_{2})

and
G_{\alpha\beta}^{>}(x_{1},x_{2})
are defined by

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}^{<}(x,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}ye^{ip\cdot y/\hbar}G_{\alpha\beta}^{<}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int d^{4}ye^{ip\cdot y/\hbar}\left\langle\psi_{\beta}^{\dagger}\left(x-\frac{y}{2}\right)\psi_{\alpha}\left(x+\frac{y}{2}\right)\right\rangle,

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}^{>}(x,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}ye^{ip\cdot y/\hbar}G_{\alpha\beta}^{>}(x_{1},x_{2})

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}ye^{ip\cdot y/\hbar}\left\langle\psi_{\alpha}\left(x+\frac{y}{2}\right)\psi_{\beta}^{\dagger}\left(x-\frac{y}{2}\right)\right\rangle,

where
p\cdot y\equiv\omega_{p}y_{0}-\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{y}
,

x_{1}=x+y/2
and
x_{2}=x-y/2
.

In non-equilibrium, we do not know
G_{\alpha\beta}^{<}(x,p)
and

G_{\alpha\beta}^{>}(x,p)
exactly due to unknown ensemble averages.
However, using (
8
), we can make an ansatz for
their forms in a power expansion of
\hbar
. Inserting (
8
)
to (
15
), the leading order contributions to the
Wigner functions have the form

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}^{<(0)}(x,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(2\pi\hbar)\delta\left(p_{0}-\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m}\right)\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm}\chi_{\alpha}(s_{1})\chi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(s_{2})f_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{(0)}(x,\mathbf{p}),

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}^{>(0)}(x,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi\hbar)\delta\left(p_{0}-\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m}\right)\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm}\chi_{\alpha}(s_{1})\chi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(s_{2})\left[\delta_{s_{1}s_{2}}-f_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{(0)}(x,\mathbf{p})\right],

(16)

where
p\equiv(p_{0},\mathbf{p})\equiv(\omega,\mathbf{p})
with
p_{0}

or
\omega
being an independent variable, and the matrix valued
spin-dependent distribution (MVSD) at
\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{0})
is
defined as
(
Sheng:2021kfc
)

\displaystyle f_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{(0)}(x,\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\exp\left[i\frac{1}{\hbar}\left(-\frac{\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{q}}{m}t+\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}\right)\right]

(17)

\displaystyle\times\left\langle a^{\dagger}\left(s_{2},\mathbf{p}-\frac{\mathbf{q}}{2}\right)a\left(s_{1},\mathbf{p}+\frac{\mathbf{q}}{2}\right)\right\rangle.

One can check that
G^{\lessgtr(0)}(x,p)
are Hermitian matrices
because
f_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{(0)*}=f_{s_{2}s_{1}}^{(0)}
, i.e.,
f^{(0)}

is a Hermitian matrix in spin space. The first order contributions
at
\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{1})
are assumed to have the form

\displaystyle\hbar G_{\alpha\beta}^{<(1)}(x,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar G_{\alpha\beta}^{>(1)}(x,p)

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hbar(2\pi\hbar)\delta\left(p_{0}-\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m}\right)\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm}\chi_{\alpha}(s_{1})\chi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(s_{2})f_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{(1)}(x,\mathbf{p}),

where

is unknown and can be
determined by solving the evolution equations. We assume
f_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{(1)*}=f_{s_{2}s_{1}}^{(1)}
,
which means
f^{(1)}
and then
G^{\lessgtr(1)}(x,p)
are Hermitian
matrices in spin space. Note that in Eq. (

) we only include
on-shell contributions to
G^{\lessgtr(1)}(x,p)
which are proportional
to
\delta\left[p_{0}-\mathbf{p}^{2}/(2m)\right]
. In principle there

.
We will address off-shell contributions in Section

Combining (
16
) and (
18
) we obtain
G^{\lessgtr}(x,p)

up to
\mathcal{O}(\hbar)

\displaystyle G^{<}(x,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{<(0)}(x,p)+\hbar G^{<(1)}(x,p)+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(2\pi\hbar)\delta\left(p_{0}-\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m}\right)\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm}\chi_{\alpha}(s_{1})\chi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(s_{2})f_{s_{1}s_{2}}(x,\mathbf{p}),

\displaystyle G^{>}(x,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{>(0)}(x,p)+\hbar G^{>(1)}(x,p)+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{2})

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi\hbar)\delta\left(p_{0}-\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m}\right)\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm}\chi_{\alpha}(s_{1})\chi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(s_{2})\left[\delta_{s_{1}s_{2}}-f_{s_{1}s_{2}}(x,\mathbf{p})\right].

Here the MVSD
f_{s_{1}s_{2}}(x,\mathbf{p})
is given by

\displaystyle f_{s_{1}s_{2}}(x,\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{(0)}(x,\mathbf{p})+\hbar f_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{(1)}(x,\mathbf{p})+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{2})

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{s_{1}s_{2}}\overline{f}(x,\mathbf{p})+\boldsymbol{\tau}_{s_{1}s_{2}}\cdot\mathbf{t}(x,\mathbf{p})+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{2}),

\boldsymbol{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3})

\displaystyle g^{<}(x,p)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm}\chi(s_{1})\chi^{\dagger}(s_{2})f_{s_{1}s_{2}}(x,\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{f}(x,\mathbf{p})+\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{S}(x,\mathbf{p}),

\displaystyle g^{>}(x,p)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm}\chi(s_{1})\chi^{\dagger}(s_{2})\left[\delta_{s_{1}s_{2}}-f_{s_{1}s_{2}}(x,\mathbf{p})\right]

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{f}(x,\mathbf{p})+\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{S}(x,\mathbf{p})-1.

These components can be extracted by taking traces

\displaystyle\overline{f}(x,\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left[g^{<}(x,p)\right],

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}(x,\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\boldsymbol{\sigma}g^{<}(x,p)\right].

(23)

So
\mathbf{S}(x,\mathbf{p})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}(x,\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm}\chi^{\dagger}(s_{2})\boldsymbol{\sigma}\chi(s_{1})f_{s_{1}s_{2}}(x,\mathbf{p})

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}=\pm}(\mathbf{n}_{1}\tau_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}\tau_{2}+\mathbf{n}_{3}\tau_{3})_{s_{2}s_{1}}f_{s_{1}s_{2}}(x,\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}_{i}t_{i}(x,\mathbf{p}),

where we have used

\displaystyle\chi^{\dagger}(s_{2})\boldsymbol{\sigma}\chi(s_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{n}_{1}\tau_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}\tau_{2}+\mathbf{n}_{3}\tau_{3})_{s_{2}s_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{n}_{3}&\mathbf{n}_{1}-i\mathbf{n}_{2}\\
\mathbf{n}_{1}+i\mathbf{n}_{2}&-\mathbf{n}_{3}\end{array}\right)_{s_{2}s_{1}}.

Here
(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{3})
are three basis
vectors given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\cos\phi\cos\theta,\sin\phi\cos\theta,-\sin\theta\right),

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\sin\phi,\cos\phi,0\right),

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta\right).

(28)

Note that
\mathbf{n}_{3}=\mathbf{n}
is the direction of the spin
quantization.

III
Kadanoff-Baym equation

The time evolution of a many-body
quantum system in non-equilibrium is described by the Kadanoff-Baym
(KB) equation
(
Kadanoff2018QuantumSM
)
[see, e.g., Ref.
(
Cassing:2008nn
)

for a review]. The KB equations can be derived from the Dyson-Schwinger
equations for two-point Green functions on the CTP

\displaystyle-i\left(i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla_{x1}^{2}\right)G_{C}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\delta_{C}^{(4)}(x_{1}-x_{2})+\hbar\int_{C}d^{4}x^{\prime}\Sigma_{C}(x_{1},x^{\prime})G_{C}(x^{\prime},x_{2}),

\displaystyle-iG_{C}(x_{1},x_{2})\left(-i\hbar\frac{\overleftarrow{\partial}}{\partial t_{2}}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\overleftarrow{\nabla}_{x2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\delta_{C}^{(4)}(x_{1}-x_{2})+\hbar\int_{C}d^{4}x^{\prime}G_{C}(x_{1},x^{\prime})\Sigma_{C}(x^{\prime},x_{2}),

(29)

where the index
C
stands for the CTP,
\delta_{C}^{(4)}(x_{1}-x_{2})

and
\Sigma_{C}(x_{1},x^{\prime})
are the delta-function and self-energy
on the CTP respectively. Note that
G_{C}
and
\Sigma_{C}
are

2\times 2
matrices in spinor space. In the case that
(t_{1},t_{2})

are on (
+,-
) time branch, Eq. (
29
) can be
put into the conventional coordinate form

\displaystyle-i\left(i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla_{x1}^{2}\right)G^{<}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\int d^{4}x^{\prime}\left[\Sigma^{R}(x_{1},x^{\prime})G^{<}(x^{\prime},x_{2})\right.

(30)

\displaystyle\left.+\Sigma^{<}(x_{1},x^{\prime})G^{A}(x^{\prime},x_{2})\right],

\displaystyle-i\left(-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2}}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla_{x2}^{2}\right)G^{<}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\int d^{4}x^{\prime}\left[G^{R}(x_{1},x^{\prime})\Sigma^{<}(x^{\prime},x_{2})\right.

(31)

\displaystyle\left.+G^{<}(x_{1},x^{\prime})\Sigma^{A}(x^{\prime},x_{2})\right],

where
\Sigma^{R}
and
\Sigma^{A}
are the retarded and advanced
self-energy respectively. Performing the Wigner transform for above
equations, we obtain

\left(i\hbar\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}+i\frac{\hbar}{2m}\mathbf{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}+p_{0}-\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{8m}\nabla_{x}^{2}\right)G^{<}(x,p)=I_{\mathrm{coll}},

(32)

and

\left(-i\hbar\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}-i\frac{\hbar}{2m}\mathbf{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}+p_{0}-\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{8m}\nabla_{x}^{2}\right)G^{<}(x,p)=I_{\mathrm{coll}}^{\dagger},

(33)

where
I_{\mathrm{coll}}
and
I_{\mathrm{coll}}^{\dagger}
are
given by

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{coll}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\hbar\left[\Sigma^{R}(x,p)G^{<}(x,p)+\Sigma^{<}(x,p)G^{A}(x,p)\right]

(34)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\hbar^{2}\left[\left\{\Sigma^{R}(x,p),G^{<}(x,p)\right\}_{P.B.}+\left\{\Sigma^{<}(x,p),G^{A}(x,p)\right\}_{P.B.}\right],

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{coll}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\hbar\left[G^{R}(x,p)\Sigma^{<}(x,p)+G^{<}(x,p)\Sigma^{A}(x,p)\right]

(35)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\hbar^{2}\left[\left\{G^{R}(x,p),\Sigma^{<}(x,p)\right\}_{P.B.}+\left\{G^{<}(x,p),\Sigma^{A}(x,p)\right\}_{P.B.}\right].

Here the Poisson bracket of two matrices is defined as

\left\{A,B\right\}_{\mathrm{PB}}\equiv\partial_{t}A\partial_{p_{0}}B-\partial_{p_{0}}A\partial_{t}B-\left(\nabla_{x}A\cdot\nabla_{p}B-\nabla_{p}A\cdot\nabla_{x}B\right).

(36)

We see that
I_{\mathrm{coll}}^{\dagger}
can be obtained by interchange
of
\Sigma
and
G
from
I_{\mathrm{coll}}
, and vice versa. With
the relations for
O=G,\Sigma
,

[O^{R}]^{\dagger}=-O^{A},\;\;\left[O^{\lessgtr}\right]^{\dagger}=\left[O^{\lessgtr}\right],

(37)

one can check that
I_{\mathrm{coll}}^{\dagger}
is really the Hermitian
conjugate of
I_{\mathrm{coll}}
. Taking the sum and difference of
Eq. (
34
) and (
35
), we obtain an
equation for the dispersion relation or the on-shell equation

\left(p_{0}-\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{8m}\nabla_{x}^{2}\right)G^{<}(x,p)=\frac{1}{2}\left(I_{\mathrm{coll}}+I_{\mathrm{coll}}^{\dagger}\right),

(38)

and the evolution equation

\hbar\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{\mathbf{p}}{m}\cdot\nabla_{x}\right)G^{<}(x,p)=-i\left(I_{\mathrm{coll}}-I_{\mathrm{coll}}^{\dagger}\right).

(39)

Equations (
38
,
39
) are one of the main
results in this paper and the starting point for the derivation of
Boltzmann equations.

In the quasi-particle picture, the retarded and advanced self-energies
and two-point functions can be approximated as

\displaystyle O^{R/A}(x,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int dk_{0}\frac{1}{k_{0}-p_{0}\mp i\epsilon}\left[O^{>}(x,k_{0},\mathbf{p})-O^{<}(x,k_{0},\mathbf{p})\right]

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{1}{2}\left[O^{>}(x,p)-O^{<}(x,p)\right]+O_{\mathrm{pr}}^{R/A},

where
O\equiv G,\Sigma
, and the principal part
O_{\mathrm{pr}}^{R/A}

is related to off-shell effects that modify the dispersion relation
of the quasi-particle which we will not consider in this paper. Using
(
40
), the collision terms (
34
)
and (
35
) can be simplified as

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{coll}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}i\hbar\left[\Sigma^{>}(x,p)G^{<}(x,p)-\Sigma^{<}(x,p)G^{>}(x,p)\right]

(41)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\hbar^{2}\left[\left\{\Sigma^{>}(x,p),G^{<}(x,p)\right\}_{P.B.}-\left\{\Sigma^{<}(x,p),G^{>}(x,p)\right\}_{P.B.}\right],

and

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{coll}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}i\hbar\left[G^{>}(x,p)\Sigma^{<}(x,p)-G^{<}(x,p)\Sigma^{>}(x,p)\right]

(42)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\hbar^{2}\left[\left\{G^{>}(x,p),\Sigma^{<}(x,p)\right\}_{P.B.}-\left\{G^{<}(x,p),\Sigma^{>}(x,p)\right\}_{P.B.}\right].

With collision terms (
41
) and (
42
),
Eq. (
39
) is our starting point for the derivation of
Boltzmann equations.

The Boltzmann equations for
\overline{f}(x,\mathbf{p})
and
\mathbf{S}(x,p)

can be obtained by taking a trace of Eq. (
39
) and a
trace of Eq. (
39
) multiplied by
\boldsymbol{\sigma}

as

\displaystyle\hbar\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{m}\mathbf{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}\right)\mathrm{Tr}\left(G^{<}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{Im}\mathrm{Tr}\left(I_{\mathrm{coll}}\right),

(43)

\displaystyle\hbar\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{m}\mathbf{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}\right)\mathrm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}G^{<}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{Im}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}I_{\mathrm{coll}}\right).

(44)

From (
19
) we know that there is an on-shell delta-function

\delta\left[p_{0}-\mathbf{p}^{2}/(2m)\right]
in both sides of above
equations, therefore one can drop these delta-functions and derive
equations for
\overline{f}(x,\mathbf{p})
and
\mathbf{S}(x,p)

without delta-functions.

IV.2
Next-to-leading order

At next to leading order, the Boltzmann equation for the unpolarized
distribution reads

\hbar(\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{m}\mathbf{p}\cdot\nabla_{x})\overline{f}_{p}^{(1)}=I_{\mathrm{qc}}^{\mathrm{scalar}}\left[f^{(0)},f^{(1)}\right]+I_{\mathrm{PB}}^{\mathrm{scalar}}\left[f^{(0)}\right],

(54)

where the subscript ’qc’ represents the quasi-classical contribution
and ’PB’ represents the contribution from the Poisson bracket term.
The quasi-classical part can be obtained from Eq. (
46
)
by replacing all zeroth order distributions
\overline{f}_{i}^{(0)}

and
\mathbf{S}_{i}^{(0)}
by
\overline{f}_{i}=\overline{f}_{i}^{(0)}+\overline{f}_{i}^{(1)}

and
\mathbf{S}_{i}=\mathbf{S}_{i}^{(0)}+\mathbf{S}_{i}^{(1)}
respectively
and expanding it to the first order, the result is

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{qc}}^{\mathrm{scalar}}\left[f^{(0)},f^{(1)}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\hbar(g_{0}-3g_{\sigma})^{2}\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}_{1}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}_{2}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}_{3}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}(2\pi\hbar)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p+p_{3}-p_{2}-p_{1})

(55)

\displaystyle\times\left\{\overline{f}_{1}^{(1)}\left[\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)}+\overline{f}_{3}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{p}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{3}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{p}^{(0)}\right]\right.

\displaystyle+\overline{f}_{2}^{(1)}\left[\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}+\overline{f}_{3}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{p}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{3}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{p}^{(0)}\right]

\displaystyle-\overline{f}_{3}^{(1)}\left[\overline{f}_{p}^{(0)}+\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{p}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{p}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)}\right]

\displaystyle-\overline{f}_{p}^{(1)}\left[\overline{f}_{3}^{(0)}+\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{3}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{3}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)}\right]

\displaystyle+(1-\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)})\mathbf{S}_{3}^{(1)}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{p}^{(0)}+(1-\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)})\mathbf{S}_{3}^{(0)}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{p}^{(1)}

I_{\mathrm{PB}}^{\mathrm{scalar}}\left[f^{(0)}\right]=0,

(56)

for the scalar part of the Boltzmann equation.

In order to prove Eq. (
56

property for the trace of Pauli matrices

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\cdots\sigma_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sigma_{k}\cdots\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\right],

(57)

where the
n
is number of Pauli matrices. To prove the above relation,
we insert
C^{2}=-1
between Pauli matrices

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\mathrm{Tr}\left[C\sigma_{i}CC\sigma_{j}C\cdots C\sigma_{k}C\right]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma_{i}^{T}\sigma_{j}^{T}\cdots\sigma_{k}^{T}]=(-1)^{n}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sigma_{k}\cdots\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\right],

(58)

where
C=i\sigma^{2}
and
C\boldsymbol{\sigma}C=\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{T}
.

With Eq. (
58
) we consider the quantities in the Poisson
bracket collision term

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=g_{c1}g_{c2}\mathrm{Im}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma^{(c2)}G_{1}\Gamma^{(c1)}G_{3}\right)\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma^{(c2)}G_{2}\Gamma^{(c1)}G_{p}\right)\right],

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=g_{c1}g_{c2}\mathrm{Im}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma^{(c2)}G_{1}\Gamma^{(c1)}G_{3}\Gamma^{(c2)}G_{2}\Gamma^{(c1)}G_{p}\right).

(59)

G_{i}

(i=1,2,3,p)

G^{\lessgtr}(x,p_{i})

or derivatives of
G^{\lessgtr}(x,p_{i})

where
G_{i}(1)

G_{i}(0)
denotes

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{c1}g_{c2}\mathrm{Im}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma^{(c2)}G_{1}\Gamma^{(c1)}G_{3}\right)\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma^{(c2)}G_{2}\Gamma^{(c1)}G_{p}\right)\right]

(60)

\displaystyle g_{c1}g_{c2}\sum_{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b}\mathrm{Im}\left\{\mathrm{Tr}\left[\Gamma^{(c2)}G_{1}(b_{1})\Gamma^{(c1)}G_{3}(b_{3})\right]\mathrm{Tr}\left[\Gamma^{(c2)}G_{2}(b_{2})\Gamma^{(c1)}G_{p}(b)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\downarrow

\displaystyle\mathrm{insert}\;C^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{c1}g_{c2}\sum_{n=\mathrm{odd}}(-1)^{n}\mathrm{Im}\left\{\mathrm{Tr}\left[\Gamma^{(c1)}G_{1}(b_{1})\Gamma^{(c2)}G_{3}(b_{3})\right]\mathrm{Tr}\left[\Gamma^{(c1)}G_{2}(b_{2})\Gamma^{(c2)}G_{p}(b)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g_{c1}g_{c2}\sum_{n=\mathrm{odd}}\mathrm{Im}\left\{\mathrm{Tr}\left[\Gamma^{(c2)}G_{1}(b_{1})\Gamma^{(c1)}G_{3}(b_{3})\right]\mathrm{Tr}\left[\Gamma^{(c2)}G_{2}(b_{2})\Gamma^{(c1)}G_{p}(b)\right]\right\},

where
n=b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}+b
is the number of scalar parts in two
traces, and we have interchanged

in the last

c1
and
c2
is implied. Note that

of two traces have an imaginary part. By comparing the last equality
with the second one of Eq. (

I_{1}=0

We now derive the Boltzmann equation for the polarization distribution
at the next-to-leading order. The contributions can be grouped into
the local (quasi-classical) and nonlocal parts of the collision term.
The local part contains no space-time derivatives and can be obtained
from Eq. (
47
) by the replacement
\overline{f}_{i}^{(0)}\rightarrow\overline{f}_{i}=\overline{f}_{i}^{(0)}+\overline{f}_{i}^{(1)}

and
\mathbf{S}_{i}^{(0)}\rightarrow\mathbf{S}_{i}=\mathbf{S}_{i}^{(0)}+\mathbf{S}_{i}^{(1)}

in the collision term and then by expanding the collision term to
the next-to-leading order. The nonlocal part comes from the Poisson
bracket term with space-time derivatives. Special care should be taken
for the derivatives in
\partial_{p_{0}}
and
\nabla_{p}
which
act on the two-point function
G^{\lessgtr}(x,p)
, giving terms with

\delta^{\prime}(p_{0}-\mathbf{p}^{2}/2m)
. These terms belong to
off-shell contributions which we will neglect in this section and
leave to the next section for treatment. Substituting Eq. (

)
into Eq. (

equation for the polarization distribution at the next-to-leading

\displaystyle\hbar\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{m}\mathbf{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}\right)\mathbf{S}_{p}^{a,(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{qc}}^{\mathrm{pol}}\left[f^{(0)},f^{(1)}\right]+I_{\mathrm{PB}}^{\mathrm{pol}}\left[f^{(0)}\right].

(62)

The explicit form of the local part (quasi-classical) of the collision
term is given by

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{qc}}^{\mathrm{pol}}\left[f^{(0)},f^{(1)}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\hbar(g_{0}-3g_{\sigma})^{2}\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}_{1}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}_{2}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}_{3}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}(2\pi\hbar)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p+p_{3}-p_{2}-p_{1})

(63)

\displaystyle\times\left\{\mathbf{S}_{3}^{a,(0)}\left[\overline{f}_{p}^{(1)}(1-\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)})+\overline{f}_{1}^{(1)}(\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{p}^{(0)})+\overline{f}_{2}^{(1)}(\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{p}^{(0)})\right]\right.

\displaystyle-\mathbf{S}_{p}^{a,(0)}\left[\overline{f}_{3}^{(1)}(1-\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)})+\overline{f}_{1}^{(1)}(\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{3}^{(0)})+\overline{f}_{2}^{(1)}(\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{3}^{(0)})\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbf{S}_{3}^{a,(1)}\left(\overline{f}_{p}^{(0)}+\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{p}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{p}^{(0)}\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)}\right)

One can check that

is local and on-shell.

The non-local part of the collision term contains on-shell and off-shell
contributions, as we have mentioned, in this section we focus on the
on-shell contribution and will treat the off-shell one in Sect.
V
.
The explicit form of the on-shell Poisson bracket term reads

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{PB}}^{\mathrm{pol}}\left[f^{(0)}\right]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 2\hbar^{2}\left(g_{0}-3g_{\sigma}\right)^{2}\epsilon_{aij}\int\frac{d^{3}p_{1}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\frac{d^{3}p_{2}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\frac{d^{3}p_{3}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}(2\pi\hbar)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p+p_{3}-p_{1}-p_{2})

\displaystyle\left.+\left(1-\overline{f}_{1}^{(0)}-\overline{f}_{2}^{(0)}\right)\left(\nabla_{p_{3}}\mathbf{S}_{3}^{(0),i}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathbf{S}_{p}^{(0),j}-\nabla_{x}\mathbf{S}_{3}^{(0),i}\cdot\nabla_{p}\mathbf{S}_{p}^{(0),j}\right)\right\}.

Here we have neglected a term with

,
where

or

denotes the coupling

for it contains derivatives of space-time. From Eq. (

we see that the collision term vanishes if there is no polarization

contrast it has been proved in Ref.

VI
Comments on nuclear force through OBEP

In order to apply our theory to a non-relativistic nucleon system
in low energy collisions, one has to go beyond the contact interaction
of the NJL type and consider nuclear force as interaction. The main
features of nuclear force can be effectively decribed by one boson
exchange potential (OBEP)
(
Bryan:1969mp
;
Machleidt:1989tm
)
.
The OBEPs through scalar, pseudoscalar, and vector meson exchanges
have terms with operators
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{2}
,

\mathbf{L}\cdot\mathbf{S}
and
S_{12}
defined as
\mathbf{L}\equiv-i\mathbf{r}\times\nabla
,

\mathbf{S}\equiv(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{2})/2
,
and

S_{12}\equiv\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left[3(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{r})(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{r})-r^{2}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{2}\right],

(73)

where
\mathbf{r}\equiv\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
. So OBEPs
are nolocal and contain couplings between spin and coordinate (equivalently
spin and momentum). In this case the spin is not a conserved quantity
as in the NJL-like model. It may be converted from local orbital angular
momentum or local vorticity
(
Weickgenannt:2020aaf
;
Weickgenannt:2021cuo
)
.
The extension to the OBEP is much more complicated and beyond the
scope of this work. It will be reserved for a future study.

VII
Summary

We derive spin Boltzmann equations for non-relativistic
spin-1/2 fermions from the KB equation in the CTP formalism. The non-relativistic
model is similar to the NJL model with four-fermion contact interaction
which conserves spins in particle scatterings. The great merit of
the model is that the spin matrix element in the collision term can
be completely worked out and be put into a compact form. One can clearly
see how spins are coupled in two-to-two scatterings of particles.
In contrast it is hard to envisage the structure of the spin matrix
element which is much more complicated in the relativistic theory

(
Sheng:2021kfc
)
.

Starting from the non-relativistic Lagrangian, the KB equation is
derived from the Dyson-Schwinger equation defined on the CTP. The
spin Boltzmann equations for the particle number and spin distribution
are derived based on Wigner functions and the KB equation. Since the
spin polarization is a quantum effect, we make an expansion in the
Planck constant
\hbar
for all quantities in the spin Boltzmann
equation. At the leading order, the equilibrium spin distribution
can be obtained under the condition of the vanishing collision term
for the spin phase space density. A spin chemical potential emerges
in the equilibrium spin distribution which is a natural consequence
of spin conservation. The off-shell parts of spin Boltzmann equations
are also discussed. The work can be extended to a system of nucleons
which interact via nuclear forces in low energy heavy-ion collisions.

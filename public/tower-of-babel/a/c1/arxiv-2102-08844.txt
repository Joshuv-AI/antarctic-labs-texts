On two mean square averages of Dirichlet L L -function
Neha Elizabeth Thomas, Arya Chandran, K Vishnu Namboothiri

Abstract
. Finding the mean square averages of the Dirichlet L L -functions over Dirichlet characters χ \chi of same parity is an active problem in number theory. Here we explicitly evaluate such averages of L ⁡ ( 3 , χ ) L(3,\chi) and L ⁡ ( 4 , χ ) L(4,\chi) using certain trigonometric sums and Bernoulli polynomials and express them in terms of the Euler totient function ϕ \phi and the Jordan totient function J s J_{s} .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11M06, 11L05, 11L03

1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a natural number
\geq 3
. A Dirichlet character
\chi
is defined to be odd if
\chi(-1)=-1
and even if
\chi(-1)=1
. The Dirichlet
L
-function
L(s,\chi)
is defined by the infinite series
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}}
where
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
Re\,(s)>1
. It is an important function in number theory especially due to its connection with the Rieman zeta function
\zeta(s)
. For rational integer
r
, the problem of computing exact values of

(1)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\text{ mod }k\\
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{r}\end{subarray}}|L(r,\chi)|^{2}

and thus finding the mean square averages of this sum has been attempted in various cases by many.

In 1982, Walum
[
15
]
gave an exact formula for the sum (
1
) with
r=1
. Louboutin (
[
6
]
) computed the sum of
|L(1,\chi)|^{2}
over all odd primitive Dirichlet characters modulo
k
. See
[
4
, Chapter 6]
for the definition of primitivity of Dirichlet characters. In
[
7
]
, Louboutin gave an exact formula for the sum of
|L(1,\chi)|^{2}
over all odd Dirichlet characters in terms of the prime divisors of
k
and the Euler totient function
\phi
. He mainly used the orthogonality properties of characters and some trigonometric identites in his computations. Using the same techniques, in
[
8
]
he derived exact formulae for the general versions of these sums in two cases :
\chi
even and
\chi
odd.

E. Alkan in
[
1
]
derived exact formulae for the sums
\sum\limits_{\chi\,\text{odd}}|L(1,\chi)|^{2}
and
\sum\limits_{\chi\,\text{even}}|L(2,\chi)|^{2}
using weighted averages of Gauss and Ramanujan sums. His formulae involved Jordan totient function and Euler totient function (See the next section for definitions). Alkan employed certain exact evaluations of trigonometric sums appearing in
[
2
]
to enable these computations. The computations he performed in this paper were so extensive and beautiful so that some of the identities he derived during these computations gave rise a sequence of papers starting with those by L. Toth
[
14
]
and K V Nambooothiri
[
11
]
.

After Alkan, many other authors also attempted the problem of finding mean square values of
L(r,\chi)
for various values of
r
. A general formula was provided by T. Okamoto and T. Onozuka in
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
using a technique suggested by S. Louboutin in
[
8
]
. Note that this technique was different from the one used by Alkan in
[
1
]
. The mean square values and certain related problems were dealt in some other papers as well, see example,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
, and the recent papers
[
5
]
,
[
18
]
. In
[
5
]
, X. Lin provided a general inductive formula for computing the sum (
1
).

Some other closely related problems were attempted by a few authors. In
[
19
]
, W. Zhang derived an asymptotic (not exact) formula for
|L(1,\chi)|^{4}
over odd Dirichlet characters. He used Abel’s identity and Cauchy’s inequality in addition to the orthogonality properties of characters to arrive at his estimate. Alkan in
[
3
]
derived the sum
\sum\limits_{\chi\,\text{odd}}(L(1,\chi))^{r}
where
r\geq 1
. In
[
9
]
, S. Louboutin computed
\sum\limits_{\chi\,\text{odd}}\chi(c)|L(1,\chi)|^{2}
where c, a positive integer with some extra conditions. Computation of such sums, known as twisted sums is another problem of active interest.

Note that Alkan’s techniques in
[
1
]
were completely different from the other derivations used in computing the square sums. It was also observed by Alkan that his techniques could be used to determine the sum (
1
) for any larger
r(\geq 3)
, but at the cost of increasingly complex computations. We here undertake these complex comuptations and use the same techniques used by Alkan in
[
1
]
to derive exact formulae for
\sum\limits_{\chi(-1)=-1}|L(3,\chi)|^{2}
and
\sum\limits_{\chi(-1)=1}|L(4,\chi)|^{2}
.
We also derive some trigonometric identities during these computations that could be of independent interest.

2.
Notations and some elementary results

In this section we introduce the basic definitions and some identities that we use throughout this paper. The definitions of terms we do not define, but appear in this paper can be found in
[
4
]
or
[
10
]
. For a positive integer
k
, if
\chi
is a Dirichlet character modulo
k
, then
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{n}
for some natural number
n
. The parity of
\chi
is the parity of this
n
. Hence
\chi
is odd if
n
is odd and even otherwise.

The Gauss sum
G(z,\chi)
for any complex number
z
is defined as

(2)

\displaystyle G(z,\chi):=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\chi(m)e^{\frac{2\pi imz}{k}}.

When
\chi=\chi_{0}
is the principal character, Gauss sum becomes the Ramanujan sum
R_{k}(z)
:

(3)

\displaystyle R_{k}(z):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}^{k}e^{\frac{2\pi imz}{k}}.

The Jordan totient function
J_{k}(n)
is defined by

(4)

\displaystyle J_{k}(n):=n^{k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{k}}\right).

A similar type of function which we use in this paper is
\phi_{k}(n)
, defined as the sum of the
k
th powers of numbers
\leq n
and relatively prime to
n
.

By
B_{q}
we mean the
q
th
Bernoulli number. See
[
4
, Chapter 12]
for definition and other properties of Bernoulli numbers and Bernoulli polynomials. The values of Bernoulli numbers we use in this paper are
B_{0}=1
,
B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}
,
B_{2}=\frac{1}{6}
,
B_{3}=0
,
B_{4}=-\frac{1}{30}
.

For a positive integer
m
, by
S(m,\chi)
we mean the sum

(5)

\displaystyle S(m,\chi):=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}(\frac{j}{k})^{m}G(j,\chi).

Some other important identities that we use in our computation are listed below:

(6)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{k})}=\frac{J_{2}(k)}{3}\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{alkan2011values}{}{}, Identity 5.16]}}.

(7)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{r}e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\binom{r}{j}k^{j}\lim\limits_{w\rightarrow\frac{2\pi im}{k}}\frac{d^{r-j}}{dw^{r-j}}\left(\frac{1}{e^{w}-1}\right)\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{alkan2011values}{}{}, Section 2]}}.

The following identities can be easily computed using (
7
):

(8)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}se^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}=\frac{k}{e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1}.

(9)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}se^{\frac{-2\pi ims}{k}}=-k-\frac{k}{e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1}.

(10)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{2}e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}=\frac{k^{2}}{e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1}-\frac{2ke^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{2}}.

(11)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{2}e^{\frac{-2\pi ims}{k}}=-k^{2}-\frac{k^{2}}{e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1}-\frac{2ke^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{2}}.

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{v+1}kr!}{i^{r}2^{r-1}\pi^{r}}L(r,\chi)=\sum\limits_{q=0}^{2[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r}{q}B_{q}S(r-q,\chi)\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{alkan2011values}{}{}, Theorem 1]} }

where
\chi
and
r\geq 1
have same parity.

Now we may derive a useful identity quickly. Using identity (5.12) in
[
2
]
we have

\displaystyle\frac{k^{4}}{\pi^{4}}L(4,\chi_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{k})}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\cos(\frac{2\pi m}{k})}{\sin^{4}(\frac{\pi m}{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{k})}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{(1-2\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{k}))}{\sin^{4}(\frac{\pi m}{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sin^{4}(\frac{\pi m}{k})}-\frac{1}{3}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi m}{k})}.

Using
\frac{k^{4}}{\pi^{4}}L(4,\chi_{0})=\frac{k^{4}}{\pi^{4}}\zeta(4)\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{4}}\right)=\frac{J_{4}(k)}{90}

[
4
, Chapter 11]
and identity (
6
) above we get

(13)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sin^{4}(\frac{\pi m}{k})}=\frac{J_{4}(k)}{45}+\frac{2}{9}J_{2}(k).

We now state four identities.

Lemma 2.1
.

(14)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{3}e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}}=\frac{k^{3}}{e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1}-\frac{3k^{2}e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{2}}+\frac{3ke^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}+3ke^{\frac{4\pi im}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{3}}.

(15)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{3}e^{\frac{-2\pi imj}{k}}=-\frac{k^{3}e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}}{e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1}-\frac{3k^{2}e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{2}}-\frac{(3ke^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}+3ke^{\frac{4\pi im}{k}})}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{3}}.

(16)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{4}e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}}=\frac{k^{4}}{e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1}-\frac{4k^{3}e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{2}}+\frac{6k^{2}e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}+6k^{2}e^{\frac{4\pi im}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{3}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(4ke^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}+16ke^{\frac{4\pi im}{k}}+4ke^{\frac{6\pi im}{k}})}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{4}}.

(17)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{4}e^{\frac{-2\pi imj}{k}}=-\frac{k^{4}e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}}{e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1}-\frac{4k^{3}e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{2}}-\frac{(6k^{2}e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}+6k^{2}e^{\frac{4\pi im}{k}})}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{3}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(4ke^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}+16ke^{\frac{4\pi im}{k}}+4ke^{\frac{6\pi im}{k}})}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{4}}.

Proof.

The identity (
14
) can be derived by putting
r=3
in (
7
) and by applying the limit.

Now we derive the second identity as follows:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{3}e^{\frac{-2\pi imj}{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{3}e^{\frac{2\pi i(k-m)j}{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{3}}{e^{\frac{2\pi i(k-m)}{k}}-1}-\frac{3k^{2}e^{\frac{2\pi i(k-m)}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{2\pi i(k-m)}{k}}-1)^{2}}+\frac{3ke^{\frac{2\pi i(k-m)}{k}}+3ke^{\frac{4\pi i(k-m)}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{2\pi i(k-m)}{k}}-1)^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{3}}{e^{\frac{-2\pi im}{k}}-1}-\frac{3k^{2}e^{\frac{-2\pi im}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{-2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{2}}+\frac{3ke^{\frac{-2\pi im}{k}}+3ke^{\frac{-4\pi im}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{-2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{3}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k^{3}}{e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1}-\frac{3k^{2}e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{2}}-\frac{(3ke^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}+3ke^{\frac{4\pi im}{k}})}{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)^{3}}.

The last line above is obtained from the fact that

e^{\frac{-2\pi im}{k}}-1=\frac{1-e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}}{e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}}=-\frac{(e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}-1)}{e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}}.

∎

3.
Main Results and proofs

We state below the main results we prove in this paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

The formula

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}|L(3,\chi)|^{2}=\frac{\pi^{6}}{90k^{6}}\phi(k)\left(\frac{J_{6}(k)}{21}-J_{2}(k)\right)

holds for all
k\geq 3
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The formula

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ even}\end{subarray}}|L(4,\chi)|^{2}=\frac{\pi^{8}}{27k^{8}}\phi(k)\left(\frac{J_{8}(k)}{700}+\frac{J_{4}(k)}{150}+\frac{2}{21}J_{2}(k)\right)

holds for all
k\geq 3
.

From the above two results, it can be seen that the average values of
|L(3,\chi)|^{2}
over all odd characters modulo
k
is

\frac{2\pi^{6}}{90k^{6}}\left(\frac{J_{6}(k)}{21}-J_{2}(k)\right)
and
|L(4,\chi)|^{2}
over all even characters modulo
k
is

\frac{2\pi^{8}}{27k^{8}}\left(\frac{J_{8}(k)}{700}+\frac{J_{4}(k)}{150}+\frac{2}{21}J_{2}(k)\right)
.

Now we proceed to prove the above two results. The outline of the proof is the following:

(1)

Use identity (
12
) connecting
L(r,\chi)
, Bernoulli numbers, and Gauss sums with
r=3,4
and expand it.

(2)

Take the sum of
|L(r,\chi)|^{2}
over
\chi
having same parity as that of
r
, further expand it using the complex number property
|z|^{2}=z\overline{z}
.

(3)

The expansion consists of terms of the form
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}j^{p}s^{q}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}e^{\frac{2\pi im(j\pm s)}{k}}
for certain values of
p,q
. Simplify these terms further.

(4)

In the simplification of terms obtained in the last step, the exponential power expands to trigonometric identities, necessiating the computation of sums of the trigonometric functions of the form
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sin^{p}(\frac{\pi m}{k})}
where
p
takes values 2, 4,…. These sums results in certain combinations of the Euler totient function and Jordan totient function which is what we intend to prove.

Proof of theorem
3.1
.

Put
r=3
in equation (
12
) and use the values of the Bernoulli numbers to get

(18)

\displaystyle L(3,\chi)=\frac{-2i\pi^{3}}{3k}\left[S(3,\chi)-\frac{3}{2}S(2,\chi)+\frac{1}{2}S(1,\chi)\right].

If
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
and
r\geq 1
are of opposite parity, then

(19)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{q=0}^{2[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r}{q}B_{q}S(r-q.\chi)=0\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{alkan2011values}{}{}, Theorem 1]} }.

When
r=2
, we get

(20)

\displaystyle S(2,\chi)-S(1,\chi)+\frac{1}{6}S(0,\chi)=0.

Since
S(0,\chi)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}G(j,\chi)=0
,

(21)

\displaystyle S(2,\chi)=S(1,\chi).

From equations (
18
) and (
21
) we have

\displaystyle L(3,\chi)=\frac{-2i\pi^{3}}{3k}\left[S(3,\chi)-S(1,\chi)\right].

Since
G(k,\chi)=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\chi(m)=0
for any non principal character
[
4
, Theroem 6.10]
, we have

(22)

\displaystyle L(3,\chi)=\frac{-2i\pi^{3}}{3k^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{k^{2}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{3}G(j,\chi)-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}jG(j,\chi)\right].

Now we are ready to compute the mean square sum.
Using the above identity we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}|L(3,\chi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{6}}{9k^{4}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{1}{k^{2}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{3}G(j,\chi)-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}jG(j,\chi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{6}}{9k^{4}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{3}G(j,\chi)-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}jG(j,\chi)\right)\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{3}\overline{G(s,\chi)}-\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s\overline{G(s,\chi)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{6}}{9k^{8}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}j^{3}s^{3}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{k-1}e^{\frac{2\pi i(mj-ns)}{k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi(n)}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{6}}{9k^{6}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}j^{3}s\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{k-1}e^{\frac{2\pi i(mj-ns)}{k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi(n)}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{6}}{9k^{6}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}js^{3}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{k-1}e^{\frac{2\pi i(mj-ns)}{k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi(n)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{6}}{9k^{4}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}js\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{k-1}e^{\frac{2\pi i(mj-ns)}{k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi(n)}.

There are exactly
\frac{\phi(k)}{2}
odd characters modulo
k\geq 3
. Therefore
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(u)=\frac{\phi(k)}{2}
or
-\frac{\phi(k)}{2}
depending on if
u=1
or
u=-1
respectively. If
u
is neither of these modulo
k
, then
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(u)=0
. Thus

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi(n)}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(mn^{-1})=\frac{\phi(k)}{2}\text{ or }-\frac{\phi(k)}{2}

when
m=n
or
m=k-n
repectively and

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi(n)}=0

otherwise.

Hence we get

(23)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod }k)\\
\chi\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}|L(3,\chi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{6}}{9k^{8}}\frac{\phi(k)}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}j^{3}s^{3}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}e^{\frac{2\pi im(j-s)}{k}}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}j^{3}s^{3}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}e^{\frac{2\pi im(j+s)}{k}}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{6}}{9k^{6}}\frac{\phi(k)}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}j^{3}s\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}e^{\frac{2\pi im(j-s)}{k}}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}j^{3}s\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}e^{\frac{2\pi im(j+s)}{k}}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{6}}{9k^{6}}\frac{\phi(k)}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}js^{3}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}e^{\frac{2\pi im(j-s)}{k}}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}js^{3}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}e^{\frac{2\pi im(j+s)}{k}}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{6}}{9k^{4}}\frac{\phi(k)}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}js\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}e^{\frac{2\pi im(j-s)}{k}}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}js\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq k\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}e^{\frac{2\pi im(j+s)}{k}}\right).

Take the first term in the above expression. If we write
\alpha=e^{\frac{2\pi im}{k}}

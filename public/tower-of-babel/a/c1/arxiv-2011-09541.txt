Regularity of a gradient flow generated by the anisotropic Landau-de Gennes energy with a singular potential
Yuning Liu, Xin Yang Lu, Xiang Xu

Abstract
In this paper we study a gradient flow generated by the Landau-de Gennes free energy that describes nematic liquid crystal configurations in the space of Q Q -tensors. This free energy density functional is composed of three quadratic terms as the elastic energy density part, and a singular potential in the bulk part that is considered as a natural enforcement of a physical constraint on the eigenvalues of Q Q . The system is a non-diagonal parabolic system with a singular potential which trends to infinity logarithmically when the eigenvalues of Q Q approach the physical boundary. We give a rigorous proof that for rather general initial data with possibly infinite free energy, the system has a unique strong solution after any positive time t 0 t_{0} . Furthermore, this unique strong solution detaches from the physical boundary after a sufficiently large time T 0 T_{0} . We also give estimate of the Hausdorff measure of the set where the solution touches the physical boundary and thus prove a partial regularity result of the solution in the intermediate stage ( 0 , T 0 ) (0,T_{0}) .

Introduction

The Landau-de Gennes theory is a continuum theory of nematic liquid crystals
[
10
]
.
When formulating static or dynamic continuum theories a crucial step is to select an appropriate order parameter that captures the microscopic
structure of the rod-like molecule systems. In our framework the order parameter is a matrix-valued function that takes values in the following so called
Q
-tensor space

\mathcal{Q}:=\Big\{M\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\big|\operatorname{tr}M=0;\,M=M^{T}\Big\}.

(1.1)

It is considered as a suitably normalized second order moment of the
probability distribution function that dictates locally preferred orientations of nematic molecular directors (cf.

[
2
,
24
,
23
]
).

To formulate the problem, let
\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the unit box/square in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n=2
or
3
. For each order parameter
Q:\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathcal{Q}
, the associated free energy functional
\mathcal{E}(Q)
consists of the elastic
and the bulk parts, which reads

\mathcal{E}(Q):=\mathcal{G}(Q)+\mathcal{B}(Q)-\alpha\|Q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}.

(1.2)

Here
\mathcal{G}
stands for the anisotropic elastic
energy that contains three quadratic terms of
\nabla{Q}
:

\mathcal{G}(Q):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\big(L_{1}{\partial}_{k}Q_{ij}{\partial}_{k}Q_{ij}+L_{2}{\partial}_{j}Q_{ik}{\partial}_{k}Q_{ij}+L_{3}{\partial}_{j}Q_{ij}{\partial}_{k}Q_{ik}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\mbox{if }Q\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\\
+\infty,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}
are material dependent constants. Here and in the sequel
\partial_{k}Q_{ij}
denotes the
k
-th spatial partial derivative of the
ij
-th component
of
Q
, and we adopt Einstein
summation convention by summing over repeated Latin letters.
Following
[
21
]
, we assume

L_{1}>3|L_{2}+L_{3}|,

(1.4)

which ensures that (
1.3
) fulfills the strong Legendre condition.

Further,

\mathcal{B}(Q)
denotes the bulk energy

\mathcal{B}(Q):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi(Q)\,\mathrm{d}{x},

where the integrand
\psi(Q)
is the singular potential introduced in
[
4
]
:

\psi(Q):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{A}_{Q}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\rho(p)\ln{\rho(p)}\,\mathrm{d}p,\quad\mbox{if }-\frac{1}{3}<\lambda_{i}(Q)<\frac{2}{3},\;1\leq i\leq 3,\\
+\infty,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(1.5)

Here
\lambda_{i}(Q)
denotes the
i
-th eigenvalue of the matrix
Q
and
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
is the admissible class defined by

\mathcal{A}_{Q}=\left\{\rho(p):\mathbb{S}^{2}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}^{+}}~\big|~\,\|\rho\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=1;\;\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\big(p\otimes{p}-\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{I}\big)\rho(p)\,\mathrm{d}{p}=Q\right\}.

It is noted that the singular potential (
1.5
) imposes physical constraints on the eigenvalues of
Q
.
Futher,
\alpha>0
in (
1.2
) is a temperature dependent constant which characterizes the relative intensity of the molecular Brownian motion and the molecular interaction
[
4
]
. We refer interested readers to
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
3
,
16
,
22
]

for detailed discussions of basic analytic properties of
\psi
, such as convexity, smoothness
in its effective domain, blow-up rates near the physical boundary, etc. Meanwhile, various problems in static and dynamic configurations concerning
\psi
can be found in
[
7
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
29
]
. Specifically, the free energy in related dynamic problems considered so far in the existing literature
[
12
,
15
,
16
,
29
]
only involves the

L_{1}
isotropic term. Therefore, we are motivated to study the dynamic problem whose free energy contains anisotropic
L_{2},L_{3}
terms.
It is worth pointing out that the presence of such terms is more than a mere
technical challenge, since
they make it impossible to recover any kind of maximum principle, which
was crucial in
[
29
]
.

This paper is concerned with a rigorous study of the gradient flow
generated by
\mathcal{E}(Q)
in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathcal{Q})
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rlr}\partial_{t}Q(t,\cdot)&\in-\partial\mathcal{E}(Q(t,\cdot)),&t>0,\\
Q(0,x)&=Q_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

subject to periodic boundary condition

Q(t,x+e_{i})=Q(t,x),\quad\mbox{for }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\partial\mathbb{T}^{n}.

(1.7)

Here in (
1.6
),
\partial\mathcal{E}(Q)
is formally the variation of the free energy
(
1.2
). However, due to the singular feature of
\psi(Q)
, it should be understood as sub-differential (see Lemma

3.3
for more details.)

Parallel to the
Q
-tensor space (
1.1
),
we introduce the
physical

Q
-tensor space by

\mathcal{Q}_{phy}:=\Big\{M\in\mathcal{Q}\big|\,-\frac{1}{3}<\lambda_{1}(M)\leq\lambda_{2}(M)\leq\lambda_{3}(M)<\frac{2}{3}\Big\},

(1.8)

where
\lambda_{i}(M)
denotes the
i
-th eigenvalue of the matrix
M
, ordered non-decreasingly. Any element in
\mathcal{Q}_{phy}
is called a physical
Q
-tensor.

Our first main result ensures the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the gradient flow (
1.6
) with rather general initial data.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n=2
or
3
. For any initial data

Q_{0}\in\overline{\{Q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathcal{Q}_{phy})\mid\mathcal{E}(Q)<\infty\}}^{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})},

(1.9)

there exists a unique
global solution
Q(t,x):\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathcal{Q}_{phy}
of (
1.6
) such that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Q_{ij}

\displaystyle=2L_{1}\Delta{Q}_{ij}+2(L_{2}+L_{3}){\partial}_{j}{\partial}_{k}Q_{ik}-\frac{2}{3}(L_{2}+L_{3}){\partial}_{k}{\partial}_{\ell}Q_{\ell k}\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial{Q}_{ij}}+\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\Big(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial{Q}}\Big)\delta_{ij}+2\alpha Q_{ij}

(1.10)

holds almost everywhere in
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
. And for any fixed
t_{0}>0
, the solution satisfies

Q\in L^{\infty}(t_{0},\infty;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})),\qquad\partial_{t}Q\in L^{2}_{loc}(t_{0},\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})),

(1.11)

and the energy dissipative equality

\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\big(\|\partial_{t}Q(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}+\|\partial\mathcal{E}(Q(t,\cdot))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\big)\,\mathrm{d}{t}=2\mathcal{E}(Q(t_{0}))-2\mathcal{E}(Q(T))

(1.12)

for all
0<t_{0}<T<+\infty
. Further,
Q(t,\cdot)
is physical in the sense that

Q(t,x)\in\mathcal{Q}_{phy},\quad\forall t>0,\;a.e.\;x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

(1.13)

It is worthy to point out that due to the energy dissipative property of the gradient flow as well as the convexity of the singular potential, for any
T>0
one can formally establish the a priori estimate of
Q
in
L^{\infty}_{loc}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))\cap L^{2}_{loc}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
. As a consequence, existence of weak solutions to (
1.6
) can be achieved by using two level
approximation schemes as in
[
29
]
, i.e., regularizing the initial data and the singular free energy. However, such
arguments involve fairly complicated approximation procedures. Fortunately Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré
[
1
]
provides a powerful framework to obtain the
solution under very general assumptions of the initial data.

To establish higher regularity, namely a uniform-in-time
H^{2}
bound of
the solution
Q
, essential difficulties arise from the anisotropic terms. Without the
L_{2}+L_{3}
terms,
the convexity of
\psi
as well as the classical
L^{1}-L^{\infty}
estimate of heat equation ensure the
strict physicality at any positive time (see section
8
in
[
29
]
for details) and henceforth conventional energy method applies.
Concerning the gradient flow (
1.6
), unfortunately the anisotropic terms make such maximum
principle argument invalid. As a consequence, the proof of higher regularity of the solution becomes quite subtle in the sense that

Q
might not stay inside any compact subset of
\mathcal{Q}_{phy}
. To overcome such a difficulty, we need to make a careful exploitation of its gradient flow structure, as well as to combine several results on the gradient flow theory given in
[
1
]
and Gamma-convergence of gradient flows discussed in
[
28
,
27
]
.
These lead to the next theorem which improves the regularity by establishing the uniform-in-time
H^{2}
bound of the unique solution to the gradient flow (
1.6
). Further, it can be shown that this unique solution detaches from its physical boundary after a sufficiently large time
T_{0}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
t_{0}>0
the solution established in Theorem

1.1
enjoys the improved regularity
Q\in L^{\infty}(t_{0},+\infty;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
, and for almost every
t\geq t_{0}
there holds

\|\Delta Q(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C_{L}\left(e^{4\alpha}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(Q(t_{0}))-\inf\mathcal{E}+1}+2\alpha\|Q(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\right),

(1.14)

where
C_{L}
is expressed by

C_{L}:=\frac{1}{2(L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|)}\sqrt{\frac{L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|}{L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|-2\sqrt{L_{1}|L_{2}+L_{3}|}}}

(1.15)

Furthermore, under the stronger assumption

L_{1}-3|L_{2}+L_{3}|-\alpha{C}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}^{2}>0,

(1.16)

where
C_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}=(2\pi)^{n}
is the Poincaré constant in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
,
there exists
T_{0}>0
such that the unique solution
is strictly physical
for all
t\geq T_{0}
in the sense that

-\dfrac{1}{3}+\kappa\leq\lambda_{i}(Q(t,x))\leq\dfrac{2}{3}-\kappa,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}

(1.17)

for some constant
\kappa\in(0,1/6)
.

During the period
(0,T_{0})
, a partial regularity result of the unique solution can be established, i.e. Hausdorff dimension
of the set where the solution touches the physical boundary
\partial\mathcal{Q}_{phy}
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
Q(t,x)

be the unique strong solution of (
1.6
) established in Theorem
1.2
. Then for a.e.
t\in(0,T_{0})
, the contact set

\Sigma_{t}:=\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\mid Q(t,x)\in{\partial}\mathcal{Q}_{phy}\}

(1.18)

has the following estimate:

•

\mbox{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Sigma_{t})\leq 2
for
n=3
.

•

\mbox{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Sigma_{t})=0
for
n=2
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Some notations and preliminaries are provided in Section 2. The proofs of the three main results, namely Theorems

1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
,
are given in Sections 3, 4, 5, respectively.

2
Preliminaries

We start with a few basic notations in
Q
-tensor theory.
For any
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
,
|Q|:=\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(Q^{t}Q)}
represents the Frobenius norm of
Q
.
The gradient of the function
\psi(Q)
will be abbreviated by
\psi^{\prime}(Q)
, and its components are denoted by
\psi^{\prime}_{ij}(Q):=\frac{{\partial}\psi(Q)}{{\partial}Q_{ij}}
.
Moreover, we denote
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathcal{Q})
the Hilbert space endowed
with the
L^{2}
metric

\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mbox{tr}(Q^{t}Q)}=\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mbox{tr}(Q^{2})},\quad\mbox{for }Q:\mathbb{T}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}.

Here and after, for brevity,
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}
will often be written as

\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
, or simply
\|\cdot\|
.

Next we provide some preliminaries of gradient flow theory in a Hilbert space.
We start with some basic definitions in a Hilbert space
H
, with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and norm
\|\cdot\|
(cf.
[
9
,
13
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

A function
f:H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is called proper if
f
is not identically equal to
+\infty
. The effective
domain of
f
is defined by

D(f)=\big\{u\in H|\,f(u)<+\infty\big\}.

By
[
4
]
, the effective domain
D(\psi)
is equivalent to
(
1.5
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, a
\lambda
-convex function
F:H\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
is a function satisfying

F((1-t)u+tv)\leq(1-t)F(u)+tF(v)-\frac{\lambda}{2}t(1-t)\|u-v\|^{2},\qquad\forall u,v\in H.

For each
u\in H
,
\partial{F}[u]
is defined as the set of
w\in H
such that

F(u)+\langle w,v-u\rangle+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|u-v\|^{2}\leq F(v),\quad\forall v\in H.

The mapping
\partial{F}:H\rightarrow 2^{H}
is called the subdifferential of
F
. Further,
We say
u\in D(\partial F)
, the domain of
\partial F
, provided
\partial{F}[u]
is not empty.

Definition 2.3
.

We say
u(t)
is a gradient flow of
F
starting from
u_{0}\in H
if it is a locally absolutely continuous
curve in
(0,+\infty)
such that

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t)\in-\partial F(u(t)),\quad\mbox{a.e.}\;t>0\\
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}u(t)=u_{0}.\end{cases}

(2.1)

The next result is due to
[
1
, Theorem 4.0.4]
which was originally stated under metric space setting. For the purpose of proving Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to rewrite it in the Hilbert space setting:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
F:H\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper,
bounded from below, and lower semicontinuous functional.
Suppose moreover that
F
is
\lambda
-convex, which, since we work
in an Hilbert space, is equivalent to
assuming that, for each
\tau\in(0,\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}})
with
\lambda^{-}:=\max\{0,-\lambda\}
and each fixed
w\in D(F)
,
the functional

\Phi(\tau,w;v)=\frac{1}{2\tau}\|v-w\|^{2}+F(v),~\forall v\in D(F)

(2.2)

satisfies the following inequality for every
v_{0},v_{1}\in D(F)
:

\displaystyle\Phi(\tau,w;t)\leq(1-t)\Phi(\tau,w;v_{0})+t\Phi(\tau,w;v_{1})-\frac{1+\lambda\tau}{2\tau}t(1-t)\|v_{0}-v_{1}\|^{2}.

Then for each
u_{0}\in\overline{D(F)}
,
u(t)=\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}J_{t/k}^{k}(u_{0})
with

J_{\tau}
being the resolvent

X\in J_{\tau}(Y)\Longleftrightarrow X\in\mbox{argmin}\Big\{F(\cdot)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|Y-\cdot\|^{2}\Big\},

(2.3)

satisfies

1.

Variational inequality:
u
is the unique solution to the
evolution variational inequality

\frac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\|u(t)-v\|^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|u(t)-v\|^{2}+F(u(t))\leq F(v),\qquad\mbox{for a.e. }t>0\mbox{ and }v\in D(F),

(2.4)

among all the locally absolutely continuous curves such that

u(t)\rightarrow u_{0}
as
t\downarrow 0^{+}
.

2.

Regularizing effect:
u
is locally Lipschitz regular, and

u(t,\cdot)\in D(\mathcal{E})
for all
t>0
.

Remark 2.1
.

It is well known that for a
\lambda
-convex function
F:H\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
, a locally absolutely continuous curve

u(t)
in
(0,+\infty)
satisfies (
2.1
) if and only if it satisfies the evolution variational inequality (
2.4
).

Now we turn to the
\Gamma
-convergence of gradient flows in a Hilbert space, a theory developed in
[
27
]
and
[
28
]
.
Let
\{u_{n}\}
be the solution to the gradient flow

\partial_{t}u_{n}=-\nabla E_{n}(u_{n})

(2.5)

of a
C^{1}
functional sequence
\{E_{n}\}
. Assume
E_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to a functional
F
, and there is a general sense of convergence
u_{n}\overset{S}{\rightarrow}u
, relative to which the
\Gamma
-convergence of
E_{n}
to
F
holds. We introduce the “energy-excess”
along a family of curves
u_{n}(t)
with
u_{n}(t)\overset{S}{\rightarrow}u(t)
by setting

\tilde{D}(t)=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{n}(u_{n}(t))-F(u(t)).

The main result of
[
28
]
is the following:

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume
E_{n}
and
F
satisfy a
\Gamma-\liminf
relation: if
u_{n}\overset{S}{\rightarrow}u
as
n\rightarrow\infty
then

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{n}(u_{n})\geq F(u).

Assume that the following two additional conditions hold:

1.

(Lower bound on the velocities) If
u_{n}(t)\overset{S}{\rightarrow}u(t)
for all
t\in[0,T)
then there exists

f\in L^{1}(0,T)
such that for every
s\in[0,T)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{s}\|\partial_{t}u_{n}(t)\|_{H}^{2}\,dt\geq\int_{0}^{s}\big[\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{H}^{2}-f(t)\tilde{D}(t)\big]\,dt.

(2.6)

2.

(Lower bound for the slopes) If
u_{n}\overset{S}{\rightarrow}u
then

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla E_{n}(u_{n})\|_{H}^{2}\,dt\geq\|\nabla F(u)\|_{H}^{2}-C\tilde{D},

(2.7)

where
C
is a universal constant, and
\|\nabla F(u)\|
denotes the minimal norm of the elements in
\partial F(u)
.

Assume
u_{n}(t)
is a family of solutions to (
2.5
) on
[0,T)
with
u_{n}(t)\overset{S}{\rightarrow}u(t)
for all
t\in[0,T)
,
such that

E_{n}(u_{n}(0))-E_{n}(u_{n}(t))=\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}u_{n}(s)\|_{H}^{2}\,ds,\quad\forall t\in[0,T).

Assume also that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{n}(u_{n}(0))=F(u(0)),

then
u\in H^{1}(0,T;H)
and is a solution to
\partial_{t}u\in-\partial F(u)
on
[0,T)
. Moreover,
\tilde{D}(t)=0
for all
t
(that is the solutions “remain well-prepared”)
and

\|\partial_{t}u_{n}\|_{H}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{H},\quad\|\nabla E_{n}(u_{n})\|_{H}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\|\nabla F(u)\|_{H}\quad\mbox{in }\;L^{2}(0,T).

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1
: Existence of solutions

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
First of all, with the choices
H=L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathcal{Q})
and
F=\mathcal{E}
(defined in
(
1.2
)),
we show that the assumptions in Proposition
2.1
are satisfied, so that there exists a unique solution
Q(t,\cdot)

in variational inequality setting (
2.4
) (see Proposition
3.1
below). Moreover, since the free energy is
-2\alpha
convex, the solution achieved in Proposition
3.1
is equivalent to the solution of the gradient flow (
1.6
) in sub-differential setting. As a consequence,
we compute explicitly the sub-differential of
\mathcal{E}
, and obtain a unique strong
solution to equation (
1.1
). Finally, we apply two theorems in
[
1
]
to show further regularity properties of
Q
in Theorem
1.1
.
Since all the following arguments are valid for both
\mathbb{T}^{3}
and
\mathbb{T}^{2}
with minor modifications, for brevity we discuss the case of
\mathbb{T}^{3}
only.

In this subsection we consider the following settings:

(H,\|\cdot\|)=L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathcal{Q}),\qquad F=\mathcal{E}(Q).

To begin with, we need to verify all assumptions in Proposition
2.1
are valid, which is given in the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

The free energy functional
\mathcal{E}
is proper, bounded from below,
-2\alpha
convex and lower
semicontinuous in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathcal{Q})
.

Proof.

First we show that the elastic energy
\mathcal{G}
is nonnegative, convex, and lower semicontinuous in

L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathcal{Q})
.
It is proved in
[
21
]
that when
L_{1}>0,L_{1}+L_{2}+L_{3}>0
,
\mathcal{G}
satisfies the strong Legendre condition, which implies the convexity of
\mathcal{G}
.
It suffices to show that
\mathcal{G}
is nonnegative when
Q\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, which follows from the coefficient assumption (
1.4
), integration by parts and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(Q)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(L_{1}\partial_{k}Q_{ij}\partial_{k}Q_{ij}+L_{2}\partial_{k}Q_{ik}\partial_{k}Q_{ij}+L_{3}\partial_{j}Q_{ij}\partial_{k}Q_{ik}\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[L_{1}\partial_{k}Q_{ij}\partial_{k}Q_{ij}+(L_{2}+L_{3})\partial_{j}Q_{ik}\partial_{k}Q_{ij}\Big]\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\big(L_{1}-3|L_{2}+L_{3}|\big)|\nabla{Q}|^{2}\,dx\geq 0.

(3.1)

Besides, since
\mathcal{G}
is convex and quadratic, it is lower semicontinuous
[
18
, Theorem 8.1]
.

Next we show that the functional
\mathcal{B}
is convex, bounded from below, and lower semicontinuous in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathcal{Q})
.
The convexity of
\mathcal{B}
follows from
[
4
,
16
]
. A lower bounded can be derived from the inequality
x\ln{x}\geq-1/e
for any
x\geq 0
:

\mathcal{B}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi(Q)\,\mathrm{d}{x}\geq-4\pi^{2}|\mathbb{T}^{3}|/e.

(3.2)

To show the lower semicontinuity of
\mathcal{B}
, let
Q_{n}\rightarrow Q
strongly in

L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
. If
\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\psi(Q_{n})=+\infty
on a set of positive measure, then
the proof is done. Thus upon subsequence we assume

Assume the opposite, i.e.

\displaystyle\geq\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{E}\psi(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}+\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\setminus E}\psi(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{E}\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\psi(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}-\dfrac{4\pi^{2}}{e}|\mathbb{T}^{3}\setminus E|=+\infty,

To sum up,

Lemma 3.3

For any

\displaystyle-\partial\mathcal{E}(Q)_{ij}

Proof.

To begin with, it is immediate to derive

\partial\mathcal{G}(Q)_{ij}=-2L_{1}\Delta Q_{ij}-2(L_{2}+L_{3})\partial_{k}\partial_{j}Q_{ik}+\frac{2}{3}(L_{2}+L_{3})\partial_{\ell}\partial_{k}Q_{k\ell}\delta_{ij}.

\partial\mathcal{B}(Q)=\Big\{\psi^{\prime}(Q)-\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{tr}(\psi^{\prime}(Q))\mathbb{I}_{3}\Big\}.

Case 1:

Let

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\big[\psi^{\prime}(Q):R\big]\,d{x}

\displaystyle=\lim_{{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{B}(Q-{\varepsilon}R)-\mathcal{B}(Q)}{{\varepsilon}}\geq-\langle\xi,R\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})},

which indicates

(\textbf{M3})
that

\mathcal{G}(Q_{n})-\alpha\|Q_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}\equiv\mathcal{G}(Q)-\alpha\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2},\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi(Q)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall n\geq 1.

In all, (
4.8
) is verified.

We proceed to show
\Gamma-\liminf
inequality. That is, for any
sequence
Q_{n}\rightarrow Q
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, it holds

\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n})\geq\mathcal{E}(Q).

(4.9)

Without loss of generality we assume

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n})<+\infty
. Again
upon subsequence, we may assume

\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n})=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n})<+\infty,\qquad\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n})<+\infty.

As discussed earlier we have
Q_{n}\rightarrow Q
strongly in

L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, and upon further extracting a subsequence we may
assume
Q_{n}\rightarrow Q
a.e. in
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. Hence together with the lower semicontinuity of
\mathcal{G}
achieved in
Lemma
3.1
it yields

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\|Q_{n}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}=\|Q(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2},\qquad\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{G}(Q_{n})\geq\mathcal{G}(Q).

(4.10)

It remains to prove

\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi(Q)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(4.11)

To proceed, we denote

D:=\big\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}:Q(x)\in D(\psi)\big\},

and we distinguish between two cases.

Case 1:
\mathcal{B}(Q)<+\infty
.
For any sufficiently small
{\varepsilon}>0
let us define

\mathbb{T}^{3}_{\varepsilon}=D\cap\left\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}:-\frac{1}{3}+{\varepsilon}\leq\lambda_{i}(Q(x))\leq\frac{2}{3}-{\varepsilon},\;1\leq i\leq 3\right\}.

(4.12)

Since
\big|\mathbb{T}^{3}\setminus{D}\big|=0
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\big[\psi_{n}(Q_{n})-\psi(Q)\big]\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\big[\psi_{n}(Q_{n})-\psi(Q)\big]\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{D\setminus\mathbb{T}^{3}_{\varepsilon}}\big[\psi_{n}(Q_{n})-\psi(Q)]\,\mathrm{d}{x}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{\varepsilon}}[\psi_{n}(Q_{n})-\psi(Q)\big]\,\mathrm{d}{x}.

(4.13)

On the one hand, for any
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{D\setminus\mathbb{T}^{3}_{\varepsilon}}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}(\inf_{n,P}\psi_{n}(P))\big|D\setminus\mathbb{T}^{3}_{\varepsilon}\big|=0,

and since
\psi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, as
|D\setminus\mathbb{T}^{3}_{\varepsilon}|\rightarrow 0
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\int_{D\setminus\mathbb{T}^{3}_{\varepsilon}}\psi(Q)\,\mathrm{d}{x}=0.

Hence for all
\delta>0
, there exists

{\varepsilon}_{0}={\varepsilon}_{0}(\delta)>0
, such that

\int_{D\setminus\mathbb{T}^{3}_{\varepsilon}}\big[\psi_{n}(Q_{n})-\psi(Q)\big]\,\mathrm{d}{x}>-\dfrac{\delta}{2},\quad\forall n\geq 1\quad\mbox{whenever }\,{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}.

(4.14)

On the other hand, it follows from

(\textbf{M5})
that
\psi_{n}(Q(x))\rightarrow\psi(Q(x))
,

\psi_{n}^{\prime}(Q(x))\rightarrow\psi^{\prime}(Q(x))
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{\varepsilon})
.
Thus it implies for any fixed
{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}
it
holds

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{\varepsilon}}[\psi_{n}(Q_{n})-\psi(Q)\big]\,\mathrm{d}{x}=0.

Specifically, for
{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}_{0}
there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
such that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}_{0}}}[\psi_{n}(Q_{n})-\psi(Q)\big]\,\mathrm{d}{x}>-\dfrac{\delta}{2},\quad\forall n\geq N_{0}

(4.15)

Combining (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) we finish the proof of
(
4.11
) by the arbitrariness of
\delta
.

Case 2:
\mathcal{B}(Q)=+\infty
.
In this case it suffices
to check

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}=+\infty.

(4.16)

If
|\mathbb{T}^{3}\setminus{D}|>0
, since
Q_{n}\rightarrow Q
a.e. it
follows from Egorov’s theorem that there exists a set
F\subset(\mathbb{T}^{3}\setminus{D})
,

|F|>0
, such that
Q_{n}\rightarrow Q
uniformly on
F
. Note that

Q(x)\in\mathcal{Q}\setminus D(\psi)
,
\forall x\in F
.
Hence the uniform convergence of
Q_{n}
to
Q
on
F
implies there
exists a sequence
{\varepsilon}_{n}\searrow 0^{+}
, such that

\lambda_{i}(Q_{n}(x))\leq-\frac{1}{3}+{\varepsilon}_{n}\;\mbox{or }\;\lambda_{i}(Q_{n}(x))\geq\frac{2}{3}-{\varepsilon}_{n},\quad\forall\,1\leq i\leq 3.

Then Fatou’s lemma and
(\textbf{M4})
yields

\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{F}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}\geq\int_{F}\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}=+\infty.

Therefore, (
4.16
) is verified
in this case.

Alternatively if
|\mathbb{T}^{3}\setminus{D}|=0
, then using similar
argument as in
Case 1
we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}=\int_{D}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}=\int_{D\setminus\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}}}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}}}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x},

where
\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}}
is given in (
4.12
). On one hand,

\int_{D\setminus\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}}}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}\geq(\inf_{n,P}\psi_{n}(P))\big|\mathbb{T}^{3}\setminus\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}}\big|\geq-\frac{4\pi|\mathbb{T}^{3}|^{2}}{e},\quad\forall{\varepsilon}>0.

(4.17)

On the other hand, since
\mathcal{B}(Q)=+\infty
and

\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}}\nearrow D
, we infer that
\forall M>0
, there exists

{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}}}\psi(Q)\,\mathrm{d}{x}>M+1+\frac{4\pi|\mathbb{T}^{3}|^{2}}{e},\quad\forall{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}.

Meanwhile,
(\textbf{M5})
indicates that

\psi_{n}(Q(x))\rightarrow\psi(Q(x))
,

\psi_{n}^{\prime}(Q(x))\rightarrow\psi^{\prime}(Q(x))
in

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}_{0}})
, which gives

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}_{0}}}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}_{0}}}\psi(Q)\,\mathrm{d}{x}.

Thus there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}_{0}}}\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}{x}>\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{{\varepsilon}_{0}}}\psi(Q)\,\mathrm{d}{x}-1>M+\frac{4\pi|\mathbb{T}^{3}|^{2}}{e},\quad\forall n\geq N.

(4.18)

Putting together (
4.17
) and (
4.18
), we
conclude that (
4.16
) is valid. Hence the proof is
complete.
∎

Since in Theorem
1.1
we allow the initial data

, one may not expect any further regularity
at time
t=0
. Thus we only aim to prove the regularity of
Q(t,\cdot)
for
positive times
t>0
.

Let
t_{0}>0
be arbitrarily given. Consider the gradient flow
sequence

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}Q_{n}\in-\partial\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n}),\quad t\geq t_{0},\\
Q_{n}(t_{0},x)=Q(t_{0},x)\end{cases}

(4.19)

subject to periodic boundary conditions (
1.7
).
Here we let time start from

only as a matter of

-2\alpha
-convex, proper, lower semicontinuous,

[

, Theorem 4.0.4]

, Proposition
3.2

and Proposition

.

As a consequence, we manage to prove

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
Q_{n}
be the solution of
(
4.19
). Then for every

\|\partial\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n}(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\rightarrow\|\partial\mathcal{E}(Q(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})},\quad\|\partial_{t}Q_{n}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\rightarrow\|\partial_{t}Q(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\;\text{ in }L^{2}(t_{0},T),

up to a subsequence.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{n}\overset{\Gamma}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{E}
. We aim to show that we are under
the hypotheses of Proposition
2.2
, where the general sense of convergence is considered to be the strong convergence
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathcal{Q})
.
Let us first check that the conditions (
2.6
), (
2.7
) are valid for the solutions
Q_{n}
of (
4.19
).

By Proposition
3.3
and
(\textbf{M3})
we have

\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n})(t)\leq\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q(t_{0}))\leq\mathcal{E}(Q(t_{0})),\quad\forall\,t>t_{0},n\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence following a similar argument in the proof of compactness part in Proposition

4.1
we get

\{Q_{n}\}\;\;\mbox{is uniformly bounded in }L^{\infty}(t_{0},T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})).

(4.20)

Next, by Proposition
3.3
and

, the energy equality

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\big(\|\partial_{t}Q_{n}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\partial\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n}(t,\cdot))\|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q(t_{0}))-2\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n}(T))

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathcal{E}(Q(t_{0}))-2\inf\mathcal{E}_{n}

(4.21)

holds for all
t_{0}<T<+\infty
. Hence

\{\partial_{t}Q_{n}\}\;\mbox{is uniformly bounded in}\;L^{2}(t_{0},T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))

(4.22)

and we can apply the Aubin-Lions lemma to yield

Q_{n}\rightarrow\tilde{Q}\;\;\mbox{strongly in }C\big([t_{0},T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\big),

(4.23)

and (
4
) implies that

\partial_{t}Q_{n}\rightarrow\partial_{t}\tilde{Q}\;\;\mbox{weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})).

(4.24)

Hence the condition (
2.6
) is satisfied by taking
f=0
. Since each

\mathcal{E}_{n}
is
-2\alpha

.

, thus all the

, and

Now we turn to the
H^{2}
-regularity of the sequence
\{Q_{n}\}
. The
following technical implies the coercivity of
\partial\mathcal{G}
:

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
P\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathcal{Q})
the operator
\partial\mathcal{G}(P)
satisfies

2(L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|)\|\Delta P\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq\langle-\partial\mathcal{G}(P),\Delta P\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq 2(L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|)\|\Delta{P}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}.

Proof.

Direct
computations yield

\displaystyle\langle-\partial\mathcal{G}(P),\Delta P\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\bigg[2L_{1}\Delta{P}_{ij}+2(L_{2}+L_{3})\partial_{k}\partial_{j}P_{ik}-\frac{2}{3}(L_{2}+L_{3})\partial_{\ell}\partial_{k}P_{k\ell}\delta_{ij}\bigg]\Delta{P}_{ij}\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=2L_{1}\|\Delta P\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+2(L_{2}+L_{3})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}P_{ik}\Delta P_{ij}\,\mathrm{d}{x},

where the last term of R.H.S. can be treated by integration by parts and the Hölder’s inequality:

\displaystyle 2(L_{2}+L_{3})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial_{k}\partial_{j}P_{ik}\Delta P_{ij}\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=2(L_{2}+L_{3})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial_{\ell}\partial_{j}P_{ik}\partial_{\ell}\partial_{k}P_{ij}\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq 2|L_{2}+L_{3}|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\sqrt{\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}(\partial_{\ell}\partial_{j}P_{ik})^{2}}\sqrt{\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}(\partial_{\ell}\partial_{k}P_{ij})^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=2|L_{2}+L_{3}|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla^{2}P|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{x}=2(|L_{2}+L_{3}|)\|\Delta P\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

∎

Next, we recall the notion of angles between two elements in a Hilbert space
(H,\langle,\rangle)
. Given two nonzero elements

u,v\in H
, the angle between
u
and
v
is the unique value

\angle(u,v):=\arccos\frac{\langle u,v\rangle}{\|u\|\|v\|}\in[0,\pi],\qquad\text{where}\quad\|u\|_{H}=\sqrt{\langle u,u\rangle}.

Then the following triangle inequality is valid.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any three unit vectors
\vec{u},\vec{v},\vec{w}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, it holds

\angle(\vec{u},\vec{v})\leq\angle(\vec{u},\vec{w})+\angle(\vec{v},\vec{w}),

(4.25)

where
\angle(\vec{u},\vec{v})\in[0,\pi]
stands for the angle between the two vectors
\vec{u}
and
\vec{v}
.

Proof.

By rotation invariance, we assume the unit vectors
\vec{u}
,
\vec{v}
lie on the
xy
-plane. And in particular, w.l.o.g. we suppose

\vec{u}
points in the direction of
x
-axis:

\vec{u}=\langle 1,0,0\rangle,\quad\vec{v}=\langle a,\sqrt{1-a^{2}},0\rangle,\quad\vec{w}=\langle c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\rangle\in\mathbb{S}^{2},\quad-1\leq a\leq 1.

Correspondingly, we have

\displaystyle\cos\angle(\vec{u},\vec{v})

\displaystyle=a,\quad\cos\angle(\vec{u},\vec{w})=c_{1},\quad\cos\angle(\vec{v},\vec{w})=ac_{1}+\sqrt{1-a^{2}}c_{2},

\displaystyle\sin\angle(\vec{u},\vec{w})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-c_{1}^{2}},\quad\sin\angle(\vec{v},\vec{w})=\sqrt{1-2a\sqrt{1-a^{2}}c_{1}c_{2}-a^{2}c_{1}^{2}-(1-a^{2})c_{2}^{2}}.

To prove (
4.25
), it is equivalent to show

\cos\angle(\vec{u},\vec{v})\geq\cos\big(\angle(\vec{u},\vec{w})+\angle(\vec{v},\vec{w})\big).

That is,

a\geq c_{1}(ac_{1}+\sqrt{1-a^{2}}c_{2})-\sqrt{1-c_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{1-2a\sqrt{1-a^{2}}c_{1}c_{2}-a^{2}c_{1}^{2}-(1-a^{2})c_{2}^{2}},

which is equivalent to

\sqrt{1-c_{1}^{2}}\sqrt{1-2a\sqrt{1-a^{2}}c_{1}c_{2}-a^{2}c_{1}^{2}-(1-a^{2})c_{2}^{2}}\geq a(c_{1}^{2}-1)+\sqrt{1-a^{2}}c_{1}c_{2}.

(4.26)

If the R.H.S. of (
4.26
) is non-positive, the proof is complete. Otherwise, it is equivalent to prove

\displaystyle\qquad(1-c_{1}^{2})\big[1-2a\sqrt{1-a^{2}}c_{1}c_{2}-a^{2}c_{1}^{2}-(1-a^{2})c_{2}^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq a^{2}(1-c_{1}^{2})^{2}+(1-a^{2})c_{1}^{2}c_{2}^{2}-2a\sqrt{1-a^{2}}(1-c_{1}^{2})c_{1}c_{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad(1-c_{1}^{2})(1-a^{2}c_{1}^{2})-(1-a^{2})c_{2}^{2}\geq a^{2}(1-c_{1}^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad(1-c_{1}^{2}-c_{2}^{2})(1-a^{2})\geq 0,

which is obviously true.
∎

Based on Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
, we show
that the
H^{2}
-norm of
Q_{n}
can be estimated in terms of

\|\partial\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n}(t))\|
uniformly in
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a.e.
t\in(t_{0},T)
, it holds

\displaystyle\big\|\Delta Q_{n}(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C_{L}\big(\big\|\partial\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n}(t,\cdot))\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+2\alpha\big\|Q_{n}(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\big),

where
C_{L}
is defined in (
1.15
).

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, we have that (up to a subsequence)
\|\partial{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(Q_{n}(t,\cdot))\|

is convergent to
\|\partial\mathcal{E}(Q(t,\cdot))\|
for almost every fixed

t\in[t_{0},T]
.
Hence for any fixed
t\in[t_{0},T]
(after
removing a set of measure zero), for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds
Recall (
4.6
), we have for every
n\geq 1
and almost every
t\in(t_{0},T)
that

\displaystyle\Big\|\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n}(t,\cdot))+\psi_{n}^{\prime}(Q_{n}(t,\cdot))-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\psi_{n}^{\prime})(Q_{n}(t,\cdot))\mathbb{I}_{3}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\partial\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n}(t,\cdot))\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+2\alpha\big\|Q_{n}(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

(4.27)

On the other hand, by
(\textbf{M6})
, and
\{Q_{n}\}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
, we know that

\Big\|\psi_{n}^{\prime}(Q_{n}(t,\cdot))-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\psi_{n}^{\prime})(Q_{n}(t,\cdot))\mathbb{I}_{3}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq\lambda_{n}\|Q_{n}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\Lambda_{n}|\mathbb{T}^{3}|<+\infty

which together with (
4
) implies that

\big\|\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n}(t,\cdot))\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}<\infty.

By lemma
4.3
, we conclude that

Q_{n}(t,\cdot)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, a.e.
t\geq 0
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the rest of this lemma, for simplicity
Q_{n}(\cdot,t)
is abbreviated by
Q_{n}
, and w.l.o.g. we assume
\|\Delta{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}>0
. By (
1.4
) and Lemma
4.3
,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n}):-\Delta{Q}_{n}\right)\,dx}{\|\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{2}}}\geq\frac{2(L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|)\|\Delta{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{2(L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|)\|\Delta{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\geq\frac{L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|}{L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|}>0.

Hence the angle between
\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n})
and
-\Delta{Q}_{n}
is bounded by

\angle\big(\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n}),-\Delta{Q}_{n}\big)\leq\arccos\frac{L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|}{L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|}.

Meanwhile, by
(\textbf{M1})
we know that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\partial\psi_{n}(Q_{n}):-\Delta{Q}_{n}\right)\,dx\geq 0,

which together with Lemma
4.4
gives

\displaystyle\angle\big(\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n}),\partial\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\angle\big(\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n}),-\Delta{Q}_{n}\big)+\angle\big(\partial\psi_{n}(Q_{n}),-\Delta{Q}_{n}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi}{2}+\arccos\frac{L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|}{L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|}.

As a consequence, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n}):\partial\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\right)\,dx

(4.28)

\displaystyle\geq\cos\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}+\arccos\frac{L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|}{L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|}\Big)\|\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|\partial\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\sqrt{L_{1}|L_{2}+L_{3}|}}{L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|}\|\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|\partial\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

(4.29)

Note that
2\sqrt{L_{1}|L_{2}+L_{3}|}/(L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|)<1
by (
1.4
).

In all, after combining Lemma
4.3
, (
4
), (
4.28
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we conclude that

\displaystyle\big(\|\partial\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+2\alpha\|Q_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}+\|\partial\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}-\frac{4\sqrt{L_{1}|L_{2}+L_{3}|}}{L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|}\|\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|\partial\psi_{n}(Q_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|-2\sqrt{L_{1}|L_{2}+L_{3}|}}{L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|}\|\partial\mathcal{G}(Q_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|-2\sqrt{L_{1}|L_{2}+L_{3}|}}{L_{1}+|L_{2}+L_{3}|}4(L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|)^{2}\|\Delta{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}

(4.30)

The proof is complete.
∎

.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Part 1: uniform-in-time bound (
1.14
)

It is proved in (

) that (up to a
subsequence)
Q_{n}(t)\rightarrow Q(t,\cdot)
in
C\big([t_{0},T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\big)
. Moreover, it follows from Proposition
4.2

that for a.e.
t\in(t_{0},T)
,
\|\Delta Q_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
is (up to a subsequence)
uniformly bounded in
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
\Delta Q(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
a.e., and from Lemma
4.2
we can further make
the following estimates

\displaystyle\|\Delta Q(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\Delta Q_{n}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}C_{L}\big(\|\partial\mathcal{E}_{n}(Q(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+2\alpha\|Q_{n}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\big)

\displaystyle=C_{L}\big(\|\partial\mathcal{E}(Q(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+2\alpha\|Q(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\big),

(4.31)

where
C_{L}
is defined in (
1.15
).

W.l.o.g. we assume
T=t_{0}+100
. In view of Lemma (
4.31
), it suffices to provide
the
L^{\infty}
-bound for
\|\partial\mathcal{E}(Q(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}
.
By equation (
1.12
) we have

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\|\partial\mathcal{E}(Q(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{t}\leq\mathcal{E}(Q(t_{0}))-\inf\mathcal{E}.

(4.32)

Thus for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\int_{t_{0}+n}^{t_{0}+n+1}\|\partial\mathcal{E}(Q(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{t}\leq\mathcal{E}(Q(t_{0}))-\inf\mathcal{E},

(4.33)

hence there exists a set of positive measure
A_{n}\subset[t_{0}+n,t_{0}+n+1]
such that

\|\partial\mathcal{E}(Q(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}(Q(t_{0}))-\inf\mathcal{E}+1,\quad\forall\,t\in A_{n}.

(4.34)

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\leq 99
, let us choose a time
s_{n}\in A_{n}
, and for
the sake of convenience we set
s_{0}=t_{0}
. Obviously
\{s_{n}\}_{n\leq 99}
is monotone increasing, and
s_{n+1}-s_{n}\leq 2
.

Let us then consider the gradient flow sequence

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}P_{n}\in-\partial\mathcal{E}(P_{n}),\\
P_{n}(0,\cdot)=Q(s_{n},\cdot),\end{cases}

(4.35)

subject to periodic boundary condition, whose solution
P_{n}
is given by the time shift
P_{n}(t,\cdot):=Q(s_{n}+t,\cdot)
,

\forall\,t\geq 0
. By Proposition
3.2
, the
function
t\mapsto e^{-2\alpha{t}}\|\partial\mathcal{E}(P_{n}(t))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
is
nonincreasing, thus together with (
4.34
) we have

\displaystyle\|\partial\mathcal{E}(Q(t+s_{n},\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\partial\mathcal{E}(P_{n}(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}\leq e^{4\alpha(s_{n+1}-s_{n})}\|\partial\mathcal{E}(P_{n}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{8\alpha}\|\partial\mathcal{E}(Q(s_{n},\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}\leq e^{8\alpha}(\mathcal{E}(Q(t_{0}))-\inf\mathcal{E}+1),

for a.e.
t\in(0,s_{n+1}-s_{n})
. Repeating this argument for all
n
finally gives

\|\partial\mathcal{E}(Q(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq e^{4\alpha}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(Q(t_{0}))-\inf\mathcal{E}+1},\qquad\mbox{for a.e. }t\in(t_{0},t_{0}+99).

In addition, we recall that all eigenvalues of
Q(t,\cdot)
are in

(-1/3,2/3)
for a.e.
t\in(t_{0},t_{0}+99)
, hence
\|Q(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
is
uniform-in-time bounded in
(t_{0},t_{0}+99)
. Thus (
1.14
) could be proved by iteration.

Part 2: strict physicality (
1.17
)
.
Now that
Q
is a solution of

\partial_{t}Q\in-\partial\mathcal{G}(Q(t,\cdot))-\psi^{\prime}(Q(t,\cdot))+\frac{\mathrm{tr}(\psi^{\prime}(Q(t,\cdot)))}{3}\mathbb{I}_{3}+2\alpha{Q}(t,\cdot)\qquad\forall\,t>t_{0},

with
Q\in L^{\infty}(t_{0},+\infty;H^{2})
, let us take the inner
product with
-\Delta Q(t,\cdot)
. Then it gives

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{d{t}}\|\nabla{Q}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle=\langle\partial\mathcal{G}(Q(t,\cdot)),\Delta{Q}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\langle\psi^{\prime}(Q(t,\cdot)),\Delta{Q}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\alpha\|\nabla{Q}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

(4.36)

Note that by (
1.16
), Lemma
4.3
,
and the Poincaré inequality, we have

\displaystyle\langle\partial\mathcal{G}(Q(t,\cdot)),\Delta{Q}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq-2(L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|)\|\Delta{Q}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\dfrac{2(L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|)}{C_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}^{2}}\|\nabla{Q}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}

(4.37)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{\prime}(Q(t,\cdot)),\Delta{Q}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi^{\prime}(Q(t,x))\Delta{Q}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(Q(t,x))|\nabla{Q}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\leq 0,

(4.38)

due to the convexity of
\psi
. Inserting (
4
) and
(
4
) into (
4
), we get

\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\nabla{Q}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq 4\Big(-\frac{L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|}{C_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}^{2}}+\alpha\Big)\|\nabla{Q}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2},\quad\forall\,t\geq t_{0}.

Since
Q(t_{0},\cdot)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, it follows from Gronwall’s inequality
that

\|\nabla{Q}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq\exp\bigg(4\Big[-\frac{L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|}{C_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}^{2}}+\alpha\Big](t-t_{0})\bigg)\|\nabla{Q}(t_{0},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\qquad\forall\,t\geq t_{0}.

(4.39)

Denote by
\bar{Q}(t):=|\mathbb{T}^{3}|^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}Q(t,\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}{x}
the
mean value of
Q(t,\cdot)
over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. Due to the convexity of

\psi
one can apply Jensen’s inequality to derive

\displaystyle\psi(\bar{Q}(t))

\displaystyle=\psi(\bar{Q}(t))|\mathbb{T}^{3}|\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi(Q(t,x))\,\mathrm{d}{x}=\mathcal{E}(Q(t))-\mathcal{G}(Q(t))+\alpha\|Q(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(Q(t_{0}))+\alpha\sup_{s\geq t_{0}}\|Q(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}|\mathbb{T}^{3}|,

since
\mathcal{G}\geq 0
by Lemma
3.1
. It is noted that the last
bound above is independent of
t\geq t_{0}
. Thus

\rho_{0}:=\inf_{t\geq t_{0}}\rho(\-Q(t,\cdot))>0,

(4.40)

where
\rho
is define in (
3.11
).

Finally, by (
4.31
) and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we
obtain

\displaystyle\|Q(t,\cdot)

\displaystyle-\bar{Q}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\|\nabla{Q}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{1/2}\|\Delta{Q}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\exp\bigg(\Big[-\frac{L_{1}-|L_{2}+L_{3}|}{C_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}^{2}}+\alpha\Big](t-t_{0})\bigg)\big\|\nabla{Q}(t_{0},\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{1/2}\cdot C(t_{0})^{1/2}

(4.41)

for some geometric constant
C^{\prime}
, and a.e.
t\geq t_{0}
. This together with
(
1.16
) implies

\|Q(t,\cdot)-\bar{Q}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}
decays uniformly to zero as

t\rightarrow\infty
. Since the convergence in the
L^{\infty}
norm
implies the uniform convergence of all eigenvalues, due to
(
4.40
) we conclude that there exists some
T_{0}>0
, such
that

\rho(Q(t,\cdot))\geq\dfrac{\rho_{0}}{2},\quad\forall\,t\geq T_{0}.

Therefore, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is
finished.
∎

5
Proof of Theorem
1.3
: Size of the contact set

In this section we shall estimate the Hausdorff dimension of the singular set
\Sigma_{t}
where the unique global solution
Q(t,x)
to (
1.6
) touches the physical boundary

\Sigma_{t}:=\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\mid Q(t,x)\in{\partial}\mathcal{Q}_{phy}\}.

(5.1)

Here
{\partial}\mathcal{Q}_{phy}
is the boundary of
\mathcal{Q}_{phy}
where the smallest eigenvalue of any element equals
-1/3
.

To begin with, we state
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
which provides a lower bound of the blowup rate of
{\partial}\psi(P)
as
P\in\mathcal{Q}_{phy}
approaches its physical boundary.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any
P\in\mathcal{Q}_{phy}
, as
\lambda_{1}(P)\rightarrow-1/3
it holds

\Big|\partial\psi(P)-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\partial\psi(P))\mathbb{I}_{3})\Big|\geq\frac{C_{1}}{\lambda_{1}(P)+\frac{1}{3}}

(5.2)

with the constant
C_{1}
given by

\displaystyle C_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{9\sqrt{2\pi}e}\cdot\inf_{\xi\geq 0}\frac{e^{-\xi}\mathrm{I}_{0}(\xi)}{e^{\frac{-\xi}{2}}\mathrm{I}_{0}(\frac{\xi}{2})}>0,

(5.3)

where
\mathrm{I}_{0}(\cdot)
is the zeroth order modified Bessel function of first kind.

Remark 5.1
.

As was pointed out in
[
17
, Appendix C]
that
d(P,\partial\mathcal{Q}_{phy})=\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}\big[\lambda_{1}(P)+\frac{1}{3}\big]
for any
P\in\mathcal{Q}_{phy}
. Together with Proposition

5.1
, it is immediate to see that there exists
a generic and suitably small constant
\delta_{0}>0
, such that

\Big|\partial\psi(P)-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\partial\psi(P))\mathbb{I}_{3})\Big|\geq\frac{\sqrt{6}C_{1}}{2d(P,\partial\mathcal{Q}_{phy})},\qquad\mbox{whenever }\;d(P,\partial\mathcal{Q}_{phy})<\delta_{0}.

(5.4)

The following technical lemma is necessary.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
s>0
there exists a sequence of coverings
V_{m}=\{B_{i,m}\}
of
\Sigma_{t}
, where
B_{i,m}=B(x_{i,m},r_{i,m})
, such that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i}r_{i,m}^{s}=\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Sigma_{t})\leq\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j}\big|5r_{j,m}^{\ast}\big|^{s}

Here
B_{i,m}=B(x_{i,m},r_{i,m})
such that
B_{i,m}\subset\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}|\,d(x,\Sigma_{t})\leq\delta\}
for some
\delta>0
, and
r_{j,m}^{\ast}
are the radii of the balls
B_{j,m}^{\ast}
, i.e., the sub-covering given by the Vitali covering lemma.

Proof.

First, by recalling the definition of Hausdorff content

\mathcal{H}_{\delta}^{s}(\Sigma_{t})\,:=\inf\Big\{\sum_{i}r_{i}^{s}:\,\Sigma_{t}\subset\bigcup_{i}B_{i},\,\sup_{i}r_{i}<\delta\Big\},

and the Hausdorff measure

\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Sigma_{t})=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{H}_{\delta}^{s}(\Sigma_{t}),

we know that for any
\delta>0
we have a sequence of coverings
V_{m}=\{B_{i,m,\delta}\}
such that

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i}r_{i,m,\delta}^{s}=\mathcal{H}_{\delta}^{s}(\Sigma_{t})

Therefore, using the standard diagonal argument we can choose a sufficiently large
m=m(\delta)
such that

0\leq\sum_{i}r_{i,m,\delta}^{s}-\mathcal{H}_{\delta}^{s}(\Sigma_{t}):=\varepsilon_{\delta}\ll 1,\qquad\mbox{where }\;\varepsilon_{\delta}\rightarrow 0\Leftrightarrow m\rightarrow+\infty,

which further gives

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i}r_{i,m}^{s}=\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Sigma_{t}),\qquad\mbox{where }\;r_{i,m}:=r_{i,m,\delta(m)}.

W.l.o.g., we may choose
m
to be bijective in
\delta
. Thus
V_{m}=\{B_{i,m}\}
is an admissible sequence to
achieve the Hausdorff measure.

Note that
\{5B_{j,m}^{\ast}\}
is another covering with balls for
\Sigma_{t}
, hence for each
m
, we have

\sum_{j}|5r_{j,m}^{\ast}|^{s}\geq\mathcal{H}_{\delta(m)}^{s}(\Sigma_{t})=\sum_{i}r_{i,m,\delta(m)}^{s}-\varepsilon_{\delta},

which implies

\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j}|5r_{j,m}^{\ast}|^{s}\geq\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{\delta(m)}^{s}(\Sigma_{t})=\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Sigma_{t}).

∎

Using Proposition
5.1
and lemma
5.1
, now we are ready to finish the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

First of all, for any
t_{0}>0
, by Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we know that the unique strong solution

Q(t,x)
to equation (
1.1
) satisfies

Q\in L^{\infty}(t_{0},+\infty;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})),\qquad\partial_{t}Q\in L^{\infty}(t_{0},T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})),

(5.5)

which together with equation (
1.1
) gives

\partial\psi(Q)-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\partial\psi(Q))\mathbb{I}_{3}\in L^{\infty}(t_{0},T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})).

Here and after, we are only concerned with
t\in(0,T_{0})
that satisfies

\Big\|\partial\psi(Q)-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\partial\psi(Q))\mathbb{I}_{3}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}<+\infty,

which obviously has a full measure in
(0,T_{0})
.

Case 1:
n=3

By Sobolev embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\hookrightarrow C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
and
Q\in L^{\infty}(t_{0},+\infty;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
, there exists a generic constant
C_{H}>0
that is independent of
t
, such that

|Q(t,x)-Q(t,y)|\leq C_{H}|x-y|^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{3}.

(5.6)

In particular, for any given
x
, let
x^{\perp}\in\Sigma_{t}
be a projection such that

|x-x^{\perp}|=d(x,\Sigma_{t}):=\mbox{dist}(x,\Sigma_{t}),

(5.7)

and henceforth we get

d(Q(t,x),\partial\mathcal{Q}_{phy})\leq|Q(t,x)-Q(t,x^{\perp})|\leq C_{H}|x-x^{\perp}|^{\frac{1}{2}}=C_{H}\sqrt{\mbox{dist}(x,\Sigma_{t})}.

This combined with Proposition
5.1
implies for any ball
B
and
\delta\ll 1
that

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\in B|\,d(x,\Sigma_{t})\leq\delta\}}\Big|\partial\psi(Q(t,x))-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\partial\psi(Q(t,x)))\mathbb{I}_{3}\Big|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}^{2}\int_{\{x\in B|\,d(x,\Sigma_{t})\leq\delta\}}d(Q(t,x),\partial\mathcal{Q}_{phy})^{-2}dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{C_{H}^{2}}\int_{\{x\in B|\,d(x,\Sigma_{t})\leq\delta\}}\frac{1}{\mbox{dist}(x,\Sigma_{t})}\,dx.

(5.8)

To proceed, by applying Lemma
5.1
with
s=2
, we obtain the existence of a sequence of covering
\{B_{i,m}\}

of
\Sigma_{t}
with balls
B_{i,m}=B(x_{i,m},r_{i,m})
such that
B_{i,m}\subset\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}|\,d(x,\Sigma_{T})\leq\delta\}
and

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i}r_{i,m}^{s}=\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Sigma_{t}).

We can assume that each such ball
B_{i,m}
intersects
\Sigma_{t}
, for otherwise we can just remove it from the covering.
By Vitali’s covering lemma, for each
m
we can choose a sub-collection of mutually disjoint balls
\{B_{j,m}^{\ast}\}
, with

B_{j,m}^{\ast}=B(x_{j,m}^{\ast},r_{j,m}^{\ast})
such that

\Sigma_{t}\subset\bigcup_{i}B_{i,m}\subset\bigcup_{j}5B_{j,m}^{\ast}.

On each such ball
B_{j,m}^{\ast}
, it follows from (
5
) that

\int_{B_{j,m}^{\ast}}\Big|\partial\psi(Q(t,x))-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\partial\psi(Q(t,x)))\mathbb{I}_{3}\Big|^{2}\,dx\geq\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{C_{H}^{2}}\int_{B_{j,m}^{\ast}}\frac{1}{\mbox{dist}(x,\Sigma_{t})}\,dx.

(5.9)

As each of such ball
B_{j,m}
intersects
\Sigma_{t}
, we can choose an arbitrary intersection point
y_{j,m}\in B_{j,m}^{\ast}\cap\Sigma_{t}
, and

\int_{B_{j,m}^{\ast}}\frac{1}{\mbox{dist}(x,\Sigma_{t})}\,dx\geq\int_{B_{j,m}^{\ast}}\frac{1}{|x-y_{j,m}|}\,dx.

(5.10)

We claim that the last integral in (
5.10
) is minimized when
y_{j,m}\in\partial B_{j,m}^{\ast}
. To prove this claim, w.l.o.g. we assume
B_{j,m}^{\ast}
is the unit ball
B
centered at the origin, and we denote
y_{j,m}=y=(y_{1},0,0)
,
0\leq y_{1}\leq 1
. Hence we consider

f(y):=\int_{B}\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,dx.

Then

\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{y_{1}}}=\int_{B}\frac{x_{1}-y_{1}}{|x-y|^{3}}\,dx,\qquad\mbox{where }\;x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}).

If
y_{1}=0
, by symmetry we see
\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{y_{1}}}=0
. If
0<y_{1}\leq 1
, we denote
A=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}|\,x_{1}\geq y_{1}\}
, and
A^{\prime}

the reflection of
A
across
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}|\,x_{1}=y_{1}\}
. Note that
A\cup A^{\prime}
is symmetric with respect to both
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}|\,x_{1}=y_{1}\}
and
the point
y=(y_{1},0,0)
. Thus we have

\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{y_{1}}}=\int_{B\setminus(A\cup A^{\prime})}\frac{x_{1}-y_{1}}{|x-y|^{3}}\,dx+\underbrace{\int_{A\cup A^{\prime}}\frac{x_{1}-y_{1}}{|x-y|^{3}}\,dx}_{=0}<0,

due to the fact that
B\setminus(A\cup A^{\prime})
is entirely contained in

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}|\,x_{1}\leq y_{1}\}
. Hence the claim is proved.

As a consequence,

\int_{B_{j,m}^{\ast}}\frac{1}{|x-y_{j,m}|}\,dx\geq\frac{4\pi}{3}|r_{j,m}^{\ast}|^{2}.

(5.11)

which together with (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) gives

\int_{B_{j,m}^{\ast}}\Big|\partial\psi(Q(t,x))-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\partial\psi(Q(t,x)))\mathbb{I}_{3}\Big|^{2}\,dx\geq\tilde{C}|r_{j,m}^{\ast}|^{2},\qquad\mbox{where }\,\tilde{C}:=\frac{4\pi}{3}\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{C_{H}^{2}}.

(5.12)

Since
\{B_{j,m}^{\ast}\}
are non-overlapping, after summing up the above inequality over
j
we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\partial\psi(Q(t,x))-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\partial\psi(Q(t,x)))\mathbb{I}_{3}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j}\int_{B_{j,m}^{\ast}}\Big|\partial\psi(Q(t,x))-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\partial\psi(Q(t,x)))\mathbb{I}_{3}\Big|^{2}\,dx\geq\tilde{C}\sum_{j}|r_{j,m}^{\ast}|^{2},

(5.13)

which together with Lemma
5.1
yields

\Big\|\partial\psi(Q(t,x))-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\partial\psi(Q(t,x)))\mathbb{I}_{3}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}\geq\tilde{C}\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j}|r_{j,m}^{\ast}|^{2}\geq\dfrac{\tilde{C}}{5^{2}}\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{t}).

(5.14)

Thus we conclude that
\mbox{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Sigma_{t})\leq 2
.

Case 2:

n=2

In the
2D
case the Sobolev embedding reads
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\hookrightarrow C^{\beta}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
for all
\beta\in(0,1)
. Correspondingly we have

\|Q(t,x)-Q(t,y)\|\leq C_{\beta}|x-y|^{\beta},\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{2},

(5.15)

and (

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\in B|\,d(x,\Sigma_{t})\leq\delta\}}\Big|\partial\psi(Q(t,x))-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\partial\psi(Q(t,x)))\mathbb{I}_{3}\Big|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{C_{\beta}^{2}}\int_{\{x\in B|\,d(x,\Sigma_{t})\leq\delta\}}\frac{1}{\mbox{dist}^{2\beta}(x,\Sigma_{t})}\,dx.

(5.16)

As a consequence, applying Lemma
5.1
with
s=2-2\beta
, and using Vitali’s covering argument exactly as in
Case 1
, one may replace
(
5.14
) by

\displaystyle\Big\|\partial\psi(Q(t,x))-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}(\partial\psi(Q(t,x)))\mathbb{I}_{3}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{C}^{\prime}\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j}|r_{j,m}^{\ast}|^{2-2\beta}\geq\dfrac{\tilde{C}^{\prime}}{5^{2-2\beta}}\mathcal{H}^{2-2\beta}(\Sigma_{t}),\quad\mbox{where }\;\tilde{C}^{\prime}=\frac{4\pi C_{1}^{2}}{C_{\beta}^{2}}.

(5.17)

In conclusion, we obtain
\mbox{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Sigma_{t})\leq 2-2\beta
for any
\beta\in(0,1)
. The proof is complete by the arbitrariness of

.
∎

Pfaffian structure of the eigenvector overlap for the symplectic Ginibre ensemble
Gernot Akemann, Sung-Soo Byun, Kohei Noda

Abstract
. We study the integrable structure and scaling limits of the conditioned eigenvector overlap of the symplectic Ginibre ensemble of Gaussian non-Hermitian random matrices with independent quaternion elements. The average of the overlap matrix elements constructed from left and right eigenvectors, conditioned to x x , are derived in terms of a Pfaffian determinant. Regarded as a two-dimensional Coulomb gas with the Neumann boundary condition along the real axis, it contains a kernel of skew-orthogonal polynomials with respect to the weight function ω ( over ) ​ ( z ) = | z − x ¯ | 2 ​ ( 1 + | z − x | 2 ) ​ e − 2 ​ | z | 2 \omega^{\rm(over)}(z)=|z-\overline{x}|^{2}(1+|z-x|^{2})e^{-2|z|^{2}} , so including a non-trivial insertion of a point charge. The mean off-diagonal overlap is related to the diagonal (self-)overlap by a transposition, in analogy to the complex Ginibre ensemble. For x x conditioned to the real line, extending previous results at x = 0 x=0 , we determine the skew-orthogonal polynomials and their skew-kernel with respect to ω ( over ) ​ ( z ) \omega^{\rm(over)}(z) . This is done in two steps and involves a Christoffel perturbation of the weight ω ( over ) ​ ( z ) = | z − x ¯ | 2 ​ ω ( pre ) ​ ( z ) \omega^{\rm(over)}(z)=|z-\overline{x}|^{2}\omega^{\rm(pre)}(z) , by computing first the corresponding quantities for the unperturbed weight ω ( pre ) ​ ( z ) \omega^{\rm(pre)}(z) . Its kernel is shown to satisfy a differential equation at finite matrix size N N . This allows us to take different large- N N limits, where we distinguish bulk and edge regime along the real axis. The limiting mean diagonal overlaps and corresponding eigenvalue correlation functions of the point processes with respect to ω ( over ) ​ ( z ) \omega^{\rm(over)}(z) are determined. We also examine the effect on the planar orthogonal polynomials when changing the variance in ω ( pre ) ​ ( z ) \omega^{\rm(pre)}(z) , as this appears in the eigenvector statistics of the complex Ginibre ensemble.

1.1.
Preliminaries: eigenvalue and eigenvector correlations in the GinSE

In this section, we will summarise known results for both eigenvalue and eigenvector statistics of the GinSE, insofar as they will be needed to formulate our main results in the next section.
We begin with the definition of the ensemble and recall what is known about it.
By using the
2\times 2
matrix complex-valued representation of the quaternions, the GinSE is defined by

(1.1)

G_{2N}:=\begin{pmatrix}A_{N}&B_{N}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
-\overline{B}_{N}&\overline{A}_{N}\end{pmatrix},

where
A_{N}
and
B_{N}
are independent
N\times N
matrices whose elements are i.i.d. complex normal Gaussian random variables with mean 0 and variance
1/2
for their real parts and for their imaginary parts. The distribution of matrix elements can thus be written as

(1.2)

P(G_{2N})=C_{2N}\exp\left[-\mbox{Tr}(G_{2N}G^{*}_{2N})\right],

where
C_{2N}
is an appropriate normalisation constant, and
∗
denotes the adjoint. It is invariant under unitary transformations, and thus the matrix
G_{2N}
can be brought into upper triangular block diagonal form by a unitary symplectic matrix
U_{2N}\in\mbox{USp}(2N)/\mbox{U}(1)^{N}
,

(1.3)

G_{2N}=U_{2N}(D_{2N}+T_{2N})U^{-1}_{2N}.

Here,
D_{2N}
denotes the
2\times 2
block diagonal matrix

(1.4)

D_{2N}:=\mathrm{diag}\begin{pmatrix}z_{j}&0\\
0&\overline{z}_{j}\end{pmatrix}_{j=1}^{N}=\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}&0\\
0&\overline{z}_{1}\end{pmatrix}\oplus\cdots\oplus\begin{pmatrix}z_{N}&0\\
0&\overline{z}_{N}\end{pmatrix}.

It contains the
2N
eigenvalues
z_{j}
that come in complex conjugated pairs
1
1

1

Due to the non-commutativity of the quaternions, the eigenvalues
z_{j}
are infinitely degenerate, after a transformation
qz_{j}q^{-1}
with
q\neq 0
. In the following we thus consider equivalence classes of complex eigenvalues, see e.g.
[
7
]
for details.
.
The matrix
T_{2N}
is strictly upper block diagonal, with
N(N-1)/2
non-zero quaternion elements in
2\times 2
complex matrix representation.
Because of this, we obtain independent Gaussian distributions for the elements of
D_{2N}
and
T_{2N}
,

(1.5)

P(G_{2N})=C_{2N}\exp\left[-\mbox{Tr}(D_{2N}D_{2N}^{*}+T_{2N}T_{2N}^{*})\right].

More general distributions of matrix elements have been considered

]
, e.g. the induced or elliptic GinSE, or normal random matrices (with

It turns out that for the eigenvector correlations we will have to consider more general weights than the Gaussian weight (

The statistical behaviour of the complex eigenvalues in planar symplectic ensembles is encoded in the
k
-point correlation functions defined as

(1.8)

\mathbf{R}_{N,k}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}):=\frac{N!}{(N-k!)}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N-k}}\mathbf{P}_{N}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})\prod_{j=k+1}^{N}dA(z_{j}).

A notable feature is that they form a Pfaffian point process, namely, for any
k=1,2,\dots,N
, we have

(1.9)

\mathbf{R}_{N,k}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})={\textup{Pf}}\bigg[\begin{pmatrix}{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(z_{j},z_{\ell})&{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(z_{j},\overline{z}_{\ell})\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(\overline{z}_{j},z_{\ell})&{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(\overline{z}_{j},\overline{z}_{\ell})\end{pmatrix}\bigg]_{j,\ell=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\overline{z}_{j}-z_{j})\,\omega(z_{j}).

Here, the
2\times 2

{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}
(also sometimes called pre-kernel) as a building block. It can be expressed in terms of SOPs
q_{k}

(1.10)

{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(z,w)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{q_{2k+1}(z)q_{2k}(w)-q_{2k}(z)q_{2k+1}(w)}{r_{k}}.

Here, the skew-inner product with respect to some measure
d\mu(z)=w(z)\,dA(z)
on
\mathbb{C}
is defined as

(1.11)

\langle f,g\rangle_{s}:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Big(f(z)\overline{g(z)}-g(z)\overline{f(z)}\Big)(z-\overline{z})\,d\mu(z).

A family of polynomials

is called planar SOP associated with weight

(1.12)

\langle q_{2k},q_{2\ell}\rangle_{s}=\langle q_{2k+1},q_{2\ell+1}\rangle_{s}=0,\qquad\langle q_{2k},q_{2\ell+1}\rangle_{s}=-\langle q_{2\ell+1},q_{2k}\rangle_{s}=r_{k}\,\delta_{k,\ell},

where
r_{k}
is their skew-norm which is positive.
It also follows from de Bruijn’s integration formula that the partition function
Z_{N}
can be expressed as

(1.13)

Z_{N}=N!\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}r_{k},

see e.g.
[

In the case of the GinSE, the weight function is given by the Gaussian distribution

(1.14)

\omega^{\rm(g)}(z):=e^{-2|z|^{2}},

with scalar product (
1.11

In the sequel, we add the superscript

In this case, we have

(1.15)

q_{2k+1}^{(\mathrm{g})}(z)=z^{2k+1},\qquad q_{2k}^{(\mathrm{g})}(z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{k!}{\ell!}z^{2\ell},\qquad r_{k}^{(\mathrm{g})}=\frac{(2k+1)!}{2^{2k+1}}.

As a consequence, it follows that

(1.16)

Z_{N}^{({\rm g})}=N!\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(2k+1)!}{2^{2k+1}}.

Furthermore, we have

(1.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}^{(\mathrm{g})}(z,w)=\sqrt{2}\bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\Bigl(\frac{(\sqrt{2}z)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!!}\frac{(\sqrt{2}w)^{2\ell}}{(2\ell)!!}-\frac{(\sqrt{2}w)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!!}\frac{(\sqrt{2}z)^{2\ell}}{(2\ell)!!}\Bigr)\bigg].\end{split}

4.2.
Recurrence relation for planar SOPs and derivation of
q_{k}^{\rm(pre)}(z)

In this section we first derive a general theorem that gives the coefficients of general SOPs when the underlying moment matrix is
tri-diagonal. It is based on showing certain recurrence relations amongst its coefficients. Because the pre-overlap weight function (
1.27
) satisfies this condition we can then apply this theorem to determine the
q_{k}^{\rm(pre)}(z)
including their skew-norms.

We first define the moment matrix of a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{C}
and its real part as

(4.1)

m_{j,k}:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}z^{j}\overline{z}^{k}d\mu(z),\qquad\widehat{m}_{j,k}:=\operatorname{Re}(m_{j,k}).

We denote the skew-moment matrix with respect to the corresponding skew-product (
1.11
) as

(4.2)

g_{j,k}:=\langle z^{j},z^{k}\rangle_{s}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\left(z^{j}\overline{z}^{k}-z^{k}\overline{z}^{j}\right)(z-\overline{z})d\mu(z)=2(\widehat{m}_{j+1,k}-\widehat{m}_{j,k+1}),\qquad G_{k}:=(g_{i,j})_{i,j=0}^{2k-1},

and we define

(4.3)

\Delta_{-1}:=1,\quad\Delta_{k}:={\textup{Pf}}(G_{k+1}),\quad\mathcal{Z}_{k+1}:=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta_{k}}{\Delta_{k-1}}.

Theorem 4.1

(
Recurrence relation of planar SOPs
)
.

We assume that the moment matrix (
4.1
) is tri-diagonal. Then, the monic SOPs associated with the corresponding skew-product (
1.11
) are obtained as

(4.4)

q_{2k}(z):=\sum_{j=0}^{2k}\alpha_{2k,j}z^{j},\quad q_{2k+1}(z):=\sum_{j=0}^{2k+1}\beta_{2k+1,j}z^{j},

with
\alpha_{2k,2k}=1
,
\beta_{2k+1,2k+1}=1
and
\beta_{2k+1,2k}=\beta_{2k+1,2k-1}=0
.
The coefficients are determined by the following recurrence relationships: for
j=1,2,...,k
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{j}\alpha_{2k,2j-1}=-\widehat{m}_{2j-1,2j}\alpha_{2k,2j},\qquad\qquad\mathcal{Z}_{j}\alpha_{2k,2j-2}=\widehat{m}_{2j,2j+1}\alpha_{2k,2j+1}+\widehat{m}_{2j,2j}\alpha_{2k,2j},

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{j}\beta_{2k+1,2j-1}=-\widehat{m}_{2j-1,2j}\beta_{2k+1,2j},\qquad\mathcal{Z}_{j}\beta_{2k+1,2j-2}=\widehat{m}_{2j,2j+1}\beta_{2k+1,2j+1}+\widehat{m}_{2j,2j}\beta_{2k+1,2j},

where
\mathcal{Z}_{j}
is given by (
4.3
) and we use the convention
\alpha_{2k,2k+1}=0
.

We mention that the initial condition
\beta_{2k+1,2k}=0
in Theorem
4.1
uniquely determines the odd SOPs, see
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We shall use an induction argument.
Suppose that the coefficients of
q_{2k}
satisfy (
4.5
) and those of
q_{2k+1}
satisfy (
4.6
).

Note that for
1\leq j\leq k-1
, we have

\displaystyle\langle q_{2j-2},q_{2k+2}\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle=\langle z^{2j-2}+\cdots,\alpha_{2k+2,2k-1}z^{2k-1}+\cdots\rangle_{s},

\displaystyle\langle q_{2j-1},q_{2k+2}\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle=\langle z^{2j-1}+\cdots,\alpha_{2k+2,2k-1}z^{2k-1}+\cdots\rangle_{s}.

By the induction assumption and replacing
2k
with
2k+2
,
it follows that for
1\leq j\leq k-1
,

\mathcal{Z}_{j}\alpha_{2k+2,2j-1}=-\widehat{m}_{2j-1,2j}\alpha_{2k+2,2j},\qquad\mathcal{Z}_{j}\alpha_{2k+2,2j-2}=\widehat{m}_{2j,2j+1}\alpha_{2k+2,2j+1}+\widehat{m}_{2j,2j}\alpha_{2k+2,2j}.

Therefore, it suffices to consider
q_{2j-2},q_{2j-1}
for
j=k,k+1
.
First, note that

\displaystyle\langle q_{2k-2},q_{2k+2}\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle=2\alpha_{2k+2,2k}\widehat{m}_{2k-1,2k}+2\alpha_{2k+2,2k-1}\widehat{m}_{2k-1,2k-1}

\displaystyle\quad-2\alpha_{2k+2,2k-3}\widehat{m}_{2k-2,2k-2}-2\alpha_{2k+2,2k-4}\widehat{m}_{2k-2,2k-3}+\Bigl\langle\sum_{j=0}^{2k-3}\alpha_{2k-2,j}z^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{2k-1}\alpha_{2k+2,j}z^{j}\Bigr\rangle_{s}.

To show (
4.5
), we need to check

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{k}\alpha_{2k+2,2k-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl\langle\sum_{j=0}^{2k-3}\alpha_{2k-2,j}z^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{2k-1}\alpha_{2k+2,j}z^{j}\bigr\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{2k+2,2k-1}\widehat{m}_{2k-1,2k-1}-\alpha_{2k+2,2k-3}\widehat{m}_{2k-2,2k-2}-\alpha_{2k+2,2k-4}\widehat{m}_{2k-2,2k-3}.

On the other hand, since

\displaystyle\quad\alpha_{2k+2,2k-1}\widehat{m}_{2k-1,2k-1}-\alpha_{2k+2,2k-3}\widehat{m}_{2k-2,2k-2}-\alpha_{2k+2,2k-4}\widehat{m}_{2k-2,2k-3}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{2k+2,2k-1}\Bigl(\widehat{m}_{2k-1,2k-1}-\frac{\widehat{m}_{2k-3,2k-2}\widehat{m}_{2k-2,2k-1}}{\mathcal{Z}_{k-1}}\Bigr)=\mathcal{Z}_{k}\alpha_{2k+2,2k-1},

it is enough to show that

\Bigl\langle\sum_{j=0}^{2k-3}\alpha_{2k-2,j}z^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{2k-1}\alpha_{2k+2,j}z^{j}\Bigr\rangle_{s}=0.

For
j=3,4,\dots,2k-2
, we write

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{k,j}&=\alpha_{2k+2,2k+2-j}\widehat{m}_{2k+1-j,2k+2-j}+\alpha_{2k+2,2k+1-j}\widehat{m}_{2k+1-j,2k+1-j}\\
&\quad-\alpha_{2k+2,2k-j-1}\widehat{m}_{2k-j,2k-j}-\alpha_{2k+2,2k-j-2}\widehat{m}_{2k-j,2k-j-1}.\end{split}

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bigl\langle\sum_{j=0}^{2k-3}\alpha_{2k-2,j}z^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{2k-1}\alpha_{2k+2,j}z^{j}\bigr\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=3}^{2k-2}\alpha_{2k-2,2k-j}u_{k,j}+\alpha_{2k-2,0}(\alpha_{2k+2,2}\widehat{m}_{1,2}+\alpha_{2k+2,1}\widehat{m}_{1,1})

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{2k-2,1}(\alpha_{2k+2,3}\widehat{m}_{2,3}+\alpha_{2k+2,2}\widehat{m}_{2,2}-\alpha_{2k+2,0}\widehat{m}_{1,1}).

By the assumption of the induction,

\displaystyle u_{k,2j}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{2k+2,2k-2j+2}\widehat{m}_{2k-2j+1,2k-2j+2}+\alpha_{2k+2,2k-2j+1}\widehat{m}_{2k-2j+1,2k-2j+1}

\displaystyle\quad+\widehat{m}_{2k-2j,2k-2j}\frac{\widehat{m}_{2k-2j-1,2k-2j}\alpha_{2k+2,2k-2j}}{\mathcal{Z}_{k-j}}

\displaystyle\quad-\widehat{m}_{2k-2j-1,2k-2j}\frac{\widehat{m}_{2k-2j+1,2k-2j}\alpha_{2k+2,2k-2j+1}+\widehat{m}_{2k-2j,2k-2j}\alpha_{2k+2,2k-2j}}{\mathcal{Z}_{k-j}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{m}_{2k-2j+1,2k-2j+2}\alpha_{2k+2,2k+2-2j}+\mathcal{Z}_{k-j+1}\alpha_{2k+2,2k-2j+1}=0.

Similarly, one can show that
u_{k,2j+1}=0
.
Hence, we have shown that
u_{k,j}=0
for all
3\leq j\leq 2k-2
, which leads to the desired conclusion.

Next, to show
\langle q_{2k-1},q_{2k+2}\rangle_{s}=0
, first observe that

\langle q_{2k-1},q_{2k+2}\rangle_{s}=2\alpha_{2k+2,2k+1}\widehat{m}_{2k,2k+1}+2\alpha_{2k+2,2k}\widehat{m}_{2k,2k}-2\mathcal{Z}_{k}\alpha_{2k+2,2k-2}+\Bigl\langle\sum_{j=0}^{2k-4}\beta_{2k-1,j}z^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{2k-2}\alpha_{2k+2,j}z^{j}\Bigr\rangle_{s}.

Therefore, it suffices to show that

\Bigl\langle\sum_{j=0}^{2k-4}\beta_{2k-1,j}z^{j},\sum_{j=0}^{2k-2}\alpha_{2k+2,j}z^{j}\Bigr\rangle_{s}=0.

Similar to the even coefficient case, this follows from
u_{k,j}=0
for
4\leq j\leq 2k-2
, which has already been made. Hence, we have verified the induction argument for
q_{2j-2},q_{2j-1}
with

The remaining task is to verify the induction argument for
q_{2j-2},q_{2j-1}
with
j=k+1

is

. Hence, there is no additional task to show the induction argument for (

). For (

This completes the proof.
∎

We now turn our attention to Theorem

For

Appendix A
Planar orthogonal polynomials and a pre-overlap weight function of different variance

In this appendix we explore the effect of changing the variance upon the construction of planar orthogonal polynomials. Due the nature of the point insertion this does not lead to a simple rescaling of the argument of the polynomials, but rather affects their coefficients in a non-trivial way.

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{C}
with an infinite number of points in its support on a domain
D
.
We define the
inner product with respect to
\mu
on
D
by

(A.1)

\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{D}f(z)\overline{g(z)}\,d\mu(z).

A family of polynomials
(p_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called planar orthogonal polynomials (OP) associated with
d\mu(z)=w(z)dA(z)
if

(A.2)

\langle p_{k},p_{\ell}\rangle=h_{k}\,\delta_{k,\ell},

where
h_{k}
is the squared norm and
\delta
is the Kronecker delta.

Below we construct the planar OPs
p_{k}^{\rm(pre)}(z)
associated with the parameter dependent weight function on
D=\mathbb{C}
,

(A.3)

\omega_{\sigma}^{(\rm pre)}(z):=\left(1+|z-a|^{2}\right)\exp\left(-\sigma|z|^{2}\right),\qquad z,a\in\mathbb{C},\quad\sigma>0,

generalising the weight (
1.27
) with
\sigma=2
. Notice that when changing variables
z\to z^{\prime}=\sqrt{\sigma}\,z
and denoting
a^{\prime}=\sqrt{\sigma}\,a
, the weight changes to

(A.4)

\omega_{\sigma}^{(\rm pre)}(z^{\prime})\,dA(z^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\left(\sigma+|z^{\prime}-a^{\prime}|^{2}\right)\exp\left(-|z^{\prime}|^{2}\right)\,dA(z^{\prime}),

changing the prefactor and thus the content of the point charge insertion.

Proposition A.1

(
Planar orthogonal polynomial associated with weight of changing variance
)
.

For any given integer
p\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
\sigma>0
, let

(A.5)

F_{p,\sigma}(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\frac{\Gamma(p-j+\sigma+1)}{\Gamma(j+1)\Gamma(p-j+1)}x^{j}.

The monic planar OPs
p_{k,\sigma}^{\rm(pre)}
associated with (
A.3
) are then obtained as

(A.6)

p_{k,\sigma}^{\rm(pre)}(z):=\sum_{j=0}^{k}{a}^{k-j}\frac{F_{j,\sigma}(\sigma|a|^{2})}{F_{k,\sigma}(\sigma|a|^{2})}z^{j},

with squared norms

(A.7)

h_{k,\sigma}^{\rm(pre)}=\frac{(k+1)!}{\sigma^{k+2}}\frac{F_{k+1,\sigma}(\sigma|a|^{2})}{F_{k,\sigma}(\sigma|a|^{2})}.

Furthermore, they satisfy the following non-standard three term recurrence relation
for
k\geq 1
:

(A.8)

z\,p_{k,\sigma}^{\rm(pre)}(z)=p_{k+1,\sigma}^{\rm(pre)}(z)+b_{k}\,p_{k,\sigma}^{\rm(pre)}(z)+z\,c_{k}\,p_{k-1,\sigma}^{\rm(pre)}(z),

where

(A.9)

b_{k}=-a\,\frac{F_{k,\sigma}(\sigma|a|^{2})}{F_{k+1,\sigma}(\sigma|a|^{2})},\qquad c_{k}=a\,\frac{F_{k-1,\sigma}(\sigma|a|^{2})}{F_{k,\sigma}(\sigma|a|^{2})}.

We also mention that the corresponding planar OPs
p_{k}^{\rm(over)}
with respect to the perturbed weight (now depending on
\sigma
) could also be constructed, using Proposition
A.1
and
[
8
, Eq.(3.6)]
.

Proof.

We denote the moment matrix associated with (
A.3
) by

(A.10)

\mathfrak{m}_{i,j}:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}z^{i}\overline{z}^{j}\omega_{\sigma}^{(\rm pre)}(z)\,dA(z).

It is well known that the kernel of OP can be obtained form the inverse moment matrix. In turn, if we derive an LDU decomposition of
\mathfrak{m}_{i,j}
, where
L
and
U
are lower and upper triangular matrices, respectively, the latter two can be easily inverted, and the planar OPs follow in turn as well. This strategy was employed in
[
5
]
which we shall follow closely.

In a first step we split off a common factor to define a reduced moment matrix
\mu_{i,j}

(A.11)

\mathfrak{m}_{i,j}=i!\frac{1}{\sigma^{i+2}}\mu_{i,j},\qquad\mu_{i,j}:=\Bigl(a\overline{a}\sigma+i+1+\sigma\Bigr)\delta_{i,j}-\overline{a}(i+1)\delta_{i+1,j}-a\sigma\delta_{i,j+1}.

If we decompose the matrix
\mu=(\mu_{i,j})
as a LDU decomposition
\mu=LDU
, where
D=(D_{p,q})
,
L=(L_{p,q})
, and
U=(U_{p,q})
with

(A.12)

D_{p,q}:=d_{p}\delta_{p,q},\quad L_{p,q}:=\delta_{p,q}+l_{p}\delta_{p,q+1},\quad U_{p,q}=\delta_{p,q}+u_{q}\delta_{q,p+1}\ \mbox{for}\ p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

Then, multiplying out
LDU
and comparing with (
A.11
), we can read off the following

(A.13)

d_{p}u_{p+1}=-\overline{a}(p+1),\qquad d_{p}l_{p+1}=-a\sigma,\qquad d_{p-1}l_{p}u_{p}\mathbf{1}_{p\geq 1}+d_{p}=a\overline{a}\sigma+p+1+\sigma.

It follows from the above relationships that

\frac{xp}{d_{p-1}}+d_{p}=x+p+1+\sigma,\qquad d_{0}=x+1+\sigma,

Pressure-robust enriched Galerkin methods for the Stokes equations Submitted to Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics in 2023.
Xiaozhe Hu, Seulip Lee, Lin Mu, Son-Young Yi

Abstract
In this paper, we present a pressure-robust enriched Galerkin (EG) scheme for solving the Stokes equations, which is an enhanced version of the EG scheme for the Stokes problem proposed in [S.-Y. Yi, X. Hu, S. Lee, J. H. Adler, An enriched Galerkin method for the Stokes equations, Computers and Mathematics with Applications 120 (2022) 115–131]. The pressure-robustness is achieved by employing a velocity reconstruction operator on the load vector on the right-hand side of the discrete system. An a priori error analysis proves that the velocity error is independent of the pressure and viscosity. We also propose and analyze a perturbed version of our pressure-robust EG method that allows for the elimination of the degrees of freedom corresponding to the discontinuous component of the velocity vector via static condensation. The resulting method can be viewed as a stabilized H 1 H^{1} -conforming ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}_{1} - ℙ 0 \mathbb{P}_{0} method. Further, we consider efficient block preconditioners whose performances are independent of the viscosity. The theoretical results are confirmed through various numerical experiments in two and three dimensions. Keywords: enriched Galerkin; finite element methods; Stokes equations; pressure-robust; static condensation; stabilization.

Introduction

We consider the Stokes equations for modeling incompressible viscous flow in an open and bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
,
with simply connected Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
: Find the fluid velocity
{\bf u}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the pressure
p:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta{\bf u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle={\bf f}

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf u}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle{\bf u}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

(1.1c)

where
\nu>0
is the fluid viscosity, and
{\bf f}
is a given body force.

Various finite element methods (FEMs) have been applied to solve the Stokes problem based on the velocity-pressure formulation (
1.1
), including conforming and non-conforming mixed FEMs
[
39
,
5
,
13
,
38
]
, discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods

[
20
,
17
]
, weak Galerkin methods
[
30
,
29
]
, and enriched Galerkin methods
[
8
,
42
]
.
It is well-known that the finite-dimensional solution spaces must satisfy the inf-sup stability condition
[
22
,
4
,
6
]
for the well-posedness of the discrete problem regardless of what numerical method is used. Therefore, there has been extensive research to construct inf-sup stable pairs for the Stokes equations in the last several decades.
Some classical
H^{1}
-conforming stable pairs include Taylor-Hood, Bernardi-Raugel, and MINI elements
[
16
]
.

Though the inf-sup condition is crucial for the well-posedness of the discrete problem, it does not always guarantee an accurate solution. Indeed, many inf-sup stable pairs are unable to produce an accurate velocity solution when the viscosity is very small. More precisely, such pairs produce the velocity solution whose error bound depends on the pressure error and is inversely proportional to the viscosity,
\nu
.
Hence, it is important to develop numerical schemes whose velocity error bounds are independent of pressure and viscosity, which we call
pressure-robust
schemes.

There have been three major directions to develop pressure-robust schemes.
The first direction is based on employing a divergence-free velocity space since, then, one can separate the velocity error from the pressure error.
Recall, however, that the velocity space of classical low-order inf-sup stable elements do not satisfy the divergence-free (incompressibility) condition strongly.
One way to develop a divergence-free velocity space is to take the curl of an
H^{2}
-conforming finite element space
[
3
,
14
,
18
]
, from which the pressure space is constructed by taking the divergence operator. Though this approach provides the desired pressure-robustness, it requires many degrees of freedom (DoFs).
Another way, which has received much attention lately, is to employ an
H(\text{div})
-conforming velocity space
[
12
,
41
,
11
,
18
,
19
,
10
]
.
However, to take account of lack of regularity, the tangential continuity of the velocity vector across the inter-elements has to be imposed either strongly
[
18
,
19
]
or weakly
[
12
,
41
,
11
,
10
]
.
The second direction is based on the grad-div stabilization
[
33
,
21
]
, which is derived by adding a modified incompressibility condition to the continuous momentum equation.
This stabilization technique reduces the pressure effect in the velocity error estimate but not completely eliminates it.
The third direction, which we consider in the present work, is based on employing a velocity reconstruction operator
[
24
]
.
In this approach, one reconstructs (
H(\text{div})
-nonconforming) discrete velocity test functions by mapping them into an
H(\text{div})
-conforming space. These reconstructed velocity test functions are used in the load vector (corresponding to the body force
{\bf f}
) on the right-hand side of the discrete system while the original test functions are used in the stiffness matrix.
This idea has been successfully explored in various numerical methods
[
25
,
26
,
15
,
29
,
31
,
32
,
40
,
23
]
.

Our main goal is to develop and analyze a pressure-robust finite element method requiring minimal number of degrees of freedom.
To achieve this goal, we consider as the base method the enriched Galerkin (EG) method proposed for the Stokes equations with mixed boundary conditions
[
42
]
. This EG method employs the piecewise constant space for the pressure. As for the velocity space, it employs the linear Lagrange space,
enriched by some discontinuous, piecewise linear, and mean-zero vector functions. This enrichment space requires only one DoF per element. Indeed, any function
{\bf v}
in the velocity space has a unique decomposition of the form
{\bf v}={\bf v}^{C}+{\bf v}^{D}
, where
{\bf v}^{C}
belongs to the linear Lagrange space and
{\bf v}^{D}
belongs to the discontinuous linear enrichment space. To take account of the non-conformity of the velocity space, an interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (IPDG) bilinear form is adopted.
This method is one of the cheapest inf-sup stable methods with optimal convergence rates for the Stokes problem. However, like many other inf-sup stable methods, this EG method produces pressure- and viscosity-dependent velocity error. In order to derive a pressure-robust EG method, we propose to take the velocity reconstruction approach mentioned above. Specifically, the velocity test functions are mapped to the first-order Brezzi-Douglas-Marini space, whose resulting action is equivalent to preserving the continuous component
{\bf v}^{C}

and mapping only the discontinuous component
{\bf v}^{D}
to the lowest-order Raviart-Thomas space.
This operator is applied to the velocity test functions on the right-hand side of the momentum equation, therefore the resulting stiffness matrix is the same as the one generated by the original EG method in
[
42
]
. By this simple modification in the method, we can achieve pressure-robustness without compromising the optimal convergence rates, which has been proved mathematically and demonstrated numerically.

Though the proposed pressure-robust EG method is already a very cheap and efficient method, we seek to reduce the computational costs even more by exploring a couple of strategies.
First, we developed a perturbed pressure-robust EG method, where a sub-block in the stiffness matrix corresponding to the discontinuous component,
{\bf u}_{h}^{D}
, of the velocity solution
{\bf u}_{h}={\bf u}_{h}^{C}+{\bf u}_{h}^{D}
is replaced by a diagonal matrix. This modification allows for the elimination of the DoFs corresponding to
{\bf u}_{h}^{D}
from the discrete system via static condensation. The method corresponding to the condensed linear system can be viewed as a new stabilized
H^{1}
-conforming
\mathbb{P}_{1}
-
\mathbb{P}_{0}
method (see
[
37
]
for a similar approach).
For an alternative strategy, we designed fast linear solvers for the pressure-robust EG method and its two variants. Since the stiffness matrix remains unchanged, the fast linear solvers designed for the original EG method in
[
42
]
can be applied to the new pressure-robust EG method and its two variants with only slight modifications. The performance of these fast solvers was investigated through some numerical examples.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section
2
introduces some preliminaries and notations, which are useful in the later sections.
In Section
3

[
42
]
and introduce our pressure-robust EG method.
Then, well-posedness and error estimates are proved in Section

In Section
5

In Section
6

We conclude our paper by summarizing the contributions of our new EG methods and discuss future research in Section
7

2
Notation and Preliminaries

We first present some notations and preliminaries that will be useful for the rest of this paper.
For any open domain
\mathcal{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
d=2,3
, we use the standard notation
H^{s}(\mathcal{D})
for Sobolev spaces, where
s
is a positive real number. The Sobolev norm and semi-norm associated with
H^{s}(\mathcal{D})
are denoted by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{s,\mathcal{D}}
and
|\cdot|_{s,\mathcal{D}}
, respectively. In particular, when
s=0
,
H^{0}(\mathcal{D})
coincides with
L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
and its associated norm will be denoted by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{0,\mathcal{D}}
.
Also,
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{D}}
denotes the
L^{2}
-inner product on
\mathcal{D}
. If
\mathcal{D}=\Omega
, we drop
\mathcal{D}
in the subscript. We extend these definitions and notations to vector- and tensor-valued Sobolev spaces in a straightforward manner.
Additionally,
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
are defined by

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)=\{z\in H^{1}(\Omega)\mid z=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\},\quad L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)=\{z\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid(z,1)=0\}.

We finally introduce a Hilbert space

H(\text{div},\Omega):=\{{\bf v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}:\text{div}\;{\bf v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}

with an associated norm

\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{H(\text{div},\Omega)}^{2}:=\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{0}^{2}+\lVert\text{div}\;{\bf v}\rVert_{0}^{2}.

To define our EG methods, we consider a shape-regular mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
on
\Omega
, consisting of triangles in two dimensions and tetrahedra in three dimensions. Let
h_{K}
be the diameter of
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Then,
the characteristic mesh size
h
is defined by

h={\max}_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{h_{K}}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}
the collection of all the interior edges/faces in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and by
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}
the boundary edges/faces . Then,
\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}
is the collection of all edges/faces in the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
On each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, let
{\bf n}_{K}
denote the unit outward normal vector on
\partial K
. Each interior edge/face
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}
is shared by two neighboring elements, that is,
e=\partial K^{+}\cap\partial K^{-}
for some
K^{+},K^{-}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We associate one unit normal vector
{\bf n}_{e}
with
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}
, which is assumed to be oriented from
K^{+}
to
K^{-}
. If
e
is a boundary edge/face, then
{\bf n}_{e}
is the unit outward normal vector to
\partial\Omega
.

We now define broken Sobolev spaces on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\mathcal{E}_{h}
.
The broken Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is defined by

H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h})=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid v|_{K}\in H^{s}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

which is equipped with a broken Sobolev norm

\lVert v\rVert_{s,\mathcal{T}_{h}}=\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\lVert v\rVert_{s,K}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Similarly, we define the space
L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{h})
on
\mathcal{E}_{h}
and its associated norm

\lVert v\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}=\left(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\lVert v\rVert_{0,e}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Also, the
L^{2}
-inner product on
\mathcal{E}_{h}
is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}
.

Finally, we define the average and jump operators, which will be needed to define the EG methods. For any
v\in H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
with
s>1/2
, let
v^{\pm}
be the trace of
v|_{K^{\pm}}
on
e=\partial K^{+}\cap\partial K^{-}
. Then, the average and jump of
v
along
e
, denoted by
\{\cdot\}
and
[\cdot]
, are defined by

\{v\}=\frac{1}{2}(v^{+}+v^{-}),\quad[v]=v^{+}-v^{-}\quad\text{on }e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}.

On a boundary edge/face,

\{v\}=v,\quad[v]=v\quad\text{on }e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{b}.

These definitions can be naturally extended to vector- or tensor-valued functions.

3
Pressure-Robust Enriched Galerkin Method

In this section, we introduce our new pressure-robust EG method for the Stokes equations.
First, we can derive the following weak formulation of the governing equations (
1.1
) in a standard way:
Find
({\bf u},p)\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{d}\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\nu(\nabla{\bf u},\nabla{\bf v})-(\nabla\cdot{\bf v},p)

\displaystyle=({\bf f},{\bf v})

\displaystyle\quad\forall{\bf v}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{d},

(3.1a)

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot{\bf u},q)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\forall q\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

(3.1b)

In this paper, we consider the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition for simplicity. However, in the case of a non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition, this weak problem can be easily modified by changing the solution space for the velocity
{\bf u}
to reflect the non-homogeneous boundary condition.

3.1
Standard EG method for the Stokes problem
[
42
]

Our pressure-robust EG method is designed based upon the EG method originally proposed in
[
42
]
, which we will refer to as the
standard EG method
for the Stokes equations.
Therefore, in this section, we first present the standard EG method to lay a foundation for the presentation of the new pressure-robust method.

For any integer
k\geq 0
,
\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{D})
denotes the set of polynomials defined on
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose total degree is less than or equal to
k
. Let

{\bf C}_{h}=\{{\bf v}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{d}\mid{\bf v}|_{K}\in[\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)]^{d}\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}

and

{\bf D}_{h}=\{{\bf v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid{\bf v}|_{K}=c({\bf x}-{\bf x}_{K}),c\in\mathbb{R}\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

where
{\bf x}_{K}
is the centroid of the element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Then, our EG finite element space for the velocity is defined by

{\bf V}_{h}={\bf C}_{h}\oplus{\bf D}_{h}.

Therefore, any
{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}
has a unique decomposition
{\bf v}={\bf v}^{C}+{\bf v}^{D}
such that
{\bf v}^{C}\in{\bf C}_{h}
and
{\bf v}^{D}\in{\bf D}_{h}
.
On the other hand, we employ the mean-zero, piecewise constant space for the pressure. That is, the pressure space is defined by

{\bf Q}_{h}=\{q\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\mid q\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

The standard EG scheme introduced in
[
42
]
reads as follows:

Algorithm 1

Standard EG (
ST-EG
) method

Find
({\bf u}_{h},p_{h})\in{\bf V}_{h}\times{\bf Q}_{h}
such that

\displaystyle{\bf a}({\bf u}_{h},{\bf v})-{\bf b}({\bf v},p_{h})

\displaystyle=({\bf f},{\bf v})

\displaystyle\quad\forall{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h},

(3.2a)

\displaystyle{\bf b}({\bf u}_{h},q)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\forall q\in{\bf Q}_{h},

(3.2b)

where

\displaystyle{\bf a}({\bf v},{\bf w})

\displaystyle:=\nu\big((\nabla{\bf v},\nabla{\bf w})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\{\nabla{\bf v}\}{\bf n}_{e},[{\bf w}]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\langle\{\nabla{\bf w}\}{\bf n}_{e},[{\bf v}]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}+\rho\langle h_{e}^{-1}[{\bf w}],[{\bf v}]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}\big),

(3.3a)

\displaystyle{\bf b}({\bf w},q)

\displaystyle:=(\nabla\cdot{\bf w},q)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle[{\bf w}]\cdot{\bf n}_{e},\{q\}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}.

(3.3b)

Here,
\rho>0
is a penalty parameter and
h_{e}=|e|^{1/(d-1)}
, where
|e|
is the length/area of the edge/face
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
.

3.2
Pressure-robust EG method

In order to define a pressure-robust EG scheme, we use a velocity reconstruction operator
\mathcal{R}:{\bf V}_{h}\to H(\text{div},\Omega)

that maps any function
{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}
into the Brezzi-Douglas-Marini space of index
1
, denoted by BDM
1

[
7
]
. Specifically, for any
{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}
,
\mathcal{R}{\bf v}\in\text{BDM}_{1}
is defined by

\displaystyle\int_{e}(\mathcal{R}{\bf v})\cdot{\bf n}_{e}p_{1}\ ds

\displaystyle=\int_{e}\{{\bf v}\}\cdot{\bf n}_{e}p_{1}\ ds

\displaystyle\quad\forall p_{1}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(e),\ \forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o},

(3.4a)

\displaystyle\int_{e}(\mathcal{R}{\bf v})\cdot{\bf n}_{e}p_{1}\ ds

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\forall p_{1}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(e),\ \forall e\in\partial\Omega.

(3.4b)

Remark 3.1
.

The above definition of
\mathcal{R}
is useful in numerical analysis of the new method. In practice, however, we reconstruct only the discontinuous component of each vector based on the following observation:
For any
{\bf v}={\bf v}^{C}+{\bf v}^{D}\in{\bf V}_{h}
, the operator
\mathcal{R}
defined in (
3.4
) keeps the continuous component
{\bf v}^{C}
the same while mapping
{\bf v}^{D}
into the lowest-order Raviart-Thomas space
[
34
]
, denoted by
\text{RT}_{0}
. Indeed,
\mathcal{R}{\bf v}^{D}\in\text{RT}_{0}
satisfies

\int_{e}\mathcal{R}{\bf v}^{D}\cdot{\bf n}_{e}\ ds=\int_{e}\{{\bf v}^{D}\}\cdot{\bf n}_{e}\ ds\quad\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}.

(3.5)

Using the definition of the operator
\mathcal{R}
, we can easily prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

The bilinear form
{\bf b}({\bf w},q)
can be written using the velocity reconstruction operator
\mathcal{R}
as follows:

{\bf b}({\bf w},q)=(\nabla\cdot\mathcal{R}{\bf w},q)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

(3.6)

We are now ready to define our pressure-robust EG method using the velocity reconstruction operator
\mathcal{R}
as below.

Algorithm 2

Pressure-robust EG (
PR-EG
) method

Find
({\bf u}_{h},p_{h})\in{\bf V}_{h}\times{\bf Q}_{h}
such that

\displaystyle{\bf a}({\bf u}_{h},{\bf v})-{\bf b}({\bf v},p_{h})

\displaystyle=({\bf f},\mathcal{R}{\bf v}),

\displaystyle\quad\forall{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h},

(3.7a)

\displaystyle{\bf b}({\bf u}_{h},q)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\quad\forall q\in{\bf Q}_{h},

(3.7b)

where the bilinear forms
{\bf a}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
{\bf b}(\cdot,\cdot)
are the same as in (
3.3
).

As the bilinear forms
{\bf a}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
{\bf b}(\cdot,\cdot)
are the same as in (
3.3
), the
PR-EG
method in Algorithm
2
and the
ST-EG
method in Algorithm
1

have the same stiffness matrix. However, this seemingly simple modification on the right-hand side vector significantly enhances the performance of the
ST-EG
method, as will be shown in the error estimates and numerical experiments.

4
Well-Posedness and Error Estimates

We first establish the inf-sup condition
[
7
]
and prove a priori error estimates for the
PR-EG
method, Algorithm
2
. To this end, we employ the following mesh-dependent norm in
{\bf V}_{h}
:

\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}=\left(\lVert\nabla{\bf v}\rVert_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\rho\lVert h_{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}}[{\bf v}]\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Also, the analysis relies on the interpolation operator
\Pi_{h}:[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}\to{\bf V}_{h}
, defined in
[
43
,
42
]
, and the local
L^{2}
-projection
\mathcal{P}_{0}:H^{1}(\Omega)\to{\bf Q}_{h}
.
Here, we only state their useful properties and error estimates without proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists an interpolation operator
\Pi_{h}:[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}\to{\bf V}_{h}
such that

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot({\bf v}-\Pi_{h}{\bf v}),1)_{K}=0

\displaystyle\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\ \forall{\bf v}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d},

(4.1a)

\displaystyle|{\bf v}-\Pi_{h}{\bf v}|_{j}\leq Ch^{m-j}|{\bf v}|_{m}

\displaystyle\quad 0\leq j\leq m\leq 2,\ \forall{\bf v}\in[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d},

(4.1b)

\displaystyle\lVert{\bf v}-\Pi_{h}{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}\leq Ch\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\quad 0\leq j\leq m\leq 2,\ \forall{\bf v}\in[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d},

(4.1c)

\displaystyle\lVert\Pi_{h}{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}\leq C|{\bf v}|_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\forall{\bf v}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{d}.

(4.1d)

Also, the local
L^{2}
-projection
\mathcal{P}_{0}:H^{1}(\Omega)\to{\bf Q}_{h}
satisfies

\displaystyle(w-\mathcal{P}_{0}w,1)=0

\displaystyle\quad\forall w\in H^{1}(\Omega),

(4.2a)

\displaystyle\lVert w-\mathcal{P}_{0}w\rVert_{0}\leq Ch\lVert w\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\forall w\in H^{1}(\Omega).

(4.2b)

The inf-sup condition and the coercivity and continuity of the bilinear form
{\bf a}(\cdot,\cdot)
can be proved
following the same lines as the proofs of Lemma 4.5 in
[
42
]
. Therefore, we only state the results here.

Lemma 4.2
.

Provided that
\rho
is large enough, there exists a constant
\alpha>0
, independent of

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}\\
{\bf v}\neq 0\end{subarray}}{sup}\frac{{\bf b}({\bf v},q)}{\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}}\geq\alpha\lVert q\rVert_{0}\quad\forall q\in{\bf Q}_{h}.

(4.3)

Lemma 4.3
.

Provided that the penalty parameter
\rho
is large enough, there exist positive constants
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
, independent of

and
\nu
, such that

\displaystyle{\bf a}({\bf v},{\bf v})

\displaystyle\geq\kappa_{1}\nu\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

(4.4)

\displaystyle|{\bf a}({\bf v},{\bf w})|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{2}\nu\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}\lVert{\bf w}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\quad\forall{\bf v},{\bf w}\in{\bf V}_{h}.

(4.5)

We now have the following well-posedness result of the

method.

Theorem 4.4
.

There exists a unique solution
({\bf u}_{h},p_{h})\in{\bf V}_{h}\times{\bf Q}_{h}
to the
PR-EG

provided that the penalty term

Proof.

Thanks to the finite-dimensionality of
{\bf V}_{h}
and
{\bf Q}_{h}
, it suffices to show the uniqueness of the solution.
Suppose there exist two solutions
({\bf u}_{h,1},p_{h,1})
and
({\bf u}_{h,2},p_{h,2})
to the
PR-EG
method, and let
{\bf w}_{h}={\bf u}_{h,1}-{\bf u}_{h,2}
and
r_{h}=p_{h,1}-p_{h,2}
.
Then, it is trivial to see that

\displaystyle{\bf a}({\bf w}_{h},{\bf v})-{\bf b}({\bf v},r_{h})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\forall{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h},

(4.6a)

\displaystyle{\bf b}({\bf w}_{h},q)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\forall q\in{\bf Q}_{h}.

(4.6b)

Taking
{\bf v}={\bf w}_{h}
and
q=r_{h}
in the above system (
4.6
) and adding the two equations, we obtain

{\bf a}({\bf w}_{h},{\bf w}_{h})=0.

Then, the coercivity of
{\bf a}(\cdot,\cdot)
yields
{\bf w}_{h}=\bf{0}
.
On the other hand,
{\bf w}_{h}=\bf{0}
reduces (

{\bf b}({\bf v},r_{h})=0\quad\forall{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}.

Then, we get

by using the inf-sup condition in (

).
Hence, the proof is complete.
∎

In order to facilitate our error analysis, we consider an elliptic projection
\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}
of the true solution
{\bf u}
defined as follows:

{\bf a}(\overline{{\bf u}}_{h},{\bf v})={\bf a}({\bf u},{\bf v})+\nu(\Delta{\bf u},{\bf v}-\mathcal{R}{\bf v})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\quad\forall{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}.

(4.7)

Then, let us introduce the following notation:

\chi_{\bf u}={\bf u}-\overline{{\bf u}}_{h},\quad\xi_{\bf u}=\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}-{\bf u}_{h},\quad\chi_{p}=p-\mathcal{P}_{0}p,\quad\xi_{p}=\mathcal{P}_{0}p-p_{h}.

(4.8)

Using the above notation, we have

{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h}=\chi_{\bf u}+\xi_{\bf u},\quad p-p_{h}=\chi_{p}+\xi_{p}.

As we already have an error estimate for
\chi_{p}
in (
4.2b
), we only need to establish error estimates for
\chi_{\bf u}
,
\xi_{\bf u}
, and
\xi_{p}
. Let us first prove the following lemma, which will be used for an estimate for
\chi_{\bf u}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}
, we have

\lVert\mathcal{R}{\bf v}-{\bf v}\rVert_{0}\leq Ch\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}.

(4.9)

Proof.

For any
\phi_{K}\in\text{RT}_{0}(K)
, using the standard scaling argument
[
35
, p. 554]
, we have

\lVert\bm{\phi}_{K}\rVert_{0,K}\leq C\sum_{e\in\partial K}h_{e}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\bm{\phi}_{K}\cdot{\bf n}_{K}\rVert_{0,e}

(4.10)

for some constant
C>0
independent of
h
.
Apply (
4.10
) to
\bm{\phi}_{K}=(\mathcal{R}{\bf v}^{D}-{\bf v}^{D})|_{K}\in\text{RT}_{0}(K)
and note that

\{{\bf v}^{D}\}-{\bf v}^{D}=\pm\frac{1}{2}[{\bf v}^{D}].

Then,

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{R}{\bf v}-{\bf v}\rVert_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert\mathcal{R}{\bf v}^{D}-{\bf v}^{D}\rVert_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\leq C\lVert h_{e}^{\frac{1}{2}}[{\bf v}^{D}]\cdot{\bf n}_{e}\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2}\leq Ch^{2}\lVert h_{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}}[{\bf v}^{D}]\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle=Ch^{2}\lVert h_{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}}[{\bf v}]\rVert_{0,\mathcal{E}_{h}}^{2}\leq Ch^{2}\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}.

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}
be the solution of the elliptic problem (
4.7
).
Assuming the true velocity solution
{\bf u}
belongs to
[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}
, the following error estimate holds true:

\lVert{\bf u}-\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}\leq Ch\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}.

(4.11)

Proof.

We already have

\lVert{\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}\leq Ch\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}

from (
4.1c
). To bound the error
\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}:=\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}
,
subtract
{\bf a}(\Pi_{h}{\bf u},{\bf v})
from both sides of (
4.7
) and take
{\bf v}=\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}
to obtain

{\bf a}(\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}},\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}})={\bf a}({\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u},\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}})+\nu(\Delta{\bf u},\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}-\mathcal{R}\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}.\\

Then, we bound the left-hand side by using the coercivity of
{\bf a}(\cdot,\cdot)
and the right-hand side by the continuity of
{\bf a}(\cdot,\cdot)
, along with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
4.9
). Then, we get

\kappa_{1}\nu\lVert\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq\kappa_{2}\nu\lVert{\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}\lVert\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}+\nu\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}\lVert\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}-\mathcal{R}\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}\rVert_{0}\leq Ch\nu\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}\lVert\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}.

Therefore, (
4.11
) follows from the triangle inequality combined with (
4.1c
).
∎

Now, we consider the following lemmas to derive auxiliary error equations.

Lemma 4.7
.

For all
{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}
, we have

(\nabla p,\mathcal{R}{\bf v})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=-{\bf b}({\bf v},\mathcal{P}_{0}p).

(4.12)

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{R}{\bf v}\cdot{\bf n}_{K}
is continuous across the interior edges (faces) and zero on the boundary edges (faces), and
\nabla\cdot\mathcal{R}{\bf v}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)
on each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Therefore, using integration by parts, the regularity of the pressure
p
, and (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\nabla p,\mathcal{R}{\bf v})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle p,\mathcal{R}{\bf v}\cdot{\bf n}_{K}\rangle_{\partial K}-(p,\nabla\cdot\mathcal{R}{\bf v})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=-(\mathcal{P}_{0}p,\nabla\cdot\mathcal{R}{\bf v})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=-{\bf b}({\bf v},\mathcal{P}_{0}p).

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

The auxiliary errors
\xi_{\bf u}
and
\xi_{p}
satisfy the following equations:

\displaystyle{\bf a}(\xi_{\bf u},{\bf v})-{\bf b}({\bf v},\xi_{p})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\forall{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h},

(4.13a)

\displaystyle{\bf b}(\xi_{\bf u},q)

\displaystyle=-{\bf b}(\chi_{\bf u},q)

\displaystyle\quad\forall q\in{\bf Q}_{h}.

(4.13b)

Proof.

To derive (
4.13a
), take the
L^{2}
-inner product of (
1.1a
) with
\mathcal{R}{\bf v}
for

to get

-\nu(\Delta{\bf u},{\bf v})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+(\nabla p,\mathcal{R}{\bf v})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=({\bf f},\mathcal{R}{\bf v})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\nu(\Delta{\bf u},{\bf v}-\mathcal{R}{\bf v})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

(4.14)

Note that standard integration by parts and the regularity of
{\bf u}
yield the following identity:

-\nu(\Delta{\bf u},{\bf v})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}={\bf a}({\bf u},{\bf v})\quad\forall{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}.

Further, using (
4.12
) for the second term on the left side of (
4.14
), we obtain

{\bf a}({\bf u},{\bf v})-{\bf b}({\bf v},\mathcal{P}_{0}p)=({\bf f},\mathcal{R}{\bf v})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\nu(\Delta{\bf u},{\bf v}-\mathcal{R}{\bf v})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

Then, subtract (
3.7a
) from the above equation and use the definition of the elliptic projection
\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}
in (
4.7
) to obtain the first error equation (
4.13a
).
Next, to prove the second error equation (
4.13b
), first note

(\nabla\cdot{\bf u},q)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}={\bf b}({\bf u},q)\quad\forall q\in{\bf Q}_{h}.

The rest of the proof is straightforward.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

We have the following error bound:

\lVert\xi_{p}\rVert_{0}\leq C\nu\lVert\xi_{\bf u}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}.

(4.15a)

Proof.

We obtain the desired bound by using (
4.3
), (
4.13a
), and (
4.5
) as follows:

\lVert\xi_{p}\rVert_{0}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}\\
{\bf v}\neq 0\end{subarray}}{sup}\frac{{\bf b}({\bf v},\xi_{p})}{\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}\\
{\bf v}\neq 0\end{subarray}}{sup}\frac{{\bf a}(\xi_{\bf u},{\bf v})}{\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}}\leq C\nu\lVert\xi_{\bf u}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}.

∎

The last two lemmas lead us to the following auxiliary error estimates.

Lemma 4.10
.

We have the following error estimates for
\xi_{\bf u}
and
\xi_{p}
:

\displaystyle\lVert\xi_{\bf u}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2},

(4.16a)

\displaystyle\lVert\xi_{p}\rVert_{0}

\displaystyle\leq C\nu h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}.

(4.16b)

Proof.

Taking
{\bf v}=\xi_{\bf u}
and
q=\xi_{p}
in (
4.13a
) and (
4.13b
), respectively, and adding the two resulting equations, we get

{\bf a}(\xi_{\bf u},\xi_{\bf u})=-{\bf b}(\chi_{\bf u},\xi_{p}).

Then, we bound the left-hand side from below using the coercivity of
{\bf a}(\cdot,\cdot)
and the right-hand side by using (
4.1a
), the trace inequality, (
4.1b
), and (

):

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}\nu\lVert\xi_{\bf u}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\bf a}(\xi_{\bf u},\xi_{\bf u})=-{\bf b}(\chi_{\bf u},\xi_{p})

\displaystyle=-\langle[\chi_{\bf u}]\cdot{\bf n}_{e},\{\xi_{p}\}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}\leq C\frac{1}{h}\lVert\chi_{\bf u}\rVert_{0}\lVert\xi_{p}\rVert_{0}\leq C\nu h\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}\lVert\xi_{\bf u}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}},

from which (

) follows.
On the other hand, the error bound (

) is an immediate consequence of (

) and (

).
∎

Theorem 4.11
.

Assuming the true solution,
({\bf u},p)
, of the Stokes problem (

) belongs to

to our

\displaystyle\lVert{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2},

\displaystyle\lVert p-p_{h}\rVert_{0}

where a perturbation is introduced in the bilinear form

\displaystyle{\bf a}^{D}({\bf v},{\bf v})

\displaystyle\geq\kappa^{D}_{1}\nu\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\forall{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h},

(5.1)

\displaystyle|{\bf a}^{D}({\bf v},{\bf w})|

.

Using a perturbed bilinear form

) method

Find

such that

\displaystyle{\bf a}^{D}({\bf u}_{h},{\bf v})-{\bf b}({\bf v},p_{h})

\displaystyle=({\bf f},\mathcal{R}{\bf v}),

\displaystyle\quad\forall{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h},

(5.3a)

\displaystyle{\bf b}({\bf u}_{h},q)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\quad\forall q\in{\bf Q}_{h}.

(5.3b)

Here, a specific choice of
{\bf a}^{D}(\cdot,\cdot)

.

of the true solution

using

(5.4)

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}
be the solution of the elliptic problem (
5.4
). If Assumption (

) holds and
{\bf u}\in[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}

(5.5)

Proof.

Let

\lVert{\bf u}-\Pi^{C}{\bf u}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}=|{\bf u}-\Pi^{C}{\bf u}|_{1}\leq Ch\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}.

Let
\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}:=\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}-\Pi^{C}{\bf u}
. Then,

{\bf a}^{D}(\Pi^{C}{\bf u},{\bf v})
from both sides of (

), taking

, and using the fact that
{\bf a}^{D}(\Pi^{C}{\bf u},{\bf v})={\bf a}(\Pi^{C}{\bf u},{\bf v})

{\bf a}^{D}(\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}},\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}})={\bf a}({\bf u}-\Pi^{C}{\bf u},\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}})+\nu(\Delta{\bf u},\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}-\mathcal{R}\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

{\bf a}^{D}(\cdot,\cdot)

\kappa^{D}_{1}\nu\lVert\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq\kappa_{2}\nu\lVert{\bf u}-\Pi^{C}{\bf u}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}\lVert\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}+\nu\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}\lVert\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}-\mathcal{R}\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}\rVert_{0}\leq Ch\nu\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2}\lVert\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}.

Therefore, (

\lVert{\bf u}-\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\lVert{\bf u}-\Pi^{C}{\bf u}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}+\lVert\xi_{\overline{{\bf u}}_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}
.

.

\displaystyle\leq Ch\lVert{\bf u}\rVert_{2},

5.2
Perturbed bilinear form
{\bf a}^{D}

In this section, we introduce one particular choice of the perturbed bilinear form
{\bf a}^{D}(\cdot,\cdot)
, which allows for the elimination of the DG
component of the velocity vector via static condensation.

For any
{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}
, we have a unique decomposition

{\bf v}={\bf v}^{C}+{\bf v}^{D}
,
where
{\bf v}^{C}\in{\bf C}_{h}
and
{\bf v}^{D}\in{\bf D}_{h}
. Therefore, we have, for
{\bf v},\ {\bf w}\in{\bf V}_{h}
,

{\bf a}({\bf v},{\bf w})={\bf a}({\bf v}^{C},{\bf w}^{C})+{\bf a}({\bf v}^{C},{\bf w}^{D})+{\bf a}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{C})+{\bf a}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{D}).

Denote the basis of
{\bf D}_{h}
by
\{\bm{\Phi}_{K}\}_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}
and write
{\bf v}^{D}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}v_{K}\bm{\Phi}_{K}
and
{\bf w}^{D}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}w_{K}\bm{\Phi}_{K}
.
Then,

{\bf a}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{D})=\sum_{K,\ K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}v_{K}w_{K^{\prime}}{\bf a}(\bm{\Phi}_{K},\bm{\Phi}_{K^{\prime}}).

Define a bilinear form
{\bf d}:{\bf D}_{h}\times{\bf D}_{h}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
by

{\bf d}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{D}):=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}v_{K}w_{K}{\bf a}(\bm{\Phi}_{K},\bm{\Phi}_{K}).

We define a perturbed bilinear form
{\bf a}^{D}(\cdot,\cdot)
of
{\bf a}(\cdot,\cdot)
by replacing
{\bf a}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{D})
with
{\bf d}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{D})
. That is, for
{\bf v},\ {\bf w}\in{\bf V}_{h}
,

{\bf a}^{D}({\bf v},{\bf w}):={\bf a}({\bf v}^{C},{\bf w}^{C})+{\bf a}({\bf v}^{C},{\bf w}^{D})+{\bf a}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{C})+{\bf d}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{D}).

(5.6)

Note that, for any
{\bf v}^{C}\in{\bf C}_{h}
,

{\bf a}^{D}({\bf v}^{C},{\bf w})={\bf a}({\bf v}^{C},{\bf w}^{C})+{\bf a}({\bf v}^{C},{\bf w}^{D})={\bf a}({\bf v}^{C},{\bf w}),\quad\forall\,{\bf w}\in{\bf V}_{h},

which verifies that the bilinear form
{\bf a}^{D}(\cdot,\cdot)
defined in (
5.6
) satisfies Assumption (
A1
).

Next, we want to prove the coercivity and continuity of
{\bf a}^{D}(\cdot,\cdot)
on
{\bf D}_{h}
with respect to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}
. This will be done in several steps. We first introduce a bilinear form
{{\bf a}_{\mathcal{E}}}
corresponding to the mesh-dependent norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}

{{\bf a}_{\mathcal{E}}}({\bf v},{\bf w}):=\nu\left((\nabla{\bf v},\nabla{\bf w})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\rho\langle h_{e}^{-1}{[\![}{\bf w}{]\!]},{[\![}{\bf v}{]\!]}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}\right),

from which we immediately see that

\nu\lVert{\bf v}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}={{\bf a}_{\mathcal{E}}}({\bf v},{\bf v}).

Also, for
{\bf v}^{D},\ {\bf w}^{D}\in{\bf D}_{h}
, we have

{{\bf a}_{\mathcal{E}}}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{D})=\sum_{K,\ K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}u_{K}v_{K^{\prime}}{{\bf a}_{\mathcal{E}}}(\bm{\Phi}_{K},\bm{\Phi}_{K^{\prime}}).

Then, we define another bilinear form
{{\bf d}_{\mathcal{E}}}:{\bf D}_{h}\times{\bf D}_{h}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
as follows:

{{\bf d}_{\mathcal{E}}}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{D}):=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}u_{K}v_{K}{{\bf a}_{\mathcal{E}}}(\bm{\Phi}_{K},\bm{\Phi}_{K}).

Note, from the definitions, that both bilinear forms
{{\bf a}_{\mathcal{E}}}
and
{{\bf d}_{\mathcal{E}}}
are symmetric and positive definite (SPD). Also, thanks to the coercivity and continuity of the bilinear form
{\bf a}(\cdot,\cdot)

{\bf V}_{h}

4.3

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}{{\bf a}_{\mathcal{E}}}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf v}^{D})\leq{\bf a}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf v}^{D})\leq\kappa_{2}{{\bf a}_{\mathcal{E}}}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf v}^{D}),

(5.7)

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}{{\bf d}_{\mathcal{E}}}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf v}^{D})\leq{\bf d}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf v}^{D})\leq\kappa_{2}{{\bf d}_{\mathcal{E}}}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf v}^{D}).

(5.8)

) on the enrichment space
{\bf D}_{h}
, and several applications of triangular and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities.
∎

So far, we have verified the coercivity and continuity conditions, (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), and Assumption (
A1
) for the perturbed bilinear form
{\bf a}^{D}(\cdot,\cdot)
defined in (
5.6
). Therefore, the resulting

PPR-EG
method in Algorithm
3
is well-posed and converges at the optimal rate as proved in Theorem
5.3
.

5.3
Elimination of the DG component of the velocity

The
PR-EG
method in Algorithm
2
results in a linear system in the following matrix form:

\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}_{DD}&\sf{A}_{DC}&\sf{G}_{D}\\
\sf{A}_{CD}&\sf{A}_{CC}&\sf{G}_{C}\\
\sf{G}^{\top}_{D}&\sf{G}^{\top}_{C}&\sf{0}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\sf{U}^{D}\\
\sf{U}^{C}\\
\sf{P}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{f}_{D}\\
\sf{f}_{C}\\
\sf{0}\end{pmatrix},

(5.11)

where
{\bf a}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{D})\mapsto\sf{A}_{DD}
,
{\bf a}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{C})\mapsto\sf{A}_{DC}
,
{\bf a}({\bf v}^{C},{\bf w}^{D})\mapsto\sf{A}_{CD}
,
{\bf a}({\bf v}^{C},{\bf w}^{C})\mapsto\sf{A}_{CC}
,
{\bf b}({\bf v}^{D},p)\mapsto\sf{G}_{D}
,
{\bf b}({\bf v}^{C},p)\mapsto\sf{G}_{C}
,
({\bf f},\mathcal{R}{\bf v}^{D})\mapsto\sf{f}_{D}
, and
({\bf f},{\bf v}^{C})\mapsto\sf{f}_{C}
.

The coefficient matrix in (
5.11
) will be denoted by
\mathcal{A}
.
On the other hand, the
PPR-EG
method, Algorithm
3
, basically replaces
\sf{A}_{DD}
in (
5.11
) with a diagonal matrix
\sf{D}_{DD}:=\texttt{diag}(\sf{A}_{DD})
, which was resulted from
{\bf d}({\bf v}^{D},{\bf w}^{D})
. Therefore, its stiffness matrix is as follows:

\mathcal{A}^{D}:=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{D}_{DD}&\sf{A}_{DC}&\sf{G}_{D}\\
\sf{A}_{CD}&\sf{A}_{CC}&\sf{G}_{C}\\
\sf{G}^{\top}_{D}&\sf{G}^{\top}_{C}&\sf{0}\end{pmatrix}.

Indeed, the diagonal block
\sf{D}_{DD}
allows us to eliminate the DoFs corresponding to the DG component of the velocity vector. Specifically, we can obtain the following two-by-two block form via static condensation:

\mathcal{A}^{E}:=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}_{CC}-\sf{A}_{CD}\sf{D}_{DD}^{-1}\sf{A}_{DC}&\sf{G}_{C}-\sf{A}_{CD}\sf{D}_{DD}^{-1}\sf{G}_{D}\\
\sf{G}^{\top}_{C}-\sf{G}^{\top}_{D}\sf{D}_{DD}^{-1}\sf{A}_{DC}&-\sf{G}^{\top}_{D}\sf{D}_{DD}^{-1}\sf{G}_{D}\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}^{E}_{u}&\sf{G}^{E}\\
(\sf{G}^{E})^{\top}&-\sf{A}^{E}_{p}\end{pmatrix}.

The method corresponding to this condensed linear system is well-posed as it is obtained from the well-posed
PPR-EG
method via state condensation (see
[
2
, Theorem 3.2]
for a similar proof). Moreover, this method has the same DoFs as the
H^{1}
-conforming
\mathbb{P}_{1}
-
\mathbb{P}_{0}
method for the Stokes equations. Therefore, this method can be viewed as a stabilized
\mathbb{P}_{1}
-
\mathbb{P}_{0}
scheme for the Stokes equations, where a stabilization term appears in every sub-block. Similar stabilization techniques have been studied in
[
36
,
37
]
.
We emphasize that this new stabilized
\mathbb{P}_{1}
-
\mathbb{P}_{0}
scheme is not only stable but also pressure-robust.
Then, the algorithm to find
\sf{U}=(\sf{U}^{D},\sf{U}^{C})^{\top}
and
\sf{P}
is summarized in Algorithm
4
.

Algorithm 4

Condensed pressure-robust EG (
CPR-EG
) method

1:

Compute
\mathsf{f}^{E}=(\sf{f}_{C}-\sf{A}_{CD}\sf{D}_{DD}^{-1}\sf{f}_{D},-\sf{G}_{D}^{\top}\sf{D}_{DD}^{-1}\sf{f}_{D})

2:

Solve
\mathcal{A}^{E}\mathsf{x}^{E}=\mathsf{f}^{E}
for
\mathsf{x}^{E}

3:

Set
(\sf{U}^{C},\sf{P})=\mathsf{x}^{E}

4:

Compute
\sf{U}^{D}=\sf{D}_{DD}^{-1}(\sf{f}_{D}-\sf{A}_{DC}\sf{U}^{C}-\sf{G}_{D}\sf{P})

5.4
Block preconditioners

In this subsection, we discuss block preconditioners for solving the linear systems resulted from the three pressure-robust EG algorithms,
i.e.
, the
PR-EG
,
PPR-EG
, and
CPR-EG
methods. We mainly follow the general framework developed in
[
27
,
28
,
1
,
2
]
to design robust block preconditioners. The main idea is based on the well-posedness of the proposed EG discretizations.

As mentioned in Section
3
, the
ST-EG
method in Algorithm
1
and the
PR-EG
method in Algorithm
2
have the same stiffness matrices but different right-hand-side vectors.
Therefore, the block preconditioners developed for the
ST-EG
method in
[
42
]
can be directly applied for the
PR-EG
method.
For the sake of completeness, we recall those preconditioners here as follows.

\mathcal{B}_{D}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}_{u}&\sf{0}\\
\sf{0}&\nu^{-1}\sf{M}_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},\quad\mathcal{B}_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}_{u}&\sf{0}\\
\sf{G}^{\top}&\nu^{-1}\sf{M}_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},\quad\mathcal{B}_{U}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}_{u}&\sf{G}\\
\sf{0}&\nu^{-1}\sf{M}_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},

where

\sf{A}_{u}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}_{DD}&\sf{A}_{DC}\\
\sf{A}_{CD}&\sf{A}_{CC}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\sf{G}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{G}_{D}\\
\sf{G}_{C}\\
\end{pmatrix},

and
\sf{M}_{p}
is the mass matrix,
i.e.
,
(p_{h},q_{h})\mapsto\sf{M}_{p}
. We want to point out that, in
[
42
]
, the block corresponding to the velocity part is based on the mesh-dependent norm on
{\bf V}_{h}
. Here, we directly use the blocks from the stiffness matrix
\mathcal{A}
. Lemma
4.3
makes sure that this still leads to effective preconditioners. As suggested
[
42
]
, while the inverse of
\sf{M}_{p}
is trivial since it is diagonal, inverting the diagonal block
\sf{A}_{u}
could be expensive and sometimes infeasible. Therefore, we approximately invert this diagonal block and define the following inexact counterparts

\mathcal{M}_{D}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{H}_{u}&\sf{0}\\
\sf{0}&\nu\sf{M}^{-1}_{p}\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathcal{M}_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{H}_{u}^{-1}&\sf{0}\\
\sf{G}^{\top}&\nu^{-1}\sf{M}_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},\quad\mathcal{M}_{U}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{H}_{u}^{-1}&\sf{G}\\
\sf{0}&\nu^{-1}\sf{M}_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},

where
\sf{H}_{u}
is spectrally equivalent to
\sf{A}_{u}^{-1}
.

Next, we consider the
PPR-EG
method, Algorithm
3
. Since it is well-posed, as shown in Theorem
5.1
, we can similarly develop the corresponding block preconditioners as follows:

\mathcal{B}^{D}_{D}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}^{D}_{u}&\sf{0}\\
\sf{0}&\nu^{-1}\sf{M}_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},\quad\mathcal{B}^{D}_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}^{D}_{u}&\sf{0}\\[5.0pt]
\sf{G}^{\top}&\nu^{-1}\sf{M}_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},\quad\mathcal{B}^{D}_{U}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}^{D}_{u}&\sf{G}\\
\sf{0}&\nu^{-1}\sf{M}_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},

where

\sf{A}^{D}_{u}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{D}_{DD}&\sf{A}_{DC}\\
\sf{A}_{CD}&\sf{A}_{CC}\\
\end{pmatrix},

and their inexact versions providing more practical values:

\mathcal{M}^{D}_{D}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{H}^{D}_{u}&\sf{0}\\
\sf{0}&\nu\sf{M}^{-1}_{p}\end{pmatrix},\hskip 0.5pt\mathcal{M}^{D}_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}(\sf{H}^{D}_{u})^{-1}&\sf{0}\\
\sf{G}^{\top}&{{\nu}^{-1}}\sf{M}_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},\hskip 0.5pt\mathcal{M}^{D}_{U}=\begin{pmatrix}(\sf{H}^{D}_{u})^{-1}&\sf{G}\\
\sf{0}&{{\nu}^{-1}}\sf{M}_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},

where
\sf{H}^{D}_{u}
is spectrally equivalent to
(\sf{A}^{D}_{u})^{-1}
.

Finally, we consider the
CPR-EG
method, Algorithm
4
.
The following block preconditioners are constructed based on the well-posedness of the
CPR-EG
method:

\mathcal{B}^{E}_{D}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}^{E}_{u}&\sf{0}\\
\sf{0}&\sf{S}_{p}^{E}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},\quad\mathcal{B}^{E}_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}^{E}_{u}&\sf{0}\\
(\sf{G}^{E})^{\top}&\sf{S}^{E}_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},\quad\mathcal{B}^{E}_{U}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{A}^{E}_{u}&\sf{G}^{E}\\
\sf{0}&\sf{S}^{E}_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},

where
\sf{S}_{p}^{E}:=\nu^{-1}\sf{M}_{p}+\sf{A}^{E}_{p}=\nu^{-1}\sf{M}_{p}+\sf{G}^{\top}_{D}\sf{D}_{DD}^{-1}\sf{G}_{D}
. In this case, the second diagonal block,
\sf{S}_{p}^{E}
, is not a diagonal matrix anymore. Therefore, in the inexact version block preconditioners, we need to replace
(\sf{S}^{E}_{p})^{-1}
by its spectrally equivalent approximation
\sf{H}^{E}_{p}
. This leads to the following inexact block preconditioners:

\mathcal{M}^{E}_{D}=\begin{pmatrix}\sf{H}^{E}_{u}&\sf{0}\\
\sf{0}&\sf{H}^{E}_{p}\end{pmatrix},\ \mathcal{M}^{D}_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}(\sf{H}^{E}_{u})^{-1}&\sf{0}\\
(\sf{G}^{E})^{\top}&(\sf{H}^{E}_{p})^{-1}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},\ \mathcal{M}^{E}_{U}=\begin{pmatrix}(\sf{H}^{D}_{u})^{-1}&\sf{G}^{E}\\
\sf{0}&(\sf{H}^{E}_{p})^{-1}\end{pmatrix}^{-1},

where
\sf{H}^{E}_{u}
and
\sf{H}^{E}_{p}
are spectrally equivalent to
(\sf{A}^{E}_{u})^{-1}
and
(\sf{A}^{E}_{p})
, respectively.

Before closing this section, we want to point out that, following the general framework in
[
27
,
28
]
, we can show that the block diagonal preconditioners are parameter-robust and can be applied to the minimal residual methods. On the other hand, following the framework presented in
[
27
,
1
,
2
]
, we can also show that block triangular preconditioners are field-of-value equivalent preconditioners and can be applied to the generalized minimal residual (GMRES) method.
We omit the proof here, but we refer the readers to our previous work
[
1
,
2
,
42
]
for similar proofs.

Conclusions

In this paper, we proposed a pressure-robust EG scheme for solving the Stokes equations, describing the steady-state, incompressible viscous fluid flow. The new EG method is based on the recent work
[
42
]
on a stable EG scheme for the Stokes problem, where the velocity error depends on the pressure error and is inversely proportional to viscosity. In order to make the EG scheme in
[
42
]
a pressure-robust scheme, we employed a velocity reconstruction operator on the load vector on the right-hand side of the discrete system. Despite this simple modification, our error analysis shows that the velocity error of the new EG scheme is independent of viscosity and the pressure error, and the method maintains the optimal convergence rates for both the velocity and pressure. We also considered a perturbed version of our pressure-robust EG method. This perturbed method allows for the elimination of the DoFs corresponding to the DG component of the velocity vector via static condensation. The resulting condensed linear system can be viewed as an
H^{1}
-conforming
\mathbb{P}_{1}
-
\mathbb{P}_{0}
scheme with stabilization terms. This stabilized
\mathbb{P}_{1}
-
\mathbb{P}_{0}
scheme is inf-sup stable and pressure-robust as well. Furthermore, we proposed an efficient preconditioning technique whose performance is robust with respect to viscosity. Our two- and three-dimensional numerical experiments verified the theoretical results. In the future, this work will be extended to more complicated incompressible flow models, such as the Oseen and Navier-Stokes equations, where the pressure-robustness is important for simulations in various flow regimes.

Homology concordance and An Infinite rank free subgroup
Hugo Zhou

Abstract
. Two knots are homology concordant if they are smoothly concordant in a homology cobordism. The group 𝒞 ^ ℤ \widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}} (resp. 𝒞 ℤ {\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}} ) was previously defined as the set of knots in homology spheres that bounds homology balls (resp. in S 3 S^{3} ), modulo homology concordance. We prove 𝒞 ^ ℤ / 𝒞 ℤ \widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}} contains a ℤ ∞ \mathbb{Z}^{\infty} subgroup. We construct our family of examples by applying the filtered mapping cone formula to L-space knots, and prove linear independence with the help of the connected knot complex.

1.
Introduction

Two knots in
S^{3}
are
smoothly concordant
if they cobound a smoothly embedded annulus in the product
S^{3}\times I
. One can consider the set of knots in
S^{3}
modulo smooth concordance. This set becomes an abelian group called the
knot concordance group
, under the operation induced by connected sum. It is a well-known result that the knot concordance group contains a
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
summand (see, for example,
[
Lev69
]
).

As a generalization of smooth concordance, the notion
homology concordance
is studied in
[
DR16
]
and
[
HLL18
]
. This paper will build on the established results in
[
HLL18
]
regarding the homology concordance classes of knots in integer homology spheres.

Given two knots
K\subset Y
,
K^{\prime}\subset Y^{\prime}
, where both
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
are integer homology spheres that are homology cobordant to
S^{3}
, we call
K
and
K^{\prime}

homology concordant
if they cobound a smoothly embedded annulus in some homology cobordism between
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
, denoted
(Y,K)\sim(Y^{\prime},K^{\prime})
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the group consists of pairs
(Y,K)
, where
Y
is a homology null-cobordant homology
3
-sphere and
K
a knot in
Y
, modulo homology concordance. The operation, still induced by connected sum now changes both the knot and the
3
-manifold:
(Y,K)\mathbin{\#}(Y^{\prime},K^{\prime})
is given by
(Y\mathbin{\#}Y^{\prime},K\mathbin{\#}K^{\prime})
. The inverse of
(Y,K)
is
(-Y,-K)
.

Similarly, let
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the group consists of pairs
(S^{3},K)
modulo homology concordance, which is a natural subgroup of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

One wants to study the group structure of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, since this is the group analogous to the knot concordance group for knots in homology spheres. Note that
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
naturally inherits the complicated group structure from the knot concordance group. In fact, there is a canonical map from the knot concordance group to
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Though whether this map is injective remains unknown, J. Levine’s examples pass through and constitute a
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
summand in
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
(For a construction of Levine’s examples, see
[
Lev69
]
or
[
Liv05
]
). Due to this observation, we consider instead
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. The quotient group
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
measures the “difference” between knots in
S^{3}
and knots in arbitrary homology spheres that bound homology balls. Adam Simon Levine showed in
[
Lev16
]
that
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is not trivial. Inspired by the study of the knot concordance group, we ask: Is
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
infinitely generated? Does it contain a
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
subgroup?

The first question has been given an affirmative answer in
[
HLL18
]
. Moreover, two approaches to the question have been demonstrated. The first approach relies on an invariant
\theta(Y,K)
, defined as the maximal difference of
d
-invariants of
1/n
-surgeries on
K
(For a definition of
d
-invariant see
[
OS03
]
). A family of pairs
(Y_{j},K_{j})
was constructed whose
\theta(Y_{j},K_{j})\rightarrow\infty
as
j
approaches infinity. With some more argument, this shows the infinite generation of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. However, it is unknown to the author at this point whether the pairs constructed in the proof have infinite order. Another approach is to generalize the knot Floer invariants including
\tau,\nu,\varepsilon,\Upsilon
which originally were only defined for knots in
S^{3}
, to homology concordance invariants. An advantage of this approach is that many of these invariants still have the same additivity properties under connected sums of the knots in an arbitrary integer homology sphere as they do for knots in
S^{3}
. We will adopt this approach in the paper, and answer the second question affirmatively, as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
contains a
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
subgroup.

One of the main tools we use in the proof is the following concordance invariant. The invariant is more or less known to the experts. Since its definition has not yet appeared in the literature, we write it down for completeness.

Theorem 1.2
.

Given a knot
K
in an integer homology sphere
Y
, suppose
C=CFK^{\infty}(Y,K)
is the knot Floer complex. There is a homology concordance invariant
C_{\mathrm{conn}}
, taking value in isomorphism classes of absolutely-graded,
(i,j)
-filtered
\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]
chain complexes. Moreover,
C
is filtered chain homotopy equivalent to
C_{\mathrm{conn}}\oplus A
as a filtered chain complex, where
A
is an acyclic complex.

The invariant is defined in the same way as the connected Heegaard Floer homology in
[
HHL18
]
, ignoring the involution in the original definition. We describe the construction in Section
6
over the ring
\mathbb{F}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}]
. The same construction works over the ring
\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]
as well.

Regarding
\tau
and
\varepsilon
invariants, we review the definitions and properties in section
2
. The key observation is:

Proposition 1.3

(Proposition 3.6(2) in
[
Hom14
]
)
.

Suppose
K
is a knot in
S^{3}
. If
\varepsilon(S^{3},K)=0
, then
\tau(S^{3},K)=0
.

We construct the
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
subgroup by explicitly finding infinitely many generating pairs. For
n>1
, let
M_{n}
denote
+1
-surgery on
T_{2,4n-1}
, the
(2,4n-1)
-torus knot, let
Y_{n}=M_{n}\mathbin{\#}-M_{n}
, and let
K_{n}\subset Y_{n}
denote the connected sum of the core of the surgery in
M_{n}
with the unknot in
-M_{n}
.

Proposition 1.4
.

For
n>1
and
(Y_{n},K_{n})
as above, we have the following,

(1)

\tau(Y_{n},K_{n})=-1
;

(2)

\varepsilon(Y_{n},K_{n})=0
;

(3)

(Y_{n},K_{n})
are linearly independent in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Theorem
1.1
directly follows from Proposition
1.4
(
3
). The construction of
(Y_{n},K_{n})
and the evaluation of their
\tau
and
\varepsilon
invariants are due to a computational result, Theorem
3.1
.

Remark 1.5
.

The quotient group
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
can also be interpreted in PL(piecewise-linear) terms. Recall that every knot in
S^{3}
bounds a PL disk in
B^{4}
by taking the cone. Such a disk is smooth outside the cone point. In
[
Lev16
]
, Adam Simon Levine gives a pair
(Y,K)
such that
Y
bounds a contractible manifold but
K
does not bound a PL disk in any homology ball whose boundary is
Y
. Note that a knot
K
in
Y
bounds a PL disk if and only if
(Y,K)
is trivial in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. One can see this by adding or deleting open balls near the cone points. Likewise, two
pairs
(Y_{0},K_{0}),(Y_{1},K_{1})\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
differ by an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
if and only if
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
cobound a PL
annulus in some homology cobordism from
Y_{0}
to
Y_{1}
. Thus, the quotient
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
can be interpreted
as the group of knots in homology null-cobordant homology spheres modulo PL concordance in
homology cobordisms.

Remark 1.6
.

In the paper
[
HLL18
]
(Remark 1.13.), a variation of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
was brought into discussion. Let
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
denote the subgroup of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
that consists of all pairs
(Y,K)
where
Y
bounds a homology ball
X
in which
K
is freely nulhomotopic (meaning
K
is trivial in
\pi_{1}(X)
, or equivalently
K
bounds a immersed disk in
X
). From the
4
-manifold perspective,
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
is arguably a more appropriate generalization of the knot concordance group, as it measures the failure of replacing immersed disks by embedded ones. It has been shown in
[
Dae18
]
that
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
is a proper subgroup of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, answering a question raised up in
[
HLL18
]
. Moreover, according to Corollary
1
of
[
Dae18
]
, each pair
(Y_{n},K_{n})
in our construction lies in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\backslash\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
since the core of the surgery solid torus normally generates the foundamental group of
+1
-surgery on
T_{2,4n-1}
. We do not know whether their linear combinations lie in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
or not.

One can also consider the analogues of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
in the topological category. Namely, we say knots
(Y_{0},K_{0})
and
(Y_{1},K_{1})
are
topologically homology concordant
if they cobound a locally flat embedded annulus in a topological homology cobordism between
Y_{0}
and
Y_{1}
(which need not carry any smooth structure), and we let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{top}}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{top}}
denote the corresponding concordance groups, as in
[
DR16
]
. Note that every homology
3
-sphere bounds a contractible topological
4
-manifold, so there is no need to impose restriction on which pairs
(Y,K)
are represented in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{top}}
as we did in smooth case (Recall that we required the homology spheres to bound homology balls in the smooth version). There is strong evidence that the natural inclusion
\varphi\colon\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{top}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{top}}
is an isomorphism (see
[
Dav18
]
).

Define the natural map
\psi\colon\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{top}}
by simply forgetting the smooth structure. The kernel of the map
\psi
consists of the pairs that are topologically homology concordant to
(S^{3},O)
, where
O
is the unknot in
S^{3}
. We can refine our construction of the infinite generating pairs such that they are in
\operatorname{ker}\psi
. The following result is suggested by JungHwan Park.

Theorem 1.7
.

For
n>1
, there exists
(Y^{\prime}_{n},J_{n})\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

(1)

(Y^{\prime}_{n},J_{n})\in\operatorname{ker}\psi
;

(2)

(Y^{\prime}_{n},J_{n})
are linearly independent in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.7
is in Section 3.

2.
Concordance invariants from knot floer homology

In this section, we review the concordance invariants that will be useful for the construction, defined in
[
HLL18
]
using tools from
[
Zem19b
]
. We assume that the reader is familiar with the knot Floer invariant package in
S^{3}
, see
[
Hom17
]
for a survey on the topic.

Recall that for a knot
K
in an integer homology sphere
Y
, over the ring
\mathbb{F}[U]
knot floer complex
C=\operatorname{CFK}^{\infty}(Y,K)
decomposes as a vector space
C=\bigoplus_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}C(i,j)
. Up to chain homotopy we can choose the filtered basis such that it is reduced, namely no differential preserves the
(i,j)
coordinate. Let
X\subset\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
be a set such that if
a,c\in X
and
a\prec b\prec c
, then
b\in X
, where
\prec
is the natural partial order on
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
. Let
CX=\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in X}C(i,j)
.
CX
is naturally a subquotient complex of
C
.

Consider the maps

\displaystyle\iota_{s}

\displaystyle\colon C\{i=0,j\leq s\}\rightarrow C\{i=0\},

\displaystyle v_{s}

\displaystyle\colon C\{\max(i,j-s)=0\}\rightarrow C\{i=0\},

\displaystyle v^{\prime}_{s}

\displaystyle\colon C\{i=0\}\rightarrow C\{\min(i,j-s)=0\},

where
\iota_{s}
is inclusion,
v_{s}
consists of quotienting by
C\{i<0,j=s\}
followed by inclusion and
v^{\prime}_{s}
consists of quotienting by
C\{i=0,j<s\}
followed by inclusion. Recall that
C\{i=0\}\simeq\widehat{\operatorname{CF}}(Y)
and
C\{i\geq 0\}\simeq\operatorname{CF}^{+}(Y)
. Also, let
\rho\colon\widehat{\operatorname{CF}}(Y)\rightarrow\operatorname{CF}^{+}(Y)
denote inclusion.

Definition 2.1

(Definition 4.2 in
[
HLL18
]
)
.

Let
K
be a knot in an integer homology sphere
Y
. Define

\displaystyle\tau(Y,K)

\displaystyle=\min\{s\mid\operatorname{im}(\rho_{*}\circ\iota_{s*})\cap U^{N}\operatorname{HF}^{+}(Y)\neq 0\ \forall N\gg 0\}

\displaystyle\nu(Y,K)

\displaystyle=\min\{s\mid\operatorname{im}(\rho_{*}\circ v_{s*})\cap U^{N}\operatorname{HF}^{+}(Y)\neq 0\ \forall N\gg 0\}

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}(Y,K)

\displaystyle=\max\{s\mid v^{\prime}_{s*}(x)\neq 0\ \forall x\in\widehat{\operatorname{HF}}(Y)\textup{ s.t. }\rho_{*}(x)\neq 0\textup{ and }\rho_{*}(x)\in U^{N}\operatorname{HF}^{+}(Y)\ \forall N\gg 0\}.

The definition of
\varepsilon
relies on the relation between
\tau,\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
; we have the following lemma:

Lemma 2.2

(Lemma 4.5 in
[
HLL18
]
)
.

Let
K
be a knot in an integer homology sphere
Y
. The following three cases are exhaustive and mutually exclusive:

•

\nu(Y,K)=\tau(Y,K)+1
and
\nu^{\prime}(Y,K)=\tau(Y,K)
,

•

\nu(Y,K)=\tau(Y,K)
and
\nu^{\prime}(Y,K)=\tau(Y,K)-1
,

•

\nu(Y,K)=\tau(Y,K)
and
\nu^{\prime}(Y,K)=\tau(Y,K)
.

The three cases in the above lemma correspond to the three different values of
\varepsilon
.

Definition 2.3

(Definition 4.6 in
[
HLL18
]
)
.

Let
K
be a knot in an integer homology sphere
Y
. Define

\varepsilon(Y,K)=\begin{cases}-1\quad&\text{if }\nu(Y,K)=\tau(Y,K)+1,\\
1\quad&\text{if }\nu^{\prime}(Y,K)=\tau(Y,K)-1,\\
0\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The following properties will be especially useful.

Proposition 2.4

(Propositions 4.7, 4.10, 4.11 in
[
HLL18
]
)
.

Both
\tau,\varepsilon
are homology concordance invariants. Moreover, given pairs
(Y,K)
and
(Y^{\prime},K^{\prime})
as group elements in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
,

(1)

\tau(-Y,K)=-\tau(Y,K)
.

(2)

\tau(Y\mathbin{\#}Y^{\prime},K\mathbin{\#}K^{\prime})=\tau(Y,K)+\tau(Y^{\prime},K^{\prime})
.

(3)

\varepsilon(-Y,K)=-\varepsilon(Y,K)
.

(4)

If
\varepsilon(Y,K)=\varepsilon(Y^{\prime},K^{\prime})
, then
\varepsilon(Y\mathbin{\#}Y^{\prime},K\mathbin{\#}K^{\prime})=\varepsilon(Y,K)
.

(5)

If
\varepsilon(Y,K)=0
, then
\varepsilon(Y\mathbin{\#}Y^{\prime},K\mathbin{\#}K^{\prime})=\varepsilon(Y^{\prime},K^{\prime})
.

Lastly, we recall the following obstruction to knots in
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
observed in
[
HLL18
]
:

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
(Y,K)
be an element in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
\varepsilon(Y,K)=0
, but
\tau(Y,K)\neq 0
, then
(Y,K)
generates a
\mathbb{Z}
-subgroup in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

According to Proposition
1.3
,
(Y,K)
is not homology concordant to any knot in
S^{3}
. In other words,
(Y,K)
represents a non-trivial element in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Suppose
\tau(Y,K)=a
, where
a\neq 0
. Now by Proposition
2.4
,
\varepsilon(\mathbin{\#}_{n}(Y,K))=0
while
\tau(\mathbin{\#}_{n}(Y,K))=na
, and this completes the proof.
∎

3.
The core of surgery on L-space knots

Starting from this section, we will present the computational result that leads to our construction of the
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
-subgroup. It relies on the knot Floer homology of the core of a Dehn surgery on L-space knots. We now explain the context and give a more precise description of the result.

A knot
K\subset S^{3}
is called an
L-space knot
if the surgery
S^{3}_{n}(K)
is an L-space for
n
large enough. These knots have relatively simple knot Floer complexes that are determined by the Alexander polynomial. See Section 7 of
[
HM17
]
for a more detailed review regarding L-space knots. Since their notations give a certain convenience in representing the symmetry relation of knot Floer complex of L-space knots, we will borrow them in this section and throughout the computation in the next two sections.

In
[
OS05
]
, Ozsváth and Szabó proved that if
K
is an L-space knot, then the Alexander polynomial of
K
is of the form

\Delta_{K}(t)=(-1)^{m}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{m-i}(t^{n_{i}}+t^{-n_{i}})

for a sequence of positive integers
0<n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots<n_{m}.
Here,
n_{m}=g(K)
is the genus of
K
. Let
n(K)\geqslant 0
be the quantity

n(K):=n_{m}-n_{m-1}+\dots+(-1)^{m-2}n_{2}+(-1)^{m-1}n_{1}.

Furthermore, let
\ell_{s}=n_{s}-n_{s-1}
. Since the unknot is the only L-space knot with
n(K)=0
(whose dual knot is the unknot in
S^{3}
), we will only consider knots with
n(K)\geqslant 1
for our computational result.

L-space knots have a knot Floer complex where differentials form a “staircase” . The knot Floer complex consists of
2m+1
generators
x_{0},x^{1}_{1},x^{2}_{1},...,x^{1}_{m},x^{2}_{m}
. The
(i,j)
-filtration of
x_{0},x^{1}_{m}
are
(0,0)
,
(-n(K),g(K)-n(K))
, respectively. The filtration of
x^{t}_{m-2s}
and
x^{t}_{m-(2s+1)}
differ only in
i
-filtration by
\ell_{m-2s}
and the filtration of
x^{t}_{m-(2s+1)}
and
x^{t}_{m-(2s+2)}
differ only in
j
-filtration by
\ell_{m-(2s+1)}
. If
m
is odd, the differentials are

\displaystyle\partial(x_{0})

\displaystyle=x_{1}^{1}+x_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\partial(x_{s}^{t})

\displaystyle=x_{s-1}^{t}+x_{s+1}^{t}\text{ for }s>0\text{ even},\ t\in\{1,2\}

whereas if
m
is even, the differentials are

\displaystyle\partial(x_{1}^{t})

\displaystyle=x_{0}+x_{2}^{t}

\displaystyle\partial(x_{s}^{t})

\displaystyle=x_{s-1}^{t}+x_{s+1}^{t}\text{ for }s>1\text{ odd},\ t\in\{1,2\}.

Observe that
n(K)
is the total length of the horizontal arrows in the top half of the complex.

Let
K
be an L-space knot in
S^{3}
. Consider
\operatorname{CFK}^{\infty}(S^{3}_{1}(K),\tilde{K})
, where
\tilde{K}
is the core circle of the surgery solid torus in
1
-surgery on
K
. We are now ready to state the following computational result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
K
is an L-space knot whose knot complex has
2m+1

n(K)=n\geqslant 1
. If
m
is odd, then
\operatorname{CFK}^{\infty}(S^{3}_{1}(K),\tilde{K})
is filtered chain homotopy equivalent to
C_{n}\oplus A
as a filtered chain complex, where
A
is an acyclic complex, and the complex
C_{n}
is generated by three generators whose
(i,j)
-filtrations are
(n-1,n),(0,0)
and
(n,n-1)
respectively, both generators in filtration
(n-1,n)
and
(n,n-1)
having an arrow going to filtration
(0,0).

If
m
is even,
\operatorname{CFK}^{\infty}(S^{3}_{1}(K),\tilde{K})
is homotopy equivalent to

as a filtered chain complex, where

is an acyclic complex, and the complex

consists of one generator with
(i,j)
-filtration
(0,0)
.

In this section, we briefly describe the filtered mapping cone formula and include an example of its application. We assume the reader is familiar with the surgery mapping cone formula by Ozsváth and Szabó

.

The filtered mapping cone formula from
[
HL19
]
, is a refinement of the surgery mapping cone formula by Ozsváth and Szabó. Let

be the reduced filtered knot Floer homology for a knot

in integer homology sphere

. The filtered mapping cone formula puts extra filtrations on the mapping cone formula for
\operatorname{HF}^{+}(Y_{1}(K))
, which in turns gives the knot Floer complex

is the core of the surgery solid torus of the
+1
-surgery on

.

Let

.
For each integer

, let

and

each denote a copy of the chain complex

, and write

and

\mathbb{Z}
-filtrations

and

on each of these complexes as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(A_{s}^{\infty}(i,j))

\displaystyle=\max(i,j-s)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(A_{s}^{\infty}(i,j))

\displaystyle=\max(i+s-1,j)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(B_{s}^{\infty}(i,j))

\displaystyle=i

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(B_{s}^{\infty}(i,j))

\displaystyle=i+s-1.

Let

(resp.

) denote the subcomplex of

(resp.

(resp.

and

y^{(k)}_{i}\otimes x^{(1)}_{0},\quad&i=1,...,k;\end{cases}

\displaystyle a_{i}=y^{(k)}_{i}\otimes y^{(1)}_{1},\quad i=1,...,k;

\displaystyle b_{i}=\begin{cases}x^{(k)}_{k}\otimes y^{(1)}_{1}+(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{\ell-n}y^{(k)}_{k}\otimes x^{(1)}_{1},\quad&i=k,\\
y^{(k)}_{i+1}\otimes x^{(1)}_{0}+y^{(k)}_{i}\otimes x^{(1)}_{1},\quad&i=1,...,k-1;\end{cases}

\displaystyle c_{i}=x^{(k)}_{i-1}\otimes y^{(1)}_{1}+(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{\ell-n}y^{(k)}_{i}\otimes x^{(1)}_{0},i=1,...,k;

\displaystyle d_{i}=x^{(k)}_{i}\otimes x^{(1)}_{0}+x^{(k)}_{i-1}\otimes x^{(1)}_{1},i=1,...,k.

Differentials are

\displaystyle\partial y^{(k+1)}_{i}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{U}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{n-1}x^{(k+1)}_{k+1}+\mathcal{U}^{n-1}\mathcal{V}^{n}x^{(k+1)}_{k}+\mathcal{U}^{n-1}\mathcal{V}^{n}d_{k},\quad&i=k+1,\\
\mathcal{U}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{n-1}x^{(k+1)}_{i}+\mathcal{U}^{n-1}\mathcal{V}^{n}x^{(k+1)}_{i-1},\quad&i=1,...,k;\end{cases}

\displaystyle\partial a_{i}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{U}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{n-1}b_{k}+\mathcal{U}^{n-1}\mathcal{V}^{n}c_{k},\quad&i=k,\\
\mathcal{U}^{\ell}\mathcal{V}^{\ell-1}b_{i}+\mathcal{U}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{n-1}c_{i+1}+\mathcal{U}^{n-1}\mathcal{V}^{n}c_{i},\quad&i=1,...,k-1;\end{cases}

\displaystyle\partial b_{i}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{U}^{\ell-1}\mathcal{V}^{\ell}d_{k},\quad&i=k,\\
\mathcal{U}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{n-1}d_{i+1}+\mathcal{U}^{n-1}\mathcal{V}^{n}d_{i},\quad&i=1,...,k-1;\end{cases}

\displaystyle\partial c_{i}=\mathcal{U}^{\ell}\mathcal{V}^{\ell-1}d_{i},\quad i=1,...,k.

Let
\mathfrak{D}_{0}
be the subcomplex generated by
\{a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i}\}_{i=1,...,k}
. Conditions (
1
),(
2
) and (
3
) are all straightforward to verify. To show (
4
), let
w_{k+1}=b_{k}
, it then follows that
\partial\big(y^{(k+1)}_{k+1}+(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{-\ell+n}w_{k+1}\big)=\mathcal{U}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{n-1}x^{(k+1)}_{k+1}+\mathcal{U}^{n-1}\mathcal{V}^{n}x^{(k+1)}_{k}.
This completes the proof of Lemma
7.6
.
∎

Remark 7.7
.

In Lemma
7.6
if
\ell=n
,
\mathfrak{D}_{0}
is actually a direct summand and
\mathfrak{C}(k,n)+C_{n}\simeq\mathfrak{C}(k+1,n).

Proof of Proposition
7.5
.

We will prove the statement by induction over
k
.
C_{n}\simeq\mathfrak{C}(1,n)
follows directly from Proposition
7.1
.

Suppose the proposition holds for
\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{n_{i}}
, where
\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{n_{i}}=\mathfrak{C}(k,n)\oplus\mathfrak{D}
as a module. Consider
\widetilde{C}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{n_{i}}+C_{\ell},
where
2\leqslant n=n_{1}\leqslant..\leqslant n_{k}\leqslant\ell.
From Lemma
7.6
we know that on the module level
\big(\mathfrak{C}(k,n)\oplus\mathfrak{D}\big)\otimes C_{\ell}=\big(\mathfrak{C}(k,n)\otimes C_{\ell}\big)\oplus\big(\mathfrak{D}\otimes C_{\ell}\big)=\mathfrak{C}(k+1,n)\oplus\mathfrak{D}_{0}\oplus\big(\mathfrak{D}\otimes C_{\ell}\big)
. Note that this gives a basis of
\widetilde{C}
that extends the basis of
C^{\prime\prime}
, which has been chosen in the proof of Lemma
7.6
.

Define
\mathfrak{D}_{1}:=\mathfrak{D}+C_{\ell}
as chain complexes. Note that
\mathfrak{D}_{1}
has underlying module
\mathfrak{D}\otimes C_{\ell}
, which appeared in the above expression, now further equipped with the subcomplex differential.

Define
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}:=\mathfrak{D}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{D}_{1}
first as a module, then equip it with the differential of the chain complex
\widetilde{C}
. Observe that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
is a subcomplex of
\widetilde{C}
because both
\mathfrak{D}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{D}_{1}
are subcomplexes. We will prove that the complex
\widetilde{C}
together with the subcomplex
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
under the basis chosen above satisfy all
4
requirements in Proposition
7.5
.

Obviously,
\widetilde{C}=\mathfrak{C}(k+1,n)\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
as a module. This completes (
1
).

To prove (
2
), observe that
\widetilde{C}/\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
is isomorphic to
\big(\widetilde{C}/\mathfrak{D}_{1}\big)/\mathfrak{D}_{0}
as a chain complex. By definition,
\widetilde{C}/\mathfrak{D}_{1}=\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{n_{i}}+C_{\ell}\big)/(\mathfrak{D}+C_{\ell})
. The natural map between
\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{n_{i}}+C_{\ell}\big)/(\mathfrak{D}+C_{\ell})
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{n_{i}}/\mathfrak{D}+C_{\ell}
is a chain complex isomorphism. By induction hypothesis
\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{n_{i}}/\mathfrak{D}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{C}(k,n)
and finally according to Lemma
7.6
,
\big(\mathfrak{C}(k,n)+C_{\ell}\big)/\mathfrak{D}_{0}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{C}(k+1,n)
as a chain complex, finishing the proof of (
2
).

From our construction of
\mathfrak{C}(k+1,n)
it is not hard to see

\displaystyle x^{(k+1)}_{i}=\begin{cases}x^{(k)}_{k}\otimes x^{(1)}_{1},\quad&i=k+1,\\
x^{(k)}_{i}\otimes x^{(1)}_{0},\quad&i=0,1,...,k;\end{cases}

generates
(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{-1}H_{*}\big(\widetilde{C}\big)
, proving (
3
).

In order to show (
4
), suppose there exists some
w_{i}\in\mathfrak{D}
and nonnegative
s_{i}
for each

such that

\partial\big(y^{(k)}_{i}+(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{-h_{i}}w_{i}\big)=\mathcal{U}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{n-1}x^{(k)}_{i}+\mathcal{U}^{n-1}\mathcal{V}^{n}x^{(k)}_{i-1}.

For

\displaystyle\partial\big(y^{(k+1)}_{i}+(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{-h_{i}}w_{i}\otimes x^{(1)}_{0}\big)

Proposition 7.8
.

\mathfrak{C}^{*}(k,m)
is a subcomplex of
\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{m_{i}}^{*},
where
2\leqslant m=m_{1}\leqslant..\leqslant m_{k}.
In particular,
\sum^{k}_{i=0}\mathcal{U}^{k-i}\mathcal{V}^{i}x^{(k),*}_{i}
is a generator of
(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{-1}H_{*}
.

Proof.

Simply take the dual complex of
\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{m_{i}}.
Since there is no incoming arrow to
\mathfrak{C}(k,m)
,
\mathfrak{C}^{*}(k,m)
is a subcomplex of
\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{m_{i}}^{*}.
It is also easy to verify that

∎

Proof of Proposition
7.2
.

Consider the complex
C^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{n_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{m_{i}}^{*}
. We fix a basis for
\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{n_{i}}
according to Proposition
7.5
. Similarly fix a basis for
\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{m_{i}}
and take the dual basis. This gives a basis for
C^{\prime}
. We will suppress the
(k)
from upper indices since both saw-edge complexes have length
k
.

Suppose
f
is a maximal self-local equivalence of
C^{\prime}
. In particular
f
is
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
-equivariant, preserves bigradings and maps one generator of
(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{-1}H_{*}
to another. Observe that there is no vertical arrow in
C^{\prime}
, therefore all generators in
C^{\prime}_{\mathrm{conn}}
survive into
H_{\mathrm{vert}}(C^{\prime}_{\mathrm{conn}})
. We only need to show
C^{\prime}_{\mathrm{conn}}=
im
f
contains more than one generators of homology.

Recall that for each
y_{i}\in\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{n_{i}},i=1,...,k
, there exists some
w_{i}\in\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{n_{i}}
, such that

\partial\big(y_{i}+(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{-h_{i}}w_{i}\big)=\mathcal{U}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{n-1}x_{i}+\mathcal{U}^{n-1}\mathcal{V}^{n}x_{i-1},

where
h_{i}
is a nonnegative integer.
Define

\widetilde{y}_{i}:=w_{i}+(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{h_{i}}y_{i},

and let

h:=
max
\{h_{i}\colon i=1,...,k\}.

Set

\displaystyle\alpha=\sum^{k}_{i=1}(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{h-h_{i}}\widetilde{y_{i}}\otimes y_{i}^{*}+(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{h+n-m}\sum^{k}_{i=0}x_{i}\otimes x^{*}_{i},

\displaystyle\beta=\sum^{k}_{i=0}\mathcal{U}^{k-i}\mathcal{V}^{i}x_{0}\otimes x^{*}_{i}.

We want to point out that
\alpha
is an actual generator of the chain complex, meaning
\alpha
contains a non-trivial component with no
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
decoration. This fact turns out to be necessary in order for the proof to work. On the other hand,
\beta
is not an actual generator of the chain complex.

We compute the bigradings of
\alpha
and
\beta
:

\displaystyle\gr(\alpha)=(-2(m+h-n),-2(m+h-n)),

\displaystyle\gr(\beta)=\gr(x_{0})=(-2k,0).

Since tensoring the complex
C^{\prime}
by
C_{m}+C_{m}^{*}
doesn’t change its local equivalence type, we can assume that
k
is large enough such that
-2k<-2(m+h-n)
.

In fact,
\alpha
and
\beta
are both generators of
(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{-1}H_{*}
. Here we slightly abuse the notation: when we refer to an element in
C^{\prime}
we actually mean its counterpart in
C^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}]}\mathbb{F}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V},(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{-1}].

It is a simple computation that both
\alpha
and
\beta
are in ker
\partial
. To show they are not in the image, first assume
\partial\big(\sum z\otimes r\big)=\beta
, where each
z\in\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{n_{i}}
and each
r\in\sum_{i=1}^{k}C_{m_{i}}^{*}
. Non-trivial components of
\partial\big(\sum z\otimes r\big)
are either of the form
(\partial z)\otimes r
or
z\otimes(\partial r)
. Consider the term
\mathcal{U}^{k-i}\mathcal{V}^{i}x_{0}\otimes x^{*}_{i}
in
\beta
. Since each
x_{i}
is in ker
\partial
,
x_{i}^{*}
never occurs in the image of
\partial
as a non-trivial component. Hence
\mathcal{U}^{k-i}\mathcal{V}^{i}x_{0}\otimes x^{*}_{i}
is of the form
\sum(\partial z)\otimes r
. It follows that
x_{0}
has to be in the image, a contradiction. Supposing
\partial\big(\sum z\otimes r\big)=\alpha
, similarly, each term
(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{h+n-m}\sum^{k}_{i=0}x_{i}\otimes x^{*}_{i}
in
\alpha
is of the form
\sum(\partial z)\otimes r
. Since
y_{i}
never occurs in the image as a non-trivial component, each term
(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{h-h_{i}}\widetilde{y_{i}}\otimes y_{i}^{*}
is of the form
\sum z\otimes(\partial r).
A brief examination shows that the only suitable elements for
z\otimes r
are
\widetilde{y_{i}}\otimes x_{j}^{*}
. However, there does not exist any combination of
\partial\big(\widetilde{y_{i}}\otimes x_{j}^{*}\big)
equal to
\alpha
. This proves that both
\alpha
and
\beta
generate
(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{-1}H_{*}
.

According to the property that self-local equivalence
f
maps one generator of
(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})^{-1}H_{*}
to another, we know
f(\alpha)
and
f(\beta)
are both non-trivial in
C^{\prime}_{\mathrm{conn}}
. However, since
f
preserves bigradings,
f(\alpha)\neq f(\beta)
. Moreover, there does not exist any positive integers
s,t
such that
f(\alpha)=\mathcal{U}^{s}\mathcal{V}^{t}f(\beta)
or
f(\beta)=\mathcal{U}^{s}\mathcal{V}^{t}f(\alpha)
. We conclude that
C^{\prime}_{\mathrm{conn}}
contains at least
2
generators of homology.

∎

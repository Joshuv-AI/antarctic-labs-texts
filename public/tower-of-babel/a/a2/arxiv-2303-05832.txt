Well-posedness of the martingale problem for super-Brownian motion with interactive branching Journal: Stochastic Processes and their Applications
Lina Ji, Jie Xiong, Xu Yang

Abstract
In this paper a martingale problem for super-Brownian motion with interactive branching is derived. The uniqueness of the solution to the martingale problem is obtained by using the pathwise uniqueness of the solution to a corresponding system of SPDEs with proper boundary conditions. The existence of the solution to the martingale problem and the local Hölder continuity of the density process are also studied.

Introduction and main results

Let
M(\mathbb{R})
be the collection of all finite Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}
endowed with the weak convergence topology. Let
C_{b}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
be the set of all bounded continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
with continuous bounded derivatives up to
n
th order.
We consider a continuous
M(\mathbb{R})
-valued process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfying the martingale problem (MP): for
\phi\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}),
the process

\displaystyle M_{t}(\phi)\equiv\left<X_{t},\phi\right>-\left<X_{0},\phi\right>-\int^{t}_{0}\left<X_{s},\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}\right>ds

(1.1)

is a continuous martingale with quadratic variation process
(\left<M(\phi)\right>_{t})_{t\geq 0}
given by

\displaystyle\left<M(\phi)\right>_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left<X_{s},{\gamma}(\mu_{s},\cdot)\phi^{2}\right>ds,

(1.2)

where
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the density process of
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0},
and
\gamma
is the interacting branching rate depending on
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. The notation
\left<\nu,\phi\right>
denotes the integral of the function
\phi
with respect to the measure
\nu.
In this paper we always assume that
X_{0}(dx)=\mu_{0}(x)dx
with
\mu_{0}\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})^{+},
where
C_{c}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
is the collection of nonnegative continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support. Obviously,
\mu_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
with
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
being the set of nonnegative functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)dx<\infty.
When
\gamma
is a constant, the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a classical super-Brownian motion. In this case the well-posedness of the MP (
1.1
,
1.2
) was established using the nonlinear partial differential equation satisfied by its log-Laplace transform, which was obtained by Watanabe
[
22
]
. Moreover, a new approach for the well-posedness of the MP was suggested by Xiong
[
26
]
, in which a relationship between the classical super-Brownian motion and a stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE) satisfied by its corresponding distribution-function-valued process was established. The weak uniqueness of the solution to the MP was also obtained by the strong uniqueness of the solution to the corresponding SPDE in
[
26
]
. See He et al.
[
8
]
for the case of super-Lévy process.

Superprocesses with interaction are natural to consider, since for many species branching and spatial motion depend on the population density. When the spatial motion is interactive, the well-posedness of the martingale problem was studied by Donnelley and Kurtz
[
3
]
, see also Perkins
[
17
, Theorem V.5.1]
and Li et al.
[
14
]
. Uniqueness for the historical superprocesses with certain interaction was investigated by Perkins
[
17
]
. Furthermore, the superprocesses with interactive immigration was studied in
[
1
,
7
,
20
]
, see also Li
[
13
, Section 10]
. The well-posedness of the martingale problem for the interactive immigration process was solved by Mytnik and Xiong
[
16
]
. See also
[
27
]
for the well-posedness of the martingale problem for a superprocess with location-dependent branching, interactive immigration mechanism and spatial motion.

However, the hard case for the superprocess with interactive branching was rarely investigated. We are interested in the case of
\gamma(\mu_{s},x)=\gamma(\mu_{s}(x)),
i.e., the interactive branching mechanism depending on the density process
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0},
where the well-posedness of the MP (
1.1
,
1.2
) is still an
open problem
. The weak uniqueness of the solution to the MP (
1.1
,
1.2
) is very difficult to prove. As a first step, throughout this paper we assume that
\gamma
satisfies the following condition:

Condition 1.1
.

Let integer
n\geq 0
and
-\infty=a_{0}<\cdots<a_{n+1}=\infty
be fixed. For any
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
, let

\displaystyle{\gamma}(f,x)=\begin{cases}g_{i}^{2}(f(a_{i+1})),&a_{i}\leq x<a_{i+1},\ i=0,\cdots,n-1,\\
g_{n}^{2}\left(\int_{a_{n}}^{\infty}f(y)dy\right),&a_{n}\leq x<\infty,\end{cases}

where
g_{i},\ i=0,\cdots,n
are continuous bounded functions from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
\mathbb{R}_{+}.

The existence and local Hölder continuity of the density process
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
are investigated in this paper, where
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a weak solution to the following SPDE:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mu_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta\mu_{t}(x)+\sqrt{\mu_{t}(x)\gamma(\mu_{t},x)}\dot{W}_{tx},

(1.3)

\Delta
denotes the one-dimensional Laplacian operator and
\dot{W}_{tx}
is the formal derivative of a white noise random measure. Let
C(\mathbb{R})^{+}
be the collection of nonnegative continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
. The notation
\langle f,g\rangle
denotes
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)g(x)dx
if it exists. The
C(\mathbb{R})^{+}
-valued process
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a weak solution of the SPDE (
1.3
) in the following sense: for any
\phi\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}),
we have

\displaystyle\langle\mu_{t},\phi\rangle\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\langle\mu_{0},\phi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mu_{s},\phi^{\prime\prime}\rangle ds

(1.4)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{\mu_{s}(x)\gamma(\mu_{s},x)}\phi(x)W(ds,dx),\quad t\geq 0

almost surely, where
W(ds,dx)
is a time-space Gaussian white noise random measure with the Lebesgue measure
dsdx
as its intensity. The existence of the solution to the MP (
1.1
,
1.2
) is given by showing the existence of the solution to (
1.3

1.1
,
1.2
) in Theorem
1.4
below. The main idea is to relate the MP to a system of SPDEs, which is satisfied by a sequence of corresponding distribution-function-valued processes on intervals. The weak uniqueness of solution to the MP follows from the pathwise uniqueness of the solution to the system of SPDEs, see Section 3. Throughout this paper we always assume that all random variables defined on the same filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}_{t},\mathbb{P})
without special explanation. Let
\mathbb{E}
be the corresponding expectation.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a solution to (

we have
\mathbb{E}[\langle\mu_{t},1\rangle]=\langle\mu_{0},1\rangle.

Proof.

By (

\displaystyle\langle\mu_{t},1\rangle=\langle\mu_{0},1\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{\mu_{s}(x)\gamma(\mu_{s},x)}W(ds,dx).

For each
n\geq 1
we define stopping time
\tau_{n}
by
\tau_{n}=\inf\{t\geq 0:\langle\mu_{t},1\rangle\geq n\}.
For
T>0,
it follows from the continuity of

that
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\langle\mu_{t},1\rangle<\infty
almost surely, which implies
\tau_{n}\rightarrow\infty
almost surely as

Further,
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{\mu_{s}(x)\gamma(\mu_{s},x)}W(ds,dx)

is a martingale (see, e.g.,
[
9

), since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{s}(x)\gamma(\mu_{s},x)dsdx\right]\leq K\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\langle\mu_{s},1\rangle ds\right]\leq Ktn.

It then implies that
\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mu_{t\wedge\tau_{n}},1\rangle\right]=\langle\mu_{0},1\rangle.
Further,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle\mu_{t\wedge\tau_{n}},1\rangle^{2}]\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle 2\langle\mu_{0},1\rangle^{2}+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{\mu_{s}(x)\gamma(\mu_{s},x)}W(ds,dx)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle 2\langle\mu_{0},1\rangle^{2}+K\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\langle\mu_{s\wedge\tau_{n}},1\rangle]ds

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle K[\langle\mu_{0},1\rangle^{2}+t\langle\mu_{0},1\rangle],

By

\mathbb{E}[\langle\mu_{t},1\rangle]=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mu_{t\wedge\tau_{n}},1\rangle\right]=\langle\mu_{0},1\rangle.
The proof ends here.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfy the MP (
1.1
,
1.2
) and
T>0
be fixed. Then for any

Proof.

For each
n\geq 1,
we define stopping time
\kappa_{n}=\inf\{t\geq 0:\left<X_{t},1\right>^{2p}\geq n\}.

Note that

almost surely because of the continuity of

almost surely. By the MP (

\displaystyle\left<X_{t},1\right>=\left<X_{0},1\right>+M_{t}(1).

(2.4)

By Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality, Hölder’s inequality and

for

one can see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\kappa_{n}}|M_{t}(1)|^{2p}\right]\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle K\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T\wedge\kappa_{n}}\left<X_{s},\gamma(\mu_{s},\cdot)\right>ds\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle K+K\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\kappa_{n}}\left<X_{s},1\right>^{2p}ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle K+K\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq(s\wedge\kappa_{n})}\left<X_{t},1\right>^{2p}\right]ds,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\kappa_{n}}\left<X_{t},1\right>^{2p}\right]\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle K+\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\kappa_{n}}|M_{t}(1)|^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle K+K\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq(s\wedge\kappa_{n})}\left<X_{t},1\right>^{2p}\right]ds,

which implies that

Lemma 4.20

\displaystyle\left<u_{t},\phi\right>\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\left<u_{0},\phi\right>+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\big[\left<u_{s},\phi^{\prime\prime}\right>+u_{s}(0)\phi^{\prime}(0)-\phi(0)\nabla u_{s}(0)]ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\nabla u_{s}(\infty))\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{\{z\leq u_{s}(x)\}}\phi(x)dx\Big]W(ds,dz).

Proof.

We take
h_{k}(x):=\int_{0}^{kx}\Phi(z)dz,
where
\Phi\in C_{c}^{2}(0,\infty)
,
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(x)dx=1
and
0\leq\Phi\leq 2.
Simliar with the proof of Lemma
4.20
, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\left<f,h_{k}^{\prime}\right>\rightarrow f(0)\qquad\qquad\left<f,h_{k}^{\prime\prime}\right>\rightarrow-f^{\prime}(0)

as
k\rightarrow\infty,
where
f\in C_{b}[0,\infty)
with
f^{\prime}(0)
existing and
f(\infty)=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}f(x)=0.
The following proof is similar with that of Lemma
4.21
, we omit it here.
∎

Next we consider the following equation:

\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{t}(x)&=u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\Delta u_{s}(x)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{u_{s}(x)}G_{s}W(ds,dz),\quad x\geq 0,\\
u_{t}(0)&=0,\end{split}

(4.19)

where
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a bounded continuous process, and
W(ds,dz)
is a time-space Gaussian white noise on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with intensity
dsdz
. Further, we assume (
4.18
) holds for the above equation. The
[0,\infty)
-distribution-function-valued process
(u_{t})_{t\geq 0}
solving (
4.19
) will be understood in the following form: for each
\phi\in C_{b}^{2}[0,\infty)
with
\phi(0)=\phi(\infty)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left<u_{t},\phi\right>\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\left<u_{0},\phi\right>+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left<u_{s},\phi^{\prime\prime}\right>ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{\{z\leq u_{s}(x)\}}\phi(x)dx\right]G_{s}W(ds,dz),\quad t\geq 0

almost surely. It is easy to see that
t{\rightarrow}\left<u_{t},\phi\right>
is continuous almost surely for each
\phi\in C_{b}^{2}[0,\infty).
The pathwise uniqueness of the solution to the general version of (
4.19
) is considered in Xiong and Yang
[
29
, Theorem 1.9]
. We give a brief proof below, since that paper has not been formally published yet.

Lemma 4.23
.

Let
(u_{t}^{i})_{t\geq 0},i=1,2
be two
[0,\infty)
-distribution-function-valued solutions to (
4.19
) with the same initial value satisfying (
4.18
). Then
\mathbb{P}\{u_{t}^{1}(x)=u_{t}^{2}(x)\ \text{for\ all}\ t,x\geq 0\}=1.

Proof.

For each
k\geq 1
we define
a_{k}=\exp\{-k(k+1)/2\}.
Then
a_{k}\rightarrow\infty
and
\int_{a_{k}}^{a_{k-1}}z^{-1}dz=k.
Let
x\rightarrow\psi_{k}(x)
be a positive continuous function supported by
(a_{k},a_{k-1})
such that
\int_{a_{k}}^{a_{k-1}}\psi_{k}(x)dx=1
and
\psi_{k}(x)\leq(2kx)^{-1}
for every
x>0.
For
k\geq 1
let

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(z)=\int_{0}^{|z|}dy\int_{0}^{y}\psi_{k}(x)dx,\quad z\in\mathbb{R}.

It is easy to see that
|\phi_{k}^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1
and
0\leq|z|\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(z)=|z|\psi_{k}(|z|)\leq 2k^{-1}
for
z\in\mathbb{R}.
Moreover, we have
\phi_{k}(z)\rightarrow|z|
increasingly as
k\rightarrow\infty.

Recall that
(u_{t}^{1})_{t\geq 0}
and
(u_{t}^{2})_{t\geq 0}
are two solutions to (
4.19
) with the same initial value. Let
q_{t}^{x}(y)=p_{t}(x,y)-p_{t}(-x,y).
It follows that for
i=1,2,

\displaystyle\left<u_{t}^{i},q_{\delta}^{x}\right>\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\left<u_{0},q_{\delta}^{x}\right>+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\Delta_{x}(\left<u_{s}^{i},q_{\delta}^{x}\right>)ds

(4.20)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{\{z\leq u_{s}^{i}(y)\}}q_{\delta}^{x}(y)dy\right]G_{s}W(ds,dz),

where
\Delta_{x}
is the second order spatial differential operator with respect to the variable
x
. For
n\geq 1,
we define the stopping time

\displaystyle\tau_{n}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:\sup_{x\geq 0}|u_{t}^{1}(x)|+\sup_{x\geq 0}|u_{t}^{2}(x)|\geq n\}.

Then
\tau_{n}\rightarrow\infty
almost surely as
n\rightarrow\infty
by (
4.18
). Let
v_{t}(x)=u_{t}^{1}(x)-u_{t}^{2}(x)
and
v_{t}^{\delta}(x)=\left<v_{t},q_{\delta}^{x}\right>.
From (
4.20
) it follows that

\displaystyle v_{t}^{\delta}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\Delta_{x}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}M_{s}^{\delta}(x,z)W(ds,dz),

where

\displaystyle M_{s}^{\delta}(x,z)=G_{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1_{\{z\leq u_{s}^{1}(y)\}}-1_{\{z\leq u_{s}^{2}(y)\}})q_{\delta}^{x}(y)dy.

It then follows from Itô’s formula that

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(v_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{\delta}(x))\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\phi_{k}^{\prime}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))\Delta_{x}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))|M_{s}^{\delta}(x,z)|^{2}dsdz

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}^{\prime}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))M_{s}^{\delta}(x,z)W(ds,dz).

Recall that
J(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-|y|}\rho(x-y)dy
satisfying (
2.55
). It leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)\mathbb{E}[\phi_{k}(v_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}^{\delta}(x))]dx

(4.21)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)\phi_{k}^{\prime}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))\Delta_{x}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))dxds\right]

(4.22)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))|M_{s}^{\delta}(x,z)|^{2}dsdzdx\right]

(4.23)

\displaystyle\quad=:\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}I_{1,k}^{\delta}(s)+I_{2,k}^{\delta}(s)\Big)ds\right].

(4.24)

By integration by parts and (
2.55
), we have

\displaystyle I_{1,k}^{\delta}(s)\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta_{x}(\phi_{k}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x)))J(x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))|\nabla_{x}v_{s}^{\delta}(x)|^{2}J(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta_{x}(\phi_{k}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))J(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{k}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))J^{\prime\prime}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle K\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{k}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))J(x)dx.

Recall that
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is bounded. Then we have

\displaystyle I_{2,k}^{\delta}(s)\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(v_{s}^{\delta}(x))\left<|v_{s}^{\delta}|,q_{\delta}^{x}\right>dx.

Letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
in (
4.21
) and using dominated convergence we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)\mathbb{E}[\phi_{k}(v_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}(x))]dx

\displaystyle\quad\leq K\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[\phi_{k}(v_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}(x))]J(x)dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad+K\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\big[\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(v_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}(x))|v_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}(x)|\big]J(x)dx.

Recall that
0\leq|y|\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(y)\leq 2/k
for all
y\in\mathbb{R},
letting
k\rightarrow\infty
in the above inequality we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)\mathbb{E}[|v_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}(x)|]dx\leq K\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)\mathbb{E}[|v_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}(x)|]dx,

which implies that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)\mathbb{E}[|v_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}(x)|]dx=0
. Letting
n\rightarrow\infty,
we obtain that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)\mathbb{E}[|v_{t}(x)|]dx=0
by Fatou’s lemma. Note that
t\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)u_{t}^{i}(x)dx
is continuous almost surely for
i=1,2
. Then
\mathbb{P}\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)|v_{t}(x)|dx=0,\ \text{for\ all}\ t\geq 0\}=1.
The result follows since
(u_{t}^{1})_{t\geq 0}
and
(u_{t}^{2})_{t\geq 0}
are two
[0,\infty)
-dsitribution-function-valued processes.
∎

Now we consider the following equation:

\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{t}(x)&=u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\Delta u_{s}(x)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{u_{s}(x)}\tilde{G}_{s}W(ds,dz),\quad x\in[0,1],\\

where
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(\tilde{G}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
are continuous processes, and
(\tilde{G}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is bounded. Moreover,
W(ds,dz)
is a time-space Gaussian white noise on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with intensity
dsdz
. The
[0,1]
-distribution-function-valued process
(u_{t}(x))_{t\geq 0,x\in[0,1]}
solving (
4.25
) will be understood in the following form: for each
\phi\in C_{b}^{2}[0,1]
with
\phi(0)=\phi^{\prime}(1)=0,
we have

\displaystyle\left<u_{t},\phi\right>\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\left<u_{0},\phi\right>+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left<u_{s},\phi^{\prime\prime}\right>+\phi(1)\mu_{s}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{\{z\leq u_{s}(x)\}}\phi(x)dx\right]\tilde{G}_{s}W(ds,dz),\quad t\geq 0

almost surely. The pathwise uniqueness of the solution to the general version of (

[
29
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Lemma 4.24
.

Let
(u_{t}^{i}(x))_{t\geq 0,x\in[0,1]},i=1,2
be two
[0,1]
-distribution-function-valued solutions to (
4.25
) with the same initial value satisfying (
4.16
). Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{u_{t}^{1}(x)=u_{t}^{2}(x)\ \text{for all}\ t\geq 0,x\in[0,1]\right\}=1.

Proof.

The conclusion can be justified by using essentially the same argument as that in the proof of Lemma
4.23

\displaystyle q_{t}^{x}(y)\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}[p_{t}(4k+x-y)-p_{t}(4k-x-y)]

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}[p_{t}(2k+x-y)-p_{t}(2k-x-y)].

We omit the details. ∎

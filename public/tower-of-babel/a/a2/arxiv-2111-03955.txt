Cauchy Problem for Incompressible Neo-Hookean materials

Abstract
In this paper we consider the Cauchy problem for neo-Hookean incompressible elasticity in spatial dimension d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . The Cauchy problem can be formulated in terms of maps x ⁡ ( t , ⋅ ) : ℝ ξ d → ℝ x d x(t,\cdot):{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\xi}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{x} with domain a reference space ℝ ξ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\xi} , and with values in space ℝ x d {\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{x} . Initial data consists of initial deformation ϕ ⁡ ( ξ ) = x ⁡ ( 0 , ξ ) \phi(\xi)=x(0,\xi) and velocity ψ ⁡ ( ξ ) = ∂ x ⁡ ( t , ξ ) / ∂ t | t = 0 \psi(\xi)=\partial x(t,\xi)/\partial t|_{t=0} , which we assume are in Sobolev spaces ( ϕ , ψ ) ∈ H s + 1 ​ ( ℝ d ) × H s ​ ( ℝ d ) (\phi,\psi)\in H^{s+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}) . If s > s c ​ r ​ i ​ t = d / 2 + 1 s>s_{crit}=d/2+1 , well-posedness is well-known. We are here interested primarily in the low regularity case, s ≤ s c ​ r ​ i ​ t s\leq s_{crit} . For d = 2 , 3 d=2,3 , we prove existence and uniqueness for s 0 < s ≤ s c ​ r ​ i ​ t s_{0}<s\leq s_{crit} , and we can prove well-posedness, but for a smaller range, s 1 < s ≤ s c ​ r ​ i ​ t s_{1}<s\leq s_{crit} , if d = 2 d=2 , s 0 = 7 4 , s 1 = 7 4 + 65 − 7 8 \displaystyle,\quad s_{0}=\frac{7}{4},\quad s_{1}=\tfrac{7}{4}+\tfrac{\sqrt{65}-7}{8} if d = 3 d=3 , s 0 = 2 , s 1 = 1 + 3 2 \displaystyle,\quad s_{0}=2,\quad s_{1}=1+\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{2}} We consider the initial deformations of the form x ⁡ ( 0 , ξ ) = A ​ ξ + φ ⁡ ( ξ ) x(0,\xi)=A\xi+\varphi(\xi) , where A A is a constant S ​ L ​ ( d , ℝ ) SL(d,{\mathbb{R}}) matrix. For the full range (in s s ) results, as indicated above, we need additional Hölder regularity assumptions on certain combinations of second order derivatives of φ \varphi . A key observation in the proof is that the equations of evolution for the vorticities decomposes into a first-order hyperbolic system, for which a Strichartz estimate holds, and a coupled transport system. Continuous dependence on initial data is proved using an argument inspired by Bona and Smith, and Kato and Lai, with a modification based on new estimates for Riesz potentials. \frac{1}{2}\frac{d\hfill}{dt}\,\int\sum|q^{i}_{ab}|^{2}\,dx\leq\|\nabla v(t)\|_{\infty}\,\int\sum|q^{i}_{ab}|^{2}\,dx\,.

Therefore, if all
q^{i}_{ab}=0
at
t=0
, then
q^{i}_{ab}
will vanish for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Similarly, if
v(t),v_{a}(t)
is a solution of (
1.10
), (
1.11
), and (
1.12
), for which (
2.1
) is true,
consider the solution of the ODE system
\dot{x}=v(t,x)
with the initial condition

x(0,\xi)=A\xi+\varphi(\xi)
. Consider the quantity
q^{i}_{a}=v^{i}_{a}-\partial x^{i}/\partial\xi^{a}
and compute
its full time derivative:

(\partial_{t}+v^{\ell}\partial_{\ell})q^{i}_{a}=(\partial_{t}+v^{\ell}\partial_{\ell})v^{i}_{a}-\frac{\partial^{2}x^{i}}{\partial t\partial\xi^{a}}\,.

The first term on the right equals
v^{k}_{a}\partial_{k}v^{i}
by equation (
1.11
), while

\frac{\partial^{2}x^{i}}{\partial t\partial\xi^{a}}=\frac{\partial v^{i}}{\partial\xi^{a}}=\frac{\partial x^{k}}{\partial\xi^{a}}\,\partial_{k}v^{i}\,.

Thus,

(\partial_{t}+v^{\ell}\partial_{\ell})q^{i}_{a}=q^{k}_{a}\partial_{k}v^{i}\,.

As with the quantity
q^{i}_{ab}
, we obtain the inequality

\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{d\hfill}{dt}\,\int\sum|q^{i}_{a}|^{2}\,dx\leq\|\nabla v(t)\|_{\infty}\,\int\sum|q^{i}_{a}|^{2}\,dx\,.

Thus, if all
q^{i}_{a}
are
0
at
t=0
, they remain
0
for
t\in[0,T]
.

We emphasize that the results in this subsection do not require
s>d/2+1
.

2.2
The Cauchy problem

In the Euler picture, we assume that the deformation gradient
(v^{i}_{a})
is split as in (
1.16
). Thus, we fix an
SL(d,{\mathbb{R}})
matrix
A=(A^{i}_{a})
and define
d
vectorfields
u_{1},\dots,u_{d}
with the components
u^{i}_{a}(t,x)=v^{i}_{a}(t,x)-A^{i}_{a}
,
i=1,\dots,d
. Equations (
1.10
), (
1.11
), and (
1.12
) now look as follows:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v^{i}+v^{j}\,\partial_{j}v^{i}-u^{k}_{b}\,\partial_{k}u^{i}_{b}-A^{k}_{b}\,\partial_{k}u^{i}_{b}+\nabla^{i}p=0\,,

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{i}_{a}+v^{k}\,\partial_{k}u^{i}_{a}-u^{k}_{a}\,\partial_{k}v^{i}-A^{k}_{a}\,\partial_{k}v^{i}=0\,,

(2.3b)

\displaystyle\hbox{div}\,v=0,\quad\hbox{div}\,u_{a}=0\,.

(2.3c)

What we do next in this paper does not depend on the particular choice of matrix
A
. The important thing is that the terms containing the elements of
A
disappear in the derivation of energy estimates.
Here is a typical calculation showing this:

\int-A^{k}_{b}\,\partial_{k}u^{i}_{b}\,v^{i}-A^{k}_{a}\,\partial_{k}v^{i}\,u^{i}_{a}\;dx=0\,,

Because of that, we choose the simplest

, the identity matrix, and work with the resulting equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v^{i}+v^{j}\,\partial_{j}v^{i}-u^{k}_{b}\,\partial_{k}u^{i}_{b}-\partial_{b}u^{i}_{b}+\nabla^{i}p=0\,,

(2.4a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{i}_{a}+v^{k}\,\partial_{k}u^{i}_{a}-u^{k}_{a}\,\partial_{k}v^{i}-\partial_{a}v^{i}=0\,,

(2.4b)

\displaystyle\hbox{div}\,v=0,\quad\hbox{div}\,u_{a}=0\,.

(2.4c)

We shall use
u
to denote the whole collection
u_{1},\dots,u_{d}
, and any norm of
u
will be understood as the maximum over
a
of the norm of
u_{a}
.
We abbreviate
v(t)
for
v(t,\cdot)
etc. Recall that
V(t)=(v(t),u(t))
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
d\geq 2
.
Let
s
be a real number greater than
1+d/2
. Assume that the initial conditions
v(0),u(0)
for equations (
2.4a
),
(
2.4b
), and (

H^{s}_{\sigma}
. Then there exists a local in time solution of those equations such that

v,u\in C([0,T]\to H^{s}_{\sigma})\,.

(2.5)

The solution is unique and depends continuously on the initial conditions.
The lifespan,
T
, of the solution is determined by the
H^{s}_{\sigma}
-norms of the initial conditions and is characterized by the condition

\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla V(\tau)\|_{\infty}\,d\tau<\infty\,,\quad\forall t<T\,.

(2.6)

The general plan of the proof will be the same as in the proof of the corresponding result for the Euler equations
(describing an ideal fluid) by Kato and Lai,
[
18
]
, complemented by some nice observations from
[
10
]
. We will sketch it anyway to emphasize certain points that will be used later.
Denote the orthogonal
projection
H^{s}\to H^{s}_{\sigma}
by

{\cal P}
. In the Fourier space,

\widehat{\left({\cal P}w\right)^{i}}(k)=\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{|k|^{2}}\right){\hat{w}}^{j}(k)\,.

Since Leray’s work
[
21
]
, projection
of the equations onto the space of divergence-free vectors is standard in hydrodynamics, and we use it as well to get rid of the pressure term. Thus, we deal with the following equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v^{i}+{\cal P}\left\{v^{j}\,\partial_{j}v^{i}-u^{k}_{b}\,\partial_{k}u^{i}_{b}-\partial_{b}u^{i}_{b}\right\}=0\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{i}_{a}+v^{k}\,\partial_{k}u^{i}_{a}-u^{k}_{a}\,\partial_{k}v^{i}-\partial_{a}v^{i}=0\,.

(2.8)

We shall not need equations (
2.4c
) since
\partial_{t}\hbox{div}\,v=0
follows from equation (
2.7
) and, from (
2.8
),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hbox{div}\,u_{a}+v^{k}\,\partial_{k}\hbox{div}\,u_{a}={}

\displaystyle u^{k}_{a}\partial_{k}\hbox{div}\,v+\partial_{a}\hbox{div}\,v\,.

This shows that if equations (
2.4c
) are satisfied at
t=0

t>0
.

Let
v(0)\in H^{s}_{\sigma}
and
u_{a}(0)\in H^{s}_{\sigma}
be given. The solution of (
2.7
), (
2.8
) with these initial conditions will be obtained as a limit of approximate solutions

v^{\epsilon},u_{a}^{\epsilon}
whose Fourier transforms are supported in the ball
\{|k|\leq\epsilon^{-1}\}
, cf.
[
10
]
.

Denote by
\rho_{\epsilon}
the Friedrichs’ mollifiers
\rho_{\epsilon}(x)=\epsilon^{-d}\rho(x/\epsilon)
, where
\rho(x)
is the inverse Fourier transform
of the characteristic function of the unit ball
\{|k|\leq 1\}
. In other words,

\widehat{\rho_{\epsilon}*f}(k)=\chi_{\{|k|\leq 1/\epsilon\}}(k)\,{\hat{f}}(k)\,.

To save space, we write
\rho_{\epsilon}[f]
instead of
\rho_{\epsilon}*f
,

\rho_{\epsilon}[f](x)=\int\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)f(y)\,dy\,.

The mollifiers
\rho_{\epsilon}
have the usual properties:

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\phi\in H^{s}
,

1.

\rho_{\epsilon}[\phi]\to\phi
in
H^{s}
as
\epsilon\to 0
;

2.

for any
m\geq 0
,
\|\rho_{\epsilon}[\phi]\|_{H^{s+m}}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)^{m/2}\,\|\phi\|_{H^{s}}
. In particular, if
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

\|\rho_{\epsilon}[\phi]\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\epsilon}\,\|\phi\|_{H^{s}}\,,

(2.9)

3.

for
m\geq 0
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

\|\rho_{\epsilon}[\phi]-\phi\|_{H^{s-m}}\leq 2^{-m/2}\,\epsilon^{m}\,\|\phi\|_{H^{s}}\,.

(2.10)

4.

If
\mathfrak{C}
is a compact subset of
H^{s}
, then, for all
m\geq 0
,

\|\rho_{\epsilon}[\phi]-\phi\|_{H^{s-m}}=\epsilon^{m}\,o(\epsilon)

(2.11)

uniformly in
\phi\in{\mathfrak{C}}
.

Proof.

The first claim is well-known. The second one follows from

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\epsilon}[\phi]\|_{H^{s+m}}^{2}={}

\displaystyle\int_{k\leq 1/\epsilon}(1+|k|^{2})^{s+m}\,|\hat{\phi}(k)|^{2}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}k

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1+\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)^{m}\,\int_{k\leq 1/\epsilon}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}\,|\hat{\phi}(k)|^{2}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}k\,,

where
{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}k=(2\pi)^{-d}dk
.
To prove the third and the fourth claims, observe that

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\epsilon}[\phi]-\phi\|_{H^{s-m}}^{2}={}

\displaystyle\int_{k>1/\epsilon}(1+|k|^{2})^{s-m}\,|\hat{\phi}(k)|^{2}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}k

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{k>1/\epsilon}\frac{1}{(1+|k|^{2})^{m}}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}\,|\hat{\phi}(k)|^{2}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}k

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle 2^{-m}\,\epsilon^{2m}\,\int_{k>1/\epsilon}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}\,|\hat{\phi}(k)|^{2}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}k\,.

By the Kolmogorov-Riesz compactness criterion, cf.
[
13
]
,

\int_{k>1/\epsilon}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}\,|\hat{\phi}(k)|^{2}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}k\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
, uniformly in
\phi\in{\mathfrak{C}}
, a compact subset of
H^{s}
.

∎

To solve (
2.7
),(
2.8
) we construct approximate solutions
v^{\epsilon},u_{a}^{\epsilon}
by considering
the following truncated system (cf.
[
10
]
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v^{i}+{\cal P}\,\rho_{\epsilon}\left[v^{j}\,\partial_{j}v^{i}-u^{k}_{b}\,\partial_{k}u^{i}_{b}-\partial_{b}u^{i}_{b}\right]={}

\displaystyle 0,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{i}_{a}+\rho_{\epsilon}\left[v^{k}\,\partial_{k}u^{i}_{a}-u^{k}_{a}\,\partial_{k}v^{i}-\partial_{a}v^{i}\right]={}

\displaystyle 0.

(2.13)

We let
v^{\epsilon}
and
u_{a}^{\epsilon}
solve this system with the initial conditions

v^{\epsilon}(0)=\rho_{\epsilon}\left[v(0)\right],\quad u_{a}^{\epsilon}(0)=\rho_{\epsilon}\left[u_{a}(0)\right].

(2.14)

The solutions of (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) will automatically have their Fourier transforms supported in the ball
\{|k|\leq 1/\epsilon\}
. That local in time and unique solutions exist follows from the fact that
equations (
2.12
), (
2.13
) can be viewed
as an ODE in the Hilbert space
\rho_{\epsilon}[H^{s}_{\sigma}]
,

\frac{dV}{dt}=F(V)\,,

(2.15)

with locally Lipschitz right hand side. The Lipschitz property of

in our case is not hard to verify. To avoid clutter, we shall drop the index on

when convenient.
For example, the
H^{s}
norm of
u\;\partial u-{\underline{u}}\;\partial{\underline{u}}
, where

, is estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\|u\;\partial u-{\underline{u}}\;\partial{\underline{u}}\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|u-\underline{u}\|_{H^{s}}\|\partial u\|_{H^{s}}+\|\underline{u}\|_{H^{s}}\|\partial(u-\underline{u})\|_{H^{s}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,\left(\|u\|_{H^{s}}+\|{\underline{u}}\|_{H^{s}}\right)\;\|u-\underline{u}\|_{H^{s}}\,.

We have used that
H^{s}
is an algebra when

and that

\|\nabla w\|_{H^{s}}\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\|w\|_{H^{s}}\,

for any

By the Cauchy-Picard theorem, cf. e.g.,
[
22

, for every initial condition

(v^{\epsilon}(0),u^{\epsilon}(0))\in\rho_{\epsilon}[H^{s}_{\sigma}]
, there exists a
T_{*}(\|(v^{\epsilon}(0),u^{\epsilon}(0))\|_{\rho_{\epsilon}[H^{s}_{\sigma}]},\epsilon)>0
and a unique solution
(v^{\epsilon}(t),u^{\epsilon}(t))
of
the problem (
2.12
), (
2.13
), (
2.14
) on the time interval
(-T_{*},T_{*})
, such that
(v^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon})\in C([-T,T]\to\rho_{\epsilon}[H^{s}_{\sigma}])
for every

. The energy estimates discussed in the next section will show that

can be chosen the same for all
\epsilon>0
.

2.3
Energy estimates

For the basic
L^{2}
estimate, let
(v^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon})
be a solution of (
2.12
), (
2.13
). Multiply (
2.12
) by
{v^{\epsilon}}
, multiply (
2.13
) by
u^{\epsilon}_{a}
, sum over
a
and integrate over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to obtain (after integration by parts and cancellations due to the divergence-free nature of
v^{\epsilon}
and
u^{\epsilon}_{a}
):

\frac{d\hfill}{dt}\,\int|v^{\epsilon}(t)|^{2}+|u^{\epsilon}(t)|^{2}\,dx=0\,

(unless otherwise specified, the expressions such as
|u|^{2}
are understood as
\sum_{a}u_{a}\cdot u_{a}
).
Thus,

\int|v^{\epsilon}(t)|^{2}+|u^{\epsilon}(t)|^{2}\,dx=\int|v^{\epsilon}(0)|^{2}+|u^{\epsilon}(0)|^{2}\,dx\leq\int|v(0)|^{2}+|u(0)|^{2}\,dx\,.

(2.16)

The higher energy estimates are standard. We present them in a schematic form. Schematically, the system
(
2.12
), (
2.13
) is

\partial_{t}V+{\cal P}\rho_{\epsilon}[V\cdot\nabla V]=0\,.

(2.17)

Let
r
be any positive real number and note that
J^{r}=(1-\Delta)^{r/2}
commutes with

{\cal P}
, with
\rho_{\epsilon}*
, and with all partial derivatives. Act with

J^{r}
on (
2.17
) and write the result in the form:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{J^{r}V}+{\cal P}\,\rho_{\epsilon}\left[V\cdot\nabla{J^{r}V}\right]=-{\cal P}\,\rho_{\epsilon}\left[J^{r}(V\cdot\nabla V)-V\cdot\nabla{J^{r}V}\right]\,.

(2.18)

It follows that

\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{d\hfill}{dt}\,\|J^{r}V(t)\|^{2}\leq\|J^{r}(V\cdot\nabla V)-V\cdot\nabla{J^{r}V}\|\;\|J^{r}V(t)\|\,.

(2.19)

By
the Kato-Ponce commutator
estimate
[
19
]
,

\|J^{r}(V\cdot\nabla V)-V\cdot\nabla{J^{r}V}\|\lesssim\|\nabla V(t)\|_{\infty}\;\|J^{r}V(t)\|\,.

(2.20)

Then it follows from (
2.19
) that

\frac{d\hfill}{dt}\,\|V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{r}}^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\infty}\;\|V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{r}}^{2}\,.

(2.21)

This implies (with
t>0
)

\|V^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{r}}\leq\|V(0)\|^{2}_{H^{r}}\;\exp\left(c(r,d)\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla V^{\epsilon}(\tau)\|_{\infty}\,d\tau\right),

(2.22)

with the constant
c(r,d)
independent of
\epsilon
.

If we take
r=s>d/2+1
, then, by Sobolev embedding,
\|\nabla V^{\epsilon}(\tau)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|V^{\epsilon}(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}
, and inequality (
2.21
) implies

\frac{dy^{\epsilon}(t)}{dt}\leq C(s,d)\,y^{\epsilon}(t)^{3/2}\,,

for
y^{\epsilon}(t)=\|V^{\epsilon}(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
. The
\epsilon
-independent estimate

y^{\epsilon}(t)\leq y(0)\,\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\,C(s,d)\,{y(0)^{1/2}}\,t\right)^{-2}

follows. Here we made use of the fact that with our choice of the mollifier, we have
\|\rho_{\epsilon}[f]\;\big|\;H^{s}\|\leq\|f\;\big|\;H^{s}\|
, and hence

y^{\epsilon}(0)\leq y(0)
.
If we take the initial conditions from the ball

B_{R}=\{\|V(0)\;\big|\;H^{s}\|^{2}\leq R^{2}\}
, then we can choose the common life-span

T_{*}=\frac{2}{C(s,d)\,R}\,.

(2.23)

2.4
Passage to the limit

When
\epsilon\to 0
, the approximate solutions
v^{\epsilon}
,
u^{\epsilon}
converge to the true solutions
v
,
u
of (
2.7
), (
2.8
).
Pick a
T<T_{*}
. All approximate solutions
V^{\epsilon}(t)
exist on the interval
[-T,T]
and by (
2.22
) have uniformly bounded
H^{s}
norms. Then there is a sequence
\epsilon_{n}\searrow 0
such that

converge weak-
*
in

to some
V=(v,u)
in that space. From the equations (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) we see that
(\partial_{t}v^{\epsilon}(t),\partial_{t}u^{\epsilon}(t))
are uniformly bounded in

. Therefore, we can arrange that

\partial_{t}V^{\epsilon_{n}}
converge weak-
*
in

to
\partial_{t}V=(\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}u)
. In addition, taking into consideration Rellich’s compact embedding theorem and the fact that
s
is sufficiently large,
we can assume that
V^{\epsilon_{n}}
converges strongly to
V

and each first partial derivative
\partial V^{\epsilon_{n}}
converges strongly to
\partial V
in every
L^{2}([-T,T]\times B_{N}(0))

(where

) and
V^{\epsilon_{n}}
and

and

The just mentioned facts are sufficient to conclude that
(v,u)
solves equations (

), (

)
in the sense of distributions and assume the prescribed values at

. To see this, let
\eta(t,x)
be any smooth divergence free vector with compact support

) and (

) by

and integrate over

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int-v^{\epsilon}\,\partial_{t}\eta\,+\left(v^{\epsilon j}\partial_{j}v^{\epsilon i}-u^{\epsilon k}_{a}\;\partial_{k}u^{\epsilon i}_{b}-\partial_{a}u^{\epsilon i}_{b}\right)\,\rho_{\epsilon}\left[\eta^{i}\right]\,dt\,dx=\int v^{\epsilon}(0)\,\eta(0)\,dx

(2.24)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int-u^{\epsilon}_{a}\,\partial_{t}\eta+\left[v^{\epsilon k}\partial_{k}u^{\epsilon i}_{a}-u^{\epsilon k}_{a}\partial_{k}v^{\epsilon i}-\partial_{a}v^{\epsilon i}\right]\,\rho_{\epsilon}[\eta^{i}]\,dt\,dx=\int u^{\epsilon}_{a}(0)\,\eta(0)\,dx

Since the terms such as

are uniformly in

\int_{0}^{T}\int u^{\epsilon k}_{a}\,\partial_{k}u^{\epsilon i}_{b}\;\eta^{i}\,dt\,dx\to\int_{0}^{T}\int u^{k}_{a}\,\partial_{k}u^{i}_{b}\;\eta^{i}\,dt\,dx.

Thus, the limit functions

and

the integral identities

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int-v\,\partial_{t}\eta\,+\left(v^{j}\partial_{j}v^{i}-u^{k}_{a}\;\partial_{k}u^{i}_{b}-\partial_{a}u^{i}_{b}\right)\,\eta^{i}\,dt\,dx=\int v(0)\,\eta(0)\,dx

and, for

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int-u_{a}\,\partial_{t}\eta+\left[v^{k}\partial_{k}u^{i}_{a}-u^{k}_{a}\partial_{k}v^{i}-\partial_{a}v^{i}\right]\,\eta^{i}\,dt\,dx=\int u_{a}(0)\,\eta(0)\,dx

(2.27)

with any divergence free smooth
\eta

(-T,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. This shows, in particular, that

(v,u)
solve equations (
2.7
), (
2.8
) in the sense of distributions.

So far we have a solution
V

V\in L^{\infty}([-T,T]\to H^{s}_{\sigma})

{\underline{V}}=(\underline{v},\underline{u})\in L^{\infty}([-T,T]\to H^{s}_{\sigma})

, as seen from (

),(

) . The equation satisfied by the difference,

\partial_{t}{\tilde{V}}+{\cal P}V\cdot\nabla{\tilde{V}}=-{\cal P}{\tilde{V}}\cdot\nabla{\underline{V}}

The same argument as for the energy estimate gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d\hfill}{dt}\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla{\underline{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}\,\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|^{2}\,.

As long as
\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla{\underline{V}}(\tau)\|_{\infty}\,d\tau
is finite on
[0,T]
, and since
{\tilde{V}}(0)=0
,
it follows that
{\tilde{V}}(t)=0
on
[0,T]
.

Now we shall prove that the solution
(v(t),u(t))
is strongly continuous in
t
.
Recall the inequality (
2.22
). We need it with

\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla V^{\epsilon}(t^{\prime})\|_{\infty}\,dt^{\prime}\lesssim M\cdot t\,,

for all

, and

\|v^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|u^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\leq\left(\|v(0)\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|u(0)\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right)\;\exp\left(C\,M\cdot t\right)\,.

Use the lower semi-continuity of the norms on the left to obtain

\|v(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|u(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\leq\left(\|v(0)\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|u(0)\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right)\;\exp\left(C\,M\cdot t\right)\,.

Since the solution
(v(t),u(t))
is weakly continuous in
H^{s}
, it follows that it is strongly continuous from the right at

. For any
t_{0}\in[-T,T]
, if we solve (
2.7
), (
2.8
)

(v(t_{0}),u(t_{0}))
at

(v(t),u(t))
due to uniqueness. Consequently,
(v(t),u(t))
is strongly continuous in

from the right at every

. The equations are invariant under the time reversal transformation
(t,v(t),u(t))\mapsto(-t,-v(-t),-u(-t))
. Again, due to uniqueness,
this implies strong continuity of
t\mapsto(v(t),u(t))\in H^{s}
from the left. This proves the strong continuity in time of
V(t)\in H^{s}_{\sigma}
, i.e.,

V\in C([0,T]\to H^{s})
.

2.5
Revisiting energy estimates

The limit case of the inequality (
2.22
) is true, viz. for any solution satisfying the conditions of Theorem
2.1
,

\|v(t)\|_{H^{r}}^{2}+\|u(t)\|_{H^{r}}^{2}\leq\left(\|v(t_{0})\|_{H^{r}}^{2}+\|u(t_{0})\|_{H^{r}}^{2}\right)\,\exp\left(c(r,d)\,\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\nabla V(\tau)\|_{\infty}\,d\tau\right)

(2.28)

for all
-T\leq t_{0}<t\leq T
and for any
0<r\leq s
. This is not proved by passing to the limit
\epsilon\to 0
in (
2.22
) but rather by appealing to the fact (cf.
[
1
, p. 64]
) that

(J^{r}v,J^{r}u)
is the unique
C([-T,T]\to L^{2})
solution of the linear non-homogeneous hyperbolic system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{{\mathfrak{v}}}+{\cal P}\,\left[v^{j}\,\partial_{j}{\mathfrak{v}}-u^{k}_{b}\,\partial_{k}{\mathfrak{u}}_{b}-\partial_{b}{\mathfrak{u}}_{b}\right]=F^{r}\,,

(2.29)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\mathfrak{u}}_{a}+\left[v^{k}\,\partial_{k}{\mathfrak{u}}_{a}-u^{k}_{a}\,\partial_{k}{\mathfrak{v}}-\partial_{a}{\mathfrak{v}}\right]=F^{r}_{a}\,,

where

\displaystyle F^{r}

\displaystyle={\cal P}\,\left[\left(v^{j}\,\partial_{j}{J^{r}v}-J^{r}(v^{j}\,\partial_{j}v)\right)-\left(u^{k}_{b}\,\partial_{k}{J^{r}u}_{b}-J^{r}(u^{k}_{b}\;\partial_{k}u_{b})\right)\right]

(2.30)

\displaystyle F^{r}_{a}

\displaystyle={\cal P}\,\left[\left(v^{j}\,\partial_{j}{J^{r}u_{a}}-J^{r}(v^{j}\,\partial_{j}u_{a})\right)-\left(u^{k}_{a}\,\partial_{k}{J^{r}v}-J^{r}(u^{k}_{a}\;\partial_{k}v)\right)\right]\,,

and

{\mathfrak{v}}(0)=J^{r}v(0),\quad{\mathfrak{u}}_{b}(0)=J^{r}u_{b}(0)\,.

The energy estimate for the solution can be written in the form (we take
t_{0}=0
for simplicity)

\left(\|{\mathfrak{v}}(t)\|^{2}+\|{\mathfrak{u}}_{a}(t)\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(\|{\mathfrak{v}}(0)\|^{2}+\|{\mathfrak{u}}_{a}(0)\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|F^{r}(\tau)\|^{2}+\|F^{r}_{a}(\tau)\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,d\tau\,.

Substitute the true values
{\mathfrak{v}}=J^{r}v
and
{\mathfrak{u}}_{a}=J^{r}u_{a}
, apply estimates (
2.20
) to the norms in the integrand, and use Gronwall’s inequality to arrive at (
2.28
).

2.6
Continuous dependence

The proof of continuous dependence of solutions on the initial conditions will follow the strategy
of Bona and Smith
[
3
]
as has been done by Kato and Lai
[
18
]
for the Euler equations.
Take a sequence of initial conditions
V_{n}(0)\in H^{s}_{\sigma}
converging (in
H^{s}
) to
V(0)
.
Let
V_{n}(t)
and
V(t)
be the corresponding solutions
of system (
2.7
), (
2.8
).
We can choose the same interval
[0,T]
for all
V_{n}(0)
. The goal is to show that the solutions
V_{n}(t)
converge to
V(t)
in
C([0,T]\to H^{s}_{\sigma}
.

Mollify the initial conditions,
V_{n}^{\epsilon}(0)=\rho_{\epsilon}[V_{n}(0)]
and
V^{\epsilon}(0)=\rho_{\epsilon}[V(0)]
, using the same mollifier as in Lemma
2.2
. Let
V_{n}^{\epsilon}
and
V^{\epsilon}
be the corresponding solutions of (
2.7
), (
2.8
) lying in the space

C([0,T]\to H^{s+1}_{\sigma})
. As the energy estimates show, we can adjust
T
so that the solutions exist on the same time interval,
[0,T]
for all
\epsilon>0
. We have

\displaystyle\sup_{[0,T]}\|V_{n}(t)-V(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq{}

\displaystyle\sup_{[0,T]}\|V_{n}(t)-{V}^{\epsilon}_{n}(t)\|_{H^{s}}+\sup_{[0,T]}\|V(t)-{V}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s}}

(2.31)

\displaystyle+\sup_{[0,T]}\|{V}^{\epsilon}_{n}(t)-{V}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\,.

As will be shown, the first two norms on the right will be small by the choice of
\epsilon
, while the last norm, for a fixed
\epsilon
, will be small for large
n
.

We start with the first two norms on the right side of (
2.31
) and prove a slightly more general result. Consider the system (
2.7
), (
2.8
) with initial conditions

V(0)=(v(0),u(0))
from a compact subset
{\mathfrak{C}}
of
H^{s}_{\sigma}
, where
s>d/2+1
.
Mollify the initial conditions,
V^{\epsilon}(0)=(\rho_{\epsilon}[v(0)],\rho_{\epsilon}[u(0)])
, and let
V^{\epsilon}(t)
be
the corresponding solutions on
[0,T]
. The interval can be chosen uniformly for
V(0)\in{\mathfrak{C}}
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
as follows from the existence part of Theorem
2.1
.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
V(0)\in{\mathfrak{C}}
and
\epsilon\to 0
,
the family
V^{\epsilon}
is uniformly Cauchy in
C([0,T]\to H^{s}_{\sigma})
.

Proof.

Consider the difference
\tilde{V}=V^{\delta}-V^{\epsilon}
, where
0<\delta<\epsilon
. At
t=0
, the
H^{s}
norm of

{\tilde{V}}(0)
goes to
0
as
\epsilon\searrow 0
.
The difference
{\tilde{V}}(t)
satisfies, schematically, the equation

\partial_{t}{\tilde{V}}+{\cal P}\,V^{\delta}\nabla{\tilde{V}}=-{\cal P}\,{\tilde{V}}\nabla V^{\epsilon}\,.

(2.32)

First get the
L^{2}
estimate. We have

\frac{1}{2}\,\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|^{2}\leq\|\nabla V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\infty}\,\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|^{2}\,,

and therefore,

\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|^{2}\leq\|{\tilde{V}}(0)\|^{2}\,\exp\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla V^{\epsilon}(\tau)\|_{\infty}\,d\tau\,.

(2.33)

This implies

\sup_{[0,T]}\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|\lesssim\epsilon^{s}\,o(\epsilon)

(2.34)

since the integral of
\|\nabla V^{\epsilon}(\tau)\|_{\infty}
is bounded and, since
V(0)\in H^{s}
, and therefore,

\displaystyle\|{\tilde{V}}(0)\|^{2}={}

\displaystyle\|V^{\delta}(0)-V^{\epsilon}(0)\|^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{1/\epsilon<|\kappa|<1/\delta}|\hat{V}(0,\kappa)|^{2}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{1/\epsilon<|\kappa|<1/\delta}\frac{|\widehat{J^{s}V(0)}(\kappa)|^{2}}{(1+|\kappa|^{2})^{s}}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{1+\epsilon^{2}}\right)^{s}\,\int_{1/\epsilon<|\kappa|<1/\delta}|\widehat{J^{s}V(0)}(\kappa)|^{2}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa\,.

Now, turn to the
H^{s}
estimates.
Act with
J^{s}
on equation (
2.32
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}J^{s}{\tilde{V}}+{\cal P}\,V^{\delta}\nabla J^{s}{\tilde{V}}={}

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle P}\,\left(V^{\delta}\nabla J^{s}{\tilde{V}}-J^{s}(V^{\delta}\nabla{\tilde{V}})\right)

\displaystyle-{\cal P}\,\left(J^{s}\left({\tilde{V}}\nabla V^{\epsilon}\right)-{\tilde{V}}\nabla J^{s}V^{\epsilon}\right)-{\cal P}\,{\tilde{V}}\nabla J^{s}V^{\epsilon}\,.

As in the derivation of energy estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d\hfill}{dt}\;\|J^{s}{\tilde{V}}(t)\|\leq{}

\displaystyle\|V^{\delta}\nabla J^{s}{\tilde{V}}-J^{s}(V^{\delta}\nabla{\tilde{V}})\|

(2.35)

\displaystyle+\|J^{s}\left({\tilde{V}}\nabla V^{\epsilon}\right)-{\tilde{V}}\nabla J^{s}V^{\epsilon}\|+\|{\tilde{V}}\|_{\infty}\,\|\nabla J^{s}V^{\epsilon}\|

By the Kato-Ponce inequality (
2.20
),

\|V^{\delta}\nabla J^{s}{\tilde{V}}-J^{s}(V^{\delta}\nabla{\tilde{V}})\|\lesssim\|\nabla V^{\delta}(t)\|_{\infty}\,\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}+\|\nabla{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}\,\|V^{\delta}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\,,

(2.36)

and

\|J^{s}\left({\tilde{V}}\nabla V^{\epsilon}\right)-{\tilde{V}}\nabla J^{s}V^{\epsilon}\|\lesssim\|\nabla{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}\,\|V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s}}+\|\nabla V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\infty}\,\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\,.

(2.37)

Thus,

\begin{split}\frac{d\hfil}{dt}\|{{\tilde{V}}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim{}&(\|\nabla V^{\delta}(t)\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\infty})\,\|{{\tilde{V}}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\\
&\quad+(\|V^{\delta}(t)\|_{H^{s}}+\|V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s}})\,\|\nabla{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}+\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}\,\|V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}\,.\end{split}

(2.38)

The
L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s}
norms of
V^{\epsilon}
and
V^{\delta}
are uniformly bounded. Since
s>d/2+1
, the functions

\|\nabla V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\infty}
and
\|\nabla V^{\delta}(t)\|_{\infty}
are (uniformly) bounded on
[0,T]
,
and, also,
\|\nabla{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}
.
The energy estimate (
2.28
) with
r=s+1
tells us that

\|V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq C\,\|\rho_{\epsilon}[V(0)]\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq C\,\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,\|V(0)\|_{H^{s}}\,.

By the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality,

\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|^{1-\frac{d}{2s}}\,\|{\tilde{V}}\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{d}{2s}}\,.

Taking into account (
2.34
), we obtain

\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\epsilon^{s-\frac{d}{2}}\,{o(\epsilon)}\,\|{\tilde{V}}\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{d}{2s}}\,.

Hence,

\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}\,\|V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}\lesssim\epsilon^{s-1-\frac{d}{2}}\,{o(\epsilon)}\,\|{\tilde{V}}\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{d}{2s}}\,\|V(0)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\epsilon^{s-1-\frac{d}{2}}\,.

Combining all this,

\frac{d\hfil}{dt}\|{{\tilde{V}}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|{{\tilde{V}}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}+\epsilon^{s-1-\frac{d}{2}}\,.

Integrating this inequality and using the fact that
\|{\tilde{V}}(0)\|_{H^{s}}\to 0
uniformly for
V(0)\in{\mathfrak{C}}
, we see that (again, uniformly for
V(0)\in{\mathfrak{C}}
) the supremum of
\|{{\tilde{V}}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}
goes to
0
as
\epsilon\searrow 0
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

In the proof above, the term
\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}\,\|V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}
occurring in equation (
2.38
) is treated differently from
[
18
]
. In the lower regularity case, where the argument of
[
18
]
does not apply, this term will be treated by a generalization of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality.

Proposition
2.3
takes care of the first two terms of the right hand side of (
2.31
).
To handle the last term,
\sup_{t}\|V_{n}^{\epsilon}(t)-V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s}}
, for a fixed small
\epsilon
, we treat the difference

{\tilde{V}}=V_{n}^{\epsilon}-V^{\epsilon}
as in the proof of the proposition and arrive at the inequality

\begin{split}\frac{d\hfil}{dt}\|{{\tilde{V}}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim{}&(\|\nabla V_{n}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\infty})\,\|{{\tilde{V}}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\\
&\quad+(\|V_{n}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s}}+\|V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s}})\,\|\nabla{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}+\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}\,\|V^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}\,.\end{split}

(2.39)

Now
\|\nabla{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty},\,\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}
and the remaining factors are uniformly bounded in

. Hence,
\sup_{t}\|{\tilde{V}}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|{\tilde{V}}(0)\|_{H^{s}}\to 0
as

.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Remark 2.5
.

It is standard that once the solution
(v,u)

3
Lagrangean picture

This section is located between the sections on high regularity and lower regularity Cauchy problem
in the Euler setting. We treat the Lagrange equations by switching to the Euler form, applying the results for the Euler setting, and switching back to Lagrange. Thus, in this section, our results will be
conditioned on the available results for the Eulerian system. At the moment, we have results only in the case
s>d/2+1
, and we can apply them right away. After we show, in the next section,
how to work with
s<d/2+1
, we shall immediately translate that into the Lagrangean setting
by applying the results of the current section.

3.1
A volume preserving diffeomorphism lemma

We start with a useful lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be a
d\times d
matrix with real (constant) entries and
\det A=1
.
Consider the map
\Phi:\,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\xi}\to x=A\xi+\varphi(\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{x}
such that

1.

\varphi\in H^{s+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\xi})
with
s>\frac{d}{2}
;

2.

\det\,\left(A+\frac{\partial\varphi(\xi)}{\partial\xi}\right)=1
.

Then
\Phi
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism and its inverse,
\Phi^{-1}
, can be written as
\xi=A^{-1}x-g(x)
where
g\in H^{s+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{x})
.

Remark 3.2
.

Notice that the regularity restriction in this lemma corresponds to
V\in H^{s}
with
s>d/2
.

Proof.

By the Sobolev embedding,
the entries of the matrix
\partial\varphi(\xi)/\partial\xi
are bounded, and so
\Phi\in C^{1}
.
Since
\det\,\left(\partial\Phi/\partial\xi\right)=1
,
\Phi
is a local diffeomorphism.
It is a global volume preserving
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
Hadamard’s global inverse function theorem (see
[
11
]
for references and proofs).
In particular,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(\xi)\,d\xi=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(\Phi^{-1}(x))\,dx

for any reasonable
f
.
Since
\Phi
is volume preserving,

\left(A+\frac{\partial\varphi(\xi)}{\partial\xi}\right)^{-1}=A^{-1}+F_{A}(\frac{\partial\varphi(\xi)}{\partial\xi})\,,

where the entries of
F_{A}
are polynomials in
\partial\varphi/\partial\xi
of degree
d-1
without constant terms.
It follows (Moser’s Lemma
B.3
) that
F_{A}(\partial\varphi/\partial\xi)
is in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\xi})
. Denote by

\psi(\xi)
the matrix-function

F_{A}(\partial\varphi/\partial\xi)
viewed as a function of
\xi
. We have

\frac{\partial\hfil}{\partial x}=\left(A+\frac{\partial\varphi(\xi)}{\partial\xi}\right)^{-1}\;\frac{\partial\hfil}{\partial\xi}\,=\,\left(A^{-1}+F_{A}(\frac{\partial\varphi(\xi)}{\partial\xi})\right)\;\frac{\partial\hfil}{\partial\xi}\,=\,(A^{-1}+\psi(\xi))\;\frac{\partial\hfil}{\partial\xi}.

Define
g(x)=A^{-1}x-\xi
. Then

\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x}=A^{-1}-\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial x}=-\psi(\xi)\,.

Note that
\psi(\xi)
is a bounded function.
Calculating the higher
x
-derivatives of
g
using that
\partial/\partial x=(A^{-1}+\psi(\xi))\,\partial/\partial\xi
,
we see that, for any integer
r
, the derivatives
\partial^{r+1}g(x)/\partial x^{r+1}
are sums
of constant multiples of the terms of the form

(\psi)^{\alpha_{0}}\,(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\xi})^{\alpha_{1}}\,(\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial\xi^{2}})^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots(\frac{\partial^{m}\psi}{\partial\xi^{m}})^{\alpha_{m}}\,,

(3.1)

where the
\alpha
’s are non-negative integers and

\alpha_{1}+2\,\alpha_{2}+\cdots+m\,\alpha_{m}=r\,.

(3.2)

Let
r
be the largest integer less than or equal to
s
.
The
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\xi)
norm of each product (
3.1
) is bounded by the
H^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\xi})
norm of
\psi

as follows from Moser’s argument. Hence, all
x
-derivatives of
g(x)
of order
r+1
are in
L^{2}(dx)
.
Assume now
s=r+\gamma
, where
\gamma\in(0,1)
. The
H^{s}
norm of a function is equivalent to the
H^{r}
norm plus
the sum of the
H^{\gamma}
norms of all partial derivatives of order
r
. This means we have to estimate the
H^{\gamma}
norm of the product (
3.1
). Note that, in general, if
f(x)
and
{\tilde{f}}(\xi)
are related by the equation
{\tilde{f}}(\xi)=f(x(\xi))
and the transformation
x\to\xi
is bi-Lipschitz (as in our case), the
H^{\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\xi})
norm of
{\tilde{f}}
and
the
H^{\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{x})
norm of
f
are equivalent if
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
.
Thus, we will estimate the
H^{\theta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\xi})
norms of the expressions (
3.1
). But this is done in a slightly more general case in the proof of Lemma
B.3
.
This completes the proof of the regularity of
g(x)
claimed in the statement of the theorem.

∎

3.2
Cauchy problem in the Lagrange setting

Recall that the basic equations in Lagrangean form are

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}x^{i}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}x^{j}}{\partial\xi^{a}\partial\xi^{a}}+\frac{\partial p}{\partial x^{i}}=0\,,\quad i=1,\dots,d,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\det\frac{\partial x}{\partial\xi}\,=\,1

(3.4)

Given a constant matrix
A\in SL(d,{\mathbb{R}})
, consider the Cauchy problem for this system with the initial conditions

x(0,\xi)=A\xi+\varphi^{L}(\xi)\,,\quad\frac{\partial x(0,\xi)}{\partial t}=v_{0}^{L}(\xi)\,.

(3.5)

We assume that

), (

), (

(3.8)

Theorem 3.3
.

Under Assumption I, the problem (

If the solutions
(v,u)
of the Eulerian system (

on the initial conditions
\varphi^{L}\in H^{s+1}

Proof.

Create the Eulerian initial data
(v(0),u(0))
as described above and solve
the problem (
2.7
), (
2.8
). Thanks to Assumption I there is a solution
(v,u)\in C([0,T]\to H^{s}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{x}))
with
(\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}u)\in C([0,T]\to H^{s-1}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{x}))
.
Note that for
s>d/2

v,u\in C([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\,;

(3.9)

in particular,
v
and
u
are bounded in the slab
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{x}

5.3
Tropical “norms” and homogeneous norms

It shall be convenient to use the following notation:

\langle g\rangle_{p}=\max(1,\|g\|_{p})\,.

These are not norms, but they are “tropical” norms in the following sense.
For a real number
a
, denote
\langle a\rangle=\max(1,|a|)
.
Let
a
,
b
, and
c
be real numbers. Then

1.

If
0\leq a\leq b
, then
\langle a\rangle\leq\langle b\rangle
.

2.

If
|a|\leq 1+|b|
, then
\langle a\rangle\leq 2\,\langle b\rangle
. Also,
1+|b|\leq 2\,\langle b\rangle
.

3.

If
\lambda\geq 0
, then
\langle\lambda\,a\rangle\leq\langle\lambda\rangle\,\langle a\rangle
.

4.

If
\lambda\geq 0
, then
\langle|a|^{\lambda}\rangle=\langle a\rangle^{\lambda}
.

5.

\langle\langle a\rangle\rangle=\langle a\rangle
.

6.

If
\langle a\rangle\leq\,\langle b\rangle
and
\langle b\rangle\leq\,\langle c\rangle
, then

\langle a\rangle\leq\,\langle c\rangle
.

7.

The triangle inequality:
\langle a+b\rangle\leq\,\langle a\rangle+\langle b\rangle
.

8.

\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(t^{\prime})\rangle\,dt^{\prime}\leq t+\int_{0}^{t}|f(t^{\prime})|\,dt^{\prime}
.

The tropical norms absorb additive constants, which simplifies formulas.

Recall that

\frac{\partial x^{i}}{\partial\xi^{a}}=v^{i}_{a}=A^{i}_{a}+u^{i}_{a}

and, therefore,

\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\langle u_{a}\rangle_{\infty}\,,\quad\|u_{a}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\langle v_{a}\rangle_{\infty}\,,\quad\langle v_{a}\rangle_{\infty}\simeq\langle u_{a}\rangle_{\infty}\,.

(5.14)

The hidden constants depend on the matrix
A
.

Throughout, we shall be using the following abbreviation for the homogeneous Sobolev, Besov, and Hölder norms:

\displaystyle\{g\}_{r}=\|g\,\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{\infty,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|\,,\quad\{g\}_{r,p}=\|g\,\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|\,,\quad\quad[g]_{\theta}=\|g\,\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|\,.

5.7
Technical inequalities when

In the three dimensional case, the general scheme of proving low regularity well-posedness is the same as in the two dimensional case. The differences are in details. Lemma

suggests that in

instead of the Hölder

with

In what follows,
2\leq p<\infty
and

will require

. Denote

, which is the scaling (regularity) parameter for the space

.
Using

, we can write

\theta=1+\frac{1}{p}+h\,.

We would like to have

as small as possible (because then we would get

can be chosen arbitrarily small positive.

We shall use the notation

As in the two dimensional case, we have the following inequalities that follow from Lemma

.

Lemma 5.11
.

Appendix A
Function spaces

For the convenience of the reader, we include some background on the function spaces used in this paper.
All spaces we use belong to the scales of Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and Lizorkin-Triebel spaces
F^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Also, we use the homogenous versions of these spaces. For their definition and basic properties we rely on
[
34
]
.

The
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
norm of a function,
f
, is denoted
\|f\|_{p}
or, if it is convenient, in one of the following forms:

\|f\;\big|\;L^{p}\|=\|f(x)\;\big|\;L^{p}(dx)\|\,.

Similar forms are used for the norms in other function spaces. The integral
\int
is the Lebesgue integral over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

{\cal S}={\cal S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing test functions, and
{\cal S}^{\prime}={\cal S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

is its dual, the space of tempered distributions. The pairing between
{\cal S}^{\prime}
and
{\cal S}
is denoted
\langle f,g\rangle

and, for a regular distribution
f\in L^{1}\subset{\cal S}^{\prime}
,
\langle f,g\rangle=\int f(x)\,g(x)\,dx
.

•

The Fourier transform
{\cal F}
is defined as

{\cal F}_{x\to\kappa}f={\hat{f}}(\kappa)=\int e^{-ix\kappa}f(x)\,dx\,,

with the inverse

f(x)={\cal F}^{-1}_{\kappa\to x}{\hat{f}}=\int e^{ix\kappa}{\hat{f}}(\kappa)\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa\,,

where
{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa=(2\pi)^{-d}\,d\kappa
.

•

Notation for the Riesz and Bessel potentials:
D^{s}=\left(-\Delta\right)^{s/2}={\cal F}^{-1}_{\kappa\to\cdot}|\kappa|^{s}{\cal F}_{x\to\kappa}
and

J^{s}=\left(1-\Delta\right)^{s/2}={\cal F}^{-1}_{\kappa\to\cdot}\left(1+|\kappa|^{2}\right)^{s/2}{\cal F}_{x\to\kappa}
, with
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

•

In this paper, all function spaces are subspaces of
{\cal S}^{\prime}
. If
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are such Banach spaces, their intersection,
A_{1}\cap A_{2}
, is viewed as a Banach space with the norm
\|f\,\big|\,A_{1}\cap A_{2}\|=\max\left(\|f\,\big|\,A_{1}\|,\,\|f\,\big|\,A_{2}\|\right)
.

A.1
Littlewood-Paley decomposition

Pick a smooth function
\psi_{0}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,1]
such that
\psi_{0}(s)=1
if
s\leq 1
,
\psi_{0}(s)=0
if
s\geq 2
, and

0<\psi_{0}(s)<1
if
1<s<2
. Set
\varphi_{0}(s)=\psi_{0}(s)-\psi_{0}(2s)
. Then
\hbox{supp}\,\varphi_{0}=[2^{-1},2]
.
For
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, define
\psi_{n}(t)=\psi_{0}(2^{-n}t)
and
\varphi_{n}(t)=\varphi_{0}(2^{-n}t)
. Then,
for all integer
n
,

\varphi_{n-1}(t)+\varphi_{m}(t)+\varphi_{n+1}(t)=1\quad\text{when}\quad t\in\hbox{supp}\;\varphi_{n}=\{2^{n-1}\leq s\leq 2^{n+1}\}\,

and

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi_{n}(t)=1\,,\quad\forall t>0\,.

Also, for all
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and for all
t\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{N}(t)+\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(t)=1\,,

(A.1)

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(t)+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{n}(t)=\psi_{N}(t)\,.

Abusing the notation we write
\varphi_{n}(\kappa)
instead of
\varphi_{n}(|\kappa|)
, where
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and similarly understood are
\psi_{n}(\kappa)
.
Also, we write
\varphi_{n}(D)
,
\psi_{n}(D)
, etc. for the corresponding
pseudodifferential operators, i.e.,

\varphi_{n}(D)\,f=\int e^{ix\kappa}\,\varphi_{n}(\kappa)\,{\hat{f}}(\kappa)\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa\,={\cal F}^{-1}\varphi_{n}{\cal F}f\,.

Thanks to the first identity in (
A.1
), any tempered distribution
f\in{\cal S}^{\prime}
can be expanded as (the Littlewood-Paley decomposition)

f={\cal F}^{-1}\psi_{N}{\cal F}f+\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}{\cal F}^{-1}\varphi_{n}{\cal F}f\;=\psi_{N}(D)f+\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(D)f\,,

(A.2)

where the series converges in
{\cal S}^{\prime}
.
We will abbreviate sometimes
f_{n}=\varphi_{n}(D)f
.

A.2
Homogeneous Besov and Lizorkin-Triebel spaces

Following Triebel
[
34
]
, define
{\cal Z}={\cal Z}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as the subspace of
\cal S
consisting of those test functions
\eta

which satisfy the condition
\int x^{\alpha}\,\eta(x)\,dx=0
for all multiindices
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
with all
\alpha_{j}\geq 0
. Equivalently,

\eta\in{\cal Z}
iff (
\eta\in{\cal S}
and)
\partial^{\alpha}{\hat{\eta}}(0)=0
for all nonnegative multiindices
\alpha
. With the topology inherited from
\cal S
,
\cal Z
is a complete locally convex space.
Polynomials when viewed as elements of
{\cal S}^{\prime}
, annihilate
\cal Z
: If
P
is a polynomial,

P(x)=\sum c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
, and if
\eta\in{\cal Z}
, then

\langle P,\eta\rangle=\langle{\hat{P}},{\hat{\eta}}\rangle=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}\,\langle(i\partial)^{\alpha}\,\delta,{\hat{\eta}}\rangle=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}\,(-i)^{\alpha}\,\langle\delta,\partial^{\alpha}{\hat{\eta}}\rangle=0\,.

Conversely, any tempered distribution
f
that annihilates
{\cal Z}
is a polynomial. Indeed, if
\langle f,\eta\rangle=0
for all
\eta\in{\cal Z}
, then, in particular,
\langle{\hat{f}},{\hat{\eta}}\rangle=0
for every
\eta

. Hence,

\hbox{supp}\,{\hat{f}}=\{0\}
. Therefore,

is a polynomial.
Denote by
{\cal Z}^{\prime}
the topological dual of
\cal Z
. If
\ell
is a linear continuous functional on
{\cal Z}
, then there exist
constants
C\geq 0
and
K\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
K\geq 0

|\ell(\eta)|\leq\,C\,\sum_{|\alpha|\leq K,|\beta|\leq K}\sup_{x}|x^{\alpha}\,\partial^{\beta}\eta(x)|\,.

By the Hahn-Banach theorem, there exists a linear extension of
\ell
from
{\cal Z}
to
\cal S
with the same inequality valid

\eta
in
\cal S

This leads to identification of
{\cal Z}^{\prime}

of
{\cal S}^{\prime}

which really means that for every

\sum_{n=-N}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(D)\,f+p_{N}\underset{N\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}f+p_{\infty}\quad\text{in}\;\;{\cal S}^{\prime}\,

(see

Interpolation inequalities.
If
0<\theta<1
,
s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}
, and if

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}\,,\quad\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}}\,,

where
1\leq p_{0},p_{1}\leq\infty
and
1\leq q_{0},q_{1}\leq\infty
, then

\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,q}\|\lesssim\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}\|^{1-\theta}\;\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}\|^{\theta}\,.

This is due to the fact that
{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,q}=\left[{\dot{B}}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}},{\dot{B}}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}\right]_{\theta}
, the complex interpolation. The analogous result is true for the Lizorkin-Triebel spaces:

\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,q}\|\lesssim\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{F}}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}\|^{1-\theta}\;\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{F}}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}\|^{\theta}\,.

Isomorphisms between spaces.

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\dot{F}}^{0}_{p,2}\simeq L^{p}\,,\quad{\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,2}\simeq{\dot{H}}^{s}_{p}\,,\;1<p<\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}}

(A.9)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\dot{F}}^{s}_{p,p}\simeq{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,p}

It is known (
[
34

r\in(0,1)

{\dot{B}}^{r}_{\infty,\infty}
-seminorm is equivalent to the homogeneous Hölder

{\dot{C}}^{r}
seminorm:

\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{rn}\;\|{\cal F}^{-1}\varphi_{n}{\cal F}f\|_{\infty}\quad\simeq\quad\{f\}_{r}=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{r}}\,,

and, for
1\leq p<\infty

{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,p}
-seminorm is equivalent to the Gagliardo seminorm

\left[f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,p}\right]_{*}=\left(\int\int\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{rp+d}}\right)^{1/p}

An equivalent seminorm in

[f]_{s}=\left(\int|\kappa|^{2\,s}\,|{\hat{f}}(\kappa)|^{2}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa\right)^{1/2}

The nonhomogeneous Besov and Lizorkin-Triebel spaces are made of tempered distributions with the norms

\|f\;\big|\;B^{s}_{p,q}\|=\|\psi_{0}(D)\,f\|_{p}+\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{snq}\|\varphi_{n}(D)f\|_{p}^{q}\right)^{1/q}

and

\|f\;\big|\;F^{s}_{p,q}\|=\|\psi_{0}(D)\,f\|_{p}+\|\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{snq}\,|\varphi_{n}(D)f|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\;\big|\;L^{p}\|\,,

respectively. Equivalent norms are obtained when the part
\|\psi_{0}(D)\,f\|_{p}
is replaced with
\|f\|_{p}
. For all
s,r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the operator
J^{r}=(1-\Delta)^{r/2}={\cal F}^{-1}_{\kappa\to\cdot}(1+|\kappa|^{2})^{r/2}{\cal F}_{x\to\kappa}
is an isomorphism between the nonhomogeneous spaces

{B}^{s+r}_{p,q}
and
{B}^{s}_{p,q}
if
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, and between
{F}^{s+r}_{p,q}
and
{F}^{s}_{p,q}
, when

1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
. The nonhomogeneous spaces are monotone with respect to the parameter
S
:

B^{s_{1}}_{p,q}\subset B^{s_{2}}_{p,q}\quad\text{and}\quad F^{s_{1}}_{p,q}\subset F^{s_{2}}_{p,q}

when
s_{1}\geq s_{2}
. The corresponding homogeneous spaces are not monotone with respect to
s
. However, the following result is easy to prove.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
s>0
,
1\leq m\leq p\leq\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
. Then
L^{m}\cap{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,q}=L^{m}\cap{B}^{s}_{p,q}
. If
s_{1}>s_{2}>0
, then

L^{m}\cap{\dot{B}}^{s_{1}}_{p,q}\subset L^{m}\cap{\dot{B}}^{s_{2}}_{p,q}
. Moreover, if
s_{1}\geq s_{2}>0
,
1\leq m\leq p_{2}\leq p_{1}\leq\infty
, and

s_{1}-\frac{d}{p_{1}}\geq s_{2}-\frac{d}{p_{2}}\,,

then
L^{m}\cap{\dot{B}}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q}\subset L^{m}\cap{\dot{B}}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},q}
(for any
1\leq q\leq\infty
).

Proof.

Assume
f\in L^{m}\cap{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,q}
. Then, for any
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}
,

\langle g,\psi_{0}(D)f\rangle=\int h(x-y)\,f(y)\,g(x)\,dx\,dy\,,

where

h(x)=\int e^{ix\kappa}\,\psi_{0}(\kappa)\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa\,.

By Young’s convolution inequality,

|\langle g,\psi_{0}(D)f\rangle|\leq\|h\|_{\ell}\;\|f\|_{m}\,\|g\|_{p^{\prime}}\,,

if

\frac{1}{\ell}=1+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{m}\,.

Since
m\leq p
, this equality defines
\ell
so that
1\leq\ell<\infty
. Clearly,
h\in L^{\ell}
, since
\psi_{0}
is smooth and has compact support. Thus,

\|\psi_{0}(D)f\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{m}\,.

This proves that
f\in L^{m}\cap{B}^{s}_{p,q}
and
L^{m}\cap{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,q}\subset L^{m}\cap{B}^{s}_{p,q}
.
If
s>0
, then
{B}^{s}_{p,q}\subset{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,q}
.
Together, these observations prove the isomorphism
of spaces:
L^{m}\cap{\dot{B}}^{s}_{p,q}=L^{m}\cap{B}^{s}_{p,q}
. The remaining statements follow from the corresponding statements for non-homogeneous Besov spaces.

∎

A.3
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and Runst’s lemma

Recall the classical Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality:

\|D^{j}g\|_{p}\lesssim\|D^{m}g\|_{p_{1}}^{\alpha}\,\|g\|_{p_{2}}^{1-\alpha}\,,

(A.10)

where
j
and
m
are integers such that
0<j<m
,
1\leq p_{1}<\infty
,
1\leq\,p_{2}\leq\infty
, and

\alpha=\frac{j}{m}\,,\quad\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\alpha}{p_{1}}+\frac{1-\alpha}{p_{2}}\,.

For more general forms/versions of this inequality see
[
32
,
7
]
.

Runst’s inequality is a type of a Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality stated in terms of the Lizorkin-Triebel spaces. Its interesting feature is that there is no restriction on the parameters
q_{1}
and
q
within the range
(0,+\infty]
(though the constants depend on their choice).

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
0<p<\infty
, and
0<q_{1},q\leq\infty
,
r>0
. Then, for any

g\in L^{\infty}\cap F^{s}_{p,q_{1}}
,

\|g\;\big|\;F^{\alpha r}_{p/\theta,q}\|\lesssim\|g\;\big|\;F^{r}_{p,q_{1}}\|^{\alpha}\,\|g\|_{\infty}^{1-\alpha}\,.

(A.11)

Also, for any

g\in L^{\infty}\cap{\dot{F}}^{r}_{p,q_{1}}
,

\|g\;\big|\;{\dot{F}}^{\alpha r}_{p/\theta,q}\|\lesssim\|g\;\big|\;{\dot{F}}^{r}_{p,q_{1}}\|^{\alpha}\,\|g\|_{\infty}^{1-\alpha}\,.

(A.12)

Proof.

The proof in the nonhomogeneous case, (
A.11
), is given by Runst, see
Lemma 1, Section 5.2 of
[
28
]
, and also Lemma 1, Section 5.3.7
[
29
]
). It relies
on Oru’s lemma,
[
7
, Lemma 3.7]
. For the homogeneous spaces one needs a slight generalization of Oru’s lemma, namely,

Lemma A.3
.

If
-\infty<s_{1}<s_{2}<+\infty
,
0<q<\infty
,
0<\alpha<1
, and
s=\alpha s_{1}+(1-\alpha)s_{2}
, then

\|2^{sj}a_{j}\|_{\ell^{q}}\lesssim\|2^{s_{1}j}a_{j}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}^{\alpha}\;\|2^{s_{2}j}a_{j}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}^{1-\alpha}

(A.13)

for any sequence
\{a_{j}\}_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}
.

We leave its proof to the reader and continue with the proof of (
A.12
).

Denote
g_{n}={\cal F}^{-1}\varphi_{n}{\cal F}g
. With
s=\alpha r
,
s_{1}=r
, and
s_{2}=0
, it follows from Lemma
A.3
that

\left(\sum_{n}2^{snq}|g_{n}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\lesssim\left(\sup_{n}2^{s_{1}n}|g_{n}|\right)^{\alpha}\;\left(\sup_{n}2^{s_{2}n}|g_{n}|\right)^{1-\alpha}=\left(\sup_{n}2^{rn}|g_{n}|\right)^{\alpha}\;\left(\sup_{n}|g_{n}|\right)^{1-\alpha}

and, consequently,

\left(\sum_{n}2^{\alpha rnq}|g_{n}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\lesssim\left(\sup_{n}2^{rn}|g_{n}|\right)^{\alpha}\;\|\sup_{n}|g_{n}|\|_{\infty}^{1-\alpha}\,.

Since
\sup_{n}2^{rn}|g_{n}|\leq\left(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{rnq_{1}}|g_{n}|^{q_{1}}\right)^{1/q_{1}}
for any
q_{1}>0
, and since

\|\sup_{n}|g_{n}|\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|g\|_{\infty}
, we have

\left(\sum_{n}2^{\alpha rnq}|g_{n}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\lesssim\left(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{rnq_{1}}|g_{n}|^{q}\right)^{\alpha/q_{1}}\;\|g\|_{\infty}^{1-\alpha}\,.

Take the
L^{p/\alpha}
norm of both sides to obtain (
A.12
).

∎

Appendix B
Norms of products and compositions of functions

B.1
Norms of products

There are several results (sometimes called the fractional Leibniz rule) on the Sobolev/Lizorkin-Triebel norms of products of functions.

Lemma B.1
.

If
s>0
and
1<p<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\|D^{s}(f_{1}\cdot f_{2})\|_{p}\lesssim\|D^{s}f_{1}\|_{q_{1}}\,\|f_{2}\|_{q_{2}}+\|f_{1}\|_{q_{3}}\,\|D^{s}f_{2}\|_{q_{4}}

(B.1)

provided

1<q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4}\leq\infty,\quad\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{1}{q_{3}}+\frac{1}{q_{4}}=\frac{1}{p}\,.

The same inequality is true with
D^{s}
replaced by the operator
J^{s}=(1-\Delta)^{s/2}
, with the same restrictions on the parameters:

\displaystyle\|f_{1}\cdot f_{2}\;\big|\;F^{s}_{p,2}\|\lesssim\|f_{1}\;\big|\;F^{s}_{p\,q_{1},2}\|\,\|f_{2}\|_{pq_{2}}+\|f_{1}\|_{pq_{3}}\,\|f_{2}\;\big|\;F^{s}_{p\,q_{4},2}\|\,.

(B.2)

As a corollary,
if
s>0
and
1<p<\infty
, then

\|f_{1}\cdot\cdots\cdot f_{N}\;\big|\;F^{s}_{p,2}\|\leq C\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|f_{j}\;\big|\;F^{s}_{p\,p_{j},2}\|\,\cdot\,\prod_{i\neq j}\|f_{i}\|_{p\,p_{i}}

(B.3)

provided

\sum_{1}^{N}\frac{1}{p_{j}}=1\,.

For the proofs of (
B.1
) and (
B.2
) see
[
12
, Theorem 1]
and
[
4
, Theorem1.1]
. Some more general inequalities are established in
[
29
]
. For earlier results see the Christ and Weinstein paper
[
8
, Prop. 3.3]
and
[
33
, Prop. 2.1.1]
.

B.2
Norms of compositions

Lemma
B.1
is used, in particular, to
estimate the
H^{s}
norms of compositions
f(u)
, where
f
is a sufficiently smooth functions and
u\in H^{s}\cap L^{\infty}
. The simplest result deals with the case
0<s\leq 1
.

Lemma B.2
.

Suppose
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a locally Lipschitz function such that
f(0)=0
.
Then, if
0<s\leq 1
and
1<p<\infty
,

\|f(u(\cdot))\,\big|\;F^{s}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|\leq C_{f}(\|u\|_{\infty})\;\|u\,\big|\;F^{s}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|

for any function
u\in F^{s}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(recall that
F^{s}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})=H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
). Here

C_{f}(\|u\|_{\infty})=\inf\{C\,:\;|f(z_{1})-f(z_{2})|\leq C\,|z_{1}-z_{2}|\,,\;\forall z_{1},z_{2}:\;|z_{1,2}|\leq\|u\|_{\infty}\}

The proof of Lemma
B.2
relies on the fact that
|f(u(x))-f(u(y))|\leq C_{f}(\|u\|_{\infty})\,|u(x)-u(y)|
, see
[
33
, Prop. 2.4.1]
. Next, consider larger
s
. In the main body of the paper we need only the case of the Sobolev scale
H^{s}
.

Lemma B.3
.

Let
s>0
. Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be
r=\lfloor s\rfloor
times continuously differentiable function such that its
r
th derivative is locally Lipschitz, and
f(0)=0
.
Then there is a continuous, nondecreasing function

C_{f,s,d}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that

\|f(u(\cdot))\,\big|\;{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|\leq C_{f,s,d}(\|u\|_{\infty})\;\|u\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

(B.4)

for any function
u\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proof.

For infinitely differential functions
f
, there is an elegant and short proof of (
B.4
) in Hörmander’s book

[
14
, Theorem 8.5.1]
.
However, having in mind finitely differentiable
f
, we present a different argument. It can be used for other purposes as well (we use it to prove
regularity of the inverse map in Lemma
3.1
).

For
s
an integer, to prove (
B.4
) we follow Moser’s argument on p. 273 of paper
[
26
]
. Let
s=r
be an integer greater than
1
.
Observe that
|f(u)|\leq C_{f}(|u|)\,|u|
, and hence
\|f(u)\|\leq C_{f}(\|u\|_{\infty})\,\|u\|
. Now it suffices to consider the derivatives of the order
r
of
f(u(x))
.
Schematically,

\partial^{r}f(u)=\sum_{k\leq r}f^{(k)}(u)\sum_{|\alpha|=k}C_{k\alpha}(\partial u)^{\alpha_{1}}(\partial^{2}u)^{\alpha_{2}}\dots(\partial^{r}u)^{\alpha_{r}}

(B.5)

where
\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+\dots+r\alpha_{r}=r
and
C_{k\alpha}
are non-negative constants. We have

\|\partial^{r}f(u)\|\leq\sum_{k\leq r}\|f^{(k)}(u)\|_{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}C_{k\alpha}\,\prod_{m=1}^{r}\|(\partial^{m}u)^{\alpha_{m}}\|_{2p_{m}}\,,

where
1\leq p_{m}\leq\infty
and
\sum_{m}1/p_{m}=1
. The right choice of
p_{m}
is

p_{m}=\frac{r}{m\,\alpha_{m}}\,,

because then

\|(\partial^{m}u)^{\alpha_{m}}\|_{2p_{m}}=\|\partial^{m}u\|_{2\alpha_{m}p_{m}}^{\alpha_{m}}

and

\|\partial^{m}u\|_{2\alpha_{m}p_{m}}=\|\partial^{m}u\|_{2r/m}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{r}}^{m/r}\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{1-m/r}\,,

by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. Collecting all the terms we obtain

\|\partial^{r}f(u)\|_{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{r}}\;\sum_{k\leq r}C_{f^{(k)}}(\|u\|_{\infty})\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{k-1}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}C_{k\alpha}\,.

Now consider the case of fractional
s
. Assume
s=r+\gamma
,
r\geq 1
is an integer and
0<\gamma<1
.
Since we have the
H^{r}
norm of
f(u)
already bounded, it remains to show that the
H^{\gamma}
norm of each term of the form

g(u)\,(\partial u)^{\alpha_{1}}(\partial^{2}u)^{\alpha_{2}}\dots(\partial^{r}u)^{\alpha_{r}}

(B.6)

is bounded (see (
B.5
)).
First, consider the case
g(u)
is not a constant. Use the product estimate (
B.2
)
to obtain

\displaystyle\|g(u)\,\prod_{k=1}^{r}(\partial^{k}u)^{\alpha_{k}}\|_{H^{\gamma}}\lesssim\|g(u)\|_{\infty}\,\|\prod_{k=1}^{r}(\partial^{k}u)^{\alpha_{k}}\|_{H^{\gamma}}+\|\prod_{k=1}^{r}(\partial^{k}u)^{\alpha_{k}}\|_{2q}\,\|g(u)\,\big|\;F^{\gamma}_{2q^{\prime},2}\|

(B.7)

where

q=\frac{s}{r}\,,\quad q^{\prime}=\frac{s}{s-r}\;.

(B.8)

Since
\|g(u)\|_{\infty}\lesssim C_{g}(\|u\|_{\infty})\,\|u\|_{\infty}
, the first term on the right will be treated later (with the case
g(\cdot)=const
). Thus, look at the second term.
The choice of
q
is dictated by the following computation. First, use Hölder’s inequality,

\|\prod_{m=1}^{r}(\partial^{m}u)^{\alpha_{m}}\|_{2q}\leq\prod_{m=1}^{r}\,\|\partial^{m}u\|_{2q\alpha_{m}p_{m}}^{\alpha_{m}}\,.

Then,

\|\partial^{m}u\|_{2q\alpha_{m}p_{m}}\leq\|u\;\big|\;F^{m}_{2q\alpha_{m}p_{m},2}\|

The norm
\|u\;\big|\;F^{m}_{2q\alpha_{m}p_{m},2}\|
will be bounded using (
A.11
) as follows:

\|u\;\big|\;F^{m}_{2q\alpha_{m}p_{m},2}\|\leq\|u\;\big|\;F^{s}_{2,2}\|^{\theta_{m}}\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{1-\theta_{m}}\;.

This means the indices should satisfy

m=s\,\theta_{m},\quad 2q\,\alpha_{m}p_{m}=\frac{2}{\theta_{m}}

and
1\leq p_{m}\leq\infty
,

\sum_{m}\frac{1}{p_{m}}=1\,.

Consequently, we must have

\theta_{m}=\frac{m}{s}

Then

2q\alpha_{m}p_{m}=\frac{2s}{m}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad m\,\alpha_{m}=\frac{2s}{2q}\,\frac{1}{p_{m}}

Since
\sum m\alpha_{m}=r
and we want
\sum 1/p_{m}=1
, we must have
q=s/r
.

To estimate the norm
\|g(u)\,\big|\;F^{\gamma}_{2q^{\prime},2}\|
apply Lemma
B.2
:

\|g(u)\,\big|\;F^{\gamma}_{2q^{\prime},2}\|\lesssim C(\|u\|_{\infty})\,\|u\,\big|\;F^{\gamma}_{2q^{\prime},2}\|

By Runst’s Lemma
A.2
,

\|u\,\big|\;F^{\gamma}_{2q^{\prime},2}\|\lesssim\|u\;\big|\;F^{s}_{2,2}\|^{1-\frac{r}{s}}\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{\frac{r}{s}}\,.

Collecting the estimates, we get

\displaystyle\|\prod_{m=1}^{r}(\partial^{m}u)^{\alpha_{m}}\|_{2q}\,\|g(u)\,\big|\;F^{\gamma}_{2q^{\prime},2}\|\lesssim

\displaystyle C(\|u\|_{\infty})\,\|u\,\big|\;H^{s}\|^{1-\frac{r}{s}}\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{\frac{r}{s}}\;\prod_{m=1}^{r}\|u\,\big|\;H^{s}\|^{\alpha_{m}\theta_{m}}\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{\alpha_{m}(1-\theta_{m})}=

\displaystyle C(\|u\|_{\infty})\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{\sum\alpha_{m}}\;\|u\|_{H^{s}}\,,

where we have used that, by construction,

\sum_{m=1}^{r}\alpha_{m}\,\theta_{m}=\frac{r}{s}\,.

Finally, consider the case
g(u)\equiv const
. To estimate
\|\prod_{m}(\partial^{m}u)^{\alpha_{m}}\;\big|\;{H^{\gamma}}\|

use (
B.3
):

\|\prod_{m}(\partial^{m}u)^{\alpha_{m}}\|_{H^{\gamma}}\lesssim\sum_{m}\|(\partial^{m}u)^{\alpha_{m}}\;\big|\;F^{\gamma}_{2q_{m},2}\|\,\prod_{j\neq m}\|(\partial^{j}u)^{\alpha_{j}}\;\big|\;L^{2q_{j}}\|\,,

where
q_{1},\dots,q_{r}
will be chosen later (and will satisfy
\sum_{j}1/q_{j}=1
).
The factors in the product are bounded first as follows:

\|(\partial^{j}u)^{\alpha_{j}}\;\big|\;L^{2q_{j}}\|\lesssim\|u\;\big|\;F^{j}_{2\alpha_{j}q_{j},2}\|^{\alpha_{j}}\,,

and then Runst’s lemma is applied:

\|u\;\big|\;F^{j}_{2\alpha_{j}q_{j},2}\|\lesssim\|u\;\big|\;F^{s}_{2,2}\|^{\beta_{j}}\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{1-\beta_{j}}\,.

Lemma
A.2
imposes the following restrictions

j=\beta_{j}\,s\,,\quad 2\beta_{j}\,\alpha_{j}\,q_{j}=2\,,

i.e.,

\beta_{j}=\frac{j}{s}\,,\quad\frac{1}{q_{j}}=\alpha_{j}\,\frac{j}{s}\,.

Thus,

\|(\partial^{j}u)^{\alpha_{j}}\;\big|\;L^{2q_{j}}\|\lesssim\|u\;\big|\;H^{s}\|^{j\,\alpha_{j}/s}\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{\alpha_{j}(1-j/s)}\,,

for
j\neq m
.

Next, consider
\|(\partial^{m}u)^{\alpha_{m}}\;\big|\;F^{\gamma}_{2q_{m},2}\|
.
Assuming
\alpha_{m}>0
, first use (
B.3
) with equal exponents:

\displaystyle\|(\partial^{m}u)^{\alpha_{m}}\;\big|\;F^{\gamma}_{2q_{m},2}\|\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha_{m}}\|(\partial^{m}u)\;\big|\;F^{\gamma}_{2\alpha_{m}q_{m},2}\|\,\prod_{j=1}^{\alpha_{m}-1}\|(\partial^{m}u)\;\big|\;L^{2\alpha_{m}q_{m}}\|

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\alpha_{m}\,\|(\partial^{m}u)\;\big|\;F^{\gamma}_{2\alpha_{m}q_{m},2}\|\,\|(\partial^{m}u)\;\big|\;L^{2\alpha_{m}q_{m}}\|^{\alpha_{m}-1}

and continue as follows:

\lesssim\|u\;\big|\;F^{m+\gamma}_{2\alpha_{m}q_{m},2}\|\,\|u\;\big|\;F^{m}_{2\alpha_{m}q_{m}}\|^{\alpha_{m}-1}

Now each norm is bounded using (
A.11
). We have

\|u\;\big|\;F^{m+\gamma}_{2\alpha_{m}q_{m},2}\|\lesssim\|u\,\big|\;H^{s}\|^{\lambda}\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{1-\lambda}\,,

where

\lambda=\frac{m+\gamma}{s}

and
q_{m}
must be chosen so that

\frac{1}{q_{m}}=\alpha_{m}\,\frac{m+\gamma}{s}\,.

Similarly,

\|u\;\big|\;F^{m}_{2\alpha_{m}q_{m}}\|\lesssim\|u\,\big|\;H^{s}\|^{\lambda_{m}}\,\|u\|_{\infty}^{1-\lambda_{m}}\,,

where
q_{m}
is as above and

\lambda_{m}=\frac{m}{s}\,.

Bringing the estimates together, we obtain

\|\prod_{m}(\partial^{m}u)^{\alpha_{m}}\;\big|\;{H^{\gamma}}\|\lesssim{\tilde{C}}(\|u\|_{\infty})\,\|u\|_{H^{s}}\,,

as claimed.

∎

Appendix C
Estimates on Riesz transforms

The Riesz transform operators
{\cal R}_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,d
, will be defined by the formula

{\cal R}_{j}f={\cal F}^{-1}\frac{\kappa^{j}}{|\kappa|}{\cal F}f\,.

It is well known (see
[
25
, Theorem 10.2.1]
) that
{\cal R}_{j}
is bounded in
{\dot{B}}^{s}_{\infty,\infty}
, when
s>0
, but not
in
{B}^{s}_{\infty,\infty}
. The Riesz transform is a special case of a more general class of pseudo-differential operators which we shall now discuss.

Denote by
S^{0}_{ph}
the space of all smooth functions
a:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\to{\mathbb{R}}
which are positively homogeneous of degree
0
and satisfy

|\partial^{\alpha}_{\kappa}\,a(\kappa)|\leq C_{\alpha}\,|\kappa|^{-|\alpha|}

for any multiindex
\alpha
. Each symbol
a\in S^{0}_{ph}
gives rise to a pseudodifferential operator
{\mathfrak{a}}:\,f\mapsto{\mathfrak{a}}[f]
, where

{\mathfrak{a}}[f](x)={\cal F}^{-1}_{\kappa\to x}a{\cal F}f=\int e^{ix\kappa}a(\kappa)\,{\hat{f}}(\kappa)\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa\,.

Any finite composition of the Riesz transforms has its symbol in
S^{0}_{ph}
.
An observation: if, as usual,
q
and
q^{\prime}
are conjugate exponents, and

1\leq q^{\prime}\leq 2\leq q\leq\infty
, then (change of variables)

\|\int e^{iy\kappa}\varphi(2^{-n}\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa\;\big|\;L^{q^{\prime}}(dy)\|=2^{n\frac{d}{q}}\,\|{\cal F}^{-1}\left(\varphi\cdot a\right)\;\big|\;L^{q^{\prime}}\|\,.

(C.1)

In particular, the integrals

\int e^{iky}\varphi_{n}(k)\,a(k)\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}k

represent smooth functions with uniformly in
n
bounded
L^{1}
norms.

Lemma C.1
.

Let the symbol
a\in S^{0}_{ph}
be given.

1.

For any
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
and any
p\in[2,+\infty]
,

\|{\mathfrak{a}}[f]\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,p}\|\lesssim\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,p}\|\,.

(C.2)

2.

Assume the parameters
r,p
, and
q
satisfy the conditions
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
r>\frac{d}{p}
.
Then there exists a constant

C_{1}
such that

\|{\mathfrak{a}}[f]\|_{\infty}\leq C_{1}\;[f]_{-1}^{\gamma_{1}}\;\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|^{1-\gamma_{1}}

(C.3)

for every
f\in{\dot{H}}^{-1}\cap{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}
,
where

\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{1}(d,r,p)=\frac{r-\frac{d}{p}}{r+1+d\,\frac{p-2}{2p}}\,.

(C.4)

3.

Assume the parameters
r,p
, and
q
satisfy the conditions
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
r>\frac{d}{p}-1
.
Then there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\|D^{-1}\,{\mathfrak{a}}[f]\|_{\infty}\leq C_{2}\;[f]_{-1}^{\gamma_{2}}\;\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|^{1-\gamma_{2}}

(C.5)

for every
f\in{\dot{H}}^{-1}\cap{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}
, where

\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{2}(d,r,p)=\frac{r+1-\frac{d}{p}}{r+1+d\,\frac{p-2}{2p}}

(C.6)

Proof.

To prove the first claim we need to show that

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\,2^{rnp}\,\|\varphi_{n}(D){\mathfrak{a}}[f]\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\,2^{rmp}\,\|f_{m}\|_{p}^{p}\,,

where
f_{m}=\varphi_{m}(D)f
.
This follows from the estimate

\|\varphi_{n}(D){\mathfrak{a}}[f]\|_{p}\lesssim\|f_{n}\|_{p}\,,

(C.7)

which is easy to prove. Indeed,

\left(\varphi_{n}(D){\mathfrak{a}}[f]\right)(x)=\sum_{\ell=-1}^{1}\left(\varphi_{n+\ell}(D){\mathfrak{a}}[\varphi_{n}(D)f]\right)(x)

and

\left(\varphi_{n+\ell}(D){\mathfrak{a}}[\varphi_{n}(D)f]\right)(x)=\int\int e^{ik(x-y)}\varphi_{n+\ell}(k)\,a(k)\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}k\;f_{n}(y)\,dy\,.

Then (see (
C.1
))

\|\varphi_{n+\ell}(D){\mathfrak{a}}[\varphi_{n}(D)f]\,\|_{p}\leq\|\int e^{iky}\varphi_{n+\ell}(k)\,a(k)\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}k\;\big|\;L^{1}(dy)\|\;\|f_{n}\|_{p}\lesssim\|f_{n}\|_{p}\,.

Thus we have (
C.7
), and this implies (
C.2
).

To prove the remaining two claims,
consider the expansion (
A.2
) for
f
,
f=g_{N}+h_{N}
, where

g_{N}={\cal F}^{-1}\psi_{N}(\kappa)\,{\hat{f}}(\kappa)\quad\text{and}\quad h_{N}={\cal F}^{-1}\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(\kappa)\,{\hat{f}}(\kappa)\,.

the integer
N
will be chosen later. We have

\|{\mathfrak{a}}[g_{N}]\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|\psi(2^{-N}\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,{\hat{f}}(\kappa)\|_{L^{1}(d\kappa)}\leq[f]_{-1}\;\|\psi(2^{-N}\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,|\kappa|\|_{L^{2}(d\kappa)}

and

\|\psi(2^{-N}\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,|\kappa|\|_{L^{2}(d\kappa)}=2^{N(1+\frac{d}{2})}\,\|\psi(\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,|\kappa|\|_{L^{2}(d\kappa)}\,.

Therefore,

\|{\mathfrak{a}}[g_{N}]\|_{\infty}\lesssim 2^{N(1+\frac{d}{2})}\,[f]_{-1}\;.

(C.8)

Next,

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}}[h_{N}]={\cal F}^{-1}\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,{\hat{f}}(\kappa)={\cal F}^{-1}\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=-1}^{1}\varphi_{n+\ell}(\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,\varphi_{n}(\kappa)\,{\hat{f}}(\kappa)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=-1}^{1}\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}{\cal F}^{-1}\left[\varphi_{n+\ell}(\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,{\hat{f}}_{n}(\kappa)\right]=\sum_{\ell=-1}^{1}\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}{\cal F}^{-1}\varphi_{n+\ell}a\,{\cal F}f_{n}\,,

and hence,

\|{\mathfrak{a}}[h_{N}]\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{\ell=-1}^{1}\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\|{\cal F}^{-1}\varphi_{n+\ell}\,a\,{\cal F}f_{n}\|_{\infty}\,.

Pick a
p\geq 1
and observe that

\displaystyle|\;[{\cal F}^{-1}\varphi_{j}\,a\,{\cal F}f_{n}](x)\;|=|\int\left(\int e^{i(x-y)\kappa}\varphi(2^{-j}\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa\right)\,f_{n}(y)\,dy|\leq

\displaystyle\|\int e^{i(x-y)\kappa}\varphi(2^{-j}\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dy)}\,\|f_{n}\|_{p}\,.

Applying (
C.1
), we obtain

\|{\mathfrak{a}}[h_{N}]\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{p}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}2^{n\frac{d}{p}}\;\|f_{n}\|_{p}\;.

(C.9)

If

r>\frac{d}{p}\,,

then, for any
q\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}2^{n\frac{d}{p}}\;\|f_{n}\|_{p}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}2^{n(\frac{d}{p}-r)}\;2^{nr}\|f_{n}\|_{p}\leq\left(\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}2^{n(\frac{d}{p}-r)q^{\prime}}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\;\left(\sum_{m=N}^{\infty}2^{nrq}\|f_{n}\|^{q}_{p}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2^{N(\frac{d}{p}-r)}\,\left(1-2^{(\frac{d}{p}-r)q^{\prime}}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\;\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|

Thus,

\|{\mathfrak{a}}[h_{N}]\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{p,q,d,r}\,2^{N(\frac{d}{p}-r)}\;\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|

(C.10)

Combine this with (
C.8
) to obtain

\|{\mathfrak{a}}[f]\|_{\infty}\lesssim 2^{N(1+\frac{d}{2})}\,[f]_{-1}+2^{N(\frac{d}{p}-r)}\;\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|\,.

(C.11)

Now choose
N
so that the two terms have (almost) the same magnitudes:

N=\lfloor\;\log_{2}\left(\frac{\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|}{[f]_{-1}}\right)^{1/(1+r+d\frac{p-2}{2p})}\;\rfloor

(
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
is the floor function).
The resulting inequality is

\|\rho[f]\|_{\infty}\lesssim[f]_{-1}^{\gamma_{1}(d,p,r)}\;\cdot\;\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|^{1-\gamma_{1}(d,p,r)}

(C.12)

with

\gamma_{1}(d,p,r)=\frac{r-\frac{d}{p}}{r+1+d\,\frac{p-2}{2p}}\,.

The second part is proved similarly. First we have

\|D^{-1}{\mathfrak{a}}[g_{N}]\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|\psi(2^{-N}\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,\frac{1}{|\kappa|}\,{\hat{f}}(\kappa)\|_{L^{1}(d\kappa)}\leq[f]_{-1}\;\|\psi(2^{-N}\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\|_{L^{2}(d\kappa)}\lesssim 2^{N\,\frac{d}{2}}\,[f]_{-1}\,.

After that, consider
\|D^{-1}{\mathfrak{a}}[h_{N}]\|_{\infty}
. We have

\|D^{-1}{\mathfrak{a}}[h_{N}]\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{\ell=-1}^{1}\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\|D^{-1}{\cal F}^{-1}\varphi_{n+\ell}\,a\,{\cal F}f_{n}\|_{\infty}\,.

Now,

\displaystyle|\;[D^{-1}{\cal F}^{-1}\varphi_{j}\,a\,{\cal F}f_{n}](x)\;|=|\int\left(\int e^{i(x-y)\kappa}\varphi(2^{-j}\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,\frac{1}{|\kappa|}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa\right)\,f_{n}(y)\,dy|\leq

\displaystyle\|\int e^{i(x-y)\kappa}\varphi(2^{-j}\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,\frac{1}{|\kappa|}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dy)}\,\|f_{n}\|_{p}\,,

and

\displaystyle\|\int e^{i(x-y)\kappa}\varphi(2^{-j}\kappa)\,a(\kappa)\,\frac{1}{|\kappa|}\,{\mathchar 22\mkern-11.0mud}\kappa\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dy)}=2^{j(\frac{d}{p}-1)}\,\|D^{-1}{\cal F}^{-1}\left(\varphi\cdot a\right)\|_{p^{\prime}}\,=C\,2^{j(\frac{d}{p}-1)}.

This leads to

\displaystyle\|D^{-1}\rho[h_{N}]\|_{\infty}\lesssim\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}2^{n(\frac{d}{p}-1)}\;\|f_{n}\|_{p}

and we proceed as in the first part. Now the restriction on
r
will be

r>\frac{d}{p}-1

and then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}2^{n(\frac{d}{p}-1)}\;\|f_{n}\|_{p}\leq{}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}2^{n(\frac{d}{p}-1-r)q^{\prime}}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\,\left(\sum_{m=N}^{\infty}2^{nrq}\,\|f_{n}\|_{p}^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2^{N(\frac{d}{p}-1-r)}\,\left(1-2^{(\frac{d}{p}-1-r)q^{\prime}}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\,\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|

This time we obtain

\|D^{-1}{\mathfrak{a}}[f]\|_{\infty}\lesssim 2^{N\,\frac{d}{2}}\,[f]_{-1}+2^{N(\frac{d}{p}-1-r)}\,\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|\,.

Again, choose
N
so that

2^{N(1+r+d\,\frac{p-2}{2p})}\approx\frac{\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|}{[f]_{-1}}\,.

i.e.,

N=\lfloor\;\log_{2}\left(\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|/[f]_{-1}\right)^{1/(1+r+d\,\frac{p-2}{2p})}\;\rfloor\,.

Plugging this value into the inequality above we obtain

\|D^{-1}\rho[f]\,\|_{\infty}\lesssim[f]_{-1}^{\gamma_{2}}\;\|f\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|^{1-\gamma_{2}}

with

\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{2}(d,p,r)=\frac{1+r-\frac{d}{p}}{1+r+d\,\frac{p-2}{2p}}\;.

∎

Appendix D
Strichartz estimates

In this section we derive the homogeneous
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
version of the Strichartz estimates in Theorem 2
[
17
]
. We work with functions of
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and the Fourier
variables are
\kappa
. Note, that the estimates are applied in Section
5
to functions of
\xi
with the dual Fourier variables
k
.

Consider the Cauchy problem

\partial_{t}w=iDw+f\,,\quad w(0)=w_{0}\,,

(D.1)

where
D=\sqrt{-\Delta}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Given
w_{0}\in{\dot{H}}^{\theta}
and
f\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}\to{\dot{H}}^{\theta})
, there exists
a unique solution
w
of equation (
D.1
) such that
w(t)
is a continuous functions of
t
with values in
{\dot{H}}^{\theta}
, and

\sup_{[0,T]}\|w(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}\|\leq C_{\theta}\,\left(\|w_{0}\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}\|+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}\|\,dt\right)

(D.2)

with the positive constant
C_{\theta}
independent of the particular choice of
w_{0}
and
f
.

Theorem D.1
.

Let
2\leq q\leq\infty
and let
p
be such that if
d=2
, then

2\leq p\leq+\infty
, and if
d\geq 3
, then
2\leq p<\frac{2(d-1)}{d-3}
.
Assume the parameters
r
and
\theta
satisfy the conditions

r\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad\theta=r+\frac{d+1}{4}\,\frac{p-2}{p}\,.

(D.3)

There exists a constant
C>0
(dependent on
d
,
r
, and
p
) such that, for any
T>0
, the following estimate is true
for the solutions of (
D.1
):

\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|w(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|^{\frac{4p}{(d-1)(p-2)}}\,dt\right)^{\frac{(d-1)(p-2)}{4p}}\leq C\,\left(\|w_{0}\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}\|+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}\|\,dt\right)

(D.4)

Corollary D.2
.

Two special cases: If
d=2
, then take
p=q=\infty
,
0<r<1
, and
\theta=r+\frac{3}{4}
, and obtain

\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|w(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{\infty,\infty}\|^{4}\,dt\right)^{1/4}\leq C\,\left(\|w_{0}\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}\|+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}\|\,dt\right)\,.

(D.5)

If
d=3
, take
2\leq p=q<\infty
, and
\theta=r+\frac{p-2}{p}
, and obtain

\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|w(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,p}\|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\,dt\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2p}}\leq C\,\left(\|w_{0}\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}\|+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}\|\,dt\right)

(D.6)

Proof of Theorem
D.1
.

For fixed
w_{0}
and
f
, the solution of the problem (
D.1
) is given by the formula

w(t)=e^{it\,D}w_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)D}f(\tau)\,d\tau\,.

Each term on the right can be analyzed separately. Take a
g\in{\cal S}
and consider
e^{it\,D}g
.
In fact, we need to look at the dyadic pieces
\varphi_{n}(D)e^{it\,D}g=e^{it\,D}g_{n}
, where
g_{n}=\varphi_{n}(D)g
. Since

e^{it\,D}g_{n}=\sum_{j=n-1}^{n+1}\varphi_{j}(D)\,e^{it\,D}g_{n}\,,

we examine the terms
\varphi_{j}(D)\,e^{it\,D}g_{n}
. The first important observation in the Strichartz analysis is
the following bound on the operator
\varphi_{j}(D)\,e^{it\,D}
as an operator from
L^{p^{\prime}}
to
L^{p}
,
2\leq p\leq\infty
:

\|\varphi_{j}(D)\,e^{it\,D}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{(d-1)\,\frac{p-2}{2p}}}\,2^{j(d+1)\,\frac{p-2}{2p}}\,.

(D.7)

As a corollary,

\|e^{it\,D}g_{n}\|_{p}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{(d-1)\,\frac{p-2}{2p}}}\,2^{n(d+1)\,\frac{p-2}{2p}}\,\|g_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\,,

(D.8)

for any
p\in[2,+\infty]
.
This estimate implies the following estimates in the homogeneous Besov spaces:

\|e^{it\,D}g\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{(d-1)\,\frac{p-2}{2p}}}\;\|g\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r+(d+1)\,\frac{p-2}{2p}}_{p^{\prime},q}\|\,,

(D.9)

r\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
The second important observation is the space-time estimate for the homogeneous term
e^{it\,D}g
. The argument uses duality. Let
h(t,x)
be a sufficiently smooth function. Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle e^{it\,D}g\,,\;h(t)\rangle\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\langle e^{it\,D}g\,,\;h(t)\rangle\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\langle g\,,\;e^{-it\,D}h(t)\rangle\,dt=\langle g\,,\;\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\,D}h(t)\,dt\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|g\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}\|\,\|D^{-\theta}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\,D}h(t)\,dt\|_{2}

Now,

\displaystyle\|D^{-\theta}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\,D}h(t)\,dt\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\langle e^{-it\,D}D^{-\theta}h(t),e^{-it^{\prime}\,D}D^{-\theta}h(t^{\prime})\rangle\,dt^{\prime}\,dt=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\langle e^{-i(t^{\prime}-t)\,D}\,D^{-2\theta}h(t),h(t^{\prime})\rangle\,dt^{\prime}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\,D}\,D^{-2\theta}h(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|\cdot\|h(t^{\prime})\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{-r}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\|\;dt^{\prime}\,dt\quad\text{(use \eqref{besov-1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{(d-1)\,\frac{p-2}{2p}}}\;\|h(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r+(d+1)\,\frac{p-2}{2p}-2\theta}_{p^{\prime},q}\|\cdot\|h(t^{\prime})\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{-r}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\|\;dt^{\prime}\,dt

Choose
\theta
so that

r+(d+1)\,\frac{p-2}{2p}-2\theta=-r

i.e.,

\theta=r+\frac{d+1}{4}\,\frac{p-2}{p}

(D.10)

and assume that

q\geq 2

(then
q^{\prime}\leq 2\leq q
and
{\dot{B}}^{-r}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\subset{\dot{B}}^{-r}_{p^{\prime},q}
). Then

\displaystyle\|D^{-\theta}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\,D}h(t)\,dt\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\|h(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{-r}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\|\cdot\|h(t^{\prime})\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{-r}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\|}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{(d-1)\,\frac{p-2}{2p}}}\;dt^{\prime}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|h(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{-r}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\|^{m}\right)^{1/m}

with

m=\frac{1}{1-\frac{d-1}{4}\,\frac{p-2}{p}}\,.

The last inequality is a consequence of
the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (see
[
15
, Theorem 4.5.3]
). It requires the following restrictions on
p
:

0\leq\frac{d-1}{2}\,\frac{p-2}{p}<1\,,

(D.11)

i.e., if
d=2
, then

2\leq p\leq+\infty
, and if
d\geq 3
, then
2\leq p<\frac{2(d-1)}{d-3}
.
In any case,

|\int_{0}^{T}\langle e^{it\,D}g\,,\;h(t)\rangle\,dt|\lesssim\|g\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}\|\cdot\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|h(t)\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{-r}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\|^{m}\right)^{1/m}

(D.12)

Hence, by duality,

\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{itD}g\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,q}\|^{\frac{4p}{(d-1)(p-2)}}\right)^{\frac{(d-1)(p-2)}{4p}}\lesssim\|g\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{\theta}\|\,,

(D.13)

where the exponent
\frac{4p}{(d-1)(p-2)}
is the conjugate of
m
. This proves estimate (
D.4
) for the homogeneous equation (
D.1
). The estimate on
\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)D}f(\tau)\,d\tau
is obtained as in the second part of the proof of Theorem 2 in
[
17
]

(with necessary slight modifications).

∎

Appendix E
Norms in Euler and Lagrange coordinates

In this section
\xi\to x(\xi)
is a
C^{1}
volume preserving diffeomorphism of the form
x(\xi)=A\xi+\varphi(\xi)
satisfying the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
. In particular,
A
is a constant
SL(d,{\mathbb{R}})
matrix and
\varphi\in H^{s+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\xi})
with
s>d/2
.
This transformation from the Lagrangian coordinates,
\xi
, to the Eulerian coordinates,
x
, pushes back the functions
f(x)
to the functions
{\tilde{f}}(\xi)=f(x(\xi))
. This is a linear isometry from
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},dx)
to
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\xi)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
. To analyze other norms, we use the superscripts
L
and
E
on functions to indicate the coordinate system used. Denote by
v^{i}_{a}(x)
the entries of the Jacobian matrix,
\partial x/\partial\xi
, expressed in Eulerian coordinates, i.e., as functions of
x
. We have
v^{i}_{a}(x)=A^{i}_{a}+u^{i}_{a}(x)
, where
u^{i}_{a}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{x})
.
Notation
v_{a}
or
u_{a}
is used to represent a generic
v_{a}
or
u_{a}
, or when in a norm,
the maximal over
a
norm, e.g.,
\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}=\max_{a}\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}
. For the norms in
L^{p}
we skip the superscripts
E
and
L
.

E.1
Homogenous Besov and Sobolev spaces

The following proposition contains inequalities between the homogeneous Besov norms
\{g\}_{r,p}
and between the homogeneous
L^{2}
Sobolev norms
[g]_{\theta}
in the Euler and Lagrange coordinates. The range of
r,p
, and
\theta
is restricted to the demands of the main body of the paper. All the spaces are over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.

Lemma E.1
.

a)

Assume
0<r<1
,
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, and
2\leq p\leq\infty
. Then, for all
g\in{\cal S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\{g^{L}\}_{r,p}\lesssim\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}^{r}\;\{g^{E}\}_{r,p}\,,\quad

\displaystyle\{g^{E}\}_{r,p}\lesssim\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}^{(d-1)r}\;\{g^{L}\}_{r,p}\,,

(E.1)

\displaystyle[g^{L}]_{\theta}\lesssim\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}^{\theta}\;[g^{E}]_{\theta}\,,\quad

\displaystyle[g^{E}]_{\theta}\lesssim\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}^{(d-1)\theta}\;[g^{L}]_{\theta}

(E.2)

b)

Assume
1<\theta<2
, then

[g^{L}]_{\theta}\lesssim\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}^{\theta-1}\;\left(\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}+\|u_{a}\|_{\infty}^{2-\theta}\;\|u_{a}\,\big|\;{\dot{F}}^{1}_{d,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|^{\theta-1}\right)\;[g^{E}]_{\theta}\,.

(E.3)

Proof.

We first prove the inequalities for Sobolev norms in part a). Compare the norms
\|g^{L}\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\xi})\|
and
\|g^{E}\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{x})\|
. We have

\displaystyle\|g^{L}\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\xi})\|^{2}={}

\displaystyle\int|\nabla_{\xi}{\tilde{g}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\int|\frac{\partial f(t,x(\xi))}{\partial x^{j}}\;\frac{\partial x^{j}(\xi)}{\partial\xi}|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\|v_{a}(t)\|_{\infty}^{2}\;\int|\frac{\partial g(x(\xi))}{\partial x^{j}}|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\|v_{a}(t)\|_{\infty}^{2}\;\int|\frac{\partial g(x)}{\partial x^{j}}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\|v_{a}(t)\|_{\infty}^{2}\;\|g^{E}\;\big|\;{\dot{H}}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{x})\|^{2}\,.

If
0<\theta<1
, then
{\dot{H}}^{\theta}
is an interpolation space between
L^{2}
and
{\dot{H}}^{1}
,

{\dot{H}}^{\theta}=(L^{2},{\dot{H}}^{1})_{\theta,2}
(see
[
2
, Theorem 6.3.1]
). This explains why
[g^{L}]_{\theta}\lesssim\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}^{\theta}\,[g^{E}]_{\theta}
. Similarly,

[g^{E}]_{\theta}\lesssim\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}^{(d-1)\theta}\,[g^{L}]_{\theta}
, where
(d-1)
appears because
of the
L^{\infty}
bound on
\partial\xi^{a}/\partial x^{i}
in terms of
\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}
.

The Besov norm inequalities in part a) can be obtained by interpolation between the inequalities for
{\dot{B}}^{r}_{2,2}\simeq{\dot{H}}^{r}
, which we already have, and the inequalities for
{\dot{B}}^{r}_{\infty,\infty}\simeq{\dot{C}}^{r}
, the homogeneous Hölder spaces. For the Hölder seminorms we have

\displaystyle\{g^{L}\}_{r}={}

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}\frac{|g^{L}(\xi)-g^{L}(\xi^{\prime})|}{|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|^{r}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}\frac{|g^{E}(x(\xi))-g^{E}(x(\xi^{\prime}))|}{|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|^{r}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}\frac{|g^{E}(x(\xi))-g^{E}(x(\xi^{\prime}))|}{|x(\xi)-x(\xi^{\prime})|^{r}}\;\frac{|x(\xi)-x(\xi^{\prime})|^{r}}{|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\sup_{x,x^{\prime}}\frac{|g^{E}(x)-g^{E}(x^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{r}}\;\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}^{r}\,.

Thus,
\{g^{L}\}_{r}\leq\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}^{r}\,\{g^{E}\}_{r}
.
The norm
\{g^{E}\}_{r}
is bounded similarly.

Now turn to part b). Assume
1<\theta<2
and proceed with

\displaystyle[g^{L}]_{\theta}\simeq{}

\displaystyle[\frac{\partial g^{L}}{\partial\xi}]_{\theta-1}

\displaystyle\underset{\eqref{ineq1}}{\lesssim}{}

\displaystyle\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}^{\theta-1}\,[\left(\frac{\partial g^{L}}{\partial\xi}\right)^{E}]_{\theta-1}

\displaystyle\simeq{}

\displaystyle\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}^{\theta-1}\,[\frac{\partial g^{E}}{\partial x}\cdot\frac{\partial x}{\partial\xi}]_{\theta-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}^{\theta-1}\;[\frac{\partial g^{E}}{\partial x}\cdot v_{a}]_{\theta-1}\,.

(E.4)

Apply the fractional product rule (
B.1
):

[\frac{\partial g^{E}}{\partial x}\cdot v_{a}^{E}]_{\theta-1}\lesssim[\frac{\partial g^{E}}{\partial x}]_{\theta-1}\,\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}+\|\frac{\partial g^{E}}{\partial x}\|_{q_{1}}\;\|v_{a}^{E}\,\big|\;{\dot{F}}^{\theta-1}_{q_{2},2}\|\,.

Note that the homogeneous Lizorkin-Triebel norms of
v_{a}
and
u_{a}
are the same. So, we have

[\frac{\partial g^{E}}{\partial x}\cdot v_{a}^{E}]_{\theta-1}\lesssim[\frac{\partial g^{E}}{\partial x}]_{\theta-1}\,\|v_{a}\|_{\infty}+\|\frac{\partial g^{E}}{\partial x}\|_{q_{1}}\;\|u_{a}^{E}\,\big|\;{\dot{F}}^{\theta-1}_{q_{2},2}\|\,.

(E.5)

The parameters
q_{1}
and
q_{2}

must satisfy
2\leq q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
and

\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\,.

We choose
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
as follows

\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\theta-1}{d}\,,\quad\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{\theta-1}{d}\,.

(E.6)

Then
{\dot{H}}^{\theta-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subset L^{q_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and so

\|\frac{\partial g^{E}}{\partial x}\|_{q_{1}}\lesssim[\frac{\partial g^{E}}{\partial x}]_{\theta-1}\lesssim[g^{E}]_{\theta}\,.

As for the other factor, use (
A.12
):

\|u_{a}^{E}\,\big|\;{\dot{F}}^{\theta-1}_{q_{2},2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|\lesssim\|u_{a}\|_{\infty}^{2-\theta}\;\|u_{a}^{E}\,\big|\;{\dot{F}}^{1}_{d,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|^{\theta-1}\,.

Collecting the pieces we arrive at (
E.3
).

∎

E.2
Vorticities

Let
v
be a vectorfield on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\hbox{div}\,v=0
. Denote
\omega^{mn}=\partial_{m}v^{n}-\partial_{n}v^{m}
.
In terms of Fourier transform,

{\hat{\omega}}^{mn}=i\,\left(\kappa^{m}{\hat{v}}^{n}-\kappa^{n}{\hat{v}}^{m}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa^{n}{\hat{v}}^{n}=0\,.

From
\omega^{mn}
one recovers
v
as follows:

{\hat{v}}^{n}=-i\;\frac{\kappa^{m}}{|\kappa|^{2}}\,{\hat{\omega}}^{mn}\,.

In our notation,

v^{n}=-iD^{-1}{\cal R}_{m}\,\omega^{mn}\,.

In dimension
d=2
,
\omega=\partial_{1}v^{2}-\partial_{2}v^{1}
and

{\hat{v}}^{1}=i\,\frac{\kappa^{2}}{|\kappa|^{2}}\,{\hat{\omega}}\,,\quad{\hat{v}}^{2}=-i\,\frac{\kappa^{1}}{|\kappa|^{2}}\,{\hat{\omega}}\,.

We also have pseudovelocities
v_{a}
with the components
v^{i}_{a}=u^{i}_{a}+A^{i}_{a}
, and the corresponding pseudovorticities

\omega_{a}=\partial_{1}v^{2}_{a}-\partial_{2}v^{1}_{a}=\partial_{1}u^{2}_{a}-\partial_{2}u^{1}_{a}
.

Lemma E.2
.

1.

In the case
d=2
,
assume that
u\in L^{2}
and
\omega=\mathop{\rm curl}u\in{\dot{C}}^{r}
for some
r\in(0,1)
. Then
u\in L^{\infty}
and
\nabla u\in L^{\infty}
, and the following inequalities are true:

\|u\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|u\|^{(r+1)/(r+2)}\;\{\omega\}_{r}^{1/(r+2)}

(E.7)

and

\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|u\|^{r/(r+2)}\;\{\omega\}_{r}^{2/(r+2)}\,.

(E.8)

2.

In the case
d=2
,
assume that
u\in L^{2}
and
\omega=\mathop{\rm curl}u\in{\dot{H}}^{\theta}
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then
u\in L^{\infty}
and the following inequality is true:

\|u\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|u\|^{\theta/(\theta+1))}\;[\omega]_{\theta}^{1/(\theta+1)}\;.

(E.9)

If
\omega\in{\dot{H}}^{\theta}
with
\theta>1
, then
\nabla u\in L^{\infty}
and

\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|u\|^{(\theta-1)/(\theta+1)}\;[\omega]_{\theta}^{2/(\theta+1)}\,.

(E.10)

3.

In the case
d\geq 3
, assume that
u\in L^{2}
and
\omega\in{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,p}
, where

r\in(0,1),\;r>\frac{d}{p},\;\;1\leq p\leq\infty.

Then
u\in L^{\infty}
,
\nabla u\in L^{\infty}
, and

\|u\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|u\|^{(r+1-d/p)/(r+1-d/p+d/2)}\;\|\omega\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,p}\|^{(d/2)/(r+1-d/p+d/2)}

(E.11)

and

\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|u\|^{(r-d/p)/(r+1-d/p+d/2)}\;\|\omega\;\big|\;{\dot{B}}^{r}_{p,p}\|^{(1+d/2)/(r+1-d/p+d/2)}\,.

(E.12)

4.

In the case
d\geq 3
assume that
u\in L^{2}
and
\omega=\mathop{\rm curl}\,u\in{\dot{H}}^{\theta}
. If
\theta>\frac{d}{2}-1
, then
u\in L^{\infty}
and

\|u\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|u\|^{(\theta+1-d/2)/(\theta+1)}\;[\omega]_{\theta}^{d/2/(\theta+1)}\,.

(E.13)

If
\theta>\frac{d}{2}
, then
\nabla u\in L^{\infty}
and

\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|u\|^{(\theta-d/2)/(\theta+1)}\,[\omega]_{\theta}^{(d/2+1)/(\theta+1)}\,.

(E.14)

The inequalities follow immediately from Lemma
C.1
part 2 and the observation that
[\omega]_{-1}\simeq\|u\|
and

\nabla u\simeq{\cal R}\omega
.

Abstract
. We consider initial boundary value problems of time-fractional advection-diffusion equations with the zero Dirichlet boundary value ∂ t α u ⁡ ( x , t ) = − A ​ u ​ ( x , t ) \partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=-Au(x,t) , where − A = ∑ i , j = 1 d ∂ i ( a i ​ j ( x ) ∂ j ) + ∑ j = 1 d b j ( x ) ∂ j + c ( x ) -A=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a_{ij}(x)\partial_{j})+\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(x)\partial_{j}+c(x) . We establish the uniqueness for an inverse problem of determining an order α \alpha of fractional derivatives by data u ⁡ ( x 0 , t ) u(x_{0},t) for 0 < t < T 0<t<T at one point x 0 x_{0} in a spatial domain Ω \Omega . The uniqueness holds even under assumption that Ω \Omega and A A are unknown, provided that the initial value does not change signs and is not identically zero. The proof is based on the eigenfunction expansions of finitely dimensional approximating solutions, a decay estimate and the asymptotic expansions of the Mittag-Leffler functions for large time. Introduction

Throughout this article, we assume that the spatial dimensions
d=1,2,3
.
We can similarly argue for
higher dimensions
d\geq 4

Let

\partial\Omega

By

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}g(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\frac{d}{ds}g(s)ds

\alpha\in(0,1)
(e.g., Podlubny
[
25

Throughout this article, we set

[formula]

(-Av)(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a_{ij}(x)\partial_{j}v(x))+\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(x)\partial_{j}v(x)+c(x)v(x),\quad x\in\Omega,

where
a_{ij}=a_{ji}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})

[formula]

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}\geq\sigma(a_{ij})\sum_{i=1}^{d}\zeta_{i}^{2}\quad\mbox{for all $x\in\overline{\Omega}$ and $\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{d}\in\mathbb{R}$}.

advection-diffusion equation:

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=-Au(x,t),\quad x\in\Omega,\,0<t<T,\\
&u|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}=0,\\
&u(x,0)=a(x),\quad x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

[formula]

C(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}dx\quad\mbox{for $w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)$}.

-A

[formula]

(-\widetilde{A}v)(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(\widetilde{a_{ij}}(x)\partial_{j}v(x))+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\widetilde{b_{j}}(x)\partial_{j}v(x)+\widetilde{c}(x)v(x),\quad x\in\Omega,

where
\widetilde{a_{ij}}=\widetilde{a_{ji}}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
,

\widetilde{b_{j}}\in C^{1}(\Omega)
,
1\leq i,j\leq d
,
\widetilde{c}\in C(\overline{\Omega})

are all real-valued.
Moreover we assume that
\widetilde{c}(x)\leq 0

, and
there exists a constant
\sigma=\sigma(\widetilde{a_{ij}})>0

[formula]

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\widetilde{a_{ij}}(x)\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}\geq\sigma(\widetilde{a_{ij}})\sum_{i=1}^{d}\zeta_{i}^{2}\quad\mbox{for all $x\in\overline{\Omega}$ and $\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{d}\in\mathbb{R}$}.

We consider an initial boundary value problem for a time-fractional
advection-diffusion equation:

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\beta}\widetilde{u}(x,t)=-\widetilde{A}\widetilde{u}(x,t),\quad x\in\widetilde{\Omega},\,0<t<T,\\
&\widetilde{u}|_{\partial\widetilde{\Omega}\times(0,T)}=0,\\
&\widetilde{u}(x,0)=\widetilde{a}(x),\quad x\in\widetilde{\Omega}.\end{array}\right.

We define an operator
A
in

attaching the zero Dirichlet

\mathcal{D}(A)
of
A
is

, and the same for
\widetilde{A}

\mathcal{D}(\widetilde{A}):=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

Then it is known that the spectrum

[
2
]
).
With some numbering, we can set

\sigma(A)=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{C},\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\mbox{Re}\,\lambda_{n}=\infty.

We note
\lambda_{n}\not\in\mathbb{R}
in general. Similarly we set

\sigma(\widetilde{A})=\{\widetilde{\lambda_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

We further asssume

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\inf_{x\in\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}b_{j}(x)-c(x)\right)+C(\Omega)\sigma(a_{ij})>0,\quad c(x)\leq 0,\quad x\in\overline{\Omega},\\
&\inf_{x\in\widetilde{\Omega}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}\widetilde{b_{j}}(x)-\widetilde{c}(x)\right)+C(\widetilde{\Omega})\sigma(\widetilde{a_{ij}})>0,\quad\widetilde{c}(x)\leq 0,\quad x\in\overline{\widetilde{\Omega}}.\end{array}\right.

Condition (1.8) yields

[formula]

\{\mbox{Re}\,\lambda_{n};\,\lambda_{n}\in\sigma(A)\}>0,\quad\{\mbox{Re}\,\widetilde{\lambda_{n}};\,\widetilde{\lambda_{n}}\in\sigma(\widetilde{A})\}>0.

For completeness, we verify (1.9) in Appendix.

In this article, for simplicity, we do not discuss the case where there exist
eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
such that Re
\lambda_{n}<0
.

Theorem.

Let
0<\alpha,\beta<1
and

\Omega\cap\widetilde{\Omega}\neq\emptyset
, and
x_{0}\in\Omega\cap\widetilde{\Omega}
,
T>0
be arbitrarily chosen. We assume that
each of initial values
a\in\mathcal{D}(A)
and
\widetilde{a}\in\mathcal{D}(\widetilde{A})
does
not change
signs in
\Omega
and
\widetilde{\Omega}
respectively, and

[formula]

\mbox{either $a\not\equiv 0$ in $\Omega$ or $\widetilde{a}\not\equiv 0$
in $\widetilde{\Omega}$}.

If

u(x_{0},t)=\widetilde{u}(x_{0},t),\quad 0<t<T,

then
\alpha=\beta
.

By Lemma 1 in Section 2, we see that
u(x_{0},\cdot)\in C(0,\infty)
and so

u(x_{0},t)
makes sense for
t>0
.

Remark.

From the proof in Section 3, we can see the following:

Let

[formula]

each of

and

2.2. Spectral decomposition and representation of solution.

It is sufficient to argue for the operator
A
in

,

We recall that
A
is defined by (1.1) with
\mathcal{D}(A)=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and satisfies (1.2), and that
the spectrum
\sigma(A)
consists only of isolated eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
with

and that

is a unique accumulation point
(e.g., Agmon
[
2
]
). For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we take

\gamma_{n}
centered at
\lambda_{n}
with sufficiently small radius
such that
\gamma_{n}
does not enclose
\lambda_{m}
with any

.
We define

[formula]

P_{n}:=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\int_{\gamma_{n}}(z-A)^{-1}dz

and then we have

[formula]

P_{n}P_{m}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}0,&n\neq m,\\
P_{n},&n=m\end{array}\right.

(e.g., Kato
[
13

).
We call
P_{n}
the eigenprojection for
\lambda_{n}
and
\varphi\in P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\neq 0
a generalized eigenfunction.

We see that dim
P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega)<\infty
, and we set

[formula]

i_{n}:=\mbox{dim}\,P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega)<\infty.

Then

[formula]

(A-\lambda_{n})^{i_{n}}P_{n}=0

(e.g.,
[
13
]
). We note that if
A
is symmetric, then
\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{R}

P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega)=\mbox{Ker}\,(\lambda_{n}-A)
and

i_{n}=\mbox{dim}\,\mbox{Ker}\,(\lambda_{n}-A)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

More we set

[formula]

D_{n}:=(\lambda_{n}-A)P_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

By (2.1) and (2.2), we prove
P_{n}\varphi=\varphi
for
\varphi\in P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega)
,
and

[formula]

P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{D}(A),\quad AP_{n}L^{2}(\Omega)\subset P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega),\quad D_{n}P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega)\subset P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega),\quad D_{n}^{i_{n}}=0.

Moreover since

exists

[formula]

A^{-1}\varphi=\sum_{k=0}^{i_{n}-1}\frac{D_{n}^{k}}{\lambda_{n}^{k+1}}\varphi\in P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for all $\varphi\in P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega)$}.

Indeed (2.5) yields

\displaystyle A\left(\sum_{k=0}^{i_{n}-1}\frac{D_{n}^{k}}{\lambda_{n}^{k+1}}\varphi\right)=(\lambda_{n}-D_{n})\left(\sum_{k=0}^{i_{n}-1}\frac{D_{n}^{k}}{\lambda_{n}^{k+1}}\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=

We state the completeness of the generalized eigenfunctions of

.

Lemma 3.

(i) For any
a\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, there exists a sequence

a_{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

a_{N}\in\sum_{k=1}^{N}P_{k}L^{2}(\Omega),\quad\lim_{N\to\infty}\|a_{N}-a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

(ii) For any
a\in\mathcal{D}(A)
, there exists a sequence

a_{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

a_{N}\in\sum_{k=1}^{N}P_{k}L^{2}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{D}(A),\quad\lim_{N\to\infty}\|Aa_{N}-Aa\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

In particular,

[formula]

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|a_{N}-a\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}=0.

Proof.

Since the linear subspace spanned by all the generalized
eigenfunctions of
A
is dense in
L^{2}(\Omega)
(e.g.,
[
2
]
), we see
part (i). Next let
a\in\mathcal{D}(A)
. Then
Aa\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
By (i) we can choose a sequence
b_{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

b_{N}\in\sum_{k=1}^{N}P_{k}L^{2}(\Omega),\quad\lim_{N\to\infty}\|b_{N}-Aa\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

Since

, we see that
A^{-1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
exists and is bounded, and

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|A(A^{-1}b_{N})-Aa\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

By (2.7), we see that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|Aa_{N}-Aa\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

Therefore, in view of

\blacksquare

We can interpret (2.9) as

E_{\alpha,1}(-(AP_{n})t^{\alpha})a=E_{\alpha,1}((-\lambda_{n}+D_{n})t^{\alpha})a=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}(-\lambda_{n}+D_{n})^{k}a,\quad t>0,

We set
A_{n}:=AP_{n}
. Then
A_{n}:P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega)\longrightarrow P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega)

\|A_{n}\|_{B(P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega))}=:\rho<\infty,

of

The Sobolev embedding yields

\|a\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\|Aa\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=C\|A_{n}a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\rho\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for all

Moreover

\|A_{n}^{k}a\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\|A_{n}^{k+1}a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|A_{n}\|_{B(P_{n}L^{2}(\Omega))}^{k+1}\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\rho^{k+1}\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\left(\frac{t^{\alpha k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}\right)\right|\|(-A_{n})^{k}a\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha(k-1)}}{\Gamma(1+(k-1)\alpha)}C\rho^{k+1}\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle C\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rho^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\rho t^{\alpha})^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}<\infty,

noting that

E_{\alpha,1}(\rho t^{\alpha})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\rho t^{\alpha})^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}

and
E_{\alpha,1}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}
is an entire

Therefore, we can justify

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{n}(x,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\left(\frac{t^{\alpha k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}\right)(-A_{n})^{k}a=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha(k-1)}}{\Gamma(1+(k-1)\alpha)}(-A_{n})^{k}a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-A_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}(-A_{n})^{k}a=-A_{n}u_{n}(x,t)

That is,

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a_{ij}(x)\partial_{j}\varphi(x))-\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(x)\partial_{j}\varphi-c(x)\varphi=\lambda\varphi\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$},\\
&\varphi|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{array}\right.

Henceforth let
\overline{\lambda}
denote the complex conjugate of

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Since
a_{ij}
,
b_{j}
and
c
are real-valued, we have

[formula]

-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a_{ij}(x)\partial_{j}\overline{\varphi}(x))-\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(x)\partial_{j}\overline{\varphi}-c(x)\overline{\varphi}=\overline{\lambda}\overline{\varphi}\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$}.

Multiplying (4.1) and (4.2) with
\overline{\varphi}
and
\varphi
respectively,
integrating by parts in
\Omega
and adding, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}((\partial_{i}\varphi)\overline{\partial_{j}\varphi}+(\overline{\partial_{i}\varphi})\partial_{j}\varphi)dx-\int_{\Omega}b_{j}((\partial_{j}\varphi)\overline{\varphi}+(\overline{\partial_{j}\varphi})\varphi)dx

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}2c|\varphi|^{2}dx=(\lambda+\overline{\lambda})\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{2}dx.

On the other hand,

((\partial_{i}\varphi)\overline{\partial_{j}\varphi}+\overline{\partial_{i}\varphi}\partial_{j}\varphi)=2(\mbox{Re}\,(\partial_{i}\varphi)\mbox{Re}\,(\partial_{j}\varphi)+\mbox{Im}\,(\partial_{i}\varphi)\mbox{Im}\,(\partial_{j}\varphi))

and so (1.2) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}((\partial_{i}\varphi)\overline{\partial_{j}\varphi}+\overline{\partial_{i}\varphi}\partial_{j}\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}\mbox{Re}\,(\partial_{i}\varphi)\mbox{Re}\,(\partial_{j}\varphi)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}\mbox{Im}\,(\partial_{i}\varphi)\mbox{Im}\,(\partial_{j}\varphi)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\sigma(a_{ij})\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(|\mbox{Re}\,(\partial_{i}\varphi)|^{2}+|\mbox{Im}\,(\partial_{i}\varphi)|^{2})=2\sigma|\nabla\varphi|^{2}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}((\partial_{j}\varphi)\overline{\varphi}+\overline{(\partial_{j}\varphi)}\varphi)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}\partial_{j}(|\varphi|^{2})dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}b_{j}\right)|\varphi|^{2}dx.

Hence, using also (1.4), we have

\displaystyle 2\sigma C(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}\left(\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}b_{j}\right)-2c(x)\right)|\varphi|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sigma\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}b_{j}\right)|\varphi|^{2}dx-2\int_{\Omega}c(x)|\varphi(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\lambda+\overline{\lambda})\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{2}dx=2(\mbox{Re}\,\lambda)\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{2}dx.

In view of (1.8), we see

2\sigma C(\Omega)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}b_{j}(x)-2c(x)>0\quad\mbox{for all $x\in\overline{\Omega}$}.

Thus Re
\lambda>0
follows and we complete the proof of (1.9).

\blacksquare

4.2. Proof of Proposition 1.

For the case of symmetric
A
, in terms of the eigenfunction expansions
by the Mittag-Leffler functions, we can directly prove the proposition
(e.g., Sakamoto and Yamamoto
[
28
]
). However, for non-symmetric
A
,
we have to take other way.
We make use of the argument by Vergara and Zacher
[
31
]
which is the
first work proving Proposition 1 (i) for a case
where the eigenfunction expansion does not work, that is,

A
is symmetric but the coefficients are time dependent.
For the proof of Proposition 1, relying on Chapter 5 in
[
16
]
which
modifies
[
31
]
, we here give a sketch of the proof of Proposition 1.
We can mostly repeat the argument in
[
16
]
and so we
describe the main differences.

For the proof, we have to return to the construction of the solution
u
to
(1.3) by the Galerkin method. More precisely, let
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be
an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\Omega)
which consists of all the eigenfunctions
of
-\Delta
with the zero Dirichlet boundary condition. We construct
approximating solutions to
u
in the form of

[formula]

u_{N}(x,t):=\sum_{k=1}^{N}p_{k}^{N}(t)\psi_{k}(x),\quad N\in\mathbb{N}

such that

[formula]

u_{N}(x,0)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}(u,\psi_{k})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\psi_{k}(x)

and

(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{N}+Au_{N},\,\psi_{\ell})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0,\quad 1\leq\ell\leq N,\quad t>0,

which is rewritten as

\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{N}(x,t)\psi_{\ell}(x)dx+\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)(\partial_{j}u_{N})(x,t)\partial_{i}\psi_{\ell}(x)dx

[formula]

-\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(\partial_{j}u_{N})\psi_{\ell}(x)dx-\int_{\Omega}c(x)u_{N}(x,t)\psi_{\ell}(x)dx=0.

Equation (4.4) is equivalent to a system of time-fractional ordinary
differential equations in
\{p_{k}^{N}(t)\}_{1\leq k\leq N}
.
Such
p_{k}^{N}
exist uniquely and
u_{N}
converges to
the solution
u
weakly in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
. We can refer to e.g.,
Chapter 4 in
[
16
]
, and we omit the details.

Multiplying (4.5) with
p_{\ell}^{N}(t)
and adding over
\ell=1,...,N
,
and integrating by parts the third term on the
left-hand side, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{N}(x,t))u_{N}(x,t)dx+\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)(\partial_{j}u_{N})(x,t)\partial_{i}u_{N}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}b_{j}\right)|u_{N}|^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}c(x)|u_{N}(x,t)|^{2}dx=0,\quad t>0.

By (1.2) and (1.4), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{N}(x,t))u_{N}(x,t)dx+C(\Omega)\sigma(a_{ij})\int_{\Omega}|u_{N}(x,t)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}b_{j}\right)|u_{N}|^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}c(x)|u_{N}(x,t)|^{2}dx\leq 0,\quad t>0.

Applying (1.8), we can choose a constant
\mu_{0}>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{N}(x,t))u_{N}(x,t)dx+\mu_{0}\|u_{N}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 0,\quad t>0\quad\mbox{for all $N\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Hence Lemma 5.1 in
[
16
]
yields

[formula]

\|u_{N}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\|u_{N}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq-\mu_{0}\|u_{N}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 0,\quad t>0.

Then, we can formally argue as follows. By setting
d_{N}(t):=\|u_{N}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and assuming
that
d_{N}(t)\neq 0
for all
t>0
, inequality (4.6) yields

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}d_{N}(t)\leq-\mu_{0}d_{N}(t),\quad t>0,\\
&d_{N}(0)=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}(a,\psi_{k})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\psi_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{array}\right.

Noting that

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}E_{\alpha,1}(-\mu_{0}t^{\alpha})=-\mu_{0}E_{\alpha,1}(-\mu_{0}t^{\alpha}),\quad E_{\alpha,1}(0)=1,

we apply the comparison principle (e.g., Luchko and Yamamoto
[
21
]
) to
obtain

d_{N}(t)\leq d_{N}(0)E_{\alpha,1}(-\mu_{0}t^{\alpha}),\quad t>0.

Therefore, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

d_{N}(t)\leq\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}E_{\alpha,1}(-\mu_{0}t^{\alpha})\leq\frac{C\|a\|_{L^{(}\Omega)}}{t^{\alpha}},\quad t>0,

where we use

|E_{\alpha,1}(-\mu_{0}t^{\alpha})|\leq\frac{C}{1+\mu_{0}t^{\alpha}},\quad t>0

(e.g., Theorem 1.6 (p.35) in
[
25
]
). This is an outline and for example,
but we need to justify in deriving (4.7) by dividing with

\|u_{N}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, because it may vanish at some
t
.
Such details are still the same as the proof of Theorem 5.1 of Chapter 5
in
[
16
]
, and so we omit.

Finally we have to prove part (ii). Let
d=1,2,3
. By
a\in\mathcal{D}(A)
,
we consider

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}U=-AU(x,t),\quad x\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\
&U|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty)}=0,\\
&U(x,0)=-Aa(x),\quad x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

There exists a unique solution
U\in C([0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
and
applying part (i) to
U
, we have

\|U(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C}{t^{\alpha}}\|Aa\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad t>0.

Moreover the uniqueness of the solution yields
U=\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u
in

\Omega\times(0,\infty)
. Therefore
U=-Au
in
\Omega\times(0,\infty)
,
and so

\|Au(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C}{t^{\alpha}}\|Aa\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad t>0.

Since
d=1,2,3
, the Sobolev embedding implies

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\leq\frac{C}{t^{\alpha}}\|Aa\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad t>0.

Thus the proof of Proposition 1 (ii) is complete.

\blacksquare

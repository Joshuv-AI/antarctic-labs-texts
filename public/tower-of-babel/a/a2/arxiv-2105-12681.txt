On the linearization of infinite-dimensional random dynamical systems
Lucas Backes, Davor Dragičević

Abstract
. We present a new version of the Grobman-Hartman’s linearization theorem for random dynamics. Our result holds for infinite dimensional systems whose linear part is not necessarily invertible. In addition, by adding some restrictions on the non-linear perturbations, we don’t require for the linear part to be nonuniformly hyperbolic in the sense of Pesin but rather (besides requiring the existence of stable and unstable directions) allow for the existence of a third (central) direction on which we don’t prescribe any behaviour for the dynamics. Moreover, under some additional nonuniform growth condition, we prove that the conjugacies given by the linearization procedure are Hölder continuous when restricted to bounded subsets of the space.

1.
Introduction

The classical Grobman-Hartman theorem represents one of the cornerstones of the modern dynamical systems theory. It asserts that if
x_{0}
is a hyperbolic fixed point of a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
f
is on a neighborhood of
x_{0}
topologically conjugated to
Df(x_{0})
.
The original references for this results are the papers of Grobman
[
16
]
and Hartman
[
17
]
. This result was extended to the case of Banach spaces independently by Palis
[
21
]
and Pugh
[
23
]
, who also simplified the original arguments of Grobman and
Hartman. It is well-known that even if
f
is
C^{\infty}
, the conjugacy can fail to be even locally Lipschitz. Indeed, it turns out that in general the conjugacy is only locally Hölder continuous
[
24
]
. The first versions of the Grobman-Hartman theorem for nonautonomous dynamics were established by
Palmer
[
22
]
in the case of continuous time and by Aulbach and Wanner
[
2
]
in the case of discrete time.

In the context of random dynamical systems, the first version of the Grobman-Hartman theorem was proved by Wanner
[
25
]
. It represents an important contribution to the study of the qualitative behaviour of random dynamical systems which are used as models of many phenomena with some degree of noise
(including stochastic differential equations). For a detailed exposition of the theory of random dynamical systems we refer to
[
1
]
. For certain extensions of the original approach developed by Wanner, we refer to
[
8
,
11
,
12
,
26
]
and references therein.
In
[
9
]
, Barreira and Valls formulated sufficient conditions under which the conjugacies in the random version of the Grobman-Hartman theorem are locally Hölder continuous. The higher regularity of conjugacies was discussed in the important works of Li and Lu
[
18
]
and
Lu, Zhang and Zhang
[
20
]
.

The goal of the present paper is to make further contributions to the linearization theory of random dynamical systems. In sharp contrast to the existing results in the literature, our first main result (see Theorem
1
) gives a random version of the Grobman-Hartman theorem such that the linear part of our dynamics
is not necessarily invertible (although the associated perturbed dynamics needs to be invertible). In addition, we don’t require for the linear part to be nonuniformly hyperbolic in the sense of Pesin but rather (besides requiring the existence of stable and unstable directions) allow for the existence of a third (central) direction on which we don’t prescribe any behaviour for the linear part.
On the other hand, we stress that our result doesn’t allow any type of perturbations along this central direction. Consequently, to the best of our knowledge, our Theorem
1
represents the first version of the Grobman-Hartman theorem in the setting when the linear part of the dynamics can have zero Lyapunov exponents (assuming that a suitable version of the multiplicative ergodic theorem can be applied to it).

In our second main result (see Theorem
2
), we formulate sufficient conditions under which the conjugacies given by Theorem
1
are Hölder continuous. When compared with the main result in
[
9
]
, our Theorem
2
is more general and sharper since for instance, we deal with the infinite-dimensional dynamics where a multiplicative ergodic theorem is not always available and, moreover, we don’t require for the linear part to be hyperbolic.

Our techniques build on our recent work with K. Palmer
[
7
]
(see also
[
5
]
), where we obtained similar results in the context of nonautonomous dynamics. However, we stress that the results in the present paper require a nontrivial adjustments of the ideas from
[
7
]
that now need to be combined with
certain ergodic theory tools.

2.
Linearization of random dynamical systems

Let
X=(X,|\cdot|)
be an arbitrary Banach space and let
\mathcal{B}(X)
denote the space of all bounded linear operators on
X
. The norm on
\mathcal{B}(X)
will be denoted by
\lVert\cdot\rVert
.
Furthermore, let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and let
\sigma\colon\Omega\to\Omega
be an invertible
\mathbb{P}
-preserving measurable transformation. For the sake of simplicity of the presentation we will assume that
\mathbb{P}
is ergodic (see Remark
6
for the discussion regarding the general case).

We recall that a measurable map
K\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty)
is said to be
tempered
if:

\lim_{n\to\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log K(\sigma^{n}(\omega))=0,\quad\text{for $\mathbb{P}$-a.e. $\omega\in\Omega$.}

(1)

Let
A\colon\Omega\to\mathcal{B}(X)
be a strongly measurable map. We recall that this means that the map
\omega\mapsto A(\omega)x
is measurable for each
x\in X
. Let
\mathcal{A}\colon\Omega\times\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\to\mathcal{B}(X)
be the
linear cocycle
generated by
A
. We recall that
\mathcal{A}
is given by

\mathcal{A}(\omega,n)=\begin{cases}\text{\rm Id}&\text{if $n=0$;}\\
A(\sigma^{n-1}(\omega))\cdots A(\omega)&\text{if $n>0$,}\end{cases}

for
\omega\in\Omega
and
n\in\{0,1,\ldots\}
. Here,
Id
denotes the identity operator on
X
.

We now introduce some assumptions related to
\mathcal{A}
. Namely, we will assume the following:

•

there exist three families of projections
(\Pi^{i}(\omega))_{\omega\in\Omega}
,
i\in\{1,2,3\}
on
X
such that

\text{\rm Id}=\Pi^{1}(\omega)+\Pi^{2}(\omega)+\Pi^{3}(\omega),\quad\text{for $\mathbb{P}$-a.e. $\omega\in\Omega$.}

In addition, for each
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, the map
\omega\to\Pi^{i}(\omega)
is strongly measurable;

•

for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
and
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
,

A(\omega)\Pi^{i}(\omega)=\Pi^{i}(\sigma(\omega))A(\omega);

(2)

•

for
i\in\{2,3\}
and for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
,

A(\omega)\rvert_{\Ima\Pi^{i}(\omega)}\colon\Ima\Pi^{i}(\omega)\to\Ima\Pi^{i}(\sigma(\omega))\quad\text{is invertible;}

(3)

•

there exist
\lambda>0
and a tempered random variable
K\colon\Omega\to[1,\infty)
such that for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and
n\geq 0

\lVert\mathcal{A}(\omega,n)\Pi^{1}(\omega)\rVert\leq K(\omega)e^{-\lambda n}

(4)

and

\lVert\mathcal{A}(\omega,-n)\Pi^{3}(\omega)\rVert\leq K(\omega)e^{-\lambda n},

(5)

where

\mathcal{A}(\omega,-n):=\bigg(\mathcal{A}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)\rvert_{\Ima\Pi^{3}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega))}\bigg)^{-1}\colon\Ima\Pi^{3}(\omega)\to\Ima\Pi^{3}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega)).

Remark 1
.

We would like to point out that the above assumptions are natural in the context of the ergodic theory. Let
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and assume that

\int_{\Omega}\log^{+}\|A(\omega)\|\,d\mathbb{P}(\omega)<+\infty.

It follows from the version of the multplicative ergodic theorem (MET) established in
[
14
]
that there exist numbers

-\infty\leq\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\ldots<\lambda_{k}

and for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
a measurable decomposition

X=E_{1}(\omega)\oplus E_{2}(\omega)\oplus\ldots\oplus E_{k}(\omega)

such that

A(\omega)E_{i}(\omega)\subset E_{i}(\sigma(\omega))\text{(with equality if $\lambda_{i}>-\infty$)}

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\mathcal{A}(\omega,n)v|=\lambda_{i},\quad\text{for $v\in E_{i}(\omega)$, $v\neq 0$ and $i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}$.}

Set

E^{s}(\omega):=\bigoplus_{i;\lambda_{i}<0}E_{i}(\omega),\quad E^{u}:=\bigoplus_{i;\lambda_{i}>0}E_{i}(\omega),

and in the case when
\lambda_{j}=0
for some
j
, let
E^{c}(\omega):=E_{j}(\omega)
(if such
j
doesn’t exist, set
E^{c}(\omega)=\{0\}
).

E^{s}(\omega)\oplus E^{c}(\omega)\oplus E^{u}(\omega)=X,\quad\text{for $\mathbb{P}$-a.e. $\omega\in\Omega$.}

(6)

Let
\Pi^{1}(\omega)\colon X\to E^{s}(\omega)
,
\Pi^{2}(\omega)\colon X\to E^{c}(\omega)
and
\Pi^{3}(\omega)\colon X\to E^{u}(\omega)
be projections associated to (
6
). Clearly, (
2
) and (
3
) holds. Finally, it follows from
[
13
, Theorem 2]

that there exist
\lambda>0
and a tempered random variable
K\colon\Omega\to[1,+\infty)
such that (
4
) and (
5
) holds.

We remark that under some additional assumptions an analogous statement can be formulated in the infinite-dimensional setting (by using
[
4
, Proposition 3.7.]
) relying on the infinite-dimensional versions of MET (see
[
10
,
15
,
19
]
and references therein).

Furthermore, we assume that
(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
and
(g_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
are two families of continuous maps
f_{\omega}\colon X\to X
,
g_{\omega}\colon X\to X
such that:

•

for every
x\in X
and
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
,
f_{\omega}(x),g_{\omega}(x)\in E^{s,u}(\omega):=\Ker\Pi^{2}(\omega)
;

•

for every
x\in X
,
\omega\mapsto f_{\omega}(x)
and
\omega\mapsto g_{\omega}(x)
are measurable;

•

there exists a tempered measurable map
D\colon\Omega\to[1,\infty)
such that

\max\{||f_{\omega}||_{\infty},||g_{\omega}||_{\infty}\}\leq D(\omega),

(7)

where for any
h\colon X\to X
, we set

\lVert h\rVert_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in X}|h(x)|;

•

there exist
c>0
such that for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and
x,y\in X
,

|f_{\omega}(x)-f_{\omega}(y)|\leq\frac{c}{K(\sigma(\omega))}|x-y|

(8)

and

|g_{\omega}(x)-g_{\omega}(y)|\leq\frac{c}{K(\sigma(\omega))}|x-y|;

(9)

•

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and
y\in E^{c}(\omega):=\Ima\Pi^{2}(\omega)
,

x\mapsto A(\omega)x+f_{\omega}(x+y)

(10)

and

x\mapsto A(\omega)x+g_{\omega}(x+y)

are invertible maps from
E^{s,u}(\omega)
onto
E^{s,u}(\sigma(\omega))
.

Remark 2
.

We observe that if
A(\omega)
is invertible and
\|A(\omega)^{-1}\|\cdot\text{Lip}(f_{\omega})<1
where
\text{Lip}(f_{\omega})
stands for the Lipschitz constant of
f_{\omega}
, then
A(\omega)+f_{\omega}
is a homeomorphism. Consequently, if
A(\omega)
is invertible for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
,
a=\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|A(\omega)^{-1}\|<+\infty
and
ac<1
, then
A(\omega)+f_{\omega}
is a homeomorphism for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e
\omega\in\Omega
and, in particular, the last hypothesis above is satisfied (see
[
3
, p. 433]
).

Remark 3
.

1.

In the case when
K
is a constant random variable, we note that the requirements (
8
) and (
9
) mean that
f_{\omega}
and
g_{\omega}
are Lipschitz with a uniform (independent on
\omega
) Lipschitz constant. We stress that even in this setting, our Theorems
1
and
2
are new results.

2.

Let us now describe a procedure for the construction of maps
f_{\omega}
satisfying (
8
) in the general case. The discussion is essentially taken from
[
6
, Section 5.2]
but we include for the sake of completeness.
Take an arbitrary
\rho>0
. By
[
1
, Proposition 4.3.3]
, there exists a random variable
D\colon\Omega\to(0,+\infty)
such that

K(\omega)\leq D(\omega)\quad\text{and}\quad D(\sigma^{n}(\omega))\leq D(\omega)e^{\rho|n|},

(11)

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Choose
T>0
sufficiently large so that
\mathbb{P}(A)>0
, where

A:=\{\omega\in\Omega:D(\omega)\leq T\}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set

A_{n}:=\sigma^{-n}(A)\setminus\cup_{k=0}^{n-1}\sigma^{-k}(A).

Letting
A_{0}=A
, we observe that
A_{n}\cap A_{m}=\emptyset
for
n\neq m
. Moreover, due to the ergodicity of
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\sigma)
, we have that
\mathbb{P}(\cup_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n})=1
. For
n\geq 0
and
\omega\in A_{n}
, let
f_{\omega}
be such that

|f_{\omega}(x)-f_{\omega}(y)|\leq\frac{c}{T}e^{-\rho|n-1|}|x-y|,\quad\text{for $x,y\in X$.}

(12)

We now claim that (
8
) holds. Take
\omega\in A_{n}
for
n\geq 1
. Choose
\omega^{\prime}\in A
such that
\omega=\sigma^{-n}(\omega^{\prime})
. By (
11
), we have that

K(\sigma(\omega))\leq D(\sigma(\omega))=D(\sigma^{-(n-1)}(\omega^{\prime}))\leq e^{\rho|n-1|}D(\omega^{\prime})\leq Te^{\rho|n-1|},

and thus

\frac{c}{T}e^{-\rho|n-1|}\leq\frac{c}{K(\sigma(\omega))}.

We conclude that (
8
) follows from (
12
). One can argue in the same manner for
\omega\in A
.

Remark 4
.

We note that the condition in the spirit of (
8
) appeared in some of the earlier versions of the Grobman-Hartman theorem for random dynamical systems (see for example
[
8
, eq.(21)]
). Furthermore, it is analogous to the condition (see
[
27
, eq.(2.2)]
) imposed in the roughness theorem for tempered exponential dichotomies established in
[
27
]
.

We are now in a position to formulate our first result.

Theorem 1
.

For
c>0
sufficiently small, there exists a family of homeomorphisms
(H_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
,
H_{\omega}\colon X\to X
such that:

•

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
,

H_{\sigma(\omega)}\circ(A(\omega)+f_{\omega})=(A(\omega)+g_{\omega})\circ H_{\omega};

(13)

•

for
x\in X
,
\omega\mapsto H_{\omega}(x)
is measurable;

•

there exists a tempered random variable
T\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty)
such that

\lVert H_{\omega}-\text{\rm Id}\rVert_{\infty}\leq T(\omega),\quad\text{for $\mathbb{P}$-a.e. $\omega\in\Omega$;}

•

H_{\omega}(x)-x\in E^{s,u}(\omega)
for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and every
x\in X
.

Moreover, the family
(H_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
satisfying the previous properties is essentially unique. More precisely, if
(H_{\omega}^{\prime})_{\omega\in\Omega}
is another family that satisfies these properties, then
H_{\omega}=H_{\omega}^{\prime}
for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
.

Proof.

For
\omega\in\Omega
, we consider two systems:

\begin{split}x(n+1)&=A(\sigma^{n}(\omega))x(n)+f_{\sigma^{n}(\omega)}(x(n)+y(n))\\
y(n+1)&=A(\sigma^{n}(\omega))y(n),\end{split}

(14)

and

\begin{split}x(n+1)&=A(\sigma^{n}(\omega))x(n)+g_{\sigma^{n}(\omega)}(x(n)+y(n))\\
y(n+1)&=A(\sigma^{n}(\omega))y(n).\end{split}

(15)

For
(\xi,\eta)\in E^{s,u}(\omega)\times E^{c}(\omega)
, let
n\mapsto(x_{1}(n,\omega,\xi,\eta),y(n,\omega,\eta))
denote the solution of (
14
) such that
(x_{1}(0,\omega,\xi,\eta),y(0,\omega,\eta))=(\xi,\eta)
. Similarly, let
n\mapsto(x_{2}(n,\omega,\xi,\eta),y(n,\omega,\eta))
denote the solution of (
15
) satisfying
(x_{2}(0,\omega,\xi,\eta),y(0,\omega,\eta))=(\xi,\eta)
.

It follows from our assumptions and
[
1
, Proposition 4.3.3]
that there exists a tempered random variable
C\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty)
such that for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

K(\sigma(\omega))D(\omega)\leq C(\omega)\quad\text{and}\quad C(\sigma^{n}(\omega))\leq C(\omega)e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}|n|}.

(16)

Let
Y_{\infty}
denote the space of all maps
h\colon\Omega\times X\to X
with the property that:

•

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
,
h(\omega,\cdot)\colon X\to X
is continuous;

•

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and
x\in X
,
h(\omega,x)\in E^{s,u}(\omega)
;

•

for
x\in X
,
\omega\mapsto h(\omega,x)
is measurable;

•

||h||_{\infty}^{\prime}:=\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega}(C(\omega)^{-1}||h(\omega,\cdot)||_{\infty})<\infty.

It is straightforward to verify that
(Y_{\infty},||\cdot||_{\infty}^{\prime})
is a Banach space. For
\omega\in\Omega
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, set

\mathcal{G}(\omega,n):=\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}(\omega,n)\Pi^{1}(\omega)&\text{for $n\geq 0$;}\\
-\mathcal{A}(\omega,n)\Pi^{3}(\omega)&\text{for $n<0$.}\end{cases}

For
h\in Y_{\infty}
, let

(\mathcal{T}h)(\omega,x):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)p(n,\omega,x),

where

\begin{split}p(n,\omega,x)&=g_{\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega)}(x_{1}(-(n+1),\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-(n+1),\omega,\eta)\\
&\phantom{=}+h(\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega),x_{1}(-(n+1),\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-(n+1),\omega,\eta)))\\
&\phantom{=}-f_{\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega)}(x_{1}(-(n+1),\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-(n+1),\omega,\eta)),\end{split}

(17)

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\omega\in\Omega
and
x\in X
, where
\xi=(\text{\rm Id}-\Pi^{2}(\omega))x
,
\eta=x-\xi
.

By (
4
), (
5
),(
7
) and (
16
), we have that

\begin{split}|(\mathcal{T}h)(\omega,x)|&\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\lVert\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)\rVert\cdot(\lVert g_{\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega)}\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert f_{\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega)}\rVert_{\infty})\\
&\leq 2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}K(\sigma^{-n}(\omega))e^{-\lambda n}D(\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega))\\
&\phantom{\leq}+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}K(\sigma^{n}(\omega))e^{-\lambda n}D(\sigma^{n-1}(\omega))\\
&\leq 2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda n}C(\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega))+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda n}C(\sigma^{n-1}(\omega))\\
&\leq 2e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}C(\omega)\bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}n}\bigg)\\
&\leq 2e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\frac{1+e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}}{1-e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}}C(\omega),\end{split}

(18)

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and
x\in X
. Hence,
||\mathcal{T}h||_{\infty}^{\prime}<\infty
.

We now claim that for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
,
x\mapsto\mathcal{T}h(\omega,x)
is a continuous map. We begin by observing that due to the continuity of maps
f_{\omega}
, we have that

(\xi,\eta)\mapsto(x_{1}(n,\omega,\xi,\eta),y(n,\omega,\eta))\quad\text{is continuous,}

(19)

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Fix now an arbitrary
\omega\in\Omega
for which (
4
), (
5
), (
16
) and (
19
) hold, and such that
h(\sigma^{n}\omega,\cdot)
is continuous for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We note that the set of
\omega
’s satisfying these properties is a full measure set.
Take
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
. Choose
N>0
such that

\sum_{|n|>N}\lVert\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)\rVert\cdot(\lVert g_{\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega)}\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert f_{\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega)}\rVert_{\infty})<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

We note that the existence of
N
follows from the estimates in (
18
). Furthemore, by using (
19
) together with the continuity of
f_{\sigma^{n}(\omega)}
,
g_{\sigma^{n}(\omega)}
and
h(\sigma^{n}(\omega),\cdot)
, we conclude that

\sum_{|n|\leq N}\|\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)\rVert\cdot|p(n,\omega,x)-p(n,\omega,x^{\prime})|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3},

whenever
|x-x^{\prime}|
is sufficiently small. The last two estimates easily imply that

|(\mathcal{T}h)(\omega,x)-(\mathcal{T}h)(\omega,x^{\prime})|<\varepsilon,

whenever
|x-x^{\prime}|
is sufficiently small. Hence,
x\mapsto\mathcal{T}h(\omega,x)
is a continuous map and
thus
\mathcal{T}h\in Y_{\infty}
.

Moreover, for
h_{1},h_{2}\in Y_{\infty}
, we have using (
9
) that

\begin{split}&|(\mathcal{T}h_{1})(\omega,x)-(\mathcal{T}h_{2})(\omega,x)|\\
&\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{c}{K(\sigma^{-n}(\omega))}\lVert\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)\rVert\cdot\lVert h_{1}(\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega),\cdot)-h_{2}(\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega),\cdot)\rVert_{\infty}\\
&\leq c\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda n}C(\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega))\lVert h_{1}-h_{2}\rVert_{\infty}^{\prime}+c\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda n}C(\sigma^{n-1}(\omega))\lVert h_{1}-h_{2}\rVert_{\infty}^{\prime}\\
&\leq ce^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\frac{1+e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}}{1-e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}}C(\omega)\lVert h_{1}-h_{2}\rVert_{\infty}^{\prime},\end{split}

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and
x\in X
. Therefore,

\lVert\mathcal{T}h_{1}-\mathcal{T}h_{2}\rVert_{\infty}^{\prime}\leq ce^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\frac{1+e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}}{1-e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}}\lVert h_{1}-h_{2}\rVert_{\infty}^{\prime},

for
h_{1},h_{2}\in Y_{\infty}
. If
c>0
is such that

ce^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\frac{1+e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}}{1-e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}}<1,

we conclude that
h\mapsto\mathcal{T}h
is a contraction on
(Y_{\infty},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}^{\prime})
. Hence,
h\mapsto\mathcal{T}h
has a unique fixed point
h\in Y_{\infty}
. Thus,

h(\omega,x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)p(n,\omega,x),

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and
x\in X
. Hence, we have that

\begin{split}&h(\sigma(\omega),x_{1}(1,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(1,\omega,\eta))\\
&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-(n-1)}(\omega),n)\bigg(g_{\sigma^{-n}(\omega)}(x_{1}(-n,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-n,\omega,\eta)\\
&\phantom{=}+h(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),x_{1}(-n,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-n,\omega,\eta)))\bigg)\\
&\phantom{=}-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-(n-1)}(\omega),n)f_{\sigma^{-n}(\omega)}(x_{1}(-n,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-n,\omega,\eta))\\
&=\Pi^{1}(\sigma(\omega))g_{\omega}(\xi+\eta+h(\omega,\xi+\eta))\\
&\phantom{=}+A(\omega)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)\bigg(g_{\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega)}(x_{1}(-(n+1),\omega,\xi,\eta)\\
&\phantom{=}+y(-(n+1),\omega,\eta)+h(\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega),x_{1}(-(n+1),\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-(n+1),\omega,\eta)))\bigg)\\
&\phantom{=}-\Pi^{1}(\sigma(\omega))f_{\omega}(\xi+\eta)\\
&\phantom{=}-A(\omega)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)f_{\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega)}(x_{1}(-(n+1),\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-(n+1),\omega,\eta))\\
&\phantom{=}+\Pi^{3}(\sigma(\omega))g_{\omega}(\xi+\eta+h(\omega,\xi+\eta))\\
&\phantom{=}+A(\omega)\sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1}\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)\bigg(g_{\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega)}(x_{1}(-(n+1),\omega,\xi,\eta)\\
&\phantom{=}+y(-(n+1),\omega,\eta)+h(\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega),x_{1}(-(n+1),\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-(n+1),\omega,\eta)))\bigg)\\
&\phantom{=}-\Pi^{3}(\sigma(\omega))f_{\omega}(\xi+\eta)\\
&\phantom{=}-A(\omega)\sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1}\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)f_{\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega)}(x_{1}(-(n+1),\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-(n+1),\omega,\eta))\\
&=A(\omega)h(\omega,x)+g_{\omega}(\xi+\eta+h(\omega,\xi+\eta))-f_{\omega}(\xi+\eta),\end{split}

and thus

h(\sigma(\omega),A(\omega)x+f_{\omega}(x))=A(\omega)h(\omega,x)+g_{\omega}(x+h(\omega,x))-f_{\omega}(x),

(20)

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and
x\in X
.

For
\omega\in\Omega

H_{\omega}\colon X\to X
by

By (

), we have that (
13
) holds.

In analogous manner, for
\bar{h}\in Y_{\infty}
we define
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\bar{h}
by

\begin{split}r(n,\omega,x)&=f_{\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega)}(x_{2}(-(n+1),\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-(n+1),\omega,\eta)\\
&\phantom{=}+\bar{h}(\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega),x_{2}(-(n+1),\omega,\xi,\eta)))\\
&\phantom{=}-g_{\sigma^{-(n+1)}(\omega)}(x_{2}(-(n+1),\omega,\xi,\eta)),\end{split}

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\omega\in\Omega
and
x\in X
, where

,

. For

Then, we have that

\bar{H}_{\sigma(\omega)}\circ(A(\omega)+g_{\omega})=(A(\omega)+f_{\omega})\circ\bar{H}_{\omega}.

(21)

In order to complete the proof of our theorem we need the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(Q_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
be a family of continuous maps
Q_{\omega}\colon X\to X
such that:

•

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
,

Q_{\sigma(\omega)}\circ(A(\omega)+f_{\omega})=(A(\omega)+g_{\omega})\circ Q_{\omega};

(22)

•

for
x\in X

\omega\mapsto Q_{\omega}(x)
is measurable;

•

there exists a tempered random variable
T\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty)
such that

\lVert Q_{\omega}-\text{\rm Id}\rVert_{\infty}\leq T(\omega),\quad\text{for $\mathbb{P}$-a.e. $\omega\in\Omega$;}

(23)

•

Q_{\omega}(x)-x\in E^{s,u}(\omega)
for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and every
x\in X
.

Then,
q(\omega,x):=Q_{\omega}(x)-x
is a fixed point of
\mathcal{T}
. In particular, the family of maps
(Q_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
with the above properties is essentially unique.

Proof of the lemma.

Given
\omega\in\Omega
and
x\in X
, let us consider
\xi=(\text{\rm Id}-\Pi^{2}(\omega))x
,
\eta=x-\xi
and
(x_{1}(n,\omega,\xi,\eta),y(n,\omega,\eta))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
as before. We start observing that condition (
22
) implies that

\begin{split}q(\sigma(\omega),A(\omega)x+f_{\omega}(x))&=Q_{\sigma(\omega)}(A(\omega)x+f_{\omega}(x))-\left(A(\omega)x+f_{\omega}(x)\right)\\
&=\left(A(\omega)+g_{\omega}\right)(Q_{\omega}(x))-A(\omega)x-f_{\omega}(x)\\
&=A(\omega)q(\omega,x)+g_{\omega}(x+q(\omega,x))-f_{\omega}(x).\end{split}

Thus, recalling that
A(\omega)x+f_{\omega}(x)=x_{1}(1,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(1,\omega,\eta)
we can rewrite the previous equality as

\begin{split}&q(\sigma(\omega),x_{1}(1,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(1,\omega,\eta))\\
&=A(\omega)q(\omega,x_{1}(0,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(0,\omega,\eta))\\
&+g_{\omega}(x_{1}(0,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(0,\omega,\eta)+q(\omega,x_{1}(0,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(0,\omega,\eta)))\\
&-f_{\omega}(x_{1}(0,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(0,\omega,\eta)),\end{split}

which implies that

\begin{split}&q(\omega,x)\\
&=A(\sigma^{-1}(\omega))q(\sigma^{-1}(\omega),x_{1}(-1,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-1,\omega,\eta))\\
&+g_{\sigma^{-1}(\omega)}(x_{1}(-1,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-1,\omega,\eta)+q(\sigma^{-1}(\omega),x_{1}(-1,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-1,\omega,\eta)))\\
&-f_{\sigma^{-1}(\omega)}(x_{1}(-1,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-1,\omega,\eta)).\end{split}

Iterating this formula we conclude that for every
k>0
,

\begin{split}q(\omega,x)&=\mathcal{A}(\sigma^{-k}(\omega),k)q(\sigma^{-k}(\omega),x_{1}(-k,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-k,\omega,\eta))\\
&+\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\mathcal{A}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)p(n,\omega,x)\\
\end{split}

where
p(n,\omega,x)
is as defined in (
17
) with
h=q
. Thus, using property (
2
) we conclude that

\begin{split}\Pi^{1}(\omega)q(\omega,x)&=\mathcal{A}(\sigma^{-k}(\omega),k)\Pi^{1}(\sigma^{-k}(\omega))q(\sigma^{-k}(\omega),x_{1}(-k,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-k,\omega,\eta))\\
&+\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\mathcal{A}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)\Pi^{1}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega))p(n,\omega,x)\\
&=\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-k}(\omega),k)q(\sigma^{-k}(\omega),x_{1}(-k,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-k,\omega,\eta))\\
&+\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)p(n,\omega,x)\\
\end{split}

for every
k>0
. It follows from (
23
) that for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and every
k>0
,

|q(\sigma^{-k}(\omega),x_{1}(-k,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-k,\omega,\eta))|\leq T(\sigma^{-k}(\omega)).

Combining this observation with (
1
) and (
4
) we conclude that

|\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-k}(\omega),k)q(\sigma^{-k}(\omega),x_{1}(-k,\omega,\xi,\eta)+y(-k,\omega,\eta)|\xrightarrow{k\to+\infty}0

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
. Consequently,

\begin{split}\Pi^{1}(\omega)q(\omega,x)&=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)p(n,\omega,x).\\
\end{split}

Similarly, one can prove that

\begin{split}\Pi^{3}(\omega)q(\omega,x)&=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1}\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)p(n,\omega,x).\\
\end{split}

Therefore, since
q(\omega,x)=Q_{\omega}(x)-x\in E^{s,u}(\omega)
for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and every
x\in X
, we conclude that

\begin{split}q(\omega,x)&=\Pi^{1}(\omega)q(\omega,x)+\Pi^{3}(\omega)q(\omega,x)\\
&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{G}(\sigma^{-n}(\omega),n)p(n,\omega,x)\\
&=(\mathcal{T}q)(\omega,x),\end{split}

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and every
x\in X
. Therefore,
q
is a fixed point for
\mathcal{T}
as claimed.
The uniqueness property follows easily from previous observations and from the fact that
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction. The proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

Our objective now is to prove that
H_{\omega}\circ\bar{H}_{\omega}=\text{\rm Id}
and
\bar{H}_{\omega}\circ H_{\omega}=\text{\rm Id}
. We begin by observing that (
13
) and (

) readily imply that

\bar{H}_{\sigma(\omega)}\circ H_{\sigma(\omega)}\circ\left(A(\omega)+f_{\omega}\right)=\left(A(\omega)+f_{\omega}\right)\circ\bar{H}_{\omega}\circ H_{\omega},

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e
\omega\in\Omega
. Moreover, note that

and thus by using the properties of
\bar{H}
and
H
we conclude that
\bar{H}_{\omega}\circ H_{\omega}(x)-x\in E^{s,u}(\omega)
for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and every
x\in X
. Moreover, there exists a tempered random variable

such that

Finally, since

and

is measurable for every

. Thus, it follows from the uniqueness given in Lemma

&\leq(e^{\rho}+c)^{n+1}\left|x_{1}(0,\omega,\xi_{x},\eta_{x})-x_{1}(0,\omega,\xi_{z},\eta_{z})\right|_{\omega}\\
&\phantom{\leq}+cZ(\omega)|\eta_{x}-\eta_{z}|\sum_{j=0}^{n}(e^{\rho}+c)^{j}e^{\rho(n-j)}\\
&\leq(e^{\rho}+c)^{n+1}\left|\xi_{x}-\xi_{z}\right|_{\omega}+cZ(\omega)|\eta_{x}-\eta_{z}|\sum_{j=0}^{n}(e^{\rho}+c)^{n}\\
&\leq(n+1)(e^{\rho}+c)^{n+1}Z(\omega)\left(\left|\xi_{x}-\xi_{z}\right|+|\eta_{x}-\eta_{z}|\right)\\
&\leq(n+1)(e^{\rho}+c)^{n+1}Z(\omega)\left(2K(\omega)+M(\omega)\right)\left|x-z\right|,\end{split}

which implies (
37
).

We now prove (
38
). Given
\omega\in\Omega
and
\eta\in E^{c}(\omega)
let us consider
F_{\omega}^{\eta}:E^{s,u}(\omega)\to E^{s,u}(\sigma(\omega))
defined by

F_{\omega}^{\eta}(\xi)=A(\omega)\xi+f_{\omega}(\xi+\eta).

(42)

It follows from our hypothesis (
10
) that
F_{\omega}^{\eta}
is invertible for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
. Moreover, one can easily check that its inverse is given by

\left(F_{\omega}^{\eta}\right)^{-1}(\xi)=A(\omega)^{-1}\xi-A(\omega)^{-1}\left(f_{\omega}\left(\left(F_{\omega}^{\eta}\right)^{-1}(\xi)+\eta\right)\right).

(43)

Consequently, for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and every
\eta,\theta\in E^{c}(\omega)
and
\xi,\zeta\in E^{s,u}(\sigma(\omega))
, we have that

\begin{split}&\left|\left(F_{\omega}^{\eta}\right)^{-1}(\xi)-\left(F_{\omega}^{\theta}\right)^{-1}(\zeta)\right|_{\omega}\\
&\leq\left|A(\omega)^{-1}\left(\xi-\zeta\right)\right|_{\omega}\\
&\phantom{\leq}+\left|A(\omega)^{-1}\left(f_{\omega}\left(\left(F_{\omega}^{\eta}\right)^{-1}(\xi)+\eta\right)-f_{\omega}\left(\left(F_{\omega}^{\theta}\right)^{-1}(\zeta)+\theta\right)\right)\right|_{\omega}\\
&\leq e^{\rho}|\xi-\zeta|_{\sigma(\omega)}+e^{\rho}\left|f_{\omega}\left(\left(F_{\omega}^{\eta}\right)^{-1}(\xi)+\eta\right)-f_{\omega}\left(\left(F_{\omega}^{\theta}\right)^{-1}(\zeta)+\theta\right)\right|_{\sigma(\omega)}\\
&\leq e^{\rho}|\xi-\zeta|_{\sigma(\omega)}+ce^{\rho}\left(\left|\left(F_{\omega}^{\eta}\right)^{-1}(\xi)-\left(F_{\omega}^{\theta}\right)^{-1}(\zeta)\right|+\left|\eta-\theta\right|\right)\\
&\leq e^{\rho}|\xi-\zeta|_{\sigma(\omega)}+ce^{\rho}\left(\left|\left(F_{\omega}^{\eta}\right)^{-1}(\xi)-\left(F_{\omega}^{\theta}\right)^{-1}(\zeta)\right|_{\omega}+\left|\eta-\theta\right|\right)\\
&\leq e^{\rho}\left(|\xi-\zeta|_{\sigma(\omega)}+\left|\eta-\theta\right|\right)+ce^{\rho}\left|\left(F_{\omega}^{\eta}\right)^{-1}(\xi)-\left(F_{\omega}^{\theta}\right)^{-1}(\zeta)\right|_{\omega}.\end{split}

Thus, for
c>0
sufficiently small we get that

\begin{split}&\left|\left(F_{\omega}^{\eta}\right)^{-1}(\xi)-\left(F_{\omega}^{\theta}\right)^{-1}(\zeta)\right|_{\omega}\leq\frac{e^{\rho}}{1-ce^{\rho}}\left(|\xi-\zeta|_{\sigma(\omega)}+\left|\eta-\theta\right|\right).\end{split}

(44)

Now, recalling (
14
) and (
42
) it follows that for every
n>0

x_{1}(-n,\omega,\xi_{x},\eta_{x})=\left(F_{\sigma^{-n}(\omega)}^{\mathcal{A}(\omega,-n)\eta_{x}}\right)^{-1}\left(x_{1}(-(n-1),\omega,\xi_{x},\eta_{x})\right).

By applying (
44
), we obtain that

\begin{split}&\left|x_{1}(-n,\omega,\xi_{x},\eta_{x})-x_{1}(-n,\omega,\xi_{z},\eta_{z})\right|_{\sigma^{-n}(\omega)}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{e^{\rho}}{1-ce^{\rho}}\right)\left|x_{1}(-(n-1),\omega,\xi_{x},\eta_{x})-x_{1}(-(n-1),\omega,\xi_{z},\eta_{z})\right|_{\sigma^{-(n-1)}(\omega)}\\
&+\left(\frac{e^{\rho}}{1-ce^{\rho}}\right)\left|\mathcal{A}(\omega,-n)\eta_{x}-\mathcal{A}(\omega,-n)\eta_{z}\right|\\
&\leq\left(\frac{e^{\rho}}{1-ce^{\rho}}\right)\left|x_{1}(-(n-1),\omega,\xi_{x},\eta_{x})-x_{1}(-(n-1),\omega,\xi_{z},\eta_{z})\right|_{\sigma^{-(n-1)}(\omega)}\\
&+Z(\omega)\left(\frac{e^{\rho}}{1-ce^{\rho}}\right)e^{\rho n}\left|\eta_{x}-\eta_{z}\right|.\\
\end{split}

By proceeding recursively, we conclude that

\begin{split}&\left|x_{1}(-n,\omega,\xi_{x},\eta_{x})-x_{1}(-n,\omega,\xi_{z},\eta_{z})\right|\\
&\leq\left|x_{1}(-n,\omega,\xi_{x},\eta_{x})-x_{1}(-n,\omega,\xi_{z},\eta_{z})\right|_{\sigma^{-n}(\omega)}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{e^{\rho}}{1-ce^{\rho}}\right)^{n}\left|x_{1}(0,\omega,\xi_{x},\eta_{x})-x_{1}(0,\omega,\xi_{z},\eta_{z})\right|_{\omega}\\
&\phantom{\leq}+Z(\omega)\left|\eta_{x}-\eta_{z}\right|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{e^{\rho}}{1-ce^{\rho}}\right)^{j+1}e^{\rho(n-j)}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{e^{\rho}}{1-ce^{\rho}}\right)^{n}\left|\xi_{x}-\xi_{z}\right|_{\omega}+Z(\omega)\left|\eta_{x}-\eta_{z}\right|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{e^{\rho}}{1-ce^{\rho}}\right)^{n+1}\\
&\leq n\left(\frac{e^{\rho}}{1-ce^{\rho}}\right)^{n+1}Z(\omega)\left(\left|\xi_{x}-\xi_{z}\right|+\left|\eta_{x}-\eta_{z}\right|\right)\\
&\leq nZ(\omega)\left(2K(\omega)+M(\omega)\right)\left(\frac{e^{\rho}}{1-ce^{\rho}}\right)^{n+1}\left|x-z\right|\\
&\leq nN(\omega)\left(\frac{e^{\rho}}{1-ce^{\rho}}\right)^{n+1}\left|x-z\right|,\\
\end{split}

which proves our claim (
38
). The proof of the lemma is completed.

Remark 6
.

Theorems
1
and
2
were stated and proved in the case when the base dynamics
(\Omega,\sigma,\mathbb{P})
is ergodic. Nevertheless, similar versions with generalized hypothesis can be obtained in the non-ergodic setting simply by combining our results with the Ergodic Decomposition Theorem: if
\mathbb{P}
is non-ergodic then we can decompose it into ergodic components, each of which “living” in disjoint subsets of
\Omega
, and apply our results to each of these components. Then, by “gluing” together each of the respective maps we get the desired result. The main differences in the statements of the results in the non-ergodic case with respect to the ergodic one appear in the constant
c
in Theorem
1
and in the Hölder exponent in Theorem
2
: both will become
\sigma
-invariant maps
c,\alpha:\Omega\to(0,1)
since these quantities will depend on the ergodic component of
\mathbb{P}
. For instance, instead of getting a constant Hölder exponent that holds for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e
\omega\in\Omega
, we will get a
\sigma
-invariant map
\alpha:\Omega\to(0,1)
such that each
H_{\omega}
and
H_{\omega}^{-1}
is
\alpha(\omega)
-Hölder continuous for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e
\omega\in\Omega
.
As already mentioned, in the non-ergodic setting our hypothesis can be relaxed accordingly. For instance, in conditions (
4
), (
5
) and (
24
) the constants
\lambda
and
\rho
can be replaced by
\sigma
-invariant maps
\lambda,\rho:\Omega\to(0,+\infty)
.

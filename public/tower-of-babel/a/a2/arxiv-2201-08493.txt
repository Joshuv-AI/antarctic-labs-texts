1.
Introduction

The terminology and notations used in this introduction can be found in Section 2.

It is well-known that Vilenkin systems do not form bases in the
space
L_{1}.
Moreover, there is a function in the Hardy space
H_{1},

such that the partial sums of
f
are not bounded in the
L_{1}
-norm. Moreover, (see Tephnadze
[
28
]
) there
exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}

\left(0<p<1\right),
such that

\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\left\|S_{2^{n}+1}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}=\infty.

The reason of the divergence of
S_{2^{n}+1}f
is that when
0<p<1
the
Fourier coefficients of
f\in H_{p}
are not uniformly bounded (see
Tephnadze
[
27
]
).

On the other hand, (for details see e.g. the books
[
23
]

and
[
33
]
) the subsequence
\{S_{2^{n}}\}
of partial
sums is bounded from the martingale Hardy space
H_{p}
to the
space
H_{p},
for all
p>0,
that is the following inequality holds:

(1)

\left\|S_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{H_{p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},\ \ p>0.

It is also well-known that (see
[
23
]
)

(2)

S_{2^{n}}f(x)\to f(x),\ \text{for all Lebesgue points of}\ f\in L_{p}(G),\ \text{where}\ p\geq 1.

Weisz
[
35
]
considered the norm convergence of Fejér means of
Vilenkin-Fourier series and proved that the inequality

(3)

\left\|\sigma_{k}f\right\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}},\text{ \ \ \ }p>1/2\text{ \ \ \ and \ \ \ }f\in H_{p},

holds. Moreover, Goginava
[
8
]
(see also
[
22
]
and
[
26
]
) proved that the assumption
p>1/2
in (
3
) is essential. In particular, he showed that there exists a
martingale
f\in H_{1/2}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\sigma_{n}f\right\|_{1/2}=+\infty.

However, Weisz
[
35
]
(see also
[
19
]
) proved that for every
f\in H_{p},
there exists an absolute constant
c_{p},
such that the following inequality holds:

(4)

\left\|\sigma_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{H_{p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},\ \ p>0.

Móricz and Siddiqi
[
16
]
investigated the approximation properties of some special Nörlund means of Walsh-Fourier series of
L_{p}
functions in
norm. Similar results for the two-dimensional case can be found in Nagy
[
18
,
17
]
. Approximation properties for general summability methods can be found in
[
4
,
3
]
. Fridli, Manchanda and Siddiqi
[
6
]
improved and extended the results of Móricz and Siddiqi
[
16
]
to martingale Hardy spaces.
The case when
\left\{q_{k}=1/k:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
was
excluded, since the methods are not applicable to Nörlund logarithmic means. In
[
7
]
Gát and Goginava proved some
convergence and divergence properties of the Nörlund logarithmic means of
functions in the Lebesgue space
L_{1}.
In particular, they proved that there exists an function in the space
L_{1},
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{n}f\right\|_{1}=\infty.

In
[
20
]
(see also
[
5
]
) it was proved that there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p},\ \ (0<p<1)
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{n}f\right\|_{p}=\infty.

In
[
21
]
(see also
[
31
]
) it was proved that there exists a martingale
f\in H_{1}
such that

(5)

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{n}f\right\|_{1}=\infty.

However, Goginava
[
9
]
proved that

\left\|L_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{1}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{1},\ \ f\in L_{1},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

From this result it immediately follows that for every
f\in H_{1},
there exists an absolute constant
c,
such that the inequality

(6)

\left\|L_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{1}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}

holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Goginava
[
9
]
also proved that for any
f\in L_{1}(G),

L_{2^{n}}f(x)\to f(x),\ \ \ \text{a.e., as}\ \ \ n\to\infty.

According to (
1
), (
4
) and (
6
), the following question is quite natural:

Question 1.
Is the subsequence
\{L_{2^{n}}\}
also bounded on the martingale Hardy spaces
H_{p}(G)
when
0<p<1?

In Theorem 2 of this paper we give a negative answer to this question. In particular, we further develop some methods considered in
[
2
,
11
]
and prove that for any
0<p<1,
there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}=\infty.

Moreover, in our Theorem 1 we generalize the result of Goginava
[
9
]
and prove that for any
f\in L_{1}(G)
and for any Lebesgue point
x
,

L_{2^{n}}f(x)\to f(x),\ \ \ \text{as}\ \ \ n\to\infty.

The main results in this paper are presented and proved in Section 4. Section 3 is used to present some auxiliary lemmas, where, in particular, Lemma 2 is new and of independent interest. In order not to disturb our discussions later on some definitions and notations are given in Section 4. Finally, Section 5 is reserved for some open questions we hope can be a source of inspiration for further research in this interesting area.

2.
Definitions and Notations

Let
\mathbb{N}_{+}
denote the set of the positive integers,
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{N}_{+}\cup\{0\}.
Denote by
Z_{2}
the discrete cyclic
group of order 2, that is
Z_{2}:=\{0,1\},
where the group operation is the
modulo 2 addition and every subset is open. The Haar measure on
Z_{2}
is
given so that the measure of a singleton is 1/2.

Define the group
G
as the complete direct product of the group
Z_{2},

with the product of the discrete topologies of
Z_{2}
‘s. The elements of
G

are represented by sequences
x:=(x_{0},x_{1},...,x_{j},...),
where
x_{k}=0\vee 1.

It is easy to give a base for the neighborhood of
x\in G
namely:

I_{0}\left(x\right):=G,\text{ \ }I_{n}(x):=\{y\in G:y_{0}=x_{0},...,y_{n-1}=x_{n-1}\}\text{ }(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Denote
I_{n}:=I_{n}\left(0\right),

\overline{I_{n}}:=G

\backslash

I_{n}
and
e_{n}:=\left(0,...,0,x_{n}=1,0,...\right)\in G,
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It is easy to show that

\overline{I_{M}}=\overset{M-1}{\underset{s=0}{\bigcup}}I_{s}\backslash I_{s+1}.

If
n\in\mathbb{N},
then every
n
can be uniquely expressed as
n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}n_{j}2^{j},
where
n_{j}\in Z_{2}

~(j\in\mathbb{N})
and only a finite numbers of
n_{j}
differ from zero. Let
\left|n\right|:=\max\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\ n_{k}\neq 0\}.

The norms (or quasi-norms) of the spaces
L_{p}(G)
and
weak-L_{p}\left(G\right),

\left(0<p<\infty\right)
are, respectively, defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{p}^{p}:=\int_{G}\left|f\right|^{p}d\mu,\ \ \ \ \left\|f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}^{p}:=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda^{p}\mu\left(f>\lambda\right).

The
k
-th Rademacher function is defined by

r_{k}\left(x\right):=\left(-1\right)^{x_{k}}\text{\qquad}\left(\text{ }x\in G,\text{ }k\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Now, define the Walsh system
w:=(w_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
on
G
as:

w_{n}(x):=\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\Pi}}r_{k}^{n_{k}}\left(x\right)=r_{\left|n\right|}\left(x\right)\left(-1\right)^{\underset{k=0}{\overset{\left|n\right|-1}{\sum}}n_{k}x_{k}}\text{\qquad}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

It is well-known that (see
e.g.
[
23
]
)

(7)

\displaystyle w_{n}\left(x+y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{n}\left(x\right)w_{n}\left(y\right).

The Walsh system is orthonormal and complete in
L_{2}\left(G\right)
(see
e.g.
[
23
]
).

If
f\in L_{1}\left(G\right)
we can establish Fourier coefficients,
partial sums of the Fourier series, Dirichlet kernels with respect
to the Walsh system in the usual manner:

\widehat{f}\left(k\right):=\int_{G}fw_{k}d\mu\,\,\,\,\left(k\in\mathbb{N}\right),

S_{n}f:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}\left(k\right)w_{k},\ \ D_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}w_{k\text{ }}\,\,\,\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right).

Recall that (for details see e.g.
[
1
]
)

(8)

D_{2^{n}}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2^{n},&\,\text{if\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace}x\in I_{n}\\
0,&\text{if}\,\,x\notin I_{n}\end{array}\right.

and

(9)

D_{n}=w_{n}\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}n_{k}r_{k}D_{2^{k}}=w_{n}\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}n_{k}\left(D_{2^{k+1}}-D_{2^{k}}\right),\text{ for \ }n=\overset{\infty}{\underset{i=0}{\sum}}n_{i}2^{i}.

Let
\left\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\right\}
be a sequence of nonnegative numbers.
The Nörlund means for the Fourier series of
f
are defined by

\frac{1}{{l_{n}}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}q_{n-k}S_{k}f.

In the special case when
\{q_{k}=1:k\in\mathbb{N}\},
we get the Fejér means

\sigma_{n}f:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}f\,.

If
q_{k}={1}/{(k+1)}
, then we get the Nörlund logarithmic means:

(10)

L_{n}f:=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{S_{k}f}{n-k},\ \ \ \ l_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}.

The
n
-th Riesz logarithmic mean of the Fourier
series of the integrable function
f
is defined by

R_{n}f:=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{S_{k}f}{k},\ \ l_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k},

We note that it is an inverse of the Nörlund logarithmic means.

The convolution of two functions

f,g\in L_{1}(G)
is defined by

\left(f\ast g\right)\left(x\right):=\int_{G}f\left(x+t\right)g\left(t\right)d\mu(t)\text{ \ \ }\left(x\in G\right).

It is well-known that if
f\in L_{p}\left(G\right),

g\in L_{1}\left(G\right)
and
1\leq p<\infty.
Then
f\ast g\in L_{p}\left(G\right)
and the corresponding inequality holds:

(11)

\left\|f\ast g\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{p}\left\|g\right\|_{1}.

The representations

L_{n}f\left(x\right)=\underset{G}{\int}f\left(t\right)P_{n}\left(x+t\right)d\mu\left(t\right)\ \ \text{and}\ \ R_{n}f\left(x\right)=\underset{G}{\int}f\left(t\right)Y_{n}\left(x+t\right)d\mu\left(t\right)

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
play a central role in the sequel, where

P_{n}:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{n-k}D_{k}\ \ \text{and}\ \ Y_{n}:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{k}D_{k}

are called the kernels of the Nörlund logaritmic and the Reisz means, respectively.

It is well-known that (see e.g. Goginava
[
9
]
and Tephnadze
[
30
]
):

(12)

\displaystyle P_{2^{n}}(x)=D_{2^{n}}(x)-\psi_{2^{n}-1}(x){Y}_{2^{n}}(x).

Moreover, for all
n\in\mathbb{N},

(13)

\displaystyle\|P_{2^{n}}\|_{1}<c<\infty\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \|Y_{n}\|_{1}<c<\infty.

In the case
f\in L_{1}(G)
the maximal functions are given by

M(f)(x)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\left|I_{n}\left(x\right)\right|}\left|\int_{I_{n}\left(x\right)}f\left(u\right)d\mu\left(u\right)\right|=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}2^{n}\left|\int_{I_{n}\left(x\right)}f\left(u\right)d\mu\left(u\right)\right|.

It is well-known (for details see e.g.
[
23
]
and
[
32
]
) that if
f\in L_{1}(G),
then the inequality

\|M(f)\|_{\text{weak}-L_{1}}\leq\|f\|_{1}.

holds.
According to a density argument of Calderon-Zygmund (see
[
32
]
) we obtain that if
f\in L_{1}\left(G\right),
then

2^{n}\left|\int_{I_{n}\left(x\right)}f\left(u\right)d\mu\left(u\right)\right|\to 0,\ \ \text{as}\ \ n\to\infty.

A point
x
on the Walsh group is called a Lebesgue point of
f\in L_{1}\left(G\right),
if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}2^{n}\int_{I_{n}(x)}f\left(t\right)d\mu(t)=f\left(x\right)\ \ \ \ \ a.e.\ \ \ x\in G.

According to (
2
) we find that if
f\in L_{1}\left(G\right),
then a.e point is a Lebesgue point.

The
\sigma
-algebra generated by the intervals
\left\{I_{n}\left(x\right):x\in G\right\}
is denoted by
\digamma_{n}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Let
f:=\left(f^{\left(n\right)},n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
be a martingale with respect to
\digamma_{n}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
(for details see e.g.
[
33
]
).

The maximal function of a martingale
f
is defined by

f^{\ast}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|f^{(n)}\right|.

For
0<p<\infty
the Hardy martingale spaces
H_{p}\left(G\right)

consist of all martingales for which

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}:=\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{p}<\infty.

If
f\in L_{1}\left(G\right),
then it is easy to show that the sequence
F=\left(S_{2^{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
is a martingale and
F^{*}=M(f).

If
f=\left(f^{\left(n\right)},\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
is a
martingale, then the Walsh-Fourier coefficients must be defined in a slightly
different manner:

\widehat{f}\left(i\right):=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{G}f^{\left(k\right)}\left(x\right)w_{i}\left(x\right)d\mu\left(x\right).

The Walsh-Fourier coefficients of
f\in L_{1}\left(G\right)
are the same
as those of the martingale
\left(S_{2^{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
obtained from
f
.

A bounded measurable function
a
is a
p
-atom if there exists an interval
I

such that

\int_{I}ad\mu=0,\ \left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\leq\mu\left(I\right)^{-1/p},\text{ \ supp}\left(a\right)\subset I.

The Hardy martingale space
H_{p}\left(G\right)
has an atomic
characterization (see Weisz

]
,

]

Lemma 1
.

A martingale
f=\left(f^{\left(n\right)},\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
is in

if and only if there exist a

\left(a_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\right)
of p-atoms and a sequence
\left(\mu_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\right)
of real numbers such that for

(14)

\underset{k=0}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\mu_{k}S_{2^{n}}a_{k}=f^{\left(n\right)},\ \ \ \ \text{where}\ \ \ \ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\mu_{k}\right|^{p}<\infty.

Moreover,

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}\backsim\inf\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\mu_{k}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p},

where the infimum is taken over all decompositions of
f
of the form (
14
).

We also state and prove the following new lemma of independent interest:

Lemma 2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in I_{2}(e_{0}+e_{1})\in I_{0}\backslash I_{1}.
Then

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}}}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}\frac{D_{j}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-j}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}+1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}}-1}\frac{w_{2j+1}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-2j-1}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}+1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}}-1}\frac{w_{2j}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-2j-1}\right|\geq\frac{1}{3}.

Proof.

Let
x\in I_{2}(e_{0}+e_{1})\in I_{0}\backslash I_{1}.
According to (
8
) and (
9
) we can conclude that

D_{j}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w_{j},&\,\text{if\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace}j\ \ \text{is odd
number,}\\
0,&\text{if}\,\,j\ \ \text{is even number,}\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}}}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1}\frac{D_{j}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}}-1}\frac{w_{2j+1}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-2j-1}=w_{1}\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}}-1}\frac{w_{2j}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-2j-1}.

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-2}+1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}-1}\left|\frac{1}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4j+3}-\frac{1}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4j+1}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-2}+1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}-1}\frac{2}{(2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4j+3)(2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4j+1)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-2}+1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}-1}\frac{2}{(2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4j)(2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4j)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-2}+1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}-1}\frac{1}{(2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}-j)(2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}-j)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{8}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{8}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\frac{1}{k}\right)\leq\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{8}=\frac{1}{4},

according to

w_{4k+2}=w_{2}w_{4k}=-w_{4k},\ \ \text{for}\ \ x\in I_{2}(e_{0}+e_{1}),

we can conclude that

\displaystyle\left|w_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-2}+\frac{w_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4}}{3}+\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}+1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}}-1}\frac{w_{2j}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-2j-1}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{w_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4}}{3}-w_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4}+\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-2}+1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}}\left(\frac{w_{4j-4}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4j+3}+\frac{w_{4j-2}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4j+1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{2w_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4}}{3}-\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-2}+1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}}\left(\frac{w_{4j-4}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4j+3}-\frac{w_{4j-4}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-4j+1}\right)\right|

The proof is complete.

4.
Main results

Our first main result reads:

Theorem 1
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
f\in L_{p}(G)
. Then

(15)

\displaystyle\|L_{2^{n}}f-f\|_{p}\to 0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n\to\infty.

Moreover,

(16)

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}L_{2^{n}}f(x)=f(x),

for all Lebesgue points of
f
.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By combining (
11
) and (
13
) we immediately get

\|L_{2^{n}}f\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\|f\|_{p}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},

which immediately implies (
15
).

To prove a.e convergence we use identity (
12
) to obtain that

(17)

\displaystyle L_{2^{n}}f\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{G}{\int}f\left(t\right)P_{2^{n}}\left(x+t\right)d\mu\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{G}{\int}f\left(t\right)D_{2^{n}}\left(x+t\right)d\mu\left(t\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\underset{G}{\int}f\left(t\right)w_{2^{n}-1}(x+t){Y}_{2^{n}}(x+t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I-II.

By applying (
2
) we can conclude that

(18)

\displaystyle I=S_{2^{n}}f(x)\to f(x)

for all Lebesgue points of
f\in L_{p}(G)
. Moreover, by using (
7
) we find that

\displaystyle II=\psi_{2^{n}-1}(x)\underset{G}{\int}f\left(t\right){Y}_{2^{n}}(x+t){\psi}_{2^{n}-1}(t)d(t).

In view of (
13
) we see that

f\left(t\right){Y}_{2^{n}}(x+t)\in L_{p}\ \ \text{ where}\ \ p\geq 1\ \ \text{for any }\ \ x\in G,

and also note that
II
describes the Fourier coefficients of an integrable function. Hence, according to the Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma it vanishes as
n\to\infty,
i.e.

(19)

\displaystyle II\to 0\ \ \text{for any }\ \ x\in G,\ \ n\to\infty.

The proof of (
16
) follows by just combining (
17
)-(
19
).

The proof is complete.

∎

Our next main result is the following answer of question 1:

Theorem 2
.

Let
0<p<1.
Then there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{2^{n}}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}=\infty.

Proof.

Let
\left\{\alpha_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
be an increasing sequence of the
positive integers such that

(20)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{-p/2}<\infty,

(21)

\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\frac{\left(2^{2\alpha_{\eta}}\right)^{1/p}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\eta}}}<\frac{\left(2^{2\alpha_{k}}\right)^{1/p}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}},

(22)

\frac{\left(2^{2\alpha_{k-1}}\right)^{1/p}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k-1}}}<\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}-8}}{\alpha_{k}^{1/2}l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}.

We note that such an increasing sequence
\left\{\alpha_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
which satisfies conditions (
20
)-(
22
) can obviously be constructed.

Let

f^{\left(n\right)}\left(x\right):=\sum_{\left\{k;\text{ }2\alpha_{k}<n\right\}}\lambda_{k}a_{k},

where

\lambda_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}

and

a_{k}={2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}}\left(D_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}-D_{2^{2\alpha_{k}}}\right).

From (
20
) and Lemma
1
we can conclude that
f=\left(f^{\left(n\right)},n\in\mathbb{N}\right)\in H_{p}.

It is easy to show that

(23)

\widehat{f}(j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}},\,\,\text{ if \thinspace\thinspace}j\in\left\{2^{2\alpha_{k}},...,\text{ ~}2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1\right\},\text{ }k\in\mathbb{N},\\
0,\text{ \thinspace\thinspace\thinspace if \thinspace\thinspace\thinspace}j\notin\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\{2^{2\alpha_{k}},...,\text{ ~}2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1\right\}\text{ .}\end{array}\right.

Moreover,

(24)

\displaystyle L_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\sum_{j=1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}}-1}\frac{S_{j}f}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-j}+\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}}}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1}\frac{S_{j}f}{{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-j}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I+II.

Let
j<2^{2\alpha_{k}}.
Then from (
21
), (
22
) and (
23
) we
have

\displaystyle\left|S_{j}f\left(x\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\sum_{v=2^{2\alpha_{\eta}}}^{2^{2\alpha_{\eta}+1}-1}\left|\widehat{f}(v)\right|\leq\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\sum_{v=2^{2\alpha_{\eta}}}^{2^{2\alpha_{\eta}+1}-1}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{\eta}(1/p-1)}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\eta}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{\eta}/p}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\eta}}}\leq\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k-1}/p+1}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k-1}}}<\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}-4}}{\alpha_{k}^{1/2}l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}.

Consequently,

(25)

\displaystyle\left|I\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\underset{j=1}{\overset{2^{2\alpha_{k}}-1}{\sum}}\frac{\left|S_{j}f\left(x\right)\right|}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k-1}/p}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k-1}}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1}\frac{1}{j}\leq\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k-1}/p}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k-1}}}.

Let
2^{2\alpha_{k}}\leq j\leq 2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1.
Then we have the
following equality

\displaystyle S_{j}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\sum_{v=2^{2\alpha_{\eta}}}^{2^{2\alpha_{\eta}+1}-1}\widehat{f}(v)w_{v}+\sum_{v=2^{2\alpha_{k}}}^{j-1}\widehat{f}(v)w_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\frac{2^{{2\alpha_{\eta}}\left(1/p-1\right)}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\eta}}}\left(D_{2^{2\alpha_{\eta}+1}}-D_{2^{2\alpha_{\eta}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2^{{2\alpha_{k}}\left(1/p-1\right)}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}\left(D_{j}-D_{2^{{2\alpha_{k}}}}\right).

This gives that

(26)

II=\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\underset{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}}}{\overset{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}{\sum}}\ \frac{1}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-j}\left(\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{\eta}\left(1/p-1\right)}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\eta}}}\left(D_{2^{2\alpha_{\eta}+1}}-D_{2^{{}_{2\alpha_{\eta}}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}\left(1/p-1\right)}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}}}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1}\frac{\left(D_{j}-D_{2^{2\alpha_{k}}}\right)}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-j}:=II_{1}+II_{2}.

Let
x\in I_{2}(e_{0}+e_{1})\in I_{0}\backslash I_{1}.
Since
\alpha_{0}\geq 1
we obtain that
2\alpha_{k}\geq 2,
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and if we apply (
8
) we get
that
D_{2^{2\alpha_{k}}}=0,

(27)

II_{1}=0

and

\displaystyle II_{2}=\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}+1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}}-1}\frac{w_{2j+1}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-2j-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}w_{1}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}\sum_{j=2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}+1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}}-1}\frac{w_{2j}}{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-2j-1}.

By now using Lemma 2 we can conclude that

(28)

\left|II_{2}\right|\geq\frac{1}{3}\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}\geq\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-1}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}.

By combining (
22
), (
24
)-(
28
) for
x\in I_{2}(e_{0}+e_{1})

and
0<p<1
we have that

\displaystyle\left|L_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}f\left(x\right)\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle II_{2}-II_{1}-I

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}-\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-3}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-3}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}\geq\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-3}}{\left(\ln 2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}+1\right)\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-3}}{(4\alpha_{k}+1)\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-6}}{\alpha^{3/2}_{k}}.

Hence, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|L_{q_{\alpha_{k}}^{s}}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-6}}{\alpha^{3/2}_{k}}\mu\left\{x\in G:\left|L_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}f\right|\geq\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-6}}{\alpha^{3/2}_{k}}\right\}^{1/p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-6}}{\alpha^{3/2}_{k}}\mu\left\{x\in I_{2}(e_{0}+e_{1}):\left|L_{2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}}f\right|\geq\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-6}}{\alpha^{3/2}_{k}}\right\}^{1/p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)-6}}{\alpha^{3/2}_{k}}(\mu\left(x\in I_{2}(e_{0}+e_{1})\right))^{1/p}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{c2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}}{\alpha_{k}^{3/2}}\rightarrow\infty,\text{ \ \ as \ }k\rightarrow\infty.

The proof is complete.
∎

5.
Open questions

It is known (for details see e.g. the books
[
23
]

and
[
33
]
) that the subsequence
\{S_{2^{n}}\}
of the partial
sums is bounded from the martingale Hardy space
H_{p}
to the Lebesgue
space
L_{p},
for all
p>0.
On the other hand, (see Tephnadze
[
28
]
) there
exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}

\left(0<p<1\right),
such that

\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\left\|S_{2^{n}+1}f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}=\infty.

However, Simon
[
24
]
proved that for all
f\in H_{p},
there exists
an absolute constant
c_{p},
depending only on
p,
such that

\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{p}^{p}}{k^{2-p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}^{p},\text{ \ \ \ }\left(0<p<1\right).

In
[
31
]
it was proved that for all
f\in H_{p},
there exists
an absolute constant
c_{p},
depending only on
p,
such that

\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|L_{k}f\right\|_{p}^{p}}{k^{2-p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}^{p},\text{ \ \ \ }\left(0<p<1\right).

Open Problem 1:
a) Let
f\in H_{p},
where
0<p<1.
Does there exist
an absolute constant
c_{p},
depending only on
p,
such that the following inequality holds:

\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\log^{p}k\left\|L_{k}f\right\|_{p}^{p}}{k^{2-p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}^{p},\text{ \ \ \ }\left(0<p<1\right)?

b) For
0<p<1/2
and any non-decreasing function
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
satisfying the conditions

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\Phi\left(n\right)=+\infty,

is it possible to find a martingale
f\in H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{p}n\left\|L_{n}f\right\|_{p}^{p}\Phi\left(n\right)}{n^{2-p}}=\infty?

Open Problem 2:
a) Let
f\in H_{p}
where
0<p\leq 1
and

\omega_{H_{p}}\left(\frac{1}{M_{n}},f\right)=o\left(\frac{\log n}{2^{n(1/p-1)}\log^{2[p]}n}\right),\ \ \ \text{ as }\ \ \ n\rightarrow\infty.

Does the following convergence result hold:

\left\|L_{k}f-f\right\|_{H_{p}}\rightarrow 0,\,\,\,\text{as \ }\,\,\,k\rightarrow\infty?

b) Let
0<p\leq 1.
Does there exist a martingale
f\in H_{p}
, for which

\omega_{H_{p}}\left(\frac{1}{2^{n}},f\right)=O\left(\frac{\log n}{2^{n(1/p-1)}\log^{2[p]}n}\right),\text{ \ as \ }n\rightarrow\infty

and

\left\|L_{k}f-f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}}\nrightarrow 0,\,\,\,\text{as\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace}k\rightarrow\infty?

Reisz logarithmic means have better approximation properties then the Fejér means and if it is converging in some sense then the Fejér means converge in the same sense. Moreover, it has similar boundedness properties as the Fejér means when we consider
(H_{p},L_{p})
and
(H_{p},weak-L_{p})
type inequalities for the maximal operators of Riesz logarithmic means for
0<p\leq 1.
In particular, it was proved in
[
29
]
that the maximal operator of the Riesz logarithmic means of Vilenkin-Fourier series is bounded from the Hardy space
H_{1/2}(G_{m})
to the space
weak-L_{1/2}(G_{m}).
It follows that it is bounded from the martingale Hardy space
H_{p}(G_{m})
to the space
L_{p}(G_{m})
when
p>1/2.
On the other hand, (for details see
[
30
]
) boundedness does not hold from the martingale Hardy space
H_{p}(G_{m})
to the space
L_{p}(G_{m})
when
0<p\leq 1/2.
Moreover, (see
[
30
]
) there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}(G_{m}),
where
0<p<1/2
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|R_{n}f\right\|_{p}=\infty.

In the endpoint case
p=1/2
it is open problem to prove divergence of the Riesz logarithmic means:

Open Problem 3:
Does there exist a martingale
f\in H_{1/2}(G_{m}),
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|R_{n}f\right\|_{1/2}=\infty?

According to estimate (
4
) it is interesting to consider boundedness of
R_{M_{n}}f
from the martingale Hardy space
H_{p}(G_{m})
to the space
L_{p}(G_{m})
when
0<p\leq 1/2,
so we pose the following:

Open Problem 4:
Does there exist a martingale
f\in H_{p}(G_{m}),
where
0<p<1/2,
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|R_{M_{n}}f\right\|_{p}=\infty?

If we prove this result we immediately find that the maximal operator

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|R_{M_{n}}f\right|

is not bounded from the martingale Hardy space
H_{p}(G_{m})
to the space
L_{p}(G_{m})
when
0<p<1/2.

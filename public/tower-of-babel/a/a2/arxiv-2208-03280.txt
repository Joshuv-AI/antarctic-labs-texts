1.
Introduction

To a large extent, the two-point distortion theorems provide us quantitative information on “how injective” a conformal function is, in the sense of estimating the distance between the images of any two points, respect to the distance (Euclidean or Hyperbolic) of these two points. That is, we seek lower and upper bounds for the distance between
f(a)
and
f(b)
for all
a
and
b
in the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
and that involves in some sense
\rho(a,b)=\mid(a-b)/(1-\overline{a}b)\mid
and the derivative of the function
f
. The first two-point distortion theorem was introduced by C. Blatter in
[
2
]
and proved that if
f
is a conformal mapping, then

\mid f(a)-f(b)\mid^{2}\geq\frac{\sinh^{2}(2d(a,b))}{8(\cosh(4d(a,b)))}\left(R(a)^{2}+R(b)^{2}\right).

Here
d(a,b)=\tanh^{-1}(\rho(a,b))
, and
R(z)=(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|
. Moreover, for different subclasses of univalent functions, such as convex, starlike, etc., there are two-point distortion theorems in which the bounds are more accurate. In particular, if
f
conformally maps the disk onto a convex domain, Kim and Minda in
[
12
]
proved the following result:

Theorem A.
Let
f:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a conformal convex mapping, then for all
p>1
and all
a
,
b
in
{\mathbb{D}}
we have that

(1)

|f(a)-f(b)|\geq\frac{\sinh(d(a,b))}{2(\cosh(pd(a,b)))^{1/p}}\left(R(a)^{p}+R(b)^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

On the other hand, univalence criteria involving both the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of locally univalent functions defined in
{\mathbb{D}}
are well known, which also provide their own two-point distortion theorems. Let
f
be a locally univalent mapping, its
Schwarzian
derivative is defined by:

(2)

Sf=\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\right)^{2}=Pf^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}Pf^{2},

here
Pf=f^{\prime\prime}/f^{\prime}
is named
Pre-Schwarzian
derivative of
f
. Nehari in
[
15
]
showed that
\|Sf\|=\sup\{|Sf(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{2}:z\in{\mathbb{D}}\}\leq 2
implies that
f
is univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}
. To see more univalence criteria that involves the Schwarzian derivative, we can see the
p
-criterion due by Nehari in
[
16
]
. In this context, Chuaqui and Pommerenke
[
4
]
gave a quantitative version of Nehari’s theorem by showing that the condition
\|Sf\|\leq 2
implies that
f
has the two-point distortion property

(3)

|f(a)-f(b)|\geq d(a,b)\sqrt{R(a)R(b)}.

This result was extended to the
p
-criterion by Ma, Mejía and Minda in
[
14
]
.

This paper aims to obtain two-point distortion theorems associated with different univalence criteria for complex harmonic functions defined in
{\mathbb{D}}
. These injectivity criteria are associated with both the Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives, but also to harmonic functions whose analytic part is convex, see
[
3
]
. The manuscript is organized as follows: In Section 2 deals with the preliminaries on complex harmonic mappings and includes the above univalence criteria, called Theorem B, Theorem C, and Theorem D. Subsections 3.1 and 3.2 are devoted to the two-point distortion theorems associated with the Becker and Nehari criteria respectively, but in their complex harmonic versions. Special attention will be paid in subsection 3.3 since we not only deal with the case when the analytic part is convex but also when it conformally maps the disk into a
linearly connected
domain which means geometrically that it has no inward cusps. Indeed, we prove that these functions form a linearly invariant family which follows the two-point distortion theorem found in this manuscript.

2.
Preliminaries

A complex-valued harmonic function
f
in a simply connected domain
\Omega
are those who satisfies that
\Delta f=\partial^{2}f/\partial z\partial\overline{z}=0
, which is equivalent to
f=u+iv
where
u
and
v
are real harmonic mappings defined in
\Omega
. A straightforward calculation give that this functions has a canonical representation
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic functions in
\Omega
, that is unique up to an additive constant. When
\Omega={\mathbb{D}}
it is convenient to choose the additive constant so that
g(0)=0
. The representation
f=h+\overline{g}
is then unique and is called the canonical representation of
f
. A result of Lewy
[
13
]
states that
f
is locally univalent if and only if its Jacobian
J_{f}=|h^{\prime}|^{2}-|g^{\prime}|^{2}
does not vanish in
\Omega
. Thus, harmonic mappings are either sense-preserving or sense-reversing depending on the conditions
J_{f}>0
or
J_{f}<0
throughout the domain
\Omega
where
f
is locally univalent, respectively. Since
J_{f}>0
if and only if
J_{\overline{f}}<0,
throughout this work we will consider sense-preserving mappings in
{\mathbb{D}}.
In this case the analytic part
h
is locally univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}
since
h^{\prime}\neq 0
, and the second complex dilatation of
f
,
\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
, is an analytic function in
{\mathbb{D}}
with
|\omega|<1
. The reader can be found in
[
5
]
a elegant reference for this topic.

Duren, Hamada & Kohr in
[
7
]
showed, among other results, that
f=h+\overline{g}
univalent harmonic mapping and normalized to
h(0)=g(0)=0
, and
h^{\prime}(0)=1
, and let
\alpha
the order of this family, which is the supremum of the absolute value of the second Taylor coefficient of
h
, then

|f(a)-f(b)|\geq\frac{1}{2\alpha}(1-\exp(-2\alpha d(a,b)))\max\{R(a),R(b)\},

where
R(z)=(1-|z|^{2})(|h^{\prime}(z)|-|g^{\prime}(z)|).
Moreover, they also proved that

|f(a)-f(b)|\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}(\exp(2\alpha d(a,b)-1))\min\{Q(a),Q(b)\},

where
Q(z)=(1-|z|^{2})(|h^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|)
. Which represent the harmonic version of Blatter’s Theorem. Note that in the analytic case,
\alpha=2
, however, in the harmonic case, the size of
\alpha
is unknown.

Hernández and Martín
[
8
]
, defined the harmonic pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives for sense-preserving harmonic mappings

f=h+\overline{g}
. Using those definitions, they generalized different results
regarding analytic functions to the harmonic case

[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. The pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of a
sense-preserving harmonic function
f
are corresponding defined by

P_{f}=\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}-\frac{\bar{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}=Ph-\frac{\bar{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\log(J_{f}),

S_{f}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}P_{f}-\frac{1}{2}(P_{f})^{2}=Sh+\frac{\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\left(\omega^{\prime}\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\omega^{\prime}\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right)^{2},

where
Ph
and
Sh
are the standard Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivative respectively, which is given by equation (
2
). It is easy to see that if
f
is analytic (
f=h
and

\omega=0
) then
P_{f}=h^{\prime\prime}/h^{\prime}
and
S_{f}=Sh
, recovering the classical definition of this
operators. In
[
8
]
the authors proved that
S_{f}=0
if and only if
f
is a Möbius harmonic transformation, which is given by
f=h+\alpha\overline{h}
with
\alpha\in{\mathbb{D}}
and
h
is a Möbius analytic function. To motivate the two-point distortion theorems associated to univalence criteria, those harmonic Möbius mappings satisfies that

|f(a)-f(b)|=\sqrt{|h^{\prime}(a)||h^{\prime}(b)|}|a-b||1+\lambda\alpha|,\quad\lambda=\frac{\overline{h(a)-h(b)}}{h(a)-h(b)}\,,\quad a\neq b.

Thus, we can re-write this equation to get

|f(a)-f(b)|=\sqrt{R_{h}(a)R_{h}(b)}\sinh(d(a,b))|1+\lambda\alpha|,\quad R_{h}(z)=|h^{\prime}(z)|(1-|z|^{2}).

In addition, for any
a\in{\mathbb{D}}
, the authors also proved that

P_{f+\overline{af}}=P_{f}
which is the key to prove the extension of Becker’s criterion of univalence for harmonic mappings. This is contained in the following theorem:

Theorem B.
Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a sense-preserving
harmonic function in the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
with dilatation
\omega
. If for all

z\in{\mathbb{D}}

(1-|z|^{2})|zP_{f}(z)|+\frac{|z\omega^{\prime}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}\leq 1,

then
f
is univalent. The constant 1 is sharp.

The classical Becker’s criterion can be found in
[
1
]
and the extension to harmonic mapping appear in
[
8
]
.

Observe that using the Schwarz-Pick lemma,
\omega:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}
satisfies that

(4)

\|\omega^{*}\|=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}}\dfrac{|\omega^{\prime}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}\leq 1.

In addition, we consider
\|\omega\|=\sup\{|\omega(z)|:\,z\in{\mathbb{D}}\}=\|\omega\|_{\infty}
.
In
[
11
]
the authors proved, using that for all
a\in{\mathbb{D}}
,
S_{f+\overline{af}}=S_{f}
, the following generalization of Nehari’s classical univalence criterion:

Theorem C.
Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a sense-preserving harmonic function in the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
with dilatation
\omega
. Then, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|\leq\varepsilon

implies that
f
is univalent.

Obviously, the classical result is obtained taking
\omega=0
, but in this case,
\varepsilon=2
. Unfortunately, the value of
\varepsilon
is still unknown for the planar harmonic mappings setting. From the proof of this theorem, we can see that this value
\varepsilon
is small enough to
\|Sh\|\leq 2
, i.e.
h
satisfies the Nehari’s classical univalence criterion. Moreover, since
S_{f}
is invariant under pre-composition of affine mapping, we have that
h+ag
satisfies the Nehari’s criterion for all
a\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
.

The last criterion that we will consider in this manuscript appears in
[
3
]
and says the following:

Theorem D.
Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a sense-preserving harmonic function in the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
with dilatation
\omega
. If
h
is a convex mapping, then
f
is univalent. In fact, when
h({\mathbb{D}})
is
c-
linearly connected domain, if
\|\omega\|_{\infty}<1/c
, then
f
is also univalent in the unit disc.

Thus, the principal goal of this notes is give to measure of the univalence of functions that satisfies this inyectivity criteria of the form of two-point distortion theorems. Generalizing the classical results for holomorphic mappings.

\varphi
as a locally univalent analytic, and
f=h+\overline{g}
be a sense-preserving harmonic mapping with dilatation
\omega
defined in the unit disc. Recall that the fact that
f
is sense-preserving, implies that
h^{\prime}\neq 0
and
\omega({\mathbb{D}})\subset{\mathbb{D}}

R_{\varphi}(z)=(1-|z^{2}|)|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|
and

3.1.
The Becker’s criterion case

Let
\varphi
be a normalized locally univalent analytic function defined in
{\mathbb{D}}
. Its order is given by

\mbox{ord}\langle\varphi\rangle=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}}\left|\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi^{\prime}}(z)(1-|z|^{2})-\overline{z}\right|=\sup_{a\in{\mathbb{D}}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}|\varphi_{a}^{\prime\prime}(0)|:\varphi_{a}(z)=\dfrac{\varphi\left(\dfrac{z+a}{1-\overline{a}z}\right)-\varphi(a)}{(1-|a|^{2})\varphi^{\prime}(a)}\right\},

which is basically the supremum of second Taylor coefficient of
\varphi_{a}
, for all
a\in{\mathbb{D}}
. A well-known result (see
[
17
]
) asserts that if
\varphi
is univalent mapping defined in the unit disk, normalized to
\varphi(0)=0
and
\varphi^{\prime}(0)=1
, and its order is
\alpha,
then

(5)

\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left(\left(\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right)\geq|\varphi(z)|\geq\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left(1-\left(\frac{1-|z|}{1+|z|}\right)^{\alpha}\right),\quad\quad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Thus, the following Theorem is an application of this when
\varphi
satisfies the classical univalence criterion due by Becker in
[
2
]
. Actually, we haven’t been able to find any reference on this, so we include a proof.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\varphi:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a locally univalent normalized as before such that
|P\varphi(z)|(1-|z|^{2})\leq 1
. Then for all
a,b\in{\mathbb{D}}
we have that

(6)

\frac{\exp(3d(a,b))-1}{3}\sqrt{R_{\varphi}(a)R_{\varphi}(b)}\geq|\varphi(a)-\varphi(b)|\geq\dfrac{1-\exp(-3d(a,b))}{3}\sqrt{R_{\varphi}(a)R_{\varphi}(b)}.

Proof.

We note that

\alpha=\mbox{ord}\langle\varphi\rangle=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}}\left|\dfrac{1}{2}(1-|z|^{2})\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi^{\prime}}(z)-\overline{z}\right|\leq\dfrac{1}{2}+|z|\leq\dfrac{3}{2},

then
\varphi_{a}
satisfies inequality (
5
).
Taking
b=\dfrac{z+a}{1-\overline{a}z}
which is equivalent to
z=\dfrac{b-a}{1-\overline{a}b}
we obtain

\left|\dfrac{\varphi(b)-\varphi(a)}{(1-|a|^{2})|\varphi^{\prime}(a)|}\right|\geq\dfrac{1}{2\alpha}\left[1-\left(\dfrac{1-\left|\dfrac{b-a}{1-\overline{a}b}\right|}{1+\left|\dfrac{b-a}{1-\overline{a}b}\right|}\right)^{\alpha}\right]\geq\dfrac{1}{3}\left[1-\left(\dfrac{1-\left|\dfrac{b-a}{1-\overline{a}b}\right|}{1+\left|\dfrac{b-a}{1-\overline{a}b}\right|}\right)^{3/2}\right].

Since
\rho(a,b)=\left|\dfrac{b-a}{1-\overline{a}b}\right|
from the last inequality we obtain

|\varphi(a)-\varphi(b)|^{2}\geq\dfrac{(1-|a|^{2})(1-|b|^{2})|\varphi^{\prime}(a)||\varphi^{\prime}(b)|}{9}\left[1-\left(\dfrac{1-\rho(a,b)}{1+\rho(a,b)}\right)^{3/2}\right]^{2}.

But,

d(a,b)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{1+\rho(a,b)}{1-\rho(a,b)}\right),

therefore taking square root we obtain the lower bound.

Since the real function
(\xi^{\alpha}-1)/\alpha
is growing in
\alpha
for any
\xi>1
, using
\alpha\leq 3/2
and
[
17
, Staz 1.1]
again, we have that

|\varphi(a)-\varphi(b)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{(1-|a|^{2})(1-|b|^{2})|\varphi^{\prime}(a)||\varphi^{\prime}(b)|}}{3}\left[\left(\dfrac{1+\rho(a,b)}{1-\rho(a,b)}\right)^{3/2}-1\right],

which ends the proof.
∎

Note that, under the hypothesis of Theorem B, the analytic part of
f=h+\overline{g}
satisfies that
|Ph(z)|(1-|z|^{2})\leq 1
then satisfies the Becker’s univalence criterion for analytic mappings in
{\mathbb{D}}
. Since
P_{f+\lambda\overline{f}}=P_{f}
then
\varphi_{\lambda}=h+\lambda g
satisfies the Becker’s univalence criterion for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{D}}
, moreover, taking limits when
|\lambda|\to 1
, we can asserts that Becker’s criterion holds for
|\lambda|\leq 1
, which is the key for the following theorem:

Theorem 2
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a sense-preserving harmonic mapping defined in
{\mathbb{D}}
, such that satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem B, then

\frac{1}{3}(\exp(3d(a,b))-1)\sqrt{Q(a)Q(b)}\geq|f(a)-f(b)|\geq\frac{1}{3}(1-\exp(-3d(a,b)))\sqrt{R(a)R(b)}.

Proof.

For
a
and
b
in
{\mathbb{D}}
, it follows that
f(a)-f(b)=h(a)-h(b)+\overline{(g(a)-g(b))}
, thus, if
g(a)\neq g(b)
there exists an unimodular constant
\lambda
such that
f(a)-f(b)=\varphi_{\lambda}(a)-\varphi_{\lambda}(b)
. In the case when
g(a)=g(b)
we can consider
\lambda=0
. In any case,
\varphi_{\lambda}
satisfies the Becker’s criterion, thus we can apply inequality (
6
) and the fact that
|h^{\prime}|+|g^{\prime}|\geq|\varphi^{\prime}_{\lambda}|\geq|h^{\prime}|-|g^{\prime}|
to obtain that

|f(a)-f(b)|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{3}\left[\left(\frac{1+\rho(a,b)}{1-\rho(a,b)}\right)^{3/2}-1\right]Q(a)Q(b),

and

|f(a)-f(b)|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{3}\left[1-\left(\frac{1-\rho(a,b)}{1+\rho(a,b)}\right)^{3/2}\right]R(a)R(b).

∎

3.2.
The Nehari’s criterion case

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a sense-preserving harmonic mapping. In
[
8
]
the authors shows that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that if
\|S_{f}\|=\sup\{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|:z\in{\mathbb{D}}\}\leq\varepsilon

{\mathbb{D}}
. Moreover, is deduce that there exists a constant

\|Sh\|\leq\varepsilon+kW_{\varepsilon}+\frac{3}{2}W_{\varepsilon}^{2},

where

if and only there exist

with dilatation

. We can assume that
\varepsilon

\varepsilon=\sup\{\delta:\delta+kW_{\delta}+\frac{3}{2}W_{\delta}^{2}\leq 2\},

thus the Theorem C asserts that

is a univalence criterion for sense-preserving harmonic mappings. From the proof of Theorem C it follows that for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{D}}
, the locally univalent analytic mapping
\varphi_{\lambda}=h+\lambda g
, satisfies that
\|S\varphi_{\lambda}\|\leq\varepsilon
since
f_{\lambda}=f+\lambda\overline{f}
has the same Schwarzian derivative. Thus we can asserts the following lemma:

Lemma 3
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a sense-preserving harmonic mapping defined in
{\mathbb{D}}
such that
\|S_{f}\|\leq\varepsilon
, then for any
\lambda\in{\mathbb{D}}
, the corresponding mapping
\varphi_{\lambda}
satisfies that
\|S\varphi_{\lambda}\|\leq 2
.

In
[
14
, Thm. 1.1 part a)]
the authors proved that if
\|S\varphi\|\leq 2t
with

then

(7)

|\varphi(a)-\varphi(b)|\leq\sqrt{\dfrac{R_{\varphi}(a)R_{\varphi}(b)}{1+t}}\sinh\left({\sqrt{1+t}\,d(a,b)}\right),

which is, together
[
4
, Thm. 2]
the key inequalities for the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a sense-preserving harmonic mapping defined in
{\mathbb{D}}
. If
\|S_{f}\|\leq\varepsilon
where
\varepsilon
as in Theorem B, then

\sqrt{\frac{Q(a)Q(b)}{2}}\sinh\left(\sqrt{2}\,d(a,b)\right)\geq|f(a)-f(b)|\geq d(a,b)\sqrt{R(a)R(b)}.

Proof.

For any
a
and
b
in the unit disk, it follows that
f(a)-f(b)=h(a)-h(b)+\overline{(g(a)-g(b))}
, thus, if
g(a)\neq g(b)
there exists an unimodular constant
\lambda
such that
f(a)-f(b)=\varphi_{\lambda}(a)-\varphi_{\lambda}(b)
. In the case when
g(a)=g(b)
we can consider
\lambda=0
. But, using Theorem 2 in
[
4
]
(equation
3
) and inequality (
7
) we have that

\frac{R_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(a)R_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(b)}{2}\sinh^{2}\left(\sqrt{2}\,d(a,b)\right)\geq|\varphi_{\lambda}(a)-\varphi_{\lambda}(b)|^{2}\geq d(a,b)^{2}R_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(a)R_{\varphi_{\lambda}}(b),

but
|h^{\prime}|+|g^{\prime}|\geq|\varphi_{\lambda}^{\prime}|\geq|h^{\prime}|-|g^{\prime}|
which completes the proof.
∎

Note that if

is analytic, which is the case when

and

3.3.
When the analytic part is a convex function

We known that
f=h+\overline{g}
is univalent in the unit disk when
h
is a convex mapping (by
[
3
, Thm. 1]
). Moreover, the result can be extended to the case when

maps conformally
{\mathbb{D}}
onto a linearly connected domain, which is the matter in sub-section 3.1.1.

Theorem 5
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a sense-preserving harmonic mapping defined in
{\mathbb{D}}
such that

is a convex function. Then for all

|f(a)-f(b)|\leq(1+\|\omega\|_{\infty})\left|\dfrac{a-b}{1-\overline{a}b}\right|\left(\dfrac{R_{h}(a)+R_{h}(b)}{2}\right),

and

|f(a)-f(b)|\geq(1-\|\omega\|_{\infty})\left|\dfrac{a-b}{1-\overline{a}b}\right|\left(\dfrac{R_{h}(a)+R_{h}(b)}{2}\right).

Proof.

We note that

(8)

\displaystyle|h(a)-h(b)|+|g(a)-g(b)|\geq|f(a)-f(b)|\geq|h(a)-h(b)|-|g(a)-g(b)|.

Considering the curve
\gamma=h^{-1}(R),
where

is the segment joining

and
h(b)
, then for

we have that

and

|g(a)-g(b)|=\left|\int_{\gamma}g^{\prime}(\zeta)\,d\zeta\right|=\left|\int_{R}\dfrac{g^{\prime}(\zeta)}{h^{\prime}(\zeta)}dw\right|\leq\|\omega\|_{\infty}\int_{R}|dw|=\|\omega\|_{\infty}|h(a)-h(b)|.

Hence, substituting this last inequality in (
8
) we obtain

(1+\|\omega\|_{\infty})|h(a)-h(b)|\geq|f(a)-f(b)|\geq(1-\|\omega\|_{\infty})|h(a)-h(b)|.

Therefore, using Theorem A follows the result.
∎

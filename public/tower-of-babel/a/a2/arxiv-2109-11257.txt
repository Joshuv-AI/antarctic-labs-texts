Arpita Roy and Abhijit Banerjee

Abstract
. The paper is devoted to study the uniqueness problem of linear delay-differential operator of a meromorphic function sharing two sets or small function together with values with its c c -shift and q q -shift operator. Results of this paper drastically improve two recent results of Meng-Liu [J. Appl. Math. Inform. 37(1-2)(2019), 133-148] and Qi-Li-Yang [Comput. Methods Funct. Theo., 18(2018), 567-582]. In addition to this, one of our result improve and extend that of Qi-Yang [Comput. Methods Funct. Theo., 20(2020), 159-178].

Introduction Definitions and Results

Throughout the paper we use standard notations of Nevanlinna theory as stated in
[
7
]
and by any meromorphic function
f
we always mean that it is defined on
\mathbb{C}
. Let
f
and
g
be such two non constant meromorphic functions.
For
a\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
, the following two quantities

\delta(a;f)=1-\limsup\limits_{r\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{N(r,a;f)}{T(r,f)}=\liminf\limits_{r\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{m(r,a;f)}{T(r,f)}

and

\Theta(a;f)=1-\limsup\limits_{r\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{\overline{N}(r,a;f)}{T(r,f)}

are respectively known as Nevanlinna deficiency and ramification index of the value
a
.

In the beginning of the nineteenth century R. Nevanlinna inaugurated the value distribution theory with his famous Five value and Four value theorems which can be considered as the backbone of the modern uniqueness theory. Illuminated by these two basic results initially the research were performed on the value sharing of meromorphic functions. After five decades, uniqueness theory moved to a new direction led by F. Gross
[
4
]
, who transformed the traditional value sharing problem to a more general set up namely to the shared set problems. Now we recall the definition of set sharing.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
S
be a set of distinct elements of
\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
.
For a non-constant meromorphic function
f
, let
E_{f}(S)=\bigcup_{a\in S}\{(z,p)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{N}:f(z)=a\;with\;multiplicity\;p\}

\left(\overline{E}_{f}(S)=\bigcup_{a\in S}\{(z,1)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{N}:f(z)=a\}\right)
. Then we say
f
,
g
share the set
S
CM(IM) if
E_{f}(S)=E_{g}(S)

\left(\overline{E}_{f}(S)=\overline{E}_{g}(S)\right)
.

Evidently, if
S
is a singleton, then it coincides with the traditional definition of CM(IM) sharing of values, which are known to the readers.

In 2001, due to a revolutionary approach by Lahiri
[
8
,
9
]
, the notion of weighted sharing of values or sets appeared in the literature and expedite the research work there in. Though now-a-days the definition is widely circulated, we invoke the definition.

Definition 1.2
.

[
8
,
9
]
Let
k
be a non-negative integer or infinity. For

we denote by
E_{k}(a;f)
the set of all
a
-points of
f
, where an
a
-point of multiplicity

is counted
m
times if
m\leq k
and
k+1
times if
m>k
. If
E_{k}(a;f)=E_{k}(a;g)
, we say that
f,g
share the value
a
with weight
k
and denote it by
(a,k)
. The IM and CM sharing corresponds to
(a,0)
and
(a,\infty)
respectively.

Definition 1.3
.

[
8
]
Let
S
be a set of distinct elements of
\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
and
k
be a non-negative integer or
\infty
. We denote by

\cup_{a\in{S}}E_{k}(a;f)

\overline{E}_{f}(S)=E_{f}(S,0)

If

By

-point is counted according to its multiplicity and by

We also denote by

Usually,

denotes any quantity satisfying

for all

Lemmas

In this section some lemmas will be presented which will be needed in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
3
]

Let
f(z)
be a meromorphic function of finite order
\rho
and let

be fixed. Then, for each

\displaystyle T(r,f(z+c))=T(r,f(z))+O(r^{\rho-1+\varepsilon})+O(\log r).

Lemma 2.2
.

[
5
]

Let
f(z)
be a meromorphic function of finite order and

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{f(z+c)}{f(z)}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{f(z)}{f(z+c)}\right)=S(r,f).

Lemma 2.3
.

[
6
]
Let
f
be a non-constant meromorphic function of finite order and
c\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

\displaystyle N\left(r,\frac{1}{f(z+c)}\right)\leq N\left(r,\frac{1}{f(z)}\right)+S(r,f),

\displaystyle N\left(r,f(z+c)\right)\leq N\left(r,f(z)\right)+S(r,f),

\displaystyle\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{f(z+c)}\right)\leq\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{f(z)}\right)+S(r,f)

and

\displaystyle\overline{N}\left(r,f(z+c)\right)\leq\overline{N}\left(r,f(z)\right)+S(r,f).

Lemma 2.4
.

[
2
]

Let
f(z)
be a meromorphic function of zero order and

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{f(qz)}{f(z)}\right)=S_{1}(r,f).

.

[

\displaystyle N(r,f(qz))=(1+o(1))N(r,f(z))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}m\left(r,\frac{1}{f(z)-\alpha_{j}}\right)+S(r,f)\leq m\left(r,\frac{1}{\tilde{L}(f(z))}\right)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{\tilde{L}(f(z))}{f(z+c)}\right)+m(r,f(z+c))+N(r,\tilde{L}(f(z)))-N\left(r,\frac{1}{\tilde{L}(f(z))}\right)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle T(r,f(z+c))-N\left(r,\frac{1}{\tilde{L}(f(z))}\right)+S(r,f)\leq T(r,f)-N\left(r,\frac{1}{\tilde{L}(f(z))}\right)+S(r,f).

According to the given condition

Now from

\displaystyle P_{*}(\tilde{L}(f(z)))=e^{\gamma(z)}\left(P_{*}(f(z+c))-1+e^{-\gamma(z)}\right).

Set

\displaystyle W(z)=\frac{P_{*}(f(z+c))}{1-e^{-\gamma(z)}}.

, then by applying Nevanlinna’s second fundamental theorem to

and using

and

we obtain,

\displaystyle T(r,P_{*}(f(z+c)))\leq T(r,W)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,0;W)+\overline{N}(r,\infty;W)+\overline{N}(r,0;W-1)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,0;P_{*}(f(z+c)))+\overline{N}(r,\infty;P_{*}(f(z+c)))+\overline{N}(r,0;P_{*}(\tilde{L}(f(z))))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\chi^{n-1}_{0}+m_{1}+m_{2})\left(T(r,f(z+c))+T(r,\tilde{L}(f(z)))\right)+N(r,\infty;f(z+c))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\chi^{n-1}_{0}+m_{1}+m_{2})\left(T(r,f(z+c))+m(r,f(z+c))+m\left(r,\frac{\tilde{L}(f(z))}{f(z+c)}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+N(r,\infty;f(z+c))\right)+N(r,\infty;f)+S(r,f).

. This gives
e^{\gamma(z)}\equiv 1
, that yields

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\tilde{L}(f(z))-\alpha_{i})\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n}(f(z+c)-\alpha_{i}).

∎

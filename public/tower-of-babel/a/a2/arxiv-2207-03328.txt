Bayesian inference of the symmetry energy and the neutron skin in 48 Ca and 208 Pb from CREX and PREX-2
Zhen Zhang, Lie-Wen Chen

Abstract
Using the recent model-independent determination of the charge-weak form factor difference Δ ​ F CW \Delta F_{\rm CW} in Ca 48 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 48$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ca} and Pb 208 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 208$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Pb} by the CREX and PREX-2 collaborations together with some well-determined properties of doubly magic nuclei, we perform Bayesian inference of the symmetry energy E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\rm sym}(\rho) and the neutron skin thickness Δ ​ r np \Delta r_{\rm np} of 48 Ca and 208 Pb within the Skyrme energy density functional (EDF). We find the inferred E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\rm sym}(\rho) and Δ ​ r np \Delta r_{\rm np} separately from CREX and PREX-2 are compatible with each other at 90 % 90\% C.L., although they are inconsistent at 68.3 % 68.3\% C.L. with CREX (PREX-2) favoring a very soft (stiff) E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\rm sym}(\rho) and rather small (large) Δ ​ r np \Delta r_{\rm np} . By combining the CREX and PREX-2 data, we obtain a soft symmetry energy around saturation density ρ 0 \rho_{0} and thinner Δ ​ r np \Delta r_{\rm np} of 48 Ca and 208 Pb, which are found to be closer to the corresponding results from CREX alone, implying the PREX-2 is less effective to constrain the E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\rm sym}(\rho) and Δ ​ r np \Delta r_{\rm np} due to its lower precision of Δ ​ F CW \Delta F_{\rm CW} . Furthermore, we find the Skyrme EDF results inferred by combining the CREX and PREX-2 data nicely agree with the measured dipole polarizabilities α D \alpha_{D} in Ca 48 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 48$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ca} and Pb 208 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 208$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Pb} as well as the neutron matter equation of state from microscopic calculations. The implications of the inferred soft E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\rm sym}(\rho) around ρ 0 \rho_{0} are discussed.

Since we focus on doubly magic nuclei, pairing interaction is not taken

\rho_{0}

\rho_{0}

The calibration and uncertainty quantification of the ten parameters is carried out using a Bayesian approach. According to Bayes’ theorem, the posterior distribution of model parameters
\bm{p}
, given experimental data
\mathcal{O}^{\rm{exp}}
for a set of observables
\mathcal{O}
, can be evaluated as

\displaystyle P\left(\bm{p}\mid\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}^{\exp}\right)=\frac{P\left(\mathcal{O}^{\exp}\mid\mathcal{M},\bm{p}\right)P(\bm{p})}{\int P\left(\mathcal{O}^{\exp}\mid\mathcal{M},\bm{p}\right)P(\bm{p})\mathrm{d}\bm{p}},

(2)

where
\mathcal{M}
is the given model,
P(\bm{p})
is the prior probability density of model parameters
\bm{p}
before being confronted with the data
\mathcal{O}^{\exp}
, and
P\left(O^{\exp}\mid\mathcal{M},\bm{p}\right)
denotes the likelihood of observing
\mathcal{O}^{\exp}
with given model
\mathcal{M}
predictions at
\bm{p}
.
The prior distribution of
\bm{p}
are normally chosen to be uniform in their empirical
ranges listed in Tab.
1
. In particular, given the rather thick (thin) neutron skin in
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 208$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Pb}
(
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 48$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ca}
) from PREX-2 (CREX), the prior ranges of
E_{\mathrm{sym}}(\rho_{0})
and
L
are taken to be as large as
22\sim 55
MeV and
-90\sim 240
MeV, respectively, to avoid the prior range dependence of the posterior results.

The likelihood function is taken to be the commonly used Gaussian form

\displaystyle P\left(\mathcal{M},O_{i}^{\exp}\mid\bm{p}\right)\propto\exp\left\{-\sum_{i}\frac{\left[\mathcal{O}_{i}(\bm{p})-\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\exp}\right]^{2}}{2\sigma_{i}^{2}}\right\},

(3)

where
\mathcal{O}_{i}(\bm{p})
is the model prediction on the
i
-th observable for a given parameter set
\bm{p},\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\exp}
is the corresponding data, and
\sigma_{i}
is the adopted error.

To estimate the posterior distribution given by Eq. (
2
), the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) process is carried out using the Metropolis-Hasting algorithm. We first run
5\times 10^{5}
burn-in MCMC steps to allow the chain to reach equilibrium, and then generate
10^{6}
MCMC steps in parameter space. The posterior distributions of model parameters and observables are estimated from 15 parallel MCMC process, i.e.,
1.5\times 10^{7}
MCMC samples.

Using Bayesian inference method and the Skyrme EDF,
we have demonstrated that the CREX and PREX-2 data are compatible with each other at

68.3\%

We have further obtained a new Skyrme interaction SkREx, which can describe the CREX and PREX-2 data at
90\%
C.L.,
the measured

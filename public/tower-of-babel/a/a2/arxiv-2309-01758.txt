Infinitesimal (BiHom-)bialgebras of any weight (I): Basic definitions and properties Thanks: * Corresponding author
Tianshui Ma, Abdenacer Makhlouf *

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to introduce and study λ \lambda -infinitesimal BiHom-bialgebras (abbr. λ \lambda -infBH-bialgebra) and some related structures. They can be seen as an extension of λ \lambda -infinitesimal bialgebras considered by Ebrahimi-Fard, including Joni and Rota’s infinitesimal bialgebras as well as Loday and Ronco’s infinitesimal bialgebras, and including also infinitesimal BiHom-bialgebras introduced by Liu, Makhlouf, Menini, Panaite. In this paper, we provide various relevant constructions and new concepts. Two ways are provided for a unitary (resp. counitary) algebra (coalgebra) to be a λ \lambda -infBH-bialgebra and the notion of λ \lambda -infBH-Hopf module is introduced and discussed. It is proved, in connexion with nonhomogeneous (co)associative BiHom-Yang-Baxter equation, that every (left BiHom-)module (resp. comodule) over a (anti-)quasitriangular (resp. (anti-)coquasitriangular) λ \lambda -infBH-bialgebra carries a structure of λ \lambda -infBH-Hopf module. Moreover, two approaches to construct BiHom-pre-Lie (co)algebras from λ \lambda -infBH-bialgebras are presented.

A triple

Infinitesimal versions of bicrossproduct, quasitriangular bialgebra, Yang-Baxter equation and Drinfeld double are defined. It is shown that the path algebra of an arbitrary quiver admits a canonical structure of infinitesimal Hopf algebra. Hom-version of infinitesimal bialgebra

[

[

with the coproduct

A generalization including both cases was provided by Ebrahimi-Fard in

Definition 1.1
.

A
BiHom-associative algebra
is a 4-tuple
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
, where
A
is a linear space,
\alpha,\beta:A\longrightarrow A
and
\mu:A\otimes A\longrightarrow A
(write
\mu(a\otimes b)=ab

\displaystyle\alpha\circ\beta=\beta\circ\alpha,\quad\alpha(ab)=\alpha(a)\alpha(b),\quad\beta(ab)=\beta(a)\beta(b),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\alpha(a)(bc)=(ab)\beta(c).

(1.2)

A BiHom-associative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is called
unitary
if there exists an element
1_{A}\in A
(called a unit) such that

\displaystyle\alpha(1_{A})=1_{A},\quad\beta(1_{A})=1_{A},\quad a1_{A}=\alpha(a),\quad 1_{A}a=\beta(a),\quad\forall a\in A.

(1.3)

A morphism
f:(A,\mu_{A},\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})\longrightarrow(B,\mu_{B},\alpha_{B},\beta_{B})
of BiHom-associative algebras is a linear map
f:A\longrightarrow B
such that
\alpha_{B}\circ f=f\circ\alpha_{A}
,
\beta_{B}\circ f=f\circ\beta_{A}
and
f\circ\mu_{A}=\mu_{B}\circ(f\otimes f)
.

Remark 1.2
.

“Yau twist”
: Let
(A,\mu)
be an associative algebra,
\alpha,\beta:A\longrightarrow A
two linear maps satisfying Eqs.(
1.1
). Then
(A,\mu\circ(\alpha\otimes\beta),\alpha,\beta)
is a BiHom-associative algebra.

Definition 1.3
.

A
BiHom-coassociative coalgebra
is a 4-tuple
(C,\Delta,\psi,\omega)
, in which
C
is a linear space,
\psi,\omega:C\longrightarrow C
and
\Delta:C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
are linear maps, such that

\displaystyle\psi\circ\omega=\omega\circ\psi,~~~(\psi\otimes\psi)\circ\Delta=\Delta\circ\psi,~~(\omega\otimes\omega)\circ\Delta=\Delta\circ\omega,

(1.4)

\displaystyle(\Delta\otimes\psi)\circ\Delta=(\omega\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta.

(1.5)

A BiHom-coassociative coalgebra
(C,\Delta,\psi,\omega)
is called
counitary
if there exists a linear map
\varepsilon:C\longrightarrow K
(called a counit) such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\circ\psi=\varepsilon,\quad\varepsilon\circ\omega=\varepsilon,\quad({\rm id}_{C}\otimes\varepsilon)\circ\Delta=\omega,\quad(\varepsilon\otimes{\rm id}_{C})\circ\Delta=\psi.

(1.6)

A morphism
g:(C,\Delta_{C},\psi_{C},\omega_{C})\longrightarrow(D,\Delta_{D},\psi_{D},\omega_{D})

such that
\omega_{D}\circ g=g\circ\omega_{C}
,
\psi_{D}\circ g=g\circ\psi_{C}

Definition 3.37
.

(
[
18
, Definition 3.18]
)
A (left) BiHom-pre-Lie coalgebra
is a 4-tuple
(C,\Delta,\psi,\omega)
where
C
is a linear space and
\Delta:C\rightarrow C\otimes C
(write
\Delta(c)=c_{[1]}\otimes c_{[2]}
),
\psi,\omega:C\rightarrow C
are linear maps satisfying

\displaystyle\psi\circ\omega=\omega\circ\psi,~\Delta\circ\psi=(\psi\otimes\psi)\circ\Delta,~\Delta\circ\omega=(\omega\otimes\omega)\circ\Delta,

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}-\Phi_{(12)}\bar{\Delta}=0,

where

\bar{\Delta}(c)=\omega\psi(c_{[1]})\otimes\omega(c_{[2][1]})\otimes c_{[2][2]}-\psi(c_{[1][1]})\otimes\omega(c_{[1][2]})\otimes\psi(c_{[2]})
and
\Phi_{(12)}(a\otimes b\otimes c)=b\otimes a\otimes c
.

Theorem 3.38
.

Let
(C,\mu,\Delta,\alpha,\beta,\psi,\omega)
be a
\lambda
-infBH-bialgebra such that
\alpha,\beta,\psi,\omega
are invertible. Then
(C,\Delta_{\star},\psi,\omega)
is a BiHom-pre-Lie coalgebra, where

\displaystyle\Delta_{\star}:C\longrightarrow C\otimes C,\ \Delta_{\star}(c)=\psi^{-1}(c_{12})\otimes\alpha^{-1}\psi\omega^{-2}(c_{11})\beta^{-1}(c_{2}).

Proof.

For all
c,d\in C
, we calculate

\displaystyle(\omega\otimes{\rm id})\Delta_{\star}(cd)\hskip-5.69054pt

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:12.2})(\ref{eq:12.4})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\beta\psi^{-1}\omega(d_{12})\otimes(\alpha^{-1}\psi(c)\alpha^{-1}\psi\omega^{-2}(d_{11}))d_{2}+\psi^{-1}\omega^{2}(c_{2})\omega(d_{1})\otimes\psi\omega^{-1}(c_{1})d_{2}

\displaystyle+\lambda\beta\omega(d_{1})\otimes\psi(c)d_{2}+\alpha\psi^{-1}\omega(c_{12})\otimes\psi\omega^{-2}(c_{11})(\beta^{-1}(c_{2})\beta^{-1}\psi(d))

\displaystyle+\lambda\alpha\psi^{-1}\omega^{2}(c_{2})\otimes\psi\omega^{-1}(c_{1})\psi(d),

and

\displaystyle({\rm id}\otimes\omega\otimes{\rm id})(\omega\psi\otimes\Delta_{\star})\Delta_{\star}(c)\hskip-8.53581pt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt\omega(c_{12})\otimes\psi^{-1}\omega(c_{212})\otimes(\alpha^{-2}\psi^{2}\omega^{-2}(c_{11})\alpha^{-1}\beta^{-1}\psi\omega^{-2}(c_{211}))\beta^{-1}(c_{22})

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt+\omega(c_{12})\otimes\alpha^{-1}(c_{112})\beta^{-1}\omega(c_{21})\otimes\alpha^{-1}\psi^{2}\omega^{-3}(c_{111})\beta^{-1}(c_{22})

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt+\lambda\omega(c_{12})\otimes\omega(c_{21})\otimes\alpha^{-1}\psi^{2}\omega^{-2}(c_{11})\beta^{-1}(c_{22})

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt+\omega(c_{12})\otimes\omega^{-1}(c_{1112})\otimes\alpha^{-1}\psi^{2}\omega^{-4}(c_{1111})(\alpha^{-1}\beta^{-1}\psi\omega^{-2}(c_{112})\beta^{-2}\psi(c_{2}))

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt+\lambda\omega(c_{12})\otimes c_{112}\otimes\alpha^{-1}\psi^{2}\omega^{-3}(c_{111})\beta^{-1}\psi(c_{2}).

Morever,

\displaystyle(\psi\otimes\omega\otimes{\rm id})(\Delta_{\star}\otimes\psi)\Delta_{\star}(c)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:1.9})}}{{=}}\omega(c_{12})\otimes\alpha^{-1}(c_{112})\beta^{-1}\omega(c_{21})\otimes\alpha^{-1}\psi^{2}\omega^{-3}(c_{111})\beta^{-1}(c_{22}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}_{\star}(c)\hskip-8.53581pt

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:1.3})(\ref{eq:1.9})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt\omega(c_{12})\otimes\omega^{-1}(c_{1112})\otimes\alpha^{-1}\psi^{2}\omega^{-4}(c_{1111})(\alpha^{-1}\beta^{-1}\psi\omega^{-2}(c_{112})\beta^{-2}\psi(c_{2}))

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07164pt+\omega^{-1}(c_{1112})\otimes\omega(c_{12})\otimes\alpha^{-1}\psi^{2}\omega^{-4}(c_{1111})(\alpha^{-1}\beta^{-1}\psi\omega^{-2}(c_{112})\beta^{-2}\psi(c_{2}))

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07164pt+\lambda\omega(c_{12})\otimes c_{112}\otimes\alpha^{-1}\psi^{2}\omega^{-3}(c_{111})\beta^{-1}\psi(c_{2})+\lambda c_{112}\otimes\omega(c_{12})\otimes\alpha^{-1}\psi^{2}\omega^{-3}(c_{111})\beta^{-1}\psi(c_{2}).

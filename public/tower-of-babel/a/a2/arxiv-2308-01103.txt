Amnon Yekutieli

Abstract
. The Künneth trick is a formula for the top cohomology of the derived tensor product of two complexes of modules over a ring. In this note we present two improvements of this formula. The first improved Künneth trick is a formula for the top cohomology of the plain tensor product of two DG modules over a nonpositive DG ring. The second trick handles the derived tensor product of two DG modules over a nonpositive DG ring. The proofs are elementary.



The
Kun̈neth trick
is a useful formula for the top cohomology of the
derived tensor product
M\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}N
of complexes of modules over a
ring
A
. It was stated without a proof as Lemma 13.1.36 in our book
[
Ye2
]
,
and earlier we stated it as Lemma 2.1 in the paper
[
Ye1
]
,
with an indirect proof relying on the convergent Künneth spectral sequence.

In this short paper we have two improved Kun̈neth tricks. Theorem

1
gives a formula for the top cohomology of

M\otimes_{A}N
, where
A
is a
nonpositive DG ring
,
M
is a bounded above

right DG
A
-module
, and
N
is a bounded above left DG
A
-module
.
Theorem
7
does the same for the
derived tensor product
M\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}N
.

In this paper we follow the definitions and notation of the
book
[
Ye2
]
. Chapter 3 of this book has a detailed treatment of DG
rings and DG modules. Chapter 7 of the book is about derived categories of DG
modules, Chapter 8 talks about derived functors, and Section 12.3 deals with

M\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}N
.
In the paragraphs below we provide a very brief recollection of this material.

We fix a commutative base ring
\mathbb{K}
(possibly
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}
), and a

nonpositive central DG
\mathbb{K}
-ring

A=\bigoplus_{i\leq 0}A^{i}
(possibly noncommutative).
We let
\bar{A}:=\operatorname{H}^{0}(A)
, which is a central
\mathbb{K}
-ring. There is
a canonical DG
\mathbb{K}
-ring homomorphism
A\to\bar{A}
.

Here are some useful special cases.
One is when
A
is a
central
\mathbb{K}
-ring
, i.e.
A=A^{0}
; in traditional
language such
A
is called a unital associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra.
Another special case is when
A
is a
weakly commutative DG ring

(see
[
Ye2
, Definition 3.3.4]
), and in this case we can take
\mathbb{K}=A^{0}
.
The conjunction of these two cases is when
A=A^{0}
is a commutative ring.

All DG
A
-modules are left DG modules by default, and there are no boundedness
conditions, except when specified.
The DG category of (left) DG
A
-modules is denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)
.
The strict subcategory
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
has the same objects as

\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)
, but its morphisms are the
strict homomorphisms, namely the degree
0
homomorphisms that commute with the
differentials, and it is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear abelian category.
Observe that for
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)
, its cohomologies

\operatorname{H}^{i}(M)
are
\bar{A}
-modules.

Given an integer
i_{0}
, we let

\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq i_{0}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)
be
the full subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)
on the DG modules
M
that are
concentrated in degrees
\leq i_{0}
, i.e.
M=\bigoplus_{i\leq i_{0}}M^{i}
.
We indentify the cateogy
\operatorname{\mathsf{M}}(A)
of
A
-modules with the full subcategory
of
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A)
on the DG modules
M
that are concentrated in degree

0
.

The derived category of DG
A
-modules is
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
. It is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear
triangulated category.
For an integer
i_{0}
, we let

\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{\leq i_{0}}(A)
be
the full subcategory on the complexes
M
whose
cohomology
is
concentrated in degrees
\leq i_{0}
. Of course
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{\leq i_{0}}(A)

is not a triangulated subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
.

Right DG
A
-modules are treated a left DG modules over the opposite DG ring

A^{\mathrm{op}}
. These are the objects of the DG category

\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})
and the triangulated category

\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})
.

Given DG modules

M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq i_{0}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})

and

N\in\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq j_{0}}(A)
,
their tensor product
M\otimes_{A}N
belongs to

\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq i_{0}+j_{0}}(\mathbb{K})
.
Similarly, given DG modules

M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{\leq i_{0}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})

and

N\in\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq j_{0}}(A)
,
their derived tensor product
M\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}N
belongs to

\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{\leq i_{0}+j_{0}}(\mathbb{K})
.

For a DG module
N\in\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq j_{0}}(A)
,
the elements of
N^{j_{0}}
are all cocycles.
Given an element
n\in N^{j_{0}}
, we denote by
[n]
the cohomology class of

n
in
\operatorname{H}^{j_{0}}(N)
.
Similarly for
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq i_{0}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})
and

m\in M^{i_{0}}
.

Theorem 1

(Plain Künneth Trick)
.

Let
A
be a nonpositive central DG
\mathbb{K}
-ring, and let

M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq i_{0}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})

and

N\in\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq j_{0}}(A)

for some integers
i_{0}
and
j_{0}
.
Then there is a unique isomorphism of
\mathbb{K}
-modules

\theta_{M,N}:\operatorname{H}^{i_{0}}(M)\otimes_{\bar{A}}\operatorname{H}^{j_{0}}(N)\xrightarrow{\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\simeq\mspace{2mu}$}}}\operatorname{H}^{i_{0}+j_{0}}(M\otimes_{A}N)

such that

\theta_{M,N}([m]\otimes[n])=[m\otimes n]

for all
m\in M^{i_{0}}
and
n\in N^{j_{0}}
.
The isomorphism
\theta_{M,N}
is functorial in
M
and
N
.

Proof.

After translating the complexes
M
and
N
, we may assume that

i_{0}=j_{0}=0
.

There is an exact sequence of
\mathbb{K}
-modules

(M\otimes_{A}N)^{-1}\xrightarrow{\mspace{5mu}\mathrm{d}\mspace{5mu}}(M\otimes_{A}N)^{0}\xrightarrow{\mspace{5mu}\pi\mspace{5mu}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(M\otimes_{A}N)\to 0,

where
\mathrm{d}
is the differential of the DG
\mathbb{K}
-module

M\otimes_{A}N
, and

\pi(m\otimes n):=[m\otimes n]
.
By degree considerations, it is not hard to see that the obvious homomorphism

M^{0}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0}\to(M\otimes_{A}N)^{0}
is bijective, and the homomorphism

(M^{-1}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0})\oplus(M^{0}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{-1})\to(M\otimes_{A}N)^{-1}

is surjective. (The second homomorphism might fail to be injective: for
elements
a\in A^{-1}
,
m\in M^{0}
and
n\in N^{0}
,
the element
(m\mspace{1.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{2.0mu}a)\otimes n-m\otimes(a\mspace{1.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{2.0mu}n)

could be nonzero in the source, but it is always zero in the target.)
Therefore we get an exact sequence of
\mathbb{K}
-modules

(2)

(M^{-1}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0})\oplus(M^{0}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{-1})\xrightarrow{\mspace{5mu}\phi\mspace{5mu}}M^{0}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0}\xrightarrow{\mspace{5mu}\pi\mspace{5mu}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(M\otimes_{A}N)\to 0,

where

\phi:=(\mathrm{d}_{M}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{N})\oplus(\operatorname{id}_{M}\otimes\mspace{2mu}\mathrm{d}_{N})
.

We have an exact sequence of
A^{0}
-modules

N^{-1}\to N^{0}\xrightarrow{\mspace{5mu}\pi_{N}\mspace{5mu}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(N)\to 0.

Applying
\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{A^{0}}(-)
to it gives the exact sequence of

\mathbb{K}
-modules

(3)

\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{-1}\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0}\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{A^{0}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(N)\to 0.

The surjection
\pi_{M}:M^{0}\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)
allows us to replace
(
3
)
with this exact sequence:

(4)

M^{0}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{-1}\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0}\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{A^{0}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(N)\to 0.

Next there is an exact sequence of
(A^{0})^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules

M^{-1}\to M^{0}\xrightarrow{\mspace{5mu}\pi_{M}\mspace{5mu}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\to 0.

Applying
(-)\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0}
to it gives the exact sequence of

\mathbb{K}
-modules

(5)

M^{-1}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0}\to M^{0}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0}\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0}\to 0.

By replacing the term

\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0}
in (
4
) with

M^{0}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0}
, and using the exact sequence (
5
),
we obtain this exact sequence:

(6)

(M^{-1}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0})\oplus(M^{0}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{-1})\xrightarrow{\mspace{3mu}\phi\mspace{3mu}}M^{0}\otimes_{A^{0}}N^{0}\xrightarrow{\mspace{3mu}\pi_{M}\otimes_{A^{0}}\pi_{N}\mspace{3mu}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{A^{0}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(N)\to 0,

where
\pi_{M}
,
\pi_{N}
and
\phi
are as above.

By comparing the exact sequences (
2
) and
(
6
) we obtain an isomorphism

\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{A^{0}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(N)\xrightarrow{\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\simeq\mspace{2mu}$}}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(M\otimes_{A}N)\mspace{2mu},\mspace{5mu}[m]\otimes[n]\mapsto[m\otimes n].

Composing this with the canonical isomorphism

\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{\bar{A}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(N)\xrightarrow{\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\simeq\mspace{2mu}$}}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{A^{0}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(N)

yields the desired isomorphism
\theta_{M,N}
.
The uniqueness and functoriality are clear.
∎

Recall that for

M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})
and

N\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)

there is the bifunctorial morphism

\eta^{\mathrm{L}}_{M,N}:M\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}N\to M\otimes_{A}N

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\mathbb{K})
, which is part of the left derived functor.

Theorem 7

(Derived Kun̈neth Trick)
.

Let
A
be a nonpositive central DG
\mathbb{K}
-ring,
and let

M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{\leq i_{0}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})
and

N\in\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{\leq j_{0}}(A)

for some integers
i_{0}
and
j_{0}
.
Then there is a unique isomorphism of
\mathbb{K}
-modules

\theta^{\mathrm{der}}_{M,N}:\operatorname{H}^{i_{0}}(M)\otimes_{\bar{A}}\operatorname{H}^{j_{0}}(N)\xrightarrow{\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\simeq\mspace{2mu}$}}}\operatorname{H}^{i_{0}+j_{0}}(M\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}N)

with the following two properties:

(i)

\theta^{\mathrm{der}}_{M,N}
is functorial in
M
and
N
.

(ii)

If
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq i_{0}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})

and
N\in\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq j_{0}}(A)
, then the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}^{i_{0}}(M)\otimes_{\bar{A}}\operatorname{H}^{j_{0}}(N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}^{i_{0}+j_{0}}(M\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}^{i_{0}+j_{0}}(M\otimes_{A}N)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta^{\mathrm{der}}_{M,N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{M,N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}^{i_{0}+j_{0}}(\eta^{\mathrm{L}}_{M,N})}

is commutative.

Proof.

As in the previous proof, we can assume that
i_{0}=j_{0}=0
.
By smart truncation of
N
we can also assume that

N\in\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq 0}(A)
.

Let
\rho:P\to M
be a semi-free resolution in

\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}_{\mathrm{str}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})

such that
\operatorname{sup}(P)=\operatorname{sup}(\operatorname{H}(M))
.
This exists by
[
Ye2
, Corollary 11.4.27]
.
The complex
P
belongs to
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}^{\leq 0}(A^{\mathrm{op}})
.

The morphism

\eta^{\mathrm{L}}_{P,N}:P\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}N\to P\otimes_{A}N

in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(A)
is an isomorphism.
To satisfy condition (ii) we have no choice but to define

\theta^{\mathrm{der}}_{P,N}
to be the unique isomorphism such that

\operatorname{H}^{0}(\eta^{\mathrm{L}}_{P,N})\circ\theta^{\mathrm{der}}_{P,N}=\theta_{P,N}
.

Next, in order to satisfy condition (i), we must define

\theta^{\mathrm{der}}_{M,N}
to be the unique isomorphism making this diagram
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{M}}(\mathbb{K})
commutative:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}^{0}(P)\otimes_{\bar{A}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}^{0}(M)\otimes_{\bar{A}}\operatorname{H}^{0}(N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}^{0}(P\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}^{0}(M\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}N)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta^{\mathrm{der}}_{P,N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}^{0}(\rho)\mspace{3mu}\otimes_{\bar{A}}\mspace{3mu}\operatorname{H}^{0}(\operatorname{id}_{N})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta^{\mathrm{der}}_{M,N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}^{0}(\operatorname{Q}(\rho)\mspace{3mu}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}\mspace{3mu}\operatorname{id}_{N})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

It is routine to verify that conditions (i) and (ii) hold.
∎

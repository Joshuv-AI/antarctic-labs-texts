HARMONIC FORMS, MINIMAL SURFACES AND NORMS ON COHOMOLOGY OF HYPERBOLIC 3 3 -MANIFOLDS
Xiaolong Hans Han

Abstract
We bound the L 2 L^{2} -norm of an L 2 L^{2} harmonic 1 1 -form in an orientable cusped hyperbolic 3 3 -manifold M M by its topological complexity, measured by the Thurston norm, up to a constant depending on M M . This generalizes two inequalities of Brock and Dunfield. We also study the sharpness of the inequalities in the closed and cusped cases, using the interaction between minimal surfaces and harmonic forms. We unify various results by defining two functionals on closed and cusped hyperbolic 3 3 -manifolds and formulate several questions and conjectures.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation and previous results

A cusped manifold is a complete noncompact hyperbolic manifold with finite volume. All manifolds are assumed oriented unless stated otherwise. By Mostow rigidity, the topology of a closed or cusped hyperbolic manifold
M
of dimension at least
3
determines its geometry. Effective geometrization attempts to seek qualitative and quantitative connections between the topological and geometric invariants of the manifold. For example, given the fundamental group, what can we say about the injectivity radius, diameter, or
2
-systole of the manifold (see
[
Whi02
,
BS11
,
BCW04
,
Bel13
]

[

]

[

,
Lüc16
]

3
-manifold by the following:

Conjecture 1.1.1
.

(
[
BV13a
]
)

Let

be a sequence of congruence covers of a fixed arithmetic hyperbolic
3
-manifold
M
. Denote the degree of the cover by
[\pi_{1}M:\pi_{1}M_{n}]

\displaystyle\textnormal{vol}(M)=6\pi\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|H_{1}(M_{n})_{tor}|}{[\pi_{1}M:\pi_{1}M_{n}]},

where

is the first homology and

For recent progress and other related works on this conjecture, see
[

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{M}\alpha\wedge*\alpha

-norms and harmonic forms also enter the picture via

\displaystyle R_{j}(M)=\frac{\det(\int_{\gamma_{p}}\alpha_{q})}{\sqrt{\det\langle\alpha_{p},\alpha_{q}\rangle}},

\displaystyle|H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})_{tor}|\cdot\frac{R_{2}^{2}(M)}{\textnormal{vol}(M)}=\frac{1}{T_{an}(M)},

,

If

\frac{C_{1}}{\textnormal{vol}(M)}\|\cdot\|_{Th}\leq\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{2}\|\cdot\|_{Th}\text{ on }H^{1}(M;\mathbb{R}),

For all closed orientable hyperbolic

Compared to
[

]
, Brock and Dunfield introduce the tools of minimal surfaces and harmonic expansions of harmonic functions in

[

1.2
A glimpse at the noncompact case

It is natural to wonder whether a version of (
1.3
) still holds when
M
is noncompact. It turns out that the
L^{2}
-norm can depend on the geometry near infinity in a subtle way, which is not captured by topology. In the cusped case, several problems arise:

1.

One immediate challenge is that the injectivity radius of
M
is zero, and the right-hand inequality of (
1.3
) is trivial. We have to find suitable geometric invariants to replace the injectivity radius. After we develop a deeper understanding of Hodge theory and minimal surfaces on noncompact hyperbolic manifolds, we see that the geometric invariants are the systole of
M
and the intrinsic diameters of maximal cusp neighborhoods.

2.

Originally, (
1.3
) holds in the setting of closed manifolds, where
H^{1}\cong H_{2}
. Brock and Dunfield use the stability of least area incompressible surfaces in closed hyperbolic
3
-manifolds heavily, where one has uniform control on the principal curvatures on such surfaces and the area is bounded from above and below by some constant multiple of the Euler characteristic. For
H_{2}(M)
in the noncompact case, there exist closed surfaces that escape to infinity through homotopy supported in the cusps, and the uniform control on curvature is no longer as clear. In addition, the Thurston norm
\|\cdot\|_{Th}
is no longer a genuine norm on
H_{2}(M)
by the following example. Let
T
be a boundary-parallel torus. If
M
has more than one cusp, then
[T]\neq 0
in
H_{2}(M)
but
\|T\|_{Th}=0
. In this case, one questions the validity and implication of such inequality. Even for a closed minimal surface
S
with genus at least
2
supported in the thick part, a priori, there is no obstruction for a portion of
S
to enter the cusp arbitrarily deep.

3.

On the other hand, if we look at
\phi\in H^{1}(M)
,
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}
may blow up. For
M
closed, by classical Hodge theory, each
\phi\in H^{1}
has a harmonic representative, naturally with finite
L^{2}
-norm. When
M
is noncompact, there exists
\phi\in H^{1}(M)
such that
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}=\infty
: through a relatively straightforward computation in
2.3.3
, we demonstrate that for an incompressible properly embedded noncompact surface like the thrice-punctured sphere, the dual
1
-form
\alpha
has infinite
L^{2}
-norm.

1.3
Main theorem

We resolve all the issues mentioned above by imposing one condition on
H^{1}(M)
:

\psi\in H^{1}(M)
:
\psi
is represented by an
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-form
\alpha
.

Denote by
\mathcal{H}^{1}
the space of
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-forms on
M
. In
[
Zuc83
]
(see also (1.4) and comments before that in
[
MP90
]
), Zucker showed that
\mathcal{H}^{1}
is isomorphic to the image of the natural inclusion of the compactly supported first cohomology
\text{Im}(H^{1}_{0}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M))
. Here, we provide a self-contained introduction to Hodge theory for noncompact hyperbolic manifolds in Section
2.2
, based on
[
MP90
]
. We then analyze the topology of the dual surfaces of
\psi
in Section
2.3
and show that the Thurston norm restricted to
\mathcal{H}^{1}
is a genuine norm in
2.3.5
. Geometrically, we point out the beautiful coincidence that the surfaces dual to elements of
\mathcal{H}^{1}
are exactly those described recently in [
[
HW17
]
, Corollary 1.2]:

Corollary 1.3.1
.

Let
S
be a closed orientable embedded surface in a cusped
3
-manifold
M^{3}
which is not a
2
-sphere or a torus. If S is incompressible and non-separating, then
S
is isotopic to an embedded least area minimal surface.

This corollary provides us with enough regularity to obtain estimates similar to the ones for stable incompressible minimal surfaces in the closed hyperbolic case. In [
[
HW17
]
, Theorem 1.1], Huang and Wang show that for each cusped
M
, there is an explicit height
\tau
to truncate cusps off
M
to obtain a compact manifold
M_{\tau}
with boundary so that any least area closed incompressible non-separating surface stays in
M_{\tau}
[
[
HW17
]
, Theorem 5.9, Corollary 5.7] (see
Section

3.1
). Readers familiar with
[
BD17
]
may propose the following generalization of (
1.3
) for a cusped manifold
M
:

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\text{vol}(M)}}\|\cdot\|_{Th}\leq\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{\textnormal{inj}(M_{\tau})}}\|\cdot\|_{Th}\text{ on }\mathcal{H}^{1}(M),

where
c
is constant. However, we have abandoned this direction in view of the following observation.
3.1.1
combined with arguments from [
[
BD17
]
, Section 4] suffices to give a similar upper bound by
\max\{\frac{c}{\sqrt{\textnormal{inj}(M_{\tau})}},\frac{c}{\textnormal{inj}(M_{\tau})}\}
in the cusped case. However, the constant
c
is cumbersome, and the boundary
\partial M_{\tau}
gives rise to terms of a different order. This simplistic approach fails to take advantage of the thick-thin decomposition and the warped product structure of the cusp neighborhoods, and does not provide the more intrinsic upper bound by systole as in
1.3.4
.

Using properties of subharmonic functions and modified Bessel functions, we prove a version of the theorem independent of the truncation:

Theorem 1.3.2
.

For all orientable cusped
3
-manifolds
M
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\textnormal{vol}(M)}}\|\cdot\|_{Th}\leq\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}\leq\max\Bigl\{\frac{10\pi\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\textnormal{sys}(M)}},4.86\pi\sqrt{1+\frac{d^{2}}{2}}\Bigr\}\|\cdot\|_{Th}\text{ on }\mathcal{H}^{1},

(1.4)

where

is the largest intrinsic diameter of the boundary tori of the maximal cusp neighborhoods and
\textnormal{sys}(M)
denotes the length of the shortest closed geodesic in
M
.

Remark 1.3.3
.

Note that when
M
is closed, its systole equals twice the injectivity radius. Thus the coefficient
\frac{10\pi\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\textnormal{sys}(M)}}
is just
\frac{10\pi}{\sqrt{\textnormal{inj}(M)}}
from (

). Let
\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq\tau}
be a compact exhaustion for

. When
\psi\in H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})
is harmonic and has infinite

-norm, we can still bound the growth rate of
\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(M_{t})}
using its topological complexity, which counts the intersection of
\partial M
and
S\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
dual to
\psi
. See
2.3.4
.

Using Dehn fillings and Thurston-Jørgensen theory, a natural corollary is

Corollary 1.3.4
.

Let
V_{0}
be a fixed positive constant. For all but finitely many cusped manifolds
M
with volume less than
V_{0}
, we have

\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{10\pi\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\textnormal{sys}(M)}}\|\cdot\|_{Th}\text{ on }\mathcal{H}^{1}(M).

(1.5)

1.4
Topology and the Thurston norm of harmonic forms

In Section
2
, we analyze the topology of the space of
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-forms on a cusped
3
-manifold
M
, using the vector space isomorphism
\mathcal{H}^{1}\cong\text{Im}(H^{1}_{0}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M))
from
[
Zuc83
,
MP90
]
. We prove that this isomorphism is actually an isometry:

Proposition 1.4.1
.

Let
M
be a cusped
3
-manifold. The space of
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-forms
(\mathcal{H}^{1}(M),\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}})
is isometric to
(\text{Im}(H^{1}_{0}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M)),\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}})
.

In
Section

2.3
, we characterize the surfaces dual to
\mathcal{H}^{1}
as the incompressible and non-peripheral closed surfaces in
M
, which are the analogue of the surfaces representing the second homology of a closed, hyperbolic manifold. The Thurston norm on
H^{1}(M)\cong H_{2}(M,\partial M)
is a genuine norm. A natural corollary (
2.3.5
) on the Thurston norm restricted to
\mathcal{H}^{1}
is the following:

Lemma 1.4.2
.

Let
M
be a cusped
3
-manifold. Then the Thurston norm
\|\cdot\|_{Th}
on
\mathcal{H}^{1}\cong\text{Im}(H_{2}(M)\rightarrow H_{2}(M,\partial M))
is a genuine norm.

In general, the Thurston norm is only a semi-norm, which makes it impossible to bound the
L^{2}
-norm from above by the Thurston norm. That the underlying metric is hyperbolic is important for (
1.3
) and
Theorem

1.3.2
. For example, on the flat
3
-torus obtained from gluing of the unit cube
\{0\leq x,y,z\leq 1\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the
L^{2}
-norm of
[dz]
is
1
while its Thurston norm is
0
. In elliptic manifolds, there are no incompressible surfaces by considering the fundamental groups, and there are no
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-forms by Bochner type arguments or Bonnet-Myers theorem. One interesting question that Chris Leininger has asked is on which of the eight geometries we have inequalities similar to (
1.3
).

1.5
Geometry and the least area norm of harmonic forms

After analyzing the topology of the surfaces dual to
\mathcal{H}^{1}
, we show that their geometry is also regular. In particular, their least area representatives do not go deep inside the cusp. The existence of least area representatives of surfaces dual to
\mathcal{H}^{1}
has recently been established in
[
HW17
,
CHMR17
,
CHMR19
]
. We show how to truncate the manifold
M
canonically to obtain a compact subdomain
M_{\tau}
in (
3.3
). All the least area surfaces dual to classes in
\mathcal{H}^{1}
are contained in
M_{\tau}
(
3.1.1
). Their areas are bounded from below and above by their genera (
3.1.2
), as in
[
Has95
,
CHMR17
]
. Thus we can define the least area norm and show that it is controlled by the Thurston norm:

Corollary 1.5.1
.

Let
M
be a cusped
3
-manifold. Then

\pi\|\cdot\|_{Th}\leq\|\cdot\|_{LA}\leq 2\pi\|\cdot\|_{Th}\text{ on }\mathcal{H}^{1}.

(1.6)

We then define the
L^{1}
-norm on
\mathcal{H}^{1}
and show that it is equal to the least area norm in
3.2.3
. This follows from the general principle that Poincaré duality is an isometry for Hölder-conjugate norms (
3.2.4
).

1.6
Sharpness and the connection between harmonic forms, foliations, and flows

It is a natural question whether (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) can be realized. Brock and Dunfield show that the left-hand inequality of (
1.3
) is qualitatively sharp, by constructing a sequence of manifolds
M_{n}
and
\phi_{n}\in H^{1}(M_{n})
such that
\frac{\|\phi_{n}\|_{Th}}{\|\phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\sqrt{\textnormal{vol}(M_{n})}}
is a constant. The manifolds
M_{n}
cover a fixed manifold
M
. The Thurston norm scales linearly in terms of the degree of the cover by the deep work of Gabai
[
Gab83
]
while the
L^{2}
-norm scales by the square root of the degree. Quantitatively, Anil and Dunfield
[
HD20
]
find examples of
M
on which the left-hand inequality of (
1.3
) is
99.6\%
sharp. Thus it is surprising that we prove that the left-hand of (
1.3
) is never realized. This holds similarly in the cusped case.

Theorem 1.6.1
.

Let
M
be a closed or cusped
3
-manifold. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\textnormal{vol}(M)}}\|\alpha\|_{Th}<\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}
,

where
\alpha\in H^{1}(M)
for closed
M
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
for cusped
M
.

When
M
is closed, one obstruction for the equality to be realized is that a harmonic
1
-form on a hyperbolic manifold cannot have constant length. In particular, this follows from the fact that the second fundamental form of a minimal surface in a space form is the real part of a holomorphic quadratic differential, with
4g-4
zeros, and cannot be everywhere nonzero. In a private communication, F. Pallete pointed out an obstruction coming from the area of a stable minimal surface in hyperbolic
3
-manifolds, which constitutes another point of view. For details, see
5.2.1
.

We provide two additional points of view in
Section

5
. One is from dynamic theory
[
Zeg93
]
, which proves the non-existence of smooth vector fields whose flow lines are all geodesic. The other concerns the non-existence of geometric foliations
[
WW20
]
. The noncompact case is easier due to the exponential decay of a harmonic
1
-form near infinity (
Theorem

2.2.3
). We see a nice interaction between harmonic forms, minimal surfaces, and foliations in Section
5
. We end by defining two functionals on
\Psi\coloneqq
{Closed or cusped orientable hyperbolic
3
-manifolds} in
Section

5.3
:

\displaystyle D_{i}(M)\coloneqq\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}}\frac{\pi\|\alpha\|_{Th}}{\sqrt{\textnormal{vol}(M)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}}
,

and

\displaystyle D_{s}(M)\coloneqq\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}}\frac{\pi\|\alpha\|_{Th}}{\sqrt{\textnormal{vol}(M)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}}
.

These functionals provide a unifying point of view for several results regarding sharpness and the examples constructed in
[
BD17
]
. They also enable us to ask questions that do not come up in the original inequalities and may be interesting invariants to study.

2
L^{2}
Harmonic Forms and Compactly Supported Cohomology

2.1
Basic definitions

Let
M
be a complete smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
. We denote the space of smooth differential
k
-forms on
M
by
\Omega^{k}(M)
and the subspace of forms with compact support by
\Omega^{k}_{0}(M)
. When the underlying manifold
M
is clear, we denote the two spaces by
\Omega^{k}
and
\Omega^{k}_{0}
. The exterior derivative is denoted by
d
.

Definition 2.1.1
.

The
k
-th de Rham’s cohomology group of
M
is defined by:

\displaystyle H^{k}_{dR}(M)=\frac{\{\phi\in\Omega^{k}(M):d\phi=0\}}{d\Omega^{k-1}(M)}
.

By de Rham’s theorem, we may identify
H^{k}_{dR}(M)
with
H^{k}(M)
, the singular cohomology, where we always use real coefficients unless specified otherwise.

Definition 2.1.2
.

The
k
-th compactly supported cohomology of
M
is defined by:

\displaystyle H^{k}_{0}(M)=\frac{\{\phi\in\Omega^{k}_{0}(M):d\phi=0\}}{d\Omega^{k-1}_{0}(M)}
.

Next, we define
L^{2}
forms. Denote by
dx_{1}
,
dx_{2}
, …,
dx_{n}
a local orthonormal basis for the
1
-forms. A
k
-form
\phi
can be expressed locally as

\phi=\sum\limits_{I=\{i_{1}<i_{2}<...<i_{k}\}}\phi_{I}dx_{i_{1}}\wedge dx_{i_{2}}\wedge...\wedge dx_{i_{k}}
,

where
\phi_{I}\in L^{2}_{loc}
are locally square-integrable functions. The space
L^{2}\Omega^{k}(M)
consists of
\phi\in\Omega^{k}
such that

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\coloneqq\int_{M}\phi\wedge*\phi=\int_{M}|\phi(x)|^{2}d\textnormal{vol}_{g}(x)<\infty
.

The space
L^{2}\Omega^{k}(M)
is a Hilbert space with inner product

\displaystyle\langle\phi,\psi\rangle=\int_{M}\phi\wedge*\psi=\int_{M}(\phi(x),\psi(x))_{g}d\textnormal{vol}_{g}(x)
.

The formal adjoint of
d
on
\Omega^{k}_{0}
is:

d^{*}:\Omega^{k+1}_{0}\rightarrow\Omega^{k}_{0}

which is defined by the following condition:

\forall\phi\in\Omega^{k+1}_{0}
and
\psi\in\Omega^{k}_{0},

\langle d^{*}\phi,\psi\rangle=\langle\phi,d\psi\rangle
.

Note that
d^{*}
can also be defined on
k
-forms using the Hodge star operator by

d^{*}\phi=(-1)^{n(k-1)+1}*d*\phi
.

The Hodge Laplace operator

\Delta=dd^{*}+d^{*}d:\Omega^{k}_{0}\rightarrow\Omega^{k}_{0}

(2.1)

is a second-order symmetric elliptic operator. When the metric is complete, by
[
Gaf55
]
, the
L^{2}
closure of the operator
\Delta
:

\Delta:L^{2}\Omega^{k}\rightarrow L^{2}\Omega^{k}

(2.2)

is essentially self-adjoint. Its domain is
\{\omega\in L^{2}\Omega^{k}:d\omega,d^{*}\omega,dd^{*}\omega,d^{*}d\omega\in L^{2}\}
.

Definition 2.1.3
.

The
L^{2}
harmonic
k
-forms are defined by

\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)=\{\phi\in L^{2}\Omega^{k}(M):\Delta\phi=0\}
,

where
\Delta=dd^{*}+d^{*}d
. The above definition is equivalent to

\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)=\{\phi\in L^{2}\Omega^{k}(M):d\phi=d^{*}\phi=0\}
,

when the metric on
M
is complete, by
[
Gaf55
]
.

Using the Hodge-de Rham decomposition of
L^{2}\Omega^{k}(M)
(also called the Kodaira decomposition) [
[
dR84
]
],

L^{2}\Omega^{k}(M)=\mathcal{H}^{k}(M)\oplus\overline{d\Omega^{k-1}_{0}(M)}\oplus\overline{d^{*}\Omega^{k+1}_{0}(M)}
.

Definition 2.1.4
.

Relative and absolute boundary conditions for forms: Consider a manifold
M
with boundary
\partial M
, with
\iota:\partial M\rightarrow M
the natural inclusion map. For a
k
-form
\alpha
, the relative boundary condition is
\iota^{*}\alpha=0
, while the absolute boundary condition is
\iota_{\nu}\alpha=0
, where
\iota_{\nu}
is the interior multiplication with the inward-pointing unit normal vector field
\nu
on
\partial M
.

The relative boundary condition resembles Dirichlet boundary condition
f|_{\partial M}=0
for a function
f
, while the absolute boundary condition resembles Neumann boundary condition
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}=0
, where
\nu
is the inward-pointing unit normal vector field on
\partial M
.

When
M
is the interior of a compact manifold
\overline{M}
with compact boundary
\partial\overline{M}
, i.e.,
M=\overline{M}-\partial\overline{M}
, the
k
th cohomology with compact support
H^{k}_{0}(M)
is isomorphic to the relative cohomology group of
\overline{M}
[
[
MP90
]
, (3.1), (3.2)]:

H^{k}_{0}(M)=H^{k}(\overline{M},\partial\overline{M})\coloneqq\frac{\{\phi\in\Omega^{k}(\overline{M}):d\phi=0,\iota^{*}\phi=0\}}{\{d\psi:\psi\in\Omega^{k-1}(\overline{M}),d\psi=0,\iota^{*}\psi=0\}}

(2.3)

where

2.2
Hodge theory on cusped
3
-manifolds

In the remainder of this section,
M
denotes a closed or cusped hyperbolic
3
-manifold unless noted otherwise. When
M
is cusped, topologically, it is homeomorphic to the interior of an irreducible compact
3
-manifold
\overline{M}
with toroidal boundary. The (topological) boundary of
M
refers to
\partial\overline{M}
. This coincides with the compactification of cusped manifolds presented in
[
MP90
]
. This subsection covers Hodge theory on cusped
3
-manifolds, in particular for the space of
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-forms
\mathcal{H}^{1}
. Most of the ideas are adapted from
[
MP90
]
, which builds a Hodge theory for geometrically finite hyperbolic
n
-manifolds with possibly infinite volume, generalizing the results on cusped manifolds from
[
Zuc83
]
, and obtains asymptotic estimates for
L^{2}
harmonic
k
-forms near infinity. The particular case that we are interested in is the following
[
MP90
,
Zuc83
]
:

Lemma 2.2.1
.

Let
M
be a cusped
3
-manifold. Then
\mathcal{H}^{1}
is isomorphic to
\text{Im}(H^{1}_{0}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M))
as vector spaces.

Note that in general, there is no relationship between harmonic forms and compactly supported cohomology or its image in
H^{1}
. For example, consider the hyperbolic disc
D=\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Its space of harmonic
1
-forms
\mathcal{H}^{1}(D)
is of infinite dimension, while
H^{1}(D)
and
H^{1}_{0}(D)
are trivial. As another example, the product space
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
has trivial
\mathcal{H}^{1}
but nontrivial
H^{1}_{0}
and
H^{1}
. These two examples are taken from a nice survey [
[
Dod16
]
, p. 292], which also discusses other subtle issues related to harmonic forms.

From
[
MP90
]
, the isomorphism in
2.2.1
can be interpreted in the following way. Take
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
; by doing a perturbation supported near infinity, we obtain
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}
such that
\alpha
is cohomologous to
\phi
in
H^{1}
. The specific construction is in
2.2.4
where we promote the isomorphism above to an isometry. From basic algebraic topology,
H^{1}_{0}(M)
is isomorphic to
H_{2}(M)
[
[
Hat02
]
, Theorem
3.35
]. There is a subtle but important difference between
\text{Im}(H^{1}_{0}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M))
and
H^{1}_{0}(M)
, which is crucial for the main theorem. Consider a boundary-parallel torus
T
in a cusp. It is clear that
T
defines a class
[T]\in H_{2}(M)
, and a
1
-form
\alpha
dual to
[T]
can be chosen to be compactly supported around
T
. If
M
has more than one cusps (boundary components), then
[T]
is nontrivial in
H_{2}(M)
. The
1
-form
\alpha
has Thurston norm zero, and it is not obvious that its harmonic norm is zero. Moreover, as
T
exits to infinity, its area approaches zero. Fortunately,
[T]=0
in
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
regardless of the number of boundary components of
M
, and hence
[T]=0
in
\text{Im}(H_{2}(M)\rightarrow H_{2}(M,\partial M))
, which is equivalent to
[\alpha]=0
in
\text{Im}(H^{1}_{0}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M))
. We can also see this from geometry by computing the
L^{2}
-norm. Lift
T
to the upper half-space model
\mathbb{H}^{3}=\{(x,y,z)|z>0\}
so that a component of its cover is
z=c
. Let
\alpha=f(z)/z\,dz
be a
1
-form dual to
T
where
f(z)
is a bump function supported in
[c-1,c+1]
with
\int f/z\,dz=1
. The
L^{2}
-norm of
\alpha
is

\displaystyle\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\int_{M}\alpha\wedge*\alpha=\int_{c-1}^{c+1}f^{2}\frac{dx\wedge dy\wedge dz}{z^{3}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\log\frac{c+1}{c-1}\right)\rightarrow 0
as
c\rightarrow\infty
.

The third equality above is a simple estimate following from
\int f/z\,dz=1
. Thus if
[\alpha]\neq 0
in
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
, one of the components of the closed surface dual to
\alpha
must have genus at least
2
, as there are no essential spheres or essential tori.

We now use the isomorphism in
2.2.1
to obtain a Fourier series expansion of
\alpha
inside a cusp neighborhood
\mathcal{C}
. By composing with an isometry if necessary, we can assume that a component
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
of the cover of
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
is given by
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}=\{(x,y,z)|z\geq\sqrt{2}\}
. The boundary torus
\mathcal{T}=\partial\mathcal{C}
is lifted to
z=\sqrt{2}
and inherits an intrinsic flat metric. The fact that
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
is cohomologous in
H^{1}
to the image of the inclusion map of
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}
implies
\alpha=df
on
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, we take advantage of the warped product structure of the cusp. Let
K_{j}(\cdot)
denote the modified Bessel functions
[
AS64
]
and
\psi_{i}
the
i
-th eigenfunction of
\Delta
on the torus
\mathcal{T}
with nonzero eigenvalue
-\lambda_{i}^{2}
(
\lambda_{0}=0
).

Lemma 2.2.2
.

Let
\alpha
be an
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-form. Then restricted to a cusp neighborhood
\mathcal{C}
, there exists a harmonic function

such that

\alpha=df,

(2.4)

whose Fourier expansion is:

f(x,y,z)=\sum_{i=1}c_{i}zK_{1}(\lambda_{i}z)\psi_{i}(x,y)

(2.5)

for some

.

Proof.

Since
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
is cohomologous to
\phi\in\text{Im}(H^{1}_{0}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M))
, we have

\alpha-\phi=df\textnormal{ for some function }f.

(2.6)

Using
2.3.2
whose proof does not depend on this lemma,
\phi
is dual to a surface whose support is disjoint from the cusp neighborhood
\mathcal{C}
, so that

on
\mathcal{C}

\alpha=df
on
\mathcal{C}
.
Since

-forms on

has a basis consisting of

.
Let
\alpha=\gamma(x,y,z,dx,dy)+\beta(x,y,z)dz=df(x,y,z)
. Using [

\displaystyle\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{i}(z)d\psi_{i}(x,y)+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\beta_{i}(z)\psi_{i}(x,y)dz.

Combined with the identity

∎

Although the function

in each cusp neighborhood, using the thick-thin decomposition,

is bounded. A bounded harmonic function on a complete finite-volume manifold is constant by

. By the properties of modified Bessel functions, both the

.

If
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
, then on a neighborhood of a rank-

\alpha=\gamma+\beta dz
where
\gamma
is a
1
-form and

. Asymptotically, they satisfy

|\gamma|=\mathcal{O}(e^{-\lambda z})

and

\beta=\mathcal{O}(e^{-\lambda z})
,

for some

.

See also
[

]

The isomorphism

\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}\coloneqq\inf\{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}\textnormal{ for }\psi\in H^{1}_{0},\textnormal{ cohomologous to }\phi\textnormal{ in }H^{1}\}.

(2.8)

Proposition 2.2.4
.

Let
M
be a cusped
3
-manifold. The space of

1
-forms
(\mathcal{H}^{1}(M),\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}})

(\text{Im}(H^{1}_{0}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M)),\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}})
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the proof of [

MP90
]
, Theorem 3.13], where they prove that the two vector spaces are isomorphic. Fix a cusp neighborhood

, with coordinate

\partial N
. Near

1

\alpha=\gamma(s)+h(s)ds,

where

. By [
[
MP90
]

\displaystyle R\alpha(s)=\int_{0}^{s}h(t)dt,

(2.10)

so that near

\alpha=dR\alpha+\gamma(0)

(2.11)

for

closed. Take a cutoff function

equal to

near

vanishes near
\partial N

F:\mathcal{H}^{1}\rightarrow\text{Im}(H^{1}_{0}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M)),F(\alpha)=\alpha-d(hR\alpha)

is an isomorphism as proven in [
[

By computation, near

\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(N)}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}|\gamma(s)|^{2}\frac{1}{s}+|h(s)|^{2}s^{3}ds
.

Since
\alpha\in L^{2}
, we have
\gamma(s)=\gamma(0)=0
near

.
Define
N_{i}\coloneqq[0,\frac{\epsilon}{i})\times\mathbb{T}^{2}

\displaystyle\|\alpha-\alpha_{i}\|_{L^{2}}=\|d(f_{i}R\alpha)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\epsilon/i}s^{3}\left|\partial_{s}f_{i}(s)\right|^{2}\left|\int_{0}^{s}h(t)dt\right|^{2}+|f_{i}(s)|^{2}|h(s)|^{2}ds

,

as

from

.

2.3
Topology of
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-forms and the Thurston norm

By (
2.3
),
H^{1}_{0}(M)\cong H^{1}(\overline{M},\partial\overline{M})\cong H_{2}(M)
. It is a classical fact that every nontrivial
[S]\in H_{2}(M)
for both closed and cusped
M
is represented by a (possibly disconnected) closed smoothly embedded incompressible surface. This follows from Poincaré duality and the regular value theorem
[
Thu86
]
. We will henceforward assume every class in
H_{2}
is represented by such a closed smoothly embedded incompressible surface.

Definition 2.3.1
.

A class
[S]\in H_{2}(M)
is peripheral if it is homologous to a union of boundary components. Otherwise, it is called non-peripheral.

We use the absolute value of the Euler characteristic to measure the topological complexity of classes whose representatives are surfaces with genus
\geq 2
. For a connected surface
S
,
\chi_{-}(S)\coloneqq\max\{-\chi(S),0\}
, where
\chi
is the Euler characteristic. Extend this to disconnected surfaces via
\chi_{-}(S\sqcup S^{\prime})=\chi_{-}(S)+\chi_{-}(S^{\prime})
. For a compact irreducible
3
-manifold
M
, the Thurston norm
[
Thu86
]
of
\alpha\in H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{Z})
is defined by

\|\alpha\|_{Th}=\min\{\chi_{-}(S)\,|\,S\text{ is a properly embedded surface dual to }\alpha\}
.

The Thurston norm extends to
H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Q})
by making it linear on rays through the origin, and then extends by continuity to
H^{1}(M;\mathbb{R})
. When
M
is closed and hyperbolic, it is non-degenerate and a genuine norm. It is only a semi-norm on
H^{1}_{0}(M)\cong H_{2}(M)
for
M
with two or more cusps. However, the story is different for
\mathcal{H}^{1}\cong\text{Im}(H_{2}(M)\rightarrow H_{2}(M,\partial M))
. We first give a classification of
S\in\text{Im}(H_{2}(M)\rightarrow H_{2}(M,\partial M))
:

1.

S
Peripheral. There exists a proper map
u:(M,\partial M)\rightarrow(I,\partial I)
, where
I
is a closed interval. If we denote by
[dx]
a generator for
H^{1}_{0}(I)
, then the
1
-form
\alpha
dual to
S
satisfies
[\alpha]=[u^{*}(dx)]
. Moreover,
S
is homologous in
H_{2}
to some boundary-parallel tori and hence represents the trivial class in
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
. Thus a nontrivial
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
cannot be dual to a closed peripheral surface.

(a)

Peripheral, trivial and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}=0

(b)

Non-peripheral, nontrivial and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}\neq 0

Figure 1
:
Classification of
\text{Im}(H_{2}(M)\rightarrow H_{2}(M,\partial M))

2.

S
Non-peripheral. By
H_{2}(M,\partial M)\cong[M:S^{1}]
,
S
is the fiber of a regular value of a smooth map
u:M\rightarrow S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. Take
u
to be harmonic or the energy-minimizing representative in its homotopy class. The nontrivial harmonic
1
-form
\alpha=u^{*}(d\theta)
is dual to
S
, where
[d\theta]
is a standard generator for
H^{1}(S^{1})
.

We have obtained

Lemma 2.3.2
.

A nonzero
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-form is dual to a non-peripheral surface.

Conversely, for a noncompact incompressible surface
S
with finite topology whose ends are properly embedded, like a thrice punctured sphere embedded inside a cusped
3
-manifolds
[
Ada85
]
, the

-norm of its dual
1
-form
\alpha
must be infinite. This is well-known to the experts, but we provide a short proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.3.3
.

If
[S]\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)\backslash\text{Im}(H_{2}(M)\rightarrow H_{2}(M,\partial M))
, then a harmonic
1
-form
\alpha
dual to
S
does not have finite
L^{2}
-norm.

Proof.

If the
1
-form
\alpha
dual to
S
has finite
L^{2}
-norm, by
2.2.4
and
\text{Im}(H^{1}_{0}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M))\cong\text{Im}(H_{2}(M)\rightarrow H_{2}(M,\partial M))
,
S
must be homologous to the image of a class
[S^{\prime}]\in H_{2}(M)
, a contradiction.
∎

In fact, if we let
\{M_{t}\}
denote a certain compact exhaustion of
M
, then we can derive a lower bound on
\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(M_{t})}
from the number of algebraic intersections of
[S]
and
[\partial M]
. Let
S
be a surface dual to
\alpha
whose number of intersections with
\partial M
realizes
\#[S]\cap[\partial M]
. Let
\mathbb{H}^{3}=\{(x,y,z)|z>0\}
. Assume that an end of
S
in a truncated cusp
\mathcal{C}
is lifted to a disk asymptotic to
z=\infty
by composing with isometries of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
if necessary. For simplicity, assume the base of
\mathcal{C}
is a unit square. Parameterize
\mathcal{C}
by
\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{H}^{3}\,|\,(x,y)\in[0,1]\times[0,1],z\geq c\}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{t}
denote points in
\mathcal{C}
whose
z
-coordinate is
\leq t
. The ends of
S
are homotopic to totally geodesic, infinite annuli inside the cusps (see, for example,
[
CHR18
]
). Assume for now
S
only has one end in
\mathcal{C}
. After a change of coordinate, we also suppose that this end is parameterized by
\{(1/2,y,z)\in\mathcal{C}\}
. The several assumptions made above do not change the order of growth. Then the (asymptotic) lower bound on
\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{t})}^{2}
is
\mathcal{O}(\log t)
. On each horizontal torus
T_{z}
,
\|\alpha\|^{2}
integrates to approximately

along a Euclidean geodesic
\{(x,1/2,z):x\in[0,1]\}
intersecting the end exactly once. Integrating in the

\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{t})}^{2}\gtrsim\int_{c}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}\frac{1}{z^{3}}dxdydz\sim\log t
.

If
S
has
k
algebraic intersections with
\partial\mathcal{C}
, since
S
is properly embedded, the

. If
M
has only one cusp

, then we define

to be the union of

with the thick part. If

to be the union of the thick part with an exhaustion of all the cusps relative to the boundary of an (embedded) maximal cusp neighborhood, where

corresponds to

\mathbb{H}^{3}
. Thus, we have proven the following.

Proposition 2.3.4
.

Let
{M_{t}}
be the above compact exhaustion of
M
. If
\alpha\in H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})
is harmonic and dual to
[S]\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)\backslash\text{Im}(H_{2}(M)\rightarrow H_{2}(M,\partial M))
, then

\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(M_{t})}\gtrsim\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log t}).

This is consistent with [
[
MP90
]
, (4.9)], whose proof is based on harmonic analysis.
A criterion for the Thurston norm to be a genuine norm is the following
[

]
: For
H_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
(or
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{Z})
), if every embedded surface representing a nonzero element has negative Euler characteristic, then
\|\cdot\|_{Th}
is a norm. When
M
is cusped, all incompressible tori are boundary-parallel, and there is no incompressible surface with positive Euler characteristic. Thus the following holds.

Proposition 2.3.5
.

When
M
is a cusped
3
-manifold,
\|\cdot\|_{Th}
on
\mathcal{H}^{1}\cong\text{Im}(H_{2}(M)\rightarrow H_{2}(M,\partial M))
is a genuine norm.

In addition to the Thurston norm, we need the

- and

-norms. For a general
L^{p}
theory, see
[

]
. The
L^{\infty}
-norm of an
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-form

is defined by

\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}}=\max_{p\in M}|\alpha_{p}|.

(2.14)

Since
\alpha
decays exponentially near infinity and

is finite-volume, its
L^{\infty}
-norm is well-defined. We also define the following
L^{1}
-norm on
\text{Im}(H^{1}_{0}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M))
:

\|\psi\|_{L^{1}}=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{M}|\phi|\textnormal{ for }\phi\in\Omega^{1}_{0}(M)\textnormal{ representing }\psi\in\text{Im}(H^{1}_{0}(M)\rightarrow H^{1}(M))\Bigr\}.

(2.15)

When
M
is closed, the definition of the
L^{1}
-norm is just:

\|\psi\|_{L^{1}}=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{M}|\phi|\textnormal{ for }\phi\in\Omega^{1}(M)\textnormal{ representing }\psi\in H^{1}(M)\Bigr\}.

(2.16)

The
L^{1}
-norm behaves very differently from the
L^{2}
-norm on the cohomology space, as it tends to be realized by non-smooth forms. A sequence of forms
\phi_{i}
with compact support converging to
\phi
realizing the

-norm of
[\phi]
is similar to a sequence of bumps functions converging to a Dirac functional. Later we will use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to bound the
L^{1}
-norm of
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
by its
L^{2}
-norm. To bound the
L^{2}
-norm of
\alpha
, we convert it into some integral of
\alpha
on a surface
S
dual to
\alpha
. When the manifold
M
is closed, it is clear by Poincaré duality that integrating against a closed integral
1
-form is equal to integrating against its dual surface. Specifically, fix a surface
S
dual to
\phi\in H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})
and let
\alpha
be the harmonic representative of
\phi
. Then we have
\int_{M}\beta\wedge\alpha=\int_{S}\beta
for every closed
2
-form
\beta
. When
M
is closed, if
\alpha
is harmonic, it is closed and coclosed. When
M
is noncompact, a similar conclusion on
\alpha
holds if the metric is complete. Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
correspond to
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, dual to
[S]\in\text{Im}(H_{2}(M)\rightarrow H_{2}(M,\partial M))
. By Poincaré duality for compactly supported cohomology [
[
BT82
]
, I.5], we have again integral equality
\int_{M}\beta\wedge\phi=\int_{S}\beta
for every closed
2
-form
\beta
. From this, we deduce

Proposition 2.3.6

(Poincaré duality for cusped manifolds)
.

Let
M
be a cusped
3
-manifold. Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
correspond to
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, dual to a surface
S
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\int_{S}*\alpha.

(2.17)

Proof.

Since

3
Minimal Surfaces and Least Area Norm

In this section, we discuss minimal surfaces and their properties in closed and cusped
3
-manifolds. A closed
2
-sided surface is minimal if its mean curvature is
0
. Let
\nu
denote a unit normal vector field on a surface
S
with a trivial normal bundle. A normal vector field
X
may be written as
f\nu
, where
f
is some smooth function. The stability operator
L
is defined as

Lf=\Delta_{S}f+|\sigma|^{2}f+Ric_{M}(\nu,\nu)f,

(3.1)

where
Ric_{M}
is the Ricci tensor of the ambient manifold
M
,
\sigma
is the second fundamental form on
S
, and
\Delta_{S}
is the intrinsic Laplacian operator of
S
. A minimal surface
S
is stable if for all compactly supported variations
F
with fixed boundary,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\right|_{t=0}Area(F(S,t))=-\int_{S}\langle F_{t},LF_{t}\rangle dA\geq 0
.

This is equivalent to the stability operator being negative semidefinite. A least area surface in a homotopy class is necessarily stable, but not vice versa. The area of a least area surface
F
with genus
g\geq 2
in a closed or cusped hyperbolic manifold
M
satisfies the following inequalities ([
[
Has95
]
, Lemma 6], [
[
MR17
]
, Section 8.1] and [
[
CHMR17
]
, Remark 3]):

2\pi(g-1)\leq area(F)\leq 4\pi(g-1);

(3.2)

this was originally observed in an unpublished work of Uhlenbeck
[
Uhl80
]
. The left-hand inequality in (
3.2
) comes from the stability of
F
in a hyperbolic
3
-manifold, while the right-hand inequality follows directly from the Gauss equation for a minimal surface in a space form. The existence of least area surface representatives in the homology class of
[S]\in H_{2}(M)
for
M
compact is well-understood (see Federer
[
Fed69
]
). Freedman, Hass, and Scott
[
FHS83
]
show that in a compact
3
-manifold, a least area surface in the homotopy class of an embedded surface is embedded. The existence of least area incompressible closed surfaces in the cusped setting requires techniques other than the usual minimizing techniques in compact settings in
[
SY79
,
SU82
]
, and was recently settled by Huang and Wang
[
HW17
]
and by Collin, Hauswirth, Mazet, and Rosenberg
[
CHMR17
,
CHMR19
]
. A cusped
M
is geometrically noncompact and the surfaces can enter the cusps arbitrarily deeply. A cusp bears some similarity with a short tube in that they are both foliated by intrinsic Euclidean tori. This foliation is useful in some area estimates used in cut-and-paste arguments, which provide obstructions for the least area closed surface to enter arbitrarily deeply inside the cusp. If the surface is sufficiently deep, the part inside the cusp will give rise to a large area contribution. One then cuts off the cusp at a torus at some height, obtains a surface with some boundary components, and glues the disks coming from the cutoff torus. This reduces the area. Similar ideas are also used in
[
HW19
]
to investigate minimal foliation questions on hyperbolic
3
-manifolds fibering over
S^{1}
. In conclusion, we have

Theorem 3.0.1
.

[
[
HW17
]
, Corollary 1.2]

Let
S
be a closed orientable embedded surface in a cusped
3
-manifold
M
without torus components. If
S
is incompressible and non-separating, then
S
is isotopic to an embedded least area minimal surface.

3.2

We are now ready to define the least area norm. We first define it with integral coefficients and then extend it by linearity and continuity. Denote

For

Let

\pi\|\cdot\|_{Th}\leq\|\cdot\|_{LA}\leq 2\pi\|\cdot\|_{Th}\text{ on }\mathcal{H}^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z}).

(3.6)

To bound

-norm on the cohomology class of a harmonic form

For

-norm satisfy

For the full proof when

]

Using the above Lemma, the least area norm extends continuously from

Let

\displaystyle=\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}}Area(S)\leq 2\pi\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\alpha\|_{Th},

with volume
\leq V_{0}
, the systole term in the right-hand side of (
1.4
) dominates the diameter term.
∎

5
Sharpness of the Inequalities and a Functional Point of View

In this section, we assume the manifold is closed as in (
1.3
) unless stated otherwise. We first study the left-hand inequality of (
1.3
).

Theorem 5.0.1
.

There does not exist a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold
M
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\textnormal{vol}(M)}}\|\alpha\|_{Th}=\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}
for some
\alpha\in H^{1}
.

We start by investigating various conditions that the
1
-form
\alpha
and its dual surface
S
must satisfy for the equality to hold.

5.1
Three conditions for the sharpness and their interactions

Recall that if
\phi\in H^{1}
corresponds to
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
, the
L^{1}
-norm on the cohomology class of
\phi
is
\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}=\inf_{\phi^{\prime}\sim\phi\in H^{1}}\int_{M}|\phi^{\prime}|
. Using
5.2.2
, let
S
be a closed minimal surface whose tangent bundle
TS\subset\ker(\alpha)
.

Proposition 5.1.1
.

If
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
is a harmonic
1
-form such that

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\textnormal{vol}(M)}}\|\alpha\|_{Th}=\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}
,

it must satisfy the following three conditions:

1.

It has constant length:
|\alpha_{p}|=c
on
M
;

2.

Its
L^{1}
-integral coincides with the
L^{1}
-norm of its cohomology class:

\int_{M}|\alpha|=\|\alpha\|_{L^{1}};

(5.1)

3.

It must satisfy

Area(S)=\pi\chi_{-}(S).

(5.2)

This is equivalent to
\int_{S}|\sigma|^{2}=2Area(S)
or
\int_{S}h_{11}^{2}+h_{12}^{2}=\int_{S}1
, where
\sigma
is the second fundamental form associated to
S
with components
h_{11}
,
h_{12}
,
h_{21}
,
h_{22}
.

The proof of the above proposition is primarily bookkeeping; see the proof of [
[
BD17
]
, Theorem 1.2, 3.2]. Condition
1
is more likely to hold on Seifert fibered manifolds than hyperbolic ones; an example is the
1
-form
dz
on the
3
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}
obtained from the gluing of a standard unit cube in the Euclidean space. Condition
2
also seems unlikely, as commented in
[
BD17
]
:
\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}
is typically realized by a non-smooth form, supported like a Dirac measure on the surface. Condition
3
is not possible as discussed in
5.2.1
. Our first observation is that Condition
1
implies Conditions
2
and
3
.
The next lemma is a straightforward corollary of [
[
Kat07
]
, Proposition 16.9.1].

Lemma 5.1.2
.

Let
M
be a closed Riemannian
n
-manifold. Let
\alpha
be a nontrivial harmonic
1
-form with constant length. Then it realizes the
L^{1}
-norm of its cohomology class.

Proof.

From [
[
Kat07
]
, Proposition 16.9.1], we have that existence of a nontrivial harmonic
1
-form
\alpha
with constant length implies that

\|\alpha\|_{L^{1}}\frac{1}{\textnormal{vol}(M)}=\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\textnormal{vol}(M)}},

(5.3)

which implies

\|\alpha\|_{L^{1}}=\sqrt{\textnormal{vol}(M)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}.

If
\alpha
has constant length
c
,

\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{L^{1}}=\textnormal{vol}(M)c=\int_{M}|\alpha|
.

∎

Now we prove that Condition
1
implies Condition
3
on a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold. The proof is partially inspired by the proof of [
[
Ste19
]
, Theorem 1.1].

Lemma 5.1.3
.

Let
M
be a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold. If
\alpha
is a nontrivial
L^{2}
harmonic
1
-form with constant length, then there exists a closed minimal surface
S
dual to
\alpha
, such that

Area
(S)=\pi\chi_{-}(S)
.

Proof.

Let
|\alpha_{p}|=c
, where
c
is a constant. By the Bochner formula for
1
-forms, we have

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\alpha|^{2}=|\nabla\alpha|^{2}-2|\alpha|^{2}\implies|\nabla\alpha|^{2}=2|\alpha|^{2}=2c^{2}
.

As a closed form,
\alpha=df
locally in a ball. Denote
\nabla f
by
V
. Using
5.2.2
, there is a closed minimal surface
S
whose tangent bundle
TS\subset\ker(\alpha)
. Since
|V|=|\alpha|
is constant, we have for all vector fields
W
on
M
,

0=\langle\nabla_{W}V,V\rangle=\textnormal{Hess}(f)(W,V).

(5.4)

The Hessian of
f
,
\textnormal{Hess}(f)=\{h_{ij}\}
, is a symmetric
3\times 3
matrix, with the first two columns corresponding to vectors tangent to
S
, and last column to vectors parallel to
V
.
Equation

5.4
implies that the last column and the last row consist of zeros. Thus we have
|\nabla\alpha|=|\nabla\alpha|_{S}|
.
The second fundamental form of
S
is

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}=\frac{\nabla\alpha}{|\alpha|}\Big|_{S},

(5.5)

which implies that

\displaystyle|\sigma_{S}|^{2}=2\implies\int_{S}h_{11}^{2}+h_{12}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}|\sigma_{S}|^{2}=\int_{S}1
.

The rest of the arguments are identical to the proof of [
[
Has95
]
, Lemma 6]. Using the Gauss-Bonnet theorem, the Gauss formula and the hyperbolicity of the metric, we have

\int_{S}h_{11}^{2}+h_{12}^{2}=-2\pi\chi(S)+\int_{S}-1=-Area(S)-2\pi\chi(S)
,

which gives
Area(S)=-\pi\chi(S)=\pi\chi_{-}(S)
.
∎

5.2
Proof of non-sharpness using harmonic
1
-forms and minimal foliations

Now we verify our intuition about constant length harmonic
1
-forms: such forms do not exist in hyperbolic manifolds. Manifolds all of whose harmonic forms are constant length are interesting in their own right, and have connections with systolic geometry. See
[
NV04
]
and citations therein. We provide four different points of view, each producing a proof of the following proposition.

Proposition 5.2.1
.

Let
M
be a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold. No nontrivial harmonic
1
-forms of
M
have constant length.

Proof.

Using [
[
MR17
]
, 8.1] and [
[
G0́9
]
, Theorem 12], a (connected) immersed stable orientable closed minimal surface
S
of genus
g
in a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold cannot have area
2\pi(g-1)=\pi\chi_{-}(S)
, contradicting
5.1.3
. If
S
dual to
\alpha
has several components, note that
\chi_{-}(\cdot)
is additive. This provides the first proof.

Alternatively, the zeros of a holomorphic quadratic differential provide an obstruction to the existence of harmonic
1
-forms of constant length. By the proof of
5.1.3
, the existence of such a
1
-form implies that the second fundamental form
\sigma_{S}
satisfies
|\sigma_{S}|^{2}=2
on the dual surface
S
. By [
[
Kat07
]
, Lemma 16.7.1],
S
is minimal. It is a classical fact known to Bianchi that the minimality of
S
and constant sectional curvature of
M
imply that

\sigma_{S}=\textnormal{Re}(h_{11}-ih_{12})(dz)^{2}=\textnormal{Re}\,\eta,

(5.6)

where
\eta=(h_{11}-ih_{12})(dz)^{2}
is an invariantly defined holomorphic quadratic differential (see [
[
Uhl83
]
, Theorem 4.1] and
[
Law70
]
). Any such differential must vanish at exactly
4g-4\geq 4
points [
[
FM12
]
, p. 312], where
g=g(S)
. Thus it is impossible that
|\sigma_{S}|^{2}=2
identically. This completes the second proof.
∎

We now describe obstructions from flows and foliations, which provide the last two proofs. We first need a lemma which relates harmonic
1
-forms of constant length to minimal foliations [
[
Kat07
]
, Lemma 16.7.1]:

Lemma 5.2.2
.

If
\alpha
is a harmonic
1
-form of constant length, then the leaves of the distribution ker(
\alpha
) are minimal surfaces, and the vector field
V
that is dual
to
\alpha
has geodesic flow lines orthogonal to ker(
\alpha
).

Combining this with the following result of Zeghib, we obtain an obstruction from dynamical system theory and provide a third proof of
5.2.1
:

Theorem 5.2.3
.

(Zeghib
[
Zeg93
]
)
There is no smooth vector field on a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold all of whose flow lines are geodesics. As a corollary, there do not exist harmonic
1
-forms of constant length on
M
.

Note that the flow lines given by harmonic
1
-forms are smooth, while Zeghib only requires continuity. On the other hand, the minimal foliations described in
5.2.2
are specific examples of the geometric foliations defined in [
[
WW20
]
, Definition 1.4]:

Definition 5.2.4
.

Let
S
be a closed surface,
\epsilon>0
a constant, and
h
an embedding

h:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\times S\rightarrow M.

(5.7)

We say
h
is a geometric
1
-parameter family of closed minimal surfaces if

1.

h
is
C^{2}
with respect to both
t
and
p\in S
.

2.

for all
t
, the leaf
h_{t}\subset M
is a minimal surface.

3.

for all
p\in S
,
f(t,p)\coloneqq\langle(h_{t})_{*}(\partial_{t}),\nu\rangle|_{t=0}
only depends on the principal curvature of
S
at p. One writes
f(0,p)=f(0,\|\sigma_{S}\|^{2}(p))
where
\sigma_{S}
is the second fundamental form of
\{0\}\times S
at
(0,p)
in
M
.

4.

f(0,\cdot):S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is not identically
0
.

The foliation in
5.2.2
with orthogonal geodesic flow lines is the strongest geometric foliation one can hope for in
5.2.4
and corresponds to
f\equiv 1
. The non-existence of such foliations is proved in the following.

Theorem 5.2.5
.

[
[
WW20
]
, Theorem 1.4]

A closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold does not admit a geometric foliation by minimal surfaces, and hence no harmonic
1
-forms have constant length.

This provides the fourth and last proof of
5.2.1
. For the cusped case, to achieve equality, the same arguments go through and show that we still need to satisfy the three conditions in
5.1.1
. Condition
2
that
\alpha
has a constant operator norm is no longer possible since it decays exponentially near the cusp.

Corollary 5.2.6
.

There does not exist a cusped
3
-manifold
M
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\textnormal{vol}(M)}}\|\alpha\|_{Th}=\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}
for some
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
.

5.3
A functional point of view

We define two functionals
D_{i}
and
D_{s}
on the collection
\Psi\coloneqq
{Closed or cusped hyperbolic
3
-manifolds}. It unifies various phenomena in this paper and in
[
BD17
]
and allow us to ask new questions. Define

\displaystyle D(\alpha)\coloneqq\frac{\pi\|\alpha\|_{Th}}{\sqrt{\textnormal{vol}(M)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}}\textnormal{ for }\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)-\{0\}.

(5.8)

Then we define

\displaystyle D_{i}(M)\coloneqq\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}}D(\alpha)\textnormal{ and }\displaystyle D_{s}(M)\coloneqq\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{1}}D(\alpha).

(5.9)

The left-hand inequality of (
1.4
) and the rigidity discussed in the last section can be phrased in terms of
D_{s}
. Let
B_{H}
denote the unit sphere in
(\mathcal{H}^{1}(M;\mathbb{R}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}})
.

Proposition 5.3.1
.

Let
M
be a closed or cusped hyperbolic
3
-manifold. Then
D_{s}(M)<1
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{j}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
such that
D(\alpha_{j})\rightarrow 1
. Define
\widetilde{\alpha_{j}}\coloneqq\alpha_{j}/\|\alpha_{j}\|_{L^{2}}
so that
\widetilde{\alpha_{j}}\in B_{H}
. Since the Thurston norm is linear, we have
D(\widetilde{\alpha_{j}})\rightarrow 1
. Since
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,R)
is finite-dimensional for both closed and cusped
M
,
B_{H}
is compact. Thus
\widetilde{\alpha_{j}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\alpha}\in B_{H}
such that
D(\widetilde{\alpha})=1
. This contradicts
Theorem

5.0.1
.
∎

From the proof, we also deduce that there exist
\alpha_{i},\;\alpha_{s}\in B_{H}
such that
D_{i}(M)=D(\alpha_{i})
and
D_{s}(M)=D(\alpha_{s})
. Using the right-hand side of (
1.3
) or (
1.4
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\|\cdot\|_{Th}}{\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{\textnormal{inj}(M)}}{10\pi}
or
\displaystyle 1/\max\{\frac{10\pi\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\textnormal{sys}(M)}},4.86\pi\sqrt{1+\frac{d^{2}}{2}}\}
.

for closed and cusped
M
, respectively. Thus
0<D_{i}
. Summarizing both sides, we have

0<D_{i}\leq D_{s}<1.

(5.10)

When the first betti number
b_{1}=1
,
D_{i}=D_{s}
. Using a computer algorithm, Dunfield and Hirani
[
HD20
]
found examples of fibered hyperbolic
3
-manifolds with
D_{s}\geq 0.95
and
D_{s}\geq 0.994
, which provide the original motivation for the present author to investigate whether
D_{s}
can equal
1
. We have the following question.

Question 5.3.2
.

Is there an
\epsilon>0
such that
\{M|D_{i}(M)>1-\epsilon\}
=
{
fibered hyperbolic
3
-manifolds with some special geometry
}
? Is there a sequence
M_{j}
such that
D_{i}(M_{j})\rightarrow 1
?

See
[
BS19
]
for related theorems on the rigidity of the ratio of Thurston norm to
L^{2}
-norm on general compact
3
-manifolds. We are also interested in the existence and properties of
M
such that
D_{i}(M_{j})<\epsilon
or
D_{s}(M_{j})<\epsilon
, where
\epsilon
is a small positive number. We first incorporate a theorem from
[
BD17
]
to show
D_{i}
can approach
0
.

Theorem 5.3.3
.

(
[
BD17
]
, Theorem 1.4)

There exists a sequence of
M_{n}
and
\phi_{n}\in H^{1}(M_{n};\mathbb{R})
so that

1.

The volumes of the
M_{n}
are uniformly bounded and
\textnormal{inj}(M_{n})\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

2.

\|\phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}/\|\phi_{n}\|_{Th}\rightarrow\infty
like
\sqrt{-\log(\textnormal{inj}(M_{n}))}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

The manifolds
M_{n}
above come from the Dehn filling on the complement of the two-component link
L=L14n21792
, all with Betti number
1
. Since all
M_{n}
have volumes bounded from above by the hyperbolic volume of the complement of
L
by the Thurston-Jørgensen theory, we have the following corollary:

Corollary 5.3.4
.

There exists a sequence of
M_{n}
obtained by Dehn filling the complement of a link so that

D_{i}(M_{n})\rightarrow 0
.

One can ask how to classify all sequences of
M_{n}
such that
D_{i}(M_{n})\rightarrow 0
. The following theorem shows what happens when we take finite covers.

Theorem 5.3.5
.

(
[
BD17
]
, Theorem 1.3)

There exists a sequence of
M_{n}
and
\phi_{n}\in H^{1}(M_{n};\mathbb{R})
so that

1.

The quantities
\textnormal{vol}(M_{n})
and
\textnormal{inj}(M_{n})\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

2.

The ratio
\displaystyle\frac{\pi\|\phi_{n}\|_{Th}}{\sqrt{\textnormal{vol}(M_{n})}\|\phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}}
is constant.

The proof uses the fact that the lift of a harmonic representative is also harmonic, and hence
\|\pi^{*}(\phi)\|_{L^{2}}=\sqrt{d}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}
, where
d
is the degree of a fixed cover
\pi:M_{n}\rightarrow M
. The Thurston norm scales linearly, by a deep theorem of Gabai [
[
Gab83
]
, Corollary 6.13]:
\|\pi^{*}(\phi)\|_{Th}=d\|\phi\|_{Th}
. Thus we have

Corollary 5.3.6
.

Let
\widetilde{M}
be a finite cover of
M
. Then

D_{i}(\widetilde{M})\leq D_{i}(M)\leq D_{s}(M)\leq D_{s}(\widetilde{M}).

(5.11)

Due to the structure of
\Psi
, we expect most values of
D_{i}
and
D_{s}
to be discrete, with accumulation points only at cusped manifolds corresponding to geometric convergence.

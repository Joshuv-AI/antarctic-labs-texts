Holomorphic foliations of degree two and arbitrary dimension
Maurício Corrêa, Alan Muniz

Abstract
. Let ℱ \mathscr{F} be a holomorphic foliation of degree 2 2 on ℙ n {\mathbb{P}^{n}} with dimension k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . We prove that either ℱ \mathscr{F} is algebraically integrable, or ℱ \mathscr{F} is the linear pull-back of a degree 2 foliation by curves on ℙ n − k + 1 \mathbb{P}^{n-k+1} , or ℱ \mathscr{F} is a logarithmic foliation of type ( 1 n − k + 1 , 2 ) (1^{n-k+1},2) , or ℱ \mathscr{F} is a logarithmic foliation of type ( 1 n − k + 3 ) (1^{n-k+3}) , or ℱ \mathscr{F} is the linear pull-back of a degree-2 foliation of dimension 2 on ℙ n − k + 2 \mathbb{P}^{n-k+2} tangent to an action of the Lie algebra 𝔞 ​ 𝔣 ​ 𝔣 ​ ( ℂ ) \mathfrak{aff}({\mathbb{C}}) . As a byproduct, we describe the geometry of Poisson structures on ℙ n {\mathbb{P}^{n}} with generic rank two.

Introduction

One of the central problems in the global theory of holomorphic foliations is the description and classification of the irreducible components of the space of foliations on the projective space
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
,
n\geq 3
, at least for low degrees. A complete classification is known for degrees
0
and
1
in any dimension, and for degree
2
only in dimension
n-1
. The space
\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(d,k,n)
of degree-
d
dimension-
k
foliations on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
is a projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}H^{0}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p}(n-k+d+1))
whose general element is an integrable
(n-k)
-form without zeros of codimension one. A classical result of Darboux implies that every degree-
0
foliation is given by the fibers of a linear rational map
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{n-p}}
, thus
\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(0,k,n)
is isomorphic to the Grassmannian
G(k,n+1)
in its Plücker embedding, see
[
15
, Théorème 3.8]
. In degree
d=1
, Loray, Pereira, and Touzet
[
23
]
, generalizing upon Jouanolou
[
22
]
, proved that
\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(1,k,n)
has two irreducible components: one parameterizing linear pullbacks of degree-one foliations by curves on
{\mathbb{P}^{n-k+1}}
; the other parameterizing rational foliations of type
(1^{n-k-1},2)
, given by the fibers of a rational map
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(1^{n-k-1},2)
. For
d=2
and
p=1
, Cerveau and Lins Neto
[
5
]
proved that
\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(2,n-1,n)
has six irreducible components and described their general element. Since then, several works investigated the classification in dimension
n-1
and higher degrees
[
6
,
17
,
25
]
; most remarkably
[
13
]
where the authors present an almost complete classification for degree
3
. In a different direction, the present work focuses on degree-
2
foliations of dimension
k\leq n-2
. The main result of this work is the following.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a degree-
2
foliation on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
of dimension
k\geq 2
. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

\mathscr{F}
is algebraically integrable and
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
is (semi)stable for
k>2
(resp.
k=2
);

(2)

\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Log}(1^{n-k+1},2;k,n)
is a logarithmic foliation of type
(1^{n-r+1},2)
;

(3)

\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Log}(1^{n+1};k,n)
is a logarithmic foliation of type
(1^{n+1})
;

(4)

\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Exc}(r_{1},\dots,r_{m},k,n)
is an exceptional foliation; or

(5)

\mathscr{F}\in{\rm LPB}(2,k,n)
is a linear pullback of a foliation by curves in
{\mathbb{P}^{n-k+1}}
.

The notation is explained in §
2
. Theorem
A
gives a precise description of the irreducible components of
\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(2,k,n)
whose general element is not algebraically integrable with either
k>2
and semistable tangent sheaf; or
k=2
and stable tangent sheaf. Suppose
\mathscr{F}
is an algebraically integrable foliation of degree
2
defined by a rational
(n-k)
-form without zeros in codimension one. In that case, it is known that
\mathscr{F}
belongs to one of the rational components:
{\rm Rat}(1^{n-k-1},2,2;n)
or
{\rm Rat}(1^{n-k},3;n)
, see Remark
2.1
. To decide whether every foliation in item (1) of Theorem
A
, with
k\leq n-2
, belongs to one of the rational components is a challenging problem whose solution still eludes us. For
k=n-1
it is true, see
[
23
, Proposition 3.5]
.

The exceptional foliations in item (4) of the Theorem come from actions of the Lie algebra of affine transformations of the line
\mathfrak{aff({\mathbb{C}})}
, and these correspond to integer partitions
r_{1}+\dots+r_{m}=n-k+3
. Thus, the number of irreducible components of
\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(2,k,n)
grows with the number
p(n-k+3)
of partitions of
n-k+3
:

\#\{\text{irreducible components of }\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(2,k,n)\}\geq p(n-k+3)+1

For a discussion on foliations associated with actions of
\mathfrak{aff({\mathbb{C}})}
and the number of irreducible components, we refer to
[
29
]
.

Along our way to proving Theorem
A
we proved the following result, see Proposition
3.3
. We observe that this result was also obtained by da Costa
[
12
]
with independent techniques.

Proposition B
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a
2
-dimensional foliation of degree
d
on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
such that
h^{0}({\rm T}\mathscr{F})>0
. Then, either
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(a)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1+a-d)
,
a\in\{0,1\}
, or
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
is not locally free.

Related to holomorphic foliations is the study of Poisson structures, see §
5
for a definition. A Poisson structure of generic rank
2
on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
corresponds to a foliation of dimension
2
and degree at most
2
. In this framework, Polishchuk found in
[
30
]
a partial classification of foliations of degree
2
on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
. Coming from the foliations side, Loray, Pereira, and Touzet
[
23
]
classified Poisson structures on Fano

[
26

, Matviichuk, Pym, and Schedler classified Poisson structures on even-dimensional projective spaces which are log symplectic along the divisor

.

Let
({\mathbb{P}^{n}},\sigma)
be a holomorphic Poisson structure such that
\sigma\wedge\sigma=0
.
Suppose that the associated symplectic foliation
\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}
has no divisorial zeros. It follows from Theorem
A
that the tangent sheaf

of the associated symplectic foliation is either split, semi-stable and logarithmic, or stable with algebraic leaves. This means that either the bivector
\sigma
is globally decomposable or non-decomposable with some Poisson divisor
D

In general, we will say that a non-decomposable bivector generic rank 2 is of the

, if so is
D
.

Due to our study of foliations, we prove the following result about the geometry of Poisson structures with generic rank 2.

.

Let
({\mathbb{P}^{n}},\sigma)
be a general holomorphic Poisson structure such that
\sigma\wedge\sigma=0

Suppose that the associated symplectic foliation

If

•

\sigma=v_{0}\wedge v_{1}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}

, and the vector fields

•

\sigma=v_{0}\wedge v
,
where
v_{0}
is a constant vector field,

is a homogeneous polynomial vector field of degree
2
,

and

is the Poisson hyperplane determined by
v_{0}\in\mathbb{P}H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},T{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1))\cong\check{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}
.

•

\sigma=v_{0}\wedge v_{1}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}
, where
\mathfrak{h}=H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},T{\mathbb{P}^{n}})
and

and

are linear vector fields such

If
\sigma
is not decomposable and of normal crossing type, so either

•

\sigma\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},\wedge^{2}T_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}/\mathbb{P}^{n-2}}(-\log(D))

are hyperplanes and

•

\sigma\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},\wedge^{2}T_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}/\mathbb{P}^{n-2}}(-\log(D))

are hyperplanes and

•

\sigma\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},\wedge^{2}T{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-\log(D))

is a quadric and there is

Consider the space of Poisson structures of generic rank 2

\mathrm{Poisson}(X)_{2}:=\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,\wedge^{2}TX)\,\Big|\,[\sigma,\sigma]=0,\sigma\wedge\sigma=0\,\right\}\subset\mathrm{Poisson}(X).

2.
Holomorphic foliations

In this section, we review the basic concepts needed throughout the work. Let
X
be a smooth complex projective variety; an experienced reader may find no difficulty relaxing this requirement. A
singular holomorphic distribution

\mathscr{F}
on
X
is the data of a saturated subsheaf
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
of the tangent sheaf
{\rm T}X
of
X
. These sheaves give a short exact sequence

(2.1)

0\longrightarrow{\rm T}\mathscr{F}\longrightarrow{\rm T}X\longrightarrow{\rm N}\mathscr{F}\longrightarrow 0

where
{\rm N}\mathscr{F}
is a torsion-free sheaf, called the
normal sheaf
of
\mathscr{F}
, and
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
is a reflexive sheaf, called the
tangent sheaf
of
\mathscr{F}
. The dimension of
\mathscr{F}
is the generic rank of
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
. If
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
is involutive, i.e.
[{\rm T}\mathscr{F},{\rm T}\mathscr{F}]\subset{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
, then
\mathscr{F}
is a foliation; this means that through a general point in
X
passes a unique connected immersed (analytic) subvariety
L\subset X
such that
{\rm T}L={\rm T}\mathscr{F}|_{L}
, called a
leaf
. A foliation
\mathscr{F}
is
algebraically integrable
if its leaves are embedded algebraic varieties.

Taking exterior power on (
2.1
) we get a morphism
\omega_{\mathscr{F}}\colon\bigwedge^{p}{\rm T}X\to\det({\rm N}\mathscr{F})
, where
p=\rk{\rm N}\mathscr{F}
, whose image is
\det({\rm N}\mathscr{F})
tensored by the ideal sheaf
\mathscr{I}_{Z}
of the vanishing locus of
\omega_{\mathscr{F}}
seen as a global section of
\Omega_{X}^{p}\otimes\det({\rm N}\mathscr{F})
. We call
Z
the
singular scheme
of
\mathscr{F}
, and denote it by
\sing(\mathscr{F})
. Since
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
is saturated,
\codim(\sing(\mathscr{F}))\geq 2
.

Given a distribution
\mathscr{F}
, the twisted
p
-form
\omega_{\mathscr{F}}\in H^{0}(\Omega_{X}^{p}\otimes\det({\rm N}\mathscr{F}))
is rather special, for any
p\in X\setminus\sing(\mathscr{F})
there exists an open neighborhood
U\ni p
and
1
-forms
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{p}\in\Omega_{X}^{1}(U)
such that

\omega_{\mathscr{F}}|_{U}=\eta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\eta_{p}.

Moreover,
\mathscr{F}
is a foliation if and only if

d\eta_{j}\wedge\omega_{\mathscr{F}}|_{U}=0,\,\text{for}\,j=1,\dots,p.

2.1.
Foliations on projective spaces

A
k
-dimensional distribution
\mathscr{F}
on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
corresponds to a twisted
(n-k)
-form
\omega\in H^{0}(\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}^{n-k}(d+n-k+1)
, where
d\geq 0
is called the
degree
of
\mathscr{F}
. The Euler sequence allows us to interpret
\omega
as a polynomial differential form

\omega=\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}dx_{i}

where
A_{i}
are homogeneous of degree
d+1
, and the contraction with the radial vector files
\rad=x_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}+\cdots+x_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
vanishes:
\iota_{\rad}\omega=0
. The local decomposability translates to the Plücker conditions

(2.2)

({i}_{v}\omega)\wedge\omega=0\quad\text{ for every }v\in\wedge^{n-k-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n+1}),

and the integrability condition becomes

(2.3)

({i}_{v}\omega)\wedge d\omega=0\quad\text{ for every }v\in\wedge^{n-k-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n+1}).

We refer to
[
22
,
11
]
for details.

Given integers
d
,
k
, and
n
, the conditions (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) define the following subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}H^{0}(\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}^{n-k}(n-k+d+1))
:

(2.4)

\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(d,k,n)=\overline{\Big\{[\omega]\mid\omega\text{ is integrable, and }\codim(\sing\omega)\geq 2\Big\}}^{Zar},

the space of
k
-dimensional foliations of degree
d
on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
. For
d\leq 1
the irreducible components of
\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(d,k,n)
are well-known. A degree-
0
foliation is defined by a linear rational map
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{n-k}}
, see
[
15
, Théorème 3.8]
. In particular,

\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(0,n-k,n)\cong G(n-k+1,n+1)

the Grassmannian of
n-k+1
-planes in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
. For degree one, there is the following classification.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
23
, Theorem 6.2]

If
\mathscr{F}
is a foliation of degree
1
and codimension
q
on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, then we are in one of the following cases:

(1)

\mathscr{F}
is defined by a dominant rational map
\rho\colon{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(1^{(q+1)},2)
, with irreducible general fiber determined by
q
linear forms and one quadratic form; or

(2)

\mathscr{F}
is the linear pullback of a foliation induced by a global holomorphic vector field on
\mathbb{P}^{n-k+1}
.

Hence,
\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(1,n-k,n)
has two irreducible components. In degree
2
, only the case of codimension-one foliations is known.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
5
, Theorem A]

The space of codimension-one foliations of degree
2
in
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
,
n\geq 3
, has
6
irreducible components:

•

\overline{S(2,n)}
composed of linear pullbacks of degree-
2
foliations on
{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
;

•

\overline{R(2,2)}
given by pencils of quadrics;

•

\overline{R(1,3)}
given by pencils of cubics with a triple hyperplane;

•

\overline{L(1,1,1,1)}
whose general member is defined by a logarithmic
1
-form with poles in
4
hyperplanes;

•

\overline{L(1,1,2)}
whose general member is defined by a logarithmic
1
-form with poles in two hyperplanes and a quadric;

•

\overline{E(n)}
the
exceptional component
whose general member is defined by an algebraic action of the Lie algebra of affine transformations
\mathfrak{aff}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Below we detail each one of these components. Actually, we describe their generalizations to any codimension.

Example 2.1

(Linear pullback foliations)
.

Given a linear projection
\rho\colon{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}^{n-k+1}}
and a
\mathscr{G}
degree-
d
foliation by curves on
{\mathbb{P}^{n-k+1}}
, then we define
\mathscr{F}=\rho^{*}\mathscr{G}
a degree-
d
foliation of dimension
k
. Varying
\rho
and
\mathscr{G}
, we get an irreducible component
{\rm LPB}(d,k,n)\subset\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(d,k,n)
. For a proof, see
[
10
, Corollary 5.1]
.

Example 2.2

(Rational foliations)
.

Given homogeneous polynomials
F_{0},\dots,F_{n-k}
, we have a rational map
\rho\colon{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(d_{0},\dots,d_{n-k})
, where
d_{j}=\deg(F_{j})
. Its fibers define an algebraically integrable foliation that we call
rational
if
\rho
does not ramify in codimension one, i.e., the maximal minors of the Jacobian matrix of
\rho
do not share a common factor. These foliations fill an irreducible component

{\rm Rat}(d_{0},\dots,d_{n-k};n)\subset\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(d,k,n),

where
d=-n+k-1+\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}d_{j}
. For a proof, see
[
11
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

In degree
2
, there are two components of rational foliations. Indeed, the equation
2=-n+k-1+\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}d_{j}
admits only two solutions:

(d_{0},\dots,d_{n-k})=(1^{n-k-2},2,2)\text{ or }(1^{n-k-1},3).

If a foliation
\mathscr{F}
of dimension
k
and degree 2 is tangent to a rational map
\rho\colon{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}(d_{0},\dots,d_{n-k})
, such that the
\mathscr{F}
-invariant divisor
\{F_{0}=\dots=F_{n-k}=0\}
is normal crossing, then by
[
4
]
we get

d_{0}+\cdots+d_{n-k}\leq n-k+3
. This implies that
\mathscr{F}
is rational.

Example 2.3

(Logarithmic foliations)
.

A logarithmic foliation on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
of dimension
k
is defined by a closed rational
(n-k)
-form

\omega=\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n-k}\leq r}\lambda_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n-k}}\frac{dF_{i_{1}}}{F_{i_{1}}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{dF_{i_{n-k}}}{F_{i_{n-k}}}

where
F_{0},\dots,F_{r}
are homogeneous polynomials of degrees
d_{0},\dots,d_{r}
, with
r\geq n-k
, and
\lambda_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n-k}}\in{\mathbb{C}}
satisfy Plücker conditions. We also assume that
\omega
does not vanish in codimension one. Owing to

[
7
, Theorem 1.17]
, if
r\geq n-k+1
these foliations form an irreducible component

{\rm Log}(d_{0},\dots,d_{r};k,n)\subset\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(d,k,n),

where
d=-n+k-1+\sum_{j=0}^{r}d_{j}
. We remark that for
r=n-k
these are rational foliations.

Remark 2.2
.

In degree
2
, the equation
2=-n+k-1+\sum_{j=0}^{r}d_{j}
has only two solutions with
r\geq n-k+1
:

(d_{0},\dots,d_{r})=(1^{n-k+1},2)\text{ or }(1^{n-k+3}).

Thus
\mathbb{F}{\rm ol}(2,k,n)
has two components of logarithmic type. We observe that a foliation of dimension
k
is a general logarithmic foliation of type
(1^{n-k+1},2)
if and only if it is the pullback, via a rational map
\rho\colon{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(1^{n-k+1},2)
, of a foliation defined by a global vector field. The proof is a straightforward computation that we leave to the reader.

A logarithmic foliation of type
(1^{n-k+3})
and dimension
k>2
is a linear pullback of a
2
-dimensional logarithmic foliation of the same type. Indeed, for
k>2
,
n-k+3<n+1
, and, up to a linear change of coordinates, the foliation is defined by fewer than
n+1
variables. For dimension
k=2
, a general logarithmic foliation of type
(1^{n+1})
is defined by a pair commuting global vector fields on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, i.e., it is associated with an action of the commutative Lie algebra
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\hookrightarrow H^{0}(T{\mathbb{P}^{n}})
.

2.2.
Foliations associated with a Lie algebra action

Given a
k
-dimensional Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}=H^{0}(T{\mathbb{P}^{n}})
, it defines an element
\omega(\mathfrak{g})\in H^{0}(\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}^{n-k}(n+1))
. If
\omega(\mathfrak{g})\neq 0
, and does not vanish in codimension one, it defines a foliation of degree equal to its dimension,
d=k
. We refer to
[
15
,
10
,
29
]
for generalities and details about these foliations. Here we will focus on
2
-dimensional Lie algebras. In
[
29
]
, Pereira and dos Santos describe the scheme of Lie subalgebras and the irreducible components of the space of foliations they define. We reproduce below some of their arguments.

Consider the algebraic variety of
2
-dimensional Lie subalgebras of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
:

{\rm SLie}(2,\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})=\{\,V\subset\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}\mid[V,V]\subset V,\,\dim V=2\,\}.

It is a closed subvariety of the Grassmannian
G(2,\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
defined by the equations imposed by the Lie bracket. A result by Motzkin-Taussky says that the algebraic variety parametrizing pairs of commuting matrices is irreducible, and it is the closure of the set of pairs of (simultaneously) diagonalizable matrices, see
[
27
, Theorem 5]
and
[
20
]

Up to isomorphism, there is only one

The variety

\dim\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}-1

Fix

2.2.1.
First integrals for exceptional foliations

The vector field
\delta_{m}
, as defined in (
2.5
), is sometimes called the basic Weitzenböck derivation. In general, a Weitzenböck derivation is a linear nilpotent derivation. They play an important role in Invariant Theory, and the name comes from Weitzenböck’s Theorem (see
[
32
]
) which asserts that any linear action of the additive group
\mathbb{G}_{a}
on a polynomial ring has a finitely generated ring of invariants. That is, the ring

\ker\delta=\{\,f\in k[y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]\mid\delta(f)=0\,\}

is finitely generated (over
k
) for
\delta
a Weitzenböck derivation. This fails for polynomial locally nilpotent derivations of higher degree, see
[
28
, Example 6.3.1]
.

In
[
14
]
, the authors give explicit generators of the field of fractions of the constant ring of the basic Weitzenböck derivation. Using this description, we can show that an exceptional foliation in
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S}_{(n+1)})
is algebraically integrable.

Proposition 2.4
.

A general exceptional foliation in
{\rm Exc}(n+1,2,n)
is algebraically integrable.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that the foliation
\mathscr{F}
defined by
\delta_{n+1}
and
\rho_{n+1}
in (
2.5
) is algebraically integrable. Note that we only need to prove that there exist
n-2
algebraically independent functions
f_{i}/g_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
such that
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
are homogeneous of the same degree, and
v(f_{i}/g_{i})=w(f_{i}/g_{i})=0
.

According to
[
14
, §5]
, the field of fractions of
\ker v
is generated by algebraically independent elements
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
given by:

\displaystyle z_{1}

\displaystyle=x_{0},

\displaystyle z_{2i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2i}(-1)^{j}x_{j}x_{2i-j},\,\text{and}

\displaystyle z_{2i+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{0}}{2n-4i}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{2i}(-1)^{j}(j+1)(n-j)x_{j+1}x_{2i-j}\right.

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{2i}(-1)^{j}(2i+1-j)(n-2i+j)x_{j}x_{2i+1-j}\Bigg)-x_{1}\sum_{j=0}^{2i}(-1)^{j}x_{j}x_{2i-j},\,\text{for}\,j\geq 1.

One may verify that
w(z_{1})=\frac{n}{2}z_{1}
,
w(z_{2i})=n-2i
, and
w(z_{2i+1})=\frac{3n}{2}-2i-1
for

. A first integral for

is then a rational function

such that

, hence, the exponents

satisfy

\begin{cases}\displaystyle a_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2a_{2i}+3a_{2i+1})=0\\
\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}a_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\left((n-2i)a_{2j}+\left(\frac{3n}{2}-2i-1\right)a_{2j+1}\right)=0\end{cases}.

The
2\times n
matrix
A
associated with this system is of rank

. Then we can find

integer-valued solutions, linearly independent over

, which provide

\mathscr{F}
. Explicitly, an independent set of first integrals is:

\left\{\frac{z_{2+i}^{2}z_{1}^{4\lfloor i/2\rfloor}}{z_{2}^{i+2}},\,i=1,\dots,n-2\right\}.

for

even one can take a square root.
∎

From the above proof, we also conclude that

is given by the

\omega=\frac{1}{z_{1}^{\frac{n^{2}-n-4}{2}}}\iota_{\rad}dz_{2}\wedge dz_{3}\wedge d(z_{1}^{2}z_{4})\wedge d(z_{1}^{2}z_{5})\wedge d(z_{1}^{4}z_{6})\wedge\dots\wedge d(z_{1}^{2\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\rfloor}z_{n}).

3.
Foliations of dimension 2 and degree 2

In this section, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a foliation on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
of dimension and degree two,
k=d=2
. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
is stable and
\mathscr{F}
is algebraically integrable with uniruled leaves;

(2)

{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
is strictly semistable and
\mathscr{F}
belongs to either

•

an exceptional component
{\rm Exc}(d_{1},\dots,d_{r},2,n)
;

•

one of the rational components
{\rm Rat}(1^{n-2},2,2;n)
or
{\rm Rat}(1^{n-1},3;n)
; or

•

one of the logarithmic components
{\rm Log}(1^{n-1},2;2,n)
or
{\rm Log}(1^{n+1};2,n)
.

(3)

{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
is not semistable and
\mathscr{F}
is a linear pullback of a degree-
2
foliation by curves on
{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}
.

Given a foliation
\mathscr{F}
as in the statement of the theorem, its tangent sheaf

is reflexive of rank two with zero first Chern class. Then
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
is semistable if and only if

, and stable if and only if

. If

is not semistable, then

is a linear pullback of a degree-

foliation by curves on
{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}
. On the other hand, if

is stable then

[
19
, Lemma 2.5]
. Since

is smooth and simply connected, it follows from Druel’s results
[

is algebraically integrable. Due to
[
24

It remains to understand the case where

is strictly semistable. If

then
\mathscr{F}
is associated with a Lie algebra action, and thus it falls in either

or an exceptional component from Proposition
2.3
. The proof of Theorem
3.1
will be completed by the propositions

of Lemma

. The vector fields in cases
(3)
and
(4)
are nilpotent; hence they induce actions of the additive group
({\mathbb{C}},+)
. This allows us to conclude the following result.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a foliation of dimension
2
and degree
2
on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
tangent to a global vector field
v\in H^{0}(T{\mathbb{P}^{n}})
from either (3) or (4) in Lemma
3.4
. Then
\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Log}(1^{n-2},2;2,n)
.

Proof.

Both cases follow the same argument, we only prove one. In case (3),
v=x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}+x_{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}
. Then it has a rational first integral
\rho\colon{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(1^{n-2},2)
given by

\rho(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n})=(x_{1}:x_{3}:x_{4}:\dots:x_{n}:x_{0}x_{3}-x_{1}x_{2}).

Then
\mathscr{F}=\rho^{*}\mathscr{H}
for
\mathscr{H}
a foliation by curves on
{\mathbb{P}}(1^{n-2},2)
. Note that
\rho
does not ramify in codimension one, hence

{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(n+1)=\det({\rm N}\mathscr{F})=\rho^{*}\det({\rm N}\mathscr{H})={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(n+\deg\mathscr{H}).

Thus
\deg(\mathscr{H})=1
and
\mathscr{H}
is defined by a global vector field. As we observed in Remark
2.2
,
\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Log}(1^{n-2},2;2,n)
.
∎

For the case (1) of Lemma
3.4
,
v=x_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}+\mu x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\in H^{0}(T{\mathbb{P}^{n}})
defines an action of the multiplicative group
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. The next result classifies the
2
-dimensional foliations invariant by such actions. Note that we don’t impose any restriction on
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
a priori.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\omega\in H^{0}(\Omega_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}^{n-2}(n+1))
not vanishing in codimension one and let
v=x_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}+\mu x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\in H^{0}(T{\mathbb{P}^{n}})
be such that
\iota_{v}\omega=0
and
\iota_{v}d\omega=a\omega
for
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
a constant. Then
\omega
defines a foliation
\mathscr{F}
of one of the following types:

(1)

a=\mu+1
,
\mu\neq-1
and
\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Log}(1^{n+1};2,n)
;

(2)

a=0
,
\mu=-1
and
\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Log}(1^{n-1},2;2,n)
;

(3)

a=0
,
\mu=-2
and
\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Rat}(1^{n-2},3;n)
;

(4)

\mu\in\{1,2\}
and
a=\mu+2
, and
\mathscr{F}
is induced by an action of
\mathfrak{aff}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Proof.

Since
\iota_{v}\omega=0
we may write

\omega=(x_{0}dx_{1}-\mu x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\beta+(\mu-1)x_{0}x_{1}\beta\,+\,\gamma,

where
\beta
and
\gamma
do not depend on
dx_{0}
nor
dx_{1}
and
\iota_{\rad}\gamma=0
. Then
\beta
and
\gamma
are
(n-2)
-forms depending on
n-1
basic differentials, which means that there exists a degree-two homogeneous polynomial
q
and a linear vector field
u=\sum_{j=2}^{n}b_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
such that
\beta=\iota_{u}\Theta
and
\gamma=q\,\iota_{\rad}\Theta
, where
\Theta=dx_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}
. Hence,

\omega=(x_{0}dx_{1}-\mu x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta+(\mu-1)x_{0}x_{1}\iota_{u}\Theta\,+\,q\,\iota_{\rad}\Theta.

We might assume that
u
is not proportional to the radial in the last
n-1
variables, i.e.,
u\wedge\sum_{j=2}^{n}x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\neq 0
, otherwise
\omega
would vanish in codimension one.

By hypothesis, there exists
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\iota_{v}d\omega=a\omega
. Computing the LHS, we will see that there are few possibilities for
a
. Start with expanding

\displaystyle d\omega

\displaystyle=(\mu+1)dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}\wedge\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta-(x_{0}dx_{1}-\mu x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge d\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta+

\displaystyle\quad+(\mu-1)(x_{0}dx_{1}+x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{u}\Theta+(\mu-1)x_{0}x_{1}d\iota_{u}\Theta+dq\wedge\iota_{\rad}\Theta+\,q\,d\iota_{\rad}\Theta

then,

\displaystyle\iota_{v}d\omega

\displaystyle=(\mu+1)(x_{0}dx_{1}-\mu x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta+(x_{0}dx_{1}-\mu x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{v}d\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta+

\displaystyle\quad+(\mu-1)(\mu+1)x_{0}x_{1}\iota_{u}\Theta+(\mu-1)x_{0}x_{1}\iota_{v}d\iota_{u}\Theta+v(q)\iota_{\rad}\Theta

\displaystyle=(x_{0}dx_{1}-\mu x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge((\mu+1)\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta+\iota_{v}d\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta)+

\displaystyle\quad+(\mu-1)x_{0}x_{1}((\mu+1)\iota_{u}\Theta+\iota_{v}d\iota_{u}\Theta)+v(q)\iota_{\rad}\Theta

and by
\iota_{v}d\omega=a\omega
imposing we get that

\displaystyle\iota_{v}d\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta

\displaystyle=(a-\mu-1)\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta,

\displaystyle\iota_{v}d\iota_{u}\Theta

\displaystyle=(a-\mu-1)\iota_{u}\Theta+c\,\iota_{\rad}\Theta

\displaystyle v(q)

\displaystyle=a\,q-(\mu-1)c\,x_{0}x_{1}

where
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
and the second equation follows from the first.

Notice that
\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta=\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}dx_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{dx_{i}}\wedge\widehat{dx_{j}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}
, where each
p_{ij}
is a quadratic polynomial not having monomials
x_{0}^{2}
,
x_{0}x_{1}
nor
x_{1}^{2}
. Moreover,

\iota_{v}d\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta=\sum_{i,j}v(p_{ij})dx_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{dx_{i}}\wedge\widehat{dx_{j}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}

and we only need to compute the action of
v
on degree two polynomials. From
v(x_{0})=x_{0}
,
v(x_{1})=\mu x_{1}
and
v(x_{j})=0
for
j\geq 2
we get that

(3.2)

\frac{v(x_{i}x_{j})}{x_{i}x_{j}}=\begin{cases}2,&i=j=0\\
2\mu,&i=j=1\\
(\mu+1),&i=0,\,j=1\\
1,&i=0,\,j\geq 2\\
\mu,&i=1,\,j\geq 2\\
0,&i,j\geq 2\end{cases}

Then, to have
\iota_{v}d\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta=(a-\mu-1)\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta
, we need
a-\mu-1\in\{0,1,\mu\}
.

Case 1.0:

Suppose that
a-\mu-1=0
and that
\mu\not\in\{-1,1\}
. Then
\iota_{v}d\iota_{u}\Theta=c\,\iota_{\rad}\Theta
implies
c=0
. Thus
v(q)=(\mu+1)q
. From (
3.2
) and
\mu\not\in\{-1,1\}
,
q=c^{\prime}x_{0}x_{1}
for
c^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{C}}
a constant.
Then

\omega=(x_{0}dx_{1}-\mu x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta+x_{0}x_{1}((\mu-1)\iota_{u}\Theta\,+c^{\prime}\,\iota_{\rad}\Theta).

Consider
w=c^{\prime}\,x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+u
and notice that

\iota_{w}\iota_{v}\iota_{\rad}(dx_{0}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n})=\iota_{w}\iota_{v}\iota_{\rad}(dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}\wedge\Theta)=\omega.

Also, since
u
does not depend on
x_{0}
nor
x_{1}
,
[v,w]=0
. Thus
\omega
defines a foliation
\mathscr{F}
on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
with trivial tangent sheaf
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{\oplus 2}
.

Case 1.1:

Suppose that
a-\mu-1=0
and that
\mu=1
. As in the previous case,
c=0
and
u
does not depend on
x_{0}
nor
x_{1}
. But now
v(q)=2q
implies that
q=q_{0}x_{0}^{2}+2q_{1}x_{0}x_{1}+q_{2}x_{1}^{2}
. Then

\omega=(x_{0}dx_{1}-x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta+(q_{0}x_{0}^{2}+2q_{1}x_{0}x_{1}+q_{2}x_{1}^{2})\iota_{\rad}\Theta.

Consider the vector field

w=\left(-q_{1}x_{0}-q_{2}x_{1}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}+\left(q_{0}x_{0}+q_{1}x_{1}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+u.

As in the previous case, we have that
\omega=\iota_{w}\iota_{v}\iota_{\rad}(dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}\wedge\Theta)
, and it defines a foliation whose tangent sheaf is trivial.

Case 1.2:

Suppose that
a-\mu-1=0
and that
\mu=-1
. Again, we have
c=0
but now
v(q)=0
, which implies that
q=q_{0}x_{0}x_{1}+q^{\prime}(x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
. Then

\omega=d(x_{0}x_{1})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta-2x_{0}x_{1}\iota_{u}\Theta\,+\,q\,\iota_{\rad}\Theta.

Then
\omega
defines a foliation on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
which is the pullback of the foliation on
{\mathbb{P}}(2,1^{(n-1)})
given by the vector field

m=(q_{0}y_{1}+q^{\prime}(y_{2},\dots,y_{n}))\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}b_{j}(y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}

via the map defined by
y_{1}=x_{0}x_{1}
and
y_{j}=x_{j}
for
j\geq 2
; recall that
u=\sum_{j=2}^{n}b_{j}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
.

Case 2.0:

Suppose that
a-\mu-1=1
and that
\mu\not\in\{-2,-1,2\}
. Then
\iota_{v}\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta=\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta
and
x_{0}
must divide
\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta
. Hence, there exist
\xi
and
c^{\prime}
such that
\iota_{u}\Theta=x_{0}\xi+c^{\prime}\iota_{\rad}\Theta
. Note that
c^{\prime}=-c
. Then

\omega=x_{0}(x_{0}dx_{1}-\mu x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\xi+(\mu-1)x_{0}^{2}x_{1}\xi+(-c(\mu-1)x_{0}x_{1}+q)\iota_{\rad}\Theta.

On the other hand,
v(q)=(\mu+2)q-(\mu-1)c\,x_{0}x_{1}
implies that
q=c(\mu-1)\,x_{0}x_{1}
. Hence,
\omega
vanishes along
V(x_{0})
which contradicts our hypothesis.

Case 2.1:

Suppose that
a-\mu-1=1
and that
\mu=-2
. Then,
v(q)=3cx_{0}x_{1}
which implies that
q=-3cx_{0}x_{1}+q^{\prime}(x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=x_{0}(x_{0}dx_{1}+2x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\xi-3x_{0}^{2}x_{1}\xi+(3c\,x_{0}x_{1}+q)\iota_{\rad}\Theta=

\displaystyle=x_{0}[(x_{0}dx_{1}+2x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\xi-3x_{0}x_{1}\xi]+q^{\prime}\iota_{\rad}\Theta.

Note that
\xi
is a constant
(n-2)
-from on
\{dx_{2},\dots,dx_{n}\}
. Then, up to a linear change on the last
n-1
coordinates, we may assume that
\xi=dx_{3}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}
. Then

\omega=\iota_{\rad}((q^{\prime}dx_{2}-d(x_{0}^{2}x_{1}))\wedge dx_{3}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}).

Now consider
f(x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
a degree
3
polynomial such that
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}}=q^{\prime}
. It follows that

\omega=\iota_{\rad}(d(f-x_{0}^{2}x_{1})\wedge dx_{3}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}),

and
\omega
defines a rational foliation of type
(3,1^{(n-2)})
.

Case 2.2:

Suppose that
a-\mu-1=1
and that
\mu=2
. Then,
v(q)=4q-cx_{0}x_{1}
which implies
q=q_{0}x_{1}^{2}+c\,x_{0}x_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=x_{0}(x_{0}dx_{1}-2x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\xi+x_{0}^{2}x_{1}\xi+(-cx_{0}x_{1}+q)\iota_{\rad}\Theta

\displaystyle=x_{0}(x_{0}dx_{1}-2x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\xi+x_{0}^{2}x_{1}\xi+q_{0}x_{1}^{2}\iota_{\rad}\Theta

As in the previous case, we may assume
\xi=dx_{3}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}
, hence

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=x_{0}(x_{0}dx_{1}-2x_{1}dx_{0})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\xi+x_{0}^{2}x_{1}\xi+q_{0}x_{1}^{2}(x_{2}\xi-dx_{2}\wedge\iota_{\rad}\xi)

\displaystyle=(x_{0}(x_{0}dx_{1}-2x_{1}dx_{0})-q_{0}x_{1}^{2}dx_{2})\wedge\iota_{\rad}\xi+(x_{0}^{2}x_{1}+q_{0}x_{1}^{2}x_{2})\xi

\displaystyle=\iota_{\rad}((x_{0}^{2}dx_{1}-2x_{1}x_{0}dx_{0}-q_{0}x_{1}^{2}dx_{2})\wedge dx_{3}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}).

Note that
q_{0}\neq 0
unless
\omega
vanishes along
V(x_{0})
. Define the vector field
w=\frac{q_{0}}{2}x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}+x_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}
. It follows that

\omega=\iota_{\rad}\iota_{w}\iota_{v}((x_{0}^{2}dx_{1}-2x_{1}x_{0}dx_{0}-q_{0}x_{1}^{2}dx_{2})\wedge dx_{3}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}).

Also notice that
[v,w]=w
hence
\omega
defines a foliation associated with an algebraic action of the affine Lie algebra.

Case 2.3:

Suppose that
a-\mu-1=1
and that
\mu=-1
. Again
x_{0}
divides
\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta
. But now
v(q)=q+2c\,x_{0}x_{1}
, which implies that
x_{0}
divides
q
. Then
\omega
vanishes along
V(x_{0})
which is absurd.

Case 2.4:

Suppose that
a-\mu-1=1
and that
\mu=1
. Then
v(q)=3q
implies
q=0
. Then,

\omega=\iota_{\rad}\iota_{v}\iota_{u}(dx_{0}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}).

On the other hand,
\iota_{v}d\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta=\iota_{\rad}\iota_{u}\Theta
implies that
u=u^{\prime}+c^{\prime}\sum_{j=2}x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
, where the coefficients of
u^{\prime}
depend uniquely on
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
. Note that we can replace
u
with
u^{\prime}
in the definition of
\omega
. Moreover,
[v,u^{\prime}]=u^{\prime}
and
\omega
defines a foliation tangent to an action of
\mathfrak{aff}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Case 3:

Suppose that
a-\mu-1=\mu
. We may exchange
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
so that we get new parameters
(\mu^{\prime},a^{\prime})=(1/\mu,a/\mu)
. For
a=2\mu+1
we get
a^{\prime}=\mu^{\prime}+2
. Then we reduce to one of the previous cases.
∎

4.
Foliations of degree two and higher dimension

In dimension
k>2
, the positivity of
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
simplifies the classification. Indeed,
c_{1}({\rm T}\mathscr{F})=k-2
, and
\mathscr{F}
is a Mukai foliation, see
[
2
]
. We have the following theorem (cf. Theorem
A
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a degree-
2
foliation on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
of dimension
k>2
. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

\mathscr{F}
is algebraically integrable;

(2)

\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Log}(1^{n-k+1},2;k,n)
is a logarithmic foliation of type
(1^{n-r+1},2)
;

(3)

\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Log}(1^{n+1};k,n)
is a logarithmic foliation of type
(1^{n+1})
;

(4)

\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Exc}(r_{1},\dots,r_{m},k,n)
is an exceptional foliation; or

(5)

\mathscr{F}\in{\rm LPB}(2,k,n)
is a linear pullback of a foliation by curves in
{\mathbb{P}^{n-k+1}}
.

The proof of this result will follow from Proposition
4.2
below. We begin assuming that
\mathscr{F}
is not a linear pullback of a foliation on
{\mathbb{P}^{r}}
with
r<n
. This is equivalent to assuming that
\mathscr{F}
has no subfoliation of degree
0
or that
H^{0}({\rm T}\mathscr{F}(-1))=0
. Indeed, for any foliation
\mathscr{G}
of degree
0
, we have
{\rm T}\mathscr{G}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)^{\oplus\dim\mathscr{G}}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
a degree-
2
foliation on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
of dimension
k>2
. If
H^{0}({\rm T}\mathscr{F}(-1))=0
then either

•

\mathscr{F}
is algebraically integrable, or

•

\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Log}(1^{n-k+1,2};k,n)
is a logarithmic foliation of type
(1^{n-r+1},2)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathscr{F}
is not algebraically integrable. By
[
1
, Proposition 7.5]
there exists a subfoliation
\mathscr{G}\subset\mathscr{F}
such that
c_{1}({\rm T}\mathscr{G})\geq c_{1}({\rm T}\mathscr{F})
, hence,

\dim\mathscr{G}-\deg\mathscr{G}\geq k-2>\dim\mathscr{G}-2

which implies that
\deg\mathscr{G}\leq 1
. Since
H^{0}({\rm T}\mathscr{F}(-1))=0
, we have
\deg\mathscr{G}=1
and
H^{0}({\rm T}\mathscr{G}(-1))=0
; hence,
\dim\mathscr{G}=k-1
. By Theorem
2.1
,
\mathscr{G}
is defined by a rational map
\rho\colon{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(1^{n-k+1},2)
given by
n-k+1
linear forms and one quadratic form. It follows that
\mathscr{F}=\rho^{*}\mathscr{H}
for some (purely transcendental) foliation by curves
\mathscr{H}
on
{\mathbb{P}}(1^{n-k+1},2)
. Since
\rho

does not ramify in codimension one,
\det({\rm N}\mathscr{F})=\rho^{*}\det({\rm N}\mathscr{H})
. Hence,
\mathscr{H}
is given by a global vector field. This means that
\mathscr{F}\in{\rm Log}(1^{n-k+1},2;k,n)
, see Remark
2.2
.
∎

Now, we apply this proposition to prove the general case.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{F}
is neither algebraically integrable nor logarithmic of type
(1^{n-k+1},2)
. By Proposition
4.2
, we must have
h=h^{0}({\rm T}\mathscr{F}(-1))>0
. Then
\mathscr{F}
is a linear pullback of a foliation
\mathscr{G}
on
{\mathbb{P}^{n-h}}
such that
H^{0}({\rm T}\mathscr{G}(-1))=0
. It follows that
\dim\mathscr{G}\leq 2
. Indeed, if we had
\dim\mathscr{G}\geq 3
we would get a contradiction applying Proposition
4.2
again.

If
\dim\mathscr{G}=1
then
\mathscr{F}\in{\rm LPB}(2,k,n)
. If
\dim\mathscr{G}=2
then, by Theorem
3.1
,
\mathscr{G}
belongs to either
{\rm Log}(1^{n-h+1};2,n-h)
or an exceptional component
{\rm Exc}(r_{1},\dots,r_{m},2,n-h)
. Thus
\mathscr{F}
belongs to either
{\rm Log}(1^{n+1};k,n)
or
{\rm Exc}(r_{1},\dots,r_{m},k,n)
, see Remark
2.4
.
∎

5.
Holomorphic Poisson structures

In this section, we will prove Corollary
C
.

A holomorphic Poisson structure on a complex manifold
X
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear Lie bracket

\{\cdot,\cdot\}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\times\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}

which satisfies the Leibniz rule
\{f,gh\}=h\{f,g\}+f\{g,h\}

and Jacobi identities

\{f,\{g,h\}\}+\{g,\{h,f\}\}+\{h,\{f,g\}\}=0

for all
f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
. The bracket corresponds to a holomorphic bivector field
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\wedge^{2}TX)
given by

\sigma(df\wedge dg)=\{f,g\}
, for all
f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
We will denote a Poisson structure on
X
as the pair
(X,\sigma)
, where
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\wedge^{2}TX)
is the corresponding Poisson bivector field. We say that the structure
(X,\sigma)
has generic rank
2k
if

\underbrace{\sigma\wedge\cdots\wedge\sigma}_{k\ \mathrm{times}}\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\underbrace{\sigma\wedge\cdots\wedge\sigma}_{k+1\ \mathrm{times}}=0
.

The holomorphic bivector
\sigma
induces a morphism

\sigma^{\#}\colon\Omega_{X}^{1}\to TX

which is called the
anchor map
and is defined by
\sigma^{\#}(\theta)=\sigma(\theta,\cdot)
, where
\theta
is a germ of a holomorphic
1
-form, and

\delta\colon TX\to\wedge^{2}TX
is given by
\delta(v)=L_{v}(\sigma)
; here
L_{v}
denotes the Lie derivative. We refer to
[
31
]
for more details.

Definition 5.1
.

The
symplectic foliation
associated with
\sigma
is the foliation given by
\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}:=\mathrm{Ker}(\sigma^{\#})
, whose dimension is the rank of the anchor map

\sigma^{\#}:\Omega_{X}^{1}\to TX
.
A Poisson variety
(X,\sigma)
is called
generically symplectic
if the anchor map
\sigma^{\#}\colon\Omega_{X}^{1}\to TX
is generically an isomorphism.
Then, the degeneracy loci of
\sigma^{\#}
is an effective anti-canonical divisor
D(\sigma)\in|-K_{X}|
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
(X,\sigma)
be a Poisson projective variety. A
Poisson connection
on a sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
E
is a

\mathbb{C}
-linear morphism of sheaves

\nabla:E\to TX\otimes E
satisfying the Leibniz rule

\nabla(fs)=\delta(f)\otimes s+f\nabla(s),

where
f
is a germ of a holomorphic function on
X
and
s
is a germ of a holomorphic section of
E
. We say that
E
is a
Poisson module
if it admits a Poisson flat connection, i.e., if its curvature
\nabla^{2}\colon E\to\Omega_{X}^{2}\otimes E
vanishes.
Equivalently, a Poisson connection defines a
\mathbb{C}
-linear bracket
\{\ ,\ \}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\times E\to E
by

\{f,s\}:=\nabla(s)(df),

where
f
is a germ of a holomorphic function on
X
and
s
is a germ of a holomorphic section of
E
.

Let
E\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 2}
be a Poisson module with a trace free Poisson connection
\nabla

Definition 5.3
.

Let

. The

is the subsheaf of

consisting of germs of vector fields that preserve the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)
. That is,
TX(-\log(D))
is the sheaf of vector fields tangent to

Proposition 5.1
.

[
31
, Proposition 4.4.1]

Let
D\subset X
a Poisson free
divisor with respect to a holomorphic bi-vector
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\wedge^{2}TX)
. Then
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\wedge^{2}TX(-\log(D))
.

Proof of Corollary
C
.

Since the associated symplectic foliation
\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}
has no divisorial zeros and
c_{1}({\rm T}\mathscr{F}_{\sigma})=0
,
\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}
is a foliation of dimension 2 and degree 2. It follows from Theorem
3.1
that one of the following holds:

(a)

\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}
is algebraically integrable and
\sigma
is not decomposable, since
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
is stable.

(b)

\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}
is the linear pull-back of a degree-two foliation by curves on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
with tangent sheaf split. So,
\sigma
is decomposable.

(c)

{\rm T}\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\cong\mathfrak{g}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, where
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{sl}(n+1,\mathbb{C})
and either
\mathfrak{g}
is an Abelian Lie algebra of dimension 2 or
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{aff}(\mathbb{C})
. So,
\sigma
is decomposable.

(d)

\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}
is the pull-back by a rational map
\rho:{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}(1^{(n-1)},2)
of a non-algebraic foliation by curves induced by a global vector field on
\mathbb{P}(1^{(n-1)},2)
. Then
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}
is not split, which implies that
\sigma
is not decomposable.

Case (a)
:
Since
\sigma
is of normal crossing type, by Remark
2.1
, the symplectic foliation
\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}
is tangent to a dominant rational map
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
given either by
f_{(1,3)}:=(L_{1}^{3},\cdots,L_{n-2}^{3},P)
or
f_{(1,2,2)}:=(L_{1}^{2},\cdots,L_{n-3}^{2},Q_{1},Q_{2})
, where
L_{i}
are linear,
Q_{i}
are quadratic, and
P
is cubic. Then the divisors
D_{(1,3)}:={L_{1}\cdots L_{n-2}P=0}
and
D_{(1,2,2)}:={L_{1}\cdots L_{n-3}Q_{1}Q_{2}=0}
are Poisson divisors of
\sigma
. If we denote by
T_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}/\mathbb{P}^{n-2}}
the corresponding relative tangent sheaf either of
f_{(1,3)}\colon{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
or of
f_{(1,2,2)}\colon{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
, then it follows from Proposition
5.1
that

•

\sigma\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},\wedge^{2}T_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}/\mathbb{P}^{n-2}}(-\log(D_{(1,3)}))
, or

•

\sigma\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},\wedge^{2}T_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}/\mathbb{P}^{n-2}}(-\log(D_{(1,2,2)}))
.

Case (b):

If
\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}
is the linear pull-back of a degree two foliation by curves on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, then there are
v_{0}
a constant vector field, and
v
a homogeneous polynomial vector field of degree
2
on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
such that
\sigma=v_{0}\wedge v
. It is clear that
[v_{0},v]=0
.
Let
H
be the Poisson hyperplane determined by
v_{0}\in\mathbb{P}H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},T{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1))\cong\check{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}
. That is,

H
is the hyperplane parameterizing lines passing through a point which are leaves of the foliation induced by
v_{0}
. It is clear that
H
is a Poisson divisor.

Case (c):

If
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\cong\mathfrak{g}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, where
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{sl}(n+1,\mathbb{C})
an Abelian Lie algebra. Since
\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}
is general, then
\mathfrak{g}
is generated by diagonal linear vector fields which are given in a suitable system of coordinates by

v_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{0i}z_{i}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}

and

v_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\lambda_{1j}z_{j}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}.

Then

\sigma=v_{0}\wedge v_{1}=\sum_{i<j}(\lambda_{0i}-\lambda_{1j})z_{i}z_{j}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\wedge\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}.

Since
v_{r}(z_{i})=\lambda_{ri}z_{i}
, for all
r=0,1
and
i=0,\dots,n
, we conclude that
v_{0},v_{1}\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},T{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-\log(D)))
and

\sigma\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}
, where
\mathfrak{h}=H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},T{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-\log(D)))
is the Abelian Lie algebra of vector fields tangent to
the arrangement of planes
D=\{z_{0}\cdots z_{n}=0\}
.

Suppose that
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\cong\mathfrak{g}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, where
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{aff}(\mathbb{C})
. In this case, we have that the Poisson structure on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}

is induced by the so-called infinitesimal action

\sigma\colon\mathfrak{g}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\to T{\mathbb{P}^{n}},

which are generated by
v_{0}
and
v_{1}
linear vector fields such

[v_{0},v_{1}]=v_{0}
. In addition, in both case where
\sigma=v_{0}\wedge v_{1}
, with
v_{1},v_{2}\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},T{\mathbb{P}^{n}})
we have that the tangent sheaf of the symplectic foliation is
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{\oplus 2}
. We have a trace-free flat Poisson connection

\nabla\colon{\rm T}\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\to T{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\otimes{\rm T}\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}

given by

\nabla=\delta+\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}&0\\
v_{0}&-v_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

If
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\cong\mathfrak{g}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
with
\mathfrak{g}
Abelian, then for
i=0,1
we have

\begin{matrix}\delta(v_{i})=L_{v_{i}}(v_{0}\wedge v_{1})=0\end{matrix},

since
[v_{1},v_{0}]=0
.
If
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\cong\mathfrak{g}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
with
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{aff}(\mathbb{C})
then

\displaystyle\delta(v_{0})

\displaystyle=L_{v_{0}}(v_{0}\wedge v_{1})=v_{0}\wedge v_{1},

\displaystyle\delta(v_{1})

\displaystyle=L_{v_{1}}(v_{0}\wedge v_{1})=0,

since
[v_{1},v_{0}]=-v_{1}
.
Now, let
p\in X\setminus Sing(\mathscr{F}_{\sigma})
and
L_{p}
the symplectic leaf of
\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}
passing through
p
.
Since
(\sigma^{\#})^{-1}\circ\delta|_{L_{p}}=d\circ(\sigma^{\#})^{-1}|_{L_{p}}\
, where
d
denotes the de Rham differential, we have that

\begin{matrix}d\circ(\sigma^{\#})^{-1}(v_{0})=(\sigma^{\#})^{-1}\circ\delta|_{F_{p}}(v_{0})=\hfill(\sigma^{\#})^{-1}(v_{0})\wedge(\sigma^{\#})^{-1}(v_{1})\\
d\circ(\sigma^{\#})^{-1}(v_{1})=(\sigma^{\#})^{-1}\circ\delta|_{F_{p}}(v_{1})=2(\sigma^{\#})^{-1}(v_{0})\wedge(\sigma^{\#})^{-1}(v_{2}).\end{matrix}

Now, defining
(\sigma^{\#})^{-1}|_{L_{p}}(v_{i})=\omega_{i}
, we obtain the transversely affine structure on
L_{p}
given by the pair
(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})
, where
\omega_{0}
is the
1
-form inducing the transversely affine holomorphic foliation on
L_{p}
.
We have that

•

if
\mathfrak{g}
is Abelian, then
(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})
is Euclidean,

•

if
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{aff}(\mathbb{C})

then
(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})
is affine.

If
{\rm T}\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}\cong\mathfrak{g}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, where
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{aff}(\mathbb{C})
, then the normal form of
\sigma
follows from the proof of Proposition
2.3
.

Case (d):

The symplectic foliation
\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}
is the pull-back by a rational map
\rho\colon{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}(1^{(n-1)},2)
of a foliation by curves on
\mathbb{P}(1^{(n-1)},2)
, and
\rho:=(L_{1},\dots,L_{n-1},Q)
, where
L_{i}
are linear and
Q
is quadratic. Then
\sigma\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},\wedge^{2}T{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-\log(D)))
, where
D:=\{L_{1}\cdots L_{n-1}Q=0\}
. Consider
v\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},T{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-\log(D)))\subset H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},T{\mathbb{P}^{n}})
, the global vector field whose leaves are fibers of

\sigma\wedge v=0
and
dF(v)=0
, where
F:=L_{1}\cdots L_{n-1}\cdot Q
. That is,

is Hamiltonian with respect to
\sigma
.

Remark 5.1
.

Let
({\mathbb{P}^{n}},\sigma)
be a holomorphic Poisson structure such that
\sigma\wedge\sigma=0
. Then the associated symplectic foliation
\mathscr{F}_{\sigma}
has at most degree 2. If
\sigma
has zeros of codimension one, then by the classification of foliations of degree 0 and 1, see Section
2.1
, we have that
either
\sigma=Q\cdot(v_{0}\wedge v_{1})
, where
v_{i}
are constant vector fields and
Q
is of degree 2, or
\sigma=H_{0}\cdot\tilde{\sigma}
, with
\{H_{0}=0\}
being a Poisson hyperplane and

•

\tilde{\sigma}=v_{0}\wedge v
, where
v_{0}
is a constant vector field,
v
is a linear vector field such that
[v_{0},v]=0
.

•

\tilde{\sigma}\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}^{n}},\wedge^{2}T_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}/\mathbb{P}^{n-2}}(-\log(D)))
, where
D=H_{1}\cup\cdots\cup H_{n-1}\cup\mathcal{Q}
is a Poisson divisor such that
H_{i}
are linear and
\mathcal{Q}
is of degree 2.

By a similar argument given in
[
23
, Theorem 9.1]
, we can deform
\sigma
to a bivector belonging to either
{\rm Log}(1^{n-1},2;2,n)
or

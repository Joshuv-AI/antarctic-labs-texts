Counting twisted sheaves and S-duality
Yunfeng Jiang

Abstract
. We provide a definition of Tanaka-Thomas’s Vafa-Witten invariants for étale gerbes over smooth projective surfaces using the moduli spaces of μ r \mu_{r} -gerbe twisted sheaves and Higgs sheaves. Twisted sheaves and their moduli are naturally used to study the period-index theorem for the corresponding μ r \mu_{r} -gerbe in the Brauer group of the surfaces. Deformation and obstruction theory of the twisted sheaves and Higgs sheaves behave like general sheaves and Higgs sheaves. We define virtual fundamental classes on the moduli spaces and define the twisted Vafa-Witten invariants using virtual localization and the Behrend function on the moduli spaces. As applications for the Langlands dual group SU ⁡ ( r ) / ℤ r \SU(r)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{r} of SU ⁡ ( r ) \SU(r) , we define the SU ⁡ ( r ) / ℤ r \SU(r)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{r} -Vafa-Witten invariants using the twisted invariants for étale gerbes, and prove the S-duality conjecture of Vafa-Witten for the projective plane in rank two and for K3 surfaces in prime ranks. We also conjecture for other surfaces.

1.1.
S-duality conjecture of Vafa-Witten

We briefly review the S-duality conjecture of
N=4
supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory on a real 4-manifold
M

[
61
]
. More details can be found in
[
61
]
, and a review is given in
[
26
]
.
This theory involves coupling constants
\theta,g
combined as follows

\tau:=\frac{\theta}{2\pi}+\frac{4\pi i}{g^{2}}.

The S-duality predicts that the transformation
\tau\to-\frac{1}{\tau}
maps the partition function for gauge group
G
to the partition function with Langlands dual gauge group
{}^{L}G
. Vafa-Witten consider a
4
-manifold
M
underlying a smooth projective surface
S
over
{\mathbb{C}}
and
G=\SU(r)
. The Langlands dual group

{}^{L}\SU(r)=\SU(r)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}
. We make these transformations more precise following
[
61
, §3]
.
Think
\tau
as the parameter of the upper half plane
{\mathbb{H}}
. Let
\Gamma_{0}(4)\subset\SL(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
be the subgroup

\Gamma_{0}(4)=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)\in\SL(2,{\mathbb{Z}}):4|c\right\}.

The group
\Gamma_{0}(4)
acts on
{\mathbb{H}}
by

\tau\mapsto\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}.

The group
SL(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
is generated by transformations

S=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right);\quad T=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right).

From
[
61
]
, invariance under
T
is the assertion that physics is periodic in
\theta
with period
2\pi
, and

S

is equivalent at
\theta=0
to the transformation
\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi}\mapsto(\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi})^{-1}
originally proposed by
Montonen and Olive
[
50

. One can check that

T(\tau)=\tau+1
, and
S(\tau)=-\frac{1}{\tau}

For a smooth projective surface
S
, let
Z(\SU(r);\tau)=Z(\SU(r);q)
be the partition function which counts the invariants of instanton moduli spaces, where we let
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
. Similarly let

Z(\SU(r)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{r};\tau)
be the partition function which counts the invariants of
\SU(r)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}
-instanton moduli spaces. As pointed out in
[
61
, §3]
, when some vanishing theorem holds, the invariants count the Euler characteristic of instanton moduli spaces. We will see a mathematical meaning of this vanishing.
Now the S-duality predicts the following:

Conjecture 1.1
.

The transformation
T
acts on
Z(\SU(r);q)
, and the
S
-transformation sends

(1.1.1)

Z\left(\SU(r);-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)=\pm r^{-\frac{\chi}{2}}\left(\frac{\tau}{i}\right)^{\frac{\omega}{2}}Z(\SU(r)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{r};\tau).

for some
\omega

\chi:=\chi(S)
is the topological Euler number of

Usually
\omega=\chi
.
This is Formula (3.18) in
[
61
]
.
In mathematics we think
Z(\SU(r);\tau)=Z(\SU(r);q)
as the partition function which counts the invariants of moduli space of vector bundles or Higgs bundles on
S
. Let

\eta(q)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{k\geq 1}(1-q^{k})

be the Dedekind eta function. Let

\widehat{Z}(\SU(r);q)=\eta(q)^{-w}\cdot Z(SU(r);q),

and we will see that
\widehat{Z}(\SU(r);\tau)
is the partition function of the moduli space of Gieseker stable Higgs sheaves.
Then S-duality predicts:

Conjecture 1.2
.

(1.1.2)

\widehat{Z}\left(SU(r);-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)=\pm r^{-\frac{\chi}{2}}\widehat{Z}(SU(r)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{r};\tau)

This is Formula (3.15) in
[
61
]
.
Then
T^{4}
acts on the
\SU(r)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}
-theory to itself; and

ST^{4}S=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
4&1\end{array}\right)
will map the
SU(r)
-theory to itself. Note that

\Gamma_{0}(4)=\langle T,ST^{4}S\rangle
is generated by
T,ST^{4}S
.
In the case of a spin manifold, we get the subgroup of
\SL(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
generated by
S
and
ST^{2}S
, which is the group

\Gamma_{0}(2)=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)\in\SL(2,{\mathbb{Z}}):2|c\right\}.

We will see this for K3 surfaces.
Therefore if the S-duality conjecture holds, the partition function
Z(\SU(r);\tau)=Z(\SU(r);q)
is a modular form with modular group

\Gamma_{0}(4)
or
\Gamma_{0}(2)
if

is a spin manifold.

In
[
61
, §4]
, Vafa-Witten checked the S-duality for the cases

[

In algebraic geometry the instanton invariants are the Euler characteristic of the moduli space of Gieseker or slope stable coherent sheaves on

But to the author’s knowledge there are few theories or defining invariants in algebraic geometry for the Langlands dual group

In the rank

1.2.
Twisted Vafa-Witten invariants

In differential geometry solutions of the Vafa-Witten equation on a projective surface
S
are given by
polystable Higgs bundles on the surface
S
, see
[
55
]
.
The moduli space of Higgs bundles
has a partial compactification by Gieseker semistable Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
on
S
, where
E
is a torsion free coherent sheaf with rank
\mathop{\rm rk}>0
, and
\phi\in\mbox{Hom}_{S}(E,E\otimes K_{S})
is a section called a Higgs field.
Tanaka and Thomas
[
55
]
,
[
56
]
have developed a theory of Vafa-Witten invariants using the moduli space
\mathcal{N}
of Gieseker semi-stable Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
on
S
with topological data
(\mathop{\rm rk}=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits,c_{1}(E),c_{2}(E))
. They actually defined the Vafa-Witten invariants using the moduli space
\mathcal{N}_{L}^{\perp}
of Higgs pairs with fixed determinant
L\in\Pic(S)
and trace-free
\phi
.

For the gauge group
\SU(r)
, as in the case of
[
55
]
, the structure group for the moduli space
\mathcal{N}_{L}^{\perp}
is

\SL_{r}(\kappa)
. The Langlands dual group of
\SL_{r}(\kappa)
is

{}^{L}\SL_{r}(\kappa)=\PGL_{r}(\kappa)
.
Thus the corresponding moduli space, for the gauge group
\SU(r)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}
, should be the moduli space of
\PGL_{r}(\kappa)
-Higgs bundles or sheaves.
We consider
\mu_{r}
-gerbes on
S
, where
\mu_{r}
is the cyclic group of order
r
. From
[
45
]
, for a
\mu_{r}
-gerbe
{\mathfrak{S}}\to S
over a surface
S
such that the corresponding Brauer class is nontrivial, the moduli stack of semistable
{\mathfrak{S}}
-twisted sheaves, in some sense, can be taken as a cover over the moduli space of semistable generalized Azumaya algebras on
S
, which in turn, is isomorphic to the the moduli space of
PGL_{r}(\kappa)
-bundles or sheaves on
S
. This statement is generalized to the twisted Higgs sheaves in
[
27
]
. Therefore it is reasonable to propose that
the Vafa-Witten invariants for the
\mu_{r}
-gerbes on
S
will give the mathematical invariants for the Langlands dual group
\SU(r)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}
.

Let
p:{\mathfrak{S}}\to S
be a
\mu_{r}
-gerbe, and fix a polarization
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)
.
The gerbe
{\mathfrak{S}}
is a surface DM stack, and all the equivalence classes of
\mu_{r}
-gerbes over
S
are classified by the second étale cohomology group

H^{2}(S,\mu_{r})
.
We have the Vafa-Witten invariants studied in
[
24
]
by the moduli space of stable Higgs sheaves on
{\mathfrak{S}}
.
But this is too broad for the S-duality conjecture, and we then restrict to a subcategory of sheaves on
{\mathfrak{S}}
, called the category of
{\mathfrak{S}}
-twisted sheaves
in
[
43
]
. We believe that this is the right category to define twisted Vafa-Witten invariants and check the S-duality.

For a
\mu_{r}
-gerbe
{\mathfrak{S}}\to S
over a surface
S
and let
I{\mathfrak{S}}
be the inertia stack, the
{\mathfrak{S}}
-twisted sheaves were defined in
[
43
]
, and we will review it in §
3.1
. Roughly speaking the twisted sheaf is a sheaf with the transition function on an open cover of
{\mathfrak{S}}
is gerbe cocycle
[{\mathfrak{S}}]\in H^{2}(S,\mu_{r})
.
A
\mu_{r}
-gerbe twisted sheaf is always given by a character morphism
\chi:\mu_{r}\to{{\mathbb{G}}_{\mbox{\tiny\rm m}}}
, and it can be understood as a
{{\mathbb{G}}_{\mbox{\tiny\rm m}}}
-gerbe twisted sheaf.
From the exact sequence

1\to\mu_{r}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\cdot)^{r}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*}\to 1

and the long exact sequence for cohomology

(1.2.1)

\cdots\to H^{1}(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*})\rightarrow H^{2}(S,\mu_{r})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2}(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*})\rightarrow\cdots

A
\mu_{r}
-gerbe
{\mathfrak{S}}\to S
corresponds to a
{{\mathbb{G}}_{\mbox{\tiny\rm m}}}
-gerbe
[\varphi([{\mathfrak{S}}])]
. A quasi-coherent sheaf
E
on the
{{\mathbb{G}}_{\mbox{\tiny\rm m}}}
-gerbe
[\varphi([{\mathfrak{S}}])]
has a canonical decomposition

E=\bigoplus_{i}E_{i}

where

is the eigensheaf on which the structure group
{{\mathbb{G}}_{\mbox{\tiny\rm m}}}
acts by
\lambda\cdot f=\lambda^{i}f

{{\mathbb{G}}_{\mbox{\tiny\rm m}}}
-gerbe twisted sheaf is a quasi-coherent sheaf

For a

-twisted sheaf

The reduced geometric Hilbert polynomial for pure sheaves, which is denoted by

Let

{\mathfrak{S}}
-twisted coherent sheaf, it is semistable if for every proper twisted subsheaf

Then fixing a geometric Hilbert polynomial

{\mathfrak{S}}
-semistable coherent sheaves

The
{\mathfrak{S}}
-twisted Higgs sheaves can be similarly defined. The twisted Higgs pair

-twisted Higgs pairs on

Let

-twisted Higgs pairs

We still restrict to the moduli stack

For the DM stack

1.3.
S-duality conjecture-Global view

Our goal is to use the twisted Vafa-Witten invariants

-gerbe over a surface

We briefly review the construction and see §

2.3.
Brauer group and optimal gerbes

In this section we talk about the Brauer group and optimal gerbes. For the basic knowledge of Brauer group, see
[
49
]
.
Let

be a scheme, the Brauer group

parametrizes equivalence classes of sheaves of Azumaya algebras on

Definition 2.10
.

An Azumaya algebra on

is an associative (non-commutative)
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
which is locally isomorphic to a matrix algebra
M_{r}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
for some

Two Azumaya algebras
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
are called equivalent if there exist non-zero vector bundles

and

such that
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}nd(E)
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}nd(E^{\prime})
are isomorphic Azumaya algebras.
The Brauer group
\Br(X)
is the set of isomorphism classes of Azumaya algebras modulo the above equivalence relation.

The cohomological Brauer group
\Br^{\prime}(X)
is the torsion part
H^{2}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{*})_{\mathop{\rm tor}\nolimits}
of the étale cohomology
H^{2}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{*})
, which classifies the equivalence classes of
{{\mathbb{G}}_{\mbox{\tiny\rm m}}}
-gerbes on
X
. The following result is due to de Jong.

Theorem 2.11
.

(de Jong
[
12
]
)
If
X
is a quasi-compact separate scheme admitting an ample invertible sheaf, then
\Br(X)=\Br^{\prime}(X)
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
{\mathfrak{X}}\to X
be a
\mu_{r}
-gerbe. The period
\per({\mathfrak{X}})
is the order of
[{\mathfrak{X}}]
in
\Br(X)
.
A
\mu_{r}
-gerbe

is called optimal if the period
\per({\mathfrak{X}})=r
.

The Brauer group also classifies Brauer-Severi varieties, and these are projective bundles

over
X
, see
[
1
]
.
For us, if we have an optimal
\mu_{r}
-gerbe
{\mathfrak{X}}\to X
over a scheme

, then from Theorem
2.11
,
\Br(X)=\Br^{\prime}(X)
. We let

be the image of
[{\mathfrak{X}}]
under the map

o:H^{2}(X,\mu_{r})\to H^{2}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{*})

which must lie in the torsion part of
H^{2}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{*})
. Any Azumaya algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
is locally free of constant rank

. Two Azumaya algebras are isomorphic if they are isomorphic as
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-algebras. By Skolem-Noether theorem,

(acting by conjugation). Therefore, the set of isomorphism classes of Azumaya algebras A of rank

is in bijection with the set

. From the exact sequence

1\to\mu_{r}\longrightarrow SL_{r}\longrightarrow PGL_{r}\to 1

we have a morphism by taking cohomology

. We set

.
Then the optimal
\mu_{r}
-gerbe
[{\mathfrak{X}}]
also defines a class

such that

. This projective bundle

is the

variety.

Remark 2.13

If a
\mu_{r}
-gerbe

is zero in

6.1.
K3 surfaces- Picard numbers, Mukai Lattice and Brauer group

This section serves some basic knowledges on smooth K3 surfaces. Our reference is
[
20
]
.
Let
S
be a smooth projective K3 surface. The cohomology
H^{*}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
is zero on odd degrees, and
H^{0}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})=H^{4}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
,
H^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}^{22}
. The Hodge diamond for the Hodge numbers

h^{p,q}=\dim H^{q}(S,\Omega_{S}^{p})
is given by:

\begin{array}[]{cccccc}&&h^{0,0}&&\\
&h^{1,0}&&h^{0,1}&\\
h^{2,0}&&h^{1,1}&&h^{0,2}\\
&h^{2,1}&&h^{1,2}&&\\
&&h^{2,2}&&\end{array}=\begin{array}[]{cccccc}&&1&&\\
&0&&0&\\
1&&20&&1\\
&0&&0&&\\
&&1&&\end{array}

Let
\Pic(S)
be the Picard group of
S
.
The Néron-Severi group of
S
is the quotient
\NS(S)=\Pic(S)/\Pic^{0}(S)
, where
\Pic^{0}(S)
is the subgroup of all line bundles that are algebraically equivalent to zero.
The group
\Num(S)
is defined as the quotient of the Picard group
\Pic(S)
by the kernel of the intersection form on
H^{*}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
. We say that line bundles from the kernel are numerically trivial.
The rank of
\NS(S)
is called the Picard number and is denoted by
\rho(S)
. For algebraic K3 surface
S
.

\Pic(S)\cong\NS(S)\cong\Num(S).

The Picard number
\rho(S)
satisfies

0\leq\rho(S)\leq 20

for a smooth complex K3 surface
S
.
Over character zero field
\kappa
, a smooth K3 surface
S
with Picard number
\rho=20
is called a
singular K3
. Over the field
\kappa
with positive characteristic
p>0
, a K3 surface always has Picard rank
\rho(S)\geq 1
, and a K3 surface with Picard number
\rho(S)=22=h^{1,1}
is called a
supersingular K3
surface, see
[
47
]
.

On the cohomology
H^{*}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
, we review the Mukai lattice as in
[
64
]
.

Definition 6.1
.

Define a symmetric bilinear form on
H^{*}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
:

\langle x,y\rangle=-\int_{S}x^{\vee}y

where
x^{\vee}
is the dual of the class
x
. We call this lattice the Mukai lattice.

Remark 6.2
.

If
x=x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2}
, for
x_{i}\in H^{2i}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
,
y=y_{0}+y_{1}+y_{2}
, for
y_{i}\in H^{2i}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
, then

\vee:H^{*}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
is the homomorphism sending
x
to
x_{0}-x_{1}+x_{2}
. Then

\langle x,y\rangle=x_{1}y_{1}-x_{0}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{0}.

Let
E
be a coherent sheaf on
S
, we define

v(E):=\Ch(E)\sqrt{\mathop{\rm td}}_{S}=\Ch(E)(1+\omega)

where
\omega
is the fundamental class of
S
and
\Ch(E)
is the Chern character of
E
. Then Riemann-Roch theorem says that

\chi(E,F)=-\langle v(E),v(F)\rangle,

where
E
and
F
are coherent sheaves on
S
. This Mukai lattice has a decomposition:

H^{*}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})=H^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus H^{0}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus H^{4}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})=(-E_{8})^{\oplus 2}\oplus H^{4},

where
H
is the hyperbolic lattice. Some properties of the Mukai lattices like isometries can be found in
[
64
]
.

We talk about the Brauer group of
S
. According to
[
19
]
, also from de Jong,

\Br(S)=\Br^{\prime}(S)=H^{2}(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*})_{\mathop{\rm tor}\nolimits}.

Consider the exact sequence

1\to\mu_{r}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\cdot)^{r}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*}\to 1

and taking long cohomology we get an exact sequence:

1\to H^{1}(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*})/r\cdot H^{1}(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*})\longrightarrow H^{2}(S,\mu_{r})\longrightarrow H^{2}(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*})_{r-\mathop{\rm tor}\nolimits}\to 1.

This is true for any
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. Therefore

\Br(S)=({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{22-\rho(S)}
, where
\rho(S)
is the Picard number. Finally we have

H^{2}(S,\mu_{r})\cong({\mathbb{Z}}_{r})^{22}.

Let us pay attention to the case

for a prime number

Proposition 6.3

The number of equivalent classes of non-essentially trivial
\mu_{p}
-gerbes on

is

Proof.

The essentially trivial
\mu_{p}
-gerbes on

is given by the Picard number

∎

Remark 6.4

In the case

-gerbes on

\displaystyle=\sum_{c_{2}}\vw^{\mbox{\tiny tw}}_{2,{\mathcal{O}},c_{2}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{g})q^{c_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{c_{2}}\vw^{\mbox{\tiny tw}}_{v}({\mathfrak{S}}_{g})q^{c_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\frac{k}{2}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq-1}}\chi(\Hilb^{k+1}(S))q^{\frac{k}{2}}\cdot q^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\frac{k}{2}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}\chi(\Hilb^{k}(S))q^{\frac{k}{2}}\cdot q^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}q^{2}G(q^{\frac{1}{2}}).

Therefore

\displaystyle Z(S,\SU(2)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2};q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in H^{2}(S,\mu_{2})}Z_{2,{\mathcal{L}}_{g}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{g},q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}q^{2}G(q^{2})+2^{\rho(S)}\cdot\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})+G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)+(2^{22}-2^{\rho(S)})\cdot\frac{1}{2}q^{2}G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}q^{2}G(q^{2})+q^{2}\left(2^{21}G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})+2^{\rho(S)-1}G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right).

∎

Let

6.6.
Vafa-Witten’s S-duality conjecture for K3 surfaces

Vafa-Witten actually predicted the formula in
[
61
, Formula 4.18]
for any complex K3 surface
S
, which does not depend on the complex structure of
S
. We modify Conjecture
5.14
a bit for K3 surfaces and show that trivial and optimal
\mu_{2}
-gerbes on
S
give Vafa-Witten’s formula. We follow the notations in §
5.5
.

We fix a smooth projective K3 surface
S
. The
\mu_{2}
-gerbes
{\mathfrak{S}}\to S
are classified by

H^{2}(S,\mu_{2})
. Let
{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}\to S
be the trivial
\mu_{2}
-gerbe;
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\ess}\to S
be a nontrivial essentially trivial

\mu_{2}
-gerbe corresponding to a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}_{g}
; and
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\opt}\to S
be an optimal

\mu_{2}
-gerbe.

Let us first give a more detailed explanation of Vafa-Witten
[
61
, Page 55]
. Vafa-Witten actually sum over the topological data for the
\PGL_{2}
-bundles or Higgs sheaves
(E,\phi)
with first Chern class
g=c_{1}(E)\in H^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})/2\cdot H^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
. From Page 55 in
[
61
]
, the square
g^{2}
satisfies
g^{2}=c_{1}(E)^{2}\equiv 0,\text{or\penalty\ }2\mod 4
. They call

g

even
if
g^{2}\equiv 0\mod 4
; and
odd
if
g^{2}\equiv 2\mod 4
. There are three type of partition functions on a K3 surface
S
corresponding to
g=0
, even but not zero and odd. Let
n_{0},n_{\even},n_{\odd}
be the number of values of
g
that are respectively, trivial, even but nontrivial, and odd. Then

\begin{cases}n_{0}=1;\\
n_{\even}=\frac{2^{22}+2^{11}}{2}-1;\\
n_{\odd}=\frac{2^{22}-2^{11}}{2}.\end{cases}

From our formula in Corollary
6.21
we modify the calculation:

(6.6.1)

\displaystyle Z(S,\SU(2)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2};q)=Z_{2,{\mathcal{O}}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{0},q)+(2^{11}-1)Z_{r,{\mathcal{L}}_{g}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{\ess},q)+(2^{22}-2^{11})Z_{2,{\mathcal{O}}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{\opt},q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}q^{2}G(q^{2})+\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})+G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)+(2^{11}-1)\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})+G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle+(2^{22}-2^{11})\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle=\Big[\frac{1}{4}q^{2}G(q^{2})+\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})+G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)\Big]+\left((2^{11}-1)+\frac{2^{22}-2^{11}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})+G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle+(2^{22}-2^{11})\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)-\left(\frac{2^{22}-2^{11}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})+G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle=\Big[\frac{1}{4}q^{2}G(q^{2})+\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})+G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)\Big]+\left(\frac{2^{22}+2^{11}}{2}-1\right)\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})+G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2^{22}-2^{11}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})-G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle=Z_{0}(q)+n_{\even}Z_{\ess}(q)+n_{\odd}Z_{\odd}(q)

where we let

Z_{0}(q)=\frac{1}{4}q^{2}G(q^{2})+\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})+G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right);

Z_{\ess}(q)=\frac{1}{2}q^{2}\left(G(q^{\frac{1}{2}})+G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)

and

which are the formula in
[
61
, Formula (4.11), (4.12)]
.

From Proposition
6.8
and Proposition

and
Z_{2,{\mathcal{L}}_{g}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{\ess},q)=Z_{\ess}(q)

For optimal
\mu_{2}
-gerbes

{\mathfrak{S}}_{\opt}\to S
, note that for a Mukai vector

Lemma 6.25

Define

Then we have

Z_{2,{\mathcal{O}}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{\opt},(-1)^{2}\cdot q)=\frac{1}{2}q^{2}G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}}).

Proof.

From the calculation in the proof of Theorem
6.20
,

\displaystyle Z_{2,{\mathcal{O}}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{\opt},(-1)^{2}\cdot q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c_{2}}\vw^{\mbox{\tiny tw}}_{2,{\mathcal{O}},c_{2}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{\opt})(-1)^{2c_{2}}q^{c_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\frac{k}{2}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq-1}}\chi(\Hilb^{k+1}(S))(-1)^{k+4}\cdot q^{\frac{k}{2}}\cdot q^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\frac{k}{2}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}\chi(\Hilb^{k}(S))(-1)^{k+3}\cdot q^{\frac{k}{2}}\cdot q^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}q^{2}G(-q^{\frac{1}{2}}).

∎

There are two types of non-zero even classes

Definition 6.26

For the type of even classes

Then

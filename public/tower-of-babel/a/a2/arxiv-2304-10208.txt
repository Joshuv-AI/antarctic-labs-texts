A note on the 1-2-3 Theorem for infinite graphs
Marcin Stawiski stawiski@agh.edu.pl

Abstract
Karoński, Łuczak and Thomason conjectured in 2004 that for every finite graph without isolated edge, the edges can be assigned weights from { 1 , 2 , 3 } \{1,2,3\} in such a way that the endvertices of each edge have different sums of incident edge weights. This is known as the 1-2-3 Conjecture, and it was only recently proved by Keusch. We extend this result to infinite graphs in the following way. If G G is a graph without isolated edge, then the edges can be assigned weights from { 1 , 2 , 3 } \{1,2,3\} is such a way that the endvertices of each edge have different sum of incident edge weights, or these endvertices have both the same infinite degree. We also investigate the extensions of theorems about total and list versions of 1-2-3 Conjecture to infinite graphs. Keywords : 1-2-3 Conjecture, weighted degree of a vertex, infinite graphs, colourings Mathematics Subject Classifications : 05C15, 05C78

Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite or infinite graph. Let
k
be a natural number. A
k-edge weighting
is a function
\omega\colon E\rightarrow\{1,\dots,k\}
. The
weighted degree
of a vertex
v
of
G
is the sum
s_{\omega}(v)=\Sigma_{w\in N(v)}\omega(vw)
. We say that
\omega
is a k-
weighting
of
G
if it is a k-edge weighting such that the endvertices of each edge have different weighted degrees. Karoński, Łuczak, and Thomason
[
1
]
in 2004 conjectured that each finite graph without isolated edge has a 3-weighting. This is known as the 1-2-3 Conjecture. This conjecture has gained a lot of attention since it was formulated. There are many partial result regarding this conjecture and similar problems. The 1-2-3 Conjecture was only recently proved in its full generality by Keusch.

Theorem 1

(Keusch
[
2
]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite graph without isolated edge, then there exists a
3
-weighting of
G
.

In this note we are interested in extending theorem of Kusch to infinite graphs in the most general way possible. Notice that the weighted degree of a vertex of infinite degree is always equal to its degree. Hence, we cannot obtain different sums between vertices of the same infinite degree. The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be a graph without isolated edge, then there exists a
3
-weighting of
G
.

The 1-2-3 Conjecture was also studied in different variants. In particular, it was studied for total weightings
[
3
,
5
]
, and in the list version
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. The most general of these versions is for total list weightings. Let
L_{V}=\{L_{v}:v\in V\}
be a set of lists of weights for vertices of
G
such that
L(v)\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}
, and
L_{E}=\{L_{e}:e\in E\}
be a set of lists of weights for edges such that
L(e)\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}
, and
L=L_{V}\cup L_{E}
. We say that a function
\omega\colon V\cup E\rightarrow\{1,\dots,l\}
is a weighting of
G
from a set of lists
L
if
\omega(v)\in L_{v}
for every vertex
v
of
G
, and
\omega(e)\in L_{e}
for every edge
e
of
G
. We define the
weighted degree
of a vertex
v
by
s_{\omega}(v)=\Sigma_{w\in N(v)}\omega(vw)+\omega(v)
. We say that a graph
G
has a
L
-weighting if there exists a weighting
\omega
of
G
from a set of lists
L
such that for every edge of
G
, each of its endvertices have different weighted degree, or both its endvertices have the same infinite degree. This generalizes the previously defined notions, as we can obtain the edge versions by simply setting each
L_{v}=\{1\}
.
The general theorem proved in this paper allows us to obtain extensions of theorems about lists and total weightings. In particular, we prove the following theorems.

Theorem 3
.

Let
G
be a graph without isolated edge, and
L
be a set of lists for
G
such that each list for a vertex has one element, and each list for an edge has at least five elements. Then there exists a
L
-weighting of
G
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a graph, and
L
be a set of lists for
G
such that each list for a vertex has at least two elements, and each list for an edge has at least three elements. Then there exists a
L
-weighting of
G
.

Theorems
2
,
3
, and
4
shall follow from more general Theorem
5
proved in the next section, and their respective versions for finite graphs.

Theorem 5
.

Let
G
be a graph without isolated edge, and
L
be a list of weights for
G
. Let
H
be a subgraph of
G
. Let
L_{H}
be the set of lists obtained from
L
by restriction to the set
V(H)\cup E(H)
. If each finite induced subgraph
H
of
G
not isomorphic to
K_{2}
has a
L_{H}
-weighting, then
G
has a
L
-weighting.

For more on the 1-2-3 Conjecture and related problems see the survey of Seamone
[
4
]
.

2
General result

Before we prove Theorem
5
in its full generality, we prove it first for locally finite graphs.

Lemma 6
.

Let
G
be a locally finite graph without isolated edge, and
L
be a list of weights for
G
. Let
H
be a subgraph of
G
. Let
L_{H}
be the set of lists obtained from
L
by restriction to the set
V(H)\cup E(H)
. If each finite induced subgraph
H
of
G
not isomorphic to
K_{2}
has a
L_{H}
-weighting, then
G
has a
L
-weighting.

Proof.

By assumption if
G
is finite, then there exists a
L
-weighting of
G
. Therefore, we can assume that
G
is infinite. Let
H
be a component of
G
. It is enough to prove that there exists a
L_{H}
-weighting of each such
H
.

Choose an arbitrary vertex
v
of
H
. Let
B(v,i)
be the ball at centre
v
and radius
i
. Define
H_{i}=H[B(v,i)]
for each
i\geqslant 2
. If
2\leqslant j<i
, and
\omega_{i}
is a
L_{H_{i}}
-weighting, then define
\omega_{i,j}^{\prime}=\omega_{i}|_{H_{j}}
. Define
\Omega_{j}^{\prime}=\{\omega_{i,j}^{\prime}\colon\omega_{i}\textnormal{ is a }L_{H_{i}}\textnormal{-weighting of }H_{i}\textnormal{ for some }i\geqslant 2\}
. Note that
\omega_{j}^{\prime}\in\Omega_{j}^{\prime}
is not necessarily a
L_{H_{j}}
-weighting of
H_{j}
. By assumption
\Omega_{j}^{\prime}
for each
j\geqslant 2
is non-empty. For each
2\leqslant k<j
if
\omega_{k}\in\Omega_{k}^{\prime}
, then there exists a
\omega^{\prime}_{j}\in\Omega_{j}^{\prime}
such that
\omega_{k}^{\prime}\subset\omega_{j}^{\prime}
. Each set
\Omega_{i}^{\prime}
is finite, therefore by Kőnig’s Lemma there exists a function weighting
\omega
of
H
from the set of lists
L_{H}
such that for each
i\geqslant 2
we have
\omega|_{H_{i}}\in\Omega_{j}^{\prime}
. We will prove that
\omega
is a
L_{H}
-weighting of
H
.

Take any edge
tu\in E(H)
. We prove that the weighted degrees of
t
and
u
in
H
is different. There exists
i\geqslant 2
such that
t,u\in B(v,i)=V(H_{i})
. Let
\omega^{\prime}=\omega|_{V(H_{i+1}}
. For each
z\in V(H_{i})
the sum
s_{\omega}(z)=s_{\omega^{\prime}}(z)
because each addend is the same in both sums. It follows that
s_{\omega}(u)=s_{\omega^{\prime}}(u)\neq s_{\omega}(t)=s_{\omega^{\prime}}(t)
.
∎

After proving the lemma above, we are ready to prove Theorem
5
.

Proof.

First, define
G^{\prime}
as the graph obtained from
G
by deleting all the edges between vertices of infinite degree. Notice that if there exists a
L_{H}
-weighting of each component
H
of
G^{\prime}
which is not isomorphic to
K_{2}
, then there exists a
L
-weighting of
G
. Let
H
be a component of
G^{\prime}
which is not isomorphic to
K_{2}
. It is enough to prove that each such
H
has a
L_{H}
-weighting.

We construct a graph
H^{\prime}
in the following way. Take a vertex
v
of infinite degree
\kappa
. Let
\{u_{i}\colon i\leqslant\kappa\}
be the set of neighbours of
v
. We replace the vertex
v
with vertices
v_{i}
for
i\leqslant\kappa
, and we add an edge between vertices
v_{i}
and
u_{i}
for each
i\leqslant\kappa
. We set
L(v_{i})=L(v)
. We repeat this procedure for each vertex
v
of infinite degree. We denote the new set of lists by
L^{\prime}
. We claim that if there exists a
L^{\prime}
-weighting of each component of
H^{\prime}
not isomorphic to
K_{2}
, then there exists a 1-2-3 weighting of
H
.

If a component
F
of
H^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
K_{2}
, then one of its vertices
u
have degree one in
H
, and the other is obtained by splitting some vertex
v
of infinite degree. Vertex
v
is a unique neighbour of
u
in
H
. Therefore, the sum in
u
is always different that the sum in
v
in
H
. If
F
is not isomorphic to
K_{2}
, then
by Lemma
6
there exists a
L^{\prime}_{F}
-weighting
\omega_{F}
. We define the weighting
\omega
of
H
in a following way. If
uw
is an edge of
H
, then
\omega(uw)=\omega_{F}(uw)
for some component
F
of
H^{\prime}
which contains both
u
and
v
. If
u
is a vertex of
H
and
w
is obtained by splitting the vertex
v
of
H
, then we put
\omega(uv)=\omega_{F}(uw)
. If both
u
and
w
are obtained by splitting the vertices
v
and
t
, then we can define
\omega(vt)
arbitrarily. We will show that the weighting
\omega
is
L_{H}
-weighting of
H
.

Let
uw
be an edge in
H
. If both
u
and
w
have finite degree, then
s(u)=s_{F}(u)\neq s_{F}(w)=s_{F}(w)
for some component
F
which contains both
u
and
v
. If
u
or
w
has infinite degree, then the sum of
s(w)
or
s(u)
is infinite. It remains to notice that if both
w
and
u
have infinite but distinct degrees, then their sum is equal to their degrees, and therefore it is distinct.
∎

Application of the general result

Theorem
2
is obtained from Theorem
5
by setting
L(v)=\{1\}
for each vertex

are now satisfied by Theorem

. To prove Theorem

Theorem 7

(Zhu
[
7
]
)
.

Let
G

L
be a set of lists for
G

L
-weighting of

The statement of the theorem above is conjectured

Again, Theorem

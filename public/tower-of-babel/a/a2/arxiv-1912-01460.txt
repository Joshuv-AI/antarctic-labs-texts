Aidyn Kassymov, Michael Ruzhansky, Durvudkhan Suragan

Abstract
. In this note we prove the reverse Stein-Weiss inequality on general homogeneous Lie groups. The obtained results extend previously known inequalities. Special properties of homogeneous norms and the reverse integral Hardy inequality play key roles in our proofs. Also, we show reverse Hardy, Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev, L p L^{p} -Sobolev and L p L^{p} -Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities on homogeneous groups.

1.
Introduction

In one of their pioneering work
[
24
]
, Hardy and Littlewood considered the one dimensional fractional integral operator on
(0,\infty)
given by

(1.1)

T_{\lambda}u(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{\lambda}}dy,\,\,\,\,0<\lambda<1,

and proved the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<q<\infty
and
u\in L^{p}(0,\infty)
with
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\lambda-1
. Then

(1.2)

\|T_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,\infty)},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
u
.

The
N
-dimensional analogue of (
1.1
) can be written by the formula:

(1.3)

I_{\lambda}u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{\lambda}}dy,\,\,\,\,0<\lambda<N.

The
N
-dimensional case of Theorem
1.1
was extended by Sobolev in

[
44
]
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1<p<q<\infty
,
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\lambda}{N}-1
. Then

(1.4)

\|I_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
u
.

Later, in
[
46
]
Stein and Weiss obtained the following two-weight extention of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, which is known as the Stein-Weiss inequality or weighted Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<\lambda<N
,
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha<\frac{N(p-1)}{p}
,
\beta<\frac{N}{q}
,
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\lambda+\alpha+\beta}{N}-1
. If
1<p\leq q<\infty
, then

(1.5)

\||x|^{-\beta}I_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\||x|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
u
.

In the proof we use special properties of homogeneous norms of the homogeneous Lie groups and reverse integral Hardy inequality, which are playing key roles in our calculations. Thus, in Theorem

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous group of homogeneous dimension
Q
. Let
|\cdot|

be a homogeneous quasi-norm on
\mathbb{G}
. Let
1<p<Q
.
Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{G}\setminus\{0\})
be a complex-valued function. Then

(1.7)

\left\|\frac{f}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\leq\frac{p}{Q-p}\|\mathcal{R}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},\,\,\,\,\,1<p<Q,

where
\mathcal{R}=\frac{d}{d|x|}
is the radial derivative. The constant
\frac{p}{Q-p}
is sharp.

In Abelian case
(\mathbb{R}^{N},+)

Q=N

is the standard Euclidean distance, from (
1.7

\left\|\frac{f}{|x|_{E}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{p}{N-p}\left\|\frac{x}{|x|_{E}}\cdot\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\,\,\,\,\,1<p<N,

where
\nabla
is the standard Euclidean gradient. By using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality from the inequality (
1.8

1.6

In this note, we show the reverse Hardy inequality on homogeneous general Lie groups.

We are also interested in Sobolev and Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities, let us recall them briefly. In the classical work of Sobolev, he showed the following inequality:

(1.9)

\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\,\,\,1<p<N,

where
p^{*}=\frac{Np}{N-p}

L^{p}
-Sobolev inequality in the following form:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous group of homogeneous dimension
Q
. Let
|\cdot|

be a homogeneous quasi-norm on
\mathbb{G}
. Let
1<p<Q
.
Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{G}\setminus\{0\})
be a complex-valued function. Then

(1.10)

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\leq\frac{p}{Q}\|\mathbb{E}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},\,\,\,\,\,1<p<\infty,

where
\mathbb{E}=|x|\frac{d}{d|x|}
is the Euler operator. The constant
\frac{p}{Q}
is sharp.

In Abelian case
(\mathbb{R}^{N},+)
with
Q=N
and
|\cdot|=|\cdot|_{E}
where
|\cdot|_{E}
is the standard Euclidean distance, from (
1.7
) we have

(1.11)

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{p}{N-p}\left\|x\cdot\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\,\,\,\,\,1<p<\infty,

where
\nabla
is the standard Euclidean gradient. In the Euclidean case, this
L^{p}
-Sobolev inequality has been considered in
[
28
]
.

In their fundamental work
[
2
]
, L. Caffarelli, R. Kohn and L. Nirenberg established:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
N\geq 1
, and let
l_{1}
,
l_{2}
,
l_{3}
,
a,\,b,\,d,\,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
l_{1},l_{2}\geq 1
,

l_{3}>0,\,\,0\leq\delta\leq 1,
and

(1.12)

\frac{1}{l_{1}}+\frac{a}{N},\,\,\,\frac{1}{l_{2}}+\frac{b}{N},\,\,\,\frac{1}{l_{3}}+\frac{\delta d+(1-\delta)b}{N}>0.

Then,

(1.13)

\||x|^{\delta d+(1-\delta)b}u\|_{L^{l_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\||x|^{a}\nabla u\|^{\delta}_{L^{l_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\||x|^{b}u\|^{1-\delta}_{L^{l_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\,\,\,u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

if and only if

(1.14)

\frac{1}{l_{3}}+\frac{\delta d+(1-\delta)b}{N}=\delta\left(\frac{1}{l_{1}}+\frac{a-1}{N}\right)+(1-\delta)\left(\frac{1}{l_{2}}+\frac{b}{N}\right),\\
a-d\geq 0,\,\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\delta>0,\\
a-d\leq 1,\,\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\delta>0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\frac{1}{l_{3}}+\frac{\delta d+(1-\delta)b}{N}=\frac{1}{l_{1}}+\frac{a-1}{N},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
u
.

On homogeneous groups,
L^{p}
-Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality was obtained in
[

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous group
of homogeneous dimension
Q
and let
\alpha,\,\beta\in\mathbb{R}

Then for all complex-valued functions
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{G}\backslash\{0\}),

1<p<\infty,

and any homogeneous quasi-norm
|\cdot|
on
\mathbb{G}
we have

(1.15)

\frac{|Q-\gamma|}{p}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{\frac{\gamma}{p}}}\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\leq\left\|\frac{1}{|x|^{\alpha}}\mathcal{R}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{\frac{\beta}{p-1}}}\right\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},

where
\gamma=\alpha+\beta+1
. If
\gamma\neq Q
then the constant
\frac{|Q-\gamma|}{p}
is sharp.

2.
Main results

Let us recall that a Lie group (on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
)
\mathbb{G}
with the dilation

D_{\lambda}(x):=(\lambda^{\nu_{1}}x_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\nu_{N}}x_{N}),\;\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n}>0,\;D_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N},

which is an automorphism of the group
\mathbb{G}
for each
\lambda>0,

is called a
homogeneous (Lie) group
. For simplicity, throughout this paper we use the notation
\lambda x
for the dilation
D_{\lambda}.
The homogeneous dimension of the homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
is denoted by
Q:=\nu_{1}+\ldots+\nu_{N}.

Also, in this note we denote a homogeneous quasi-norm on
\mathbb{G}
by
|x|
, which
is a continuous non-negative function

(2.1)

\mathbb{G}\ni x\mapsto|x|\in[0,\infty),

with the properties

i)

|x|=|x^{-1}|
for all
x\in\mathbb{G}
,

ii)

|\lambda x|=\lambda|x|
for all
x\in\mathbb{G}
and
\lambda>0
,

iii)

|x|=0
iff
x=0
.

Moreover, the following polarisation formula on homogeneous Lie groups will be used in our proofs:
there is a (unique)
positive Borel measure
\sigma
on the
unit quasi-sphere

\mathfrak{S}:=\{x\in\mathbb{G}:\,|x|=1\},

so that for every
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
we have

(2.2)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}f(ry)r^{Q-1}d\sigma(y)dr.

The quasi-ball centred at
x\in\mathbb{G}
with radius
R>0
can be defined by

B(x,R):=\{y\in\mathbb{G}:|x^{-1}y|<R\}.

We refer to
[
15
]
for the original appearance of such groups, and to
[
13
,
35
]
for a recent comprehensive treatment.

Let us consider the integral operator

(2.3)

I_{\lambda,|\cdot|}u(x)=|x|^{\lambda}*u=\int_{\mathbb{G}}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy,\,\,\,\lambda>0,

where
*
is the convolution.
Let us recall briefly the reverse Hölder’s inequality.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
1
]
, Theorem 2.12, p. 27))
.

Let
p\in(0,1)
, so that
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}<0
. If non-negative functions satisfy
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
and
0<\int_{\mathbb{G}}g^{p^{\prime}}(x)dx<+\infty,
we have

(2.4)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(x)g(x)dx\geq\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}g^{p^{\prime}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

Let us also recall a well-known fact about quasi-norms.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
13
]
, Theorem 3.1.39)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous Lie group. Then there exists a homogeneous
quasi-norm on
\mathbb{G}
which is a norm, that is, a homogeneous quasi-norm
|\cdot|

which satisfies the triangle inequality

(2.5)

|xy|\leq|x|+|y|,\,\,\,\forall x,y\in\mathbb{G}.

Furthermore, all homogeneous quasi-norms on
\mathbb{G}
are equivalent.

The next theorem is the reverse integral version of Hardy inequalities on general homogeneous groups that will be instrumental in our proof.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous
group of homogeneous dimension
Q
. Assume that
p\in(0,1)
and
q<0
. Suppose that
W,U\geq 0
are locally integrable functions on
\mathbb{G}
. Then the inequality

(2.6)

\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{B(0,|x|)}f(y)dy\right)^{q}W(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C_{1}(p,q)\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)U(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for some
C_{1}(p,q)>0
and all non-negative measurable functions
f,
if and only if

(2.7)

0<A_{1}:=\inf_{x\neq a}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,|x|)}W(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B(0,|x|)}U^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right].

Moreover, the biggest constant
C_{1}(p,q)
in (
2.6
) has the following relation to
A_{1}
:

(2.8)

A_{1}\geq C_{1}(p,q)\geq\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{q}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}A_{1}.

Also, inequality

(2.9)

\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,|x|)}f(y)dy\right)^{q}W(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C_{2}(p,q)\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)U(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for some
C_{2}(p,q)>0
and all non-negative measurable functions
f,
if and only if

(2.10)

0<A_{2}:=\inf_{x\neq a}\left[\left(\int_{B(0,|x|)}W(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,|x|)}U^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right].

Moreover, the biggest constant
C_{2}(p,q)
in (
2.9
) has the following relation to
A_{2}
:

(2.11)

A_{2}\geq C_{2}(p,q)\geq\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{q}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}A_{2}.

Remark 2.4
.

In our sense, the negative exponent
q<0
of
0
, we understand in the following form:

0^{q}=(+\infty)^{-q}=+\infty,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,0^{-q}=(+\infty)^{q}=0.

Now we formulate the reverse Stein-Weiss inequality on
\mathbb{G}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous group of homogeneous dimension
Q\geq 1
and let
|\cdot|
be an arbitrary homogeneous quasi-norm on
\mathbb{G}
.
Let
\lambda>0
,
p,q^{\prime}\in(0,1)
,
0\leq\alpha<-\frac{Q}{q}
,
0\leq\beta<-\frac{Q}{p^{\prime}}
,
\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\alpha+\beta+\lambda}{Q}+2
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
and
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
. Then for all non-negative functions
f\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{G})
and
h\in L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
we have

(2.12)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}f(x)h(y)|y|^{\beta}dxdy\geq C\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{G})}\|h\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
f
and
h
.

Corollary 2.6
.

By setting
\alpha=\beta=0
we get the reverse Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality on the homogeneous groups, in the following form:

(2.13)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}f(x)h(y)dxdy\geq C\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{G})}\|h\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},

for all non-negative functions
f\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{G})
and
h\in L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
with
\lambda>0
,
p,q^{\prime}\in(0,1)
,
\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\lambda}{Q}+2
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
and
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
.

Remark 2.7
.

In the Abelian (Euclidean) case
{\mathbb{G}}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},+)
, hence
Q=N
and
|\cdot|
can be any homogeneous quasi-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, in particular with the usual Euclidean distance, i.e.
|\cdot|=\|\cdot\|_{E}
, this was investigated in
[
4
]
.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

By using reverse Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
(Theorem
2.1
) in (
2.12
), we calculate,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha}f(x)|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}&h(y)|y|^{\beta}dydx=\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}h(y)|y|^{\beta}dy\right)f(x)dx\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Holin}}}{{\geq}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}h(y)|y|^{\beta}dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{G})}.\end{split}

So for (
2.12
), it is enough to show that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}h(y)|y|^{\beta}dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C\|h\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},

and by changing
u(y)=h(y)|y|^{\beta}
, this is equivalent to

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\leq C\||y|^{-\beta}u\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.

We have that

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\geq\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|x|^{\alpha}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy,

then

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q<0}}{{\leq}}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|x|^{\alpha}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}.

Therefore, we obtain

(2.14)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q<0}}{{\geq}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}:=I^{\frac{1}{q}}_{1}.

Similarly with (
2.14
), we have

(2.15)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q<0}}{{\geq}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{\mathbb{G}}\setminus B(0,2|x|)}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}:=I^{\frac{1}{q}}_{2}.

By summarising above facts, from (
2.14
)-(
2.15
), we have

(2.16)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq\frac{I^{\frac{1}{q}}_{1}}{2}+\frac{I^{\frac{1}{q}}_{2}}{2}.

From now on, in view of Proposition
2.2
we can assume that our quasi-norm is actually a norm.

Step 1.
Let us consider
I_{1}
. By using Proposition
2.2
and the properties of the quasi-norm with
|y|\leq\frac{|x|}{2}
, we get

(2.17)

|x|=|x^{-1}|=|x^{-1}yy^{-1}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{tri}}}{{\leq}}|x^{-1}y|+|y^{-1}|=|y^{-1}x|+|y|\leq|y^{-1}x|+\frac{|x|}{2}.

Then for any
\lambda>0
, we have

2^{-\lambda}|x|^{\lambda}\leq|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}.

It means,

2^{-\lambda}\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\leq\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy,

so that

\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}\leq 2^{-\lambda q}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}.

Therefore, we get

\begin{split}I_{1}&=\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\\
&\leq 2^{-\lambda q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{(\alpha+\lambda)q}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx.\end{split}

If condition (
2.7
) in Theorem
2.3
with
W(x)=|x|^{(\alpha+\lambda)q}
and
U(y)=|y|^{-\beta p}
in (
2.6
) is satisfied, then we have

I_{1}\leq 2^{-\lambda q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{B(0,\frac{|x|}{2})}u(y)dy\right)^{q}|x|^{(\alpha+\lambda)q}dx\leq C_{1}\||y|^{-\beta}u\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.

Let us verify condition (
2.7
). By using assumption
\beta<-\frac{Q}{p^{\prime}}
, we obtain

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\frac{\alpha+\beta+\lambda}{Q}+2<\frac{\alpha+\lambda}{Q}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+2,

that is,
\frac{Q+(\alpha+\lambda)q}{Qq}>0,
then
Q+(\alpha+\lambda)q<0
and by using the polar decomposition (
2.2
):

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,|x|)}W(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}&=\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,|x|)}|y|^{(\alpha+\lambda)q}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&=\left(\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}r^{Q-1}r^{(\alpha+\lambda)q}drd\sigma(\omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&=\left(|\mathfrak{S}|\int_{|x|}^{\infty}r^{Q-1+(\alpha+\lambda)q}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&=\left(-\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+(\alpha+\lambda)q}|x|^{Q+(\alpha+\lambda)q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+(\alpha+\lambda)q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}|x|^{\frac{Q+(\alpha+\lambda)q}{q}}.\end{split}

Since
\beta<-\frac{Q}{p^{\prime}}
, we have

-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})+Q>-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})-\beta p^{\prime}=0.

So,
-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})+Q>0
. Then, let us consider

(2.18)

\begin{split}\left(\int_{B(0,|x|)}U^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}&=\left(\int_{B(0,|x|)}|y|^{-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(\int^{|x|}_{0}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}r^{-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})}r^{Q-1}drd\sigma(\omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(|\mathfrak{S}|\int^{|x|}_{0}r^{-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})+Q-1}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})+Q}|x|^{-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})+Q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}|x|^{\frac{-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})+Q}{p^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

Therefore, we have

(2.19)

\begin{split}A_{1}&=\inf_{x\neq a}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,|x|)}W(y)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B(0,|x|)}U^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+(\alpha+\lambda)q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\inf_{x\neq a}|x|^{\frac{(\alpha+\lambda)q+Q}{q}+\frac{-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})+Q}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+(\alpha+\lambda)q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\inf_{x\neq a}|x|^{Q\left(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{\alpha+\beta+\lambda}{Q}\right)}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+(\alpha+\lambda)q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\inf_{x\neq a}|x|^{Q\left(2-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\alpha+\beta+\lambda}{Q}\right)}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+(\alpha+\lambda)q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q-\beta p(1-p^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}>0.\end{split}

Then by using (
2.6
), we obtain

(2.20)

I_{1}\leq 2^{-\lambda q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{(\alpha+\lambda)q}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\leq 2^{-\lambda q}C^{q}_{1}\||y|^{-\beta}u\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},

so that

(2.21)

I^{\frac{1}{q}}_{1}\geq 2^{-\lambda}C_{1}\||y|^{-\beta}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}=2^{-\lambda}C_{1}\|h\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.

Step 2.
As in the previous case
I_{1}
, now we consider
I_{2}
. From
2|x|\leq|y|
, we calculate

|y|=|y^{-1}|=|y^{-1}xx^{-1}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{tri}}}{{\leq}}|y^{-1}x|+|x|\leq|y^{-1}x|+\frac{|y|}{2},

that is,

\frac{|y|}{2}\leq|y^{-1}x|.

Then, if condition (
2.10
) with
W(x)=|x|^{\alpha q}
and
U(y)=|y|^{-(\beta+\lambda)p}
is satisfied, then we have

I_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,2|x|)}|x|^{\alpha}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\\
\leq 2^{-\lambda q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,2|x|)}u(y)|y|^{\lambda}dy\right)^{q}dx\leq 2^{-\lambda q}\||y|^{-\beta}u\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.

Now let us check condition (
2.10
). We have

\begin{split}\left(\int_{B(0,|x|)}W(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}&=\left(\int_{B(0,|x|)}|y|^{\alpha q}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&=\left(\int_{0}^{|x|}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}r^{\alpha q}r^{Q-1}drd\sigma(\omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\alpha q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}|x|^{\frac{Q+\alpha q}{q}},\end{split}

where
Q+\alpha q>0
. From
\alpha<-\frac{Q}{q}
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\alpha+\beta+\lambda}{Q}+2<-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{\beta+\lambda}{Q}+2=\frac{\beta+\lambda}{Q}+1+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}},\end{split}

then

(2.22)

(\beta+\lambda)p^{\prime}+Q<0.

By using this fact, we have

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,|x|)}U^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}&=\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,|x|)}|y|^{-(\beta+\lambda)(1-p^{\prime})p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}r^{Q-1}r^{-(\beta+\lambda)(1-p^{\prime})p}drd\sigma(\omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(|\mathfrak{S}|\int_{|x|}^{\infty}r^{-(\beta+\lambda)(1-p^{\prime})p+Q-1}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{betalambda}}}{{=}}\left(-\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q-(\beta+\lambda)(1-p^{\prime})p}|x|^{Q-(\beta+\lambda)(1-p^{\prime})p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(-\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+(\beta+\lambda)p^{\prime}}|x|^{Q+(\beta+\lambda)p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+(\beta+\lambda)p^{\prime}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}|x|^{\frac{Q+(\beta+\lambda)p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

Combining these facts we have

(2.23)

\begin{split}A_{2}&=\inf_{x\neq a}\left(\int_{B(0,|x|)}W(y)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,|x|)}U^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\alpha q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+(\beta+\lambda)p^{\prime}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\inf_{x\neq a}|x|^{\frac{Q+\alpha q}{q}+\frac{Q+(\beta+\lambda)p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\alpha q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+(\beta+\lambda)p^{\prime}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\inf_{x\neq a}|x|^{\frac{Q}{q}+\alpha+\frac{Q}{p^{\prime}}+\beta+\lambda}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\alpha q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+(\beta+\lambda)p^{\prime}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\inf_{x\neq a}|x|^{Q\left(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{\alpha+\beta+\lambda}{Q}\right)}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\alpha q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+(\beta+\lambda)p^{\prime}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\inf_{x\neq a}|x|^{Q\left(2-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\alpha+\beta+\lambda}{Q}\right)}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\alpha q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+(\beta+\lambda)p^{\prime}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}>0.\end{split}

Therefore, we have

I_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,2|x|)}|x|^{\alpha}u(y)|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}dy\right)^{q}dx\leq 2^{-\lambda q}C^{q}_{2}\||y|^{-\beta}u\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.

Then, we have

(2.24)

I^{\frac{1}{q}}_{2}\geq 2^{-\lambda}C_{2}\||y|^{-\beta}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}=2^{-\lambda}C_{2}\|h\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.

Finally, by using (
2.21
) and (
2.24
) in (
2.16
), we obtain

(2.25)

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}&\geq\frac{I^{\frac{1}{q}}_{1}}{2}+\frac{I^{\frac{1}{q}}_{2}}{2}\\
&\geq\frac{2^{-\lambda}(C_{1}+C_{2})}{2}\||y|^{-\beta}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\\
&=\frac{2^{-\lambda}(C_{1}+C_{2})}{2}\||y|^{-\beta}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\\
&=C_{3}\||y|^{-\beta}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},\end{split}

where
C_{3}=\frac{2^{-\lambda}(C_{1}+C_{2})}{2}>0
.

Theorem
2.5
is proved.
∎

Let us give improved reverse Stein-Weiss inequality.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous group of homogeneous dimension
Q\geq 1
and let
|\cdot|
be an arbitrary homogeneous quasi-norm on
\mathbb{G}
.
Let
\lambda>0
,
p,q^{\prime}\in(0,1)
and
\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\alpha+\beta+\lambda}{Q}+2
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
,
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
. Then for all non-negative functions
f\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{G})
and
h\in L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
, inequality (
2.12
) holds, that is,

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}f(x)h(y)|y|^{\beta}dxdy\geq C\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{G})}\|h\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},

if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(a)

0\leq\alpha<-\frac{Q}{q}
.

(b)

0\leq\beta<-\frac{Q}{p^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Let us prove (a). By using some notations from proof of Theorem
2.5
and (
2.16
), we obtain

(2.26)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq I^{\frac{1}{q}}_{2},

and by using Step 2 in the proof of Theorem
2.5
and from (
2.24
), we have
I_{2}^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C\||y|^{-\beta}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},
then we get

(2.27)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq I^{\frac{1}{q}}_{2}\geq C\||y|^{-\beta}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.

Let us prove (b). By (
2.16
), we obtain

(2.28)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq I^{\frac{1}{q}}_{1},

and by using Step 1 in the proof of Theorem
2.5
and from (
2.14
), we have
I_{1}^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C\||y|^{-\beta}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},
then we get

(2.29)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|x|^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}|y^{-1}x|^{\lambda}u(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq I^{\frac{1}{q}}_{1}\geq C\||y|^{-\beta}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.

∎

Let us give reverse Hardy,
L^{p}
-Sobolev and
L^{p}
-Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities on
\mathbb{G}
.
Assume now that
f
is a radially decreasing function, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}f:=\frac{d}{d|x|}f<0
. Let us give the reverse Hardy inequality on homogeneous Lie groups, the reverse to Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 2.9

(Reverse Hardy inequality)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous Lie group with homogeneous dimension
Q\geq 1
. Assume that
p\in(0,1)
. Then for any non-negative, real-valued and radially decreasing function
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{G}\setminus\{0\})
, we have

(2.30)

\left\|\frac{f}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\geq\frac{p}{Q-p}\|\mathcal{R}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.

Proof.

Let us denote
\mathcal{R}_{1}=-\mathcal{R}
, so that we have
\mathcal{R}_{1}f>0
. By using polar decomposition (
2.2
), integration by parts and reverse Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

(2.31)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{f^{p}(x)}{|x|^{p}}dx&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\frac{f^{p}(ry)}{r^{p}}r^{Q-1}drd\sigma(y)\\
&=-\frac{p}{Q-p}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{f^{p-1}(x)}{|x|^{p-1}}\mathcal{R}f(x)dx\\
&=\frac{p}{Q-p}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{f^{p-1}(x)}{|x|^{p-1}}\mathcal{R}_{1}f(x)dx\\
&\geq\frac{p}{Q-p}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|}\right\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\|\mathcal{R}_{1}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.\end{split}

This gives

(2.32)

\left\|\frac{f}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\geq\frac{p}{Q-p}\|\mathcal{R}_{1}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},

implying (
2.30
).
∎

Let us define by
\mathbb{E}=|x|\mathcal{R}
the Euler operator. Then we have the reverse
L^{p}
-Sobolev inequality, the reverse to Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 2.10

(Reverse
L^{p}
-Sobolev inequality)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous Lie group with homogeneous dimension
Q\geq 1
. Assume that
p\in(0,1)
. Then for any non-negative, real-valued and radially decreasing function
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{G}\setminus\{0\})
, we have

(2.33)

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\geq\frac{p}{Q}\|\mathbb{E}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.

Proof.

Let us denote
\mathbb{E}_{1}=|x|\mathcal{R}_{1}
, so that
\mathbb{E}_{1}f>0.
By using polar decomposition (
2.2
), integration by parts and reverse Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

(2.34)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)dx&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}f^{p}(ry)r^{Q-1}drd\sigma(y)\\
&=-\frac{p}{Q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p-1}(x)|x|\mathcal{R}f(x)dx\\
&=\frac{p}{Q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p-1}(x)|x|\mathcal{R}_{1}f(x)dx\\
&=\frac{p}{Q}\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p-1}(x)\mathbb{E}_{1}f(x)dx\\
&\geq\frac{p}{Q}\left\|f\right\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\|\mathbb{E}_{1}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.\end{split}

This gives

(2.35)

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\geq\frac{p}{Q}\|\mathbb{E}_{1}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},

implying (
2.33
).
∎

Let us give the reverse
L^{p}
-Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality on
\mathbb{G}
, that is, the reverse to Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem 2.11

(Reverse
L^{p}
-Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous Lie group with homogeneous dimension
Q\geq 1
. Assume that
p\in(0,1)
. Then for any nonnegative, real-valued and radially decreasing function
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{G}\setminus\{0\})
, we have

(2.36)

\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{\frac{\gamma}{p}}}\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\geq\frac{p}{Q-\gamma}\left\|\frac{\mathcal{R}f}{|x|^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{\frac{\beta}{p-1}}}\right\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma=\alpha+\beta+1
, such that
Q>\gamma
.

Proof.

By using polar decomposition (
2.2
), integration by parts and reverse Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

(2.37)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{f^{p}(x)}{|x|^{\gamma}}dx&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\frac{f^{p}(ry)}{r^{\gamma}}r^{Q-1}drd\sigma(y)\\
&=-\frac{p}{Q-\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{f^{p-1}(x)}{|x|^{\gamma-1}}\mathcal{R}f(x)dx\\
&=\frac{p}{Q-\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{f^{p-1}(x)}{|x|^{\alpha+\beta}}\mathcal{R}_{1}f(x)dx\\
&=\frac{p}{Q-\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{f^{p-1}(x)}{|x|^{\beta}}\frac{\mathcal{R}_{1}f(x)}{|x|^{\alpha}}dx\\
&\geq\frac{p}{Q-\gamma}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{\frac{\beta p^{\prime}}{p}}}\right\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\left\|\frac{\mathcal{R}_{1}f}{|x|^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\\
&=\frac{p}{Q-\gamma}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{\frac{\beta\frac{p}{p-1}}{p}}}\right\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\left\|\frac{\mathcal{R}_{1}f}{|x|^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\\
&=\frac{p}{Q-\gamma}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{\frac{\beta}{p-1}}}\right\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\left\|\frac{\mathcal{R}_{1}f}{|x|^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.\end{split}

This gives

(2.38)

\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{\frac{\gamma}{p}}}\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\geq\frac{p}{Q-\gamma}\left\|\frac{\mathcal{R}_{1}f}{|x|^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{\frac{\beta}{p-1}}}\right\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})},

which implies (
2.36
).
∎

Remark 2.12
.

In (
2.36
), if we take
\gamma=p
and
\alpha=0
, then we have the reverse Hardy inequality. Also, if we take
\gamma=0
and
\beta=0
, then we have the reverse
L^{p}
-Sobolev inequality.

Minimality of vortex solutions to Ginzburg–Landau type systems for gradient fields in the unit ball in dimension N ≥ 4 N\geq 4
Radu Ignat, Mickael Nahon, Luc Nguyen

Abstract
We prove that the degree-one vortex solution is the unique minimizer for the Ginzburg–Landau functional for gradient fields (that is, the Aviles–Giga model) in the unit ball B N B^{N} in dimension N ≥ 4 N\geq 4 and with respect to its boundary value. A similar result is also proved for 𝕊 N \mathbb{S}^{N} -valued maps in the theory of micromagnetics. Two methods are presented. The first method is an extension of the analogous technique previously used to treat the unconstrained Ginzburg–Landau functional in dimension N ≥ 7 N\geq 7 . The second method uses a symmetrization procedure for gradient fields such that the L 2 L^{2} -norm is invariant while the L p L^{p} -norm, 2 < p < ∞ 2<p<\infty , and the H 1 H^{1} -norm are lowered. Keywords: Minimality, vortex solutions, gradient fields, Ginzburg–Landau, Aviles–Giga, Hardy’s inequality, symmetrization. MSC 2020: 26D10, 35J20, 35J50, 35Q56.

\int_{B^{N}}L_{*}(\nabla v)\cdot(\nabla v)\,dx=\int_{B^{N}}\Big((\Delta v)^{2}-\frac{N-1}{r^{2}}|\nabla v|^{2}\Big)\,dx\geq 0\text{ for }v\in H_{0}^{2}(B^{N},\mathbb{R}),

i.e.
L_{*}
is positive definite on the subspace of gradient fields in
H^{1}_{0}(B^{N},\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
This is a consequence of the sharp Hardy inequality for gradient fields (see e.g.
[

,

,

,

):

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\Delta v)^{2}\,dx\geq c_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dx,\text{ for }v\in H_{0}^{2}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})\text{ where }c_{N}:=\begin{cases}\frac{N^{2}}{4}&\text{ if }N\geq 5\\
3&\text{ if }N=4\\
\frac{25}{36}&\text{ if }N=3,\\
0&\text{ if }N=2.\end{cases}

(1.16)

For the general case
\epsilon>0
, we combine the above idea with the machineries in
[

]
and

]
, based on the Hardy decomposition method.

Unfortunately, the above strategy does not work in dimension

Proposition 4

In dimension

, there exists a function

Method 2

As mentioned above, the second method of proof uses a symmetrization procedure. For that, we use the spherical coordinates: for every
x\in B^{N}
, we write
x=r\theta
with
r=|x|
and
\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}
. For
v\in H^{1}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
, we associate the radial function
\check{v}=\check{v}(r)
given by the formula

\check{v}(r)=-\int_{r}^{1}\Big(\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\nabla v(s\theta)|^{2}d\sigma(\theta)\Big)^{1/2}\,ds\leq 0,\quad r\in(0,1).

(1.17)

One can think of this as a kind of rearrangement in the spherical harmonic decomposition of
v
(see Section
3.2
for more detailed discussion).

We prove the following.

Theorem 5
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
v\in H^{1}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
and
\check{v}
be associated to
v
by (
1.17
). The following conclusions hold.

(i)

The map
v\mapsto\check{v}
is a Lipschitz continuous map from
H^{1}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
into
H^{1}_{0}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\nabla\check{v}(r\theta)|^{2}\,d\sigma(\theta)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\nabla v(r\theta)|^{2}\,d\sigma(\theta)\text{ for a.e. }r\in(0,1).

(ii)

Let
G:[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be continuous. If
G
is convex in the second variable, then

\displaystyle\int_{B^{N}}G(r,|\nabla\check{v}|^{2})\,dx\leq\int_{B^{N}}G(r,|\nabla v|^{2})\,dx.

In particular, for any
2<p<\infty
,

\int_{B^{N}}|\nabla\check{v}|^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{B^{N}}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx.

(iii)

If
v\in H_{0}^{1}(B^{N})
, i.e. if
v=0
on
\partial B^{N}
and
1\leq p\leq 2
, then

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}}|\check{v}(r\theta)|^{p}\,d\sigma(\theta)\geq\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}}|v(r\theta)|^{p}\,d\sigma(\theta)\text{ for a.e. }r\in(0,1).

(iv)

Assume in addition that
v\in H^{2}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
with the boundary condition
\nabla v(x)=cx
on
\partial B^{N}
for some constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
\check{v}\in H^{2}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
and
\nabla\check{v}(x)=|c|x
on
\partial B^{N}
. If
N\geq 5
, then

\int_{B^{N}}(\Delta\check{v})^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{B^{N}}(\Delta v)^{2}\,dx.

(1.18)

If
N\in\{2,3,4\}
, (

) continues to hold provided that
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}v(r\theta)\theta\,d\sigma(\theta)=0
for a.e.

. In either case, equality is attained if and only if

is radially symmetric and
|v^{\prime}|=\check{v}^{\prime}
in
(0,1)
.

To apply Theorem

In this section we give the first proof of Theorem

based on the strategy in
[

,
32
]
and exploiting the additional structure of a gradient field for the first
N
-components of the current admissible configurations.

Recall that
\ell(\epsilon)
is the first eigenvalue of the operator
L_{\epsilon}=-\Delta-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-f_{\epsilon}^{2})
and that the escaping radially symmetric critical points
U_{\epsilon,\eta}^{\pm}
with
g_{\epsilon,\eta}>0
exist if and only if
W^{\prime}(1)>0
and
\ell(\epsilon)+\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\tilde{W}^{\prime}(0)<0
(equivalently
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
and
\eta>\eta_{0}(\epsilon)
). For fixed
\epsilon>0,\eta>0
, we let

\Phi=\begin{cases}U_{\epsilon,\eta}^{+}&\text{ if }W^{\prime}(1)>0\text{ and }\ell(\epsilon)+\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\tilde{W}^{\prime}(0)<0\text{ (i.e. there is an escaping solution),}\\
\bar{U}_{\epsilon}&\text{ otherwise}\text{ (i.e. there is no escaping solution),}\end{cases}

and

(f,g)=\begin{cases}(f_{\epsilon,\eta},g_{\epsilon,\eta})&\text{ if }W^{\prime}(1)>0\text{ and }\ell(\epsilon)+\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\tilde{W}^{\prime}(0)<0,\\
(f_{\epsilon},0)&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

so that
\Phi(x)=(f(r)\frac{x}{r},g(r))
.

We consider the differential operators
L_{\epsilon,\eta}
and
T_{\epsilon,\eta}
:

L_{\epsilon,\eta}=-\Delta-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-f^{2}-g^{2}),\quad T_{\epsilon,\eta}=-\Delta-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-f^{2}-g^{2})+\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\tilde{W}^{\prime}(g^{2}).

For any
v\in H^{2}_{0}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
, we let

F_{\epsilon,\eta}[\nabla v]=\int_{B^{N}}\left((\Delta v)^{2}-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-f^{2}-g^{2})|\nabla v|^{2}\right)\,dx=\int_{B^{N}}L_{\epsilon,\eta}(\nabla v)\colon(\nabla v)\,dx.

(2.1)

Note that
\int_{B^{N}}|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}\,dx=\int_{B^{N}}(\Delta v)^{2}\,dx
since
v\in H^{2}_{0}(B^{N})
. Note also that, in the non-escaping case,
\Phi=\bar{U}_{\epsilon}
,
L_{\epsilon,\eta}=L_{\epsilon}
,
T_{\epsilon,\eta}=\bar{T}_{\epsilon,\eta}
and
F_{\epsilon,\eta}=F_{\epsilon}
introduced in sections
1.3
and
1.4
.

As in
[
31
,
32
]
, the starting point of the proof is the following consequence of the convexity of
W
and
\tilde{W}
:

Lemma 6
.

For any
v\in H^{2}_{0}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
and
p\in H^{1}_{0}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
,

E_{\epsilon,\eta}\big[\Phi+(\nabla v,p)\big]-E_{\epsilon,\eta}[\Phi]\geq\frac{1}{2}F_{\epsilon,\eta}[\nabla v]+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B^{N}}T_{\epsilon,\eta}p\cdot p\,dx.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle E_{\epsilon,\eta}\big[\Phi+(\nabla v,p)\big]-E_{\epsilon,\eta}[\Phi]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B^{N}}\Big\{2\nabla\Phi:\nabla(\nabla v,p)+|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}+|\nabla p|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}[W(1-|\Phi+(\nabla v,p)|^{2})-W(1-|\Phi|^{2})]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}[\tilde{W}((g+p)^{2})-\tilde{W}(g^{2})]\Big\}\,dx.

(2.2)

By the convexity of
W
and
\tilde{W}
, we have

\displaystyle W(1-|\Phi+(\nabla v,p)|^{2})-W(1-|\Phi|^{2})

\displaystyle\geq W^{\prime}(1-|\Phi|^{2})\left(|\Phi|^{2}-|\Phi+(\nabla v,p)|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-W^{\prime}(1-|\Phi|^{2})\left(2\Phi\cdot(\nabla v,p)+|\nabla v|^{2}+p^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\tilde{W}((g+p)^{2})-\tilde{W}(g^{2})

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{W}^{\prime}(g^{2})\left((g+p)^{2}-g^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{W}^{\prime}(g^{2})\left(2gp+p^{2}\right).

Since
\Phi
is a critical point of
E
, we also have

\int_{B^{N}}\Big\{\nabla\Phi:\nabla(\nabla v,p)-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-|\Phi|^{2})\Phi\cdot(\nabla v,p)\\
+\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\tilde{W}^{\prime}(g^{2})gp\Big\}\,dx=0.

Inserting the last two estimates into (
2.2
) we arrive at

\displaystyle E_{\epsilon,\eta}\left[\Phi+(\nabla v,p)\right]-E_{\epsilon,\eta}[\Phi]

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{B^{N}}\left(|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}+|\nabla p|^{2}-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-f^{2}-g^{2})\left(|\nabla v|^{2}+p^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\tilde{W}^{\prime}(g^{2})p^{2}\right)\,dx,

which is precisely the conclusion.
∎

Lemma 7

Let

Lemma 8
.

The first eigenvalue of

is generated by

Proof of Proposition

.

It is enough to prove the estimate for

We denote by

where

. In particular we have

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}}\phi_{k}\phi_{l}\,d\sigma(\theta)=\delta_{kl}\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}}\nabla_{{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}}\phi_{k}\cdot\nabla_{{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}}\phi_{l}\,d\sigma(\theta)=\lambda_{k}\delta_{kl}.

(2.3)

Consider the decomposition of
v
in spherical harmonics: we write

v(r\theta)=\sum_{k\geq 0}v_{k}(r)\phi_{k}(\theta)\text{ for }r\in(0,1),\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}

where

. We have

\nabla v=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(v_{k}^{\prime}\phi_{k}\frac{x}{r}+\frac{1}{r}v_{k}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\phi_{k}\right),\ \Delta v=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(v_{k}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{N-1}{r}v_{k}^{\prime}-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{r^{2}}v_{k}\right)\phi_{k}.

Using the orthogonality relations (
2.3
) and the identities

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-2}v_{k}^{\prime\prime}v_{k}^{\prime}\,dr

\displaystyle=-\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-3}(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}\,dr,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-4}v_{k}^{\prime}v_{k}\,dr

\displaystyle=-\frac{N-4}{2}\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-5}v_{k}^{2}\,dr\text{ for }k\geq 1,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-3}v_{k}^{\prime\prime}v_{k}\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Big[-r^{N-3}(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{(N-3)(N-4)}{2}r^{N-5}v_{k}^{2}\Big]\,dr\text{ for }k\geq 1,

we get

\begin{split}&\int_{B^{N}}(\Delta v)^{2}dx=\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{B^{N}}\left(v_{k}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{N-1}{r}v_{k}^{\prime}-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{r^{2}}v_{k}\right)^{2}\phi_{k}^{2}dx\\
&\quad=\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{0}^{1}\left(r^{N-1}(v_{k}^{\prime\prime})^{2}+(N-1+2\lambda_{k})r^{N-3}(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}+\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{k}+2N-8))r^{N-5}v_{k}^{2}\right)dr,\end{split}

(2.4)

and

\displaystyle\int_{B^{N}}W^{\prime}(1-f^{2}-g^{2})|\nabla v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{0}^{1}W^{\prime}(1-f^{2}-g^{2})\left(r^{N-1}(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}+\lambda_{k}r^{N-3}v_{k}^{2}\right)dr.

Inserting these into (
2.1
), we split
F_{\epsilon,\eta}
into three terms as follows:

\displaystyle F_{\epsilon,\eta}[\nabla v]=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\Big\{\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-1}\left((v_{k}^{\prime\prime})^{2}-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-f^{2}-g^{2})(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}\right)\,dr}_{\text{I}_{k}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{k}r^{N-1}\left(r^{-2}(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-f^{2}-g^{2})r^{-2}v_{k}^{2}\right)\,dr}_{\text{II}_{k}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}\left((N-1+\lambda_{k})r^{N-3}(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}+\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{k}+2N-8)r^{N-5}v_{k}^{2}\right)\,dr}_{\text{III}_{k}}\Big\}.

For terms
\text{I}_{k}
and
\text{II}_{k}
we will apply the Hardy decomposition Lemma
7
using

L_{\epsilon,\eta}f=-\frac{N-1}{r^{2}}f.

More precisely, for any function
w\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
we have the identity

\displaystyle\int_{B^{N}}L_{\epsilon,\eta}(fw)\cdot(fw)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{N}}\big(f^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}+w^{2}L_{\epsilon,\eta}f\cdot f\big)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{N}}f^{2}\left(|\nabla w|^{2}-\frac{N-1}{r^{2}}w^{2}\right)\,dx.

(2.5)

•

Estimate of
\text{I}_{k}
: For the first term we use the decomposition
v_{k}^{\prime}=f\frac{v_{k}^{\prime}}{f}
, i.e.
w=\frac{v_{k}^{\prime}}{f}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B^{N}\setminus\{0\},\mathbb{R})
in (
2.5
):

\text{I}_{k}=\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-1}L_{\epsilon,\eta}(v_{k}^{\prime})\cdot(v_{k}^{\prime})dr=\int_{0}^{1}\Big[r^{N-1}f^{2}\left|\left(\frac{v_{k}^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{\prime}\right|^{2}-(N-1)r^{N-3}(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}\Big]dr.

We let
\zeta(r)=r^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}
so that, when seen as a radial function in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
,
\zeta
verifies

-\nabla\cdot(f^{2}\nabla\zeta)=-f^{2}\Delta\zeta-2ff^{\prime}\zeta^{\prime}=\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4r^{2}}f^{2}\zeta-2ff^{\prime}\zeta^{\prime}\geq\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4r^{2}}f^{2}\zeta,

since
\zeta^{\prime}<0
and
f,f^{\prime}>0
in
(0,1)
. By the Hardy decomposition Lemma
7
for the operator
\nabla\cdot(f^{2}\nabla)
and the decomposition
\frac{v_{k}^{\prime}}{f}=\zeta\frac{v_{k}^{\prime}}{f\zeta}
, we thus have

\begin{split}\text{I}_{k}&\geq\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-1}\left(f^{2}\zeta^{2}\left|\left(\frac{v_{k}^{\prime}}{f\zeta}\right)^{\prime}\right|^{2}+\left(\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}-(N-1)\right)r^{N-3}(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}\right)dr.\end{split}

(2.6)

•

Estimate of
\text{II}_{k}
: First notice the elementary identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-3}(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left(r^{N-1}\Big(\left(\frac{v_{k}}{r}\right)^{\prime}\Big)^{2}+2r^{N-4}v_{k}v_{k}^{\prime}-r^{N-5}v_{k}^{2}\right)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left(r^{N-1}\Big(\left(\frac{v_{k}}{r}\right)^{\prime}\Big)^{2}-(N-3)r^{N-5}v_{k}^{2}\right)dr

so

\text{II}_{k}=\lambda_{k}\int_{0}^{1}\left(r^{N-1}L_{\epsilon,\eta}(\frac{v_{k}}{r})\cdot(\frac{v_{k}}{r})-(N-3)r^{N-5}v_{k}^{2}\right)dr.

This time we use the decomposition
\frac{v_{k}}{r}=f\frac{v_{k}}{rf}
(i.e.
w=\frac{v_{k}}{rf}
in (
2.5
)) to obtain

\text{II}_{k}=\lambda_{k}\int_{0}^{1}\left(r^{N-1}f^{2}\left|\left(\frac{v_{k}}{rf}\right)^{\prime}\right|^{2}-2(N-2)r^{N-5}v_{k}^{2}\right)dr.

By the Hardy decomposition Lemma
7
for the operator
\nabla\cdot(f^{2}\nabla)
and the decomposition
\frac{v_{k}}{rf}=\zeta\frac{v_{k}}{rf\zeta}
as above we get the estimate

\begin{split}\text{II}_{k}&\geq\lambda_{k}\int_{0}^{1}\left(r^{N-1}f^{2}\zeta^{2}\left|\left(\frac{v_{k}}{rf\zeta}\right)^{\prime}\right|^{2}+\left(\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}-2(N-2)\right)r^{N-5}v_{k}^{2}\right)dr.\end{split}

(2.7)

•

Estimate of
\text{III}_{k}
: For the last term we simply apply the Hardy inequality once: for any
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}((0,1),\mathbb{R})
,
\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-3}(v^{\prime})^{2}dr\geq\frac{(N-4)^{2}}{4}\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-5}v^{2}dr
. This gives

\text{III}_{k}\geq\int_{0}^{1}\left((N-1)r^{N-3}(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}+\lambda_{k}\left(\lambda_{k}+2N-8+\frac{(N-4)^{2}}{4}\right)r^{N-5}v_{k}^{2}\right)dr.

(2.8)

Summing the estimates (
2.6
), (
2.7
), (
2.8
) we get

\displaystyle F_{\epsilon,\eta}[\nabla v]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}r^{N-3}(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}+\lambda_{k}\left(\frac{N^{2}}{2}-3N+1+\lambda_{k}\right)r^{N-5}v_{k}^{2}\right)dr

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}r^{N-3}(v_{k}^{\prime})^{2}+\lambda_{k}\left(\frac{N^{2}}{2}-2N\right)r^{N-5}v_{k}^{2}\right)dr\text{ since }\lambda_{k}^{2}\geq(N-1)\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{B^{N}}\frac{(\partial_{r}v)^{2}}{r^{2}}dx+\left(\frac{N^{2}}{2}-2N\right)\int_{B^{N}}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}-(\partial_{r}v)^{2}}{r^{2}}dx.

The result is proved.

3
Symmetrization and second proof of main results in dimension

3.1
A symmetrization of scalar functions

In this section, we consider a spherical average rearrangement which is probably known to the experts. See e.g.
[
52
, Chapter 1, Section 9]
for a similar rearrangement in the context of the Laplace operator. Let
1\leq q<\infty
. For a function
g\in L^{q}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
, define a radial symmetrization
\check{g}
of
g
by

\check{g}(r)=\Big\{\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|g(r\theta)|^{q}\,d\sigma(\theta)\Big\}^{1/q}\geq 0,\quad r\in(0,1).

(3.1)

When
q=2
, we can also think of this as a rearrangement in the spherical harmonic decomposition of
g
.

Theorem 11
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
1\leq q<\infty
,
g\in L^{q}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
and
\check{g}
be associated to
g
by (
3.1
). We have the following conclusions.

(i)

The map
g\mapsto\check{g}
is a
1
-Lipschitz continuous map from
L^{q}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
into itself:

\|\check{g}-\check{h}\|_{L^{q}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})}\leq\|g-h\|_{L^{q}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})}.

Moreover,
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\check{g}(r\theta)|^{q}\,d\sigma(\theta)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|g(r\theta)|^{q}\,d\sigma(\theta)
for a.e.
r\in(0,1)
.

(ii)

Let
G:[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be continuous. If
G
is convex in the second variable, then

\displaystyle\int_{B^{N}}G(r,|\check{g}(x)|^{q})\,dx\leq\int_{B^{N}}G(r,|g(x)|^{q})\,dx.

In particular, for any
q<p<\infty
,

\int_{B^{N}}|\check{g}|^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{B^{N}}|g|^{p}\,dx.

(iii)

Assume in addition that
g\in W^{1,q}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
. Then
\check{g}\in W^{1,q}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
and

\int_{B^{N}}|\nabla\check{g}|^{q}\,dx\leq\int_{B^{N}}|\nabla g|^{q}\,dx.

(3.2)

Equality is attained if and only if
g
is radially symmetric and
|g|=\check{g}
in
(0,1)
.

Proof.

Proof of (i):
From the definition of the radial function
\check{g}(x)=\check{g}(r)
we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\check{g}(r\theta)|^{q}\,d\sigma(\theta)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|g(r\theta)|^{q}d\sigma(\theta)\text{ for a.e. }r\in(0,1),

which implies
\check{g}\in L^{q}(B^{N})
. Also, by the reverse triangle inequality, we have for
g,h\in L^{q}(B^{N})
that

\displaystyle\|\check{g}-\check{h}\|_{L^{q}(B^{N})}^{q}

\displaystyle=|{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\int_{0}^{1}|\check{g}(r)-\check{h}(r)|^{q}\,r^{N-1}\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\big|\|g(r\cdot)\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{N-1}})}-\|h(r\cdot)\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{N-1}})}\big|^{q}\,r^{N-1}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|g(r\cdot)-h(r\cdot)\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{N-1}})}^{q}\,r^{N-1}\,dr=\|g-h\|_{L^{q}(B^{N})}^{q}.

Therefore
g\mapsto\check{g}
is a
1
-Lipschitz continuous map on
L^{q}(B^{N})
.

Proof of (ii):
By Jensen inequality,

\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}G(r,|\check{g}(r\theta)|^{q})\,d\sigma(\theta)=G\left(r,\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\check{g}(r\theta)|^{q}\,d\sigma(\theta)\right)\\
=G\left(r,\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|g(r\theta)|^{q}\,d\sigma(\theta)\right)\leq\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}G(r,|g(r\theta)|^{q})\,d\sigma(\theta).

Integrating in
r
gives the second bullet point. In particular, with
G(r,s)=s^{p/q}
with
p>q
, we see that the
L^{p}
-norm of
\check{g}
is no more than that of
g
.

Proof of (iii):
Consider first the case
g
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\bar{B}^{N})
, which is a dense subset of
W^{1,q}(B^{N})
. For technical reasons, we introduce, for
\mu>0
,

\check{g}_{\mu}(r)=\Big\{\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}(g(r\theta)^{2}+\mu)^{q/2}\,d\sigma(\theta)\Big\}^{1/q}\geq\mu^{1/2},\quad r\in(0,1).

Note that
\check{g}_{\mu}\rightarrow\check{g}
in
L^{q}(B^{N})
as
\mu\rightarrow 0
. We have, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|\check{g}_{\mu}(r)|^{q-1}|\check{g}_{\mu}^{\prime}(r)|

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(g(r\theta)^{2}+\mu)^{(q-1)/2}|\partial_{r}g(r\theta)|\,d\sigma(\theta)

\displaystyle\leq|\check{g}_{\mu}(r)|^{q-1}\Big\{\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\partial_{r}g(r\theta)|^{q}\,d\sigma(\theta)\Big\}^{1/q}.

As
\check{g}_{\mu}\geq\mu^{1/2}>0
, this implies

\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\nabla\check{g}_{\mu}(r\theta)|^{q}\,d\sigma(\theta)=|\check{g}_{\mu}^{\prime}(r)|^{q}\leq\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\partial_{r}g(r\theta)|^{q}\,d\sigma(\theta).

Integrating over
r\in(0,1)
gives

\int_{B^{N}}|\nabla\check{g}_{\mu}|^{q}\,dx\leq\int_{B^{N}}|\partial_{r}g|^{q}dx.

This implies
\check{g}_{\mu}
is bounded in
W^{1,q}(B^{N})
and hence converges weakly to
\check{g}
in
W^{1,q}(B^{N})
as
\mu\rightarrow 0
. Hence

\int_{B^{N}}|\nabla\check{g}|^{q}\,dx\leq\int_{B^{N}}|\partial_{r}g|^{q}dx,

(3.3)

which proves (
3.2
) for
g\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\bar{B}^{N})
.

Suppose now
g\in W^{1,q}(B^{N})
. Pick
\{g_{(j)}\}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\bar{B}^{N})
such that
g_{(j)}\rightarrow g
in
W^{1,q}(B^{N})
. By (i),
\check{g}_{(j)}\rightarrow\check{g}
in
L^{q}(B^{N})
. Also, by (
3.3
),

\int_{B^{N}}|\nabla\check{g}_{(j)}|^{q}\,dx\leq\int_{B^{N}}|\partial_{r}g_{(j)}|^{q}dx.

(3.4)

This implies that
\check{g}_{(j)}
is bounded in
W^{1,q}(B^{N})
and hence converges weakly in
W^{1,q}(B^{N})
to
\check{g}
. Sending
j\rightarrow\infty
we see that (
3.3
) remains valid for
g\in W^{1,q}(B^{N})
, which proves (
3.2
). Moreover, equality holds in (
3.2
) if and only if
|\nabla g|=|\partial_{r}g|
a.e., i.e.
g
is radially symmetric.
∎

3.2
A symmetrization of gradient fields and proof of Theorem
5

Recall the symmetrization
\check{v}
for a function
v\in H^{1}(B^{N},\mathbb{R})
is given by the formula (
1.17
):

\check{v}(r)=-\int_{r}^{1}\Big\{\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\nabla v(s\theta)|^{2}d\sigma(\theta)\Big\}^{1/2}\,ds\leq 0,\quad r\in(0,1).

We will use the following density result.

Lemma 12
.

For
N\geq 2
, the set
\mathcal{S}
of functions in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\bar{B}^{N})
which are constant in a neighborhood of the origin is dense in
H^{2}(B^{N})
. Moreover, if
v\in H^{2}(B^{N})
verifies
\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}}v(r\theta)\theta d\sigma(\theta)=0
for almost every
r\in(0,1)
, then its approximation sequence in
\mathcal{S}
may be chosen with the same property.

Proof.

It is well known that
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\bar{B}^{N})
is dense in
H^{2}(B^{N})
. Thus, to show that
\mathcal{S}
is dense in
H^{2}(B^{N})
, we only need to show that a given
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\bar{B}^{N})
can be approximated by a sequence of functions in
\mathcal{S}
. In the proof,
C
denotes a constant that can change between lines but depends only on the dimension
N
. Pick a cut-off function
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\varphi\equiv 1
in
(-\infty,1/2]
,
\varphi\equiv 0
in
[1,\infty)
. For
j\geq 10
and
x\in B^{N}
, let

\varphi_{(j)}(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi(j|x|)&\text{ if }N\geq 3,\\
1-\varphi(\frac{\ln\ln\frac{1}{|x|}}{2\ln\ln j})&\text{ if }N=2.\end{cases}

Note that
\varphi_{(j)}(x)=0
for
|x|\geq\frac{1}{j}
and
\varphi_{(j)}(x)=1
when
|x|
is small enough. Define

v_{(j)}=v(0)\varphi_{(j)}+v(1-\varphi_{(j)})=v-(v-v_{0})\varphi_{(j)}\in\mathcal{S},\quad j\geq 1.

We estimate

\displaystyle|v(x)-v(0)|\leq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{N})}|x|,

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{(j)}\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}\leq Cj^{-N/2},

\displaystyle\|\nabla\varphi_{(j)}\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}+\|r\nabla^{2}\varphi_{(j)}\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}\leq C\omega_{N}(j)\text{ with }\omega_{N}(j)=\begin{cases}Cj^{-(N-2)/2}&\text{ if }N\geq 3,\\
\frac{C}{(\ln j\ln\ln j)^{1/2}}&\text{ if }N=2.\end{cases}

We thus have

\displaystyle\|(v-v(0))\varphi_{(j)}\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq Cj^{-N/2}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{N})},

\displaystyle\|\nabla[(v-v(0))\varphi_{(j)}]\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq Cj^{-N/2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{N})}+C\omega_{N}(j)\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{N})},

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}[(v-v(0))\varphi_{(j)}]\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq Cj^{-N/2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{N})}+C\omega_{N}(j)\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{N})}.

Clearly, these estimates imply that
v_{(j)}\rightarrow v
in
H^{2}(B^{N})
. We have proved that
\mathcal{S}
is dense in
H^{2}(B^{N})

Now suppose
v\in H^{2}(B^{N})
and
\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}}v(r\theta)\theta d\sigma(\theta)=0
. Let
v_{(j)}\in\mathcal{S}
be such that
v_{(j)}\rightarrow v
in
H^{2}(B^{N})
. Define
\tilde{v}_{(j)}(r\theta)=v_{(j)}(r\theta)-\sum_{k=1}^{N}v_{(j),k}(r)\phi_{k}(\theta)
where
v_{(j),k}(r)=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}}v_{(j)}(r\theta)\phi_{k}(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)
. It is clear that
\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}}\tilde{v}_{(j)}(r\theta)\theta d\sigma(\theta)=0
. Since
v_{(j)}
is constant near
0
,
v_{(j),k}
is supported away from
0
, and so
\tilde{v}_{(j)}\in\mathcal{S}
. Finally, since the map
w\in H^{2}(B^{N})\mapsto(r\theta\mapsto w_{k}(r)\phi_{k}(\theta))
is continuous in
H^{2}(B^{N})
and
v_{k}\equiv 0
for
k=1,\ldots,N
we have

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|\tilde{v}_{(j)}-v\|_{H^{2}(B^{N})}\leq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|v_{(j)}-v\|_{H^{2}(B^{N})}+\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}v_{(j),k}(r)\phi_{k}(\theta)\Big\|_{H^{2}(B^{N})}=0.

The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Proof of (i):

Proof of (ii):

Proof of (iii):

(3.5)

3.4
Symmetry for solutions to a nonlinear eigenvalue problem

For
d>0
,
1\leq p<2
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the energy functional

J[v]=\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|v\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}^{2}

on the set

S_{p,d}=\big\{v\in H^{2}_{0}(B^{N}):\|v\|_{L^{p}(B^{N})}=d\big\}.

Let
\lambda_{1}(\Delta^{2})
denote the first eigenvalue of the bi-Laplacian in
H_{0}^{2}(B^{N})
. When
\lambda<\lambda_{1}(\Delta^{2})
, after adjusting by a scaling factor to remove the Lagrange multiplier, minimizers of
J
on
S_{p,d}
correspond to solutions of the elliptic problem (
1.20
):

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}v=\lambda v+|v|^{p-2}v\text{ on }B^{N},\\
v=\partial_{r}v=0\text{ on }\partial B^{N}.\end{cases}

(For
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}(\Delta^{2})
, the partial differential equation is different, namely

\Delta^{2}v=\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}(\Delta^{2})v&\text{ if }\lambda=\lambda_{1}(\Delta^{2}),\\
\lambda v-|v|^{p-2}v&\text{ if }\lambda>\lambda_{1}(\Delta^{2}),\end{cases}

and we do not consider these cases here for simplicity.)

Problem (
1.20
) has been studied by many authors and a summary of known results would go beyond the scope of the present paper. We refer the reader to e.g.
[
4
,
7
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
36
]
and the references therein.

We prove:

Corollary 13
.

Let
N\geq 5
and
1\leq p<2
. For
\lambda<\lambda_{1}(\Delta^{2})
, minimizers of
J
over
S_{p,d}
are radially symmetric, do not change sign and are either radially non-decreasing or radially non-increasing.

Proof.

Note that as
\lambda<\lambda_{1}(\Delta^{2})
,
J
is coercive on
H_{0}^{2}(B^{N})
. By the compactness embedding theorem,
J
has a minimizer over
S_{p,d}
.

Let
v
be a minimizer of
J
over
S_{p,d}
; in particular,
J[v]\geq 0
. By Theorem
5
, we have

\begin{cases}\|\Delta\check{v}\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}\leq\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})},\\
\|\check{v}\|_{L^{p}(B^{N})}\geq\|v\|_{L^{p}(B^{N})}=d,\\
\|\check{v}\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}\geq\|v\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}.\end{cases}

(3.12)

Let

\bar{v}=\mu\check{v}\text{ where }\mu=d\|\check{v}\|_{L^{p}(B^{N})}^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Eq:C6vv}}}{{\leq}}1

so that
\bar{v}\in S_{p,d}
. We compute, keeping in mind that
\mu\leq 1
,

J[\bar{v}]=\mu^{2}J[\check{v}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Eq:C6vv}}}{{\leq}}\mu^{2}J[v]\leq J[v],

where for the last inequality we use the fact that
J[v]\geq 0
. It follows that
\bar{v}
is also a minimizer of
J
over
S_{p,d}
, which in turn implies
J[\check{v}]=J[v]
and all inequalities in (
3.12
) are saturated. Appealing to the equality case in Theorem
5
(iv), we see that
v
is radially symmetric and
\check{v}^{\prime}=|v^{\prime}|
.

It remains to prove that
v
and
\partial_{r}v
do not change sign. Indeed, we have

|v(r)|=|v(r)-v(1)|=\Big|\int_{r}^{1}v^{\prime}(s)\,ds\Big|\leq\int_{r}^{1}|v^{\prime}(s)|\,ds=\int_{r}^{1}\check{v}^{\prime}(s)\,ds=|\check{v}(r)|.

As
\|\check{v}\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}=\|v\|_{L^{2}(B^{N})}
, it follows that equality is attained in the above inequality, i.e.
v^{\prime}
does not change sign. As
v(1)=0
, it follows also that
v
does not change sign.
∎

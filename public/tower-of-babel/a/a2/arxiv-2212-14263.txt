Abstract
. In this paper, we define and study absolute matrix order ideals in absolute matrix order unit spaces. As an application of absolute matrix order unit property, we construct some kinds of absolute matrix order ideals in absolute matrix order unit spaces. Later, we show that the Grothendieck group of a such kind of absolute matrix order ideal for order projections is a subgroup of Grothendieck group of corresponding absolute matrix order unit space for order projections.

\mathbb{A}

C^{*}

a\in\mathbb{B}
for all
a\in\mathbb{A}^{+}
and
b\in\mathbb{B}^{+}
such that
a\leq b.
This concept helped a lot in the study of certain notions in a
C^{*}
-algebra. For example, the notions of minimal, maximal, purely infinite and central projections in

A tiny version of the notion of Hereditary sub-
C^{*}

\mathbb{X}

\mathbb{Y}

\mathbb{X}

x\in\mathbb{Y}
for all
x\in\mathbb{X}^{+}

y\in\mathbb{Y}^{+}
such that
x\leq y.
Since the self adjoint part

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
\mathbb{X}
stands for a complex vector space and
\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X})
for the set of all the
l\times m
matrices over
\mathbb{X}.
Then
\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X})
becomes a vector space over
\mathbb{C}
under the operations entry-wise addition and entry-wise scalar multiplication. If
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{C},
then we write
\mathbb{M}_{m,n}
for
\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{C}).
The zero element in
\mathbb{M}_{m,n}
is denoted by
0^{l,m}
and for
l=m,
the zero element in
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})
is denoted by
0^{l}.
Sometimes, we drop the subscripts and write zero element in
\mathbb{M}_{l,m}
by
0
only. In fact,
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})
is
\mathbb{M}_{l}
-bimodule under the following matrix multiplications: for
x=[x_{i,j}]\in\mathbb{M}_{m,n}(\mathbb{X}),
we have
\varsigma x=\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{m}\varsigma_{i,l}x_{l,j}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{l,n}(\mathbb{X})
if
\varsigma=[\varsigma_{i,j}]\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}
and
x\varsigma=\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}x_{i,l}\varsigma_{l,j}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{m,s}(\mathbb{X})
if
\varsigma\in\mathbb{M}_{n,s}.
Given
x\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X}),y\in\mathbb{M}_{n,s}(\mathbb{X}),
we write:

x\circledast y=\begin{bmatrix}x&0\\
0&y\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{l+n,m+s}(\mathbb{X}).

We also write:
x^{l}=x\circledast x\circledast\cdots\circledast x\in\mathbb{M}_{lm}(\mathbb{X})
for every
x\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{X}).

For origin of such matricial notions, we refer to see
[
35
]
.

We start with the definition of absolutely matrix ordered spaces, a matricial version of absolutely ordered spaces. Absolutely ordered spaces were introduced and studied by Karn in
[
23
]
. It is very near to a lattice structure.

Definition 2.1

(
[
24
]
, Definition 4.1)
.

Let
(\mathbb{X},\{\ \mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}\})
be a matrix ordered space and also let
|\cdot|_{l,m}:\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X})\to\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
be functions for each pair
l,m\in\mathbb{N}
. For
l=m,
we write
|\cdot|_{l,m}=|\cdot|_{l}.
Then
\left(\mathbb{X},\{\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{X})^{+}\},\{|\cdot|_{l,m}\}\right)
is said to be an
absolutely matrix ordered space
, if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

(\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{X})_{sa},\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{X})^{+},|\cdot|_{m})
is an absolutely ordered space for every
m\in\mathbb{N}.

(2)

For all
x\in\mathbb{M}_{m,n}(\mathbb{X}),y\in\mathbb{M}_{l,s}(\mathbb{X}),\varsigma_{1}\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}
and
\varsigma_{2}\in\mathbb{M}_{n,s},
we have

(a)

|\varsigma_{1}x\varsigma_{2}|_{l,s}\leq\|\varsigma_{1}\|||x|_{m,n}\varsigma_{2}|_{n,s}.

(b)

|x\circledast y|_{m+l,n+s}=|x|_{m,n}\circledast|y|_{l,s}.

Example 2.2

(
[
24
]
, Example 4.4)
.

Given a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathbb{A},
we know that
\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{A})
is also a
C^{*}
-algebra. Next, let
\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{A})^{+}
be the set of all the positive elements contained in
\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{A}).
Then
(\mathbb{A},\{\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{A})^{+}\})
becomes a matrix ordered space and
(\mathbb{A},\{\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{A})^{+}\},\{|\cdot|_{l,m}\})
becomes an absolutely matrix ordered space, where each
|\cdot|_{l,m}:\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{A})\to\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{A})^{+}
is defined by
|x|_{l,m}=\sqrt{x^{*}x}
for
x\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{A}).

The following result describes some properties of absolutely matrix ordered spaces.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
24
]
, Proposition 4.2)
.

In every absolutely matrix ordered space
(\mathbb{X},\{\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{X})^{+}\},\{|\cdot|_{l,m}\}),

and

with

(1)

|\varsigma x|_{l,n}=|x|_{m,n}.

(2)

\left|\begin{bmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right|_{m+n}=|x^{*}|_{n,m}\circledast|x|_{m,n}.

(3)

\begin{bmatrix}|x^{*}|_{n,m}&x\\
x^{*}&|x|_{m,n}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{m+n}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.

(4)

|x|_{m,n}=\left|\begin{bmatrix}x\\
0\end{bmatrix}\right|_{m+l,n}
and
|x|_{m,n}\circledast 0_{s}=\left|\begin{bmatrix}x&0\end{bmatrix}\right|_{m,n+s}.

(5)

(
[
25
]
, Lemma 3.1
).
Moreover, if
l=m=n
(in this case,
\varsigma
is unitary), we also have:
|\varsigma^{*}x\varsigma|_{n}=\varsigma^{*}|x|_{n}\varsigma.

Now, we recall the definitions of order unit property and absolute order unit propertie introduced and studied by Karn in
[
23
]
.

Definition 2.6

(
[
23
]
, Definition 5.1)
.

Let
(\mathbb{X},e)
be an absolute matrix order unit space and
x\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
. We write:
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}^{x}=\{y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}:\epsilon x\pm y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}~\textrm{for some $\epsilon>0$}~\}.
Then
x\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
is said to have the
order unit property
in
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}
provided
\pm y\leq\|y\|x
for every
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}^{x}.
Moreover,
x
is said to have the
absolute order unit property
in
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}
provided
|y|\leq\|y\|x
for every
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}^{x}.

The sets of all the elements having order unit property and absolute order unit property in
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}
are denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})
and
|\mathcal{M}|_{l}(\mathbb{X})
respectively. For
l=1,
we write:
\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{X})=\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{X})
and
|\mathcal{M}|_{1}(\mathbb{X})=|\mathcal{M}|(\mathbb{X}).

We explain the notions of order projections and partial isometries in absolute matrix order unit spaces. These notions are generalizations of the notions of projections and partial isometries in a

-algebra respectively .

Definition 2.7

(
[
25
]
, Definition 3.1)
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be an absolute matrix order unit space. We say that
x\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{X})
is an
order projection
provided
x\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}
with
|2x-e^{n}|_{n}=e^{n}.
Next, if
x\in\mathbb{M}_{m,n}(\mathbb{X}),
then we say that
x
is a
partial isometry
provided
|x|_{m,n}\in\mathbb{OP}_{n}(\mathbb{X})
and
|x^{*}|_{n,m}\in\mathbb{OP}_{m}(\mathbb{X}).

The notations
\mathbb{OP}_{n}(\mathbb{X})
and
\mathbb{PI}_{m,n}(\mathbb{X})
stand for the sets of all the order projections and all the partial isometries in
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{X})
and
\mathbb{M}_{m,n}(\mathbb{X})
respectively. We write:
\mathbb{PI}_{m,n}(\mathbb{X})=\mathbb{PI}_{n}(\mathbb{X})
for

We also write:
\mathbb{OP}_{1}(\mathbb{X})=\mathbb{OP}(\mathbb{X})
and
\mathbb{PI}_{1}(\mathbb{X})=\mathbb{PI}(\mathbb{X}).

Next result recalls an elementary property of order projections.

Lemma 2.8
.

[
25

In an absolute matrix order unit space
\mathbb{X},
we have:
p\circledast q\in\mathbb{OP}_{l+m}(\mathbb{X})

p\in\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X})
and

The following result provides a characterization of order projections in terms of order unit property and absolute order unit property.

Proposition 2.9
.

[

In an absolute matrix order unit space
(\mathbb{X},e),
let
x\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}
such that
0\leq x\leq e^{l}.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

x\in\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X}).

(2)

x
and
e-x\in\mathcal{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}).

(3)

x
and
e-x\in|\mathcal{M}|_{l}(\mathbb{X}).

3.
Order ideals in absolute matrix order unit spaces

In this section, we recall matrix order unit property and define absolute matrix order unit property. These are matricial versions of order unit property and absolute order unit property which were introduced by Karn in
[
23
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

[
27
]

Given
x\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
in an absolute matrix order unit space
\mathbb{X},
we say that
x
has the matrix order unit property in
\mathbb{X}
provided
x^{l}
has the order unit property in
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}
for any
l\in\mathbb{N}.
Further, if
x^{l}
has the absolute order unit property in
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}
for any
l\in\mathbb{N},
we say that
x
has the absolute matrix order unit property in
\mathbb{X}.

The sets of all the elements having matrix order unit property and absolute matrix order unit property are denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
and
|\mathcal{M}|_{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
respectively.

The following result elaborates some properties enjoyed by matrix order and absolute matrix order unit properties.

Proposition 3.2
.

Given
x\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
in an absolute matrix order unit space
(\mathbb{X},e),
the following statements hold:

(1)

x\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
if and only if, we have
\begin{bmatrix}\|y\|_{l}x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\|y\|_{l}x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
whenever
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\epsilon x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
for some
l\in\mathbb{N},\epsilon>0
and
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}).

(2)

x\in|\mathcal{M}|_{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
if and only if, we have
\|y\|_{l}x^{l}-|y|_{l}~\textrm{and}~\|y\|_{l}x^{l}-|y^{*}|_{l}\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
whenever
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\epsilon x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
for some
l\in\mathbb{N},\epsilon>0
and
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}).

Moreover, for
\|x\|\leq 1,
we also have:

(3)

x
and
e-x\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{X})
if and only if
x
and
e-x\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}(\mathbb{X}).

(4)

x
and
e-x\in|\mathcal{M}|(\mathbb{X})
if and only if
x
and
e-x\in|\mathcal{M}|_{\infty}(\mathbb{X}).

Proof.

(1)

Let
x\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}(\mathbb{X}).
Assume that for
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}),
there exists
\epsilon>0
satisfying
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\epsilon x^{n}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}~\textrm{for some}~\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
\epsilon>0.
In this case, we also have
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{n}&-y\\
-y^{*}&\epsilon x^{n}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-\mathbb{I}_{l}&0\\
0&\mathbb{I}_{l}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\epsilon x^{n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-\mathbb{I}_{l}&0\\
0&\mathbb{I}_{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.
Since
x^{2l}\in\mathcal{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})
, we get that
\pm\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\leq\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2l}\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{l}\end{bmatrix}=\|y\|_{l}\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{l}\end{bmatrix}.
Thus
\begin{bmatrix}\|y\|_{l}x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\|y\|_{l}x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.

Next, we prove the converse part. For that let
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}
satisfying
\pm y\leq\epsilon x^{l}~\textrm{for some}~\epsilon>0.
Then
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y&\epsilon x^{l}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{l}\\
\mathbb{I}_{l}\end{bmatrix}(\epsilon x^{l}+y)\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{l}&\mathbb{I}_{l}\end{bmatrix}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{l}\\
-\mathbb{I}_{l}\end{bmatrix}(\epsilon x^{n}-y)\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{l}&-\mathbb{I}_{l}\end{bmatrix}\right)\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.
By assumption, we conclude that
\begin{bmatrix}\|y\|_{l}x^{l}&y\\
y&\|y\|_{l}x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.
In this case, we also conclude that

\|y\|x^{l}\pm y=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{l}&\pm\mathbb{I}_{l}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\|y\|_{l}x^{l}&y\\
y&\|y\|_{l}x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{l}\\
\pm\mathbb{I}_{l}\end{bmatrix}\right)\in\mathbb{X}^{+}.

Thus
x^{l}\in\mathcal{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})
for every
l\in\mathbb{N}.
Hence
x\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}(\mathbb{X}).

(2)

Let
x\in|\mathcal{M}|_{\infty}(\mathbb{X}).
Also, let
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})
such that
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\epsilon x^{n}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
for some
\epsilon>0.
Then, we also have
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{n}&-y\\
-y^{*}&\epsilon x^{n}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.
Since
x^{2l}\in|\mathcal{M}|_{2l}(\mathbb{X})
, we get that
\begin{bmatrix}|y^{*}|_{l}&0\\
0&|y|_{l}\end{bmatrix}=\left|\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right|_{2l}\leq\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2l}\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{l}\end{bmatrix}=\|y\|_{l}\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{l}\end{bmatrix}.
Thus
\begin{bmatrix}\|y\|_{l}x^{l}-|y^{*}|_{l}&0\\
0&\|y\|_{l}x^{l}-|y|_{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
so that

\|y\|_{l}x^{l}-|y^{*}|_{l}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{l}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\|y\|_{l}x^{l}-|y^{*}|_{l}&0\\
0&\|y\|_{l}x^{l}-|y|_{l}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{l}\\
0\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}

and

\|y\|_{l}x^{l}-|y|_{l}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathbb{I}_{l}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\|y\|_{l}x^{l}-|y^{*}|_{l}&0\\
0&\|y\|_{l}x^{l}-|y|_{l}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\mathbb{I}_{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.

Now, let’s prove the converse part. Let
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}
such that
\pm y\leq\epsilon x^{l}~\textrm{for some}~\epsilon>0.
Then
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y&\epsilon x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.
By assumption, we get that
\|y\|_{l}x^{l}-|y|_{l}\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.
Thus
x^{l}\in|\mathcal{M}|_{l}(\mathbb{X})
for every
l\in\mathbb{N}.
Hence
x\in|\mathcal{M}|_{\infty}(\mathbb{X}).

(3)

Suppose that
\|x\|\leq e.
In this case, we have
0\leq x\leq e
and consequently
0\leq e-x\leq e.
By Lemma
2.8
and Proposition
2.9
,
x
and
e-x\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{X})
if and only if
x\in\mathbb{OP}(\mathbb{X})
if and only if
x^{l}\in\mathbb{OP}(\mathbb{X})
if and only if
x^{l}
and
e^{l}-x^{l}\in\mathcal{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})
for every
l\in\mathbb{N}.
Thus
x
and
e-x\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{X})
if and only if
x
and
e-x\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}(\mathbb{X}).

Now, using Lemma
2.8
and Proposition
2.9
, and repeating the proof of (3), we can also prove (4).
∎

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\left(\mathbb{X},\{\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{X})^{+}\},\{|\cdot|_{l,m}\}\right)
be an absolutely matrix ordered space and
\mathbb{Y}
be a subspace of
\mathbb{X}.
For each
l\in\mathbb{N},
we write:
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{Y})_{sa}=\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}\cap\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{Y})~\textrm{and}~\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{Y})^{+}=\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}\cap\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{Y}).
Then
\mathbb{Y}
is said to be absolute matrix order ideal of
\mathbb{X},
if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

(\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{Y})_{sa},\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{Y})^{+})
is an order ideal of
(\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa},\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+})
for each
l\in\mathbb{N}.

(2)

|\cdot|_{l,m}:\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{Y})\to\mathbb{M}(\mathbb{Y})_{m}^{+}
is well defined for each pair of
l,m\in\mathbb{N}.
In other words,
\left(\mathbb{Y},\{\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{Y})^{+}\},\{|\cdot|_{l,m}\}\right)
is itself an absolutely matrix ordered space.

Further, let
(\mathbb{X},e)
be an absolute matrix order unit space. Then
\mathbb{Y}
is said to be absolute matrix order unit ideal, if along with condition (1), the following additional condition is also satisfied:

(3)

(\mathbb{Y},\{\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{Y})^{+}\},\{|\cdot|_{l,m}\},y)
is an absolute matrix order unit space such that
\|\cdot\|_{m}^{y}=\|\cdot\|_{m}
on
\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{Y}),
where
\|\cdot\|_{m}~\textrm{and}~\|\cdot\|_{m}^{y}
are the matrix norms determined by the order units
e
and
y
respectively for each
m\in\mathbb{N}.

Example 3.4
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra with unity element
1
and
p
be a non-zero projection in
\mathbb{A}.
Put
\mathbb{A}_{p}=\{pap:a\in\mathbb{A}\}.
Then
(\mathbb{A}_{p},p)
is an absolute matrix order unit ideal of
\mathbb{A}.
To see this, we verify only order ideal condition as other conditions are routine to verify
(
we refer to see
[
18
]
).
Now, let
a,b\in\mathbb{A}
be such that

Then
(1-p)b(1-p)=0
so that
\|\sqrt{b}(1-p)\|^{2}=0.
In this case,
\sqrt{b}(1-p)=0
and we get that

and

Thus
b=pbp\in\mathbb{A}_{p}.

Next result generalize the last example in absolute matrix order unit spaces for more general elements.

Theorem 3.5
.

In an absolute matrix order unit space
\mathbb{X},
let
x\in|\mathcal{M}|_{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
such that
\|x\|=1.
We write:

\mathbb{X}_{x}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{X}:\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x&y\\
y^{*}&\epsilon x\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{X})^{+}~\textrm{for some}~\epsilon>0\right\}

and

\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X})_{x^{l},x^{m}}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X}):\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\epsilon x^{m}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{l+m}(\mathbb{X})^{+}~\textrm{for some}~\epsilon>0\right\}.

For
l=m,
write:
\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X})_{x^{l},x^{m}}=\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{x^{l}}.
The following statements are true:

(1)

\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X}_{x})=\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X})_{x^{l},x^{m}}
.

(2)

\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x})^{+}=\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x})\bigcap\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
forms a proper cone.

(3)

x
forms an order unit for
\mathbb{X}_{x}.

(4)

For every
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x}),
we write:

\|y\|_{l}^{x}=\inf\left\{\epsilon>0:\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\epsilon x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}\right\}.

It turns out that
\|\cdot\|_{l}^{x}=\|\cdot\|_{l}
on
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x})
for every
l\in\mathbb{N}.

(5)

|y|_{l,m}\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{X}_{x})^{+}
for every
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X}_{x}).

In this case,
(\mathbb{X}_{x},\{\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{X}_{x})^{+}\},\{|\cdot|_{l,m}\},x)
forms
an absolute matrix order unit ideal of
\mathbb{X}.

Proof.

It is routine to verify (2). Now, let’s prove the other statements.

(1)

Let
y=[y_{i,j}]\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X}_{x}).
There exists
\epsilon_{i,j}>0
satisfying
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon_{i,j}x&y_{i,j}\\
y_{i,j}^{*}&\epsilon_{i,j}x\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
for every
y_{i,j}\in\mathbb{X}_{p}.
Put
\epsilon_{0}=\max\{\epsilon_{i,j}:1\leq i\leq l,1\leq j\leq m\}.
Then
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon_{0}x&y_{i,j}\\
y_{i,j}^{*}&\epsilon_{0}x\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
for every pair of
i
and
j.

Next, let
\varsigma_{i,j}\in\mathbb{M}_{l+m,2}
such that

\varsigma_{i,j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{at (i,1) and (l+j,2)}\\
0&\text{elsewhere}.\end{cases}

so that

\begin{bmatrix}m\epsilon_{0}x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&l\epsilon_{0}x^{m}\end{bmatrix}=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\varsigma_{i,j}\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon_{0}x&y_{i,j}\\
y_{i,j}^{*}&\epsilon_{0}x\end{bmatrix}\varsigma_{ij}^{*}\in\mathbb{M}_{l+m}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.

For
\epsilon=\max\{m,l\}\epsilon_{0},
we get that

\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\epsilon x^{m}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{l+m}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.

Thus
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X})_{x^{l},x^{m}}.

Now, we prove the converse part. For that let
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X})_{x^{l},x^{m}}.
There exists
\epsilon>0
satisfying
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\epsilon x^{m}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{l+m}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.
Then

\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x&y_{i,j}\\
y_{i,j}^{*}&\epsilon x\end{bmatrix}=\varsigma_{i,j}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\epsilon x^{m}\end{bmatrix}\varsigma_{i,j}\in\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.

Thus
y_{i,j}\in\mathbb{X}_{x}
for every
i,j
and consequently
\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X}_{x})=\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X})_{x^{l},x^{m}}.

(3)

By (1), for every
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x}),
there exists
\epsilon>0
satisfying
\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\epsilon x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.
Thus
x
forms an order unit for
\mathbb{X}_{x}.

(4)

Let
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}).
Since
x\in|\mathcal{M}|_{\infty}(\mathbb{X}),
we have

\pm\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\leq\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2l}\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{l}\end{bmatrix}=\|y\|_{l}\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{l}\end{bmatrix}.

Thus
\begin{bmatrix}\|y\|_{l}x^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\|y\|_{l}x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
so that
\|y\|_{l}^{x}\leq\|y\|_{l}.

Now,
x\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
with
\|x\|=1.
Then
\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
and
\left\|\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\right\|=1
so that
\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{l}\end{bmatrix}\leq\begin{bmatrix}e^{l}&0\\
0&e^{l}\end{bmatrix}.
As
x
is order unit for
\mathbb{X}_{x},
we have

\pm\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\leq\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{2l}^{x}\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{l}\end{bmatrix}=\|y\|_{l}^{x}\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{l}\end{bmatrix}

and consequently

\pm\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\leq\|y\|_{l}^{x}\begin{bmatrix}e^{l}&0\\
0&e^{l}\end{bmatrix}.

Thus
\begin{bmatrix}\|y\|_{l}^{x}e^{l}&y\\
y^{*}&\|y\|_{l}^{x}e^{l}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{2l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
so that
\|y\|_{l}\leq\|y\|_{l}^{x}.
Hence
\|y\|_{l}^{x}=\|y\|_{l}
for every
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x}).

(5)

Let
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X}_{x}).
For
x\in|\mathcal{M}|_{\infty}(\mathbb{X})
and
\epsilon=\left\|\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{m+n},
we have

\begin{bmatrix}|y^{*}|_{m,l}&0\\
0&|y|_{l,m}\end{bmatrix}=\left|\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right|_{l+m}\leq\epsilon\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{m}\end{bmatrix}.

Then
|y|_{l,m}\leq\epsilon x^{m}
so that
|y|_{l,m}\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{X}_{x})^{+}.
Thus, the maps
|\cdot|_{l,m}:\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X}_{x})\to\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathbb{X}_{x})^{+}
are well defined.

It is routine to verify
\perp=\perp_{\infty}^{a}
on
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x})^{+}
for every
l\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence
(\mathbb{X}_{x},\{\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x})^{+}\},x,\{|\cdot|_{l,m}\})
forms an absolute matrix order unit ideal of
\mathbb{X}.

∎

The following result shows that matrix norm in absolute matrix order unit spaces takes two discrete values
0
and
1
whenever it is restricted to the order projections.

Proposition 3.6
.

In an absolute matrix order unit space
\mathbb{X},
let
p\in\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X})
such that
p\neq 0.
Then
\|p\|_{l}=1.

Proof.

Let
p\in\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X}).
Then
0\leq p\leq e^{l}
so that
\|p\|_{l}\leq\max\{\|0\|_{l},\|e^{l}\|_{l}\}=\|e^{l}\|_{l}=1.
Next, by Proposition
2.9
, we also have
0\leq p\leq\|p\|_{l}p.
Thus
\|p\|_{l}\leq\max\{\|0\|_{l},\|\|p\|_{l}p\|_{l}\}=\|p\|_{l}^{2}
and consequently
1\leq\|p\|_{l}
for
p\neq 0.
Finally, we conclude that
\|p\|_{l}=1.

∎

The notion of absolute and completely absolute value presrving maps have been introduced and studied in
[
24
]
. The following result tells that kernels of completely absolute value preserving maps are absolute matrix order ideals.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
be absolutely matrix ordered spaces and
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
be a completely absolute value preserving map. Put
Ker(\varphi)=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:\varphi(x)=0\}.
Then the following statements are true:

(1)

\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(Ker(\varphi))=Ker(\varphi_{l,m})
for every pair
l,m\in\mathbb{N}.

(2)

Ker(\varphi_{l})
is self-adjoint for every
l\in\mathbb{N}.

(3)

Ker(\varphi)
is an absolutely matrix order ideal of
\mathbb{X}.

(4)

\varphi=0
if and only if
Ker(\varphi)=\mathbb{X}
if and only if
Ker(\varphi)^{+}=\mathbb{X}^{+}.

Moreover, if
X
is absolute matrix order unit space, then

(5)

\varphi=0
if and only if
\varphi(e)=0.

Proof.

(1)

Let
[x_{i,j}]\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(Ker(\varphi)).
Then
\varphi(x_{i,j})=0
for every pair of
i
and
j
and consequently
\varphi_{l,m}([x_{i,j}])=[\varphi(x_{i,j})]=0.
Thus
[x_{i,j}]\in Ker(\varphi_{l,m})
so that
\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(Ker(\varphi))\subset Ker(\varphi_{l,m}).
Tracing back the proof, we also conclude that
Ker(\varphi_{l,m})\subset\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(Ker(\varphi)).
Hence
\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(Ker(\varphi))=Ker(\varphi_{l,m}).

(2)

\varphi
is absolute value preserving, we have
\varphi_{l}(x^{*})=\varphi_{l}(x)^{*}
for every
x\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})
and
l\in\mathbb{N}.
Now, the result follows.

(3)

Put
\overline{\varphi}_{l}(x)=\varphi_{l}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}.
Then
\overline{\varphi}_{l}:\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}\to\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{Y})_{sa}
defines an absolute value preserving map with
Ker(\overline{\varphi}_{l})=Ker(\varphi_{l})_{sa}.
By
[
24
, Theorem 2.7]
,
Ker(\overline{\varphi}_{l})
is an order ideal of
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}.
Thus
Ker(\phi)
forms a matrix order ideal of
\mathbb{X}.
Next, let
x=[x_{i,j}]\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(Ker(\varphi)).
Without loss of genearlity, assume that
l\geq m
so that
[x,0]\in\mathbb{M}_{l-m}(Ker(\varphi)).
Then
\varphi_{l}([x,0])=0
and consequently by Proposition
2.3
(4), we have
\varphi_{m}(|x|_{l,m})\circledast 0_{l-m}=\varphi_{l}(|x|_{l,m}\circledast 0_{l-m})=\varphi_{l}(|[x,0]|_{l})=|\varphi_{l}([x,0])|_{l}=0.
Thus
|x|_{l,m}\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(Ker(\varphi))^{+}
so that
|\cdot|_{l,m}:\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(Ker(\varphi))\to\mathbb{M}_{m}(\varphi)^{+}
is well defined for every pair of
l,m\in\mathbb{N}.
Similarily, the case
l<m
can be taken care. Hence the result follows.

(4)

Let
\varphi=0.
Then
Ker(\varphi)=\mathbb{X}
so that
Ker(\varphi)^{+}=\mathbb{X}^{+}.
Conversely, assume that
Ker(\varphi)^{+}=\mathbb{X}^{+}.
Let
x\in\mathbb{X}_{sa}
and
x=x^{+}-x^{-}
be the orthogonal decomposition of
x.
Since
\varphi(x^{+})=\varphi(x^{-})=0,
we get
\varphi(x)=\varphi(x^{+})-\varphi(x^{-})=0.
Next, let
x\in\mathbb{X}.
Put
x_{1}=\frac{x-x^{*}}{2}
and
x_{2}=\frac{x-x^{*}}{2i}
so that
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{X}_{sa}
with
x=x_{1}+ix_{2}.
Then
\varphi(x_{1})=\varphi(x_{2})=0
and consequently
\varphi(x)=\varphi(x_{1})+i\varphi(x_{2})=0.
Hence
\varphi=0.

If
X
is an absolute matrix order unit space, then

(5)

\varphi=0
implies
\varphi(e)=0.
Conversely, assume that
\varphi(e)=0.
Then
e\in Ker(\varphi).
Now, let
x\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
so that
0\leq x\leq\|x\|e.
Since
Ker(\varphi)
is an order ideal, we get
x\in Ker(\varphi)~i.e.~\varphi(x)=0.
Thus
\phi(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
so that
Ker(\varphi)^{+}=\mathbb{X}^{+}.
Hence, by (4), the result follows.

∎

Finally, we prove that
\frac{\mathbb{X}}{Ker(\varphi)},
the quotient of
\mathbb{X}
by
Ker(\varphi),
is always an absolutely matrix ordered space. However, under the assumption of absolute matrix order unit property, it is identified with
\varphi(\mathbb{X})
as an absolute matrix order unit ideal of

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
be absolutely matrix ordered spaces and
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
be a completely absolute value preserving map. Put
\mathbb{X}_{0}=\frac{\mathbb{X}}{Ker(\varphi)}.
Then

(1)

\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{0})=\frac{\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})}{Ker(\varphi_{l})}.

We write:
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{0})^{+}=\{Ker(\varphi_{l})+x:x\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}\}.

(2)

Ker(\varphi_{l})+x\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{0})^{+}
if and only if
\varphi_{l}(x)\geq 0.

(3)

|\cdot|_{l,m}^{0}:\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X}_{0})\to\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{0})^{+}
given by
Ker(\varphi_{l,m})+x\longmapsto Ker(\varphi_{l})+|x|_{l,m}
are well defined maps.

(4)

\left(\mathbb{X}_{0},\{\mathbb{M}_{l}(X_{0})^{+}\},\{|\cdot|^{0}_{l,m}\}\right)
is an absolutely matrix ordered space identified with
\varphi(\mathbb{X}).

Moreover, if
\mathbb{X}~\textrm{and}~\mathbb{Y}
are absolute matrix order unit spaces with
\|\varphi(e)\|=1
and
\varphi(e)\in|\mathcal{M}|_{\infty}(\mathbb{Y}),
then we also have:

(5)

\mathbb{X}_{0}
is an absolute matrix order ideal of
\mathbb{Y}
as identified with
\mathbb{Y}_{\varphi(e)}=\varphi(\mathbb{X}).

Proof.

(1)

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{0})\ni\begin{bmatrix}Ker(\varphi)+x_{i,j}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\begin{bmatrix}z_{i,j}+x_{i,j}\end{bmatrix}:z_{i,j}\in Ker(\varphi)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\begin{bmatrix}z_{i,j}\end{bmatrix}:z_{i,j}\in Ker(\varphi)\}+\begin{bmatrix}x_{i,j}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ker(\varphi_{l})+\begin{bmatrix}x_{i,j}\end{bmatrix}\in\frac{\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})}{Ker(\varphi_{l})}.

Thus
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{0})=\frac{\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})}{Ker(\varphi_{l})}
so that
\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{0})^{+}=\{Ker(\varphi_{l})+x:x\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}\}.

(2)

Let
Ker(\varphi_{l})+x\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{0})^{+}.
Then there exists
z\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})^{+}
such that
Ker(\varphi_{l})+x=Ker(\varphi_{l})+z.
Thus
x-z\in Ker(\varphi_{l})
and we have
\varphi_{l}(x)=\varphi_{l}(z)\geq 0.
Conversely, let
\varphi_{l}(x)\geq 0.
For
x_{1}=\frac{x-x^{*}}{2}
and
x_{2}=\frac{x-x^{*}}{2i}\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}
such that
x_{1}+ix_{2}=x,
we have
\varphi(x_{1})+i\varphi(x_{2})=\varphi(x)\geq 0.
In this case, we get
\varphi_{l}(x_{2})=0
so that
Ker(\varphi_{l})+x=Ker(\varphi_{l})+x_{1}.
Therefore, without loss of generality, we assume that
x\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X})_{sa}.
Next, let
x=x^{+}-x^{-}
be the orthogonal decomposition of
x.
By
[
24
, Proposition 2.6]
, we get that
\varphi_{l}(x)^{+}=\varphi_{l}(x^{+})
and
\varphi_{l}(x)^{-}=\varphi_{l}(x^{-}).
Since
\varphi_{l}(x)\geq 0
and orthogonal decomposition is always unique, we conclude that
\varphi_{l}(x^{-})=0.
Thus
Ker(\varphi_{l})+x=Ker(\varphi_{l})+x^{+}\in\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{0})^{+}.

(3)

Let
Ker(\varphi_{l,m})+x=Ker(\varphi_{l,m})+y.
Then
x-y\in Ker(\varphi_{l,m})
so that
\varphi_{l,m}(x)=\varphi_{l,m}(y).
Without loss of generality, we assume that
l\geq m.
Since
\varphi_{l}
is absolute value preserving, by Proposition
2.3
(4), we get

\displaystyle\varphi_{m}(|x|_{l,m})\circledast 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{l}(|\begin{bmatrix}x&0\end{bmatrix}|_{l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\varphi_{l}(\begin{bmatrix}x&0\end{bmatrix})|_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\begin{bmatrix}\varphi_{l,m}(x)&0\end{bmatrix}|_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\begin{bmatrix}\varphi_{l,m}(y)&0\end{bmatrix}|_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{m}(|y|_{l,m})\circledast 0.

and consequently
\varphi_{m}(|x|_{l,m})=\varphi_{m}(|y|_{l,m}).
Thus
|x|_{l,m}-|y|_{l,m}\in Ker(\varphi_{m})
so that
Ker(\varphi_{m})+|x|_{l,m}=Ker(\varphi_{m})+|y|_{l,m}.
Hence the maps
|\cdot|^{0}_{l,m}
are well-defined.

(4)

Let
x\in\mathbb{M}_{k,l}(\mathbb{X})
and
y\in\mathbb{M}_{m,n}(\mathbb{X}).
Then by
2.1
(2)(b), we get

\displaystyle|(Ker(\varphi_{l,m})+x)\circledast(Ker(\varphi_{l,m})+y)|^{0}_{k+m,l+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|Ker(\varphi_{k+m,l+n})+(x\circledast y)|_{k+m,l+n}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ker(\varphi_{l+n})+(|x\circledast y|_{k+m,l+n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ker(\varphi_{l+n})+(|x|_{k,l}\circledast|y|_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(Ker(\varphi_{l})+|x|_{k,l})\circledast(Ker(\varphi_{n})+|y|_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|Ker(\varphi_{k,l})+x|_{k,l}\circledast|Ker(\varphi_{m,n})+y|_{m,n}.

Next, let
\varsigma_{1}\in\mathbb{M}_{k,m}
and
\varsigma_{2}\in\mathbb{M}_{n,l}.
Again by
2.1
(2)(a), we have:

\displaystyle|\varsigma_{1}(Ker(\varphi_{m,n})+y)\varsigma_{2}|_{k,l}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|Ker(\varphi_{k,l})+\varsigma_{1}y\varsigma_{2}|_{k,l}^{0}

\displaystyle=

4.
Relation between
\mathcal{K}_{0}(\mathbb{X}_{x})
and
\mathcal{K}_{0}(\mathbb{X})

In this section, we derive a relation between
\mathcal{K}_{0}(\mathbb{X}_{x})
and
\mathcal{K}_{0}(\mathbb{X}).
For that, we start with the following characterization of order projections in
\mathbb{X}_{x}
in terms of order projections in
\mathbb{X}.

Lemma 4.1
.

In an absolute matrix order unit space
\mathbb{X},
let
x\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
such that
\|x\|=1.
Then
\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x})=\{p\in\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X}):p\leq x^{l}\}=\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X})\cap\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x})
for every
l\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Let
p\in\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X})\cap\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x}).
By proposition
3.6
, we get that
0\leq p\leq x^{l}.
For
\|x\|=1,
we also get that
0\leq x^{l}\leq e^{l}.
Then
0\leq x^{l}-p\leq e^{l}-p.
Since
p\in\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X}),
we have
e^{l}\perp e^{l}-p.
By
[
23
, Definition 3.4(4)]
, we conclude
p\perp x^{l}-p.
Thus
p\in\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x}).
Hence the reslut follows.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

In an absolute matrix order unit space
\mathbb{X},
we have:
\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{p})=\{q\in\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X}):q\leq p^{l}\}=\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X})\cap\mathbb{M}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{p})
for every
p\in\mathbb{OP}(\mathbb{X})\setminus\{0\}
and
l\in\mathbb{N}.

The partial isometric equivalence (denoted by

25

in
\mathbb{X}_{x}

Proposition 4.3
.

In an absolute matrix order unit space
\mathbb{X},
let
p,q\in\mathbb{OP}_{\infty}(\mathbb{X}_{x})
for some
x\in\mathbb{X}^{+}
such that
\|x\|=1.
Then
p\sim q
in
\mathbb{X}_{x}
if and only if
p\sim q
in
\mathbb{X}.

Proof.

Suppose that
p\sim q
in
\mathbb{X}
for some
p\in\mathbb{OP}_{l}(\mathbb{X}_{x})
and
q\in\mathbb{OP}_{m}(\mathbb{X}_{x}).
There exists

satisfying
p=|y^{*}|_{m,l}
and
q=|y|_{l,m}.
For
\|p\|\leq 1
and
\|q\|\leq 1,
we have
p\leq x^{l}
and
q\leq x^{m}.
Then

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}x^{l}&0\\
0&x^{m}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}p&0\\
0&q\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}|y^{*}|_{m,l}&0\\
0&|y|_{l,m}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}\right|_{l+m}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\pm\begin{bmatrix}0&y\\
y^{*}&0\end{bmatrix}

y^{*}&x^{m}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{M}_{l+m}(\mathbb{X})^{+}.
Thus
y\in\mathbb{M}_{l,m}(\mathbb{X}_{x})
and consequently
p\sim q
in
\mathbb{X}_{x}.

Next,
p\sim q
in
\mathbb{X}_{x}
implies
p\sim q
in
\mathbb{X}

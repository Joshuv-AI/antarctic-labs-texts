Vertex electrical model: lagrangian and non-negative properties
D. Talalaev

Abstract
. This note is a modest addition to the work [ 1 ] . Here we construct an embedding of the space of electrical networks to the totally non-negative Lagrangian Grassmannian in a generic situation with the help of the technique of vertex integrable models of statistical mechanics.



1.
Introduction

The question of embedding the space of electrical networks to a Lagrangian non-negative Grassmannian already has a rather rich history and should be perceived in the context of other archetypal models on weighted graphs. The latter include the dimer model and the Ising model. For the first of them, there is an embedding of the model space into a non-negative Grassmannian
[
2
]
, for the second - into a non-negative orthogonal Grassmannian
[
3
]
. The electrical network model is related to the Lagrangian version of the non-negative Grassmannian. This statement has been proven with the help of various techniques in the works
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
1
]
.
Here we apply the technique of integrable vertex models of statistical physics to the Grassmannian embedding problem. The actual idea of the vertex representation is to express the observables in a factorized form, each of whose multipliers satisfies local controllable properties. These are usually versions of the Yang-Baxter equation, the local Yang-Baxter equation. The vertex representation of electrical networks involves the solution of the local Yang-Baxter equation encoding Ohm’s law. This solution is related to the so-called electrical solution of the Zamolodchikov tetrahedron equation
[
7
]
. This representation was found in Sergeyev’s work
[
8
]
and led to the modern understanding of electrical manifolds proposed in the work
[
9
]
.

By an electrical network
e
, we mean a connected graph
\Gamma
without loops with a function of weights on the edges
c:E\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
(the conductivity function) and a subset of boundary vertices

V_{B}=\{1,\ldots,n\}\subset V=\{1,\ldots,N\}.
Let
c_{ij}
be the conductivity of an edge connecting vertices
i
-th and
j
-th which is zero if the vertices are not connected.

Definition 1.1
.

The Kirchhoff matrix of a network is a matrix

\displaystyle T_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}-c_{ij}&\mbox{if}&i\neq j\\
\sum_{k\neq i}c_{ik}&\mbox{if}&i=j\end{array}\right.

The network response matrix
M_{R}(e)
relates the currents and potentials at the boundary points of the network

\displaystyle I=M_{R}U,

where
I:V_{B}\to\mathbb{R}
are the currents and
U:V_{B}\to\mathbb{R}
are the potentials in boundary points.
Let us present the Kirchhoff matrix in a block form where the
n\times n
block
A
corresponds to the subset of boundary points:

\displaystyle T=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
B^{T}&C\end{array}\right)

then the response matrix is given by an expression

\displaystyle M_{R}=A-BC^{-1}B^{T},

which is just the Schur complement of
C.
We denote by
E_{n}
the variety of electrical networks with
n
boundary points. The main statements of this note are the theorem
2.4
where we construct an embedding of the subvariety of electrical networks on standart graphs
\subset E_{n}
to the lagrangian grassmanian
LG(n-1)
and the theorem
2.10
which demonstrate the total non-negativity of this embedding in the case of odd
n.

Vertex model

The set of standard graphs is a subset of the set of critical graphs (see

In this paper we deal only with standard graphs. In the problem of parametrization of electrical networks, they represent, in a certain sense, a point of generic position.

For
e\in E_{n}
on a standard graph the authors of
[

]
defined the boundary partition function
M_{B}(e)
. It is a matrix which depends on at most
n(n-1)/2
parameters, these parameters are the conductivities of the edges of the network. According to
[

]

Sp(n)
.

Let us introduce the notations

\displaystyle T_{2n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}1&0&0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1&0&\cdots\\
0&1&0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&1&\cdots\\
0&0&1&\cdots&0&0&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&0&\ddots\end{array}\right);\qquad S_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&0&0&\cdots&-1\\
-1&1&0&\cdots&0\\

\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\end{array}\right).

One of the main results from
[
9
]
is the following

Lemma 2.1
.

For an electrical network
e\in E_{n}
on the standard critical graph
\Sigma_{n}
the row spaces of the matrices

\displaystyle W_{1}=(S_{n},M_{R})\quad\mbox{and}\quad W_{2}=(M_{B},Id_{n})S_{2n}T_{2n}

define the same point in
\mathrm{Gr}(n-1,2n)
.

2.1.
Lagrangian property

The modified boundary measurement matrix
\check{M}_{B}(e)
for the standard graph is given by

\displaystyle\check{M}_{B}=\prod_{i<j}\check{\phi}_{j-i,j-i+1}\left((-1)^{i+j}r_{ij}^{(-1)^{i+j}}\right).

Here
E_{ij}
are just elementary matrices with
1
on the
i,j
-th place and the multipiers in the product are given by

\displaystyle\check{\phi}_{ii+1}(s)=1-s(E_{ii}+E_{i,i+1}-E_{i+1,i}-E_{i+1,i+1})\in Mat_{n},

the parameters
r_{ij}=1/c_{ij}
are the resistances of the corresponding edges.
Then

\displaystyle M_{B}=\omega_{0}\check{M}_{B},

where

\omega_{0}
represents the longest element in the Weil group

\displaystyle\omega_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\cdots&0&1\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&1&\cdots&0&0\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0\end{array}\right),

Lemma 2.2
.

The row space of the matrix

\displaystyle W_{0}=(M_{B},Id_{n})

is an isotropic subspace with respect to the bilinear form

\displaystyle\eta=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g&0\\
0&g\end{array}\right);

where

\displaystyle g=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&-1&1&\cdots&(-1)^{n-1}\\
1&0&-1&\cdots&(-1)^{n}\\
-1&1&0&\cdots&(-1)^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(-1)^{n}&(-1)^{n-1}&\cdots&\cdots&0\end{array}\right).

Proof.

We want to show that

\displaystyle W_{0}\eta W_{0}^{t}=0

which is equivalent to the following

\displaystyle M_{B}gM_{B}^{t}+g=0.

By Theorem 4.11 of
[
9
]
the modified boundary measurement matrix
\check{M}_{B}(e)

preserves the skew-symmetric form
g

(35)

\displaystyle\check{M}_{B}g\check{M}_{B}^{t}=g.

This equality follows from the same one for each generator

\check{\phi}_{i,i+1}(s).

Therefore

\displaystyle M_{B}gM_{B}^{t}=\omega_{0}\check{M}_{B}g\check{M}_{B}^{t}\omega_{0}=\omega_{0}g\omega_{0}=-g.

∎

The row space of
W_{0}
contains the vector
\xi=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
which generates the left kernel of
S_{2n}.

Lemma 2.3
.

The row space of the matrix
W_{2}
lies in a subspace
\widetilde{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
of dimension
2n-2,
i.e.
W_{2}\in\mathrm{Gr}(n-1,\widetilde{V}).

Proof.

Let
\mu=(1,-1,1,\ldots,(-1)^{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\displaystyle\check{\phi}_{i,i+1}\mu=\mu.

Hence

\displaystyle\check{M}_{B}\mu=\mu;\qquad M_{B}\mu=\omega_{0}\mu=\pm\mu;

with
+
for odd
n
and
-
for even
n
.
Hence the row space of
W_{0}
lies in the orthogonal complement to the
2n
-vector

w=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mu\\
\mp\mu\end{array}\right)=(1,-1,1,-1,\ldots,1,-1).

The kernel of
S_{2n}
lies in this complement. Hence the image of
w^{\perp}
under the action of
S_{2n}T_{2n}
is a
(2n-2)
-dimensional space which we denoted by
\widetilde{V}
.
∎

Note that
\xi^{t}\eta=w^{t}
for even
n
and
\xi^{t}\eta=0
for
n
odd. Therefore
\xi^{t}\eta v=0

\forall v\in w^{\perp}
and

\displaystyle\xi\in Ann(\eta|_{w^{\perp}}),

therefore the form
\eta
can be projected to the quotient
w^{\perp}/\langle\xi\rangle
.

From now on we will work inside
w^{\perp}/\langle\xi\rangle
. It is convenient to prove Lagrangian and nonnegativity properties in the appropriate bases of this space. Summarizing the above arguments we obtain

Theorem 2.4
.

The projection of the row space of
W_{0}
to the space
w^{\perp}/\langle\xi\rangle
is a lagrangian subspace with respect to the reduction of
\eta.

2.2.
Nonnegativity

In this subsection we present a demonstration of the total nonnegative property of the subspace corresponding to an electrical network. Strictly speaking, this method works only for standard graphs moreover only for the case of odd
n
. But the arguments seem to allow generalization.

Choose a basis in
w^{\perp}

\displaystyle f_{i}=e_{i}+e_{i+1},\qquad i=1,\ldots,2n-1.

The matrix

\displaystyle(\check{M}_{B},Id_{n})=\omega_{0}(M_{B},Id_{n})\left(\begin{array}[]{c}Id_{n}\\
\omega_{0}\end{array}\right)

represents the same point in
\mathrm{Gr}(n,2n)
as
(M_{B},Id_{n})
after an appropriate change of basis in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}.

Remark 2.5
.

The representation (
2.2
) is important for us, it provides an explicit expression for the point of
\mathrm{Gr}(n,2n)
in terms of the vertex representation of boundary measurements matrix. (
2.2
) is suitable to relate the nonegativity property of the whole matrix with its nontrivial block. In what follows we adapt this strategy making a reduction to
w^{\perp}/\langle\xi\rangle.

Note that
\check{M}_{B}
preserves
\mu
on the right and the vector
\zeta=(1,1,1,\ldots)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
on the left:

\displaystyle\zeta^{t}\check{M}_{B}=\zeta^{t};\qquad\check{M}_{B}\mu=\mu.

Hence its left action preserves the subspace
V_{\mu}=\mu^{\perp}.
Let us choose the following basis in
V_{\mu}

\displaystyle f_{i}=e_{i}+e_{i+1},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n-1.

The operators
\chi_{ii+1}(t)=\check{\phi}_{ii+1}(t)|_{V_{\mu}}
in this basis (let us make attention that we act on vectors on the left) takes the form

\displaystyle\chi_{ii+1}(t)=1+t(E_{i+1i}-E_{i-1i})

with the exceptions:

\displaystyle\chi_{12}(t)=1+tE_{21}

and

\displaystyle\chi_{n-1,n}(t)=1-tE_{n-2,n-1}.

Also let us define the matrix

\displaystyle\Delta=Diag(1,-1,-1,1,1,-1,\ldots)\in Mat_{n-1}.

Let us introduce the next notation:

\displaystyle u_{ii+1}(t)=1+t(E_{ii-1}+E_{ii+1})

with the appropriate exceptions for
i=1
and
n-1.

This matrix is totally nonnegative for
t\geq 0
for obvious reasons.

Lemma 2.6
.

The matrix

\displaystyle\Delta\chi_{i,i+1}(t)\Delta

takes the form

\displaystyle\Delta\chi_{ii+1}(t)\Delta=u_{ii+1}((-1)^{i}t).

Remark 2.7
.

The similar formulas for the generators of the symplectic group was obtained in
[
12
]
in their exploring of the positivity properties of the electrical group.

Lemma 2.8
.

The matrix

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{B}=\Delta\check{M}_{B}|_{V_{\mu}}\Delta

in the basis
\{f_{i}\}
is totally nonnegative.

Proof.

The proof is a consequence of the Binet-Cauchy formula and of the observation that this matrix is a product of totally nonegative factors from Lemma
2.6
. The subspace
V_{\mu}
is invariant with respect to the action of all factors of the decomposition

(39)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{B}=\Delta\check{M}_{B}|_{V_{\mu}}\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta\left(\prod_{i<j}\check{\phi}_{j-i,j-i+1}\left((-1)^{i+j}r_{ij}^{(-1)^{i+j}}\right)\right)|_{V_{\mu}}\Delta

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta\prod_{i<j}\chi_{j-i,j-i+1}\left((-1)^{i+j}r_{ij}^{(-1)^{i+j}}\right)\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i<j}u_{j-i,j-i+1}\left(r_{ij}^{(-1)^{i+j}}\right).

Lemma
2.6
provides that all factors are totally nonnegative.
∎

Here we need a version of the lemma 3.9 from
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
A
be a totally nonnegative matrix
n\times n
than the matrix
n\times 2n

\displaystyle\psi(A)=(Id_{n},\omega_{0}D_{n}A)

gives a point in the nonnegative Grassmannian.

An
n=4
example of
\psi(A)
is given by

\displaystyle\psi(A)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}1&0&0&0&-a_{41}&-a_{42}&-a_{43}&-a_{44}\\
0&1&0&0&a_{31}&a_{32}&a_{33}&a_{34}\\
0&0&1&0&-a_{21}&-a_{22}&-a_{23}&-a_{24}\\
0&0&0&1&a_{11}&a_{12}&a_{13}&a_{14}\end{array}\right).

Proof.

In fact we can demonstrate a stronger statement which identifies some minors of
A
with principal minors of
\psi(A).
Let
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
be a subset in
[n].
We denote by
Inv(I)\subset[n]
the subset

\displaystyle Inv(I)=\{n-i_{k}+1,\ldots,n-i_{1}+1\},

and by
\widetilde{I}
the shifted subset in
[2n]

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}=\{i_{1}+n,\ldots,i_{k}+n\}.

Then the following is true

(46)

\displaystyle\Delta_{[n]\backslash I\cup\widetilde{J}}\psi(A)=\Delta_{Inv(I)}^{J}A.

Let us demonstrate this by two steps. The first step considers the set
I
concentrated at the end of
[n]
, that is of the type
I=\{n-k+1,\ldots,n-1,n\}.
In this case the principal minor
\Delta_{[n]\backslash I\cup\widetilde{J}}\psi(A)
coincides with the minor
\Delta_{I}^{\widetilde{J}}\psi(A)
. The last one coincide up to the sign with the minor
\Delta_{I}^{J}A.
Let us show that the sign is the same. The lower
k
rows of
\omega_{0}D_{n}A
are just the first
k
rows of
A
in altered order with some rows with altered signs. The length of the longest element in
S_{k}
is
\frac{k(k-1)}{2}
. The number of rows with altered signs is
\left[\frac{k}{2}\right].
They coinside and hence the signs of minors coincide.

Now let us demonstrate the statement for minors for generic
I
by induction with respect to the lexicographic order. Let
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
be such that for all
I^{\prime}>I
the formula
46
is true. For examle the statement is true for the
I^{\prime}=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}+1,\ldots,i_{k}\}
(with the shift in only one index).
We have

\displaystyle\Delta_{[n]\backslash I^{\prime}\cup\widetilde{J}}\psi(A)=\Delta_{Inv(I^{\prime})}^{J}A.

We know that
\Delta_{[n]\backslash I\cup\widetilde{J}}\psi(A)
and
\Delta_{Inv(I)}^{J}A
coincide up to a sign. Let us remark the passing from
I^{\prime}
to
I
the sign of the left side expression changes twice, the first one due to the fact that the neighboring rows of right block of
\psi(A)
have different signs, and the secondly: the change of the neighboring columns in the left block of
\psi(A)
also changes the sign.
∎

Theorem 2.10
.

Let us consider the subspace
V_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
generated by

\displaystyle\{\pm f_{2n-1},\ldots,f_{n+5},-f_{n+4},-f_{n+3},f_{n+2},f_{n+1},f_{1},-f_{2},-f_{3},f_{4},f_{5},-f_{6},\ldots,\pm f_{n-1}\}

For
n
odd the projection of the space
w^{\perp}/\langle\xi\rangle
to
V_{0}
is an isomorphism. The image of the row space of
(\check{M}_{B},Id_{n})
after this projection is represented by the matrix

\displaystyle\psi(\Delta\check{M}_{B}|_{V_{\mu}}\Delta).

which corresponds to a nonnegative point of the Grassmannian.

Proof.

The vector
\xi\in w^{\perp}
has a representation

\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{2i-1}.
The projection of
w^{\perp}
to the subspace generated by
\{f_{i}\}
except
f_{n}
induces an isomorphism on
w^{\perp}/\langle\xi\rangle.
This projection could be described by the action of the blocks of the matrix
(\check{M}_{B},Id_{n})
on the basis on the left. This action after the sign change given by the matrix
\Delta
is represented by the matrix

\displaystyle(\Delta\check{M}_{B}|_{V_{\mu}}\Delta,Id_{n-1})=(\mathcal{M}_{B},Id_{n-1}).

The following matrix multiplication

\displaystyle\omega_{0}D_{n-1}\times(\mathcal{M}_{B},Id_{n-1})\times\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&Id_{n-1}\\
\omega_{0}D_{n-1}&0\end{array}\right)=(Id_{n-1},\omega_{0}D_{n-1}\mathcal{M}_{B})

transforms the matrix
(\mathcal{M}_{B},Id_{n-1})
to the form
\psi(\mathcal{M}_{B}).
Let us comment that the left multiplication does not change the point of the Grassmannian and the right multiplication is just the change of the basis in
V_{0},
it transforms the matrix of the symplectic form and the subspace remains Lagrangian.
∎

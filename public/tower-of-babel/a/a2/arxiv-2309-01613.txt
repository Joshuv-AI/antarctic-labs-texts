Stable configurations of entangled graphs and weaves with repulsive interactions
Motoko Kotani, Hisashi Naito, Eriko Shinkawa

Abstract
Entangled objects such as entangled graphs and weaves are often seen in nature. In the present article, two identical graphs entangled, and weaves with two different color threads are studied. A method to identify stable configurations in the three dimensional space of a given topological entangled structure, a planar graph with crossing information, is proposed by analyzing the steepest descent flow of the energy functional with repulsive interactions. The existence and uniqueness of a solution in the entangled case. In the untangled case, a weave has a unique tangle decomposition with height order, whose components are moving away each other in the order t 1 / 3 t^{1/3} as time t t goes to the infinity.

Introduction

Entangled structures are commonly observed in nature and in our daily lives.
Examples include DNA sequences, molecular structures of polymer networks and woven carbon nanotubes used in functional materials.
In mathematics, the notion of knots and links has been developed as a branch of Topology since the 19th century,
for describing of entangled loop structures with its own interests,

It is motivated in applications.

3
Weaves

A systematic analysis of topological weaves has been intensively done by Fukuda–Kotani–Mahmoudi (
[
6
,
7
,
15
]
).
The present article is the first attempt to develop methods to identify stable configurations of weaves in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.
It turns out much more intricated treatment is required for weaves than for entangled graphs with the reason we see in this section.

A topological weave is defined as a collection of several families
T^{a}
,
T^{b},\ldots
of threads which intertwine
but do not interact each other.
A pair of two threads
t^{a}_{i}
and
t^{b}_{j}
belonging to distinct families
T^{a}
and
T^{b}
,
respectively, makes exactly one vertex
v_{i,j}
with over-under information.

Because a weaves is a thickend plane woven by two colored yarn with a given design in the plane,
it should be constructed from the data called
weaving design
in the plane,
which consists of a set of parallel blue infinite lines (isometric to
{\mathbb{R}}
),
and a set of parallel red infinite lines (isometric to
{\mathbb{R}}
) in the
x
-
y
plane (see Fig.
3.1
).
Every pair of a blue line and a red line intersect exactly once and gives an intersecting point.
We give over-under information
\{1,-1\}
or
\{-1,1\}
at the intersection point, which we call the
crossing information

S
.
The weaving structure should be constructed to have the weaving design as the image of its orthogonal projection to the
x
-
y
plane;
Namely

1.

a blue thread in the weave one-to-one corresponds to a blue line in the weaving design,

2.

a red thread in the weave one-to-one corresponds to red line in the weaving design,

3.

a vertex
v_{i,j}
in the weave one-to-one corresponds to the intersecting point of two lines (blue and red) in in the weaving design,

4.

the blue thread is above to the red thread along the
z
direction at the intersection
of the corresponding blue line and the corresponding red line
if the corresponding crossing information
S
is
\{1,-1\}
and, the vice versa in the case of
\{-1,1\}
.

We study its stable configuration in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
which achieves the least energy in the SRRI model.
Because even the collection of threads in the same family is not connected,
the analysis needs to more careful considerations than the case of entangled graphs.
We therefore introduce a notion
weavely connected
in the present paper.
By using them, we define entangled/untangled weaving,
and prove the existence and the uniqueness of the solution of the Euler-Lagrange equation of the SRRI energy of the weaving.

On the other hand,
by Theorem

,
for any

,
there exist bounded functions
C_{W_{1},b}(v_{i,j})
and
C_{W_{1},r}(v_{i,j})

\displaystyle z_{b}(t,v_{i,j})

\displaystyle=C_{W_{1},b}(v_{i,j})+w_{1}^{-1}M_{W_{1}}(t),

\displaystyle z_{r}(t,v_{i,j})

\displaystyle=C_{W_{1},r}(v_{i,j})+w_{1}^{-1}M_{W_{1}}(t).

Hence, there exists positive constant
c_{W_{1}}=c_{W_{1}}(E_{0},\{\lambda^{X}_{k}\},\{w_{k}\})>0
and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\in T^{b}_{W_{1}}\\
r_{j}\in T^{r}_{W_{1}}\end{subarray}}(z_{b}(v_{i,j})+z_{r}(v_{i,j}))

\displaystyle=c_{W_{1}}M_{W_{1}}(t)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\in T^{b}_{W_{1}}\\
r_{j}\in T^{r}_{W_{1}}\end{subarray}}(C_{W_{1},b}(v_{i,j})+C_{W_{1},r}(v_{i,j})),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\in T^{b}_{W_{1}^{c}}\\
r_{j}\in T^{r}_{W_{1}^{c}}\end{subarray}}(z_{b}(v_{i,j})+z_{r}(v_{i,j}))

\displaystyle=c_{W_{1}^{c}}M_{W_{1}^{c}}(t)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\in T^{b}_{W_{1}}\\
r_{j}\in T^{r}_{W_{1}}\end{subarray}}(C_{W_{1}^{c},b}(v_{i,j})+C_{W_{1}^{c},r}(v_{i,j})),

and we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\in T^{b}_{W_{1}}\\
r_{j}\in T^{r}_{W_{1}}\end{subarray}}(z_{b}(v_{i,j})+z_{r}(v_{i,j}))-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\in T^{b}_{W_{1}^{c}}\\
r_{j}\in T^{r}_{W_{1}^{c}}\end{subarray}}(z_{b}(v_{i,j})+z_{r}(v_{i,j}))

(3.36)

\displaystyle=c_{W_{1}}M_{W_{1}}(t)-c_{W_{1}^{c}}M_{W_{1}^{c}}(t)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\in T^{b}_{W_{1}}\\
r_{j}\in T^{r}_{W_{1}}\end{subarray}}(C_{W_{1},b}(v_{i,j})+C_{W_{1},r}(v_{i,j})),

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\in T^{b}_{W_{1}}\\
r_{j}\in T^{r}_{W_{1}}\end{subarray}}(C_{W_{1}^{c},b}(v_{i,j})+C_{W_{1}^{c},r}(v_{i,j})).

Hence there exists a positive constants
C_{0}=C_{0}(E_{0},\{\lambda^{X}_{k}\},\{w_{k}\})>0

and

, which are independent of

,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\in T^{b}_{W_{1}}\\

r_{j}\in T^{r}_{W_{1}^{c}}\end{subarray}}(z_{b}(v_{i,j})+z_{r}(v_{i,j}))\right|

(3.37)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}|W_{W_{1}}(t)-M_{W_{1}^{c}}(t)|

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\in T^{b}_{W_{1}}\\
r_{j}\in T^{r}_{W_{1}}\end{subarray}}(C_{W_{1},b}(v_{i,j})+C_{W_{1},r}(v_{i,j}))-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{i}\in T^{b}_{W_{1}}\\
r_{j}\in T^{r}_{W_{1}}\end{subarray}}(C_{W_{1}^{c},b}(v_{i,j})+C_{W_{1}^{c},r}(v_{i,j}))\right|

) and (
3.36
),

(3.38)

\displaystyle=C_{0}\left|M_{W_{1}}-M_{W_{1}^{c}}\right|+C_{1}.

Now combining (
3.33
) and (

),
we obtain

Proposition 3.20
.

Assume that a weaving structure
W=(T^{b},T^{r},S)
has the tangle components decomposition
\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{K}\}
with height order.
Then there exists positive constants
C_{0}=C_{0}(E_{0},\{\lambda^{X}_{k}\},\{w_{k}\})>0

and

,

,

3.3

Proof.

We prove for
k=1
, which works with other

Recall that the equation is

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(M_{W_{1}}(t)-M_{W_{1}^{c}}(t))

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{i,j}\in W_{1}\triangle W_{1}^{c}}\frac{2}{|z_{b}(v_{i,j})-z_{r}(v_{i,j})|^{2}},

(3.41)

and by Proposition
3.19
,
we have

c_{0}(t+c_{1})^{1/3}\leq|M_{W_{1}}(t)-M_{W_{1}^{c}}(t)|,

and

\displaystyle c_{0}(t+c_{1})^{1/3}

\displaystyle\leq w_{1}^{1/3}|M_{W_{1}}(t)-M_{W_{1}^{c}}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq|z_{b}(t)-z_{r}(t)+|z_{b}(t)-w_{1}^{-1}M_{W_{1}}(t)|+|z_{b}(t)-w_{1}^{-1}M_{W_{1}}(t)|.

On the other hand,
by Theorem
3.18
,
we have

\displaystyle|z_{b}(t,v)-w_{1}^{-1}M_{W_{1}}(t)|\leq C,

\displaystyle|z_{r}(t,v)-w_{1}^{-1}M_{W_{1}}(t)|\leq C,

then for sufficiently large

\displaystyle C_{0}(t+C_{1})^{1/3}

\displaystyle\leq w_{1}^{-1}|M_{W_{1}}(t)-M_{W_{1}^{c}}(t)|\leq|z_{b}(t,v_{i,j})-z_{r}(t,v_{i,j})|.

Therefore, we obtain

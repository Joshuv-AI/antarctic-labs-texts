A new DG method for a pure–stress formulation of the Brinkman problem with strong symmetry ∗ ∗ Funding : This research was supported by Spain’s Ministry of Economy Project PID2020-116287GB-I00, by the Monash Mathematics Research Fund S05802-3951284, and by the Australian Research Council through the Discovery Project grant DP220103160.


Abstract
A strongly symmetric stress approximation is proposed for the Brinkman equations with mixed boundary conditions. The resulting formulation solves for the Cauchy stress using a symmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method. Pressure and velocity are readily post-processed from stress, and a second post-process is shown to produce exactly divergence-free discrete velocities. We demonstrate the stability of the method with respect to a DG-energy norm and obtain error estimates that are explicit with respect to the coefficients of the problem. We derive optimal rates of convergence for the stress and for the post-processed variables. Moreover, under appropriate assumptions on the mesh, we prove optimal L 2 L^{2} -error estimates for the stress. Finally, we provide numerical examples in 2D and 3D.

Introduction

Scope and related work

Brinkman equations constitute one of the simplest homogenized models for viscous fluid flow in highly heterogeneous porous media. The analysis of the underlying PDE system exhibits challenges due to the presence of two parameters: viscosity and permeability. Likewise, the design and analysis of numerical methods that maintain robustness with respect to the very relevant cases of low effective viscosity (Darcy-like regime) vs very large permeability (Stokes-like regime), is still an issue of high interest.

For classical velocity–pressure formulations, a number of contributions are available to deal with the inf-sup compatibility of velocity and pressure spaces irrespective of the regime (Darcy-like or Stokes-like). See, e.g.,
[
17
,
18
,
20
,
21
,
23
]
.
On the other hand, mixed finite element formulations for Brinkman equations (that is, using other fields apart from the classical velocity–pressure pair) include pseudostress–based methods
[
14
,
19
]
(see also VEM counterparts in
[
8
,
15
]
, and
[
26
]
for the DG case with strong imposition of pseudostress symmetry, closer to the present contribution), vorticity–velocity–pressure schemes in augmented and non-augmented form
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
28
]
. There, the analysis of continuous and discrete problems depends either on the Babuška–Brezzi theory, or on a generalized inf-sup argument.

The method advanced in this paper is based on a pure–stress formulation, obtained from taking the deviatoric part of the stress and eliminating pressure, and using the momentum equation to write velocity as a function of the external and internal forces (forcing plus the divergence of the Cauchy stress). We consider the case of mixed boundary conditions, and use a symmetric interior penalty DG discretization. The space for discrete stresses incorporates symmetry strongly, as in
[
26
]
(see also
[
29
]
for elasticity and
[
25
]
for viscoelasticity).
At the discrete level, the velocity and the pressure are easily recovered in terms of the discrete Cauchy stress through post-processing. While the pressure post-processing is derivative-free, the usual post-process of velocity through the momentum balance entails taking the discrete divergence of the approximate stress, therefore leading to accuracy loss. As a remedy we propose a more involved reconstruction, starting from the initial post-processed velocity and solving an additional problem in mixed form using
H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits)
-conforming approximations. The newly computed velocity is exactly divergence-free and we prove that it enjoys the same convergence order as the stress approximation.

Other advantages of the present formulation include the physically accurate imposition of outflow boundary conditions fixing the normal traces of the full Cauchy stress (which is not straightforward to incorporate with vorticity- or pseudostress-based methods), having symmetric and positive definite linear systems after discretization, and straightforwardly handling heterogeneous and anisotropic permeability distributions. In this regard, the analysis of the formulation uses a DG-energy norm chosen in such a way that the error estimates are independent of the jumps of the permeability. This implies a similar property concerning the estimates for the velocity approximation. However, for the pressure we can only achieve stability and optimal rates of convergence in terms of the mesh parameter.

Notational preliminaries.

We denote the space of real matrices of order
d\times d
by
\mathbb{M}
and let
\mathbb{S}:=\{\bm{\tau}\in\mathbb{M};\ \bm{\tau}=\bm{\tau}^{\mathtt{t}}\}
be the subspace of symmetric matrices, where
\bm{\tau}^{\mathtt{t}}:=(\tau_{ji})
stands for the transpose of
\bm{\tau}=(\tau_{ij})
. The component-wise inner product of two matrices
\bm{\sigma},\,\bm{\tau}\in\mathbb{M}
is defined by
\bm{\sigma}:\bm{\tau}:=\sum_{i,j}\sigma_{ij}\tau_{ij}
. We also introduce the deviatoric part
\bm{\tau}^{\mathtt{D}}:=\bm{\tau}-\frac{1}{d}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\right)\bm{I}
of a tensor
\bm{\tau}
, where
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\tau_{ii}
and
\bm{I}
stands here for the identity in
\mathbb{M}
.

Let
\Omega
be a polyhedral Lipschitz bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(d=2,3)
, with boundary
\partial\Omega
. Along this paper we convene to apply all differential operators row-wise. Hence, given a tensorial function
\bm{\sigma}:\Omega\to\mathbb{M}
and a vector field
\bm{u}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we set the divergence
\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the gradient
\bm{\nabla}\bm{u}:\Omega\to\mathbb{M}
, and the linearized strain tensor
\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}):\Omega\to\mathbb{S}
as

(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma})_{i}:=\sum_{j}\partial_{j}\sigma_{ij}\,,\quad(\bm{\nabla}\bm{u})_{ij}:=\partial_{j}u_{i}\,,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}):=\frac{1}{2}\left[\bm{\nabla}\bm{u}+(\bm{\nabla}\bm{u})^{\mathtt{t}}\right].

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
H^{s}(\Omega,E)
stands for the usual Hilbertian Sobolev space of functions with domain
\Omega
and values in E, where
E
is either
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
\mathbb{M}
. In the case
E=\mathbb{R}
we simply write
H^{s}(\Omega)
. The norm of
H^{s}(\Omega,E)
is denoted
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{s,\Omega}
and the corresponding semi-norm
|\cdot|_{s,\Omega}
, indistinctly for
E=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{M}
. We use the convention
H^{0}(\Omega,E):=L^{2}(\Omega,E)
and let
(\cdot,\cdot)
be the inner product in
L^{2}(\Omega,E)
, for
E=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{M}
, namely,

(\bm{u},\bm{v}):=\int_{\Omega}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{v},\ \forall\bm{u},\bm{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau}):=\int_{\Omega}\bm{\sigma}:\bm{\tau},\ \forall\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{M}).

The space
H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)
stands for the vector fields
\bm{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. We denote the corresponding norm
\lVert\bm{v}\rVert^{2}_{H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}:=\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}
. Similarly, the space of tensors in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{S})
with divergence in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
is denoted
H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})
. We maintain the same notation
\lVert\bm{\tau}\rVert^{2}_{H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}:=\lVert\bm{\tau}\rVert_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}
for the corresponding norm. Let
\bm{n}
be the outward unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
. The Green formula

(\bm{\tau},\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}))+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau},\bm{v})=\int_{\partial\Omega}\bm{\tau}\bm{n}\cdot\bm{v}\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}),

can be used to extend the normal trace operator
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{S})\ni\bm{\tau}\to(\bm{\tau}|_{\partial\Omega})\bm{n}
to a linear continuous mapping
(\cdot|_{\partial\Omega})\bm{n}:\,H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})\to H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the dual of
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Throughout this paper, we shall use the letter
C
to denote a generic positive constant independent of the mesh size
h
and the physical parameters
\kappa
and
\mu
, that may stand for different values at its different occurrences. Moreover, given any positive expressions
X
and
Y
depending on
h
,
\kappa
, and
\mu
, the notation
X\,\lesssim\,Y
means that
X\,\leq\,C\,Y
.

2
The pure-stress formulation of the Brinkman problem

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded and connected Lipschitz domain with boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
. Our purpose is to solve the Brinkman model

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}

\displaystyle=2\mu\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u})-p\bm{I}\quad\text{in $\Omega$},

(2.1a)

\displaystyle\bm{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{\mu}(\bm{f}+\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma})\quad\text{in $\Omega$},

(2.1b)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{in $\Omega$},

(2.1c)

that describes the flow of a fluid with dynamic viscosity
\mu>0
, with velocity field
\bm{u}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and pressure
p:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, in a porous medium characterized by a permeability coefficient
\kappa\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. The volume force is
\bm{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
is given and we assume that

0<\kappa_{-}\leq\kappa(\bm{x})\leq\kappa_{+}\quad\text{a.e. in $\Omega$}.

We assume a no-slip boundary condition
\bm{u}=\bm{0}
on a subset
\Gamma_{D}\subset\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
of positive surface measure and impose the normal stress boundary condition
\bm{\sigma}\bm{n}=\bm{0}
on its complement
\Gamma_{N}:=\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{D}
, where
\bm{n}
represents the exterior unit normal vector on
\Gamma
. In the case
\Gamma_{D}=\Gamma
we impose the zero mean value restriction
\left(p,1\right)=0
on the pressure to enforce uniqueness.

We want to impose the stress tensor
\bm{\sigma}
as a primary variable. To this end, we write the deviatoric part of (
2.1a
) and use equation (
2.1b
) to eliminate
p
and
\bm{u}
, respectively, and end up with the boundary value problem

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bm{\sigma}^{\mathtt{D}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\varepsilon}\Big({\kappa}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}+\bm{f})\Big)\quad\text{in $\Omega$},

(2.2a)

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{\mu}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}+\bm{f})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{on $\Gamma_{D}$},

(2.2b)

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}\bm{n}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{on $\Gamma_{N}$}.

(2.2c)

In the case of a subset
\Gamma_{N}
with positive surface measure, the essential boundary condition (
2.2c
) requires the introduction of the closed subspace of
H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})
given by

H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S}):=\left\{\bm{\tau}\in H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S});\quad\left\langle\bm{\tau}\bm{n},\bm{v}\right\rangle_{\Gamma}=0\quad\text{$\forall\bm{v}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})$,\, $\bm{v}|_{\Gamma_{D}}=\mathbf{0}$}\right\},

where
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\Gamma}
holds for the duality pairing between
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, the energy space is given by

X:=\begin{cases}H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})&\text{if $\Gamma_{D}\neq\Gamma$},\\
H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})&\text{if $\Gamma_{D}=\Gamma$}.\end{cases}

Testing (
2.2a
) with
\bm{\tau}\in X
and integrating by parts yields

\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\bm{\sigma}^{\mathtt{D}},\bm{\tau}^{\mathtt{D}}\right)=\left(\bm{\nabla}\big(\kappa(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}+\bm{f})\big),\bm{\tau}\right)=-\left({\kappa}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}+\bm{f}),\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\right).

This leads us to propose the following variational formulation of the problem: Find
\bm{\sigma}\in X
such that

a(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})+\big(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\big)=-\big(\kappa\bm{f},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\big),\quad\forall\bm{\tau}\in X.

(2.3)

where

a(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\bm{\sigma}^{\mathtt{D}},\bm{\tau}^{\mathtt{D}}\right)+\theta\,\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma},1\right)\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\tau},1\right),

with
\theta=1
if a no-slip boundary condition is imposed everywhere on
\Gamma
(namely, if
\Gamma_{D}=\Gamma
) and
\theta=0
otherwise. We point out that, in the case
\theta=1
, testing problem (
2.3
) with
\bm{\tau}=\bm{I}
gives
\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}),1\right)_{\Omega}=0
, which corresponds to the zero mean value restriction on the pressure. We are then opting for a variational insertion of this condition in order to free the energy space
X=H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})
from this constraint.

Theorem 2.1
.

The variational problem (
2.3
) admits a unique solution and there exists a constant
C>0
, depending
\Omega
and
\kappa
, such that

\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert_{H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}\leq C\lVert\bm{f}\rVert_{0,\Omega}.

Proof.

Let us first notice that, by virtue of

\left\lVert\bm{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}=\left\lVert\bm{\tau}^{\mathtt{D}}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\tfrac{1}{d}\left\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lVert\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}\leq d\,\left\lVert\bm{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega},

the bilinear form defining the variational problem (
2.3
) is bounded:

\left|a(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})+\big(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\big)\right|\leq\max\{\tfrac{1}{2}+\theta d|\Omega|,\kappa_{+}\}\lVert\bm{\sigma}\rVert_{H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}\,\lVert\bm{\tau}\rVert_{H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}\quad\forall\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau}\in X.

Moreover, as a consequence of the Poincaré–Friedrichs inequalities (see, e.g.,
[
7
, Proposition 9.1.1]
)

\left\lVert\bm{\tau}^{\mathtt{D}}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}^{2}\geq\alpha\left\lVert\bm{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega},\quad\forall\bm{\tau}\in H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S}),\quad\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\tau},1\right)=0,

(2.4)

and
(see
[
14
, Lemma 2.4]
)

\left\lVert\bm{\tau}^{\mathtt{D}}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}^{2}\geq\alpha\left\lVert\bm{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)},\quad\forall\bm{\tau}\in H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S}),

(2.5)

the bilinear form is also coercive on
X
and the well-posedness of (
2.3
) is a consequence of Lax–Milgram Lemma. Indeed, if
\Gamma_{N}
has positive surface measure (which corresponds to
\theta=0
) the coercivity of the bilinear form follows directly from (
2.5
). In the case
\Gamma_{D}=\Gamma
(and
\theta=1
), we can take advantage of the
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})
-orthogonal decomposition
\bm{\tau}=\bm{\tau}_{0}+\frac{1}{d|\Omega|}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\tau},1\right)\bm{I}
and the properties
\bm{\tau}^{\mathtt{D}}=(\bm{\tau}_{0})^{\mathtt{D}}
,
\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{0}=\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}
and
\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{0},1\right)=0
to deduce the coercivity from (
2.4
) as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\bm{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}&=\left\lVert\bm{\tau}_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\frac{1}{d|\Omega|}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\tau},1\right)^{2}+\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\Big(\left\lVert(\bm{\tau}_{0})^{\mathtt{D}}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}\Big)\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{d|\Omega|}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\tau},1\right)^{2}+\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}\leq\beta\left(a(\bm{\tau},\bm{\tau})+\left\lVert\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}\right),\end{split}

(2.6)

for all
\bm{\tau}\in H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})
, with
\beta=\max\left\{(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})\frac{1}{\kappa_{-}},\frac{2}{\alpha},\frac{1}{d|\Omega|}\right\}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Once the stress tensor
\bm{\sigma}
is known, the remaining variables can be recovered from

\bm{u}=\frac{\kappa}{\mu}\big(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}+\bm{f}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad p=-\tfrac{1}{d}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}.

(2.7)

Moreover, we point out that testing (
2.3
) with
\bm{\tau}=\phi\bm{I}
, with
\phi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
smooth and compactly supported in
\Omega
we readily deduce the incompressibility condition
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{u}=0
in
\Omega
.

3
Auxiliary results concerning discretization

From now on, we assume that there exists a polygonal/polyhedral disjoint partition
\big\{\Omega_{j},\ j=1,\ldots,J\big\}
of
\bar{\Omega}
such that
\kappa|_{\Omega_{j}}:=\kappa_{j}
constant, for all
j=1,\ldots,J
.

We consider a sequence
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h}
of shape regular meshes that subdivide the domain
\bar{\Omega}
into triangles/tetrahedra
K
of diameter
h_{K}
. The parameter
h:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\{h_{K}\}
represents the mesh size of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We assume that
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is aligned with the partition
\bar{\Omega}=\cup_{j=1}^{J}\bar{\Omega}_{j}
and that
\mathcal{T}_{h}(\Omega_{j}):=\left\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h};\ K\subset\Omega_{j}\right\}
is a shape regular mesh of
\bar{\Omega}_{j}
for all
j=1,\cdots,J
and all
h
.

For all
s\geq 0
, we consider the broken Sobolev space

H^{s}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j}):=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega);\ v|_{\Omega_{j}}\in H^{s}(\Omega_{j}),\ \forall j=1,\ldots,J\right\},

corresponding to the partition
\bar{\Omega}=\cup_{j=1}^{J}\bar{\Omega}_{j}
.
Its vectorial and tensorial versions are denoted
H^{s}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
H^{s}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{M})
, respectively. Likewise, the broken Sobolev space with respect to the subdivision of
\bar{\Omega}
into
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is

H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h},E):=\left\{\bm{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega,E):\quad\bm{v}|_{K}\in H^{s}(K,E)\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},\quad\text{for $E\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{M}\}$}.

For each
\bm{v}:=\{\bm{v}_{K}\}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\bm{\tau}:=\{\bm{\tau}_{K}\}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})
the components
\bm{v}_{K}
and

represent the restrictions

and

. When no confusion arises, the restrictions of these functions will be written without any subscript.

Hereafter, given an integer

and a domain

denotes the space of polynomials of degree at most
m
on

. We introduce the space

\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):\ v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(K),\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},

of piecewise polynomial functions relatively to
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We also consider the space
\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathcal{T}_{h},E)
of functions with values in
E
and entries in
\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, where

is either
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{M}
or
\mathbb{S}
.

Let us introduce now notations related to DG approximations of
H(\text{div})
-type spaces. We say that a closed subset
F\subset\overline{\Omega}
is an interior edge/face if
F
has a positive
(d-1)
-dimensional measure and if there are distinct elements
K
and

such that
F=\bar{K}\cap\bar{K}^{\prime}
. A closed subset

is an edge/face of

and

. We consider the set
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{0}
of interior edges/faces, the set
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}
of boundary edges/faces and let
\mathcal{F}(K):=\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h};\quad F\subset\partial K\}
be the set of edges/faces composing the boundary of

.

We assume that the boundary mesh
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}
is compatible with the partition
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma_{N}
in the sense that, if

\mathcal{F}_{h}^{D}=\left\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}:\,F\subset\Gamma_{D}\right\}
and
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{N}=\left\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}:\,F\subset\Gamma_{N}\right\},

then
\Gamma_{D}=\cup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{D}}F
and
\Gamma_{N}=\cup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{N}}F
. We denote

\mathcal{F}_{h}:=\mathcal{F}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}:=\mathcal{F}_{h}^{0}\cup\mathcal{F}_{h}^{N},

and for all

\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}=\mathcal{F}^{0}_{h}
. Finally, we introduce the set
\mathcal{F}(K):=\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h};\quad F\subset\partial K\}

.

We will need the space given on the skeletons of the triangulations

by
L^{2}(\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}):=\bigoplus_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}L^{2}(F)
. Its vector valued version is denoted

. We endow

with the inner product

.

2.3
) satisfies the following consistency property

\displaystyle\begin{split}a(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})&+\left(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{\tau}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\},\llbracket\bm{\tau}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\\[4.30554pt]
&=-\left(\kappa\bm{f},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{\tau}\right)+\left(\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\kappa\bm{f}\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\},\llbracket\bm{\tau}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}},\quad\forall\bm{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S}).\end{split}

(4.6)

Proof.

Taking into account that
\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma},1\right)=0
in the case
\Gamma_{D}=\Gamma
, and testing (
2.3
) with a tensor
\bm{\tau}:\Omega\to\mathbb{S}
whose entries are indefinitely differentiable and compactly supported in
\Omega
, we deduce that
\bm{u}:=\frac{\kappa}{{\mu}}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}+\bm{f})
satisfies
\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u})=\frac{1}{2{\mu}}\bm{\sigma}^{\mathtt{D}}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{S})
. Moreover, applying a Green’s formula in (
2.3
) yields
\bm{u}=\mathbf{0}
on
\Gamma_{D}
. Hence, by virtue of Korn’s inequality,
\bm{u}\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{\bm{v}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}):\ \bm{v}|_{\Gamma_{D}}=\mathbf{0}\right\}
, which ensures that

\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}=-\bm{f}+{\frac{\mu}{\kappa}}\bm{u}\in H^{1}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Furthermore, an integration by parts on each element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
gives

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\bm{\sigma}^{\mathtt{D}},\bm{\tau}\right)=-\left({\mu}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}),\bm{\tau}\right)=\left({\mu}\bm{u},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{\tau}\right)-\left({\mu}\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\bm{u}\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\},\llbracket\bm{\tau}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\quad\forall\bm{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S}),

and the result follows by substituting
\bm{u}:={\frac{\kappa}{\mu}}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}+\bm{f})
in the last expression.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists
\mathtt{a}_{0}>0
such that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq C{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\sigma}-\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*},

(4.7)

holds true for all
\mathtt{a}\geq\mathtt{a}_{0}
, with
C
and
\mathtt{a}_{0}
independent of
h
,
\kappa
, and
\mu
.

Proof.

We introduce
\bm{e}_{h}=\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S})
and
notice that, thanks to (
4.6
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}a(\bm{e}_{h},\bm{\tau})&+\left(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{e}_{h},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{\tau}\right)-\left(\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{e}_{h}\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\},\llbracket\bm{\tau}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\\[4.30554pt]
&-\left(\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{\tau}\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\},\llbracket\bm{e}_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}+\mathtt{a}\left(\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\llbracket\bm{e}_{h}\rrbracket,\llbracket\bm{\tau}_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}=G(\bm{\tau})\quad\forall\bm{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S}),\end{split}

(4.8)

where

G(\bm{\tau}):=-a(\bm{\sigma}-\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})-\left(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}-\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma}),\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{\tau}\right)+\left(\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}-\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\},\llbracket\bm{\tau}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}.

Taking
\bm{\tau}=\bm{e}_{h}
in (
4.8
) yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}a(\bm{e}_{h},\bm{e}_{h})&+\left\lVert\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{e}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\mathtt{a}\left\lVert\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\bm{e}_{h}\rrbracket\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}=2\left(\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{e}_{h}\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\},\llbracket\bm{e}_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}+G(\bm{e}_{h}).\end{split}

(4.9)

Let us bound now each of terms of the right-hand side of (
4.9
) by means of the Cauchy–Schwarz and Young inequalities. For the first term, proceeding as in (
4.4
) gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\left(\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{e}_{h}\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\},\llbracket\bm{e}_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{*}}\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\left\lVert\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{e}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}+4C_{\textup{tr}}^{2}\left\lVert\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\bm{e}_{h}\rrbracket\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}.\end{split}

(4.10)

Next, we estimate
G(\bm{e}_{h})
in two steps: from the one hand,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-a(\bm{\sigma}-\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma},\bm{e}_{h})-\left(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}-\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma}),\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{e}_{h}\right)\\[4.30554pt]
&\qquad\leq\tfrac{1}{2}a(\bm{e}_{h},\bm{e}_{h})+\tfrac{1}{4}\left\lVert\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{e}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\tfrac{1}{2}a(\bm{\sigma}-\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma},\bm{\sigma}-\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma})+\left\lVert\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}-\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega},\end{split}

(4.11)

and from the other hand,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}-\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\},\llbracket\bm{e}_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}&\leq\left\lVert\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\bm{e}_{h}\rrbracket\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\left\lVert\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}-\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\bm{e}_{h}\rrbracket\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}-\pi_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}.\end{split}

(4.12)

Substituting (
4.10
)-(
4.12
) in (
4.9
) and rearranging terms we deduce that (
4.7
) is satisfied if
\mathtt{a}\geq\mathtt{a}_{0}:=1+4C_{\textup{tr}}^{2}
.
∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\bm{\sigma}
and
\bm{\sigma}_{h}
be the solutions of problems (
2.3
) and (
4.1
), respectively. If
\bm{\sigma}\in H^{r+1}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{M})
, with
r\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq C\,{\sqrt{\kappa}_{+}}h^{\min\{r,k\}}{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert^{2}_{r+1,\Omega_{j}}\right)^{1/2}},\end{split}

(4.13)

for all
\mathtt{a}\geq\mathtt{a}_{0}
, with
C>0
independent of
h
,
\kappa
, and
\mu
.

Proof.

The result is a direct combination of (
4.7
) and the interpolation error estimates provided by Lemma
3.4
.
∎

5
Discrete post-processing of velocity and pressure

We recall that the velocity field and the pressure can be recovered from the stress tensor at the continuous level by

\bm{u}:={\frac{\kappa}{\mu}}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}+\bm{f})\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad p:=-\tfrac{1}{d}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}\in L^{2}(\Omega).

These same expressions permit us to reconstruct, with local and independent calculations on each element, a discrete velocity
\bm{u}_{h}
and a discrete pressure
p_{h}
that converge to their continuous counterpart at an optimal rate of convergence, as shown in the following results.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
\bm{\sigma}
and
\bm{\sigma}_{h}
be the solutions of problems (
2.3
) and (
4.1
), respectively. We introduce
\bm{u}_{h}:={\frac{\kappa}{\mu}}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}+Q^{k-1}_{h}\bm{f})
. If
\bm{\sigma}\in H^{r+1}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{M})
and
\bm{f}\in H^{r}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
r\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
h
,
\kappa
, and
\mu
such that, for all
\mathtt{a}\geq\mathtt{a}_{0}
,

\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq C{\frac{\kappa_{+}}{\mu}}\,h^{\min\{r,k\}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert^{2}_{r+1,\Omega_{j}}+\left\lVert\bm{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{r,\Omega_{j}}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

The result follows immediately from (
4.13
) and from the fact that

\left\lVert\kappa(\bm{f}-Q^{k-1}_{h}\bm{f})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\,h^{\min\{r,k\}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\kappa_{j}^{2}\left\lVert\bm{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{r,\Omega_{j}}\right)^{1/2}.

∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\bm{\sigma}
and
\bm{\sigma}_{h}
be the solutions of problems (
2.3
) and (
4.1
), respectively. We consider
p_{h}:=-\tfrac{1}{d}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}_{h}
. If
\bm{\sigma}\in H^{r+1}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{M})
with
r\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
h
and
\mu
, but depending on
\kappa_{+}/\kappa_{-}
such that, for all
\mathtt{a}\geq\mathtt{a}_{0}
,

\lVert p-p_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq C\,h^{\min\{r,k\}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert^{2}_{r+1,\Omega_{j}}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

We notice that
p-p_{h}=\tfrac{1}{d}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\bm{\sigma})\in Q
where

Q:=\begin{cases}L^{2}(\Omega)&\text{if $\Gamma_{D}\neq\Gamma$},\\
L^{2}_{*}(\Omega)=\left\{\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega):\ \left(\phi,1\right)=0\right\}&\text{if $\Gamma_{D}=\Gamma$}.\end{cases}

It follows from the well-known inf-sup condition (see for example
[
11
, Lemma 53.9]
)

\sup_{\bm{v}\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{\left(\phi,\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}\right)}{\left\lVert\bm{v}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}}\geq\delta\lVert\phi\rVert_{0,\Omega},\quad\forall\phi\in Q,

that

\delta\lVert p-p_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq\sup_{\bm{v}\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{\left(p-p_{h},\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}\right)}{\left\lVert\bm{v}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}}.

(5.1)

Using an elementwise integration by parts formula gives

\displaystyle\left(p-p_{h},\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}\right)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{d}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h})\bm{I},\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v})\right)=\left((\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h})^{\mathtt{D}},\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v})\right)-\left(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v})\right)

\displaystyle=\left((\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h})^{\mathtt{D}},\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v})\right)+\left(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}),\bm{v}\right)-\left(\bm{v},\llbracket\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}},\quad\forall\bm{v}\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Hence, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\left(p-p_{h},\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}\right)\leq\lVert(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h})^{\mathtt{D}}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lVert\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v})\rVert_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle+\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h})\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{0,\Omega},+\lVert h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\rrbracket\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\lVert h_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{v}\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}},

and the multiplicative trace inequality (
3.2
) to estimate the term
\lVert h_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{v}\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}
, we deduce that

\left(p-p_{h},\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}\right)\lesssim\Big(\lVert(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h})^{\mathtt{D}}\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h})\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\lVert h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\rrbracket\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\Big)\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{1,\Omega}.

Plugging the forgoing estimate in (
5.1
) yields

\lVert p-p_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\Big(\lVert(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h})^{\mathtt{D}}\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h})\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\lVert h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\rrbracket\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\Big),

and it follows from (
4.13
) that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
h
, but depending on
\kappa_{+}/\kappa_{-}
such that

\lVert p-p_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch^{\min\{r,k\}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert^{2}_{r+1,\Omega_{j}}\right)^{1/2},

which gives the result.
∎

We aim now to obtain a velocity field
\bm{u}_{h}^{*}\simeq\bm{u}
that preserves exactly the incompressibility condition
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{u}=0
in
\Omega
. The computational cost for such an enhancement is more demanding. For
k\geq 2
, it is achieved by solving the following elliptic problem in mixed form: Find
\bm{u}_{h}^{*}\in H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\lambda_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k-2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\bm{u}_{h}^{*},\bm{v}\right)+\left(\lambda_{h},\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}\right)&=\left(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}\right)\quad\forall\bm{v}\in H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d}),\\[4.30554pt]
\left(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{u}_{h}^{*},\eta\right)&=0\quad\forall\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{k-2}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).\end{split}

(5.2)

Clearly,
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{u}_{h}^{*}=0
in
\Omega
. We need now to estimate the error
\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}^{*}
in the
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\bm{\sigma}
and
\bm{\sigma}_{h}
be the solutions of problems (
2.3
) and (
4.1
), respectively.
If
\bm{\sigma}\in H^{r+1}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{M})
and
\bm{f}\in H^{r}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
r\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
h
,
\kappa
, and
\mu
such that, for all
\mathtt{a}\geq\mathtt{a}_{0}
,

\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{*}_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq C\,{\max\{1,\frac{\kappa_{+}}{\mu}\}}\,h^{\min\{r,k\}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert^{2}_{r+1,\Omega_{j}}+\left\lVert\bm{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{r,\Omega_{j}}\right)^{1/2},

where
\bm{u}_{h}^{*}
is obtained by solving the auxiliary problem (
5.2
).

Proof.

We begin by considering the following auxiliary problem:
Find
\bm{w}\in H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)
and
\lambda\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\bm{w},\bm{v}\right)+\left(\lambda,\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}\right)&=\left(\bm{u},\bm{v}\right)\quad\forall\bm{v}\in H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega),\\[4.30554pt]
\left(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{w},\eta\right)&=0\quad\forall\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega).\end{split}

(5.3)

We denote by
V
the kernel of the bilinear form
H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\ni(\bm{v},\eta)\mapsto\left(\eta,\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}\right)
, that is

V:=\left\{\bm{v}\in H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega):\ \mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}=0\ \text{in $\Omega$}\right\}.

The fact that
(\bm{v},\bm{v})=\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{0,\Omega}^{2}=\lVert\bm{v}\rVert^{2}_{H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}
for all
\bm{v}\in V
and the well-known inf-sup condition (cf.
[
7
]
)

\sup_{\bm{\tau}\in H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}\frac{\left(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v},\eta\right)}{\lVert\bm{\tau}\rVert_{H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}}\geq\beta\lVert\eta\rVert_{0,\Omega},\quad\forall\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega),

permit us to apply the Babuška–Brezzi theory to deduce that problem (
5.3
) is well-posed. Moreover, it can easily be seen that its unique solution is given by
\bm{w}=\bm{u}
and
\lambda=0
.

We recall that the BDM-mixed finite element pair
\left\{H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathcal{P}_{k-2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\right\}
satisfies the discrete inf-sup condition

\sup_{\bm{\tau}\in H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{\left(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v},\eta\right)}{\lVert\bm{\tau}\rVert_{H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}}\geq\beta^{\prime}\lVert\eta\rVert_{0,\Omega},\quad\forall\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{k-2}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\quad k\geq 2.

Moreover, we notice that discrete kernel
V_{h}
of the bilinear form
H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{P}_{k-2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\ni(\bm{v},\eta)\mapsto\left(\eta,\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}\right)
is a subspace of
V
since

V_{h}:=\left\{\bm{v}_{h}\in H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d}):\ \mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}_{h}=0\ \text{in $\Omega$}\right\}.

Hence, the Galerkin method based on the BDM-element and defined by:
Find
\bm{w}_{h}\in H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\lambda_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k-2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\bm{w}_{h},\bm{v}\right)+\left(\lambda_{h},\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}\right)&=\left(\bm{u},\bm{v}\right)\quad\forall\bm{v}\in H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d}),\\[4.30554pt]
\left(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{w}_{h},\eta\right)&=0\quad\forall\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{k-2}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\end{split}

(5.4)

is stable, convergent and we have the Céa estimate (recall that
\bm{w}=\bm{u}
and
\lambda=0
)

\displaystyle\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}=\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h}\rVert_{H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}\lesssim\lVert\bm{u}-\Pi_{h}^{\texttt{BDM}}\bm{u}\rVert_{H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}=\lVert\bm{u}-\Pi_{h}^{\texttt{BDM}}\bm{u}\rVert_{0,\Omega}.

We notice now that problem (
5.2
) is none other than a discretization of problem (
5.3
) obtained from the Galerkin method (
5.4
) after replacing the right-hand side
\left(\bm{u},\bm{v}\right)
by
\left(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}\right)
for all
\bm{v}\in H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, by virtue of Strang’s Lemma, it immediately holds

\displaystyle\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}=\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{*}_{h}\rVert_{H(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\bm{u}-\Pi^{BDM}_{h}\bm{u}\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}.

Finally, we deduce from (
3.13
) and Corollary
5.1
that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
h
,
\kappa
, and
\mu
such that

\lVert\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{*}_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq C\,{\max\biggl\{1,\frac{\kappa_{+}}{\mu}\biggr\}}\,h^{\min\{r,k\}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert^{2}_{r+1,\Omega_{j}}+\left\lVert\bm{f}\right\rVert^{2}_{r,\Omega_{j}}\right)^{1/2},

and the result follows.
∎

6
L^{2}
-error estimates for the stress

The analysis of this section is restricted to the case
\Gamma_{D}=\Gamma
, so that
\theta=1
,
X=H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})
and
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{*}=\mathcal{F}_{h}^{0}
. The deduction of error estimates for the stress in the
L^{2}
-norm relies on the construction of adequate approximations of the exact solution
\bm{\sigma}\in H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})
and of the discrete solution
\bm{\sigma}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S})
in the space
H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})\cap\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S})
.

One of the tools that are needed to achieve this is the averaging operator
\mathcal{A}_{h}:\,\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})\to\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})\cap H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})
defined on each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and for any
\bm{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})
, by the conditions

\displaystyle\int_{F}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\tau})\bm{n}_{K}\cdot\bm{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{F}\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\bm{\tau}\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\}_{F}\bm{n}_{K}\cdot\bm{\phi}\quad\forall\bm{\phi}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(F,\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\forall F\in\mathcal{F}(K),

(6.1a)

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\tau}:\bm{\nabla}\bm{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K}\bm{\tau}:\bm{\nabla}\bm{v}\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(K,\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(6.1b)

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\tau}:\bm{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K}\bm{\tau}:\bm{\xi}\quad\forall\bm{\xi}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(K,\mathbb{M}),\quad\text{$\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\xi}=\bm{0}$ in $K$},\quad\text{$\bm{\xi}\bm{n}=\bm{0}$ on $\partial K$}.

(6.1c)

Lemma 6.1
.

The projector
\mathcal{A}_{h}:\,\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})\to\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})\cap H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})
is uniquely characterized by the conditions (
6.1a
)-(
6.1c
) and it satisfies

\left\lVert\bm{\tau}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch\left\lVert h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\bm{\tau}\rrbracket\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{0}_{h}}\quad\forall\bm{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M}),

(6.2)

with
C>0
independent of
h
.

Proof.

See
[
24
, Proposition 5.2]
.
∎

A combination of (
6.2
) and (
4.13
) shows that, under sufficient regularity assumption on the exact solution
\bm{\sigma}\in H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})
,
\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}
converges to zero in the
L^{2}
-norm. However, it is clear that the operator
\mathcal{A}_{h}
does not preserve symmetry. To remedy this drawback, we follow
[
13
,
16
,
26
,
29
]
and use a symmetrization procedure that requires the stability the Scott–Vogelius element
[
27
]
for the Stokes problem. We refer to
[
11
, Section 55.3]
for a detailed account on the conditions (on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and on
k
) under which this stability property is guaranteed in the 2D and 3D cases.

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume that
\left\{\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}
is a stable Stokes pair on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Then, there exists a linear operator

\mathcal{S}_{h}:\,\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})\cap H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})\to\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S})\cap H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S}),

such that, for all
\bm{\tau}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})\cap H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})
,

i)

\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\bm{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}\bm{\tau}_{h})=\mathbf{0}
in
\Omega
,

ii)

and

\left\lVert\bm{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}\bm{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\left\lVert\bm{\tau}_{h}-\bm{\tau}_{h}^{\mathtt{t}}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}.

Proof.

See for example
[
29
, Lemma 5.2]
.
∎

Remark 6.1
.

The reason we are limiting the analysis of this section to the case
\Gamma_{D}=\Gamma
is due to the fact that, to the authors knowledge, the symmetrization procedure provided by operator
\mathcal{S}_{h}
has only been addressed in the literature for homogeneous Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions.

The next result shows that, under sufficient regularity assumptions on the exact solution
\bm{\sigma}
of problem (
2.3
),
\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})
and
\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})
converge to zero at optimal order in the
L^{2}
-norm.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
\bm{\sigma}
and
\bm{\sigma}_{h}
be the solutions of problems (
2.3
) and (
4.1
), respectively.
Assume that the pair
\left\{\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathcal{P}_{k}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}
is Stokes-stable on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Then, if
\bm{\sigma}\in H^{r+1}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{M})
, with
r\geq 1
, there exists
C>0
independent of
h
and
\mu
, but depending on
\kappa_{+}/\kappa_{-}
, such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq C\,h^{\min\{r+1,k+1\}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert^{2}_{r+1,\Omega_{j}}\right)^{1/2},\end{split}

(6.3)

for all
\mathtt{a}\geq\mathtt{a}_{0}
.

Proof.

We point out that, as a consequence of property ii) in Lemma
6.2
and the symmetry of
\bm{\sigma}
, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}&\leq\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\\[4.30554pt]
&\lesssim\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})^{\mathtt{t}}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}.\end{split}

(6.4)

Using this time property ii) of Lemma
6.2
in combination with the symmetry of
\bm{\sigma}_{h}
and (
6.2
) give

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}&\leq\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\\[4.30554pt]
&\lesssim\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim h\left\lVert h_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\bm{\sigma}_{h}\rrbracket\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{0}_{h}}=h\left\lVert h_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\rrbracket\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{0}_{h}}.\end{split}

(6.5)

The result follows now by using (
3.14
) in (
6.4
) and (
4.13
) in (
6.5
).
∎

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\bm{\sigma}
and
\bm{\sigma}_{h}
be the solutions of problems (
2.3
) and (
4.1
), respectively.
Assume that the pair
\left\{\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathcal{P}_{k}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}
is Stokes-stable on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Then, if
\bm{\sigma}\in H^{r+1}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{M})
, with
r\geq 1
, there exists
C>0
independent of
h
and
\mu
, but depending on
\kappa_{+}/\kappa_{-}
, such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}^{\mathtt{D}}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}^{\mathtt{D}}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq C\,h^{\min\{r+1,k+1\}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert^{2}_{r+1,\Omega_{j}}\right)^{1/2},

for all
\mathtt{a}\geq\mathtt{a}_{0}
.

Proof.

It follows from the consistency property (
4.6
) that

\displaystyle a(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\bm{\tau}_{h})+\left(\kappa(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}),\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}\right)+\left(\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\},\llbracket\bm{\sigma}_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{0}_{h}}=0,

(6.6)

for all
\bm{\tau}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S})\cap H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})
. Using an integration by parts in each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
gives

\displaystyle\left(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}_{h}\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\kappa\bm{\sigma}_{h}:\nabla(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h})+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial K}\kappa\bm{\sigma}_{h}\bm{n}_{K}\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h},

and taking advantage of (
6.1b
) yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}\right)&=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\kappa\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}:\nabla(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h})+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial K}\kappa\bm{\sigma}_{h}\bm{n}_{K}\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}\\[4.30554pt]
&=\int_{K}\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial K}(\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\bm{n}_{K}\cdot\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h},\end{split}

(6.7)

for all
\bm{\tau}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S})\cap H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})
.
Next, by construction of
\mathcal{A}_{h}
it holds

\int_{F}(\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\bm{n}_{K}\cdot\bm{\phi}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{F}\llbracket\bm{\sigma}_{h}\rrbracket_{F}\cdot\bm{\phi}\quad\forall\bm{\phi}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(F,\mathbb{R}^{d})&\text{if $F\in\mathcal{F}^{0}_{h}$},\\[4.30554pt]
0\quad\forall\bm{\phi}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(F,\mathbb{R}^{d})&\text{if $F\in\mathcal{F}^{\partial}_{h}$},\end{cases}

which means that (
6.7
) can be equivalently written

\left(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}\right)=\left(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}\right)+\left(\left\{\kern-4.30554pt\left\{\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}\right\}\kern-4.30554pt\right\},\llbracket\bm{\sigma}_{h}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{0}_{h}}.

Substituting back the last identity in (
6.6
) yields

\displaystyle a(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\bm{\tau}_{h})+\left(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}),\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}\right)=0\qquad\forall\bm{\tau}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S})\cap H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S}).

(6.8)

On the other hand, we notice that by virtue of (
3.15
) and property i) of Theorem
6.2
,

\displaystyle\left(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}),\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}),\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\kappa\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})),\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\tau}_{h}\right),

and hence, taking
\bm{\tau}_{h}=\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S})\cap H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})
in (
6.8
) gives

\displaystyle a(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}))+\left\lVert\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}))\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}=0.

(6.9)

Consequently,

\displaystyle a(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h})

\displaystyle=a\left(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right)+a\left(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+a\left(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})-\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right)

\displaystyle\leq a\left(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right)+a\left(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})-\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right),

and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality implies that

a(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}.

(6.10)

The result follows from (
6.3
) and from the fact that
\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}),1\right)=0
.
∎

It only remains now to show that the convergence of
p_{h}:=-\tfrac{1}{d}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}_{h}
to
p:=-\tfrac{1}{d}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\bm{\sigma}
is also enhanced by one order in the
L^{2}
-norm, where where
\bm{\sigma}
and
\bm{\sigma}_{h}
are the solutions of problems (
2.3
) and (
4.1
), respectively.

Corollary 6.2
.

Assume that
\left\{\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathcal{P}_{k}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}
is a stable Stokes pair on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. If
\bm{\sigma}\in H^{r+1}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{M})
, with
r\geq 1
, there exists
C>0
independent of
h
and
\mu
, but depending on
\kappa_{+}/\kappa_{-}
, such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert p-p_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq C\,h^{\min\{r+1,k+1\}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}\right\rVert_{r+1,\Omega_{j}},

for all
\mathtt{a}\geq\mathtt{a}_{0}
.

Proof.

Using the triangle inequality and (
6.10
) yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}a(\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}),\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}))^{\frac{1}{2}}&\lesssim a(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\[4.30554pt]
&+\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\\[4.30554pt]
&\lesssim\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega},\end{split}

(6.11)

which permits us to deduce from (
6.9
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}))\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}&\leq a\big(\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h}),\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}a\big(\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h},\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\[4.30554pt]
&\lesssim\Big(\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\Big)^{2}.\end{split}

(6.12)

Consequently, it follows from (
6.11
), (
6.12
) and the coercivity property (
2.6
) that

\left\lVert\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}.

Finally, by virtue of the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}\left\lVert p-p_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\bm{\Pi}^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\bm{\sigma})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\bm{\sigma}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega},

and the result follows from (
6.3
).
∎

We remark that the limit case of
\kappa=0
cannot be studied with the present formulation. Moreover, while for the other limit case of
\mu=0

2D maze and channel flow with heterogeneous permeability.

Now we focus on two different geometries. For the first 2D example we consider a maze-shaped geometry of length 2.2 and height 1 (in adimensional units). We use an unstructured simplicial barycentric trisected grid with 66006 elements, representing, for
k=1
, a total of 594054 degrees of freedom. The rightmost segment of the boundary is the outlet (
\Gamma_{N}
), where the outflow condition
\bm{\sigma}\bm{n}=\bm{0}
is imposed. The remainder of the boundary is
\Gamma_{D}
, split between the leftmost segment of the boundary (the inlet, where we impose the parabolic profile
\bm{g}_{\mathrm{in}}=(100(y-0.45)(0.55-y),0)^{\tt t}
) and the walls where
\bm{g}_{\mathrm{wall}}=\bm{0}
. The external force is zero, the viscosity is
\mu=10^{-6}
, and the permeability
\kappa(\bm{x})
is characterized by a non-homogeneous field taking the value
\kappa_{+}=10^{-5}
everywhere on the domain, except on 60 small disks distributed randomly, where the permeability smoothly goes down to the much smaller value
\kappa_{-}=10^{-10}
:

\kappa(\bm{x})=\max(\kappa_{+}(1-\sum_{i=1}^{60}\exp[-800\{(x-q_{x}(i))^{2}+(y-q_{y}(i))^{2}\}]),\kappa_{-}),

where
(q_{x}(i),q_{y}(i))
denote the coordinates of the randomly located points.
The stabilization parameter is
\mathtt{a}^{*}=15
. Figure
7.1
displays the permeability field, the Cauchy stress magnitude, the line integral convolutions of post-processed velocity, and the distribution of post-processed pressure, where we observe that the expected symmetry of the flow is disrupted by the heterogeneous permeability. These results indicate well-resolved approximations.

\mathtt{a}^{*}=25
and
\mu=10^{-6}\,[\text{KPa}\cdot\text{s}]
. On the inlet (the left segment of the boundary)
we impose the inflow velocity

3D flow through porous media.

To close this section, in this test we use a cylindrical geometry of radius 0.2 [m] and height 0.7 [m], discretized into an unstructured simplicial barycentric quadrisected mesh of 48756 tetrahedra. We set the viscosity to the value of water
\mu=8.9\cdot 10^{-4}\,[\mathrm{Pa}\cdot\mathrm{s}]
and again use inlet (the face
z=0
), wall (the surface
r=0.2
[m]), and outlet (the face
z=0.7
[m]) type of boundary conditions mimicking a channel flow with homogeneous right-hand side in the momentum balance
\bm{f}=\bm{0}
. The inlet velocity is
\bm{g}_{\mathrm{in}}=(0,0,1)^{\tt t}\,[\mathrm{m/s}]
. A synthetic permeability field is generated based on a porosity of approximately 0.4. For this we generate a normalized uniform random distribution, apply a Gaussian filter with standard deviation of 2, and then use a quantile of 40%. The resulting field is rescaled between
\kappa_{-}=10^{-12}\,[\text{m}^{2}]
and
\kappa_{+}=10^{-4}\,[\text{m}^{2}]
. Figure
7.3
presents the permeability and contour iso-surfaces of velocity magnitude, which indicate the formation of wormhole-like structures with higher velocity.

A simple analytical model of magnetic jets A simple analytical model of magnetic jets – References 2022
Andrzej A. Zdziarski, Łukasz Stawarz, Marek Sikora, Krzysztof Nalewajko

Abstract
We propose a simple analytical jet model of magnetic jets, in which radially-averaged profiles of main physical quantities are obtained based on conservation laws and some results of published GRMHD jet simulations. We take into account conversion of the magnetic energy flux to bulk acceleration in jets formed around rotating black holes assuming the mass continuity equation and constant jet power, which leads to the Bernoulli equation. For assumed profiles of the bulk Lorentz factor and the radius, this gives us the profile of the toroidal magnetic field component along the jet. We then consider the case where the poloidal field component is connected to a rotating black hole surrounded by an accretion disc. Our formalism then recovers the standard formula for the power extracted from a rotating black hole. We find that the poloidal field strength dominates over the toroidal one in the comoving frame up to large distances, which means that jets should be more stable to current-driven kink modes. The resulting magnetic field profiles can then be used to calculate the jet synchrotron emission.



1
Introduction

Accreting black holes (BHs) of all sizes produce relativistic jets. The physical mechanisms producing the jets have been subject of intense research. The most promising mechanisms for the jet production appear to be those linked to magnetic fields
(
Blandford &
Znajek 1977
;
Blandford &
Payne 1982
)
. These models also predict the jet power to scale with the BH mass (e.g.,
Heinz &
Sunyaev 2003
;
Tchekhovskoy et al. 2009
;
Tchekhovskoy et al. 2010
;
Tchekhovskoy et al. 2011
).

The observed power of extragalactic jets is often found to be very large,
\gtrsim\dot{M}_{\rm accr}c^{2}
, where
\dot{M}_{\rm accr}
is the accretion rate (e.g.,
Ghisellini et al. 2014
;
Zamaninasab et al. 2014
;
Pjanka
et al. 2017
). The only model capable to account for those powers appears to be that with electromagnetic extraction of the spin power
(
Blandford &
Znajek 1977
)
from a BH surrounded by a magnetically arrested disc (MAD;
Bisnovatyi-Kogan & Ruzmaikin 1974
;
Narayan et al. 2003
), as demonstrated using GRMHD numerical simulations by
Tchekhovskoy et al. 2011
and
McKinney et al. 2012
.

The structure of relativistic jets undergoing simultaneous acceleration and collimation has been investigated by complex semi-analytical relativistic MHD models, see
Li
et al. 1992
,
Vlahakis &
Königl 2003
,
Beskin &
Nokhrina 2006
,
Lyubarsky 2009
. Numerical simulations with relativistic MHD have been performed by
Komissarov et al. 2007
;
Komissarov et al. 2009
. Then, numerical GRMHD simulations have been done in a number of papers, see
Tchekhovskoy 2015
and
Davis &
Tchekhovskoy 2020
for reviews. Here, we provide a simple analytical formalism reproducing main aspects of those models. We assume the continuity and energy conservation equations, and use the relationship between the toroidal and poloidal components of the magnetic field for the case of spin-power extraction based on the numerical simulations of
Tchekhovskoy et al. 2009
. However, we do not require the presence of a MAD.

Our model predicts the profiles of the radially-averaged toroidal and poloidal field components along the jet. We find the poloidal component to dominate over the toroidal one in the comoving frame up to large distances, which follows from the conversion of the toroidal field to the bulk acceleration taking place in the acceleration and collimation zone (ACZ). These profiles can then be used to calculate self-absorbed synchrotron emission from extended jets to model their flat radio spectra. Such models are generally based on the works of
Blandford &
Königl 1979
and
Königl 1981
, with a later formulation
(
Zdziarski et al. 2019
)
of the synchrotron emission and self-absorption for arbitrary profiles of the bulk Lorentz factor,
\Gamma(z)
, the jet radius,
r(z)
, and the magnetic field strength, where
z
is the distance from the BH centre.

The jet structure

The jet is launched from close vicinity of the BH and it first goes through the ACZ. Details of these processes are poorly understood. The jet radial profile depends sensitively on the profile of the external pressure, which is usually uncertain. Still, those profiles can be derived
(
Vlahakis &
Königl 2003

\Gamma(z)

\Gamma_{\rm max}

We assume the conservation of the rest mass-flow rate,

\dot{M}_{\rm j}=2\pi r^{2}\rho^{\prime}c\beta\Gamma,

If most of the rest energy is in ions, it is supplied to the jet by the accretion flow from large distances. Then, the expression of the usable power would have the factor
\Gamma(\Gamma-1)
instead of
\Gamma^{2}
in the first term above

The Poynting flux in an ideal-MHD jet (i.e., with the electric vector perpendicular to the magnetic one,
{\mathbf{E}}\cdot{\mathbf{B}}=0
) is
(1/4\pi){\mathbf{B}}\times({\mathbf{v}}\times{\mathbf{B}})
. In a given point of the jet, this yields the energy-flow rate component parallel to the flow as
(1/4\pi)(\beta cB_{\phi}^{2}-v_{\phi}B_{\rm p}B_{\phi})
where
B_{\phi}
and
B_{\rm p}
are the toroidal and poloidal field components in the local observer frame (hereafter the BH frame), respectively, and
v_{\phi}
is the particle rotation velocity taken at an average jet radius. Hereafter, we neglect the effect of jet rotation, given it is slow beyond the Alfvénic region, and the radiation contribution from that region is at most small, and thus we neglect the (negative) component of the jet power due to the poloidal field. Given that

B_{\phi}=\Gamma B^{\prime}_{\phi},\quad B_{\rm p}=B^{\prime}_{\rm p},

(7)

we obtain equation (
6
). Then, the jet magnetisation parameter, defined as the ratio of the Poynting flux to the rest-energy flux, is

\sigma\equiv\frac{{B^{\prime}_{\phi}}^{2}}{4\pi\rho^{\prime}c^{2}}.

(8)

By dividing the expression for the jet power by that for the rest-mass flow, we obtain the Bernoulli equation, expressing conversion of the magnetic energy into the bulk motion. Hereafter, we assume the contribution from the internal enthalpy of the particles, i.e., the second term in the right-hand side of equation (
6
), to be small. This is often found to be satisfied in modelling individual jet sources, see, e.g.,
Ghisellini et al. 2014
,
Zdziarski et al. 2022b
. This also justifies the neglect of the internal energy density term in the denominator of equation (
8
). Thus, the expression for the magnetisation parameter following from the Bernoulli equation becomes

\sigma(z)=\frac{\Gamma_{\rm max}\left(1+\sigma_{\rm min}\right)}{\Gamma(z)}-1,

(9)

where a decreasing
\sigma
corresponds to a conversion of the Poynting flux into the jet acceleration, and
\sigma_{\rm min}
is achieved at
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\rm max}
. The jet power is then,

P_{\rm j}=\dot{M}_{\rm j}c^{2}\left(1+\sigma_{\rm min}\right)\Gamma_{\rm max}.

(10)

Note that this includes the
\dot{M}_{\rm j}c^{2}
term supplied by the accretion flow. We then use equations (
5
), (
8
) and (
9
) to calculate the (square-averaged) toroidal magnetic field strength along the jet,

{B^{\prime}_{\phi}}^{2}(z)=\frac{2\dot{M}_{\rm j}c\sigma(z)}{r(z)^{2}\beta(z)\Gamma(z)}.

(11)

Note that since the jet is accelerated at the expense of the enthalpy flux of the toroidal component of the magnetic field,
{B^{\prime}_{\phi}}
decreases in the ACZ faster than
\propto r(z)^{-1}
, by
(\sigma/\Gamma)^{1/2}
. We note that the formulation of equations (

–

follows uniquely from the assumed profiles of
\Gamma(z)
and
r(z)
and the assumption that the acceleration is at the expense of the magnetic energy, and it is independent of the details of this process. An additional assumption is that the internal energy is small compared to the rest energy, but in most actual models it is at most comparable.

The minimum value of the jet magnetisation parameter,
\sigma_{\rm min}
, is achieved in the conical part of the jet. We consider here two assumptions about this parameter,

\sigma_{\rm min}=\begin{cases}(\Theta_{0}\Gamma_{\rm max}/s)^{2}=\left[a_{r}a_{\Gamma}/(sq_{2})\right]^{2},&{\rm case\ I};\cr{\rm free},&{\rm case\ II},\cr\end{cases}\

(12)

Tchekhovskoy et al. 2009
and
Komissarov et al. 2009
, where
s
is constrained by causality to
\lesssim 1

\Theta_{0}\Gamma_{\rm max}

\sigma_{\rm min}

\sigma_{\rm min}
(and

\sigma\gtrsim 1

Zdziarski et al. 2015

Pjanka
et al. 2017

In order to find the evolution of the jet poloidal magnetic field,
B^{\prime}_{\rm p}
, along the outflow, we use the conservation of the jet magnetic flux,
\Phi_{B}\equiv 2\pi\int_{0}^{r(z)}{\rm d}r^{\prime}B^{\prime}_{\rm p}(r^{\prime})

r^{\prime}
denotes the running radius of the jet cross section,

B^{\prime}_{\phi}\mathrel{\hbox{\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\propto$}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}z^{-(1/q_{1}+1/q_{2})}
,
B_{\phi}\mathrel{\hbox{\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\propto$}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}}z^{-1/q_{2}}
,
B^{\prime}_{\rm p}\propto z^{-2/q_{2}}
in the ACZ, and
B^{\prime}_{\phi}\propto B_{\phi}\propto z^{-1}
,
B^{\prime}_{\rm p}\propto z^{-2}
in the conical part. All components of
B
are
\propto(P_{\rm j}/M)^{1/2}M^{-1/2}
. The kink in the dependencies for
B_{\phi}
and
B^{\prime}_{\phi}
is artefact of the assumption that the jet stops its acceleration sharply at
z_{\rm t}
, which results in the derivative of
\Gamma(z)
being not continuous. Similar plots are shown in
Vlahakis &
Königl 2003
and
Komissarov et al. 2009
for parameters relevant to gamma-ray bursts. As we noted above, they found
B^{\prime}_{\rm p}=B^{\prime}_{\phi}
to take place in the ACZ, which difference is related to our specific assumptions of equations (
1
–
4
) as well as to their assumed
\sigma_{\rm min}\approx 1
. We also note that equation (
4
) gives the opening angle of
\Theta_{0}=a_{r}a_{\Gamma}/(q_{2}\Gamma_{\rm max})
, which can be large for a low
\Gamma_{\rm max}
. This is then in contrast to the observations of accreting BHs in stellar binary systems, where low values of both
\Gamma_{\rm max}
and
\Theta_{0}
are commonly observed
(
Miller-Jones et al. 2006
;
Zdziarski et al. 2022a
)
. This discrepancy can be resolved assuming a low enough
a_{\Gamma}
.

We then compare our obtained value of the poloidal magnetic field strength in the jet close to the horizon to that in a MAD disc,
B_{z}
. We follow the estimate of the MAD magnetospheric radius of equation (1) of
Xie &
Zdziarski 2019
,

B_{z}^{2}\approx\frac{r_{\rm g}\dot{M}_{\rm accr}c^{2}}{r_{\rm disc}^{3}v_{r}h_{\rm disc}/r_{\rm disc}}\approx\frac{r_{\rm g}P_{\rm j}}{r_{\rm disc}^{3}v_{r}a_{*}^{2}0.3h_{0.3}^{2}},

(21)

where in the second equality we used the jet power of equation (
20
) at the maximum flux and neglecting the numerical coefficient of 1.7, and
v_{r}
is the accretion radial velocity. We note that
v_{r}
will not be equal that for a usual accretion disc, given the ‘choked’ nature of MAD accretion. We then compare this estimate at
r_{\rm disk}=r=r_{\rm H}
to the
B^{\prime}_{\rm p}
of equation (
15
). We find that the equality is obtained for

(v_{r}/c)h_{0.3}^{2}\approx(\ell/5)(r_{\rm H}/r_{\rm g}),

(22)

which requires
h_{0.3}^{2}v_{r}/c\sim 0.1
. A similar estimate was done in
Tchekhovskoy 2015
.

3
Summary and discussion

We presented a simple analytical model of magnetized jets. We parametrized the ACZ by profiles of the bulk Lorentz factor and radius [
\Gamma(z)
and
r(z)
, equations (
1
) and (
2
), respectively], and assumed the jet is conical and at a constant speed beyond the ACZ. However, our general results do not depend on the specific form of
\Gamma(z)
and
r(z)
. We then assumed a constant mass flow rate through the jet, equation (
5
), which is equivalent to assuming the jet is mass-loaded at its base. Next, we assumed the jet power is conserved, which corresponds to the radiated power being
\ll P_{\rm j}
, which is often satisfied. Given the above assumptions, we obtained the profiles of the radius-averaged magnetization parameter and the toroidal component of the magnetic field strength, with an additional assumption of the internal particle density being less than the rest energy density. We then related the poloidal component of the magnetic field to the toroidal one assuming the rotation of the magnetic field lines related to the BH angular velocity, and took into account the radial profiles of the field components being not uniform. This gave us the conserved magnetic flux. This flux, when used in the formula for the BH spin-extraction power, yields then the initially assumed jet power, showing the self-consistency of our approach. The main free parameters of our model are
P_{\rm j}
,
\Gamma_{\rm max}
and
\sigma_{\rm min}
.

Using the above formulation, we found that the poloidal component of the magnetic field can dominate up to one order of magnitude beyond the length,
z_{\rm t}
, of the ACZ, see equation (
16
) and Fig.
1
. The cause of this effect is the conversion of the toroidal magnetic field component into the bulk acceleration in the ACZ, which results in
B^{\prime}_{\phi}\propto r^{-1}(\sigma/\Gamma)^{1/2}
, which yields in turn the decline with the radius a significantly faster than
r^{-1}
. This effect was already noticed in
Vlahakis &
Königl 2003
and
Komissarov et al. 2009
. Furthermore, we stress that the detailed profiles depend on the profiles of
\Gamma(z)
and
r(z)
, and the
B^{\prime}_{\rm p}
–
B^{\prime}_{\phi}
equipartition may occur at lower values of
z
than those shown in Fig.
1
. Still, we note the overall dependence on the assumed
\Gamma(z)
and
r(z)
is relatively weak. At the base of the jet, we have
\Gamma\sim 1
and
r\sim r_{\rm H}
while in the conical part we have
\Gamma_{\rm max}\gg 1
and
\sigma_{\rm min}<1
. The profiles of
B^{\prime}_{\phi}
and
B^{\prime}_{\rm p}
have to then smoothly join the two regimes.

We have made several assumptions, listed above. While some of them correspond to the standard magnetic jet model, e.g.,
Tchekhovskoy et al. 2009
, the presented formalism provides a framework in which these assumptions can be modified. E.g., we can modify equation (
5
) to allow for mass loading along the jet, or modify equation (
6
) to account for radiative losses. We can also modify the angular velocity of the field lines to that of the model of the jet powered by the magnetic fields attached to the accretion disc
(
Blandford &
Payne 1982
)
. Furthermore, we could assume the geometric profile of
r(z)
and could calculate the profile of
\Gamma(z)
using the momentum conservation along a representative streamline (e.g., assuming self-similarity). However, this would involve divergence of tensors, and can be done only for individual stream lines requiring the knowledge of the values of
B^{\prime}_{\phi}
and
B^{\prime}_{\rm p}
along them. This would significantly complicate the presented formalism (cf.
Vlahakis &
Königl 2003
).

We then consider the jet stability for the
m=1
kink modes driven by poloidal electric current, see
Appl
et al. 2000
. In the force-free case with the magnetic pitch,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\equiv r^{\prime}B^{\prime}_{\rm p}(r^{\prime})/B^{\prime}_{\phi}(r^{\prime})
, independent of
r^{\prime}
(as assured by equation
13
), the growth time scale for a linear perturbation is
\tau_{\rm kink}^{\prime}\simeq 7.5\mathcal{P}^{\prime}/v_{\rm A,p}^{\prime}
, where
v_{\rm A,p}^{\prime}=c\sqrt{\sigma_{\rm p}/(1+\sigma_{\rm p})}
is the comoving-frame Alfvén speed at the jet axis and
\sigma_{\rm p}={B^{\prime}_{\rm p}}^{2}(r^{\prime}=0)/(4\pi\rho^{\prime}c^{2})
. With equation (
13
),
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\approx 2\beta\Gamma r_{\rm H}/(a_{*}\ell)
, and then

\tau^{\prime}_{\rm kink}(z)\approx\frac{15\beta\Gamma r_{\rm H}}{a_{*}\ell v_{\rm A,p}^{\prime}}.

(23)

We can then calculate the growth of the perturbation amplitude,
E^{\prime}_{1}
,
of the electric field along the jet,

\int{\rm d}\ln E^{\prime}_{1}=\int_{z_{\rm m}}^{z}{\rm d}z^{\prime}/[\Gamma(z^{\prime})\beta(z^{\prime})c\tau_{\rm kink}^{\prime}(z^{\prime})].

(24)

We note that
\tau_{\rm kink}^{\prime}
depends on the value of
B_{\rm p}^{\prime}(r^{\prime}=0)
, which may be divergent according to the profiles adopted in equation (
14
). In such case, we approximate
v_{\rm A,p}^{\prime}\approx c
, although we note that in reality the profile of
B_{\rm p}^{\prime}(r^{\prime})
should approach a finite value at
r^{\prime}=0
, which would lower
v_{\rm A,p}^{\prime}
and increase
\tau_{\rm kink}^{\prime}
. The numerical solutions to equation (
24
), which we include in the bottom panels of Fig.
1
correspond to the fastest growth of perturbation amplitude allowed by the functions
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(z)
and
\Gamma(z)
. We find that the perturbation amplitude shows two phases – an initial phase of slow growth followed by a phase of fast growth. The transition between these two phases corresponds roughly to the distance at which
B_{\phi}^{\prime}
starts to dominate over
B_{\rm p}^{\prime}
, which is usually somewhat beyond
z_{\rm t}
.

In M87,
Asada &
Nakamura 2012
and
Asada et al. 2014
found
q_{2}\approx 1.7
and
q_{1}\approx q_{2}/(q_{2}-1)\approx 2.4
, respectively, and
z_{\rm t}\approx 2\times 10^{5}r_{\rm g}
. However, the initial acceleration was found to be in the non-relativistic regime, and the above power-law dependence of
\Gamma
was reproduced only for
z\gtrsim 2\times 10^{4}r_{\rm g}
. Then,
Hada et al. 2013
found
q_{2}\approx 1.8
at
z\gtrsim 200r_{\rm g}
, in an approximate agreement with
Asada &
Nakamura 2012
, but they found a steeper profile with
q_{2}\approx 1.3
at lower
z
. These results point out to further complexity of the jet shape and velocity profile in observed sources. That, however, affects only our equations (
1
–
3
) and the relation between
z_{\rm t}
and
\Gamma_{\rm max}
, which could be accounted for in a future work.

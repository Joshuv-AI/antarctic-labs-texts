Eigenvalues, eigenvector-overlaps, and regularized Fuglede–Kadison determinant of the non-Hermitian matrix-valued Brownian motion
Syota Esaki, Makoto Katori, Satoshi Yabuoku

Abstract
The non-Hermitian matrix-valued Brownian motion is the stochastic process of a random matrix whose entries are given by independent complex Brownian motions. The bi-orthogonality relation is imposed between the right and the left eigenvector processes, which allows for their scale transformations with an invariant eigenvalue process. The eigenvector-overlap process is a Hermitian matrix-valued process, each element of which is given by a product of an overlap of right eigenvectors and that of left eigenvectors. We derive a set of stochastic differential equations (SDEs) for the coupled system of the eigenvalue process and the eigenvector-overlap process and prove the scale-transformation invariance of the obtained SDE system. The Fuglede–Kadison (FK) determinant associated with the present matrix-valued process is regularized by introducing an auxiliary complex variable. This variable is necessary to give the stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) for the time-dependent random field defined by the regularized FK determinant and for its squared and logarithmic variations. Time-dependent point process of eigenvalues and its variation weighted by the diagonal elements of the eigenvector-overlap process are related to the derivatives of the logarithmic regularized FK-determinant random-field. We also discuss the PDEs obtained by averaging the SPDEs. Keywords non-Hermitian matrix-valued Brownian motion, eigenvalue process, eigenvector-overlap process, scale-transformation invariance, regularized Fuglede–Kadison determinant, stochastic partial differential equations, time-dependent point processes Mathematics Subject Classification 60B20; 60H10; 60H15; 60G55

\displaystyle\langle dM_{jk},dM_{\ell m}\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\langle dM_{jk},d\overline{M_{\ell m}}\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=\delta_{j\ell}\delta_{km}\frac{dt}{N},\quad 1\leq j,k,\ell,m\leq N,\,t\geq 0.

(1.3)

2.1
Holomorphic functions of a matrix

We consider a deterministic complex matrix

\mathsf{m}=(m_{jk})_{1\leq j,k\leq N}=(m_{jk}^{{\rm R}}+im_{jk}^{\rm I})_{1\leq j,k\leq N}\in\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}},

where

m_{jk}^{{\rm R}},m_{jk}^{\rm I}\in\mathbb{R},1\leq j,k\leq N
.
We define the closed set
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}}
defined by

\Omega=\Big\{\mathsf{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}}:\ \text{there is degeneracy in the eigenvalues of $\mathsf{m}$.}\Big\}.

For complex numbers
z=x+iy,z^{\prime}=x^{\prime}+iy^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
with

x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,
an ordering
z<z^{\prime}
is introduced as

z<z^{\prime}\iff\text{ $x<x^{\prime}$ or [$x=x^{\prime}$ and $y<y^{\prime}$]},

that is, we consider the lexicographic order on
\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We assume
\mathsf{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}}\setminus\Omega
.
Then, the eigenvalues

(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}

can be labeled so that they satisfy the ordering

\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots<\lambda_{N}
.
Hence, we can define the functions
\Phi_{j}:\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}}\setminus\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
:

\lambda_{j}=\Phi_{j}(\mathsf{m}),\quad 1\leq j\leq N.

For each eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq N
,
we have its right eigenvector
\bm{r}_{j}=(r_{jk})_{k=1}^{N}

and its left eigenvector
\bm{l}_{j}=(l_{jk})_{k=1}^{N}
such that

\mathsf{m}\bm{r}_{j}=\lambda_{j}\bm{r}_{j},\quad\bm{l}_{j}^{\rm t}\mathsf{m}=\lambda_{j}\bm{l}_{j}^{\rm t}.

We remark that for each
\lambda_{j}
,

1\leq j\leq N
, the right and the left eigenvalues are
not uniquely determined and

\bm{r}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathbb{C}
and
\bm{l}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathbb{C}
.
However, we can prove the following.
See also
[
37
, Proposition 4.6]
,
[
28
, Proposition 3.5]
.

Lemma 2.1

The functions

\Phi_{j}:\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}}\setminus\Omega\ni\mathsf{m}\mapsto\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
,

1\leq j\leq N
are holomorphic.
In addition, for each eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq N
,
there exist vector-valued holomorphic functions

{\bf\Psi}_{j}=(\Psi_{jk})_{k=1}^{N}:\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}}\setminus\Omega\to\mathbb{C}^{N}
and

\widehat{{\bf\Psi}}_{j}=(\widehat{\Psi}_{jk})_{k=1}^{N}:\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}}\setminus\Omega\to\mathbb{C}^{N}
such that

{\bf\Psi}_{j}(\mathsf{m})
gives one of the right eigenvectors
and
\widehat{{\bf\Psi}}_{j}(\mathsf{m})

gives one of the left eigenvectors
associated with
\lambda_{j}
, respectively.

Proof
The first claim is shown by the implicit function theorem of holomorphic functions.
The second claim is given in

[

, Section II.4]
.

Assume that the matrix

We define the matrix

The setting is summarized as follow:

\displaystyle\mathsf{s}^{-1}\mathsf{s}=\mathsf{s}\mathsf{s}^{-1}=I,\quad(\mathsf{s}^{-1})^{\dagger}\mathsf{s}^{\dagger}=\mathsf{s}^{\dagger}(\mathsf{s}^{-1})^{\dagger}=I,

\displaystyle\mathsf{s}^{-1}\mathsf{m}\mathsf{s}=\bm{\lambda}:={\rm diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}),\quad\mathsf{s}^{\dagger}\mathsf{m}^{\dagger}(\mathsf{s}^{-1})^{\dagger}=\overline{\bm{\lambda}}={\rm diag}(\overline{\lambda_{1}},\dots,\overline{\lambda_{N}}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{a}

\displaystyle:=\mathsf{s}^{\dagger}\mathsf{s},\quad\mathsf{a}^{-1}=\mathsf{s}^{-1}(\mathsf{s}^{-1})^{\dagger}.

The eigenvector-overlap matrix of the
right and the left eigenvectors

\mathsf{o}=(o_{jk})_{1\leq j,k\leq N}

(2.2)

(2.3)

It is obvious by the definitions that

Lemma 2.2

Let

\ell\not=j

\displaystyle a_{jk}^{-1}a_{k\ell}(\mathsf{s}^{-1}\mathsf{w}\mathsf{s})_{\ell j},\quad a_{kj}a^{-1}_{\ell k}(\mathsf{s}^{-1}\mathsf{w}\mathsf{s})_{j\ell},

\displaystyle a^{-1}_{jk}a_{\ell j}\overline{(\mathsf{s}^{-1}\mathsf{w}\mathsf{s})_{\ell k}},\quad a_{kj}a^{-1}_{j\ell}\overline{(\mathsf{s}^{-1}\mathsf{w}\mathsf{s})_{k\ell}}

Associated with the scale transformation (
2.3
),
we set
\widetilde{\mathsf{s}}:=(\widetilde{\bm{r}}_{1}\cdots\widetilde{\bm{r}}_{N})

\widetilde{\mathsf{a}}=(\widetilde{a}_{jk}):=\widetilde{\mathsf{s}}^{\dagger}\widetilde{\mathsf{s}}

(2.6)

By the similar calculation, we have

\ell\not=j

We note that

Therefore, the above implies the invariance
of the equalities (

2.3

The proof is hence complete. ∎

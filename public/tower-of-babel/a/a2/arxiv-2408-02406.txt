Let

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

(L_{a}^{p)}(\Omega))_{loc}

It is known by Lemma 3.1 in
[
19
]

[
21
]
that the embedding

L^{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{a}^{p)}(\Omega).

holds if and only if
a\in L^{1}(\Omega).
This implies that if
a\in L^{1}(\Omega)
then
L^{p}(\Omega)_{loc}\hookrightarrow L_{a}^{p)}(\Omega)_{loc}.

[

]
,
[

]
,

]

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
and
a(x),b(x)
be weight functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Fix a compact
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with nonempty interior. The generalized grand Wiener amalgam space
W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}\mathbb{R}^{n}))
consists of all functions ( classes of )
f\in(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))_{loc}
such that the control function

\displaystyle F^{p)}_{f,u}(x)

\displaystyle=\|f.\chi_{Q+x}\|_{L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\varepsilon\leq p-1}\varepsilon\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f(t)\chi_{Q+x}(t)\right|^{p-\varepsilon}a(t)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\varepsilon\leq p-1}\varepsilon\left\|{f.\chi_{Q+x}}\right\|_{L^{p-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n},a^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}})}

lies in
L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
The norm on
W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),
(or shortly
W(L_{a}^{p)},L_{b}^{q)}),
is

(2.1)

\|f\|_{W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}=\|F^{p)}_{f}\|_{L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\|f.\chi_{Q+x}\|_{(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|_{L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).}

Proposition 2.2
.

The generalized grand Wiener amalgam space
W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is translation and modulation invariant.

Proof.

Let
f\in W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}\mathbb{R}^{n})).
Then
f\chi_{Q+x}\in L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
F^{p)}_{f}(x)=\|f.\chi_{Q+x}\|_{L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\in L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).
It is known from Proposition 1 in
[18]
that
L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is translation invariant. Thus we have
Tyf\in L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(2.2)

\displaystyle F^{p)}_{T_{y}f}(x)=\|(T_{y}f).\chi_{Q+x}\|_{L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|f.\chi_{Q+x-y}\|_{L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=(T_{y}F^{p)}_{f})(x)

for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Again since
L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is translation invariant, from (3.1) and (3.2), we find

\displaystyle\|T_{y}f\|_{W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}=\|(T_{y}F^{p)}_{f})(x)\|_{L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty,

and so
T_{y}f\in W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).
That means
W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is translation invariant.

Now let
f\in W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{a}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),
and
\text{ }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then

\displaystyle F^{p)}_{M_{\xi}f}(x)

\displaystyle=\|({M_{\xi}f})\chi_{Q+x}\|_{(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\varepsilon\leq p-1}\varepsilon\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|e^{i\xi t}f(t)\chi_{Q+x}(t)\right|^{p-\varepsilon}a(x)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\varepsilon\leq p-1}\varepsilon\left\|{f.\chi_{Q+x}}\right\|_{L^{p-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n},a^{\frac{\varepsilon}{p}})}=\|f\chi_{Q+x}\|_{L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

By (3.3) we find

\displaystyle\|M_{\xi}f\|_{W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{a}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=\|F^{p)}_{M_{\xi}f}(x)\|_{{L_{a}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}

\displaystyle=\|\|f\chi_{Q+x}\|_{L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|_{L_{a}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{a}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}<\infty.

Thus
W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is modulation invariant.
∎

4.
The Hardy-Littlewood Maximal Operator on Generalized Grand Wiener Amalgam spaces

For a locally integrable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the (centered) Hardy- Littlewood maximal function
Mf
of
f
by

\displaystyle Mf(x)=sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{\mid B_{r}(x)\mid}\int_{B_{r}(x)}\mid f(y)\mid dy,

where
B_{r}(x)
is the open ball centered at
x.
The supremum is taken over all balls
B_{r}(x).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q\leq r<\infty
. If
a\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
then the Hardy-Littlewood Maximal Operator M

\displaystyle M:W(L^{r},L^{q})(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow W(L_{a}^{p)},L_{b}^{q)})(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is bounded

Proof.

It is known that the Hardy-Littlewood Maximal Operator M

\displaystyle M:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is bounded for
1<p<\infty
( see Theorem 1, in
[
20
]
).Thus there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|Mh\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{1}\|h\|_{L^{p}}

for all
h\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Since
a\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
then we write

(4.2)

\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L_{a}^{q)}(\Omega).

Since
p\leq q,
by the properties of Wiener amalgam space and from
(4.2)
we observe that

\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=W(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\hookrightarrow W(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\hookrightarrow W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

Hence the unit map

\displaystyle I:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

is bounded. So, there exists
C_{2}>0
such that for all
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|I(g)\|_{W(L_{a}^{p)},L_{b}^{q)})}=\|g\|_{W(L_{a}^{p)},L_{b}^{q)})}\leq C_{2}\|g\|_{L^{p}}.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Since

\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),

by (4.1), (4.3) and the hypothesis
p\leq q,
we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|Mf\|_{W(L_{a}^{p)},L_{b}^{q)})}\leq C_{2}\|Mf\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{1}C_{2}\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

Thus the Hardy-Littlewood Maximal Operator M,

\displaystyle M:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

is bounded. Since
p\leq r,
by the properties of Wiener amalgam space we have the embedding

(4.5)

\displaystyle W(L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\hookrightarrow W(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then by (4.4) and (4.5),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|Mf\|_{W(L_{a}^{p)},L_{b}^{q)})}\leq C_{1}C_{2}\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{1}C_{2}C_{3}\|f\|_{W(L^{r},L^{p})}

for some
C_{3}>0
.
Finally from (5.6) the Hardy-Littlewood Maximal Operator M,

\displaystyle M:W(L_{a}^{r)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\rightarrow W(L_{a}^{p)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L_{b}^{q)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

is bounded.

]

w(x)
next to
a(x)

\displaystyle\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{p)}_{a}(\Omega,\omega)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\varepsilon\leq p-1}\left(\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\left|f\right|^{p-\varepsilon}a(x)^{\varepsilon}\omega(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\varepsilon\leq p-1}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{p-\varepsilon}(\Omega,a^{\varepsilon}(x)\omega(x))}.

Macroscopic Fluctuation Theory for Ginzburg-Landau dynamics with long range interactions
Cédric Bernardin, Raphaël Chetrite

Abstract
Focusing on a famous class of interacting diffusion processes called Ginzburg-Landau (GL) dynamics, we extend the Macroscopic Fluctuations Theory (MFT) to these systems in the case where the interactions are long-range, and consequently, the macroscopic effective equations are described by non-linear fractional diffusion equations.

1.2
Objectives

The aim of this paper is to study the extension of the MFT framework for a certain class of lattice field models, known in the literature as Ginzburg-Landau (GL) dynamics. A convenient picture is to see them as stochastic fluctuating interfaces, see Figure
1
. The novelty of the current paper w.r.t. the existing literature is that long-range interactions are considered. The hydrodynamic equation is then governed by a fractional diffusion equation

(see Eq. (

4.2

We have also that

\sigma(\Phi)=\left\langle\phi^{2}\right\rangle_{\nu_{\Phi}}-(\left\langle\phi\right\rangle_{\nu_{\Phi}})^{2}=\cfrac{1}{S^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)}=2\Phi^{2}\ .

(6.8)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\gamma\to 2^{-}}\cfrac{2-\gamma}{2}\ \int_{[0,1]^{2}}dudv\ K(v-u)B(\Phi(u),\Phi(v))\left[S^{\prime}(\Phi(v))-S^{\prime}(\Phi(u))\right]\left[G(v)-G(u)\right]\\
&=\int_{[0,1]}du\ B(\Phi(u),\Phi(u))\ S^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\Phi(u))\ \Phi^{\prime}(u)\ G^{\prime}(u)\ ,\end{split}

which proves that, after a suitable renormalisation, the hydrodynamic (resp. hydrostatic) equation (
3.1
) (resp. (
3.12
)) converges as
\gamma\to 2^{-}
to the solution of the parabolic equation

\partial_{t}\Phi_{t}=\partial_{u}\left(D(\Phi)\ \partial_{u}\Phi\right),\quad D(\Phi)=\ S^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\Phi)B(\Phi,\Phi)>0\ ,

(resp. of the elliptic equation
0=\partial_{u}\left(D(\Phi_{ss})\ \partial_{u}\Phi_{ss}\right)
)
with Dirichlet boundary conditions provided by
\Phi_{\ell}
on the left and
\Phi_{r}
on the right. To see the previous convergence we write

\begin{split}&\int_{[0,1]^{2}}dudv\ K(v-u)B(\Phi(u),\Phi(v))\left[S^{\prime}(\Phi(v))-S^{\prime}(\Phi(u))\right]\left[G(v)-G(u)\right]\\
&=\int_{|v-u|\leq\varepsilon}dudv\ K(v-u)B(\Phi(u),\Phi(v))\left[S^{\prime}(\Phi(v))-S^{\prime}(\Phi(u))\right]\left[G(v)-G(u)\right]\\
&+\int_{|v-u|\geq\varepsilon}dudv\ K(v-u)B(\Phi(u),\Phi(v))\left[S^{\prime}(\Phi(v))-S^{\prime}(\Phi(u))\right]\left[G(v)-G(u)\right]\ ,\end{split}

where
\varepsilon\to 0
as
\gamma\to 2^{-}
such that

\lim_{\gamma\to 2^{-}}\varepsilon^{4-\gamma}=\lim_{\gamma\to 2^{-}}\varepsilon^{3-\gamma}=0\quad\text{but}\quad\lim_{\gamma\to 2^{-}}\varepsilon^{2-\gamma}=1\ .

For example we can take
\varepsilon=\exp\left(\tfrac{\log(1-\sqrt{2-\gamma})}{2-\gamma}\right)
.

In the first integral we can replace the integrand
[S^{\prime}(\Phi(v))-S^{\prime}(\Phi(u))]\ [G(v)-G(u)]
by
\tfrac{d}{dv}[S^{\prime}(\Phi(v))]|_{v=u}\ G^{\prime}(u)\ (v-u)^{2}
and the error term resulting in the integral is (by Taylor expansion) of order
\int_{|v-u|\leq\varepsilon}dudv\ |v-u|^{3-\gamma}\lesssim\varepsilon^{4-\gamma}
. The second integral can be bounded by a constant times
\int_{|v-u|\geq\varepsilon}dudv\ |v-u|^{1-\gamma}=\int_{1\geq|z|\geq\varepsilon}dz\ |z|^{1-\gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{1}du\ {\sc 1}_{0<u+z<1}\right)\leq 2\tfrac{1-\varepsilon^{2-\gamma}}{2-\gamma}
. We have to multiply these error terms by
2-\gamma
and then they disappear. Hence as
\gamma\to 2^{-}
, we are left with the convergence of

(2-\gamma)\ \int_{|v-u|\leq\varepsilon}dudv\ B(\Phi(u),\Phi(v))\ \frac{d}{du}[S^{\prime}(\Phi(u))]\ G^{\prime}(u)\ K(v-u)\ (v-u)^{2}

which can be replaced by

(2-\gamma)\ \int_{|v-u|\leq\varepsilon}dudv\ B(\Phi(u),\Phi(u))\ \frac{d}{du}[S^{\prime}(\Phi(u))]\ G^{\prime}(u)\ |v-u|^{1-\gamma}

with a cost at most of order
(2-\gamma)\varepsilon^{3-\gamma}
. The last integral is equal to

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}du\ B(\Phi(u),\Phi(u))\ \frac{d}{du}[S^{\prime}(\Phi(u))]\ G^{\prime}(u)\ \left(\int_{|z|\leq\varepsilon}dz\ |z|^{1-\gamma}{\sc 1}_{0<u+z<1}\right)\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}du\ B(\Phi(u),\Phi(u))\ \frac{d}{du}[S^{\prime}(\Phi(u))]\ G^{\prime}(u)\ \left(\int_{\sup(-u,-\varepsilon)}^{\inf(1-u),\varepsilon)}dz\ |z|^{1-\gamma}\right)\ .\end{split}

For every
u\in(0,1)
we have that

\lim_{\gamma\to 2^{-}}(2-\gamma)\ \int_{\sup(-u,-\varepsilon)}^{\inf((1-u),\varepsilon)}dz\ |z|^{1-\gamma}=\lim_{\gamma\to 2^{-}}(2-\gamma)\ \int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}dz\ |z|^{1-\gamma}=2\lim_{\gamma\to 2^{-}}\varepsilon^{2-\gamma}=2\ ,

the first equality resulting from the fact that for
\gamma
sufficiently large,
-u<-\varepsilon<\varepsilon<1-u
. By using the dominated convergence theorem, the proof is complete.

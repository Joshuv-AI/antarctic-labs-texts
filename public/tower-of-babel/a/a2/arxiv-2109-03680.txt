ON SOME GENERALIZATIONS TO FLOOR FUNCTION IDENTITIES OF RAMANUJAN
Andrzej Kukla, Sai Teja Somu

Abstract
We give some generalizations to three identities of Srinivasa Ramanujan involving greatest integer function.



1
Introduction

Let
\lfloor x\rfloor

x

Journal of the Indian Mathematical Society
. The problem (See
[
5

If

\displaystyle\quad\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{n+2}{6}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{n+4}{6}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{n+3}{6}\right\rfloor

(1)

\displaystyle\quad\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{4}}\right\rfloor

(2)

\displaystyle\quad\left\lfloor\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{n+1}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\sqrt{4n+2}\right\rfloor.

(3)

Chen in

We give few generalizations of all the identities (1), (2), (3).

Proposition 1

For all integers
a,b

a\neq 0,\ b>0
and any real number

\left\lfloor\frac{x}{a}\right\rfloor=\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}\left\lfloor\frac{x+ia}{ab}\right\rfloor.

(4)

Proposition 2
.

Let

Generalization of (1)

We begin by proving Proposition 1,

\left\lfloor\frac{x}{a}\right\rfloor=\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}\left\lfloor\frac{x+ia}{ab}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

From Hermite’s identity (See
[
4
]
), we have for any real number
t
, natural number
b
,

\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}\left\lfloor t+\frac{i}{b}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor bt\right\rfloor,

substitute
t=\frac{x}{ab}
to get the desired identity,

\left\lfloor\frac{x}{a}\right\rfloor=\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}\left\lfloor\frac{x+ia}{ab}\right\rfloor.

∎

Note that when
a=1
, it is the standard floor function identity
\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor=\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}\left\lfloor\frac{x+i}{b}\right\rfloor.

Now let us see a proof of Ramanujan’s identity (1) using Proposition 1.

2.1
Proof of (1) using Proposition 1

Proof.

Set
a=2,\ b=3
and
a=3,\ b=2
,
x=n
in Proposition 1 to get:

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{6}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{n+2}{6}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{n+4}{6}\right\rfloor,

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{n}{6}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{n+3}{6}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor.

Adding both the equations and canceling out
\lfloor\frac{n}{6}\rfloor
on both sides we get (1).
∎

2.2
Corollaries of Proposition 1

We have the following corollary of Proposition 1.

Corollary 5
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and natural number
n
the following identity is true

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n^{j+1}}+\frac{i}{n}\right\rfloor=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor&\text{for }x\geq 0\\
\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor+1&\text{for }x<0.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We start by noticing that by Proposition 1 we can rewrite the inside sum as

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n^{j+1}}+\frac{i}{n}\right\rfloor=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left\lfloor\frac{x+in^{j}}{n^{j+1}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n^{j+1}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n^{j}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n^{j+1}}\right\rfloor.

This implies that

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n^{j+1}}+\frac{i}{n}\right\rfloor=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n^{j}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n^{j+1}}\right\rfloor\right)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n^{k+1}}\right\rfloor\bigg).

As
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lfloor\frac{x}{n^{k+1}}\rfloor=0
, if
x\geq 0
and
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lfloor\frac{x}{n^{k+1}}\rfloor=-1
otherwise, we have

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left\lfloor\frac{x}{n^{j+1}}+\frac{i}{n}\right\rfloor=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor&\text{for }x\geq 0\\
\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor+1&\text{for }x<0.\end{array}\right.

∎

Corollary 5 is a generalization of question 6 from the 1968 International Math Olympiad, which can be proved by setting
n=2
, and
x=m
, when
m
is natural number

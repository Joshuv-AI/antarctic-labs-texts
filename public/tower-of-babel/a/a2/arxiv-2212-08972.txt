On parabolic partial differential equations with Hölder continuous diffusion Coefficients
Majed Sofiani

Abstract
. We investigate existence and regularity of weak solutions of a 1-dimensional parabolic differential equation with a non-constant Hölder diffusion coefficient and a rough forcing term. Such an equation appears in studying the 1-dimensional Ericksen-Leslie model for nematic liquid crystals where our result applies. The result presented here uses the Hölder continuity of the diffusion coefficient which comes from the physical background and the analysis of the Ericksen-Leslie model. Moreover, the dependence of the Hölder exponent of the solution is explicit on the Hölder exponent of the diffusion coefficient.

1.1.
Physical background and motivation

As mentioned above, this work is motivated by a study of Ericksen-Leslie model for Poiseulle flow of nematic liquid crystals. The state variables for nematics are the velocity vector
\bf u
and the director field
\bf n.

In the case where the flow moves in the
z
-direction and is uniform in
x,y
and the molecules lie on the
x-z
plane, we write the velocity field as
\mathbf{u}(x,t)=(0,0,u(x,t))^{T}
and the director field as
\mathbf{n}(x,t)=(\sin\theta(x,t),0,\cos\theta(x,t))^{T},
where
\theta
is the angle measured from the
z
axis. The system reduces to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\displaystyle u_{t}&=\left(g(\theta)u_{x}+h(\theta)\theta_{t}\right)_{x},\\
\theta_{tt}+\gamma_{1}\theta_{t}&=c(\theta)\big(c(\theta)\theta_{x}\big)_{x}-h(\theta)u_{x},\end{split}

(1.7)

where the functions
c,

g,
and
h
are explicitly given in the literature

\displaystyle\begin{split}g(\theta):=&\alpha_{1}\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta+\frac{\alpha_{5}-\alpha_{2}}{2}\sin^{2}\theta+\frac{\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{6}}{2}\cos^{2}\theta+\frac{\alpha_{4}}{2},\\
h(\theta):=&\alpha_{3}\cos^{2}\theta-\alpha_{2}\sin^{2}\theta=\frac{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\cos(2\theta)}{2},\\
c^{2}(\theta):=&K_{1}\cos^{2}\theta+K_{3}\sin^{2}\theta.\end{split}

(1.8)

In (
1.8
),
K_{1},K_{3}
are Frank’s constants for splay and bending deformation of the material and
\alpha_{j}^{\prime}s
with (
\gamma_{1}=\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{2},\,\gamma_{2}=\alpha_{6}-\alpha_{5}
) are the Leslie dynamics parameters. See
[
1
]
for a detailed discussion and derivation of the Ericksen-Leslie system for Poiseulle flows.

In
[
1
]
, a special case (
g=h=1
and
\gamma_{1}=2
) was investigated and a crucial quantity
J:=u_{x}+\theta_{t}
was realized. It turns out that
J
has better regularity than
\theta_{t}
and
u_{x}
individually. That is, each of
\theta_{t}
and
u_{x}
might blow up in finite time but
J
will always be bounded and in fact Hölder continuous. The system is written as

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(u_{x}+\theta_{t}\right)_{x},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\theta_{tt}+2\theta_{t}

\displaystyle=c(\theta)\big(c(\theta)\theta_{x}\big)_{x}-u_{x}.

(1.10)

In terms of
J,

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=J_{x},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\theta_{tt}+\theta_{t}

\displaystyle=c(\theta)(c(\theta)\theta_{x})_{x}-J.

(1.12)

To close the system, using the definition of
J
and (
1.11
), it is easy to see that

J_{t}=J_{xx}+\theta_{tt}.

Using the wave equation we obtain an equation for
J

\displaystyle J_{t}=J_{xx}+c(\theta)(c(\theta)\theta_{x})_{x}-u_{x}-\theta_{t}.

(1.13)

This parabolic PDE (
1.13
) has a corresponding parabolic PDE for the general case (
1.7
), with non-constant
g
and
h,
satisfied by a quantity
A
expressed as follows

A(x,t):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}J(z,t)\,dz

where

in this case is slightly modified,

J:=u_{x}+\frac{h}{g}\theta_{t}.

For full context of the derivation of the quantity and its equation we refer the readers to
[
2
]
.

The parabolic PDE satisfied by
A
has a similar form as equation (
1.1
) considered in this paper. We will investigate this equation in a general setup that is closely related to the situation for the system (
1.7
). The results presented here are crucial for the proof of the global existence of weak solution for the full Ericksen-Leslie model for Poiseulle flows of nematic liquid crystals in

. That is mainly because the major difference is the appearance of the function

1.2.
A relevant work from
[
6
]
and Duhamel formula.

In this subsection we recall a result from
[
6
]
on the existence of classical solutions to parabolic PDEs. The framework therein is relevant and is our starting point for the analysis in this work. The results in
[
6
]
are shown for the n-dimensional space and any Hölder exponent
0<\beta<1.
We focus on the relevant situation when
n=1
and
\beta=1/2.

Let
k(x,t)\in C^{\beta}
with respect to both variables on
\Omega_{T}.

Consider, the following homogeneous equation

\displaystyle w_{t}-k(x,t)w_{xx}+\gamma w=0.

(1.14)

Note that, for fixed
(\xi,\tau),
the function

\displaystyle H^{\xi,\tau}(x-\xi,t-\tau)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k(\xi,\tau)}\sqrt{t-\tau}}e^{-\frac{(x-\xi)^{2}}{4k(\xi,\tau)(t-\tau)}}

(1.15)

is the heat kernel with frozen coefficient
k(\xi,\tau),
that is,

H_{t}^{\xi,\tau}-k(\xi,\tau)H_{xx}^{\xi,\tau}=0.

We can write the fundamental solution
\Gamma
of (
1.14
) implicitly as follows

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,t,\xi,\tau)

\displaystyle=H^{\xi,\tau}(x-\xi,t-\tau)+\int_{\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}H^{y,s}(x-y,t-s)\Phi(y,s;\xi,\tau)\,dy\,ds,

(1.16)

where the function
\Phi
is determined by the condition that

\Gamma_{t}-k(x,t)\Gamma_{xx}+\gamma\Gamma=0.

In
[
6
]
, the author showed such function
\Phi
exists and satisfies

\displaystyle|\Phi(y,s;\xi,\tau)|\leq\frac{C}{(s-\tau)^{\frac{3-\beta}{2}}}e^{\frac{-d(y-\xi)^{2}}{4(s-\tau)}}=\frac{C}{(s-\tau)^{\frac{5}{4}}}e^{\frac{-d(y-\xi)^{2}}{4(s-\tau)}},

(1.17)

where
C
and
d
are constants depending only on
k.
We will use
C
to represent other constants that are not necessarily the same. In addition, the author showed that if
q(x,t)
is Hölder continuous on
\Omega_{T}
and
\psi_{0}(x)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
, then the solution of the following Cauchy problem

-u_{t}+k(x,t)u_{xx}-\gamma u=q(x,t),

u(x,0)=\psi_{0}(x),

is given by the Duhamel formula

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(x,t,\xi,0)\psi_{0}(\xi)\,d\xi+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(x,t;\xi.\tau)q(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau,

(1.18)

and it is a classical solution.

In addition, the following estimates were shown therein,

\displaystyle|\Gamma(x,t;\xi,\tau)|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t-\tau}}e^{-\frac{d(x-\xi)^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\approx H(x-\xi,t-\tau),

(1.19)

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{x}(x,t;\xi,\tau)|\leq\frac{C}{{t-\tau}}e^{-\frac{d(x-\xi)^{2}}{4(t-\tau)}}\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-\tau}}H(x-\xi,t-\tau),

(1.20)

where
d
is a constant depending on
k
and
\approx
means equality up to a factor depending on
k(\xi,\tau).

1.3.
The main idea of this work

For the application in our work
[
2
]
, we encounter a rougher, particularly not Hölder continuous, data for the non-homogeneous term
q(x,t).
For this reason, the result in
[
6
]
can not be applied. Nevertheless, an extension of the framework in
[
6
]
is needed in the sense of proving the existence of a weak solution in the case when a rough source data imposed to the PDE.
In addition, Hölder estimates for the weak solution and its
x
-derivative are needed for the work in
[
2
]
.
We estimate the
L^{\infty},L^{2}
and the
C^{\alpha}
norms of the solution and its derivative with respect to
x.
We use the mollifying technique to prove the existence of weak solution. Precisely, we mollify the data
f
and
G
and then pass to the limit with respect to the
L^{\infty}
norm. Briefly, if we mollify
f
and
G
we can write the solution in a classical sense following
[
6
]
. Then, using the
L^{\infty}
estimates of
u
and
u_{x}
we can take the limit of the weak formulation with respect to the mollifier parameter. It is worth mentioning that Hölder continuity of solutions to parabolic PDE’s has been extensively studied and many results have been shown, for example
[
8
,
15
]
and the references there. However, since the equation here is motivated by a concrete model as discussed before,
the result here assumes Hölder continuity of the coefficient and gives more explicit formula for the solution and also shows the dependence on the Hölder exponent of the coefficient.

Following the discussion in §
1.2
, we can represent the solution to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) as follows,

\displaystyle w(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(x,t,\xi,0)w_{0}(\xi)\,d\xi+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(x,t;\xi.\tau)[G(\xi,\tau)+f(\xi,\tau)]\,d\xi\,d\tau.

(1.21)

We will show that this representation of the solution indeed satisfies the weak formulation in Definition
1
. We will consider the three terms of the expression (
1.21
) separately and denote each term as follows,

\displaystyle W(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(x,t,\xi,0)w_{0}(\xi)\,d\xi,

\displaystyle W_{G}(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(x,t;\xi.\tau)G(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau,

and

\displaystyle W_{f}(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(x,t;\xi.\tau)f(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau.

Analogously, denote the
x
derivative of the expressions as

\displaystyle W_{x}(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{x}(x,t,\xi,0)w_{0}(\xi)\,d\xi.

\displaystyle W_{G,x}(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{x}(x,t;\xi.\tau)G(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau,

and

\displaystyle W_{f,x}(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{x}(x,t;\xi.\tau)f(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau.

The key estimates on the solution
w
are presented in the next propositions.

Proposition 1.1
.

Fix
T>0.

Under the assumptions (
1.3
)- (
1.5
), the functions
W_{G},W_{f},W_{G,x},
and
W_{f,x}
belong to the following space,

L^{\infty}\cap C^{\alpha}(\bar{\Omega}_{T}),

for any

Moreover, for any compact set

one has

\max\big\{\|W\|_{(L^{\infty}\cap C^{\alpha})(F)},\|W_{x}\|_{(L^{\infty}\cap C^{\alpha})(F)}\big\}<\infty,

and

W(x,t),W_{x}(x,t)\to w_{0}(x),w^{\prime}_{0}(x)\mbox{ point-wise as }t\to 0^{+}.

As a consequence of the first assertion in Proposition
1.1
, we have that for any constant
K>0,
there exists
\delta>0,
depending on
G,f
and
k,
sufficiently small such that

\|W_{\{\square\}}(x,t)\|_{(L^{\infty}\cap C^{\alpha})(\overline{\Omega}_{\delta})}\leq K,

for
\{\square\}:=\{G\},\{f\},\{G,x\}
and
\{f,x\}.

Due to the lack of decay of the source term
G,
we can only obtain
L^{2}
decay of the terms
W_{f},W_{f,x}
and
W_{G,x}.
More precisely,

Proposition 1.2
.

For any constant
K>0,
there exists
\delta>0,
depending on
G,f
and
k,
sufficiently small such that

\|W_{\{\circ\}}(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(\overline{\Omega}_{\delta})}\leq K,

for
\{\circ\}:=\{f\},\{f,x\},
and
\{G,x\}.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows. In Section 2, we present a proof of Proposition
1.1

1.2
in Section 4.

\displaystyle|W_{f,x}|\lesssim

(2.4)

2.2.
The

Recall,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{x}(x,t;\xi,\tau)\square(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}H_{x}^{\xi,\tau}(x-\xi,t-\tau)\square(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg[\int_{\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}H_{x}^{y,s}(x-y,t-s)\Phi(y,s;\xi,\tau)\,dy\,ds\bigg]\square(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau.

We call (

) the ’principal term’ and (

for both terms.

Starting with the principal term, we write

explicitly:

\displaystyle H_{x}^{\xi,\tau}(x-\xi,t-\tau)=-\frac{(x-\xi)}{4\sqrt{\pi}k(\xi,\tau)^{3/2}(t-\tau)^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{(x-\xi)^{2}}{4k(\xi,\tau)(t-\tau)}}.

Without loss of generality, fix

and consider the intervals

On

we use

\displaystyle\big|H_{x}(x_{2}-\xi,t-\tau)-H_{x}(x_{1}-\xi,t-\tau)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k^{3/2}_{L}}\bigg|\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{(t-\tau)^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{(x_{2}-\xi)^{2}}{4k_{U}(t-\tau)}}\bigg|+\frac{1}{k^{3/2}_{L}}\bigg|\frac{x_{1}-\xi}{(t-\tau)^{3/2}}\bigg[e^{-\frac{(x_{1}-\xi)^{2}}{4k(\xi,\tau)(t-\tau)}}-e^{-\frac{(x_{2}-\xi)^{2}}{4k(\xi,\tau)(t-\tau)}}\bigg]\bigg|,

\displaystyle\big|H_{x}(x_{2}-\xi,t-\tau)-H_{x}(x_{1}-\xi,t-\tau)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k^{3/2}_{L}}\bigg|\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{(t-\tau)^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{(x_{1}-\xi)^{2}}{4k_{U}(t-\tau)}}\bigg|+\frac{1}{k^{3/2}_{L}}\bigg|\frac{x_{2}-\xi}{(t-\tau)^{3/2}}\bigg[e^{-\frac{(x_{2}-\xi)^{2}}{4k(\xi,\tau)(t-\tau)}}-e^{-\frac{(x_{1}-\xi)^{2}}{4k(\xi,\tau)(t-\tau)}}\bigg]\bigg|.

We will consider the case on

The estimates on

follow similarly.

Dividing the inequality by

we get

\displaystyle\frac{\big|H_{x}(x_{2}-\xi,t-\tau)-H_{x}(x_{1}-\xi,t-\tau)\big|}{|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{1-\alpha}}{|t-\tau|^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{(x_{2}-\xi)^{2}}{4k_{U}(t-\tau)}}+\frac{|x_{1}-\xi|}{|t-\tau|^{3/2}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}}\bigg|e^{-\frac{(x_{1}-\xi)^{2}}{4k(\xi,\tau)(t-\tau)}}-e^{-\frac{(x_{2}-\xi)^{2}}{4k(\xi,\tau)(t-\tau)}}\bigg|.

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{1-\alpha}}{|t-\tau|^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{(x_{2}-\xi)^{2}}{4k_{U}(t-\tau)}}|\square(\xi,\tau)|\,d\xi\,d\tau

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|x_{1}-\xi|}{|t-\tau|^{3/2}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}}\bigg|e^{-\frac{(x_{1}-\xi)^{2}}{4k(t-\tau)}}-e^{-\frac{(x_{2}-\xi)^{2}}{4k(t-\tau)}}\bigg||\square(\xi,\tau)|\,d\xi\,d\tau

(2.8)

Note that for
\xi\in B_{1}
we have

\frac{(x_{2}-x_{1})(x_{2}+x_{1}-2\xi)}{4k(\xi,\tau)(t-\tau)}>0.

The following lemma will be needed,

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
a\geq 0
and
0<\lambda\leq 1,
one has

1-e^{-a}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}a^{\lambda}.

Proof.

The case
\lambda=1
can be shown straightforwardly by the mean value theorem. For
0<\lambda<1,
define
F(a)=\frac{1}{\lambda}a^{\lambda}+e^{-a}-1.
This is a continuous function on
[0,\infty)
for any
\lambda\in(0,1).
Since
F(0)=0
and
\lim_{a\to\infty}F(a)=\infty.
It suffices to show that the values of
F
at local extrema are all non-negative.

If
F
has no extrema then it is monotone and we are done. Otherwise, suppose
\tilde{a}>0
is a local extremum of
F,
that is
F^{\prime}(\tilde{a})=0,
which gives

{\tilde{a}}^{\lambda-1}=e^{-{\tilde{a}}}.

Now we have,

F(\tilde{a})=\frac{1}{\lambda}\tilde{a}^{\lambda}+{\tilde{a}}^{\lambda-1}-1:=\tilde{F}.

We claim that
\tilde{F}>0
for all
\tilde{a}>0
and hence if
\tilde{a}
is a local minimum then
F(\tilde{a})
is still positive. To show that we notice,

\tilde{F}\to\infty,

as
\tilde{a}\to 0^{+},
and

\tilde{F}\to\infty,

as
\tilde{a}\to\infty.
Moreover, there is only one extremum point
a^{*}
that is

{a^{*}}^{\lambda-1}+(\lambda-1){a^{*}}^{\lambda-2}=0,

which gives

a^{*}=1-\lambda.

It must be a global minimum and finally

\tilde{F}(1-\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(1-\lambda)^{\lambda}+(1-\lambda)^{\lambda-1}-1>0

if and only if

\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}+1-(1-\lambda)^{1-\lambda}>0,

if and only if

1-\lambda(1-\lambda)^{1-\lambda}>0,

which is clearly true since
0<\lambda<1.

∎

Now using Lemma
2.1
for (

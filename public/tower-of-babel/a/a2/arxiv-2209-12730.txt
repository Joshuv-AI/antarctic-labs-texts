Large nearest neighbour balls in hyperbolic stochastic geometry
Moritz Otto, Christoph Thäle

Abstract
Consider a stationary Poisson process in a d d -dimensional hyperbolic space. For R > 0 R>0 define the point process ξ R ( k ) \xi_{R}^{(k)} of exceedance heights over a suitable threshold of the hyperbolic volumes of k k th nearest neighbour balls centred around the points of the Poisson process within a hyperbolic ball of radius R R centred at a fixed point. The point process ξ R ( k ) \xi_{R}^{(k)} is compared to an inhomogeneous Poisson process on the real line with intensity function e − u e^{-u} and point process convergence in the Kantorovich-Rubinstein distance is shown. From this, a quantitative limit theorem for the hyperbolic maximum k k th nearest neighbour ball with a limiting Gumbel distribution is derived. Keywords . Geometric extreme value theory, hyperbolic stochastic geometry, nearest neighbour balls, Poisson process approximation. MSC . 52A55, 60D05, 60G55.

In this paper we are interested in quantitative limit theorems for so-called large

of independent random points which are uniformly distributed on the
d
-dimensional unit cube
[0,1]^{d}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. For

,
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and

be the distance of

to its

define the random variable

C_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{1}\Big\{{\mathcal{H}}_{e}^{d}(B_{e}(X_{i},r(i,n)))\geq{t+\log n\over n}\Big\},

that, after suitable normalization,

. Moreover, the rate of convergence, measured in the total variation distance, is of order

th nearest neighbour balls, which says that

converges, after suitable centring, to a Gumbel distribution, as

with intensity

While the results and references just mentioned deal with

19

In principle it is possible to rephrase the Euclidean model of large
k
th nearest neighbour balls in a hyperbolic space, where the cube (which does not exist in hyperbolic geometry) is replaced by a hyperbolic ball of radius one, say. However, in this case, one can localize the problem and work with approximations in the corresponding tangent spaces. Within these tangent spaces the model is Euclidean again and we get back a result similar to that in
[
4
,
11
,
17
]
. For this reason, we modify the set-up as follows: We start with a stationary Poisson process in
{\mathbb{H}}^{d}
and look at large
k
th nearest neighbour balls associated with points in a family of hyperbolic balls of radius
R\to\infty
. Up to a rescaling, in a Euclidean space this set-up is the same as fixing the radius of the ball and increasing the intensity of the Poisson process (or equivalently the number of points). However, this is no more the case in a hyperbolic space. Even more, since the problem in this form cannot locally be approximated by Euclidean models in tangent spaces, we will arrive at results which are of a different nature and ‘feel’ the negative curvature of the underlying space.

In the next section we formally describe the framework we work with and present our results.

3.1
Hyperbolic geometry

In this section we collect some preliminary materials from hyperbolic geometry, which are relevant in our context, and refer to the monograph
[
28
]
and the survey article

]
for further information. We recall that
B(z,r)
stands for a

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{d}(B_{r})=\omega_{d}\int_{0}^{r}\sinh^{d-1}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{d}}f(x)\,{\mathcal{H}}^{d}({\rm d}x)=\omega_{d}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sinh^{d-1}(u)\,f(\exp_{p}(uv))\,{\rm d}u\sigma_{p}({\rm d}v),

where

Lemma 4
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Then there are constants
\Gamma_{d},\gamma_{d}>0
only depending on
d
such that
\gamma_{d}e^{r(d-1)}\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{d}(B_{r})\leq\Gamma_{d}e^{r(d-1)}
for all
r\geq 2
.

Proof.

It is elementary to check that
\sinh(u)\geq u
for any
u\geq 0
and that
\sinh(u)\geq e^{u}/3
for
u\geq 1
. Since

we conclude that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{d}(B_{r})

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{d}\int_{0}^{1}u^{d-1}\,\mathrm{d}u+{\omega_{d}\over 3^{d-1}}\int_{1}^{r}e^{(d-1)u}\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle={\omega_{d}\over d}+{\omega_{d}\over(d-1)3^{d-1}}(e^{r(d-1)}-e^{d-1})

\displaystyle\geq{\omega_{d}\over(d-1)3^{d-1}}(e^{r(d-1)}-e^{d-1})

\displaystyle\geq\gamma_{d}e^{r(d-1)}

with the choice
\gamma_{d}={\omega_{d}\over 2(d-1)3^{d-1}}
, where for the last inequality we used that

. On the other hand,
\sinh(u)\leq e^{u}/2

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{d}(B_{r})\leq{\omega_{d}\over 2^{d-1}}\int_{0}^{r}e^{(d-1)u}\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\Gamma_{d}e^{r(d-1)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{s\omega_{d-1}}{2}\Big(e^{-3(d-2)}\int_{2}^{r-s/2}e^{(d-1)t}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{2}t^{d-2}\,{\rm d}t\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{s\omega_{d-1}}{2(d-1)}\Big(\frac{e^{(d-1)(r-s/2)}}{e^{3(d-2)}}-e^{-d+4}+2^{d-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{1}se^{(d-1)(r-s/2)},

where we used the fact that

for
d\geq 3
.

To obtain an upper bound, let

be the

. We use the polar integration formula (

) in the hyperbolic hyperplane containing

\displaystyle=s{\omega_{d-1}}\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(t)\,\sinh^{d-2}(t)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq{s\omega_{d-1}\over 2^{d-2}}\int_{0}^{r}e^{(d-1)t}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle={s\omega_{d-1}\over(d-1)2^{d-2}}(e^{(d-1)r}-1)

where we additionally applied the inequalities

3.2
Poisson approximation

In this section we rephrase a special case of
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
which we will use to prove Theorem

\mathbb{X}
and denote by

\mathbb{X}
. As usual, we identify each element in
{\bf N}_{\mathbb{X}}
with its support. Let

whose intensity measure is denoted by

. Let

denote the system of closed sets in
\mathbb{X}
endowed with the Fell topology, see
[

, p. 256]
. To each point

as well as a closed set

with

means that

\displaystyle g(x,\mu)

\displaystyle=g(x,\mu\cap S_{x})

\displaystyle f(x,\mu)

\displaystyle=f(x,\mu\cap S_{x})\quad\text{if}\quad g(x,\mu)=1.

To rephrase the result from
[
4
]
we need the total variation distance

between two measures

where the supremum runs over all Borel subsets

. Now, let

with intensity measure

, and define the quantities

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{X}}{\mathbb{E}}[g(x,\eta+\delta_{x})\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{S}(x,\eta)\not\subset S_{x}\}]\,{\mathbb{E}}\eta({\rm d}x),

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{\mathbb{X}}\mathds{1}\{S_{x}\cap S_{z}\neq\varnothing\}{\mathbb{E}}[g(x,\eta+\delta_{x})]{\mathbb{E}}[g(z,\eta+\delta_{z})]\,{\mathbb{E}}\eta({\rm d}z){\mathbb{E}}\eta({\rm d}x),

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{X}}\int_{\mathbb{X}}\mathds{1}\{S_{x}\cap S_{z}\neq\varnothing\}{\mathbb{E}}[g(x,\eta+\delta_{x}+\delta_{z})g(z,\eta+\delta_{x}+\delta_{z})]\,{\mathbb{E}}\eta({\rm d}z){\mathbb{E}}\eta({\rm d}x),

where
\delta_{(\cdot)}
denotes the Dirac measure. Assuming finally that

[

, Theorem 4.1]
says that the Kantorovich-Rubinstein distance

can be estimated from above by

\displaystyle\mathbf{d_{KR}}(\xi[\eta],\zeta)\leq\mathbf{d_{TV}}({\mathbb{E}}\xi[\eta],{\mathbb{E}}\zeta)+2(E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3}).

(3.5)

We refer to

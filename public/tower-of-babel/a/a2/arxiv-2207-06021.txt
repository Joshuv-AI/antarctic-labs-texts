The h h -vectors of the edge rings of a special family of graphs
Akihiro Higashitani, Nayana Shibu Deepthi

Abstract
. The h h -vectors of homogeneous rings are one of the most important invariants that often reflect ring-theoretic properties. On the other hand, there are few examples of edge rings of graphs whose h h -vectors are explicitly computed. In this paper, we compute the h h -vector of a special family of graphs, by using the technique of initial ideals and the associated simplicial complex.

1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper, we assume all graphs to be connected and having no loops and multiple edges.

Many researchers have conducted intensive studies on the edge rings and toric ideals of graphs.
Especially, Ohsugi and Hibi initiated the investigation and have been developing the theory of edge rings.
For instance, the characterization for the edge rings to be normal was given in
[
10
]
.
Note that, almost at the same time, the same result was obtained by Simis-Vasconcelos-Villarreal independently in
[
12

.
For the introduction to the edge rings and toric ideals of graphs, we refer the readers to
[
4

and
[

One of the most important invariants of homogeneous rings is the Hilbert series.
In fact, the Gorensteinness of homogeneous normal Cohen–Macaulay domains is characterized by the symmetry of their
h
-vectors (
[
13
]
).
Moreover, there are many other results claiming that the
h
-vectors of homogeneous (or semi-standard graded) normal Cohen–Macaulay rings (or domains)
have some connection with their ring-theoretic properties (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
5
,
7
,
16
]
, and so on).
On the other hand, the
h
-vector of an edge ring is scarcely known.
As far as we know, the
h
-vectors (or their counterparts) of the edge rings of the following graphs have been computed:

•

Let
K_{m,n}
denote the complete bipartite graph with
m+n
vertices. Then

h(\Bbbk[K_{m,n}];t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\min\{m,n\}}\binom{m-1}{i}\binom{n-1}{i}t^{i}.

•

Let
K_{m}
denote the complete graph with
m
vertices. Then

h(\Bbbk[K_{m}];t)=1+\frac{m(m-3)}{2}t+\sum_{i=2}^{\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor}\binom{m}{2i}t^{i}.

•

The Hilbert functions of the edge rings of complete multipartite graphs were computed in
[
11
, Theorem 2.6]
.

•

The Hilbert series of the edge rings of bipartite graphs are described using their interior polynomials (
[
8
]
).

(On the notations used above, see Section
2
.)
Regarding the results on
K_{m,n}
and
K_{m}

[

.

2.
Preliminaries

First, we recall some fundamental materials like, notions on commutative algebra, the edge rings and toric ideals of graphs.

2.1.
Homogeneous rings

In this subsection, we collect some notions on homogeneous rings used in this paper.
We refer the readers to
[
2
,
15
]
for more detailed information on homogeneous rings.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay homogeneous ring of dimension
d
over a field
\Bbbk
.
Then the Hilbert series of
R
looks as follows:

\sum_{i\geq 0}\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{i}t^{i}=\frac{h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}}{(1-t)^{d}},

where
R_{i}
denotes the homogeneous part of
R
of degree
i
,
\dim_{\Bbbk}
denotes the dimension as a
\Bbbk
-vector space, and we assume that
h_{s}\neq 0
.
We call the polynomial
h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}
appearing in the numerator as the
h
-polynomial
of
R
, denoted by
h(R;t)
,
and the sequence of the coefficients
(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{s})
as the
h
-vector
of
R
, denoted by
h(R)
.

Remark 2.1
.

It follows from
[
13
]
that, if
R
is a normal Cohen–Macaulay homogeneous domain, then
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
h(R)
is symmetric,
i.e.,
h_{i}=h_{s-i}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,s
.
Hence, once we get the
h
-vector of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{n}]
, as described in Theorem
1.1
, we obtain that
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{n}]
is Gorenstein if and only if
n\leq 2
.

We denote the
Cohen–Macaulay type
of
R
by
r(R)
.
It is well known that
r(R)
coincides with the number of elements in the minimal system of generators of the canonical module of
R
.

2.2.
Edge rings

In this subsection, we recall the definitions of edge rings and toric ideals of graphs, and some fundamental theorems regarding them.

First of all, let us recall the definition of edge rings.
Let
G
be a graph on the vertex set
[d]
with edge set
E(G)=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
.
We define a polynomial ring
R=\Bbbk[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}]
in
d
variables and another one
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
in
m
variables, where
\Bbbk
is a field.
Let
\pi:S\rightarrow R
be the ring homomorphism defined by
\pi(x_{i})={\bf t}^{e_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
,
where
{\bf t}^{e}:=t_{u}t_{v}
for any edge
e=\{u,v\}\in E(G)
.
The image
\operatorname{Im}(\pi)
, which is a subalgebra of
R
, is called the
edge ring
of
G
,
and the kernel
\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)
, an ideal of
S
, is called the
toric ideal
of
G
.
We denote the edge ring of
G
by
\Bbbk[G]
and the toric ideal of
G
by
I_{G}
. Clearly, we have the ring isomorphism
\Bbbk[G]\cong S/I_{G}
.
It is known that
\Bbbk[G]
is
d
-dimensional if
G
is not bipartite (cf.
[
10
, Proposition 1.3]
).

Given an edge
e=\{i,j\}\in E(G)
, let
\rho(e)=\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}
, where
\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are the unit vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
A_{G}=\{\rho(e):e\in E(G)\}
. For
\mathbb{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}A_{G}=\left\{\sum_{e\in E(G)}a_{e}\rho(e):a_{e}\in\mathbb{A}\right\}
.
In this paper, we only consider the cases where
\mathbb{A}
is
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
or
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
We can regard
\Bbbk[G]
as a monoid algebra of an affine monoid
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}A_{G}
.
In particular, the structure of the cone
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}A_{G}
plays a crucial role in the study of
\Bbbk[G]
.

Next, let us describe the cone
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}A_{G}
in terms of linear inequalities.
For the description, we need to introduce some more notions on graphs.

•

For a subset
W\subset V(G)
, let
G\setminus W
be the subgraph on
V(G)\setminus W
with the edge set
\{e\in E(G):e\subset V(G)\setminus W\}
.
If
W=\{w\}
, then we write
G\setminus w
instead of
G\setminus\{w\}
.

•

For
v\in V(G)
, let
N_{G}(v)=\{u\in V(G):\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}
, and for any subset
W\subset V(G)
, let
N_{G}(W)=\bigcup\limits_{w\in W}N_{G}(w)
.

•

A non-empty subset
T\subset V(G)
is called an
independent set
if
\{v,w\}\not\in E(G)
for any
v,w\in T
.

•

We call a vertex
v
of
G

regular
if each connected component of
G\setminus v
contains an odd cycle.

•

We say that an independent set
T
of
V(G)
is a
fundamental set
if

–

the bipartite graph on the vertex set
T\cup N_{G}(T)
with the edge set
\{\{v,w\}:v\in T,w\in N_{G}(T)\}\cap E(G)
is connected, and

–

T\cup N_{G}(T)=V(G)
, or each of the connected components of the graph
G\setminus(T\cup N_{G}(T))
contains an odd cycle.

It follows from
[
10
, Theorem 1.7 (a)]
that
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}A_{G}
consists of the elements
(x_{v})_{v\in V(G)}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
satisfying all the following inequalities:

(1)

\begin{split}x_{u}&\geq 0\;\;\text{ for any regular vertex }u;\\
\sum_{v\in N_{G}(T)}x_{v}&\geq\sum_{u\in T}x_{u}\;\;\text{ for any fundamental set }T.\end{split}

Next, from
[
4
, Section 5.3]
, we shall recall how to describe the generators of the toric ideal of a graph.
Let
G
be a graph. Given
\Gamma=(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{2r}})
, a sequence of edges of
G
,
we call
\Gamma
an
even closed walk
if
|e_{i_{j}}\cap e_{i_{j+1}}|=1
for
j=1,\ldots,2r
, where
i_{2r+1}=i_{1}
.
Moreover, we associate a binomial
f_{\Gamma}=f_{\Gamma}^{(+)}-f_{\Gamma}^{(-)}
, where

f_{\Gamma}^{(+)}=\prod_{k=1}^{r}x_{i_{2k-1}}\;\text{ and }\;f_{\Gamma}^{(-)}=\prod_{k=1}^{r}x_{i_{2k}}.

We say that an even closed walk
\Gamma
is
primitive
if there is no even closed walk
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
G
with
f_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\neq f_{\Gamma}

such that
f_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{(+)}
divides
f_{\Gamma}^{(+)}
and
f_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{(-)}
divides
f_{\Gamma}^{(-)}
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
4
, Lemma 5.10]
)
.

The toric ideal
I_{G}
is generated by the binomials
f_{\Gamma}
for all primitive even closed walks
\Gamma
.

Finally, we recall the condition for the normality of
\Bbbk[G]
.
We say that
G
satisfies the
odd cycle condition
if for any pair of odd cycles
C
and
C^{\prime}
in
G
,
if
C
and
C^{\prime}
do not share any common vertex, then there is a bridge between
C
and
C^{\prime}
,
i.e., there exist
v\in V(C)
and
v^{\prime}\in V(C^{\prime})
such that
\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E(G)
.

We see that
\Bbbk[G]
is normal if and only if

(2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}A_{G}=\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}A_{G}\cap\mathbb{Z}A_{G}

holds. (See, e.g.,
[
2
, Section 6.1]
.) On the normality of
\Bbbk[G]
, the following is known:

Theorem 2.3

(
[
10
,
12
]
)
.

A graph
G
satisfies (
2
) if and only if
G
satisfies the odd cycle condition.

Remark that
\Bbbk[G]
is a normal Cohen–Macaulay homogeneous domain if
G
satisfies the odd cycle condition.
Cohen–Macaulayness follows from Hochster’s theorem (see
[
2
, Theorem 6.3.5]
).

\displaystyle x_{1}<_{\text{lex}}y_{1}<_{\text{lex}}z_{1}<_{\text{lex}}\dots<_{\text{lex}}x_{n}<_{\text{lex}}y_{n}<_{\text{lex}}z_{n}.

Proof.

Let

and

be two generators. If

and

Suppose

\displaystyle S(f,g)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{lcm}(\operatorname{in}_{<_{\text{lex}}}(f),\operatorname{in}_{<_{\text{lex}}}(g))}{\operatorname{in}_{<_{\text{lex}}}(f)}f-\frac{\mathrm{lcm}(\operatorname{in}_{<_{\text{lex}}}(f),\operatorname{in}_{<_{\text{lex}}}(g))}{\operatorname{in}_{<_{\text{lex}}}(g)}g

\displaystyle=z_{q}f-z_{j}g

\displaystyle=z_{q}(x_{i}y_{i}z_{j}-z_{i}x_{j}y_{j})-z_{j}(x_{i}y_{i}z_{q}-z_{i}x_{q}y_{q})

\displaystyle=z_{i}(x_{q}y_{q}z_{j}-z_{q}x_{j}y_{j}).

Note that, up to sign,

and therefore

will reduce to

The initial ideal

3.
Computation of the

-polynomial of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{n}]

Let
\Delta_{n}
be the simplicial complex whose Stanley-Reisner ideal coincides with the initial ideal of the toric ideal

with respect to

.
(For the introduction to Stanley-Reisner theory, consult, e.g.,
[

Let
\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{n})
be the set of all facets of
\Delta_{n}
.
By definition, any facet of our simplicial complex

can be expressed
as the maximal set that does not contain the triplet
\{x_{i},y_{i},z_{j}\};\hskip 5.69046pt1\leq i<j\leq n.

Since

and

which is maximal and does not contain the triplet
\{x_{i},y_{i},z_{j}\};\hskip 5.69046pt1\leq i<j\leq n.

Let us try to get a more concrete representation for the facets in

.
Consider any

.

Case 1:

Let

. This implies

, since

, then we have

If

and

.

Case 3:

Let us consider

. If there exists some

, then we have

Remark 3.1

(Mac-Mullen characterization of

Here, we recall a well-known method to compute the

(For example, see

Let

Let

Then the

4.
On the almost Gorensteinness of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{n}]

In this section, we are in the process of obtaining a theoretical proof to state that
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{n}]
is almost Gorenstein for all
n\geq 2
.

We first recall the necessary and sufficient condition for a homogeneous ring to be almost Gorenstein from
[
5
]
.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
5
, Corollary 2.7]
)
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay homogeneous ring of dimension
d
over a field
\Bbbk
and let
h(R)=(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{s})
be its
h
-vector.
Then
R
is almost Gorenstein if and only if the following equality holds:

\displaystyle r(R)-1=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}((h_{s}+\cdots+h_{s-j})-(h_{0}+\cdots+h_{j}))=:\tilde{e}(R).

Note that
r(R)-1\leq\tilde{e}(R)
is always satisfied, so almost Gorensteinness is equivalent to the inequality
r(R)\geq\tilde{e}(R)+1
.

Let us assume that
R
is a domain. Then there is an ideal
I_{R}
which is isomorphic to a canonical module of
R
as an
R
-module.
We know that the number of elements of a minimal system of generators of
I_{R}
coincides with
r(R)
.

Let us consider
R=\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{n}]
. Note that
\mathcal{G}_{n}
satisfies the odd cycle condition, so
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{n}]
is normal.
By the first part of Theorem
1.1
, we can compute
\tilde{e}(R)
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left\{\left(\binom{n}{n}+\cdots+\binom{n}{n-j}\right)-\left(\binom{n}{0}+\big(\binom{n}{1}-1\big)+\cdots+\binom{n}{j}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}1=n-2.

Hence by Proposition
4.1
, it is enough to show that
r(R)\geq n-1
.
Furthermore,
r(R)
is equal to the number of elements of a minimal system of generators of
I_{R}
,
which is the relative interior of
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}A_{\mathcal{G}_{n}}\cap\mathbb{Z}A_{\mathcal{G}_{n}}
(see
[
2
, Theorem 6.3.5]
).
Let

.
In what follows, it suffices to show that we need at least
(n-1)
elements as a minimal system of generators for the relative interior of the cone
C
.

Let us denote the vertices of
\mathcal{G}_{n}
as follows:

\displaystyle V(\mathcal{G}_{n})=\{u_{i}^{(1)},u_{i}^{(2)}:i=1,\ldots,n\}\cup\{w\}\;\;\text{ and we let }

\displaystyle x_{i}=\{w,u_{i}^{(1)}\},\;y_{i}=\{w,u_{i}^{(2)}\}\text{ and }z_{i}=\{u_{i}^{(1)},u_{i}^{(2)}\}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,n.

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}=\{c_{1,1}\mathbf{e}_{1,1}+\cdots+c_{1,n}\mathbf{e}_{1,n}+c_{2,1}\mathbf{e}_{2,1}+\cdots+c_{2,n}\mathbf{e}_{2,n}+c^{\prime}\mathbf{e}^{\prime}:c_{1,i},c_{2,i},c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}\},

(resp.

In what follows, we verify that

,
and they should be included in a minimal system of generators of

The first step
: We check that

. Here, we see the following:

•

Each of

s and
u_{i}^{(2)}

•

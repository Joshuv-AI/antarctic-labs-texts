Monopole Floer homology and invariant theta characteristics
Francesco Lin

Abstract
. We describe a relationship between the monopole Floer homology of three-manifolds and the geometry of Riemann surfaces. Consider an automorphism φ \varphi of a compact Riemann surface Σ \Sigma with quotient ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} . There is a natural correspondence between theta characteristics L L on Σ \Sigma which are invariant under φ \varphi and self-conjugate spin c structures 𝔰 L \mathfrak{s}_{L} on the mapping torus M φ M_{\varphi} of φ \varphi . We show that the monopole Floer homology groups of ( M φ , 𝔰 L ) (M_{\varphi},\mathfrak{s}_{L}) are explicitly determined by the eigenvalues of the (lift of the) action of φ \varphi on H 0 ​ ( L ) H^{0}(L) , the space of holomorphic sections of L L . Decategorifying our computation, we also obtain that the dimension of H 0 ​ ( L ) H^{0}(L) equals the Reidemeister-Turaev torsion of ( M φ , 𝔰 L ) (M_{\varphi},\mathfrak{s}_{L}) . Finally, we combine our description with the Atiyah-Bott G G -spin theorem to provide explicit computations of the Floer homology groups for all automorphisms φ \varphi of prime order in terms of ramification data.

These results allow for direct computations of the Floer homology groups in many concrete examples where we can access the geometry of

Consider a hyperelliptic Riemann surface of genus

\varphi

\end{turn}\hss}\mskip-2.0mu\mathit{HM}}{\vphantom{\widehat{\mathit{HM}}}}{}_{\bullet}(M_{\varphi},\mathfrak{s}_{L};\Gamma_{\eta})

\smash{\mskip 2.0mu\raise 15.93944pt\hbox to0.0pt{\begin{turn}

The proof of this result essentially follows, via our previous results, from the determination of the dimension of

We then focus our attention on the case of automorphism of prime odd order. In this case, there is only one invariant theta characteristic

Consider an automorphism

We will see that in the case of order

The key observation behind Theorem

Computations in complex and symplectic geometry

We have seen how one can use the understanding of the geometry of curves and their automorphisms to compute the corresponding topological invariants of three-manifolds; we now discuss some instances in which the opposite procedure leads to interesting results.

Computations of
H^{0}(L)
.
It is well known that
h^{0}(L)=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}H^{0}(L)
is not invariant under deformations of the complex structure on
\Sigma

[
Hit74
]
. On the other hand, an amusing consequence of Theorem
0.1
is a ‘topological’ proof that for a
\varphi
-invariant theta characteristic
L
,
h^{0}(L)
is invariant under
\varphi
-invariant deformations of the complex structure. As mentioned earlier, in general the
G
-spin theorem is not enough to compute the action of
\tilde{\varphi}^{*}
on
H^{0}(L)
, and in fact not even its dimension. One can instead use Theorem
0.1
to obtain information about
H^{0}(L)
and the action of
\tilde{\varphi}^{*}
on it, provided one can compute the relevant Floer homology groups from other topological manners.

Let us first point out that our results imply that

can be computed purely in terms of ‘classical’ topological invariants.

Theorem 0.8
.

Suppose

, and consider a
\varphi
-invariant theta characteristic

. Then

is the Reidemeister-Turaev torsion

.

follows because Theorem

The latter group is isomorphic to the version with a non-exact perturbation

Relation with periodic Floer homology.
Hutchings and Thaddeus (see
[
HS05
]
) defined the periodic Floer homology groups of an area preserving diffeomorphism of a surface; this construction, inspired by embedded contact homology, looks at unions of finite orbits of the map (unlike the usual fixed point Floer homology, which is defined in all dimensions, and only looks at fixed points). It was shown by Lee and Taubes
[
LT12
]
that this symplectic invariant is equivalent to the monopole Floer homology of the mapping torus
M_{\varphi}
, so that our main result implies computation of certain cases of such invariants. The most notable feature is that because we are dealing with torsion spin
c
structures
\mathfrak{s}_{L}
, the corresponding construction on the symplectic side always involves the
non
-monotone (and indeed Calabi-Yau) case; on the monopole Floer homology side, this means that one needs again to work with a Novikov-type local system
\Gamma
, and appeal to the isomorphism for
\mathfrak{s}
torsion
\smash{\mskip 2.0mu\raise 15.93944pt\hbox to0.0pt{\begin{turn}
{$\widehat{\phantom{\mathit{HM}}}$}
\end{turn}\hss}\mskip-2.0mu\mathit{HM}}{\vphantom{\widehat{\mathit{HM}}}}{}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},\Gamma)\cong\smash{\mskip 2.0mu\raise 15.93944pt\hbox to0.0pt{\begin{turn}
{$\widehat{\phantom{\mathit{HM}}}$}
\end{turn}\hss}\mskip-2.0mu\mathit{HM}}{\vphantom{\widehat{\mathit{HM}}}}{}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c;\Gamma)
where
c\neq 0
is the class of a non-exact perturbation
[
KM07
, Theorem
31.1.3
]
.

It would be interesting to find a direct link between the symplectic and algebraic geometry for such finite order classes; of course a subtlety is that a finite order automorphism is very far from having non-degenerate periodic orbits in the sense of
[
HS05
]
.

Structure of the paper.

In Section
1
we recall the basic concepts relating spin structures and theta characteristic on Riemann surfaces with symmetries, and discuss the special features of having quotient
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Section
2

Throughout the paper, it will be convenient to switch between holomorphic and spin geometry; we discuss some background on this dictionary here (See for example
[

]
,
[

]
,

Consider a
2n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
equipped with a spin structure
\mathfrak{s}
. To this, we can associate a pair of spinor bundles
S^{\pm}\rightarrow X
(which are
2^{n}
-dimensional hermitian bundles), and the corresponding (chiral) Dirac operators

D^{\pm}:S^{\pm}\rightarrow S^{\mp}.

In the case
X

\Sigma
of genus
g
, this construction can be interpreted purely in terms of Kähler geometry; this is because
\mathrm{SO}(2)=\mathrm{U}(1)
, and
\mathrm{Spin}(2)\rightarrow\mathrm{SO}(2)
corresponds to the double cover of
\mathrm{U}(1)
onto itself. Namely, a spin structure is the same as a choice of one of the
2^{2g}
square roots
L
of the canonical bundle (i.e.
L^{2}=K
); these are classically known as theta characterstics. One can then identify
S^{+}=L
and
S^{-}=\bar{L}
with the natural connections and the Dirac operator
D^{+}
can be interpreted as

(4)

D^{+}=\sqrt{2}\bar{\partial}:\Gamma(L)\rightarrow\Gamma(\bar{K}\otimes L)=\Gamma(\bar{L})

where we identify

Remark 5
.

Throughout the paper, we use the standard conventions for complex geometry (see for example
[

]
) so that

is given by

\bar{\partial}f=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}}d\bar{z}\quad\text{with}\quad\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d}{dx}+i\frac{d}{dy}\right).

The factor of

.

The space of holomorphic sections

can be identified as the space of positive harmonic spinors

is interpreted as the adjoint operator

with the space of negative harmonic spinors

These ideas were used in

to provide the first examples of manifolds where the dimension of space of harmonic spinors depends on the underlying metric. There, it is also shown that in general the dimension of

only depends on the conformal class of the metric, hence in the two-dimensional case only on the Riemann surface structure. On the other hand, the parity of

is independent of the underlying holomorphic structure (a theta characteristic is called even/odd accordingly). This was first shown by Mumford
[

, and reinterpreted via mod

[

]
.

Consider now a holomorphic automorphism

Lemma 1.1

Suppose

Example 2

Consider for example the torus obtained by identifying the opposite side of a square with the product Lie spin structure; in this case the spinor bundles

Remark 6

It is true in general that the action of

(which are conjugate bundles) are conjugate.

Consider then the action of

on the space of holomorphic sections

The spectrum

is the set of eigenvalues of the action of

on

A key observation for our analysis of transversality is the following.

Suppose the quotient of

In light of this lemma, we will denote the eigenvalues of

as

(5)

and

has multiplicity by

Proof.

Consider the (complex linear) multiplication map

Then we obtain

2.
The Seiberg-Witten equations on the mapping torus
M_{\varphi}

This section contains the key technical work behind the proof of Theorem
0.1
. Consider automorphism
\varphi
of
\Sigma
with
\Sigma/\varphi=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, leaving invariant a theta characteristic
L
; denote by
\tilde{\varphi}
a fixed lift, and denote its order by
d
. Choose a
\varphi
-invariant metric on
\Sigma
, and consider the induced metric on the mapping torus
M_{\varphi}
. Under the natural
d
-fold cover

, the self-conjugate spin
c

\mathfrak{s}_{L}
pulls back to the unique torsion one,

. It is well known that the unperturbed equations on

do not admit irreducible solutions (see for example
[

]
), hence neither

admits them. We will then consider perturbations of the form

of reducible solutions on

. We will show that for suitable choices of

[

]
: the key issue here is to make sure that the perturbed Dirac operators at the reducible critical points has no kernel.

The perturbation, and the overall strategy of proof, closely follows the analysis in
[
KM07
, Chapter 37]
for the case of flat three-manifolds, with a significant complication: their argument strongly relies of the fact that on a flat three-manifold harmonic spinors and
1
-forms are parallel, hence are determined by their value at a given point. This is not true for the manifolds we consider; on the other hand, we will see that the assumption

allows to reach the same conclusions via the study of the pulled-back equations on

and the action of

.

2.1.
The equations on

.

In this subsection we follow quite closely parts of
[
Lop10
]
. Consider the manifold

with the product metric. For later conveniences, we will consider the

factor to be
\mathbb{R}/2\pi d\mathbb{Z}
, where

is the order of the lift

Consider the trivial spin

\Sigma\times S^{1}

(6)

as a square root

D_{B_{0}}=\begin{bmatrix}i\frac{d}{dt}&\sqrt{2}\overline{\partial}_{B_{0}}^{*}\\
\sqrt{2}\overline{\partial}_{B_{0}}^{*}&-i\frac{d}{dt}\end{bmatrix}

where we added the dependence of the holomorphic derivative on the spin connection

for clarity.
From the three-dimensional viewpoint, the decomposition (
6

\pm i
-eigenspaces of
\rho(dt)
; indeed, if

and

\Sigma

dt\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}i&0\\
0&-i\end{bmatrix},\quad dx\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix},\quad dy\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}0&i\\
i&0\end{bmatrix}.

We can write any other spin

for some imaginary valued
1

; in turn, it will be convenient to decompose

where each

is an imaginary valued
1

\Sigma
and

is a real valued function on

. As

, we have

F_{B^{t}}=2db=2[d_{\Sigma}a_{t}+(id_{\Sigma}f-\dot{a_{t}})\wedge dt]

where

is the exterior differential on
\Sigma

\ast F_{B^{t}}=2[(\ast_{\Sigma}d_{\Sigma}a_{t})dt-i\ast_{\Sigma}(d_{\Sigma}f+i\dot{a_{t}})].

Consider a section
\Psi
of
S\rightarrow Y
as a pair
(\alpha,\beta)
of time-dependent sections of
S^{\pm}\rightarrow\Sigma
. We will consider for
\delta\geq 0
the perturbed Seiberg-Witten equations

(8)

\displaystyle D_{B}\Psi=

\displaystyle\delta\Psi

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\rho(\ast F_{B^{t}})+(\Psi\Psi^{*})_{0}=

\displaystyle 0,

which are the equations for the critical points for the perturbed Chern-Simons-Dirac functional
\mathcal{L}(B,\Psi)-(\delta/2)\|\Psi\|^{2}
.
In terms of
(\alpha,\beta)
, recall that we have

(\Psi\Psi^{*})_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(|\alpha|^{2}-|\beta|^{2})&\alpha\beta^{*}\\
\beta\alpha^{*}&\frac{1}{2}(|\beta|^{2}-|\alpha|^{2})\end{bmatrix}.

In order to study (
8
), we pull-back everything to a
2\pi d
-periodic configuration on
\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma
. We can use the gauge transformation
u=e^{i\int_{0}^{t}f}
to kill the

. Notice that the new configuration is not necessarily

-periodic anymore, but
|\alpha|^{2}
and
|\beta|^{2}
still are because

is circle valued. The Dirac operator

is then just

\displaystyle D_{B}=\begin{bmatrix}i\frac{d}{dt}&\sqrt{2}\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}^{*}\\
\sqrt{2}\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}&-i\frac{d}{dt}\end{bmatrix}

where
\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}=\overline{\partial}_{B_{0}}+a_{t}^{0,1}
. Hence
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfy the system

(9)

\displaystyle i\dot{\alpha}+\sqrt{2}\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}^{*}\beta=\delta\alpha

(10)

\displaystyle-i\dot{\beta}+\sqrt{2}\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}\alpha=\delta\beta.

Differentiating the second equation in the variable

, we then compute

\displaystyle\ddot{\beta}

\displaystyle=-i\sqrt{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}\alpha\right)+i\delta\dot{\beta}

\displaystyle=-i\sqrt{2}\left(\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}\dot{\alpha}+\dot{a_{t}}^{0,1}\wedge\alpha\right)+i\delta\dot{\beta}

(11)

\displaystyle=-i\sqrt{2}\left(i\sqrt{2}\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}^{*}\beta-i\delta\bar{\partial}_{B_{t}}\alpha+\dot{a_{t}}^{0,1}\wedge\alpha\right)+i\delta\dot{\beta}

(12)

\displaystyle=2\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}^{*}\beta+|\alpha|^{2}\beta-\delta\sqrt{2}\bar{\partial}_{B_{t}}\alpha+i\delta\dot{\beta}

(13)

\displaystyle=2\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}^{*}\beta+|\alpha|^{2}\beta-\delta^{2}\beta.

where the steps are:

•

to obtain
(\ref{compdir1})
, we apply
\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}
to (

•

to obtain

, we use that

\beta\alpha^{*}=-i\sqrt{2}\dot{a}_{t}^{0,1}.

To see this, notice first that if

. Furthermore, the component of

is given by multiplication by

, hence our claim.

•

to obtain (

10
).

Taking inner products with
\beta
, and integrating over

, we get

\langle\ddot{\beta},\beta\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}=2\|\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}^{*}\beta\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}+\|\alpha\otimes\beta\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}-\delta^{2}\|\beta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

where

is implicit in the notation for the

-norms. As

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\|\beta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}=\mathrm{Re}\frac{d}{dt}\langle\dot{\beta},\beta\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}=\mathrm{Re}\langle\ddot{\beta},\beta\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}+\|\dot{\beta}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2},

integrating for

and using periodicity of

we get the identity

\|\dot{\beta}\|^{2}+2\|\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}^{*}\beta\|^{2}+\|\alpha\otimes\beta\|^{2}=\delta^{2}\|\beta\|^{2}

for the

-norms over

. A similar computation implies

\|\dot{\alpha}\|^{2}+2\|\overline{\partial}_{B_{t}}\alpha\|^{2}+\|\alpha\otimes\beta\|^{2}=\delta^{2}\|\alpha\|^{2}.

Using this, we can prove the following well-know fact.

Lemma 2.1
.

The only solutions to the Seiberg-Witten equations for
\delta=0
are the reducible ones.

Proof.

In this case, our identities imply that
\alpha
and
\beta
are time-independent, and are holomorphic sections of
S^{+}
and
S^{-}
respectively; because

, by unique continuation one of them must be identically zero. To conclude, we use the diagonal component of the second Seiberg-Witten equation:

(14)

i\ast_{\Sigma}d_{\Sigma}a_{t}+\frac{1}{2}(|\alpha|^{2}-|\beta|^{2})=0.

Integrating over
\{t\}\times\Sigma
, we obtain

0=\int_{\Sigma}|\alpha|^{2}-|\beta|^{2},

so both
\alpha
and

∎

It is important to notice that while the reducible solutions form a smooth manifold (the
(2g+1)
-dimensional torus of flat connections), this is not a Morse-Bott singularity; this is because the Hessian of
\mathcal{L}
in the normal direction is degenerate exactly where

has kernel, and we have the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

Identify the torus of flat connections as the product of
S^{1}\times\mathrm{Jac}(\Sigma)
. Then

has kernel exactly at
\{0\}\times\Theta
, where
\Theta
is the theta divisor of
\Sigma
(which we identify as the divisor in the space of holomorphic line bundles of degree

which admit non-zero holomorphic sections).

As a consequence, the structure of the singular set is highly dependent on the conformal structure of
\Sigma

Proof.

Consider a spin
c
connection with

flat of the form
B=A+i\lambda dt
where
B
is pulled back from a connection on
\Sigma
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. The Dirac operator is then given by

D_{B}=\begin{bmatrix}i\frac{d}{dt}-\lambda&\sqrt{2}\overline{\partial}_{A}^{*}.\\
\sqrt{2}\overline{\partial}_{A}&-i\frac{d}{dt}+\lambda\end{bmatrix}

Consider an element
(\alpha,\beta)
in the kernel of

. We can expand the spinors in Fourier modes with respect to

\alpha=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i\frac{n}{d}t}\alpha_{n},\qquad\beta=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i\frac{n}{d}t}\beta_{n},

with

. We get for each

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{n}{d}+\lambda\right)\alpha_{n}+\sqrt{2}\overline{\partial}_{A}^{*}\beta_{n}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\overline{\partial}_{A}\alpha_{n}+\left(\frac{n}{d}+\lambda\right)\beta_{n}

\displaystyle=0.

Applying

of the second equation, and substituting the first one in, we see that that

2\overline{\partial}_{A}^{*}\overline{\partial}_{A}\alpha_{n}=-\left(\frac{n}{d}+\lambda\right)^{2}\alpha_{n}

so that integrating by parts over

Similarly, we have the identity

Suppose that

, so that

). Furthermore,

, so that (the line bundle corresponding to the homorphic structure defined by)

. Finally, notice that

does because they are adjoint to each other and have index zero by Riemann-Roch.
∎

2.2.
Perturbing the equations on

, part 1.

Let us begin by discussing the behavior of the perturbed equations on

\delta>0

As in the proof of Lemma

-manifold for automorphisms that lift to spin automorphisms of the same order. Via Lemma
1.1
, we can use this to study a spin action of odd order on a compact Riemann surface. The result is the following, where we consider the unique hyperbolic metric inducing the given conformal structure (notice that uniqueness implies that the metric is
\varphi
-invariant).

Proposition 4.2

(Theorem
8.1
of
[
RRT21
]
)
.

Consider an automorphism
\varphi
of odd order
d
acting on a spin Riemann surface, and consider the lift
\tilde{\varphi}
with the same order. Suppose that
\varphi
has fixed points
p_{1},\dots,p_{m}
, and the action of
d\varphi_{p_{i}}
on
T_{p_{i}}
is given by rotation by
\theta_{i}
, where
0<|\theta_{i}|<\pi
. Then

\chi(\tilde{\varphi}^{*})=i\sum_{i}\cos(\tilde{\theta}_{i})\csc(\theta_{i})=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{i}\csc(\tilde{\theta}_{i}),

where we denote by
\tilde{\theta}_{i}
the square root of
\theta_{i}
for which
d\cdot\tilde{\theta}_{i}\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 10
.

The formula implies that
\chi(\tilde{\varphi}^{*})\in i\mathbb{R}
; this is consistent with the fact that on a surface the actions of
\tilde{\varphi}^{*}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}
are dual to each other, cf. Remark
6
.

Remark 11
.

Notice that the theorem does not assume that
\Sigma/\varphi=\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In general, given a
\varphi
-invariant spin structure it is already an interesting question to determine whether such spin structure is even or odd (cf.
[
EOP08
]
for the case in which the quotient is an elliptic curve).

We will now show how to use this theorem to compute the Floer homology for all automorphism of prime odd order; we begin by proving Proposition
0.6
and
0.7
.

Proof of Proposition
0.6
.

If the rotation angle at a fixed point is
\theta=e^{\pm 2\pi i/3}
, the corresponding half-angle is given by
\tilde{\theta}=e^{\mp 2\pi i/3}
; as
\csc(\pm 2\pi/3)=\pm 2\sqrt{3}/3
, the character of
\tilde{\varphi}^{*}
is

\chi(\tilde{\varphi}^{*})=\frac{i\sqrt{3}}{3}(n_{-}-n_{+}).

By Lemma
1.3
,
\varphi
acts on
H^{0}(L)
as multiplication by either
e^{\pm 2\pi i/3}
, in which case the character of
\varphi
is

\pm(e^{2\pi i/3}-e^{-2\pi i/3})\cdot h^{0}(L)=\pm i\sqrt{3}\cdot h^{0}(L).

So
H^{0}(L)
has dimension
|n_{+}-n_{-}|/3
, and the conclusion follows from the computation in Example
4
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
0.7
.

In this case the character
\chi(\tilde{\varphi}^{*})
will be an integral combination of

\displaystyle e^{2\pi i/5}-e^{-2\pi i/5}=2i\sin(\frac{2\pi}{5})

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{\frac{5+\sqrt{5}}{2}}

\displaystyle e^{4\pi i/5}-e^{-4\pi i/5}=2i\sin(\frac{4\pi}{5})

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{\frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{2}}.

As these are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, and the spectrum of
\tilde{\varphi}^{*}
contains only one of in each pair
e^{\pm 2\pi i/5},e^{\pm 4\pi i/5}
by Lemma
1.3
, the spectrum is determined uniquely by
\chi(\tilde{\varphi}^{*})
. We compute

\displaystyle\csc(\widetilde{2\pi/5})/2

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{5}\cdot 2\sin(2\pi/5)-\frac{2}{5}\cdot 2\sin(4\pi/5)

\displaystyle\csc(\widetilde{4\pi/5})/2

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{5}\cdot 2\sin(2\pi/5)-\frac{1}{5}\cdot 2\sin(4\pi/5),

where
\widetilde{2\pi/5}=6\pi/5
and
\widetilde{4\pi/5}=2\pi/5
. Hence, if the automorphism has
p
fixed points of rotation
2\pi/5
and
q
fixed points of rotation
4\pi/5
(where we count with signs), then the character is given by

\chi(\tilde{\varphi}^{*})=i\left(\frac{-p+2q}{5}\cdot 2\sin(\frac{2\pi}{5})+\frac{-2p-q}{5}\cdot 2\sin(\frac{4\pi}{5})\right).

We can construct examples in which the action of
\tilde{\varphi}^{*}
on
\mathcal{H}^{+}
has eigenvalues
e^{2\pi i/5}
and
e^{4\pi i/5}
with the desired multiplicities
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
(where negative multiplicity corresponds to the conjugate eigenvalue) by taking

p=-m-2n\quad\text{and}\quad q=2m-n.

We can write down such automorphism explictly as the natural
\mathbb{Z}/5
-action on a curve of the form
y^{5}=f(x)
for a suitable polynomial
f
, following
[
Mir95
, Chapter III.2]
. Here, near a root of
f
of multiplicity
a
the local model of the singularity of the curve is
z^{5}=w^{a}
, and the local rotation is given by
b\cdot 2\pi/5
where
b
is the inverse of
a
modulo
5
. The conclusion of the Proposition follows from the computations in Example
6
and
7
.
∎

To conclude, we discuss the proof of Theorem
0.5
, whose proof is a generalization of the method we used in the examples above. The main observation is the following.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p
an odd prime, and let
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p}
. Then the numbers
\zeta^{i}-\zeta^{-i}
for
i=1,\dots,(p-1)/2
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

Suppose there is a non-trivial relation with rational coefficients
a_{1},\dots,a_{p-1/2}
. Then

a_{1}(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})+a_{2}(\zeta^{2}-\zeta^{-2})+\dots+a_{(p-1)/2}(\zeta^{(p-1)/2}-\zeta^{-(p-1)/2})=0.

Because there is no constant term, this reduces to a polynomial of degree
<p-1
satisfied by
\zeta
. This is a contradiction because
\zeta
has minimal polynomial
1+\zeta+\zeta^{2}+\dots+\zeta^{p-1}
over
\mathbb{Q}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
0.5
.

Given an automorphism of odd prime order
p
, we can use the
G
-spin theorem to compute
\chi(\tilde{\varphi}^{*})
in terms of ramification data. Now,
\chi(\tilde{\varphi}^{*})
is an integral linear combination of
\zeta^{i}-\zeta^{-i}
for
i=1,\dots(p-1)/2
, and by the previous lemma the coefficients are uniquely determined. Furthermore, no conjugate pairs
\zeta^{i},\zeta^{-i}
appear in the spectrum, so we obtain that
\chi(\tilde{\varphi}^{*})
determines completely the spectrum of the action of
\tilde{\varphi}^{*}
on
H^{0}(L)
; we can then use this to determine the Floer chain complex and its homology as in Section
3
.
∎

On the derivation of Amplitude Detuning and Chromaticity Formulas for Particle Accelerators
J. Dilly, M. le Garrec

Abstract
This note aims to provide a complete and comprehensible derivation of the contributions to amplitude detuning in first order from multipoles up to dodecapolar order. In addition, the principle of feed-down is explored and example terms are given. The influence of an AC-Dipole is also included in the amplitude detuning. The derivation starts from very basic principles and maths useful for the derivation, such as Taylor and Binomial expansion is given at the beginning of the note.

Motivation

Contributions from different magnetic field orders to the Hamiltonian of the accelerator system are the bread and butter
of any accelerator physicist concerned with the accelerator optics.
While everyone should derive the form of these contribution at least once in their life by oneself -
drawing Pascal triangles and calculating the binomial coefficients of
(x+iy)^{n}
- a collection of all
those formulas is hard to come by.
This note takes care of this circumstance and provides the reader not only with the final results of
a multitude of parameters related to the Hamiltonian, but also tries to be thorough in the derivation of these relations,
so that any reader may easily follow the steps that lead us to the equations presented.

There are additional relations presented, which are generally known within the community,
but whose specific formulas are hard to come by if not - to the best search capacity of the author - non-existent.
Examples for those are the specific relations of dodecapole fields to the second order Amplitude Detuning in

and contributions to chromaticity split by field order in

Wholly new to the reader will be probably the change of Second Order Amplitude Detuning under the influence of driven excitation
with an AC-Dipole, as layed out in

2.4
Expectation Values

The magnetic terms of the Hamiltonian (see
Section

4
) often depend on the phase
\phi_{z}
at the contributing element.
As
\phi_{z}(s)
describes not the phase advance but the actual phase at
s
,
its value changes per turn (unless the system is in a resonant state).
Measurements of any phase dependent observable
f(\phi_{x},\phi_{y})
over a reasonable amount of turns
are therefore governed by the expectation value

\langle f(\phi_{x},\phi_{y})\rangle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\oint\oint f(\phi_{x},\phi_{y})\;d\phi_{x}d\phi_{y}\;.

(7)

This consideration is in general not valid close to resonances, as here only certain parts of the phase-space might be covered.

3
Multipole Expansion

The multipole expansion of a general magnetic field in x and y reads

B_{y}+iB_{x}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}(B_{n}+iA_{n})(x+iy)^{n-1}\;.

(16)

The normal and skew field gradients
B_{n}
and
A_{n}
can therefore be calculated from
the complex field:

B_{n}+iA_{n}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\left.\frac{\partial^{n-1}\left(B_{y}+iB_{x}\right)}{\partial(x+iy)^{n-1}}\right\rvert_{x=0,y=0}

(17)

The
main field component
(read:
only
component) of a
perfect
2N-pole magnet
is
B_{N}
for a normal and
A_{N}
for a skew magnet.
The multipole expansion of the magnetic field for a non-perfect magnet are based around this main component.
The coefficients
b_{n}
and
a_{n}
represent the normal and skew relative field errors at the reference radius
r_{ref}
:

B_{y}+iB_{x}=\begin{cases}B_{N}\sum\limits^{\infty}_{n=1}(b_{n}+ia_{n})(\frac{x+iy}{r_{ref}})^{n-1}\;\text{, for normal magnets}\\
A_{N}\sum\limits^{\infty}_{n=1}(b_{n}+ia_{n})(\frac{x+iy}{r_{ref}})^{n-1}\;\text{, for skew magnets}\end{cases}\;.

(18)

b_{n}
and
a_{n}
are dimensionless but usually given in ’units’ of
10^{-4}
.
Therefore the normal field component
B_{n}
of
an normal 2N-pole magnet is then

B_{n}\,[$\mathrm{T}\text{\,}\mathrm{m}$^{1-n}]=B_{N}\cdot\frac{b_{n}}{r_{ref}^{n-1}}\;,

(19)

and similar for the skew field component
A_{n}
as well as the normal
and skew field components for a skew 2N-pole magnet with the main
field
A_{N}
.

3.1
Field Normalization

The field gradients can furthermore be normalized to B
\rho
(
beam rigidity
),
the main dipole field and its bending radius (see
Fig.

1
):

K_{n}+iJ_{n}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{B}\rho}\left.\frac{\partial^{n-1}\left(B_{y}+iB_{x}\right)}{\partial(x+iy)^{n-1}}\right\rvert_{x=0,y=0}

(20)

In the following
q
is the particle charge and
p
is the beam momentum and
the Lorentz factor is defined as

\gamma_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_{0}^{2}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}}}}\;.

(21)

:

\vec{F}_{Lorentz}

and a positive charged particle traversing along

.

The beam rigidity can be calculated from the equality of the Lorentz force and the centripetal force:

\displaystyle F_{\text{Lorentz}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\qquad F_{\text{Centripetal}}

(22)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\;

\displaystyle qv\mathrm{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{\gamma_{0}m_{0}v^{2}}{\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{pv}{\rho}

(23)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\;

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{p}{q}

\displaystyle\;.

(24)

With
Eqs.

20
and
24
, the field strengths
K_{n}
,
J_{n}
relate to
B_{n}
and
A_{n}
:

K_{n}\,[$\mathrm{m}$^{-n}]=\frac{q}{p}\,(n-1)!\,B_{n},\quad J_{n}\,[$\mathrm{m}$^{-n}]=\frac{q}{p}\,(n-1)!\,A_{n}\;.

(25)

The main field component of a normal dipole

B_{1}=\frac{p}{q}\,K_{1}\;.

Therefore, combining

\displaystyle B_{n}\quad

\displaystyle\overset{\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:fieldCompNormalization}}}{=}\quad\frac{p}{q}\,\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\,K_{n}\quad\overset{\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:absoluteFromRelativeField}}}{=}\quad B_{1}\cdot\frac{b_{n}}{r_{ref}^{n-1}}

\displaystyle\Biggl|\;\begin{subarray}{c}\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:fieldCompDipole}\\
\end{subarray}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{p}{q}\,\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\,K_{n}=\frac{p}{q}\,K_{1}\cdot\frac{b_{n}}{r_{ref}^{n-1}}

\displaystyle\Biggl|\;\begin{subarray}{c}\cdot\;\frac{q}{p}\\
\cdot\;(n-1)!\end{subarray}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow K_{n}=K_{1}\cdot\frac{b_{n}}{r_{ref}^{n-1}}\cdot(n-1)!

(27)

which is a very useful relation to have.

Hamiltonian Terms

The time dependent Hamiltonian for static magnetic field is
(
[

]
Eq. (2.22))

H=T-V=c\sqrt{\left|\bm{p}-q\bm{A}\right|^{2}+m^{2}c^{2}}+q\phi.

(28)

After changing dependent variable to
s
and in a curved trajectory reference system
(
[
WolskiBeamdynamicshigh2014
]
Eq. (2.30)):

H=\frac{\delta}{\beta_{0}}-(1+\frac{x}{\rho})\sqrt{(\delta+\frac{1}{\beta_{0}})^{2}-(p_{x}-a_{x})^{2}-(p_{y}-a_{y})^{2}-\frac{1}{\beta_{0}^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}}}-(1+\frac{x}{\rho})a_{s}\;,

(29)

with the energy deviation

\delta=\frac{E}{cp}-\frac{1}{\beta_{0}}

(30)

and the scaled vector potential

\bm{a}=\frac{q}{p}\bm{A}\;.

(31)

4.1
Vector Potential

With the
B
being the curl of
A

in the coordinates
(x,y,s)
of the curved trajectory, see

[
WolskiBeamdynamicshigh2014
]
Eq. (3.36) - Eq. (3.38):

\textbf{B}(x,y,s)=\nabla\times\textbf{A}(x,y,s)=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial A_{s}}{\partial y}-\frac{1}{1+\frac{x}{\rho}}\frac{\partial A_{y}}{\partial s}\\
\frac{1}{1+\frac{x}{\rho}}\frac{\partial A_{x}}{\partial s}-\frac{\partial A_{s}}{\partial x}-\frac{A_{s}}{\rho+x}\\
\frac{\partial A_{y}}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial A_{x}}{\partial y}\end{bmatrix}\overset{\overset{\text{gauge}}{A_{x}=A_{y}=0}}{=}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial A_{s}}{\partial y}\\
-\frac{\partial A_{s}}{\partial x}-\frac{A_{s}}{\rho+x}\\
0\end{bmatrix}\;.

(32)

For
n>1
(not a dipole) the path through the magnet center is straight and hence
\rho\rightarrow\infty
:

B_{y}+iB_{x}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}A_{s}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}A_{s}\;.

(33)

And hence

A_{s}=-\Re\left[\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{1}{n}(B_{n}+iA_{n})(x+iy)^{n}\right]\;.

(34)

As can be seen by calculating the derivative:

\displaystyle B_{y}+iB_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}A_{s}

\displaystyle+i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}A_{s}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:asDefinition}}}{=}

\displaystyle\qquad\Re\left[\sum_{n\geq 2}(B_{n}+iA_{n})(x+iy)^{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle-i\Re\left[\sum_{n\geq 2}i(B_{n}+iA_{n})(x+iy)^{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:realOfiz}}}{=}

\displaystyle\qquad\Re\left[\sum_{n\geq 2}(B_{n}+iA_{n})(x+iy)^{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle+i\Im\left[\sum_{n\geq 2}(B_{n}+iA_{n})(x+iy)^{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}(B_{n}+iA_{n})(x+iy)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\;,

(35)

which is the multipole expansion of
Eq.

16
without the contributions from dipoles.
The magnetic field contribution to the Hamiltonian is then,
according to
Eqs.

29
,
31
and
34

\displaystyle\begin{split}H&=\qquad-\frac{q}{p}\Re\left[\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{1}{n}(B_{n}+iA_{n})(x+iy)^{n}\right]\\
&\overset{\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:fieldCompNormalization}}}{=}\qquad-\Re\left[\sum_{n\geq 2}(K_{n}+iJ_{n})\frac{(x+iy)^{n}}{n!}\right]\end{split}

(36)

4.3
Feed-Down

A charged particle that traverses a magnetic field off-center with the horizontal and vertical offsets
\Delta x
and
\Delta y

experiences so called feed-down effects.
The effective magnetic field this particle sees can be described as a superposition of the main magnetic
field of the magnet, plus fields of lower order which scale with the particles offset.
Feed-down can be calculated via Taylor Expansion
x\mapsto x+\Delta x
and
y\mapsto y+\Delta y

from the Hamiltonian terms
Eq.

36
.

As a convention, terms that have the general coefficients as Hamiltonian terms of a certain order (as in

Eqs.

37
and
38
), but contain
K_{n}
and
J_{n}
values of different order
are denoted by adding these in parenthesis in the superscript to the Hamiltonian terms name,
e.g.
N_{n}^{(J_{m})}
is the normal Hamiltonian of order
n
governed by the skew magnetic field strength
J_{m}
of order
m
.

4.3.1
General Terms up to Fourth Order Feed-Down

From
Eqs.

37
and
38
we can calculate the derivatives with respect to
x,y

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}{N_{n}^{(L_{m})}}\quad

\displaystyle\overset{\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:normalHamilStrength}}}{=}\quad\phantom{-}\frac{1}{n!}\,L_{m}\,\Re\left[n(x+iy)^{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\,L_{m}\,\Re\left[(x+iy)^{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}N_{n-1}^{(L_{m})}

(51)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}{N_{n}^{(L_{m})}}\quad

\displaystyle\overset{\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:normalHamilStrength}}}{=}\quad\phantom{-}\frac{1}{n!}\,L_{m}\,\Re\left[in(x+iy)^{n-1}\right]\quad

\displaystyle\overset{\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:realOfiz}}}{=}-\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\,L_{m}\,\Im\left[(x+iy)^{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}S_{n-1}^{(L_{m})}

(52)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}{S_{n}^{(L_{m})}}\quad

\displaystyle\overset{\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:skewHamilStrength}}}{=}\quad-\frac{1}{n!}\,L_{m}\,\Im\left[n(x+iy)^{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\,L_{m}\,\Im\left[(x+iy)^{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}S_{n-1}^{(L_{m})}

(53)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}{S_{n}^{(L_{m})}}\quad

\displaystyle\overset{\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:skewHamilStrength}}}{=}\quad-\frac{1}{n!}\,L_{m}\,\Im\left[in(x+iy)^{n-1}\right]\quad

\displaystyle\overset{\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:imagOfiz}}}{=}-\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\,L_{m}\,\Re\left[(x+iy)^{n-1}\right]

with

The Taylor expansion (

and

\begin{split}&N_{n}(x+\Delta x,y+\Delta y)=\;\\
&N_{n}(x,y)\\

5
Amplitude Detuning

The tune, expanded around beam center, reads in terms of emittance
\epsilon_{z}=2J_{z}
(see also
[
MacleanAmplitudeDetuningMeasurements2015
]
):

Q_{z}(\epsilon_{x},\epsilon_{y})=Q_{z0}+(\frac{\partial Q_{z}}{\partial\epsilon_{x}}\epsilon_{x}+\frac{\partial Q_{z}}{\partial\epsilon_{y}}\epsilon_{y})+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}Q_{z}}{\partial\epsilon_{x}^{2}}\epsilon_{x}^{2}+2\frac{\partial^{2}Q_{z}}{\partial\epsilon_{x}\epsilon_{y}}\epsilon_{x}\epsilon_{y}+\frac{\partial^{2}Q_{z}}{\partial\epsilon_{y}^{2}}\epsilon_{y}^{2}\right)+...\;.

(57)

Further,
\phi_{z}
and
J_{z}
are canonical coordinates in the Courant-Snyder coordinate system, and hence the Hamiltonian equations read:

\frac{\partial\phi_{z}}{\partial s}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial J_{z}},\qquad\frac{\partial J_{z}}{\partial s}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial\phi_{z}}

(58)

With the definition of the tune as

Q_{z}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint\frac{\partial\phi_{z}}{\partial s}ds

(59)

the change of the tune within one magnet of length
L
therefore becomes

\Delta Q_{z}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{L}\frac{\partial H}{\partial J_{z}}ds\;,

(60)

where
H
is the contribution to the Hamiltonian of that magnet.
As we are interested in the effect over many turns, we actually need to calculate the
expectation value (see
Section

2.4
):

\Delta Q_{z}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{L}\frac{\partial\langle H\rangle}{\partial J_{z}}ds\;,

(61)

5.1
From Normal Octupoles

Using the Hamiltonian for the normal octupole (
Eq.

45
) with
action-angle coordinates

z=\sqrt{2J_{z}\beta_{z}}\cos{\phi_{z}}\;,

(62)

we get

N_{4}=\frac{1}{4!}K_{4}(4J_{x}^{2}\beta_{x}^{2}\cos^{4}{\phi_{x}}+24J_{x}J_{y}\beta_{x}\beta_{y}\cos^{2}{\phi_{x}}\cos^{2}{\phi_{y}}+4J_{y}^{2}\beta_{y}^{2}\cos^{4}{\phi_{y}})\;.

(63)

When calculating the expectation value
\langle N_{4}\rangle
(see

7

Eq.

63
,
we can apply
\langle\cos^{2}\rangle
from

15

\langle N_{4}\rangle=\frac{1}{16}K_{4}(J_{x}^{2}\beta_{x}^{2}+4J_{x}J_{y}\beta_{x}\beta_{y}+J_{y}^{2}\beta_{y}^{2})\;.

(64)

Further simplifications can be made under the assumption that
\phi_{z}
,
J_{z}
and
\beta_{z}
are approximately constant within one magnet.
In the case of a normal octupole

60
then becomes

\Delta Q_{x,y}^{N_{4}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{L}\frac{\partial\langle N_{4}\rangle}{\partial J_{x,y}}ds\quad\overset{\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:n40ExpCalc}}}{=}\quad\frac{1}{32\pi}K_{4}L\frac{\partial}{\partial J_{x,y}}(J_{x}^{2}\beta_{x}^{2}+4J_{x}J_{y}\beta_{x}\beta_{y}+J_{y}^{2}\beta_{y}^{2})

(67)

The first order detuning with the action
2J_{z}
in

57

\frac{\partial Q_{x}^{N_{4}}}{\partial(2J_{x})}=\frac{K_{4}L}{32\pi}\,\beta^{2}_{x}

\frac{\partial Q_{x}^{N_{4}}}{\partial(2J_{y})}\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:tuneChangeN4}}{=}\frac{\partial Q_{y}^{N_{4}}}{\partial(2J_{x})}=-\frac{K_{4}L}{16\pi}\,\beta_{x}\beta_{y}

(69)

\frac{\partial Q_{y}^{N_{4}}}{\partial(2J_{y})}=\frac{K_{4}L}{32\pi}\,\beta^{2}_{y}

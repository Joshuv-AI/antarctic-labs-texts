Fundamental Theorems for Timelike Surfaces in the Minkowski 4-Space
Victoria Bencheva, Velichka Milousheva

Abstract
. In the present paper, we study timelike surfaces free of minimal points in the four-dimensional Minkowski space. For each such surface we introduce a geometrically determined pseudo-orthonormal frame field and writing the derivative formulas with respect to this moving frame field and using the integrability conditions, we obtain a system of six functions satisfying some natural conditions. In the general case, we prove a Fundamental Bonnet-type theorem (existence and uniqueness theorem) stating that these six functions, satisfying the natural conditions, determine the surface up to a motion. In some particular cases, we reduce the number of functions and give the fundamental theorems.

Primary 53B30, Secondary 53A35, 53B25

1.
Introduction

In the local theory of surfaces in Euclidean and pseudo-Euclidean spaces, one of the basic problems is to determine the surface by a system of some functions satisfying some differential equations. This is the fundamental Bonnet-type theorem giving the natural conditions under which the surface is determined up to a motion. In the Euclidean space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}
, the general fundamental theorem states that each surface free of minimal points is determined up to a motion in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}
by eight invariant functions satisfying some natural conditions (differential equations)
[
1
]
.
For the class of minimal surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}
, the number of the invariant functions and the number of the differential equations determining the surfaces are reduced to two
[
2
]
.
The surfaces with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field are determined uniquely up to a motion by three invariant functions satisfying a system of three partial differential equations
[
3
]
.

Similar results hold for spacelike surfaces in the Minkowski 4-space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
. The local theory of spacelike surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
whose mean
curvature vector at any point is a non-zero spacelike vector or timelike vector, is developed in
[
4
]
. This class of surfaces is determined up to a motion in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
by eight invariant functions
satisfying some natural conditions. Spacelike surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
whose
mean curvature vector at any point is a lightlike vector are called marginally
trapped surfaces. These surfaces were defined by Roger Penrose in order to
study the global properties of spacetime
[
5
]
and play an
important role in the theory of cosmic black holes. Recently, marginally trapped surfaces satisfying some extra conditions have
been studied intensively from a mathematical viewpoint
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
, etc. The invariant theory of marginally trapped surfaces is developed in
[
9
]
, where it is proved that the marginally trapped surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
are determined up to a motion by seven invariant functions.
Maximal spacelike and minimal timelike surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
are studied in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
, respectively, and it is proved that their geometry is determined by two invariant functions, satisfying a system of two partial differential equations.

In the present paper, we study timelike surfaces free of minimal points in the Minkowski 4-space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
. For each such surface we introduce a
pseudo-orthonormal frame field
\displaystyle\{x,y,n_{1},n_{2}\}
, which is geometrically determined by the two lightlike directions in the tangent space of the surface and the mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
. We call this pseudo-orthonormal frame field a
geometric frame field
of the surface. Writing the derivative formulas with respect to the geometric frame field and using the integrability conditions, we obtain a system of six functions satisfying some differential equations (natural conditions). We prove a Fundamental Bonnet-type theorem stating that, in the general case, these six functions, satisfying the natural conditions, determine the surface up to a motion in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
(Theorem
3.2
). Then we give the fundamental theorems in some particular cases (Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.4
).

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
be the four-dimensional Minkowski space endowed with the standard flat metric

\displaystyle\langle.,.\rangle
of signature
\displaystyle(3,1)
given in local coordinates by

\displaystyle dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+dx_{3}^{2}-dx_{4}^{2}
. The considerations in this paper are local and all functions are supposed to be of class
\displaystyle C^{\infty}
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}=(\mathcal{D},z)
be a surface in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, where
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\displaystyle z:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
is an immersion, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is locally parametrized by
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}:z=z(u,v),\,\,(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}
.

A surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
is said to be:

-
timelike
, if the restriction of
\displaystyle\langle.,.\rangle
to each tangent space of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is indefinite;

-
spacelike
, if the restriction of
\displaystyle\langle.,.\rangle
to each tangent space of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is positive definite;

-
lightlike
, if the restriction of
\displaystyle\langle.,.\rangle
to each tangent space of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is degenerate.

This paper is devoted to the study of timelike surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, so
\displaystyle\langle.,.\rangle
induces a Lorentzian metric
\displaystyle g
on
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
.

We denote by
\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}
and
\displaystyle\nabla
the Levi Civita connections on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
, respectively. So, we have the following formulas of Gauss and Weingarten

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}y=\nabla_{x}y+\sigma(x,y);\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}\xi=-A_{\xi}x+D_{x}\xi,\end{array}

where
\displaystyle x
and
\displaystyle y
are vector fields tangent to
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
and
\displaystyle\xi
is a normal vector field. These formulas determine
the second fundamental tensor
\displaystyle\sigma
, the normal connection
\displaystyle D

and the shape operator
\displaystyle A_{\xi}
with respect to
\displaystyle\xi
. In general,
\displaystyle A_{\xi}
is not diagonalizable.

The mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is defined as

H=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{tr}\,\sigma}.

If the mean curvature vector vanishes identically, i.e.
\displaystyle H=0
, the surface is called
minimal
.

It is well known that for a timelike surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, locally there exists a coordinate system
\displaystyle(u,v)
such that the metric tensor
\displaystyle g
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
has the following form
[
12
]
:

g=-f^{2}(u,v)(du\otimes dv+dv\otimes du)

for some positive function
\displaystyle f(u,v)
. We suppose that
\displaystyle z=z(u,v),(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}
is such a local parametrization on
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
.
Then, the coefficients of the first fundamental form are

E=\langle z_{u},z_{u}\rangle=0;\quad F=\langle z_{u},z_{v}\rangle=-f^{2}(u,v);\quad G=\langle z_{v},z_{v}\rangle=0,

where
\displaystyle z_{u}
and
\displaystyle z_{v}
denote the derivatives of the vector function
\displaystyle z(u,v)
, i.e.

\displaystyle z_{u}=\frac{\partial z}{\partial u},\;z_{v}=\frac{\partial z}{\partial v}
.
Since
\displaystyle\langle z_{u},z_{u}\rangle=0
and
\displaystyle\langle z_{v},z_{v}\rangle=0
, the parameters
\displaystyle(u,v)
are called isotropic parameters of the surface.

Let us consider the pseudo-orthonormal tangent frame field of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
defined by
\displaystyle x=\displaystyle{\frac{z_{u}}{f}}
,
\displaystyle y=\displaystyle{\frac{z_{v}}{f}}
. Obviously,

\displaystyle\langle x,x\rangle=0
,
\displaystyle\langle x,y\rangle=-1
,
\displaystyle\langle y,y\rangle=0
. Hence, the mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
is given by

H=-\sigma(x,y).

Since we consider surfaces free of minimal points, i.e.
\displaystyle H\neq 0
at all points, we can choose a unit normal vector field
\displaystyle n_{1}
which is collinear with
\displaystyle H
, i.e.
\displaystyle H=\nu n_{1}
for a smooth function
\displaystyle\nu=||H||
. Then,
\displaystyle\sigma(x,y)=-\nu n_{1}
.
We consider the unit normal vector field
\displaystyle n_{2}
such that
\displaystyle\{n_{1},n_{2}\}
is an orthonormal frame field of the normal bundle (
\displaystyle n_{2}
is determined up to orientation). So, we can write the following formulas for the second fundamental tensor
\displaystyle\sigma
:

(2.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\sigma(x,x)=\lambda_{1}n_{1}+\mu_{1}n_{2};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\sigma(x,y)=-\nu n_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\sigma(y,y)=\lambda_{2}n_{1}+\mu_{2}n_{2},\end{array}

where
\displaystyle\lambda_{1},\mu_{1},\lambda_{2},\mu_{2}
are smooth functions defined by:

\lambda_{1}=\langle\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}x,n_{1}\rangle;\quad\mu_{1}=\langle\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}x,n_{2}\rangle;\quad\lambda_{2}=\langle\widetilde{\nabla}_{y}y,n_{1}\rangle;\quad\mu_{2}=\langle\widetilde{\nabla}_{y}y,n_{2}\rangle.

Having in mind that
\displaystyle x=\displaystyle{\frac{z_{u}}{f}}
,
\displaystyle y=\displaystyle{\frac{z_{v}}{f}}
and using
\displaystyle\langle z_{u},z_{u}\rangle=0,\langle z_{u},z_{v}\rangle=-f^{2}(u,v),\langle z_{v},z_{v}\rangle=0
,
after differentiation we obtain:

(2.2)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\nabla_{x}x=\displaystyle{\frac{f_{u}}{f^{2}}\,x};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\nabla_{x}y=\quad\qquad-\displaystyle{\frac{f_{u}}{f^{2}}\,y};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\nabla_{y}x=\displaystyle{-\frac{f_{v}}{f^{2}}\,x};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\nabla_{y}y=\quad\qquad\,\,\,\displaystyle{\frac{f_{v}}{f^{2}}\,y}.\end{array}

Denoting
\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\frac{f_{u}}{f^{2}}=x(\ln f)
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{2}=\frac{f_{v}}{f^{2}}=y(\ln f)
, from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) we obtain the following derivative formulas:

(2.3)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}x=\gamma_{1}x\qquad\quad+\lambda_{1}n_{1}+\mu_{1}n_{2};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}y=\quad\quad-\gamma_{1}y-\nu n_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{y}x=-\gamma_{2}x\quad\quad-\nu n_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{y}y=\quad\quad\,\,\,\gamma_{2}y\,\,+\lambda_{2}n_{1}+\mu_{2}n_{2}.\end{array}

For the normal frame field
\displaystyle\{n_{1},n_{2}\}
by use of (
2.3
) we can derive the formulas:

(2.4)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}n_{1}=-\nu x+\lambda_{1}y\quad\quad+\beta_{1}n_{2};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{y}n_{1}=\lambda_{2}x-\nu y\quad\quad\,\,\,\,\,+\beta_{2}n_{2};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}n_{2}=\quad\quad+\mu_{1}y-\beta_{1}n_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\widetilde{\nabla}_{y}n_{2}=\mu_{2}x\quad\quad\,\,\,\,-\beta_{2}n_{1},\end{array}

where
\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\langle\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}n_{1},n_{2}\rangle
and
\displaystyle\beta_{2}=\langle\widetilde{\nabla}_{y}n_{1},n_{2}\rangle
.

Remark 2.1
.

The pseudo-orthonormal frame field
\displaystyle\{x,y,n_{1},n_{2}\}
is geometrically determined:
\displaystyle x,y
are the two lightlike directions in the tangent space (determined up to notation);
\displaystyle n_{1}
is the unit normal vector field collinear with the mean curvature vector field
\displaystyle H
;
\displaystyle n_{2}
is determined by the condition that
\displaystyle\{n_{1},n_{2}\}
is an orthonormal frame field of the normal bundle (
\displaystyle n_{2}
is determined up to a sign). We call this pseudo-orthonormal frame field
\displaystyle\{x,y,n_{1},n_{2}\}
a
geometric frame field
of the surface
[
13
]
.

Formulas
(
2.3
) and (
2.4
) are the derivative formulas of the surface with respect to the geometric frame field
\displaystyle\{x,y,n_{1},n_{2}\}
.

The functions
\displaystyle\beta_{1}
and
\displaystyle\beta_{2}
characterize the class of surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field. In
[
13
]
, we proved that:

•

The timelike surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
has parallel mean curvature vector field if and only if
\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=0
and
\displaystyle\nu=const
.

•

The timelike surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
has parallel normalized mean curvature vector field if and only if
\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=0
and
\displaystyle\nu\neq const
.

In the present paper we study timelike surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
with
\displaystyle\beta_{1}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2}\neq 0
.

3.
Fundamental theorems

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}:z=z(u,v),(u,v)\in\mathcal{D},\,\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a local parametrization with respect to isotropic parameters on a timelike surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
and
\displaystyle\{x,y,n_{1},n_{2}\}
be the geometric pseudo-orthonormal frame field introduced above.

Since the Levi Civita connection
\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}
of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
is flat, we have

(3.1)

\widetilde{R}(x,y,x)=0;\quad\widetilde{R}(x,y,y)=0;\quad\widetilde{R}(x,y,n_{1})=0;\quad\widetilde{R}(x,y,n_{2})=0,

where

\widetilde{R}(x,y,z)=\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}\widetilde{\nabla}_{y}z-\widetilde{\nabla}_{y}\widetilde{\nabla}_{x}z-\widetilde{\nabla}_{[x,y]}z

for arbitrary vector fields
\displaystyle x,y,z
. Using (
3.1
) and the derivative formulas (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we
obtain the following integrability conditions:

(3.2)

x(\lambda_{2})+y(\nu)+2\gamma_{1}\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2}\beta_{1}=0;

(3.3)

x(\nu)+y(\lambda_{1})+2\gamma_{2}\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}\beta_{2}=0;

(3.4)

x(\mu_{2})+2\gamma_{1}\mu_{2}+\nu\beta_{2}+\lambda_{2}\beta_{1}=0;

(3.5)

y(\mu_{1})+2\gamma_{2}\mu_{1}+\nu\beta_{1}+\lambda_{1}\beta_{2}=0;

(3.6)

x(\gamma_{2})+y(\gamma_{1})+2\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}-\nu^{2}+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}\mu_{2}=0;

(3.7)

x(\beta_{2})-y(\beta_{1})+\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}\mu_{2}+\gamma_{1}\beta_{2}-\gamma_{2}\beta_{1}=0.

Remark 3.1
.

If we assume that both
\displaystyle\mu_{1}
and
\displaystyle\mu_{2}
are zero functions, i.e.
\displaystyle\mu_{1}(u,v)=0
and
\displaystyle\mu_{2}(u,v)=0
for all
\displaystyle(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}
, then the surface consists of inflection points, i.e. at each point
\displaystyle p\in\mathcal{M}^{2}
the first normal space
\displaystyle{\rm Im}\,\sigma_{p}={\rm span}\{\sigma(x,y):x,y\in T_{p}\mathcal{M}^{2}\}
is one-dimensional.
In such case, the surface is developable or lies in a 3-dimensional space
[
14
]
.

So, further we consider timelike surfaces in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
that are free of inflection points, i.e. we assume that
\displaystyle\mu_{1}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}\neq 0
at least in a sub-domain
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{0}
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
\displaystyle\mu_{1}\neq 0
.
We will consider separately the two cases:

•

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\neq 0
and
\displaystyle\mu_{2}\neq 0
in a sub-domain;

•

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\neq 0
and
\displaystyle\mu_{2}=0
in a sub-domain.

Case I
.
\displaystyle\mu_{1}\neq 0
and
\displaystyle\mu_{2}\neq 0
in a sub-domain.

In this case we call the surface a timelike surfaces of
first type
.

From (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) we express the functions
\displaystyle\beta_{1}
and
\displaystyle\beta_{2}
as follows:

(3.8)

\beta_{1}=\frac{1}{\mu_{2}}\Bigl(x(\lambda_{2})+y(\nu)+2\gamma_{1}\lambda_{2}\Bigr);\qquad\beta_{2}=\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}\Bigl(x(\nu)+y(\lambda_{1})+2\gamma_{2}\lambda_{1}\Bigr).

Having in mind that
\displaystyle x=\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial}{\partial u},\,y=\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial}{\partial v}
and

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\frac{f_{u}}{f^{2}},\,\gamma_{2}=\frac{f_{v}}{f^{2}}
, from (
3.8
) we obtain

\beta_{1}=\frac{1}{f\mu_{2}}\Bigl((\lambda_{2})_{u}+\nu_{v}+\lambda_{2}(\ln f^{2})_{u}\Bigr);\qquad\beta_{2}=\frac{1}{f\mu_{1}}\Bigl(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\Bigr).

Hence, the functions
\displaystyle\beta_{1}
and
\displaystyle\beta_{2}
are expressed by
\displaystyle f,\nu,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
. So, we have six functions
\displaystyle f,\nu,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
satisfying the following four equations, which are derived from (
3.4
), (
3.5
), (
3.6
), and (
3.7
), respectively:

(\lambda^{2}_{2}+\mu_{2}^{2})_{u}+(\ln f^{4})_{u}(\lambda^{2}_{2}+\mu_{2}^{2})+2\lambda_{2}\nu_{v}+\frac{2\nu\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\Bigl(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\Bigr)=0;

(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\mu_{1}^{2})_{v}+(\ln f^{4})_{v}(\lambda^{2}_{1}+\mu_{1}^{2})+2\lambda_{1}\nu_{u}+\frac{2\nu\mu_{1}}{\mu_{2}}\Bigl((\lambda_{2})_{u}+\nu_{v}+\lambda_{2}(\ln f^{2})_{u}\Bigr)=0;

\frac{2ff_{uv}-2f_{u}f_{v}}{f^{4}}+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}\mu_{2}-\nu^{2}=0;

\begin{array}[]{l}(\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2}\lambda_{1})\left(f^{2}\mu_{1}\mu_{2}-(\ln f^{2})_{uv}\right)+\nu_{uu}\mu_{2}-\nu_{vv}\mu_{1}+(\lambda_{1})_{uv}\mu_{2}-(\lambda_{2})_{uv}\mu_{1}+\\
+\,\mu_{2}(\lambda_{1})_{u}(\ln f^{2})_{v}-\mu_{1}(\lambda_{2})_{v}(\ln f^{2})_{u}-\frac{\mu_{2}(\mu_{1})_{u}}{\mu_{1}}\left(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\right)+\\
+\,\frac{\mu_{1}(\mu_{2})_{v}}{\mu_{2}}\left((\lambda_{2})_{u}+\nu_{v}+\lambda_{2}(\ln f^{2})_{u}\right)=0.\end{array}

Now we can give the fundamental theorem:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle f(u,v)>0
,
\displaystyle\nu(u,v)
,
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(u,v)
,
\displaystyle\mu_{1}(u,v)
,
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(u,v)
,
\displaystyle\mu_{2}(u,v)
,
\displaystyle\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\neq 0
be six smooth functions,
defined in a domain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}},\,\,{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and satisfying the conditions

(3.9)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\text{(i)}\;\;(\lambda^{2}_{2}+\mu_{2}^{2})_{u}+(\ln f^{4})_{u}(\lambda^{2}_{2}+\mu_{2}^{2})+2\lambda_{2}\nu_{v}+\frac{2\nu\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\Bigl(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\Bigr)=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\text{(ii)}\;\;(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\mu_{1}^{2})_{v}+(\ln f^{4})_{v}(\lambda^{2}_{1}+\mu_{1}^{2})+2\lambda_{1}\nu_{u}+\frac{2\nu\mu_{1}}{\mu_{2}}\Bigl((\lambda_{2})_{u}+\nu_{v}+\lambda_{2}(\ln f^{2})_{u}\Bigr)=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\text{(iii)}\;\;\frac{2ff_{uv}-2f_{u}f_{v}}{f^{4}}+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}+\mu_{1}\mu_{2}-\nu^{2}=0;\\
\text{(iv)}\;\;\begin{array}[]{l}(\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2}\lambda_{1})\left(f^{2}\mu_{1}\mu_{2}-(\ln f^{2})_{uv}\right)+\nu_{uu}\mu_{2}-\nu_{vv}\mu_{1}+(\lambda_{1})_{uv}\mu_{2}-(\lambda_{2})_{uv}\mu_{1}+\\
+\,\mu_{2}(\lambda_{1})_{u}(\ln f^{2})_{v}-\mu_{1}(\lambda_{2})_{v}(\ln f^{2})_{u}-\frac{\mu_{2}(\mu_{1})_{u}}{\mu_{1}}\left(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\right)+\\
+\,\frac{\mu_{1}(\mu_{2})_{v}}{\mu_{2}}\left((\lambda_{2})_{u}+\nu_{v}+\lambda_{2}(\ln f^{2})_{u}\right)=0.\end{array}\end{array}

If
\displaystyle\{x_{0},y_{0},(n_{1})_{0},(n_{2})_{0}\}
is a pseudo-orthonormal frame at
a point
\displaystyle p_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, then there exists a subdomain
\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{D}}

and a unique timelike surface of first type

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}:z=z(u,v),\,\,(u,v)\in{\mathcal{D}}_{0}
, parametrized by isotropic parameters, such that
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
passes through
\displaystyle p_{0}
,
\displaystyle\{x_{0},y_{0},(n_{1})_{0},(n_{2})_{0}\}
is the geometric
frame of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
at
\displaystyle p_{0}
.

Proof.

Using the given functions we define
\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{f_{u}}{f^{2}}
,
\displaystyle\gamma_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{f_{v}}{f^{2}}
,
\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{(\lambda_{2})_{u}+\nu_{v}+\lambda_{2}(\ln f^{2})_{u}}{f\mu_{2}}
,
\displaystyle\beta_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}}{f\mu_{1}}
and consider the following system of partial differential equations for the unknown vector
functions
\displaystyle x=x(u,v),\,y=y(u,v),\,n_{1}=n_{1}(u,v),\,n_{2}=n_{2}(u,v)

in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
:

(3.10)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054ptx_{u}=f\left(\gamma_{1}\,x+\lambda_{1}\,n_{1}+\mu_{1}\,n_{2}\right)&\quad x_{v}=f\left(-\gamma_{2}\,x-\nu\,n_{1}\right)\\
\vskip 5.69054pty_{u}=f\left(-\gamma_{1}\,y-\nu\,n_{1}\right)&\quad y_{v}=f\left(\gamma_{2}\,y+\lambda_{2}\,n_{1}+\mu_{2}\,n_{2}\right)\\
\vskip 5.69054pt(n_{1})_{u}=f\left(-\nu\,x+\lambda_{1}\,y+\beta_{1}\,n_{2}\right)&\quad(n_{1})_{v}=f\left(\lambda_{2}\,x-\nu\,y+\beta_{2}\,n_{2}\right)\\
\vskip 5.69054pt(n_{2})_{u}=f\left(\mu_{1}\,y-\beta_{1}\,n_{1}\right)&\quad(n_{2})_{v}=f\left(\mu_{2}\,x-\beta_{2}\,n_{1}\right)\end{array}

For convenience, we denote

\mathcal{W}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\vskip 5.69054ptx\\
\vskip 5.69054pty\\
\vskip 5.69054ptn_{1}\\
n_{2}\\
\end{array}\right)\!\!;\;\;\mathcal{A}=f\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\vskip 5.69054pt\gamma_{1}&0&\lambda_{1}&\mu_{1}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt0&-\gamma_{1}&-\nu&0\\
\vskip 5.69054pt-\nu&\lambda_{1}&0&\beta_{1}\\
0&\mu_{1}&-\beta_{1}&0\\
\end{array}\right)\!\!;\;\;\mathcal{B}=f\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\vskip 5.69054pt-\gamma_{2}&0&-\nu&0\\
\vskip 5.69054pt0&\gamma_{2}&\lambda_{2}&\mu_{2}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\lambda_{2}&-\nu&0&\beta_{2}\\
\mu_{2}&0&-\beta_{2}&0\\
\end{array}\right)\!.

Then, system (
3.10
) can be written in the following matrix form:

(3.11)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\mathcal{W}_{u}=\mathcal{A}\,\mathcal{W},\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\mathcal{W}_{v}=\mathcal{B}\,\mathcal{W}.\end{array}

The integrability conditions of system (
3.11
) are
\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{uv}=\mathcal{W}_{vu}
,
i.e.

(3.12)

\displaystyle{\frac{\partial a_{i}^{k}}{\partial v}-\frac{\partial b_{i}^{k}}{\partial u}+\sum_{j=1}^{4}(a_{i}^{j}\,b_{j}^{k}-b_{i}^{j}\,a_{j}^{k})=0,\quad i,k=1,\dots,4,}

where by
\displaystyle a_{i}^{j}
and
\displaystyle b_{i}^{j}
(
\displaystyle i,j=1,\dots,4
) we denote the
elements of the matrices
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}
, respectively. Taking into consideration the conditions given in (
3.9
), one can check that
equalities (
3.12
) are fulfilled. Hence, there exists a subdomain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}\subset\mathcal{D}
and unique vector functions
\displaystyle x=x(u,v),\,y=y(u,v),\,n_{1}=n_{1}(u,v)
,
\displaystyle n_{2}=n_{2}(u,v),\,\,(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
, which satisfy system (
3.10
) and the initial conditions

x(u_{0},v_{0})=x_{0},\quad y(u_{0},v_{0})=y_{0},\quad n_{1}(u_{0},v_{0})=(n_{1})_{0},\quad n_{2}(u_{0},v_{0})=(n_{2})_{0}.

In order to prove that the vector functions
\displaystyle x(u,v),\,y(u,v),\,n_{1}(u,v),\,n_{2}(u,v)
form
a pseudo-orthonormal frame in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
for each
\displaystyle(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
, we consider the functions:

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\vskip 5.69054pth_{1}=\langle x,x\rangle;&\quad h_{3}=\langle n_{1},n_{1}\rangle-1;&\quad h_{5}=\langle x,y\rangle+1;&\quad h_{7}=\langle x,n_{2}\rangle;&\quad h_{9}=\langle y,n_{2}\rangle;\\
\vskip 5.69054pth_{2}=\langle y,y\rangle;&\quad h_{4}=\langle n_{2},n_{2}\rangle-1;&\quad h_{6}=\langle x,n_{1}\rangle;&\quad h_{8}=\langle y,n_{1}\rangle;&\quad h_{10}=\langle n_{1},n_{2}\rangle;\end{array}

defined for
\displaystyle(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
. Having in mind that
\displaystyle x(u,v),\,y(u,v),\,n_{1}(u,v),\,n_{2}(u,v)
satisfy (
3.10
), we obtain the system

(3.13)

\displaystyle{\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial u}=k_{i}^{j}\,h_{j}},\qquad\displaystyle{\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial v}=m_{i}^{j}\,h_{j}};\qquad i=1,\dots,10,

where
\displaystyle k_{i}^{j},m_{i}^{j},\,\,i,j=1,\dots,10
are
functions of
\displaystyle(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
. System (
3.13
) is a linear
system of partial differential equations for the functions

\displaystyle h_{i}(u,v)
, satisfying the conditions
\displaystyle h_{i}(u_{0},v_{0})=0
for all
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,10
, since
\displaystyle\{x_{0},\,y_{0},\,(n_{1})_{0},\,(n_{2})_{0}\}
is a pseudo-orthonormal frame. Therefore,
\displaystyle h_{i}(u,v)=0,\,\,i=1,\dots,10
for
each
\displaystyle(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
. Hence, the vector functions

\displaystyle x(u,v)
,
\displaystyle y(u,v)
,
\displaystyle n_{1}(u,v)
,
\displaystyle n_{2}(u,v)
form a pseudo-orthonormal frame
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{4}_{1}
for each
\displaystyle(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
.

Now, we consider the following system of PDEs for the vector function

\displaystyle z(u,v)
:

(3.14)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\vskip 5.69054ptz_{u}=f\,x\\
z_{v}=f\,y\end{array}

Using (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), one can check that the integrability
conditions
\displaystyle z_{uv}=z_{vu}
of system (
3.14
)
are fulfilled. Consequently, there exists a subdomain
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{0}\subset\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
a unique vector function
\displaystyle z=z(u,v)
,
\displaystyle(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
, satisfying
\displaystyle z(u_{0},v_{0})=p_{0}
.

Finally, we consider the surface
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}:z=z(u,v),\,\,(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
. Obviously,
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
is a timelike surface in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
parametrized by isotropic parameters
\displaystyle(u,v)
, since
\displaystyle\langle z_{u},z_{u}\rangle=0
,
\displaystyle\langle z_{v},z_{v}\rangle=0
,
\displaystyle\langle z_{u},z_{v}\rangle=-f^{2}(u,v)
.

∎

Case II
.
\displaystyle\mu_{1}\neq 0
and
\displaystyle\mu_{2}=0
in a sub-domain.

In this case, we have the following subcases:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt(a)\hskip 8.53581pt\lambda_{2}\neq 0;\\
(b)\hskip 8.53581pt\lambda_{2}=0.\end{array}

Subcase II (a)
:
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}\neq 0
.

In this subcase we call the surface a timelike surface of
second type
.

Now, using the integrability conditions under the assumptions
\displaystyle\mu_{2}=0
and
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}\neq 0
, we obtain the following expressions for the functions
\displaystyle\beta_{1}
and
\displaystyle\beta_{2}
:

\beta_{2}=\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}(x(\nu)+y(\lambda_{1})+2\gamma_{2}\lambda_{1})=\frac{1}{f\mu_{1}}(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+(\ln f^{2})_{v}\lambda_{1});

\beta_{1}=-\frac{\nu\beta_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}=-\frac{\nu}{f\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}}(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+(\ln f^{2})_{v}\lambda_{1});

and the next four equations:

(\lambda_{2})_{u}+\nu_{v}+(\ln f^{2})_{u}\lambda_{2}=0;

\frac{2ff_{uv}-2f_{u}f_{v}}{f^{4}}-(\nu^{2}-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})=0;

(\mu_{1})_{v}+(\ln f^{2})_{v}\mu_{1}-\frac{\nu^{2}-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}\mu_{1}}(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+(\ln f^{2})_{v}\lambda_{1})=0;

\begin{array}[]{l}\lambda_{2}\Bigl(\!\nu_{uu}\!+\!(\lambda_{1})_{uv}\!+\!(\lambda_{1})_{u}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\!+\!\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{uv}\!\Bigr)\!+\!\nu\Bigl(\!\nu_{uv}\!+\!(\lambda_{1})_{vv}\!+\!(\lambda_{1})_{v}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\!+\!\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{vv}\!\Bigr)+\\
+f^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}-\Bigl(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\Bigr)\Bigl(\lambda_{2}(\ln|\mu_{1}|)_{u}+\nu_{v}-\nu(\ln|\mu_{1}|)_{v}-\nu(\ln|\lambda_{2}|)_{v}\Bigr)=0.\end{array}

Finally, we can formulate the fundamental theorem in this subcase:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\displaystyle f(u,v)>0
,
\displaystyle\nu(u,v)
,
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(u,v)
,
\displaystyle\mu_{1}(u,v)
,
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(u,v)\neq 0
, be five smooth functions,
defined in a domain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}},\,\,{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and satisfying the conditions

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\text{(i)}\;\;(\lambda_{2})_{u}+\nu_{v}+(\ln f^{2})_{u}\lambda_{2}=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\text{(ii)}\;\;(\mu_{1})_{v}+(\ln f^{2})_{v}\mu_{1}-\frac{\nu^{2}-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}\mu_{1}}(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+(\ln f^{2})_{v}\lambda_{1})=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\text{(iii)}\;\;\frac{2ff_{uv}-2f_{u}f_{v}}{f^{4}}-(\nu^{2}-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})=0;\\
\text{(iv)}\;\;\begin{array}[]{l}\lambda_{2}\Bigl(\!\nu_{uu}\!+\!(\lambda_{1})_{uv}\!+\!(\lambda_{1})_{u}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\!+\!\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{uv}\!\Bigr)\!+\!\nu\Bigl(\!\nu_{uv}\!+\!(\lambda_{1})_{vv}\!+\!(\lambda_{1})_{v}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\!+\!\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{vv}\!\Bigr)+\\
+f^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}-\Bigl(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\Bigr)\Bigl(\lambda_{2}(\ln|\mu_{1}|)_{u}+\nu_{v}-\nu(\ln|\mu_{1}|)_{v}-\nu(\ln|\lambda_{2}|)_{v}\Bigr)=0.\end{array}\end{array}

If
\displaystyle\{x_{0},y_{0},(n_{1})_{0},(n_{2})_{0}\}
is a pseudo-orthonormal frame at
a point
\displaystyle p_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, then there exists a subdomain
\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{D}}

and a unique timelike surface of second type

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}:z=z(u,v),\,\,(u,v)\in{\mathcal{D}}_{0}
, parametrized by isotropic parameters, such that
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
passes through
\displaystyle p_{0}
and
\displaystyle\{x_{0},y_{0},(n_{1})_{0},(n_{2})_{0}\}
is the geometric
frame of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
at
\displaystyle p_{0}
.

Proof.

Let us denote
\displaystyle\beta_{1}=-\frac{\nu}{f\mu_{1}\lambda_{2}}(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+(\ln f^{2})_{v}\lambda_{1})
,

\displaystyle\beta_{2}=\frac{1}{f\mu_{1}}(\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+(\ln f^{2})_{v}\lambda_{1})
,
\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{f_{u}}{f^{2}}
,
\displaystyle\gamma_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{f_{v}}{f^{2}}
,
and consider the following system of partial differential equations for the unknown vector
functions
\displaystyle x=x(u,v),\,y=y(u,v),\,n_{1}=n_{1}(u,v),\,n_{2}=n_{2}(u,v)

in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
:

(3.15)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054ptx_{u}=f\left(\gamma_{1}\,x+\lambda_{1}\,n_{1}+\mu_{1}\,n_{2}\right)&\quad x_{v}=f\left(-\gamma_{2}\,x-\nu\,n_{1}\right)\\
\vskip 5.69054pty_{u}=f\left(-\gamma_{1}\,y-\nu\,n_{1}\right)&\quad y_{v}=f\left(\gamma_{2}\,y+\lambda_{2}\,n_{1}\right)\\
\vskip 5.69054pt(n_{1})_{u}=f\left(-\nu\,x+\lambda_{1}\,y+\beta_{1}\,n_{2}\right)&\quad(n_{1})_{v}=f\left(\lambda_{2}\,x-\nu\,y+\beta_{2}\,n_{2}\right)\\
\vskip 5.69054pt(n_{2})_{u}=f\left(\mu_{1}\,y-\beta_{1}\,n_{1}\right)&\quad(n_{2})_{v}=f\left(-\beta_{2}\,n_{1}\right)\end{array}

We denote

\mathcal{W}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\vskip 5.69054ptx\\
\vskip 5.69054pty\\
\vskip 5.69054ptn_{1}\\
\vskip 5.69054ptn_{2}\\
\end{array}\right)\!\!;\;\;\mathcal{A}=f\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\vskip 5.69054pt\gamma_{1}&0&\lambda_{1}&\mu_{1}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt0&-\gamma_{1}&-\nu&0\\
\vskip 5.69054pt-\nu&\lambda_{1}&0&\beta_{1}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt0&\mu_{1}&-\beta_{1}&0\\
\end{array}\right)\!\!;\;\;\mathcal{B}=f\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\vskip 5.69054pt-\gamma_{2}&0&-\nu&0\\
\vskip 5.69054pt0&\gamma_{2}&\lambda_{2}&0\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\lambda_{2}&-\nu&0&\beta_{2}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt0&0&-\beta_{2}&0\\
\end{array}\right)\!

and rewrite system (
3.15
) in the matrix form:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\mathcal{W}_{u}=\mathcal{A}\,\mathcal{W},\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\mathcal{W}_{v}=\mathcal{B}\,\mathcal{W}.\end{array}

Further, the proof of the theorem follows the steps in the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

∎

Subcase II (b)
:
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=0
.

We call the surfaces satisfying
\displaystyle\mu_{2}=0
and
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=0
timelike surfaces of
third type
.

In this subcase, under the assumption
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=\mu_{2}=0
from the integrability conditions we obtain that
\displaystyle\nu=\nu(u)
,
\displaystyle\beta_{2}=0
,

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=-\frac{1}{\nu f}\left((\mu_{1})_{v}+\mu_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\right)
and the following three equations:

(3.16)

\nu_{u}+(\lambda_{1})_{v}+\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}=0;

(\mu_{1})_{vv}+(\mu_{1})_{v}(\ln f^{2})_{v}+\mu_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{vv}=0;

\frac{2ff_{uv}-2f_{u}f_{v}}{f^{4}}=\nu^{2}.

The last equality implies that the function
\displaystyle\nu
is expressed by the function
\displaystyle f
and its derivatives as follows:

(3.17)

\nu^{2}=f^{-2}\left(\ln f^{2}\right)_{uv}.

Hence, the function
\displaystyle f^{-2}\left(\ln f^{2}\right)_{uv}
depends only on the parameter
\displaystyle u
, since
\displaystyle\nu=\nu(u)
.
Using (
3.17
) we can write equality (
3.16
) in the form:

(3.18)

(\lambda_{1})_{v}+\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}+\left(f^{-1}\sqrt{\left(\ln f^{2}\right)_{uv}}\right)_{u}=0.

Now, we can give the fundamental theorem in this subcase.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\displaystyle f(u,v)>0
,
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(u,v)
and
\displaystyle\mu_{1}(u,v)
be three smooth functions,
defined in a domain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}},\,\,{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and satisfying the conditions

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\text{(i)}\;\;\left(f^{-2}\left(\ln f^{2}\right)_{uv}\right)_{v}=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\text{(ii)}\;\;(\lambda_{1})_{v}+\lambda_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}+\left(f^{-1}\sqrt{\left(\ln f^{2}\right)_{uv}}\right)_{u}=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\text{(iii)}\;\;(\mu_{1})_{vv}+(\mu_{1})_{v}(\ln f^{2})_{v}+\mu_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{vv}=0.\end{array}

If
\displaystyle\{x_{0},y_{0},(n_{1})_{0},(n_{2})_{0}\}
is a pseudo-orthonormal frame at
a point
\displaystyle p_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, then there exists a subdomain
\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{D}}

and a unique timelike surface of third type

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}:z=z(u,v),\,(u,v)\in{\mathcal{D}}_{0}
, parametrized by isotropic parameters, such that
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
passes through
\displaystyle p_{0}
,
\displaystyle\{x_{0},y_{0},(n_{1})_{0},(n_{2})_{0}\}
is the geometric
frame of
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}
at the point
\displaystyle p_{0}
.

Proof.

Let us denote
\displaystyle\nu=f^{-1}\sqrt{\left(\ln f^{2}\right)_{uv}}
,
\displaystyle\beta_{1}=-\left((\ln f^{2})_{uv}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left((\mu_{1})_{v}+\mu_{1}(\ln f^{2})_{v}\right)
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{f_{u}}{f^{2}}
,
\displaystyle\gamma_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{f_{v}}{f^{2}}
,
and consider the following system of partial differential equations for the unknown vector
functions
\displaystyle x=x(u,v),\,y=y(u,v),\,n_{1}=n_{1}(u,v),\,n_{2}=n_{2}(u,v)

in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054ptx_{u}=f\left(\gamma_{1}\,x+\lambda_{1}\,n_{1}+\mu_{1}\,n_{2}\right)&\quad x_{v}=f\left(-\gamma_{2}\,x-\nu\,n_{1}\right)\\
\vskip 5.69054pty_{u}=f\left(-\gamma_{1}\,y-\nu\,n_{1}\right)&\quad y_{v}=f\gamma_{2}\,y\\
\vskip 5.69054pt(n_{1})_{u}=f\left(-\nu\,x+\lambda_{1}\,y+\beta_{1}\,n_{2}\right)&\quad(n_{1})_{v}=-f\nu\,y\\
\vskip 5.69054pt(n_{2})_{u}=f\left(\mu_{1}\,y-\beta_{1}\,n_{1}\right)&\quad(n_{2})_{v}=0\end{array}

Further, the proof of the theorem follows the steps in the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

∎

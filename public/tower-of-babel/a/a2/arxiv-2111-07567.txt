Logistic equations found many of their applications in the real-life world. One of the most important applications is in mathematical biology where the parabolic semilinear logistic equation describes the evolution
of spatial distribution of a biological population in the presence of constant rates of reproduction and mortality (Verhulst’s law), see

In

\displaystyle-\text{div}(a(\nabla u))=\lambda u^{q-1}-f(x,u)~\text{in}~\Omega,u=0~\text{on}~\partial\Omega.

The fractional counterpart is not so intensively studied. Recently, in

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u=\lambda u^{q-1}-u^{r-1}~\text{in}~\Omega,~u>0~\text{in}~\Omega,~u=0~\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

As far as we know, there is no study that involves the Choquard nonlinearity as logistic reaction. We aim to fill this gap in the present paper. Using the variational methods, we show the existence and uniqueness of a positive solution between a given sub-supersolution pair. The main difficulty that arises here, is the lack of comparison principles. We overcome this difficulty by introducing a specific notion of global sub and supersolutions (inspired cooperative systems, see Section
2
). Then we present some practical situations where such sub and supersolution pair can be constructed (see Corollaries
2.14
and
2.15
). We also consider the corresponding Brezis-Nirenberg type problem:

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u=\lambda u^{p-1}+u^{p_{s}^{\ast}-1}-\displaystyle\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{r}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy\right)|u(x)|^{r-2}u(x)~\text{in}~\Omega,~u=0~\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

(1.5)

We show the existence of nontrivial solutions by proving a compactness result for Palais-Smale sequence at level strictly below some critical level. We make use of the auxiliary functions and estimates introduced in
[
37
]
to construct such Palais-Smale sequences.

Finally, by using the minimization techniques on the Nehari nodal set, we show in the subcritical case the existence of a least energy sign changing solution to the problem

(P_{\lambda})\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u=\lambda u^{q-1}-\displaystyle\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{r}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy\right)|u(x)|^{r-2}u(x)~&\text{in}~\Omega,\\
u=0~&\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

with
1<p<2r<q<p_{s}^{\ast}.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some preliminary results that are required in the later sections and also give the statements of the main results. Firstly, we collect some well known results regarding fractional Sobolev spaces. For
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
, the fractional Sobolev spaces are defined as

W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})=\left\{u\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}):\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dxdy<\infty\right\}

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}=|u|_{p}+\left(\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We also define

W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)=\{u\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}):u=0~\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\Omega\}

with respect to the norm

\|u\|=\left(\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\int\limits_{Q}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

where
Q={\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\setminus(\Omega^{c}\times\Omega^{c})
. The space
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
is a uniformly convex, separable Banach space whose dual space is denoted by
W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
is the conjugate exponent of
p
(see
[
19
]
). The embedding
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\nu}(\Omega)
is continuous for all
\nu\in[1,p_{s}^{\ast}]
and compact for all
\nu\in[1,p_{s}^{\ast})
. We also set from
[
30
, Definition 2.1]
, the following space:

\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{p}_{\text{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}):\exists~U\Supset\Omega~\text{s.t.}~u\in W^{s,p}(U),~\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|U(x)|^{p-1}}{(1+|x|)^{N+ps}}dx<\infty\right\}.

By
[
30
, Lemma 2.3]
, for any
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
we can define
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u\in W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
by setting for all
v\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u,v\rangle=\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{p-1}(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dxdy.

The definition above agrees with (
1.1
) as soon as
u
is smooth enough (for instance, if
u\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
). By
[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
,
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}:W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
is a monotone, continuous,
(S)_{+}
-operator, namely, whenever
(u_{n})
is a
sequence in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)

and

\limsup_{n}\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u_{n},u_{n}-u\rangle\leq 0,

then
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
.

Another useful property referred as
T
-monotonicity of
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}
is the following (see
[
20
, Proof of Lemma 3.2]
):

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

such that

and satisfying

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u-(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}v,(u-v)^{+}\rangle\leq 0.

Regarding the spectral properties of the fractional p-Laplacian, we refer the reader to

We just recall that the eigenvalue problem which is stated as

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u=\lambda u^{p-1}~\text{in}~\Omega,u=0~\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\Omega,

The crucial result to handle the nonlocal Choquard type of nonlinearity is the following well-known
Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
33
]
Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
Let

and
0<\alpha<N
with
1/m+1/n+\alpha/N=2
,
g\in L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),h\in L^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. Then, there exist a sharp constant
C(m,n,N,\alpha)
independent of

and

such that

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{g(x)h(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dxdy\leq C(m,n,N,\alpha)|g|_{m}|h|_{n}.

(2.3)

∎

In particular, let
g=h=|u|^{r}

then by Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality we get,

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)^{r}|u(y)|^{r}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dxdy

is well defined if
|u|^{r}\in L^{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
with
t=\frac{2N}{2N-\alpha}>1
.
Thus, from Sobolev embedding theorems, we must have

p_{s,\alpha}^{\#}\leq r\leq p_{s,\alpha}^{\ast},

where
p_{s,\alpha}^{\ast}=\displaystyle\frac{p(N-\alpha/2)}{N-ps}
.
From this, for
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
we have

\left(\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{r}|u(y)|^{r}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq C(r,n,\alpha)^{\frac{1}{r}}|u|_{tr}^{2},

where
C(r,N,\alpha)
is suitable constant and

p_{s,\alpha}^{\#}\leq r\leq p_{s,\alpha}^{\ast}
.

The following result naturally follows.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

[u]_{r}^{2r}=\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{r}|u(x)|^{r}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dxdy,

where
p_{s,\alpha}^{\#}\leq r\leq p_{s,\alpha}^{\ast}.

Then
[\cdot]_{r}
defines a norm on
X_{r}=\{u:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},[u]_{r}<\infty\}
. Also

is complete.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of
[
40
, Lemma 3.4]
.
∎

\underline{u}\leq\overline{u}

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\underline{u},v\rangle\leq\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}f(x,\underline{u})vdx-\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|\bar{u}(y)|^{r}|\underline{u}(x)|^{r-2}\underline{u}(x)v(x)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dxdy,

for all
v\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)_{+}
.

Now, we discuss the regularity of nonnegative weak solutions of the problem
(\textbf{P})
. In this matter, we first state a useful technical result:

Lemma 2.10
.

[
11
, Lemma A.1]

Let
1<p<\infty
and
f:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a convex function. Then

|a-b|^{p-2}(a-b)[A|f^{\prime}(a)|^{p-2}f^{\prime}(a)-B|f^{\prime}(b)|^{p-2}f^{\prime}(b)]

\geq|f(a)-f(b)|^{p-2}(f(a)-f(b))(A-B).

for every
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
and every
A,B\geq 0
.

We then state the following regularity result:

Theorem 2.11
.

Suppose
f
satisfies
(f_{4})
or
(f_{5})
, then any nonnegative weak solution
u
of
(\textbf{P})
satisfy
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{\mu}(\overline{\Omega})
, for some
\mu\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

We divide the proof in two cases:

Case 1

Suppose
f
satisfies
(f_{4})
. Since
u
is a weak solution of
(P_{\lambda})
, it satisfies weakly in
\Omega

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u=f(x,u)-\displaystyle\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{r}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy\right)|u(x)|^{r-2}u(x).

(2.7)

We set
\phi=\xi|h^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(u)|^{p-2}h^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(u)
, where
\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\xi>0
and for
0<\epsilon<<1
,

h_{\epsilon}(t):=(\epsilon^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Testing (
2.7
) with
\phi
and in addition by choosing

in Lemma
2.10
, we get

\int\limits_{Q}\frac{|h_{\epsilon}(u(x))-h_{\epsilon}(u(y))|^{p-2}(h_{\epsilon}(u(x))-h_{\epsilon}(u(y)))(\xi(x)-\xi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}f(x,u)|h^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(u(x))|^{p-1}\xi(x)dx-\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{r}|u(x)|^{r-1}|h^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(u(x))|^{p-1}\xi(x)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dydx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}f(x,u)\xi(x)dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{r}|u(x)|^{r-1}\xi(x)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dydx.

(2.8)

The inequality (
2
) holds since

is nonnegative and
h^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\leq 1
. Since

converges to

as

, passing to the limit in (

) and using Fatou’s lemma

\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{||u(x)|-|u(y)||^{p-2}(|u(x)|-|u(y)|)(\xi(x)-\xi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}f(x,u)\xi(x)dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{r}|u(x)|^{r-1}\xi(x)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dydx,

(2.9)

for every
0\leq\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).
By density, (
2.9
) holds for
0\leq\xi\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)_{+}
. Now, following
[
7
, Theorem 2.12]
, we see that
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Since
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
0<\alpha<N
, we observe that

f(x,u)-\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{r}u(x)^{r-1}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),

and so by
[
37
, Theorem 1.1]
,

for some
\mu\in(0,s]
.

Case 2

Suppose
f
satisfies
(f_{5})
. Similarly as in Case
1
, we get

\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{||u(x)|-|u(y)||^{p-2}(|u(x)|-|u(y)|)(\xi(x)-\xi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx\leq\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}u^{q-1}\xi(x)dx.

By following
[

, Theorem 6.4]
, we see that

and hence we can find

such that

(see Section

). Now following

for some

.
∎
The energy functional associated to the problem
(P_{\lambda})
is given by

J_{\lambda}=\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{p}-\lambda\frac{|u|_{q}^{q}}{q}+\frac{[u]_{r}^{2r}}{2r}.

We define

Clearly,

Note that we are abusing the notation

Set

(2.10)

and

(2.11)

3.
Regular Case

This section is devoted to the case when
f
is regular. We shall discuss the existence and the uniqueness of the nonnegative weak solution to the problem
(\textbf{P})
. We first prove the existence of the weak solution under the conditions stated in Theorem
2.12
.

Proof.

We choose
M>|\overline{u}|_{\infty}
and define

\hat{f}(x,t)=\begin{cases}f(x,\underline{u}(x),&\text{if}~t<\underline{u}(x),\\
f(x,t),&\text{if}~\underline{u}(x)\leq t\leq\overline{u}(x),\\
f(x,\overline{u}(x)),&\text{if}~\overline{u}(x)<t.\end{cases}

(3.1)

We also define the continuous, nonnegative and affine function
T_{M}
such that

T_{M}(t)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if}~t<0,\\
t,&\text{if}~0\leq t\leq M,\\
0,&\text{if}~2M\leq t.\end{cases}

(3.2)

Now, consider the problem

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u=\hat{f}(x,u)-\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|T_{M}(u(y))|^{r}(T_{M}(u(x)))^{r-1}T^{\prime}_{M}(u(x))}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy&\text{in}~\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

(3.3)

The associated energy functional is given by

\hat{J}(u)=\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{p}-\int\limits_{\Omega}\hat{F}(x,u)dx+\frac{[T_{M}(u)]_{r}^{2r}}{2r},

where
\hat{F}(x,t)=\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{t}\hat{f}(x,\tau)d\tau.

Clearly
\hat{J}\in C^{1}(W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega),{\mathbb{R}}).
Also by Sobolev embedding and Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality,
\hat{J}
is coercive and weakly lower semicontinuous. Thus there exist
u_{0}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
that satisfies

\hat{J}(u_{0})=\min_{u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}\hat{J}(u).

So
u_{0}
satisfies (
3.3
) in the weak sense. Testing (
3.3
) with
(u_{0}-\bar{u})^{+}
and using the fact that
\bar{u}
is a strong supersolution, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u_{0},(u_{0}-\bar{u})^{+}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}\hat{f}(x,u_{0})(u_{0}-\bar{u})^{+}dx

\displaystyle-\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|T_{M}(u_{0}(y))|^{r}(T_{M}(u_{0}(x)))^{r-1}T^{\prime}_{M}(u_{0}(x))(u_{0}-\bar{u})^{+}(x)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}{f}(x,u_{0})(u_{0}-\bar{u})^{+}dx\leq\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\bar{u},(u_{0}-\bar{u})^{+}\rangle.

Using Proposition
2.1
, we conclude that
u_{0}\leq\bar{u}
. This also implies
u_{0}<M
. Again testing (
3.3
) with
(\underline{u}-u_{0})^{+}
and using the fact that
\underline{u}
is a uniform subsolution with respect to
\overline{u}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u_{0},(\underline{u}-u_{0})^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}\hat{f}(x,u_{0})(\underline{u}-u_{0})^{+}

\displaystyle-\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|T_{M}(u_{0}(y))|^{r}(T_{M}(u_{0}(x)))^{r-1}T^{\prime}_{M}(u_{0}(x))(\underline{u}-u_{0})^{+}(x)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}{f}(x,\underline{u})(\underline{u}-u_{0})^{+}-\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{0}(y)|^{r}(u_{0}(x))^{r-1}(\underline{u}-u_{0})^{+}(x)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}{f}(x,\underline{u})(\underline{u}-u_{0})^{+}-\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|\overline{u}(y)|^{r}(\underline{u}(x))^{r-1}(\underline{u}-u_{0})^{+}(x)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\underline{u},(\underline{u}-u_{0})^{+}\rangle.

Again using Proposition
2.1
, we see that
\underline{u}\leq u_{0}
. Hence,
0\leq\underline{u}\leq u_{0}\leq\overline{u}<M
and so
u_{0}
is a nonnegative weak solution of
(\textbf{P})
. ∎

Remark 3.1
.

If the conditions
\overline{u}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\underline{u}\geq 0
are dropped, then following the same ideas we can show the existence of weak solution for
p_{s,\alpha}^{\#}\leq r\leq p_{s,\alpha}^{\ast}
without the cut-off argument.

Next we prove the uniqueness result stated in Theorem
2.13
.

Proof.

We first show that any weak solution
u
of
(\textbf{P})
satisfies

C_{1}d^{s}\leq u\leq C_{2}d^{s}~\text{a.e. in}~\Omega,

(3.4)

for some
C_{1},C_{2}>0
. Since
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
(see Theorem
2.11
), by
[
30
, Theorem 4.4]
we have that
u\leq C_{1}d^{s}
a.e. in
\Omega
. Next, set

c(x)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{r}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy.

Since
0<\alpha<N
and
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have

c(x)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{r}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy\leq|u|_{\infty}^{r}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\leq M_{1}.

Also,

c(x)\geq\frac{1}{(\text{diam}\Omega)^{\alpha}}\int\limits_{\Omega}|u(y)|^{r}dy\geq M_{2}.

Hence by Proposition
2.2
,
u\geq C_{1}d^{s}
a.e. in
\Omega
for some
C_{1}>0
. Now let

V:=\{w:\Omega\rightarrow(0,\infty):w^{\frac{1}{p}}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\},

and define

\Phi:V\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
by

\Phi(w)=J(w^{\frac{1}{p}}).

First, we claim that
\Phi
is strictly convex. Indeed,

\displaystyle\Phi(w)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\int\limits_{Q}\frac{|w^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)-w^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx-\int\limits_{\Omega}F(x,w^{\frac{1}{p}})dx+\frac{1}{2r}\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|w(y)|^{\frac{r}{p}}|w(x)|^{\frac{r}{p}}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}(w)+I_{2}(w)+I_{3}(w),

where
\displaystyle I_{1}(w)=\frac{1}{p}\int\limits_{Q}\frac{|w^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)-w^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx
,
\displaystyle I_{2}(w)=-\int\limits_{\Omega}F(x,w^{\frac{1}{p}})dx
, and
\displaystyle I_{3}(w)=\frac{1}{2r}\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|w(y)|^{\frac{r}{p}}|w(x)|^{\frac{r}{p}}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dydx
.

Since
p<r
,
I_{3}
is strictly convex. Next note that

\frac{dF(x,t^{\frac{1}{p}})}{dt}=\frac{1}{p}f(x,t^{\frac{1}{p}}){t^{\frac{1-p}{p}}},

which by
(f_{2})
is decreasing. This proves that
I_{2}
is convex. It remains to show that
I_{1}
is convex. For this, let
w_{1},w_{2}\in V
. Then
w_{1}=u_{1}^{p}
and
w_{2}=u_{2}^{p}
, for some
u_{1},u_{2}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
. Now,

\displaystyle I_{1}((1-t)w_{1}+tw_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\int\limits_{Q}\frac{|[(1-t)w_{1}(x)+tw_{2}(x)]^{\frac{1}{p}}-[(1-t)w_{1}(x)+tw_{2}(x)]^{\frac{1}{p}}|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\int\limits_{Q}\frac{|[(1-t)u_{1}^{p}(x)+tu_{2}^{p}(x)]^{\frac{1}{p}}-[(1-t)u_{1}^{p}(y)+tu_{2}^{p}(y)]^{\frac{1}{p}}|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx.

(3.5)

Let
\sigma_{t}(x)=[(1-t)u_{1}^{p}(x)+tu_{2}^{p}(x)]^{\frac{1}{p}}
for all

t\in(0,1).
Then
by
[
9

,

|\sigma_{t}(x)-\sigma_{t}(y)|^{p}\leq(1-t)|u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)|^{p}+t|u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)|^{p}.

(3.6)

From (
3
) and (
3.6
), we get

\displaystyle I_{1}((1-t)w_{1}+tw_{2})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-t)}{p}\int\limits_{Q}\frac{|u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx+\frac{t}{p}\int\limits_{Q}\frac{|u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-t)I_{1}(w_{1})+tI_{1}(w_{2}).

This proves the claim. Now, let
u_{0}
be a global minimizer of
J
. Then from (
3.4
),
v_{0}=u_{0}^{p}
is a global minimizer of
\Phi
. If possible, suppose
u_{1}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)_{+}
is another positive critical point of
J
and let
v_{1}=u_{1}^{p}\in V
. By (
3.4
),
u_{1}/u_{0},u_{0}/u_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and so
\frac{u_{1}^{p}-u_{0}^{p}}{u_{1}^{p-1}}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
. Now, consider
l:[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

l(t)=\Phi((1-t)u_{0}+tu_{1}).

It is easy to see that
l
is strictly convex and differentiable function. Note that
t=0
is a global minimizer of
l
and so
l^{\prime}(0)=0
. Then, by strict convexity of
l
, we have
l^{\prime}(1)>0
. But

l^{\prime}(1)=\langle J^{\prime}(u_{1}),\frac{u_{1}^{p}-u_{0}^{p}}{u_{1}^{p-1}}\rangle=0,

as
u_{1}
is a critical point of

. Thus, we arrive at a contradiction. This proves the uniqueness.
∎

Next we give the proof of Corollaries

and

to complete this section.

Proof of Corollary
2.14

Proof.

We construct a strong supersolution
\bar{u}_{\lambda}
and a uniform subsolution
\underline{u}_{\lambda}
with respect to
\bar{u}_{\lambda}
. To this end, first consider

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u=\lambda u^{q-1}~\text{in}~\Omega,u=0~\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

(3.7)

We claim that this problem has a unique positive solution
\bar{v}_{\lambda}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega).

For
\lambda>0
and

, we define

g_{\lambda}(t)=\lambda(t^{+})^{q-1},

We set for all

E_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{p}-\int\limits_{\Omega}G_{\lambda}(u)dx.

Clearly,

and

\langle E_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u,v\rangle-\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{q-1}vdx.

Let
\lambda>0
be fixed. By Sobolev embedding theorem, it is easily seen that
E_{\lambda}
is coercive and sequentially weakly lower semicontinuous. Thus there exists
\bar{u}_{\lambda}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
such that

E_{\lambda}(\bar{u}_{\lambda})=\inf_{W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}E_{\lambda}=:m_{\lambda}

(3.8)

Now for all
\nu>0
,

E_{\lambda}(\nu\hat{u}_{1})=\nu^{p}\frac{\|\hat{u}_{1}\|^{p}}{p}-\lambda\nu^{q}\frac{|\hat{u}_{1}|_{q}^{q}}{q}.

Since
q<p
,
E_{\lambda}<0
for all
\nu>0
small enough. Hence in (
3.8
) we have
m_{\lambda}<0
, implying
\bar{u}_{\lambda}\not\equiv 0
. From (
3.8
), we have
E_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\lambda})=0
in
W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, i.e., we have weakly in
\Omega

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\bar{u}_{\lambda}=\lambda(\bar{u}_{\lambda}^{+})^{q-1}~\text{in}~\Omega.

(3.9)

Testing (
3.9
) with
\bar{u}_{\lambda}^{-}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, we get

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}_{\lambda}^{-}\|^{p}\leq

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\bar{u}_{\lambda},\bar{u}_{\lambda}^{-}\rangle=\int\limits_{\Omega}(\bar{u}_{\lambda}^{+})^{q-1}\bar{u}_{\lambda}^{-}=0.

This implies
\bar{u}_{\lambda}\geq 0
. Hence (
3.9
) reads as

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\bar{u}_{\lambda}=\lambda\bar{u}_{\lambda}^{q-1}~\text{weakly in}~\Omega.

(3.10)

By using
[

, Theorem 4.1]
, we have
\bar{u}_{\lambda}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and then by
[

, Theorem 4.4]
, we see that
\bar{u}_{\lambda}\leq C_{1}d^{s}
, for some
C_{1}>0
. Note that
\epsilon\hat{u}_{1}
is a subsolution of (

), for
\epsilon
small enough and so
\bar{u}_{\lambda}\geq\epsilon\hat{u}_{1}
. Thus, there exist
C_{2}>0
such that
C_{2}d^{s}\leq\bar{u}_{\lambda}
. Hence, we have that

C_{2}\leq\frac{\bar{u}_{\lambda}}{d^{s}}\leq C_{1}.

(3.11)

Now, let
v_{\lambda}
be another positive solution of (
3.7
), i.e., we have weakly in
\Omega

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}v_{\lambda}=\lambda v_{\lambda}^{q-1}.

(3.12)

As above,
v_{\lambda}
also satisfy (

In this section, we consider the problem

We follow the techniques used in

Remark 6.1

Due to the nonlocal interactions between

, we have the following inequalities:

(a)

(b)

(c)

Remark 6.2

For any

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u^{\pm},u^{\pm}\rangle\leq\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u,u^{\pm}\rangle\leq\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u,u\rangle.

satisfies
\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u),u^{+}\rangle\geq\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u^{+}),u^{+}\rangle

l_{u}u^{+}+m_{u}u^{-}\in M_{\lambda}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(l_{u}u^{+}+m_{u}u^{-}),l_{u}u^{+}\rangle=0.

Now

\displaystyle\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(l_{u}u^{+}),l_{u}u^{+}\rangle\leq\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(l_{u}u^{+}+m_{u}u^{-}),l_{u}u^{+}\rangle\leq 0

i.e.,

\frac{1}{l_{u}^{2r-p}}\|u^{+}\|^{p}+[u^{+}]_{r}^{2r}\leq\lambda l_{u}^{q-2r}|u^{+}|_{q}^{q}.

(6.11)

On subtracting (
6.10
) from (
6.11
), we get

\left(\frac{1}{l_{u}^{2r-p}}-\frac{1}{l_{0}^{2r-p}}\right)\|u^{+}\|^{p}\ \leq\lambda(l_{u}^{q-2r}-l_{0}^{q-2r})|u^{+}|_{q}^{q}.

(6.12)

If
l_{0}>l_{u}
, we get a contradiction to (
6.12
). Hence we have
l_{0}\leq l_{u}
. Now

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(l_{0},0)=

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(l_{0}u^{+})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(l_{0}u^{+})-\frac{1}{2r}\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(l_{0}u^{+}),l_{0}u^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)l_{0}^{p}\|u^{+}\|^{p}+\lambda\left(\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{1}{q}\right)l_{0}^{q}|u^{+}|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)\|l_{u}u^{+}\|^{p}+\lambda\left(\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{1}{q}\right)|l_{u}u^{+}|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)\|l_{u}u^{+}+m_{u}u^{-}\|^{p}+\lambda\left(\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{1}{q}\right)|l_{u}u^{+}+m_{u}u^{-}|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(l_{u}u^{+}+m_{u}u^{-})-\frac{1}{2r}\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(l_{u}u^{+}+m_{u}u^{-}),l_{u}u^{+}+m_{u}u^{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(l_{u}u^{+}+m_{u}u^{-}).

This yields the contradiction. Similarly
H_{u}
cannot achieve its global maximum on
(0,m)
with
m>0
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.8
.

There exists
u_{\lambda}\in M_{\lambda}
such that
J_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})=c_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset M_{\lambda}
be a minimizing sequence i.e.,
J_{\lambda}(u_{n})\rightarrow c_{\lambda}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
In particular,
J_{\lambda}(u_{n})
is bounded and so we can find
C>0
such that
J_{\lambda}(u_{n})\leq C
for all
n
. Now as in Lemma
6.7
, we see that
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
. Then by reflexivity and the compact embedding there exist
u_{\lambda}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
such that

u_{n}^{\pm}\rightharpoonup u_{\lambda}^{\pm}~\text{in}~W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega),

(6.14)

u_{n}^{\pm}\rightarrow u_{\lambda}^{\pm}~\text{in}~L^{\nu}(\Omega),~\text{for all}~\nu\in[1,p_{s}^{\ast}),

(6.15)

u_{n}^{\pm}(x)\rightarrow u_{\lambda}^{\pm}(x)~\text{a.e}~x\in\Omega.

(6.16)

Clearly, by Lemma
6.6
,
u_{\lambda}^{\pm}\not=0
. So by Lemma
6.4
there exist
(l_{\lambda},m_{\lambda})\in[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)
such that
l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}+m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-}\in M_{\lambda}
. We claim that
l_{\lambda},m_{\lambda}\leq 1
. Without loss of generality assume that
l_{\lambda}\leq m_{\lambda}
. Then from
\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}+m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-}),m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-}\rangle=0
and
l_{\lambda}\leq m_{\lambda}
, we have as in Lemma
6.3

\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}^{2r-p}}X^{-}(u_{\lambda})\geq\lambda m_{\lambda}^{q-2r}|u_{\lambda}^{-}|_{q}^{q}-Y^{-}(u_{\lambda}).

(6.17)

Again since
u_{n}\in M_{\lambda}
, we have
\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}^{-}\rangle=0
. This implies

X^{-}(u_{n})=\lambda|u_{n}^{-}|^{q}_{q}-Y^{-}(u_{n}).

(6.18)

Taking limit
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
6.18
) and using (
6.15
), (
6.16
) and Fatou’s Lemma, we obtain

X^{-}(u_{\lambda})\leq\lambda|u_{\lambda}^{-}|_{q}^{q}-Y^{-}(u_{\lambda}).

(6.19)

On subtracting (
6.19
) from (
6.17
), we obtain

\left(\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}^{2r-p}}-1\right)X^{-}(u_{\lambda})\geq\lambda(m_{\lambda}^{q-2r}-1)|u_{\lambda}^{-}|_{q}^{q}.

(6.20)

Clearly,
m_{\lambda}>1
yields a contradiction to (
6.20
). Hence we have
l_{\lambda}\leq m_{\lambda}\leq 1
. Now,

\displaystyle c_{\lambda}\leq

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}+m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}+m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-})-\frac{1}{2r}\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}+m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-}),l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}+m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)\|l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}+m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-}\|^{p}+\lambda\left(\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{1}{q}\right)|l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}+m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-}|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)\left[l_{\lambda}^{p}\int\limits_{\Omega^{+}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{+}}\frac{|u_{\lambda}^{+}(x)-u_{\lambda}^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx+\int\limits_{\Omega^{-}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{-}}\frac{|u_{\lambda}^{-}(x)-u_{\lambda}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)\left[\int\limits_{\Omega^{+}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{-}}\frac{|l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}(x)-m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx+\int\limits_{\Omega^{-}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{+}}\frac{|l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}(y)-m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-}(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)\left[l_{\lambda}^{p}\int\limits_{\Omega^{+}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{|u_{\lambda}^{+}(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx+l_{\lambda}^{p}\int\limits_{\Omega^{c}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{+}}\frac{|u_{\lambda}^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)\left[m_{\lambda}^{p}\int\limits_{\Omega^{-}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{|u_{\lambda}^{-}(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx+m_{\lambda}^{p}\int\limits_{\Omega^{c}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{-}}\frac{|u_{\lambda}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\left(l_{\lambda}^{q}|u_{\lambda}^{+}|_{q}^{q}+m_{\lambda}^{q}|u_{\lambda}^{-}|_{q}^{q}\right).

(6.21)

Since

,

\displaystyle 0\leq l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}(x)+m_{\lambda}(-u_{\lambda}^{-}(y))\leq u_{\lambda}^{+}(x)-u_{\lambda}^{-}(y)

\displaystyle\implies|l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}(x)-m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-}(y)|^{p}\leq|u_{\lambda}^{+}(x)-u_{\lambda}^{-}(y)|^{p}.

(6.22)

Similarly we have

|m_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{-}(x)-l_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}^{+}(y)|^{p}\leq|u_{\lambda}^{-}(x)-u_{\lambda}^{+}(y)|^{p}.

(6.23)

Using (
6
) and (
6.23
) in (

\displaystyle c_{\lambda}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)\left[\int\limits_{\Omega^{+}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{+}}\frac{|u_{\lambda}^{+}(x)-u_{\lambda}^{+}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx+\int\limits_{\Omega^{-}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{-}}\frac{|u_{\lambda}^{-}(x)-u_{\lambda}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx\right]

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2r}\right)\left[\int\limits_{\Omega^{+}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{-}}\frac{|u_{\lambda}^{+}(x)-u_{\lambda}^{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx+\int\limits_{\Omega^{-}}\int\limits_{\Omega^{+}}\frac{|u_{\lambda}^{+}(y)-u_{\lambda}^{-}(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dydx\right]

A Nesterov type algorithm with double Tikhonov regularization: fast convergence of the function values and strong convergence to the minimal norm solution
Mikhail Karapetyants, Szilárd Csaba László

Abstract




Abstract.
We investigate the strong convergence properties of a Nesterov type algorithm with two Tikhonov regularization terms in connection to the minimization problem of a smooth convex function
f.
We show that the generated sequences converge strongly to the minimal norm element from
\argmin f
. We also show that from a practical point of view the Tikhonov regularization does not affect Nesterov’s optimal convergence rate of order
\mathcal{O}(n^{-2})
for the potential energies
f(x_{n})-\min f
and
f(y_{n})-\min f
, where
(x_{n}),\,(y_{n})
are the sequences generated by our algorithm. Further, we obtain fast convergence to zero of the discrete velocity, but also some estimates concerning the value of the gradient of the objective function in the generated sequences.

Key Words.
inertial algorithm, convex optimization, Tikhonov regularization, strong convergence

AMS subject classification.
34G25, 47J25, 47H05, 90C26, 90C30, 65K10

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space endowed with the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and norm
\|\cdot\|
and consider the optimization problem

\inf_{x\in\mathcal{H}}f(x)

(1)

where
f:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a convex, continuously Fréchet differentiable function, with
L
-Lipschitz continuous gradient, whose set of minimizers
\argmin f
is nonempty.

We associate to the optimization problem (
1
) the following inertial-gradient type algorithm.
Let
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathcal{H}

and for all
k\geq 1
set

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}y_{k}=x_{k}+b_{k-1}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-c_{k}x_{k}\\
x_{k+1}=y_{k}-s{\nabla}f(y_{k})-s\epsilon_{k}y_{k}.\end{array}\right.

(2)

We assume that
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L}\right)
and the sequence
(c_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is nonnegative for
k
big enough and satisfies
\lim_{k\to+\infty}c_{k}=0
. Further, we assume that
(\epsilon_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is a non-increasing positive sequence that satisfies
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\epsilon_{k}=0.

Observe that in case the inertial parameter
(b_{k})_{k\geq 0}
satisfies
\lim_{k\to+\infty}b_{k}=1,
then Algorithm (
2
) has the form of the famous Nesterov algorithm, (see
[
22
,
15
]
and also
[
11
,
18
]
), with two Tikhonov regularization terms. Indeed, the terms
c_{k}x_{k}
and
\epsilon_{k}y_{k}
in Algorithm (
2
) play the role of Tikhonov regularization terms, consequently our aim is to obtain the strong convergence of the generated sequences to the element of minimal norm from
\argmin f,
(see
[
1
,
3
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
20
,
25
,
26
]
) and at the same time to preserve the optimality of Nesterov algorithm concerning the convergence rate of order
\mathcal{O}(k^{-2})
for the potential energy
f(x_{k})-\min f
, (see
[
22
,
15
]
). Our analysis reveals that the inertial parameter and the Tikhonov regularization parameters are strongly correlated. This fact is in concordance with some recent results from the literature concerning the strong convergence of the trajectories of some continuous second order dynamical systems to a minimal norm minimizer of a convex function or to the minimal norm zero of a maximally monotone operator
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
19
]
.
Concerning the discrete case, that is, the case of inertial algorithms that converge strongly to the minimal norm solution of a convex optimization problem, there are only few results in the literature, see
[
10
,
20
]
and also those refer to proximal inertial algorithms obtained via implicit discretizations of some second order continuous dynamical systems, (see
[
24
,
19
]
).

Indeed, in
[
10
]
the following inertial-proximal algorithm was considered in connection to the optimization problem (
1
):
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathcal{H},\,x_{k+1}={\rm prox}_{f}\left(x_{k}+\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{k}\right)(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-\frac{c}{k^{2}}x_{k}\right),

where
\alpha>3,\,c>0
and
\prox\nolimits_{f}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H},\quad\prox\nolimits_{f}(x)=\argmin_{y\in\mathcal{H}}\left(f(y)+\frac{1}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}\right),
denotes the proximal point operator of the convex function
f
. Due to our best knowledge this is the first inertial algorithm in the literature for which both strong convergence results for the generated sequences and fast convergence of the potential energy
f(x_{k})-\min f
and discrete velocity
\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|
were obtained. However, from practical point of view, it is not natural that the minimizers of a smooth function to be approximated via proximal, i.e. backward, steps. Another drawback of this algorithm is that does not assure the full strong convergence of the generated sequences to the minimum norm minimizer
x^{*}
. Indeed, according to
[
10
]
only the strong convergence result
\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|=0
is provided. In order to overcome these deficiencies in
[
20
]
the author assumed that the objective function in (
1
) is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous only and associated to this optimization problem the following inertial-proximal algorithm:
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathcal{H},\,x_{k+1}={\rm prox}_{\lambda_{k}f}\left(x_{k}+\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{k^{q}}\right)(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-\frac{c}{k^{p}}x_{k}\right),

where
\alpha,\,q,\,c,\,p>0
and
(\lambda_{k})
is a sequence of positive real numbers. According to
[
20
]
, in case the stepsize
\lambda_{k}\equiv 1
and
0<q<1,\,1<p<q+1
the full convergence of the generated sequences to the minimum norm minimizer
x^{*}
is obtained, i.e.
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|=0
. Further, the fast convergence of the potential energy
f(x_{k})-\min f
and discrete velocity
\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|
were shown.

In concordance to the results emphasized above, the main goal of this paper is to obtain similar results for gradient type inertial algorithms. Unfortunately our parameters in Algorithm (
2
) will not have such simple forms as the parameters in
[
10
]
or
[
20
]
and this is due to the fact that we cannot use a discrete Lyapunov function of similar form as the ones considered in
[
10
,
20
]
, instead we have to construct a new discrete Lyapunov function suitable for our analysis. Therefore, the forms of the given sequences
(b_{k})_{k\geq 0},\,(c_{k})_{k\geq 1}
are crucial in order to obtain our results. More precisely, for given Tikhonov regularization parameter
(\epsilon_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and a fixed stepsize
s
consider the sequence
(q_{k})_{k\geq 0}
which after an index
k
big enough, satisfies

(Q)\,\,\,\,(1-s\epsilon_{k+1})^{2}q_{k+1}^{2}-(1-s\epsilon_{k})^{2}q_{k}^{2}-2sq_{k+1}+s(1-s\epsilon_{k})^{2}q_{k}\leq 0,\,q_{k}\geq\frac{2s}{(1-s\epsilon_{k})^{2}}.

Then, the inertial parameter
(b_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and the regularization parameter
(c_{k})_{k\geq 1}
from Algorithm (
2
) are defined via the conditions

(B)\,\,\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}b_{k-1}=0,\mbox{ if }k=1\mbox{ or }(1-s\epsilon_{k-1})(1-s\epsilon_{k})q_{k-1}q_{k}=0\\
\\
\displaystyle b_{k-1}=\frac{(q_{k-1}-s)((1-s\epsilon_{k-1})^{2}q_{k-1}-2s)}{(1-s\epsilon_{k-1})(1-s\epsilon_{k})q_{k-1}q_{k}},\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and

(C)\,\,\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}c_{k}=0,\mbox{ if }k=1\mbox{ or }(1-s\epsilon_{k-1})(1-s\epsilon_{k})q_{k-1}q_{k}=0\\
\\
\displaystyle c_{k}=\frac{2s}{(1-s\epsilon_{k-1})(1-s\epsilon_{k})^{2}q_{k}}\left(\frac{s}{q_{k-1}}-\frac{s^{2}\epsilon_{k}}{q_{k-1}}-s(\epsilon_{k-1}-\epsilon_{k})\right),\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Note that despite of the complex form of these parameters, from a practical perspective, Algorithm (
2
) can easily be implemented.

A comprehensive analysis of the above conditions will be carried out in section 4. Here we just underline that in case we specify the parameters as
\epsilon_{k}=\frac{c}{k^{p}},\,c,p>0
and we take
q_{k}=ak^{q},\,a>0,\,0<q<1
then (Q) is satisfied for every fixed stepsize
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L}\right)
and the main result of the paper can be summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
p<2q
let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm (
2
). Then,
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
converge strongly to
x^{*}
, where
\{x^{*}\}=\pr_{\argmin f}(0)
is the minimum norm minimizer of our objective function
f.

Further,
f(x_{k})-\min f=\mathcal{O}\left(k^{-p}\right),\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty,\mbox{ and }f(y_{k})-\min f=\mathcal{O}\left(k^{-p}\right),\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty.
Additionally,
\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|=\ o\left(k^{-\frac{p}{2}}\right)\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty,\,\|{\nabla}f(y_{k})\|=\ o\left(k^{-\frac{p}{2}}\right)\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty\mbox{ and }\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|\ =\ o\left(k^{-\frac{p}{2}}\right)\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we present some preliminary results and notions that we need to carry out our analysis. In section 3 we prove the main result of the paper. We obtain strong convergence of the sequences generated by Algorithm (
2
) and also fast convergence of the potential energy and discrete velocity. In section 4 we consider the parameters in a simple form and discuss the conditions these parameters must satisfy in order to obtain the results presented at section 3. Further, in section 5 via some numerical experiments we show that Algorithm (
2
) indeed assures the convergence of the generated sequences to a minimal norm solution and also that both Tikhonov regularization terms are indispensable in order to obtain this result. Finally, we conclude our paper with some future research plans.

2
Preliminary results

In order to obtain strong convergence for the sequence
(x_{k})
generated by Algorithm (
2
) we need some preliminary results. The first one is the Descent Lemma
[
21
]
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
f:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function, with
L-
Lipschitz continuous gradient. Then,

f(x)\leq f(y)+\langle{\nabla}f(y),x-y\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|y-x\|^{2},\,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

Further, we need the following property of smooth, convex functions, see
[
21
]
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
f:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex smooth function, with
L-
Lipschitz continuous gradient. Then,

\frac{1}{2L}\|{\nabla}f(y)-{\nabla}f(x)\|^{2}+\langle{\nabla}f(y),x-y\rangle+f(y)\leq f(x),\mbox{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

Our first original result is a modified descent lemma, which in particular contains Lemma 1 from
[
5
]
, however has a considerable simplified proof.

Lemma 4
.

Let
f:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex smooth function, with
L-
Lipschitz continuous gradient and let
s>0.
Then,

f(y-s{\nabla}f(y))\leq f(x)+\langle{\nabla}f(y),y-x\rangle+\left(\frac{L}{2}s^{2}-s\right)\|{\nabla}f(y)\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2L}\|{\nabla}f(y)-{\nabla}f(x)\|^{2},\,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

(3)

Assume further that
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L}\right].
Then,

f(y-s{\nabla}f(y))\leq f(x)+\langle{\nabla}f(y),y-x\rangle-\frac{s}{2}\|{\nabla}f(y)\|^{2}-\frac{s}{2}\|{\nabla}f(y)-{\nabla}f(x)\|^{2},\,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

(4)

Proof.

Indeed, by taking
x=y-s{\nabla}f(y)
in Lemma
2
, we get

f(y-s{\nabla}f(y))\leq f(y)+\left(\frac{L}{2}s^{2}-s\right)\|{\nabla}f(y)\|^{2},\,\forall y\in\mathcal{H}.

(5)

From Lemma
3
we have

f(y)\leq f(x)+\langle{\nabla}f(y),y-x\rangle-\frac{1}{2L}\|{\nabla}f(y)-{\nabla}f(x)\|^{2},\,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

(6)

Combining (
5
) and (
6
) we get

f(y-s{\nabla}f(y))\leq f(x)+\langle{\nabla}f(y),y-x\rangle+\left(\frac{L}{2}s^{2}-s\right)\|{\nabla}f(y)\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2L}\|{\nabla}f(y)-{\nabla}f(x)\|^{2},\,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H},

which is nothing else that (
3
).

Assume that
0<s\leq\frac{1}{L}.
Then,

\frac{L}{2}s^{2}-s\leq-\frac{s}{2}\mbox{ and }-\frac{1}{2L}\leq-\frac{s}{2},

hence (
3
) leads to (
4
), that is

f(y-s{\nabla}f(y))\leq f(x)+\langle{\nabla}f(y),y-x\rangle-\frac{s}{2}\|{\nabla}f(y)\|^{2}-\frac{s}{2}\|{\nabla}f(y)-{\nabla}f(x)\|^{2},\,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

∎

We continue the present section by emphasizing the main idea behind the Tikhonov regularization, which will assure strong convergence results for the sequence generated our algorithm (
2
) to a minimizer of minimal norm of the objective function
f
. By
\overline{x}_{k}
we denote the unique solution of the strongly convex minimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\left(f(x)+\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{2}\|x\|^{2}\right).

We know, (see for instance
[
8
]
), that
\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\overline{x}_{k}=x^{\ast}
, where
x^{\ast}=\argmin\limits_{x\in\argmin f}\|x\|
is the minimal norm element from the set
\argmin f.
Obviously,
\{x^{*}\}=\pr_{\argmin f}0
and we have the inequality
\|\overline{x}_{k}\|\leq\|x^{\ast}\|
(see
[
12
]
).

Since
\overline{x}_{k}
is the unique minimizer of the strongly convex function
f_{k}(x)=f(x)+\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{2}\|x\|^{2},
obviously one has

{\nabla}f_{k}(\overline{x}_{k})={\nabla}f(\overline{x}_{k})+\epsilon_{k}\overline{x}_{k}=0.

(7)

Further, from Lemma A.1 c) from
[
20
]
we have

\left\|\overline{x}_{k+1}-\overline{x}_{k}\right\|\leq\min\left(\frac{\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{k+1}}{\epsilon_{k+1}}\|\overline{x}_{k}\|,\frac{\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{k+1}}{\epsilon_{k}}\|\overline{x}_{k+1}\|\right).

(8)

Note that since
f_{k}
is strongly convex, from the gradient inequality we have

f_{k}(y)-f_{k}(x)\geq\langle{\nabla}f_{k}(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{2}\|x-y\|^{2},\mbox{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

(9)

In particular

f_{k}(x)-f_{k}(\overline{x}_{k})\geq\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{2}\|x-\overline{x}_{k}\|^{2},\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathcal{H}.

(10)

Moreover, observe that for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
, one has

f(x)-f(y)=(f_{k}(x)-f_{k}(\overline{x}_{k}))+(f_{k}(\overline{x}_{k})-f_{k}(y))+\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{2}(\|y\|^{2}-\|x\|^{2})\leq f_{k}(x)-f_{k}(\overline{x}_{k})+\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{2}\|y\|^{2}.

(11)

Note that
{\nabla}f_{k}(x)={\nabla}f(x)+\epsilon_{k}x
, consequently if
{\nabla}f
is L-Lipschitz continuous then the Lipschitz constant of the gradient of
f_{k}
is
L+\epsilon_{k}.

Hence, if we apply Lemma
4
to
f_{k}
we get that for all
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L+\epsilon_{k}}\right]
one has

f_{k}(y-s{\nabla}f_{k}(y))\leq f_{k}(x)+\langle{\nabla}f_{k}(y),y-x\rangle-\frac{s}{2}\|{\nabla}f_{k}(y)\|^{2}-\frac{s}{2}\|{\nabla}f_{k}(y)-{\nabla}f_{k}(x)\|^{2},\,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

(12)

Now, we can rewrite Algorithm (
2
) in a more convenable equivalent form, by using the strongly convex function
f_{k}.
Indeed, since
{\nabla}f_{k}(x)={\nabla}f(x)+\epsilon_{k}x
, Algorithm (
2
) can equivalently be written as:

x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
and for all
k\geq 1

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}y_{k}=x_{k}+b_{k-1}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-c_{k}x_{k}\\
x_{k+1}=y_{k}-s{\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k}).\end{array}\right.

(13)

3
Strong convergence

In this section we provide sufficient conditions such that the sequences generated by (
13
) converge strongly to the minimum norm minimizer of
f
and at the same time fast convergence of the function values in the generated sequences and also fast convergence of the discrete velocity to zero are obtained. Moreover, we also show some pointwise estimates for the gradient of the objective function.

In order to obtain our general result concerning the strong convergence of the sequences generated by the algorithm (
13
) we need to use (
12
), hence we adjust the indexes in algorithm (
13
) as follows.

Assume that
0<s<\frac{1}{L}
and let
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following assumption holds.

(S)\,\,\,\,s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L+\epsilon_{k_{0}}}\right]\subseteq\left(0,\frac{1}{L+\epsilon_{k}}\right],\mbox{ for all }k\geq k_{0}.

Note that such index
k_{0}
exists, since
\epsilon_{k}
is nonincreasing and
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty.

Further, since
\epsilon_{k}
is nonincreasing there exists
k_{1}\geq k_{0}
such that
1-s\epsilon_{k}>0
for all
k\geq k_{1}.

Consider the sequence
(q_{k})_{k\geq 0}
which after an index
k_{2}\geq k_{1}
, satisfies the condition (Q), that is

(Q)\,\,\,\,(1-s\epsilon_{k+1})^{2}q_{k+1}^{2}-(1-s\epsilon_{k})^{2}q_{k}^{2}-2sq_{k+1}+s(1-s\epsilon_{k})^{2}q_{k}\leq 0,\,q_{k}\geq\frac{2s}{(1-s\epsilon_{k})^{2}}

for all
k\geq k_{2}.

Note that
q_{k}\geq 2s>0
for all
k\geq k_{2}.

Let
\overline{k}=k_{2}+1
and observe that
(1-s\epsilon_{k-1})(1-s\epsilon_{k})q_{k-1}q_{k}>0
for all
k\geq\overline{k}
, consequently the sequences
b_{k}
and
c_{k}
defined at (B) and (C) have the following forms:

b_{k-1}=\frac{(q_{k-1}-s)((1-s\epsilon_{k-1})^{2}q_{k-1}-2s)}{(1-s\epsilon_{k-1})(1-s\epsilon_{k})q_{k-1}q_{k}},\mbox{ for all }k\geq\overline{k}

and

c_{k}=\frac{2s}{(1-s\epsilon_{k-1})(1-s\epsilon_{k})^{2}q_{k}}\left(\frac{s}{q_{k-1}}-\frac{s^{2}\epsilon_{k}}{q_{k-1}}-s(\epsilon_{k-1}-\epsilon_{k})\right),\mbox{ for all }k\geq\overline{k}.

The following general result holds.

Theorem 5
.

For a sequence
(q_{k})
satisfying (Q) and the stepsize
s
satisfying (S), consider the sequences
(b_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(c_{k})_{k\geq 1}
defined at (B) and (C) and let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0},\,(y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm (
2
).
Assume that the sequence
\left(\frac{q_{k}\epsilon_{k}}{q_{k-1}\epsilon_{k-1}}\right)_{k\geq\overline{k}}
is bounded, the sequence
(q_{k}^{2}\epsilon_{k})_{k\geq\overline{k}-1}
is increasing, further
\lim_{k\to+\infty}q_{k}^{2}\epsilon_{k}=+\infty
and

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{q_{k}(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{k+1})}{\epsilon_{k}}=0
.

Then,
(x_{k})
converges strongly to
x^{*}
, where
\{x^{*}\}=\pr_{\argmin f}(0)
is the minimum norm minimizer of our objective function
f.
Moreover
\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|\ =\ o\left(\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}}\right)\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty,
hence
(y_{k})
also converges strongly to
x^{*}.

Further, the following estimates hold.

f_{k}(x_{k})-f_{k}(\overline{x}_{k})=o(\epsilon_{k})\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty,

f(x_{k})-\min f=\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{k}\right),\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty\mbox{ and }f(y_{k})-\min f=\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{k}\right),\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty,

\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|\ =\ o\left(\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}}\right)\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty,

and

\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|=\ o\left(\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}}\right)\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty\mbox{ and }\|{\nabla}f(y_{k})\|=\ o\left(\sqrt{\epsilon_{k}}\right)\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty.

Proof.

Assume that
k\geq\overline{k}
. We take
y=y_{k},\,x=x_{k}
in (
12
) and we get

f_{k}(x_{k+1})\leq f_{k}(x_{k})+\langle{\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k}),y_{k}-x_{k}\rangle-\frac{s}{2}\|{\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{s}{2}\|{\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k})-{\nabla}f_{k}(x_{k})\|^{2},\,\forall k\geq\overline{k}.

(14)

Now we take
y=y_{k},\,x=x^{*}
in (
12
) and taking into account that
{\nabla}f(x^{*})=0
we get

f_{k}(x_{k+1})\leq f_{k}(x^{*})+\langle{\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k}),y_{k}-x^{*}\rangle-\frac{s}{2}\|{\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{s}{2}\|{\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k})-\epsilon_{k}x^{*}\|^{2},\,\forall k\geq\overline{k}.

(15)

Consider the sequence
(p_{k})_{k\geq\overline{k}}
defined by

p_{k}=\frac{(1-s\epsilon_{k})^{2}q_{k}^{2}}{2s}-q_{k},

(16)

for all
k\geq\overline{k}.
Note that due to assumption
(Q)
one has
p_{k}\geq 0
for all
k\geq\overline{k}.

We multiply (
14
) with
p_{k}
and (
15
) with
q_{k}
and add to get

\displaystyle(p_{k}+q_{k})(f_{k}(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*}))-

\displaystyle p_{k}(f_{k}(x_{k})-f(x^{*}))\leq

(17)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k}),(p_{k}+q_{k})y_{k}-p_{k}x_{k}+(s\epsilon_{k}-1)q_{k}x^{*}-\frac{s}{2}(p_{k}+q_{k}){\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k})\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{s}{2}p_{k}\|{\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k})-{\nabla}f_{k}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{s}{2}q_{k}\|{\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{q_{k}}{2}\epsilon_{k}(s\epsilon_{k}-1)\|x^{*}\|^{2},

for all
k\geq\overline{k}.

Now by neglecting the nonpositive terms from the right hand side of (
17
) we obtain

\displaystyle(p_{k}+q_{k})(f_{k}(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*}))-

\displaystyle p_{k}(f_{k}(x_{k})-f(x^{*}))-q_{k}\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{2}\|x^{*}\|^{2}\leq

(18)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k}),(p_{k}+q_{k})y_{k}-p_{k}x_{k}+(s\epsilon_{k}-1)q_{k}x^{*}-\frac{s}{2}(p_{k}+q_{k}){\nabla}f_{k}(y_{k})\right\rangle,

for all
k\geq\overline{k}.

Further, by using the fact that
(\epsilon_{k})
is nonincreasing we have

f_{k}(x_{k+1})=f_{k+1}(x_{k+1})+\frac{\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{k+1}}{2}\|x_{k+1}\|^{2}\geq f_{k+1}(x_{k+1}),

consequently it holds

Theorem 6
.

Let
0<q<1
and
0<p<2q
and for a fixed the stepsize
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L}\right)

consider the sequences
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}},\,(y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by Algorithm (
29
). Then,
(x_{k})
and
(y_{k})
converge strongly to
x^{*}
, where
\{x^{*}\}=\pr_{\argmin f}(0)
is the minimum norm minimizer of our objective function
f.

Further,

f_{k}(x_{k})-f_{k}(\overline{x}_{k})=o(k^{-p})\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty,

f(x_{k})-\min f=\mathcal{O}\left(k^{-p}\right),\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty\mbox{ and }f(y_{k})-\min f=\mathcal{O}\left(k^{-p}\right),\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty,

\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|\ =\ o\left(k^{-\frac{p}{2}}\right)\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty

and

\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|\ =\ o\left(k^{-\frac{p}{2}}\right)\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty\mbox{ and }\|{\nabla}f(y_{k})\|\ =\ o\left(k^{-\frac{p}{2}}\right)\mbox{ as }k\to+\infty

Proof.

We only need to show that the following conditions from the hypotheses of Theorem
5
hold:

•

the sequence
\frac{q_{k}\epsilon_{k}}{q_{k-1}\epsilon_{k-1}}=\frac{k^{q-p}}{(k-1)^{q-p}}
is bounded if we take the starting index big enough;

•

the sequence
q_{k}^{2}\epsilon_{k}=a^{2}ck^{2q-p}
is increasing after a starting index big enough;

•

\lim_{k\to+\infty}q_{k}^{2}\epsilon_{k}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}a^{2}ck^{2q-p}\ =\ +\infty
;

•

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{q_{k}(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{k+1})}{\epsilon_{k}}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{ak^{q}\left(\frac{1}{k^{p}}-\frac{1}{(k+1)^{p}}\right)}{\frac{1}{k^{p}}}\ =\ 0
.

First of all, the sequence
\frac{k^{q-p}}{(k-1)^{q-p}}
is indeed bounded if we take the starting index big enough since
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{k^{q-p}}{(k-1)^{q-p}}=1
. Secondly, the sequence
a^{2}ck^{2q-p}
is increasing when

and
\lim_{k\to+\infty}a^{2}ck^{2q-p}\ =\ +\infty

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{ak^{q}\left(\frac{1}{k^{p}}-\frac{1}{(k+1)^{p}}\right)}{\frac{1}{k^{p}}}\ =\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{ak^{q}}{k+1}\frac{(k+1)^{p}-k^{p}}{(k+1)^{p-1}}=0,

.

We emphasize that Algorithm (

) can be seen as a Nesterov type algorithm with two Tikhonov regularization terms.
Indeed, the extrapolation parameter

6
Conclusions, perspectives

Due to our best knowledge, Algorithm (
2
) and in particular Algorithm (
29
) are the first inertial gradient type algorithms considered in the literature that assure strong convergence to the minimum norm minimizer of a smooth convex function and also fast convergence of the function values and discrete velocity. As we have emphasized in the paper these algorithms can be seen as Nesterov type algorithms with two Tikhonov regularization terms. Despite of the complex structure of the inertial parameter and one of the Tikhonov regularization parameters our algorithms can easily be implemented, therefore are suitable for use in practical problems arising in image processing and machine learning. As a future related research we mention here the forward-backward algorithms with Tikhonov regularization associated to the minimization problem having in its objective the sum of a proper convex lower semicontinuous function and a smooth convex function with Lipschitz continuous gradient. In our opinion similar results to those provided in Theorem
6
can be obtained. Indeed, the success of such research is promising taking into account that in
[
20
]
strong convergence of an inertial-proximal algorithm to the minimal norm minimizer of a proper convex and lower semicontinuous function is shown, meanwhile in the present paper we obtained similar results for inertial gradient type algorithm in connection to a smooth convex optimization problem.

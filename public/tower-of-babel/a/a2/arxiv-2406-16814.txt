Convergence analysis of a stochastic heavy-ball method for linear ill-posed problems
Qinian Jin, Yanjun Liu

Abstract
. In this paper we consider a stochastic heavy-ball method for solving linear ill-posed inverse problems. With suitable choices of the step-sizes and the momentum coefficients, we establish the regularization property of the method under a priori selection of the stopping index and derive the rate of convergence under a benchmark source condition on the sought solution. Numerical results are provided to test the performance of the method.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider linear ill-posed inverse problems governed by the system

\displaystyle A_{i}x=y_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,p

(1.1)

consisting of
p
linear equations, where, for each
i=1,\cdots,p
,
A_{i}:X\to Y_{i}

is a bounded linear operator between two real Hilbert spaces
X
and
Y_{i}
. Such inverse problems
arise in many practical applications, including, for instance,

\bullet

linear inverse problems with discrete data; see
[
1
]
.

\bullet

reconstruction problems arising in various tomography imaging, such as computed tomography and photoacoustic/thermoacoustic tomography, see
[
4
,
14
,
16
]
.

\bullet

discretizing infinite-dimensional linear inverse problems by projection
or collocation methods leads to large scale linear system of the form (
1.1
);
see
[
6
]
.

Throughout the paper we assume (
1.1
) has a solution. Since (
1.1
) may have
many solutions, we will determine the
x_{0}
-minimal norm solution
x^{\dagger}
, i.e. the
solution with the shortest distance to
x_{0}
, where
x_{0}\in X
is an initial guess. Let

Y:=Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{p}
be the product space of
Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{p}
with the
natural inner product inherited from those of
Y_{i}
. By setting
y:=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{p})

and introducing the bounded linear operator
A:X\to Y
by

Ax:=(A_{1}x,\cdots,A_{p}x),\quad x\in X,

we may rewrite (
1.1
) equivalently as

\displaystyle Ax=y.

(1.2)

In practical applications, data are usually acquired by measurements. Therefore, instead of
the exact data
y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{p})
, we have only noisy data
y^{\delta}:=(y_{1}^{\delta},\cdots,y_{p}^{\delta})

satisfying

\displaystyle\|y_{i}^{\delta}-y_{i}\|\leq\delta_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,p,

(1.3)

where
\delta_{i}>0
denotes the noise level corresponding to the data in the space
Y_{i}
. Let

\delta:=\sqrt{\delta_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\delta_{p}^{2}}

which denotes the total noise level of the noisy data. It is thus important to design algorithm
to produce stable approximation of
x^{\dagger}
using only the noisy data
y^{\delta}
. Due to the
ill-posedness of the underlying problems, regularization techniques are required to achieve
the goal. Many regularization methods have been developed to solve ill-posed inverse problems;
see
[
6
]
and the references therein.

The most prominent iterative regularization method for solving (
1.2
) is the Landweber iteration

\displaystyle x_{n+1}^{\delta}=x_{n}^{\delta}-\eta A^{*}(Ax_{n}^{\delta}-y^{\delta})

(1.4)

which can be viewed as the gradient method with constant step size
\eta>0
applied to the quadratic function

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-y^{\delta}\|^{2},\quad x\in X,

(1.5)

where
A^{*}:Y\to X
denotes the adjoint of
A
given by

A^{*}v=\sum_{i=1}^{p}A_{i}^{*}v_{i},\quad\forall v:=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{p})\in Y

in terms of the adjoint
A_{i}^{*}
of
A_{i}
for all
i=1,\cdots,p
. The regularization property of
Landweber iteration has been carried out in detail in the literature, see
[
6
]
. It is known
that Landweber iteration is a slowly convergent method.

In order to accelerate the Landweber iteration, multiple-step iterations have been
considered to modify the method. In particular, based on using orthogonal polynomials,
the two-step methods of the form

\displaystyle x_{n+1}^{\delta}=x_{n}^{\delta}-\alpha_{n}\eta A^{*}(Ax_{n}^{\delta}-y^{\delta})+\beta_{n}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n-1}^{\delta})

(1.6)

have been investigated in
[
8
]
for
0<\eta<1/\|A\|^{2}
and suitable choices of
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
.
On the other hand, to determine a minimizer of the minimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}f(x)

(1.7)

with a continuous differentiable objective function
f
, the heavy ball method

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\alpha_{n}\eta\nabla f(x_{n})+\beta_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})

(1.8)

has been proposed in
[
17
]
to speed up the usual gradient descent method. The
performance of (
1.8
) depends crucially on the property of
f
and the choices of

\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
, and its analysis turns out to be very challenging. When
f
is
twice continuous differentiable, strongly convex and has Lipschitz continuous gradient,
it has been demonstrated in
[
17
]
, under suitable constant choices of
\alpha_{n}
and

\beta_{n}
, that the iterative sequence
\{x_{n}\}
enjoys a provable linear convergence
faster than the gradient descent. In the recent paper
[
7
]
, for merely convex
objective functions, a convergence rate has been derived in terms of the objective
function value under the choices
\alpha_{n}=1/(n+2)
and
\beta_{n}=n/(n+2)
with a suitable
small constant
\eta>0
. Note that, for the quadratic function
f
given in (
1.5
),
the method (
1.8
) becomes the method (
1.6
) and thus (
1.6
) is a special
case of (
1.8
). However, the convergence results obtained in
[
7
,
17
]
are not applicable
straightforwardly to (
1.6
) when used to solve ill-posed inverse problems. Indeed, if the results
in
[
7
,
17
]
were applicable, one would obtain a solution of the linear least square problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}\|Ax-y^{\delta}\|^{2}

(1.9)

that generally may not have a solution unless
y^{\delta}\in\mbox{Ran}(A)\oplus\mbox{Ran}(A)^{\perp}
, see

[
6
]
, which is rarely satisfied because
y^{\delta}
contains irregular noise and
\mbox{Ran}(A)

is non-closed. Even if (
1.9
) has a solution, this solution could be far away from the soluton of
(
1.2
) because of the ill-posedness.

Note that the implementation of the Landweber iteration (
1.4
) and the heavy ball method
(
1.6
) requires to calculate

\displaystyle A^{*}(Ax_{n}^{\delta}-y^{\delta})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}A_{i}^{*}(A_{i}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i}^{\delta})

(1.10)

at each iteration step. In case
p
is huge, this calculation can be time-consuming. In order
to reduce the computational load per iteration, we may consider using one random term selected
from the right hand side of (
1.10
) to carry out the iteration step. Applying this
randomization strategy to the Landweber iteration (
1.4
) leads to the stochastic gradient
descent method for solving ill-posed problems, see
[
9
,
13
,
15
]
for instance.
Applying the same randomization strategy to (
1.6
) gives the following stochastic
heavy-ball method

\displaystyle x_{n+1}^{\delta}=x_{n}^{\delta}-\alpha_{n}\eta_{i_{n}}A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta})+\beta_{n}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n-1}^{\delta})

(1.11)

we will consider in this paper, where
x_{-1}^{\delta}=x_{0}^{\delta}:=x_{0}\in X
is the initial guess,

i_{n}\in\{1,\cdots,p\}
is randomly chosen via the uniform distribution, and
\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{p}

are preassigned step sizes. This method differs from the stochastic gradient descent method in that
a momentum term
\beta_{n}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n-1}^{\delta})
is added to define iterates. The appearance of this
momentum term brings challenging issues for analyzing the method. However, suitable choices of the
step-size coefficient
\alpha_{n}
and the momentum coefficient
\beta_{n}
may accelerate the convergence
speed. How to choose
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
is an important question. In this paper we will consider the
method (
1.11
) with
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=\frac{1}{n+2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta_{n}=\frac{n}{n+2}

(1.12)

and investigate the convergence behavior of the iterates. The stochastic version of the heavy ball
method (
1.8
) for the general minimization problems (
1.7
), where the objective
f
is
a finite sum of convex functions, has been considered in
[
18
]
. The convergence result
in
[
18
]
however is not applicable to (
1.11
) when used to solve ill-posed problems
because of the same reason explained above. Therefore, new analysis should be developed to understand
the stochastic heavy ball method for ill-posed problems. Like all the other iterative regularization
methods, when (
1.11
) is used to solve ill-posed inverse problems, it exhibits the semi-convergence
phenomenon, i.e. the iterate tends to the sought solution at the beginning, and, after a critical
number of iterations, the iterate leaves away from the sought solution as the iteration proceeds.
Thus, properly terminating the iteration is crucial for producing acceptable approximate
solutions. One may hope to determine a stopping
index
n_{\delta}
such that
\|x_{n_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|
to be as small as possible and

\|x_{n_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
. In this paper, by establishing
a stability estimate and exploring the structure of the method, we propose
a priori

stopping rules to terminate the method (
1.11
). When the sought solution
x^{\dagger}

satisfies a benchmark source condition, we derive the estimate on
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]
which enables us to derive a converge rate when the stopping index is properly
chosen. Furthermore, we extend the method (
1.11
) to determine solutions of ill-posed
problems in Banach spaces with special features other than the minimal norm property.
To the best of our knowledge, our paper is the first study on the stochastic heavy ball
method as an iterative regularization method in the context of ill-posed inverse problems.
We hope our work can stimulate interest on this topic which contains many interesting
and challenging questions that deserve further study.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we first establish a stability estimate,
and, when the sought solution satisfies a benchmark source condition, we derive a
convergence rate result, we then use a density argument to demonstrate the regularization
property of our method under an
a priori
stopping rule without relying on any
source conditions. In Section
3
, we extend the method to cover ill-posed
inverse problems in Banach spaces with the ability of capturing special feature of sought
solutions. Finally, in Section
4
, we report extensive numerical results to
illustrate the performance of the method.

2.
Convergence analysis

In this section we will provide a convergence analysis on the method (
1.11
) with

\{\alpha_{n}\}
and
\{\beta_{n}\}
given by (
1.12
) under
a priori
stopping rules.
For convenience of references, we reformulate the method as the following algorithm.

Algorithm 1
.

Take an initial guess
x_{0}\in X
, choose suitable positive numbers
\eta_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,p
,
and set
x_{0}^{\delta}:=x_{0}
. For
n\geq 0
do the following:

(i)

Pick an index
i_{n}\in\{1,\cdots,p\}
randomly via the uniform distribution;

(ii)

Update
x_{n+1}^{\delta}
by (
1.11
) with
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
given in (
1.12
).

Note that, once
x_{0}\in X
and
\eta_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,p
, are fixed, the sequence

\{x_{n}^{\delta}\}
is completely determined by the sample path
\{i_{0},i_{1},\cdots\}
; changing
the sample path can result in a different iterative sequence and thus
\{x_{n}^{\delta}\}
is a
random sequence. Let
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\emptyset
and, for each integer
n\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{F}_{n}
denote
the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the random variables
i_{k}
for
0\leq k<n
. Then

\{\mathcal{F}_{n}:n\geq 0\}
form a filtration which is natural to Algorithm
1
. Let
{\mathbb{E}}

denote the expectation associated with this filtration, see
[
3
]
. The tower property

{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathbb{E}}[\phi|\mathcal{F}_{n}]]={\mathbb{E}}[\phi]\quad\mbox{ for any random variable }\phi

will be frequently used.

In order to carry out the convergence analysis, we adopt the iterate moving-average viewpoint to
rewrite the method (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) equivalently as

\displaystyle\begin{split}z_{n+1}^{\delta}&=z_{n}^{\delta}-\eta_{i_{n}}A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}),\\
x_{n+1}^{\delta}&=\frac{n+1}{n+2}x_{n}^{\delta}+\frac{1}{n+2}z_{n+1}^{\delta}\end{split}

(2.1)

with
z_{0}^{\delta}=x_{0}^{\delta}=x_{0}
. Furthermore, we also need to consider the sequence

\{x_{n}\}
defined by Algorithm
1
using exact data
y
in place of the noisy
data
y^{\delta}
, i.e.

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\alpha_{n}\eta_{i_{n}}A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}})+\beta_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})

(2.2)

with
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
given by (
1.12
) and
x_{-1}=x_{0}
. It is easy to see that
(
2.2
) can be equivalently stated as

\displaystyle\begin{split}z_{n+1}&=z_{n}-\eta_{i_{n}}A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}}),\\
x_{n+1}&=\frac{n+1}{n+2}x_{n}+\frac{1}{n+2}z_{n+1}\end{split}

(2.3)

with
z_{0}=x_{0}
. In the following analysis we will frequently use the polarization
identity which states that in any real Hilbert space there holds

\displaystyle\|u-v\|^{2}=\|u\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2}-2\langle u,v\rangle,\quad\forall u,v.

(2.4)

In particular there holds

\displaystyle 2\langle u-v,u\rangle\geq\|u\|^{2}-\|v\|^{2},\quad\forall u,v.

(2.5)

2.1.
Stability estimate

According to the definition of
x_{n}^{\delta}
and
x_{n}
, one can easily see that, for any fixed
integer
n\geq 0
, there holds
\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n}\|\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
along any sample
path and thus
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n}\|^{2}]\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
. The following result gives
a quantitative estimate of this kind of stability.

Lemma 2.1
.

Consider the sequences
\{x_{n}^{\delta}\}
and
\{x_{n}\}
defined by Algorithm
1
using noisy
data and exact data respectively. Assume that

0<\eta_{i}<1/\|A_{i}\|^{2}
for
i=1,\cdots,p
. Then for all integers
n\geq 0
there holds

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n}\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\bar{\eta}}{c_{0}p}n\delta^{2},

(2.6)

where
\bar{\eta}:=\max\{\eta_{i}:i=1,\cdots,p\}
and
c_{0}:=\min\{1-\eta_{i}\|A_{i}\|^{2}:i=1,\cdots,p\}
.

Proof.

By using the equivalent definition of
\{x_{n}^{\delta}\}
and
\{x_{n}\}
given by (
2.1
) and (
2.3
)
respectively, we first show that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\bar{\eta}}{c_{0}p}n\delta^{2}

(2.7)

for all integers
n\geq 0
. Since
z_{0}^{\delta}=z_{0}=x_{0}
, it is trivial for
n=0
. We now derive
(
2.7
) for all
n\geq 1
. From (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) it follows that along any
sample path there hold

\displaystyle\begin{split}z_{n+1}^{\delta}-z_{n+1}&=z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}-\eta_{i_{n}}A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}),\\
x_{n+1}^{\delta}-x_{n+1}&=\frac{n+1}{n+2}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})+\frac{1}{n+2}(z_{n+1}^{\delta}-z_{n+1}).\end{split}

(2.8)

Therefore, by the polarization identity (
2.4
) we have

\displaystyle\|z_{n+1}^{\delta}-z_{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}-\eta_{i_{n}}A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}\|^{2}-2\eta_{i_{n}}\langle z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n},A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\,+\eta_{i_{n}}^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}\|^{2}-2\eta_{i_{n}}\langle A_{i_{n}}(z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}),A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\,+\eta_{i_{n}}^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}\|^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}\|^{2}-2\eta_{i_{n}}\langle A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n}),A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\eta_{i_{n}}\langle A_{i_{n}}((z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n})-(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})),A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\,+\eta_{i_{n}}^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}\|^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}.

According to the second equation in (
2.8
) we have

\displaystyle(z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n})-(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})=n\left((x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-(x_{n-1}^{\delta}-x_{n-1})\right).

Thus

\displaystyle\|z_{n+1}^{\delta}-z_{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}\|^{2}-2\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\eta_{i_{n}}\langle y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}-y_{i_{n}},A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\,-2n\eta_{i_{n}}\langle A_{i_{n}}((x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-(x_{n-1}^{\delta}-x_{n-1})),A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\,+\eta_{i_{n}}^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}\|^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}.

By virtue of (
1.3
) and the polarization identity (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|z_{n+1}^{\delta}-z_{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}\|^{2}-2\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+2\eta_{i_{n}}\delta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\|

\displaystyle\quad\,-n\eta_{i_{n}}\left(\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}-\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n-1}^{\delta}-x_{n-1})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\,-n\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}((x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-(x_{n-1}^{\delta}-x_{n-1}))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\eta_{i_{n}}^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}\|^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}\|^{2}-(n+2-\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}\|^{2})\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+n\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n-1}^{\delta}-x_{n-1})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+2\eta_{i_{n}}\delta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}+y_{i_{n}}\|.

Taking the conditional expectation on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n+1}^{\delta}-z_{n+1}\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{n}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}(x_{n-1}^{\delta}-x_{n-1})-y_{i}^{\delta}+y_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}(n+2-\eta_{i}\|A_{i}\|^{2})\eta_{i}\|A_{i}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i}^{\delta}+y_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{2}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\delta_{i}\|A_{i}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i}^{\delta}+y_{i}\|.

By using the tower property of expectation and noting that
n+2-\eta_{i}\|A_{i}\|^{2}\geq n+1+c_{0}

for
i=1,\cdots,p
, we then obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n+1}^{\delta}-z_{n+1}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}\|^{2}]+\frac{n}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}(x_{n-1}^{\delta}-x_{n-1})-y_{i}^{\delta}+y_{i}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{n+1+c_{0}}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i}^{\delta}+y_{i}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{2}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\delta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i}^{\delta}+y_{i}\|].

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\delta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i}^{\delta}+y_{i}\|]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\delta_{i}\left({\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i}^{\delta}+y_{i}\|^{2}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{0}}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i}^{\delta}+y_{i}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{1}{c_{0}p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\delta_{i}^{2}.

Consequently

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|z_{n+1}^{\delta}-z_{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{n+1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i}^{\delta}+y_{i}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|z_{n}^{\delta}-z_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{n}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}(x_{n-1}^{\delta}-x_{n-1})-y_{i}^{\delta}+y_{i}\|^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{c_{0}p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\delta_{i}^{2}

for all integers
n\geq 0
. Since
z_{0}^{\delta}=z_{0}
, by recursively using this inequality we thus obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|z_{n+1}^{\delta}-z_{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{n+1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n})-y_{i}^{\delta}+y_{i}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+1}{c_{0}p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\delta_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\bar{\eta}(n+1)\delta^{2}}{c_{0}p}

for all integers
n\geq 0
which implies (
2.7
).

Next we show (
2.6
). It is trivial for
n=0
as
x_{0}^{\delta}=x_{0}
.
Assume it is true for some integer
n\geq 0
. It follows from the second equation in
(
2.8
) and the convexity of the function
x\to\|x\|^{2}
that

\|x_{n+1}^{\delta}-x_{n+1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{n+1}{n+2}\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{n+2}\|z_{n+1}^{\delta}-z_{n+1}\|^{2}.

Therefore, by using the induction hypothesis and (
2.7
), we can obtain

{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n+1}^{\delta}-x_{n+1}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{\bar{\eta}(n+1)\delta^{2}}{c_{0}p}
.
This completes the proof of (
2.6
).
∎

2.2.
Rate of convergence

In this subsection we will focus on deriving an estimate on
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]

when the
x_{0}
-minimal norm solution
x^{\dagger}
satisfies the benchmark source condition

\displaystyle x^{\dagger}-x_{0}=A^{*}\lambda^{\dagger}

(2.9)

for some
\lambda^{\dagger}:=(\lambda_{1}^{\dagger},\cdots,\lambda_{p}^{\dagger})\in Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{p}
.
Based on this result we can make an
a priori
choice of the stopping index to
derive a convergence rate in terms of the noise level
\delta
. By noting that

\displaystyle\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n}\|+\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|)^{2}\leq 2(\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n}\|^{2}+\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}),

we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]\leq 2{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x_{n}\|^{2}]+2{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}].

(2.10)

Thus, by virtue of Lemma
2.1
, we only need to estimate
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]

under the source condition (
2.9
). To this end, we make use of the equivalent
formulation of the method (
2.3
) as follows

\displaystyle x_{n}=x_{0}+A^{*}\lambda_{n},

(2.11)

where
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is a sequence in
Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{p}
defined as follows:

\lambda_{-1}=\lambda_{0}=0
and when
\lambda_{n}=(\lambda_{n,1},\cdots,\lambda_{n,p})
is defined, we calculate

x_{n}
by (
2.11
) and define

\lambda_{n+1}:=(\lambda_{n+1,1},\cdots,\lambda_{n+1,p})
by

\displaystyle\lambda_{n+1,i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\lambda_{n,i}+\beta_{n}(\lambda_{n,i}-\lambda_{n-1,i})&\mbox{if }i\neq i_{n},\\[4.30554pt]
\lambda_{n,i_{n}}+\beta_{n}(\lambda_{n,i_{n}}-\lambda_{n-1,i_{n}})-\alpha_{n}\eta_{i_{n}}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}})&\mbox{if }i=i_{n}.\end{array}\right.

One can easily check that this defined sequence
\{x_{n}\}
coincides with the one defined by
(
2.3
). We have the following result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Consider the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
defined by Algorithm
1
using exact data and let

\{\lambda_{n}\}
be defined as in (
2.2
) with
\lambda_{-1}=\lambda_{0}=0
. Assume that
the source condition (
2.9
) holds. Let

u_{n}:=\lambda_{n}+n(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-1})\quad\mbox{and}\quad r_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\|u_{n,i}-\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}

for all integers
n\geq 0
, where
u_{n,i}
denotes the
i
-th component
of
u_{n}
in
Y_{i}
. Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[r_{n+1}+\frac{n+2}{p}\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[r_{n}+\frac{n}{p}\|x_{n-1}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|A_{i}x_{n}-y_{i}\|^{2}\right].

Proof.

By the definition of
u_{n+1}
and the formulae of
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
, it is easy
to see that

\displaystyle u_{n+1,i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{n,i}&\mbox{ if }i\neq i_{n},\\
u_{n,i_{n}}-\eta_{i_{n}}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}})&\mbox{ if }i=i_{n}.\end{array}\right.

Thus, by the polarization identity (
2.4
) and
A_{i_{n}}x^{\dagger}=y_{i_{n}}
we have

\displaystyle r_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq i_{n}}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\|u_{n+1,i}-\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\eta_{i_{n}}}\|u_{n+1,i_{n}}-\lambda_{i_{n}}^{\dagger}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq i_{n}}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\|u_{n,i}-\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\eta_{i_{n}}}\|u_{n,i_{n}}-\lambda_{i_{n}}^{\dagger}-\eta_{i_{n}}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq i_{n}}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\|u_{n,i}-\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\eta_{i_{n}}}\|u_{n,i_{n}}-\lambda_{i_{n}}^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\langle u_{n,i_{n}}-\lambda_{i_{n}}^{\dagger},A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-x^{\dagger})\rangle

\displaystyle=r_{n}+\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}-2\langle A_{i_{n}}^{*}(u_{n,i_{n}}-\lambda_{i_{n}}^{\dagger}),x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\rangle.

Taking the conditional expectation on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[r_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]

\displaystyle=r_{n}+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}x_{n}-y_{i}\|^{2}-\frac{2}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{p}A_{i}^{*}(u_{n,i}-\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}),x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=r_{n}+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}x_{n}-y_{i}\|^{2}-\frac{2}{p}\left\langle A^{*}(u_{n}-\lambda^{\dagger}),x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle.

Consequently

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[r_{n+1}]={\mathbb{E}}[{\mathbb{E}}[r_{n+1}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}[r_{n}]+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}x_{n}-y_{i}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{2}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle A^{*}(u_{n}-\lambda^{\dagger}),x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle\right].

By the definition of
u_{n}
, (
2.9
) and (
2.11
) we have

A^{*}(u_{n}-\lambda^{\dagger})=x_{n}-x^{\dagger}+n(x_{n}-x_{n-1})
. Therefore

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[r_{n+1}]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}[r_{n}]+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}x_{n}-y_{i}\|^{2}]-\frac{2}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{2n}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle x_{n}-x_{n-1},x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}[r_{n}]+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}x_{n}-y_{i}\|^{2}]-\frac{2}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{n}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}-\|x_{n-1}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right],

where for the last step we used (
2.5
). Regrouping the terms thus completes the proof.
∎

In order to use Lemma
2.2
to derive an estimate on
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]

with
\eta_{i}
being as large as possible, we need the following result which enables us to cancel
out the summation term on the right hand side of the inequality in Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{z_{n}\}
be defined by (
2.3
). Assume
0<\eta_{i}<1/\|A_{i}\|^{2}

for
i=1,\cdots,p
. Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|z_{n+1}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{n+1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}x_{n}-y_{i}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|z_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{n}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}x_{n-1}-y_{i}\|^{2}\right]-\frac{c_{0}}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}x_{n}-y_{i}\|^{2}],

where
c_{0}
is the positive constant defined in Lemma
2.1
.

Proof.

By the definition of
z_{n+1}
and the polarization identity (
2.4
) we have

\displaystyle\|z_{n+1}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|z_{n}-x^{\dagger}-\eta_{i_{n}}A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|z_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}-2\eta_{i_{n}}\langle A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}}),z_{n}-x^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\,+\eta_{i_{n}}^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}})\|^{2}.

Note that
z_{n}=x_{n}+n(x_{n}-x_{n-1})
and
A_{i_{n}}x^{\dagger}=y_{i_{n}}
. Therefore,
we may use (
2.5
) to obtain

\displaystyle 2\eta_{i_{n}}\langle A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}}),z_{n}-x^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\eta_{i_{n}}\langle A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}},A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}}\rangle+2n\eta_{i_{n}}\langle A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}},A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 2\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}+n\eta_{i_{n}}\left(\|A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}-\|A_{i_{n}}x_{n-1}-y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}\right).

Consequently

\displaystyle\|z_{n+1}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+n\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}x_{n-1}-y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,-\left(n+2-\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}\|^{2}\right)\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}x_{n}-y_{i_{n}}\|^{2}.

Taking the full expectation gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n+1}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}[{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n+1}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]+\frac{n}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}x_{n-1}-y_{i}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}(n+2-\eta_{i}\|A_{i}\|^{2})\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}x_{n}-y_{i}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]+\frac{n}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}x_{n-1}-y_{i}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{n+1+c_{0}}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}x_{n}-y_{i}\|^{2}].

(2.14)

Regrouping the terms we thus obtain the desired inequality.
∎

By using Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
, we can obtain the following
estimate on
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]
under the source condition (
2.9
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Consider the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
defined by Algorithm
1
using exact data.
Assume
0<\eta_{i}<1/\|A_{i}\|^{2}
for
i=1,\cdots,p
. If the
x_{0}
-minimal norm
solution
x^{\dagger}
satisfies the source condition (
2.9
), then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{pM_{0}}{c_{0}(n+1)}

for all integers
n\geq 0
, where
c_{0}>0
is the constant from Lemma
2.1
and

M_{0}:=\|x_{0}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+c_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\|\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}
.

Proof.

By virtue of Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|z_{n+1}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{n+1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}x_{n}-y_{i}\|^{2}+c_{0}\left(r_{n+1}+\frac{n+1}{p}\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|z_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{n}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}x_{n-1}-y_{i}\|^{2}+c_{0}\left(r_{n}+\frac{n}{p}\|x_{n-1}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right)\right]

for all integers
n=0,1,\cdots
. Recursively using this inequality gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|z_{n+1}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{n+1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}x_{n}-y_{i}\|^{2}+c_{0}\left(r_{n+1}+\frac{n+1}{p}\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|z_{0}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+c_{0}r_{0}\right]=\|x_{0}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+c_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\|\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}=:M_{0}.

This in particular implies that

\displaystyle\frac{(n+1)c_{0}}{p}{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]\leq M_{0}

which shows the desired estimate.
∎

Based on Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.4
, we can now prove the following
convergence rate result on the stochastic gradient method with heavy-ball momentum
under an
a priori
stopping rule.

Theorem 2.5
.

Consider the sequence
\{x_{n}^{\delta}\}
defined by Algorithm
1
.
Assume
0<\eta_{i}<1/\|A_{i}\|^{2}
for
i=1,\cdots,p
. If the
x_{0}
-minimal norm
solution
x^{\dagger}
satisfies the source condition (
2.9
), then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{2pM_{0}}{c_{0}(n+1)}+\frac{2\bar{\eta}}{c_{0}p}n\delta^{2}

for all integers
n\geq 0
. Consequently, if the integer
n_{\delta}
is chosen such that

(n_{\delta}+1)/p\sim\delta^{-1}
, then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]\leq C\delta,

where
C
is a constant depending only on
c_{0}
,
\bar{\eta}
and
M_{0}
.

Proof.

By invoking the inequality (
2.10
), we can use Lemma
2.1
and
Lemma
2.4
to conclude the proof immediately.
∎

2.3.
Convergence

In Theorem
2.5
we have established a convergence rate result for Algorithm
1

when the
x_{0}
-minimal norm solution
x^{\dagger}
satisfies the source condition (
2.9
). This source
condition might be too strong to be satisfied in applications. It is necessary to establish
a convergence result on Algorithm
1
without using any source condition on
x^{\dagger}
.
In this subsection we will prove the following convergence result.

Theorem 2.6
.

Consider the sequence
\{x_{n}^{\delta}\}
defined by Algorithm
1
.
Assume that
0<\eta_{i}<1/\|A_{i}\|^{2}
for
i=1,\cdots,p
and let
x^{\dagger}
denote the unique

x_{0}
-minimal norm solution of (
1.1
). Then for the integer
n_{\delta}

chosen such that
n_{\delta}\to\infty
and
\delta^{2}n_{\delta}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
there holds

{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\delta\to 0.

Considering the stability estimate given in Lemma
2.1
, we will establish Theorem
2.6

by showing that
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
defined by
Algorithm
1
using exact data. Due to the randomness of
i_{n}
and the momentum term

\beta_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})
involved in (
2.2
),
it is highly nontrivial to derive
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]\to 0
straightforwardly. We will
use a perturbation argument developed in
[
10
,
11
]
. Namely, as an
x_{0}
-minimal
norm solution, there holds
x^{\dagger}-x_{0}\in\mbox{Null}(A)^{\perp}=\overline{\mbox{Ran}(A^{*})}
,
and thus we may choose
\hat{x}_{0}\in X
as close to
x_{0}
as we want such that

x^{\dagger}-\hat{x}_{0}\in\mbox{Ran}(A^{*})
. Define
\{\hat{x}_{n}\}
by

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{n+1}=\hat{x}_{n}-\alpha_{n}A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}\hat{x}_{n}-y_{i_{n}})+\beta_{n}(\hat{x}_{n}-\hat{x}_{n-1})

(2.15)

with
\hat{x}_{-1}=\hat{x}_{0}
and the index
i_{n}\in\{1,\cdots,p\}
chosen uniformly at random.
We will establish
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]\to 0
as
n\to\infty

by deriving estimates on
{\mathbb{E}}[\|\hat{x}_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]
and
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n}\|^{2}]
.

For
{\mathbb{E}}[\|\hat{x}_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]
we can apply the same argument in the proof of
Lemma
2.4
to the sequence
\{\hat{x}_{n}\}
to obtain the following result.

Lemma 2.7
.

Consider the sequence
\{\hat{x}_{n}\}
defined by (
2.15
), where
\hat{x}_{0}
is chosen such that

x^{\dagger}-\hat{x}_{0}\in\emph{Ran}(A^{*})
. Assume that
0<\eta_{i}<1/\|A_{i}\|^{2}
for
i=1,\cdots,p
.
Then for any integer
n\geq 0
there holds

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|\hat{x}_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{p\hat{M}_{0}}{c_{0}(n+1)},

where
\hat{M}_{0}:=\|\hat{x}_{0}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+c_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\|\hat{\lambda}_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}

and
\hat{\lambda}^{\dagger}:=(\hat{\lambda}_{1}^{\dagger},\cdots,\hat{\lambda}_{p}^{\dagger})\in Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{p}

is such that
x^{\dagger}-\hat{x}_{0}=A^{*}\hat{\lambda}^{\dagger}
.

We next derive estimate on
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n}\|^{2}]
in terms of
\|x_{0}-\hat{x}_{0}\|^{2}
.
We have the following result.

Lemma 2.8
.

Consider the sequences
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{\hat{x}_{n}\}
defined by (
2.2
) and (
2.15
)
respectively. Assume that
0<\eta_{i}\leq 1/\|A_{i}\|^{2}
for
i=1,\cdots,p
. Then for all
n\geq 0

there holds

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n}\|^{2}]\leq\|x_{0}-\hat{x}_{0}\|^{2}.

(2.16)

Proof.

Note that the sequence
\{\hat{x}_{n}\}
from (
2.15
) can be equivalently defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{z}_{n+1}&=\hat{z}_{n}-\eta_{i_{n}}A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}\hat{x}_{n}-y_{i_{n}}),\\
\hat{x}_{n+1}&=\frac{n+1}{n+2}\hat{x}_{n}+\frac{1}{n+2}\hat{z}_{n+1}\end{split}

(2.17)

with
\hat{z}_{0}=\hat{x}_{0}
. We first use (
2.3
) and (
2.17
) to show that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n}-\hat{z}_{n}\|^{2}]\leq\|x_{0}-\hat{x}_{0}\|^{2},\quad\forall n\geq 0

(2.18)

which is trivial for
n=0
. In order to establish (
2.18
) for all integers
n\geq 1
, by
virtue of (
2.3
) and (
2.17
) we can see that along any sample path there holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}z_{n+1}-\hat{z}_{n+1}&=z_{n}-\hat{z}_{n}-\eta_{i_{n}}A_{i_{n}}^{*}A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n}),\\
x_{n+1}-\hat{x}_{n+1}&=\frac{n+1}{n+2}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})+\frac{1}{n+2}(z_{n+1}-\hat{z}_{n+1}).\end{split}

(2.19)

Therefore, by using the polarization identity (
2.4
) we can obtain

\displaystyle\|z_{n+1}-\hat{z}_{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|z_{n}-\hat{z}_{n}-\eta_{i_{n}}A_{i_{n}}^{*}A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|z_{n}-\hat{z}_{n}\|^{2}+\eta_{i_{n}}^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}^{*}A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\eta_{i_{n}}\langle z_{n}-\hat{z}_{n},A_{i_{n}}^{*}A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\rangle

\displaystyle=\|z_{n}-\hat{z}_{n}\|^{2}+\eta_{i_{n}}^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}^{*}A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\eta_{i_{n}}\langle(z_{n}-\hat{z}_{n})-(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n}),A_{i_{n}}^{*}A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\eta_{i_{n}}\langle x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n},A_{i_{n}}^{*}A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\rangle.

Using the second equation in (
2.19
) and the polarization identity (
2.4
) we further have

\displaystyle\|z_{n+1}-\hat{z}_{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{n}-\hat{z}_{n}\|^{2}+\eta_{i_{n}}^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}\|^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,-2n\eta_{i_{n}}\langle A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})-A_{i_{n}}(x_{n-1}-\hat{x}_{n-1}),A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|z_{n}-\hat{z}_{n}\|^{2}+\eta_{i_{n}}^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}\|^{2}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,-n\eta_{i_{n}}\left(\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\|^{2}-\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n-1}-\hat{x}_{n-1})\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\,-n\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})-A_{i_{n}}(x_{n-1}-\hat{x}_{n-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,-2\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{n}-\hat{z}_{n}\|^{2}+n\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n-1}-\hat{x}_{n-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,-(n+2-\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}\|^{2})\eta_{i_{n}}\|A_{i_{n}}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\|^{2}.

Taking the full expectation gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n+1}-\hat{z}_{n+1}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}[{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n+1}-\hat{z}_{n+1}\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n}-\hat{z}_{n}\|^{2}]+\frac{n}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}(x_{n-1}-\hat{x}_{n-1})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}(n+2-\eta_{i}\|A_{i}\|^{2})\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\|^{2}].

Since
0<\eta_{i}\leq 1/\|A_{i}\|^{2}
for
i=1,\cdots,p
, we can obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n+1}-\hat{z}_{n+1}\|^{2}]+\frac{n+1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n}-\hat{z}_{n}\|^{2}]+\frac{n}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}(x_{n-1}-\hat{x}_{n-1})\|^{2}]

for all integers
n\geq 0
. Recursively using this inequality gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n+1}-\hat{z}_{n+1}\|^{2}]+\frac{n+1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}(x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n})\|^{2}]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{0}-\hat{z}_{0}\|^{2}]=\|x_{0}-\hat{x}_{0}\|^{2}

which shows (
2.18
).

Next we show (
2.16
). It is trivial for
n=0
. Assume
it is also true for some
n
. By noting from the second equation in (
2.19
) that

x_{n+1}-\hat{x}_{n+1}
is a convex combination of
x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n}
and

z_{n+1}-\hat{z}_{n+1}
, we can obtain

\|x_{n+1}-\hat{x}_{n+1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{n+1}{n+2}\|x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{n+2}\|z_{n+1}-\hat{z}_{n+1}\|^{2}

Thus, we may use the induction hypothesis and (
2.18
) to obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n+1}-\hat{x}_{n+1}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+1}{n+2}{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n}\|^{2}]+\frac{1}{n+2}{\mathbb{E}}[\|z_{n+1}-\hat{z}_{n+1}\|^{2}]\leq\|x_{0}-\hat{x}_{0}\|^{2}.

The proof is therefore complete.
∎

Based on Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
2.8
, we can now prove the convergence of
Algorithm
1
using exact data.

Theorem 2.9
.

Consider the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
defined by Algorithm
1
using exact data. Assume that

0<\eta_{i}<1/\|A_{i}\|^{2}
for
i=1,\cdots,p
and let
x^{\dagger}
denote the unique
x_{0}
-minimal
norm solution of (
1.1
). Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]=0.

Proof.

Since
x^{\dagger}-x_{0}\in\overline{\mbox{Ran}(A^{*})}
, for any
\varepsilon>0
we
can find
\hat{x}_{0}\in X
such that
\|x_{0}-\hat{x}_{0}\|<\varepsilon
and

x^{\dagger}-\hat{x}_{0}\in\mbox{Ran}(A^{*})
. Define
\{\hat{x}_{n}\}
as in (
2.15
). Then we have from Lemma
2.8
that

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n}\|^{2}\right]\leq\|x_{0}-\hat{x}_{0}\|^{2}<\varepsilon^{2}.

Moreover, it follows from Lemma
2.7
that

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|\hat{x}_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]\leq C(n+1)^{-1}

for some constant
C
which may depend on
\varepsilon
but is independent of
n
. Consequently

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[(\|x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n}\|+\|\hat{x}_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n}-\hat{x}_{n}\|^{2}+\|\hat{x}_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon^{2}+2C(n+1)^{-1}.

Therefore

\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]\leq 2\varepsilon^{2}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we must have
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]\to 0

as
n\to\infty
.
∎

By using Theorem
2.9
and Lemma
2.1
we are now ready to prove Theorem
2.6
, the main convergence result on Algorithm
1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

According to (
2.10
) we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x_{n_{\delta}}\|^{2}\right]+2{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n_{\delta}}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right].

Since
n_{\delta}\to\infty
, we may use Theorem
2.9
to obtain

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n_{\delta}}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\right]\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\delta\to 0.

By using Lemma
2.1
and
\delta^{2}n_{\delta}\to 0
, we also have

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|x_{n_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x_{n_{\delta}}\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\bar{\eta}}{c_{0}p}\delta^{2}n_{\delta}\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\delta\to 0.

Therefore
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
.
∎

Due to the ill-posedness of the underlying problems, any iterative regularization method
using noisy data, include Algorithm
1
, exhibits the semi-convergence property.
Therefore, a proper termination of the iteration is crucial for a satisfactory reconstruction.
In Theorem
2.4
and Theorem
2.9
we provided convergence analysis of
Algorithm
1
under
a priori
choices of the stopping index, where
\eta_{i_{n}}

is chosen by

\eta_{i_{n}}=\mu_{0}/\|A_{i_{n}}\|^{2},\quad 0<\mu_{0}<1;

(2.20)

In particular, Theorem
2.4
indicates that the choice of the stopping
index depends crucially on the information of the source condition which is usually unknown.
A wrong guess of the source condition may lead to a bad choice of the stopping index and hence
a bad reconstruction result. For deterministic iterative regularization methods,
a posteriori

stopping rules have been widely used to resolve this issue and the discrepancy principle is the
most prominent one (
[
6
]
). In
[
13
]
the discrepancy principle has been
successfully incorporated into the stochastic mirror descent method, including the stochastic
gradient descent method as a special case, to remove the semi-convergence property. It is natural
to expect that this can also been done for Algorithm
1
. Assuming the noise levels

\delta_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,p
, are known, we may incorporate the discrepancy principle into
Algorithm
1
by taking
\eta_{i_{n}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\eta_{i_{n}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mu_{0}/\|A_{i_{n}}\|^{2}&\mbox{ if }\|A_{i_{n}}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta}\|>\tau\delta_{i_{n}},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise},\end{array}\right.

where
0<\mu_{0}<1
and
\tau>1
are user specified constants. Although no convergence theory is
available for Algorithm
1
with
\eta_{i_{n}}
chosen by (
2.3
), we will provide
numerical simulations to demonstrate that this procedure indeed can reduce the effect of semi-convergence
property.

3.
Extension

In the previous section we have considered the stochastic heavy ball method for solving
linear ill-posed system (
1.1
) in Hilbert spaces and demonstrated the convergence of the
iterates to the unique
x_{0}
-minimal norm solution. In many applications, the sought solution
may not sit in a Hilbert space and it may have some special feature to be recovered.
Algorithm
1
does not have the capability to deal with these issues. In this section
we will extend Algorithm
1
to cover these situations. Consider again the linear system
(
1.1
) but with each
A_{i}:X\to Y_{i}
being a bounded linear operator from a fixed Banach
spaces
X
to a Hilbert space
Y_{i}
. We introduce a proper, lower semi-continuous,
strongly convex function
\mathcal{R}:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
with the aim of capturing the feature of the
sought solution. Here
\mathcal{R}
is called
\mu
-strongly convex for some constant
\mu>0
if

\mathcal{R}(t\bar{x}+(1-t)x)+\mu t(1-t)\|\bar{x}-x\|^{2}\leq t\mathcal{R}(\bar{x})+(1-t)\mathcal{R}(x)

for all
\bar{x},x\in\mbox{dom}(\mathcal{R})
and
0\leq t\leq 1
, where
\mbox{dom}(\mathcal{R}):=\{x\in X:\mathcal{R}(x)<\infty\}

is called the effective domain of
\mathcal{R}
. We then consider finding a solution
x^{\dagger}
of (
1.1
)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(x^{\dagger})=\min\left\{\mathcal{R}(x):A_{i}x=y_{i},\ i=1,\cdots,p\right\}

(3.1)

which is called a
\mathcal{R}
-minimizing solution of (
1.1
). Assuming (
1.1
) has a solution in

\mbox{dom}(\mathcal{R})
, by a standard argument it is easy to see that the
\mathcal{R}
-minimizing solution
exists and is unique. We will determine the unique
\mathcal{R}
-minimizing solution
x^{\dagger}
approximately
by extending the stochastic heavy ball method. To this end, we consider the dual problem
of (
3.1
) which is given by

\displaystyle\min_{\lambda\in Y}\left\{\mathcal{R}^{*}(A^{*}\lambda)-\langle\lambda,y\rangle\right\},

(3.2)

where
\mathcal{R}^{*}:X^{*}\to(-\infty,\infty]
denotes the convex conjugate of
\mathcal{R}
, i.e.

\mathcal{R}^{*}(\xi):=\sup_{x\in X}\left\{\langle\xi,x\rangle-\mathcal{R}(x)\right\},\quad\forall\xi\in X^{*}.

Since
\mathcal{R}
is strongly convex,
\mathcal{R}^{*}
is continuous differentiable, its gradient

\nabla\mathcal{R}^{*}
maps
X^{*}
to
X
and

\|\nabla\mathcal{R}^{*}(\bar{\xi})-\nabla\mathcal{R}^{*}(\xi)\|\leq\frac{\|\bar{\xi}-\xi\|}{2\mu},\quad\forall\bar{\xi},\xi\in X^{*};

see
[
19
, Corollary 3.5.11]
. Applying the heavy ball method (
1.8
) to solve
(
3.2
) gives

\displaystyle\lambda_{n+1}=\lambda_{n}-\alpha_{n}\eta(A\nabla\mathcal{R}^{*}(A^{*}\lambda_{n})-y)+\beta_{n}(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n-1}),

where
\eta>0
is a suitable constant.
By setting
\xi_{n}:=A^{*}\lambda_{n}
and
x_{n}=\nabla\mathcal{R}^{*}(A^{*}\lambda_{n})
, we then obtain

\displaystyle x_{n}=\nabla\mathcal{R}^{*}(\xi_{n}),\quad\xi_{n+1}=\xi_{n}-\alpha_{n}\eta A^{*}(Ax_{n}-y)+\beta_{n}(\xi_{n}-\xi_{n-1}).

In case only noisy data
y^{\delta}
is available, we may replace
y
by
y^{\delta}
in the above equation
to obtain the method

\displaystyle x_{n}^{\delta}=\nabla\mathcal{R}^{*}(\xi_{n}^{\delta}),\quad\xi_{n+1}^{\delta}=\xi_{n}^{\delta}-\alpha_{n}\eta A^{*}(Ax_{n}^{\delta}-y^{\delta})+\beta_{n}(\xi_{n}^{\delta}-\xi_{n-1}^{\delta}).

Note that

\displaystyle A^{*}(Ax_{n}^{\delta}-y^{\delta})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}A_{i}^{*}(A_{i}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i}^{\delta}).

(3.3)

Therefore, the implementation of the above method requires to calculate
A_{i}^{*}(A_{i}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i}^{\delta})

for all
i=1,\cdots,p
at each iteration step; in case
p
is huge, this requires a huge amount of
computational work. In order to reduce the computational load per iteration, we may consider using
a randomly selected term from the right hand side of (
3.3
) and then carry out the iteration step.
Repeating this procedure leads to the following stochastic method for solving (
3.1
) in
Banach spaces.

Algorithm 2
.

Let
\xi_{-1}^{\delta}=\xi_{0}^{\delta}=0\in X^{*}
and take suitable numbers
\eta_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,p
.
For
n\geq 0
do the following:

(i)

Calculate
x_{n}^{\delta}
by

x_{n}^{\delta}=\nabla\mathcal{R}^{*}(\xi_{n}^{\delta})=\arg\min\{\mathcal{R}(x)-\langle\xi_{n}^{\delta},x\rangle\};

(ii)

Pick an index
i_{n}\in\{1,\cdots,p\}
randomly via the uniform distribution;

(iii)

Update
\xi_{n+1}^{\delta}
by

\displaystyle\xi_{n+1}^{\delta}

\displaystyle=\xi_{n}^{\delta}-\alpha_{n}\eta_{i_{n}}A_{i_{n}}^{*}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta})+\beta_{n}(\xi_{n}^{\delta}-\xi_{n-1}^{\delta})

with
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
given by (
1.12
).

According to the definition of
x_{n}^{\delta}
and the subdifferential calculus, we have

\xi_{n}^{\delta}\in\partial\mathcal{R}(x_{n}^{\delta})
, where
\partial\mathcal{R}
denotes the subdifferential of
\mathcal{R}
, i.e.

\partial\mathcal{R}(x):=\{\xi\in X^{*}:\mathcal{R}(\bar{x})\geq\mathcal{R}(x)+\langle\xi,\bar{x}-x\rangle\mbox{ for all }\bar{x}\in X\},\quad\forall x\in X.

In order to analyze the method, we use the Bregman distance induced by
\mathcal{R}
. Given
x\in X

with
\partial\mathcal{R}(x)\neq\emptyset
and
\xi\in\partial\mathcal{R}(x)
we define

D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi}(\bar{x},x):=\mathcal{R}(\bar{x})-\mathcal{R}(x)-\langle\xi,\bar{x}-x\rangle,\quad\forall\bar{x}\in X

which is called the Bregman distance induced by
\mathcal{R}
at
x
in the direction
\xi
.

In the following we derive an estimate on
{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]
under the
benchmark source condition

\displaystyle\xi^{\dagger}:=A^{*}\lambda^{\dagger}\in\partial\mathcal{R}(x^{\dagger})

(3.4)

on the sought solution
x^{\dagger}
, where

\lambda^{\dagger}:=(\lambda_{1}^{\dagger},\cdots,\lambda_{p}^{\dagger})\in Y:=Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{p}
.
We need the following elementary result (see
[
13
]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}
and
\{b_{n}\}
be two sequences of nonnegative numbers such that

a_{n}^{2}\leq b_{n}^{2}+c\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j},\quad n=0,1,\cdots,

where
c\geq 0
is a constant. If
\{b_{n}\}
is non-decreasing, then

a_{n}\leq b_{n}+cn,\quad n=0,1,\cdots.

Theorem 3.2
.

Consider Algorithm
2
and assume that
0<\eta_{i}<2\mu/\|A\|^{2}
for
i=1,\cdots,p
.
If the unique
\mathcal{R}
-minimizing solution
x^{\dagger}
satisfies the source condition (
3.4
),
then there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
\mu
,
\|A\|^{2}
and
\eta_{i}
such that

{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]\leq C\left(\frac{pM_{0}}{n+1}+\frac{1}{p}(n+1)\delta^{2}+\delta^{2}\right),

where
M_{0}:=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\|\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}
. Consequently, if the integer
n_{\delta}
is
chosen such that
(n_{\delta}+1)/p\sim\delta^{-1}
, then

{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n_{\delta}}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]=O(\delta).

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\{x_{n}^{\delta}\}
and
\{\xi_{n}^{\delta}\}
from Algorithm
2

can be equivalently written as

x_{n}^{\delta}=\nabla\mathcal{R}^{*}(\xi_{n}^{\delta}),\qquad\xi_{n+1}^{\delta}=A^{*}\lambda_{n+1}^{\delta},

where
\lambda_{n+1}^{\delta}:=(\lambda_{n+1,1}^{\delta},\cdots,\lambda_{n+1,p}^{\delta})\in Y
is defined by

\displaystyle\lambda_{n+1,i}^{\delta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\lambda_{n,i}^{\delta}+\beta_{n}(\lambda_{n,i}^{\delta}-\lambda_{n-1,i}^{\delta})&\mbox{if }i\neq i_{n},\\[4.30554pt]
\lambda_{n,i_{n}}^{\delta}+\beta_{n}(\lambda_{n,i_{n}}^{\delta}-\lambda_{n-1,i_{n}})^{\delta}-\alpha_{n}\eta_{i_{n}}(A_{i_{n}}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i_{n}}^{\delta})&\mbox{if }i=i_{n}\end{array}\right.

with
\lambda_{-1}=\lambda_{0}=0
. As in Lemma
2.2
we define

u_{n}^{\delta}:=\lambda_{n}^{\delta}+n(\lambda_{n}^{\delta}-\lambda_{n-1}^{\delta})\quad\mbox{and}\quad r_{n}^{\delta}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\|u_{n,i}^{\delta}-\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}

for all integers
n\geq 0
, where
u_{n,i}^{\delta}
denotes the
i
-th component of
u_{n}^{\delta}
in
Y_{i}
.
Following the proof of Lemma
2.2
we can obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[r_{n+1}^{\delta}]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}[r_{n}^{\delta}]+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i}^{\delta}\|^{2}]-\frac{2}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle A^{*}(u_{n}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger}),x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{2\bar{\eta}^{1/2}}{p}\delta\left({\mathbb{E}}[r_{n}^{\delta}]\right)^{1/2}.

By the definition of
u_{n}^{\delta}
and
\xi_{n}^{\delta}
, we have
A^{*}(u_{n}^{\delta}-\lambda^{\dagger})=\xi_{n}^{\delta}-\xi^{\dagger}+n(\xi_{n}^{\delta}-\xi_{n-1}^{\delta})
. Consequently

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[r_{n+1}^{\delta}]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}[r_{n}^{\delta}]+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i}^{\delta}\|^{2}]-\frac{2}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle\xi_{n}^{\delta}-\xi^{\dagger},x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{2n}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\langle\xi_{n}^{\delta}-\xi_{n-1}^{\delta},x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\rangle\right]+\frac{2\bar{\eta}^{1/2}}{p}\delta\left({\mathbb{E}}[r_{n}^{\delta}]\right)^{1/2}.

By straightforward calculation we can see that

\langle\xi_{n}^{\delta}-\xi^{\dagger},x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\rangle=D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{n}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{n}^{\delta})+D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi^{\dagger}}(x_{n}^{\delta},x^{\dagger})

and

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{n}^{\delta}-\xi_{n-1}^{\delta},x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle=D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{n}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{n}^{\delta})-D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{n-1}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{n-1}^{\delta})+D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{n-1}^{\delta}}(x_{n}^{\delta},x_{n-1}^{\delta})

\displaystyle\geq D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{n}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{n}^{\delta})-D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{n-1}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{n-1}^{\delta}).

Therefore

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[r_{n+1}^{\delta}]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}[r_{n}^{\delta}]+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}{\mathbb{E}}[\|A_{i}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i}^{\delta}\|^{2}]-\frac{2}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\left[D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{n}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{n}^{\delta})+D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi^{\dagger}}(x_{n}^{\delta},x^{\dagger})\right]

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{2n}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\left[D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{n}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{n}^{\delta})-D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{n-1}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{n-1}^{\delta})\right]+\frac{2\bar{\eta}^{1/2}}{p}\delta\left({\mathbb{E}}[r_{n}^{\delta}]\right)^{1/2}.

Letting
\Delta_{n}^{\delta}:=D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi_{n}^{\delta}}(x^{\dagger},x_{n}^{\delta})
and regrouping the terms, we can obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[r_{n+1}^{\delta}+\frac{2(n+1)}{p}\Delta_{n}^{\delta}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[r_{n}^{\delta}+\frac{2n}{p}\Delta_{n-1}^{\delta}\right]+\frac{1}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i}^{\delta}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\,-\frac{2}{p}D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi^{\dagger}}(x_{n}^{\delta},x^{\dagger})+\frac{2\bar{\eta}^{1/2}}{p}\delta\left({\mathbb{E}}[r_{n}^{\delta}]\right)^{1/2}.

(3.5)

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}\|A_{i}x_{n}^{\delta}-y_{i}^{\delta}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\eta}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(\|A_{i}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})\|+\delta_{i}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\eta}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left((1+\varepsilon)\|A_{i}(x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})\|^{2}+(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\delta_{i}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon)\bar{\eta}\|A(x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})\|^{2}+(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\bar{\eta}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\bar{\eta}\|A\|^{2}\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\bar{\eta}\delta^{2},

where
\varepsilon>0
is any number. Since
\mathcal{R}
is
\mu
-strongly convex, we have

D_{\mathcal{R}}^{\xi^{\dagger}}(x_{n}^{\delta},x^{\dagger})\geq\mu\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}.

Combining the above two equations with (
3.5
) gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[r_{n+1}^{\delta}+\frac{2(n+1)}{p}\Delta_{n}^{\delta}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[r_{n}^{\delta}+\frac{2n}{p}\Delta_{n-1}^{\delta}\right]-\frac{1}{p}\left(2\mu-(1+\varepsilon)\bar{\eta}\|A\|^{2}\right)\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{1}{p}(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\bar{\eta}\delta^{2}+\frac{2\bar{\eta}^{1/2}}{p}\delta\left({\mathbb{E}}[r_{n}^{\delta}]\right)^{1/2}.

According to the given condition on
\eta_{i}
we have
\bar{\eta}<2\mu/\|A\|^{2}
.
We may take
\varepsilon:=2\mu/(\bar{\eta}\|A\|^{2})-1>0
to obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[r_{n+1}^{\delta}+\frac{2(n+1)}{p}\Delta_{n}^{\delta}\right]\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[r_{n}^{\delta}+\frac{2n}{p}\Delta_{n-1}^{\delta}\right]+\frac{1}{p}C_{0}\delta^{2}+\frac{2\bar{\eta}^{1/2}}{p}\delta\left({\mathbb{E}}[r_{n}^{\delta}]\right)^{1/2},

where
C_{0}:=(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\bar{\eta}
. Recursively using this inequality gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[r_{n+1}^{\delta}+\frac{2(n+1)}{p}\Delta_{n}^{\delta}\right]\leq M_{0}+\frac{(n+1)C_{0}\delta^{2}}{p}+\frac{2\bar{\eta}^{1/2}}{p}\delta\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left({\mathbb{E}}[r_{k}^{\delta}]\right)^{1/2},

(3.6)

where

\displaystyle M_{0}:={\mathbb{E}}\left[r_{0}^{\delta}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\|\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}\|^{2}.

This in particular implies that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[r_{n+1}^{\delta}]\leq M_{0}+\frac{(n+1)C_{0}\delta^{2}}{p}+\frac{2\bar{\eta}^{1/2}}{p}\delta\sum_{k=0}^{n}({\mathbb{E}}[r_{k}^{\delta}])^{1/2}.

Thus, by using Lemma
3.1
we can obtain

\displaystyle({\mathbb{E}}[r_{k}^{\delta}])^{1/2}\leq\left(M_{0}+\frac{C_{0}k}{p}\delta^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{2\bar{\eta}^{1/2}}{p}k\delta\leq M_{0}^{1/2}+\left(\frac{C_{0}k}{p}\right)^{1/2}\delta+\frac{2\bar{\eta}^{1/2}}{p}k\delta

for all integers
k\geq 0
. Consequently

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}({\mathbb{E}}[r_{k}^{\delta}])^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq M_{0}^{1/2}(n+1)+\left(\frac{C_{0}}{p}\right)^{1/2}\delta\sum_{k=0}^{n}k^{1/2}+\frac{2\bar{\eta}^{1/2}}{p}\delta\sum_{k=0}^{n}k

\displaystyle\leq M_{0}^{1/2}(n+1)+\left(\frac{2C_{0}^{1/2}(n+1)^{3/2}}{3p^{1/2}}+\frac{\bar{\eta}^{1/2}(n+1)^{2}}{p}\right)\delta.

Combining this with (
3.6
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{2(n+1)}{p}{\mathbb{E}}[\Delta_{n}^{\delta}]

\displaystyle\leq M_{0}+\frac{(n+1)C_{0}\delta^{2}}{p}+\frac{2(\bar{\eta}M_{0})^{1/2}}{p}(n+1)\delta

\displaystyle\quad\,+2\left(\frac{2C_{0}^{1/2}(n+1)^{3/2}}{3\bar{\eta}^{1/2}p^{3/2}}+\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)\bar{\eta}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2M_{0}+\frac{(n+1)C_{0}\delta^{2}}{p}+\frac{\bar{\eta}}{p^{2}}(n+1)^{2}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+2\left(\frac{2C_{0}^{1/2}(n+1)^{3/2}}{3\bar{\eta}^{1/2}p^{3/2}}+\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)\bar{\eta}\delta^{2}.

Therefore, by the strong convexity of
\mathcal{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\mu{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\Delta_{n}^{\delta}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{pM_{0}}{2(n+1)}+\frac{1}{2}C_{0}\delta^{2}+\frac{\bar{\eta}}{2p}(n+1)\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\left(\frac{2C_{0}^{1/2}(n+1)^{1/2}}{3\bar{\eta}^{1/2}p^{1/2}}+\frac{n+1}{p}\right)\bar{\eta}\delta^{2}.

By using
2C_{0}^{1/2}(n+1)^{1/2}/(3\bar{\eta}^{1/2}p^{1/2})\leq C_{0}/(9\bar{\eta})+(n+1)/p

we further obtain

\displaystyle\mu{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{n}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{pM_{0}}{2(n+1)}+\frac{5\bar{\eta}}{2p}(n+1)\delta^{2}+\frac{11}{18}C_{0}\delta^{2}

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Without using any source condition on
x^{\dagger}
, it is expected for Algorithm

that if
n_{\delta}
is chosen such that
n_{\delta}\to\infty

\delta^{2}n_{\delta}\to 0

5.
Conclusion

Heavy ball method was proposed by Polyak in 1964 to accelerate the gradient method by adding a
momentum term. This method and its stochastic variant have received much attention in optimization
community in recent years due to the development of machine learning and the appearance of large
scale problems. In this paper we considered a stochastic heavy ball method for solving linear
ill-posed inverse problems. With suitable choices of the step-sizes and the momentum coefficients,
we established the regularization property of the method and derived the rate of convergence under
a benchmark source condition on the sought solution. There are many challenging and interesting
questions that deserve for further study.
These include the choices of
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
and the design of stopping rules. Note that
in this paper we only considered the method (
1.11
) with
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
chosen
by (
1.12
). There must exist other possible choices of
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
that might
yield faster convergence. It is also of interest to consider adaptive choices of
\alpha_{n}

and
\beta_{n}
to produce fast convergent method. Furthermore, in our paper, we considered
terminating the method (
1.11
) by
a priori
stopping rules which usually require
extra information, such as source information, of the sought solution. These extra information
are hardly available in practical applications. How to design efficient
a posteriori
stopping
rules for the method (
1.11
) is therefore significantly important.

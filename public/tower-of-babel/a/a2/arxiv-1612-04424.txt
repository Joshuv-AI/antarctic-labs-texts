A positive characterization of rational maps
Dylan P. Thurston

Abstract
. When is a topological branched self-cover of the sphere equivalent to a post-critically finite rational map on ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1} ? William Thurston gave one answer in 1982, giving a negative criterion (an obstruction to a map being rational). We give a complementary, positive criterion: the branched self-cover is equivalent to a rational map if and only if there is an elastic graph spine for the complement of the post-critical set that gets “looser” under backwards iteration.

1.2.
Future directions

Theorem
1
raises several questions and future
directions.

First, there is the question of the iteration (
n
in the theorem
statement), and whether it is strictly necessary to iterate to give a
positive certificate for any given rational map. It is first of all
clear that the necessary value of
n
depends on the elastic graph
spine
G
(including a dependence on the elastic constants). See

[
Thu16
, Figure 2]
for one concrete example. So
then the question becomes whether we can always find an elastic
spine
G
so that

n=1
suffices. There are some
rational where
n=1
does
not
suffice for any
G
by a case
analysis. These
include the barycentric subdivision rational map
[
CFKP03
, Figure
25]
,

f(z)=\frac{4}{27}\frac{(z^{2}-z+1)^{3}}{(z(z-1))^{2}},

and rational map #3.2 in the census of quadratic rational maps with
at most
4
post-critical points
[
BBL+00
]
,

f(z)\approx\frac{13.531903z-13.531903}{(z+2.3829758)^{2}}.

Analysis of the first case is made easier using the symmetries of the
map. Analysis of the second case is more involved, and we do not give
the details. In both these cases, the Julia set is a Sierpiński
carpet. (The second example is the unique quadratic map in that census
with a
Sierpiński carpet Julia set.)

For many other examples, it seems that
n=1
does suffice. In
particular, a crucial role appears to be played by the
crochet
maps
, rational maps where two Fatou components can be
connected by a path that intersects the Julia set in only countably
many points. (The terminology was introduced by Dudko, Hlushchanka,
and Schleicher, in ongoing work.) They are also conjecturally the
rational maps for which the Ahlfors regular conformal dimension is
equal to
1
.

Conjecture 1.18
.

For any crochet post-critically finite rational map,
n=1
suffices
in Theorem
1
: There is an elastic
(orbi)graph spine
G
for which
\Emb_{G}[\varphi]<1
without the need
to iterate.

On the other hand, any graph-based criterion is unlikely to work when

.
The issue is that for a graph virtual endomorphism that is
contracting

(i.e., in the language of Section
6
,

\overline{E}{}^{\infty}_{\infty}[\pi,\varphi]<1
), by a result of
Nekrashevych
[
Nek14
]
the Julia set is
homeomorphic to the
inverse limit of graphs
\Gamma_{n}
with respect to the
maps
\varphi^{n}_{n-1}
. (See Definition
5.1
for
terminology.) But any inverse limit of graphs has topological
dimension
1
, and so cannot be homeomorphic to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

One might also ask for a generalization of
Theorem
1
to allow the
post-critical set
P
to be infinite, probably with some other
restrictions. For W. Thurston’s original theory, this a fruitful area
of research, with a series of papers

[
CJS03a
,
CJS03b
,
ZJ09
,
CT11
,
CT18
]

leading ultimately to an obstruction theorem for
maps where the accumulation set of
P
is finite (the
geometrically
finite maps
).

In another direction, exponential maps and other transcendental maps

\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
have been a fruitful area of research, with substantial
more complexity than the rational map case. In particular, the natural
analog of Question
1.2
is almost entirely open. This deals
with topological maps of transcendental type that are post-singularly
finite (to include the forward orbits of asymptotic values). For the
obstruction criterion, there is a result for the exponential family

[
HSS09
]
, but this is a special case and it is not
clear what to expect in general. It is natural to ask whether
there is any analogue of the positive criterion presented in this
paper.

In both these cases (geometrically finite or transcendental maps), any
graphical model needs to allow for infinite graphs: either because the
post-critical set is infinite (and we want some sort of spine for its
complement), or because the degree of the cover is infinite. Some of
the ingredients in this paper carry over without issue. For instance,
Propositions
4.8

and
4.9
, relating extremal length on graphs
and their thickening to a surface, hold for infinite graphs. (Indeed,
Theorem
2
can be generalized
considerably, to allow grafting along arbitrary embedded arcs and/or
circles, with some weakening of the conclusion.)
Likewise the quasi-conformal surgery techniques
recalled in Section
3
have been well-studied
in these more general contexts.

Other ingredients appear harder to generalize to the setting of
infinite graphs (or infinitely-generated
\pi_{1}
). For instance, in
earlier work, we gave several equivalent conditions for conformal
embeddings of Riemann surfaces of finite type with some “space”
around them
[
KPT15
]
. These conditions are unlikely to
be equivalent in the setting of arbitrary Riemann surfaces.

Another possible generalization is to higher-dimensional maps, for
instance studying maps of higher-dimensional manifolds that are
post-critically finite in a suitable sense. It is not entirely clear
what the right questions or conjectures should be, but one can often
find suitable combinatorial models for such maps as virtual
endomorphisms of CW complexes. Nekrashevych has both a general theory

[
Nek14
]
and concrete examples

[
Nek16
]
. The major obstacle to developing
a theory similar to the one in this paper in the higher-dimensional
setting is finding the right analogue of the energies
E^{p}_{p}
recalled
in Section
6
. The definitions rely heavily on
the underlying objects being 1-dimensional. (See
[
Thu19
, Definition

]
for a possible direction
towards a generalization.) Furthermore, the proof of
existence of minimizers from earlier work is combinatorial and
produces piecewise-linear minimizing maps; this approach will not work
in higher dimensions. This remains work in progress.

Another direction is investigating the meaning of the additional
energies
\overline{E}{}^{p}_{p}
in Section
6
. In work in
progress with Kevin Pilgrim, we relate these energies to the Ahlfors
regular conformal dimension
[
PT
]
.

Definition 2.1

The theory of asymptotic energies developed at the beginning of
Section
5
applies to any energy that is
sub-multiplicative and invariant under homotopy and
covers. In particular, it applies to any of the
p
-conformal energies

E^{p}_{p}
defined in
[
Thu19
, Appendix

]
.
These energies
E^{p}_{p}[\varphi]
are a simultaneous generalization of best
Lipschitz constant
\Lip[\varphi]=E^{\infty}_{\infty}[\varphi]
,
and the embedding energy
\Emb[\varphi]=\bigl(E^{2}_{2}[\varphi]\bigr)^{2}
.

Recall that a
p
-conformal graph, for
1<p\leq\infty
, is a graph
with a
p
-length
\alpha(e)
on each edge
e
, which we will treat as
a metric. A
1
-conformal graph is instead a weighted graph, with a
weight
w(e)>0
on each edge.
For
\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muG_{1}\to G_{2}
a PL map between

p
-conformal graphs,

E^{p}_{p}[\varphi]
is defined by

(6.1)

\displaystyle E^{p}_{p}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\esssup_{y\in G_{2}}\,\frac{n_{\varphi,w}(y)}{w(y)}&p=1\\
\displaystyle\esssup_{y\in G_{2}}\,\Biggl(\sum_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\lvert\varphi^{\prime}(x)\rvert^{p-1}\Biggr)^{1/p}&1<p<\infty\\
\Lip(\varphi)&p=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle E^{p}_{p}[\varphi]

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf_{\psi\in[\varphi]}E^{p}_{p}(\psi).

(In the
p=1
case, we count weighted preimages:

n_{\varphi,w}(y)=\sum_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}w(x)
.)
Like

\Emb
, the energy
E^{p}_{p}
is
sub-multiplicative and
invariant under covers, whether or not we take homotopy classes. For
a graph virtual endomorphism

\pi,\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}
, we thus have asymptotic energies

\overline{E}{}^{p}_{p}[\pi,\varphi]
. By
Proposition
5.7
,
\overline{E}{}^{p}_{p}
is invariant under
homotopy equivalence.

E^{p}_{p}[\varphi]
can be characterized as a stretch
factor for energies of maps to length graphs
[
Thu19
, Theorem

]
. For

1\leq p\leq\infty
and
f\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muG\to K
a PL
map from a
p
-conformal graph to a length graph, there are energies

(6.2)

\displaystyle E^{p}_{\infty}(f)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lVert f^{\prime}\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle E^{p}_{\infty}[f]

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf_{g\in[f]}E^{p}_{\infty}(g).

Then, for
\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muG_{1}\to G_{2}
a PL map between
p
-conformal graphs,

(6.3)

E^{p}_{p}[\varphi]=\sup_{[f]\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muG_{2}\to K}\frac{E^{p}_{\infty}[f\circ\varphi]}{E^{p}_{\infty}[f]}

where the supremum runs over all length graphs
K
and homotopy
classes
[f]
of maps.

To study the behavior of
\overline{E}{}^{p}_{p}
as
p
varies,
we compare
p
-conformal energies and
q
-conformal energies
for
p\leq q
.

Definition 6.4
.

For a metric graph
G
, let

\displaystyle m(G)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\min_{e\in\Edge(G)}\alpha(e)

\displaystyle M(G)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{e\in\Edge(G)}\alpha(e).

Lemma 6.5
.

For
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
and
f\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muG\to K
a
constant-derivative map from a metric graph to a length graph,

E^{q}_{\infty}(f)\leq m(G)^{-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}}E^{p}_{\infty}(f).

The same inequality is true when we minimize over the homotopy class.

Proof.

In general, there is an inequality

(6.6)

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sum_{i}x_{i}{}^{q}\Bigr)^{1/q}\leq\Bigl(\sum_{i}x_{i}{}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}.

With positive weights
w_{i}
with
m=\min_{i}w_{i}
, this becomes

(6.7)

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sum_{i}w_{i}x_{i}{}^{q}\Bigr)^{1/q}\leq m^{-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}}\cdot\Bigl(\sum_{i}w_{i}x_{i}{}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}.

(Apply Equation (
6.6
) to the sequence

w_{i}{}^{1/q}x_{i}
.) Apply Equation (
6.7
) to the
definition of
E^{p}_{\infty}
.

To get the statement for homotopy classes, apply this inequality to
a map
f
that minimizes
E^{p}_{\infty}(f)
in its homotopy class.
∎

Lemma 6.8
.

For
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
and
f\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muG\to K
a PL
map from a metric graph to a length graph,

E^{p}_{\infty}(f)\leq M(G)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\cdot E^{q}_{\infty}(f).

The same inequality is true when we minimize over the homotopy class.

Proof.

Use sub-multiplicativity of the energies
[
Thu19
, Proposition
]
:

E^{p}_{\infty}(f)\leq E^{p}_{q}(\mathrm{id})E^{q}_{\infty}(f).

From the definition of
E^{p}_{q}

[
Thu19
, Equation
(
)]
, we see
that
E^{p}_{q}(\mathrm{id})=M(G)^{\frac{q-p}{pq}}
. (Alternatively, apply
Hölder’s inequality to Equation (
6.2
).)

To get the statement for homotopy classes, apply this inequality to
a map
f
that minimizes
E^{q}_{\infty}(f)
in its homotopy class.
∎

We can now see that these energies give nothing new for
ordinary
(non-virtual) graph endomorphisms (i.e., outer automorphisms of the
free group). Define the asymptotic energy of an ordinary
endomorphism
\varphi
to be

\overline{E}{}[\varphi]\coloneqq\overline{E}{}[\mathrm{id},\varphi]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{E[\varphi^{\circ n}]}
.

Proposition 6.9
.

For
[\varphi]\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\Gamma\to\Gamma
an endomorphism of a graph,

\overline{E}{}^{p}_{p}[\varphi]=\overline{\Lip}[\varphi].

Proof.

By Lemmas
6.5

and
6.8
there is a constant
C\geq 1
so that

\frac{1}{C}\frac{\Lip[f\circ\varphi^{\circ n}]}{\Lip[f]}\leq\frac{E^{p}_{\infty}[f\circ\varphi^{\circ n}]}{E^{p}_{\infty}[f]}\leq C\frac{\Lip[f\circ\varphi^{\circ n}]}{\Lip[f]}.

Equation (
6.3
) then shows that
E^{p}_{p}[\varphi^{\circ n}]

is within a factor of
C
of
\Lip[\varphi^{\circ n}]
. The constant
factor disappears in the limit defining
\overline{E}{}^{p}_{p}[\varphi]
.
∎

For virtual endomorphisms, the situation is more interesting.

Proposition 6.10
.

For
\pi,\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}
a virtual
endomorphism of graphs,
\overline{E}{}^{p}_{p}[\pi,\varphi]
is a non-increasing
function of
p
: if
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
,

\overline{E}{}^{q}_{q}[\pi,\varphi]\leq\overline{E}{}^{p}_{p}[\pi,\varphi].

Proof.

Pick a metric structure
G_{0}
on
\Gamma_{0}
and lift it to get a
series of metric graphs
G_{n}
as usual. Then for any
f\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muG_{0}\to K
a map to a length graph,

\frac{E^{q}_{\infty}[f\circ\varphi^{n}]}{E^{q}_{\infty}[f]}\leq\frac{1}{C}\frac{E^{p}_{\infty}[f\circ\varphi^{n}]}{E^{p}_{\infty}[f]}

for some constant
C
, since

m(G_{n})=m(G_{0})
.
Now
E^{q}_{q}[\varphi^{n}]\leq E^{p}_{p}[\varphi^{n}]/C
, and the constant factor
disappears in the limit as usual.
∎

Proposition 6.11
.

For
\pi,\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}
a virtual
endomorphism of graphs with

a covering of degree

, if

,

\overline{E}{}^{q}_{q}[\pi,\varphi]\geq d^{-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}}\cdot\overline{E}{}^{p}_{p}[\pi,\varphi].

Proof.

Pick a metric structure
G_{0}
on
\Gamma_{0}
and lift it as in
Proposition
6.10
.
Observe that
M(G_{n})=d^{n}M(G_{0})
. Then by Lemmas
6.5

and
6.8
for any length graph

and
[f]\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muG_{0}\to K
,

\frac{E^{p}_{\infty}[f\circ\varphi^{n}]}{E^{p}_{\infty}[f]}\leq\left(\frac{M(G_{n})}{m(G_{0})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\frac{E^{q}_{\infty}[f\circ\varphi^{n}]}{E^{q}_{\infty}[f]}=C\cdot d^{n\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\cdot E^{q}_{q}[\varphi^{n}]

for some constant

. Then taking the supremum over

and
[f]
,
taking the

\overline{E}{}^{p}_{p}[\pi,\varphi]\leq d^{1/p-1/q}\cdot\overline{E}{}^{q}_{q}[\pi,\varphi]
.
∎

Corollary 6.12
.

\overline{E}{}^{p}_{p}[\pi,\varphi]
is a continuous function of
p
.

.

The cases

,

, or

•

The most important case is

For

The other natural special case is

7.
Obstructions

7.1.
Obstructions for rational maps

We now relate Theorem
1
to W. Thurston’s
Obstruction Theorem. With an eye to generalizations, we rephrase it in
terms of elastic
multi-curves without the assumption of
hyperbolic type.

Definition 7.1
.

An
elastic multi-curve

A=(C,\alpha,c)
on a
surface
\Sigma
is a multi-curve
c\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muC\hookrightarrow\Sigma
,
together with an elastic structure (i.e., metric)
\alpha
on
C
. The

support
of
A
is the underlying multi-curve
(C,c)
.

There are two natural operations on elastic multi-curves. First,
if
\pi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\Sigma
is a covering map and
A
is an
elastic multi-curve on
\Sigma
, there is a multi-curve

\widetilde{A}=\pi^{-1}(A)
on
\widetilde{\Sigma}
obtained by pull-back in the
usual way.

Second, if
A=(C,\alpha,c)
is an elastic multi-curve on
\Sigma
,
then the
join

\mathop{\mathrm{Join}}\nolimits(A)
is the elastic multi-curve obtained by

•

deleting all components of
C
whose images are null-homotopic or
bound a punctured disk, and

•

replacing any components

(C_{1},\alpha_{1}),\dots,(C_{k},\alpha_{k})
of
A
whose images are parallel with a single
component
(C_{0},\alpha_{0})
, with elastic length obtained by the
harmonic sum:

\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\alpha_{k}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+\frac{1}{\alpha_{k}}}.

(The harmonic sum comes from the parallel law for resistors or for
springs.)

Definition 7.2
.

An
obstruction
for
f
is

•

an elastic multi-curve
A
on
\Sigma\setminus P
and

•

a map
\psi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muA\to\mathop{\mathrm{Join}}\nolimits(f^{-1}(A))

so that

•

\psi
commutes up to homotopy with the maps to

\Sigma\setminus P
and

•

\Emb(\psi)\leq 1
.

Remark 7.3
.

Contrast the obstruction map
\psi
with the map

\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muf^{-1}(G)\to G
in the statement of
Theorem
1
: the maps are going the opposite
direction.

Theorem 7.4

(W. Thurston, Douady-Hubbard
[
DH93
]
)
.

Let
f\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu(\Sigma,P)\righttoleftarrow
be a topological branched
self-cover so that the first return map is not a Lattés map on any
component. Then
f
is
equivalent to a rational map iff there is no obstruction for
f
.

The usual formulation of Theorem
7.4
refers
to the maximum eigenvalue of a matrix constructed out of the multi-curves
underlying
A
. The
above formulation is equivalent by Perron-Frobenius theory, as we
spell out in Proposition

below. Intuitively, Theorem
7.4
says that

is rational iff there is no conformal collection of annuli that
gets (weakly) wider under backwards iteration.

7.3.
Duality

There is a notion of
p
-conductance
, dual
to
p
-length.
Recall that we
can describe electrical
networks either in terms of resistances or in terms of
conductances. Resistances add in series and change by a harmonic sum
in parallel. Conductances add in parallel and change by a harmonic sum
in series. Alternately, we can think about the relation between
extremal length and modulus of conformal annuli.

There is a similar story for general
p
-conformal graphs when

1<p<\infty
. Recall
[
Thu19
, Definition

]
that we can think of
an edge
e
in a
p
-conformal graph as an equivalence class of
rectangles of length
\ell
and height
w
, with the
p
-length
\alpha

given by

\alpha=\frac{\ell}{w^{1/(p-1)}}.

Now consider a dual view, interchanging the role of length and
height. The
p
-conductance
is

\gamma\coloneqq\alpha^{1-p}=\frac{w}{\ell^{p-1}}=\frac{w}{\ell^{1/(p^{\vee}-1)}}

where
p^{\vee}=p/(p-1)
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
.

Propositions
7.7
and
7.9
say
that
p
-conductances add in parallel.

In checking whether a
p
-conformal multi-curve
A
is a
p
-obstruction,
there are two basic operations. Let us recall what
happens to the
p
-lengths.

•

We pass to a cover by taking
f^{-1}(A)
(i.e., the pullback by
the covering map). A connected cover of a circle is necessarily a
(longer) circle, which is series composition. Thus if a component of

f^{-1}(A)
covers a component of
A
by a degree
d
map, the

p
-length gets multiplied by
d
.

•

We merge parallel components in the
\mathop{\mathrm{Join}}\nolimits_{p}

operation. The
p
-weights change by the
p
-harmonic sum
(Equation (
7.6
)).

If we work with the dual
p
-conductances instead, the two operations
switch in complexity.

•

If a circle of
p
-conductance
\gamma
is
covered by a degree
d
map, the pull-back
p
-conductance is

d^{1-p}\gamma
.

•

The parallel composition in
\mathop{\mathrm{Join}}\nolimits_{p}
becomes simpler:
add the constituent
p
-conductances.

7.4.
Obstruction matrices and invariant multi-curves

We now investigate when we can choose
p
-lengths on a given curve
to make it into a

-obstruction. As a result of the previous
section, if we use

-conductances to

Definition 7.12
.

Let
\pi,\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muX_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}

In other words, consider all components

\pi^{*}C_{i}

that push forward to

covers

\pi^{*}C_{i}

The matrix

is designed to mimic the action of
\varphi_{*}\pi^{*}
on

p
-conformal multi-curves. Suppose

has
n

\gamma=(\gamma_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}

to be the
p
-conformal multi-curve in which

Lemma 7.13

For

\varphi_{*}\pi^{*}A(\gamma)=A(M_{C,p}\gamma)+A^{\prime}

Proof.

Immediate from Proposition
7.9

Observe that

\lambda(M_{C,p})

Proposition 7.14
.

Let
\pi,\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muX_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}

Proof.

If

\varphi_{*}\pi^{*}A(\gamma)=A(\lambda\gamma)+A^{\prime}.

The

Conversely, suppose that we have a

Corollary 7.15
.

If

Definition 7.16
.

Let
\pi,\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muX_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}

On the other hand, in

We can often switch between back-invariant and forward-invariant
multi-curves as follows. First, some graph-theory terminology. In a directed
graph, a

(SCC) is a maximal set

Given a virtual endomorphism
\pi,\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muX_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}

whose vertices are the components of

gives a forward-invariant multi-curve, although
not all forward-invariant multi-curves arise in this way.

Proposition 7.17
.

Let
\pi,\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muX_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}
be a virtual

be a multi-curve on

. Then there is an

\lambda(M_{C_{0},p})=\lambda(M_{C,p})
.

Proof.

M_{C,p}
is block triangular with respect to the partial
order on the SCCs of
\Gamma(C)
, so its maximum eigenvalue will be
equal to the
Perron-Frobenius eigenvalue of a diagonal block corresponding to an

to be empty. Otherwise,

∎

Proposition 7.18
.

Let
\pi,\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\Sigma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Sigma_{0}

Although back-invariant multi-curves are more traditional,

7.5.

Let
\pi,\varphi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}

Proposition 7.19

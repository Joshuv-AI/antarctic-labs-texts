Expressiveness of Extended Bounded Response 𝖫𝖳𝖫 \mathsf{LTL}
Alessandro Cimatti, Luca Geatti, Nicola Gigante, Angelo Montanari, Stefano Tonetta

Abstract
Extended Bounded Response 𝖫𝖳𝖫 \mathsf{LTL} with Past ( 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖤𝖡𝖱 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P} ) is a safety fragment of Linear Temporal Logic with Past ( 𝖫𝖳𝖫 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL{+}P} ) that has been recently introduced in the context of reactive synthesis. The strength of 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖤𝖡𝖱 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P} is a fully symbolic compilation of formulas into symbolic deterministic automata. Its syntax is organized in four levels. The first three levels feature (a particular combination of) future temporal modalities, the last one admits only past temporal operators. At the base of such a structuring there are algorithmic motivations: each level corresponds to a step of the algorithm for the automaton construction. The complex syntax of 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖤𝖡𝖱 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P} made it difficult to precisely characterize its expressive power, and to compare it with other 𝖫𝖳𝖫 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL{+}P} safety fragments. † † The work was partially supported by the Italian GNCS project a Cognitive frAmework to synTHesize Adaptive contRollers in Safety-crItical Scenarios (CATHARSIS) (L. Geatti, N. Gigante and A. Montanari). In this paper, we first prove that 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖤𝖡𝖱 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P} is expressively complete with respect to the safety fragment of 𝖫𝖳𝖫 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL{+}P} , that is, any safety language definable in 𝖫𝖳𝖫 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL{+}P} can be formalized in 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖤𝖡𝖱 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P} , and vice versa. From this, it follows that 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖤𝖡𝖱 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P} and 𝖲𝖺𝖿𝖾𝗍𝗒 ​ - ​ 𝖫𝖳𝖫 \mathsf{Safety{\text{-}}\mathsf{LTL}} are expressively equivalent. Then, we show that past modalities play an essential role in 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖤𝖡𝖱 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P} : we prove that the future fragment of 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖤𝖡𝖱 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P} is strictly less expressive than full 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖤𝖡𝖱 + 𝖯 \mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P} .



1
Introduction

Linear Temporal Logic (
\mathsf{LTL}
) was introduced in the late seventies

[
14
]
as a modal logic for reasoning over computer
programs, modeling their computations as state sequences (
i
.
e
., linear orders)
that represent the state a computer program is in at a given time.

\mathsf{LTL}
originally used temporal modalities for moving only in the future of
a time point. Later, it turned out that adding modalities for moving in the
past (we refer to this logic as
\mathsf{LTL{+}P}
) does not add expressive power to
\mathsf{LTL}

[
11
]
, but only succinctness
[
12
]
.
The definition of the operators in the syntax of
\mathsf{LTL{+}P}
was proved to be
carefully designed. In fact, Kamp
[
9
]
as well as Gabbay
et al
.
[
7
]
proved that the properties that one can formalize in

\mathsf{LTL{+}P}
are exactly those definable in the first-order fragment of the

monadic second-order theory of one successor
(
\mathsf{S1S}
, for short), which
is in turn decidable
[
3
,
2
]
.

Among the different properties that one can define in
\mathsf{LTL{+}P}
, two notable
classes are the set of
safety
and
co-safety
properties. Safety
properties express the intuitive requirement that
something bad never
happens
, and thus each counterexample of a safety property is finite.
Co-safety properties are duals of safety properties: each state sequence that
satisfies the property has a finite witness. The safety and co-safety classes
play a crucial role in verification and synthesis, since their main feature of
having
finite
witnesses makes in general the problems much simpler

[
10
,
19
]
.

Several safety fragments of
\mathsf{LTL}
have been introduced over the years. One of
the most natural examples is
\mathsf{Safety{\text{-}}\mathsf{LTL}}

[
17
,
4
,
19
]
. The
\mathsf{Safety{\text{-}}\mathsf{LTL}}
logic is defined
as the set of all and only those formulas of
\mathsf{LTL}
(with only future modalities)
such that, when in negated normal form, do
not
contain existential
temporal operators (like the
until
operator). In
[
4
]
, Chang

et al
. proved that all the safety properties definable in
\mathsf{LTL}
are expressible
in
\mathsf{Safety{\text{-}}\mathsf{LTL}}
as well, and
vice versa
.

Extended Bounded Response
\mathsf{LTL}
with Past (
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
) is a recently introduced
safety fragment of
\mathsf{LTL{+}P}
with an efficient reactive synthesis problem.
In addition to the fact that realizability from
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
specifications is

\mathsf{EXPTIME}
-complete (while
\mathsf{LTL}
realizability is
\mathsf{2EXPTIME}
-complete), in
practice realizability and synthesis from
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
specifications turned out to
be much more efficient than other approaches
[
5
]
.
The syntax of
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
is articulated over layers: the first three layers
comprise a combination of future temporal modalities, while the last layer
includes only past temporal operators. Each of the layers was carefully
designed in order to correspond to a step of the algorithm for constructing
a symbolic automaton starting from an
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
specification.
This results into a great performance improvement in practice, but the syntax
of
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
makes it hard to find its exact expressive power,
and, consequently, makes it hard also to compare it with other safety
fragments of
\mathsf{LTL{+}P}
, like, for instance,
\mathsf{Safety{\text{-}}\mathsf{LTL}}
.

In this paper we prove that
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
is expressively complete with respect to
the safety fragment of
\mathsf{LTL{+}P}
. As a by-product, we obtain that
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
and

\mathsf{Safety{\text{-}}\mathsf{LTL}}
are expressively equivalent.
The core of the proof exploits a
normal form theorem
for each safety
property definable in
\mathsf{LTL{+}P}

[
18
,
4
]
, which
establishes a correspondence between safety properties definable in
\mathsf{LTL{+}P}
and
properties of the form
\mathsf{G}^{\prime}
, where
′
is the
globally

operator of
\mathsf{LTL}
and
\alpha
is a pure past formula.
Consequently, it is clear that the
pure past layer
of
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
plays a crucial role for the expressive equivalence of
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
.
We show that this layer is really necessary. In fact, we prove that
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}}
,
that is
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
devoid of the pure past layer, is
strictly less

expressive than full
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
.
This is shown by proving that all the formulas of
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}}
can constrain, for
any time point
i
in an infinite state sequence, only a
bounded
prefix
before (or interval around)
i
.
This implies that formulas that are able to constrain, for each time point
i
,
a prefix of unbounded (although finite) length before
i
, like for instance

\mathsf{G}(p_{1}\to\mathsf{H}p_{2}^{\prime}
(where
′
is the
historically
past
operator of
\mathsf{LTL{+}P}
), are
not
definable in
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}}
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we give
the necessary background. The expressive power of
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
is proved in

Section

3
. In
Section

4
, we prove that the future
fragment of
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
is strictly less expressive than
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
. Finally, we
summarize the results of the paper in
Section

5
.

Linear Temporal Logic

Linear Temporal Logic (
\mathsf{LTL}
) is a modal logic interpreted over infinite,

[
14
,
6
]

Syntactically,

\mathsf{X}^{\prime}

.

\phi_{1}\penalty\mathbin{\resizebox{}{0}{$\mathsf{U}$}}\phi_{^{\prime}}
,

.

\phi_{2}
will eventually hold and
\phi_{1}
will hold
until
then).

\mathsf{LTL}
with Past
(
\mathsf{LTL{+}P}
) extends
\mathsf{LTL}
with the addition of temporal
operators able to talk about what happened in the

with respect to

by adding the following

\mathsf{Y}^{\prime}
,
i
.

., there exists a

.

(iii)

,

.

\displaystyle\phi\vcentcolon=p

\displaystyle\mathrel{|}\lnot^{\prime}\mathrel{|}\phi_{1}\lor\phi_{^{\prime}}\mathrel{|}\phi_{1}\land\phi_{^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{|}\mathsf{X}\phi_{^{\prime}}\mathrel{|}\phi_{1}\penalty\mathbin{\resizebox{}{0}{$\mathsf{U}$}}\phi_{^{\prime}}\mathrel{|}\phi_{1}\penalty\mathbin{\resizebox{}{0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}\phi_{^{\prime}}\mathrel{|}\mathsf{F}\phi_{^{\prime}}\mathrel{|}\mathsf{G}\phi_{^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{|}\mathsf{Y}\phi_{^{\prime}}\mathrel{|}\phi_{1}\penalty\mathbin{\resizebox{}{0}{$\mathsf{S}$}}\phi_{^{\prime}}\mathrel{|}\phi_{1}\penalty\mathbin{\resizebox{}{0}{$\mathsf{T}$}}\phi_{^{\prime}}\mathrel{|}\mathsf{O}\phi_{^{\prime}}\mathrel{|}\mathsf{H}\phi_{^{\prime}}\mathrel{|}\mathsf{Z}\phi_{^{\prime}}

Suppose that

Lemma 4
.

Let

{}^{i,i}\sigma^{j}\models\psi

Take any value for
i
,

, and

i\geq m_{\psi}+d_{\psi}
,

(ii)

j\geq i+d_{\psi}
,

(iii)

k\geq j+d_{\psi}

We proceed by induction on the structure of the formula
\psi

For the base case, we consider three cases:

(i)

formulas in
\mathsf{LTL_{BP}}
, that is such that all its temporal operators refer
to the past and are bounded;

(ii)

formulas of type
\mathsf{G}^{\prime}
, where
\alpha\in\mathsf{LTL_{BP}}
;

(iii)

formulas of type
\alpha\penalty\mathbin{\resizebox{}{0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\prime}
, where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathsf{LTL_{BP}}

We consider the case of a formula
\alpha\in\mathsf{LTL_{BP}}
, and suppose that

{}^{i,i}\sigma^{j}\models\alpha
.
By definition of
{}^{i,i}\sigma^{j}
and
{}^{i,k}\sigma^{j}
, it always holds
that
{}^{i,i}\sigma^{j}_{0}={}^{i,k}\sigma^{j}_{0}
. Since
\alpha\in\mathsf{LTL_{BP}}

refers only to the current state or to the past, it follows that

{}^{i,i}\sigma^{j}\models\alpha
if and only if
{}^{i,k}\sigma^{j}\models\alpha
.

Consider now the case for
\alpha\penalty\mathbin{\resizebox{}{0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\prime}
, where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathsf{LTL_{BP}}
. Since
m_{\alpha\penalty\mathbin{\resizebox{}{0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\prime}}=0
(

.
e
., the are no
next

operators in this formula), we can apply
Lemma

3
, having that

{}^{i,i}\sigma^{j}\models\alpha\penalty\mathbin{\resizebox{}{0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\prime}
if and only if

{}^{i,k}\sigma^{j}\models\alpha\penalty\mathbin{\resizebox{}{0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\prime}
. Since
\mathsf{G}^{\prime}=\bot\penalty\mathbin{\resizebox{}{0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\prime}
, this proves also the case for the
globally

operator.

For the inductive step, since by hypothesis
\psi
belongs to the canonical
form of
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}}
, it suffices to consider only the case for the
next
operator
,
conjunctions
and
disjunctions

Consider first the case for the
next
operator, and suppose that

{}^{i,i}\sigma^{j}\models\mathsf{X}\psi^{^{\prime}}

k
,
i
and
j

such that

{}^{i,k}\sigma^{j}\models\mathsf{X}\psi^{^{\prime}}

By definition of the next operator, it holds that
{}^{i,i}\sigma^{j},1\models\psi^{\prime}

We consider now the case for conjunctions, and suppose that

i
and

By

.

\llbracket\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}}\rrbracket\subsetneq\llbracket\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}\rrbracket

5
Conclusions

We considered the logic
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
, a recently introduced safety fragment of
\mathsf{LTL}
with an efficient realizability problem. The syntax of
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
made it
difficult to exactly characterize its expressive power. We studied the
expressive power of
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
and of its pure future fragment,
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}}
, and
compare it with other safety fragments of
\mathsf{LTL}
.
It turned out that
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
is expressively complete with respect to the safety
fragment of
\mathsf{LTL}
, and, consequently, it is expressively equivalent to

\mathsf{Safety{\text{-}}\mathsf{LTL}}
.
We found out that past modalities are crucial for the expressive power of

\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
. In fact,
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}}
is strictly less expressive than full
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
.
This was somehow surprising, since it proves that, despite not being
fundamental for the expressiveness of full
\mathsf{LTL}
, past modalities are crucial
for fragments of
\mathsf{LTL}
, like, for instance,
\mathsf{LTL_{EBR}{+}P}
.

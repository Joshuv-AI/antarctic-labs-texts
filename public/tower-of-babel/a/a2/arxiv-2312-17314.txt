Abstract
In this article, we propose the Δ \Delta 2HDMS as a combined explanation of several excesses and anomalies observed at the LHC. Especially, t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} differential distributions point towards the associated production of new electroweak scale Higgs bosons decaying into bottom quarks and W W bosons ( > 5 ​ σ >\!5\sigma ) with masses consistent with the di-photon excesses at ≈ 95 \approx\!\!95\, GeV and ≈ 151.5 \approx\!\!151.5\, GeV ( 3.8 ​ σ 3.8\sigma and 4.9 ​ σ 4.9\sigma , respectively). Furthermore, CMS found indications for resonant t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} production at ≈ 400 \approx\!400 GeV ( 3.5 ​ σ 3.5\sigma ) and both ATLAS and CMS reported elevated four-top and t ​ t ¯ ​ W t\bar{t}W cross-sections. The Δ \Delta 2HDMS is obtained by supplementing the SM Higgs ( H 2 H_{2} ) with a second scalar doublet ( H 1 H_{1} ), real scalar singlet ( S S ), and a Higgs triplet with zero hypercharge ( Δ \Delta ). We fix the masses of the neutral triplet-like and the singlet-like scalars by the di-photon excesses, i.e. m Δ 0 = 151.5 m_{\Delta^{0}}=151.5\, GeV and m S = 95 m_{S}=95\, GeV, respectively. H H , the CP-even component of H 1 H_{1} , is produced via gluon fusion from a top-loop and decays dominantly to S + Δ 0 S+\Delta^{0} whose subsequent decays to W + ​ W − W^{+}W^{-} and b ​ b ¯ b\bar{b} explain the differential t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} distributions for σ ⁡ ( p ​ p → H → S ​ Δ 0 ) ≈ 5 \sigma(pp\to H\to S\Delta^{0})\approx\!5 pb. Choosing the top-Yukawa of H 1 H_{1} accordingly, the CP-odd Higgs boson A A turns out to have the right production cross-section to account for the resonant t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} excess at 400 GeV, while the top-associated production of H H and A A results in new physics pollution of Standard Model t ​ t ¯ ​ W t\bar{t}W and t ​ t ¯ ​ t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t}t\bar{t} cross sections, as preferred by the data.

IV
Conclusions and Outlook

We propose the
\Delta
2HDMS as a combined explanation of the LHC multi-lepton anomalies, the resonant di-photon (
\approx
95 GeV and
\approx
151.5 GeV) and top quark excesses (
\approx
400 GeV) with significances of
>\!5\sigma
,
3\sigma\!-\!5\sigma
and
3.4\sigma
, respectively. The model is obtained by extending the SM with a second Higgs boson doublet as well as a real
SU(2)_{L}
singlet and a real
SU(2)_{L}
triplet and naturally predicts (at tree-level) a positive shift in the
W
mass in agreement with the current global fit for
v_{\Delta}=O({\rm 1GeV})
. In particular, a benchmark points with

\displaystyle m_{\Delta^{\pm}}

\displaystyle\approx m_{\Delta^{0}}=151.5\,{\rm GeV},\;m_{S}=95\,{\rm GeV},

(7)

\displaystyle m_{H^{\pm}}

\displaystyle\approx m_{A}\approx 400\,{\rm GeV},v_{\Delta}\approx 2\,{\rm GeV},\;v_{S}=O({\rm GeV}),

\displaystyle m_{H}

\displaystyle=290\,{\rm GeV},\;\mu_{t}=0.7,\;\lambda=O(1),\;\tan\beta\gtrapprox 5,

is consistent with all signals and bounds.

While we provided proof of principle that all these anomalies can be explained without violating other bounds, in the future, a detailed examination of the NP effects in the differential
t\bar{t}W
and
t\bar{t}Z

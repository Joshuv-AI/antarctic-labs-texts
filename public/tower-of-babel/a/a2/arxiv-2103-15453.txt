Disentangling Parallelism and Interference in Game Semantics
Simon Castellan ORCID:0000-0001-5886-5793, Pierre Clairambault ORCID:0000-0002-3285-6028

Abstract
. Game semantics is a denotational semantics presenting compositionally the computational behaviour of various kinds of effectful programs. One of its celebrated achievement is to have obtained full abstraction results for programming languages with a variety of computational effects, in a single framework. This is known as the semantic cube or Abramsky’s cube , which for sequential deterministic programs establishes a correspondence between certain conditions on strategies (“innocence”, “well-bracketing”, “visibility”) and the absence of matching computational effects. Outside of the sequential deterministic realm, there are still a wealth of game semantics-based full abstraction results; but they no longer fit in a unified canvas. In particular, Ghica and Murawski’s fully abstract model for shared state concurrency ( 𝖨𝖠 ⫽ \mathsf{IA}_{\sslash} ) does not have a matching notion of pure parallel program – we say that parallelism and interference ( i.e. state plus semaphores) are entangled . In this paper we construct a causal version of Ghica and Murawski’s model, also fully abstract for 𝖨𝖠 ⫽ \mathsf{IA}_{\sslash} . We provide compositional conditions parallel innocence and sequentiality , respectively banning interference and parallelism, and leading to four full abstraction results. To our knowledge, this is the first extension of Abramsky’s semantic cube programme beyond the sequential deterministic world.

Introduction

How to
prove
that a program
P
is correct, or
equivalent to
P^{\prime}
? This simple question,
prerequisite for formally validating software, lies at
the heart of decades of work in semantics.
Its study prompted a wealth of developments, each with its
methodology and scope.
Operational semantics
axiomatizes
execution
directly on syntax, while
denotational semantics
gives meaning
to programs by embedding them in a syntax-independent
mathematical space.

Operational semantics
is powerful and extensible,
perfectly fit for
formalization in a proof assistant – it is, for instance, behind the
celebrated
CompCert project
[
DBLP:journals/cacm/Leroy09
]
. On the other
hand, its deployment often follows from
ad-hoc choices, and it is not robust to variations in the language. It
is tied
to syntax and struggles with compositionality

1

1

Operational
semantics
can
be made compositional, but behind lie denotational
structures: for instance, the operational semantics behind the recent

Compositional CompCert

[
DBLP:conf/popl/StewartBCA15
]
“bears
much in common” (quoting the paper) with Ghica and Tzevelekos’
operational reconstruction of game semantics

[
DBLP:journals/entcs/GhicaT12
]
.
.
Denotational semantics
is
syntax-independent, and often more principled. It is a great tool to
reason about program equivalence (two programs being equivalent if they
denote the same object), to prove general properties of languages
(
e.g.
termination), and it comes with compositional reasoning
principles. The wider mathematical space in which programs are embedded
sometimes suggests new useful constructs (it is the birth story of
Linear Logic
[
DBLP:journals/tcs/Girard87
]
). In exchange, it is more
mathematically demanding and often quite brittle: distinct fragments of
the same language may require radically different representations.
Traditional denotational semantics (
e.g.
Scott domains) model
programs as functions, through their input/output behaviour. Effects
(
e.g.
state, non-determinism,
etc
) can be captured via
monads which do not readily combine. Though combining effects has been a
driving question in denotational semantics these past decades, it is
hardly a streamlined process. For instance, though there is significant
recent research activity around domain settings supporting probabilities
and higher-order

[
DBLP:conf/lics/StatonYWHK16
,
DBLP:journals/pacmpl/VakarKS19
]
, it is
unclear how they combine with non-determinism

[
DBLP:journals/mscs/Goubault-Larrecq17
]
, let alone concurrency; nor
how all these models relate together.

Game semantics

[
ho
,
ajm
]
, though also denotational, takes a
different approach: instead of a function it represents a
program as a
strategy
, a collection of
(representations of) its interactions against execution
environments. Once executions are first-class citizens
(called
plays
) one can characterise those achievable with
specific effects. This led to a wealth of fully abstract models,
rewarded in 2017 by the Alonzo Church Award (from the ACM SIGLOG, the
EATCS, the EACSL, and the Kurt Gödel Society). To cite the
announcement:

“Game semantics has changed
the landscape of programming language semantics by giving a unified
view of the denotational universes of many different languages. This is
a remarkable achievement that was not previously thought to be within
reach.”

But are games models truly “unified”? For
deterministic
sequential
programs, absolutely: various degrees of control
and state are indeed captured as additional conditions on one single
canvas
[
abramsky1999game
]
– this is the
semantic
cube
or
Abramsky cube
. But beyond the sequential
deterministic world, the picture is not so clear. The classic fully
abstract models for
finite non-determinism

[
DBLP:conf/lics/HarmerM99
]
, for
probabilistic choice

[
DBLP:conf/lics/DanosH00
]
or for
parallelism

[
gm
]
all
rely on the presence of state. Until recently, there were no fully
abstract model for any of these
without state
– or in
the language of game semantics, there were no notions of
non-deterministic, probabilistic or parallel
innocence
. Following
the phrasing of the title, our understanding of these
effects was
entangled
with state.

However, this picture is currently shifting.
Recently, two notions of non-deterministic innocence were proposed
independently
[
lics14
,
DBLP:conf/lics/TsukadaO15
]
– the two
settings also handling probabilistic innocence

[
DBLP:journals/corr/TsukadaO14
,
lics18
]
. Technically, these settings
differ significantly. But conceptually, both enrich strategies with
explicit
branching
information. Though the novelty may seem
minor, this is in fact a major schism
with respect to traditional game semantics, in that this branching
information is typically not observable. So instead of a strategy being
merely a formal description of how a program is observed by a certain
type of contexts, the model starts to carry more intensional,

causal
information, typically inaccessible to the environment but
which nonetheless finds its use in capturing compositionally the
computational behaviour expressible by certain programming features.
This suggests that to disentangle parallelism and state, we must
adequately represent the
branching structure
of parallel
computation, the (non-observable) causal patterns of pure
parallel programs.

Enter
concurrent games
. Concurrent games are a family of game
semantics models questioning in various ways the premise that the basic
building block should be totally, chronologically ordered

plays
. Pioneered by Melliès and others

[
DBLP:conf/lics/AbramskyM99
,
DBLP:conf/concur/Mellies04
,
DBLP:conf/concur/MelliesM07
,
DBLP:conf/tlca/FaggianP09
]

they have lately been under intense development, prompted by
new definitions due to Rideau and Winskel
[
lics11
]
. The name comes from their relationship with the
so-called
true concurrency
approach to concurrency theory,
following which one represents causal dependence and independence of
events explicitly rather than resorting to
interleavings. Besides making concurrent games a natural target to
model concurrent languages and process calculi

[
DBLP:conf/concur/CastellanC16
,
DBLP:journals/pacmpl/CastellanY19
]
,
it provides us with the required causal description of programs.

1.
\mathsf{IA}_{\sslash}

We present first a game semantics of

Game semantics presents higher-order computation as an exchange of

from the type a structure, called a

or an
arena
,
which presents all the observable computational events available when
interacting on this type, along with their respective causal
dependencies.

A
may be partially ordered by inclusion; this
forms a
pointed dcpo
. All operations on strategies are continuous
with respect to
\subseteq
.

Writing
\text{$\downuparrows$-$\mathsf{Strat}$}_{\oc}(A,B)
for the dcpo of strategies on
A\to B=\,\oc A\multimap B
, the operation

\begin{array}[]{rcrcl}F&:&\text{$\downuparrows$-$\mathsf{Strat}$}_{\oc}(\text{\boldmath$1$},(A\to A)\to A)&\to&\text{$\downuparrows$-$\mathsf{Strat}$}_{\oc}(\text{\boldmath$1$},(A\to A)\to A)\\
&&\sigma&\mapsto&\lambda f^{A\to A}.\,f\,(\sigma\,f)\end{array}

written in
\lambda
-calculus syntax following the cartesian closed
structure of
\text{$\downuparrows$-$\mathsf{Strat}$}_{\oc}
, is continuous. Its least fixed point
\mathcal{Y}_{A}\in\text{$\downuparrows$-$\mathsf{Strat}$}_{\oc}(\text{\boldmath$1$},(A\to A)\to A)
is transported to

\text{$\downuparrows$-$\mathsf{Strat}$}_{\oc}(\Gamma,(A\to A)\to A)
by composition with the terminal
projection. For each
\sigma\in\text{$\downuparrows$-$\mathsf{Strat}$}_{\oc}(\Gamma,A\to A)
,

\mathcal{Y}_{A}\,\sigma\approx\sigma\,(\mathcal{Y}_{A}\,\sigma)

so a fixed point operator up to
\approx
, as needed to interpret
recursion.

It is a curiosity already in AJM games
[

]
that the
recursive equation for the fixpoint combinator must be solved in the
domain of
concrete
strategies, rather than

\approx
-equivalence classes. To the best of our knowledge it is not
known if the partial order induced by inclusion on

\approx
-equivalence classes of strategies has the adequate
completeness properties to solve this,
i.e.
if the
quotient of
\downuparrows
-
\mathsf{Strat}
and
\text{$\downuparrows$-$\mathsf{Strat}$}_{\oc}
by
\approx
are dcpo-enriched
categories.

\mathbf{callcc}
(or some other

that the answer is no, by capturing plays that “respect the call
stack discipline”, and refining the whole interpretation
to show that this invariant is preserved. This is the goal of the
notion of

. First we enrich arenas:

{defi}

A
Question/Answer labeling
on arena
A
is a function

\lambda_{A}:|A|\to\{\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{A}\}\,

invariant under symmetry (if
\theta:x\cong_{A}y
, then for
all
a\in x
,
\lambda_{A}(a)=\lambda_{A}(\theta(a))
) and satisfying:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{\emph{question-opening:}}&\text{if $a\in|A|$ is minimal,
then $\lambda_{A}(a)=\mathcal{Q}$,}\\
\text{\emph{answer-closing:}}&\text{if $\lambda_{A}(a)=\mathcal{A}$, then $a$
is maximal for $\leq_{A}$,}\\
\text{\emph{answer-linear:}}&\text{if $\lambda_{A}(a_{1})=\lambda_{A}(a_{2})=\mathcal{A}$ with $a\rightarrowtriangle_{A}a_{1},a_{2}$, then $a_{1}=a_{2}$ or $a_{1}\mathrel{\#}_{A}a_{2}$.}\end{array}

arenas
have a Question/Answer
labeling.

intuitively correspond to variable calls,
while

correspond to returns.

3.3.1.
Definability

Call a

on arena

invariant under P-view,
i.e.

\ulcorner s\urcorner=s
–

such that for all

Opponent always points to the previous move
. We motivated

subterms
” of
a strategy – it might therefore not be a surprise that those are the

\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner=\{\ulcorner s\urcorner\mid s\in\sigma\}

for the set of P-views of
\sigma

\sigma
is innocent,
then it is simple that
\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner\subseteq\sigma

Moreover,

such that for all

\ulcorner t\urcorner\in\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner

For
\sigma:A

\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner
is not a

verified to be a

– and in particular
uniform
.
Moreover, we have:

Proposition 10
.

For
\sigma,\tau:A

A

\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner=\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\tau\urcorner\!\urcorner
iff
\sigma=\tau
.

Likewise,
\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner\approx\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\tau\urcorner\!\urcorner
if and only if

\sigma\approx\tau
.

Proof 3.1
.

We only detail the second statement. Firstly, if
\sigma\approx\tau

\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner\approx\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\tau\urcorner\!\urcorner
as

\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner\subseteq\sigma
and
\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\tau\urcorner\!\urcorner\subseteq\tau

and the bisimulation game of Definition
2.2.2
preserves

If
\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner\approx\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\tau\urcorner\!\urcorner

\ulcorner s\urcorner\cong_{A}\ulcorner t\urcorner

\ulcorner s\urcorner a^{+}\in\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner

\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner

Proposition 11
.

Consider

\sigma,\tau:A

If
\mathscr{P}(\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner)=\mathscr{P}(\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\tau\urcorner\!\urcorner)

Proof 3.2
.

Let
\sigma,\tau:\llbracket A\rrbracket

\mathscr{P}(\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\!\urcorner)=\mathscr{P}(\ulcorner\!\ulcorner\tau\urcorner\!\urcorner)

By Proposition

Full abstraction of a
denotational model with respect to a language was defined in Section

\mathsf{PCF}
as it stands. For instance,

However, we do of course have
\lambda x^{\mathbb{U}}.\,x;\,x\sim\lambda x^{\mathbb{U}}.\,x

[
plotkin

The celebrated “full abstraction for

(following
[

abstraction

\llbracket-\rrbracket

\mathsf{PCF}

f\sim g\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\forall\alpha\in\mathcal{C}(A\to B,\llbracket\mathbb{U}\rrbracket),\quad(\alpha\circ\overline{f}=\alpha\circ\overline{g})\,,

f,g\in\mathcal{C}(A,B)

\overline{f},\overline{g}\in\mathcal{C}(\text{\boldmath$1$},A\to B)
obtained via cartesian closure, and

\mathcal{C}

equivalence. We say that
\mathcal{C}

\mathsf{PCF}
iff the quotiented model
\mathcal{C}^{\sim}

abstract.

3.6.
Towards Concurrency

The reader may rightly complain that Figure

28

Though we focused on control and interference, there are fully

However, there is no such “semantic hypercube”: the works cited above

disentangle parallelism and

Build a model
\rightarrowtriangle
-
\mathsf{Strat}

and

\text{$\rightarrowtriangle$-$\mathsf{Strat}$}+\text{sequentiality}&\text{is

\text{$\rightarrowtriangle$-$\mathsf{Strat}$}+\text{parallel innocence}+\text{sequentiality}&\text{is fully abstract for}&\mathsf{PCF}\,,\end{array}

\mathsf{IA}_{\sslash}

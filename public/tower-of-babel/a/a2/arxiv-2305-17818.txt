Abstract
. We prove Besov boundary regularity for solutions of the homogeneous Dirichlet problem for fractional-order quasi-linear operators with variable coefficients on Lipschitz domains Ω \Omega of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Our estimates are consistent with the boundary behavior of solutions on smooth domains and apply to fractional p p -Laplacians and operators with finite horizon. The proof exploits the underlying variational structure and uses a new and flexible local translation operator. We further apply these regularity estimates to derive novel error estimates for finite element approximations of fractional p p -Laplacians and present several simulations that reveal the boundary behavior of solutions.



1.
Introduction

In recent years, fractional-order and, more generally, nonlocal operators have received a great deal of attention in applied sciences and engineering. This is mainly because such operators arise in jump processes modeling the ubiquitous phenomenon of anomalous diffusion
[
41
]
. In this vein,
the fractional Laplacian, an outstanding nonlocal operator, arises as a limit of a long-jump random walk
[
46
]
. Among other applications of nonlocal operators, we mention finance
[
21
,
38
]
, ground-water solute transport
[
8
]
, and biological systems with binding, crowding, or trapping, such as electrodiffusion of ions within nerve cells
[
36
,
37
]
.

For problems with a variational structure, finite element methods provide the best approximation in the energy norm, and are amenable to an analysis with low regularity conditions. In our setting, the latter is fundamental because solutions of fractional-order problems generically develop algebraic boundary layers. Solution regularity estimates in the Sobolev scale are a key ingredient to prove a priori convergence rates for the finite element discretization of such problems.

However, most progress in that direction and most computational studies have been limited to either linear or semi-linear problems. This paper deals with fractional-order quasi-linear operators. We prove elliptic regularity estimates up to the boundary of the domain, which is only assumed to be bounded and Lipschitz. The model operator we consider is the so-called
(p,s)
-fractional Laplacian (
s\in(0,1)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
), but our theory is also valid for a broader class of operators, including operators with finite horizon. In this regard, we remark that our regularity estimates for finite-horizon operators are even new for linear problems.
As an application of our regularity estimates, we consider direct finite element discretization of the problems under study and prove convergence rates in the energy norm.

Let us make precise the problem setting in this paper. Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
(
d\geq 1
) be a bounded, Lipschitz domain,
s\in(0,1)
, and
p\in(1,\infty)
. We consider energy functionals with domain the fractional-order Sobolev space
\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
, namely functions in
W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
that vanish in
\Omega^{c}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. More precisely, for a given function
G\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
, with
(x,y,\rho)\mapsto G(x,y,\rho)
, and
f\in(\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega))^{\prime}
, we are interested in minimizers of the energy

(1.1)

\mathcal{F}(u):=\iint_{Q_{\Omega}}G\left(x,y,\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\,dydx-\langle f,u\rangle.

Above,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
stands for the duality pairing between
(\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega))^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
and

Q_{\Omega}:=({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}})\setminus(\Omega^{c}\times\Omega^{c}).

Specific requirements on
G
are listed in Hypothesis
2.1
below. The Gateaux differential of
\mathcal{F}
at
u
is given by
\mathcal{A}\colon\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\to(\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega))^{\prime}
,

(1.2)

\mathcal{A}u(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\left[G_{\rho}\left(x,y,\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)-G_{\rho}\left(y,x,\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\right]\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+s}}\,dy.

For the moment, let us assume that
G
satisfies the relation
G(x,y,\rho)=G(y,x,-\rho)
for a.e.
x,y,\rho
. While this assumption allows us to write the minimization problem in a strong form in a concise fashion, it is not necessary for our theoretical results.
Under this additional condition, we have
G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho)=-G_{\rho}(y,x,-\rho)
for a.e.
x,y,\rho
and we can write

(1.3)

\mathcal{A}u(x):=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}G_{\rho}\left(x,y,\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+s}}\,dy.

Minimizers of (
1.1
) are weak solutions of the homogeneous Dirichlet problem for the operator
\mathcal{A}
:

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathcal{A}u=f&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\mbox{in }\Omega^{c}.\end{array}\right.

We assume standard hypotheses on
G
in order to apply the direct method in the calculus of variations. As a prototypical example, we consider
G(x,y,\rho)=\frac{C_{d,s,p}}{2p}|\rho|^{p}
with
C_{d,s,p}
defined below. Then,
G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho)=\frac{C_{d,s,p}}{2}|\rho|^{p-2}\rho
, and

(1.5)

\mathcal{A}u(x)=(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x):=C_{d,s,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,dy

is the so-called
fractional
(p,s)
-Laplacian
(or
fractional
p
-Laplacian of order
s
). We define the normalizing constant
C_{d,s,p}
as

(1.6)

C_{d,s,p}=\frac{s(1-s)p\;\Gamma(\frac{ps+d}{2})\;2^{2s-2}}{\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{(p-2)s+3}{2})\Gamma(2-s)}.

This choice is somewhat arbitrary, but for
p=2
it allows us to recover the
integral fractional Laplacian,
which is the pseudodifferential operator with symbol
|\xi|^{2s}
. Moreover,
for every smooth function
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
we have the asymptotic behaviors
[
14
,
39
,
22
]

(1.7)

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}v=|v|^{p-2}v,\quad\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}v=-\nabla\cdot(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v).

We also point out that the integral in (
1.5
) needs to be understood in the principal value sense if
s\geq 1-\frac{1}{p}
.

Another way to write the operator in (
1.5
) is

(1.8)

(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)^{p-2}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy,

which suggests that, heuristically, one can understand the fractional
(p,s)
-Laplacian as a weighted fractional Laplacian of order
s
, with a weight
\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)^{p-2}
. This is analogous to the local case, for which the
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta)_{p}u:=\mbox{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
can be regarded as a Laplacian with weight
|\nabla u|^{p-2}
. The Dirichlet problem for the local
p
-Laplacian arises in a number of models of physical processes, including non-Newtonian fluids
[
5
]
, turbulent flows in porous media
[
25
]
, and global climate modeling
[
26
]
. We refer to
[
7
]
for a historical account and other applications of this operator, and to
[
6
,
18
,
31
]
for its numerical treatment.

The representation (
1.8
) also shows that the operator (
1.5
) corresponds to a degenerate diffusion if
p>2
and to a singular one if
p<2
. We refer to
[
17
]
for several motivations for considering nonlinear operators like (
1.5
), to
[
42
]
for a thorough discussion about existence and regularity results for problems driven by the fractional
(p,s)
-Laplacian, and to
[
23
]
for a monotone finite difference scheme with consistency error estimates for
C^{4}
functions and applications to the Cauchy problem for such an operator.

Depending on whether the resulting operator
\mathcal{A}
in (
1.2
) is degenerate or singular, our regularity estimates are somewhat different from one another. To make the point clear, let us focus on the case of the
(p,s)
-Laplacian, although we emphasize that our estimates are valid for more general operators (cf.
Theorem

3.1
below). We derive shift estimates in Besov norms of the form

(1.9)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}_{p^{\prime},1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }p\geq 2,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{1}{2}}_{p^{\prime},1}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }p\leq 2,

where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
; these Besov estimates extend classical ones
[
43
, Theorems 2 and 2’]
to the fractional setting. To check optimality, we consider the prototypical
1d
-function
v(x)=x_{+}^{s}
which mimics the boundary behavior of solutions of (
1.4
) for the operator (
1.5
). A simple calculation using second differences shows that
v\in B^{s+1/p}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
, which revals that (
1.9
) is optimal while (
1.10
) is suboptimal. Moreover, by a simple embedding argument, (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) give rise to Sobolev regularity estimates. Estimate (
1.9
) for
p=2
turns out to be consistent with well-known optimal regularity for solutions to the Dirichlet problem for the integral fractional Laplacian on smooth domains, cf.
[
1
,
33
,
47
]
. Importantly, our estimates are valid for Lipschitz domains and in that sense generalize the ones derived in
[
4
,
11
]
to a quasi-linear setting. Additionally, our estimates are valid under general conditions on the function
G
. In this vein, we point out to
[
30
]
where, for a class of nonlinear operators related to the ones in this work, analysis is performed in fractional-order Orlicz-Sobolev spaces and Hölder regularity estimates are derived for Dirichlet problems on bounded
C^{1,1}
domains.

-Laplacian (

2.
Notation and assumptions

This section establishes the notation and collects some preliminary results.
We provide some discussion on function spaces and Lipschitz domains. We analyze function space characterizations by means of translation operators, discuss the relation between these translations and the regularity of minimizers of (
1.1
), and introduce a suitable localized translation operator to derive regularity estimates. Finally, we make explicit assumptions on the energy, discuss some of their consequences, and comment on how they apply to the model operator (
1.5
).

2.1.
Sobolev and Besov spaces

Here, we briefly review some important facts about Sobolev and Besov spaces. We follow the notation from
[
11
]
and refer to that work for further details.

Given
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, we consider the zero-extension Sobolev space

\widetilde{W}^{\sigma}_{p}(\Omega):=\big\{v\in W^{\sigma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\colon\textrm{supp~}v\subset\overline{\Omega}\big\};

this is a Banach space furnished with the norm

\|v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{\sigma}_{p}(\Omega)}:=|v|_{W^{\sigma}_{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}=\left(\frac{C_{d,s,p}}{2}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+\sigma p}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/p}.

Because functions in
\widetilde{W}^{\sigma}_{p}(\Omega)
vanish in
\Omega^{c}
, the integrand above vanishes on
\Omega^{c}\times\Omega^{c}
and one can effectively compute the integral over
Q_{\Omega}=({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}})\setminus(\Omega^{c}\times\Omega^{c})
.

We define Besov spaces by real interpolation. Given a pair of compatible Banach spaces
(X_{0},X_{1})
,
u\in X_{0}+X_{1}
,
t>0
, and
p\in[1,\infty)
, we consider the
K
-functional

(2.1)

K(t,u):=\inf\left\{\|u_{0}\|_{X_{0}}+t\|u_{1}\|_{X_{1}}\colon u=u_{0}+u_{1},\ u_{0}\in X_{0},\ u_{1}\in X_{1}\right\}.

For
\theta\in(0,1)
and
q\in[1,\infty]
, we define the interpolation spaces

\big[X_{0},X_{1}\big]_{\theta,q}:=\{u\in X_{0}+X_{1}\colon\|u\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,q}}<\infty\},

where

(2.2)

\|u\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta,q}}:=\begin{cases}\Big[q\theta(1-\theta)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-(1+\theta q)}|K(t,u)|^{q}\,dt\Big]^{1/q}&\mbox{if }1\leq q<\infty,\\
\sup_{t>0}\ t^{-\theta}|K(t,u)|&\mbox{if }q=\infty.\end{cases}

The normalization factor
q\theta(1-\theta)
in (
2.2
) guarantees the correct scalings in the limits
\theta\to 0
,
\theta\to 1
and
q\to\infty
for norm continuity; see
[
40
, Appendix B]
for a detailed proof in the case
p=2
. Because we are interested in spaces with differentiability order between zero and two, we let
X_{0}:=L^{p}(\Omega)
,
X_{1}:=W^{2}_{p}(\Omega)
,
\sigma\in(0,2)
and
q\in[1,\infty]
to define the Besov spaces

B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega):=\big[L^{p}(\Omega),W^{2}_{p}(\Omega)\big]_{\sigma/2,q},\quad\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega):=\{v\in B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega):\textrm{supp~}v\subset\overline{\Omega}\}.

By reiteration, we have the following result regarding interpolation of Besov spaces (cf.
[
9
, Theorem 6.4.5]
): given
\sigma_{0}\neq\sigma_{1}
,
1\leq p,q_{0},q_{1},r\leq\infty
and
0<\theta<1
,

(2.3)

\left(B^{\sigma_{0}}_{p,q_{0}}(\Omega),B^{\sigma_{1}}_{p,q_{1}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,r}=B^{\sigma}_{p,r}(\Omega),\quad\mbox{where }\sigma=(1-\theta)\sigma_{0}+\theta\sigma_{1}.

Importantly, we have
B^{\sigma}_{p,p}(\Omega)=W^{\sigma}_{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\sigma\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}
. In the case
\sigma=1
, we only have the equality
B^{1}_{2,2}(\Omega)=H^{\sigma}(\Omega)
, while
B^{1}_{p,p}(\Omega)\subset W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
if
p<2
and
B^{1}_{p,p}(\Omega)\supset W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
if
p>2
, cf.
[
3
, §7.67]
.
Moreover, we have the following inclusions between Besov spaces on bounded Lipschitz domains
[
45
, §3.2.4, §3.3.1]
:

\begin{array}[]{llll}B^{\sigma}_{p,q_{0}}(\Omega)\subset B^{\sigma}_{p,q_{1}}(\Omega),&\mbox{ if }\sigma>0,&1\leq p\leq\infty,&1\leq q_{0}\leq q_{1}\leq\infty;\\
B^{\sigma_{1}}_{p,q_{1}}(\Omega)\subset B^{\sigma_{0}}_{p,q_{0}}(\Omega)&\mbox{ if }0<\sigma_{0}<\sigma_{1},&1\leq p\leq\infty,&1\leq q_{0},q_{1}\leq\infty.\end{array}

We are interested in making precise the statement about inclusion of a higher-order Besov space with integrability index
p\in[1,\infty)
and second parameter
q=\infty
into a lower-order Sobolev space with the same integrability index. Concretely, the next lemma shows the scaling of the continuity constant.

Lemma 2.1

(embedding)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\sigma\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}
, and
\varepsilon\in(0,2-\sigma)
. Then,
B^{\sigma+\varepsilon}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)\subset W^{\sigma}_{p}(\Omega)
with

(2.4)

\|v\|_{W^{\sigma}_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left(\frac{\sigma(2-\sigma)}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|v\|_{B^{\sigma+\varepsilon}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\forall\,v\in B^{\sigma+\varepsilon}_{p,\infty}(\Omega).

Proof.

We exploit the characterization of Besov and fractional-order Sobolev spaces as interpolation spaces between integer-order Sobolev spaces. More precisely, if the
K
-functional corresponds to interpolation between the spaces
X_{0}=L^{p}(\Omega)
and
X_{1}=W^{2}_{p}(\Omega)
, we recall the norm definitions

\|v\|_{B^{\sigma+\varepsilon}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}=\sup_{t>0}\,\Big(t^{-\frac{\sigma+\varepsilon}{2}}|K(t,v)|\Big),

and

\|v\|_{W^{\sigma}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}=\frac{p\sigma(2-\sigma)}{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{\sigma p}{2}}|K(t,v)|^{p}dt

for
\sigma\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}
, according to the remark following (
2.3
).
We split the integral above as the sum of the integrals between
0
and
N
and between
N
and
\infty
, with
N>0
to be chosen. A straightforward calculation gives

\int_{0}^{N}t^{-1-\frac{\sigma p}{2}}|K(t,v)|^{p}dt\leq\sup_{t>0}\,t^{-\frac{(\sigma+\varepsilon)p}{2}}|K(t,v)|^{p}\,\int_{0}^{N}t^{-1+\frac{\varepsilon p}{2}}dt=\frac{2N^{\frac{\varepsilon p}{2}}}{\varepsilon p}\|v\|_{B^{\sigma+\varepsilon}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}^{p}.

Additionally, for any
v\in B^{\sigma+\varepsilon}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)
and
t\geq 0
, we choose the trivial decomposition
v=v+0
in (
2.1
) to obtain

|K(t,v)|\leq\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|v\|_{W^{\sigma}_{p}(\Omega)}.

This gives rise to

\int_{N}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{\sigma p}{2}}|K(t,v)|^{p}dt\leq\|v\|_{W^{\sigma}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\int_{N}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{\sigma p}{2}}dt=\frac{2N^{-\frac{\sigma p}{2}}}{p\sigma}\|v\|_{W^{\sigma}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p},

and thus

\|v\|_{W^{\sigma}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq\frac{\sigma(2-\sigma)N^{\frac{\varepsilon p}{2}}}{2\varepsilon}\|v\|_{B^{\sigma+\varepsilon}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}^{p}+\frac{(2-\sigma)N^{-\frac{\sigma p}{2}}}{2}\|v\|_{W^{\sigma}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

It now suffices to fix
N
sufficiently large so that
\frac{(2-\sigma)N^{-\frac{\sigma p}{2}}}{2}<1
and kick back the last term in the right hand side above to arrive to the desired estimate (
2.4
).
∎

For
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, we define

B^{-\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega):=\big(L^{p}(\Omega),W^{-1}_{p}(\Omega)\big)_{\sigma,q},

and point out that, if
p,q\in(1,\infty]
and
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\in[1,\infty)
are the conjugate exponents,
we have the duality
[
11
]

\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega)=(B^{-\sigma}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega))^{\prime}.

It is common practice to furnish Besov spaces with equivalent norms based on
L^{p}
-norms of difference quotients, instead of the interpolation norm. Given
\rho>0
,

\Omega_{\rho}:=\{x\in\Omega:\textrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<\rho\},\quad\Omega^{\rho}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\textrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>\rho\},

and a set of admissible directions
D\subset{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, typically a ball, we denote

|v|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega;D)}:=\Big(q\sigma(2-\sigma)\int_{D}\frac{\|v_{h}-2v+v_{-h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{|h|})}^{q}}{|h|^{d+q\sigma}}dh\Big)^{1/q}

for
p,q\in[1,\infty)
while for
q=\infty
we let

|v|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}(\Omega;D)}:=\sup_{h\in D}\frac{\|v_{h}-2v+v_{-h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{|h|})}}{|h|^{\sigma}},

where
v_{h}(x):=v(x+h)
is the translation with vector
h\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. It is well-known that, if
D
is a ball, then the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+|\cdot|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega;D)}
is equivalent to the Besov norm
\|\cdot\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega)}
defined through interpolation
[
3
, Theorem 7.47]
. Moreover,
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
shows that balls
D
can be replaced by suitable convex cones in the definition of Besov seminorms for
q=\infty
.
More precisely, let us assume
D\subset{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is bounded and star-shaped with respect to the origin. We say that

D

generates

{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
if there exists
\rho_{0}(D)>0
such that for every
\rho\leq\rho_{0}(D)
and
every
h\in D_{\rho}(0)
, the ball of radius
\rho
and center
0
, there exists
\{h_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}\subset D\cup(-D)
satisfying

h=\sum_{j=1}^{d}h_{j},\quad\sum_{j=1}^{d}|h_{j}|\leq c|h|

with a constant
c>0
only dependent on
D
.
The following equivalence is proved in
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
.

Proposition 2.1

(Besov seminorms using cones)
.

Let
D
be a convex cone generating
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and let
B\subset{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a ball. If
\sigma\in(0,2)
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, then for every function
v\colon{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}
we have
|v|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}(\Omega;D)}\simeq|v|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}(\Omega;B)}
.

We will decompose
\Omega
into overlapping balls and apply Proposition
2.1
to subdomains
\omega
made of intersections of such balls with
\Omega
. The convex cone
D
will depend on
\omega
and will be dictated by the Lipschitz property of
\Omega
. However, we will omit writing
D
in
B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\omega)
for simplicity of notation but without compromising clarity.

We can estimate higher-order Besov norms, possibly of order higher than one, in terms of difference quotients of Besov norms of order less than one. We express this instrumental reiteration property as follows and refer to
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Proposition 2.2

(reiteration of Besov seminorms)
.

Let
\omega\subset{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
s\in(0,1)
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
\sigma\in(0,1]
and let
D
be a set generating
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and star-shaped with respect to the origin. Then,

\begin{split}&|v|_{B^{s+\sigma}_{p,q}(\omega)}\lesssim\left(\int_{D}\frac{|v-v_{h}|^{q}_{W^{s}_{p}(\omega)}}{|h|^{d+q\sigma}}\,dh\right)^{1/q},\quad q\in[1,\infty),\\
&|v|_{B^{s+\sigma}_{p,\infty}(\omega)}\lesssim\sup_{h\in D}\frac{1}{|h|^{\sigma}}|v-v_{h}|_{W^{s}_{p}(\omega)}.\end{split}

2.2.
Lipschitz domains

We next briefly state a few well-known but relevant results regarding Lipschitz domains in
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.

Definition 2.1

(admissible outward vectors)
.

For every
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and
\rho\in(0,1]
, we define the set of admissible outward vectors

\mathcal{O}_{\rho}(x_{0})=\{h\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\colon|h|\leq\rho,(B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega)+th\subset\Omega^{c},\ \forall t\in[0,1]\}.

An important fact about bounded Lipschitz domains is that they satisfy a uniform cone property. This can be stated in the following fashion
[
32
, §1.2.2]
.

Proposition 2.3

(uniform cone property)
.

If
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, then there exist
\rho\in(0,1]
,
\theta\in(0,\pi]
and a map
\textbf{n}\colon{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\to S^{d-1}
such that, for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
,

\mathcal{C}_{\rho}(\textbf{n}(x),\theta):=\{h\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\colon|h|\leq\rho,\ h\cdot\textbf{n}\geq|h|\cos\theta\}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\rho}(x).

Besov seminorms can be equivalently written as sums of norms over partitions, as long as the partitions have some overlap. We refer to
[
11
, Lemma 2.6]
.

Lemma 2.2

(localization)
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
\sigma\in(0,2)
. Let
\{D_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
be a finite covering of
\Omega
by balls of radius
\rho
,
D_{j}=D_{\rho}(x_{j})
. Then,
v\in B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega)
if and only if
v\big|_{\Omega\cap B_{j}}\in B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega\cap D_{j})
for all
j=1,\ldots,J
, and

(2.5)

\|v\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega)}^{p}\simeq\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|v\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega\cap D_{j})}^{p}.

Moreover, for
\delta\geq\rho
, let
\{D_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
be a finite covering of
\Omega^{\delta}

and let
v\colon{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\mbox{supp}(v)\subset\overline{\Omega}
. Then,
v\in\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega)
if and only if
v\big|_{D_{j}}\in B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{j})
for all
j=1,\ldots,J
, and

(2.6)

\|v\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega)}^{p}\simeq|v|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega)}^{p}\simeq\sum_{j=1}^{J}|v|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{j})}^{p}.

The equivalence constants above depend on
s,p,q,\Omega
and the covering chosen.

2.3.
Localized translation operator

Our next goal is to construct a smooth operator that resembles a translation around a certain given point
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, while coincides with the identity away from
x_{0}
. Such
localized translation operator
plays an instrumental role in our derivation of regularity estimates.

Given
x_{0}
and
\rho
, we fix a cut-off function
\phi
such that
0\leq\phi\leq 1
,
\phi\equiv 1
on the ball
D_{\rho}(x_{0})
of radius
\rho
centered at
x_{0}
,
\mbox{supp}(\phi)\subset B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
. Given
h\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, we define

(2.7)

T_{h}v(x):=v\big(x+h\phi(x)\big)=\big(v\circ S_{h}\big)(x),

where the map
S_{h}:=I+h\phi
is defined from
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
to
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. We restrict our consideration to
|h|
small enough such that the Jacobian of
S_{h}
satisfies

\frac{1}{2}I\preccurlyeq\nabla S_{h}=I+h\otimes\nabla\phi\preccurlyeq 2I.

and thus
S_{h}
is a one-to-one mapping from
D_{2\rho}(x_{0})
to
D_{2\rho}(x_{0})
. It is also one-to-one from
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
to
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and coincides with the identity in
D_{2\rho}(x_{0})^{c}
.

In
[
43
,
11
]
, the localized translation operator

\widetilde{T}_{h}v:=\phi v_{h}+(1-\phi)v

was employed instead. The translation operator
T_{h}
in (
2.7
) is somewhat more flexible than
\widetilde{T}_{h}
, in the sense that it gives rise to cleaner regularity estimates in which a priori one gains one full derivative; compare the right-hand side in (
3.5
) below with the one in
[
11
, formula (3.3)]
. This leads to a simpler bootstrapping argument than in
[
11
]
. We return to this point in
Remark

9
below.

Remark 1

(properties of
S_{h}
and
S_{h}^{-1}
)
.

Some important properties of the transformations
S_{h}
and
S_{h}^{-1}
follow immediately from their definitions. We have the inequalities

(2.8)

\displaystyle|S_{h}(x)-x|\lesssim|h|,\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left|\det(\nabla S_{h}(x))-1\right|\lesssim|h|,\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{|x-y|}{|S_{h}(x)-S_{h}(y)|}-1\right|\lesssim|h|,\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

Analogous properties also hold for
S^{-1}_{h}
.

Remark 2

(boundedness of translations)
.

Clearly, the operator
T_{h}
in (
2.7
) is bounded from
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
to
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and, more in general, from
W^{k}_{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
to
W^{k}_{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, it is also bounded from
B^{\sigma}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
to
B^{\sigma}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
for any non-integer
\sigma>0
. Moreover, if
h\in\mathcal{O}_{\rho}(x_{0})
is an admissible outward vector (cf. Definition
2.1
) and
v\in\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle x\in\Omega^{c}\cap D_{2\rho}(x_{0}):\quad 0\leq\phi(x)\leq 1\Rightarrow S_{h}(x)=x+h\phi(x)\in\Omega^{c}\Rightarrow T_{h}v(x)=0,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega^{c}\setminus D_{2\rho}(x_{0}):\quad\phi(x)=0\Rightarrow T_{h}v(x)=v(x)=0.

Therefore,
T_{h}
is also a bounded operator from
\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega)
to
\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 2.3

(moduli of continuity)
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
. For all
\sigma\in(0,1)
we have

(2.11)

\|v-T_{h}v\|_{L^{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\lesssim|h|^{\sigma}\|v\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\quad\forall v\in B^{\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0})).

Moreover, for all
r>0
and
\sigma\in[0,1]
, we have

(2.12)

\|v-T_{h}v\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\lesssim|h|^{\sigma}\|v\|_{B^{r+\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\quad\forall v\in B^{r+\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0})).

Proof.

In first place, we remark that because
T_{h}v=v\circ S_{h}
,
T_{h}v-v
vanishes on
D_{2\rho}(x_{0})^{c}
. The boundedness of
T_{h}
on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
yields

\|v-T_{h}v\|_{L^{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))},

while a standard calculation gives

(2.13)

\|v-T_{h}v\|_{L^{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\lesssim|h|\,\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}.

Estimate (
2.11
) follows by interpolation.

We next consider higher-order derivatives. Given a positive integer
k
and
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=k
, a direct calculation gives for any sufficiently smooth
v
and
x\in D_{2\rho}(x_{0})
,

\left|\left(D^{\alpha}v\right)\circ S_{h}(x)-D^{\alpha}\left(v\circ S_{h}(x)\right)\right|\lesssim|h|\|\phi\|_{C^{k}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}\sum_{0<|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq k}\big|\big(D^{\alpha^{\prime}}v\big)\circ S_{h}(x)\big|.

Consequently, applying (
2.13
) to
\left(D^{\alpha}v\right)\circ S_{h}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|v-T_{h}v\|_{W^{k}_{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}=\|v-v\circ S_{h}\|_{W^{k}_{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\!\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq k}\!\!\!\!\|\!\left(D^{\alpha}v\right)\!\circ\!S_{h}\!-\!D^{\alpha}\left(v\!\circ\!S_{h}\right)\!\|_{L^{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\!+\!\|\!\left(D^{\alpha}v\right)\!\circ\!S_{h}\!-\!D^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim|h|\!\!\sum_{0<|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq k+1}\|D^{\alpha^{\prime}}v\|_{L^{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\leq|h|\|v\|_{W^{k+1}_{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}.

By using the boundedness of
T_{h}
on
W^{k}_{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and interpolation, we deduce that

\|v-T_{h}v\|_{W^{k}_{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\lesssim|h|^{\sigma}\|v\|_{B^{k+\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\quad\forall v\in B^{k+\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))

for
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Finally, we obtain (
2.12
) by combining this estimate with (
2.3
).
∎

Remark 3
.

The only properties of
S_{h}
we exploited in the previous lemma are the fact that it maps
D_{2\rho}(x_{0})
onto
D_{2\rho}(x_{0})
, and that the resulting translation operator
T_{h}v=v\circ S_{h}
is stable in
W^{k}_{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))
and satisfies properties like (
2.13
). Thus, the same arguments can be applied to the translation operator
v\circ S_{h}^{-1}
. In particular, we have

\|v-v\circ S_{h}^{-1}\|_{W^{k}_{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\lesssim|h|^{\sigma}|v|_{B^{k+\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\quad\forall v\in B^{k+\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))

for
\sigma\in(0,1)
, and

\|v-v\circ S_{h}^{-1}\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\lesssim|h|^{\sigma}\|v\|_{B^{r+\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\quad\forall v\in B^{r+\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))

for
r>0,\sigma\in[0,1]
.

2.4.
Assumptions on the energy

We recall the energy (
1.1
),

\mathcal{F}(u)=\iint_{Q_{\Omega}}G\left(x,y,\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\,dydx-\langle f,u\rangle.

Here, we list the conditions we require on this functional, discuss some consequences of these conditions, and how they apply to the problems we are interested in.

Hypothesis 2.1
.

The function
G\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
satisfies the following conditions.

1. Convexity.
The function
\rho\mapsto G(x,y,\rho)
is uniformly convex for each
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.

2. Space continuity.
There exists
\beta\in(0,1]
such that
G
is
\beta
-Hölder continuous with respect to the space variables:
for all
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and
\rho\in\mathbb{R},

(2.14)

|G(x,y,\rho)-G(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},\rho)|\leq C(|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{\beta})|\rho|^{p}.

3.
p
-growth.
There exists some
p\in(1,\infty)
such that, for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
,
G(x,y,\rho)
is differentiable with respect to
\rho
with

(2.15)

|G(x,y,\rho)|\leq C|\rho|^{p},\qquad|G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho)|\leq C|\rho|^{p-1}.

4. Monotonicity.
There exists
\alpha>0
such that, if
2\leq p<\infty
in the
p
-growth
condition, then for all

x,y,\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},

(G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho)-G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho^{\prime}))(\rho-\rho^{\prime})\geq\alpha|\rho-\rho^{\prime}|^{p},

while if
1<p<2
, then for all
x,y,\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},

(G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho)-G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho^{\prime}))(\rho-\rho^{\prime})\geq\alpha|\rho-\rho^{\prime}|^{2}\big||\rho|+|\rho^{\prime}|\big|^{p-2}.

5. Continuity.
There exists
c>0
such that, if
2\leq p<\infty
in the
p
-growth
condition, then

|G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho)-G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho^{\prime})|\leq c|\rho-\rho^{\prime}|\,\big||\rho|+|\rho^{\prime}|\big|^{p-2}\quad\forall x,y,\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\ \rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},

while if
1<p<2
, then

|G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho)-G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho^{\prime})|\leq c|\rho-\rho^{\prime}|^{p-1}\quad\forall x,y,\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\ \rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 4

(symmetry)
.

While not strictly needed for our purposes, the following assumption is practical for the analysis and applies to a general class of operators:

6. Symmetry.
The function
G
is symmetric with respect to the space variables and with respect to
\rho
,

G(x,y,\rho)=G(y,x,\rho),\quad G(x,y,\rho)=G(x,y,-\rho),\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\rho\in\mathbb{R}.

Under this symmetry assumption, the operator
\mathcal{A}
associated with the energy minimization problem becomes (
1.3
); otherwise, it takes the form (
1.2
).

Remark 5

(monotonicity)
.

The
monotonicity
hypothesis above implies the following estimates for the operator
\mathcal{A}
in (
1.2
). If
2\leq p<\infty
, there exists
\alpha>0
such that,
for all
u,v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega),

(2.16)

\langle\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v,u-v\rangle\geq\alpha\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p};

hence
\mathcal{A}
is
p
-coercive in
\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
. Instead, if
1<p<2
, we have for all
u,v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)

(2.17)

\langle\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v,u-v\rangle\geq\alpha\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{2}\left(\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{p-2},

whence
\mathcal{A}
is
2
-coercive on bounded sets in
\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
:

\langle\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v,u-v\rangle\geq C(R)\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{2}

provided
\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)},\|v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\leq R
.
It is worth realizing that (
2.16
) cannot hold for
p<2
and
G
smooth and convex

[
43
, Remark 2.1]
. This fact is responsible for the dichotomy between (
1.9
) and (
1.10
)
and reveals that our variational approach, which hinges on (
2.17
) for
p<2
, cannot improve upon (
1.10
).

Remark 6

(continuity)
.

The
continuity
hypothesis implies that the operator
\mathcal{A}
satisfies the following bounds: if
p\in(1,2]
then for all
u,v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
,

(2.18)

\|\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1},

while if
p\in[2,\infty)
then for all
u,v\in\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
,

(2.19)

\|\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{p-2}\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}.

Remark 7

(solution operator)
.

The uniform convexity of
G
yields existence and uniqueness of weak solutions: given
f\in W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, the
problem

(2.20)

u_{f}\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\quad\langle\mathcal{A}u_{f},v\rangle=\langle f,v\rangle\quad\forall v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega),

admits a unique solution, where

\langle\mathcal{A}u,v\rangle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\widetilde{G}\left(x,y,\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy

and
\widetilde{G}(x,y,\rho):=G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho)/\rho
.
Testing (
2.20
) with
v=u_{f}
(or, equivalently, setting
v\equiv 0
in (
2.16
)–(
2.17
)), we immediately reach the stability estimate

(2.21)

\|u_{f}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

We now assess the continuity properties of the solution operator
f\mapsto u_{f}
.
If we assume
\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq K
, then
u_{f}
satisfies
\|u_{f}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\leq\left(\frac{K}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
in view of (
2.21
). This shows that, denoting

\overline{B}_{K}:=\{f\in W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\colon\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq K\},

and using (
2.17
),
then the solution operator defined on
\overline{B}_{K}
is Lipschitz continuous,

(2.22)

\|u_{f}-u_{g}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\leq c(K)\|f-g\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

for
p\in(1,2)
.
In contrast, if
p\in[2,\infty)
, then the solution map is Hölder continuous on
W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
because of (
2.16
),

(2.23)

\|u_{f}-u_{g}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\|f-g\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Fractional
(p,s)
-Laplacians.
For
p\in(1,\infty)
we consider
G(x,y,\rho)=\frac{C_{d,s,p}}{2p}|\rho|^{p}
in (
1.1
), which gives rise to (
1.5
). It is clear that the parameter
p
in this definition corresponds to the parameter
p
in Hypothesis
2.1
, and therefore the
convexity
and
p
-growth
conditions (
2.15
) hold. Moreover, because
G
is independent of the space variables, it is trivially symmetric with respect to
x,y
, and we can take
\beta=1
in (
2.14
).
The
monotonicity
and
continuity
assumptions are satisfied because of the following auxiliary identities
[
31
, §5]
:
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\left||a|^{p-2}a-|b|^{p-2}b\right|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}C|a-b|^{p-1}&\mbox{if }1<p\leq 2,\\
C|a-b|(|a|+|b|)^{p-2}&\mbox{if }2\leq p<\infty,\end{array}\right.

and

\left(|a|^{p-2}a-|b|^{p-2}b\right)(a-b)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\alpha|a-b|^{2}(|a|+|b|)^{p-2}&\mbox{if }1<p\leq 2,\\
\alpha|a-b|^{p}&\mbox{if }2\leq p<\infty.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, the Hypothesis
2.1
covers fractional
(p,s)
-Laplace operators (
1.5
).

2.5.
Regularity of functionals

Inspired by
[
43
]
, we introduce a notion of regularity of functionals that measures their sensitivity with respect to a family of perturbations.

Definition 2.2

(
(T,D,\sigma)
-regularity)
.

Let
V
be a Banach space,
K\subset V
and
\sigma>0
. Given a family of maps
T_{h}\colon K\to K
, with
h
varying on a given set
D\subset{\mathbb{R}^{d}},
we say that a functional
\mathcal{F}
is
(T,D,\sigma)
-regular on
K
if, for all
v\in K
,

\omega(v)=\omega(v;\mathcal{F},T,D,\sigma):=\sup_{h\in D}\frac{\mathcal{F}(T_{h}v)-\mathcal{F}(v)}{|h|^{\sigma}}<\infty.

Remark 8

(subadditivity)
.

The modulus
\omega
of
(T,D,\sigma)
-regularity is subadditive with respect to the
\mathcal{F}
-argument:

(2.24)

\omega(v;\mathcal{F}_{1}+\mathcal{F}_{2},T,D,\sigma)\leq\omega(v;\mathcal{F}_{1},T,D,\sigma)+\omega(v;\mathcal{F}_{2},T,D,\sigma).

Thus, in order to prove the
(T,D,\sigma)
-regularity of
\mathcal{F}_{1}+\mathcal{F}_{2}
, it suffices to show the regularity of each of the two functionals separately.

A key consequence of the
monotonicity
assumption in Hypothesis
2.1
is the following estimate
[
43
, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1]
.

Lemma 2.4

(regularity and minimizers)
.

Let
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
,
\rho>0
and
h\in\mathcal{O}_{\rho}(x_{0})
. Consider translation operators
T_{h}\colon\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\to\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
as in (
2.7
).
If
u
solves (
1.4
), the functional
\mathcal{F}
defined in (
1.1
) satisfies Hypotheses
2.1
and it is
(T,D,\sigma)
-regular on
\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
for some
\sigma>0
, then the following hold:

•

If
p\geq 2
, then

(2.25)

\alpha\|u-T_{h}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq p\omega(u)|h|^{\sigma}.

•

If
1<p<2
, then

(2.26)

\alpha\|u-T_{h}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C(p)\left(\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}+\|T_{h}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{2-p}\omega(u)|h|^{\sigma}.

Let us explain the crucial role of Lemma
2.4
in the proof of regularity of solutions, and how localized translations come into play. Let
D_{\rho}(x_{0})
be a ball with center
x_{0}\in\Omega
and radius
\rho
satisfying
Proposition

2.3
(uniform cone property), and let
D=\mathcal{C}_{\rho}(\textbf{n}(x_{0}),\theta)
. For
p\in[2,\infty)
we combine
Proposition

2.2
(reiteration of Besov seminorms) with (
2.25
) to obtain

(2.27)

\begin{split}|u|^{p}_{B^{s+\sigma/p}_{p,\infty}(D_{\rho}(x_{0}))}&\lesssim\sup_{h\in D}\frac{|u-u_{h}|^{p}_{W^{s}_{p}(D_{\rho}(x_{0}))}}{|h|^{\sigma}}\\
&\lesssim\sup_{h\in D}\frac{\|u-T_{h}u\|^{p}_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}}{|h|^{\sigma}}\lesssim\omega(u;\mathcal{F},T,D,\sigma),\end{split}

for
\sigma\in(0,1]
.
Instead for
p\in(1,2)
, combining
Proposition

2.2
with (
2.26
) yields

(2.28)

\begin{split}|u|^{p}_{B^{s+\sigma/2}_{p,\infty}(D_{\rho}(x_{0}))}&\lesssim\sup_{h\in D}\frac{\|u-T_{h}u\|^{2}_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}}{|h|^{\sigma}}\\
&\lesssim\Big(\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}+\|T_{h}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\Big)^{2-p}\omega(u;\mathcal{F},T,D,\sigma).\end{split}

This shows that proving that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies
Definition

2.2
(
(T,D,\sigma)
-regularity) for some
\sigma\in(0,1]
gives rise to local regularity of solutions.

3.
Regularity

In this section we obtain regularity estimates for solutions to (
2.20
). For that purpose, we analyze the regularity of the functional
\mathcal{F}
in (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.2
, and exploit the crucial property that the operator
T_{h}
(
2.7
) is locally a translation and
T_{h}v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
for all
v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
.

We split the energy in (
1.1
) as
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{G}-\mathcal{F}_{1}
, with

(3.1)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}_{G}(u):=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}G\left(x,y,\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\,dydx,\\
&\mathcal{F}_{1}(u):=\langle f,u\rangle\end{split}

and recall that, by the subadditivity property (

), we can treat the two terms separately.

3.1.
Regularity of the functionals

As a first step towards deriving regularity estimates for minimizers of (

), we prove the regularity of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{G}
with respect to the family of admissible outward vectors, cf.
Definition

2.1
. We begin with an estimate on the linear part of the functional.

Proposition 3.1

(regularity of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
)
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty]
,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
,
\sigma\in(0,1]
and
t\in(-1,\sigma)
.
If
f\in B^{t}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, then

\mathcal{F}_{1}
is
(T,\mathcal{O}_{\rho}(x_{0}),\sigma)
-regular in
\dot{B}^{\sigma-t}_{p,q}(\Omega)
for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
\rho>0
.
Namely, for all
v\in\dot{B}^{\sigma-t}_{p,q}(\Omega)

(3.2)

\sup_{h\in\mathcal{O}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{1}(T_{h}v)-\mathcal{F}_{1}(v)}{|h|^{\sigma}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{t}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)}\|v\|_{B^{\sigma-t}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}.

Proof.

We split the proof into three steps depending on the range of
\sigma
. Let
\sigma\in(0,1]
,

r\geq 0
, and
v\in\dot{B}^{r+\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega)
.

1.
Case
t\in(-1,0]
:
We use (
2.7
) to write
T_{h}v=v\circ S_{h}
with
S_{h}=I+h\phi
, and recall that
\mbox{supp}(\phi)\subset D_{2\rho}(x_{0})
, so that

v-T_{h}v\in\dot{B}^{r}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))
.
We thus obtain

\mathcal{F}_{1}(T_{h}v)-\mathcal{F}_{1}(v)=\langle f,T_{h}v-v\rangle\leq\|f\|_{B^{-r}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)}\|T_{h}v-v\|_{\dot{B}^{r}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}.

Next, we resort to (
2.12
) to deduce

\|T_{h}v-v\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(B_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\leq|h|^{\sigma}\|v\|_{B^{r+\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))},

which implies for all
v\in\dot{B}^{r+\sigma}_{p,q}(\Omega)

(3.3)

\left|\mathcal{F}_{1}(T_{h}v)-\mathcal{F}_{1}(v)\right|\lesssim|h|^{\sigma}\|f\|_{B^{-r}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)}\|v\|_{B^{r+\sigma}_{p,q}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}.

This establishes (
3.2
) upon setting
r=-t
.

2.
Case
t=\sigma
: Let
f\in B^{\sigma}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)
and change variables to write

\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,v(S_{h}(x))\,dx=\int_{S_{h}(\Omega)}f(S_{h}^{-1}(x))v(x)|\det\nabla S_{h}^{-1}(x)|\,dx,

whence,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}_{1}(T_{h}v)-\mathcal{F}_{1}(v)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}\left[f(S_{h}^{-1}(x))|\det\nabla S_{h}^{-1}(x)|-f(x)\right]v(x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\|(1-|\det\nabla S_{h}^{-1}|)(f\circ S_{h}^{-1})\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\|v\|_{L^{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}\|f\circ S_{h}^{-1}-f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)},

because
(1-|\det\nabla S_{h}^{-1}|)(f\circ S_{h}^{-1})
and
f\circ S_{h}^{-1}-f
vanish on
D_{2\rho}(x_{0})^{c}
.
We use Remark
1
(properties of
S_{h}
and
S_{h}^{-1}
) and
Lemma

2.3
(moduli of continuity) to deduce

(3.4)

\left|\mathcal{F}_{1}(T_{h}v)-\mathcal{F}_{1}(v)\right|\lesssim|h|^{\sigma}\|f\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{p}(D_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}.

3.
Case
t\in(0,\sigma)
:
Since the mapping
(f,v)\to\mathcal{F}_{1}(T_{h}v)-\mathcal{F}_{1}(v)
is bilinear, we may interpolate between (
3.3
) with
r=0
and (
3.4
), with the same
p
and
q
in both expressions, to infer that
(
3.2
) holds as well in this case.
∎

Next, we prove the regularity of the non-linear term
\mathcal{F}_{G}
, defined in (
3.1
).

Proposition 3.2

(regularity of
\mathcal{F}_{G}
)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and assume that
G
satisfies Hypothesis
2.1
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\beta\in(0,1]
.
Then, the functional
\mathcal{F}_{G}\colon\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
defined in (
3.1
) is
(T,\mathcal{O}_{\rho}(x_{0}),\beta)
-regular in
\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
\rho>0
.
Namely, for all
v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
it holds that

(3.5)

\sup_{h\in\mathcal{O}_{\rho}(x_{0})}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{G}(T_{h}v)-\mathcal{F}_{G}(v)}{|h|^{\beta}}\lesssim\iint_{Q_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}dydx,

where
Q_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}:=(B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}})\cup({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times B_{2\rho}(x_{0}))
.

Proof.

The change of variables
(x,y)\mapsto(S_{h}^{-1}(x),S_{h}^{-1}(y))=:(x_{h},y_{h})
leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{G}(T_{h}v)-\mathcal{F}_{G}(v)

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\frac{G\left(x,y,\frac{T_{h}v(x)-T_{h}v(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)-G\left(x,y,\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)}{|x-y|^{d}}\,dydx

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\frac{G\left(x_{h},y_{h},\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x_{h}-y_{h}|^{s}}\right)}{|x_{h}-y_{h}|^{d}}\,J(x,y)dydx

\displaystyle-\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}G\left(x,y,\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\,dydx,

where
J(x,y):=|\det(\nabla S^{-1}_{h}(x))\det(\nabla S^{-1}_{h}(y))|
for conciseness. We further split

\mathcal{F}_{G}(T_{h}v)-\mathcal{F}_{G}(v)=I+II+III,

with

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\frac{G\left(x_{h},y_{h},\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x_{h}-y_{h}|^{s}}\right)-G\left(x_{h},y_{h},\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)}{|x_{h}-y_{h}|^{d}}\,J(x,y)\,dydx,

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle:=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\frac{G\left(x_{h},y_{h},\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)-G\left(x,y,\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)}{|x_{h}-y_{h}|^{d}}\,J(x,y)\,dydx,

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle:=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}G\left(x,y,\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\left(\frac{J(x,y)}{|x_{h}-y_{h}|^{d}}-\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\right)dydx.

We observe that, because
S_{h}
is a one-to-one mapping on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
that coincides with the identity over
B_{2\rho}(x_{0})^{c}
, all the integrals above need to be computed on the set
Q_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}=(B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}})\cup({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times B_{2\rho}(x_{0}))
.

Applying the Mean Value Theorem and using (
2.9
), (
2.10
) and the growth condition (
2.15
), it follows that

(3.6)

\left|I\right|\lesssim|h|\iint_{Q_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}dydx.

Next, we resort to the
\beta
-Hölder continuity of
G
with respect to the space variables (
2.14
), use (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), and obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left|II\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\iint_{Q_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}}\left(|x_{h}-x|^{\beta}+|y_{h}-y|^{\beta}\right)\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}dydx

\displaystyle\lesssim|h|^{\beta}\iint_{Q_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}dydx.

Finally, we use (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) to derive

\left|\frac{J(x,y)}{|S^{-1}_{h}(x)-S^{-1}_{h}(y)|^{d}}-\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}\right|\leq\frac{|h|}{|x-y|^{d}}.

Therefore, combining this bound with condition (
2.15
), we obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle|III|

\displaystyle\lesssim|h|\iint_{Q_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}dydx.

Collecting (
3.6
), (
3.7
), and (
3.8
), we deduce

|\mathcal{F}_{G}(T_{h}v)-\mathcal{F}_{G}(v)|\lesssim|h|^{\beta}\iint_{Q_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}dydx

and conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 9

(translation operator)
.

The estimate (
3.5
) depends crucially on the structure of the translation operator (
2.7
). Indeed, localizing through a composition enabled us to perform a simple argument based on change of variables. In contrast, if one aims to exploit the convexity of the functional
\mathcal{F}_{G}
by using a translation operator of the form

\widetilde{T}_{h}=\phi v_{h}+(1-\phi)v

for a suitable cutoff
\phi
, as in
[
43
,
11
]
, then one obtains a less accurate estimate where the right-hand side involves higher-order norms of
u

[
11
, Proposition 3.2]
.

3.2.
Regularity of solutions

We are now in position to prove the regularity of minimizers of the energy (
1.1
) under Hypothesis
2.1
. We first prove estimates in the Besov scale, and afterwards derive estimates in Sobolev norms by using embeddings. Finally, we show how to accommodate the theory to include finite-horizon operators.

Theorem 3.1

(maximal Besov regularity)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
s\in(0,1)
,
G\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
satisfy Hypothesis
2.1
, with
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\beta\in(0,1]
, and let
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}

If
p\geq 2
and
f\in B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{p^{\prime}}}_{p^{\prime},1}(\Omega)
, then
u\in\dot{B}^{s+\frac{\beta}{p}}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)
and

(3.9)

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\frac{\beta}{p}}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{p^{\prime}}}_{p^{\prime},1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

If
p<2
and

(3.10)

Proof.

Let
\gamma=\frac{\beta}{p^{\prime}}
if

and
\gamma=\frac{\beta}{2}
if
p<2
. We first observe that problem (
1.4
) is well-posed, because

2.21
), we have

\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{-s+\gamma}_{p^{\prime},1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}.

We split the proof into three steps.

1. Because
\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain, by Proposition
2.3
(uniform cone property) there exist
\rho
,
\varphi
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}(\textbf{n}(x),\varphi)\subset\mathcal{O}_{\rho}(x)
for all

. We consider a finite covering of

by balls
D_{j}=D(x_{j},\rho)
of radius

. By the localization estimate (

), it suffices to bound Besov seminorms over each of the balls
D_{j}
.

We consider one of the balls
D_{j}
in the covering and set
\mathcal{C}_{j}=\mathcal{C}_{\rho}(\textbf{n}(x_{j}),\varphi)
. Importantly, if

we can guarantee that

Let
\sigma\in(0,\beta]
and

; our proof will use suitable bounds on the

-regularity modulus of
\mathcal{F}
. We exploit the subadditivity of this functional, and Propositions

(regularity of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
) with

(regularity of

(3.12)

\displaystyle\omega(u;\mathcal{F},T,\mathcal{C}_{j},\sigma)\leq\omega(u;\mathcal{F}_{G},T,\mathcal{C}_{j},\sigma)+\omega(u;\mathcal{F}_{1},T,\mathcal{C}_{j},\sigma)

\displaystyle\lesssim\iint_{Q_{B_{2\rho}(x_{j})}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}dydx+\|f\|_{B^{t}_{p^{\prime},1}(D_{2\rho}(x_{j})\cap\Omega)}\|u\|_{B^{\sigma-t}_{p,\infty}(D_{2\rho}(x_{j}))}.

In view of (
2.27
) and (
2.28
), using (
3.11
) we deduce

(3.13)

|u|_{B^{s+\sigma/q}_{p,\infty}(D_{j})}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{q-p}{p-1}}\omega(u;\mathcal{F},T,\mathcal{C}_{j},\sigma)

provided
q:=\max\{2,p\}
. We next distinguish between
p\in[2,\infty)
and
p\in(1,2)

(
3.12
) and (
3.13
) with

.

2.
Case

. Upon choosing

Adding over

where the hidden constant depends on

Thus, replacing

by

3.
Case

. We choose

in (

in (

for

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma/2}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}\left(|u|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\|f\|_{B^{-s+\beta/2}_{p^{\prime},1}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma+s-\beta/2}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}\right)

.
We next resort to (

) to obtain the following counterpart of (

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma/2}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\left(C_{1}\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{p^{\prime}}+C_{2}\|f\|_{B^{-s+\beta/2}_{p^{\prime},1}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma+s-\beta/2}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}

and with constants

and

We now proceed as in Step 2 to exploit the improvement of the differentiability index from the right-hand side

\sigma_{0}=0,\sigma_{k+1}=\sigma\in(0,\beta]

\sigma_{k}=\beta(1-2^{-k})

.
We prove by induction that

with a uniformly bounded constant
\Lambda_{k}
. We first note that, according to (

) is true for

.
Suppose that (
3.17
) holds for some

in (

\begin{split}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma_{k+1}/2}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}&\leq\left(C_{1}\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{p^{\prime}}+C_{2}\|f\|_{B^{-s+\beta/2}_{p^{\prime},1}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma_{k}/2}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}\\

where
C_{3}:=C_{3}(\Omega,d,s,p,\beta)
is the constant of
the continuous embedding
W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\subset B^{-s+\beta/2}_{p^{\prime},1}(\Omega)
.
Since
1\leq 2-2^{-k}\leq 2
, we have
C_{3}^{2-2^{-k}}\leq\max\{C_{3},C_{3}^{2}\}=:C_{4}
which gives rise to (

) with constant

\Lambda_{k+1}:=\left(C_{1}C_{4}+C_{2}\Lambda_{k}\right)^{1/2}.

It only remains to show that
\Lambda_{k}\leq\Lambda
for some
\Lambda>0
and all
k\geq 0
. Let
\Lambda:=\max\{\Lambda_{0},(2C_{1}C_{4}+C_{2}^{2})^{1/2}\}
, then the same argument as in Step 2 is also valid in this setting.
Finally, estimate (
3.10
) follows by letting

.
∎

Theorem
3.1
gives the maximal Besov regularity one can expect via our variational
approach for solutions to problem (
1.4
). We recall that (
3.9
) is optimal while
(
3.10
) is suboptimal for the 1

profile
(1-x^{2})_{+}^{s}
in

. This is
due to the Hypothesis
2.1
(monotonicity) for

, which cannot be improved for a function

\rho\mapsto G(\cdot,\cdot,\rho)
convex and differentiable
[
43
, Remark 2.1]
.

One can immediately deduce Sobolev regularity by combining
Theorem

3.1
with Lemma
2.1
(embedding). Even though such a lemma is stated for the spaces
B^{\sigma+\varepsilon}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)
and

, the proof for the zero-extension spaces

and

follows by the same arguments.

Corollary 3.1

(maximal Sobolev regularity)
.

Let the assumptions of
Theorem

3.1
be satisfies. For
p\in[2,\infty)
and

(3.18)

Remark 10

(interior Sobolev regularity)
.

Estimates (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) are valid up to the boundary of the domain. The obstruction to higher regularity is due to boundary behavior. In the superquadratic case
p\geq 2
, and for the
(p,s)
-fractional Laplacian (
1.5
) –that gives rise to
\beta=1
in Hypothesis
2.1
– reference
[
15
]
derives the following higher-order interior regularity provided

:

\begin{split}\mbox{if }s\leq\frac{p-1}{p+1},&\quad\mbox{then }u\in\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}W^{s\frac{p+1}{p-1}-\varepsilon}_{p,loc}(\Omega),\\
\mbox{if }s>\frac{p-1}{p+1},&\quad\mbox{then }u\in W^{1}_{p,loc}(\Omega)\mbox{ and }\nabla u\in\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}W^{s\frac{p+1}{p}-\frac{p-1}{p}-\varepsilon}_{p,loc}(\Omega).\end{split}

We point out that if

The technique in Theorem
3.1
also allows one to derive regularity estimates in Besov spaces with a lower-order differentiability index whenever

is less regular than in such a theorem. We shall not consider this procedure, but rather prove a lower regularity pickup by using interpolation theory. For that purpose, we need the following nonlinear interpolation estimate (cf.
[
44
, Théorème I.1]
).

Proposition 3.3

(nonlinear interpolation)
.

Let
A_{0}\subset A_{1}
,
B_{0}\subset B_{1}
be Banach spaces,
U\subset A_{1}
a nonempty open set and
T\colon U\to B_{1}
be a a function that maps
A_{0}\cap U
into
B_{0}
. Let us assume that there exist constants
c_{0}
,
c_{1}
such that

\begin{split}&\|Tf\|_{B_{0}}\leq c_{0}\|f\|_{A_{0}}^{\alpha_{0}},\quad\forall f\in A_{0}\cap U,\\
&\|Tf-Tg\|_{B_{1}}\leq c_{1}\|f-g\|_{A_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}},\quad\forall f,g\in U\end{split}

for some
\alpha_{0}>0
,
\alpha_{1}\in(0,1]
. Then, if
\theta\in(0,1)
and
q\in[1,\infty]
,
T
maps
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,q}
into
(B_{0},B_{1})_{\eta,r}
, where
\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{0}}\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}
and
r=\max\{1,\frac{q}{(1-\eta)\alpha_{0}+\eta\alpha_{1}}\}
.

Corollary 3.2

(regularity pickup for rough data)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
G\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
satisfy Hypothesis
2.1
, with
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\beta\in(0,1]
, and let
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
, and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then, the solution operator

is bounded between the following spaces

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }p\geq 2\mbox{ and }f\in W^{-s+\theta\frac{\beta}{p^{\prime}}}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\ u\in\widetilde{W}^{s+\theta\frac{\beta}{p}}_{p}(\Omega);

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }1<p<2\mbox{ and }f\in W^{-s+\theta\frac{\beta}{2}}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\ u\in\widetilde{W}^{s+\theta\frac{\beta}{2}}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega).

Proof.

The proof follows by a direct application of Proposition
3.3
. For

, we combine (
3.9
) and (
2.23
), while for
1<p<2
we combine (
3.10
) and (
2.22
).
∎

3.3.
Operators with finite horizon

Thus far, we have obtained regularity estimates under Hypothesis
2.1
. In particular, that hypothesis dictates the smoothness, growth and behavior at
x=y
of
G(x,y,\rho)
. However, such global behavior constraints can be significantly relaxed to incorporate, for instance,
finite-horizon operators.
For simplicity, we now assume
G
takes the form

(3.20)

G(x,y,\rho)=\frac{1}{2p}\,\psi\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\delta}\right)\,|\rho|^{p},

where
1<p<\infty
and
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a given function.
In case
\psi
is supported in the unit interval
[0,1]
, the parameter
\delta>0
above is the horizon of the resulting operator

(3.21)

\mathcal{A}_{\delta}u(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\psi\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\delta}\right)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,dy.

Let us assume that
\psi\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
.
Then, the choice (
3.20
) trivially fulfills conditions
convexity
,
p
-growth
, and
continuity
; moreover, we note it satisfies the
symmetry
condition. The only two missing assumptions from Hypothesis
2.1
in this setting are
space continuity
and
monotonicity
. At this point, we can define the
energy norm
induced by
G
,

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}:=\langle\mathcal{A}_{\delta}v,v\rangle^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\psi\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\delta}\right)\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,dydx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and realize it satisfies

(3.22)

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq\left(\frac{\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}}{C_{d,s,p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\|v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\quad\forall v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega),

where
C_{d,s,p}
is the constant given in (
1.6
).

Some form of non-degeneracy is needed in order to have a reverse inequality to (
3.22
). We assume there exists some
r>0
such that
\psi\geq\psi_{0}>0
on the interval
[0,r]
. This implies the following property:

4’. Local monotonicity.
There exists
\alpha>0
such that for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
with
|x-y|\leq r\delta
and all
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle(G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho)-G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho^{\prime}))(\rho-\rho^{\prime})\geq\alpha|\rho-\rho^{\prime}|^{p}

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{ if }p\geq 2,

\displaystyle(G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho)-G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho^{\prime}))(\rho-\rho^{\prime})\geq\alpha|\rho-\rho^{\prime}|^{2}||\rho|+|\rho^{\prime}||^{p-2}

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{ if }1<p<2.

Lemma 3.1

(non-degeneracy)
.

Let
\psi\geq\psi_{0}>0
on the interval
[0,r]
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(d,p,s,\Omega,r,\psi_{0},\delta)
such that

(3.23)

\|v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\quad\forall v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega).

Proof.

We invoke the localization estimate of
[
27
, Lemma 7]
, which reads

\displaystyle\iint_{B_{R}(0)\times B_{R}(0)}

\displaystyle\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,dydx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{3R}{r\delta}\right)^{p(1-s)}\iint_{B_{R}(0)\times B_{R}(0)}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,\chi_{\{|x-y|\leq r\delta\}}\,dydx,

and is valid for all
R>r\delta>0
and
s\in(0,1)
. This implies

\iint_{B_{R}(0)\times B_{R}(0)}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,dydx\leq\frac{2(3R)^{p(1-s)}}{(r\delta)^{p(1-s)}\psi_{0}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{p}.

regardless of whether or not the support of
\psi
is compact.
Given
v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
, we use this bound with
R>0
sufficiently large so that
\Omega\subset B_{R}(0)
and
\mbox{dist}(\Omega,\partial B_{R}(0))\geq\frac{R}{2}
, exploit the fact that
|x-y|\geq\frac{R}{2}
for all
x\in\Omega
and
y\in B_{R}(0)^{c}
and integrate in polar coordinates to get

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{d,s,p}}{2}\iint_{B_{R}(0)\times B_{R}(0)}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,dydx

\displaystyle+C_{d,s,p}\iint_{B_{R}(0)\times B_{R}(0)^{c}}\frac{|v(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,dydx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(3R)^{p(1-s)}C_{d,s,p}}{(r\delta)^{p(1-s)}\psi_{0}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{p}+\frac{2^{sp}C_{d,s,p}\omega_{d-1}}{sp\,R^{sp}}\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p},

where
\omega_{d-1}=|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|
denotes the
(d-1)
-dimensional measure of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}=\partial B_{1}(0)
in
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. We next use the well-known Poincaré inequality

\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega,p)\|v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega),

and take
R>0
sufficiently large such that

\frac{2^{sp}C_{d,s,p}\omega_{d-1}}{sp\,R^{sp}}\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\quad\forall v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega),

to obtain a constant
C=C(d,p,s,\Omega,r,\psi_{0},\delta)
such that (
3.23
) holds.
∎

By combining (
3.22
) and (
3.23
), we deduce that the energy norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
is equivalent to the
\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
-norm. Consequently, the Dirichlet problem for the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\delta}
defined in (
3.21
) is well-posed in
\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
uniformly in
s
: if
f\in W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique
u\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
satisfying

\langle\mathcal{A}_{\delta}u,v\rangle=\langle f,v\rangle\quad\forall v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega),

and we have the stability bound
\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}
.

Another consequence of the equivalence between the energy norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
and the
\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
-norm is that the variational approach of Sections
3.1
and (
3.2
) hinges on the regularity of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{G}
and still applies in this context regardless of the support of
\psi
. We state this next.

Corollary 3.3

(operator with Hölder continuous
\psi
)
.

Let
\psi
in (
3.20
) be globally
\beta
-Hölder continuous with
\beta\in(0,1]
and satisfy
\psi\geq\psi_{0}>0
on the interval
[0,r]
for
r>0
. Then the maximal regularity estimates (
3.9
) for
2\leq p<\infty
and (
3.10
) for
1<p<2
are valid regardless of the support of
\psi
.

Remark 11

(tempered fractional Laplacians)
.

Besides being applicable to finite-horizon operators with
\beta
-Hölder continuous kernel, the previous result is valid for a family of
tempered
fractional
p
-Laplacians. Concretely, we let
\lambda=\frac{1}{\delta}>0
and
\psi(\rho)=e^{-\lambda\rho}
to obtain

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}u(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{e^{\lambda|x-y|}\,|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,dy.

In the linear setting (
p=2
), this operator arises from the study of tempered Lévy flights and has been investigated for example in
[
24
]
. For the homogeneous Dirichlet problem associated to the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}
above, the regularity estimates in Theorem
3.1
are valid with
\beta=1
.

Finite-horizon operators in practice usually involve a discontinuous function
\psi
such as the characteristic
function of
[0,r]
. This does not fit within the preceding theory but maximal Besov regularity is still valid,
at least for linear operators, provided
\psi
is Hölder in a neighborhood of the origin. We explore this next,
but before we point out that even local regularity seems excessive, an interesting question to investigate.

We need to make the following local regularity assumption to compensate for the lack of
space continuity

hypothesis of
G
:

2’. Local regularity.
There exists
r>0
such that
\psi
is of class
C^{\beta}
on the interval
[0,r]

for some
\beta\in(0,1]
, and
\psi\geq\psi_{0}>0
on
[0,r]
.

Theorem 3.2

(linear finite-horizon operator with discontinuous
\psi
)
.

Let
\psi
satisfy the previous local regularity assumption with some
\beta\in(0,1].

If
G(x,y,\rho)
is of the form (
3.20
) with
p=2
, then the
following maximal regularity holds

(3.24)

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,1}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

We resort to a perturbation argument. We proceed in several steps.

1.
Perturbation
:
Let
\widetilde{\psi}\in C^{\beta}[0,\infty)
coincide with
\psi
on
[0,r]

and rewrite the equation
\mathcal{A}_{\delta}u=f
with
u=0
in
\Omega^{c}
as

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\delta}u=f+\big(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\delta}u-\mathcal{A}_{\delta}u\big)=\widetilde{f}.

The operator
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\delta}-\mathcal{A}_{\delta}
is a convolution operator that reads as follows in terms of the function
\varphi:=\widetilde{\psi}-\psi
, which vanishes on
[0,r]
:

\mathcal{B}_{\delta}u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\Big(\frac{|z|}{\delta}\Big)\frac{u(x)-u(x-z)}{|z|^{d+2s}}dz=Ku(x)+k*u(x).

Above,
k(z)=\varphi\big(\frac{|z|}{\delta}\big)|z|^{-d-2s}
and
K=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k(z)dz<\infty
because

\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

2.
Properties of
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
:
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}:\dot{B}^{t}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)\to{B}^{t}_{2,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})

is a linear bounded operator

\|\mathcal{B}_{\delta}u\|_{\dot{B}^{t}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{t}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\forall\,0<t<1.

It suffices to examine
k*u
which, using its linear structure and Young’s inequality with

K=\|k\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, yields

\|k*u(\cdot+h)-k*u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq K\|u(\cdot+h)-u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\forall\,h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Consequently, we deduce the asserted estimate from

\frac{\|\mathcal{B}_{\delta}u(\cdot+h)-\mathcal{B}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{|h|^{t}}\leq K\frac{\|u(\cdot+h)-u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{|h|^{t}}\quad\forall\,0<t<1.

3.
Regularity of functionals
: Combining the local estimate (
3.2
) of
Proposition
3.1
(regularity of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
) for
\widetilde{f}
, the argument of
Theorem
3.1
(maximal regularity) leading to (
3.12
) and
(
3.14
) yields the following estimate for any
\sigma\in(0,\beta]
and
t\in(-s,\sigma)

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma/2}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{t}_{2,1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|f\|_{B^{t}_{2,1}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{B^{\sigma-t}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\mathcal{B}_{\delta}u\|_{B^{t}_{2,1}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{B^{\sigma-t}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)},

where we have used the definition of
\widetilde{f}
and Step 2 to obtain the last term. To be able to iterate
this estimate we observe that
\|\mathcal{B}_{\delta}u\|_{B^{t}_{2,1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{B}_{\delta}u\|_{B^{t+2\epsilon}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}
for any

\epsilon>0
and to simplify the subsequent derivation we take
\epsilon=\frac{s}{2}
so that
t+2\epsilon=t+s>0
. We thus have
\|\mathcal{B}_{\delta}u\|_{B^{t}_{2,1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|u\|_{B^{t+2\epsilon}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}

and

(3.25)

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma/2}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{t}_{2,1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|f\|_{B^{t}_{2,1}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{B^{\sigma-t}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{B^{t+s}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{B^{\sigma-t}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}.

4.
Preliminary regularity
: We now claim that
u\in\dot{B}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)
with

(3.26)

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,1}(\Omega)}.

If
s\geq\frac{\beta}{2}
this is straightforward, because of the stability bound
\|u\|_{\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{-s}(\Omega)}
and the continuity of the embedding
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)\subset\dot{B}^{\beta/2}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)
.

In the case
s<\frac{\beta}{2}
, we iterate (
3.25
). We set
\sigma_{0}:=0
and

(3.27)

\sigma_{k+1}:=\sigma_{k}+\frac{s}{2}.

In the first few iterations we could have
0<\sigma_{k}\leq-s+\frac{\beta}{2}
, in which case we set
\sigma:=2\sigma_{k+1}=s+2\sigma_{k}\in[s,\beta-s]\subset(0,\beta]
and
t:=\sigma_{k}=\frac{\sigma-s}{2}\in[0,\frac{\sigma}{2})\subset(-s,\sigma)
. This choice of parameters yields
\sigma-t=t+s=s+\sigma_{k}
, whence (
3.25
) reads

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma_{k+1}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{t}_{2,1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|f\|_{B^{t}_{2,1}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{B^{s+\sigma_{k}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{B^{s+\sigma_{k}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Additionally, continuity of the embedding
B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,1}(\Omega)\subset B^{t}_{2,1}(\Omega)
, gives

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma_{k+1}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|u\|_{B^{s+\sigma_{k}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}^{2},

and the bound
\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma_{k}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,1}(\Omega)}

valid for
k=0
implies

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma_{k+1}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,1}(\Omega)},

for as long as
\sigma_{k}\leq-s+\frac{\beta}{2}
.
Moreover, because
\sigma_{k+1}=\sigma_{k}+\frac{s}{2},
we have a regularity improvement by the fixed amount
\frac{s}{2}
in each iteration. Therefore, after a finite (but
s
-dependent) number
k_{*}
of iterations, we reach
\sigma_{k_{*}}>-s+\frac{\beta}{2}
and deduce the validity of the regularity bound (
3.26
).

5.
Final regularity
: We now assume (
3.26
) and define the new sequence

\sigma_{0}:=0,\quad\sigma_{k+1}:=\frac{\beta}{4}+\frac{\sigma_{k}}{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\sigma_{k}=\frac{\beta}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)\to\frac{\beta}{2}.

We fix
\sigma=\sigma_{k}+\frac{\beta}{2}\in(0,\beta]
and
t=-s+\frac{\beta}{2}\in(-s,\sigma)
in (
3.25
), and note that
s+\sigma/2=s+\sigma_{k+1},

\sigma-t=s+\sigma_{k},
and
t+s=\frac{\beta}{2}
, to arrive at

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma_{k+1}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,1}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{B^{s+\sigma_{k}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{B^{\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{B^{s+\sigma_{k}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}.

Using (
3.26
), we get

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma_{k+1}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\left(C_{1}\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,1}(\Omega)}+C_{2}\|u\|_{B^{s+\sigma_{k}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,1}(\Omega)},

with constants
C_{1},C_{2}
depending on
\Omega,d
and
s
. We finally proceed as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
(maximal Besov regularity) to show

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma_{k+1}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\Lambda\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,1}(\Omega)}

for a finite number
\Lambda>0
. We trivially have

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma_{0}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\Lambda_{0}\|f\|_{B^{-s+\beta/2}_{2,1}(\Omega)}

for some
\Lambda_{0}(d,s,\Omega)>0
. We define
\Lambda_{k+1}:=(C_{1}+C_{2}\Lambda_{k})^{1/2}
and realize that

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\sigma_{k+1}}_{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\Lambda_{k+1}\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{2}}_{2,1}(\Omega)}.

Since
\Lambda_{k+1}\leq\max\{\Lambda_{0},(2C_{1}+C_{2})^{1/2}\}=:\Lambda
, passing to the limit
k\to\infty
, the desired estimate (
3.24
) follows immediately.
∎

Remark 12

(truncated fractional Laplacians)
.

Estimate (
3.24
) holds whenever
\psi
is locally
\beta
-Hölder continuous at the origin. Consequently, it applies with
\beta=1
to (linear)
truncated
fractional Laplacians
[
16
]

\mathcal{A}u(x):=C(d,s,\delta)\int_{B_{\delta}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy,

for which
\psi(\rho)=\chi_{[0,1]}(\rho)
.

In the same fashion as
Corollary

3.1
, the following maximal Sobolev regularity holds for operators of the form (
3.21
) and, in particular, for linear truncated fractional Laplacians and tempered fractional
p
-Laplacians.

Corollary 3.4

(maximal Sobolev regularity)
.

Under the hypothesis of either
Corollary

3.3
for any
p\in(1,\infty)

or of
Theorem

3.2
for
p=2
,
for all
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and
q=\max\{2,p\}
there holds

\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s+\frac{\beta}{q}-\varepsilon}_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{q-p}{p-1}}\|f\|_{B^{-s+\frac{\beta}{q^{\prime}}}_{p^{\prime},1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{q-1}}.

4.
Approximation

As an application of our regularity estimates, we consider discretizations of the problem
(
1.4
) by means of the finite element method with piecewise linear continuous functions. From now on, we assume that
G
satisfies Hypothesis
2.1
.

Let
h_{0}>0
; for
h\in(0,h_{0}]
, we let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
denote a triangulation of
\Omega
, i.e.,
\mathcal{T}_{h}=\{T\}
is a partition of
\Omega
into simplices
T
of diameter
h_{T}
and
h=\max_{T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}h_{T}
.
We assume the family
\{{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\}_{h>0}
to be shape-regular, namely,

\sigma:=\sup_{h>0}\max_{T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\frac{h_{T}}{\rho_{T}}<\infty,

where
\rho_{T}
is the diameter of the largest ball contained in
T
. We take elements to be closed sets.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{h}
be the set of interior vertices of
{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
,
N
be its cardinality and
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be the standard piecewise linear Lagrangian basis, with
\varphi_{i}
associated to the node
\texttt{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}
. With this notation, the set of discrete functions is

\widetilde{\mathbb{V}}_{h}:=\left\{v:{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}\colon v\in C_{0}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}),\ v=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}\varphi_{i}\right\},

where
v
is trivially extended by zero outside
\Omega
. It is clear that
\widetilde{\mathbb{V}}_{h}\subset\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
for all
s\in(0,1),p\in(1,\infty)
. Therefore, we consider a direct finite element discretization and seek
u_{h}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{V}}_{h}
such that

(4.1)

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\widetilde{G}\left(x,y,\frac{u_{h}(x)-u_{h}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{(u_{h}(x)-u_{h}(y))(v_{h}(x)-v_{h}(y))}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy=\langle f,v_{h}\rangle

for all
v_{h}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{V}}_{h}
, where we recall that
\widetilde{G}(x,y,\rho)=G_{\rho}(x,y,\rho)/\rho
. Clearly,
u_{h}
solves (
4.1
) if and only if it is the minimizer of the restriction of the convex functional
\mathcal{F}
from (
1.1
) over the linear space
\widetilde{\mathbb{V}}_{h}
; existence of discrete solutions follows immediately. Moreover, if we take
v_{h}=u_{h}
in (
4.1
), then we immediately obtain the discrete stability bound

(4.2)

\|u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

4.1.
Localization and interpolation estimates

The seminorm
|\cdot|_{W^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}
is nonlocal and, consequently, is non-additive with respect to domain partitions. To localize it, we first define the star (or patch) of a set
A\in\Omega
by

S_{A}^{1}:=\bigcup\big\{T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\colon T\cap A\neq\emptyset\big\}.

Given
T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
, the star
S_{T}^{1}
of
T
is the first ring of
T
and the star
S_{T}^{2}
of
S_{T}^{1}

is the second ring of
T
. We have the following localization estimate, that can be proved with the same arguments as in
[
28
,
29
]
:

(4.3)

|v|_{W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\left[\int_{T}\int_{S_{T}^{1}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\;dy\;dx+C(d,\sigma)\frac{2^{p}}{sph_{T}^{sp}}\|v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(T)}\right]

for all
v\in W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
.

This localization of fractional-order seminorms implies that, in order to prove global interpolation estimates in
W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
, it suffices to produce over the set of patches
\{T\times S_{T}^{1}\}_{T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}
plus local, zero-order contributions.

We point out, however, that clearly if one wants to have a zero-extension norm on the left hand side in (
4.3
), then interactions between
\Omega
and
\Omega^{c}
must be accounted for in the right hand side. For that purpose, following
[
12
]
, we introduce the
extended
stars

\widetilde{S}_{T}^{1}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}S_{T}^{1}&\textrm{if }T\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset,\\
B_{T}&\textrm{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

where
B_{T}=B(x_{T},Ch_{T})
is the ball of center
x_{T}
and radius
Ch_{T}
, with
x_{T}
being the barycenter of
T
, and
C=C(\sigma)
a shape regularity dependent constant such that
S_{T}^{1}\subset B_{T}
. The extended second ring
\widetilde{S}_{T}^{2}
of
T
is given by

\widetilde{S}_{T}^{2}:=\bigcup\big\{\widetilde{S}_{T^{\prime}}^{1}\colon T^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}},T^{\prime}\cap S_{T}^{1}\neq\emptyset\big\}.

The localization of the
W^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
-seminorm reads
[
12
, Lemma 4.1]
:

(4.4)

\|v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}=|v|_{W^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}^{p}\leq\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\left[\int_{T}\int_{\widetilde{S}_{T}^{1}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\;dy\;dx+C(d,\sigma)\frac{2^{p}}{sph_{T}^{sp}}\|v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(T)}\right]

for all
v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
.

Our use of (
4.4
) will be restricted to
v
being an interpolation error; in such a case,
v
has vanishing means over elements and thus we can estimate the scaled
L^{p}
contributions in terms of local
W^{s}_{p}
seminorms by using Poincaré inequalities.
We consider a suitable (such as Clément or Scott-Zhang) quasi-interpolation operator,

\Pi_{h}:\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{V}_{h},

which is stable in
W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
and for which one can prove the following local approximation estimates (see, for example,
[
2
,
13
,
20
]
):

(4.5)

\begin{split}&\|v-\Pi_{h}v\|_{L^{p}(T)}\leq C\,h_{T}^{pr}|v|_{W^{r}_{p}(S_{T}^{1})}^{p},\\
&\int_{T}\int_{\widetilde{S}_{T}^{1}}\frac{|(v-\Pi_{h}v)(x)-(v-\Pi_{h}v)(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,dy\,dx\leq C\,h_{T}^{p(r-s)}|v|_{W^{r}_{p}(\widetilde{S}_{T}^{2})}^{p},\end{split}

for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
,
s\in(0,1)
,
r\in(s,2]
,
v\in W^{r}_{p}(\widetilde{S}_{T}^{2})
, where
C=C(\Omega,d,s,\sigma,right)
.

Combining (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we deduce localized interpolation error estimates.

Proposition 4.1

(localized interpolation estimates)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
r\in(s,2]
, and
\Pi_{h}:\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
be a
quasi-interpolation operator as above. If
v\in\widetilde{W}^{r}_{p}(\Omega)
, then

(4.6)

\|v-\Pi_{h}v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq C\left(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}h_{T}^{p(r-s)}|v|_{W^{r}_{p}(\widetilde{S}_{T}^{2})}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
C=C(\Omega,d,s,\sigma,right)
.

4.2.
Error estimates in
\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)

We next derive some error estimates for the finite element solutions discussed in §
4
. We borrow techniques from the finite element analysis of classical (local) quasi-linear problems. The technique presented in
[
31
]
or
[
19
, §5.3]
exploits the continuity and monotonicity of the operator, but not the fact that
u
and
u_{h}
solve respective minimization problems. We obtain enhanced rates by adapting an approach by Chow
[
18
]
for the classical
p
-Laplacian.

Theorem 4.1

(error estimates)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, assume that
G
satisfies Hypothesis
2.1
, let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\beta\in(0,1]
be as in such assumptions and let
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. Assume
f\in B^{-s+\gamma}_{p^{\prime},1}(\Omega)
, where
\gamma=\max\{\beta/p^{\prime},\beta/2\}
. Let
u
and
u_{h}
be the respective solutions of (
2.20
) and (
4.1
). Then, if
p\in(1,2]
it holds that

(4.7)

\|u-u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\inf_{v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}}\|u-v_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p/2}\lesssim h^{\frac{\beta p}{4}}|\log h|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

On the other hand, if
p\in[2,\infty)
, we have the error bound

(4.8)

\|u-u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\inf_{v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}}\|u-v_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{2/p}\lesssim h^{\frac{2\beta}{p^{2}}}|\log h|^{\frac{2}{p^{2}}}.

Proof.

For any
v\in\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
, using either (
2.17
) or (
2.16
), we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(v)-\mathcal{F}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(u+t(v-u))-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(u),v-u\rangle\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\mathcal{A}(u+t(v-u))-\mathcal{A}u,t(v-u)\rangle\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\frac{C}{p}\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{2}\left(\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}+\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{p-2},&\mbox{if }p\in(1,2],\\
\frac{\alpha}{p}\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p},&\mbox{if }p\in[2,\infty).\end{array}\right.

In addition, if we use either (
2.18
) or (
2.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(v)-\mathcal{F}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\mathcal{A}(u+t(v-u))-\mathcal{A}u,t(v-u)\rangle\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\frac{C}{p}\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p},&\mbox{if }p\in(1,2],\\
\frac{C}{p}(\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}+\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)})^{p-2}\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{2},&\mbox{if }p\in[2,\infty).\end{array}\right..

The proof now follows easily. Indeed, for any
v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}\subset\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
, we have, for
p\in(1,2]
,

\displaystyle c(\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}+\|u-u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)})^{p-2}\|u-u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{F}(u_{h})-\mathcal{F}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{F}(v_{h})-\mathcal{F}(u)\leq c\|u-v_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

By the stability estimates (
2.21
) and (
4.2
), we have

\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}+\|u-u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{-s}_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{-s+\gamma}_{p^{\prime},1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

and therefore

\|u-u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|u-v_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p/2},\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},

which proves the first inequality in (
4.7
).

Similar considerations yield, for
p\in[2,\infty)
,

\|u-u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\lesssim(\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}+\|u-v_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)})^{p-2}\|u-v_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{2},\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},

and thus the first inequality in (
4.8
) holds.

We now set
v_{h}={\Pi_{h}}u
, use the stability of
\Pi_{h}
in
\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
, the quasi-interpolation estimate (
4.6
) and the regularity bounds (
3.18
) or (
3.19
), to conclude:

\|u-u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\begin{cases}\|u-v_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p/2}\lesssim h^{\frac{\beta p}{4}-\frac{\varepsilon p}{2}}\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}},&\mbox{ if }p\in(1,2],\\
\|u-v_{h}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{2/p}\lesssim h^{\frac{2\beta}{p^{2}}-\frac{2\varepsilon}{p}}\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{p^{2}}},&\mbox{ if }p\in[2,\infty).\end{cases}

for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small. The result follows by setting
\varepsilon=|\log h|^{-1}
.
∎

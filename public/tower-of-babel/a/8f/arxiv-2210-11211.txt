Horn maps of holomorphic functions locally pseudo-conjugate on their local parabolic basin
Dimitri Le Meur

Abstract




Abstract

The lifted horn map
h
of a holomorphic function
f
with a simple parabolic point at
z_{0}
is well known to be a local conjugacy complete invariant at
z_{0}
; this is a classical result proved independently by Écalle
[
É75
]
, Voronin
[
Vor81
]
, Martinet and Ramis
[
MR83
]
. Landford and Yampolski have shown in
[
LY
]
that, if two functions
f_{1},f_{2}
with simple parabolic points at
z_{1},z_{2}
are globally conjugate on their immediate parabolic basins, with the conjugacy and its inverse continuous at
z_{1}
, resp.
z_{2}
, their horn maps must be equal as analytic ramified coverings. In this article, we introduce a notion of local conjugacy on immediate parabolic basins, and show that two functions
f_{1},f_{2}
with simple parabolic points are locally conjugate on parabolic basins (without the hypothesis of continuity) if and only if the non-lifted horn maps
\mathbf{h}_{i}
are equivalent as ramified coverings. This result is a first step to understand better invariant classes by parabolic renormalization.

Let

3.1

Attracting, repelling cylinders

Definition 3.1

Let

•

•

Remark 3.2
.

The grand orbits of

The set

belongs to

Let

The map

•

in

.

•

an injection if and only if every grand orbit of

•

a homeomorphism if and only if

(i.e. it intersects each grand orbit of

We check the continuity of

•

\Phi_{A}^{\;2}\circ(\Phi_{A}^{\;1})^{-1}(w)=-w+\tilde{\sigma}_{i}

Proposition 3.11
.

We assume that
f(z)=z+az^{2}+bz^{3}+o(z^{3})
where
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
b\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\gamma=1-\frac{b}{a^{2}}
be the iterative residue of
f
. This quantity
\gamma
is the residue at
0
of the meromorphic form
\frac{dz}{f(z)-z}+\frac{dz}{z}
.

Let
P_{A},P_{R}
be attracting, repelling petals of
f
, and
\Phi_{A}
,
\Phi_{R}
be Fatou coordinates defined on these petals. We have the following asymptotic expansion, when
z\mapsto 0
avoiding a sector containing the repelling resp. attracting axis:

\Phi_{A}(z)=\frac{-1}{az}-\gamma\log\frac{-1}{az}+\mathrm{cst}+O(z)

\Phi_{R}(z)=\frac{-1}{az}-\gamma\log\frac{1}{az}+\mathrm{cst}+O(z)

where
\log
denotes the principal branch of the logarithm.

Elements of proof.

This is a classical proposition presented in
[
Mil
]
. Nevertheless, we give some elements of proof, from a more general demonstration presented in the first chapter of
[
Ché08
]
. We will not detail the precision of the approximations. We consider only the attracting Fatou coordinate (for the repelling coordinate, the reasoning is done with the local inverse branch
f^{-1}
of
f
). We consider the two following vector fields:
\frac{f(z)-z}{dz}
,
\frac{\log(f^{\prime}(z))(f(z)-z)}{(f^{\prime}(z)-1)dz}
. The second one is well defined in a neighborood of
z=0
, via the pricipal branch of the logarithm (since
f^{\prime}(0)=1
) and the analytic continuation at
0
.

The flow associated to these vector fields will constitute approximations of the Fatou coordinate. We will not give a proof concerning the precision of the approximation. Nevertheless, let motivate the choice of these vector fields. Where
z
is close of the parabolic point, the distance between
f(z)
and
z
is insignificant with respect to the distance between
z
and the parabolic point
0
. Thus the quantity
f(z)-z
must be almost constant along an integral curve of the vector field
\frac{f(z)-z}{dz}
between
t=0
and
t=1
close to the parabolic point, which suggests that its flow is a good candidate to be an approximation of the Fatou coordinate. Note that
\frac{\log(f^{\prime}(z))}{f^{\prime}(z)-1}
is close to
1
: it is a corrective factor which enables us to precise the approximation. The introduction of this corrective term, which is an idea of Buff, is motivated by the following fact. When we perturb
f
to get a function
f_{n}
close to
f
, the new function
f_{n}
has two fixed points which are in general distinct in a neighborhood of
0
. The vector field
\frac{f_{n}(z)-z}{dz}
has for eigenvalues at these fixed points
f^{\prime}_{n}(z)-1
, so its flow admits for multiplier at these points
\exp(f^{\prime}_{n}(z)-1)
. Furthermore, the fixed points of
f_{n}
has multiplier
f_{n}^{\prime}(z)
. The corrective factor ensures that the vector field
\frac{\log(f^{\prime}(z))(f(z)-z)}{(f^{\prime}(z)-1)dz}
has for eigenvalue
\ln(f^{\prime}_{n}(z))
, so the associated flow has for multiplier
f^{\prime}_{n}(z)
at these fixed points.

The flow of a vector field is obtained by integrating the differential form associated to this vector field, which is the inverse of this vector field (the complex dimension is
1
). The anti-derivatives of the differential forms
\frac{dz}{f(z)-z}
,
\frac{f^{\prime}(z)-1}{\log(f^{\prime}(z))(f(z)-z)}
will constitute good approximations of Fatou coordinates. The expansion:

\frac{f^{\prime}(z)-1}{\log(f^{\prime}(z))(f(z)-z)}=\frac{1}{f(z)-z}+\frac{1}{z}+O(1)

suggests that an anti-derivative of the differential form
\frac{dz}{f(z)-z}+\frac{dz}{z}
will give a better approximation of
f
than an antiderivative of
\frac{dz}{f(z)-z}
.

We have:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f(z)-z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{az}\frac{1}{1+\frac{b}{a}z}+O(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{az^{2}}(1-\frac{b}{a}z)+O(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{az^{2}}-\frac{b}{a^{2}z}+O(1)

This shows that
\gamma=1-\frac{b}{a^{2}}
is the residue at
0
of the meromorphic form
\frac{dz}{f(z)-z}+\frac{dz}{z}
.

We obtain by integrating:

\displaystyle\int\frac{dz}{f(z)-z}+\frac{dz}{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{az}-\gamma\log\frac{-1}{az}+\mbox{cst}+O(z)

Where
\log
is the principal branch of the logarithm, and
z
close to
0
and avoiding a sector containing the repelling axis. This corresponds to the terms appearing in the asymptotic expansion of the attracting Fatou coordinate. ∎

4

Horn maps

In this section, we remind the elements of a standard theory, with a presentation which might be original. We suppose in this section and those that follow that the Fatou coordinates
\Phi_{A}
,
\Phi_{R}
are normalized (see definition
3.12
).

Let
P_{R}
be a repelling petal. The grand orbits of

are equal to grand orbits of
P_{R}

3.3

On the other hand, the map

Definition 4.1
.

Set

\mathbf{h}=\overline{\Phi}_{A}\circ i\circ\overline{\Psi}_{R}:\overline{\Psi}_{R}^{-1}\Big((P_{R}\cap B^{f})/f\Big)\subset\mathcal{C}_{R}\to\mathcal{C}_{A}

The horn map is said to be normalized if both attracting and repelling Fatou coordinates are normalized.

be the normalized extended attracting Fatou coordinate, and

by condition 2. Finally, define
Q_{R}^{1}:=P_{R}^{1}
and
P_{R}^{2}:=Q_{R}^{2}
to have condition 5.

This enables us to build
\phi^{\prime}:V_{2}^{0}\to P_{A}^{1}\cup Y_{R}^{1}
, the formula being the same for
\phi
with the roles of
f_{1},f_{2}
inverted:

\phi^{\prime}(z)=\begin{cases}(\Phi_{A}^{1})^{-1}\circ T_{\sigma}\circ\Phi_{A}^{2}(z)\mbox{ if $z\in P_{A}^{2}$ }\\
\Psi_{R}^{1,\mbox{ext}}\circ\widetilde{\psi}^{-1}\circ\Phi_{R}^{2}(z)\mbox{ if $z\in(P_{R}^{2})^{0}$ }\end{cases}

Notice that the biholomorphism induced by
\phi^{\prime}
from
P_{A}^{2}
to
P_{A}^{1}
turns out to be exactly the inverse of the biholomorphism induced by
\phi
from
P_{A}^{1}
to

. Thus

is a synchronous pseudo-conjugacy.
∎

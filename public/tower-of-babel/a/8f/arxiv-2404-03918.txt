Dirac cohomology, branching laws and Wallach modules
Chao-Ping Dong, Yongzhi Luan, Haojun Xu

Abstract
. The idea of using Dirac cohomology to study branching laws was initiated by Huang, Pandžić and Zhu in 2013 [ 11 ] . One of their results says that the Dirac cohomology of π \pi completely determines π | K \pi|_{K} , where π \pi is any irreducible unitarizable highest weight ( 𝔤 , K ) (\mathfrak{g},K) module. This paper aims to develop this idea for the exceptional Lie groups E 6 ​ ( − 14 ) E_{6(-14)} and E 7 ​ ( − 25 ) E_{7(-25)} : we recover the K K -spectrum of the Wallach modules from their Dirac cohomology.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a connected simple real Lie group with a maximal compact subgroup
K:=G^{\theta}
, where
\theta
is a Cartan involution of
G
. Although many results quoted below hold in a wider setting, for simplicity, we assume that the pair
(G,K)
is Hermitian symmetric. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{k}_{0}
be the Lie algebra of
G
and
K
, respectively. Then

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{k}_{0}+\mathfrak{p}_{0}

is the Cartan decomposition on the Lie algebra level, and

G=K\,\rm{exp}(\mathfrak{p}_{0})

is the Cartan decomposition on the Lie group level.
Fix a maximal torus
T
of
K
, and let
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
be its Lie algebra. Note that
T
is a maximally compact
\theta
-stable Cartan subgroup of
G
. We will drop the subscript
0
from a real Lie algebra to denote its complexification. We fix a positive root system
\Delta_{\mathfrak{k}}^{+}=\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
once for all. Choose a positive roots system
\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}
of
\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
containing
\Delta_{\mathfrak{k}}^{+}
. Let
\Delta_{n}^{+}:=\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}\setminus\Delta_{\mathfrak{k}}^{+}
and put
\Delta_{n}^{-}=-\Delta_{n}^{+}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}^{+}=\oplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{n}^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{-}=\oplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{n}^{-}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
. Then
[\mathfrak{p}_{+},\mathfrak{p}_{+}]=[\mathfrak{p}_{-},\mathfrak{p}_{-}]=0
and

\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{-}.

We denote the half sum of positive roots in
\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}
,
\Delta_{\mathfrak{k}}^{+}
, and
\Delta_{n}^{+}
by
\delta
,
\delta_{\mathfrak{k}}
, and
\delta_{n}
, respectively.

Let
E_{\mu}
be a
K
-type
, that is, a finite-dimensional irreducible
K
-module with highest weight
\mu
.
The
generalized Verma module
is the
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module defined as

(1)

N(\mu)\coloneqq\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+})}E_{\mu},

where
\mathcal{U}(\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}})
stands for the universal enveloping algebra, and
\mathfrak{p}^{+}
acts trivially on
E_{\mu}
.
Thus as
\mathfrak{k}
-modules, we have

(2)

N(\mu)\cong S(\mathfrak{p}^{-})\otimes E_{\mu}.

The
\mathfrak{k}
-module structure of the symmetric algebra
S(\mathfrak{p}^{-})
was determined by Schmid
[
17
]
.

The unique irreducible quotient of
N(\mu)
containing
E_{\mu}
is denoted by
L(\mu)
, and is called an
irreducible highest weight module
. Any irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module which is also a highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module is isomorphic to some
L(\mu)
, which has infinitesimal character
\mu+\delta
. The unitary ones among these
L(\mu)
were classified in the 1980s by Enright, Howe, Wallach
[
7
]
, and by Jakobsen
[
13
]
. It turns out that, among all the irreducible unitary highest weight modules, the (a few non-trivial)
Wallach modules
are basic ones. For instance,
E_{6(-14)}
has one Wallach module, while
E_{7(-25)}
has two. They are unipotent representations, and can not be obtained via cohomological induction.

As we shall state in Section
2
, the Dirac operator for real reductive Lie groups was introduced by Parthasarathy in 1972
[
16
]
, and it was further sharpened to be Dirac cohomology by Vogan in 1997
[
18
]
. After the verification of Vogan conjecture by Huang and Pandžić in 2002
[
8
]
, classifying the
Dirac series
(that is, irreducible unitary representations with non-zero Dirac cohomology) became an interesting open problem. As pointed out in
[
10
]
, Dirac series include all the irreducible unitary highest weight modules when
(G,K)
is Hermitian symmetric.

Recently, the Dirac series of many groups have been classified. See
[
2
]
for complex classical Lie groups, and see
[
6
]
for
GL(n,\mathbb{R})
. On the other hand, it was initiated by Huang, Pandžić and Zhu about a decade ago that Dirac cohomology can be utilized to study branching laws. One of their results, quoted as Theorem
2.3
below, says that one can use the Dirac cohomology of
L(\mu)
to recover the entire
K
-spectrum of
L(\mu)
. This result is quite surprising since among all the
K
-types of
L(\mu)
, only
a few
(namely, the spin lowest
K
-types of
L(\mu)
in the language of
[
5
]
) contribute to
H_{D}(L(\mu))
. In other words, the (finitely many) spin LKTs of
L(\mu)
completely determine the entire
K
-spectrum. This paper aims to carry out this idea for
E_{6(-14)}
and
E_{7(-25)}
. Indeed, after developing some branching results, we shall recover the
K
-spectrum of the Wallach modules from their Dirac cohomology.

The paper is organized as follows: we collect necessary preliminaries on Dirac cohomology in Section
2
. Then we develop some results on decomposing the tensor product of irreducible finite-dimensional modules in Section
3
. We investigate
E_{6(-14)}
in Section
4
and
E_{7(-25)}
in Section
5
.
Throughout this paper, we denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of non-negative integers.

2.
Preliminaries on Dirac cohomology

We keep the notations of the introduction.

2.1.
Dirac cohomology

Let
B(\cdot,\cdot)
be a nondegenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}
. For the universal enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
, and the Clifford algebra
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{p})
of
\mathfrak{p}
with respect to the bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
, the
Dirac operator
introduced by Parthasarathy
[
16
]
was defined as follows

(3)

D\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\otimes Z_{i}\,\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{p})\,,

where
\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{p}
with respect to the bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
. Note that the Dirac operator
D
is independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis
\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\}
.

We denote the spinor module of the Clifford algebra
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{p})
by
\mathcal{S}
.
For any
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
X
, the action of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{p})
on
X\otimes\mathcal{S}
is defined by

(u\otimes a)\cdot(x\otimes s)\coloneqq ux\otimes as\,,

where
u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
,
a\in\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{p})
,
x\in X
and
s\in\mathcal{S}
.
In fact, if we denote the spin double covering group of
K
by
\tilde{K}
, then
X\otimes\mathcal{S}
is a
\left(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{p}),\tilde{K}\right)
-module. See Lemma 3.2.2 of
[
9
]
for details.

Since the Dirac operator
D
is in
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{p})
, we know that
D
acts on
X\otimes\mathcal{S}
. Moreover, the action
D:\,X\otimes\mathcal{S}\longrightarrow X\otimes\mathcal{S}
is a
\tilde{K}
-module homomorphism.

Now
Dirac cohomology
of
X
, as defined by Vogan
[
18
]
, is the following
\tilde{K}
-module

(4)

H_{D}(X)\coloneqq\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D/(\mathop{\hbox{Im}}\nolimits D\cap\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D).

If
H_{D}(X)
is nonzero, the well-known Vogan conjecture says that the infinitesimal character of
X
should be determined by
H_{D}(X)
. It was confirmed by Huang and Pandžić in 2002
[
8
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
8
]
)

Let
X
be an irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module. If the Dirac cohomology
H_{D}(X)
contains a
\tilde{K}
-type
E_{\mu}
of highest weight
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, then the infinitesimal character of
X
is
\mu+\delta_{\mathfrak{k}}
.

It is interesting to note that when
X
is unitary, the
\widetilde{K}
-modules
\mathop{\hbox{Im}}\nolimits D
and
\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D
intersect trivially and
H_{D}(X)=\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D=\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D^{2}
.

Because the dimension of the parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
is an even number, we can decompose the spinor module
\mathcal{S}
as the following
\mathfrak{k}
-modules

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}^{+}\oplus\mathcal{S}^{-}\,,

where
\mathcal{S}^{+}
(
\mathcal{S}^{-}
, respectively) is the direct sum of the even (odd, respectively) power of
\mathcal{S}\simeq\bigwedge\mathfrak{p}^{+}
.
For any irreducible unitary
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
X
, we consider the following
\tilde{K}
-equivariant operator

D^{\pm}:X\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\pm}\longrightarrow X\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\mp}\,,

which comes from the restriction of
D

X\otimes\mathcal{S}^{+}\xrightarrow{D}X\otimes\mathcal{S}^{-}\xrightarrow{D}X\otimes\mathcal{S}^{+}\,.

Since
\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D\cap\mathop{\hbox{Im}}\nolimits D=\{0\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle X\otimes\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D\oplus\mathop{\hbox{Im}}\nolimits D\,,

\displaystyle X\otimes\mathcal{S}^{+}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D^{+}\oplus\mathop{\hbox{Im}}\nolimits D^{-}\,,

\displaystyle X\otimes\mathcal{S}^{-}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D^{-}\oplus\mathop{\hbox{Im}}\nolimits D^{+}\,,

and an isomorphism

D^{\pm}:\,\mathop{\hbox{Im}}\nolimits D^{\mp}\longrightarrow\mathop{\hbox{Im}}\nolimits D^{\pm}\,.

Let us define

H_{D}^{\pm}=\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D^{\pm}\,.

Then we have

H_{D}=H_{D}^{+}\oplus H_{D}^{-}\,.

2.2.
Dirac cohomology and
K
-character

Definition 2.2
.

[
11
, page 1259]

Assume the
K
-type decomposition of the admissible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
X
is

X=\bigoplus_{\lambda}\,m_{\lambda}E_{\lambda}\,.

The
K
-character
of
X
is the following formal series

(5)

\mathop{\hbox{ch}}\nolimits_{K}X=\sum_{\lambda}m_{\lambda}\mathop{\hbox{ch}}\nolimits_{K}E_{\lambda}\,,

where
\mathop{\hbox{ch}}\nolimits_{K}E_{\lambda}
is the character of the irreducible
K
-module
E_{\lambda}
.

We will often work on the
\tilde{K}
-module in the following contents, but we still use
\mathop{\hbox{ch}}\nolimits_{K}
to denote the corresponding
\tilde{K}
character.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
11
, Theorem A]

Let
N(\mu)
be the generalized Verma module defined as in (
1
), and define the irreducible highest weight module
L(\mu)
as in the introduction. Assume that
L(\mu)
is unitary. Let

(6)

H_{D}^{+}(L(\mu))=\sum_{\xi}E_{\xi},\quad H_{D}^{-}(L(\mu))=\sum_{\eta}E_{\eta},

where the summing indices
\xi
and
\eta
run over some finite subsets of the dominant
\mathfrak{k}
-weights. Then

(7)

\mathop{\hbox{ch}}\nolimits_{K}L(\mu)=\sum_{\xi}\mathop{\hbox{ch}}\nolimits_{K}N(\xi-\delta_{n})-\sum_{\eta}\mathop{\hbox{ch}}\nolimits_{K}N(\eta-\delta_{n}).

Here the summing indices
\xi
and
\eta
are the same as in (
6
).

Originally, Theorem
2.3
was stated for lowest weight modules. Yet it holds for highest weight modules as well: one just needs to change
+\delta_{n}
to be
-\delta_{n}
.

We emphasize that by Theorem
2.1
, to compute
H_{D}(L(\mu))
, one only needs to look at the finitely many spin-lowest
K
-types of
L(\mu)
. Now Theorem
2.3
says that we can get all the
K
-types of
L(\mu)
from its spin-lowest
K
-types.

3.
Tensor product of finite-dimensional irreducible modules

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
.
Fix a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then we have root system
\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
. Let
W
be the Weyl group of
\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
.
We fix a positive root system
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
. Let
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{l}\}
be the corresponding simple roots, and let
\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2},\dots,\varpi_{l}
be the corresponding fundamental weights. Let
\delta
be half sum of the positive roots.
For integers
n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{l}
, we will use
[n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{l}]
to stand for the vector
n_{1}\varpi_{1}+n_{2}\varpi_{2}+\cdots+n_{l}\varpi_{l}
. For instance,
\delta=[1,1,\dots,1]
.

Let
E_{\lambda^{\prime}}
and
E_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}
be two irreducible finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-modules with highest weights
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
, respectively. This section aims to prepare results on the decomposition of
E_{\lambda^{\prime}}\otimes E_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}
. The root systems that we adopt here are the same as those in Knapp
[
14
]
. To make the following statements concise, we introduce the notation
\widetilde{E}_{\lambda}

to stand for the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
E_{\lambda}
if
\lambda
is dominant integral with respect to
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
, and to stand for the zero module otherwise.

For an arbitrary vector
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we denote by
\{\mu\}
the unique element in
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
which is dominant for
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
and is conjugate to
\mu
under the action of
W
.
We define
t(\mu)
to be 0 if
\mu
is singular for
\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
; otherwise, there exists a unique
\sigma\in W
such that
\sigma\mu=\{\mu\}
. In this case, we define
t(\mu)
to be the sign of
\sigma
, that is,
(-1)^{l(\sigma)}
, where
l(\sigma)
stands for the length of
\sigma
. The following result is Exercise 9 of
[
12
, Section 24]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
be two dominant integral weights w.r.t.
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
.
We have that

(8)

E_{\lambda^{\prime}}\otimes E_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Pi(\lambda^{\prime})}m_{\lambda}\,t(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\delta)\,E_{\{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\delta\}-\delta}.

Now we take
\mathfrak{g}
to be
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}(2n,\mathbb{C})

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be type

. Then we have that

(a)

The tensor product

,

\widetilde{E}_{[a_{1},0,\cdots,0,a_{n-1},0]+\nu}

\varpi_{n-1}

\bullet

\varpi_{n-1}
,

for

;
-\varpi_{1}+\varpi_{n-2i+1}-\varpi_{n-1}

(c)

The tensor product

\widetilde{E}_{[a_{1},0,\cdots,0,a_{n-1},0]+\nu}
, where

runs over

\bullet

\varpi_{n}
,
-\varpi_{1}+\varpi_{n-1}
,

Proof.

We only prove case (b) for
n
odd, other cases are similar. Take
\lambda^{\prime}=[0,\cdots,0,1,0]

and
\lambda^{\prime\prime}=[a_{1},0,\cdots,0,a_{n-1},0]
. We use Lemma 3.1 to compute
E_{\lambda^{\prime}}\otimes E_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}

Firstly, note that
\lambda^{\prime}=\left(\frac{1}{2},\dots,\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}\right)
. This gives us the extreme weights

(9)

\left(\pm\frac{1}{2},\pm\frac{1}{2},\cdots,\pm\frac{1}{2}\right),

with
-\frac{1}{2}
occurring odd times. It is easy to see that there are
2^{n-1}
such weights since

\sum_{i\,\rm{odd}}\binom{n}{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\binom{n}{i}=2^{n-1}\,.

On the other hand, by the Weyl dimension formula, we compute that
\mathop{\hbox{dim}}\nolimits E_{\lambda^{\prime}}=2^{n-1}
. Thus we conclude that
\Pi(\lambda^{\prime})
consists of the above extreme weights, each occurring once.

Now let us find out those
\lambda
s in (
9
) such that
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\delta
is regular.
Indeed,
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\delta
equals

\left(n-1+a_{1}+\frac{a_{n-1}}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2},n-2+\frac{a_{n-1}}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2},\dots,1+\frac{a_{n-1}}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2},-\frac{a_{n-1}}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\right).

Since
a_{1},a_{n-1}
are non-negative integers, it follows that
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\delta
is always dominant.
Denote
\lambda
by
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n})
and denote
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\delta
by
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{n})
. For
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\delta
to be regular, we should require that

\bullet

\mu_{i}>\mu_{i+1}
for any
1\leq i\leq n-1
;

\bullet

\mu_{n-1}+\mu_{n}\neq 0
.

The first requirement excludes the cases where

\lambda_{i}=-\frac{1}{2}
while
\lambda_{i+1}=\frac{1}{2}

for any
1<i\leq n-1
. The second requirement rules out the cases where
\lambda_{n-1}=\lambda_{n}=-\frac{1}{2}
. Now the surviving
\lambda
s are exactly
\varpi_{n-1}
,
-\varpi_{1}+\varpi_{n}
,
\varpi_{n-2i}-\varpi_{n-1}
for
1\leq i<\frac{n}{2}
;
-\varpi_{1}+\varpi_{n-2i+1}-\varpi_{n-1}
for
1<i\leq\frac{n}{2}
. This finishes the proof.

Theorem 5.1
.

We have that

(14)

L(-4\zeta)|_{K}\cong\bigoplus_{f\in\mathbb{N}}E_{[0,0,0,0,0,f,-2f-12]}.

and that

L(-8\zeta)|_{K}\cong\bigoplus_{a,f\in\mathbb{N}}E_{[a,0,0,0,0,f,-4a-2f-24]}.

Proof.

The formula (
15
) is easy to deduce. We focus on (
14
).
Firstly, note that a
K
-type in the positive part can not cancel with a
K
-type in the negative part if they differ from each other in certain coordinates. This hints us to organize all the involved
K
-types according to their second, third, fourth and fifth coordinates.

For instance, Table
3
collects all the
K
-types
\mu=[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{7}]
such that
\mu_{2}=\mu_{3}=\mu_{4}=\mu_{5}=0
.
In this table, the term
(2,-1,3,9)
in the LHS of the second row stands for the
K
-types

[a+\textbf{2},0,0,0,0,f\textbf{-1},\textbf{3}-4a-2f-6n]=[a^{\prime},0,0,0,0,f^{\prime},\textbf{9}-4a^{\prime}-2f^{\prime}-6n],

where
a^{\prime}=a+2
and
f^{\prime}=f-1
, with
a,f\in\mathbb{N}
. Collecting the bolded numbers gives
(2,-1,3,9)
. Thus it completely cancels out with the RHS term
(2,0,1,9)
, which stands for the
K
-types

[a+\textbf{2},0,0,0,0,f+\textbf{0},\textbf{1}-4a-2f-6n]=[a^{\prime},0,0,0,0,f,\textbf{9}-4a^{\prime}-2f-6n],

where
a^{\prime}=a+2
, with
a,f\in\mathbb{N}
. Whenever
a
or
f
runs over some range other than
\mathbb{N}
, we will explicitly write it out.

In Table
3
, almost each LHS completely cancels out with its RHS counterpart, except for the first row and the fourth row. In the first row, after cancellation with the RHS, the LHS leaves us with

(16)

[0,0,0,0,0,f,15-2f-6n],

where
a=0,f\in\mathbb{N}
. In the fourth row, after cancellation with the LHS, the RHS leaves us with
[0,0,0,0,0,f-1,11-2f-6n]
, where
a=0,f\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that

[0,0,0,0,0,f-1,11-2f-6n]=[0,0,0,0,0,f^{\prime},9-2f^{\prime}-6n],

where
f^{\prime}=f-1
runs over
\mathbb{N}
as well. This term further cancels out with (
16
), and eventually we are left with
[0,0,0,0,0,f,15-2f]
, where
f\in\mathbb{N}
, in the LHS.

When
(\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\mu_{4},\mu_{5})
take other values, one can proceed similarly to conclude that the positive part completely cancels out with the negative part. Here we only present the
(0,0,1,0)

Finally, subtracting

leads us to (

Collapsed Ricci Limit Spaces as Non-Collapsed 𝐑𝐂𝐃 \boldsymbol{{\rm RCD}} Spaces This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Scalar and Ricci Curvature in honor of Misha Gromov on his 75th Birthday. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/Gromov.html
Shouhei HONDA

Abstract
In this short note we provide several conjectures on the regularity of measured Gromov–Hausdorff limit spaces of Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below, from the point of view of the synthetic treatment of lower bounds on Ricci curvature for metric measure spaces.

1
Convergence theory of Riemannian manifolds

Let
\big(M^{n}_{i},g_{i}\big)
be a sequence of
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifolds with the pointed Gromov–Hausdorff (pGH) convergence:

\displaystyle(M^{n}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{g_{i}},x_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X,\mathsf{d},x)

(1.1)

to some pointed proper metric space
(X,\mathsf{d},x)
, where a metric space is said to be proper if any bounded closed subset is compact.
Then the convergence theory of Riemannian manifolds states that under suitable curvature restriction:

(\star)

Establish regularity results on
(X,\mathsf{d})
.

(\star\star)

Find relationships between
M^{n}_{i}
and
X
.

It is worth pointing out that Gromov’s cerebrated precompactness theorem states that a sequence of pointed proper metric spaces
(X_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},x_{i})
has a pGH-convergent subsequence if and only if open balls
B_{R}(x_{i})
are uniformly metric doubling for any fixed
R>0
, that is, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a collection of points
y_{i,1},\ldots,y_{i,k}\in B_{R}(x_{i})
such that
B_{R}(x_{i})\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}B_{\epsilon}(y_{i,j})
holds.

In this short note we provide several conjectures related to the problem
(\star)
under lower Ricci curvature bounds. Before introducing them, let us start to discuss the case of sectional curvature shortly in order to clarify the difference from the case of Ricci curvature.

Note that metric (metric measure, respectively) spaces with a lower bound of sectional (Ricci, respectively) curvature we will discuss below satisfy this uniform metric doubling property because of the Bishop–Gromov inequality. Thus such a sequence always has a pGH-convergent subsequence, which shows us that the setting (
1.1
) appears naturally in the following settings.

2
Lower bound on sectional curvature

Let us consider (
1.1
) in the case when the sectional curvature of
\big(M^{n}_{i},g_{i}\big)
is bounded below by a constant
K\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sec}_{M^{n}_{i}}^{g_{i}}\geq K.

(2.1)

Then
(X,\mathsf{d})
is a
k
-dimensional Alexandrov space of curvature bounded below by
K
for some
k\in[0,n]\cap\mathbb{N}
, which is a direct consequence of the stability of Alexandrov spaces with respect to the pGH convergence proved in
[
13
]
. In particular nice geometric properties of
(X,\mathsf{d})
are carried from Alexandrov geometry which gives the best framework on the synthetic treatment of lower bounds on sectional curvature for metric spaces. For example any point
p
of
X
has a neighbourhood
U_{p}
of
p
which is homeomorphic to the tangent cone at
p
, which is proved in
[
13
,
46
]
(see also
[
36
]
). This gives a geometric answer to the problem
(\star)
.
The fibration theorem proved in
[
52
]
also gives a geometric answer to the problem
(\star\star)
.

3
Lower bound on Ricci curvature

Next let us consider (
1.1
) in the case:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}_{M^{n}_{i}}^{g_{i}}\geq K(n-1).

(3.1)

It is trivial that (
2.1
) implies (
3.1
).
After passing to a subsequence by
[
15
,
24
]
, with no loss of generality we can assume that the pointed measured Gromov–Hausdorff (pmGH) convergence holds:

\displaystyle\left(M^{n}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{g_{i}},x_{i},\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{g_{i}}}{\mathrm{vol}_{g_{i}}B_{1}(x_{i})}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pmGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X,\mathsf{d},x,\mathfrak{m})

(3.2)

for some Borel measure
\mathfrak{m}
on
X
.
Then
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is so-called a
Ricci limit space
.

The structure theory of Ricci limit spaces is established in
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
. For example, the Laplacian
\Delta
on
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is well-defined via the rectifiablity as a metric measure space. Moreover it is proved in
[
17
]
that the spectrums behave continuously with respect to the convergence (
3.2
) if
(X,\mathsf{d})
is compact, which confirms a conjecture raised in
[
24
]
. This gives an analytic answer to the problem
(\star\star)
.

On the geometric side, in general, a similar fibration result as in
[
52
]
is not satisfied in this setting. A counterexample can be found in
[
10
]
. However we know that if

is compact with no singular set, and (
3.2
) is non-collapsed (as explained below), then

is homeomorphic to
M^{n}_{i}
for any sufficiently large

, which is proved in
[
15
]
. This gives a geometric answer to the problem
(\star\star)
.

Let us consider the problem
(\star)
. It is proved in
[

]
that the same geometric property as in the previous section also holds for regular points if the sequence (
3.2
) is

for some

\mathcal{H}^{n}
is the

-dimensional Hausdorff measure. That is, if
\mathfrak{m}=a\mathcal{H}^{n}
, then any
n
-dimensional regular point
p
of

has a neighbourhood
U_{p}
of

which is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In connection with these observations,
it is also interesting to ask:

(\spadesuit)

When can we find other non-collapsed sequence of
k
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifolds
\big(\hat{M}^{k}_{i},\hat{g}_{i}\big)
with Ricci curvature bounded below by a constant such that

\displaystyle\left(\hat{M}^{k}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{\hat{g}_{i}},\hat{x}_{i},\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{\hat{g}_{i}}}{\mathrm{vol}_{\hat{g}_{i}}B_{1}(\hat{x}_{i})}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pmGH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X,\mathsf{d},x,\mathfrak{m})

(3.3)

) is collapsed)?

In general this question

has a negative answer. A counterexample can be found in
[

as a “metric horn” which will be discussed later. See also
[

for other examples along this direction. Let us emphasize that if (

) holds (as a non-collapsed sequence), then

.

We are now in a position to provide the first conjecture:

Conjecture 3.1
.

If
\mathfrak{m}=b\mathcal{H}^{k}
holds for some
b,k\in(0,\infty)
, then
\big(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{k}\big)
is a non-collapsed
\RCD(K(n-1),k)
space.

The synthetic condition of lower bounds on Ricci curvature for metric measure spaces,
\RCD(K,N)
condition, is explained in the next section.

Let us compare Conjecture
3.1
with the following conjecture raised in
[
15
]
:

Conjecture 3.2

(Cheeger–Colding)
.

It holds that there exists
m\in[k,n]\cap\mathbb{N}
such that for all
y\in X
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{H}^{k}(B_{r}(y))}{\mathrm{Vol}_{m}(r)}\downarrow\qquad(r\uparrow\infty)

holds, where

is the Hausdorff dimension of
(X,\mathsf{d})
and
\mathrm{Vol}_{m}(r)
denotes the volume of a ball of radius

In connection with Conjecture
3.2
, it is natural to ask:

(\heartsuit)

Is
\big(X,\mathsf{d},\mathcal{H}^{k}\big)
an
\RCD(K_{1},N_{1})
space for some
K_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and some
N_{1}\in[1,\infty]

.

Because if this question

has a positive answer for some suitable

, then Conjecture

\RCD

(\heartsuit)
has a negative answer for a metric horn. See the next section.

4
Synthetic treatment of lower bound on Ricci curvature

A triple
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is said to be a metric measure space if
(X,\mathsf{d})
is a complete separable metric space and
\mathfrak{m}
is a Borel measure on
X
with full support. The pioneer papers
[
40
,
49
,
50
]
define
\mathrm{CD}(K,N)
conditions for metric measure spaces from the point of view of optimal transportation theory (see also
[
51
]
). Roughly speaking, a metric measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is said to be a
\mathrm{CD}(K,N)
space if the Ricci curvature is bounded below by
K\in\mathbb{R}
, and the dimension is bounded above by
N\in[1,\infty]
.
After that, adding Riemannian structure to
\mathrm{CD}(K,N)
spaces, the definition of
\RCD(K,N)
spaces is introduced in
[
4
,
23
,
25
]
. Let us give an equivalent definition of
\RCD(K,N)
spaces under assuming a bit of knowledges on the Sobolev space
H^{1,2}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
.

A metic measure space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is an
\RCD(K,N)

space
for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and some
N\in[1,\infty]
if the following four conditions hold:

•

(Volume growth condition) There exist
C>1
and
x\in X
such that
\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(y))\leq Ce^{C\mathsf{d}(x,y)^{2}}
holds for any
y\in X
and any
r>0
.

•

(Riemannian structure) The Sobolev space
H^{1,2}=H^{1,2}(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is a Hilbert space. In particular for all
f_{i}\in H^{1,2}

(i=1,2)
,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f_{1},\nabla f_{2}\rangle:=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{|\nabla(f_{1}+tf_{2})|^{2}-|\nabla f_{1}|^{2}}{2t}\in L^{1}(X,\mathfrak{m})

is well-defined, where
|\nabla f_{i}|
denotes the minimal relaxed slope of
f_{i}
.

•

(Sobolev-to-Lipschitz property) Any function
f\in H^{1,2}
satisfying
|\nabla f|(y)\leq 1
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
y\in X
has
1
-Lipschitz representative.

•

(Bochner inequality) For any
f\in D(\Delta)
with
\Delta f\in H^{1,2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla f|^{2}\geq\frac{(\Delta f)^{2}}{N}+\langle\nabla\Delta f,\nabla f\rangle+K|\nabla f|^{2}

holds in the weak sense, that is,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}\Delta\phi|\nabla f|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\geq\int_{X}\phi\left(\frac{(\Delta f)^{2}}{N}+\langle\nabla\Delta f,\nabla f\rangle+K|\nabla f|^{2}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

for any
\phi\in D(\Delta)\cap L^{\infty}(X,\mathfrak{m})
with
\Delta\phi\in L^{\infty}(X,\mathfrak{m})
and
\phi\geq 0
, where

\displaystyle D(\Delta):=\bigg\{

\displaystyle f\in H^{1,2};\,\exists\,h=:\Delta f\in L^{2},

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}\,\int_{X}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\psi\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=-\int_{X}h\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m},\,\forall\,\psi\in H^{1,2}\bigg\}.

See also

\RCD(K_{1},5)
space, that is, it is non-collapsed. In particular the Bishop–Gromov inequality yields

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{5}(B_{r}(p))}{r^{5}}>0,

which contradicts (
4.4
).

Regularity on reference measure

Let
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\RCD(K,N)
space with
k=\dim(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
.
We recall some structure results on the reference measure
\mathfrak{m}
.
Based on results obtained in
[
22
,
29
,
38
,
44
]
, it is proved in
[
8
]
that the limit

\displaystyle\phi(x):=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{k}r^{k}}

(5.1)

exists in
(0,\infty)
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in X
(we denote by
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{*}
the set of all such points
x\in X
) and coincides with the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the restriction of
\mathfrak{m}
to
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{*}
with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{k}
, where
\omega_{k}=\mathcal{H}^{k}(B_{1}(0_{k}))
.
In particular we have
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(X,\mathsf{d})\geq k
because
\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathcal{R}_{k}^{*})>0
holds, which comes from the finiteness of
\phi
on
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{*}
with
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{R}_{k}^{*})>0
.
Moreover if
\mathfrak{m}=b\mathcal{H}^{l}
holds for some
b,l\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, then
l=k
holds because
\mathcal{H}^{l}
and
\mathcal{H}^{k}
are mutually absolutely continuous on
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{*}
.

Let
f
be a function defined on a Borel subset
A
of
X
.
We say that
f
is differentiable for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in A
if there exists a family of Borel subset
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
A
such that
\mathfrak{m}(A\setminus\bigcup_{i}A_{i})=0
holds and that
f|_{A_{i}}
is Lipschitz for any
i
. See
[
2
,
33
]
. Typical examples can be found in Sobolev functions, that is, we see that any
g\in H^{1,p}(U,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
for some open subset
U
of
X
and some
p\in[1,\infty)
is differentiable for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in U
via the standard telescopic argument.
4
4

4

The characteristic function
\chi_{B_{1}(0_{n})}
of
B_{1}(0_{n})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is differentiable for
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, but for all
p\in[1,\infty)
it is not in

.
Let us prove this fact for reader’s convenience.

Fix
x\in U
and find
R>0
with
B_{3R}(x)\subset U
. For any
L>1
let us define:

\displaystyle A_{L}:=\big\{y\in B_{R}(x);\,\big||\nabla g|_{p}^{p}\big|_{B_{r}(y)}\leq L^{p},\,\forall\,r\in(0,R]\big\},

where
f_{A}
is the average of
f
over
A\subset X
and
|\nabla g|_{p}
denotes the minimal (
p
-)weak upper gradient of
g
(see
[
27
]
for the independence on
p
of

). Note that
\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x)\setminus A_{L})\to 0
holds as
L\uparrow\infty
by the maximal function theorem. Thus it is enough to check:

(\clubsuit)

there exists a Borel subset
\hat{A}_{L}
of
A_{L}
such that
\mathfrak{m}\big(A_{L}\setminus\hat{A}_{L}\big)=0
holds and that
g|_{\hat{A}_{L}}
is Lipschitz.

The
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality proved in
[
48

(for more general class,
\mathrm{CD}(K,\infty)
spaces), with the volume doubling property which follows from the Bishop–Gromov inequality yields

\displaystyle\big|g-g_{B_{r}(y)}\big|_{B_{r}(y)}\leq C(K,N)Lr,\qquad\forall\,y\in A_{L},\quad\forall\,r\in(0,R].

See also
[
30
]
. In particular for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, any
r\in[0,R]
and any

, we have

\displaystyle\big|g_{B_{2^{-(i+1)}r}(y)}-g_{B_{2^{-i}r}(y)}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|g-g_{B_{2^{-i}r}(y)}\big|_{B_{2^{-(i+1)}r}(y)}

\displaystyle\leq C(K,N,R)\big|g-g_{B_{2^{-i}r}(y)}\big|_{B_{2^{-i}r}(y)}\leq C(K,N,R)2^{-i}Lr.

Thus taking the sum with respect to
i
yields that any Lebesgue point
y\in A_{L}
of
g
satisfies

\displaystyle\big|g(y)-g_{B_{r}(y)}\big|\leq C(K,N,R)Lr.

(5.2)

Since it is easy to see
|g_{B_{r}(y)}-g_{B_{r}(z)}|\leq C(K,N,R)L
for all

with
\mathsf{d}(y,z)\leq r
, (
5.2
) shows

\displaystyle|g(y)-g(z)|\leq C(K,N,R)Lr

(5.3)

for all Lebesgue points
y,z\in A_{L}
of
g
with
\mathsf{d}(y,z)\leq r
.
In particular taking

in (

) completes the proof of
(\clubsuit)
.

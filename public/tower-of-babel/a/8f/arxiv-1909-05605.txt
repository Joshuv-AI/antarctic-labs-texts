Dynamic Structures of Monomials on p p -adic Integers for Small Primes p p
Myunghyun Jung, Donggyun Kim

Abstract
We study the dynamic structures of the monomial x m x^{m} over the ring of p p -adic integers for every positive integer m m and for primes p = 2 , 3 p=2,3 and 5 5 . The dynamic structures are described by investigating minimal decompositions which consist of minimal subsystems and attracting basins.

p
-adic dynamical system; monomial; minimal decomposition

1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be the ring of
p
-adic integers and
f
a polynomial over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. The dynamical system
(\mathbb{Z}_{p},f)
has a general theorem of the minimal decomposition due to Fan and Liao in 2011
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
be a polynomial of integral coefficients with degree at least
2
. We have the following decomposition

\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\mathcal{P}\bigsqcup\mathcal{M}\bigsqcup\mathcal{B}

where

1.

\mathcal{P}
is the finite set consisting of all periodic points of
f
,

2.

\mathcal{M}=\bigsqcup_{i}\mathcal{M}_{i}
is the union of all (at most countably many) clopen invariant sets such that each
\mathcal{M}_{i}
is a finite union of balls and each subsystem
f:\mathcal{M}_{i}\to\mathcal{M}_{i}
is minimal, and

3.

each point in
\mathcal{B}
lies in the attracting basin of a periodic orbit or of a minimal subsystem.

This decomposition is usually referred to as a
minimal decomposition
and the invariant subsets
\mathcal{M}_{i}
are called
minimal components
.

Although we have a general decomposition theorem, it is still difficult to describe the explicit minimal decomposition for a given polynomial, as there have been few works done on them. Multiplications on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for
p\geq 3
were studied by Coelho and Parry in 2001
[
1
]
, and affine maps and quotient maps of affine maps for all
p
were studied by Fan, Li, Yao and Zhou in 2007
[
4
]
and by Fan, Fan, Liao and Wang in 2014
[
6
]
, respectively. The quadratic maps on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for
p=2
were investigated by Fan and Liao in 2011
[
5
]
, respectively. Recently, the Chebyshev polynomials and Fibonacci polynomials on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
were investigated by Fan and Liao in 2016
[
7
]
and by Jung, Kim and Song in 2019
[
9
]
, respectively. The monomial
x^{m}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a basic function, but it’s dynamic structures are not studied except only the case
x^{2}
for every
p
by Fan and Liao in 2016
[
8
]
.

In this paper, we study the dynamic structures of the monomial
x^{m}
over the ring of
p
-adic integers for every positive integer
m
and for primes
p=2,3
and
5
. The dynamic structures are described by investigating minimal decompositions which consist of minimal subsystems and attracting basins following the style in Theorem
1.1
.

2
Preliminary

2.1
p
-adic spaces

We first review the basic definitions and properties of the
p
-adic space.
Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers,
\mathbb{Z}
the set of integers and
p
a prime number. We consider a family of rings
\{\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}\}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
with canonical projections
\{\varphi_{ij}:\mathbb{Z}/p^{j}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/p^{i}\mathbb{Z}\}
for all integers
i
and
j
with
i\leq j
. The
ring of
p
-adic integers
, denoted
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, is the projective limit of the projective system of rings
\{\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}\}
with projections
\{\varphi_{ij}\}
.

Then the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the set of all sequence
(x_{k})
with the property
x_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
and
\varphi_{ij}(x_{j})=x_{i}
for all
i\leq j
. A point
x_{k}
in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
can be represented by
x_{k}=a_{0}+a_{1}p+a_{2}p^{2}+a_{3}p^{3}+\cdots+a_{k-1}p^{k-1}
with unique
a_{i}\in\{0,1,\cdots,p-1\}
and the point
x=(x_{k})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
by
x:a_{0},a_{0}+a_{1}p,a_{0}+a_{1}p+a_{2}p^{2},a_{0}+a_{1}p+a_{2}p^{2}+a_{3}p^{3},\cdots
as a sequence form, or simply
x=a_{0}+a_{1}p+a_{2}p^{2}+a_{3}p^{3}+\cdots
.

An element
x
can be written as
x=p^{\nu}(b_{0}+b_{1}p+b_{2}p^{2}+b_{3}p^{3}+\cdots)
with
\nu\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
b_{i}\in\{0,1,\cdots,p-1\}
. In this case, we say that
p^{\nu}

divides

x
and denote
p^{\nu}|x
.

The
p
-adic order
or
p
-adic valuation
for
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is defined as the function
\nu_{p}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\infty

\nu_{p}(x)=\begin{cases}\textrm{max}\{\nu\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}:p^{\nu}|x\}&\textrm{if}~x\neq 0,\\
\infty&\textrm{if}~x=0,\end{cases}

and the
p
-adic metric
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is defined as the function
|\cdot|_{p}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

|x|_{p}=\begin{cases}p^{-\nu_{p}(x)}&\textrm{if}~x\neq 0,\\
0&\textrm{if}~x=0.\end{cases}

With the
p
-adic metric,
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
becomes a compact space.

Note that
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
can be viewed as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}:a_{i}=0\text{ for every }i\geq k\},

and for
x=a_{0}+a_{1}p+a_{2}p^{2}+a_{3}p^{3}+\cdots
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the element
x\pmod{p^{k}}
means
a_{0}+a_{1}p+a_{2}p^{2}+a_{3}p^{3}+\cdots+a_{k-1}p^{k-1}
, which is now in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
It can be checked that
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We state properties of the
p
-adic valuation.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
x
and
y
be elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then,
\nu_{p}
has the properties:

1.

\nu_{p}(xy)=\nu_{p}(x)+\nu_{p}(y).

2.

\nu_{p}(x+y)\geq\min\{\nu_{p}(x),\nu_{p}(y)\}
, with equality when
\nu_{p}(x)
and
\nu_{p}(y)
are unequal.

2.2
Dynamics of
p
-adic polynomials

Let
f
be a polynomial over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and let
l
be a positive integer. Let
\sigma=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\}
with
x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{l}\mathbb{Z}
be a cycle of
f
of length
k
(also called a
k
-cycle), i.e.,

f(x_{1})=x_{2},\cdots,f(x_{i})=x_{i+1},\cdots,f(x_{k})=x_{1}~(\mathrm{mod}~p^{l}).

In this case, we also say that the cycle
\sigma
is
at level l
. Let

X_{\sigma}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}X_{i}~\mathrm{where}~X_{i}:=\{x_{i}+p^{l}t+p^{l+1}\mathbb{Z}:~t=0,\cdots,p-1\}.

Then

f(X_{i})\subset X_{i+1}~(1\leq i\leq k-1)~\mathrm{and}~f(X_{k})\subset X_{1},

and so
f
is invariant on the clopen set
X_{\sigma}
. The cycles in
X_{\sigma}
of
f
(mod
p^{l+1}
) are called
lifts of
\sigma
(from level
l
to level
l+1
). Note that the length of a lift of
\sigma
is a multiple of
k
.

Let
\mathbb{X}_{i}:=x_{i}+p^{l}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and let
\mathbb{X}_{\sigma}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{X}_{i}
. For
x\in\mathbb{X}_{\sigma}
, denote

\displaystyle a_{l}(x):=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}f^{\prime}\big(f^{j}(x)\big)\mathrm{~and~}

(1)

\displaystyle b_{l}(x):=\frac{f^{k}(x)-x}{p^{l}}.

(2)

Sometimes we abbreviate
a_{l}(x)
and
b_{l}(x)
to
a_{l}
and
b_{l}
, respectively. The following definition and proposition were treated by Fan et al
[
3
,
4
]
. The following definition is for
p=2
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say
\sigma

strongly grows at level l
if
a_{l}\equiv 1~(\mathrm{mod}~4)
and
b_{l}\equiv 1~(\mathrm{mod}~2)
.

We say
\sigma

strongly splits at level l
if
a_{l}\equiv 1~(\mathrm{mod}~4)
and
b_{l}\equiv 0~(\mathrm{mod}~2)
.

We say
\sigma

weakly grows at level l
if
a_{l}\equiv 3~(\mathrm{mod}~4)
and
b_{l}\equiv 1~(\mathrm{mod}~2)
.

We say
\sigma

weakly splits at level l
if
a_{l}\equiv 3~(\mathrm{mod}~4)
and
b_{l}\equiv 0~(\mathrm{mod}~2)
.

We say
\sigma

grows tails at level l
if
a_{l}\equiv 0~(\mathrm{mod}~2)
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[

]
,
[

]
)
.

Let

be a cycle of

at level

1.

If
\sigma

, then

2.

If

and

for all

, then the lifts of

Let

be a cycle of

at level

. If

strongly splits.

Let
\sigma
be a weakly splitting cycle of

at level

while the other weakly grows and then strongly splits.

If
\sigma

, then

has a
k
-periodic orbit in the clopen set

.

We say

Let

If

, then

(mod

If

is a splitting cycle at level

3
Minimal decompositions of monomials on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

From this section, we study the dynamical structure of monomials
f(x)=x^{m}
on the ring of
p
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
through the minimal decomposition which consists of periodic points, minimal components and attracting basins, see Theorem
1.1
.

When
m=1
, that is,
f(x)=x
, every point in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a fixed point, whence the minimal decomposition is trivial. Therefore from now on, we consider the cases
m\geq 2
.

For arbitrary prime number
p
, we have the following.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
be an integer and
f(x)=x^{m}
. Then,
f(x)
has a fixed point 0 in the clopen set
p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
lying in its attracting basin.

Proof.

Since
f(0)=0
,
\{0\}
is a cycle of length 1 at level 1.
Now we compute the quantity
a_{1}
for the cycle
\{0\}
, as defined in (
1
). Since
f^{\prime}(x)=mx^{m-1}
, then
a_{1}(0)=0
. Therefore,
\{0\}
grows tails at level 1 by Definition
2.2
for
p=2
and Definition
2.4
for
p\geq 3
. Then, by statement 3 of Proposition
2.5
, the statement is proved.
∎

In this section, we consider the case
p=2
. For integers
m\geq 2
, we divide into cases that
m\equiv 0\pmod{2},\ m\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
m\equiv-1\pmod{4}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f(x)=x^{m}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and assume
m\geq 2
with
m\equiv 0~(\mathrm{mod}~2)
. Then, the minimal decomposition of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for
f(x)
is

\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{0,1\}\bigsqcup\Big((2\mathbb{Z}_{2}-\{0\})\cup\big((1+2\mathbb{Z}_{2})-\{1\}\big)\Big).

Here,
\{0,1\}
is the set of fixed points and the other sets are the attracting basin of
\{0,1\}
.

Proof.

Since
m
is even, we obtain that
f(1)=1
and
a_{1}(1)=f^{\prime}(1)\equiv 0~(\mathrm{mod}~2)
by notation (
1
). So,
\{1\}
is a cycle of length 1 at level 1 which grows tails by Definition
2.2
. By property 5 of Proposition
2.3
,
f(x)
has a fixed point 1 in the clopen set
1+2\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with
1+2\mathbb{Z}_{2}
lying in its attracting basin. With Proposition
3.1
the proof is completed.
∎

Consider the case
m\equiv 1~(\mathrm{mod}~4)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
f(x)=x^{m}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and assume
m\geq 2
with
m\equiv 1~(\mathrm{mod}~4)
. Let
t=\nu_{2}(m-1)
. For every nonnegative integer
l
, there are strongly splitting 1-cycles
\{1+2^{l+2}\}
and
\{-1+2^{l+2}\}
at level
l+3
such that all the lifts at level
t+l+2
strongly grow.

Proof.

Since
m\equiv 1~(\mathrm{mod}~4)
we have
t\geq 2
. Write
m=2^{t}k+1
, then
k
is odd and
f(x)=x^{2^{t}k+1}
. We compute that
f(\pm 1)=(\pm 1)^{2^{t}k+1}=\pm 1
. So,
\pm 1
are fixed points at any level. Let
l
be any nonnegative integer and view
\{1\}
and
\{-1\}
be cycles of length 1 at level
l+2
.

We compute the quantity
a_{l+2}
, as defined in (
1
),

\displaystyle a_{l+2}(\pm 1)=f^{\prime}(\pm 1)=(2^{t}k+1)(\pm 1)^{2^{t}k}\equiv 1~(\mathrm{mod}~4),

and the quantity
b_{l+2}
, as defined in (
2
),

\displaystyle b_{l+2}(\pm 1)=\frac{f(\pm 1)-(\pm 1)}{2^{l+2}}\equiv 0~(\mathrm{mod}~2).

By Definition
2.2
, the cycles
\{1\}
and
\{-1\}
strongly split at level
l+2
.

The lifts of
\{1\}
and
\{-1\}
from level
l+2
to level
l+3
are
\{1,1+2^{l+2}\}
and
\{-1,-1+2^{l+2}\}
, respectively. The points
\pm 1+2^{l+2}
are fixed points at level
l+3
.

Now we compute the quantity
a_{l+3}
for the above points, as defined in (
1
),

\displaystyle a_{l+3}(\pm 1+2^{l+2})

\displaystyle=(2^{t}k+1)(\pm 1+2^{l+2})^{2^{t}k}

\displaystyle\equiv 1~(\mathrm{mod}~4).

We compute the quantity
b_{l+3}
for the above cycles, as defined in (
2
),

\displaystyle f(\pm 1+2^{l+2})-(\pm 1+2^{l+2})

\displaystyle=(\pm 1+2^{l+2})^{2^{t}k+1}-(\pm 1+2^{l+2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2^{t}k+1}\tbinom{2^{t}k+1}{j}(\pm 1)^{2^{t}k+1-j}(2^{l+2})^{j}-(\pm 1+2^{l+2})

\displaystyle\equiv 0~(\mathrm{mod}~2^{l+4}),

So,
b_{l+3}(\pm 1+2^{l+2})=\frac{f(1+2^{l+2})-(1+2^{l+2})}{2^{l+3}}\equiv 0~(\mathrm{mod}~2)
. Hence, the 1-cycles
\{1+2^{l+2}\}
and
\{-1+2^{l+2}\}
strongly split at level
l+3
.

If we show the following expression, by statement 2 of Proposition
2.3
, the proof is complete:

\nu_{2}\big(b_{l+3}(x)\big)=t-1\ \text{for all}\ x\in\pm 1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Suppose that
x=1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a\in 1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Then

\displaystyle f(1+

\displaystyle 2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)-(1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)

\displaystyle=(1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)^{2^{t}k+1}-(1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2^{t}k+1}\tbinom{2^{t}k+1}{j}(1+2^{l+2})^{2^{t}k+1-j}(2^{l+3}a)^{j}-(1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a).

For an integer
j\geq 2
, the summand has the valuation

\displaystyle\nu_{2}\big(\tbinom{2^{t}k+1}{j}

\displaystyle(1+2^{l+2})^{2^{t}k+1-j}(2^{l+3}a)^{j}\big)

\displaystyle\geq t+\frac{j-1}{2}-1+j(l+3)-(j-1)

\displaystyle\geq t+2l+4.

So, we obtain

\displaystyle f(1+

\displaystyle 2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)-(1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)

\displaystyle\equiv(1+2^{l+2})^{2^{t}k+1}+\tbinom{2^{t}k+1}{1}(1+2^{l+2})^{2^{t}k}(2^{l+3}a)-(1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)

\displaystyle\equiv\big((1+2^{l+2})^{2^{t}k}-1\big)(1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)

\displaystyle\equiv\big(\sum_{j=0}^{2^{t}k}\tbinom{2^{t}k}{j}(2^{l+2})^{j}-1\big)(1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)~(\mathrm{mod}~2^{t+l+3}).

For an integer
j\geq 3
, the summand has the valuation

\displaystyle\nu_{2}\big(\tbinom{2^{t}k}{j}(2^{l+2})^{j}\big)

\displaystyle\geq t+\frac{j-1}{2}-1+j(l+2)-(j-1)

\displaystyle\geq t+3l+4.

So, we obtain

\displaystyle f(1+

\displaystyle 2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)-(1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)

\displaystyle\equiv\big(\tbinom{2^{t}k}{1}2^{l+2}+\tbinom{2^{t}k}{2}(2^{l+2})^{2}\big)(1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)

\displaystyle\equiv 2^{t+l+2}~(\mathrm{mod}~2^{t+l+3}).

Hence,
\nu_{2}\big(b_{l+3}(x)\big)=\nu_{2}\big(\frac{f(1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)-(1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a)}{2^{l+3}}\big)=t-1
.

When
x=-1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}a\in-1+2^{l+2}+2^{l+3}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we can prove it similarly. Therefore the proof is completed.
∎

\displaystyle=(-1+5^{l+1}i+5^{l+2}a+5^{t+l+1}\mathbb{Z}_{5})

’s and

Proof.

1. The lifts of
\{1+5^{l+1}i,1-5^{l+1}i\}
from level
l+2
to level
t+l+1
are of the form

3.1

Consider the cases
m\equiv 2~(\mathrm{mod}~5)
and
m\equiv-2~(\mathrm{mod}~5)
. We notice from Theorem
5.3
and
5.5
that the minimal decomposition heavily depends on the values
\nu_{5}(m-1)
and
\nu_{5}(m+1)
. For the cases
m\equiv 2~(\mathrm{mod}~5)
and
m\equiv-2~(\mathrm{mod}~5)
, our computation shows that there is a similar pattern for the minimal decomposition which heavily depends on the values
\nu_{5}(m-\mathfrak{i})
and
\nu_{5}(m+\mathfrak{i})
.

When we write
\mathfrak{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}5^{i}
, the value
a_{i}
can be computed using the values
a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1}
, by Hensel’s lemma, in this way, the pattern of
a_{i}
is irregular. Hence we do not have a general formula for the values
\nu_{5}(m-\mathfrak{i})
and
\nu_{5}(m+\mathfrak{i})
and so the minimal decomposition for this case can not be proved using previous methods. We need a different method which we do not know yet.

We state our computational result as a conjecture for general
m
and give an example for a specific
m
.

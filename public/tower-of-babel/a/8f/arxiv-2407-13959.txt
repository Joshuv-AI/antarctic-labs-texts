1.
Introduction

Edge partitions of graphs have been intensively studied in recent years. Most of these studies deal with partitions of the complete graph, and concentrate on some specific property of the sub graphs that form the partition (for examples see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
8
,
11
]
). In this paper we are interested in partitions of the complete graph
K_{2d}
such that each graph of the partition is a spanning-tree (i.e. cycle-free
d
-partition of
K_{2d}
). While specific examples of such partitions are well known (see
[
9
,
10
]
), it seems that the properties of the set
\mathcal{P}^{cf}_{d}(K_{2d})
(of all cycle-free
d
-partitions of the complete graph
K_{2d}
) have not been studied.

It is well known that if
\Gamma
is a subgraph of the complete graph
K_{n}
with
|E(\Gamma)|\geq n
then
\Gamma
has a cycle. Using this fact, one can show that if
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d}
are cycle-free subgraphs of
K_{n}
such that the set of edges
E(\Gamma_{1}),\dots,E(\Gamma_{d})
form a partition for
E(K_{n})
, then
d\geq\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor
. In particular, once we fix
d
the largest
n
for which such a partition exists is
n=2d
. Moreover, in this limit case, if
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d}
is a cycle-free
d
-partition for
K_{2d}
then
|E(\Gamma_{i})|=2d-1
for each
1\leq i\leq d
, and so each
\Gamma_{i}
is a spanning-tree for
K_{2d}
. It turns out that the set of cycle-free
d
-partitions of the complete graph
K_{2d}
is naturally endowed with a collection of involutions, that play an important role in the study of a certain determinant-like map
det^{S^{2}}:V_{d}^{\otimes d(2d-1)}\to k
from
[
12
,
14
]
(see Subsection
2.2
for some details).

More precisely, using some linear algebra considerations related to the map
det^{S^{2}}
, in
[
12
]
it was shown there that for any cycle-free
d
-partition
(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
of
K_{2d}
and any three vertices
x
,
y
and
z
, there exists a unique cycle-free
d
-partition
(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})^{(x,y,z)}
which coincides with the initial partition everywhere except on at least two of the edges
(x,y)
,
(x,z)
, and
(y,z)
. Moreover, this defines an involution
(x,y,z)
on the set of cycle-free
d
-partitions
\mathcal{P}^{cf}_{d}(K_{2d})
. It follows from
[
12
]
that the map
det^{S^{2}}
is unique (up to a scalar) if and only if the action induced by the involutions
(x,y,z)
on the set of cycle-free
d
-partitions
\mathcal{P}^{cf}_{d}(K_{2d})
is transitive. This was shown to be the case for
d=3
in
[
12
]
, while the general case is still an open question.

Even thought they have a very natural combinatorial definition, as far as we know, the involutions
(x,y,z)
on
\mathcal{P}^{cf}_{d}(K_{2d})
have not been studied before their introduction in
[
12
]
.

In this paper we translate to combinatorial language some of the linear algebra results from
[
12
]
. This makes the uniqueness problem easier to state and allows us to reduce it to a simpler question, the so called twin-star hypothesis. Using this reduction we check the conjecture in the case
d=4
.
In Section
2
we recall some definitions and results from
[
12
]
. We define the cycle-free
d
-partitions of the complete graph
K_{2d}
and introduce the twin-star graph

and the graph

, which will play an important role in the paper. We also recall Lemma

, which is the main ingredient in defining the involution mentioned above. We explain how these partitions arise naturally in relation to the determinant-like map
det^{S^{2}}
.

In Section

of cycle-free

-partitions of the complete graph

In Section

is introduced. A cycle-free

-partition satisfies the hypothesis if it is weakly equivalent to one which has at least one graph isomorphic to

. We note here that this hypothesis is true for

All graphs considered in this paper are simple (i.e. no loops and no multiple edges). For a graph
\Gamma
we denote by

and

its set of vertices and edges respectively. Since our graphs are not oriented, we will not distinguish between the edge

2.2.
The map
det^{S^{2}}

The results in this subsection are not needed in this paper. We provide them only as partial motivation for studying cycle-free
d
-partitions of
K_{2d}
.

Remark 2.19
.

Let
V_{d}
be a
k
vector space over a field
k
, with
dim_{k}(V_{d})=d
. The determinant map is the unique (up to a constant) nontrivial linear map

det:V_{d}^{\otimes d}\to k,

with the property that
det(\otimes_{1\leq i\leq d}(v_{i}))=0
if there exist
1\leq x<y\leq d
such that
v_{x}=v_{y}
.

It was shown in
[
14
]
that for all
d\geq 1
there exists a nontrivial linear map

det^{S^{2}}:V_{d}^{\otimes d(2d-1)}\to k,

with the property that
det^{S^{2}}(\otimes_{1\leq i<j\leq 2d}(v_{i,j}))=0
if there exist
1\leq x<y<z\leq 2d
such that
v_{x,y}=v_{x,z}=v_{y,z}
.

It was conjectured in
[
13
]
that such a map is unique up to a constant. The conjecture is known to be true for the case
d=2
(
[
13
]
) and
d=3
(
[
12
]
). It follows from the results in
[
12
]
that the conjecture is true for
V_{d}
if and only if the action of the group
G_{d}
on
\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
is transitive.

Let’s briefly recall from
[
12
]
the connection between the map
det^{S^{2}}
and
\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
. Fix
\mathcal{B}_{d}=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{d}\}
a basis for
V_{d}
. Then, to every edge
d
-partition
(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
of
K_{2d}
we can associate a unique element

f_{(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})}=\otimes_{1\leq i<j\leq 2d}(f_{i,j})\in V_{d}^{\otimes d(2d-1)},

determined by
f_{i,j}=e_{k}
if and only if
(i,j)\in V(\Gamma_{k})
. The following result supports the conjecture about uniqueness of the map
det^{S^{2}}
.

Theorem 2.20
.

[
14
]

Let
(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
be an edge

-partition of
K_{2d}
, and

be the associated element in

. Then

.

Remark 2.21
.

Finally, it follows from results in
[
12
]
that if the conjecture is true then the map

can be written as

(2.1)

det^{S^{2}}(\otimes_{1\leq i<j\leq 2d}(v_{i,j}))=\sum_{(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})\in\mathcal{P}^{cf}_{d}(K_{2d})}\varepsilon^{S^{2}}_{d}((\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d}))\prod_{t=1}^{d}(\prod_{(i,j)\in\Gamma_{t}}v_{i,j}^{t}),

where the sum is taken over the set
\mathcal{P}^{cf}_{d}(K_{2d})
of homogeneous, cycle-free, edge
d
-partitions of the complete graph
K_{2d}
,
v_{i,j}=\sum_{t=1}^{d}v_{i,j}^{t}e_{t}
, and
\varepsilon^{S^{2}}_{d}:\mathcal{P}^{cf}_{d}(K_{2d})\to\{-1,1\}
is determined (up to a sign) by the fact that
\varepsilon^{S^{2}}_{d}((\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})^{(x,y,z)})=-\varepsilon^{S^{2}}_{d}((\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d}))
for all
1\leq x<y<z\leq 2d
. One should notice the similarity with the formula of the determinant.

Remark 2.22
.

Besides its connection to combinatorics, the map
det^{S^{2}}
has applications to geometry
[
16
]
, and to the equilibrium problem in physics
[
15
]
.

3.
Equivalence relations on the set of cycle-free
d
-partitions of
K_{2d}

In this section we state the main conjecture concerning this paper. We also introduce two equivalence relations on the set
\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
and discuss some of their properties.

3.1.
Involution Equivalence

First recall from Lemma
2.10
that the set
\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
is not empty.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
and
\mathcal{Q}=(\Theta_{1},\dots,\Theta_{d})\in\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
be two cycle-free
d
-partitions of
K_{2d}
. We say that they are
involution equivalent
if there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
, cycle-free
d
-partitions
\mathcal{P}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
for
0\leq i\leq n
, and
1\leq x_{i}<y_{i}<z_{i}\leq 2d
for
1\leq i\leq n
such that

\mathcal{P}_{0}=\mathcal{P},~~~\mathcal{P}_{n}=\mathcal{Q},~~{\rm and}~~\mathcal{P}_{i}^{(x_{i},y_{i},z_{i})}=\mathcal{P}_{i-1}~~{\rm for~~all}~~1\leq i\leq n.

The results from
[
12
,
14
]
show that the uniqueness (up to a constant) of the map
det^{S^{2}}
is equivalent with the following purely combinatorial conjecture.

Conjecture 3.2
.

Any two cycle-free
d
-partitions of
K_{2d}
are involution equivalent.

Remark 3.3
.

It is known from
[
12
]
that the conjecture is true for
d=2
and
d=3
. The main result of this paper is showing that Conjecture
3.2
is implied by the so called twin-star hypothesis. We will also check that the conjecture is true for
d=4
.

Remark 3.4
.

Recall from Definition
2.17
that the group
G_{d}
is generated by the involutions
(x,y,z):\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})\to\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
for
1\leq x<y<z\leq 2d
. This means that two cycle free
d
-partitions
\mathcal{P}=(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
and
\mathcal{Q}=(\Theta_{1},\dots,\Theta_{d})
of
K_{2d}
are involution equivalent if and only if there exists
g\in G_{d}
such that
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{Q}^{g}
. In particular, Conjecture
3.2
is equivalent with
G_{d}
acting transitively on the set
\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
.

3.2.
Weak Equivalence

Before we prove the main result of this paper we need another equivalence relation on the set
\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
.

Notice that there is an obvious action of the group
S_{2d}\times S_{d}
on the set
\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
induced by the action of the group
S_{2d}
on the vertices of the graph
K_{2d}
, and the action of the group
S_{d}
on the order of the subgraphs
\Gamma_{i}
. More precisely, we have.

Definition 3.5
.

If
\sigma\in S_{2d}
, and
(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})\in\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
we define

\sigma\circ(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})=(\sigma\circ\Gamma_{1},\dots,\sigma\circ\Gamma_{d}),

where for a subgraph
\Gamma
of
K_{2d}
with
E(\Gamma)=\{(i_{1},j_{1}),...,(i_{2d-1},j_{2d-1})\}
we take the edges of
\sigma\circ\Gamma
to be

E(\sigma\circ\Gamma)=\{(\sigma(i_{1}),\sigma(j_{1})),...,(\sigma(i_{2d-1}),\sigma(j_{2d-1}))\}
.

For
\tau\in S_{d}
we take

\tau\odot(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})=(\Gamma_{\tau^{-1}(1)},\dots,\Gamma_{\tau^{-1}(d)}).

These two actions commute with each other and so we get an action of the group
S_{2d}\times S_{d}
on
\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
.

Remark 3.6
.

One can see that this action is transitive for
d=2
. For
d=3
it was shown in
[
12
]
that there are
19
equivalence classes for the action of
S_{6}\times S_{3}
on the set
\mathcal{P}_{3}^{cf}(K_{6})
.

The above actions are compatible as follows.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
be a cycle-free
d
-partition of
K_{2d}
,
\sigma\in S_{2d}
,
\tau\in S_{d}
, and
1\leq x<y<z\leq 2d
. Then

(\sigma,\tau)\cdot((\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})^{(x,y,z)})=((\sigma,\tau)\cdot(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d}))^{(\sigma(x),\sigma(y),\sigma(z))}.

Proof.

It follows directly form the definitions.
∎

The following technical result will be used in the next section. In order to make the paper easier to read, we postpone its proof to Appendix
A
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
E_{d}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
be the cycle-free
d
-partition of
K_{2d}
introduced in Lemma
2.10
.
Then for every
(\sigma,\tau)\in S_{2d}\times S_{d}
there exists an element
g\in G_{d}
such that

(\sigma,\tau)\cdot E_{d}=(E_{d})^{g}.

In other words
(\sigma,\tau)\cdot E_{d}
is involution equivalent to
E_{d}
.

Combining the action of
S_{2d}\times S_{d}
on
\mathcal{P}_{d}^{cf}(K_{2d})
with involution equivalence we get the following definition.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
and
\mathcal{Q}=(\Theta_{1},\dots,\Theta_{d})
be two cycle-free
d
-partitions of
K_{2d}
. We say that they are
weakly equivalent
if there exist
n\geq 0
, cycle-free
d
-partitions
\mathcal{P}_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq n
, integers
1\leq x_{i}<y_{i}<z_{i}\leq 2d
for
1\leq i\leq n
, and
(\sigma_{i},\tau_{i})\in S_{2d}\times S_{d}
for
1\leq i\leq n
such that

\mathcal{P}_{0}=\mathcal{P},~~~\mathcal{P}_{n}=\mathcal{Q},~~{\rm and}~~(\sigma_{i},\tau_{i})\cdot(\mathcal{P}_{i}^{(x_{i},y_{i},z_{i})})=\mathcal{P}_{i-1}~~{\rm for~~all}~~1\leq i\leq n.

Remark 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
and
\mathcal{Q}=(\Theta_{1},\dots,\Theta_{d})
be two cycle-free
d
-partitions of
K_{2d}
. From Remark
3.4
and Lemma
3.7
we get that
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are weakly equivalent if and only if there exist
g\in G_{d}
and
(\sigma,\tau)\in S_{2d}\times S_{d}
such that

\mathcal{P}=((\sigma,\tau)\cdot\mathcal{Q})^{g}.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
and
\mathcal{Q}=(\Theta_{1},\dots,\Theta_{d})
be two cycle-free
d
-partitions of
K_{2d}
.

1) If
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are involution equivalent then they are weakly equivalent.

2) If
\mathcal{P}
is weakly equivalent to
E_{d}
then
\mathcal{P}
is involution equivalent to
E_{d}
.

Proof.

The first statement is obvious. The second statement is a consequence of Lemma
3.8
and Remark
3.10
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

Notice that if Conjecture
3.2
is true then
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are involution equivalent if and only if they are weakly equivalent.

4.
Twin-star Hypothesis

In this section we introduce the twin-star hypothesis and discuss how it relates to Conjecture
3.2
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that the twin-star hypothesis
\mathcal{TS}(d)
holds true if every cycle-free
d
-partition of
K_{2d}
is weakly equivalent to a cycle-free
d
-partition
(\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
with the property that at least one of the graphs
\Gamma_{i}
is isomorphic to the twin-star graph
TS_{d}
.

Notice that if Conjecture
3.2
is true for a certain
d
then the twin-star hypothesis is also true for that
d
. In particular, since Conjecture
3.2
is true is true for
d=2
, and
d=3
we know that
\mathcal{TS}(2)
and
\mathcal{TS}(3)
hold true. The main result of this paper is to prove the converse of this statement.

For this we need a few lemmas.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
be a cycle-free
d
-partition of
K_{2d}
such that
\Gamma_{d}
coincides with the graph
\Omega_{d}^{(d)}
. Then for each
1\leq i\leq d-1
the graph
\Gamma_{i}
has exactly one edge adjacent to the vertex
2d-1
, and exactly one edge adjacent to the vertex
2d
.

Proof.

First notice that
\Gamma_{d}=\Omega_{d}^{(d)}
has
d
edges adjacent to the vertex
2d
. So, when we remove the vertex
2d
from
K_{2d}
the cycle-free
d
-partition
(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
will induce a cycle-free
d
-partition
(\Theta_{1},\dots,\Theta_{d})
of
K_{2d-1}
such that
\Theta_{d}
has
d-1
edges. On the other hand the graph
K_{2d-1}
has
(d-1)(2d-1)
edges, so

(4.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}|E(\Theta_{i})|=|E(K_{2d-1})|-|E(\Theta_{d})|=(d-1)(2d-1)-(d-1)=(d-1)(2d-2).

Next, one can see that for each
1\leq i\leq d-1
the graph
\Theta_{i}
is cycle-free and has
2d-1
vertices, so it cannot have more than
2d-2
edges. Combining this with Equation (
4.1
) we get that

|E(\Theta_{i})|=2d-2,

for all
1\leq i\leq d-1
.

Finally, because
|E(\Gamma_{i})|=2d-1
,
E|(\Theta_{i})|=2d-2
and the graph
\Theta_{i}
is obtained from
\Gamma_{i}
by removing the vertex
2d
we get that graph
\Gamma_{i}
has exactly one edge adjacent to the vertex
2d
.

A similar argument shows that for each
1\leq i\leq d-1
the graph
\Gamma_{i}
has exactly one edge adjacent to the vertex
2d-1
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Let
(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
be a cycle-free
d
-partition of

such that
\Gamma_{d}
coincides with the graph
\Omega_{d}^{(d)}

. Moreover the set of edges

Remark 4.9
.

Notice that
I_{2d}
is the tree with the largest possible diameter in
K_{2d}
. One can easily show that the star graph
S_{2d}
cannot appear as one of the graphs
\Gamma_{i}
of a cycle-free
d
-partition
(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
of
K_{2d}
. This means that the twin-star graph
TS_{d}
has the smallest diameter among all possible graphs
\Gamma_{i}
that can appear in a cycle-free
d
-partition
(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
of
K_{2d}
. So, in a certain way, Theorem
4.8
is the opposite limit case of the twin-star hypothesis.

Remark 4.10
.

One would like to reverse the process from Theorem
4.8
. For example, start with a partition
(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{d})
such that
\Gamma_{d}
is
I_{2d}
, and use involution equivalence to get a partition where
\Gamma_{d}
is equal to the twin-star
TS_{d}
. Unfortunately, as noticed in Remark
2.15
, the definition of the involution
(x,y,z)
is not local, and so it’s not clear if this process can be done.

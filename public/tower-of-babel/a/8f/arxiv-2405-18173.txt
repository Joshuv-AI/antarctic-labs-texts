Life span of solutions to a semilinear parabolic equation on locally finite graphs
Yuanyang Hu, Mingxin Wang

Abstract




The first author was supported by NSFC Grant 12201184 and China Postdoctoral Science Foundation Grant 2022M711045, and the second author was supported by NSFC Grant 12171120.

Abstract.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a locally finite connected graph. We develop the first eigenvalue method on
G
introduced in 1963 by Kaplan
[
25
]
on Euclidean space, the discrete Phragmén-Lindelöf principle of parabolic equations and upper and lower solutions method on
G
. Using these methods, we establish the estimates and asymptotic behaviour of the life span of solutions to a semilinear heat equation with initial data
\lambda\psi(x)
for different scales of
\lambda
on
G
under some different conditions. Our results are different from the continuous case, which is related to the structure of the graph
G
.

Keywords:
Heat equations on graphs; Blow up; Lifespan; Asymptotic behaviour; Maximum principle.

AMS subject classifications (2020)
: 35A01, 35K91, 35R02, 58J35.

1
Introduction

Consider the following Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u+u^{p}\;&\text{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=\phi(x)\;\;&\text{on}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is the Laplace operator,
p>1
,
T>0
,
\phi(x)
is a nonnegative, nontrival, bounded and continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In the pioneering paper
[
14
]
, Fujita proved that the local classical solution of (
1.1
) blows up in finite time when
1<p<1+\frac{2}{N}
, while (
1.1
) admits a global classical solution when
1+\frac{2}{N}<p
and
\phi(x)
is small. Later, the nonexistence of nontrivial global solutions of (
1.1
) when
p=1+\frac{2}{N}
was proved by
[
22
,
28
]
.

It is well-known that (
1.1
) admits a unique nonnegative classical solution
\tilde{u}
, which is local. Define the life span of the solution
\tilde{u}
as

T[\phi]=\sup\left\{T>0:\,\right.(1.1)\text{ possesses~a~unique~non-negative ~classical~solution in }\left.\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)\right\}.

Lee-Ni
[
29
]
showed that, if
\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}\phi(x)>0
, then

T[\lambda\phi]<+\infty
for any
\lambda>0
and there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}

so that
C_{1}\lambda^{1-p}\leq T[\lambda\phi]\leq C_{2}\lambda^{1-p}
for sufficiently small
\lambda>0
.
Furthermore, Gui-Wang
[
20
]
proved that
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}T[\lambda\phi]\lambda^{p-1}=\frac{1}{p-1}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{-(p-1)}
, and if

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\phi(x)=\phi_{\infty}>0
, then
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}T[\lambda\phi]\lambda^{p-1}=\frac{1}{p-1}\phi_{\infty}^{-(p-1)}
.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{N}
be the
N
-dimensional hyperbolic space. Bundle-Pozio-Tesei [1] and Wang-Yin
[
38
]
studied the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u+h(t)u^{p}&\text{ in }\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=\phi(x)\geq,\not\equiv 0&\text{ on }\mathbb{H}^{N},\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}
stands for the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
,
\phi
is a bounded and continuous function on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
.
They discovered that, in order to produce a ”Fujita” phenomenon, it is necessary to take a weight function
h(t)={\rm e}^{\mu t}
in (
1.2
) compared to the problem (
1.1
), where
\mu>0
.
Let
p_{H}^{*}=1+\frac{\mu}{\lambda_{0}}
with
\lambda_{0}=\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}
. Then they proved if
1<p<p_{H}^{*}
, any nontrivial solution of the problem (
1.2
) with
h(t)={\rm e}^{\mu t}
blows up in finite time, and if
p\geq p_{H}^{*}
, the problem (
1.2
) admits global solutions for sufficiently small data with
h(t)={\rm e}^{\mu t}
. This is different from what happens in the Euclidean setting addressed by
[
14
,
22
,
28
]
.

Later, Wang-Yin
[
40
]
studied the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{t}=\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u+{\rm e}^{\mu t}u^{p}&\text{in}&\mathbb{H}^{N}\times\left(0,T[\lambda\phi]\right),\\[2.84526pt]
u(x,0)=\lambda\phi(x)&\text{on}&\mathbb{H}^{N},\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where
\lambda
is a positive parameter,
\phi\not\equiv 0
is a non-negative bounded and continuous function on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
, and
T[\lambda\phi]
is the lifespan of the solution
u
to the problem (
1.3
). They showed that
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\lambda^{p-1}T[\lambda\phi]=\frac{1}{p-1}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})}^{-(p-1)}
, if
1<p<p_{H}^{*}
, then there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
so that

C_{1}\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}\leq T[\lambda\phi]\leq C_{2}\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}\;\;\text{ as }\;\lambda\rightarrow 0\text{, }

and if
p\geq p_{H}^{*}
and
u_{0}
decays more slowly than a natural exponential function at infinity, then
\tilde{C}_{1}\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}\leq T_{\lambda}\leq\tilde{C}_{2}\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}
as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
for some constants
\tilde{C}_{1}
,
\tilde{C}_{2}>0
. From the conclusions in
[
40
]
, the life span of the solution to the problem on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
and the corresponding problem on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(see
[
20
]
) have different growth rates as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
.

In recent years, there has been a growing interest in non-existence and existence of global solutions of the Cauchy problem on miscellaneous space, such as graphs (
[
30
]
), metric measure spaces (
[
11
]
) and manifolds (
[
34
]
).

In order to make our statement more clear, we make some preliminaries firstly. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with the vertex set
V
and the edge set
E
. For
x,y\in V
, let
xy
be the edge from
x
to
y
. We write
y\sim x
if
xy\in E
. Let
\omega:V\times V\to[0,\infty)
be an edge weight function satisfying
\omega_{xy}=\omega_{yx}
for all
x,y\in V
and
\omega_{xy}>0
iff
x\sim y
. For each point
x\in V
, define its degree

{\rm deg}(x)=\#\{y\in V:\,x\sim y\},

that is, deg
(x)
is the number of the edges with endpoint
x
. A graph
G=(V,E)
is called locally finite if deg
(x)
is a finite number for each point
x\in V
. Let
\mu:V\to(0,\infty)
be a positive measure. We also write the graph
G
as a quadruple
G=G(V,E,\omega,\mu)
. Throughout this paper, unless otherwise stated, we always assume that
G=G(V,E,\omega,\mu)
is an infinite locally finite connected graph without loops or without multiple edges. Moreover, we require
\omega_{\min}=\inf_{e\in E}\omega_{e}>0
.

Lemma 1.1
.

(
[
17
, Lemma 1.4]
)
If
G=(V,E)
is a locally finite connected graph, then the set of vertices
V
is either finite or countable.

Denote the space of real-valued functions on
V
by
V^{\mathbb{R}}
.
For any
g\in V^{\mathbb{R}}
, define the integral of
g
on
V
by

\int_{V}g{\rm d}\mu=\sum_{x\in V}g(x)\mu(x)
. Given a weight and a measure, we define

\mu_{\max}=\sup_{x\in V}\mu(x),\;\;\;D_{\omega}=\frac{\mu_{\max}}{\omega_{\min}},\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;D_{\mu}=\sup_{x\in V}\frac{m(x)}{\mu(x)},

where
m(x)=\sum\limits_{y\in V:\,y\sim x}\omega_{xy},\;\,x\in V
.
Define the distance
d(x,y)
by the smallest number of edges of a path between two vertices
x
and
y
. We define balls centered at
x
with radius
r
:
B_{x}^{r}=\{y\in V:d(x,y)\leq r\}
. The volume of a subset
A\subset V
is defined as

\mathcal{V}(A)=\sum_{x\in A}\mu(x)
.
We usually write
\mathcal{V}(B_{x}^{r})
by
\mathcal{V}(x,r)
.

Consider the following Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{t}=\Delta u+u^{p}&\text{ in }&V\times(0,+\infty),\\[2.84526pt]
u(x,0)=\phi(x)\geq,\not\equiv 0&\text{ on }&V,\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

where
\phi
is a bounded function on
V
and
\Delta
is the usual graph Laplace operator on G defined by

\Delta u(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in V}\omega_{xy}(u(y)-u(x)),~x\in V.

(1.5)

Under the assumption that
G
satisfies
{CDE}^{\prime}(n,0)
(cf. Definition
2.2
) and uniform polynomial volume growth of degree

, Lin-Wu
[

]
proved that if
1<p<\frac{2}{m}+1
, then any solution of (
1.4
) blows up in finite time, and if
1+\frac{2}{m}<p
, then there exists a nonnegative global solution to (
1.4
) for a sufficiently small initial data. And most remarkably, the behaviors of the solutions for the problem (
1.4
) strongly depend on
m
and
p
.

In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem

\left\{\!\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\Delta u+u^{p}&\text{in}\;\;V\times(0,T),\\[2.84526pt]
u(x,0)=\lambda\psi(x)&\text{on}\;\;V,\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

where
G=G(V,E,\omega,\mu)
is a locally finite connected weighted graph,
\Delta
is the usual graph Laplacian defined by (
1.5
),
p>1
and
\lambda>0
are parameters,
T>0
, and

\psi:V\to[0,\infty)\;\text{is a bounded function, but not identically zero}.

(1.7)

Throughout this paper, for an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
and positive integer
n
, we define

\displaystyle C^{n}_{V}(I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f:V\times I\to\mathbb{R}:\,f(x,\cdot)\in C^{n}(I)\;\;{\rm for\;each}\;\,x\in V\},

\displaystyle L^{1}_{V}(I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f:V\times I\to\mathbb{R}:\,f(x,\cdot)\in L^{1}(I)\;\;{\rm for\;each}\;\,x\in V\}.

For a function
u:\,V\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
, when the term
u_{t}
appears, we always think that

.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u=u(x,t;\lambda\psi):\,V\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is called a solution of (
1.6
) in
[0,T)
if
u
satisfies (
1.6
),

is continuous with respect to

for any fixed

and

for any

.

By upper and lower solutions method (see Theorem

below) and Lemma

, we see that (

) admits a unique local solution

in

for some

.

We define the life span (maximum existence time) of the solution

of (

T_{\lambda}=T_{\lambda}(G,\Delta)=\text{sup}\{T>0:u(x,t;{\lambda\psi})~\text{solves}~\eqref{1.6}~\text{in}~[0,T)\}.

(1.8)

In fact,

is the blow up time of the solution

when

.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
D_{\mu}<+\infty
, then there exists
\Lambda>0
such that when
\lambda>\Lambda
,

and

\lim\limits_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{p-1}T_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{(p-1)\|\psi\|_{\ell^{\infty}{(V)}}^{p-1}}.

[

]
and
[

]
, there is no geometric conditions regarding the graph

in Theorem

.

Let
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
be the

of degree

is defined to be a connected graph with no loops, in which every vertex is adjacent to

and

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta_{T_{q}}u+u^{p},~(x,t)\in T_{q}\times(0,T),\\

2
Preliminaries

2.1
The Laplacian on graphs

Define the set of
\ell^{p}
integrable functions on
V
with respect to
the measure
\mu
by

\ell^{p}(V,\mu)=\left\{f\in V^{\mathbb{R}}:\sum_{x\in V}\mu(x)|f(x)|^{p}<\infty\right\},\;\;1\leq p<\infty,

with the norm

\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(V,\mu)}=\left(\sum_{x\in V}\mu(x)|f(x)|^{p}\right)^{1/p},\;\;\;f\in\ell^{p}(V,\mu).

For
p=\infty
, we define

\ell^{\infty}{(V)}=\left\{f\in V^{\mathbb{R}}:\sup_{x\in V}|f(x)|<\infty\right\},

with the norm

\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}{(V)}}=\sup_{x\in V}|f(x)|,\;\;\;f\in\ell^{\infty}{(V)}.

For any
H\in{V}^{\mathbb{R}}
, the graph Laplacian
\Delta
on
G
is defined by

\Delta H(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in V:\,y\sim x}\omega_{xy}(H(y)-H(x)),~x\in V.

It follows from
[
21
]
that

D_{\mu}<\infty
if and only if
\Delta
is bounded on
\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
. For
f,g\in V^{\mathbb{R}}
, the gradient form of
f
and
g
reads

\Gamma(f,g)(x)=\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in V:\,y\sim x}{\omega}_{xy}(f(y)-f(x))(g(y)-g(x)),

the iterated gradient form
\Gamma_{2}
is defined by

\Gamma_{2}(f,g)(x)=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta\Gamma(f,g)-\Gamma(f,\Delta g)-\Gamma(\Delta f,g))(x).

We will write
\Gamma(f)=\Gamma(f,f)
and
\Gamma_{2}(f)=\Gamma_{2}(f,f)
.

In this paper, we always assume that
\Omega
is a finite connected subset of
V
. We define the boundary of
\Omega
by

\partial\Omega=\{x\in\Omega:\text{there exists}\,y\in\Omega^{c}~\text{such that}~y\sim x\},

and the interior of
\Omega
by
\Omega^{\circ}=\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega
is nonempty. For any
f\in{\ell^{2}}(\Omega;\mu)
, we can extend it to a function
\tilde{f}\in{\ell^{2}}(V;\mu)
by

\tilde{f}(x)=\left\{\!\!\begin{array}[]{ll}f(x),&x\in\Omega^{\circ},\\[2.84526pt]
0,&x\notin\Omega^{\circ}.\end{array}\right.

We define the Dirichlet Laplace operator
\Delta_{\Omega}:{\ell^{2}}(\Omega;\mu)\to{\ell^{2}}(\Omega;\mu)
by
\Delta_{\Omega}f=(\Delta\tilde{f})\big|_{\Omega^{\circ}}
, i.e.,

\Delta_{\Omega}f(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in V}(\tilde{f}(y)-\tilde{f}(x))\omega_{yx}=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in V}(\tilde{f}(y)-f(x))\omega_{yx},\quad x\in\Omega^{\circ}.

By
[
9
,
17
]
, the eigenvalue problem

\left\{\!\!\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{\Omega}\phi=\lambda\phi,&x\in\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
\phi=0,&x\in\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

admits a sequence of eigenvalues
0<\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{2}(\Omega)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}(\Omega)
, where
N=\#\Omega^{\circ}
. Moreover the eigenfunction
\phi(x)
corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
could be chosen such that
\sum_{x\in\Omega}\phi(x)\mu(x)=1
and
\phi(x)>0
in

2.2

For a function

Let us recall the curvature dimension conditions introduced in

Definition 2.1

.

Let

,

and

We say that

is satisfied if

is satisfied for all

.

For convenience, we summarize some important properties of the heat kernel

For any

. Direct calculations yield that

\displaystyle w(x,t+\delta)-w(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\sum_{y\in V}P(t+\delta-s,x,y){\rm e}^{Ms}H(y,s)\mu(y){\rm d}s

(3.21)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{y\in V}[P(t+\delta-s,x,y)-P(t-s,x,y)]{\rm e}^{Ms}H(y,s)\mu(y){\rm d}s.\quad\;\;

Clearly,

for

by (

\sum_{y\in V}P(t+\delta-s,x,y){\rm e}^{Ms}H(y,s)\mu(y)

by Proposition
2.4

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\sum_{y\in V}P(t+\delta-s,x,y){\rm e}^{Ms}H(y,s)\mu(y){\rm d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in V}P(0,x,y){\rm e}^{Mt}H(y,t)\mu(y)

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{Mt}H(x,t).

Recalling the definition of the heat kernel

is continuous in

. Similar to the above,

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{y\in V}[P(t+\delta-s,x,y)-P(t-s,x,y)]{\rm e}^{Ms}H(y,s)\mu(y){\rm d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{y\in V}P_{t}(t-s,x,y){\rm e}^{Ms}H(y,s)\mu(y){\rm d}s

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{y\in V}\Delta_{x}P(t-s,x,y){\rm e}^{Ms}H(y,s)\mu(y){\rm d}s

by Proposition
2.1
(i).

(iv) and (

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in V\\
z\sim x\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in V}\left|[P(t-s,z,y)-P(t-s,x,y)]\frac{\omega_{zx}}{\mu(x)}{\rm e}^{Ms}H(y,s)\mu(y)\right|

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in V\\
z\sim x\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in V}P(t-s,z,y)\frac{\omega_{zx}}{\mu(x)}{\rm e}^{Ms}|H(y,s)|\mu(y)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in V\\

z\sim x\end{subarray}}2C_{H}\frac{\omega_{zx}}{\mu(x)}{\rm e}^{Ms}

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in V}\Delta_{x}P(t-s,x,y){\rm e}^{Ms}H(y,s)\mu(y)

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in V\\
z\sim x\end{subarray}}[P(t-s,z,y)-P(t-s,x,y)]{\rm e}^{Ms}H(y,s)\mu(y)\frac{\omega_{zx}}{\mu(x)}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in V\\
z\sim x\end{subarray}}\sum_{y\in V}[P(t-s,z,y)-P(t-s,x,y)]{\rm e}^{Ms}H(y,s)\mu(y)\frac{\omega_{zx}}{\mu(x)},

(ii)  Set

\displaystyle\underline{u}\leq\underline{u}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\underline{u}_{k}\leq\bar{u}_{k}\leq\cdots\leq\bar{u}_{1}\leq\bar{u},\;\;\forall\;k\geq 1.

\displaystyle\hat{u}(x,t)-\hat{u}(x,t_{1})

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\left[\Delta\hat{u}(x,s)-M\hat{u}(x,s)+f(x,s,\hat{u}(x,s))+M\hat{u}(x,s)\right]{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t}[\Delta\hat{u}(x,s)+f(x,s,\hat{u}(x,s)]{\rm d}s.

Step 5

Lemma 4.1

Let
\Omega\subset V
be finite and
\psi(x)\not\equiv 0

\Omega

\phi>0

Proof.

Step 1
. Let

\eta(t)=\int_{\Omega}u(\cdot,t)\phi(\cdot){\rm d}\mu=\sum_{x\in\Omega}u(x,t)\phi(x)\mu(x),~t\geq 0.

For clarity and simplicity, we denote
u_{\Omega}=u|_{\Omega}
in the following.

Step 2
. We claim that

\phi(x)\Delta u(x,t)\geq\phi(x)\Delta_{\Omega}(u_{\Omega})(x,t),~~x\in\Omega,\;t\geq 0.

In fact, since

\displaystyle u(y,t)\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\geq 0=\widetilde{u_{\Omega}}(y,t)&\text{when}~y\not\in\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
=\widetilde{u_{\Omega}}(y,t)&\text{when}~y\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

&=\phi(x)\Delta_{\Omega}u_{\Omega}(x,t).\end{split}

Step 3
. Noticing that
\Delta_{\Omega}
is self-adjoint and
\int_{\Omega}\phi{\rm d}\mu=1

\begin{split}\eta^{\prime}(t)&\geq\int_{\Omega}\phi(\cdot)\Delta_{\Omega}u_{\Omega}(\cdot,t){\rm d}\mu+\int_{\Omega}\phi(\cdot)u^{p}(\cdot,t){\rm d}\mu\\

&=-\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}u(\cdot,t)\phi(\cdot){\rm d}\mu+\int_{\Omega}u^{p}(\cdot,t)\phi(\cdot){\rm d}\mu\\
&\geq-\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}u(\cdot,t)\phi(\cdot){\rm d}\mu+\left(\int_{\Omega}u(\cdot,t)\phi(\cdot){\rm d}\mu\right)^{p}\\
&=-\lambda_{1}\eta(t)+\eta^{p}(t),~t>0.\end{split}

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{\lambda_{1}t}\left(\eta^{\prime}(t)+\lambda_{1}\eta(t)\right)

(4.6)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{\lambda_{1}t}\eta^{p}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{\lambda_{1}t}{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{1}pt}\xi^{p}(t)

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{(1-p)\lambda_{1}t}\xi^{p}(t),~t>0.

\lambda\psi(x)\geq,\not\equiv 0
on

\eta(0)=\int_{\Omega}\lambda\psi\phi{\rm d}\mu>0
,
and hence

for

) allows us to deduce that

, which implies

(4.7)

Step 4

,

Since

it is derived by (

and hence

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\eta(t)\leq\int_{\Omega}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{\ell^{\infty}(V)}\phi(x){\rm d}\mu=\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{\ell^{\infty}(V)},\;~t\geq 0,

This is a contradiction as

) holds.

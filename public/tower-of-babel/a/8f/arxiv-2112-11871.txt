1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, the symbols
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}
will stand for the sets of real and positive real numbers, respectively, and
I
will always denote a nonempty open real interval.

Given a strictly monotone continuous function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
and an
n
-tuple of positive valued functions
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}):I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
, the
n
-variable nonsymmetric generalized Bajraktarević mean

A_{f,p}:I^{n}\to I
is given by the following formula:

\displaystyle A_{f,p}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{p_{1}(x_{1})f(x_{1})+\dots+p_{n}(x_{n})f(x_{n})}{p_{1}(x_{1})+\dots+p_{n}(x_{n})}\bigg)\qquad(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in I).

(1)

This is an extension of the notion introduced by Bajraktarević in the symmetric setting in
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
which was the case when
p_{1}=\dots=p_{n}
, i.e., when all the weight functions are the same. In the sequel, the sum of these weight functions will be denoted by
p_{0}
, i.e.,
p_{0}:=p_{1}+\dots+p_{n}
. It is easy to see that
A_{f,p}
is a strict mean, i.e.,

\displaystyle\min(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\leq A_{f,p}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\leq\max(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\qquad(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in I)

holds and the inequalities are strict if
\min(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})<\max(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.

The equality problem of nonsymmetric generalized Bajraktarević means has been solved by the authors in
[
8
]
. The main goal of this paper is to investigate the local and global comparison problem of two
n
-variable nonsymmetric generalized Bajraktarević means, i.e., to find necessary as well as sufficient conditions for the functional inequality

\displaystyle A_{f,p}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\leq A_{g,q}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

(2)

where
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
are strictly monotone functions and
p,q:I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. If there exists an open set
U\subseteq I^{n}
which contains the diagonal of
I^{n}
and (
2
) holds for all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in U
, then we say that
A_{f,p}
is locally smaller than
A_{g,q}
. If (
2
) holds for all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in I^{n}
, then we say that
A_{f,p}
is globally smaller than
A_{g,q}
. Clearly, the global comparability of the means implies their local comparability.

The (global) comparison problem of classical Bajraktarević means (with arbitrary number of variables) was solved by Daróczy and Losonczi
[
5
]
. Their result was extended to the context of deviation means by Daróczy
[
4
]
and by Daróczy and Páles
[
6
]
. The so-called Gini means (cf.
[
7
]
) according to the result of Aczél and Daróczy
[
1
]
are precisely the continuous and homogeneous Bajraktarević means on
I=\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Their comparison was also characterized by Daróczy and Losonczi
[
5
]
. For the setting when
I
is a proper subinterval of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the comparison problem of Gini means was solved by Losonczi
[
9
]
and this result was extended to the setting of Gini means with complex parameters by Páles
[
11
]
. Another generalization of Bajraktarević means (in terms of a measure and two functions) was introduced and their comparison problem was investigated by Losonczi and Páles
[
10
]
. The notion of local and global comparison in broad classes of means was introduced by Páles and Zakaria
[
12
]
, where such comparison problems were in the focus of research. Motivated by theses preliminaries, in this paper we establish necessary as well as sufficient conditions both for the local and for the global comparability of nonsymmetric generalized Bajraktarević means. We note that, for nonsymmetric generalized Bajraktarević means, the equality in local and in global sense are equivalent properties. However, as we shall see, this is not the case for the comparison problem.

2.
Local comparison of Bajraktarević means

In order to investigate inequality (
2
) in the local sense, we recall the following result from
[
12
]
. In the formulation of the statements below, for an
n
variable function
\Phi:I^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, the function
\Phi^{\Delta}:I\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\displaystyle\Phi^{\Delta}(x):=\Phi(x,\dots,x)\qquad(x\in I).

Theorem 1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
M,N:I^{n}\to I
be
n
-variable means such that
M
is locally smaller than
N
. Assume that
M
and
N
are partially differentiable at each diagonal points of
I^{n}
. Then, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{i}M^{\Delta}=\partial_{i}N^{\Delta}.

(3)

If, in addition,
M
and
N
are twice differentiable at each diagonal point of
I^{n}
, then the symmetric
(n-1)\times(n-1)
-matrix

\displaystyle\big(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}N^{\Delta}-\partial_{i}\partial_{j}M^{\Delta}\big)_{i,j=1}^{n-1}

(4)

is positive semidefinite.

On the other hand, if the equality
(
3
)
holds for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, furthermore,
M
and
N
are twice continuously differentiable at each point of the diagonal of
I^{n}
and the symmetric
(n-1)\times(n-1)
-matrix given by
(
4
)
is positive definite on
I
, then
M
is locally smaller than
N
.

In order to apply this result for the nonsymmetric generalized Bajraktarević means, we need to compute their partial derivatives at the diagonal points of
I^{n}
. For this aim, we recall the following result, which was obtained by the authors in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\ell\in\{1,2\}
, let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be an
\ell
times differentiable function on
I
with a nonvanishing first derivative, and let
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}):I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. Then we have the following assertions.

(1)

If
\ell=1
,
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and
p_{i}
is continuous on
I
, then the first-order partial derivative
\partial_{i}A_{f,p}
exists on
\mathop{\hbox{\rm diag}}\nolimits(I^{n})
and

\displaystyle\partial_{i}A_{f,p}^{\Delta}=\frac{p_{i}}{p_{0}}.

(2a)

If
\ell=2
,
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and
p_{i}
is continuously differentiable on
I
, then the second-order partial derivative
\partial_{i}^{2}A_{f,p}
exists on
\mathop{\hbox{\rm diag}}\nolimits(I^{n})
and

\displaystyle\partial_{i}^{2}A_{f,p}^{\Delta}=2\frac{p_{i}^{\prime}(p_{0}-p_{i})}{p_{0}^{2}}+\frac{p_{i}(p_{0}-p_{i})}{p_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}.

(2b)

If
\ell=2
,
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
with
i\neq j
, furthermore,
p_{i}
and
p_{j}
are differentiable on
I
, then the second-order partial derivative
\partial_{i}\partial_{j}A_{f,p}
exists on
\mathop{\hbox{\rm diag}}\nolimits(I^{n})
and

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\partial_{j}A_{f,p}^{\Delta}=-\frac{(p_{i}p_{j})^{\prime}}{p_{0}^{2}}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}.

In addition, if
f
and
p
are
\ell
times continuously differentiable, then
A_{f,p}
is also
\ell
times continuously differentiable on
I^{n}
.

Our first main result establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for the local comparability of nonsymmetric generalized Bajraktarević means.

Theorem 3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
be differentiable functions with nonvanishing first derivatives and
p,q:I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
be continuous functions. Assume that
A_{f,p}
is locally smaller than
A_{g,q}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{p_{i}}{p_{0}}=\frac{q_{i}}{q_{0}}\qquad(i\in\{1,\dots,n\}).

(5)

If, in addition,
f,g
are twice differentiable and
p,q
are continuously differentiable, then the function

\displaystyle\frac{q_{0}^{2}|g^{\prime}|}{p_{0}^{2}|f^{\prime}|}

(6)

is increasing.

On the other hand, if
f,g
and
p,q
are twice continuously differentiable,
(
5
)
holds and the function
(
6
)
has a positive derivative, then
A_{f,p}
is locally smaller than
A_{g,q}
.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
2
, the differentiability of
f,g
and continuity of
p,q
implies that the partial derivatives of the means
M:=A_{f,p}
and
N:=A_{q,q}
exist at the diagonal points of
I^{n}
. Therefore, by Theorem
1
, we have that (
3
) is valid. Thus, using assertion (1) of Lemma
2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{p_{i}}{p_{0}}=\partial_{i}A_{f,p}^{\Delta}=\partial_{i}A_{g,q}^{\Delta}=\frac{q_{i}}{q_{0}}\qquad(i\in\{1,\dots,n\}),

which shows that (
5
) is valid.
In what follows, let
r_{0}
denote the ratio function
q_{0}/p_{0}
. Then, according to (
5
), we have that
q_{i}=r_{0}p_{i}
.

If, in addition,
f,g
are twice differentiable and
p,q
are continuously differentiable, then Lemma
2
implies that the second-order partial derivatives of the means
M:=A_{f,p}
and
N:=A_{q,q}
exist at the diagonal points of
I^{n}
. Therefore, by Theorem
1
, we have that the matrix given by (
4
) is positive semidefinite. This yields that

\displaystyle\partial_{i}^{2}(A_{g,q}-A_{f,p})^{\Delta}\geq 0.

(7)

Applying assertion (2a) of Lemma
2
and the equality
q_{i}=r_{0}p_{i}
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have that

\displaystyle\partial_{i}^{2}(A_{g,q}-A_{f,p})^{\Delta}

\displaystyle=2\frac{q_{i}^{\prime}(q_{0}-q_{i})}{q_{0}^{2}}+\frac{q_{i}(q_{0}-p_{i})}{q_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime}}-2\frac{p_{i}^{\prime}(p_{0}-p_{i})}{p_{0}^{2}}-\frac{p_{i}(p_{0}-p_{i})}{p_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=2\frac{(r_{0}^{\prime}p_{i}+r_{0}p_{i}^{\prime})(p_{0}-p_{i})}{r_{0}p_{0}^{2}}+\frac{p_{i}(p_{0}-p_{i})}{p_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime}}-2\frac{p_{i}^{\prime}(p_{0}-p_{i})}{p_{0}^{2}}-\frac{p_{i}(p_{0}-p_{i})}{p_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{i}(p_{0}-p_{i})}{p_{0}^{2}}\bigg(2\frac{r_{0}^{\prime}}{r_{0}}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime}}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\bigg).

The factor
\frac{p_{i}(p_{0}-p_{i})}{p_{0}^{2}}
is positive, therefore, the inequality (
7
) simplifies to

\displaystyle 0\leq 2\frac{r_{0}^{\prime}}{r_{0}}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime}}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}=\bigg(\log\bigg(\frac{q_{0}^{2}|g^{\prime}|}{p_{0}^{2}|f^{\prime}|}\bigg)\bigg)^{\prime}.

(8)

This proves that the function (
6
) is increasing.

In the last part of the proof, assume that
f,g
and
p,q
are twice continuously differentiable, (
5
) holds and the function (
6
) has a positive derivative. Then the means
M:=A_{f,p}
and
N:=A_{q,q}
are twice continuously differentiable on
I^{n}
and (
8
) is valid with strict inequality everywhere on
I
.
We show that the matrix (
4
) is positive definite. The diagonal entries of this matrix have been computed above. For the entries off the diagonal, i.e., for
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
with
i\neq j
, according assertion (2b) of Lemma
2
and the equalities
q_{i}=r_{0}p_{i}
and and
q_{j}=r_{0}p_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(A_{g,q}-A_{f,p})^{\Delta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(q_{i}q_{j})^{\prime}}{q_{0}^{2}}-\frac{q_{i}q_{j}}{q_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime}}+\frac{(p_{i}p_{j})^{\prime}}{p_{0}^{2}}+\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2r_{0}r_{0}^{\prime}p_{i}p_{j}+r_{0}^{2}(p_{i}p_{j})^{\prime}}{r_{0}^{2}p_{0}^{2}}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime}}+\frac{(p_{i}p_{j})^{\prime}}{p_{0}^{2}}+\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p_{0}^{2}}\bigg(2\frac{r_{0}^{\prime}}{r_{0}}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime}}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\bigg).

Therefore, for
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(A_{g,q}-A_{f,p})^{\Delta}=\frac{p_{i}(\delta_{i,j}p_{0}-p_{j})}{p_{0}^{2}}\bigg(2\frac{r_{0}^{\prime}}{r_{0}}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime}}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\bigg).

(Here
\delta_{i,j}
denotes to Kronecker symbol.)
In order to prove that the matrix (
4
) is positive definite, according to Sylvester’s criterion, it is necessary and sufficient that the
k\times k
-minors of this matrix should have a positive determinant.

For
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\det\Big(\big(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(A_{g,q}-A_{f,p})^{\Delta}\big)_{i,j=1}^{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=\det\Bigg(\bigg(\frac{p_{i}(\delta_{i,j}p_{0}-p_{j})}{p_{0}^{2}}\bigg(2\frac{r_{0}^{\prime}}{r_{0}}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime}}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg)_{i,j=1}^{k}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\bigg(2\frac{r_{0}^{\prime}}{r_{0}}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime}}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\bigg)^{k}\frac{(p_{1}\cdots p_{k})^{2}}{p_{0}^{2k}}\det\Bigg(\bigg(\frac{\delta_{i,j}p_{0}}{p_{j}}-1\bigg)_{i,j=1}^{k}\Bigg).

Now we compute the last determinant of the above expression:

\displaystyle\det

\displaystyle\Bigg(\bigg(\frac{\delta_{i,j}p_{0}}{p_{j}}-1\bigg)_{i,j=1}^{k}\Bigg)=\begin{vmatrix}\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{1}}-1&-1&\dots&-1&-1\\
-1&\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{2}}-1&\dots&-1&-1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
-1&-1&\dots&\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{k-1}}-1&-1\\
-1&-1&\dots&-1&\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{k}}-1\\
\end{vmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{1}}&0&\dots&0&-\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{k}}\\
0&\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{2}}&\dots&0&-\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{k}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{k-1}}&-\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{k}}\\
-1&-1&\dots&-1&\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{k}}-1\\
\end{vmatrix}=\begin{vmatrix}\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{1}}&0&\dots&0&-\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{k}}\\
0&\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{2}}&\dots&0&-\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{k}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{k-1}}&-\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{k}}\\
0&0&\dots&0&\dfrac{p_{0}}{p_{k}}\big(1-\big(\dfrac{p_{1}}{p_{0}}+\cdots+\dfrac{p_{k}}{p_{0}}\big)\big)\\
\end{vmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{0}^{k}}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}}\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{p_{1}}{p_{0}}+\cdots+\frac{p_{k}}{p_{0}}\bigg)\bigg)=\frac{p_{0}^{k-1}(p_{0}-(p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}))}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\det\Big(\big(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(A_{g,q}-A_{f,p})^{\Delta}\big)_{i,j=1}^{k}\Big)=\bigg(2\frac{r_{0}^{\prime}}{r_{0}}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime}}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\bigg)^{k}\frac{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}(p_{0}-(p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}))}{p_{0}^{k+1}}.

Here, each factor is an everywhere positive function, hence Sylvester’s criterion is validated, which then implies that the matrix (
4
) is positive definite. In view of the last assertion of Theorem
1
, it follows that
M=A_{f,p}
is locally smaller than
N=A_{q,q}
.
∎

In what follows, we consider the local comparison problem of nonsymmetric generalized Bajraktarević means when
I
is an open subinterval of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and the weight functions
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
and
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}
are proportional to power functions.

Corollary 4
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
and let
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
be differentiable functions with nonvanishing first derivatives. Let
n\geq 2
,
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}),(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and define

\displaystyle p_{i}(x):=\lambda_{i}x^{\alpha_{i}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad q_{i}(x):=\mu_{i}x^{\beta_{i}}\qquad(i\in\{1,\dots,n\},\,x\in I).

(9)

Assume that
A_{f,p}
is locally smaller than
A_{g,q}
. Then there exist
\gamma>0
and
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\mu_{i}=\gamma\lambda_{i}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i}+\delta\qquad(i\in\{1,\dots,n\}).

(10)

If, in addition,
f
and
g
are twice differentiable, then

\displaystyle x\mapsto x^{2\delta}\frac{|g^{\prime}(x)|}{|f^{\prime}(x)|}

(11)

is increasing on
I
.

On the other hand, if
f,g
are twice continuously differentiable,
(
10
)
holds and the function
(
11
)
has a positive derivative, then
A_{f,p}
is locally smaller than
A_{g,q}
.

Proof.

Assume that
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
be differentiable functions with nonvanishing first derivatives and that
A_{f,p}
is locally nonsmaller than
A_{g,q}
.
Then, by Theorem
3
, the first-order necessary condition (
5
) holds. This, for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, implies that

\displaystyle\frac{p_{i}}{p_{j}}=\frac{q_{i}}{q_{j}},

that is,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{i}x^{\alpha_{i}}}{\lambda_{j}x^{\alpha_{j}}}=\frac{\mu_{i}x^{\beta_{i}}}{\mu_{j}x^{\beta_{j}}}\qquad(x\in I).

Therefore,

\displaystyle x^{\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j}-\beta_{i}}=\frac{\mu_{i}\lambda_{j}}{\mu_{j}\lambda_{i}}\qquad(x\in I).

The left hand side is a power function which is constant on
I
, hence
\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}=\beta_{j}-\alpha_{j}
and
\mu_{i}/\lambda_{i}=\mu_{j}/\lambda_{j}
. Hence, there exist
\gamma>0
and
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
such that (
10
) holds.

If, additionally,
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
are twice differentiable functions, then by the second-order necessary condition of Theorem
3
, the function (
6
) is increasing. On the other hand, using (
5
) and then (
10
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{q_{0}^{2}|g^{\prime}|}{p_{0}^{2}|f^{\prime}|}(x)=\frac{q_{i}^{2}|g^{\prime}|}{p_{i}^{2}|f^{\prime}|}(x)=\frac{\mu_{i}^{2}x^{2\beta_{i}}|g^{\prime}(x)|}{\lambda_{i}^{2}x^{2\alpha_{i}}|f^{\prime}(x)|}=\gamma^{2}x^{2\delta}\frac{|g^{\prime}(x)|}{|f^{\prime}(x)|}.

(12)

Therefore, the function (
11
) must be increasing.

To prove the reversed statement, suppose that
f,g
are twice continuously differentiable, (
10
) holds and the function (
11
) has a positive derivative on
I
.

Then, for
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
x\in I
,

\displaystyle\frac{q_{i}}{q_{0}}(x)=\frac{\mu_{i}x^{\beta_{i}}}{\mu_{1}x^{\beta_{1}}+\dots+\mu_{n}x^{\beta_{n}}}=\frac{\gamma\lambda_{i}x^{\delta+\alpha_{i}}}{\gamma\lambda_{1}x^{\delta+\alpha_{1}}+\dots+\gamma\lambda_{n}x^{\delta+\alpha_{n}}}=\frac{\lambda_{i}x^{\alpha_{i}}}{\lambda_{1}x^{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+\lambda_{n}x^{\alpha_{n}}}=\frac{p_{i}}{p_{0}}(x),

which shows that (
5
) is valid on
I
. Furthermore, applying the identity (
12
), we can see that the function (
6
) has a positive derivative. Therefore, in view of the second part of Theorem
3
, it follows that
A_{f,p}
is locally smaller than
A_{g,q}
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of the above corollary, we can characterize the local comparison of nonsymmetric generalized power means.

Corollary 5
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Define

by

Theorem 6

Multiplying both sides of (

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{p_{i}(x_{i})(f(x_{i})-f(y))}{p_{0}(y)f^{\prime}(y)}

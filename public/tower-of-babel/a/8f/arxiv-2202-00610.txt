First-order Temporal Logic on Finite Traces Journal: TOCL Volume: 0 0 0 0 0 DOI: 0000001.0000001 CCS: Theory of computation Modal and temporal logics CCS: Computing methodologies Temporal reasoning
Alessandro Artale, Andrea Mazzullo, Ana Ozaki

Abstract
. Formalisms based on temporal logics interpreted over finite strict linear orders, known in the literature as finite traces , have been used for temporal specification in automated planning, process modelling, (runtime) verification and synthesis of programs, as well as in knowledge representation and reasoning. In this paper, we focus on first-order temporal logic on finite traces . We first investigate preservation of equivalences and satisfiability of formulas between finite and infinite traces, by providing a set of semantic and syntactic conditions to guarantee when the distinction between reasoning in the two cases can be blurred. Moreover, we show that the satisfiability problem on finite traces for several decidable fragments of first-order temporal logic is ExpSpace -complete, as in the infinite trace case, while it decreases to NExpTime when finite traces bounded in the number of instants are considered. This leads also to new complexity results for temporal description logics over finite traces. Finally, we investigate applications to planning and verification, in particular by establishing connections with the notions of insensitivity to infiniteness and safety from the literature.



Semantic Properties, Decidable Fragments, and Applications

1.
Introduction

The study of formalisms based on propositional or first-order temporal
logics on linear flows of time has found a wide spectrum of
applications, ranging from verification of programs and model checking
(
Pnueli 1977
;
Manna and Pnueli 1995
;
Baier and Katoen 2008
)
,
to automated planning
(
Bacchus and
Kabanza 1998
;
Bacchus and
Kabanza 2000
;
Baier and
McIlraith 2006
)
, process
modelling
(
van der Aalst and Pesic 2006
;
Maggi et al. 2011
)
, and knowledge
representation.
In the latter context, several decidable fragments of first-order
temporal logic with the linear time operator
until

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
,
denoted
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
, have been
investigated
(
Hodkinson et al. 2000
;
Hodkinson et al. 2003
;
Gabbay et al. 2003
)
.

Temporal description
logics
(see
(
Wolter and
Zakharyaschev 1998
;
Baader
et al. 2003b
;
Artale and
Franconi 2005
;
Artale et al. 2017
;
Lutz
et al. 2008
)

and references therein), obtained by suitably combining (linear)
temporal logic operators with
description logics
(
DLs
)
constructs, are well-known examples of such fragments.
These logics usually lie within the
two-variable monodic

fragment of
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
, denoted as

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{2}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}}
, obtained by restricting the language to
formulas having at most two variables, and so that the temporal
operators are applied only to subformulas with at most one free
variable.
For instance, by
using the reflexive temporal operators
\Diamond^{+}
, meaning
sometimes
in the future
, and
\Box^{+}
, meaning
always in the future
,
the following formula

(1)

\small\forall x\big(\mathit{Reviewer}(x)\to\Diamond^{+}\Box^{+}\forall y\big(\mathit{Submission}(y)\land\mathit{reviews}(x,y)\to\Diamond^{+}\mathit{Evaluated}(y)\big)\big)

is a
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{2}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}}
formula stating that every reviewer
will reach a (present or future) moment after which all the
submissions they review will be eventually evaluated.
Other decidable languages considered
in the literature are the
monadic
fragment

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{mo}}
and the
one-variable
fragment

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{1}}
, with formulas having, respectively, at most
unary predicates and at most one variable.
The complexity of the satisfiability problem ranges from
ExpSpace
-complete, for
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{2}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}}
,
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{mo}}
and
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{1}}

(
Gabbay et al. 2003
;
Hodkinson et al. 2003
)
,
down to
NExpTime
- or
ExpTime
-complete, for temporal extensions of the DL
\smash{\mathcal{ALC}}
without temporalised roles and with restrictions on
the application of
temporal operators
(
Lutz
et al. 2008
;
Baader
et al. 2012
)
.
Recent
work on temporal extensions of lightweight DLs in the
DL-Lite
and

\mathcal{EL}
families
(
Artale et al. 2014
;
Gutiérrez-Basulto et al. 2015
)
, used in conceptual
data modelling and underlying prominent profiles of the OWL standard,
shows that the complexity of reasoning can be even lowered down to

NP
or
NLogSpace
.

A widely studied semantics for temporal logics is defined on
structures based on the strict linear order of the natural
numbers
(
Pnueli 1977
;
Goldblatt 1987
;
Gabbay
et al. 1994
)
. However, linear temporal
structures with only a finite number of time points, often called

finite traces
, have been investigated as
well
(
Gabbay
et al. 1994
;
Cerrito
et al. 1999
)
, receiving a renewed interest in the
literature
(
De Giacomo and
Vardi 2013
;
Fionda and Greco 2016
;
Fionda and Greco 2018
)
. The finiteness
of the time dimension represents indeed a fairly natural restriction
for several applications. In automated planning, or when modelling
(business) processes with a declarative formalism, we consider finite
action plans and terminating services, often within a given temporal
bound
(
Bauer and Haslum 2010
;
De Giacomo et al. 2014b
;
De
Giacomo et al. 2014a
;
Camacho et al. 2017
)
. In
runtime verification only the current finite behaviour of the system
is taken into account, while infinite models are considered when checking
whether a given requirement is satisfied in some/all infinite
extensions of the finite trace
(
Giannakopoulou
and Havelund 2001
;
Bauer
et al. 2010
)
.
These
needs from critical applications of
temporal logics can be reflected by a semantics based on finite
traces, with formulas having different satisfaction conditions
compared to the infinite case.
For instance, by using the formula
last
to refer to the last time
point of a finite trace, we have that Formula (
1
) above is
equivalent
on finite traces to

(2)

\small\forall x\big(\mathit{Reviewer}(x)\to\Diamond^{+}\big(\textit{last}\land\forall y\big(\mathit{Submission}(y)\land\mathit{reviews}(x,y)\to\mathit{Evaluated}(y)\big)\big)\big),

stating that every reviewer will eventually reach a “deadline”, represented by the
last
formula, when all the submission they review are
evaluated.
This follows from the fact that, on finite traces, formulas
\Diamond^{+}\Box^{+}\varphi
and
\Diamond^{+}(\textit{last}\land\varphi)
are equivalent
(
De Giacomo and
Vardi 2013
)
, and
\textit{last}\land\forall x(\varphi\to\Diamond^{+}\psi)
is in turn equivalent to
\textit{last}\land\forall x(\varphi\to\psi)
.

This work focuses on first-order temporal logic on finite traces,
defined by extending the first-order language with linear time
operators interpreted on finite traces.
Part of the results contained in the current paper, establishing
bridges between finite and infinite traces semantics, have partially
been presented in
(
Artale
et al. 2018
;
Artale
et al. 2019b
;
Artale
et al. 2020
)
.
We
comment on the main results presented in this paper while illustrating
its
structure.
Section
2
is
devoted to a discussion of related work in this area. Then, after
introducing in Section
3
the required preliminary notions
about first-order (linear) temporal logic, we provide the following
main contributions.
In Section
4
, we focus on bridging reasoning on finite
and infinite traces semantics. In general, indeed, the sets of
formulas equivalent on finite and on infinite traces do not coincide,
as witnessed by the following examples: the formula

\Diamond^{+}\Box\bot
, forcing the existence of a (present or
future) last instant of time, is equivalent to
\top
only on finite
traces, whereas
\Box^{+}\Diamond\top
, stating that there is always
a future instant after the current one, is equivalent to
\top
on
infinite, but not on finite, traces. We establish here semantic and
syntactic conditions to guarantee that
two formulas equivalent on finite (respectively, infinite) traces are
also equivalent on infinite (respectively, finite) traces.
We also devise a semantic criterion and a syntactically defined class
of formulas to guarantee preservation of satisfiability from finite to
infinite traces.
In Section
5
, we
study the complexity of reasoning over finite traces for the

two-variable monodic
fragment
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{2}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}}
,
the
monadic
fragment
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{mo}}
and the

one-variable
fragment
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{1}}
, showing that the
complexity
remains
ExpSpace
-complete on finite traces, but lowers down to

NExpTime
if we restrict to traces with a bound
k
(given in
binary) on the number of instants. Moreover, we show that these
fragments enjoy two kinds of
bounded model properties
:
the
bounded trace property
, which limits the finite traces
satisfying a formula to be at most double exponential in the size of
the input formula, and the
bounded domain property
, which
limits the number of domain elements in a
k
-bounded trace (finite
trace with at most
k
instants) to be at most double exponential both
in the size of the input formula and in the (binary representation) of

k
.
In
the context of temporal DLs, we show
that complexity results similar to the infinite trace case, as well as
bounded model properties, also hold for the temporal DL
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
when
interpreted on finite traces. We further show a more challenging
result, i.e., that the complexity further reduces to
ExpTime
if
only global TBox
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
axioms together with a temporal ABox are
interpreted on
k
-bounded traces.
Finally, in Section
6
, we investigate connections with
the planning and verification literature.
Concerning
the former scenario, we study in our setting the notion of

insensitivity to infiniteness

(
De
Giacomo et al. 2014a
)
, a property
applying to formulas that, once satisfiable on finite traces, remain
satisfiable on infinite traces verifying an
end event
forever
and falsifying all other atomic formulas. Concerning the verification aspect, we establish connections between
the finite and infinite trace characterisations, as introduced in
Section
4
, and the notions of

safety

(
Sistla 1994
;
Baier and Katoen 2008
)
, as well as other related notions from
the literature on runtime verification
(
Bauer
et al. 2010
)
.
Section
7
concludes the paper.

3.
First-order Temporal Logics

The
first-order

temporal language

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}

(
Gabbay et al. 2003
)
,
that we present in the following,
is obtained by
extending the usual first-order language with the temporal
operator
until

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
interpreted over
linear
structures, called
traces
.

3.1.
Syntax

The
alphabet
of
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
consists of countably infinite
and pairwise disjoint sets of
predicates

\textsf{N}_{\textsf{P}}

(with
\textsf{ar}(P)\in\mathbb{N}
being the
arity
of

P\in\textsf{N}_{\textsf{P}}
),
constants
(or

individual names
)
{\sf N_{I}}
, and
variables

\mathsf{Var}
;
the
logical operators

\lnot
(
negation
) and
\land
(
conjunction
); the
existential quantifier

\exists
, and the
temporal operator

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
(
until
). The
formulas
of
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
are of the
form:

\varphi::=P(\bar{\tau})\mid\neg\varphi\mid(\varphi\land\varphi)\mid\exists x\varphi\mid(\varphi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\varphi),

where
P\in\textsf{N}_{\textsf{P}}
,

\bar{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{\textsf{ar}(P)})
is a tuple of

terms
, i.e., constants or variables,
and

x\in\textsf{Var}
.
Formulas without the until operator are called
non-temporal
.
We write
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
to indicate that the free
variables of a formula
\varphi
are exactly
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
.
We write

\varphi\{x/y\}

for
the result of uniformly substituting the free occurrences of
y
in

\varphi
by
x
.
For
p\in\mathbb{N}
, the
p
-variable fragment
of
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
,
denoted by
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{p}}
, consists of
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formulas with
at most
p
variables.
The (propositional) language

L​TL
is

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{0}}
with formulas constructed without the existential quantifier.
The
monodic fragment
of
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
,
denoted by
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}}
, consists of formulas such that all
subformulas of the form
\varphi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\psi
have
at most one

free variable.
The
monadic fragment

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{mo}}
is the fragment of
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
with formulas
not containing predicates of arity

\mathop{\geq}2
.
Finally,
the
constant-free one-variable
monadic
fragment
,

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{1,mo}_{\not c}}
,
is obtained from the one-variable
monadic
fragment by disallowing constants.

3.2.
Semantics

A
first-order temporal interpretation
(or
trace
) is a
pair

\mathfrak{M}=(\Delta^{\mathfrak{M}},(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathfrak{T}})
, where
\mathfrak{T}
is a
sub-order of
(\mathbb{N},<)
of the form
[0,\infty)
or
[0,l]
,
with
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
and each
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is a classical first-order interpretation with
a non empty domain
\Delta^{\mathfrak{M}}
(or simply
\Delta
): we have

P^{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\subseteq\Delta^{\textsf{ar}(P)}
, for each

P\in\textsf{N}_{\textsf{P}}
, and

a^{\mathcal{M}_{i}}=a^{\mathcal{M}_{j}}\in\Delta
for all
a\in{\sf N_{I}}
and

i,j\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e., constants are
rigid designators
(with
fixed interpretation, denoted simply by
a^{\mathcal{M}}
).
The stipulation that all time points share the same domain
\Delta
is
called the
constant domain assumption
(meaning that objects are
not created or destroyed over time), and it is the most general choice
in the sense that increasing, decreasing, and varying domains can all
be reduced to it
(
Gabbay et al. 2003
)
.
An
assignment in
\mathfrak{M}
(or simply an
assignment
, when
\mathfrak{M}
is clear from the
context) is a function
\mathfrak{a}
from
\mathsf{Var}
to
\Delta
, and
the
value of a term
\tau
in
\mathfrak{M}
under
\mathfrak{a}
is defined
as:
\mathfrak{a}(\tau)=\mathfrak{a}(x)
, if
\tau=x
, and

\mathfrak{a}(\tau)=a^{\mathcal{M}}
, if
\tau=a\in{\sf N_{I}}
. Given a tuple of

m
terms
\bar{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{m})
, we set

\mathfrak{a}(\bar{\tau})=(\mathfrak{a}(\tau_{1}),\ldots,\mathfrak{a}(\tau_{m}))
. Given a formula
\varphi
, the
satisfaction of

\varphi
in
\mathfrak{M}
at time point
n\in\mathfrak{T}
under an assignment

\mathfrak{a}
, written
\mathfrak{M},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
, is inductively
defined as:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathfrak{M},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}P(\bar{\tau})&\text{iff}&\mathfrak{a}(\bar{\tau})\in P^{\mathcal{M}_{n}},\\
\mathfrak{M},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\neg\psi&\text{iff}&\text{not }\mathfrak{M},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi,\\
\mathfrak{M},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\land\chi&\text{iff}&\mathfrak{M},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\text{ and }\mathfrak{M},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi,\\
\mathfrak{M},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\psi&\text{iff}&\mathfrak{M},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}\psi,\text{
for some assignment }\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\text{ that can differ from }\mathfrak{a}\text{ only on }x,\\
\mathfrak{M},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi&\text{iff}&\text{there is }m\in\mathfrak{T},m>n\colon\mathfrak{M},m\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi\text{ and, }\text{for all }i\in(n,m),\mathfrak{M},i\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi.\\
\end{array}

We say that
\varphi
is
satisfied in
\mathfrak{M}

under
\mathfrak{a}
, writing
\mathfrak{M}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
, if

\mathfrak{M},0\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
, and that

\varphi
is
satisfied in
\mathfrak{M}
(or that
\mathfrak{M}
is a

model
of
\varphi
), denoted by
\mathfrak{M}\models\varphi
, if

\mathfrak{M}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
, for some
\mathfrak{a}
.
Moreover,
\varphi
is said to be
satisfiable
if it is satisfied in
some
\mathfrak{M}
.
A formula
\varphi

logically implies
a formula
\psi

if, for every interpretation
\mathfrak{M}
and every assignment
\mathfrak{a}
,

\mathfrak{M}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
implies
\mathfrak{M}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
,
and we
write
\varphi\models\psi
.
We say that
\varphi
and
\psi
are

equivalent
, writing
\varphi\equiv\psi
, if
\varphi\models\psi
and

\psi\models\varphi
.
Since the satisfaction of a formula
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
under an assignment
\mathfrak{a}
depends only on the values of its free variables under
\mathfrak{a}
, we may write

\mathfrak{M},n\models\varphi[d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}]
in place of

\mathfrak{M},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, where
\mathfrak{a}(x_{1})=d_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}(x_{n})=d_{n}
.
Also, given an assignment
\mathfrak{a}
and an element
d
in an
interpretation’s domain
\Delta

we denote by
\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]
the assignment obtained by
modifying
\mathfrak{a}
so that
x
maps to
d
.

In the following, we call
finite trace
a trace
with
\mathfrak{T}=[0,l]
, often denoted by

\mathfrak{F}=(\Delta^{\mathfrak{F}},(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in[0,l]})
, while
infinite
traces
, based on
\mathfrak{T}=[0,\infty)
, will be denoted by

\mathfrak{I}=(\Delta^{\mathfrak{I}},(\mathcal{I}_{n})_{n\in[0,\infty)})
.
We say that a

Similarly, we write

\varphi

In addition to the standard

top

To refer to the last time point in a finite trace, we

4.
Finite vs. Infinite Traces

In this section, we compare finite and infinite traces semantics.
First, in Section
4.1
, we lift to the first-order
temporal logic setting a well-known reduction of propositional linear
temporal logic formula satisfiability from finite traces to infinite
ones. Then, in Section
4.2
, we establish model-theoretic
conditions under which it is guaranteed that formulas equivalent on
finite (respectively, infinite) traces are also equivalent on infinite
(respectively, finite) traces.
In addition, we syntactically define classes of formulas that are
shown to satisfy such model-theoretic conditions, and for which the
corresponding results on preservation of formula equivalences are thus
inherited.
We finally restrict ourselves to the problem of preserving
satisfiability of a formula, from finite to infinite traces. For this
case as well, we define a class of formulas for which it holds that
satisfiability on finite traces implies satisfiability on infinite
traces.

4.1.
Reduction to Satisfiability on Infinite Traces

In the following, we show how to reduce the formula satisfiability
problem on finite traces to the same problem on infinite traces.
Similar to the encoding proposed in
(
De Giacomo and
Vardi 2013
)
for (propositional)

L​TL
, to capture the finiteness of the temporal dimension, we
introduce a fresh unary predicate
E
, standing for the
end of
time
, with the following properties:

(i)
there is at least one instant before the end of time;

(ii)
the end of time comes for all objects;

(iii)
the end of time comes at the same time for every object;

(iv)
the end of time is permanent.
We axiomatise these properties as follows:

\displaystyle\psi_{f}^{1}

\displaystyle=\forall x\lnot E(x)

(Point (
i
)),

\displaystyle\psi_{f}^{2}

\displaystyle=\forall x\lnot E(x)\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\forall xE(x)

(Points
(ii)
,
(iii)
),

\displaystyle\psi_{f}^{3}

\displaystyle=\Box\forall x(E(x)\to{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}E(x))

\displaystyle\text{(Point~$(iv)$)}.

We now characterise models satisfying the
end of time formula

\psi_{f}=\psi_{f}^{1}\land\psi_{f}^{2}\land\psi_{f}^{3}
.
Let

\mathfrak{F}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in[0,l]})

and

\mathfrak{I}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{I}_{n})_{n\in[0,\infty)})

be, respectively, a finite and an infinite trace with the same domain
\Delta
and such that

a^{\mathcal{F}}=a^{\mathcal{I}}
,
for all
a\in{\sf N_{I}}
.
We denote by

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I}

the

end extension of
\mathfrak{F}
with
\mathfrak{I}
,
defined as the
concatenation
of
\mathfrak{F}
with
\mathfrak{I}

such that:

\displaystyle E^{\mathcal{F}\cdot_{E}\mathcal{I}_{n}}=\begin{cases}\emptyset,&\text{if $n\in[0,l]$};\\
\Delta,&\text{if $n\in[l+1,\infty)$}.\\
\end{cases}

Clearly, end extensions
characterise the satisfiability of

\psi_{f}
. We formalise this in the next lemma.

Lemma 4.1.

For every infinite trace
\mathfrak{I}
,
\mathfrak{I}\models\psi_{f}
iff

\mathfrak{I}=\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
, for some finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
and some infinite
trace
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

\psi_{f}
is satisfied in
\mathfrak{I}
iff there is
k>0
such that, for
all
d\in\Delta
, it holds that:
d\not\in E^{\mathcal{I}_{j}}
, for all

j\in[0,k)
; and
d\in E^{\mathcal{I}_{i}}
, for all
i\in[k,\infty)
.
That is,
\mathfrak{I}=\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
, for some finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
and some
infinite trace
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
.
∎

We now introduce a translation
\cdot^{\dagger}

for
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formulas,
used together with the end of time
formula,
\psi_{f}
, to capture
satisfiability on finite traces.
More formally, a
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formula
\varphi
is satisfiable
on finite traces
if and only if its translation
\varphi^{\dagger}
is
satisfied in an infinite trace
that also satisfies the formula
\psi_{f}
.
The translation
\cdot^{\dagger}
is
defined as:

\displaystyle(P(\bar{\tau}))^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=P(\bar{\tau}),

\displaystyle(\lnot\psi)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\lnot\psi^{\dagger},

\displaystyle(\psi\land\chi)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\psi^{\dagger}\land\chi^{\dagger},

\displaystyle(\exists x\psi)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\exists x\psi^{\dagger},

\displaystyle(\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\psi^{\dagger}\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}(\chi^{\dagger}\land\psi_{f}^{1}).

Before
showing the correctness of the translation, the
following lemma shows the relevance of end extensions when
interpreting translated formulas.

Lemma 4.2.

Let
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I}
be an end extension of a finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
. For
every
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formula
\varphi
and every assignment
\mathfrak{a}
,

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi\ \text{iff}\ \ \mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi^{\dagger}
.

Proof.

Let

\mathfrak{F}=(\Delta^{\mathfrak{F}},(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in[0,l]})

be a finite trace
and let

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I}=(\Delta^{\mathfrak{F}\cdot\mathfrak{I}},(\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{n})_{n\in[0,\infty)})

be an end extension of
\mathfrak{F}
.
We prove by structural induction the following more general statement.
For all
n\in[0,l]
and all assignments
\mathfrak{a}
:

\mathfrak{F},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi\ \text{iff}\ \mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi^{\dagger}.

For the base case
\varphi=P(\bar{\tau})
, the statement follows
from the definitions of
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I}
and
\cdot^{\dagger}
, while
the proof of the inductive cases
\varphi=\lnot\psi
,

\varphi=(\psi\land\chi)
, and
\varphi=\exists x\psi
is
straightforward.

We show the inductive case
\varphi=(\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi)
. We have that

\mathfrak{F},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi
iff there is
m\in(n,l]

such that
\mathfrak{F},m\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and for all
i\in(n,m)
,

\mathfrak{F},i\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. By the inductive hypothesis,
this happens iff there is
m\in(n,l]
such that

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I},m\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi^{\dagger}
and for all
i\in(n,m)
,

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I},i\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi^{\dagger}
. Since
E^{\mathcal{F}\cdot_{E}\mathcal{I}_{j}}=\emptyset

for all
j\in[0,l]
, this means that

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi^{\dagger}\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}(\chi^{\dagger}\land\forall x\lnot E(x))
. That is,

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}(\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi)^{\dagger}
.
∎

Using the previous lemmas, we can show the correctness of the
reduction of the
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
satisfiability problem on finite
traces to the same problem for
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
on infinite
traces.

Theorem 4.3.

A
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formula
\varphi
is satisfiable on finite
traces iff
\varphi^{\dagger}\land\psi_{f}
is satisfiable on infinite
traces.

Proof.

If

\varphi
is satisfied in some finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
, then (by
Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
) any end
extension
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I}
satisfies
\varphi^{\dagger}\land\psi_{f}
. Conversely, suppose
that
\varphi^{\dagger}\land\psi_{f}
is satisfied in some infinite trace
\mathfrak{I}

under an assignment
\mathfrak{a}
. By Lemma
4.1
,

\mathfrak{I}=\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
, for some finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
and some infinite trace
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
.
Since
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi^{\dagger}
, by Lemma
4.2
, we have that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
.
∎

4.2.
Blurring the Distinction Between Finite and Infinite Traces

While certain formulas, such as
\Box\top
, are satisfiable both on
finite and infinite traces, others, e.g.,
\Diamond\textit{last}
and

\Box^{+}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}\top
, are only satisfiable on finite traces and on
infinite traces, respectively.
It is thus of interest to understand in which cases satisfiability on
finite and infinite traces coincide, so that solving the problem in
one case answers to the other as well.
A similar question can be posed for the problem of equivalences
between formulas.
For example,
\Diamond\Box(\varphi\vee\psi)
and
\Diamond\Box\varphi\vee\Diamond\Box\psi
are
equivalent on finite traces but not on infinite traces
(
Bauer and Haslum 2010
)
.
Moreover,
\Box^{+}\Diamond^{+}\varphi
and
\Diamond^{+}\Box^{+}\varphi
are not equivalent
on infinite traces, whereas on finite traces they are both equivalent
to

\Diamond^{+}(\textit{last}\land\varphi)

(
De Giacomo and
Vardi 2013
)
. Conversely,
\bot
and

last

are only equivalent on infinite traces.

In this section we address these questions and investigate the
distinction between reasoning on finite and on infinite
traces.
We first propose semantic properties under which it is guaranteed that
formula
equivalences are preserved from finite to infinite traces, or vice versa,
thus allowing to blur the distinction between these semantics. Then, we syntactically define classes of formulas satisfying some of these semantic properties, so to provide a sufficient criterion for the preservation of equivalences
from finite to infinite traces, or vice versa.
Finally, we focus on preserving satisfiability from the finite to the infinite case, devising a wider class of formulas for which this preservation holds.

4.2.1.
Finite vs. Infinite Traces: Semantic Characterisation

For a
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formula
\varphi
and a quantifier

Q\in\{\exists,\forall\}
,
we say that
\varphi
satisfies
\smash{\textsf{F}_{Q}}

(or that

\varphi
is
\smash{\textsf{F}_{Q}}

) if, for all finite traces
\mathfrak{F}

and all assignments
\mathfrak{a}
, it satisfies the
finite trace
property
:

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi\Leftrightarrow Q\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F}).\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi,

and, similarly, that
\varphi
satisfies
\smash{\textsf{I}_{Q}}

(or
that
\varphi
is
\smash{\textsf{I}_{Q}}

) if, for all infinite traces

\mathfrak{I}
and all assignments
\mathfrak{a}
, it satisfies the
infinite
trace property
:

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi\Leftrightarrow Q\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I}).\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi.

To
show intuitive examples, let us
consider the case where
\varphi
is a Boolean combination of atomic
formulas.
Examples
of formulas satisfying
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}

and
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

are formulas of the form
\Diamond^{+}\varphi
.
Formulas of the form
\Diamond\varphi

are also
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

, but in general not

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}

, as witnessed, for instance, by
\Diamond\top
. On the
other hand, the properties
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}

and

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\forall}}

capture for example formulas of the form

\Box^{+}\varphi
.
Formulas of the form
\Box\varphi

are also
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\forall}}

, but not necessarily

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}

, because of, e.g.,
\Box\bot
. These observations,
that can easily be checked, are also immediate consequences of
Lemmas
4.11
and
4.12
below.

We also restrict to
the “one directional” version of the above properties.
We denote by
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\circ Q}}

and
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\circ Q}}

, where

\circ\in\{\Rightarrow,\Leftarrow\}
, the corresponding
‘
\Rightarrow
’ and ‘
\Leftarrow
’ directions of the
\smash{\textsf{F}_{Q}}

and

\smash{\textsf{I}_{Q}}

properties, respectively.
Finally, given a property
\mathsf{P}
, we denote by
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\mathsf{P})
the set of
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formulas satisfying
\mathsf{P}
.

The semantic properties
\smash{\textsf{F}_{Q}}

and
\smash{\textsf{I}_{Q}}

capture different classes
of
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formulas, as
illustrated by the following example.

Example 4.4.

The following formulas satisfy exactly one of
the corresponding finite or infinite trace properties.

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}

\Diamond^{+}\textit{last}\lor\Diamond P(x)

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}

\forall x\Diamond^{+}P(x)

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

\Box{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}\top\lor\textit{last}

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\forall}}

\Box^{+}P(x)\lor\Diamond^{+}(P(x)\land\textit{last})

Indeed, by using the formulas from Example
4.4
, we can prove
the following.

Proposition 4.5.

The sets

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}$ })
,

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}$ })
,

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}$ })
,
and

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\forall}}$ })

are mutually incomparable with respect to inclusion.

Proof.

For every

X,Y\in\{{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}$ }),{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}$ }),{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}$ }),{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\forall}}$ })\}
,
we use the formulas from Example
4.4
to show that

.

•

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}$ })\not\subseteq Y
, with
Y\in\{{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}$ }),{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}$ }),{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\forall}}$ })\}
.
It can be seen that the formula
\Diamond^{+}\textit{last}\lor\Diamond P(x)
is
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}

. However,

–

it is not
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}

:
(under any assignment)
the formula
is satisfied in a finite trace
\mathfrak{F}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{F}_{0}))
, with

as its only time point and such that

, but an extension
\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
such that
\mathfrak{I}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{I}_{n})_{n\in[0,\infty)})
, with
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathcal{I}_{0}
and
P^{\mathcal{I}_{n}}=\emptyset
, for every

, does not satisfy it;

–

it is not
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

:
(under any assignment) an infinite trace
\mathfrak{I}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{I}_{n})_{n\in[0,\infty)})
such that

, for every
n\in[0,\infty)
does not satisfy the formula, whereas any (and thus some) prefix

satisfies it;

–

it is not
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\forall}}

: shown as in the previous case.

•

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}$ })\not\subseteq Y

.
It can be seen that the formula
\forall x\Diamond^{+}P(x)

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}
. However,

–

it is not
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}

: (under any assignment) the finite trace

as its only time point and such that

, does not satisfy the formula, whereas an extension

, with

–

it is not

We now consider the problem of formula equivalence, by showing under
which semantic properties equivalence between formulas can be
blurred.
The following theorem provides sufficient conditions to

(

Theorem 4.7.

Given
X\in\{{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}$ }),{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}$ }),{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}$ })\cap{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}$ })\}

and
\varphi,\psi\in X
, it holds that

\varphi\equiv_{i}\psi
implies
\varphi\equiv_{f}\psi
.

Proof.

First, assume

\varphi,\psi\in{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}$ })
.
Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a finite trace and
\mathfrak{a}
an assignment such that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
. By

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}

, there is an infinite trace

\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
such that
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
. Since

\varphi\equiv_{i}\psi
, we have that
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. By

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}

,
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
.
The converse
direction can be obtained similarly, by swapping
\varphi
and
\psi
.
The proof for

\varphi,\psi\in{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}$ })

is analogous.

We now show the statement for

\varphi,\psi\in{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}$ })\cap{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}$ })
.
Given a finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
and an assignment

\mathfrak{a}
, suppose that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
. Since
\varphi

is
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}

, we have that for some

\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
,
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
. By assumption,

\varphi\equiv_{i}\psi
, so
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. As
\psi
is

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}

,
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi

implies that, for all
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})
,

\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. Thus, in particular,

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. The other direction can be obtained
by swapping
\varphi
and
\psi
.
∎

does not hold for formulas that satisfy

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

or
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\forall}}

Theorem 4.8.

Given

.

First, suppose that
\varphi,\psi\in{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}$ })
.
Let
\mathfrak{I}
be an infinite trace
and
\mathfrak{a}
an assignment
such that

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
. By
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

,
there is
\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})
such that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
.
As
\varphi\equiv_{f}\psi
, this means that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. Since
\psi
is
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

,
we have
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. The converse direction is
obtained similarly, by swapping
\varphi
and
\psi
. The proof
for

\varphi,\psi\in{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\forall}}$ })

\varphi,\psi\in{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}$ })\cap{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}$ })

\mathfrak{I}
be an infinite trace and

\mathfrak{a}
be an assignment such that
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
. As

\varphi

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}

\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}

Lemma 4.11.

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\forall
-formulas are
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}

and

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
-formulas are
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

.
Thus,

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!
-formulas are both
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}

and

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

.

Proof.

We first show that all

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\forall
-formulas
are
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}

.
In Claim
1
, we show that all

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\forall
-formulas
are

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}

(in fact, for the
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}

case,
we can also allow

\varphi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\psi

in the grammar).
Then, in Claim
2
,
we show that all

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\forall
-formulas
are

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Leftarrow\forall}}

.

Claim 1.

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\forall
-formulas
are

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}

.

Proof of Claim
1
.

Given a finite trace
\mathfrak{F}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in[0,l]})
and an assignment
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathfrak{F}
,
we show that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
implies
that, for every
\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
,

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
.
The proof is by structural induction on
\varphi
.
By Proposition
4.9
, the statement holds for the base cases of
\varphi=P(\bar{\tau})
and
\varphi=\lnot P(\bar{\tau})
.
We now proceed with the inductive steps.

•

\varphi=\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\chi
. Suppose that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\chi
. This means that
there exists
n\in[0,l]
such that

\mathfrak{F},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{F}^{n}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
, and, for every
i\in[0,n)
,

\mathfrak{F},i\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{F}^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
.
By the inductive hypothesis,
we have that there exists
n\in[0,l]
such that

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
, for all

\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F}^{n})
, and, for every
i\in[0,n)
,

\mathfrak{F}^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
.
Since, for every

\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
and
m\in(0,l]
, we have that
\mathfrak{I}=\mathfrak{F}_{m-1}\cdot\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
, for some
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F}^{m})
, the previous
step implies that, for all
\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
, there exists

n\in[0,l]
such that
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
, and, for
every
i\in[0,n)
,
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
.
That is,

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\chi
.

•

\varphi=\forall x\psi
. Suppose that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\forall x\psi
.
This means that, for all
d\in\Delta
,

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
.
By the inductive hypothesis, we have that, for all
d\in\Delta
and
all
\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
,

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
.
Thus,
for all
\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
,

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\forall x\psi
.

•

\varphi=\exists x\psi
. Suppose that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\psi
.
This means that there is
d\in\Delta
such that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
. By the inductive
hypothesis, we have that, for all
\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
,

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
. Thus,
for all
\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
,

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\psi
.

•

The other cases can
be proved in a straightforward way using the
inductive hypothesis.
∎

Claim 2.

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\forall
-formulas are
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Leftarrow\forall}}

.

Proof of Claim
2
.

We show the following (stronger) claim: for every
finite trace

\mathfrak{F}=\linebreak(\Delta,(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in[0,l]})
, and every
\mathfrak{a}
in

\mathfrak{F}
,

\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
implies

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
. The proof is by structural induction
on
\varphi
.
The proof
for the base cases

\varphi=P(\bar{\tau})
and
\varphi=\lnot P(\bar{\tau})

is straightforward.
We now proceed with the inductive steps.

•

\varphi=\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\chi
. If

\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\chi
, then there is

n\geq 0
such that
\mathfrak{F}^{\omega},{n}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi

and, for every
i\in[0,n)
,

\mathfrak{F}^{\omega},{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. This means that there
is
n\geq 0
such that
(\mathfrak{F}^{\omega})^{n}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi

and, for every
i\in[0,n)
,

(\mathfrak{F}^{\omega})^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
.
If

n>l
, then
(\mathfrak{F}^{\omega})^{n}=(\mathfrak{F}^{l})^{\omega}
.
Hence, without loss of generality, we can assume that
n\leq l
,
for which it holds by definition that

(\mathfrak{F}^{\omega})^{n}=(\mathfrak{F}^{n})^{\omega}
. Thus, by the
inductive hypothesis, we obtain
\mathfrak{F}^{n}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
,
and, for every
i\in[0,n)
,
\mathfrak{F}^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
,
meaning that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\chi
.
Hence,

(\mathfrak{F}^{l})^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and, for every

i\in[0,l)
,
(\mathfrak{F}^{i})^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. By
the inductive hypothesis, we obtain that

\mathfrak{F}^{l}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and, for every
i\in[0,l)
,

\mathfrak{F}^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
, again implying that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\chi
.

•

\varphi=\forall x\psi
. If
\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\forall x\psi
, then for all

d\in\Delta
,
\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
.
By the inductive hypothesis, for all
d\in\Delta
,

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
. So,

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\forall x\psi
.

•

\varphi=\exists x\psi
. If
\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\psi
, then there
is
d\in\Delta
such that

\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
. By the inductive hypothesis, for
some
d\in\Delta
,
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
.
Thus,
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\psi
.

•

The remaining cases follow by a straightforward application
of the inductive hypothesis.
∎

We
now show the second part of
Lemma
4.11
, i.e., that

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
-formulas
are
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

. In
Claim
4
, we show that

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
-formulas
are
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}

. Then,
in Claim
5
, we show that

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
-formulas
are

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Leftarrow\exists}}

. Before proving
Claim
4
, we show the following
statement.

Claim 3.

Let
\varphi
be an

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
-formula.
For every finite
trace
\mathfrak{F}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in[0,l]})
, every prefix

\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{n})_{n\in[0,l^{\prime}]})\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{F})
, and every
assignment
\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
implies

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
.

Proof of Claim
3
.

The proof is by structural induction on
\varphi
. Clearly, the
statement holds for the base cases of
\varphi=P(\bar{\tau})
and

\varphi=\lnot P(\bar{\tau})
.
We now proceed with the inductive cases.

•

\varphi=\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi
. If

\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi
, then there is

n\in(0,l^{\prime}]
such that
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
and,
for every
i\in(0,n)
,
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime},i\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
.
That is, for some
n\in(0,l^{\prime}]
,

\mathfrak{F}^{\prime n}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and, for every
i\in(0,n)
,

\mathfrak{F}^{\prime i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. As
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime m}
is a prefix
of
\mathfrak{F}^{m}
, for every
m\in[0,l^{\prime}]
, and since
l^{\prime}\leq l
,
we have by the induction hypothesis that there exists

n\in(0,l]
such that
\mathfrak{F}^{n}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and,
for every
i\in(0,n)
,
\mathfrak{F}^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
.
Equivalently, for some
n\in(0,l]
,

\mathfrak{F},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and, for every
i\in(0,n)
,

\mathfrak{F},i\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. Thus,

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi
.

•

The other cases can be proved by straightforward applications of
the inductive hypothesis.∎

We can now proceed with the following claim.

Claim 4.

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
-formulas are

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}

.

Proof of Claim
4
.

Given
an infinite trace
\mathfrak{I}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{I}_{n})_{n\in[0,\infty)})

and an assignment
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathfrak{I}
, we show that

\mathfrak{I}\models^{a}\varphi
implies that there exists

\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})
such that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
.
The proof is by structural induction on
\varphi
.
By Proposition
4.9
, the statement holds for the base
cases of
\varphi=P(\bar{\tau})
and
\varphi=\lnot P(\bar{\tau})
.
We now proceed with the inductive steps.

•

\varphi=\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi
. Suppose that

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi
, meaning that there
exists
n>0
such that
\mathfrak{I},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and, for
every
i\in(0,n)
,
\mathfrak{I},i\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
. In other
words, there exists
n>0
such that

\mathfrak{I}^{n}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and, for every
i\in(0,n)
,

\mathfrak{I}^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. By the inductive hypothesis,
the previous step implies that there exists
n>0
such that

\mathfrak{I}^{n}_{n_{j}}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
, for some

n_{j}\geq n
,
and, for every
i\in(0,n)
,

\mathfrak{I}^{i}_{i_{k}}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
, for some

i_{k}\geq i
.
For such an
n>0
, let

\overline{n_{j}}=\min\{n_{j}\mid\mathfrak{I}^{n}_{n_{j}}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi\}
and, for every
i\in(0,n)
, let

\overline{i_{k}}=\min\{i_{k}\mid\mathfrak{I}^{i}_{i_{k}}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\}
. In addition, let
m
be the maximum
among
\overline{n_{j}}
and
\overline{i_{k}}
, for

i\in(0,n)
.
We have that

\mathfrak{I}^{n}_{\overline{n_{j}}}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I}^{n}_{m})
,
and
\mathfrak{I}^{i}_{\overline{i_{k}}}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I}^{i}_{m})
, for
every
i\in(0,n)
. Since

\mathfrak{I}^{n}_{\overline{n_{j}}}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and

\mathfrak{I}^{i}_{\overline{i_{k}}}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
, by
Claim
3
we obtain that, for some
n>0
,

\mathfrak{I}^{n}_{m}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and

\mathfrak{I}^{i}_{m}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
, for every
i\in(0,n)
.
In conclusion, there exists
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{I}_{m}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})

such that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi
.

•

\varphi=\exists x\psi
.
Suppose that

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\psi
. This means that there is

d\in\Delta
such that
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
. By
the inductive hypothesis,
there is
\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})
such that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
, for some
d\in\Delta
.
So,
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\psi
.

•

\varphi=\psi\land\chi
.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\land\chi
. This means that

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
and
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
. By the
inductive hypothesis, there are
\mathfrak{F},\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})

such that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
and
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
.
By definition of
\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
, either
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
is a prefix of

\mathfrak{F}
or vice versa. Assume without loss of generality that
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}

is a prefix of
\mathfrak{F}
. By Claim
3
, if

\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
, then
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
. Then,

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
and
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\wedge\chi
.

•

The remaining cases follow by a straightforward application
of the inductive hypothesis.∎

We now conclude the proof of Lemma
4.11
by showing the
following claim.

Claim 5.

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
-formulas are

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Leftarrow\exists}}

.

Proof of Claim
5
.

Given
an infinite trace
\mathfrak{I}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{I}_{n})_{n\in[0,\infty)})

and an assignment
\mathfrak{a}
, we show that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
,
for some
\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})
, implies
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
.
The proof is by structural induction on
\varphi
.
By Proposition
4.9
, the statement holds for the base
cases of
\varphi=P(\bar{\tau})
and
\varphi=\lnot P(\bar{\tau})
. We now
proceed with the inductive steps.

•

\varphi=\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi
. Suppose that there is

\mathfrak{F}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in[0,l]})\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})
such that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi
. This means that there
exists
n\in(0,l]
such that
\mathfrak{F},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi

and, for every
i\in(0,n)
,
\mathfrak{F},i\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
.
In other words, there exists
n\in(0,l]
such that

\mathfrak{F}^{n}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and, for every
i\in(0,n)
,

\mathfrak{F}^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. By the inductive hypothesis,
the previous step implies that, for some
n\in(0,l]
,

\mathfrak{I}^{n}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and, for every
i\in(0,n)
,

\mathfrak{I}^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. Thus, there exists
n>0

such that
\mathfrak{I},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
and, for every

i\in(0,n)
,
\mathfrak{I},i\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
, meaning that

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\chi
.

•

\varphi=\exists x\psi
.
Suppose that there is
\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})
such that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\psi
. This means that there is

d\in\Delta
such that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
.
By the inductive hypothesis,
we obtain
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
,
for some
d\in\Delta
. Hence,

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\psi
.

•

The remaining cases follow by a straightforward application of
the inductive hypothesis. ∎

The results of Lemma
4.11
are tight in the sense that we
cannot extend the grammar rule for
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
-formulas (and not even for

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!
-formulas) with
\forall x\varphi
while still satisfying

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

,
and we cannot extend the grammar rule for
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\forall
-formulas
with
\varphi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\psi
and satisfy
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}

. Simple
counterexamples are
\forall x\Diamond^{+}P(x)

and
\Diamond\top
, which are not
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

and

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}

, respectively. To see that

\forall x\Diamond^{+}P(x)
is not
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}

,
and thus not
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

, consider the model given by an
infinite trace

\mathfrak{I}

with a (countably) infinite domain

\Delta=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n},\ldots\}
, where
the
n
-th
domain element
is in the extension of
P
exactly at time point
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
i.e.,
\mathfrak{I},n\models P(a_{n})
and
\mathfrak{I},i\not\models P(a_{n})
, for any

i\neq n
.
It can be seen that there is no finite prefix of this infinite trace
where
\forall x\Diamond^{+}P(x)
holds.
On the other hand,
\Diamond\top
holds in any infinite trace, but not on a
finite trace with only one time point. Thus,

\Diamond\top

is not
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Leftarrow\forall}}

, and hence not

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}

.

We now move to the case of

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\exists
-,

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}
-,
and

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas,
by proving a result similar to
Lemma
4.11
.

Lemma 4.12.

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\exists
-formulas are

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}

and

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}
-formulas are
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\forall}}

.
Thus,

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas are
both

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}

and

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\forall}}

.

Proof.

We first show that all

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\exists
-formulas
are
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}

.
In Claim
6
we show that all

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\exists
-formulas
are

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}

. Then, in Claim
7
we
show that all

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\exists
-formulas
are

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Leftarrow\exists}}

(in fact, for the

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Leftarrow\exists}}

case,
we can also
allow

\varphi\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}\psi

in the grammar).

Claim 6.

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\exists
-formulas are

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}

.

Proof of Claim
6
.

We show the stronger claim that, for every finite trace

\mathfrak{F}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in[0,l]})
and every assignment

\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
implies

\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
. The proof is by structural
induction on
\varphi
.
By Proposition
4.9
, the statement holds for the base
cases of
\varphi=P(\bar{\tau})
and
\varphi=\lnot P(\bar{\tau})
.
We now proceed with the inductive cases.

•

\varphi=\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\chi
. Suppose that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\chi
. This means that,
for every
n\in[0,l]
, we have

\mathfrak{F},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
, or there exists
i\in[0,n)

such that
\mathfrak{F},i\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. That is, for every

n\in[0,l]
, it holds that
\mathfrak{F}^{n}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
,
or there exists
i\in[0,n)
such that

\mathfrak{F}^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. By the inductive hypothesis,
this implies that, for every
n\in[0,l]
, we have

(\mathfrak{F}^{n})^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
, or there exists

i\in[0,n)
such that

(\mathfrak{F}^{i})^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. For every

m\in[0,l]
, it holds that

(\mathfrak{F}^{m})^{\omega}=(\mathfrak{F}^{\omega})^{m}
, thus we obtain, for
every
n\in[0,l]
, that

(\mathfrak{F}^{\omega})^{n}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
, or there exists

i\in[0,n)
such that

(\mathfrak{F}^{\omega})^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. Moreover, since

(\mathfrak{F}^{\omega})^{l}=(\mathfrak{F}^{\omega})^{m}
, for every
m>l
,
we have that
(\mathfrak{F}^{\omega})^{m}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
, or
there exists
i\in[0,m)
such that

(\mathfrak{F}^{\omega})^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. In conclusion,
for every
n\geq 0
,
\mathfrak{F}^{\omega},n\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\chi
,
or there exists
i\in[0,n)
such that

\mathfrak{F}^{\omega},i\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. Hence,

\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\chi
.

•

\varphi=\forall x\psi
.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\forall x\psi
.
This means that, for all
d\in\Delta
,

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
.
By the inductive hypothesis, for all
d\in\Delta
,

\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
.
Thus,

\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\forall x\psi
.

•

\varphi=\exists x\psi
.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\psi
.
This means that there is
d\in\Delta
such that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
.
By the inductive hypothesis,
we have

\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\psi
,
for some
d\in\Delta
.
That is,

\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\psi
.

•

The other cases can
be proved in a straightforward way using the
inductive hypothesis.
∎

Claim 7.

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\exists
-formulas are

\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Leftarrow\exists}}

.

Proof of Claim
7
.

Given a finite trace
\mathfrak{F}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in[0,l]})
and
an assignment
\mathfrak{a}

4.2.3.
Preserving Formula Satisfiability: From Finite to Infinite Traces

In this section, we consider the problem of preserving satisfiability
of a
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formula
\varphi
from finite to infinite
traces, i.e.,
under which conditions,
knowing that
\varphi
is finitely satisfiable, we can conclude that
\varphi
is
also satisfiable on infinite traces.
Identifying classes of formulas for which this question can be
positively answered is of interest also to develop
more
efficient automated reasoners.
Indeed, under certain conditions which guarantee that
satisfiability of a formula on finite traces implies its satisfiability on infinite ones,
solvers can simply stop trying to build the lasso of an infinite trace,
once a finite trace satisfying the formula is found.

In order to connect this problem with the results obtained in the
previous sections, we make the following observations.
First, in Theorem
4.3
, we have seen that

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formulas interpreted on finite traces can be
translated into equisatisfiable
formulas on infinite traces. However, such translation is not always
needed, since for some classes of formulas
satisfiability is already preserved.
For instance, given

\varphi\in{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}$ })
,
we clearly have that, if
\varphi
is
satisfiable on finite traces, then it is
satisfiable on infinite traces.
Moreover, the problem of preserving satisfiability from finite to
infinite traces can be seen as a special case of the problem of
preserving formula equivalences from infinite to finite ones, where we
are only interested in determining if a
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formula

\varphi
that is equivalent to
\bot
on infinite traces (i.e.,
unsatisfiable on infinite traces) is also unsatisfiable on finite
traces. This is not the case in general.
For instance,
last
, which is equivalent to
\bot
on
infinite traces but satisfiable on finite traces, is
a formula
for which
satisfiability is not preserved from finite to infinite traces.
Instead, from Theorem
4.10
, we obtain in particular that,
for every

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\forall
-
or

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\exists
-formulas
satisfiability is preserved from finite to infinite traces.

However, the results of the previous section do not allow us to determine classes of formulas that involve both operators

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!

and

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
,
and for which satisfiability from finite to infinite traces is preserved.
Formulas like

\Diamond^{+}\Box^{+}P(x)
and

\Box^{+}\Diamond^{+}P(x)
,
for instance,
are such that their
satisfiability is preserved from finite to infinite traces, but they do not fall in any of the fragments identified above.
Our aim in the rest of this section is to show that indeed
satisfiability from finite to infinite traces
is preserved for a larger class of formulas, introduced in the following.

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas

\varphi,\psi
are built according to the grammar (with

P\in\textsf{N}_{\textsf{P}}
):

P(\bar{\tau})\mid\neg P(\bar{\tau})\mid\varphi\wedge\psi\mid\varphi\vee\psi\mid\exists x\varphi\mid\forall x\varphi\mid\varphi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\psi\mid\varphi\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\psi.

It can be seen that the set of

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas is just a syntactic variant (in negation
normal form) of the fragment

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!}\mathcal{QL}}
of

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
, i.e., the fragment allowing only for

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!
and

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
as temporal operators. A typical example of an

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formula, used to express properties in the context
of specification and verification of reactive systems, is
\Box^{+}\forall x(P(x)\to\Diamond^{+}Q(x))

(
Gabbay et al. 2003
)
.

We show in the following that the language generated by the grammar
rule for

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas contains only formulas whose
satisfiability on finite traces implies satisfiability on infinite
traces. This result, formalised by the following theorem, is an
immediate consequence of Lemma
4.14
below.

Theorem 4.13.

All
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas satisfiable on finite traces are
satisfiable on infinite traces.

The converse of Theorem
4.13
, however, does not hold,
as illustrated by the next example. Consider the

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formula

\displaystyle\Box^{+}\forall x\big((P(x)\land\lnot Q(x))\lor(Q(x)\land\lnot P(x)\big)\ \land

(3)

\displaystyle\Box^{+}\forall x\big((P(x)\to\Diamond^{+}Q(x))\land(Q(x)\to\Diamond^{+}P(x))\big).

We have
that
(\ref{eq:infnotfinform})
is satisfiable on infinite traces
only, since it requires
P(x)
and

Q(x)
to alternate infinitely often.
Therefore, for
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas,
satisfiability on infinite traces does not imply satisfiability on finite traces.

In order to prove Theorem
4.13
, we introduce the
following preliminary notion.
A
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formula
\varphi

is
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\omega}}

iff, for all finite traces

\mathfrak{F}
and all assignments
\mathfrak{a}
, it satisfies the
frozen
trace property
:

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi.

We denote by
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\omega}}$ })
the set of

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formulas that are
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\omega}}

. Clearly,
if
\varphi\in{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\omega}}$ })
is satisfiable on
finite traces, then
\varphi
is satisfiable on infinite traces. Thus,
Theorem
4.13
above is an immediate consequence of the
following lemma.

Lemma 4.14.

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas
are
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\omega}}

.

Proof.

We write
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\omega}}

and
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Leftarrow\omega}}

for the “one directional” version of
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\omega}}

.
In Claim
11
we show that all

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas
are
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\omega}}

.
Then, in Claim
12
, we show that all

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas
are
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Leftarrow\omega}}

.

Claim 11.

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas
are
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\omega}}

.

We show, by structural induction on

We now show the remaining inductive case.

•

\varphi=\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\chi

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\chi

\mathfrak{F}^{i}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi

such that

5.
Complexity of Decidable Fragments on Finite and
k
-Bounded Traces

In this section, we study the complexity of the satisfiability problem
for formulas taken from well-known decidable fragments of first-order
temporal logic, ranging from the constant-free one-variable monadic, to the monadic monodic, or the
two-variables monodic, fragments (as introduced in
Section
3.1
). First, we consider satisfiability on
arbitrary finite traces, showing that the complexity does not change
compared to the infinite case, i.e., it remains

ExpSpace
-complete. Then, we analyse the case of satisfiability on

k
-bounded traces, proving that the complexity lowers down to

NExpTime
-complete. Finally, we show that these fragments
interpreted on finite traces enjoy both the bounded trace and the
bounded domain properties, that is, they are satisfiable on finite
traces iff they are satisfied on finite traces with a bounded number
of time points, and of elements in the domain, respectively, with a
bound that depends on the size of the formula.
We
conclude the section with an excursus on temporal
DLs,
by investigating the complexity of the
satisfiability problem in the temporal extension of the
DL

\smash{\mathcal{ALC}}
.

5.1.

ExpSpace
-hardness holds already for the constant-free one-variable
monadic fragment
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{1,mo}_{\not c}}

considered as a notational variant of the propositional language of

\mathbf{LTL}^{f}\times\bf{S5}

|S|\leq 2^{|\mathsf{tp}(\varphi)|}
, and thus
(S,\mathfrak{R})
is as required.
Otherwise, suppose that there are
n,m\in[0,l]
such that

S(n)\neq S(m)
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that

.
Since
S
is finite, by (repeatedly) applying Claim

to

, we obtain a quasimodel
(S^{\prime},\mathfrak{R}^{\prime})
such that

contains only distinct quasistates, hence

|S^{\prime}|\leq 2^{|\mathsf{tp}(\varphi)|}
.
∎

The theorem now follows from
Lemmas

,
5.11
, and
5.7
, since

|\mathsf{tp}(\varphi)|\leq 2^{|\varphi|}
.
∎

We now establish that an
\mathcal{L}
formula interpreted on

k
-bounded traces
has the
bounded domain property
, i.e., if it is satisfiable on

k
-bounded traces,
then
it is satisfied on a trace having
finite
domain
with bounded cardinality.

Theorem 5.12.

Satisfiability of an
\mathcal{L}\in\{{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{mo}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}},{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{2}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}}\}

-bounded traces
implies satisfiability
of
\varphi

on traces having finite domain
of cardinality
at most

|\mathsf{tp}(\varphi)|^{k}\cdot 2^{|\mathsf{tp}(\varphi)|}
.

Proof.

We require the following preliminary definitions and result. A quasimodel
(S,\mathfrak{R})
for
\varphi
, with
S=(S(0),\ldots,S(l))
, is said to be
finitary
if
S(i)
is finitely realisable, for every

, and
\mathfrak{R}
is finite.
The next lemma is an adaptation of
(

to the case of

-bounded traces.

Lemma 5.13.

An
\mathcal{L}
sentence
\varphi
is satisfiable on
k
-bounded traces having
finite domain iff there is a finitary quasimodel for
\varphi
with a
sequence of quasistates of length at most
k
.

Now, suppose that
\varphi
is satisfied on a
k
-bounded trace. By
Lemma
5.7
, there is a quasimodel
(S,\mathfrak{R})
for
\varphi
, with

S=(S(0),\ldots,S(l))
and

. It is known that, for

\mathcal{L}\in\{{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{mo}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}},{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{2}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}}\}
formulas, a state candidate is realisable iff it is finitely
realisable,
since monadic and
2
-variable first-order formulas enjoy the
exponential (hence, finite)
model property
(
Börger et al. 1997
, Proposition 6.2.7, Corollary 8.1.5)
.
Thus, we have that every

, is finitely realisable.
Moreover, because

is finite, we have that

, which is a set of functions from

Therefore,

is finitary and, by Lemma

is satisfiable on

k
-bounded traces
having finite domain.
Finally,
having recalled
that monadic and
2
-variable first-order formulas enjoy the exponential model property, we can assume without loss of generality that a first-order interpretation realising a quasistate in

has domain of cardinality at most

2^{|\mathsf{tp}(\varphi)|}
.
Thus, one can adjust the construction in
(
Gabbay et al. 2003
, Lemma 11.41)
to obtain, from a finitary quasimodel

for
\varphi
, with

k
-bounded trace that satisfies

with domain

\Delta=\{(r,i)\mid r\in\mathfrak{R},0\leq i<m_{\varphi}\}

for some

.
Since

.
∎

Since
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{2}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}}
satisfiability on finite traces implies satisfiability on
k
-bounded traces, for some

the
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{2}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}}
formula

\vartheta_{i}=P(a)\land\Box^{+}\forall x\Big(P(x){\rightarrow}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}\Box^{+}\big(\neg P(x)\wedge\exists y(R(x,y)\wedge P(y))\big)\Big),

which only admits models with an infinite domain

et al. 2008

is unsatisfiable over finite traces.

5.4.
Temporal Description Logics

We conclude this section investigating the complexity of the
satisfiability problem in temporal DLs.
We consider the temporal language
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}

(
Gabbay et al. 2003
)
as a temporal
extension of the DL
\smash{\mathcal{ALC}}

(
Baader et al. 2003a
)
.
Let
{\sf N_{C}},{\sf N_{R}}\subseteq\textsf{N}_{\textsf{P}}
be, respectively,
countably infinite and disjoint
sets of unary and binary predicates called
concept
and

role names
.
A
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
concept
is an expression of the form:

C,D::=A\mid\lnot C\mid C\sqcap D\mid\exists R.C\mid C\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}D,

where
A\in{\sf N_{C}}
and
R\in{\sf N_{R}}
.
A
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
axiom
is either a
concept inclusion (CI)
of the
form
C\sqsubseteq D
, or an
assertion
,
\alpha
, of the form

A(a)
or
R(a,b)
, where
C,D
are
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
concepts,
A\in{\sf N_{C}}
,

R\in{\sf N_{R}}
, and
a,b\in{\sf N_{I}}
.

\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
formulas
have the form:

\varphi,\psi::=\alpha\mid C\sqsubseteq D\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\land\psi\mid\varphi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\psi.

The semantics of
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}

is given again (with a small abuse of notation) over
finite traces

\mathfrak{F}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in[0,l]})
,
where

l\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Delta
is a non-empty domain, and,
for every
n\in[0,l]
,

{\mathcal{F}_{n}}

is an
\smash{\mathcal{ALC}}
interpretation with domain
\Delta
,
mapping
each concept name
A\in{\sf N_{C}}
to a subset
A^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}
of
\Delta
, each
role name
R\in{\sf N_{R}}
to a binary relation
R^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}
on
\Delta
, and
each individual name
a\in{\sf N_{I}}
to a domain element
a^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}
in
such a way that
a^{\mathcal{F}_{i}}=a^{\mathcal{F}_{j}}
, for all
i,j\in[0,l]
(thus,
we just use the notation
a^{\mathcal{F}}
).
The interpretation is extended to
concepts as usual:

\displaystyle(\lnot C)^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}

\displaystyle=\Delta\setminus C^{\mathcal{F}_{n}},

\displaystyle(C\sqcap D)^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}

\displaystyle=C^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\cap D^{\mathcal{F}_{n}},

\displaystyle(\exists R.C)^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}

\displaystyle=\{d\in\Delta\mid\text{there is }e\in C^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\text{ such that }(d,e)\in R^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\},

\displaystyle(C\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}D)^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}

\displaystyle=\{d\in\Delta\mid\text{there is }m\in(n,l]\text{
such that }d\in D^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\text{ and }d\in C^{\mathcal{F}_{i}},\text{ for all }i\in(n,m)\}.

Given a
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
formula
\varphi
, the
satisfaction
of
\varphi
in
\mathfrak{F}
at time
point
n\in[0,l]
, written
\mathfrak{F},n\models\varphi
, is
inductively defined as:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathfrak{F},n\models C\sqsubseteq D&\text{ iff }&C^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\subseteq D^{\mathcal{F}_{n}},\\
\mathfrak{F},n\models A(a)&\text{ iff }&a^{\mathcal{F}}\in A^{\mathcal{F}_{n}},\\
\mathfrak{F},n\models R(a,b)&\text{ iff }&(a^{\mathcal{F}},b^{\mathcal{F}})\in R^{\mathcal{F}_{n}},\\
\mathfrak{F},n\models\lnot\varphi&\text{ iff }&\mathfrak{F},n\not\models\varphi,\\
\mathfrak{F},n\models\varphi\land\psi&\text{ iff }&\mathfrak{F},n\models\varphi\text{ and }\mathfrak{F},n\models\psi,\\
\mathfrak{F},n\models\varphi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\psi&\text{ iff }&\text{there is }m\in(n,l]\text{ such that }\mathfrak{F},m\models\psi\text{ and }\mathfrak{F},i\models\varphi,\text{ for all }i\in(n,m).\end{array}

We say that a
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
formula
\varphi
is
satisfiable on finite traces

if there exists a finite trace

\mathfrak{F}
such that

\mathfrak{F},0\models\varphi
.
If
\varphi
is satisfiable on finite traces with at most
k
instants, with

given in binary, we say that
\varphi
is
satisfiable on
k
-bounded traces
.

Since a
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
formula can be mapped into an equisatisfiable

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{2}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}}
formula
(
Gabbay et al. 2003

we can transfer
the upper bounds of Propositions

and

to

\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
on finite and
k
-bounded traces, respectively. The lower
bounds can be obtained
from Propositions

and
5.4
,
since
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{1,mo}_{\not c}}
can be seen as a fragment of

\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
without role names
(
Gabbay et al. 2003
)
. Thus, the following holds.

Theorem 5.14.

\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
satisfiability is
ExpSpace
-complete on finite traces, and

NExpTime
-complete on
k
-bounded traces.

Moreover, from Theorems
5.9

and
5.12
, we obtain immediately that
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
on
finite traces has both the bounded trace and domain properties.

We also consider the satisfiability problem on
k
-bounded

traces of
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}

restricted to global CIs

(
Lutz
et al. 2008

)

defined as the fragment of
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
in which formulas can only be of

The

4
4

4

The main challenge in solving this problem when the number of time

sub-formulas (i.e., formulas of the form

To show the following theorem, we rely again on

Our definitions here are similar to those in

Theorem 5.15.

\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}

-bounded traces restricted to global

Proof.

It is enough to show that satisfiability in

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)

\varphi

\mathfrak{F},0\models\varphi

for all

are those fresh concept names we used to extend

We define
(S,\mathfrak{R})
in the following way. First,
for all

,

\displaystyle\sigma_{d}(n)

\displaystyle=\{C\in\mathsf{cl}^{\mathsf{c}}(\varphi)\mid d\in C^{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\},

We

is a quasimodel for

) direction of

(\Leftarrow)

\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
. Thus:

E^{\mathcal{F}\cdot\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{n}}=\begin{cases}\emptyset,&\text{for all $n\in[0,l]$}\\
\Delta,&\text{for all }n\in[l+1,\infty)\\
\end{cases}

Since
\mathfrak{I}\models\chi_{f}
, for all
n\in[l+1,\infty)
, for all
objects
d
and all tuples of objects
\bar{d}
in
\Delta
, we have
that: if

d\in E^{\mathcal{F}\cdot_{E}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{n}}=\Delta
,
then

(d,\bar{d})\notin P^{\mathcal{F}\cdot_{E}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{n}}
, for
all
P\in\Sigma\setminus\{E\}
. This is equivalent to

P^{\mathcal{I}_{n}}=\emptyset
, for all
P\in\Sigma\setminus\{E\}
and

n\in[l+1,\infty)
. Therefore, we have that
\mathfrak{I}_{\mid_{\Sigma}}=\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}
.
∎

Lemma 6.2.

Let
\varphi
be a
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formula,
\mathfrak{F}
a finite trace, and
\mathfrak{a}
an assignment. We have that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi\text{ iff \,}\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi^{\dagger}
.

Proof.

By definition of
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}
and as a consequence of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

We can now state the following characterisation result for insensitive formulas, which extends
(
De
Giacomo et al. 2014a
, Theorem 4)

to the first-order language

{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
.

Theorem 6.3.

A
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formula
\varphi
is insensitive to infiniteness iff

\vartheta_{f}\models_{i}\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\dagger}
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
Assume that
\varphi
is insensitive. We want to prove
that, for every infinite trace
\mathfrak{I}
and all assignments
\mathfrak{a}
,
if
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\vartheta_{f}
, then

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\dagger}
. Suppose

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\vartheta_{f}
. By Lemma
6.1
,

\mathfrak{I}_{\mid_{\Sigma}}=\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}
, for a finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
. Moreover, thanks to
Lemma
6.2
,
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
iff

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi^{\dagger}
. Since
\varphi
is by hypothesis
insensitive, for every finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
and all assignments

\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
iff
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
.
Thus,
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
if and only if

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi^{\dagger}
. That is,

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\dagger}
, and therefore

\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\dagger}
(since all the
predicates occurring in
\varphi,\varphi^{\dagger}
are in
\Sigma
).

(\Leftarrow)
Assume that

\vartheta_{f}\models\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\dagger}
. By
Lemma
6.1
, for every infinite trace
\mathfrak{I}
and every
assignment
\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{I}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\vartheta_{f}
means that

\mathfrak{I}_{\mid_{\Sigma}}=\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}
, for a finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
. Given our assumption,
this implies
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\dagger}
, that
is
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
iff

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi^{\dagger}
, for all assignments
{\mathfrak{a}}
. By
Lemma
6.2
,
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi^{\dagger}
if and
only if
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
. In conclusion, we obtain that, for
all assignments
{\mathfrak{a}}
,
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
iff
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
, meaning that
\varphi
is insensitive.
∎

We
now analyse syntactic features of insensitive
formulas. Firstly, non-temporal
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formulas are
insensitive. Moreover, this property is preserved under non-temporal
operators. We generalise
(
De
Giacomo et al. 2014a
, Theorem 5)

in our setting
as follows.

Theorem 6.4.

Let
\varphi,\psi
be insensitive
formulas. Then

\lnot\varphi
,
\exists x\varphi
, and
\varphi\land\psi
are insensitive.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a finite trace and
\mathfrak{a}
be an assignment. For

\lnot\varphi
, we have that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\lnot\varphi
iff

\mathfrak{F}\not\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
. Since
\varphi
is insensitive by
hypothesis, this means that
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\not\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
.
Therefore,
\lnot\varphi
is insensitive as well. For
\exists x\varphi
,
we have that
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\varphi
iff

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\varphi
, for some
d\in\Delta
.
Given that
\varphi
is insensitive, this is equivalent to

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}[x\mapsto d]}\varphi
, for some
d\in\Delta
.
That is,
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\exists x\varphi
, and so
\exists x\varphi

is insensitive. For
\varphi\land\psi
, we have that

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi\land\psi
is equivalent to

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
and
\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. Since both

\varphi
and
\psi
are assumed to be insensitive, the previous step is
equivalent to:
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
and

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi\land\psi
.
∎

Concerning temporal operators, in
(
De
Giacomo et al. 2014a
)
it is shown how
several standard temporal patterns derived from the declarative
process modelling language
declare

(
van der Aalst and Pesic 2006
)
are
insensitive. On the other hand, negation affects the insensitivity of
temporal formulas. For instance, given a non temporal
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}

formula

\psi
, we have that

\Diamond^{+}\psi
is insensitive while
\Diamond^{+}\lnot\psi
is not.
Dually,
\Box^{+}\lnot\psi
is insensitive, while
\Box^{+}\psi
is
not. Therefore, if a
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formula
\varphi
is insensitive, it
cannot be concluded that formulas of the form
\Diamond^{+}\varphi
or

\Box^{+}\varphi
are insensitive.

Finally,
we have that insensitivity is sufficient to ensure that if formulas
are equivalent on infinite traces, then they are equivalent on finite
traces.

Theorem 6.5.

For all insensitive
formulas
\varphi,\psi
,
\varphi\equiv_{i}\psi
implies
\varphi\equiv_{f}\psi
.

Proof.

Given a finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
and an assignment
\mathfrak{a}
, if

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
then, as
\varphi
is insensitive,

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\varphi
. By assumption,
\varphi\equiv_{i}\psi
,
so
\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. As
\psi
is insensitive,

\mathfrak{F}\cdot_{E}\mathfrak{E}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
implies

\mathfrak{F}\models^{\mathfrak{a}}\psi
. The converse direction is obtained
by swapping
\varphi
and
\psi
.
∎

Since
\bot
is insensitive, we obtain the following immediate
corollary of the previous result.

Corollary 6.6.

All insensitive formulas satisfiable on finite traces are
satisfiable on infinite traces.

However, the converse directions of the above results do not not hold,
as witnessed, e.g., by formula
\vartheta_{i}

(cf. Section
5.3
), which is trivially insensitive, but
satisfiable only on infinite traces.
We can obtain the converse directions by using our
Theorem
4.8
. For instance,

\Diamond^{+}P(x)\lor\Diamond^{+}R(x)
and
\Diamond^{+}(P(x)\lor R(x))
are
insensitive and
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\exists}}

formulas for which equivalence
on finite and infinite traces coincide.

6.2.
Verification

In this section we show how our comparison between finite and infinite
traces can be related to the literature on temporal logics for
verification. In particular, we establish connections between the
finite and infinite trace properties, introduced in
Section
4.2
, and:
(i)
the definition of safety in

L​TL
on infinite traces
(
Sistla 1994
;
Baier and Katoen 2008
)
;
(ii)
maxims related to
monitoring procedures in runtime verification
(
Bauer
et al. 2010
)
.

6.2.1.
Safety

Recall that a safety property intuitively guarantees that “bad
things” never happen during the execution of a program.
In verification,
L​TL
is often used as a specification language for
such properties, and the notion of safety is defined accordingly on
infinite traces
(
Sistla 1994
;
Baier and Katoen 2008
)
. In the rest of this section, we
will thus restrict ourselves to
L​TL
. A typical example of an

L​TL
formula used to specify a safety property is represented by

\Box\lnot P
, where
P
is an atom standing for an action or task.
Dual to safety properties are
co-safety properties
, expressing
that “good things” will eventually happen in the execution of a
program. The
L​TL
formula
\Diamond P
is a standard example of a
formula specifying a co-safety property.

To fix notions that will be used in the rest of this section, we start
by recalling the definitions of
safety
and
co-safety
fragments
of
L​TL
.
The
L​TL
safety formulas

(
Sistla 1994
)
are defined as the
L​TL

formulas obtained from
\bot
,
\top
, and
literals
(i.e.,

propositional letters

P
, or negated propositional letters

\lnot P
), by applying conjunction
\land
, disjunction
\lor
,
strong next
\bigcirc
, and reflexive release
\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
operators.
The
L​TL
co-safety formulas

(
Kupferman and
Vardi 1999
)
are
dually defined
as those
L​TL
formulas obtained from
\bot
,
\top
, and literals,
by applying conjunction
\land
, disjunction
\lor
, strong next

\bigcirc
, and reflexive until
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!
operators.
It is known
(
Sistla 1994
;
Kupferman and
Vardi 1999
;
Camacho
et al. 2018b
)
that every safety formula

\varphi

expresses a safety property
, i.e.,
for every infinite trace
\mathfrak{I}
such that
\mathfrak{I}\not\models\varphi
,
there exists
\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})
so that, for all

\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
, it holds that
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}\not\models\varphi
. We
call such a finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
a
bad prefix for
\varphi
, and we
define
\textit{BadPre}(\varphi)
as the set of bad prefixes for
\varphi
. On the
other hand, every co-safety formula
\varphi

expresses a co-safety
property
, i.e., for every infinite trace
\mathfrak{I}
satisfying
\varphi
,
there is a
good prefix for
\varphi
, that is, a finite prefix

\mathfrak{F}
of
\mathfrak{I}
such that every infinite extension
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{F}

satisfies
\varphi
.
Given the equivalence,
{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}\lnot\varphi\equiv_{i}\lnot{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}\varphi
,
holding on infinite traces, we have that the
L​TL
formulas that are,
respectively, in the
\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}
- and
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
-fragments defined
in Section
4.2
express, respectively, safety and
co-safety properties.

In order to establish connections between safety properties and finite
traces semantics, we now require, following
(
Baier and Katoen 2008
)
, further
definitions and notation.
Let
\mathsf{N}^{0}_{\mathsf{P}}
be the subset of
\textsf{N}_{\textsf{P}}
containing

0
-ary predicates, i.e., propositional letters.
Given a suborder

\mathfrak{T}
of
(\mathbb{N},<)
of the form
[0,\infty)
or
[0,l]
,
with
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
a
trace
is now viewed simply as a sequence

\mathfrak{M}=(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathfrak{T}}
with

\mathcal{M}_{n}\in 2^{\mathsf{N}^{0}_{\mathsf{P}}}
.
The notion of an
L​TL
formula
\varphi
being satisfied in a trace

\mathfrak{M}
,
\mathfrak{M}\models\varphi
, is given similarly as above
(cf. Section
3.2
), and we let
\textit{Trace}_{i}(\varphi)
,
respectively,
\textit{Trace}_{f}(\varphi)
, be the set of infinite, respectively,
finite, traces satisfying
\varphi
.
The sets of prefixes of a trace
\mathfrak{M}
and of extensions of a finite trace
\mathfrak{F}

are defined as in Section
3.2
.
Moreover, given a set of infinite traces,
\mathcal{C}
, and of finite
traces,
\mathcal{D}
, we define

\textit{Pre}(\mathcal{C})=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{I}\in\mathcal{C}}\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I}),\qquad\textit{Ext}(\mathcal{D})=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{F}\in\mathcal{D}}\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F}).

For an
L​TL
formula
\varphi
, we may write
\textit{Pre}(\varphi)
and
\textit{Ext}(\varphi)
in
place of, respectively,
\textit{Pre}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\varphi))
and

\textit{Ext}(\textit{Trace}_{f}(\varphi))
.
Moreover, given a set of infinite traces
\mathcal{C}
, we define the

closure of
\mathcal{C}
as

\textit{Clo}(\mathcal{C})=\{\mathfrak{I}\mid\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})\subseteq\textit{Pre}(\mathcal{C})\}
.
Consider, for instance,
the set of infinite traces

\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{i\in[0,\infty)}\{\mathfrak{I}^{i}\}
, where

\mathfrak{I}^{i}=(\mathcal{I}^{i}_{n})_{n\in[0,\infty)}
is such that

\mathcal{I}^{i}_{n}=\{P\}
, if
i=n
, and
\mathcal{I}^{i}_{n}=\emptyset
,
otherwise. We have that the infinite trace

\mathfrak{I}=(\mathcal{I}_{n})_{n\in[0,\infty)}
defined so that

\mathcal{I}_{n}=\emptyset
, for every
i\in[0,\infty)
, is such that

\mathfrak{I}\not\in\mathcal{C}
, whereas
\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Clo}(\mathcal{C})
.
The following preliminary lemma adapts to our setting a result
presented in
(
Baier and Katoen 2008
, Lemma 3.25)
.

Lemma 6.7.

Let
\varphi,\chi
be

L​TL

formulas, with

\varphi\in\textsl{L\!TL}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}$ })\cap\textsl{L\!TL}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}$ })

and
\chi
expressing a safety property. It holds that:

\varphi\models_{i}\chi\quad\text{iff}\quad\textit{Trace}_{f}(\varphi)\cap\textit{BadPre}(\chi)=\emptyset.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)

By contraposition, assume there exists
\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{Trace}_{f}(\varphi)\cap\textit{BadPre}(\chi)
.
We have that
\mathfrak{F}\models\varphi
, and
since

\varphi
is
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}

, there exists
\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
such that
\mathfrak{I}\models\varphi
.
Given that
\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{BadPre}(\chi)
and that
\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})
, we have in particular that
\mathfrak{I}\not\models\chi
.
Therefore,
\varphi\not\models_{i}\chi
.

(\Leftarrow)

By contraposition, assume that
\varphi\not\models_{i}\chi
, i.e., there exists an infinite trace
\mathfrak{I}
such that
\mathfrak{I}\models\varphi
and
\mathfrak{I}\not\models\chi
.
Since
\chi
expresses a safety property, there exists
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})
such that,
for all
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}\in\textit{Ext}(\hat{\mathfrak{F}})
,

\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}\not\models\chi
.
Thus,

\hat{\mathfrak{F}}\in\textit{BadPre}(\chi)
.
Moreover, since
\varphi
is
\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}

, we have that every
\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})
is such that
\mathfrak{F}\models\varphi
. Hence,
\hat{\mathfrak{F}}\in\textit{Trace}_{f}(\varphi)
, and so
\textit{Trace}_{f}(\varphi)\cap\textit{BadPre}(\chi)\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Using the previous result, we can show the following characterisation
of two
L​TL
formulas being equivalent on finite traces in terms of
safety properties, under the assumption that they satisfy the
properties
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}

and

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}

, as it is the case for
L​TL

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas (cf. Section
4.2
). This
characterisation mirrors the one given in
(
Baier and Katoen 2008
, Corollary
3.29)
, but it is presented here in terms of finite traces and

L​TL
formulas, as opposed to the one in
(
Baier and Katoen 2008
)
based on sets
of traces and transition systems.

Proposition 6.8.

For every

\varphi,\psi\in\textsl{L\!TL}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}$ })\cap\textsl{L\!TL}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}$ })
,
the following are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi\equiv_{f}\psi
;

(ii)

for every
L​TL
formula
\chi
expressing a safety property,

\varphi\models_{i}\chi
iff
\psi\models_{i}\chi
.

Proof.

We are going to show that the following statements are equivalent:

(i^{\prime})

\textit{Trace}_{f}(\varphi)\subseteq\textit{Trace}_{f}(\psi)
;

(ii^{\prime})

for every
L​TL
formula
\chi
expressing a safety property,

\psi\models_{i}\chi
implies
\varphi\models_{i}\chi
.

Then, by swapping
\varphi
and
\psi
, the required result will follow.

(i^{\prime})\Rightarrow(ii^{\prime})

Assume that
\textit{Trace}_{f}(\varphi)\subseteq\textit{Trace}_{f}(\psi)
and that, for every
L​TL
formula
\chi
expressing a safety property,
\psi\models_{i}\chi
. By Lemma
6.7
, this is equivalent to
\textit{Trace}_{f}(\psi)\cap\textit{BadPre}(\chi)=\emptyset
. Since
\textit{Trace}_{f}(\varphi)\subseteq\textit{Trace}_{f}(\psi)
, we have
\textit{Trace}_{f}(\varphi)\cap\textit{BadPre}(\chi)=\emptyset
, which means, again by Lemma
6.7
, that
\varphi\models_{i}\chi
.

(ii^{\prime})\Rightarrow(i^{\prime})
Assume that, for every
L​TL
formula
\chi

expressing a safety property,
\psi\models_{i}\chi
implies

\varphi\models_{i}\chi
and consider the set
\textit{Clo}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi))
. It
is known
(
Baier and Katoen 2008
)
that the closure
\textit{Clo}(\mathcal{C})
of a set of
infinite traces
\mathcal{C}
corresponds to the
topological closure

of
\mathcal{C}
in the topological space

((2^{\mathsf{N}^{0}_{\mathsf{P}}})^{\omega},\tau)
,
where
\tau
is the topology
induced by the metric

\delta\colon(2^{\mathsf{N}^{0}_{\mathsf{P}}})^{\omega}\to\mathbb{R}_{0}
(where
\mathbb{R}_{0}
is the set of non-negative
real numbers), defined as follows:
\delta(\mathfrak{I},\mathfrak{I})=0
;

\delta(\mathfrak{I},\mathfrak{I}^{\prime})=2^{-n}
, with

n=\min\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid\mathcal{I}_{m}\neq\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{m}\}
, for

\mathfrak{I}\neq\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
. Moreover, as shown in
(
Maretic
et al. 2014
, Corollary
14)
, the following holds.

Claim 16.

For every
L​TL
formula
\varphi
, there is an
L​TL
formula
\varphi^{\prime}
such that
\textit{Clo}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\varphi))=\textit{Trace}_{i}(\varphi^{\prime})
.

We now require the following claim.

Claim 17.

For every
L​TL
formula
\chi
,
\chi
expresses a safety property
iff
\textit{Clo}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\chi))=\textit{Trace}_{i}(\chi)
.

Proof of Claim
17
.

Following
(
Baier and Katoen 2008
)
, we call
safety property
a set of
infinite traces
\mathcal{S}
such that, if
\mathfrak{I}\not\in\mathcal{S}
, then
there is
\mathfrak{F}\in\textit{Pre}(\mathfrak{I})
such that, for every

\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F})
, it holds that
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}\not\in\mathcal{S}
.
From the definitions, we have that
\chi
expresses a safety
property iff
\textit{Trace}_{i}(\chi)
is a safety property. It is known
that safety properties coincide with closed sets in the topological
space
((2^{\mathsf{N}^{0}_{\mathsf{P}}})^{\omega},\tau)

defined above
(
Baier and Katoen 2008
)
.
The claim then follows from the fact that a set is closed in a
topological space iff it is equal to its topological closure, and
the fact that the topological closure of a set
\mathcal{S}
in

((2^{\mathsf{N}^{0}_{\mathsf{P}}})^{\omega},\tau)

coincides with
\textit{Clo}(\mathcal{S})
.
∎

We can now finish the proof of the
(ii^{\prime})\Rightarrow(i^{\prime})
direction.
From Claim
16
we have that there exists an
L​TL
formula

\psi^{\prime}
such that
\textit{Clo}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi))=\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi^{\prime})
. Since

\textit{Clo}(\cdot)
is a closure operator, we have that

\textit{Clo}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi^{\prime}))=\textit{Clo}(\textit{Clo}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi)))=\textit{Clo}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi))=\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi^{\prime}).

Thus, by Claim
17
, we have that
\psi^{\prime}
expresses a
safety property. Moreover, the fact that
\textit{Clo}(\cdot)
is a closure
operator implies also that

\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi)\subseteq\textit{Clo}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi))=\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi^{\prime})
,
thus
\psi\models_{i}\psi^{\prime}
. From
(ii^{\prime})
, we obtain that

\varphi\models_{i}\psi^{\prime}
. Thus,

\textit{Trace}_{i}(\varphi)\subseteq\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi^{\prime})
and, by
Claim
16
,

\textit{Trace}_{i}(\varphi)\subseteq\textit{Clo}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi))
. Since
\textit{Pre}(\cdot)

is monotonic, i.e., for every set of infinite traces
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}

such that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, we have

\textit{Pre}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\textit{Pre}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
, it follows that

\textit{Pre}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\varphi))\subseteq\textit{Pre}(\textit{Clo}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi)))
.
Moreover, since
\varphi
is
\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}

, it holds that

\textit{Trace}_{f}(\varphi)\subseteq\textit{Pre}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\varphi))
, and since
\psi
is

\smash{\textsf{I}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}

, we have

\textit{Pre}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi))\subseteq\textit{Trace}_{f}(\psi)
. Finally, it can be
seen that
\textit{Pre}(\textit{Clo}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi)))=\textit{Pre}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi))
.
Hence, we obtain:

\textit{Trace}_{f}(\varphi)\subseteq\textit{Pre}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\varphi))\subseteq\textit{Pre}(\textit{Clo}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi)))=\textit{Pre}(\textit{Trace}_{i}(\psi))\subseteq\textit{Trace}_{f}(\psi).\qed

6.2.2.
Runtime verification maxims

We recall that in runtime verification the task is to evaluate a
property with respect to the current history (which is finite at each
given instant) of a dynamic system, and to check whether this property
is satisfied in all its possible future evolutions

(
Bauer
et al. 2010
;
Baader and
Lippmann 2014
;
De Giacomo et al. 2014b
)
.
Here we discuss the relationship between our semantic conditions and
the
maxims
for runtime verification in (variants of)
L​TL

introduced by
Bauer
et al. 2010

(
Bauer
et al. 2010
)
, which relate
finite trace semantics to the infinite case. Although the authors
consider also semantics for
L​TL
that allow for more than two
truth-values, in this section we will restrict our attention to
L​TL

interpreted on finite traces only.

Bauer
et al. 2010
suggest that any
L​TL
semantics to be used in
runtime verification should satisfy, for every
L​TL
formula
\varphi
,
the maxims
of
impartiality
and
anticipation
,
defined as follows.

Impartiality:

For every finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
,

\mathfrak{F}\models\varphi\Rightarrow\forall\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F}).\mathfrak{I}\models\varphi
and
\mathfrak{F}\not\models\varphi\Rightarrow\forall\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F}).\mathfrak{I}\not\models\varphi
.

Anticipation:

For every finite trace
\mathfrak{F}
,

\mathfrak{F}\models\varphi\Leftarrow\forall\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F}).\mathfrak{I}\models\varphi
and
\mathfrak{F}\not\models\varphi\Leftarrow\forall\mathfrak{I}\in\textit{Ext}(\mathfrak{F}).\mathfrak{I}\not\models\varphi
.

It can be easily seen that
L​TL
on finite traces does not satisfy,
for every
L​TL
formula
\varphi
, impartiality and anticipation. An
example of a formula that does not satisfy impartiality is
\Box^{+}P
,
whereas
\Diamond\top
violates anticipation.
However, the properties of impartiality and anticipation can be used
to define the corresponding sets of
L​TL
formulas that satisfy them.
Indeed, we have that impartiality is captured by

\textsl{L\!TL}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\forall}}$ })\cap\textsl{L\!TL}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Leftarrow\exists}}$ })
, while anticipation corresponds to

\textsl{L\!TL}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Leftarrow\forall}}$ })\cap\textsl{L\!TL}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\Rightarrow\exists}}$ })
. Therefore, any set of
L​TL

formulas satisfying both impartiality and anticipation
is included in the intersection

\textsl{L\!TL}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\forall}}$ })\cap\textsl{L\!TL}(\textnormal{$\smash{\textsf{F}_{\exists}}$ })
.
Concerning the possibility to
syntactically characterise these formulas, we have that, due to
Lemmas
4.11
and
4.12
, impartiality and
anticipation are not guaranteed to be preserved for

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!
-
or

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas.

7.
Conclusion

We investigated first-order temporal logic
on finite traces, by comparing its semantics with the usual one based
on infinite traces, and by studying the complexity of formula
satisfiability in some of its decidable fragments.

In an effort to systematically clarify the correlations between finite
vs. infinite reasoning we introduced various semantic conditions that
allow to formally specify when it is possible to blur the distinction
between finite and infinite traces.
Grammars for
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formulas satisfying some of these
conditions have been provided as well. In particular, we have shown
that for
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!
- and
\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas, equivalence over finite
and infinite traces coincide. Moreover, we have shown that, for
the class of
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
-formulas,
satisfiability is preserved from finite to infinite traces.

Concerning the complexity of the satisfiability problem in decidable
fragments on finite traces, we have shown that the constant-free
one-variable monadic fragment
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{1,mo}_{\not c}}
, the
one-variable fragment
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{1}}
, the monadic monodic
fragment
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{mo}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}}
, and the the two-variable
monodic fragment
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}^{2}_{\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$ \cr\kern 1.71793pt\raisebox{0.60275pt}{\scalebox{0.55}{$1$}}\crcr}}\rule{0.0pt}{4.52083pt}\kern-2.1097pt}}}
, while being

ExpSpace
-complete over arbitrary finite traces, lower down to

NExpTime
-complete when
interpreted on traces with at most
k
time points. Similar results
have been shown here for
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
, a temporal extension of the
description logic
\smash{\mathcal{ALC}}
, interpreted on finite or
k
-bounded
traces. Moreover, we proved that
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
restricted to global CIs is

ExpTime
-complete on traces with at most
k
time points.

Finally, we have lifted results related to the notion of insensitivity
to infiniteness
(
De
Giacomo et al. 2014a
)
, introduced in the planning context, to
our first-order setting. Moreover, we have analysed the connections
between notions from the verification literature (in particular,
safety
(
Sistla 1994
)
, as well as the runtime verification maxims of
impartiality and anticipation
(
Bauer
et al. 2010
)
), and our framework of
semantic conditions relating reasoning over finite and infinite
traces.

As future work,
we are interested in strengthening the results obtained in
Section
4
, so to obtain semantic and syntactic conditions
that are both necessary and sufficient (as opposed to sufficient only)
to characterise equivalences on finite and infinite traces. We
conjecture also that for
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!\forall
- and

\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!\exists
-formulas, i.e.,
{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
formulas in
negation normal form involving only one kind of reflexive temporal
operator (either
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\!
or
\mathbin{\mathcal{R}}^{+}\!
, respectively), the
equivalences on finite traces coincide with the equivalences on
infinite traces.

Moreover, we plan to to study the axiomatisability of
fragments of
first-order temporal logic on finite traces, and to apply the semantic
conditions introduced in this work to the analysis of monitoring
functions for runtime verification
(
Bauer
et al. 2010
;
Baader and
Lippmann 2014
;
De Giacomo et al. 2014b
)
. It
would also be interesting to determine the precise complexity of the
satisfiability problem in
\smash{\textsl{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}{\mathcal{ALC}}}
on finite traces with just global
CIs, as well as in those DLs from the temporal
DL-Lite
family for
which this problem remains open
(
Artale
et al. 2019c
)
.

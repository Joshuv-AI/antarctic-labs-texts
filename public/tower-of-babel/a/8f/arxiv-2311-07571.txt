Universal signatures of Majorana zero modes in critical Kitaev chains
Nicolas Laflorencie

Abstract
Many topological or critical aspects of the Kitaev chain are well known, with several classic results. In contrast, the study of the critical behavior of the strong Majorana zero modes (MZM) has been overlooked. Here we introduce two topological markers which, surprisingly, exhibit non-trivial signatures over the entire (1+1) Ising critical line. We first analytically compute the MZM fidelity ℱ MZM {\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM} –a measure of the MZM mapping between parity sectors. It takes a universal value along the (1+1) Ising critical line, ℱ MZM = 8 / π {\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}=\sqrt{8}/\pi , independent of the energy. We also obtain an exact analytical result for the critical MZM occupation number 𝒩 MZM {{\cal N}}_{\rm MZM} which depends on the Catalan’s constant 𝒢 ≈ 0.91596559 {\cal G}\approx 0.91596559 , for both the ground-state ( 𝒩 MZM = 1 / 2 − 4 ​ 𝒢 / π 2 ≈ 0.12877 {{\cal N}}_{\rm MZM}=1/2-4{\cal{G}}/\pi^{2}\approx 0.12877 ) and the first excited state ( 𝒩 MZM = 1 / 2 + ( 8 − 4 ​ 𝒢 ) / π 2 ≈ 0.93934 {{\cal N}}_{\rm MZM}=1/2+(8-4{\cal{G}})/\pi^{2}\approx 0.93934 ). We further compute finite-size corrections which identically vanish for the special ratio Δ / t = 2 − 1 \Delta/t=\sqrt{2}-1 between pairing and hopping in the critical Kitaev chain.

Generalities—

Much attention has recently turned towards emerging Majorana bound states in certain condensed matter systems

Lieb et al. 1961
and Pfeuty
Pfeuty 1970

who realized that non-trivial topological properties could emerge in such a simple quantum model when rephrased in fermionic language, nowadays referred to as the Kitaev chain

\displaystyle{\cal{H}}_{\rm K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j}\left([t_{j}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}+\Delta_{j}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j+1}^{{\dagger}}+{\rm{h.c.}}]-\mu_{j}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}\right)~~~~~

The total number of fermions

Strong Majorana zero modes—
The idea of

Majorana zero-mode (MZM), popularized by Fendley

Fendley 2016

L\gg 1

(2) it anti-commutes with the discrete symmetry (here the parity
\left\{{{\mathbb{P}}},\psi\right\}=0

(3) it is normalizable

Introducing two Majorana fermions at each site

b_{j}={\rm{i}}(c^{\dagger}_{j}-c^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{j})

{\cal{H}}_{\rm K}={\rm{i}}\sum_{j}\left(X_{j}b_{j}a_{j+1}-Y_{j}a_{j}b_{j+1}+h_{j}a_{j}b_{j}\right).

(3)

Assuming site-independent couplings (
h/X\geq 0

X>Y\geq 0

\psi_{a}=\frac{1}{N_{a}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Theta_{j}^{a}a_{j}\quad{\rm{and}}\quad\psi_{b}=\frac{1}{N_{b}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Theta_{j}^{b}b_{L+1-j},

(4)

\left|\Theta_{j}^{a,b}\right|\propto\exp\left(-\frac{j-1}{\xi_{\rm zm}}\right).

II
Majorana zero mode fidelity in the clean Kitaev chain

II.1
Relation between the fidelity and the parity gap

We first consider a uniform chain of
L
sites with free ends. The parity gap is the energy difference within the even-odd doublet, defined by

\Delta_{\rm parity}^{(n)}={\cal{S}}_{n}\Bigl(\langle\,n_{-}\,|{\cal{H}}|\,n_{-}\,\rangle-\langle\,n_{+}\,|{\cal{H}}|\,n_{+}\,\rangle\Bigr),

(10)

where the sign
{\cal S}_{n}
ensures that
\Delta_{\rm parity}^{(n)}
is positive. In fact, this term has a physical meaning: it reflects the sign of the end-to-end correlations between the boundary spins
not b
.

The even-odd mapping is rigorously given by

\frac{\psi_{a}+{\rm{i}}{\cal S}_{n}\mathbb{P}\psi_{b}}{2}|\,n_{p}\,\rangle={\cal F}_{\rm MZM}^{(n)}|\,n_{-p}\,\rangle-\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n}\alpha_{n^{\prime}}|\,n^{\prime}_{-p}\,\rangle,

(11)

yielding the following expression for
the parity gap

\Delta_{\rm parity}^{(n)}=\frac{{\cal{S}}_{n}\langle\,n_{-}\,|\left[{\cal H},\psi_{a}\right]|\,n_{+}\,\rangle-{\rm{i}}\langle\,n_{-}\,|\left[{\cal H},\psi_{b}\right]|\,n_{+}\,\rangle}{2{\cal F}_{\rm MZM}^{(n)}}~~~~~\,\,

(12)

where one recognizes the commutators between the Hamiltonian and the MZMs
\left[{\cal{H}},\psi_{a,b}\right]
. This expression will be used below to get exact forms of the MZM fidelity.

II.2
The transverse field Ising chain: analytical results

The special point where hopping equals pairing in the fermionic Kitaev chain Eq. (
1
) corresponds to the paradigmatic transverse field Ising (TFI) chain model which is a cornerstone of quantum statistical physics
Pfeuty 1970
;
Suzuki et al. 2013
:

\displaystyle{\cal{H}}_{\rm TFI}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j}\left(X\sigma_{j}^{x}\sigma_{j+1}^{x}+h\sigma_{j}^{z}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm{i}}\sum_{j}\left(Xb_{j}a_{j+1}+h_{j}a_{j}b_{j}\right).

Its relative simple form will allow us to provide some details on the analytical calculation of
{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}^{(n)}
.
The central quantities are the MZM commutators with
\cal H_{\rm TFI}
, here given by

\left[{\cal{H}}_{\rm TFI},\psi_{a}\right]=-\frac{2{\rm{i}}X}{{{N}}_{a}}\Gamma^{L}b_{L},\quad\left[{\cal{H}}_{\rm TFI},\psi_{b}\right]=\frac{2{\rm{i}}X}{{{N}}_{b}}\Gamma^{L}a_{1},

(14)

where
\Gamma=h/X
is the control parameter for the transition. If one expresses the boundary operators in the spin language
a_{1}=\sigma_{1}^{x}
and
b_{L}=-{\rm{i}}\mathbb{P}\sigma_{L}^{x}
, the parity gap Eq. (
12
) can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rm parity}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{X}{{\cal F}_{\rm MZM}^{(n)}}\Bigl(\frac{m_{1}^{s}}{N_{b}}+\frac{m_{L}^{s}}{N_{a}}\Bigr)\Gamma^{L}.

(15)

Here we have introduced the surface magnetization
Peschel 1984
;
Karevski 2000

m_{1,L}^{s}=\left|\langle\,n_{-}\,|\sigma_{1,L}^{x}|\,n_{+}\,\rangle\right|
whose magnitude is independent of the state
n
in the free-fermion case, so is the parity gap (see A+). Moreover, the MZM norms Eq. (
6
), both equal for clean chains, are given by

N_{a,b}=\sqrt{\frac{1-\Gamma^{2L}}{1-\Gamma^{2}}}\equiv{\mathsf{N}_{0}},

(16)

which hence leads to the following expression for the (energy-independent) MZM fidelity of finite clean TFI chains

{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}(L)=\frac{2Xm^{s}}{\Delta_{0}\mathsf{N}_{0}}\Gamma^{L},

(17)

where
\Delta_{0}
is the lowest energy gap,
\mathsf{N}_{0}=N_{a,b}
is the MZM norm in Eq. (
16
), and
m^{s}=m_{1,L}^{s}
is the surface magnetization, similar at each boundary of a clean chain.
Eq. (
17
) turns out to be valid across the entire phase diagram for finite chains, as we discuss now for the three physical regimes.

II.2.1
Disordered phase
{\Gamma>1}

The trivial (disordered) phase for
h>J
has a finite energy gap above the GS,

\Delta_{0}=2X(\Gamma-1)
, a diverging MZM norm
{\mathsf{N}}_{0}\sim\Gamma^{L-1}
, and a power-law vanishing surface magnetization
m^{s}\sim L^{-3/2}

Pfeuty 1970
. This, when put together, leads to the following power-law decay for the MZM fidelity in the topologically trivial phase

{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}^{\rm trivial}(L)\sim L^{-3/2},

(18)

which asymptotically matches the expected vanishing in the disordered phase. However, the relatively slow algebraic decay is not a trivial result, in a regime where one would have naively expected a faster exponential decay, see below Sec.
II.3
for a numerical check of this result.

II.2.2
Ordered regime
{\Gamma<1}

In contrast, in the topological (ordered) regime one can show that
m^{s}=1/\mathsf{N}_{0}=\sqrt{1-\Gamma^{2}}
. Moreover, the gap is exponentially small
Pfeuty 1970
;
Cabrera and Jullien 1987
;
Campostrini et al. 2015

\Delta_{0}=2X(1-\Gamma^{2})\Gamma^{L}+{\cal{O}}(\Gamma^{2L})
, which yields

{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}^{\rm topo}(L)=1-{\cal{O}}(\Gamma^{2L}),

(19)

as expected from the strong MZM definition. The finite-size corrections
\sim\exp(-2L/\xi_{\rm zm})
are exponentially small, controlled by the MZM localization length

\xi_{\rm zm}=\frac{1}{\ln\left(1/\Gamma\right)}.

(20)

II.2.3
Quantum critical point
{\Gamma=1}

Perhaps the most remarkable result concerns the quantum critical point (QCP) itself. Indeed, for
\Gamma=1
both the finite-size gap and the surface magnetization vanish,
\Delta_{0}\sim 1/L
and
m^{s}\sim 1/\sqrt{L}
, while the MZM norm
\mathsf{N}_{0}=\sqrt{L}
. This implies for Eq. (
17
) a finite critical fidelity, a results already visible in Fig.
1
(c) where a finite-size crossing occurs.
One can be more precise, using the critical scaling of the gap
Cabrera and Jullien 1987

\Delta_{0}(L)=Xk_{\rm min}(L),

(21)

where
k_{\rm min}
is the lowest mode (determined from the boundary conditions) that one can write

k_{\rm min}(L)=\frac{\pi}{L+\ell_{\rm eff}}.

(22)

OBC imply
Cabrera and Jullien 1987

\sin k(L+1)=-\sin kL
, which yields
\ell_{\rm eff}=\frac{1}{2}
.

The critical scaling of the surface magnetization can be obtained when the QCP is approached from the ordered side
\Gamma\to 1^{-}
, where
m^{s}\approx\sqrt{2/\xi_{\rm zm}}
for
large enough MZM localization length. At the QCP where
\xi_{\rm zm}
is formally infinite, it is standard to replace this length scale by the lattice size
L
. Interestingly we numerically find that the correct length scale to use is precisely the one which enters in the wave vector
k_{\rm min}
, i.e.
L+\ell_{\rm eff}=L+1/2
(see Appendix
B
). When plugged into Eq. (
17
), we then arrive at a rather simple expression for the critical fidelity

{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}^{\rm critical}(L)=\frac{\sqrt{8}}{\pi}+\left(\pi\sqrt{2}L\right)^{-1}+{\cal{O}}\left(L^{-2}\right),

(23)

which converges to
\sqrt{8}/\pi\approx 0.90032
, with finite-size algebraic corrections
\sim L^{-1}
.

III.1
Analytical results

We now discuss how universal this result is: we repeat a similar calculation for the more generic Kitaev-XY model Eq. (

III.1.1
Derivation of the critical fidelity

We restart from the definition of the parity gap Eq. (
12
). The first important quantity to compute are the commutators
\left[{\cal{H}}_{K},\psi_{a,b}\right]
, which are straightforward to get at criticality

\left[{\cal{H}},\psi_{a}\right]=\frac{-2{\rm{i}}X^{2}}{{{N}}_{a}(X-Y)}b_{L}\,;\,\left[{\cal{H}},\psi_{b}\right]=\frac{2{\rm{i}}X^{2}}{{{N}}_{b}(X-Y)}a_{1},

(24)

where the norm is

N_{a,b}={\mathsf{N}}_{0}=\frac{X}{X-Y}\sqrt{L-\ell^{\prime}_{\rm eff}}

(25)

with (see Appendix
C.1.2
)

\ell^{\prime}_{\rm eff}=\frac{y(2+y)}{1-y^{2}},\quad(y=Y/X).

(26)

Injecting this expression in Eq. (
12
) we arrive at

{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}^{\rm critical}(L)=\frac{2Xm^{s}}{\Delta_{0}\sqrt{L-\ell^{\prime}_{\rm eff}}}\,,

(27)

where as before
\Delta_{0}
is the lowest gap, and
m^{s}
the surface magnetization, both evaluated along the Ising quantum critical line. There, one can use
Campostrini et al. 2014

\Delta_{0}(L)\approx(X-Y)k_{\rm min}=\frac{(X-Y)\pi}{L+\ell_{\rm eff}}

(28)

and similar to the TFI case (see Appendix
B.3
)

m^{s}(L)\approx\frac{X-Y}{X}\sqrt{\frac{2}{L+\ell_{\rm eff}}},

(29)

which gives after replacing in Eq. (
27

\displaystyle{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}^{\rm critical}(L)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{8}}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{L+\ell_{\rm eff}}{L-\ell^{\prime}_{\rm eff}}}

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{8}}{\pi}+\frac{\sqrt{2}\left(\ell_{\rm eff}+\ell^{\prime}_{\rm eff}\right)}{\pi}L^{-1}+{\cal{O}}\left(L^{-2}\right).~~~

(31)

III.1.2
Finite-size corrections towards the universal value

Here a few comments are in order. First we find that the asymptotic critical value of the MZM fidelity
\sqrt{8}/\pi
turns out to be universal along the Ising quantum critical line. Then, the leading finite size corrections are proportional to
L^{-1}
, with a prefactor which involves
\ell^{\prime}_{\rm eff}
and
\ell_{\rm eff}
. We can check that the TFI result Eq. (
23
) is perfectly recovered using
\ell_{\rm eff}=1/2
and
\ell^{\prime}_{\rm eff}=0
at
Y=0
.
For finite
Y
however, it is more cumbersome to explicitly compute
\ell^{\prime}_{\rm eff}
. Interestingly, Ref.
Campostrini et al. 2014
gave the following ansatz for this effective length shift

\ell_{\rm eff}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{y(3+y)}{1-y^{2}}.

(32)

Plugging this ansatz in Eq. (
30
) we see that the finite size corrections change sign and cancel out exactly for
y+1=\sqrt{2}
, i.e. when
h_{c}/X=\sqrt{2}
.

The vanishing of finite-size corrections is due to the fact that the effective length scales emerging in the norm

\ell_{\rm eff}+\ell^{\prime}_{\rm eff}=0
. While the analytical expression for

in Eq. (

is plotted together with ED estimates extracted from the gap Eq. (

IV
The zero-mode occupation

IV.1
Simple expectation in the topological regime

The MZM occupation number for an eigenstate
{|\,n_{p}\,\rangle}
is

\displaystyle{\cal{N}}_{\rm MZM}^{(n_{p})}=\langle\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi\rangle_{n_{p}}=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(1+{\rm{i}}\langle\psi_{a}\psi_{b}\rangle_{n_{p}}\Bigr),

(33)

In the topological regime, the parity fidelity is
{\cal F}_{\rm MZM}=1
and therefore Eq. (
11
) becomes

|\,n_{-p}\,\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\psi_{a}+{\rm{i}}{\cal S}_{n}\mathbb{P}\psi_{b}\right)|\,n_{p}\,\rangle,

(34)

where
{\cal{S}}_{n}=\pm 1
encodes the sign of the correlation between the boundary spins in the state
{|\,n_{p}\,\rangle}
.
It is then straightforward to show that for any eigenstate
{|\,n_{p}\,\rangle}
the MZM occupation

{\cal{N}}_{\rm MZM}^{(n_{p})}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-p{\cal{S}}_{n}\right).

(35)

However, in the general case the MZM correlator
{\rm{i}}\langle\psi_{a}\psi_{b}\rangle_{n_{p}}
is not a simple object to directly evaluate, except very deep in the topological regime where it reduces to the end-to-end correlation, which for cat-states is
\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\sigma_{L}^{x}\rangle_{n_{p}}=\pm 1

Away from the
\Gamma\to 0

\displaystyle{\rm{i}}\langle\psi_{a}\psi_{b}\rangle_{n_{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{i}}}{N_{a}N_{b}}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\Theta_{j}^{a}\Theta_{j^{\prime}}^{b}\langle a_{j}b_{L+1-j^{\prime}}\rangle_{n_{p}},

(39)

it is expected to be
{\rm{i}}\langle\psi_{a}\psi_{b}\rangle_{n_{p}}=\pm 1
. This will no longer be true in the non-topological regime where the norms
N_{a,b}
diverge, and therefore
\langle\psi_{a}\psi_{b}\rangle_{n_{p}}\to 0
, yielding
{\cal{N}}_{\rm MZM}\to 1/2
, as observed in Fig.

IV.2
Free fermion formulation

The non-interacting Kitaev chain Hamiltonian is easily diagonalized by a Bogoliubov transformation (see Appendix), and takes the following quadratic form

{\cal{H}}_{\rm K}=2\sum_{m=1}^{L}\epsilon_{m}\left(\phi^{\dagger}_{m}\phi_{m}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}-\frac{1}{2}\right),

(40)

where
\phi_{m}
are new fermionic modes, and the single particle energies are such that
0\leq\epsilon_{1}\leq\epsilon_{2}\leq\ldots\epsilon_{L}
.
In the topological regime, for large enough chain length
L
one expects

{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}=1
, which implies that
\Psi^{\dagger}=\phi^{\dagger}_{1}
.
This will essentially be true for system sizes much larger that the correlation length
\xi_{\rm zm}
(i.e. the localization length of the MZM).
However, when
\xi_{\rm zm}
is not small as compared to the chain length
L
, the situation becomes quite interesting. Indeed, in this case the fidelity being different from unity, it is convenient to rewrite
\Psi^{\dagger}
as

\displaystyle\Psi^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}\phi_{1}^{\dagger}

(41)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 2}\left(\frac{{\cal{A}}_{m}+{\cal{B}}_{m}}{2}\right)\phi^{\dagger}_{m}+\left(\frac{{\cal{A}}_{m}-{\cal{B}}_{m}}{2}\right)\phi^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{m},

where
{\cal{A}}_{m}
and
{\cal{B}}_{m}
depends on both the MZM coefficients
\Theta_{j}^{a,b}
and the Bogoliubov transformation (see Appendix). This allows us to express the MZM occupation number as follows

\displaystyle{{\cal N}}_{\rm MZM}^{(n_{p})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}^{2}\langle\phi^{\dagger}_{1}\phi_{1}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}\rangle_{n_{p}}

(42)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 2}\left(\frac{1}{4}\left[{\cal{A}}_{m}-{\cal{B}}_{m}\right]^{2}+{\cal{A}}_{m}{\cal{B}}_{m}\langle\phi^{\dagger}_{m}\phi_{m}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}\rangle_{n_{p}}\right).~~~~~~

Hence, since many-body eigenpairs
\left\{{|\,n_{p}\,\rangle}\,;\,{|\,n_{-p}\,\rangle}\right\}
only differ by their occupation of the lowest single-particle mode
m=1
, the difference in their MZM occupation is simply given by

\displaystyle\delta{\cal{N}}_{\rm MZM}^{(n_{p})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\langle\,n_{p}\,|}\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi{|\,n_{p}\,\rangle}-{\langle\,n_{-p}\,|}\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi{|\,n_{-p}\,\rangle}

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-p{\cal{S}}_{n}{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}^{2},

(44)

where the sign prefactor depends on both the parity
p
and the the sign
{\cal{S}}_{n}
of the end-to-end spin correlation. This result is generally true across the entire phase diagram (even in the trivial regime, but only for finite
L
because otherwise the strong MZM operator is not defined anymore).

This can be numerically checked, see Fig.
4
where the MZM occupations have been computed for random high-energy eigenstates, with or without the
m=1
mode occupied. While
{\cal{N}}_{\rm MZM}\to 0~{\rm{or}}~1
in the topological regime for all energies, results are much more spread in the trivial regime where
{\cal{N}}_{\rm MZM}\to 1/2
slowly with increasing
L
. We also nicely check in Fig.
4
(c) that the difference Eq. (
43
) does not depend on the energy and is given by the square of the fidelity Eq. (
44
).

IV.3
Analytical derivation at criticality

Apart from the simple exact relation Eq. (
44
) which links the difference between MZM occupations and the fidelity, there is no easy way to analytically evaluate
{\cal{N}}_{\rm MZM}
for a generic eigenstate having the following form in the fermionic basis

{|\,n_{p}\,\rangle}=\prod_{m~{\rm{occupied}}}\phi^{\dagger}_{m}{|\,\rm GS\,\rangle}.

(45)

Nevertheless, at criticality, using Eq. (
33
) and Eq. (
39
), it is straightforward to arrive for the TFI chain model at

\displaystyle{\cal{N}}_{\rm critical}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(1+{\rm{i}}\langle\psi_{a}\psi_{b}\rangle\Bigr),

(46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(1+\frac{\rm{i}}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{L}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\langle a_{i}b_{j}\rangle\Bigr)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{L}\sum_{ij}\langle c^{\dagger}_{i}c^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{j}\rangle,

where we have used the simple form of the critical MZM at the Ising QCP of the TFI chain

\psi_{a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}a_{j}\quad{\rm{and}}\quad\psi_{b}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}b_{j}.

(47)

It turns out that one can obtain a rather simple analytical expression for the above sum, building on the fact that an
L
-sites XY chain is equivalent to two decoupled Ising chains with
L/2
sites
Fisher 1994
;
Iglói and Juhász 2008
. After a bit of manipulation we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}\langle c^{\dagger}_{i}c^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{L}\sum_{j=1}^{L/2}\frac{j}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi j}{L}\right)}

(48)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[L\to\infty]{}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{x}{\sin(x)}{\rm{d}}x

(49)

where one recognizes the Catalan’s constant
\cal G

Catalan 1865
, given by

{\cal{G}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{x}{\sin(x)}{\rm{d}}x\approx 0.915965594177\ldots

(50)

Therefore, the GS occupation of the MZM at criticality is expected to be

{\cal{N}}_{\rm critical}^{\rm(GS)}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}{\cal{G}}\approx 0.128773127289\ldots,

(51)

for the GS,
and for the first excited state
{|\,\rm FES\,\rangle}=\phi^{\dagger}_{1}{|\,\rm GS\,\rangle}

{\cal{N}}_{\rm critical}^{\rm(FES)}={\cal{N}}_{\rm critical}^{\rm(GS)}+{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}^{2}\approx 0.939342596428\ldots

(52)

V
Conclusions and discussions

V.1
Summary of the main results

In this work, we have revisited the topological transition occurring in the paradigmatic Kitaev chain model. We introduced two topological markers to probe the Majorana zero mode (MZM) physics: the MZM fidelity
{\cal F}_{\rm MZM}
, see Eq. (
9
), and the MZM occupation number
{\cal{N}}_{\rm MZM}
, Eq. (
8
). Whereas these two quantities have long been known to take simple values in both trivial and topological regimes, we have analytically shown that both markers take a non-trivial universal value, constant along the quantum (1+1) Ising critical line of the XY-Kitaev chain model, as summarized in Tab.
2
.

), and the MZM occupation number

, Eq. (

), for the ground-state (GS) and the first excited state (FES). They all take non-trivial values at the Ising quantum critical point between trivial and topological regimes, values that are universal for (1+1) Ising criticality.

The MZM fidelity, independent of the energy, has been analytically computed by establishing a connection with the surface magnetization
m^{s}
, the lowest-energy gap
\Delta_{0}
, and the norm of the commutator
{\cal{C}}_{{}_{\Psi}}=\Bigl|\left[{\cal{H}}_{K},\Psi\right]\Bigr|

{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}={m^{s}{\cal C}_{{}_{\Psi}}}/{\Delta_{0}}
. These three quantities exhibit algebraic behavior at criticality, which compensates and gives rise to a universal value, that we strikingly find to remain constant along the critical Ising line:
{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}^{\rm critical}=\sqrt{8}/\pi
. We have also computed the finite-size corrections to this asymptotic result: we analytically identified a singular point along the Ising critical line where such corrections vanish completely for the special value
\Delta/t=\sqrt{2}-1
of the ratio between pairing and hopping in the Kitaev chain. These predictions were successfully compared with exact diagonalization results (up to several thousand lattice sites).

Concerning the MZM occupation number
{\cal{N}}_{\rm MZM}
we have obtained exact results, universal along the Ising critical line, for two first energy levels, see Tab.
2
, and also for the zero-mode occupation difference
\delta{\cal{N}}_{\rm MZM}^{(n_{p})}
between all many-body eigenpairs
\left\{{|\,n_{p}\,\rangle}\,;\,{|\,n_{-p}\,\rangle}\right\}
that is given by
{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}^{2}
, see Eq. (
44
).

V.2
Open questions and possible future directions

The rather simple analytical expression of the MZM operators Eq. (
4
) has clearly helped us to derive exact results at criticality. In addition, the free-fermion nature allowed to numerically verify our analytical predictions with great accuracy using very large Kitaev chains, up to
L=16384
lattice sites. Nevertheless, there are still many open questions and directions for which we can imagine several extensions to go beyond the case of non-interacting clean Kitaev chains.

A first natural development concerns the effects of the environment, such as in open systems described by non-Hermitian models
Kawabata et al. 2019
;
Bergholtz et al. 2021
, or when quenched disorder is added directly to the Kitaev Hamiltonian
Motrunich et al. 2001
;
Bravyi and König 2012
;
DeGottardi et al. 2013
;
Hegde and Vishveshwara 2016
;
Gergs et al. 2016
;
Monthus 2018a
;
Lieu et al. 2018
;
Monthus 2018a
;
Levy and Goldstein 2019
;
Francica et al. 2023
. This last case has a particularly long and dense history, especially for the magnetic version of the problem, going back to the seminal work of D. S. Fisher
Fisher 1992
;
Fisher 1995
. Subsequent progress has been made
Young and Rieger 1996
;
Fisher and Young 1998
;
Iglói and Rieger 1998
;
Refael and Moore 2004
;
Iglói and Monthus 2005
;
Bonesteel and Yang 2007
;
Fidkowski et al. 2008
;
Vojta et al. 2009
;
Laflorencie 2022
;
Zabalo et al. 2023
, giving a fairly complete description of the non-interacting random problem, and especially of the very unusual properties of the infinite randomness criticality fixed point (IRFP)
Fisher 1995
;
Fisher 1994
. It would therefore be quite interesting and relevant to revisit the Kitaev chain model in the presence of quenched disorder using the topological markers introduced in the present paper, extending the results obtained at the clean (1+1) Ising critical point to the physics of the IRFP.

Another very important ingredient concerns the effect of interactions on MZMs
Fidkowski and Kitaev 2010
;
Fidkowski and Kitaev 2011
;
Hassler and Schuricht 2012
;
Thomale et al. 2013
;
Katsura et al. 2015
. In particular it is known that the construction of MZM operators is a very difficult task in the general interacting case
Goldstein and Chamon 2012
;
Kells 2015
;
Fendley 2016
;
Kemp et al. 2017
;
Else et al. 2017
;
Więckowski et al. 2018
;
Yates et al. 2020a
.
However, a distinction should be made between the integrable case, e.g. for the XYZ model where an exact construction has been shown possible in the clean case
Fendley 2016
, and the non-integrable case, e.g. provided by the interacting Ising-Majorana chain model
Kemp et al. 2017
;
Else et al. 2017
;
Kemp 2019
;
Więckowski and Ptok 2019
;
Kemp et al. 2020
;
Yates et al. 2020a
;
Pandey et al. 2023
where the MZMs are only
almost strong

Else et al. 2017
;
Kemp et al. 2017
;
Yates et al. 2020a
;
Yates et al. 2020b
. Nevertheless, it would be very interesting to consider the possibility of checking in one way or another the universality of our results against finite interaction along the self-dual Ising critical line of the interacting Majorana chain model
Rahmani et al. 2015
;
Chepiga and Laflorencie 2023a
.

A clearly challenging direction touches the combined effects of disorder and interactions which brings a full set of very exciting questions, some of them being related to the celebrated many-body localization (MBL) problem
Alet and Laflorencie 2018
;
Abanin et al. 2019
. For instance it was recently found using large-scale DMRG simulations that interactions are not relevant to the IRFP of the random TFI chain model at zero temperature
Chepiga and Laflorencie 2023b
, while Monthus had previously shown
Monthus 2018b
that a strong-disorder RG treatment generates higher-order terms that prevent a conclusion, in contrast to the XXZ case
Fisher 1994
. The disordered XYZ chain was also shown to display very rich physics both at zero temperature
Roberts and Motrunich 2021
, and at high energy
Slagle et al. 2016
;
You et al. 2016
.
In such a context, the possible existence and stability of MZMs and their fate at criticality in the presence of both disorder and interactions remains a fascinating subject, as it has been little discussed and has shown contrasting conclusions
Kells et al. 2018
;
Sahay et al. 2021
;
Wahl et al. 2022
;
Laflorencie et al. 2022
;
Hannukainen et al. 2023
;
Moudgalya et al. 2020
.

Finally, it is also worth mentioning the very interesting case of driven systems
Khemani et al. 2016
;
Yates et al. 2018
;
Bauer et al. 2019
which has strong experimental relevance
Yates and Mitra 2021
;
Mi 2022
;
Harle et al. 2023
;
Ling et al. 2023
.

A.1
Exact diagonalization of the Kitaev model

In the general case where translational invariance is absent, we use the Nambu formalism
Young and Rieger 1996
in order to solve the (not necessarily homogeneous) free-fermion Kiteav chain Hamiltonian Eq. (
1
).
Introducing
\varphi^{\dagger}=\left(c_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots c_{L}^{\dagger}\,c_{1}\cdots c_{L}\right)
, we arrive at the simple matrix representation of the Hamiltonian

{\cal{H}}_{\rm K}=\varphi^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}M&{\widetilde{M}}\\
-{\widetilde{M}}&-M\\
\end{pmatrix}\varphi.

(53)

M
and
{\widetilde{M}}
are
L\times L
matrices, such that

\displaystyle M_{j,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}=h_{j},

\displaystyle M_{j,j+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{j+1,j}=-\frac{t_{j}}{2}=-\frac{X_{j}+Y_{j}}{2},

(54)

while
M_{i,j}=0
elsewhere (unless periodic boundary conditions are used:
M_{1,L}=M_{L,1}=p{t_{L}}/{2}
, where
p=\pm 1
is the fermionic parity).
The matrix
{\widetilde{M}}
instead is anti-symmetric:

{\widetilde{M}}_{j,j+1}=-{\widetilde{M}}_{j+1,j}=-\frac{\Delta_{j}}{2}=-\frac{X_{j}-Y_{j}}{2},

(55)

and
{\widetilde{M}}_{i,j}=0\,\,{\rm{otherwise}}
.
The free-fermion problem can be solved by diagonalizing the
2L\times 2L
Hamiltonian matrix Eq. (
53
), and we then rewrite the quadratic Hamiltonian

{\cal{H}}_{\rm K}=2\sum_{m=1}^{L}\epsilon_{m}\left(\phi^{\dagger}_{m}\phi_{m}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}-\frac{1}{2}\right),

(56)

with single particle energies
0\leq\epsilon_{1}\leq\epsilon_{2}\leq\ldots\epsilon_{L}
.

A.2
Majorana zero mode operators and fidelity

The new fermionic modes are given by the Bogoliubov transformation

\phi_{m}^{\dagger}=\sum_{i=j}^{L}\left(u^{m}_{j}\,c_{j}^{\dagger}+v^{m}_{j}\,c_{j}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}\right)
,
with real
u^{m}_{j}
and
v^{m}_{j}
.
The inverse transformation is

c_{j}^{{\dagger}}=\sum_{m=1}^{L}\left(u_{j}^{m}\phi_{m}^{{\dagger}}+v_{j}^{m}\phi_{m}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}\right)
, from which
one can express the MZM operators Eq. (
4
)

\displaystyle\psi_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{a}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Theta_{j}^{a}a_{j}=\sum_{m=1}^{L}{\cal{A}}_{m}\left(\phi^{\dagger}_{m}+\phi^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{m}\right)

(57)

\displaystyle\psi_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{b}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Theta_{j}^{b}b_{L+1-j}={\rm{i}}\sum_{m=1}^{L}{\cal{B}}_{m}\left(\phi^{\dagger}_{m}-\phi^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{m}\right),

(58)

where

\displaystyle{\cal{A}}_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{a}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Theta_{j}^{a}\left(u^{m}_{j}+v^{m}_{j}\right)

(59)

\displaystyle{\rm{and}}\quad{\cal{B}}_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{b}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Theta_{j}^{b}\left(u^{m}_{L+1-j}-v^{m}_{L+1-j}\right).

(60)

The zero-energy Dirac fermion creation operator Eq. (
7
) is therefore given by

\Psi^{\dagger}=\sum_{m=1}^{L}\left(\frac{{\cal{A}}_{m}+{\cal{B}}_{m}}{2}\right)\phi^{\dagger}_{m}+\left(\frac{{\cal{A}}_{m}-{\cal{B}}_{m}}{2}\right)\phi^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{m},

(61)

from what we can simply express the MZM fidelity.
Indeed, since many-body eigenpairs only differ by their occupation of the lowest single-particle mode
m=1
one has

{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}=\frac{{\cal{A}}_{1}+{\cal{B}}_{1}}{2},

(62)

which further simplifies in the clean case where left and right boundaries are equivalent, yielding

{\cal{F}}_{\rm MZM}=\frac{1}{{\mathsf{N}}_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Theta_{j}^{a}\left(u^{1}_{j}+v^{1}_{j}\right).

(63)

Appendix B
Surface magnetization

B.1
Definition

The surface magnetization
Peschel 1984
;
Karevski 2000
is defined by

{{m}}_{1,L}^{s}=\langle\,n_{-}\,|\sigma_{1,L}^{x}|\,n_{+}\,\rangle.

(64)

The boundary magnetization can be expressed as follows

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}^{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{1}=c_{1}^{\dagger}+c_{1}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}

(65)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{L}\left(u^{m}_{1}+v^{m}_{1}\right)\left(\phi^{\dagger}_{m}+\phi^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{m}\right),

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{L}^{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm{i}}{\mathbb{P}}b_{L}=-{\mathbb{P}}\left(c_{L}^{\dagger}-c_{L}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}\right)

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\mathbb{P}}\sum_{m=1}^{L}\left(u^{m}_{L}-v^{m}_{L}\right)\left(\phi^{\dagger}_{m}-\phi^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{m}\right).

B.2
Invariance across the many-body spectrum

Since two partner states
{|\,n_{p}\,\rangle}
and
{|\,n_{-p}\,\rangle}
only differ by their occupation of the lowest single-particle mode
m=1
, one gets

m_{1}^{s}=u^{1}_{1}+v^{1}_{1},

(67)

which we take positive by convention. On the other hand, at the right boundary we have

m_{L}^{s}=\left(u^{1}_{L}-v^{1}_{L}\right)\langle\,n_{-}\,|\left(\phi^{\dagger}_{m}-\phi^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{m}\right)|\,n_{+}\,\rangle,

(68)

whose sign depends on the state.
Indeed, if the even (
p=+1
) many-body eigenstate
|\,n_{+}\,\rangle
has the lowest single-particle mode
m=1
which is empty (like the GS for instance), we have

m_{L}^{s}=u^{1}_{L}-v^{1}_{L}
(
=m_{1}^{s}
if the system is clean). Conversely if the mode
m=1
is occupied, one gets
m_{L}^{s}=v^{1}_{L}-u^{1}_{L}
(
=-m_{1}^{s}
again if the system is clean). We therefore see that the magnitude of the surface magnetization does not depend on the many-body energy
E_{n}^{\pm}
, but only on the coeffiient of the lowest (
m=1
) single particle mode. Only its relative sign between left and right boundaries oscillates across the spectrum, see also Tab.
1
.

B.3
Critical scaling of the surface magnetization

In the ordered regime, for
L\gg\xi_{\rm zm}
, one can use the mapping
{|\,n\,\rangle}_{+}=\psi_{a}{|\,n\,\rangle}_{-}
, that brings us to the following relation

\displaystyle m_{1}^{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\,n_{-}\,|\sigma_{1}^{x}\psi_{a}|\,n_{-}\,\rangle=\frac{1}{N_{a}},

(69)

where the MZM norm results from a geometrical sum of exponentials, see Eq. (
5
) and Eq. (
6
), simply yielding

\frac{1}{N_{a}}=\sqrt{1-\exp(-2/\xi_{\rm zm})}.

(70)

When approaching the critical point from the ordered regime, the length scale
\xi_{\rm zm}
becomes very large, and therefore the surface magnetization is given by

m_{1}^{s}\approx\sqrt{2/\xi_{\rm zm}}.

(71)

.

When
\xi_{\rm zm}\gg L
, it is rather standard to replace this length scale by the lattice size
L
in Eq. (
71
). In the main text, we have argued that the correct length scale to use is the one which also enters in the wave vector Eq. (
22
)
k_{\rm min}
, i.e.
L+\ell_{\rm eff}=L+1/2
.

Fig.
7
provides the numerical justification for this: ED data for
m_{1}^{s}(L)
are shown against
1/L
up to
L=10^{4}
for the transverse field Ising chain at criticality. A comparison with the analytical form
\sqrt{\frac{2}{L+\ell_{\rm eff}}}
is shown for
\ell_{\rm eff}=0
(open black circles) and
\ell_{\rm eff}=1/2
(open red squares). It is clearly visible that using a finite shift
\ell_{\rm eff}=1/2
gives a much better description (see inset).

Appendix C
MZM in the general case of the XY-Kitaev chain

C.1
Analytical expression in the ordered regime

C.1.1
Iterative procedure

Using Majoranas, the clean XY-Kitaev model reads

{\cal{H}}_{\rm XY-K}={\rm{i}}\sum_{j}\left(Xb_{j}a_{j+1}-Ya_{j}b_{j+1}+ha_{j}b_{j}\right).

(72)

Assuming simple linear combinations for the MZM operators

\Psi_{a}=\frac{1}{{{N}}_{a}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Theta_{j}\,a_{j}\quad{\rm and}\quad\Psi_{b}=\frac{1}{{{N}}_{b}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Theta_{j}\,b_{L+1-j},

(73)

and using
\left[{\cal{H}}_{\rm XY-K},a_{j}\right]=2{\rm i}\left(Xb_{j-1}-hb_{j}+Yb_{j+1}\right)
and
\left[{\cal{H}}_{\rm XY-K},b_{j}\right]=-2{\rm i}\left(Ya_{j-1}-ha_{j}+Xa_{j+1}\right)
, we iteratively arrive at the simple recursion relation for
\Theta_{j}=\Theta_{j}^{a,b}
:

\Theta_{j+1}=\frac{h}{X}\Theta_{j}-\frac{Y}{X}\Theta_{j-1},

(74)

such that

\displaystyle\left[{\cal{H}}_{\rm XY-K},\Psi_{a}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\rm{i}}\frac{1}{{{N}}_{a}}\left(Y\Theta_{L-1}-h\Theta_{L}\right)b_{L}

(75)

\displaystyle\left[{\cal{H}}_{\rm XY-K},\Psi_{b}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2{\rm{i}}\frac{1}{{{N}}_{b}}\left(Y\Theta_{L-1}-h\Theta_{L}\right)a_{1}.

(76)

One solve the recursion Eq. (
74
) with initial conditions
\Theta_{0}=0
and
\Theta_{1}=1
, restricting to positive couplings
h,\,X,\,Y\geq 0
, and
X\geq Y
(other cases can be easily derived).

C.1.2
MZM

We note in passing that the phase diagram of the XY-Kitaev chain model, shown in Fig.
1
(a), can simply be inferred from the existence of normalizable MZMs, which requires that the largest eigenvalue of the Eq. (
74
) has its modulus less that one, i.e. if
X+Y>h
. Contrary to the TFIM case, here the topological regime is richer as one can distinguish two types of MZM decays, incommensurate and commensurate.

(i) Incommensurate regime (
h^{2}<4XY
).

In this case,

\Theta_{j}=\frac{2X}{\sqrt{4XY-h^{2}}}\,\sin\left(\varphi j\right)\,{\rm{e}}^{-j/\xi_{\rm zm}}

(77)

displays oscillations and exponential decay, controlled by

\cos\varphi=\frac{h}{2\sqrt{XY}}\quad{\rm{and}}\quad\frac{1}{\xi_{\rm zm}}={\ln\sqrt{\frac{X}{Y}}}.

(78)

The MZM normalization factor
N_{a}=N_{b}\equiv{\mathsf{N}}_{0}
can be evaluated in the large
L
limit

\frac{1}{{{\mathsf{N}}}_{0}}\xrightarrow[L\to\infty]{}\sqrt{2}\sin\varphi\left[\frac{1}{1-\frac{Y}{X}}+\frac{\frac{Y}{X}-\cos\left(2\varphi\right)}{1-2\frac{Y}{X}\cos\left(2\varphi\right)+\left(\frac{Y}{X}\right)^{2}}\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(79)

(ii) Commensurate regime (
h^{2}\geq 4XY
).
In this case

\displaystyle\Theta_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{X}{\alpha h}\left(\frac{h}{2X}\right)^{j}\times\Bigl[\left(1+\alpha\right)^{j}-\left(1-\alpha\right)^{j}\Bigr]

(80)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[j\gg 1]{}\begin{cases}\frac{X}{\alpha h}{\rm{e}}^{-j/\xi_{\rm zm}}&\mbox{if }2\sqrt{XY}>h>X+Y\\
\infty&\mbox{if }h>X+Y,\end{cases}

where the edge mode localization length
\xi_{\rm zm}
is given by

\frac{1}{\xi_{\rm zm}}=\ln\left[\frac{2X}{(1+\alpha)h}\right],

(81)

and
\alpha=\sqrt{1-4XY/h^{2}}
.
The MZM normalization factor can be expressed in the large chain
L
limit:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{{\mathsf{N}}}_{0}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[L\to\infty]{}

\displaystyle 2\alpha\Bigl[\frac{1}{(1+\alpha)^{-2}-\left(\frac{h}{2X}\right)^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)^{-2}-\left(\frac{h}{2X}\right)^{2}}

(82)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(1-\alpha^{2})^{-1}-\left(\frac{h}{2X}\right)^{2}}\Bigr]^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad{\rm if}~\alpha\neq 0.

When
Y=0
(
\alpha=0
), we simply recover the TFIM result Eq. (
16
), i.e.
1/{\mathsf{N}}_{0}\xrightarrow[L\to\infty]{}\sqrt{1-h^{2}/X^{2}}
.

C.2
Critical behavior

(i) Critical commutators.

At criticality when
h=X+Y
, the MZM coefficients do not decay anymore and are given by

\Theta_{j}^{\rm critical}=\frac{X}{X-Y}\left[1-\left(\frac{Y}{X}\right)^{j}\right].

(83)

Therefore, the critical commutators are easy to compute: Eq. (
76
) thus becomes

\displaystyle\left[{\cal{H}}_{\rm XY-K},\Psi_{a}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2{\rm{i}}\frac{X^{2}}{{{N}}_{a}(X-Y)}\left[1-\left(\frac{Y}{X}\right)^{L+1}\right]b_{L}

\displaystyle\left[{\cal{H}}_{\rm XY-K},\Psi_{b}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\rm{i}}\frac{X^{2}}{{{N}}_{b}(X-Y)}\left[1-\left(\frac{Y}{X}\right)^{L+1}\right]a_{1},

(84)

which indeed rapidly converges (
Y<X
) at large enough
L
to the results Eq. (
24
) that we rewrite here

\displaystyle\left[{\cal{H}}_{\rm XY-K},\psi_{a}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-2{\rm{i}}X^{2}}{{{N}}_{a}(X-Y)}b_{L}

(85)

\displaystyle\left[{\cal{H}}_{\rm XY-K},\psi_{b}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2{\rm{i}}X^{2}}{{{N}}_{b}(X-Y)}a_{1}.

(86)

(ii) Critical MZM norm.

Building on the critical coefficients Eq. (
83
), one easily gets the norm (using
y=Y/X
)

\displaystyle N_{a,b}={\mathsf{N}}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left(\Theta_{j}^{\rm critical}\right)^{2}}

(87)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{X}{X-Y}\sqrt{L-\frac{y(2+y)}{1-y^{2}}}

thus yielding Eq. (
25
) with the effective length shift
\ell^{\prime}_{\rm eff}
given by Eq. (
26
).

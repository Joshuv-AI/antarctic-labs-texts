A regularized variance-reduced modified extragradient method for stochastic hierarchical games
Shisheng Cui, Uday V. Shanbhag, Mathias Staudigl

Abstract
We consider an N N -player hierarchical game in which the i i th player’s objective comprises of an expectation-valued term, parametrized by rival decisions, and a hierarchical term. Such a framework allows for capturing a broad range of stochastic hierarchical optimization problems, Stackelberg equilibrium problems, and leader-follower games. We develop an iteratively regularized and smoothed variance-reduced modified extragradient framework for iteratively approaching hierarchical equilibria in a stochastic setting. We equip our analysis with rate statements, complexity guarantees, and almost-sure convergence results. We then extend these statements to settings where the lower-level problem is solved inexactly and provide the corresponding rate and complexity statements. Our model framework encompasses many game theoretic equilibrium problems studied in the context of power markets. We present a realistic application to the virtual power plants, emphasizing the role of hierarchical decision making and regularization.

Introduction

In this paper we consider a class of stochastic hierarchical optimization problems and games, generalizing many learning problems involving sequential optimization. Consider a collection of
N

We let

Our contributions and related work

Hierarchical optimization, games and uncertain generalizations.

To date, hierarchical optimization has been studied under the umbrella of
bilevel programming
[
16
,
17
]
and mathematical programs with
equilibrium constraints (MPECs)
[
49
,
55
]
.
Algorithmic schemes for resolving MPECs where the lower-level problem
is an optimization problem, or a variational inequality, have largely emphasized either implicit
approaches
[
55
]
or regularization/penalization-based
techniques
[
49
,
47
,
34
]
.
Yet, there appears to have been a glaring lacuna in non-asymptotic rate and
complexity guarantees for resolving hierarchical optimization and their
stochastic and game-theoretic variants. This gap has been
partially addressed in the recent papers
[
14
]
and
[
13
]
. Both papers
present variance-reduced solution strategies for various versions of
hierarchical optimization problems and games, respectively, relying on variance
reduction via a sequence of increasing mini-batches. Finite-time and almost
sure convergence to solutions is proved under convexity/monotonicity
assumptions on the problem data. However, the framework for monotone games
in
[
13
]
requires exact solutions of lower-level problems,
significantly impacting its efficient implementation in large-scale settings.
We complement this literature by developing a novel regularized
smoothed variance reduction method for the family of hierarchical games
(
P
), building on a disciplined operator splitting approach.
Notably, we provide an inexact generalization allowing for random
error-afflicted lower-level solutions, addressing a significant shortcoming
in
[
13
]
. Specifically, in this paper we improve
[
13
]
along
two important dimensions: (i) First, we allow for inexact resolution of
lower-level problems to accommodate large-scale stochastic follower problems;
(ii) Second, we provide a novel variance-reduction framework for addressing
this problem. Despite the need for inexactness, our statements match the
state-of-the-art both in terms of rate and oracle complexity. Algorithmically,
these advancements are achieved via a novel stochastic operator splitting
approach that combines ideas from iterative smoothing and regularization

[
71
]
, with modern variance reduction approaches originating in
machine learning
[
29
]
.

Zeroth-order optimization, smoothing, and regularization.

Zeroth-order (gradient-free) optimization is being increasingly embraced for
solving machine learning problems where explicit expressions of the gradients
are difficult or infeasible to obtain. In hierarchical optimization problems,
this is particularly relevant when solutions of the lower level problem are
injected into the leader’s upper-level problem. In machine learning, this
problem is known as approximating the hypergradient. Various techniques for
estimating this object have been studied recently, ranging from truncated von
Neuman series
[
26
]
and fully first-order methods

[
44
]
. Instead of computationally expensive first-order (or
higher) information about the problem data, we develop an online stochastic
approximation approach based solely on function evaluations in order to
approximate the directional derivative of the coupling function between the
upper and the lower level. Approximating the directional derivative of the thus
obtained implicit function has a long history
[
64
]
and has been
employed for resolving stochastic optimization

[
45
,
68
,
69
]
and variational
inequality problems
[
70
,
71
]
.

Variance Reduction.

Variance reduction is a commonly employed method exploiting the finite-sum
structure of variational problems arising in machine learning and engineering.
The classical stochastic variance reduced gradient (SVRG)

[
37
]
is embedded within a double loop structure and
tailored to the prototypical finite-sum structure in empirical risk
minimization. Indeed, in the classical SVRG formulation full gradients are
computed “from time-to-time” in the outer loop while cheap variance reduced
gradients are used in the frequently activated inner loop subroutine. This
construction has been extended to saddle-point problems and stochastic monotone
inclusions in
[
56
]
. Extensions to monotone
mixed
-variational
inequality problems were recently provided in

[
2
,
1
]
and
[
10
]
. In
contrast, we consider stochastic hierarchical games over general sample spaces,
complicated by the presence of nested optimization problems embodied by the
interaction between leaders and followers. While the assumptions we make in
this work allow us to recast the problem as a mixed variational inequality,
several challenges persist. First, subgradients of
g_{i}(\cdot,\mathbf{y}_{i}(\cdot))

are unavailable; Second, enlisting smoothing approaches requires

\mathbf{y}_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})
, unavailable in closed form; Third, we are not restricted to
finite-sum regimes and allow for general sample spaces by employing
increasingly large batch-sizes to approximate the gradient in the outer loop.

Solutions to mixed VIs with expectation-valued operators
have been developed recently in cases where the random variable takes values in
a finite set, and/or when the VI is derived from a zero-sum game displaying a
finite-sum structure
[
2

methods to the mixed VI (

following facts:

(i)
L

. The assumptions made thus far do not

Hence, a direct application of gradient, or extragradient methods, is a
difficult task in our setting. To cope with this technical difficulty, we
develop a smoothing approach, yielding a family of approximating models

(ii) Randomness in the operator

principle, one could apply splitting techniques to deal with the non-smooth
function via a proximal smoothing. However, this approach requires

to be proximable for which we have no a-priori guarantee since
it is the value function of the leader, derived
from the solution of the follower. With these preparatory remarks in mind, we now explain the design of our algorithmic solution strategy for the hierarchical game problems (

A variance reduced forward-backward-forward algorithm for hierarchical games

an equilibrium of the hierarchical game (

constructed as follows. For

V^{\eta}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

(3.1)

V^{\eta}(\mathbf{x})=(V_{i}^{\eta}(\mathbf{x}))_{i\in\mathcal{I}},\mbox{ where }V^{\eta}_{i}(\mathbf{x})\triangleq V_{i}(\mathbf{x})+\eta\mathbf{x}_{i}\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}.

Tikhonov regularization is a classical tool to obtain stronger convergence results in numerical schemes. It has been examined for deterministic
[
38
]
and stochastic equilibrium problems
[
41
,
71
]
.

when queried at a given point

Iterative Regularization methods

Lemma

3.2

The Algorithm

Our algorithm for the hierarchical game
setting consists of a double-loop structure: The outer loop allows the

N
players to make multiple independent queries of the stochastic oracle

\hat{V}_{1}(\cdot,\xi),\ldots,\hat{V}_{N}(\cdot,\xi),
and draw multiple independent samples from the surface of a unit sphere, allowing for the simulation of the random estimator (
3.2
). However, since the multiple calls are a negative entry
on the oracle complexity of the method, we impose some control on the number of
mini-batches to be constructed by the agents. Within the inner-loop subroutine, the agents only receive single samples from their stochastic oracles, and employ this new information in an extragradient-type algorithm. We give a precise construction in the following paragraphs.

3.2.1

The outer loop

Let
t=0,\ldots,T-1
be the iteration counter for the outer loop. We denote by
b_{t}\in\mathbb{N}
the pre-defined sample rate defining the number of random variables each player is allowed to generate in round
t
. Specifically, each player generates an iid sample
\xi^{1:b_{t}}_{i,t}\triangleq\{\xi_{i,t}^{(s)};1\leq s\leq b_{t}\}
and constructs the mini-batch estimator

(3.5)

\bar{V}^{t}_{i}\triangleq\frac{1}{b_{t}}\sum_{s=1}^{b_{t}}\hat{V}_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{t},\xi_{i,t}^{(s)}),

Let
\bar{V}^{t}=(\bar{V}_{1}^{t},\ldots,\bar{V}_{N}^{t})
. A similar
assumption is made to obtain point-estimators of the gradient of the implicit
function
h_{i}(\cdot)
. Hence,

\mathbf{W}^{1:b_{t}}_{i,t}\triangleq\{\mathbf{W}^{(s)}_{i,t};1\leq s\leq b_{t}\}
denotes an i.i.d.
sample of
b_{t}
random vectors drawn uniformly at random from
\mathbb{S}_{i}

and define the mini-batch estimator

Given the inputs

Discussion

The key innovation of the scheme
\VRHGS
lies in the combination of smoothing (to allow for hierarchy), regularization (to contend with ill-posedness), and variance-reduction (to mitigate bias) within a stochastic forward-backward-forward framework. Our double-loop solution strategy mimics the computational architecture of SVRG, which takes a full gradient sample of the finite sum problem “once in a while", while performing frequent single-sample updates in between. Our method, adapted to general probability spaces, proceeds similarly: The “shadow sequence"
\mathbf{z}^{(t)}_{i,k+1/2}
uses costly mini-batch estimators computed in the outer loop; these are maintained in memory while executing the inner loop (i.e. only “once in a while" updated). The additional forward steps to obtain the iterates
\mathbf{z}^{(t)}_{i,k+1}
make use of fresh one-shot estimators of the payoff gradient and the finite difference estimator. All steps are overlaid by a Tikhonov regularization, while smoothing facilitates accommodation with hierarchical objectives. From a computational perspective, our scheme performs a single projection onto the leaders’ feasible set
\mathcal{X}_{i}
. This can save considerably on computational time in cases where the projection operator is costly to evaluate, and constitutes a major difference compared to viable alternative algorithmic schemes like the extragradient or optimistic mirror descent. Hence, our method reduces the sample-complexity of recent mini-batch variance reduction techniques for stochastic VIs
[
8
]
, while concomitantly reducing the computational bottlenecks of double-call algorithms
[
35
,
39
]
by lifting one projection step. Finally, similar rate and complexity statements emerge when allowing for inexact generalizations that allow for
\epsilon
-approximate solutions of lower-level problem.

4.4

Inexact generalization

A key shortcoming in the implementation of
\VRHGS
is the need for exact solutions of the lower-level problem. Naturally, when the solution map
\mathbf{y}_{i}(\cdot)
corresponds to the solution of a large-scale stochastic optimization/VI problem, this claim is hard to justify. In this section, we allow for an inexact solution
\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})
associated with an error level
\varepsilon
, defined as

(4.2)

\mathbb{E}[\norm{\by^{\eps}(\bx)-\by(\bx)}|\mathbf{x}]\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{ a.s.}

Under the inexact lower level solution
\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\varepsilon}
, we let
h_{i}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\triangleq g_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}_{i}))
.

Remark 4.2
.

We can obtain the inexact solution
\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}_{i})
with rather efficient numerical methods. First, we can parallelize the computation since the problems
\VI(\phi_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\cdot),\mathcal{Y}_{i})
are uncoupled. Second, the mapping
\phi_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\cdot)
is assumed to be strongly monotone. Hence, we can solve the VI to
\varepsilon
-accuracy with exponential rate using for instance the method in
[
12
]
.

As in the exact regime, we assume that player
i
has access to an oracle with which she can construct a spherical approximation of the gradient of the implicit function
h_{i}^{\varepsilon}
. Hence, for given
\delta>0
, we let

h^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n_{i}}}h^{\varepsilon}_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}+\delta\mathbf{w})\frac{\differential\mathbf{w}}{{\mathbf{Vol}}_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{n_{i}})}.

We denote the resulting estimators by
H^{\delta,\varepsilon}_{i,\mathbf{x}_{i}}(\mathbf{W}_{i})=n_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\nabla_{(\mathbf{W}_{i},\delta)}h^{\varepsilon}_{i}(i,\mathbf{x}_{i})
, and the mini-batch versions
H^{\delta,\varepsilon,b}_{i,\mathbf{x}_{i}}\triangleq\frac{1}{b}\sum_{s=1}^{b}H^{\delta,\varepsilon}_{i,\mathbf{x}_{i}}(\mathbf{W}_{i}^{(s)}).
With these concepts in hand, we can adapt
\VRHGS
to run exactly the same way as described in Algorithm
1
and Algorithm
2
, replacing the appearance of quantities involving
h_{i}
with its inexact version
h^{\varepsilon}_{i}

Proof of the Main Theorem

6.1

Analysis of the exact scheme

The proof on the finite time-complexity estimate starts by a technical derivation of an energy-type inequality that gives us an upper bound on the change of the energy function
\frac{1}{2}\norm{\bz^{(t)}_{k+1}-\bx}^{2}

4.3
. From there, we proceed as in the standard analysis of stochastic approximation schemes
[
52
]
, and derive an upper bound on the gap function of the mixed variational inequality.

7

Conclusion

In this work, we proposed a new solution approach to solve a fairly large class of stochastic hierarchical games. Using a combination of smoothing, zeroth-order gradient approximation, and iterative regularization, we develop a novel variance reduction method for stochastic VIs affected by general stochastic noise. We demonstrate consistency of the method by proving that solution trajectory converges almost surely to a particular equilibrium of the game and derive a
\mathcal{O}(1/T)
convergence rate in terms of the expected gap function, using a suitably defined averaged trajectory. This rate result is robust to inexact solutions of the lower level problem of the follower and aligns with state-of-the-art variance reduction methods tailored to finite-sum problems. Furthermore, our approach is based on Tseng’s splitting technique, which shares the same number of function calls as the popular extragradient method, but saves on one projection step. This implies that our scheme reduces the oracle complexity relative to vanilla mini-batch approaches and at the same time reduces the computational bottlenecks in every single iteration. This leaves open the door for many future investigations, involving bias and non-convexities, that we leave for future research.

(

\text{find }\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{K}\text{ such that }\langle\phi(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\rangle\geq 0\qquad\forall\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{K}.

We note in passing that if

\MVI(\phi,r)
is equivalent to the generalized equation

(A.1)

0\in\phi(\mathbf{x})+\partial r(\mathbf{x}),

where
\partial r(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|r(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\geq r(\mathbf{x})+\langle p,\mathbf{x}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}\rangle\quad\forall\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
is the subgradient of
r
at
\mathbf{x}
.

In order to measure the distance of a candidate point to the solution set we introduce as a merit function for
\MVI(\phi,r)
the gap function

\Gamma_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{K}}\left(\langle\phi(\mathbf{z}),\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}\rangle+r(\mathbf{x})-r(\mathbf{z})\right),

where
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a compact subset to handle the possibility of unboundedness of
\dom(r)
. As proven in
[
54
]
, this restricted version of the gap function is a valid measure as long as
\mathcal{K}
contains any solution of
\MVI(\phi,r)
.

For existence and uniqueness questions of variational problems, we usually rely on monotonicity and continuity properties of the map
\phi
.

Definition A.2
.

A mapping
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
\mu
-monotone if there exists
\mu\geq 0
such that

\langle\phi(\mathbf{x})-\phi(\mathbf{y}),\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\rangle\geq\mu\norm{\bx-\by}^{2}\qquad\forall\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

A
0
-monotone mapping is called monotone.

Fact A.3

(Solution Convexity of Monotone VIs)
.

Consider the problem
\VI(\phi,\mathcal{K})
, where
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is monotone on
\mathcal{K}\subset\dom(\phi)
, and
\mathcal{K}
is a closed convex set. Then, the solution set

\mathcal{S}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{K}|\langle\phi(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\rangle\geq 0\qquad\forall\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{K}\}

is closed and convex. If
\phi
is
\mu
-monotone with
\mu>0
on
\mathcal{K}
, then
\mathcal{S}
is a singleton.

See
[
18
, Theorem 2F.1, 2F.6]
for a proof of this Fact.
We now extend the scope of variational inequalities and introduce parameters into the problem data. This is effectively the lower level problem solved by the followers in our hierarchical game model. Let
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty compact convex set, and
\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
a closed convex set. The object of study is the parameterized generalized equation

(A.2)

0\in\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+\NC_{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathbf{y})

for a given function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the normal cone

(A.3)

\NC_{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathbf{y})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varnothing&\text{if }\mathbf{y}\notin\mathcal{Y},\\
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}|\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}}\langle\xi,\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{y}\rangle\leq 0\quad\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}\}&\text{if }\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}.\end{array}\right.

Specifically, we are interested in understanding the properties of the solution mapping

(A.4)

\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}|0\in\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+\NC_{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathbf{y})\}

This is a subclass of classical problems, thoroughly summarized in
[
18
]
, and dating back to the landmark paper
[
59
]
. The interested reader can find proofs of the facts stated below, as well as much more information on this topic in these references. We point out that many of the strong assumption made below can be relaxed, at the price of more complicated verification steps. Our aim is to present a simple and not entirely unrealistic set of verifiable conditions under which our model assumptions provably hold; A more general result can be found in
[
67
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Fact A.4
.

Consider problem (
A.2
) with the following assumptions on the problem data:

•

\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is compact and convex,

•

\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is closed convex,

•

\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is strictly differentiable on
\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{Y}
, where
\mathcal{K}
is an open set containing
\mathcal{X}
;

•

\phi(x,\cdot)
is strongly monotone for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
.

Then
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{x})=\{\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})\}
, and
\mathbf{y}(\cdot)
is Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{X}
.

A.3

Tikhonov Regularization

Tikhonov regularization is a classical method in numerical analysis aiming for introducing additional stability into a computational scheme. Given problem
\MVI(\phi,r)
and
\eta>0
, we define the Tikhonov regularized mixed variational inequality problem as
\MVI(\phi^{\eta},r)
, in which the operator is defined as
\phi^{\eta}(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\phi(\mathbf{x})+\eta\mathbf{x}
. It is easy to see that if
\phi
is
0
-monotone, then
\phi^{\eta}
is
\eta
-monotone (i.e. strongly monotone). Hence, for every
\eta>0
, problem
\MVI(\phi^{\eta},r)
has a unique solution
\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta)
. The first result we are going to demonstrate is that the net
\{\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta)\}_{\eta\geq 0}
is bounded.

Proposition A.5
.

Consider problem
\MVI(\phi,r)
admitting a nonempty solution set
\SOL(\phi,r)
. Then, for all
\eta>0
, we have

\norm{\bx^{\ast}(\eta)}\leq\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\SOL(\phi,r)}\norm{\bx}.

Proof.

Using the characterization of a point
\mathbf{x}^{\ast}\in\SOL(\phi,r)
as a solution of a monotone inclusion, we have

-\phi^{\eta}(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta))\in\partial r(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta))\text{ and }-\phi(\mathbf{x}^{\ast})\in\partial r(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}).

Since
\partial r
is maximally monotone, it follows

\displaystyle\langle\phi^{\eta}(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta))-\phi(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}),\mathbf{x}^{\ast}-\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta)\rangle\geq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\langle\phi(\mathbf{x}^{\ast})-\phi(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta)),\mathbf{x}^{\ast}-\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta)\rangle\leq\eta\langle\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta),\mathbf{x}^{\ast}-\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta)\rangle.

Since
\phi(\cdot)
is monotone, it follows
\langle\phi(\mathbf{x}^{\ast})-\phi(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta)),\mathbf{x}^{\ast}-\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta)\rangle\geq 0
, so that

\displaystyle 0\leq\langle\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta),\mathbf{x}^{\ast}-\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta)\rangle=\langle\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta),\mathbf{x}^{\ast}\rangle-\norm{\bx^{\ast}(\eta)}^{2}.

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies
\norm{\bx^{\ast}(\eta)}\leq\norm{\bx^{\ast}}
. Since
\mathbf{x}^{\ast}
has been chosen arbitrarily, the claim follows.

\blacksquare

We next study the asymptotic regime in which
\eta\to 0^{+}
. Since the net
\{\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta)\}_{\eta>0}
is bounded, the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem guarantees the existence of a converging subsequence
\eta_{t}\to 0
such that
\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(t)\equiv\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta_{t})\to\hat{\mathbf{x}}
. Since
\partial r
is maximally monotone, the set
\gr(\partial r)
is closed in the product topology
[
5
]
. Hence,
\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\dom(\partial r)
. Moreover, for all
t

(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(t),-\phi(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(t))-\eta_{t}\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(t))\in\gr(\partial r)\qquad\forall t>0.

Continuity and Proposition
A.5
, together with the just mentioned closed graph property, yields for
t\to\infty
,

(\hat{\mathbf{x}},-\phi(\hat{\mathbf{x}}))\in\gr(\partial r)\qquad\forall t>0.

The next claim follows.

Proposition A.6
.

Every accumulation point of the Tikhonov sequence
\{\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta)\}_{\eta>0}
defines a solution of the problem
\MVI(\phi,r)
.

We next deduce a non-asymptotic estimate of the Tikhonov sequence. Let
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
be a positive sequence of regularization parameters satisfying
\eta_{t}\downarrow 0
. Exploiting again the variational characterization of the unique solutions
\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}\equiv\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta_{t})
, we have

-\phi^{\eta_{t-1}}(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1})\in\partial r(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1})\text{ and }-\phi^{\eta_{t}}(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t})\in\partial r(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}).

By monotonicity of
\partial r
, we obtain

\langle\phi^{\eta_{t-1}}(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1})-\phi^{\eta_{t}}(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}),\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1}\rangle\geq 0.

Hence, by monotonicity of
\phi
, it follows

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\langle\phi(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1})-\phi(\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}),\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1}\rangle\geq\langle\eta_{t}\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}-\eta_{t-1}\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\eta_{t}\langle\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1}\rangle+(\eta_{t}-\eta_{t-1})\langle\mathbf{x}_{t-1}^{\ast},\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}-\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1}\rangle.

Whence,

\eta_{t}\norm{\bx^{\ast}_{t}-\bx^{\ast}_{t-1}}^{2}\leq(\eta_{t}-\eta_{t-1})\langle\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1},\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t-1}-\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}\rangle\leq(\eta_{t}-\eta_{t-1})\norm{\bx^{\ast}_{t-1}}\cdot\norm{\bx^{\ast}_{t}-\bx^{\ast}_{t-1}}.

The next claim follows:

Proposition A.7
.

For any monotonically decreasing sequence
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\infty)
satisfying
\eta_{t}\downarrow 0
we have

\left(\frac{\eta_{t}-\eta_{t-1}}{\eta_{t}}\right)\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\SOL(\phi,r)}\norm{\bx}\geq\norm{\bx^{\ast}(\eta_{t})-\bx^{\ast}(\eta_{t-1})}.

Lastly, we provide an exact localization result on the Tikhonov sequence.

Proposition A.8
.

Let
\eta_{t}\downarrow 0
and
\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}\equiv\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta_{t})
the corresponding sequence of solutions to the regularized problem
\MVI(\phi^{\eta_{t}},r)
. Then,
\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\SOL(\phi,r)}\norm{\bx}
exists and is uniquely attained and
\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}\to\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\SOL(\phi,r)}\norm{\bx}
.

Proof.

The set
\SOL(\phi,r)
agrees with the zeros of the monotone inclusion problem (
A.1
). Since
\partial r
is maximally monotone, and
\phi
is continuous and monotone, it follows from Corollary 24.4 in
[
5
]
that the set of zeros is closed and convex. Hence, the problem
\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\SOL(\phi,r)}\norm{\bx}
admits a unique solution, proving the first part of the Proposition. For the second part, let
\eta_{t}\downarrow 0
and
\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}\equiv\mathbf{x}^{\ast}(\eta_{t})
the corresponding sequence of unique solution of
\MVI(\phi^{\eta_{t}},r)
. Since
\{\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}\}_{t}
is bounded (Proposition
A.5
), we can pass to a converging subsequence. By an abuse of notation, omitting the relabeling, let us take the full sequence to be converging with limit point
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
. By Proposition
A.6
, we know
\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\SOL(\phi,r)
. In particular,
\norm{\hat{\bx}}\geq\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\SOL(\phi,r)}\norm{\bx}
. But then, in view of Proposition
A.5
, it follows
\norm{\hat{\bx}}=\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\SOL(\phi,r)}\norm{\bx}
. Since the accumulation point
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
is arbitrary, the entire sequence
\{\mathbf{x}^{\ast}_{t}\}_{t}
converges with limit
\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\SOL(\phi,r)}\norm{\bx}
.
\blacksquare

\blacksquare

A.4

Smoothing

We let
\mathbb{B}_{n}\triangleq\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\norm{\bx}\leq 1\}
denote the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The unit sphere is denoted by
\mathbb{S}_{n}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\norm{\bx}=1\}
. The volume of the unit ball with radius
\delta
with respect to
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure is
{\mathbf{Vol}}_{n}(\delta\mathbb{B}_{n})=\delta^{n}\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)}
, where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Gamma function. Therefore, the measure

\differential\mu_{n}(\bf{u})\triangleq\mathbf{1}_{\{\bf{u}\in\mathbb{B}_{n}\}}\frac{\differential\bf{u}}{{\mathbf{Vol}}_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{n})}
defines a uniform distribution on the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recall that
{\mathbf{Vol}}_{n-1}(\delta\mathbb{S}_{n})=\frac{n}{\delta}{\mathbf{Vol}}_{n}(\delta\mathbb{B}_{n})
for all
\delta>0
. Given
\delta>0
, and
\mathcal{X}
be a closed convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We define the set
\mathcal{X}_{\delta}\triangleq\mathcal{X}+\delta\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

Definition A.9
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. The spherical smoothing of
h
is defined by

(A.5)

h^{\delta}(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\frac{1}{{\mathbf{Vol}}_{n}(\delta\mathbb{B}_{n})}\int_{\delta\mathbb{B}_{n}}h(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u})\differential\mathbf{u}=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}h(\mathbf{x}+\delta\mathbf{w})\frac{\differential\mathbf{w}}{{\mathbf{Vol}}_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{n})}.

The following properties of the spherical smoothing can be deduced from
[
53
, Section 9.3.2]
; see also
[
14
, Lemma 1]
.

Fact A.10
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function that is
L_{h}
-Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{X}_{\delta}
. Then, for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

(A.6)

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{h^{\delta}(\bx)-h^{\delta}(\by)}\leq L_{h}\norm{\bx-\by},

(A.7)

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{h^{\delta}(\bx)-h(\bx)}\leq L_{h}\delta.

Using Stoke’s Theorem, one can easily show that

(A.8)

\begin{split}\nabla h^{\delta}(\mathbf{x})&=\frac{n}{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}h(\mathbf{x}+\delta\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}\frac{\differential\mathbf{v}}{{\mathbf{Vol}}_{n-1}(\mathbb{S}_{n})}\\
&=\frac{n}{\delta}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{W}\sim\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})}[\mathbf{W}h(\mathbf{x}+\delta\mathbf{W})]\\
&=\frac{n}{\delta}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{W}\sim\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})}[\mathbf{W}\left(h(\mathbf{x}+\delta\mathbf{W})-h(\mathbf{x})\right)],\end{split}

where
\mathbf{W}\sim\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})
means that
\mathbf{W}
is uniformly distributed on
\mathbb{S}_{n}
. A simple application of Jensen’s inequality shows then that the spherical smoothing admits a Lipschitz continuous gradient, whose modulus depends on the smoothing parameter
\delta
.

Fact A.11
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function that is
L_{h}
-Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{X}_{\delta}
. Then, for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

(A.9)

\norm{\nabla h^{\delta}(\bx)-\nabla h^{\delta}(\by)}\leq\frac{L_{h}n}{\delta}\norm{\bx-\by}.

The smoothed function and its gradient is used to construct a variance reduced gradient estimator for the implicit cost function of player
i
in our hierarchical game problem.

A.5

Random sampling

In this section we explain how to construct a random oracle to sample the gradient of the smoothed implicit function
h_{i}^{\delta}
. Let
b\in\mathbb{N}
denote the batch size. In each round of the algorithm, agent
i
enters the outer loop procedure, which asks this agent to construct a
n_{i}\times b
matrix
\mathbf{W}_{i}^{1:b}=[\mathbf{W}_{i}^{(1)};\ldots;\mathbf{W}_{i}^{(b)}]
satisfying
\norm{\bW_{i}^{(s)}}=1
for all
1\leq s\leq b
. To construct the uniformly distributed unit vector
\mathbf{W}_{i}^{(s)}
, we generate
n_{i}
random numbers
w_{i}^{s}(k)\sim\mathsf{N}(0,1)
, and then compute

\mathbf{W}_{i}^{(s)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{n_{i}}w_{i}^{s}(k)^{2}}}[w_{i}^{(s)}(1);\ldots;w_{i}^{(s)}(n_{i})]^{\top}\quad s=1,\ldots,b.

The outcome of this procedure is a
n_{i}\times b
random matrix
\mathbf{W}^{1:b}_{i}=[\mathbf{W}_{i}^{(1)};\ldots;\mathbf{W}^{(b)}_{i}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}\times b}
with
\norm{\bW_{i}^{(s)}}=1
for all
1\leq s\leq b
. In fact, since the Gaussian is spherical, the columns of this matrix will be iid uniformly distributed on
\mathbb{S}_{n_{i}}
. Having constructed these random vectors, each agent constructs a gradient estimator involving the finite-difference approximation of the directional derivative

H^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}(\mathbf{W}_{i})\triangleq n_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\nabla_{(\mathbf{W}_{i},\delta)}h_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}),,\quad\nabla_{(\mathbf{w},\delta)}h_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\triangleq\frac{h_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}+\delta\mathbf{w})-h_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})}{\delta},

as well as its Monte-Carlo variant (with some abuse of notation)

H^{\delta,b}_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}\triangleq\frac{1}{b}\sum_{s=1}^{b}H^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}(\mathbf{W}_{i}^{(s)}).

Note that, since
h_{i}(\cdot)
is
L_{1,i}
-Lipschitz and directionally differentiable on the convex compact set
\mathcal{X}
, we have

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\nabla_{(\mathbf{w},\delta)}h_{i}(\mathbf{x})=h^{\circ}_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{w})

as well
\absolutevalue{h^{\circ}_{i}(\bx,\bw)}\leq L_{1,{i}}
for all
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{S}_{n_{i}}
. To understand the statistical properties of this estimator, we need the next Lemma. To simplify the notation, we omit the index of player
i
.

Lemma A.12
.

Suppose
h
is
L_{h}
-Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{X}_{\delta}\triangleq\mathcal{X}+\delta\mathbb{B}
. Define
\mathtt{e}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{W}^{1:b})\triangleq H^{\delta,b}_{\mathbf{x}}-\nabla h^{\delta}(\mathbf{x})
, where
\mathbf{W}^{(i)}
is an i.i.d sample drawn uniformly from the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}_{n}
, i.e.
\mathbf{W}^{1:b}\sim\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})^{\otimes b}
. Then

(a)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{W}^{1:b}\sim\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})^{\otimes b}}[\mathtt{e}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{W}^{1:b})]=0
;

(b)

\norm{H_{\bx}^{\delta}(\bw)}^{2}\leq L_{h}^{2}n^{2}
for all
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
;

(c)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{W}^{1:b}\sim\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})^{\otimes b}}[\norm{\ce_{\bx}(\bW^{1:b})}^{2}]\leq\frac{n^{2}L^{2}_{h}}{b}.

Proof.

By linearity of the expectation operator and independence, we see

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{W}^{1:b}\sim\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})^{\otimes b}}[H^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{W}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{W}^{(b)})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b}\sum_{s=1}^{b}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{W}\sim\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})}[H^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{W})]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{W}\sim\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})}[H^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{W})]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:gradsmoothing}}}{{=}}\nabla h^{\delta}(\mathbf{x}).

This proves part (a). Part (b) is a simple consequence of the following Lipschitz argument:

\norm{H^{\delta}_{\bx}(\bw)}^{2}=\left(\frac{n}{\delta}\right)^{2}\norm{\bw}^{2}\absolutevalue{h(\bx+\delta\bw)-h(\bx)}^{2}\leq L^{2}_{h}n^{2},

using
\norm{\bw}=1
. For part (c), observe that for the random i.i.d. sample
\mathbf{W}^{1:b}=\{\mathbf{W}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{W}^{(b)}\}
taking values in
\mathbb{S}_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{\ce_{\bx}(\bW^{1:b})}^{2}=\frac{1}{b^{2}}\norm{\sum_{i=1}^{b}\left(\frac{n}{\delta}H_{\bx}^{\delta}(\bW^{(i)})-\nabla h^{\delta}(\bx)\right)}^{2}=\frac{1}{b^{2}}\norm{\sum_{i=1}^{b}X_{i}}^{2},

where
X_{i}\triangleq\frac{n}{\delta}H_{\mathbf{x}}^{\delta}(\mathbf{W}^{(i)})-\nabla h^{\delta}(\mathbf{x})
are i.i.d zero-mean random variables, almost surely bounded in squared norm. By independence, we have
\mathbb{E}[\langle X_{i},X_{j}\rangle]=0
for
i\neq j
, so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{W}^{1:b}\sim\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})^{\otimes b}}\left[\norm{\ce_{\bx}(\bW^{1:b})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{W}\sim\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})}\left[\norm{H_{\bx}^{\delta}(\bW)}^{2}\right]-\norm{\nabla h^{\delta}(\bx)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{b}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{W}\sim\mathsf{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})}\left[\norm{H^{\delta}_{\bx}(\bW)}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{n^{2}L_{h}^{2}}{b}.

\blacksquare

\blacksquare

A.6

Inexact Implementation

In the main text we have assumed that the solution of the lower level problem is available exactly. In practice, this is difficult to guarantee, particularly when the lower-level problem is large and possible stochastic. Motivated by this concern, we outline a modification of our hierarchical game solver in this section, reliant on access to an
\varepsilon
-inexact solution of the lower-level problem.

Definition A.13
.

Let
\delta_{0}>0
be given and set
\mathcal{X}_{i,\delta_{0}}=\mathcal{X}_{i}+\delta_{0}\mathbb{B}_{n_{i}}
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
. Given
\varepsilon>0
and
i\in\mathcal{I}
, we call
\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}_{i}:\mathcal{X}_{i,\delta}\to\mathcal{Y}_{i}
an
\varepsilon
-solution of the lower level problem
\VI(\phi_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\cdot),\mathcal{Y}_{i})
if

\mathbb{E}[\norm{\by_{i}^{\eps}(\bx_{i})-\by_{i}(\bx_{i})}|\mathbf{x}_{i}]\leq\varepsilon\quad\quad\text{a.s. }

We note that such a solution is immediately available by employing
\mathcal{O}(1/\varepsilon^{2})
steps of a single-sample stochastic approximation scheme for resolving VI
(\phi_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\cdot),\mathcal{Y}_{i})
. Similarly, if projection onto
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
is a computationally costly operation, then a geometrically increasing mini-batch scheme provides a similar oracle complexity but requires only
\mathcal{O}(\ln(1/\eps^2))
steps. (cf.
[
46
]
).

Under the inexact lower level solution
\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\varepsilon}
, we let
h_{i}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}_{i})=g_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}_{i}))
denote the resulting implicit function coupling leader
i
and the associated follower. As in the exact regime, we assume that player
i
has access to an oracle with which she can construct a spherical approximation of the gradient of the implicit function
h_{i}^{\varepsilon}
. Hence, for given
\delta>0
, we let

h^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n_{i}}}h^{\varepsilon}_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}+\delta\mathbf{w})\frac{\differential\mathbf{w}}{{\mathbf{Vol}}_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{n_{i}})}.

Using the notation for Section
A.5
, we denote the resulting estimators by
H^{\delta,\varepsilon}_{i,\mathbf{x}_{i}}(\mathbf{W}_{i})\triangleq n_{i}\mathbf{W}_{i}\nabla_{(\mathbf{W}_{i},\delta)}h^{\varepsilon}_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})
, while the corresponding mini-batch counterpart as

H^{\delta,\varepsilon,b}_{i,\mathbf{x}_{i}}\triangleq\frac{1}{b}\sum_{s=1}^{b}H^{\delta,\varepsilon}_{i,\mathbf{x}_{i}}(\mathbf{W}_{i}^{(s)}),

Next, we derive some bounds of the thus constructed estimator. To reduce notational clutter, we omit the label of player
i
in the next Lemma.

Lemma A.14
.

Let Assumption
2
hold true. Then, for all
\delta,\varepsilon>0
and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

(A.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\norm{H^{\delta,\eps}_{\bx}(\bW)}^{2}\right|\mathbf{x}]\leq 3\left(\frac{n}{\delta}\right)^{2}(2L^{2}_{2}\varepsilon^{2}+L_{1}^{2}\delta^{2})\text{ and }

(A.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\norm{H^{\delta,\eps}_{\bx}(\bW)-H^{\delta}_{\bx}(\bW)}^{2}|\mathbf{x}\right]\leq\left(\frac{2L_{2,h}n\varepsilon}{\delta}\right)^{2}

almost surely.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\norm{H^{\delta,\eps}_{\bx}(\bW)}^{2}=\left(\frac{n}{\delta}\right)^{2}\norm{\bW\left[h^{\eps}(\bx+\delta\bW)-h(\bx+\delta\bW)+h(\bx+\delta\bW)+h(\bx)-h(\bx)-h^{\eps}(\bx)\right]}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\left(\frac{n}{\delta}\right)^{2}\left[\norm{\bW(h^{\eps}(\bx+\delta\bW)-h(\bx+\delta\bW)}^{2}+\norm{\bW(h^{\eps}(\bx)-h(\bx))}^{2}+\norm{\bW(h(\bx+\delta\bW)-h(\bx))}^{2}\right].

We bound each of the three terms separately. First, by Assumption
2
.(iv), we note

\displaystyle\norm{\bW(h^{\eps}(\bx+\delta\bW)-h(\bx+\delta\bW)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{g(\bx+\delta\bW,\by^{\eps}(\bx+\delta\bW))-g(\bx+\delta\bW,\by(\bx+\delta\bW))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq L^{2}_{2}\norm{\by^{\eps}(\bx+\delta\bW)-\by(\bx+\delta\bW)}^{2}.

Consequently, using Definition
A.13
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\norm{\bW(h^{\eps}(\bx+\delta\bW)-h(\bx+\delta\bW)}^{2}|\mathbf{x}\right]\leq L^{2}_{2}\varepsilon^{2}.

Second,

\displaystyle\norm{\bW(h^{\eps}(\bx)-h(\bx))}^{2}\leq\norm{g(\bx,\by^{\eps}(\bx))-g(\bx,\by(\bx)}^{2}\leq L^{2}_{2}\norm{\by(\bx)-\by^{\eps}(\bx)}^{2}.

Invoking again Definition
A.13
, it follows

\mathbb{E}\left[\norm{\bW(h^{\eps}(\bx)-h(\bx))}^{2}|\mathbf{x}\right]\leq L^{2}_{2}\varepsilon^{2}.

Third, by Assumption
2
.(ii) and since
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{S}
, we conclude

\displaystyle\norm{\bW(h(\bx+\delta\bW)-h(\bx))}^{2}\leq L_{1}^{2}\norm{\delta\bW}^{2}=L^{2}_{1}\delta^{2}.

Summarizing all these bounds, we obtain (
A.10
). To show (
A.11
), we first note

\displaystyle\norm{H^{\delta,\eps}_{\bx}(\bW)-H^{\delta}_{\bx}(\bW)}

\displaystyle\leq n\norm{\bW\frac{g(\bx+\delta\bW,\by^{\eps}(\bx+\delta\bW))-g(\bx+\delta\bW,\by(\bx+\delta\bW))}{\delta}}

\displaystyle+n\norm{\bW\frac{g(\bx,\by^{\eps}(\bx))-g(\bx,\by(\bx))}{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{2,h}n}{\delta}\norm{\by^{\eps}(\bx+\delta\bW)-\by(\bx+\delta\bW)}+\frac{nL_{2,h}}{\delta}\norm{\by^{\eps}(\bx)-\by(\bx)}.

Taking expectations on both sides, it follows

\mathbb{E}\left[\norm{H^{\delta,\eps}_{\bx}(\bW)-H^{\delta}_{\bx}(\bW)}|\mathbf{x}\right]\leq\frac{2L_{2,h}n\varepsilon}{\delta}.

\blacksquare

\blacksquare

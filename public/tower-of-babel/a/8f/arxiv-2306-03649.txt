Abstract
. In this paper we obtain several properties of translating solitons for a general class of extrinsic geometric curvature flows given by a homogeneous, symmetric, smooth non-negative function γ \gamma defined in an open cone Γ ⊂ ℝ n \Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} . The main results are tangential principles, nonexistence theorems for closed and entire solutions, and a uniqueness result that says that any strictly convex γ \gamma -translator defined on a ball with a single end 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}^{2} -asymptotic to a cylinder is the “bowl”-type solution found in [ 9 ] up to vertical translations .

Introduction

Geometric evolution equations for hypersurfaces have had a remarkable development over the last decades. This kind of equations lead to interesting non-linear PDE’s that have been used to solve important open questions in mathematics and physics.

In this paper we are interested in hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
that evolve under translations in a fixed unitary direction when an extrinsic geometric flows is applied to them.

More precisely, let
M_{0}=F_{0}(M)
be an immersed hypersurface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, we will say that
M_{0}
evolves under a
\gamma
-flow if there exist a
1
-parameter family of immersions
F:M\times[0,T)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
such that

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left<{\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial t}(x,t),\nu(x,t)}\right>=\gamma(\lambda(x,t)),\mbox{ in }M\times(0,T),\\
F(x,0)=F_{0}(x),\end{cases}

where
\left<{\cdot,\cdot}\right>
and
\nu(x,t)
are the euclidean inner product and the inward unit normal vector of
M_{t}:=F(M,t)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, respectively. In addition, the function
\gamma
is evaluated in the principal curvature vector
\lambda(x,t)=(\lambda_{1}(x,t),\ldots,\lambda_{n}(x,t))
of
M_{t}
related to
\nu(x,t)
and satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\gamma:\Gamma\to(0,\infty)
is a smooth symmetric function (i.e.:
\gamma(\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\lambda_{\sigma}(n))=\gamma(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})

\Gamma

\Gamma_{+}:=\left\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\lambda_{i}>0\right\}\subset\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

(2)

\gamma

Recall that the principal curvatures of

where

Proof of Thoerem
1.2

Now we are going to prove that there is no closed
\gamma
-translator in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
such that
\gamma:\Gamma\to(0,\infty)
satisfies properties
1
-
3
with
\alpha>0
, where
\Gamma=\left\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n};\gamma(\lambda)>0\right\}
, and the principal curvatures of the
\gamma
-translator belong to
\Gamma
.

The proof is based in the form of the equation that satisfies the height function of the
\gamma
-translator.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let us assume that
\Sigma
is a compact
\gamma
-translator without boundary in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, and without loss of generality, we will also assume that
0\in\Sigma
.

Next, we choose normal coordinates centred at
p\in\Sigma
given by
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset T_{p}\Sigma
. This means that
\left\{e_{i}\right\}
is an orthornomal basis of eigenvalues of the shape operator of
\Sigma
(i.e.:
h^{i}_{j}(p)=h_{ij}(p)=\lambda_{i}(p)\delta_{j}^{i}
where
h^{i}_{j}
,
h_{ij}
and
\delta_{j}^{i}
are the coefficients of the shape operator, second fundamental form and delta Kronecker of
\Sigma
, respectively).

Then, it follows that the height function
u(p)=\left<{p,e_{n+1}}\right>
satisfies the following equations

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}u=\left<{e_{i},e_{n+1}}\right>,\>|\nabla u|=\left|e_{n+1}^{\top}\right|,

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}u=\left<{\nabla_{j}e_{i},e_{n+1}}\right>=\left<{\nabla_{j}e_{i},\nu}\right>\left<{\nu,e_{n+1}}\right>=-h_{ij}\left<{\nu,e_{n+1}}\right>,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\gamma}u:=\dfrac{\partial\gamma}{\partial h_{ij}}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}u=-\alpha\gamma\left<{\nu,e_{n+1}}\right>=-\alpha\left<{\nu,e_{n+1}}\right>^{2}=\alpha(\left|e_{n+1}^{\top}\right|^{2}-1).

Here
(\cdot)^{\top}
denotes the orthogonal projection to
T_{p}\Sigma
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
. In addtion, in the third line we used property
2
to write the term
\dfrac{\partial\gamma}{\partial h_{ij}}h_{ij}=\alpha\gamma
. We note that, by property
3
, it follows that the operator
\Delta_{\gamma}
is elliptic when the principal curvatures belong to
\Gamma
.

In consequence,
u
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle\Delta_{\gamma}u-\alpha\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+\alpha=0.

Finally, by compactness and continuity,
u
must attains an interior minimum (recall that
\partial\Sigma=\emptyset
). However, this is impossible since
\Delta_{\gamma}u\geq 0
and
\nabla u=0
at the point where the minimum of
u
is reached. Therefore, there cannot exist any closed
\gamma
-translator such that its principal curvatures belong to
\Gamma
.
∎

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}^{\>ij}(s)\dfrac{\partial h^{i}_{j}}{\partial s}(s)-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+|Du_{s}|^{2}}}

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}^{\>ij}(s)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\left[\left(\dfrac{\delta_{ik}}{\sqrt{1+|Du_{s}|^{2}}}-\dfrac{D_{i}u_{s}D_{k}u_{s}}{(1+|Du_{s}|^{2})^{3/2}}\right)D_{kj}u_{s}\right]+\dfrac{\left<{Du_{s},Dv}\right>}{(1+|Du_{s}|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}

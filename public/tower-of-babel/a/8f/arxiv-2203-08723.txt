Improved short-segment detection statistic for continuous gravitational waves Preprint: APS/123-QED
P. B. Covas 1 , R. Prix 1

Abstract
Continuous gravitational waves represent one of the long-sought types of signals that have yet to be detected. Due to their small amplitude, long observational datasets (months-years) have to be analyzed together, thereby vastly increasing the computational cost of these searches. All-sky searches face the most severe computational obstacles, especially searches for sources in unknown binary systems, which need to break the data into very short segments in order to be computationally feasible. In this paper, we present a new detection statistic that improves sensitivity by up to 19 % 19\text{\,}\mathrm{\%} compared to the standard ℱ \mathcal{F} -statistic for segments shorter than a few hours.



I
Introduction

Continuous gravitational waves (CWs) are long-lasting and nearly monochromatic
signals, expected to be emitted by asymmetric rotating neutron stars

Prix 2016
;
Haskell and Schwenzer 2021
.
While there have been many searches for CWs performed to date, none has yet been able to make a detection.
One of the main difficulties in finding CWs is their small amplitude, expected
to be many orders of magnitude below the noise floors of current detectors.
In order to accumulate a detectable signal-to-noise ratio, large datasets
(spanning months–years) therefore have to be combined in a search, which
complicates the analysis and results in a vast increase in computing cost due to
the astronomically large number of templates to be searched.

We can divide searches for CWs into three main categories, ordered by increasing
computational cost: (i) targeted searches, (ii) directed searches, and (iii)
all-sky searches.
Directed and all-sky searches typically cannot combine all the data coherently
due to the unfeasible computational cost this would entail, and instead have to
employ semi-coherent algorithms by breaking the data into shorter segments
(e.g., see
Brady and Creighton 2000
;
Prix and Shaltev 2012
).
Although the total signal power does not depend on the number of segments used,
the background (i.e., noise) distribution worsens when the number of segments
increases.
Therefore, while using more segments dramatically alleviates the computational
cost of the search, it also reduces the resulting sensitivity.
The optimal search setup consists of using the longest segments possible within
a given computational cost budget
Prix and Shaltev 2012
.

There are various ways to compute semi-coherent detection statistics, but one
widely-used approach we focus on here consists in summing coherent

\mathcal{F}
-statistics
Jaranowski et al. 1998
;
Cutler and Schutz 2005
across segments, known as
the
StackSlide
approach (see also
Dreissigacker et al. 2018
for more
discussion and an overview of different methods currently in use).
The corresponding
\mathcal{F}
-statistic segments used have so far always been longer than

\sim$11\text{\,}\mathrm{h}$
.
At the other end of the spectrum are the “power” statistics, which directly
use Fourier power over short segments, typically no longer than

\sim{$1\text{\,}\mathrm{h}$}
, as the per-segment statistic.

A recent search
Covas et al. 2022
has bridged this gap for the first
time by employing a semi-coherent
\mathcal{F}
-statistic on short segments of

900\text{\,}\mathrm{s}
, designed to improve the sensitivity of an all-sky search
for signals from unknown neutron stars in binary systems.
Using the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic on such short segments has resulted in unexpected and
previously-unknown numerical difficulties, with the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic becoming
singular in many segments, especially those containing data from only one
detector.
This degeneracy can be easily understood from the underlying antenna-pattern
matrix becoming ill-conditioned for short coherence times and the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic
relying on inverting this matrix.
Intuitively this corresponds to a failure of the maximum-likelihood estimation
of the four unknown amplitude parameters (describing the “
+
” and
“
\times
” polarizations) for short coherence times, during which the
detector hardly moves.

In an attempt to fix this singularity by constructing a well-behaved
“fallback” statistic we have discovered a new detection statistic that is not
just well-behaved in the short-segment limit but turns out to be
more
sensitive
than the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic for segments shorter than a few hours, even
when the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic is far from numerically singular.

This paper is organized as follows: in Sec.
II
we
introduce the standard
\mathcal{F}
-statistic; Sec.
III
discusses
the singular limit of the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic for short segments, and
Sec.
IV
introduces the new statistic. Section

IV
characterizes the sensitivity improvement of the new
statistic compared to the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic, and Sec.
V

provides numerical tests for these analytical results and further characterizes
the new statistic, followed by conclusions in Sec.
VI
.
Appendix

discusses an alternative short-segment
statistic construction that turns out to be unsuitable for multi-detector
setups.

II.1
The continuous-waves likelihood

We can parameterize the gravitational-wave signal from a non-axisymmetric
rotating neutron star by four amplitude parameters
\mathcal{A}

The four amplitude parameters consist of the overall signal amplitude

\iota

The phase-evolution parameters consist of the frequency of the signal

The CW signal depends non-linearly on the physical amplitude parameters

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{1}

\displaystyle=A_{+}\cos\phi_{0}\cos 2\psi-A_{\times}\sin\phi_{0}\sin 2\psi\,,

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2}

\displaystyle=A_{+}\cos\phi_{0}\sin 2\psi+A_{\times}\sin\phi_{0}\cos 2\psi\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{3}

\displaystyle=-A_{+}\sin\phi_{0}\cos 2\psi-A_{\times}\cos\phi_{0}\sin 2\psi\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{4}

\displaystyle=-A_{+}\sin\phi_{0}\sin 2\psi+A_{\times}\cos\phi_{0}\cos 2\psi\,,

\displaystyle h_{1}^{X}

\displaystyle\equiv a^{X}(t)\,\cos\phi^{X}(t),\quad h_{2}^{X}\equiv b^{X}(t)\,\cos\phi^{X}(t),

(3)

\displaystyle h_{3}^{X}

\displaystyle\equiv a^{X}(t)\,\sin\phi^{X}(t),\quad h_{4}^{X}\equiv b^{X}(t)\,\sin\phi^{X}(t),

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}^{\mu}x_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}^{\mu}\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{A}^{\nu}\,,

II.2
The coherent
\mathcal{F}
-statistic

The likelihood ratio in Eq. (
4
) can be analytically maximized
over the four amplitude coordinates
\mathcal{A}^{\mu}
, yielding the maximum-likelihood estimates

\mathcal{A}^{\prime\mu}=\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}\,x_{\nu}\,,

(15)

where
\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}
is the inverse of the antenna-pattern matrix. Substituting
back into Eq. (
4
) defines the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic as

2\mathcal{F}(x;\lambda)\equiv\ln\mathcal{L}(x;\lambda;\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=x_{\mu}\,\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}x_{\nu}\,.

(16)

It will be useful to write this out more explicitly in terms of the
antenna-pattern-matrix elements of Eq. (
9
) as

2\mathcal{F}=\frac{(x_{1}^{2}+x_{3}^{2})B+(x_{2}^{2}+x_{4}^{2})A-2(x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}x_{4})C}{\gamma\,D}.

(17)

An alternative derivation of the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic as a Bayes factor

Prix and Krishnan 2009
reveals the underlying amplitude priors to be
unphysical, which is why the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic is not statistically optimal.
The main advantage of the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic is the analytical elimination of the
four amplitude parameters, which gives it a computational advantage over any
alternative that would require explicit numerical operations to deal with the
unknown amplitude parameters (e.g., see

Whelan et al. 2014
;
Wette 2021
;
Bero and Whelan 2019
for further
discussion).

Another useful property of the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic is its known
\chi^{2}
probability
distribution with
\nu=4
degrees of freedom and noncentrality parameter

\rho^{2}\equiv(s|s)=\mathcal{A}^{\mu}\,\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{A}^{\nu}\,.

(18)

The known mean and variance of this distribution are

\displaystyle E[2\mathcal{F}]

\displaystyle=\nu+\rho^{2},

(19)

\displaystyle\text{var}[2\mathcal{F}]

\displaystyle=2(\nu+2\rho^{2})\,.

It will be useful to express the noncentrality as

\rho^{2}=h_{0}^{2}\,\gamma\,\left(\alpha_{1}\,A+\alpha_{2}\,B+2\alpha_{3}\,C\right),

(20)

in terms of amplitude angle factors
\alpha_{i}(\cos\iota,\psi)
defined as

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{4}\left(1+\cos^{2}\!\iota\right)^{2}\cos^{2}2\psi+\cos^{2}\!\iota\,\sin^{2}2\psi

(21)

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{4}\left(1+\cos^{2}\!\iota\right)^{2}\sin^{2}2\psi+\cos^{2}\!\iota\,\cos^{2}2\psi

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{4}\left(1-\cos^{2}\!\iota\right)^{2}\sin 2\psi\,\cos 2\psi.

One can see that averaging over the unknown
\cos\iota
and
\psi
yields

\langle{\alpha_{1}}\rangle_{\cos\iota,\psi}=\langle{\alpha_{2}}\rangle_{\cos\iota,\psi}=2/5

and
\langle{\alpha_{3}}\rangle_{\cos\iota,\psi}=0
, and therefore the angle-averaged
noncentrality parameter can be found as

\langle{\rho^{2}}\rangle_{\cos\iota,\psi}=\frac{2}{5}\,h_{0}^{2}\,\gamma\,(A+B).

(22)

II.3
The semi-coherent
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
-statistic

The use of the coherent
\mathcal{F}
-statistic as a search method is practically limited
to very small parameter spaces, due to the rapidly-growing computing cost of a
coherent template bank with increasing observation times
T
.
Wide-parameter-space searches therefore have to employ cheaper

semi-coherent
methods, which typically result in better sensitivity at
constrained computing cost
Brady and Creighton 2000
.
In the following we focus on the semi-coherent version of the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic.

We can break the total observation time
T
into
\ell=1,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{seg}}

segments
of duration
T_{\mathrm{seg}}
, and define the semi-coherent

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
-statistic as

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}\equiv\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{seg}}}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\,,

(23)

where
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
is the coherent
\mathcal{F}
-statistic computed on segment
\ell
.
Because this is a sum of
\chi^{2}
-distributed statistics, the semi-coherent

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
-statistic is also
\chi^{2}
-distributed, with
\widehat{\nu}
degrees
of freedom and noncentrality parameter
\widehat{\rho}^{2}
, given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\nu_{\ell}=4\,N_{\mathrm{seg}}\,,

(24)

\displaystyle\widehat{\rho}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\rho^{2}_{\ell}\,.

The optimal choice for the number and length of segments is subject to a
computing-cost-constrained optimization problem

Prix and Shaltev 2012
;
Ming et al. 2016
;
Ming et al. 2018
,
but the general trend is that the larger the parameter space, the
shorter the segments must be in order for the search to be computationally
feasible.

For example, a recent all-sky search for continuous waves from neutron stars
in unknown binary systems
Covas et al. 2022
had to use very short
segments of
T_{\mathrm{seg}}=$900\text{\,}\mathrm{s}$
over an observation time of
T=$6\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{t}\mathrm{h}\mathrm{s}$
.
This has revealed previously-unknown problems and limitations of the

\mathcal{F}
-statistic over such short baselines, discussed in the next section.

Problems with short-segment
\mathcal{F}

III.1
The ill-conditioned short-segment limit

The
\mathcal{F}
-statistic relies on the inverse antenna-pattern matrix
\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}
,

However, for a single detector the short-duration limit of the matrix

III.2
Fallback construction for singular
\mathcal{F}
-statistic

As discussed in the previous sections, the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic becomes singular in
the limit
T_{\mathrm{seg}}\rightarrow 0
for single-detector segments due to due failure
of maximum-likelihood estimation for all four amplitude parameters
\mathcal{A}^{\mu}
that
describe the full “
+
” and “
\times
” polarization content of the CW.
These four amplitudes translate into
\nu=4
degrees of freedom of the
resulting
\chi^{2}
distribution governing the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic.

These considerations suggest a strategy for avoiding degeneracy by reducing the
number of amplitude parameters (i.e., degrees of freedom) we try to infer about
the signal: even with a single stationary detector,
two
amplitudes should
always be perfectly well-determined, namely those corresponding to the “
+
”
polarization aligned with the arms of the detector.
In the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic framework, the two antenna-pattern functions
a(t)
and

b(t)
represent the responses to the two GW polarizations projected on a
sky-position dependent coordinate frame
Whelan et al. 2014
.

Guided by this intuition, we construct a new detection statistic by selecting
the “dominant response” between
a
and
b
in each sky position.
In other words, we implicitly use only two well-determined amplitude parameters
and neglect the other two, effectively reducing the
\chi^{2}
degrees of freedom
of the resulting statistic to
\nu=2
2
2

2

A somewhat-related
\nu=2

degrees-of-freedom variant is the
\mathcal{G}
-statistic

,
which assumes that the two amplitude parameters
\cos\iota
and
\psi
are

known
from observations for a given pulsar.
.

An interesting alternative construction (described in
Appendix
A
) consists in completely neglecting the
antenna-pattern response, resulting in a pure “demodulated power” statistic.
While this works for a single detector, it does not generalize well to multiple
detectors (suffering destructive interference), contrary to the
dominant-response statistic introduced here.

III.2.1
The dominant-response
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
-statistic

Let us consider the special case

, such that the likelihood Eq. (

\ln\mathcal{L}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}=\mathcal{A}^{1}\,x_{1}+\mathcal{A}^{3}\,x_{3}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma\,A\left[(\mathcal{A}^{1})^{2}+(\mathcal{A}^{3})^{2}\right]\,.

(26)

Maximization over the remaining two amplitude coordinates

(27)

Similarly, if had assumed

instead, which implies

, we would find

2\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}=\frac{x_{2}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}}{\gamma\,B}\,.

Given the noise expectations of Eq. (

\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}},\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}
are squared sums of Gaussian unit-variance

\chi^{2}
-distributed with

The corresponding noncentrality parameters
\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}^{2},\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}^{2}

can be expressed explicitly as

\displaystyle\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{1}^{2}+s_{3}^{2}}{\gamma\,A}=h_{0}^{2}\gamma\left[\alpha_{1}\,A+\alpha_{2}\frac{C^{2}}{A}+2\alpha_{3}\,C\right],

(30)

\displaystyle\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{2}^{2}+s_{4}^{2}}{\gamma\,B}=h_{0}^{2}\gamma\left[\alpha_{1}\frac{C^{2}}{B}+\alpha_{2}\,B+2\alpha_{3}\,C\right].

Note that the
only

\mathcal{F}
-statistic noncentrality

\rho^{2}

in

\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}^{2}
and

in
\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}^{2}
.
Given that
\mathcal{M}
is a positive-definite matrix, i.e.,

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0

\{\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}^{2},\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}^{2}\}<\rho^{2}
, i.e., unsurprisingly the “signal
power” in the statistic is reduced by neglecting one of the two antenna

Further note that
{C^{2}}/{A}=B-{D}/{A}
and
{C^{2}}/{B}=A-{D}/{B}
, and
therefore

\displaystyle\rho^{2}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}

\displaystyle=\rho^{2}-h_{0}^{2}\gamma\,\alpha_{2}\frac{D}{A}\,,

\displaystyle\rho^{2}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}

\displaystyle=\rho^{2}-h_{0}^{2}\gamma\,\alpha_{1}\frac{D}{B}\,.

We see that both
\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}^{2},\,\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}^{2}
approach

as

Following Eq. (

\displaystyle\langle{\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}^{2}}\rangle_{\cos\iota,\psi}

\displaystyle=\langle{\rho^{2}}\rangle_{\cos\iota,\psi}-\frac{2}{5}h_{0}^{2}\gamma\,\frac{D}{A},

(32)

\displaystyle\langle{\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}^{2}}\rangle_{\cos\iota,\psi}

\displaystyle=\langle{\rho^{2}}\rangle_{\cos\iota,\psi}-\frac{2}{5}h_{0}^{2}\gamma\,\frac{D}{B}.

Given the sensitivity of
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}
is determined by the respective

an
\mathcal{F}
-statistic
fallback
on degenerate segments: use either
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}

or
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}
depending on which of
\langle{\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}^{2}}\rangle
or
\langle{\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}^{2}}\rangle

dominates for the current sky position, which only depends on the ordering of

A
and
B
.
We therefore define the
dominant-response
statistic
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
:

\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}\equiv\begin{cases}\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}&\text{if }A>B,\\
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(33)

with the corresponding noncentrality parameter given by

\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}^{2}=\{\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{A}}}^{2}\text{ if }A>B\text{ else }\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{B}}}^{2}\}
.

III.2.2
Semicoherent generalization
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}

The coherent dominant-response statistic
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
defined in the previous
section can be summed semi-coherently in the same way as the standard
\mathcal{F}
-statistic,
i.e., following Eq. (
23
) we define

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}\equiv\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{seg}}}\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}{}_{,\ell}\,,

(34)

which is
\chi^{2}
-distributed with
\widehat{\nu}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}=2\,N_{\mathrm{seg}}
degrees of
freedom and noncentrality parameter
\widehat{\rho}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}^{2}
given by

\widehat{\rho}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}^{2}=\sum_{\ell}\rho^{2}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}{}_{,\ell}\,.

(35)

The dominant-response
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
statistic can be semi-coherently combined
with the standard
\mathcal{F}
-statistic on a per-segment basis, for example, switching to

\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
whenever
D
becomes too small in a segment.
The resulting “hybrid” semi-coherent
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
-statistic would be

\chi^{2}
-distributed with
\widehat{\nu}^{\prime}=4\,N_{4}+2\,N_{2}

degrees of freedom, where
N_{\nu}
is the number of segments using the

\chi_{\nu}^{2}
-statistic, such that
N_{4}+N_{2}=N_{\mathrm{seg}}
.
The corresponding noncentrality
parameter
\widehat{\rho}^{{}^{\prime}2}
would be the sum of per-segment
\rho^{2}

and
\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}^{2}
depending on the statistic used in a given segment.

IV
Surpassing the
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
-statistic sensitivity

The new
dominant-response

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
-statistic of the previous section
was constructed as a “fallback” for the
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
-statistic in the degenerate
limit of an ill-conditioned antenna-pattern matrix
\mathcal{M}
.
As we saw in Eq. (
30
), the corresponding noncentrality parameter

\rho_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}^{2}
of
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
tends towards the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic signal
power
\rho^{2}
in the limit of
D\rightarrow 0
.
However,
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
only has
two
degrees of freedom per segment
instead of the
four
for the standard
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
-statistic.
Fewer degrees of freedom means a lower detection threshold at a given
false-alarm probability, which implies that there exists a range of nonzero
D

values for which
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
will be
more sensitive
than the

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
-statistic, even when the
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
-statistic is still perfectly well-defined
and numerically stable
3
3

3

This is another illustration that the

\mathcal{F}
-statistic is not optimal, as first shown in
Prix and Krishnan 2009
.
.

IV.1
Estimating the maximal sensitivity gain

We characterize the sensitivity
Wette 2012
;
Dreissigacker et al. 2018

of a statistic
\mathfrak{s}
by the smallest required “upper limit” signal amplitude

h_{0}^{*}=h_{0,p_{\mathrm{fa}}}^{p_{\mathrm{det}}}
for a population of signals to reach a certain detection
probability
p_{\mathrm{det}}
at a given false-alarm probability
p_{\mathrm{fa}}
.
These probabilities are defined as

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{fa}}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\mathfrak{s}_{\mathrm{fa}}}^{\infty}P\left(\mathfrak{s}|h_{0}=0\right)\,d\mathfrak{s}\,,

(36)

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{det}}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\mathfrak{s}_{\mathrm{fa}}}^{\infty}P\left(\mathfrak{s}|h_{0}=h_{0}^{*}\right)\,d\mathfrak{s}\,,

(37)

where
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathrm{fa}}
denotes the detection threshold at the given false-alarm
probability
p_{\mathrm{fa}}
.

One can obtain an analytic estimate for the critical signal amplitude
h_{0}^{*}
in
the limit of a large number of segments
N_{\mathrm{seg}}\gg 1
, where the

\chi^{2}_{\widehat{\nu}}
distributions tend toward Gaussians with the same mean and
standard deviation.
Following

Krishnan et al. 2004
;
Wette 2012
;
Prix and Shaltev 2012
, we
define the rescaled false-alarm threshold as

\alpha\equiv\frac{\mathfrak{s}_{\mathrm{fa}}-\widehat{\nu}}{2\sqrt{\widehat{\nu}}}.

(38)

Using the Gaussian approximation we can solve Eq. (
36
) for

\alpha=\mathrm{erfc}^{-1}(2\,p_{\mathrm{fa}})\,,

(39)

in terms of the inverse of the complementary error function
\mathrm{erfc}(x)
.
Similarly we define

\beta\equiv-\mathrm{erfc}^{-1}(2\,p_{\mathrm{det}})\,,

(40)

which is a monotonically increasing function of
p_{\mathrm{det}}
with
\beta>0
for

p_{\mathrm{det}}>0.5
.
Further assuming a signal population of fixed power
\widehat{\rho}^{2}
(instead of
fixed amplitude
h_{0}
) we can express Eq. (
37
) as

\beta=\frac{\widehat{\rho}^{2}-2\alpha\sqrt{\widehat{\nu}}}{2\sqrt{\widehat{\nu}+2\widehat{\rho}^{2}}}\,.

(41)

For a given
\alpha(p_{\mathrm{fa}})
and
\beta(p_{\mathrm{det}})
we can solve this for the
critical signal power as

\widehat{\rho}^{2}_{*}(p_{\mathrm{fa}},\,p_{\mathrm{det}})=2\alpha\sqrt{\widehat{\nu}}+4\beta^{2}\left(1+\sqrt{1+Q}\right),\\

(42)

with
Q\equiv(\widehat{\nu}+4\alpha\sqrt{\widehat{\nu}})/(4\beta^{2})
.
As shown in
Wette 2012
, this approximation produces a biased
estimate for the critical signal amplitude
h_{0,p_{\mathrm{fa}}}^{p_{\mathrm{det}}}
, but we can
use it to analyze the
scaling
of sensitivity with search parameters.

We can use Eq. (
38
) to express the respective detection thresholds in
the large-
N_{\mathrm{seg}}
limit as

\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathrm{fa}}}{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}{}_{\mathrm{fa}}}=\frac{\widehat{\nu}+2\alpha\sqrt{\widehat{\nu}}}{\widehat{\nu}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}+2\alpha\sqrt{\widehat{\nu}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}}}\overset{N_{\mathrm{seg}}\gg 1}{\longrightarrow}\frac{\widehat{\nu}}{\widehat{\nu}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}}=2\,.

(43)

while the critical signal power of Eq. (
42
) tends to

\widehat{\rho}_{*}^{2}\overset{N_{\mathrm{seg}}\gg 1}{\longrightarrow}2\,(\alpha+\beta)\,\sqrt{\widehat{\nu}}\,.

(44)

Halving the degrees of freedom from
\widehat{\nu}=4N_{\mathrm{seg}}
for the standard

\mathcal{F}
-statistic to
\widehat{\nu}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}=2N_{\mathrm{seg}}
for the dominant-response statistic

\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
therefore results in a maximal sensitivity gain of

\frac{h_{0,\mathcal{F}}^{*}}{h_{0,\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}}^{*}}\sim\frac{\widehat{\rho}_{*,\mathcal{F}}}{\widehat{\rho}_{*,\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}}}\overset{N_{\mathrm{seg}}\gg 1}{\longrightarrow}\left(\frac{\widehat{\nu}}{\widehat{\nu}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}}\right)^{1/4}=2^{1/4}\,,

(45)

i.e., a gain of up to
\approx$18.9\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
compared to the standard

\mathcal{F}
-statistic.

Note that in this derivation, we assumed
\widehat{\rho}^{2}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}\approx\widehat{\rho}^{2}
, i.e.,

D\approx 0
in Eq. (
31
), therefore the maximal sensitivity gain would
decrease when
D
increases, such as when using longer segments or more detectors.

IV.2
The optimal choice between
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}

We have seen in the previous section that when there is no loss in
noncentrality, i.e.,
\widehat{\rho}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}^{2}\approx\widehat{\rho}^{2}
, the halving of the
\chi^{2}

degrees of freedom results in up to
18.9\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
sensitivity gain in
the large-
N_{\mathrm{seg}}
limit.
This argument suggests there should be a critical level of noncentrality loss
below which
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
is more sensitive than
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
.

In order to estimate this transition we use Eq. (
44
) to express

\beta=\widehat{\rho}^{2}/\sqrt{\widehat{\nu}}-2\alpha
, where
\beta
is a monotonic
function of detection probability
p_{\mathrm{det}}
.
Therefore the more sensitive statistic is characterized by a higher

\widehat{\rho}^{2}/\sqrt{\widehat{\nu}}
.
This can be reformulated into the condition

\widehat{\mu}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}\equiv\frac{\widehat{\rho}^{2}-\widehat{\rho}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}^{2}}{\widehat{\rho}^{2}}<\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{\sqrt{2}}\approx 0.29\,,

(46)

in terms of the
noncentrality mismatch

\widehat{\mu}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
.
Therefore up to a loss of
\widehat{\mu}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}\lesssim$29\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
in
noncentrality there is still a net sensitivity gain for the
dominant-response statistic
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
due to the reduction in degrees of
freedom.

This condition is not practically usable as it depends on the unknown signal
amplitude parameters
\cos\iota
and
\psi
, but we can use the averages of
Eq. (
32
) to obtain an estimate

\widehat{m}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}\equiv\langle{\mu_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}}\rangle_{\cos\iota,\psi}\sim\frac{\sum_{\ell}\frac{D_{\ell}}{\max(A,B)_{\ell}}}{\sum_{\ell}(A_{\ell}+B_{\ell})}\,,

(47)

where we approximated the average of the fraction as the fraction of averages,
which is generally biased but numerically turns out to be a relatively good
approximation in this case.
Therefore we expect the dominant-response
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
-statistic to be more
sensitive than the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic when

\widehat{m}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}\lesssim 0.29\,.

(48)

For sufficiently-short segments, this criterion will be satisfied essentially
over the whole sky, favoring
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
, while for long-enough
segments it will not be satisfied anywhere, favoring
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
, with an
intermediate regime of segment lengths where the optimal choice will depend on
the sky position.

V
Numerical results

In this section, we numerically test the theoretical predictions derived in the
previous sections and further characterize the performance of the new statistic

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
.
We use two complementary numerical methods for sensitivity estimation in order
to cross-check and validate the implementations and results, namely

1.

Direct numerical integration of the known
\chi^{2}
distributions for the
two statistics
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
, using the explicit noncentrality
expressions for
\widehat{\rho}^{2}
and
\widehat{\rho}^{2}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
given in
Eq. (
20
) and Eq. (
30
), respectively.
This is essentially the sensitivity-estimation method described in in

Wette 2012
;
Dreissigacker et al. 2018
.

2.

Sampling
synthesized
statistic values (per segment) by drawing noise-realizations
for the four Gaussian variates
n_{\mu}
with covariance matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}
of
Eq. (
14
), and adding signal contributions

s_{\mu}=\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}\,\mathcal{A}^{\nu}
for
\mathcal{A}^{\mu}
drawn from their priors.
Both the standard
\mathcal{F}
-statistic as well as
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
are fully determined by
the four numbers
x_{\mu}=n_{\mu}+s_{\mu}
, as seen in Eq. (
17
) and
Eq. (
27
), (
28
), respectively.
This synthetic sampling method has been used previously in

Prix and Krishnan 2009
;
Keitel et al. 2012
.

Both these methods are substantially faster than full injection-and-recovery
simulations and “cleaner” in the sense of not being affected by
noise-estimation biases and intrinsic implementation losses when computing

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
-statistics on actual data.
Therefore, these methods allow us to explore larger parameter spaces and
characterize the intrinsic properties of these statistics.
Compared to direct integration, sampling of synthetic statistic values is more
limited due to the finite number of samples generated in a given amount of time,
resulting in lower accuracy and limitations on how small false-alarm
probabilities
p_{\mathrm{fa}}
can be simulated.

For these tests we assume perfect Gaussian noise and data without gaps. For the
sake of example, sensitivity is characterized at an “upper limit” confidence
level of
p_{\mathrm{det}}=$90\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
and for a
single-template
false-alarm
level of
p_{\mathrm{fa}}=${10}^{-10}$
.
This choice would correspond to an overall false-alarm level of

\sim$1\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
over a parameter-space region containing
\sim${10}^{8}$

(independent) templates, which would be a reasonably realistic choice for typical
wide-parameter-space searches.

The sensitivity of a search can be conveniently characterized by its

sensitivity depth

Behnke et al. 2014
;
Dreissigacker et al. 2018
, defined
as

0pt_{p_{\mathrm{fa}}}^{p_{\mathrm{det}}}\equiv\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}}}{h_{0,p_{\mathrm{fa}}}^{p_{\mathrm{det}}}}\,,

(49)

in terms of the overall noise power spectral density
\mathcal{S}
of Eq. (
7
)
and the sensitivity amplitude
h_{0,p_{\mathrm{fa}}}^{p_{\mathrm{det}}}
introduced in
Sec.
IV.1
.
This definition scales out the noise-floor dependence and better characterizes
the intrinsic method sensitivity.

These results confirm that indeed the sensitivity gain correlates strongly with
the noncentrality mismatch and that the transition does happen close to the
theoretically predicted value, albeit with the empirical prediction of

\widehat{m}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}^{*}\sim 0.27
being closer to the truth.

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we have shown that the standard
\mathcal{F}
-statistic becomes singular
for very short segments due to the degeneracy of the antenna-pattern matrix.
This observation lead us to construct a well-behaved “fallback” statistic,
referred to as the
dominant-response

\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
-statistic.
Somewhat surprisingly, however, this new statistic turns out to be
more
sensitive
than the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic by up to
\approx$19\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
, even
outside the degeneracy region of the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic, when semi-coherently
combining of a large number of short segments.
We have characterized the new
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
-statistic analytically and numerically
and shown that it is more sensitive than the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic for segments shorter
than
T_{\mathrm{span}}\lesssim

15\,000\text{\,}\mathrm{s}20\,000\text{\,}\mathrm{s}
(depending on the number
of detectors used).

We have further shown that the sensitivity gain is determined by the
noncentrality mismatch
\widehat{m}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
of Eq. (
47
), and theoretical and
empirical estimates place the transition at about
\widehat{m}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}\sim 0.27
, below
which
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
is more sensitive than
\mathcal{F}
.

The new detection uses the same signal phase model as the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic and is
therefore expected
Prix 2007
to require essentially the same number
of templates and computing cost for a given parameter space.

Future work will focus on re-analyzing this limit in a Bayesian framework
following the approach of
Prix and Krishnan 2009
.

Appendix A
Constant-response statistic
\mathcal{F}_{\phi}

As an alternative construction to the dominant-response statistic
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}

introduced in Sec.
III.2.1
, it is interesting to consider
another special case obtained by assuming a constant amplitude response, i.e.,
completely neglecting the antenna-pattern modulation, and define a template

\displaystyle h^{X}_{\phi}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}\,\cos(\phi^{X}(t)+\phi_{0})

(51)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{c}}\,\cos\phi^{X}(t)+\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{s}}\,\sin\phi^{X}(t)\,,

where
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{c}}\equiv\mathcal{A}\,\sin\phi_{0}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{s}}\equiv\mathcal{A}\,\cos\phi_{0}
.
Using this template family the log-likelihood of Eq. (
4
)
takes the form

\ln\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{s}}\,x_{\mathrm{s}}+\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{c}}\,x_{\mathrm{c}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\,\mathcal{A}^{2}\,,

(52)

where we defined

x_{\mathrm{c}}\equiv(x|\cos\phi)\,,\quad x_{\mathrm{s}}\equiv(x|\sin\phi)\,,

(53)

in terms of the (multi-detector) scalar product
(x|y)
of Eq. (
6
).
Maximizing this log-likelihood over the two unknown amplitudes

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{c}},\,\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{s}}
yields
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{s,c}}^{\prime}=\gamma^{-1}\,x_{\mathrm{s,c}}
, and substituting
back results in the partially-maximized likelihood

2\mathcal{F}_{\phi}\equiv 2\ln\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\gamma^{-1}\left(x_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}+x_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}\right),

(54)

defining the
constant-response

\mathcal{F}_{\phi}
-statistic.
Note that
x_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}+x_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}
in the single-detector case is precisely the
Fourier power of
x(t)
demodulated into the source frame.

In the short-segment limit
T_{\mathrm{seg}}\rightarrow 0
for a single detector this would
tend exactly to the dominant-response
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
-statistic, as in this limit

a(t)\rightarrow a_{0}
,
b(t)\rightarrow b_{0}
, and therefore

A\rightarrow a_{0}^{2}
,
B\rightarrow b_{0}^{2}
and also

x_{\mu}\rightarrow\{a_{0},b_{0}\}\,x_{\mathrm{s,c}}
.

Similarly to Sec.
III.2.1
one can show that this statistic
follows a
\chi^{2}
distribution with
\nu=2
degrees of freedom, namely

E[2\mathcal{F}_{\phi}]=2+\rho_{\phi}^{2}\,,

(55)

with a noncentrality parameter

\rho_{\phi}^{2}\equiv\gamma^{-1}(s_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}+s_{\mathrm{s}}^{2})\,,

(56)

in terms of the signal projections
s_{\mathrm{s,c}}\equiv(s|\{\sin,\cos\}\phi)
.
Using the scalar product of Eq.(
6
) with the basis functions
of Eq. (
3
), we find

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{c}}

\displaystyle=\gamma\,\left(\mathcal{A}^{1}\,\langle\boldsymbol{a}\rangle+\mathcal{A}^{2}\,\langle\boldsymbol{b}\rangle\right)\,,

(57)

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{s}}

\displaystyle=\gamma\,\left(\mathcal{A}^{3}\,\langle\boldsymbol{a}\rangle+\mathcal{A}^{4}\,\langle\boldsymbol{b}\rangle\right)\,,

using the noise-weighted multi-detector average defined in Eq. (
12
).
Combining this with Eq. (
21
) we can express the constant-response
noncentrality as

\rho_{\phi}^{2}=h_{0}^{2}\gamma\,\left[\alpha_{1}\,\langle\boldsymbol{a}\rangle^{2}+\alpha_{2}\langle\boldsymbol{b}\rangle^{2}+2\alpha_{3}\langle\boldsymbol{a}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{b}\rangle\right]\,,

(58)

which has an uncanny formal resemblance to the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic signal power
expression of Eq. (
20
), except that the mean squares of
antenna-pattern functions of Eq. (
11
) are now replaced by squared
means, i.e.,
\langle\boldsymbol{a^{2}}\rangle\mapsto\langle\boldsymbol{a}\rangle^{2}
,
\langle\boldsymbol{b^{2}}\rangle\mapsto\langle\boldsymbol{b}\rangle^{2}
and

\langle\boldsymbol{ab}\rangle\mapsto\langle\boldsymbol{a}\rangle\langle\boldsymbol{b}\rangle
.
This observation implies that for a single detector in the short-segment limit,
this statistic will again have the same noncentrality as the
\mathcal{F}
-statistic with
only half the degrees of freedom, gaining the same
\approx$18.9\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
in
sensitivity as the dominant-response
\mathcal{F}_{\!{}_{\mathrm{AB}}}
-statistic of
Sec. (
III.2.1
).

For longer observation times as well as multiple detectors, however, the
antenna-pattern function can cancel out in the averages, resulting in
potentially significant sensitivity losses. Especially for non-aligned
detectors, the multi-detector averages can cancel strongly, potentially making

\mathcal{F}_{\phi}
more suitable as a “veto” rather than a detection statistic.

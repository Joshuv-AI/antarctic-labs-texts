Uniform pro-

We shall also assume that

•

•

•

Moreover, we assume that

(C1)

:

F_{\infty}
contains

,

(C2)

:

all but finitely many primes of

.

Let

be the set of primes

of

are are ramified in

. Note that by assumption,

be the set of primes of

that lie above the set

. Any prime of

, hence,

is finite as well. Setting

. The quantity

. The filtration

. For

.

Given

to be the Galois group

2.2.
Iwasawa Invariants

Let
F_{\infty}/F
be a uniform pro-
p
extension of
F
which satisfied the conditions (C1) and (C2) specified in
\lx@sectionsign\ref{s 2.1}
. Given
n\geq 0
, we identify
\Gamma_{n}
with
\operatorname{Gal}\left(F_{n}^{\operatorname{cyc}}/F_{n}\right)
and choose a topological generator
\gamma_{n}
of
\Gamma_{n}
. The Iwasawa algebra

\Lambda(\Gamma_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{m}[\Gamma_{n}/\Gamma_{n}^{p^{m}}]

is identified with the formal power ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
, where
T
is a formal variable. This isomorphism is precribed by mapping the generator
(\gamma_{n}-1)
to
T
.

Associated to a finitely generated and torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
-module
M
are its associated Iwasawa invariants
\mu(M)\geq 0
and
\lambda(M)\geq 0
. In greater detail, let
f_{M}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
be the characteristic element associated to
M
, which we recall is well defined up to multiplication by a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. According to the Weierstrass Preparation theorem,
f(T)
may be factored as a product
f(T)=p^{\mu}P(T)u(T)
, where
P(T)
is a distinguished polynomial and
u(T)
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. The
\mu
-invariant
\mu(M)
is the quantity
\mu\geq 0
, in the above factorization, and
\lambda(M)
is the degree of
P(T)
. Since
f(T)
is uniquely determined up to a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
, and the above factorization is unique, it follows that the Iwasawa invariants
\mu(M)
and
\lambda(M)
are well defined.

The modules we shall consider do arise from the
p
-primary parts of ideal classes in the cyclotomic tower of
F_{n}
. Given a number field
K
, let
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(K)
be the
p
-Sylow subgroup of the class group of
K
. Denote by
X_{n}=X(F_{n}^{\operatorname{cyc}})
the inverse limit
\varprojlim_{K}\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(K)
, where
K
ranges over all number field extensions of
F_{n}
that are contained in
F_{n}^{\operatorname{cyc}}
. The inverse limit is taken with respect to norm maps. Denote by
\mu_{p}(K_{n})
and
\lambda_{p}(K_{n})
the associated
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants of
X_{n}
as a
\Lambda(\Gamma_{n})
-module. It is easy to see that
\mu_{p}(K_{n})=0
if and only if
X_{n}
is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module. Moreover, it is easy to see that
\lambda_{p}(X_{n})
coincides with the rank of
X_{n}
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module (see
[
14
, Proposition 13.25]
).

3.
Iwasawa’s Riemann-Hurwitz formula

Throughout, let
p
will be an odd prime number. In this section, we introduce the methods used in proving our main results. The main contribution comes from an analog of the Riemann-Hurwitz formula due to Iwasawa
[
8
]
. The result generalizes Kida’s formula
[
9
]
, which is proven for CM-fields. In section
3.1
, we shall introduce certain cohomology classes which realize the obstructions to capitulation of ideals and ideal classes. In section
3.2
, we state the results of Iwasawa, which shall be used in proving our main result.

3.1.
Obstructions to Capitulation of ideals and ideal classes

Capitulation cokernels are of natural interest in Iwasawa theory, for instance, the reader may refer to
[
10
]
where the relationship between capitulation and Greenberg’s conjecture is studied. This section introduces cohomological obstructions to the capitulation of ideals and ideal classes. For further details, the reader is referred to
[
8
,
12
]
.

By a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-field, we mean a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cyc}}
. Given a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-field
\mathcal{K}
it is easy to see that there is a number field

such that

.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
K
and
K^{\prime}
be number fields such that
K\subset K^{\prime}
and
K^{\operatorname{cyc}}=K^{\prime\operatorname{cyc}}
. Then,
\mu_{p}(K)=0
if and only if
\mu_{p}(K^{\prime})=0
, and furthermore,
\lambda_{p}(K)=\lambda_{p}(K^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
X(K^{\operatorname{cyc}})
be the inverse limit
\varprojlim_{E}\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(E)
, where
E
ranges through all number fields contained in
K^{\operatorname{cyc}}
. The
\lambda
-invariant
\lambda_{p}(K)
is equal to the rank of
X(K^{\operatorname{cyc}})
and hence,
\lambda_{p}(K)=\lambda_{p}(K^{\prime})
. It is easy to see from the structure theory of Iwasawa modules that
\mu_{p}(K^{\prime})=[K^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]\mu_{p}(K)
, hence,
\mu_{p}(K)=0
if and only if
\mu_{p}(K^{\prime})=0
.
∎

Given a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-field
\mathcal{K}
, we define

to be

for any number field for whuch

to mean that

for any number field

such that

. Lemma

be a

F^{\operatorname{cyc}}=F_{0}^{\operatorname{cyc}}\subset F_{1}^{\operatorname{cyc}}\subset\dots F_{n}^{\operatorname{cyc}}\subset F_{n+1}^{\operatorname{cyc}}\subset\dots,

and note that for
n\geq 0

\operatorname{Gal}\left(F_{n+1}^{\operatorname{cyc}}/F_{n}^{\operatorname{cyc}}\right)=H_{n}/H_{n+1}\simeq\left(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\right)^{(d-1)}.

For
n\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{F}_{n}
to be the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-field
F_{n}^{\operatorname{cyc}}
. Choose a filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j)}\mid j=0,\dots,(d-1)\}
of
\mathcal{F}_{n+1}/\mathcal{F}_{n}
for which

•

\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j)}\subset\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j+1)}
is a Galois extension with
[\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j+1)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j)}]=p
,

•

\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(0)}=\mathcal{F}_{n}
and
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(d-1)}=\mathcal{F}_{n+1}
.

Note that it follows from Theorem
3.6
that
h^{i}(\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j+1)}/\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j)})
is well defined for

. We set

B_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}p^{d-2-j}\left(h^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j+1)}/\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j)})-h^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j+1)}/\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j)})\right),

and set

(4.1)

C_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(p-1)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}p^{(d-1)(n-1-i)}B_{i},

and it is understood that
C_{0}=0
.

Lemma 4.1
.

With respect to notation above, letting

(4.2)

\begin{split}&\xi_{n}^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{max}\left\{h^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i}^{(j+1)}/\mathcal{F}_{i}^{(j)})-h^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j+1)}/\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j)})\mid i\leq n\text{ and }j\leq d-2\right\},\\
&\xi_{n}^{-}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{min}\left\{h^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i}^{(j+1)}/\mathcal{F}_{i}^{(j)})-h^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j+1)}/\mathcal{F}_{n}^{(j)})\mid i\leq n\text{ and }j\leq d-2\right\},\\
\end{split}

we find that for
n\geq 0
,
C_{n}
satisfies the bounds

\left(p^{n(d-1)}-1\right)\xi_{n}^{-}\leq C_{n}\leq\left(p^{n(d-1)}-1\right)\xi_{n}^{+}.

Proof.

We prove that
C_{n}\leq\left(p^{n(d-1)}-1\right)\xi_{n}^{+}
. The proof of the lower bound is similar. For
i\leq n
, we find that

B_{i}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}p^{d-2-j}\xi_{i}^{+}=\frac{p^{d-1}-1}{p-1}\xi_{i}^{+}\leq\frac{p^{d-1}-1}{p-1}\xi_{n}^{+}.

Plugging this inequality into (
4.1
), we obtain the bound

\begin{split}C_{n}&=(p-1)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}p^{(d-1)(n-1-i)}B_{i}\\
&\leq\xi_{n}^{+}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(p^{(d-1)(n-i)}-p^{(d-1)(n-1-i)}\right)\\
&=\xi_{n}^{+}\left(p^{n(d-1)}-1\right),\\
\end{split}

which completes the proof.
∎

Consider the group
A_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=A\left(F_{n}^{\operatorname{cyc}}\right)
. According to Theorem
3.5
,
A_{n}=\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)^{\lambda_{n}}\oplus A_{n}^{\prime}
, where
\lambda_{n}=\lambda(F_{n})
and
A_{n}^{\prime}
has bounded exponent. Moreover,
A_{n}^{\prime}=0
if and only if
\mu_{p}(F_{n})=0
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
F
be a number field and
p
be an odd prime number. Let
F_{\infty}
be a uniform pro-
p
extension of
F
satisfying conditions (C1) and (C2) of section

. The following assertions hold:

(1)

\mu_{p}(F_{n})=0
for all
n\geq 0
,

(2)

A_{n}=\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)^{\lambda_{p}(F_{n})}
for all
n\geq 0
,

(3)

for
n\geq 0
,
\lambda(F_{n})
satisfies the bounds

(4.3)

\begin{split}&\lambda_{p}(F_{n})\geq p^{n(d-1)}\left(\lambda_{p}(F)+\xi_{n}^{-}\right)-\xi_{n}^{-},\\
&\lambda_{p}(F_{n})\leq p^{n(d-1)}\left(\lambda_{p}(F)+\#S(F^{\operatorname{cyc}})+\xi_{n}^{+}\right)-\xi_{n}^{+},\end{split}

where
\xi_{n}^{+}
and
\xi_{n}^{-}
are the quantities specified by (
4.2
).

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
3.6
and by induction on
n
that
\mu(F_{n})=0
. It is thus a consequence of Theorem
3.5
that
A_{n}=\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)^{\lambda_{p}(F_{n})}
for all
n\geq 0
. Recall that for
n\geq 0
, the quantity
C_{n}
is defined by (
4.1
). According to Lemma
4.1
, we find that for
n\geq 0
,

\left(p^{n(d-1)}-1\right)\xi_{n}^{-}\leq C_{n}\leq\left(p^{n(d-1)}-1\right)\xi_{n}^{+}.

Therefore in order to prove the bounds on
\lambda_{p}(F_{n})
, it suffices to prove that

(4.4)

p^{n(d-1)}\lambda_{p}(F)+C_{n}\leq\lambda_{p}(F_{n})\leq p^{n(d-1)}\left(\lambda_{p}(F)+\#S(F^{\operatorname{cyc}})\right)+C_{n}.

Recall that for every value of
k
, we have chosen a filtration

F_{k}^{\operatorname{cyc}}=\mathcal{F}_{k}^{(0)}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{F}_{k}^{(j)}\subset\mathcal{F}_{k}^{(j+1)}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{F}_{k}^{(d-1)}=F_{n+1}^{\operatorname{cyc}},

such that the successive extensions are Galois with Galois group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Concatenate the above filtrations to obtain a filtration

F^{\operatorname{cyc}}=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{E}_{i}\subset\mathcal{E}_{i+1}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{E}_{n(d-1)}=F_{n}^{\operatorname{cyc}}.

Let
\lambda_{i}
denote the
\lambda
-invariant of
\mathcal{E}_{i}
,
where
\mathcal{E}_{k(d-1)+j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{F}_{k}^{(j)}
for
0\leq j\leq d-1
. By induction, the
\mu
-invariant of each of the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-fields above is
0
. The relation

\lambda_{i+1}=p\lambda_{i}+\sum_{w\nmid p}(e(w)-1)+(p-1)\left(h^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{i+1}/\mathcal{E}_{i})-h^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i+1}/\mathcal{E}_{i})\right)

follows from Theorem
3.6
. Recursively applying the above, the obtain the bounds (
4.4
).
∎

Definition 4.2
.

We say that the successive obstructions to capitulation are uniformly bounded if there exists
B>0
independent of
n
such that for all
i\geq 0

\left|h^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i+1}/\mathcal{F}_{i})-h^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i+1}/\mathcal{F}_{i})\right|\leq B.

We say that the successive obstructions to capitulation eventually vanish if there exists
n_{0}
such that for all
i\geq n_{0}
,

h^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i+1}/\mathcal{F}_{i})=h^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i+1}/\mathcal{F}_{i})=0.

Remark 4.3
.

The above result shows that if there is a uniform bound
B>0
independent of
n
such that for all
i\geq 0

\left|h^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i+1}/\mathcal{F}_{i})-h^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i+1}/\mathcal{F}_{i})\right|\leq B,

then, it follows that

p^{n(d-1)}(\lambda_{p}(F)-B)<\lambda_{p}(F_{n})<p^{n(d-1)}(\lambda_{p}(F)+\#S(F^{\operatorname{cyc}})+B).

At this point in time, we are not able to show the uniform boundedness of successive obstruction quotients in our examples since it is a difficult condition to computationally verify given that it concerns the structure of class groups over infinite Galois extensions. However, it is a question worth exploring further. We expect the formulas for the growth of the the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
corank of
A(F_{n})
should be asymptotic as a function of
n
to
\lambda(G)p^{(d-1)n}
, where
\lambda(G)
is a non-negative constant. More precisely, we expect that as a function of
n
, the quotient
A(F_{n})/p^{(d-1)n}
converges to a constant
\lambda(G)
. The quantity
\lambda(G)
should be thought of as noncommutative analog of the classical
\lambda
-invariant. In light of the bounds for
\lambda_{p}(F_{n})
of Theorem
4.1
, it is reasonable to expect that the successive obstruction to capitulation
\xi_{n}^{+}
and
\xi_{n}^{-}
introduced in this article, are bounded independent of
n
.

4.2.
An explicit example

We illustrate Theorem

(2)

the

\begin{split}&\lambda_{p}(F_{n})\geq p^{n}\left(\lambda_{p}(F)+\xi_{n}^{-}\right)-\xi_{n}^{-},\\

4.3.
Torsion fields generated by Elliptic curves

A large class of examples arise from infinite torsion fields generated by elliptic curves. Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
without complex multiplication and let
p
be an odd prime. Let

be the group of
p^{n}

The

p

\rho_{E,p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Let

be the fixed field of the kernel of

\operatorname{Gal}(F_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})
with the image of
\rho_{E,p}
in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

be the number field which is fixed by the kernel of

and set

. Set

to be the kernel of the reduction modulo-

Then,

It follows from Serre’s Open image theorem (see

for all but finitely many primes

is isomorphic to

Note that since

contains

are satisfied provided

\begin{split}&\lambda_{p}(F_{n})\geq p^{3n}\left(\lambda_{p}(F)+\xi_{n}^{-}\right)-\xi_{n}^{-},\\
&\lambda_{p}(F_{n})\leq p^{3n}\left(\lambda_{p}(F)+\#S(F^{\operatorname{cyc}})+\xi_{n}^{+}\right)-\xi_{n}^{+},\end{split}

Universal properties of boundary and interface charges in continuum models of one-dimensional insulators
Sebastian Miles, Dante M. Kennes, Herbert Schoeller, Mikhail Pletyukhov

Abstract
We study single-channel continuum models of one-dimensional insulators induced by periodic potential modulations which are either terminated by a hard wall (the boundary model) or feature a single region of dislocations and/or impurity potentials breaking translational invariance (the interface model). We investigate the universal properties of excess charges accumulated near the boundary and the interface, respectively. We find a rigorous analytic proof for the earlier observed linear dependence of the boundary charge on the phase of the periodic potential modulation as well as extend these results to the interface model. The linear dependence on the phase shows a universal value for the slope, and is intersected by discontinuous jumps by plus or minus one electron charge at the phase points where localized states enter or leave a band of extended states. Both contributions add up such that the periodicity of the excess charge in the phase over a 2 ​ π 2\pi -cycle is maintained. While in the boundary model this property is usually associated with the bulk-boundary correspondence, in the interface model a correspondence of scattering state and localized state contributions to the total interface charge is unveiled on the basis of the so-called nearsightedness principle.



I
Introduction

The notion of topology has provided a powerful, novel viewpoint on a wide variety of phenomena in physics [
JackiwRebbi
-
hatsugai_1993
], successfully solidifying and expanding our understanding of condensed matter systems [
hasan_kane_2010
-
asboth_etal_16
].
Two fields that, e.g., have greatly profited from the paradigm of topology are the Quantum Hall effect (QHE) [
LaughlinQHE
] and the microscopic theory of polarization, now known as
modern theory of polarization
(MTP) [
king_smith_vanderbilt_93
].

The field of the QHE significantly profited from the insight that the integer
n
characterizing the Quantum Hall conductance
\sigma_{xy}=ne^{2}/h
is linked to the topological invariant characterizing bands, now known as the TKNN invariant [
thouless_etal_prl_82
] and mathematically identified [
thouless_1983
] with the first Chern index
C_{1}
. The importance of phase-singular points of the Bloch states
|\psi_{k\alpha}\rangle
(with the quasimomentum
k
in the occupied bands
\alpha
) for both the conductance quantization and for the above mentioned identification of
n=C_{1}
was quickly recognized [
kohmoto_1985
]. This understanding has subsequently led Y. Hatsugai to the formulation [
hatsugai_1993
] of the seminal
bulk-boundary correspondence
, which links the first Chern index (and thereby the topological characterization of the Bloch states in the Brillouin zone in terms of the Bloch wavefunction’s phase vorticities) to the number of edge modes contained within the band gaps of the insulating system.

On the other hand, the MTP relies heavily on concepts of topology since it establishes a connection between localized charges
Q_{B}
at boundaries and the Zak-Berry phase
\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}}{2\pi}=i\int_{BZ}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\langle\psi_{k\alpha}|\frac{d}{dk}\psi_{k\alpha}\rangle dk
[
zak_89
] of the bulk Bloch bands even beyond any symmetry constraints [
king_smith_vanderbilt_93
]. This is the so-called
surface charge theorem
[
king_smith_vanderbilt_surf_93
,
marzari_etal_12
]. However, this connection is in general not unique but only determined up to an unknown integer [
vanderbilt_18
], since the MTP does not give a prescription which specific gauge of
|\psi_{k\alpha}\rangle
should be used:
Any nontrivial gauge transformation with a nonzero winding number changes this relation by an integer.

Recently, systems have been proposed that capture both, the QHE and aspects of the MTP, simultaneously establishing an analogy between the two [
FracInDots
,
thakurati_etal_18
]. Following the key idea of the mapping between a two-dimensional QHE model, which is translationally invariant in one dimension, and a one-dimensional (1D) model of an insulator with a modulated periodic potential,
establishes the correspondence between the Hall conductance and the derivative of the boundary charge
Q_{B}
with respect to the phase of the modulations of the 1D periodic potential. Studying a lattice model of a 1D insulator with quite a large number (
Z=10
) of sites per unit cell,
reveals a linear dependence of
Q_{B}
on the phase, which is accompanied by discontinuous jumps by plus/minus one electron charge at the phase points where an edge state enters/leaves the band. Thereby,

Q_{B}
was elevated to be a relevant physical observable quantifying the spectral flow of the boundary eigenvalue problem. The slope of the linear dependence
is universally given by the number of the occupied bands (in units of
2\pi/e
), suggesting that it should be identified not only with
n
, but with
C_{1}
as well.

A wider class of generalized Aubry-André-Harper models [
aa_model
,
harper_model
] has been later studied analytically [
pletyukhov_etal_prbr_20
,
pletyukhov_etal_prb_20
] in the semi-infinite geometry with a hard-wall boundary condition at the origin.
The main conclusions of [
thakurati_etal_18
] have been confirmed, also proving that the slope of the linear dependence is indeed determined by the Chern index. In addition, it was shown that on top of the linear dependence for every finite
Z
there is an additional non-universal
\frac{2\pi}{Z}
-periodic function, contributing to
Q_{B}
, which is expected to vanish at larger
Z
. Moreover, a distinguished gauge has been identified, in which the relation between the Zak-Berry phase and
Q_{B}
holds exactly.
This gauge is fixed by choosing the last component of the Bloch vector to be real. As a consequence, the winding and the phase-singular points of the first Bloch vector component were shown to determine the universal values of the change
\Delta Q_{B}
under a lattice shift by one site and for the jumps in
Q_{B}
, respectively.

In addition, in Ref. [
weber_etal_prl_20
] quantum fluctuation of the boundary charge have been studied, and the so-called
surface fluctuation theorem
has been formulated, relating the boundary charge fluctuations generically to the fluctuations of the bulk polarization. Furthermore, for one-dimensional systems, a universal
1/E_{g}
low-energy scaling was established, with
E_{g}
denoting the size of the gap in which the chemical potential lies. Other universal low-energy properties of the Wannier functions in these models have been recently addressed in Ref. [
wannier_paper
].

An interface model combining the two semi-infinite lattice models with a mutual phase mismatch has been studied in Ref. [
RatBCPlet
]. It has been shown that the interface charge
Q_{I}
is given — up to an integer — by the dipole moment mismatch of the two subsystems. Furthermore, it was shown how to determine the integer for a specific type of the interface (realized by a hopping attenuation between the two subsystems at no phase mismatch) expressing it as a winding number of some complex function defined in the Brillouin zone. A principle stating that a local perturbation to a many-electron system may only lead to an integer-valued change in accumulated charges is called the
nearsightedness principle
[
NearSightednessPrin
]. On its basis we expect an ubiquitous occurrence of winding number expressions for this kind of changes: A charge robustness with respect to numerous system parameters and its sharp quantization can be only guaranteed by a topological expression.

In this paper, we study single-channel continuum models of one-dimensional insulators. Mathematically they correspond to the limit
Z\to\infty
(number of sites per unit cell),
a\to 0
(lattice spacing) at the fixed
L=Za
(unit cell length) of the previously studied lattice models. We investigate properties of both boundary and interface localized charges and reveal their universal features. Most of them are expected on the basis of our previous studies [
pletyukhov_etal_prbr_20
-
RatBCPlet
]: A linear phase dependence of
Q_{B}
and
Q_{I}
, discontinuous jumps at the points where a localized state enters/leaves a band of extended states, both the surface charge and surface fluctuation theorems and the bulk-boundary correspondence. However, there are additional features which are specific only to continuum models. In particular, the absence of the umklapp processes leads to the rigid values of the Chern indices (
=1
for each band), as well as to additional constraints on numbers of edge states leaving and entering a specific band and on Bloch wavefunction’s vorticity values. We find that these constraints stem from the properties of the Hill’s equation which were thoroughly studied in the mathematical literature (see, e.g., Refs. [
arscott
-
MagnusWinkler
]). As an additional motivation to study continuum models we literally quote the citation from Ref. [
wang_troyer
] which we share without reserve: ”There are several advantages to working with a continuum model instead of a tight-binding lattice model… Continuum models apply to a broader range of experimental situations…”.

As for the continuum interface models, we establish that the quantized part of
Q_{I}
is intimately related to analytic properties of the reflection coefficient.
This observation accords with the recent results of Ref. [
nakata
].
In particular, extracting a function
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}
, which is defined in the Brillouin zone for every band
\alpha
, from a scattering matrix denominator, we demonstrate that
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}
plays a role similar to that of the Bloch state phase in the boundary problem.

In sum, the present paper consolidates the findings of our previous papers [
pletyukhov_etal_prbr_20
-
RatBCPlet
] on the subject and firmly establishes the universal properties of both the boundary and the interface charges drawing a unified analogy between the two quantities.

The paper is organised as follows. In Sec.
II
a general construction of the Bloch states based on the approach of [
kohn_59
] for a generic but periodic 1D potential is discussed. In Sec.
III
we first determine eigenstates, both extended and localized, of the boundary problem with a hard-wall boundary introduced into the origin of the translationally invariant system. Next, we define the boundary charge which contains contributions from all eigenstates below the chemical potential put into the gap
\nu
. We rigorously prove the surface charge theorem, emphasizing the role of the gauge choosing the last component of the Bloch state to be real. Then we establish the phase dependence of edge state dispersions, recover the bulk-boundary correspondence, and state the universal form for both the total boundary charge and the band’s individual contributions to it. In Sec.
IV
we formulate the interface eigenvalue problem and construct both its scattering and localized eigenstates. Next, we evaluate the total interface charge discussing individual contributions to it from both of these types of eigenstates. This section is concluded with a novel expression for the phase dependence of the interface charge. All revealed properties are listed in the Summary Section
V
. The appendices provide detailed mathematical proofs of all intermediate statements and identities used in our derivations.

II
Bloch states construction for a periodic 1D potential

In this section we describe
a construction of the Bloch states for a periodic one-dimensional
potential following the ideas of W. Kohn [
kohn_59
]. In particular, we review properties of these eigenstates and thereby fix notations for
subsequent discussions. Throughout the paper we use the units
e=\hbar=1
.

The 1D Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(x)+V(x)\,\psi(x)=E\,\psi(x)

(1)

with a periodic potential
V(x)=V(x+L)
is a particular form of the Hill’s equation whose properties are comprehensively described in the mathematical literature (see, e.g., [
arscott
-
MagnusWinkler
]). For the following illustrations we arbitrarily choose a specific potential landscape shown in Fig.
1
, which does not have any internal symmetries. It will be used in all exemplary calculations of this paper.

At fixed
E
, a general solution of (
1
) can be expressed as combination of two linearly independent solutions,
\psi_{1}(x,E)\equiv\psi_{1}(x)
and
\psi_{2}(x,E)\equiv\psi_{2}(x)
, that is

\displaystyle\psi(x)=c_{1}\,\psi_{1}(x)+c_{2}\,\psi_{2}(x).

(2)

Choosing
\psi_{1}(x)
and
\psi_{2}(x)
to satisfy the initial conditions
\psi_{1}(0)=1
,
\psi^{\prime}_{1}(0)=0
and
\psi_{2}(0)=0
,
\psi^{\prime}_{2}(0)=1
, respectively, we fix the fundamental matrix of the equation

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi_{1}(x)&\psi_{2}(x)\\
\psi^{\prime}_{1}(x)&\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x)\end{array}\right).

Its determinant, the Wronskian,

\displaystyle W(x)=\psi_{1}(x)\,\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x)-\psi_{2}(x)\,\psi^{\prime}_{1}(x)\equiv 1\neq 0

(5)

is a nonzero constant: It follows that
W^{\prime}(x)=0
, and the specific value 1 results from the chosen initial conditions. This property guarantees linear independence of the vectors
\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}(x)\\
\psi^{\prime}_{1}(x)\end{pmatrix}
and
\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{2}(x)\\
\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}
at all
x
. Note that all elements of (
II
) are real.

According to the Bloch theorem, a smooth and bounded solution of (
1
) can be represented in the form

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)=u_{k}(x)\,e^{ikx},

(6)

where
u_{k}(x)
is a smooth and periodic function,
u_{k}(x)=u_{k}(x+L)
, and
k\in[-\frac{\pi}{L},\frac{\pi}{L})
. Thus, we obtain the boundary conditions for
\psi_{k}(x)

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(0)

\displaystyle=\psi_{k}(L)\,e^{-ikL},

(7)

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}_{k}(0)

\displaystyle=\psi^{\prime}_{k}(L)\,e^{-ikL}.

(8)

On the other hand, the representation (
2
) is still valid, implying a
k
dependence of the coefficients,

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)=c_{1,k}\,\psi_{1}(x)+c_{2,k}\,\psi_{2}(x).

(9)

From the boundary conditions (
7
), (
8
) we find

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=c_{1,k}\,[\psi_{1}(L)-e^{ikL}]+c_{2,k}\,\psi_{2}(L),

(10)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=c_{1,k}\,\psi^{\prime}_{1}(L)+c_{2,k}\,[\psi^{\prime}_{2}(L)-e^{ikL}].

(11)

To find nonzero
c_{1,k}
and
c_{2,k}
, we claim

\displaystyle\,[\psi_{1}(L)-e^{ikL}]\,[\psi^{\prime}_{2}(L)-e^{ikL}]-\psi_{2}(L)\,\psi^{\prime}_{1}(L)=0.

(12)

Taking into account (
5
), we establish the dispersion relation

\displaystyle\cos kL=\frac{1}{2}\,[\psi_{1}(L)+\psi^{\prime}_{2}(L)]\equiv D(E),

(13)

where
D(E)
is the so called Lyapunov function. Solving (
13
), one traditionally obtains the bands
\epsilon_{k\alpha}
labelled by the band index
\alpha
and possessing the property
\epsilon_{k\alpha}=\epsilon_{-k,\alpha}
(see Fig.
2
). As
E\to-\infty
, we can neglect
V(x)
in (
1
) and thereby establish that asymptotically
D(E)\approx\cosh(L\sqrt{2m|E|})\to+\infty
. Since
D(E)
is a continuous function, this means that the bottom energy of the lowest band (
\alpha=1
) satisfies the equation
D(E)=+1
. In addition, within the band
\alpha
, the function
D(E)
is monotonous [which follows, e.g., from the Schwarz inequality (
205
)], and

\displaystyle\text{sign}\left[\frac{\partial D(E)}{\partial E}\right]=(-1)^{\alpha}.

(14)

Then, from (
10
) we find

\displaystyle\frac{c_{2,k}}{c_{1,k}}=-\frac{\psi_{1}(L)-e^{ikL}}{\psi_{2}(L)},

(15)

and inserting this into (
9
) we obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)=c_{1,k}\left[\psi_{1}(x)-\frac{\psi_{1}(L)-e^{ikL}}{\psi_{2}(L)}\,\psi_{2}(x)\right]

(16)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{k}}}\left[\psi_{1}(x)\,\psi_{2}(L)-\psi_{1}(L)\,\psi_{2}(x)+\psi_{2}(x)\,e^{ikL}\right].

(17)

Hereby we introduced the normalization
N_{k}
by virtue of the relation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}dx\,\,|\psi_{k}(x)|^{2}=1.

(18)

Noticing that (see Appendix
A
for details)

\displaystyle-\psi_{2}(x-L)=\psi_{1}(x)\,\psi_{2}(L)-\psi_{1}(L)\,\psi_{2}(x),

(19)

and explicitly writing the band index
\alpha
, we get the Bloch states in the form

\displaystyle\psi_{k\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{k\alpha}}}\left[-\psi_{2}(x-L)+\psi_{2}(x)\,e^{ikL}\right],

(20)

along with

\displaystyle u_{k\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-ikx}}{\sqrt{N_{k\alpha}}}\left[-\psi_{2}(x-L)+\psi_{2}(x)\,e^{ikL}\right].

(21)

Here
\psi_{2}(x)=\psi_{2}(x,\epsilon_{k\alpha})
depends on
k
and
\alpha
via
\epsilon_{k\alpha}
, but in the following we omit for brevity an explicit indication of this dependence.

Note that
\psi_{k\alpha}(x)=\psi_{k+\frac{2\pi}{L},\alpha}(x)
and
u_{k\alpha}(x)=u_{k\alpha}(x+L)
, while the reciprocal relations are not valid, i.e.
u_{k\alpha}(x)\neq u_{k+\frac{2\pi}{L},\alpha}(x)
,
\psi_{k\alpha}(x)\neq\psi_{k\alpha}(x+L)
.

It is remarkable that

\displaystyle\psi_{k\alpha}(0)=u_{k\alpha}(0)=u_{k\alpha}(L)=\frac{\psi_{2}(L)}{\sqrt{N_{k\alpha}}}

(22)

is real. This property fixes the gauge of the Bloch states which will prove to be advantageous for the calculation of the boundary charge in Sec.
III
.

In general, we also have the relations

\displaystyle\psi^{*}_{k\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\psi_{-k,\alpha}(x),

(23)

\displaystyle u^{*}_{k\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=u_{-k,\alpha}(x),

(24)

which can be realised in arbitrary gauge.

Finally, we introduce the Bloch states

\displaystyle\Psi_{k\alpha}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{L}{2\pi}}\,\psi_{k\alpha}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{L}{2\pi}}\,u_{k\alpha}(x)\,e^{ikx},

(25)

which satisfy the following normalization and completeness relations

\displaystyle\int dx\,\,\Psi_{k\alpha}^{*}(x)\,\Psi_{k^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}(x)=\delta(k-k^{\prime})\,\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}},

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}dk\,\,\Psi_{k\alpha}(x)\,\Psi_{k\alpha}^{*}(x^{\prime})=\delta(x-x^{\prime}).

(27)

Note that the prefactor
\sqrt{L/(2\pi)}
in (
25
) is needed in order to reconcile (
26
) with the normalization
\int_{0}^{L}dx\,|u_{k\alpha}(x)|^{2}=1
.

Later on the states (
25
) are used for constructing physical observables. Some properties of
N_{k\alpha}
,
\epsilon_{k\alpha}
, and
\psi_{2}(L,\epsilon_{k\alpha})
, which are also useful for this purpose, are quoted in Appendix
B
.

III
Boundary charge

In this section we discuss solutions of the Schrödinder equation (
1
) in the half-space
x>0
. This eigenvalue problem is equipped with the open boundary condition
\psi(x=0)=0
. Analogously to the case of 1D single-channel lattice models [
pletyukhov_etal_prb_20
], extended eigenstates of the present boundary problem can be constructed in terms of the Bloch states introduced in the previous section. In addition, we construct edge states which are exponentially localized near the boundary.

The central object of our study in this section is the boundary charge
Q_{B}
accumulated near the boundary. For a system’s filling determined by the chemical potential
\mu_{\nu}
lying in the
\nu
th band gap,
Q_{B}
receives contributions not only from an edge state eventually residing in this gap, but also from all occupied bands and the lower-gaps’ edge states. We extend our previous lattice considerations [
pletyukhov_etal_prb_20
] to the case of continuum models and establish specific expressions for all contributions to
Q_{B}
. In particular, we provide a new rigorous proof of the surface charge theorem also fixing an expression for the integer part of
Q_{B}
which is usually left unknown (cf., e.g., Ref. [
vanderbilt_18
]).

Studying the dependence of
Q_{B}
on the modulation phase of the potential (i.e., on the distance by which
V(x)
is being shifted towards the boundary), we observe a manifestation of the bulk-boundary correspondence in the spirit of Ref. [
hatsugai_1993
]. We also firmly establish the universal linear phase dependence of
Q_{B}
, with the slope being given by the Chern index of a band (bands). Thereby we provide a rigorous proof to the earlier numerical findings of Ref. [
thakurati_etal_18
]. Moreover, we reveal that in continuum models the Chern index for each band is strictly equal to
+1
, and that the number of bounces of an edge state energy in a certain gap between two adjacent band edges during one pump cycle is given by the gap index.

Finally, we investigate the boundary charge fluctuations and conclude that in the narrow-gap limit they possess universal properties satisfying the surface fluctuation theorem which was stated in the previous works [
weber_etal_prl_20
,
wannier_paper
].

III.1
Eigenstates of the semi-infinite model

In this subsection we construct the extended eigenstates of (
1
) in the right half-space
x>0
and use them to evaluate each band’s contribution to the boundary charge.

The boundary problem eigenstates can be expressed in terms of the states (
25
):

\displaystyle\Psi_{\text{b},k\alpha}(x)=\Psi_{k\alpha}(x)-\Psi_{-k,\alpha}(x),\quad k\in\left[0,\frac{\pi}{L}\right],

(28)

They have the eigenenergies
\epsilon_{k\alpha}
(i.e. form the same bands as in the translationally invariant model) and satisfy the boundary condition

\displaystyle\Psi_{\text{b},k\alpha}(0)=0.

(29)

Their normalization reads

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\,\Psi^{*}_{\text{b},k\alpha}(x)\,\Psi_{\text{b},k^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}(x)=\delta(k-k^{\prime})\,\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}.

(30)

Note that the set of all
\Psi_{\text{b},k\alpha}(x)
is not complete, since localized edge states are also permitted. These edge states will be discussed in Sec.
III.4
.

The simplicity of the form of (
28
) is due to the special gauge choice which is fixed by the condition (
22
). Using (
28
) we define the boundary charge [
thakurati_etal_18
,
pletyukhov_etal_prb_20
] associated with the band
\alpha

\displaystyle Q_{B,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\,[\rho_{\alpha}(x)-\bar{\rho}_{\alpha}]\,f(x)

(31)

in terms of the charge density

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/L}dk\,\,|\Psi_{\text{b},k\alpha}(x)|^{2},

(32)

the average charge density in the bulk

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}dk\,\,|\Psi_{k\alpha}(x)|^{2}=\frac{1}{L},

(33)

and the envelope function
f(x)
. The latter possesses the following properties: 1)
f(x)\approx 1
in the range
|x|<X_{1}
; 2) in the range
X_{1}<|x|<X_{1}+X_{2}
it smoothly crosses over to zero; 3)
f(x)\approx 0
for
|x|>X_{1}+X_{2}
(see Fig.
3
). Here
X_{1,2}\gg\xi\gg L
are scales large in comparison with the localization length
\xi=\kappa^{-1}
(see Sec.
III.4

for the latter definition).

\displaystyle Q_{F,\alpha}=

(34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{P,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\,[\bar{\rho}_{\alpha}(x)-\bar{\rho}_{\alpha}]\,f(x)

(38)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{L}d\bar{x}\,\,[\bar{\rho}_{\alpha}(\bar{x})-\bar{\rho}_{\alpha}]\,f(\bar{x}+nL),

(39)

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}dk\,\,|\Psi_{k\alpha}(x)|^{2}=\bar{\rho}_{\alpha}(x+L),

(40)

in the following way. Observing that the main contribution to (
39
) comes from the range
X_{1}<nL<X_{1}+X_{2}
, we expand the envelope function
f(\bar{x}+nL)\approx f(nL)+\bar{x}f^{\prime}(nL)
. The leading contribution identically vanishes after averaging over
\bar{x}
(which is defined as
x\!\mod L
), and we get

\displaystyle Q_{P,\alpha}\approx\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(nL)\int_{0}^{L}d\bar{x}\,\,\bar{x}\,[\bar{\rho}_{\alpha}(\bar{x})-\bar{\rho}_{\alpha}].

(41)

Using the properties of the envelope function, we approximately evaluate the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(nL)\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\,\,f^{\prime}(y)

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}[f(\infty)-f(0)]=-\frac{1}{L},

(43)

and thus reproduce (
36
).

Evaluating the sum in (
35
) we express the Friedel charge in the form

\displaystyle Q_{F,\alpha}

\displaystyle=-L\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,\,\,\text{Re}\,\left\{\frac{\psi_{k\alpha}^{2}(x)\,e^{-ikL}}{-2i\,\sin(kL+i0^{+})}\right\}.

(44)

Note that a convergence factor
+i0^{+}
was added to the sum over
n
: We exchanged this summation with the integral over
k
, and this action had to be complemented by proper regularization.

Using a variant of the Sokhotski–Plemelj theorem

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sin(kL+i0^{+})}=\frac{1}{\sin kL}-i\pi\,\delta(kL)+i\pi\,\delta(kL-\pi),

(45)

we obtain

\displaystyle Q_{F,\alpha}^{\text{I}}=

\displaystyle-L\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,\,\text{Re}\,\left\{\frac{\psi_{k\alpha}^{2}(x)\,e^{-ikL}}{-2i\,\sin kL}\right\},

(46)

\displaystyle Q_{F,\alpha}^{\text{II}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}d(kL)

\displaystyle\times\text{Re}\left\{\psi_{k\alpha}^{2}(x)\,e^{-ikL}\,\,[\delta(kL)-\delta(kL-\pi)]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{L}dx\,\,\text{Re}\left\{\psi_{0,\alpha}^{2}(x)+\psi_{\frac{\pi}{L},\alpha}^{2}(x)\right\}.

(47)

Since
\psi_{0,\alpha}(x)
and
\psi_{\frac{\pi}{L},\alpha}(x)
are both real and normalized by (
18
), we get
Q_{F,\alpha}^{\text{II}}=-\frac{1}{2}
.

In turn,

\displaystyle Q_{F,\alpha}^{\text{I}}=

\displaystyle-L\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\frac{dk}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\times\text{Re}\,\left\{[\psi_{k\alpha}(x)\,e^{-ikL}-\psi_{-k,\alpha}(x)\,e^{ikL}]\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left.\psi_{k\alpha}(x)\,e^{-ikL}\,\frac{e^{ikL}}{-2i\,\sin kL}\right\}

\displaystyle+\frac{L}{2}\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,\,|\psi_{k\alpha}(x)|^{2}

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-L\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,\,\frac{\psi_{2}(x-L)}{N_{k\alpha}}

\displaystyle\times\left[\psi_{2}(x-L)-\psi_{2}(x)\,\cos kL\right]+\frac{1}{2}.

(49)

So finally we get

\displaystyle Q_{F,\alpha}=

\displaystyle-L\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,\,\frac{\psi_{2}(x-L)}{N_{k\alpha}}

\displaystyle\times\left[\psi_{2}(x-L)-\psi_{2}(x)\,\cos kL\right].

(50)

III.2
Berry phase expression for polarization

In this subsection we extend the observation previously made for the lattice models in Ref. [
pletyukhov_etal_prb_20
] that the boundary charge is given by the Zak-Berry phase [
zak_89
]. This statement is generally known as the surface charge theorem [
vanderbilt_18
]; however the role of the gauge of the Bloch states used for a construction of the Zak-Berry connection is usually not elucidated. Therefore this theorem is often stated up to an unknown integer which can be changed by a winding number of the phase of the gauge transformation.

The importance of the gauge (
21
) (i.e., with the real last component of the Bloch vector
u_{k\alpha}
) was first recognized in Refs. [
pletyukhov_etal_prbr_20
,
pletyukhov_etal_prb_20
]. In particular, it was shown therein that while working in this specific gauge no additional integer contribution to
Q_{B}
arises in lattice models. Below we show that this also holds true for continuum models.

Let us consider the expression

\displaystyle\text{Im}\,\left[u_{k\alpha}^{*}(x)\,e^{ik(L-x)}\,\frac{d}{dk}u_{k\alpha}(x)\,e^{-ik(L-x)}\right]

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\,\text{Im}\,\left[\psi_{k\alpha}^{*}(x)\,e^{ikL}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial k}\psi_{k\alpha}(x)\,e^{-ikL}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{d\epsilon_{k\alpha}}{dk}\,\,\text{Im}\,\left[\psi_{k\alpha}^{*}(x)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial E}\psi_{k\alpha}(x)\right]

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{L}{N_{k\alpha}}\left[\psi_{2}(x-L)-\psi_{2}(x)\,\cos kL\right]\,\psi_{2}(x-L)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N_{k\alpha}}\frac{d\epsilon_{k\alpha}}{dk}\,\sin kL\,\,F(x),

(53)

where

\displaystyle F(x)=\psi_{2}(x)\,\frac{\partial\psi_{2}(x-L)}{\partial E}-\psi_{2}(x-L)\,\frac{\partial\psi_{2}(x)}{\partial E}.

(54)

Due to the property

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}dx\,F(x)=0,

(55)

whose proof is given in Appendix
A
, we can write

\displaystyle Q_{F,\alpha}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,\,\text{Im}\,\left[u_{k\alpha}^{*}(x)\,\frac{d}{dk}u_{k\alpha}(x)\right]

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{L}dx\,\,(L-x)\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,\,|u_{k\alpha}(x)|^{2}.

(56)

Combining this expression with (
37
), we obtain

\displaystyle Q_{B,\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}+\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,\,A_{k\alpha},

(57)

\displaystyle A_{k\alpha}

\displaystyle=-i\int_{0}^{L}dx\,\,u_{k\alpha}^{*}(x)\,\frac{d}{dk}u_{k\alpha}(x),

(58)

where the last line expresses the Zak-Berry connection.

III.3
Universal properties of the boundary charge

The interesting behaviour of
Q_{B}
under the shift of a potential towards the boundary was initially observed in numerical studies of Ref. [
thakurati_etal_18
]. It was revealed that
Q_{B}
linearly depends on the distance of the shift (i.e. on the potential modulation phase), the slope acquiring quantized universal values. In this subsection we provide an analytic proof of the linear dependence and discuss other universal properties of the boundary charge.

Suppose we shift our system towards the wall by
x_{\varphi}
. It means that we have to consider the boundary problem with a new potential

\displaystyle V^{(\varphi)}(x)=V(x+x_{\varphi}).

(59)

The new solution
\psi_{k\alpha}^{(\varphi)}(x)
is related to the old one
\psi_{k\alpha}(x)
by

\displaystyle\psi_{k\alpha}^{(\varphi)}(x)

\displaystyle=\psi_{k\alpha}(x+x_{\varphi})\,\,e^{-i\Phi_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})},

(60)

\displaystyle u_{k\alpha}^{(\varphi)}(x)

\displaystyle=u_{k\alpha}(x+x_{\varphi})\,\,e^{ikx_{\varphi}}\,\,e^{-i\Phi_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})}.

(61)

To enable the usage of (
57
), (
58
) in the shifted system, we must claim similarly to (
22
) that
\psi_{k\alpha}^{(\varphi)}(0)
is real. This condition fixes (up to the irrelevant sign) the phase factor

\displaystyle e^{i\Phi_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})}\sim\psi_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})=u_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})\,\,e^{ikx_{\varphi}}.

(62)

Note that it is periodic in
k
due to the analogous property of
\psi_{k\alpha}(x)
. In addition,

\displaystyle e^{i\Phi_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi}+L)}=e^{i\Phi_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})}\,\,e^{ikL}.

(63)

The boundary charge in the shifted system can be written with respect to the reference system value
Q_{B,\alpha}(0)\equiv Q_{B,\alpha}
as

\displaystyle\Delta Q_{B,\alpha}(x_{\varphi},0)

\displaystyle=Q_{B,\alpha}(x_{\varphi})-Q_{B,\alpha}(0)

(64)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,\,[A_{k\alpha}^{(\varphi)}-A_{k\alpha}]

(65)

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{\varphi}}{L}-\text{wn}\,[e^{i\Phi_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})}],

(66)

where

\displaystyle\text{wn}\,[e^{i\Phi_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})}]=-\text{wn}\,[e^{-i\Phi_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})}]

(67)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}dk\,\,e^{-i\Phi_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})}\,\frac{d}{dk}e^{i\Phi_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})}

(68)

is the winding number of the corresponding phase factor. In this derivation we used the periodicity of
u_{k\alpha}(x)
in
x
. Due to the property (
63
), the boundary charge
Q_{B,\alpha}(x_{\varphi})
is manifestly periodic under a shift
x_{\varphi}\to x_{\varphi}+L
, as it should be.

Piecewise, i.e. outside the points where the winding number jumps, the slope

\displaystyle L\,\frac{dQ_{B,\alpha}(x_{\varphi})}{dx_{\varphi}}=C_{1,\alpha}=1

(69)

is universal and coincides with the first Chern index of the band
\alpha
defined on the two-dimensional torus
(k,x_{\varphi})
:

\displaystyle C_{1,\alpha}=

\displaystyle-\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{L}dx_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\times 2\,\text{Im}\int_{0}^{L}dx\,\,\frac{d\psi_{k\alpha}^{(\varphi)\,*}(x)}{dk}\,\,\frac{d\psi_{k\alpha}^{(\varphi)}(x)}{dx_{\varphi}}.

(70)

This index is a sign-weighted count of the phase singular points across the band
\alpha
[
kohmoto_1985
,
pletyukhov_etal_prb_20
], thus giving (up to the sign) the net change of the winding number over one period in
x_{\varphi}
. The value of
C_{1,\alpha}
must be synced — as done in the first equality of (
69
) — with the slope of the linear part of
Q_{B,\alpha}(x_{\varphi})
in order to maintain the periodicity
Q_{B,\alpha}(x_{\varphi})=Q_{B,\alpha}(x_{\varphi}+L)
.
In Appendix
C
we show by explicit evaluation of (
70
) that
C_{1,\alpha}=1
for each band, i.e. prove the fulfillment of the second equality in (
69
).

The result (
69
) differs to some extent from the findings of Ref. [
pletyukhov_etal_prb_20
] for a lattice model with
Z
sites per unit cell. There, the slope (with respect to
\frac{\varphi}{2\pi}
) could take integer values
C_{1,\alpha}=1+s_{\alpha}Z
with some integer
s_{\alpha}
. The limit
Z\to\infty
should bring us to the case of the presently studied continuum models. In order to get finite Chern indices, it is necessary to ensure that upon taking this limit the potential as a function of the phase variable does not make any kinks between the lattice sites. This procedure ensures
s_{\alpha}=0
and yields the result
C_{1,\alpha}=1
which is consistent with that of our present consideration.

The bulk-boundary correspondence [
hatsugai_1993
] implies that the found value of
C_{1,\alpha}
should be supported by the same value for the difference between numbers of edge states entering and leaving the band
\alpha
on the interval
0\leq x_{\varphi}<L
. We inspect this property in the next subsection.

To complete the discussion of the boundary charge we also outline expressions for the boundary charge in the left subsystem

\displaystyle Q_{B,\alpha}^{L}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}dx\,\,[\rho_{\alpha}(x)-\bar{\rho}_{\alpha}]\,f(x),

(71)

\displaystyle=Q_{F,\alpha}^{L}+Q_{P,\alpha}^{L}=-1-Q_{B,\alpha},

(72)

where

\displaystyle Q_{F,\alpha}^{L}=

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{0}dx\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}dk\,\,\text{Re}\,\Psi_{k\alpha}^{2}(x)

(73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-L\,\,\text{Re}\,\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{-\pi/L}^{\pi/L}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,\,\psi_{k\alpha}^{2}(x)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n=-\infty}^{0}e^{2i(kL-i0^{+})(n-1)}

(74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1-Q_{F,\alpha},

(75)

\displaystyle Q_{P,\alpha}^{L}=

\displaystyle-Q_{P,\alpha}.

(76)

For simplicity we choose
f(x)=f(-x)
in (
71
). In general, the values of
X_{1,2}
may however differ for each half-space, but this does not affect the results (
75
) and (
76
).

III.4
Edge states

Besides the eigenstates (
28
) the semi-infinite model with the open boundary condition possesses edge localized states. We notice that the function (
17
) vanishes at
x=0
, if the condition

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(L)=0

(77)

is fulfilled. This can not happen within the energy range of any band, since
according to the property (
218
) the function
\psi_{2}(L)
is sign-definite there. Therefore, the condition (
77
) can be only fulfilled in band gaps. For this to happen we should allow for complex valued
k
.

The other condition (
13
) relating
k
and
E
to each other contains the function
D(E)
, which is real at every
E
. This imposes the constraint that complex-valued
k
can only be of the form either
k=\pm\pi/L+i\kappa
or
k=i\kappa
, both with real
\kappa
.

The condition (
5
) is also fulfilled at every
E
. Combining it with (
77
), we obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(L)=\frac{1}{\psi^{\prime}_{2}(L)}.

(78)

Furthermore, from (
13
) it follows

\displaystyle(-1)^{s}\,\cosh\kappa L=\frac{1}{2}\left(\psi_{1}(L)+\frac{1}{\psi_{1}(L)}\right).

(79)

Here
s=0
corresponds to the choice
\text{Re}\,k=0
, and
s=1
corresponds to the choice
\text{Re}\,k=\pm\pi/L
.

Equation (
79
) has two solutions:
\psi_{1}(L)=(-1)^{s}\,e^{\kappa L}
and
\psi_{1}(L)=(-1)^{s}\,e^{-\kappa L}
. They give the following eigenfunctions of edge states

\displaystyle\psi_{\text{e}}(x)

\displaystyle\sim[-\psi_{1}(L)+e^{ikL}]\,\,\psi_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=-2\,(-1)^{s}\,\sinh\kappa L\,\,\psi_{2}(x),

(80)

\displaystyle\psi_{\text{e}}(x)

\displaystyle\sim[-\psi_{1}(L)+e^{ikL}]\,\,\psi_{2}(x)\equiv 0,

(81)

respectively. Thus, a nontrivial solution has

\displaystyle e^{\kappa L}=(-1)^{s}\,\psi_{1}(L)>0.

(82)

Labelling each band gap by the index
\alpha
of the band which lies beneath it, we search for the relation between
s
and
\alpha
. Comparing (
203
) and (
204
), we find that at the edge state energy
E=\epsilon_{\text{e},\alpha}
it holds

\displaystyle\text{sign}\,\,\psi_{1}(L,\epsilon_{\text{e},\alpha})=\text{sign}\,\frac{\partial\psi_{2}(L,\epsilon_{\text{e}})}{\partial E}=\text{sign}\,\,D(\epsilon_{\text{e},\alpha}).

(83)

But
\text{sign}\,D(\epsilon_{\text{e},\alpha})=(-1)^{\alpha}
(see Fig.
2
), and therefore

\displaystyle(-1)^{s}=(-1)^{\alpha},

(84)

i.e. edge states residing in odd gaps have
\text{Re}\,k=\pm\pi/L
, while edge states residing in even gaps have
\text{Re}\,k=0
.

Being primarily interested in the right subsystem (
x>0
), we impose the condition
\kappa>0
in order to have a normalizable (decaying) solution. From (
82
) it follows that a nontrivial edge state solution in the right subsystem exists, if

\displaystyle|\psi_{1}(L)|>1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\kappa=\frac{1}{L}\,\ln|\psi_{1}(L)|>0.

(85)

In turn, a nontrivial edge state solution in the left subsystem exists, if

\displaystyle|\psi_{1}(L)|<1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\kappa=\frac{1}{L}\,\ln|\psi_{1}(L)|<0.

(86)

In Appendix
D
we prove that in each band gap there is precisely one edge state, corresponding either to the right subsystem (
x>0
,
\kappa>0
) or to the left subsystem (
x<0
,
\kappa<0
). Its normalized wavefunction reads

\displaystyle\Psi_{\text{e},\alpha}(x)=\frac{\Theta(x\,\,\text{sign}\,\kappa)}{\sqrt{N_{\text{e},\alpha}}}\,\psi_{2}(x),

(87)

with the corresponding normalizations

\displaystyle N_{\text{e},\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\,\psi_{2}^{2}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{L}dx\,\,\psi_{2}^{2}(x+nL)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\alpha}}{4m}\,\frac{\partial\psi_{2}(L)}{\partial E}\,\frac{1}{\sinh\kappa L},\quad\kappa>0,

(88)

\displaystyle N_{\text{e},\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}dx\,\,\psi_{2}^{2}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{-L}^{0}dx\,\,\psi_{2}^{2}(x-nL)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(-1)^{\alpha}}{4m}\,\frac{\partial\psi_{2}(L)}{\partial E}\,\frac{1}{\sinh\kappa L},\quad\kappa<0.

(89)

These expressions are derived with help of (
188
), (
202
) and (
19
), (
203
). We remark that at the edge state energy
E=\epsilon_{\text{e},\alpha}
it always holds
(-1)^{\alpha}\,\frac{\partial\psi_{2}(L)}{\partial E}>0
(see the discussion in Appendix
D
).

III.5
Phase dependence of edge states

If we shift the potential
V(x)
towards the hard wall by
x_{\varphi}

\epsilon_{\text{e},\alpha}
will change its position within the band gap
\alpha
. At some values of
x_{\varphi}

\epsilon_{\text{e},\alpha}(x_{\varphi})

x_{\varphi}

We start with expressing the fundamental system

V^{(\varphi)}(x)=V(x+x_{\varphi})

(90)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}^{(\varphi)}(x)

(91)

Applying the defining relations

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{(\varphi)}(0)

\displaystyle=1,\quad\psi_{1}^{(\varphi)\,\prime}(0)=0,

(92)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}^{(\varphi)}(0)

\displaystyle=0,\quad\psi_{2}^{(\varphi)\,\prime}(0)=1,

(93)

\displaystyle=c_{11}\,\psi_{1}(x_{\varphi})+c_{12}\,\psi_{2}(x_{\varphi}),

(95)

and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle D^{(\varphi)}(E)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\{\psi_{1}^{(\varphi)}(L)+\psi_{2}^{(\varphi)\,\prime}(L)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\{\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x_{\varphi})\,\psi_{1}(L+x_{\varphi})-\psi^{\prime}_{1}(x_{\varphi})\,\psi_{2}(L+x_{\varphi})\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\psi_{2}(x_{\varphi})\,\psi^{\prime}_{1}(L+x_{\varphi})+\psi_{1}(x_{\varphi})\,\psi^{\prime}_{2}(L+x_{\varphi})\right\},

and employing (
187
), (

\displaystyle D^{(\varphi)}(E)=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\psi_{1}(L)+\psi^{\prime}_{2}(L)\right\}=D(E).

(103)

Due to this property all band edges are flat with respect to
x_{\varphi}
in Fig.
4

We also note that the relation (
60

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{k\alpha}^{(\varphi)\,\prime}(0)}{\psi_{k\alpha}^{(\varphi)}(0)}=\frac{\psi^{\prime}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})}{\psi_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})}.

(104)

With help of (

91

III.5

The edge state conditions in the shifted system

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\alpha}\,e^{\kappa(x_{\varphi})L}

III.6
Band-touching points and vorticities

At the band-touching point
x^{*}_{\varphi}
the condition

\displaystyle\kappa(x_{\varphi}^{*})=0

(108)

is fulfilled.
Following Ref. [
hatsugai_1993
], we assign to this point the vorticity

\displaystyle\text{sign}\,\frac{d\kappa(x_{\varphi}^{*})}{dx_{\varphi}}.

(109)

Using in the following the right system (
x>0
) as the reference one, we observe that the vorticity
-1
counts an edge state entering the band (change of
\kappa
from positive to negative), while the vorticity
+1
counts an edge state leaving the band (change of
\kappa
from negative to positive).

Evaluating (
109
) on the basis of (
107
) and (
187
), (
188
) we obtain

\displaystyle\text{sign}\,\frac{d\kappa(x_{\varphi}^{*})}{dx_{\varphi}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\alpha}\,\text{sign}\,\left[\psi^{\prime}_{1}(L)\,\frac{d}{dx_{\varphi}}\frac{\psi_{2}(x_{\varphi}^{*})}{\psi_{1}(x_{\varphi}^{*})}\right]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\alpha}\,\text{sign}\,\left[\psi^{\prime}_{1}(L)\right]

(110)

and

\displaystyle\text{sign}\,\frac{d\kappa(x_{\varphi}^{*})}{dx_{\varphi}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\alpha}\,\text{sign}\,\left[\psi_{2}(L)\,\frac{d}{dx_{\varphi}}\frac{\psi_{1}(x_{\varphi}^{*})}{\psi_{2}(x_{\varphi}^{*})}\right]

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{\alpha}\,\text{sign}\,\left[\psi_{2}(L)\right].

(111)

From these expressions (which are equivalent, see below) we make an important conclusion: The vorticity value is a property of the band edge, i.e. it has the same value for all touching points at the same band edge. Distinguishing between the top edge of the band
\alpha
and the bottom edge of the band
(\alpha+1)
, both facing the same band gap
\alpha
, and using (
218
) we establish

\displaystyle\text{sign}\,\frac{d\kappa(x_{\varphi}^{*})}{dx_{\varphi}}\bigg|_{\alpha+1,\,\text{bottom}}

\displaystyle=-1,

(112)

\displaystyle\text{sign}\,\frac{d\kappa(x_{\varphi}^{*})}{dx_{\varphi}}\bigg|_{\alpha,\,\text{top}}

\displaystyle=+1.

(113)

Due to the continuity of
\epsilon_{\text{e},\alpha}(x_{\varphi})
in
x_{\varphi}
the edge state trajectory for the right system originates in the lower band (at the top of the band
\alpha
) and terminates in the upper band (at the bottom of the band
\alpha+1
).

To demonstrate the equivalence of (
110
) and (
111
), we relate the functions
\psi^{\prime}_{1}(L)
and
\psi_{2}(L)
at the band edge, where
D(E)=(-1)^{\alpha}
, via

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}_{1}(L)\,\psi_{2}(L)

\displaystyle=-1+\psi^{\prime}_{2}(L)\,\psi_{1}(L)

\displaystyle=-1+[2(-1)^{\alpha}-\psi_{1}(L)]\,\,\psi_{1}(L)

\displaystyle=-[(-1)^{\alpha}-\psi_{1}(L)]^{2}\,\leq\,0.

(114)

This means that
\text{sign}\,[\psi^{\prime}_{1}(L)]=-\text{sign}\,[\psi_{2}(L)]
(unless we have a touching point at
x_{\varphi}^{*}=0
, which can be always excluded by choosing a different reference system).

The observation about the vorticity values made above allows us to conclude
that

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}^{(-)}=M_{\alpha+1}^{(+)},

III.7
Bulk-boundary correspondence

To prove the bulk-boundary correspondence, we have to show that

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}^{(-)}-M_{\alpha}^{(+)}=C_{1,\alpha}=+1.

Let us prove that

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}^{(-)}

\displaystyle=\alpha,

(117)

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}^{(+)}

\displaystyle=\alpha-1.

First, we summarize the conditions satisfied at the touching points. Combining (

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(L)\,\psi_{1}(x^{*}_{\varphi})+\psi_{2}(x^{*}_{\varphi})\,\psi^{\prime}_{2}(L)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\alpha}\,\psi_{2}(x^{*}_{\varphi}).

Eliminating

we express this condition in the form

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(x_{\varphi}^{*})\,\psi_{2}(L)-[\psi_{1}(L)-(-1)^{\alpha}]\,\psi_{2}(x_{\varphi}^{*})=0.

Hereby one can recognize an equation for the roots of the periodic (even
\alpha

A problem of counting zeros of periodic and antiperiodic solutions of a linear differential equation with periodic coefficients has a long tradition in the mathematical literature [

;
mckean
]. Adapting the existing statements and their proofs initially formulated in Ref. [

\displaystyle M^{(-)}_{\alpha}=M_{\alpha+1}^{(+)}=\alpha.

The discussed property is demonstrated in Fig.

with the slope

on the interval

III.8
Total boundary charge

Setting the chemical potential

IV
Interface charge

This section discusses the properties of interface charges
Q_{I}
that accumulate at interfaces with nontrivial conjunctions of two half-infinite subsystems. Similarly to our proceedings in Sec.
III
and particularly in Ref. [
RatBCPlet
] (see Appendix C therein) we determine the scattering eigenstates by modelling the interface microscopically. This construction, in turn, provides the scattering matrix that has already been shown to be determining for the properties at the interface [
fulga_2011
,
fulga_2012
]. In addition, we construct interface localized states — exponentially localized states with bilateral support. Energetically these states reside in the gaps between bands formed by the scattering eigenstates.

At the interface we allow for a mutual phase mismatch
x_{\varphi}\neq x_{\varphi\prime}
of the potentials in the two subsystems on the right and left in addition to a local impurity potential
\lambda\,\delta(x)
. Fixing the value of
x_{\varphi^{\prime}}
and cyclically pumping
x_{\varphi}
, we establish a number of similarities between
Q_{I}(x_{\varphi})
and the boundary charge discussed in the previous section. In particular, we derive for
Q_{I}(x_{\varphi})
analogs of the universal expressions (
66
) and (
123
). In doing so we identify a function
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})
whose windings and phase singular points play a tremendous determining role in describing a spectral flow of the interface problem, not to speak of the equation
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})=0
determining the interface localized states.

It is then recognized that the linear change of
Q_{I}(x_{\varphi})
is associated with an incomplete compensation of the dipole-moment contributions (
37
) from the right and left subsystems, which accords with the main motif of Ref. [
RatBCPlet
]. Other changes to
Q_{I}(x_{\varphi})
are found to be discontinuously integer-valued, and this serves as an additional justification for the nearsightedness principle, which was heuristically postulated in Ref. [
NearSightednessPrin
].

IV.1
Scattering eigenstates

We consider the periodic potential
V^{(\varphi)}(x)=V(x+x_{\varphi})
in the right half-space, and the periodic potential
V^{(\varphi^{\prime})}(x)=V(x+x_{\varphi^{\prime}})
in the left half-space. In addition, we add an extra impurity potential at the interface

In this subsection we construct scattering eigenstates of this model using the two bases,
\Psi_{k\alpha}^{(\varphi)}(x)
and

We make the following ansatz for the scattering eigenstates

\displaystyle=\Theta(-x)\,\left[\Psi_{k\alpha}^{(\varphi^{\prime})}(x)+r_{k\alpha}\,\Psi_{-k,\alpha}^{(\varphi^{\prime})}(x)\right]

labeled by

) we similarly obtain Eq. (
166
) in terms of
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}
as

\displaystyle Q_{I,\alpha}=

\displaystyle-1+\frac{x_{\varphi}-x_{\varphi^{\prime}}}{L}-\text{wn}\,[\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}].

(169)

This is the first main result of this section. It establishes the universal form of a band’s contribution to the interface charge. This form is similar to the one obtained for the boundary charge (
66
). The phase of the function
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}
plays here a role similar to that of the phase
e^{i\Phi_{k\alpha}}
in the boundary problem.

Without loss of generality we set
x_{\varphi^{\prime}}=0
. Then, taking into account (
20
) as well as (
135
), (
136
), we conclude

\displaystyle\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})=

\displaystyle-[-\psi_{2}(x_{\varphi}-L)+\psi_{2}(x_{\varphi})e^{ikL}]

\displaystyle\times[-\psi_{1}(L)+e^{-ikL}+2m\lambda\,\psi_{2}(L)]

\displaystyle+[-\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x_{\varphi}-L)+\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x_{\varphi})e^{ikL}]\,\psi_{2}(L).

(170)

Considering
Q_{I,\alpha}
as a function of
x_{\varphi}
, we notice its periodicity
Q_{I,\alpha}(x_{\varphi})=Q_{I,\alpha}(x_{\varphi}+L)
, which is due to the properties
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi}+L)=\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})e^{ikL}
and
\text{wn}\,[\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi}+L)]=\text{wn}\,[\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})]+1
. Note that the latter follow from the definitions (
170
) and (
68
) of
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})
and of the winding number, respectively.

The behavior
Q_{I,\alpha=2}(x_{\varphi})
is illustrated in Figs.
7
(e) (for
\lambda=1
) and
8
(e) (for
\lambda=-1
) as well as in the analogous panels in Appendix
H
(for other values of
\lambda
). For a detection of the touching points we plot the real-valued functions
\tilde{d}_{\frac{\pi}{L},2}(x_{\varphi})
(for the bottom band edge) and
\tilde{d}_{0,2}(x_{\varphi})
(for the top band edge) and determine their roots. Their roots give the band’s touching points at the corresponding edge. Whenever the localized state enters/leaves the band, the value of
Q_{I,\alpha=2}(x_{\varphi})
jumps by
\pm 1
. A relation between the winding number change and the spectral flow of the localized states into/out of the bands is discussed in greater detail in the next subsection.

IV.4
Total interface charge

The total interface charge in the system with the chemical potential
\mu_{\nu}
at the top of the
\nu
th band is given by a sum of contributions from all states below
\mu_{\nu}
,

\displaystyle Q_{I}^{(\nu)}(x_{\varphi})=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}Q_{I,\alpha}(x_{\varphi})+\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\nu-1}Q_{\text{i},\alpha}(x_{\varphi}).

(171)

Here
Q_{\text{i},\alpha}(x_{\varphi})
is a number of the interface localized states in the gap
\alpha
. Note that in contrast to (
122
) the summation in the second term of (
171
) begins from
\alpha=0
(”zeroth gap”, i.e. the energy range beneath the lowest band), since an interface localized can occur there as well.

To establish the
x_{\varphi}
dependence of
Q_{I}^{(\nu)}
, we first derive
Q_{I}^{(\nu)}(0)
at finite
\lambda
, that is the zero phase mismatch result. Then, exploiting the continuity of the spectral flow with
x_{\varphi}
, we address the sought-after dependence.

In the zero mismatch limit
x_{\varphi}=0
, the bands’ contributions are evaluated in terms of

\displaystyle\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(0)=2\,\psi_{2}(L)\,[i\sin kL-m\lambda\,\psi_{2}(L)].

(172)

This expression immediately follows from (
170
) after applying the defining relations of the functions
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
, stated after Eq. (
2
), and the properties (
189
), (
190
).
Since the function
\psi_{2}(L)=\psi_{2}(L,\epsilon_{k\alpha})
does not change its sign within the band
\alpha
[see (
218
)], the complex function
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(0)
does not wind with
k
around the origin, leaving
\text{wn}\,[\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(0)]=0
. Then from (
169
) it follows that at
x_{\varphi}=x_{\varphi^{\prime}}=0
each band provides the
-1
contribution to the interface charge, that is
Q_{I,\alpha}(0)=-1
.

To compute
Q_{I}^{(\nu)}(0)
by means of (
171
), we also need to establish the number of localized states. At
x_{\varphi}=x_{\varphi^{\prime}}=0
they obey the equation

\displaystyle-(-1)^{\alpha}m\lambda\,\psi_{2}(L)=\sinh\kappa L,

(173)

which follows from
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(0)=0
[note that
\psi_{2}(L)=0
does not give a nontrivial solution inside a gap away from bands’ edges]. It is convenient to rewrite (
173
) as

\displaystyle-m\lambda\,\frac{\psi_{2}(L)}{D(E)}=\frac{\sqrt{D^{2}(E)-1}}{|D(E)|}.

(174)

According to (
218
) and (
204
), the function in the left-hand side is monotonically decreasing across the gap from a positive to a negative value for
\lambda>0
, and it is monotonically increasing from a negative to a positive value for
\lambda<0
. In turn, the function in the right-hand side is positive within the gap and vanishes at its ends. Since both sides of (
174
) are continuous in
E
, they have a single intersection point for any
\lambda\neq 0
, which gives the energy of the interface localized state. Thus, each gap hosts one localized state.

It is necessary to additionally inspect the ”zeroth gap”, i.e. the energy range below the first band. The right-hand side of (
174
) is monotonically decreasing
from
+1
(at
E=-\infty
) down to
0^{+}
. Remarking that

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle E\to-\infty}}{{\approx}}\frac{\sinh(L\sqrt{2m|E|})}{\sqrt{2m|E|}},

(175)

we conclude that the left-hand side of (
174
) for
\lambda>0
is monotonically decreasing from
0^{-}
(at
E=-\infty
) to a negative value, and for
\lambda<0
it is monotonically increasing from
0^{+}
(at
E=-\infty
) to a positive value. Thus, only in the latter case we obtain an additional localized solution. It is a remnant of the bound state in the attractive (
\lambda<0
) delta-potential and the flat background potential, which has the energy
E=-\frac{m\lambda^{2}}{2}
[note that this result is easily recovered from (
174
) with help of (
175
)].

Summarizing the above results in the zero mismatch limit
x_{\varphi}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle Q_{I}^{(\nu)}(0)=\begin{cases}-1,&\lambda>0,\\
0,&\lambda<0.\end{cases}

(176)

Next, we investigate how these universal values change with
x_{\varphi}
.

Figure 10:
A sketch of the proof of (
179
). a) Fixing notations for the present and absent localized state before and after the touching point, respectively. b) The winding number changes upon traversing the touching point: The contour of
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi})
is swept across the origin. The precise value of the change (
+1
or
-1
) depends on a mutual orientation of the normal and the tangent vectors to the contour in the vicinity of the origin.

Moving along the localized state dispersion in
x_{\varphi}
[Fig.
10
(a)], we establish that at the touching point
x_{\varphi}^{*}
[obeying the equation
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi}^{*})=0
with
\text{Re}\,k=0,\pi/L
and
\kappa=0
]
it holds

\displaystyle\frac{dE(x_{\varphi}^{*})}{dx_{\varphi}}=(-1)^{\alpha}L\,\sinh\kappa L\,\frac{d\kappa(x_{\varphi}^{*})}{dx_{\varphi}}=0,

(177)

as well as

\displaystyle\frac{d\kappa(x_{\varphi}^{*})}{dx_{\varphi}}=-\frac{\frac{\partial\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}}{\partial x_{\varphi}}}{\frac{\partial\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}}{\partial\kappa}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\alpha}\frac{\partial\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}}{\partial x_{\varphi}}}{L\psi_{2}(L)[\psi_{1}(x_{\varphi}^{*})+\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x_{\varphi}^{*})-2m\lambda\,\psi_{2}(x_{\varphi}^{*})]}.

(178)

On the other hand, looking at the touching point from the band’s perspective, we notice that the winding number of
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}
associated with the band
\alpha
changes upon going across the touching point by

\displaystyle\Delta\,\text{wn}\,[\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}]

\displaystyle=\text{wn}\,[\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi}^{*}+\varepsilon)]-\text{wn}\,[\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}(x_{\varphi}^{*}-\varepsilon)]

\displaystyle=\text{sign}\,\left[\frac{\partial\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}}{\partial x_{\varphi}}\,\frac{\partial\,\text{Im}\,\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}}{\partial k}\right],

(179)

where

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\,\text{Im}\,\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}}{\partial k}=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\alpha}L\psi_{2}(L)

\displaystyle\times[\psi_{1}(x_{\varphi}^{*})+\psi^{\prime}_{2}(x_{\varphi}^{*})-2m\lambda\,\psi_{2}(x_{\varphi}^{*})].

(180)

The derivation of (
179
) is sketched in Fig.
10
(b). For
k=0,\pi/L
the function
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}
becomes real. These values correspond to points on the real axis, where the parametric plot of
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}
(or the loop) intersects the horizontal axis. Moving one of these points (say, the left one, as shown on the sketch) across the origin (where the touching point condition is satisfied) by means of tuning
x_{\varphi}
leads to a change in the winding number. This change (by either
+1
or
-1
) depends on the orientation of the tangent vector to the loop in the vicinity of the origin. A careful inspection of all possible scenarios leads us to the expression (
179
).

By analogy with (
109
) we introduce the
interface vorticity
as a sign of (
178
). It quantifies whether the localized interface state leaves the band (the value
+1
indicates the change of
\kappa
from negative to positive) or enters the band (the value
-1
indicates the change of
\kappa
from positive to negative). Comparing (
178
) with (
179
), (
180
) we show that this vorticity equals

\displaystyle\text{sign}\,\left[\frac{d\kappa_{\alpha}(x_{\varphi}^{*})}{dx_{\varphi}}\bigg|_{\alpha}\right]=\Delta\,\text{wn}\,[\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}].

(181)

Here in the left-hand side we additionally mark that the localized state in the gap
\alpha
(encoded in
\kappa_{\alpha}
) touches the band
\alpha
(encoded in the lowest subscript
\alpha
). Analogously we show that upon touching the upper band it holds

\displaystyle\text{sign}\,\left[\frac{d\kappa_{\alpha}(x_{\varphi}^{*})}{dx_{\varphi}}\bigg|_{\alpha+1}\right]=\Delta\,\text{wn}\,[\tilde{d}_{k,\alpha+1}].

(182)

The expressions (
181
) and (
182
) have a transparent physical meaning: When the localized state enters/leaves the band, the band’s contribution to the total interface charge changes accordingly, so that there is no net contribution to
Q_{I}^{(\nu)}
. The only uncompensated changes occur at the topmost occupied band edge (since we put there the chemical potential and therefore do not count the localized states touching that band edge). Hence we obtain the following expression for the total interface charge

\displaystyle Q_{I}^{(\nu)}(x_{\varphi})=

\displaystyle Q_{I}^{(\nu)}(0)+\frac{\nu}{L}x_{\varphi}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{\nu+l}\Theta(x_{\varphi,j}^{-})+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\Theta(x_{\varphi,j}^{+}).

(183)

This is the second main result of this section: It states the universal dependence of the total interface charge on the pumping parameter
x_{\varphi}
.
It is similar to (
123
), the main difference being that the localized state energy may now both enter and leave a band at the same band edge. Here
\{x_{\varphi,j}^{\pm}\}
is a (model-specific) set of points, where the interface localized state enters/leaves the topmost occupied band edge. The difference between the numbers of
x_{\varphi,j}^{-}
and
x_{\varphi,j}^{+}
must be exactly equal to
\nu
: This is needed to ensure the periodicity
Q_{I}^{(\nu)}(x_{\varphi}+L)=Q_{I}^{(\nu)}(x_{\varphi})
.

The behavior
Q_{I}^{(3)}(x_{\varphi})
for the three occupied bands is illustrated in Figs.

8
(b) (for
\lambda=-1
) as well as in the analogous panels in Appendix
H
for the values
\lambda=\pm 0.1
, which additionally give an idea about the behavior of
Q_{I}^{(3)}(x_{\varphi})
close to
\lambda=0
.

V
Summary

In this work, we have studied universal properties of the boundary change
Q_{B}(x_{\varphi})
in one-dimensional single-channel continuum insulator models. We have found a rigorous proof of the linear dependence of
Q_{B}
on the phase
x_{\varphi}
of the periodic potential modulation. In continuum models, this result appears in an undisguised form: All nonuniversal
\frac{2\pi}{Za}
-periodic contributions previously found in the lattice model considerations [
pletyukhov_etal_prb_20
] are suppressed in the continuum limit
Z\to\infty
(where
Z
is a number of sites in a unit cell, and
a
is a lattice spacing).

The total boundary charge consists of both band contributions and potentially edge states residing in the band gaps. We have extended the earlier findings [
thakurati_etal_18
,
pletyukhov_etal_prb_20
] for both the individual band contributions
Q_{B,\alpha}
and the total boundary charge to the class of continuum models studied here. In particular, in the first case the slope of the linear dependence is given by the first Chern index
C_{1,\alpha}
of band
\alpha
, which acquires the same value
C_{1,\alpha}=1
for each band. This value is in tune with the difference between the numbers
M_{\alpha}^{(\mp)}
of the touching points, at which the edge state dispersions (evolved in
x_{\varphi}
) leave and enter the band. We have shown that over one pumping cycle
x_{\varphi}\to x_{\varphi}+L
(i.e. pushing the periodic potential towards the boundary over one period), in continuum models, there are exactly
\alpha
edge states which leave the band
\alpha
from its top side, and
\alpha-1
edge states which enter the band from its bottom side. At the corresponding touching points,
Q_{B,\alpha}
acquires discontinuous jump contributions
\pm 1
in such a way that the periodicity
Q_{B,\alpha}(x_{\varphi}+L)=Q_{B,\alpha}(x_{\varphi})
is maintained. Thus, the equality
C_{1,\alpha}=M_{\alpha}^{(-)}-M_{\alpha}^{(+)}
is not only a manifestation of the bulk-boundary correspondence, but also an expression for charge conservation.

An analogous result has been obtained for the total boundary charge
Q_{B}^{(\nu)}
in a system with
\nu
fully occupied bands: The slope
\nu
(with respect to
\frac{x_{\varphi}}{L}
) of the linear dependence is compensated by the occurrence of exactly
\nu
edges states removing a single-electron charge during one pumping cycle.

Studying the boundary charge fluctuations, we have confirmed the earlier stated surface fluctuation theorem [
weber_etal_prl_20
,
wannier_paper
], which expresses the universal low-energy
1/E_{g}
scaling of these fluctuations with the gap size
E_{g}
.

The other interesting facet of the excess charges studied in the present work is the interface charge
Q_{I}
and its universal properties. Bringing two semi-infinite systems with different phases
x_{\varphi}\neq x_{\varphi^{\prime}}
of the periodic potential modulation into contact and introducing an additional delta-potential barrier between them, we have revealed similar universal dependencies of
Q_{I}
on
x_{\varphi}
at fixed
x_{\varphi^{\prime}}
, i.e., a slope 1 for the single band contribution and a slope
\nu
for the total interface charge (in a system with
\nu
occupied bands). To restore the periodicity
Q_{I}(x_{\varphi})=Q_{I}(x_{\varphi}+L)
, the linear growth is accompanied by discontinuous jumps
\pm 1
at the points where the interface localized state enters/leaves the band. Thereby, the required charge conservation is respected. However, in interface models there is no analog of the bulk-boundary correspondence, since the spectral flow of the interface localized states is in accord with changes in winding numbers of a novel function called
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}
, and not with those of the phases of the Bloch states. The function
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}
which we introduced not only represents a specific combination of the Bloch states in the bulk of both right and left subsystems, but also contains information about their interface. It connects to the denominator of the scattering matrix, and thus inherits the analytic features of the reflection coefficient. We have elucidated the tremendous role of the function
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}
in describing the universal properties of the interface charge; in particular, the contribution from its winding number to
Q_{I}
and the equation
\tilde{d}_{k\alpha}=0
to determine interface localized states. The integer-valued changes of
Q_{I}
analysed in our study, which are associated with the winding number changes and an emergence/cease of localized states, serve as a manifestation of the nearsightedness principle [
NearSightednessPrin
].

A perspective extension of the present work consists in studying a multichannel generalization of the continuum one-dimensional insulator models and revealing similar dependencies for both boundary and interface charges [
mueller_etal_2021
].

1.
Introduction

Let
U(FG)
be the group of invertible elements of the group algebra
FG
of a group
G
over a field
F
. The study of units and their properties is one of the most challenging problems in the theory of group rings. Explicit calculations in
U(FG)
are usually difficult, even when
G
is fairly small and
F
is a finite field. The results obtained in this direction are also useful for the investigation of the Lie properties of group rings, the isomorphism problem and other open questions in this area, see
[
2
]
.

Let
J(FG)
be the Jacobson radical of
FG
and let
V=1+J(FG)
. The
F
-algebra
FG/J(FG)
is semisimple whenever
G
is a finite group. It is known from the Wedderburn structure theorem that

FG/J(FG)\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{r}M(n_{i},K_{i})

where
r
is the number of non-isomorphic irreducible
FG
modules,
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
K_{i}
’s are finite dimensional division algebras over
F
. In this context a result by Ferraz
[
3
, Theorem 1.3 and Prop 1.2]
) is very useful in determining the Wedderburn decomposition of
FG/J(FG)
.

If
FG
is semisimple, then
J(FG)=0
and by
[
8
, Prop 3.6.11]
,

FG\cong F(G/G^{\prime})\oplus\Delta(G,G^{\prime})

where
F(G/G^{\prime})
is the sum of all the commutative simple components of
FG
, whereas
\Delta(G,G^{\prime})
is the sum of all the non-commutative simple components of
FG
. We conclude that, if
FG
is semisimple, then

FG\cong F(G/G^{\prime})\oplus(\oplus_{i=1}^{l}M(n_{i},F_{i})).

Now, if
dim_{F}(Z(FG))=r
and if the number of commutative simple components is
s
, then
l\leq r-s
.

Many authors
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
have studied the structure of
U(FG)
for a finite group
G
and for a finite field
F
. The structure of
U(F(C_{3}\times D_{10}))
for
p=3
is given in
[
11
]
.
In this article, we provide an explicit description for the Wedderburn decomposition of
FG/J(FG)
,
G=C_{3}\times D_{10}
and
F
a finite field of characteristic
p\neq 3
, using the theory developed by Ferraz
[
3
]
. With the help of this description we obtain the structure of
U(F(C_{3}\times D_{10}))
.

Our notations are same as in
[
1
,
10
]
.

2.
Structure of
U(F(C_{3}\times D_{10}))

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
F
be a finite field of characteristic
p
with
|F|=q=p^{k}
and let
G=C_{3}\times D_{10}
.

(i)

If
p=2
, then
U(FG)\cong

\begin{cases}C_{2}^{3k}\rtimes\big(C_{2^{k}-1}^{3}\times GL(2,F)^{6}\big),&\text{if $q\equiv 1,4$ mod $15$;}\\
C_{2}^{3k}\rtimes\big(C_{2^{k}-1}\times C_{2^{2k}-1}\times GL(2,F_{2})^{3}\big),&\text{ if $q\equiv 2,-7$ mod $15$.}\\
\end{cases}

(ii)

If
p=5
, then

U(FG)\cong V\rtimes\begin{cases}C_{5^{k}-1}^{6},&\text{if $q\equiv 1$ mod $6$};\\
C_{5^{k}-1}^{2}\times C_{5^{2k}-1}^{2},&\text{if $q\equiv-1$ mod $6$}.\end{cases}

where

V\cong(C_{5}^{15k}\rtimes C_{5}^{6k})\rtimes C_{5}^{3k}
and
Z(V)\cong C_{5}^{9k}
.

(iii)

If
p>5
, then
U(FG)\cong

\begin{cases}C_{p^{k}-1}^{6}\times GL(2,F)^{6},&\text{if $q\equiv 1,-11$ mod $30$;}\\
C_{p^{k}-1}^{2}\times C_{p^{2k}-1}^{2}\times GL(2,F)^{2}\times GL(2,F_{2})^{2},&\text{ if $q\equiv-1,11$ mod $30$;}\\
C_{p^{k}-1}^{6}\times GL(2,F_{2})^{3},&\text{ if $q\equiv 7,13$ mod $30$;}\\
C_{p^{k}-1}^{2}\times C_{p^{2k}-1}^{2}\times GL(2,F_{2})^{3},&\text{ if $q\equiv-7,-13$ mod $30$.}\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
G=\langle x,y,z\mid x^{2}=y^{5}=z^{3}=1,xy=y^{4}x,xz=zx,yz=zy\rangle.
The conjugacy classes in
G
are:

\displaystyle[z^{i}]

\displaystyle=\{z^{i}\}\text{ for }i=0,1,2;

\displaystyle[yz^{i}]

\displaystyle=\{y^{\pm 1}z^{i}\}\text{ for }i=0,1,2;

\displaystyle[y^{2}z^{i}]

\displaystyle=\{y^{\pm 2}z^{i}\}\text{ for }i=0,1,2;

\displaystyle[xz^{i}]

\displaystyle=\{xz^{i},xy^{\pm 1}z^{i},xy^{\pm 2}z^{i}\}\text{ for }i=0,1,2.

(i)

p=2
. Clearly,
\widehat{T_{2}}=1+x\widehat{y}
.

Let
\alpha=\sum_{k=0}^{1}\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=5(j+3k)}^{5(j+3k)+4}a_{i}x^{k}y^{i-5(j+3k)}z^{j}
. If
\alpha\widehat{T_{2}}=0
, then we have

\alpha+\sum_{k=0}^{1}\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=5(j+3k)}^{5(j+3k)+4}a_{i}x^{k+1}\widehat{y}z^{j}=0.

For
k=0,1,2
and
i=0,1,2,3,4
this yields the following equations:

\displaystyle a_{5k+i}+\sum_{j=0}^{4}a_{5k+j+15}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle a_{5k+15+i}+\sum_{j=0}^{4}a_{5k+j}

\displaystyle=0.

After simplification we get,
a_{5k}=a_{5k+i}=a_{5k+i+15}
for
i=0,1,2,3,4
and
k=0,1,2
. Hence

Ann(\widehat{T_{2}})=\{\sum_{i=0}^{2}\beta_{i}(1+x)\widehat{y}z^{i}\mid\beta_{i}\in F\}.

Since
z,\widehat{y}\in Z(FG)
,
Ann^{2}(\widehat{T_{2}})=0
and
Ann(\widehat{T_{2}})\subseteq J(FG)
. Thus by
[
12
, Lemma 2.2]
,
J(FG)=Ann(\widehat{T_{2}})
and
dim_{F}(J(FG))=3
. Hence
V\cong C_{2}^{3k}

and by
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
,

U(FG)\cong C_{2}^{3k}\rtimes U(FG/J(FG)).

Now it only remains to find the Wedderburn decomposition of
FG/J(FG)
.

As
[1]
,
[y]
,
[y^{2}]
,
[z]
,
[z^{2}]
,
[yz]
,
[yz^{2}]
,
[y^{2}z]
, and
[y^{2}z^{2}]
are the
2
-regular conjugacy classes of
G
,
m=15
and
dim_{F}(FG/J(FG))=27
. Now the following cases occur:

(a)

If
q\equiv 1,4
mod
15
, then
|S_{F}(\gamma_{g})|=1
for
g=1
,
y
,
y^{2}
,
z
,
z^{2}
,
yz
,
yz^{2}
,
y^{2}z
,
y^{2}z^{2}
. Consquently,
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
, yields nine components in the decomposition of
FG/J(FG)
. In view of the dimension requirements, the only possibility is:

FG/J(FG)\cong F^{3}\oplus M(2,F)^{6}.

(b)

If
q\equiv 2,-7
mod
15
, then

|S_{F}(\gamma_{g})|=1
for
g=1
and
|S_{F}(\gamma_{g})|=2
for
g=y
,
z
,
yz
,
yz^{2}
. So, due to the dimension restrictions, we have

FG/J(FG)\cong F\oplus F_{2}\oplus M(2,F_{2})^{3}.

(ii)

p=5
. If
K=\langle y\rangle
, then
G/K\cong H\cong\langle x,z\rangle\cong C_{6}
. Thus
from the ring epimorphism
\eta:FG\rightarrow FH
, given by

\displaystyle\eta(\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{4}y^{i}z^{j}(a_{i+5j}+a_{i+5j+15}x))=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{4}z^{j}(a_{i+5j}+a_{i+5j+15}x),

we get a group epimorphism
\phi\colon U(FG)\rightarrow U(FH)
and
ker\phi\cong 1+J(FG)=V
. Further, we have the inclusion map

i\colon U(FH)\rightarrow U(FG)
such that
\phi i=1_{U(FH)}
. Thus
U(FG)\cong V\rtimes U(FC_{6})
.

The structure of
U(FC_{6})
is given in
[
10
, Theorem 4.1]
.

If
v=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{4}y^{i}z^{j}(a_{i+5j}+a_{i+5j+15}x)\in U(FG)
, then
v\in V
if and only if
\sum_{i=0}^{4}a_{i}=1
and
\sum_{i=0}^{4}a_{i+5k}=0
for
k=1,2,3,4,5
.
Hence

V=\{1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}(y^{i}-1)z^{j}(b_{i+4j}+b_{i+4j+12}x)\mid b_{i}\in F\}

and
|V|=5^{24k}
. Since,
J(FG)^{5}=0
,
V^{5}=1
.

Now we show that
V\cong(C_{5}^{15k}\rtimes C_{5}^{6k})\rtimes C_{5}^{3k}
. The proof is split into the following steps:

Step 1:
Let
R=\{1+ay(1-y)^{3}x\mid a\in F\}\subseteq V
. Then
R\cong C_{5}^{k}
.

If

r_{1}=1+ay(1-y)^{3}x\in R

and

r_{2}=1+by(1-y)^{3}x\in R

where
a,b\in F
, then

r_{1}r_{2}=1+(a+b)y(1-y)^{3}x\in R.

Therefore,
R
is an abelian subgroup of
V
of order
5^{k}
. Hence
R\cong C_{5}^{k}
.

Step 2:

|C_{V}(R)|=5^{21k}
, where
C_{V}(R)=\{v\in V\mid r^{v}=r\text{ for all }r\in R\}
.

Let

r=1+ay(1-y)^{3}x\in R

and

v=1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}(y^{i}-1)z^{j}(b_{i+4j}+b_{i+4j+12}x)\in V

where
a,b_{i}\in F
. Then

v=1+v_{1}+v_{2}x
,
v_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}b_{i+4j}(y^{i}-1)z^{j}
and
v_{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}b_{i+4j+12}(y^{i}-1)z^{j}
. So
v^{-1}=v^{4}=1+4v_{1}+4v_{2}x
mod
(y-1)^{2}FG
. Thus

\displaystyle r^{v}=1+v^{-1}ay(1-y)^{3}xv=r+2a\widehat{y}\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}ib_{i+4j}z^{j}x.

Thus
r^{v}=r
if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{4}ib_{i+4j}=0
for
j=0,1,2
. Hence

\displaystyle C_{V}(R)=\{1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}[(y^{i}-1)+i(y^{4}-1)]c_{i+3j}z^{j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}(y^{i}-1)c_{i+4j+9}z^{j}x\mid c_{i}\in F\}

and
|C_{V}(R)|=5^{21k}
.

Step 3:

C_{V}(R)\cong C_{5}^{15k}\rtimes C_{5}^{6k}
.

Consider the sets

S=\{1+y^{3}(y-1)^{2}[yb_{1}+y(y+2)b_{2}+b_{3}+(yb_{4}+(y+1)^{2}b_{5})x]\}

and

T=\{1+y^{3}(y-1)[(y-1)(yc_{1}+(y+1)^{2}c_{2})+(yc_{3}+(y^{2}+y+1)c_{4})x]\}

where
b_{1+j}=\sum_{i=0}^{2}p_{i+3j}z^{i}
for
j=0,1,2,3,4
and
c_{1+j}=\sum_{i=0}^{2}q_{i+3j}z^{i}
for
j=0,1,2,3
. With some computation it can be shown that
S
and
T
are abelian subgroups of
C_{V}(R)
. So
S\cong C_{5}^{15k}
and
T\cong C_{5}^{12k}
.

Now, let

s=1+y^{3}(y-1)^{2}[yb_{1}+y(y+2)b_{2}+b_{3}+(yb_{4}+(y+1)^{2}b_{5})x]\in S

and

t=1+y^{3}(y-1)[(y-1)(yc_{1}+(y+1)^{2}c_{2})+(yc_{3}+(y^{2}+y+1)c_{4})x]\in T.

Then

\displaystyle s^{t}=

\displaystyle 1+y^{3}(y-1)^{2}\{yb_{1}+y(y+2)b_{2}+b_{3}+k_{1}y^{3}(1-y)

\displaystyle+[yb_{4}+(y+1)^{2}b_{5}+(y-1)^{2}(k_{2}+k_{3})]x\}\in S

where

\displaystyle k_{1}

\displaystyle=(c_{4}+2c_{3})(b_{4}-b_{5}),k_{2}=(c_{4}+2c_{3})(b_{2}-b_{3})

\displaystyle k_{3}

\displaystyle=2(c_{4}^{2}-c_{3}c_{4}-c_{3}^{2})(b_{4}-b_{5}).

Let

U=S\cap T=\{1+y^{3}(y-1)^{2}[yc_{1}+(y+1)^{2}c_{2}]\}

where
c_{1+j}=\sum_{i=0}^{2}q_{i+3j}z^{i}
for
j=0,1
. Thus
U\cong C_{5}^{6k}
.
So for some subgroup
W\cong C_{5}^{6k}
of
T
,
T=U\times W
and
W\cap S=1
. Hence
C_{V}(R)\cong S\rtimes W\cong C_{5}^{15k}\rtimes C_{5}^{6k}
.

Step 4:
Let
M=\{1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}r_{j}z^{j}y(y+1)^{2}(1-y)(1+x)\mid r_{i}\in F\}\subseteq V
. Then
M\cong C_{5}^{3k}
.

Let

m_{1}=1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}r_{j}z^{j}y(y+1)^{2}(1-y)(1+x)\in M

and

m_{2}=1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}s_{j}z^{j}y(y+1)^{2}(1-y)(1+x)\in M

where
r_{j},s_{j}\in F
. Then

\displaystyle m_{1}m_{2}=1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}(r_{j}+s_{j})z^{j}y(y+1)^{2}(1-y)(1+x)\in M.

Therefore,
M
is an abelian subgroup of
V
of order
5^{3k}
. Hence,
M\cong C_{5}^{3k}
.

Step 5:

V\cong C_{V}(R)\rtimes M
.

Let

\displaystyle n=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}[(y^{i}-1)+i(y^{4}-1)]c_{i+3j}z^{j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}(y^{i}-1)c_{i+4j+9}z^{j}x\in C_{V}(R)

and let

m=1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}r_{j}z^{j}y(y+1)^{2}(1-y)(1+x)\in M

where
c_{i},r_{i}\in F
. Then

\displaystyle n^{m}=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}[(y^{i}-1)+i(y^{4}-1)]c_{i+3j}z^{j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}(y^{i}-1)c_{i+4j+9}z^{j}x+(k_{1}+k_{2}x)\in C_{V}(R)

where

\displaystyle k_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2}r_{j}z^{j}\{\sum_{k=0}^{2}(c_{10+4k}-c_{11+4k}-c_{12+4k}+c_{13+4k})z^{k}

\displaystyle+3\sum_{j=0}^{2}r_{j}z^{j}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\sum_{k=0}^{2}i(c_{i+4k+9}z^{k})\}y(1-y)^{3}

\displaystyle k_{2}=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=0}^{2}r_{j}z^{j}\{\sum_{k=0}^{2}(c_{2+3k}-c_{3+3k})z^{k}(1-y)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=0}^{2}r_{j}z^{j}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\sum_{k=0}^{2}ic_{i+4k+9}z^{k}\}y(1-y)^{3}-2\sum_{j=0}^{2}r_{j}z^{j}\sum_{i=0}^{4}d_{i}y^{i}

where

\displaystyle d_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2}(4c_{10+4j}+4c_{11+4j}+c_{12+4j}+c_{13+4j})z^{j},

\displaystyle d_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2}(4c_{10+4j}+3c_{11+4j}+3c_{12+4j})z^{j},

\displaystyle d_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2}(4c_{11+4j}+3c_{12+4j}+3c_{13+4j})z^{j},

\displaystyle d_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2}(2c_{10+4j}+2c_{11+4j}+c_{12+4j})z^{j},

\displaystyle d_{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2}(2c_{11+4j}+2c_{12+4j}+c_{13+4j})z^{j}.

Clearly,
C_{V}(R)\cap M=1
. Therefore,
V=C_{V}(R)\rtimes M
.

In the sequel, we show that
Z(V)\cong C_{5}^{9k}
.

If
v=1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}(y^{i}-1)z^{j}(b_{i+4j}+b_{i+4j+12}x)\in C_{V}(y)=\{v\in V\mid vy=yv\}
, then

vy-yv=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\sum_{j=0}^{2}y(1-y^{i})(y^{3}-1)b_{i+4j+12}z^{j}x.

Thus

if and only if
b_{i}=b_{i+j}
for
j=1,2,3
and
i=13,17,21
.
Hence

C_{V}(y)=\{1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}(y^{i}-1)c_{i+4j}z^{j}+\widehat{y}\sum_{j=0}^{2}c_{j+13}z^{j}x\mid c_{i}\in F\}.

Since
Z(V)\subseteq C_{V}(y)
,

Z(V)=\{s\in C_{V}(y)\mid sv=vs\text{ for all }v\in V\}.

Let
u=1+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}(y^{i}-1)c_{i+4j}z^{j}+\widehat{y}x\sum_{j=0}^{2}c_{j+13}z^{j}\in C_{V}(y)
. Since

and
\widehat{y}\in Z(FG)
,

Thus
c_{i}=c_{i+3}
for
i=1,5,9
and
c_{j}=c_{j+1}
for

.

We conclude that

.

(iii)

If
p>5
, then
J(FG)=0
. Thus

is semisimple and

. As

, we have

Since

,

.

(a)

mod

, then

for all

. Therefore by

mod

. Thus

.

We get

. Hence,

.

mod

, then

mod

. Thus

.

.

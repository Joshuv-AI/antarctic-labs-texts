Abstract

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{c-iT}^{c+iT}\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}\frac{x^{s}}{s}\,ds+O\left(\frac{x\log^{2}x}{T}\right)

Step 1: Integral around

\Gamma

\epsilon

\displaystyle=x\left(1+\epsilon e^{i\theta}\log x+O(\epsilon^{2})\right)\left(1-\epsilon e^{i\theta}+O(\epsilon^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=x\left(1+\epsilon e^{i\theta}(\log x-1)+O(\epsilon^{2})\right)

Thus, the integrand becomes:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}\frac{x^{s}}{s}=x\left(\epsilon e^{i\theta}-\gamma\epsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+O(\epsilon^{3})\right)\left(1+\epsilon e^{i\theta}(\log x-1)+O(\epsilon^{2})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 50.0pt=x\left(\epsilon e^{i\theta}+\epsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}(\log x-1-\gamma)+O(\epsilon^{3})\right)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}\frac{x^{s}}{s}=\int\limits_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}x\left(\epsilon e^{i\theta}+\epsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}(\log x-1-\gamma)+O(\epsilon^{3})\right)i\epsilon e^{i\theta}\,d\theta

\psi(x)
:

\displaystyle\psi(x)=x-\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+O(\sqrt{x})

(16)

Proof.

A priori from the definition of
\psi(x)
involving the Von Mangoldt Function,
\Lambda(n)
, we apply the
M
\ddot{o}
bius Inversion Formula

[
15
, Section 14.1 , pp. 30]
on
\Lambda(n)
to obtain,

\displaystyle\Lambda(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\log\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)=\sum_{n\leq x}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\log\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)

(17)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\leq x}\mu(d)\sum_{k\leq\frac{x}{d}}\log(k)\hskip 20.0pt[\mbox{ Interchanging order of summation }]

(18)

(
N.B.
Here, we set
n=dk
, so that the inner sum is over
k
with
k\leq\frac{x}{d}
.)

We approximate the sum of
\log(k)
by integrating the logarithm function from 1 to
\frac{x}{d}
, and then applying Integration by Parts.

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq\frac{x}{d}}\log(k)=\int_{1}^{\frac{x}{d}}\log(t)\,dt+O\left(\log\left(\frac{x}{d}\right)\right)=\left[t\log(t)-t\right]_{1}^{\frac{x}{d}}+O\left(\log\left(\frac{x}{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{d}\log\left(\frac{x}{d}\right)-\frac{x}{d}+1+O\left(\log\left(\frac{x}{d}\right)\right)=\frac{x}{d}\log\left(\frac{x}{d}\right)-\frac{x}{d}+1+O(\log x)

\displaystyle[\mbox{ Since $\log(\frac{x}{d})$ is of the same order as $\log x$ for large $x$ }]

Subsequently, from (
18
) we get,

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\sum_{d\leq x}\mu(d)\left(\frac{x}{d}\log\left(\frac{x}{d}\right)-\frac{x}{d}+1+O(\log(x))\right)

\displaystyle=x\sum_{d\leq x}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\log\left(\frac{x}{d}\right)-x\sum_{d\leq x}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}+\sum_{d\leq x}\mu(d)+O(x\log x)\sum_{d\leq x}\mu(d)

A priori using the results obtained in proposition (
2.12
),

\displaystyle x\sum_{d\leq x}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\log\left(\frac{x}{d}\right)=x\log(x)\sum_{d\leq x}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}-x\sum_{d\leq x}\frac{\mu(d)\log(d)}{d}

(19)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle O(x\log x)\sum_{d\leq x}\mu(d)=O(x\log x)\cdot O(\sqrt{x})=O(x^{3/2}\log x)

(20)

Combining (
19
) and (
20
) yields,

\displaystyle\psi(x)=x-\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+O(x)+O(x^{3/2}\log x)

Since
x\ll x^{3/2}\log x
asymptotically, hence we conclude that,

\displaystyle\psi(x)=x-\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+O(\sqrt{x})

And the proof is thus complete.
∎

3.2
An important approximation for
\psi(x)

A tricky application of the
Prime Number Theorem
(
2.5
) yields the following estimate,

\displaystyle\psi(x)=x+O\left(xe^{-c\sqrt{\log x}}\right)

(21)

or some constant
c>0
. However, it is indeed possible to obtain a simpler, and more effective bound for the
Chebyshev
\psi
-Function
.

Lemma 3.14
.

We have,

\displaystyle\psi(x)=x+O\left(x\log^{2}x\right)

(22)

Proof.

This proof thouroughly utilizes results from Analytic Number Theory, specifically the properties of the Chebyshev function
\psi(x)
and the distribution of primes. We shall also leverage results from the analytic properties of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
.

Important to note that, the proof relies on properties of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
and its non-trivial zeros. However, we
do not
assume the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) here explicitly.

The explicit formula for
\psi(x)
involves the zeros of
\zeta(s)
:

\psi(x)=x-\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}-\log(2\pi),

where the sum is over the non-trivial zeros
\rho
of
\zeta(s)
, and the term
\log(2\pi)
arises due to the existence of pole at
s=1
.

Suppose,
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
be a non-trivial zero of
\zeta(s)
. The zeros are symmetric about the real axis, so we consider only the upper half-plane. For each zero
\rho
of
\zeta(s)
, the term
\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}
contributes to
\psi(x)
.
To estimate the error term, consider the sum over the non-trivial zeros
\rho
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}

In addition to above, we use the fact that the non-trivial zeros
\rho
have,
\beta<1
. The contribution of each such
\rho
can be bounded by,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}\right|\leq x^{\beta}|\rho|^{-1}

Now, the number of zeros with
|\gamma|\leq T
is
O(T\log T)
. We choose
T=x
to cover the relevant range of zeros.

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|\leq x}|\rho|^{-1}=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq x}\left(\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\leq\sum_{|\gamma|\leq x}\left(\gamma^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\leq\sum_{|\gamma|\leq x}\frac{1}{|\gamma|}=O(\log x)

Combining these estimates, we obtain,

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}=O(x\log x)

(23)

Including the logarithmic term from the pole at
s=1
, the error term in
\psi(x)
becomes,

\displaystyle\psi(x)=x+O(x\log x)

Since we know that the error term actually involves
\log^{2}x
due to the density of the zeros of
\zeta(s)
and more refined approximations, thus, we can further improve our estimate to,

\displaystyle\psi(x)=x+O(x\log^{2}x)

as desired.
∎

As the title of this section suggests, we shall now proceed towards understanding how we can approximate
\pi(x)
using properties of
\psi(x)
.

Theorem 3.15
.

The following holds for the Prime counting function,
\pi(x)
:

\displaystyle\pi(x)=\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\right)

(24)

Proof.

Before we delve into the proof, notice that
\Lambda(n)
is zero except when
n
is a power of a prime, specifically
n=p^{k}
. We can hence rewrite
\psi(x)
as,

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\sum_{p\leq x}\log p\cdot\left(1+\left\lfloor\frac{\log x}{\log p}\right\rfloor\right)=\sum_{p\leq x}\log p+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{p\leq x^{1/k}}\log p

(25)

The error term in (
25
) comes from the higher powers of primes,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{p\leq x^{1/k}}\log p

Which is then dominated by the term corresponding to
k=2
. In other words,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{p\leq x^{1/k}}\log p=O(\sqrt{x}\log x).

Combining the main term and the error terms in (
25
),

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\sum_{p\leq x}\log p+O(\sqrt{x}\log x)

(26)

On the contrary, an application of the
Prime Number Theorem
allows us to have the following approximation,

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\log p=\pi(x)\log x+\epsilon(x)

(27)

Given our earlier discussion, the
error term
,
\epsilon(x)
can be bounded by,

\displaystyle\epsilon(x)=O(\sqrt{x}\log x).

To isolate
\pi(x)
from (
27
), we divide both sides by
\log x
,

\displaystyle\pi(x)=\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}-\frac{\epsilon(x)}{\log x}

Substituting the error term bound:

\displaystyle\pi(x)=\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{\sqrt{x}\log x}{\log x}\right)=\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}+O(\sqrt{x})

A priori using the fact that,
\sqrt{x}\ll\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}
asymptotically, and combining the error terms, and applying Theorem (
2.5
) again enables us to assert that,

\displaystyle\pi(x)=\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\right).

∎

3.3
An Improved Estimate for
\pi(x)

First, let us recall that, (
16
) in Theorem (
3.13
) gives us an order estimate for
\psi(x)
in terms of
M(x)
, whereas, we have derived a unique representation of
\pi(x)
applying properties of
\psi(x)
, and analytic properties of
\zeta(s)
, as mentioned in (
24
) in Theorem (
3.15
).

We substitute (
16
) into (
24
) to obtain,

\displaystyle\pi(x)=\frac{x}{\log x}-\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+\frac{O(\sqrt{x})}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\right)

Combining the error terms,

\displaystyle\pi(x)=\frac{x}{\log x}-\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\log x}\right)+O\left(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\right).

(28)

Note that,
\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\log x}\ll\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}
asymptotically. Thus, the dominant error term is
O\left(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\right)
. As a consequence, we have the following improved estimate for
\pi(x)
as follows.

Theorem 3.16
.

\displaystyle\pi(x)=\frac{x}{\log x}-\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\right)

(29)

4
Proving Ramanujan’s Inequality

In order to prove
Ramanujan’s Inequality
, our primary intention will be to investigate the sign of the function,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(x):=(\pi(x))^{2}-\frac{ex}{\log(x)}\pi\left(\frac{x}{e}\right)

(30)

for large values of
x
using the relationship between
\pi(x)
and the Mertens function
M(x)
. Given the complexity of the order relations an the extent of robust computations involving these functions as discussed in the previous section, we’ll have to analyze the expressions and error terms cautiously.

4.1
An order expression for
\mathcal{G}(x)

A priori from (
29
) of Theorem (
3.16
), we can deduce a simplified expression for
\pi(x/e)
as,

\displaystyle\pi(x/e)=\frac{x/e}{\log(x/e)}-\frac{1}{\log(x/e)}\sum_{n\leq x/e}M\left(\frac{x/e}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{x/e}{(\log(x/e))^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 50.0pt=\frac{x/e}{\log x-1}-\frac{1}{\log x-1}\sum_{n\leq x/e}M\left(\frac{x/e}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{x/e}{(\log x-1)^{2}}\right)

Subsequently,

\displaystyle\frac{ex}{\log x}\pi(x/e)=\frac{x^{2}}{\log x(\log x-1)}-\frac{ex}{\log x(\log x-1)}\sum_{n\leq x/e}M\left(\frac{x/e}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 250.0pt+O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log x(\log x-1)^{2}}\right)

(31)

Furthermore, squaring
\pi(x)
yields,

\displaystyle(\pi(x))^{2}=\left(\frac{x}{\log x}-\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{2}}{\log^{2}x}-\frac{2x}{(\log x)^{2}}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+\frac{1}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)^{2}+O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log^{3}x}\right)

(32)

Therefore, using (
31
) and (
32
), we deduce that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(x)=\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log^{2}x}-\frac{2x}{(\log x)^{2}}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+\frac{1}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)^{2}+O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log^{3}x}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 25.0pt-\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log x(\log x-1)}-\frac{ex}{\log x(\log x-1)}\sum_{n\leq x/e}M\left(\frac{x/e}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log x(\log x-1)^{2}}\right)\right).

Simplifying each term:

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}(\log x-1)}-\frac{2x}{(\log x)^{2}}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+\frac{1}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 180.0pt+\frac{ex}{\log x(\log x-1)}\sum_{n\leq x/e}M\left(\frac{x/e}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log^{3}x}\right)

(33)

4.2
Asymptotic Behavior of indivudual Terms

A priori using
(iii)
(cf. Prop. (
2.12
)) involving the Mertens function
M(x)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)=O\left(\sqrt{x}\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)=O(x)

(34)

So, we can further approximate each term of (
33
) as follows,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)^{2}=O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log^{2}x}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{ex}{\log x(\log x-1)}\sum_{n\leq x/e}M\left(\frac{x/e}{n}\right)=O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log x(\log x-1)}\right).

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(x)=-\frac{2x}{(\log x)^{2}}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log^{2}x}\right)+O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log x(\log x-1)}\right)+O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log^{3}x}\right).

\displaystyle\hskip 100.0pt=-\frac{2x}{(\log x)^{2}}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\log^{2}x}\right)

(35)

For sufficiently large values of
x
. Therefore, we conclude that,
\mathcal{G}(x)<0
, for large values of
x
, and this concludes our proof of the inequality.

Later, we shall try to establish a better range of the values of
x
for which
Ramanujan’s Inequality
does hold true.

5
A modified Bound for
\pi(x)

The derivations which we’ve made in the previous sections yielded several order estimates involving
M(x)
,
\psi(x)
and especially
\pi(x)
. In this section, we shall discuss how this method enables us to find more optimal bounds for
\pi(x)
.

5.1
Upper Bound for
\pi(x)

A priori from Lemma (
3.14
), we have that for some positive constant
\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle\psi(x)\leq x+\alpha x(\log x)^{2}

(36)

Substituting this into the the estimate (
24
) of Theorem (
3.15
) involving
\pi(x)
,

\displaystyle\pi(x)\leq\frac{x+\alpha x(\log x)^{2}}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)=\frac{x}{\log x}+\alpha x\log x+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right).

(37)

5.2
Lower Bound for
\pi(x)

A priori using (
34
) we can derive,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)=O\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right).

Therefore, applying the above derivation to (
29
) in Theorem (
3.16
), we assert that,

\displaystyle\pi(x)\geq\frac{x}{\log x}-\frac{\beta x}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)=\left(1-\beta\right)\frac{x}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right).

(38)

for some positive constant
\beta>0
.

In summary, (
37
) and (
38
) allows us to state the following.

Theorem 5.17
.

The following bounds on
\pi(x)
is valid for sufficiently large values of
x
:

\displaystyle\left(1-\beta\right)\frac{x}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)\leq\pi(x)\leq\frac{x}{\log x}+\alpha x\log x+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)

(39)

for positive constants
\alpha,\beta>0
.

Remark 5.18
.

The result (
39
) in Theorem (
5.17
) provides an alternative justification to the validity of the famous
Prime Number Theorem
. This can be observed from the fact that,
\pi(x)
is bounded on both sides by a constant multiple of
\frac{x}{\log x}
plus an error term, which can be minimized for large values of
x
.

Adhering to
Sterneck
’s deduction
[

]
, as mentioned in section

\mathcal{G}
for its sign for large values of

6.1
Monotonicity of the function
\mathcal{G}

Our aim in this section is to establish the following claim.

Proposition 6.19
.

The function
\mathcal{G}(x)
as defined in (
30
) is monotone decreasing for
x\geq 201
.

Proof.

We intend on verifying that,
\mathcal{G}(x+\epsilon)-\mathcal{G}(x)<0
for every large
x
and for every
\epsilon>0
arbitraily chosen.

Observe from definition that the difference,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(x+\epsilon)-\mathcal{G}(x)=\left[(\pi(x+\epsilon))^{2}-(\pi(x))^{2}\right]-\left[\frac{e(x+\epsilon)}{\log(x+\epsilon)}\pi\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{e}\right)-\frac{ex}{\log x}\pi(x/e)\right]

(40)

Now we evaluate,

\displaystyle(\pi(x+\epsilon))^{2}-(\pi(x))^{2}=\left(\pi(x+\epsilon)-\pi(x)\right)\left(\pi(x+\epsilon)+\pi(x)\right)

(41)

Using the explicit forms (
29
):

\displaystyle\pi(x+\epsilon)=\frac{x}{\log x}+\frac{\epsilon}{\log x}-\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x+\epsilon}M\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{(\log(x+\epsilon))^{2}}\right)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\pi(x+\epsilon)-\pi(x)=\frac{\epsilon}{\log x}-\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x+\epsilon}M\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{n}\right)+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 260.0pt+O\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{(\log(x+\epsilon))^{2}}-\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)

(42)

For small
\epsilon
, we use the linear approximation:

\displaystyle\frac{x+\epsilon}{\log(x+\epsilon)}\approx\frac{x}{\log x}+\frac{\epsilon}{\log x}-\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}

And,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log(x+\epsilon)}\approx\frac{1}{\log x}-\frac{\epsilon}{x(\log x)^{2}}

Thus, we can compute further in (
42
) as follows,

\displaystyle\pi(x+\epsilon)-\pi(x)=\frac{\epsilon}{\log x}-\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x+\epsilon}M\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 180.0pt+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)

(43)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\pi(x+\epsilon)+\pi(x)=2\frac{x}{\log x}+\frac{\epsilon}{\log x}-\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x+\epsilon}M\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 200.0pt+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)

(44)

Combining (
43
) and (
44
) yields,

\displaystyle(\pi(x+\epsilon))^{2}-(\pi(x))^{2}=\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\log x}-\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x+\epsilon}M\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{n}\right)+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 200.0pt\left(2\frac{x}{\log x}+\frac{\epsilon}{\log x}-\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)+O\left(\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}-2\frac{x^{2}\epsilon}{(\log x)^{3}}-2\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\sum_{n\leq x+\epsilon}M\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{n}\right)+2\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 280.0pt+O\left(\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)

(45)

As for the second term in (
40
),

\displaystyle\frac{e(x+\epsilon)}{\log(x+\epsilon)}\pi\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{e}\right)-\frac{ex}{\log x}\pi(x/e)\approx\frac{e(x+\epsilon)}{\log(x+\epsilon)}\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{e\log(x+\epsilon/e)}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{ex}{\log x}\left(\frac{x}{e\log(x/e)}\right)

(Using the earlier approximations for
\pi\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{e}\right)
and
\pi\left(\frac{x}{e}\right)
)

\displaystyle=\frac{(x+\epsilon)^{2}}{(\log(x+\epsilon))^{2}}-\frac{x^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{3}}\right)=\frac{(x+\epsilon)^{2}-x^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 200.0pt=\frac{2x\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{3}}\right)

(46)

Hence, substituting (
45
) and (
46
) in (
40
) and simplifying,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(x+\epsilon)-\mathcal{G}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(2\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{2}}-2\frac{x^{2}\epsilon}{(\log x)^{3}}-2\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\sum_{n\leq x+\epsilon}M\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{n}\right)+2\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 250.0pt-\left(\frac{2x\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)+O\left(\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\sum_{n\leq x+\epsilon}M\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{n}\right)-\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{3}}\right)

(47)

We utilize
Sterneck
’s conjecture,

\displaystyle|M(x)|<\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{x},\hskip 10.0pt\mbox{ for }x\geq 201.

to bound the difference as follows,

\displaystyle-2\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\sum_{n\leq x+\epsilon}M\left(\frac{x+\epsilon}{n}\right)-\sum_{n\leq x}M\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)\leq-2\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{x+\epsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{x}\right)

(48)

Subsequently,
\epsilon>0
enables us to conclude from (
47
) and (
48
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(x+\epsilon)-\mathcal{G}(x)\leq-2\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{x+\epsilon}-\sqrt{x})\right)-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 50.0pt=-\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}(\sqrt{x+\epsilon}-\sqrt{x})-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x\epsilon}{(\log x)^{3}}\right)

(49)

Since
\sqrt{x+\epsilon}-\sqrt{x}>0
for any
\epsilon>0
and the error term

is smaller, it implies,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(x+\epsilon)-\mathcal{G}(x)<0\text{ for every }x+\epsilon>x,\epsilon>0

Thus,
\mathcal{G}(x)
is monotone decreasing for large
x
such that,
x\geq 201
.

∎

6.2
A better range for the values of

Just to recall, Proposition (

) comments on the monotonicity of

for

1.
Introduction

The investigations on shifted primes with large prime factors drew attention to people in a brilliant article of Goldfeld
[
12
]
. Historically, this topic had aroused great concern among the community due to its unexpected connection with the first case of Fermat’s last theorem, thanks to the theorems of Fouvry
[
10
]
, Adleman and Heath-Brown
[
1
]
.

For any positive integer
n
, let
P^{+}(n)
be the largest prime factor of
n
with convention
P^{+}(1)=1
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of primes and
\pi(x)
the number of primes not exceeding
x
. For
0<c<1
let

T_{c}(x)=\#\left\{p\leq x:p\in\mathcal{P},P^{+}(p-1)\geq p^{c}\right\}.

As early as 1969, Goldfeld
[
12
]
proved

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{\frac{1}{2}}(x)/\pi(x)\geq 1/2.

Goldfeld further remarked that his argument also leads to another fact

\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)>0,

(1)

provided that
c<\frac{7}{12}
. It turns out that exploring large
c
to satisfy Eq. (
1
) is rather difficult and important. For improvements on the values of
c
, see
Motohashi
[
21
]
, Hooley
[
15
,
16
]
, Deshouillers–Iwaniec
[
7
]
and Fouvry
[
10
]
.
Up to now, the best numerical value of
c
satisfying Eq. (
1
), with a payoff by replacing
\pi(x)
with
\pi(x)/\log x
, is 0.677, obtained by Baker and Harman
[
2
]
.

In a former note
[
8
]
of this sequel, I showed that the following inequality

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)<1/2

(2)

holds for some absolute constant
c<1
.
As a corollary, I disproved a 2017 conjecture of Chen and Chen
[
6
]
who conjectured that

\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\geq 1/2

for any
1/2\leq c<1
.
The proof in my former note is based on the following deep result which is a corollary of the elaborate Brun–Titchmarsh inequality.

Proposition 1
.

[
24
, Lemma 2.2]

There exist two functions
K_{2}(\theta)>K_{1}(\theta)>0
, defined on the interval
(0,17/32)
such that for each fixed real
A>0
, and all sufficiently large
Q=x^{\theta}
, the inequalities

K_{1}(\theta)\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(m)}\leq\pi(x;m,1)\leq K_{2}(\theta)\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(m)}

hold for all integers
m\in(Q,2Q]
with at most
O\left(Q(\log Q)^{-A}\right)
exceptions, where the implied constant depends only on
A
and
\theta
. Moreover, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, these functions can be chosen to satisfy the following properties:

\bullet

K_{1}(\theta)
is monotonic decreasing, and
K_{2}(\theta)
is monotonic increasing.

\bullet

K_{1}(1/2)=1-\varepsilon
and
K_{2}(1/2)=1+\varepsilon
.

The constant
c
in Eq. (
2
) is not specified therein due to the indeterminate amounts
K_{1}(\theta)
in Proposition
1
. In fact,
K_{1}(\theta)
(and hence
c
) can be explicitly given if one checks carefully the articles of Baker–Harman
[
3
]
for
1/2\leq\theta\leq 13/25
and Mikawa
[
19
]
for
13/25\leq\theta\leq 17/32
. Actually,
K_{1}(\theta)\geq 0.16
for
1/2\leq\theta\leq 13/25

[
3
, Theorem 1]
and
K_{1}(\theta)\geq 1/100
for Mikawa’s range
[
19
, Eq. (4)]
. However, it seems that the constant
c
in Eq. (
2
) obtained upon this way will be very close to
1
(see the proofs in
[
8
]
).

In
[
8
]
, I also pointed out that Chen and Chen’s conjecture is already in contradiction with the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture according to the works of Pomerance
[
22
]
, Granville
[
13
]
, Wang
[
23
]
and Wu
[
24
]
. In fact, one has

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)=\left(1-\rho\left(\frac{1}{c}\right)\right)\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}~c\rightarrow 1

(3)

under the assumption of the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture, where
\rho(u)
is the Dickman function, defined as the unique continuous solution of the equation
differential–difference

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho(u)=1,&0\leq u\leq 1,\\
u\rho^{\prime}(u)=-\rho(u-1),&u>1.\end{cases}

However, things involving with the study on Chen–Chen’s conjecture as well as my prior result (i.e., Eq. (
2
)) got somewhat misleading to some extent. In fact, indicated by a former result of Erdős
[
9
, Lemma 4]
, as early as 1935, people could already conclude from his proof, by combining with Lemma
2
of Wu (see below), that Eq. (
3
) is true in part. That is,

Theorem 1

(Erdős)
.
1
1

1

Actually, the original result of Erdős states that

\#\left\{p\leq(\log x)^{1+\rho}:P^{+}(p-1)\leq\log x\right\}>c_{\rho}\frac{(\log x)^{1+\rho}}{\log\log x}

for some given constants
\rho
and
c_{\rho}
. Essentially, Theorem
2
can be deduced from his proof of this result by adding Wu’s lemma.
2
2

2

Shortly after the manuscript of this note was put on the arXiv, Brad Rodgers informed the author that, joint with Bharadwaj, they independently obtained the same result with a general form in the probabilistic language. Their article was now also on the arXiv, see
[
5
, Theorem 8 and Corollary 9]
.

Unconditionally, we have

\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}~c\rightarrow 1.

Since Erdős’ conclusion, by his statements therein, is not very clear in the formulations as its appearance in Theorem
2
, I feel that it is meaningful and necessary to restate them again to form the explicit theorem above due to the recent publication history mentioned here. It would therefore, as I believe, be of interest to push the Erdős theorem a little further to the following quantitative form as explicit as possible.

Theorem 2
.

For
8/9\leq c<1
, we have

\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\leq 8\left(1/c-1\right).

We note that the restriction on
c\geq 8/9
in our theorem is natural since otherwise the upper bound would exceed
1
which is certainly meaningless.
Theorem
2
can also be compared with the prior results of Goldfeld
[
12
]
, Luca
et al.

[
18
]
and Chen–Chen
[
6
]
which state that

\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\geq 1-c

for any
0<c\leq 1/2
. Their bounds were recently improved in part by Feng–Wu
[
11
]
and Liu–Wu–Xi
[
17
]
.
From Theorem
2
, we clearly have two corollaries, one of which is the Erdős’ theorem (i.e., Theorem
2
). The other one revisits the main result Eq. (
2
) of my former note in a quantitative form.

Corollary 1
.

For
c>16/17
, we have

\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)<1/2.

2.
Proofs

From now on,
p
will always be a prime. The proof of Theorem
2
is based on the following lemma deduced from the sieve method (see e.g.
[
14
, page 172, Theorem 5.7]
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
g
be a natural number, and let
a_{i},b_{i}~(i=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,g)
be integers satisfying

E:=\prod_{i=1}^{g}a_{i}\prod_{1\leq r<s\leq g}(a_{r}b_{s}-a_{s}b_{r})\neq 0.

Let
\rho(p)
denote the number of solutions of

\prod_{i=1}^{g}(a_{i}n+b_{i})\equiv 0\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p),

and suppose that

\rho(p)<p\quad\text{for~all~}p.

Let
y
and
z
be real numbers satisfying

1<y\leq z.

Then

\left|\{n:z-y<n\leq z,a_{i}n+b_{i}~\text{prime~for~}i=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,g\}\right|\\
\leq 2^{g}g!\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\rho(p)-1}{p-1}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-g+1}\!\!\!\frac{y}{\log^{g}y}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log\log 3y+\log\log 3|E|}{\log y}\right)\right),

where the constant implied by the
O
-symbol depends at most on
g
.

We also need the following important relation established by Wu
[
24
, Theorem 2]
.

Lemma 2
.

For
0<c<1
, let

T^{\prime}_{c}(x)=\#\{p\leq x:p\in\mathcal{P},P^{+}(p-1)\geq x^{c}\}.

Then for sufficiently large
x
we have

T_{c}(x)=T^{\prime}_{c}(x)+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right).

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
x
be a sufficiently large number throughout our proof. Instead of investigating
T_{c}(x)
, we deal with
T^{\prime}_{c}(x)
firstly.
For
1/2\leq c<1
it is easy to see

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{c}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{c}\leq q<x\\
q\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
q|p-1\end{subarray}}1.

(1)

On putting
p-1=qh
in the sum of Eq. (
1
) and then exchanging the order of the sums, we get

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{c}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{c}\leq q<x\\
q\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h<x/q\\
qh+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h<x^{1-c}\\
2|h\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2<q<x/h\\
q,qh+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}1.

(2)

For any
h
with
2|h
and
h<x^{1-c}
, let
\rho(p)
denote the number of solutions of

\displaystyle n(hn+1)\equiv 0\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p).

Then

\displaystyle\rho(p)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if~}p|h,\\
2,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Now, by Lemma
1
with
g=2,a_{1}=1,b_{1}=0,a_{2}=h,b_{2}=1
and
z=y=x/h
we have

3|E|=3h\ll x,\quad 3y=3x/h\ll x\quad\text{and}\quad y=x/h\geq\sqrt{x},

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2<q<x/h\\
q,qh+1\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}1\leq 16\mathfrak{S}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|h\\
p>2\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p-2}\right)\frac{x/h}{\log^{2}(x/h)}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}\right)\right),

(3)

where the empty product for
\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|h\\
p>2\end{subarray}}
above denotes
1
as usual and

\mathfrak{S}=\prod_{p>2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p-1}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}=\prod_{p>2}\left(1-\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}\right).

Inserting Eq. (
3
) into Eq. (
2
) we obtain

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{c}(x)

\displaystyle\leq(1+o(1))16\mathfrak{S}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h<x^{1-c}\\
2|h\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|h\\
p>2\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p-2}\right)\frac{x/h}{\log^{2}(x/h)}.

(4)

It can be noted that

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|h\\
p>2\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p-2}\right)\leq 2\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|h\\
p>2\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)

(5)

since the gaps between odd primes are at least
2
, from which we can already give a nontrivial upper bound of
T^{\prime}_{c}(x)
via partial summations. To make our bound more explicit than Eq. (
5
), we employ a nice result of Banks and Shparlinski
[
4
, Lemma 2.3]
(on taking
a=1
therein) which states that for
2\leq z<x^{1-c}
,

\displaystyle S(z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h<z\\
2|h\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{h}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|h\\
p>2\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p-2}\right)=\frac{1+o(1)}{2\mathfrak{S}}\log z.

(6)

And for
1\leq z<2
, we appoint
S(z)=0
.
We get from partial summations that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h<x^{1-c}\\
2|h\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|h\\
p>2\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)\!\frac{1/h}{\log^{2}(x/h)}=\frac{S\left(x^{1-c}\right)}{\left(\log x^{c}\right)^{2}}-\int_{1}^{x^{1-c}}S(z)d\left(\log\frac{x}{z}\right)^{-2}.

(7)

Now, routine computations provide us

\frac{S\left(x^{1-c}\right)}{\left(\log x^{c}\right)^{2}}=\frac{1+o(1)}{\mathfrak{S}}\frac{(1-c)}{2c^{2}}(\log x)^{-1}

and

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{x^{1-c}}S(z)d\left(\log\frac{x}{z}\right)^{-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+o(1)}{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{1}^{x^{1-c}}\frac{\log z}{z}\left(\log\frac{x}{z}\right)^{-3}dz

\displaystyle=\frac{1+o(1)}{\mathfrak{S}}\int_{x^{c}}^{x}\frac{\log x-\log u}{u}(\log u)^{-3}du\quad\quad(u=x/z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+o(1)}{\mathfrak{S}}\left(\frac{1-c}{2c^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2c}\right)(\log x)^{-1},

thanks to the estimate Eq. (
6
). Putting together the formulas above, one see that the right–hand side of Eq. (
7
) equals

\displaystyle\frac{1+o(1)}{\mathfrak{S}}\left(\frac{1}{2c}-\frac{1}{2}\right)(\log x)^{-1}.

(8)

Taking Eq. (
8
) into Eq. (
4
) we immediately obtain that

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{c}(x)

\displaystyle\leq(1+o(1))8\left(\frac{1}{c}-1\right)\frac{x}{\log x}.

Therefore, by Lemma
2
we have

T_{c}(x)=T^{\prime}_{c}(x)+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)\leq(1+o(1))8\left(\frac{1}{c}-1\right)\frac{x}{\log x}.

Our theorem now follows from the prime number theorem.
∎

3.
Remarks

Under the assumption of the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture, it is reasonable to predict that the exact value of
c
in Corollary
1
should be
e^{-1/2}=0.60653\cdots
from Eq. (
3
) and the following recursion formula (see e.g.
[
20
, Eq. (7.6)]
) on Dickman’s function

\rho(v)=u-\int_{u}^{v}\frac{\rho(t-1)}{t}dt\quad(1\leq u\leq v).

It therefore seems to be of independent interest to improve, as far as possible, the numerical value of
c
in Corollary
1
. We leave this as a challenge to dear readers who are interested in this.

Though we provided nontrivial upper bounds on
T_{c}(x)
for
8/9\leq c<1
in Theorem
2
for some absolute constant
c_{0}
, the expansion of these bounds to
1/2\leq c<1
is, however, an unsolved problem.

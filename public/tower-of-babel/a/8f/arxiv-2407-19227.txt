Non-Homogeneous Generalized Fractional Skellam Process
Kartik Tathe, Sayan Ghosh

Abstract
. This paper introduces the Non-homogeneous Generalized Skellam process (NGSP) and its fractional version NGFSP by time changing it with an independent inverse stable subordinator. We study distributional properties for NGSP and NGFSP including probability generating function, probability mass function (p.m.f.), factorial moments, mean, variance, covariance and correlation structure. Then we investigate the long and short range dependence structures for NGSP and NGFSP, and obtain the governing state differential equations of these processes along with their increment processes. We obtain recurrence relations satisfied by the state probabilities of Non-homogeneous Generalized Counting process (NGCP), NGSP and NGFSP. The weighted sum representations for these processes are provided. We further obtain martingale characterization and arrival time distribution for NGSP and NGFSP. An alternative version of NGFSP with a closed-form p.m.f. is introduced along with a discussion of its distributional and asymptotic properties. In addition, we study the running average processes of GCP and GSP which are of independent interest. The p.m.f. of NGSP and the simulated sample paths of GFSP, NGFSP and related processes are plotted. Finally, we discuss an application to a high-frequency financial data set pointing out the advantages of our model compared to existing ones.

1.
Introduction:

A non-negative integer-valued stochastic process
\{N(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfying
N(t_{1})\leq N(t_{2})
for
t_{1}<t_{2}
is called a counting process. A counting process
\{N(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
with independent and stationary increments is called a Poisson process with rate
\lambda>0
if
N(t)
has Poisson distribution with parameter
\lambda t
. Various generalizations of the Poisson process have been studied in the literature as discussed below.

Laskin 2003
introduced the time fractional Poisson process using a special form of the fractional Kolmogorov–Feller equation which uses R-L fractional derivative instead of ordinary derivative in the governing state differential equation.
Meerschaert et al. 2011
obtained the same process by time changing (replacing the time variable) the Poisson process with an independent inverse stable subordinator. Time fractional generalization provides the flexibility of non-stationary and dependent increments.
Leonenko et al. 2017
introduced a non-homogeneous generalization of the time fractional Poisson process by replacing the time variable with an appropriate function of time (rate function).

For insurance modeling,
Kostadinova and Minkova 2013
introduced another generalization of Poisson process, namely the Poisson process of order
k
(PPoK) to study claim arrivals in groups of size
k
where the number of arrivals in a group is uniformly distributed over
k
points. Expanding further,
Sengar et al. 2019
proposed a generalization of the above process to deal with dependent inter-arrival times.

Allowing various jump sizes with different intensities,
Di Crescenzo et al. 2016
introduced the Generalized fractional Counting process (GFCP) and studied its connections with the Poisson process. They showed that it is equal in distribution to a compound Poisson process. The non-homogeneous version of Generalized Counting process (NGCP) was considered by
Kataria et al. 2022
.

The Skellam process is an integer valued Lévy process obtained by taking the difference of two independent Poisson processes. It was first studied by
Irwin 2018
for equal means and
Skellam 1946
for unequal means.
Kerss et al. 2014
studied the time-fractional version of Skellam process by time-changing the Skellam process with an independent inverse stable subordinator.
Kataria and Khandakar 2022b
introduced and studied the Generalized Fractional Skellam process (GFSP) by considering the difference of two independent GFCPs. Recently,
Gupta et al. 2020
studied another generalization of Skellam process, namely the Skellam process of order
k
by taking the difference of two independent PPoKs along with its time-changed version. They also discussed the space-fractional and tempered space-fractional Skellam processes by time-changing the Skellam process with independent stable subordinator and tempered stable subordinator, respectively.

The applications of Skellam process cover various areas, such as modeling of intensity difference of pixels in cameras (see
Hwang et al. 2007
) and modeling the difference in number of goals of two opponent teams in a football game (see
Karlis and Ntzoufras 2008
).
Kerss et al. 2014
studied the use of fractional Skellam process in high frequency financial data analysis for modeling up and down jump processes related to tick-by-tick stock price data. Also they fitted the inter arrival time data using both exponential and Mittag-Leffer distributions and showed that the Mittag-Leffler distribution provides a better fit than the exponential distribution. However, it can be observed from figure (3) of
Kerss et al. 2014
that their fractional Skellam process is not always suitable for modelling the arrivals in the up and down jump processes. In practice, the arrivals may not consistently occur at constant rates and also the magnitude of jumps (price changes) may fluctuate depending on various factors. Therefore further refinement is required to accommodate higher-order jumps and varied patterns in arrivals. Such type of data may also arise in other situations like queuing systems and traffic flow. These issues motivate us to propose a more flexible model than existing ones and investigate its characteristics for better insight.

In this article, we introduce the Non-homogeneous Generalized Skellam process (NGSP) and its fractional version (NGFSP). The remaining paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we discuss some preliminaries including definitions and results. In section
3
, we introduce the NGSP and study its distributional properties. In section
4
, the fractional version of NGSP namely, the NGFSP is introduced and its characteristics are studied. Section
5
deals with the increment processes of NGSP and NGFSP. In Section
6
, we introduce an alternative version of NGFSP and study its distributional and dependence properties along with some limiting results. Section
7
discusses the running average processes for GCP and GSP along with their important characteristics. In section
8
, we plot the pmf of NGSP and the simulated sample paths of NGFSP and related processes.
Finally, in section
9
, we provide an application of our model to high frequency financial data.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present some preliminary results and definitions which will be used later in the paper.

2.1.
Multinomial Theorem

For
x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(x_{1}+x_{2}+...+x_{m})^{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+...+k_{m}=n\\
k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{n}{k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{m}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}^{k_{j}}

where

\binom{n}{k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{m}}=\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!...k_{m}!}\,\,.

2.2.
Modified Bessel’s function of first kind

The modified Bessel’s function of first kind is given as (see
Abramowitz and Stegun 1972
)

\displaystyle I_{n}(z)=\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)^{k}}{k!\Gamma(n+k+1)}=\frac{1}{2^{n+2k}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n+2k}}{k!(n+k)!}

and satisfies the following properties (see
Sneddon 1966
, p. 128)

\displaystyle I_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=I_{-n}(z),\quad\frac{d}{dz}I_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left(I_{n-1}(z)+I_{n+1}(z)\right).

2.3.
R-L fractional derivative

For
\gamma>0
, the R-L fractional derivative is defined as (see
Kilbas et al. 2006
)

D_{t}^{\gamma}f(t):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\Gamma(m-\gamma)}\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f(s)}{(t-s)^{\gamma+1-m}}ds,&\quad m-1<\gamma<m,\\
\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}f(t),&\quad\gamma=m,\end{cases}

where
m
is a positive integer.

2.4.
Caputo–Djrbashian fractional derivative

The Caputo–Djrbashian fractional derivative is defined as (see
Leonenko et al. 2017
)

\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{df(\tau)}{d\tau}\frac{d\tau}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha}},\quad 0<\alpha<1.

Its Laplace transform is

\mathcal{L}\left\{\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}f\right\}(s)=s^{\alpha}\mathcal{L}\{f\}(s)-s^{\alpha-1}f(0^{+}).

The following relationship holds for the R-L fractional derivative and the Caputo–Djrbashian fractional derivative (see
Meerschaert and Straka 2013
):

\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}f(t)=D_{t}^{\alpha}f(t)-f(0+)\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}.

2.5.
Stable subordinator and its inverse

A stable subordinator
\{D_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
,
0<\alpha<1
, is a non-decreasing Lévy process. Its Laplace transform is given by
E(e^{-sD_{\alpha}(t)})=e^{-ts^{\alpha}}
,
s>0
and the associated Bernstein function is
f(s)=s^{\alpha}
. Its first passage time
\{Y_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is called the inverse stable subordinator and defined as

Y_{\alpha}(t):=\inf\{x\geq 0:D_{\alpha}(t)>t\}.

The density of
Y_{\alpha}(t)
denoted by
h_{\alpha}(x,t)
(see
Meerschaert et al. 2011
) has the Laplace transform (see
Meerschaert and Straka 2013
)
\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(x,t)=s^{\alpha-1}e^{-xs^{\alpha}}.

The mean, variance and covariance (see
Leonenko et al. 2014
) of
Y_{\alpha}(t)
are

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y_{\alpha}(t))=\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)},\quad\mathbb{V}(Y_{\alpha}(t))=\left(\frac{2}{\Gamma(2\alpha+1)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma^{2}(\alpha+1)}\right)t^{2\alpha},

\displaystyle\text{Cov}\left(Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma^{2}(\alpha+1)}\left(\alpha s^{2\alpha}B(\alpha,\alpha+1)+F(\alpha:s,t)\right),0<s<t,

where
F(\alpha:s,t)=\alpha t^{2\alpha}B(\alpha,\alpha+1:s/t)-(ts)^{\alpha}
. Here
B(\alpha,\alpha+1)
and
B(\alpha,\alpha+1:s/t)
denote the beta function and the incomplete beta function, respectively. From
Vellaisamy and Maheshwari 2018
), we have

F(\alpha:s,t)\sim\frac{-\alpha^{2}}{(\alpha+1)}\frac{s^{\alpha+1}}{t^{1-\alpha}}\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty.

Thus we obtain for fixed
s
and large
t

\text{Cov}\left(Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma^{2}(\alpha+1)}\left(\alpha s^{2\alpha}B(\alpha,\alpha+1)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(\alpha+1)}\frac{s^{\alpha+1}}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right),\quad 0<s<t.

2.6.
LRD and SRD properties

For fixed
s>0
, suppose the correlation function of a stochastic process
\{Y(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
has the following asymptotic behavior (see
D’Ovidio and Nane 2014
or
Vellaisamy and Maheshwari 2018
):

\text{Corr}\left(Y(s),Y(t)\right)\sim c(s)t^{-\theta}\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty\quad\text{for some}\quad c(s)>0.

Then
\{Y(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
has long range dependence (LRD) property if
\theta\in(0,1)
and short range dependence (SRD) property if
\theta\in(1,2)
.

2.7.
Lemma

(
Xia 2018
) If
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a Lévy process and its Riemann integral is defined by
Y(t)=\int_{0}^{t}X(s)ds,

then the characteristic function of
Y(t)
satisfies

\phi_{Y(t)}(u)=\mathbb{E}[e^{iuY(t)}]=e^{t\left(\int_{0}^{1}\log\phi_{X(1)}(tuz)dz\right)},\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.

2.8.
Non-homogeneous Generalized Counting process (NGCP)

A special case of the GFCP corresponding to
\alpha=1
is called the Generalized Counting process (GCP). A non-homogeneous version of the GCP namely NGCP
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
was considered by
Kataria et al. 2022
with deterministic and time-dependent intensity functions
\lambda_{j}(t):[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty),1\leq j\leq k
and cumulative rate functions
\Lambda_{j}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{j}(u).du\,\,
, such that
\mathcal{M}(0)=0
. The mean, variance, and covariance of NGCP are given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}(t)\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t),\quad\mathbb{V}\left(\mathcal{M}(t)\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\Lambda_{j}(t),\quad\text{and}\quad\text{Cov}\left(\mathcal{M}(s),\mathcal{M}(t)\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\Lambda_{j}(s\wedge t).

3.
Non-Homogeneous generalized Skellam Process

In this section, we introduce the Non-homogeneous Generalized Skellam Process and study its various characteristics.

Definition 3.1
.

The Non-Homogeneous Generalized Skellam Process (NGSP) is defined as

\mathcalboondox{S}(t):=\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)

where
\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
are two independent NGCPs with intensity parameters

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(t):[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma_{j}(t):[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty),\quad j=1,2,...,k

respectively such that
\mathcal{M}_{1}(0)=0
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}(0)=0
.

The cumulative rate functions of
\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
are

\displaystyle\Lambda_{j}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{j}(u)du<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad T_{j}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{j}(u)du<\infty

respectively. If
0\leq s<t
, then

\displaystyle\Lambda_{j}(s,t)=\int_{s}^{t}\lambda_{j}(u)du=\Lambda_{j}(t)-\Lambda_{j}(s)\quad\text{and}\quad T_{j}(s,t)=\int_{s}^{t}\gamma_{j}(u)du=T_{j}(t)-T_{j}(s).

Remark 3.1
.

Note that for
\lambda_{j}(t)=\lambda_{j}
and
\gamma_{j}(t)=\gamma_{j}
, NGSP reduces to the Generalized Skellam Process (see
Kataria and Khandakar 2022b
). Moreover if
\lambda_{j}(t)=\lambda
and
\gamma_{j}(t)=\gamma
, then NGSP reduces to the Skellam Process of order
k
(see
Gupta et al. 2020
) and to the usual Skellam process for
k=1
.

3.1.
Probability Generating Function of NGSP

The probability generating function (pgf) for NGSP can be obtained using the pgf of NGCP (see
Kataria et al. 2022
) as follows:

(3.1)

\displaystyle G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)(u^{j}-1)\right)\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)(u^{-j}-1)\right)=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)(u^{j}-1)+T_{j}(t)(u^{-j}-1)\right)\right).

It satisfies the following differential equation:

\frac{d}{dt}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)=G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\left(\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{j}(t)\right)(u^{j}-1)+\left(\frac{d}{dt}T_{j}(t)\right)(u^{-j}-1)\right]\right\}.

3.2.
Mean, Variance and Covariance

Using the mean, variance and covariance of NGCP (see section
2.8
), we can obtain the corresponding expressions for NGSP as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\mathcalboondox{S}(t))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)-T_{j}(t)\right),\quad\mathbb{V}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(t)\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)+T_{j}(t)\right)\,\,,

\displaystyle\text{Cov}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(s),\mathcalboondox{S}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Cov}(\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(s),\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(t))=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\Lambda_{j}(s\wedge t)+T_{j}(s\wedge t)\right).

Also note that
\mathbb{V}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(t)\right)-\mathbb{E}(\mathcalboondox{S}(t))=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)(j^{2}-j)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)(j^{2}+j)>0,

which implies the NGSP exhibits over-dispersion.

Remark 3.2
.

The
k
th raw moment of NGSP is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\mathcalboondox{S}^{k}(t))

\displaystyle=\left(u\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\right)^{k}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\big|_{u=1}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j!\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)-T_{j}(t)\right),\quad k\geq 1.

3.3.
Correlation structure of NGSP

For fixed
s<t
, the correlation function of NGSP is

\displaystyle\text{Corr}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(s),\mathcalboondox{S}(t)\right)=\frac{\text{Cov}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(s),\mathcalboondox{S}(t)\right)}{\sqrt{\mathbb{V}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(s)\right)}\sqrt{\mathbb{V}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(t)\right)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\Lambda_{j}(s)+T_{j}(s)\right)}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\Lambda_{j}(s)+T_{j}(s)\right)}\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\Lambda_{j}(s)+T_{j}(s)\right)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\Lambda_{j}(s)+T_{j}(s)\right)}}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)+T_{j}(t)\right)}}\,\,.

Assume both NGCPs have Weibull rate functions given by

\Lambda_{j}(t)=\left(\frac{t}{b_{1j}}\right)^{c_{1j}},\,\,T_{j}(t)=\left(\frac{t}{b_{2j}}\right)^{c_{2j}};\quad b_{1j},b_{2j}>0,~c_{1j},c_{2j}\geq 0\,\,

and
c_{1j}\geq c_{2j}
without loss of generality. Then the correlation function reduces to

\displaystyle\text{Corr}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(s),\mathcalboondox{S}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\left(\frac{s}{b_{1j}}\right)^{c_{1j}}+\left(\frac{s}{b_{2j}}\right)^{c_{2j}}\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\left(\frac{t}{b_{1j}}\right)^{c_{1j}}+\left(\frac{t}{b_{2j}}\right)^{c_{2j}}\right)}}=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}s^{c_{2j}}\left(\left(\frac{s^{c_{1j}-c_{2j}}}{b_{1j}^{c_{1j}}}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{b_{2j}}\right)^{c_{2j}}\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\left(\frac{t^{c_{1j}-c_{2j}}}{b_{2j}^{c_{2j}}}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{b_{2j}}\right)^{c_{2j}}\right)}}t^{-c_{2j}/2}

which may be expressed as

\text{Corr}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(s),\mathcalboondox{S}(t)\right)=C_{0}t^{-d/2},\,\,\text{where}\,\,d=c_{2j}\,\,\text{and}\,\,C_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}s^{c_{2j}}\left(\left(\frac{s^{c_{1j}-c_{2j}}}{b_{1j}^{c_{1j}}}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{b_{2j}}\right)^{c_{2j}}\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\left(\frac{t^{c_{1j}-c_{2j}}}{b_{2j}^{c_{2j}}}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{b_{2j}}\right)^{c_{2j}}\right)}}.

Hence we observe that for Weibull rate functions, a NGSP possesses the LRD property if
d\in(0,2)
and the SRD property if
d\in(2,\infty)
.

3.4.
Factorial moments for NGSP

Theorem 3.1
.

The
r^{th}
factorial moment of NGSP for
r\geq 1
is given by

\Psi_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(r,t)=\sum_{m=0}^{r-1}\binom{r-1}{m}\frac{d^{m}}{du^{m}}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,r-m)+T_{j}(t)P(-j,r-m)\right]\right\}

where
P(a,b)=a(a-1)\ldots(a-b+1)
for
a\geq b
.

Proof.

From (
3.1
), the pgf of NGSP is given by

G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)=\exp\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)(u^{j}-1)+T_{j}(t)(u^{-j}-1)\right)\right].

For
r=1
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(1,t)=\frac{d}{du}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}

\displaystyle=G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)(ju^{j-1})+T_{j}(t)(-ju^{-j-1})\right)\bigg|_{u=1}

\displaystyle=\binom{0}{0}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,1)+T_{j}(t)P(-j,1)\right]\right\}

which implies the proposition statement is true for
r=1
. For
r=2
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(2,t)=\frac{d^{2}}{du^{2}}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}

\displaystyle=G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)(j(j-1)u^{j-2})+T_{j}(t)((-j)(-j-1)u^{-j-2})\right)\bigg|_{u=1}

\displaystyle+\frac{d}{du}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)(ju^{j-1})+T_{j}(t)(-ju^{-j-1})\right)\bigg|_{u=1}

\displaystyle=\binom{1}{0}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,2)+T_{j}(t)P(-j,2)\right]

\displaystyle+\binom{1}{1}\frac{d}{du}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,1)+T_{j}(t)P(-j,1)\right]

which implies the proposition statement is true for
r=2
. Now assume the statement is true for
r=n
, i.e.,

\Psi_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(n,t)=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{m}\frac{d^{m}}{du^{m}}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,n-m)+T_{j}(t)P(-j,n-m)\right]\right\}

For
r=n+1
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(n+1,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{n+1}}{du^{n+1}}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}=\frac{d}{du}\Psi_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(n,t)\bigg|_{u=1}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{du}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{m}\frac{d^{m}}{du^{m}}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,n-m)u^{j-n+m}+T_{j}(t)P(-j,n-m)u^{-j-n+m}\right]\bigg|_{u=1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{m}\frac{d^{m+1}}{du^{m+1}}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,n-m)u^{j-n+m}+T_{j}(t)P(-j,n-m)u^{-j-n+m}\right]\bigg|_{u=1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{m}\frac{d^{m}}{du^{m}}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,n-m+1)u^{j-n+m-1}+T_{j}(t)P(-j,n-m+1)u^{-j-n+m-1}\right]\bigg|_{u=1}

\displaystyle=G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,n+1)+T_{j}(t)P(-j,n+1)\right]+\frac{d^{n}}{du^{n}}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,1)+T_{j}(t)P(-j,1)\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\left[\binom{n-1}{m-1}+\binom{n-1}{m}\right]\frac{d^{m}}{du^{m}}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,n+1-m)+T_{j}(t)P(-j,n+1-m)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\frac{d^{m}}{du^{m}}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,n+1-m)+T_{j}(t)P(-j,n+1-m)\right]\right\}

which implies the proposition statement is true for
r=n+1
. Hence the result follows by mathematical induction.
∎

3.5.
State Probabilities of NGSP

Theorem 3.2
.

The state probabilities of NGSP are given by

p(n,t)=P\{\mathcalboondox{S}(t)=n\}=e^{-(A+B)}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{|n|}{2}}I_{|n|}\left(2\sqrt{AB}\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}

where
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)=A
,
\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)=B
and
I_{n}(.)
is the modified Bessel’s function of first kind.

Proof.

We know that the marginal distribution of the increment process
\{\mathcal{I}(t,v)\}_{t\geq 0}
of NGCP with cumulative intensity parameter
\Lambda_{j}(t)
for
v>0
and
n>0
is given by (see
Kataria et al. 2022
)

\displaystyle q_{n}(t,v)=P\{\mathcal{I}(t,v)=n\}

\displaystyle=P\{\mathcal{M}(t+v)-\mathcal{M}(v)=n\}=\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(\Lambda_{j}(v,t+v)^{x_{j}}\right)}{x_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(v,t+v)}.

where
\Omega(k,n)=\{(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{k})|\sum_{j=1}^{k}jx_{j}=n\,\,\text{for non-negative integers}\,\,\,x_{j}\}
. Substituting
v=0
in the above equation, the marginal distribution of NGCP can be obtained as follows:

q_{n}(t,0)=P\{\mathcal{M}(t)=n\}=\sum_{\Omega(k,n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)^{x_{j}}\right)}{x_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)}.

Hence the state probabilities of NGSP for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
are given by

\displaystyle p(n,t)

\displaystyle=P\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)=n\}\mathbb{I}_{\{n\geq 0\}}+P\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)=n\}\mathbb{I}_{\{n<0\}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}[P\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)=m+n\}P\{\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)=m\}\mathbb{I}_{\{n\geq 0\}}+P\{\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)=m+|n|\}P\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)=m\}\mathbb{I}_{\{n<0\}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\Omega(k,m+n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)^{x_{j}}\right)}{x_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)}\right)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\Omega(k,m)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(T_{j}(t)^{x_{j}}\right)}{x_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)}\right)\mathbb{I}_{\{n\geq 0\}}

(3.2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\Omega(k,m+|n|)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(T_{j}(t)^{x_{j}}\right)}{x_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)}\right)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\Omega(k,m)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)^{x_{j}}\right)}{x_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)}\right)\mathbb{I}_{\{n<0\}}

For
n\geq 0
, putting
x_{j}=a_{j}
and
m=y+\sum_{j=1}^{k}(j-1)a_{j}
in (
3.2
), we get

\displaystyle p(n,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\Omega(k,m+n)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)\right)^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)}\right)\left(\sum_{\Omega(k,m)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(T_{j}(t)\right)^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}=y+n}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)\right)^{a_{j}}}{a_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)}\right)\left(\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}=y}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left(T_{j}(t)\right)^{a_{j}}}{a_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)}\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle=e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)+T_{j}(t)\right)}\sum_{y=0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{(n+y)!y!}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)\right)^{n+y}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)\right)^{y}\right]

where we have used the multinomial theorem (see section
2.1
) in the last step. Now denoting
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)=A
and
\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)=B
, and using modified Bessel’s function of the first kind (see section
2.2
) in (
3.3
), we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle p(n,t)=e^{-(A+B)}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}I_{n}\left(2\sqrt{AB}\right)\,\,.

Similarly, for
n<0
,
p(n,t)
can be obtained by replacing
n
with
-n
in (
3.4
). Thus

\displaystyle p(n,t)

\displaystyle=e^{-(A+B)}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{|n|}{2}}I_{|n|}\left(2\sqrt{AB}\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}\,\,.

∎

3.6.
Recurrence relations satisfied by the state probabilities of NGSP

Theorem 3.4
.

The state probabilities of NGSP satisfy the following recurrence relation

p(n,t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)p(n-j,t)-T_{j}(t)p(n+j,t)\right),\quad n\geq 1\,.

Proof.

Using the definition of pgf for NGSP, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{du}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{du}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}u^{i}p(i,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(i+1)p(i+1,t)u^{i}.

Also from (
3.1
), we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{du}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)u^{j-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-j)T_{j}(t)u^{-j-1}\right)G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t).

Comparing the RHS of (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(i+1)p(i+1,t)u^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)u^{j-1}-T_{j}(t)u^{-j-1}\right)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}u^{i}p(i,t)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\left(\sum_{i=j-1}^{k-1}p(i-j+1,t)u^{i}+\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}p(i-j+1,t)u^{i}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{k}jT_{j}(t)\left(\sum_{i=-j-1}^{k-1}p(i+j+1,t)u^{i}+\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}p(i+j+1,t)u^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{i+1}j\Lambda_{j}(t)p(i-j+1,t)u^{i}+\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)p(i-j+1,t)u^{i}

(3.10)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=-k-1}^{k-1}\sum_{j=-i-1}^{k}jT_{j}(t)p(i+j+1,t)u^{i}-\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k}jT_{j}(t)p(i+j+1,t)u^{i}\,\,.

Now equating the coefficients of
u^{i}
for
i\leq k-1
on both sides of (
3.10
), we obtain

(i+1)p(i+1,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{i+1}j\Lambda_{j}(t)p(i-j+1,t)-\sum_{j=-i-1}^{k}jT_{j}(t)p(i+j+1,t)

which reduces to

(3.11)

p(n,t)=\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\Lambda_{j}(t)p(n-j,t)-\sum_{j=-n}^{k}jT_{j}(t)p(n+j,t)\right),\quad-k\leq n\leq k

as in the first summation
p(n-(n+1),t),p(n-(n+2),t),...,p(n-k,t)
are all vanishing and in the second summation
T_{-n}(t),T_{-n+1}(t),...,T_{-1}(t)
all are zeroes. Also
p(-i,t)=0
for
1\leq i\leq k
.
Again, by equating the coefficients of
u^{i}
for
i\geq k
on both sides of (
3.10
), we obtain

(i+1)p(i+1,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)p(i-j+1,t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}jT_{j}(t)p(i+j+1,t)

which reduces to

(3.12)

p(n,t)=\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)p(n-j,t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}jT_{j}(t)p(n+j,t)\right),\quad n\geq k+1.

Combining (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), we have

p(n,t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)p(n-j,t)-T_{j}(t)p(n+j,t)\right],\quad n\geq 1.

∎

The next result follows from Theorem
3.4
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The state probabilities of NGCP satisfy the following recurrence relation

q(n,t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{min\{n,k\}}j\Lambda_{j}(t)q(n-j,t),\quad n\geq 1.

3.7.
Transition probabilities of NGSP

Theorem 3.5
.

The transition probabilities of NGSP are given by

P\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(t+\delta)=m\mid\mathcalboondox{S}(t)=n\right)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta),&m>n,\,m=n+i,i=1,2,...k;\\
\mu_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta),&m<n,m=n-i,\,i=1,2,...k;\\
1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(t)\delta-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta),&m=n;\\
o(\delta),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

i.e., at most
k
events can occur in a very small interval of time and even though the probability for more than
k
events is non-zero, it is negligible.

Proof.

Denote
\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)
to be the first and second processes respectively in Definition
3.1
. Then

\displaystyle P\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(t+\delta)=n+i\mid\mathcalboondox{S}(t)=n\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}P(\text{the first process has $i+j$ arrivals and the second process has $j$ arrivals})

\displaystyle+P(\text{the first process has $i$ arrivals and the second process has $0$ arrivals})+o(\delta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}\left(\lambda_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\right)*\left(\mu_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\right)+\left(\lambda_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\right)*\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\right)

\displaystyle+o(\delta)=\lambda_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\,\,.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle P\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(t+\delta)=n-i\mid\mathcalboondox{S}(t)=n\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}P(\text{the first process has $j$ arrivals and the second process has $i+j$ arrivals})

\displaystyle+P(\text{the first process has $0$ arrivals and the second process has $i$ arrivals})+o(\delta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}\left(\lambda_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\right)*\left(\mu_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\right)+\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\right)*\left(\mu_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\right)

\displaystyle+o(\delta)=\mu_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\,\,.

Finally

\displaystyle P\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(t+\delta)=n\mid\mathcalboondox{S}(t)=n\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}P(\text{the first process has $j$ arrivals and the second process has $j$ arrivals})

\displaystyle+P(\text{the first process has $0$ arrivals and the second process has $0$ arrivals})+o(\delta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\right)*\left(\mu_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\right)+\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\right)*\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)\right)

\displaystyle+o(\delta)=1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(t)\delta-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta)

which proves the theorem.
∎

3.8.
NGSP as a weighted sum

Theorem 3.6
.

The NGSP is equal in distribution to the weighted sum of
k
independent Non-homogeneous Skellam processes (NSPs).

Proof.

Denote the NSPs by
\{\mathcal{S}_{j}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}:=\{\mathcal{N}_{1j}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}-\{\mathcal{N}_{2j}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, where
\{\mathcal{N}_{1j}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\mathcal{N}_{2j}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
are independent non-homogeneous Poisson processes with respective cumulative intensity functions
\Lambda_{j}(t)
and
T_{j}(t)
for
j=1,2,...,k
. The mgfs of
\mathcal{N}_{1j}(t)
and
\mathcal{N}_{2j}(t)
are given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left(u\mathcal{N}_{1j}(t)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)(e^{u}-1)\right),\quad\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left(u\mathcal{N}_{2j}(t)\right)\right)=\exp\left(T_{j}(t)(e^{u}-1)\right).

So the m.g.f. of the weighted sum
\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\mathcal{S}_{j}(t)
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left(u\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\mathcal{S}_{j}(t)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(u\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\mathcal{N}_{1j}(t)\right)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-u\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\mathcal{N}_{2j}(t)\right)\right]

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)(e^{uj}-1)+T_{j}(t)(e^{-uj}-1)\right)\right).

The m.g.f. of NGSP can be obtained from (
3.1
) by replacing ‘u’ with ‘
e^{u}
’ as follows:

(3.14)

\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left(u\mathcalboondox{S}(t)\right)\right)=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)(e^{uj}-1)+T_{j}(t)(e^{-uj}-1)\right)\right)

Therefore from (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), we get

\mathcalboondox{S}(t)\overset{d}{=}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\mathcal{S}_{j}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left(\mathcal{N}_{1j}(t)-\mathcal{N}_{2j}(t)\right).

∎

The next result follows from Theorem
3.6
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The NGCP is equal in distribution to the weighted sum of
k
independent Non-homogeneous Poisson processes (NPPs).

3.9.
Martingale Characterization of NGSP

Theorem 3.7
.

The process
\left\{\mathcalboondox{S}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)-T_{j}(t)\right)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale with respect to the natural filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma\left(\left\{\mathcalboondox{S}(s)\right\},~0<s\leq t\right)
.

Proof.

For all
0<s\leq t
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)-T_{j}(t)\right)\big|\left\{\mathcalboondox{S}(s)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\big|\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(s)\right\}\right)

(3.15)

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}jT_{j}(t)\big|\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(s)\right\}\right).

Using the tower property of conditional expectation, the first term on the RHS of (
3.15
) may be written as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\big|\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(s),\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)\right\}\right)\big|\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(s)\right\}\right]

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\big|\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)\right\}\right)\big|\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(s)\right\}\right]

where we have used the conditional independence between
\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(s)\right\}
given
\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)\right\}
. Since
the process
\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale (see
Kataria et al. 2022
) with respect to the natural filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma\left(\mathcal{M}(s),0<s\leq t\right)
, we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\big|\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(s).

Using (
3.9
) and (
3.17
), the first term on the RHS of (
3.15
) reduces to

(3.18)

\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\big|\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(s)\right\}\right)=\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(s)

as
\sigma(\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)\right\}\subset\sigma(\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(s)\right\})
. Similarly, the second term on the RHS of (
3.15
) reduces to

(3.19)

\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}jT_{j}(t)\big|\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(s)\right\}\right)=\mathcal{M}_{2}(s)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}jT_{j}(s).

Substituting (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) in (
3.15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)-T_{j}(t)\right)\big|\left\{\mathcalboondox{S}(s)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcalboondox{S}(s)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left(\Lambda_{j}(s)-T_{j}(s)\right)

for all
0<s\leq t
, thereby proving the result.
∎

3.10.
Arrival time and first passage time of NGSP

Proposition 3.3
.

Consider the
n^{th}
arrival time of NGSP or the arrival time of
n^{th}
NGSP event defined as

\tau_{n}=min\{t\geq 0:\mathcalboondox{S}(t)=n\}\,.

Then the distribution function of
\tau_{n}
is

F_{\tau_{n}}(t)=\sum_{x=n}^{\infty}\left(e^{-(A+B)}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}I_{|x|}\left(2\sqrt{AB}\right)\right)\,.

Proof.

Using Theorem
3.2
, we have for
n\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle F_{\tau_{n}}(t)

\displaystyle=P\left(\tau_{n}\leq t\right)=P\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(t)\geq n\right)=\sum_{x=n}^{\infty}P\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(t)=x\right)=\sum_{x=n}^{\infty}\left(e^{-(A+B)}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}I_{|x|}\left(2\sqrt{AB}\right)\right)\,.

∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
T_{n}
be the time of the first upcrossing of the level
n
for NGSP given by

T_{n}=\text{inf}\{s\geq 0:\mathcalboondox{S}(s)\geq n\}\,.

Then

P\{T_{n}>t\}=\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}\left(e^{-(A+B)}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}I_{|x|}\left(2\sqrt{AB}\right)\right)\,.

Proof.

Using Theorem
3.2
, we have for
n\in\mathbb{Z}

P\{T_{n}>t\}=P\{\mathcalboondox{S}(t)<n\}=\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}P\left(\mathcalboondox{S}(t)=x\right)=\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}\left(e^{-(A+B)}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}I_{|x|}\left(2\sqrt{AB}\right)\right)\,.

∎

4.
Fractional variant of NGSP

In this section, we introduce the fractional variant of NGSP and study its various characteristics.

Definition 4.1
.

We define the fractional variant of NGSP by time changing it with an independent inverse stable subordinator
\{Y_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and call it the Non-homogeneous Generalized Fractional Skellam process (
NGFSP
) denoted by

\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t):=\begin{cases}\mathcalboondox{S}(Y_{\alpha}(t))&\text{if}~0<\alpha<1,\\
\mathcalboondox{S}(t)&\text{if}~\alpha=1.\end{cases}

The fractional variant of NGSP can also be obtained by taking the difference of two independent NGFCPs (the fractional variant of NGCP) as

\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t):=\begin{cases}\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\alpha}(t)-\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\alpha}(t)&\text{if}~0<\alpha<1,\\
\mathcalboondox{S}(t)&\text{if}~\alpha=1.\end{cases}

where
\{\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
are two independent NGFCPs.

4.1.
Probability generating function of NGFSP

The pgf of NGFSP is given by

\displaystyle G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(u,t)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,x)h_{\alpha}(x,t)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)(u^{j}-1)+T_{j}(t)(u^{-j}-1)\right)\right)h_{\alpha}(x,t)dx

where
h_{\alpha}(x,t)
is the density of the inverse stable subordinator
\{Y_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
.

4.2.
Mean, Variance, and Covariance

We will first find the moments of NGFCP, which will be then used to find the moments of NGFSP. Using the moments of NGCP in section
2.8
, the mean of NGFCP is

(4.20)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}(t)\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)|Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}(x)\right)h_{\alpha}(x,t)dx=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]

and the second moment is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}(t)\right)^{2}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\mathcal{M}(x)\right)^{2}\right]h_{\alpha}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\mathbb{V}\left(\mathcal{M}(x)\right)+\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}(x)\right)\right)^{2}\right]h_{\alpha}(x,t)dx

(4.21)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right]+\mathbb{E}\left(\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]^{2}\right).

Using (
4.20
) and (
4.21
), the variance of NGFCP is

(4.22)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right]+\mathbb{V}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right).

Next, using the law of total covariance, we can find the covariance of NGFCP as follows:

\displaystyle\text{Cov}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}(s),\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Cov}\left(\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(s)),\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\text{Cov}\left(\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(s)),\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\mid Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]

(4.23)

\displaystyle+\text{Cov}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(s))\mid Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right),\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\mid Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right].

The first term on the R.H.S. of (
4.23
) can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(s))\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(t))|Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(s))|Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(t))|Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(x)\mathcal{M}(y)\right]f_{\left(Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)}(x,y)dxdy-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(x)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(y)\right]f_{\left(Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)}(x,y)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{Cov}\left(\mathcal{M}(x),\mathcal{M}(y)\right)f_{\left(Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)}(x,y)dxdy

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(s)\wedge Y_{\alpha}(t))\right)\quad(\text{from section}~\ref{ngcp})

(4.24)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(s\wedge t))\right)\ \quad(\text{since}\,\,\{Y_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}\,\,\text{is an increasing process}).

The second term on the R.H.S. of (
4.23
) can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(s))|Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(t))|Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(s))|Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(t))|Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(x)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(y)\right]f_{\left(Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)}(x,y)dxdy-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(s))\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(x)\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(y)\right)f_{\left(Y_{\alpha}(s),Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)}(x,y)dxdy-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(s))\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{M}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(s))\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(s))\right]\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right]\right)

(4.25)

\displaystyle=\text{Cov}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(s)),\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right)\,.

On substituting (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) in (
4.23
), we have

(4.26)

\displaystyle\text{Cov}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}(s),\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}(t)\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(s\wedge t))\right)+\text{Cov}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(s)),\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right)\,.

Using (
4.20
), (
4.22
) and (
4.26
), we can obtain the mean, variance, and covariance of NGFSP as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)-T_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]\,,

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))+T_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right]+\mathbb{V}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left[\Lambda_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)+T_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]\right),

\displaystyle\text{Cov}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(s),\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(s\wedge t))+T_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(s\wedge t))\right)\right)+\text{Cov}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(s)),\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right)

\displaystyle+\text{Cov}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}jT_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(s)),\sum_{j=1}^{k}jT_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right).

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t)\right)-\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))+T_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right]+\mathbb{V}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left[\Lambda_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)+T_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)-T_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(j^{2}-j)\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{k}(j^{2}+j)\mathbb{E}\left[T_{j}(Y_{\alpha}(t))\right]+\mathbb{V}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left[\Lambda_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)+T_{j}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)\right)\right]\right)\geq 0,

which implies NGFSP exhibits over-dispersion. It can be shown similarly that NGFCP is also over-dispersed.

4.3.
Factorial moments for NGFSP

Using the factorial moments of NGSP and the p.g.f. of NGFSP, we can obtain the factorial moments for NGFSP.

Theorem 4.1
.

The
r^{th}
factorial moment of NGFSP for
r\geq 1
is given by

\Psi_{\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}}(r,t)=\sum_{m=0}^{r-1}\binom{r-1}{m}\frac{d^{m}}{du^{m}}G^{\alpha}_{\mathcalboondox{S}}(u,t)\bigg|_{u=1}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{j}(t)P(j,r-m)+T_{j}(t)P(-j,r-m)\right]\right\}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
3.1
and hence omitted.
∎

4.4.
Transition and state probabilities for NGFSP

The following results on transition probabilities and recurrence relations for state probabilities of NGFSP correspond to those for NGSP.

Theorem 4.2
.

The transition probabilities of NGFSP are given by

P\left(\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t+\delta)=m\mid\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t)=n\right)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta),&m>n,\,m=n+i,i=1,2,...k;\\
\mu_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta),&m<n,m=n-i,\,i=1,2,...k;\\
1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(t)\delta-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}(t)\delta+o(\delta),&m=n;\\
o(\delta),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
3.5
and hence omitted.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

The state probabilities
p^{\alpha}(n,t)
of NGFSP satisfy the following recurrence relation

p^{\alpha}(n,t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left(\Lambda_{j}(t)p^{\alpha}(n-j,t)-T_{j}(t)p^{\alpha}(n+j,t)\right),\quad n\geq 1\,.

Moreover for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the state probabilities satisfy the following system of fractional differential equations with initial conditions
p^{\alpha}(0,0)=1\,\,\text{and}\,\,p^{\alpha}(n,0)=0,\,\,n\neq 0
:

(4.27)

\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}p^{\alpha}(n,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}p(n-1,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{j}(t)+\frac{A}{B}\gamma_{j}(t)\right)-p(n,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{j}(t)+\gamma_{j}(t)\right)\right.\\
\left.+\frac{n}{2}p(n,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{A}\lambda_{j}(t)-\frac{1}{B}\gamma_{j}(t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}p(n+1,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\gamma_{j}(t)+\frac{B}{A}\lambda_{j}(t)\right)\right)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du\,.

Proof.

The proof for recurrence relations satisfied by
p^{\alpha}(n,t)
is similar to the proof of Proposition
3.4
and hence omitted. Next from Definition
4.1
, we have

(4.28)

\displaystyle p^{\alpha}(n,t)=P(\mathcalboondox{S}(Y_{\alpha}(t))=n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}P(\mathcalboondox{S}(u)=n)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du=\int_{0}^{\infty}p(n,u)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du

where
p(n,t)
is the pmf of
\{\mathcalboondox{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. Note that
p^{\alpha}(n,0)=p(n,0)
as
h_{\alpha}(u,0)=\delta_{0}(u)
. Taking R-L fractional derivative on both sides of (
4.28
), we get

\displaystyle D_{t}^{\alpha}p^{\alpha}(n,t)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}p(n,u)\frac{\partial}{\partial u}h_{\alpha}(u,t)du

\displaystyle=-p(n,u)h_{\alpha}(u,t)\big|_{0}^{\infty}+\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{\alpha}(u,t)\frac{d}{du}p(n,u)du

\displaystyle=-p(n,0)h_{\alpha}(0+,t)+\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{\alpha}(u,t)\frac{d}{du}p(n,u)du

\displaystyle=p(n,0)\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}+\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{\alpha}(u,t)\frac{d}{du}p(n,u)du

where we have used the fact

D_{t}^{\alpha}h_{\alpha}(u,t)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial u}h_{\alpha}(u,t)
and
h_{\alpha}(0+,t)=\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}
(see
Meerschaert and Straka 2013
). Now using the relationship between R-L Fractional derivative and the Caputo–Djrbashian fractional derivative (see section
2.4
) and Proposition
3.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}p^{\alpha}(n,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{\alpha}(u,t)\frac{d}{du}p(n,u)du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{2}p(n-1,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{j}(t)+\frac{A}{B}\gamma_{j}(t)\right)-p(n,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{j}(t)+\gamma_{j}(t)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{n}{2}p(n,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{A}\lambda_{j}(t)-\frac{1}{B}\gamma_{j}(t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}p(n+1,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\gamma_{j}(t)+\frac{B}{A}\lambda_{j}(t)\right)\right]h_{\alpha}(u,t)du.

∎

Remark 4.1
.

The density of the inverse stable subordinator in terms of the Wright function
M_{\alpha}(.)
is given by (see
Meerschaert et al. 2014
)

h_{\alpha}(x,t)=\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}M_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{t^{\alpha}}\right),\qquad M_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-z)^{n}}{n!\Gamma(1-n\alpha-\alpha)}.

Hence from Theorem
3.2
, the integral representation of the pmf of NGFSP is

\displaystyle p^{\alpha}(n,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}p(n,u)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du=t^{-\alpha}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(A+B)}I_{|n|}(2\sqrt{AB})M_{\alpha}\left(ut^{-\alpha}\right)du,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

4.5.
NGFSP as a weighted sum

A non-homogeneous fractional Skellam process is obtained from a non-homogeneous Skellam process by time changing it with an independent inverse stable subordinator. Using the weighted sum representation for NGSP, we can derive the following representation for NGFSP.

Proposition 4.1
.

The NGFSP is equal in distribution to the weighted sum of
k
independent Non-homogeneous fractional Skellam processes (NFSPs).

4.6.
Arrival time and first passage time of NGFSP

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\tau_{n}^{\alpha}
be the
n^{th}
arrival time of NGFSP defined as
\tau_{n}^{\alpha}=min\{t\geq 0:\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t)=n\}
. Then the distribution function of
\tau_{n}^{\alpha}
is

F_{\tau_{n}^{\alpha}}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{x=n}^{\infty}\left(e^{-(A+B)}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}I_{|x|}\left(2\sqrt{AB}\right)\right)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du

where
A=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(u)
and
B=\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(u)
.

Proof.

Using (
4.28
), we have for
n\in\mathbb{Z}

F_{\tau_{n}^{\alpha}}(t)=\sum_{x=n}^{\infty}P\left(\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t)=x\right)=\sum_{x=n}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}p(x,u)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{x=n}^{\infty}p(x,u)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du=\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{\tau_{n}}(u)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du.

The result follows now from Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
T_{n}^{\alpha}
be the time the first upcrossing of the level
n
for NGFSP given by

T_{n}^{\alpha}=\text{inf}\{s\geq 0:\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(s)\geq n\}\,.

Then

P\{T_{n}^{\alpha}>t\}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}\left(e^{-(A+B)}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}I_{|x|}\left(2\sqrt{AB}\right)\right)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du\,.

Proof.

Using (
4.28
), we have for
n\in\mathbb{Z}

P\{T_{n}^{\alpha}>t\}=\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}P\left(\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t)=x\right)=\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}p(x,u)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}p(x,u)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du=\int_{0}^{\infty}P\{T_{n}>t\}h_{\alpha}(u,t)du.

The result follows now from Proposition
3.4
.
∎

5.
Increment processes of the NGSP and NGFSP

In this section, we introduce the increment processes of the NGSP and the NGFSP, and study their marginals and governing equations.

Definition 5.1
.

The increment process of the NGSP is a stochastic process
\{I(t,v)\}_{t\geq 0}
for
v\geq 0
defined as

\displaystyle I(t,v)

\displaystyle=\mathcalboondox{S}(t+v)-\mathcalboondox{S}(v)=\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}(t+v)-\mathcal{M}_{1}(v)\right)-\left(\mathcal{M}_{2}(t+v)-\mathcal{M}_{2}(v)\right)=I_{1}(t,v)-I_{2}(t,v)

where
\{I_{1}(t,v)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{I_{2}(t,v)\}_{t\geq 0}
are the increment processes of the NGCPs
\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

The marginals of the increment process of NGSP are given by

q_{l}(t,v)=P\left(I(t,v)=l\right)=e^{-(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})}\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{B^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{|n|}{2}}I_{|n|}\left(2\sqrt{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\right).

where

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(v,t+v)=A^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(v,t+v)=B^{\prime}.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle q_{l}(t,v)

\displaystyle=P\left(I_{1}(t,v)-I_{2}(t,v)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P\left(I_{1}(t,v)=m+l\right)P\left(I_{2}(t,v)=m\right)I_{\{l\geq 0\}}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P\left(I_{2}(t,v)=m+|l|\right)P\left(I_{1}(t,v)=m\right)I_{\{l<0\}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\Omega(k,m+l)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(\Lambda_{j}(v,t+v))^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(v,t+v)}\right)\left(\sum_{\Omega(k,m)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(T_{j}(v,t+v))^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(v,t+v)}\right)I_{\{l\geq 0\}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\Omega(k,m+l)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(\Lambda_{j}(v,t+v))^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(v,t+v)}\right)\left(\sum_{\Omega(k,m)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(T_{j}(v,t+v))^{x_{j}}}{x_{j}!}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(v,t+v)}\right)I_{\{l<0\}}.

Following the steps of the proof for Theorem
3.2
, it can be shown that

q_{l}(t,v)=e^{-(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})}\left(\frac{A^{\prime}}{B^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{|n|}{2}}I_{|n|}\left(2\sqrt{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\right),\quad l\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

∎

Definition 5.2
.

The increment process of NGFSP is obtained by time changing the increment process of NGSP with an independent inverse stable subordinator as follows:

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}(t,v)

\displaystyle=\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(t+v)-\mathcalboondox{S}^{\alpha}(v)=\mathcalboondox{S}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)+v\right)-\mathcalboondox{S}(v).

Proposition 5.1
.

The marginals of the increment process of NGFSP are given by

\displaystyle q_{l}^{\alpha}(t,v)

\displaystyle=P\left(\mathcalboondox{S}\left(Y_{\alpha}(t)+v\right)-\mathcalboondox{S}(v)=l\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}q_{l}(t,u)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du,\quad l\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

where
q_{l}(t,u)
is given in Theorem
5.1
.

Remark 5.1
.

Putting
v=0
in Theorem
5.1
and Proposition
5.1
, we get the p.m.f.s of NGSP (see Theorem
3.2
) and NGFSP (see (
4.28
)) respectively.

Theorem 5.2
.

The p.m.f. of the increment process of NGFSP satisfies the following system of fractional differential-integral equations:

\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}q_{l}^{\alpha}(t,v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}p(n-1,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{j}(t)+\frac{A}{B}\gamma_{j}(t)\right)-p(n,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{j}(t)+\gamma_{j}(t)\right)\right.\\
\left.+\frac{n}{2}p(n,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{A}\lambda_{j}(t)-\frac{1}{B}\gamma_{j}(t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}p(n+1,t)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\gamma_{j}(t)+\frac{B}{A}\lambda_{j}(t)\right)\right)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du.

Proof.

The characteristic function of
I_{\alpha}(t,v)
is

(5.29)

\phi_{I_{\alpha}(t,v)}(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{I(u,v)}(\xi)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du

where
\phi_{I(u,v)}(\xi)
is the characteristic function of
I(t,v)
given by

(5.30)

\phi_{I(u,v)}(\xi)=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(v,t+v)(e^{i\xi j}-1)+T_{j}(v,t+v)(e^{-i\xi j}-1)\right)\right),\quad i=\sqrt{-1}.

Now taking the Laplace transform of (
5.29
) and using (
5.30
) along with the Laplace transform of inverse stable subordinator (see section
2.5
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left\{\phi_{I_{\alpha}(t,v)}(\xi)\right\}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{I(u,v)}(\xi)\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(u,s)du

\displaystyle=s^{\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(v,u+v)(e^{i\xi j}-1)+T_{j}(v,u+v)(e^{-i\xi j}-1)\right)\right)e^{-us^{\alpha}}du

\displaystyle=s^{\alpha-1}\left\{\left[\frac{e^{-us^{\alpha}}}{s^{\alpha}}\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(v,u+v)(e^{i\xi j}-1)+T_{j}(v,u+v)(e^{-i\xi j}-1)\right)\right)\right]_{0}^{\infty}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{s^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{j}(u+v)(e^{i\xi j}-1)+\gamma_{j}(u+v)(e^{-i\xi j}-1)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(v,u+v)(e^{i\xi j}-1)+T_{j}(v,u+v)(e^{-i\xi j}-1)\right)\right)\times e^{-us^{\alpha}}du\right\}.

It follows that

\displaystyle s^{\alpha}\mathcal{L}\left\{\phi_{I_{\alpha}(t,v)}(\xi)\right\}(s)-s^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{j}(u+v)(e^{i\xi j}-1)+\gamma_{j}(u+v)(e^{-i\xi j}-1)\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(v,u+v)(e^{i\xi j}-1)+T_{j}(v,u+v)(e^{-i\xi j}-1)\right)\right)s^{\alpha-1}e^{-us^{\alpha}}du

\displaystyle\implies\mathcal{L}\left\{\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}\phi_{I_{\alpha}(t,v)}(\xi)\right\}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{j}(u+v)(e^{i\xi j}-1)+\gamma_{j}(u+v)(e^{-i\xi j}-1)\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}(v,u+v)(e^{i\xi j}-1)+T_{j}(v,u+v)(e^{-i\xi j}-1)\right)\right)\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(u,s)du

where we used the Laplace transform of fractional Caputo–Djrbashian derivative (see section
2.4
). Next, on taking the inverse Laplace transform and using (
5.30
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{d^{\alpha}}{dt^{\alpha}}\phi_{I_{\alpha}(t,v)}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{j}(u+v)(e^{i\xi j}-1)+\gamma_{j}(u+v)(e^{-i\xi j}-1)\right)\phi_{I(u,v)}(\xi)h_{\alpha}(u,t)du.

The result follows by using the inversion formula for the characteristic functions
\phi_{I_{\alpha}(t,v)}
and
\phi_{I(u,v)}
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

On substituting
v=0
in Theorem
5.2
, we get the system of fractional differential equations satisfied by the state probabilities
p^{\alpha}(n,t)
of NGFSP (see Theorem
4.3
).

6.
An Alternative Version of NGFSP

In this section, we introduce an alternative version of NGFSP and study its properties. The main advantage of this version over the usual one is its closed-form p.m.f. making it suitable for further analysis. First we need to discuss some related processes which give rise to this version.

Leonenko et al. 2017
studied the fractional version of a non-homogeneous Poisson process by time changing it with an independent inverse stable subordinator. They also mentioned an alternative fractional version, which we call the Non-homogeneous Time Fractional Poisson Process (NTFPP)

\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)=N_{1}(Y_{\alpha}(\Lambda(t)))\,

where
\{N_{\lambda}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is the homogeneous Poisson process with rate
\lambda
and
\Lambda(t)
is the rate function of
\{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. The p.m.f. of TFPP is given by

p^{\alpha}(n,t)=\frac{(\lambda t^{\alpha})^{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+n)!}{k!}\frac{(-\lambda t^{\alpha})^{k}}{\Gamma\left((k+n)\alpha+1\right)},

using which the p.m.f. of NTFPP can be obtained by substituting
\lambda=1
and
t=\Lambda(t)
as follows:

(6.31)

\tilde{p}^{\alpha}(n,t)=\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{n\alpha}}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+n)!}{k!}\frac{\left(-\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{\alpha}\right)^{k}}{\Gamma\left((k+n)\alpha+1\right)}=\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{n\alpha}E_{\alpha,n\alpha+1}^{n+1}\left(-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}\right),\quad n\geq 0.

Note that the RHS of (
6.31
) may be written as

(6.32)

\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{n\alpha}E_{\alpha,n\alpha+1}^{n+1}\left(-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}\right)=\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{n\alpha}E_{\alpha,n\alpha+1}^{n}\left(-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}\right)-\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{(n+1)\alpha}E_{\alpha,(n+1)\alpha+1}^{n+1}\left(-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}\right).

Let
\tilde{T}^{\alpha}_{n}
denote the
n
th arrival time of NTFPP. By definition, we have

(6.33)

P\{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)=n\}=P\{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)\leq n\}-P\{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)<n\}=P\{\tilde{T}^{\alpha}_{n+1}>t\}-P\{\tilde{T}^{\alpha}_{n}>t\}=\tilde{F}^{\alpha}_{n}(t)-\tilde{F}^{\alpha}_{n+1}(t)

where
\tilde{F}^{\alpha}_{n}
denotes the distribution function of
\tilde{T}^{\alpha}_{n}
. From (
6.32
) and (
6.33
), it follows that

(6.34)

\tilde{F}^{\alpha}_{n}(t)=\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{n\alpha}E_{\alpha,n\alpha+1}^{n}\left(-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}\right)

using mathematical induction. Next, the probability generating function (p.g.f.) of TFPP is
G(u,t)=E_{\alpha,1}(\lambda t^{\alpha}(u-1))
for
|u|\leq 1
. So the p.g.f. of NTFPP is

(6.35)

\tilde{G}^{\alpha}(u,t)=\mathbb{E}\left(u^{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)}\right)=E_{\alpha,1}((\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}(u-1)).

For other properties of NTFPP, see
Maheshwari and Vellaisamy 2019b
.
As a generalization of NTFPP, we introduce an alternative version of NGFCP as follows.

Definition 6.1
.

We define the Non-Homogeneous Generalized Fractional Counting process (NHGFCP) as

\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)=\mathbb{M}(Y_{\alpha}(\Lambda(t))

where
\{\mathbb{M}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a GCP with rates
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
and
\Lambda(t)
is the rate function of
\{\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
.

Similar to the compound Poisson representation of GFCP (see
Di Crescenzo et al. 2016
), the corresponding representation for NHGFCP is given by

(6.36)

\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)\overset{d}{=}\sum_{i=1}^{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)}X_{i}

where
\{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a NTFPP with rate function
\Lambda(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)
and
\Lambda_{j}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{j}(u)du
. Moreover
\{X_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a sequence of i.i.d. random variables, independent of
\{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
such that

(6.37)

P\{X_{n}=j\}=\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)}{\Lambda(t)}\,,\qquad j=1,2,...,k\,.

We are now ready to introduce an alternative version of NGFSP.

Definition 6.2
.

The Non-Homogeneous Generalized Fractional Skellam Process NHGFSP is defined as

\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)=\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)-\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)

where
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
are two independent NHGFCPs with rate functions
\Lambda(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)
and
T(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)
respectively.

Proposition 6.1
.

The moment generating function (m.g.f.) of NHGFSP is given by

M_{\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)}(s)=E_{\alpha,1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)(e^{sj}-1)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\right)\times E_{\alpha,1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)(e^{sj}-1)(T(t))^{\alpha-1}\right).

Proof.

The m.g.f. of
Y(t)=\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(e^{sY(t)}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(e^{sY(t)}|\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{s\sum_{i=1}^{x}X_{i}}\right)P\{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)=x\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\left(\mathbb{E}\left(e^{sX_{1}}\right)\right)^{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)e^{sj}\right)^{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)}\right)

(6.38)

\displaystyle=E_{\alpha,1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)(e^{sj}-1)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\right)\quad(\text{using \eqref{pgf.ntfpp}}).

Similarly, the m.g.f. of
\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}_{2}^{\alpha}(t)
is
E_{\alpha,1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)(e^{sj}-1)(T(t))^{\alpha-1}\right)
. Since
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
are independent, the m.g.f. of NHGFSP is

M_{\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)}(s)=E_{\alpha,1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)(e^{sj}-1)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\right)\times E_{\alpha,1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}(t)(e^{sj}-1)(T(t))^{\alpha-1}\right).

∎

Theorem 6.1
.

The p.m.f. of NHGFSP for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
t\geq 0
is given by

\displaystyle P\{\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)=n\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}[P\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)=m+n\}P\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)=m\}\mathbb{I}_{\{n\geq 0\}}+P\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)=m+|n|\}P\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)=m\}\mathbb{I}_{\{n<0\}}]

where for
j\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle P\{\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)=j\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+...+\alpha_{k}=r\\
\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+...+k\alpha_{k}=j\end{subarray}}\binom{r}{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{k}}\prod_{s=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{s}(t)\right)^{\alpha_{s}}\times\frac{1}{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{r(1-\alpha)}}E_{\alpha,r\alpha+1}^{r+1}\left(-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}\right),

\displaystyle P\{\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)=j\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+...+\alpha_{k}=r\\
\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+...+k\alpha_{k}=j\end{subarray}}\binom{r}{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{k}}\prod_{s=1}^{k}\left(T_{s}(t)\right)^{\alpha_{s}}\times\frac{1}{\left(T(t)\right)^{r(1-\alpha)}}E_{\alpha,r\alpha+1}^{r+1}\left(-(T(t))^{\alpha}\right).

Proof.

From (
6.36
), we have

(6.39)

\displaystyle P\{\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)=j\}=\sum_{r=0}^{j}P\{X_{1}+X_{2}+...+X_{k}=j\}P\{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)=r\}.

Since
X_{1},X_{2},...,X_{k}
are identically distributed, it follows from (
6.37
) that

(6.40)

\displaystyle P\{X_{1}+X_{2}+...+X_{k}=j\}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+...+\alpha_{k}=r\\
\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+...+k\alpha_{k}=j\end{subarray}}\binom{r}{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},...\alpha_{k}}\prod_{s=1}^{k}\left(\frac{\Lambda_{s}(t)}{\Lambda(t)}\right)^{\alpha_{s}}.

Therefore from (
6.31
), (
6.39
) and (
6.40
), we get

\displaystyle P\{\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)=j\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+...+\alpha_{k}=r\\
\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+...+k\alpha_{k}=j\end{subarray}}\binom{r}{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{k}}\prod_{s=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{s}(t)\right)^{\alpha_{s}}\times\frac{1}{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{r(1-\alpha)}}E_{\alpha,r\alpha+1}^{r+1}\left(-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}\right).

Similarly we have

\displaystyle P\{\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)=j\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+...+\alpha_{k}=r\\
\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+...+k\alpha_{k}=j\end{subarray}}\binom{r}{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{k}}\prod_{s=1}^{k}\left(T_{s}(t)\right)^{\alpha_{s}}\times\frac{1}{\left(T(t)\right)^{r(1-\alpha)}}E_{\alpha,r\alpha+1}^{r+1}\left(-(T(t))^{\alpha}\right).

Since
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
are independent, the p.m.f. of NHGFSP is

\displaystyle P\{\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)=n\}

\displaystyle=P\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)-\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)=n\}\mathbb{I}_{\{n\geq 0\}}+P\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)-\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)=n\}\mathbb{I}_{\{n<0\}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}[P\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)=m+n\}P\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)=m\}\mathbb{I}_{\{n\geq 0\}}+P\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)=m+|n|\}P\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)=m\}\mathbb{I}_{\{n<0\}}].

∎

6.1.
Mean, Variance and Covariance

Using the p.g.f. in (
6.35
), the first and second moments of NTFPP are given by

\mathbb{E}\left(\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)\right)=\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)},\quad\mathbb{V}\left(\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)\right)=\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\left[1+\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\left\{\frac{\alpha B(\alpha,1/2)}{2^{2\alpha}-1}-1\right\}\right]

where
B(a,b)=\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)/\Gamma(a+b)
. Hence using (
6.36
), the mean and variance of NHGFCP are

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)\right)\mathbb{E}\left(X_{1}\right)=\left(\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\right),

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\left(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)\right)\mathbb{V}(X_{1})+[\mathbb{E}\left(X_{1}\right)]^{2}\mathbb{V}\left(\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\Lambda_{j}(t)\right)+\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\right)^{2}\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\left\{\frac{\alpha B(\alpha,1/2)}{2^{2\alpha-1}}-1\right\}\right].

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\left(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)\right)-\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{2\alpha}}{\Gamma^{2}(\alpha+1)}\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\right)^{2}\left\{\frac{\alpha B(\alpha,1/2)}{2^{2\alpha-1}}-1\right\}>0

since
\alpha B(\alpha,1/2)>2^{2\alpha-1}
. Thus NHGFCP is over-dispersed. Next for
s<t
,

\displaystyle\text{Cov}[\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(s),\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(s)(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)-\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(s))+(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(s))^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(s)]\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(s)]\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)-\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(s))+\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(s))^{2}-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(s)]\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)]=\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(s)).

It follows that for arbitrary
s,t>0
, we have
\text{Cov}[\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(s),\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t)]=\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(s\wedge t))
. Using the above expressions, we can obtain the mean, variance and covariance of NHGFSP as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\right)+\left(\frac{\left(T(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}jT_{j}(t)\right),

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\left(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\Lambda_{j}(t)\right)+\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\Lambda_{j}(t)\right)^{2}\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\left\{\frac{\alpha B(\alpha,1/2)}{2^{2\alpha-1}}-1\right\}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\left(T(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\left[\left(\frac{1}{T(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}T_{j}(t)\right)+\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}jT_{j}(t)\right)^{2}\frac{\left(T(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\left\{\frac{\alpha B(\alpha,1/2)}{2^{2\alpha-1}}-1\right\}\right],

\displaystyle\text{Cov}[\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(s),\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(s\wedge t))+\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(s\wedge t))=\mathbb{V}\left(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(s\wedge t)\right).

Moreover since
\mathbb{V}\left(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)\right)>\mathbb{E}\left(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)\right)
, NHGFSP is over-dispersed.

6.2.
Correlation structure

For fixed
s,t>0
, the correlation function of NHGFSP where
s<t
is

\displaystyle\text{Corr}(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(s),\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{Cov}(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(s),\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t))}{\sqrt{\mathbb{V}(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(s))}\sqrt{\mathbb{V}(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t))}}=\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(s))}{\mathbb{V}(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t))}}.

Note that

\mathbb{V}\left(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)\right)>\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{2\alpha}+\left(T(t)\right)^{2\alpha}}{[\Gamma(\alpha+1)]^{2}}

Thus we have

\displaystyle\text{Corr}(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(s),\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t))

\displaystyle<c(s)(\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{2\alpha}+\left(T(t)\right)^{2\alpha})^{-1/2}\quad\text{where}~c(s)=\sqrt{\mathbb{V}\left(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(s)\right)}>0.

Suppose
\Lambda(t)=(t/b)^{a}
and
T(t)=(t/d)^{c}
(Weibull’s rate functions) where
a,b,c,d>0
are some constants. Then NHGFSP has the LRD property if
a\wedge c<1/\alpha
and the SRD property if
1/\alpha<a\wedge c<2/\alpha
.

6.3.
Waiting time

We want to find the distribution of the waiting time for NHGFCP until the first occurrence of a jump of size
j
for
1\leq j\leq k
. First note that the NHGFCP can be written as

\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}(t))=\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}_{j}(t)

where
\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}_{j}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)}\bm{1}_{\{X_{1}=j\}}
is the number of jumps of size
j
performed by the NHGFCP in the interval
(0,t]
. Next consider the random variables

H_{j}=\inf\{s>0:\mathcal{\widetilde{M}}^{\alpha}_{j}(s)=1\},\quad G_{j}\sim\text{Geo}\left(\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)}{\Lambda(t)}\right).

Here
H_{j}
is the first occurrence time of a jump of size
j
for the NHGFCP and
G_{j}
is a geometric random variable with parameter
\Lambda_{j}(t)/\Lambda(t)
denoting the order of the first jump of size
j
among all jumps.

Theorem 6.2
.

The distribution function of
H_{j}
is

P(H_{j}\leq t)=\Lambda_{j}(t)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha+1}\left(-\Lambda_{j}(t)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\right).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle P(H_{j}\leq t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(H_{j}\leq t\mid G_{j}=n)P(G_{j}=n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tilde{F}^{\alpha}_{n}(t)\left(\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)}{\Lambda(t}\right)\left(1-\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)}{\Lambda(t}\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{n\alpha}E_{\alpha,n\alpha+1}^{n}\left(-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}\right)\left(\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)}{\Lambda(t}\right)\left(1-\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)}{\Lambda(t}\right)^{n-1}\quad(\text{from}~\eqref{eq3})

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{j}(t)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{n\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)}{\Lambda(t)}\right)^{n}E_{\alpha,(n+1)\alpha+1}^{n+1}\left(-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}\right).

Using formula (2.3.1) of
Mathai and Haubold 2008

E^{\delta}_{\beta,\gamma+\alpha}(z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\gamma-1}(1-u)^{\alpha-1}E^{\delta}_{\beta,\gamma}(zu^{\beta})

for
\beta=\alpha
,
\gamma=n\alpha+1
,
\delta=n+1
and
z=-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle P(H_{j}\leq t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{n\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)}{\Lambda(t)}\right)^{n}\int_{0}^{1}u^{n\alpha}(1-u)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,n\alpha+1}^{n+1}\left(-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}u^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{\alpha-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)}{\Lambda(t)}\right)u^{\alpha}\right]^{n}E_{\alpha,n\alpha+1}^{n+1}\left(-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}u^{\alpha}\right)du.

Using formula (2.30) of
Beghin and Orsingher 2010

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\lambda wt^{\alpha})^{n}E_{\alpha,n\alpha+1}^{n+1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})=E_{\alpha,1}(\lambda(w-1)t^{\alpha},\quad|w|\leq 1,t>0,

we have

P(H_{j}\leq t)=\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,1}\left(-\Lambda_{j}(t)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}u^{\alpha}\right)du.

Using formula (2.2.14) of
Mathai and Haubold 2008

\int_{0}^{1}z^{\beta-1}(1-z)^{\sigma-1}E_{\alpha,\beta}(xz^{\alpha})dz=\Gamma(\sigma)E_{\alpha,\sigma+\beta}(x)

for
\sigma=\alpha
,
\beta=1
,
x=-\Lambda_{j}(t)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}
and
z=u
, we have

P(H_{j}\leq t)=\Lambda_{j}(t)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha+1}\left(-\Lambda_{j}(t)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\right).

∎

Moreover for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t>0
, the
n
th arrival time
\widetilde{\tau}^{\alpha}_{n}
and the first passage time
\widetilde{T}^{\alpha}_{n}
for state
n
of a NHGFSP are respectively

P(\widetilde{\tau}^{\alpha}_{n})\leq t)=\sum_{x=n}^{\infty}P(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)=x),\quad P(\widetilde{T}^{\alpha}_{n}>t)=\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}P(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)=x)

where the p.m.f. of NHGFSP is given in Theorem
6.1
.

6.4.
Convergence results

Here we study the asymptotic behaviour of NTFPP and NHGFSP as their parameters become large.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
. For a fixed
t>0
, as
\Lambda(t)\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\frac{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)}{\mathbb{E}(\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t))}\xrightarrow{p}1.

Proof.

Using the triangular inequality, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)}{\mathbb{E}(\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t))}-1\right|\right]\leq 2.

Hence applying the dominated convergence theorem, we obtain

(6.41)

\lim_{\Lambda(t)\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)}{\mathbb{E}(\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t))}-1\right|\right]=\lim_{\Lambda(t)\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{n}{\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}}-1\right|\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{n\alpha}E_{\alpha,n\alpha+1}^{n+1}\left(-(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha}\right).

Note that the generalized Mittag-Leffler function for large
x
behaves as
E^{\delta}_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\sim\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-\delta})
for
|x|>1
(see

Saxena et al. 2004
) so the limit in (
6.41
) is
0
. Thus the random variable
\frac{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)}{\mathbb{E}(\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t))}
converges in mean to 1, which implies
\frac{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)}{\mathbb{E}(\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t))}\xrightarrow{p}1
.
∎

The
r
th order moment of a TFPP is given by
\mathbb{E}[N^{\alpha}(t)]^{r}=\sum_{q=0}^{r}S_{\alpha}(r,q)(\lambda t^{\alpha})^{q}
where
S_{\alpha}(r,q)
is the fractional Sterling number (see Eqs. (32) and (40) of
Laskin 2009
). So the
r
th order moment of a NTFPP is given by
\mathbb{E}[\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)]^{r}=\sum_{q=0}^{r}S_{\alpha}(r,q)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha q})
. Using this expression, the following result can be obtained similarly as in Theorem
6.3
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. For a fixed
t>0
, as
\Lambda(t)\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\frac{[\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)]^{r}}{\mathbb{E}[\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)]^{r}}\xrightarrow{p}1.

Next we discuss a convergence result for NHGFSP.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,t]
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. For
1\leq j\leq k
and fixed
t>0
, as
\Lambda_{j}(t),T_{j}(t)\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\frac{[\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)]^{r}}{[\mathbb{E}(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t))]^{r}}\xrightarrow{p}1.

Proof.

Using Hoppe’s formula and (
6
), the
r
th order moment of the process
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)]^{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{r}}{ds^{r}}E_{\alpha,1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}(\Lambda_{j}(t)(e^{sj}-1)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\right)\Bigg|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{r}\frac{(E_{\alpha,1}(x))^{(n)}\big|_{x=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)(e^{sj}-1)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}}}{n!}A_{r,n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}(\Lambda_{j}(t)(e^{sj}-1)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\right)\Bigg|_{s=0},

where
f^{(n)}
denotes the
n
th derivative of
f
and

\displaystyle A_{r,n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)(e^{sj}-1)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=0}^{n}\binom{n}{h}\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)(e^{sj}-1)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\right)^{n-h}

\displaystyle\times\frac{d^{r}}{ds^{r}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)(e^{sj}-1)(\Lambda(t))^{\alpha-1}\right)^{h}.

After some algebra, we obtain

(6.42)

\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}(t)]^{r}=\sum_{n=0}^{r}\frac{\left(\Lambda(t)\right)^{n\alpha}}{\Gamma(n\alpha+1)}\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{k}=n}\binom{n}{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}\prod_{l=1}^{k}(\Lambda_{l}(t))^{i_{l}}\times\sum_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=r}\binom{r}{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{d^{j_{l}}}{ds^{j_{l}}}(e^{ls}-1)^{i_{l}}\right]\Bigg|_{s=0}.

Using (
6.42
), it can be shown that
\frac{[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)]^{r}}{\mathbb{E}([\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)]^{r})}
converges in mean to
1
as
\Lambda_{j}(t)\rightarrow\infty
similar to Theorem
6.3
. This implies
\frac{[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)]^{r}}{\mathbb{E}([\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)]^{r})}\xrightarrow{p}1
. Similarly it can be shown that
\frac{[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)]^{r}}{\mathbb{E}([\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)]^{r})}\xrightarrow{p}1
as
T_{j}(t)\rightarrow\infty
. In other words,
[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)]^{r}\xrightarrow{p}\mathbb{E}([\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)]^{r})
and
[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)]^{r}\xrightarrow{p}\mathbb{E}([\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)]^{r})
. Hence for
r\in\mathbb{N}
and fixed
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle[\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)]^{r}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r}{j}[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)]^{r-j}[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)]^{j}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{p}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r}{j}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{1}(t)]^{r-j}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\alpha}_{2}(t)]^{j}=[\mathbb{E}(\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t))]^{r}

as
\Lambda_{j}(t),T_{j}(t)\rightarrow\infty
for
1\leq j\leq k
, thereby proving the result.
∎

Proposition
6.2
and Theorem
6.4
show that the processes
\{\tilde{N}^{\alpha}(t)\}^{r}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\widetilde{\mathcalboondox{S}}^{\alpha}(t)\}^{r}_{t\geq 0}
where
r\in\mathbb{N}
exhibit cut-off behaviour at mean times (see
Barrera and Fernandez 2009
) with respect to the relevant parameters, that is, they converge abruptly to equlibrium as the parameters grow large.

7.
Running average processes of GSP and GCP

In this section, we introduce the running average processes of GSP and GCP and study their properties.

7.1.
Running Average of GSP

Let
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a GSP such that
\mathcal{S}(t)=\mathbb{M}_{1}(t)-\mathbb{M}_{2}(t)
where
\{\mathbb{M}_{1}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\mathbb{M}_{2}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
are independent GCPs with intensity parameters
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{k}
respectively.

Definition 7.1
.

We define the running average process of a GSP by taking the time-scaled integral of its path (see
Xia 2018
,
Gupta et al. 2020
) as follows:

\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(s)ds.

Note that
\{\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfies the following differential equation with initial condition
\mathcal{S}_{A}(0)=0
:

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)\right)=\frac{1}{t}\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)-\frac{1}{t^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{A}(s)ds

which shows that it has continuous sample paths of bounded total variation.

Proposition 7.1
.

The characteristic function of the running average process of a GSP is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)}(u)=e^{t\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{(e^{iuj}-1)}{iuj}-1\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}\left(\frac{(1-e^{-iuj})}{iuj}-1\right)\right\}}.

Proof.

Let us denote
K(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(s)ds
. Since
\mathcal{S}(t)_{t\geq 0}
is a Lévy process, using Lemma
2.7
, the characteristic function of
K(t)
is given as

\phi_{K(t)}(u)=e^{t\left(\int_{0}^{1}\log\phi_{\mathcal{S}(1)}(tuz)dz\right)}.

Using (
3.1
), the characteristic function of
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is

\phi_{\mathcal{S}(t)}(u)=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}t\left(\lambda_{j}(e^{iuj}-1)+\mu_{j}(e^{-iuj}-1)\right)\right)

which implies

\int_{0}^{1}\log\phi_{\mathcal{S}(1)}(tuz)dz=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{(e^{ituj}-1)}{ituj}-1\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}\left(\frac{(1-e^{-ituj})}{ituj}-1\right).

Hence the characteristic function of
\{\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is

\phi_{\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)}(u)=\phi_{K(t)/t}(u)=\phi_{K(t)}(u/t)=e^{t\left(\int_{0}^{1}\log\phi_{\mathcal{S}(1)}(tuz)dz\right)}=e^{t\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{(e^{iuj}-1)}{iuj}-1\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}\left(\frac{(1-e^{-iuj})}{iuj}-1\right)\right\}}.

∎

Note that
\phi_{\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)}(u)
has a singularity at
u=0
, which can be removed by defining
\phi_{\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)}(0)=1
.

Proposition 7.2
.

The Lévy measure for a GSP (see
Kataria and Khandakar 2022b
) is given by

\nu_{\mathcal{S}}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\delta_{i}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}\delta_{i}(x)

where
\delta_{i}(.)
is the Dirac measure.

Proof.

The proof follows using the independence of two GCPs used in the definition of GSP (see
Kataria and Khandakar 2022a
and Proposition 7 of
Gupta et al. 2020
).
∎

The following result provides the distribution of the running average process of a GSP.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\{Y(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a compound Poisson process given by

Y(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}X_{i}

where
\{N(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a Poisson process with intensity parameter
(\Lambda+T)t
,
\Lambda=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}
and
T=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}
. Moreover
X_{i}
s are i.i.d. random variables with mixed double uniform distribution function, which are independent of
\{N(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. Then

Y(t)\overset{d}{=}\mathcal{S}_{A}(t).

Proof.

Since
X_{i}
s are i.i.d. random variables with mixed double uniform distribution, their pdf is

\displaystyle f_{X_{1}}(x)=

\displaystyle wf_{U[0,j]}(x)I_{[0,j]}(x)+(1-w)f_{U[-j,0]}(x)I_{[-j,0]}(x).

Let
w=\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda+T}
so
1-w=\frac{T}{\Lambda+T}
. For
-j\leq x\leq j
and
1\leq j\leq k
, we have

\displaystyle f_{X_{1}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda+T}f_{U[0,j]}(x)I_{[0,j]}(x)+\frac{T}{\Lambda+T}f_{U[-j,0]}(x)I_{[-j,0]}(x)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda+T}I_{[0,j]}(x)\frac{1}{j}+\frac{T}{\Lambda+T}I_{[-j,0]}(x)\frac{1}{j}\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Lambda+T}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\frac{1}{j}I_{[0,j]}(x)+\frac{1}{\Lambda+T}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}\frac{1}{j}I_{[-j,0]}(x).

The characteristic function of
X_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{X_{1}}(u)=\int_{0}^{j}e^{iux}f_{X}(x)dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Lambda+T}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{j}\frac{e^{iux}\lambda_{j}}{j}dx+\frac{1}{\Lambda+T}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{-j}^{0}\frac{e^{iux}\mu_{j}}{j}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Lambda+T}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{e^{iuj}-1}{iuj}\right)+\frac{1}{\Lambda+T}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}\left(\frac{1-e^{-iuj}}{iuj}\right).

So the characteristic function of
Y(t)
is

\displaystyle\phi_{Y(t)}(u)=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}X_{i}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left(iu\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}X_{i}\right)\big|N(t)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left(iu\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right)\right)P\left(N(t)=n\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\phi_{X_{1}}(u)\right)^{n}P\left(N(t)=n\right)\,\,=P^{*}\left(\phi_{X_{1}}(u)\right)

where
P^{*}(.)
is the pgf of
\{N(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. Since
N(t)\sim\text{Poisson}\left((\Lambda+T)t\right))
, we have
P^{*}(s)=e^{-(\Lambda+T)t(1-s)}
. Hence

\phi_{Y(t)}(u)=e^{-(\Lambda+T)t\left(1-\phi_{X_{1}}(u)\right)}=e^{-(\Lambda+T)t\left(1-\frac{1}{\Lambda+T}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{e^{iuj}-1}{iuj}\right)-\frac{1}{\Lambda+T}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}\left(\frac{1-e^{-iuj}}{iuj}\right)\right)}\\
=\phi_{\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)}(u),

thereby proving the result.
∎

Corollary 7.1
.

If
k=1
, then the running average process of a GSP and hence its compound Poisson representation reduce to those of a Skellam process. However, if
\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{k}
and
\mu_{1}=\cdots=\mu_{k}
, then the running average process of a GSP and hence its compound Poisson representation reduce to those of a Skellam process of order
k
(see
Gupta et al. 2020
).

The
r^{th}
order moment of
X_{i}
can be calculated using the definition

m_{r}=\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{r}]=(-i)^{r}\frac{d^{r}\phi_{X_{i}}(u)}{du^{r}}

and the Taylor series expansion of the characteristic function
\phi_{X_{1}}(u)
around 0 such that

\frac{(e^{iuj}-1)}{iuj}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(iuj)^{n}}{(n+1)!}\quad\And\quad\frac{(1-e^{-iuj})}{iuj}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-iuj)^{n}}{(n+1)!}.

Using the properties of a compound Poisson process, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[N(t)]\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]=\frac{t}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left(\lambda_{j}-\mu_{j}\right),\quad\mathbb{V}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]=\mathbb{E}[N(t)]\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{2}]=\frac{t}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\lambda_{j}+\mu_{j}\right),

\displaystyle\text{Cov}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(s),\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(s)]\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t-s)]+\mathbb{E}[(\mathcal{S}_{A}(s))^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(s)]\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]=\frac{s}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\lambda_{j}+\mu_{j}\right),~s<t.

The mean and variance of GSP are
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}(t)]=t\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left(\lambda_{j}-\mu_{j}\right)
and
\mathbb{V}[\mathcal{S}(t)]=t\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\lambda_{j}+\mu_{j}\right)
so

\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]}{\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}(t)]}=\frac{1}{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\mathbb{V}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]}{\mathbb{V}[\mathcal{S}(t)]}=\frac{1}{3}.

Note that

\mathbb{V}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]=\frac{t}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\lambda_{j}+\mu_{j}\right)-\frac{t}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\left(\lambda_{j}-\mu_{j}\right)=t\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(2j^{2}-3j)\lambda_{j}+(2j^{2}+3j)\mu_{j}}{6}.

For
k=1
, we have
\mathbb{V}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]>0(<0)
if
5\mu_{1}>\lambda_{1}(5\mu_{1}<\lambda_{1})
. Accordingly the running average process of a Poisson process is over-dispersed (under-dispersed). Next for
k>1
with
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(2j^{2}+3j\right)\mu_{j}>\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(3j-2j^{2}\right)\lambda_{j}
, we have
\mathbb{V}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]>0
which implies the running average process of a GSP is over-dispersed. Otherwise, it is under-dispersed. The next result shows the dependence structure of a GSP.

Proposition 7.3
.

The running average process of a GSP has the LRD property.

Proof.

For
s<t
, we have

\displaystyle\text{Corr}\left(\mathcal{S}_{A}(s),\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)\right)=\frac{\text{Cov}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(s),\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]}{\sqrt{\mathbb{V}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(s)]}\sqrt{\mathbb{V}[\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)]}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{s}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\lambda_{j}+\mu_{j}\right)}{\sqrt{\frac{s}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\lambda_{j}+\mu_{j}\right)}\sqrt{\frac{t}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\left(\lambda_{j}+\mu_{j}\right)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{s}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\,=C(s)t^{-\theta},

where
C(s)=\sqrt{s}
and
\theta=\frac{1}{2}<1
. Hence
\{\mathcal{S}_{A}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
has the LRD property.
∎

Remark 7.1
.

As NGSP and NGFSP are not Lévy processes due to non-stationary state probabilities and dependent increments, we can not define their Lévy measures. Moreover Lemma
2.7
can’t be used to find the distributions of their running average processes.

7.2.
Running Average of GCP

Let
\{\mathbb{M}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a GCP with intensity parameters
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
.
Its running average process
\{\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
can be defined by taking the time-scaled integral of its path similar to that of a GSP. It follows that
\{\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
has continuous sample paths of bounded total variation.

Proposition 7.4
.

The characteristic function of the running average process of a GCP is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)}(u)=e^{t\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{(e^{iuj}-1)}{iuj}-1\right)}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Proposition
7.1
and hence omitted.
∎

We set
\phi_{\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)}(0)=1
to remove the singularity of
\phi_{\mathbb{M}_{A}}(t)
at
u=0
. The next result shows the distribution of the running average process of a GCP.

Theorem 7.2
.

The running average process of GCP has a compound Poisson representation given by

\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)\overset{d}{=}\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}X_{i},

where
X_{i}
s are independent and identically distributed random variables, independent of a Poisson process
\{N(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
with parameter
\Lambda t
and
\Lambda=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}
.
Moreover the random variable
X_{i}
has the pdf

f_{X_{i}}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{V_{j}}(x)f_{U_{j}}(x),

where

\displaystyle p_{V_{j}}(x)=P(V_{j}=x)=\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\Lambda};\quad j=1,2,...,k\quad\text{and}\quad U_{j}\sim\text{Uniform}[0,j];\quad 0\leq x\leq j.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
7.1
and hence omitted.
∎

Corollary 7.2
.

If
k=1
, then the running average process of a GCP and hence its compound Poisson representation reduce to those of a standard Poisson process (see
Xia 2018
), and to those of a Poisson process of order
k
(see
Gupta et al. 2020
) if
\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{k}
.

Remark 7.2
.

In Theorem
7.2
,
V_{j}
s have the same distribution as that of the random variables
X_{i}
s in the compound Poisson representation of GCP (see
Di Crescenzo et al. 2016
).

Using the properties of a compound Poisson process, it can be shown that

\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)]=\frac{t}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\lambda_{j},\quad\mathbb{V}[\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)]=\frac{t}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\lambda_{j},\quad\text{Cov}[\mathbb{M}_{A}(s),\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)]=\frac{s}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\lambda_{j}\,,\qquad s<t.

The mean and variance of GCP are
\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{M}(t)]=t\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\lambda_{j}
and
\mathbb{V}[\mathbb{M}(t)]=t\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\lambda_{j}
so

\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)]}{\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{M}(t)]}=\frac{1}{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\mathbb{V}[\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)]}{\mathbb{V}[\mathbb{M}(t)]}=\frac{1}{3}.

Note that

\mathbb{V}[\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)]=\frac{t}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{2}\lambda_{j}-\frac{t}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j\lambda_{j}=t\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{j^{2}}{3}-\frac{j}{2}\right).

For
k=1
, we have
\mathbb{V}[\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)]<0
which implies that the running average process of a Poisson process is always under-dispersed. However for
k>1
,
\mathbb{V}[\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{M}_{A}(t)]>0(<0)
if
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(3j^{2}-2j)>0(<0)
. So the running average process of a GCP may be over-dispersed or under-dispersed. The next result about the dependence structure of a GCP follows from Proposition
7.3
.

Proposition 7.5
.

The running average process of a GCP has the LRD property.

Finally, we discuss the importance of running average processes in the following remark.

Remark 7.3
.

Note that the Riemann integral in Lemma
2.7
can be restated as
Y(t)=\int_{0}^{t}X(s)ds=\int_{a}^{a+t}X(s-a)ds
, that is, the process
\{X(s)\}_{s\geq 0}
is shifted back in time by ‘
a
’ units for the interval
(a,a+t)
. This implies that we can examine the running average of the original process for any time interval (of same length) by time shifting. Also the microscopic structure of the process during various time intervals is revealed by studying the corresponding running averages. For example, if we are modeling share price data for a particular day, then we can study its mean behavior during various time periods of trading activity, which differ in practice.

8.
Simulation

In this section, we present algorithms and plots of simulated sample paths for GFSP, NGFSP and NHGFSP along with the p.m.f. for NGSP. We need to simulate two GFCPs, two NGFCPs and two NHGFCPs, and then take their differences to simulate GFSP, NGFSP and NHGFSP respectively.

For simulation of GFCP, we use its compound Poisson representation given by
Di Crescenzo et al. 2016
:

\mathbb{M}^{\alpha}(t)\overset{d}{=}\sum_{i=1}^{N^{\alpha}(t)}X_{i}

where
X_{i}
s are i.i.d. random variables such that
P\{X_{1}=j\}=\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\Lambda}

and
N^{\alpha}(t)
is an independent Fractional Poisson process with rate
\Lambda=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}
. For simulation of NGFCP, we first modify the compound Poisson representation of GCP as

\mathbb{M}(t)\overset{d}{=}\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}X_{i}

where
X_{i}
s are i.i.d. random variables such that
P\{X_{1}=j\}=\frac{\Lambda_{j}(t)}{\Lambda(t)}

and
N(t)
is an independent Poisson process with parameter
\Lambda(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Lambda_{j}(t)
. Without loss of generality, we take
\Lambda_{j}(t)=(a_{j}/b_{j})(e^{b_{j}t}-1)+\mu_{j}(t)
(Gompertz Makeham rate function) where
a_{j},b_{j},\mu_{j}>0
. Then we time change the GCP by an independent inverse stable subordinator to obtain NGFCP. For simulation of NHGFCP, see (
6.36
). First we provide the algorithm for simulating sample path of a GFSP.

9.
Application

In this section, we discuss the application of NGFSP in high-frequency financial data analysis to model complex changing patterns in high-frequency trade executions during rapid market movements.

Previous studies on this subject include various models based on the difference of two point processes.
Barndorff-Nielsen et al. 2012
and
Carr 2011
considered the difference of Poisson processes (also known as Skellam process) to model financial transactions whereas
Bacry et al. 2013a
,
Bacry et al. 2013b
) evaluated the use of Hawkes processes. The main drawback of these models is their exponential inter-arrival times (i.e. time between trades), which contradict empirical evidence. To overcome this drawback,
Kerss et al. 2014
used the fractional Skellam process of type I which is the difference between two time fractional Poisson processes. The main advantage of this process is the Mittag-Leffler inter-arrival times, which are empirically observed to have long memory. Additionally, see
Barndorff-Nielsen et al. 2012
for the application of integer-valued Lévy processes in financial econometrics, where they have used the difference between two stable subordinators.

Kerss et al. 2014
reviewed three-month transaction records of a forward asset (called future) containing 5,465,779 timestamped transactions recorded over market opening hours. They defined the bid-price function on the basis of the price of the forward asset at time
t
denoted by
F(t)
. The bid-ask price spread was fixed to a single tick of 0.0001.
Kerss et al. 2014
defined
F(t)^{bid}
as follows:

(9.44)

F(t)^{bid}=\begin{cases}F(t-1)^{bid}\qquad\text{if}\,\,F(t-1)^{bid}\leq F(t)\leq F(t-1)^{ask}\\
F(t)\quad\qquad\qquad\text{if}\,\,F(t)<F(t-1)^{bid}\\
F(t)-0.0001\quad\text{if}\,\,F(t)>F(t-1)^{ask}.\end{cases}

The data was filtered by deleting all the entries where the bid price had not changed from the previous bid price (case 1 of (
9.44
)), i.e., no up or down jump had occurred, leaving 682,550 records. The price data was modeled using the fractional Skellam process of type I, as shown in the first graph in Figure 2 of
Kerss et al. 2014
, which shows both the original and the filtered data. Up jump data (the upward changes in prices as defined by case 3 of (
9.44
)) and the down jump data (the downward changes in prices as defined by case 2 of (
9.44
)) can be modeled using the time fractional Poisson process as shown in the second graph in Figure 2 of
Kerss et al. 2014
. The up and down jump time series was constructed by removing all trades with negative jumps as well as removing all the duplicate time stamps, leaving 253,092 and 281,833 observations for up and down jumps, respectively. Finally, Figure 3 of
Kerss et al. 2014
showed that the Mittag-Leffler distribution provides a better fit than the exponential distribution for inter-arrival times (time between trades).

Next, we explain how our proposed model NGFSP better fits both the first and second graphs in Figure 2 of
Kerss et al. 2014
. It is observed from the original data that almost ninety-eight per cent of jumps are of single tick magnitude. However, to make the model more sensible, it needs to be extended to allow jumps greater than one tick, thereby accounting for the remaining data. In practice, prices can fluctuate in both directions at greater magnitudes based on a number of different reasons. Such price changes should be incorporated into a model by allowing larger jump sizes with varying intensities. It has also been commonly observed that fluctuations in asset prices are not always consistent. The market conditions or volatility may vary based on the time of day or on various days of the month, and the fluctuations may follow specific patterns. For example, volatility is usually higher during the opening or closing hours on a given trading day or on days affected by some catastrophic news. To address this non-homogeneity in price movement, a model should accommodate various possible non-homogeneous rates rather than just homogeneous rates.

Our proposed model allows jumps of different magnitudes with different intensities and also incorporates different non-homogeneous rate functions. This makes NGFSP well-suited to analyze high-frequency financial data. Moreover note that NGFCP has Mittag-Leffler inter-arrival times which provide a better fit than exponentially distributed inter-arrival times for such data. In summary, NGFCP may be used to model the up and down jump processes while NGFSP may be used to model the original stock price data.

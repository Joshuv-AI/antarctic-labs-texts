Positivity properties of divisors on Toric Vector Bundles
Courtney George, Christopher Manon

Abstract
. We use presentations of the Cox rings of projectivized toric vector bundles and elements of matroid theory to compute Newton-Okounkov bodies, effective cones, and Nef cones of these spaces. As an application we analyze the Fano property, and establish Fujita’s freeness and ampleness conjectures for several classes of projectivized toric vector bundles.



1.
Introduction

Let
N\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
be a lattice with dual lattice
M=\textup{Hom}(N,{\mathbb{Z}})
, and let
T
denote the algebraic torus with cocharacter lattice
N
. Let
\Sigma\subset N\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
be a complete, polyhedral fan, and let
X(\Sigma)
denote the corresponding complete toric variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
. We assume throughout that
X(\Sigma)
is a projective variety. A
toric vector bundle

\pi:\mathcal{E}\to X(\Sigma)
is a vector bundle equipped with a linear action by
T
which intertwines through
\pi
with the
T
action on
X(\Sigma)
.

Toric vector bundles are classified by Kaneyama in
[
Kan75
]
, and by Klyachko in
[
Kly89
]
. The Klyachko data of toric vector bundle consists of a collection
F^{\rho}
of compatible integral filtrations in the identity fiber
\pi^{-1}Id=E
of
\mathcal{E}
, one for each ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
. In this way, toric vector bundles admit a combinatorial classification, much like toric varieties. In addition to the customary polyhedral data that comes with
X(\Sigma)
, we have the linear combinatorics of matroids data living in the arrangement of Klyachko’s spaces. This theme repeats for other invariants of toric vector bundles as well, for example the global section space
H^{0}(X(\Sigma),\mathcal{E})
can be read off the so-called
parliament of polytopes
of DiRocco, Jabbusch, and Smith
[
DRJS18
]
, which can be viewed as a labelling of a matroid by polyhedra.

The projectivization
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
of a toric vector bundle is a smooth, projective variety with a free class group. The Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(X(\Sigma))
(see
[
ADHL15
]
) of a toric variety is well-known to be a polynomial ring in
n=|\Sigma(1)|
variables. In contrast, the Cox rings of projectivized toric vector bundles are a much richer class of objects. For example, by results of González, Hering, Payne, and Süß, both the Cox ring of
\bar{M}_{0,n}
and the additive invariant rings of Nagata can be realized as Cox rings of certain projectivized toric vector bundles. In particular, it is possible for
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
to be infinitely generated. In the case that
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
is finitely generated,
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is called a
Mori dream space
(see
[
HK00
]
).

Cox rings of toric vector bundles are shown in
[
KM
]
to be
multi-Rees algebras
associated to a collection filtrations on a polynomial ring coming from a tropicalized linear space. In
[
KM
]
algebraic and combinatorial criteria for this type of ring to finitely generated, along with an algorithm to construct a generating set, are given. In this paper we use these descriptions of
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
, along with aspects of associated matroids, to study the convex geometry associated to toric vector bundles and their projectivizations.

1.1.
Newton-Okounkov bodies

Thoughout the paper we focus on projectivized toric vector bundles
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
whose Cox ring
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
is generated in minimal degree (
Sym
-degree
1
, see Section
2
), although we do examples (Example
3.8
) where higher degree generators appear.

In Section
2
we recall work of Kaveh and the second author
[
KM
]
, where it is shown that a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
can be constructed from a choice of linear ideal
L\subset{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
and an
n\times m
matrix
D
called a
diagram
. The rows of the diagram
D
are points in the tropical variety
\textup{Trop}(L)
, and the columns
D_{j}
each determine a divisor class
\mathbf{d}_{j}\in\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))
. The linear ideal determines a (representable) matroid
\mathcal{M}(L)
on the elements
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}
.

We use tropical techniques of
[
KM19
]
to construct global Newton-Okounkov bodies of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
, along with Newton-Okounkov bodies of complete linear series on
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
.
Before we state our main result, we introduce some terminology. The tropical variety
\textup{Trop}(L)
can be identified with the Bergman fan of of
\mathcal{M}(L)
. A maximal face
K\subset\textup{Trop}(L)
corresponds to a flag
F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{r}
of flats in
\mathcal{M}(L)
, we let
E_{K}
be the
r\times m
matrix with rows the indicator vectors of these flats. Finally, we let:

M=\begin{bmatrix}D&-I\\
E_{K}&0\end{bmatrix}.

For each divisor class
(\alpha,\beta)\in\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})\cong\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))\times{\mathbb{Z}}
we define a certain polytope
P_{\alpha,\beta}\subset{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}^{n+m}
, which we show is naturally a Cayley polytope in nice cases, see Section
3
. Finally, a cone in a tropical variety is said to be a
prime cone
if its associated initial ideal is a prime ideal (see
[
KM19
]
). The following is our main theorem on Newton-Okounkov bodies of projectivized toric vector bundles.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a toric vector bundle with
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
generated in
Sym
-degree 1, and suppose that there is a maximal flag of flats
F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{r}
in the matroid
\mathcal{M}(L)
such that the inverse image of the corresponding maximal face of the Bergman fan
\textup{Trop}(L)
is a prime cone of
\textup{Trop}(\mathcal{I})
, then:

(1)

\Delta=M\circ{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}^{m+n}
is a global Newton-Okounkov body of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
,

(2)

\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}=M\circ P_{\alpha,\beta}
is the Newton-Okounkov body of
(\alpha,\beta)\in\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
.

We describe several large families of projectivized toric vector bundles which satisfy the conditions of Theorem
1.1
. As two applications, we construct integral polytopes whose lattice points enumerate the global sections of
\mathcal{E}
itself, and we construct the Newton-Okounkov bodies of any tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}X(\Sigma)
of a smooth toric variety
X(\Sigma)
.

1.2.
The Nef Cone

Next we describe the Nef cone of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
in terms of the flats of the
initial matroids

\mathcal{M}(\textup{in}_{\sigma}L)
(see Section
4
). For each facet
\sigma\in\Sigma
there is an associated initial ideal
\textup{in}_{\sigma}L\subset{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
. We let
F
denote a maximal non-trivial flat of the matroid
\mathcal{M}(\textup{in}_{\sigma}L)
. For each pair
\sigma\in\Sigma
,
F\subset\mathcal{M}(\textup{in}_{\sigma}L)
we define a certain point
p_{\sigma,F}\in{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
, with an associated explicit monoid
S_{\sigma,F}\subset\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
and cone
C_{\sigma,F}\subset\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a toric vector bundle with
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
generated in
Sym
-degree 1, then

\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})=\bigcap_{\sigma,F}S_{\sigma,F},

\textup{Nef}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})=\bigcap_{\sigma,F}C_{\sigma,F}.

We make use Theorem
1.2
by imposing conditions on
\mathcal{M}(L)
and the
\mathcal{M}(\textup{in}_{\sigma}L)
which simplify the structure of
S_{\sigma,F}
and
C_{\sigma,F}
. The next corollary summarizes results we obtain for various classes of toric vector bundles.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a toric vector bundle with
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
generated in
Sym
-degree 1, then:

(1)

If
\mathcal{E}
is monomial, then any Nef class is basepoint-free,

(2)

If
\mathcal{E}
is uniform or sparse over
{\mathbb{P}}^{\underline{n}}
, then any Nef class is basepoint-free, and any ample class is very ample.

For the definitions of monomial bundles, sparse bundles, and uniform bundles see Section
2
. The class of sparse bundles includes all rank
2
vector bundles and tangent bundles of smooth toric varieties. Uniform bundles are those bundles given by
(L,D)
where
L
is a very general linear ideal.

Corollary
1.3
implies that several large classes of bundles that we study satisfy one of Fujita’s freeness and ampleness conjectures:

Conjecture 1.4
.

Let
X
be smooth of dimension
n
and let
A\in\textup{CL}(X)
be ample, then

(1)

for
m\geq n+1
,
K_{X}+mA
is globally generated,

(2)

for
m\geq n+2
,
K_{X}+mA
is very ample.

Mori’s cone theorem (
[
Laz04
, Theorem 1.5.33]
) shows that if
X
and
A
are as in
1.4
, then
K_{X}+mA
is Nef if
m\geq n+1
and ample if
m\geq n+2
(see
[
Laz04
, Section 10.4]
). Therefore, to prove Fujita’s freeness and ampleness conjectures it is sufficient to show that Nef implies globally generated and ample implies very ample on
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
. Smooth toric varieties can be shown to satisfy Fujita’s conjectures along these lines. Our strategy for establishing these conjectures for various projectivized toric vector bundles also follows this argument; however, we give an example of a rank
2
projectivized toric vector bundle over a surface where Nef does not imply basepoint-free.

Fujita’s conjectures are true for smooth toric varieties, and the freeness conjecture has been shown for all toric varieties by Fujino
[
Fuj
]
. Payne
[
Pay06
]
shows the ampleness conjecture for toric varieties with Gorenstein singularities. For curves, both conjectures follow from Riemann-Roch, and both conjectures hold for surfaces by a result of Reider
[
Rei
]
. The freeness conjecture has also been proved for smooth projective varieties up to dimension
5

[
EL
,
Kaw
,
YZ20
]
. In the toric vector bundle case, if the rank of
\mathcal{E}
is
2
, then
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is known to be a complexity-
1

T
-variety and satisfies Fujita’s freeness conjecture by a result of Altmann and Ilten
[
AI
]
. In that same paper, Altmann and Ilten also suggest that the Fujita conjectures could be studied for projectivized toric vector bundles. We also mention here the work of Fahrner
[
Fah
]
, on algorithms to test if Mori dream spaces satisfy Fujita’s freeness conjecture. Our treatment of
\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
is very much in line with Fahrner’s work.

1.3.
Fano bundles, non-Fano bundles

In many of the cases we consider the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
is presented as a complete intersection, so we are able to explicitly construct the anticanonical class
-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}}
, along with the Nef cone via Theorem
1.2
. We focus on a large class of toric vector bundles, the
Kaneyama bundles
in Section
5
, and show that these bundles are almost never Fano.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Kaneyama toric vector bundle over
X(\Sigma)
with diagram the diagonal matrix with entries
a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}
, then:

(1)

If
-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}}
is Nef, then
X(\Sigma)={\mathbb{P}}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n_{\ell}}
. Moreover, if
\ell>1
then
-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}}
is Nef if and only if
a_{i}=1
or
0
for all
i
. If
\ell=1
then
-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}}
is Nef if and only if
\sum_{i=2}^{n}(a_{i}-a_{1})\leq(n-a_{1})
,

(2)

-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}}
is ample if and only if
X(\Sigma)={\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
and
\sum_{i=2}^{n}(a_{i}-a_{1})<(n-a_{1})
.

Curiously, the class of Kaneyama bundles includes the tangent bundles of smooth toric varieties. As a corollary to Theorem
1.5
we show that the only smooth toric variety with an ample tangent bundle is projective space (Remark
5.7
). This is a toric special case of a theorem due to Mori in the general case
[
Mor
]
, proving a conjecture of Hartshorne
[
Har
]
. See
[
Wu
]
for a different combinatorial proof that the only smooth toric variety with ample tangent bundle is projective space. We also show that the only smooth toric variety with a Nef tangent bundle is a product of projective spaces.

2.
Background on Toric Vector Bundles

In this section we recall the classification of toric vector bundles in
[
KM
]
, as well as the description of the Cox rings of projectivized toric vector bundles. For background on toric varieties we direct the reader to the book of Cox, Little, and Schenck
[
CLS11
]
.

Let
\Sigma
be a rational polyhedral fan in
N\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
, and let
X(\Sigma)
denote the corresponding normal toric variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
. We always assume that
\Sigma
is a smooth fan. We let
\Sigma(k)
be the set of faces of dimension
k
in
\Sigma
. The most important of these sets for our purposes is the set of rays
\Sigma(1)
. We let
n=|\Sigma(1)|
. For a maximal face
\sigma\in\Sigma(d)
,
q_{\sigma}\in X(\Sigma)
denotes the corresponding
T
-fixed point of
X(\Sigma)
.

Let
\textup{Pic}_{T}(X(\Sigma))
denote the
T
-linearized line bundles on
X(\Sigma)
. It is well-known that an element of
\textup{Pic}_{T}(X(\Sigma))
corresponds to an integral piecewise-linear function
\psi:|\Sigma|\to{\mathbb{R}}
, and in turn that
\psi
is determined by its values
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})
on the ray generators
u_{1},\ldots u_{n}
of the fan
\Sigma
. Accordingly,
\textup{Pic}_{T}(X(\Sigma))
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Recall that two elements
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}),(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
are
T
-linearizations of the same underlying line bundle if and only if their difference
(r_{1}-s_{1},\ldots,r_{n}-s_{n})
is of the form
(\langle u_{1},m\rangle,\ldots,\langle u_{n},m\rangle)
for some character
m\in M
. This leads to the exact sequence:

0\to M\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\to\textup{Pic}(X(\Sigma))\to 0.

The toric variety
X(\Sigma)
is smooth, so we also have that
\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))\cong\textup{Pic}(X(\Sigma))
. Under this isomorphism, the
T
-linearized line bundle
\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r})
corresponds to the
T
-divisor class
\sum r_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}
, where
\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))
is the divisor class of the prime divisor corresponding to the ray
\rho_{i}\in\Sigma(1)
.

2.1.
A tropical characterization of toric vector bundles

The construction in
[
KM
, Section 4]
associates a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
of rank
r
over
X(\Sigma)
to a linear ideal
L\subset{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
and an
n\times m
integral matrix
D
. The matrix
D
is called the
diagram
of
\mathcal{E}
. We let
D_{j}
denote the
j
-th column of
D
. The rows of
D
are in bijection with the rays of
\Sigma
; we let
w_{i}
denote the row associated to
\rho_{i}\in\Sigma(1)
. These data must satisfy compatibility conditions:

(1)

The polynomial ring
{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]/L
has dimension
r
,

(2)

Each
w_{i}
is a point in the tropicalized linear space
\textup{Trop}(L)\subset{\mathbb{Q}}^{m}
,

(3)

For any face
\sigma\in\Sigma
the rows
w_{i}
for
\rho_{i}\in\sigma(1)
must all belong to a common
apartment

A_{\mathbb{B}}\subset\textup{Trop}(L)
.

Apartments
A_{\mathbb{B}}\subset\textup{Trop}(L)
are distinguished polyhedral subcomplexes of
\textup{Trop}(L)
. In what follows let
\mathcal{M}(L)
denote the matroid defined by
L
on the set
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
. In particular, a subset
I\subset\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
is declared independent when no elements of
L
are supported on
I
. There is an apartment
A_{\mathbb{B}}
for each basis
\mathbb{B}\subset\mathcal{M}(L)
, in particular,
A_{\mathbb{B}}
is the set of
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})\in\textup{Trop}(L)
, where
v_{j}
is equal to the minimum of the
v_{k}
where
y_{k}
appears in the
\mathbb{B}
-expression for
y_{j}
. It is straightforward to show that the apartments cover
\textup{Trop}(L)
and that each
A_{\mathbb{B}}
is piecewise-linear isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Q}}^{r}
.

Let
\textup{GF}(L)
denote the
Gröbner fan
of
L
, see
[
Stu96
,
CLO05
]
. As
L
is linear, the fan
\textup{GF}(L)
coincides with the inner normal fan of the
matroid polytope
of the matroid
\mathcal{M}(L)
. In particular,
\textup{GF}(L)
is a complete fan whose maximal faces correspond to bases in
\mathcal{M}(L)
. The tropical variety
\textup{Trop}(L)
coincides with the
Bergman fan
of
\mathcal{M}(L)
, and is naturally identified with the support of a subfan of
\textup{GF}(L)
. The apartment
A_{\mathbb{B}}\subset\textup{Trop}(L)
is precisely the intersection of
\textup{Trop}(L)
with the maximal face of
\textup{GF}(L)
associated to
\mathbb{B}
.

Let
\sigma\in\Sigma(d)
be a maximal face, and let
\textup{in}_{\sigma}(L)
be the initial ideal corresponding to the minimal face of the Gröbner fan of
L
containing the rows of
D
which come from
\sigma(1)
. We let
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
denote the fiber of
\mathcal{E}
over the
T
-fixed point
q_{\sigma}
. We have
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}=\textup{Proj}({\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]/\textup{in}_{\sigma}(L))
. In particular, any standard basis
y_{i_{1}},\ldots,y_{i_{r}}
of
\textup{in}_{\sigma}(L)
forms a coordinate system for
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
.

The set of diagrams
D\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{m\times n}
which satisfy
(2)
and
(3)
above is denoted
\Delta(\Sigma,L)
. Observe that
\Delta(\Sigma,L)
is a rational polyhedral fan. By
[
KM
, Theorem 1.4]
, any integral point
D\in\Delta(\Sigma,L)
corresponds to a toric vector bundle over
X(\Sigma)
, and moreover any toric vector bundle can be obtained from some pair
(L,D)
. However, a fixed toric vector bundle can be realized by many distinct
(L,D)
.

Given
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
and
D\in\Delta(\Sigma,L)
we obtain a new point
D\hat{\otimes}\mathbf{r}\in\Delta(\Sigma,L)
by adding
r_{i}
to all entries in
w_{i}
. This operation defines an action of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
on
\Delta(\Sigma,L)
. When
\mathbf{r}
is integral there is a corresponding
T
-linearized line bundle
\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r})
on
X(\Sigma)
, and the diagram
D\hat{\otimes}\mathbf{r}
corresponds to the bundle
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r})
. Tensoring with a line bundle does not change the projectivization of a vector bundle. For this reason, when we are dealing with a projectivized toric vector bundle, we can assume without loss of generality that all entries of
D
are non-negative, with at least one
0
entry in each row. Accordingly, we call such a diagram
D

non-negative
, and we refer to the
non-negative form
of a diagram
D
. Equivalently, we can work with the fan
\Delta(\Sigma,L)/{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}\subset{\mathbb{Q}}^{n\times m}/{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
. It is straightforward to show that if
L\subseteq L^{\prime}
then
\Delta(\Sigma,L^{\prime})\subseteq\Delta(\Sigma,L)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
L\subseteq L^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
be linear ideals, and suppose that
D\in\Delta(\Sigma,L^{\prime})\subset\Delta(\Sigma,L)
, and let
\mathcal{E}
,
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
denote the the toric vector bundles on
X(\Sigma)
corresponding to
L,L^{\prime}
respectively, then there is a surjection of toric vector bundles
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The existence of the map
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
follows from
[
KM
, Section 3]
. It can be checked that this map is surjection by passing to the maps
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}\to\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
defined on the torus fixed point fibers and noting that
\textup{in}_{\sigma}L\subseteq\textup{in}_{\sigma}L^{\prime}
by assumption.
∎

We finish this section by explaining the connection between the pair
(L,D)
defining a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
and the Klyachko data
[
Kly89
]
of
\mathcal{E}
. Let
E
denote the fiber of
\mathcal{E}
over the identity of the torus
T
, viewed as an open subvariety of
X(\Sigma)
, for a ray
\rho_{i}\in\Sigma(1)
let
u_{i}
denote the integral ray generator. By Klyachko’s result, a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
corresponds to the following data:

(1)

for every ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
a decreasing integral filtration:

E\supseteq\cdots\supseteq F^{\rho}_{r}\supseteq F^{\rho}_{r+1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq 0.

(2)

for every maximal face
\sigma\in\Sigma
a splitting labelled by characters:
E=\bigoplus_{m\in M}L_{m}
,

(3)

such that if
\rho_{i}\subset\sigma
we have
F^{\rho_{i}}_{r}=\bigoplus_{\langle u_{i},m\rangle\geq r}L_{m}
.

From the perspective of the data
(L,D)
,
E
is the first graded component of the quotient ring
{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]/L
, and each space
F^{\rho_{i}}_{r}
is the flat of the matroid
\mathcal{M}(L)
defined by those
y_{j}
with
D_{ij}\geq r
. The existence of the splitting in
(2)
above is then the condition that requires the rows
w_{i}
for
i\in\sigma(1)
to live in a common apartment for every face
\sigma\in\Sigma
.

2.2.
The Cox ring of a projectivized toric vector bundle

The Cox ring of a toric vector bundle is described in
[
HMP10
]
,
[
Gon12
]
,
[
GHPS12
]
,
[
Nod
]
, and
[
KM19
]
. We assume that
X(\Sigma)
is smooth so that
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is smooth, and
\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})\cong\textup{Pic}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})\cong\textup{Pic}(X(\Sigma))\times{\mathbb{Z}}
. The line bundles in the
{\mathbb{Z}}
component of this splitting correspond to the Serre sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{E}}(\ell)
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
which emerge from regarding
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
as
\textup{Proj}_{X(\Sigma)}(\bigoplus\textup{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathcal{E}))
. The
\textup{Pic}(X(\Sigma))
component of this splitting comes from pullbacks
\pi^{*}\mathcal{L}
of line bundles on the base:
\mathcal{L}\in\textup{Pic}(X(\Sigma))
. Then, any line bundle on
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is of the form
\pi^{*}\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{E}}(\ell)
, and by the projection formula (see
[
Har13
, Chapter 2, Exercise 7.9]
, and
[
HMP10
]
) we have
H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E},\pi^{*}\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{E}}(\ell))\cong H^{0}(X(\Sigma),\mathcal{L}\otimes\textup{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathcal{E}))
. This leads to the following expression for the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
:

\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})=\bigoplus_{\mathcal{L}\in\textup{Pic}(X(\Sigma)),\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}H^{0}(X(\Sigma),\mathcal{L}\otimes\textup{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathcal{E})).

We obtain a finer grading of
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
by the

\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}H_{T}^{0}(X(\Sigma),\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r})\otimes\textup{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathcal{E})).

The second expression has additional useful features. The section spaces

admit a combinatorial description by their
parliament of polytopes

[
DRJS18
]
. Moreover, these spaces are the components of a multi parameter filtration of the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{]}/L\cong\textup{Sym}(E)
defined by the data of the diagram
D
. For a polynomial
p\in{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
and
w\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
, the
w
-weight of
p(\bar{y})=\sum C_{\alpha}y^{\alpha}
is defined to be
\min\{\langle w,\alpha\rangle\mid C_{\alpha}\neq 0\}
. Now let
(L,D)
define the toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
. For a row
w
in
D
we define
F^{w}_{r}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]/L\cong\textup{Sym}(E)
to be the set of elements

such that there exists a polynomial
p\in{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
which maps to
f
in the quotient, with
w
-weight greater than or equal to
r
. Let
F^{w}_{r}(\ell)
denote the intersection of this space with the elements of
{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]/L
of homogeneous degree
\ell
. The following is shown in
[
KM
, Section 5]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For
\mathbf{r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have:

H_{T}^{0}(X(\Sigma),\mathcal{O}(-\mathbf{r})\otimes\textup{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathcal{E}))=\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq n}F^{w_{i}}_{r_{i}}(\ell).

If the requirement that
\Sigma
be complete is relaxed, then the apartment conditions defining
\Delta(\Sigma,L)
can be essentially omitted by deleting all higher codimension torus orbits. Alternatively, if
D
is any point on
\textup{Trop}(L)^{n}
and
\Sigma
defines a toric surface, one can always enlarge the set of generators of
\textup{Sym}(E)
to find

so that the associated extended diagram
D^{\prime}\in\Delta(\Sigma,L^{\prime})
defines the same set of filtrations on
\textup{Sym}(E)
. As a consequence, we see that the Cox rings of projectivized toric vector bundles are precisely the class of
multi-Rees algebras
defined by configurations of integral points on a tropicalized linear space
\textup{Trop}(L)
.

The second author and Kaveh define an algorithm
[
KM
, Algorithm 5.6]
which constructs a finite generating set of
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
, provided one exists. This is an adaptation of
[
KM19
, Algorithm 1.18]
, which constructs a finite Khovanskii basis for an algebra quasivaluation. This algorithm can be implemented in symbolic algebra software like Macaulay2
[
GS
]
.

Let

Now we fix some notation for special classes in

We let

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}

with

Every bundle
\mathcal{E}
with

has a distinguished embedding into a split toric vector bundle. Let

be defined by the pair

corresponding to

Proposition 2.6
.

There is a surjection of toric vector bundles:
\mathcal{V}_{D}\to\mathcal{E}
, and a corresponding neat embedding
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}\to{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{V}_{D}

Proof.

The surjection

, and therefore the embedding

. By definition, the Serre sheaf

\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})

∎

Proposition
2.6
implies that
\textup{Eff}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})\cong\textup{Eff}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{V}_{D})

For

,

defines an integral point of the
\beta
-th Minkowski sum of the standard
m-1
simplex, and

maps to
\sum b_{j}\beta_{j}+a
under the map
{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}\to\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

The integral points in
P_{\alpha,\beta}
coincide with a basis of
H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{V}_{D},\mathcal{O}(\alpha,\beta))
.

By considering a slight change of coordinates we show that
P_{\alpha,\beta}
is always a
generalized Cayley polytope
. Pick a section to the surjection
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\to\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))
; this amounts to choosing a
T

X(\Sigma)
in a way which is compatible with addition in
\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))
. Such a section determines a splitting
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\cong M\oplus\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))
. Accordingly, each divisor class
\mathbf{d}\in\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))
is assigned a poltyope
\Delta(\mathbf{d})
; we have
\Delta(\mathbf{d}_{1}+\mathbf{d}_{2})=\Delta(\mathbf{d}_{1})+\Delta(\mathbf{d}_{2})
, and the rational points in

are precisely the image of those

which map to
\mathbf{d}
under the projection

. Let
\psi:{\mathbb{Q}}^{n+m}\to{\mathbb{Q}}^{m}\times M\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
be the projection map induced from this splitting.

Proposition 2.8
.

Assume that
\beta_{j}\mathbf{d}_{j}+\alpha\in\textup{Eff}(X(\sigma))
for all
1\leq j\leq m
. The polytope
P_{\alpha,\beta}
is mapped isomorphically onto
\psi\circ P_{\alpha,\beta}
. Moreover,
\psi\circ P_{\alpha,\beta}
is the generalized Cayley polytope obtained by placing
\Delta(\beta\mathbf{d}_{j}+\alpha)
above
\beta e_{j}\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{m}
.

Proof.

First observe that
P_{\alpha,\beta}
naturally maps onto the
\beta
-Minkowski sum of the standard
m-1
simplex by sending
(a,b)
to
b
. Now we map
(a,b)
to the point
(m,\sum a_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i},b)=(m,\sum b_{j}b+\alpha,b)
, and then to
(m,b)
. The composition of these maps is
\psi
,
m\in\Delta(\sum b_{j}b+\alpha)
, and all such
m
are obtained this way be definition. But the middle component in the interim step is determined by

. This shows that
P_{\alpha,\beta}\cong\psi\circ P_{\alpha,\beta}
. Finally, the
b
fiber of

is

, which is the

fiber the required Cayley polytope.
∎

2.4.
CI Bundles

For any linear form
\ell(\bar{y})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}y_{j}\in L
there is a corresponding relation
p(\bar{X},\bar{Y})=\frac{1}{X^{\delta}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}X^{D_{j}}Y_{j}\in\mathcal{I}
, where
\delta
denotes the least common multiple of the monomials
X^{D_{j}}
with
C_{j}\neq 0
. In
[
KM
]
a combinatorial classification of those toric vector bundles which are presented as a complete intersection by relations of the form
p(\bar{X},\bar{Y})
is given. Fix a generating set
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m-r}\in L
, and let
M
be the
m-r\times m
matrix of coefficients of
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m-r}
. For each
A\subset[n]
we define a new matrix
M_{A}
. The entry
M_{A}(k,j)
equals the
k,j
-th entry of
M
if the rows
w_{i}
,
i\in A
of
D
have a common minimal entry at
j
, and is
0
otherwise. We let
m_{A}
be the rank of
M_{A}
. The following is
[
KM
, Proposition 6.2]
.

Theorem 2.9
.

The Cox ring
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
is presented in
Sym
-degree
1
by the polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m-r}
if and only if for every
A\subset[n]
we have
r<|A|+m_{A}
, and for every
B\subset[n]
and

we have
1+m_{\{i\}}<|B|+m_{B}
.

For
\ell=\sum C_{j}y_{j}\in L
let
\textup{trop}(\ell)
denote the piecewise-linear function
\textup{MIN}\{y_{j}\mid C_{j}\neq 0\}
. The subsets of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{m}
where
\textup{trop}(\ell)
is a linear function subdivide
{\mathbb{Q}}^{m}
into a complete polyhedral fan
\Sigma(\ell)
. We let
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma,\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m-r})\subset\textup{Trop}(L)^{n}
denote the fan obtained by refining
\Delta(\Sigma,L)
with respect to
\Sigma(\ell_{k})^{n}
for
1\leq k\leq m-r
. Observe that the diagrams
D
which satisfy the inequalities of Proposition
2.9
correspond to integral points in faces of
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma,\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m-r})
. Moreover, this fan clearly only depends on the supports of the forms
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r}
, and there are only a finite number of sets of supports of generators of
L
. It follows that we may further refine
\Delta(\Sigma,L)
with respect to all generating sets of
L
to get a fan
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma,L)
.

Theorem 2.10
.

There is an integral polyhedral fan
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma,L)\subset{\mathbb{Q}}^{n\times m}
such that
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
is presented in
Sym
-degree
1
by polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m-r}
for a collection of linear generators
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m-r}\in L
if and only if
D
is an integral in the relative interior of certain faces of
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma,L)
.

We call a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
corresponding to such a diagram

a
complete intersection
toric vector bundle or
CI
toric vector bundle for short. For a homogenized linear relation
p_{k}\in\mathcal{I}
we let
(\mathbf{c}_{k},1)\in\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
denote the corresponding degree.

Proposition 2.11
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is a
CI
bundle with
\mathcal{I}=\langle p_{1},\ldots,p_{m-r}\rangle
, then:

-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}}=(K_{\Sigma}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{d}_{j}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-r}\mathbf{c}_{k},r).

There are several interesting subclasses of CI toric vector bundles. By requiring that additional conditions be satisfied by the pair
(L,D)
we can simplify the verification of the hypothesis of Proposition
2.9
. First we consider what happens when we take
L
to be a very general linear ideal.

Definition 2.12

(Uniform Toric Vector Bundles)
.

We say a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
is
uniform
if it is the toric vector bundle determined by a pair
(L,D)
where
\mathcal{M}(L)
is a uniform matroid.

The Klyachko spaces of a uniform toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
can be realized as flats of a uniform matroid
U^{r}_{m}
. The conditions of Proposition
2.9
simplify considerably when
\mathcal{E}
is assumed to be uniform.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a uniform toric vector bundle corresponding to the pair
(L,D)
, where
L
is a very general linear ideal, then
\mathcal{E}
is
CI
if and only if for every collection
A\subset[n]
, the non-zero entries of the rows of
D
corresponding to the indices in
A
are contained in
r+|A|-2
columns.

Here the condition that
L
be very general means that
L
is the kernel of a matrix with all non-vanishing minors. The condition for a uniform toric vector bundle to be a complete intersection toric vector bundle only depends on the location of the
0
entries in
D
. As a consequence, the faces of the fan
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma,L)
which contain the diagrams of
CI
uniform bundles can be described as certain
n
-fold products of closed faces from the Bergman fan
\textup{Trop}(U^{r}_{m})
.

A uniform bundle has the most tractable linear ideal
L
. The next class of toric vector bundle has a simple diagram
D
.

Definition 2.14

(Sparse Toric Vector Bundles)
.

We say that a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
is

if it determined by a pair
(L,D)
where there non-negative form of
D
has at most one non-zero entry in each row.

By
[

, Corollary 6.7]
any sparse toric vector bundle

is a complete intersection toric vector bundle, and so has
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
a Mori dream space. This class of toric vector bundles was first studied by González, Hering, Payne, and Süß in
[
GHPS12
]
, where they were described as toric vector bundles with at most one step of codimension 1 in the Klyachko filtrations. The sparse toric vector bundles include tangent bundles of smooth toric varieties, first studied in
[
HS10
]
, and all rank
2
toric vector bundles
[
Gon12
]
. The projectivization of a sparse toric vector bundle is an example of an
arrangement variety

[
CM20
]
, and the projectivizations of toric vector bundles which are both uniform and sparse are
general arrangement varieties

[
HHW19
]
.

2.5.
New CI bundles from old CI bundles

We describe a number of extension theorems, allowing us to form infinitely many new CI bundles of the same type. We describe these new bundles by adding columns to
D
. We suppose an

matrix
M
has been given such that

. In particular, we take the rows of

to be a minimal generating set of

. In what follows we consider the extension

of a pair

, where

and

for

\mathcal{E}
be the toric vector bundle corresponding to the pair
(D,L)
and let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
be the toric vector bundle corresponding to the pair

are non-negative.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a CI toric vector bundle, and suppose that every entry of the

is larger than every entry in the

-th row of

Let

denote the homogenizations of the rows of

and

denote the homogenizations of the rows of

. For any row of
D^{\prime}
, the initial forms of the

agree with those of the

. It follows that the initial ideal
\textup{in}_{\hat{w}_{i}}(\mathcal{I})
is prime and generated by these initial forms, the theorem then follows from Proposition
2.4

∎

When
\mathcal{E}
is a uniform bundle it is more straightforward to find extensions.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a uniform CI toric vector bundle defined by

defined by an

The diagram

also satisfies these conditions.
∎

Example 2.17
.

Let
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{T}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, the tangent bundle of

3.
Newton-Okounkov bodies

In this section we use tropical methods to compute Newton-Okounkov bodies of projectivized toric vector bundles. First we compute the
global
Newton-Okounkov body of the Cox ring, and then specialize to find Newton-Okounkov bodies of divisors. The latter case includes the divisor
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{E}}(1)
, where the Newton-Okounkov body can be rightly thought of as the Newton-Okounkov body of
\mathcal{E}
itself.

For an introduction to Newton-Okounkov bodies see
[
LM09
,
KK12
]
. In general, one starts with a positively graded ring
R
equipped with a full-rank discrete valuation
\mathfrak{v}
. The Newton-Okounkov body, a closed, compact, convex body
\Delta(R,\mathfrak{v})
is assigned to this data. When
R
is the section ring of a divisor on a projective varieties, various properties of the associated linear series can be read off
\Delta(R,\mathfrak{v})
. One can also consider a valuation on the Cox ring of a projective variety which is appropriately homogeneous with respect to the class-group grading, leading to information about how the Newton-Okounkov bodies of all divisors fit together.

In
[
KM19
]
it is shown that Newton-Okounkov bodies can be computed directly from a suitable tropical variety of the ring
R
. We take this approach toward compute global Newton-Okounkov bodies of projectivized CI toric vector bundles
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}

3.2.
The global Newton-Okounkov body of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}

We compute the global Newton-Okounkov body of

by following the procedure described in
[

]

(1)

Find a dimension

(with respect to the fan structure induced from the Gröbner fan of

(2)

Select linearly independent integral points

(3)

Compute
\Delta_{C,\mathbf{u}}=M\circ{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}^{m+n}
, where

The matrix

on

[

in the notation, however, a careful choice of

For any prime cone

It remains to append

by

to account for the grading by

more vectors of the form

\mathcal{I}
with respect to the weight

is the Bergman fan of the matroid

denote the matrix with rows equal to

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

be a prime cone of the form

be the matrix with rows

For a general dimension

The case covered in Proposition

Proposition 3.4

Suppose that

Proof.

A consequence of the condition in Theorem

4.
Positivity properties

In this section we study the pseudoeffective cone
\textup{PsEff}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
, the effective monoid
\textup{Eff}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
, the Nef cone (semiample cone)
\textup{Nef}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
, and the basepoint-free monoid
\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
of a projectivized toric vector bundle
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
. Throughout, the spaces we deal with will be Mori dream spaces, usually projectivized toric vector bundles with
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
generated in
Sym
-degree
1
. Working with this class of varieties has two useful consequences. First, the semiample cone coincides with the Nef cone, and second, the cones we deal with are determined by the monoids in
\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
generated by the classes of certain generators of
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
.

Let
X
be a smooth Mori dream space with free class group
\textup{CL}(X)
. For a point
p\in X
we let
S_{p}\subset\textup{CL}(X)
denote the monoid of classes
\mathbf{d}\in\textup{CL}(X)
which carry a section
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{d}))
with
s(p)\neq 0
. We let
C_{p}={\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}S_{p}\subset\textup{CL}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
. The following is straightforward, but is essential for making the computation of
\textup{Nef}(X)
tractable.

Proposition 4.1
.

With
X
as above, suppose that
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in\mathcal{R}(X)
are a homogeneous generating set of the Cox ring, then we have
S_{p}={\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\{\deg(f_{i})\mid f_{i}(p)\neq 0\}
. Moreover,

\textup{Bpf}(X)=\bigcap_{p\in X}S_{p}

\textup{Nef}(X)=\bigcap_{p\in X}C_{p}

Clearly only a finite number of distinct
S_{p}
are possible, so the computation of
\textup{Bpf}(X)
and
\textup{Nef}(X)
can always be achieved with a finite number of points in
X
. When we consider a smooth toric variety
X(\Sigma)
these points can always be chosen to be the
T
-fixed points. We let
S_{\sigma}
and
C_{\sigma}
denote the monoid and cone associated to the torus fixed point defined by a maximal face of
\Sigma
. Since
\Sigma
is smooth, the monoid
S_{\sigma}
is freely generated, and moreover any smooth polytope (say with inner normal fan
\Sigma
) is very ample (
[
CLS11
, Proposition 2.4.4]
). The following proposition summarizes what we need from these observations.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
X(\Sigma)
be a smooth, projective toric variety, then

\textup{Bpf}(X(\Sigma))=\bigcap_{\sigma\in\Sigma}S_{\sigma},

\textup{Nef}(X(\Sigma))=\bigcap_{\sigma\in\Sigma}C_{\sigma}.

Each monoid
S_{\sigma}
is freely generated. As a consequence,
\textup{Bpf}(X(\Sigma))
is a saturated monoid and any Nef class on
X(\Sigma)
is basepoint free. Moreover, any ample class on
X(\Sigma)
is very ample.

By Proposition

it suffices to find a finite set of points

such that

for every other

. Let

give projective coordinates on the projective space

, and let

be the corresponding linear subspace. The coordinate hyperplanes

define a stratification of

with closed strata

. Let

denote the poset defined by these strata, where

. We are interested in the

. Observe that for any maximal flat

must be a single point.

Definition 4.4
.

For
\sigma\in\Sigma
a maximal face, and

, let

and

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be toric vector bundle over a smooth toric variety

, and suppose that the Cox ring

is generated in

-degree

\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})=\bigcap_{\sigma,F}S_{\sigma,F},

\textup{Nef}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})=\bigcap_{\sigma,F}C_{\sigma,F}.

Proof.

First we observe that if

, then the

such that

form a flat of
\mathcal{M}(\textup{in}_{\sigma}L)
. It follows that the

which are non-zero at

from some maximal flat

. The Cox ring

4.2.
Monomial Bundles

Theorem
4.5
suggests that Nef classes are easier to control on projectivized toric vector bundles with tractable matroids
\mathcal{M}(\textup{in}_{\sigma}L)
. The next definition is made with this idea in mind.

Definition 4.6

(Monomial Bundle)
.

A toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over a smooth toric variety

defined by

is said to be

is a monomial ideal.

A bundle defined by
L
and
D\in\Delta(\Sigma,L)
is monomial precisely when, for each maximal face
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the minimal face of the Gröbner fan of

containing the rows of
D
corresponding to
\sigma
is a maximal face.

Example 4.7

(Kaneyama bundles)
.

Any member of the the class of
Kaneyama bundles
studied in Section
5
is monomial by Proposition
5.3
. In particular, any tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}X(\Sigma)
of a smooth toric variety is monomial.

Given a monomial bundle
\mathcal{E}
over

which comes from the pair
(L,D)
, we show several ways to build a new monomial bundle
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
coming from an extension
(L^{\prime},D^{\prime})
as in Section

. Recall that we realize

as
\ker(M)

as
\ker(M^{\prime})
with

, and

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a monomial and CI bundle. Then
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is monomial and CI bundle if
x_{ij}\in X
is larger than the minimal element of any circuit of
M
.

Proof.

We use the condition for CI described in Theorem
2.9
. Note that, if the circuit condition holds, then the minimal columns of

are always contained in the

part. Therefore, since

is CI and adding columns adds equally to both the support and the rank, we get that

is CI.
Similarly, since our initial forms are taken with respect to weights with the results ordered minimally, adding larger elements to a row does not impact the initial forms. Therefore,
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is monomial.
∎

For the next few results, we specialize extended toric vector bundles to the uniform case. Then, we can not have

be any matrix; rather, let

where the entries of

still allow

to be very general.

Lemma 4.9

Let

be a uniform toric vector bundle corresponding to

such that that

has exactly one zero column.

Proof.

By Proposition 3.9,
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
corresponding to
(L^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is CI. Therefore, we need only check that
\text{in}_{\sigma}(L^{\prime})
is monomial for all maximal
\sigma\in\Sigma
. Since
\mathcal{E}
is uniform,
\Sigma(1)=e_{j}
for

and the maximal cones
\sigma\in\Sigma
are generated by
r-1
such rays. Since

has no zero columns, every choice of
r-1
rows of

corresponds to a maximal cone of
\Sigma
.
∎

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a uniform monomial toric vector bundle. Then
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is a monomial toric vector bundle if and only if
X\geq 0
has no more than

zero entries per column.

Proof.

By the above lemma, it suffices to confirm that every choice of

has exactly one zero column. Note that this is only possible if

Now that we have established that the class of monomial bundles
\mathcal{E}
with

is sufficiently rich, we come to the main result of this section. In what follows we let
F_{j}=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}\setminus\{y_{j}\}

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a monomial bundle over

with
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
generated in

, then:

(1)

The maximal flats of

are all of the form

for some

(2)

The monoids

are all freely generated,

(3)

The cones

are all smooth,

(4)

Any Nef class on

is basepoint-free,

First we observe that if each

4.3.
Toric Vector Bundles over
{\mathbb{P}}^{\underline{n}}

If the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
of a projectivized toric vector bundle is generated in
Sym
-degree
1
, Theorem
4.5
implies
\textup{Nef}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
is an intersection of cones of the form

. Computing such an intersection simplifies when each cone

coincides with the Nef cone of

; this is precisely what happens when
X(\Sigma)={\mathbb{P}}^{\underline{n}}={\mathbb{P}}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n_{\ell}}
.

Recall that a
coloop
of a matroid
\mathcal{M}
is an element whose complement is a flat. Coloops play an important role in computing the Nef cone of a monomial bundle, they are also the key player for positivity properties of toric vector bundles bundles over
{\mathbb{P}}^{\underline{n}}
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a toric vector bundle over
{\mathbb{P}}^{\underline{n}}
with
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
generated in
Sym
-degree
1
, and suppose further that for any
1\leq j\leq m
there is a maximal face
\sigma
such that

is a coloop of
\mathcal{M}(\textup{in}_{\sigma}L)
, then:

(1)

Any Nef class on
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is basepoint-free,

(2)

Any ample class on
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is very ample,

(3)

{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
satisfies Fujita’s freeness and ampleness conjectures.

Proof.

It suffices to prove
1
and
2
. We consider the split toric vector bundle
\mathcal{V}_{D}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{O}(D_{j})
determined by the columns of the diagram
D
. We have a surjection
\mathcal{F}_{D}\to\mathcal{E}
and an embedding
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}\to{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{F}_{D}
, and the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{F}_{D})
presents
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
via the generators
X_{i}
,
Y_{j}
. As a consequence, we get inclusions
\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{V}_{D})\subseteq\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
as subsets of
\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{V}_{D})\cong\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
. Our assumption implies that
\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})=\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}(-\textup{Nef}({\mathbb{P}}^{\underline{n}}),0)+{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}(\mathbf{d}_{j},1)=\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{V}_{D})
. This directly implies
1
. Also, it follows that any Nef class or basepoint-free class on
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
extends to a Nef or basepoint-free class on
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{V}_{D}
. The space
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{V}_{D}
is a smooth toric variety, so any ample class is very ample (in fact projectively normal in this case), so the same must be true for
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
. This proves
2
and
3
.
∎

The argument we use to prove Theorem
4.12
works any time a Mori dream space
X
has a neat embedding into a smooth toric variety

inducing an equality
\textup{Bpf}(X)=\textup{Bpf}(Z)
in
\textup{CL}(X)=\textup{CL}(Z)
. We propose that an embedding with this property be called
neat and tidy
. The next lemma provides a combinatorial check for the coloop property in the hypthosesis of Theorem

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be toric vector bundle over
X(\Sigma)
associated to
(L,D)
, and suppose that every circuit of the ideal

has at least one
0

is a coloop of
\mathcal{M}(\textup{in}_{\sigma}L)
.

Proof.

We will show that there must be some
\sigma\in\Sigma
for which
y_{j}
is not supported on any linear polynomial in
\textup{in}_{\sigma}(L)
. If this is the case, we can find a standard basis for
\textup{in}_{\sigma}(L)
containing

, and define

by setting
y_{j}(p)=1,y_{k}(p)=0

.

Observe that if

is supported on a linear form in

∎

5.
Fano projectivized toric vector bundles

We have shown that

Fano. In this section we obtain a complete classification of Fano

5.1.
(non)-Fano diagonal bundles

Let
\pi:\mathcal{E}\to X(\Sigma)
be the toric vector bundle associated to the pair
(L,D)
, where
D
is a diagonal matrix. The next Proposition characterizes when a fan
\Sigma
and a fixed linear ideal
L
admit a diagonal bundle.

Proposition 5.1
.

Fix a linear ideal
L\subset{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
, an
m\times m
diagonal matrix
D
with positive diagonal entries, and a complete polyhedral fan
\Sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{Q}}
. The data
(L,D)
defines a toric vector bundle over
Y(\Sigma)
if and only if for every collection
\sigma(1)\subset\Sigma(1)
the corresponding
\{y_{i}\mid i\in\sigma(1)\}
form an independent set of
\mathcal{M}(L)
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in\Sigma
be a face with rays
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{d}\in\sigma(1)
. Let
R_{1},\ldots,R_{d}
be the corresponding rows of
D
. Each
R_{i}
contains a single non-zero entry; without loss of generality we suppose that the non-zero entry of
R_{i}
occurs in the
i
-th position along the row. Suppose that
D\in\Delta(L,\Sigma)
, then
R_{1},\ldots,R_{d}
are all contained in a common apartment of
\textup{Trop}(L)
. We claim that
\textup{in}_{\sigma}(L)
is generated by circuits which do not contain
y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}
. The ideal
\textup{in}_{\sigma}(L)
is itself an initial ideal of
\textup{in}_{R_{i}}(L)
for any
i
. But the circuits of
\textup{in}_{R_{i}}(L)
cannot contain
y_{i}
. This is the case for
1\leq i\leq d
. It follows that
y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}
are independent. Now suppose that for every
\sigma\in\Sigma
the
\{y_{i}\mid i\in\sigma(1)\}
form an independent set. Let
\mathbb{B}
be a basis which contains this set, and consider the maximal face
C_{\mathbb{B}}
of the Gröbner fan of
L
corresponding to
\mathbb{B}
. We claim
R_{i}\in C_{\mathbb{B}}
for each
i\in\sigma(1)
. This amounts to checking that the value of each
y_{j}
given by the prevaluation defined by
R_{i}
is computable on the
\mathbb{B}
-expression for
y_{j}
. We’ve assumed that
L
contains no binomials or monomials, so any expression for
y_{j}

j\neq i
involves an element other than
y_{i}
. As the only non-zero entry of
R_{i}
is at the
i
-th spot, this means
y_{j}
’s value is the minimum of the values of the elements appear in its
\mathbb{B}
-expression. This proves that
R_{i}

i\in\sigma(1)
all belong to the apartment of
\mathbb{B}
.
∎

If
D
is diagonal and
rank(\mathcal{E})=m
, then it is straightforward to see that
\mathcal{E}
is a split toric vector bundle. At the other extreme are the tangent bundles
\mathcal{T}X(\Sigma)
of smooth toric varieties, where
D
is the
n\times n
identity matrix. The next result is concerned with bundles of this latter form.

Definition 5.2
.

We say
\mathcal{E}
is a Kaneyama bundle if
\mathcal{E}
has rank equal to the dimension of
X(\Sigma)
, and has non-negative diagram a diagonal matrix.

The terminology “Kaneyama bundle” is in reference to the work of Kaneyama
[
Kan
]
, where it is shown that half of the irreducible toric vector bundles of rank
n
over
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
are of this form. The other half are the dual bundles of Kaneyama bundles.

By Proposition
5.1
, an ideal
L
and a (positive) diagonal matrix
D
defines a Kaneyama bundle over
X(\Sigma)
precisely when
\{y_{i}\mid i\in\sigma(1)\}
is a basis of
\mathcal{M}(L)
for every maximal face
\sigma\in\Sigma
. For now on we fix a Kaneyama bundle
\mathcal{E}
and let
d_{1}\leq\cdots\leq d_{n}
denote the values along the diagonal of
D
. Any Kaneyama bundle is sparse, so it is straightforward to get an expression for the anticanonical class of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
:

-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}}=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}-1)\mathbf{e}_{i},r)

We wish to know when
-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}}\in\textup{Nef}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
. The following cones will be useful for this computation. Let
i\in\sigma(1)
and let

S_{\sigma,j}=\sum_{i\notin\sigma(1)}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}(\mathbf{e}_{i},0)+{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}(d_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j},1)\subset\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Kaneyama bundle over
X(\Sigma)
associated to the positive diagonal matrix
D\in\Delta(\Sigma,L)
, then
\mathcal{E}
is a monomial bundle, and:

\textup{Nef}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})=\bigcap_{i\in\sigma(1)}S_{\sigma,i}.

In particular,
\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
is saturated, any Nef class is basepoint free, and
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
satisfies Fujita’s freeness conjecture.

Proof.

We show that Kaneyama bundle is monomial, and that the set
\{y_{i}\mid i\in\sigma(1)\}
forms the unique basis of
\mathcal{M}(\textup{in}_{\sigma}L)
. Proposition
4.11
then implies the statement. Fix a maximal face
\sigma
and let
\tau
be the face of the Gröbner fan
\textup{GF}(L)
containing the rows
w_{i}=(0,\ldots,d_{i},\ldots 0)
for
i\in\sigma(1)
. It suffices to show that a
w\in\tau^{\circ}
we have
y_{j}\in\textup{in}_{w}L
for
j\notin\sigma(1)
. By assumption, the
y_{i}
,
i\in\sigma(1)
form a basis of
\mathcal{M}(L)
. We let
y_{j}=\sum C_{i}y_{i}
be the circuit expressing
y_{j}
as a linear combination of the
y_{i}

i\in\sigma(1)
. We must have
\textup{in}_{w}(\textup{in}_{w_{i}}L)=\textup{in}_{w}L
, so for all
i\in\sigma(1)
, the initial form of
y_{j}-\sum C_{i}y_{i}
cannot be supported on
y_{i}
, so
y_{j}\in\textup{in}_{w}L
.
∎

If
-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}}
is to be a Nef class on
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
we must have that
-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}}
is contained in each
S_{\sigma,i}
. This means that for each
i\in\sigma(1)
there is an
m(i)\in M
and
a_{j}\geq 0
for

such that:

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(d_{k}-1)\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_{k}+\sum_{j\notin\sigma(1)}a_{j}\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_{j}-rd_{i}\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\rho_{k},m(i)\rangle\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_{k}.

where
\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_{k}
now denotes the
k
-th standard basis vector in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
.

Now we assume
\Sigma
is a smooth fan,
\sigma\in\Sigma
is a maximal face. We let
\epsilon_{i}\in M,i\in\sigma(1)
be a dual basis to
\rho_{i},i\in\sigma(1)
. For the sake of simplicity we assume
\sigma(1)=\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Considering
1\in\sigma(1)
, we write
m(1)=\sum m_{\ell}(1)\epsilon_{\ell}
. Specializing to the coefficient of
\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_{1}
for
i\in\sigma(1)
we get the following:

(d_{1}-1)-rd_{1}=m_{1}(1),\ \ (d_{2}-1)=m_{2}(1),\ \ \ldots,(d_{r}-1)=m_{r}(1).

This leads to the matrix
X_{\sigma}
, with
\ell,i
-th entry
m_{\ell}(i)
:

X_{\sigma}=\begin{bmatrix}(1-r)d_{1}-1&d_{2}-1&\cdots&d_{r}-1\\
d_{1}-1&(1-r)d_{2}-1&\cdots&d_{r}-1\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
d_{1}-1&d_{2}-1&\cdots&(1-r)d_{r}-1\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Now for
\rho_{j}

j\notin\sigma(1)
we write
\rho_{j}=\sum t_{i}\rho_{i}
. The quantity
\langle\rho_{j},m(i)\rangle
is then the dot product of
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
with the
i
-th row of
X_{\sigma}
. This quantity equals
(d_{j}-1)+a_{j}\geq 0
, so we conclude that each
\rho_{k}
lies in the cone with
H
-description
X_{\sigma}\mathbf{t}\geq 0
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Any solution to
X_{\sigma}\mathbf{t}\geq 0
must be a member of the negative orthant.

Proof.

First we assume that
d_{i}\geq 2
. Let
K
be a diagonal matrix with all positive entries. Note that
K\mathbf{t}
lies in the negative orthant if and only if
\mathbf{t}
lies in the negative orthant. Consequently, we can consider solutions to
X_{\sigma}K\mathbf{t}\geq 0
; this reduces us to the following matrix:

W_{\sigma}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}&1&\cdots&1\\
1&\alpha_{2}&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
1&1&\cdots&\alpha_{r}\\
\end{bmatrix},

where
\alpha_{i}=\frac{(1-r)d_{i}-1}{d_{i}-1}<0
. Let
\eta
be an extremal ray of this cone, and suppose that all but the first entry of
W_{\sigma}\eta
is
0
. Applying the difference of any two rows on
\eta
then shows that the signs on the
2
-nd through
r
-th entries of
\eta
must coincide. Comparing with the
1
-st entry shows that all entries of
\eta
must be negative.

Now, if say
d_{1}=1
, and all other entries are
\geq 2
, the argument above shows the signs of a solution all agree in entries
\geq 2
, so the same must be true for the first entry. An induction argument now proves this for any number of
d_{i}=1
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be as above, and let
\Sigma
be smooth, then if
-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}}\in\textup{Nef}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
we must have that for any maximal face
\sigma\in\Sigma
a ray
\rho_{k}
not contained in

is contained in its negative.

Corollary
5.5
places very strong restrictions on the fan

.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth fan with the property that for any maximal face
\sigma\in\Sigma
a ray
\rho_{k}
not contained in
\sigma
is contained in its negative, then
\Sigma
is the fan of a product of projective spaces.

Proof.

Consider
e_{1},...,e_{n}
standard basis vectors which form a maximal face and
\rho=\sum a_{i}e_{i}
:

\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\ldots&0\\
0&1&\ldots&0\\
&&\ddots&\\
0&0&\ldots&1\\
-\rho_{1}&-\rho_{2}&\ldots&-\rho_{n}\end{bmatrix}.

The collection
\{e_{2},...,e_{n},\rho\}
also form a maximal face and, by the negative ray condition,

\pm 1=\det\begin{bmatrix}e_{2}\\
e_{3}\\
\vdots\\
\rho\end{bmatrix}=(-1)^{n+1}(-\rho_{1}),

so
\rho_{1}=1
and any entries that were to appear in the matrix below
\rho_{1}
would have to be zero. However, this omission can be done with any of the
e_{i}
, giving

\begin{bmatrix}e_{1}\\
e_{2}\\
\vdots\\
e_{n}\\
-\rho_{s_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
-\rho_{s_{l}}\end{bmatrix},

where
s_{1}\sqcup...\sqcup s_{l}=[n]
. Then, we can regroup
\{e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{k}},\rho_{s_{i}}\}
where
i_{j}\in s_{i}
. These collections of rays form a maximal face and are exactly the maximal faces of a product of projective spaces.
∎

Now we directly compute
\textup{Nef}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
when
\mathcal{E}
is a Kaneyama bundle over
{\mathbb{P}}^{\underline{n}}={\mathbb{P}}^{n_{1}}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n_{\ell}}
where
\ell>1
. Each
S_{\sigma,i}
can be written
(-\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}^{\underline{n}},0)+{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}((0,\ldots,d_{i},\ldots,0,1)
. let
\delta_{s}
be the minimum of the
d_{i}
corresponding to rays from a the
s
-th component of
{\mathbb{P}}^{\underline{n}}={\mathbb{P}}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n_{\ell}}
, then we have:

\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})=\bigcap_{s=1}^{\ell}(-\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}^{\underline{n}}),0)+{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}(0,\ldots,\delta_{s},\ldots,0,1)=(-\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}^{\underline{n}}),0)+{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}(0,\ldots,0,1).

The
2
nd equality holds only if
\ell>1
. The anticanonical class of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is:

-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}}=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}-1)\mathbf{e}_{i},r)=(\ldots,(\sum d_{i})-n_{s},\ldots,\sum n_{s}).

where
\sum d_{i}
is over those rows belonging to the
s
-th component of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n_{\ell}}
. This class is Nef if and only if
d_{i}=1
for all
i\in\Sigma(1)
, and this class is never ample as long as
\ell>1
. In particular,
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is Fano only if
\mathcal{E}
is a Kaneyama bundle over
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.

Remark 5.7
.

[Tangent bundles and Mori’s Theorem]
The tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}X(\Sigma)
is a Kaneyama bundle for any smooth toric variety
X(\Sigma)
. The linear ideal
L_{\Sigma}
is the ideal of relations which holds among the ray generators of
\Sigma
, and the matrix is the identity matrix. By above, the anticanonical class of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{T}X(\Sigma)
is then
-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{T}X(\Sigma)}=(0,d)\in\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))\times{\mathbb{Z}}
. Notably, the anticanonical class is a multiple of
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{T}X(\Sigma)}(1)=(0,1)\in\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{T}X(\Sigma))
. Recall that a bundle
\mathcal{E}
is said to be ample if
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{E}}(1)
is ample. A consequence of this section is then that the tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}X(\Sigma)
is ample if and only if
-K_{{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{T}X(\Sigma)}
is ample, which in turn holds if and only if
X(\Sigma)={\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. This is the toric special case of Mori’s proof
[
Mor
]
of a conjecture of Hartshorne
[
Har
]
. For a different combinatorial proof of Mori’s theorem in the toric case, see
[
Wu
]
. In fact, we prove that the only smooth, projective toric variety with a Nef tangent bundle is a product of projective spaces.

Question 5.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth, projective variety with Nef tangent bundle, is
X
a product of projective spaces?

5.2.
Fano Kaneyama bundles over
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}

We finish this section by looking at the only remaining case: Kaneyama bundles over
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. For rank reasons, the only linear ideal which can work is the hypersurface
L=\langle y_{0}+\cdots+y_{n}\rangle
; so we reduce to the case of Kaneyama bundles. Here there is still something to say.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}
be the Kaneyama bundle over
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
corresponding to the non-negative integers
\mathbf{a}=\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\}
with
a_{0}\leq\ldots\leq a_{n}
, then
\textup{Eff}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}})={\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}\{(-1,0),(a_{n},1)\}
,
\textup{Nef}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}})={\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}\{(-1,0),(a_{0},1)\}
, and
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}
is Fano if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i}-a_{0})\leq(n-a_{0})
.

Proof.

The first two statements are direct computations. The anticanonical class of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}
is
(-n-1,0)+(\sum a_{i},n+1)-(0,1)=(\sum(a_{i}-1),n)
. For this class to be ample we must have
\sum(a_{i}-1)<na_{0}
, or rather

a_{0}-1\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\sum(a_{i}-1)<\frac{n}{n+1}a_{0}.

For this to be able to hold we must have
a_{0}<n+1
. Moreover, if we write
a_{i}=a_{0}+\delta_{i}
, then this inequality holds exactly when
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{i}<n+1-a_{0}
.
∎

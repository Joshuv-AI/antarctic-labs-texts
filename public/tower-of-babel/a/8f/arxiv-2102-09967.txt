Abstract
. A collection of integer sequences is jointly ergodic if for every ergodic measure preserving system the multiple ergodic averages, with iterates given by this collection of sequences, converge in the mean to the product of the integrals. We give necessary and sufficient conditions for joint ergodicity that are flexible enough to recover the known examples of jointly ergodic sequences and also allow us to answer some related open problems. An interesting feature of our arguments is that they avoid deep tools from ergodic theory that were previously used to establish similar results. Our approach is primarily based on an ergodic variant of a technique pioneered by Peluse and Prendiville in order to give quantitative variants for the finitary version of the polynomial Szemerédi theorem.

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Introduction

The study of
multiple ergodic averages was initiated in the seminal work of Furstenberg
[
30
]
, where an ergodic theoretic proof of Szemerédi’s theorem on arithmetic progressions was given. Since then a variety of multiple ergodic averages has been studied, resulting in
new and far reaching combinatorial consequences. A rather general family of problems is as follows: We are given
a collection of integer sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
and an invertible measure preserving system
(X,\mu,T)
. We would like to understand the limiting behavior (as
N\to\infty
) in
L^{2}(\mu)
of the averages

(1)

{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}

for all functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
where
{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}
denotes the average
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}
. The reader can find a large collection of related convergence
results in
[
24
]
. We remark though that the limit is not easy to compute even in the case where the iterates are integer polynomials. In particular, if the sequences satisfy some non-trivial linear relations, then simple examples of ergodic compact abelian group rotations show that the averages (
1
) are not going to converge to the product of the integrals of the individual functions.

The question we seek to answer is under what conditions on the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
the iterates
T^{a_{1}(n)},\ldots,T^{a_{\ell}(n)}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
behave independently enough, so
that for every ergodic system, or totally ergodic system (cf. Section
1.3
), the averages (
1
) converge in
L^{2}(\mu)
to the product of the integrals.
This gives rise to the following notion of “joint ergodicity” that was first introduced in a somewhat different setting in
[
2
]
.

Definition
.

We say that the collection of sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is

(i)

jointly ergodic for the system
(X,\mu,T)
, if for all functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
we have

(2)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}=\int f_{1}\,d\mu\cdot\ldots\cdot\int f_{\ell}\,d\mu

where convergence takes place in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

(ii)

jointly ergodic
, if it is jointly ergodic for every ergodic system.

It is a direct consequence of Furstenberg’s correspondence principle
[
31
]
that if the collection of sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
is jointly ergodic, then every set of integers with positive upper density contains patterns of the form
m,m+a_{1}(n),\ldots,m+a_{\ell}(n)
, for some
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
In fact, a stronger property holds (which fails when say
a_{1}(n)=n,a_{2}(n)=2n
, see
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
), namely, for every set of integers
\Lambda
we have

(3)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,\bar{d}(\Lambda\cap(\Lambda+a_{1}(n))\cap\cdots\cap(\Lambda+a_{\ell}(n)))\geq(\bar{d}(\Lambda))^{\ell+1},

where
\bar{d}(\Lambda):=\limsup_{N\to\infty}|\Lambda\cap[1,N]|/N
.

For
\ell=1
, by appealing to the spectral theorem for unitary operators (or the theorem of Herglotz on positive definite sequences),
one can show that a single sequence
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is (jointly) ergodic if and only if for every
t\in(0,1)
we have

(4)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,e(a(n)t)=0

where
e(t):=e^{2\pi it}
. In particular, the sequences
[n^{c}]
, where
c\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}}
,
and
[n^{2}\alpha+n\beta]
, where
\alpha,\beta
are non-zero real numbers such that

\alpha/\beta
is irrational, are (jointly) ergodic (see
[
14
, Theorem A]
). It also follows that a single sequence is jointly ergodic for every totally ergodic system if and only if (
4
) holds for every irrational
t\in[0,1)
, a condition that is known to be satisfied, for instance, by all non-constant integer polynomials.

For
\ell\geq 2
, joint ergodicity is a much tougher property to establish.
In
[
22
]
it was shown that the collection of sequences
[n^{c_{1}}],\ldots,[n^{c_{\ell}}]
, where
c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}}
are distinct, is jointly ergodic, and further examples involving polynomials with real coefficients and other sequences arising from smooth functions and bracket polynomials can be found in
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
23
,
37
,
39
]
. Moreover, a collection of polynomial sequences
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
is known to be jointly ergodic
for all totally ergodic systems if and only if the polynomials are rationally independent
[
26
]
; a typical case is the pair
n,n^{2}
, which was first handled in
[
32
]
.
The proofs of most of these results rely on deep tools from ergodic theory, such as the Host-Kra theory of characteristic factors
[
35
]

and equidistribution results on nilmanifolds. But a general criterion for establishing joint ergodicity, like the one mentioned for
\ell=1
, is still lacking, the reason being that we do not have a good substitute for the spectral theorem for unitary operators that would allow us to represent correlation sequences of the form
\int f_{0}\cdot T^{n_{1}}f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot T^{n_{\ell}}f_{\ell}\,d\mu
, for sparse values of
n_{1},\ldots,n_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, in a useful way.

Using ergodic rotations on compact abelian groups we see that a necessary condition for joint ergodicity for the collection of sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is that
for all
t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}\in[0,1)
, not all of them zero, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,e(a_{1}(n)t_{1}+\cdots+a_{\ell}(n)t_{\ell})=0.

Another necessary condition is the “good for seminorm estimates” property that is defined in Section
1.3
and is known to be satisfied for an ample supply of sequences.
The main
objective of this article is to prove the rather surprising fact that these two conditions are also sufficient for joint ergodicity. We do this in Theorem
1.1
.
These necessary and sufficient conditions enable us to recover painlessly all the known results about joint ergodicity of sequences that we are aware of and also
prove some new ones. For example, in Theorem
1.8
we make progress towards a conjecture in

[
22
, Problem 2]
regarding joint ergodicity of Hardy field sequences,
improving upon the best known results in
[
11
,
22
]
, and in Theorem
1.9
we verify a conjecture from
[
11
, Conjecture 6.1]
. A special case of this last result asserts that a collection of sequences of the form

[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}n^{b_{i}}]
, where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
,
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\in(0,+\infty)
are positive,
is jointly ergodic for all totally ergodic systems if and if it is linearly independent;
this was previously known when all the exponents are integers
[
26
]
or none of the exponents is an integer
[
11
]
.

An interesting feature of our main result is that its proof avoids deep tools from ergodic theory and
leads to vastly simpler proofs of several known results. In particular,
our arguments
do not rely on the Host-Kra theory of characteristic factors or on equidistribution results on nilmanifolds, as previous results in the area did. Moreover, in Corollary
1.4
we show that our main result has some far-reaching consequences related to equidistribution properties of general sequences on nilmanifolds. Our approach is to adapt and utilize in our ergodic theory setup a technique developed by Peluse
[
43
]
and Peluse and Prendiville
[
45
]
(see also
[
47
]
for an exposition of the technique) and used in
[
44
,
45
]
to establish quantitative results for finitary variants of special cases of the polynomial Szemerédi theorem.
Although the main ideas are elementary, they are a bit cumbersome to implement in full generality, so in order to facilitate reading, we first present our argument
in the simpler case of the multiple ergodic averages

{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{n}f\cdot T^{n^{2}}g.

It is a classical result of Furstenberg and Weiss that these averages converge in
L^{2}(\mu)
to the product of the integrals for totally ergodic systems, and that the rational Kronecker factor is a characteristic factor for the above averages.
We present in Section
2
a proof of this result
using the general principles of the argument used in
[
45
]
, but also take advantage of various simplifications that our infinitary setup allows (the complete argument is only a few pages long). Subsequently, in Sections
3
and
4
we extend this method to give a proof of our main result, Theorem
1.1
, which gives necessary and sufficient conditions for joint ergodicity. We then use it in Section
5
to give a result about characteristic factors (Theorem
1.6
) that also applies to arbitrary collections of rationally independent integer polynomials.
In Section
6
, by applying the previous results, we recover known results for Hardy field sequences and also address some related open problems. An additional advantage of our approach is that it relies on vastly simpler arguments than previously used.

Finally, in Section
7
, we deal
with similar problems for flows. This section is independent from the previous ones, and the methodology used is completely different than the one used to cover discrete time averages. We prove pointwise convergence results that apply to actions of not necessarily commuting transformations. We remark that even for commuting transformations, the corresponding results for discrete time actions are not known, and for general non-commuting transformations they are known to be false
[
29
, Theorem 1.7]
.
Our main result regarding flows is given in Theorem
1.11
, and representative special cases assert that
for all ergodic measure preserving actions
T^{t},S^{t},R^{t}
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, on a probability space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
and functions
f,g,h\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
,
the following limits exist

\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{1}{y}\int_{0}^{y}f(T^{t}x)\cdot g(S^{t^{2}}x)\,dt,\qquad\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{1}{y}\int_{0}^{y}f(T^{2^{t}}x)\cdot g(S^{3^{t}}x)\cdot h(R^{4^{t}}x)\,dt

pointwise for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
, and are equal to the product of the integrals of the individual functions.
We also establish analogous results without any ergodicity assumptions.
When the actions
T^{t}
and
S^{t}
commute, the corresponding result for the first average was recently obtained
in
[
16
]
, using harmonic analysis techniques from
[
17
]
.

1.2.
Definitions and notation

With
{\mathbb{N}}
we denote the set of positive integers and with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
the set of non-negative integers.
With
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
we denote the set of non-negative real numbers. For
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
we let
e(t):=e^{2\pi it}
. With
[t]
we denote the integer part of
t
and with
\{t\}
the fractional part of
t
.
With
{\mathbb{T}}
we denote the one dimensional torus and we often identify it with
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
or with
[0,1)
.
With
\Re(z)
we denote the real part of the complex number
z
.

For
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
we let
[N]:=\{1,\dots,N\}
(not to be confused with the integer part). If
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}^{s}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a bounded sequence for some
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
A
is a non-empty finite subset of
{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
, we let

{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in A}\,a(n):=\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{n\in A}\,a(n).

If
a,b\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}
are functions we write

a(t)\prec b(t)
if
\lim_{t\to+\infty}a(t)/b(t)=0
.
We say that the function
a\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}
has
at most polynomial growth
if there exists
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
a(t)\prec t^{d}
.

1.3.
Joint ergodicity of general sequences

A
measure preserving system
, or simply
a system
, is a quadruple
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)

where
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
is a Lebesgue probability space and
T\colon X\to X
is an invertible, measurable, measure preserving transformation.
We typically omit the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{X}
and write
(X,\mu,T)
. Throughout, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we denote by
T^{n}
the composition
T\circ\cdots\circ T
(
n
times) and let
T^{-n}:=(T^{n})^{-1}
and
T^{0}:=\id_{X}
. Also, for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we denote by
T^{n}f
the function
f\circ T^{n}
.

We say that the system
(X,\mu,T)
is
ergodic
if the only functions
f\in L^{1}(\mu)
that
satisfy
Tf=f
are the constant ones. It is
totally ergodic
, if
(X,\mu,T^{d})
is ergodic for every
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

To facilitate discussion we introduce the following notions.

Definition
.

If
(X,\mu,T)
is a system we let

(i)

\spec(T):=\{t\in[0,1)\colon Tf=e(t)\,f\text{ for some non-zero }f\in L^{2}(\mu)\}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{E}(T):=\{f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)\colon Tf=e(t)\,f\text{ for some }t\in[0,1)\text{ and }|f|=1\}
.

For the definition of the ergodic seminorms
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{s}
we refer the reader to Section
3.1
.

Definition
.

We say that the collection of sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is:

(i)

good for seminorm estimates for the system
(X,\mu,T)
, if there exists
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that whenever
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)

satisfy
\lvert\!|\!|f_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s}=0
for some
m\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
and
f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in\mathcal{E}(T)
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}=0

where convergence takes place in
L^{2}(\mu)
.
It is
good for seminorm estimates
, if it is good for seminorm estimates for every ergodic system.

(ii)

good for equidistribution for the system
(X,\mu,T)
, if for all
t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}\in\spec(T)
, not all of them
0
, we have

(5)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,e(a_{1}(n)t_{1}+\cdots+a_{\ell}(n)t_{\ell})=0.

It is
good for equidistribution
if it is good for equidistribution for every system, or equivalently, if (
5
) holds for all
t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}\in[0,1)
, not all of them
0
.

(iii)

good for irrational equidistribution for the system
(X,\mu,T)
, if for all
t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}\in\spec(T)
, not all of them rational, equation (
5
) holds.
It is
good for irrational equidistribution
, if it is good for irrational equidistribution for every system, or equivalently, if (
5
) holds for all
t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}\in[0,1)
, not all of them rational.

Remarks
.

\bullet
Using a standard argument (see for example
[
23
, Lemma 4.12]
)
we get that if the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for seminorm estimates for
the product system
(X\times X,\mu\times\mu,T\times T)
for some
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then for all
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
with
\lvert\!|\!|f_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1}=0
for some
m\in[\ell]
and
every bounded sequence of complex numbers
(c_{n})
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,c_{n}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot T^{a_{m}(n)}f_{m}=0

in
L^{2}(\mu)
. We are going to use this fact in Section
5.1
.

\bullet
If
\mu=\int\mu_{x}\,d\mu
is the ergodic decomposition of the measure
\mu
, it is known that if
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s,\mu}=0
, then
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s,\mu_{x}}=0
for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X

[
36
, Chapter 8, Proposition 18]
. As a consequence, if a collection of sequences is good for seminorm estimates for every ergodic system, then it is also good for seminorm estimates for arbitrary systems.

\bullet
If the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for irrational equidistribution, then
the sequences
1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
have to be linearly independent.
Indeed, suppose that
c_{0}+c_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+c_{\ell}a_{\ell}=0
for some
c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}
not all of them
0
. After multiplying by an appropriate constant we can assume that at least one of the numbers
c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell}
is irrational and this immediately implies that the collection
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
is not good for irrational equidistribution.
We remark also, that although
for collections of polynomial sequences linear independence implies good irrational equidistribution, this is not true in general, for instance, it fails when have a single sequence that equals
2^{n}
or
[n\sqrt{2}]
.

\bullet
If
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}
are distinct positive real numbers, then it is known that the sequences
[n^{c_{1}}],\ldots,[n^{c_{k}}]
are good for seminorm estimates
[
22
, Theorem 2.9]
and it is not hard to show using known equidistribution results that they are good for irrational equidistribution (see Proposition
6.4
below). If in addition the exponents
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}
are not integers, then the sequences are also
good for equidistribution (see Proposition
6.3
below).

If a collection of sequences is jointly ergodic for a system, then it is good for seminorm estimates (since
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{1}=0
implies
\int f\,d\mu
=0) and equidistribution for this system (since verifying (
2
) for eigenfunctions of the system implies (
5
)).
It is a rather surprising fact that the converse is also true; this is the context
of our main result that we now state.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be an ergodic system and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be sequences. Then the following two conditions are equivalent:

(i)

The sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are jointly ergodic for
(X,\mu,T)
.

(ii)

The sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for seminorm estimates and equidistribution for
(X,\mu,T)
.

Remarks
.

\bullet

Even when
\ell=1
and the sequence
a_{1}
is strictly increasing, we cannot omit the seminorm condition from the equivalence. Indeed, if a system is weakly mixing but not strongly mixing, then any sequence is good for equidistribution for this system, but clearly, there exists a strictly increasing sequence that is not ergodic for this system. See though Problem
1
below
for a related open problem.

\bullet
Even if we use the full force of the Host-Kra theory of characteristic factors
[
35
]
, it is not clear how to prove Theorem
1.1
. So the more elementary approach we follow
offers substantial technical advantages.

\bullet
It would be interesting to examine if the arguments used in

[
40
]
(which also crucially use ideas from
[
43
,
45
]
)
can be modified to give similar necessary conditions so that (
2
)
holds pointwise; of course, in this case one has to use stronger quantitative variants of the conditions used in
(ii)
.

Since the spectrum of a totally ergodic system contains only irrationals in
(0,1)
and the number
0
, we immediately deduce the following result:

Corollary 1.2
.

The following two conditions are equivalent:

(i)

The sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are jointly ergodic for every totally ergodic system.

(ii)

The sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for seminorm estimates and (
5
) holds for all
t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}\in[0,1)

that are irrational or
0
, but not all of them
0
.

In particular,
this is satisfied by collections of linearly independent polynomials with integer coefficients and zero constant terms (which are also known to be good for seminorm estimates), thus providing a simpler proof of the main result in
[
26
]
. See also Theorem
1.9
below for an application to a vastly more general collection of Hardy field sequences.

The second application is a strong multiple recurrence property, that is not shared for example by collections of linear sequences, or more general polynomial sequences.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be sequences that are good for seminorm estimates and equidistribution for the
system
(X,\mu,T)
. Then for every set
A\in\mathcal{X}
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,\mu(A\cap T^{-a_{1}(n)}A\cap\cdots\cap T^{-a_{\ell}(n)}A)\geq(\mu(A))^{\ell+1}.

By invoking the correspondence principle of Furstenberg
[
31
]
, one deduces that if the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
are good for seminorm estimates and equidistribution, then for every set of integers
\Lambda
equation (
3
) holds.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be an ergodic nilsystem. Then the following two conditions are equivalent:

(i)

The sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are jointly ergodic for

The sequences

Remark
.

Roughly speaking, this statement asserts that a collection of sequences is jointly ergodic for a nilsystem if and only if it is jointly ergodic for its Kronecker factor.

The previous result implies that if the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for equidistribution for the ergodic nilsystem
(X,\mu,T)
, then for all
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in C(X)

(6)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,f_{1}(T^{a_{1}(n)}x)\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{\ell}(T^{a_{\ell}(n)}x)=\int f_{1}\,d\mu\cdots\int f_{\ell}\,d\mu

x\in X

For our last application we assume that
X
is a compact metric space and

is a homeomorphism. The system
(X,T)
is called a

and it is called

is dense in

. Using Theorem

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be sequences that are good for seminorm estimates and equidistribution. Then

(i)

For every minimal system
(X,T)
, for a residual set of

1.4.
Characteristic factors for general sequences

If some of the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are integer polynomials, then
Theorem
1.1
is not applicable to all ergodic systems because the equidistribution property is not satisfied. We will state a result about characteristic factors that gives useful information in such cases and is strong enough to easily imply related convergence and multiple recurrence results.

Definition
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be a system (not necessarily ergodic).

(i)

The
rational Kronecker factor

\mathcal{K}_{rat}(T)
of a system
(X,\mu,T)
is the
L^{2}(\mu)
closure of the linear span of rational eigenfunctions of the system, meaning, non-zero functions
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
such that
Tf=e(t)f
for some
t\in{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(ii)

We say that the rational Kronecker factor
\mathcal{K}_{rat}(T)
is a
characteristic factor for the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
, if whenever at least one of the functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
is orthogonal to
\mathcal{K}_{rat}(T)
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}=0

where convergence takes place in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

For the purposes of the next result we will also need a strengthening of a notion introduced in the previous subsection.

Definition
.

We say that the collection of sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is

very good for seminorm estimates for the system
(X,\mu,T)
, if there exists
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that if
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)

and
\lvert\!|\!|f_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s}=0
for some
m\in[\ell]

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}=0

where convergence takes place in
L^{2}(\mu)

very good for seminorm estimates

It is easy to verify that if for a given collection of sequences the rational Kronecker factor is characteristic for every measure preserving system, then this collection of sequences is very good for seminorm estimates (with

Theorem 1.6

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}

(i)

is a characteristic factor for the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}

(ii)

The sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are very good for seminorm estimates and good for irrational equidistribution.

1.5.
Joint ergodicity of Hardy field sequences

In this subsection we record some rather straightforward applications of the
previous results for
collections of Hardy field sequences (defined in Section

) that satisfy some
growth assumptions that we define next. Our method also gives alternative proofs of known results from

22
,
26

37
]
that avoid the theory of characteristic factors and equidistribution results on nilmanifolds. For the purposes of this subsection we assume that all Hardy fields

considered have

(8)

\text{if }a,b\in\mathcal{H},\text{ then }a\circ b^{-1}\in\mathcal{H}\text{ and }a(\cdot+h)\in\mathcal{H}\text{ for every }h\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

It is known that there

]
); so this covers all the interesting examples considered in practice, like

Definition
.

We say that a function

(i)

(ii)

We denote by
\mathcal{T}

the class of all tempered functions and by
\mathcal{T}+\mathcal{P}
the class of all linear combinations of tempered and polynomial functions with real coefficients.

It is known that if a sequence from a Hardy field has at most polynomial growth and

[

]
(see the discussion around (

A variant of the conjecture that uses appropriately chosen weighted averages

1.6.
Joint ergodicity for nilsystems

Combining Corollary
1.4
and known equidistribution results on the circle from

Theorem 1.10
.

The following collections of sequences are jointly ergodic for every ergodic nilsystem:

(i)

[n^{(\log{n})^{c_{1}}}],\ldots,[n^{(\log{n})^{c_{\ell}}}]
, where

(ii)

[p_{1}(n)\sin{n}],\ldots,[p_{\ell}(n)\sin{n}]
, where

are linearly independent polynomials.

(iii)

[n^{k}\sin{n}],[n^{k}\sin(2n)],\ldots,[n^{k}\sin(\ell n)]
, where

.

We do not know if for these sequences equation (

(or even for

-almost every
x\in X
) and all

that joint ergodicity (for arbitrary ergodic systems) of the previous collections of sequences would follow if one establishes that they are good for seminorm estimates; see Problem

1.7.
Joint ergodicity for flows

A
1
-parameter measure preserving action (or flow) on a probability space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
is a family
T^{t}
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
of invertible measure preserving transformations,
that satisfy
T^{s+t}=T^{s}\circ T^{t}
,
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
(
T^{0}
is the identity transformation), and
such that the map

{\mathbb{R}}\times X\to X
, defined by
(t,x)\mapsto T^{t}x
, is measurable (
X
is equipped with
\mathcal{X}
and
{\mathbb{R}}
with the Borel
\sigma
-algebra).

We give a joint ergodicity result for not necessarily commuting measure preserving flows, when the iterates
are given by functions that satisfy suitable growth conditions. It turns out that in the case of flows we can also prove pointwise convergence results; in contrast, for discrete time actions the corresponding results are false
without any commutativity assumptions (even for mean convergence, see
[
29
, Theorem 1.7]
) and for general commuting actions they are unknown.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon[c,+\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be functions from a Hardy field. Suppose that there exists
\delta>0
such that
t^{\delta}\prec a_{1}(t)
and

(a_{j+1}(t))^{\delta}\prec a_{j}(t)\prec(a_{j+1}(t))^{1-\delta}
for
j=1,\ldots,\ell-1
.
Then for all measure preserving actions
T_{1}^{t},\ldots,T_{\ell}^{t}
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, on a probability space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have

(9)

\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{1}{y}\int_{0}^{y}f_{1}(T_{1}^{a_{1}(t)}x)\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{\ell}(T_{\ell}^{a_{\ell}(t)}x)\,dt=\tilde{f_{1}}\cdots\tilde{f_{\ell}}

pointwise for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
, where for
j=1,\ldots,\ell
we denote by
\tilde{f_{j}}
the orthogonal projection of
f_{j}
on the space of functions that are
T_{j}^{t}
-invariant for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Remarks
.

\bullet
When
\ell=2
and
T_{1},T_{2}
commute, the result was established in
[
16
]
(for
a_{1}(t)=t
,
a_{2}(t)=t^{2}

\ell=2

[

uses the transference principle of Calderón in order to get access to a harmonic analysis result from
[

.

\bullet
If the transformations
T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}
commute, and

are given by polynomials, then mean convergence for (

[

[

The result applies for the collection of functions
t^{c_{1}},\ldots,t^{c_{\ell}}
where

1.8.
Open problems

Theorem
1.1
shows that the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
are jointly ergodic if and only if
they are
(i)
good for seminorm estimates and
(ii)
good for equidistribution. It
is a tantalizing problem to determine whether the equidistribution assumption alone suffices to
deduce joint ergodicity (in which case condition
(i)
can be inferred from condition
(ii)
).
Although at first this may seem like an unlikely scenario, we have not been able to construct a counterexample, which leaves the following question open:

Problem 1
.

If the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
are good for equidistribution, are they
jointly ergodic?

Remark
.

The corresponding question for pointwise convergence has a negative answer even when
\ell=1
. For instance if
a(n):=n^{3}+[(\log{n})^{a}]
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where
a\in\big(1,\frac{6}{5}\big)
, then it follows from
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
that the sequence
a(n)
is good for the mean ergodic theorem (hence good for equidistribution), and from
[
14
, Theorem 3.7]
it follows that it is bad for the pointwise ergodic theorem. So one has to impose stronger quantitative equidistribution assumptions on the sequences in order to get legitimate pointwise convergence criteria.

In support of a positive answer to the previous question let us mention that the answer is positive for
\ell=1
; this follows from the spectral theorem (see the discussion around (
4
)). Moreover, Corollary
1.4
implies that if the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for equidistribution, then they are jointly ergodic for nilsystems.

We also record a problem regarding special sequences arising from smooth functions without a vanishing derivative.

Problem 2
.

Show that the collections of the sequences in Theorem
1.10
are
good for seminorm estimates.

It is known that these collections of sequences are good for equidistribution (see the proof of Theorem
1.10
for relevant references), so solving this problem for any of the examples given in Theorem
1.10
would imply (by Theorem
1.1
) that the corresponding collection of sequences is jointly ergodic. We remark that related multiple recurrence results and combinatorial consequences are largely unknown for these collections of sequences.

By (

2.6.
Step 5

(Proof of Theorem

We are now ready to conclude the proof of Theorem

It is known that if a function is orthogonal to the rational Kronecker factor of a system, then the same property holds with respect to almost every ergodic component (see for example

We first show that if

) holds.
Arguing by contradiction, suppose that (

) fails.
Then if
\tilde{f}_{2}
is given by (

\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{2}>0

2.7.
Weyl-type estimates

We record some pretty standard Weyl-type estimates that were used in the previous argument (see for example
[
33
, Proposition 4.3]
).

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
a>0
and
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
. There exists
Q=Q(a,d)>0
,
C=C(a,d)>0
, such that if

|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]\,}e(t_{1}n+\cdots+t_{d}n^{d})|\geq a

for some
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}\in[0,1)
, then for every

there exist non-negative integers
p,q\leq Q
(depending on
i
)
such that
\big|t_{i}-\frac{p}{q}\big|\leq\frac{C}{N^{i}}
.

From this we deduce the following (we only use it for
\ell=2
and
p_{1}(n)=n
,
p_{2}(n)=n^{2}
):

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
a>0
and
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then there exists a finite set of rationals
R=R(a,d)
with the following property: If
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
are rationally independent polynomials of degree at most
d
such that
for some
l\in[\ell]
and
t_{l}\in[0,1)
we have

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{t_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}},i\in[\ell]\setminus\{l\}}|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]\,}e(p_{1}(n)t_{1}+\cdots+p_{\ell}(n)t_{\ell})|\geq a,

then
t_{l}\in R
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
l=\ell=d
and that all the polynomials have zero constant term.
Let
p_{j}(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{i,j}n^{i}
,
j=1,\ldots,\ell
, for some
c_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The assumption gives that there exist
N_{k}\to\infty
and
t_{i,k},t_{\ell}\in[0,1)
,
i=1,\ldots,\ell-1
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that

|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,e(p_{1}(n)t_{1,k}+\cdots+p_{\ell-1}(n)t_{\ell-1,k}+p_{\ell}(n)t_{\ell})|\geq\frac{a}{2}

for all

. Then for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,e(ns_{1,k}+\cdots+n^{\ell}s_{\ell,k})|\geq\frac{a}{2}

where

s_{i,k}:=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}c_{i,j}t_{j,k}+c_{i,\ell}t_{\ell},\quad i=1,\ldots,\ell,\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

The previous lemma implies that there exist positive integers
Q,C
that depend only on
a,\ell
such that for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
there exist non-negative integers
p_{i,k},q_{k}\leq Q
and
m_{i,k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
i=1,\ldots,\ell
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that

\Big|s_{i,k}-\frac{p_{i,k}}{q_{k}}-m_{i,k}\Big|\leq\frac{C}{N_{k}^{i}},\quad i=1,\ldots,\ell.

Since the polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}
are rationally independent, the matrix
(c_{i,j})_{i,j\in[\ell]}
is invertible. We deduce
that there exist
l,l_{1},\ldots,l_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with size smaller than
L=L(Q)\geq Q
, such that

t_{\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{l_{i}}{l}s_{i,k}.

Combining the previous two facts we get that there exist
\tilde{p}_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and non-negative integers

\tilde{q}_{k}\leq L^{2}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\tilde{p}_{k}}{\tilde{q}_{k}}=t_{\ell}.

Since
t_{\ell}\in[0,1)
and
\tilde{q}_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, are positive integers bounded by
L^{2}
,
it follows that
t_{\ell}=\frac{p}{q}
for some non-negative integers
p,q\leq L^{2}
. So we can take
R
to be the set of all rationals with
numerator and denominator at most
L^{2}
.
∎

2.8.
More general results

Before embarking into the proof of our main results let us make some remarks about the extend to which the previous argument applies to more general families of sequences.

If in place of
n,n^{2}
we have sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
that are good for seminorm estimates and equidistribution, a similar, but technically more complicated argument can be used to prove joint ergodicity and is given in the next two sections.

If in place of
n,n^{2}
we have two rationally independent integer polynomials
p_{1},p_{2}
, then modulo a few additional technical complications, the previous argument
can be adapted as in the next two sections in order to prove that the rational Kronecker factor is characteristic for
p_{1},p_{2}
(for this extension we also need the Weyl estimates of Lemma
2.8
).

If in place of
n,n^{2}
we have rationally independent polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}
, where
\ell\geq 3
, or if we work in the more general setup of Theorem
1.6
, then a new non-trivial obstacle arises.
8
8

8

It is caused by the fact that we can no longer use the spectral theorem (as in the case
\ell=2
) in order to carry out Step 4 of Section
2.5
. For
\ell=3
the problem occurs
when
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{rat}(T)
and we want to deduce that an estimate of the form (
43
) below implies an estimate of the form
(
45
). In the case of sequences that are good for equidistribution, this problem does not arise because we can replace the functions
f_{1},f_{2}
by constants.
In order to overcome this problem
we use a consequence of the main result in
[
35
]
that enables us to reduce matters to the case where the system is totally ergodic (the reduction is carried out in Section
5.1
),
a case that is covered by Theorem
1.1
. Alternatively, we could have used an additional inductive argument, as in
[
44
]
, and avoid the use of deep results form
[
35
]
, thus leading to a more “elementary proof” for the special case of Theorem
1.6
that covers all rational independent polynomials. We chose not to do so, firstly, because
this would lead to a much more complicated argument and, secondly, because this approach requires to impose stronger equidistribution assumptions (of quantitative nature) than those used in Theorem

.

In this section we gather some basic notation and results about the ergodic seminorms

3.1.
The ergodic seminorms

Throughout, we use the following notation:

Definition
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be a system and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. If
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{s}
,
\underline{n}^{\prime}=(n_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,n_{k}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{k}
,
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{s})\in\{0,1\}^{s}
, and
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
, we let

(i)

\epsilon\cdot\underline{n}:=\epsilon_{1}n_{1}+\cdots+\epsilon_{s}n_{s}
;

(ii)

|\underline{n}|:=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}
;

(iii)

\mathcal{C}^{l}z=z
if
l
is even and
\mathcal{C}^{l}z=\overline{z}
if
l
is odd;

(iv)

\underline{n}^{\epsilon}:=(n_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}},\ldots,n_{s}^{\epsilon_{s}})
, where
n_{j}^{0}:=n_{j}
and
n_{j}^{1}:=n_{j}^{\prime}
for
j=1,\ldots,s
;

(v)

\Delta_{\underline{n}}f:=\Delta_{n_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{n_{s}}f=\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\mathcal{C}^{|\epsilon|}T^{\epsilon\cdot\underline{n}}f
(here we allow
\underline{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}
).

Given a system
(X,\mu,T)

we will use the seminorms
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{s}
,
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, that were introduced in
[
35
]
for ergodic systems and can be defined similarly for general systems (see for example
[
36
, Chapter 8, Proposition 16]
). They are often refereed to as Gowers-Host-Kra seminorms and are inductively defined for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
as follows (for convenience we also define
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{0}
, which is not a seminorm):

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{0}:=\int f\,d\mu,

and for
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
we let

(25)

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1}^{2^{s+1}}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\lvert\!|\!|\Delta_{n}f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}^{2^{s}}.

We write
\left\|f\right\|_{s,T}
when it is not clear from the context with respect to which transformation the seminorm is computed. The limit in (
25
) can be shown to exist by successive applications of the mean ergodic theorem and for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
we have
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}\leq\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1}
(see
[
35
]
or
[
36
, Chapter 8]
).
It follows immediately from the definition that

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{1}=\left\|{\mathbb{E}}(f|{\mathcal{I}}(T))\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

where
{\mathcal{I}}(T):=\{f\in L^{2}(\mu)\colon Tf=f\}
and

(26)

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}^{2^{s}}=\lim_{N_{1}\to\infty}\cdots\lim_{N_{s}\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n_{1}\in[N_{1}]}\cdots{\mathbb{E}}_{n_{s}\in[N_{s}]}\int\Delta_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}}f\,d\mu.

It can be shown that we can take any
s^{\prime}\leq s
of the iterative limits to be simultaneous limits (i.e. average over
[N]^{s^{\prime}}
and let
N\to\infty
)
without changing the value of the limit. This was originally proved in
[
35
]
, for a much simpler proof see
[
7
]
.
For
s^{\prime}=s
this gives the identity

(27)

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}^{2^{s}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n}\in[N]^{s}}\int\Delta_{\underline{n}}f\,d\mu,

and for
s^{\prime}=s-2
it gives the identity

(28)

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}^{2^{s}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n}\in[N]^{s-2}}\lvert\!|\!|\Delta_{\underline{n}}f|\!|\!\rvert_{2}^{4}.

Also, we can show that if
T,S
are two commuting measure preserving transformations on
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu)
, then we have the implication

(29)

{\mathcal{I}}(T)\subset{\mathcal{I}}(S)\implies\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s,T}\leq\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s,S}

for every
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.
We give the proof for
s=2
, the argument is similar for general
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Using (
26
), the mean ergodic theorem, and the hypothesis

{\mathcal{I}}(T)\subset{\mathcal{I}}(S)
, we get

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{2,T}^{4}=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}(T^{n}f\cdot f|{\mathcal{I}}(T))\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}(T^{n}f\cdot f|{\mathcal{I}}(S))\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}.

Using the mean ergodic theorem for the system
(X,\mu,S)
and the fact that the transformations
T,S
commute, we get that the last limit is equal to

\lim_{N\to\infty}\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[M]}{\mathbb{E}}_{m\in[N]}\int f\cdot T^{n}\overline{f}\cdot S^{m}\overline{f}\cdot T^{n}S^{m}f\,d\mu.

Exchanging the limits over
M
and
N
(which we can do by
[
34
, Corollary 3]
) and iterating the previous procedure one more time, we deduce that

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{2,T}^{4}\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}\lim_{M\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[M]}{\mathbb{E}}_{m\in[N]}\int f\cdot S^{n}\overline{f}\cdot S^{m}\overline{f}\cdot S^{n+m}f\,d\mu=\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{2,S}^{4}.

3.2.
Soft inverse theorems

Recall that if
(X,\mu,T)
is a system, with
\mathcal{E}(T)
we denote the set of its eigenfunctions with modulus one.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be an ergodic system and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be a function with
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\leq 1
. Then

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{2}^{4}\leq\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{E}(T)}\Re\Big(\int f\cdot\chi\,d\mu\Big).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}(T)
be the Kronecker factor of the system, meaning, the closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mu)
spanned by all eigenfunctions of the system.
It is well known (and not hard to prove, see for example
[
36
, Chapter 8, Theorem 1]
) that

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{2}=\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}|\!|\!\rvert_{2}

where
\tilde{f}:={\mathbb{E}}(f|\mathcal{K}(T))
.
Since the system is ergodic, the subspace
\mathcal{K}(T)
has an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of modulus one, say
(\chi_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
(the basis is countable because the system is Lebesgue). Then

\tilde{f}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}\,\chi_{j}
where

c_{j}:=\int\tilde{f}\cdot\overline{\chi}_{j}\,d\mu=\int f\cdot\overline{\chi}_{j}\,d\mu,\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

It follows that

\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}|\!|\!\rvert_{2}^{4}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|c_{j}|^{4}\leq\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}(|c_{j}|^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|c_{j}|^{2}=\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}(|c_{j}|^{2})\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\leq\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Big|\int f\cdot\overline{\chi}_{j}\,d\mu\Big|,

where the first identity follows by orthonormality and direct computation, the second identity by the Parseval identity, and the last estimate since all functions involved are bounded by
1
.
The result now follows since the set
\mathcal{E}(T)
is invariant under multiplication by unit modulus constants.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be an ergodic system and

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s+2}>0

By (

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n}\in[N]^{s}}\lvert\!|\!|\Delta_{\underline{n}}f|\!|\!\rvert_{2}^{4}>0.

Using Proposition

From this the asserted estimate readily follows.

We will use the following variant of the so called Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

1
, and the identity

\Delta_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}}f=T^{n_{s}}(\Delta_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{s-1}}f)\cdot\Delta_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{s-1}}\overline{f},

we get that it suffices to show that (for later convenience we rename the variable
n_{s}
as
n_{s}^{\prime}
)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n}\in[N]^{s-1}}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n_{s}^{\prime}\in[N]}\,\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}b_{j,N}(\underline{n},n_{s}^{\prime})\cdot T^{n_{s}^{\prime}}(\Delta_{\underline{n}}f)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

Using Lemma
2.5
for the average over
n_{s}^{\prime}
and composing with
T^{{-n_{s}^{\prime}}}
, we get that it suffices to show that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n}\in[N]^{s-1}}{\mathbb{E}}_{n_{s},n_{s}^{\prime}\in[N]}\,{\bf 1}_{[N]}(n_{s}+n_{s}^{\prime})\,\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}b_{j,N}(\underline{n},n^{\prime}_{s}+n_{s})\cdot\overline{b}_{j,N}(\underline{n},n^{\prime}_{s})\int T^{n_{s}}(\Delta_{\underline{n}}f)\cdot\Delta_{\underline{n}}\overline{f}\,d\mu=0,

or, equivalently, that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n_{s}^{\prime}\in[N]}{\mathbb{E}}_{(\underline{n},n_{s})\in[N]^{s}}\,{\bf 1}_{[N]}(n_{s}+n_{s}^{\prime})\,\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}b_{j,N}(\underline{n},n^{\prime}_{s}+n_{s})\cdot\overline{b}_{j,N}(\underline{n},n^{\prime}_{s})\int\Delta_{\underline{n},n_{s}}f\,d\mu=0.

This would follow if we show that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{n_{s}^{\prime}\in[N]}\Big|{\mathbb{E}}_{(\underline{n},n_{s})\in[N]^{s}}\,{\bf 1}_{[N]}(n_{s}+n_{s}^{\prime})\,\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}b_{j,N}(\underline{n},n^{\prime}_{s}+n_{s})\cdot\overline{b}_{j,N}(\underline{n},n^{\prime}_{s})\int\Delta_{\underline{n},n_{s}}f\,d\mu\Big|=0,

or equivalently, that
for any choice of
n^{\prime}_{s,N}\in[N]
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{(\underline{n},n_{s})\in[N]^{s}}\,{\bf 1}_{[N]}(n_{s}+n_{s,N}^{\prime})\,\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}b_{j,N}(\underline{n},n^{\prime}_{s,N}+n_{s})\cdot\overline{b}_{j,N}(\underline{n},n^{\prime}_{s,N})\int\Delta_{\underline{n},n_{s}}f\,d\mu=0.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get that it suffices to show that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{(\underline{n},n_{s})\in[N]^{s}}\,\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}b^{\prime}_{j,N}(\underline{n},n_{s})\cdot\Delta_{\underline{n},n_{s}}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0,

where we let

b^{\prime}_{s-1,N}(\underline{n},n_{s}):={\bf 1}_{[N]}(n_{s}+n_{s,N}^{\prime})\cdot b_{s-1,N}(\underline{n},n^{\prime}_{s,N}+n_{s})\cdot\overline{b}_{s-1,N}(\underline{n},n^{\prime}_{s,N}),\quad(\underline{n},n_{s})\in[N]^{s},\,N\in{\mathbb{N}},

and for
j=1,\ldots,s-2
, we let

b^{\prime}_{j,N}(\underline{n},n_{s}):=b_{j,N}(\underline{n},n^{\prime}_{s,N}+n_{s})\cdot\overline{b}_{j,N}(\underline{n},n^{\prime}_{s,N}),\quad(\underline{n},n_{s})\in[N]^{s},\,N\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Note that we now have a product of
s-1
sequences, instead of
s
, and for
j=1,\ldots,s-1
the sequences
b^{\prime}_{j,N}
do not depend on the variable
n_{j}
. Hence, we can continue like that (on the next step we eliminate the sequences
b^{\prime}_{s-1,N}
, etc), and after
s
steps we deduce that it suffices to show that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n}\in[N]^{s}}\,\big(1-\frac{n_{1}}{N}\big)\ \int\Delta_{\underline{n}}f\,d\mu=0.

Using partial summation with respect to the variable
n_{1}
it suffices to show the following

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n}\in[N]^{s}}\,\int\Delta_{\underline{n}}f\,d\mu=0.

This follows from our assumption
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s}=0
and (
27
) and completes the proof.

∎

4.
Joint ergodicity of general sequences

The primary goal of this
section is to prove Theorem
1.1
. At the end of the section we also deduce Corollaries
1.3
and
1.4
from Theorem
1.1
. For a better understanding of the argument,
we advice the reader to first go through the technically less demanding model case that was treated in Section
2
.

It is clear that Property
(i)
of Theorem
1.1
implies Property
(ii)
. Indeed we can use appropriate eigenfunctions to prove the equidistribution property and the fact that
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{1}=0
implies
\int f\,d\mu=0
to prove the seminorm property with
s=1
.
So we only prove that Property
(ii)
implies Property
(i)
.

4.1.
Goal

It will be more convenient to prove the
following statement (the case
m=\ell
implies Theorem
1.1
):

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be an ergodic system and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}

be sequences that are good for seminorm estimates and equidistribution for
(X,\mu,T)
. Then for every
m\in\{0,1,\ldots,\ell\}

the following holds:

(
P_{m}
) If
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
are such that
f_{j}\in\mathcal{E}(T)
for
j=m+1,\ldots,\ell
, then

(30)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}=\int f_{1}\,d\mu\cdot\ldots\cdot\int f_{\ell}\,d\mu

where convergence takes place in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

In the following steps we fix
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, a system, and a collection of sequences, and we are going to prove
that property
(P_{m})
of Proposition
4.1
holds
by using (finite) induction on
m\in\{0,1,\ldots,\ell\}
.

4.2.
Proof strategy

Let us briefly describe the main idea of the proof, which is again motivated by the technique used in
[
45
]
. If
(
30
) fails, then in Step 1 below, using our assumption that the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for seminorm estimates for
(X,\mu,T)
, we deduce that
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+2}>0
for some
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
where
\tilde{f}_{m}
is given by (
35
). This then implies that

(31)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n}\in[N]^{s}}\,\Re\Big(\int\Delta_{\underline{n}}\tilde{f}_{m}\cdot\chi_{\underline{n}}\,d\mu\Big)>0,

where
\chi_{\underline{n}}
,
\underline{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
, are appropriate unit modulus eigenfunctions of the system
(X,\mu,T)
.
In Steps 2 and 3, using suitable manipulations of (
31
), the good equidistribution properties of the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
, and the induction hypothesis, we deduce that the eigenfunctions
\chi_{\underline{n}}
satisfy some algebraic relations, which allow us to conclude that they are products of sequences in
s-1
variables.
9
9

9

We crucially use in this step that the sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}
are good for equidistribution; if
\ell\geq 3
and we only knew that they were good for irrational equidistribution (for example if they were polynomial sequences), and our aim was to prove that the rational Kronecker factor is a characteristic factor, then our argument would have been much more complicated (as is the case in
[
44
]
).
In view of this, (
31
) combined with Lemma
3.4
enables us to show that

\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1}>0
. Iterating this step
s+1
times we deduce that
\int\tilde{f}_{m}\,d\mu>0
. Given this, it will be an easy matter in Step 4 to contradict the assumptions of Proposition
4.1
.

Let us say a few words about the aforementioned “suitable manipulations” performed in Steps 2 and 3, since they are more complicated than those used for the case
s=1
in Section
2
.
Suppose for simplicity that
\ell=m=s=2
;
hence
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{4}>0
,
and our goal is to show that
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{3}>0
.
In this case, our assumption implies that

(32)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n}\in[N]^{2}}\,\Re\Big(\int\Delta_{\underline{n}}\tilde{f}_{2}\cdot\chi_{\underline{n}}\,d\mu\Big)>0,

for some eigenfunctions
\chi_{\underline{n}}
with modulus
1
. Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality twice (as in Lemma
3.3
) we deduce that (the symmetries of the ergodic seminorms are crucially used here)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}\in[N]^{2}}\,\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,\Re\Big(\int\Delta_{\underline{n}-\underline{n}^{\prime}}g_{k}\cdot T^{a_{1}(n)}(\Delta_{\underline{n}-\underline{n}^{\prime}}\overline{f}_{1})\cdot T^{a_{2}(n)}\chi_{\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}}\,d\mu\Big)>0,

where for
\underline{n}=(n_{1},n_{2})\in[N]^{2}
and
\underline{n}^{\prime}=(n_{1}^{\prime},n_{2}^{\prime})\in[N]^{2}
, the eigenfunctions
\chi_{\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}}
are given by

(33)

\chi_{\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}}=\chi_{n_{1},n_{2}}\cdot\overline{\chi}_{n_{1},n_{2}^{\prime}}\cdot\overline{\chi}_{n_{1}^{\prime},n_{2}}\cdot\chi_{n_{1}^{\prime},n_{2}^{\prime}}.

Note that the last estimate is substantially simpler to analyze than the
\ell=2
case of (
30
), since in the inside average over the variable
n
, the iterate
T^{a_{2}(n)}
is applied to an eigenfunction of the system and not to an arbitrary function in
L^{2}(\mu)
.
Using Property
(P_{1})
of Proposition
4.1
(which is our induction hypothesis), we can assume that the function
\Delta_{\underline{n}-\underline{n}^{\prime}}\overline{f}_{1}
is constant for all
\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
. Using the good equidistribution properties of the sequence
a_{2}
, we deduce that for
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exist constants
\underline{n}_{N}^{\prime}\in[N]^{2}
and sets
\Lambda_{N}\subset[N]^{2}
with
\liminf_{N\to\infty}|\Lambda_{N}|/N>0
, such that
\chi_{\underline{n},\underline{n}_{N}^{\prime}}
is constant for
\underline{n}\in\Lambda_{N}
.
Using (
33
), this gives that for those values of
\underline{n}\in[N]^{2}
the
2
-variable sequence
\chi_{n_{1},n_{2}}
is a product of two
1
-variable sequences. If we use this information and an appropriate variant of (
32
), where we average over
\Lambda_{N}
instead of
[N]^{2}
(by being more careful we can guarantee that this holds), we deduce by Lemma
3.4

that
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{3}>0
.

4.3.
Step 1

(Characteristic factors)
The first step is to show that if (
30
) fails, then it also fails for some function
f_{m}
of special form that is given by (
35
) below.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be sequences,
(X,\mu,T)
be a system, and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be such that

(34)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\cdots T^{a_{\ell}(n)}f_{\ell}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}>0.

Let
m\in[\ell]
. Then there exist
N_{k}\to\infty
and
g_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, with
\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\leq 1
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
for

(35)

\tilde{f}_{m}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{-a_{m}(n)}g_{k}\cdot\prod_{j\in[\ell],j\neq m}T^{a_{j}(n)-a_{m}(n)}\overline{f}_{j},

where the limit is a weak limit (note that then
\tilde{f}_{m}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
), we have

(36)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T^{a_{m}(n)}\tilde{f}_{m}\cdot\prod_{j\in[\ell],j\neq m}T^{a_{j}(n)}f_{j}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}>0.

Furthermore, if
\ell=m=1
, and
\int f_{1}\,d\mu=0
, then we can choose
\tilde{f}_{1}
of the form (
35
) and such that
\int\tilde{f}_{1}\,d\mu=0
.

Proof.

The first part of the statement can be proved
by adjusting the proof of Proposition
2.2
in a straightforward way, so we omit its proof.

We give a proof of the second part related to the case
\ell=1
since it requires some minor adjustments. We can assume that
\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\leq 1
. Suppose that for some sequence
N_{k}\to\infty
we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}>0.

After expanding the square and composing with
T^{-a_{1}(n)}
we get

(37)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int f_{1}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{-a_{1}(n)}\overline{g}_{k}\,d\mu>0

where

(38)

g_{k}:={\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}f_{1},\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

After passing to a subsequence we can assume that the averages
{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{-a_{1}(n)}\overline{g}_{k}
converge weakly in
L^{2}(\mu)
as
k\to\infty
and we let

\tilde{f}_{1}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{-a_{1}(n)}\overline{g}_{k}.

Note that if
\int f_{1}\,d\mu=0
, then (
38
) gives that
\int g_{k}\,d\mu=0
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}

and as a consequence

\int\tilde{f}_{1}\,d\mu=0
.
Moreover, using (
37
) we conclude that
\int f_{1}\cdot\tilde{f}_{1}\,d\mu>0
, and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives

0<\int|\tilde{f}_{1}|^{2}\,d\mu=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\int\tilde{f}_{1}\cdot T^{-a_{1}(n)}g_{k}\,d\mu=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}\tilde{f}_{1}\cdot g_{k}\,d\mu.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality again, we deduce that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,T^{a_{1}(n)}\tilde{f}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}>0

as required.
∎

4.4.
Step 2

(Seminorms of averages of functions)
Our next goal is to use Lemma
3.3
in order to show that if the seminorm of an average of functions is positive, then some related positiveness property holds for the individual functions
(the
s=1
case was proved in Proposition
2.4
).

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be an ergodic system,
f_{n,k}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
,
k,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be bounded by
1
, and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be defined by

f:=\,\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,f_{n,k},

for some
N_{k}\to\infty
,
where the average is assumed to converge weakly.
If
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s+2}>0
for some
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, then there exist

a>0
, a subset
\Lambda
of
{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
with positive lower density, and

\chi_{\underline{n}}\in\mathcal{E}(T)
,
\underline{n}\in\Lambda
, such that

(39)

\Re\Big(\int\Delta_{\underline{n}}f\cdot\chi_{\underline{n}}\,d\mu\Big)>a,\quad\underline{n}\in\Lambda,

and

(40)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}\in[N]^{s}}\,\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,\Re\Big(\int\Delta_{\underline{n}-\underline{n}^{\prime}}f_{n,k}\cdot\chi_{\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}}\cdot{\bf 1}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime})\,d\mu\Big)>0,

where

\chi_{\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}}:=T^{-|\underline{n}|}\big(\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\mathcal{C}^{|\epsilon|}\chi_{\underline{n}^{\epsilon}}\big),\quad\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{s},

and (recall that
n^{\epsilon}
is defined in Section
3.1
)

\Lambda^{\prime}:=\{(\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2s}\colon\underline{n}^{\epsilon}\in\Lambda\text{ for all }\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}\}.

Remarks
.

\bullet
The key point is that from a lower bound for the expression (
39
) that involves the terms

\Delta_{\underline{n}}f
, we can infer a lower bound for the expression (
40
) that involves the easier to handle terms
\Delta_{\underline{n}}f_{n,k}
.

\bullet
For
s=0
the conclusion is that there exists
\chi\in\mathcal{E}(T)
such that
\Re\big(\int f\cdot\chi\,d\mu\big)>0
.

\bullet

We plan to apply this proposition in the next step for the function
\tilde{f}_{m}
given by (
35
) in place of
f
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
we have that there exist
a>0
, a subset
\Lambda
of
{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
with positive lower density, and
\chi_{\underline{n}}\in\mathcal{E}(T)
,
\underline{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
, such that

\Re\Big(\int\Delta_{\underline{n}}f\cdot\chi_{\underline{n}}\,d\mu\Big)>a,\quad\underline{n}\in\Lambda.

Hence,

\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n}\in[N]^{s}}\,\Re\Big(\int\Delta_{\underline{n}}f\cdot\chi_{\underline{n}}\cdot{\bf 1}_{\Lambda}(\underline{n})\,d\mu\Big)>0.

Since
\Delta_{\underline{n}}f=\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\mathcal{C}^{|\epsilon|}T^{\epsilon\cdot\underline{n}}f
,
\underline{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
, and

f=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,f_{n,k}
(the limit is a weak limit), we deduce that

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,\Re\Big({\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n}\in[N]^{s}}\,\int\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}\setminus\{\underline{1}\}}\mathcal{C}^{|\epsilon|}T^{\epsilon\cdot\underline{n}}f\cdot T^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}}f_{n,k}\cdot\chi_{\underline{n}}\cdot{\bf 1}_{\Lambda}(\underline{n})\,d\mu\Big)>0.

For fixed
k,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we apply Lemma
3.3
for
f_{\underline{1}}:=f_{n,k}
,
f_{\epsilon}:=\mathcal{C}^{|\epsilon|}f
for
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}\setminus\underline{1}
, and

g_{\underline{n}}:=\chi_{\underline{n}}\cdot{\bf 1}_{\Lambda}(\underline{n})
,
\underline{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
, and deduce that

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}\in[N]^{s}}\,\int\Delta_{\underline{n}-\underline{n}^{\prime}}f_{n,k}\cdot T^{-|\underline{n}|}\Big(\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\big(\mathcal{C}^{|\epsilon|}\chi_{\underline{n}^{\epsilon}}\cdot{\bf 1}_{\Lambda}(\underline{n}^{\epsilon})\big)\Big)\,d\mu>0.

Since the limsup of a sum is at most the sum of the limsups, the second asserted estimate follows immediately from this one.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.5.
Step 3

(Going from
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{s+2}

\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{1}

Our next goal is to use Proposition

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be an ergodic system and

a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}

. Suppose that property
(P_{m-1})
of Proposition

\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+2}>0

Proof.

We can assume that the functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}

. We will show that if
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+2}>0
for some

, then
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1}>0
. Applying this successively
s+1

\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{1}>0
, or equivalently, that
\int\tilde{f}_{m}\,d\mu\neq 0
.

Since
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+2}>0
, we can use Proposition
4.3
for
\tilde{f}_{m}
in place of
f
and

f_{n,k}:=T^{-a_{m}(n)}g_{k}\cdot\prod_{j\in[\ell],j\neq m}T^{a_{j}(n)-a_{m}(n)}\overline{f}_{j},\quad k,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

We deduce that there exist

a>0
, a subset
\Lambda
of
{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
with positive lower density,

\chi_{\underline{n}}\in\mathcal{E}(T)
,
\underline{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
, such that

(41)

\Re\Big(\int\Delta_{\underline{n}}\tilde{f}_{m}\cdot\chi_{\underline{n}}\,d\mu\Big)>a,\quad\underline{n}\in\Lambda,

and

(42)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}\in[N]^{s}}\,\limsup_{k\to\infty}\\
{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,\Re\Big(\int T^{-a_{m}(n)}(\Delta_{\underline{n}-\underline{n}^{\prime}}g_{k})\cdot\prod_{j\in[\ell],j\neq l}T^{a_{j}(n)-a_{m}(n)}(\Delta_{\underline{n}-\underline{n}^{\prime}}\overline{f}_{j})\cdot\chi_{\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}}\cdot{\bf 1}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime})\,d\mu\Big)>0,

where

\chi_{\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}}:=T^{-|\underline{n}|}\big(\prod_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\mathcal{C}^{|\epsilon|}\chi_{\underline{n}^{\epsilon}}\big),\quad\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d},

and

\Lambda^{\prime}:=\{(\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2d}\colon\underline{n}^{\epsilon}\in\Lambda\text{ for all }\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{s}\}.

Our goal is to use (
42
) in order to show that the eigenfunctions

\chi_{\underline{n}}
in (
41
) satisfy certain algebraic relations that enable us to
deduce, using Lemma
3.4
, that
\lvert\!|\!|\tilde{f}_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{s+1}>0
.

We thus start by analyzing (
42
). After composing with
T^{a_{m}(n)}

we get

\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\underline{n},\underline{n}^{\prime}\in[N]^{s}}\,\limsup_{k\to\infty}\\

6.2.

We will use the following equidistribution result:

Theorem 6.1

(Boshernitzan

Let

We will also use the following reduction, variants of which have been frequently used in the literature. We give its proof for completeness.

Lemma 6.2

Let

are three times differentiable, their derivatives are non-zero on

, and also

and
\frac{a(t)}{ta^{\prime}(t)}
is bounded for

Proof.

We can assume that the first limit is
0
.
Let

F(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,ds,\quad t>0.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Using our assumption we have that there exists
M>0
such that
|F(t)|\leq\varepsilon
for
t>M
and also
a,a^{-1}\in C^{3}([M,+\infty))
.
Since
f
is bounded, it suffices to show that
\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{1}{y}\int_{a^{-1}(M)}^{y}f(a(t))\,dt=0
. We assume that
y
is large enough so that
a(y)\geq M
. Using the change of variables
s=a(t)
we get

\frac{1}{y}\int_{a^{-1}(M)}^{y}f(a(t))\,dt=\frac{1}{y}\int_{M}^{a(y)}f(t)\cdot(a^{-1})^{\prime}(t)\,dt.

Since

f(t)=(tF(t))^{\prime}

for Lebesgue almost every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
we have

\frac{1}{y}\int_{M}^{a(y)}f(t)\cdot(a^{-1})^{\prime}(t)\,dt=\frac{1}{y}\int_{M}^{a(y)}(tF(t))^{\prime}\cdot(a^{-1})^{\prime}(t)\,dt.

Integration by parts (
tF(t)
and
(a^{-1})^{\prime}(t)
are absolutely continuous on
[M,a(y)]
) gives that the last integral is equal to

(61)

\frac{a(y)F(a(y))(a^{-1})^{\prime}(a(y))}{y}-\frac{C}{y}-\frac{1}{y}\,\int_{M}^{a(y)}tF(t)\cdot(a^{-1})^{\prime\prime}(t)\,dt

for some
C\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

The first term in (
61
) is equal to

\frac{a(y)F(a(y))(a^{-1})^{\prime}(a(y))}{y}=\frac{\int_{0}^{a(y)}f(t)\,dt}{ya^{\prime}(y)}=\frac{\int_{0}^{a(y)}f(t)\,dt}{a(y)}\cdot\frac{a(y)}{ya^{\prime}(y)}.

Notice that since
a(t)
is a Hardy field function with
a(t)\succ t^{\delta}
, we have that

\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{a(y)}{ya^{\prime}(y)}=\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{\log{y}}{\log{a(y)}}<\frac{1}{\delta}.

Moreover, by assumption we have

\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{1}{y}\int_{0}^{y}f(t)\,dt=0
and
\lim_{y\to+\infty}a(y)=+\infty
. We deduce that the first term in (
61
) converges to
0
as
y\to+\infty
.

It remains to show that the limsup as
y\to+\infty
of last term in (
61
) is bounded by a constant multiple of
\varepsilon
. Since
|F(t)|\leq\varepsilon
for
t>M
, it suffices to show that the limsup as
y\to+\infty
of the expression

\frac{1}{y}\int_{M}^{a(y)}t\cdot|(a^{-1})^{\prime\prime}(t)|\,dt

is bounded by a quantity that is independent of
\varepsilon
. Since
(a^{-1})^{\prime\prime}
has eventually constant sign, say in
[M_{1},+\infty)
, we can replace
M
with
M_{1}
and remove the absolute value.
After doing so, integration by parts leads to the expression

\frac{1}{y}\Big(a(y)\cdot(a^{-1})^{\prime}(a(y))-C_{1}-\int_{M_{1}}^{a(y)}(a^{-1})^{\prime}(t)\,dt\Big)

for some
C_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.
The last expression is equal to

\frac{a(y)}{ya^{\prime}(y)}-\frac{C_{1}}{y}-\frac{y-a^{-1}(M_{1})}{y}.

As we showed before, the limit of the first term is bounded and the limit of the other two terms as
y\to+\infty
is
1
. This completes the proof.
∎

We review some basic facts from the spectral theory of unitary

(we use Theorem 9.58 and the variant of Theorems 9.17 that applies to flows). If

,

Furthermore, if

, then there exists a bounded operator

,

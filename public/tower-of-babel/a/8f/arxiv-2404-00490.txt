Abstract
. In a recent note F. Lin showed that if a rational homology sphere Y Y admits a taut foliation then the Heegaard Floer module HF − ⁡ ( Y ) \HF^{-}(Y) contains a copy of 𝔽 ⁡ [ U ] / U \mathbb{F}[U]/U as a summand [ 19 ] . 1.
Introduction

Heegaard Floer homology is a package of invariants introduced by Ozsváth and Szabó in 2001
[
30
]
. It assigns to each three-manifold
Y
an
\mathbb{F}[U]
-module,
\HF^{-}(Y)
, that can be used to study – for example – surgery problems, contact structures on three-manifolds, and the symplectic topology of four-manifolds; see
[
20
,
22
,
21
,
31
,
24
]
.

While a quarter of a century has passed since it was defined, there are still many mysterious open questions regarding Heegaard Floer homology. One of these asks if the structure of the module
\HF^{-}(Y)
is related to the existence of taut foliations on
Y
.

Recall that a foliation of a three-manifold
Y
is a decomposition of
Y
into a disjoint union of
2
-dimensional sub-manifolds (leaves) that locally looks like the decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
into horizontal planes. Every three-manifold
Y
admits a foliation. However, it is not necessarily the case that every manifold admits a foliation if additional constrains are required – for example if the foliation is asked to be “Reebless” or “taut”. In the other direction, if a manifold admits such a foliation, this tells us something about the topology of
Y

[
26
,
38
]
.

A foliation is
taut
if there exists a simple closed curve that intersects every leaf. In the 1980’s Gabai
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
showed that every three-manifold with
b_{1}>0
admits a taut foliation. At the time of writing it is unknown which
rational homology spheres
(connected, oriented, three manifolds with
b_{1}=0
) admit taut foliations. Understanding this problem is one of the greatest open challenges of three-manifold topology.

There is a conjectural relationship between rational homology spheres that admit taut foliations and those whose Heegaard Floer homology is free as follows:

Conjecture 1.1

(Boyer, Gordon and Watson
[
6
]
, Juhász
[
17
]
: the
L
-space conjecture)
.

For an irreducible rational homology sphere
Y
the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

Y
is an
L
-space – i.e.
\HF^{-}(Y)
is free.

(2)

Y
does not support a taut foliation,

(3)

\pi_{1}(Y)
has no left-ordering; that is an ordering
<
such that:
a<b
iff
ga<gb
.

Note that the equivalence of (1) and (3) would pin down a property of the fundamental group characterizing
L
-spaces, thus answering one of the fundamental questions in Ozsváth and Szabó’s problem list
[
28
, Question 11]
.

Question 1.2
.

Is there a topological classification of
L
-spaces?

In this paper we advance the hypothesis that the
L
-space conjecture is linked to another fundamental problem in Heegaard Floer theory:

Question 1.3

(The Geography Question)
.

Which
\mathbb{F}[U]
-modules are realized as the Heegaard Floer group
\HF^{-}(Y)
of some three-manifold
Y
?

It is immediate from the definition that
\HF^{-}(Y)
is finitely generated for any three-manifold manifold
Y
. Indeed, Ozsváth and Szabó showed that for a rational homology sphere
Y
,
\HF^{-}(Y)
is of rank
|H_{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})|
as a
U
-module
[
30
, Theorem 10.3]
.

1.1.
Lin’s theorem about taut foliations

The
L
-space conjecture holds for graph manifolds, some families of knot surgeries, and some branched coverings – see
[
5
,
12
,
4
,
18
]
, for example. Nevertheless there is no consensus on whether the
L
-space holds true in full generality. The only part of the conjecture which is known to be true in general is the following: if a rational homology sphere
Y
admits a taut foliation then the Heegaard Floer group
\HF^{-}(Y)
has non-trivial torsion
[
29
, Theorem 1.4]
. Recently Lin proved the following refinement of this result.

Theorem 1.4

(Lin
[
19
]
)
.

Suppose
Y
admits a taut foliation. Then the Heegaard Floer module
\HF^{-}(Y)
contains a copy of
\mathbb{F}[U]/U
as an
\mathbb{F}[U]
-summand.

For a rational homology sphere
Y
, we say that
\HF^{-}(Y)
satisfies the
Lin geography restriction
if
\HF^{-}(Y)
has either no
U
-torsion or an
\mathbb{F}[U]/U\simeq\mathbb{F}
summand. In what follows we use Heegaard Floer surgery formulae
[
23
,
37
,
39
]
to prove that a broad class of rational homology spheres have Heegaard Floer homology satisfying the Lin geography restriction. Thus we present evidence for the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.5
.

The Heegaard Floer module
\HF^{-}(Y)
satisfies the Lin geography restriction for all rational homology spheres
Y
.

It follows from Lin’s theorem, Theorem
1.4
, that either Conjecture
1.5
is true, or the
L
-space conjecture is false. Note that for a large class of graph manifolds the Lin geography restriction was first verified by Bodnar, and Plamenevskaya
[
2
]
.

1.2.
Statement of the main results

We shall make use of the following definition.

Definition 1.6
.

An
\mathbb{F}[U]
-module
M
satisfies the strong geography restriction if it contains a direct summand

\mathbb{F}[U]/U^{\ell}\oplus\mathbb{F}[U]/U^{\ell-1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{F}[U]/U^{2}\oplus\mathbb{F}[U]/U

where
\ell=\min\{\ell\geq 0:U^{\ell}\cdot M_{\text{red}}=0\}
.

Our first theorem concerns surgeries on knots.

Theorem 1.7
.

If
Y
is surgery on a knot in
S^{3}
then the Heegaard Floer module
\HF^{-}(Y)
satisfies the strong geography restriction.

In particular if
Y
is surgery on a knot then either
Y
is an
L
-space, or
\HF^{-}(Y)
has an
\mathbb{F}[U]/U
-summand,
i.e.
it satisfies the Lin geography restriction. This answers a question that was recently asked by Baldwin on his research blog
[
16
]
. Our second theorem covers large surgeries on links.

Theorem 1.8
.

If
Y
is large surgery on a link in
S^{3}
then the Heegaard Floer module
\HF^{-}(Y)
satisfies the strong geography condition.

Here “large surgery on
L
” means that the framing matrix of
L
is “larger” than the smallest hyperbox containing the link Floer homology polytope of
L
– see Definition
3.6
for further details. Recall that the link Floer homology polytope of
L
contains equivalent information to the Thurston norm of
L
, under mild hypothesese
[
36
, Theorem 1.1]
. In the case of knots “large” simply means at least
2g(K)-1
.

Remark 1.9
.

Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.8
hold true in the more general case of null-homologous knots and links in any
L
-space.

Given the above results it is natural to ask the following question.

Question 1.10
.

Does
\HF^{-}(Y)
satisfy the strong geography restriction for every rational homology sphere
Y
?

Of course, given that every rational homology sphere is integral surgery on
some
link, removing the hypothesis that
Y
is
large
surgery on a link in the statement of Theorem
1.8
would provide an affirmative answer to Question
1.10
. Indeed, we know many examples of links – for instance torus links, and connected sums of Hopf links – whose small surgeries satisfy at least the Lin geography restriction.

The realization problem

Theorem
1.8
leads naturally to the following question.

Question 1.11
.

Which rational homology spheres arise as large surgery along links in

There are relatively few results in this direction. Gordon-Luecke showed that a reducible integral homology sphere can never be surgery on a knot

. Examples of irreducible rational homology spheres which are not surgery on a knot in

, and Auckley

. Other examples have been given by Doig

, Hoffman-Walsh

Proposition 1.12
.

Suppose an integral homology sphere

is large surgery on a link. Then
\HF^{-}(Y)\cong\mathbb{F}[U]\oplus\mathbb{F}^{\oplus n}
for some

.

For the Brieskorn homology spheres

[

showed that

has an
\mathbb{F}[U]/U^{2}

For

2.
Three-manifolds arising as surgery on knots

2.1.
Heegaard Floer groups

Recall that Heegaard Floer homology
[
30
]
associates to a pointed Heegaard diagram
\mathcal{H}=(\Sigma,\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{g},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{g},z)
of a three-manifold
Y
a
\mathbb{Q}
-graded, finitely generated, free chain complex
\text{CF}^{-}(\mathcal{H})
over
\mathbb{F}[U]
. The differential counts
J
-holomorphic disks in the symmetric product
\text{Sym}^{g}(\Sigma)
.

Since the chain homotopy type of
\text{CF}^{-}(\mathcal{H})
does not depend on
\mathcal{H}
but only on the three-manifold
Y
it is denoted by
\text{CF}^{-}(Y)
. We shall also consider the localization
\text{CF}^{\infty}(Y)=\text{CF}^{-}(Y)\otimes\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]
and the quotient
\text{CF}^{+}(Y)=\text{CF}^{\infty}(Y)/\text{CF}^{-}(Y)
. Note that the homologies of these chain complexes are related by an exact sequence

\dots\to\HF^{-}(Y)\to\HF^{\infty}(Y)\to\HF^{+}(Y)\to\dots\ ,

and that they decompose as direct sums along
\text{spin}^{c}
structures:

\text{CF}^{\circ}(Y)=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{s}\in\text{Spin}^{c}(Y)}\text{CF}^{\circ}(Y,\mathfrak{s})\ .

This gives rise to a splitting
\HF^{\circ}(Y)=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{s}\in\text{Spin}^{c}(Y)}\HF^{\circ}(Y,\mathfrak{s})
in homology, where
\circ
denotes any of the symbols
-,\infty,+
.

Remark 2.1
.

The group
\HF^{-}(Y)
is a finitely generated, graded
\mathbb{F}[U]
-module. Since
\mathbb{F}[U]
is a principle ideal domain,
\HF^{-}(Y)
can be decomposed as a direct sum of elementary modules of the form:

(1)

T^{-}=\mathbb{F}[U]
so called towers, and

(2)

T^{-}_{k}:=F[U]/U^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
.

Note that for a rational homology sphere there is exactly one tower for each
\text{spin}^{c}
structure, that is
\text{rk}_{\mathbb{F}[U]}\ \HF^{-}(Y)=|\text{Spin}^{c}(Y)|=|H_{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})|

[
30
, Theorem 10.3]
.

Remark 2.2
.

The group
\HF^{+}(Y)
is complete torsion when considered as a module over
\mathbb{F}[U]
. That is:
U^{i}\cdot\alpha=0
for all
\alpha\in\HF^{+}(Y)
, and
i>>0
. Nevertheless, there is a decomposition
\HF^{+}(Y)=F\oplus\HF^{+}_{\text{red}}(Y)
where
F
is a direct sum of a few copies of
T^{+}=\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]/U\cdot\mathbb{F}[U]
, and
\HF_{\text{red}}^{+}(Y)\simeq\text{Tor}(\HF^{-}(Y))
. When working with the
\HF^{+}
version we prefer the notation
T^{+}_{k}
.

2.2.
Knot Floer groups: one complex to rule them all

Heegaard Floer homology also associates to each knot
K\subset S^{3}
a filtered chain complex that can be used to compute the Heegaard Floer homology groups of all surgeries along
K
.

Definition 2.3
.

A knot type chain complex is a finitely-generated, graded,
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
-filtered chain complex

C_{*}=\left(\bigoplus_{\mathbf{x}\in B}\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}],\ \partial\ \right)

over the ring
\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]
satisfying the following properties

•

\partial
is
\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]
-linear, and given a basis element
\mathbf{x}\in B
,
\partial\mathbf{x}=\sum_{\mathbf{y}}n_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}U^{m_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}}\cdot\mathbf{y}
for suitable coefficients
n_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{F}
, and non-negative exponents
m_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}\geq 0
,

•

multiplication by
U
drops the absolute
\mathbb{Q}
-grading (Maslov grading)
M
by two, and the two
\mathbb{Z}
-filtrations (denoted by
\mathcal{F}
and
j
, and called the Alexander and algebraic filtrations) by one.

•

there is a grading-preserving isomorphism
H_{*}(C_{*},\partial)\simeq\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]
.

A knot
K\subset S^{3}
can be encoded by the choice of an additional basepoint
w
in a suitable Heegaard diagram
\mathcal{H}
of
S^{3}
. As explained in
[
33
]
and
[
39
]
, this gives rise to a filtration
\mathcal{F}_{K}
of
\text{CF}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
and hence to a knot type complex

\CFK^{\infty}(K)=(\text{CF}^{\infty}(S^{3}),\mathcal{F}_{K},j)\ ,

where
j
denotes the filtration with
j(\mathbf{x})=0
for every intersection point
\mathbf{x}
of the Heegaard diagram.
A knot type chain complex is usually presented by a planar drawing. This is done according to the following procedure:

•

represent each
\mathbb{F}
-generator
U^{m}\cdot\mathbf{x}
of
C_{*}
,
\mathbf{x}\in B
, as a point on the planar lattice
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
in position
(i,j)=\left(A(\mathbf{x})-m,j(\mathbf{x})-m\right)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
,

•

label each
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-generator
U^{m}\cdot\mathbf{x}
of
C_{*}
with its Maslov grading
M(\mathbf{x})-2m\in\mathbb{Z}
,

•

connect two
\mathbb{F}
-generators
U^{n}\cdot\mathbf{x}
and
U^{m}\cdot\mathbf{y}
with an arrow if in the differential of
U^{n}\cdot\mathbf{x}
the coefficient of
U^{m}\cdot\mathbf{y}
is non-zero.

With these conventions in place any region of the plane
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
identifies a sub-vector space
C_{*}S=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{U^{m}\cdot\mathbf{x}\in C_{*}:\left(A(\mathbf{x})-m,j(\mathbf{x})-m\right)\in S\}
of
C_{*}
. Note that for
C_{*}S
to be a sub-complex of
C_{*}
the regions
S
needs to satisfy a certain convexity condition:

Definition 2.4
.

A region of the Euclidean plane
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is south-west if it is closed, non-empty, and such that:
(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in S\Rightarrow\{(x,y)\ |\ x\leq\overline{x},y\leq\overline{y}\}\subseteq S.

The other case in which
C_{*}S
is a chain complex is when
S=S_{1}\setminus S_{2}
can be expressed as the difference of two south-west regions
S_{2}\subset S_{1}
, and
C_{*}S
is identified with quotient chain complex
C_{*}S_{1}/C_{*}S_{2}
. Note that the latter is not free: if
x\in S_{1}
is a generator and
U^{i}\cdot x\in S_{2}
then
U^{i}\cdot x=0
in
C_{*}S
.

Thus we get chain complexes
C_{*}^{-}=C_{*}\{j\leq 0\}
, and
C_{*}^{+}=\{j\geq 0\}=C_{*}/C_{*}^{-}
. In the case of knot Floer homology these chain complexes compute
\HF^{-}(Y)=\mathbb{F}[U]
and
\HF^{+}(Y)=\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]/U\mathbb{F}[U]
respectively.

2.3.
Review of the knot surgery formula

We now review the homological algebra governing the knot surgery formula.

Let
C_{*}
be a knot type chain complex. For
s\in\mathbb{Z}
let
A_{s}^{+}=C_{*}\{\max(i,j-s)\geq 0\}
and
B^{+}_{s}=C_{*}\{j\geq 0\}=B^{+}
. We define maps
v_{s}^{+}:A_{s}^{+}\to B^{+}
and
h_{s}^{+}:A_{s}^{+}\to B^{+}
. The first is the quotient

v_{s}^{+}:A_{s}^{+}=C_{*}\{\max(i,j-s)\geq 0\}\to C_{*}\{i\geq 0\}=B^{+}

with kernel
C_{*}\{j\geq s\text{ and }i<0\}
. The definition of
h_{s}^{+}
is slightly more complicated and requires the choice of some additional data as in the following definition.

Definition 2.5

(Flip map)
.

A flip map is a chain homotopy equivalence
n:C_{*}\{j\geq 0\}\to C_{*}\{i\geq 0\}
. A master type complex is a pair
(C_{*},n)
where
C_{*}
is a knot type complex, and
n
is a flip map of
C_{*}
.

Note that if
C_{*}=\CFK^{\infty}(K)
is the knot Floer complex of some knot in
S^{3}
,
C_{*}\{i\geq 0\}
and
C_{*}\{j\geq 0\}
represent two chain complexes for
\text{CF}^{+}(S^{3})
with respect to the choice of two different basepoints on the same Heegaard diagram. The flip map
n:C_{*}\{j\geq 0\}\to C_{*}\{i\geq 0\}
is induced by a sequence of Heegaard moves taking one basepoint into the other. We call
(\CFK^{\infty}(K),n)
the
knot Floer master complex
.

For a master type complex
(C_{*},n)
we define
h_{s}^{+}:A_{s}^{+}\to B^{+}
as the composition

h^{+}_{s}:A_{s}^{+}=C_{*}\{\max(i,j-s)\geq 0\}\to C_{*}\{j\geq s\}\to C_{*}\{j\geq 0\}\to C_{*}\{i\geq 0\}=B^{+}

where the first map is the projection
\pi:C_{*}\{\max(i,j-s)\geq 0\}\to C_{*}\{j\geq s\}
, the second map
C_{*}\{j\geq s\}\to C_{*}\{j\geq 0\}
is multiplication by
U^{s}
, and the last one is the flip map
n:C_{*}\{j\geq s\}\to C_{*}\{i\geq 0\}
.

Finally we set

\mathbb{A}^{+}=\bigoplus_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{+}_{s}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \mathbb{B}^{+}=\bigoplus_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}B^{+}_{s}\ .

We have maps
v^{+}=\sum_{s}v_{s}^{+}:\mathbb{A}^{+}\to\mathbb{B}^{+}
, and
h^{+}=\sum_{s}h_{s}^{+}:\mathbb{A}^{+}\to\mathbb{B}^{+}
. We define the map
D_{p}^{+}:\mathbb{A}^{+}\to\mathbb{B}^{+}
as the sum
D_{p}^{+}:v^{+}+\sigma_{p}\circ h^{+}
, where
\sigma_{p}:\bigoplus_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}B^{+}\to\bigoplus_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}B^{+}
denotes the shift map
\sigma_{p}:(\dots,a_{s},\dots)\mapsto(\dots,b_{s}=a_{s-p},\dots)
. Thus:

D^{+}_{p}:(\dots,a_{s},\dots)\mapsto(\dots,b_{s}=v_{s}^{+}(a_{s})+h_{s-p}^{+}(a_{s-p}),\dots)\ .

Taking the mapping cone of the map
D_{p}^{+}:\mathbb{A}^{+}\to\mathbb{B}^{+}
we get a chain complex
\mathbb{X}^{+}(p)=\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\oplus\mathbb{B}^{+}\ ,\partial_{\text{cone}}=\partial+D_{p}^{+}\right)

that in
[
32
]
Ozsváth and Szabó show to be quasi-isomorphic to
\text{CF}^{-}(S^{3}_{p}(K))
.

There is also a rational surgery formula. Set
\mathbb{A}_{i}^{+}=\underset{s\in\mathbb{Z}}{\bigoplus}(s,A^{+}_{\lfloor\frac{i+ps}{q}\rfloor}(K))
and
{\mathbb{B}^{+}=\underset{s\in\mathbb{Z}}{\bigoplus}(s,B^{+})}
. Let
\mathbb{X}^{+}_{i,p/q}
be the chain complex defined as the mapping cone of the map
D^{+}_{i,p/q,j}:\mathbb{A}_{i}^{+}\to\mathbb{B}^{+}
, which we define by
(s,A_{\lfloor\frac{i+ps}{q}\rfloor})\to(s,B^{+})+(s+1,B^{+})
by
\psi_{m}:(s,x)\mapsto((s,v_{m}^{+}(x)),(s+1,h_{m}^{+}(x)),)
and extending linearly. In
[
37
]
Ozsváth and Szabó show that
\HF^{+}(S^{3}_{p/q}(K))
is isomorphic to
H_{*}(\mathbb{X}^{+}_{i,p/q})
.

2.4.
Modules with similar skylines

The following definition and lemma will be used repeatedly in the remainder of the paper.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
A
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{F}[U]
module with

A\simeq\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\bigoplus}T^{-}\oplus\underset{1\leq j\leq m}{\bigoplus}T_{h(j)}^{-}\ .

where
h(j)>0
for all
j
. If
B
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{F}[U]
module we say that
A
and
B
have similar skylines if

B\simeq\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\bigoplus}T^{-}\oplus\underset{1\leq j\leq m}{\bigoplus}T_{h^{\prime}(j)}^{-}\oplus\mathbb{F}^{k}

for some function
h^{\prime}
with
|h^{\prime}(j)-h(j)|\leq 1
for all
j
and
k\geq 0
.

Remark 2.7
.

Note that “having similar skylines” is a reflexive relation: if
B
has a similar skyline to
A
then
A
has a similar skyline to
B
.

We say that an
\mathbb{F}[U]
-module
M
is
of
\HF^{+}
-type
if

M\simeq\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\bigoplus}T^{+}\oplus\underset{1\leq j\leq m}{\bigoplus}T_{h(j)}^{+},

for some
m,n,h
. In this case we write

M_{\text{red}}=\underset{1\leq j\leq m}{\bigoplus}T_{h(j)}^{+}

for the so called
reduced
part. Note that for any three-manifold
\HF^{+}(Y)
is of
\HF^{+}
-type. There is a natural notion of similar skylines for modules of
\HF^{+}
-type, defined exactly as in the case of finitely generated
\mathbb{F}[U]
-modules.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Suppose that we have an exact triangle

(1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

We treat the case of finitely generated

Let

\mathbb{F}^{n}

\mathbb{F}^{n}\simeq\mathbb{F}^{n_{a}}\oplus\mathbb{F}^{n_{b}}

Consider now the maps

and
h_{b}(j)\geq h_{a}(j)
for

and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}(L)
define the same
\text{spin}^{c}
structure if their difference
\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
lies in the span of the columns of the linking matrix
\Lambda
:

\Lambda_{ij}=\begin{cases}&lk(L_{i},L_{j})\ \ \ \text{ if }i\not=j\\
&\ \ \ \ n_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{ otherwise }\end{cases}\ .

To avoid ambiguity one can restrict to a fundamental domain
P(\Lambda)\subset\mathbb{H}(L)
of the action so that every point
\mathbf{s}\in P(\Lambda)
corresponds to a unique
\text{spin}^{c}
structure of
S^{3}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell}}(L)
.

Sometimes instead of working with the fundamental domain
P(\Lambda)
we shall consider a small enlargement of it that we shall denote by
\overline{P(\Lambda)}
. This consists of all the points in
P(\Lambda)
union all points
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{H}(L)
whose coordinates differ from a point in
P(\Lambda)
by at most one. This can be interpreted as “the lattice closure” of
P(\Lambda)
.

Example 3.1
.

Consider
n
-surgery on a knot
K\subset S^{3}
. In the previous section we chose
P(\Lambda)=(-n/2,n/2]\cap\mathbb{Z}
. In this case
\overline{P(\Lambda)}=(-n/2-1,n/2+1]\cap\mathbb{Z}
.

Example 3.2
.

For an algebraically split link
L=L_{1}\cup\dots L_{\ell}
in
S^{3}
, that is a link such that
\text{lk}(L_{i},L_{j})=0
for all
i\not=j
, the linking matrix is diagoinal

\Lambda=\left(\begin{matrix}n_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&n_{\ell}\end{matrix}\right)

and one can pick
P(\Lambda)
so that it has the shape of a hyperbox with vertices at the points with coordinates
(\pm n_{1}/2,\dots,\pm n_{\ell}/2)
.

Example 3.3
.

The linking matrix of a framed
2
-component link
L\subset S^{3}
is a
2\times 2
matrix

\Lambda=\left(\begin{matrix}n_{1}&\lambda\\
\lambda&n_{2}\end{matrix}\right)

where
\lambda=\text{lk}(L)
. In this case
P(\Lambda)
has the shape of a parallelogram with vertices at the points with coordinates

\pm\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}n_{1}\\
\lambda\end{matrix}\right)\pm\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}\lambda\\
n_{2}\end{matrix}\right)\ .

Its two sides have length
(n_{1}^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{1/2}
and
(n_{2}^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{1/2}
respectively, and acute angle
\theta
such that:

\cos\theta=\left(\frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}\right)\cdot\frac{\lambda}{\left(\left(\frac{\lambda}{n_{1}}\right)^{2}+1\right)^{1/2}\cdot\ \ \left(\left(\frac{\lambda}{n_{2}}\right)^{2}+1\right)^{1/2}}\ .

Note that as the framings
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
grow larger
P(\Lambda)
approaches a rectangle (
\theta\to\pi/2
as
n_{1},n_{1}\to\infty
) and its sides grow in length.

For a general
\ell
-component link the domain
P(\Lambda)
has the shape of a parallelopiped with sides parallel to the column vectors of the linking matrix
\Lambda
. Note that as the framings grow larger
P(\Lambda)
approaches the shape of an
\ell
-dimensional hyperbox.
We shall take
P(\Lambda)
to be the unique domain with the points

\pm\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\vec{\Lambda}_{i}

as corners.

3.2.
The Ozsváth-Manolescu surgery formula

Given
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})\in\mathbb{H}(L)
, as in the case of knots, we define
A_{\mathbf{s}}^{-}=\mathfrak{A}^{-}(\mathcal{H},\mathbf{s})
to be the subcomplex of
\CFL^{-}(\mathcal{H})
spanned by all generators
x=U_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots U_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}}\cdot\mathbf{x}
with
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}
, and
m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell}\geq 0
such that
\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathbf{x})-m_{i}=\mathcal{F}_{i}(x)\leq s_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
.

Theorem 3.4

(Large surgery formula, Ozsváth & Manolescu
[
23
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
, and
\mathbf{s}\in P(\Lambda)
a
\text{spin}^{c}
structure. If
\mathbf{n}
is sufficiently large, that is
n_{i}>>0
for
i\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
, then
\text{CF}^{-}(S^{3}_{\mathbf{r}}(L),\mathbf{s})
and
A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}}
are quasi-isomorphic.

Note that multiplication by any of the variables
U_{1},\dots,U_{\ell}
gives rise to a chain map
A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}}\to A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}}
. It turns out that these maps are chain homotopic to each other, and that in Theorem
3.4
the map induced on homology can be identified with the
U
-action of the Heegaard Floer group
\HF^{-}(S^{3}_{\mathbf{n}}(L),\mathbf{s})
.

3.3.
A definition of large

Given a link
L
we let
\mathcal{L}
denote its link Floer polytope. This is the convex hull of all those
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{H}(L)
for which
\widehat{\HFL}(L,\mathbf{s})\not=0
. In this context we will denote by
Q=[-q_{1},q_{1}]\times[-q_{2},q_{2}]\dots[-q_{n},q_{\ell}]
the smallest hyperbox containing
\mathcal{L}
. Note that the link Floer polytope can be expressed as the Minkowski sum of the dual of the Thurston polytope and of a hyperbox of side length one, unless
L
contains a split unlinked component
[
36
, Theorem 1.1]
. In particular if
L
does not have a split unknotted component then
Q
has non-vanishing volume.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
\mathbf{n}>>0
then
Q\subset P(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

For a given
\mathbf{n}
the hyperbox
Q
will be covered by a few different translates of the fundamental domain
P(\Lambda)
. Let
\Lambda_{t}
be the matrix obtained from the linking matrix
\Lambda
by increasing its diagonal enteries by
t>0
. As
t\to+\infty
the
\ell
-dimensional parallelogram
P(\Lambda_{t})
increase in size and eventually contains all the translates of
P(\Lambda)
covering the hyperbox
Q
.
∎

Definition 3.6
.

\mathbf{n}
-framed surgery on a link
L
is large if
Q\subset\overline{P(\Lambda)}
.

Note that if we take the framing
\mathbf{n}
to be large in the sense of Definition
3.6
then we can apply the large surgery formula stated in Theorem
3.4
. This is explained implicitly by Manolesu-Ozsváth
[
23
, P 112.]
, see also the proof given by Borodzik-Gorsky for
[
3
, Lemma 3.10]
. To expand on this point slightly, observe that Manolescu-Ozsváth’s general integer surgery formula can be “horizontally truncated” – see
[
23
, Section 10.1]
– to the large surgery formula in the case that
\Lambda
is large in the sense of Definition
3.6
. The key observation that allows one to prove this is that appropriate versions of the map from
[
23
, Lemma 10.1]
are isomorphisms for
\mathbf{s}\not\in Q
.

3.4.
Lemma
2.9
revisited

We now move towards proving Theorem
1.8
. We start by introducing some notation, definition, and lemmas.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})
and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}=(s_{1}^{\prime},\dots,s_{\ell}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{H}(L)
be two lattice points. We say that
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
is adjacent to
\mathbf{s}
if
0\leq s_{i}^{\prime}-s_{i}\leq 1
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
.

The key lemma is as follows.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{s}
be two lattice points. If
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
is adjacent to
\mathbf{s}
then
H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}})
and
H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}})
have similar skylines.

Proof.

If
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
is adjacent to
\mathbf{s}
then there is an inclusion of sub-complexes
A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}}\subseteq A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}
. Furthermore
H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}/A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}})
is annihilated by multiplication by
U
. Thus we can look at the short exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}/A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

and obtain an exact triangle of the form:

(4)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}^{k}\simeq H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}/A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}})}

For some
k\geq 0
. It follows from Lemma
2.15
that
H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}})
and
H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}})
have similar skylines.
∎

We conclude this section with a discussion of integer homology spheres that arise as large surgeries on links.

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose large surgery on an
\ell
-component link
L
yields an integral homology sphere. Then the link Floer polytope of
L
is contained in
[-1,1]^{\ell}
.

Proof.

Suppose
L
and
\ell
are as in the statement of the proposition and let
S^{3}_{\mathbf{n}}(L)
be a large integer homology sphere surgery on
L
. Observe that the only lattice point that
P(\Lambda)
contains is
\mathbf{0}
. It follows that
\overline{P(\Lambda)}=[-1,1]^{\ell}
. By the definition of large we have that
\mathcal{L}\subseteq Q\subseteq[-1,1]^{\ell}
, as desired.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.12
.

Suppose
Y
is as in the statement of the corollary. Write
Y=S^{3}_{\mathbf{n}}(L)
where
\mathbf{n}
is large and
L
has
\ell
components. By Lemma
3.9
we have that
L
has link Floer polytope supported in
[-1,1]^{\ell}
. It follows that
H_{*}(A^{-}_{(1,1,\dots,1)})\simeq T^{-}
. Since
Y
is an integral homology sphere
P(\Lambda)
contains only the origin
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{H}(L)
. Observe that
\mathbf{0}
is adjacent to
(1,1,\dots,1)
so the result follows from Lemma
3.8
.
∎

3.5.
Lattice paths

We shall consider paths of adjacent lattice points.

Definition 3.10
.

A sequence of lattice points
(\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{k})
is a lattice path if each lattice point
\mathbf{s}_{j+1}
in the sequence is adjacent to his predecessor
\mathbf{s}_{j}
.

We now prove a preliminary version of Theorem
1.8
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Suppose that
L\subset S^{3}
is a link with
\ell
components. Then there exists some
\mathbf{n}_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}_{\geq 0}
such that for all
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{n}_{0}
the surgery three-manifold
S^{3}_{\mathbf{n}}(L)
satisfies the Lin geography restriction.

Proof of Theorem
3.11
.

Let
Q=[-q_{1},q_{1}]\times[-q_{2},q_{2}]\dots[-q_{n},q_{\ell}]
be a hyperbox containing
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{H}(L)
, the link Floer polytope of
L
. It is shown in
[
35
]
that
\CFL^{-}(L)
is generated by module elements
x\in\CFL^{-}(L)
that have Alexander multi-grading
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{L}
. Indeed,
\vec{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbf{x})\in\mathcal{L}
for all intersection points
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}
. Consequently
H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}})\simeq\HF^{-}(S^{3})=\mathbb{F}[U]
for all lattice points
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})
such that

\mathcal{L}\subset\{(j_{1},\dots,j_{\ell})\in\mathbb{H}(L):s_{i}\leq j_{i}\}\ .

Choose a lattice point
\mathbf{s}\in P(\Lambda)
such that
H_{*}(A_{\mathbf{s}}^{-})
contains a direct summand
\mathbb{F}[U]/U^{n}
. If there is no such
\mathbf{s}
then
L
is an
L
-space link and there is nothing to prove. Starting from
\mathbf{s}
form a lattice path
(\mathbf{s}_{0}=\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{k})
where each vector is obtained from its predecessor by increasing all components by one. Taking
k\geq 1
to be sufficiently large we can ensure that
\mathbf{s}_{k}
lies in the semispace
\{(j_{1},\dots,j_{\ell})\in\mathbb{H}(L):q_{i}\geq j_{i}\}
so that
\mathcal{L}\subset A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}}
, and
H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}})=\mathbb{F}[U]
.

Of course if
\mathbf{n}>>0
then the lattice points in the path
(\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{s}_{k})
all belong to
P(\Lambda)
and by Theorem
3.4
we have identifications

T^{-}_{n}\subset\HF^{-}(S^{3}_{\mathbf{n}}(L),\mathbf{s}_{0})\simeq H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}_{1}})\ ,\ \dots\ ,\ \HF^{-}(S^{3}_{\mathbf{n}}(L),\mathbf{s}_{k})\simeq H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}_{k}})=\mathbb{F}[U]\ .

At this point we can apply Lemma
3.8
to conclude that consecutive terms in this sequence have similar skylines. Thus a term in the sequence has an
\mathbb{F}
-summand and in turn we have that
\HF^{-}(S^{3}_{\mathbf{n}}(L))
satisfies the Lin geography restriction.
∎

3.6.
Proof of Theorem
1.8

We continue the discussion initiated in the previous section, maintaining the same notation, with a simple Lemma.

Lemma 3.12
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{H}(L)
is some lattice point. Then there is a lattice point
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in Q
such that
H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}})\simeq H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
L
is an
\ell
component link. Let
Q=[-q_{1},q_{1}]\times[-q_{2},q_{2}]\dots[-q_{\ell},q_{\ell}]
and
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},s_{2},\dots s_{\ell})
. Suppose that
i
is an index such that
s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[-q_{i},q_{i}]
. It suffices to show that
H_{*}(A_{\mathbf{s}}^{-})\simeq H_{*}(A_{\mathbf{s}_{i}}^{-})
where
\mathbf{s}_{i}=(s_{1},\dots s_{i-1},q_{i}\dots s_{\ell})
if
s_{i}
is positive or
\mathbf{s}_{i}=(s_{1},\dots s_{i-1},-q_{i}\dots s_{\ell})
if
s_{i}
is negative.

Case one:
s_{i}>0
.
We can represent the generators of the link Floer chain complex
\CFL^{-}
as a collection of lattice points: we mark a lattice point
\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{\ell})\in\mathbb{H}(L)
as in
\CFL^{-}
if there is an
\mathbb{F}
-generator
x=U_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots U_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}}\cdot\mathbf{x}
such that
j_{i}=\mathcal{F}_{i}(x)=\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathbf{x})-m_{i}
. In this picture multiplication by a variable
U_{i}
acts as

(j_{1},\dots,j_{\ell})\mapsto(j_{1},\dots,j_{i}-1,\dots,j_{\ell})\ ,

and the sub-complex
A_{\mathbf{s}}^{-}
is identified with the set of generators lying inside the semi-space of lattice points
\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{\ell})\in\mathbb{H}(L)
with coordinates
j_{i}\leq s_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,\ell
.

Note that
\CFL^{-}
can be completely reconstructed by translating the lattice points corresponding to the
\mathbb{F}[U_{1},\dots,U_{\ell}]
-generators (intersection points in the Lagrangian Floer set-up), and that these all lie inside the Floer polytope
\mathcal{L}

[
34
]
. It follows that there are no lattice points marked as generators in the strip

\{\mathbf{j}\leq\mathbf{s}\}\setminus\{\mathbf{j}\leq\mathbf{s}_{i}\}\subset\mathbb{H}(L)
, and we have the equality
A_{\mathbf{s}}^{-}=A_{\mathbf{s}_{i}}^{-}
.

Case two:
s_{i}<0
.
Observe that
\CFL^{-}(L)
comes equipped with a
U_{i}
filtration for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
\{K_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq\ell}
be the components of
L
. Observe that the effect of switching the orientation of the component
K_{i}
is to switch the
U_{i}
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}
filtrations; see
[
34
]
. We introduce some new notation to keep track of this; if
L
is a link let
A_{(s_{1},s_{2},,\dots s_{n}),t}^{-}(L)
denote the subcomplex of
\CFL^{-}(L)
generated by elements with each
\mathcal{F}_{j}
filtration at most
s_{j}
and
U_{i}
filtration at most
t
. Now let
L_{i}
denote the link
L
with the orientation of
L
reversed. It follows that

H_{*}(A^{-}_{\mathbf{s}}(L))\simeq H_{*}(A^{-}_{(s_{1},s_{2},\dots s_{i-1},0,s_{i+1}\dots s_{\ell}),s_{i}}(L_{i})).

Since
s_{i}<-q_{i}
, multiplication by
U_{i}^{s_{i}}
induces an isomorphism:

H_{*}(A^{-}_{(s_{1},s_{2},\dots s_{i-1},-s_{i},s_{i+1}\dots s_{n}),0}(L_{i}))\to H_{*}(A^{-}_{(s_{1},s_{2},\dots s_{i-1},0,s_{i+1}\dots s_{n}),s_{i}}(L_{i}))

As in case one we have that
H_{*}(A^{-}_{(s_{1},s_{2},\dots-s_{i},\dots s_{n}),0},L_{i})\simeq H_{*}(A^{-}_{(s_{1},s_{2},\dots q_{i},\dots s_{n}),0},L_{i})
for all
i
. The result follows.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

Note that the argument of Lemma
3.12
specifies the constant
b
in the statement of
[

.

Proof of Theorem

.

for some
\ell

with
\mathbf{n}
large in the sense of Definition

If

.

(2)

,

(3)

then using Lemma

\text{spin}^{c}

-summand. Let

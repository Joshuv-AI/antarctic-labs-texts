Surface density of states and tunneling spectroscopy of a spin-3/2 superconductor with Bogoliubov Fermi Surfaces Preprint: APS/123-QED
Ryoi Ohashi, Shingo Kobayashi, Shotaro Kanazawa, Yukio Tanaka, Yuki Kawaguchi

Abstract
Bogoliubov Fermi surfaces (BFSs) of superconducting states arise from point or line nodes by breaking time-reversal symmetry. Because line and point nodes often accompany topologically protected zero-energy surface Andreev bound states (SABSs) and thereby lead to a characteristic zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) in tunneling spectroscopy, we investigate how these properties change when the line and point nodes deform into BFSs. In this paper, we consider spin-quintet J pair = 2 J_{\rm pair}=2 pairing states of spin-3/2 electrons with BFSs and calculate the surface density of states and the charge conductance. Comparing the obtained results with the cases of spin-singlet d -wave pairing states having the same symmetry, we find that the ZBCP associated with point and/or line nodes is blunted or split in accordance with the appearance of the BFSs. On the other hand, when the spin-singlet d -wave state has point nodes but does not have SABS on the surface, we obtain a nonzero small electron conductivity at zero bias through the zero-energy states on the BFSs.



I
Introduction

Nodal structures of a superconducting pair potential lead to the characteristic properties of unconventional superconductors. An example includes the power-law temperature dependence of bulk physical quantities at a low temperature, such as specific heat, spin relaxation rate, thermal conductivity, and London penetration depth
Sigrist and Ueda 1991
. These signatures have been indeed observed in high-temperature superconductors and heavy fermion superconductors
Tsuei and Kirtley 2000
;
Stewart 1984
.
Another characteristic originating from nodal structures is the appearance of surface Andreev bound states (SABSs) at zero energy due to the change of the internal phase degrees of freedoms of pair potentials
Buchholtz and Zwicknagl 1981
;
Hara and Nagai 1986
;
Hu 1994
;
Matsumoto and Sigrist 1999
;
Furusaki et al. 2001
;
Vorontsov et al. 2008
.
The existence of zero-energy SABSs is related to the bulk topological properties of superconductors
Schnyder et al. 2008
;
Qi and Zhang 2011
;
Tanaka et al. 2012
;
Alicea 2012
;
Beenakker 2013
;
Sato and Ando 2017
;
Tanaka et al. 2024
. Namely, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the number of zero-energy SABSs and the bulk topological invariant via the bulk-boundary correspondence. When the superconducting pair potential has line or point nodes, a topological invariant takes a nonzero value in a subspace of the Brillouin zone, leading to SABSs with dispersion or zero-energy flat-band SABSs in the surface Brillouin zone
Sato et al. 2011
;
Schnyder and Ryu 2011
;
Brydon et al. 2011
;
Matsuura et al. 2013
;
Kobayashi et al. 2014
;
Kobayashi et al. 2016
;
Kobayashi et al. 2018
.

Experimentally, SABSs manifest themselves in the low voltage profile of the tunneling spectroscopy of quasiparticles.
For instance, in the case of spin-singlet
d
-wave superconductors with line nodes, the zero-energy flat-band SABSs lead to a zero bias conductance peak (ZBCP) in normal metal/superconductor (N/S) junctions
Tanaka and Kashiwaya 1995
;
Kashiwaya et al. 1996
;
Kashiwaya and Tanaka 2000
, which was observed in tunneling spectroscopy experiments on high
T_{\rm c}
cuprate superconductors
Alff et al. 1997
;
Wei et al. 1998
;
Iguchi et al. 2000
;
Kashiwaya and Tanaka 2000
.
On the other hand, in the case of 3D chiral superconductors, which have a pair of point nodes in addition to a line node
Kobayashi et al. 2015
;
Tamura et al. 2017
, the dispersive SABSs originated from the point nodes also lead to a ZBCP, but the height of the ZBCP is strongly suppressed as compared to the case of the zero-energy flat-band SABSs
Yamashiro et al. 1997
;
Yamashiro et al. 1998
;
Tanaka et al. 2009a
;
Tanaka et al. 2009b
;
Tanaka et al. 2010
;
Yada et al. 2011
;
Yamakage et al. 2012
;
Lu et al. 2015
.

Recently, a new type of nodal structure, called the Bogoliubov Fermi surface (BFS), is predicted for time-reversal symmetry (TRS) breaking superconductivity in a multi-band system
Agterberg et al. 2017
;
Brydon et al. 2018
;
Setty et al. 2020a
;
Setty et al. 2020b
;
Timm and Bhattacharya 2021
;
Kobayashi et al. 2022
, where zero-energy quasiparticle states appear on a surface in the 3D bulk Brillouin zone.
The unique feature of this superconducting state is that the BFSs arise from the inflation of point or line nodes due to the splitting of superconducting energy bands around the nodes enforced by a TRS breaking effect
Brydon et al. 2018
;
Timm et al. 2017
.
This implies that the BFSs are topological objects characterized by multiple topological invariants
Bzdušek and Sigrist 2017
; namely, one characterizes the BFS and the others are associated with the underlying point or line nodes.
It is expected that the multiple topological natures come together in tunneling spectroscopy and bring about a unique low voltage profile distinguished from the conventional ZBCP attributed to line or point nodes.
Although tunneling spectroscopy in a superconductor with the BFSs has been studied in Refs.
Lapp et al. 2020
;
Banerjee et al. 2022
;
Setty et al. 2020b
;
Pal et al. 2023
, the influence of multiple topological natures on tunneling conductance has yet to be clarified.

In addition, recent experiments on the iron-based superconductor
Fe
(
Se
,

S
)
have indicated the existence of the Fermi surface in the superconducting states.
In these experiments, the signature of the BFS has been detected as a nonzero residual density of states at zero energy at a low temperature in specific heat, thermal conductivity, and tunneling spectroscopy
Sato et al. 2018
;
Hanaguri et al. 2018
;
Mizukami et al. 2023
.
More recently, the direct evidence of the BFS has been reported as an unusual superconducting gap anisotropy with zero gap regions using high-energy resolution laser-based angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy
Nagashima et al. 2022
.
Thus, a detailed study on the influence of the BFSs on experimental observables is called for.

In this paper, we study the SABSs and tunneling spectroscopy of spin-quintet
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
pairing states of spin-3/2 electrons.
We calculate the surface density of states (SDOS) and the charge conductance in N/S junctions utilizing the recursive Green’s function method
Umerski 1997
and the generalized Lee-Fisher formula
Lee and Fisher 1981
;
Inoue et al. 2016
which are available for the lattice models of Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) Hamiltonian
Kawai et al. 2017
;
Yada et al. 2014
;
Takagi et al. 2020
.
Here, we consider the three
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
states,
T_{2g}(0,i,1)
,
T_{2g}(1,\omega,\omega^{2})
and
E_{g}(1,i)
, all of which possess the BFSs.
Because these states can be mapped to spin-singlet
d
-wave pairing states with line and/or point nodes,
we compare the obtained results for the
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
pairing states with those for the corresponding spin-singlet
d
-wave pairing states of spin-1/2 electrons.
The effects of the BFSs on the SABSs and the charge conductance are summarized as follows.
When the spin-singlet
d
-wave state exhibits the zero-energy peak (ZEP) in the SDOS and the ZBCP due to the existence of the topologically protected zero-energy surface flat bands or surface arc states, the corresponding
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
pairing state has blunted or split peaks.
This is because the broken TRS in the
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
states violates the topological protection for the zero-energy SABSs in the
d
-wave pairing states and makes the SABSs more dispersive.
On the other hand, in the case when the
d
-wave state has no SABS, and hence, has zero SDOS and zero charge conductance at zero energy, the small SDOS at zero energy coming from the bulk BFSs and the charge conductance through the zero-energy states become visible in the
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
pairing state.
These features will serve as a guide to detect the BFSs by tunneling spectroscopy.

, and

II
Bogoliubov Fermi Surface in a doubly-degenerate two-band model

We briefly review the underlying physics of the appearance of BFSs
Brydon et al. 2018
.
BFSs can appear in even-parity superconducting states of inversion symmetric systems with broken TRS.
Examples include spin-singlet superconductors under an external magnetic field
Yang and Sondhi 1998
;
Setty et al. 2020b
;
Zhu et al. 2021
and multi-band superconductors with TRS-breaking inter-band pairings. Here, we consider the latter case, i.e., the case when TRS is preserved in a normal state and spontaneously broken in a superconducting state.
A minimal model for such systems is a doubly-degenerate two-band model.

We start from a normal-state Hamiltonian given in the band basis as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bm{k}}}(\bm{c}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger})^{\rm T}H_{0}({\bm{k}})\bm{c}_{\bm{k}},

(1)

\displaystyle H_{0}({\bm{k}})

\displaystyle=\Diag[E_{{\bm{k}},+},E_{{\bm{k}},+},E_{{\bm{k}},-},E_{{\bm{k}},-}]-\mu\mathds{1}_{4},

(2)

where
E_{{\bm{k}},+}
and
E_{{\bm{k}},-}
are the energy dispersion of the two bands, each of which is doubly degenerate due to the TRS,
\mu
is the chemical potential,
\mathds{1}_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
identity matrix, and

\bm{c}_{\bm{k}}=(c_{{\bm{k}}+\uparrow},c_{{\bm{k}}+\downarrow},c_{{\bm{k}}-\uparrow},c_{{\bm{k}}-\downarrow})^{\rm T}
and

\bm{c}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}=(c^{\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}+\uparrow},c^{\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}+\downarrow},c^{\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}-\uparrow},c^{\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}-\downarrow})^{\rm T}

with
c_{{\bm{k}}\pm s}
being the annihilation operator of an electron in the Bloch state
|{\bm{k}},\pm,s\rangle
in the
\pm
band with momentum
{\bm{k}}
and pseudospin
s=\uparrow,\downarrow
.
From the assumptions of inversion symmetry and TRS,
the space inversion operator
P
and the time-reversal operator
T
transforms the Bloch states as

\displaystyle P|{\bm{k}},\pm,s\rangle

\displaystyle=|-{\bm{k}},\pm,s\rangle,

(3)

\displaystyle T|{\bm{k}},\pm,s\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sign(s)|-{\bm{k}},\pm,-s\rangle,

(4)

where
\sign(\uparrow)=1
and
\sign(\downarrow)=-1
. Due to the inversion symmetry, the energy dispersion satisfies
E_{{\bm{k}},\pm}=E_{-{\bm{k}},\pm}
.

The superconducting state is described by the Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) Hamiltonian given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bm{k}}}\begin{pmatrix}(\bm{c}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger})^{\rm T}&\bm{c}_{-{\bm{k}}}^{\rm T}\end{pmatrix}H_{\bm{k}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{c}_{\bm{k}}\\
\bm{c}_{-{\bm{k}}}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix},

(5)

\displaystyle H_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}H_{0}({\bm{k}})&\Delta({\bm{k}})\\
\Delta^{\dagger}({\bm{k}})&-H_{0}^{\rm T}(-{\bm{k}})\end{pmatrix},

(6)

where the pairing term
\Delta({\bm{k}})
should have
even or odd parity.
For the case of even-parity pairing, which is our interest,
the general form of

is given by

\displaystyle\Delta({\bm{k}})=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{{\bm{k}},+}i\sigma_{y}&(\psi_{{\bm{k}},I}\sigma_{0}+\bm{d}_{\bm{k}}\cdot\bm{\sigma})i\sigma_{y}\\
(\psi_{{\bm{k}},I}\sigma_{0}-\bm{d}_{\bm{k}}\cdot\bm{\sigma})i\sigma_{y}&\psi_{{\bm{k}},-}i\sigma_{y}\end{pmatrix},

(7)

where
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
and
\sigma_{0}
are the vector of Pauli matrices and the identity matrix in the pseudospin space, respectively,
and all functions in the matrix are even functions of
\bm{k}
.
Here,
\psi_{{\bm{k}},\pm}
denotes the pseudospin-singlet pair in each band, whereas

\psi_{{\bm{k}},I}
and

are the pseudospin-singlet and triplet components, respectively, in the inter-band pairing.
Note that the pseudospin-triplet pairing should be band singlet due to the Fermi-Dirac statistics, which is the reason for the different signs in front of
{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}
in the off-diagonal terms.
By defining the pseudospin operator for
+
and
-
bands as

\displaystyle\bm{S}_{+}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\sigma}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\ \ \bm{S}_{-}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\bm{\sigma}\end{pmatrix},

(8)

the polarization in the pairing state at momentum
{\bm{k}}
is given by

\displaystyle\Tr[\Delta^{\dagger}({\bm{k}})\bm{S}_{\pm}\Delta({\bm{k}})]=2i{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}\times{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}^{*}\pm 4{\rm Re}(\psi_{{\bm{k}},I}^{*}{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}),

(9)

which means that TRS is spontaneously broken when

is a complex vector such that
{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}^{*}\neq{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}

BFS is a topological object defined for an inversion-symmetric BdG Hamiltonian with even-parity pairing.
Here, we consider the even-parity pairing Hamiltonian of Eq. (
7
).
We further assume that only the
+
band has a normal-state Fermi surface and the energy scales of the inter-band and intra-band pairings are much smaller than the energy splitting
|E_{{\bm{k}},+}-E_{{\bm{k}},-}|
in the vicinity of the Fermi surface.
Then, the BFSs appear when the intra-band pair potential,
\psi_{{\bm{k}},+}
, has nodes and the inter-band pair potentials,
\psi_{{\bm{k}},I}
and
\bm{d}_{\bm{k}}

(See Appendix
A

On the other hand, when only the
+

\psi_{{\bm{k}},+}

The corresponding excitation spectrum
\sqrt{(E_{{\bm{k}},+}-\mu)^{2}+|\psi_{{\bm{k}},+}|^{2}}

when
\psi_{{\bm{k}},+}
has
d

The BFSs appear only when the inter-band coupling exists and breaks TRS: In such a case, the polarization (
9

Hence, the existence of the TRS-breaking inter-band pairing is crucial for the appearance of BFSs, and it deforms the point and line nodes of
\psi_{{\bm{k}},+}

In the following sections, we investigate how the properties of the nodal
d
-wave pairing states in a single-band system change when the nodes deform into BFSs by taking into account the inter-band pairing.

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied surface Andreev bound states (SABSs) of superconductors having Bogoliubov Fermi surfaces (BFSs) and their contributions to the charge conductance at normal metal/superconductor (N/S) junctions.
We consider spin quintet
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
pairings of spin-3/2 electrons, which exhibit anisotropic behavior due to the asymmetry in the spin space even when the pair potential is isotropic in the momentum space.
When the system is projected to an effective pseudo-spin-1/2 system, the
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
pairs are mapped to spin-singlet
d
-wave pairs belonging to the same irreducible representation as the pristine
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
pair.
In particular, the
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
pairs possessing BFSs, which are our interest, are mapped to the
d
-wave pair potentials with line nodes and/or point nodes, which accompany topological zero-energy surface flat bands and surface arc states on specific surfaces.
Since such topological surface states are featured by the zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) at an N/S junction, we have investigated how the situation changes in the spin-3/2
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
pairs with BFSs having the same symmetry.

We have calculated the surface density of states (SDOS) and quasiparticle tunneling conductance in N/S junctions based on the recursive Green’s function method and a generalized Lee-Fisher formula.
We have discussed three spin-3/2
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
pairing states:
T_{2g}(0,i,1),T_{2g}(1,\omega,\omega^{2})
, and
E_{g}(1,i)
states.
The corresponding spin-1/2
d
-wave superconducting states have zero-energy surface flat bands, surface arc states, and no SABS, respectively, on the (001) surface.
In the first case,
T_{2g}(0,i,1)
, the doubly-degenerate zero-energy surface flat bands in the
d
-wave pairing state split due to the appearance of the BFSs.
Thus, the zero-energy peak (ZEP) of the SDOS and the ZBCP observed in the spin-1/2
d
-wave pairing case is blunted, although the zero-energy surface states remain on a ring on the surface Brillouin zone.
In the second case,
T_{2g}(1,\omega,\omega^{2})
, the location of the surface arc states, which is topologically restricted in the case of the spin-1/2
d
-wave pairing state, changes in the spin-3/2 system. Accordingly, the crossing of the arcs disappears, leading to the splitting of the ZEP of the SDOS and the ZBCP into two small peaks at nonzero energies.
In the third case,
E_{g}(1,i)
, there is no SABS on the (001) surface for both cases of spin-1/2 and spin-3/2 systems.
Because there is no ZEP of the SDOS, we can investigate the electronic transport through the zero-energy states on the BFSs, and indeed, we obtained non-zero charge conductance at zero bias, which increases as the BFSs become larger.
Summarising above, the topological protections existing in the spin-1/2
d
-wave pairings are easily violated in the corresponding spin-3/2
J_{\textrm{pair}}=2
pairings, leading to blunting or splitting of ZBCPs. At the same time, zero-energy states on BFSs directly contribute to the electron transport at zero bias, which is visible in the absence of ZBCP.

We should comment here that though we have considered BFSs in spin-3/2
J_{\rm pair}=2
pairing states, the qualitative behavior of the tunneling spectroscopy generally holds for other multi-band systems.
Let
\Delta_{\rm inter}
denote the strength of the TRS-breaking inter-band pair potential.
The splitting of the BdG spectrum, which is doubly-degenerate for
d
-wave states, is proportional to
(\Delta_{\rm inter}/\mu)^{2}
, and, thus, the splitting width of the ZEP of the SDOS and ZBCP is also proportional to
(\Delta_{\rm inter}/\mu)^{2}
.
In the absence of ZBCP, the charge conductance at zero bias is proportional to the area of BFSs, which scales as
(\Delta_{\rm inter}/\mu)^{2}
.
Since the factor
(\Delta_{\rm inter}/\mu)^{2}
is usually very small, the low-energy bulk LDOS may not be an appropriate amount to experimentally study the contribution of BFSs.
On the other hand, it can be enhanced in materials with very small normal-state Fermi surface, such as the iron-based superconductor
Fe
(
Se
,

S
)

Sato et al. 2018
;
Hanaguri et al. 2018
;
Mizukami et al. 2023
.

There are several remaining problems. Recently, the relevance of odd-frequency pairing and BFSs are discussed
Kim et al. 2021
;
Dutta et al. 2021
;
Miki et al. 2021
.
It is reasonable that the odd-frequency pair amplitude exists even in the bulk state since a gapless superconducting state is realized.
On the other hand, it is known that an even-frequency superconductor has a nonzero odd-frequency pairing amplitude at an interface or a surface due to the breaking of the translational symmetry
Tanaka and Golubov 2007
;
Tanaka et al. 2007a
;
Tanaka et al. 2007b
;
Tanaka et al. 2012
.
Especially, in the presence of zero energy SABS, the magnitude of the odd frequency pairing at the interface is amplified.
It is an interesting future work to analyze the obtained results focusing on the symmetry of the Cooper pair including odd-frequency pairings
and resulting anomalous proximity effect
Tanaka and Golubov 2007
;
Tanaka and Kashiwaya 2004
;
Tanaka et al. 2005
.

Another possible direction is to study the effect of a magnetic field on the SABSs.
Under an applied magnetic field, the Doppler shift of quasiparticle energy spectra occurs, resulting in the splitting of the ZBCP in spin-singlet
d
-wave superconductors
Covington et al. 1997
;
Fogelström et al. 1997
, which can be understood as the consequence of the emergent BFSs due to the TRS-symmetry-breaking external magnetic field
Setty et al. 2020b
;
Zhu et al. 2021
.
The splitting of the ZBCP under an external magnetic field is also discussed in 3D chiral superconductors and 3D topological insulator/superconductor hybrid systems
Tamura et al. 2017
;
Burset et al. 2015
.
Since the magnetic field is also expected to change the volume of the BFSs in a multi-band system, tunneling spectroscopy under a magnetic field may provide a way to detect the effects of BFSs.
On the other hand, there is an argument that a large BFS induces instability of superconductivity
Agterberg et al. 2017
;
Menke et al. 2019
;
Bhattacharya and Timm 2023
, and whether a large BFS can be realized remains an open question.

Appendix A
Conditions for the appearance of BFSs

As a general property, the BdG Hamiltonian possesses particle-hole symmetry:

\displaystyle CH_{-{\bm{k}}}C^{-1}=-H_{\bm{k}}

(39)

where
C=\mathcal{K}\tau_{x}

with
\tau_{i=x,y,z}
being the Pauli matrices in the Nambu space and
\mathcal{K}
being the complex conjugation operator.
Under the assumption
H_{0}({\bm{k}})=H_{0}(-{\bm{k}})
,
the Hamiltonian is also symmetric under inversion:

\displaystyle PH_{-{\bm{k}}}P^{-1}=H_{\bm{k}},

(40)

where
P=\tau_{0}

(
P=\tau_{z}
) for even-parity (odd-parity) pairing states.
Thus, the product of these symmetries satisfies

\displaystyle(CP)H_{\bm{k}}(CP)^{-1}=-H_{\bm{k}}.

(41)

It is generally proved that any
CP
symmetric Hamiltonian
H_{\bm{k}}
can be unitarily transformed into an antisymmetric matrix
\tilde{H}_{\bm{k}}
, if the square of
CP
is unity:
(CP)^{2}=+1
, which is the case for even-parity pairing states
Agterberg et al. 2017
.
To be more concrete, we obtain the antisymmetric matrix,
\tilde{H}_{\bm{k}}=-\tilde{H}_{\bm{k}}^{\rm T}
, by
the unitary transformation
\tilde{H}_{\bm{k}}=\Omega H_{\bm{k}}\Omega^{\dagger}
, where

\displaystyle\Omega=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
-i&i\end{pmatrix}

(42)

is a unitary matrix acting on the Nambu space.
It follows that the eigenspectrum of
H_{\bm{k}}
is characterized by the Pfaffian of
\tilde{H}_{\bm{k}}
,
{\rm Pf}(\tilde{H}_{\bm{k}})
, which is always real owing to the relation
{\rm Pf}(\tilde{H}_{\bm{k}})^{2}={\rm det}H_{\bm{k}}>0

for two-band systems.
In general, when the number of bands is even (odd),
{\rm Pf}(\tilde{H}_{\bm{k}})
is always real (purely imaginary), because
H_{\bm{k}}
always has pairs of positive and negative eigenvalues due to particle-hole symmetry.
Thus, the Brillouin zone is divided into two regions according to the signs of
{\rm Pf}(\tilde{H}_{\bm{k}})
, and zero-energy states appear at the boundary between the two regions.
In the case of a three-dimensional system, zero-energy states appear on a surface. That is the BFS
Agterberg et al. 2017
;
Brydon et al. 2018
.

The discussion above just says that BFSs
may
appear in even-parity pairing states.
Below, we show that BFSs indeed appear when (i) the intra-band pair potential has nodes, and (ii) the inter-band pairing breaks TRS.
For the even-parity pairing state given by Eq. (
7
), the Pfaffian is calculated as

\displaystyle P({\bm{k}})\equiv

\displaystyle\Pf(\tilde{H}_{\bm{k}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\psi_{{\bm{k}},+}\psi_{{\bm{k}},-}-\psi_{{\bm{k}},I}^{2}+{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\xi_{{\bm{k}},+}\xi_{{\bm{k}},-}+{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}^{*}+|\psi_{{\bm{k}},I}|^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\xi_{{\bm{k}},+}^{2}|\psi_{{\bm{k}},-}|^{2}+\xi_{{\bm{k}},-}^{2}|\psi_{{\bm{k}},+}|^{2}

\displaystyle-({\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}^{*}+|\psi_{{\bm{k}},I}|^{2})^{2},

(43)

where
\xi_{{\bm{k}},\pm}=E_{{\bm{k}},\pm}-\mu
.
Suppose that the
+
band has a normal-state Fermi surface,
{\rm FS}_{+}\equiv\{{\bm{k}}|\xi_{{\bm{k}},+}=0\}
, and its intra-band pair potential
\psi_{\bm{k},+}
has a node along a certain direction.
Along the nodal direction, the Pfaffian (
43
) reduces to

\displaystyle\left.P({\bm{k}})\right|_{\psi_{\bm{k},+}=0}=

\displaystyle\left(\xi_{{\bm{k}},+}\xi_{{\bm{k}},-}+{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}^{*}+|\psi_{{\bm{k}},I}|^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-\left|{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}\times{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}^{*}\right|^{2}-4\left|{\rm Re}(\psi_{{\bm{k}},I}^{*}{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\xi_{{\bm{k}},+}^{2}|\psi_{{\bm{k}},-}|^{2}.

(44)

We further assume that the energy scales of the inter-band and intra-band pairings are much smaller than the energy splitting
|E_{{\bm{k}},+}-E_{{\bm{k}},-}|
in the vicinity of
{\rm FS}_{+}
.
Then, there should exist a point
{\bm{k}}_{0}
in the vicinity of
{\rm FS}_{+}
such that the first term of the right hand side of Eq. (
44
) disappears.
The Pfaffian at
{\bm{k}}={\bm{k}}_{0}
is then given by

\displaystyle\left.P({\bm{k}})\right|_{{\bm{k}}={\bm{k}}_{0}}=

\displaystyle-\left|{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}\times{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}^{*}\right|^{2}-4\left|{\rm Re}(\psi_{{\bm{k}},I}^{*}{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{|\psi_{{\bm{k}},-}|^{2}({\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{d}}_{\bm{k}}^{*}+|\psi_{{\bm{k}},I}|^{2})^{2}}{\xi_{{\bm{k}},-}^{2}}.

(45)

From the assumption that
|\xi_{{\bm{k}},-}|\simeq|E_{{\bm{k}},+}-E_{{\bm{k}},-}|
is much larger than the pair potentials,
the last term of Eq. (
45
) is negligible,
and the right-hand-side of Eq. (
45
) becomes negative if the inter-band pairing breaks TRS [see Eq. (
9
)].
On the other hand, far from the normal state Fermi surfaces of both
+
and
-
bands,
the Pfaffian (
43
) becomes positive because of the
(\xi_{{\bm{k}},+}\xi_{{\bm{k}},-})^{2}
term.
Thus, the sign of the Pfaffian changes in the Brillouin zone and BFSs appear.

Appendix D
Generalized Lee-Fisher formula in Nambu space

We derive the Lee-Fisher formula
Lee and Fisher 1981
generalized for the case with internal degrees of freedom in Nambu space.
We consider a normal-state Hamiltonian with generic spin-dependent hopping terms

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm hop}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}\sum_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\left[f_{l+1,{\bm{k}}_{\perp},\lambda}^{\dagger}t_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bm{k}}_{\perp})f_{l,{\bm{k}}_{\perp},\lambda^{\prime}}+\textrm{H. c.}\right],

(67)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}\left[(\bm{f}_{l+1,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}^{\dagger})^{\rm T}\bm{t}({\bm{k}}_{\perp})\bm{f}_{l,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}+\textrm{H. c.}\right],

(68)

where
t_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bm{k}}_{\perp})
is an element of the hopping matrix with
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
being the indices for the internal degrees of freedom.
The second line is just rewriting the first line using the matrix forms.
We can rewrite
\mathcal{H}_{\rm hop}
using the Nambu representation as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm hop}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}\begin{pmatrix}(\bm{f}_{l+1,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}^{\dagger})^{\rm T}&(\bm{f}_{l+1,-{\bm{k}}_{\perp}})^{\rm T}&(\bm{f}_{l,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}^{\dagger})^{\rm T}&(\bm{f}_{l,-{\bm{k}}_{\perp}})^{\rm T}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}&\tau_{3}\bm{T}({\bm{k}}_{\perp})\\[5.69054pt]
\bm{\tau}_{3}\bm{T}^{\dagger}({\bm{k}}_{\perp})&\bm{0}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{f}_{l+1,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}\\
\bm{f}_{l+1,-{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}^{\dagger}\\
\bm{f}_{l,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}\\
\bm{f}_{l,-{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix},

(69)

where
\bm{T}({\bm{k}}_{\perp})
is given by Eq. (
36
).
Here,
\tau_{3}
in front of
\bm{T}(\bm{k}_{\perp})
and
\bm{T}^{\dagger}(\bm{k}_{\perp})
comes from the Fermi statistics.
The current operator
J
is defined from the equation of continuity:

\displaystyle-|e|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sum_{{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}\left(\bm{f}_{l,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}^{\dagger}\right)^{\rm T}\bm{f}_{l,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}+\frac{J(l)-J(l-1)}{a}=0,

(70)

where
e<0
is the elementary charge and
a
is the lattice constant.
Considering the fact that the current comes from the hopping Hamiltonian,
the current operator at site
l
is given by

\displaystyle J(l)=

\displaystyle\frac{|e|a}{i\hbar}\sum_{{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}\left[(\bm{f}_{l+1,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}^{\dagger})^{\rm T}\bm{t}({\bm{k}}_{\perp})\bm{f}_{l,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}-\textrm{H. c.}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|e|a}{i2\hbar}\sum_{{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}\begin{pmatrix}(\bm{f}_{l+1,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}^{\dagger})^{\rm T}&(\bm{f}_{l+1,-{\bm{k}}_{\perp}})^{\rm T}&(\bm{f}_{l,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}^{\dagger})^{\rm T}&(\bm{f}_{l,-{\bm{k}}_{\perp}})^{\rm T}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}&\bm{T}({\bm{k}}_{\perp})\\[5.69054pt]
-\bm{T}^{\dagger}({\bm{k}}_{\perp})&\bm{0}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{f}_{l+1,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}\\
\bm{f}_{l+1,-{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}^{\dagger}\\
\bm{f}_{l,{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}\\
\bm{f}_{l,-{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix},

(71)

which indicates that
\bm{T}({\bm{k}}_{\perp})
is the hopping matrix for the current operator in the Nambu description.
Using this
\bm{T}({\bm{k}}_{\perp})
and following the procedure to derive the Lee-Fisher formula
Lee and Fisher 1981
, see, e.g., Appendix 1 of Ref.
Inoue et al. 2016
for details, we obtain Eq. (
34
).

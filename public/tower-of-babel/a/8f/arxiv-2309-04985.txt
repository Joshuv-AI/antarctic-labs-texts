Equation of spin motion for a particle with electric and magnetic charges and dipole moments
Alexander J. Silenko

Abstract
The general classical equation of spin motion is rigorously derived for a particle with electric and magnetic charges and dipole moments in electromagnetic fields. The equation describing the spin motion relative to the momentum direction in storage rings is also obtained. The importance of the derivation follows from a possible appearance of magnetic charges and EDMs due to the pseudoscalar CP -violating electromagnetic interaction caused by dark matter axions.

The equation of spin motion in electromagnetic fields for a charged (or uncharged) particle with electric and magnetic dipole moments generalizing the well-known Thomas-Bargmann-Michel-Telegdi equation has the form (see Refs.

+\left.\frac{\eta}{2}\left({\bm{E}}-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}(\bm{\beta}\cdot{\bm{E}})\bm{\beta}+\bm{\beta}\times{\bm{B}}\right)\right].\end{array}

(1)

Here
\displaystyle G=(g-2)/2,~g=2mc\mu/(es)

\displaystyle\eta=2mcd/(es)

\displaystyle s
is the spin number,
\displaystyle\bm{\beta}=\bm{v}/c

The relativistic equation of spin motion has the general form

\frac{da^{\mu}}{d\tau}=A_{1}F^{\mu\nu}a_{\nu}+A_{2}u^{\mu}F^{\nu\lambda}u_{\nu}a_{\lambda}+A_{3}\widetilde{F}^{\mu\nu}a_{\nu}+A_{4}u^{\mu}\widetilde{F}^{\nu\lambda}u_{\nu}a_{\lambda}.

(8)

In this frame, the equation of spin motion for the spin

\frac{d\bm{\zeta}}{dt}=2\mu{\bm{\zeta}}\times{\bf B}+2d\bm{\zeta}\times{\bf E}.

(9)

For an arbitrary spin

(10)

Comparing this equation with Eq. (
8

A_{1}=\frac{\mu}{s},\qquad A_{3}=-\frac{d}{s}.

(11)

Values of

We should be rather careful. Interactions of the electric charge and dipole with the electric field and the magnetic charge and dipole with the magnetic field are very similar. However, Eq. (

Therefore, the normal (Dirac) magnetic moment originated from the electric charge has the sign opposite to the sign of the “normal” EDM caused by the magnetic charge. As a result, the connection between the EDM and the magnetic charge should have the form
\displaystyle G^{*}=(g^{*}-2)/2,~g^{*}=-2mcd/(e^{*}s)

Heterogeneous Matrix Factorization: When Features Differ by Datasets
Naichen Shi naichens@umich.edu, Salar Fattahi fattahi@umich.edu, Raed Al Kontar alkontar@umich.edu

Abstract
In myriad statistical applications, data are collected from related but heterogeneous sources. These sources share some commonalities while containing idiosyncratic characteristics. One of the most fundamental challenges in such scenarios is to recover the shared and source-specific factors. Despite the existence of a few heuristic approaches, a generic algorithm with theoretical guarantees has yet to be established. In this paper, we tackle the problem by proposing a method called H eterogeneous M atrix F actorization to separate the shared and unique factors for a class of problems. HMF maintains the orthogonality between the shared and unique factors by leveraging an invariance property in the objective. The algorithm is easy to implement and intrinsically distributed. On the theoretic side, we show that for the square error loss, HMF will converge into the optimal solutions, which are close to the ground truth. HMF can be integrated auto-encoders to learn nonlinear feature mappings. Through a variety of case studies, we showcase HMF’s benefits and applicability in video segmentation, time-series feature extraction, and recommender systems.

Introduction

In the Internet of Things (IoT), data are frequently gathered at the edge devices such as mobile phones or sensors, which often operate under different conditions such as temperature, pressure, or vibration
(
Kontar et al. 2021
)
.This is an example of data collection from a diverse range of related sources. Resultingly, data exhibit both shared features, which represent common knowledge, and unique features, associated with source-specific individual characteristics.

A central challenge in analyzing the data patterns among heterogeneous sources is to decouple the shared and unique features from the observations. A natural approach to handle it is to use mutually orthogonal vectors to model the shared and unique features
(
Lock et al. 2013
;
Yang and Michailidis 2016
;
Zhou et al. 2015
;
Sagonas et al. 2017
;
Gaynanova and Li 2019
;
Park and Lock 2020
;
Liang et al. 2023
)
. Along this line, JIVE
(
Lock et al. 2013
)
uses an alternative minimization algorithm to optimize the joint and individual factors. While JIVE can find meaningful patterns in some genetic applications, the orthogonality is not well-respected in the algorithm. Resultingly, the separation is not ideal, and the estimates of the shared components have limited fidelities
(
Zhou et al. 2015
)
. Several works attempt to improve JIVE by analyzing the singular vectors of observation matrices
(
Zhou et al. 2015
)
, considering more complicated structures
(
Park and Lock 2020
)
, and many more. However, many of these algorithms focus on specific statistical problems and employ heuristic algorithms that do not have a strong convergence guarantee. Distributing these algorithms is also an arduous task.

Very recently, personalized PCA (perPCA)
(
Shi and Kontar 2024
)
has emerged as a promising method for distributedly recovering shared and unique features with strong convergence guarantees. Despite its eminent performance on an array of applications, perPCA is limited to handling symmetric covariance matrices and cannot be readily extended to asymmetric and incomplete observation settings, where only a small subset of data is available.

Hence, there is a need for an extensible algorithm to separate shared and unique factors with a provable convergence guarantee. In this paper, we propose a framework called
H
eterogeneous
M
atrix
F
actorization () to capture the shared and unique factors under the orthogonality constraint. Our formulation is based on nonconvex Matrix Factorization (MF), a widely used technique in data analytics for identifying low-rank structures within high-dimensional matrices. Such low-rank structures can capture underlying physical processes or latent features that are highly informative for understanding patterns in high-dimensional observations
(
Wright and Ma 2022
)
, thus are extremely suitable to represent the shared and unique features among data. Also, the matrix factorization formulation can be extended to an auto-encoder model, which employs the representation power of neural networks.

More specifically, we consider a group of
N
observation matrices
\{\mathbf{M}_{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{N}
from
N
related but heterogeneous sources. We model the common information in these matrices as low-rank components whose columns span the same subspace. Accordingly, the unique signals are modeled by low-rank components with source-specific column subspaces. The common and unique subspaces are orthogonal to represent the inductive bias that the shared and unique features are independent. In nonlinear models, we similarly use shared and source-specific nonlinear embeddings to model common and unique data patterns.

To learn the shared and unique features, exploits an invariance property of the objective to handle the constraints by introducing two correction steps. The application of the invariance property is one of the key distinctions between and previous works, as it allows to maintain orthogonality between shared and unique factors without changing the objective. With the correction step, is proved to converge linearly to an optimal solution under the SE loss with suitable stepsize and initial optimality gap. We also provide an upper bound on the statistical error between the updates and the ground truths.

We use a wide range of numerical experiments to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed . The case studies on video segmentation, temporal signal analysis, and movie recommendation showcase the benefits of extracting shared and unique factors.

Related Work

Matrix Factorization
MF has been applied to a diverse range of fields, including image processing
(
Lee and Seung 1999
)
, time series analysis
(
Yu et al. 2016
)
, and many others, making it one of the most popular methods in data analytics. Numerous works analyze the theoretical properties of first-order algorithms that solve the (asymmetric) matrix factorization problem
\min_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}-\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{T}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}
or its variants
(
Li et al. 2018a
;
Ye and Du 2021
;
Sun and Luo 2016
;
Park et al. 2017
;
Tu et al. 2016
)
. Among them,
(
Sun and Luo 2016
)
analyzes the local landscape of the optimization problem and establishes the local linear convergence of a series of first-order algorithms.
(
Park et al. 2017
;
Ge et al. 2017
)
study the global geometry of the optimization problem.
(
Tu et al. 2016
)
propose the Rectangular Procrustes Flow algorithm that is proved to converge linearly into the ground truth under proper initialization and a balancing regularization of the form
\left\lVert\mathbf{U}^{T}\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}^{T}\mathbf{V}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}
. Despite the abundance of literature on standard matrix factorization and matrix completion, these works do not consider the case where data contain heterogeneous trends.

Distributed matrix factorization
Recent development of edge computation has fueled a trend to move matrix factorization to the edge. Distributed matrix factorization (DMF)
(
Gemulla et al. 2011
)
exploits the distributed gradient descent to factorize large matrices.
(
Chai et al. 2021
)
proposes a cryptographic framework where multiple clients use their local data to collaboratively factorize a matrix without leaking private information to the server. These works use one set of feature matrices
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathbf{V}
to fit data from all clients, and hence, they do not account for source-by-source feature differences either.

Personalized modeling
As discussed, there are a few methods attempting to find the joint and individual components by heuristic algorithms. Besides the works discussed in the introduction section, some works leverage additional structural assumptions in data. Among them, SLIDE
(
Gaynanova and Li 2019
)
analyzes the group sparsity structure. iNMF
(
Yang and Michailidis 2016
)
adds nonnegativity constraints to the recovered matrices. RJIVE
(
Sagonas et al. 2017
)
aims to cope with the gross noise in the observations. However, to our best knowledge, none of these algorithms can guarantee that the iterates will converge into the optimal solutions. Our work improves upon the previous work by providing a flexible formulation for matrix factorization and proposing a first-order algorithm equipped with a convergence guarantee. 3
Model

We consider the setting where
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
noisy observation matrices
\mathbf{M}_{(1)},\mathbf{M}_{(2)},\cdots,\mathbf{M}_{(N)}
are collected from
N
different but related sources. These matrices
\mathbf{M}_{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2,(i)}}
are assumed to have the same number of rows
n_{1}
.

3.1
Linear model

The natural way to model the commonality and uniqueness among the matrices is assuming that each matrix is driven by
r_{1}
shared factors and
r_{2,(i)}
unique factors and also contaminated by noise. More specifically, we consider the generation model for matrix
\mathbf{M}_{(i)}
is defined as

\mathbf{M}_{(i)}={\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{g}{\mathbf{V}^{\star}}_{(i),g}^{T}+{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}{\mathbf{V}^{\star}}_{(i),l}^{T}+{\mathbf{E}^{\star}}_{(i)}

(1)

where
{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times r_{1}}
,
{\mathbf{V}^{\star}}_{(i),g}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2,(i)}\times r_{1}}
,
{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times r_{2,(i)}}
,
{\mathbf{V}^{\star}}_{(i),l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2,(i)}\times r_{2,(i)}}
,
{\mathbf{E}^{\star}}_{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2,(i)}}
. We use
⋆
to denote the ground truth. In the above model,
r_{1}
is the rank of the global (shared) feature matrices, while
r_{2,(i)}
is the rank of local (unique) feature matrices from source
i
. The matrix
{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{g}{\mathbf{V}^{\star}}_{(i),g}^{T}
models the shared low-rank components of the observation matrix, as the column space is the same across different sources. The matrix
{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}{\mathbf{V}^{\star}}_{(i),l}^{T}
models the unique low-rank part. The rationale of the low-rank matrix factorization is to use a small number of latent vectors to explain the observations. Thus the ranks
r_{1}
and
r_{2,(i)}
are often much smaller than matrix dimensions.
{\mathbf{E}^{\star}}_{(i)}
models the noise from source
i
.

In matrix factorization problems, the representations
{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{g}
and
{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}
often correspond to latent data features. For better interpretability, it is often desirable to have the underlying features disentangled so that each feature can vary independently of others
(
Higgins et al. 2017
)
. Under this rationale, we consider the model where shared and unique factors are orthogonal,

{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{g}^{T}{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}=0,\ \forall i\in[N]

(2)

We use
[N]
to denote the set
\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
. The orthogonality of features implies that the shared and unique features span different subspaces, thus describing different patterns in the observation. The orthogonality condition is consistent with literature
(
Lock et al. 2013
;
Zhou et al. 2015
)
.

Under the data generation model (
1
) and (
2
), our goal is to find
\mathbf{U}_{g},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\}
from observations
\{\mathbf{M}_{(i)}\}
. To achieve this goal, we propose a generic constrained optimization problem.

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})

(3)

\displaystyle\text{such that }\mathbf{U}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}=0,\ \forall i\in[N]

where
\mathbf{x}=\left(\mathbf{U}_{g},\{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\}_{i=1}^{N}\right)
collects the decision variables and
\tilde{f}_{i}
is a regularized empirical risk consisting of two parts.

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})

(4)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\ell\left(\mathbf{M}_{(i)},\mathbf{U}_{g}\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{T}\right)}_{f_{i}}+\underbrace{\frac{\beta}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}_{g_{i}}

We will explain them respectively. Term
f_{i}
is the empirical risk between the sum of shared and unique signals and the observation matrix
\mathbf{M}_{(i)}
.
\ell
is a loss metric that can incorporate a wide range of applications. For example, in matrix factorization, the squared error loss is
\ell^{se}\left(\mathbf{M}_{(i)},\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{(i)}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}_{(i)}-\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}
, i.e., the square error between the observed and reconstructed matrices. In matrix completion, only a limited number of entries are observed. We use
\Omega_{(i)}
to denote the set of indices of the observed entries,
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega_{(i)}}\left(\cdot\right)
to denote the projection

[\mathcal{P}_{\Omega_{(i)}}\left(\mathbf{M}\right)]_{j,k}=\left\{\begin{aligned} [\mathbf{M}]_{j,k}&\quad\text{if entry }(j,k)\in\Omega_{(i)}\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

for a general matrix
\mathbf{M}
. Then the projected square error loss
\ell^{pse}
is defined as
\ell^{pse}\left(\mathbf{M}_{(i)},\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{(i)}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\Omega_{(i)}}\left(\mathbf{M}_{(i)}-\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{(i)}\right)\right\rVert_{F}^{2}
. There are also numerous other loss metrics
\ell
, such as Huber loss used in robust matrix factorization
(
Wang and Fan 2022
)
or cross-entropy loss used in community detection
(
Yang and Leskovec 2013
)
.

The constraints
\mathbf{U}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}=0
reflect our prior belief about the data generation process. Since we consider the model where the true shared and unique features are orthogonal (
2
), it is natural to encode the orthogonality into constraints. Explicitly enforcing the orthogonality constraints is one of the major differences between our work and the previous works including JIVE. As we will see in numerical examples, such constraints can encourage shared and unique features to capture more distinct patterns.

Term
g_{i}
is a regularization term that pushes the
\mathbf{U}
matrices to be orthonormal. A well-known theoretical issue in matrix factorization is that objectives like the term
f_{i}
are homogeneous with respect to the variables, which in turn leads to the non-smoothness of the loss function.
Regularization terms are added to ensure the smoothness of the loss. Several works on matrix completion (e.g.
(
Park et al. 2017
;
Tu et al. 2016
;
Fattahi and Sojoudi 2020
)
) consider balancing regularization terms of the form
\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{T}\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}
to encourage column factors
\mathbf{U}_{g}
and row factors
\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}
to have similar singular values. Under a similar rationale, we leverage the regularization terms in
g_{i}
to prevent too radical
\mathbf{U}_{g}
and
\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}
’s, as such regularizations are easier to work with in the presence of the constraints
\mathbf{U}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}=0
. Here, the parameter
\beta
controls the strength of the regularization.

Since term
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
are both nonconvex, and the feasible set corresponding to the constraint
\mathbf{U}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}=0
is also nonconvex, the problem (
3
) is nonconvex. In the next section, we will propose a gradient-based and distributed algorithm to solve the nonconvex problem.

3.2
Extension to auto-encoders

The model (
1
) is based on linear embeddings of
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathbf{V}
. As a natural extension, we use auto-encoders
(
Goodfellow et al. 2016
)
to find the nonlinear embeddings in data. If some entries of a matrix
\mathbf{M}
are missing, we use
\mathbf{M}^{obs}
to denote the padded matrix
\mathbf{M}
whose unobserved entries are simply replaced with
0
. For auto-encoders, the feature matrix
\mathbf{U}
is replaced by an encoder network
\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
that maps a column of matrix
\mathbf{M}^{obs}_{(i)}
into an embedding vector with dimension
r
, and the coefficient matrix
\mathbf{V}
is replaced by a decoder neural network
\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{V}}:\mathbb{R}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
that maps an embedding vector with dimension
r
to the reconstructed observation vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We use
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathbf{V}
to denote trainable weights that parametrize the neural networks. To accommodate the heterogeneity of features from
N
different
\mathbf{M}^{obs}_{(i)}
, we also introduce a shared encoder
\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}
and
N
unique encoders
\{\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\}
to generate the embeddings. All encoders have the same architecture but are parametrized by different weights. In analogy to (
3
), we thus propose the following objective for the nonlinear version of ,

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{U}_{g},\{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\},\mathbf{V}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[\ell\Big(\mathbf{M}_{(i)},\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{V}}\Big(\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\left(\mathbf{M}^{obs}_{(i)}\right)+\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\left(\mathbf{M}^{obs}_{(i)}\right)\Big)\Big)+\mathcal{R}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}\right)\Big]

(5)

\displaystyle\text{such that, }\mathcal{F}(\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}([\mathbf{M}^{obs}_{(i)}]_{:,j}))^{T}\mathcal{F}(\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}([\mathbf{M}^{obs}_{(i)}]_{:,j}))=0,\quad\forall j\in[n_{(i)}],\quad i\in[N]

In (
5
), we use a compact notation
\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}(\mathbf{M}^{obs}_{(i)})
to denote the application of
\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}
on each column of
\mathbf{M}^{obs}_{(i)}
.
\ell
is still a loss metric.
\mathcal{R}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}\right)
is a regularization term that prevents the over-fitting of the neural network. In the constraint,
\mathcal{F}\left(\cdot\right)
is the folding operator that folds an
r
-dimensional vector into
k
vectors, each with dimension
r/k
. The constraint
\mathcal{F}(\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}([\mathbf{M}^{obs}_{(i)}]_{:,j}))^{T}\mathcal{F}(\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}([\mathbf{M}^{obs}_{(i)}]_{:,j}))=0
is a “group orthogonalization condition” that requires the
k
folded vectors generated by the shared encoders to be orthogonal to
k
folded vectors generated by the unique encoders for each column of
\mathbf{M}^{obs}_{(i)}
. Such group orthogonalization constraint is a natural extension of the linear constraint in (
3
), and encourages
\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}
and
\texttt{Net}_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}
to encode different embeddings.

We call this formulation heterogeneous auto-encoders (
HAE
).

4
Algorithm

This section will develop the heterogeneous matrix factorization () algorithm and its extenstion to optimize the heterogeneous auto-encoder (
5
).

4.1
Heterogeneous Matrix Factorization

We use
\tilde{f}
to denote the summation of
\tilde{f}_{i}
’s over different sources
i
,

\tilde{f}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{f}_{i}

(6)

The objective
\tilde{f}
is differentiable. Thus to optimize (
3
), one can calculate the gradient of
\tilde{f}_{i}
with respect to its variables
as,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i}=\ell^{\prime}\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}+2\beta\mathbf{U}_{g}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g}-\mathbf{I}\right)\\
&\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}=\ell^{\prime T}\mathbf{U}_{g}\\
&\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}=\ell^{\prime}\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}+2\beta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\left(\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}-\mathbf{I}\right)\\
&\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}=\ell^{\prime T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(7)

where
\ell^{\prime}
denotes the gradient of the empirical loss
\ell
,
\ell^{\prime}=\nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{(i)}}\ell(\mathbf{M}_{(i)},\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{(i)})
.

The constraint
\mathbf{U}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}=0
poses challenges to the optimization. One naive idea is to use projected gradient descent to handle this constraint. However, the projection can not be easily implemented as the feasible region is nonconvex. A few works also introduce infeasible updates when the constraint is complicated, including ADMM
(
Hong and Luo 2017
)
or SQP
(
Curtis and Overton 2012
)
. Such approaches often introduce additional tuning parameters to balance the objective and the constraint.

We take a different route by exploiting a special invariance property
in (
3
). More specifically, for any
\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}\times r_{2,(i)}}
, we can apply the transform
\varphi_{\mathbf{R}}
on
\mathbf{U}_{g}
and
\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathbf{R}}:\left(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right)\mapsto\left(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}+\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\mathbf{R}^{T},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}-\mathbf{U}_{g}\mathbf{R},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right)

without changing
f_{i}
:
f_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})=f_{i}\left(\varphi_{\mathbf{R}}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right)\right)
.

The invariance property is a result of the special bi-linear structure in (
4
). One can use such invariance to ensure the feasibility of the iterations. By choosing
\mathbf{R}=\left(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{U}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}
, the transform
\varphi_{\mathbf{R}}
automatically corrects
\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}
and
\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}
, such that
\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}
is orthogonal to
\mathbf{U}_{g}
, without changing
f_{i}
.
Based on this fact, we can propose an iterative algorithm. In each epoch, we use
\varphi_{\mathbf{R}}
to correct the variables to ensure feasibility. Then we use gradient descent on
(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})
to decrease the regularized objective. A pseudo-code is shown in Algorithm

5.1
Square Error

The square error, denoted as
\ell^{se}(\mathbf{M}_{(i)},\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{(i)})=\left\lVert\mathbf{M}_{(i)}-\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}
, measures the sum of the square of the element-wise difference between
\mathbf{M}_{(i)}
and
\hat{\mathbf{M}}_{(i)}
. Setting
\ell
to
\ell^{se}
naturally generates the formulation of matrix factorization. Consequently, we thoroughly investigate the convergence behavior of with respect to the SE.

Before presenting the convergence results, we briefly review the concept of misalignment proposed in
(
Shi and Kontar 2024
)
. Intuitively speaking, misalignment characterizes the avarage “minimal difference” among the subspace spanned by a series of vectors. More formally,

Definition 1
.

(
\theta
-misalignment) We say
\{{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are
\theta
-misaligned if there exists a positive constant
\theta\in(0,1)
such that,
\lambda_{\max}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}}\right)\leq 1-\theta
.

where we define the projection matrix
\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
for a matrix
\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
as
\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}}=\mathbf{U}\left(\mathbf{U}^{T}\mathbf{U}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{U}^{T}
.

Conclusion and Discussion of Limitations

This work proposes that solves a constrained matrix factorization problem to extract shared and unique features from heterogeneous data, and extends the model to auto-encoders. One avenue for future research is to consider scenarios where the rank of feature matrices
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are unknown and must be over-estimated instead
(
Ma and Fattahi 2022
;
Stöger and Soltanolkotabi 2021
)
. Such a setting (also known as overparameterization) has been extensively studied for the classical matrix factorization literature. Another promising direction is to improve the initialization condition for the guaranteed convergence of our proposed algorithm. Although the theoretical guarantee of our algorithm (Theorem
3
) relies on the availability of a good initial point, we hypothesize that this requirement can be relaxed, as the algorithm works well in practice with a small and random initialization.

In each iteration of Algorithm
2
, we calculate the shared and unique embeddings for each source
i
. For two tensors
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{b\times d\times c}
, the projection operator
\text{Proj}:\mathbb{R}^{b\times d\times c}\times\mathbb{R}^{b\times d\times c}\to\mathbb{R}^{b\times d\times c}
outputs another tensor. Its
i,j,k
-th element is defined as,

[\text{Proj}\left(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\right)]_{i,j,k}=[[\mathcal{A}]_{i}\left([\mathcal{A}]_{i}^{T}[\mathcal{A}]_{i}\right)^{-1}[\mathcal{A}]_{i}^{T}[\mathcal{B}]_{i}]_{j,k}

, where we use
[\mathcal{A}]_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times c}
to denote the
i
-th slice of
\mathcal{A}
. Intuitively, the projection operator projects each slice of
\mathcal{B}
onto the subspace spanned by the column vectors of
\mathcal{A}
in the corresponding slice.

The unfolding operator
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
is the inverse of the folding operator
\mathcal{F}
. It can be easily implemented via the
Rearrange
function in pytorch. After the projection step,
\mathcal{F}(\text{Emb}_{(i),l,\tau+1})
is orthogonal to
\mathcal{F}(\text{Emb}_{(i),g,\tau+1})
. Thus, the orthogonality constraint in (
5
) is strictly satisfied.

The folding, projection, and unfolding operations are fully differentiable. Hence, after calculating the regularized loss, we can use auto-differentiation packages to take the gradient of the regularized loss with respect to the parameters of the neural network. The gradients are then passed to the AdamW optimizer to update the parameters. After updating all the parameters, the weights for the decoder and for the shared encoder are averaged.

The algorithm repeats for
R
rounds.

Then we prove the first inequality of Claim

. Notice that,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}}-\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 1-\theta+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}}-\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}}\right\rVert_{F}

From (
14
), we know that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}}-\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert{\mathbf{F}^{\star}}{}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}{\theta\sigma_{\min}^{4}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}}-\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}}-\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert{\mathbf{F}^{\star}}{}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}{\theta\sigma_{\min}^{4}N}}\leq\theta/2

where the last inequality comes from the fact that
\left\lVert\mathbf{E}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\sigma_{\min}\theta^{1.5}\frac{1}{6\sqrt{2}}\frac{\sigma_{\min}}{\sigma_{\max}}
thus
\left\lVert\mathbf{F}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}\leq 2\sigma_{\max}\left\lVert\mathbf{E}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}+\left\lVert\mathbf{E}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq 3\sigma_{\max}\left\lVert\mathbf{E}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\sigma_{\min}^{2}\theta^{1.5}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
.

Next we will show a lower bound on
\sigma_{min}\left(\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g}^{T}+\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),l}^{T}\right)
. A fact is that

\displaystyle\sigma_{min}\left(\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g}^{T}+\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),l}^{T}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{min}\left({\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{g}{\mathbf{V}^{\star}}_{(i),g}^{T}+{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}{\mathbf{V}^{\star}}_{(i),l}^{T}\right)-\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g}^{T}-{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{g}{\mathbf{V}^{\star}}_{(i),g}^{T}\right\rVert-\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),l}^{T}-{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}{\mathbf{V}^{\star}}_{(i),l}^{T}\right\rVert

We know from (
B
) that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g}^{T}-{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{g}{\mathbf{V}^{\star}}_{(i),g}^{T}\right\rVert_{F}+\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),l}^{T}-{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}{\mathbf{V}^{\star}}_{(i),l}^{T}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}}-\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{g}}\right\rVert_{F}\sigma_{\max}+\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}}-\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}}\right\rVert_{F}\sigma_{\max}+2\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}^{\star}}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}

Therefore, by (
14
), the above is bounded by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\max}\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}}-\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{g}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}}-\mathbf{P}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\star}}_{(i),l}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}+2\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}^{\star}}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{\max}\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert{\mathbf{F}^{\star}}{}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}{\theta\sigma_{\min}^{4}}}+2\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}^{\star}}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}^{\star}}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}\left(2+\frac{6\sigma_{\max}^{2}}{\sqrt{\theta}\sigma_{\min}^{2}}\right)\leq\sigma_{\min}\frac{2-\sqrt{3}}{2}

where we applied the condition
\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}^{\star}}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\sigma_{\min}\frac{2-\sqrt{3}}{2}\left(2+\frac{6}{\sqrt{\theta}}\left(\frac{\sigma_{\max}}{\sigma_{\min}}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}
in the last inequality. Thus

\displaystyle\sigma_{min}\left(\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g}^{T}+\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),l}^{T}\right)\geq\sigma_{\min}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}

Also, since
\mathbf{R}_{(i)}
’s correspond to the residuals of the optimal solutions, we have,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{R}_{(i)}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{R}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\mathbf{R}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}^{\star}}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}\leq\sigma_{\min}/2

(16)

where we applied the condition
\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}^{\star}}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\sigma_{\min}\theta\frac{1}{64\sqrt{N}}\frac{\sigma_{\min}}{\sigma_{\max}}
in the last inequality.

As a result, we have
\sigma_{min}^{2}\left(\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g}^{T}+\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),l}^{T}\right)-\left\lVert\mathbf{R}_{(i)}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\hat{\sigma}_{\text{gap}}^{2}
.

Finally, we will prove the third condition in Claim
2
. We can do so by using the condition
\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}^{\star}}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\sigma_{\min}\theta\frac{1}{64\sqrt{N}}\frac{\sigma_{\min}}{\sigma_{\max}}
in (
16
), and considering the fact that
\hat{\sigma}_{\text{gap}}^{2}=\sigma_{\min}^{2}/2
,
\hat{\theta}=\theta/2
, and
\hat{\sigma}_{\max}=2\sigma_{\max}
.

This completes our proof.

\square

In the following, we will always assume Claims
1
and
2
are true and prove our results under the assumptions of Lemma
5
.

The two claims are intuitively understandable. Claim
1
upper bounds the singular values of
\mathbf{M}_{(i)}
’s. This is the standard requirement in matrix factorization. Claim
2
implies
\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}
’s should be
\hat{\theta}
-misaligned for some nonzero
\hat{\theta}
. Also, Claim
2
restricts the norm of
\mathbf{R}_{(i)}
’s to be smaller than a factor of the smallest nonzero singular value of
\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g}^{T}+\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l}\hat{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),l}^{T}
. This requirement is satisfied when the norm of the noise in input
\mathbf{M}_{(i)}
is not too large, i.e.,
\mathbf{M}_{(i)}
is not far from the linear combination of two low-rank matrices.

As introduced in the main paper, we use
\mathcal{B}\left(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\right)
to denote the set of solutions with bounded norms:

\mathcal{B}\left(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\right)=\{\mathbf{U}_{g},\{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}\},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\}\big|\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}\right\rVert,\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\right\rVert\leq\zeta_{1},\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}\right\rVert,\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert\leq\zeta_{2}\}

(17)

The following lemma gives an upper bound on the Lipschitz constant when all iterates are bounded.

Lemma 6

(Lipschitz continuity)
.

In region
\mathcal{B}\left(B_{1},B_{2}\right)
as defined in (
17
),
g_{i}
and
\tilde{f}_{i}
are Lipschitz continuous:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla g_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla g_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}

(18)

\displaystyle\leq L_{g}\sqrt{\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{g}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}

and

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}

(19)

\displaystyle\leq L\sqrt{\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{g}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}

for
\{\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}}\},\{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}\},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}\}\}
,
\{\mathbf{U}_{g},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}\},\{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\}\}\in\mathcal{B}\left(B_{1},B_{2}\right)
, where
L_{g}
and
L
are constants dependent on
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
,
\hat{\sigma}_{\max}
, and
\beta
,

L_{g}=2\beta(3B_{1}^{2}+1)

and,

L=\sqrt{(\hat{\sigma}_{\max}+3B_{1}B_{2})^{2}+B_{1}^{2}B_{2}^{2}+2B_{1}^{4}+B_{2}^{4}+(B_{2}^{2}+2\beta(3B_{1}^{2}+1))^{2}}

Proof.

We will firstly show the Lipschitz continuity of
g_{i}
. We know that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}g_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}g_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l})

\displaystyle=2\beta\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}\left((\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}})^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{I}\right)-2\beta\mathbf{U}_{g}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g}-\mathbf{I}\right)

Therefore by some applications of triangle inequalities, we have,

\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}g_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}g_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{g}\right\rVert 2\beta(3B_{1}^{2}+1)

This proves inequality (
18
). The Lipschitz continuity of
\tilde{f}_{i}
can be proved similarly. We will calculate the gradient of
\tilde{f}_{i}
over each variable, and bound the norm of the difference of the gradients.

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}})^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})^{T}-\mathbf{M}_{(i)}\right)\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}}+2\beta\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}((\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}})^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{I})

\displaystyle-\left(\mathbf{U}_{g}(\mathbf{V}_{(i),g})^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}(\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})^{T}-\mathbf{M}_{(i)}\right)\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}-2\beta\mathbf{U}_{g}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g}-\mathbf{I})

Also by triangle inequalities, when
(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})
and
(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})
are in
\mathcal{B}\left(B_{1},B_{2}\right)
, we have:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq(B_{2}^{2}+2\beta(3B_{1}^{2}+1))\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{g}\right\rVert_{F}+(\hat{\sigma}_{\max}+3B_{1}B_{2})\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle+B_{2}^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}+B_{1}B_{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}

Then on the derivative over
\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}})^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})^{T}-\mathbf{M}_{(i)}\right)^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\left(\mathbf{U}_{g}(\mathbf{V}_{(i),g})^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}(\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})^{T}-\mathbf{M}_{(i)}\right)^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g}

Thus by similar calculations, we have,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq(\hat{\sigma}_{\max}+3B_{1}B_{2})\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{g}\right\rVert_{F}+B_{1}^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle+B_{1}B_{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}+B_{1}^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}

And,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq B_{2}^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{g}\right\rVert_{F}+B_{1}B_{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle+(B_{2}^{2}+2\beta(3B_{1}^{2}+1))\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}+(\hat{\sigma}_{\max}+3B_{1}B_{2})\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}

Also,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq B_{1}B_{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{g}\right\rVert_{F}+B_{1}^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle+(\hat{\sigma}_{\max}+3B_{1}B_{2})\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}+B_{1}^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}

Combining the
4
inequalities, we have:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq L^{2}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{g}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)

where
L
is a constant defined as,

L=\sqrt{(\hat{\sigma}_{\max}+3B_{1}B_{2})^{2}+B_{1}^{2}B_{2}^{2}+2B_{1}^{4}+B_{2}^{4}+(B_{2}^{2}+2\beta(3B_{1}^{2}+1))^{2}}

\square

The Lipschitz continuity allows us to prove the following sufficient descent lemma.

Lemma 7

(Sufficient descent inequality)
.

For
\{\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\},\{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\}\}\in\mathcal{B}\left(B_{1},B_{2}\right)
, if
\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert\leq\frac{3}{4}
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\frac{3}{4}
for every
i
, and the stepsize
\eta
small enough,

\displaystyle\eta\leq

\displaystyle\min\Big\{\frac{1}{10}\Big(2B_{1}B_{2}(2B_{1}B_{2}+\hat{\sigma}_{\max})+2\beta B_{1}^{2}(B_{1}^{2}+1)+\Big(B_{2}(2B_{1}B_{2}+\hat{\sigma}_{\max})

\displaystyle+2\beta B_{1}(B_{1}^{2}+1)\Big)^{2}\Big),\frac{1}{2\left(\frac{L}{2}+272\beta B_{1}^{2}+400L_{g}B_{1}^{2}\right)},1\Big\}

where
L
and
L_{g}
are defined in Lemma
6
, then the iterates of algorithm 1 satisfy the following inequality,

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+1},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+1},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}\})-\tilde{f}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\eta}{2}\left(\left\lVert\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)

Proof.

We first look at the correction step. Notice that the function value
f_{i}
before and after correction is the same:

\displaystyle f_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+1},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+1},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1})

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathbf{M}_{(i)}-\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+1}^{T}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+1}\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}^{T}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Large||\mathbf{M}_{(i)}-\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\left(\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\right)^{-1}\right)^{T}

\displaystyle-\left(\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}\right)\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}^{T}\Large||_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathbf{M}_{(i),\tau+1}-\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}^{T}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}^{T}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=f_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+\frac{1}{2}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1})

For
g_{i}
, we have:

\displaystyle g_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+1})-g_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}g_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}),\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{L_{g}}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

where
L_{g}
is the Lipschitz continuity constant of
g_{i}
.

\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right)+\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\left(\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}}\right)\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\frac{3}{4}
. Thus by Lemma
20
, we have,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{I}-\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{3}\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right)+\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\left(\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}}\right)\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{3}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert+B_{1}^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}}\right\rVert_{F}\right)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{4}{3}B_{1}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert+\left(\frac{4}{3}B_{1}^{2}+B_{1}\right)\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}}\right\rVert_{F}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{32}{9}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert^{2}+2\left(\frac{4}{3}B_{1}^{2}+B_{1}\right)^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

Similar upper bounds hold for
\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}
. Summing them up, we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{32}{9}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert^{2}\right)+

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{4}{3}B_{1}^{2}+B_{1}\right)^{2}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{g}}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{P}^{\star}}_{(i)}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{64}{9\beta}+32\left(\frac{4}{3}B_{1}^{2}+B_{1}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\hat{\theta}\hat{\sigma}_{\text{gap}}^{2}}\right)\phi_{\tau}

We applied Lemma
9
, Lemma
10
, and (
28
) in the last inequality.

\square

We continue to prove the upper bound of the norm of
\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}
and
\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}
introduced in (
21
).

Lemma 12
.

Under the same conditions of Lemma
11
, we have the following,

\displaystyle N\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigg[\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\Bigg]\leq C_{2}\phi_{\tau}

(32)

where

C_{2}=16+\frac{704\hat{\sigma}_{\max}^{2}}{\beta}+352\hat{\sigma}_{\max}^{2}C_{1}+C_{1}

(33)

This lemma presents a full perturbation analysis of
\tilde{f}
. It shows that if the function value is close to optimal, all iterates must also be close to one set of the optimal solutions.

Proof.

We use the decomposition (
27
) to derive upper bounds on the norm of
\Delta\mathbf{V}
’s.

By some algebra, we know that,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}}+\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}}\right)\mathbf{M}_{(i)}-\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}+\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\left(\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)^{-1}-\mathbf{I}\right)\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}

\displaystyle+\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)^{-1}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\mathbf{M}_{(i)}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\left(\left(\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right)^{-1}-\mathbf{I}\right)\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}

\displaystyle+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\left(\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right)^{-1}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\mathbf{M}_{(i)}

We will bound each term above. By triangle inequality, we have,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\left(\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)^{-1}-\mathbf{I}\right)\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)^{-1}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\mathbf{M}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)^{-1}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}\left\lVert\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)^{-1}\right\rVert\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}_{(i)}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{\frac{11}{8}}\hat{\sigma}_{\max}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}+4\sqrt{\frac{11}{8}}\hat{\sigma}_{\max}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}

where we again used Lemma
19
to show that when
\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\frac{3}{4}
, we have
\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)^{-1}\right\rVert\leq 1+3=4
.

A similar bound holds that,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\left(\left(\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right)^{-1}-\mathbf{I}\right)\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\left(\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right)^{-1}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\mathbf{M}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{\frac{11}{8}}\hat{\sigma}_{\max}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}+4\sqrt{\frac{11}{8}}\hat{\sigma}_{\max}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}

Then by Lemma
21
, we have,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}}+\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}}\right)\mathbf{M}_{(i)}-\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle-\Big(4\sqrt{\frac{11}{8}}\hat{\sigma}_{\max}(\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}+\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F})

\displaystyle+4\sqrt{\frac{11}{8}}\hat{\sigma}_{\max}(\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F})\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle-44\hat{\sigma}_{\max}^{2}(\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}^{2})-44\hat{\sigma}_{\max}^{2}(\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle-44\hat{\sigma}_{\max}^{2}(\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}^{2})-44\hat{\sigma}_{\max}^{2}(\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2})

where we used the condition
\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert\leq\frac{3}{4}
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert\leq\frac{3}{4}
in the third inequality.

Summing both sides for
i
from
1
to
N
, and considering Lemma
9
, Lemma
11
, and inequality (
28
), we have,

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq\left(16+\frac{704\hat{\sigma}_{\max}^{2}}{\beta}+352\hat{\sigma}_{\max}^{2}C_{1}\right)\phi_{\tau}

We can complete the proof by adding
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}
on both sides.

\square

Now we can look at the following lemma which shows the gradient is aligned with the direction pointing from current iterate to one set of the optimal solution.

Lemma 13

(Gradient points to an optimal solution)
.

Under the same conditions as Lemma
12
, if additionally
\phi_{\tau}\leq\frac{1}{\left(2\sqrt{2\max\{\hat{\sigma}_{\max}^{2},1\}+4\beta}\right)^{2}C_{2}^{3}}
, the following inequality would hold,

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f},\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Bigg[\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f},\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f},\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rangle

(34)

\displaystyle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f},\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rangle\Bigg]\geq\phi_{\tau}

This lemma shows that the geometry of the problem is benign.

Proof.

Similar to
(
Sun and Luo 2016
)
, we define two notations
a_{i,\tau}
,
b_{i,\tau}
as,

\displaystyle a_{i,\tau}=\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}+\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}

(35)

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}+\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}

and

\displaystyle b_{i,\tau}=\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}+\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}

(36)

Then we can calculate the inner product between the derivatives of
f_{i}
and the difference between iterates and optimal values,

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}f_{i},\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}f_{i},\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}f_{i},\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}}f_{i},\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}-\mathbf{M}_{(i)},a_{i,\tau}+2b_{i,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle a_{i,\tau}+b_{i,\tau}+\mathbf{R}_{(i)},a_{i,\tau}+2b_{i,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\lVert a_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+2\left\lVert b_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+3\left\langle a_{i,\tau},b_{i,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbf{R}_{(i)},a_{i,\tau}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\mathbf{R}_{(i)},b_{i,\tau}\right\rangle

As

\displaystyle 2f_{i}-\left\lVert\mathbf{R}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}-\mathbf{M}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}-\left\lVert\mathbf{R}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert a_{i,\tau}+b_{i,\tau}+\mathbf{R}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}-\left\lVert\mathbf{R}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert a_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert b_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+2\left\langle a_{i,\tau},b_{i,\tau}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\mathbf{R}_{(i)},a_{i,\tau}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\mathbf{R}_{(i)},b_{i,\tau}\right\rangle

We know that,

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}f_{i},\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}f_{i},\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}f_{i},\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}}f_{i},\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2f_{i}+\left\lVert b_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\langle a_{i,\tau},b_{i,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbf{R}_{(i)},a_{i,\tau}\right\rangle-\left\lVert\mathbf{R}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

Similarly, we can calculate the inner product

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}g_{i},\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\beta\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}+\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}\right)

\displaystyle+2\beta\text{Tr}\left(3\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)+2\beta\text{Tr}\left(\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)

Since

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}+\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}+\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left\lVert\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+2\text{Tr}\left(\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}\right)

\displaystyle+2\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)+2\text{Tr}\left(\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

We know that

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}g_{i},\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\beta\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\mathbf{I}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+2\beta\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)+\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

As a result,

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}g_{i},\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}g_{i},\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2g_{i}+2\beta\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right)+2\beta\text{Tr}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}^{T}\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right)

\displaystyle+\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{4}+\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{4}

\displaystyle\geq 2g_{i}-2\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{3}-2\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{3}+\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{4}+\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{4}

Therefore, we can sum up the two inequalities and derive,

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i},\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}-\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}-\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}}\tilde{f}_{i},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}-\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 2\tilde{f}_{i}-\left\lVert\mathbf{R}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert b_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\langle a_{i,\tau},b_{i,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbf{R}_{(i)},a_{i,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-2\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{3}-2\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{3}+\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{4}+\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{4}

We can sum up both sides for
i
from
1
to
N
, considering the KKT condition that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle\mathbf{R}_{(i)},a_{i,\tau}\right\rangle=0
, and the definition of
\phi_{\tau}
that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}2\tilde{f}_{i}-\left\lVert\mathbf{R}_{(i)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=2\phi_{\tau}
, we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i},\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}-\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{g,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}-\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}-\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}}\tilde{f}_{i},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}-\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle\geq 2\phi_{\tau}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[\left\lVert b_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\langle a_{i,\tau},b_{i,\tau}\right\rangle-2\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{3}-2\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{3}+\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{4}+\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{4}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq 2\phi_{\tau}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[-\left\lVert a_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}\left\lVert b_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}-2\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{3}-2\beta\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{3}\Big]

For the higher order terms,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert a_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}\left\lVert b_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert a_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert b_{i,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\hat{\sigma}_{\max}(\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F})+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\hat{\sigma}_{\max}^{2}(\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2})+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\max\{\hat{\sigma}_{\max}^{2},1\}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\ell}B_{1}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{M}_{(i)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{F}+B_{4}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),g}\right\rVert_{F}

And,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\ell}B_{2}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{M}_{(i)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{F}+B_{4}\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}

Also,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\ell}B_{1}\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{M}_{(i)}^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{F}+B_{4}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}

Combining the
4

\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{M}_{(i)}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{F}\leq B_{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}-\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{F}+B_{1}\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{F}+B_{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{F}+B_{1}\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{F}
, we have:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}})-\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq L_{gen}^{2}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{g}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}^{{}^{\prime}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)

where
L_{gen}
is a constant defined as,

L_{gen}=\sqrt{8\left(B_{2}^{2}+B_{1}^{2}\right)L_{\ell}^{2}\max\{B_{1}^{2},B_{2}^{2}\}+2B_{4}^{2}}=O(L_{\ell})

\square

With the established Lipschits continuity of the objective, we can prove the convergence of Algorithm 1. Theorem
18
is a formal statement of such convergence guarantee.

Theorem 18

(Formal version of Theorem 3 in the main paper)
.

Under the following conditions,

1.

There exists conctants
B_{1},B_{2},B_{4}>0
such that the iterates are bounded,
(\mathbf{U}_{g}\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}\}\in\mathcal{B}\left(B_{1},B_{2}\right)
and the gradient norm is alsop upper bounded
\ell^{\prime}\leq B_{4}
.

2.

\ell
is
L_{\ell}
-Lipschitz continuous and lower bounded by a constant.

3.

The constant stepsize
\eta
is upper bounded by
\eta\leq\frac{1}{L_{gen}}=O(\frac{1}{L_{\ell}})
.

then for Algorithm with
\beta=0
, the following holds,

\displaystyle\min_{\tau\in[1,\cdots,T]}\left\lVert\nabla\tilde{f}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\}\right)\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=O\left(\frac{1}{T}\right)

(43)

Proof.

The proof is straightforward given Lemma
17
.
As
\beta=0
, we have,

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+1},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+1},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1})

\displaystyle=\ell\left(\mathbf{M}_{(i)},\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+1}^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+1}\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}^{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\ell\Big(\mathbf{M}_{(i)},\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\left(\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\right)^{-1}\right)^{T}

\displaystyle+\left(\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\left(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}^{T}\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}\right)\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}^{T}\Big)

\displaystyle=\ell\left(\mathbf{M}_{(i)},\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}^{T}+\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}^{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+\frac{1}{2}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1})

Combining this and Lemma
17
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+1},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+1},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1})-\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau})

\displaystyle=\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+\frac{1}{2}},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1})-\tilde{f}_{i}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau})

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i},\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}-\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i},\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{L_{gen}}{2}\Big(\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1}-\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}-\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\left(\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}_{i},\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\frac{\tilde{f}}{N}\right\rangle+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{gen}}{2}\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\frac{\tilde{f}}{N}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)

Summing both side for
i
from
1
to
N

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+1},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+1},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}\})-\tilde{f}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\})

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\left(\left\lVert\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\eta^{2}\frac{L_{gen}}{2}\Big(\left\lVert\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\Big)

Therefore, when

, we have:

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau+1},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau+1},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau+1},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau+1}\})-\tilde{f}(\mathbf{U}_{g,\tau},\{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g,\tau},\mathbf{U}_{(i),l,\tau},\mathbf{V}_{(i),l,\tau}\})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\eta}{2}\left(\left\lVert\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{g}}\tilde{f}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),g}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{U}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_{(i),l}}\tilde{f}_{i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)

Appendix E
Auxiliary Lemmas

This section discusses some helper lemmas useful for our main proofs. These lemmas are mostly derived from basic linear algebra.

Lemma 19
.

For a symmetric matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
, if
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\frac{3}{4}
, we have,

\left\lVert\mathbf{I}-\left(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}\right)^{-1}\right\rVert_{F}\leq 4\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{I}-\left(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}\right)^{-1}\right\rVert_{F}=\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}\right)^{-1}\left(-\mathbf{A}\right)\right\rVert_{F}\leq\left(1-\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}\right)^{-1}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}\leq 4\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}

\square

The next lemma presents a similar result.

Lemma 20
.

For a symmetric matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
, if
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\frac{3}{4}
, we have,

\left\lVert\mathbf{I}-\left(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\frac{4\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}}{3}

Proof.

Since
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\frac{3}{4}<1
, we can use the series

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{I}-\left(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{F}=\left\lVert\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2n-1)!!(-1)^{n}}{2^{n}n!}\mathbf{A}^{n}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2n-1)!!(-1)^{n}}{2^{n}n!}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}^{n}=\left(1-\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}}{\sqrt{1-\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}}\left(\sqrt{1-\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}}+1\right)}\leq\frac{4\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}}{3}

\square

Lemma 21
.

For two matrices
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
, we have,

\left\lVert\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}-\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+2\left\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+2\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}-\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}

\square

Lemma 22
.

For two matrices
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
, if
A
is invertable and
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\rVert\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert<1
, we have,

\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}\right)^{-1}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\rVert+\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\rVert^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert}{1-\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\rVert\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}\right)^{-1}-\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\rVert=\left\lVert\left(\left(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right)^{-1}-\mathbf{I}\right)\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\rVert\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\rVert\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert}{1-\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\rVert\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert}.

The proof is completed by invoking triangle inequality.

\square

The following lemma is a well-known result and provides an upper bound on the norm of product matrices.

Lemma 23
.

For two matrices
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}

\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{F}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{F}

and

\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{2}

Proof.

The proof is straightforward and can be found in
(
Sun and Luo 2016
)
.

\square

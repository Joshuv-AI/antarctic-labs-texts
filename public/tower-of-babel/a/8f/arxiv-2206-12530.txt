Backward Stochastic Differential Equations and Backward Stochastic Volterra Integral Equations with Anticipating Generators
Hanxiao Wang, Jiongmin Yong, Chao Zhou

Abstract




(
This paper is dedicated to Professor Alain Bensoussan on the occasion of his 80th birthday
)

Abstract.
For a backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE, for short), when the generator is not progressively measurable, it might not admit adapted solutions, shown by an example. However, for backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (BSVIEs, for short), the generators are allowed to be anticipating. This gives, among other things, an essential difference between BSDEs and BSVIEs. Under some proper conditions, the well-posedness of such kind of BSVIEs is established. Further, the results are extended to path-dependent BSVIEs, in which the generators can depend on the future paths of unknown processes. An additional finding is that for path-dependent BSVIEs, in general, the situation of anticipating generators is not avoidable and
the adaptedness condition similar to that imposed for anticipated BSDEs by Peng–Yang
[
22
]

is not necessary.

Keywords.
backward stochastic Volterra integral equation, backward stochastic differential equation, anticipating generator,
path-dependence.

AMS subject classifications.
60H20, 60H10, 45D05, 45G10.

Introduction

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space on which a standard one-dimensional
Brownian motion
W=\{W(t);0\leqslant t<\infty\}
is defined.
The augmented natural filtration of
W
is denoted by
\mathbb{F}=\{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
.
Let
T>0
be a fixed time horizon. We denote

L^{p}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})=\big\{\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}~|~\xi\hbox{ is ${\cal F}_{T}$-measurable, }\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{p}<\infty\big\}.

For any given
\xi\in L^{p}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p>1
, let us consider the following backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE, for short) in its integral form:

Y(t)=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}g(s,Y(s),Z(s))ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(s)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

(1.1)

where
g:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a given
{\cal B}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\otimes{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable map, which is called the
generator
.
Any pair
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
of
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes satisfying (
1.1
) in the Itô’s sense is called an
adapted solution
. To guarantee the well-posedness of (
1.1
), the following standard conditions are normally assumed.

(H1)
p
The process
s\mapsto g(s,y,z)
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable
on
[0,T]
, for all
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The map
(y,z)\mapsto g(s,y,z)

is uniformly Lipschitz continuous, and

\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|g(s,0,0)|ds\Big)^{p}<\infty.

In the above, the uniformly Lipschitz condition can be relaxed a little. For
convenience, if
s\mapsto g(s,y,z)
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable, we refer
to
g(\cdot)
as an
adapted generator
. If
s\mapsto g(s,y,z)
is merely

\mathscr{F}_{T}
-measurable, we refer to it as an
anticipating generator
. The following
result is by now standard (see Pardoux–Peng
[
21
]
and El Karoui–Peng–Quenez
[
6
]
, for example).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(H1)
p
hold with
p>1
. Then, for any

\xi\in L^{p}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, BSDE (
1.1
) admits a unique adapted solution

(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
and the following holds:

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y(t)|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big[|\xi|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|g(s,0,0)|ds\Big)^{p}\Big].

(1.2)

Hereafter,
K>0
stands for a generic constant which could be different from line to
line. Moreover, if
\bar{g}(\cdot)
is another generator satisfying
(H1)
p
as well,
and
\bar{\xi}\in L^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then by letting
(\bar{Y}(\cdot),\bar{Z}(\cdot))

be the corresponding adapted solution, one has the following stability estimate:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y(t)-\bar{Y}(t)|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z(s)-\bar{Z}(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big[|\xi-\bar{\xi}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\big|g(s,Y(s),Z(s))-\bar{g}(s,Y(s),Z(s))\big|ds\Big)^{p}\Big].

(1.3)

We point out that under (H1)
p
, the generator of BSDE (
1.1
) is adapted. Therefore,
it is natural to ask:
Can the generator
g(\cdot)
of BSDEs be anticipating?
From
(
1.1
), one intuitively sees that although the sum of the first two terms on the
right-hand side is
\mathscr{F}_{T}
-measurable, but the last term is an Itô’s integral
over
[t,T]
, which might make the whole sum
\mathbb{F}
-adapted. However, this is not that
simple. Let us present the following simple example.

Example 1.2
.

Consider the following BSDE:

Y(t)=\int_{t}^{T}W(T)ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(s)dW(s),\qquad t\in[0,T].

(1.4)

We see that the above BSDE has the zero terminal state and has the generator:

g(s,y,z)=W(T),\qquad(s,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},

which is anticipating, and independent of
(y,z)
. We claim that BSDE (
1.4
) does not have an adapted solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
. To see this, let us denote

\psi(t)=\int_{t}^{T}W(T)ds=W(T)(T-t),\qquad t\in[0,T].

Clearly,
\psi(\cdot)
is an
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable process (not
\mathbb{F}
-adapted).
Then, (
1.4
) can be rewritten as the following

Y(t)=\psi(t)-\int_{t}^{T}Z(s)dW(s),\qquad t\in[0,T].

(1.5)

Now, we introduce the following equation:

{\cal Y}(t)=\psi(t)-\int_{t}^{T}{\cal Z}(t,s)dW(s),\qquad t\in[0,T],

(1.6)

which is a special form of backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (BSVIEs, for short; see the next section for details). From Yong
[
42
]
, it is known that
such a BSVIE admits a unique adapted solution
({\cal Y}(\cdot),{\cal Z}(\cdot,\cdot))
, which has the explicit form:

({\cal Y}(s),\,{\cal Z}(t,s))=(W(s)(T-s),\,T-t),\qquad 0\leqslant t\leqslant s\leqslant T.

Thus, if (
1.4
) (i.e., (
1.5
)) has an adapted solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
, then we must
have

Z(s)={\cal Z}(t,s)=T-t,\qquad 0\leqslant t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

(1.7)

because the adapted solution of (
1.4
) is also an adapted solution of
BSVIE (
1.6
). Clearly, (
1.7
) cannot be true.

The above example tells us the following: (i) BSDEs with anticipating generators might not have adapted solutions, and they could have adapted solutions within the framework of BSVIEs; (ii) BSVIEs with anticipating generators might have adapted solutions, under some additional conditions. The purpose of this paper is to explore the results of BSVIEs with anticipating generators. We will establish a general theory for such kind of BSVIEs, and beyond.

Besides the mathematical considerations, one might be wondering if there are some
practical motivations for BSVIEs with anticipating generators. We now describe some
possible situations.

Consider a position process
\psi(\cdot)
on the time horizon
[0,T]
, which is

\mathscr{F}_{T}
-measurable (not necessarily
\mathbb{F}
-adapted). As in Yong
[
41
]
and Wang–Sun–Yong
[
29
]
,
one may use a BSVIE to describe the equilibrium dynamic risk measure of

\psi(\cdot)

\psi(\cdot)
.
.
More precisely, if

for some suitable generator

, then

\rho(t,\psi(\cdot))=-Y(t),\qquad t\in[0,T],

is an
equilibrium dynamic risk measure
of position process
\psi(\cdot)
. It is not
hard to understand that the generator

-progressively measurable random field. These cases were covered in the literature; see

is anticipating (or

These random events could include something like default payments, bankruptcy of some relevant companies/institutes, the approval/disapproval of FDA for some medicines, election results, pass/not pass some tax bills, possible in-coming monetary policies, some possible regional wars, to mention a few. The common feature of all these is that they are not

g(t,s,\mathbb{E}[\theta\,|\,{\cal F}_{T}],y,z)
, or simply allow

to be

\mathscr{F}_{T}
-measurable

As a natural extension of BSDEs, BSVIE was initially studied by Lin

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we will recall a standard approach in establishing the well-posedness of BSVIEs with adapted generators, from which, we will see why such a standard method does not apply to the case of anticipating generators.

Recall that in (
1.8
) and (
1.9
),
g(\cdot)
and
\psi(\cdot)
are called the
generator
and the

free term
of the BSVIE. The solution that we are looking for is a pair

(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
of processes satisfying certain conditions. In order to make
the above more precise, let us denote

\Delta[0,T]=\big\{(t,s)\in[0,T]^{2}\bigm|0\leqslant t<s\leqslant T\big\},

which is an upper triangle domain in
[0,T]^{2}
. Note that
\Delta[0,T]
does not contain the diagonal segment
\{(s,s)\bigm|s\in[0,T]\}
. This will allow our BSVIEs to have some singularities along the diagonal segment. We now introduce some spaces. Let
{\cal H}^{p}_{\Delta}[0,T]
be the set of all pairs
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
of processes having the following properties:
Y:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\mathbb{F}
-adapted,
Z:\Delta[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is

{\cal B}(\Delta[0,T])\otimes{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable, for each
t\in[0,T]
,
s\mapsto Z(t,s)
is

\mathbb{F}
-adapted on
[t,T]
, and

\|(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\|_{{\cal H}^{p}_{\Delta}[0,T]}\equiv\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|Y(t)|^{p}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|Z(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}dt\Big]\Big\}^{1\over p}<\infty.

Clearly, the above is a norm under which
{\cal H}^{p}_{\Delta}[0,T]
is a Banach space. Let

{\cal H}^{p}[0,T]
be the set of all pairs
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
of processes having the
following properties:
Y:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\mathbb{F}
-adapted,
Z:[0,T]^{2}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
{\cal B}([0,T]^{2})\otimes{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable, for each
t\in[0,T]
,

s\mapsto Z(t,s)
is
\mathbb{F}
-adapted on
[0,T]
, and

\|(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\|_{{\cal H}^{p}[0,T]}\equiv\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|Y(t)|^{p}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}dt\Big]\Big\}^{1\over p}<\infty.

The above is also a norm under which
{\cal H}^{p}[0,T]
is a Banach space. Note that the difference between
{\cal H}^{p}_{\Delta}[0,T]
and
{\cal H}^{p}[0,T]
is that for any
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
, if it is in the former, then
Z(\cdot\,,\cdot)
is defined only on the upper triangle domain

, whereas if it is in the latter,
Z(\cdot\,,\cdot)
is defined on the square domain
[0,T]^{2}
. Next, let

{\cal M}^{p}[0,T]
be the subspace of

satisfying the following additional condition:

Y(t)=\mathbb{E}[Y(t)]+\int_{0}^{t}Z(t,s)dW(s),\qquad\hbox{\rm a.e.}\,t\in[0,T],~\hbox{\rm a.s.}

(2.1)

Note that for any
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in{\cal M}^{p}[0,T]
, one has

\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}|Z(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}\leqslant K_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}Z(t,s)dW(s)\Big|^{p}=K_{p}\mathbb{E}|Y(t)-\mathbb{E}[Y(t)]|^{p}\leqslant 2^{p-1}K_{p}\mathbb{E}|Y(t)|^{p}.

In the above, the first inequality is called a
martingale moment inequality

(see
7.1
in Appendix), with
K_{p}
only depending on
p>1
, and
K_{2}=1
. Thus, for any
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in{\cal M}^{p}[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\|_{{\cal H}^{p}[0,T]}^{p}=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|Y(t)|^{p}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 2^{(p-2)^{+}\over 2}\|(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\|_{{\cal H}_{\Delta}^{p}[0,T]}^{p}+2^{(p-2)^{+}\over 2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}|Z(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}dt

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 2^{(p-2)^{+}\over 2}\|(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\|_{{\cal H}_{\Delta}^{p}[0,T]}^{p}+2^{p-1+{(p-2)^{+}\over 2}}K_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|Y(t)|^{p}dt

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant K\|(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\|_{{\cal H}_{\Delta}^{p}[0,T]}^{p}\leqslant K\|(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\|_{{\cal H}^{p}[0,T]}^{p}.

(2.2)

Definition 2.1

(i)

Let a pair

3

We now would like to improve the above approach and introduce some additional

generators can be established.
It turns out that the main part of this approach for Type-I and Type-II BSVIEs are essentially the same. Therefore, we directly discuss the case of Type-II BSVIEs, indicating some differences in the process.

\displaystyle=\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}\Big[g_{0}(t,s,y(s),z(t,s),z(s,t))+g_{1}(t,s,y(s),0,z(s,t))\Big]ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{T}\Big[g_{1}(t,s,y(s),Z(t,s),z(s,t))-g_{1}(t,s,y(s),0,z(s,t))\Big]ds

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\qquad t\in[0,T].

(3.12)

The generator is

\widetilde{g}(t,s,\zeta)=g_{1}(t,s,y(s),\zeta,z(s,t))-g_{1}(t,s,y(s),0,z(s,t)),\qquad(t,s,\zeta)\in\Delta[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

with the free term

\widetilde{\psi}(t)=\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}\Big[g_{0}(t,s,y(s),z(t,s),z(s,t))+g_{1}(t,s,y(s),0,z(s,t))\Big]ds.

Clearly,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widetilde{g}(t,s,0)=0,\qquad(t,s)\in\Delta[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle|\widetilde{g}(t,s,\zeta_{1})-\widetilde{g}(t,s,\zeta_{2})|\leqslant L_{z}^{1}(t,s)|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|,\quad(t,s)\in\Delta[0,T],~\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{array}

Thus, BSVIE (
3.12
) admits a unique adapted solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in{\cal H}^{p}_{\Delta}[0,T]
. We naturally extend
Z(\cdot\,,\cdot)
from
\Delta[0,T]
to
[0,T]^{2}
via the following

Y(t)=\mathbb{E}[Y(t)]+\int_{0}^{t}Z(t,s)dW(s),\qquad t\in[0,T].

(3.13)

Then, by (
2.2
) we have
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in{\cal M}^{p}[0,T]
.
This means a solution map
(y(\cdot),z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\mapsto(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
is well-defined from
{\cal M}^{p}[0,T]
to itself.

Now, let
(y_{i}(\cdot),z_{i}(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in{\cal M}^{p}[0,T]
and let
(Y_{i}(\cdot),Z_{i}(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in{\cal M}^{p}[0,T]
be the adapted M-solution to

) with

replaced by
(y_{i}(\cdot),z_{i}(\cdot\,,\cdot))

\displaystyle Y_{1}(t)-Y_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{T}\Big[g_{0}(t,s,y_{1}(s),z_{1}(t,s),z_{1}(s,t))-g_{0}(t,s,y_{2}(s),z_{2}(t,s),z_{2}(s,t))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+g_{1}(t,s,y_{1}(s),Z_{1}(t,s),z_{1}(s,t))-g_{1}(t,s,y_{2}(s),Z_{1}(t,s),z_{2}(s,t))\Big]ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{T}G(t,s)[Z_{1}(t,s)-Z_{2}(t,s)]ds-\int_{t}^{T}[Z_{1}(t,s)-Z_{2}(t,s)]dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

(3.14)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle G(t,s)={1\over|Z_{1}(t,s)-Z_{2}(t,s)|^{2}}\Big[g_{1}(t,s,y_{2}(s),Z_{1}(t,s),z_{2}(s,t))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-g_{1}(t,s,y_{2}(s),Z_{2}(t,s),z_{2}(s,t))\Big][Z_{1}(t,s)-Z_{2}(t,s)]^{\top}{\bf 1}_{\{Z_{1}(t,s)\neq Z_{2}(t,s)\}}.\end{array}

Clearly,

|G(t,s)|\leqslant L_{z}^{1}(t,s),\qquad(t,s)\in\Delta[0,T].

By
7.3
, we see that BSVIE (
3.14
) admits a unique adapted solution

(Y_{1}(\cdot)-Y_{2}(\cdot),Z_{1}(\cdot\,,\cdot)-Z_{2}(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in{\cal M}^{p}[0,T]
. Moreover,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Y_{1}(t)-Y_{2}(t)|^{p}+\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{1}(t,s)-Z_{2}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\leqslant K_{p}^{0}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}\big[g_{0}(t,s,y_{1}(s),z_{1}(t,s),z_{1}(s,t))-g_{0}(t,s,y_{2}(s),z_{2}(t,s),z_{2}(s,t))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+g_{1}(t,s,y_{1}(s),Z_{1}(t,s),z_{1}(s,t))-g_{1}(t,s,y_{2}(s),Z_{1}(t,s),z_{2}(s,t))\big]ds\Big|^{p}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\leqslant K_{p}^{0}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\big[L_{y}(t,s)|y_{1}(s)-y_{2}(s)|+L_{z}^{0}(t,s)|z_{1}(t,s)-z_{2}(t,s)|+L_{\widehat{z}}(t,s)|z_{1}(s,t)-z_{2}(s,t)|\big]ds\Big)^{p}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\leqslant(1+\varepsilon_{0})K_{p}^{0}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L^{0}_{z}(t,s)|z_{1}(t,s)-z_{2}(t,s)|ds\Big)^{p}+K\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)|y_{1}(s)-y_{2}(s)|ds\Big)^{p}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+K\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{\widehat{z}}(t,s)|z_{1}(t,s)-z_{2}(t,s)|ds\Big)^{p},\qquad\hbox{\rm a.e.}\;t\in[0.T].\end{array}

Now, for any

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta pt}|Y_{1}(t)-Y_{2}(t)|^{p}dt+\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta pt}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{1}(t,s)-Z_{2}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}dt\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\leqslant(1+\varepsilon_{0})K_{p}^{0}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta pt}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L^{0}_{z}(t,s)|z_{1}(t,s)-z_{2}(t,s)|ds\Big)^{p}dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+K\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta pt}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)|y_{1}(s)-y_{2}(s)|ds\Big)^{p}dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+K\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta pt}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{\widehat{z}}(t,s)|z_{1}(s,t)-z_{2}(s,t)|ds\Big)^{p}dt.\end{array}

Note that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta pt}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L^{0}_{z}(t,s)|z_{1}(t,s)-z_{2}(t,s)|ds\Big)^{p}dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L^{0}_{z}(t,s)^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}e^{\beta pt}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|z_{1}(t,s)-z_{2}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L^{0}_{z}(t,s)^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta pt}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|z_{1}(t,s)-z_{2}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}dt,\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta pt}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)|y_{1}(s)-y_{2}(s)|ds\Big)^{p}dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}e^{-{\beta p(s-t)\over p-1}}L_{y}(t,s)^{p\over p-1}ds\Big)^{p-1}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta ps}|y_{1}(s)-y_{2}(s)|^{p}ds\Big)dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}e^{-{\beta p(1+\varepsilon)(s-t)\over(p-1)\varepsilon}}ds\Big)^{(p-1)\varepsilon\over 1+\varepsilon}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)^{p(1+\varepsilon)\over p-1}ds\Big)^{p-1\over 1+\varepsilon}dt\,\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta ps}|y_{1}(s)-y_{2}(s)|^{p}ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\Big({(p-1)\varepsilon\over\beta p(1+\varepsilon)}\Big)^{(p-1)\varepsilon\over 1+\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)^{p(1+\varepsilon)\over p-1}ds\Big)^{p-1\over 1+\varepsilon}dt\,\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta ps}|y_{1}(s)-y_{2}(s)|^{p}ds.\end{array}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta pt}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{\widehat{z}}(t,s)|z_{1}(s,t)-z_{2}(s,t)|ds\Big)^{p}dt

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta(t-s)}L_{\widehat{z}}(t,s)e^{\beta s}|z_{1}(s,t)-z_{2}(s,t)|ds\Big)^{p}dt

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\Big({(p-1)\varepsilon\over\beta p(1+\varepsilon)}\Big)^{(p-1)\varepsilon\over 1+\varepsilon}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{\widehat{z}}(t,s)^{p(1+\varepsilon)\over(p-1)}ds\Big)^{(p-1)\over 1+\varepsilon}\Big]\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta ps}|z_{1}(s,t)-z_{2}(s,t)|^{p}dsdt

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\Big({(p-1)\varepsilon\over\beta p(1+\varepsilon)}\Big)^{(p-1)\varepsilon\over 1+\varepsilon}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{\widehat{z}}(t,s)^{p(1+\varepsilon)\over(p-1)}ds\Big)^{(p-1)\over 1+\varepsilon}\Big]\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta pt}\int_{0}^{t}|z_{1}(t,s)-z_{2}(t,s)|^{p}dsdt

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\leqslant(1+\varepsilon_{0})K_{p}^{0}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L^{0}_{z}(t,s)^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\b{p}t}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|z_{1}(t,s)-z_{2}(t,s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\Big({(p-1)\varepsilon\over\beta p(1+\varepsilon)}\Big)^{(p-1)\varepsilon\over 1+\varepsilon}K\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)^{p(1+\varepsilon)\over p-1}ds\Big)^{p-1\over 1+\varepsilon}dt\Big]\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta ps}|y_{1}(s)-y_{2}(s)|^{p}ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\Big({(p-1)\varepsilon\over\beta p(1+\varepsilon)}\Big)^{(p-1)\varepsilon\over 1+\varepsilon}T^{1-{p\over 2}}K\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{\widehat{z}}(t,s)^{p(1+\varepsilon)\over(p-1)}ds\Big)^{(p-1)\over 1+\varepsilon}\Big]\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta pt}|y_{1}(t)-y_{2}(t)|^{p}dt.\end{array}

Remark 3.2
.

Let us revisit

(H4)
p
The map

A closely related work of path-dependent BSVIEs is the following so-called

The main feature of (

at the future time

[

\displaystyle f(s,y(s),y(s+\delta),z(s),z(s+\delta))=\tilde{f}(s,y(s),\mathbb{E}_{s}[y(s+\delta)],z(s),\mathbb{E}_{s}[z(s+\delta)]),

\displaystyle\forall(y(\cdot),z(\cdot))\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T+\delta];\mathbb{R}^{n}))\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T+\delta;\mathbb{R}^{n}),

and

The feasible approach (see

The main reason is that the generator of BSVIEs is allowed to be anticipating,
whereas for BSDEs, the generator must be adapted.

, FBSDE (
5.1

(X^{t}(\cdot),Y^{t}(\cdot),Z^{t}(\cdot))

Then from the fact

X^{t}(T)=x^{t}+\int_{t}^{T}b^{t}(r,Z^{t}(r))dr,

we have

Y^{t}(s)=\xi^{t}x^{t}+\int_{t}^{T}\xi^{t}b^{t}(r,Z^{t}(r))dr+\int_{s}^{T}g^{t}(r,Z^{t}(r))dr-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{t}(r)dW(r),\qquad s\in[t,T].

By taking

in the above, we get

Y^{t}(t)=\xi^{t}x^{t}+\int_{t}^{T}\xi^{t}b^{t}(r,Z^{t}(r))dr+\int_{t}^{T}g^{t}(r,Z^{t}(r))dr-\int_{t}^{T}Z^{t}(r)dW(r),\qquad t\in[0,T].

Thus, the process
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
, defined by

Y(t)\triangleq Y^{t}(t),\quad Z(t,s)\triangleq Z^{t}(s),\qquad(t,s)\in\Delta[0,T],

is a solution to BSVIE (
5.2
).
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Note that (
5.1
) is a multi-dimensional coupled FBSDE and the forms of
b^{t}(\cdot),g^{t}(\cdot)
are very general.
Thus, the coefficients
\xi^{t}
,
b^{t}(\cdot)
and
g^{t}(\cdot)
might not satisfy the monotone condition imposed for
coupled FBSDEs in the literature; see,
[
15

40

20

45
]
, for example.
To ensure that (
5.1
) admits a unique solution for any given time horizon
[t,T]\subseteq[0,T]
,
we can impose the small-time assumption, that is,
\|\xi^{t}b^{t}_{z}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}
is controlled by some constant
K_{T}>0
depending on
T
.
On the other hand, by
5.1
, we know that the well-posedness of FBSDE (
5.1
)
is equivalent to that of the BSVIE (
5.2
) with the following generator:

g(t,s,z)\equiv\xi^{t}b^{t}(s,z)+g^{t}(s,z),\quad(t,s,z)\in\Delta[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Clearly,
g(\cdot)
is anticipating, because
\xi^{t}
is
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable.
The corresponding decomposition (see (
3.1
)) is given by

g_{1}(t,s,y,z,\widehat{z})=\mathbb{E}_{s}[\xi^{t}]b^{t}(s,z)+g^{t}(s,z),\quad g_{0}(t,s,y,z,\widehat{z})=\{\xi^{t}-\mathbb{E}_{s}[\xi^{t}]\}b^{t}(s,z).

The corresponding
L_{z}^{0}(\cdot,\cdot)
can be given by
\|\{\xi^{t}-\mathbb{E}_{s}[\xi^{t}]\}b^{t}_{z}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
Thus, (
3.5
) is in some sense equivalent to the small-time assumption for coupled FBSDEs.

6
Conclusion

In conclusion, the BSVIEs with anticipating generators are explored for the first time.
The path-dependent BSVIEs are also introduced and studied.
Under proper conditions, the well-posedness of these kinds of BSVIEs are established.
Some essential differences between BSDEs and BSVIEs are revealed: (i) If the generator is allowed to be anticipating,
BSDEs are ill-posed in general, but they can still have unique adapted solutions within the framework of BSVIEs;
(ii) For anticipated BSDEs, the adaptedness condition is not avoidable, but it is no longer necessary within the framework of
(path-dependent) BSVIEs. In addition, we find that if the generator of BSVIEs is allowed to be anticipating,
it has an interesting connection with coupled FBSDEs.

The following provides a clear path of how a BSVIE is reduced to a BSDE by changing the measurable condition of
the generator.

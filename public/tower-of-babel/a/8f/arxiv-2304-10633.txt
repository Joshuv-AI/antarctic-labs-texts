Using mixed dihedral groups to construct normal Cayley graphs, and a new bipartite 2 2 -arc-transitive graph which is not a Cayley graph
Daniel R. Hawtin

Abstract
A mixed dihedral group is a group H H with two disjoint subgroups X X and Y Y , each elementary abelian of order 2 n 2^{n} , such that H H is generated by X ∪ Y X\cup Y , and H / H ′ ≅ X × Y H/H^{\prime}\cong X\times Y . In this paper we give a sufficient condition such that the automorphism group of the Cayley graph Cay ​ ( H , ( X ∪ Y ) ∖ { 1 } ) \hbox{\rm Cay}(H,(X\cup Y)\setminus\{1\}) is equal to H : A ⁡ ( H , X , Y ) H:A(H,X,Y) , where A ⁡ ( H , X , Y ) A(H,X,Y) is the setwise stabiliser in Aut ⁡ ( H ) \Aut(H) of X ∪ Y X\cup Y . We use this criterion to resolve a questions of Li, Ma and Pan from 2009, by constructing a 2 2 -arc transitive normal cover of order 2 53 2^{53} of the complete bipartite graph 𝐊 16 , 16 {\rm\bf K}_{16,16} and prove that it is not a Cayley graph. Key words: 2 2 -arc-transitive, normal cover, Cayley graph, edge-transitive, mixed dihedral group 2000 Mathematics subject classification: 05C38, 20B25

Introduction

In

1.1
Cayley graphs

Let
G
be a finite group
G
and

. Then

is
inverse-closed

s^{-1}\in S
for all

generates

. For any

\hat{g}:x\mapsto xg\ \mbox{for $x\in G$ and set $\hat{G}=\{\hat{g}\ \mid g\in G\}$.}

Then

is a regular permutation group on

(that is,

, and it is convenient to identify

[

).
Let

\Aut(G,S)=\{\alpha\in\Aut(G):S^{\alpha}=S\}.

(1)

Then the normaliser of

in

is

(see

6
]
). A Cayley graph

is said to be

if
\Aut(\Gamma)
is equal to

(see
[
13
]
), and to be

is transitive on

(see
[

]
). In particular for a normal-edge-transitive Cayley graph

asked for conditions on

contributes to our understanding of these graphs.

Preliminaries

All graphs we consider are finite, connected, simple and undirected.
Let
\Gamma
be a graph. Denote by
V(\Gamma)
,
E(\Gamma)
and
\Aut(\Gamma)
the vertex set, edge set, and full automorphism group of
\Gamma
, respectively. For
v\in V(\Gamma)
, let
\Gamma(v)
denote the set of vertices adjacent to
v
. A graph
\Gamma
is said to be
regular
if there exists an integer
k
such that
|\Gamma(v)|=k
for all vertices
v\in V(\Gamma)
. A graph
\Gamma
is bipartite if
E(\Gamma)\neq\emptyset
and

is of the form
\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime}
such that each edge consists of one vertex from
\Delta
and one vertex from
\Delta^{\prime}
. If
\Gamma
is connected then this vertex partition is uniquely determined and the two parts
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
are often called the

of
\Gamma
.

Let
G\leq\Aut(\Gamma)
. For
v\in V(\Gamma)
, let
G_{v}=\{g\in G\ :\ v^{g}=v\}
, the stabiliser of
v
in
G
. We say that
\Gamma
is
G
-vertex-transitive
or
G
-edge-transitive
if
G
is transitive on
V(\Gamma)
or
E(\Gamma)
, respectively.
When
G=\Aut(\Gamma)
, a
G
-vertex-transitive or
G
-edge-transitive graph
\Gamma
is simply called
vertex-transitive
or
edge-transitive
, respectively. A regular graph
\Gamma
is said to be
G
-locally primitive
or
locally
(G,2)
-arc-transitive
if
G\leq\Aut(\Gamma)
and
G_{v}
is primitive or
2
-transitive on
\Gamma(v)
, respectively, for each
v\in V(\Gamma)
. Similarly, when
G=\Aut(\Gamma)
, a
G
-locally primitive or locally
(G,2)
-arc-transitive graph
\Gamma
is simply called
locally primitive
or
locally
2
-arc-transitive
, respectively.

2.1
Normal quotients and normal covers of graphs

The normal quotient method for investigating vertex- or edge-transitive graphs proceeds as follows.
Assume that
G\leq\Aut(\Gamma)
is such that
\Gamma
is
G
-vertex-transitive or
G
-edge-transitive. Let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
such that
N
is intransitive on
V(\Gamma)
. The
N
-normal quotient graph
of
\Gamma
is defined as the graph
\Gamma_{N}
with vertices the
N
-orbits in

and with two distinct
N
-orbits adjacent if there exists an edge in
\Gamma
consisting of one vertex from each of these orbits. If
\Gamma
is regular, and if
\Gamma_{N}
and

have the same valency, then we say that
\Gamma
is an
N
-normal cover
of
\Gamma_{N}
.

The following lemma is about the normal quotients of locally primitive graphs.

Lemma 2.1

[
4
, Lemma 2.2]

Let
\Gamma
be a connected
G
-locally primitive bipartite graph of valency
k>1
with bipartition
V(\Gamma)=O_{1}\cup O_{2}
, so each
|O_{i}|>1
. Suppose that
N\unlhd G
is such that
N
fixes both
O_{1}
and
O_{2}
setwise, and
N
is intransitive on

and on

. Then

(1)

N
acts semiregularly on
V(\Gamma)
,
N
is the kernel of
G
acting on
V(\Gamma_{N})
and
G/N\leq\Aut(\Gamma_{N})
.

(2)

For
N\leq H\leq G
,
H
is regular on

if and only if

is regular on

.

2.2
Cliques, clique graphs and line graphs

A
clique
of a graph
\Gamma
is a subset
U\subseteq V(\Gamma)
such that every pair of vertices in
U
forms an
edge of
\Gamma
. A clique
U
is
maximal
if no subset
of
V(\Gamma)
properly containing
U
is a clique. The
clique graph
of
\Gamma
is defined as the graph
\Sigma(\Gamma)
with vertices the maximal cliques of
\Gamma
such that two distinct maximal cliques are adjacent in
\Sigma(\Gamma)
if and only if their intersection is non-empty. Similarly the

of
\Gamma
is defined as the graph
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)
with vertex set
E(\Gamma)
such that two distinct edges

are adjacent in
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)
if and only if
e\cap e^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
.

2.3
Cayley graphs and bi-Cayley graphs

Recall that a group
G
of permutations of a set

is called

if it is transitive, and some (and hence all) stabilisers

are trivial. More generally

is called

As discussed in Section

has a subgroup

with two orbits. The following results from

]
will be useful.

Lemma 2.2

[
4
, Lemma 2.3]

Let

be a connected bi-Cayley graph of a group

such that the two orbits of

on

contain no edges of
\Gamma
and let

. Then, for each

, the stabiliser

acts faithfully on
\Gamma(v)
.

Lemma 2.3

[
4
, Lemma 2.6]

Let
\Gamma
be a connected
(G,2)
-arc-transitive graph, and let

. Suppose that

is an

-normal cover of

, for some normal

-subgroup

\Aut(\Sigma)=\Aut(\Gamma)=\mathcal{H}\rtimes A(\mathcal{H},X,Y)

\Box

Now we complete the proof of Theorem

. Parts (1) and (2) were proved in Lemmas

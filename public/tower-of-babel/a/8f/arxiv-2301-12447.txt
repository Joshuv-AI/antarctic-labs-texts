Sergiy Maksymenko

Abstract
. Let ℱ \mathcal{F} be a Morse-Bott foliation on the solid torus T = S 1 × D 2 T=S^{1}\times D^{2} into 2 2 -tori parallel to the boundary and one singular central circle. Introduction

The paper is devoted to computations of homotopy types of diffeomorphism groups of a solid torus and lens spaces preserving foliations by level sets of ‘‘simplest’’ Morse-Bott functions whose sets of critical points consist of extreme circles only.

Note that the homotopy types of diffeomorphism groups of compact manifolds in dimensions
1,2
are described completely,
[
46
,
7
,
8
,
15
]
; in dimension
3
there is a lot of information, e.g.
[
18
,
13
,
20
]
; while in dimensions
\geq 4
only very specific cases are computed, e.g.
[
40
,
44
,
16
,
6
,
27
,
2
]
.
Computations for the groups of leaf preserving diffeomorphisms of foliations are usually related with perfectness properties of such groups proved independently by T. Rybicki
[
42
]
and T. Tsuboi
[
48
]
, which extended the results by M.-R. Herman
[
19
]
, W. Thurston
[
47
]
, J. Mather
[
37
,
38
]
, D. B. A. Epstein
[
9
]
on simplicity of groups of compactly supported diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity.

For foliations with singularities there is much less information, e.g.
[
11
,
43
,
33
,
28
]
.
On the other hand, the most relevant references to the present paper are results by K. Fukui, S. Ushiki
[
12
]
and K. Fukui
[
10
]
on the homotopy type of regular foliations on
3
-manifolds with finitely many Reeb components.

Just to illustrate what is a difficulty with singular foliations consider a simple example.
Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function,
\mathcal{F}
a partition into the level sets of
f
, and
h=(a,b,c)\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
a diffeomorphism leaving invariant each leaf of
\mathcal{F}
.

First assume
f(x,y,z)=z
.
Then
\mathcal{F}
is a usual ‘‘regular’’ foliation consisting of planes parallel to the
xy
-plane, and
h
should be given by a formula
h(x,y,z)=\bigl(a(x,y,z),b(x,y,z),z\bigr)
, where for each fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}
the map
(x,y)\mapsto\bigl(a(x,y,z),b(x,y,z)\bigr)
is a diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
In other words,
h
can be regarded as a one-parametric family of diffeomorphisms of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, or an isotopy of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and even as a map of
\mathbb{R}
into the group of diffeomorphisms of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
In particular, the information of the homotopy type of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
might be useful in this case.

On the other hand, suppose that
f(x,y,z)=x^{2}+y^{2}
.
In this case
\mathcal{F}
consists of vertical cylinders (being regular leaves) and the
z
-axis (being a singular leaf).
Then the assumption that
h
preserves level sets of
f
means that the coordinate functions of
h
satisfy the following identity:
a(x,y,z)^{2}+b(x,y,z)^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}
.
The problem of describing deformation properties of such diffeomorphisms seems to be much more complicated.

Given smooth manifolds
M,P
there is a natural action
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,P)\times\mathcal{D}(M)\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,P)
,
(f,h)\mapsto f\circ h
, of the group
\mathcal{D}(M)
of diffeomorphisms of
M
on the space of smooth maps
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,P)
, e.g.
[
36
,
45
,
39
]
, see also Section
4
.
Then for each
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,P)
its stabilizer

\mathcal{S}(f)=\{h\in\mathcal{D}(M)\mid f\circ h=f\}

consists of diffeomorphisms leaving invariant each level set of
f
, i.e. preserving a singular foliation by level sets of
f
.

In a series of papers by the author, for the case when
M
is a compact surface and
P
is a one-dimensional manifold, i.e.
P=\mathbb{R}
or
S^{1}
, there were studied a ‘‘large’’ subset
\mathcal{F}(M,P)\subset\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,P)
of maps
f\colon M\to P
with isolated critical points (which includes all Morse maps).
It was shown that for those
f\in\mathcal{F}(M,P)
the path components of the stabilizer
\mathcal{S}(f)
are either contractible or homotopy equivalent to the circle (the latter happens only for few types of maps), see
[
30
,
31
,
34
,
35
]
for details, references, and applications to the homotopy types of the corresponding orbits of such functions, and
[
29
]
for applications to persistent homologies.
However, the technique of those papers is essentially based on the fact that the leaves of those singular foliations are one-dimensional: this allowed to regard them as orbits of some vector fields (at least for oriented
M
).

In a recent series of joint papers by the author with O. Khokhliuk
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
there were developed several techniques for computations of homotopy types of diffeomorphism groups of Morse-Bott and more general classes of ‘‘singular’’ foliations in higher dimensions.
In particular, the previous paper
[
26
]
computes the homotopy types of diffeomorphism groups of leaf preserving diffeomorphisms for mentioned above foliations of the solid torus and lens spaces.
In the present paper we find the relationship between those groups and the groups of diffeomorphisms sending leaves to leaves, see Theorems
2.1
,
2.2
,
7.1
,
8.3

Some definitions and notations

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a partition of a set
M
.
Then a map
h\colon M\to M
is
\mathcal{F}
-foliated
if for each leaf
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
its image
h(\omega)
is a (possibly distinct from
\omega
) leaf of
\mathcal{F}
as well.
Also,
h
is
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving
, if
h(\omega)=\omega
for each leaf
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
.
More generally, if
\mathcal{G}
is a partition of a set
N
, then a map
h\colon M\to N
is
(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})
-foliated
, if for each leaf
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
its image
h(\omega)
is a leaf of
\mathcal{G}
.

All manifolds and their diffeomorphisms are assumed to be of class
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
.
If
M
is a manifold, and
X\subset M
is a subset, then we will denote by
\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F},X)
, (resp.
\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F},X)
), the groups of
\mathcal{F}
-foliated, (resp.,
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving),
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms of
M
endowed with the corresponding strong
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
Whitney topologies.
If
X=\varnothing
, then we will omit it from notation and denote the above groups by
\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F})
and
\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F})
respectively.

Throughout the paper
D^{2}=\{|{z}|\leq 1\}
is the unit disk in the complex plane and
S^{1}=\partial D^{2}
the unit circle.
For an abelian group
A
we will denote by
\mathrm{Dih}(A)
the
dihedral extension of
A
, i.e. the semidirect product
A\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
corresponding to the natural action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
on
A
generated by the isomorphism
o:A\to A
,
o(a)=-a
.
For example,
\mathrm{Dih}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is the dihedral group
\mathbb{D}_{n}
, and
\mathrm{Dih}(\mathrm{SO}(2))=\mathrm{O}(2)
.

2.
Main results

In this section we will formulate two principal results (Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
) concerning ‘‘simplest’’ Morse-Bott foliations on a solid torus and lens spaces.
More detailed statements will be presented throughout the paper.

Solid torus

Let
\mathbf{T}=S^{1}\times D^{2}
be the solid torus.
Consider the following Morse-Bott function
f\colon\mathbf{T}\to\mathbb{R}
,
f({w},{z})=|{z}|^{2}
, and let
\mathcal{F}=\{f^{-1}(t)\mid t\in[0;1]\}
be the partition of
\mathbf{T}
into the level sets of
f
.
Then
f^{-1}(0)=S^{1}\times 0
is the ‘‘central circle’’ of
\mathbf{T}
consisting of all critical points of
f
, and thus being a non-degenerate critical submanifold.
For all other values
t\in(0;1]
,
f^{-1}(t)=S^{1}\times\{|{z}|^{2}=t\}
is a
2
-torus (product of two circles) ‘‘parallel’’ to
\partial\mathbf{T}
.
In particular,
f^{-1}(1)=\partial\mathbf{T}
.

N. Ivanov
[
21
]
proved that the group
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T},\partial\mathbf{T})
of all diffeomorphism of
\mathbf{T}
fixed on the boundary is contractible.
Also in a recent joint paper
[
26
]
of the author with O. Khokhliuk it is computed the weak homotopy type of
\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F})
and shown that all homotopy groups of
\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F},\partial\mathbf{T})
vanish.
One of the main results of this paper accomplishes the results of
[
26
]
with the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

The pair of groups
\bigl(\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F}),\ \mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F},\partial\mathbf{T})\bigr)
is a strong deformation retract of the pair
\bigl(\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F}),\ \mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F},\partial\mathbf{T})\bigr)
.

The proof identifies the groups
\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F})
and
\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F})
with the stabilizers of
f
with respect to the natural ‘‘left-right’’ and ‘‘right’’ actions of diffeomorphism groups of
\mathbf{T}
and
[0;1]
on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbf{T},[0;1])
, see Section
4
, and then uses the author’s result from
[
32
]
claiming that those stabilizers are homotopy equivalent.
The above identification exploits the well-known Whithey theorem on even smooth functions, see Lemma
3.3
.
In fact, it will be established a more general statement concerning smooth fiber-wise definite homogeneous functions of degree
2
on total spaces of vector bundles, see Theorem
5.7
.

Notice that the mapping class group
\pi_{0}\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T})
can be identified with some subgroup
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
, and the natural projection
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T})\to\pi_{0}\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T})\cong\mathcal{T}
admits a section homomorphism
s\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T})
.
As a consequence of results from
[
21
,
26
]
, we obtain, see Theorem
7.1
, that the following inclusions are weak homotopy equivalences:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\ \lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\penalty\ s\penalty\ }\ \mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F})\ \subset\ \mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F})\ \subset\ \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T}),

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{id}\}\ \subset\ \mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F},\partial\mathbf{T})\ \subset\ \mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F},\partial\mathbf{T})\ \subset\ \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T},\partial\mathbf{T}).

In particular, the latter groups are weakly contractible.
As an application, we see that every principal
\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F},\partial\mathbf{T})
- or
\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F},\partial\mathbf{T})
-fibration over any CW-complex has a section, and therefore it is trivial.
Moreover, the structure group of every principal
\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F})
- or
\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F})
-fibration reduces to the discrete group
\mathcal{T}
, so the corresponding
\mathcal{T}
-bundle is a covering map.

Lens spaces

Recall that any
3
-manifold
L_{{\xi}}
obtained by gluing two copies of the solid torus
\mathbf{T}
by some diffeomorphism of their boundaries
{\xi}\colon\partial\mathbf{T}\to\partial\mathbf{T}
is called a
lens space
, see e.g.
[
41
,
4
,
3
,
14
,
20
]
for basic properties of lens spaces.

More precisely, a lens space can be defined as follows.
Let
L:=\mathbf{T}\times\{0,1\}
be two copies of
\mathbf{T}
.
Identify each point
(x,0)
with
({\xi}(x),1)
and denote the obtained quotient space by
L_{{\xi}}
.
Let also
{p}\colon L\to L_{{\xi}}
be the corresponding quotient map,
\mathbf{T}_{i}:={p}(\mathbf{T}\times\{i\})
,
i=0,1
, and
C_{i}:=S^{1}\times 0\times i
be the ‘‘central circle’’ of the torus
\mathbf{T}_{i}
.

As just noted above,
\mathbf{T}
has a Morse-Bott foliation
\mathcal{F}
into the central circle and
2
-tori ‘‘parallel’’ to the boundary.
In particular,
\partial\mathbf{T}
is a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
.
Since
{\xi}
identifies
\partial\mathbf{T}\times\{0\}
with
\partial\mathbf{T}\times\{1\}
, it induces the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}}
on
L_{{\xi}}
whose leaves are the images of the corresponding leaves of foliations on those tori.
In particular,
\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}}
has two singular leaves (the central circles)
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
and all other leaves are
2
-tori parallel each other.
We will call this foliation
polar
.
It appears very often in studying lens spaces, due to the effect observed by F. Bonahon
[
3
]
that each of those tori is preserved up to isotopy by diffeomorphisms of
L_{{\xi}}
.

One can also define a Morse-Bott function
g\colon L_{{\xi}}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}}
coincides with the partition of
L_{{\xi}}
into the level sets of
g
.
For example, define the function
\widehat{g}:L\to[0;2]
by

\widehat{g}({w},{z})=\begin{cases}|{z}|^{2},&({w},{z})\in\mathbf{T}\times\{0\},\\
2-|{z}|^{2},&({w},{z})\in\mathbf{T}\times\{1\}.\end{cases}

Then
\widehat{g}
induces a Morse-Bott
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
g\colon L_{{\xi}}\to[0;2]
such that
\widehat{g}=g\circ{p}
and the set of critical points of
g
is a union of two circles
g^{-1}(0)=C_{0}
and
g^{-1}(1)=C_{1}
.
Evidently,
\mathbf{T}_{0}=g^{-1}\bigl([0;1]\bigr)
,
\mathbf{T}_{1}=g^{-1}\bigl([1;2]\bigr)
, so
\partial\mathbf{T}_{0}=\partial\mathbf{T}_{1}=g^{-1}(1)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})
be the group of
\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}}
-foliated diffeomorphisms of
L_{{\xi}}
,
\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})
be its (normal) subgroup consisting of
\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}}
-leaf preserving ones, and

\mathcal{D}^{fol}_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})=\{h\in\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})\mid h(C_{i})=C_{i},i=0,1\}

be the another normal subgroup of
\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})
leaving invariant each central circle
C_{i}
.
Then we have the following inclusions:

(2.1)

\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})\ \subset\ \mathcal{D}^{fol}_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})\ \subset\ \mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}}).

Evidently, if
\mathcal{D}^{fol}_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})
does not coincide with all the group
\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})
, then
\mathcal{D}^{fol}_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})
has index
2
in
\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})
.
Our second result is the following:

Theorem 2.2
.

The group
\mathcal{D}^{lp}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})
is a strong deformation retract of
\mathcal{D}^{fol}_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{{\xi}})
.

In fact, we will prove it for a more general class of foliations by level sets of Morse-Bott functions on a closed manifold having only extreme critical submanifolds, see Theorem
6.7
.
Moreover, using previous results we will also deduce homotopy types of all the groups in (
2.1
), see Theorem
8.3
.

.

Preliminaries

Morphisms of topological groups and monoids

Let
j\colon A\to X
be a topological inclusion, i.e. a homeomorphism of
A
onto some subset of
X
.
It will be convenient to say that
A
is a
(strong) deformation retract of
X
with respect to
j
, if so is the image
j(A)
.
We will need the following simple lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1\to A\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \alpha\penalty\ }B\xrightarrow{\penalty\ p\penalty\ }\!\!\!\!\!\to C\to 1
be a short exact sequence of continuous homomorphisms of topological groups in which
\alpha
is a topological embedding.
Suppose there exists a continuous homomorphism
{{\theta}}\colon C\to B
being a section of
p
, i.e.
p\circ{{\theta}}=\mathrm{id}_{C}
.

(1)

Then the map
\zeta\colon B\to A\times C
,
\zeta(b)=\bigl({{\theta}}(p(b^{-1}))\cdot b,\ p(b)\bigr)
, is a
homeomorphism
.

(2)

Suppose that there exists a strong deformation retraction of
C
into the unit
e_{C}
of
C
.
Then
A
is a strong deformation retract of
B
with respect to the inclusion
\alpha
.
Suppose, in addition, that
{{\theta}}(C)
is contained in some subgroup
B^{\prime}\subset B
, and denote
A^{\prime}=\alpha^{-1}(A\cap B^{\prime})
.
Then the pair
(A,A\cap B^{\prime})
is a strong deformation retract of
(B,B^{\prime})
via the inclusion
\alpha
.

Proof.

The first statement is trivial.
Suppose
H\colon C\times[0;1]\to C
is a strong deformation retraction of
C
into
e_{C}
, i.e.
H_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{C}
,
H_{t}(e_{C})=e_{C}
for
t\in[0;1]
, and
H_{1}(C)=\{e_{C}\}
.
Then the map

(3.1)

G\colon B\times[0;1]\to B,\qquad G(b,t)={{\theta}}\bigl(H(p(b^{-1}),1-t))\bigr)\cdot b,

is a strong deformation retraction of
B
onto
A
, so
G_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{B}
,
G_{t}
is fixed on
A
for
t\in[0;1]
, and
G_{1}(B)\subset A
.

Moreover, if
{{\theta}}(C)\subset B^{\prime}
, then (
3.1
) shows that if
b\in B^{\prime}
, then
G(b,t)\in B^{\prime}
as well.
In other words,
B^{\prime}
is invariant under
G
, and thus
G
induces a strong deformation retraction of
B^{\prime}
onto
j(A\cap B^{\prime})
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{{\sigma}}\colon A\to B
be a homomorphism of monoids, so
{{\sigma}}(e_{A})=e_{B}
and
{{\sigma}}(aa^{\prime})={{\sigma}}(a){{\sigma}}(a^{\prime})
for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A
.
Then for every invertible
a\in A
, its image
{{\sigma}}(a)
is invertible in
B
.

Proof.

We have that
e_{B}={{\sigma}}(e_{A})={{\sigma}}(aa^{-1})={{\sigma}}(a){{\sigma}}(a^{-1})
.
Therefore,
({{\sigma}}(a))^{-1}={{\sigma}}(a^{-1})
.
∎

Smooth even functions

We will need the following statement.

Lemma 3.3

(H. Whitney,
[
50
]
)
.

Let
a>0
,
I_{a}=[-a;a]
, and
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(I_{a},\mathbb{R})
be an even function, that is
\gamma(-t)=\gamma(t)
for all
t\in I_{a}
.
Then there exists a unique
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0;a],\mathbb{R})
such that
\gamma(t)=\phi(t^{2})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Moreover, let
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{ev}}(I_{a},\mathbb{R})
be the space of even
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
functions on
I_{a}
.
Then the correspondence
\gamma\to\phi
is an
\mathbb{R}
-linear map
\delta:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{ev}}(I_{a},\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0;a],\mathbb{R})
being continuous between the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
topologies.

Sketch of proof.

Notice that
\phi\colon[0;a]\to\mathbb{R}
is uniquely defined by
\phi(t)=\gamma(\sqrt{|{z}|})
,
t\in[0;a]
.
Such formula implies that
\phi
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
only for
t\not=0
and the main difficulty was to show that
\phi
is in fact
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
near
0
as well.
Smoothness of
\phi
is proved by Whitney, and we need to derive the second statement about continuity of
\delta
.

Uniqueness of
\phi
easily implies that
\delta
is an
\mathbb{R}
-linear map.
Hence, it suffices to verify continuity of
\delta
at the zero function
0
only.
One easily checks by induction that the identity
\gamma(t)=\phi(t^{2})
implies that for every for
r\geq 1
there exists some constant
A_{r}>0
depending only on
r
such that:

\sup\limits_{t\in[0;a]}\bigl|\tfrac{d^{r}\!\phi}{dt^{i}}(t)\bigr|\leq A_{r}\sum_{i=0}^{r+1}\sup\limits_{t\in I_{a}}\bigl|\tfrac{d^{i}\gamma}{dt^{i}}(t)\bigr|,

This implies, that for each
r\geq 0
the map
\delta
is continuous from
\mathcal{C}^{r+1}
topology of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{ev}}(I_{a},\mathbb{R})
into
\mathcal{C}^{r}
topology of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0;a],\mathbb{R})
.
Hence, it is continuous between
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
topologies.

For example, notice that
\gamma^{\prime}(t)=2t\phi^{\prime}(t^{2})
.
Hence,
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=0
, and thus, by Hadamard lemma,
\gamma^{\prime}(t)=t\delta(t)
, where
\delta(t)=\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}\gamma^{\prime\prime}(st)ds
.
Therefore,

\phi^{\prime}(t^{2})=\tfrac{1}{2}\delta(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}\gamma^{\prime\prime}(st)ds,

and

\sup\limits_{t\in[0;a]}|\phi^{\prime}(t)|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\sup\limits_{t\in I_{a}}|\gamma^{\prime\prime}(t)|.

We leave the other cases
r\geq 2
for the reader.
∎

4.
Stabilizers of functions under actions of diffeomorphism groups

Left-right actions of diffeomorphism groups

Let
M
be a smooth compact manifold.
Then the product
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{D}(M)
of groups of diffeomorphisms naturally acts from the
left
on the space of smooth functions
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
by the following action map, see e.g.
[
39

for detailed discussions and references:

\displaystyle\mu:\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{D}(M)\times\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle\mu({\phi},h,f)={\phi}\circ f\circ h^{-1}.

It is usually referred as
left-right
.
Notice also that
\mathcal{D}(M)=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathcal{D}(M)
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{D}(M)
, and thus we have an induced (still
left
) action

\displaystyle\mu:\mathcal{D}(M)\times\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle\mu(h,f)=f\circ h^{-1},

which will be referred below as
right
1
1

1

In fact, it will become a right action if we define it by
\mu(h,f)=f\circ h
.
However, it will be convenient to use the terms “left-right” and “right” to refer the sides at which we apply the corresponding diffeomorphisms to
f
.
.
In terms of ‘‘arrows’’ these actions ‘‘move down’’ the horizontal arrow
f
:

\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Let

and
X\subset M
be a subset.
Denote by
\mathcal{D}(M,X)
the subgroup of
\mathcal{D}(M)
consisting of diffeomorphisms equal to the identity on

Then one can define the stabilizers of

with respect to the above left-right and right actions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}(f,X)

\displaystyle:=\{({\phi},h)\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{D}(M,X)\mid{\phi}\circ f\circ h^{-1}=f\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f,X)

\displaystyle:=\{h\in\mathcal{D}(M,X)\mid f\circ h^{-1}=f\}.

If
X
is empty, then we will omit it from the notation.

Notice also that we have a canonical inclusion
j\colon\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f,X)\subset\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}(f,X)
,
j(h)=(\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}},h)
, and therefore sometimes we will identify
\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f,X)
with its image
\{\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}}\}\times\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f,X)

Example 4.1
.

Define
{\phi},f,h,q:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by
{\phi}(t)=4t
,
f(x)=x^{2}
,
h(x)=2x
,
q(x)=-x
.
Then the identities
4x^{2}=(2x)^{2}
and
(-x)^{2}=x^{2}
, mean respectively that
{\phi}\circ f=f\circ h
and
f\circ q=f
, so
({\phi},h)\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}(f)
and
q\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f)
.

We will describe now the geometrical meaning of the above right- and left-right stabilizers in terms of level sets of
f
.
Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f^{-1}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be the partition of
M
into the level sets of
f
,
{\phi}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})
, and
h\in\mathcal{D}(M)
.
Then it is easy to see that the following conditions are equivalent:

(R1)

f\circ h^{-1}=f
, so
h\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f)
;

(R2)

f=f\circ h
;

(R3)

h(f^{-1}(t))=f^{-1}(t)
for every

, i.e.
h
is an
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving diffeomorphism.

Similarly, the following conditions are also equivalent:

(LR1)

{\phi}\circ f\circ h^{-1}=f
, so
h\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}(f)
;

(LR2)

{\phi}\circ f=f\circ h
;

(LR3)

h(f^{-1}(t))=f^{-1}({\phi}(t))
for all

In particular, condition
(LR3)
implies that

\mathcal{F}
-foliated diffeomorphism.
However, if

is an
\mathcal{F}
-foliated diffeomorphism, then a priori we can not claim that there exists

such that
({\phi},h^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}(f)
.

Let us clarify the relations between stabilizers and foliated diffeomorphisms.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
and
A:=f(M)\subset\mathbb{R}
be its image.
Then for every
({\phi},h)\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}(f)
, we have that
{\phi}(A)=A
.
Moreover, if
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})
is another diffeomorphism such that
\psi={\phi}

(\psi^{\prime},h)\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}(f)

Proof.

Let
a\in A

Then
{\phi}(a)={\phi}\circ f(x)=f\circ h(x)\in A

{\phi}(A)\subset A
.
Applying the same arguments to the inverse
({\phi}^{-1},h^{-1})=({\phi},h)^{-1}\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}(f)

{\phi}^{-1}(A)\subset A

{\phi}(A)=A

Moreover, if
\psi={\phi}
on

\psi\circ f(x)={\phi}\circ f(x)=f\circ h(x)

Thus,
(\psi,h)\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}(f)
as well.
∎

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
and

Then its image

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p_{1}\colon\mathcal{D}(A)\times\mathcal{D}(M)\to\mathcal{D}(A)

(3)

Condition
{\rm(J)}

In
[
32
]
the author gave wide conditions on
f
under which the inclusion
j\colon\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f)\subset\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img+}(f)
is a homotopy equivalence, see Theorem
4.7
below.
We will briefly recall that result.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function.
Say that
f

has property
(J)
at
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
if there exists a neighborhood
U
of
u
and
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
functions
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
such that

f(x)-f(u)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}f^{\prime}_{x_{i}}(x)\,\alpha_{i}(x)
,
x\in U
.

Example 4.5
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
homogeneous function of order
k
, that is
f(tx)=t^{k}f(x)
for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then, by the well known Euler identity

f=f^{\prime}_{x_{1}}\tfrac{x_{1}}{k}+\cdots+f^{\prime}_{x_{n}}\tfrac{x_{n}}{k},

so
f
has property (J) at the origin
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Equivalently, denote by
\mathcal{C}_{u}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the algebra of germs at
u
of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
functions
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, and for each
f\in\mathcal{C}_{u}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
let
J_{u}(f)
be the ideal in
\mathcal{C}_{u}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
generated by partial derivatives of
f
.
Then property (J) means that the germ at
u
of the function
g(x)=f(x)-f(u)
belongs to
J_{u}(f)
.
The following simple lemma shows that the property (J) does not depend on local coordinates at
u
, and so it is well-defined for functions on manifolds.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})\colon(\mathbb{R}^{m},v)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},u)
be a germ of a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
map, and
h^{*}\colon\mathcal{C}_{u}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{C}_{v}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
h^{*}(f)=f\circ h
, be the induced algebra homomorphism.
Then

J_{v}(h^{*}(f))\subset h^{*}\bigl(J_{u}(f)\bigr).

In particular, if
h
is a diffeomorphism, then
f\in J_{u}(f)
iff
h^{*}(f)\in J_{u}(h^{*}(f))
.

Proof.

Let
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Then for each
k=1,\ldots,m
we have that

\tfrac{\partial(h^{*}(f))}{\partial y_{k}}(y)=\tfrac{\partial(f\circ h)}{\partial y_{k}}(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(h(y))\ \tfrac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial y}(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}h^{*}(f^{\prime}_{x_{i}})(y)\ \tfrac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial y}(y),

i.e. partial derivatives of
h^{*}(f)
are linear combinations with smooth coefficients of the images of partial derivatives
h^{*}(f^{\prime}_{x_{i}})
of
f
.
Hence,
J_{v}(h^{*}(f))\subset h^{*}\bigl(J_{u}(f)\bigr).

∎

Theorem 4.7

(
[
32
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
M
be a smooth connected compact manifold,
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
, and
A=f(M)
be its image.
Suppose that

(a)

f
takes a constant value at each connected component of
\partial M
and has only finitely many critical values;

(b)

f
has property
{\rm(J)}
, see Definition
4.4
, at every critical point
x\in M
.

Then
\mathrm{id}_{A}\times\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f)
is a strong deformation retract of
\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img+}(f)
.

Remarks to the proof.

First note that if
f
is constant, then
A
is a point, and conditions
(a)
and
(b)
are satisfied.
Moreover, in this case
\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f)=\mathcal{D}(M)
, and
\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img+}(f)=\mathrm{id}_{A}\times\mathcal{D}(M)=\mathrm{id}_{A}\times\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f)
, so the statement of the theorem is trivial.

Thus assume that
f
has at least two critical points.
Let
Q=\{a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}\subset A
be all the values of
f
at critical points and boundary components of
M
.
Condition
(a)
implies that
Q
is finite.
Let also
\mathcal{D}(A,Q)
be the subgroup of
\mathcal{D}(A)
fixed on
Q
.
One easily checks that if
(\phi,h)\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img+}(f)
, then
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(A,Q)
, i.e.
p_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img+}(f))\subset\mathcal{D}(A,Q)
.
Moreover, each
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(A,Q)
preserves orientation of
A
, and
\mathcal{D}(A,Q)
is convex in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(A,A)
, and therefore contractible.

The main technical result of
[
32
, Theorem 1.3]
is based on condition
(b)
and claims that there exists a continuous homomorphism
\theta\colon\mathcal{D}(A,Q)\to\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img+}(f)
such that
\phi\circ f=f\circ\theta(\phi)
.
In other words,
\theta
is a section of
p_{1}\colon\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img+}(f)\to\mathcal{D}(A,Q)
, so the statement of this theorem follows from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

5.
Fiberwise homogeneous functions on vector bundles

In this section we prove Theorem
5.7
including Theorem
2.1
as a particular case.

Smooth homogeneous functions

Recall that a continuous function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is
homogeneous
of some degree
k\geq 0
, whenever
f(tv)=t^{k}f(v)
for all
t>0
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The following simple lemma seems to be a classical result.
However, the author did not find precise references, though there are discussions on this question in internet resources, e.g.
[
5
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a homogeneous
\mathcal{C}^{k}
function of integer degree
k\geq 0
.
Then
f
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
.

Proof.

If
k=0
, then the assumption that
f
is continuous and homogeneous of degree
0
, means that
f(tv)=t^{0}f(v)=f(v)
for all
t\geq 0
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In particular, for
t=0
we get that
f(v)=f(0)
for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, so
f
is constant.

For
k>0
, applying
\tfrac{\partial}{\partial v_{i}}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, to both sides of the identity
f(tv)=t^{k}f(v)
, we get
tf^{\prime}_{v_{i}}(tv)=t^{k}f^{\prime}_{v_{i}}(tv)
, whence
f^{\prime}_{v_{i}}(tv)=t^{k-1}f^{\prime}_{v_{i}}(tv)
, i.e. every partial derivative
f^{\prime}_{v_{i}}
is a homogeneous
C^{k-1}
function.
Then, by induction on
k
,
f^{\prime}_{v_{i}}
must be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k-1
.
Hence, by Euler’s identity,
f(v)=\tfrac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}f^{\prime}_{v_{i}}(v)
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
.
∎

We will identify
B
with the image of the zero section of
{p}
in
E
.
A continuous function
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
is called
homogeneous
of degree
k\geq 0
(or
k
-homogeneous
) whenever
f(tx)=t^{k}f(x)
for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in E
.

More generally, let
{p}\colon E\to B
be a smooth vector bundle of rank
n
over a manifold
B
.
Assume that
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
is homogeneous.
Then
B\subset f^{-1}(0)
, however, in general, this inclusion is non-strict.
We will say that
f
is
definite
, whenever
f(x)>0
for all
x\in E\setminus B
.
In particular,
B=f^{-1}(0)
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
{p}\colon E=B\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to B
be a trivial vector bundle, and
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
be a
k
-homogeneous
\mathcal{C}^{r}
function with
k\leq r
.
Then

(5.1)

f(y,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\\
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}=k\end{subarray}}a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}(y)\,v_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots v_{n}^{i_{n}},

where each
a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}\colon B\to\mathbb{R}
is some
\mathcal{C}^{r-k}
function.

Proof.

Notice that
k
-homogeneity of
f
means that for every
(y,u)\in B\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\geq 0
we have that
f(y,tu)=t^{k}f(y,u)
.
Now the proof follows from the Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
homogeneous function of degree
k\geq 2
.
Then
f
satisfies condition
(J)
at each
x\in B
.

Proof.

Due to Lemma
4.6
one can pass to a local trivialization of
{p}
at
x
, and thus assume that
{p}\colon E\to B
is a trivial vector bundle.
Since

, every point of

is critical.
Then by (
5.1
) and ‘‘Euler’s identity with respect to the coordinates

’’ we have that
f(y,v)=\tfrac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}f^{\prime}_{v_{i}}(y,v)
.
Hence,

satisfies property (J) at

.
∎

Stabilizers of homogeneous functions

Let
{p}\colon E\to B
be a smooth vector bundle of rank
n
over a manifold
B
.
Let also
f\colon E\to[0;+\infty)
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function such that
1
is a regular value of
f
, so
\mathbf{T}=f^{-1}([0;1])
is a submanifold of
E
, and
\mathcal{F}=\{f^{-1}(t)\mid t\in[0;1]\}
be the partition of
\mathbf{T}
into level sets of
f
.

The following statement is a particular case of
[
32
, Theorem 1.3]
.
However, the proof is essentially simpler, and it additionally takes to account the behavior of diffeomorphisms on
\partial\mathbf{T}
.

Lemma 5.4

(c.f.
[
32
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
k\geq 1
,
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
be a
k
-homogeneous
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function,
\mathbf{T}=f^{-1}([0;1])
, and
\mathcal{F}=\{f^{-1}(t)\mid t\in[0;1]\}
be the partition of
\mathbf{T}
into level sets of
f
.
Then there exists a homomorphism
{{\theta}}\colon\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])\to\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F},\partial\mathbf{T})
such that
\phi\circ f=f\circ{{\theta}}(h)
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])
.
In particular,
(\phi,{{\theta}}(\phi))\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img}(f)
.

If
\mathbf{T}
is compact, then
{{\theta}}
is continuous, and the pair
\bigl(\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f),\ \mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f,\partial\mathbf{T})\bigr)
is a strong deformation retract of the pair
\bigl(\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img}(f),\ \mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img}(f,\partial\mathbf{T})\bigr)
with respect to the natural inclusion
j\colon\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f)\equiv\{\mathrm{id}_{[0;1]}\}\times\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f)\subset\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img}(f)
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])
, so
\phi\colon[0;1]\to[0;1]
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function such that
\phi(0)=0
,
\phi(1)=1
, and
\phi^{\prime}>0
.
Then by the arguments of Hadamard lemma:

\phi(t)=\smallint\limits_{0}^{t}\phi^{\prime}(u)du=\left|\begin{subarray}{c}\text{replace $u=st$,}\\
\text{then $du=s\,dt$}\end{subarray}\right|=t\underbrace{\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}(st)dt}_{g_{\phi}(t)}=tg_{\phi}(t),

where
g_{\phi}\colon[0;1]\to\mathbb{R}
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
.
Moreover,
g_{\phi}(t)>0
for all
t\in[0;1]
and
g_{\phi}(0)=\phi^{\prime}(0)
.

Define the map

\displaystyle{{\theta}}\colon\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{T}),

\displaystyle{{\theta}}(\phi)(x)=\left[g_{\phi}(f(x))\right]^{1/k}x.

We claim that the image of
{{\theta}}
is contained in
\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F})
, and the induced map
{{\theta}}\colon\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])\to\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F})
is the desired homomorphism.

1)

First we show that
{{\theta}}
is a homomorphism of
monoids
with respect to the natural composition of maps.
Then by Lemma
3.2
it sends invertible elements to invertible, and therefore
{{\theta}}(h)
will be a diffeomorphism of
\mathbf{T}
.

a) Indeed, if
\phi=\mathrm{id}_{[0;1]}
, then
g_{\phi}(t)\equiv 1
, whence
{{\theta}}(\mathrm{id}_{[0;1]})=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbf{T}}
.

b) Furthermore, let
\phi_{0},\phi_{1}\in\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])
be two diffeomorphisms.
Then
\phi_{0}(t)=tg_{0}(t)
and
\phi_{1}(t)=tg_{1}(t)
for unique
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
functions
g_{0},g_{1}\colon[0;1]\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle{{\theta}}(\phi_{0})(x)

\displaystyle=\left[g_{0}(f(x))\right]^{1/k}x,

\displaystyle{{\theta}}(\phi_{1})(x)

\displaystyle=\left[g_{1}(f(x))\right]^{1/k}x.

Hence

\displaystyle{{\theta}}(\phi_{1})\circ{{\theta}}(\phi_{0})(x)

\displaystyle={{\theta}}(\phi_{1})\bigl(\left[g_{0}(f(x))\right]^{1/k}\,x\bigr)

\displaystyle=\left[g_{1}\Bigl(f\bigl(\left[g_{0}(f(x))\right]^{1/k}\,x\bigr)\Bigr)\right]^{1/k}\cdot\left[g_{0}(f(x))\right]^{1/k}\,x=

\displaystyle=\left[g_{1}\bigl[g_{0}(f(x))\cdot f(x)\bigr]\right]^{1/k}\cdot\left[g_{0}(f(x))\right]^{1/k}\,x.

On the other hand,
\phi_{1}\circ\phi_{0}(t)=\phi_{0}(t)\cdot g_{1}(\phi_{0}(t))=t\cdot\underbrace{g_{0}(t)\cdot g_{1}(tg_{0}(t))}_{\bar{g}}
, whence

\displaystyle{{\theta}}\bigl(\phi_{1}\circ\phi_{0}\bigr)(x)

\displaystyle=\left[\bar{g}(f(x))\right]^{1/k}\,x

\displaystyle=\left[g_{0}(f(x))\cdot g_{1}\bigl[f(x)\cdot g_{0}(f(x))\bigr]\right]^{1/k}\,x={{\theta}}(\phi_{1})\circ{{\theta}}(\phi_{0})(x).

2)

Let us verify that
(\phi,{{\theta}}(\phi))\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}(f)
.
Indeed,

f\circ{{\theta}}(\phi)(x)=f\Bigl(\left[g_{\phi}\circ f(x)\right]^{1/k}\,x\Bigr)=(g_{\phi}\circ f(x))\cdot f(x)=\phi\circ f(x).

This implies that
(\phi,{{\theta}}(\phi))\in\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}(f)
, and in particular,
{{\theta}}(\phi)\in\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F})
.

3)

Finally, note that
g_{\phi}(1)=\phi(1)/1=1
, whence if
x\in\partial\mathbf{T}=f^{-1}(1)
, then
{{\theta}}(\phi)(x)=x
, so
{{\theta}}(\phi)
is fixed on
\partial\mathbf{T}
.
Thus,
{{\theta}}(\phi)\in\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F},\partial\mathbf{T})
.

4)

Suppose
\mathbf{T}
is compact.
Then continuity of
{{\theta}}
directly follows from the formulas for
{{\theta}}(h)
.

Consider the following short exact sequence, see (
4.1
):

1\to\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f)\xrightarrow{j}\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img}(f)\xrightarrow{p_{2}}\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])\to 1.

Then the map
\widehat{{{\theta}}}:\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])\to\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img}(f)
,
\widehat{{{\theta}}}(\phi)=(\phi,{{\theta}}(\phi))
, is a continuous section of
p_{1}
and its image is contained in
\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img}(f,\partial\mathbf{T})
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])
is contractible into
\mathrm{id}_{[0;1]}
via the homotopy

\displaystyle H\colon\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])\times[0;1]\to\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1]),

\displaystyle H(\phi,t)=(1-t)\phi+t\mathrm{id}_{[0;1]}.

Then, due to Lemma
3.1
, the pair
(\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f),\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf R}}(f,\partial\mathbf{T}))
is a strong deformation retract of
(\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img}(f),\mathcal{S}_{\text{\sf LR}}^{img}(f,\partial\mathbf{T}))
with respect to the inclusion map
j
.
∎

Foliated maps for homogeneous functions

Let
{p}_{i}\colon E_{i}\to B_{i}
for
i=0,1
be a smooth vector bundle of some rank
n_{i}
over a compact manifold
B_{i}
and
f_{i}\colon E_{i}\to[0;+\infty)
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function such that
1\in\mathbb{R}
is a regular value for
f
.
Then
\mathbf{T}_{i}:=f_{i}^{-1}([0;1])
is a submanifold of
E_{i}
.
Let also

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\{f_{i}^{-1}(t)\mid t\in[0;1]\}

be the partition of
\mathbf{T}_{i}
into the level sets of
f
.

A map
h\colon\mathbf{T}_{0}\to\mathbf{T}_{1}
will be called
(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1})
-foliated
if for each leaf
\omega
of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
its image
h(\omega)
is contained in some leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1})
the subset of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbf{T}_{0},\mathbf{T}_{1})
consisting of
(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1})
-foliated maps.
If
\mathbf{T}_{0}
and
\mathbf{T}_{1}
are diffeomorphic, then we denote by
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1})
the
set
of all
(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1})
-foliated diffeomorphisms.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose the functions
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are
2
-homogeneous and definite.

(1)

Then for each
(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1})
-foliated map
h\colon\mathbf{T}_{0}\to\mathbf{T}_{1}
there exists a unique function
{{\sigma}}(h)\colon[0;1]\to[0;1]
such that
{{\sigma}}(h)\circ f_{0}=f_{1}\circ h
.
Moreover, if
h
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
, then
{{\sigma}}(h)
is also
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
and the correspondence
h\mapsto{{\sigma}}(h)
is a continuous map
{{\sigma}}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1})\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0;1],[0;1])
.

(2)

If
\mathbf{T}_{0}
and
\mathbf{T}_{1}
are diffeomorphic, then
{{\sigma}}(h)\in\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])
is a preserving orientation diffeomorphism of
[0;1]
for each
h\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1})
.

(3)

Suppose
{p}_{0}={p}_{1}
is the same vector bundle and
f_{0}=f_{1}\colon E_{0}=E_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
, so
\mathbf{T}_{0}=\mathbf{T}_{1}
,
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathcal{F}_{1}
, and thus
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1})=\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
.
Then
{{\sigma}}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{0})\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0;1],[0;1])
is a (continuous) homomorphism of monoids.
In particular,
{{\sigma}}(\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F}_{0}))\subset\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])
.

Proof.

(1)

The assumption that
h\colon\mathbf{T}_{0}\to\mathbf{T}_{1}
is
(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1})
-foliated means that for each leaf
A_{t}=f_{0}^{-1}(t)
,
t\in[0;1]
of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
there exist a unique leaf
B_{t^{\prime}}=f_{1}^{-1}(t^{\prime})
such that
h(A_{t})\subset B_{t^{\prime}}
.
Define the function
{\phi}:[0;1]\to[0;1]
by
{\phi}(t)=t^{\prime}
.
Then
{\phi}\circ f_{0}=f_{1}\circ h
, and then we put
{{\sigma}}(h)={\phi}
.
Hence, such
{{\sigma}}(h)
is unique.

Suppose
h
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
.
To prove that
{{\sigma}}(h)
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
as well we will use Whitney Lemma
3.3
.
Fix any point
w\in\partial\mathbf{T}_{0}
, so
f_{0}(w)=1
, and consider the path
\eta\colon[-1;1]\to\mathbf{T}_{0}
given by
\eta(t)=tw
.
Then

(5.2)

f_{0}\circ\eta(t)=f_{0}(tw)=t^{2}f_{0}(w)=t^{2}.

In particular, for each
t\in[-1;1]
the points
\eta(t)
and
\eta(-t)
belong to the same leaf
f_{0}^{-1}(t^{2})
of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
.

Define the following
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\gamma=f_{1}\circ h\circ\eta\colon[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
.
Since
h
sends level sets of
f_{0}
to level sets of
f_{1}
, the points
h(\eta(t))
and
h(\eta(-t))
also belong to the same level set of
f_{1}
, that is

\gamma(-t)=f_{1}\circ h\circ\eta(-t)=f_{1}\circ h\circ\eta(t)=\gamma(t).

Hence,
\gamma
is an even function.
Then, due to Lemma
3.3
, there exists a unique
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
{\phi}:[0;1]\to[0;1]
such that
\gamma(t)={\phi}(t^{2})
.
Thus,

(5.3)

f_{1}\circ h\circ\eta(t)=\gamma(t)={\phi}(t^{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{equ:f_0_eta__t2}}}{{=}}{\phi}\circ f_{0}\circ\eta(t).

We claim that
f_{1}\circ h\equiv{\phi}\circ f_{0}
on all of
\mathbf{T}_{0}
.
Indeed, let
y\in\mathbf{T}_{0}
and
f_{0}(y)=s
for some
s\in[0;1]
.
Then
f_{0}(\eta(\sqrt{s}))=s
as well, so the points
y
and
\eta(\sqrt{s})
belong to the same level set
f_{0}^{-1}(s)
of
f_{0}
.
Hence,
h(y)
and
h(\eta(\sqrt{s}))
also belong to the same level set of
f_{1}
.
Therefore,

(5.4)

f_{1}\circ h(y)=f_{1}\circ h\circ\eta(\sqrt{s})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{equ:fh_phif}}}{{=}}{\phi}\circ f_{0}\circ\eta(\sqrt{s})={\phi}\circ f_{0}(y).

Continuity of the correspondence
h\mapsto{\phi}
also follows from Lemma
3.3
.

(2)

Suppose
h
is a diffeomorphism.
We should show that then
{\phi}
is a preserving orientation diffeomorphism of
[0;1]
.
It suffices to check the following two properties of
\gamma
.

(a)

\gamma^{\prime}(t)>0
for
t>0
;

(b)

\gamma(t)=t^{2}\delta(t)
for some
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\delta:[0;1]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\delta(0)>0
.

Assuming they are proved, let us show that
{\phi}\in\mathcal{D}^{+}([0;1])
.
Indeed, it is evident that
{\phi}(0)=0
and
{\phi}(1)=1
, so we need to verify that
{\phi}^{\prime}>0
.
Since
{\phi}(s)=\gamma(\sqrt{s})
for
s\in[0;1]
, we get from
(a)
that
{\phi}^{\prime}(s)>0
for
s\in(0;1]
.
Furthermore, as
{\phi}(0)=0
, we have by the Hadamard lemma that
{\phi}(s)=s\psi(s)
for some
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\psi\colon[0;1]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\psi(0)={\phi}^{\prime}(0)
.
Then, due to
(b)
,
t^{2}\delta(t)=\gamma(t)={\phi}(t^{2})=t^{2}\psi(t^{2})
, whence
\delta(t)=\psi(t^{2})
, and therefore
{\phi}^{\prime}(0)=\psi(0)=\delta(0)>0
.

Proof of
(a)
.

Note that if
\zeta\colon[a,b]\to\mathbf{T}_{0}
is a smooth path not passing through
B
, then for each
t\in[a;b]
the following conditions are equivalent:

•

t
is a regular point of the function
f_{0}\circ\zeta\colon[a;b]\to\mathbb{R}
;

•

\zeta
is transversal at
t
to the level set
f_{0}^{-1}(f_{0}\circ\zeta(t))
.

Since
f_{0}\circ\eta(t)=t^{2}
, it follows that the function
f_{0}\circ\eta\colon[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
has a unique critical point
t=0
, and thus
\eta
is transversal to all level sets
f_{0}^{-1}(s)
for all
s\in(0;1]
.

On the other hand, as
h
sends level sets of
f_{0}
to level sets of
f_{1}
, the path
h\circ\eta
must also be transversal to the level sets of
f_{1}
for all
t\not=0
.
More precisely, if
\eta
is transversal at some
t\not=0
to the leaf
L=f_{0}^{-1}(t^{2})
, then
h\circ\eta
is transversal at
t
to the leaf

h(L)=f_{1}^{-1}(f_{1}\circ h\circ\eta(t))=f_{1}^{-1}(\gamma(t)).

This implies that
the function
\gamma=f_{1}\circ h\circ\eta
has a unique critical point
t=0
.

Moreover, since
\gamma(-1)=\gamma(1)=1
and
\gamma(0)=0
, we see that
\gamma
decreases on
[-1,0)
and increases on
(0;1]
.
In particular,
\gamma^{\prime}(t)<0
for
t<0
and
\gamma^{\prime}(t)>0
for
t>0
.

Proof of
(b)
.

Let
q_{0}:U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to U
and
q_{1}:V\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to V
be vector bundle trivializations of
{p}
over open neighborhood
U
of
\eta(0)
and
V
of
h(\eta(0))
respectively.
Then
h
has local representation as an embedding
h=(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})\colon U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\supset W\to V\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
of some open neighborhood
W
of
\eta(0)
in
U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
h_{0}:W\to V
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
map, and each
h_{i}:W\to\mathbb{R}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function.

One can assume that the image of
\eta
is contained in
W
, and
w=(x,\bar{u})\in U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
are the coordinates of
w
, so
\eta(t)=(x,t\bar{u})
.

Then by Corollary
5.2
the restriction of
f_{1}
to each fiber of
{p}
is a
2
-homogeneous polynomial, i.e.
f_{1}(y,v)=\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}a_{ij}(y)v_{i}v_{j}
for all
(y,v)\in V\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
One can assume that
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
, so we get a symmetric matrix
A(y)=(a_{ij})
, such that
f_{1}(y,v)=vA(y)v^{t}
, where
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
, and
v^{t}
is the transposed vector column.
Hence,

\gamma(t)=f_{1}\circ h\circ\eta(t)=f_{1}\bigl(h(x,t\bar{u})\bigr)=\bar{h}(x,t\bar{u}))\cdot A(h_{0}(x,t\bar{u}))\cdot\bar{h}^{t}(x,t\bar{u})),

where
\widehat{h}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})
.
By the Hadamard lemma
h_{i}(x,u)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}h_{ij}(x,u)u_{j}
for some
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
functions
h_{ij}\colon W\to\mathbb{R}
such that
h_{ij}(x,0)=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial u_{j}}h_{i}
.
Let
J(x,u)=(h_{ij}(x,u))
the matrix whose
i
th row consists of the functions
h_{i1},\ldots,h_{in}
.
Notice that
J(x,0)
is the Jacobi matrix of the composition

(x\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap W\xrightarrow{\penalty\ h\penalty\ }V\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\xrightarrow{\penalty\ p_{2}\penalty\ }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Since
h
is a diffeomorphism leaving invariant zero section
B
, it follows that
J(x,0)
is non-degenerate.

One can also write
\widehat{h}(x,u)=J(x,u)u^{t}
, whence

\gamma(t)=t\bar{u}\cdot J(x,t\bar{u})^{t}\cdot A(h_{0}(x,t\bar{u}))\cdot J(x,t\bar{u})\cdot t\bar{u}^{t},

which implies that

\delta(t)=\gamma(t)/t^{2}=uJ(x,tu)^{t}\cdot A(h_{0}(x,t\bar{u}))\cdot J(x,tu)u^{t}.

Therefore,

\delta(0)=u\underbrace{J(x,0)^{t}\cdot A(h_{0}(x,0))\cdot J(x,0)}_{B(x)}u^{t}=uB(x)u^{t}.

The assumption that
f_{1}
is definite means that
A(h_{0}(x,0))
is non-degenerate, whence
B
is symmetric and non-degenerate as well, and therefore
\delta(0)=uB(x)u^{t}>0
.

(3)

Suppose
{p}_{0}={p}_{1}
is the same vector bundle and
f_{0}=f_{1}\colon E_{0}=E_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Since
\mathrm{id}_{[0;1]}\circ f_{0}=f_{0}\circ\mathrm{id}_{\mathbf{T}}
, it follows from uniqueness of
{{\sigma}}
, that
{{\sigma}}(\mathrm{id}_{\mathbf{T}})=\mathrm{id}_{[0;1]}
.
Moreover, if
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{fol}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
, then

(5.5)

{{\sigma}}(h^{\prime})\circ{{\sigma}}(h)\circ f_{0}={{\sigma}}(h^{\prime})\circ f_{0}\circ h=f_{0}\circ h^{\prime}\circ h={{\sigma}}(h^{\prime}\circ h)\circ f_{0}.

Now uniqueness of
{{\sigma}}
implies that
{{\sigma}}(h^{\prime})\circ{{\sigma}}(h)={{\sigma}}(h^{\prime}\circ h)
.
Thus,
{{\sigma}}
is a homomorphism of monoids.
∎

Example 5.6
.

Notice that the assumption that
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are of the same homogeneity order is essential for
\phi
to be a diffeomorphism.
Indeed, let
f_{0},f_{1}\colon B\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0;1]
be given by
f_{0}({w},x)=|x|^{2}
and
f_{1}(x)=|x|^{4}
.
Then
f_{0}
is
2
-homogeneous, while
f_{1}
is
4
-homogeneous,
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathcal{F}_{1}
, and
\mathbf{T}_{0}=f_{0}^{-1}([0;1])=f_{1}^{-1}([0;1])=\mathbf{T}_{1}
.
Let also
h=\mathrm{id}_{B\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.
Then
h
is a
(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1})
-foliated diffeomorphism, while
\phi\colon[0;1]\to[0;1]
,
\phi(t)=t^{2}
, is a unique
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
map satisfying

It is well known that

admits a smooth structure, and if

is another diffeomorphism satisfying either of the following conditions:

{\xi}({w},{z})=({w}^{r}{z}^{p},{w}^{s}{z}^{q}),\quad({w},{z})\in\partial\mathbf{T},

and

•

with
\mathsf{X}=\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}0\ &1\\[1.42262pt]

\mathsf{X}=\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}1\ &2\\[1.42262pt]

0\ &1\end{smallmatrix}\!\right)

For

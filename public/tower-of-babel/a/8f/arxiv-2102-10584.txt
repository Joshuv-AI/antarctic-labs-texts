A note on double domination in graphs
Abel Cabrera Martínez, Juan A. Rodríguez-Velázquez

Abstract
Recently, Haynes, Hedetniemi and Henning published the book Topics in Domination in Graphs , which comprises 16 contributions that present advanced topics in graph domination, featuring open problems, modern techniques, and recent results. One of these contributions is the chapter Multiple Domination , by Hansberg and Volkmann, where they put into context all relevant research results on multiple domination that have been found up to 2020. In this note, we show how to improve some results on double domination that are included in the book.



Keywords
:
Double domination; double dominating set; dominating set; independent set; vertex cover.

1
Introduction

Given a simple graph
G
, the
open neighbourhood
of a vertex
v\in V(G)
is defined to be
N(v)=\{u\in V(G):\;u\text{ is adjacent to }v\}
, and the
closed neighbourhood
of
v
is
N[v]=N(v)\cup\{v\}
.

Harary and Haynes
[
1
,
2
]
introduced, in two different papers published in
1996 and 2000, the concept of double domination and, more generally, the concept of
k
-tuple
domination. Given a positive integer
k
, a subset
D\subseteq V(G)
is said to be a
k
-
tuple dominating set
if

|D\cap N[v]|\geq k

for every vertex
v\in V(G)
.
Of course, this definition requires that the graph in question has minimum degree at
least
k-1
.

The
k
-
tuple domination number

\gamma_{\times k}(G)
is defined to be

\gamma_{\times k}(G)=\min\{|D|:\,D\text{ is a }k\text{-tuple dominating set}\}.

The case
k=2
corresponds to double domination, and
\gamma_{\times 2}(G)
is known as the
double domination number
, while for
k=1
, the
1
-tuple dominating sets are the classical
dominating sets
, and hence the
domination number
is

\gamma(G)=\gamma_{\times 1}(G)
.

For a comprehensive survey on
k
-tuple domination in graphs, we cite the book
Topics in Domination
in Graphs
, published in 2020, which was edited by Haynes, Hedetniemi and Henning
[
3
]
.
In particular, there is a chapter,
Multiple Domination
, by Hansberg and Volkmann, where they
put into context all relevant research results on multiple domination concerning
k
-domination,
k
-tuple domination, and total
k
-domination that have been found up to 2020. In this note, we show how to improve some of the results on double domination that are included in the book.

2
Upper bounds on the double domination number

We begin with the following definitions.
We define a
\gamma(G)
-set as a dominating set
D

with
|D|=\gamma(G)
. The same agreement will be assumed for optimal parameters associated to other characteristic sets defined in the paper.

Recall that a set of vertices of a graph, no two of which are adjacent, is called an
independent set
. The
independence number

\alpha(G)
is defined to be

\alpha(G)=\max\{|S|:\,S\text{ is an independent set}\}.

A set
S\subseteq V(G)
which is both dominating and independent is called an
independent dominating set
. Moreover, the
independent domination number

i(G)
is

i(G)=\min\{|S|:\,S\text{ is an independent dominating set}\}.

In 2007, Blidia, Chellali and Favaron
[
4
]
proved the following relationship between the double domination number, the independent domination number and the independence number of a graph.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
4
]

For any graph
G
with no isolated vertex,

\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq\alpha(G)+i(G).

As shown in
[
4
]
, the bound above is tight. Even so, the next result shows that, in a certain sense, Theorem
2.1
has room for improvement.
In fact,
this can be seen as a corollary of Theorem
2.2
, as
\gamma(G)\leq i(G)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any graph
G
with no isolated vertex,

\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq\alpha(G)+\gamma(G).

Proof.

Let
S
be an
\alpha(G)
-set and
D
a
\gamma(G)
-set.
Given a vertex
u\in S\cup D
, the external private neighbourhood of
u
with respect to
S\cup D
is defined to be

\operatorname{epn}(u,S\cup D)=\{v\in V(G)\setminus(S\cup D):\,N(v)\cap(S\cup D)=\{u\}\}.

Note that if
u\in S
, then
\operatorname{epn}(u,S\cup D)\subseteq\operatorname{epn}(u,S)
.

Now, we define
W^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
as a set of minimum cardinality among all supersets
W
of
S\cup D
such that the following properties are satisfied.

(a)

If
x\in S\cap D
and
\operatorname{epn}(x,S\cup D)\neq\varnothing
, then
W\cap\operatorname{epn}(x,S\cup D)\neq\varnothing
.

(b)

If
x\in S\cap D
and
\operatorname{epn}(x,S\cup D)=\varnothing
, then
W\cap N(x)\neq\varnothing
.

By definition,

W^{\prime}
is a dominating set of
G

whose induced subgraph has no isolated vertex
and
|W^{\prime}|\leq|S|+|D|
. To conclude that
W^{\prime}
is a double dominating set of
G
, we only need to prove that
|N(v)\cap W^{\prime}|\geq 2
for every
v\in V(G)\setminus W^{\prime}
. Suppose to the contrary that there exists
v\in V(G)\setminus W^{\prime}
such that
v\in\operatorname{epn}(u,W^{\prime})
for some vertex
u\in W^{\prime}
. Now, since
S
and
D
are dominating sets of
G
, we have that
u\in S\cap D
. By (a), there exists
v^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}\cap\operatorname{epn}(u,S\cup D)
. Since
v,v^{\prime}\in\operatorname{epn}(u,S)
and
\operatorname{epn}(u,S)
forms a clique, vertex
v
has at least two neighbours
u,v^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
W^{\prime}
is a double dominating set of
G
and so,
\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq|W^{\prime}|\leq|S|+|D|=\alpha(G)+\gamma(G)
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the family of graphs
G_{t,r}
defined as follows. For any pair of integers
t,r\in\mathbb{Z}
, with
t\geq 2
and
1\leq r\leq t-1
, the graph
G_{t,r}
is obtained from two different copies of a star
T_{1}\cong T_{2}\cong K_{1,t}
such that
V(G_{t,r})=V(T_{1})\cup V(T_{2})
,
V(T_{1})=\{u,v_{1},\dots,v_{t}\}
and
V(T_{2})=\{u^{\prime},v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{t}^{\prime}\}
,
u
and
u^{\prime}
are the support vertices of the stars, and
E(G_{t,r})=E(T_{1})\cup E(T_{2})\cup\{uu^{\prime},v_{1}v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{r}v_{r}^{\prime}\}.
Figure
1
shows a graph of this family.
Observe that
\gamma_{\times 2}(G_{t,r})=2t-r+2
,
\gamma(G_{t,r})=2
,
i(G_{t,r})=t+1
and
\alpha(G_{t,r})=2t-r
for every
G_{t,r}\in\mathcal{H}
. Therefore, for these graphs Theorem
2.2
gives the exact value
\gamma_{\times 2}(G_{t,r})=\alpha(G_{t,r})+\gamma(G_{t,r})
, while Theorem
2.1
gives
\gamma_{\times 2}(G_{t,r})\leq\alpha(G_{t,r})+\gamma(G_{t,r})+t-1=\alpha(G_{t,r})+i(G_{t,r})
.

A set
S
of vertices of
G
is a
vertex cover
if every edge of
G
is incident with at least one vertex in
S
. The
vertex cover number
of
G
, denoted by
\beta(G)
, is defined as

\beta(G)=\min\{|S|:\,S\text{ is a vertex cover}\}.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
G
be a graph with no isolated vertex of order
n\geq 3
. Let
\mathcal{L}(G)
and
\mathcal{S}(G)
be the set of leaves and support vertices of
G
, respectively. Then

\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq\beta(G)+\gamma(G)+|\mathcal{L}(G)|-|\mathcal{S}(G)|.

Proof.

Let
S
be a
\beta(G)
-set and
D
a
\gamma(G)
-set such that
\mathcal{S}(G)\subseteq S\cap D
. Let us define
W^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
as a set of minimum cardinality among all sets
W
satisfying the following properties.

•

S\cup D\cup\mathcal{L}(G)\subseteq W
.

•

If
v\in(S\cap D)\setminus\mathcal{S}(G)
, then
N(v)\cap W\neq\varnothing
.

Since
S\cup\mathcal{L}(G)\subseteq W^{\prime}
, we conclude that every vertex outside
W^{\prime}
is adjacent to at least two vertices in
W^{\prime}
. Now, let
x\in W^{\prime}
. If
x\in S\cap D
, then
N(x)\cap W^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
by definition. If
x\in(S\cup D)\setminus(S\cap D)
, then
N(x)\cap W^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
, as
S
and
D
are dominating sets of
G
. Therefore,
W^{\prime}
is a double dominating set of
G
, and since
\mathcal{S}(G)\subseteq S\cap D
we obtain that
\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq|W^{\prime}|\leq|S|+|D|+|\mathcal{L}(G)|-|\mathcal{S}(G)|=\beta(G)+\gamma(G)+|\mathcal{L}(G)|-|\mathcal{S}(G)|
, which completes the proof.
∎

The bound above is tight. For instance, it is achieved by any graph
G_{t,r}\in\mathcal{H}
, where
\mathcal{H}
is the family of graphs defined after the proof of Theorem
2.2
. In this case,

\gamma_{\times 2}(G_{t,r})=2t-r+2
,
\gamma(G_{t,r})=|\mathcal{S}(G_{t,r})|=2
,
|\mathcal{L}(G_{t,r})|=2(t-r)
and
\beta(G_{t,r})=r+2
for every
G_{t,r}\in\mathcal{H}
.

In 2000, Harary and Haynes
[
2
]
gave the following upper bound on the double domination number of a graph in terms of the order and the domination number.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
2
]

For any graph
G
of order
n
and minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq 2
,

\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+\gamma(G),&\text{if }\,n=3,5\\[5.0pt]
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+\gamma(G)-1,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Furthermore, the bound is tight.

We proceed to show that Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
allow us to improve the bound given in Theorem
2.4
. To this end, we need to state the following well-known result, due to Gallai, which states the relationship between the independence number and the vertex cover number of a graph.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
6
]
(Gallai’s theorem)

For any graph
G
of order
n
,

\alpha(G)+\beta(G)=n.

By combining Theorems
2.2
,
2.3
and
2.5
, we have the next result, which clearly improves the bound given in Theorem
2.4
for graphs of minimum
degree at least two and order
n\geq 6
, whenever
\alpha(G)\notin\{\frac{n\pm 2}{2},\frac{n}{2}\}
for
n
even, and
\alpha(G)\notin\{\frac{n\pm 1}{2},\frac{n\pm 3}{2}\}
for
n
odd.

Theorem 2.6
.

For any graph
G
of order
n
and minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq 2
,

\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq\min\{\alpha(G),n-\alpha(G)\}+\gamma(G).

The bound above is tight. For instance, it is achieved by any complete graph, as
\gamma_{\times 2}(K_{n})=\alpha(K_{n})+\gamma(K_{n})=2
, while Theorem
2.4
gives
\gamma_{\times 2}(K_{n})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
for
n>5
and
\gamma_{\times 2}(K_{5})\leq 3
.
Now, in order to show another example where
the difference between the bounds given by Theorems
2.6
and
2.4
can be as large as desired,
we consider the family
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
of graphs defined as follows. For every integer

the graph

has order

In 1985, Fink and
Jacobson
[
7
,
8
]
extended the idea of domination in graphs to the more general notion of
k
-domination. A subset
D\subseteq V(G)
is said to be a
k
-dominating set if
|D\cap N(v)|\geq k
for every vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus D
. The
k
-domination number
\gamma_{k}(G)
is defined to be

\gamma_{k}(G)=\min\{|D|:\,D\text{ is a }k\text{-dominating set}\}.

The case
k=1
corresponds to standard domination.

Bonomo et al.
[
9
]
obtained the following relationship between the double domination number and the
2
-domination number.

Theorem 2.7
.

[
9
]

For any graph
G
with no isolated vertex,

\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq 2\gamma_{2}(G)-1.

The inequality of this theorem was proved previously for trees
in
[
5
]
.
In fact, the result was stated as
\gamma_{\times 2}(T)\leq 2\gamma_{2}(T)-2
for every tree
T
of order at least four.

Since
\gamma(G)\leq\gamma_{2}(G)
, the next result improves the bound given in Theorem
2.7
whenever
\gamma(G)\leq\gamma_{2}(G)-2
.

Theorem 2.8
.

For any graph
G
with no isolated vertex,

\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq\gamma_{2}(G)+\gamma(G).

Proof.

Let
S
be a
\gamma_{2}(G)
-set and
D
a
\gamma(G)
-set. Now, we define
W^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
as a set of minimum cardinality among all supersets
W
of
S\cup D
such that
N(x)\cap W\neq\varnothing
for every
x\in S\cap D
.

By definition,
W^{\prime}
is a double dominating set of
G
and
|W^{\prime}|\leq|S|+|D|
. Therefore,
\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq|W^{\prime}|\leq|S|+|D|=\gamma_{2}(G)+\gamma(G)
, which completes the proof.
∎

The bound above is tight. For instance, it is achieved by the graph
G
shown in Figure
3
, where
\gamma(G)=2
,
\gamma_{2}(G)=4
and
\gamma_{\times 2}(G)=6
. For a simple example where
the difference between the bounds given by Theorems
2.8
and
2.7
can be as large as desired, we can take
G\cong K_{1,n-1}
with
n\geq 3
. In this case, Theorem

2.8
gives the exact value
\gamma_{\times 2}(K_{1,n-1})=n
, while Theorem
2.7
gives
\gamma_{\times 2}(K_{1,n-1})\leq 2n-3
.

A
total dominating set
, in a graph
G
with no isolated vertices,
is a set
S
of vertices of
G
such that every vertex of
G
is adjacent to at least one vertex
in
S
.
The
total domination number

\gamma_{t}(G)
is defined to be

\gamma_{t}(G)=\min\{|S|:\,S\text{ is a total dominating set}\}.

The theory of total domination has been extensively studied. For instance, we cite the book
[
10
]
by Henning and Yeo.

A graph is
claw-free
if and only if it does not contain the complete bipartite graph
K_{1,3}
as an induced subgraph.

Theorem 2.9
.

[
4
]

For any claw-free graph
G
with no isolated vertex,

\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq\min\{2\gamma_{t}(G),3\gamma(G)\}.

As shown in
[
4
]
, the bound above is tight. Even so, the next result shows that Theorem
2.9
has room for improvement, as
\gamma(G)\leq\gamma_{t}(G)
and
\gamma_{t}(G)\leq 2\gamma(G)
.

Theorem 2.10
.

For any claw-free graph
G
with no isolated vertex,

\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq\gamma_{t}(G)+\gamma(G).

Proof.

Let
S
be a
\gamma_{t}(G)
-set and
D
a
\gamma(G)
-set. As above, given a vertex
v\in S\cup D
, the external private neighbourhood of
v
with respect to
S\cup D
will be denoted by
\operatorname{epn}(v,S\cup D)
.

Now, we define
W^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
as a set of minimum cardinality among all supersets
W
of
S\cup D
such that the following property is satisfied.

(
\star
)

If
x\in S\cap D
and
\operatorname{epn}(x,S\cup D)\neq\varnothing
, then
W\cap\operatorname{epn}(x,S\cup D)\neq\varnothing
.

By definition,
W^{\prime}
is a total dominating set of
G
and
|W^{\prime}|\leq|S|+|D|
. It remains to show that
|N(v)\cap W^{\prime}|\geq 2
for every
v\in V(G)\setminus W^{\prime}
. Suppose, to the contrary, that there exists
v\in V(G)\setminus W^{\prime}
which is an external private neighbour of
u\in W^{\prime}
, i.e.,
N(v)\cap W^{\prime}=\{u\}
. Since both
S
and
D
are dominating sets,
u\in S\cap D
. Now, by (
\star
), there exists
v^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}\cap(\operatorname{epn}(u,S\cup D)\setminus\{v\})
and, since
S
is a total dominating set, there exists
u^{\prime}\in N(u)\cap S
. Hence, if
G
is a claw-free graph, then
v
and
v^{\prime}
have to be adjacent vertices, which is a contradiction. Thus,
W^{\prime}
is a double dominating set of
G
. Therefore,

\gamma_{\times 2}(G)\leq|W^{\prime}|\leq|S|+|D|=\gamma_{t}(G)+\gamma(G)
, which completes the proof.
∎

The bound above is tight. For instance, it is achieved by any graph of order
n+1
obtained from a complete graph of order
n\geq 2
by adding a pendant edge.

the difference between the bounds given by Theorems
2.10
and
2.9
can be as large as desired, we can take the case of path and cycle graphs of order large enough.

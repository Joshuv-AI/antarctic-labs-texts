Existence and dynamical behaviour of vectorial standing waves with prescribed mass for Hartree-Fock type systems
Shuai Yao, Juntao Sun, Tsung-fang Wu

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate vectorial standing waves with prescribed mass for the Hartree-Fock type system (HF system) with the double coupled feature. Such system is viewed as an approximation of the Coulomb system with two particles appeared in quantum mechanics. By exploring the interaction of the double coupled terms, we prove the existence/nonexistence and symmetry of vectorial energy ground states for the corresponding stationary problem. Furthermore, we obtain the relation between vectorial energy ground states and vectorial action ground states in some cases. Finally, we establish conditions for global well-posedness and finite time blow-up to HF system with the initial data, and prove orbital stability/strong instability of standing waves.



Keywords:
Hartree-Fock system; vectorial ground state;
well-posedness; dynamical behaviour

Introduction

Our starting point is the system of Hartree-Fock equations:

\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}\psi_{k}+\Delta\psi_{k}-\left(|x|^{-1}\ast\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\psi_{j}|^{2}\right)\psi_{k}+(V_{\text{ex}}\psi)_{k}=0,&\text{ }\forall k=1,...,N,\end{array}

(1)

where
\psi_{k}:\mathbb{R\times R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
(V_{\text{ex}}\psi)_{k}
is the
k
’th component of
the exchange
potential
defined by

\begin{array}[]{ll}(V_{\text{ex}}\psi)_{k}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}(y)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\psi_{k}(y)\bar{\psi}_{j}(y)}{|x-y|}dy,&\text{ }\forall k=1,...,N.\end{array}

(2)

This system appeared in quantum mechanics in the study of a system of
N

particles, which is an approximation of the Coulomb system. With respect to system (
1
), it has the advantage of
being consistent with the Pauli principle, see e.g.
[
13
,
34
]
.

We note that the exchange potential (
2
) is the most difficult term
to be treated in system (
1
). A very simple approximation of this
potential was given by Slater
[
35
]
in the form:

\begin{array}[]{ll}(V_{\text{ex}}\psi)_{k}\approx C\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\psi_{j}|^{2}\right)^{1/3}\psi_{k},&\text{ }\forall k=1,2,...,N,\end{array}

(3)

where
C>0
. For more different local approximations, we refer to
[
21
,
28
]
and references therein.

For the single-partical states, i.e.
N=1,
the exchange potential (
3
) becomes
(V_{\text{ex}}\psi)_{1}=C|\psi_{1}|^{2/3}\psi_{1}
, and then

equation:

\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi-\left(|x|^{-1}\ast|\psi|^{2}\right)\psi+|\psi|^{2/3}\psi=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}

(4)

where we rename
\psi_{1}
as
\psi,
and take
C=1
for simplicity. Sánchez and Soler
[
32
]
analyzed the asymptotic behaviour of solutions to
equation (
4
). If the term
|\psi|^{2/3}\psi
is replaced with
|\psi|^{p-2}\psi

(2<p<6)
(or, more generally,
f(\psi))
, then
equation (
4
) becomes Schrödinger–Poisson equation (also
called Schrödinger–Maxwell equation), where the existence,
stability/instability and dynamics of standing waves

has been extensively studied in recent years, see e.g.
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
12
,
29
,
30
,
33
,
38
,
39
]
.

For the two-particle states, i.e.
N=2,
system (

more complicated. In order to further simplify it, we assume the exchange
potential

V_{\text{ex}}\psi=C\binom{(\left|\psi_{1}\right|^{p-2}+\beta\left|\psi_{1}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}\left|\psi_{2}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}})\psi_{1}}{(\left|\psi_{2}\right|^{p-2}+\beta\left|\psi_{1}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\left|\psi_{2}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}-2})\psi_{2}},

where
C>0,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and

. Clearly, if
p=\frac{8}{3},

then (

V_{\text{ex}}\psi=C\binom{(\left|\psi_{1}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}+\beta\left|\psi_{1}\right|^{-\frac{2}{3}}\left|\psi_{2}\right|^{\frac{4}{3}})\psi_{1}}{(\left|\psi_{2}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}}+\beta\left|\psi_{1}\right|^{\frac{4}{3}}\left|\psi_{2}\right|^{-\frac{2}{3}})\psi_{2}},

which can be viewed as an approximation of the exchange potential (
3
). Taking, for simplicity,
C=1
, system (
1
) becomes the following
problem, depending on the parameter

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}\psi_{1}+\Delta\psi_{1}-\gamma\left(|x|^{-1}\ast\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}|\psi_{j}|^{2}\right)\psi_{1}+|\psi_{1}|^{p-2}\psi_{1}+\beta|\psi_{2}|^{\frac{p}{2}}|\psi_{1}|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}\psi_{1}=0&\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\
i\partial_{t}\psi_{2}+\Delta\psi_{2}-\gamma\left(|x|^{-1}\ast\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}|\psi_{j}|^{2}\right)\psi_{2}+|\psi_{2}|^{p-2}\psi_{2}+\beta|\psi_{1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}|\psi_{2}|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}\psi_{2}=0&\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{array}\right.

(6)

The typical characteristic of such system lies on the presence of the double
coupled terms, including a Coulomb interacting one and a cooperative pure
power one.

An important topic is to establish conditions for the well-posedness and
blow-up of system (
6
) with the initial data. When
\gamma=0,

there has been a number of works on local existence, global existence and
scattering theory, see e.g.
[
11
,
31
,
41
]
. However, for
\gamma>0
,
there have no any results on these aspects in the existing literature so far. We
observe that the presence of the double coupled terms yields substantial
difficulties and requires more detailed dispersive estimates. One objective
in this paper is to investigate the global well-posedness and finite time
blow-up of system (
6
) with the initial data.

Another interesting topic on system (
6
) is to study the standing
waves of the form

\psi_{1}(t,x)=e^{i\lambda t}u(x)\text{ and }\psi_{2}(t,x)=e^{i\lambda t}v(x),

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and

is a vector function to be found.
Thus

must verify

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+\lambda u+\gamma\phi_{u,v}u=|u|^{p-2}u+\beta|v|^{\frac{p}{2}}|u|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}u&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta v+\lambda v+\gamma\phi_{u,v}v=|v|^{p-2}v+\beta|u|^{\frac{p}{2}}|v|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}v&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}\right.

(7)

where
\phi_{u,v}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(y)+v^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}dy.

For a solution

of system (

Definition 1.1

A vector function
\left(u,v\right)
is said to be

\left(i\right)
nontrivial if either

\left(ii\right)
semitrivial if it is nontrivial but either

or
v=0;

\left(iii\right)
vectorial if both of
u
and
v
are not zero;

According to the role of the frequency
\lambda,
there are two different
ways to study solutions of system (
7
):

(i)
the frequency
\lambda
is a fixed and assigned parameter;

(ii)
the frequency
\lambda
is an unknown of the problem.

For case
(i)
, one can see that solutions of system (
7
) can be
obtained as critical points of the functional defined in
\mathbf{H}:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by

\displaystyle E_{\lambda,\gamma,\beta}(u,v):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2})dx+\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|u|^{2}+|v|^{2})dx+\frac{\gamma}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u,v}(u^{2}+v^{2})dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|u|^{p}+|v|^{p}+2\beta|u|^{\frac{p}{2}}|v|^{\frac{p}{2}})dx.

When either
3<p<4
and
\beta>0,
or
4\leq p<6
and
\beta\geq 2^{2q-1}-1,
d’Avenia, Maia and Siciliano
[
9
]
studied the existence
of radial vectorial solutions to system (
7
) with
\lambda=1\
via
the method of Nehari-Pohozaev manifold defined in
\mathbf{H}_{r}:=H_{rad}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H_{rad}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

developed by Ruiz
[
30
]
. Very recently, we
[
37
]
considered another
interesting case, i.e.
2<p<3,
and proved the existence of radial vectorial
solutions for system (
7
) with
\lambda=1
when either
\gamma>0

small enough or
\beta>\beta(\gamma).
In addition, by developing a novel
constraint method, for
\beta>\beta(\gamma),
we obtained a vectorial

action ground state
in
\mathbf{H}
when either
3\leq p<4
and
0<\gamma<\gamma_{0},
or
\frac{1+\sqrt{73}}{3}\leq p<4
and
\gamma>0.

Here the action ground state is defined in the following sense:

Definition 1.2

We say that
(u,v)
is an action ground state of system (
7
) if it is a solution of system (
7
) having least energy among
all the solutions:

E_{\lambda,\gamma,\beta}(u,v)=\inf\{E_{\lambda,\gamma,\beta}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}):E_{\lambda,\gamma,\beta}^{\prime}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=0\text{ and }(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\mathbf{H}\backslash\{(0,0)\}\}.

Alternatively one can look for solutions of system (
7
) with
\lambda
unknown. In this case
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
appears as a
Lagrange multiplier and

-norms of solutions are prescribed. This
study seems to be particularly meaningful from the physical point of view,
since solutions of system (
6
) with the initial data conserve their
mass along time. Moreover, this study often offers a good insight of the
dynamical properties of solutions for system (
6
), such as stability
or instability. For these reasons, another objective in this paper is to

7
) having
prescribed mass

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u^{2}+v^{2})dx=c>0,

(8)

which has not been concerned before. From
the variational point of view, solutions of problem (
7
)-(
8
) can be obtained as critical points of the energy functional
I_{\gamma,\beta}:\mathbf{H\rightarrow}\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle I_{\gamma,\beta}(u,v):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2})dx+\frac{\gamma}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u,v}(u^{2}+v^{2})dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|u|^{p}+|v|^{p}+2\beta|u|^{\frac{p}{2}}|v|^{\frac{p}{2}})dx

on the constraint

S_{c}:=\left\{(u,v)\in\mathbf{H}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u^{2}+v^{2})dx=c\right\}.

As
2<p<6,
it is standard that
I_{\gamma,\beta}
is of class

on
S_{c}.
We will be particularly interested in

,
defined as follows:

Definition 1.3

We say that
(u,v)
is an energy ground state of system (
7
) if it is a solution of system (
7
) having minimal energy
among all the solutions:

I_{\gamma,\beta}^{\prime}|_{S_{c}}(u,v)=0\text{ and }I_{\gamma,\beta}(u,v)=\inf\{I_{\gamma,\beta}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}):I_{\gamma,\beta}^{\prime}|_{S(c)}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=0\text{ and }(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in S_{c}\}.

If
I_{\gamma,\beta}
has a global minimizer, then this definition
naturally extends the notion of ground states from linear quantum mechanics.
Moreover, it allows to deal with cases when
I_{\gamma,\beta}
is
unbounded from below on
S_{c}
. We also recall the notion of stability and
instability as follows:

Definition 1.4

(i)
The set of the ground states
\mathcal{Z}(c)
is orbitally
stable if for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for
the initial data
(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))\in\mathbf{H}
satisfying
\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{Z}(c)}\|(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))-(u,v)\|_{\mathbf{H}}\leq\delta,
we have

\sup_{t\in(0,T_{\max})}\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{Z}(c)}\|(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))-(u,v)\|_{\mathbf{H}}\leq\varepsilon,

where
(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))
is the solution to system (
6
)
with the initial data
(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))
for

, and

denotes the maximum existence time of
solution.

(ii)
A standing wave
(e^{i\lambda t}u,e^{i\lambda t}v)
is strongly
unstable if for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))\in\mathbf{H}
such that

We note that the definition of stability implicitly requires that system (

When

(9)

In recent years, the existence and multiplicity of solutions to system (

1.1
Main results

For sake of convenience, we set

A(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2})dx,\quad B(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u,v}(u^{2}+v^{2})dx,

Lemma 3.5

Let
\frac{18}{7}<p<3.
Then the energy functional
I_{\gamma,\beta}
is bounded from below by a constant not depending on
c,
on
S_{r,c}:=\{(u,v)\in\mathbf{H}_{r}:\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}=c\}.

Proof.
For
(u,v)\in S_{r,c},
it follows from (
25
) that

\displaystyle C(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|u|^{p}+|v|^{p}+2\beta|u|^{\frac{p}{2}}|v|^{\frac{p}{2}})dx

(26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\beta)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|u|^{p}+|v|^{p})dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(p)(1+\beta)\left[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\frac{5p-12}{3}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u(x)^{2}u(y)^{2}}{|x-y|}dxdy\right)^{\frac{6-p}{6}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{\frac{5p-12}{3}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{v(x)^{2}v(y)^{2}}{|x-y|}dxdy\right)^{\frac{6-p}{6}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(p)(1+\beta)\left(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\frac{5p-12}{3}}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{\frac{5p-12}{3}}\right)\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u(x)^{2}u(y)^{2}}{|x-y|}dxdy\right)^{\frac{6-p}{6}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{v(x)^{2}v(y)^{2}}{|x-y|}dxdy\right)^{\frac{6-p}{6}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\mathcal{C}(p)(1+\beta)A(u,v)^{\frac{5p-12}{6}}B(u,v)^{\frac{6-p}{6}}.

Then by (
26
) we have

I_{\gamma,\beta}(u,v)\geq\frac{1}{2}A(u,v)+\frac{\gamma}{4}B(u,v)-\frac{4\mathcal{C}(p)(1+\beta)}{p}A(u,v)^{\frac{5p-12}{6}}B(u,v)^{\frac{6-p}{6}},

which implies that
I_{\gamma,\beta}
is bounded from below by a constant
not depending on
c
. In fact, we set the function of two variables

f(x,y):=\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{\gamma}{4}y-\frac{4\mathcal{C}(p)(1+\beta)}{p}x^{\frac{5p-12}{6}}y^{\frac{6-p}{6}},\text{ }\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}.

By a direct calculation, there exists stationary point
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
not depending on
c
such that
f_{x}(x_{0},y_{0})=f_{y}(x_{0},y_{0})=0
. Furthermore, we have

f_{xx}(x_{0},y_{0})=\frac{\mathcal{C}(p)(1+\beta)(5p-12)(18-5p)}{9p}x_{0}^{-\frac{24-5p}{6}}y_{0}^{\frac{6-p}{6}}

and

f_{xx}(x_{0},y_{0})f_{yy}(x_{0},y_{0})-f_{xy}^{2}(x_{0},y_{0})=\frac{8[\mathcal{C}(p)(1+\beta)]^{2}(5p-12)(6-p)(3-p)}{27p^{2}}x_{0}^{-\frac{18-5p}{3}}y_{0}^{-\frac{p}{6}}>0,

which implies that the function
f(x,y)
achieves its minimum at point
(x_{0},y_{0})
, and so the energy functional
I_{\gamma,\beta}
is bounded
from below. This completes the proof.

Lemma 3.6

Let
2<p<3.
Then it holds
\lim_{c\rightarrow 0}\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)}{c}=0
.

Proof.
Notice that

\frac{\sigma_{0,\beta}(c)}{c}\leq\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)}{c}<0,

where

\sigma_{0,\beta}(c):=\inf_{(u,v)\in S_{c}}I_{0,\beta}(u,v)

with

I_{0,\beta}(u,v):=\frac{1}{2}A(u,v)-\frac{1}{p}C(u,v).

Due to
[
17
]
, there exists a minimizer
(u_{0},v_{0})
for
\sigma_{0,\beta}(c)
satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u_{0}+\lambda_{c}u_{0}=|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0}+\beta|v_{0}|^{\frac{p}{2}}|u_{0}|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}u_{0}&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta v_{0}+\lambda_{c}v_{0}=|v_{0}|^{p-2}v_{0}+\beta|u_{0}|^{\frac{p}{2}}|v_{0}|^{\frac{p}{2}-2}u_{0}&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}\right.

(27)

which indicates that

\frac{\lambda_{c}}{2}=\frac{C(u_{0},v_{0})-A(u_{0},v_{0})}{2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|u_{0}|^{2}+|v_{0}|^{2})dx}\geq\frac{\frac{1}{p}C(u_{0},v_{0})-\frac{1}{2}A(u_{0},v_{0})}{2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|u_{0}|^{2}+|v_{0}|^{2})dx}=\frac{-\sigma_{0,\beta}(c)}{c}>0,

where
\lambda_{c}
is the Lagrange multiplier associated to the minimizer
(u_{0},v_{0})
. Next, we prove that
\lim_{c\rightarrow 0}\lambda_{c}=0
.
Suppose on the contrary. Then we can assume that there exists a sequence
\{c_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
satisfying
c_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
such that
\lambda_{c_{n}}>M
for some
M\in(0,1)
.
Since the minimizers
(u_{n},v_{n}):=(u_{c_{n}},v_{c_{n}})
satisfy system (
27
), we have

\displaystyle M(\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+M(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{c_{n}}(\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(u_{n},v_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K(p,\beta)(\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{p}+\|v_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{p})

for some
K(p,\beta)>0
. This implies that there exists
M_{1}>0
such that

A(u_{n},v_{n})\geq M_{1}
. But as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, by (
13
), we get

0\geq I_{0,\beta}(u_{n},v_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}A(u_{n},v_{n})-\frac{(4+2\beta)\mathcal{S}_{p}}{p}c_{n}^{\frac{6-p}{4}}A(u_{n},v_{n})^{\frac{3(p-2)}{4}}\geq\frac{1}{2}M_{1}-o_{n}(1),

which is a contradiction. This completes the proof.

Lemma 3.7

Let
2<p<3
. Then it holds
\inf_{c>0}\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)=-\infty
.

Proof.
Now we argue by contradiction and assume that there exists
K>0
such that
\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)>-K
for all
c>0
. In this case we would have
\lim_{c\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)}{c}=0
. Since
\lim_{c\rightarrow 0}\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)}{c}=0
and
\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)<0
, the function
c\rightarrow\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)}{c}
attains a global minimum, i.e. there exists
c_{0}
such
that

\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c_{0})}{c_{0}}\leq\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)}{c}\text{ for all }c>0.

(28)

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.1
, one has

\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(2c_{0})}{2c_{0}}\leq\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c_{0})+\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c_{0})}{2c_{0}}=\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c_{0})}{c_{0}}.

(29)

It follows from (
28
) and (
29
) that the function
c\rightarrow\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)}{c}
attains a global minimum
also at
2c_{0}
. Repeating such step, we deduce that

\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(kc_{0})}{kc_{0}}=\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c_{0})}{c_{0}},\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

which implies that
\liminf_{c\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)}{c}<0.
This contradicts with the fact that
\lim_{c\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)}{c}=0
. This completes the proof.

Next, we claim that

\hat{v}\neq 0

\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)\leq I_{\gamma,\beta}(u_{s},v_{s})=\frac{s^{2}}{2}A(u,v)+\frac{\gamma s}{4}B(u,v)-\frac{s^{\frac{3(p-2)}{2}}}{3}C(u,v)\text{
for }s>0,

which implies that
\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)\leq 0
as

.
So,
\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)
has no minimizer for
\beta<\frac{3\sqrt{\gamma}}{2}-1
.

For
3<p<\frac{10}{3}
, we follow the idea in
[

. Suppose by
contradiction that there exist

\widetilde{\beta}<\beta_{\star}(c,\gamma,p)

has a

\beta_{\star}(c,\gamma,p)
, we
find that
\beta_{\star}(c,\gamma,p)
is continuous and decreasing on

so that

On the other hand, by the characterization of

, it follows that
\sigma_{\gamma,\widetilde{\beta}}(c)=0

\sigma_{\gamma,\widetilde{\beta}}(\widetilde{c})<\frac{\widetilde{c}}{c}\sigma_{\gamma,\widetilde{\beta}}(c)=0,

which implies that
\widetilde{\beta}>\beta_{\star}(\widetilde{c},\gamma,p)

has no

We complete the proof.

Lemma 4.1

Let

\gamma,\lambda>0

Proof.

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{3\varpi}(\|u_{\lambda}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{\lambda}\|_{2}^{2})

Proposition 4.2

Let

\gamma,\lambda>0

(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})
is a

5
The existence of local minimizer

For each
(u,v)\in S_{1}
and

(u,v)_{t}:=(u_{t},v_{t})=(t^{3/2}u(tx),t^{3/2}v(tx))

\Phi_{c,(u,v)}(t)

\Phi_{c,(u,v)}(t):=I_{\gamma,\beta}(c^{1/2}(u,v)_{t})=\frac{t^{2}}{2}cA(u,v)+\frac{\gamma t}{4}c^{2}B(u,v)-\frac{t^{\frac{3p-6}{2}}}{p}c^{\frac{p}{2}}C(u,v)\text{ for }t>0.

Notice that

14

[

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\gamma,\beta}^{-}(c)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\left\{c^{1/2}(u,v)\in S_{c}:\Phi_{c,(u,v)}^{\prime}(1)=0,\Phi_{c,(u,v)}^{\prime\prime}(1)<0\right\};

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\gamma,\beta}^{0}(c)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\left\{c^{1/2}(u,v)\in S_{c}:\Phi_{c,(u,v)}^{\prime}(1)=0,\Phi_{c,(u,v)}^{\prime\prime}(1)=0\right\}.

\displaystyle\|h_{2}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\|_{L_{t}^{s_{2}}W^{1,r_{2}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}\|\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{s_{2}}W^{r_{2}}}+\|\psi_{1}\|_{L_{t}^{s_{2}}W^{r_{2}}}\|\psi_{1}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}}\|\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}}

\displaystyle+\|\psi_{1}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}\|\psi_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{s_{2}}W^{r_{2}}}.

So, for
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\in\mathcal{B}_{R,T}
, we get

\|\Psi(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\|_{L_{t}^{\tilde{s}}W^{1,\tilde{r}}}\leq C\|(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))\|_{\mathbf{H}}+CT^{\theta}R^{p-1}+CT^{\delta}R^{3}.

Now we choose
R=2C\|(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))\|_{\mathbf{H}}>0

and
0<T<1
such that
CT^{\theta}R^{p-2}+CT^{\delta}R^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}.
This implies that
\mathcal{B}_{R,T}
is an invariant set of
\Psi
.
Furthermore, for
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}),(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in\mathcal{B}_{R,T}
, by using the same argument, we get

d(\Psi(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})-\Psi(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}))\lesssim(T^{\theta}+T^{\delta})d((\psi_{1},\psi_{2})-(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})),

which leads to

d(\Psi(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})-\Psi(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}))\leq\frac{1}{2}d((\psi_{1},\psi_{2})-(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})).

This implies that
\Psi
is a contraction map. In particular,
\Psi
has a
unique fixed point in this space. Moreover, by the classical arguments in

[
5
]
, the solution
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
satisfies the mass
conservation law

\|\psi_{1}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\psi_{2}(t)\|_{2}^{2}=\|\psi_{1}(0)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\psi_{2}(0)\|_{2}^{2}

and the energy conservation law

\displaystyle I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla\psi_{1}(t)|^{2}+|\nabla\psi_{2}(t)|^{2})dx+\frac{\gamma}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t)}(|\psi_{1}(t)|^{2}+|\psi_{2}(t)|^{2})dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|\psi_{1}(t)|^{p}+|\psi_{2}(t)|^{p}+2\beta|\psi_{1}(t)|^{\frac{p}{2}}|\psi_{2}(t)|^{\frac{p}{2}})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0)).

We give the proof of Theorem
1.14
:
Since the proof is similar
to the classical arguments in
[
24
]
, we only give a sketch. Assume on the
contrary. Then there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
\left\{\psi_{1}^{n}(0),\psi_{2}^{n}(0)\right\}
and
\left\{t_{n}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{Z}(c)}\|(\psi_{1}^{n}(0),\psi_{2}^{n}(0))-(u,v)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\rightarrow 0

and

\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{Z}(c)}\|(\psi_{1}^{n}(t_{n}),\psi_{2}^{n}(t_{n}))-(u,v)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

(65)

According to mass and energy conservation laws, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|\psi_{i}^{n}(t_{n})\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|\psi_{i}^{n}(0)\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{and }I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}^{n}(t_{n}),\psi_{2}^{n}(t_{n}))=I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}^{n}(0),\psi_{2}^{n}(0))\text{ for }i=1,2.

Define

\widetilde{\psi}_{i}^{n}=\frac{\psi_{i}^{n}(t_{n})}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|\psi_{i}^{n}(t_{n})\|_{2}^{2}}c\text{ for }i=1,2.

A direct calculation shows that

\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|\widetilde{\psi}_{i}^{n}\|_{2}^{2}=c\quad\text{and
}I_{\gamma,\beta}(\widetilde{\psi}_{1}^{n},\widetilde{\psi}_{2}^{n})=\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)+o_{n}(1),

which indicates that
(\widetilde{\psi}_{1}^{n},\widetilde{\psi}_{2}^{n})

is a minimizing sequence for
\sigma_{\gamma,\beta}(c)
. By Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
, it is precompact in
\mathbf{H}
, which is a
contradiction with (
65
). Therefore, the set
\mathcal{Z}(c)
is
orbitally stable.

6.2
The global well-posedness

We give the proof of Theorem
1.15
:
Since system (
6
)
with the initial data is locally well-posed by Theorem
1.13
, we obtain
that either
T_{\max}=+\infty
, or
T_{\max}<+\infty
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}^{-}}A(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))\rightarrow+\infty
. Then the global existence boils down to obtaining a priori bound
on
A(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))
.

(i)

2<p\leq\frac{10}{3}
. By mass and energy conservation laws and (
13
), we have

\displaystyle A(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))+\frac{2}{p}C(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))+4(1+\beta)\mathcal{S}_{p}\left(\|\psi_{1}(0)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\psi_{2}(0)\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{6-p}{4}}\left(A(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))\right)^{\frac{3(p-2)}{4}}.

If
2<p<\frac{10}{3},
then there exists a constant
M>0
independent of
t

such that
A(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))\leq M
. If
p=\frac{10}{3},
then
also there exists a constant
M>0
independent of
t
such that
A(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))\leq M
provided that
\|\psi_{1}(0)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\psi_{2}(0)\|_{2}^{2}
is small.

(ii)

10/3<p<6
. If by contradiction
T_{\max}<+\infty
, we have
\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}^{-}}A(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))\rightarrow+\infty
. Since

I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})-\frac{2}{3(p-2)}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=\frac{3p-10}{6(p-2)}A(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})+\frac{3p-8}{12(p-2)}B(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}),

together with the energy conservation law, we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}^{-}}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=-\infty.
Then by the continuity, these exists
t_{0}\in(0,T_{\max})
such that
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(t_{0}),\psi_{2}(t_{0}))=0
, where we
have used the assumption of
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))>0.
Using again the energy conservation law and the assumption of
I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))<m_{\gamma,\beta}(c)
, we
deduce that

m_{\gamma,\beta}(c)>I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))=I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(t_{0}),\psi_{2}(t_{0}))\geq\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{M}_{\gamma,\beta}(c)}I_{\gamma,\beta}(u,v)=m_{\gamma,\beta}(c),

which is a contradiction. So,
T_{\max}=+\infty.

6.3
Finite time blow-up and strong unstablility

Lemma 6.4

Assume that the conditions of Theorem
1.10
or Theorem
1.11
hold. Let
(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))\in S_{c}
such that
I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))<m_{\gamma,\beta}(c)
. Then

\Phi_{(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))}(t):=\frac{t^{2}}{2}A(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))+\frac{\gamma t}{4}B(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))-\frac{t^{\frac{3(p-2)}{2}}}{p}C(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))

has a unique global maximum point
t^{-}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))
.
Furthermore, if

0<t^{-}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))<1\text{ and }(|x|\psi_{1}(0),|x|\psi_{2}(0))\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

then the solution
(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))
of system (
6
) with
initial datum
(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))
blows-up in finite time.

Proof.
Let
(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))\in S_{c}
. Since
\frac{10}{3}\leq p<6,
it
is easy to prove that
\Phi_{(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))}(t)
has a unique
global maximum point
t^{-}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))
and
\Phi_{(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))}(t)
is strict decreasing and concave on
(t^{-}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0)),+\infty)
. We claim that if
t^{-}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))\in(0,1)
, then

\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))\leq I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))-m_{\gamma,\beta}(c).

(66)

The fact of
t^{-}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))\in(0,1)
implies that
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))<0
. Then there holds

\displaystyle I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))}(1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Phi_{(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))}(t^{-}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0)))-(t^{-}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))-1)\Phi_{(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))}^{\prime}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{\gamma,\beta}((\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))_{t^{-}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))})-|\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))|(1-t^{-}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0)))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle m_{\gamma,\beta}(c)-|\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{\gamma,\beta}(c)+\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0)).

This proves the claim.

Now let us consider the solution
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
with initial data
(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))
. Similar to the argument in Soave
[
36
, Lemma
5.3]
, the map
(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))\mapsto t^{-}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))
is continuous, and together with
0<t^{-}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))<1,
we have
t^{-}(\psi_{1}(\tau),\psi_{2}(\tau))<1
for every
|\tau|
small, say
|\tau|<\overline{\tau}
for some
\overline{\tau}>0
. By (
66
) and the assumption of
I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))<m_{\gamma,\beta}(c)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(\tau),\psi_{2}(\tau))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(\tau),\psi_{2}(\tau))-m_{\gamma,\beta}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(0),\psi_{2}(0))-m_{\gamma,\beta}(c):=-\eta<0,

for every such
\tau
, and hence
t^{-}(\psi_{1}(\overline{\tau}),\psi_{2}(\overline{\tau}))<1
. Appplying the continuity argument yields

\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))\leq-\eta\text{ for
}t\in(T_{\min},T_{\max}).

Since
(|x|\psi_{1}(0),|x|\psi_{2}(0))\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, by the Virial identity
[
5
, Proposition 6.5.1]
,
the function

\bar{f}(t):=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{2}|\psi_{i}(t,x)|^{2}dx

is of class
C^{2}
. We now use the method established by Cazenave
[
5
]
.
By multiplying the first equation in system (
6
) by
i|x|^{2}\overline{\psi}_{1}
and the second equation in system (
6
) by
i|x|^{2}\overline{\psi}_{2}
, taking the real part and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have

\bar{f}^{\prime}(t)=2\mathrm{Re}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{2}\overline{\psi}_{i}\partial_{t}\psi_{i}dx=4\mathrm{Im}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\overline{\psi}_{i}x\cdot\nabla\psi_{i}dx,

and

\displaystyle\bar{f}^{\prime\prime}(t)=-4\mathrm{Im}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(2x\cdot\nabla\psi_{i}+3\overline{\psi}_{i})\psi_{i}dx=8\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,\beta}(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))\leq-8\eta<0

for
t\in(T_{\min},T_{\max})
. Integrating twice in time gives

0\leq\bar{f}(t)\leq\bar{f}(0)+\bar{f}^{\prime}(0)t-4\eta t^{2}.
Since the right hand side becomes negative for
t
sufficiently large, it is
necessary that both
T_{\min}
and
T_{\max}
are bounded, which in turn
implies finite time blow-up. The proof is complete.

We give the proofs of Theorems

Next, we prove Theorem

Leading correction to the local density approximation for exchange in large- Z Z atoms
Nathan Argaman, Jeremy Redd, Antonio C. Cancio, Kieron Burke

Abstract
The large- Z Z asymptotic expansion of atomic energies has been useful in determining exact conditions for corrections to the local density approximation in density functional theory. The correction for exchange is fit well with a leading Z ​ ln ​ Z Z\text{ln}Z term, and we find its coefficient numerically. The gradient expansion approximation also has such a term, but with a smaller coefficient. Analytic results in the limit of vanishing interaction with hydrogenic orbitals (a Bohr atom) lead to the conjecture that the coefficients are precisely 2.7 times larger than their gradient expansion counterparts, yielding an analytic expression for the exchange-energy correction which is accurate to ∼ 5 % \sim 5\% for all Z Z .



For almost a century, the non-relativistic semiclassical expansion
of the total binding energy of atoms
Englert 1988
has guided the development
of density functional approximations, beginning with
Thomas-Fermi (TF) theory
Thomas 1927
;
Fermi 1928
and the
local density approximation (LDA) for exchange
Bloch 1929
;
Dirac 1930
.
In the seventies, Lieb and Simon proved
Lieb and Simon 1977
that the dominant term
in that expansion is given exactly by TF theory,
and in the eighties Schwinger and Englert showed explicitly that the LDA
recovers the dominant term for the atomic exchange
energy
Schwinger 1981
;
Englert and Schwinger 1982
;
Englert and Schwinger 1985a
. Recent analytic and
numerical evidence shows the same is
true for atomic correlation energies
Burke et al. 2016
;
Cancio et al. 2018
.

For exchange, recent focus has been on the
leading correction to LDA
Perdew et al. 2006
;
Elliott and Burke 2009
, see Fig. 1. Most modern
generalized gradient approximations (GGAs) — the starting point of most modern exchange-correlation approximations — yield a well-defined correction that can be compared to atomic data for large
Z
. The popular approximations known as PBE
Perdew et al. 1996
and B88
Becke 1988
both yield highly accurate approximations to this term for atoms, which are about double that of the gradient-expansion approximation
Kirzhnits 1957
;
Dreizler and Gross 1990
(GEA), yielding some of the insight behind PBEsol
Perdew et al. 2008
. The behavior for large
Z
has been built into several recent non-empirical approximations (SCAN
Sun et al. 2015
, APBE
Constantin et al. 2011
, acGGA
Cancio et al. 2018
).

The original works
Perdew et al. 2006
;
Elliott and Burke 2009
on expanding the beyond-LDA exchange energy for atoms,

\Delta E_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}=E_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}-E_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}^{\rm LDA}\,,

(1)

used simple powers of
Z^{1/3}
, based on the scaling behavior of the gradient expansion
for the
slowly-varying electron gas.

Here we provide three lines of evidence for the existence of a
Z\text{ln}Z
contribution, showing that the
analytic forms used as ‘exact conditions’ are likely incorrect, and should be replaced by those suggested below.

Thus, the current work not only contributes to the very long-standing search for the
expansion of the energy of atoms in mathematical physics, but also provides
a crucial correction to exact conditions which are built into
the latest modern density functional approximations, used throughout condensed matter physics, materials science, and chemistry.

A second direction shows analytically that

A third direction is a study of the Bohr atom

Next, we estimate
\Delta E_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}
theoretically. The LDA exchange energy is given by

E_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}^{\rm LDA}=-a_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}\int d^{3}r\ n^{4/3}({\bf r})\;,

(4)

where
a_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}=3\left(3/\pi\right)^{1/3}\!/4

Bloch 1929
;
Dirac 1930
, and insertion of the TF density
Lee et al. 2009
into this expression directly gives the dominant contribution
Burke et al. 2016
to exchange as
Z\to\infty
,

E^{\rm TF}=-A\,Z^{5/3}
.
For the beyond-LDA contribution to the exchange energy, Eq. (
1
), we try the GEA
Hohenberg and Kohn 1964
;
Dreizler and Gross 1990
,

\Delta E_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}^{\rm GEA}=-\mu^{\rm GE}a_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}\int\,n^{4/3}({\bf r})\,s^{2}({\bf r})\,d^{3}r\;,

(5)

where
s=|\nabla n|/(2k_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm F}n)
is the dimensionless gradient parameter,
k_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm F}=(3\pi^{2})^{1/3}n^{1/3}
is the local Fermi wavenumber, and

\mu^{\rm GE}=10/81

Kleinman and Lee 1988
.
Application of Eq. (
5
) to the slowly-varying gas
, or to a neutral atom using the density scaling of
Perdew et al. 2006
,
yields a term of order
Z
when scaled toward the TF limit.
However, the
present analysis amounts to scaling
the potential, in the sense of Refs.
Cangi et al. 2011
;
Cangi et al. 2013
.

While potential- and density-scaling are interchangeable for the dominant term
of the large-
Z
asymptotic expansion (TF theory), additional terms
appear for
potential scaling, such as the Scott correction to the
kinetic energy

5

so

(6)

This approximation fails in the region where the inner shell (1s) electrons dominate; see Fig. 2, which
shows

of alkaline earths up to

Keeping only the divergent contribution to Eq. (

\Delta E_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}^{\rm GEA}\simeq-\frac{9\mu^{\rm GE}}{8\pi^{2}}Z\int_{Z^{-1}}^{Z^{-1/3}}\frac{dr}{r}\;,

which yields a logarithmic term,

\Delta E_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}^{\rm GEA}=-\frac{3\mu^{\rm GE}}{4\pi^{2}}Z\text{ln}Z+O(Z)\;.

(8)

We define

B=-\lim_{Z\to\infty}\Delta E_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}/(Z\text{ln}Z)\;,

(10)

The presence of such a logarithmic term in the GEA for atoms was noticed in

We have no rationale for the difference between the result of the GEA, Eq. (

3

Perdew et al. 1986

Thus,
the beyond-LDA exchange energy of large-
Z
atoms has a leading
Z\text{ln}{Z}
term both numerically and in GEA,
but their coefficients disagree.

A similar analysis can be applied to the analog of Eq. (

Last, we turn to the implications for approximate functional development.
Our derivation applies to most GGA’s for the exchange energy, usually written in terms of an enhancement factor
F_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}
:

E_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}^{\rm GGA}=-a_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}\int\,n^{4/3}({\bf r})F_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}\bigl(s({\bf r})\bigr)\,d^{3}r\;.

(19)

Typically,
F_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}\approx 1+\mu^{\rm GGA}s^{2}+...
for small
s
, which dominates in the TF limit. Thus Eq. (
10
) applies, with
\mu^{\rm GE}
replaced by
\mu^{\rm GGA}
.
This yields
16.7
mHa for PBE and
20.9
mHa for B88, differing from the value of 25.4 mH of Eq. (
3
). However, both yield accurate
E_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}
for
Z
between 10 and 100, due to differences in the remaining terms of a large-
Z
fit. Thus, functionals that have been fit to large-
Z
data, such as SCAN, are accurate for all practical calculations.
In the future both the
O(Z\text{ln}Z)
and the
O(Z)
terms
should be addressed in developing approximate density functionals.

Using the hydrogen atom as a ‘norm’
Sun et al. 2015
, the conjecture above yields:

\Delta E^{\rm normed}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm X}=-Z\left\{\frac{\text{ln}Z}{4\pi^{2}}+\frac{5}{16}-0.2564\right\}

(20)

for neutral atoms,
which is indistinguishable from the straight line of Fig 1, and
contains no empirical parameters.

In conclusion, the present work is a step in the process of improving density functional approximations using asymptotic expansions for non-relativistic atoms: it identifies a logarithmic divergence in the coefficient of the leading
O(Z)
contribution to the beyond-LDA exchange energy, resulting in a leading
Z\text{ln}Z
term.

Further steps would involve studying existing approximations, evaluating the coefficients of both their
Z\text{ln}Z
terms and their
O(Z)
terms. Obtaining very-high-
Z
data for real atoms is crucial, possibly using simplified methods.

Analogous data for molecules and solids would also be very helpful, especially to determine any differences
based on the lack of classical turning surfaces in solids
Kaplan et al. 2021
.

But first and foremost, a derivation of the
Z\text{ln}Z
term from semiclassical theory, including the correct value of its coefficient, would provide a fundamental, detailed understanding of the exchange energy, and would be instrumental in guiding future developments in density functional theory.

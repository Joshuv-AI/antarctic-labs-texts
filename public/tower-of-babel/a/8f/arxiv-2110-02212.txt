One-Shot Yield–Cost Relations in General Quantum Resource Theories
Ryuji Takagi, Bartosz Regula, Mark M. Wilde

Abstract
Although it is well known that the amount of resources that can be asymptotically distilled from a quantum state or channel does not exceed the resource cost needed to produce it, the corresponding relation in the non-asymptotic regime hitherto has not been well understood. Here, we establish a quantitative relation between the one-shot distillable resource yield and dilution cost in terms of transformation errors involved in these processes. Notably, our bound is applicable to quantum state and channel manipulation with respect to any type of quantum resource and any class of free transformations thereof, encompassing broad types of settings, including entanglement, quantum thermodynamics, and quantum communication. We also show that our techniques provide strong converse bounds relating the distillable resource and resource dilution cost in the asymptotic regime. Moreover, we introduce a class of channels that generalize twirling maps encountered in many resource theories, and by directly connecting it with resource quantification, we compute analytically several smoothed resource measures and improve our one-shot yield–cost bound in relevant theories. We use these operational insights to exactly evaluate important measures for various resource states in the resource theory of magic states.

In this work, we establish a fundamental quantitative relation between the one-shot distillable resource and dilution cost that is applicable to general resource theories of quantum channels, accomplishing the two extensions in a single framework.
We further extend the one-shot bound to the asymptotic regime and obtain strong converse bounds between transformation rates, revealing also new relations in the asymptotic manipulation of quantum states.
Our framework not only accommodates well-known quantum resources that have been previously studied using related approaches — such as quantum entanglement and coherence — but also other settings that involve dynamical resources as central objects of study, e.g., quantum communication.
Notably, a major part of our main results does not even assume the convexity or closedness of the set of free channels, unlike most of the works dealing with general resource theories.
Moreover, our bounds hold for all chosen sets of free operations satisfying only minimal requirements, thus accommodating all physically motivated choices of free operations, including ones that may be equipped with complex structures.

An important technical aspect of our result is that, for the bounds to be most effective, they require the collapse of a class of resource measures based on Rényi divergences. Focusing on resource theories of states, we provide an operational interpretation for this and related conditions in terms of a special class of free operations that reflects the essential features of the “twirling” operation commonly encountered in various theories

We use this class of operations to obtain analytical expressions for a number of smoothed resource measures, leading also to an improved yield–cost bound for the case of state manipulation.
Finally, to demonstrate the applicability of our results, we use these operational insights in the resource theory of magic

Howard and Campbell 2017
;
Wang et al. 2019

We focus on discussing the main results and their implications in the main text, while deferring all proofs and technical details to the Appendices.

II
Resource theories of states

We begin by introducing the framework of resource theories of quantum states, in which quantum states are the central objects under study

1

In this manuscript, we restrict our attention to the cases in which underlying Hilbert spaces are finite dimensional.
.
Quantum information processing necessarily involves the manipulation of quantum states but commonly is performed under some physical restrictions, which limits the accessible set of quantum states and operations.
A primary example is a scenario in which two parties, Alice and Bob, are physically separated.
Unless they are connected by a quantum communication channel, they can only apply local quantum operations and exchange classical messages.
This restriction limits the accessible set of operations to the class known as local operations and classical communication (LOCC).
Importantly, LOCC channels cannot create entanglement for free but can only prepare states that are separable
Horodecki et al. 2009
.

The framework of resource theories allows us to deal formally with such restrictions
Chitambar and Gour 2019
.
It provides a recipe to quantify the amount of resource that cannot be created due to the restriction and characterize feasible resource manipulation using only accessible operations.
Each resource theory comes with a set
\mathbb{F}
of
free states
and a set
\mathbb{O}
of
free operations
.
They are subsets of quantum states and quantum channels (completely positive trace-preserving maps), respectively, and are considered to be accessible for free under a given setting.
The minimal requirement for free operations is that they should not create resourceful states out of free states, i.e.,
\Lambda\in\mathbb{O}\Rightarrow\Lambda(\sigma)\in\mathbb{F},\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
.
Physical considerations usually impose more constraints, but it is useful to consider the set of free operations consisting of all the operations satisfying this minimal requirement; we call this the maximal set of free operations and write it as
\mathbb{O}_{\max}
.
Consequently, any valid set of free operations is a subset of
\mathbb{O}_{\max}
.

A major strength of resource theories is that they allow us to evaluate the resourcefulness of a given state quantitatively.
It is made possible by introducing
resource measures
, which are functions from quantum states to real numbers that represent the amount of resource contained in the state.
The requirement for resource measures is that they output the same value for free states, and they do not increase under the application of free operations.

Here, we consider two resource measures that can be defined for an arbitrary set
\mathbb{F}
of free states and an arbitrary set
\mathbb{O}
of free operations.
The first one is known as the
generalized robustness
, or alternatively,
max-relative entropy measure

Vidal and Tarrach 1999
;
Steiner 2003
;
Datta 2009a
;
Datta 2009b
defined as

\displaystyle D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\left\{\left.\log(1+s)\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\frac{\rho+s\tau}{1+s}\in\mathbb{F},\,\tau\in\mathbb{D}\right\}

(1)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left.\lambda\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq 2^{\lambda}\sigma,\,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right\},

where
\mathbb{D}
is the set of all quantum states.
The other type of resource measure relevant to this work is
min-relative entropy measure

Datta 2009a
, defined as

\displaystyle D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}D_{\min}(\rho\|\sigma),

(2)

where
D_{\min}(\rho\|\sigma)\coloneqq-\log\Tr[\Pi_{\rho}\sigma]
, with
\Pi_{\rho}
denoting the projector onto the support of
\rho
.

These quantities lay out a platform for establishing relations between resource manipulation and resource quantification.
Resource distillation and dilution particularly stand out as important subclasses of resource manipulation tasks.
Resource distillation is a protocol to transform a given state to a state in the family
\mathbb{T}
of
reference states
using free operations, while resource dilution is the opposite task in which a reference state is to be transformed to the desired state.
The optimal performance of these tasks is characterized by the one-shot
distillable resource
and
dilution cost
, defined respectively as

\displaystyle d^{\epsilon}_{\mathbb{O}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup\left\{\left.\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)\!\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\!F(\Lambda(\rho),\Phi)\geq 1\!-\!\epsilon,\;\Phi\!\in\mathbb{T},\;\Lambda\in\mathbb{O}\right\},

(3)

\displaystyle c^{\epsilon}_{\mathbb{O}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\left\{\left.\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)\!\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\!F(\rho,\Lambda(\Phi))\geq 1\!-\!\epsilon,\;\Phi\!\in\mathbb{T},\;\Lambda\in\mathbb{O}\right\},

where
F(\rho,\sigma)\coloneqq\|\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\vphantom{\rho}\sigma}\|_{1}^{2}
is the fidelity
Uhlmann 1976
, and
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{F}}
refers to an arbitrary resource monotone.
For simplicity, we take
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{F}}=D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}
in the above definition throughout this manuscript.

To provide more insight into these definitions and connect them with notions of distillation and dilution familiar from commonly encountered resource theories, let us consider the case of quantum entanglement. Here,
\mathbb{F}
denotes all separable quantum states, and the family
\mathbb{T}
of reference states can be taken simply as copies of the maximally entangled qubit Bell state:

\mathbb{T}=\{\Phi_{2}^{\otimes n}\,|\;n\in\mathbb{N}\},

(4)

where
\ket{\Phi_{2}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{00}+\ket{11})
and we use the shorthand notation
\Phi_{2}=\ket{\Phi_{2}}\!\bra{\Phi_{2}}
. Combined with the fact that
Shimony 1995

D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi_{2}^{\otimes n})=n,

(5)

the quantities
d^{\epsilon}_{\mathbb{O}}
and
c^{\epsilon}_{\mathbb{O}}
can be alternatively understood as asking, respectively, how many copies of
\ket{\Phi_{2}}
can be obtained from a given state, or how many copies of
\ket{\Phi_{2}}
are necessary to produce a given state. We use more general definitions of distillation yield and resource cost to accommodate potentially more complex families of reference states and resource theories where a simple choice of reference states
\mathbb{T}
might not be available.

III
Resource theories of channels

We now introduce resource theories of quantum channels, extending the framework described in the previous section.
To take into account the various structures that may be imposed on channels, we employ the approach of Ref.
Regula and Takagi 2021b
and consider a subset
\mathbb{O}_{\rm all}
of quantum channels as the set of channels of interest.
Each situation designates a set
\mathbb{O}\subset\mathbb{O}_{\rm all}
of
free channels
that does not contain any resource considered precious under the given setting.
We also need to introduce the set of free operations that are accessible to manipulate channels.
General channel transformations are described by quantum
superchannels

Chiribella et al. 2008a
;
Chiribella et al. 2008b
.
Superchannels are linear maps that map quantum channels to quantum channels, and they are physically realizable by sandwiching an input channel with a pre-processing channel and a post-processing channel, both of which can also be connected by a quantum memory.
Letting
\mathbb{O}_{\rm all}^{\prime}
denote the set of output channels of interest, the set of relevant superchannels is specified as

\mathbb{S}_{\rm all}\coloneqq\left\{\left.\Xi\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\Xi(\mathcal{E})\in\mathbb{O}_{\rm all}^{\prime},\,\forall\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}_{\rm all}\right\}.

(6)

Then, one can consider a subset
\mathbb{S}\subset\mathbb{S}_{\rm all}
of superchannels as
free superchannels
, serving as free operations.
The minimal requirement for free operations forces
\Theta\in\mathbb{S}\Rightarrow\Theta(\mathcal{M})\in\mathbb{O}^{\prime},\,\forall\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{O}
.
Analogous to the case of state theories, we also define the maximal set of free superchannels as the set of superchannels satisfying the above minimal condition and call it
\mathbb{S}_{\max}
.

We can analogously introduce resource quantifiers that do not increase under any set of free superchannels.
The relevant channel resource measures that we need are the max-relative entropy measure
Díaz et al. 2018
;
Takagi and Regula 2019
;
Liu and Winter 2019
;
Liu and Yuan 2020
, defined for every
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}_{\rm all}
as

D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{E})\coloneqq\inf_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{O}}\sup_{\psi}D_{\max}(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\psi)\|\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{M}(\psi)),

(7)

and the min-relative entropy measure
Liu and Winter 2019
;
Liu and Yuan 2020
, defined as

D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{E})\coloneqq\inf_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{O}}\sup_{\psi}D_{\min}(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\psi)\|\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{M}(\psi)),

(8)

where the optimization is restricted to every pure input state
\psi
, without loss of generality
Gilchrist et al. 2005
.

Then, we can formalize the tasks of resource distillation and dilution.
Resource distillation is a task that transforms a given channel
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}_{\rm all}
to a channel in the set
\mathbb{T}\subset\mathbb{O}_{\rm all}^{\prime}
of reference channels using free superchannels
\mathbb{S}
, while resource dilution transforms a reference channel to the desired channel
\mathcal{E}
.
The optimal performance of these tasks is characterized by the one-shot distillable resource and dilution cost, defined respectively as

\displaystyle d^{\epsilon}_{\mathbb{S}}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup\left\{\left.\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T})\!\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\!F(\Theta(\mathcal{E}),\mathcal{T})\geq 1\!-\!\epsilon,\;\mathcal{T}\!\in\mathbb{T},\;\Theta\in\mathbb{S}\right\},

(9)

\displaystyle c^{\epsilon}_{\mathbb{S}}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\left\{\left.\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T})\!\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\!F(\mathcal{E},\Theta(\mathcal{T}))\geq 1\!-\!\epsilon,\;\mathcal{T}\!\in\mathbb{T},\;\Theta\in\mathbb{S}\right\},

where

F(\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})\coloneqq\min_{\psi}F(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{1}(\psi),\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{2}(\psi))

(10)

is the worst-case fidelity
Gilchrist et al. 2005
(also called channel fidelity), and
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}
refers to an arbitrary resource monotone, which we take as
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}=D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}
in the above definition for simplicity.

In our discussion below, we will make the natural assumption that the reference channel
\mathcal{T}
is a pure target channel, in the sense that
\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{T}(\psi)
is pure for every pure state
\psi
. This covers general types of distillation processes encountered in physical resource theories, where
\mathcal{T}
plays the role of a noiseless resource. If the input and output systems are identical, then the condition implies that
\mathcal{T}
is unitary, while if the input and output systems have different dimensions, then it implies that
\mathcal{T}
is an isometry.
When the input space is trivial — which is the case, e.g., in the special case of resource theories of states — then
\mathcal{T}
reduces to a pure-state preparation channel.

IV
Yield–cost relations

Our first main result is a relation between the one-shot distillable resource and dilution cost, which we dub a yield–cost relation, applicable to (1) dynamical resource theories of channels, (2) any set of free channels, and (3) any set of free superchannels defined for the given free channels.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that for every channel
\mathcal{T}
in the chosen reference set
\mathbb{T}
,
\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{T}(\psi)
is pure for every pure state
\psi
, and
\mathcal{T}
satisfies
D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})=D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})
.
Then, for all
\epsilon_{1}\in[0,1)
and
\epsilon_{2}\in[0,1-\epsilon_{1}]
, for every quantum channel
\mathcal{E}
, and for every set
\mathbb{S}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{\max}
of free superchannels, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{S}}^{\epsilon_{1}}(\mathcal{E})\leq c_{\mathbb{S}}^{\epsilon_{2}}(\mathcal{E})+\log f(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}),

(11)

where
f(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
is a function defined as

\displaystyle f(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})\coloneqq\min\left\{(1-\epsilon_{1}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}})^{-1},(\sqrt{1-\epsilon_{2}}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}})^{-2}\right\}

(12)

for
\epsilon_{1}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}<1
and

\displaystyle f(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})\coloneqq(\sqrt{1-\epsilon_{2}}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}})^{-2}

(13)

otherwise.

For
\epsilon_{1}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}<1
, each quantity can be tighter than the other for certain error regions.
Indeed, direct calculation reveals that
(1-\epsilon_{1}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}})^{-1}\leq(\sqrt{1-\epsilon_{2}}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}})^{-2}
if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1-\sqrt{1-\epsilon_{2}})(1-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}})\leq\epsilon_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\sqrt{1-\epsilon_{2}})(1-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}).

(14)

This gives a fundamental relation between distillable resource and dilution cost in the one-shot regime under any chosen set of free superchannels.
In particular, when transformation errors are taken into account, the one-shot cost could be smaller than the one-shot yield.
Our result establishes a quantitative trade-off relation between a potential yield–cost gap and the transformation inaccuracy.

The only assumption required for the result to hold is the choice of reference channels which satisfy
D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})=D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})
. The collapse of the two measures to the same value is a common property of maximally resourceful states or channels
Liu et al. 2019
;
Regula et al. 2020
;
Regula and Takagi 2021b
, which are precisely the most appropriate references employed in distillation and dilution protocols in practice.
We review examples of reference states and channels in several physical settings in Appendix
B
.
We will also return to this condition in Section
V
to provide more intuition behind the assumption and give an operationally motivated understanding of it.

Theorem
1
is a direct consequence of the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an arbitrary quantum channel, and let
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}
be arbitrary real numbers such that
\epsilon_{1}\in[0,1)
,
\epsilon_{2}\in[0,1-\epsilon_{1}]
. Also, let
\mathcal{T}_{1}
be a channel for which
\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{1}(\psi)
is pure for every pure state
\psi

\Theta_{1}\in\mathbb{S}
such that

, and let
\mathcal{T}_{2}
be an arbitrary channel for which there exists
\Theta_{2}\in\mathbb{S}
such that
F(\Theta_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{2}),\mathcal{E})\geq 1-\epsilon_{2}
. Then

\displaystyle D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T}_{1})\leq D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T}_{2})+\log f(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}).

(15)

where

is the function introduced in Theorem
1
.

A natural question following these results is whether they smoothly connect to an asymptotic yield–cost relation.
In asymptotic distillation (and analogously for dilution), the goal of the tasks is commonly to obtain as many copies of a fixed reference channel as possible from multiple copies of the given channel, in such a way that the transformation error vanishes in the limit of infinite copies. The figure of merit is then the ratio of the number of obtained copies of the reference channel to the number of used copies of the given channel.
The following result provides a relation between the optimal rates for the asymptotic distillation and dilution in a more general setting, in which the errors do not necessarily approach zero in the asymptotic limit.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an arbitrary input channel, and let
\mathcal{T}
be some target reference channel for which
\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{T}(\psi)
is pure for every pure state
\psi
.
Let
d
be any rate of distillation such that there exists a sequence
\{\Theta_{n}\}_{n}
of free superchannels with

\displaystyle 1-F\left(\Theta_{n}(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}),\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor dn\rfloor}\right)\eqqcolon\delta_{n}.

(16)

Also, let
c
be any rate of dilution such that there exists a sequence
\{\Theta_{n}\}_{n}
of free superchannels with

\displaystyle 1-F\left(\Theta_{n}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lceil cn\rceil}),\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}\right)\eqqcolon\epsilon_{n}.

(17)

Suppose that the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

It holds that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\epsilon_{n}+\delta_{n}<1
;

(ii)

The resource theory is closed under tensor products; that is,
\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2}\in\mathbb{O}\Rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}\in\mathbb{O}
.

Then the following inequality holds

\displaystyle d\cdot D^{\infty}_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})\leq c\cdot D^{\infty}_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T}),

(18)

where

\displaystyle D^{\infty}_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D_{\min}\left(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes n}\right),

(19)

\displaystyle D^{\infty}_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D_{\max}\left(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes n}\right).

Let us briefly discuss the assumptions of Theorem
3
.
Condition (i) simply means that the errors of distillation and dilution do not get too large at the same time; it is satisfied, for instance, when we take
\lim_{n\to\infty}\epsilon_{n}=0
and
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\delta_{n}<1
or vice versa. Condition (ii) is a basic property obeyed by virtually all theories encountered in practice.

Although the result of Theorem
3
is appealing since it follows straightforwardly from our one-shot relation in Theorem
1
, one can ask whether it is possible to derive asymptotic relations that have better dependence on quantities such as
D^{\infty}_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})
and

, as well as are free from the constraint on the target channel
\mathcal{T}
, that is, the constraint that
\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{T}(\psi)
is pure for every pure state
\psi
.
We discuss several variations on this idea in Appendix
C

\mathcal{T}
but come with an additional condition on the sequences

and
\{\delta_{n}\}_{n}

3

The result of Theorem
3
implies in particular the strong converse bounds given in Corollary
4

We define the asymptotic distillable resource
d_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}
as the largest achievable rate at which copies of
\mathcal{T}
can be extracted from a given channel, and, analogously, the asymptotic resource cost
c_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}
as the least rate at which copies of
\mathcal{T}
are needed to produce a given channel. Imposing that the error in such transformations vanishes in the asymptotic limit, we have

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\!\sup\left\{\,\!d\,\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\left|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\Theta_{n}\in\mathbb{S}}F\left(\Theta_{n}(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}),\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor dn\rfloor}\right)\!=\!1\!\right.\right\}

(20)

\displaystyle c_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\!\inf\left\{\,\!c\,\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\left|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\Theta_{n}\in\mathbb{S}}F\left(\Theta_{n}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lceil cn\rceil}),\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}\right)\!=\!1\!\right.\right\}.

As counterparts to the above asymptotic quantities, we define also the corresponding strong converse quantities as
Hayashi 2016
;
Khatri and Wilde 2020

\displaystyle{\tilde{d}}_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\!\inf\left\{\,\!d\,\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\left|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\Theta_{n}\in\mathbb{S}}F\left(\Theta_{n}(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}),\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor dn\rfloor}\right)\!=\!0\!\right.\right\}

(21)

\displaystyle{\tilde{c}}_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\!\sup\left\{\,\!c\,\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\left|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\Theta_{n}\in\mathbb{S}}F\left(\Theta_{n}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lceil cn\rceil}),\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}\right)\!=\!0\!\right.\right\},

with the interpretation of the strong converse distillable resource as the smallest rate at which the error is guaranteed to converge to one in the asymptotic limit, and that for the strong converse dilution cost as the largest rate at which the error is guaranteed to converge to one.
The following equivalent expressions for the strong converse quantities hold:

\displaystyle{\tilde{d}}_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle=\!\sup\left\{\,\!d\,\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\left|\;\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\Theta_{n}\in\mathbb{S}}F\left(\Theta_{n}(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}),\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor dn\rfloor}\right)\!>\!0\!\right.\right\}

(22)

\displaystyle{\tilde{c}}_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle=\!\inf\left\{\,\!c\,\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\left|\;\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\Theta_{n}\in\mathbb{S}}F\left(\Theta_{n}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lceil cn\rceil}),\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}\right)\!>\!0\!\right.\right\},

which can alternatively be understood as weakening the requirements imposed on the transformation error in Eq. (
20
).
Here the interpretation is as follows: unlike the quantities in (
20
), the strong converse quantities no longer ask that the error converges to zero, but represent the best rates at which the error does not converge to one — they therefore provide a general threshold on achievable asymptotic transformations even when perfect conversion is not required.
Note that
d_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})\leq{\tilde{d}}_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})
and
c_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})\geq{\tilde{c}}_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})
by definition.

Theorem
3
implies the following strong converse bounds.

Corollary 4
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an arbitrary input channel, and let
\mathcal{T}
be some target reference channel for which
\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{T}(\psi)
is pure for every pure state
\psi
.
Suppose that the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

The resource theory is closed under tensor products; that is,
\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2}\in\mathbb{O}\Rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}\in\mathbb{O}
;

(ii)

The target channel satisfies
D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})=D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})
and
D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes n})=nD_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})
for every
n
.

Then the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})\leq{\tilde{c}}_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T}),\quad{\tilde{d}}_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})\leq c_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T}).

(23)

We remark here that the definitions of strong converse quantities and Corollary
4
imply the following fundamental inequality:

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})\leq c_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T}).

(24)

Here, condition (i) is generally satisfied in physical theories, but condition (ii) imposes non-trivial requirements on the choice of a suitable target channel
\mathcal{T}
. In particular, in addition to the collapse of the max- and min-relative entropy measures to the same value, it requires that the min-relative entropy measure is additive, i.e.
D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes n})=nD_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})
. Although this constitutes a seemingly restrictive condition, the existence of channels or states satisfying this requirement is actually a common property of important resource theories, obeyed, e.g., by quantum entanglement, quantum thermodynamics, magic, and quantum communication (cf.
(
Regula and Takagi 2021a
, Table 1)
). Intuitively, the condition can be thought of as characterizing how well the given reference
\mathcal{T}
serves as an intermediary channel in distillation and dilution protocols.

We stress that all the results in this section automatically provide bounds for state theories as special cases; when one is interested in a resource theory of quantum states with free states
\mathbb{F}
and free operations
\mathbb{O}
, the same results hold by replacing free superchannels
\mathbb{S}
with free operations
\mathbb{O}
and free channels
\mathbb{O}
with free states
\mathbb{F}
.
Furthermore, in the case of state transformations, the generalized quantum Stein’s lemma
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
implies that the asymptotic distillable resource coincides with its strong converse rate when the set of free states and the reference state satisfy some mild assumptions — see Appendix
D
for details.
When the generalized quantum Stein’s lemma holds, for the asymptotic distillation and dilution for an arbitrary state
\rho
with respect to a reference state
\Phi
under free operations
\mathbb{O}
, we can improve the bound in Corollary
4
to

\displaystyle{\tilde{d}}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\rho,\Phi)\leq{\tilde{c}}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\rho,\Phi).

(25)

This applies in particular to the resource theory of quantum entanglement, which solves an open problem posed in Ref.
Wilde 2021
. The implication of (
25
) is that the yield–cost relation still holds even when neither of the errors is required to vanish in the asymptotic limit, which gives a significant strengthening of the inequalities in (
23
).

We remark that another approach to asymptotic transformation rates of states and the consequent relations between asymptotic resource yield and cost in general resource theories was considered in Ref.
Kuroiwa and Yamasaki 2020
.

V
Operational account for reference states

We saw that the relation between
D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}
and
D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}
plays a major role in establishing the yield–cost relation in general resource theories.
Here, we give operational insights into the properties of these quantifiers and in particular the condition
D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})=D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})
that we have encountered previously.
By studying the interplay between these and other related measures, we will be able to quantify a number of smoothed resource monotones for target reference states in general resource theories.
In this section, we restrict our attention to theories of quantum states, and we use
\mathbb{F}
and
\mathbb{O}
to specify the set of free states and free operations, respectively.
In what follows, we also assume that
\mathbb{F}
is a convex and closed set.

In addition to
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}
and
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}
, we also consider another type of robustness measure known as the
standard robustness

Vidal and Tarrach 1999
, defined as

\displaystyle D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\left.\log(1+s)\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\frac{\rho+s\tau}{1+s}\in\mathbb{F},\tau\in\mathbb{F}\right\},

(26)

We also define two smooth robustness quantities as
Datta 2009a
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010b

\displaystyle D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\rho)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\left\{\left.D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;F(\rho^{\prime},\rho)\geq 1-\epsilon\right\},

(27)

\displaystyle D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\rho)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\left\{\left.D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;F(\rho^{\prime},\rho)\geq 1-\epsilon\right\},

(28)

and two types of hypothesis-testing relative entropy measures
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
;
Regula et al. 2018
;
Regula et al. 2020
:

\displaystyle D_{H,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\rho)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\rho\|\sigma),

(29)

\displaystyle D_{H,\aff(\mathbb{F})}^{\epsilon}(\rho)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf_{\sigma\in\aff(\mathbb{F})}D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\rho\|\sigma),

where

D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\rho\|\sigma)\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq P\leq\mathbbm{1}\\
\Tr(P\rho)\geq 1-\epsilon\end{subarray}}\log\Tr(P\sigma)^{-1}

(30)

is the hypothesis-testing relative entropy
Buscemi and Datta 2010
;
Brandão and Datta 2011
;
Wang and Renner 2012
.
We used
\aff(\mathbb{F})
to denote the affine hull of
\mathbb{F}
, which is the smallest affine subspace that contains
\mathbb{F}
.
The min-relative entropy is obtained as a special case of the hypothesis-testing relative entropy measure as
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=D_{H,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon=0}(\rho)
.
We use this correspondence to define the affine min-relative entropy measure
Regula et al. 2020
;
Regula and Takagi 2021b
as

\displaystyle D_{\min,\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho)

\displaystyle\coloneqq D_{H,\aff(\mathbb{F})}^{\epsilon=0}(\rho)

(31)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\sigma\in\aff(\mathbb{F})}D_{H}^{\epsilon=0}(\rho\|\sigma).

Then, the following ordering holds for an arbitrary state
\rho
:

\displaystyle D_{\min,\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho)\leq D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\leq D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\leq D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\rho),

(32)

where the first inequality follows because
\mathbb{F}\subseteq\aff(\mathbb{F})
, the second inequality because
D_{\min}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)
for all states
\rho
and
\sigma
, and the third inequality from the definitions of the generalized and standard robustness measures.
Depending on the structure of
\mathbb{F}
, some of these measures may exhibit drastic behavior.

D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}
and
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}
take finite values for every state
\rho
as long as
\mathbb{F}
contains at least one full-rank state, which is satisfied by most of the relevant theories.
However,
D_{s,\mathbb{F}}
may diverge if
\mathbb{F}
is
reduced dimensional

Regula et al. 2020
, meaning that
\mathbb{F}
has zero volume in the set of all states and
{\rm span}(\mathbb{F})
is not the whole space of self-adjoint operators.
Examples of reduced-dimensional theories include the theories of coherence and thermal non-equilibrium.
On the other hand, for
full-dimensional
theories, which are not reduced dimensional and include the theories of entanglement and magic as examples,

D_{s,\mathbb{F}}
remains finite but
D_{\min,\aff(\mathbb{F})}
takes the value zero for every state
\rho
.
Therefore,
D_{s,\mathbb{F}}
is usually a relevant resource quantifier in full-dimensional theories while
D_{\min,\aff(\mathbb{F})}
is relevant in reduced-dimensional theories
Regula et al. 2020
;
Regula and Takagi 2021b
.

Although these measures have been introduced in a rather abstract manner, they play crucial roles in the quantitative characterization of distillation and dilution — under certain assumptions on the target state
\Phi
, the value of the one-shot resource yield
d^{\epsilon}_{\mathbb{O}}(\rho)
under the maximal set of free operations is directly related to
D^{\epsilon}_{H,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
or
D^{\epsilon}_{H,\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho)
, while the value of the resource cost
c^{\epsilon}_{\mathbb{O}}(\rho)
corresponds to the value of
D^{\epsilon}_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
or
D^{\epsilon}_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

Liu et al. 2019
;
Regula et al. 2020
.
A necessary requirement for a precise description of distillation or dilution to be possible was that, when evaluated on the reference state
\Phi
, the resource measures all collapse to the same value; specifically, in full-dimensional theories one needs that

\displaystyle D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi),

(33)

while in reduced-dimensional theories one instead requires

\displaystyle D_{\min,\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\Phi)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi).

(34)

In some theories, such as entanglement or coherence, the existence of such states is natural: the maximally entangled or coherent states always satisfy the requirement. It is rather remarkable that maximally resourceful states satisfy
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)
in
any
convex resource theory
Regula et al. 2020
, but this is still not sufficient to guarantee that Eqs. (
33
)–(
34
) hold in general — we expect this to be a resource-dependent property which needs to be verified explicitly in each specific setting.

Here, we find that Eqs. (
33
)–(
34
) can have operational implications, which then help evaluate other resource measures introduced in this section.
In particular, we provide an understanding of the conditions (
33
)–(
34
) in terms of a twirling-like free operation.

Lemma 5
.

If a state
\Phi
satisfies
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)
or
D_{\min,\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\Phi)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)
, there exists a free operation
\Lambda\in\mathbb{O}_{\max}
defined by an operator
0\leq P^{\star}\leq\mathbbm{1}
with
\Tr[P^{\star}\Phi]=1
of the form

\displaystyle\Lambda(\cdot)=\Tr[P^{\star}\cdot]\Phi+\Tr[(\mathbbm{1}-P^{\star})\cdot]\sigma^{\star},

(35)

such that
\sigma^{\star}
is a state orthogonal to
\Phi
, satisfying
\Tr[P\sigma^{\star}]=\Tr[\Phi\sigma^{\star}]=0
.
When
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)
, one can further take
\sigma^{\star}
to be a free state.

This free operation possesses a property similar to well-known group twirling operations such as the isotropic twirling
\int U\otimes U^{*}(\cdot)U^{\dagger}\otimes U^{*\dagger}\,\mathrm{d}U
in entanglement theory
Horodecki and Horodecki 1999
, in that it maps states to the given reference state or its complement, while stabilizing a specific state
\Phi
.
Such twirling operations were found to be useful to evaluate several entanglement measures for states invariant under them
Vollbrecht and Werner 2001
.
In particular, the existence of such an operation allows us to obtain exact expressions for smoothed resource measures, which
a priori
require a non-trivial optimization over all states within an error
\epsilon
.

Proposition 6
.

If a state
\Phi
satisfies
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=:r
, then for every
\epsilon\in[0,1)
it holds that

\displaystyle D_{H,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi)=r+\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}

(36)

and

\displaystyle D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi)=\max\left\{r-\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon},0\right\}.

(37)

Similarly, if a state
\Phi
satisfies
D_{\min,\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\Phi)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=:r
, then

\displaystyle D_{H,\aff(\mathbb{F})}^{\epsilon}(\Phi)=D_{H,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi)=r+\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}

(38)

and

\displaystyle D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi)=\max\left\{r-\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon},0\right\}.

(39)

A crucial property which makes this result possible is that optimal states in the optimization of the smoothed resource measures can always be taken of the form
\Phi_{\kappa}=\kappa\Phi+(1-\kappa)\sigma^{\star}
, as obtained through the operation in Lemma
5
.
Such states share many useful properties with isotropic states of entanglement theory, and indeed several quantitative insights in the description of isotropic states in works such as Ref.
Vollbrecht and Werner 2001
can be extended to general resource theories. In particular, the entropic resource quantifiers can be computed exactly for such states.

Proposition 7
.

Suppose a state
\Phi
satisfies
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=:r
, and let
\sigma^{\star}
be the state in (
35
).
Then,

\displaystyle D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi_{\kappa})=\begin{cases}0&0\leq\kappa<1\\
r&\kappa=1,\end{cases}

(40)

and

\displaystyle D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi_{\kappa})

\displaystyle=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi_{\kappa})

(41)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{r-\log\frac{1}{\kappa},0\right\}

for every
\Phi_{\kappa}=\kappa\Phi+(1-\kappa)\sigma^{\star}
with
\kappa\in[0,1]
.
Similarly, if a state
\Phi
satisfies
D_{\min,\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\Phi)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=:r
, then

\displaystyle D_{\min,\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\Phi_{\kappa})

\displaystyle=D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi_{\kappa})

(42)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&0<\kappa<1\\
\log\frac{1}{1-2^{-r}}&\kappa=0\\
r&\kappa=1,\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi_{\kappa})

\displaystyle=\max\left\{r-\log\frac{1}{\kappa},\,\log\frac{1-\kappa}{1-2^{-r}}\right\}

(43)

for every
\Phi_{\kappa}=\kappa\Phi+(1-\kappa)\sigma^{\star}
with
\kappa\in[0,1]
.

We present a more complete discussion of the quantitative properties of the isotropic-like states
\Phi_{\kappa}
in Appendix
E
, where we also show how the smoothed entropic measures can be computed for this class of states.

These exact evaluations of the resource measures allow us to employ the argument in Ref.
Wilde 2021
to obtain an alternative bound to that given in Theorem
1
.

Theorem 8
.

If the chosen reference set obeys
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)\;\forall\Phi\in\mathbb{T}
or
D_{\min,\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\Phi)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)\;\forall\Phi\in\mathbb{T}
, then for every set
\mathbb{O}\subseteq\mathbb{O}_{\max}
of free operations and all
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\geq 0
satisfying
\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}<1
, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon_{1}}(\rho)\leq c_{\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon_{2}}(\rho)+\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon^{\prime}},

(44)

where
\epsilon^{\prime}\coloneqq\left(\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}(1-\epsilon_{2})}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}(1-\epsilon_{1})}\right)^{2}
.

This improves the bound in Theorem
1
, as it gives a tighter upper bound for all regions of
(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
with
\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}<1
, as we prove in Appendix
G
.
We also remark that this bound becomes essentially tight when
\mathbb{O}=\mathbb{O}_{\max}
,
\rho\in\mathbb{T}
, and either
\epsilon_{1}
or
\epsilon_{2}
is 0.
This can be shown by using
d_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}^{\epsilon_{1}}(\rho)=D_{H,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon_{1}}(\rho)
and
c_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}^{\epsilon_{2}}(\rho)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon_{2}}(\rho)
when
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
, and
d_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}^{\epsilon_{1}}(\rho)=D_{H,\aff(\mathbb{F})}^{\epsilon_{1}}(\rho)
and
c_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}^{\epsilon_{2}}(\rho)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon_{2}}(\rho)
when
D_{\min,\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\rho)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
(up to some floor or ceiling due to a discrete structure of
\mathbb{T}
)
Regula and Takagi 2021b
, as well as explicitly evaluating these measures by Proposition
6
.

So far we have employed the property of several resource measures evaluated for a reference state to show the existence of a twirling-like operation (
35
) and presented its applications.
We now also investigate the opposite direction, asking whether the existence of a certain type of free operation provides insights into the relation between different resource measures.
The following result addresses this question.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\mathcal{S}(\Phi)
be a set of channels stabilizing
\Phi
, defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\Phi)\coloneqq\left\{\left.\Tr[P\cdot]\Phi+\tilde{\Lambda}(\cdot)\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;0\leq P\leq\mathbbm{1},\Tr[\Phi P]=1,\tilde{\Lambda}\in{\rm CP}\right\}

(45)

where
CP
is the set of completely positive maps.
Then, if there exists a free operation
\Lambda\in\mathbb{O}_{\max}\cap\mathcal{S}(\Phi)
, the equality

\displaystyle D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)

(46)

holds.
In addition, if
\Lambda
is completely free, i.e.,
\mathrm{id}\otimes\Lambda\in\mathbb{O}_{\max}
where
\mathrm{id}
is the identity map on an arbitrary ancillary system, then

\displaystyle D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi^{\otimes n})=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi^{\otimes n}),\ \forall n.

(47)

Moreover, if
\tilde{\Lambda}
can be taken as a free subchannel in (
45
), i.e.,
\tilde{\Lambda}(\sigma)\propto\cone(\mathbb{F}),\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
, then

\displaystyle D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi),

(48)

where
\cone(X)\coloneqq\left\{\left.\lambda x\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\lambda\geq 0,\ x\in X\right\}
.

Together with Lemma
5
, we conclude a general operational correspondence between the collapse of resource measures and the existence of twirling-like free operations: we have that
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)
if and only if there exists a map of the form (
35
) with a free state
\sigma^{\star}
.

However, it is not always easy to find a channel
\Lambda\in\mathbb{O}_{\max}\cap\mathcal{S}(\Phi)
to apply Lemma
9
.
The following result helps to find such a channel by formally relating the group-theoretic twirling operations inspired by the original LOCC twirlings
Werner 1989
;
Horodecki and Horodecki 1999
to the form of (
45
) necessary to apply Lemma
9
.

Proposition 10
.

Let
\Phi
be a pure state.
Suppose that there exists a convex set
\mathbb{O}\subseteq\mathbb{O}_{\max}
of free operations and a finite or a compact Lie group
G
with a unitary representation
\{U(g)\}_{g\in G}
that satisfies
U(g)\cdot U(g)^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{O}
and
U(g)\ket{\Phi}=e^{i\phi_{g}}\ket{\Phi}\ \forall g\in G
for some set of eigenvalues
\{e^{i\phi_{g}}\}_{g}
.
If
\ket{\Phi}
is the unique simultaneous eigenvector of all
U(g)
’s with eigenvalues
\{e^{i\phi_{g}}\}_{g}
, then
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)
holds.
Moreover, if
\mathbb{O}
is completely free, then
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi^{\otimes n})=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi^{\otimes n})
holds for every positive integer
n
.

This result allows us to find an appropriate free operation to ensure the condition required in Theorems
1
and
8
.
We indeed find that Lemma
9
and Proposition
10
are helpful to obtain new insights into specific resource theories, which we now demonstrate.

VI
Application: Theory of magic

As we emphasized before, our results immediately hold in general types of quantum resources of both states and channels, and we direct the interested reader, e.g., to the recent Refs.
Liu et al. 2019
;
Regula and Takagi 2021a
;
Fang and Liu 2022
;
Regula and Takagi 2021b
;
Yuan et al. 2020
as well as to Appendix
B
for a discussion of how such general methods can be applied in specific theories such as quantum communication. Here, we discuss a non-trivial example in which the quantification of resource measures for many important states has still not been understood — the theory of magic (non-stabilizerness)
Veitch et al. 2014
;
Howard and Campbell 2017
. Applying the results of our work, we show how they can be used to provide new quantitative insights and reveal broad and useful relations for this resource.

To realize scalable fault-tolerant quantum computation, it is essential to encode the whole computation in a higher-dimensional space using an error-correcting code.
Clifford gates particularly stand out as a subset of quantum gates that admits efficient fault-tolerant encoding on many error-correcting codes.
However, to form a universal gate set, we also need to implement a non-Clifford gate.
It is usually accomplished by the gate teleportation technique
Gottesman and Chuang 1999
;
Zhou et al. 2000
, which simulates the action of a non-Clifford gate by combining a Clifford operation and a
magic state
, which cannot be created solely by Clifford operations.
Since magic states are hard to produce in a fault-tolerant manner, they are precious resources under this setting, motivating us to consider the quantification and manipulation of them using a resource-theoretic formalism
Veitch et al. 2014
;
Howard and Campbell 2017
.

In particular, the optimal performance of magic state distillation and dilution has been a central question in the field, as it is the most resource-demanding part to realize fault-tolerant universal quantum computation.
Our results establish a relation between the optimal performance of these two, under the assumption that the reference resource states satisfy the aforementioned conditions on resource measures.
Here, we investigate these conditions for some well-known states that can be good candidates for reference states in distillation and dilution protocols.
We find that our approach, particularly Lemma
9
and Proposition
10
, provides new operational insights into the evaluation of resource measures, which may be of independent interest.

Stabilizer states
are those that can be represented by a probabilistic mixture of eigenstates of Pauli operators.
In the resource theory of magic, stabilizer states form the set
\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}
of free states.
Although it is often assumed that quantum computation is carried out in multi-qubit systems, higher-dimensional qu
d
it systems also stand as potential candidates for a large-scale quantum computing architecture
Campbell et al. 2012
;
Howard and Vala 2012
.
Resource theories have been developed for both settings
Veitch et al. 2014
;
Howard and Campbell 2017
;
Wang et al. 2020
, and we will consider several standard magic states defined in these scenarios.

Let us start with multi-qubit systems.
For single-qubit states, the farthest states from the set of stabilizer states are positioned at
\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\pm 1,\pm 1,\pm 1)
in Bloch coordinates, which are connected to each other by Clifford unitaries.
We call one of them the
face state
, which is written as

\displaystyle\ket{F}\!\bra{F}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}+(X+Y+Z)/\sqrt{3}}{2},

(49)

where
X,Y,Z
are qubit Pauli operators.
Then, Lemma
9
and Proposition
10
allow us to obtain the following relation:

Proposition 11
.

The qubit face state satisfies

\displaystyle D_{\min,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(F^{\otimes n})=D_{\max,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(F^{\otimes n}).

(50)

for every integer
n\geq 1
.

Since the face state is a maximizer of
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}
, called a golden state
Liu et al. 2019
;
Regula et al. 2020
, the case of
n=1
recovers the result in Ref.
Liu et al. 2019
.
Also, noting that the stabilizer extent introduced in Ref.
Bravyi et al. 2019
is identical to
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}

Regula 2018
, Eq. (
50
) can be shown by using the additivity of
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}(F^{\otimes n})
and
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}(F^{\otimes n})
, as well as the equivalence of
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}(F)
and
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}(F)
shown in
Ref.
Bravyi et al. 2019
.
Our result provides a different operational approach to this relation based on a strategy with a broad potential applicability.

We also get an analogous result for a special class of magic states that includes the
T
state and the Toffoli state, which are usually used for gate teleportation protocols.

Proposition 12
.

Let
V
be a unitary in the third level of the Clifford hierarchy, and let
\ket{\phi}
be a stabilizer state defined on a multi-qudit system with local dimension
d
, where
d=2
or
d
is an odd prime. Then, the state
\ket{\psi}=V\ket{\phi}
satisfies
D_{\min,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(\psi^{\otimes n})=D_{\max,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(\psi^{\otimes n})
for every integer
n\geq 1
.

This provides an alternative proof of the result in Ref.
Bravyi et al. 2019
and extends it to qudit systems, including the qudit generalization of the
T
state
Howard and Vala 2012
. Note that
D_{\max,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(\psi^{\otimes n})=nD_{\max,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(\psi)
holds for every

, and indeed also for single-qubit mixed states

.

Although
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}
and
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}
coincide for the above cases, one can actually show that for every single-qubit state,
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}
is strictly less than
D_{s,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}
.
The natural question is whether there exists a state for which the three quantities collapse to the same value, allowing us to use both Theorems
1
and
8
.
No such state has been previously known in the theory of multi-qubit magic, which prevented previously known bounds for one-shot distillation and dilution from being tight
Liu et al. 2019
;
Regula et al. 2020
. To show that a suitable choice does indeed exist, let us consider the three-qubit fiducial Hoggar state
Andersson et al. 2015
;
Howard and Campbell 2017
;
Stacey 2016
;
Stacey 2019
;
Zhu 2012
defined as

\displaystyle\ket{{\rm Hog}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(1+i,\,0,\,-1,\,1,\,-i,\,-1,\,0,\,0)^{T}.

(51)

The Hoggar state serves as one of the fiducial states from which group covariant SIC-POVMs can be generated

.
We show that the three measures collapse for the Hoggar state.

Proposition 13
.

The three-qubit Hoggar state satisfies

\displaystyle D_{\min,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}({\rm Hog})=D_{\max,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}({\rm Hog})

\displaystyle=D_{s,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}({\rm Hog})

(52)

\displaystyle=\log\frac{12}{5},

and

\displaystyle D_{\min,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}({\rm Hog}^{\otimes n})=D_{\max,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}({\rm Hog}^{\otimes n})

(53)

for every integer
n\geq 1
.

We note that the value of
D_{s,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}({\rm Hog})
was reported in Ref.
Howard and Campbell 2017
.

Let us now turn our attention to qutrit states.
In the qutrit magic theory, two classes of states were identified to have the maximum sum negativity of the discrete Wigner function
Veitch et al. 2014
.
The first class is represented by the Strange state
Appleby 2005
;
Zhu 2010
;
Veitch et al. 2014
;
Wang et al. 2020
, defined as

\displaystyle\ket{S}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{1}-\ket{2}).

(54)

We can use Lemma
9
to show the collapse of the three measures for this state.

Proposition 14
.

The qutrit Strange state satisfies

\displaystyle D_{\min,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(S)=D_{\max,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(S)=D_{s,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(S)=1

(55)

and

\displaystyle D_{\min,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(S^{\otimes n})=D_{\max,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(S^{\otimes n})

(56)

for every integer
n\geq 1
.

Our results complement the findings in Ref.
Wang et al. 2020
, which considered
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}^{\prime}}
and
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}^{\prime}}
with respect to a larger set of free operators (not necessarily normalized quantum states) based on the negativity of the discrete Wigner function and found that
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}^{\prime}}(S)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}^{\prime}}(S)=\log(5/3)
.

Another maximizer of the sum negativity is the Norrell state
Veitch et al. 2014
defined as

\displaystyle\ket{N}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(-\ket{0}+2\ket{1}-\ket{2}).

(57)

We can get a similar collapse for the measures, but to a different value.

Proposition 15
.

The qutrit Norrell state satisfies

\displaystyle D_{\min,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(N)=D_{\max,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(N)

\displaystyle=D_{s,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(N)

(58)

\displaystyle=\log\frac{3}{2}.

We note that
D_{\min,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(N)=D_{\max,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(N)=\log\frac{3}{2}
was originally reported in Ref.
Wang et al. 2020
.

These results may make one wonder whether there is a general characterization for when the three measures take the same value.
Although we still do not have a definitive answer for this question, we can make the following observation.
For a given state
\psi
, Lemma
5
implies that if
D_{\min,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(\psi)=D_{\max,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(\psi)
, we must have
D_{\max,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(\psi)<D_{s,{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}(\psi)
unless there exists a free state acting on
\supp\!\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}-\psi}{d-1}\right)
.
This is equivalent to the condition that the
weight resource measure

Lewenstein and Sanpera 1998
;
Regula and Takagi 2021a
, defined for the set
\mathbb{F}
of free states as

\displaystyle W_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\left.w\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho=w\sigma+(1-w)\tau,\ \sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right\}

(59)

satisfies

W_{{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}\!\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}-\psi}{d-1}\right)>0.

(60)

This condition can be explicitly verified to hold for the Strange state and Norrell state, for which we have seen the three measures to coincide.
On the other hand, for the qutrit
T
state
Howard and Vala 2012
, defined as

\ket{T}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(e^{2\pi i/9}\ket{0}+\ket{1}+e^{-2\pi i/9}\ket{2}),

(61)

one can check that
W_{{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{STAB}}}}\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}-T}{2}\right)=0
.
Combining it with Proposition
12
, we recover the fact that

\displaystyle D_{\min,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}(T)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}(T)<D_{s,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}(T).

(62)

Interestingly, numerical investigations suggest that, for most qutrit states, the weight measure for the complement is equal to zero, indicating that the non-zero gap between
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}
and
D_{s,\mathbb{F}_{\rm STAB}}
is a generic feature shared by the majority of qutrit states.
We leave a thorough investigation on the relationship between the weight resource measure and the robustness measures for future work.

VII
Conclusions

We established a quantitative relation between the one-shot distillable resource and resource cost for general quantum resource theories, including both state-based resources as well as dynamical resources of quantum channels.
We also showed the corresponding bounds in the asymptotic regime and recovered the familiar relation between the distillable resource and resource cost in the form of strong converse bounds.
We investigated the conditions that appear in the yield–cost relation and related it to a class of free operations that have similar properties to twirling operations.
We employed such operations to obtain analytical expressions for several smoothed resource measures for general resource theories of states and tightened the yield–cost relation.
We then applied our operational technique to evaluate resource measures for several standard resource states in the resource theory of magic, recovering previous results with different techniques and presenting new evaluations of measures for some magic states.

Outstanding questions include the extension of the results that were only shown for state theories in this work to channel theories, such as the two-sided strong converse bound (
25
) and the relation to the twirling-like operation. The difficulty in characterizing the manipulation of quantum channels, and in particular their asymptotic properties
Gour and Winter 2019
, makes such questions non-trivial to answer.
Another interesting direction is to use the operational techniques introduced here to shed light on settings other than magic theory.
On the other hand, it will also be beneficial to gain a deeper understanding of the structure of magic theory, for which additional operational insights might be helpful.

Appendix B
Applicability to specific settings

We briefly review several examples of physical settings, as well as a reference channel
\mathcal{T}
, that satisfy the conditions of Theorem
1
.
Examples discussed here are either state preparation channels with one-dimensional input or unitaries with input and output of the same dimension, ensuring that
\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{T}(\psi)
is pure for every pure state
\psi
.
They further satisfy
D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})=D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})
and thus serve as appropriate references for distillation and dilution protocols that meet the conditions in Theorem
1
.

Let us first consider state theories, where
\mathbb{O}
is a set of channels preparing free states (denoted by
\mathbb{F}
in Section
II
).
The theory of bipartite entanglement
Horodecki et al. 2009
, in which separable states serve as free states, takes a maximally entangled state
\ket{\Phi^{+}_{m}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}m^{-1/2}\ket{ii}
as a reference state and obeys
D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\Phi^{+}_{m})=D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\Phi^{+}_{m})=\log m
.
In the case of the theory of coherence
Streltsov et al. 2017
, where the free states are the diagonal states with respect to a given preferred basis, a maximally coherent state, i.e., a uniform superposition of the basis states
\ket{\phi^{+}_{m}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}m^{-1/2}\ket{i}
, meets the condition
D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\phi^{+}_{m})=D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\phi^{+}_{m})=\log m
.
The theory of thermal non-equilibrium
Brandão et al. 2013
is defined by fixed temperature and Hamiltonian.
The “work bit” represented by the eigenstate of the Hamiltonian with the highest energy is considered as a standard unit resource, and it also satisfies the conditions of Theorem
1
.
In fact, the above observation can be generalized to an arbitrary state theory; every theory with an arbitrary convex set
\mathbb{F}
of free states is equipped with a
golden state

\Phi_{\rm gold}

Liu et al. 2019
such that
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi_{\rm gold})=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi_{\rm gold})

Regula et al. 2020
.
See also Section
VI
in the main text for further discussion on the theory of magic states.

Many important dynamical resource theories are relevant to the setting of quantum communication.
A central purpose of quantum communication is to transmit a quantum state from one party to another.
In such a scenario, the identity channel connecting the two parties is considered as the most useful channel.
It is then natural to take the
m
-dimensional identity channel
\mathrm{id}_{m}
as a reference in distillation and dilution protocols, and indeed, the identity channel satisfies the conditions in Theorem
1
under several coding schemes that are specified by different choices of
\mathbb{O}

Regula and Takagi 2021a
;
Regula and Takagi 2021b
. For instance, in the theory of no-signalling–assisted communication, we get
D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathrm{id}_{m})=D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathrm{id}_{m})=2\log m
, while in quantum communication assisted by LOCC, separable, or positive partial transpose codes we get
D_{\min,\mathbb{O}}(\mathrm{id}_{m})=D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathrm{id}_{m})=\log m
.
A related setting is a framework that quantifies how much quantum memory a given channel can preserve
Rosset et al. 2018
.
This theory takes the set of entanglement-breaking channels as free channels, and the identity channel again serves as an appropriate reference channel
Yuan et al. 2021
.
In relation to Section
VI
, dynamical resource theories of magic
Seddon and Campbell 2019
;
Wang et al. 2019
admit several unitary gates as reference channels satisfying the conditions in Theorem
1

Regula and Takagi 2021a
.

Appendix C
Alternative asymptotic bounds

Besides Theorem
3
, we can also show alternative asymptotic bounds, which are tighter but have additional conditions on the achievable errors.
Another advantage of our alternative bounds is that they do not require the reference channel
\mathcal{T}
to be pure, i.e.,
\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{T}(\psi)
does not need to be pure for every pure state
\psi
.

Let us begin by presenting some useful lemmas.
We first introduce the smoothed hypothesis-testing relative entropy measure as

\displaystyle D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon,\delta}(\mathcal{E})\coloneqq\inf_{F(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\mathcal{E})\geq 1-\delta}D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}).

(78)

Then, we can relate this smoothed measure and the standard hypothesis-testing measure as follows.

Lemma 19
.

For an arbitrary channel
\mathcal{E}
and all
\epsilon,\delta\geq 0
satisfying
\epsilon+\sqrt{\delta}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})\leq D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon+\sqrt{\delta},\delta}(\mathcal{E}).

(79)

Proof.

The hypothesis-testing measure can explicitly be written by

\displaystyle D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=-\log\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{O}}\min_{\psi}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq P\leq\mathbbm{1}\\
\Tr[P\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\psi)]\geq 1-\epsilon\end{subarray}}\Tr[P\,\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{M}(\psi)],

(80)

where we define
\log 0\coloneqq-\infty
here and throughout the manuscript.

Note that for an arbitrary channel
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
satisfying
F(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\mathcal{E})\geq 1-\delta
, an arbitrary positive semidefinite operator
P
satisfying
0\leq P\leq\mathbbm{1}
, and an arbitrary state
\psi
,

\displaystyle\left|\Tr[P\,\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\psi)]-\Tr[P\,\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\psi)]\right|

(81)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\psi)-\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\psi)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{1-F(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\mathcal{E})},

where the first inequality is because of the optimization form of the trace distance
\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}=\max_{0\leq P\leq\mathbbm{1}}\Tr[P(\rho-\sigma)]
satisfied for arbitrary two states
\rho
and
\sigma

Nielsen and Chuang 2011
, and the second inequality is because

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\psi)-\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\psi)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{1-F(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\psi),\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\psi))}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{1-F(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})},

(82)

where we used the relation between trace distance and fidelity
Fuchs and van de
Graaf 1999
in the first inequality and the definition of channel fidelity (
10
) in the second inequality.
This implies that as long as
F(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\mathcal{E})\geq 1-\delta
, every
P
with
0\leq P\leq\mathbbm{1}
and state
\psi
satisfying
\Tr[P\,\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\psi)]\geq 1-\epsilon
also satisfy
\Tr[P\,\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\psi)]\geq 1-\epsilon-\sqrt{\delta}
.
This gives

\displaystyle\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{O}}\min_{\psi}\!\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq P\leq\mathbbm{1}\\
\Tr[P\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\psi)]\geq 1-\epsilon\end{subarray}}\Tr[P\,\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{M}(\psi)]

(83)

\displaystyle\geq\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{O}}\min_{\psi}\max_{F(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\mathcal{E})\geq 1-\delta}\!\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq P\leq\mathbbm{1}\\
\Tr[P\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\psi)]\geq 1-\epsilon-\sqrt{\delta}\end{subarray}}\Tr[P\,\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{M}(\psi)]

\displaystyle\geq\max_{F(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\mathcal{E})\geq 1-\delta}\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{O}}\min_{\psi}\!\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq P\leq\mathbbm{1}\\
\Tr[P\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\psi)]\geq 1-\epsilon-\sqrt{\delta}\end{subarray}}\Tr[P\,\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{M}(\psi)]

where in the last line we used the max-min inequality.
Taking
-\log
on both sides concludes the proof.
∎

The following result, which is a variant of Theorem 1 in Ref.
Regula and Takagi 2021b
, is also useful.

Lemma 20
.

If there exists a free superchannel
\Theta\in\mathbb{S}_{\max}
such that
F(\Theta(\mathcal{E}),\mathcal{N})\geq 1-\epsilon
, then for an arbitrary resource monotone
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\delta}(\mathcal{E})\geq\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{(\sqrt{\delta}+\sqrt{\epsilon})^{2}}(\mathcal{N})

(84)

for every
0\leq\delta\leq 1
, where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\delta}(\mathcal{E})\coloneqq\inf_{F(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\mathcal{E})\geq 1-\delta}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}).

(85)

Proof.

Let
P(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\sqrt{1-F(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{N})}
be the sine distance of quantum channels (also known as purified distance), which satisfies the triangle inequality

\displaystyle P(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})\leq P(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{N})+P(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{T})

(86)

for all channels
\mathcal{E}
,
\mathcal{N}
,
\mathcal{T}

Gilchrist et al. 2005
.
Noting that
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\delta}
is a resource monotone for all
\delta
, we can use Lemma
18
to get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\delta}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{P(\mathcal{N}^{\prime},\mathcal{N})\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\delta}(\mathcal{N}^{\prime})

(87)

\displaystyle=\inf_{P(\mathcal{N}^{\prime},\mathcal{N})\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}}\inf_{P(\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{N}^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{\delta}}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}).

Using the triangle inequality of the purified distance, we get
P(\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{N})\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}+\sqrt{\delta}
for all channels
\mathcal{N}
,
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}
that satisfy
P(\mathcal{N}^{\prime},\mathcal{N})\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}
and
P(\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{N}^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{\delta}
.
Noting also that
P(\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{N})\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}+\sqrt{\delta}
is equivalent to
F(\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{N})\geq 1-(\sqrt{\epsilon}+\sqrt{\delta})^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\delta}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{F(\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{N})\geq 1-(\sqrt{\epsilon}+\sqrt{\delta})^{2}}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime})

(88)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{(\sqrt{\delta}+\sqrt{\epsilon})^{2}}(\mathcal{N}).

∎

Remark
.

We can improve the triangle inequality (
86
).
Define the following “best-case” fidelity

\displaystyle F_{\max}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{\rho_{RA}}F(\mathrm{id}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\rho_{RA}),\mathrm{id}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{N}(\rho_{RA}))

\displaystyle=\max_{\rho}F(\mathcal{E}(\rho),\mathcal{N}(\rho)),

(89)

where the supremum in the first line is taken over every possible ancillary system
R
, and the second equality is due to the data-processing inequality, ensuring that tracing out the ancillary system does not decrease the fidelity.
The last form particularly allows for an efficient computation via the following semidefinite program, which follows from (
89
) and the known semi-definite program for fidelity
Watrous 2013
:

\sqrt{F_{\max}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{N})}=\\
\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\geq 0,\\
X\in\text{L}(\mathcal{H})\end{subarray}}\left\{\left.\Re[\Tr[X]]\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{E}(\rho)&X^{\dagger}\\
X&\mathcal{N}(\rho)\end{bmatrix}\geq 0,\,\Tr[\rho]=1\right\},

(90)

where
\text{L}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the set of linear operators acting on the output Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
for the channels
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{N}
.
Then, we have the following inequality.

Lemma 21
.

Every set of channels
\mathcal{E}
,
\mathcal{N}
, and
\mathcal{T}
with
P(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{N})^{2}+P(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{T})^{2}\leq 1
satisfies

\displaystyle P(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})\leq P(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{N})\sqrt{F_{\max}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{T})}+P(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{T})\sqrt{F_{\max}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{N})}.

(91)

Proof.

Recall that the purified distance
P(\rho,\sigma)\coloneqq\sqrt{1-F(\rho,\sigma)}
defined for arbitrary two states
\rho
and
\sigma
satisfies the following triangle inequality
Tomamichel 2015

\displaystyle P(\rho,\tau)\leq P(\rho,\sigma)\sqrt{F(\sigma,\tau)}+P(\sigma,\tau)\sqrt{F(\rho,\sigma)}

(92)

for every set of three states
\rho,\sigma,\tau
such that
P(\rho,\sigma)^{2}+P(\sigma,\tau)^{2}\leq 1
.
We can employ this to obtain

\displaystyle P(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle=\max_{\psi}P(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\psi),\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{T}(\psi))

(93)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\psi}P(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{E}(\psi),\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{N}(\psi))\sqrt{F_{\max}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{T})}

\displaystyle+\max_{\psi}P(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{N}(\psi),\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal{T}(\psi))\sqrt{F_{\max}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle=P(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{N})\sqrt{F_{\max}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{T})}+P(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{T})\sqrt{F_{\max}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{N})}.

∎

Eq. (
91
) is tighter than (
86
) in general because
F_{\max}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{N})\leq 1
for every
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{N}
.
Although (
86
) and Lemma
20
suffice to establish the forthcoming results, Lemma
21
may find use in other different settings.

We are ready to show the first alternative asymptotic bound, which is tighter than Theorem
3
and does not assume anything on
\mathcal{T}
, while having less flexibility in the achievable errors.

Proposition 22
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an arbitrary input channel and let
\mathcal{T}
be some target reference channel.
Let
d
be any rate of distillation such that there exists a sequence
\{\Theta_{n}\}_{n}
of free superchannels with

\displaystyle 1-F\left(\Theta_{n}(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}),\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor dn\rfloor}\right)\eqqcolon\delta_{n}.

(94)

Also, let
c
be any rate of dilution such that there exists a sequence
\{\Theta_{n}\}_{n}
of free superchannels with

\displaystyle 1-F\left(\Theta_{n}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lceil cn\rceil}),\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}\right)\eqqcolon\epsilon_{n}.

(95)

Suppose that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{n}=0,\quad\liminf_{n\to\infty}\epsilon_{n}<1.

(96)

Then, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle d\cdot\tilde{D}^{\infty}_{H,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})\leq c\cdot\tilde{D}^{\infty}_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T}),

(97)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes n})

(98)

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\max,\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}).

Proof.

Let
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
be a subsequence of indices such that
\epsilon_{\infty}\coloneqq\lim_{k\to\infty}\epsilon_{n_{k}}<1
.
Then, for every
\xi
with
0<\xi<1-\epsilon_{\infty}
, we get

\displaystyle c\,\tilde{D}^{\infty}_{\max,\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})

(99)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\lceil cn_{k}\rceil}{n_{k}}\frac{1}{\lceil cn_{k}\rceil}D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}^{\eta}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lceil c{n_{k}}\rceil})

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}D_{\max,\mathbb{O}}^{(\sqrt{\eta}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{n_{k}}})^{2}}(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes{n_{k}}})

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\left[D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\xi}(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes{n_{k}}})-\log f\left(\xi,(\sqrt{\eta}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{n_{k}}})^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{k}}\left[D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\xi,\delta_{n_{k}}}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor dn_{k}\rfloor})-\log f\left(\xi,(\sqrt{\eta}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{n_{k}}})^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{k}}\left[D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\xi-\sqrt{\delta_{n_{k}}}}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor dn_{k}\rfloor})-\log f\left(\xi,(\sqrt{\eta}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{n_{k}}})^{2}\right)\right].

The second inequality follows from Lemma
20
, the third inequality from Lemmas
16
–
17
and the fact that
\xi+(\sqrt{\eta}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{n_{k}}})^{2}<1
holds for
\eta
sufficiently close to
0
and for sufficiently large
k
, the fourth inequality from Lemma
18
noting that
D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\xi}
is a resource monotone for a fixed
\xi
, and the fifth inequality from Lemma
19
together with that
\xi-\sqrt{\delta_{n_{k}}}\in[0,1]
for sufficiently large
k
.

The second term of the last line,
\log f\left(\xi,(\sqrt{\eta}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{n_{k}}})^{2}\right)/n_{k}
, vanishes at the limit of
k\to\infty
, which also removes the
\eta
dependence.
Also, for every
\delta^{\prime}>0
, we have
\sqrt{\delta_{k}}<\delta^{\prime}
for sufficiently large
k
. Noting that
D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon}
is nondecreasing with respect to
\epsilon
, we can bound the last line as

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\delta^{\prime}\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\xi-\delta^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor dn_{k}\rfloor})

(100)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta^{\prime}\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\lfloor dn_{k}\rfloor}{n_{k}}\frac{1}{\lfloor dn_{k}\rfloor}D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\xi-\delta^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor dn_{k}\rfloor}).

Since this holds for every
\xi
with
0<\xi<1-\epsilon_{\infty}
, we can further take
\lim_{\xi\to 0}
and use

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\to 0}\lim_{\delta^{\prime}\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lfloor dn_{k}\rfloor}D_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\xi-\delta^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor dn_{k}\rfloor})\geq\tilde{D}_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{T})

(101)

to get

\displaystyle c\,\tilde{D}_{\max,\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{T})\geq d\,\tilde{D}_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}).

(102)

∎

In particular, this gives a strong converse inequality

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})\,\tilde{D}_{\max,\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{T})\geq d_{\mathbb{S}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{T})\,\tilde{D}_{H,\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}).

(103)

In the case of state transformations with mild assumptions (c.f., Appendix
D
), the asymptotic equipartition property
\tilde{D}_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\Phi)=\tilde{D}_{H,\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\Phi)
holds

, and consequently we get

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\Phi)\leq\tilde{c}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E},\Phi).

(104)

In addition, if
\Phi
satisfies
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi^{\otimes m})=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi^{\otimes m})=mD_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)\ \forall m
, this leads to the double-sided strong converse inequality

\displaystyle\tilde{d}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\rho,\Phi)\leq\tilde{c}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\rho,\Phi)

(105)

as we discuss in Appendix
D
.

Finally, if we further impose a stronger condition to the achievable errors, we can obtain an even tighter bound.

Proposition 23
.

Let us consider the setting in which we instead have the condition
\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{n_{k}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\epsilon_{n_{k}}=0
for some subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
.
For every resource monotone
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}
, define

\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{R}}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n})

(106)

\displaystyle\underline{\mathfrak{R}}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}).

Then,

\displaystyle d\cdot\underline{\mathfrak{R}}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})\leq c\cdot\overline{\mathfrak{R}}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T}).

(107)

Proof.

\displaystyle c\,\overline{\mathfrak{R}}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T})

(108)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\lceil cn_{k}\rceil}{n_{k}}\frac{1}{\lceil cn_{k}\rceil}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\eta}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lceil c{n_{k}}\rceil})

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{(\sqrt{\eta}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{n_{k}}})^{2}}(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes{n_{k}}})

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{(\sqrt{\eta}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{n_{k}}}+\sqrt{\delta_{n_{k}}})^{2}}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor d{n_{k}}\rfloor}),

where the second and third lines follow from Lemma
20
.

Since
\lim_{k\to\infty}\epsilon_{n_{k}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{n_{k}}=0
, for arbitrary constant
\xi>0
, it is ensured that
\epsilon_{n_{k}}<\xi
and
\delta_{n_{k}}<\xi
for sufficiently large
k
.
Since
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon}
is nonincreasing with respect to
\epsilon
, we can bound the last expression as

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{(\sqrt{\eta}+2\sqrt{\xi})^{2}}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor dn_{k}\rfloor}).

(109)

Since this holds for every
\xi>0
, we can bound the expression in (
108
) by taking the limit
\xi\to 0
as

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\lim_{\xi\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{(\sqrt{\eta}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{n_{k}}}+\sqrt{\delta_{n_{k}}})^{2}}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor dn_{k}\rfloor})

(110)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\eta\to 0}\lim_{\xi\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{(\sqrt{\eta}+2\sqrt{\xi})^{2}}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor d{n}\rfloor})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\lim_{\xi\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\lfloor dn\rfloor}{n}\frac{1}{\lfloor dn\rfloor}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{O}}^{(\sqrt{\eta}+2\sqrt{\xi})^{2}}(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\lfloor d{n}\rfloor})

\displaystyle\geq d\,\underline{\mathfrak{R}}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{O}}(\mathcal{T}),

resulting in the desired inequality in (
107
).
∎

This extends and complements similar relations known for state transformations in settings such as entanglement
Donald et al. 2002
;
Watrous 2018
and a general class of resources

;
Kuroiwa and Yamasaki 2020
.
In particular, this reduces to the intuitive bound

when

.
Note, however, that the regularized resource measure may take 0 for all channels in some cases

.

Appendix D
Strong converse property of distillable resource

Ref.
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
discussed a generalization of quantum Stein’s lemma for resource theories satisfying mild assumptions.
Using this, Ref.
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
characterized the asymptotic distillable entanglement under the set of non-entangling operations with the regularized relative entropy of entanglement.
In fact, their argument shows more than that — the regularized relative entropy of entanglement also serves as a strong converse distillation rate.

Here, we extend this strong converse property to general resource theories by combining the results in Refs.
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
and
Regula et al. 2020
in the case when the generalized quantum Stein’s lemma holds.
This can then turn the one-sided strong converse inequality in Corollary
4
to a double-sided inequality, making both quantities — yield and cost — strong converse rates for each other.

Before stating the result, we recall the following characterization of the fidelity of distillation.

Lemma 24

(
Regula et al. 2020
)
.

For an arbitrary convex and closed set
\mathbb{F}
, if
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=r
, then

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}}F(\mathcal{E}(\rho),\Phi)=G_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho;2^{r}),

(111)

where

G_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho;K)\coloneqq\\
\sup\left\{\left.\Tr[W\rho]\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;0\leq W\leq\mathbbm{1},\ \Tr[W\sigma]\leq\frac{1}{K}\ \forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right\}.

(112)

Then, we get the following relations.

Proposition 25
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a set of free states which:

1.

is convex and closed,

2.

contains a full-rank state,

3.

is closed under partial trace and composition of free states.

Also, let
\Phi
be a state that satisfies
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi^{\otimes m})=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi^{\otimes m})=mD_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)\;\forall m
. Then,

\displaystyle\tilde{d}_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}^{\infty}(\rho,\Phi)=d_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}^{\infty}(\rho,\Phi).

(113)

In particular, for every
\mathbb{O}\subseteq\mathbb{O}_{\rm max}
we have that

\displaystyle\tilde{d}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\rho,\Phi)\leq\tilde{c}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\infty}(\rho,\Phi).

(114)

Proof.

Let
F_{\mathbb{O}}(\rho\to\phi)
be the fidelity of distillation from
\rho
to
\phi
under free operations
\mathbb{O}
, defined as

\displaystyle F_{\mathbb{O}}(\rho\to\phi)\coloneqq\sup_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}}F(\mathcal{E}(\rho),\phi).

(115)

Lemma
24
and the assumed additivity of
D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}
gives

\displaystyle F_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}(\rho^{\otimes n}\to\Phi^{\otimes ny})=G_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\otimes n};2^{rny}),

(116)

where
r=D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)
.

Note that
G_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho;K)
is a convex optimization program, and one can obtain its dual program by following standard techniques in convex optimization theory
Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004
(cf.
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
).
For operators
W\geq 0
,
Y\geq 0
,
Z\in\cone(\mathbb{F})
, consider the Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\rho,W;Y,Z)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Tr[W\rho]+\Tr\left[(\mathbbm{1}-W)Y\right]+\Tr\left[(\mathbbm{1}-KW)Z\right]

(117)

\displaystyle=\Tr[Y]+\Tr[Z]+\Tr[W(\rho-Y-KZ)].

This form leads to a dual program:

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\left.\Tr[Y]+\Tr[Z]\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;Y\geq 0,Y\geq\rho-KZ,Z\in\cone(\mathbb{F})\right\}

(118)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left.\Tr[\rho-KZ]_{+}+\Tr[Z]\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;Z\in\cone(\mathbb{F})\right\}

where we defined
\Tr[A]_{+}
to be the trace over the positive part of the operator
A
.
Since Slater’s condition
(
Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004
, Sec. 5.2.3)
is satisfied, which can be confirmed by taking
W=\mathbbm{1}/(K+1)
in (
112
), we get

\displaystyle G_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho;K)=\inf_{\tilde{\sigma}\in\cone(\mathbb{F})}\left(\Tr[\rho-\tilde{\sigma}]_{+}+\frac{1}{K}\Tr[\tilde{\sigma}]\right).

(119)

Taking
\tilde{\sigma}=2^{rnb}\sigma
for some
b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
allows us to write

\displaystyle G_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\otimes n};2^{rny})=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F},b\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\Tr[\rho-2^{rnb}\sigma]_{+}+2^{-r(y-b)n}\right).

(120)

for all
n
and
y
.
Let
R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)
be the regularized relative entropy resource measure
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
;
Brandão and Gour 2015
defined as

\displaystyle R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\inf_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}D(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma),

(121)

where
D(\rho\|\sigma)
is the relative entropy defined for arbitrary two states
\rho
and
\sigma
taking
\Tr[\rho\log\rho]-\Tr[\rho\log\sigma]
if
\supp(\rho)\subseteq\supp(\sigma)
and
+\infty
otherwise.
Let us take
y=\frac{1}{r}R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)+\epsilon
for some
\epsilon>0
. Then, for each
n
, we can take
b=\frac{1}{r}R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}
to get

\displaystyle G_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\otimes n};2^{rn(R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)+\epsilon)})

(122)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\left(\Tr[\rho^{\otimes n}-2^{rn(\frac{1}{r}R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)+\frac{\epsilon}{2})}\sigma]_{+}\right)+2^{-\frac{rn\epsilon}{2}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\left(\Tr[\rho^{\otimes n}-2^{n(R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)+\frac{r\epsilon}{2})}\sigma]_{+}\right)+2^{-\frac{rn\epsilon}{2}}.

Ref.
(
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
, Prop. III.1)
showed that the first term approaches 0 in the limit of

for every
\epsilon>0
.
Therefore, combining it with (
116
), we get that for all
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\to\Phi^{\otimes n(R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)/r+\epsilon)}\right)=0.

(123)

On the other hand, if we take
y=\frac{1}{r}R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)-\epsilon
, the optimum
b
for each
n
needs to satisfy
b<y
because otherwise
G_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\otimes n},r^{ny})
would diverge as
n\to\infty
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle G_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\otimes n};r^{ny})\geq\inf_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\Tr[\rho-2^{n(R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)-\epsilon r)}\sigma]_{+}.

(124)

The right-hand side approaches 1 for every
\epsilon>0

Brandão and Plenio 2010b
and thus leads to

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\to\Phi^{\otimes n(R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)/r-\epsilon)}\right)=1

(125)

for every
\epsilon>0
.
(
123
) and (
125
) imply

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}^{\infty}(\rho,\Phi)=\tilde{d}_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}^{\infty}(\rho,\Phi)=\frac{R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)}{r}=\frac{R_{{\rm rel},\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\rho)}{D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)}.

(126)

Finally, combining this with Corollary
4
, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{d}_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}(\rho,\Phi)\leq\tilde{c}_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}(\rho,\Phi).

(127)

Noting that

\displaystyle\tilde{d}_{\mathbb{O}}(\rho,\Phi)\leq\tilde{d}_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}(\rho,\Phi),\quad\tilde{c}_{\mathbb{O}_{\max}}(\rho,\Phi)\leq\tilde{c}_{\mathbb{O}}(\rho,\Phi)

(128)

for every
\mathbb{O}\subseteq\mathbb{O}_{\max}
immediately leads to

\displaystyle\tilde{d}_{\mathbb{O}}(\rho,\Phi)\leq\tilde{c}_{\mathbb{O}}(\rho,\Phi),

(129)

which concludes the proof.
∎

It is now easy to check that

Next, we show that

\displaystyle\Lambda(\tau)

(141)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda(\Phi)+(2^{D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)}-1)\Lambda(\sigma^{\star})}{2^{D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\epsilon=0,\ 0<\kappa<1\\
\frac{1}{1-2^{-r}}&\epsilon=0,\ \kappa=0\\
r+\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}&0\leq\epsilon<1,\ \kappa=1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=\begin{cases}r-\log\frac{1}{\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}}&\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}\geq 2^{-r}\\

Proof.

D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)=r

\displaystyle D_{H,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})

\displaystyle=\log\min_{\tau\in\mathbb{F}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq Q\leq\mathbbm{1}\\

\Tr[Q\Phi_{\kappa}]\geq 1-\epsilon\end{subarray}}\Tr[Q\tau]^{-1}

\tilde{\mathbb{F}}

\Lambda

\displaystyle D_{H,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=\begin{cases}0&\epsilon=0,\ 0\leq\kappa<1\\

\displaystyle D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})

, and the minimization over
\alpha
and
\eta
gives
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=r-\log\frac{1}{\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}}
.
These can concisely be written as

\displaystyle D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=\max\left\{r-\log\frac{1}{\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}},0\right\},

(162)

which concludes the proof for the expression of
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}
.

The smooth standard robustness can be computed similarly.
Note that
D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi)=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi\|\sigma)
where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\rho_{1}\|\rho_{2})\coloneqq\\
&\inf\left\{\left.\log(1+s)\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\frac{\rho_{1}^{\prime}+s\tau}{1+s}=\rho_{2},\ \tau\in\mathbb{F},\ F(\rho_{1}^{\prime},\rho_{1})\geq 1-\epsilon\right\},\end{aligned}

(163)

and
D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}
is contractive under every free operation
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}_{\max}
, as for all states
\rho
and
\sigma
,

\displaystyle D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle=D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\tilde{\rho}\|\sigma)

(164)

\displaystyle\geq D_{s,\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{E}(\tilde{\rho})\|\mathcal{E}(\sigma))

\displaystyle\geq D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{E}(\rho)\|\mathcal{E}(\sigma))

where we set
\tilde{\rho}
as the optimal state realizing the standard robustness, and in the third line we used that
F(\mathcal{E}(\tilde{\rho}),\mathcal{E}(\rho))\geq F(\tilde{\rho},\rho)\geq 1-\epsilon
.
Thus, we can use Lemma
26
to compute
D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})
as

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in[0,2^{-r}]\\
\eta\in[\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon},\eta_{\max}^{\epsilon}]\end{subarray}}\left\{\left.\log s\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\eta\Phi+(1-\eta)\sigma^{\star}\leq_{\mathbb{F}}s(\alpha\Phi+(1-\alpha)\sigma^{\star})\right\}

(165)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in[0,2^{-r}]\\
\eta\in[\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon},\eta_{\max}^{\epsilon}]\end{subarray}}\left\{\left.\log s\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;s\alpha-\eta\geq 0,\ s(1-\alpha)-(1-\eta)\geq 0,\right.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{s(1-\alpha)-(1-\eta)}{s\alpha-\eta}\geq 2^{r}-1\right\}

\displaystyle=\log\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in[0,2^{-r}]\\
\eta\in[\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon},\eta_{\max}^{\epsilon}]\end{subarray}}\max\left\{\frac{\eta}{\alpha},\frac{1-\eta}{1-\alpha},\frac{1-2^{r}\eta}{1-2^{r}\alpha}\right\}

where in the first line, we used the notation
A\leq_{\mathbb{F}}B\iff B-A\in\cone(\mathbb{F})
.
When
\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}\leq 2^{-r}
, we immediately get
D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=0
, which is achieved at
\alpha=\eta
.
On the other hand, when
\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}\geq 2^{-r}
,
we have
\frac{\eta}{\alpha}>\frac{1-\eta}{1-\alpha}
and
\frac{\eta}{\alpha}>\frac{1-2^{r}\eta}{1-2^{r}\alpha}
, in which the minimization over
\alpha
and
\eta
results in

D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=r-\log\frac{1}{\eta_{\min}}
.
These two can be combined as

\displaystyle D_{s,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=\max\left\{r-\log\frac{1}{\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}},0\right\}.

(166)

The proof for the case when
D_{\min,\aff(\mathbb{F})}(\Phi)=D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi)
goes analogously, where we basically change the region of optimization over free states from
\tilde{\mathbb{F}}
to
\{\tilde{\sigma}\}
defined in (
145
).
Since
D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\rho\|\sigma)
satisfies the data-processing inequality even if
\sigma
is not a positive operator
Regula and Takagi 2021b
, we can employ the same argument as that in Lemma
26
to get

\displaystyle D_{H,\aff(\mathbb{F})}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=\log\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq Q\leq\mathbbm{1}\\
\Tr[Q\Phi_{\kappa}]\geq 1-\epsilon\end{subarray}}\Tr[Q\tilde{\sigma}]^{-1}

(167)

where we used that the map
\Lambda
in (
35
) transforms all
\sigma\in\aff(\mathbb{F})
to
\tilde{\sigma}
.
We can then restrict the optimization to
Q=\eta P^{\star}+\lambda(\mathbbm{1}-P^{\star})
with the condition (
152
).
Recall that
\mathcal{A}
is the set of
(\lambda,\eta)
that satisfies (
152
).
Then, we can compute
D_{H,\aff(\mathbb{F})}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})
as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\log\sup_{(\lambda,\eta)\in\mathcal{A}}\Tr[\{\eta P^{\star}+\lambda(\mathbbm{1}-P^{\star})\}\{2^{-r}\Phi+(1-2^{-r})\sigma^{\star}\}]^{-1}\\
&\quad=-\log\inf_{(\lambda,\eta)\in\mathcal{A}}[\eta\,2^{-r}+\lambda(1-2^{-r})].\end{aligned}

(168)

When
\kappa=1
, the condition on
\mathcal{A}
becomes (
154
).
Then, we evaluate this as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\log\left[(1-\epsilon)2^{-r}\right]=r+\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}.\end{aligned}

(169)

On the other hand, when
\epsilon=0
and
0<\kappa<1
, we are constrained to
\lambda=\eta=1
, leading to value 0.
When
\kappa=0
, we have
\lambda=1
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, giving
\log\frac{1}{1-2^{-r}}
.
When
\kappa=1
, we have
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
,
\eta=1
, giving
r
.

Noting that the above argument gives the same conclusion for
D_{H,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}
as well, we reorganize the above form to reach

\displaystyle D_{H,\aff(\mathbb{F})}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})

\displaystyle=D_{H,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})

(170)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\epsilon=0,\ 0<\kappa<1\\
\log\frac{1}{1-2^{-r}}&\epsilon=0,\ \kappa=0\\
r+\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}&0\leq\epsilon<1,\ \kappa=1\end{cases}

As for
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}
, we follow the same argument up to (
161
) to get

\displaystyle D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=\log\inf_{\eta\in[\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon},\eta_{\max}^{\epsilon}]}\max\left\{\frac{\eta}{2^{-r}},\frac{1-\eta}{1-2^{-r}}\right\}.

(171)

If
\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}\geq 2^{-r}
, we always have
\frac{\eta}{2^{-r}}\geq\frac{1-\eta}{1-2^{-r}}
and thus
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=\log\frac{\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}}{2^{-r}}
.
If
\eta_{\max}^{\epsilon}\leq 2^{-r}
, we always have
\frac{\eta}{2^{-r}}\leq\frac{1-\eta}{1-2^{-r}}
and thus
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=\log\frac{1-\eta_{\max}^{\epsilon}}{1-2^{-r}}
.
If
\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}\leq 2^{-r}\leq\eta_{\max}^{\epsilon}
, then

D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=0
, which is achieved at
\eta=2^{-r}
.

Summarizing, we get

\displaystyle D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\kappa})=\begin{cases}r-\log\frac{1}{\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}}&\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}\geq 2^{-r}\\
\log\frac{1-\eta_{\max}^{\epsilon}}{1-2^{-r}}&\eta_{\max}^{\epsilon}\leq 2^{-r}\\
0&\eta_{\min}^{\epsilon}\leq 2^{-r}\leq\eta_{\max}^{\epsilon}\end{cases},

(172)

concluding the proof.

Appendix F
Proof of Theorem
8

Proof.

The proof combines Proposition
6
and the argument in Ref.
Wilde 2021
.
For two states
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\in\mathbb{T}
, consider the following sequence of transformations:

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}\xrightarrow[\mathbb{O}]{\epsilon_{2}}\rho\xrightarrow[\mathbb{O}]{\epsilon_{1}}\Phi_{1}.

(175)

Let
\Lambda_{2}
and
\Lambda_{1}
be free transformations corresponding to the first and the second transformation above.
Define the purified distance
P(\rho,\sigma)\coloneqq\sqrt{1-F(\rho,\sigma)}
, which satisfies the following triangle inequality
Tomamichel 2015

\displaystyle P(\rho,\tau)\leq P(\rho,\sigma)\sqrt{F(\sigma,\tau)}+P(\sigma,\tau)\sqrt{F(\rho,\sigma)}

(176)

for every set of three states
\rho,\sigma,\tau
such that
P(\rho,\sigma)^{2}+P(\sigma,\tau)^{2}\leq 1
.
Applying this to our setting, we get that
\Lambda_{1}\circ\Lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{O}
achieves the transformation
\Phi_{2}\xrightarrow[\mathbb{O}]{\epsilon^{\prime}}\Phi_{1}
with

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\prime}\coloneqq\left(\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}(1-\epsilon_{1})}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}(1-\epsilon_{2})}\right)^{2}

(177)

whenever
\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}\leq 1
is satisfied.
Moreover,
\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}<1
ensures
\epsilon^{\prime}<1
. This can be shown as follows.
Direct calculation gives

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\prime}=\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}+2\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}\left(\sqrt{(1-\epsilon_{1})(1-\epsilon_{2})}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}\right).

(178)

Let us change parameters as
\Delta\coloneqq 1-(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2})
and
p\coloneqq\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}
, which behave as independent variables under the condition that
(1-\Delta)^{2}-4p\geq 0
,
0<\Delta\leq 1
,
p\geq 0
, noting that
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}
are solutions of the quadratic equation
x^{2}-(1-\Delta)x+p=0
while satisfying
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\geq 0
and
\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}<1
.
Then, we get

\displaystyle 1-\epsilon^{\prime}=\Delta-2\sqrt{p}\left(\sqrt{\Delta+p}-\sqrt{p}\right).

(179)

This gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\Delta}(1-\epsilon^{\prime})=1-\frac{\sqrt{p}}{\sqrt{\Delta+p}}>0,

(180)

implying that
1-\epsilon^{\prime}
is a strictly increasing function of
\Delta
for an arbitrary fixed
p
.
Since
(1-\epsilon^{\prime})|_{\Delta=0}=0
for every
p
, we get that
1-\epsilon^{\prime}>0
for all
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
satisfying
\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}<1
.

Therefore, for every dilution operation
\Lambda_{2}
with error
\epsilon_{2}
and distillation operation
\Lambda_{1}
with error
\epsilon_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi_{1})

\displaystyle\leq D_{H,\mathbb{F}}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}(\Phi_{2})

(181)

\displaystyle=D_{\min,\mathbb{F}}(\Phi_{2})+\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon^{\prime}},

where the first line follows from Lemma
18
and the second line from Proposition
6
. Optimizing over all feasible
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
and using the definition of distillable resource and resource cost gives the desired relation

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon_{1}}(\rho)\leq c_{\mathbb{O}}^{\epsilon_{2}}(\rho)+\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon^{\prime}}.

(182)

Appendix G
Comparing the bounds in Theorem
1
and Theorem
8

Here, we show that the bound in Theorem
8
is always tighter than the bound in Theorem
1
.

Let us first compare the expression in Theorem
1
valid for
\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}<1
and the bound in Theorem
8
.
Comparing the denominators of the expressions inside the logarithm, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left(1-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)-\left(\sqrt{1-\epsilon_{2}}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}}\right)^{2}\\
&=-2\epsilon_{1}+2\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}-2\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}(1-\epsilon_{2})(1-\epsilon_{1})}+2\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}(1-\epsilon_{2})}\\
&=2\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}(1-\epsilon_{2})}\left(1-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}(1-\epsilon_{1})}\right)-2\epsilon_{1}(1-\epsilon_{2})\\
&=2\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}(1-\epsilon_{2})}\left(1-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}(1-\epsilon_{1})}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}(1-\epsilon_{2})}\right)\\
&=2\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}(1-\epsilon_{2})}\left(1-\sqrt{\epsilon^{\prime}}\right)\end{aligned}

(183)

where
\epsilon^{\prime}
is the one introduced in (
177
).
As shown in Appendix
F
, we have
\epsilon^{\prime}<1
(and thus
1-\sqrt{\epsilon^{\prime}}>0
) for all
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
satisfying
\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}<1
.
This implies
\left(1-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)-\left(\sqrt{1-\epsilon_{2}}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}}\right)^{2}>0
, and in particular

\displaystyle\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon^{\prime}}<\log\left(\sqrt{1-\epsilon_{2}}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}}\right)^{-2}

(184)

for
\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}<1
.

We next compare the expression in Theorem
1
to the bound in Theorem
8
for
\epsilon_{1}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}<1
.
Direct calculation gives

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left(1-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)-\left(1-\epsilon_{1}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}\right)\\
&=\epsilon_{1}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}-\left(\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}(1-\epsilon_{2})}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}(1-\epsilon_{1})}\right)^{2}\\
&=\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}-\epsilon_{2}+2\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}-2\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}(1-\epsilon_{1})(1-\epsilon_{2})}.\end{aligned}

(185)

Let us define
g(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})\coloneqq\left(1-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)-\left(1-\epsilon_{1}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}\right)
and consider varying
\epsilon_{1}
for a fixed
\epsilon_{2}
.
Although we are interested in the region
0\leq\epsilon_{1}<1-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}
, let us consider an extended region
0\leq\epsilon_{1}<1-\epsilon_{2}
as a domain of

.
Since
1-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}\leq 1-\epsilon_{2}

g(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})\geq 0
for
0\leq\epsilon_{1}\leq 1-\epsilon_{2}
, then
g(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})\geq 0
for
0\leq\epsilon_{1}\leq 1-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}

g(0,\epsilon_{2})=g(1-\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{2})=\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}-\epsilon_{2}\geq 0

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon_{1}}g(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})

\displaystyle=2\epsilon_{2}-\frac{\epsilon_{2}(1-\epsilon_{2})(1-2\epsilon_{1})}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}(1-\epsilon_{1})(1-\epsilon_{2})}}

(186)

\displaystyle\propto 2\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}(1-\epsilon_{1})(1-\epsilon_{2})}-(1-\epsilon_{2})(1-2\epsilon_{1}),

the local minima occur at
\epsilon_{1}^{\star}
such that

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}^{\star}\epsilon_{2}(1-\epsilon_{1}^{\star})(1-\epsilon_{2})}-(1-\epsilon_{2})(1-2\epsilon_{1}^{\star})=0,

(187)

for which we get a simplified form of

\displaystyle g(\epsilon_{1}^{\star},\epsilon_{2})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}-\epsilon_{2}+2\epsilon_{1}^{\star}\epsilon_{2}-(1-\epsilon_{2})(1-2\epsilon_{1}^{\star})

(188)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}-1+2\epsilon_{1}^{\star}.

Eq. (
187
) can be alternatively written as

\displaystyle-1+\epsilon_{1}^{\star}+\left(\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}^{\star}(1-\epsilon_{2})}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}(1-\epsilon_{1}^{\star})}\right)^{2}=0,

(189)

leading to

\displaystyle\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}^{\star}(1-\epsilon_{2})}=(1-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}})\sqrt{1-\epsilon_{1}^{\star}},

(190)

for the region
\epsilon_{1}^{\star}\leq 1
.
This gives the expression of the local minimum as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}^{\star}=\frac{1-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}}{2}.

(191)

Plugging this into (
188
) gives

\displaystyle g(\epsilon_{1}^{\star},\epsilon_{2})

\displaystyle=0.

(192)

This concludes the proof that
\left(1-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)-\left(1-\epsilon_{1}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}\right)\geq 0
for all
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
satisfying
\epsilon_{1}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}<1

\displaystyle\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon^{\prime}}\leq\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon_{1}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}}.

(193)

\Lambda\in\mathbb{O}_{\max}\cap\mathcal{S}(\Phi)

Appendix I
Proof of Proposition
10

Proof.

Let us define the group twirling operation

\displaystyle\Xi(\cdot)\coloneqq\int_{G}\mathrm{d}g\,U(g)\cdot U(g)^{\dagger}

(202)

where the integral is taken over the Haar measure of the group
G
.
We first show a general form of how the twirling operation acts on an arbitrary state
\rho
(in particular, Eq. (
212
)).
Although Eq. (
212
) was already presented in Ref.
(
Chiribella 2006
, Sec. 2.6)
, we here provide a self-contained explanation for completeness.

An arbitrary unitary representation
\{U(g)\}_{g\in G}
of a finite or a compact Lie group
G
can be decomposed into a direct sum of irreducible representations
Fulton and Harris 2013
.
Accordingly, the Hilbert space that each
U(g)
acts on admits the following decomposition:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\mu}\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(r)}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(m)}

(203)

where
\mu
labels the irreducible representations,
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(r)}
denotes the subspace on which each irreducible representation acts nontrivially, and
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(m)}
denotes the multiplicity subspace.
We write the dimensions of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(r)}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(m)}
as
d_{\mu}^{(r)}
and
d_{\mu}^{(m)}
respectively.

Each
U(g)
can be written as

\displaystyle U(g)=\bigoplus_{\mu}U_{\mu}^{(r)}(g)\otimes\mathbbm{1}^{(m)}_{\mu}.

(204)

Also, Schur’s lemma
Fulton and Harris 2013
imposes a structure to every
symmetric state

\sigma
satisfying
U(g)\sigma U(g)^{\dagger}=\sigma\ \forall g
as

\displaystyle\sigma=\bigoplus_{\mu}q_{\mu}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(r)}}{d_{\mu}^{(r)}}\otimes\eta_{\mu}^{(m)}

(205)

where
\{q_{\mu}\}_{\mu}
is a probability distribution,
\eta_{\mu}^{(m)}
is a quantum state, and
\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(r)}
is the projector onto
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(r)}
.

It is easy to see that every output of the group twirling operation is a symmetric state, because, for every
g\in G
,

\displaystyle U_{g}\Xi(\rho)U_{g}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}g^{\prime}U(g)U(g^{\prime})\rho U(g^{\prime})^{\dagger}U(g)^{\dagger}

(206)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\mathrm{d}g^{\prime}\,U(gg^{\prime})\rho U(gg^{\prime})^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\mathrm{d}g^{\prime}\,U(g^{\prime})\rho U(g^{\prime})^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\Xi(\rho)

where in the third line we used the left and right invariance of the Haar measure.

Let us also define the projection onto invariant subspaces as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\cdot)\coloneqq\sum_{\mu}\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(r)}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(m)}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(r)}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(m)}.

(207)

The projection turns every state
\rho
into the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\rho)=\bigoplus_{\mu}p_{\mu}\sigma_{\mu}^{(r,m)}

(208)

where
\{p_{\mu}\}_{\mu}
is probability distribution, and
\sigma_{\mu}^{(r,m)}
is a quantum state acting on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(r)}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(m)}
.
Also, the forms of (
204
) and (
207
) imply that
U_{g}\mathcal{P}(\rho)U_{g}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{P}(U_{g}\rho U_{g}^{\dagger})\ \forall\rho,\forall g\in G
, and hence

\displaystyle\Xi\circ\mathcal{P}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}\circ\Xi(\rho)

(209)

\displaystyle=\Xi(\rho)

for every state
\rho
, where in the second line, we used the fact that
\Xi
maps every state to a symmetric state, which has the form (
205
).
Then,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Xi(\rho)=\Xi(\mathcal{P}(\rho))\\
&=\bigoplus_{\mu}\int_{G}\mathrm{d}g\,p_{\mu}\left(U_{\mu}^{(r)}(g)\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(m)}\right)\sigma_{\mu}^{(r,m)}\left(U_{\mu}^{(r)}(g)^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(m)}\right).\end{aligned}

(210)

Since
\Xi(\rho)
is a symmetric state having a structure of (
205
), and
U_{\mu}^{(r)}
in (
210
) acts only on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(r)}
nontrivially, we must have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{G}\mathrm{d}g\,p_{\mu}&\left(U_{\mu}^{(r)}(g)\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(m)}\right)\sigma_{\mu}^{(r,m)}\left(U_{\mu}^{(r)}(g)^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(m)}\right)\\
&\quad=p_{\mu}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(r)}}{d_{\mu}^{(r)}}\otimes\sigma_{\mu}^{(m)},\ \forall\mu.\end{aligned}

(211)

where
\sigma_{\mu}^{(m)}\coloneqq\Tr_{r}[\sigma_{\mu}^{(r,m)}]
and
\Tr_{r}
denotes the partial trace over the system with
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(r)}
.
Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\Xi(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}p_{\mu}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(r)}}{d_{\mu}^{(r)}}\otimes\sigma_{\mu}^{(m)}

(212)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(r)}}{d_{\mu}^{(r)}}\otimes\Tr_{r}\left[\mathcal{P}(\rho)\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(r)}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(m)}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(r)}}{d_{\mu}^{(r)}}\otimes\Tr_{r}\left[\rho\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(r)}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(m)}\right].

We now show that this group twirling operation satisfies the condition for the operation that appears in Lemma
9
.
By assumption,
\ket{\Phi}
is an eigenvector of
U_{g}
with eigenvalue
e^{i\phi_{g}}
for every
g\in G
.
This implies that
\ket{\Phi}
is in the invariant subspace of
U(g)
corresponding to the one-dimensional irreducible representation
\{e^{i\phi_{g}}\}_{g\in G}
.
We assign the label
\mu^{\star}
for this representation.
Then, every
U(g)
has the form

\displaystyle U(g)=\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\phi_{g}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mu^{\star}}^{(m)}&\\
&\bigoplus_{\mu\neq\mu^{\star}}U_{\mu}^{(r)}(g)\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(m)},\end{pmatrix}

(213)

implying that there exist
d_{\mu^{\star}}^{(m)}
simultaneous eigenvectors of all
U(g)
’s with eigenvalues
\{e^{i\phi_{g}}\}_{g}
.
Using the assumption that
\ket{\Phi}
is the unique simultaneous eigenvector with eigenvalues
\{e^{i\phi_{g}}\}_{g}
, we identify
d_{\mu^{\star}}^{(m)}=1
.
Noting
d_{\mu^{\star}}^{(r)}=1
and
\mathbbm{1}_{\mu^{\star}}^{(r)}=\Phi
, we get from (
212
) that

\displaystyle\Xi(\rho)

\displaystyle=\Tr[\rho\Phi]\Phi+\sum_{\mu\neq\mu^{\star}}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(r)}}{d_{\mu}^{(r)}}\otimes\Tr_{r}\left[\rho\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(r)}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mu}^{(m)}\right].

(214)

The second term is a completely positive map.
Also, the convexity of
\mathbb{O}
and the assumption that
U_{g}\cdot U_{g}^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{O}
imply that
\Xi\in\mathbb{O}\subseteq\mathbb{O}_{\max}
.
Thus, we can apply Lemma
9
to conclude the proof.

Proof of Proposition

The Strange state is a fiducial state for a SIC-POVM for dimension 3.

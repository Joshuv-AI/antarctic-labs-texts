Youssou Ciss

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we propose an estimator of Foster, Greer and Thorbecke class of measures
\displaystyle P(z,\alpha)=\int_{0}^{z}\Big(\frac{z-x}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}f(x)\,dx
, where
z>0
is the poverty line,
f
is the probabily density function of the income distribution and
\alpha
is the so-called poverty aversion. The estimator is constructed with a bias reduced kernel estimator. Uniform almost sure consistency and uniform mean square consistenty are established.
A simulation study indicates that our new estimator performs well.

Key words and expressions :
poverty line, bias reduction kernel, uniform almost sure consistency, uniforme mean square consistency, rate of convergence.

1
Introduction and definition of the estimator

Let
F
be the cumulative distribution function of the income variable
X
from a population with continuous density function
f
. The FGT (Foster, Greer, Thorbecke)
[
Foster et al. (1984)
]
class of poverty index measures by the real
\alpha\geq 0
is defined by :

\begin{split}&P(z,\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z}\left(\frac{z-x}{z}\right)^{\alpha}f(x)\,dx&\text{if}\,z>0,\\
\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(1.1)

where
z
is the poverty line.

Let
\displaystyle X_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,X_{n}
be a random sample of size
n
from income random variables (r.v.) with distribution function
F
.
Seidl (1988)
[
Seidl (1988)
]
introduced
An empirical estimator of the FGT poverty index
P(z,\alpha)
as following :

\widehat{P}_{n}(z,\alpha)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{X_{i}}{z}\Big)_{+}^{\alpha}\qquad\text{where}\qquad x_{+}=\max(0,x).\quad

This estimator was fully useful in a large range of applications in economics (Widely used in practice in econometrics and actuarial). It is an unbiased consistent estimator and is asymptoticaly normal.
Lo
et al.

[
Lo et al. (2009)
]
used empirical processes and extreme-values methodology to study this estimator. Seck
[
Seck (2011)
]
, Seck and Lo
[
Seck et al. (2009)
]
used some non-weighted poverty measures, viewed as stochastic processes and indexed by real numbers or monotone functions, to follow up the poverty evolution between two periods.
Dia
[
Dia (2008)
]
and also Ciss
et al.

[
Ciss et al. (2015)
]
proposed new kernels estimators, based on the Riemann sum, respectively, for
\alpha=0
and
\alpha\geq 1
and
\alpha\in]0,1[
.

Dia
[
Dia (2008)
]
and Ciss
et al.

[
Ciss et al. (2015)
]
considered also the classical nonparametric estimator of the density
f
(Parzen-Rosenblatt) :

\hat{f}(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{h}K\Big(\frac{x-X_{i}}{h}\Big),

where
h
is a function of
n
which tends to zero as
n
tends to infinity and
K
verifies the following hypotheses :

(\mathbf{H_{1}})\sup_{-\infty<x<+\infty}|K(x)|<+\infty,\quad(\mathbf{H_{2}})\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}K(x)\,dx=1,\quad(\mathbf{H_{3}})\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}|xK(x)|=0

(1.2)

and proposed as estimator of FGT poverty index, the following one :

{P}_{n}(z,\alpha)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{[z/h]}\Big(\frac{z-ih}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}K\Big(\frac{ih-X_{j}}{h}\Big)\,.

(1.3)

Recently, Zakaria et al.
[
Zakaria et al. (2018)
]
considered the following adaptive kernel estimator of the density
f
:

\hat{f}^{\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{h\lambda_{i}}K\Big(\frac{x-X_{i}}{h\lambda_{i}}\Big)

and proposed as estimator of FGT poverty index, the following estimator :

{P}^{\lambda}_{n}(z,\alpha)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{[z/h\lambda_{j}]}\Big(\frac{z-ih\lambda_{j}}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}K\Big(\frac{ih\lambda_{j}-X_{j}}{h\lambda_{j}}\Big)\,\quad\text{for}\quad\alpha=0\quad\text{or}\quad\alpha\geq 1,

(1.4)

where
\lambda_{j}
, is a parameter that varies according to the local concentration of the data.

Both
[
Parzen (1962)
]
and
[
Silverman (1986)
]
pointed out that if
\int uK(u)du=0
and
f
is twice continuously differentiable in a neighborhood of
x
, then

Bias(\hat{f}(x))=E(\hat{f}(x))-f(x)=\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(x)h^{2}\int u^{2}K(u)du+O(h^{3}).

(1.5)

In order to reduce the bias of the classical kernel estimator
\hat{f}
, we consider the following estimator introduced in
[
Xie et al. (2014)
]

\tilde{f}(x)=\hat{f}(x)-\widehat{Bias}(\hat{f}(x)),

which can be written in the following form

\tilde{f}(x)=\hat{f}(x)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\hat{f}^{\prime\prime}(x)\int u^{2}K(u)du=\frac{1}{nh}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K\Big(\frac{x-X_{i}}{h}\Big)-\frac{h}{2n}\int u^{2}K(u)du\sum_{i=1}^{n}K^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{x-X_{i}}{h}\Big).

(1.6)

Further, in this paper, we assume that the hypotheses
\displaystyle\mathbf{H_{1}},\mathbf{H_{2}},\mathbf{H_{3}}
hold for both
K
and
K^{\prime\prime}
, they are Riemann integrable and twice continuously differentiable, and that
f
is bounded with support included in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We denote by
x_{0}
the infinimum of this support.
Let’s substite in   (
1.1
)
f
by
\tilde{f}
. We obtain

\tilde{J}_{n}(z,\alpha)=\int_{0}^{z}\Big(\frac{z-x}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}(nh)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K\Big(\frac{x-X_{i}}{h}\Big)\,-\frac{h}{2n}\int u^{2}K(u)du\int_{0}^{z}\Big(\frac{z-x}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{x-X_{i}}{h}\Big).

(1.7)

Let
\displaystyle\Delta_{hi}=[hi,h(i+1)[,0\leq i<[\frac{z}{h}]
be a partition of
[0,z]
and using the Riemann sum definition of the integral, we establish that it correspond to the integral
\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{n}(z,\alpha)
of the following sum :

\tilde{P}_{n}(z,\alpha)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{[z/h]}\Big(\frac{z-ih}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\Bigg[K\Big(\frac{ih-X_{j}}{h}\Big)\,-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int u^{2}K(u)duK^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{ih-X_{j}}{h}\Big)\Bigg]+\mathcal{V}_{n,b}(z)

(1.8)

where
\displaystyle\Big[\frac{\cdot}{h}\Big]
design the integer part of
\displaystyle\frac{\cdot}{h}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{n,b}(z)\to 0
in probability as
n\to+\infty
(for more details, one can refer to Section
4.1
). Finaly, we propose as estimator of FGT poverty index, the following new estimator :

\begin{split}P_{n,b}(z,\alpha)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{[z/h]}\Big(\frac{z-ih}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\Bigg[K\Big(\frac{ih-X_{j}}{h}\Big)\,-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int u^{2}K(u)duK^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{ih-X_{j}}{h}\Big)\Bigg],\end{split}

(1.9)

\text{for}\quad\alpha=0\quad\text{or}\quad\alpha\geq 1
.

Additional hypotheses are made about both
K
and
K^{\prime\prime}
, that is :

(\mathbf{H_{4}})

K
is of bounded variation function
\displaystyle V_{-\infty}^{u}K
on
\mathbb{R}
and let
V(\mathbb{R})
be its total variation.

(\mathbf{H_{5}})

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|uK(u)|du<+\infty
and
\int u^{2}K(u)du<+\infty
.

(\mathbf{H_{6}})

There exists a nonincreasing function
\lambda
such that
\displaystyle\lambda(\frac{u}{h})=O(h)
on bounded intervals,

\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},|K(x)-K(y)|\leq\lambda|x-y|\;\text{and}\;\lambda(u)\longrightarrow 0\;\text{when}\;u\rightarrow 0,\text{and}\;u\geq 0.

2
Main results

Our main resultats are relative to the following additional about the density function
f
:

\mathbf{C_{1}}:\quad f
is uniformly continuous.

\mathbf{C_{2}}:\quad f
is twice almost everywhere differentiable
\displaystyle f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

2.1
Uniform almost sure consistency and behavior of the bias

Theorem 1

Assume that the hypotheses
\mathbf{H_{4}}
and
\mathbf{C_{1}}
hold. Then for all
M>0
, estimator
\displaystyle P_{n,b}(z,\alpha)
converges uniformly almost surely on
[0,M]
to
\displaystyle P(z,\alpha)
as
\displaystyle n\rightarrow+\infty\quad i.e.

P\Big(\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{z\in[0,M]}|P_{n,b}(z,\alpha)-P(z,\alpha)|=0\Big)=1,

provided
\displaystyle nh^{2}(\log\log n)^{-1}\to+\infty
as
\displaystyle n\to+\infty
.

Theorem 2

Assume that the hypotheses
\mathbf{H_{4}},\mathbf{H_{5}}
and
\mathbf{C_{2}}
hold. Then for all
M>0
, the estimator
\displaystyle P_{n,b}(z,\alpha)
converges uniformly almost surely on
[0,M]
to
\displaystyle P(z,\alpha)
as
\displaystyle n\rightarrow+\infty\quad i.e.

P\Big(\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{z\in[0,M]}|P_{n,b}(z,\alpha)-P(z,\alpha)|=0\Big)=1,

provided that
\displaystyle nh^{2}(\log\log n)^{-1}\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
.

For the demonstration of the theorems, we use the Theorem 2 of Kiefer
[
Kiefer (1961)
]
and the following lemmas showing that
P_{n,b}(z,\alpha)
is uniformly asymptotic unbiased on all bounded interval.

Lemma 1

If the hypothese
\mathbf{C_{1}}
holds , then
\forall\,M>0
, we have

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{z\in[0,M]}|\mathbb{E}(P_{n,b}(z,\alpha))-P(z,\alpha)|=0,.

Lemma 2

If the hypotheses
\mathbf{H_{5}}\;\text{and}\;\mathbf{C_{2}}
hold, then :

\begin{split}&\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbb{E}(P_{n,b}(z,\alpha))-P(z,\alpha)|\leq h\Bigg(\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f^{{}^{\prime}}(x)|\,dx\Big)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|u|+1)|K(u)|\,du\Big)\\
&+2(\alpha D+Ah)\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|K(u)|\,du\Bigg)+h^{3}\int u^{2}K(u)du,\end{split}

(2.1)

where

D=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}F(x)\quad\text{and}\quad A=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}f(x).

Remark 1

If
K
satisfies the hypothese
\mathbf{H_{5}}
, then by using
\mathbf{H_{1}}
, the kernel

\displaystyle\widehat{K}=\frac{K^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}K^{2}(y)\,dy}
also satisfy it.

From the two previous lemmas, we get the following corollaries :

Corollary 1

Under the assumptions of Lemma
1
, we have uniformly on
[0,M]
(resp
\mathbb{R}
)

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{[\frac{z}{h}]}\Big(1-\frac{ih}{z}\Big)^{2\alpha}\Bigg[K\Big(\frac{ih-X_{j}}{h}\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\int u^{2}K(u)duK^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{ih-X_{j}}{h}\Big)\Bigg]^{2}\Bigg)=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(y)\,dy\Big)P(z,2\alpha).\end{split}

Corollary 2

If the assumptions of Theorem
2
hold and if
\displaystyle h=O(n^{-1}\log\log n)^{1/4}
, then for all
M>0
, we have almost surely :

\sup_{z\in[0,M]}|\mathbb{E}(P_{n,b}(z,\alpha))-P(z,\alpha)|=O(n^{-1}\log\log n)^{3/4}.

2.2
Uniforme mean square consistency

Theorem 3

If
\mathbf{H_{6}}
and
\mathbf{C_{1}}
hold. Then :

1.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}n\mathbb{V}ar(P_{n,b}(z,\alpha))=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}K^{2}(y)\,dy\Big)P(z,2\alpha)-\Big(P(z,\alpha)\Big)^{2}
.

2.

\text{For all}\quad M>0
,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{z\in[0,M]}\mathbb{E}\Big(P_{n,b}(z,\alpha)-P(z,\alpha)\Big)^{2}=0.

Theorem 4

Assume that
\mathbf{H_{6}}
and
\mathbf{C_{2}}
hold. Then :

1.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}n\mathbb{V}ar(P_{n,b}(z,\alpha))=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K^{2}(y)\,dy\Big)P(z,2\alpha)-\Big(P(z,\alpha)\Big)^{2}
.

2.

\text{Moreover, if }\quad\mathbf{H_{5}}\quad\text{holds, we have \, for all}\quad M>0
,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{z\in[0,M]}\mathbb{E}\Big(P_{n,b}(z,\alpha)-P(z,\alpha)\Big)^{2}=0.

For the proof of this theorem, we assume that the hypothese
\mathbf{C_{1}}
or
\mathbf{C_{2}}
holds and before that, we prove the Theorem
5
below by using the following lemma.

Lemma 3

Let
0\leq\theta_{i}\leq 1,i=1,2
. Then for all
x,y
and
x\neq y
we have

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Big((h)^{-2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}&\Bigg(\Bigg|\Bigg[K\big(\frac{u-x+\theta_{1}}{h}\big)-\frac{1}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}\big(\frac{u-x+\theta_{1}}{h}\big)\Bigg]\\
&\times\Bigg[K\big(\frac{u-y+\theta_{2}}{h}\big)-\frac{1}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}\big(\frac{u-y+\theta_{2}}{h}\big)\Bigg]\Bigg|\Bigg)f(u)\,du\Big)=0.\end{split}

Theorem 5

Assume that the hypothese
\mathbf{H_{6}}
holds. Then for all
M>0
,

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{z\in[0,M]}\sum_{0\leq i\neq j\leq[\frac{z}{h}]}\Big(1-\frac{ih}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big(1-\frac{jh}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big[K\Big(\frac{u-ih}{h}\Big)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{u-ih}{h}\Big)\Big]\\
&\times\Big[K\Big(\frac{u-jh}{h}\Big)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{u-jh}{h}\Big)\Big]f(u)\,du=0.\end{split}

Remark 2

The estimator
P_{n,-b}(z,\alpha)
has asymptotic efficiency with respect to
\widehat{P}_{n}(z,\alpha)
,

e(z,\alpha)=\bigg((\int_{\mathbf{R}}K^{2}(y)\,dy)P(z,2\alpha)-(P(z,\alpha))^{2}\bigg)/P(z,2\alpha)-(P(z,\alpha))^{2}.

The integral
\int_{\mathbf{R}}K^{2}(y)\,dy
is strictly less than 1 for the conventional kernels
[
Parzen (1962)
]
p.1068. Then we have in this case
e(z,\alpha)<1
. In theorem
4
, the speeed of convergence in mean square is of the order of
O(\frac{1}{n^{2}})
if
h
is of the order of
O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})
.

4
Details of the Proofs

4.1
Construction of the estimator

For
z>0
and

\Delta_{h,i}=[hi,h(i+1)[\qquad i=0,\cdot\cdot\cdot,\big[\frac{z}{h}\big]
.
We have the following Riemann sum over the interval
\left[0,z\right]
:

\begin{split}S_{n}(z,\alpha)=\frac{1}{n}&\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{[\frac{z}{h}]-1}\Big(1-\frac{ih}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\Bigg[K\Big(\frac{ih-X_{j}}{h}\Big)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int u^{2}K(u)duK^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{ih-X_{j}}{h}\Big)\Bigg]\\
&+\Big(z-h[\frac{z}{h}]\Big)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Bigg(1-\frac{h[\frac{z}{h}]}{z}\Bigg)^{\alpha}\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{h}\Bigg[K\Bigg(\frac{[\frac{z}{h}]h-X_{j}}{h}\Bigg)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int u^{2}K(u)duK^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{[\frac{z}{h}]h-X_{j}}{h}\Big)\Bigg],\end{split}

corresponding to the integral

J_{n}(z,\alpha)=\int_{0}^{z}\Big(\frac{z-x}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{h}\Bigg[K\Big(\frac{x-X_{j}}{h}\Big)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int u^{2}K(u)duK^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{x-X_{j}}{h}\Big)\Bigg]\,dx.

The sum
S_{n}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle S_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{[\frac{z}{h}]}\Big(1-\frac{ih}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\Bigg[K\Big(\frac{ih-X_{j}}{h}\Big)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int u^{2}K(u)duK^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{ih-X_{j}}{h}\Big)\Bigg]+\mathcal{V}_{n,b}(z),

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{n,b}(z)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Big(z-h[\frac{z}{h}]\Big)-h}{h}\Big(1-\frac{h[\frac{z}{h}]}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\Bigg[K\Big(\frac{[\frac{z}{h}]h-X_{j}}{h}\Big)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int u^{2}K(u)duK^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{[\frac{z}{h}]h-X_{j}}{h}\Big)\Bigg].

Now, we have to show that

\mathcal{V}_{n,b}\longrightarrow 0
in probability as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Since
[\frac{z}{h}]\leq\frac{z}{h}<[\frac{z}{h}]+1
, we get for
\alpha\geq 0
:

|\mathcal{V}_{n,b}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Bigg|K\left(\frac{[\frac{z}{h}]h-X_{j}}{h}\right)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int u^{2}K(u)duK^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{[\frac{z}{h}]h-X_{j}}{h}\right)\Bigg|.

(4.1)

Note that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
hx+h[\frac{z}{h}]\longrightarrow z
as
n\longrightarrow\infty
. Thus, for
\alpha=0
, using the continuity of
f
and the fact that
K,K^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we have for
n
large enough :

\begin{split}\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigg\{K\Big(\frac{[\frac{z}{h}]h-X_{j}}{h}\Big)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)ds&K^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{[\frac{z}{h}]h-X_{j}}{h}\Big)\Bigg\}\Big]\Big|\\

\begin{split}\sum_{0\leq i\neq j\leq[\frac{z}{h}]}\Big(1-\frac{ih}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big(1-\frac{jh}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg|&\Bigg[K(\frac{u-ih}{h})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{u-ih}{h})\Bigg]\\
&\times\Bigg[K(\frac{jh-u}{h})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{jh-u}{h})\Bigg]\Bigg|f(u)\,du\\
&\qquad=\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Delta:|x-y|>0\}}\Phi_{n}(x,y)\,dxdy\end{split}

where

\begin{split}\Phi_{n}(x,y)&=\frac{1}{(h)^{2}}\sum_{0\leq i\neq j\leq[\frac{z}{h}]}\chi_{\Delta_{h\,i}\times\Delta_{h,j}}(x,y)\Big(1-\frac{ih}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big(1-\frac{jh}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\\
&\quad\times\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg|\Bigg[K(\frac{u-ih}{h})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{u-ih}{h})\Bigg]\Bigg[K(\frac{jh-u}{h})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{u-ih}{h})\Bigg]\Bigg|f(u)\,du.\end{split}

If
\displaystyle(x,y)\in\Delta_{h,i}\times\Delta_{h,j}\quad i\neq j
with the representation

x=hi+\theta_{1}h,\quad y=hj+\theta_{2}h\quad 0\leq\theta_{l}<1,\quad l=1,2

We have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{(h)^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{ih}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}&\Big(1-\frac{jh}{z}\Big)^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg|\Bigg[K(\frac{u-x+\theta_{1}h}{h})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{u-x+\theta_{1}h}{h})\Bigg]\\
&\times\Bigg[K(\frac{u-y+\theta_{2}h}{h})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{u-y+\theta_{2}h}{h})\Bigg]\Bigg|f(u)\,du\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{(h)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg|\Bigg[K(\frac{u-x+\theta_{1}h}{h})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{u-x+\theta_{1}h}{h})\Bigg]\\
&\quad\times\Bigg[K(\frac{u-y+\theta_{2}h}{h})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{u-y+\theta_{2}h}{h})\Bigg]\Bigg|f(u)\,du\end{split}

(4.11)

The right hand-side of (
4.11
) tends to zero as
\displaystyle n\to+\infty
by lemma
3
.

Let
\displaystyle\delta=\frac{z}{2}
. Write

\begin{split}\frac{1}{(h)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}&\Bigg|\Bigg[K(\frac{u-x+\theta_{1}h}{h})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{u-x+\theta_{1}h}{h})\Bigg]\\
&\times\Bigg[K(\frac{u-y+\theta_{2}h}{h})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{u-y+\theta_{2}h}{h})\Bigg]\Bigg|f(u)\,du=\int_{|v|\leq\delta}+\int_{|v|>\delta}.\end{split}

Then, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Delta:|x-y|>0\}}&\Phi_{n}(x,y)\,dxdy\\
&\leq\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Delta:|x-y|>0\}}\sum_{0\leq i\neq j\leq[\frac{z}{h}]}\chi_{\Delta_{h,i}\times\Delta_{h,j}}(x,y)(\int_{|v|\leq\delta}+\int_{|v|>\delta}).\end{split}

The proof of the remainder is conducted as follow :

First consider

\int_{\{(x,y)\in\Delta:|x-y|>0\}}\int_{|v|\leq\delta}.

Let
\displaystyle A=\sup_{x\in[0,z]}f(x)
. The notations being as in the proof lemma
2
with
\displaystyle\delta=\frac{z}{2}
, we have in accordance with inequality (
4.7
)

\begin{split}\int_{|v|\leq\delta}\leq A\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi_{-\frac{\delta}{h}\leq u\leq\frac{\delta}{h}}&\Bigg|K(u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(u)\Bigg|\\
&\times\Bigg|\Bigg[K(\frac{x-\theta_{1}h-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{x-\theta_{1}h-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)\Bigg](h)^{-1}\Bigg|\,du.\end{split}

For every
u

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Bigg|\Bigg[K(\frac{x-\theta_{1}h-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{x-\theta_{1}h-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)\Bigg](h)^{-1}\Big|=0.

We have

\begin{split}&\Bigg|\Bigg[K(\frac{x-\theta_{1}h-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{x-\theta_{1}h-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)\Bigg](h)^{-1}\Bigg|\\
&=\Bigg|\Bigg[K(\frac{x-\theta_{1}h-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{x-\theta_{1}h-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)\Bigg]\\
&-\Bigg[K(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)\Bigg]\\
&+\Bigg[K(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)\Bigg]\Bigg|(h)^{-1}\\
&\leq\Bigg(\lambda(\frac{2z}{h})+\Bigg|K(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}h-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)\Bigg|\Bigg)(h)^{-1}.\end{split}

Moreover,

\begin{split}&\Bigg|K(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)\Bigg|(h)^{-1}\\
&=\Big|\frac{2z+x-y+hu}{h}\Big|\Bigg|K(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)\Bigg|\frac{1}{|2z+x-y+hu|}\end{split}

Let
\displaystyle B=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}|y||K(y)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(y)|
and
\displaystyle C=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}|K(y)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(y)|
, then we have

\begin{split}\Big|\frac{2z+x-y+hu}{h}\Big|\Bigg|K(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)\Bigg|\leq B+2hC.\end{split}

Therefore, we have

\begin{split}\Bigg|K(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{2z+x-\theta_{1}-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)\Bigg|(h)^{-1}\leq\frac{B+2hC}{|2z+x-y+hu|}\end{split}

Hence

\begin{split}\Bigg|\Bigg[K(\frac{x-\theta_{1}h-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{x-\theta_{1}h-y+\theta_{2}h}{h}+u)\Bigg](h)^{-1}\Bigg|\leq\lambda(\frac{2z}{h})+\frac{B+2hC}{|2z+x-y+hu|}\end{split}

We conclude that for
h
small enough

\begin{split}\int_{|v|\leq\delta}&\leq\frac{A}{|2z+x-y+hu|}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg|K(u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(u)\Bigg|(B+2hC)\,du<\frac{AD}{|2z+x-y+hu|}\\
&\leq\frac{AD}{|2z+x-y+hu|}\\
&\leq\frac{AD}{(2z+x-y+hu)}\end{split}

\displaystyle D
being the finite bound of
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg|K(u)-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(u)\Bigg|(B+2C)du
.

Finally, we have

\int_{|v|\leq\delta}\leq\frac{AD}{(2z+x-y+hu)}+O(h).

Since
\displaystyle-\delta\leq hu\leq\delta
we have
\displaystyle\frac{z}{2}\leq 2z+x-y+hu\leq\frac{7z}{2}
.

Hence

\int_{|v|\leq\delta}\leq\frac{2AD}{z}+O(h).

Therefore, by Lebesgue-dominated convergence, we have

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int\int_{\Delta}\Big(\int_{|v|\leq\delta}\Big)\,dxdy=\int\int_{\Delta}\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\int_{|v|\leq\delta}\Big)\,dxdy=0\end{split}

(4.12)

Consider then
\displaystyle\int_{|v|>\delta}
.

We use the second part, by analogous reasoning, of the proof of lemma
2

\begin{split}\int_{|v|>\delta}\leq\frac{2}{\delta}\sup_{|v|>\delta}\Big|\frac{v}{h}\Big|\Bigg|K(\frac{v}{h})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{v}{h})\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|(h)^{-1}\Bigg[K(\frac{u}{h})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int s^{2}K(s)dsK^{\prime\prime}(\frac{u}{h})\Bigg]\Big|f(u+y-\theta_{2}h)\,du.\end{split}

We have

Hence

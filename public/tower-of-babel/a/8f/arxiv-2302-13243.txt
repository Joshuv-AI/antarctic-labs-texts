Quantum z = 2 z=2 Lifshitz criticality in one-dimensional interacting fermions
Ke Wang

Abstract
We consider Lifshitz criticality (LC) with the dynamical critical exponent z = 2 z=2 in one-dimensional interacting fermions with a filled Dirac Sea. We report that interactions have crucial effects on Lifshitz criticality. Single particle excitations are destabilized by interaction and decay into the particle-hole continuum, which is reflected in the logarithmic divergence in the imaginary part of one-loop self-energy. We show that the system is sensitive to the sign of interaction. Random-phase approximation (RPA) shows that the collective particle-hole excitations emerge only when the interaction is repulsive. The dispersion of collective modes is gapless and linear. If the interaction is attractive, the one-loop renormalization group (RG) shows that there may exist a stable RG fixed point described by two coupling constants. We also show that the on-site interaction (without any other perturbations at the UV scale) would always turn on the relevant velocity perturbation to the quadratic Lagrangian in the RG flow, driving the system flow to the conformal-invariant criticality. In the numerical simulations of the lattice model at the half-filling, we find that, for either on-site positive or negative interactions, the dynamical critical exponent becomes z = 1 z=1 in the infrared (IR) limit and the entanglement entropy is a logarithmic function of the system size L L . The work paves the way to study one-dimensional interacting LCs.



Introduction.
Strong infrared (IR) fluctuations
play an important role in low-dimensional field theories in the absence of a gap. In classical two-dimensional (2D) field theories with continuous symmetries, emerging logarithmic IR divergences
destroy the long-range order
Mermin and Wagner 1966
;
Berezinsky 1971
and results in the absence of spontaneous symmetry breaking
Coleman 1973
;
Kosterlitz and Thouless 1973
. For quantum one-dimensional (1D) fermions, similar logarithmic divergences in loop diagrams destabilize single quasi-particles and lead to the breakdown of Fermi Liquids
Abrikosov et al. 1975
;
Giamarchi 2004
. The low-energy excitations become bosonic
Tomonaga 1950
upon a weak interaction turned on. This is the celebrated Luttinger Liquid (LL) theory
Luttinger 1963
;
Haldane 1981
;
Imambekov et al. 2012
. Non-Fermi liquids in two-dimension remain as a puzzle due to strong IR fluctuations
Lee 2009
;
Borges et al. 2022
;
Metlitski and Sachdev 2010
.

Quantum criticality (QC) emerges when the phase transition happens between gapped phases. Many of them are described by conformal field theories
Wilson 1969
;
Belavin et al. 1984
;
Affleck 1986
;
Blöte et al. 1986
;
Di Francesco et al. 1997
;
Mattis and Lieb 1965
(CFTs). There are also occasions. One example is multi-critical points
Verresen et al. 2018
;
Boudreault et al. 2021
;
Chepiga and Mila 2021
with the non-unit dynamical critical exponent
z
. They are called Lifshitz criticalities (LCs)
Hornreich et al. 1975
;
Hořava 2009
. The non-relativistic exponent
z
is not rare. For example, free Schödinger equation and unitary Fermi gas are characterzied by
z=2
Nishida and Son 2007
;
Golkar and Son 2014
. Symmetry algebra is the Schödinger algebra
Hagen 1972
;
Niederer 1973
instead of conformal algebra.

Interactions are essential to LCs since the interaction energy can exceed the kinetic energy. The scale of kinetic energy is
K\sim t/L^{z}
for the 1D system with a finite-size
L
. The contact interaction has the scale
V\sim u_{0}/L
. Here
t
and
u_{0}
are two finite coefficients to ensure the energy dimension. The ratio of two energy scales is
V/K\sim(u_{0}/t)L^{z-1}
. Therefore, when
z>1
, the interaction effects dominate.

In this paper, we consider one-dimensional interacting fermions with the dynamical critical exponent
z=2
and a filled Fermi Sea. In this case, besides interaction energy exceeding kinetic energy, interaction also causes strong quantum fluctuations. For example, interactions cause the creation of virtual particle-hole pairs (vacuum polarizations) which renormalize the low-energy behaviors. Therefore, one has to consider interaction effects carefully to obtain the right low-energy descriptions. Here we study interacting LCs from both aspects of field theories and numerical simulations. Below we start with the perturbative calculations.

Conclusion and Remarks.
In this work, we study the
z=2
field theory in the one-dimensional interacting fermions with a Dirac Sea both analytically and numerically. Perturbative calculations indicate that the lifetime of a single particle vanishes. For the positive coupling constant, we obtain consistent results from RPA calculation and numerical simulations: the low-energy theory is described by CFT with
c=2
and stable low-energy excitations are gapless bosons. Numerical simulations confirm this prediction.

For the negative coupling constant, the RPA result shows that there are no sharply well-defined collective modes. We show that there may exist a non-trivial RG fixed point characterized by two non-zero coupling constants. But if one only turns on
u_{0}
at the UV scale, the velocity term is generated in the RG flow and dramatically changes the scaling of the quadratic Lagrangian.

Our next step is to numerically scan the phase diagram versus hopping parameters/the on-site interaction to check whether there exists a non-trivial RG fixed point and further study its properties.

\displaystyle\Pi_{s,-s}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{4\pi}\frac{1}{k^{2}-a^{2}-i\delta}=-\frac{i}{4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|-s\omega/2+q^{2}/4|}}

(S8)

where
a^{2}=|q^{2}/4-s\omega/2|

Therefore, one can reach a simple expression,

\displaystyle\Pi_{s,s^{\prime}}(q,\omega)=\frac{1}{4}\frac{|s-s^{\prime}|}{\sqrt{-2s\omega+q^{2}}}.

(S9)

This is Eq. 4 in the main-text.

I.2
One-loop Self energy: imaginary part

Correction to the green function may be obtained from inserting the self energy,

\displaystyle\delta G_{+}(k,\omega)=G_{+}(k,\omega)\Sigma_{+}(k,\omega)G_{+}(k,\omega).

(S10)

At the one loop level, the self energy is approximated by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{+}(k,\omega)\simeq-iu_{0}^{2}\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}G_{-}(k-q,\omega-\nu)\Pi_{+,-}(q,\nu)

(S11)

Note that the other vertex-like diagram (usually called Fock diagram) in the second order perturbation does not contribute since the coupling constant does not contain the scattering process
++\rightarrow--
. Note that in the Fermi liquid theory, the Fock diagram contributes since it involves the backscattering events between two
different
points in the Fermi surface. However, we only have a single Fermi point here.
Now we trace the imaginary part of
\Sigma_{+}
.

\displaystyle\text{Im}\Sigma_{+}(k,\omega)\equiv F_{1}+F_{2};

(S12)

where
F_{1/2}
involves the product of two imaginary/real parts of
G
and
\Pi
,

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}\text{Im}G_{-}(k-q,\omega-\nu)\text{Im}\Pi_{+,-}(q,\nu),

(S13)

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}\text{Re}G_{-}(k-q,\omega-\nu)\text{Re}\Pi_{+,-}(q,\nu).

(S14)

Below we evaluate two integrals of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
.

Evaluation of
F_{1}
.
Note that
\text{Im}G_{-}(k-q,\omega-\nu)=\pi\delta(\omega-\nu+\epsilon_{k-q})
and

\displaystyle\text{Im}\Pi_{+-}(q,\nu)=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\nu-q^{2}/2}}\Theta(\nu-q^{2}/2)

(S15)

Then one may obtain

\displaystyle F_{1}=-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{2\sqrt{2}}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\pi\delta(\omega-\nu+\epsilon_{k-q})\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu-q^{2}/2}}\Theta(\nu-q^{2}/2).

(S16)

Integration over
\nu
gives,

\displaystyle F_{1}=-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{4\sqrt{2}}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega+(k-q)^{2}-q^{2}/2}}\Theta(\omega+(k-q)^{2}-q^{2}/2)

Now we see one special kind of term appearing above,
(k-q)^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{2}
. We will see this term many times when we calculate the self-energy involved with one-loop diagrams. This special has a clear physical origin: poles of the polarization operator is
not
same as ones of propagators. The former one locates the minimum of particle-hole continuum while the latter one is the free fermion excitation.

The difference of two poles,
(k-q)^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{2}
, can be reformulated by
\frac{1}{2}(q-2k)^{2}-k^{2}
. Redefining the integration variable, one finds

\displaystyle J_{+}=-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{4\sqrt{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q^{2}/2+\omega-k^{2}}}\Theta(q^{2}/2+\omega-k^{2}).

(S17)

There emerges one important quantity,
Q^{2}\equiv|\omega-k^{2}|
. Now we consider two different scenarios based on the sign of
\omega-k^{2}
.

•

\omega-k^{2}\geq 0
. The integral reads

\displaystyle J_{+}=-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q^{2}+Q^{2}}}=-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\ln\frac{\Lambda+\sqrt{\Lambda^{2}+Q^{2}}}{Q},\quad Q\geq 0

(S18)

where
\Lambda
is UV cutoff. Note that there is no Fermi-energy as the upper bound of the momentum. We only need
\Lambda<\pi/a
where
a
is the lattice spacing. We may claim the result below

\displaystyle J_{+}\simeq-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\sqrt{\omega-k^{2}}}.

(S19)

One may observe a blow-up at the on-shell condition
\omega=k^{2}
. It is traced back to a infrared divergence in the integral. Even at off-shell case,
J_{+}
is still logarithmically large .

•

\omega-k^{2}\leq 0
. The integral reads

\displaystyle J_{+}=-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{2}\int_{Q}^{\infty}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q^{2}-Q^{2}}}=-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\sqrt{|\omega-k^{2}|}}\right)

(S20)

As a summary of
J_{+}
, we find the expression valid for arbitrary
\omega-k^{2}
,

\displaystyle\boxed{J_{+}\simeq-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\sqrt{|\omega-k^{2}|}}.}

(S21)

Evaluation of
F_{2}
.

In the previous calculation, we only focus on the imaginary part.
The real part of polarization becomes

\displaystyle\text{Re}\Pi_{+-}(q,\nu)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{q^{2}/2-\nu}}\Theta(q^{2}/2-\nu)

(S22)

The real part of the propagator carries the principal part,

\displaystyle\text{Re}G_{-}(k-q,\omega-\nu)=P\frac{1}{\omega-\nu+(k-q)^{2}}.

(S23)

Defining a new variable
x=q^{2}/2-\nu
, one may rewrite the denominator in the propagator as

\displaystyle\omega-\nu+(k-q)^{2}=x+X(q),\quad X(q)\equiv\omega+k^{2}-2kq+q^{2}/2=\frac{1}{2}(q-2k)^{2}+\omega-k^{2}.

(S24)

With a proper redefinition of
q
, one obtain the expression of
F_{2}

\displaystyle F_{2}\simeq-u_{0}^{2}\int\frac{dx}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}P\frac{1}{x+\frac{1}{2}q^{2}+\omega-k^{2}}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{x}}\Theta(x).

(S25)

One need to consider two different scenarios.

•

\omega-k^{2}\geq 0
.

\displaystyle F_{2}\simeq-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\int\frac{dx}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\frac{1}{q^{2}+2x+2Q^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\Theta(x)=-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{4}\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{dx}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+Q^{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}

(S26)

We use the UV cut-off to regulate the integral,

\displaystyle\int^{+\Lambda^{2}}_{0}\frac{dx}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+Q^{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\simeq\frac{1}{\pi}\ln\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{Q}\right)

(S27)

Therefore,
F_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle F_{2}=-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\ln\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{Q}\right)

(S28)

•

\omega-k^{2}<0
.

\displaystyle F_{2}\simeq-2u_{0}^{2}\int\frac{dx}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}P\frac{1}{q^{2}+2x-2Q^{2}}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{x}}\Theta(x)\simeq-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{4}\int^{+\infty}_{Q^{2}}\frac{dx}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x-Q^{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}

(S29)

In the last ”equality”, we approximation
F_{2}
by ignoring the contribution from
x<Q^{2}
. Not hard to see that the integral gives the same integral with Eq.
S28
.

As a conclusion, we find that the imaginary of self-energy contains a logarithmic divergence,

\displaystyle\text{Im}\Sigma_{+}(k,\omega)=-\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\sqrt{|\omega-k^{2}|}}

(S30)

which is Eq. 5 in the maintext.

II
RPA Self-energy

Consider the RPA self energy for particle-like excitations,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\text{RPA},+}(k,\omega)\simeq-iu_{0}\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}G_{-}(k-q,\omega-\nu)\frac{1}{1-u_{0}\Pi_{+,-}(q,\nu)}

(S31)

II.1
Real Part of
\Sigma_{\text{RPA}}

Now we would like to trace its real part.

\displaystyle\text{Re}\Sigma_{\text{RPA},+}(k,\omega)=u_{0}\text{Im}\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}G_{-}(k-q,\omega-\nu)\frac{1}{1-u_{0}\Pi_{+,-}(q,\nu)}

(S32)

Again, one may separate it into two terms,

\displaystyle R_{1}=u_{0}\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}\text{Im}G_{-}(k-q,\omega-\nu)\text{Re}\frac{1}{1-u_{0}\Pi_{+,-}(q,\nu)},

(S33)

\displaystyle R_{2}=u_{0}\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}\text{Re}G_{-}(k-q,\omega-\nu)\text{Im}\frac{1}{1-u_{0}\Pi_{+,-}(q,\nu)}.

(S34)

Contribution from
R_{1}
.
Recall that
\text{Im}G_{-}(k-q,\omega-\nu)
reads
\pi\delta(\omega-\nu+\epsilon_{k-q})
. Then one may write Eq.
S33
as

\displaystyle R_{1}=\pi u_{0}\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}\delta(\omega-\nu+\epsilon_{k-q})\left(\frac{\Theta(-\nu+q^{2}/2)}{1-\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{-2\nu+q^{2}}}}+\text{Re}\frac{\Theta(\nu-q^{2}/2)}{1+i\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{2\nu-q^{2}}}}\right)

(S35)

The delta function set
\nu=\omega+\epsilon_{k-q}
. We see the combination
(k-q)^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{2}
again. As usual we use
2(k-q)^{2}-q^{2}=(q-2k)^{2}-2k^{2}
and replace the integral variable
q
by
q^{\prime}=q-2k
. This gives

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{u_{0}}{2}\int\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\left(\frac{\Theta(-2\omega-q^{\prime 2}+2k^{2})}{1-\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{-2\omega-q^{\prime 2}+2k^{2}}}}+\text{Re}\frac{\Theta(2\omega+q^{\prime 2}-2k^{2})}{1+i\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{2\omega+q^{\prime 2}-2k^{2}}}}\right)

(S36)

Again, one has to study two different situations based on the sign of
2k^{2}-2\omega
.

•

Case 1:
\omega<k^{2}
. In this case,

\displaystyle R_{1}=u_{0}\int^{\sqrt{2k^{2}-2\omega}}_{0}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1-\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{-2\omega-q^{\prime 2}+2k^{2}}}}+u_{0}\int_{\sqrt{2k^{2}-2\omega}}^{+\Lambda}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{4}\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{q^{\prime 2}+2\omega-2k^{2}}}

(S37)

Let us express the term above in the momentum scale
Q=\sqrt{2k^{2}-2\omega}
,

\displaystyle R_{1}=u_{0}\left(\int^{Q}_{0}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1-\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{Q^{2}-q^{\prime 2}}}}+\int_{Q}^{+\Lambda}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{4}\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{q^{\prime 2}-Q^{2}}}\right)

(S38)

Case 1a:
u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}>0
.

One may use the indefinite integral below,

\displaystyle\int dx\frac{1}{1-b/\sqrt{a^{2}-x^{2}}}=-\frac{b^{2}}{\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{bx}{\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}}\sqrt{a^{2}-x^{2}}}\right)-\frac{b^{2}}{\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\right)+b\tan^{-1}\frac{x}{\sqrt{a^{2}-x^{2}}}+x

to evaluate the first integral.
Setting
b=u_{0}/2
and
a=Q
, one reach

\displaystyle\int^{Q}_{0}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1-\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{Q^{2}-q^{\prime 2}}}}=-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{8\pi}\frac{\pi/2\text{sgn}(u_{0})+\arctan(Q/\sqrt{u^{2}_{0}/4-Q^{2}})}{\sqrt{u^{2}_{0}/4-Q^{2}}}+\frac{u_{0}}{4\pi}\frac{\pi}{2}+[u_{0}\text{-independent}]

(S39)

The second integral reads

\displaystyle\int_{Q}^{+\Lambda}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{4}\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{q^{\prime 2}-Q^{2}}}=-\int_{Q}^{+\Lambda}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{u_{0}^{2}/4}{q^{\prime 2}-Q^{2}+u_{0}^{2}/4}+[u_{0}\text{-independent}].

(S40)

Here we usually ignore the
u_{0}\text{-independent}
contribution in the integral. Reason is following: they give corrections to the self-energy linear in
u_{0}
which is fundamentally tap-dole diagram contributions. These contributions also do not contain any
k,\omega
-dependence. One may use the formula below

\displaystyle\int dx\frac{1}{x^{2}+a^{2}}=\frac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)

(S41)

to estimate the second integral.
Notice that the denominator is always positive due to
u_{0}^{2}
. Setting
a^{2}=u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}
, the integral gives

\displaystyle-\int_{Q}^{+\Lambda}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{u^{2}_{0}/4}{q^{\prime 2}-Q^{2}+u_{0}^{2}/4}\simeq-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{16\sqrt{u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}}}+\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}}}\arctan\frac{Q}{\sqrt{u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}}}

(S42)

When
u_{0}>0
, one would see that a divergence at
Q^{2}=u_{0}^{2}/4
in Eq.
S39
. This is understood as the particle-hole collective modes. Sum two integrals, one reaches

\displaystyle R_{1}=\left(\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{8}-(\text{sgn}(u_{0})+1)\frac{u_{0}^{3}}{16\sqrt{u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}}}\right)\Theta(u_{0}^{2}/8-|k^{2}-\omega|)\Theta(k^{2}-\omega).

(S43)

If
u_{0}>0
, the on-shell condition gives
\omega=k^{2}+\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{8}-\frac{u_{0}^{3}}{8\sqrt{u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}}}
, causing emergence of Fermi-velocity.

Case 1b:
u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}<0
.
Consider the first integral. One may use the formula

\displaystyle\arctan ix=\frac{i}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)

(S44)

One may need to evaluate

\displaystyle-\frac{b^{2}}{\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\Big[\tan^{-1}\left(-iu_{0}\infty\right)+\tan^{-1}\left(-i\frac{a}{\sqrt{|b^{2}-a^{2}|}}\right)\Big]+b\frac{\pi}{2}

(S45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{b^{2}}{\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\Big[\frac{i}{2}\log\left(\frac{1-u_{0}\infty}{1+u_{0}\infty}\right)+\frac{i}{2}\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}}-a}{\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}}+a}\right)\Big]+b\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{b^{2}}{\sqrt{|b^{2}-a^{2}|}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{a-\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}}}{\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}}+a}\right)\Big]+b\frac{\pi}{2}

Then the first integral in
R_{1}
gives

\displaystyle\int^{Q}_{0}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1-\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{Q^{2}-q^{\prime 2}}}}=\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{16\pi\sqrt{|u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}|}}\log\left(\frac{Q+\sqrt{|u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}|}}{Q-\sqrt{|u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}|}}\right)+\frac{u_{0}}{8}

(S46)

The second integral in
R_{1}
gives

\displaystyle-\int_{Q}^{+\Lambda}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{u_{0}^{2}/4}{q^{\prime 2}-Q^{2}+u_{0}^{2}/4}\simeq-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{16\pi\sqrt{|u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}|}}\ln\left(\frac{Q+\sqrt{|u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}|}}{Q-\sqrt{|u_{0}^{2}/4-Q^{2}|}}\right).

(S47)

In this case, one may write down the epxression of
R_{1}
as

\displaystyle R_{1}=\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{8}\Theta(k^{2}-\omega-u_{0}^{2}/8),\quad\text{if }u_{0}^{2}/4<2|k^{2}-\omega|

(S48)

•

Case 2:
\omega>k^{2}
. In this case expression is much simpler,

\displaystyle R_{1}=u_{0}\int_{0}^{+\Lambda}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{4}\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{q^{\prime 2}+Q^{2}}}

(S49)

Here we define
Q=\sqrt{2\omega-2k^{2}}
. This integral can be easily integrated out,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\Lambda}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{4}\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{q^{\prime 2}+Q^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\Lambda}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{q^{\prime 2}+Q^{2}}{q^{\prime 2}+Q^{2}+\frac{1}{4}u_{0}^{2}}=\int_{0}^{+\Lambda}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\left(1-\frac{u_{0}^{2}/4}{q^{\prime 2}+Q^{2}+\frac{1}{4}u_{0}^{2}}\right)

(S50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda/2\pi-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{q^{\prime 2}+Q^{2}+\frac{1}{4}u_{0}^{2}}=\Lambda/2\pi-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{8}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{Q^{2}+u_{0}^{2}/4}}

(S51)

Again we ignore the constant term in the integral. One may obtain the contribution to the self-energy

\displaystyle R_{1}=-\frac{u_{0}^{3}}{16}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega-2k^{2}+u_{0}^{2}/4}}\Theta(\omega-k^{2}).

(S52)

If we set
\omega=k^{2}
, then it reduces to be
-\text{sgn}(u_{0})\pi u_{0}^{2}/4
. When
u_{0}
is negative, result is consistent with the one from the calculation in the case
\omega<k^{2}
.
Therefore we can unify the expression of the
R_{1}
to be

\displaystyle\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{8}\Theta(k^{2}-\omega)-(\text{sgn}(u_{0})+1)\frac{u_{0}^{3}}{16\sqrt{u_{0}^{2}/4-2k^{2}+2\omega}}\Theta(u_{0}^{2}/8-|k^{2}-\omega|)\Theta(k^{2}-\omega)-\frac{u_{0}^{3}}{16}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega-2k^{2}+u_{0}^{2}/4}}\Theta(\omega-k^{2})

Contribution from
R_{2}
.

From its definition, one may write down the expression of
R_{2}
,

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{0}\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}P\frac{1}{\omega-\nu+(k-q)^{2}}\text{Im}\frac{\Theta(\nu-q^{2}/2)}{1+i\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{2\nu-q^{2}}}}

(S53)

Performing Im, one reaches

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-u_{0}\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}P\frac{1}{\omega-\nu+(k-q)^{2}}\frac{\Theta(\nu-q^{2}/2)}{1+\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{8\nu-4q^{2}}}\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{2\nu-q^{2}}}

(S54)

Defining a new variable
x=-q^{2}/2+\nu
, one may rewrite the denominator in the propagator as

\displaystyle\omega-\nu+(k-q)^{2}=-x+X(q),\quad X(q)\equiv\omega+k^{2}-2kq+q^{2}/2=\frac{1}{2}(q-2k)^{2}+\omega-k^{2}.

(S55)

With a proper redefinition of
q
, one obtain the expression of
R_{2}

\displaystyle R_{2}\simeq-u_{0}\int\frac{dx}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}P\frac{1}{-x+q^{\prime 2}+\omega-k^{2}}\frac{\Theta(x)}{1+\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{8x}}\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{x}}.

(S56)

We consider the integration over
q
firstly.

•

If
\omega-k^{2}<0
, then the integral below vanishes,

\displaystyle\int_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{dq}{2\pi}P\frac{1}{q^{2}-a^{2}}=\int_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\frac{1}{2a}\left(P\frac{1}{q-a}-P\frac{1}{q+a}\right)=0.

(S57)

Here
a
is any real number. Therefore
R_{2}=0
in this case.

•

If
\omega-k^{2}>0
, one has to handle the integral

\displaystyle\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}P\frac{1}{q^{2}+a^{2}}=\frac{1}{2a}

(S58)

Notice that no poles exist. Therefore one can ignore the principal part here. Therefore

R_{2}
reduces to be

\displaystyle R_{2}\simeq-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{4}\int_{0}^{b}\frac{dx}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{8x}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{b-x}}.

(S59)

Here we denote
b=\omega-k^{2}
. One may use the formula below,

\displaystyle\int dx\frac{1}{1+a/x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{b-x}}=2\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{b-x}}\right)-\frac{2\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{a+b}}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{(a+b)x}{a(b-x)}\right).

(S60)

Setting
a=u_{0}^{2}/8
, one obtain the expression of
R_{2}
,

\displaystyle R_{2}\simeq-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{8}\left(1-\frac{|u_{0}|/2}{\sqrt{2\omega-2k^{2}+u_{0}^{2}/4}}\right)=\left(-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{8}+\frac{u^{3}_{0}\text{sgn}(u_{0})}{16}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega-2k^{2}+u_{0}^{2}/4}}\right)\Theta(\omega-k^{2})

(S61)

Recall
R_{1}
is given by

\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{8}\Theta(k^{2}-\omega)-(\text{sgn}(u_{0})+1)\frac{u_{0}^{3}}{16\sqrt{u_{0}^{2}/4-2k^{2}+2\omega}}\Theta(u_{0}^{2}/8-|k^{2}-\omega|)\Theta(k^{2}-\omega)-\frac{u_{0}^{3}}{16}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega-2k^{2}+u_{0}^{2}/4}}\Theta(\omega-k^{2})

Summing
R_{1}+R_{2}
, we arivie the RPA self energy,

\displaystyle\text{Re}\Sigma_{\text{RPA}}=-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{8}\text{sgn}(\omega-k^{2})-\frac{u_{0}^{3}}{16\sqrt{u_{0}^{2}/4+2\omega-2k^{2}}}\Big(1-\text{sgn}(\omega-k^{2})\text{sgn}(u_{0})\Big)

(S62)

II.2
Imaginary Part of
\Sigma_{\text{RPA}}

Since we have evaluated the one-loop level imaginary self-energy, here we only aim to check whether the functional form is affected in the RPA level. Consider

\displaystyle\text{Im}\Sigma_{\text{RPA},+}(k,\omega)=-u_{0}\text{Re}\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}G_{-}(k-q,\omega-\nu)\frac{1}{1-u_{0}\Pi_{+,-}(q,\nu)}.

(S63)

Now we look at the piece which represents the physical process that a single-particle decays into particle-hole continuum,

\displaystyle I_{1}=u_{0}\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\text{Im}G_{-}(k-q,\omega-\nu)\text{Im}\frac{1}{1-u_{0}\Pi_{+,-}(q,\nu)}

(S64)

One repeats the procedure we have done in the one-loop calculation and finds

\displaystyle I_{1}=\frac{u_{0}}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\text{Im}\frac{1}{1+i\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{q^{2}/2+\omega-k^{2}}}}\Theta(q^{2}/2+\omega-k^{2})

(S65)

Expanding the denominator to the first order in
u_{0}
, one can see the one-loop expression
F_{1}
. For simplicity, we consider
\omega-k^{2}\geq 0
. It gives

\displaystyle I_{1}=-\frac{u_{0}}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dq}{2\pi}\frac{u_{0}}{2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{q^{2}/2+\omega-k^{2}}}\times\frac{1}{1+\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{8(q^{2}/2+\omega-k^{2})}}=-\frac{u_{0}}{2}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{\sqrt{q^{{}^{\prime}2}+\omega-k^{2}}}{q^{{}^{\prime}2}+\omega-k^{2}+\frac{u^{2}_{0}}{8}}.

(S66)

In the first equality, we take the imaginary part of the integrand. In the second equality, we define

III
Momentum-Shell Renormlaization group

In the momentum-shell method, the RG is implemented by: (1), integrating out the fast modes (2) obtaining the effective Lagrangian (3) Introduce the re-scaled parameters and field operators (which are used to keep Guassian action invariant).

Now we have a bare theory defined by Lagrangian and action. Separate the field operator into slow and fast modes

\displaystyle\psi(k)=\psi_{>}(k)+\psi_{<}(k),\quad>:\Lambda/s<k<\Lambda,\quad<:0<k<\Lambda/s.

(S69)

The operator

is defined by
\psi_{>}(k)=\psi(k)

\psi_{<}(k)
. Then the bare action/Lagrangian can be written as

\displaystyle Z=\int D[\psi_{>}]D[\psi_{<}]e^{S(\psi_{>},\psi_{<})},\quad S(\psi_{>},\psi_{<})=S_{0}(\psi_{>})+S_{0}(\psi_{<})+S_{\text{int}}(\psi_{>},\psi_{<})

(S70)

Effective action of
\psi_{<}
can be obtained by integrating out the fast modes,

\displaystyle Z=\int D[\psi_{<}]e^{S_{0}(\psi_{<})}\int D[\psi_{>}]e^{S_{0}(\psi_{>})}e^{S_{\text{int}}(\psi_{>},\psi_{<})}=Z_{>}\int D[\psi_{<}]e^{S_{0}(\psi_{<})}\langle e^{S_{\text{int}}(\psi_{>},\psi_{<})}\rangle_{>}

(S71)

This equation determines the effective Lagrangian of
\psi_{<}
.
The average of interacting exponential is performed for the Gaussian action of
\psi_{>}
. The technical part is about evaluating the perturbation of
S_{\text{int}}
, which follows from

\displaystyle\log\langle e^{S_{\text{int}}(\psi_{>},\psi_{<})}\rangle_{>}\simeq\langle S_{\text{int}}\rangle_{>}+\frac{\langle S_{\text{int}}^{2}\rangle_{>}-\langle S_{\text{int}}\rangle^{2}_{>}}{2}+O(S^{3}_{\text{int}})

(S72)

The result is generally valid from cumulant expansion. Here I have hided the dependence of
S
on the field operators. Then the effective Lagrangian/action can be obtained perturbatively,

\displaystyle S_{\text{eff}}(\psi_{<})\simeq S_{0}(\psi_{<})+\langle S_{\text{int}}\rangle_{>}+\frac{\langle S_{\text{int}}^{2}\rangle_{>}-\langle S_{\text{int}}\rangle^{2}_{>}}{2}+...

(S73)

Here I only preserve up to the second order. One can perform higher order perturbation, which is fundamentally equivalent to plotting connected Feynman diagrams. Although the first/second orders are written separately, they may contribute to the same coupling constant. Similar behaviors may happen for higher perturbations. One shall be careful about arguing that high-order perturbations do not change results.

To study the RG flow of coupling constant in
S_{\text{eff}}(\psi_{<})

\displaystyle k^{\prime}=sk,\quad\omega^{\prime}=s^{z}\omega,\quad\psi^{\prime}(k^{\prime})=s^{-(2z+1)/2}\psi_{<}(k)

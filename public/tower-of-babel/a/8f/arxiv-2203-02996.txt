The growth mechanism of boundary layers for the 2D Navier-Stokes equations
Fei Wang, Yichun Zhu

Abstract
. We give a detailed description of formation of the boundary layers in the inviscid limit problem. To be more specific, we prove that the magnitude of the vorticity near the boundary is growing to the size of 1 / ν 1/\sqrt{\nu} and the width of the layer is spreading out to be proportional the ν \sqrt{\nu} in a finite time period. In fact, the growth time scaling is almost ν \nu . August 8, 2026



1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem for the two dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equations

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u-\nu\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=0

(1.2)

\text{div}u=0

(1.3)

u|_{t=0}=u_{0}

on the half-space domain
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}:y\geq 0\}
, with the periodic boundary condition in
x
, and the no-slip boundary condition

(1.4)

u|_{y=0}=0.

Here we take the torus
\mathbb{T}=[-\pi,\pi]
, but our results apply for any torus
\mathbb{T}=[-a,a]
for
a>0
. The goal of this paper is to study the growth mechanism of the boundary layers in the inviscid limit problem of the Navier-Stokes system for initial data that are analytic only near the boundary of the domain, and are Sobolev smooth away from the boundary. As an easy implication, we know this growing procedure is still valid for analytic initial data or Sobolev initial data with support away from the boundary, which are considered in
[
SC98a
,
SC98b
]
, and
[
Mae14
]
respectively.

In mathematical fluid dynamics, one of the fundamental problems is to determine whether the solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) could be described
to the leading order by the solutions of the Euler equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{E}+u^{E}\cdot\nabla u^{E}+\nabla p^{E}=0

\displaystyle\text{div}u^{E}=0

in the inviscid limit
\nu\to 0
. In
[
Kat84
]
, Kato gave a criteria for the inviscid limit property to hold in the topology
L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{2}(\mathbb{H}))
, namely, he proved that the limit of energy dissipation in a thin layer of size
\nu

\lim_{\nu\to 0}~\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\{y\lesssim\nu\}}|\nabla u|^{2}dxdydt=0

is equivalent to the strong convergence in the energy norm.
Further refinements and extensions based on Kato’s original argument could be found in
[
BT13
,
CEIV17
,
CKV15
,
Kel08
,
Kel17
,
Mas98
,
TW97
,
Wan01
]
; cf. also the recent review
[
MM16
]
.

However, verifying the proposed properties on the sequence of Navier-Stokes solutions in the above mentioned work based on the knowledge of the initial datum is in general an outstanding open problem. In fact, even the question of whether the weak
L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
inviscid limit holds (against test functions compactly supported in the interior of the domain), remains open. We refer the readers to
[
CV18
,
DN18
,
CLFNLV18
]
for conditional results in this direction.
The main
difficulty of the inviscid limit problems comes from the contribution from the behavior of the fluid near a nontrivial boundary.
To be more specific, in the region close to the boundary
\partial\mathbb{H}
, the mismatch of boundary conditions between the viscous Navier-Stokes flow (no-slip) and the inviscid Euler flow (slip) is the main obstacle of obtaining a uniform in
\nu
estimate of the solutions.
Another difficulty is due to the fast growth of vorticity close to the boundary
\partial\mathbb{H}
as
\nu\to 0
.

Due to the mismatch of boundary conditions, in general the Navier-Stokes solutions could not be approximated well by Euler solutions near the boundary, In order to study this phenomena, Prandtl proposed the following decomposition

(1.5)

u(x,y,z,t)=u^{E}(x,y,z,t)+u^{P}\left(x,y,\frac{z}{\sqrt{\nu}},t\right)+\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\nu})

for the Navier-Stokes solution
u
,
where
u^{P}
is the solution of the Prandtl boundary layer equations. So far, the Prandtl equation are very well-understood by an extensive literature .
For instance, see
[
AWXY14
,
DGV19
,
GVM15
,
IV16
,
KMVW14
,
KV13
,
LY16
,
LCS03
,
MW15
,
Ole66
,
SC98a
]
for the well-posedness and
[
GVD10
,
GN11
,
GVN12
,
LY17
]
for the ill-posedness.

However, establishing the validity of the expansion (
1.5
) only making regularity assumption on the initial data without any symmetry presents a number of outstanding challenges and fewer results are available. In their seminal works
[
SC98a
,
SC98b
]
in 1998,
Sammartino-Caflisch establish the validity of the Prandtl expansion for analytic initial data in the half space
\mathbb{H}
in dimension two. While in 2014, Maekawa (
[
Mae14
]
) proved that the inviscid
limit also holds for initial datum with Sobolev regularity whose associated vorticity is supported away from the boundary, taking advantage of the weak interaction between the boundary vorticity and the bulk flow inside the domain. For different proofs of the Caflisch-Sammartino and
Maekawa results in 2D and 3D, we refer to
[
WWZ17
,
FTZ16
,
FTZ18
,
NN18
]
.

The gap between the Sammartino-Caflisch
[
SC98a
,
SC98b
]
and the Maekawa
[
Mae14
]
results was closed by Kukavica, Vicol, and the first author
[
KVW20a
,
KVW20b
]
, in which we established the strong inviscid limit in the energy norm, for initial data that is only analytic close to the boundary of the domain, and has finite Sobolev regularity in the complement. Later the first author
[
Wan20
]
addressed the problem in the 3D case with initial data only analytic near the boundary.
Up to now, the class of initial data considered in
[
KVW20a
,
KVW20b
,
Wan20
]
appears to be the largest class of initial data that the strong inviscid limit is known to hold, in the absence of structural or symmetry assumptions. We also mention that these results was extended to a general bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by Bardos, Nguyen, Nguyen and Titi in
[
BNNT21
]
. Furthermore, the validity of the Prandtl expansion under such kind of class was established in
[
KNVW21
]
by Kukavica, Nguyen, Vicol, and Wang, which close the inviscid limit problem completely for this functional setting only analytic near the boundary.

Recently in
[
GVMM16
,
GVMM20
]
, Gerard-Varet, Maekawa, and Masmoudi proved the stability of Gevrey perturbations in
x
and Sobolev perturbation in
y
for shear flows of the Prandtl type, when the Prandtl shear flow is both monotonic and concave. Lastly, we mention that the vanishing viscosity limit is also known to hold in the presence of certain symmetry assumptions on the initial data, which is maintained by the flow; see e.g.
[
BW02
,
HMNW12
,
Kel09
,
LFMNL08
,
LFMNLT08
,
MM16
,
Mat94
,
MT08
,
GKF+17
]
and references therein. Also, the very recent works
[
GVM18
,
GI18b
,
GI18a
,
Iye18
]
establish the vanishing viscosity limit and the validity of the Prandtl expansion for the stationary Navier-Stokes equation, in certain regimes.

In the previous works, a lot of attention is paid towards the direction of either verifying the convergence of Navier-Stokes system to Euler equations in an energy norm or proving the Prandtl expansion for the Navier-Stokes equations. However, the formation of the boundary layers is not well studied to the knowledge of the authors, which is one of key elements of the inviscid limit problem. From the perspectives of physics, it seems unlikely that the boundary is immediately there if it is absent initially. Intuitively, there should be a process where the magnitude of the vorticity near the boundary is growing to the size of
1/\sqrt{\nu}
and the width of the layer is spreading out to be proportional the
\sqrt{\nu}
eventually. In this paper, we verify this conjecture and prove that the growth time scaling is almost
\nu
. One interesting phenomena in our proof is that the size of the vorticity and the width of the layer seem to grow linearly in time. However, according to
[
KVW17
]
, we believe this should not be the optimal result and conjecture that the growing speed should be
1/(T-t)
if the initial vorticity is huge.

As mentioned in the beginning, we aim to study the growth mechanism of the boundary layers for initial data that are analytic only near the boundary of the domain, and are Sobolev smooth away from the boundary. Typically our result is more interesting when there is no boundary layer initially. On the other hand, whether the growing boundary layer is monotonic and concave is still open so far, which is directly relevant to the results in
[
GVMM16
,
GVMM20
]
where they assume the initial boundary layer is so. Compared with the previous work
[
KVW20a
,
KVW20b
,
Wan20
]
, the main novelty here is to design a time dependent weight function (see (
2.1
)) to capture the involving feature of the boundary layer. However, this new weight is not compatible with the norm we used in the precious work. Modifying the time coefficient in the norm which recovers the norm by integral accordingly is one of the main difficulty in this paper. Also direct estimate in the proof will not give the optimal scaling of time as
\nu
, instead we adjust the time coefficient in the analytic norm to get the almost optimal scaling, see Remark
2.2
.

The functional setting and main results

2.1.
Notations

Throughout this paper, we will use
f_{\xi}\in\mathbb{C}
to denote the Fourier transform of
f(x,y)
with respect to the
x
variable at frequency
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}
, i.e.,
f(x,y)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{\xi}(y)e^{ix\xi}
. We also write
u_{i,\xi}
or
(u_{i})_{\xi}
for the Fourier transformation of
u_{i}
where
i=1,2
. Let
\mathfrak{Re}z
and
\mathfrak{Im}z
be the real part and imaginary part of a complex number
z
respectively. We define the complex domain
\Omega_{\mu}
by

\Omega_{\mu}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:0\leq\mathfrak{Re}z\leq 1,\ |\mathfrak{Im}z|\leq\mu\mathfrak{Re}z\}\cup\{z\in\mathbb{C}:1\leq\mathfrak{Re}z\leq 1+\mu,\ \ |\mathfrak{Im}z|\leq 1+\mu-\mathfrak{Re}z\},

where we assume that
\mu<\mu_{0}
for some small constant
\mu_{0}\in(0,1/10)
.
For simplicity, we write
e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}
to denote
e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-\mathfrak{Re}z)_{+}|\xi|}
for a complex number z without causing any confusion. We use
f\lesssim g
to represent
f\leq Cg
for some constant
C
.

2.2.
Functional settings

Define the weight function

(2.1)

w_{t}(y)=\begin{cases}w\left(y\left(\frac{\nu^{2}}{t\gamma^{2}}\right)^{\beta}\right)+\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}t}{\nu^{2}}\right)_{+},\ \ \ \ 0<t\leq\nu^{2}/\gamma^{2}\\
w(y),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ t>\nu^{2}/\gamma^{2}\end{cases},

where

(2.2)

\begin{aligned} w(y)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\nu},\ &0<y\leq\sqrt{\nu}\\
y,\ &\sqrt{\nu}\leq y\leq 1\\
1,\ &1\leq y\leq 1+\mu_{0}\end{cases}\end{aligned},

\beta\in(0,1/4)
, and
\gamma>\gamma_{0}
is some sufficiently large constant to be determined. When
t=0
, we set
w_{t}(y)=1
.
As in
[
KVW20a
]
, we introduce a weighted analytic norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\mu,\nu,t}}=\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}w_{t}(y)|f(y)|

for a complex function
f
, where we use
w_{t}(y)
to stand for
w_{t}(\mathbb{R}e\ y)
for the sake of simplicity. For
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
sufficiently small to be determined below, we set

\|f\|_{X_{\mu,t}}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}f_{\xi}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\mu,\nu,t}},

and

(2.3)

\|f\|_{X(t)}=\sup_{\mu<\mu_{0}-\gamma\sqrt{t}}\left(\sum_{0\leq i+j\leq 1}\|\partial_{x}^{i}(y\partial_{y})^{j}f\|_{X_{\mu,t}}+\sum_{i+j=2}(\mu_{0}-\mu-\gamma\sqrt{t})^{1/2+\alpha}\|\partial_{x}^{i}(y\partial_{y})^{j}f\|_{X_{\mu,t}}\right),

where
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
. Similarly as in
[
KVW20a
]
, we define
{L}^{1}
based norms

\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}=\sup_{0\leq\theta<\mu}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega_{\theta})},

\|f\|_{Y_{\mu}}=\sum_{\xi}\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}f_{\xi}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}},

and

\|f\|_{Y(t)}=\sup_{\mu<\mu_{0}-\gamma t}\left(\sum_{0\leq i+j\leq 1}\|\partial_{x}^{i}(y\partial_{y})^{j}f\|_{Y_{\mu}}+\sum_{i+j=2}(\mu_{0}-\mu-\gamma t)^{\alpha}\|\partial_{x}^{i}(y\partial_{y})^{j}f\|_{Y_{\mu}}\right).

We also need a weighted
L^{2}
norm

\|f\|_{S}^{2}=\|yf\|^{2}_{L^{2}(y\geq 1/2)}=\sum_{\xi}\|yf_{\xi}\|_{L^{2}(y\geq 1/2)}^{2},

and a weighted version of the Sobolev
H^{3}
norm

\|f\|_{Z}=\sum_{0\leq i+j\leq 3}\|\partial_{x}^{i}\partial_{y}^{j}f\|_{S}.

Furthermore, it is convenient to introduce

\|f\|_{S_{\mu}}=\sum_{\xi}\|yf_{\xi}\|_{L^{2}(y\geq 1+\mu)}.

At last, we define the cumulative time-dependent norm by

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\omega\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{t}=\|\omega\|_{X(t)}+\|\omega\|_{Y(t)}+\|\omega\|_{Z}.

2.3.
Main results

In this section, we state our main theorems and the proof will be presented in Section
6
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mu_{0}>0
and assume that
\omega_{0}
is such that

(2.4)

\sum_{i+j\leq 2}\|\partial_{x}^{i}(y\partial_{y})^{j}\omega_{0}\|_{X_{\mu_{0},0}}+\sum_{i+j\leq 2}\|(\partial_{x})^{i}(y\partial_{y})^{j}\|_{Y_{\mu_{0}}}+\sum_{i+j\leq 3}\|\partial_{x}^{i}(y\partial_{y})^{j}\omega_{0}\|_{S}\leq M<\infty.

Then there exists a sufficiently large
\gamma>0
and a time
T>0
, depending on
M
and
\mu_{0}
, such that the solution
\omega
to the system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) satisfies

(2.5)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\omega(t)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{t}\leq CM.

Remark 2.2
.

In the above result, the time scaling for the growth procedure is
t\sim\nu^{2}
, which is far from optimal.
By redefining the weight function

(2.6)

w_{t}(y)=\begin{cases}1,&t=0\\
w\left(y\left(\frac{1}{t}\left(\frac{\nu}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{n}/(2^{n}-1)}\right)^{\beta}\right)+\left(1-\left(\frac{\gamma}{\nu}\right)^{2^{n}/(2^{n}-1)}t\right)_{+},\ \ \ &0<t\leq(\nu/\gamma)^{{2^{n}}/{2^{n}-1}}\\
w(y),&t>(\nu/\gamma)^{2^{n}/2^{n}-1}\end{cases}

and the analytic norm

(2.7)

\|f\|_{X(t)}=\sup_{\mu<\mu_{0}-\gamma\sqrt{t}}\left(\sum_{0\leq i+j\leq 1}\|\partial_{x}^{i}(y\partial_{y})^{j}f\|_{X_{\mu,t}}+\sum_{i+j=2}\left(\mu_{0}-\mu-\gamma t^{1/2^{n}}\right)^{1/2+\alpha}\|\partial_{x}^{i}(y\partial_{y})^{j}f\|_{X_{\mu,t}}\right),

following the proof in Section
6
and the appendix, we can upgrade the time scaling to
t\sim\nu^{1+\epsilon}
for arbitrarily small
\epsilon
, which is almost optimal.

The above result immediately implies the following inviscid limit for 2-D Navier-Stokes equation.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\omega_{0}
and
T>0
be as in Theorem
2.1
. Denote by
u^{\nu}
the solution of the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) with viscosity
\nu>0
. Also denote by
\bar{u}
the solution of the Euler equations with initial datum
u_{0}
. Then we have

\lim_{\nu\to 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u^{\nu}-\bar{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H})}=0.

Lemma 3.1

(I. Kukavica, V. Vicol, and F. Wang)
.

The weight function

We also recall the following properties for the Green function of 2D Navier-Stokes equation which can be found in Section 3 in
[
NN18
]
.
After taking the Fourier transform in the tangential
x
variable, the Navier-Stokes equations may be rewritten as

(3.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega_{\xi}-\nu\Delta_{\xi}\omega_{\xi}=N_{\xi}

\displaystyle\nu(\partial_{y}+|\xi|)\omega_{\xi}=B_{\xi},

for
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}
, where

N_{\xi}(s,y)=-(u\cdot\nabla\omega)_{\xi}(s,y),

B_{\xi}(s)=(\partial^{-1}(u\cdot\nabla\omega))_{\xi}(s)|_{y=0}=-(\partial_{y}\Delta^{-1}_{\xi}N_{\xi}(s))|_{y=0},

and

\Delta_{\xi}=-\xi^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}.

Denoting the Green’s function for this system by
G_{\xi}(t,yz)
, we may represent the solution of this system as

\displaystyle\omega_{\xi}(t,y)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{\xi}(t,y,z)\omega_{0\xi}(z)dz+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)N_{\xi}(s,z)dzds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}G_{\xi}(t-s,y,0)B_{\xi}(s)ds,

where
\omega_{0\xi}(z)
is the Fourier transform of the initial data.
The next Lemma gives an estimate of the Green’s function
G_{\xi}
of the Stokes system.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
NN18
]
)
.

The Green’s function
G_{\xi}
for the system
3.1
is given by

G_{\xi}=\tilde{H}_{\xi}+R_{\xi},

where

(3.2)

\tilde{H}_{\xi}(t,y,z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu t}}(e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4\nu t}}+e^{-\frac{(y+z)^{2}}{4\nu t}})e^{-\nu\xi^{2}t}

is the one dimensional heat kernel for the half space with homogeneous Neumann boundary condition. The residual kernel
R_{\xi}
has the property
(\partial_{y}-\partial_{z})R_{\xi}(t,y,z)=0
, meaning that it is a function of
y+z
, and it satisfies the bounds

(3.3)

|\partial_{z}^{k}R_{\xi}(t,y,z)|\lesssim b^{k+1}e^{-\theta_{0}b(y+z)}+\frac{1}{(\nu t)^{(k+1)/2}}e^{-\theta_{0}\frac{(y+z)^{2}}{\nu t}}e^{-\frac{\nu\xi^{2}t}{8}},\ k\in\mathbbm{N}_{0},

where
\theta_{0}>0
is a constant independent of
\nu
. The boundary reminder coefficient
b
in (
3.3
) is given by

b=b(\xi,\nu)=|\xi|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}.

The implicit constant in (
3.3
) depends only on
k
and
\theta_{0}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Based on (
3.3
), the residual kernel

satisfies

(3.4)

|(y\partial_{y})^{k}R_{\xi}(t,y,z)|\lesssim be^{-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}b(y+z)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu t}}e^{-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}\frac{(y+z)^{2}}{\nu t}}e^{-\frac{\nu\xi^{2}t}{8}},

for

, point-wise in

.

4.
Estimate of the X-analytic norm for

In this section, we assume

w_{t}(y)=w\left(y\left(\frac{\nu^{2}}{t\gamma^{2}}\right)^{\beta}\right)+\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}t}{\nu^{2}}\right)_{+}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
\mu
and
\tilde{\mu}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}(y\partial y)\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\mu,\nu,t}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}N_{\xi}(s,z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{\mu},\nu,s}^{\infty}}+\left\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}(z\partial z)N_{\xi}(s,z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{\mu},\nu,s}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-y\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}y}\phi\left(\frac{z}{y}\right)\partial_{z}\tilde{H}_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)N_{\xi}(s,z)dz-\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{z}{y}\right)H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)N_{\xi}(s,z)dz

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

\displaystyle\left|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}}w_{t}(y)I_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{3s}{4t}y}y\partial_{y}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}dz\right|\cdot\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}}N_{\xi}(s,z)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\mu,\nu,t}}.

(2) Estimate of

For any

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi^{\prime}(\frac{z}{y})H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\frac{1}{2}y}^{\frac{3}{4}y}e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)w_{s}(z)N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\right\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}y}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)dz\cdot\left\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}N_{\xi}(s,z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\mu,\nu,s}}.

To proceed, we derive an estimate of the following term:

(4.2)

\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\leq z\leq\frac{3}{4}y\}}(|H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)+|y\partial_{y}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)|)\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{y}L^{1}_{z}},

where

y\partial_{y}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)=\frac{y(y-z)}{2\nu(t-s)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\nu\xi^{2}(t-s)}.

Since
0\leq z\leq\frac{3}{4}y
in equation (
4.2
), we have
y\leq 4(y-z)
and

\displaystyle y\partial_{y}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{\nu(t-s)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\nu\xi^{2}(t-s)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{8\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\nu\xi^{2}(t-s)}.

Therefore, the quantity in (
4.2
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu}\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}y}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}\Big(e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4\nu(t-s)}}+e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{8\nu(t-s)}}\Big)dz

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu}\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}y}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{8\nu(t-s)}}dz.

We are now going to estimate the term
w_{t}(y)/w_{s}(z)
. Recall that

w(y)\lesssim\sqrt{\nu}e^{\frac{y}{C\sqrt{\nu}}}

for some sufficiently large
C
. Setting
4c<C
and noticing that
z<\frac{3}{4}y
, we arrive at

\displaystyle w\left(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)e^{-\frac{\left(y-z\right)^{2}}{c^{2}\nu\left(t-s\right)}}\leq

\displaystyle w\left(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{16c^{2}\nu\left(t-s\right)}}\leq w\left(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{16c^{2}\nu t}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle w\left(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)e^{-\frac{y}{4c\sqrt{\nu t}}}\lesssim\sqrt{\nu}e^{\frac{y\nu^{2\beta}}{C\sqrt{\nu}t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}}e^{-\frac{y}{4c\sqrt{\nu t}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{\nu}e^{\frac{y}{C\sqrt{\nu}\gamma^{2\beta}t^{\beta}}}e^{-\frac{y}{4c\sqrt{\nu t}}}.

Since
\beta\in(0,1/2)
and
\gamma>\gamma_{0}
, for
t\leq T
, there is

e^{\frac{y}{C\gamma^{2\beta}\sqrt{\nu}t^{\beta}}}e^{-\frac{y}{4c\sqrt{\nu t}}}\lesssim e^{\frac{y}{C\gamma_{0}^{2\beta}\sqrt{\nu}t^{\beta}}}e^{-\frac{y}{4c\sqrt{\nu t}}}\lesssim 1

for
\nu
arbitrary small.
Therefore,

(4.3)

w\left(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{c^{2}\nu(t-s)}}\lesssim\sqrt{\nu}\lesssim w\left(z\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)

holds.
We now consider two cases.

(1)

When
s>\nu^{2}/2\gamma^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}=\frac{w\left(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)+\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}t}{\nu^{2}}\right)_{+}}{w\left(z\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{s^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)+\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}s}{\nu^{2}}\right)_{+}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{w\left(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)}{w\left(z\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{s^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)}+\frac{\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}t}{\nu^{2}}\right)_{+}}{w\left(z\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{s^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)+\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}s}{\nu^{2}}\right)_{+}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{w\left(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)}{w\left(z\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{s^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)}+1.

By equation (
4.3
), it is easy to get

\frac{w\left(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)}{w\left(z\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{s^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)}\leq e^{\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{c^{2}\nu(t-s)}}.

Imposing
c>4
, the term (
4.2
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu}\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}y}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{8\nu(t-s)}}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu}\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}y}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{16\nu(t-s)}}dz.

Letting
\frac{y-z}{\sqrt{t-s}}=u
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu}\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}y}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{8\nu(t-s)}}dz\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{\frac{y}{\sqrt{t-s}}}_{\frac{1-\frac{3}{4}}{\sqrt{t-s}}y}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{16\nu}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}du

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\frac{y}{\sqrt{t-s}}e^{-\frac{1}{16\nu}(\frac{1-\frac{3}{4}}{\sqrt{t-s}})^{2}y^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\lesssim 1.

Therefore, we have

\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu}\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}y}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{8\nu(t-s)}}dz\lesssim 1.

(2)

When
s<\nu^{2}/2\gamma^{2}
, there is
w_{s}(z)\geq 1/2
and thus

\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\lesssim 1.

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu}\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}y}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{8\nu(t-s)}}dz\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu}\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}y}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{8\nu(t-s)}}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu}\frac{3}{4}\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{128\nu(t-s)}}dz\lesssim 1.

In summary, we have

\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}(I_{1}+I_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\mu,\nu,t}}\lesssim\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}N_{\xi}(s)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\mu,\nu,s}}.

(3) Estimate of
\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)I_{3}|
:

Recalling the definition of
I_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)I_{3}|

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\left|\int_{\frac{1}{2}y}^{1+\mu}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\frac{y}{z}\left(1-\phi\left(\frac{z}{y}\right)\right)H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)zw_{s}(z)dz\right|.

In this case, there is
z>\frac{1}{2}y
which implies
y/z<2
, and thus

w(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}})\lesssim w(2z\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{s^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}})\lesssim w(z\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{s^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}})

holds.
Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)I_{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\int_{\frac{1}{2}y}^{1+\mu}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}(1-\phi(\frac{z}{y}))H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}dz

\displaystyle\times\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}z\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\mu,\nu,s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\int_{\frac{1}{2}y}^{1+\mu}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}dz\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}z\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\mu,\nu,s}}.

We are done with the
I_{3}
term once we have the estimate of the quantity

(4.4)

\left\|\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{y}L^{1}_{z}}.

Note that

\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\lesssim\frac{w(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}})}{w(z\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{s^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}})}+\frac{(1-\frac{t\gamma^{2}}{\nu^{2}})_{+}}{(1-\frac{s\gamma^{2}}{\nu^{2}})_{+}}\lesssim 1

and

e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}\leq e^{\frac{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)^{2}}{2\nu(t-s)}}e^{\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}\nu(t-s)}.

Hence, if we choose
\epsilon_{0}
small enough, there is

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}y}^{1+\mu}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)^{2}}{2\nu(t-s)}}e^{\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}\nu(t-s)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\nu\xi^{2}(t-s)}dz\lesssim 1,

from where we further arrive at

\left\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}I_{3}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\mu,\nu,t}}\lesssim\left\|e^{(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}z\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\mu,\nu,s}}.

(4) Estimate of
\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)I_{4}|
:

Recalling the definition of
I_{4}
and splitting the integral interval into
[1+\mu,1+\tilde{\mu}]
and
[1+\tilde{\mu},\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)I_{4}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\Big|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)y\int_{1+\mu}^{\infty}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\Big|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)y\int_{1+\mu}^{1+\tilde{\mu}}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\Big|

\displaystyle+\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\Big|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)y\int_{1+\tilde{\mu}}^{\infty}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\Big|\int_{1+\mu}^{1+\tilde{\mu}}e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\frac{y}{z}e^{\epsilon_{0}{(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}}w_{s}(z)z\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\Big|

\displaystyle+\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\Big|\int_{1+\tilde{\mu}}^{\infty}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)ye^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\Big|.

For the first integral, noticing that
z>1+\mu>y
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\Big|\int_{1+\mu}^{1+\tilde{\mu}}e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\frac{y}{z}e^{\epsilon_{0}{(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}}w_{s}(z)z\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\int_{1+\mu}^{1+\tilde{\mu}}e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}dz\Big\|e^{\epsilon_{0}{(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}}z\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)\Big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\tilde{\mu},\nu,s}}.

While for the second integral, using
w_{t}(y)y\lesssim 1
and
z>1+\tilde{\mu}>1+\mu
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\Big|\int_{1+\tilde{\mu}}^{\infty}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)ye^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\Big|\int_{1+\tilde{\mu}}^{\infty}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\Big(\int_{1+\tilde{\mu}}^{\infty}|H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}|^{2}dz\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{1+\tilde{\mu}}^{\infty}|\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)|^{2}dz|\Big)^{1/2}.

Therefore, in order to bound the
I_{4}
term, we are left to estimate the following two quantities:

(4.6)

\Big\|\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{1+\mu\leq z\leq 1+\tilde{\mu}\}}e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{y}L^{1}_{z}},

and

(4.7)

\Big\|\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{z\geq 1+\tilde{\mu}\}}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{y}L^{2}_{z}}.

Since
z\geq 1+\mu\geq y
and
t>s
, there is
y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}}\leq z\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{s^{\beta}}
.
Hence, we have

\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\leq\frac{w\left(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)}{w\left(z\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{s^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)}+\frac{\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}t}{\nu^{2}}\right)_{+}}{\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}s}{\nu^{2}}\right)_{+}}\lesssim 1.

Following the same steps as in (
4.5
), the quantity defined in (
4.6
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\Big|\int_{1+\mu}^{1+\tilde{\mu}}e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}dz\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}\Big|\int_{1+\mu}^{1+\tilde{\mu}}e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)dz\Big|\lesssim 1.

By similar arguments, for
\epsilon_{0}
small enough, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{1+\tilde{\mu}}^{\infty}\Big|H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)e^{\epsilon_{0}(z-y)_{+}|\xi|}\Big|^{2}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{1+\tilde{\mu}}^{\infty}\Big|e^{\frac{\epsilon_{0}^{2}(z-y)^{2}}{2\nu(t-s)}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\nu(t-s)|\xi|^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\nu\xi^{2}(t-s)}\Big|^{2}dz\lesssim\int_{1+\tilde{\mu}}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{16\nu(t-s)}}\Big|^{2}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{16\nu(t-s)}}dz\frac{\tilde{\mu}-\mu}{\sqrt{\nu(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{(\tilde{\mu}-\mu)^{2}}{16\nu(t-s)}}\frac{1}{\tilde{\mu}-\mu}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{\mu}-\mu}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\Omega_{\mu}}|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)I_{4}|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|e^{\epsilon_{0}{(1+\mu-z)_{+}|\xi|}}z\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(s,z)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\tilde{\mu},\nu,s}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{\mu}-\mu}}\|\partial_{z}N_{\xi}(z)\|_{L^{2}(z\geq 1+\tilde{\mu})},

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
\mu
and
\tilde{\mu}
satisfy

with
\tilde{\mu}-\mu\geq\frac{1}{c}(\mu_{0}-\mu-\gamma\sqrt{s})
for some constant

. Then we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\Big\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}\xi^{i}(y\partial y)^{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\Big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\mu,\nu,t}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}N_{\xi}(s,z)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{\mu},\nu,s}^{\infty}}+\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}(y\partial_{y})(\partial_{x}^{i}N)_{\xi}(s,z)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{\mu},\nu,s}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}-\mu-\gamma\sqrt{s}}}\|\partial_{y}^{j}(\partial_{x}^{i}N)_{\xi}(s,z)\|_{L^{2}(z\geq 1+\tilde{\mu})},

for all

.

. The proof of case

(i,j)=(0,1)
. The result when
(i,j)=(1,0)
follows from the bound of
(i,j)=(0,0)
replacing

by
\partial_{x}N
.
∎

By summing up the above estimates in
\xi
, we have the following corollary.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that
\mu
and
\tilde{\mu}
satisfies
0<\mu<\tilde{\mu}<\mu_{0}-\gamma\sqrt{s}
with
\tilde{\mu}-\mu\geq\frac{1}{c}(\mu_{0}-\mu-\gamma\sqrt{s})
for some constant
c\geq 1
. Then we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle\Big\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}\xi^{i}(y\partial y)^{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}R_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)N_{\xi}(s,z)dz\Big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\mu,\nu,t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}\xi^{i}(y\partial_{y})^{j}N_{\xi}(s,y)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\tilde{\mu},\nu,s}}+\|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}N_{\xi}(s,y)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\tilde{\mu},\nu,s}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}-\mu-\gamma\sqrt{s}}}\|\xi^{i}\partial_{y}^{j}N_{\xi}(s,y)\|_{L^{2}(y\geq 1+\tilde{\mu})},

for all
i+j\leq 1
and
s\in[0,t]
.

Proof.

By the same argument as in the previous lemma, we only need to prove the result for the case
(i,j)=(0,1)
. In fact, we show that the quantities (
4.2
), (
4.4
), (
4.6
), and (
4.7
) share similar bounds as in Lemma
4.1
with kernel
H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)
replaced by
R_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)
. To be more specific, we are going to estimate the following term,

\displaystyle\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\left(|R_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)+|y\partial_{y}R_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)|\right)\lesssim\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\left(be^{-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}b(y+z)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu t}}e^{-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}\frac{(y+z)^{2}}{\nu t}}e^{-\frac{\nu\xi^{2}t}{8}}\right),

which requires the estimate of the quantity in (
4.2
) with
H_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)
replaced by
R_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)
. We consider two cases.

(1) When
s>\nu^{2}/2\gamma^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{w\left(y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)}{w\left(z\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{s^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\right)}+1\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\left(\sqrt{\nu}\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\leq y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\leq\nu\}}+y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{t^{\beta}\gamma^{2\beta}}>\nu\}}\right)+1

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\nu^{2\beta-1/2}\frac{y}{s^{\beta}}+1\lesssim\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}+1,

and

\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}\lesssim e^{y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{C\gamma^{2\beta}\sqrt{\nu}t^{\beta}}}\lesssim e^{y\frac{\nu^{2\beta}}{C\gamma_{0}^{2\beta}\sqrt{\nu}t^{\beta}}}.

Now (
4.2
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathbbm{1}_{0\leq 1\leq 1+\mu}\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\leq z\leq\frac{3}{4}y\}}\frac{w_{t}(y)}{w_{s}(z)}(|R_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)+|y\partial_{y}R_{\xi}(t-s,y,z)|)\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{y}L^{1}_{z}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

For

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-|\xi|z}N_{\xi}(s,z)dz

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1+\mu}e^{-|\xi|z}N_{\xi}(s,z)dz-\int_{1+\mu}^{\infty}e^{-|\xi|z}N_{\xi}(s,z)dz

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1+\mu}|e^{\epsilon_{0}(1+\mu-y)_{+}|\xi|}w_{t}(y)T_{1}(t-s,y)I_{1}|

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.3

The proof of Theorem
2.3
is the same as that of Theorem 3.2 in

KVW20a
]
with small modification. We include it here for the sake of completeness.

Proof.

Let
T>0
be as in Theorem
2.1
. In view of Kato’s criterion, we only need to show that

(6.7)

\lim_{\nu\to 0}\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{H}}|\nabla u|^{2}dxdyds=0.

Note that

\displaystyle\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{H}}|\nabla u|^{2}dxdyds=

\displaystyle\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{H}}|\omega|^{2}dxdyds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\{y\leq 1/2\}}|\omega|^{2}dxdyds+\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\{y\geq 1/2\}}|\omega|^{2}dxdyds

Precise estimates of invariant distances on strongly pseudoconvex domains
Łukasz Kosiński, Nikolai Nikolov, and Ahmed Yekta Ökten

Abstract
. Studying the behavior of real and complex geodesics we provide sharp estimates for the Kobayashi distance, the Lempert function, and the Carathéodory distance on strongly pseudoconvex domain with 𝒞 2 , α \mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha} -smooth boundary. Similar estimates are also provided for the Bergman distance on strongly pseudoconvex domains with 𝒞 3 , 1 \mathcal{C}^{3,1} -smooth boundary.

32F45

1.
Introduction

In the last decades, the metric geometric properties of the Kobayashi distance have been explored and have led to many fruitful applications. This line of inquiry started with the work of Balogh and Bonk
[
BB
]
where they proved the Gromov hyperbolicity of the Kobayashi distance on strongly pseudoconvex domains to reobtain the well-known results about extensions of biholomorphic maps between strongly pseudoconvex domains. Their results follow from the key estimate

k_{\Omega}(z,w)=g_{\Omega}(z,w)+\mathcal{O}(1),

where
k_{\Omega}
is the Kobayashi distance of the strongly pseudoconvex domain
\Omega
and
g_{\Omega}
is a function obtained from the Carnot-Carathéodory metric on
\partial\Omega
. The downside of the estimate of Balogh-Bonk is that it is precise only when the distances are large. Nevertheless, that was sufficient for their purposes as Gromov hyperbolicity is only relevant for geometry in the large.

The goal of this paper is to provide upper and lower bounds for the Kobayashi distance on strongly pseudoconvex domains with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary in terms of simple quantities coming from the Euclidean geometry of the domain. Our estimates remain sensitive even when the Kobayashi distance is small. Precisely, we show that there are universal constants
0<c<C
(depending only on a domain) such that

\log\left(1+c{A_{\Omega}(z,w)}\right)\leq k_{\Omega}(z,w)\leq\log\left(1+CA_{\Omega}(z,w)\right),

that is

cA_{\Omega}\leq e^{k_{\Omega}}-1\leq CA_{\Omega},

where

A_{\Omega}(z,w)=\dfrac{\|(z-w)_{z}\|+\|z-w\|^{2}+\|z-w\|\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)}{{\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)}}.

Above
\delta_{\Omega}(x):=\displaystyle{\inf\{\lVert x-y\rVert:y\in\partial\Omega\}}
is the distance to
\partial\Omega
, and
v_{z}
is the complex normal component of the vector
v
taken with respect to the closest point to
z
on
\partial\Omega
, denoted throughout the paper by
\pi_{\Omega}(z)
; and
v_{z}:=\langle v,\eta_{\pi(z)}\rangle
, where
\eta_{p}
denote the outer unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
at
p

and
\langle z,w\rangle:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}z_{j}\bar{w}_{j}
. If such a closest point is not unique, the complex normal may be taken with respect to any of them.

Our estimate encapsulates the one of Balogh and Bonk. Furthermore, due to the earlier comparison results in
[
NT2
]
, this estimate extends to the Lempert function and the Carathéodory distance; and to the Bergman distance as well, provided that higher boundary regularity assumptions are satisfied.

The estimates we obtain for the Kobayashi distance rely on the previous work, namely the estimate
[
NA
, Corollary 8]
for the upper bound, and the estimates
[
NT1
, Proposition 1.6, Proposition 1.7]
and
[
KN
, Proposition 2]
for the lower bound. Actually, we shall observe that they remain critical only when the points
z,w\in\Omega
tend to a
p\in\partial\Omega
”tangentially” (see Section
5
for the definition). In fact, some of our results are a natural continuation of
[
KN
]
. In particular, we prove a conjecture formulated there to extend
[
KN
, Proposition 2]
to the ”tangential” case.

Notably, we also complement some results of
[
KN
]
regarding the Euclidean behavior of real and complex geodesics of strongly pseudoconvex domains. We compare the lengths of real geodesics with their maximal boundary distance and describe Euclidean diameters of complex geodesics in terms of the boundary distance of the points which define them and of the ’angle’ the complex line defined by the two points makes with the boundary of the domain. In particular, we improve a result of Huang
[
Hua3
, Corollary 1.2]
(see also
[
KN
, Proposition 7]
for more precise estimates) about the behavior of extremal maps for the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric on strongly pseudoconvex domains.

Our approach works in the
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth case, where
\alpha>0
. The reason for this is that some methods rely heavily on the theory developed by Lempert
[
Lem1
,
Lem2
,
Lem3
]
and extended later by Huang
[
Hua1
,
Hua2
,
Hua3
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the basic concepts related to invariant distances and functions. Next, in Section
3
, we present our main results, and in Section
4
, we discuss their possible applications. Section
5
contains the main tools that will be used in the paper. We will describe here the key details of our approach.
The proofs are split into three parts. In Sections
6
and
7
we present the ones concerning the behavior of real and complex geodesics on strongly pseudoconvex domains. Building on those, in Section
8
, we establish our estimates of invariant distances.
Finally, in Section
9
, we study the basic concepts about the Euclidean geometry of strongly pseudoconvex domains and show that our estimates can be simplified in the strictly linearly convex case.

2.
Basic definitions

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
,
z,w\in\Omega
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Recall that the Carathéodory distance and the Lempert function on
\Omega
are given as

c_{\Omega}(z,w):=\tanh^{-1}\tilde{c}_{\Omega}(z,w)\>\>\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>\>\>l_{\Omega}(z,w):=\tanh^{-1}\tilde{l}_{\Omega}(z,w),

where
\Delta
is the
unit disc,
\tilde{c}_{\Omega}(z,w):=\sup\{|\alpha|:\exists f\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega,\Delta)\hbox{ with }f(z)=0,f(w)=\alpha\}
and
\tilde{l}_{\Omega}(z,w):=\inf\{|\alpha|:\exists\varphi\in\mathcal{O}(\Delta,\Omega)\hbox{ with }\varphi(0)=z,\varphi(\alpha)=w\}
. In general, the Lempert function does not satisfy the triangle inequality. The Kobayashi pseudodistance
k_{\Omega}
is defined as the largest pseudodistance
which does not exceed the Lempert function. Thus in general
c_{\Omega}(z,w)\leq k_{\Omega}(z,w)\leq l_{\Omega}(z,w)
, where
z,w\in\Omega
. The celebrated Lempert theorem asserts that all these functions coincide if
\Omega
is convex (
[
Lem1
]
).

Those notions have infinitesimal counterparts. For a point
z
and a vector
v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
, the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric is given by

\kappa_{\Omega}(z;v)=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}}\{|\alpha|:\exists\varphi\in\Omega(\Delta,\Omega)\hbox{ with }\varphi(0)=z,\alpha\varphi^{\prime}(0)=v\},

and the Kobayashi-Royden length of an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:I\rightarrow\Omega
is defined as

l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma):=\int_{I}\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma(t);\gamma^{\prime}(t))dt.

It follows from
[
Ven
, Theorem 1.2]
that
k_{\Omega}
is an integrated form of the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric, that is

k_{\Omega}(z,w)=\inf l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma)
, where the infimum is taken over all absolutely continuous curves joining
z
to
w
.

It is well-known that if a domain
\Omega
is strongly pseudoconvex, then it is complete hyperbolic, i.e.
(\Omega,k_{\Omega})
forms a complete metric space. If this is the case, it follows from the well-known Hopf-Rinow theorem
[
BH
, Proposition 3.7]
that
(\Omega,k_{\Omega})
is a geodesic space, i.e. any
z,w\in\Omega
, can be joined by a (real) Kobayashi geodesic, i.e. an isometry between an interval and the Kobayashi distance of
\Omega
. The holomorphic analogue of real geodesics are complex geodesics, which are holomorphic maps
\varphi:\Delta\rightarrow\Omega
that are isometries with respect to the Kobayashi distance.

In general, real Kobayashi geodesics need not exist, but there are substitutes. An absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:I\rightarrow\Omega
is said to be a
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-geodesic if for any
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\in I
we have

l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]})\leq\lambda k_{\Omega}(\gamma(t_{1}),\gamma(t_{2}))+\epsilon.

It is noted in
[
NÖ
]
that any
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-geodesic can be parametrized as a
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-almost geodesic in the sense of
[
BZ
, Definition 4.1]
. In particular, any
(\lambda,\epsilon)
-geodesic is a quasi-geodesic in the sense of
[
BZ
, Definition 4.7]
.

Finally recall that the Bergman distance
b_{\Omega}(z,w)
is the inner distance obtained by the Bergman metric

\beta_{\Omega}(z;v):=\dfrac{M_{\Omega}(z;v)}{K^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)},\>\>\>\>\>z\in\Omega,\>\>v\in\mathbb{C}^{N},

where

M_{\Omega}(z;v)=\sup\{\|df_{z}v\|:f\in L^{2}_{h}(\Omega),\|f\|_{\Omega}\leq 1,f(z)=0\}

and
K_{\Omega}
is the Bergman kernel of
\Omega
on the diagonal. This metric stems from a Hermitian form. We refer the reader to
[
JP
, section 12.7]
for details.

3.
Statement of the results

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary,
\alpha\in(0,1]
and let
d_{\Omega}
be either the Carathéodory distance, the Kobayashi distance or the Lempert function on
\Omega
. Then, there exist constants
0<c<C
(depending only on
\Omega
) such that

\log\left(1+c{A_{\Omega}(z,w)}\right)\leq d_{\Omega}(z,w)\leq\log\left(1+C{A_{\Omega}(z,w)}\right),\>\>\>z,w\in\Omega.

If additionally
\Omega
has
\mathcal{C}^{3,1}
-smooth boundary, then the above assertion holds also when
d_{\Omega}
is the Bergman distance on
\Omega
.

The estimates given in Theorem
1
will be proved for the Kobayashi distance first. Then, combined with the comparison results in
[
NT2
]
, we will derive the assertions for the Carathéodory distance as well as the Lempert function, and also for the Bergman distance.

We will show in Section
9
that if
\Omega
is strictly linearly convex, the term
\|z-w\|^{2}
in
A_{\Omega}(z,w)
in the estimates from Theorem
1
is superfluous.

The lower bound in Theorem
1
for the Kobayashi distance
k_{\Omega}
will be derived from Proposition
2
, stated below, in combination with the estimate provided in
[
NT1
, Theorem 1.6, Theorem 1.7]
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary. Then, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\log\left(1+\dfrac{c\|(z-w)_{z}\|}{\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)}\right)\leq k_{\Omega}(z,w),\>\>\>z,w\in\Omega.

A partial case of the above result was proved in
[
KN
, Proposition 2]
.

Let us introduce some additional notation. For functions
f,g
taking non-negative values on a given set
X
, we shall write
f\gtrsim g
or
g\lesssim f
if there exists a
c>0
such that
f(x)\geq cg(x)
for all
x\in X
. The meaning of
f\sim g
is analogous: it says that
f\gtrsim g
and
f\lesssim g
.

In order to prove Proposition
2
we will need several additional results, interesting on their own, about the behavior of real and complex geodesics of strongly pseudoconvex domains.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary. If
z,w\in\Omega
and
\gamma_{z,w}:I\rightarrow\Omega
is a real Kobayashi geodesic joining
z
to
w
, then

(1)

D^{1/2}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{z,w})\gtrsim l(\gamma_{z,w}),

where
D_{\Omega}(\gamma_{z,w}):=\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(\gamma(t)):t\in I\}
and
l(\gamma_{z,w})
denotes the Euclidean length of
\gamma(I)
. The
constants implicit in the estimate above depend only on
\Omega
.

The behavior of the geodesics of the unit ball
shows that the above result is sharp. In particular, the power
1/2
of the maximum boundary distance cannot be improved.

Let us point out that Proposition
3
is an improvement of the first part of
[
NÖ
, Corollary 12]
. However, the technique there is entirely different from the one applied in our paper. It should also be mentioned that the first part of
[
NÖ
, Corollary 12]
is valid if the boundary is just
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth, while it is not clear whether Proposition
3
remains true under lower boundary regularity assumptions.

Throughout the paper, let
d_{e}(.)
denote the Euclidean diameter of a set.

Using Proposition
3
we will also prove the following result, conjectured in
[
KN
]
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary. For any complex geodesic
\varphi:\Delta\rightarrow\Omega
parametrized so that

D_{\Omega}(\varphi):=\max_{\zeta\in\Delta}\delta_{\Omega}(\varphi(\zeta))=\delta_{\Omega}(\varphi(0))

the following uniform estimates hold:

(2)

D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\Omega}(\varphi)\sim d_{e}(\varphi(\Delta))\sim\max_{z\in\Delta}\|\varphi^{\prime}(z)\|.

Building upon this estimate, a stronger result that precisely describes the behavior of complex geodesics will be presented. For the class of domains
we are considering, it complements
[
KN
, Theorem 3]
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary. For any complex geodesic
\varphi:\Delta\rightarrow\Omega

and
z,w\in\varphi(\Delta)
we have

(3)

\dfrac{\|(z-w)_{z}\|}{\|z-w\|}+\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)+\|z-w\|\sim d_{e}(\varphi)

Note that since
\Omega
is a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary, the complex geodesics
\varphi:\Delta\rightarrow\Omega
extend
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-smoothly to the closed unit disc (see
[
Lem3
, Lemma 3.1]
). Thus, as the infinitesimal version of Theorem
5
we obtain:

Corollary 6
.

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary,
\varphi:\Delta\rightarrow\Omega
be a complex geodesic
which extends continuously to the closed disc
and
\zeta\in\overline{\Delta}
.
Then

\dfrac{\|(\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta))_{\varphi(\zeta)}\|}{\|\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta)\|}+\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(\varphi(\zeta))\sim d_{e}(\varphi),\>\>\>\>\>\zeta\in\Delta,

and

\dfrac{\|(\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta))_{\varphi(\zeta)}\|}{\|\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta)\|}\sim d_{e}(\varphi),\>\>\>\>\>\zeta\in\partial\Delta.

This result describes the angle of intersection of the boundary of the complex geodesic and the boundary of the domain. It gives a converse to the first statement of
[
KN
, Proposition 7]
, and is an improvement of
[
Hua3
, Corollary 1.2]
and
[
Hua2
, Theorem 2]
.

Remark 7
.

Switching
z,w
in the estimates in Theorem
1
one can immediately see that
A_{\Omega}(z,w)\sim A_{\Omega}(w,z)
. This
symmetry
of
A_{\Omega}
can be deduced directly, even in the case when
\Omega
has
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
-smooth boundary, thus allowing for lower regularity settings.

To see this set
\tilde{\delta}_{\Omega}
to be the signed boundary distance function, i.e.
\tilde{\delta}_{\Omega}=-\delta_{\Omega}
on
\overline{\Omega}
and
\tilde{\delta}_{\Omega}=\delta_{\Omega}
on
\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus\Omega
,
g(z)=2\overline{\partial}\tilde{\delta}_{\Omega}(z)
and let
\eta_{\pi(z)}
denote the outer unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
at
\pi_{\Omega}(z)
. It is well-known that
\eta_{\pi_{\Omega}(z)}=g(\pi_{\Omega}(z))=g(z)
. If
\Omega
has
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
-smooth boundary,
g
is Lipschitz, so

\|(z-w)_{z}-(z-w)_{w}\|=|\langle z-w,g(z)-g(w)\rangle|=\mathcal{O}(\|z-w\|^{2}).

Furthermore, assuming that
\delta_{\Omega}(w)\leq\delta_{\Omega}(z)
we have
\delta_{\Omega}(z)-\delta_{\Omega}(w)=\|(z-w)_{z}^{\mathbb{R}}\|+\mathcal{O}(\|z-w\|^{2}),
where

v_{z}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\Re(\langle v,\eta_{\pi(z)}\rangle)\eta_{\pi(z)}
is the real normal component of the vector
v
taken with respect to
\pi_{\Omega}(z)
.
Consequently, as
\|(z-w)^{\mathbb{R}}_{z}\|\leq\|(z-w)_{z}\|
, we have

\|z-w\|\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)\lesssim\|z-w\|\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)+\|z-w\|\|(z-w)_{z}^{\mathbb{R}}\|^{1/2}+\mathcal{O}(\|z-w\|^{2})\\
\lesssim\|z-w\|\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)+\|(z-w)_{z}\|+\mathcal{O}(\|z-w\|^{2}).

In the last inequality we used the trivial fact that
\|z-w\|\|(z-w)_{z}^{\mathbb{R}}\|^{1/2}\leq\|z-w\|^{2}+\|(z-w)_{z}\|
.

Combining the two estimates achieved above, one easily infers that
A_{\Omega}(z,w)\sim A_{\Omega}(w,z)
.

4.
Applications, and relationship to previous work

We will now present some applications of our results, and how some classical estimates can be
derived from them as corollaries.

We can localize the estimates given in Theorem
1
for the Kobayashi distance as well as for the Lempert function. Precisely, the following holds:

Corollary 8
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
p\in\partial\Omega
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth strongly pseudoconvex boundary point. Then, there exist constants
0<c<C
such that

\log\left(1+c{A_{\Omega}(z,w)}\right)\leq k_{\Omega}(z,w)\leq l_{\Omega}(z,w)\leq\log\left(1+C{A_{\Omega}(z,w)}\right),

for
z,w\in\Omega
near
p
.

We will provide a proof of this Corollary in Section
8
. Let us mention that localization for the Carathéodory distance seems to require global assumptions, see
[
NT2
, Theorem 1.6.]
.

Recall the estimate of Balogh and Bonk:

Theorem 9
.

[
BB
, Corollary 1.3]

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth boundary. There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\Omega
such that

g_{\Omega}(z,w)-C\leq k_{\Omega}(z,w)\leq g_{\Omega}(z,w)+C.

Here

g_{\Omega}(z,w)=\log\left(\dfrac{d^{H}_{\Omega}(\pi_{\Omega}(z),\pi_{\Omega}(w))^{2}+\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(z),\delta_{\Omega}(w)\}}{\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)}\right),

where
d^{H}_{\Omega}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the distance obtained from the Carnot-Carathéodory metric on
\partial\Omega
.

Proposition 10
.

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary. Then, the estimates in Theorem
9
follow from Theorem
1
.

Proof of Proposition
10
.

The classical Box-Ball estimate recalled in
[
BB
, Proposition 3.1]
states that

(4)

d^{H}_{\Omega}(\pi(z),\pi(w))^{2}\sim{\|(\pi_{\Omega}(z)-\pi_{\Omega}(w))_{\pi_{\Omega}(z)}\|+\|\pi_{\Omega}(z)-\pi_{\Omega}(w)\|^{2}}.

Thus,

d^{H}_{\Omega}(\pi(z),\pi(w))^{2}+\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(z),\delta_{\Omega}(w)\}\sim\\
\|(\pi_{\Omega}(z)-\pi_{\Omega}(w))_{\pi_{\Omega}(z)}\|+\|\pi_{\Omega}(z)-\pi_{\Omega}(w)\|^{2}+\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(z),\delta_{\Omega}(w)\}.

Theorem
1
implies that

(5)

k_{\Omega}(z,w)=\log\left(1+{A_{\Omega}(z,w)}\right)+\mathcal{O}(1).

We claim that

(6)

(A_{\Omega}(z,w)+1)\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)\sim\\
\|(\pi_{\Omega}(z)-\pi_{\Omega}(w))_{\pi_{\Omega}(z)}\|+\|\pi_{\Omega}(z)-\pi_{\Omega}(w)\|^{2}+\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(z),\delta_{\Omega}(w)\}.

Without loss of generality assume that
\delta_{\Omega}(w)\leq\delta_{\Omega}(z)
. Observe that

\|\pi(z)-\pi(w)\|\leq\|z-w\|+\|\pi(z)-z\|+\|\pi(w)-w\|
, whence
|\|\pi(z)-\pi(w)\|-\|z-w\||\leq 2\delta_{\Omega}(z)
. This implies that

\|(\pi_{\Omega}(z)-\pi_{\Omega}(w))_{\pi_{\Omega}(z)}\|+\|\pi_{\Omega}(z)-\pi_{\Omega}(w)\|^{2}+\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(z),\delta_{\Omega}(w)\}\sim\\
\|(z-w)_{z}\|+\|z-w\|^{2}+\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(z),\delta_{\Omega}(w)\}.

It remains to show that

(7)

\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(z),\delta_{\Omega}(w)\}\lesssim(A_{\Omega}(z,w)+1)\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)

and

(8)

\|z-w\|\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)+\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)\lesssim\|(z-w)_{z}\|+\|z-w\|^{2}+\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(z),\delta_{\Omega}(w)\}.

To see (
7
) note that
\max\{\delta_{\Omega}(z),\delta_{\Omega}(w)\}-\min\{\delta_{\Omega}(z),\delta_{\Omega}(w)\}=\delta_{\Omega}(z)-\delta_{\Omega}(w)=\|(z-w)_{z}^{\mathbb{R}}\|+\mathcal{O}(\|z-w\|^{2})
.
As
\|(z-w)_{z}^{\mathbb{R}}\|\leq\|(z-w)_{z}\|
we have

\delta_{\Omega}(z)\leq\delta_{\Omega}(w)+\|(z-w)_{z}\|+C\|z-w\|^{2}\leq\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)+\|(z-w)_{z}\|+C\|z-w\|^{2},

and (
7
) follows.

To prove (
8
) it is enough to observe that
2\|z-w\|\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)\leq\delta_{\Omega}(z)+\|z-w\|^{2}
and
\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)\leq\delta_{\Omega}(z)
. Thus, combining (
7
) and (
8
) we see that (
6
) holds.

Proposition
10
now follows directly from (
5
).∎

One can see that the estimate (
6
) has a real analogue replacing the complex normal direction with the real normal direction.

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain and recall the well-known estimate
[
Ma
]
for the Kobayashi-Royden metric:

(9)

\frac{\|v_{z}\|}{\delta_{\Omega}(z)}+\frac{\|v\|}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(z)}}\sim\kappa_{\Omega}(z;v),\>\>\>\>\>z\in\Omega,\>\>v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}.

Recall that by
[
Pan
, Proposition 3.1]
the Kobayashi-Royden metric is the derivative of the Lempert function.

As
x\sim\log(1+x)
if
x\geq 0
is bounded above, the estimate of the Lempert function given in Theorem
1
implies that for fixed
z\in\Omega
and
w\in\Omega
sufficiently close to
z
we have

l_{\Omega}(z,w)\sim{A_{\Omega}(z,w)}.

Hence we conclude that if
\Omega
has
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary, we can get the asymptotic equality (
9
) as a consequence of Theorem
1
in the
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth case by fixing
z\in\Omega
and letting
w\in\Omega
tend to
z
in the direction of
v
.

By using the estimates we provide in Theorem
1
for the Bergman distance instead of the Lempert function we recover (see
[
JP
, Theorem 19.4.6]
) the same estimates for the Bergman metric on strongly pseudoconvex domains with
\mathcal{C}^{3,1}
-smooth boundary:

\frac{\|v_{z}\|}{\delta_{\Omega}(z)}+\frac{\|v\|}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(z)}}\sim\beta_{\Omega}(z;v),\>\>\>\>\>z\in\Omega,\>\>v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}.

5.
Preliminaries

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary. Some of the estimates considered in the paper are critical when two points
z,w\in\Omega
approach a point
p\in\partial\Omega

complex tangentially
, which throughout the paper means that they converge to a point
p\in\partial\Omega
with
\|(z-w)_{p}\|/\|z-w\|
tending to zero (if this is not the case we shall say that they approach the point
complex transversally
). Here,
v_{p}
is the complex normal component of the vector
v
taken with respect to
p\in\partial\Omega
.

To study these cases we will apply a scaling method together with the Fridman-Ma transformations of the domain, as connected together in
[
KNT
]
. What is also novel in our approach is the very careful choice of points on geodesics along which we are going to scale as well as quantities that we are going to estimate. Thanks to them we will be able to carry out precise estimates.

For the convenience of the reader, we will briefly recall how to transform a domain and outline the scaling construction we are going to use.

5.1.
Transformation of a domain

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary.
It is well-known that if
x
is sufficiently close to the boundary, then there exists a unique point,
p:=\pi_{\Omega}(x)\in\partial\Omega
with
\|p-x\|=\delta_{\Omega}(x)
.

We will first explain how to modify the domain
\Omega
in an appropriate form. The idea is based on the Fridman-Ma
[
FM
]
transformation of the domain (see also
[
KNT
, Section 2]
for more details on this construction).

Given
p\in\partial\Omega
there exist a biholomorphic mapping
F_{p}:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{C}^{N}
that sends
p
to
e_{1}=(1,\ldots,0)
and such that
F_{p}(\Omega)
is contained in a ball tangent to
\mathbb{B}^{N}
, and near
e_{1}
it has a defining function of the form

(10)

\rho(z)=-1+|z|^{2}+o(|z-e_{1}|^{2+\alpha}),\quad\text{as }z\to e_{1}.

It follows from
[
KNT
, Lemma 2]
(see also the proof of
[
DGZ
, Theorem 4.1]
) that for any
z\in\Omega
that is sufficiently close to
p
there exists
q\in\partial\Omega
, close to
p
, so that
F_{q}(z)\in(0,1)\times\{0\}^{N-1}.

Let us denote
F=F_{q}
. Due to the
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smoothness of the boundary,
\|F(z)-F(w)\|\sim\|z-w\|
and
\delta_{\Omega}(z)\sim\delta_{F(\Omega)}(F(z))
for
z,w\in\Omega
.

Moreover, we will later see in Lemma
20
that under these transformations we have

(11)

\displaystyle\|(F(z)-F(w))_{F(z)}\|+\|F(z)

\displaystyle-F(w)\|^{2}+\|F(z)-F(w)\|\delta^{1/2}_{F(\Omega)}(F(z))\sim

\displaystyle\|(z-w)_{z}\|+\|z

\displaystyle-w\|^{2}+\|z-w\|\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)

for
z,w\in\Omega
close to
p
and also that

(12)

\|(F(z)-F(w))_{F(z)}\|\lesssim\|(z-w)_{z}\|+\|z-w\|^{2}+\|z-w\|\delta_{\Omega}(z).

Note that as
\Omega
has
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-smooth boundary,
F_{p}
depends
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
-continuously on
p
. In particular, this shows that the constants in the estimates (
11
) and (
12
) change locally uniformly with respect to the base point.

All of this discussion can be summarized in the following lemma.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary and
z\in\overline{\Omega}
be sufficiently close to
\partial\Omega
. Then, there exists
F\in\mathcal{O}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{C}^{N})
which is a biholomorphism onto its image, sending
z
to
(1-s,0,...,0)
, which satisfies (
11
) and (
12
) with constants that are uniform with respect to
z
, and such that a defining function of
F(\Omega)
near
e_{1}
is of the form (
10
).

z=(z_{1},z^{\prime})

m_{t}(z_{1})=\dfrac{z_{1}+t}{1+tz_{1}}

5.3.
Complex geodesics

The behavior of complex geodesics in strongly pseudoconvex domains is well understood.

Lemma 14
.

[
KNT
, Lemma 3]

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary.

(1)

Let
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, there exists a small enough
\epsilon>0
such that if
z,w\in\Omega
are points satisfying
\|z-w\|\leq\epsilon
,
\delta_{\Omega}(z)\leq\epsilon
,
\delta_{\Omega}(w)\leq\epsilon
,
\frac{\|(z-w)_{z}\|}{\|z-w\|}\leq\epsilon
then
z,w
lie on the image of a complex geodesic
\varphi_{z,w}
whose Euclidean diameter is smaller than
\epsilon^{\prime}
.

(2)

For
\epsilon>0
small enough, if
z,w\in\Omega
are points satisfying
\|z-w\|\leq\epsilon
,
\delta_{\Omega}(z)\leq\epsilon
,
\delta_{\Omega}(w)\leq\epsilon
,
\frac{\|(z-w)_{z}\|}{\|z-w\|}\leq\epsilon
, then there exists a unique real geodesic joining
z
to
w
. Furthermore, that real geodesic is induced from a complex one.

(3)

There is a constant
C>0
depending on
\Omega
such that for any complex geodesic
\varphi:\Delta\rightarrow\Omega
with the Euclidean diameter less than
\epsilon^{\prime}
, we have
\frac{\|(x-y)_{x}\|}{\|x-y\|}\leq C\epsilon^{\prime}
for any
x\neq y\in\varphi(\Delta)
.

The first part of the result above is essentially due to
[
BFW
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
BST
, Lemma 4.5]
for an infinitesimal version of this result), the second part is given in
[
KNT
, Corollary 4]
, and the third part follows from
[
KN
, Theorem 3]
.

Remark 15
.

It follows from Lemma
14
that if
z
and
w
are close to a boundary point
p
and
z-w
is close to a complex tangent at
p
, then they are contained in a complex geodesic, say
\varphi
, whose diameter must be small. In particular, after the transformations that were outlined in Lemma
11
, we may assume that the range of
\varphi
is contained in
\Omega\cap U
, where
U
is a sufficiently small neighborhood of
p
such that
\Omega\cap U
is strictly convex. This implies that
\varphi
is also complex geodesics in
\Omega\cap U
and that
k_{\Omega}(z,w)=k_{\Omega\cap U}(z,w)
. In particular, we can confine our study to the case of strictly convex domains. Moreover, according to
[
KN
, Remark 2]
, for a suitably chosen
t\in(0,1)
an analytic disc
A^{-1}_{t}(\varphi)
is
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-close to a complex geodesic of the ball that is contained in
\{0\}\times\mathbb{C}^{N-1}
. This reasoning allows us to effectively understand the behavior of
\varphi
.

Lemma 16
.

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary and
\varphi:\Delta\rightarrow\Omega
be a complex geodesic with a sufficiently small diameter. Take
x\in\varphi(\Delta)
such that
\delta_{\Omega}(x)=\max_{\zeta\in\Delta}\delta_{\Omega}(\varphi(\zeta))=\|x-p\|
, where
p=\pi_{\Omega}(x)
and reparametrize the complex geodesic so that
\varphi(0)=x
. Then
\varphi^{\prime}(0)\in T_{p}^{\mathbb{C}}(\partial\Omega)
.

Proof.

By a rotation and translation we may assume that
x=(-s,0,\ldots,0)
and
\pi(x):=\pi_{\Omega}(x)=(0,\ldots,0)
. It is well-known that the map
\pi_{\Omega}
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth. Furthermore we have
\pi(\varphi(0))=(0,\ldots,0)
and
\operatorname{Re}(\pi_{1}(z))=\mathcal{O}(\|z\|^{2})
, where
\pi_{1}
denotes the first component of
\pi
.

To prove the assertion we need to show that
\varphi^{\prime}(0)\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{C}^{N-1}.
We proceed by a contradiction. Let
\varphi^{\prime}(0):=\eta=(\eta_{1},...,\eta_{N})
with
\eta_{1}\neq 0
. Then
\varphi(\lambda)=(-s,0,...,0)+\lambda\eta+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2})
. Composing with a rotation of the disc, we may assume that
\eta_{1}<0
. Then for
t\in\Delta
with
t>0
we see that

\delta_{\Omega}(\varphi(t))=\|\varphi(t)-\pi(\varphi(t))\|\geq\operatorname{Re}(\varphi(t)_{1})-\operatorname{Re}(\pi(\varphi(t))_{1})\geq s-\eta_{1}t+\mathcal{O}(t^{2})>s=\delta_{\Omega}(x)

which contradicts the extremality of
x\in\varphi(\Delta)
.
∎

6.
Proofs of Propositions 3 and 4

Proof of Proposition
3
.

The key role in our approach is played by a Gehring-Hayman type inequality for strongly pseudoconvex domains:

Lemma 17
.

[
KNT
, Theorem 1]

Let
\Omega
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary. Then, for any
z,w\in\Omega
and any Kobayashi geodesic
\gamma_{z,w}:I\to\Omega
joining
z
to
w
, with constants depending only on
\Omega
we have the following length estimate

(14)

l(\gamma_{z,w})\sim\|z-w\|.

Therefore, in order to prove Proposition
3
it is enough to show that there exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
z,w\in\Omega
and a Kobayashi geodesic
\gamma_{z,w}
joining
z
to
w
, the inequality

(15)

D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\Omega}(\gamma_{z,w})\geq c\|z-w\|

holds.

It is well known that any strongly pseudoconvex domain satisfies visibility property with respect to Kobayashi geodesics. Hence (
15
) requires a proof only if points
z
and
w
are close to the same point in the boundary. Take sequences
z_{n},w_{n}
converging to
p\in\partial\Omega
. We need to show that (
15
) holds for
z_{n}
,
w_{n}
if
n
is sufficiently big.

We shall split the proof of Proposition
3
into two different cases, depending on whether
z_{n},w_{n}
approach
p
complex transversally or tangentially.

Case I.

z_{n},w_{n}
approach
p\in\partial\Omega
complex transversally, i.e. there exists
\alpha>0
such that

(16)

\dfrac{\|(z_{n}-w_{n})_{p}\|}{\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|}\geq\alpha.

We shall construct particular quasi-geodesics joining
z_{n}
with
w_{n}
. Recall here that quasi-geodesics need not be continuous.

Let
\hat{z}_{n}=\pi_{\Omega}(z_{n}),\hat{w}_{n}=\pi_{\Omega}(w_{n})
be the unique points on
\partial\Omega
that are closest to
z_{n},w_{n}
, respectively (look at tails of sequences, if necessary). Let
\eta_{z_{n}},\eta_{w_{n}}
denote the inner unit normal vectors to
\partial\Omega
at
\hat{z}_{n},\hat{w}_{n}
respectively. Let, moreover,
\sigma_{z_{n}},\sigma_{w_{n}}
denote the curves defined by
\sigma_{z_{n}}(t)=z_{n}+t\eta_{z_{n}},

\sigma_{w_{n}}(t)=w_{n}+t\eta_{w_{n}}
, where
0\leq t\leq t_{n}:=\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|^{2}
.

It follows from
[
Zim
, Lemma 3.2]
that
\sigma_{z_{n}}
and
\sigma_{w_{n}}
are
(2,\epsilon)
-geodesics for some
\epsilon>0
. Below, for the sake of completeness, we shall provide a sketch of the argument explaining this. Of course, it is sufficient to do so for
\sigma_{z_{n}}
.

As
\Omega
is strongly pseudoconvex

(17)

k_{\Omega}(z,w)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\left|\log\left(\dfrac{\delta_{\Omega}(z)}{\delta_{\Omega}(w)}\right)\right|-c,\>\>\>\>\>z,w\in\Omega,

for some uniform
c>0
(see also
[
NÖ
, Theorem 20]
for an extension of this estimate).
Moreover,

(18)

\kappa_{\Omega}(z;v)\leq\delta^{-1}_{\Omega}(z;v),\>\>\>\>\>z\in\Omega,\>v\in\mathbb{C}^{N},\>v\neq 0.

It then follows from (
17
) and (
18
) that

l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\sigma_{z_{n}}|_{[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]})=\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\kappa_{\Omega}(\sigma_{z_{n}}(t);\sigma^{\prime}_{z_{n}}(t))dt=\log\left(\dfrac{\delta_{\Omega}(\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{2}))}{\delta_{\Omega}(\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{1}))}\right)\leq 2k_{\Omega}(\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{1}),\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{2}))+2c

so
\sigma_{z_{n}}
is actually a
(2,2c)
-geodesic.

We will now show that
\sigma_{n}:=\sigma_{z_{n}}\cup\sigma_{w_{n}}
is a quasi-geodesic. Here we parametrize the curves so that
\sigma_{n}
is defined on
I_{n}:=[0,t_{n}]\cup[t_{n},2t_{n}]
, begins at
z_{n}
and ends at
w_{n}
.

Let us consider two points on the curve
\sigma_{n}
that are of the form
\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{1}),\sigma_{w_{n}}(\tau_{2})
. Integrating as above we get

(19)

l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\sigma_{n}|_{[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]})=l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\sigma_{z_{n}}|_{[\tau_{1},t_{n}]})+l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\sigma_{w_{n}}|_{[t_{n},\tau_{2}]})\leq\\
\log\left(\dfrac{\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|^{2}}{\delta_{\Omega}(\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{1}))}\right)+\log\left(\dfrac{\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|^{2}}{\delta_{\Omega}(\sigma_{w_{n}}(\tau_{2}))}\right).

It follows from the conditions put on the curves, the definition of
t_{n}
, and (
16
), that

\frac{\|(\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{1})-\sigma_{w_{n}}(\tau_{2}))_{p}\|}{\|\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{1})-\sigma_{w_{n}}(\tau_{2})\|}\geq\alpha^{\prime}

for some uniform
\alpha^{\prime}>0
. By
[
KN
, Proposition 2]
we deduce that

(20)

k_{\Omega}(\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{1}),\sigma_{w_{n}}(\tau_{2}))\geq\log\left(1+\dfrac{c\|(\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{1})-\sigma_{w_{n}}(\tau_{2}))_{p}\|}{\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{1}))\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(\sigma_{w_{n}}(\tau_{2}))}\right)\geq\\
\log\left(1+\dfrac{c^{\prime}\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|}{\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{1}))\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(\sigma_{w_{n}}(\tau_{2}))}\right).

The last inequality above follows from

\|(\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{1})-\sigma_{w_{n}}(\tau_{2}))_{p}\|\gtrsim\|(z_{n}-w_{n})_{p}\|\gtrsim\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|.

Then (
19
) in combination with (
20
) provides us with
\epsilon>0
such that

l^{\kappa}_{\Omega}(\sigma_{n}|_{[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]})\leq 2k_{\Omega}(\sigma_{z_{n}}(\tau_{1}),\sigma_{w_{n}}(\tau_{2})))+\epsilon.

This shows that
\sigma_{n}
are
(2,\epsilon^{\prime})
-geodesics, where
\epsilon^{\prime}=\max\{\epsilon,2c\}
. Note also that
D_{\Omega}(\sigma_{n})\geq\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|^{2}.

As
\Omega
is strongly pseudoconvex, it is Gromov hyperbolic with respect to the Kobayashi distance (see
[
BB
, Theorem 1.4]
for the original argument or combine
[
Zim
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
BGNT
, Theorem 1.2]
for an alternate approach). By the geodesic stability lemma
[
BH
, Theorem 1.7, page 401]
one can deduce that the curves
\sigma_{n}
and geodesics
\gamma_{z_{n},w_{n}}
are close to each other in the Hausdorff topology. That is to say, it holds for all
n
that there exists a constant
H>0
with

(21)

\max\{k_{\Omega}(\sigma_{n}(t),\gamma_{z_{n},w_{n}}(I^{\prime}_{n})),k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{z_{n},w_{n}}(t^{\prime}),\sigma_{n}(I_{n})):t\in I_{n},t^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}_{n}\}\leq H.

Above
I_{n}^{\prime}
denotes the interval
\gamma_{z_{n},w_{n}}
as defined on and
k_{\Omega}(z,A):=\inf k_{\Omega}(z,a)
, where the infimum is taken over all
a\in A
.
Let
x_{n}
be a point in
\sigma_{n}(I_{n})
such that
\delta_{\Omega}(x_{n}):=D_{\Omega}(\sigma_{n})
. It follows from (
21
) that there exists
y_{n}\in\gamma_{z_{n},w_{n}}(I^{\prime}_{n})
such that

. By (

\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{\delta_{\Omega}(x_{n})}{\delta_{\Omega}(y_{n})}\right)\leq H+c.

Consequently

D_{\Omega}(\gamma_{z_{n},w_{n}})\geq\delta_{\Omega}(y_{n})\geq C\delta_{\Omega}(x_{n})\geq C^{\prime}\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|^{2},

where the constant
C^{\prime}>0
is uniform. This shows that (
15
) holds for the sequences

. As those were arbitrary sequences, by a compactness argument we are done.

Case II.
Points
z_{n},w_{n}
tend to

tangentially, i.e.

\dfrac{\|(z_{n}-w_{n})_{p}\|}{\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|}\rightarrow 0.

We start the proof of this case by noting that the first part of the Lemma

implies that provided that

is large enough both

are contained in a complex geodesic tending to

\varphi_{n}
so that
\varphi_{n}(0)=x_{n}
. Clearly,

tends to zero since the diameters of
\varphi_{n}
do. Moreover, the derivatives
\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(0)

|(\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(0))_{1}|\lesssim s_{n}\|\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(0)\|.

The following claim is about the complex geodesics

and will be also used in the sequel:

Claim 18
.

Passing to a subsequence, there exists
t_{n}\in(0,1)
with
1-t_{n}=s_{n}+o(s_{n})
such that the complex geodesics

tend in

-topology to a complex geodesic of the unit ball, contained in

Proof of Claim
18
.

Choose
t_{n}\in[0,1-s_{n}]
to be the smallest number so that the range
A^{-1}_{t_{n}}\circ\varphi_{n}(\overline{\Delta})
intersects
\{\operatorname{Re}z_{1}=0\}
and set

\tilde{\varphi}_{n}:=A^{-1}_{t_{n}}\circ\varphi_{n}.

As the diameters of
\varphi_{n}
tend to zero, the sequence
(t_{n})
converges to

. Then, by Lemma

and
[

, Remark 2]
, a subsequence of
(\tilde{\varphi}_{n})
tends to a complex geodesic of the ball. Recall that (see for instance
[

) images of complex geodesics of the unit ball

are the intersection of
\mathbb{B}^{N}
with complex affine lines. The estimate in (

) together with elementary calculations shows that for any

one has

{\|(A^{-1}_{t}\circ\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(0))_{1}\|}/{\|(A^{-1}_{t}\circ\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(0))^{\prime}\|}\lesssim s^{1/2}_{n}.
In particular,
[(A^{-1}_{t_{n}}\circ\varphi_{n})^{\prime}(0)]

to a point of the form

.
The range of
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}
intersects

, so
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}

∎

Let us denote the limit of geodesics that appears in Claim

\psi
.

Remark 19
.

Observe that Remark
13
, the third part of Lemma

, and the

-convergence of

to

\|\tilde{z}_{n}-\tilde{w}_{n}\|\sim\|\tilde{z}_{n}^{\prime}-\tilde{w}_{n}^{\prime}\|\sim\dfrac{\|z_{n}^{\prime}-w_{n}^{\prime}\|}{(1-t^{2}_{n})^{1/2}}\sim\dfrac{\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|}{(1-t^{2}_{n})^{1/2}}.

We are coming back to the proof of Case 2. The second part of Lemma
14
implies that there exists a unique real geodesic
\gamma_{n}:I_{n}\rightarrow\Omega
joining

to
w_{n}
and its image is contained in the image of
\varphi_{n}
. Set
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}=A^{-1}_{t_{n}}\circ\gamma_{n}
.

Recall that the real geodesics of the unit disc
\Delta
are arcs of circles perpendicular to the unit circle, thus the maximum boundary distance of a real geodesic
\gamma
on
\psi
is larger than the Euclidean distance of its endpoints up to a multiplicative constant. In particular, when the complex geodesic intersects a fixed compact set of
\mathbb{B}^{N}
, for any real geodesic
\gamma
joining

with

there exists

lying in the range of

Then, thanks to Claim

,
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-convergence of
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}
to
\psi
implies that for large

there exists
\tilde{u}_{n}\in\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(I_{n})
such that

\delta_{\Omega_{n}}(u_{n})\gtrsim\|\tilde{z}_{n}-\tilde{w}_{n}\|
. In particular, for any
\tilde{q}\in\partial\Omega_{n}
one has

(23)

\|\tilde{u}_{n}-\tilde{q}\|\gtrsim\|\tilde{z}_{n}-\tilde{w}_{n}\|.

Let
q_{n}:=A_{t_{n}}(\tilde{q}_{n})
be the closest point on
\partial\Omega
to
u_{n}:=A_{t_{n}}(\tilde{u}_{n})
.

We claim that

(24)

D_{\Omega}(\gamma_{n})\gtrsim(1-t_{n}^{2})\|\tilde{z}_{n}-\tilde{w}_{n}\|.

To see this we distinguish two cases (passing to a subsequence, if necessary). The first one is that
\|\tilde{u}_{n}-\tilde{q}_{n}\|\sim\|\tilde{u}_{n}^{\prime}-\tilde{q}_{n}^{\prime}\|\gtrsim\|\tilde{u}_{n}^{1}-\tilde{q}_{n}^{1}\|
. Here and in the sequel we use superscripts to denote the coordinates, while subscripts are left for indices. It follows from (

) and Remark

Proof of Theorem

.

Within this part we shall use the scaling argument, as well. However, we need to deal with the intricate term

which makes the proof more subtle. We will first show that the estimates we want to achieve are preserved under the transformations we give in Section

Lemma 20
.

Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be bounded domains with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth boundaries and
f:\overline{\Omega}_{1}\rightarrow\overline{\Omega}_{2}
be a biholomorphism. For
z,w\in\Omega_{1}
one has

(28)

\|(f(z)-f(w))_{f(z)}\|\lesssim\|(z-w)_{z}\|+\|z-w\|^{2}+\delta_{\Omega_{1}}(z)\|z-w\|

and

(29)

\|(z-w)_{z}\|+\|z-w\|^{2}+\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega_{1}}(z)\|z-w\|\sim\\
\|(f(z)-f(w))_{f(z)}\|+\|f(z)-f(w)\|^{2}+\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega_{2}}(f(z))\|f(z)-f(w)\|.

Proof of Lemma
20
.

Let us prove (
28
). By isometric transformations we may assume that
z=(-s,0,\ldots,0)
and
\pi_{\Omega}(z)=(0,\ldots,0)
. Post-composing
with
(df|_{0})^{-1}
, we may assume
df|_{0}
is identity, and
we have

f(z)=(z_{1}+P(z),z^{\prime}+Q(z))+\vec{o}(\|z\|^{2}),

where
P,Q
are quadratic polynomials and
\vec{o}
means that the quantity we consider is a vector in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. We may write
f(z)-f(w)=(z_{1}-w_{1}+P(z)-P(w),w^{\prime}+Q(z)-Q(w))+\vec{o}(|z_{1}-w_{1}|+\|w^{\prime}\|^{2}).
Since
|z_{1}-w_{1}|=\|(z-w)_{z}\|
and
\|w^{\prime}\|^{2}\leq\|z-w\|^{2}
, we are able to estimate the
\vec{o}(|z_{1}-w_{1}|+\|w^{\prime}\|^{2})
term.

Let
\tilde{\delta}_{\Omega}
be the signed boundary distance function and
g_{\Omega}(z):=2\overline{\partial}\tilde{\delta}_{\Omega}(z)
. Recall that
the unit normal to
\partial\Omega
at
\pi_{\Omega}(z)
is given by
g_{\Omega}(z)=g_{\Omega}(\pi_{\Omega}(z))
.

Note that
f(z)=(-s+\gamma_{1}s^{2},\gamma^{\prime}s^{2})
, whence

\|g_{\Omega_{2}}(f(z))-g_{\Omega_{2}}(0)\|=\|g_{\Omega_{2}}(f(z))-(1,0,...,0)\|\lesssim s=\delta_{\Omega_{1}}(z).

Then, by Cauchy-Schwarz and triangle inequalities, we can write

\|(f(z)-f(w))_{f(z)}\|=\langle f(z)-f(w),(1,0,...,0)+\vec{\mathcal{O}}(\delta_{\Omega_{1}}(z))\rangle=\\
|z_{1}-w_{1}|+\|P(z)-P(w)\|+(\|w^{\prime}\|+\|Q(z)-Q(w)\|)\mathcal{O}(\delta_{\Omega_{1}}(z)).

The first term above is just
\|(z-w)_{z}\|
. To bound
\|P(z)-P(w)\|
write
\|P(z)-P(w)\|\leq\|P(z_{1},0,...,0)-P(w_{1},0,...,0)\|+\|P(w_{1},0,...,0)-P(w)\|
. Observe that
\|P(z_{1},0,...,0)-P(w_{1},0,...,0)\|\lesssim|z_{1}^{2}-w_{1}^{2}|\lesssim|z_{1}-w_{1}|=\|(z-w)_{z}\|.
To deal with
\|P(w_{1},0,...,0)-P(w)\|
write
P(w_{1},0,...,0)-P(w)=w_{1}L(w^{\prime})+S(w^{\prime})
, where
L
is linear and
S
is a quadratic polynomial. Clearly,
|S(w^{\prime})|\lesssim\|w^{\prime}\|^{2}\leq\|z-w\|^{2}
. Now, observe that

|w_{1}P(w^{\prime})|\leq|z_{1}||P(w^{\prime})|+|z_{1}-w_{1}||P(w^{\prime})|\lesssim\delta_{\Omega_{1}}(z)\|w^{\prime}\|+|z_{1}-w_{1}|\lesssim\delta_{\Omega_{1}}(z)\|z-w\|+\|(z-w)_{z}\|.

Putting all of these estimates together we infer that

|P(z)-P(w)|\leq|P(z_{1},0,...,0)-P(w_{1},0,...,0)|+|P(w_{1},0,...,0)-P(w)|\lesssim\\
\delta_{\Omega_{1}}(z)\|z-w\|+\|(z-w)_{z}\|+\|z-w\|^{2}.

Similar estimates as above show that
(\|Q(z)-Q(w)\|+\|w^{\prime}\|)\delta_{\Omega_{1}}(z)\lesssim\delta_{\Omega_{1}}(z)\|z-w\|.
Consequently,

\|(f(z)-f(w))_{f(z)}\|\lesssim|z_{1}-w_{1}|+\|P(z)-P(w)\|+(\|w^{\prime}\|+\|Q(z)-Q(w)\|)\delta_{\Omega_{1}}(z)\lesssim\\
\delta_{\Omega_{1}}(z)\|z-w\|+\|(z-w)_{z}\|+\|z-w\|^{2}.

To show (
29
) note that the distances are preserved, up to multiplicative constants, by
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth maps. Therefore is enough to show that the term
\|(f(z)-f(w))_{f(z)}\|
can be estimated by
\|(z-w)_{z}\|+\|z-w\|^{2}+\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega_{1}}(z)\|z-w\|
(to get the opposite estimate it is enough to apply the above to the inverse of
f
). This follows immediately from (
28
).

∎

5
. The lower bound for the diameter follows from
[
KN
, Theorem 3]
and Proposition
4
.

We shall prove the upper one. We shall actually give a stronger estimate

(30)

d_{e}(\varphi)\lesssim\|(z-w)_{z}\|/\|z-w\|+\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z).

Parametrize
\varphi
so that
\delta_{\Omega}(\varphi(0))=\max_{\zeta\in\Delta}\delta_{\Omega}(\varphi(\zeta))
and let
z\in\varphi(\Delta)
. We will show that the uniform estimate

\dfrac{\delta_{\Omega}(x)}{\delta_{\Omega}(z)}\sim\dfrac{1}{\delta_{\Delta}(\varphi^{-1}(z))}

Thus one side of (

\dfrac{1}{\delta_{\Delta}(\varphi^{-1}(z))}\sim\dfrac{1}{1-|\varphi^{-1}(z)|^{2}}=\kappa_{\Delta}(\varphi^{-1}(z);1)=\\

Set

and for each

apply the transformation given in Lemma
11
. Then
x_{n}=(1-s_{n},0,...,0)
, where

tends to zero. Claim
18
then shows that passing to a subsequence, if necessary, we can find automorphisms of the ball

, where

are uniformly
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}

. By Proposition
4
we have
1-t_{n}\sim s_{n}\sim d^{2}_{e}(\varphi_{n}).

Rotations in
\{0\}\times\mathbb{C}^{N-1}
do not affect the estimates we want to achieve, so taking a proper one we may assume that
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}
is uniformly
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-close to the geodesic
\psi(\zeta)=(0,\zeta,0,...,0)
,
\zeta\in\Delta
.

Let us write
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}(\zeta)=(0,\zeta,0^{\prime})+(e_{n}^{1}(\zeta),e^{2}_{n}(\zeta),e^{3}_{n}(\zeta))
, where
0^{\prime},e^{3}_{n}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{C}^{N-2}
. Let
\omega_{n}=\varphi^{-1}_{n}(w_{n})
. As
\varphi_{n}=A_{t_{n}}\circ\tilde{\varphi}_{n}
, explicit computations give the formulas:

(34)

\displaystyle z_{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{e^{1}_{n}(\zeta_{n})+t_{n}}{1+t_{n}e^{1}_{n}(\zeta_{n})},\dfrac{(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}(\zeta_{n}+e^{2}_{n}(\zeta_{n}))}{1+t_{n}e^{1}_{n}(\zeta_{n})},\dfrac{(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}e^{3}_{n}(\zeta_{n})}{1+t_{n}e^{1}_{n}(\zeta_{n})}\right),

\displaystyle w_{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{e^{1}_{n}(\omega_{n})+t_{n}}{1+t_{n}e^{1}_{n}(\omega_{n})},\dfrac{(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}(\omega_{n}+e^{2}_{n}(\omega_{n}))}{1+t_{n}e^{1}_{n}(\omega_{n})},\dfrac{(1-t_{n}^{2})^{1/2}e^{3}_{n}(\omega_{n})}{1+t_{n}e^{1}_{n}(\omega_{n})}\right).

. Taking an automorphism of the ball
A^{-1}_{t_{n}}
as in Claim

shows that for large

the analytic disc

is uniformly close to a complex geodesic of the ball contained in

1-t_{n}\sim s_{n}=\delta_{\Omega}(x_{n})
. Set
\tilde{z}_{n}=A^{-1}_{t_{n}}(z_{n})
,
\tilde{w}_{n}=A^{-1}_{t_{n}}(w_{n})
.

We observed in Remark
19

and the proof of Proposition

that the following estimates hold:
(1-t^{2}_{n})\delta_{\Omega_{n}}(\tilde{z}_{n})\lesssim\delta_{\Omega}(z_{n}),
and
(1-t^{2}_{n})\delta_{\Omega_{n}}(\tilde{w}_{n})\lesssim\delta_{\Omega}(w_{n}).

We claim more, namely that there are asymptotic equalities within them. Precisely, we will show the following: if
y_{n}
lies in the range of
\varphi_{n}
and
\tilde{y}_{n}
is its pull-back to
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}
, then

(40)

(1-t^{2}_{n})\delta_{\Omega_{n}}(\tilde{y}_{n})\sim\delta_{\Omega}(y_{n}).

To see this, put
\delta^{\prime}_{\Omega}(y_{n})=\inf\{\|u-y_{n}\|:u\in\partial\Omega,u^{1}=y^{1}_{n}\},\delta^{\prime}_{\Omega_{n}}(\tilde{y}_{n})=\inf\{\|u-\tilde{y}_{n}\|:u\in\partial\Omega_{n},u^{1}=\tilde{y}^{1}_{n}\}
, and
\delta^{1}_{\Omega}(y_{n})=\inf\{\|u-y_{n}\|:u\in\partial\Omega,u^{\prime}=y^{\prime}_{n}\}
. Observe that, as
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}
are uniformly
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-close to a complex geodesic of the ball lying in
\{0\}\times\mathbb{C}^{N-1}
, we have

\delta_{\Omega_{n}}(\tilde{y}_{n})\sim\delta^{\prime}_{\Omega_{n}}(\tilde{y}_{n}).

Moreover by Remark

\delta^{\prime}_{\Omega}(y_{n})\sim(1-t^{2}_{n})^{1/2}\delta^{\prime}_{\Omega_{n}}(\tilde{y}_{n})
.

To get (
40
) it is enough to show that

(41)

\delta_{\Omega}(y_{n})\lesssim\delta^{1}_{\Omega}(y_{n})\lesssim(1-t^{2}_{n})^{1/2}\delta^{\prime}_{\Omega}(y_{n}).

The first estimate in (
41
) is trivial, only the second one requires a proof. Since
\tilde{y}_{n}
belongs to the image of
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}
, the estimate
|\tilde{y}^{1}_{n}|<\epsilon_{n}
holds, where
\epsilon_{n}
is a sequence of positive numbers converging to zero. Pushing
\tilde{y}_{n}
forward by
A_{t_{n}}
one can write
y_{n}^{1}=t_{n}+(1-t_{n})o_{n},
where
\text{o}_{n}
are complex numbers converging to
0

t_{n}+i(1-t_{n})\text{o}_{n}=\tau_{n}+i(1-\tau_{n})o_{n}
, where
o_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
(to be precise, we define
\tau_{n}:=t_{n}+(1-t_{n})\operatorname{Re}\text{o}_{n}
and
o_{n}:=\operatorname{Im}\text{o}_{n}/(1-\operatorname{Re}\text{o}_{n})
). It is clear that
o_{n}
converges to
0
and that
1-\tau_{n}\sim 1-t_{n}
. Thus we can write
y_{n}=(\tau_{n}+i(1-\tau_{n})o_{n},y^{\prime}_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}+i\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{C}^{N-1})
.

Take
\sigma_{n}>\tau_{n}
such that
(\sigma_{n}+i(1-\tau_{n})o_{n},y_{n}^{\prime})\in\partial\Omega
. Moreover, take
\xi^{\prime}_{n}
such that
a_{n}:=(\tau_{n}+i(1-\tau_{n})o_{n},\xi^{\prime}_{n})
is in
\partial\Omega
and satisfies
\|a_{n}-y_{n}\|=\delta^{\prime}_{\Omega}(z_{n})
.

Near
p=(1,0,...,0)
we can write
\partial\Omega
as
\operatorname{Re}z_{1}=u(\text{Im}z_{1},z^{\prime})
, where

u(\text{Im}z_{1},z^{\prime})=1-\frac{|\text{Im}z_{1}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{\|z^{\prime}\|^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}(|\text{Im}z_{1}|^{2+\alpha}+\|z^{\prime}\|^{2+\alpha}).

Then, in particular,
\tau_{n}=u((1-\tau_{n})o_{n},\xi^{\prime}_{n})
and
\sigma_{n}=u((1-\tau_{n})o_{n},y^{\prime}_{n})
.

Clearly
\delta^{1}_{\Omega}(y_{n})\leq|\tau_{n}-\sigma_{n}|=\|u((1-\tau_{n})o_{n},\xi^{\prime}_{n})-u((1-\tau_{n})o_{n},y^{\prime}_{n})\|
and
\delta^{\prime}_{\Omega}(y_{n})=\|\xi^{\prime}_{n}-y^{\prime}_{n}\|
. Since
\|\xi^{\prime}_{n}\|,\|y^{\prime}_{n}\|
behave as
\mathcal{O}((1-\tau^{2}_{n})^{1/2})
, we get

\frac{\delta^{1}_{\Omega}(y_{n})}{\delta^{\prime}_{\Omega}(y_{n})}\leq\dfrac{\|u((1-\tau_{n})o_{n},\xi^{\prime}_{n})-u((1-\tau_{n})o_{n},y^{\prime}_{n})\|}{\|\xi^{\prime}_{n}-y^{\prime}_{n}\|}\lesssim\|\xi^{\prime}_{n}\|+\|y^{\prime}_{n}\|\lesssim(1-\tau^{2}_{n})^{1/2}\sim(1-t^{2}_{n})^{1/2}.

This yields (
41
) and consequently leads to (
40

Summing up, it follows from what we showed, Remark
19
, and the proof of Proposition
3
that the following estimates hold:

\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|\sim(1-t^{2}_{n})^{1/2}\|\tilde{z}_{n}-\tilde{w}_{n}\|,\>\>\>\delta_{\Omega}(z_{n})\sim(1-t^{2}_{n})\delta_{\Omega_{n}}(\tilde{z}_{n}),\>\>\>\delta_{\Omega}(w_{n})\sim(1-t^{2}_{n})\delta_{\Omega_{n}}(\tilde{w}_{n}).

All of them together with (

k_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})=k_{\Omega_{n}}(\tilde{z}_{n},\tilde{w}_{n})\leq\log\left(1+\dfrac{C\|\tilde{z}_{n}-\tilde{w}_{n}\|}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega_{n}}(\tilde{z}_{n})\delta_{\Omega_{n}}(\tilde{w}_{n})}}\right)\leq\log\left(1+\dfrac{C^{\prime}\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|(1-t^{2}_{n})^{1/2}}{\sqrt{\delta_{\Omega}(z_{n})\delta_{\Omega}(w_{n})}}\right),

with a uniform

Recall that by Claim

(1-t^{2}_{n})^{1/2}\sim s^{1/2}_{n}=\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(x_{n})\sim d_{e}(\varphi_{n}(\Delta))

Extension of estimates to the Lempert function and the Carathéodory distance
. Recall the following comparison results by
[

[

l_{\Omega}(z,w)\leq c_{\Omega}(z,w)+C_{0}g_{\Omega}(z,w)\>\>\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>\>\>l_{\Omega}(z,w)\leq C_{0}c_{\Omega}(z,w),

where

and

g_{\Omega}(z,w)=\dfrac{\|z-w\|}{\|z-w\|^{1/2}+\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)+\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)}.

Observe that
g_{\Omega}(z,w)
is bounded above. In particular, as the Kobayashi distance is bounded below by the Carathéodory distance and bounded above by the Lempert function, we see that there exists a constant
C_{1}>1
such that

(42)

\displaystyle k_{\Omega}(z,w)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\Omega}(z,w)+C_{1},\>\>k_{\Omega}(z,w)\leq C_{1}c_{\Omega}(z,w),

\displaystyle l_{\Omega}(z,w)

\displaystyle\leq k_{\Omega}(z,w)+C_{1},\>\>l_{\Omega}(z,w)\leq C_{1}k_{\Omega}(z,w).

Proof of the upper bound for the Lempert function.
Suppose that the upper bound fails. Then we can find
z_{n},w_{n}\in\Omega
tending to
a,b\in\overline{\Omega}
, and
c_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
tending to infinity such that

(43)

l_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\geq\log\left(1+{c_{n}A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})}\right).

We need to consider two cases (pass to a subsequence, if necessary):

Case I.
The sequence
l_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})
is bounded from above. Then trivially
k_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\lesssim 1
. In particular, by the lower bound we obtained for the Kobayashi distance, this implies that
A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\lesssim 1
. Applying the upper bound for the Kobayashi distance and (
42
) we get

l_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\leq C_{1}k_{\Omega}(z_{n},z_{n})\leq{C_{2}A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})}.

Note that for
r^{\prime}\geq 0
one can find a constant
C^{\prime}>1
such that
r\leq\log(1+C^{\prime}r)
holds for
0\leq r\leq r^{\prime}
. In light of this, as
A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})
are uniformly bounded, the inequality above contradicts (
43
).

Case II.
The sequence
l_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})
is bounded away from
0
. It follows from (
42
) and the lower bound we obtained for the Kobayashi distance that

, and consequently
A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})
, are bounded away from
0
. Using (
42
) we get

l_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\leq k_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})+C_{3}\leq\log\left(1+{CA_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})}\right)+C_{3}\leq\\
\log\left({C_{4}A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})}\right)+C_{3}\leq\log\left(1+{C_{5}A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})}\right),

where the constant
C_{4}
depends on
\epsilon
such that
A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\geq\epsilon
.

Having this estimate, we once again see that a sequence fulfilling (
43
) cannot exist.

Proof of the lower bound for the Carathéodory distance.
Suppose that this lower bound fails. We can then find
z_{n},w_{n}\in\Omega
tending to
a,b\in\overline{\Omega}
and
c_{n}>0
tending to zero such that

(44)

c_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\leq\log\left(1+{c_{n}A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})}\right).

By passing to a subsequence, if necessary, we distinguish two cases.

Case I.
The sequence
c_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})
is bounded from above. Due to (
42
) and the lower bound we obtained for the Kobayashi distance we have that
k_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\lesssim 1
, whence

. Using (
42
), the lower bound we obtained for the Kobayashi distance and the fact that

for

, we get

c_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\geq C^{-1}_{1}k_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\geq C^{-1}_{1}\log\left(1+{cA_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})}\right)\geq\\
{c_{2}A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n}))}\geq\log\left(1+{c_{2}A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})}\right).

This contradicts (
44
).

Case II.
The sequence
c_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})
escapes to infinity. Then, clearly,
k_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\to\infty,.
Therefore, by the upper bound we obtained for the Kobayashi distance,
A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\to\infty
, as well. Using the lower bound we obtained for the Kobayashi distance, by (
42
) we get

c_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})\geq k_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})-C_{1}\geq\log\left(1+{cA_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})}\right)-C_{1}\geq\\
\log\left({cA_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})}\right)-C_{1}\geq\log\left({c_{3}A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})}\right)\geq\log\left(1+{\dfrac{c_{3}}{2}A_{\Omega}(z_{n},w_{n})}\right)

provided that
n
is big enough.We again see that a sequence satisfying (
44
) cannot exist.

9.
Strictly linearly convex case

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Set

h_{\Omega}(z,w)=\|(z-w)_{z}\|+\|z-w\|\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z).

Corollary
8
shows that if
p\in\partial\Omega
is a
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth strongly pseudoconvex boundary point, then there exists
C>1
such that

(48)

C^{-1}<\frac{(e^{k_{\Omega}(z,w)}-1)\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(z)\delta^{1/2}_{\Omega}(w)}{h_{\Omega}(z,w)+\|z-w\|^{2}}<C,\quad z\neq w\in\Omega,\ z,w\mbox{ near }p.

It is clear that none of the summands of
h_{\Omega}
can be removed in such an estimate
(
C
may vary). On the other hand, the proof of Lemma
20
asserts that the terms
\|z-w\|^{2}
in the upper estimates in Theorem
1
and Corollary
8
are also essential. One can observe this fact directly. Indeed, consider a domain that near the origin is given by
D=\{\rho<0\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
, where
\rho(z)=\operatorname{Re}(z_{1}-z_{2}^{2})+|z_{2}|^{2}
. If we take
z_{n},w_{n}\in D
such that
z_{n}=(\delta_{n},0),w_{n}=(0,\epsilon_{n})
with
\delta_{n}=o(\epsilon_{n})
, we find that
\|(z_{n}-w_{n})_{z_{n}}\|+\delta^{1/2}_{D}(z_{n})\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|=o(\|z_{n}-w_{n}\|^{2})
. Hence, the term
\|z-w\|^{2}
in the lower bound cannot be estimated by the other two terms. In particular, it cannot be, in general, removed from the upper bound in Theorem
1
as well as in Corollary
8
.

Interestingly, the scenario changes when dealing with strictly linearly convex domains. This section is devoted to exploring this matter and uncovering some results that stand on their own significance.

Let
\tilde{\delta}_{\Omega}
denote the signed boundary distance function of
\Omega
. Recall that
\Omega
is called strictly linearly convex at a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth boundary point

p
if the restriction of the Hessian of
\tilde{\delta}_{\Omega}
to the complex tangent plane
T^{\mathbb{C}}_{p}(\partial\Omega)
is strictly
positive definite. We claim that the
\|z-w\|^{2}
term in (
48
) is
superfluous if and only if
\Omega
is strictly linearly convex at
p\in\partial\Omega
. Precisely, our aim is to show the following:

Proposition 22
.

Let
p
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth boundary point of a domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}^{N}.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\Omega
is strictly linearly convex at
p
;

(2)

\displaystyle c_{2}=\liminf_{\Omega\ni z,w\to p}\frac{h_{\Omega}(z,w)}{\|z-w\|^{2}}>0;

(3)

\displaystyle c_{3}=\liminf_{\Omega\ni w\to p}\frac{\|(p-w)_{p}\|}{\|p-w\|^{2}}>0.

An immediate consequence of Proposition
22
is that
c_{2}=c_{3}=0
if
\Omega
is not strictly linearly convex at
p
. Also, if
\Omega
is strictly linearly convex, then
\|(p-z)_{p}\|\neq 0

for any
p\in\partial\Omega,

z\in\overline{\Omega},

z\neq p.
A standard compactness argument yields:

Corollary 23
.

A bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth boundary

such that

Set

\displaystyle c_{4}=\liminf_{\partial\Omega\ni w\to p}\frac{\|(p-w)_{p}\|}{\|p-w\|^{2}},

G=\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}

\tilde{\delta}_{\Omega}
to

\Omega
is strictly linearly convex at

.

On PPT square conjecture.
Wladyslaw Adam Majewski

Abstract
. A detailed analysis of the PPT square conjecture is given.



1.
Introduction

The notion of positive partial transpose maps, PPT maps for short, is playing an important role in the quantum information theory. We remind the reader that linear maps
T:B(\mathcal{H})\to B(\mathcal{H})
that are both completely positive and completely copositive are called PPT maps.
The important point to note here is an observation that various classes of positive maps are used to describe quantum and useful maps in bipartite systems. In particular, quantum maps which are useless for preserving quantum entanglement are called entanglement breaking maps.
T
is an entanglement breaking map iff it can be written as

(1)

T(a)=\sum_{k}s_{k}(a)P_{k},

where
s_{k}(a)=(aw_{k},w_{k})
,
a\in B(\mathcal{H})
,
P_{k}=|v_{k}><v_{k}|
for some sets of vectors
w_{k},v_{k}
in
\mathcal{H}
; see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
.

Recently, Christandl,
[
3
]
, raised the following conjecture, see also
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
:

Conjecture 1.1
.

(PPT square conjecture)

The composition
T_{1}\circ T_{2}
of a pair of

T_{1}
and
T_{2}
PPT maps on
B(\mathcal{H})
is always entanglement breaking.

It was argued that PPT square conjecture
1.1
is equivalent to

Conjecture 1.2
.

[
7
]

If
T_{1}
is positive and
T_{2}
is PPT map then
T_{1}\circ T_{2}
is decomposable.

In Section 2 we give definitions of the concepts just introduced. Furthermore, we will define and discuss the associated classes of positive maps. Section 3 is devoted to a study of PPT square conjecture. In particular, we will present the general approach to the problem. Keeping in mind that entanglement breaking maps are so important in Quantum Information, in Section 4 we will give a detailed analysis of the relevant tensor cones and associated classes of maps. This will be done for finite dimensional cases.

2.
Preliminaries

To facilitate access to the considered maps, here, we will make the following assumptions: let
\{e_{i}\}_{1}^{d<\infty}
be a basis in
\mathcal{H}
, where
d=\dim\mathcal{H}
. The conjugation
J:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is defined as

(2)

Jf=J\left(\sum_{i}(f,e_{i})e_{i}\right)=\sum_{i}\overline{(f,e_{i})}e_{i},

where
f\in\mathcal{H}
. In particular, a vector
f\in\mathcal{H}
is called real if
Jf=f
. The transposition
t(\cdot)
of
a\in B(\mathcal{H})
with respect to the basis
\{e_{i}\}
is given by

(3)

t(a)=Ja^{*}J.

Our next observation is that any
V\in B(\mathcal{H})
can be written as

(4)

Vf=V\left(\sum_{k}(f,e_{k})e_{k}\right)=\sum_{k}(f,e_{k})Ve_{k}=\sum_{k}\left(Ve_{k}\otimes\overline{e_{k}}\right)f,

where
f\in\mathcal{H}
, and
v\otimes\overline{w}\ \cdot z=(z,w)v
; for details see
[
8
]
.

In the very special case, if
RanV=1
one has
Ve_{i}=\lambda_{i}x
, where
x
is a certain vector in
\mathcal{H}
and
\lambda_{i}\in{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 6.33339pt\vrule height=6.14993pt\hss}\hbox{
$\displaystyle\rm C$}}}
. Moreover, in this case,

(5)

V=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}x\otimes\overline{e_{i}}=x\otimes\overline{\sum_{i}\overline{\lambda_{i}}e_{i}}=x\otimes\overline{y}.

Thus, (
1
) can be rewritten as

(6)

T(a)=\sum_{k}v_{k}\otimes\overline{w_{k}}\cdot a\cdot w_{k}\otimes\overline{v_{k}}\equiv\sum_{k}V_{k}\cdot a\cdot V^{*}_{k},

and, obviously,
Ranv_{k}\otimes\overline{w_{k}}\equiv RanV_{k}=1
.

However, as a quantum system demands infinite dimensional Hilbert space, from now on we make the assumption that Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
is, in general, infinite dimensional.
We need to distinguish the following families of maps
T:B(\mathcal{H})\to B(\mathcal{H})
; positive, decomposable, completely positive, PPT, and superpositive. To present the concise and rigorous description we will use the selection rule which gives the required characterization in terms of tensor cones, see
[
9
]
for all details.

•

positive maps; they are determined by the tensor cone

conv\left(B(\mathcal{H})_{+}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})_{+}\right)=C_{p}
. Here and subsequently we are using the identification
\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})=B(\mathcal{H})_{*}
. Here,
\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})
stands for the trace class operators which is the predual space
B(\mathcal{H})_{*}
with respect to
B(\mathcal{H})
.

•

decomposable maps; they are determined by the tensor cone

conv\{\left(B(\mathcal{H})\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})\right)_{+}\cap\left(\mathrm{id}\otimes t\right)\left(B(\mathcal{H})\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})\right)_{+}\}\equiv C_{d}.

•

completely positive maps; they are determined by the tensor cone

\left(B(\mathcal{H})\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})\right)_{+}\equiv C_{cp}
.

•

PPT maps; they are determined by the tensor cone

conv\{\left(B(\mathcal{H})\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})\right)_{+}\cup\left(\mathrm{id}\otimes t\right)\left(B(\mathcal{H})\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})\right)_{+}\}\equiv C_{PPT.}

•

super positive maps; they are determined by the largest tensor cone - the injective tensor cone
C_{i}
.
Equivalently,
T(a)=\sum_{i}V^{*}_{i}aV_{i}
is superpositive iff there exists a Krauss decomposition such that for all
V_{i}
one has the
RanV_{i}=1
, cf Stømer’s book
[
11
]
.

We remind the reader that the selection rule is designed to define certain classes of positive maps with the prescribed properties, see
[
9
]
. It reads as follows: the class of positive maps
P_{\alpha}
is defined as

(7)

P_{\alpha}=\{T\in L(B(\mathcal{H}),B(\mathcal{H}));\tilde{T}(\sum a_{i}\otimes b_{i})\equiv\sum TrT(a_{i})t(b_{i})\geq 0\ \rm{for\ any}\sum a_{i}\otimes b_{i}\in C_{\alpha}\}

where
C_{\alpha}
is the specified tensor cone. In other words, properties of a positive map
T
are specified by the associated functional
\tilde{T}
, for all details see
[
9
]
.

3.
PPT maps and PPT square conjecture

To get some intuition we begin this section with an example.

Example 3.1
.

Let
V_{1}=v\otimes\overline{e_{1}}+Jv\otimes\overline{e_{2}}
,
V_{2}=Jv\otimes\overline{e_{1}}+v\otimes\overline{e_{2}}
where
v
is a not real vector in
\mathcal{H}
,
\{e_{i}\}
a basis in
\mathcal{H}
.
Define

(8)

T(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{2}V_{i}aV^{*}_{i},\quad a\in B(\mathcal{H}).

Then

(9)

T(a)=(ae_{1},e_{1})v\otimes\overline{v}+(ae_{2},e_{1})v\otimes\overline{Jv}+(ae_{1},e_{2})Jv\otimes\overline{v}+(ae_{2},e_{2})Jv\otimes\overline{Jv}

+(ae_{1},e_{1})Jv\otimes\overline{Jv}+(ae_{2},e_{1})Jv\otimes\overline{v}+(ae_{1},e_{2})v\otimes\overline{Jv}+(ae_{2},e_{2})v\otimes\overline{v},

and

(10)

t\circ T(a)=T(a).

Further, we note

(11)

T\circ T(a)=\sum_{ij}V_{i}V_{j}aV_{i}^{*}V_{j}^{*},

with

(12)

V_{1}^{2}=\left(v\otimes\overline{e_{1}}+Jv\otimes\overline{e_{2}}\right)\left(v\otimes\overline{e_{1}}+Jv\otimes\overline{e_{2}}\right)

=(v,e_{1})v\otimes\overline{e_{1}}+(e_{1},v)v\otimes\overline{e_{2}}+(v,e_{2})Jv\otimes\overline{e_{1}}+(e_{2},v)Jv\otimes\overline{e_{2}},\quad\rm{etc.}

It is clear that
RanV_{i}=2
while
RanV_{i}^{2}
is also equal to
2
if
v
is not orthogonal to
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
, (
v\in\mathcal{H}
is not a real vector). Obviously,
T
is a PPT map. In general,
T
is not unital. However, there do exist such vectors
v
(in the plane
<e_{1},e_{2}>
determined by vectors
e_{1},e_{2}
) that
T(\bf{1})=\bf{1}
.
Finally we want to see that
T
is superpositive.
The important point to note here is the fact that the Krauss decomposition, cf (
1
), (
6
), is not unique. Therefore, to show that
T
is superpositive we apply the characterization of these maps given in terms of the injective cone, see the previous section. To this end
it would be enough to observe that, in 2-dimensional case, the tensor cone
conv\{\left(B(\mathcal{H})\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})\right)_{+}\cup\left(\mathrm{id}\otimes t\right)\left(B(\mathcal{H})\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})\right)_{+}\}

is equal to the injective cone
C_{i}
. We will see that
this claim is dual to the equivalence, in two dimensional case, of the tensor cone
C_{d}
determining decomposable maps with the tensor cone determining positive maps
C_{p}
.
Thus to proceed with the examination of superpositivity, we should introduce the notion of duality and develop its description. Then, the superpositivity of
T
will follow.

To define the dual tensor cone, firstly, we will define the notion of duality, cf
[
10
]
. To this end we begin with an observation that the pair
<B(\mathcal{H}),\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})>
of vector spaces
B(\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})
is separating dual, i.e.

(13)

a\times\varrho\mapsto<a,\varrho>\equiv\mathrm{Tr}a\varrho\in{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 6.33339pt\vrule height=6.14993pt\hss}\hbox{
$\displaystyle\rm C$}}};\ a\in B(\mathcal{H}),\ \varrho\in\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H}),

and for any
a\neq 0
(
\sigma\neq 0
) there is
\varrho\neq 0
(
b\neq 0
) with
\mathrm{Tr}a\varrho\neq 0
(
\mathrm{Tr}b\sigma\neq 0
respectively), where
a,b\in B(\mathcal{H})
and
\varrho,\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})
.

The separating dual pairing is defined as, cf Section 15 in
[
10
]
,

<\sum_{n}a_{n}\otimes\varrho_{n},\sum_{m}b_{m}\otimes\sigma_{m}>\ \to{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 6.33339pt\vrule height=6.14993pt\hss}\hbox{
$\displaystyle\rm C$}}}

(14)

<\sum_{n}a_{n}\otimes\varrho_{n},\sum_{m}b_{m}\otimes\sigma_{m}>=\sum_{n}\sum_{m}\mathrm{Tr}a_{n}\sigma_{m}\cdot\mathrm{Tr}\varrho_{n}b_{m}.

Now, we are in position to define

Definition 3.2
.

The dual cone
C^{d}_{\alpha}
is defined as

(15)

C^{d}_{\alpha}=\{\sum_{n}a_{n}\otimes\varrho_{n};\sum_{m}\sum_{n}\mathrm{Tr}a_{n}\sigma_{m}\cdot\mathrm{Tr}b_{m}\varrho_{n}\geq 0\ \rm{for\ all}\ \sum_{m}b_{m}\otimes\sigma_{m}\in C_{\alpha}\}.

In particular, it is an easy observation that

(16)

C^{d}_{p}=\{\sum_{n}a_{n}\otimes\varrho_{n};\sum_{m}\sum_{n}\mathrm{Tr}a_{n}\sigma_{m}\cdot\mathrm{Tr}b_{m}\varrho_{n}\geq 0,\ {for\ all}\ b_{m}\geq 0,\sigma_{m}\geq 0\}=C_{i}.

In addition, we note:

Remark 3.3
.

(1)

As tensor cones are defined for the projective tensor product, the above tensor products
\otimes
denote the projective tensor product
\otimes_{\pi}
.

(2)

Now it is an easy exercise to prove the final claim of the above example.

(3)

Finally, the above notion of duality suggests that two conjectures (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are dual each other.
We return to this issue at the end of this section.

We now turn to an explanation of what is going on with the composition of a positive map with a PPT map. This will be done using the structure of positive maps, and in particular, the selection rule; for details see
[
9
]
.

Our first observation is that Conjecture
1.2
can be rephrased as follows: for any positive map
T_{1}
the functional
\tilde{T_{0}}
associated with the restriction
T_{0}\equiv T_{1}|_{T_{2}C_{d}}
, where
T_{2}
is a PPT map, should be positive on the tensor cone
\left(B(\mathcal{H})\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})\right)_{+}\cap\left(id\otimes t\right)\left(B(\mathcal{H})\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})\right)_{+}\equiv C_{d}
.

The associated functional
\tilde{T_{0}}
to the map
T_{0}
has the form, cf
[
9
]

(17)

\tilde{T_{0}}(\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i})=\sum_{i}\mathrm{Tr}T_{0}(a)b_{i}^{t},

where
b^{t}\equiv t(b)
and
\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}
is taken from the specified tensor cone.

As Conjecture
1.2
claims the decomposability of the composition
T_{1}\circ T_{2}
the specified cone should be
C_{d}
.

To proceed with the study of Conjecture
1.2
we will look more closely at the dual relations between the distinguished tensor cones. It easy to check that

(18)

C^{d}_{d}=\{\sum_{n}A_{n}\otimes\varrho_{n};\sum_{m,n}\mathrm{Tr}A_{n}\sigma_{m}\cdot\mathrm{Tr}B_{m}\varrho_{n}\geq 0\ \rm{for\ all}\ \sum_{m}B_{m}\otimes\sigma_{m}\in C_{d}\}=C_{PPT}.

As for the preimage
R^{-1}(B)
of a mapping
R
one has
R\circ R^{-1}(B)\subseteq B
, it is easy to see

(19)

0\leq<\mathcal{T}_{2}C_{d},(\mathcal{T}^{d}_{2})^{-1}C_{PPT}>

where
\mathcal{T}_{i}\equiv T_{i}\otimes id
,
i=1,2
, and
(\mathcal{T}^{d}_{2})
stands for the dual map of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
. ( We emphasize that, in general, the map
\mathcal{T}^{d}_{2}
has not the inverse map. Therefore, we employed the inverse image technique.)

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{2}
be such that
\mathcal{T}_{2}C_{d}\subseteq C_{p}
. Then, the Conjecture
1.2
holds.

Proof.

Under the assumption, the composition
\mathcal{T}_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}_{2}
is well defined on
C_{d}
for any positive map
T_{1}
.
Consequently, the associated functional
\widetilde{T_{1}\circ T_{2}}
is positive defined on the cone
C_{d}
. Thus, the selection rule implies that
T_{1}\circ T_{2}
is a decomposable map.
∎

Further we note
<C_{p},C_{i}>\geq 0
as the corresponding cones are dual each other. Hence
<\mathcal{T}_{2}C_{d},C_{i}>\geq 0
and
<C_{d},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{d}C_{i}>\geq 0
. Thus

(20)

\mathcal{T}_{2}C_{d}\subseteq C_{p},\quad\rm{implies}\quad\mathcal{T}^{d}_{2}C_{i}\subseteq C_{PPT}

Consider
\mathcal{T}_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}^{d}_{2}
where now
T_{1}
is a PPT map. It is obvious that
\mathcal{T}_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}^{d}_{2}
is well defined on the cone
C_{i}
.
Consequently,
\widetilde{T_{1}\circ T_{2}^{d}}
is well defined on
C_{i}
, and the selection rule implies that
T_{1}\circ T^{d}_{2}

is a superpositivity map. And this shows that the examined Conjectures
1.1
and
1.2
are dual each other in the above sense.

What is left is to examine the condition
\mathcal{T}_{2}C_{d}\subseteq C_{p}
. This will be done in the next Section, for finite dimensional case.

4.
Finite dimensional case

In this section we indicate how techniques described in the previous section may be used to examine the relations (
20
).

As finite dimensional models are prevailing in Quantum Information, from now on, again, we make the assumption that the considered Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is finite dimensional. Needless to say the finite dimension of
\mathcal{H}
will be assumed only for simplicity. It is possible to proceed the study of the condition
\mathcal{T}_{2}C_{d}\subseteq C_{p}
in more general setting. It would require the more careful analysis of trace class operators, continuity of corresponding functionals and maps. But, we will not develop this point here. The advantage of finite dimensional approach lies in the fact that it sheds some new light on the structure of the set of positive maps, in particular, on the origin of non-decomposable maps.

We have seen that the condition (
20
) is crucial and we wish to examine in details this condition.
To this end we will look more closely at the image
\mathcal{T}_{2}(C_{d})
of
C_{d}
where
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is a PPT map. We remind that any
a\in B(\mathcal{H})\otimes B(\mathcal{H})
can be uniquely represented as

(21)

a=\sum_{k,l=1}^{dim\mathcal{H}}A_{kl}\otimes e_{kl}=\sum_{k,l=1}^{dim\mathcal{H}}e_{ij}\otimes B_{ij},

where
A_{kl}=\mathrm{Tr}_{(2)}a\cdot\mathbf{1}\otimes e_{lk}\in B(\mathcal{H})
and the system
\{e_{kl}\}_{k,l=1}^{dim\mathcal{H}}\in B(\mathcal{H})
consists of matrix units (so
e_{kl}^{*}=e_{lk},\ e_{kl}e_{mn}=\delta_{lm}e_{kn},\ \sum_{i}e_{ii}=\mathbf{1})
. Obviously, similar characterization is valid for
B_{kl}
. Furthermore, as
\dim\mathcal{H}<\infty
, we have used the identification :
\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})=B(\mathcal{H})
.
\mathrm{Tr}_{(2)}
stands for the partial trace with respect to the second term.

So

(22)

\left(B(\mathcal{H})\otimes B(\mathcal{H})\right)_{+}\ni a^{*}a=\sum_{kln}A_{kl}^{*}A_{kn}\otimes e_{ln},

and

(23)

\left(\mathrm{id}\otimes t\right)(a^{*}a)=\sum_{kln}A^{*}_{kl}A_{kn}\otimes e_{nl}.

Consequently,
C_{d}
consists of those elements
\sum_{klm}A_{kl}^{*}A_{kn}\otimes e_{ln}

for which

(24)

[\sum_{k}A^{*}_{kl}A_{kn}\otimes e_{ln}]\geq 0,\quad[\sum_{k}A^{*}_{kn}A_{kl}\otimes e_{ln}]\geq 0.

However, the matrix
[\sum_{k}A^{*}_{kl}A_{kn}\otimes e_{ln}]\geq 0
iff there exist
B_{kn}\in B(\mathcal{H})
such that

[\sum_{k}A^{*}_{kn}A_{kl}\otimes e_{ln}]=[\sum_{k}B^{*}_{kl}B_{kn}\otimes e_{ln}].

Thus
C_{d}
consists of the following elements
\sum_{klm}A_{kn}^{*}A_{kl}\otimes e_{ln}=\sum_{klm}B^{*}_{kl}B_{kn}\otimes e_{ln}.

However, to examine the image of
C_{d}
under a PPT map
\varphi
we need more informations about PPT maps. As the first step we recall Evans result
on
n+1
positive maps
[
12
]
. For
n+1
positive map
\varphi
the matrix inequality

(25)

||\varphi||(\varphi(a^{*}_{i}a_{j}))^{n}_{i,j=1}\geq(\varphi(a^{*}_{i})\varphi(a_{j}))^{n}_{ij=1}

is valid for all
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}
, where
\mathfrak{A}
is a
C^{*}
algebra. Furthermore, if a bounded linear map
\varphi:\mathfrak{A}\to B(\mathcal{H})
satisfies these inequalities for all
n
, then
\varphi
is completely positive.

Applying (
25
) to elements of
C_{d}
we obtain

(26)

(\varphi(A^{*}_{kl}A_{km}))_{l,m=1}^{n}\geq(\varphi(A^{*}_{kl})\varphi(A_{k,m})_{l,m=1}^{n}

as we assumemed
||\varphi||=1
(what follows from the assumption that
\varphi
is unital). If
\varphi
is PPT map then
\varphi\circ t
is also CP map. Thus

(27)

(\varphi\circ t(A^{*}_{kl}A_{km}))_{l,m=1}^{n}\geq(\varphi\circ t(A^{*}_{kl})\ \varphi\circ t(A_{km})_{l,m=1}^{n}

Hence

(28)

(\varphi(A^{*}_{km}(A_{kl})))_{m,l=1}^{n}\geq(\varphi\circ(t(A^{*}_{kl})\ \varphi\circ(t(A_{km}))_{l,m=1}^{n}

as for the matrix
[A^{*}_{kl}A_{km}]_{lm}\geq 0
its transposition is
[A^{*}_{km}A_{kl}]_{ml}
. Consequently, the PPT property of
\varphi
ensures that

(29)

(\varphi\otimes id)(C_{d})\subseteq C_{d}.

One can say even more: namely for a PPT map
\varphi
one has

(30)

(\varphi\otimes id)(C_{cp})\subseteq C_{d}\quad\rm{and}\quad(\varphi\circ t\otimes id)(C_{cp})\subseteq C_{d},

what clearly indicates the big difference between CP map and PPT map.

The task is now to examine the structure of
\varphi(C_{d})\subseteq C_{d}
. To answer this question,
we begin by noting that
C_{d}
is a convex closed set, thus also
C_{d}^{B}=\{A\in C_{d};||A||\leq B\}
. In particular,
C_{d}^{B}
is a convex compact set. As
\varphi
is a continuous map,
\varphi(C^{B}_{d})
is a compact convex set. This clearly forces, by Krein-Milman theorem, that
\varphi(C^{B}_{d})
is a convex hull of extreme points.

To proceed we need:

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\varphi
is a PPT map then
\varphi\otimes t
is a positive map on
B(\mathcal{H})\otimes B(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

It is easily seen that

(\varphi\otimes id)\circ(id\otimes t)=\varphi\otimes t=\varphi\circ t\circ t\otimes t=(\varphi\circ t\otimes id)\circ t\otimes t.

As
t\otimes t
is a positive map,
t\circ\varphi
as well as
\varphi\circ t
are CP maps (cf
[
2
]
) and the composition of two positive maps is a positive map the claim is proved.
∎

We next follow the deep Woronowicz
[
13
]
and Størmer
[
11
]
idea of looking for a specific vector
v
in the range of
\varphi(a)
,
a\in C_{d}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let for any
a\in(\varphi\otimes id)(C_{d})
there exists a simple tensor
x\otimes y
in
Ran(a)
,
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
. Then, for PPT map
\varphi
one has
(\varphi\otimes id)(C_{d})\subseteq C_{p}.

Proof.

Our proof starts with an observation that the assumption
x\otimes y\in Ran(a)
and the assumption of finite dimensionality of
\mathcal{H}
implies existence of
\epsilon>0
such that
a\geq\epsilon|x><x|\otimes|y><y|
.
Obviously, cf. Lemma
4.1
,

(id\otimes t)\circ(\varphi\otimes id)(a_{0})=(\varphi\otimes id)\circ(id\otimes t)(a_{0})\in(\varphi\otimes id)(C_{d}),

for
a_{0}\in C_{d}
.
In particular,
(\varphi\otimes id)-\epsilon|x><x>\otimes|\bar{y}><\bar{y}|\in(\varphi\otimes id)(C_{d})
where
\bar{y}=Jy
and
J
is a conjugation on
\mathcal{H}
associated with the transposition
t
.
Consequently, any
a-\epsilon|x><x|\otimes|y><y|\in(\varphi\otimes id)(C_{d})
is majorized. In particular, as it is true for any
b_{0}\in Ext{(\varphi\otimes id)(C_{d})}
(
Ext
stands for extremal points) we infer that for
b_{0}
there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that
b_{0}=\lambda_{0}|x><x|\otimes|y><y|
. Hence, it follows easily that under given assumptions

(31)

(\varphi\otimes id)(C_{d})\subseteq C_{p},

as required.
∎

We note that the existence of a product vector
x\otimes y
in the range of
(\varphi\otimes id)(a)
,
a\in C_{d}
is a nontrivial condition. Namely, there should be

\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}\ni f=\sum_{p}f_{p}\otimes e_{p}
,
f_{p}\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}x\otimes e_{i}=x\otimes y=\sum_{ijp}\varphi(a_{ij})\otimes e_{ij}\cdot f_{p}\otimes e_{p}=\sum_{ij}\varphi(a_{ij})f_{j}\otimes e_{i},

where
\lambda_{i}=(y,e_{i})
. Hence, for each
i
and
a=\sum_{ij}a_{ij}\otimes e_{ij}\in C_{d}
one should have

\lambda_{i}x=\sum_{j}\varphi(a_{ij})f_{j}.

Let us now turn to an examination of conditions

(32)

x\otimes y\in Ran(a);\quad a\in(\varphi\otimes id)(C_{d}),

The question of existence of product vectors in a subspace of the tensor product of two Hilbert spaces can be formulated in terms of algebraic geometry. In particular, properties of projective spaces as well as the Segre variety appeared to be crucial. For more information the reader may consult
[
14
]
. The following Proposition stated in
[

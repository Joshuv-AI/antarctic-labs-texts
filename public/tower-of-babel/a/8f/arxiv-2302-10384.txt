Long time solutions of quasilinear Klein-Gordon equations with small weakly decaying initial data
Hou Fei, Yin Huicheng[12pt] 1. Department of Mathematics, Nanjing University, Nanjing 210093, China2. School of Mathematical Sciences and Mathematical Institute, Nanjing Normal University, Nanjing 210023, China

Abstract
It is well known that for the quasilinear Klein-Gordon equation with quadratic nonlinearity and sufficiently decaying small initial data, there exists a global smooth solution if the space dimensions d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . When the initial data are of size ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 in the Sobolev space, for the semilinear Klein-Gordon equation satisfying the null condition, the authors in the article (J.-M. Delort, Daoyuan Fang, Almost global existence for solutions of semilinear Klein-Gordon equations with small weakly decaying Cauchy data, Comm. Partial Differential Equations 25 (2000), no. 11-12, 2119–2169) prove that the solution exists in time [ 0 , T ε ) [0,T_{\varepsilon}) with T ε ≥ C ​ e C ​ ε − μ T_{\varepsilon}\geq Ce^{C\varepsilon^{-\mu}} ( μ = 1 \mu=1 if d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , μ = 2 / 3 \mu=2/3 if d = 2 d=2 ). In the present paper, we will focus on the general quasilinear Klein-Gordon equation without the null condition and further show that the existence time of the solution can be improved to T ε = + ∞ T_{\varepsilon}=+\infty if d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 and T ε ≥ e C ​ ε − 2 T_{\varepsilon}\geq e^{C\varepsilon^{-2}} if d = 2 d=2 . In addition, for d = 2 d=2 and any fixed number α > 0 \alpha>0 , if the weighted L 2 L^{2} norm of the initial data with the weight ( 1 + | x | ) α (1+|x|)^{\alpha} is small, then the solution exists globally and scatters to a free solution. The arguments are based on the introduction of a good unknown, the Strichartz estimate, the weighted L 2 L^{2} -norm estimate and the resonance analysis. Keywords. Quasilinear Klein-Gordon equation, global solution, good unknown, Strichartz estimate, weighted L 2 L^{2} -norm, resonance analysis 2020 Mathematical Subject Classification. 35L70, 35L72.

2.1
Linear dispersive estimate and Strichartz estimate

For the function
f(x)
on
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{d}
, define its Fourier transformation as

\hat{f}(\xi):=\mathscr{F}_{x}f(\xi)=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx.

Choose a smooth cutoff function
\psi:\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits\rightarrow[0,1]

[-5/4,5/4]

[-8/5,8/5]

\begin{split}&\psi_{k}(x):=\psi(|x|/2^{k})-\psi(|x|/2^{k-1}),\quad k\in\mathop{\mathbb{Z}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits,k\geq 0,\\
&\psi_{-1}(x):=1-\sum_{k\geq 0}\psi_{k}(x)=\psi(2|x|),\quad\psi_{I}:=\sum_{k\in I\cap\mathop{\mathbb{Z}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits\cap[-1,\infty)}\psi_{k},\end{split}

where
I
is any interval of
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
.
Let

be the Littlewood-Paley projection onto frequency
2^{k}

\mathscr{F}(P_{k}f)(\xi):=\psi_{k}(\xi)\mathscr{F}f(\xi),\quad k\in\mathop{\mathbb{Z}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits,k\geq-1.

In addition, for any interval
I
,

is defined by

P_{I}f:=\sum_{k\in I\cap\mathop{\mathbb{Z}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits\cap[-1,\infty)}P_{k}f.

Lemma 2.1

(Linear dispersive estimate)
.

For any function
f
, integer

\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{d})}\lesssim 2^{k(d/2+1)}t^{-d/2}\|P_{k}f\|_{L^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{d})},

where and below for the non-negative quantities

Proof.

It is easy to check that

P_{k}=P_{k}P_{[k-1,k+1]}.

(2.2)

Then we have

\begin{split}P_{k}e^{it\Lambda}f(x)&=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{d}}K_{k}(t,x-y)P_{k}f(y)dy,\\

The estimate of

Let

\partial_{t}\sqrt{1+q}&=\frac{1}{2}(1+q)^{-1/2}(\partial_{t}Q^{jl}+2\partial_{t}Q^{0j}Q^{0l})\zeta_{j}\zeta_{l}\Lambda^{-2}(\zeta)\\
&=(1+q)^{-1/2}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(u,\partial u,\partial\partial_{x}u)+\mathcal{F}_{2}(u,\partial u,\partial\partial_{x}u)),\end{split}

where
\mathcal{F}_{1}(0,0,0)=0
,
\mathcal{F}_{1}(u,\partial u,\partial\partial_{x}u)
is linear in
(u,\partial u,\partial\partial_{x}u)
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}(u,\partial u,\partial\partial_{x}u)
is at least second order of
(u,\partial u,\partial\partial_{x}u)
.
Therefore,

\begin{split}T_{\partial_{t}Q^{0j}\zeta_{j}}u&=T_{(\mathcal{F}_{1}+\mathcal{F}_{2})\zeta_{j}\Lambda^{-1}(\zeta)}\Lambda u+E((\mathcal{F}_{1}+\mathcal{F}_{2})\zeta_{j},\Lambda^{-1}(\zeta))\Lambda u,\\
T_{\partial_{t}\sqrt{1+q}}\Lambda u&=T_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\Lambda u+T_{((1+q)^{-1/2}-1)\mathcal{F}_{1}+(1+q)^{-1/2}\mathcal{F}_{2}}\Lambda u.\end{split}

(B.4)

Inserting (
B.4
) into (
B.3
) with the fact
E(1,a)f=E(a,1)f=0
yields (
3.5
).

Remark B.1
.

Suppose that the nonlinearity (

),

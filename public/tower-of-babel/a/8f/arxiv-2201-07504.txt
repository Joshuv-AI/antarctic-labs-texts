Subgroup congruences for groups of prime power order
Stefanos Aivazidis, Maria Loukaki

Abstract
. Given a p p -group G G and a subgroup-closed class 𝔛 \X , we associate with each 𝔛 \X -subgroup H H certain quantities which count 𝔛 \X -subgroups containing H H subject to further properties. We show in Theorem I that each one of the said quantities is always ≡ 1 ​ ( mod ​ p ) \equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right) if and only if the same holds for the others. In Theorem II we supplement the above result by focusing on normal 𝔛 \X -subgroups and in Theorem III we obtain a sharpened version of a celebrated theorem of Burnside relative to the class of abelian groups of bounded exponent. Various other corollaries are also presented.

1.
Introduction and main results

The starting point of the work reported here was a theorem
announced by Lior Yanovski. Yanovski wrote an e-mail to the

“group-pub-forum”
online discussion group enquiring if anyone was
aware of an already existing proof for the following:

If
G
is a finite
p
-group, then the number of maximal
abelian subgroups of
G
is
\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
.

Some discussion ensued, but ultimately no one claimed to have
seen this result before. Marty Isaacs asked Yanovski to share
his proof with the other pubbers and Yanovski obliged. (This result can now be found in
[
Yan21
]
.)
Later, Isaacs went on to generalise Yanovski’s theorem
by replacing the condition
“maximal abelian subgroups”

with the stronger

“maximal abelian subgroups of exponent
\leqslant p^{k}
, where
p^{k}>2
”
. (This generalisation is the main result in
[
IY22
]
.)

The present paper is the culmination of our desire to look deeper
both into Yanovski’s method of proof by Möbius inversion
and to provide a unified framework for counting
subgroups with specified properties within
p
-groups.
In particular, we work with general classes of groups
\X
which are
subgroup-closed.
Our first main result is the following.

Theorem I
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group
and let
\X
be a subgroup-closed class of finite groups.
Then the following are equivalent:

(A)

For each
\X
-subgroup
H
of
G
,
if
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)\neq 0
then
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
.

(B)

For each
\X
-subgroup
H
of
G
we have that

\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
.

(C)

For each
\X
-subgroup
H
of
G
and for all integers
k

such that
|H|\leqslant p^{k}\leqslant\sup_{G}(H)

we have that
\mathfrak{c}_{k,G}(H)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
.

Here
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)
is the number of
\X
-subgroups
which contain
H
as a maximal subgroup and
\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)

is the number of
\X
-subgroups which are maximal subject to
containing
H
.
In general, there may exist several subgroups which are maximal
subject to containing
H
and some of these certainly may have
different orders. We write
\sup_{G}(H)
to denote the smallest
such order. Our notation
\mathfrak{c}_{k,G}(H)
stands for the
number of
\X
-subgroups which contain
H
and lie at a given level,
i.e. have fixed order
p^{k}
.

Our second main theorem acts as a supplement
to the first and reads:

Theorem II
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group
and let
\X
be a subgroup-closed class of finite groups.
The group
G
satisfies one (and thus all) of the conditions
(A)
,
(B)
,
(C)
in Theorem
I
if and only if
G
satisfies the following two conditions:

(1)

Every maximal normal
\X
-subgroup of
G
is also a maximal
\X
-subgroup of
G
.

(2)

For each normal
\X
-subgroup
H
of
G
and for all integers
k

such that
|H|\leqslant p^{k}\leqslant\nsup_{G}(H)

we have that
\mathfrak{c}_{k,G}(H)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
.

Our notation
\nsup_{G}(H)
is the appropriate analogue of
\sup_{G}(H)

for normal subgroups.
All notational conventions and details of our set-up will be reiterated
and further explained in the next section.

In the last section of our paper we demonstrate how our main theorems may be
used jointly with certain auxiliary results to recover the previously mentioned results of Yanovski and Isaacs, as well as a classical
result due to Miller which guarantees the existence of abelian subgroups
in a certain range.
Moreover, we amplify a celebrated theorem due to Burnside. In particular, our last main result,
which may reasonably be said to have some independent interest,
asserts the following.

Theorem III
.

Suppose that
G
is a group of order
p^{n}
and exponent
p^{e}
.
Let
A\leqslant G
be maximal among the normal abelian subgroups of
G

having exponent
p^{s}
, where
s\geqslant 2
.
If
|A|=p^{r}
then

n\leqslant\frac{1}{2}(r-s+1)(r-s+2e-2)+r\,,

and thus

r\geqslant-(e-s+1/2)+\sqrt{(e-s+1/2)^{2}+2n}

2.
Proofs of Main Results

We denote by
\X

\X

\X

.

Given a prime

a finite

•

\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)\coloneqq\left\lvert\{H<K\leqslant G:K\in\X\text{ and }|K:H|=p\}\right\rvert

\X

•

\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)\coloneqq\left\lvert\{H\leqslant K\leqslant G:\text{$K$ is a maximal $\X$-subgroup}\}\right\rvert

\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)
is the number of maximal
\X
-subgroups

•

\mathfrak{c}_{k,G}(H)\coloneqq\left\lvert\{H\leqslant K\leqslant G:K\in\X\text{ and }|K|=p^{k}\}\right\rvert

is the number of

\X
-subgroups of

which contain

•

\sup_{G}(H)\coloneqq\min\{|K|:H\leqslant K\text{ and $K$ is a maximal $\X$-subgroup}\}

in other words,
\sup_{G}(H)
is the
“lowest level”

\X
-subgroup of

•

\nsup_{G}(H)\coloneqq\min\{|K|:H\leqslant K\text{ and $K$ is a maximal normal $\X$-subgroup}\}

in other words,
\nsup_{G}(H)
is the
“lowest level”

at which a maximal normal
\X
-subgroup of
G
containing
H
occurs.

Before we begin with the proof of Theorem

we record some preliminary observations.

Remark 1.
For

and

as above we have
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)=\mathfrak{a}_{N}(H)
,

. This is easily seen as every

directly above

Remark 2.
It is clear that in
(A)

\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)\neq 0

since if

is
already a maximal
\X
-subgroup of

then we have that
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)=0
.

Remark 3.
If

is an
\X
-subgroup of
G

and has order

then
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)
is simply
\mathfrak{c}_{m+1,G}(H)

(which, in turn, will be

in case

is a maximal
\X
-subgroup of
G
).

Remark 4.
Assuming that
M
is a maximal
\X
-subgroup of

which contains

\sup_{G}(H)\leqslant|M|=\sup_{M}(H)
.

We are ready to prove Theorem

.

(A)

\to

)

]
.

Proof of Theorem
I
.

(A)

\to

(B)

We induce on the index
|G:H|
.
In case
H=G
we have (trivially)
\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)=1

and thus
\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
.
Observe that the exact same conclusion is reached if

H
is a maximal
\X
-subgroup of
G
,
since
\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)=1

in that case as well.
We may therefore assume that
H
is not a maximal
\X
-subgroup.
Denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of maximal
\X
-subgroups of
G
so that

\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)=\left\lvert\{H\leqslant K\leqslant G:K\in\mathcal{M}\}\right\rvert.

Now let
\mathcal{I}(G)
be the set of subgroups of
G
and

\delta_{G}:\mathcal{I}(G)\to\{0,1\}
be the indicator function
of the maximal
\X
-subgroups of
G
, i.e.

\delta_{G}(K)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}1,\text{ if }K\in\mathcal{M}\\
0,\text{ otherwise. }\end{array}\right.

Then
\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)=\sum\limits_{H\leqslant K}\delta_{G}(K)
.
Hence, by Möbius inversion, we get

(2.1)

\delta_{G}(H)=\sum_{H\leqslant K}\mu(H,K)\mathfrak{b}_{G}(K).

But
\mu(H,H)=1
, while

\mu(H,K)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(-1)^{r}p^{\binom{r}{2}},\text{ if }H\unlhd K\text{ and }K/H\cong\left(C_{p}\right)^{r}\\
0,\text{ otherwise }\end{array}\right.

(cf.
[
KT84
, Prop. 2.4]
).
Therefore equation (
2.1
) becomes

(2.2)

\delta_{G}(H)=\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)+\sum_{H<K}\mu(H,K)\mathfrak{b}_{G}(K)\equiv\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)-\sum_{|K:H|=p}\mathfrak{b}_{G}(K)\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right);

the congruence above holds true due to the fact that

\mu(H,K)\equiv 0\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
in case
|K:H|\geqslant p^{2}
.
Equation (
2.2
) can now be rewritten as

(2.3)

\delta_{G}(H)\equiv\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)-\sum_{|K:H|=p}\mathfrak{b}_{G}(K)\equiv\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|K:H|=p\\
K\in\X\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{b}_{G}(K)\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right),

where the second congruence follows from
the fact that
\mathfrak{b}_{G}(K)
vanishes if
K\not\in\X
.
We conclude that

(2.4)

\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|K:H|=p\\
K\in\X\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{b}_{G}(K)\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)

since
H\not\in\mathcal{M}
and thus
\delta_{G}(H)=0
.
Notice that the number of terms in the sum above
equals
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)
and that for each
K\in\X

such that
|K:H|=p
we have
\mathfrak{b}_{G}(K)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)

by the induction hypothesis applied to
K
.
Therefore

(2.5)

\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)\equiv\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right),

where the last congruence holds by assumption.

(B)

\to

(C)

If
|H|=p^{m}
the claim holds trivially for
\mathfrak{c}_{m,G}(H)
,
so we fix a
k
with
p^{m}<p^{k}\leqslant\sup_{G}(H)
.
We let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{1},\ldots,M_{s}\}
be the set of
maximal
\X
-subgroups of
G
which contain
H

and
\mathcal{K}=\{K_{1},\ldots,K_{t}\}
be the set of
\X
-subgroups
of
G
which contain
H
and have order
p^{k}
.
We are assuming that
s=\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)

and our goal is to prove that
t=\mathfrak{c}_{k,G}(H)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
.
Note that every subgroup in
\mathcal{M}
contains some
subgroup in
\mathcal{K}
and every subgroup in
\mathcal{K}
is
contained in some subgroup in
\mathcal{M}
.

For each
i
such that
1\leqslant i\leqslant t
,
let
b_{i}
be the number of subgroups in
\mathcal{M}

which contain
K_{i}
. Observe that
b_{i}=\mathfrak{b}_{G}(K_{i})

and thus
b_{i}\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
by assumption.
Similarly, for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
let
c_{j}

be the number of members of
\mathcal{K}
which are
contained in
M_{j}
.
Calculating the size of the set of ordered pairs

such that

in two ways yields

(2.6)

\sum_{i=1}^{t}b_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{j}.

At this point, observe that

.
Since

is an
\X
-group, for every integer

with

the quantity
\mathfrak{c}_{k,M_{j}}(H)

is simply the number of subgroups of

which have order

and contain

.
We now apply the standard result (cf.

that the number of subgroups of a

to conclude that

.

We recall that

.
Therefore the right-hand-side of (

is congruent to

and thus

(2.7)

1\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{t}b_{i}\equiv t\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right).

Since

was arbitrary, we have established the claim.

(C)

\to

(A)

In case

is a maximal

\X
-subgroup of
G
, we have
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)=0

and thus
(A)
holds.
Therefore we may assume that
\sup_{G}(H)>|H|
.
Let

.
Then

\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)=\mathfrak{c}_{m+1,G}(H)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)

and thus

is true in all cases.
Our proof is complete.

\blacksquare

second main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
II
.

Assume first that
G
satisfies
(A)
,
(B)
,
(C)
in Theorem
I
.

For the proof of
(1)
, assume that

H
is a maximal normal
\X
-subgroup
H
of
G
.
According to condition
(A)

either
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)=0
or
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
.
The latter is not possible however, since
it is easy to see that if
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)\neq 0

then
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)
is congruent
\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
to the

\X
-subgroups directly above it.
Thus if
H
is a maximal normal
\X
-subgroup of
G

it must be the case that
\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)=0

and thus

is a maximal
\X
-subgroup.

It remains to show
(2)
.
Let
H\unlhd G
with
|H|=p^{m}
. If
H
is a maximal normal
\X
-subgroup then

\mathfrak{c}_{m,G}(H)=1
and

holds trivially with

.
So we may assume that
H
is not a maximal normal
\X
-subgroup. Hence
p^{m}<\nsup_{G}(H)
. Clearly for

we have
\mathfrak{c}_{m,G}(H)=1
and the claim holds. So we fix
k
with
p^{m}<p^{k}\leqslant\nsup_{G}(H)
.

We look at the following sets of
\X
-subgroups of

that contain

(1)

\mathcal{M}=\{M_{1},\ldots,M_{t}\}
consists of
maximal normal
\X
-subgroups of order

. So if

we have

.

(2)

\mathcal{K}=\{K_{1},\ldots,K_{u}\}
consists of
non-maximal
\X
-subgroups of order
p^{k}
.

(3)

\mathcal{L}=\{L_{1},\ldots,L_{s}\}
consists of
non-normal maximal
\X
-subgroups of order
\leqslant p^{k}
. The first

of them

are of order exactly

.

(4)

\mathcal{T}=\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{w}\}
consists of
maximal
\X
-subgroups of order
>p^{k}
.

First observe that if
S
is an
\X
-subgroup of
G
that contains

then the same holds for any
G
-conjugate
S^{g}
of

. In addition

is maximal if and only if

is maximal. Hence the number

of non-normal maximal

is a multiple of

as it is a union of conjugacy classes of subgroups that are not normal. The same holds for

that contain

.

Furthermore, the definition of
\mathfrak{c}_{k,G}(H)
implies that
\mathfrak{c}_{k,G}(H)=u+t+s_{1}
. Hence the previous observation implies

(2.8)

\mathfrak{c}_{k,G}(H)=u+t+s_{1}\equiv u+t\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)

Now the total number

of maximal

-subgroups that contain

is

. In addition,

is congruent to 1
\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
, by hypothesis. Hence

(2.9)

1\equiv\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)=s+t+w\equiv t+w\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)

where the last equivalence holds because

.

Let
b_{i}=|\{T_{j}\in\mathcal{T}\mid K_{i}\subseteq T_{j}\}|

and

for all

and all

. The fact that every maximal normal

contains any of the

. So

and in addition,

\sum_{i=1}^{u}b_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{w}c_{j}.

Observe that

satisfies Condition B. Also,

. But

Hence

For the other direction, assume that

(1)
and

, that

The base case

, then since

, the claim follows by the induction

There only remains to consider the case

, i.e. when

-subgroup of

. But then the

-subgroup of

-subgroup of

forces

to be a maximal

and thus

3.
Abelian Subgroups

The existence of (maximal) normal abelian subgroups of fixed order
in a finite
p
-group
G
has been studied by several authors,
see for example
[
Gla06
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
.
In this section we wish to explore how our Theorems

can be applied to the class of abelian groups
of given order and bounded exponent.

We begin with the following.

Theorem

(
[

, Thm. 1]
)
.

Let

, where

are abelian subgroups
of a
p
-group
G
,
|B:A|=p
,
\exp(B)\leqslant p^{k}

and

\mathcal{A}
be the set of all

,

This theorem of Berkovich says, essentially, that if

,

,
G
is a finite
p
-group
and

is a subgroup of

\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)=0

\mathfrak{a}_{G}(H)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)

p
-groups

\leqslant p^{k}

Since
(A)

\X
of finite abelian

-groups of exponent
\leqslant p^{k}

for some fixed choice of

), we see that
(B)
holds as well and thus
we recover the main result in the recent paper of Isaacs and Yanovski.

Corollary I

(
[
IY22
, Thm. C]
)
.

Let
P
be a
p
-group, and suppose that
e>2
is a power of
p
.
Also, let
H\leqslant P
be an abelian subgroup with exponent dividing
e
,
and let
n
be the number of subgroups
A
of
P
that contain
H

and that are maximal with respect to the property
that
A
is abelian and has exponent dividing
e
.
Then
n\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
.

Moreover, given a finite
p
-group
G
of exponent
p^{k}

we let
\X
be the class of finite abelian
p
-groups
of exponent
\leqslant p^{k}
.
Then
(A)
in Theorem
I
holds for the
class
\X
and thus
(B)
holds.
The maximal
\X
-subgroups which contain a given
\X
-subgroup

H
of
G
are then simply the maximal abelian subgroups
of
G
containing
H
. The cardinality of the first set
is our quantity
\mathfrak{b}_{G}(H)\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)

and thus we recover Yanovski’s result mentioned
in the introduction.

A celebrated theorem due to Burnside asserts that
if
G
is a
p
-group of order
p^{n}
and
A
is a maximal
abelian normal subgroup of
G
, where
|A|=p^{s}
, then

n\leqslant\frac{s(s+1)}{2}
.
Since this holds for all maximal abelian normal subgroups of
G
,
we see that
\nsup_{G}(1)\geqslant p^{s}
with
s

the least positive integer for which
n\leqslant\frac{s(s+1)}{2}
holds
relative to the class of finite abelian groups
\X=\mathfrak{A}
.

Now every finite
p
-group satisfies Theorem
I

with
\X=\mathfrak{A}
and thus every finite
p
-group
G

satisfies Theorem
II
.
If
k\leqslant s
therefore, then
k
lies in the range
for which
(2)
of Theorem
II
works.
But

\frac{k(k-1)}{2}\leqslant\frac{s(s-1)}{2}<n

where the last inequality is a consequence of the minimality of
s
.
We deduce that
\binom{k}{2}<n
and thus we recover the following classic result due to Miller.

Corollary II

(
[
Ber08
, Thm. 13.12]
)
.

Suppose that
G
is a group of order
p^{n}
,

n,k\in\mathbb{N}
and
n>\binom{k}{2}
.
Then the number of abelian subgroups of order
p^{k}
in
G

is congruent to
1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
.

The next theorem provides an analogue of Burnside’s theorem
for maximal abelian normal subgroups of given exponent.

In order to prove the theorem, we need the following auxiliary result
which is essentially Lemma 1 in
[
Laf80
]
.
We follow the notation in
[
Laf80
]
and write

\Omega(G)=\begin{cases}\Omega_{2}(G),\text{\quad if }p=2\\
\Omega_{1}(G),\text{\quad if }p>2\end{cases}

for any
p
-group
G
.
We also write
d(G)
for the minimum number of generators of
G
.

Lemma I
.

Let
C
be a
p
-group of exponent
\leqslant p^{e}
.
Assume further that
\Omega_{s}(C)\leqslant\zent(C)
.
Then

|C|/|\Omega_{s}(C)|\leqslant|\Omega_{1}(C)|^{e-s}

for every integer
s
with
p^{s}>2
.

Proof.

First note that
\Omega(C)\leqslant\Omega_{s}(C)\leqslant Z(C)

for every integer
s
with
p^{s}>2
.
Hence Corollary 1 of Lemma 1 in
[
Laf74
]
implies that

\Omega_{i}(C)=\{x\in C\mid x^{p^{i}}=1\}
for every
i\geqslant 0
.
Therefore
\Omega_{i+1}(C)/\Omega_{i}(C)

is an elementary abelian group for all
i\geqslant 1
.
So

\left\lvert\Omega_{i+1}(C)/\Omega_{i}(C)\right\rvert\leqslant|\Omega_{i+1}(C)/\fratt(\Omega_{i+1}(C))|\leqslant d(\Omega_{i+1}(C)).

In addition, for every
i\geqslant 1
, we have

\Omega(\Omega_{i}(C))\leqslant\Omega(C)\leqslant\Omega_{s}(C)\leqslant\zent(C).

Applying Corollary 2 in
[
Laf74
]
on
\Omega_{i}(C)
we get

d(\Omega_{i}(C))\leqslant d(\Omega_{1}(\Omega_{i}(C))=d(\Omega_{1}(C)),

where the last equality follows from the fact that

\Omega_{1}(\Omega_{i}(C))=\Omega_{1}(C)
.

Now notice that if
d(\Omega_{1}(C))=t
then
|\Omega_{1}(C)|=p^{t}
.
Hence for every
i\geqslant s
we get
|\Omega_{i+1}(C)/\Omega_{i}(C)|\leqslant p^{t}
.
As
C=\Omega_{e}(C)
we need to repeat the argument
(e-s)
times to get

|C|/|\Omega_{s}(C)|\leqslant p^{t(e-s)}
, and the result follows.

\blacksquare

We are now ready to prove our last
main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
III
.

We denote by
d=d(A)
the rank of the subgroup
A
and begin with
the observation that
d\leqslant r-s+1
.
This is so because

A=B\times D
for some subgroups
B
,
D
of
A
,
where
B
is cyclic of order
p^{s}
.
Thus

d(A)=d(D)+1\leqslant r-s+1,

the inequality being a consequence of
p^{d(D)}\leqslant|D|
.

Our strategy will be to write

|G|=|G:C||C:A||A|,

where
C\coloneqq\cent_{G}(A)
,
and to obtain bounds for the indices involved.
As regards the quantity
|G:C|
, the
N/C
Theorem
tells us that
|G:C|\leqslant\left\lvert\aut(A)\right\rvert_{p}
,
the
p
-part of
\left\lvert\aut(A)\right\rvert
.
But

\left\lvert\aut(A)\right\rvert_{p}\leqslant p^{\frac{1}{2}d(2r-d-1)}\leqslant p^{\frac{1}{2}(r-s+1)[2r-(r-s+1)-1]}=p^{\frac{1}{2}(r-s+1)(r+s-2)}.

The first inequality follows from the fact that

\left\lvert\aut(A)\right\rvert
divides

p^{d(r-d)}\left\lvert\gl_{d}(p)\right\rvert

(cf.
[
Hup67
, Satz 3.19]
).
To see why the second inequality is true,
let
f(x)=\frac{1}{2}x(2r-x-1)
and note that

f(d)-f(r-s+1)=\frac{1}{2}\overbrace{(2+d-r-s)}^{\leqslant 0}\overbrace{(r-s+1-d)}^{\geqslant 0}\leqslant 0\,;

we have
2+d-r-s\leqslant 0
since
2\leqslant s
and

d\leqslant r
, while
r-s+1-d\geqslant 0
by our initial observation.

Regarding the quantity
|C:A|
, we use Lemma
I

to bound it.
By a theorem of Alperin
[
Alp64
]

\Omega_{s}(C)=A

and thus
|C:A|\leqslant|\Omega_{1}(C)|^{e-s}

by Lemma
I
.
But

|\Omega_{1}(C)|=p^{d(\Omega_{1}(C))}\leqslant p^{d(A)}\leqslant p^{r-s+1}.

The equality is a consequence of the fact that

\Omega_{1}(C)\leqslant\Omega_{s}(C)=A\leqslant\zent(C).

The first inequality follows from the containment

\Omega_{1}(C)\leqslant\Omega_{s}(C)=A
and the fact that
A

is abelian.
Thus

|C:A|\leqslant\left(p^{r-s+1}\right)^{e-s}=p^{(e-s)(r-s+1)}.

Then

(3.1)

\displaystyle|G|=|G:C||C:A||A|

\displaystyle\leqslant p^{\frac{1}{2}(r-s+1)(r+s-2)}p^{(e-s)(r-s+1)}p^{r}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=p^{\frac{1}{2}(r-s+1)(r-s+2e-2)+r},

which is clearly equivalent to what we wanted to prove.

Now, the inequality

2n\leqslant r^{2}+r(2e-2s+1)+(s-1)(s-2e+2).

along with the fact that for every
e\geqslant 2
we get
(s-1)(s-2e+2)\leqslant 0

(since
s\leqslant e\leqslant 2e-2
) implies

2n\leqslant r^{2}+r(2e-2s+1).

Solving the quadratic for
r
yields

r\geqslant-(e-s+1/2)+\sqrt{(e-s+1/2)^{2}+2n}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.

\blacksquare

In Theorem
III
we have not addressed the case
s=1

which corresponds to the class of elementary abelian subgroups of a
p
-group.
But this case has already been handled by Laffey in
[
Laf80
]
.
The main result of that paper is the following.

Theorem

(
[
Laf80
, Thm. 1]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group,
and suppose that
|G|=p^{n}
,
\exp(G)=p^{e}
and
p^{r}=\min\{|A|\}
,
where the minimum is taken over
all maximal elementary abelian normal subgroups
A
of
G
.
Then

n\leqslant\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2}r(3r-1)+er,&\text{ if }p=2,\\
&\frac{1}{2}r(r-1)+er,&\text{ if }p>2.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

The quantity
p^{r}
in Laffey’s result is precisely our
\nsup_{G}(1)

relative to the class
\mathfrak{Y}
of elementary abelian
p
-groups.
Moreover, Laffey’s inequality implies that (relative to the class
\mathfrak{Y}
)

\nsup_{G}(1)=r\geqslant\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -y+&\sqrt{y^{2}+\frac{2n}{3}}&\text{ if }p=2,\\
-y+&\sqrt{y^{2}+2n}&\text{ if }p>2\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where

y=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{6}(2e-1)\text{ if }p=2\\
\frac{1}{2}(2e-1)\text{ if }p>2.\end{cases}

Thus we can apply Theorem
II

(2)
to the class
\mathfrak{Y}
(of elementary abelian subgroups) when
H=1
to deduce the following.

Corollary III
.

Suppose that
G
is a group of order
p^{n}
and exponent
p^{e}
, while
y
is the function of
e
defined above.
Then for all
1\leqslant k
such that

k\leqslant\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -y+&\sqrt{y^{2}+\frac{2n}{3}}&\text{ if }p=2,\\
-y+&\sqrt{y^{2}+2n}&\text{ if }p>2\end{aligned}\end{cases}

the number of elementary abelian subgroups of order
p^{k}
is
\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
. In particular, there exists at least one
elementary abelian normal subgroup of order
p^{k}
for all such
k
.

Additionally, solving the inequality

-y+\sqrt{y^{2}+2n}\geqslant m

allows one to conclude that a finite
p
-group
G
of odd order

p^{n}
and exponent
p^{e}
is guaranteed to have an elementary abelian normal subgroup
of order
p^{k}
for all
k\leqslant m
provided that

e\leqslant\frac{n}{m}-\frac{m-1}{2}

holds.
Compare the corollary above with Theorems 6 and 7 in
[
Ber98
]
.

\X

.

“converse”
true?

Suppose that
\X
enjoys the following

-groups

, if a subgroup is

\X
-subgroup of

\X
-subgroup of

.

-group

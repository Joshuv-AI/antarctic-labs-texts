Convergence of bi-spatial pullback random attractors and stochastic Liouville type equations for nonautonomous stochastic p p -Laplacian lattice system


Abstract
We consider convergence properties of the long-term behaviors with respect to the coefficient of the stochastic term for a nonautonomous stochastic p p -Laplacian lattice equation with multiplicative noise. First, the upper semi-continuity of pullback random ( ℓ 2 , ℓ q ) (\ell^{2},\ell^{q}) -attractor is proved for each q ∈ [ 1 , + ∞ ) q\in[1,+\infty) . Then, a convergence result of the time-dependent invariant sample Borel probability measures is obtained in ℓ 2 \ell^{2} . Next, we show that the invariant sample measures satisfy a stochastic Liouville type equation and a termwise convergence of the stochastic Liouville type equations is verified. Furthermore, each family of the invariant sample measures is turned out to be a sample statistical solution, which hence also fulfills a convergence consequence. Keywords: Stochastic p p -Laplacian lattice system; convergence of bi-spatial attractors; convergence of invariant measures; stochastic Liouville type theorem; sample statistical solutions. \displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u),\Psi^{\prime}(u)\rangle\mu^{\alpha}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\varsigma+\alpha\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}(u,\Psi^{\prime}(u))\mu^{\alpha}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\tilde{W}(\varsigma)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(u)(u,u)\mu^{\alpha}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\varsigma,

.
In this part, the compactness of the
(\ell^{2},\ell^{q})
-attractor in

Note that the stochastic Liouville type equation depends on the parameter
\alpha
.
The weak convergence of invariant sample measures
\mu^{\alpha}_{\tau,\omega}

also implies convergence of each integral in the left side of the equation (
1.4
).
This actually has asserted the convergence of the stochastic Liouville type equation
(
1.4

In fact, however, the termwise convergence of (
1.4
) as

and deserves more attention.
We will thereby consider and prove the termwise convergence of
the stochastic Liouville type equations.

Let
\{\mu^{\alpha}_{\tau,\omega}\}_{(\tau,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega}
be the invariant sample measures constructed
in
[
27

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
In order to prove the termwise convergence of the stochastic Liouville type equation
with respect to
\alpha

one needs to further guarantee the convergence in

and of the stochastic integrals
of the
\mu^{\alpha}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}

The convergence in

follows from Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem,
and indeed from continuity of the solution
(t,\phi)\mapsto u_{\alpha}(t,\tau,\omega,\phi)

and compactness of the pullback random attractors.
Comparatively, the convergence of the stochastic integrals is not a natural consequence.
However, the convergence of other terms in the (stochastic Liouville type) equation
can well assure the convergence of the stochastic integral terms.

Statistical solutions were defined to describe the time evolution of
the probability distribution functions associated with the fluid flow (
[
9
]
).
A significant feature of a statistical solution is its basis,
the Liouville type equation discussed above.
In
[
4

Afterwards, Wang, Zhu and Li not only developed the stochastic Liouville type equation,
but also gave a reasonable definition of

corresponding to the stochastic Liouville type equation in
[
33
]
.
In this article, we follow the definition of sample statistical solution
and prove that each family of invariant sample measures is
a sample statistical solution.
And then the convergence result of sample statistical solutions can be derived
from the convergence properties of invariant sample measures
stochastic Liouville type equations with a supplementary argument for the convergence
in
{\mathcal{C}}([s,t])
with
s\leqslant t
by Arzelà-Ascoli theorem.

Section

-pullback

In Section

For metric spaces

Let

For the case when

(u,v):=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{i}v_{i}\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for all }u=(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},\,v=(v_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}

Generally, we conventionally denote

(u,v):=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{i}v_{i}\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for all }u=(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},\,v=(v_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}

We have the following embedding

1\leqslant p\leqslant q\leqslant\infty\hskip 11.38092pt\Rightarrow\hskip 11.38092pt\ell^{p}\subset\ell^{q}\mbox{ and }\|u\|_{q}\leqslant\|u\|_{p}\mbox{ for }u\in\ell^{p}.

(2.1)

For notational convenience in the sequel,
given each

,
p\geqslant 1
and

,
we use

,

and

According to this, it is easy to see that

.
We also set

to be

Let

,

.
By (

(u,|v|^{r})=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{i}|v_{i}|^{r}\leqslant\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|u_{i}|\|v\|_{\infty}^{r}\leqslant\|u\|_{1}\|v\|_{q}^{r}\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for }p=1,\,q\geqslant 1,\,r\geqslant 0.

(2.2)

Define a certain multiplication

(u\otimes v)_{i}:=u_{i}v_{i}\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for all }u=(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},\,v=(v_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Define also the self-mappings

on the spaces of
bi-infinite sequences respectively as

(Bu)_{i}:=u_{i+1}-u_{i},\hskip 22.76228pt(B^{*}u)_{i}:=u_{i-1}-u_{i}\hskip 22.76228pt\mbox{with}\hskip 22.76228ptAu:=B^{*}(|Bu|^{p-2}\otimes(Bu))

It is obvious that

,

(2.3)

(Au,u)=\|Au\|\|u\|=2^{p}\|u\|^{p-1}\|u\|\leqslant 2^{p}\|u\|^{p}.

Then (

) can be rewritten as

{\rm d}u(t)=[-\nu(t)Au+f(t,u)-\lambda u]{\rm d}t+\alpha u{\rm d}W(t),\;t>\tau,\hskip 11.38092ptu(\tau)=u_{\tau}\in\ell^{2},

where

The Wiener process

is determined as follows.

Let

\lim_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{z(\theta_{t}\omega)}{t}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{t}z(\theta_{s}\omega){\rm d}s}{t}=0.

(2.5)

Hence we only consider the set

and define the Wiener process

2.2
The nonautonomous random dynamical system

We now present the already-known results in
[

]
here for the following discussion.
Let

3
Convergence of pullback random attractors

3.1
Preliminaries on pullback random attractors

Let
X
be a Polish space and
2^{X}
be the collection of all subsets of
X
.
An
X
-valued time-parametrized random variable
R(\tau,\omega)
is called
a
nonautonomous random variable
in
X
.
A
nonautonomous random set

D
in
X
is a family of nonempty
subsets
D(\tau,\omega)\in 2^{X}
with two parameters

(\tau,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega
, which is
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{P})
-measurable (
{\mathcal{F}}
-measurable for short)
with respect to
\omega\in\Omega
(see
[
5
,
25
]
),
i.e., the mapping
\omega\mapsto{\rm d}_{X}(x,D(\tau,\omega))
is
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{R}))
-measurable for each fixed

(x,\tau)\in X\times\mathbb{R}
, where
{\rm d}_{X}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the metric induced by norm of
X
.

In order to present the concept of bi-spatial pullback attractors for an NRDS,
we consider two separable Banach spaces
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
and
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
.
An NRDS
\varphi
on
X
is assumed to take its values in the terminate space

Y
, i.e.,

\varphi(t,\tau,\omega,X)\subset Y,\;\mbox{for all }t,\,\tau\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ with }t>0\mbox{ and }\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.s. }\omega\in\Omega.

For convergence uniformity, we assume the space pair
(X,Y)
is
limit-identical

in the following sense,

x_{n}\in X\cap Y,\hskip 11.38092pt\|x_{n}-x_{0}\|_{X}\rightarrow 0\mbox{ and }\|x_{n}-y_{0}\|_{Y}\rightarrow 0\mbox{ imply }x_{0}=y_{0}\in X\cap Y.

The norm of
X\cap Y
is often defined as
\|x\|_{X\cap Y}=\|x\|_{X}+\|x\|_{Y}
for all

x\in X\cap Y
, which makes
(X\cap Y,\|\cdot\|_{X\cap Y})
a normed linear space.
It was proved in
[
20
]
that,
(X\cap Y,\|\cdot\|_{X\cap Y})
is a Banach space
if and only if
(X,Y)
is a limit-identical pair.
Moreover, when
(X,Y)
is limit-identical and
A\subset X\cap Y
,
A
is compact in

X\cap Y
if and only if
A
is compact in
X
and
Y
, respectively.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}=\{{\mathcal{A}}(\tau,\omega)\}_{(\tau,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega}
be a nonautonomous random set in
X\cap Y

and
D=\{D(\tau,\omega)\}_{(\tau,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega}
be a nonautonomous random set in
X
.
We say that
A

pullback attracts
in (the topology of)
X
if

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm dist}_{X}(\varphi(t,\tau-t,\theta_{-t}\omega,D(\tau-t,\theta_{-t}\omega)),{\mathcal{A}}(\tau,\omega))=0,

(3.1)

where
{\rm dist}_{X}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the
Hausdorff semi-distance

under the norm of an arbitrary normed space
X
, i.e., for two nonempty sets
A,B\subset X
,

{\rm dist}_{X}(A,B):=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\|a-b\|_{X}.

Here the pullback attraction can also be taken in (the topology of)
X\cap Y
instead
with
X
in (
3.1
) replaced by
X\cap Y
.

We say
{\mathcal{D}}
is a
universe
in
X
, if
{\mathcal{D}}
is
an inclusion-closed collection of nonautonomous
random sets, i.e., when
D\in{\mathcal{D}}
and a nonautonomous random set
D^{\prime}
satisfies

D^{\prime}(\tau,\omega)\subset D(\tau,\omega)
for each
(\tau,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega
, then
D^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}
.

In this article, we adopt the much weaker definitions of bi-spatial pullback (random) attractors
presented in
[
6
,
27
]
as follows, where we do not require the attractor
to be
{\mathcal{F}}
-measurable
in the initial phase space.
The existence theorem of pullback random bi-spatial attractors can be found in
[
27
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\varphi
be an NRDS on

taking its values in

and

be a universe in

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\psi_{3i}(\varsigma)|u_{i}|^{q-1}+\psi_{4i}(\varsigma)\right)|\Psi^{\prime}_{i}(u)|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(2^{p}\nu_{0}\|u\|^{p-1}+\lambda\|u\|+\|\psi_{4}(\varsigma)\|_{1}\right)\|\Psi^{\prime}(u)\|_{V}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{3i}(\varsigma)\left(\frac{q-1}{q}|u_{i}|^{q}+\frac{1}{q}|\Psi^{\prime}(u)|^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(2^{p}\nu_{0}\|u\|^{p-1}+\lambda\|u\|+\|\psi_{4}(\varsigma)\|_{1}\right)\|\Psi^{\prime}(u)\|_{V}

\displaystyle+\frac{q-1}{q}\|\psi_{3}(\varsigma)\|_{1}\|u\|^{q}+\frac{1}{q}\|\psi_{3}(\varsigma)\|_{1}\|\Psi^{\prime}(u)\|_{V}^{q}.

(5.12)

Now we consider an arbitrary interval
[s,t]\subset\mathbb{R}
.
By (2) of the definition of
{\mathcal{T}}
, we know
\|\Psi^{\prime}(u)\|_{V}
is bounded;
by the compactness of
{\mathcal{U}}_{\alpha}[s,t]
, we also know that
\|u\|
is bounded
on
{\mathcal{U}}_{\alpha}[s,t]
.
Hence we have a positive constant
{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}
independent of
\varsigma
such that

\left|\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u),\Psi^{\prime}(u)\rangle\right|\leqslant{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}(1+\|\psi_{3}(\varsigma)\|_{1}+\|\psi_{4}(\varsigma)\|_{1})

and then

\displaystyle\left|\int_{H}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u),\Psi^{\prime}(u)\rangle\mu^{\alpha}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-t}\omega}({\rm d}u)\right|=\left|\int_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha_{n}}(\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-t}\omega)}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u),\Psi^{\prime}(u)\rangle\mu^{\alpha}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-t}\omega}({\rm d}u)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}(1+\|\psi_{3}(\varsigma)\|_{1}+\|\psi_{4}(\varsigma)\|_{1}),

(5.13)

which ensures that the first mapping in (
5.9
) belongs to
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})

by the assumption
(F3)
.

The continuity of the remaining two mappings in (
5.8
) is obvious,
as long as we notice that
\Phi^{\prime}(u)
is continuous from
{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(t,\theta_{t-\tau}\omega)

to
H
and so is
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(u)
from
{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(t,\theta_{t-\tau}\omega)

to
L(H\times H,\mathbb{R})
by the definition of the class
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Next, we recall the definition of
{\mathcal{T}}
that
\Psi^{\prime}(u)
is bounded from
V
to
V

and
\Psi^{\prime\prime}(u)
is bounded from
V
to
L(H\times H,\mathbb{R})
.
By the embedding

, we also know that
\Psi^{\prime}(u)
is bounded in
H
.
Combining the boundedness of
\|u\|
on the compact set
{\mathcal{U}}_{\alpha}[s,t]
, one can directly
see that
|(u,\Psi^{\prime}(u))|

Then similar to the analysis in (
5.13
), we can obtain that the second mapping
in (

) belongs to

and the mapping (
5.10
)

The proof is hence accomplished.∎

Remark 5.3

Here the requirement that the integrand of the stochastic integral to

Now we are well prepared to show the stochastic Liouville type theorem.

Theorem 5.4

Suppose that the assumptions
(F1)
,
(F2)

Proof.

Based on the definition of the class

\displaystyle\int_{H}\Psi(\phi)\mu^{\alpha}_{t,\theta_{t-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}\phi)-\int_{H}\Psi(\phi)\mu^{\alpha}_{s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}\phi)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(t,\theta_{t-\tau}\omega)}\Psi(\phi)\mu^{\alpha}_{t,\theta_{t-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}\phi)-\int_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega)}\Psi(\phi)\mu^{\alpha}_{s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}\phi)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega)}\left(\Psi(u_{\alpha}(t,s,\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\phi))-\Psi(\phi)\right)\mu^{\alpha}_{s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}\phi)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega)}\int_{s}^{t}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u_{\alpha}(\varsigma)),\Psi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}(\varsigma))\rangle{\rm d}\varsigma\cdot\mu^{\alpha}_{s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}\phi)

\displaystyle+\alpha\int_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega)}\int_{s}^{t}\left(u_{\alpha}(\varsigma),\Psi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}(\varsigma))\right){\rm d}\tilde{W}(\varsigma)\cdot\mu^{\alpha}_{s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}\phi)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\int_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega)}\int_{s}^{t}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(u_{\alpha}(\varsigma))\left(u_{\alpha}(\varsigma),u_{\alpha}(\varsigma)\right){\rm d}\varsigma\cdot\mu^{\alpha}_{s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}\phi)

\displaystyle M_{1}+M_{2}+M_{3},

where

the stochastic datum
\theta_{-\tau}\omega

to

\displaystyle M_{1}=

\displaystyle\LIM_{\tau^{\prime}\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{1}{s-\tau^{\prime}}\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\int_{H}\int_{s}^{t}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u_{\alpha}(\varsigma,\sigma,\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\phi)),\Psi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}(\varsigma,\sigma,\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\phi))\rangle{\rm d}\varsigma\cdot\mu^{\alpha}_{\sigma,\theta_{\sigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}\phi){\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\LIM_{\tau^{\prime}\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{1}{s-\tau^{\prime}}\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u_{\alpha}(\varsigma,\sigma,\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\phi)),\Psi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}(\varsigma,\sigma,\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\phi))\rangle\mu^{\alpha}_{\sigma,\theta_{\sigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}\phi){\rm d}\varsigma{\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle\LIM_{\tau^{\prime}\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{1}{s-\tau^{\prime}}\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u),\Psi^{\prime}(u)\rangle\mu^{\alpha}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\varsigma{\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u),\Psi^{\prime}(u)\rangle\mu^{\alpha}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\varsigma,

where we have replaced

\varsigma-\sigma

\sigma

\displaystyle M_{2}=

\displaystyle\alpha\LIM_{\tau^{\prime}\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{1}{s-\tau^{\prime}}\!\!\!\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\!\!\!\int_{H}\!\!\int_{s}^{t}\left(u_{\alpha}(\varsigma,\sigma,\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\phi),\Psi^{\prime}(u_{\alpha}(\varsigma,\sigma,\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\phi))\right){\rm d}\tilde{W}(\varsigma)\cdot\mu^{\alpha}_{\sigma,\theta_{\sigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}\phi){\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}\left(u,\Psi^{\prime}(u)\right)\mu^{\alpha}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\tilde{W}(\varsigma)\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{and}

(5.17)

M_{3}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(u)\left(u,u\right)\mu^{\alpha}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\varsigma.

Then the stochastic Liouville type equation (

5.2
Convergence of stochastic Liouville type equation

Since the stochastic Liouville type equation relies on the coefficient
\alpha
of
the stochastic term, it is also an interesting problem to study what will happen
when a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\subset\mathbb{R}
tends to some number
\alpha_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}
.
Actually, in Theorem
4.4
, we have obtained the weak convergence of a subsequence
of
\{\mu^{\alpha_{n}}_{\tau,\omega}\}_{(\tau,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega}
for a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
in
\mathbb{R}

with
\alpha_{n}\rightarrow\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Following this result, one can further deduce the following theorem.

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose that the assumptions
(F1)
,
(F2)
,

(F3)
,
(F4)
and
(F5)
hold for
f
.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}
be the pullback random
(\ell^{2},\ell^{q})
-attractor of
\varphi_{\alpha}

with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
given in Theorem
3.6
and

\{\mu^{\alpha}_{\tau,\omega}\}_{(\tau,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega}
be the invariant sample measures constructed in
Theorem
4.2
.
Then for each
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
, each convergent sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}

in
\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha_{n}\rightarrow\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
there exists a subsequence
\{\alpha_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
of
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}

depending on
\tau
and
\omega
,
and an invariant sample measure
\mu_{\tau,\omega}^{\alpha_{0}}
of
\varphi_{\alpha_{0}}
such that
for each
\Psi\in{\mathcal{T}}
and
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with
s\leqslant t
,
the stochastic Liouville type equation

\displaystyle\int_{H}\Psi(u)\mu^{\alpha_{n_{k}}}_{t,\theta_{t-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u)-\int_{H}\Psi(u)\mu^{\alpha_{n_{k}}}_{s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u),\Psi^{\prime}(u)\rangle\mu^{\alpha_{n_{k}}}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\varsigma+\alpha_{n_{k}}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}(u,\Psi^{\prime}(u))\mu^{\alpha_{n_{k}}}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\tilde{W}(\varsigma)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha_{n_{k}}^{2}}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(u)(u,u)\mu^{\alpha_{n_{k}}}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\varsigma

(5.19)

converges termwise to the following stochastic Liouville type equation

\displaystyle\int_{H}\Psi(u)\mu^{\alpha_{0}}_{t,\theta_{t-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u)-\int_{H}\Psi(u)\mu^{\alpha_{0}}_{s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u),\Psi^{\prime}(u)\rangle\mu^{\alpha_{0}}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\varsigma+\alpha_{0}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}(u,\Psi^{\prime}(u))\mu^{\alpha_{0}}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\tilde{W}(\varsigma)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha_{0}^{2}}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{H}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(u)(u,u)\mu^{\alpha_{0}}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u){\rm d}\varsigma.

(5.20)

To prove Theorem
5.5
, we still need some auxiliary results.

Lemma 5.6
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
5.5
,
for each
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
,
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with
s\leqslant t

and a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
with
\alpha_{n}\rightarrow\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
,
the set

{\mathcal{U}}[s,t]:=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{U}}_{\alpha_{n}}[s,t]

is precompact in
\ell^{2}
, where
{\mathcal{U}}_{\alpha}[s,t]
is defined in (
5.5
).

Proof.

We pick a sequence
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
in
{\mathcal{U}}[s,t]

arbitrarily with
\phi_{k}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}(\sigma_{k},\theta_{\sigma_{k}-\tau}\omega)
,
and it suffices to show that this sequence has a convergent subsequence
with its limit in
\ell^{2}
.

Similar to the proof of Lemma
5.1
, we assume that
\sigma_{k}\rightarrow\sigma_{0}\in[s,t]
as
k\rightarrow\infty

and take

\sigma_{*}=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sigma_{k}\in[s,t].

Then we have

\tilde{\phi}_{k}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}(\sigma_{*},\theta_{\sigma_{*}-\tau}\omega)
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\phi_{k}=u_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}(\sigma_{k},\sigma_{*},\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\tilde{\phi}_{k}).

Since
\tilde{\phi}_{k}\in\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}(\sigma_{*},\theta_{\sigma_{*}-\tau}\omega)
,
which is precompact in
\ell^{2}
(by Theorem
3.5
and Lemma
3.11
),
we can also assume that

\tilde{\phi}_{k}\rightarrow\phi_{*},\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{as }n\rightarrow\infty.

(5.21)

Recalling the condition (4) of a continuous NRDS in Section
2

(or
[
27
, Theorem 2.5]
) and the uniform continuity in Lemma
3.8
,
we can infer from
\alpha_{n_{k}}\rightarrow\alpha_{0}
,
\sigma_{k}\rightarrow\sigma_{0}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{k}\rightarrow\phi_{*}
that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{k}-u_{\alpha_{0}}(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{*},\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\phi_{*})\|\leqslant

\displaystyle\|u_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}(\sigma_{k},\sigma_{*},\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\tilde{\phi}_{k})-u_{\alpha_{0}}(\sigma_{k},\sigma_{*},\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\tilde{\phi}_{k})\|

\displaystyle+\|u_{\alpha_{0}}(\sigma_{k},\sigma_{*},\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\tilde{\phi}_{k})-u_{\alpha_{0}}(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{*},\theta_{-\tau}\omega,\phi_{*})\|\rightarrow 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
We have proved the conclusion.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
5.5
,
for each convergent sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
in
\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha_{n}\rightarrow\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
and
\Psi\in{\mathcal{T}}
, the family (
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
) of mappings

\varsigma\mapsto\int_{H}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u),\Psi^{\prime}(u)\rangle\mu^{\alpha_{n}}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u)

(5.22)

is dominated by a Lebesgue integrable function over
[s,t]
.

Proof.

By the compactness of
\overline{{\mathcal{U}}[s,t]}
by Lemma
5.6
,
we know that
\|u\|
is bounded on
\overline{{\mathcal{U}}[s,t]}
.
Hence by (
5.12
), we have a positive constant
{\mathscr{M}}
independent of
s
and
n
such that

\left|\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u),\Psi^{\prime}(u)\rangle\right|\leqslant{\mathscr{M}}(1+\|\psi_{3}(\varsigma)\|_{1}+\|\psi_{4}(\varsigma)\|_{1})

and then

\displaystyle\left|\int_{H}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u),\Psi^{\prime}(u)\rangle\mu^{\alpha_{n}}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u)\right|=\left|\int_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha_{n}}(\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega)}\langle\tilde{F}(\varsigma,u),\Psi^{\prime}(u)\rangle\mu^{\alpha_{n}}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u)\right|

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}(1+\|\psi_{3}(\varsigma)\|_{1}+\|\psi_{4}(\varsigma)\|_{1}),

which is Lebesgue integrable over

5.23
).
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
5.5
,
for each convergent sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
in
\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha_{n}\rightarrow\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
and
\Psi\in{\mathcal{T}}
, the family (
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
) of mappings

\varsigma\mapsto\int_{H}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(u)(u,u)\mu^{\alpha_{n}}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u)

.

Indeed by the condition (3) of the definition of
{\mathcal{T}}
, we can easily see that
for all

,

and

\left|\int_{H}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(u)(u,u)\mu^{\alpha_{n}}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u)\right|=\left|\int_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha_{n}}(\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega)}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(u)(u,u)\mu^{\alpha_{n}}_{\varsigma,\theta_{\varsigma-\tau}\omega}({\rm d}u)\right|\leqslant{\mathscr{N}}.

∎

Now we are ready to show Theorem
5.5
as follows.

Proof of Theorem
5.5
.

By Theorem
4.4
, we have known that for each
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\omega\in\Omega

and each convergent sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}

) and (

Remark 5.9

6

In

(in

\mathbb{P}

(2)

For almost all

\mu^{\alpha}_{s,\theta_{s-\tau}\omega}
-integrable for every
\phi\in V
and bilinear mapping

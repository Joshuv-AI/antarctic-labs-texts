Validity and failure of the integral representation of Γ \Gamma -limits of convex non-local functionals
Andrea Braides, Gianni Dal Maso

Abstract
We prove an integral-representation result for limits of non-local quadratic forms on H 0 1 ​ ( Ω ) H^{1}_{0}(\Omega) , with Ω \Omega a bounded open subset of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , extending the representation on C c ∞ ​ ( Ω ) C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega) given by the Beurling-Deny formula in the theory of Dirichlet forms. Introduction

In this paper we continue our investigation on functionals defined on Sobolev spaces in which a non-local part, in the form of a double integral, is present beside a usual local part depending on the gradient. In general this question can be formulated as the characterization of limits of functionals of the form

F_{k}(u)=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}f_{k}(u(x)-u(y))d\mu_{k}(x,y)+\int_{\Omega}g_{k}(x,\nabla u(x))\,dx,

defined in some Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with
p>1
. Different types of stability of such a class can be studied: in
[
2
]
we have given a notion of convergence on measures
\mu_{k}
that guarantees the separate stability of the integrals on
\Omega\times\Omega
and on
\Omega
, while in
[
3
]
we have explored conditions under which a limit of a sequence of such functionals may still be of this form, but the integrands of the limit are determined by the interaction between the local and non-local terms. The theory of Dirichlet forms
[
6
]
gives the stability of such a class under the only condition that
f_{k}
and
g_{k}
be quadratic, with very mild conditions on the measures
\mu_{k}
(see
[
9
]
).

In this paper we analyze the properties of the
\Gamma
-limits of these quadratic functionals, and show that, rather surprisingly, the same stability property does not hold if we consider only a slight variation of quadratic forms; namely, when all integrands are
p
-th powers with
p\neq 2
. Previous examples of lack of stability were known in the case of relaxation results for non-convex functionals
F
, where the local term is dropped (or, equivalently, if and
f_{k}=f
is not convex and
g_{k}\equiv 0
). In that case, the lower-semicontinuous envelope in the weak
L^{p}
topology of a double integral can be non-representable in the same form
[
1
,
7
,
8
]
. Our counterexample shows that a similar issue arises even for sequences of convex and equicoercive functionals in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

In this paper, we first show a result connected to the theory of Dirichlet forms.
If we consider quadratic forms defined in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
of the type

\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}a_{k}(x,y)\,dxdy+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx,

(1.1)

with
\Omega
a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
a_{k}
positive functions equibounded in
L^{1}(\Omega\times\Omega)
and not concentrating on the boundary, we prove that the corresponding
\Gamma
-limit, in the weak topology of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, can be written on functions
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
in the form

\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx,

where
\mu
is a positive bounded Radon measure (see Theorems
2.2
and
2.4
, and Proposition
2.7
). The main effort is spent in proving the boundedness of such a measure, which does not seem to follow directly from the representation obtained from the Beurling-Deny formula
[
6
, Theorem 4.5.2]
.

We will show that if
p\neq 2
(and
1<p\leq d
) this does not hold. More precisely, a counterexample can be obtained as follows. Given
x_{0}\in\Omega
and a sequence
\varepsilon_{k}
of positive numbers converging to
0
, we set

a_{k}(x,y)=\begin{cases}|B_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x_{0})|^{-1}&\hbox{ if }y\in B_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x_{0})\\
0&\hbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
B_{r}(x)
denotes the ball of centre
x
and radius
r
.
In this case the functionals

F_{k}(u)=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}a_{k}(x,y)\,dxdy+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx,

(1.2)

defined for
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, have a
\Gamma
-limit, in the weak topology of
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, that can be directly expressed on that space as

F(u)=\int_{\Omega}|u(x)-m_{p}(u)|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx,

(1.3)

where
m_{p}(u)
is the unique minimizer of
t\mapsto\int_{\Omega}|u(x)-t|^{p}\,dx
.
In the proof of this result the inequality
p\leq d
is crucial, implying that a single point has zero
p
-capacity; this also explains the fact that the
\Gamma
-limit is independent of
x_{0}
.

If
p\neq 2
we will show that there exist no continuous function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
and no non-negative bounded Radon measure
\mu
on
\Omega\times\Omega
such that

\int_{\Omega}|u(x)-m_{p}(u)|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}f(u(x),u(y))d\mu(x,y)

(1.4)

for all

(see Corollary
3.10
).

Note that the representation of

as above is not in contrast with the representability as a double integral when

. Indeed, in that case

m_{2}(u)={1\over|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u(y)\,dy,

so that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(x)-m_{2}(u)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx-{1\over|\Omega|}\Bigl(\int_{\Omega}u(y)\,dy\Bigr)^{2}

(1.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\,dxdy,

and

is just a multiple of the Lebesgue measure on
\Omega\times\Omega
.

The same observations lead to an example of failure of integral representability in the theory of relaxation.
This can be obtained by considering the functional defined on
C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
by

F_{x_{0}}(u)=\int_{\Omega}|u(x)-u(x_{0})|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx,

where

is a given point in
\Omega

W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is given by the same

F_{x_{0}}(u)
can be interpreted as an integral on
\Omega\times\Omega
with respect to the
d
-dimensional Hausdorff measure restricted to
\Omega\times\{x_{0}\}
, which is the weak limit of the measures
\mu_{k}=a_{k}\,dxdy
defined above.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
F_{k}

\Gamma
-converges in the weak topology of

(a)

(a) If

(2.5)

As for (e), we first prove that

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that
F_{k}

\Gamma
-converges in the weak topology of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
to a functional
F
. Then there exist two positive Radon measures
\mu
and
\nu
on
\Omega\times\Omega
and
\Omega
, respectively, such that

(a)
for
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)

F(u)=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)+\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}d\nu(x)+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx;

(2.6)

(b)

\mu
is symmetric; i.e.,
\mu(A\times B)=\mu(B\times A)
for every pair of Borel sets
A
and
B
contained in
\Omega
;

(c)
setting
\Delta:=\{(x,x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}

\mu((\Omega\times\Omega)\cap\Delta)=0.

(2.7)

Proof.

From the previous proposition it follows that the bilinear form
B
defined therein is a
Dirichlet form
(see
[
6
]
), and that
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
is a
core
for

. Consequently by the Beurling-Deny Theorem (
[
6

), we have the decomposition

F(u)=F^{n}(u)+F^{\ell}(u)+F^{c}(u)\hbox{ for every $u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)$},

where
F^{\ell}
is a local term,
F^{n}
is a non-local term, and
F^{c}
is a local term depending only on the derivatives. More precisely, there exist a symmetric matrix of Radon measures
\mu_{ij}
, and two positive Radon measures
\mu
and
\nu
such that

\displaystyle F^{n}(u)=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y),\quad F^{\ell}(u)=\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}d\nu(x),

(2.8)

\displaystyle F^{c}(u)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}{\partial u(x)\over\partial x_{i}}{\partial u(x)\over\partial x_{j}}d\mu_{ij}(x)

(2.9)

for every

. Note that it is not restrictive to suppose that
\mu
is symmetric and
(
2.7
) holds since

on
\Delta
.

We now show that in our case
\displaystyle F^{c}(u)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx
.
Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx\leq F(u)\leq M\,({\rm osc}_{\Omega}u)^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx

(2.10)

for every

using the lower semicontinuity of the first integral for the first inequality and (
2.3
) for the second one.

Given
\omega\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\varphi
and
\psi
be defined by

\varphi(x)=\omega(x)\cos(x\cdot\xi)\ \hbox{ and }\ \psi(x)=\omega(x)\sin(x\cdot\xi).

By a direct computation we have

\displaystyle F^{c}(\varphi)+F^{c}(\psi)=F^{c}(\omega)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}d\mu_{ij}(x),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi(x)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\omega(x)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}(x)|\xi|^{2}dx,

so that

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}d\mu_{ij}(x)-\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}(x)|\xi|^{2}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|F(\varphi)-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi(x)|^{2}\,dx\Big|+\Big|F(\psi)-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}\,dx\Big|+\Big|F(\omega)-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\omega(x)|^{2}\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle+F^{\ell}(\varphi)+F^{\ell}(\psi)+F^{\ell}(\omega)+F^{n}(\varphi)+F^{n}(\psi)+F^{n}(\omega)

Using (
2.10
) we get

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}d\mu_{ij}(x)-\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}(x)|\xi|^{2}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 12M\|\omega\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+3F^{\ell}(\omega)+F^{n}(\varphi)+F^{n}(\psi)+F^{n}(\omega).

(2.11)

Let
K={\rm supp}\,\omega
. We observe that

\displaystyle F^{n}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{K\times K}|\omega(x)\cos(x\cdot\xi)-\omega(y)\cos(y\cdot\xi)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)

\displaystyle+\int_{K\times(\Omega\setminus K)}|\omega(x)\cos(x\cdot\xi)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)+\int_{(\Omega\setminus K)\times K}|\omega(y)\cos(y\cdot\xi)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\|\omega\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\mu(K\times K)+\int_{K\times(\Omega\setminus K)}|\omega(x)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)+\int_{(\Omega\setminus K)\times K}|\omega(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\|\omega\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\mu(K\times K)+\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|\omega(x)-\omega(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)

\displaystyle 4\|\omega\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big(\mu(K\times K)+M\big)+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\omega(x)|^{2}\,dx,

where in the last inequality we again use (

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\bigg|\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}d\mu_{ij}(x)-\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}(x)|\xi|^{2}dx\bigg|

Applying this estimate with

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\bigg|\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}d\mu_{ij}(x)-\int_{\Omega}\omega^{2}(x)|\xi|^{2}dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{1\over\lambda^{2}}\Big(\|\omega\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(8\mu(K\times K)+20M)+5\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\omega(x)|^{2}\,dx\Big).

Letting

Example 2.3

Let

In order to prove the liminf inequality we fix

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)|^{2}a_{k}(x,y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle=2k\int_{R_{k}}|u_{k}(x)|^{2}\,dx-4k\int_{R_{k}}u_{k}(x)\,dx\int_{\Omega}u_{k}(y)\,dy+2\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}(y)|^{2}\,dy.

Moreover, using a Poincaré-inequality argument in

Theorem 2.4
.

Let

\Omega

(a)

\mu(\Omega\times\Omega)<+\infty

Proof.

We begin by proving that

We now complete the proof of claim (b), showing that the measure

\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)\leq M\,({\rm osc}_{\Omega}u)^{2}\hbox{ for all $u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)$. }

Since the proof is rather complex we first consider the case

For given
\eta>0

With given

From (
2.17

2.24

The next argument is, given

\displaystyle|\mu_{1}(A\times B)-\mu_{2}(A\times B)|

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
F\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty)
and let
\mu
and
\nu
be two bounded positive Radon measures on
\Omega\times\Omega
and
\Omega
, respectively, that satisfy
(a)
,
(b)
, and
(c)
of Theorem
2.2
. Suppose also that
F
be lower semicontinuous in the weak topology of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and that there exists
M>0
such that (
2.16
) holds for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. Then

(a)
if
B\subset\Omega
is a Borel set with zero capacity, then
\mu(B\times\Omega)=\nu(B)=0
;

(b)
for every

F(u)=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|\widetilde{u}(x)-\widetilde{u}(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)+\int_{\Omega}|\widetilde{u}(x)|^{2}d\nu(x)+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx;

(2.31)

(c)
if, in addition,
F(\Psi_{m}(u))\leq F(u)
for all
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\Psi_{m}(t)=(m\wedge t)\vee(-m)
, then (
2.31
) holds for every
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We observe that, by the strong continuity of
u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx
,

u\mapsto\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)+\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}d\nu(x)\ \hbox{ is lower semicontinuous}

(2.32)

on
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
with respect to the strong topology of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\Omega
with zero capacity. We now prove that

\displaystyle\mu(K\times(\Omega\setminus K))=0\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\nu(K)=0.

(2.33)

Given
\eta>0
let
U
be an open set such that
K\subset U\subset\Omega
and

\displaystyle\mu((U\setminus K)\times\Omega)\leq\eta\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\nu(U\setminus K)\leq\eta,

(2.34)

and let
w\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
be such that
0\leq w\leq 1
on the whole
\Omega
,
w=1
in a neighbourhood of
K
, and
w\leq{1\over 4}
on
\Omega\setminus U
. Since
K
has zero capacity there exist a sequence
u_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
converging to

strongly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, such that
0\leq u_{k}\leq 1
on the whole
\Omega
,
u_{k}=0
on
K
and
u_{k}=w
on
\Omega\setminus U
. Then we have

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)+\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}(x)|^{2}d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{K\times(\Omega\setminus U)}|w(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)+\int_{(\Omega\setminus U)\times(\Omega\setminus U)}|w(x)-w(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)

\displaystyle+\mu((U\setminus K)\times(U\setminus K))+2\mu((U\setminus K)\times(\Omega\setminus U))+\int_{\Omega\setminus U}|w(x)|^{2}d\nu(x)+\nu(U\setminus K)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{1\over 8}\mu(K\times(\Omega\setminus U))+\int_{(\Omega\setminus U)\times(\Omega\setminus U)}|w(x)-w(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega\setminus U}|w(x)|^{2}d\nu(x)+3\eta,

while

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|w(x)-w(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)+\int_{\Omega}|w(x)|^{2}d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\int_{K\times(\Omega\setminus U)}|1-w(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)+\int_{(\Omega\setminus U)\times(\Omega\setminus U)}|w(x)-w(y)|^{2}d\mu(x,y)

\displaystyle+\nu(K)+\int_{\Omega\setminus U}|w(x)|^{2}d\nu(x).

Hence, noting that

if

to
u
and (

\displaystyle{9\over 8}\mu(K\times(\Omega\setminus U))+\nu(K)\leq{1\over 8}\mu(K\times(\Omega\setminus U))+3\eta.

Taking into account that
\mu(K\times(U\setminus K))\leq\eta

2.34

\displaystyle\mu(K\times(\Omega\setminus K))+\nu(K)\leq 4\eta,

We now claim that

\displaystyle\mu(K\times K)=0,

Given

\displaystyle K\times K\subset\bigcup_{i,j}(K_{i}\times K_{j})=\bigcup_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{D}}(K_{i}\times K_{j})\cup\bigcup_{(i,j)\not\in\mathcal{D}}(K_{i}\times K_{j}),

Proposition 2.7

The counterexample

We fix

Definition 3.1

For all

Lemma 3.2
.

The map

Proof.

Let
u_{k}\to u
in

. Then the sequence

|m_{p}(u_{k})|\leq\|u_{k}-m_{p}(u_{k})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq 2\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Theorem 3.3
.

If

then the
\Gamma
-limit of
F_{k}
with respect to the weak

-convergence is the functional

F(u)=\int_{\Omega}|u(x)-m_{p}(u)|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx

(3.2)

for every

.

Proof.

Let
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u

. Then also
u_{k}\to u
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Hence, using Jensen’s inequality, the minimality of

, and applying Lemma

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\liminf_{k\to+\infty}{1\over|B_{\varepsilon_{k}}|}\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{\varepsilon_{k}}}|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)|^{p}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\bigg|u_{k}(x)-{1\over|B_{\varepsilon_{k}}|}\int_{B_{\varepsilon_{k}}}u_{k}(y)\,dy\bigg|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}(x)-m_{p}(u_{k})|^{p}dx\geq\int_{\Omega}|u(x)-m_{p}(u)|^{p}dx.

Since the term
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx

To prove the upper bound, we first construct a recovery sequence if

Remark 3.6

Proposition 3.9

If there exists a bounded Radon measure

Exclusion bounds for neutral gauge bosons
Zoltán Péli, Zoltán Trócsányi

Abstract
We study how the recent experimental results constrain the gauge sectors of U(1) extensions of the standard model using a novel representation of the parameter space. We determine the bounds on the mixing angle between the massive gauge bosons, or equivalently, the new gauge coupling as a function of the mass M Z ′ M_{Z^{\prime}} of the new neutral gauge boson Z ′ Z^{\prime} in the approximate range ( 10 − 2 , 10 4 ) (10^{-2},10^{4}) GeV/ c 2 c^{2} . We consider the most stringent bounds obtained from direct searches for the Z ′ Z^{\prime} . We also exhibit the allowed parameter space by comparing the predicted and measured values of the ρ \rho parameter and those of the mass of the W W boson. Finally, we discuss the prospects of Z ′ Z^{\prime} searches at future colliders.

II
Model definition

We consider the extensions of the standard model by a
U(1)_{z}

gauge group with a complex scalar field
\chi
and three generations
of right handed neutrinos. The new fields are neutral under the
standard model gauge interactions. An example for such a model is the
superweak extension of the standard model (SWSM)

Trócsányi 2020

gauge and gravity anomaly cancellation, otherwise leave the

z
charges arbitrary. In this section we collect the details
of the model only to the extent used in the present analyses.

II.3
Modified
\rho

The well known SM tree-level relationship between

\rho=1

\rho=\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{\mathrm{c}_{W}^{2}M_{Z}^{2}}\,.

(15)

In the extended model it is no longer equal to one at the tree level as it is modified to

\rho=1-\mathrm{s}_{Z}^{2}\,(1-\xi^{2})\,.

(16)

Experimentally, from global fits

Utilizing the smallness of

we can also express the

\rho=1-\kappa^{2}/(1-\tau^{2})+\mathcal{O}\bigl(\mathrm{s}_{Z}^{4}\bigr)

\rho=1-\frac{v^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}-M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\big(z_{\phi}(\mu)\,g_{z}\big)^{2}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(\mathrm{s}_{Z}^{4}\bigr)\,,

v_{Z^{\prime},u}\simeq\frac{\mathrm{s}_{Z}}{3}\biggl(\frac{1}{\mathscr{Z}}-4\mathrm{c}_{W}^{2}\biggr),&\quad v_{Z^{\prime},d}\simeq\frac{\mathrm{s}_{Z}}{3}\biggl(\frac{1}{\mathscr{Z}}+2\mathrm{c}_{W}^{2}\biggr)\,.\end{split}

(25)

We may also use the new gauge couplings as input parameters. To write the V-A couplings as functions of
g_{z}
, we first observe that
Eq. (
13
) implies

\begin{split}\kappa&\simeq-\mathrm{s}_{Z}\quad\text{for}\quad\xi\to 0,\\
\kappa&\simeq\mathrm{s}_{Z}\xi^{2}\quad\text{for}\quad\xi\to\infty.\end{split}

(26)

Then, for a heavy
Z^{\prime}
the axial couplings are

a_{Z^{\prime},f}\simeq z_{N}\frac{2g_{z}}{g_{Z^{0}}}a_{f}^{(h)}

and
v_{Z^{\prime},f}\simeq z_{N}\frac{2g_{z}}{g_{Z^{0}}}v_{f}^{(h)}
.
Using the definition of
\kappa
in Eq. (
10
),
we obtain

a_{\nu}^{(h)}=a_{u}^{(h)}=-\frac{1}{4}\,,\quad a_{\ell}^{(h)}=a_{d}^{(h)}=+\frac{1}{4}\,,

(27)

while from (
23
)

\begin{split}v_{\nu}^{(h)}&=1-\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{Z}\,,\quad\>v_{\ell}^{(h)}=1-\frac{3}{2}\mathscr{Z}\,,\\
v_{u}^{(h)}&=-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{5}{6}\mathscr{Z}\,,\;v_{d}^{(h)}=-\frac{1}{3}\ -\frac{1}{6}\mathscr{Z}\,.\end{split}

(28)

A light
Z^{\prime}
boson implies
a_{Z^{\prime},f}\simeq 0

and
v_{Z^{\prime},f}\simeq z_{N}\frac{2g_{z}}{g_{Z^{0}}}v_{f}^{(\ell)}
where

\begin{split}v_{\nu}^{(\ell)}&=1\,\,,\qquad\qquad\quad\;\;\;v_{\ell}^{(\ell)}=1-2\mathrm{c}_{W}^{2}\mathscr{Z}\,,\\
v_{u}^{(\ell)}&=-\frac{1}{3}\ +\frac{4}{3}\mathrm{c}_{W}^{2}\mathscr{Z}\,,\;v_{d}^{(\ell)}=-\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{3}\mathrm{c}_{W}^{2}\mathscr{Z}\,.\end{split}

(29)

III
Direct Z-prime boson searches

Collider experiments such as LEP, Tevatron and the LHC performed direct searches for a
Z^{\prime}
boson. The nonobservation of such a particle can be and was translated into exclusion bands for the parameters of certain models predicting a
Z^{\prime}
boson.

According to Ref.
Appelquist et al. 2003
, the results of the LEPII experiment show
that
Z^{\prime}
is either heavier than the largest center-of-mass energy (
209~\mathrm{GeV}
) or

|z_{\ell}g_{z}|\lesssim 10^{-3}
. Tevatron searched for a
Z^{\prime}
in the range

200~\mathrm{GeV}<M_{Z^{\prime}}<800\mathrm{GeV}

Carena et al. 2004
. Finally, ATLAS
Aad et al. 2019
and CMS
Sirunyan et al. 2021
at the LHC performed the most
recent searches up to
M_{Z^{\prime}}<5500\mathrm{GeV}
. Below
M_{Z}
, the NA64 and BaBar experiments together with FASER below the mass of the pion provide strong
bounds for a light
Z^{\prime}
boson
Banerjee et al. 2019
;
Lees et al. 2017
. In this study we focus on the
exclusion bounds obtained from ATLAS and CMS for a heavy, and NA64, BaBar and FASER for a light
Z^{\prime}
.

In order to compare model predictions to experimental results at colliders
one has to compute the cross section for the process

pp\to Z^{\prime}+X\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}+X
, which is usually performed in the narrow
width approximation

\begin{split}\sigma\bigl(pp&\to Z^{\prime}+X\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}+X\bigr)=\\
&=\sigma\bigl(pp\to Z^{\prime}X\bigr)\mathrm{Br}\bigl(Z^{\prime}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\bigr),\end{split}

(30)

assuming that the total width of the
Z^{\prime}
boson
\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}
is much smaller than
its mass,
\gamma_{Z^{\prime}}=\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}/M_{Z^{\prime}}\ll 1
. Eq. (
30
) is
usually presented as

\begin{split}\sigma\bigl(pp&\to Z^{\prime}+X\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}+X\bigr)=\\
&=\frac{\pi}{6s}\biggl(c_{U}w_{U}\bigl(s,M_{Z^{\prime}}\bigr)+c_{D}w_{D}\bigl(s,M_{Z^{\prime}}\bigr)\biggr),\end{split}

(31)

where and
U\in\{u,c,t\}
,
D\in\{d,s,b\}
and the coefficients
c_{q}
collect model dependent contributions to the cross section

c_{q}=\bigl(2\sqrt{2}G_{F}M_{Z}^{2}\rho\bigr)\mathrm{Br}\bigl(Z^{\prime}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\bigr)\bigl(a_{Z^{\prime},q}^{2}+v_{Z^{\prime},q}^{2}\bigr)\,.

(32)

The hadronic structure functions
w_{U/D}
(cf. Ref.
Carena et al. 2004
)
collect the QCD corrections.
For the production of a heavy neutral gauge boson they depend only
on the
M
of the gauge boson and the center of
mass energy squared
s
,

\begin{split}w_{U/D}&=\sum_{q\in U/D}\int_{0}^{1}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\!\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{0}^{1}\!\mathrm{d}z~\delta\biggl(\frac{M^{2}}{s}-z~x_{1}x_{2}\biggr)\\
&\times\biggl[f_{g}\bigl(x_{1},M\bigr)\biggl(f_{q}\bigl(x_{2},M\bigr)+f_{\overline{q}}\bigl(x_{2},M\bigr)\biggr)\Delta_{gq}\bigl(z,M^{2}\bigr)\\
&+\biggl(f_{q}\bigl(x_{1},M\bigr)f_{\overline{q}}\bigl(x_{2},M\bigr)\biggr)\Delta_{qq}\bigl(z,M^{2}\bigr)+\big(x_{1}\leftrightarrow x_{2}\bigr)\biggr],\end{split}

(33)

where the functions
f_{i}(x,\mu_{F})
are the parton distribution functions
inside the proton for parton
i
at factorization scale
\mu_{F}
.
We use the NNPDF3.0 NLO PDF set in our
numerical computations. At the next-to-leading order (NLO) accuracy
the coefficient functions
\Delta_{ab}
for vector boson production are
known
Altarelli et al. 1979
. One also needs to compute the total decay width of the

Z^{\prime}
to obtain the cross section (
30
). We collect the
coefficient functions and the decay width formulae of the
Z^{\prime}
boson,
needed to compute the cross section in Eq. (
31
) in App.
C
.

IV
Numerical analysis

IV.1
Parameter scanning

The model predictions can be expressed as functions of the free
parameters of the theory. At the most fundamental level, these
are the free
z
charges, new couplings and VEV ratio,

z_{\phi}\,,\>z_{N}\,,\>g_{z}\,,\>g_{yz}\,,\>\tan\beta\,,

(34)

which are not independent and a certain combination of them appears in the model predictions. For instance, the tree level
\rho
parameter estimates the constraints from the electroweak precision observables and it depends only on

\bigl(\mathrm{s}_{Z}\,,\>M_{Z^{\prime}}\bigr)\quad\text{or}\quad\bigl(z_{N}\,g_{z}\,,\>M_{Z^{\prime}}\,,\>\mathscr{Z}\bigr)\,.

(35)

The NA64 experiment presents exclusion bounds for a dark photon
in the
(\epsilon,M_{A^{\prime}})
plane. Those constraints can be translated to our model parameters
using the relation derived in App.
D
.
This shows one that exclusion bounds depend on either

\bigl(\mathrm{s}_{Z}\,,\>M_{Z^{\prime}}\,,\>\mathscr{Z}\bigr)\quad\text{or}\quad\bigl(z_{N}\,g_{z}\,,\>M_{Z^{\prime}}\,,\>\mathscr{Z}\bigr),

(36)

Presently, the most stringent bounds on the parameter space for heavy
Z^{\prime}

bosons can be obtained from direct searches using the Drell-Yan pair production process

p+p\to Z^{\prime}+X\to\ell^{+}+\ell^{-}+X,

(37)

described in Sect.
III
. The corresponding cross section (
31
) can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\sigma&=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{3s}\frac{\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}}\mathrm{Br}\bigl(Z^{\prime}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\bigr)\\
&\times\biggl(\mathrm{Br}\bigl(Z^{\prime}\to U\overline{U}\bigr)w_{U}\bigl(s,M_{Z^{\prime}}\bigr)\\
&\quad+\mathrm{Br}\bigl(Z^{\prime}\to D\overline{D}\bigr)w_{D}\bigl(s,M_{Z^{\prime}}\bigr)\biggr),\end{split}

(38)

where the branching fractions are listed in App.
C
,
while
w_{U/D}
are given by Eq. (
33
). In this case as well, the predictions depend on the parameter set (
36
) or equivalently on

\bigl(\gamma_{Z^{\prime}},M_{Z^{\prime}},\mathscr{Z}\bigr)

(39)

where
\gamma_{Z^{\prime}}=\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}/M_{Z^{\prime}}
.

IV.2
Constraints on a light neutral gauge boson

Light vector-type particles, usually called dark photons
(A^{\prime})
, are often considered as a portal to a secluded sector in particle physics or downright as dark matter candidates.
Presently, the most stringent,
90\%
CL exclusion bound in the dark photon mass range
M_{A^{\prime}}\in(1~\mathrm{MeV},8~\mathrm{GeV})
comes from the combined results of the NA64
Banerjee et al. 2019
, BaBar
Lees et al. 2017
and more recently the FASER
Abreu et al. 2024
experiments.
The dark photon model probed in these experiments have a single vector type coupling
\epsilon e
to the electromagnetic current.
The parameters
\bigl(\epsilon,M_{A^{\prime}}\bigr)
can be matched to a generic U(1)
z
as detailed in App.
D
.
We scanned the parameter planes
\bigl(\mathrm{s}_{Z},M_{Z^{\prime}}\bigr)
and

\bigl(|z_{N}\,g_{z}|,M_{Z^{\prime}}\bigr)
for several benchmark values
of
\mathscr{Z}
. Using the value for the
\rho
parameter given
in Eq. (
17
) we set upper bounds

|\mathrm{s}_{Z}|\lesssim 4.5\cdot 10^{-3}\quad\text{and}\quad|z_{N}\,g_{z}|\lesssim\frac{1.7\cdot 10^{-3}}{|\mathscr{Z}|}

(40)

for
M_{Z^{\prime}}\ll M_{Z}
.

The experimental bounds obtained from NA64, BaBar and FASER all
depend on
v_{\ell}^{\ell}
given in Eq. (
29
).
The former two experiments searched for invisible decay products
of dark photons, whereas the latter one searches for decays

A^{\prime}\to e^{+}e^{-}
which introduces further dependence on the
corresponding branching fractions. The mapping of
\epsilon

onto
\mathrm{s}_{Z}
or
|z_{N}\,g_{z}|
and
M_{Z^{\prime}}
thus leads to a
dependence on
\mathscr{Z}
in the exclusion bands. For instance,
as
\mathscr{Z}
approaches
1/(2\mathrm{c}_{W}^{2})
, the reduced vector
coupling tends to zero
v_{\ell}^{\ell}\to 0
, which renders

|\mathrm{s}_{Z}|
and
|z_{N}\,g_{z}|
unconstrained. The exclusion band
obtained from the FASER experiment is even more sensitive as
the branching fraction
\mathrm{Br}(Z^{\prime}\to e^{+}e^{-})
also depends
on
v_{\ell}^{\ell}
.

IV.4
Constraints on the parameter space of specific U(1) extensions

We showcase the exclusion bounds on two specific U(1)
z
extensions,
one with a light
Z^{\prime}
boson and one with a heavy
Z^{\prime}
boson. We consider here the uncertainty due to the RGE running of
\eta
and we also use the mass of the
W
boson as a constraint.

The tree level
\rho
parameter discussed in
Sect.
II.3
is a useful quantity to gauge the
exclusion from electroweak precision observables in a model independent manner.
The effect of one-loop BSM corrections might become important for a given region in the parameter space and thus the use of a precise prediction is warranted.
The drawback is that the radiative BSM corrections are in
general complicated functions of the free parameters and the
z
charges.
Once the
z
charges are set and
\eta
is considered as an uncertainty, there are two free parameters from the gauge sector (
M_{Z^{\prime}}
and either
\mathrm{s}_{Z}
or
g_{z}
)
and two from the scalar sector (
M_{S}
and
\mathrm{s}_{S}
).
Using these four parameters we compute the complete one-loop
corrections to
M_{W}
in U(1)
z
extensions presented in Ref.
Péli and Trócsányi 2023

based on the computational method of Ref.
Athron et al. 2022
,
consult also Ref.
Péli 2023
for the renormalization of
\mathrm{s}_{Z}
.
Our input parameters are

M_{W}^{\text{SM}}=80.353~\mathrm{GeV},\quad M_{W}^{\text{exp.}}=80.377~\mathrm{GeV},

(42)

with a combined experimental and theoretical uncertainty of
\sigma=15\,\mathrm{MeV}
.

The BSM corrections can either amount to a positive or negative contribution to
M_{W}
.
A heavy
Z^{\prime}
boson and a light
S

) increase,
while a light
Z^{\prime}
boson and a heavy
S
scalar (
M_{S}>M_{H}
) decrease the predicted value of
M_{W}
.
In this work we focus on the effect of the gauge sector and thus we present exclusion bounds obtained from
M_{W}
at
\mathrm{s}_{S}=0
, i.e. when the extended scalar sector does not affect the mass of the
W
boson.

Our case study for a U(1)
z
extension with a light
Z^{\prime}
boson is
the SWSM (recall that
z_{N}=1/2
and
z_{\phi}=1
). This model can

10\,\mathrm{MeV}\lesssim M_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim m_{\pi}\ll M_{Z}
and the dark
matter candidate is the lightest sterile neutrino, which is
considered to be lighter than
M_{Z^{\prime}}/2
, while the other sterile
neutrinos are much heavier

V
Conclusions

In this paper we studied the parameter space of U(1)
z

extensions of the standard model with an additional complex scalar field
and three families of right handed neutrinos with generation independent

z
charges and no exotic fermions. Anomaly cancellation
constrains the

Commutator technique for the heat kernel of minimal higher derivative operators
A. O. Barvinsky, A. V. Kurov, W. Wachowski

Abstract
We suggest a new technique of the asymptotic heat kernel expansion for minimal higher derivative operators of a generic 2 ​ M 2M -th order, F ⁡ ( ∇ ) = ( − □ ) M + ⋯ F(\nabla)=(-\Box)^{M}+\cdots , in the background field formalism of gauge theories and quantum gravity. This technique represents the conversion of the recently suggested Fourier integral method of generalized exponential functions [Phys. Rev. D105, 065013 (2022), arXiv:2112.03062] into the commutator algebra of special differential operators, which allows one to express expansion coefficients for F ⁡ ( ∇ ) F(\nabla) in terms of the Schwinger-DeWitt coefficients of a minimal second order operator H ⁡ ( ∇ ) H(\nabla) . This procedure is based on special functorial properties of the formalism including the Mellin-Barnes representation of the complex operator power H M ​ ( ∇ ) H^{M}(\nabla) and naturally leads to the origin of generalized exponential functions without directly appealing to the Fourier integral method. The algorithm is essentially more straightforward than the Fourier method and consists of three steps ready for a computer codification by symbolic manipulation programs. They begin with the decomposition of the operator into a power of some minimal second order operator H ⁡ ( ∇ ) H(\nabla) and its lower derivative “perturbation part” W ⁡ ( ∇ ) W(\nabla) , F ⁡ ( ∇ ) = H M ​ ( ∇ ) + W ⁡ ( ∇ ) F(\nabla)=H^{M}(\nabla)+W(\nabla) , followed by considering their multiple nested commutators. The second step is the construction of special local differential operators—the perturbation theory in powers of the lower derivative part W ⁡ ( ∇ ) W(\nabla) . The final step is the so-called procedure of their syngification consisting in a special modification of the covariant derivative monomials in these operators by Synge world function σ ⁡ ( x , x ′ ) \sigma(x,x^{\prime}) with their subsequent action on the HaMiDeW coefficients of H ⁡ ( ∇ ) H(\nabla) .

Summary of results

Each minimal operator (
1.4
) of order
2M

M

\hat{H}(\nabla)
of the form (

2M-1

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\nabla)=\hat{H}^{M}(\nabla)+\hat{W}(\nabla),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\hat{W}(\nabla)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{2M-1}\hat{W}_{k}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{k}}(x)\nabla_{a_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{a_{k}}.

(2.2)

The idea of this decomposition is that
\hat{W}(\nabla)

\hat{H}^{M}(\nabla)

\hat{P}

\hat{H}(\nabla)

-\Box

The virtue of the decomposition (
2.1
) is that it allows one to reduce the heat kernel expansion of
[
19
]

\hat{H}^{M}(\nabla)
and
\hat{W}(\nabla)
and reexpress the result in terms of the well-known Schwinger-DeWitt coefficients
\hat{a}_{l}(H|x,x^{\prime})
of the minimal second order operator
\hat{H}(\nabla)
. These coefficients turn out to be acted upon by certain explicitly calculable local differential operators originating from this commutator algebra.

Thus, the main result of the paper can be formulated as follows. The heat kernel of a higher-order minimal operator (
2.1

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{F}(\tau|x,x^{\prime})\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau^{1/M})^{d/2}}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\;\tau^{\frac{m}{M}}\;\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\;\mathcal{E}_{M,\frac{d}{2}+Mk-m}\Big(\!-\frac{\sigma(x,x^{\prime})}{2\,\tau^{1/M}}\Big)\,\hat{a}_{m,k}(F|x,x^{\prime})

(2.3)

3
Perturbation theory

Here we derive the above results. The basic idea of derivation is rather simple. As shown in
[
19
]
, the off-diagonal heat kernel expansion for an arbitrary
M
-th power
\hat{H}^{M}(\nabla)
of the minimal second order operator (
1.3
) can be obtained from its Schwinger-DeWitt expansion (
1.2
) by the procedure of term-by-term direct/inverse Mellin transform. The result consists in the replacement of the original exponential function by a sequence of generalized ones and by taking relevant fractional powers of the proper time,

\hat{K}_{H^{M}}(\tau|x,x^{\prime})=\frac{\Delta^{1/2}(x,x^{\prime})}{(4\pi\tau^{1/M})^{d/2}}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\tau^{l/M}\\
\times\mathcal{E}_{M,d/2-l}\Big(-\frac{\sigma(x,x^{\prime})}{2\tau^{1/M}}\Big)\,\hat{a}_{l}(H|x,x^{\prime}).

(3.1)

This equation is a particular case of expansions considered in
[
19
]
, but for completeness we present its derivation in Appendix
A
.

To circumvent the problem that not every minimal operator of order
2M
can be represented as an
M
-th power of the minimal second order one, we can use the decomposition (
2.1
) and consider the operator
\hat{H}^{M}(\nabla)
as an “unperturbed part”, and the remainder operator
\hat{W}(\nabla)
of a positive background dimensionality as a “perturbation”. To develop such a perturbation theory in powers of the operator
\hat{W}(\nabla)
we choose the ansatz

\exp\big(\!-\tau\hat{F}\big)=\hat{U}_{\tau}\;\exp\big(\!-\tau\hat{H}^{M}\big),

(3.2)

where
\hat{U}_{\tau}
can be considered as the deformation operator of the heat kernel
K_{H^{M}}(\tau|x,x^{\prime})
into the original heat kernel
\hat{K}_{F}(\tau|x,x^{\prime})
. Substituting this ansatz into the heat equation for
\hat{K}_{F}(\tau|x,x^{\prime})
with the operator
\hat{F}(\nabla)
decomposed according to Eq.(
2.1
) we obtain the heat equation for
\hat{U}_{\tau}
with the perturbation operator
\hat{W}_{\tau}
in the “interaction picture” representation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\hat{U}_{\tau}=-\hat{U}_{\tau}\hat{W}_{\tau},\quad\hat{W}_{\tau}=e^{-\tau\hat{H}^{M}}\hat{W}\,e^{\tau\hat{H}^{M}}.

(3.3)

The solution is given by the anti-chronologically ordered exponent

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \hat{U}_{\tau}&=\bar{T}\exp\Big(-\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}dt\;\hat{W}_{t}\Big)\\
&=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}dt_{n}\int\limits_{0}^{t_{n}}dt_{n-1}\cdots\!\!\int\limits_{0}^{t_{2}}dt_{1}\;\hat{W}_{t_{1}}\cdots\hat{W}_{t_{n}}\end{aligned}

(3.4)

(the anti-chronological nature is, of course, explained by the fact that the interaction Hamiltonian
\hat{W}_{\tau}
in (
3.3
) stands to the right of
\hat{U}_{\tau}
).

Each of
\hat{W}_{t}
in (
3.4
) can be expanded in the proper time parameter
t
by using the well-known relation

e^{-tB}\;A\;e^{tB}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\;\llbracket A,B\rrbracket_{k},

(3.5)

where the brackets
\llbracket\ldots,\ldots\rrbracket_{k}
denote the multiple nested commutator (
2.7
). This leads to the expansion

\hat{W}_{\tau}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\tau^{k}}{k!}\;\hat{V}_{k}(\nabla),

(3.6)

with the coefficients
\hat{V}_{k}(\nabla)=\llbracket\hat{W}(\nabla),\hat{H}^{M}\rrbracket_{k}
(see Eq.(
2.6
)). Growing number of commutators in
\hat{V}_{k}(\nabla)
for growing
k
implies the growth in background dimensionality because every commutator of
\hat{W}(\nabla)
with
\hat{H}^{M}(\nabla)
gives rise either to an extra power of the curvature (generated by the commutator of covariant derivatives) or the covariant derivative of the spacetime dependent coefficients in both operators. Purely matrix commutators in the vector space of hat indices also increase this background dimensionality due to a positive dimension of commuted objects. (The exception from this rule is
\Box^{M}
-part of
\hat{H}^{M}
, which has zero background dimensionality,
dim\,\Box^{M}=0
, but this part is proportional to the unit matrix,
\Box^{M}\propto\hat{1}
, and the matrix commutation problem does not arise.) This explains why the background dimensionality of
\hat{V}_{k}(\nabla)
is linearly growing with
k
,
dim\,\hat{V}_{k}(\nabla)=k+1
, so that the expansion (
3.6
) in powers of the proper time turns out to be sufficient for obtaining the needed gradient and curvature expansion of the heat kernel.

Now, substituting the expansions (
3.6

3.4

\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}dt_{n}\int\limits_{0}^{t_{n}}dt_{n-1}\cdots\!\!\int\limits_{0}^{t_{2}}dt_{1}\;t_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{k_{n}}=\frac{\tau^{n+|\bm{k}|}}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{n}!\,c(\bm{k})}

we obtain the expansion of the operator
\hat{U}_{\tau}
with the coefficients
\hat{U}_{k}(\nabla)
given by Eqs.(
2.10
)-(
2.11
),

\hat{U}_{\tau}(\nabla)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\tau^{k}\;\hat{U}_{k}(\nabla).

(3.8)

According to Eq.(
3.2
) the last step of our derivation consists in the action of the obtained operator
\hat{U}_{\tau}(\nabla_{x})
on the heat kernel
\hat{K}_{H^{M}}(\tau|x,x^{\prime})

will start acting not only on the Schwinger-DeWitt coefficients

but also on the argument
x
in generalized exponential functions

contained in this expansion. Remarkably, these functions can be easily pulled to the left through the operators
\hat{U}_{k}(\nabla_{x})

3

\hat{U}_{k}(\nabla_{x})\to\{\hat{U}_{k}(\nabla_{x})\}_{n}

This is based on two important identities. The first one is the operator identity applicable to any differentiable function of the Synge world function

and any differential operator

of a finite order

\hat{O}(\nabla)f(\sigma)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}(-2)^{n}\frac{d^{n}f(\sigma)}{d\sigma^{n}}\big\{\hat{O}(\nabla)\big\}_{n}.

(3.9)

This identity should be understood as the operator relation when acting to the right on any test function. Its proof is given below in the Appendix
B

\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\mathcal{E}_{M,\alpha}(z)=\mathcal{E}_{M,\alpha+n}(z),

(3.10)

Thus, bearing in mind the order

of the differential operator

Here, when passing to the second line, we replaced the summation variables

\tau

2.3
) and the expression (

The structure of the commutator version expansion (

Finally, we show that all coefficients

4.1
UV divergences of the one-loop effective
action of a simple fourth-order derivative theory

Consider a toy model of a scalar field in four dimensional spacetime with the fourth-order inverse propagator

Only the first term and

where according to (

\displaystyle\,\sum\limits_{l=2\delta_{0k}}^{2+k}\{\hat{U}_{k}(\nabla_{x})\}_{2(k-1)+l}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Delta^{1/2}(x,x^{\prime})\,\hat{a}_{l}(-\Box|x,x^{\prime}),

(

(4.13)

because in view of Eqs.(

4.2
Vector operator of Hořava gravity theory

Another example demonstrating the power of the commutator technique is the calculations of UV divergences in projectable Hořava gravity theory
[
33
]
. In contrast to the previous example, however, this calculation cannot be done manually because of extremely high complexity of the computations which have to be performed with the use of symbolic manipulations in both methods – either in terms of universal functional traces or with the commutator algebra. We will consider only the vector sector of the theory (the contribution of Faddeev-Popov ghosts and shift functions of this model) and demonstrate that both methods lead to the same result, but clearly observe that the number of calculational stages in the functional traces method and their complexity are much higher than in the commutator approach.

The contribution of the vector sector on a static metric background in this model is given by the 4-dimensional functional determinant of the Lorentz violating operator
-\hat{1}\partial_{t}^{2}+\hat{F}(\nabla)

\displaystyle F^{a}_{\>\>b}(\nabla)=(-\Box)^{3}\delta^{a}_{b}-\Box^{2}\nabla_{b}\nabla^{a}

\displaystyle+\frac{1-2\lambda}{2(1-\lambda)}\nabla^{a}\Box\nabla^{c}\nabla_{b}\nabla_{c}+\frac{1-2\lambda}{2(1-\lambda)}\nabla^{a}\Box\nabla_{b}\Box

Dimensional reduction allows one to reexpress the spacetime functional determinant in terms of the purely spatial one

\displaystyle\varGamma

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\hat{V}_{0}\hat{V}_{1}+\frac{1}{6}\hat{V}_{1}\hat{V}_{0}-\frac{1}{6}\hat{V}_{0}^{3}+(\ldots),

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{4}

5
Discussion and conclusions

The advantage of the commutator technique lies in its universality, because it allows one to avoid auxiliary procedures like a perturbative extraction of the operator square roots or covariant Fourier transforms of
[
19
]
which lead to complicated iteration schemes for auxiliary structures. In particular, this technique makes verifiable the calculations for a generic fourth-order operator and allows one to get rid of errors which might be inevitable within less efficient schemes.

Despite transparency of the suggested formalism its implementation even in simple examples of the above type looks very involved and demands the use of symbolic manipulation programs like xAct package of Wolfram
Mathematica

[
35
,
36
]
. However, the analytic framework of the above commutator algebra does not yet seem to be completely exhausted, because a number of hidden properties of generalized exponential functions, relevant coefficients
\hat{a}_{m,k}(F|x,x^{\prime})
and Synge world function still remain to be revealed and properly understood. In particular, it should be emphasized that it is not yet clear what type of hidden identities between these objects can be responsible for an explicit proof of the equivalence of the commutator technique and original Fourier method of
[
19
]
. One of the examples of the puzzling efficiency of commutator version as compared to the Fourier transform is the problem of the range of summation over the index
k
of
\hat{a}_{m,k}(F|x,x^{\prime})
. It looks as follows.

General formalism of Fourier method suggested that it should be bounded from above by
4m

[
19
]
, though concrete calculations made within this method showed that
\hat{a}_{m,k}(F|x,x^{\prime})
start vanishing above
k=2m
, which is exactly a firm prediction (
2.15
) of the commutator algebra technique. This indicates to a stronger universality of the commutator technique, but leaves unclear what kind of relations between the objects of the above type might be responsible for that. Possible source of these relations might be the actual independence of the final result from the choice of the minimal operator
\hat{H}(\nabla)
in the decomposition (
2.1
). Indeed, the choice of the potential term
\hat{P}(x)
in
\hat{H}(\nabla)
is an arbitrary free element of the formalism, and the freedom in its choice can be used for obtaining certain nontrivial identities involving the coefficients
\hat{a}_{m,k}(F|x,x^{\prime})
, generalized exponentials, Synge world function and its derivatives.

Commutator algebra technique for heat kernel seems to be an essential step forward beyond other calculational methods. However, the boundaries of this research direction are not yet pushed to their logical extreme, because there still remains a realm of nonminimal differential operators which might play important role in the search for renormalizable, UV complete theory of gravity
[
33
]
and other applications in high energy physics, quantum gravity and other areas of theoretical physics. Thus, the heat kernel technique should be extended to the operators with the minimality condition replaced by causality
[
3
,
4
]
and beyond. Known functorial properties of the heat kernel formalism, already used in the above studies, might give a clue to the solution of this problem, and this is a subject of future research.

\hat{K}_{H^{p}}(\tau|x,x^{\prime})=\Delta^{1/2}(x,x^{\prime})\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{K}_{m}^{(p,d)}(\tau,\sigma)\,\hat{a}_{m}(H|x,x^{\prime}),

(A.7)

where the new set of basis kernels
\mathbb{K}_{m}^{(p,d)}(\tau,\sigma)
is given by the integrals

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}_{m}^{(p,d)}(\tau,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{w-i\infty}^{w+i\infty}ds\,\tau^{-s}\Gamma(s)\,\mathbb{G}_{m}(ps,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau^{m/p}}{(4\pi\tau^{1/p})^{d/2}}\,\mathcal{E}_{p,\frac{d}{2}-m}\left(-\frac{\sigma}{2\tau^{1/p}}\right),

(A.8)

which are again easily calculable by changing the integration variable
s\to t=d/2-m-ps
and using the Mellin-Barnes representation (
A.3
) of the generalized exponential function with
\nu=p
,
\alpha=d/2-m
and
z=\sigma/2\tau^{1/p}
.

So raising a minimal second order operator
\hat{H}(\nabla)
to a power
p
simply amounts to replacing the basis kernels of the Schwinger-DeWitt expansion
\tau^{m}e^{-\sigma/2\tau}/(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}
by
\mathbb{K}_{m}^{(p,d)}(\tau,\sigma)
(
A.8
), while keeping its off-diagonal coefficients
\hat{a}_{m}(H|x,x^{\prime})
unchanged. We call this remarkable property “the generalized functoriality”. For integer
p=M
the formulae (
A.7
)-(
A.8
) yield the expansion (
3.1
).

To prove (

N

The differentiable function

\nabla_{a_{1}}^{x}\cdots\nabla_{a_{N}}^{x}f\big(\sigma(x,x^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle\nabla_{a_{1}}^{x}\cdots\nabla_{a_{N}}^{x}f\big(\sigma(x,x^{\prime})\big)\;

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}f^{(n)}(0)\,\Big(e^{-k\sigma}\frac{d^{n}}{dk^{n}}\,\nabla_{a_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{a_{N}}e^{k\sigma}\Big)\,\bigg|_{\,k=0}

Appendix C
Syngifications of the covariant derivative monomials

We provide the most general expression for the trace of the form
[\{\nabla^{2n}\}_{n-1}]

\displaystyle\big[\{\nabla_{a_{1}}\nabla_{a_{2}}\dots\nabla_{a_{2n}}\}_{n-1}\big]=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\bigg(\sum_{k<l}g^{(n-1)}_{a_{1}a_{2}\dots\hat{a}_{k}\hat{a}_{l}\dots a_{2n}}\,\nabla_{a_{k}}\nabla_{a_{l}}

(C.1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k<l<p<q}g^{(n-2)}_{a_{1}a_{2}\dots\hat{a}_{k}\hat{a}_{l}\hat{a}_{p}\hat{a}_{q}\dots a_{2n}}\,\big[\nabla_{a_{k}}\nabla_{a_{l}}\nabla_{a_{p}}\nabla_{a_{q}}\sigma\big]\bigg)\;,\quad k,l,p,q=1,2,\dots,2n\;.

The first sum is taken over all terms with
k
-th and
l
-th indices,
k<l
, which are withdrawn from the completely symmetric tensor
g^{(n)}_{a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{2n}}
and relocated to the two covariant derivatives
\nabla_{a_{k}}\nabla_{a_{l}}
, the hat over index indicating that this index is absent in the symmetric tensor
g^{(n-1)}_{a_{1}a_{2}\dots\hat{a}_{k}\hat{a}_{l}\dots a_{2n}}
whose rank now is
2(n-1)
. In the second sum a similar procedure concerns four indices relocated to the fourth order covariant derivative of the world function with the following transition to its coincidence limit. Obviously the ordering of covariant derivatives in both sums is preserved the same as in the left hand side of the equation. We demonstrate the implementation of the described above rules on a simple example

\displaystyle\big[\{\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nabla_{c}\nabla_{d}\}_{1}\big]=\,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\big(g_{cd}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}+g_{bd}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{c}+g_{bc}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{d}+g_{ac}\nabla_{b}\nabla_{d}+g_{ad}\nabla_{b}\nabla_{c}+g_{ab}\nabla_{c}\nabla_{d}\big)

(C.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\big[\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nabla_{c}\nabla_{d}\sigma(x,x^{\prime})\big].

Other examples of traces with metric contractions are

\displaystyle\big[\{\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\}_{0}\big]=\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b},

(C.3)

\displaystyle\big[\{\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\Box\}_{1}\big]=-\frac{1}{2}\Big(g_{ab}\Box+(d+2)\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}+2\nabla_{b}\nabla_{a}-\tfrac{2}{3}R_{ij}\Big),

\displaystyle\big[\{\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\Box^{n}\}_{n}\big]=\Big(\!-\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{n}(d+4)(d+6)\dots(d+2n-2)\Big(n(d+2n)g_{ab}\Box+\big(d^{2}+2(n+1)(d+n)\big)\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}

\displaystyle+2n(d+n+1)\nabla_{b}\nabla_{a}\Big)+O[{\cal R}],\quad n\geq 2.

For
n=2
the last equation holds with the overall coefficient
(d+4)(d+6)\dots(d+2n-2)
replaced by 1.

For generality we also consider syngifications of the form
[\{\nabla^{2n+1}\}_{n-1}]
. The most general one in this case has the form

\displaystyle\big[\{\nabla_{a_{1}}\nabla_{a_{2}}\dots\nabla_{a_{2n+1}}\}_{n-1}\big]=\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1}\bigg(\sum_{k<l<p}g^{(n-1)}_{a_{1}a_{2}\dots\hat{a}_{k}\hat{a}_{l}\hat{a}_{p}\dots a_{2n+1}}\,\nabla_{a_{k}}\nabla_{a_{l}}\nabla_{a_{p}}

(C.4)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k<l<p<q}g^{(n-2)}_{a_{1}a_{2}\dots\hat{a}_{k}\hat{a}_{l}\hat{a}_{p}\hat{a}_{q}\hat{a}_{r}\dots a_{2n+1}}\big[\nabla_{a_{k}}\nabla_{a_{l}}\nabla_{a_{p}}\nabla_{a_{q}}\sigma(x,x^{\prime})\big]\,\nabla_{a_{r}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k<l<p<q<r}g^{(n-2)}_{a_{1}a_{2}\dots\hat{a}_{k}\hat{a}_{l}\hat{a}_{p}\hat{a}_{q}\hat{a}_{r}\dots a_{2n+1}}\,\left[\nabla_{a_{k}}\nabla_{a_{l}}\nabla_{a_{p}}\nabla_{a_{q}}\nabla_{a_{r}}\sigma(x,x^{\prime})\right]\bigg)\;,\quad k,l,p,q,r=1,2,\dots,2n+1\;.

where the sums are taken over all relocations of three, four and five indices of derivatives which retain their original order.

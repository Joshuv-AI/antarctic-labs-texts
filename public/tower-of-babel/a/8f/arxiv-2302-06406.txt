On generalized preconditioners for time-parallel parabolic optimal control Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Arne Bouillon, Giovanni Samaey, Karl Meerbergen

Abstract
The ParaDiag family of algorithms solves differential equations by using preconditioners that can be inverted in parallel through diagonalization. In the context of optimal control of linear parabolic pde s, the state-of-the-art ParaDiag method is limited to solving self-adjoint problems with a tracking objective. We propose three improvements to the ParaDiag method: the use of alpha-circulant matrices to construct an alternative preconditioner, a generalization of the algorithm for solving non-self-adjoint equations, and the formulation of an algorithm for terminal-cost objectives. We present novel analytic results about the eigenvalues of the preconditioned systems for all discussed ParaDiag algorithms in the case of self-adjoint equations, which proves the favorable properties of the alpha-circulant preconditioner. We use these results to perform a theoretical parallel-scaling analysis of ParaDiag for self-adjoint problems. Numerical tests confirm our findings and suggest that the self-adjoint behavior, which is backed by theory, generalizes to the non-self-adjoint case. We provide a sequential, open-source reference solver in Matlab for all discussed algorithms.

\par\begin{equation}\begin{aligned} P_{\sigma}(\alpha)^{-1}\widehat{A}_{\sigma}&=P_{\sigma}(\alpha)^{-1}(P_{\sigma}(\alpha)+(\widehat{A}_{\sigma}-P_{\sigma}(\alpha)))\\

It is possible to visuali{z}{}e \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{cor:pd-track:anal:thetas} to gain more insight into how the two preconditioners (that is, $\alpha=1$ and $\alpha=-1$) perform, as well as how they compare. Recall from \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:pd-track:anal} that every eigenvalue $\sigma_{m}$ of $K$ corresponds to two non-unity eigenvalues $\theta_{m,\{1,2\}}$ of the preconditioned system matrix, which are complex conjugates of each other. \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{fig:pd-track:eigs} plots the $\theta$ with positive imaginary part for the cases $\alpha=\pm 1$ in two distinct ways. This section considers dissipative problems, where $K$ is positive definite and hence $\sigma>0$.
\par\lx@cref{creftypepluralcap\lx@tilde refnum}{fig:pd-track:plane:m1} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{fig:pd-track:plane:p1} are based on the view that $\widehat{\gamma}$ is typically known (one can set the regulari{z}{}ation parameter $\gamma$ and the time step $\tau$), while the eigenvalues $\sigma$ (and thus $\widehat{\sigma}=\tau\sigma$) could lie anywhere. For each $\widehat{\gamma}$ value, these figures mark the preconditioned eigenvalues for a whole range of $\widehat{\sigma}>0$ options. We can see \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{cor:pd-track:anal:semidisc} in action: for $\alpha=-1$ the eigenvalues lie inside $\mathcal{D}_{0.5,+}$ while, for $\alpha=1$, they lie outside it. \lx@cref{creftypepluralcap\lx@tilde refnum}{fig:pd-track:eigs-abs:m1} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{fig:pd-track:eigs-abs:p1} add $\widehat{\sigma}$ as a dimension to gain more insight into its influence.
\par We stress that from these figures, little if anything can be said about how ParaDiag scales when increasing $L$, the topic of \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:scale}. Instead, we conclude that \emph{for a fixed number of time steps}, ParaDiag can be expected to converge quickly unless both \begin{itemize}\itemize@item@the equation in the absence of control evolves slowly \emph{relatively to the size of the time interval} ($\widehat{\sigma}\approx 0$); and
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@there is little control \emph{relatively to the size of the time interval} ($\widehat{\gamma}\approx 0$).
\end{itemize}
If these conditions for potentially slow convergence are met, the difference between $\alpha$ values becomes important. \begin{itemize}\itemize@item@When $\alpha=1$, the smaller $\widehat{\sigma}$ and $\widehat{\gamma}$ become, the more $\theta$ blows up. This will lead to preconditioned eigenvalues that lie far away from each other, resulting in slow convergence.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@When $\alpha=-1$, small values of $\widehat{\sigma}$ and $\widehat{\gamma}$ slightly pull the eigenvalues away from unity. However, they always stay relatively close due to \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{cor:pd-track:anal:semidisc}. In addition, clustering may even become better for very small $\widehat{\sigma}$ and $\widehat{\gamma}$: \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{fig:pd-track:plane:m1} shows that the worst clustering occurs at intermediate $\widehat{\gamma}$s.

\par\par\lx@newpage Specifically for the {gmres} method, it is possible to harness \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{cor:pd-track:anal:semidisc} into an upper bound on the convergence of the iterative method.
\begin{theorem}Consider the tracking-type all-at-once system with implicit-Euler time discreti{z}{}ation \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pd-track:existing:aao-rescaled} where $L>4$ and $K$ is self-adjoint. When using ParaDiag with {gmres} preconditioned by $P(-1)$ (see \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pd-track:alpha:Palpha}) and if $0<\varphi<1$ for all $\varphi$ (which occurs whenever $K$ is positive definite), the following holds. For any $0<\rho<2$, there exists a $\kappa_{\rho}>0$ such that the residual ${\bm{{r}}}^{k}$ at {gmres} iteration $k$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert{\bm{{r}}}^{k}\right\rVert}_{2}/\mathinner{\!\left\lVert{\bm{{r}}}^{0}\right\rVert}_{2}\leq\kappa(V)\kappa_{\rho}\rho^{-k}{.}\end{equation}Here, $\kappa(V)$ is the condition number of the eigenvector matrix $V$ of $P(-1)^{-1}{\widehat{A}}$. The {gmres} residual decreases exponentially with a mesh- and problem-independent factor.
\@qedbox{}\end{theorem}
\@proof From \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{trefethenNumericalLinearAlgebra1997a}{}{}]}, we retrieve the formula
\begin{equation}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert{\bm{{r^{k}}}}\right\rVert}_{2}/\mathinner{\!\left\lVert{\bm{{r^{0}}}}\right\rVert}_{2}\leq\kappa(V)\inf_{p_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}}{\sup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert p_{k}(\sigma)\right\rvert}}}\end{equation}
which asks us to solve a polynomial-approximation problem: find a degree-$k$ polynomial that takes the value $1$ at the origin and, yet, is as small as possible on all the eigenvalues of the preconditioned matrix. However, if we want a generally applicable bound, we do not know these eigenvalues. Luckily, we have \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{cor:pd-track:anal:semidisc}: if we can find a polynomial that is small on the \emph{entire} semi-dis{k} $\mathcal{D}_{0.5,+}$, then a fortiori, it must also be small on whatever eigenvalues a specific problem happens to generate.
\par To eliminate the explicit condition $p(0)=1$, we take the following steps. If we can find a degree-$k$ polynomial $\widehat{p}$ that satisfies $\widehat{p}(0)=0$ and approximates $1$ on $\mathcal{D}_{0.5,+}$, it has the same error as $p(z)=1-\widehat{p}(z)$. Since $\widehat{p}(0)=0$, it must be that $\widehat{p}(z)=zq(z)$, where $q$ is of degree $k-1$. If we find a polynomial $q$ that approximates $1/z$ on $\mathcal{D}_{0.5,+}$, we have a function $\widehat{p}(z)=zq(z)$ which approximates $1$ with no higher error than that of $q$ in approximating $1/z$ (this follows easily from the fact that $\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z\right\rvert}\leq 1$).
\par In summary, \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:thm:pd-track:anal:expgmres:gmresorig}'s rightmost factor is bounded by the best degree-$(k-1)$ polynomial-approximation error to $1/z$ on $\mathcal{D}_{0.5,+}$, which is bounded by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{lmm:apdx-proof:pd-track:polappr}.
\@qedbox{}\end@proof

We now extend ParaDiag to the more general, non-self-adjoint setting where $K\neq K^{*}$ is possible, resulting in the optimality system \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:intro:intro:optsys}. The method supports using any adjoint spatial discreti{z}{}ation, which could be of interest, but we will limit ourselves to $K^{*}$. Then \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pd-track:existing:aao} becomes
\begin{equation}A\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{{y}}}\\

\end{bmatrix}\coloneqq\left(\begin{bmatrix}B&\tau\frac{I_{t}}{\gamma}\\

and, after rescaling,

\par\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{ParaDiag for terminal-cost objectives}
Both the literature on ParaDiag and this paper have thus far focused on the tracking objective in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:intro:intro:obj}. We next develop a ParaDiag-type preconditioner for problems with the terminal-cost objective function, without requiring self-adjointness. The method is designed in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:pd-tc:new}, after which it is analy{z}{}ed for self-adjoint problems in \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:pd-tc:anal} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{sec:pd-tc:interp}.
\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{A new preconditioner}
The optimality system in the terminal-cost case can be discreti{z}{}ed with time step $\tau$ to form the all-at-once system
\begin{equation}A\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{{y}}}\\
{\bm{{\lambda}}}\\
\end{bmatrix}\coloneqq\left(\begin{bmatrix}B&\frac{\tau}{\gamma}I_{t}\\
-E&B^{\mathstrut\scriptscriptstyle{\top}}\\
\end{bmatrix}\otimes I_{x}+\tau\begin{bmatrix}I_{t}\otimes K\\
-E\otimes K^{*}&I_{t}\otimes K^{*}\\
\end{bmatrix}\right)\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{{y}}}\\
{\bm{{\lambda}}}\\
\end{bmatrix}={\bm{{b}}}\end{equation}
where $E$ is a matrix with as only non-zero a one in the bottom right corner. Recall that the exposition assumes an explicit Euler discreti{z}{}ation, which implies
\begin{equation}B=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
-1&1&\\
&\mathinner{\mathchoice{\vbox{\kern 3.26465pt\vbox to7.48764pt{\hbox{$\displaystyle\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\displaystyle\mathpunct{\kern 2.2639pt}\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\displaystyle\mathpunct{\kern 2.2639pt}\mathpunct{\kern 2.2639pt}\mathpunct{.}$}}}}{\vbox{\kern 3.26465pt\vbox to7.48764pt{\hbox{$\textstyle\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\textstyle\mathpunct{\kern 2.2639pt}\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\textstyle\mathpunct{\kern 2.2639pt}\mathpunct{\kern 2.2639pt}\mathpunct{.}$}}}}{\vbox{\kern 2.17635pt\vbox to5.06042pt{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mathpunct{\kern 1.36111pt}\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mathpunct{\kern 1.36111pt}\mathpunct{\kern 1.36111pt}\mathpunct{.}$}}}}{\vbox{\kern 1.81467pt\vbox to4.13484pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathpunct{\kern 0.97221pt}\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathpunct{\kern 0.97221pt}\mathpunct{\kern 0.97221pt}\mathpunct{.}$}}}}\mkern 3.0mu}&\mathinner{\mathchoice{\vbox{\kern 3.26465pt\vbox to7.48764pt{\hbox{$\displaystyle\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\displaystyle\mathpunct{\kern 2.2639pt}\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\displaystyle\mathpunct{\kern 2.2639pt}\mathpunct{\kern 2.2639pt}\mathpunct{.}$}}}}{\vbox{\kern 3.26465pt\vbox to7.48764pt{\hbox{$\textstyle\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\textstyle\mathpunct{\kern 2.2639pt}\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\textstyle\mathpunct{\kern 2.2639pt}\mathpunct{\kern 2.2639pt}\mathpunct{.}$}}}}{\vbox{\kern 2.17635pt\vbox to5.06042pt{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mathpunct{\kern 1.36111pt}\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mathpunct{\kern 1.36111pt}\mathpunct{\kern 1.36111pt}\mathpunct{.}$}}}}{\vbox{\kern 1.81467pt\vbox to4.13484pt{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathpunct{\kern 0.97221pt}\mathpunct{.}$}\vfill\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathpunct{\kern 0.97221pt}\mathpunct{\kern 0.97221pt}\mathpunct{.}$}}}}\mkern 3.0mu}\\

\par ParaDiag methods are fully reliant on the presence of good preconditioners, preferably with a mesh-independent convergence rate. Such a preconditioner must be invertible efficiently and in parallel. Leaving the bottom-left block of \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pd-tc:new:aao} out of the preconditioner makes this task significantly easier. Indeed, it allows replacing the $B$ blocks by alpha-circulant $C(\alpha)$ blocks to form the preconditioner
\begin{equation}P(\alpha)=\begin{bmatrix}C(\alpha)&\frac{\tau}{\gamma}I_{t}\\
&C(\alpha)^{*}\\
\end{bmatrix}\otimes I_{x}+\tau\begin{bmatrix}I_{t}\otimes K\\
&I_{t}\otimes K^{*}\\
\end{bmatrix}{,}\end{equation}
which is block-triangular. Thus multiplication by $P(\alpha)^{-1}$ is possible by first inverting the bottom-right block of \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pd-tc:new:Palpha} (which pertains to the adjoint variable $\lambda$) and only then solving a second system to find the state $y$. \par\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Parallel-scaling analysis for self-adjoint problems}
An oft-used metric in the context of parallel algorithms is \emph{weak scalability} (for time-parallel methods, it was studied in e.g.\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{benedusiExperimentalComparisonSpacetime2021,caceressilvaParallelintimePararealImplementation2014}{}{}]}). The aim is that a program's execution time stays constant when increasing the problem size (in our case the number of time steps $L$, as we investigate \emph{time}-parallelism) in tandem with the number of processors, keeping their ratio constant. For our optimal-control problem \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:intro:intro:optprob}, we identify two regimes \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ganderPARAOPTPararealAlgorithm2020a}{}{}]}. \begin{itemize}\itemize@item@If we increase the time horizon $T$ together with $L$, the time step $\tau$ stays constant. In this regime, $\widehat{\sigma}$ and $\widehat{\gamma}$ (from \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pd-track:anal:phipsi} or \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pd-tc:anal:phipsi}, depending on the objective function) do not change. The amount of work is increased by an expanding time scope, not by using a more accurate discreti{z}{}ation.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@We can also keep $T$ constant but instead increase the amount of time steps $L$ by lowering $\tau$. Then $\widehat{\sigma}$ and $\widehat{\gamma}$ increase with it. The amount of work is increased by using a more fine-grained mesh for the same problem.
\end{itemize}
This section will use the analytic results from \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:pd-track:anal} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{sec:pd-tc:anal} to perform a theoretical analysis of ParaDiag's weak scaling, which \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:num} later verifies in practice. The approach is to assume the inversion of our preconditioners scales well in all regimes, as attested to by previous ParaDiag algorithms that use similar preconditioners \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{goddardNoteParallelPreconditioning2019,wuDiagonalizationbasedParallelintimeAlgorithms2020b,wuParallelInTimeBlockCirculantPreconditioner2020a}{}{}]}. Then, all that needs to be analy{z}{}ed is the number of such inversions: if the iterative solver's iteration count stays constant when increasing the problem size, we have achieved good weak scalability. As a proxy for the actual iteration count, we will use the distribution of the preconditioned eigenvalues -- if they converge when increasing time parallelism, we will assume for the iteration count to do the same.
\par This section is limited to implicit Euler and self-adjoint, dissipative equations. We thus have $\sigma>0$ and the obvious $\gamma,T>0$. We aim to show that each of the eigenvalues $\theta$ converges to some finite, non-zero value in the relevant scaling limit.

In \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:thm:pd-track:eigs:eigs}, we have $0<z_{2}<1<z_{1}$ (see \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{lmm:apdx-proof:pd-track:zs}(a)). \begin{equation}\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty,T\rightarrow\infty,T=\tau L}\theta_{\{1,2\}}=1+\frac{1}{z_{2}-z_{1}}(-(z_{2}-\varphi\pm\psi{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}))=\frac{z_{1}-\varphi\pm\psi{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}{.}\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty,T\rightarrow\infty,T=\tau L,\alpha\rightarrow 0}\theta_{1}=\psi/(1-\varphi^{2}){,}\end{equation}

\par\par\@@unnumbered@section{paragraph}{Tracking}
Using {Matlab}'s symbolic toolbox allows us to solve the limit
\begin{equation}\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty,\tau\rightarrow 0,\tau L=T,\alpha=\pm 1}\theta_{\{1,2\}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\tanh\bigl(\frac{T\sqrt{\gamma\sigma^{2}+1}}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}\bigr)^{-\alpha}(\sqrt{\gamma}\sigma\pm{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu})}{2\sqrt{\gamma\sigma^{2}+1}}{.}\end{equation}
This is a finite expression for both $\alpha=\pm 1$ (the denominator cannot reach zero) and is non-zero as well (the real part of the numerator is always positive). \lx@cref{creftypepluralcap\lx@tilde refnum}{fig:scale:tau:tr-pDT} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{fig:scale:tau:tr-mDT} show that the eigenvalues stay almost constant when scaling a finite $L$ from $10^{3}$ to $10^{4}$, which we assumed implies weak scalability.
\par\par\@@unnumbered@section{paragraph}{Terminal cost}
This case is slightly simpler and can be computed by hand.
\begin{equation}\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty,\tau\rightarrow 0,\tau L=T,\alpha\rightarrow 0}\theta_{1}=1+(1-\exp(-2\sigma T))/(\gamma\sigma){,}\end{equation}
which is again finite. It also cannot reach zero: the exponential has a negative argument (because $\sigma>0$), so both terms of the sum are positive. \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{fig:scale:tau:tc-DT} illustrates that the scaling translates well to finite $L$ values, again implying weak scalability.
\par\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Numerical results}
This section presents the results of numerical tests assessing the performance of our ParaDiag methods. \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:num:track} first discusses tracking ParaDiag (\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{alg:pd-track:new}), considering both $\alpha=1$ and the novel $\alpha=-1$ variant. \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:num:tc} then moves on to the new terminal-cost method (\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{alg:pd-tc:new}).
\par Our ParaDiag algorithms are tested with a {Matlab} code we call {pintopt}. Coding an efficient parallel ParaDiag implementation is a significant task \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{caklovicParallelintimeCollocationMethod2023b}{}{}]} and is not the focus of the current paper. Hence, {pintopt} is sequential and not optimi{z}{}ed for speed, but rather serves as a readable and well-documented reference implementation that can be used to study iteration counts. The code is publicly available.
\par\par All results use {gmres} as the iterative solver and are displayed in tables detailing the iteration counts for different parameter configurations. In each table, the rows investigate weak scaling, while the columns vary a different parameter such as the end time $T$ or the regulari{z}{}ation parameter $\gamma$. The tables at the left perform scaling of $L$ by increasing $T$ -- those on the right by decreasing $\tau$.
\par As a base problem, we study a parabolic diffusion equation, which is self-adjoint and dissipative such that our theoretical results are directly applicable. The problem involves a heat equation in two dimensions on the spatial domain $\Omega=[0,1]^{2}$. It reads
\begin{equation}\partial_{t}y=\Delta y+u\end{equation}
with periodic boundary conditions and, in the case of tracking, a target trajectory
\begin{equation}y_{\mathrm{d}}(t,x)=\Bigl(\Bigl(12\pi^{2}+\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}\gamma}\Bigr)(t-T)-\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{(12\pi^{2})^{2}\gamma}\Bigr)\Bigr)\sin(2\pi x_{1})\sin(2\pi x_{2})\end{equation}
or, in the case of terminal cost, a target state
\begin{equation}y_{\mathrm{target}}(x)=\sin(2\pi x_{1})\sin(2\pi x_{2}){.}\end{equation}
This is the two-dimensional version of a problem studied in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{gotschelEfficientParallelinTimeMethod2019a}{}{}]}. In contrast to that paper, we use a non-smooth initial condition
\begin{equation}y_{\mathrm{init}}(x)=\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}\gamma}(1-T)\mathrm{sign}(\sin(2\pi x_{1}))\sin^{2}(2\pi x_{2}){,}\end{equation}
shown in Figure \ref{fig:num:intro:yinit-rough}.

We want to test our algorithms with a more challenging, non-smooth case.
\par Next to the self-adjoint equation \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:num:intro:diff} covered fully by this paper's analysis, we also consider a non-self-adjoint advection-diffusion equation that our new algorithms can solve, but for which we do not have theoretical results. Extending the previous equation with an advection term, consider
\begin{equation}\partial_{t}y=d\Delta y-\partial_{x_{1}}y-\partial_{x_{2}}y+u\end{equation}
where $d\in\mathbb{R}$ controls the amount of diffusion and may vary. For this equation, we use the same $y_{\mathrm{d}}$, $y_{\mathrm{target}}$ and $y_{\mathrm{init}}$, given in \lx@cref{creftypeplural\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:num:intro:yd}, \lx@cref{refnum}{eq:num:intro:ytarget} and\lx@nobreakspace\lx@cref{refnum}{eq:num:intro:yinit-rough}, as for the diffusion equation. \par The same experiments were done for terminal-cost objectives in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{tab:num:tc}, although $\alpha=10^{-4}$ was chosen here. The results are very promising. For the diffusion equation, iteration counts are low across the board, with no scenario surpassing $4$ iterations. The only exception is that of a \emph{very} small $\gamma$ (that is, a large $\widehat{\gamma}$), which was indeed theori{z}{}ed to work poorly in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:pd-tc:interp}.

Again, the qualitative insights from the self-adjoint case carry over: a small regulari{z}{}ation parameter $\gamma$ can cause slow convergence. \par\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Conclusions}
This paper has extended optimi{z}{}ation ParaDiag in three ways. For the existing algorithm \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{wuDiagonalizationbasedParallelintimeAlgorithms2020b}{}{}]}, aimed at tracking objectives, we proposed an alpha-circulant extension to improve the edge case of the regime with small final time $T$ and a generali{z}{}ation to non-self-adjoint problems. We also designed a new algorithm to treat terminal-cost objectives, which is robust with respect to changing $T$. In doing so, we greatly expanded the range of problems for which efficient ParaDiag algorithms are available.
\par Secondly, we were able to formulate a precise expression for the preconditioned eigenvalues of all optimi{z}{}ation ParaDiag methods, in the self-adjoint case. This significantly improves our understanding of these algorithms, for which very little theory was available before. We used this knowledge for two purposes. \begin{itemize}\itemize@item@For dissipative, self-adjoint equations with a tracking objective, and when using the new parameter $\alpha=-1$ to construct a preconditioner, we were able to prove a guaranteed {gmres} convergence factor of $1/2$.
\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@In a theoretical parallel-scaling analysis, we conjectured good weak scalability of all ParaDiag variants in the limit for many time steps.
\end{itemize}
This scalability was confirmed by numerical experiments that used {gmres} iteration counts as an indicator of performance. In addition, these tests suggested the theoretical conclusions carry over to the non-self-adjoint case, even though our analysis does not apply there.
\par As a third contribution, our progress clears the way for exciting research in the future. With a more general method, into which some theoretical insight is available, potential next steps include non-linear ParaDiag algorithms, such as those already proposed for {ivp} ParaDiag \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ganderTimeParallelizationNonlinear2017a,liuFastBlockAcirculant2020a}{}{}]}. Here, the robustness of the $\alpha=-1$ choice for tracking could prove important in dealing with low-$\widehat{\sigma}$ lineari{z}{}ations.
\par Another interesting avenue for future work building on our results is the study of different time-discreti{z}{}ation methods -- especially if they can be written as \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pd-track:anal:resc} or \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pd-tc:anal:resc} since then, our theoretical results apply. In addition, ParaDiag has shown promise for hyperbolic problems, which are often challenging for time-parallel methods. Next steps in this context could include a study of the methods in this paper for advection equations, or improvements to optimi{z}{}ation ParaDiag for wave equations \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{wuParallelInTimeBlockCirculantPreconditioner2020a}{}{}]} similar to those in this paper. On the computational side, different techniques have been applied to solve the smaller systems in the inversion procedure for {ivp} ParaDiag more efficiently \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{liuROMacceleratedParallelintimePreconditioner2020,heVankatypeMultigridSolver2022a}{}{}]}. Adapting these methods to optimi{z}{}ation ParaDiag could substantially improve performance.
\par We lastly mention some alternatives to the preconditioners proposed in this paper that may be worthwhile to pursue. First, a very interesting recent result \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{kressnerImprovedParallelintimeIntegration2022}{}{}]} in the domain of {ivp} ParaDiag suggests using alpha-circulant approximations, but not as preconditioners. Instead, it is noted that in the {ivp} situation, the exact system matrix is $P(\alpha)$ with $\alpha=0$ and its inversion is seen as an interpolation problem, with as data points several inversions with $\alpha_{j}\neq 0$. Our terminal-cost preconditioner \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pd-tc:new:Palpha} is not suitable for this, as it has $P(0)\neq A$. However, for the tracking preconditioner \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pd-track:alpha:Palpha}, $P(0)={\widehat{A}}$ does hold. As this text makes $\alpha\neq 1$ feasible for tracking, we can use different $\alpha_{j}$ with magnitude $1$ as data points and \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{kressnerImprovedParallelintimeIntegration2022}{}{}]}'s technique could now apply to tracking-type optimal-control ParaDiag. Second, for the terminal-cost case, alternative preconditioners (especially those that retain the $E$ block in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pd-tc:new:aao}) may improve on the convergence and scaling of our proposal in the regime with low $\widehat{\sigma}$ and high $\widehat{\gamma}$.

\par First, $w_{1}$ takes $\mathcal{D}_{0.5,+}$, moves it with its bottom corner to the origin and magnifies it by a factor of two. The mapping that accomplishes this is $w_{1}=2z-1+{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}$. Then $w_{2}$ maps the exterior of the semi-dis{k} into three quadrants. This can be done by the mapping $w_{2}=1/w_{1}+{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}/2$. Next, $w_{3}$ collapses three quadrants into a half-plane with the mapping $w_{3}=w_{2}^{2/3}$. We can then turn a half-plane into the exterior of the unit dis{k} through a M\"{o}bius transformation of the form $w_{4}=\frac{w_{3}-\beta^{*}}{w_{3}-\beta}$ for some $\beta$. Recall that $w$ should map $\infty$ to $\infty$; this can be done by taking $\beta$ to be the image of $\infty$ up until now. If $z=\infty$, we obtain $w_{1}=\infty$, $w_{2}={\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}/2$, and $w_{3}=({\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}/2)^{2/3}$. So setting $\beta=({\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}/2)^{2/3}$, $w_{4}$ is now determined. Finally, we find $w_{4}^{\prime}(z=\infty)=(-3\sqrt{3}+9{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu})/4$, so with $w_{5}=\exp(-2\pi{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu}/3)w_{4}$ we end up with $w^{\prime}(\infty)=(3\sqrt{3})/2>0$.
\par\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{fig:apdx-proof:pd-track:conform} illustrates the mapping $w$. The pole at $z=0$ maps to $w(0)=-2$, which is at distance $R=2$ from the origin. This concludes the proof.

Abstract
. We show that any function f : ℍ n → ℍ f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H} with f ⁡ ( z + c ) = f ⁡ ( z ) + c f(z+c)=f(z)+c , z ∈ ℍ n z\in\mathbb{H}^{n} , for some c > 0 c>0 has a property that any limit function of a family { f ⁡ ( t ​ z ) t } t > 0 \{\frac{f(tz)}{t}\}_{t>0} when t → ∞ t\to\infty is linear.

1.
Introduction

Our paper is motivated by results of V. Markovic and D. Gekhtman
[
3
,
1
,
2
]
.
Recently, Vladimir Markovic
[
3
]
solved a long-standing open problem in Teichmüller theory, by showing that
Carathéodory and Kobayashi metrics on Teichmuller spaces are generally different. One of the crucial step in the proof is the following rigidity result.
Here,
\mathbb{H}
denote the upper half-plane.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
be a holomorphic function satisfying the conditions:

(1)

f(\lambda,\dots,\lambda)=\lambda
,

(2)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{j}}(\lambda,\dots,\lambda)=\alpha_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n
, where
\alpha_{j}\in[0,1]
;

(3)

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}=1
;

for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{H}
. Assume that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(1)

f(z_{1}+c,\dots,z_{n}+c)=f(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})+c\quad\text{ for any }z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{H}.

Then
f(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}z_{j}
.

Theorem
1
for n=2 is proved in
[
3
]
and for any
n\geq 2
in
[
1
]
. If the proof for
n=2
in
[
3
]

is complex analytic, the proof for
n\geq 2
is based on some results from ergodic theory.
The aim of our paper is to analyze the properties of holomorphic mappings
f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
satisfying (
1
) for some constant
c>0
.
In particular, using the ideas from
[
3
]
, we show that any limit holomorphic function of the family
\{\frac{1}{t}f(tz)\}_{t>0}
is linear, i.e., any holomorphic function
h:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{f(t_{j}z)}{t_{j}}=h(z)
for a sequence
t_{j}\to\infty
is linear. In consequence, we have the following generalization of Theorem
1
for any
n\geq 2
by purely analytic proof.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
be a holomorphic mapping satisfying (
1
) for some
c>0
.
Assume that there exists a sequence
t_{j}\to\infty
and a holomorphic
mapping
h:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
such that
\frac{1}{t_{j}}f(t_{j}z)\to h(z)
for any
z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
Then
h
is linear.

Moreover, if there exists a point
w\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that
\Im(f(w)-h(w))=0
then there is a constant
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
with
f=h+\alpha
.

2.
Proofs

Let us start with the following simple remark (see discussion on page 19 in
[
3
]
).

Proposition 3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
be a holomorphic function and let
c>0
be a constant so that

f(z+c)=f(z)+c\quad\text{ for any }z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

If
\frac{1}{t_{j}}f(t_{j}z)\to h(z)
,
z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, when
j\to\infty
, where
h:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
is a holomorphic mapping then

(2)

h(z+s)=h(z)+s\quad\text{ for any }z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\text{ and any }s\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Note that
\frac{1}{t_{j}}f(t_{j}z)\to h(z)
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
Fix
s>0
. For any
j\geq 1
we have
t_{j}s=\ell_{j}c+q_{j}
, where
\ell_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq q_{j}<c
.
Then

h(z+s)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{j}}f(t_{j}z+t_{j}s)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{j}}\left(f\Big(t_{j}(z+\frac{q_{j}}{t_{j}})\Big)+\ell_{j}c\right)=h(z)+s.

∎

In
[
3
]
(see also
[
1
]
) it is proved.

Theorem 4
.

Let
h:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
be a holomorphic mapping such that

h(z+s)=h(z)+s\quad\text{ for any }z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\text{ and any }s\in\mathbb{R}.

Then there exist
\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}=1
and

h(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})=\alpha_{0}+\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\lambda_{j},

where
\Im\alpha_{0}\geq 0
,
\alpha_{j}\geq 0
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}=1
.

Frist recall the following version of a well-known result (see e.g. Proposition B.1 in
[
3
]
, Lemma 6.1 in
[
1
]
). For the sake of completeness, we give a proof.

Lemma 5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic mapping such that for some
A,B>0
we have

\Re(f(z))\leq A\max\{|z_{1}|,\dots,|z_{n}|\}+B,\quad\text{ for any }z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Then there exist
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
f(z)=\alpha_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}z_{j}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
n=1
.
The proof follows from the ideas from Nevannlina theory (see e.g.
[
4
]
).
Fix
R>0
. For any
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|<R
we have

\Re(f(z))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Re(f(Re^{i\theta}))\Re\left(\frac{Re^{i\theta}+z}{Re^{i\theta}-z}\right)d\theta.

And, therefore,

\displaystyle\Re(f^{\prime}(z))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Re(f(Re^{i\theta}))\frac{2Re^{i\theta}}{(Re^{i\theta}-z)^{2}}d\theta,

\displaystyle\Re(f^{\prime\prime}(z))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Re(f(Re^{i\theta}))\frac{-4Re^{i\theta}}{(Re^{i\theta}-z)^{3}}d\theta.

Using the equality
|x|=2x_{+}-x
we get

(3)

|\Re(f^{\prime\prime}(z))|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{4R}{(R-|z|)^{3}}\cdot\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\Re(f(Re^{i\theta}))|d\theta=\\
\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{4R}{(R-|z|)^{3}}\left(2\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\Re(f(Re^{i\theta})))_{+}d\theta-2\pi\Re f(0)\right).

Using the inequality
(\Re(f(Re^{i\theta}))_{+}\leq AR+B
and taking
R\to\infty
we get
\Re(f^{\prime\prime})=0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and put

H_{N}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n-1})=f(z_{1}+\lambda,\dots,z_{n-1}+\lambda,\lambda)-\lambda

where
\Im z_{j}>-N
and
\Im\lambda>N
. Note that the right hand side does not depend on
\lambda
and on
N
. Hence,

H=\lim_{N\to\infty}H_{N}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n-1})
and

f(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=z_{n}+H(z_{1}-z_{n},\dots,z_{n-1}-z_{n}).

We know that
f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
, hence

(4)

\Im(z_{n}+H(z_{1}-z_{n},\dots,z_{n-1}-z_{n}))>0

when
\Im z_{1},\dots,\Im z_{n}>0
.
Let us show that for any
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
we have

(5)

\Im(-H(w))\leq 2\max\{|w_{1}|,\dots,|w_{n-1}|\}.

Indeed, in the inequality (
4
) take
z_{1}=\dots=z_{n}\in\mathbb{H}
. Then
\Im H(0)\geq 0
.
If
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
is such that
w\not=0
then put

z_{n}=2i\max\{|w_{1}|,\dots,|w_{n-1}|\}

and
z_{j}=z_{n}+w_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n-1
. Note that
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{H}
. From the inequality (
4
) we get the inequality (
5
).
From Lemma
5
we have
H(w)=a_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}a_{j}w_{j}
. And, therefore,

f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})=\alpha_{0}+\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\lambda_{j},

where
\alpha_{j}\geq 0
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}=1
.
∎

For a convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
we denote by
d_{\Omega}
its hyperbolic distance. Recall that

d_{\mathbb{H}}(a,b)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{|a-\bar{b}|+|a-b|}{|a-\bar{b}|-|a-b|}=\log\frac{|a-\bar{b}|+|a-b|}{2\sqrt{\Im a\Im b}},

where
a,b\in\mathbb{H}
. We have also

d_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(z,w)=\max_{j=1,\dots,n}d_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{j},w_{j}),

where
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}),w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

As a simple corollary of the above formula we get.

Lemma 6
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{H}
be such that
|a|<|b|
. Then

\left|d_{\mathbb{H}}(a,b)-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{|b|^{2}}{\Im a\Im b}\right|\leq\frac{|a|}{|b|}.

From this result it is easy follows.

Corollary 7
.

Let
f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
be a holomorphic mapping and let
w=(iw_{1},\dots,iw_{n})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}>0
, be a fixed point.
Assume that for a sequence
t_{j}\to\infty
we have

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{f(t_{j}w)}{t_{j}}=a
, where
a\in\mathbb{H}
.
Then for any
z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that
\Im z=\Im w
we have

\Im f(z)\geq\frac{|a|^{2}}{\Im a}\geq\Im a.

Proof.

Fix a point
z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that
\Im z=\Im w
. Then

d_{\mathbb{H}}(f(z),t_{j}a)=d_{\mathbb{H}}(f(z),f(t_{j}w))+d_{\mathbb{H}}(f(t_{j}w),t_{j}a).

Note that

d_{\mathbb{H}}(f(t_{j}w),t_{j}a)=d_{\mathbb{H}}\Big(\frac{f(t_{j}w)}{t_{j}},a\Big)\to 0\quad\text{ when }j\to\infty.

We have

d_{\mathbb{H}}(f(z),f(t_{j}w))\leq d_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(z,t_{j}w)=\max_{k}d_{\mathbb{H}}(z_{k},t_{j}w_{k})=\frac{1}{2}\log t_{j}+o(1)

and

d_{\mathbb{H}}(f(z),t_{j}a)=\frac{1}{2}\log t_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{|a|^{2}}{\Im f(z)\Im a}+o(1).

Taking
j\to\infty
we get
\Im f(z)\geq\frac{|a|^{2}}{\Im a}\geq\Im a
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Note that for a linear function we have
\Im h(z)=\Im h(i\Im z)
.
From the above result we have

\Im f(z)\geq\Im h(z),\quad\text{ for any }z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

Moreover, if
\Im(f(z_{0})-h(z_{0}))\leq 0
then a function
F(z)=e^{i(f(z)-h(z))}
is such that
|F|\leq 1
and
|F(z_{0})|=1
. So, from the maximum principle we get
F=e^{is}
where
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and, therefore,
f=h+c
, where
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Remark 8
.

Let
f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
be a holomorphic function and let
m>0
be such that
f(mz)=mf(z)
for any
z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
It is immediate, if
f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
are holomorphic mappings such that
f_{j}(mz)=mf_{j}(z)
then

a_{1}f_{1}+a_{2}f_{2}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
,
a_{1},a_{2}>0
, also has the same property.

Note that for any
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in[0,1]
,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}=1
, the function

g_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}}(z)=z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}

has the property
g_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}}(mz)=mg_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}}(z)
. So, for
n\geq 2
there exist a big family of functions which satisfy the property
f(mz)=mf(z)
.

Quasi-Hermitian extended SSH models
Iao-Fai Io, Cheng-Yuan Huang, Jhih-Shih You, Hao-Chun Chang, Hsien-chung Kao

Abstract
We consider the quasi-Hermitian limit of a non-Hermitian extended Su–Schrieffer–Heeger model, in which the hopping amplitudes obey a specific relation so that the system may be mapped to a corresponding Hermitian one and its energy spectrum is completely real. Analogous to the Hermitian case, one may use the modified winding number ν ¯ \bar{\nu} to determine the total number of edge states on the boundaries so that a modified bulk-boundary correspondence may be achieved. Due to the skin effect in non-Hermitian systems, the spectral winding numbers ν E L \nu_{E}^{L} and ν E R \nu_{E}^{R} must be used to classify such systems further. It dictates how the edge states would be distributed over the left and right boundaries. We then naively extend the criteria to the cases that the quasi-Hermitian condition is violated. For all the cases that we consider no inconsistency has been found.

When this occurs, we expect that the system may be mapped to a Hermitian type 1 extended SSH model, and all the energy eigenvalues would be real. For this reason, we will call this constraint the quasi-Hermitian condition (QHC). Note that quasi-Hermiticity is only a subset of pseudo-Hermiticity since there obviously exist NH extended systems that do not satisfy the QHC but still have a real energy spectrum.

Now, let’s show explicitly how the system may be mapped to a Hermitian type 1 extended SSH model when the QHC is satisfied. First, we would like to mention that when this is the case, only two of the equations in eqs. (
32
) are independent. They may be reduced to the following forms:

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\bar{u}_{1}+\bar{u}_{2}=-\left(t_{0}^{L}t_{1}^{L}+t_{1}^{R}t_{2}^{L}\right)/(2rt_{0}^{L}t_{2}^{L}),

(42)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt2+4\bar{u}_{1}\bar{u}_{2}=\left(t_{0}^{L}t_{0}^{R}+t_{1}^{L}t_{1}^{R}+t_{2}^{L}t_{2}^{R}-E^{2}\right)/(r^{2}t_{0}^{L}t_{2}^{L}),

(43)

in terms of
\bar{u}_{1}=\left(\bar{s}_{1}+\bar{s}_{1}^{-1}\right)/2
and
\bar{u}_{2}=\left(\bar{s}_{2}+\bar{s}_{2}^{-1}\right)/2
. Upon using eqs. (
28
), we see
\alpha_{I}=\left\{\left(t_{0}^{L}+t_{1}^{R}s_{I}^{-1}+t_{2}^{R}s_{I}^{-2}\right)/E\right\}\beta_{I}
, with
I=1,\ldots,4
. After some algebra, the characteristic equation for
\bar{s}
may be obtained from the condition that the determinant formed by the coefficients of
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{4}
in eqs. (
36
) is vanishing:

\displaystyle\hskip-73.97733pt\left\{\bar{t}_{0}U_{N+2}(\bar{u}_{1})+\bar{t}_{1}U_{N+1}(\bar{u}_{1})+\bar{t}_{2}U_{N}(\bar{u}_{1})\right\}\left\{\bar{t}_{0}U_{N}(\bar{u}_{2})+\bar{t}_{1}U_{N-1}(\bar{u}_{2})+\bar{t}_{2}U_{N-2}(\bar{u}_{2})\right\}

(44)

\displaystyle\hskip-83.93553pt+\left\{\bar{t}_{0}U_{N+2}(\bar{u}_{2})+\bar{t}_{1}U_{N+1}(\bar{u}_{2})+\bar{t}_{2}U_{N}(\bar{u}_{2})\right\}\left\{\bar{t}_{0}U_{N}(\bar{u}_{1})+\bar{t}_{1}U_{N-1}(\bar{u}_{1})+\bar{t}_{2}U_{N-2}(\bar{u}_{1})\right\}

(45)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt-2\left\{\bar{t}_{0}U_{N+1}(\bar{u}_{1})+\bar{t}_{1}U_{N}(\bar{u}_{1})+\bar{t}_{2}U_{N-1}(\bar{u}_{1})\right\}\left\{\bar{t}_{0}U_{N+1}(\bar{u}_{2})+\bar{t}_{1}U_{N}(\bar{u}_{2})+\bar{t}_{2}U_{N-1}(\bar{u}_{2})\right\}

(46)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt+2\left\{\bar{t}_{0}^{2}+\bar{t}_{1}^{2}+\bar{t}_{2}^{2}-2\bar{t}_{0}\bar{t}_{2}\left(1+2\bar{u}_{1}\bar{u}_{2}\right)\right\}=0.

(47)

Similar to the case of the NH SSH model, we have further introduced
\bar{t}_{2}=\sqrt{t_{2}^{L}t_{2}^{R}}
. In terms of
\bar{t}_{0},\bar{t}_{1}
and
\bar{t}_{2}
, eq. (
42

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt\bar{u}_{1}+\bar{u}_{2}=-\bar{t}_{1}\left(\bar{t}_{0}+\bar{t}_{2}\right)/(2\bar{t}_{0}\bar{t}_{2}),

(48)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptE^{2}=\bar{t}_{0}^{2}+\bar{t}_{1}^{2}+\bar{t}_{2}^{2}+2\bar{t}_{0}\bar{t}_{2}\left(2+4\bar{u}_{1}\bar{u}_{2}\right).

(49)

The above characteristic equation is exactly the same as the one obtained in Ref.
Ext-SSH
if we identify the parameters
(\bar{t}_{0},\bar{t}_{1},\bar{t}_{2})
with
(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2})
.

By substituting the first part in the above equation into eq. (
44
), we obtain a characteristic equation in terms of
\bar{u}_{1}
which is a polynomial of degree
2N+2
. By closer examination, we see that there is an additional factor
\left[\bar{u}_{1}+\bar{t}_{1}\left(\bar{t}_{0}+\bar{t}_{2}\right)/(4\bar{t}_{0}\bar{t}_{2})\right]^{2}
in the characteristic equation, which does not correspond to any physical states and can be ignored. Since both equations are symmetric with respect to
\bar{u}_{1}
and
\bar{u}_{2}
, we may always require
\bar{u}_{1}>\bar{u}_{2}
to remove the redundancy in the solutions of
\bar{u}_{1}
and
\bar{u}_{2}
. Note that here
\bar{u}_{1}
and
\bar{u}_{2}
are generally complex, and the relation
\bar{u}_{1}>\bar{u}_{2}
is defined by
{\rm Re}\{\bar{u}_{1}\}>{\rm Re}\{\bar{u}_{2}\}
or
{\rm Re}\{\bar{u}_{1}\}={\rm Re}\{\bar{u}_{2}\}
and
{\rm Im}\{\bar{u}_{1}\}>{\rm Im}\{\bar{u}_{2}\}
. Eventually, we find
N
roots of
u_{1}
consistent with what we expected. We would like to mention that by solving the characteristic equation in eq. (
44
), we may also obtain the energy spectrum, which agrees completely with that achieved by numerical diagonalization.

Analogous to the Hermitian case, the edge states have almost zero energy, and the characteristic
s
’s associated with these states satisfy the condition in eq. (
28
) and
\beta\approx 0
. This gives rise to

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptt_{0}^{R}+t_{1}^{L}s+t_{2}^{L}s^{2}\approx 0,

(50)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptt_{0}^{L}+t_{1}^{R}s^{-1}+t_{2}^{R}s^{-2}\approx 0.

(51)

In terms of
\bar{s}
, the first one leads to the following quadratic relation

\displaystyle\bar{t}_{0}+\bar{t}_{1}\bar{s}+\bar{t}_{2}\bar{s}^{2}\approx 0.

(52)

Thus, the system may be classified according to the modified winding number
\bar{\nu}
, which dictates in the above quadratic equation the number of roots with absolute values less than 1, just like in the Hermitian case
Ext-SSH
. It is known that there are three different phases, and we expect that
\bar{\nu}
would also determine the total number of edge states on the system’s boundaries.

1)

\bar{t}_{2}+\bar{t}_{0}>\bar{t}_{1}
, and
\bar{t}_{2}>\bar{t}_{0}
:
The system is in the topological phase with
\bar{\nu}=2
. There are four edge states in total on the boundaries.

2)

\bar{t}_{2}+\bar{t}_{0}<\bar{t}_{1}
:
The system is in the topological phase with
\bar{\nu}=1
. There are two edge states in total on the boundaries.

3)

\bar{t}_{2}+\bar{t}_{0}>\bar{t}_{1}
, and
\bar{t}_{2}<\bar{t}_{0}
:
The system is in the trivial phase with
\bar{\nu}=0
. There are no edge states.

Again, this may also be obtained using a generalized BZ or modified PBCs
Skin-effect4
;
GBZ4
;
GBZ5
;

.

Because of the skin effect, the edge states may not be evenly distributed over the left and right boundaries. Similar to the NH SSH model, we may use
\nu_{E}
to determine where they would reside. In the continuum limit, we have from eq. (
30
)

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037ptE^{2}\left(p\right)=\left(t_{0}^{R}+t_{1}^{L}e^{ip}+t_{2}^{L}e^{2ip}\right)\left(t_{0}^{L}+t_{1}^{R}e^{-ip}+t_{2}^{R}e^{-2ip}\right).

(53)

Again, we have
\nu_{E}=\nu_{E}^{L}+\nu_{E}^{R}
, with
\nu_{E}^{L}
the spectral winding number corresponding to the factor
\left(t_{0}^{R}+t_{1}^{L}e^{ip}+t_{2}^{L}e^{2ip}\right)
, and
\nu_{E}^{R}
corresponding to
\left(t_{0}^{L}+t_{1}^{R}e^{-ip}+t_{2}^{R}e^{-2ip}\right)
. Just like in the Hermitian case, the number of solutions to eqs. (
50
) and (
51
) that have magnitudes smaller or larger than 1 indicates the possible numbers of edge states on the left and right boundaries, respectively. In addition, we must bear in mind that the total number of edge states is
2\bar{\nu}
. Now, let’s summarize the correspondence between
(\nu_{E}^{L},\nu_{E}^{R})
and the locations of the edge states:

1)

\bar{\nu}=2
:

i)

\nu_{E}=2
. All four edge states are on the left boundary.

ii)

\nu_{E}=1
. Three edge states are on the left boundary and one on the right.

iii)

\nu_{E}=0
. Two edge states are on the left boundary and two on the right.

iv)

\nu_{E}=-1
. One edge state is on the left boundary and three on the right.

v)

\nu_{E}=-2
. All four edge states are on the right boundary.

2)

\bar{\nu}=1
:

i)

(\nu_{E}^{L},\nu_{E}^{R})=(2,0),(1,0),\framebox{(2, -1)}
. Both edge states are on the left boundary.

ii)

(\nu_{E}^{L},\nu_{E}^{R})=\framebox{(2, -1)},(2,-2),(1,-1),(0,0),\framebox{(1, -2)}
. One edge state is on the left boundary and one is on the right.

iii)

(\nu_{E}^{L},\nu_{E}^{R})=\framebox{(1, -2)},(0,-1),(0,-2)
. Both edge states are on the right boundary.

3)

\bar{\nu}=0
:
\nu_{E}=2,1,0,-1,2
. No edge state.

Note that we put a box around
(2,-1)
and
(1,-2)
to indicate that there is an ambiguity in determining the locations of the edge states when
(\nu_{E}^{L},\nu_{E}^{R})
equal to these values.

From eqs. (
40
) and (
41
), we see if
(t_{0}^{L},t_{0}^{R},t_{1}^{L},t_{1}^{R},t_{2}^{L},t_{2}^{R})

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt(t_{0}^{L},t_{1}^{R},t_{2}^{R})\to(\tilde{r}^{-1}t_{0}^{L},t_{1}^{R},\tilde{r}t_{2}^{R}),

\displaystyle\hskip-88.2037pt(t_{0}^{R},t_{1}^{L},t_{2}^{L})\to(\tilde{r}t_{0}^{R},t_{1}^{L},\tilde{r}^{-1}t_{2}^{L}),

(54)

then
(\bar{t}_{0},\bar{t}_{1},\bar{t}_{2})
would remain the same, while the skin effect factor will change by a factor of
\tilde{r}
. Since the characteristic equation and energy spectrum only depend on

\tilde{r}
to scale the magnitude of the
(s_{I})^{\prime}s
corresponding to the edge states. Hence we may vary the value of
\nu_{E}
between
-2
and
2

\nu_{E}
zero, the edge states the trajectory of
E
will enclose all the “bulk” states so it gives us another way to identify the edge states in the system, especially when we consider general NH extended SSH models.

.5
V. Conclusion and discussion

In this paper, we study the NH extended SSH models. Just like in the Hermitian case, there are two types of NH extended SSH models. We first focus on the quasi-Hermitian case, in which the hopping amplitudes obey a specific relation so that the system may be mapped to a corresponding Hermitian one and its energy spectrum is completely real. It is well-known that the conventional BBC may not be naively applied in an NH system. However, we may use the modified winding number
\bar{\nu}
to classify the system and determine the total number of edge states on the boundaries analogous to the Hermitian case. Moreover, we show that the spectral winding numbers,
(\nu_{E}^{L},\nu_{E}^{R})
, may be used to classify the system further. It dictates how the edge states would be distributed over the left and right boundaries.

By analyzing the EOM and BCs of a finite chain of such a system, we are able to find the characteristic equation in terms of
\bar{u}_{1}
and
\bar{u}_{2}
, which in turn satisfy a linear relation implied by the secular equation. By solving numerically the characteristic and secular equations, we may find the energy spectrum of the system. The results are then compared to those obtained by numerically solving the matrix eigenvalue problem. They agree well with each other.

We then relax the QHC and consider general NH extended SSH models. Although we are still able to express the characteristic equation in terms of one single variable
\bar{u}=\left(\bar{s}_{12}+\bar{s}_{12}^{-1}\right)/2
, the energy eigenvalues of such a system are complex in general. We continue to employ the classification in terms of
\bar{\nu}
and
(\nu_{E}^{L},\nu_{E}^{R})
that we obtained under the QHC to check whether it is still applicable for general NH extended SSH models. For all the cases we considered, no inconsistency has been found. In principle, one may use the generalized BZ to calculate the modified winding number in general NH extended SSH models but we are not able to obtain an analytic expression at the moment. It is worth mentioning that similar to the Hermitian case, the two types of extended SSH models are related by a duality and may be mapped to each other by re-grouping the “atoms” in a unit cell.

It is known that non-Hermiticity also opens up the possibility that the Hamiltonian matrix becomes defective, and Jordan decomposition must be exploited to diagonalize such systems. As a result, generalized eigenvectors would appear. In the literature, this is referred to as an exceptional point in the parameter space of the hopping amplitudes. Finding the condition for exceptional points and their implications in NH systems remains an intriguing problem. Another interesting direction is to extend our analysis of 1D NH systems to 2D or higher dimensions. It is known that a relation between the 1D winding number and the 2D Chern number may be obtained if there is a certain symmetry in the system
Ext-SSH
. It would be interesting to carry out a similar study of NH systems to see whether such a relation still exists.

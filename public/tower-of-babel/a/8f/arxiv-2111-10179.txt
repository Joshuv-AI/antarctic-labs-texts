Backstepping-based Integral Sliding Mode Control with Time Delay Estimation for Autonomous Underwater Vehicles
Hossein Nejatbakhsh Esfahani, Behdad Aminian, Esten Ingar Grøtli, Sébastien Gros

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to propose a high performance control approach for trajectory tracking of Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs). However, the controller performance can be affected by the unknown perturbations including model uncertainties and external time-varying disturbances in an undersea environment. To address this problem, a Backstepping-based Integral Sliding Mode Control with Time Delay Estimation (BS-ISMC-TDE) is designed. To improve the performance of a conventional backstepping control algorithm, an Integral Sliding Mode Control (ISMC) approach is adopted in the backstepping design to attenuate the steady-state error. Moreover, an adaptive Time Delay Estimation (TDE) strategy is proposed to provide an estimation of perturbations by observing the inputs and the states of the AUV one step into the past without an exact knowledge of the dynamics and the upper bound of uncertainties. From the simulation results, it is shown that the proposed control approach using both adaptive and conventional TDE parts outperforms a Backstepping-based Integral Sliding Mode Control (BS-ISMC).



I
Introduction

In recent years, underwater vehicles have been extensively
developed because of needs in marine industries and their applications such as nondestructive test of the marine structures or underwater oil/gas pipeline and ocean exploration. One of the main reasons that the autonomous marine operation systems in particular the Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) are getting more attention is potentially dangerous environment and precise tasks which are difficult and time consuming to accomplish manually
[
1
]
.

However, the control of the AUVs in a fully autonomous mode is a major challenging problem since this platform includes a coupled and highly nonlinear dynamic. There are severe external time-varying disturbances that affect the tracking performance for such an autonomous platform. Furthermore, the uncertainties raised from the hydrodynamics forces, the added inertia matrix, the added Coriolis and centripetal matrix, and the hydrodynamic damping terms should be considered and tackled.

In
[
2
]
, an adaptive fuzzy Sliding Mode Control (SMC) approach was proposed while a neural network compensator was adopted to cope with the uncertainties. A second-order sliding-mode control algorithm for suppressing the parameter uncertainties and external disturbances was proposed in
[
3
]
. To eliminate the effects of uncertainties and chattering phenomenon, a hybrid robust control scheme including the SMC and
H^{\infty}
was proposed in
[
4
]
. A robust model predictive control of underwater-vehicle manipulator systems was proposed in
[
5
]
. In this robust control scheme, an uncertainty compensator based on fuzzy logic was designed. In the context of backstepping (BS) algorithm as another well-known robust control approach for AUVs, some works were conducted in
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
. Although the BS is a well-known, simple and useful control approach for the nonlinear systems, the algorithm is not enough robust against both matched and mismatched uncertainties. Therefore, people usually combine a BS with adaptive control approaches or SMC method. Besides, BS requires a full knowledge of the dynamic and the derivatives of the states, which it is mostly difficult to have a quite accurate model for an AUV.

To improve the trajectory tracking accuracy of AUV, authors in
[
8
]
proposed to leverage the properties of two robust control schemes as a combined backstepping-based terminal sliding mode controller. In the SMC-based backstepping control algorithms, the SMC law is adopted in the first step of the backstepping design to attenuate the steady-state error and to improve the robustness in presence of mismatched and state dependant uncertainties.

The advantages of using SMC include fast response and convergence, promising transient performance and acceptable robustness. The Time Delay Estimation (TDE) concept is a key part of Time Delay Control (TDC) approach so as to take advantage of its model-free nature
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. Indeed, the TDE provides an estimation of perturbations by observing the inputs and the states of the dynamical model one step into the past without an exact knowledge of the dynamics and the upper bound of uncertainties. To address the properties of the SMC and TDC in a unified controller, researchers proposed to combine these robust control schemes in
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
.

To improve the tracking accuracy for motion control of AUVs under external time-varying disturbances and uncertainties, we propose to leverage three robust control approaches including: Backstepping (BS), Integral Sliding Mode Control (ISMC) and Time Delay Control (TDC) in order to provide a high performance and sufficiently robust control scheme. We also propose a gain adaptation strategy for the TDE part to tune a crucial matrix (
\bar{M}
) in the context of TDC algorithms.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section II, a four degrees of freedom AUV is modeled and described. Section III, the proposed control approach and a stability analysis based on Lyapunov theory are presented. The effectiveness of the proposed robust control scheme for the trajectory tracking scenarios is investigated and illustrated in Section IV. Finally, conclusions are drawn in Section V.

III
Control Approach

In this section, we first propose to leverage the properties of both robust control approaches Backstepping and ISMC as a Backstepping-based Integral Sliding Mode (BS-ISMC) and then enhance the performance of BS-ISMC with considering a TDE part as a compensator to cope with the lumped uncertainties including the unknown uncertain dynamic in the context of the robust control design.

III-A

Backstepping-based Integral Sliding Mode

Let us define the tracking error and its time derivative as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{d},

(11a)

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}=\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d}

(11b)

As the first step, the following Lyapunov function is used to ensure the stability of the system:

\displaystyle V_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}},

(12a)

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{2}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d})

(12b)

As an step in the backstepping design, the state variable
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{2}=\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}
in the state-space model (
9
) can be interpreted as a virtual control signal. Then, using the virtual control
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{2}=\alpha_{0}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}})=-k_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}+\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d}
, the origin of
\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}=-k_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}
is globally asymptotically stable since
\dot{V}_{0}=-k_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}<0,\quad\forall\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}\neq 0\right)\in\mathcal{R}
where
k_{1}
is a positive diagonal matrix. An integral action can be added to the virtual control as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{2}=\alpha(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}})=-k_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}-k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}(\tau)d\tau+\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d}

(13)

where
k_{2}
is also a positive diagonal matrix. Then, the time derivative of tracking error can be modified as follows:

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}=-k_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}-k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}(\tau)d\tau

(14)

The Lyapunov function addressing the integral action can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle V_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}+\frac{1}{2}k_{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}(\tau)d\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}(\tau)d\tau\right)^{\top}

(15)

The time derivative of the above function reads as:

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{1}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}+k_{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}(\tau)d\tau\right)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}

(16)

Then, substituting (
14
) to (
16
), the derivative of Lyapunov function is delivered as a negative semi-definite
-k_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}\leq 0
and then the system augmented by an integral action is asymptotically stable in the first stage of the backstepping design. The sliding mode surface based on the deviation of the proposed virtual control form its desired signal in the backstepping control scheme can be defined as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}=\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}-\alpha(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}})=\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}+k_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}+k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}(\tau)d\tau-\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d}

(17)

Considering the tracking error in (
11a
) and the above sliding surface, the time derivative of tracking error can be obtained as follows:

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}-k_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}-k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}(\tau)d\tau

(18)

Let us define a composite Lyapunov function as:

\displaystyle V=V_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}

(19)

considering equations (
16
), (
18
) and (
19
):

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}+k_{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}(\tau)d\tau+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}-k_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}-k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}(\tau)d\tau\right)+k_{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\left(\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}+k_{1}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}+k_{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}\right)

(20)

Substituting
\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}=\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}-\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d}
and (
9
) to the above equation:

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}-k_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}-k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}(\tau)d\tau\right)+k_{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}})+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}}+k_{1}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}+k_{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}-\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d}\right)=-k_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}

\displaystyle+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}})+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}}+k_{1}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}+(I+k_{2})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}-\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d}\right)

(21)

Then, the above Lyapunov function can be negative definite where the following condition is satisfied:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}})+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}}+k_{1}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}+(I+k_{2})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}-\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d}=-k_{3}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}

(22)

where
k_{3}
is a positive diagonal matrix. Therefore, the equivalent control input can be obtained as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}_{e}=M(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}})\left[-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}})-k_{1}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}-(I+k_{2})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}-k_{3}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}+\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d}\right]

(23)

Then, the Backstepping-based Integral Sliding Mode Control (BS-ISMC) law is designed as:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}_{e}-M(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}})\Gamma\text{sgn}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}})

(24)

where
\Gamma
is a positive diagonal matrix and the second part of the above control law reads as a reaching mode in the context of sliding mode control algorithms.

III-B

BS-ISMC with Time Delay Estimation

According to the TDE concept as a part of Time Delay Control (TDC) approach
[
12
]
, we first introduce a constant diagonal inertia matrix
\bar{M}
. Then, the control law (
24

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}=

\displaystyle\bar{M}\left[-k_{1}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}-(I+k_{2})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}-k_{3}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}+\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d}-\Gamma\text{sgn}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}})\right]

\displaystyle+(C_{n}+D_{n}+\text{g}_{n})\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}+\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}}_{d}-\delta

(25)

where the nominal model and perturbation model are described as
N=(C_{n}+D_{n}+\text{g}_{n})\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}=\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}}_{d}-\delta
, respectively.
Let us define an estimation based on the TDE concept for the applied perturbations including the model uncertainties and external disturbances as follows

13
]

\displaystyle\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}_{t-L}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}(t-L)-N(t-L)-\bar{M}\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}(t-L)

(26)

where
L
reads as a small delay time for the TDE part and,

\displaystyle\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}(t-L)=\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}(t)-2\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}(t-L)+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}(t-2L)}{L^{2}}

(27)

Substituting (
26
) to (
III-B
), the proposed BS-ISMC-TDE control law is designed as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}(t-L)+\bar{M}\left[-k_{1}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}-(I+k_{2})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}-k_{3}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}+\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d}\right]

\displaystyle+\bar{M}\left[-\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}(t-L)-\Gamma\text{sgn}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}})\right]+N-N(t-L)

(28)

III-C

Stability Analysis

A closed-loop system for (
9
) with the proposed BS-ISMC-TDE control scheme can be uniformly ultimately bounded (UUB)

Lemma 1

The TDE error
\epsilon_{i}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}_{i}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}_{i}
is bounded
\epsilon_{i}^{+}\geq\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}_{i}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}
if the following condition is satisfied:

\displaystyle\left\|I-M^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}})\bar{M}\right\|<1

(29)

In practice,
\bar{M}
is adjusted by increasing its diagonal
elements from a small positive value to a large value before the system approaches the oscillatory response. Let us obtain the first-time derivative of sliding surface (
17

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}=\bar{M}^{-1}\left(\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{p}}}+\epsilon-N\right)+k_{1}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}}+k_{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{e}}-\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{d}

(30)

Substituting (
24
) to
\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}}
in the above equation, the closed loop dynamics can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}=\bar{M}^{-1}\epsilon-\Gamma sgn(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}})-k_{3}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}

(31)

Then, the first-derivative of the Lyapunov function (
19
) considering the term including the sliding surface is expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}

\displaystyle=-k_{3}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\left(\bar{M}^{-1}\epsilon-\Gamma sgn(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})\right)

\displaystyle=-k_{3}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}-\Gamma|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}|+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\bar{M}^{-1}\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq-\left(k_{3}+\Gamma\right)|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}|+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\bar{M}^{-1}\epsilon

Thus, if
{k_{3}}_{ii}+\Gamma_{ii}>\bar{M}_{ii}^{-1}\epsilon_{i}^{+}
, then
\dot{V}<0
. However, a proper adjustment of the constant matrix

\displaystyle\bar{V}=\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\bar{M}_{ii}\right)^{2}

Considering (
1
), the first derivative of the above Lyapunov function can be defined by the following inequality:

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{V}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}^{\top}\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{M}_{ii}\dot{\bar{M}}_{ii}\leq-k_{3}|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}|+

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{M}}_{ii}=\Gamma_{ii}|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{i}|-\alpha_{i}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{i}}{\bar{M}_{ii}^{2}}\right)

Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed to leverage three robust control schemes for improving a trajectory tracking control of autonomous underwater vehicles in presence of external time-varying disturbances and uncertainties. In the proposed ,Backstepping-based Integral Sliding Mode Control with Time Delay Estimation (BS-ISMC-TDE), the backstepping part is responsible for tracking the desired trajectory while the Integral Sliding Mode Control (ISMC) is designed for tackling the uncertainties and external time varying disturbances. To improve the accuracy of the tracking in presence of some unknown parameters and to compensate the effect of unmodeled dynamics, the Time Delay Estimation (TDE) is applied to the BS-ISMC. Besides, an adaptive mechanism for adjusting the TDE part is proposed to address a problem with a poorly-adjusted
\bar{M}
. As a future work, we will investigate a fully adaptive BS-ISMC-TDE in which all gains related to the BS and ISMC parts are autonomously adjusted as well.

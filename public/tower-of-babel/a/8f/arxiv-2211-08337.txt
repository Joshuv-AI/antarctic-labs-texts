Hopf algebras of multiple polylogarithms, and holomorphic 1-forms Thanks: C. Z. was supported in part by DMS-1711405. 2020 Mathematics Classification. Primary 11G55. Secondary 19E15, 14D07, 32G20. Key words and phrases: Hopf algebras of polylogarithms, variation matrices, iterated integrals, multiple polylogarithms, variations of mixed Hodge structures, symbol map.
Zachary Greenberg, Dani Kaufman, Haoran Li, Christian K. Zickert

Abstract
. We associate to a multiple polylogarithm a holomorphic 1-form on the universal abelian cover of its domain. We relate the 1-forms to the symbol and variation matrix and show that the 1-forms naturally define a lift of the variation of mixed Hodge structure associated to a polylogarithm. The results are conveniently described in terms of a variant ℍ symb \mathbb{H}^{\symb} of Goncharov’s Hopf algebra of multiple polylogarithms. In particular, we show that the association of a 1-form to a multiple polylogarithm induces a map from the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the Lie coalgebra of indecomposables of ℍ symb \mathbb{H}^{\symb} to the de Rham complex.



1.
Summary of Results

The
multiple polylogarithm

[
Gon95
]
of
weight

n_{1}+\dots+n_{d}
and
depth

d
is defined by the power series

(1)

\Li_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=\sum_{k_{1}<\dots<k_{d}}\frac{x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{k_{d}}}{k_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots k_{d}^{n_{d}}}.

It defines a multivalued (single valued on the universal cover) holomorphic function on the space

S_{d}(\mathbb{C})=\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\bigm|x_{i}\neq 0,\,\prod_{r=i}^{j}x_{r}\neq 1\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq j\leq d\right\}.

There is a simple model for the universal abelian cover of

, namely

(3)

\widehat{S}_{d}(\mathbb{C})=\left\{(u_{i},v_{i,j})\in\mathbb{C}^{d+\binom{d+1}{2}}\bigm|\exp(\sum_{r=i}^{j}u_{i})+\exp(v_{i,j})=1\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq j\leq d\right\}.

Our goal is to associate to a multiple polylogarithm
\Li_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})
of depth
d
a holomorphic 1-form
w_{\mathbf{n}}
on
\widehat{S}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, and to study the structure and properties of these forms. Most of our results are defined using a Hopf algebra
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
of symbolic polylogarithms.

.

When
d=1
,
\widehat{S}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
is the space
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid e^{u}+e^{v}=1\}
introduced by Neumann
[
Neu04
]
and playing a prominent role e.g. in
[
Zic15
,
GTZ15
,
Zic19
]
.

1.1.
Hopf algebras of polylogarithms

Motivated by attempts to construct the Hopf algebra of regular functions on the motivic Galois group, Goncharov
[
Gon05
,
Gon02
]
constructed several Hopf algebras related to polylogarithms and iterated integrals. The slightly modified variant
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
considered here is a free Hopf algebra with generators
[x_{i_{1}\to i_{2}},\dots,x_{i_{d}\to i_{d+1}}]_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
and
[x_{i}]_{0}
in one-to-one correspondence with functions

(4)

\Li_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}(x_{i_{1}\to i_{2}},\dots,x_{i_{d}\to i_{d+1}}),\qquad\log(x_{i}),\quad\text{ where }\qquad x_{i\to j}=\prod_{r=i}^{j-1}x_{r}.

We stress that the variables
x_{i}
are in order and form an unbroken sequence, e.g. there are no generators corresponding to
\Li_{2,1}(x_{2},x_{1})
or
\Li_{2,2}(x_{1},x_{3})
. The coproduct is the one defined by Goncharov
[
Gon05
, Prop. 6.1]
except that the
inverse terms
(e.g.
\Li_{2,1}(x_{2}^{-1},x_{1}^{-1})
) appearing in Goncharov’s formula are inverted using a function
\INV
, which is a variant of Goncharov’s
inversion formula

[
Gon01
, Sec. 2.6]
. We refer to Section
2
for the precise definition.

A key property of
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
is that the coproduct of
[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
can be written entirely in terms of
contractions
. For
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{d+1})
a strictly increasing subsequence with
i_{d+1}\leq n+1
, the contraction of
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
by
\mathbf{i}
is given by

(5)

\mathbf{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=(x_{i_{1}\to i_{2}},\dots,x_{i_{d}\to i_{d+1}}).

As a result we obtain that for any
contraction system
, i.e. a collection of sets
\{X_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
with contractions
\mathbf{i}\colon X_{n}\to X_{d}
(see Definition
2.12
) we have a Hopf algebra freely generated by symbols
[\alpha]_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
for
\alpha\in X_{d}
and
[\alpha]_{0}
for
\alpha\in X_{1}
. The coproduct is induced by that of
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
. We have natural contraction systems associated to the following situations (see Section
2.2
):

•

A field
F
.

•

A field
F
together with a torsion free
\mathbb{Z}
-extension
\pi\colon E\to F^{*}
.

•

An open subset of a complex manifold
M
.

•

Any simplicial set
S_{\bullet}
.

We thus obtain Hopf algebras (a sheaf of Hopf algebras in the third case)

(6)

\mathbb{H}^{\symb}(F),\qquad\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{\symb}_{E}(F),\qquad\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{M},\qquad\mathbb{H}^{\symb}_{S_{\bullet}}.

The “hat” indicates that the contraction system involves a universal abelian cover (or an algebraic analogue).

For any graded Hopf algebra
H
there is an associated Lie coalgebra
L=\frac{H_{>0}}{H_{>0}H_{>0}}
of indecomposables. We thus have a Lie coalgebra
\mathbb{L}^{\symb}
along with Lie coalgebras

(7)

\mathbb{L}^{\symb}(F),\qquad\widehat{\mathbb{L}}^{\symb}_{E}(F),\qquad\widehat{\mathbb{L}}_{M},\qquad\mathbb{L}^{\symb}_{S_{\bullet}}.

Remark 1.2
.

We stress that
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
is a Hopf algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
, but
\mathbb{L}^{\symb}
is a Lie coalgebra over
\mathbb{Z}
, as are all the Lie coalgebras in (
7
).

1.3.
Variation matrices and mixed Hodge structure

The monodromy of a multiple polylogarithm can be computed using a variation matrix. For the classical polylogarithms the variation matrix was defined by Deligne and Beilinson
[
BD94
]
and for the multiple polylogarithms by Zhao
[
Zha16
]
. For example, for
\Li_{3}(x)
and
\Li_{1,1}(x,y)
the variation matrices are given by

(12)

\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\\
\Li_{1}(x)&1&&\\
\Li_{2}(x)&\log(x)&1&\\
\Li_{3}(x)&\frac{1}{2}\log(x)^{2}&\log(x)&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\\
\Li_{1}(y)&1&&\\
\Li_{1}(xy)&0&1&\\
\Li_{1,1}(x,y)&\Li_{1}(x)&\Li_{1}(y)-\Li_{1}(x^{-1})&1\end{pmatrix}.

We give a purely symbolic definition of variation matrices as matrices with entries in
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
and show that

(13)

\Delta(V^{T})=V^{T}\otimes V^{T}.

This result plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
It is known (see e.g.
[
Zha16
]
) that the variation matrix
V
for a depth
d
polylogarithm satisfies a differential equation
\mathrm{d}V=\omega V
, where
\omega
is a matrix of
1
-forms on
S_{d}(\mathbb{C})
. Also, if
V
is an
N\times N
matrix, then
V
defines a variation of mixed Hodge structure on
S_{d}(\mathbb{C})
with Hodge filtration induced by the standard filtration on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, weight filtration coming from the span of column vectors of
V
, and connection form
\nabla=\mathrm{d}-\omega
.

The form
\omega
is exact on
\widehat{S}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
with a canonical primitive
\Omega
. In Section
5
we show that
\widehat{V}=e^{-\Omega}V
defines a variation of mixed Hodge structure on
\widehat{S}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
whose connection form is given by
\mathrm{d}-\widehat{\omega}
where
\widehat{\omega}
is determined by the 1-forms (rescaled by
(n-1)
). This gives a Hodge theoretic interpretation of the 1-forms appearing in Zickert
[
Zic19
]
(which as mentioned in Example
1.4
are scaled by
(n-1)
). We note that the matrix
\widehat{V}
is much sparser than
V
.

Structure of the paper

we define
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}

2.2.
Associated Hopf algebras

One can use
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
to produce other Hopf algebras in a natural way. To see this we introduce some terminology. For
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{d+1})
a strictly increasing sequence with
i_{d+1}\leq n+1
define

(24)

\mathbf{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=(x_{i_{1}\rightarrow i_{2}},\dots,x_{i_{d}\rightarrow i_{d+1}}).

We thus think of
\mathbf{i}
as defining a
contraction
from depth
n
to depth
d
. Note that if
\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{f+1})
defines contraction from depth
d
to depth
f
the sequence

(25)

\mathbf{i}|\mathbf{j}=(i_{j_{1}},\dots,i_{j_{f+1}}),

defines a contraction from depth
n
to depth
f
, and we have

(26)

\mathbf{j}(\mathbf{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))=(\mathbf{i}|\mathbf{j})(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}).

We shall formalize this property of contractions below.

Definition 2.12
.

A
contraction system
is a collection of sets
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
,
\dots
together with contraction maps
\mathbf{i}\colon X_{n}\to X_{d}
(one for each
\mathbf{i}
as above) satisfying
\mathbf{j}\circ\mathbf{i}=\mathbf{i}|\mathbf{j}\colon X_{n}\to X_{f}
.

Note that if
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
we have

(27)

[(i_{1},i_{2})X]_{0}+[(i_{2},i_{3})X]_{0}=[(i_{1},i_{3})X]_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{\symb}.

Definition 2.13
.

For any contraction system
X
the
free contraction algebra
,
\mathbb{H}(X)
, is generated by symbols
[\alpha]_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}
with
\alpha\in X_{d}
and
[\alpha]_{0}
with
\alpha\in X_{1}
modulo the relation

(28)

[(i_{1},i_{2})\alpha]_{0}+[(i_{2},i_{3})\alpha]_{0}=[(i_{1},i_{3})\alpha]_{0}.

For each
\alpha\in X_{n}
define an
evaluation at
\alpha
by

(29)

\ev_{\alpha}([\mathbf{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})]_{\mathbf{n}})=[\mathbf{i}(\alpha)]_{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{H}(X),

with
\mathbf{n}
denoting either a vector
(n_{1},\dots,n_{d})
or
0
.

Observation 2.14
.

The coproduct of
[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}\in\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
can be expressed entirely in terms of contractions of
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.

Theorem 2.15
.

The free contraction algebra
\mathbb{H}(X)
has a natural structure as a graded Hopf algebra with coproduct
\Delta_{X}
induced by the coproduct on
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
. Formally,

(30)

\Delta_{X}([\alpha]_{0})=1\otimes[\alpha]_{0}+[\alpha]_{0}\otimes 1,\qquad\Delta_{X}([\alpha]_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}})=\ev_{\alpha}(\Delta([x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}).

Proof.

We must prove that
\Delta_{X}
is coassociative. Since
\mathbf{j}\mathbf{i}\alpha=(\mathbf{i}|\mathbf{j})\alpha
, we have

(31)

\ev_{\mathbf{i}\alpha}([\mathbf{j}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})]_{\mathbf{n}})=[\mathbf{j}\mathbf{i}\alpha]_{\mathbf{n}}=[(\mathbf{i}|\mathbf{j})\alpha]_{\mathbf{n}}=\ev_{\alpha}([(\mathbf{i}|\mathbf{j})(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})]_{\mathbf{n}})=\ev_{\alpha}([\mathbf{j}\mathbf{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})]_{\mathbf{n}}).

Since this holds for all
\mathbf{j}
we have

(32)

\ev_{\mathbf{i}\alpha}(\Delta[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]_{\mathbf{n}})=\ev_{\alpha}(\Delta[(\mathbf{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})]_{\mathbf{n}}).

Hence,

(33)

\Delta_{X}\ev_{\alpha}([\mathbf{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})]_{\mathbf{n}})=\Delta_{X}([\mathbf{i}\alpha]_{\mathbf{n}})=\ev_{\mathbf{i}\alpha}(\Delta([x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]_{\mathbf{n}}))=\ev_{\alpha}\Delta([\mathbf{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})]_{\mathbf{n}}).

Since this holds for all
\mathbf{i}
, we have
\Delta_{X}\circ\ev_{\alpha}=\ev_{\alpha}\circ\Delta
. From this we see that coassociativity of
\Delta
implies that of
\Delta_{X}
.
∎

2.4.
The antipode

For a connected Hopf algebra with product
\mu
, coproduct
\Delta
, unit
\eta
, and counit
\varepsilon
, the antipode
S
is uniquely determined by
\mu(S\otimes\id)\circ\Delta=\eta\circ\varepsilon
.

Proposition 2.30
.

The antipode satisfies
S(V)=V^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
I

\Delta(V^{T})=V^{T}\otimes V^{T}

I=\eta\varepsilon(V^{T})=\mu(S\otimes\id)\Delta(V^{T})=S(V^{T})V^{T}.

2.5.
Derivatives

Using the generating series (
1
) for
\Li_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})

one easily computes the partial derivatives
\partial_{k}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}
and thus its total derivative
\mathrm{d}\Li_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})
. The result can be conveniently expressed using the generating series

(46)

\Li(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}|t_{1},\dots,t_{d})=\sum_{n_{i}\geq 1}\Li_{n_{1},\dots,n_{d}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})t_{1}^{n_{1}-1}\cdots t_{d}^{n_{d}-1}.

Namely, we have

(47)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle\Li(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}|t_{1},\dots,t_{d})=\Li(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}|t_{1},\dots,t_{d})\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\mathrm{d}\log(x_{k})t_{k}\right)

\displaystyle-\Li(x_{2},\dots,x_{d}|t_{2},\dots,t_{d})~\mathrm{d}\log(1-x_{1})

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=2}^{d}\Li(x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}x_{k},\dots,x_{d}|t_{1},\dots,t_{k-1},t_{k+1},\dots,t_{d})~\mathrm{d}\log(1-x_{k})

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\Li(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}x_{k+1},\dots,x_{d}|t_{1},\dots,t_{k-1},t_{k+1},\dots,t_{d})\big(-\mathrm{d}\log(1-x_{k})+\mathrm{d}\log(x_{k})\big).

Motivated by this we now define a derivation on
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
.
Let
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
be the free
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
module generated by symbols
\mathrm{d}[\prod_{r=i}^{j}x_{r}]_{1}
and
\mathrm{d}[x_{i}]_{0}
and define a derivation
\mathrm{d}\colon\mathbb{H}^{\symb}\to\mathrm{d}\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
by

(48)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}[y_{1},\dots,y_{d}|

\displaystyle t_{1},\dots,t_{d}]=[y_{1},\dots,y_{d}|t_{1},\dots,t_{d}]\left(\sum_{k=1}^{k}\mathrm{d}[y_{k}]_{0}t_{k}\right)

\displaystyle+[y_{2},\dots,y_{d}|t_{2},\dots,t_{d}]\mathrm{d}[x_{1}]_{1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=2}^{d}[y_{1},\dots,y_{k-1}y_{k},\dots,y_{d}|t_{1},\dots,t_{k-1},t_{k+1},\dots,t_{d})\mathrm{d}[y_{k}]_{1}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}[]y_{1},\dots,y_{k}y_{k+1},\dots,y_{d}|t_{1},\dots,t_{k-1},t_{k+1},\dots,t_{d}]\big(\mathrm{d}[y_{k}]_{1}+\mathrm{d}[y_{k}]_{0}\big)

and imposing the Leibniz rule on products.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}_{d}
denote the weight
d
subspace and let
\Delta_{k,l}
denote the composition of
\Delta
with projection onto
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{H}^{\symb}_{l}
.

Lemma 2.31
.

The restriction of
\mathrm{d}
to
\mathbb{H}^{\symb}_{d}
equals
\phi\circ\Delta_{d-1,1}
, where
\phi
takes
x\otimes y
to
x~\mathrm{d}y
.

Proof.

It is easy to show that
\phi\circ\Delta_{d-1,1}[y_{1},\dots,y_{d}|t_{1},\dots,t_{d}]
equals the righthand side of (
48
). To see that it still holds for products, let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be symbols in weight
k
and
l
, respectively. We then have (for
n=k+l
)

(49)

\phi\circ\Delta_{n-1,1}(P_{1}P_{2})=\phi\big(\Delta_{k-1,1}(P_{1})(P_{2}\otimes 1)+(P_{1}\otimes 1)\Delta_{l-1,1}(P_{2})\big)=P_{2}~\mathrm{d}P_{1}+P_{1}~\mathrm{d}P_{2}=\mathrm{d}(P_{1}P_{2}).

The result follows.
∎

Remark 2.32
.

One can similarly define
\mathrm{d}\overline{\mathbb{H}}^{\symb}

\omega=\mathrm{d}V_{1}
, where

is the weight 1 part of

. The same holds for

and for the submatrices associated to

This follows from Lemma

∎

3.
The Hopf algebra of tensors and the symbol map

Our main reference for this section is
[
DD19
]
.

3.1.
The tensor algebra

For an abelian group
A
let
T^{*}A
denote the tensor algebra of
A
. When convenient, we regard elements as words consisting of letters in
A
. It is well known that
T^{*}A
is a graded Hopf algebra (graded by word length
|\,|
) with coproduct
\Delta
given by
deconcatenation

(50)

\Delta(w)=\sum_{w=w_{1}w_{2}}w_{1}\otimes w_{2}

and product given by the
shuffle product

\shuffle
defined recursively by

(51)

(aw_{1})\shuffle(bw_{2})=a(w_{1}\shuffle(bw_{2}))+b((aw_{1})\shuffle w_{2}),\qquad|a|=|b|=1,

where the recursion starts with
w\shuffle 1=1\shuffle w=w
.

3.2.
The symbol map

For a graded Hopf algebra
H
, let
\Delta_{1,\dots,1}\colon H\to T^{*}H_{1}
denote the maximal iteration of the coproduct. More precisely, the restriction of
\Delta_{1,\dots,1}
to
H_{n}
is defined inductively by

(52)

\Delta_{1,\dots,1}=(\id\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1})\circ\Delta_{1,n-1}=(\Delta_{1,\dots,1}\otimes\id)\circ\Delta_{n-1,1}

where the second equality follows from coassociativity of
\Delta
.

Definition 3.1
.

We call
\Delta_{1,\dots,1}\colon\mathbb{H}^{\symb}\to T^{*}\mathbb{H}^{\symb}_{1}
the
symbol map
, and call the image of
x\in\mathbb{H}^{\symb}
the
symbol of
x
.

Example 3.2
.

The symbols of
[x_{1}]_{n}
,
[x_{1},x_{2}]_{1,1}
, and
[x_{1},x_{2}]_{2,1}
are given by
(n\geq 2)

(53)

\begin{gathered}-[x_{1}]_{0}^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes[x_{1}]_{1},\qquad([x_{1}]_{0}-[x_{1}]_{1}+[x_{2}]_{1})\otimes[x_{1}x_{2}]_{1}+[x_{1}]_{1}\otimes[x_{2}]_{1},\\
[x_{1}]_{0}\otimes([x_{1}]_{0}-[x_{1}]_{1}+[x_{2}]_{1})\otimes[x_{1}x_{2}]_{1}+[x_{2}]_{1}\otimes[x_{1}x_{2}]_{0}\otimes[x_{1}x_{2}]_{1}+[x_{1}]_{0}\otimes[x_{1}]_{1}\otimes[x_{2}]_{1}\end{gathered}

The following result is probably well known, but we are not aware of a reference with a proof.

Proposition 3.3
.

\Delta_{1,\dots,1}
is a morphism of graded Hopf algebras.

Proof.

We only prove that
\Delta_{1,\dots,1}
preserves the product, since this is all we need. The proof that
\Delta_{1,\dots,1}
preserves the coproduct is simpler and left to the reader. We must show that

(54)

\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(ab)=\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a)\shuffle\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b).

Suppose
|a|=k
, and
|b|=l
. Letting

(55)

\Delta_{1,k-1}(a)=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes a_{i},\qquad\Delta_{1,l-1}(b)=\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\otimes b_{j},

we have

(56)

\Delta_{1,k+l-1}(ab)=(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes a_{i})(1\otimes b)+(1\otimes a)(\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\otimes b_{j})=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes(a_{i}b)+\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\otimes(ab_{j}).

By the inductive definition of
\Delta_{1,\dots,1}
we have

(57)

\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a)=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a_{i}),\qquad\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b)=\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b_{j}).

By induction on weight we have

(58)

\displaystyle\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(ab)

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1})\circ\Delta_{1,k+l-1}(ab)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a_{i}b)+\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(ab_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes(\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a_{i})\shuffle\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b))+\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\otimes(\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a)\shuffle\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b_{j})).

On the other hand, we have

(59)

\displaystyle\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a)\shuffle\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a_{i})\right)\shuffle\left(\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b_{j})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}(\alpha_{i}\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a_{i}))\shuffle(\beta_{j}\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b_{j}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\big(\alpha_{i}\otimes(\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a_{i})\shuffle(\beta_{j}\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b_{j}))\big)+\beta_{j}\otimes((\alpha_{i}\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a_{i}))\shuffle\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b_{j}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes(\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a_{i})\shuffle(\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b_{j})))+\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\otimes((\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a_{i}))\shuffle\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b_{j}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes(\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a_{i})\shuffle\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b))+\sum_{j}\beta_{j}\otimes(\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a)\shuffle\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b_{j})).

This shows that
\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(ab)=\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(a)\shuffle\Delta_{1,\dots,1}(b)
as desired.
∎

3.3.
Killing products

For any graded, connected, commutative Hopf algebra
H
with product
\mu
, coproduct
\Delta
, and antipode
S
, there is a natural projection map
\Pi\colon H\to H
whose kernel is
H_{>0}H_{>0}
. Namely, we have (see
[
CDG21
, Sec. 2.2]
)

(60)

\Pi=\id+Y^{-1}\mu(S\otimes Y)\Delta^{\prime},

where
\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta-\id\otimes 1-1\otimes\id
is the restricted coproduct, and
Y
multiplies a homogeneous element by its weight. In the case of
T^{*}A
, we have

(61)

\Pi(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n})=a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}(n-i)a_{i}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{1}\shuffle a_{i+1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n}.

It is not difficult to see that this formula is equivalent to the recursive formula

(62)

\Pi(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n})=\frac{n-1}{n}\big(\Pi(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n-1})\otimes a_{n}-\Pi(a_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n})\otimes a_{1}\big).

The recursion starts with
\Pi(a)=a
.

Recall the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, which states that there is an equivalence of categories between local systems (locally constant sheaves) over a complex manifold

(

We give a brief review of the basic definitions pertaining to variations of mixed Hodge-Tate structures:
The (rational)
Hodge-Tate structure

\mathbb{Q}(n)
of weight
-2n
is the unique Hodge structure with only

. A

is a rational vector space

of

and an increasing weight filtration

which are compatible, i.e. the weight piece

\mathbb{Q}(k)

over a complex manifold

\mathcal{H}
of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces with a decreasing Hodge filtration

of

and an increasing weight filtration

such that
(\mathcal{H}_{x},F_{x}^{\bullet},(W_{x})_{\bullet})
is a mixed Hodge-Tate structure and

satisfies Griffiths transversality
\nabla F^{p}\subseteq F^{p-1}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}
.

Theorem 5.4

(
[

]

6.
Goncharov’s Hopf algebra of iterated integrals

For a set
S
Goncharov
[
Gon05
]
defined a graded Hopf algebra
\mathcal{I}(S)
. It is freely generated by symbols
I(a_{0};a_{1},\dots,a_{d};a_{d+1})
in weight
d\geq 1
(
a_{i}\in S
). The coproduct is given by

(101)

\Delta I(a_{0};a_{1},\cdots;a_{d};a_{d+1})=\\
\sum_{0=i_{0}<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}<i_{k+1}=d+1}I(a_{i_{0}};a_{i_{1}},\cdots,a_{i_{k}};a_{i_{k+1}})\otimes\prod_{p=0}^{k}I(a_{i_{p}};a_{i_{p}+1},\cdots,a_{i_{p+1}-1};a_{i_{p+1}}),

where
I(a_{0};\emptyset;a_{1})=1
. An elementary proof that
\Delta
is coassociative is given below. By assigning complex numbers to the
a_{i}
we can realize
I(a_{0};a_{1},\dots,a_{d};a_{d+1})
as an iterated integral

(102)

\int_{\gamma}\frac{dt}{t-a_{1}}\cdots\frac{dt}{t-a_{d}},

where
\gamma
is a path from
a_{0}
to
a_{d+1}
(there is a canonical renormalization in the case when the integral diverges
[
Gon01
]
).

Remark 6.1
.

We note that our
\mathcal{I}(S)
is Goncharov’s
\widetilde{\mathscr{I}}_{\bullet}(S)
(see
[
Gon05
, p. 225]
).

6.1.
The variation matrix for iterated integrals

Fix a sequence
\mathbf{a}=\{a_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
of elements in
S
. We now define a matrix
V=V_{\mathbf{a}}
whose (unordered) rows and columns are parameterized by strictly increasing sequences
\mathbf{i}=(i_{0},\dots,i_{n+1})
of non-negative integers. For such
\mathbf{i}
define

(103)

I_{\mathbf{i}}=I(a_{i_{0}};a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{n}};a_{i_{n+1}}).

We write
|(i_{0},\dots,i_{n+1})|=n
, and
\mathbf{j}\leq\mathbf{i}
if
\mathbf{j}
is a subsequence of
\mathbf{i}
with
j_{0}=i_{0}
and
j_{|\mathbf{j}|+1}=i_{|\mathbf{i}|+1}
. If so, it follows that for each
p\in\{0,\dots,|\mathbf{j}|+1\}
there exists
k_{p}
with
i_{k_{p}}=j_{p}
. For
p\leq|\mathbf{j}|
we denote the
p^{th}
subsequence of
\mathbf{i}
,
(i_{k_{p}},i_{k_{p}+1},\dots,i_{k_{p+1}})
by
\mathbf{i}\cap\mathbf{j}(p)
.
With this notation we see that

(104)

\Delta I_{\mathbf{i}}=\sum_{\mathbf{j}\leq\mathbf{i}}I_{\mathbf{j}}\otimes\prod_{p=0}^{|\mathbf{j}|}I_{\mathbf{i}\cap\mathbf{j}(p)}.

Definition 6.2
.

The
variation matrix
for
\mathbf{a}
is the matrix
V
whose

(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})
entry
V_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}
is given by

(105)

V_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}=\begin{cases}\prod_{p=0}^{|\mathbf{j}|}I_{\mathbf{i}\cap\mathbf{j}(p)}&\text{if }\mathbf{j}\leq\mathbf{i}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Example 6.3
.

Let
\mathbf{i}=(0,1,3,4,5)
and
\mathbf{j}=(0,3,5)
, then
V_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}=I(a_{0};a_{1};a_{3})I(a_{3};a_{4};a_{4})
.

However, for
\mathbf{j}=(0,2,5)
we don’t have
\mathbf{j}\leq\mathbf{i}
and so
V_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}=0
.

Example 6.4
.

If
\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{i}
, every factor is of the form
I(a_{i_{p}};\emptyset;a_{i_{p+1}})=1
. So
V_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}=1
.

Example 6.5
.

If
\mathbf{j}=(i_{0},i_{|\mathbf{i}|+1})
, we have
V_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}=I_{\mathbf{i}}
. Hence, any iterated integral is an entry in the variation matrix
V_{\mathbf{a}}
for some
\mathbf{a}
.

Theorem 6.6
.

We have
\Delta V^{T}=V^{T}\otimes V^{T}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathbf{j}\leq\mathbf{i}
. We have

(106)

\Delta V_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}}=\prod_{p=0}^{|\mathbf{j}|}\Delta I_{\mathbf{i}\cap\mathbf{j}(p)}=\prod_{p=0}^{|\mathbf{j}|}\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\leq\mathbf{i}\cap\mathbf{j}(p)}I_{\mathbf{k}}\otimes\prod_{r=0}^{|\mathbf{k}|}I_{(\mathbf{i}\cap\mathbf{j}(p))\cap\mathbf{k}(r)}\Big)=\\
\sum_{\mathbf{j}\leq\mathbf{l}\leq\mathbf{i}}\Big(\prod_{p=0}^{|\mathbf{j}|}I_{\mathbf{l}\cap\mathbf{j}(p)}\otimes\prod_{q=0}^{|\mathbf{l}|}I_{\mathbf{i}\cap\mathbf{l}(q)}\Big)=\sum_{\mathbf{j}\leq\mathbf{l}\leq\mathbf{i}}V_{\mathbf{l},\mathbf{j}}\otimes V_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{l}}

This proves the result.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

Goncharov’s coproduct is coassociative. ∎

Proof.

It is enough to show that
(\Delta\otimes\id)\Delta V^{T}=(\id\otimes\Delta)\Delta V^{T}
. This follows from Theorem
6.6
, which shows that both sides equal
V^{T}\otimes V^{T}\otimes V^{T}
.
∎

Proof.

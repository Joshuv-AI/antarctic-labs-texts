Relational correspondences for L L -fuzzy rough approximations defined on De Morgan Heyting algebras
Jouni Järvinen Software Engineering, LUT School of Engineering ScienceMukkulankatu 19, 15210 Lahti, Finland jouni.jarvinen@lut.fi, Michiro Kondo Department of Mathematics, School of System Design and TechnologyTokyo Denki University, Senju Asahi-cho 5, Adachi, Tokyo, Japan mkondo@mail.dendai.ac.jp

Abstract
We consider fuzzy rough sets defined on De Morgan Heyting algebras. We present a theorem that can be used to obtain several correspondence results between fuzzy rough sets and fuzzy relations defining them. We characterize fuzzy rough approximation operators corresponding to compositions of reflexive, transitive, mediate, Euclidean and adjoint fuzzy relations defined on De Morgan Heyting algebras by using only a single axiom. Keywords: rough set, L L -fuzzy set, L L -fuzzy relation, relational correspondence, De Morgan Heyting algebra



1
Introduction

Rough sets were introduced by Z. Pawlak in
[
13
]
to deal with concepts that cannot be defined precisely in terms of our knowledge. In rough set theory, knowledge about objects
U
is given in terms of an
indistinguishability relation

E
, which is an equivalence
E
on
U
interpreted so that two elements
are
E
-related if we cannot distinguish them in terms of the knowledge
E
.
For each subset,
X
the
lower approximation

X_{E}
is the set of
elements whose
E
-equivalence class is included in
X
.
The set
X_{E}
is interpreted as the set of elements that certainly belong to
X

in view of the knowledge
E
. The
upper approximation

X^{E}
consists of elements whose
E
-class intersects with
X
.
The set
X^{E}
can be seen as the set of elements which possibly belong to

X
. This means that every vague concept can be approximated from below and
above by two sets that are definable by the knowledge
E
.

In the literature, numerous studies can be found in which equivalences are replaced by an arbitrary binary relation reflecting, for instance, similarity or preference between the elements; see
[
10
,
20
]
, for example.
Also in such a generalized setting, the lower approximation
X_{R}
consists of
elements which necessarily are in
X
and
X^{R}
is the set of elements which possibly are in
X
, in the view on knowledge
R
.

Fuzzy sets introduced by L. Zadeh
[
21
]
are generalizations of traditional
sets such that the set-memberships are expressed by a real number of the
interval
[0,1]
. Similarly, Zadeh defined fuzzy relations as generalizations
of traditional binary relations. Soon after Zadeh’s paper, J. A. Goguen

[
4
]
defined so-called
L
-fuzzy sets and
L
-fuzzy relations,
in which the
[0,1]
-interval is replaced by a complete lattice
L
having

0
as the smallest and
1
as the greatest elements. Since then, all kinds
of structures, such as Heyting algebras or residuated lattices, have been
presented as a basis for fuzzy sets
[
12
]
.

The first approach to integrate rough set theory and fuzzy set theory is
the paper by D. Dubois and H. Prade
[
3
]
, where
they introduced fuzzy rough sets. A comprehensive evaluation of the most
relevant fuzzy rough set models proposed in the literature can be found in

[
2
]
.

Correspondences are understood as conditions that connect the properties of
relations to the properties of approximation operations. For instance,
if
R
is an arbitrary binary relation on
U
, then
R
is symmetric if
and only if
(X_{R})^{R}\subseteq X\subseteq(X^{R})_{R}
holds for all subsets

X
of
U
. Correspondences for binary relations are well-studied in the literature. For instance,
reflexive, symmetric, and transitive relations are characterized in

[
5
]
. In
[
20
]
, the authors considered serial,
reflexive, symmetric, transitive and Euclidean relations.
Correspondences for serial, reflexive, mediate, transitive and alliance relations are give in
[
23
]
. However, there seems to be a problem in
the characterization of alliance relations, and we consider this issue
in Example
3.27
.

As noted above, there are several ways to generalize rough sets to
fuzzy rough sets. This means that there are various types of correspondence
results in the literature depending on the setting.
For instance, in
[
14
]
the authors
considered
L
-fuzzy rough approximations in the case
L
is a
complete residuated lattice. They characterized serial, reflexive,
symmetric, Euclidean, and transitive
L
-fuzzy relations.
In
[
18
]
, the authors presented correspondences
for reflexive, symmetric, transitive, and Euclidean
[0,1]
-fuzzy relations.
Similar study of
[0,1]
-fuzzy rough sets can be found in
[
7
]
.
Some other recent types of fuzzy rough approximations and their correspondences
are considered in
[
6
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
22
]
, for example.

In
[
11
]
, the authors consider such
L
-fuzzy relations that
L
is a Heyting algebra provided with an antitone involution
′
. This
means that
L
is a so-called De Morgan Heyting algebra.
Three new types (mediate, Euclidean, adjoint) of
L
-fuzzy rough approximation operators were characterized in that paper.
In this work, we adopt their approach. We will present a new uniform method in Theorem
3.2
which
covers the results in
[
11
]
, and several others.

L. Liu
[
8
]
initiated the study of characterizing fuzzy rough set approximations by only one axiom. In
[
11
]
, the authors provided an axiomatic approach to
L
-fuzzy rough approximation operators, where
L

is a De Morgan Heyting algebra. They also presented
single axioms to characterize
L
-fuzzy rough approximation operators corresponding to mediate, Euclidean, adjoint
L
-fuzzy relations as well as their compositions. In that work, they gave the following open problem:
“Using single axioms to characterize
L
-fuzzy rough approximation operators corresponding to compositions
of serial, reflexive, symmetric, transitive, mediate, Euclidean and adjoint
L
-fuzzy relations.” In this work, we present a general solution to this
problem, which covers also other types of relations and their compositions.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2

we recall from the literature notions and basic results used in this
work. More precisely, in Section
2.1
, we consider
the essential correspondence results related to (crisp)
binary relations and rough approximation operations. In this
work, we consider fuzzy rough sets on De Morgan Heyting algebras, and Section
2.2
covers the
basic facts of these types of algebras.

Section
3
is devoted to the correspondence results characterizing properties of
L
-fuzzy relations in terms of fuzzy rough approximation operators.
In Section
3.1
we present a result which is frequently
used in other subsections to prove correspondences. We present

3.2
),
transitive and mediate (Section
3.3
),
Euclidean and adjoint (Section
3.4
), and functional and positive alliance relations
(Section
3.5
). There are
several ways to generalize serial relations to their fuzzy counterparts, and
in Section
3.6
we consider three such generalizations.

Finally, in Section
4
we solve the problem
given in
[
11
]
and some concluding remarks end the work.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we first recall correspondences between (crisp) binary
relations and rough approximation operations.
The second part of this section considers
De Morgan Heyting algebras and fuzzy rough approximation operations defined
on them.

2.1
Binary relations and rough approximation operators

A
binary relation
on
U
is a set of ordered pairs
(x,y)
,
where
x
and
y
are elements of
U
. If
x
and
y
are
R
-related,
this is denoted by
x\,R\,y
or
(x,y)\in R
.

Let us introduce some general properties of binary relations. A binary relation
R
on
U
is

(i)

serial
, if for all
x\in U
, there is
y\in U
such that
x\,R\,y
,

(ii)

reflexive
, if
x\,R\,x
for all
x\in U
,

(iii)

symmetric
, if
x\,R\,y
, then
y\,R\,x
for all
x,y\in U
,

(iv)

transitive
, if
x\,R\,y
and
y\,R\,z
, then
x\,R\,z
for all
x,y,z\in U
,

(v)

mediate
, if
x\,R\,y
for some
x,y\in U
, there is
z\in U
such that
x\,R\,z
and
z\,R\,y
,

(vi)

Euclidean
, if
x\,R\,y
and
x\,R\,z
, then
y\,R\,z
for all
x,y,z\in U
.

Next, we recall (see e.g.
[
5
,
20
]
) rough approximation operations
defined by arbitrary relations. For any subset
X\subseteq U
, the

lower approximation
of
X
is defined as

X_{R}=\{x\in U\mid x\,R\,y\mbox{ implies }y\in X\}.

The
upper approximation
of
X
is

X^{R}=\{x\in U\mid\emph{thereexistsy\in Xsuchthatx\,R\,y}\}.

The following correspondence results can be found in the literature; see
[
5
,
20
,
23
]
, for instance:

(i)

R
is serial
\iff U^{R}=U\iff\emptyset_{R}=\emptyset\iff(\forall X\subseteq U)\,X_{R}\subseteq X^{R}
,

(ii)

R
is reflexive
\iff(\forall X\subseteq U)\,X_{R}\subseteq X\iff(\forall X\subseteq U)\,X\subseteq X^{R}
,

(iii)

R
is symmetric
\iff(\forall X\subseteq U)\,(X_{R})^{R}\subseteq X\iff(\forall X\subseteq U)\,X\subseteq(X^{R})_{R}
,

(iv)

R
is transitive
\iff(\forall X\subseteq U)\,(X^{R})^{R}\subseteq X^{R}\iff(\forall X\subseteq U)\,X_{R}\subseteq(X_{R})_{R}
.

(v)

R
is mediate
\iff(\forall X\subseteq U)\,X^{R}\subseteq(X^{R})^{R}\iff(\forall X\subseteq U)\,(X_{R})_{R}\subseteq X_{R}
.

(vi)

R
is Euclidean
\iff(\forall X\subseteq U)\,X^{R}\subseteq(X^{R})_{R}\iff(\forall X\subseteq U)\,(X_{R})^{R}\subseteq X_{R}
.

In this work, we consider similar correspondences in the setting of

L
-fuzzy rough sets, when a complete De Morgan Heyting algebra
is defined on
L
.

2.2
Approximation operators in De Morgan Heyting algebras

We begin by recalling some definitions from
[
1
]
.
A
Heyting algebra
is an algebra

(H,\vee,\wedge,\Rightarrow,0,1)

of type
(2,2,2,0,0)
for which

(H,\vee,\wedge,0,1)
is a bounded distributive lattice and
\Rightarrow
is the operation of
relative pseudocomplementation
,
that is, for
a,b,c\in H
,
a\wedge c\leq b
iff
c\leq a\Rightarrow b
.

A
De Morgan Heyting algebra
is an algebra

\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,\Rightarrow,^{\prime},0,1)

of type
(2,2,2,1,0,0)
such that

(L,\vee,\wedge,\Rightarrow,0,1)
is a Heyting algebra and
′
is an antitone involution, that is,

(x\wedge y)^{\prime}=x^{\prime}\vee y^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime\prime}=x
for all
x\in L
.

Example 2.1
.

Let us consider the 4-element lattice

L:=0<a,b<1
in which the elements
a
and
b
are incomparable. Then

(L,\vee,\wedge,\Rightarrow,0,1)
is a well-known Heyting algebra.

There are two ways to define the operation
′
in
L
.
We can define

0^{\prime}=1,\ a^{\prime}=b,\ b^{\prime}=a,\ 1^{\prime}=0
.

It is also possible to define

0^{\prime}=1,\ a^{\prime}=a,\ b^{\prime}=b,\ 1^{\prime}=0
.

In both cases, we have a De Morgan Heyting algebra defined on
L
.

A complete lattice
L
satisfies the
join-infinite distributive law
if for any
S\subseteq L
and
x\in L
,

x\wedge\big(\bigvee S\big)=\bigvee\{x\wedge y\mid y\in S\}.

(JID)

The dual condition is the
meet-infinite distributive law
, (MID). It is well known that a complete lattice defines a Heyting algebra
if and only if it satisfies (JID). Each De Morgan Heyting algebra
\mathbf{L}
defined on a complete lattice
L
satisfies both (JID) and (MID),
because
′
is an order-isomorphism between
(L,\leq)
and its dual
(L,\geq)
. In this work, De Morgan Heyting algebras defined on a complete lattice
are called
complete De Morgan Heyting algebras
.

Let
\mathbf{L}
be a complete De Morgan Heyting algebra and
U
a universe. An
L
-fuzzy set
on
U
is a mapping
A\colon U\to L
.
We often drop the word ‘fuzzy’ and speak about
L
-sets. The family of all
L
-sets on
U
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
.

The set
\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
may be ordered
pointwise
by setting for
A,B\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
,
A\leq B
if and only if
A(x)\leq B(x)
for all
x\in U
.
If
\mathbf{L}
is a complete De Morgan Heyting algebra, then
\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
forms a complete De Morgan Heyting algebra such that for all

\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
and
x\in U
,

\big({\bigvee}_{i}A_{i}\big)(x)={\bigvee}_{i}A_{i}(x)\quad\text{ and }\quad\big({\bigwedge}_{i}A_{i}\big)(x)={\bigwedge}_{i}A_{i}(x)

The operations
\Rightarrow
and
′
are defined for
A,B\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
and
x\in U
by

(A\Rightarrow B)(x)=A(x)\Rightarrow B(x)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad A^{\prime}(x)=A(x)^{\prime}.

The map
\mathbf{0}\colon x\mapsto 0
is the smallest and
\mathbf{1}\colon x\mapsto 1
is the greatest element of
\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
, respectively.

An
L
-fuzzy relation
R
on
U
is a mapping
U\times U\to L
. We often use the term “
L
-relation” instead of “
L
-fuzzy relation”.
The following definition of
L
-approximations can be found in
[
11
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{L}
be a complete De Morgan Heyting algebra,
R
an
L
-relation on
U
and
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
. The

upper
L
-fuzzy approximation
and
lower
L
-fuzzy approximation of
A
are defined by

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}(A)(x)={\bigvee}_{y}\big(R(x,y)\wedge A(y)\big)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathsf{L}(A)(x)={\bigwedge}_{y}\big(R(x,y)^{\prime}\vee A(y)\big),

respectively.

If there is no danger of confusion, we may denote
\mathsf{U}(A)
and
\mathsf{L}(A)
simply by
\mathsf{U}A
and
\mathsf{L}A
. In addition,

L
-fuzzy approximations are called simply
L
-approximations.

In
[
11
]
, the following properties of
L
-approximations generalizing well-known properties of crisp rough approximations are proved.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
U
be a set,
L
a complete De Morgan Heyting algebra and
R
an
L
-relation on
U
.
For
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
,
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
and
x\in U
, the following assertions hold:

(1)

\mathsf{L}\mathbf{0}=\mathbf{0}
and
\mathsf{U}\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}
;

(2)

(\mathsf{U}A)^{\prime}=\mathsf{L}(A^{\prime})
and
(\mathsf{L}A)^{\prime}=\mathsf{U}(A^{\prime})
;

(3)

\mathsf{U}({\bigvee}_{i}A_{i})={\bigvee}_{i}\mathsf{U}(A_{i})
and
\mathsf{L}({\bigwedge}_{i}A_{i})={\bigwedge}_{i}\mathsf{L}(A_{i})
;

(4)

\mathsf{U}({\bigwedge}_{i}A_{i})\leq{\bigwedge}_{i}\mathsf{U}(A_{i})
and
\mathsf{L}({\bigvee}_{i}A_{i})\geq{\bigvee}_{i}\mathsf{L}(A_{i})
.

For
a\in L
, we define a ‘constant’
L
-set
\overline{a}
by

\overline{a}(x)=a\text{ for all $x\in U$}.

This means that
\overline{0}=\mathbf{0}
and
\overline{1}=\mathbf{1}
. For any
x\in U
, we define a map
I_{x}
by

I_{x}(y)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $x=y$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The idea is that the map
I_{x}
corresponds to the singleton
\{x\}
.
For any
x,y\in U
,
\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)=\bigvee_{z}(R(x,z)\wedge I_{y}(z))
. Because
I_{y}(z)=1
iff
z=y
, we obtain the following
equality, which will be used frequently in this work:

(\forall x,y\in U)\,\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)=R(x,y).

(2.1)

It is noted in
[
11
]
that each
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
can be written in
two ways:

A={\bigvee}_{x}\big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\big)={\bigwedge}_{x}\big(\overline{A(x)}\vee(I_{x})^{\prime}\big).

(2.2)

Note that
(I_{x})^{\prime}
corresponds the set-theoretical complement
U\setminus\{x\}
of the singleton
\{x\}
, and
the latter equality is clear by Lemma 3.17 of
[
11
]
.
The following facts were also proved in
[
11
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
L
be a complete De Morgan Heyting algebra and
R
an
L
-relation on
U
. For all
a\in L
and
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
,

(1)

\mathsf{U}(\overline{a})\leq\overline{a}\leq\mathsf{L}(\overline{a})
;

(2)

\mathsf{U}(\overline{a}\wedge A)=\overline{a}\wedge\mathsf{U}(A)
and
\mathsf{L}(\overline{a}\vee A)=\overline{a}\vee\mathsf{L}(A)
;

(3)

\mathsf{U}(\overline{a}\vee A)\leq\overline{a}\vee\mathsf{U}(A)
and
\mathsf{L}(\overline{a}\wedge A)\geq\overline{a}\wedge\mathsf{L}(A)
.

3
Correspondence results

In this section, we assume that
\mathbf{L}
is a complete De Morgan Heyting algebra,
U
is a universe, and
R
is an
L
-relation on
U
.

3.1
A general result

In this subsection, we present a general result which can be used to
obtain several correspondence results. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a finite
combination of rough approximation operators
\mathsf{L}
and
\mathsf{U}
.
Because the operators
\mathsf{L}
and
\mathsf{U}
are order-preserving, the operator
\mathcal{S}
is order-preserving.
From this it follows that

{\bigvee}_{i}\mathcal{S}(A_{i})\leq\mathcal{S}\big({\bigvee}_{i}A_{i}\big)\ \text{ and }\ {\bigwedge}_{i}\mathcal{S}(A_{i})\geq\mathcal{S}\big({\bigwedge}_{i}A_{i}\big).

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
a\in L
,

\bar{a}\wedge\mathcal{S}(I_{x})\leq\mathcal{S}(\bar{a}\wedge I_{x}).

Proof.

We prove the claim by induction.
If
n=1
, then the two cases
\bar{a}\wedge\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\leq\mathsf{U}(\bar{a}\wedge I_{x})
and
\bar{a}\wedge\mathsf{L}(I_{x})\leq\mathsf{L}(\bar{a}\wedge I_{x})

are clear by Lemma
2.4
(2) by setting
A=I_{x}
.

Suppose that the claim holds for all combinations consisting of
n

\mathsf{L}
and
\mathsf{U}
operators. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a combination
n+1

operators. Then
\mathcal{S}=\mathsf{U}\circ\mathcal{S}_{1}
or
\mathcal{S}=\mathsf{L}\circ\mathcal{S}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
are combinations
of
\mathsf{U}
and
\mathsf{L}
of length
n
.

If
\mathcal{S}=\mathsf{U}\circ\mathcal{S}_{1}
, then

\overline{a}\wedge\mathcal{S}(I_{x})=\overline{a}\wedge\mathsf{U}(\mathcal{S}_{1}(I_{x}))=\mathsf{U}(\overline{a}\wedge\mathcal{S}_{1}(I_{x}))\leq\mathsf{U}(\mathcal{S}_{1}(\bar{a}\wedge I_{x}))=\mathcal{S}(\bar{a}\wedge I_{x}).

If
\mathcal{S}=\mathsf{L}\circ\mathcal{S}_{2}
, then

\overline{a}\wedge\mathcal{S}(I_{x})=\overline{a}\wedge\mathsf{L}(\mathcal{S}_{2}(I_{x}))\leq\mathsf{L}(\overline{a}\wedge\mathcal{S}_{2}(I_{x}))\leq\mathsf{L}(\mathcal{S}_{2}(\bar{a}\wedge I_{x}))=\mathcal{S}(\bar{a}\wedge I_{x}).\qed

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a finite combination of rough approximation operators
\mathsf{L}
and
\mathsf{U}
.
If
\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\leq\mathcal{S}(I_{x})
for all
x\in U
, then
\mathsf{U}(A)\leq\mathcal{S}(A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(A)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\leq\mathcal{S}(I_{x})
for all
x\in U
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}(A)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{U}\big({\bigvee}_{x}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x})\big)

(by (
2.2
))

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{x}\mathsf{U}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x})

(by Prop.
2.3
(3))

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{x}\big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\big)

(by Lemma
2.4
(2))

\displaystyle\leq{\bigvee}_{x}\big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge\mathcal{S}(I_{x})\big)

(by assumption)

\displaystyle\leq{\bigvee}_{x}\mathcal{S}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x})

(by Lemma
3.1
)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{S}\big({\bigvee}_{x}\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\big)

(
\mathcal{S}
preserves
\leq
)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{S}(A)

(by (
2.2
))

∎

3.2
Reflexive and symmetric relations

Let us start with the following definition.

Definition 3.3
.

An
L
-relation
R
is
reflexive
, if

(\forall x\in U)\,R(x,x)=1
.

The following lemma gives a characterization of reflexivity.

Lemma 3.4
.

An
L
-relation
R
is reflexive if and only if for all
x\in U
,

I_{x}\leq\mathsf{U}({I_{x}})
.

Proof.

If
R
is reflexive,
\mathsf{U}({I_{x}})(x)=R(x,x)=1=I_{x}(x)
for all
x\in U
. If
R
is not reflexive, then there is
x\in U
such that
R(x,x)\neq 1
.
Now,
I_{x}(x)=1
and
\mathsf{U}(I_{x})(x)=R(x,x)\neq 1
, giving
I_{x}\nleq\mathsf{U}({I_{x}})
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mathbf{L}
complete De Morgan Heyting algebra and
R
an
L
-relation on
U
.
Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

R
is reflexive;

(2)

(\forall A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U))\,A\leq\mathsf{U}A
;

(3)

(\forall A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U))\,\mathsf{L}A\leq A
.

Proof.

Suppose that
R
is reflexive. Then, by Lemma
3.4
,
I_{x}\leq\mathsf{U}({I_{x}})
for all
x\in U
.
We have that for
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
,

\displaystyle\textstyle A

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{x}\Big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\Big)\leq{\bigvee}_{x}\Big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge\mathsf{U}({I_{x}})\Big)={\bigvee}_{x}\mathsf{U}\Big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{U}\Big({\bigvee}_{x}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge{I_{x}})\Big)=\mathsf{U}A.

Thus, (1) implies (2). Suppose that (2) holds. Then for all
x\in U
,

I_{x}\leq\mathsf{U}({I_{x}})
, which by Lemma
3.4
yields that
R
is reflexive. Thus, also (2) implies (1).

We prove that (2) and (3) are equivalent. Assume (2) holds. Then for any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
,
A^{\prime}\leq\mathsf{U}(A^{\prime})=(\mathsf{L}A)^{\prime}
, which implies
\mathsf{L}A\leq A
.
Similarly, if (3) holds, then
\mathsf{L}(A^{\prime})\leq A^{\prime}
implies
A\leq(\mathsf{L}(A^{\prime}))^{\prime}=\mathsf{U}(A)
.
∎

A binary relation
\rho
is symmetric whenever, for all
x,y\in U
,

x\,\rho\,y
implies
y\,\rho\,x
. The symmetry condition can be expressed in the form

(x\,\rho^{c}\,y)\vee(y\rho\,x)
, where
\rho^{c}
denotes the complement of the relation
\rho
, that is,

\rho^{c}=(U\times U)\setminus\rho
. Based on this fact, we
present the following definition.

Definition 3.6
.

An
L
-relation
R

symmetric
if

(\forall x,y\in U)\,R(x,y)^{\prime}\vee R(y,x)=1
.

We can now express symmetry in terms of

and
L
-approximations.

Lemma 3.7
.

An
L
-relation

is symmetric if and only if for all
x\in U
,

I_{x}\leq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})
.

Proof.

For all
x\in U
,

\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})(x)={\bigwedge}_{y}(R(x,y)^{\prime}\vee\mathsf{U}(I_{x})(y))={\bigwedge}_{y}(R(x,y)^{\prime}\vee R(y,x)).

If
R
is symmetric, then
\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})(x)=1
, which means that

\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})(x)\geq I_{x}(x)
. On the other hand,
if
\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\geq I_{x}
, then
\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})(x)=1
and

\bigwedge_{y}(R(x,y)^{\prime}\vee R(y,x))=1
.
This gives that for each
y
,
R(x,y)^{\prime}\vee R(y,x)=1
.
∎

We can now write the following characterization of symmetric
L
-relations.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathbf{L}
complete De Morgan Heyting algebra and
R
an
L
-relation on
U
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

R
is symmetric;

(2)

(\forall A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U))\,A\leq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}A
;

(3)

(\forall A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U))\,\mathsf{U}\mathsf{L}A\leq A
.

Proof.

Suppose that
R
is symmetric. Then, by Lemma
3.7
,

I_{x}\leq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})(x)
for each

. We have that

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{x}\big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\big)\leq{\bigvee}_{x}\big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\big)\leq{\bigvee}_{x}\mathsf{L}\big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge\mathsf{U}({I_{x}})\big)

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{x}\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}\big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge{I_{x}}\big)\leq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}\Big({\bigvee}_{x}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge{I_{x}})\Big)=\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}A.

This means that (1) implies (2). If we set

in (2), we get that

Hence,

∎

For a fuzzy relation

3.3
Transitive and mediate relations

Definition 3.10
.

An
L
-relation is

, if

(\forall x,y,z\in U)\,R(x,z)\wedge R(z,y)\leq R(x,y)
.

Obviously, transitivity is equivalent to that
(\forall x,y\in U)\,\bigvee_{z}(R(x,z)\wedge R(z,y))\leq R(x,y)
.

Lemma 3.11
.

An
L
-relation

is transitive if and only if for all
x\in U
,
\mathsf{U}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\leq\mathsf{U}(I_{x})
.

Proof.

For all

,

\mathsf{U}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})(y)={\bigvee}_{z}\big(R(y,z)\wedge\mathsf{U}(I_{x})(z)\big)={\bigvee}_{z}(R(y,z)\wedge R(z,x)),

which is below
R(y,x)=\mathsf{U}(I_{x})(y)
by the transitivity of

.

On the other hand, for all
x,y\in U
,

\mathsf{U}\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)={\bigvee}_{z}(R(x,z)\wedge R(z,y))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)=R(x,y).

If
\mathsf{U}\mathsf{U}(I_{y})\leq\mathsf{U}(I_{y})
, then the transitivity condition holds for
x
and
y
, which completes the proof.
∎

The next proposition characterizes transitive

-relations in terms of approximation operations.

.

(2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}\mathsf{U}A

\displaystyle=\mathsf{U}\mathsf{U}\Big(\Big({\bigvee}_{x}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x})\Big)\Big)=\mathsf{U}\Big({\bigvee}_{x}\mathsf{U}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x})\Big)=\mathsf{U}\Big({\bigvee}_{x}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big({\bigvee}_{x}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge\mathsf{U}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\Big)\leq{\bigvee}_{x}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge\mathsf{U}(I_{x}))=\mathsf{U}\Big({\bigvee}_{x}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x})\Big)

3.4
Euclidean and adjoint relations

A binary relation
\rho
is Euclidean if
x\,\rho\,y
and
x\,\rho\,z
imply
y\,\rho\,z
. Obviously,

x\,\rho\,y
and
x\,\rho\,z
imply also
z\,\rho\,y
.
As noted in
[
11
]
, this is equivalent to
that
x\,\rho\,z
and
z\,\rho^{c}\,y
imply
x\,\rho^{c}\,y
.

Definition 3.16
.

An
L
-relation
R
on
U
is called

if

(\forall x,y,z\in U)\,R(x,y)^{\prime}\geq R(x,z)\wedge R(z,y)^{\prime}.

Obviously, being Euclidean is equivalent to the condition

(\forall x,y\in L)\,R(x,y)^{\prime}\geq{\bigvee}_{z}(R(x,z)\wedge R(z,y)^{\prime}).

(euc)

Lemma 3.17
.

An
L
-relation
R
is Euclidean if and only if for all
x\in U
,
\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\leq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in U
. As we have noted,
\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)=R(x,y)
and

\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)={\bigwedge}_{z}(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(z))={\bigwedge}_{z}(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee R(z,y)).

If

is Euclidean, then
R(x,y)^{\prime}\geq\bigvee_{z}(R(x,z)\wedge R(z,y)^{\prime})

\displaystyle R(x,y)

\displaystyle=R(x,y)^{\prime\prime}\leq\big({\bigvee}_{z}(R(x,z)\wedge R(z,y)^{\prime})\big)^{\prime}={\bigwedge}_{z}(R(x,z)\wedge R(z,y))^{\prime}

\displaystyle={\bigwedge}_{z}(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee R(z,y)^{\prime\prime})={\bigwedge}_{z}(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee R(z,y)),

This means
\mathsf{U}(I_{y})\leq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{y})
.

On the other hand, if
\mathsf{U}(I_{y})\leq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{y})
, then
for all
x\in U
,
\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)\leq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)
and thus,

R(x,y)\leq{\bigwedge}_{z}(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee R(z,y)).

Applying De Morgan operation
′
for the both sides of the relation, we
obtain that (
euc
) holds.
∎

-relations in terms of approximations.

.

(\forall A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U))\,\mathsf{U}\mathsf{L}A\leq\mathsf{L}A

The equivalence of (2) and (3) is obvious.

Lemma 3.20

3.5

A binary relation
\rho
is functional when each
x\in U
is
\rho
-related to at most one element.
This means that
x\,\rho\,y
implies that for all

,
x\,\rho^{c}\,y
.
Based on this, we present the following definition.

Definition 3.22
.

Thus, an
L
-relation

on

is

if

(\forall x,y)\,R(x,y)\leq\bigwedge_{z\neq y}R(x,z)^{\prime}.

The condition means that

holds for all

and

.

An

-relation

is functional if and only if for all

\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\leq\mathsf{L}(I_{x})
.

Proof.

For all

,
\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)=R(x,y)

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(I_{y})(x)

\displaystyle={\bigwedge}_{z}(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee(I_{y})(z))=\Big({\bigwedge}_{z\neq y}(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee 0)\Big)\wedge(R(x,y)^{\prime}\vee 1)

\displaystyle=\Big({\bigwedge}_{z\neq y}R(x,z)^{\prime}\Big)\wedge 1={\bigwedge}_{z\neq y}R(x,z)^{\prime}.

Because for all

,
\mathsf{U}(I_{y})\leq\mathsf{L}(I_{y})
is equivalent to that
\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)\leq\mathsf{L}(I_{y})(x)

.

\mathbf{L}

and
(\{3,4\}^{\rho})_{\rho}=\{1,2,4\}_{\rho}=\{1,3,4\}
. This means that

(\{3,4\}^{\rho})_{\rho}\nsubseteq\{3,4\}^{\rho}.

This obviously implies that there is a positive alliance
\rho
such that

(\{3,4\}^{\rho})^{c}\nsubseteq((\{3,4\}^{\rho})_{\rho})^{c}=((\{3,4\}^{\rho})^{c})^{\rho},

contradicting Theorem 2(5) in
[
9
]
.

For
L
-relations, we can present the following generalized definition.

Definition 3.28
.

An
L
-relation
R
is said to be a
positive alliance
if

R(x,y)^{\prime}\leq{\bigvee}_{z}(R(x,z)\wedge R(z,y)^{\prime}).

We can now write the following lemma characterizing positive alliance relations in terms of the approximations of the identity functions.
But as in the case of (crisp) binary relations, we obviously
cannot present a full correspondence result.

Lemma 3.29
.

An
L
-relation
R
is a positive alliance if and only if for all
x\in U
,
\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\geq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in I
. We have

\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)={\bigwedge}_{z}(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee\mathsf{U}(I_{y}(z)))={\bigwedge}_{z}\big(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee R(z,y)\big).

Thus,

(\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x))^{\prime}=\Big({\bigwedge}_{z}(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee R(z,y))\Big)^{\prime}={\bigvee}_{z}\big(R(x,z)\wedge R(z,y)^{\prime}\big).

Now,
\mathsf{U}(I_{x})\geq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{x})
for all
x\in U
if and only if
\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)\geq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)
for all
x,y\in U
if and only if

(\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x))^{\prime}\leq(\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}((I_{y})(x))^{\prime}
for all
x,y\in U
, and the equivalence follows from this.
∎

3.6
Serial relations

A binary relation
\rho
on
U
is serial if for all
x\in U
,
there exists
y\in U
such that
x\,R\,y
. It is known that
being serial is equivalent to the fact that
X_{\rho}\subseteq X^{\rho}

for all
X\subseteq U
.

Our aim of this section is to find a definition for serial
L
-relations
on
U
such that a relation is serial if and only if

(\forall A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U))\,\mathsf{L}A\leq\mathsf{U}A.

(3.1)

In this subsection, we consider three definitions.
Let us begin with the following one.

Definition 3.30
.

An
L
-relation is
serial
if

(\forall x\in U)(\exists y\in U)\,R(x,y)=1
.

Lemma 3.31
.

If an
L
-relation is serial in the sense of Definition
3.30
,
then (
3.1
) holds.

Proof.

Let
x\in U
. There exists
z\in U
such that
R(x,z)=1
. Now

\mathsf{L}(A)(x)={\bigwedge}_{y}\big(R(x,y)^{\prime}\vee A(y)\big)\leq R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee A(z)=1^{\prime}\vee A(z)=0\vee A(z)=A(z).

On the other hand,

\mathsf{U}(A)(x)={\bigvee}_{y}\big(R(x,y)\wedge A(y)\big)\geq R(x,z)\wedge A(z)=1\wedge A(z)=A(z).

We have that
\mathsf{L}(A)(x)\leq A(z)\leq\mathsf{U}(A)(x)
and the claim is proved.
∎

The converse implication does not hold, as shown in our following example.

Example 3.32
.

Let us consider the 4-element lattice
L:=0<a,b<1
,
where
a
and
b
are incomparable. If we define
0^{\prime}=1
,
a^{\prime}=b
,
b^{\prime}=a
, and
1^{\prime}=0
, then
\mathbf{L}
forms a complete
De Morgan Heyting algebra.

Note that
L
is now a Boolean algebra. This means that for all elements

x
and
y
of
L
, the join
x^{\prime}\vee y
coincides with

x\Rightarrow y
, where
\Rightarrow
is the Heyting implication of
L
. Hence, for any
L
-relation
R
,

\mathsf{L}(A)={\bigwedge}_{y}(R(x,y)\Rightarrow A(y))
. Therefore,
we can apply Proposition 3 of
[
14
]
.

Let
U=\{x,y\}
and define
R(x,x)=R(y,y)=a
and

R(x,y)=R(y,x)=b
. Thus,
R(x,x)\vee R(x,y)=a\vee b=1

and
R(y,y)\vee R(y,x)=1
. Now
[
14
, Proposition 3]

implies that
\mathsf{L}A\leq\mathsf{U}A
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{A}
.
But now
R
is not serial in the sense of Definition
3.30
.

We have now showed that seriality of Definition
3.30
is not equivalent to condition (
3.1
). Let us try a different
approach, expressed in the following definition.

Definition 3.33
.

An
L
-relation is
serial
if

(\forall x,y\in U)\,R(x,y)^{\prime}\leq{\bigvee}_{z\neq y}R(x,z).

Obviously, also this definition can be seen as a fuzzy generalization
of serial binary relations. Indeed, let
\rho
be a serial binary relation
on
U
. If
x\,\rho\,y
does not hold some
y
, then there exists

z\neq y
such that
x\,R\,z
holds.

Proposition 3.34
.

An
L
-fuzzy relation is serial in the sense of
Definition
3.33
if and only if

(\forall x\in U)\,\mathsf{L}(I_{x})\leq\mathsf{U}(I_{x}).

(3.2)

Proof.

For all
x,y\in U
,

\mathsf{L}(I_{y})(x)={\bigwedge}_{z}(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee I_{y}(z)).

If
z\neq y
,
R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee I_{y}(z)=R(x,y)^{\prime}
and
if
z=y
, then
R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee I_{y}(z)=1
. Thus,

\mathsf{L}(I_{y})(x)=\big({\bigwedge}_{z\neq y}R(x,z)^{\prime}\big)\wedge 1=\big({\bigwedge}_{z\neq y}R(x,z)^{\prime}\big)=\big({\bigvee}_{z\neq y}R(x,z)\big)^{\prime}.

On the other hand, by (
2.1
),
\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)=R(x,y)

for all
x,y\in U
. Therefore, (
3.2
)
is equivalent to

(\forall x,y\in U)\,\Big({\bigvee}_{z\neq y}R(x,z)\Big)^{\prime}\leq R(x,y).

By applying the operation
′
to both sides of the inequality, we
obtain the condition of Definition
3.33
.
∎

Unfortunately, (
3.2
) is not equivalent to
(
3.1
), as can be seen in the following example.

Example 3.35
.

Let
L:=0<a,b<1
, where
a
and
b
are incomparable.
Define
0^{\prime}=1
,
a^{\prime}=a
,
b^{\prime}=b
, and
1^{\prime}=0
. We set
U=\{x,y\}

and define the
L
-relation
R
on
U
by
R(x,x)=R(x,y)=a
and
R(y,x)=R(y,y)=b
.

Let us consider the
L
-set
\mathbf{0}
defined by
x\mapsto 0
. We have that

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(\mathbf{0})(x)={\bigwedge}_{w}(R(x,w)^{\prime}\vee\mathbf{0}(w))=(R(x,x)^{\prime}\vee\mathbf{0}(x))\wedge(R(x,y)^{\prime}\vee\mathbf{0}(y))=

\displaystyle R(x,x)^{\prime}\wedge R(x,y)^{\prime}=a^{\prime}\vee a^{\prime}=a\vee a=a

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}(\mathbf{0})(x)={\bigvee}_{w}(R(x,w)\wedge\mathbf{0}(w))=0.

This means that
\mathsf{L}(\mathbf{0})\nleq\mathsf{U}(\mathbf{0})
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}(I_{x})(x)

\displaystyle=R(x,x)=a,

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}(I_{x})(y)

\displaystyle=R(y,x)=b,

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)

\displaystyle=R(x,y)=a,

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(y)

\displaystyle=R(y,y)=b

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(I_{x})(x)

\displaystyle={\bigwedge}_{z}(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee I_{x}(z))=(R(x,x)^{\prime}\vee I_{x}(x))\wedge(R(x,y)^{\prime}\vee I_{x}(y))

\displaystyle=1\wedge R(x,y)^{\prime}=R(x,y)^{\prime}=a^{\prime}=a,

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(I_{x})(y)

\displaystyle={\bigwedge}_{z}(R(y,z)^{\prime}\vee I_{x}(z))=(R(y,x)^{\prime}\vee I_{x}(x))\wedge(R(y,y)^{\prime}\vee I_{x}(y))

\displaystyle=1\wedge R(y,y)^{\prime}=R(y,y)^{\prime}=b^{\prime}=b,

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(I_{y})(x)

\displaystyle={\bigwedge}_{z}(R(x,z)^{\prime}\vee I_{y}(z))=(R(x,x)^{\prime}\vee I_{y}(x))\wedge(R(x,y)^{\prime}\vee I_{y}(y))

\displaystyle=R(x,x)^{\prime}\wedge 1=R(x,x)^{\prime}=a^{\prime}=a,

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(I_{y})(y)

\displaystyle={\bigwedge}_{z}(R(y,z)^{\prime}\vee I_{y}(z))=(R(y,x)^{\prime}\vee I_{y}(x))\wedge(R(y,y)^{\prime}\vee I_{y}(y))

\displaystyle=R(y,x)^{\prime}\wedge 1=R(y,x)^{\prime}=b^{\prime}=b.

We have shown that
\mathsf{L}(I_{x})\leq\mathsf{U}(I_{x})
for all
x\in U
. This means
that (
3.2
) is not equivalent to (
3.1
).

In case of binary relations, it is also true that a relation

\rho
is serial if and only if
U^{\rho}=U
. We end this section
by showing that we can have a simple correspondence generalizing this.

Definition 3.36
.

An
L
-relation
R
is
serial
if

(\forall x\in U)\,\bigvee_{y}R(x,y)=1
.

Now we can write to following characterization.

Lemma 3.37
.

An
L
-relation
R
is serial if and only if
\mathsf{U}(\mathbf{1})=\mathbf{1}
.

Proof.

Because

\mathsf{U}(\mathbf{1})(x)={\bigvee}_{y}(R(x,y)\wedge\mathbf{1}(y))={\bigvee}_{y}R(x,y),

the claim is obvious.
∎

It is clear that the seriality of Definition
3.30
implies
the seriality of Definition
3.36
.
The converse is not true. For instance, the
L
-relation
R
of
Example
3.32
is serial in the sense of
Definition
3.36
, but

R(x,y)\neq 1
for all
x,y\in U
.

We also proved in Lemma
3.31
that
the seriality of Definition
3.30
implies
(
3.1
). Our final example of this subsection
shows that seriality of Definition
3.36
does
not imply (
3.1
).

Example 3.38
.

Let
L
be the 4-element lattice
0<a,b<1
, where
a
and
b

are incomparable. If we define
0^{\prime}=1
,
a^{\prime}=a
,
b^{\prime}=b
, and
1^{\prime}=0
,
then we have a complete De Morgan Heyting algebra.
Let
U=\{x,y\}
and define

R(x,x)=R(y,y)=a
and
R(x,y)=R(y,x)=b
.
It is now clear that
R
is serial in the sense of
Definition
3.36
, because
R(x,x)\vee R(x,y)=a\vee b=1
and
R(y,x)\vee R(y,y)=b\vee a=1
.

Let
U=\{a,b\}
and define an
L
-set
A\colon U\to L
by
A(x)=b
and
A(y)=a
. Now

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(A)(x)=(R(x,x)^{\prime}\vee A(x))\wedge(R(x,y)^{\prime}\vee A(y))=(a^{\prime}\vee b)\wedge(b^{\prime}\vee a)

\displaystyle=(a\vee b)\wedge(b\vee a)=1\wedge 1=1.

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}(A)(x)=(R(x,x)\wedge A(x))\vee(R(x,y)\wedge A(y))=(a\wedge b)\vee(b\wedge a)=0\vee 0=0.

Therefore,
\mathsf{L}(A)\nleq\mathsf{U}(A)
.

4
From
L
-approximations to
L
-relations

In the previous section, we defined the
L
-approximations
\mathsf{L}A
and
\mathsf{U}A
for any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
in terms of an
L
-relation
R
on
U
.
In this section, we consider a converse problem, that is, whether we can
define an
L
-relation of certain type
for a dual pair of
L
-fuzzy operators. As we already noted in
Section
2.2
, for every
L
-relation
R
on
U
,
we have

\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)=R(x,y),

for all
x,y\in U
and
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
. This provides a ‘rule’ for defining relations when upper approximations are known.
We call any map on
\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
as an
L
-fuzzy operator
on
U
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be an
L
-fuzzy operator. For any
a\in L
,
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
and
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{U}(A)=\bigvee_{x}\big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge\mathcal{U}(I_{x})\big)
;

(2)

\mathcal{U}(\overline{a}\wedge\bigvee_{i}A_{i})=\overline{a}\wedge\bigvee_{i}\mathcal{U}(A_{i})
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): Suppose that (1) holds. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\overline{a}\wedge A)

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{x}\big(\overline{(\overline{a}\wedge A)(x)}\wedge\mathcal{U}(I_{x})\big)={\bigvee}_{x}\big(\overline{a\wedge A(x)}\wedge\mathcal{U}(I_{x})\big)

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{x}\big(\overline{a}\wedge\overline{A(x)})\wedge\mathcal{U}(I_{x})\big)=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{x}\big(\overline{A(x)})\wedge\mathcal{U}(I_{x})\big)

\displaystyle=\overline{a}\wedge\mathcal{U}(A)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\big({\bigvee}_{i}A_{i}\big)

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{x}\big(\overline{\big({\bigvee}_{i}A_{i}\big)(x)}\wedge\mathcal{U}(I_{x})\big)={\bigvee}_{x}\big({\bigvee}_{i}\overline{(A_{i})(x)}\wedge\mathcal{U}(I_{x})\big)

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{x}{\bigvee}_{i}\left(\overline{A_{i}(x)}\wedge\mathcal{U}(I_{x})\right)={\bigvee}_{i}{\bigvee}_{x}\left(\overline{A_{i}(x)}\wedge\mathcal{U}(I_{x})\right)

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{i}\,\mathcal{U}(A_{i}).

Combining these two, we obtain that

\mathcal{U}\left(\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}A_{i}\right)=\overline{a}\wedge\mathcal{U}\left({\bigvee}_{i}A\right)=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}\,\mathcal{U}(A_{i}).

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1): Suppose that (2) holds. Since
A=\bigvee_{x}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x})
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(A)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{U}\left({\bigvee}_{x}\left(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\right)\right)=\mathcal{U}\left(\overline{1}\wedge{\bigvee}_{x}\left(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\overline{1}\wedge{\bigvee}_{x}\,\mathcal{U}\left(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\right)={\bigvee}_{x}\mathcal{U}\left(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\right)

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{x}\left(\overline{A(x)}\wedge\mathcal{U}(I_{x})\right).\qed

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be an
L
-fuzzy operator on
U
and let each
\mathcal{S}_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq n
, be an
L
-fuzzy operator on
U
such that
\mathcal{U}\leq S_{j}
.
Then there exists a unique
L
-fuzzy relation
R
on
U
such that
\mathsf{U}(A)=\mathcal{U}(A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)

if and only if

\mathcal{U}(\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}A_{i})=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}\left(\mathcal{U}(A_{i})\wedge\mathcal{S}_{1}(A_{i})\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathcal{S}_{n}(A_{i})\right)

(4.1)

for
a\in L
and
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
Since
\mathcal{U}\leq\mathcal{S}_{j}
, for all
j\leq n
, we have
\mathcal{U}\leq\mathcal{S}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathcal{S}_{n}
and hence

\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{S}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathcal{S}_{n}.

Suppose that there is an
L
-relation
R
such that
\mathcal{U}(A)=\mathsf{U}(A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\big(\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}A_{i}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{U}\big(\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}A_{i}\big)=\Big(\overline{a}\wedge\big({\bigvee}_{i}\mathsf{U}(A_{i})\big)\Big)=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}\mathsf{U}(A_{i})

\displaystyle=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}\mathcal{U}(A_{i})=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}(\mathcal{U}(A_{i})\wedge\mathcal{S}_{1}(A_{i})\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathcal{S}_{n}(A_{i})).

(\Leftarrow)
Suppose that (
4.1
) holds for
a\in L
and
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
. This gives

\mathcal{U}\big({\bigvee}_{i}\,A_{i}\big)=\mathcal{U}\big(\overline{1}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}\,A_{i}\big)=\overline{1}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}\,\mathcal{U}(A_{i})={\bigvee}_{i}\,\mathcal{U}(A_{i}).

and
\mathcal{U}(\overline{a}\wedge A)=\overline{a}\wedge\mathcal{U}(A)
for all
a\in L
and
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
, which are essential properties
of upper approximation operators.

Let us define an
L
-relation
R
on
U
by setting for all
x,y\in U
,

R(x,y)=\mathcal{U}(I_{y})(x).

Then,
\mathsf{U}(I_{y})(x)=R(x,y)=\mathcal{U}(I_{y})(x)
for all
x,y\in U
, and hence
\mathsf{U}(I_{x})=\mathcal{U}(I_{x})
for all
x\in U
. For
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(A)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{U}\big({\bigvee}_{x}\Big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\big)\Big)={\bigvee}_{x}\mathcal{U}\big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\big)

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{x}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge\mathcal{U}(I_{x}))={\bigvee}_{x}(\overline{A(x)}\wedge\mathsf{U}(I_{x}))

\displaystyle={\bigvee}_{x}\mathsf{U}\big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\big)=\mathsf{U}\Big({\bigvee}_{x}\big(\overline{A(x)}\wedge I_{x}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{U}A.

It is also clear that the induced relation
R
is unique, since if there is an
L
-relation
Q
such that
\mathsf{U}_{Q}(A)=\mathcal{U}(A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
, we have

R(x,y)=\mathcal{U}(I_{y})(x)=\mathsf{U}_{Q}(I_{y})(x)=Q(x,y)
for all
x,y\in U
and thus
R=Q
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{L}
be
L
-fuzzy operators.
We say that
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{L}
are
dual
if
\mathcal{U}(A^{\prime})=(\mathcal{L}(A))^{\prime}
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
.
Note that this is equivalent to
\mathcal{L}(A^{\prime})=(\mathcal{U}(A))^{\prime}
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
. Notice also that we used the notion of dual operations already in Section
2
, even it was not defined there. The dual operators define each other uniquely, that is,

\mathcal{U}(A)=(\mathcal{L}(A^{\prime}))^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{L}(A)=(\mathcal{U}(A^{\prime}))^{\prime},

for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
.

Remark 4.3
.

Suppose that the
L
-fuzzy operators
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{L}
on

are dual. Theorem
4.2
can be equivalently expressed in terms of

for any

.

\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{L}
coincide with the upper and lower approximation operators of

\mathcal{U}(\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}A_{i})=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}(\mathcal{U}(A_{i})\wedge\mathcal{L}(A_{i}))

for all

and

.

Proof.

(1) This follows directly from Theorem
4.2
by selecting
n=1
and
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\mathsf{U}
.

(2) By Proposition
3.15
,
R
is mediate if and only if
\mathsf{U}A\leq\mathsf{U}\mathsf{U}A
for any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
from which the result follows by
Theorem
4.2
by setting
n=1
and
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\mathsf{U}\mathsf{U}
.

(3) The relation
R
is Euclidean if and only if for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
,
\mathsf{U}A\leq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}A
by Proposition
3.18
. If we set
n=1
and

\mathcal{S}_{1}=\mathsf{L}\mathsf{U}
, the claim follows from Theorem
4.2
.

(4) By Proposition
3.21
,
R
is adjoint if and only if for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
,
\mathsf{U}A\leq\mathsf{U}\mathsf{L}A
and the claim follows from this by
Theorem
4.2
.

(5) The relation
R
is functional if and only if
\mathsf{U}A\leq\mathsf{L}A
for any
L
-set
A
on
U
by Proposition
3.24
. The result is now clear by this.
∎

Using Theorem
4.2
we can also give conditions for compositions of properties (cf. Theorems 4.8, 4.9, 4.10, and 4.12 in
[
11
]
). The proof
of the following proposition is clear.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{L}
be dual
L
-fuzzy operator on
U
.
There exists a unique mediate, Euclidean and adjoint
L
-relation
R
on
U
such that
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{L}
coincide with the upper and lower approximation operators of
R
,
respectively, if and only if

\mathcal{U}(\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}A_{i})=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}(\mathcal{U}(A_{i})\wedge\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{U}(A_{i}))\wedge\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{U}(A_{i}))\wedge\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{L}(A_{i})))

for all
a\in L
and
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
.

In
[
11
]
, the authors presented the following problem: “Using single axioms to characterize
L
-fuzzy rough approximation operators corresponding to compositions
of serial, reflexive, symmetric, transitive, mediate, Euclidean and adjoint L-fuzzy relations.” Next, we solve this problem so that only the parts concerning serial and symmetric relations remains open.

Our following theorem is closely related to Theorem
4.2
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be an
L
-fuzzy operator on
U
and let each
\mathcal{T}_{k}
,
1\leq k\leq m
, be an
L
-fuzzy operator on
U
such that

\mathcal{U}\geq\mathcal{T}_{k}
. Then there exists a unique
L
-fuzzy relation
R
on
U
such that
\mathsf{U}A=\mathcal{U}(A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)

if and only if

\mathcal{U}(\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}A_{i})=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}(\mathcal{U}(A_{i})\vee\mathcal{T}_{1}(A_{i})\vee\cdots\vee\mathcal{T}_{m}(A_{i}))

(4.2)

for
a\in L
and
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
Since
\mathcal{U}\geq\mathcal{T}_{k}
, for all
k\leq m
, we have
\mathcal{U}\leq\mathcal{T}_{1}\vee\cdots\vee\mathcal{T}_{m}
and hence

\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{T}_{1}\vee\cdots\vee\mathcal{T}_{m}.

If there is an
L
-relation
R
such that
\mathsf{U}A=\mathcal{U}A
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\big(\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}A_{i}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{U}\big(\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}A_{i}\big)=\big(\overline{a}\wedge\mathsf{U}\big({\bigvee}_{i}A_{i}\big)\big)=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}\mathsf{U}(A_{i})

\displaystyle=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}\mathcal{U}(A_{i})=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}(\mathcal{U}(A_{i})\vee\mathcal{T}_{1}(A_{i})\vee\cdots\vee\mathcal{T}_{m}(A_{i})).

The direction
(\Leftarrow)
is proved as in Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Now we can write the following corollary of Theorem
4.6
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{L}
be dual
L
-fuzzy operators on
U
.

(1)

There exists a unique reflexive
L
-relation
R
on

\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{L}
coincide with the upper and lower approximation operators of

\mathcal{U}(\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}A_{i})=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}\left(\mathcal{U}(A_{i})\vee A_{i})\right)

for all
a\in L
and
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
.

(2)

There exists a unique transitive
L
-relation
R
on

\mathsf{U}
and
\mathsf{L}
coincide with the upper and lower approximation operators of

, respectively, if and only if

\mathcal{U}(\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}A_{i})=\overline{a}\wedge{\bigvee}_{i}(\mathcal{U}(A_{i})\vee\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{U}(A_{i})))

for all
a\in L
and
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
.

Proof.

(1) By Proposition
3.5
,
R
is reflexive if and only if
A\leq\mathsf{U}A
for any

from which the result follows by

4.6
by setting

\mathcal{T}_{1}(A)=A
.

(2) By Proposition
3.12
,
R
is transitive if and only if
\mathsf{U}\mathsf{U}A\leq\mathsf{U}A
for any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{L}(U)
from which the result follows by
Theorem
4.6
by setting
n=1
and
\mathcal{T}_{1}=\mathsf{U}\mathsf{U}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{U}
be an
L
-fuzzy operator on
U
. Assume that each
\mathcal{S}_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq n
, is an
L
-fuzzy operator on

\mathcal{U}\leq\mathcal{S}_{j}

\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}\wedge\mathcal{S}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathcal{S}_{n})\vee(\mathcal{T}_{1}\vee\cdots\vee\mathcal{T}_{m}).

(4.3)

Some concluding remarks

In this work, we were able to solve the open problem presented in
[
11
]
so that only the parts concerning serial and symmetric relations remain open.

One obvious reason for this is that we do not have
a condition for symmetry or seriality which is of the suitable form so
that the theorems given in Section
4

could be applied. Interestingly, seriality and symmetry are also such that there are some issues how to generalize them as fuzzy relations, at least
in case of De Morgan Heyting algebras.

Abstract
. We define a random analytic function φ \varphi on the unit disc by letting a Gaussian multiplicative measure to be one of its Clark measures. We show that φ \varphi is almost surely a Blaschke product and we provide rather sharp estimates for the density of its zeroes.



1.
Introduction

Given an analytic self map
\varphi:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}
of the unit disc in the complex plane, for each
\alpha\in{\mathbb{T}}\coloneqq\partial{\mathbb{D}}
the measure
\nu_{\alpha}\coloneqq\nu_{\varphi,\alpha}
is defined via

(1)

\mathrm{Re}\left(\frac{\alpha+\varphi(z)}{\alpha-\varphi(z)}\right)\;=\;\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|e^{i\theta}-z|^{2}}\nu_{\alpha}(d\theta),\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

The measure
\nu_{\alpha}
, or especially its singular part, describes how strongly and where on the boundary the function
\varphi
takes the value
\alpha
– note that this can happen only at the boundary. The
Clark measures

\nu_{\alpha}
thus obtained for
\alpha\in{\mathbb{T}}
have been much studied especially in connection with applications to perturbative operator theory, including the spectral theory of
1
-dimensional Schrödinger equation. Clark measures are defined in
[
Cla72
]
and further studied by Aleksandrov
[
Ale87
,
Ale89
,
Ale94
,
Ale95
,
Ale96
]
, so they are also called Aleksandrov measures. We refer to the reviews
[
PS06
]
and
[
Sak07
]
for the basic properties of this interesting family of measures.

In the present paper we will employ the Clark measures to construct random analytic functions on the unit disc by defining
\varphi:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}
as the analytic self map of
{\mathbb{D}}
such that its Clark measure at point
\alpha=1
equals a given random measure
\nu
. Especially, we will consider the choice

(2)

\nu_{\varphi,1}=\mu_{\gamma}\coloneqq\text{``}\exp(\gamma X(\theta))\text{''},\qquad\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2}].

Here
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2}]
is a fixed parameter, and
\text{``}\exp(\gamma X(\theta))\text{''}
stands for the (random)
Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure
corresponding to a log-correlated Gaussian field
X
. The most natural choice for
X
on
{\mathbb{T}}
is the so-called “canonical log-correlated field”

X_{c}(\theta)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-1/2}\big(A_{n}\cos(n\theta)+B_{n}\sin(n\theta)\big),\qquad\theta\in[0,2\pi),

where the
A_{n},B_{n}
are independent, identically distributed standard Gaussians. In general, log-correlated Gaussian fields
X
on
{\mathbb{T}}
have the covariance structure

(3)

\mathbb{E}\left[X(\theta_{1})X(\theta_{2})\right]=\log\frac{1}{|e^{i\theta_{1}}-e^{i\theta_{2}}|}+g(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})

where
g:{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a (symmetric) continuous function. For the canonical log-correlated field
X_{c}
, one has
g\equiv 0
.

Multiplicative chaos measures form a natural and important class of random positive measures that has re-appeared during last
15
years in various important roles in statistical physics and other applications. We recall their proper definition and basic properties in Section
2
, and we also recall the definition of critical chaos measures at the threshold
\gamma=\sqrt{2}
when it is needed. Overall, we refer the reader to the review
[
RV14
]
for a general background.

It is known that the mesures
\mu_{\gamma}
for
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2})
and
\gamma=\sqrt{2}
are almost surely purely singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{T}}
. The basic theory of Clark measures then implies that
\varphi
is almost surely an inner function, i.e.
|\varphi(e^{i\theta})|=1
for almost every
\theta
, see Proposition
11
. Any inner function
\varphi
admits the decomposition (see
[
Gar07
, Theorem 5.5]
)

\varphi(z)=B(z)S(z),

where
B
is a
Blaschke product
:

B(z)=e^{ic}z^{m}\prod\limits_{|z_{n}|\neq 0}\frac{\overline{z}_{n}}{|z_{n}|}\frac{z_{n}-z}{1-\overline{z}_{n}z}

with
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
m\geq 0
,
z_{n}\in{\mathbb{D}}
and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{n}|)<\infty
, and
S
is a
singular inner function
, i.e.

S(z)=\exp\left(-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{e^{i\theta}+z}{e^{i\theta}-z}d\eta(\theta)\right)

where the measure
d\eta
is positive and singular to
d\theta
.

The harmless convention
\mathrm{Im}(\varphi(0))=0
will be used in this paper. Since we focus on the case
\alpha=1
, by the Herglotz representation formula,

(4)

\varphi(z)=\frac{h(z)-1}{h(z)+1},\qquad h(z)\coloneqq\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{e^{i\theta}+z}{e^{i\theta}-z}\nu_{1}(d\theta).

Our first result shows that the singular part of our random function
\varphi
is almost surely trivial:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2}]
(where
\gamma=\sqrt{2}
refers to the critical chaos) and let
\mu_{\gamma}
be the chaos measure on
{\mathbb{T}}
corresponding to a log-correlated field
X
with the covariance structure (
3
). Assume that
g\in W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
for some
s>1
. Then, the inner function
\varphi
defined via (
4
) with the choice
\nu_{1}=\mu_{\gamma}
is almost surely a Blaschke product.

This result can be thought of as a random analogue of a famous result of Frostman
[
Gar07
, Theorem 6.4]
, which states that given an inner function
f
, for almost every
a\in{\mathbb{D}}
the “Frostman shift”
\tau_{a}\circ f
is a pure Blaschke product. Here
\tau_{a}
is the Möbius automorphism
\tau_{a}:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}
, and
\tau_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z}.

We also provide an estimate for the density of the zeroes of the random Blaschke product
\varphi
:

Theorem 2
.

Assume that
g\in W^{2,2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
in (
3
) and let
z_{1},z_{2},\dots
stand for the zeroes of
\varphi
. Then we have the following phase transition for the density of zeroes:

(1)

Almost surely,
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{k}|)^{\beta}<\infty
for
1-\gamma^{2}/8<\beta
.

(2)

Almost surely,
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{k}|)^{\beta}=\infty
for
1-\gamma^{2}/8>\beta
.

The above result is slightly surprising in the sense that the density of the zeroes goes up when the parameter
\gamma
of the chaos measure decreases. On the other hand, it is natural when one notes that in a heuristic way the support of
\mu_{\gamma}
“decreases” in a sense when
\gamma
increases. One may ask what happens in the supercritical case, especially how does the analogue of Theorem
2
look like for
\gamma>\sqrt{2}
. One of the questions that we plan to study in the future is how the zero set behaves realization wise as the parameter
\gamma
varies.

The present note is a part of our long-term project to study some aspects of random spectral theory via Clark measures. We thank Alexei Poltoratski for reawakening our interest in these questions, and especially we are grateful to discussions with Håkan Hedenmalm who independently suggested us (during a visit to the University of Helsinki a couple of years ago) that one should expect a result to the direction of Theorem
1
above.

2.
Log-correlated Gaussian fields and Gaussian multiplicative chaos

In the following, we consider several random (generalized) functions defined on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, on the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}=\partial\mathbb{D}
, or on an interval of the real axis, say
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\subset\mathbb{R}
. We will usully denote by
X,Y,Z,\dots
some log-correlated Gaussian fields. The parameter
\gamma\in\left(0,\sqrt{2}\right)
will be fixed and
\mu\coloneqq\mu_{\gamma}
will denote the (subcritical) Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure with parameter
\gamma
. The critical case
\gamma=\sqrt{2}
will be recalled separately in the proofs.

Since this note is intended for readers from both probability and analysis backgrounds, we recall some definitions and basic facts about log-correlated Gaussian fields and Gaussian multiplicative chaos.

2.1.
Log-correlated Gaussian fields on the unit circle

Consider the Gaussian field
X_{c}
on the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}
defined by a random Fourier series:

(5)

X_{c}(\theta)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}(A_{n}\cos(n\theta)+B_{n}\sin(n\theta)),\qquad\theta\in[0,2\pi),

where
A_{n},B_{n}
and i.i.d. standard Gaussian variables. We refer to this particular log-correlated field as the
canonical field
(on
{\mathbb{T}}
). Let us recall why the Gaussian field
X_{c}(\theta)
is log-correlated – actually its covariance kernel writes in the simple form

(6)

C(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[X(\theta)X(\theta^{\prime})\right]=-\log\left|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\theta^{\prime}}\right|.

Indeed, a direct calculation yields

C(\theta,\theta^{\prime})=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cos(n(\theta-\theta^{\prime})).

To check that this is the same as
-\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\theta^{\prime}}|
, write

-\log\left|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\theta^{\prime}}\right|=-\log\left|1-e^{i(\theta-\theta^{\prime})}\right|=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1-e^{i(\theta-\theta^{\prime})}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1-e^{-i(\theta-\theta^{\prime})}\right)

and develop the Taylor series.

Due to the divergence of
-\log\left|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\theta^{\prime}}\right|
on the diagonal
\theta=\theta^{\prime}
, the Gaussian field
X_{c}
cannot be defined as a random function, but only as a random distribution in the sense of Schwartz. Indeed, one verifies that the field
X_{c}
almost surely lives in the negative order
Sobolev space
W^{-s,2}
for all
s>0
. Recall that the Sobolev spaces
W^{s,2}
for
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
are defined as

W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}})\coloneqq\left\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})\penalty\ ;\penalty\ ||f||_{s,2}^{2}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(1+|n|^{2}\right)^{s}|\hat{f}(n)|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

Then
W^{-s,2}
and
W^{s,2}
are mutual duals under the natual pairing. For relevant properties of these classical function spaces
W^{s,2}
with
s\in\mathbb{R}
in relation to Gaussian multiplicative chaos, we refer to
[
JSW19
, Section 2.2]
.

We will consider more general log-correlated covariance kernels of the form

K(\theta,\theta^{\prime})=C(\theta,\theta^{\prime})+g(\theta,\theta^{\prime})

with
g\in W^{s,2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
for some
s>1
. The Gaussian field
X
on
{\mathbb{T}}
with kernel
K
is also called a log-correlated Gaussian field, and the above regularity remark applies also to
X
.

2.2.
Gaussian multiplicative chaos on the unit circle

Fix
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2})
. The theory of Gaussian multiplicative measures developed by Kahane
[
Kah85
]
as well as its recent developments take care of rigorously exponentiating a log-correlated Gaussian field such as
\gamma X
.

Definition 3

(Gaussian multiplicative chaos measures)
.

Let
Y
denote any log-correlated Gaussian field on
{\mathbb{T}}
with covariance kernel

K_{Y}(e^{i\theta},e^{i\theta^{\prime}})=-\log\left|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\theta^{\prime}}\right|+g\left(e^{i\theta},e^{i\theta^{\prime}}\right)

where
g
is a (symmetric) continuous function on
{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}
.

Let
Y_{\varepsilon}
be a standard
\varepsilon
-mollification of
Y
using a compact and smooth test function. The Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure
\mu_{Y}
on
{\mathbb{T}}
, associated to
Y
with parameter
\gamma\in\left(0,\sqrt{2}\right)
, is defined as the following limit taken in probability:

d\mu_{Y}(\theta)\coloneqq\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}e^{\gamma Y_{\varepsilon}(e^{i\theta})-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{\varepsilon}(e^{i\theta})^{2}\right]}d\theta.

Here the convergence of the measures is in the sense of weak
∗
convergence, i.e. for any continuous test function
\phi:{\mathbb{T}}\to\mathbb{R}
one has

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\phi(e^{i\theta})d\mu_{Y}(\theta)=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}e^{\gamma Y_{\varepsilon}(e^{i\theta})-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{\varepsilon}(e^{i\theta})^{2}\right]}\phi(e^{i\theta})d\theta.

The Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure
\mu_{Y}
is unique (i.e. does not depend on the mollification) and non-trivial for all
\gamma^{2}<2
(we refer to this as the subcritical regime) and degenerate (i.e. almost surely zero everywhere) if
\gamma^{2}\geq 2
.

The convergence in law or in probability for Gaussian multiplicative chaos measures (i.e. that the above definition makes sense) was established essentially in
[
Kah85
]
, and later on the theory has been developed in many works, e.g.
[
BM03
,
RV10
,
Sha16
,
Ber17
]
.

Remark 4

(
1d
-Gaussian multiplicative chaos measures)
.

The more classical setting, completely similar to the above proposition, defines the Gaussian multiplicative chaos measures on a compact subset of the real line
\mathbb{R}
. In this note, we will use mostly the interval
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\in\mathbb{R}
. Both of them are
1d
-Gaussian multiplicative chaos measures, as are the chaos measures defined on
{\mathbb{T}}
.

We first record some important known properties of the Gaussian multiplicative chaos measures in one-dimension, and we will provide references after stating the results.

Fact 5

(Existence of moments)
.

Let
Y
be a log-correlated Gaussian field on
{\mathbb{T}}
or
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]
and
\mu_{Y}
the associated Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure with
\gamma\in\left(0,\sqrt{2}\right)
as in Definition
3
. Then for all sets
T\subset{\mathbb{T}}
or
T\subset\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]
with non-empty interior,

\mathbb{E}\left[\mu_{Y}(T)^{p}\right]<\infty

if and only if
p<\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}
(this includes finiteness of all negative moments).

Especially one may note that in the subcritical regime
\gamma^{2}<2
, the first moment of the mass of a
1d
-Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure always exists.

Fact 6

(Exact scaling log-correlated fields)
.

Consider the log-correlated Gaussian field
Z
on the interval
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]
with covariance kernel:

K_{Z}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})=\log\frac{1}{|\theta-\theta^{\prime}|}.

Then
Z
is (locally) translation invariant and exact scale invariant: in particular, for all
0<r<1
, the following Gaussian fields are equal in law:

\left(Z(\theta)+\sqrt{-\log r}N\right)_{\theta\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left(Z\left(r^{-1}\theta\right)\right)_{\theta\in\left[-\frac{r}{2},\frac{r}{2}\right]}.

where
N
is a standard Gaussian independent of the field
Z
.

As a consequence, the following equality in law holds for the Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure
\mu_{Z}
associated to the field
Z
with parameter
\gamma\in\left(0,\sqrt{2}\right)
and all
[x,x+r]\subset\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]
:

\int_{[x,x+r]}d\mu_{Z}(\theta)\sim r^{1+\gamma^{2}/2}e^{\gamma\sqrt{-\log r}N}\int_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]}d\mu_{Z}(\theta).

We refer to the last identity as “exact scaling” relation in the sequel.

Fact 7

(Kahane’s convexity inequality)
.

Let
Y_{1}
,
Y_{2}
be two continuous centered Gaussian processes indexed by
{\mathbb{T}}
or on any interval
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, such that for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{1}(\theta)Y_{1}(\theta^{\prime})\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{2}(\theta)Y_{2}(\theta^{\prime})\right].

Then, for all convex function
F
with at most polynomial growth at infinity and any positive finite measure
\sigma
on
{\mathbb{T}}
or on
I
,

\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\int e^{Y_{1}(\theta)-\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}(\theta)^{2}]}d\sigma(\theta)\right)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(\int e^{Y_{2}(\theta)-\mathbb{E}[Y_{2}(\theta)^{2}]}d\sigma(\theta)\right)\right].

Proofs of the above properties.

See respectively
[
RV14
, Theorem 2.5]
,
[
RV14
, Theorem 2.11]
,
[
RV14
, Theorem 2.16]
and
[
RV14
, Theorem 2.1]
. To apply the last convexity inequality to log-correlated Gaussian fields, we systematically use the regularization procedure in Proposition
3
to meet the continuity assumption: this is standard and we will not write out the regularization everytime.
∎

Remark 8

(Comparison of moments)
.

For people with background in analysis we now demonstrate a standard use of Kahane’s convexity inequality mainly for comparing moments of two different chaos measures, since we need to do this later on with careful book-keeping of the constants involved. Consider thus
p<\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}
and the function
F(x)=x^{p}
for
x>0
. Then either
F
or
-F
is convex. Consider a general log-correlated Gaussian field
Y
as in Definition
3
and an exact scaling log-correlated Gaussian field
Z
as in Fact
6
. Since the correction term
g(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
in the covariance function of the field
Y
is bounded in absolute value by a finite constant
C
, we can apply Kahane’s inequality to the following Gaussian fields (with
N
a normal Gaussian variable independent of
Y
and
Z
):

\left(Y(\theta)+\sqrt{C}N\right)_{\theta}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left(Z(\theta)\right)_{\theta},

or

Y(\theta)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left(Z(\theta)+\sqrt{C}N\right)_{\theta},

to conclude that there exists some positive constant
K
such that, for all positive functions
P\left(e^{i\theta}\right)
,

\frac{1}{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}P(e^{i\theta})d\mu_{Y}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}P(e^{i\theta})d\mu_{Z}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right]\leq K\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}P(e^{i\theta})d\mu_{Y}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right].

This relation tells us that modulo a multiplicative constant, the moments of the positively weighted mass of a general Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure associated to any log-correlated Gaussian field
Y
scales similarly like in the exact scaling case with the field
Z
. We will write the above equation as

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}P(e^{i\theta})d\mu_{Z}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right]\asymp\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}P(e^{i\theta})d\mu_{Y}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right].

The constant in
\asymp
only depends on the parameter
\gamma
, the correction term
g\left(e^{i\theta},e^{i\theta^{\prime}}\right)
, and
p
.

To summarize, when estimating the moment of a positively weighted mass of a Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure
\mu_{Y}
, we can switch the underlying Gaussian field to an exact scaling log-correlated Gaussian field
Z
of Fact
6
, up to a deterministic multiplicative constant.

When
\mu=\mu_{X_{c}}
is the Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure on
{\mathbb{T}}
associated to
X
with the canonical covariance kernel in (
6
), the measure
d\mu(\theta)
is then invariant under rotations of the unit circle. In fact, with this particular choice, the measure
\mu
transforms nicely under any Möbius transformation of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. Since we don’t need this fact as we study general log-correlated Gaussian fields, we leave the interested reader to consult
[
Var17
]
for the fascinating connections between Gaussian multiplicative chaos measures and Conformal Field Theory.

We record a simple auxiliary result.

Lemma 9

(Negative moments)
.

Let
I_{1},I_{2}
be two open intervals of the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}
or the interval
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]
and let
\mu
be any Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure on
{\mathbb{T}}
or
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]

. Then the almost surely positive random variable

For
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2}]
, any

has all orders of negative moments on any open interval by Fact

\left(\inf\{a,b\}\right)^{-p}\leq a^{-p}+b^{-p}

∎

(Structure of the carrier)
.

For
\gamma\in\left(0,\sqrt{2}\right)

See

It follows immediately that the analytic self-map

Proposition 11

(Inner function)
.

The random analytic function
\varphi

.

It is a classical fact

3.
Blaschke product property for the canonical field

We find it instrumental to first provide a short and simple proof of the Blaschke product property (see Theorem
1
) in the case of the canonical log-correlated Gaussian field
X_{c}
. In addition, it prepares the reader gently for the perturbation philosophy applied also in the more involved proofs in the sequel.

3.1.
A Frostman type lemma

To prove the Blaschke product property, we first provide a variant of the classical Frostman lemma
[
Gar07
, Theorem II.6.4]
adapted to our situation.

Lemma 12

(A Frostman type lemma)
.

Let
\varphi
be a random inner function
1
1

1

Naturally we assume that the map
(z,\omega)\mapsto\varphi(z)
is jointly measurable, where
\omega
stands for an element in the probability space.
on the unit disc. Then
\varphi
is almost surely a Blaschke product if for some
\varepsilon>0

(7)

\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mathbb{E}\left(\log\frac{1}{|\varphi(z)|}\right)^{1+\varepsilon}<\infty.

Proof.

The inner function
\varphi
is a Blaschke product if and only if (see
[
Gar07
, Theorem II.2.4, Corollary I.6.6]
)

\liminf\limits_{r\to 1^{-}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left(\frac{1}{|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|}\right)d\theta=0.

Define
F:{\mathbb{D}}\to[0,\infty]
by setting
F(z)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\log\left(\frac{1}{|\varphi(z)|}\right)
. Since almost surely
\varphi
is an inner function, and hence a.s. its boundary values are unimodular a.e. at the boundary
\partial{\mathbb{D}}
, we deduce by Fubini that for a.e.
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
it holds that a.s.
\lim_{r\to 1}\log(1/|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|)=0
. By our assumption, we may apply uniform integrability for the expectation in the definition of
F(re^{i\theta})
and deduce that

\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}F(re^{i\theta})=0\qquad\textrm{for almost every}\qquad\theta\in[0,2\pi).

Thereafter (note that
F
is bounded under our assumption) we may apply dominated convergence theorem to deduce that
\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(re^{i\theta})d\theta=0
, whereafter Fubini’s theorem and Fatou’s lemma yield that

\mathbb{E}\left[\liminf\limits_{r\to 1^{-}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left(\frac{1}{|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|}\right)d\theta\right]\leq\liminf\limits_{r\to 1^{-}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(re^{i\theta})d\theta=0.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 14
.

Since in the proof of Lemma
12
we used the assumption only to guarantee uniform integrability under the expectation, and at the same time boundedness of
F
, we could equally well replace the condition
(
7
) by

\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\mathbb{E}\;\psi\left(\log\frac{1}{|\varphi(z)|}\right)<\infty,

where
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is any increasing function with
\lim_{x\to\infty}\psi(x)/x=\infty.

3.2.
Proof of the Blaschke product property

We now prove Theorem
1
for the canonical log-correlated Gaussian field
X_{c}
. Since the measure
\mu
is invariant under rotations of
{\mathbb{T}}
, it is enough to show that

\sup\limits_{r\in[0,1)}\mathbb{E}\left(\log^{+}\frac{1}{|h(r)-1|}\right)^{1+\varepsilon}<\infty.

Since for any
p\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon>0
we have

\left(\log^{+}\frac{1}{x}\right)^{1+\varepsilon}\leq\frac{C(p,\varepsilon)}{x^{p}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|h(r)-1|\geq|\mathrm{Im}(h(r))|,

it is sufficient to consider the imaginary part
\mathrm{Im}(h(r))
and show that for some
p\in(0,1)
,

\sup\limits_{r\in[0,1)}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Im}(h(r))|^{p}}\right]<\infty.

Notice that the imaginary part
\mathrm{Im}(h(r))
writes as a signed Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure:

\mathrm{Im}(h(r))=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{-2r\sin(\theta)}{|r-e^{i\theta}|^{2}}d\mu(\theta).

The key idea is to take out a Fourier mode in the canonical field
X_{c}
and write the following independent decomposition of
X_{c}
:

X_{c}(\theta)=B_{1}\sin(\theta)+\widetilde{X}(\theta).

It follows that (where
\widetilde{\mu}
is the Gaussian multiplicative measure associated with the log-correlated Gaussian field
\widetilde{X}
):

d\mu(\theta)=\exp\left(\gamma B_{1}\sin(\theta)-\frac{\gamma^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)}{2}\right)d\widetilde{\mu}(\theta),

and the following derivative has constant sign:

-\frac{\partial\mathrm{Im}(h(r))}{\partial B_{1}}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{2\gamma r\cdot\sin^{2}(\theta)}{|r-e^{i\theta}|^{2}}\cdot\exp\left(\gamma B_{1}\sin(\theta)-\frac{\gamma^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)}{2}\right)d\widetilde{\mu}(\theta)\geq 0.

Now if
B_{1}\geq 0
, by looking at the interval where
\sin(\theta)\geq 0
, the integral is bigger than

e^{-\gamma^{2}/2}\int_{\theta\in[0,\pi]}\frac{2\gamma r\cdot\sin^{2}(\theta)}{|r-e^{i\theta}|^{2}}d\widetilde{\mu}(\theta).

Similarly if
B_{1}\leq 0
, by looking at the interval where
\sin(\theta)\leq 0
, the integral is bigger than

e^{-\gamma^{2}/2}\int_{\theta\in[\pi,2\pi]}\frac{2\gamma r\cdot\sin^{2}(\theta)}{|r-e^{i\theta}|^{2}}d\widetilde{\mu}(\theta).

In any case, for all
B_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
r\in[1/2,1)
,

-\frac{\partial\mathrm{Im}(h(r))}{\partial B_{1}}\geq C\inf\left\{\int_{\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{3\pi}{4}]}d\widetilde{\mu}(\theta),\int_{\theta\in[\frac{5\pi}{4},\frac{7\pi}{4}]}d\widetilde{\mu}(\theta)\right\}.

The above is a lower bound on the derivative of
\mathrm{Im}(h(r))
with respect to the Gaussian variable
B_{1}
using the auxiliary field
\widetilde{X}
. We now record an elementary observation.

Lemma 15
.

Let
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}}d\rho
denote the distribution density of a standard Gaussian random variable. Let
v:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a differentiable function such that
v^{\prime}(\rho)\geq b>0
for all
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{|v(\rho)|^{p}}d\rho\leq Cb^{-p},

with
C=C(p)
, for any
p\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be the only point where
v(x_{0})=0
. Then

|v(\rho)-v(x_{0})|\geq b|\rho-x_{0}|

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{|v(\rho)-v(x_{0})|^{p}}d\rho\leq b^{-p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{|\rho-x_{0}|^{p}}d\rho\right).

It remains to see that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{|\rho-x_{0}|^{p}}d\rho\leq\int_{x_{0}-1}^{x_{0}+1}\frac{1}{|\rho-x_{0}|^{p}}d\rho+\int_{-\infty}^{x_{0}-1}e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}}d\rho+\int_{x_{0}+1}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}}d\rho\eqqcolon C(p)

which is finite as long as
p\in(0,1)
.
∎

To conclude, let
\rho
parametrize the Gaussian variable
B_{1}
and let
b
be the above lower bound

b=C\inf\left\{\int_{\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{3\pi}{4}]}d\widetilde{\mu}(\theta),\int_{\theta\in[\frac{5\pi}{4},\frac{7\pi}{4}]}d\widetilde{\mu}(\theta)\right\}

independent of
B_{1}
, and apply Lemma
15
. Taking the expectation with respect to
B_{1}
is equivalent to integrating over
\rho
, and we get the following for the conditional expectation on
B_{1}
with
p\in(0,1)
:

\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathrm{Im}(h(r))|^{-p}\,\big|\,\widetilde{X}\right]\leq C\left(\inf\left\{\int_{\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{3\pi}{4}]}d\widetilde{\mu}(\theta),\int_{\theta\in[\frac{5\pi}{4},\frac{7\pi}{4}]}d\widetilde{\mu}(\theta)\right\}\right)^{-p}.

Now taking the expectation with respect to all other Gaussian variables,

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Im}(h(r))|^{p}}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\inf\left\{\int_{\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{3\pi}{4}]}d\widetilde{\mu}(\theta),\int_{\theta\in[\frac{5\pi}{4},\frac{7\pi}{4}]}d\widetilde{\mu}(\theta)\right\}\right)^{-p}\right].

The last quantity, independent of
r
, is finite for all
p\in(0,1)
by Lemma
9
: this finishes the proof.

4.
On the density of zeroes: preparations

In this section, we prepare our study for the density problem, Theorem
2
. Section
4.1
first develops a quantitative version of the Frostman-type Lemma

In Section
4.2
we then get rid of the log-singularity using a perturbative method, whose idea is similar to the one illustrated in the previous section, although the situation here is much more complicated since we need to extract two independent perturbations with suitable properties. Their existence is nontrivial since we are dealing with a general log-correlated field, and the technical details are postponed to Section
4.3
. The upshot of Section
4
is Corollary
23
below, which yields a sufficient condition to the first part of Theorem

in the form of a moment estimate of a weighted auxiliary positive Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure.

4.1.
Lemma on the density of the zeroes

The goal here is to provide a simple quantitative version of Lemma
12
. In the sequel, the symbol
\lesssim
means smaller or equal to within a multiplicative constant independent of
r=|z|
.

Lemma 16
.

Let
z_{1},z_{2},\dots
stand for the zeroes of a Blaschke product
f:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}
and let
0<\beta<1
. We have

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-|z_{k}|)^{\beta}<\infty

if and only if
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta-2}\log(1/|\varphi(z)|)dA(z)<\infty
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\varphi
is a finite Blaschke product. We set
v(z)=(a-|z|^{2})^{\beta}
with
0<\beta<1
and first let the auxiliary parameter
a
satisfy
a>1
. Compute

\Delta v(z)=4\beta\big(\beta|z|^{2}-a\big)(a-|z|^{2})^{\beta-2}\asymp(a-|z|^{2})^{\beta-2}\quad\textrm{for}\;\,z\in{\mathbb{D}},

where the implicit constants in
\asymp
are independent of
a>1
. We note that
v
is smooth on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
up to the boundary, and so is
z\mapsto\log(1/|\varphi(z)|)
apart from its poles, and the latter function vanishes on the boundary. Hence we may apply the Green’s formula and the fact
\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta\log|\varphi(z)|=\sum\limits_{z\in\mathbb{C};\;\varphi(z)=0}\delta_{z}
on the unit disc to obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}(a-|z_{k}|^{2})^{\beta}

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}(a-|z|^{2})^{\beta-2}\log(1/|\varphi(z)|)dA(z)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial D}v(z)\nabla(\log(1/|\varphi(z)|))\cdot z,

where
\cdot
stands for the
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
inner product. By letting
a\downarrow 1^{+}
we obtain

\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}(1-|z_{k}|^{2})^{\beta}\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta-2}\log(1/|\varphi(z)|)dA(z).

This same relation is then obtained for a general Blaschke product by applying it first to a partial Blaschke product and using monotone convergence. Finally, the lemma follows by noting that in our setup the series
\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}(1-|z_{k}|^{2})^{\beta}
and
\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}(1-|z_{k}|)^{\beta}
converge simultaneously.
∎

Corollary 17

The first part of Theorem

holds, if for all

and all

close enough to

, we have for our random inner function

\sup\limits_{|z|=r}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(\frac{1}{|\varphi(z)|}\right)\right]\lesssim(1-r)^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{8}-\varepsilon}.

To establish the first part of Theorem

and

respectively the real and imaginary parts of

We can write them explicitly in terms of the Poisson kernel and its conjugate:

\begin{split}x(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}P_{z}(e^{i\theta})d\mu(\theta),\qquad&P_{z}(e^{i\theta})=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|z-e^{i\theta}|^{2}},\\

4.2.
Control of the log-singularity via perturbation

We first establish an auxiliary result in the spirit of Lemma
15
, factorizing out this time two independent Gaussian components. The idea is the same: by establishing a lower bound in the derivatives of certain directions, we can reduce the estimate with log-singularity to a relatively classical moment estimate. Now that we parametrize the Gaussian perturbation in higher dimensions, the estimation becomes considerably more complicated and we need to study level sets of convex functions with suitable derivative bounds.

The proof of the following proposition is technical and will be postponed to Section

Proposition 19

Combine Corollary

4.3.
Existence of a rank two independent Gaussian summand

We now prove Proposition
19
by showing that one may actually choose
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
to be two trigonometric polynomials. We start first by considering suitable compact perturbations of the identity operator.

Lemma 24
.

Assume that
T
is compact and self-adjoint on a separable Hilbert space
H
and
\mathrm{Id}+T\geq 0
.
Then there are vectors
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{l_{0}}
with the following property: for a given vector
u\in H
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

T-\varepsilon u\otimes u\geq 0.

if and only if

\langle u,\varphi_{j}\rangle=0\qquad\textrm{for}\qquad j=1,\dots,l_{0}.

Proof.

Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis of
H
consisting of eiegnvectors of
T
, ordered so that the eigenvectors that correspond to eigenvalue
-1
are listed first, and write the spectral decomposition

T=-\sum_{j=1}^{l_{0}}\varphi_{j}\otimes\varphi_{j}+\sum_{j>l_{0}}\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}\otimes\varphi_{j}.

Here we used the compactness of the operator
T
to deduce that
l_{0}
may be taken finite, and we also see that there is
\varepsilon>0
such that
\lambda_{j}>\varepsilon-1
for all
j>l_{0}.

Assume then that
u\in H
is normalized and satisfies the stated condition so that
u=\sum_{j>l_{0}}u_{j}\varphi_{j}
with
\sum_{j>l_{0}}u_{j}^{2}=1
. If
x\in H
is arbitrary,
x=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x_{j}\varphi_{j}
, we may compute

\displaystyle\langle x,(\mathrm{Id}+T-\varepsilon u\otimes u)x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j>l_{0}}(1+\lambda_{j})x_{j}^{2}-\varepsilon(\sum_{j>l_{0}}u_{j}x_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\sum_{j>l_{0}}x_{j}^{2}-\varepsilon(\sum_{j>l_{0}}x_{j}^{2})(\sum_{j>l_{0}}u_{j}^{2})=0.

In turn, the necessity of the condition is seen by observing that if
b=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}\varphi_{j}
, with
b_{j_{0}}\neq 0
for some
1\leq j_{0}\leq l_{0}
, we obtain
\langle\varphi_{j_{0}},(\mathrm{Id}+T-\varepsilon b\otimes b)\varphi_{j_{0}}\rangle=-b_{j_{0}}^{2}<0
.
∎

The following example shows that it is not always enough to consider perturbations with one base element. In the context of Proposition
19
this means that choosing
f_{1}
to be simply a trigonometric monomial will not always suffice.

Example 25
.

Let
(e_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis of
H
, and set
a\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j/2}e_{j}
. Then
\mathrm{Id}-a\otimes a\geq 0
. However, for all
n\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
we have
\mathrm{Id}-a\otimes a-\varepsilon e_{n}\otimes e_{n}\not\geq 0
.

In our next auxiliary result, we generalize Lemma
24
to cover operators of form
C+A
, where the compact operator
C
is assumed to dominate
A
in a suitable sense (i.e.
A
is compact with respect to
C
, see condition (
10
) below).

Lemma 26
.

Assume that
C,A
are compact (symmetric) and self-adjoint operators so that both
C\geq 0
and
C+A\geq 0
. Assume also that the kernel
M\coloneqq\mathrm{Ker}(C)
is finite-dimensional, and denote by
P
the orthogonal projection onto
M
. We write
C_{1}\coloneqq C+P
and assume that

(10)

C_{1}^{-1/2}AC_{1}^{-1/2}\qquad\textrm{extends to a compact operator on}\penalty\ H.

Then, there are elements
v_{1},\dots,v_{l_{0}}\in H
such that if
u\in H
satisfies
\langle u,v_{j}\rangle=0
for
j=1,\dots,l_{0},
then

C+A-\varepsilon C_{1}^{1/2}u\otimes C_{1}^{1/2}u\geq 0

for small enough
\varepsilon>0.

Proof.

We may actually assume that
M=\{0\}
by replacing
C
by
C_{1}
and
A
by
A-P
. The claim then follows easily by applying the previous lemma with
T=C_{1}^{-1/2}AC_{1}^{-1/2}
.
∎

Corollary 27
.

In the situation of Lemma
26
, let
\{e_{j}\}
denote an orthonormal basis of
H
consisting of eigenvectors of
C_{1}
. Then for any
E\subset{\mathbb{N}}
with
|E|\geq l_{0}+1
there is a nontrivial linear combination
b=\sum_{j\in E}a_{j}e_{j}
and
\varepsilon>0
so that

C+A-\varepsilon b\otimes b\geq 0.

Proof.

Simply note that if
u
is a linear combination of eigenvectors of
C_{1}
, then
b=C_{1}^{1/2}u
is also.
∎

We then apply the previous observations to our situation.

Lemma 28
.

Assume that
C
is the covariance of the canonical field on
{\mathbb{T}}
and let
A\in W^{2,2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
be a symmetric (real-valued) function such that

C+A\geq 0
. We shall also denote by
C,A
the corresponding operators on
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
. Then there are nontrivial finite trigonometric polynomials
f_{1},f_{2}
such that

C+A-f_{1}\otimes f_{1}-f_{2}\otimes f_{2}\geq 0

and

\inf_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\left(|f_{1}(\theta)|^{2}+|f_{2}(\theta)|^{2}\right)>0.

Proof.

Let us denote by
(e_{j})_{j\geq 0}
the standard trigonometric basis of
L^{2}
, where
e_{0}
is constant, and
e_{2j-1},e_{2j}
are the sine and cosine functions with frequency
j
for
j\geq 1
. In this basis
C,C_{1}
are the diagonal operators

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm diag\,}(0,1,1,1/2,1/2,1/3,1/3,\dots),

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm diag\,}(1,1,1,1/2,1/2,1/3,1/3,\dots)\;\eqqcolon\;{\rm diag\,}(m),

and
P=e_{0}\otimes e_{0}
is one-dimensional. Especially,
C_{1}
is the Fourier-multiplier
T_{m}
, where
m
is the above defined vector, and we see immediately that
C:W^{s,2}(T)\to W^{s+1,2}(T)
is an isomorphism. The powers
C^{\alpha}
are naturally defined. Lemma
26
applies as soon as we verify that (
10
) is satisfied. This follows e.g. by checking that
C_{1}^{-1/2}AC_{1}^{-1/2}
is Hilbert-Schmidt, which is clearly equivalent to
(1-\Delta_{x})^{1/4}(1-\Delta_{y})^{1/4}A\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
. Writing this in terms of the Fourier coefficients and using Cauchy-Schwarz, we see that this is implied by the fact that
A\in W^{1,2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}).

Let us denote by
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{l}
an orthonormal basis of the eigenspace correponding to eigenvalue
-1
of the operator
C_{1}^{-1/2}AC_{1}^{-1/2}
. Then the condition for a trigonometric polynomial
f
ensuring that
C+A-\varepsilon f\otimes f\geq 0
for some
\varepsilon>0
can be written in the form

\langle f,C_{1}^{-1/2}\varphi_{j}\rangle=0,\qquad j=1,\dots l,

where the computation is a priori formal. However, the element
C_{1}^{-1/2}\varphi_{j}
belongs to
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
since we check as above that
C_{1}^{-1}AC_{1}^{-1}
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{2}
(since
A\in W^{2,2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
), and hence we obtain

C_{1}^{-1/2}\varphi_{j}=C_{1}^{-1/2}\big(-C_{1}^{-1/2}AC_{1}^{-1/2}\varphi_{j}\big)=-C_{1}^{-1}AC_{1}^{-1}(C_{1}^{1/2}\varphi_{j})\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}).

Let us denote
C_{1}^{-1/2}\varphi_{j}\eqqcolon\psi_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,l
. This means that
C+A-\varepsilon f\otimes f\geq 0
for some
\varepsilon>0
as soon as the trigonometric polynomial
f
satisfies

(11)

\langle f,\psi_{j}\rangle=0,\qquad j=1,\dots l.

We still need to find two trigonometric polynomials
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, such that they both satisfy (
11
) and without common zeroes. For this purpose, we first fix
f_{1}
to be any non-trivial trigonometric polynomial satisfying (
11
). This can obviously be done by choosing the degree of
f_{1}
to be at least
l+1
. The polynomial
f_{1}
has only finitely many zeroes. Using a perturbative argument and induction on the number of zeroes, it clearly suffices to find
f_{2}
such that it does vanish in a given point
\theta_{0}
and it satisfies (
11
). For any sequence
y=(y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
denote by
P_{N}y
the finite sequence
P_{N}y\coloneqq(y_{n})_{0\leq n\leq N}
. A trigonometric polynomial
f_{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}c_{j}e_{j}
vanishes at
\theta_{0}
exactly when
\langle f_{2},P_{N}g_{0}\rangle=0
, where

g_{0}\coloneqq(1,\sin(\theta_{0}),\cos(\theta_{0}),\sin(2\theta_{0}),\dots).

Thus the choice of
f_{2}
as a trigonometric polynomial is not possible if and only if

P_{N}g_{0}\in{\rm span\,}\{P_{N}\psi_{1},\dots,P_{N}\psi_{l}\}\qquad\textrm{ for all}\;\;N.

However, then an elementary argument implies that
g_{0}\in{\rm span\,}\{\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{l}\}
, which is impossible since
g_{0}\notin\ell^{2}.
Now the positivity of both
C+A-\varepsilon_{1}f_{1}\otimes f_{1}
and
C+A-\varepsilon_{2}f_{2}\otimes f_{2}
implies

C+A-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{1}f_{1}\otimes f_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{2}f_{2}\otimes f_{2}\geq 0,

and this completes the proof.
∎

5.
Proof of the upper bound on the density of zeroes

In this section we verify the sufficient condition in Corollary
23
, which implies the first part of Theorem
2
, and it also automatically implies the general form of Theorem
1
. Since

(12)

\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{x}(z)\wedge 1\right]\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{x}(z)^{p}\right],

for all
p\in(0,1)
, it suffices to establish a
p

with

. We only need the fact that
\widetilde{x}(z)
is generated by a general log-correlated Gaussian field in the rest of the proof, so some notations are deliberately not strict (the proofs work for any Gaussian multiplicative chaos measures).

Lemma 29
.

With the assumptions of Theorem
1
we have for any

the estimate

\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{x}(z)^{p}\right]\lesssim(1-|z|)^{p(1-p)\gamma^{2}/2}.

Proof.

We denote by
A_{\widetilde{\mu},\varepsilon}(\theta)
stands for the average of the Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure
d\widetilde{\mu}
over the interval
[\theta-\varepsilon,\theta+\varepsilon]
. Let us consider our Gaussian field
\widetilde{X}
on the interval
\theta\in(-1/2,1/2)
. Let
X_{1}
be an exactly scaling field on
(-1/2,1/2)
. At this point we refer to Fact
6
in Section
2
for the properties of the exactly scaling field. For the chaos
\mu_{1}
constructed from the exactly scaling field

it holds that

\mu_{1}(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\sim 2\varepsilon^{1+\gamma^{2}/2}\exp\big(\gamma N(0,\log{1/\varepsilon})\big)\mu^{\prime}_{1}(-1/2,1/2),

where
\mu^{\prime}_{1}
is a copy of
\mu_{1}
and the normal variable
N(0,\log{1/\varepsilon})
is independent of
\mu^{\prime}_{1}
. This distributional equality yields immediately that
\mathbb{E}\left[(\mu_{1}(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))^{p}\right]=c_{p}\varepsilon^{p(1+\gamma^{2}(1-p)/2)}
(use Fact
5
in Section
2
). By the reasoning in Remark
8
we deduce the corresponding upper bound for
\widetilde{\mu}
, i.e.

(13)

\mathbb{E}\left[(\widetilde{\mu}(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))^{p}\right]\lesssim\varepsilon^{p(1+\gamma^{2}(1-p)/2)}.

To obtain the statement of the lemma we may assume (e.g. by Harnack) that
z=r\in(0,1)
, where
1-r=2^{-k}
for an integer
k\geq 2
. Let us denote
q:=p(1+\gamma^{2}(1-p)/2).
By a standard property of the Poisson extension (that can be read directly from the kernel) we may upper bound
\widetilde{x}(r)
by the convex combination

(14)

\widetilde{x}(r)\lesssim 2^{-k}\widetilde{\mu}({\mathbb{T}})+\sum_{j=0}^{k}2^{-j}A_{\widetilde{\mu},2^{j-k}}(\theta).

In view of this and (
13

for

\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{x}(r)^{p}\right]\lesssim 2^{-kp}\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\mu}({\mathbb{T}})^{p}\right]+\sum_{j=0}^{k}2^{-jp}2^{(j-k)q}2^{(k-j)p}\lesssim 2^{k(p-q)}\lesssim(1-r)^{q-p},

which yields the claim by the definition of

.
∎

Corollary 30

For all
\varepsilon>0
and

\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{x}(z)\wedge 1\right]\lesssim(1-|z|)^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{8}-\delta},

This follows simply by choosing

in Lemma

.
∎

This completes the proof for the first part of Theorem

in view of (

We end this section by presenting an alternative to Corollary

that is based on the following known result:

Lemma 31

Let

be a log-correlated Gaussian field on the unit circle

Second proof of Corollary

The argument below does not depend on the log-correlated Gaussian field so let

∎

6.
Proof of the lower bound on the density of zeroes

We now prove the second part of Theorem
2
. Recall that
A_{\mu,\varepsilon}(\theta)
stands for the average of the Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure
d\mu
over the interval
[\theta-\varepsilon,\theta+\varepsilon]
.
We start by recalling a useful result on the multifractal spectrum of chaos measure estimating the size of the set of points
e^{i\theta}\in{\mathbb{T}}
such that
A_{\mu,1-r}(\theta)
is (roughly speaking) of order
(1-r)^{o(1)}
. According to
[
Ber23

(see also an earlier discussion of multifractal spectra in
[
RV14
, Section 4, especially equation (4.6)]

it holds that

{\rm dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(G_{0})=1-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{8},\quad\text{where}\quad G_{0}\coloneqq\left\{\theta\in[0,2\pi)\penalty\ \big|\penalty\ \lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{\log A_{\mu,1-r}(\theta)}{\log(1-r)}=0\right\}.

Fix
\delta>0
. Since
G_{0}\subset\bigcup_{k\geq 1}G_{k}
with

G_{k}=\left\{\theta\in[0,2\pi)\penalty\ \big|\penalty\ \frac{\log A_{\mu,1-r}(\theta)}{\log(1-r)}\in(-\delta,\delta)\quad\text{for all}\quad r\in(1-1/k,1)\right\},

we may pick
r_{0}\in(1/2,1)
so that

\text{dim}_{\mathcal{H}}(G)\geq 1-\delta/2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{8}

with

\quad G\coloneqq\left\{\theta\in[0,2\pi)\penalty\ \big|\penalty\ \frac{\log A_{\mu,1-r}(\theta)}{\log(1-r)}\in(-\delta,\delta)\quad\text{for all}\quad r\in(r_{0},1)\right\}.

We denote for given
\varepsilon>0
the
\varepsilon
-thickening of the set
G
by

, i.e.
G_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\{\theta\penalty\ |\penalty\ {\rm d\,}(\theta,G)\leq\varepsilon\}
. By standard estimates for the Hausdorff content (see
[
Mat95

), we then have the following lower bound for the Lebesgue measure of the
\varepsilon
-thickening

, that

(15)

|G_{\varepsilon}|\gtrsim\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/8+\delta}.

We then define the random subset
H\subset{\mathbb{D}}
by setting

H\coloneqq\{re^{i\theta}\penalty\ |\penalty\ r\in(r_{0},1),\quad\theta\in G_{1-r}\}

and claim that

(16)

c_{0}(1-|z|)^{\delta}\leq x(z)\leq c_{1}(1-|z|)^{-\delta}\quad\text{for}\quad z\in H,

where the constants

are independent of

For the upper bound we proceed as in (

. Using additonally the fact that

\begin{split}x(z)&\;\;\leq\;\;c^{\prime}x(z^{\prime})\;\;\lesssim\;\;c^{\prime}\sum_{2^{k}(1-r)\;\leq 1}2^{-2k}A_{\mu,2^{k}(1-r)}(\theta^{\prime})\\
&\;\;\leq\;\;c^{\prime}\sum_{2^{k}(1-r)\leq 1-r_{0}}2^{-2k}A_{\mu,2^{k}(1-r)}(\theta^{\prime})+c^{\prime}(1-r_{0})^{-1}\mu({\mathbb{T}})\\
&\;\;\lesssim\;\;c^{\prime}(1-|z|)^{-\delta}+c^{\prime\prime},\end{split}

where

is a random constant. This yields (
16
).

In order to control the contribution of the imaginary part
y
we will use a simple deterministic argument that relies on the fact that when the averages of the measure are in control, then the imaginary part cannot be too large either. Assume that
z=re^{i\theta}\in H
. Since the conjugate kernel has the upper bound
\lesssim\max(|e^{i\theta}-z|^{-1},(1-r)^{-1})
and we have

\mu(\{\theta^{\prime}\,\big|\,|\theta^{\prime}-\theta|\leq(1-r)2^{k}\})=(1-r)2^{k}A_{\mu,2^{k}(1-r)}(\theta),

it follows by considering dyadic annuli around
\theta
such that the inner radii are
(1-r)2^{k}
, with

, and the interval
(\theta-(1-r),\theta+(1-r))
separately, that

\displaystyle|y(z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{r_{0}}\mu({\mathbb{T}})+\sum_{2^{k}(1-r)\leq 1-r_{0}}A_{\mu,2^{k}(1-r)}(\theta)\;\leq\;c+c^{\prime}\log\left(\frac{1-r_{0}}{1-r}\right)(1-|z|)^{-\delta}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{2}(1-|z|)^{-2\delta},

where
c_{2}
is a random constant.

We are now ready to conclude the desired lower bound.
For
z\in H
it holds that
\log|\varphi(z)|\gtrsim(1-|z|)^{-(\delta+4\delta)}
. By (

) we obtain that

\int_{|z|=r}-\log|\varphi(z)|d|z|\;\gtrsim\;(1-r)^{(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{8}+\delta)}(1-r)^{(5\delta)}.

Since

can be chosen arbitrary small this implies the claim by Lemma

.

={}&\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{R_{k}}\frac{1}{|\theta/2|^{s}}e^{\gamma\tilde{Y}_{\varepsilon/2}(\theta/2)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{Y}_{\varepsilon/2}(\theta/2)^{2}\right]}d\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right)^{p}\right]\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{=}}{}&2^{sp-p}\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{R_{k}}\frac{1}{|\theta|^{s}}e^{\gamma\tilde{Y}_{\varepsilon}(\theta)+\gamma\sqrt{\log 2}N-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{Y}_{\varepsilon}(\theta)^{2}\right]-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\log 2}d\theta\right)^{p}\right]\\
={}&2^{sp-p(1+\gamma^{2}/2)+p^{2}\gamma^{2}/2}\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{R_{k}}\frac{1}{|\theta|^{s}}e^{\gamma\tilde{Y}_{\varepsilon}(\theta)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{Y}_{\varepsilon}(\theta)^{2}\right]}d\theta\right)^{p}\right]\\
={}&2^{sp-p(1+\gamma^{2}/2)+p^{2}\gamma^{2}/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{R_{k}}\frac{1}{|\theta|^{s}}d\mu_{\tilde{Y}}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right],\end{split}

where in step
(\ast)
we used the scaling relation.

The careful reader might has perhaps observed, that we have above actually treated only the subcritical chaos, i.e.
\gamma<\sqrt{2}
. In order to extend to the case of critical chaos, we simply use the definition via the so-called Seneta-Heyde normalization, where one defines the critical chaos
\mu^{\gamma_{c}}_{Y}
as the weak-
\star
limit in probability

\mu^{\gamma_{c}}_{Y}=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sqrt{-\log\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma_{c}},

where
\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma_{c}}
is the
\varepsilon
-regularized measure with parameter
\gamma_{c}=\sqrt{2}
,

d\mu^{\gamma_{c}}_{Y}(\theta)=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}e^{\gamma_{c}Y_{\varepsilon}(\theta)-\frac{\gamma_{c}^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{\varepsilon}(\theta)^{2}\right]}d\theta.

Via this definition one immediately checks that (
17
) remains valid also for
\gamma=\sqrt{2}
. For other equivalent definitions and a review on the critical Gaussian chaos measures, see
[
Pow20
]
.

Notice that the exponent
sp-p(1+\gamma^{2}/2)+p^{2}\gamma^{2}/2
is negative if and only if
s<s(p)=1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}(1-p)
. Similar scaling relation holds between
Q_{k+1}
and
Q_{k}
. When

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{[-\pi/8,\pi/8]}\frac{1}{|\theta|^{s}}d\mu_{\tilde{Y}}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right]<\infty,

we have, since
Q_{k+1}\subset Q_{k}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{Q_{1}}\frac{1}{|\theta|^{s}}d\mu_{\tilde{Y}}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right]&=2^{sp-p(1+\gamma^{2}/2)+p^{2}\gamma^{2}/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{Q_{0}}\frac{1}{|\theta|^{s}}d\mu_{\tilde{Y}}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right]\\
&>2^{sp-p(1+\gamma^{2}/2)+p^{2}\gamma^{2}/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{Q_{1}}\frac{1}{|\theta|^{s}}d\mu_{\tilde{Y}}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right],\end{split}

in which case it is necessary to have
s<s(p)=1+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}(1-p)
. On the other hand, if this is satisfied, then subadditivity yields for any
0<p<1
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{[-\pi/8,\pi/8]}\frac{1}{|\theta|^{s}}d\mu_{\tilde{Y}}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right]\leq\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{R_{k}}\frac{1}{|\theta|^{s}}d\mu_{\tilde{Y}}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right]=C_{0}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{(sp-p(1+\gamma^{2}/2)+p^{2}\gamma^{2}/2)k}

where
C_{0}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{R_{0}}\frac{1}{|\theta|^{s}}d\mu_{\tilde{Y}}(\theta)\right)^{p}\right]<\infty
since the Gaussian chaos measures in
1d
(even in the critical case) have finite
p
-moments for
p<1
. ∎

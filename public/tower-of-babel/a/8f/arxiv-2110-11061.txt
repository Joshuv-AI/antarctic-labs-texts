Abstract
. A classical result due to Lovász (1967) shows that the isomorphism type of a graph is determined by homomorphism counts. That is, graphs G G and H H are isomorphic whenever the number of homomorphisms K → G K\to G is the same as the number of homomorphisms K → H K\to H for all graphs K K . Variants of this result, for various classes of finite structures, have been exploited in a wide range of research fields, including graph theory and finite model theory. We provide a categorical approach to homomorphism counting based on the concept of polyadic (finite) set. The latter is a special case of the notion of polyadic space introduced by Joyal (1971) and related, via duality, to Boolean hyperdoctrines in categorical logic. We also obtain new homomorphism counting results applicable to a number of infinite structures, such as finitely branching trees and profinite algebras.

A celebrated result of Lovász
[

Lovász proved this result, more generally, for finite

This is a prototypical example of homomorphism counting result. Extensions and variations of this theorem have been exploited in a broad range of research areas, such as graph theory, finite model theory and quantum information
[
11
,
16
,
29
]
.

Categorical generalisations of Lovász’ result were proved by Pultr

and Isbell

[

]
, utilising a direct generalisation of Lovász’ combinatorial counting argument. A different approach, which is generalised in the present paper, was adopted in a recent joint work with Dawar and Jakl

. These results apply to a large class of

categories, i.e. categories with only finitely many morphisms between any two objects. Examples include graphs, finite groups and, more generally, finite members of any equationally defined class of universal algebras.

The aim of this article is two-fold. On the one hand, we show that homomorphism counting results are basically a consequence of a more general result about polyadic (finite) sets, a special case of Joyal’s polyadic spaces
[
21
]
. The latter are dual—in the sense of Stone duality for Boolean algebras
[
42
]
—to Boolean hyperdoctrines, a fundamental tool of categorical logic
[

]
; for more details, see
[
30
]
. This exposes a new connection between homomorphism counting, as studied in graph theory and related fields, and the structural methods of category theory and categorical logic.

On the other hand, we exploit the framework of polyadic sets, combined with a topological argument, to extend homomorphism counting results beyond categories of finite structures. We thus obtain new general results that apply, for example, to finitely branching trees and (topologically finitely generated) profinite algebras. An application to finite-variable logics in finite model theory, which relies on the notion of game comonad introduced by Abramsky, Dawar
et al.
, is also presented.

The present paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we recall some basic categorical definitions and facts, and introduce the notion of (left- and right-) combinatorial category, in which the isomorphism types of objects are determined by homomorphism counts. In Section
3
we study polyadic (finite) sets and prove some of their main properties. These results are used in Section
4
to establish homomorphism counting results for locally finite categories. In Section
5
we prove a general theorem characterising the isomorphism type of certain objects in categories that are not necessarily locally finite, and specialise it to the setting of locally finitely presentable categories. Several applications of these results are discussed in Section
6
.

Example 2.2

Let

Broadly speaking, a homomorphism counting result asserts that the existence of a natural isomorphism

Definition 2.3

A category

There is of course a dual notion of left-combinatorial category:

A category

Let us hasten to point out that the term

Polyadic sets and Stirling kernels

Definition 3.3

A

3.3.
Pointwise isomorphisms

The main result of this section (Proposition

Recall that two parallel functors

In view of Proposition

4.
Homomorphism Counting in Locally Finite Categories

We shall now exploit the framework of Section
3
, and in particular Proposition
3.15
, to establish a homomorphism counting result for so-called locally finite categories (Theorem
4.3
below).

Definition 4.1
.

A category
\A
is
locally finite
if, for all objects
a,b\in\A
, the set
\hom_{\A}(a,b)
is finite.

To start with, we record for future reference a well known and easily proved fact about finite monoids. Recall that a monoid
(M,{\cdot},1)
satisfies the
left-cancellation law
provided that, for all
x,y,z\in M
,

x\cdot y=x\cdot z\kern 5.0pt\Longrightarrow\quad y=z.

Lemma 4.2
.

A finite monoid satisfying the left-cancellation law is a group.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\A
be a locally finite category admitting a proper factorisation system
({Q},{M})
such that
\A
is
{Q}
-well-founded. Then
\A
is right-combinatorial.

Proof.

Let
\A
be as in the statement. For any object

, the representable functor

is a polyadic finite set by Lemma
3.1
, and its Stirling kernel sends an object

to the finite set

consisting of the embeddings

(see Example
3.9
).

Now, if
a,b\in\A
are any two objects such that
\mathcal{y}_{a}(c)\cong\mathcal{y}_{b}(c)
for all

then, by Proposition
3.15
,
{M}(c,a)\cong{M}(c,b)
for all
c\in\A
. In particular, as
{M}(a,a)
is non-empty (it contains the identity arrow), there exists an embedding
i\in{M}(a,b)
. Similarly, there exists an embedding

. Note that, by Lemma
A.2
(a)
,
j\circ i\in{M}(a,a)
. Lemma
4.2
, combined with the fact that every embedding is a monomorphism, entails that the set
{M}(a,a)
equipped with the composition operation is a group. So,

has an inverse. It follows by Lemma
A.2
(b)
,
(e)
that

is an isomorphism.
∎

A variant of Theorem

Example 4.6

Example 4.8
.

Let
\sigma

be the category of

First, recall that epimorphisms and monomorphisms in

Now, note that the factorisation system

5.
Beyond Locally Finite Categories

In Theorem
4.3
we saw that a large class of locally finite categories is right-combinatorial. The main result of this section (Theorem
5.10
below) is a ‘local’ extension of this fact to categories that need not be locally finite. This result is then specialised to the case of locally finitely presentable categories.

5.1.
Nerves and hom-spaces

Throughout this section we fix a category
\D
admitting a proper factorisation system
({Q},{M})
, and a full subcategory
\C
of
\D
such that:

(i)

\C
is a
dense

\D
, i.e. every

\C{\downarrow}a\to\D

(ii)

\C

\C\hookrightarrow\D
.

(iii)

For every composite

.

Remark 5.1
.

Because
\C

Further, item (iii) amounts to saying that the proper factorisation system

in

Also, note that item (iii) implies that

In Theorem

It is useful to observe that the nerve functor is full and faithful because

Since we want to be able to count morphisms from objects of

An object

Example 5.3

The previous definition makes sense for all categories

Lemma 5.7

(i)

The colimit cocone consists of embeddings.

(ii)

Intuitively, an object

.

Note that, by Lemma

consists entirely of embeddings.

Theorem 5.10
.

For any two
\C

\C
-type,

a\cong b\kern 5.0pt\Longleftrightarrow\kern 5.0pt\hom_{\D}(c,a)\cong\hom_{\D}(c,b)\ \text{ for all }c\in\C.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in\D
be as in the statement. For the non-trivial direction, suppose that
\hom_{\D}(c,a)\cong\hom_{\D}(c,b)
for all
c\in\C
. By assumption,
b
is the colimit of a directed diagram
\{b_{i}\mid i\in I\}
in
\C
satisfying the conditions in Definition

.
Therefore,
b\cong\operatornamewithlimits{colim}_{D}b_{i}
entails

\mathcal{y}_{a}(b)\cong\lim_{i\in I}N_{a}(b_{i}),

i.e. the map associating with a compatible cocone

the unique connecting morphism

is a bijection. If each finite set

is equipped with the discrete topology, then the induced inverse limit topology
\tau
on

coincides with the topology of

and so we have a homeomorphism of Stone spaces

(2)

E_{a}(b)\cong\lim_{i\in I}N_{a}(b_{i}).

Just observe that the diagram of the

\C{\downarrow}b\to\D
, and so the identity map

is continuous. In turn, since any two comparable compact Hausdorff topologies on a set must coincide, the two topologies are one and the same.

The space

is an embedding. Conversely, if

is homeomorphic to

By assumption, the nerves

are pointwise isomorphic. It follows by Proposition

and Lemma

{M}(b_{i},a)\cong{M}(b_{i},b)

for every

. As the sets

are non-empty (they contain the colimit maps), we conclude that the space

is the inverse limit of non-empty finite discrete spaces and thus

(see e.g.

5.2.
Locally finitely presentable categories

In this section we specialise Theorem
5.10
to the case of locally finitely presentable categories. To start with, we recall some basic definitions; for a more thorough treatment, the reader can consult e.g.
[
5
]
.

An object
a
of a category
\A
is
finitely presentable
(respectively,
finitely generated
) if the associated covariant hom-functor

\hom_{\A}(a,-)\colon\A\to\Set

preserves directed colimits (respectively, directed colimits of monomorphisms). A category
\A
is said to be
locally finitely presentable
if it is cocomplete, every object is a directed colimit of finitely presentable objects, and there exists, up to isomorphism, only a set of finitely presentable objects.

Let
\A
be a locally finitely presentable category. Then
\A
admits a proper factorisation system
({Q},{M})
where
{Q}
consists of the strong epimorphisms and
{M}
of the monomorphisms. Further, an object
a\in\A
is finitely generated if, and only if, there exist a finitely presentable object
b\in\A
and a quotient (i.e., a strong epimorphism)
b\twoheadrightarrow a
. For a proof of these facts see, e.g.,
[
5
, Propositions 1.61 and 1.69(ii)]
. Throughout, we denote by
\A_{\mathrm{fp}}
and
\A_{\mathrm{fg}}
the full subcategories of
\A
consisting, respectively, of the finitely presentable and finitely generated objects.

In this context, the role of the dense subcategory
\C
in Theorem
5.10
is played by
\A_{\mathrm{fg}}
. However, it is an easy observation that any object of finite
\A_{\mathrm{fp}}
-type is also of finite
\A_{\mathrm{fg}}
-type:

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
\A
be a locally finitely presentable category. An object of
\A
is of finite
\A_{\mathrm{fp}}
-type if, and only if, it is of finite
\A_{\mathrm{fg}}
-type.

Proof.

For the non-trivial direction, suppose that
a
is of finite
\A_{\mathrm{fp}}
-type and consider an arbitrary
b\in\A_{\mathrm{fg}}
. If
f\colon c\twoheadrightarrow b
is a quotient with
c
finitely presentable, the map
-\circ f\colon\hom_{\A}(b,a)\to\hom_{\A}(c,a)
is injective. Since the latter set is finite, so is the former.
∎

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
\A
be a locally finitely presentable category. For any two objects
a,b\in\A
of finite
\A_{\mathrm{fp}}
-type,

a\cong b\kern 5.0pt\Longleftrightarrow\kern 5.0pt\hom_{\A}(c,a)\cong\hom_{\A}(c,b)\ \text{ for all }c\in\A_{\mathrm{fg}}.

Proof.

Recall that
\A_{\mathrm{fg}}
is a dense subcategory of
\A
because so is
\A_{\mathrm{fp}}
(see e.g.
[
5
, Proposition 1.22]
), and it is closed in
\A
under
{Q}
-images (see e.g.
[
5
, Proposition 1.69(i)]
). Further, it is a folklore result that
\A_{\mathrm{fg}}
has all finite colimits and these are preserved by the inclusion functor
\A_{\mathrm{fg}}\hookrightarrow\A
.
Finally, every object of
\A
is
\A_{\mathrm{fg}}
-separable (cf.
[
5
, Proposition 1.62 and Theorem 1.70]
). Therefore, an application of Theorem
5.10
with
\D\coloneqq\A
and
\C\coloneqq\A_{\mathrm{fg}}
, combined with Lemma
5.11
, yields the statement.
∎

Corollary 5.13
.

Let

If

6.1.
Trees

If
(P,{\leq})
is a poset, then
C\subseteq P
is a
chain
if it is linearly ordered. A

is a poset
(P,\leq)
such that, for all
u\in P
, the set

{\downarrow}u\coloneqq\{v\in P\mid v\leq u\}

is a finite chain.
The
covering relation

\prec
associated with a partial order
\leq
is defined by
u\prec v
if and only if
u<v
and there is no
w
such that
u<w<v
.
The
roots
of a forest are the minimal elements. A
tree
is a forest with at most one root (note that a tree is either empty, or has a unique root, the least element in the order).
Morphisms of trees are maps which preserve the root and the covering relation.
The category of trees is denoted by
\T
. Monomorphisms and strong epimorphisms in
\T
coincide, respectively, with the injective and surjective tree morphisms.

It is well known that
\T
is a locally finitely presentable category in which the finitely presentable objects, which coincide with the finitely generated ones, are precisely the finite trees. Moreover, it is not difficult to see that a tree
(P,\leq)
has finite
\T_{\mathrm{fp}}
-type if, and only if, it is
finitely branching
. That is, for every
u\in P
, the set
\{v\in P\mid u\prec v\}
is finite.

Thus, Theorem
5.12
entails at once the following result:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
P,Q
be any two finitely branching trees. Then
P\cong Q
if and only if, for all finite trees
R
, the number of tree morphisms
R\to P
is the same as the number of tree morphisms
R\to Q
.

Remark 6.2
.

In the last part of the proof of Theorem
5.10
, we used the fact that

\forall i\in I.\ {M}(b_{i},a)\neq\emptyset\kern 5.0pt\Longrightarrow\kern 5.0pt{M}(\operatornamewithlimits{colim}_{i\in I}b_{i},a)\neq\emptyset

where, using the notation of the aforementioned theorem, the objects

sit in the category
\C
and
a
is a
\C
-separable object of finite
\C
-type.

This can be regarded as a generalisation of König’s Lemma for trees, stating that every finitely branching infinite tree contains an infinite simple path. Just observe that a countably infinite simple path

is a colimit in
\T
of finite simple paths

of (increasing) length

. If
Q
is an infinite tree, then for all

there exists an embedding

. If, in addition,

is finitely branching, then it has finite
\T_{\mathrm{fp}}
-type (and is

-separable) and so there is an embedding

.
7
7

7

In the specific case of trees, embeddings could be replaced by arbitrary arrows. Just note that every morphism in

whose domain is linearly ordered is automatically injective.

In this sense, a version of König’s Lemma holds, in particular, in every locally finitely presentable category.

6.2.
Profinite algebras

In this section we focus on profinite universal algebras; a nice expository paper on the subject is
[
6
]
.

Let
{V}
be an arbitrary variety of universal algebras, regarded as a category with morphisms the homomorphisms. The full subcategory
{V}_{\mathrm{fin}}
of
{V}
defined by the finite algebras is essentially small and has finite limits, which are computed in the category of sets. The same holds for any full subcategory
\C
of
{V}_{\mathrm{fin}}
that is closed under subalgebras and finite products.

Let us fix a full subcategory
\C
of
{V}_{\mathrm{fin}}
closed under subalgebras and finite products. The category
\pro(\C)
can be identified with a full subcategory of the category
{K}_{{V}}
, whose objects are the topological
{V}
-algebras carrying a compact Hausdorff topology and whose morphisms are the continuous homomorphisms.
8
8

8

Under this identification, an algebra in
\C
is regarded as a topological algebra with respect to the discrete topology.
Explicitly, a topological algebra
A\in{K}_{{V}}
belongs to
\pro(\C)
if and only if, whenever
f,g\colon B\to A
are distinct morphisms in
{K}_{{V}}
, there exists a morphism
h\colon A\to C
with
C\in\C
such that
h\circ f\neq h\circ g
. We shall refer to the objects of
\pro(\C)
as
pro-
\C
algebras. If
\C={V}_{\mathrm{fin}}
, these coincide with the usual profinite
{V}
-algebras.

The monomorphisms in
{K}_{{V}}
are precisely the injective maps, i.e. the maps that provide (topological and algebraic) isomorphisms with the image. This holds in
\pro(\C)
as well, because the latter is a reflective subcategory of
{K}_{{V}}
. In particular, the subobjects in
\pro(\C)
can be identified with the closed subalgebras. For the previous assertions, cf.
[
6
]
and the references therein. It follows that
\C
is closed under (strong) subobjects in
\pro(\C)
.

Finally, recall that a universal algebra
A
is said to be
finitely generated
if there exists a finite subset
S\subseteq A
such that the inclusion-smallest subalgebra
\langle S\rangle
of
A
containing
S
is
A
itself. In the setting of topological algebras it is customary to relax the previous condition and say that a topological algebra

is

if there exists a finite subset

such that
\langle S\rangle
is dense in the topology of
A
.
Now, if
A\in\pro(\C)
is topologically finitely generated and

, the set

is finite. Just observe that, if
S\subseteq A
is a finite subset such that
\langle S\rangle
is dense in

, the obvious restriction function

\hom_{\pro(\C)}(A,C)\to C^{S}

is injective because a continuous map into a Hausdorff space is completely determined by its behaviour on any dense subset of its domain.

Hence, the following is an immediate consequence of Corollary
5.14
:

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
{V}
be a variety of universal algebras,
\C\subseteq{V}_{\mathrm{fin}}
a class of finite algebras closed under subalgebras and finite products, and
A,B
two topologically finitely generated pro-
\C

A\cong B
if and only if, for all (discrete) algebras
C\in\C
, the number of continuous homomorphisms
A\to C
is the same as the number of continuous homomorphisms
B\to C
.

The previous result applies, e.g., when
\C
is the class of finite lattices, or finite Heyting algebras, or finite semigroups, and provides a characterisation of the isomorphism relation for (topologically finitely generated) profinite lattices, profinite Heyting algebras, and profinite semigroups, respectively.

Next, we restrict our attention to topologically finitely generated profinite groups, whose topological structure is determined by the algebraic one.

Instantiating Theorem
6.3
with
\C
the class of finite groups, we obtain the following result:
Two topologically finitely generated profinite groups
G,H
are isomorphic (as topological groups) if and only if, for all finite discrete groups
K
, the number of continuous group homomorphisms
G\to K
is the same as the number of continuous group homomorphisms
H\to K
.
9
9

9

This fact was first conjectured by Mima Stanojkovski (private e-mail communication).

In turn, a remarkable result of Nikolov and Segal
[
31
,
32
]
states that the topological structure of a topologically finitely generated profinite group is completely determined by its algebraic structure. More precisely, the subgroups of finite index of a topologically finitely generated profinite group coincide with the open subgroups. (In the special case of pro-
p
-groups, this was proved by Serre in the 1970s, cf.
[
40
, Exercise 6 p. 32]
.) This implies that (i) any group homomorphism from a topologically finitely generated profinite group to a finite discrete group is continuous, and (ii) any two topologically finitely generated profinite groups are isomorphic as topological groups if, and only if, they are isomorphic as abstract groups. The homomorphism counting result in the previous paragraph can then be restated as follows:
Two topologically finitely generated profinite groups
G,H
are isomorphic if and only if, for all finite groups
K
, the number of group homomorphisms
G\to K
is the same as the number of group homomorphisms
H\to K
.

A similar result, whose statement we shall omit, holds for (topologically finitely generated) Abelian profinite groups, also known as
proabelian groups
, by taking as
\C
the class of finite Abelian groups.

For an example where
\C
is a proper subclass of
{V}_{\mathrm{fin}}
, let
\C
consist of the finite
p
-groups. We deduce that:
Two topologically finitely generated pro-
p
-groups
G,H
are isomorphic if and only if, for all finite
p
-groups
K
, the number of group homomorphisms
G\to K
is the same as the number of group homomorphisms
H\to K
.

A typical homomorphism counting result will count the number of morphisms (into, or from a given object) in the monoid
\mathbb{N}
of natural numbers. Now, recall that the proof of Theorem
5.10
(and similarly, the proof of Theorem
4.3
in the locally finite case) consists essentially of two steps. First, using the Stirling kernel construction, we show that the functors
{M}(-,a)
and
{M}(-,b)
are pointwise isomorphic whenever the functors
\hom(-,a)
and

are pointwise isomorphic. Second, Numakura’s Lemma is invoked to show that

if, for all

{M}(c,a)\neq\emptyset\kern 5.0pt\Longleftrightarrow\kern 5.0pt{M}(c,b)\neq\emptyset.

Dispensing with the first step, we obtain a (weaker) result whereby the isomorphism type of an object is determined by counting the number of embeddings (or, dually, quotients) in the two-element monoid

. We state a special case of this result, which generalises a known fact in profinite group theory (cf.
[

Proposition 6.4

Let
{V}
be a variety of universal algebras,
\C\subseteq{V}_{\mathrm{fin}}
a class of finite algebras closed under subalgebras and finite products, and

two topologically finitely generated pro-
\C
algebras. Suppose that, for all (discrete) algebras

, there exists a quotient

10

10

That is, a continuous surjective homomorphism.

A\twoheadrightarrow C

.

6.3.
Finite-variable logics

In this section we assume familiarity with the basic notions of first-order logic and finite model theory. We shall present an application of the results in Section
5
to homomorphism counting in finite model theory; this is the topic of the recent work
[
9
]
. Following
[
9
]
, we apply the framework of
game comonads
in (finite) model theory introduced by Abramsky, Dawar
et al.
in
[
1
,
2
]
.

Let us fix a finite relational signature
\sigma
and a positive integer
k
. We recall from
[
1
]
the

\mathbb{P}_{k}
on the category
{R}(\sigma)
of
\sigma
-structures, which models

-pebble games.

Set
\mathbf{k}\coloneqq\{1,\dots,k\}
. Given a
\sigma
-structure

, we consider the set
{(\mathbf{k}\times A)^{+}}
of

in

, i.e. all non-empty finite sequences of elements of
\mathbf{k}\times A
. A pair
(p,a)\in{\mathbf{k}\times A}
is called a

. Intuitively, the move
(p,a)
corresponds to placing the pebble
p
on the element
a
. Whenever
[(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{l},a_{l})]
is a play,
p_{i}
is called the
pebble index
of the move
(p_{i},a_{i})
. Define the map

\varepsilon_{A}\colon(\mathbf{k}\times A)^{+}\to A,\ \ [(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{l},a_{l})]\mapsto a_{l}

sending a play to the element of
A
in its last move. Let
\mathbb{P}_{k}(A)
be the
\sigma
-structure with universe
(\mathbf{k}\times A)^{+}
and such that, for every
R\in\sigma
of arity
j
, its interpretation
R^{\mathbb{P}_{k}(A)}
consists of the tuples of plays
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{j})
such that:

(i)

The
s_{i}

(ii)

Whenever
s_{i}
is a prefix of
s_{i^{\prime}}
, the pebble index of the last move in
s_{i}
does not appear in the suffix of
s_{i}
in
s_{i^{\prime}}
.

(iii)

{(\varepsilon_{A}(s_{1}),\dots,\varepsilon_{A}(s_{j}))\in R^{A}}
.

This assignment extends to a functor
{R}(\sigma)\to{R}(\sigma)
by setting, for all homomorphisms of
\sigma
-structures

,

\mathbb{P}_{k}(h)\colon\mathbb{P}_{k}(A)\to\mathbb{P}_{k}(B),\ \ [(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{l},a_{l})]\mapsto[(p_{1},h(a_{1})),\dots,(p_{l},h(a_{l}))].

In fact,
\mathbb{P}_{k}
is a comonad on
{R}(\sigma)
when equipped with the counit
\varepsilon

\delta_{A}\colon\mathbb{P}_{k}(A)\to\mathbb{P}_{k}\mathbb{P}_{k}(A)
given by

[(p_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(p_{l},a_{l})]\mapsto[(p_{1},s_{1}),\ldots,(p_{l},s_{l})],

where
s_{i}\coloneqq[(p_{1},a_{1}),\ldots,(p_{i},a_{i})]
for

.
Moreover, it follows directly from
[
1
, Proposition 22]
that a
\sigma
-structure
A
admits a coalgebra structure for
\mathbb{P}_{k}
if, and only if, it has
tree-width
less than
k
(the notion of tree-width for relational structures was introduced in
[
12
]
and generalises the homonymous concept for graphs).

Denote by
\mathcal{L}
(respectively,
\mathcal{L}(\text{\small{$\overset{w.o.}{=}$}})
) the extension of first-order logic (respectively, first-order logic

) obtained by adding

. The

and

are denoted by

and
\mathcal{L}^{k}(\text{\small{$\overset{w.o.}{=}$}})
, respectively.
Further, consider the adjunction

associated with the comonad

. For any two finite
\sigma
-structures

and

we have

i.e.

precisely when

satisfy the same sentences of

[
1

and
[
9
, §VI]
.

It is well known that
{R}(\sigma)
is a locally finitely presentable category, and every finitely presentable
\sigma
-structure is finite. Since we assumed that the signature

is finite, the converse holds as well, and so the finitely generated objects in

coincide with the finitely presentable ones and are precisely the finite

[

.
It follows easily from the criterion in Lemma

in the appendix that

is finitary, and it can be verified that it satisfies the conditions in the definition of comonad of finite rank (cf. also Remark

). As the forgetful functor

is a finite
\sigma
-structure.

Therefore, in view of Corollary
5.15
and Remark

, the following statements are equivalent for all finite
\sigma

:

(1)

.

(2)

For all finite
\sigma
-structures
C
with tree-width less than
k
, the number of homomorphisms
C\to A
is the same as the number of homomorphisms
C\to B
.

To characterise equivalence in the logic
\mathcal{L}^{k}

with equality
, we proceed as follows. Let
\sigma^{\prime}
be the relational signature obtained by adding a binary relation symbol

to
\sigma
. There is an adjunction
{H\dashv J\colon{R}(\sigma)\to{R}(\sigma^{\prime})}

•

For a proof of this fact, see

.

Since the comonad

. It follows from

Thus, Corollary

.

Let

Proper Factorisation Systems

In this section we recall the notion of proper factorisation system in a category
\A

Given arrows
e
and
m
in
\A
, we say that
e
has the
left lifting property
with respect to
m
, or that
m
has the
right lifting property
with respect to
e
, if for every commutative square as on the left-hand side below

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\cdot}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\cdot}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\cdot}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\cdot}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\cdot}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\cdot}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\cdot}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\cdot}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m}

there exists a (not necessarily unique)

, i.e. an arrow

such that the right-hand diagram above commutes. If this is the case, we write

. For any class

of morphisms in
\A

(respectively

) be the class of morphisms having the left (respectively right) lifting property with respect to every morphism in

Definition A.1
.

A pair of classes of morphisms
({Q},{M})
in a category
\A
is a
weak factorisation system
provided it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

Every arrow
f
in
\A
decomposes as
f=m\circ e
with
e\in{Q}
and
m\in{M}
.

(ii)

{Q}={}^{\pitchfork}{M}
and
{M}={Q}^{\pitchfork}
.

A
proper factorisation system
is a weak factorisation system
({Q},{M})
such that all arrows in
{Q}
are epimorphisms and all arrows in
{M}
are monomorphisms.

We refer to
{Q}
-morphisms and
{M}
-morphisms, respectively, as
quotients
(denoted by
\twoheadrightarrow
) and
embeddings
(denoted by
\rightarrowtail
).

It is easy to see that any proper factorisation system is an
orthogonal
factorisation system, meaning that the diagonal fillers are unique. In particular, factorisations are unique up to (unique) isomorphism.

Furthermore, recall that a
strong epimorphism
is an epimorphism that has the left lifting property with respect to all monomorphisms, and a
strong monomorphism
is a monomorphism that has the right lifting property with respect to all epimorphisms. Given any proper factorisation system
({Q},{M})
, the following inclusions are immediate:

\displaystyle\{\text{strong monomorphisms}\}\subseteq{M}\subseteq\{\text{monomorphisms}\},

\displaystyle\{\text{strong epimorphisms}\}\subseteq{Q}\subseteq\{\text{epimorphisms}\}.

Next, we state some well known properties of weak factorisation systems (cf.
[
13
]
or
[

]
):

Lemma A.2
.

Let
({Q},{M})
be a weak factorisation system in
\A
. The following statements hold:

(a)

{Q}
and
{M}
are closed under compositions.

(b)

{Q}\cap{M}=\{\text{isomorphisms}\}
.

(c)

The pullback of an embedding along any morphism, if it exists, is again an embedding.

(d)

The pushout of a quotient along any morphism, if it exists, is again a quotient.

Moreover, if

.

Appendix B
Comonads of Finite Rank

Given a monad on a locally finitely presentable category, we may ask if the associated category of Eilenberg-Moore algebras is locally finitely presentable. The answer is positive, provided the monad is finitary (see e.g.
[
5
, p. 124]
). The analogous question for
comonads
requires more care and leads to the notion of comonad of finite rank (Definition
B.3
below).
In this section we shall collect some basic facts about comonads of finite rank; most of the material presented here can be found in Diers’ monograph
[
10
]
, albeit in a more condensed form (detailed references are provided below).

Let
(T,\delta,\varepsilon)
be a comonad on a category
\A
, with comultiplication
\delta
and counit
\varepsilon
. Recall that an
Eilenberg-Moore coalgebra
for

is a pair

where
a
is an object of
\A
and

is a morphism in

such that the following diagrams commute.

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{a}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T(g)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T(a)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}_{a}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon_{a}}

A morphism of coalgebras
(a,g)\to(b,h)
is an arrow

in
\A
satisfying

. Eilenberg-Moore coalgebras and their morphisms form a category, denoted by

. The forgetful functor

Recall that a functor is
finitary
if it preserves directed colimits. By extension, we say that a comonad is finitary if so is its underlying functor.

The next lemma, which provides sufficient conditions for a comonad to be finitary, is an immediate consequence of a result of Adámek, Milius, Sousa and Wissmann
[
4
, Theorem 3.4]
. Note that item (ii) below is a direct generalisation of the ‘

[

(we are grateful to Samson Abramsky for pointing this out to us).

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\A
be a locally finitely presentable category in which every finitely generated object is finitely presentable, and let
T
be a comonad on
\A
that preserves monomorphisms. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

T
is finitary.

(ii)

For all
a\in\A
, every subobject
b\rightarrowtail T(a)
with
b
finitely presentable factors through the
T
-image of a finitely presentable subobject of
a
.

The following useful fact was proved by Diers in
[
10
, p. 45]
, where some details were omitted; for the sake of completeness, we offer a detailed proof.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
T
be a finitary comonad on a category
\A
, with canonically associated adjunction

. The following statements hold:

(a)

preserves directed colimits.

(b)

preserves and reflects finitely presentable objects.

Proof.

For item (a), let

be an arbitrary directed diagram in

. Since

is finitary and

preserves colimits (as it is left adjoint),

UF(\operatornamewithlimits{colim}_{i\in I}a_{i})\cong\operatornamewithlimits{colim}_{i\in I}UF(a_{i})\cong U(\operatornamewithlimits{colim}_{i\in I}F(a_{i})).

is an isomorphism.

reflects isomorphisms (see e.g.

.

U(a,g)
is the colimit of the diagram
U\circ D\colon\mathbf{EM}(T)_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}(a,g)\to\A
. Further, since
U
preserves finitely presentable objects,
U\circ D
factors as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{EM}(T)_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}(a,g)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\A_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}U(a,g)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U\circ D}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\prime}}

where
D^{\prime}
is the canonical diagram associated with
U(a,g)
and
J
is the functor sending a morphism
(b_{1},h_{1})\to(b_{2},h_{2})
in
\mathbf{EM}(T)_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}(a,g)
to its image under
U
. Because the colimit of
D^{\prime}
is
U(a,g)
, it suffices to show that the functor
J
is cofinal. We prove that the following two properties hold, which in turn imply that
J
is cofinal (cf.
[
7
, Proposition 2.11.2]
):

•

For all
c\in\A_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}U(a,g)
there is
c\to J(d)
with
d\in\mathbf{EM}(T)_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}(a,g)
.

•

For any two morphisms
\beta_{1}\colon c\to J(d_{1})
and
\beta_{2}\colon c\to J(d_{2})
with
c\in\A_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}U(a,g)
and
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbf{EM}(T)_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}(a,g)
, there are
d\in\mathbf{EM}(T)_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}(a,g)
and morphisms
\varphi_{1}\colon d_{1}\to d
,
\varphi_{2}\colon d_{2}\to d
in
\mathbf{EM}(T)_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}(a,g)
satisfying
J(\varphi_{1})\circ\beta_{1}=J(\varphi_{2})\circ\beta_{2}
.

The first property is precisely item (ii) in the definition of a comonad of finite rank.
For the second one, consider any two objects
\gamma_{1}\colon(b_{1},h_{1})\to(a,g)
and
\gamma_{2}\colon(b_{2},h_{2})\to(a,g)
of
\mathbf{EM}(T)_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}(a,g)
, an object
\alpha\colon c\to U(a,g)
of
\A_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}U(a,g)
, and arrows
\beta_{1}\colon c\to U(b_{1},h_{1})
and
\beta_{2}\colon c\to U(b_{2},h_{2})
such that

U(\gamma_{1})\circ\beta_{1}=\alpha=U(\gamma_{2})\circ\beta_{2}.

Since
D
is directed, there exist an object
\gamma\colon(b,h)\to(a,g)
in
\mathbf{EM}(T)_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}(a,g)
and morphisms
\psi_{1}\colon(b_{1},h_{1})\to(b,h)
and
\psi_{2}\colon(b_{2},h_{2})\to(b,h)
in
\mathbf{EM}(T)
such that
\gamma\circ\psi_{1}=\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma\circ\psi_{2}=\gamma_{2}
.
It follows that

U(\gamma)\circ U(\psi_{1})\circ\beta_{1}=U(\gamma_{1})\circ\beta_{1}=U(\gamma_{2})\circ\beta_{2}=U(\gamma)\circ U(\psi_{2})\circ\beta_{2}.

By item (iii) in the definition of a comonad of finite rank, and the fact that
U
reflects finitely presentable objects, there exist
\gamma^{\prime}\colon(b^{\prime},h^{\prime})\to(a,g)
in
\mathbf{EM}(T)_{\mathrm{fp}}{\downarrow}(a,g)
and
\lambda\in\hom_{\mathbf{EM}(T)}((b,h),(b^{\prime},h^{\prime}))
satisfying
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}\circ\lambda
and

U(\lambda)\circ U(\psi_{1})\circ\beta_{1}=U(\lambda)\circ U(\psi_{2})\circ\beta_{2}.

Therefore, the morphisms
\varphi_{1}\coloneqq\lambda\circ\psi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}\coloneqq\lambda\circ\psi_{2}
satisfy
J(\varphi_{1})\circ\beta_{1}=J(\varphi_{2})\circ\beta_{2}
as desired.
∎

Corollary B.6
.

Let
T
be a comonad of finite rank on a locally finitely presentable category
\A
. If every finitely generated object in
\A
is finitely presentable, then the same holds in
\mathbf{EM}(T)
.

Proof.

Suppose that every finitely generated object in
\A
is finitely presentable, and let
x
be an arbitrary finitely generated object of
\mathbf{EM}(T)
. By Theorem
B.5
, the category
\mathbf{EM}(T)
is locally finitely presentable and the forgetful functor
U\colon\mathbf{EM}(T)\to\A
preserves and reflects finitely presentable objects. Thus there is a strong epimorphism
f\colon y\twoheadrightarrow x
in
\mathbf{EM}(T)
with
y
finitely presentable. As left adjoint functors preserve strong epimorphisms (see e.g.
[
7
, Proposition 4.3.9]
),
U(f)
is a strong epimorphism in
\A
. Since
U
preserves finitely presentable objects, it follows that
U(x)
is finitely generated in
\A
and thus finitely presentable. Using the fact that
U
reflects finitely presentable objects, we see that
x
is finitely presentable.
∎

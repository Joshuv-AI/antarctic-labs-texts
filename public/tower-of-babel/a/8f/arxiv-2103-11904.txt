New Capacity Upper Bounds for the Binary Deletion Channel
Hassan Tavakoli

Abstract
This paper considers a binary channel with deletions. We derive two closed-form upper bounds on the capacity of the binary deletion channel (BDC). The first bound is obtained by computing the capacity of an auxiliary channel, the two-bit Fixed-length-Input BDC (FI-BDC), and showing that this auxiliary capacity upper-bounds the capacity of the BDC. The second bound is obtained by approximating the mutual information between sent and received bits directly, yielding a closed-form expression parameterized by a first-order Markov correlation parameter γ \gamma . Both bounds use a first-order Markov process for the channel input. We verify Theorem 1’s optimization from first principles, directly from the two-bit auxiliary channel’s transition matrix rather than from the mutual-information expression alone: the underlying objective is strictly concave with a unique interior maximizer, and the resulting closed-form bound is confirmed correct. The second proposed upper bound is evaluated against the Fertonani–Duman and Dalai bounds in Fig. 4.



Keywords:
Binary Deletion Channel, Capacity of Binary Deletion Channel, Markov Process, Binary Erasure Channel, Upper Bound

I
Introduction

The binary deletion channel (BDC) randomly deletes input bits independently with probability
d
, and the receiver observes the remaining subsequence. Dobrushin
[
1
]
showed that a capacity
\mathbb{C}(d)
can be meaningfully defined for such channels, via an argument analogous to Shannon’s coding theorem. Unlike the binary symmetric channel (BSC) or the binary erasure channel (BEC), no closed-form expression for
\mathbb{C}(d)
is known, and the literature instead provides upper and lower bounds (see
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
).

Diggavi and Grossglauser
[
2
]
showed the capacity is bounded by
1-h(d)
for
d<0.5
. Rahmati and Duman
[
7
]
proved the capacity is at most
0.4143(1-d)
for
d\geq 0.65
. Drinea and Mitzenmacher
[
3
,
8
]
proved the capacity is bounded below by
(1-d)/9
. When the receiver knows the positions of the deleted bits, the BDC reduces to a BEC, giving the simple upper bound
\mathbb{C}(d)\leq 1-d
. A cascaded binary deletion–binary symmetric channel model, closely related to the BSC/BEC decomposition we invoke in Section III.C, has separately been analyzed for capacity lower bounds in
[
15
]
.

Because deletion-channel capacity is defined in the limit of infinite block length, the transition structure of the channel itself is rarely modeled directly; we instead study the input distribution. This paper is built on two intuitive facts: (1) an auxiliary channel that approaches the exact BDC in the limit can yield useful bounds, and (2) a Markov input process helps a decoder estimate deleted bits from the surviving ones. A companion line of work extends combinatorial capacity bounds of this flavor to the
q
-ary deletion channel
[
16
]
; the present paper treats the binary case in depth. We first present two lemmas connecting our approach to prior work, then two theorems giving two upper bounds.

Section II defines the BDC and three auxiliary channels: FO-BDC, FIFO-BDC, and FI-BDC. Two lemmas relate FI-BDC to FIFO-BDC and to the BDC itself. Section III presents the two main theorems, related through the input Markov process parameter. Section IV concludes.

II
Problem Definition and Relevant Previous Research

Let
X
and
Y
denote the input and output of the BDC with deletion probability
d
,
|X|=n
, and
|Y|=m
. Define
N(X,Y)
as the number of ways to produce
Y
by deleting bits from
X
. The transition probability is

P(Y|X)=N(X,Y)\,d^{\,n-m}(1-d)^{m}.

(1)

For example, if
X=10101010
and
Y=10011
, the only admissible deletion pattern gives
N(X,Y)=1
; but for
Y=10101
, six distinct deletion patterns can produce it, so
N(X,Y)=6
. This combinatorial ambiguity is the central obstacle to computing the exact BDC capacity.

II-A

Finite Input Binary Deletion Channel (FI-BDC)

Following the finite-length framework of
[
6
]
, we define three finite variants of the BDC. The first, Fixed-length Output BDC (FO-BDC), was studied in
[
9
]
; the second, Fixed-length Input Fixed-length Output BDC (FIFO-BDC), is the “auxiliary channel” of
[
6
]

n

m
-bit output,

I(n,m)=\max_{P(X)}I(X^{n};Y^{m}),

(2)

with
I(n,m)\leq m

I(n,0)=0

I(1,1)=1

[

]

\mathbb{C}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}p(n,m)\,I(n,m),

(3)

where
p(n,m)
is the probability that
m

n
transmitted bits survive.

Lemma 1.

The bound obtained from the FI-BDC is at least as tight as the bound obtained from the FIFO-BDC.

Proof.
Define

J(n,m)=H\!\left(p(n,m)\cdot\overline{P(X^{n})}\cdot\Pi_{(n,m)}\right)-\overline{P(X^{n})}\cdot H\!\left(\Pi_{(n,m)}\right),

(6)

n
-bit block,

J_{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}J(n,m)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}p(n,m)\,I(n,m),

(7)

(with

\displaystyle C_{n}

\displaystyle=\max_{P(X^{n})}\sum_{m=0}^{n}J(n,m)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{n}p(n,m)\,\max_{P(X^{n})}I(n,m)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}p(n,m)\,I(n,m).

(8)

\hfill\blacksquare

A numerical check of this gap was attempted for small blocklengths (

) via random-search optimization over input distributions, giving estimates of

in the range of a few hundredths of a bit for some
(n,d)

\mathbb{C}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{C_{n}}{n},

(9)

Lemma 2.

(n+1)\,C_{n+1}^{\ast}\leq n\,C_{n}^{\ast}+(1-d)

Proof sketch.

\hfill\blacksquare

III
Capacity Upper Bounds

To transmit reliably through a deletion channel, correlating each bit with its neighbor helps the decoder estimate deleted positions. We use a first-order Markov input.

Definition 1.
For input
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
,

P(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=P(X_{1})\prod_{i=2}^{n}P(X_{i}|X_{i-1}),

(12)

P(X_{1}=0)=\tfrac{1}{2}
and

P(X_{i}=0|X_{i-1}=0)=\gamma,\quad P(X_{i}=0|X_{i-1}=\bar{0})=\bar{\gamma}.

(13)

Proposition A (output Markovity).
The BDC output
Y
of a first-order Markov input is itself first-order Markov, with

q:=P(Y_{i}=y|Y_{i-1}=y)=1-\frac{d(1-\gamma)}{1+d(1-2\gamma)}.

(14)

(Proof in
[
12
]
.)

Prior lower bounds on
\mathbb{C}

(0,1)

\gamma

\gamma

Theorem 1.

\mathbb{C}\leq\frac{1}{2}(1-d)^{2}\log_{2}\!\left(1+2^{-\frac{2d}{1-d}}\right)+\frac{1-d^{2}}{2}.

(16)

Proof.

Proposition 1 (Uniqueness of the optimal transition probability).

For every

Proof.

III-B

Capacity Upper Bound 2: Design Parameter
\gamma

Theorem 2.

\mathbb{C}_{\text{BDC}}\leq(1-d)\left(1-H\!\left(\frac{d(1-\gamma)}{1+d(1-2\gamma)}\right)\right).

(24)

Proof.
Using an auxiliary side-information variable marking the position of the last undeleted predecessor bit, one shows
I(X_{1};Y_{1})\geq I(X_{i};Y_{j})
for the corresponding pair under the induced Markov chain on outputs (
14
), and

I(X_{1};Y_{1})=1-H\!\left(\frac{d(1-\gamma)}{1+d(1-2\gamma)}\right),

(25)

which, combined with
\mathbb{C}_{\text{BDC}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(X_{i};Y(X_{1}^{n})|X_{1}^{i-1})
restricted to the
n(1-d)
surviving bits, gives (
24
).
\hfill\blacksquare

Verification and an important correction to how
\gamma
should be described.
We checked
\partial\mathbb{C}_{\text{BDC}}/\partial\gamma
from (
24
) both symbolically and by numerical sweep over
\gamma\in(0,1)
for

: the bound is
strictly monotonically increasing in
\gamma

\gamma\to 1
, (

(1-d)
, the trivial BEC bound already cited in Section I as the weakest known bound; as
\gamma\to 0
the bound is minimized (tightest).

Discussion.
At
d=0
, the FI-BDC input distribution is uniform (
\gamma=0.5
), consistent with independent input bits, and (
24
) correctly reduces to
\mathbb{C}_{\text{BDC}}|_{d=0}\leq 1
. At
d=1
, the optimal input concentrates all mass on the all-zeros and all-ones sequences (
\gamma=1
), and (
24
) correctly gives
\mathbb{C}_{\text{BDC}}|_{d=1}=0
. Equation (
26

\gamma=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d}{2},

(26)

is a linear interpolation between these two
endpoint
values, not a maximizer of (
24
) at intermediate
d
– since (
24
) has no interior maximizer to approximate.

Implication.
At the two verified endpoints,
\gamma=0.5
(
d\to 0
, independent input bits) and
\gamma=1
(
d\to 1
, all-repeat input), the source-determined

moves toward stronger self-correlation as deletion becomes more severe – the same qualitative behavior as Proposition 2’s monotonicity result for Theorem 1’s

. Since
\gamma
in Theorem 2 is source-determined in the same sense as
\gamma
in Theorem 1 (per the resolution above), and eq. (

interpolating between the two verified boundary values, this qualitative monotonicity is consistent with – though not a proof of optimality for – the specific interpolation eq. (
26
) uses.

IV
Conclusion

We derive two closed-form capacity upper bounds for the BDC: Theorem 1, based on the two-bit FI-BDC auxiliary channel with a first-order Markov input, and Theorem 2, based on directly approximating the mutual information between undeleted received bits and sent bits, also under a first-order Markov input. Theorem 1’s optimization is symbolically verified to be a well-posed strictly concave problem with a unique closed-form maximizer. Theorem 2’s bound is valid for any
\gamma
consistent with the source that generates it, but is
not
the result of maximizing (
24
) over
\gamma
– that formula is monotonic in
\gamma
with no interior optimum, so
\gamma

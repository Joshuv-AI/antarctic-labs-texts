1.
Introduction and main results

In this paper, we introduce and study what we call
variationally
p
-harmonious functions
. Their definition is based on a variational notion of average for functions in a Lebesgue space. In fact, we define a
p
-mean
of a function
v\in L^{p}(E)
as a real number
\nu_{p}[v]

such that

(1.1)

\|v-\nu_{p}[v]\|_{p,E}=\min_{\nu\in\mathbb{R}}\|v-\nu\|_{p,E}.

Here,
L^{p}(E)
denotes the standard Lebesgue space for
1\leq p\leq\infty
(equipped with a suitably normalized norm), where
E
is a Lebesgue measurable set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with finite measure. The existence and uniqueness of
\nu_{p}[v]
is guaranteed for
1<p<\infty
, since it is the projection of
v
on the subspace of constant functions. Also,
\nu_{\infty}[v]
can be explicitly determined. When
p=1
, in order to uniquely determine
\nu_{1}[v]
, we need to assume that
v
is continuous in an open set
E
. In general,
\nu_{p}[v]
is nonlinear in
v
, unless
p
equals
2
, in which case we recover the standard mean value of a function.

If
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we can associate with
\nu_{p}
the operator
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}
that acts on a function
v\in C(\overline{\Omega})
— the space of continuous functions on
\overline{\Omega}
— by the rule

\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v](x)=\nu^{r_{\varepsilon}(x)}_{p}[v](x)\ \mbox{ for }\ x\in\overline{\Omega},

where
\nu^{r}_{p}[v]
denotes the
p
-mean of
v
, when we choose
E
as the ball
B_{r}(x)
, centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and with radius
r>0
, and
r_{\varepsilon}(x)=\min[\varepsilon,\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,\Gamma)]
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. Here,
\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,\Gamma)
denotes the distance of a point
x\in\overline{\Omega}
to the boundary
\Gamma
of
\Omega
(see Section
2.1
for details).
The use of the function
r_{\varepsilon}
is inspired by the work in
[
10
,
11
]
, in which, for the planar case, it is associated to another family of nonlinear means.

By Theorem
2.7
below,
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}
maps the space
C(\overline{\Omega})
into itself.
Thus, it is natural to study the set of functions
v\in C(\Omega)

satisfying the fixed-point equation:

v=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v]\ \mbox{ in }\ \Omega.

We call these functions

variationally
p
-harmonious
in
\Omega
.
Affine functions are
p
-harmonious for any
p\in[1,\infty]
. Classical harmonic functions are
2
-harmonious. For
p\neq 2
,
p
-harmonic functions are not a subset of
p
-harmonious functions. Needless to say, functions that satisfy in
\Omega
the inequalities
v\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v]
or
v\geq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v]
will be named in this paper
variationally
p
-subharmonious
or
p
-superharmonious
.

Harmonious functions have been introduced in
[
16
]
, for the case
p=\infty
, in relationship with the so-called absolutely minimizing Lipschitz extensions. Classes of
p
-harmonious functions for a general
p\in(1,\infty]
have been introduced in
[
21
,
22
]
, and in
[
10
,
11
]
also for the case
p=1
in the plane, in connection with dynamic programming principles associated with
tug-of-war
games (see also
[
24
,
25
]
).
We also refer the reader to
[
1
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
for other related works that motivate these issues.

The classes of
p
-harmonious functions just mentioned are based on a definition of
p
-mean different from that given in (
1.1
). That mean, although defined explicitly, occasionally fails to fulfil certain desired properties, such as monotonicity and continuity. Instead, the
p
-mean defined variationally in (
1.1
), whose study in the context of
p
-harmonious functions was introduced in
[
13
]
, naturally enjoys those properties. In particular, as we shall see in Section
5
, it satisfies the structural requirements, codified in
[
8
]
, which yield the convergence of the underlying dynamic programming principles to
p
-harmonic functions.

In Section
3
, we will show that variationally
p
-harmonious functions satisfy the weak and strong comparison principle, despite they are not solutions of a partial differential equation.
These results are a by-product of the variational definition in (
1.1
) and are corollaries of analogous ones derived in Section
2
for the operator
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}
.

Once these local properties of variationally
p
-harmonious functions have been established, we consider the Dirichlet-type problem of finding a function
u=u^{\varepsilon}
in
C(\overline{\Omega})
such that

(1.2)

u=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u]\ \mbox{ in }\ \Omega,\quad u=g\ \mbox{ on }\ \Gamma.

Here,
g
is any given function in
C(\Gamma)
. In order to obtain existence and uniqueness for this problem, we proceed in the wake of classical Perron’s method for harmonic functions. In fact, we define two classes of continuous functions on
\overline{\Omega}
,
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{g}
, which contain what we call the
variational
p
-subsolutions and
p
-supersolutions
of problem (
1.2
), respectively. Precisely,
v\in{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
(resp.
v\in{\mathcal{S}}^{g}
) if and only if

v\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v]\ \mbox{ (resp. $v\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v]$)}\ \mbox{ in }\ \Omega,\quad v\leq g\ \mbox{ (resp. $v\geq g$)}\ \mbox{ on }\ \Gamma.

The desired solution
u^{\varepsilon}
of (
1.2
) is then obtained by checking that the functions defined by

\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\sup_{v\in S_{g}}v(x),\quad\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\inf_{w\in S^{g}}w(x)\ \mbox{ for }\ x\in\overline{\Omega}

coincide on
\overline{\Omega}
, provided
\Gamma
satisfies some sufficient regularity assumption. Then,
u^{\varepsilon}
can be defined by
u^{\varepsilon}\equiv\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}=\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}
on
\overline{\Omega}
, and the assumed regularity assumptions on
\Gamma
guarantee that
u^{\varepsilon}
satisfies the boundary condition in (
1.2
), for every given
g\in C(\Gamma)
.

In fact, similarly to what done in the classical case, the existence of a solution of (
1.2
) is related to a suitable notion of barrier: given a point
x_{0}\in\Gamma
, a
barrier
at
x_{0}
for the problem (
1.2
)
is a continuous function
w=w^{x_{0}}
on
\overline{\Omega}
, which is variationally
p
-superharmonious in
\Omega
, positive in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{x_{0}\}
, and such that
w(x_{0})=0
. If
x_{0}\in\Gamma
admits a barrier, we say that
x_{0}
is a
regular point
for the Dirichlet problem (
1.2
).
Our existence and uniqueness result then reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(Existence and uniqueness of
p
-harmonious functions)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and take
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
\Omega
contains at least a ball of radius
\varepsilon_{0}
. Then, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, the Dirichlet-type problem (
1.2
) admits a unique solution
u^{\varepsilon}
of class
C(\overline{\Omega})

for every
g\in C(\Gamma)
if and only if all points in
\Gamma
are regular for (
1.2
).

In Corollary
4.4
, as it happens in the classical case, we will show that all points of
\Gamma
are regular if
\Gamma
satisfies the exterior sphere condition.

If we compare Theorem
1.1
to similar results obtained in the literature (see
[
11
,
20
,
22
]
), we can see some differences. Apart for the use of a different notion of
p
-mean,
which is there given by the explicit definition

\eta^{\varepsilon}_{p}[v](x)=\frac{N+2}{N+p}\frac{1}{|B_{\varepsilon}(x)|}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}v\,dy+\frac{p-2}{2\,(N+p)}\left[\max_{\overline{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}v+\min_{\overline{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}v\right]

for
v\in C(\Omega)
,
the role of the operator
\mu^{\varepsilon}_{p}
used in (
1.2
) is replaced in
[
20
,
22
]
by the mapping

C(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\ni v\mapsto\eta^{\varepsilon}_{p}[v]\,\mathcal{X}_{\Omega}+g^{*}\,\mathcal{X}_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}},

where
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
,

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega:\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(y,\Gamma)\leq\varepsilon\}
and
g^{*}
is a continuous extension of the boundary data
g
to
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
. Thus, the solution of the relevant Dirichlet-type problem obtained with these ingredients is in general discontinuous on
\Gamma
, differently from what stated in Theorem
1.1
. On the other hand, the construction of a
p
-harmonious function in those cases is obtained by purely monotonicity arguments, whereas we need the existence of a barrier.

The final purpose of this paper is to show that the operator
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}

gives an approximation scheme for the unique viscosity solution of the Dirichlet problem:

(1.3)

\Delta_{p}^{G}u=0\ \mbox{ in }\ \Omega,\quad u=g\ \mbox{ on }\ \Gamma.

Here,
\Delta_{p}^{G}
denotes

the game theoretic (or homogeneous)
p
-Laplacian
, (formally) defined by

(1.4)

\Delta^{G}_{p}u=\frac{\Delta u}{p}+\frac{p-2}{p}\,\frac{\langle\nabla^{2}u\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle}{|\nabla u|^{2}}.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we have that

\Delta^{G}_{p}u=\frac{1}{p}\,\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)}{|\nabla u|^{p-2}}.

The normalizing factor
p
allows a formal definition in the limiting case
p=\infty
. With this choice,
\Delta^{G}_{2}=\Delta/2
, where
\Delta
is the classical Laplace operator, and

\Delta_{\infty}u=\frac{\langle\nabla^{2}u\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle}{|\nabla u|^{2}}.

We observe that
\Delta_{p}^{G}
is uniformly elliptic. However, it has discontinuous coefficients and is not variational. Thus, the fulfilment of (
1.3
) must be intended in a suitable viscosity sense, which we will detail in Section
5
. Notice that
game-theoretic
p
-harmonic functions
, i.e. the viscosity solutions of the first equation in (
1.3
), have been proved to coincide with
p
-harmonic functions
, i.e. the weak solutions of the
p
-Laplace equation
,
\Delta_{p}u=0
, where

\Delta_{p}u=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u).

(See
[
14
]
.) With these premises, we can state our convergence result.

Theorem 1.2

(Limits of
p
-harmonious functions)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain containing a ball of radius
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
Suppose that there exists
\varepsilon_{\Gamma}\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
such that,
for every
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{\Gamma}
,
\Gamma
is made of regular points for the problem (
1.2
).

For
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{\Gamma}
, let
u^{\varepsilon}
be the unique solution in
C(\overline{\Omega})
of (
1.2
). Then, for every
g\in C(\Gamma)
, there exists
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
such that

u^{\varepsilon}
converges to
u
uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
u
is the unique viscosity solution of (
1.3
).

Results similar to this theorem have been obtained in
[
8
,
20
,
22
]
, based on the
p
-mean
\eta_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v].
In particular, this theorem was expected to hold
since the average
\nu_{p}
enjoys all the structural assumptions,
proposed in
[
8
]
, for the convergence of the underlying dinamic programming principle. Those assumptions are additivity with constants,
1
-homogeneity and monotonicity for essentially bounded functions (see Section
2.1
).
However, Theorem
1.2
cannot be proved as a direct application of the general result contained in
[
8
]
, since the relevant definitions of
p
-harmonious functions are different.
Therefore, we adapt the argument used in
[
8
]
to the case of the operator
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}
considered in this paper. We succeed in this goal with the help of an argument established in
[
5
]
and the proof of an asymptotic mean value property for
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}
, which characterizes game-theoretic
p
-harmonic functions. This is based on the formula

(1.5)

\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[\phi](x)=\phi(x)+\frac{p}{2(N+p)}\Delta_{p}^{G}\phi(x)\,\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2})\ \mbox{ as }\ \varepsilon\to 0,

which holds for any
x\in\Omega
and any
\phi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
with
\nabla\phi(x)\neq 0
.
The formula (
1.5
) is obtained by adapting
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
.

As a final remark, we comment on the relationship between
the barriers for (
1.2
) and those for (
1.3
).
By combining Theorem
1.1

with Theorem
1.2
, we can observe that
the existence of a barrier for (
1.2
) for any
\varepsilon
implicitly
shows the existence of a barrier also for (
1.3
)
since the solvability of (
1.2
) or (
1.3
)
is equivalent to the existence of a barrier in the corresponding sense
together with the fact that the solvability of (
1.2
)
for any
\varepsilon
implies the solvability of (
1.3
).

We summarize the contents of this paper. In Section
2
, we present some old and new properties on the mean
\nu_{p}
such as
continuity and monotonicity of the operator
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}
. In Section
3
, we introduce what we call variationally
p
-harmonious functions and show that they satisfy weak and strong comparison principles.
We shall prove existence and uniqueness for the Dirichlet problem for variationally
p
-harmonious functions in Section
4
. Finally, in Section
5
, we will show that limits of variationally
p
-harmonious functions are
p
-harmonic in a viscosity sense.

2.
The
p
-mean and the operator
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}

In this section, we collect some properties of the
p
-means that are instrumental in the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

2.1.
Definition of
p
-mean and general properties

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a measurable set of finite measure.
For a measurable function
v:E\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, we shall use the notation:

\displaystyle\|v\|_{p,E}=\left(\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}|v|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\ \mbox{ for }\ 1\leq p<\infty\ \mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\infty,E}=\inf\{t\geq 0:|\{x\in E:|v(x)|>t\}|\},

whenever these quantities are finite.
Since
E
has finite measure, we can (and will) always assume that
|E|=1
, without loss of generality.

Let
v\in L^{p}(E)
. We define a
p
-mean
of
v
as a real number
\nu_{p}[v]

such that

\|v-\nu_{p}[v]\|_{p,E}=\min_{\nu\in\mathbb{R}}\|v-\nu\|_{p,E}.

As shown in
[
13
]
,
\nu_{p}[v]
is uniquely defined for
1<p\leq\infty
and is a natural generalization of the classical mean value of
v
. In fact,
\nu_{2}[v]
is the average of
v
on
E
. We also know that
\nu_{1}[v]
may not be unique. For instance, if
E=[-2,2]
and
v=\mathcal{X}_{[-1,1]}
, then any number
\nu
in
[0,1]
fulfils the definition when
p=1
.
However,
\nu_{1}[v]
is uniquely defined if
v\in L^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega)
, when
\Omega
is an open domain of finite measure in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(see
[
23
]
). For the sake of brevity, from now on, in the case
p=1
we shall assume that
v\in L^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega)
.

It is useful to know that
\nu_{p}[v]
is the unique root of the equation

(2.1)

\int_{E}|v-\nu|^{p-2}[v-\nu]\,dy=0,

for
1\leq p<\infty
, and

\nu_{\infty}[v]=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mathop{\mathrm{esssup}}_{E}v+\mathop{\mathrm{essinf}}_{E}v\right\}

(see
[
13
]
).
For
p=1
, we mean that
|t|^{p-2}t=\mathrm{sgn}(t)
, which equals
1
if
t>0
,
-1
if
t<0
, and
0
if
t=0
.

Remark 2.1
.

(i) It can be proved that
\nu_{p}[v]\to\nu_{\infty}[v]
as
p\to\infty
. This fact can be obtained by taking the limit as
p\to\infty
in the equality

\left\{\int_{E_{p}^{+}}(v-\nu_{p}[v])^{p-1}dy\right\}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=\left\{\int_{E_{p}^{-}}(\nu_{p}[v]-v)^{p-1}dy\right\}^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

that follows from (
2.1
). Here,
E_{p}^{\pm}=\bigl\{y\in E:v\gtreqless\nu_{p}[v]\bigr\}
.

(ii)
Notice that while
\nu=\nu_{p}[v]
is uniquely defined for any function
v
in
L^{p}(E)
with
1<p<\infty
, still, its characterization as solution of (
2.1
) should be analyzed in dependence on the values of
p
. In fact, notice that the integral

\int_{E}|v-\nu|^{p-2}\,dy

is always finite for
2\leq p<\infty
, but may not be so for
1<p<2
.
Thus, while for
2\leq p<\infty
the mean
\nu_{p}[v]
can also be characterized as

\nu_{p}[v]=\frac{\int_{E}|v-\nu_{p}[v]|^{p-2}v\,dy}{\int_{E}|v-\nu_{p}[v]|^{p-2}\,dy}

(unless
v
is a.e. constant on
E
), such a characterization may fail to be true for
1<p<2
, unless we extend it by setting
\nu_{p}[v]=0
, if the denominator is infinite.

For instance, if
E
is the unit disk
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
v(x,y)=x^{3}-y^{3}
, by uniqueness we have that
\nu_{p}[v]=0
, since (
2.1
) is satisfied by this value for any
1<p<\infty
. Nevertheless, we have that

\int_{B}|v|^{p-2}\,dy=\infty\ \mbox{ and }\ \int_{B}|v|^{p-2}v\,dy=0\ \mbox{ for }\ 1<p<\frac{4}{3}.

Elementary properties of
\nu_{p}[v]
are the following (see
[
13
, Proposition 2.7 and Proposition 2.5]
): for every function
v\in L^{p}(E)
, it holds that

(a)

(additivity with constants)

\nu_{p}[v+c]=\nu_{p}[v]+c
for every
c\in\mathbb{R}
;

(b)

(homogeneity)

\nu_{p}[\alpha v]=\alpha\nu_{p}[v]
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
;

(c)

(monotonicity)

\nu_{p}[v_{1}]\leq\nu_{p}[v_{2}]
if
v_{1}\leq v_{2}
a.e. in
E
.

For our purposes in this paper, we need to show that
\nu_{p}
is
strictly monotonic
.

Lemma 2.2

(Strict monotonicity)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a measurable set with finite measure.
Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in L^{p}(E)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and assume that
v_{1}\leq v_{2}
a.e. in
E

and that the measure of the set

E^{\sharp}=\{x\in E:\,v_{1}(x)<v_{2}(x)\}
is positive.
Then it holds that
\nu_{p}[v_{1}]<\nu_{p}[v_{2}]
.
In particular, if
v_{1}\leq v_{2}
a.e. in
E

and
\nu_{p}[v_{1}]=\nu_{p}[v_{2}]
, we have that
v_{1}=v_{2}
a.e. in
E
.

Proof.

For
p=\infty
, our claim follows from an inspection. If
1\leq p<\infty
, the assumptions on
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
and the fact that
the function
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto|t-\nu|^{p-2}(t-\nu)
is strictly increasing
give that

\int_{E^{\sharp}}|v_{1}-\nu|^{p-2}(v_{1}-\nu)dy<\int_{E^{\sharp}}|v_{2}-\nu|^{p-2}(v_{2}-\nu)dy

for every
\nu
. Thus, the same holds with
E^{\sharp}
replaced by
E
, and our claims follow from the characterization (
2.1
).
∎

In
[
13
, Theorem 2.4]
, we proved the continuity of the operator
\nu_{p}
.
That result can be relaxed.

Lemma 2.3

(Continuity)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a measurable set with finite measure and fix
1<p<\infty
. Let
v\in L^{p}(E)
and let
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of measurable functions that converges to
v
a.e. in
E
.

Suppose that either

(i)

\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is increasing and
v_{n}\geq 0
on
E
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, or

(ii)

there exists a function
w\in L^{p}(E)
such that
|v_{n}|\leq w
a.e. in
E
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then
\nu_{p}[v_{n}]\to\nu_{p}[v]
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

(i) Set
\nu_{n}=\nu_{p}[v_{n}]
and
\nu=\nu_{p}[v]
. The sequence of numbers
\nu_{n}
converges or diverges to
+\infty
, since it increases. Let
\overline{\nu}
be its limit. Since
v_{n}\leq v
, we have that
\nu_{n}\leq\nu
and hence
\overline{\nu}\leq\nu
. Now, observe that

0=\int_{E}|v_{n}-\nu_{n}|^{p-2}(v_{n}-\nu_{n})\,dy\geq\int_{E}|v_{n}-\overline{\nu}|^{p-2}(v_{n}-\overline{\nu})\,dy,

since the function
\mathbb{R}\ni s\mapsto|t-s|^{p-2}(t-s)
is decreasing and
\nu_{n}\leq\overline{\nu}
.
The integrand at right-hand side of the last inequality is bounded from below by the number
-|\overline{\nu}|^{p-2}\overline{\nu}
. We thus can apply the Monotone Convergence Theorem and pass to the limit to obtain that

\int_{E}|v-\nu|^{p-2}(v-\nu)\,dy=0\geq\int_{E}|v-\overline{\nu}|^{p-2}(v-\overline{\nu})\,dy.

This inequality gives that
\overline{\nu}\geq\nu
, and the proof is complete.

(ii)
The assumptions in (ii) give that
v_{n}\to v
in
L^{p}(E)
by the Dominated Convergence Theorem. Our claim then follows from
[
13
, Theorem 2.4]
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

When
p=\infty
, we obtain the convergence
\nu_{\infty}(v_{n})\to\nu_{\infty}(v)
as
n\to\infty
, if either
v_{n}\to v
uniformly or, in the case (i), if
E
is a bounded open set and
v_{n},v\in C(E)
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In fact, in this case the monotonicity of the sequence gives its uniform convergence, by Dini’s monotone convergence theorem.

When
p=1
, in order to obtain the desired convergence, we must require that
v_{n},v\in L^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega)
, where
\Omega
is an open domain of finite measure.

2.2.
The operator
\mu^{\varepsilon}_{p}
and its properties

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set. Let
B_{r}(x)
be a ball of radius
r
, centered at
x
and contained in
\Omega
. We let
\nu^{r}_{p}[v](x)
be the
p
-mean of
v
relative to the set
E=B_{r}(x)
. In order to well-define
\nu^{r}_{p}[v](x)
, we shall always assume that

v\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for
1<p\leq\infty
and
v\in C(\Omega)\cap L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
when
p=1
.

Next, for each fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we set
r_{\varepsilon}(x)=\min[\varepsilon,\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,\Gamma)]
and define:

\mu^{\varepsilon}_{p}[v](x)=\nu^{r_{\varepsilon}(x)}_{p}[v](x)\ \mbox{ for every }\ x\in\Omega.

We stress the fact that, differently from
\nu^{r}_{p}[v](x)
that can be defined if
x
is far enough from
\Gamma
,
\mu^{\varepsilon}_{p}[v](x)
is well-defined for any
x\in\Omega
. Notice that, from (
2.1
), we easily infer that
\nu^{r}_{p}[v](x)
is the unique root
\nu
of the equation

(2.2)

\int_{B}|v(x+rz)-\nu|^{p-2}[v(x+rz)-\nu]\,dz=0

for
1\leq p<\infty
, and

(2.3)

\nu_{\infty}^{r}[v](x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\operatorname{esssup}_{z\in B}v(x+rz)+\operatorname{essinf}_{z\in B}v(x+rz)\right),

where
B
denotes the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
centered at the origin.
These facts tell us that we can assume that
\mu^{\varepsilon}_{p}[v]=v
on
\Gamma
, at least formally. In fact, for
x\in\Gamma
,
r_{\varepsilon}(x)=0
and (
2.2
)-(
2.3
) give that
\nu=v(x)
when
r=0
. This issue will be discussed in more detail in Theorem
2.7
below.

The following result extends a formula obtained in
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and let
\phi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
. Then, for every bounded open set
A
with
\overline{A}\subset\Omega
and such that
\nabla\phi\neq 0
on
\overline{A}
, it holds that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[\phi]-\phi}{r_{\varepsilon}^{2}}=\frac{p}{2(N+p)}\,\Delta^{G}_{p}\phi\ \mbox{ uniformly on }\ \overline{A}.

Proof.

Let
x_{\varepsilon}
a point in
\overline{A}
maximizing the difference

\left|\frac{\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[\phi]-\phi}{r_{\varepsilon}^{2}}-\frac{p}{2(N+p)}\,\Delta^{G}_{p}\phi\right|

on
\overline{A}
. Up to a subsequence, we have that
x_{\varepsilon}\to x
for some
x\in\overline{A}
, and hence
r_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. The conclusion then follows from
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
.
∎

The following claim is a straightforward consequence of Lemma
2.2
.

Corollary 2.6

(Monotonicity)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a domain of finite measure. Let
v\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
v\in L^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega)
for
p=1
.
Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
be such that
v_{1}\leq v_{2}
a.e. in
\Omega
. Then
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v_{1}]\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v_{2}]
on
\overline{\Omega}
. Moreover, we have that
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v_{1}](x)<\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v_{2}](x)
whenever the set

\{y\in B_{r_{\varepsilon}(x)}(x):v_{1}(y)<v_{2}(y)\}

has positive measure.

Next, we show that
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}
acts quite naturally on
\mathrm{USC}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\mathrm{LSC}(\overline{\Omega})
,
the classes of functions that are upper and lower semicontinuous on
\overline{\Omega}
, respectively. We state our result for the case of lower semicontinuous functions. The remaining case will easily follow.

Theorem 2.7

(Invariance semicontinuity property)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded open set.
If
v\in\mathrm{LSC}(\overline{\Omega})
, then we have that
\mu^{\varepsilon}_{p}[v]\in\mathrm{LSC}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We first prove that
\mu^{\varepsilon}_{p}[v]\in C(\Omega)
. We take a point
x\in\Omega
and any sequence of points
x_{n}\in\Omega
converging to
x
. Then, we observe that

\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v](x)=\mu_{p}^{1}[w^{x}](0),

where
w^{x}:B\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(2.4)

w^{x}(z)=v(x+r_{\varepsilon}(x)z)\ \text{ for }\ z\in B.

In other words,
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v](x)=\nu_{p}[w^{x}]
, when we choose
E
to be the unit ball
B
.

Being as
v\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and
r_{\varepsilon}
continuous, we infer that
w^{x_{n}}\to w^{x}
in
L^{p}(B)
as
n\to\infty
. Thus,
[
13
, Theorem 2.4]
gives that
\nu_{p}[w^{x_{n}}]\to\nu_{p}[w^{x}]
, and hence we conclude that

\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v](x_{n})\to\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v](x)
.

We are left to prove the desired semicontinuity at points in
\Gamma
. Thus, we take any sequence of points
x_{n}\in\overline{\Omega}
converging to some point
x\in\Gamma
.
Observe that, since
\overline{\Omega}
is compact,
v
admits its minimum
m>-\infty
on it. Next, we proceed as before and consider the function
w^{x_{n}}
in (
2.4
). This is well-defined at points
x_{n}\in\Omega
. At points
x_{n}\in\Gamma
, instead, we consistently mean that
w^{x_{n}}=v(x_{n})
. Since
\nu_{p}[w^{x_{n}}]=\nu_{p}[w^{x_{n}}-m]+m
and
w^{x_{n}}\geq m
, we can always suppose that
w^{x_{n}}\geq 0
.
By the semicontinuity of
v
and the continuity of
r_{\varepsilon}
, we know that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}w^{x_{n}}(z)\geq w^{x}(z)\ \mbox{ for any }\ z\in B.

Now, we have that

\inf_{k\geq n}\left(w^{x_{k}}\right)\to\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(w^{x_{n}}\right)\ \mbox{ on $B$ as }\ n\to\infty,

and the convergence is monotone increasing.
Thus, we can apply Lemma
2.3
(i) to obtain that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu_{p}\!\left[\inf_{k\geq n}\left(w^{x_{k}}\right)\right]=\nu_{p}\!\left[\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(w^{x_{n}}\right)\right].

Notice that this holds even if there are infinitely many terms
x_{n}
that belong to
\Gamma
.

Therefore, we conclude that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v](x_{n})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\nu_{p}[w^{x_{n}}]\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\nu_{p}\!\left[\inf_{k\geq n}\left(w^{x_{k}}\right)\right]=\\
\nu_{p}\!\left[\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(w^{x_{n}}\right)\right]\geq\nu_{p}[w^{x}]=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v](x).

This gives that
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v]
is lower semicontinuous on
\overline{\Omega}
.
∎

3.
Variationally
p
-harmonious functions

Slightly differently from what done in
[
8
,
22
]
, we give the following definitions.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain. Set
1\leq p\leq\infty
and fix
\varepsilon>0
such that
\Omega
contains at least one ball of radius
\varepsilon
. We say that
v\in L^{p}(\Omega)
is a
variationally
p
-subharmonious (or variationally
p
-superharmonious)
function in
\Omega
if

v(x)\leq\mu^{\varepsilon}_{p}[v](x)\quad\mbox{(or $v(x)\geq\mu^{\varepsilon}_{p}[v](x)$) \ for almost every \ $x\in\Omega$.}

A
variationally
p
-harmonious
function is both variationally
p
-subharmonious and
p
-superharmonious. We stress the fact that the term “variationally” refers to the variational definition of the operator
\mu^{\varepsilon}_{p}
.

Example 3.1
.

(i) It is easily seen that constant functions are variationally
p
-harmonious for any
p\in[1,\infty]
.

(ii) For
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, let
a(y)=\langle\xi,y\rangle+c
be an affine function. We have that
a(y)=a_{0}(y)+\langle\xi,x\rangle+c
, where
a_{0}(y)=\langle\xi,y-x\rangle
and
\langle\xi,x\rangle+c
is constant. It is easy to infer that
\nu_{p}^{r}[a_{0}](x)=0
, by the central symmetry of
a_{0}
with respect to
x
. Thus, we conclude that

\nu_{p}^{r}[a](x)=\nu_{p}^{r}[a_{0}](x)+\nu_{p}^{r}[\langle\xi,x\rangle+c](x)=\langle\xi,x\rangle+c=a(x).

It is then clear that
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[a]=a
.

Therefore, we infer that affine functions are always variationally
p
-harmonious. It is then evident that convex and concave functions are variationally
p
-subharmonious and
p
-superharmonious, respectively.

(iii) Fix an
\alpha>0
and set
\gamma_{\alpha}(y)=|y|^{-\alpha}
for
y\neq 0
. Clearly, we have that
\gamma_{\alpha}\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if
\alpha p<N
and
\gamma_{\alpha}\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
for any
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Next, let

\Delta_{p}^{G}\phi=\frac{1}{p}\,\left\{\Delta\phi+(p-2)\,\frac{\langle\nabla^{2}\phi\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi\rangle}{|\nabla\phi|^{2}}\right\}

denote the
game-theoretic
p
-laplacian
of a smooth function away from its critical points.
We easily compute that

\Delta^{G}_{p}\gamma_{\alpha}(y)=\frac{\alpha\,[\alpha(p-1)+p-N]}{p}\,|y|^{-(\alpha+2)}\ \mbox{ for }\ y\neq 0.

By applying
[
13
, formula (3.6) in Theorem 3.2]
, for any
x\neq 0
, we obtain that

\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}(x)-\nu^{r}_{p}[\gamma_{\alpha}](x)}{r^{2}}=-\frac{\alpha[\alpha(p-1)+p-N]}{2(N+p)}\,|x|^{-(\alpha+2)}+o(1)

as
r\to 0
.

Now, let
\Omega
be a bounded open set such that
\overline{\Omega}
does not contain the origin.
Depending on the sign of
\alpha(p-1)+p-N
, there exists
r_{\Omega}>0
such that the inequalities
\gamma_{\alpha}\geq\nu_{p}^{r}[\gamma_{\alpha}]
or
\gamma_{\alpha}\leq\nu^{r}_{p}[\gamma_{\alpha}]
are uniformly satisfied in
\overline{\Omega}
for
0<r<r_{\Omega}
.
Therefore, for
0<r<r_{\Omega}
,
\gamma_{\alpha}
is variationally
p
-subharmonious in
\Omega
, if

p>\frac{\alpha+N}{\alpha+1},

and variationally
p
-superharmonious in
\Omega
, if

p<\frac{\alpha+N}{\alpha+1}.

3.1.
Comparison principles

Despite they are not solutions of any partial differential equation, variationally
p
-harmonious functions satisfy comparison principles.

Theorem 3.1

(Weak comparison principle)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded open set. Let
v\in\mathrm{USC}(\overline{\Omega})
and
w\in\mathrm{LSC}(\overline{\Omega})
be
p
-subharmonious and
p
-superharmonious in
\Omega
, respectively.

If
v\leq w
on
\Gamma
, then
v\leq w
on
\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction. We have that
v-w\in\mathrm{USC}(\overline{\Omega})
, and hence it attains its maximum
M
on
\overline{\Omega}
. Suppose that
M

is positive. Since
v\leq w
on
\Gamma
, the set

A=\{x\in\Omega:v(x)-w(x)=M\}
contains at least one point
x_{0}
. Let
\Omega^{\prime}
be the connected component of
\Omega
that contains
x_{0}
. The set
A^{\prime}=\{x\in\Omega^{\prime}:v(x)-w(x)=M\}
is thus non-empty and also closed in
\Omega^{\prime}
, being the pre-image of the closed set
\{M\}\subset\mathbb{R}
under the continuous function
v-w:\Omega^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
.

To show that
A^{\prime}
is open, we take any
x\in A^{\prime}
and observe that

\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v-M](x)\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[w](x)\leq w(x)

since
v-M\leq w
in
\Omega
by construction and
w
is
p
-superharmonious. Thus, we infer that

w(x)\geq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v-M](x)=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v](x)-M\geq v(x)-M=w(x)

since
v
is
p
-subharmonious.
In particular,
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[w](x)=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v-M](x)
,
which gives that
w\equiv v-M
on
B_{r_{\varepsilon}(x)}(x)
, by Lemma
2.2
. In other words,
we have that
B_{r_{\varepsilon}(x)}(x)
is contained in
A^{\prime}
. Thus, we have proved that
A^{\prime}
is also open and, since
\Omega^{\prime}
is connected, we have that
A^{\prime}=\Omega^{\prime}
. Thus,
v-w\equiv M
on
\Omega^{\prime}
and, by continuity, we infer that
M\equiv v-w
on the boundary of
\Omega^{\prime}
, which is contained in
\Gamma
. Therefore, we reach the contradiction
0<M\leq 0
.
∎

By more or less the same arguments, we can prove the following strong comparison principle.

Corollary 3.2

(Strong comparison principle)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain. Let
v,w\in C(\Omega)
be
p
-subharmonious and
p
-superharmonious in
\Omega
, respectively.

Suppose that
v\leq w
in
\Omega
. Then, either
v<w
in
\Omega
or, else,
v\equiv w
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Suppose that
v-w=0
at some point in
\Omega
. Then, we repeat the same argument in the proof of Theorem
3.1
with
A^{\prime}=\{x\in\Omega:v(x)-w(x)=0\}
and
M=0
. We thus infer that
A^{\prime}
is non-empty, closed, and open. Since
\Omega
is connected, we then obtain that
A^{\prime}=\Omega
, and hence
v\equiv w
in
\Omega
.
∎

4.
Existence of
p
-harmonious functions

In this section, we shall prove Theorem
1.1
. We start with some definitions.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with boundary
\Gamma
and let
g:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. For a fixed number
\varepsilon>0
, we introduce two classes of functions:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{g}=\{v\in C(\overline{\Omega}):\,v\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v]\ \mbox{ in }\ \Omega,\ v\leq g\ \mbox{ on }\ \Gamma\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{g}=\{v\in C(\overline{\Omega}):\,v\geq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v]\ \mbox{ in }\ \Omega,\ v\geq g\ \mbox{ on }\ \Gamma\}.

It is clear that
{\mathcal{S}}^{g}=-{\mathcal{S}}_{-g}
. The elements of
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{g}
will be called
variational
p
-subsolutions
and, respectively,
p
-supersolutions
of (
1.2
). These classes are non-empty since they contain the constant functions on
\overline{\Omega}
defined by

v\equiv\min_{\Gamma}g\ \mbox{ and }\ w\equiv\max_{\Gamma}g,

respectively.

Our aim is to obtain a solution of (
1.2
) as an envelope of functions in
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
or in
{\mathcal{S}}^{g}
. In fact, we define the
lower and upper Perron solutions

\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}
and
\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}
of (
1.2
) by

\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\sup_{v\in{\mathcal{S}}_{g}}v(x)\ \mbox{ and }\ \overline{u}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\inf_{w\in{\mathcal{S}}^{g}}w(x)\ \mbox{ for }\ x\in\overline{\Omega}.

The following lemma collects some elementary properties of these functions.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and let
g\in C(\Gamma)
. Then,

(i)

it holds that

\min_{\Gamma}g\leq\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}\leq\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}\leq\max_{\Gamma}g\ \mbox{ on }\ \overline{\Omega};

(ii)

u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
is a solution of (
1.2
)
if and only if
u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{g}\cap{\mathcal{S}}^{g}
;

(iii)

it holds that
u=\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}=\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}
on
\overline{\Omega}
, if
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
is a solution of (
1.2
).

Proof.

(i) By the weak comparison principle (Theorem
3.1
), we have that
v\leq w
on
\overline{\Omega}
for every
v\in{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
and
w\in{\mathcal{S}}^{g}
, being as
v\leq g\leq w
on
\Gamma
. Thus,
\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}\leq\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}
, clearly, by the properties of supremum and infimum. The other two inequalities easily follow from the fact that the two relevant constant functions belong to the classes
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
or
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
.

(ii)
The statement is trivial by the definitions of
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{g}
.

(iii) If
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
is a solution of (
1.2
), then (ii) gives that

u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{g}\cap{\mathcal{S}}^{g}
, and hence
\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}\leq u\leq\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}\leq\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}
on
\overline{\Omega}
.
∎

In analogy with the classical case and as already mentioned in the introduction, we shall say that
a function
w
is a
barrier
at a point
x_{0}\in\Gamma
for the Dirichlet-type problem (
1.2
) if

w\in C(\overline{\Omega})
,

w
is superharmonious in
\Omega
,
and

w>0
in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{x_{0}\}
with
w(x_{0})=0
.
If
x_{0}\in\Gamma
admits a barrier, we say that
x_{0}
is a
regular point
for (
1.2
).

Proposition 4.2
.

If
x_{0}\in\Gamma
is a regular point for
(
1.2
)
,
then

\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}(x_{0})=\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}(x_{0})=g(x_{0}).

Proof.

The proof proceeds as in the classical case. Let
\eta>0
. Since
g\in C(\Gamma)
, we can find
\delta>0
such that

|g-g(x_{0})|<\eta
on
\Gamma\cap B_{\delta}(x_{0})
. Also, if
w
is a barrier at
x_{0}
,
the following number is well-defined:

M_{\delta}=\max_{\Gamma\setminus B_{\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{|g-g(x_{0})|}{w}.

Thus, we infer that

|g-g(x_{0})|\leq\eta+M_{\delta}\,w\ \mbox{ on }\ \Gamma.

This fact gives that the functions
g(x_{0})+\eta+M_{\delta}\,w
and
g(x_{0})-\eta-M_{\delta}\,w
belong to the classes
{\mathcal{S}}^{g}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
, respectively. Hence, by definition

g(x_{0})-\eta-M_{\delta}\,w\leq\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}\leq\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}\leq g(x_{0})+\eta+M_{\delta}\,w\ \mbox{ on }\ \overline{\Omega}

and, in particular

g(x_{0})-\eta\leq\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\leq\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\leq g(x_{0})+\eta,

being as
w(x_{0})=0
. The desired conclusion then ensues, since
\eta>0
is arbitrary.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

(i) Let us assume that every point in
\Gamma
admits a barrier for (
1.2
).
Lemma
4.1
and the definition of
\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}
give that
\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}
is bounded and lower semicontinuous on
\overline{\Omega}
, that is
\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\cap\mathrm{LSC}(\overline{\Omega})
.
The assumption also gives that
\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}=g
on
\Gamma
, thanks to Proposition
4.2
.

Next, we define a sequence of functions by the following iterative scheme:

u_{1}=\underline{u}^{\varepsilon},\quad u_{j+1}=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u_{j}]\ \mbox{ on }\ \overline{\Omega},\mbox{ for }j\geq 1.

Proposition
2.7
ensures that all the functions
u_{j}
belong to
L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\cap\mathrm{LSC}(\overline{\Omega})
.
Clearly, it holds that
u_{j}=g
on
\Gamma
for every
j\geq 1
.

Now, since
v\leq\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}
on
\overline{\Omega}
for every
v\in{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
,
we have that
v\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[v]\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}]
in
\Omega

for every
v\in{\mathcal{S}}_{g}
, which gives
\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}]
in
\Omega
. Hence, we can infer that
u_{1}\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u_{1}]=u_{2}
on
\overline{\Omega}
.
Then by monotonicity, we have that
u_{2}=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u_{1}]\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u_{2}]=u_{3}
and, by iterating,
we obtain that
u_{j}\leq u_{j+1}
on
\overline{\Omega}
for every
j\geq 1
.
Thus, the sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is increasing on
\overline{\Omega}
. By iteration, we also have that

\min_{\Gamma}g\leq u_{j}\leq\max_{\Gamma}g\ \mbox{ on }\ \overline{\Omega},\ \mbox{ for every }\ j\geq 1,

because, by Lemma
4.1
(i), we see that

\min_{\Gamma}g\leq u_{1}=\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}\leq\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}\leq\max_{\Gamma}g\ \mbox{ on }\overline{\Omega}.

Thus, the sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges pointwise on
\overline{\Omega}
to a function
u
, which is lower semicontinuous on
\overline{\Omega}
and such that

\min_{\Gamma}g\leq u\leq\max_{\Gamma}g\ \mbox{ on }\overline{\Omega}\ \mbox{ and }\ u=g\ \mbox{ on }\ \Gamma.

Moreover,
applying Lemma
2.3
gives that

\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u_{j}]\to\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u]
as
j\to\infty
in
\Omega
,
and hence
u=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u]
in
\Omega
, since
u_{j}=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u_{j}]
in
\Omega
, for every
j\geq 1
. As a result, the function
u
satisfies the Dirichlet problem (
1.2
).

By playing on the fact that
{\mathcal{S}}^{g}=-{\mathcal{S}}_{-g}
, we can determine a decreasing sequence of functions
U_{j}
, bounded and upper semicontinuous on
\overline{\Omega}
, which converge to another solution
U
of (
1.2
). Clearly, such a sequence is initialized by choosing
U_{1}=\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}
, so that, by comparison, the relevant iterating schemes tell us that
U\geq u
on
\overline{\Omega}
, being as
U_{1}=\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}\geq\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}=u_{1}
on
\overline{\Omega}
.

Thus, we are left to prove that
u=U
in
\overline{\Omega}
. Notice first that
U-u
is non-negative and belongs to
\mathrm{USC}(\overline{\Omega})
, being as
U\in\mathrm{USC}(\overline{\Omega})
and
u\in\mathrm{LSC}(\overline{\Omega})
.
Hence, there exists
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
such that

\max_{\overline{\Omega}}(U-u)=(U-u)(x_{0}).

Our goal is to prove that
(U-u)(x_{0})=0
.

By contradiction, we assume that
(U-u)(x_{0})>0
and consider the set

\Omega^{\sharp}=\bigl\{x\in\overline{\Omega}:(U-u)(x)=(U-u)(x_{0})>0\bigr\}.

Since
U-u=g-g=0
on
\Gamma
, we have that
\Omega^{\sharp}
is a subset of
\Omega
.
Theorem
2.7
then tells us that
U-u
is continuous in
\Omega
, and hence we can infer that
\Omega^{\sharp}
is closed in
\Omega
, because it is the pre-image of the singleton
\{(U-u)(x_{0})\}\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Next, notice that
U-U(x_{0})\leq u-u(x_{0})
in
\Omega
, since
U-u\leq(U-u)(x_{0})
in
\Omega
, so that, if we take a point
x\in\Omega
, we get that

\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[U](x)-U(x_{0})=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[U-U(x_{0})](x)\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u-u(x_{0})](x)=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u](x)-u(x_{0}),

by the monotonicity of
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}
. Thus, we infer that

U(x)-U(x_{0})=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[U-U(x_{0})](x)\leq\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u-u(x_{0})](x)=u(x)-u(x_{0}),

since both
U
and
u
are variationally
p
-harmonious in
\Omega
.

Now, if
x\in\Omega^{\sharp}
, we know that
u(x)-u(x_{0})=U(x)-U(x_{0})
, and hence we obtain that

U(x)-U(x_{0})=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[U-U(x_{0})](x)=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u-u(x_{0})](x)=U(x)-U(x_{0}).

The definition of
\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}
and Lemma
2.2
then tell us that

U-U(x_{0})\equiv u-u(x_{0})
, and hence
U-u\equiv(U-u)(x_{0})
on the ball
B_{r}(x)
with
r=r_{\varepsilon}(x)>0
. As a consequence, we have shown that
B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega^{\sharp}
, that is
\Omega^{\sharp}
is open, since
x
is arbitrarily chosen in
\Omega^{\sharp}
. All in all,
\Omega^{\sharp}
is a closed and open subset of
\Omega
and, moreover, is non-empty, since it contains at least
x_{0}
. Therefore,
\Omega^{\sharp}=\Omega
, because
\Omega
is connected, that is
U-u\equiv(U-u)(x_{0})
in
\Omega
.

In conclusion, we find a contradiction by taking a sequence of points
x_{n}\in\Omega
that converge to any point
\overline{x}\in\Gamma
. In fact, since
U-u\in\mathrm{LSC}(\overline{\Omega})
, we have that

0<(U-u)(x_{0})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}(U-u)(x_{n})\leq(U-u)(\overline{x})=g(\overline{x})-g(\overline{x})=0.

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
w:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
be any function such that
\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}\leq w\leq\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}
on
\overline{\Omega}
. Consider the sequence of functions defined by

w_{1}=w,\quad w_{j+1}=\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[w_{j}]\ \mbox{ on }\ \overline{\Omega}\ \mbox{ for }j=1,2,\cdots.

Then, we have that

\lim_{j\to\infty}w_{j}\to u^{\varepsilon}=\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}=\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}\ \mbox{ on }\ \overline{\Omega}.

Proof.

By comparison, we can infer that

u_{j}\leq w_{j}\leq U_{j}\ \mbox{ on }\ \overline{\Omega}

for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, we have that

u\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}w_{j}\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}w_{j}\leq U=u,

which gives the desired claim.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, satisfying the uniform exterior sphere condition at any point in
\Gamma
. Then there exists
0<\varepsilon_{\Gamma}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
such that
for every
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{\Gamma}
, all points in
\Gamma
are regular for (
1.2
).

In particular, for every
g\in C(\Gamma)
, the Dirichlet-type problem (
1.2
) admits the unique solution
u^{\varepsilon}=\underline{u}^{\varepsilon}=\overline{u}^{\varepsilon}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
defined by Perron’s method.

Proof.

Our assumption is that there exists s radius
R>0
such that, for every
x_{0}\in\Gamma
, we can find a ball

B_{R}(y_{0})
such that
\overline{B_{R}(y_{0})}\cap\overline{\Omega}=\{x_{0}\}
. Hence, we define a function
w
by

w(x)=\frac{1}{R^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{|x-y_{0}|^{\alpha}}\ \mbox{ for }\ x\in\overline{\Omega}.

It is clear that
w\in C(\overline{\Omega})
,
w
is positive in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{x_{0}\}
,
w(x_{0})=0
. Finally, if we choose, say,
\alpha=(N+1)/(p-1)
, by Example
3.1
(iii), for
0<\varepsilon<\min(\varepsilon_{0},r_{\Gamma})
,
w
is
p
-superharmonious in
\Omega
.

Therefore, with these specifications,
w
is the desired barrier.
∎

5.
Limits of variationally
p
-harmonious functions

In this section, we shall prove Theorem
1.2
. We will first collect in Section
5.1
some known facts on the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
), that we recall here:

\Delta_{p}^{G}u=0\ \mbox{ in }\ \Omega,\quad u=g\ \mbox{ on }\ \Gamma.

5.1.
Generalized viscosity solutions for (
1.3
)

We start with the classical definition of a viscosity solution of an elliptic degenerate equation.
Consider a continuous mapping
F:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})\times{{\mathcal{S}}}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
. Here,
{\mathcal{S}}^{N}
is the set of symmetric
N\times N
matrices.
The upper and lower semi-continuous envelopes
F^{*}

and
F_{*}
of
F
are the functions defined by

F^{*}(x,s,\xi,X)=\limsup_{(y,t,\eta,Y)\to(x,s,\xi,X)}F(y,t,\eta,Y)

for
(x,s,\xi,X)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})\times{{\mathcal{S}}}^{N}
, and by
F_{*}=-(-F)^{*}
.

We recall from
[
7
,
15
]
that a bounded and upper (resp. lower) semi-continuous function

u
is
a viscosity subsolution (resp. supersolution)
of
F=0
in
\overline{\Omega}
if,
for any
(x,\phi)\in\overline{\Omega}\times C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\nabla\phi(x)\neq 0
and such that
u-\phi
has a local maximum (resp. minimum) at
x
with
\phi(x)=u(x)
,
it holds that

F_{*}(x,\phi(x),\nabla\phi(x),\nabla^{2}\phi(x))\leq 0\quad(\mbox{resp. }F^{*}(x,\phi(x),\nabla\phi(x),\nabla^{2}\phi(x))\geq 0).

A viscosity solution
of
F=0
in
\overline{\Omega}
is a function
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
which is both a viscosity subsolution and supersolution of
F=0
in
\overline{\Omega}
.

Also, in
[
3
,
4
,
12
]
, the following definitions are considered.
A function
u
in
\mathrm{USC}(\overline{\Omega})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
(resp. in
\mathrm{LSC}(\overline{\Omega})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
) is
a generalized viscosity subsolution (resp. supersolution)
of (
1.3
) if, for any
(x,\phi)\in\overline{\Omega}\times C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\nabla\phi(x)\neq 0
and such that

u-\phi
has a local maximum (resp. minimum) at
x
with
u(x)=\phi(x)
, it holds that

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}^{G}\phi(x)\leq 0\ \mbox{ (resp.}-\Delta_{p}^{G}\phi(x)\geq 0)\ \hskip 63.0pt\mbox{ for }\ x\in\Omega,

\displaystyle\min\left\{-\Delta_{p}^{G}\phi(x),\phi(x)-g(x)\right\}\leq 0

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\mbox{ (resp. }\max\left\{-\Delta_{p}^{G}\phi(x),\phi(x)-g(x)\right\}\geq 0)\ \mbox{ for }\ x\in\Gamma.

A generalized viscosity solution
of (
1.3
) is a continuous function on
\overline{\Omega}
, which is both a generalized viscosity subsolution and supersolution.
This weaker notion of viscosity solution appears naturally as the limiting situation of the dynamic programming principle with respect to (
1.5
) as is observed below. This formulation has been proved to be equivalent (we refer the reader to
[
2
,
8
]
) to the standard definition of viscosity solution of
F=0
for the operator
F
defined by

F(x,s,\xi,X)=-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X)}{p}-\frac{p-2}{p}\frac{\langle X\xi,\xi\rangle}{|\xi|^{2}}

for
(x,s,\xi,X)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})\times{{\mathcal{S}}}^{N}
. Here,
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X)
stands for the trace of the matrix
X
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.2
, we follow an argument used in
[
5
]
. To this aim, we need to set up some further notation.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
G:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})\times{{\mathcal{S}}}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be the mapping defined by

G(x,s,\xi,X)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X)}{p}-\frac{p-2}{p}\frac{\langle X\xi,\xi\rangle}{|\xi|^{2}}\ &\mbox{ if }\ x\in\Omega,\\
s-g(x)&\mbox{ if }\ x\in\Gamma,\end{cases}

for
(x,s,\xi,X)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})\times{{\mathcal{S}}}^{N}
.
Then, we compute:

G^{*}(x,s,\xi,X)=G_{*}(x,s,\xi,X)=-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X)}{p}-\frac{p-2}{p}\frac{\langle X\xi,\xi\rangle}{|\xi|^{2}}\ \mbox{ if }\ x\in\Omega

and, if
x\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle G^{*}(x,s,\xi,X)=\displaystyle\max\left\{-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X)}{p}-\frac{p-2}{p}\frac{\langle X\xi,\xi\rangle}{|\xi|^{2}},s-g(x)\right\},

\displaystyle G_{*}(x,s,\xi,X)=\displaystyle\min\left\{-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X)}{p}-\frac{p-2}{p}\frac{\langle X\xi,\xi\rangle}{|\xi|^{2}},s-g(x)\right\}.

Proof.

The first formula for
G^{*}=G_{*}=G
follows from the continuity of
G
at
interior points of
\Omega
. When
x\in\Gamma
instead,
we observe that, for every sufficiently small
\delta>0
, the supremum
of
G
on
(B_{\delta}(x)\cap\overline{\Omega})\times B_{\delta}(s)\times B_{\delta}(\xi)\times B_{\delta}(X)
(where the balls must be intended in the relevant Euclidean spaces) equals

\max\left\{-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(X)}{p}-\frac{p-2}{p}\frac{\langle X\xi,\xi\rangle}{|\xi|^{2}},s-g(x)\right\}.

The formula for
G^{*}
then ensues. The one for
G_{*}
easily follows from the formula
G_{*}=-(-G)^{*}
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Let
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\nabla\phi(x)\neq 0
.
Then, we have that

G(x,\phi(x),\nabla\phi(x),\nabla^{2}\phi(x))=\begin{cases}-\Delta^{G}_{p}\phi(x)\ &\mbox{ if }\ x\in\Omega,\\
\phi(x)-g(x)\ &\mbox{ if }\ x\in\Gamma,\end{cases}

and hence, we obtain for
x\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle G^{*}(x,\phi(x),\nabla\phi(x),\nabla^{2}\phi(x))=\max\left\{-\Delta^{G}_{p}\phi(x),\,\phi(x)-g(x)\right\},

\displaystyle G_{*}(x,\phi(x),\nabla\phi(x),\nabla^{2}\phi(x))=\min\left\{-\Delta^{G}_{p}\phi(x),\,\phi(x)-g(x)\right\}.

By Remark
5.2

together with
[
8
, Theorem 3.3]

and the classical weak comparison principle for the viscosity
solution of (
1.3
) (see
[
14
,
17
]
), we immediately obtain
the following weak comparison principle
for
G=0
in
\overline{\Omega}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
u\in\mathrm{USC}(\overline{\Omega})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
v\in\mathrm{LSC}(\overline{\Omega})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
If
u,v
are respectively a generalized viscosity subsolution and a generalized viscosity supersolution of
G=0
in
\overline{\Omega}
, then we have that
u\leq v
on
\overline{\Omega}
.

5.2.
An approximation scheme by
p
-harmonious functions

We introduce a family
of mappings
{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}\times\overline{\Omega}\times C(\overline{\Omega})\to\mathbb{R}
defined for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
by

{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(s,x,u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{2(N+p)\varepsilon}{p}\,\frac{s-\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[u](x)}{r_{\varepsilon}(x)^{2}}\ &\mbox{ if }\ x\in\Omega,\\
\varepsilon\,[s-g(x)]\ &\mbox{ if }\ x\in\Gamma\end{cases}

for
(s,x,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times\overline{\Omega}\times C(\overline{\Omega})
.
Here,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
is taken so small so as to be sure that
the set
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is
still a domain for every
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

If
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
is such that

\nabla\phi(x)\neq 0
, then we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\limsup_{(\varepsilon,y,\delta)\to(0^{+},x,0)}\frac{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\phi(y)+\delta,y,\phi+\delta)}{\varepsilon}=G^{*}(x,\phi(x),\nabla\phi(x),\nabla^{2}\phi(x)),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\liminf_{(\varepsilon,y,\delta)\to(0^{+},x,0)}\frac{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\phi(y)+\delta,y,\phi+\delta)}{\varepsilon}=G_{*}(x,\phi(x),\nabla\phi(x),\nabla^{2}\phi(x)).

Proof.

By using elementary properties of
p
-means (see
[
13
, Proposition 2.7]
), for every
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that

\frac{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\phi(y)+\delta,y,\phi+\delta)}{\varepsilon}=\\
\frac{2(N+p)}{p}\,\frac{\phi(y)+\delta-\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[\phi+\delta](y)}{r_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}}=\frac{2(N+p)}{p}\,\frac{\phi(y)-\mu_{p}^{\varepsilon}[\phi](y)}{r_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}}\ \mbox{ if }\ y\in\Omega,

and

\frac{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\phi(y)+\delta,y,\phi+\delta)}{\varepsilon}=\phi(y)+\delta-g(y)\quad\text{if \,}y\in\Gamma.

Then if
x\in\Omega
, by the uniform convergence claimed in Lemma
2.5

and the continuity of
\phi
and
g
, we then simply infer that

\limsup_{(\varepsilon,y,\delta)\to(0^{+},x,0)}\frac{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\phi(y)+\delta,y,\phi+\delta)}{\varepsilon}=\\
\begin{cases}-\Delta^{G}_{p}\phi(x)\ &\mbox{ if }\ x\in\Omega,\\
\max\left\{-\Delta^{G}_{p}\phi(x),\phi(x)-g(x)\right\}\ &\mbox{ if }\ x\in\Gamma.\end{cases}

The first claim is thus proved. The second claim follows similarly.
∎

We are now ready to prove our final result.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Up to re-defining
\varepsilon_{0}
, Theorem
1.1
tells us that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, there exists a unique function
u^{\varepsilon}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
that solves the equation

(5.1)

{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon}(x),x,u^{\varepsilon})=0\ \mbox{ for }\ x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Also, by Lemma
4.1
(i),
we have that
u^{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
\overline{\Omega}
from below and above
by the minimum and the maximum of
g
on
\Gamma
, and hence
independently of
\varepsilon
. Thus, by this property, the functions defined for

x\in\overline{\Omega}
by

u_{*}(x)=\liminf_{(\varepsilon,y)\to(0^{+},x)}u^{\varepsilon}(y)\quad\mbox{and}\quad u^{*}(x)=\limsup_{(\varepsilon,y)\to(0^{+},x)}u^{\varepsilon}(y)

are bounded in
\overline{\Omega}
. Also,
u_{*}
and
u^{*}
are lower semi-continuous and, respectively, upper semi-continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
, by a standard result.

The strategy of the proof is to show that
u_{*}
and
u^{*}
are a viscosity supersolution
and a viscosity subsolution of
G=0
in
\overline{\Omega}
. In fact, if we prove that,
the comparison principle in Theorem
5.3
would give that
u^{*}\leq u_{*}
on
\overline{\Omega}
. Thus, since
u_{*}\leq u^{*}
on
\overline{\Omega}
by construction, we would infer that
u_{*}\equiv u^{*}
on
\overline{\Omega}
. As a consequence, the function defined on
\overline{\Omega}
by

u=u_{*}\equiv u^{*}
would be continuous and a viscosity solution of
G=0
on
\overline{\Omega}
. Then,
u
would be the unique viscosity solution of (
1.3
)
by
[
8
, Theorem 3.3]
and Remark
5.2
.

We also point out that, while
u^{\varepsilon}
converges pointwise to
u
by construction,
we can further prove that this convergence is uniform on
\overline{\Omega}
. In fact,

u
turns out to be the limit of the monotonic sequences defined
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\overline{\Omega}
by

\displaystyle v_{k}(x)=\sup\Bigl\{u^{\varepsilon}(y)\,|\,0<\varepsilon<1/k,\,|y-x|<1/k,\,y\in\overline{\Omega}\Bigr\},\vskip 2.0pt

\displaystyle w_{k}(x)=\inf\Bigl\{u^{\varepsilon}(y)\,|\,0<\varepsilon<1/k,\,|y-x|<1/k,\,y\in\overline{\Omega}\Bigr\}.

The uniform convergence on
\overline{\Omega}
of these sequences follows from Dini’s monotone convergence theorem (see
[
26
, Theorem 7.13]
),
since they are monotonic,
\overline{\Omega}
is compact,
v_{k}
is lower semicontinuous,
w_{k}
is upper semicontinuous, and
u
is continuous on
\overline{\Omega}
. The uniform convergence of
u^{\varepsilon}
then follows by
observing that

w_{k}(x)\leq u^{\varepsilon}(x)\leq v_{k}(x)\ \mbox{ for }\ x\in\overline{\Omega}\quad\mbox{and}\quad 0<\varepsilon<1/k.

Therefore, to complete the proof, we are left to show that
u_{*}
and

u^{*}
are a viscosity supersolution and a viscosity subsolution of
G=0
in
\overline{\Omega}
. We shall prove that fact only for
u^{*}
, since
for
u_{*}
we can proceed similarly.

We preliminarily notice that,
by
[
13
, Theorem 2.5]
, the mapping
{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}
is decreasing
in the third variable, in the sense that for
(s,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\overline{\Omega}
and
u,v\in C(\overline{\Omega})
, it holds

{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(s,x,u)\geq{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(s,x,v)\quad\text{if \,}u\leq v\quad\text{in \,}\overline{\Omega}.

Now, let
(x,\phi)\in\overline{\Omega}\times C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\nabla\phi(x)\neq 0
be such that
u^{*}-\phi
has a local maximum at
x
with
u^{*}(x)=\phi(x)
.
Without loss of generality, we can always suppose that the maximum is global and strict,
that is
u^{*}-\phi<u^{*}(x)-\phi(x)=0
in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{x\}
.
By a standard argument in the theory of viscosity solutions (see
[
15
]
),
we know that there exists a sequence of elements
(\varepsilon_{j},x_{j})\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})\times\overline{\Omega}
such that,
for each fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{j}
is a global maximum point
for
u^{\varepsilon_{j}}-\phi
and

(\varepsilon_{j},x_{j},u^{\varepsilon_{j}}(x_{j}))\to(0,x,u^{*}(x))\ \mbox{ as }\ j\to\infty.

If we set
\delta_{j}=u^{\varepsilon_{j}}(x_{j})-\phi(x_{j})
, then
\delta_{j}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
and
u^{\varepsilon_{j}}-\phi\leq\delta_{j}
on
\overline{\Omega}
.

Now, we use (
5.1
) at
x_{j}
for
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{j}
and
u=u^{\varepsilon_{j}}
, and infer that

0={\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(u^{\varepsilon_{j}}(x_{j}),x_{j},u^{\varepsilon_{j}})=\\
{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\phi(x_{j})+\delta_{j},x_{j},u^{\varepsilon_{j}})\geq{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\phi(x_{j})+\delta_{j},x_{j},\phi+\delta_{j}).

Here, the last inequality follows from the aforementioned monotonicity of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon_{j}}
. Then since
\nabla\phi(x)\neq 0
, we can apply Lemma
5.4

to obtain that

0\geq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\phi(x_{j})+\delta_{j},x_{j},\phi+\delta_{j})}{\varepsilon_{j}}\geq\\
\liminf_{(\varepsilon,y,\delta)\to(0^{+},x,0)}\frac{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\phi(y)+\delta,y,\phi+\delta)}{\varepsilon}=F_{*}(x,\phi(x),\nabla\phi(x),\nabla^{2}\phi(x)).

Hence,
u^{*}
is a viscosity subsolution of
G=0
on
\overline{\Omega}
.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
is complete.
∎

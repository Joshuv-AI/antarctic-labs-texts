1.
INTRODUCTION

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
with an infinite residue field and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Pierre Samuel proved that for sufficiently large
n
, the
Hilbert-Samuel function

H_{I}(n)=\lambda(R/I^{n})
(where
\lambda()
denotes the length) of an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
is a polynomial
P_{I}(n)
, known as the
“Hilbert-Samuel polynomial of I”.
Since
P_{I}(n)
takes only numerical values for every integer
n
, by the theory on numerical polynomials we can write

P_{I}(n)=e_{0}(I)\dbinom{n+d-1}{d}-e_{1}(I)\dbinom{n+d-2}{d-1}+\ldots+(-1)^{d}e_{d}(I)

Here,
e_{0}(I),e_{1}(I),\ldots,e_{d}(I)
are unique integers known as the
Hilbert coefficients.
The leading coefficient
e_{0}(I)
of the polynomial
P_{I}(n)
is called the
multiplicity
of the ideal
I
and is denoted by
e(I).
Hilbert coefficients can be used to characterize the properties of the ring
R
, the ideal
I
and the blow-up algebras associated with
I
.

Marley revealed an interesting connection between the Hilbert coefficients and the reduction number of an ideal
I
. Recall that a reduction of
I
is an ideal
J\subseteq I
such that
JI^{n}=I^{n+1}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N.}

If
J
is a reduction of
I
, the
reduction number
of
I
with respect to
J
denoted by
\operatorname{rd}_{J}(I)
is the smallest
n
such that
JI^{n}=I^{n+1}.

A reduction is
minimal
if it is minimal with respect to containment among all reductions. The
reduction number
of
I
is defined as

\operatorname{rd}(I):=\min\{\operatorname{rd}_{J}(I):J
is a minimal reduction of
I\}
. The concept of reduction and minimal reduction was first introduced by Northcott and Rees. Reduction number is useful in studying the Cohen-Macaulay properties of the associated graded ring
\operatorname{gr}(I)=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I^{n}/I^{n+1}
of
R
with respect to
I.

The reduction number
\operatorname{rd}(I)
is said to be independent if
\operatorname{rd}(I)=\operatorname{rd}_{J}(I)
for every minimal reduction
J
of
I
. In the case of dimension one, the reduction number
\operatorname{rd}(I)
of an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
is independent. It was independently proved by Huckaba
[
6
]
and Trung
[
21
]
that if
0pt\operatorname{gr}(I)\geq d-1
, then
\operatorname{rd}(I)
is independent. Sally raised the following question in
[
18
]
: If
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring of dimension
d
, then is
\operatorname{rd}(\mathfrak{m})
independent? As a natural extension, one can ask if
\operatorname{rd}(I)
is independent for any
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I.
There are examples that suggest that
\operatorname{rd}(I)
is not independent in general. In this paper, we have investigated the conditions under which
\operatorname{rd}(I)
is independent (see corollaries
3.10
,
3.11
,
3.15
,
3.17
).

Define
\operatorname{n}(I):=\min\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:P_{I}(t)=H_{I}(t)
for
t>n\}
. Ooishi
[
12
]
called
\operatorname{n}(I)
the postulation number of
I
and proved that if
\dim R=1
, then
\operatorname{rd}(I)=\operatorname{n}(I)+1.
Marley
[
10
, Theorem 2.15]
proved that if
0pt\operatorname{gr}(I)\geq d-1
, then
\operatorname{rd}(I)=\operatorname{n}(I)+d.
Much of this paper was inspired by the following question raised by Marley
[
10
, Question 2.1]
:

Question 1.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
with an infinite residue field and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Is it always the case that
\operatorname{rd}(I)\leq\operatorname{n}(I)+d
?

In this paper, we have answered the question proposed by Marley in a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay ring. We have observed that the stability index of the Ratliff-Rush filtration
\rho(I)
plays an important role in understanding the relationship between the reduction number and postulation number of an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
. Recall that the the
stability index
of the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
is defined as:

\rho(I):=\min\{i\geq 1:\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq i\}
.

Thus, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I
. Let
S=R/(x)
and
I^{\prime}=I/(x)
. If
\operatorname{n}(I)\neq\rho(I)-1
, then
\operatorname{rd}(I)\geq\operatorname{n}(I)+2.
Furthermore, if
\operatorname{n}(I)=\rho(I)-1
, then
\operatorname{rd}(I)\leq\operatorname{n}(I)+2
.

It is worth asking if we can generalize Theorem 1.2 to a higher dimensional Cohen-Macaulay ring. The main difficulty in generalizing the result is that there is no simple relation between
\rho(I)
and
\rho\left(I/(x)\right)
. Under some conditions on the depth of the associated graded ring, we prove the following proposition:

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 3
and
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal such that
I
is integrally closed in
R
and
0pt\operatorname{gr}(I)=d-2.
If
\operatorname{n}(I)=-(d-3),
then
\operatorname{rd}(I)\geq\operatorname{n}(I)+d.

Another inspiration for this paper is to understand the relationship between
\rho(I)
and
\rho\left(I/(x)\right)
. If the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial element
x
, then
\rho(I)\geq\rho\left(I/(x)\right).
This provides a necessary condition for the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
to behave well mod a superficial element.
We observe that
\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}=\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}+(x)}{(x)}=\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}
for
n\gg 0.
This fact is closely related to the surjectivity of the map
\pi_{n}:\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}}{I^{n}}\longrightarrow\frac{\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}}{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}
for
n\gg 0.
Set
\omega(I):=\min\big\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\pi_{n}
is surjective for all
n\geq k\big\}.
Note that if
\omega(I)=1,
then the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial element. It is of some interest to find an upper bound for
\omega(I).
We prove the following Proposition:

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay ring of dimension
d\geq 2,

I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I.
If
b_{1}^{I}\neq-1
, then
\omega(I)=\max\{1,b_{1}^{I}+2\}\leq\max\{1,a_{2}(\operatorname{gr}(I))+1\}.

As an immediate corollary to Proposition 1.4 we prove (see
4.2
).

\rho(I)\geq\rho\left(I/(x)\right),
if
\rho(I)\geq\omega(I).

Northcott in his work has investigated the relation between the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial and the Hilbert-Samuel function in a one dimensional Cohen-Macaulay ring and has proved
[
11
]
that
P_{I}(n+1)-H_{I}(n+1)\geq P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n)
for all
n.

However, there are examples that illustrate that when the dimension of the ring is greater than one, the values of
P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n)
do not easily correlate with one another. Marley has proved a similar statement
[
10
, Theorem 2.4]
: Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal such that
0pt\operatorname{gr}(I)\geq d-1.
Then for
0\leq i\leq d
and for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}

(-1)^{d-i}\Delta^{i}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))\geq 0.

According to what we have observed, the condition on the depth of the associated graded ring can be relaxed by the weaker condition on the good behavior of the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
mod a superficial sequence. We prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2,

I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Let the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in I.
Then for all
0\leq i\leq d
and for all
n\geq\rho(I)

(-1)^{d-i}\Delta^{i}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))\geq 0.

As a corollary to the above theorem, we have partially answered (see corollary
5.8
) the following question posed by Marley
[
10
, Question 2.3]
:

Question 1.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Suppose
P_{I}(k)=H_{I}(k)
for some
k\geq 1
. Then is necessarily
\operatorname{n}(I)<k?

This paper is organized into five sections. Definitions, introductory ideas, notations, and a technical lemma are all covered in Section 2. In Section 3, we have established the relationship between the postulation number of an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
and the postulation number of
I^{\prime}=I/(x)
, where
x\in I
is a superficial element for
I.
We have proved Theorem 1.2 and its consequences related to the independence of reduction number
\operatorname{rd}(I).
We also prove Proposition 1.3 in this section. In Section 4, we prove Proposition 1.4. In Section 5, we prove Theorem 1.5 and have partially answered Marley’s Question 1.6 in a
d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay ring. The techniques used in sections 4 and 5 were inspired by Purthenpurakal
[
13
,
14
]
. We provide examples in support of our claims.

3.
Relation between the reduction number and the postulation number of an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal

Marley in
[
10
]
has proved that if
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
with an infinite residue field and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
such that
0pt\operatorname{gr}(I)\geq d-1
then
\operatorname{n}(I)+1=\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime}),
where
I^{\prime}=I/(x),
and
x\in I
is a superficial element for
I
. He has also proved with the above hypothesis that
\operatorname{rd}(I)=\operatorname{n}(I)+d.
Inspired by Marley’s work, one can ask whether these results can be extended without any condition on depth on the associated graded ring. In this section, we attempt to extend Marley’s results.

In the next proposition, we observe that the stability index
\rho(I)
of the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
plays an important role to describe the behaviour of the postulation number of
I
and the postulation number of
I
going mod a superficial element.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
with an infinite residue field and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Let
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I
. Set
S=R/(x)
and
I^{\prime}=I/(x)
.

(1)

If
\operatorname{n}(I)>\rho(I)-1
, then
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})=\operatorname{n}(I)+1.

(2)

If
\operatorname{n}(I)=\rho(I)-1
, then
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{n}(I)+1.

(3)

If
\operatorname{n}(I)<\rho(I)-1
, then
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})\geq\operatorname{n}(I)+1.

Proof.

Since
x
is a superficial element for
I
, therefore,
(I^{n+1}:x)=I^{n}
for all
n\geq\rho(I)
. By Lemma
2.4
, we have
H_{I^{\prime}}(n)=H_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n-1)
for all
n\geq\rho(I)+1.
By equation (1) at
\rho(I)
, we have
H_{I^{\prime}}(\rho(I))>H_{I}(\rho(I))-H_{I}(\rho(I)-1).

(1)

Suppose
\operatorname{n}(I)>\rho(I)-1.
For any
m>\operatorname{n}(I)+1\geq\rho(I)+1
, we have
H_{I^{\prime}}(m)=H_{I}(m)-H_{I}(m-1)=P_{I}(m)-P_{I}(m-1)=P_{I^{\prime}}(m)
so,
\operatorname{n}({I^{\prime}})\leq\operatorname{n}(I)+1
. If
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})<\operatorname{n}(I)+1,
then
H_{I^{\prime}}(\operatorname{n}(I)+1)=P_{I^{\prime}}(\operatorname{n}(I)+1),
which implies
H_{I}(\operatorname{n}(I)+1)-H_{I}(\operatorname{n}(I))=P_{I}(\operatorname{n}(I)+1)-P_{I}(\operatorname{n}(I))
. Thus,
H_{I}(\operatorname{n}(I))=P_{I}(\operatorname{n}(I))
, a contradiction. Hence,
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})=\operatorname{n}(I)+1.

(2)

Suppose
\operatorname{n}(I)=\rho(I)-1.
Then for any
m\geq\rho(I)+1
, we have
H_{I^{\prime}}(m)=H_{I}(m)-H_{I}(m-1)=P_{I}(m)-P_{I}(m-1)=P_{I^{\prime}}(m)
. Therefore, we get
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})<\rho(I)+1
which implies
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{n}(I)+1.

(3)

Suppose
\operatorname{n}(I)<\rho(I)-1
. We have
H_{I^{\prime}}(\rho(I))>P_{I^{\prime}}(\rho(I))
as
H_{I^{\prime}}(\rho(I))>H_{I}(\rho(I))-H_{I}(\rho(I)-1)=P_{I}(\rho(I))-P_{I}(\rho(I)-1)=P_{I^{\prime}}(\rho(I)).
Therefore, we get
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})\geq\rho(I)=\operatorname{n}(I)+1
which implies
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})\geq\operatorname{n}(I)+1.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

From Lemma
3.1
, we observe that
for
\operatorname{n}(I)=\rho(I)-1
,
H_{I}(\rho(I)-1)\neq P_{I}(\rho(I)-1)

If

At

Thus,
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})\geq\rho(I)=\operatorname{n}(I)+1.

H_{I^{\prime}}(m)=P_{I^{\prime}}(m).
Thus,
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})<\rho(I)+1
. Therefore,
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{n}(I)+1.
Thus, if

for

, we have
\operatorname{n}(I^{\prime})=\operatorname{n}(I)+1
.

4.
Ratliff-Rush filtration mod a superficial element

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
. Let
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I,
set
S=R/(x).
Consider the natural map
\pi:R\longrightarrow S.
It is easy to see that
\pi(\widetilde{I^{n}})\subseteq\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}
for all
n\geq 1.
We briefly recall the good behaviour of the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
mod a superficial element from the literature
[
14
, Section 2]
. The Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well mod
x
if
\pi(\widetilde{I^{n}})=\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}
for all
n\geq 1,
i.e.,
\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}+(x)}{(x)}=\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}
for all
n\geq 1.

This natural map
\pi
induces the map
\pi_{n}:\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}}{I^{n}}\longrightarrow\frac{\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}}{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}
for all
n\geq 1.
And for a fixed
n\geq 1,

\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}+(x)}{(x)}=\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}
if and only in
\pi_{n}
is surjective.

Define
\rho(I/(x)):=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}=\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}
for all
n\geq k\}
.
Then for all
n\geq\rho(I/(x))
,
\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}+(x)}{(x)}=\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}.
Therefore,
\pi_{n}
is surjective for all
n\geq\rho(I/(x))
. Define
\omega(I):=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\pi_{n}
is surjective for all
n\geq k\}
. We call this
\omega(I)
as the
surjectivity index
of the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I.
Note that if the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
mod a superficial element behaves well then
\omega(I)=1.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay ring of dimension
d\geq 2,

I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I.
If
b^{I}_{1}\neq-1
, then
\omega(I)=\max\{1,b_{1}^{I}(R)+2\}\leq\max\{1,a_{2}(\operatorname{gr}(I))+1\}.

Proof.

Set
S=R/(x)
,
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{R}(I)}
. Using the short exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}(I)
-modules
[
13
, 5]

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}(I)\longrightarrow L^{I}(R)\longrightarrow L^{I}(R)(-1)\longrightarrow 0

and the corresponding long exact sequence in local cohomolgy, we have that
b_{i}^{I}(R)\leq a_{i+1}(\operatorname{gr}(I))-1
for
i=0,1,\ldots,d-1
.
From
[
14
, 2.1]
note that,
\pi
induces a natural
\mathcal{R}(I)
-linear map
\pi^{*}:L^{I}(R)\longrightarrow L^{I/(x)}(S).
This induces a map on the local cohomology modules

H^{0}\left(\pi^{*}\right):H^{0}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)\longrightarrow H^{0}\left(L^{I/(x)}(S)\right)

By
[
13
, proposition 4.7]

H^{0}\left(\pi^{*}\right)_{n}=\pi_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Note that
H^{1}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)_{n}=0
for all
n\geq b_{1}^{I}(R)+1
. Using the the second fundamental exact sequence
[
13
, 6.2]
and

[
13
, 6.3]
we get,

H^{0}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)_{n}\xrightarrow{\pi_{n}}H^{0}\left(L^{I/(x)}(S)\right)_{n}\longrightarrow 0,
for all
n\geq b_{1}^{I}(R)+2

Thus,
\pi_{n}
is surjective for all
n\geq b_{1}^{I}(R)+2
, which implies
\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}+(x)}{(x)}=\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}
for all
n\geq b_{1}^{I}(R)+2.
Therefore,
\omega(I)\leq\max\{1,b_{1}^{I}(R)+2\}.

Let if possible
b_{1}^{I}+1\geq\omega(I).
Then,
\pi_{n}
is surjective for all
n\geq b_{1}^{I}+1.
By the long exact sequence in local cohomology in (4), we get that the
\mathcal{R}(I)
-linear map

0\longrightarrow\left(H^{1}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)\right)_{n-1}\longrightarrow\left(H^{1}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)\right)_{n}
is injective for all
n\geq b_{1}^{I}+1.

Therefore, at
n=b_{1}^{I}+1
, we have
\left(H^{1}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)\right)_{n}=0,
which implies that
\left(H^{1}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)\right)_{b_{1}^{I}}=0,
which is a contradiction. Thus,
b_{1}^{I}+1<\omega(I),
which implies
b_{1}^{I}+2\leq\omega(I).

Therefore,
\omega(I)=\max\{1,b_{1}^{I}(R)+2\}\leq\max\{1,a_{2}(\operatorname{gr}(I))+1\}.

∎

As an immediate corollary we get

Corollary 4.2
.

[With the hypothesis as in
4.1
] If
\rho(I)\geq\omega(I),
then
\rho(I)\geq\rho(I/(x)).

Proof.

Since
\rho(I)\geq\omega(I)
, we have
\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}+(x)}{(x)}=\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}
for all
n\geq\rho(I).
Also,
\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}=\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}+(x)}{(x)}
for all
n\geq\rho(I).
Therefore,
\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}=\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}
for all
n\geq\rho(I).
Thus,
\rho(I)\geq\rho(I/(x)).

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay ring of dimension two,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I.
If
\rho(I)\geq\operatorname{rd}(I)-1,
then
\rho(I)\geq\rho(I/(x)).

Proof.

Suppose
\rho(I)\geq\operatorname{rd}(I)-1.
Then by
[
9
, Lemma 1.2]
, we have
a_{2}(\operatorname{gr}(I))+2\leq\operatorname{rd}(I).
This implies
\rho(I)\geq\max\{1,a_{2}(\operatorname{gr}(I))+1\}\geq\omega(I).
Therefore, by Corollary
4.2
it follows that
\rho(I)\geq\rho(I/(x)).

∎

Remark 4.4
.

As a particular case, if the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
mod a superficial element
x
behaves well then
\omega(I)=1
. Also,
\rho(I)\geq 1
, thus from Corollary
4.2
, we have
\rho(I/(x))\leq\rho(I).

The following example illustrates Corollary
4.3
.

Example 4.5
.

[
15
, Exapmle 3.3]

Let
R=\mathbb{Q}[[x,y]]
and
I=(x^{4},x^{3}y,xy^{3},y^{4}).
Since
x^{2}y^{2}\in(I^{2}:I)
and
x^{2}y^{2}\notin I
, therefore,
\tilde{I}\not=I
and hence
0pt\operatorname{gr}(I)=0.
Note that
p=x^{4}+y^{4}
is the superficial element for
I
, as
e_{0}(I)=16=e_{0}(I/(p))
and
e_{1}(I)=6=e_{1}(I/(p))
and
J=(x^{4},y^{4})
is a minimal reduction of
I.
Using Macaulay 2, we get that,
\rho(I)=2
and
\operatorname{rd}(I)=2.
This implies
\rho(I)>\operatorname{rd}(I)-1.
Also
\operatorname{rd}_{J/(p)}(I/(p))=2,
by
[
16
, Proposition 4.2]

\rho(I/(p))\leq\operatorname{rd}_{J/(p)}(I/(p))=\operatorname{rd}(I)<\rho(I)+1.
Thus,
\rho(I/(p))\leq\rho(I).

We end this section with a corollary, which gives us the class of
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals in a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring for which the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well mod a superficial element.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay ring of dimension two,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal such that
I
is integrally closed and
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I.
If
\operatorname{rd}(I)=2,
then the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well mod x.

Proof.

From
[
9
, Lemma 1.2]
we observe that
a_{2}(\operatorname{gr}(I))+2\leq\operatorname{rd}(I)
, also by Proposition
4.1
we have,
\omega(I)\leq\max\{1,a_{2}(\operatorname{gr}(I))+1\}
, which implies
\omega(I)\leq\max\{1,\operatorname{rd}(I)-1\}
. Since
\operatorname{rd}(I)=2
, thus
\omega(I)\leq 1
, which implies
\omega(I)=1
. Therefore, the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well mod
x.

∎

5.
Ratliff-Rush filtration mod a superficial sequence

In this section, we extend the theorem by Marley
[
10
, Theorem 2.3]
in a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
. We observe that the stronger condition on the depth of the associated graded ring can be replaced by the weaker condition on the good behaviour of the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
. First, we recall the behaviour of the Ratliff-Rush filtration of an ideal
I
mod a superficial sequence
[
14
, Section 4]
.

Assume
s\leq d-1
. Let
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{s}\in I/I^{2}
be a superficial sequence for
I
. Set
R_{i}=R/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i})
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,s.
We have the natural map of quotients
\pi_{i,j}:R_{i}\longrightarrow R_{j}
, for every
0\leq i<j\leq s.
Note that

\pi_{i,j}\left(\widetilde{I^{n}R_{i}}\right)\subseteq\widetilde{I^{n}R_{j}}
for all
n\geq 1.

This map induces the following map:

\pi_{i,j}^{n}:\frac{\left(\widetilde{I^{n}R_{i}}\right)}{\left(I^{n}R_{i}\right)}\longrightarrow\frac{\left(\widetilde{I^{n}R_{j}}\right)}{\left(I^{n}R_{j}\right)}
for every
n\geq 1.
For a fixed
n\geq 1,
we observe that
\pi_{i,j}\left(\widetilde{I^{n}R_{i}}\right)=\widetilde{I^{n}R_{j}}
if and only in
\pi_{i,j}^{n}
is surjective. For
0\leq i<j<k\leq s
, we have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{I^{n}R_{i}}/I^{n}R_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{I^{n}R_{j}}/I^{n}R_{j}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{I^{n}R_{k}}/I^{n}R_{k}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i,j}^{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i,k}^{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{j,k}^{n}}

Remark 5.1
.

If
\pi_{i,j}^{n}
and
\pi_{j,k}^{n}
are surjective then
\pi_{i,k}^{n}
is surjective.

Definition 5.2
.

[
14
, Definition 4.4]

Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well mod superficial sequence
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{s}
if
\pi_{0,s}(\widetilde{I^{n}})=\widetilde{I^{n}R_{s}}
for all
n\geq 1.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{s}\in I/I^{2}
be a superficial sequence for
I
,
then for each
i=1,2,\ldots,s

\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i})\right):=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}:I^{n}R_{i}=\widetilde{I^{n}R_{i}}
for all
n\geq k\}.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2,

I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{s}\in I/I^{2}
be a superficial sequence for
I.
Then
\pi_{0,s}^{n}
is surjective for all
n\geq\max\{1,b^{I}+2\}.

Proof.

We will prove by induction on
s.
If
s=1,
we have proved in Proposition
4.1
. Let
s\geq 2,
we assume that
\pi_{0,s-1}^{n}
is surjective for all
n\geq b^{I}+2,
and will prove that
\pi_{0,s}^{n}
is surjective for all
n\geq b^{I}+2.

By
s=1
case
\pi_{0,1}^{n}
is surjective for all
n\geq b^{I}+2.
Using the second fundamental exact sequence (3) and the corresponding long exact sequence in local cohomolgy in (4), we get
H^{i}\left(L^{I/(x)}(R/(x))\right)_{n}=0
for all
n\geq b^{I}+2
and for all
i=1,2,\ldots,s-1.
Thus, by induction hypothesis
\pi_{1,s}^{n}
is surjective for all
n\geq b^{I}+2.
Now, using Remark
5.1
, and the commutative diagram, we have
\pi_{0,s}^{n}
is surjective for all
n\geq b^{I}+2.

∎

As an easy consequence of the preceding proposition, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 5.5
.

[With the hypothesis as in
5.4
] If
\rho(I)\geq b^{I}+2
, then
\rho(I)\geq\rho\left(\frac{I}{(x_{1},x_{2,}\ldots,x_{s})}\right).

Proof.

For all
n\geq\rho(I),
we have
I^{n}=\widetilde{I^{n}}.
Since
\rho(I)\geq b^{I}+2,
therefore,
\pi_{0,s}^{n}
is surjective for all
n\geq\rho(I),
which implies
\pi_{0,s}(\widetilde{I^{n}})=\widetilde{I^{n}R_{s}}
for all
n\geq\rho(I).
Thus,
\pi_{0,s}(I^{n})=\widetilde{I^{n}R_{s}}
for all
n\geq\rho(I),
which implies
\rho(I)\geq\rho\left(\frac{I}{(x_{1},x_{2,}\ldots,x_{s})}\right).

∎

As a generalization of Marley’s result
[
10
, Theorem 2.3]
, we now establish Theorem
5.7
. Before we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2,

I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. If the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod the superficial sequence
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-1}\in I,
then

\rho(I)\geq\rho\left(I/(x_{1})\right)\geq\ldots\geq\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-1})\right).

Proof.

We prove by induction on
d
.
Suppose
d=2.
Let
x_{1}\in I
be superficial for
I.
The Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod
x_{1},
thus,
\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}+(x_{1})}{(x_{1})}=\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}+(x_{1})}}{(x_{1})}
for all
n\geq 1.
Also
\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq\rho(I)
. Therefore,
\frac{I^{n}+(x_{1})}{(x_{1})}=\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}+(x_{1})}}{(x_{1})}
for all
n\geq\rho(I),
which implies
\rho\left(I/(x)\right)\leq\rho(I).

We assume the lemma for
d-2
and prove for
d-1.

Since the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod the superficial sequence
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-1}\in I,
then

\pi_{d-2,d-1}
is surjective. Therefore,
\pi_{d-2,d-1}\left(\widetilde{I^{n}R_{d-2}}\right)=\widetilde{I^{n}R_{d-1}}
for all
n\geq 1.
Also
I^{n}R_{d-2}=\widetilde{I^{n}R_{d-2}}
for all
n\geq\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right).
Thus,
I^{n}R_{d-1}=\widetilde{I^{n}R_{d-1}}
for all
n\geq\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right).
Therefore,
\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right)\geq\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-1})\right).

∎

Now we generalize the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 2.3]
by relaxing the condition on the depth of the associated graded ring.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2,

I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Let the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behave well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in I.
Then for all
0\leq i\leq d
and for all
n\geq\rho(I)

(-1)^{d-i}\Delta^{i}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))\geq 0.

Proof.

Note that it suffices to prove the case
i=d.
Let
g(n)=P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n).

Suppose
(-1)^{d-i}\Delta^{i}(g(n))\geq 0
for all
n\geq\rho(I)
and for some
i>0.
Then
(-1)^{d-i}\Delta^{i}(g(n))=\Delta^{1}[(-1)^{d-i}\Delta^{i-1}(g(n))]\geq 0
for all
n\geq\rho(I).
However,
g(n)=0
for sufficiently large
n
so
(-1)^{d-i}\Delta^{i-1}(g(n))=0
for sufficiently large
n.
By the property of difference function we have
(-1)^{d-i}\Delta^{i-1}(g(n))\leq 0
for all
n\geq\rho(I).

Therefore,
(-1)^{d-(i-1)}\Delta^{i-1}(g(n))\geq 0
for all
n\geq\rho(I),
which gives the theorem for
i-1.
Hence it is enough to prove
\Delta^{d}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))\geq 0
for all
n\geq\rho(I).

We prove this by induction on d.

Case-1: Suppose
d=2.
Let
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I.
Set
S=R/(x)
and
I^{\prime}=I/(x),
then
S
is a one dimensional Cohen-Macaulay ring.
By Lemma
2.4
and Proposition
3.1
, we have,

H_{I^{\prime}}(n+1)=H_{I}(n+1)-H_{I}(n)
for all
n\geq\rho(I)

P_{I^{\prime}}(n+1)=P_{I}(n+1)-P_{I}(n)
for all
n

Consider

\begin{split}\Delta^{2}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))&=\Delta^{1}[\Delta^{1}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))]\\
&=\Delta^{1}[(P_{I}(n+1)-P_{I}(n))-(H_{I}(n+1)-H_{I}(n))]\\
&=\text{$\Delta^{1}[P_{I^{\prime}}(n+1)-H_{I^{\prime}}(n+1)]$ for all $n\geq\rho(I)$}\\
&\geq\text{0 for all $n\geq\rho(I)$}.\end{split}

Case-2: Suppose
d>2.
Set
S=R/(x_{1})
and

I_{1}=I/(x_{1})
. Since the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in,
by Lemma
5.6
, we have
\rho(I)\geq\rho\left(I/(x_{1})\right)\geq\ldots\geq\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right).
We assume
\Delta^{d-1}(P_{I_{1}}(n)-H_{I_{1}}(n))\geq 0
for all
n\geq\rho(I_{1}).

Consider

\begin{split}\Delta^{d}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))&=\Delta^{d-1}[\Delta^{1}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))]\\
&=\Delta^{d-1}[(P_{I}(n+1)-P_{I}(n))-(H_{I}(n+1)-H_{I}(n))]\\
&=\text{$\Delta^{d-1}[P_{I_{1}}(n+1)-H_{I_{1}}(n+1)]$ for all $n\geq\rho(I)$}\\
&\geq\text{0 for all $n\geq\rho(I)-1$}.\end{split}

Therefore, we have
\Delta^{d}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))\geq 0
for all
n\geq\rho(I).

∎

The following corollary partially answers Marley’s Question
1.6
.

Corollary 5.8
.

[With the hypothesis as in
5.7
] If
P_{I}(k)=H_{I}(k)
for some
k\geq\rho(I)
, then
P_{I}(n)=H_{I}(n)
for all
n\geq k.

Proof.

By Proposition,
5.7
we have that

(-1)^{d-1}\Delta^{1}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))\geq 0
for all
n\geq\rho(I).

Hence

(-1)^{d-1}(P_{I}(n+1)-H_{I}(n+1))\geq(-1)^{d-1}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))
for all
n\geq\rho(I).

Also
P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n)=0
for sufficiently large
n.
Thus we get

0\geq(-1)^{d-1}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))\geq(-1)^{d-1}(P_{I}(k)-H_{I}(k))
for all
n\geq k.

It is given that
P_{I}(k)=H_{I}(k)
for some
k\geq\rho(I),
then
P_{I}(n)=H_{I}(n)
for all
n\geq k.

∎

Consequently, as a corollary we obtain Marley’s result.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2,

I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal such that
0pt\operatorname{gr}(I)\geq d-1.
Then for all
0\leq i\leq d
and for all
n\geq 1

(-1)^{d-i}\Delta^{i}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))\geq 0.

Proof.

Since the
0pt\operatorname{gr}(I)\geq d-1,
from the proof of
5.7
and
[
4
, 1.3]
we have
\rho(I)=\rho\left(I/(x_{1})\right)=\ldots=\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right)=1.
Thus,
\Delta^{d}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))\geq 0
for all
n\geq 1.

∎

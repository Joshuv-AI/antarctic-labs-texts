Dynamical localization for a random scattering zipper Thanks: The research of H.B was partially funded by the ANR Project RAW, ANR-20-CE40-0012 Orcid number of H.B. : 0000-0001-5255-3887
Hakim Boumaza, Amine Khouildi

Abstract
. This article establishes a proof of dynamical localization for a random scattering zipper model. The scattering zipper operator is the product of two unitary by blocks operators, multiplicatively perturbed on the left and right by random unitary phases. One of the operator is shifted so that this configuration produces a random 5-diagonal unitary operator per blocks. To prove the dynamical localization for this operator, we use the method of fractional moments. We first prove the continuity and strict positivity of the Lyapunov exponents in an annulus around the unit circle, which leads to the exponential decay of a power of the norm of the products of transfer matrices. We then establish an explicit formula of the coefficients of the finite resolvent in terms of the coefficients of the transfer matrices using Schur’s complement. From this we deduce, through two reduction results, the exponential decay of the resolvent, from which we get the dynamical localization.



1.
Introduction : model and main results

In this paper we aim at proving a result of dynamical localization for a random scattering zipper of arbitray size. Let
L\geq 1
an integer. A scattering zipper is a system obtained by concatenation of elementary unitary diffusion with a fixed number
2L
of incoming and outgoing channels each. The deterministic scattering zipper operator was introduced in
[
MSB13
]
and a random version of this model was introduced in
[
BM15
]
. We now precise this latter random version of the random scattering zipper adding some hypotheses on the randomness which allow to prove a dynamical localization result for this new model.

Let

be the unitary subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{\mathrm{L}}(\mdmathbb{C})
of order

. Let

(1.1)

\tilde{\Omega}=\left(U(L)\times\left\{-1,1\right\}^{L}\times[0,2\pi]^{L}\right)^{2}

endowed with the probability measure

defined on

for the usual topology on the Lie group. Here

and

Remark 1.1
.

In view of the definition of the probability space

With these notations, we can now introduce our model of random scattering zipper. Consider the random family of unitary operators
\{\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
where, for every
\omega\in\Omega
, the operator
\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}
acts on
\ell^{2}(\mdmathbb{Z},\mdmathbb{C}^{L})
and is defined by:

(1.6)

\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}=\mdmathbb{V}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{W}_{\omega}

where

(1.7)

\mdmathbb{V}_{\omega}=\begin{pmatrix}\ddots&&&\\
&S^{(0)}_{\omega}&&\\
&&S^{(2)}_{\omega}&\\
&&&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}\circ s_{g}^{L}\quad\mbox{ , }\quad\mdmathbb{W}^{\omega}=\begin{pmatrix}\ddots&&&\\
&S^{(-1)}_{\omega}&&\\
&&S^{(1)}_{\omega}&\\
&&&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}

and
s_{g}
is the shift operator to the left
(v_{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{Z}}\mapsto(v_{n+1})_{n\in\mdmathbb{Z}}
. The
2L\times 2L
blocks
S^{(n)}_{\omega}
are unitary matrices in the unitary group
U(2L)
of the particular form

(1.8)

S^{(n)}_{\omega}=S(\alpha,U^{(n)}_{\omega},V^{(n)}_{\omega})=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\rho(\alpha)U^{(n)}_{\omega}\\
V^{(n)}_{\omega}\tilde{\rho}(\alpha)&-V^{(n)}_{\omega}\alpha^{*}U^{(n)}_{\omega}\end{pmatrix},

for a fixed
\alpha\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mdmathbb{C})
such that
\|\alpha\|<1
, and with
\rho(\alpha)=(1-\alpha\alpha^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\tilde{\rho}(\alpha)=(1-\alpha^{*}\alpha)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Throughout this article,
\|\cdot\|
denotes any subordinate norm on
\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mdmathbb{C})
. We introduce the set

(1.9)

\mbox{U}(2L)_{\mathrm{inv}}\;=\;\left\{\left.\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mbox{U}(2L)\;\right|\;\alpha,\gamma,\delta\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mdmathbb{C})\mbox{ and }\beta\in\mathrm{GL}_{\mathrm{L}}(\mdmathbb{C})\;\right\}.

which also has the representation (see
[
MSB13
]
):

(1.10)

\mbox{U}(2L)_{\mathrm{inv}}=\left\{\left.S(\alpha,U,V)\in\mbox{\rm U}(2L)\;\right|\;\|\alpha^{*}\alpha\|<1\;\mbox{ and }U,V\in\mbox{U}(L)\right\},

where for any
U,V\in\mathrm{U}(L)
and any
\alpha\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mdmathbb{C})
with
\|\alpha^{*}\alpha\|<1
,

(1.11)

S(\alpha,U,V)\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\rho(\alpha)U\\
V\widetilde{\rho}(\alpha)&-V\alpha^{*}U\end{pmatrix}.

Note that the family
\{\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
has a very important property of
2\mdmathbb{Z}
-ergodicity which implies the existence of an almost sure spectrum for this family. See
[
CL12
]
or
[
Kir08
]
for a definition of this ergodicity property and for the existence of the almost-sure spectrum. Note that if
\Sigma
denotes the almost-sure spectrum of
\{\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
,
\Sigma\subset\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
since the operators
\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}
are unitary. The
2\mdmathbb{Z}
-ergodicity also implies the existence of almost-sure pure point, absolutely continuous and singular continuous spectra.

The block shifting between
\mdmathbb{V}_{\omega}
and
\mdmathbb{W}_{\omega}
in the product defining
\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}
allows us to consider the scattering zipper model as a matrix-valued version of the Blatter-Browne model introduced in
[
BB88
]
to understand Zener tunneling effect in diodes. It can also be considered as a matrix-valued version of the Chalker-Coddington model on a band studied in
[
ABJ10
]
. Indeed,
\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}
has the same band structure as that found in the Blatter-Browne and quasi-one-dimensional Chalker-Coddington models, but with an arbitrarily large diagonal bandwidth. Note that since
\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}
is a unitary band operator, the general results of
[
BHJ03
]
apply to it. To avoid any confusion, note that the zipper scattering model is very different from the Chalker-Coddington model over the entire
\mdmathbb{Z}^{2}
network, since it is one-dimensional rather than two-dimensional. The scattering zipper model can also be seen as a version of CMV matrices with matrix coefficients. CMV matrices are the unitary analog of Jacobi matrices and were originally introduced in the study of orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle (see
[
Sim05
]
for a comprehensive review of this vast subject). On the other hand, Schulz-Baldes studied in
[
SB07
]
a generalization of Jacobi matrices with matrix coefficients. The scattering zipper was introduced by Marin and Schulz-Baldes in
[
MSB13
]
with the aim of defining a unitary analog to Jacobi matrices with matrix coefficients that shares with CMV matrices their spectral properties and a simple way of representing their coefficients. In particular, the block factorization of CMV matrices into two diagonal operators (see
[
CMV03
]
) is used to define the scattering zipper, without having to resort to an interpretation in terms of orthogonal polynomials on the unit disk.

We can summarize this discussion in a diagram in which the horizontal arrows represent the transition from a scalar-valued operator to a matrix-valued operator, and the vertical arrows represent the transition from a self-adjoint model to its unitary analogue.

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\mbox{Jacobi matrices}&\longrightarrow&\mbox{Jacobi matrices with matrix coefficients}\\
\downarrow&&\quad\quad\quad\quad\downarrow\\
\mbox{CMV matrices}&\longrightarrow&\mbox{Scattering zipper}\end{array}

Let us now present the main results obtained in this paper. Let
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mdmathbb{Z}}
be the canonical basis of
\ell^{2}(\mdmathbb{Z})\otimes\mdmathbb{C}^{L}
. For
i,j\in\mdmathbb{Z}
, we set
e_{\{i,j\}}:=e_{iL+j}
, which corresponds to the j-th component in the i-th L-block. We now state the main result of this article.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists
r_{0}>0
such that for every
\alpha\in\mathrm{GL}_{\mathrm{L}}(\mdmathbb{C})
with
\|\alpha\|\leq r_{0}
, there exists
C_{r_{0}}>0
and
b>0
such that for all
\{k,p\}
and
\{l,q\}
in
\mdmathbb{Z}\times\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket
,

(1.12)

\mdmathbb{E}\left[\sup_{n\in\mdmathbb{Z}}\left|\left\langle e_{\{k,p\}},(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega})^{n}e_{\{l,q\}}\right\rangle\right|\right]\leq C_{r_{0}}e^{-b|k-l|}.

The estimate (
1.12
) means that the family
\{\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
satisfies the condition of dynamical localization as defined for example in
[
HJS09
]
. This property is dynamic in nature and follows the evolution of wave packets over discrete time
n\in\mdmathbb{Z}
. It tells us that the solutions of the Schrödinger equation are localized in space in the vicinity of their initial position and this, uniformly over time. This reflects the absence of quantum transport.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is based upon a number of intermediate results. First, following the result of positivity of the Lyapunov exponents on the unit circle
\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
obtained by Boumaza and Marin in
[
BM15
]
, we prove their strict positivity on an annulus

(1.13)

\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}:=\{z\in\mdmathbb{C};1-\epsilon<|z|<1+\epsilon\}

for some
\epsilon\in(0,1]
. The proof is done by combining the positivity of the Lyapunov exponents on the unit circle and their continuity on
\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

For
z\in\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
let
G_{\omega}(z)
be the resolvent at
z
of the operator
\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}
and let
G_{\omega}(z,\cdot,\cdot)
be its Green kernel. For
a,b\in\mdmathbb{Z}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
,
a<b
, we also denote by
G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z)
the resolvent of the restriction of the operator
\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}
to the interval
[a,b]
and by
G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,\cdot,\cdot)
its Green kernel :

(1.14)

G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z)=\left(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{[a,b]}-z\right)^{-1},

and for
k,l\in\mdmathbb{Z}
,

(1.15)

G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)=\langle e_{k},\left(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{[a,b]}-z\right)^{-1}e_{l}\rangle.

The precise definition of the restricted operator
\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{[a,b]}
is given at Section
3.1
.

We study the fractional moments of
G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,\cdot,\cdot)
for
z\in\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
and we first prove that they are uniformly bounded.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
s\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
there exists
C(s)>O
such that, for every
z\in\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
, every
a,b\in\mdmathbb{Z}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
,
a<b
, and every
k,l\in\mdmathbb{Z}
such that
|k-l|>4
,

(1.16)

\mdmathbb{E}\left(\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)\|^{s}\right)\leq C(s).

Once we have obtained this uniform bound, combining it with the positivity of the smallest Lyapunov exponent and proving estimates on the blocks of the products of transfer matrices, we prove the exponential decay of some blocks of the restricted Green kernel to suitable intervals.

Theorem 1.4
.

There exists
r_{0}>0
,
\epsilon_{0}>0
,
s_{0}\in(0,1)
,
p_{0}>1
,
C_{s_{0},r_{0}}>0
and
\gamma>0
such that, for every
\alpha\in GL_{L}(\mdmathbb{C})
with
\|\alpha\|\leq r_{0},
every
s\in(0,s_{0}]
and every
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}]
,

(1.17)

\mdmathbb{E}\left(\|G_{\omega}^{[2n,2m+1]}(z,2n,2m+1)\|^{s}\right)\leq Ce^{-\gamma|m-n|}

for every
z\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}\setminus\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
and for every
m
and
n
in
\mdmathbb{Z}
such that
|m-n|>p_{0}
.

Theorem
1.4
is central in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Unlike traditional approaches, like the one used by Hamza, Joye, and Stolz (see
[
HJS09
]
) which relies on expressing the Green’s function in terms of eigenfunctions with particular boundary conditions to obtain exponential decay through transfer matrices, we must adopt a different method. The block configuration of our model does not allow us to directly apply this strategy since the expression for the Green kernel obtained in this case is too complicated to estimate (see
[
DPS08
]
for such an expression of the Green kernel). Therefore, we use the Schur complement as an alternative. This method allows us to identify a term whose norm expectation must be bounded to demonstrate the decay of the reduced case.

In order to be able to reduce our analysis to the blocks for which we have the exponential decaying (
1.17
), we prove two reduction results. We start by showing that it suffices to deal with even-sized scattering zipper operators.

Proposition 1.5
.

Assume
\alpha\in\mathrm{GL}_{\mathrm{L}}(\mdmathbb{C})
is such that
\|\alpha\|<1
. Let
s\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
,
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and let
k,l\in\mdmathbb{Z}
such that
|k-l|>4
. There exists
C(s,\epsilon)>0
such that:

(1.18)

\mdmathbb{E}\left(\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)\|^{s}\right)^{2}\leq C(s,\epsilon)\sum_{i,j=0}^{1}\mdmathbb{E}(\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,2n+2i,2m+1+2j)\|^{4s})^{\frac{1}{2}},

for all
z\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}
and
n,m
such that
k\in\{2n,2n+1\}
and
l\in\{2m,2m+1\}
.

The proof involves bounding the norm of the "even" blocks
\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,2n,2m)\|
and
\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,2n+1,2m)\|
by the norms of the "odd" blocks
\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,2n,2m+1)\|
and
\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,2n+1,2m+1)\|
. A necessary condition for establishing this is the invertibility of
\alpha
. This invertibility is necessary to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 1.6
.

If
\alpha\in\mathrm{GL}_{\mathrm{L}}(\mdmathbb{C})
, then for every
\epsilon>0
, for every
n\in\mdmathbb{Z}
and for every

, the matrix

\alpha+zV^{(n)}_{\omega}\alpha U_{\omega}^{(n)}
is invertible almost surely, and for every

, there exists
C(s)>0
such that

(1.19)

\forall n\in\mdmathbb{Z},\ \ \mdmathbb{E}\left(\|(\alpha+zV_{\omega}^{(n)}\alpha U_{\omega}^{(n)})^{-1}\|^{s}\right)\leq C(s).

Once we get Lemma
1.6

1.5

The second reduction result show that it suffices to control the Green kernel of the restricted operator to some finite interval in order to control the Green kernel of
\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\epsilon>0
. One has

(1.20)

\mdmathbb{E}\left(\|G_{\omega}(z,k,l)\|^{s}\right)^{2}\leq C(s)\mdmathbb{E}\left(\|G_{\omega}^{[k,l]}(z,k,l)^{2s}\|\right)

for every

and every

such that

.

The proof of Lemma
1.7

Combining Proposition

.

There exists
r_{0}>0

,
\epsilon_{0}>0

, such that for every
\alpha\in\mathrm{GL}_{\mathrm{L}}(\mdmathbb{C})
with

,

(1.21)

\mdmathbb{E}\left(\|G_{\omega}(z,k,l)\|^{s}\right)\leq C_{s,r_{0}}e^{-\gamma|k-l|}

for every
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}]
, every
k,l\in\mdmathbb{Z}
and every

.

The proof of Theorem

and every

for every

and every

Remark 1.10
.

The parameter
\epsilon_{0}
is chosen to guarantee strictly positive Lyapunov exponents over the annulus
\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon_{0}}
and is introduced in Corollary
2.8
.

Results of strict positivity of the positive Lyapunov exponent are already known for models of unit-band operators (see
[
BHJ03
,
HJS06
]
). Bourget, Howland and Joye’s article
[
BHJ03
]
was one of the first to present the study of unitary models with one diffusion channel, and was followed by other articles by Alain Joye on the subject, such as
[
Joy04
]
and
[
Joy05
]
. For a synthetic presentation of these results and the associated physical models, we refer to Alain Joye’s text,
[
Joy11
]
. Note that the unitary models considered in these first articles are all with scalar coefficients, and not with matrix coefficients as in the case of the scattering zipper.

One can see the random scattering zipper model as a unitary version of the quasi-one-dimensional Anderson model. There is already a unitary version of the scalar-valued Anderson model for which Hamza, Joye and Stolz have proven dynamical localization in
[
HJS09
]
. The random scattering zipper can therefore also be seen as a quasi-one-dimensional version of the unitary Anderson model. This is why, as we will see in the rest of the paper, it is possible to follow the strategy of the proof of the dynamical localization result of
[
HJS09
]
. Based upon the Fractional Moments Method (see
[
AW15
]
for an overview of this method), this strategy is also the one used in the proof of localization in
[
ABJ10
,
ABJ12
]
for the Chalker-Coddington model.

However, it would be possible to consider a multiscale analysis approach to study this unitary model, as Cedzich and Werner did in
[
CW21
]
in the context of unitary quantum walks. This would then make it possible to address the question of localization for a random scattering zipper model in which the unitary phases would reveal a much more singular randomness than a uniform Haar law. A last possible approach would be to adapt to the quasi-one-dimensional case the proofs of localization results for CMV matrices in
[
Zhu24
,
BDF+19
]
which are based on large deviations inequalities.

The rest of the paper is devoted to the proof of the results we have just staten in this introductory section. In Section
2
we introduce the transfer matrices associated to the quasi-one-dimensional model
\{\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
of unitary type (see
[
Bou23
]
). We also define the Lyapunov exponents associated to the sequence of transfer matrices and we prove their continuity, hence their positivity on some annulus
\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}
. Section
3.5
is the core of the article. In this section we prove the exponential decay of the fractional moments of the Green kernel. First, we show that it suffices to prove this exponential decay for some particular blocks of the resolvent by proving Proposition
1.5
and Proposition
3.4
. To prove the exponential decay of the fractional moments of these particular blocks we look precisely at the behavior of the four
L\times L
blocks of the products of transfer matrices and prove boundedness of some "Wronskian" involving these blocks. Finally in Section
4
, we prove the exponential decay of moments of order
2
and we deduce from the spectral theorem for unitary operators the proof of the dynamical localization for
\{\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
.

2.
Transfer matrices and Lyapunov exponents

2.1.
Transfer matrices.

Using the transfer matrix formalism, we reduce the study of the asymptotic behavior of a solution
\phi
of

(2.1)

\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\phi=z\phi,\quad\mbox{for}\ z\in\mdmathbb{C},

to the asymptotic behavior of a product of random matrices.

In the unitary setting, the role played by the symplectic group for the quasi-one-dimensional models of Schrödinger type will be played by the group
\mbox{U}(L,L)
of the matrices of size
2L\times 2L
which preserve the form
\mathcal{L}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}&0\\
0&-\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
in the sense that
T
is in
\mbox{U}(L,L)
if and only if
T^{*}\mathcal{L}T=\mathcal{L}
.

To compute the transfer matrices we proceed as follows. Instead of looking at the input-output relations of the scattering matrix
S_{\omega}^{(n)}
, we look for a new matrix which allows to express
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\phi_{n+1}\\
{}_{n+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
in terms of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\phi_{n}\\
{}_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for
\phi
a solution of (
2.1
) and
\psi=\mdmathbb{W}_{\omega}\phi
. This is done by transforming the scattering matrices
S_{\omega}^{(n)}
belonging to
\mbox{U}(2L)_{\mathrm{inv}}
into elements of
U(L,L)
via the bijection:

\varphi\ :\ \begin{array}[]{ccl}\mbox{U}(2L)_{\mathrm{inv}}&\to&\mathrm{U}(L,L)\\[5.69054pt]
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)&\mapsto&\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\gamma-\delta\beta^{-1}\alpha&\delta\beta^{-1}\\
-\beta^{-1}\alpha&\beta^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\end{array}

Let
z\in\mdmathbb{C}
. We have the following relations, proven in
[
MSB13
]
:

(2.2)

\forall n\in\mdmathbb{Z},\ \left(\begin{smallmatrix}\phi_{2n}\\
{}_{2n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\varphi(z^{-1}S^{(2n)}_{\omega})\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{}_{2n-1}\\
\phi_{2n-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{}_{2n+1}\\
\phi_{2n+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\varphi(S^{(2n+1)}_{\omega})\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\phi_{2n}\\
{}_{2n}\end{smallmatrix}\right).

These relations lead to introduce the application
T(z,\cdot):\Omega\to\mathrm{GL}_{2L}(\mdmathbb{C})
,

(2.3)

\forall\omega\in\Omega,\ T(z,\omega)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}V_{\omega}^{(0)}&0\\
0&(U_{\omega}^{(0)})^{*}\end{smallmatrix}\right)T_{0}(z)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}V^{(1)}_{\omega}&0\\
0&(U^{(1)}_{\omega})^{*}\end{smallmatrix}\right)T_{1}

with

T_{0}(z)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}z^{-1}(\widetilde{\rho}(\alpha))^{-1}&(\widetilde{\rho}(\alpha))^{-1}\alpha^{*}\\
\alpha(\widetilde{\rho}(\alpha))^{-1}&z(\rho(\alpha))^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad T_{1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}(\widetilde{\rho}(\alpha))^{-1}&(\widetilde{\rho}(\alpha))^{-1}\alpha^{*}\\
\alpha(\widetilde{\rho}(\alpha))^{-1}&(\rho(\alpha))^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right).

We define the 2-shift transformation
\tau_{2}:\Omega\mapsto\Omega
by:

\forall\omega\in\Omega,\forall n\in\mdmathbb{Z},\ (\tau_{2}(\omega))_{n}=\omega_{n+2}.

Then,
\tau_{2}
is ergodic on
(\Omega,\mathcal{B},\mdmathbb{P})
and

(2.4)

\forall\omega\in\Omega,\ \forall z\in\mdmathbb{C},\ \forall n\in\mdmathbb{Z},\ T(z,\tau_{2}^{n}(\omega))=\varphi(z^{-1}S^{(2n)}_{\omega})\cdot\varphi(S^{(2n-1)}_{\omega}).

The matrix
T(z,\tau^{n}(\omega))
is the n-th transfer matrix associated to
\{\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
. Then
(T(z,\tau^{n}(\omega)))_{n\in\mdmathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of
i.i.d.
random matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{2L}(\mdmathbb{C})
because of the definition of the probability
\mdmathbb{P}
as a tensor product of probability measures.

Note that for
z\in\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
,
T(z,\tau^{n}(\omega))\in U(L,L)
.

2.2.
Lyapunov exponents.

The transfer matrices
T(z,\cdot)
generate a cocycle
\Phi(z,\cdot,\cdot):\Omega\times\mdmathbb{Z}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2L}(\mdmathbb{C})
on the ergodic dynamical system
(\Omega,\mathcal{B},\mdmathbb{P},(\tau^{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{Z}})
defined by

\forall\omega\in\Omega,\ \forall n\in\mdmathbb{Z},\ \Phi(z,\omega,n)\;=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}T(z,\tau_{2}^{n-1}(\omega))\dots T(z,\omega)&\mbox{ if }&n>0\\
\mathrm{I}_{2\mathrm{L}}&\mbox{ if }&n=0\\
(T(z,\tau_{2}^{n}(\omega)))^{-1}\dots(T(z,\tau_{2}^{-1}(\omega)))^{-1}&\mbox{ if }&n<0.\end{array}\right.

From this cocycle we define the Lyapunov exponents associated to the ergodic family
\{\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
. Let
z\in\mdmathbb{C}
. For
\mdmathbb{P}
-almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, the following limits exists and are equal:

(2.5)

\Psi(z,\omega):=\lim_{n\to+\infty}((\Phi(z,\omega,n))^{*}\Phi(z,\omega,n))^{1/2n}=\lim_{n\to-\infty}((\Phi(z,\omega,n))^{*}\Phi(z,\omega,n))^{1/2|n|}.

For every
k\in\llbracket 1,2L\rrbracket
, let
\lambda_{k}(z,\omega)
the
k
-th eigenvalue of
\Psi(z,\omega)
, the eigenvalues being ordered in increasing order. There are then real numbers
\lambda_{k}(z)\geq 0
such that, for
\mdmathbb{P}
-almost every
\omega\in\Omega
,
\lambda_{k}(z,\omega)=\lambda_{k}(z)
. We then define the Lyapunov exponents associated to the ergodic family
\{\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
as being the real numbers
{}_{k}(z)
defined by :

\forall z\in\mdmathbb{C},\ \forall\ k\in\llbracket 1,2L\rrbracket,\ {}_{k}(z):=\log(\lambda_{k}(z)).

Note that when
z\in\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
, the fact that the transfer matrices belong to
U(L,L)
implies a symmetry relation on the Lyapunov exponents which is the same as in the case where the transfer matrices are in the symplectic group:

(2.6)

\forall k\ \in\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket,\ {}_{2L-k+1}(z)=-{}_{k}(z).

In
[
BM15
]
, we proved, for a random scattering zipper which can be seen as a particular case of (
1.6
) that,

(2.7)

\forall z\in\mdmathbb{S}^{1},\ {}_{1}(z)>{}_{2}(z)>\cdots>{}_{L}(z)>0.

Using Kotani’s theory (see
[
BM15
]
which adapts results of
[
KS88
]
), this implies the absence of almost-sure absolutely continuous spectrum of
\{\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
.

The proof of (
2.7
) relies on the study of the so-called Furstenberg group associated to
\{\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
. This is the group generated by the common law of the transfer matrices. Let
z\in\mdmathbb{C}
. If
\mu_{z}
is the common law of all the transfer matrices
T(z,\tau^{n}(\omega))
, we set

(2.8)

G_{\mu_{z}}\;=\;\overline{\langle\text{supp}\mu_{z}\rangle}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2L}(\mdmathbb{C}).

The closure is taken for the topology on
\mathrm{GL}_{2L}(\mdmathbb{C})
which is induced by the usual topology on
\mathcal{M}_{2L}(\mdmathbb{C})
.

In
[
BM15
]
we proved that for every
z\in\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
,
G_{\mu_{z}}=U(L,L)
. Actually, the algebraic construction made in the proof of
[
BM15
, Proposition 2]
is valid for any
z\in\mdmathbb{C}
and we get

(2.9)

\forall z\in\mdmathbb{C},\ U(L,L)\subseteq G_{\mu_{z}}.

Hence one gets, using a similar proof as the one of
[
BM15
, Theorem 1]
, that

(2.10)

\forall z\in\mdmathbb{C},\ {}_{1}(z)>\cdots>{}_{L}(z)>{}_{L+1}(z)>\cdots>{}_{2L}(z).

But for
z\in\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
the transfer matrices are no longer in
U(L,L)
and we no longer have the symmetry relation (
2.6
). Hence there is no reason for
{}_{L}(z)
to be postive if
z
is not in
\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
. Still, what remains true is following equality:

(2.11)

\forall n\in\mdmathbb{Z},\ \forall\omega\in\Omega,\ \forall z\in\mdmathbb{C}^{*}:\ (T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z))^{-1}=\mathcal{L}\left(T_{\omega}^{(n)}(\bar{z}^{-1})\right)^{*}\mathcal{L}.

The proof of (
2.10
) is based upon the Cayley transform, whose matrix in the canonical basis of
\mdmathbb{C}^{2L}
is

(2.12)

\displaystyle C=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}&-i\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}\\
\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}&i\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{2L}(\mdmathbb{C})

and which maps the group
U(L,L)
onto the complex symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{N}}^{*}(\mdmathbb{C})
. It also use the following transformation which separates the real and imaginary parts of a matrix with complex coefficients and place them in blocks:

(2.13)

\pi\ :\ \begin{array}[]{cll}\mathcal{M}_{2L}(\mdmathbb{C})&\to&\mathcal{M}_{4L}(\mdmathbb{R})\\[5.69054pt]
A+iB&\mapsto&\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&-B\\
B&A\end{smallmatrix}\right).\end{array}

It allows to apply directly the results of
[
BL85
]
to get separability of the Lyapunov exponents and their integral representation from the properties of
p
-contractivity and
L_{p}
-strong irreducibility of the Furstenberg group as defined in
[
BL85
]
.

2.3.
Continuity and strict positivity of the Lyapunov exponents.

Among the key elements for the development of the fractional moment method, the continuity and positivity of Lyapunov exponents occupy a prominent place. As we have just explained, Theorem 1 of
[
BM15
]
gives us the positivity of these exponents, but this assertion remains limited to the circle
\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
.

In order to manipulate the resolvent efficiently, it is necessary to choose a spectral parameter
z
located outside of
\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
. The objective of this subsection is therefore twofold. Firstly, we will demonstrate the continuity of Lyapunov exponents on
\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. Secondly, using
[
BM15
, Theorem 1]
, we will highlight the positivity of these exponents in some annulus
\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega
,
n\in\mdmathbb{Z}
and
z\in\mdmathbb{C}
. In order to simplify the further computations, from now, we will denote by
T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z):=T(z,\tau_{2}^{n}(\omega))
the
n
-th transfer matrix.

We start by giving upper and lower bounds on the norm of
\rho(\alpha)
which will be used throughout the rest of the article.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
L\geq 1
. Let
\|.\|
be a subordinate norm on
\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mdmathbb{C})
. For
\alpha\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mdmathbb{C})
such that
\|\alpha\|<1
and
\rho(\alpha)=(\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}-\alpha\alpha^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, the following inequalities hold:

1.

\|{\rho(\alpha)}\|\geq\sqrt{1-\|\alpha\|^{2}}
.

2.

\|{\rho(\alpha)}\|\leq 2-\sqrt{1-\|\alpha\|^{2}}
.

3.

\|{\rho(\alpha)}^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{{\sqrt{1-\|\alpha\|^{2}}}}
.

4.

\|{\rho(\alpha)}^{-1}\|\geq\frac{1}{{2-\sqrt{1-\|\alpha\|^{2}}}}
.

and the same for
\tilde{\rho}(\alpha)=(\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}-\alpha^{*}\alpha)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

Point
(1)
follows from the triangle inequality, points
(2)
and
(3)
are direct consequences of the power series expansion of
z\mapsto\sqrt{1-z}
and
z\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-z}}
and the sub-multiplicativity of the matrix norm. Finally, to prove
(4)
it suffices to note that for a subordinate norm, we have

\frac{1}{\|A^{-1}\|}\leq\|A\|
. The proof is identical for
\tilde{\rho}(\alpha)
.
∎

We now prove several estimates on the transfer matrices which are ingredients of the proof of the continuity of the Lyapunov exponents.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(\alpha,\epsilon)>0
such that:

(2.14)

\forall z\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon},\ \forall n\in\mdmathbb{Z},\ \forall\omega\in\Omega,\ \|T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z)\|\leq C_{1}.

Proof.

For simplicity, we denote
\rho=\rho(\alpha)
and
\widetilde{\rho}=\widetilde{\rho}(\alpha)
.

T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}V^{(2n)}_{\omega}&0\\
0&(U^{(2n)}_{\omega})^{*}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}z^{-1}\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&-\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\alpha^{*}\\
-\alpha\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&z\rho^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}V^{(2n-1)}_{\omega}&0\\
0&(U^{(2n-1)}_{\omega})^{*}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&-\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\alpha^{*}\\
-\alpha\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&\rho^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Thus, for
z\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}
,

\displaystyle\|T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z)\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left(\begin{smallmatrix}z^{-1}\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&-\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\alpha^{*}\\
-\alpha\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&z\rho^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\|.\left\|\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&-\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\alpha^{*}\\
-\alpha\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&\rho^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(|z^{-1}\||\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\|+\|\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\alpha^{*}\|+\|\alpha\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\|+|z\||\rho^{-1}\|).(\|\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\|+\|\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\alpha^{*}\|+\|\alpha\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\|+\|\rho^{-1}\|)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}\|{\rho}^{-1}\|+2\|{\rho}^{-1}\|.\|\alpha\|+(1+\epsilon)\|\rho^{-1}\|\right)\left(\|{\rho}^{-1}\|+2\|{\rho}^{-1}\|\|\alpha\|+\|\rho^{-1}\|\right).

We define
c_{\epsilon}:=\max\{\frac{1}{1-\epsilon};1+\epsilon\}>1
. Then, since
\frac{1}{c_{\epsilon}}<1
,

\displaystyle\|T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z)\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(c_{\epsilon}\|{\rho}^{-1}\|+2\|{\rho}^{-1}\|.\|\alpha\|+c_{\epsilon}\|\rho^{-1}\|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\epsilon}^{2}\|\rho^{-1}\|^{2}\left(2+2\|\alpha\|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4c_{\epsilon}^{2}\frac{1}{{1-\|\alpha\|^{2}}}\left(1+\|\alpha\|\right)^{2}:=C_{1}(\alpha,\epsilon).

using Lemma
2.1
at the last inequality.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1).
Let
p\in\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket
. There exists
C_{2}=C_{2}(p,\alpha,\epsilon)>0
such that:

(2.15)

\forall z\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon},\forall n\in\mdmathbb{Z},\forall\omega\in\Omega,\ \ \left\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z)\right\|\leq C_{2}.

Proof.

The inequality (
2.15
) comes from (
2.14
) and the general fact: if
M\in GL_{2L}(\mdmathbb{C})
then for any
p
,
\|\wedge^{p}M\|\leq\|M\|^{p}
(see
[
BL85
]
). Note that here,
\wedge^{p}
denotes the
p^{\mathrm{th}}
exterior power.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. There exists
C_{3}=C_{3}(\alpha,\epsilon)>0
such that:

(2.16)

\forall n\in\mdmathbb{Z},\forall\omega\in\Omega,\forall z_{1},z_{2}\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon},\ \|T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})-T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\|\leq C_{3}\mid z_{1}-z_{2}\mid.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mdmathbb{Z}
,
\omega\in\Omega
and
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}
.

\displaystyle\|T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})-T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\begin{pmatrix}V^{(2n)}_{\omega}&0\\
0&(U^{(2n)}_{\omega})^{*}\end{pmatrix}\left[\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}^{-1}\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&-\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\alpha^{*}\\
-\alpha\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&z_{1}\rho^{-1}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}z_{2}^{-1}\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&-\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\alpha^{*}\\
-\alpha\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&z_{2}\rho^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\right]\begin{pmatrix}V^{(2n-1)}_{\omega}&0\\
0&(U_{\omega}^{(2n-1)})^{*}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&-\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\alpha^{*}\\
-\alpha\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&\rho^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&-\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\alpha^{*}\\
-\alpha\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&\rho^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\right\|.\left\|\begin{pmatrix}(z_{1}^{-1}-z_{2}^{-1})\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&0\\
0&(z_{1}-z_{2})\rho^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\right\|.

Since
\|\alpha\|<1
and using Lemma
2.1
, there exists
C_{3}^{\prime}=C_{3}^{\prime}(\alpha)>0
such that:

\displaystyle\|T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})-T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}^{\prime}\left\|\left(\begin{smallmatrix}(z_{1}^{-1}-z_{2}^{-1})\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}&0\\
0&(z_{1}-z_{2})\rho^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}^{\prime}\left(\|(z_{1}^{-1}-z_{2}^{-1})\widetilde{\rho}^{-1}\|+\|(z_{1}-z_{2})\rho^{-1}\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}^{\prime}\|\rho^{-1}\|\left(\frac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|}{|z_{1}.z_{2}|}+|z_{1}-z_{2}|\right).

Since
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are in
\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}
,
|z_{i}|>1-\epsilon
,
\frac{1}{|z_{1}.z_{2}|}<\frac{1}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}
and using lemma
2.1
we find,

\|T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})-T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\|\leq C_{3}^{\prime}\frac{1}{{\sqrt{1-\|\alpha\|^{2}}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|\leq C_{3}|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

∎

We now prove the last step before proving the continuity of the Lyapunov exponents.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. Let
p\in\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket
. There exists
C_{4}=C_{4}(\alpha,\epsilon,p)>0
such that:

(2.17)

\forall n\in\mdmathbb{Z},\forall\omega\in\Omega,\forall z_{1},z_{2}\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon},\ \|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})-\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\|\leq C_{4}\mid z_{1}-z_{2}\mid.

Proof.

As the transfer matrices are invertible, we can reproduce the reasoning from the proof of
[
Bou07
, Lemma 6.2.6]
and use the following inequality:

\left\|\wedge^{p}M-\wedge^{p}N\right\|\leq\|N-M\|\left(\|N\|^{p-1}+\|M\|\cdot\|N\|^{p-2}+\ldots+\|M\|^{p-1}\right)

Then it suffices to apply Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
to conclude.
∎

Having these four lemmas, one can now deduce the continuity of the Lyapunov exponents.

For
p\in\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket
,
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,
\bar{x}\in P(\mdmathbb{C}^{2L})
and
z\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}
, we define:

(2.18)

\Phi(z,\bar{x})=\mdmathbb{E}\left(\ln{\frac{\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z)\bar{x}\|}{\|\bar{x}\|}}\right)

where
P(\mdmathbb{C}^{2L})
is the projective space associated with
\mdmathbb{C}^{2L}
. Let
L_{p}
be the
p
-Lagrangian of
\wedge^{p}\mdmathbb{C}^{2L}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

The map
(z,\bar{x})\mapsto\Phi(z,\bar{x})
has the following properties:

(1)

The map
\bar{x}\mapsto\Phi(z,\bar{x})
is continuous on
P({L}_{p})
.

(2)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
z_{1}
,
z_{2}\ \in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}
:

\sup_{\bar{x}\in P({L}_{p})}|\Phi(z_{1},\bar{x})-\Phi(z_{2},\bar{x})|<C|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

(3)

The function

\displaystyle{\mdmathbb\displaystyle S}_{\epsilon}\times P({L}_{p})\to\mdmathbb{R}

\displaystyle\Phi:

\displaystyle(z,\bar{x})\mapsto\Phi(z,\bar{x})

is continuous.

Proof.

We start by demonstrating the first point. According to Lemma
2.3
,
\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z)
is uniformly bounded in
z
and
\omega
and

\forall z\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon},\ \forall\omega\in\Omega,\ \ln{\frac{\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z)\bar{x}\|}{\|\bar{x}\|}}\leq\ln{\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z)\|}\leq\ln(C_{2})

hence if
\bar{x}_{m}\to\bar{x}
in
\mdmathbb{P}({L}_{p})
, it suffices to apply the dominated convergence theorem to show that:

\Phi(z,\bar{x}_{m})=\mdmathbb{E}\left(\frac{\|(\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z)\bar{x}_{m}\|}{\|\bar{x}_{m}\|}\right)\underset{m\to+\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 20.48618pt}}\mdmathbb{E}\left(\frac{\|(\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z)\bar{x}\|}{\|\bar{x}\|}\right)=\Phi(z,\bar{x}).

For the second assertion, we begin by taking
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
in
\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}
. We have:

\Phi(z_{1},\bar{x})-\Phi(z_{2},\bar{x})=\mdmathbb{E}\left(\ln{\frac{\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})\bar{x}\|}{\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\bar{x}\|}}\right).

Writing

\displaystyle\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1}))\bar{x}\|

\displaystyle=\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})\cdot\wedge^{p}(T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2}))^{-1}\cdot\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\bar{x}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})\cdot\wedge^{p}(T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})))^{-1}\|\cdot\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\bar{x}\|

and the same exchanging
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
, one gets:

\left|\ln\left(\frac{\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})\bar{x}\|}{\|{\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\bar{x}}\|}\right)\right|\leq\ln{(\max\{\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})(\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2}))^{-1}\|;\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})(\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1}))^{-1}\|\})}

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})(T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2}))^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\left(\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})-\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\right)\cdot(\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2}))^{-1}+I_{2L}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2}))^{-1}\|\cdot\|\left(\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})-\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\right)\|+1

and the same exchanging
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
. This gives the following inequality:

\left|\ln\left(\frac{\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})\bar{x}\|}{\|{\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\bar{x}}\|}\right)\right|\leq\ln{\left[\max_{i=1,2}\left\{\|(\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{i}))^{-1}\|\right\}\cdot\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})-\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\|+1\right]}\\
.

Since for every
x>0
,
\ln(x)\leq x-1
, using (
2.11
) to be able to apply Lemma
2.3
to the inverse of the transfer matrices, we get:

\left|\mdmathbb{E}\left(\ln{\frac{\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})\bar{x}\|}{\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\bar{x}\|}}\right)\right|\leq C_{2}\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})-\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\|.

Lemma
2.5
ensures the existence of a constant
C_{4}>0
such that:

\|\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{1})-\wedge^{p}T_{\omega}^{(n)}(z_{2})\|\leq C_{4}\mid z_{1}-z_{2}\mid.

Since
C_{2}
and
C_{4}
do not depend on
\bar{x}
, we get the second point.

For the third point, it suffices to combine the two previous points.
Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and take
(z_{1},\bar{x}_{1})
and
(z_{2},\bar{x}_{2})
close enough to have:

\displaystyle\mid\Phi(z_{1},\bar{x}_{1})-\Phi(z_{2},\bar{x}_{2})\mid

3.
Exponential decay of the fractional moments of the Green’s function

In this Section we provide a proof of the exponential decay of fractional moments of the Green’s matrix, which is the result of Theorem
1.8
. We will begin our analysis by reducing the problem to the proof of the exponential decay of some particular coefficients of the Green kernel, following the approaches described in previous works such as
[
HJS09
,
ABJ10
]
. Then, we will adopt a new strategy to establish exponential decay in the reduced case.

The usual scheme to establish exponential decay of fractional moments is to find an explicit expression of the Green’s function in terms of the solutions of the equation
(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)\phi=0
as in
[
HJS09
]
. In our case, it is difficult to use this approach due to the matrix structure of the model which implies a formula (see
[
DPS08
]
) whose terms are difficult to control. Therefore, we proceed differently, in two steps: first, we bound the norm of the Green’s function by multiplying it by a non-constant quantity and the norm of the inverses of the transfer matrices which, as we have already established, decay exponentially. Then, we demonstrate that the first quantity is bounded, under a condition on the norm of the matrix
\alpha
.

F_{z}-\widehat{F}_{z}=-2zP_{A}\left[(\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}(\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega})P_{A}(e^{-ia}-1)P_{A}(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\right]P_{A}.

F_{z}-\widehat{F}_{z}=-P_{A}(\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}P_{A}(e^{-ia}-1)P_{A}(2z(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1})P_{A}.

Then, using the equality
2z(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}=(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}+z)(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}-\mathrm{Id},
it follows that

\displaystyle F_{z}-\widehat{F}_{z}

\displaystyle=P_{A}(\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}P_{A}(e^{-ia}-1)[F_{z}-\mathrm{Id}]P_{A}

\displaystyle=-P_{A}\left[\mathrm{Id}+z(\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega})\right](\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}P_{A}(e^{-ia}-1)[F_{z}-\mathrm{Id}]P_{A}

\displaystyle=P_{A}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Id}+(\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}+z)(\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}-z)^{-1})P_{A}(e^{-ia}-1)[\mathrm{Id}-F_{z}]\right]P_{A}

\displaystyle=P_{A}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Id}+\hat{F}_{z})(e^{-ia}-1)[\mathrm{Id}-F_{z}]\right]P_{A}.

Therefore,
P_{A}(F_{z}-\hat{F})P_{A}=P_{A}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Id}+\hat{F}_{z})(e^{-ia}-1)(\mathrm{Id}-F_{z})\right]P_{A},
and we can write:

(3.8)

F_{z}-\widehat{F}_{z}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Id}+\hat{F}_{z})(e^{-ia}-1)(\mathrm{Id}-F_{z}),

which is equivalent to
F_{z}\left(I+\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Id}+\hat{F}_{z})(e^{-ia}-1)\right)=F_{z}+\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Id}+\hat{F}_{z})(e^{-ia}-1)
.

Hence
F_{z}\frac{1}{2}(e^{-ia}-1)\left(\frac{e^{-ia}+1}{e^{-ia}-1}\mathrm{Id}+\hat{F}_{z})\right)=\frac{1}{2}(e^{-ia}-1)\left(\mathrm{Id}+\hat{F}_{z}\frac{e^{-ia}+1}{e^{-ia}-1}\right)
.

Let us define
m_{a}:=-i\left(\frac{e^{-ia}+1}{e^{-ia}-1}\right)
. Then we have:

(3.9)

F_{z}(i.m_{a}+\hat{F}_{z})=\mathrm{Id}+i.m_{a}\hat{F}_{z}.

Hence,
F_{z}
and
\hat{F}_{z}
satisfy the same relation as the one found in the proof of
[
HJS09
, Theorem 3.1]
and they have the same dissipative properties. Then, one can follow the proof of
[
HJS09
, Theorem 3.1]
for
\tau\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi}
to finish the proof of (
1.16
).

Note that if at the beginning of the proof we choose both
k
and
l
odd instead of even, we have to write the same proof as in the even case but by writing the factorization (
3.3
) on the factor
\mdmathbb{W}_{\omega}
and replacing
\widehat{V}^{(2k)}_{\omega}
by
\widehat{U}^{(2k)}_{\omega}
and
\theta^{(2k)}_{\omega}
by
\Theta^{(2k)}_{\omega}
.
∎

3.3.
Reduction results.

In this section, we aim to focus our study on structures of odd lengths, and then on those of finite lengths. Thess reductions imply the need to ensure the invertibility of
\alpha
. In what follows, we fix
a,b\in\mdmathbb{Z}
,
|a-b|>4
.

3.3.1.
Reduction to appropriate elements.

The goal of this part is to estimate, in expectation, elements of the type
\left\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,2n,2m)\right\|^{s}
and
\left\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,2n+1,2m)\right\|^{s}
by elements of the type
\left\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,2n,2m+1)\right\|^{s}
and
\left\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,2n+1,2m+1)\right\|^{s}
, for which we know explicit expressions. Challenge in proving the reduction to even elements consists of being able to bound the even columns (and rows) of the Green’s matrix by the odd columns (and rows), or more easily, by a sum. We use the notations introduced in Section
3.1
. We start with a lemma that will allow us to have a useful recurrence relation for the rest of the proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
l\in\llbracket a,b\rrbracket
. For every
k\in\llbracket a+1,b-1\rrbracket,\ k\neq l
and every
z\in\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\mdmathbb{S}^{1}
,

(3.10)

\left[{{}_{k}^{-1}}^{*}{}_{k-1}\right]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k-1,l)+{{}_{k}^{-1}}^{*}[\delta_{k}+z\alpha_{k}^{*}]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)+zG_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+1,l)=0.

(3.11)

zG_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)+{\beta_{k}^{-1}}^{*}\left[\alpha_{k+1}+z\delta_{k}^{*}\right]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+1,l)+{\beta_{k}^{-1}}^{*}\beta_{k+1}G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+2,l)=0.

Similar inequalities are applicable for the columns.

Proof.

By the definition of the resolvent:

\left[(\mdmathbb{U}^{[a,b]}_{\omega}-z\mathrm{Id})G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z)\right](k,l)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}&\text{if }k=l\\
0&\text{if }k\neq l\end{array}\right.

Thus, for
k
even and
l\neq k,k+1
, we find:

\left[(\mdmathbb{U}^{[a,b]}_{\omega}-z\mathrm{Id})G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z)\right](k,l)=(\mdmathbb{U}^{[a,b]}_{\omega}-z\mathrm{Id})(k,\cdot)\cdot G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,\cdot,l)=0

This, given the 5-diagonal by blocks structure of the operator, results in:

(3.12)

\alpha_{k}{}_{k-1}G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k-1,l)+(\alpha_{k}\delta_{k-1}-z\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}})G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)+\beta_{k}\alpha_{k+1}G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+1,l)+\beta_{k}\beta_{k+1}G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+2,l)=0,

and similarly for
k+1
:

\left[(\mdmathbb{U}^{[a,b]}_{\omega}-z\mathrm{Id})\cdot G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z)\right](k+1,l)=(\mdmathbb{U}^{[a,b]}_{\omega}-z\mathrm{Id})(k+1,\cdot)\cdot G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,\cdot,l)=0.

This results in:

(3.13)

{}_{k}{}_{k-1}G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k-1,l)+{}_{k}\delta_{k-1}G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)+(\delta_{k}\alpha_{k+1}-z\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}})G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+1,l)+\delta_{k}\beta_{k+1}G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+2,l)=0

Multiplying (
3.12
) by
\delta_{k}\beta_{k}^{-1}
on the left and taking the difference one gets

\displaystyle\left[\delta_{k}\beta_{k}^{-1}\alpha_{k}{}_{k-1}-{}_{k}{}_{k-1}\right]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k-1,l)+[\delta_{k}\beta_{k}^{-1}(\alpha_{k}\delta-z\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}})-{}_{k}\delta_{k-1}]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)

\displaystyle+[\delta_{k}\beta_{k}^{-1}\beta_{k}\alpha_{k+1}-(\delta_{k}\alpha_{k}-z\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}})]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+1,l)+[\delta_{k}\beta_{k}^{-1}\beta_{k}\beta_{k+1}-\delta_{k}\beta_{k+1})]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+2,l)=0.

After simplification, this yields:

\left[(\delta_{k}\beta_{k}^{-1}\alpha_{k}-{}_{k}){}_{k-1}\right]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k-1,l)+[\delta_{k}\beta_{k}^{-1}(\alpha_{k}\delta_{k-1}-z\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}})-{}_{k}\delta_{k-1}]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)+zG_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+1,l)=0.

We have:

(\alpha_{k}{}_{k}^{-1}\delta_{k}-\beta_{k})=({}_{k}^{-1})^{*}\text{ and }\beta_{k}^{*}\alpha_{k}=-\delta_{k}^{*}{}_{k}\Leftrightarrow\alpha_{k}{}_{k}^{-1}=-(\beta_{k}^{-1})^{*}\delta_{k}^{*}.

This allows us to write:

(3.14)

\left[({}_{k}^{-1})^{*}{}_{k-1}\right]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k-1,l)+({}_{k}^{-1})^{*}[\delta_{k}+z\alpha_{k}^{*}]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)+zG_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+1,l)=0.

A similar equality can be found by multiplying (
3.13
) by
\alpha_{k}{}_{k}^{-1}
and taking the difference:

\displaystyle\left[\alpha_{k}{}_{k}^{-1}{}_{k}{}_{k-1}-\alpha_{k}{}_{k}\right]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k-1,l)+[(\alpha_{k}{}_{k}^{-1}{}_{k}\delta_{k-1})-(\alpha_{k}\delta_{k-1}-z\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}})]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)

\displaystyle+[\alpha_{k}{}_{k}^{-1}(\delta_{k}\alpha_{k+1}-z\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}})-\beta_{k}\alpha_{k+1}]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+1,l)+[\delta_{k}{}_{k}^{-1}\delta_{k}\beta_{k+1}-\beta_{k}\beta_{k+1}]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+2,l)=0.

Hence,

zG_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)+\left[(\alpha_{k}{}_{k}^{-1}\delta_{k}-\beta_{k})\alpha_{k+1}-z\alpha_{k}{}_{k}^{-1}\right]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+1,l)+[(\alpha_{k}{}_{k}^{-1}\delta_{k}-\beta_{k})\beta_{k+1}]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+2,l)=0.

For any
k
, we have:

\alpha_{k}{}_{k}^{-1}\delta_{k}-\beta_{k}=(\beta_{k}^{-1})^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{k}^{*}\alpha_{k}=-\delta_{k}^{*}{}_{k}\Leftrightarrow\alpha_{k}{}_{k}^{-1}=-(\beta_{k}^{-1})^{*}\delta_{k}^{*}.

This allows us to write:

(3.15)

zG_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)+(\beta_{k}^{-1})^{*}\left[\alpha_{k+1}+z\delta_{k}^{*}\right]G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+1,l)+(\beta_{k}^{-1})^{*}\beta_{k+1}G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+2,l)=0.

To find the equalities for the columns
l
(while fixing
k
), it suffices to repeat the same process but start with the multiplication in the reverse direction:

\left[G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z)(\mdmathbb{U}^{[a,b]}_{\omega}-z\mathrm{Id})\right](k,l)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}&\text{if }k=l\\
0&\text{if }k\neq l\end{array}\right..

∎

\delta_{k}+z\alpha_{k}^{*}

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\alpha\in\mathrm{GL}_{\mathrm{L}}(\mdmathbb{C})
, then for any
\epsilon>0
,
k\in\mdmathbb{Z}
, and
z\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}

\delta_{k}+z\alpha_{k}^{*}
is
\mdmathbb{P}

Proof.

Let
z\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}
and
k\in\mdmathbb{Z}
. We define:

inv

\displaystyle=\{\omega\in\Omega;\ \delta_{k}+z\alpha_{k}^{*}\in\mathrm{GL}_{\mathrm{L}}(\mdmathbb{C})\}

\displaystyle=\{\omega\in\Omega;\ \widetilde{\rho}^{-1}[zV^{(k)}_{\omega}\alpha^{*}-\alpha^{*}U^{(k)}_{\omega}]\in\mathrm{GL}_{\mathrm{L}}(\mdmathbb{C})\}

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\{\omega\in\Omega;\ \det(zV^{(k)}_{\omega}\alpha^{*}-\alpha^{*}U^{(k)}_{\omega})\neq 0\}

To show that
\mdmathbb{P}({}_{\mathrm{inv}})=1
, it suffices to demonstrate that:

\exists(U_{0};V_{0})\in\Omega;\ \det(zV_{0}\alpha^{*}-\alpha^{*}U_{0})\neq 0

since we are working with the Haar measure
\nu_{L}
of the phases and since
\alpha
is invertible.

If
z\neq 1
, we set
(U_{0},V_{0})=(\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}},\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}})
. In this case:

\det(zV_{0}\alpha^{*}-\alpha^{*}U_{0})=\det(z\alpha^{*}-\alpha^{*})=(1-z)^{L}(-1)^{L}\det\alpha^{*}\neq 0.

If
z=1
, it suffices to take
(U_{0},V_{0})=(i\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}},\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}})
. In this case:

\det(zV_{0}\alpha^{*}-\alpha^{*}U_{0})=\det(\alpha^{*}-i\alpha^{*})=\det((1-i)\alpha^{*})=(1-i)^{L}\det\alpha^{*}\neq 0.

∎

With Lemma
3.2
one can isolate the term
G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)
in (
3.10
).

We define
inv
as in (
3.16
).For
\omega\in{}_{\mathrm{inv}}

3.10

\displaystyle G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)

\displaystyle=\left({{}_{k}^{-1}}^{*}[\delta_{k}+z\alpha_{k}^{*}]\right)^{-1}\left[{{}_{k}^{-1}}^{*}{}_{k-1}G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k-1,l)+zG_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+1,l)\right].

(3.17)

\displaystyle=[\delta_{k}+z\alpha_{k}^{*}]^{-1}\left[{{}_{k-1}G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k-1,l)+z{}_{k}^{-1}}^{*}G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k+1,l)\right].

Hence, to estimate the expectancy
\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)\|^{s}
in terms of

Proof.

of Lemma
1.6
. One has:

\mdmathbb{E}\left[\|(\delta_{k}+z\alpha_{k}^{*})^{-1}\|^{s}\right]=\int\|(-zV^{(k)}_{\omega}\alpha U^{(k)}_{\omega}+\alpha)^{-1}\|^{s}d\omega=\int\frac{1}{(\sigma_{L}(\alpha-zV^{(k)}_{\omega}\alpha U^{(k)}_{\omega}))^{s}}d\omega

Here,
\sigma_{L}
represents the
L^{\text{th}}
singular value, which is the smallest singular value. Then,

\forall k\in\mdmathbb{Z},\ \sigma_{L}\left(\alpha-zV^{(k)}_{\omega}\alpha U^{(k)}_{\omega}\right)=\sigma_{L}\left(\alpha-ze^{i\theta^{(k)}_{\omega}}\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega}\alpha\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega}e^{i\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega}}\right).

We can simplify some expressions. Indeed, we know that
\theta^{(k)}_{\omega}
,
\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega}
,
\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega}
, and
\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega}
are mutually independent. Furthermore,
\theta^{(k)}_{\omega}=\text{diag}(\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1},\ldots,\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,L})

are i.i.d.. The same goes for
(\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,j})_{j\in\llbracket 1,L\rrbracket}
. Consequently, we can replace
\theta^{(k)}_{\omega}
with
\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}
and
\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega}
with
\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}
in the expectancy (and not deterministically!), which gives:

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{E}\left(\sigma_{L}\left(\alpha-ze^{i\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega}}\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega}\alpha\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega}e^{i\theta^{(k)}_{\omega}}\right)^{-s}\right)

\displaystyle=\mdmathbb{E}\left(\sigma_{L}\left(\alpha-ze^{i\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}}\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega}\alpha\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega}e^{i\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}}\right)^{-s}\right)

\displaystyle=\mdmathbb{E}\left(\sigma_{L}\left(\alpha-ze^{i(\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}+\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1})}\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega}\alpha\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega}\right)^{-s}\right).

Since
\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega}
and
\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega}
are in

U(L)\cap H_{L}(\mdmathbb{C})
, hence one writes

and
\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega}=(\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}
to obtain:

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{E}\left(\sigma_{L}\left(\alpha-ze^{i(\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}+\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1})}\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega}\alpha\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega}\right)^{-s}\right)=\mdmathbb{E}\left(\sigma_{L}\left(\alpha-ze^{i(\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}+\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1})}(\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha(\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-s}\right)

\displaystyle=\mdmathbb{E}\left(\sigma_{L}\left(\left((\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{*}\alpha\left((\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{*}-ze^{i(\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}+\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1})}(\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha(\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-s}\right).

Since
(\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
(\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}
belong in particular to
H_{L}(\mdmathbb{C})
, we find that

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{E}\left(\sigma_{L}\left(\alpha-ze^{i(\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}+\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1})}\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega}\alpha\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega}\right)^{-s}\right)

\displaystyle=\mdmathbb{E}\left(\sigma_{L}\left(\left((\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha(\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}-ze^{i(\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}+\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1})}(\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha(\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-s}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mdmathbb{E}\left(\sigma_{L}\left((\widehat{V}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\alpha-ze^{i(\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}+\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1})}\alpha\right)(\widehat{U}^{(k)}_{\omega})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-s}\right)

\displaystyle=\mdmathbb{E}\left(\sigma_{L}\left((1-ze^{i(\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}+\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1})})\alpha\right)^{-s}\right).

Furthermore, for every
\omega\in\Omega
, every

and every

,

\sigma_{L}\left((1-ze^{i(\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}+\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1})})\alpha\right)=|1-ze^{i(\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}+\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1})}|\sigma_{L}(\alpha).

Therefore, we obtain the inequality for the partial expectation:

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{E}_{\theta,\Theta}[\|(\delta_{k}+z\alpha_{k}^{*})^{-1}\|^{s}]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,2\pi]\times[0,2\pi]}\frac{1}{|1-ze^{i(\Theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1}+\theta^{(k)}_{\omega,1})}|^{s}.(\sigma_{L}(\alpha))^{s}}d(\theta,\Theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\sigma_{L}(\alpha))^{s}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{|1-ze^{i\tilde{\theta}}|^{s}}d\tilde{\theta},

since

follows the uniform law on
\mdmathbb{S}^{1}

Ham07

∎

3.3.2.
Reduction to a suitable finite interval.

We will now prove that it suffices to bound the norm of the finite Green’s function moments for a restriction to a suitable finite interval to bound the Green’s function on the infinite volume. This reduction is based on comparing the blocks of the infinite matrix with those of the finite matrix. Let
a,b\in\mdmathbb{Z}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
,
a<b
.

Proposition 3.4
.

For
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
C(s,\epsilon)>0
such that

(3.20)

\mdmathbb{E}\left(\|G_{\omega}^{[a,b]}(z,k,l)\|^{s}\right)^{2}\leq C(s,\epsilon)\mdmathbb{E}\left(\|G_{\omega}^{[k,l]}(z,k,l)^{2s}\|\right)

for every
z\in\mdmathbb{S}_{\epsilon}
and all
k,l\in[a+1,b-1]
,
|k-l|>4
.

Proof.

We only deal with the case
k=2n,l=2m
where
|k-l|\geq 4
and
m,n\in\mdmathbb{Z}
, the other cases being treated the same way.

Using the definition of
\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{[a,b]}
, we have

(3.21)

\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{[a,b]}=\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{[a,2n-1]}\oplus\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{[2n,b]}+\Gamma_{n}^{e}

where
\Gamma_{n}^{e}
is given by

\Gamma_{n}^{e}(k,l)=\begin{cases}-\beta_{2n-2}\alpha+\beta_{2n-2}V,&k=2n-2,\ l=2n-1\\
-\beta_{2n-2}\beta_{2n-1},&k=2n-2,\ l=2n\\

, we use Theorem
1.3
to show that there exists
C_{3}>0
such that

\mdmathbb{E}\left[\left\|G_{\omega}(z,k,l)\right\|^{s_{0}}\right]\leq C_{3}e^{4\gamma}e^{-\gamma|k-l|}.

By choosing
C=\max\left\{{C}_{2},C_{3}e^{4\gamma}\right\}
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

4.
Dynamical Localization

In this Section we prove dynamical localization for
\{\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
and thus demonstrate the main result of the article, Theorem
1.2
. To do this, we will rely on two important aspects: firstly, the use of the exponential decay of the moments of the resolvent, already demonstrated in Section
3.5
, to obtain an estimate of the seond order moments of the resolvent; secondly, we connect the powers of the operator to its resolvent through a general lemma about unitary operators.

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in\Omega
. We use here the factorization (
3.3
) of
\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}=\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{D}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{V}_{\omega}^{\prime}\mdmathbb{W}_{\omega}
,
with the unitaries
\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mdmathbb{Z}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}&0\\
0&\widehat{V}^{(2k)}_{\omega}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
,
\mdmathbb{D}_{\omega}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mdmathbb{Z}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}&0\\
0&e^{i\theta^{(2k)}_{\omega}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\mdmathbb{V}_{\omega}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mdmathbb{Z}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\rho(\alpha)U^{(2k)}_{\omega}\\
\widetilde{\rho}(\alpha)&-\alpha^{*}U^{(2k)}_{\omega}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\circ s^{L}
.

We set
\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}=\mdmathbb{V}_{\omega}^{\prime}\mdmathbb{W}_{\omega}
and define, for
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{L})\in\mdmathbb{C}^{L}
,

(4.1)

\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}^{\delta}:=\bigoplus_{k\in\mdmathbb{Z}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}&0\\
0&\widehat{V}^{(2k)}_{\omega}e^{i(\theta^{(2k)}_{\omega}-\delta)}\end{pmatrix}\ \mbox{ and }\ \mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{\delta}:=\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}^{\delta}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}.

Recall that we denote by
(e_{\{n,l\}})_{n\in\mdmathbb{Z},l\in\{1,\ldots,L\}}
the canonical basis of
\ell^{2}(\mdmathbb{Z})\otimes\mdmathbb{C}^{L}
and let
P_{2n+1}
be the projection onto
\mathrm{Span}(e_{\{2n+1,1\}},..,e_{\{2n+1,L\}})
. Identifying
P_{2n+1}
with its matrix in the canonical basis, one has
P_{2n+1}=\mathrm{diag}(0,\ldots,0,\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}},0,\ldots,0)
with
\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}
in the
(2n+1)^{\text{th}}
position. The projection
P_{2n+1}
corresponds to the projection on the
(2n+1)^{\text{th}}
block. We define
\eta_{2n+1}=e^{i(\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega}-\delta)}-e^{i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega}}
to get:

(4.2)

\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{\delta}=\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}(\mdmathbb{D}_{\omega}+\eta_{2n+1}P_{2n+1})\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}=\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}+\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\eta_{2n+1}P_{2n+1}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}.

By the geometric resolvent:

\forall z\in\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\mdmathbb{S}^{1},\ (\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{\delta}-z)-(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}=-(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{\delta}-z)^{-1}\left[\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\eta_{2n+1}P_{2n+1}\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}\right](\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}.

We multiply on the left by
\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}
and on the right by
\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}
to get:

\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{\delta}-z)\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}-\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}=-\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{\delta}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}[\eta_{2n+1}P_{2n+1}]\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}.

We define
F_{\delta}(z):=\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{\delta}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}
and
F(z)=\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}
.
Then,

F_{\delta}(z)-F(z)=-F_{\delta}(z)(\eta_{2n+1}P_{2n+1})F(z).

For
\delta=(0,\ldots,\delta_{k},0,\ldots,0)
,
\eta_{2n+1}=\text{diag}(0,\ldots,e^{i(\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}-\delta_{k})}-e^{i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}},\ldots,0)
and we set
\eta_{2n+1,k}=e^{i(\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}-\delta_{k})}-e^{i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}
.
Furthermore,

\langle e_{\{2n+1,l\}}|(F_{\delta}(z)-F(z))e_{\{2n+1,k\}}\rangle=\langle e_{\{2n+1,l\}}|(-F_{\delta}(z)(\eta_{2n+1}P_{2n+1})F(z))e_{\{2n+1,k\}}\rangle.

Noting that, when focusing on block
2n+1
, we define
F_{\delta}(z,l,k):=F_{\delta}(z,\{2n+1,l\},\{2n+1,k\})
. Thus, for all
\delta
:

\displaystyle|F_{\delta}(z,k,k)|

\displaystyle=\left|\langle e_{\{2n+1,k\}}|F_{\delta}(z)e_{\{2n+1,k\}}\rangle\right|=\left|\langle e_{\{2n+1,k\}}|\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{\delta}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}e_{\{2n+1,k\}}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|e_{\{2n+1,k\}}\|\|\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{\delta}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\|\|e_{\{2n+1,k\}}\|=\|(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}^{\delta}-z)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{1-|z|}.

Then :

(4.3)

\displaystyle F_{\delta}(z,l,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{F(z,l,k)}{1+\eta_{2n+1,k}F(z,k,k)}=\frac{1}{\eta_{2n+1,k}+(F(z,k,k))^{-1}}.\frac{F(z,l,k)}{F(z,k,k)}.

But:
F(z,k,k)=\frac{1}{\eta_{2n+1,k}+(F(z,k,k))^{-1}}\times 1
and
1-|z|\leq|\eta_{2n+1,k}+(F(z,k,k))^{-1}|.
Thus, we also have
1-|\eta_{2n+1,k}+(F(z,k,k))^{-1}|\leq|z|.

Recall that
\eta_{2n+1,k}=e^{i(\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}-\delta_{k})}-e^{i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}

and introduce
\delta_{k}\in[0,2\pi]
such that:

e^{-i\delta_{k}}=\frac{1-e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}}{|1-e^{i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}|}.

Thus,

\displaystyle 1-\left|e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}\frac{1-e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}}{|1-e^{i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}|}-e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}+(F(z,k,k))^{-1}\right|\leq|z|

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 1-\left|\frac{1-e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}}{|1-e^{i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}|}-(1-e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1})\right|\leq|z|

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 1-\left|1-|1-e^{i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}|\right|\leq|z|.

Since
|1-e^{i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}|\leq 1
, we get:

1-\left|1-|1-e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}|\right|=1-(1-|e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}|)=|e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}|

and as
1-|z|^{2}\leq 1-|1-e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}F(z,k,k)|^{2}
and by
(
4.3
)
:

(4.4)

(1-|z|^{2})|F_{\delta}(l,k,z)|^{2}\leq\frac{1-\left|1-e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}\right|^{2}}{|\eta_{2n+1,k}+(F(z,k,k))^{-1}|^{2}}\frac{|F(z,l,k)|^{2}}{|F(z,k,k)|^{2}}.

By (
4.4
) and since for every
x\in\mdmathbb{R}
and every
s\in(0,1)
,
\min(1,|x|^{s})\leq|x|^{s},

(1-|z|^{2})|F_{\delta}(l,k,z)|^{2}\leq\frac{1-\left|1-e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}\right|^{2}}{|\eta_{2n+1,k}+(F(z,k,k))^{-1}|^{2}}\frac{|F(z,l,k)|^{s}}{|F(z,k,k)|^{s}}.

Given the definition of
\eta_{2n+1,k}
:

(1-|z|^{2})|F_{\delta}(l,k,z)|^{2}\leq\frac{1-\left|1-e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}\right|^{2}}{|e^{-i\delta}-(1-e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1})|^{2}}\frac{|F(z,l,k)|^{s}}{|F(z,k,k)|^{s}}

Let
y=1-e^{-i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(F(z,k,k))^{-1}
. Then
|y|<1
, and we can write:

(1-|z|^{2})|F_{\delta}(z,l,k)|^{s}=|e^{i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}(1-y)|^{s}=|1-y|^{s}.

We obtain:

(1-|z|^{2})|F_{\delta}(z,l,k)|^{2}\leq\frac{(1-|y|^{2})|1-y|^{s}}{|e^{-i\delta_{k}}-y|^{2}}|F(z,l,k)|^{s}.

By taking the expectation:

\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})|F(z,l,k)|^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})|F(z,l,k)|^{2}\right)d\delta.

Moreover, since the Haar measure on the unit circle is invariant by rotations, the random variables
e^{i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}-\delta_{k}}
and
e^{i\theta^{(2n+1)}_{\omega,k}}
have the same distribution.
Hence:

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})|F(z,l,k)|^{2}\right)d\delta=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})|F_{\delta}(z,l,k)|^{2}\right)d\delta.

By Fubini’s theorem, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})|F_{\delta}(z,l,k)|^{2}\right)d\delta=\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|F_{\delta}(z,l,k)|^{2}d\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mdmathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sup_{|y|<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{(1-|y|^{2})|1-y|^{s}}{|e^{-i\delta}-y|^{2}}|F(z,l,k)|^{s}d\delta\right)\leq 2^{s}\mdmathbb{E}\left(|F(z,l,k)|^{s}\times\sup_{|y|<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{(1-|y|^{2})}{|e^{-i\delta}-y|^{2}}d\delta\right)

since
|1-y|^{s}\leq 2^{s}
for
y\in\mdmathbb{C}
such that
|y|<1
. Using
[
HJS09
, Eq. (5.24)]
,

\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})|F(z,k,l)|^{2}\right)\leq 2^{s}\mdmathbb{E}\left(|F(z,k,l)|^{s}\right)\text{ for all }s\in(0,1).

Using the band structure of
\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}^{*}
, we have:

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{E}\left(|F(z,l,k)|^{s}\right)

\displaystyle=\mdmathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle e_{\{m,l\}}\mid\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}e_{\{2n+1,k\}}\rangle\right|^{s}\right)

\displaystyle=\mdmathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}^{*}e_{\{m,l\}}\mid(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}e_{\{2n+1,k\}}\rangle\right|^{s}\right).

We then replace
e_{\{m,j\}}
with its original expression in the canonical basis (
i.e.

e_{m+Lj}
), apply Minkowski’s inequality and use the fact that the coefficients of
\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}
are bounded to write:

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{E}\left(|F(z,l,k)|^{s}\right)\leq C_{1}(s)\sum_{|j-l|\leq 4L}\mdmathbb{E}\left(\left|\langle\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}^{*}e_{m+Lj}|(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}e_{\{2n+1,k\}}\rangle\right|^{s}\right).

Since
\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}
is block diagonal with unitary blocks in
U(L)
,

\forall m,n\in\mdmathbb{Z},\forall j,k\in\{1,\ldots,L\},\ \left|\langle e_{\{mL+j\}}\mid(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}e_{\{2n+1,k\}}\rangle\right|\leq\|G_{\omega}(z,\tilde{m},2m+1)\|^{s}

where
\tilde{m}
is the index of the block containing
mL+j
and equals
\left[\frac{mL+j}{L}\right].

Thus

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{E}\left(|F(z,l,k)|^{s}\right)\leq C_{1}(s)\sum_{|j-l|\leq 4L}\mdmathbb{E}\left(\|G_{\omega}(z,\left[\tfrac{mL+j}{L}\right],2m+1)\|^{s}\right).

Now, we need to connect
\ \mdmathbb{E}\left(|F(z,l,k)|^{s}\right)
and
\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})\left|\langle e_{\{m,l\}}|\left(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z\right)^{-1}e_{\{2n+1,k\}}\rangle\right|^{2}\right).

Since
\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}
is a direct sum of matrices of the form
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{L}}&0\\
0&\widehat{V}^{(2k)}_{\omega}e^{i\theta^{(2k)}_{\omega}}\end{smallmatrix}\right),
we consider two cases. The first case is when
k
is an even block. Then
\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}e_{k}=e_{k}
and since
\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}
is unitary, we find:

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left((1-|z|^{2})\left|\langle e_{l}|\left(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z\right)^{-1}e_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}\right)=\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})\left|\langle e_{l}|\left(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z\right)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}e_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})\left|\langle\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}e_{l}|\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}\left(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z\right)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}e_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}\right)=\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})\left|\langle\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}e_{l}|F(z)e_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}\right).

In the case where
k
is an odd block,
\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}e_{k}=\widehat{V}^{(2k)}_{\omega}e^{i\theta^{(2k)}_{\omega}}e_{k}
, and we have:

\displaystyle\left|\langle e_{l}|\left(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z\right)^{-1}e_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}=\left|\langle\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}e_{l}|\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}\left(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z\right)^{-1}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}^{*}e_{\{m,k\}}\rangle\right|^{2}=\left|\langle\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}e_{l}|F(z)\mdmathbb{Y}_{\omega}^{*}e_{\{m,k\}}\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{j=1}^{L}\langle\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}e_{l}|F(z)((\widehat{V}^{(m)}_{\omega}e^{i\theta^{(m)}_{\omega}})^{*})_{j,k}e_{\{m,j\}}\rangle\right|^{2}=\left|\sum_{j=1}^{L}((\widehat{V}^{(m)}_{\omega}e^{i\theta^{(m)}_{\omega}})^{*})_{j,k}\langle\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}e_{l}|F(z)e_{\{m,j\}}\rangle\right|^{2}

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left|\langle e_{l}|\left(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z\right)^{-1}e_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left|((\widehat{V}^{(m)}_{\omega})^{*})_{j,k}\right|^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left|\langle\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}e_{l}|F(z)e_{\{m,j\}}\rangle\right|^{2}\right)

Since
\widehat{V}^{(m)}_{\omega}
is a unitary matrix, its
k^{\mathrm{th}}
column is of norm
1
and
\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left|((\widehat{V}^{(m)}_{\omega})^{*})_{j,k}\right|^{2}=1.
Hence,

\left|\langle e_{l}|\left(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z\right)^{-1}e_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left|\langle\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}e_{l}|F(z)e_{\{m,j\}}\rangle\right|^{2}.

Therefore, for
k=mL+j
,

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})\left|\langle e_{l}|\left(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z\right)^{-1}e_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})\left|\langle\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}e_{l}|F(z)e_{\{m,j\}}\rangle\right|^{2}\right)

Due to the band structure of the operator
\mdmathbb{X}_{\omega}
, there exists a constant
C_{2}(s)
such that:

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})\left|\langle e_{l}|\left(\mdmathbb{U}_{\omega}-z\right)^{-1}e_{k}\rangle\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(s)\sum_{|p-q|\leq 4L}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mdmathbb{E}\left((1-|z|^{2})\left|F(z,p,j)\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(s)\ 2^{s}\sum_{|p-q|\leq 4L}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mdmathbb{E}\left(\left|F(z,p,j)\right|^{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(s)C_{2}(s)

\displaystyle\ 2^{s}\sum_{|p-l|\leq 4L}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\sum_{|q-p|\leq 4L}\mdmathbb{E}\left(\left\|G_{\omega}\left(z,\left[\frac{q}{L}\right],\left[\frac{mL+j}{L}\right]\right)\right\|^{s}\right).

We conclude the proof with the exponential decay of
\mdmathbb{E}(\|G_{\omega}(z,q,mL+j)\|^{s})
given by Theorem
1.8
and the fact that each of the three sums has a finite number of terms.
∎

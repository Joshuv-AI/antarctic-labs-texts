Harmonic Means of Wishart Random Matrices
Asad Lodhia

Abstract
. We use free probability to compute the limiting spectral properties of the harmonic mean of n n i.i.d. Wishart random matrices 𝐖 i \mathbf{W}_{i} whose limiting aspect ratio is γ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \gamma\in(0,1) when 𝔼 ⁡ [ 𝐖 i ] = 𝐈 \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{W}_{i}]=\mathbf{I} . We demonstrate an interesting phenomenon where the harmonic mean 𝐇 \mathbf{H} of the n n Wishart matrices is closer in operator norm to 𝔼 ⁡ [ 𝐖 i ] \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{W}_{i}] than the arithmetic mean 𝐀 \mathbf{A} for small n n , after which the arithmetic mean is closer. We also prove some results for the general case where the expectation of the Wishart matrices are not the identity matrix.



1.
Introduction

Positive definite random matrices are often studied in probability
theory and statistics. The most famous (and arguably most widely used)
matrix model supported on the set of positive semidefinite matrices is
the Wishart ensemble. Let
\{\mathbf{X}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a sequence of
centered independent identically distributed matrices that have
dimension
P\times N
whose entries have at least two finite moments.
Suppose each column of
\mathbf{X}_{i}
is an independent
P
-dimensional random
vector. The matrices

\mathbf{W}_{i}:=\frac{\mathbf{X}_{i}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{*}}{N}

are called Wishart matrices. If the columns of each
\mathbf{X}_{i}
are i.i.d. observations from a Gaussian distribution it suffices to specify their
covariance matrix
\mathbf{\Sigma}=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{W}_{i}]
to obtain their distribution.
In statistics the estimation of such a covariance matrix is a
fundamental task. Our interest in this paper will be the mathematical
study of estimates in operator norm of the covariance in the
high-dimensional regime
\frac{P}{N}\to\gamma\in(0,1)
.

The notational choice in the previous paragraph may seem odd to the
reader. If the columns of
\mathbf{X}_{i}
are drawn i.i.d., one may combine
them, say by computing the arithmetic mean

\mathbf{A}:=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{W}_{i}}{n}.

(1)

This reduces the variance by a factor of
n^{-1}
and is
equivalent to adjoining the columns of the
\mathbf{X}_{i}
into a single

P
-by-
Nn
matrix, since

\mathbf{A}=\frac{\big[\mathbf{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{X}_{n}\big]\big[\mathbf{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{X}_{n}\big]^{*}}{Nn}.

In the regime where
P/N\to\gamma\in(0,1)
the sample
covariance matrix
\mathbf{W}_{i}
does not converge to its expected value

\mathbf{\Sigma}
. Instead, when
\mathbb{E}\mathbf{W}_{i}=\mathbf{I}
, the spectral measure of
each
\mathbf{W}_{i}
satisfies the Marčenko-Pastur Law with parameter

\gamma
:

\rho_{\mathrm{MP},\gamma}(x):=\frac{\sqrt{\big((1+\sqrt{\gamma})^{2}-x\big)\big(x-(1-\sqrt{\gamma})^{2}\big)}}{2\pi\gamma x}\mathbf{1}_{\big[(1-\sqrt{\gamma})^{2},(1+\sqrt{\gamma})^{2}\big]}(x).

(2)

In fact, under sufficient moment conditions
[
16
]
, we have the
stronger result that

\|\mathbf{W}_{i}-\mathbf{I}\|\to\gamma+2\sqrt{\gamma}\quad\mathrm{a.s.},

where
\|\mathbf{M}\|
represents the operator norm of the matrix
\mathbf{M}
. It
is important to note here that the value of the operator norm in this
particular case is due to the right-edge of the spectrum of the
Marčenko-Pastur Law. Subtraction of the matrix
\mathbf{I}
shifts all of
the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{W}_{i}
exactly by one and the eigenvalue with
largest absolute value is at the right edge of the spectrum.
Heuristically, our error is due to overestimating the largest
eigenvalue. When we average the
\mathbf{W}_{i}
resulting operator norm bound
becomes

\|\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{I}\|\to\frac{\gamma}{n}+2\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{n}}\qquad\hbox{a.s.}

The above limit follows from our interpretation of the arithmetic mean
as a sample covariance matrix with aspect ratio
P/Nn\to\gamma/n
.
Notice that the change in the operator norm error is not simply a
rescaling by
n^{-1/2}
, even though the entrywise variance has changed
by
n^{-1}
. The purpose of this paper is to explore an alternative to
the arithmetic mean that takes into account the positive definiteness
of
\mathbf{W}_{i}
when
P<N
.

The space of positive definite matrices is a cone and has a natural
partial ordering. When
\mathbf{M}_{1}
and
\mathbf{M}_{2}
are
P\times P
positive
semidefinite matrices, we say

\mathbf{M}_{1}\preceq\mathbf{M}_{2}

if and only if
\mathbf{M}_{2}-\mathbf{M}_{1}
is positive semidefinite. Under this
ordering one can show various generalizations of classical
inequalities. Of particular interest in this paper, if
\mathbf{M}_{1}
,

\ldots
,
\mathbf{M}_{k}
are positive definite (and therefore invertible),
the classic arithmetic mean harmonic mean (AMHM) generalizes as

[
9
, Theorem 1]

k\Big(\mathbf{M}_{1}^{-1}+\cdots+\mathbf{M}_{k}^{-1}\Big)^{-1}\preceq\frac{\mathbf{M}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{M}_{k}}{k}.

(3)

The matrix on the left,

k\Big(\mathbf{M}_{1}^{-1}+\cdots+\mathbf{M}_{k}^{-1}\Big)^{-1},

is the harmonic mean of
\mathbf{M}_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\mathbf{M}_{k}
. This paper shows
that
\mathbf{A}
can give
worse
estimates in operator norm than the
matrix harmonic mean

\mathbf{H}:=n\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{W}_{i}^{-1}\bigg)^{-1}.

(4)

When
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{W}_{i}]=\mathbf{I}
, we show for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
and the
operator norm of
\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{I}
is always smaller than
\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{I}
when

n=2
. For general
n\geq 2
, this advantage disappears when
n

exceeds a critical value
n^{*}(\gamma)
that is a function only of

\gamma
.

A heuristic explanation of this result is the AMHM inequality
\mathbf{H}\preceq\mathbf{A}
. We know from our discussion above that
\mathbf{A}
is, in
some sense, an overestimate of its expectation
\mathbf{I}
. By taking a
matrix smaller in the positive definite cone, we are compensating for
this overestimation. As will be shown below,
\|\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{I}\|
will be the
absolute value of the
smallest
eigenvalue of
\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{I}
, so

\mathbf{H}

underestimates

\mathbf{I}
. When
n
is large, the spectral
measure of
\mathbf{H}
approaches a point mass at
(1-\gamma)

whereas the spectral measure of
\mathbf{A}
approaches a point mass
at
1
(the spectral measure of
\mathbf{I}
). This explains why
eventually, for
n
large enough,
\mathbf{A}
is a better estimate.

The analysis presented in this paper
is complete for the case where
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{W}_{i}]=\mathbf{I}
but
we will be able to comment on Wishart matrices with general non-singular
covariance, by the observation that if
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{W}_{i}]=\mathbf{I}
, then

\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{W}_{i}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]=\mathbf{\Sigma}.

This fact implies that
for both the arithmetic and harmonic mean we simply need to multiply
on both sides by
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1/2}
to get the arithmetic and
harmonic mean of a Wishart matrix with a general covariance
\mathbf{\Sigma}
.
With some conditions on
\mathbf{\Sigma}
, we can ensure that
the result sketched above still holds in this more general case.

Notation

In this paper
\mathbf{I}
will be the identity matrix,
its dimension will be clear from the context. For a matrix
\mathbf{M}
,

\|\mathbf{M}\|
will always denote its operator norm and
\mathbf{M}^{*}
its
conjugate transpose. Given a set
A
, the function
\mathbf{1}_{A}(x)

is the indicator function associated to that set. For a unital

C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, the norm will be denoted
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}}
, the
unit element will be denoted
1_{\mathcal{A}}
and
*
will denote the
involution.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

\ldots

\lim_{P,N\to\infty}\|\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{I}\|\to\gamma-\frac{2\gamma}{n}+2\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{n}}\sqrt{1-\gamma+\frac{\gamma}{n}}\quad\mathrm{a.s.}

Remark 1
.

Note that for small

\lim_{P,N\to\infty}\|\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{I}\|=\gamma-\frac{2\gamma}{n}+2\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{n}}\sqrt{1-\gamma+\frac{\gamma}{n}}<\frac{\gamma}{n}+2\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{n}}=\lim_{P,N\to\infty}\|\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{I}\|,

the inequality is always true for

As mentioned in the Introduction,

Another natural question is whether the results above carry over to the
case where
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{W}_{i}]\neq\mathbf{I}
. A simple submultiplicativity argument
combined with the above Theorem gives the following result:

Corollary 2.1.1
.

Assume
n\leq n^{*}(\gamma)
. Let
\mathbf{\Sigma}
be a sequence of deterministic

P\times P
positive definite covariance matrices (with
P
-dependence
suppressed) such that

\limsup_{P,N\to\infty}\frac{\|\mathbf{\Sigma}\|\|\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}\|\|\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{I}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{I}\|}<1,

then

\limsup_{P,N\to\infty}\frac{\|\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{\Sigma}\|}{\|\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{\Sigma}\|}<1\qquad\mathrm{a.s.}

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{\Sigma}
are positive definite, we have

\big\|\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{\Sigma}\|\quad\hbox{and}\quad\big\|\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|^{2}=\big\|\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}\big\|.

Now, by submultiplicativity of the operator norm, it follows that

\big\|\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{\Sigma}\big\|\leq\big\|\mathbf{\Sigma}\big\|\|\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{I}\|\leq\big\|\mathbf{\Sigma}\big\|\big\|\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}\big\|\frac{\|\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{I}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{I}\|}\|\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{\Sigma}\|,

since with probability one
\mathbf{A}\neq\mathbf{I}
we know the quantity on the
right is non-zero. Hence we can rearrange to obtain the inequality

\frac{\|\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{\Sigma}\|}{\|\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{\Sigma}\|}\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{\Sigma}\|\|\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}\|\|\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{I}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{I}\|},

now taking the
\limsup
of both sides yields the required result.
∎

Free Probability Theory

In order to prove the main results of this paper, we require some
tools from the theory of free probability. Free probability is a
generalization of classical probability invented by Dan Voiculescu in
the 1980s for the purpose of investigating some properties of operator
algebras
[
13
]
. We require this theory because the sequence of

\{\mathbf{W}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
given in Definition
1
behave as
the “joint law” of a collection of non-commutative random variables
(see Definition
3
). In Section
5

we will use this fact to directly compute the limiting spectral
measure of the harmonic mean
\mathbf{H}
. Our primary references for the
exposition in this section are
[
1
, Chapter 5]
and

[
6
, Chapters 1–7]
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}},*)
denote a unital
C^{*}
-algebra with
involution
*
. This means
\mathcal{A}
is a complex vector space equipped
with a complete norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}}
(i.e.,
\mathcal{A}
is a Banach space),
a bilinear product

(x,y)\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\mapsto xy\in\mathcal{A}.

and a unit element

1_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{A}\hbox{ such that }1_{\mathcal{A}}x=x,\hbox{ for all }x\in\mathcal{A}.

\mathcal{A}
is a unital Banach algebra if in addition the norm satisfies

\displaystyle\|1_{\mathcal{A}}\|_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\|ab\|_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\leq\|a\|_{\mathcal{A}}\|b\|_{\mathcal{A}}.

When
\mathcal{A}
has an involution operation

a\in\mathcal{A}\mapsto a^{*}\in\mathcal{A}\quad\hbox{and}\quad(a^{*})^{*}=a

which satisfies for all
a
,
b\in\mathcal{A}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle(a+b)^{*}

\displaystyle=a^{*}+b^{*},

\displaystyle(ab)^{*}

\displaystyle=b^{*}a^{*},

\displaystyle(\lambda a)^{*}

\displaystyle=\bar{\lambda}a^{*},

\displaystyle\|a^{*}a\|_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\|a\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}},

then we say that
\mathcal{A}
is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. An element
a\in\mathcal{A}
of a
C^{*}
-algebra is invertible if there exists a
b

ab=ba=1_{\mathcal{A}}
. Notice that the algebraic structure of

\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
.

If
\mathcal{A}

\mathrm{spec}(a)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:(\lambda 1_{\mathcal{A}}-a)\hbox{ is

identical to the definition of a positive-semidefinite matrix.

3

A sequence of non-commutative random variables

\ldots
,

,
\ldots
,

,

,

\ldots
,

,
\ldots
,

.

The random matrices
\mathbf{W}_{i}

-probability space

(6)

We define the
\mathfrak{p}_{j}
and the state

Definition 3
.

Let

,

In the next section, we will use a result from

(7)

Lemma 3.1
.

The non-commutative random variable
\mathfrak{h}
in (
7

well-defined and can be approximated by a sequence of non-commutative
polynomials in
\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{n}\}
.

Proof.

A simple proof of this property comes directly from the fact that each
of our

are represented as bounded linear operators on a
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Since their spectral measure is

\rho_{\mathrm{MP},\gamma}
which is supported on the positive reals,
they are all invertible so each
\mathfrak{p}_{j}
is invertible and so is the
sum

\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{-1}+\cdots+\mathfrak{p}_{n}^{-1}.

We may approximate
\mathfrak{h}
with non-commutative polynomials in

\mathfrak{p}_{i}
by utilizing the Neumann series. Let

\Delta:=\max\big\{\|\mathfrak{p}_{i}\|_{\mathcal{F}},\|\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{F}},\|\mathfrak{h}\|_{\mathcal{F}},\|\mathfrak{h}^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}\big\},

now consider the partial sum of the geometric series

\frac{1}{2\Delta}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\bigg(1_{\mathcal{F}}-\frac{\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}{2\Delta}\bigg)^{k}

by definition of
\Delta
, along with usual bounds on geometric series
we have

\bigg\|\mathfrak{h}-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\bigg(1_{\mathcal{F}}-\frac{\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}{2\Delta}\bigg)^{k}\bigg\|_{\mathcal{F}}=\bigg\|\mathfrak{h}\bigg(1_{\mathcal{F}}-\frac{\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}{2\Delta}\bigg)^{m+1}\bigg\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\frac{\Delta}{2^{m+1}},

which goes to

as

. Similarly, each
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{-1}
can
be expanded as the infinite series

\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{-1}=\frac{1}{2\Delta}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1_{\mathcal{F}}-\frac{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}{2\Delta}\bigg)^{k},

with similar error bounds as the expansion for
\mathfrak{h}
. Since

\mathfrak{h}^{-1}
is the sum of
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{-1}
we need only insert the

\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{-1}
into the truncated

\mathfrak{h}
to get a non-commutative polynomial in

\mathfrak{p}_{i}
that approximates
\mathfrak{h}
in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{F}}
.
∎

4.
Strong Convergence of the Harmonic Mean

The following Theorem from

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\{\mathbf{W}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}

In order for this theorem to imply our desired results, we will use the
fact that
\mathbf{H}
can be approximated by polynomials in the matrices

\mathbf{W}_{i}
. A concentration bound on the largest eigenvalues of both

\mathbf{W}_{i}
and
\mathbf{W}_{i}^{-1}

Lemma for matrices satisfying Definition
1
and

2

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\{\mathbf{W}_{i}\}
satisfy Definition
1
or
Definition
2

\kappa>0
that depends only on

parameter of the entries of

\sum_{P=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(B_{P})<\infty.

Proof.

For any

:

\mathbb{P}\big(\big\{\max\big(\|\mathbf{W}_{i}\|,\|\mathbf{W}_{i}^{-1}\|,\|\mathbf{H}\|,\|\mathbf{H}^{-1}\|\big)>t\big\}\big)\\
\leq n\mathbb{P}\big(\{\|\mathbf{W}_{1}\|>t\}\big)+n\mathbb{P}\big(\{\|\mathbf{W}_{1}^{-1}\|>t\}\big)+\mathbb{P}\big(\{\|\mathbf{H}\|>t\}\big)+\mathbb{P}\big(\{\|\mathbf{H}^{-1}\|>t\}\big),

By the AMHM inequality in (
3

\{\|\mathbf{H}\|>t\}\subset\{\|\mathbf{A}\|>t\}

so the triangle inequality and union bound applied to
\|\mathbf{A}\|
gives

\mathbb{P}\big(\{\|\mathbf{H}\|>t\}\big)\leq n\mathbb{P}\big(\{\|\mathbf{W}_{1}\|>t\}\big).

The triangle inequality and a union bound also yield

\mathbb{P}\big(\{\|\mathbf{H}^{-1}\|>t\}\big)\leq n\mathbb{P}\big(\{\|\mathbf{W}_{1}^{-1}\|>t\}\big),

so we have

\mathbb{P}\big(\big\{\max\big(\|\mathbf{W}_{i}\|,\|\mathbf{W}_{i}^{-1}\|,\|\mathbf{H}\|,\|\mathbf{H}^{-1}\|\big)>t\big\}\big)\\
\leq 2n\mathbb{P}\big(\{\|\mathbf{W}_{1}\|>t\}\big)+2n\mathbb{P}\big(\{\|\mathbf{W}_{1}^{-1}\|>t\}\big).

(8)

There are several methods to bound the first probability

One is an
\epsilon
-net argument that is

\epsilon
-net argument given in

12

2

1
with

We take more care to bound the second probability on the right-hand

since this is equal to

For the model in Definition

&&L_{2}&D_{2(N-P+1)}\end{bmatrix}

\min_{j\leq P-2}\frac{1}{N}\Big(L_{2(P-j)}^{2}+D^{2}_{2(N-j)}-(D_{2(N-j+1)}L_{2(P-j)}+D_{2(N-j)}L_{2(P-j-1)})\Big)\bigg],

For the model in Definition

\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big\{\sqrt{N}\|\mathbf{W}_{i}^{-1}\|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\epsilon\big(\sqrt{N}-\sqrt{P-1}\big)\Big\}\Big)\leq(C_{2}\epsilon)^{N-P+1}+\exp(-c_{2}N)

Lemma 4.3

Remark 3
.

Note that the proof of this Lemma is restricted to matrices satisfying

1
only due to the application of

Proof.

We first show for any monomial

It suffices to prove the above convergence for the monomial

since for matrices

\|\mathbf{M}_{1}^{k}-\mathbf{M}_{2}^{k}\|\leq\|\mathbf{M}_{1}-\mathbf{M}_{2}\|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|\mathbf{M}_{1}\|^{j}\|\mathbf{M}_{2}\|^{k-j}

By Lemma

\mathbf{H}=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\mathbf{I}-\frac{\mathbf{H}^{-1}}{2\kappa}\bigg)^{l},

Note that on

The inverse of

\mathbf{H}^{-1}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{W}_{j}^{-1}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2\kappa}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\mathbf{I}-\frac{\mathbf{W}_{j}}{2\kappa}\bigg)^{l},

It follows that there is a sequence of non-commutative polynomials

This implies that on

\mathbf{H}
.
Furthermore, for each

\lim_{N,P\to\infty}\|Q_{d}(\mathbf{W}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{W}_{n})\|\to\|Q_{d}(\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{n})\|,

is identical to the one described above and

is replaced by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Next, we work on the event

\mathbb{P}(B_{P})
implies
\mathbb{P}(\limsup B_{P})=0
. As

\begin{split}\big|\|\mathbf{H}\|-\|\mathfrak{h}\|_{\mathcal{F}}\big|\leq&\big|\|\mathbf{H}\|-\|Q_{d}(\mathbf{W}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{W}_{n})\|\big|\\

Since

The second term vanishes as

. For any

5.
Harmonic Mean of Free Poisson Random Variables

In Sections
3
and
4
we proved that
the limiting spectral measure of
\mathbf{H}
is the law of the
non-commutative random variable
\mathfrak{h}
. Additionally, we proved

\|\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{I}\|\to\|\mathfrak{h}-1_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{\mathcal{F}}\quad\mathrm{a.s.}

We can conclude the proof of Theorem
2.1
by computing
the distribution of
\mathfrak{h}
and the value of
\|\mathfrak{h}-1_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{\mathcal{F}}
,
which follows from a now standard type of calculation from free
probability theory, which is called
additive free convolution

[
14
]
.

Let
\sigma
be a compactly supported probability measure on

\mathbb{R}
. The Cauchy-Stieltjes transform of
\sigma
is denoted

m_{\sigma}(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sigma(\diff x)}{z-x}.

Let
K_{\sigma}(z)
be the functional inverse of
m_{\sigma}(z)
.
We define the
R
-transform of
\sigma
as

R_{\sigma}(z):=K_{\sigma}(z)-\frac{1}{z}.

For two compactly supported probability measures
\sigma_{1}
and

\sigma_{2}
, on
\mathbb{R}
, the additive free convolution of
\sigma_{1}
and

\sigma_{2}
, denoted
\sigma_{1}\boxplus\sigma_{2}
, is the unique
probability measure obtained by the relation

R_{\sigma_{1}\boxplus\sigma_{2}}(z)=R_{\sigma_{1}}(z)+R_{\sigma_{2}}(z).

The additive free convolution is significant in free probability
because if
\mu_{a}
and
\mu_{b}
are the laws of two freely independent
non-commutative random variables
a
and
b
, respectively, then the
law of the non-commutative random variable
a+b
is the measure
\mu_{a}\boxplus\mu_{b}
. Note that for notational ease in what follows, we
use
R_{a}
to denote
R
-transform of the (compactly supported)
measure that is the law of the non-commutative random variable
a
.
Here, we use the additive free convolution to compute the law of

\mathfrak{h}
via the following steps:

(1)

We use the Cauchy-Stieltjes transform of each
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
to
compute the Cauchy-Stieltjes transform of
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{-1}
. The fixed
point equation for the Cauchy-Stieltjes transform of
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is a
quadratic equation. This results in a fixed point equation for

\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{-1}
which is also a quadratic equation.

(2)

Using the definition of the
R
-transform above, we obtain a
quadratic fixed point equation for the
R
-transform of each

\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{-1}
.

(3)

Because each
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is freely independent of any other

\mathfrak{p}_{j}
for
i\neq j
,
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{-1}
is freely independent of

\mathfrak{p}_{j}^{-1}
. We may now compute the
R
-transform

R_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{-1}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus\mathfrak{p}_{n}^{-1}}(z)=nR_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)

where
\mathfrak{p}
has the same law as all of the
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
.

(4)

With the
R
-transform of
\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{-1}+\cdots+\mathfrak{p}_{n}^{-1}
in hand, we compute the Cauchy-Stieltjes transform of

\mathfrak{h}
. As a consequence of steps 1–3, this function satisfies a
quadratic fixed point equation which can be solved.

(5)

We invert the Cauchy-Stieltjes transform of
\mathfrak{h}
using the
usual Plemelj inversion formula. This gives the law of
\mathfrak{h}
,
which upon shifting by
1
and using faithfulness of the state
\nu

yields the operator norm of
\mathfrak{h}-1_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Our approach in the calculations outlined above is from the paper

[
7
]
, which provides a general framework for computing various
transforms for non-commutative random variables whose Stieltjes
transforms satisfy polynomial equations. In our case, each

m_{\mathfrak{p}}
satisfies the following fixed point equation
[
5
]

\gamma zm_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)(1-z-\gamma)+1=0.

(10)

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Denote the Cauchy-Stieltjes transform of the law of each

\mathfrak{p}_{i}
by

m_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{MP},\gamma}(\diff x)}{z-x}.

To obtain the law of
\mathfrak{h}
, we first compute the law of

n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{-1},

which is the additive free convolution of the
n
freely independent
random variables
\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{-1}\}
. Since they all have the same
parameter
\gamma>0
, we need only compute, for a fixed
\mathfrak{p}
with
the same law as
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, the
R
-transform

R_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}(z)=nR_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z).

(11)

With the law of
n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}
in hand, we simply invert and rescale
to obtain the law of
\mathfrak{h}
, which allows us to compute the value of

\|\mathfrak{h}-1_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{\mathcal{F}}
.

The law of
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}
is the push-forward measure of

\rho_{\mathrm{MP},\gamma}
by the mapping
x\mapsto\frac{1}{x}
.
We denote this measure by
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}
.
Using the push-forward, we have

\displaystyle m_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mu_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(\diff x)}{z-\frac{1}{x}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{x\mu_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(\diff x)}{xz-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{z^{2}}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{z}-x}\bigg\}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(\diff x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{z^{2}}m_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}\bigg(\frac{1}{z}\bigg),

rearranging and replacing
z
with
\frac{1}{z}
yields

m_{\mathfrak{p}}\bigg(\frac{1}{z}\bigg)=z-z^{2}m_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z).

(12)

The fixed point equation (
10
) for
m_{\mathfrak{p}}
can be
rewritten as

\frac{\gamma}{z}m_{\mathfrak{p}}\bigg(\frac{1}{z}\bigg)^{2}+\bigg(1-\gamma-\frac{1}{z}\bigg)m_{\mathfrak{p}}\bigg(\frac{1}{z}\bigg)+1=0,

(13)

inserting (
12
) into (
13
) yields

\gamma z\big(1-zm_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)\big)^{2}+\big(z(1-\gamma)-1\big)\big(1-zm_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)\big)+1=0,

which when simplified yields

\gamma z^{2}m_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)^{2}-m_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)\big[z(1+\gamma)-1\big]+1=0.

(14)

Equation (
14
) yields the following equation
when for
K_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)

\gamma z^{2}K_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)^{2}-(1+\gamma)zK_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)+z+1=0,

substituting the
R
-transform
R_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)
gives the equation

\gamma z^{2}\bigg(R_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)+\frac{1}{z}\bigg)^{2}-(1+\gamma)z\bigg(R_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)+\frac{1}{z}\bigg)+z+1=0,

and simplifying further gives

\gamma zR_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)^{2}+(\gamma-1)R_{\mathfrak{p}^{-1}}(z)+1=0.

(15)

Using the additive convolution formula (
11
) in
(
15
) gives

\gamma zR_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}(z)^{2}+n(\gamma-1)R_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}(z)+n^{2}=0.

(16)

We will solve for
m_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}
, by reversing the procedure we
performed above to obtain the
R
-transform given the Stieltjes
transform. Inserting the definition of the
R
-transform into
(
16
) gives

\gamma z\bigg(K_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}(z)-\frac{1}{z}\bigg)^{2}+n(\gamma-1)\bigg(K_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}(z)-\frac{1}{z}\bigg)+n^{2}=0,

simplifying this gives

\gamma z^{2}K_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}(z)^{2}+\big\{(n-2)\gamma-n\big\}zK_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}(z)+n^{2}z-(n-1)\gamma+n=0,

so that

\gamma z^{2}m_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}(z)^{2}+\big\{(n-2)\gamma-n\big\}zm_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}(z)+n^{2}\tilde{m}(z)-(n-1)\gamma+n=0,

changing variables
z\mapsto\frac{1}{z}
gives

\frac{\gamma}{z^{2}}m_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}\bigg(\frac{1}{z}\bigg)^{2}+\bigg\{\frac{(n-2)\gamma-n}{z}+n^{2}\bigg\}m_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}\bigg(\frac{1}{z}\bigg)-(n-1)\gamma+n=0.

(17)

As in the pushforward calculation that gave (
12
)
before, we have the relationship

m_{n\mathfrak{h}^{-1}}\bigg(\frac{1}{z}\bigg)=z-z^{2}m_{n^{-1}\mathfrak{h}}(z),

which when substituted into (
17
) gives

\gamma(1-zm_{n^{-1}\mathfrak{h}}(z))^{2}+\big\{(n-2)\gamma-n+n^{2}z\big\}(1-zm_{n^{-1}\mathfrak{h}}(z))-(n-1)\gamma+n=0

which simplifies to the quadratic

\gamma zm_{n^{-1}\mathfrak{h}}(z)^{2}+n\{1-\gamma-nz\}m_{n^{-1}\mathfrak{h}}(z)+n^{2}=0,

rescaling the law of
n^{-1}\mathfrak{h}
gives the final equation for

m_{\mathfrak{h}}
:

\frac{\gamma z}{n}m_{\mathfrak{h}}(z)^{2}+\{1-\gamma-z\}m_{\mathfrak{h}}(z)+1=0.

(18)

The solution to the quadratic equation (
18
)
is

m_{\mathfrak{h}}(z)=\frac{n\Big\{z-1+\gamma-\sqrt{z^{2}-2z(1-\gamma+\frac{2\gamma}{n})+(1-\gamma)^{2}}\Big\}}{2\gamma z},

(19)

where the branch cut of the square root has been taken to be the
positive real line. We have chosen this particular root of the
quadratic due to the decay condition
m_{\mathfrak{h}}(z)\sim\frac{1}{z}
as

z\to\infty
and the requirement that
m_{\mathfrak{h}}(z)
must be complex
analytic off the real line. See, for example,
[
11
, §2.4.3]
for a
more detailed calculation (for Wigner matrices) that explains the
selection of the branch cut when solving fixed point equations for the
Stieltjes transform. See
[
2
, §3.3]
for a derivation of the
MP-law using these techniques. To recover the law
\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}
from
the above Stieltjes transform, we follow the usual inversion formula,
which appears in
[
1
, Theorem 2.4.3]
,

\lim_{y\to 0}-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{a}^{b}\Im m_{\mathfrak{h}}(x+iy)\diff x=\int_{a}^{b}\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}(\diff x),

where
a<b
are continuity points of the measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}
. By
computing directly, we get that
\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}
is absolutely continuous
with respect to Lebesgue measure with density

\frac{\diff\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}}{\diff x}=\frac{n}{2\pi\gamma x}\sqrt{(e_{+}-x)(x-e_{-})}\mathbf{1}_{[e_{-},e_{+}]}(x)

where
e_{\pm}
are defined in Theorem
2.1
. Using
faithfulness of the state
\nu
, we may conclude that the operator norm
of
\mathfrak{h}-1_{\mathcal{A}}
is the largest element in absolute value of the
support of the measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}
after it has been shifted to the
left by one:

e_{\pm}-1=-\gamma+\frac{2\gamma}{n}\pm 2\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{n}}\sqrt{1-\gamma+\frac{\gamma}{n}},

the choice of
-
sign makes the absolute value largest:

\|\mathfrak{h}-1_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{\mathcal{F}}=\gamma-\frac{2\gamma}{n}+2\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{n}}\sqrt{1-\gamma+\frac{\gamma}{n}},

(20)

this concludes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
∎

6.
General Covariance Matrix

From the last Section, we know that
\mathbf{H}
converges in the strong
sense to a non-commutative random variable,
\mathfrak{h}
, whose law we
computed in the previous section. As mentioned in the Introduction, we
can study the harmonic mean of general population
\mathbf{\Sigma}
by
multiplication
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. In this
section, we will obtain a fixed point equation for both the limiting
spectral measure of
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}

and its centered version

\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{\Sigma}
in terms of
the limiting cdf
F
of
\mathbf{\Sigma}
assuming
(\mathfrak{h},\mathbf{\Sigma})
converge
as a set of non-commutative freely independent random variables

(\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{h})
, where
\mathfrak{s}
has law given by the measure
\diff F
.

We use another tool from free probability called the

multiplicative free convolution

[
15
]
. To define the

S
-transform, for a non-commutative random variable
a
in some
non-commutative
C^{*}
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\|\cdot\|,*,\phi)

define the function

g_{a}(z):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi(a^{n})z^{n},

we will assume the law
\mu_{a}
of
a
is a compactly supported measure
supported on
\mathbb{R}
. We have the relationship

g_{a}(z)=\frac{1}{z}m_{a}\bigg(\frac{1}{z}\bigg)-1.

(21)

Assume
\phi(a)\neq 0
so that
\ell_{a}(z)
is guaranteed to
exist and is the functional inverse of
g_{a}(z)
:

\ell_{a}\big(g_{a}(z)\big)=g_{a}\big(\ell_{a}(z)\big)=z.

The
S
transform of a non-commutative random variable
a
is defined as

S_{a}(z):=\frac{1+z}{z}\ell_{a}(z).

(22)

For freely independent non-commutative random variables
a
and
b

with
\phi(a)\neq 0
and
\phi(b)\neq 0
, we have the
rule

S_{ab}(z)=S_{a}(z)S_{b}(z).

(23)

Supposing the law of both
a
and
b
are known,
\mu_{a}
and
\mu_{b}

respectively. We will derive a fixed point equation for the Stieltjes
transform
m_{a}(z)
in terms using the formula
(
23
). First, note that (
23
) can
be written as

\ell_{ab}(z)=\ell_{a}(z)S_{b}(z)

(24)

replacing
z
with
g_{ab}\big(\frac{1}{z}\big)
gives

\frac{1}{z}=\ell_{a}\bigg(g_{ab}\bigg(\frac{1}{z}\bigg)\bigg)S_{b}\bigg(g_{ab}\bigg(\frac{1}{z}\bigg)\bigg),

now applying (
21
) to this yields

\frac{1}{z}=\ell_{a}\big(zm_{ab}(z)-1\big)S_{b}\big(zm_{ab}(z)-1\big),

rearranging yields

\ell_{a}\big(zm_{ab}(z)-1\big)=\frac{1}{zS_{b}\big(zm_{ab}(z)-1\big)},

applying
g_{a}
on both sides yields

zm_{ab}(z)-1=g_{a}\Bigg(\frac{1}{zS_{b}\big(zm_{ab}(z)-1\big)}\Bigg),

using (
21
) once more gives

m_{ab}(z)=S_{b}\big(zm_{ab}(z)-1\big)m_{a}\Big(zS_{b}\big(zm_{ab}(z)-1\big)\Big),

which written in integral form is:

m_{ab}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\diff F(x)}{z-\frac{x}{S_{b}(zm_{ab}(z)-1)}}.

(25)

We will use (
25
) to prove Theorem
2.2
.

.

By assumption of the Theorem, it will suffice to study the law of

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}\mathfrak{s}^{\frac{1}{2}},

(26)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}:=\mathfrak{s}^{\frac{1}{2}}\{

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}-1_{\mathcal{F}}\}\mathfrak{s}^{\frac{1}{2}},

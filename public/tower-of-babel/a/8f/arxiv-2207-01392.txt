Causal bubbles in globally hyperbolic spacetimes
Leonardo García-Heveling, Elefterios Soultanis

Abstract
. We give an example of a spacetime with a continuous metric which is globally hyperbolic and exhibits causal bubbling. The metric moreover splits orthogonally into a timelike and a spacelike part. We discuss our example in the context of energy conditions and the recently introduced synthetic timelike curvature-dimension (TCD) condition. In particular we observe that the TCD-condition does not, by itself, prevent causal bubbling.



1.
Introduction

Spacetimes where the Lorentzian metric is merely continuous often appear in mathematical General Relativity as weak solutions of Einstein’s Equations
[
4
,
10
,
19
]
. Chruściel and Grant
[
7
]
were the first to study their causal structure, discovering a phenomenon called
causal bubbling
(see below). Later, Sämann
[
18
]
studied globally hyperbolic spacetimes with continuous metrics in detail and showed that, just as in the smooth case, global hyperbolicity can be equivalently characterized by any of the following:

(i)

Non-total imprisonment and compact causal diamonds,

(ii)

Existence of a Cauchy hypersurface,

(iii)

Existence of a Cauchy time function.

In the same paper (see Section 7) Sämann raises the question whether globally hyperbolic spacetimes can be causally bubbling. In this short note we give an example of a
globally hyperbolic spacetime with continuous metric
-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\rho\mathrm{d}x^{2}
exhibiting causal bubbling
.

We define causal bubbling on a spacetime
(M,g)
with continuous metric
g
as follows. Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\pm}(p)=J^{\pm}(p)\setminus\overline{I^{\pm}(p)}

The set
\mathcal{B}^{\pm}(p)
is called a (future/past)
causal bubble
if it is non-empty.

Our example stands out from previously known examples of causally bubbling spacetimes for two reasons.

(a)

It is manifestly globally hyperbolic,

(b)

2.
The example

Consider the
(1+1)
-dimensional spacetime
\mathbb{R}^{2}
equipped with the continuous Lorentzian metric

(2.1)

\displaystyle g:=-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\rho(t,x)\mathrm{d}x^{2},\quad\rho(t,x):=1+\sqrt{(t-|x|)_{+}}.

With the natural choice of time orientation,
t
is a time function. Since the lightcones of the metric (
2.1
) are narrower than those of the Minkowski metric,
t
is a Cauchy time function, and hence the spacetime is globally hyperbolic.

To see that the causal future of the origin contains a causal bubble, we begin by considering the ODE for the null curves
\gamma(s)=(\alpha(s),\beta(s))
:

\displaystyle 0=-\alpha^{\prime}(s)^{2}+\left(1+\sqrt{(\alpha(s)-|\beta(s)|)_{+}}\right)\beta^{\prime}(s)^{2}.

A null curve
\gamma
starting at the origin, parametrized as
(\alpha(s),s)
thus satisfies
\alpha^{\prime}(s)^{2}=1+\sqrt{(\alpha(s)-s)_{+}}
or, by denoting
y(s)=\alpha(s)-s\geq 0
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(s)+1=\sqrt{1+\sqrt{y(s)}},\quad y(0)=0.

In addition to the trivial solution
y\equiv 0
, the initial value problem (
2.2
) also admits another solution, expressed in implicit form as

\displaystyle s=\frac{4}{3}\left[\left(1+\sqrt{y(s)}\right)^{3/2}-1\right]+2\sqrt{y(s)}.

Indeed, differentiating both sides yields

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=2\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\sqrt{y(s)}(1+\sqrt{y(s)})^{1/2}+2\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\sqrt{y(s)}

\displaystyle=y^{\prime}(s)\frac{\sqrt{1+\sqrt{y(s)}}+1}{\sqrt{y(s)}}=\frac{y^{\prime}(s)}{\sqrt{1+\sqrt{y(s)}}-1}.

Denoting

\displaystyle f(y):=\frac{4}{3}\left[\left(1+\sqrt{y}\right)^{3/2}-1\right]+2\sqrt{y},

we conclude that
\gamma=(s+f^{-1}(s),s)
is a null curve as well as the straight line given by
\gamma=(s,s)
. In fact there is a 1-parameter family of null curves starting at
0
, given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\gamma_{u}(s):=\begin{cases}(s,s)&\textrm{for }0\leq s<u,\\
(s+f^{-1}(s-u),s)&\textrm{for }s\geq u,\end{cases}

where
u\in[0,\infty]
. One can check that the curves
\gamma_{u}
are smooth for all parameter values except
s=u
, where they are only
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
regular. Note also that on smooth, two-dimensional spacetimes, every null curve is a null geodesic. Hence, for
u<\infty
,
\gamma_{u}|_{(u,\infty)}
is a null geodesic, as it is contained in the region where the metric is smooth. At
s=u
, however,
\gamma_{u}
is not locally length maximizing.

.

The non-empty open set

3.
Discussion

3.1.
Strong energy condition

As in previously known examples
[
7
,
9
,
11
,
14
]
causal bubbling arises from the branching of null geodesics. Chruściel and Grant noted
[
7
, Rem. 1.19]
that, in the Riemannian case, branching is associated with curvature being unbounded from below. Indeed, in our example (in
\{t\neq|x|\}
) the Ricci scalar, given by

R=\begin{cases}-\frac{\rho+(\rho-1)^{2}}{4\rho^{2}(\rho-1)^{3}}&\textrm{ if }t>|x|\\
0&\textrm{ if }t<|x|,\end{cases}

diverges to
-\infty
as
t\searrow|x|
. Note however that in dimension
1+1
the Ricci
tensor
is given by
\operatorname{Ric}=\frac{1}{2}Rg
and thus

(3.1)

{\rm Ric}(v,v)\geq 0\quad\textrm{ for all causal vectors $v$ in $\{t\neq|x|\}$}.

In other words the strong energy condition is satisfied. Physically speaking causal bubbling appears to be a consequence of the presence of infinite (but positive) effective energy density (see
[
12
]
for an in-depth discussion of energy conditions).

3.2.
Synthetic curvature bounds

Recently, a
synthetic
notion of
timelike curvature dimension (TCD) bounds
on (non-smooth)
Lorentzian pre-length spaces
has been put forth using optimal transport
[
17
,
5
]
, in analogy with the very successful metric theory
[
21
,
22
,
15
,
1
]
. The
entropic convexity
condition defining TCD
(K,N)
-spaces asks that the
Rényi entropy

{\rm Ent}(f\operatorname{vol})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int f\log f\mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}

is
(K,N)
-convex along timelike geodesics in a space of probability measures. We refer to
[
13
, Definition 2.8]
and
[
3
, Definition 2.17]
(see also
[
5
,
6
]
) for the definitions and properties of Lorentzian pre-length spaces and
(K,N)
-convexity, respectively. On smooth spacetimes, such convexity properties characterize the strong energy condition, cf.
[
17
]
.

Despite satisfying (
3.1
) the Rényi entropy associated to the volume measure of the metric (
2.1
) is not
(K,N)
-convex along Lorentz-Wasserstein geodesics, i.e.
the metric (
2.1
) does not satisfy the entropic convexity condition for any
(K,N)
.
2
2

2

While Lorentzian pre-length spaces are required (by definition) to have the push up property the entropic convexity condition makes sense regardless of the validity of push up.
This follows from the fact that
t\mapsto-\log\rho(t,x)
is not
(K,N)
-convex for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, cf.
[
20
]
.

However, restricted to a suitable subset, our example demonstrates that the TCD-condition does not prevent causal bubbling. Indeed, the closed set
Y\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{t\geq|x|\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the restriction of the metric (
2.1
) satisfies the (weak) entropic
(0,2)
-convexity condition but contains causal bubbles. Notice that
(Y,g)
is obtained as the uniform pointwise limit of the sequence of smooth metrics

(3.2)

\displaystyle g_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\rho_{j}(t,x)\mathrm{d}x^{2},\quad\rho_{j}(t,x)=\rho(t+1/j,x)

on
Y
, which all satisfy the strong energy condition (
3.1
) everywhere on
Y
and are thus TCD
(0,2)
-spaces.
3
3

3

Note that, while the spaces
(Y,g_{j})
are Lorentzian
pre-
length spaces, every point on
\{t=|x|\}\subset Y
has empty chronological past and thus they fail to be Lorentzian length spaces, cf.
[
13
, Definition 3.22]
.

In closing we point out that, in the metric setting, branching of geodesics is excluded by the
Riemannian curvature-dimension
(RCD) condition but not by the (weak) CD-condition, see
[
8
]
and
[
16
]
, respectively.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that all the conclusions in this note, including those about curvature, are valid in higher dimensions as seen by considering metrics
g=-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\rho(t,|\bm{x}|)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

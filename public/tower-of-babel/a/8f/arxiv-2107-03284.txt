Abstract
We show that Funnel MPC, a novel Model Predictive Control (MPC) scheme, allows tracking of smooth reference signals with prescribed performance for nonlinear multi-input multi-output systems of relative degree one with stable internal dynamics. The optimal control problem solved in each iteration of Funnel MPC resembles the basic idea of penalty methods used in optimization. To this end, we present a new stage cost design to mimic the high-gain idea of (adaptive) funnel control. We rigorously show initial and recursive feasibility of Funnel MPC without imposing terminal conditions or other requirements like a sufficiently long prediction horizon.

model predictive control, funnel control, reference tracking, nonlinear systems, initial

Introduction

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a well-established control technique which relies on the iterative
solution of Optimal Control Problems (OCPs), see the textbooks
[
11
,
24
]
. Thanks to its applicability to multi-input multi-output nonlinear systems and
its ability to take control and state constraints directly into account, it is nowadays widely used
and has seen various applications; see e.g.
[
23
]
.

A key property for applying MPC is recursive feasibility, meaning that solvability of the OCP at a
particular time instant automatically implies solvability of the OCP at the successor time instant.
Often, suitably designed terminal conditions (costs and constraints) are incorporated in the iteratively solved OCP to ensure recursive feasibility, see e.g.
[
24
]

and the references therein. However, such (artificially introduced) terminal conditions complicate
the task of finding an initially-feasible solution by imposing additional state constraints. As a
consequence, the domain of the MPC feedback controller might become significantly smaller. An
alternative approach, which is based on so-called cost controllability
[
7
]
, is using a
sufficiently-long prediction horizon, see e.g.
[
5
]
and the references
therein or
[
9
]
for an extension to continuous-time systems. It is worth to be noted
that both techniques become significantly more involved in the presence of time-varying state (or
output) constraints.

To overcome the outlined restrictions for a large system class, Funnel MPC (FMPC) was proposed
in
[
3
]
, which allows for reference tracking such that the tracking error
evolves in a pre-specified, potentially time-varying performance funnel. To this end, output
constraints were incorporated in the OCP. Then, both initial and recursive feasibility were
rigorously shown by using properties of the system class in consideration – without imposing
additional terminal conditions and independent of the length of the prediction horizon. Moreover,
the range of applied control values and the overall performance were further improved by using a
“funnel-like” stage cost, which penalises the tracking error and becomes infinite when approaching
the funnel boundary.

In the present paper, we show that such funnel-inspired stage costs (slightly modified compared to its
predecessor proposed in
[
3
]
) automatically ensure initial and recursive
feasibility for a class of nonlinear systems with relative degree one and, in a certain
sense, input-to-state stable internal dynamics without adding the (artificial) output constraints
used in
[
3
]
.
To this end, novel
optimization-based arguments are employed, which somehow resemble ideas underlying penalty methods.
We are convinced that, in principle, similar techniques may be used to extend the
presented analysis to systems with higher relative degree. This conjecture is substantiated by
numerical simulations, for which FMPC shows superior performance compared to both MPC with quadratic
stage cost and funnel control.

The novel stage cost used in FMPC is inspired by funnel control. The latter is a model-free
output-error feedback of high-gain type introduced in 2002 by
[
14
]
, see also the
recent work
[
2
]
for a comprehensive literature overview. The funnel controller is
adaptive, inherently robust and allows reference tracking for a fairly large class of systems solely
invoking structural assumptions, i.e. stable internal dynamics, known relative degree with a
sign-definite high-frequency gain matrix. Most importantly, tracking is achieved within a prescribed
funnel, that means a prescribed transient behaviour is guaranteed. The funnel controller proved
useful for tracking problems in various applications such as temperature control of chemical reactor
models
[
15
]
, control of industrial servo-systems
[
12
]
, underactuated multibody
systems
[
4
]
and DC-link power flow control
[
27
]
. Since funnel control,
contrary to MPC, does not use a model of the system, the controller only reacts on the current
system state and cannot “plan ahead”. This often results in high control values and a rapidly
changing control signal with peaks. Furthermore, the controller requires a high sampling rate to
stay feasible, see e.g.
[
3
]
. In applications, this results in quite
demanding hardware requirements.

Instead of guaranteeing that the output signal always evolves within predefined boundaries, previous
results for reference tracking with MPC mostly focus on ensuring asymptotic stability of the
tracking error, see e.g.
[
1
,
18
]
. These approaches usually
modify the optimization problem by adding terminal constraints. In
[
1
]

and
[
18
]
asymptotic stability of the tracking error is guaranteed by designing
terminal sets and terminal costs around a specific reference signal. A tracking MPC scheme without
such constraints is studied in
[
17
]
. The theoretical results for this scheme
rely on utilizing a sufficiently long prediction horizon instead.
In order to ensure reference tracking in the presence of disturbances or dynamic uncertainties,
tube-based robust MPC schemes use tubes around the reference signal which always confine the actual
system output, see e.g.
[
22
,
21
,
10
,
19
]
.
These tubes encompass the uncertainties of the system and can usually not be arbitrarily chosen a
priori. By adding terminal costs, terminal sets and constraints to the optimization problem it is
ensured that the system output always evolves within these tubes.
In
[
33
,
28
]
complex nonlinear incremental Lyapunov functions and a
corresponding incrementally stabilizing feedback is calculated offline in order to ensure that the
control objective is satisfied.
For linear systems the tracking of a reference signal within constant bounds is studied
in
[
8
]
. This procedure relies on the calculation of robust control
invariant (RCI) sets in order to ensure that state, input and performance constraints are met. An
extension of this approach which also accounts for external disturbances can be found
in
[
34
]
. These RCI sets, however, are not trivial to calculate for a given system
and the algorithm proposed in
[
8
]
may in general not terminate in finite time.
Barrier function based MPC (see e.g.
[
32
]
) follows a similar idea as FMPC. This
approach also uses, as part of the cost function, a term which diverges to infinity for states
converging to the boundary of a given set. However, utilizing terminal conditions (costs and
constraints) remains necessary in order to ensure recursive feasibility and that constraints are
met. By using a different kind of cost function, FMPC can circumvent this disadvantage.

By combining ideas from funnel control with MPC, the resulting Funnel MPC allows tracking of
sufficiently smooth reference signals for nonlinear multi-input multi-output systems of relative
degree one within a prescribed performance funnel. FMPC circumvents the shortcomings of both
approaches and enables us to benefit from the best of both worlds: guaranteed feasibility (funnel
control), a (slightly) enlarged system class (regularity of the high gain matrix is sufficient), and
superior performance (MPC).

The present paper is organized as follows.
We start by formulating the considered control problem and the MPC algorithm in
Section
2
. After presenting the considered system class and detailing our structural
assumptions, we present the main result of this paper. By using a “funnel-like” stage cost function, it
is possible to track a reference signal within a prescribed funnel with MPC and guarantee initial
and recursive feasibility for any prediction horizon and without any terminal or output
constraints. After presenting simulations and promising preliminary results of numerical experiments
on an extension of FMPC in Section
3
, we carry out the proof of the main result
over several steps in Section
4
. Finally, conclusions are drawn in
Section
5
.

Remark 2.1
.

In many practical applications perfect tracking is neither possible nor desired. Usually, the
objective rather is to ensure the tracking error to be less than an (arbitrary small)
prespecified constant after a prespecified period of time and to guarantee that the error does
not exceed this bound at a later time.
Tracking within a funnel, or in other words practical tracking, is advantageous since it allows
tracking for system classes where asymptotic tracking is not possible or requires – when compared to
asymptotic tracking – much less control effort.
Note that the function
\varphi
is a design parameter, thus its choice is completely up to the
designer.
Moreover, arbitrary funnel functions – and not restricted to
constant or monotonous decreasing funnels – give the user more flexibility in
finding a suitable trade-off between tracking performance and control effort. Typically, the
specific application dictates the constraints on the tracking error and thus indicates suitable
choices for
\varphi
. During safety critical system phases, the funnel will be small, while
during non-critical phases the funnel can be widened again to reduce the control effort.

Remark 2.4
.

If a stabilizing state feedback
u=Fx
is applied to a system of the form

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=Ax(t)+Bu(t),

(10a)

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}(t)

\displaystyle=f(x(t),\eta(t))

(10b)

with controllable

and

, then the

and

However, as stated by Sussmann and Kokotovic in

cannot, in general, choose

so as to make the number

large without making

large as
well. As first pointed out by Sussmann in
[

]
, the so called
peaking-phenomenon can cause the nonlinear part (
10b
) of
the system to have finite escape time even if the system

\dot{\eta}(t)=f(0,\eta(t))

has
0
as a global asymptotically stable equilibrium.
The presumed BIBS condition (

) not only avoids this problem, but is even more
essential since our control objective is to guarantee that the system output

. Without this assumption and even with
perfect tracking, the internal dynamics might (

) be unbounded and thus cause an

We summarize our assumptions and define the general system class to be considered.

Definition 2.5

(System class)
.

We say that the system (
1
) belongs to the
system class

\mathcal{N}^{m}
,
written
(f,g,h)\in\mathcal{N}^{m}
, if it has global relative degree

,
h^{-1}(0)
is isomorphic
to
\mathds{R}^{n-m}
, the distribution

is involutive, and the system satisfies the BIBS condition (

).

Remark 2.6
.

Relaxing standard requirements in funnel-control (see e.g.
[
14
,
2
]
), the high-frequency
gain matrix
\Gamma(x)
does not need to be sign-definite.
We only require the much weaker assumption of

being invertible.

We further emphasize that these structural assumptions are sufficient conditions for our results, but
they are not necessary. First promising preliminary simulation results show that Funnel MPC can also
successfully be applied to a more general system class (see Section

).

2.5
Novel stage cost design

To overcome the drawbacks of the MPC scheme
2.2
outlined in Section
2.3
, we propose for
\varphi\in\mathcal{G}
,
y_{\rf}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{m})
, and design parameter

{\lambda_{u}\in\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}
the new
stage cost function

\displaystyle\ell_{\varphi}:\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathds{R}^{n}\times\mathds{R}^{m}

\displaystyle\to\mathds{R}\cup\{\infty\},

(11)

\displaystyle(t,x,u)

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1-\varphi(t)^{2}\left\|h(x)-y_{\rf}(t)\right\|^{2}}-1+\lambda_{u}\left\|u\right\|^{2},&\varphi(t)\left\|h(x)-y_{\rf}(t)\right\|\neq 1\\
\infty,&\text{else},\end{cases}

to be used in the MPC Algorithm
2.2
instead of
\ell

from (
3
). The term
\frac{1}{1-\varphi(t)^{2}\left\|h(x)-y_{\rf}(t)\right\|^{2}}

penalises the distance of the tracking error to the funnel boundary, whereas the parameter
\lambda_{u}

again influences the penalization of the control input. Note that we allow for
\lambda_{u}=0
.

The cost function
\ell_{\varphi}
is motivated by the following standard result on funnel control
from
[
14
, Thm. 7]
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Assume that
(f,g,h)\in\mathcal{N}^{m}
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{G}
,
y_{\rf}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{m})
,

t^{0}\in\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
, and
x^{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\varphi}_{t^{0}}
. Further assume that the high-frequency gain matrix

\Gamma(x)
as in (
7
) is positive definite for all
x\in\mathds{R}^{n}
. Then the application
of the output feedback
u(t):=\mu_{\rm FC}(t,y(t))
with

\mu_{\rm FC}(t,y)=-k(t,y)e(t,y),\hskip 20.00003ptk(t,y)=\frac{1}{1-\varphi(t)^{2}\left\|e(t,y)\right\|^{2}},\hskip 20.00003pte(t,y)=y-y_{\rf}(t)

(12)

to (
1
) leads to the closed-loop initial value problem

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=f(x(t))-g(x(t))\frac{y(t)-y_{\rf}(t)}{1-\varphi(t)^{2}\left\|y(t)-y_{\rf}(t)\right\|^{2}},\hskip 10.00002ptx(t^{0})=x^{0},

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=h(x(t)),

which has a solution, every
solution can be extended to a unique global solution
x:[t^{0},\infty)\to\mathds{R}^{n}
, and
x,u,y
are bounded
with essentially bounded weak derivatives. The tracking error evolves uniformly within the
performance funnel, i.e.,

\ \exists\,\varepsilon>0\ \forall\,t>0:\ \left\|e(t)\right\|\leq\varphi(t)^{-1}-\varepsilon.

Remark 2.8
.

The following holds according to Proposition
2.7

\ \forall\,x^{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\varphi}_{t^{0}}\ \exists\,M>0:\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})\neq\emptyset.

, and

.

2.6
Main result

We are now in the position to define the Funnel MPC (FMPC) algorithm. It is the
MPC Algorithm
2.2
without the output constraint (
4
) and
cost function
\ell
as in (

as

Algorithm 2.9

(FMPC)

Given:
System (
1
), reference signal

, funnel function
\varphi\in\mathcal{G}
,
M>0
,
t^{0}\in\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
,

x^{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\varphi}_{t^{0}}
, and stage cost function
\ell_{\varphi}
as in (
11
).

Set
the time shift
\delta>0
, the prediction horizon
T\geq\delta
and initialize the current time

\widehat{t}:=t^{0}
.

Steps:

1.

Obtain a measurement of the state at
\hat{t}
and set
\widehat{x}:=x(\widehat{t})
.

2.

Compute a solution

of the Optimal
Control Problem (OCP)

\mathop{\operatorname{minimize}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in L^{\infty}([\widehat{t},\widehat{t}+T],\mathds{R}^{m}),\\
\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}\leq M\end{subarray}}\ \int_{\widehat{t}}^{\widehat{t}+T}\ell_{\varphi}(t,x(t;\widehat{t},\widehat{x},u),u(t))\ \text{d}t

(13)

Apply the feedback law

to system (

Increase

We show that the Funnel MPC Algorithm

is initially and recursively feasible for
every prediction horizon

. Application of FMPC to system (

guarantees tracking of a reference trajectory

.

Consider system (
1
) with
(f,g,h)\in\mathcal{N}^{m}
. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{G}
,
\ y_{\rf}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{m})
,

and

such that the FMPC

2.9
with

and
\delta>0

and the corresponding input is given by

Furthermore, each global solution
x
with corresponding input

satisfies:

1.

\ \forall\,t\geq t^{0}:\hskip 10.00002pt\left\|u_{\rm FMPC}(t)\right\|\leq M
.

Remark 2.11
.

1.

The OCP (
13
) has neither state nor terminal constraints.
Nevertheless, application of the FMPC Algorithm
2.9
to the
system (
1
) ensures that a global solution of the closed-loop system exists
and the error evolves within the funnel. However, note that this solution is not unique
in general. The reason is that the solution of the OCP (

) found in

shows that the

[
5
]

[
24
]

merely the input constraints given by

The proof is carried out over several steps in Section

. In
Section
4.1
we first assume that the set

is non-empty and prove that the
optimization problem (

) has a solution

and this solution is an
element of
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0}){}
. We further show that the stage cost function
\ell_{\varphi}
as
in (

ensures that the

\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}
. In Section
4.2
we
prove initial and recursive feasibility of the Funnel MPC Algorithm
2.9
by
showing that there exists
M>0
such that the set
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,\widehat{t},\widehat{x})
is initially
(i.e., at
\widehat{t}=t^{0}
) and recursively (i.e., at
\widehat{t}=t^{0}+\delta n
after
n
steps of
Algorithm
2.9
) non-empty, where
\widehat{x}
is the state of the system at
time
\widehat{t}
.

In this section we like to present some promising preliminary results on an extension of the FMPC

For that we introduce the general notion of relative degree for system (
1
).
Recall that the Lie derivative of
h
along
f
is defined by

\left(L_{f}h\right)(x)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial h_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)\,f_{i}(x)\right)_{j=1,\ldots,n}=h^{\prime}(x)f(x),

and we may successively define
L_{f}^{k}h=L_{f}(L_{f}^{k-1}h)
with
L_{f}^{0}h=h
.
Furthermore, for the matrix-valued function
g

(L_{g}h)(x)=\left[(L_{g_{1}}h)(x),\ldots,(L_{g_{m}}h)(x)\right],

where
g_{i}
denotes the
i

g
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.
Then system (
1
) is said to have
(global) relative degree

r\in\mathbb{N}
, if

\displaystyle\ \forall\,k\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}\ \forall\,x\in\mathds{R}^{n}:\ (L_{g}L_{f}^{k-1}h)(x)=0\hskip 10.00002pt\wedge\hskip 10.00002pt(L_{g}L_{f}^{r-1}h)(x)\in\text{GL}_{m}(\mathds{R}),

see
[
16
, Sec. 5.1]
.
The generalised
high-frequency gain matrix
is defined as

\Gamma(x):=\left(L_{g}L_{f}^{r-1}h\right)(x)\in\text{GL}_{m}(\mathds{R}),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathds{R}^{n}.

(19)

.

In other words, the relative degree is the number of times the output has to be differentiated in order
for the input to appear explicitly on the right side of the equation.

\mathop{\operatorname{minimize}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in L^{\infty}([t^{0},t^{0}+T],\mathds{R}^{m}),\\

We like to point out that there is a subtle difference between a Lebesgue integrable function (which
belongs to

in

To make this difference clearer we call a measurable function

on a Borel set

\zeta^{+}:=\max\{\zeta,0\}
and
\zeta^{-}:=\max\{-\zeta,0\}

Proposition

In

this issue was resolved by appending state constraints to the
optimal control problem. In the following we show that such constraints are unnecessary. In
fact, if an arbitrary control function

such that

\ell_{\varphi}(\cdot,x(\cdot;t^{0},x^{0},u),u(\cdot))
is quasi-integrable over
[t^{0},t^{0}+T]
is applied to the system,
then it is guaranteed that the error

evolves within the funnel. To show this, an elementary lemma
is proved first.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T>0
and
g:[0,T]\to\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
be Lipschitz continuous.
If
\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{g(s)}\ \text{d}s<\infty
, then
g(t)>0
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

First assume that there exists
\tau\in(0,T)
such that
g(\tau)=0
. Choose
\varepsilon>0
such that

{(\tau-\varepsilon,\tau+\varepsilon)\subset[0,T]}
. Since
g
is Lipschitz continuous, we have that

\ \exists\,C>0\ \forall\,s\in(\tau-\varepsilon,\tau+\varepsilon):\ g(s)=\left|g(s)-g(\tau)\right|\leq C\left|s-\tau\right|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\infty>\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{g(s)}\ \text{d}s\geq\int_{\tau-\varepsilon}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\frac{1}{g(s)}\ \text{d}s\geq\int_{\tau-\varepsilon}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\frac{1}{C\left|s-\tau\right|}\ \text{d}s=\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{C\left|s\right|}\ \text{d}s=\infty,

a contradiction. A similar proof applies in the cases
\tau=0
and
\tau=T
.
∎

Remark 4.2

Lemma

non-integrability of
\tfrac{1}{x}
over the interval
[0,1]
. As pointed out in
Remark
4.4
, as result of this, a finite value of the cost function ensures
that the tracking error
e:=y-y_{\text{ref}}
remains within the prescribed funnel
boundaries.
The logarithm on the other hand is integrable over the interval
[0,1]
:

\int_{0}^{1}\ln(x^{n})\text{d}x=\underbrace{\ln(x^{n})x\Big|_{0}^{1}}_{=0}-\int_{0}^{1}x\frac{n}{x}\text{d}x=-n.

Therefore, such a cost function alone can in general not guarantee that the state always remain within
the desired region and therefore the usage of terminal conditions (costs and constraints)
remains necessary.

If the initial value
x^{0}
is within the set
\mathcal{D}^{\varphi}_{t^{0}}
, then any control
u
with

J^{\varphi}_{T}(u;t^{0},x^{0})<\infty
guarantees that, if applied to the system (
1
), the error

e(t)=y(t)-y_{\rf}(t)
remains strictly within the funnel. Since
J^{\varphi}_{T}(u;t^{0},x^{0})
is positive
for all control functions
u\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
, this raises the question as to whether there exists an
optimal
u^{\star}
which minimizes
J^{\varphi}_{T}(\cdot;t^{0},x^{0})
and is a solution to the optimal
control problem (
23
). The answer is affirmative and shown in the next
theorem.

Theorem 4.6
.

Consider system (
1
) with
(f,g,h)\in\mathcal{N}^{m}
. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{G},\ y_{\rf}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{m})
,
T>0
,
M>0
,
t^{0}\in\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
, and
x^{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\varphi}_{t^{0}}
such that

{\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})\neq\emptyset}
.
Then, there exists a function
u^{\star}\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
such that

J^{\varphi}_{T}(u^{\star};t^{0},x^{0})=\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})}J^{\varphi}_{T}(u;t^{0},x^{0})=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in L^{\infty}([t^{0},t^{0}+T],\mathds{R}^{m}),\\
\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}\leq M\end{subarray}}J^{\varphi}_{T}(u;t^{0},x^{0}).

Proof.

The proof essentially follows the lines of
[
25
, Prop. 2.2]
.

To simplify the notation, assume without loss of generality
that
t^{0}=0
and consider only
the interval
[0,T]
.
It follows from Remark
4.4
that

J^{\varphi}_{T}(u;t^{0},x^{0})\geq 0
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
.
Hence the infimum
J^{\star}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})}J^{\varphi}_{T}(u;t^{0},x^{0})
exists.
Let
(u_{k})\in(\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0}))^{\mathds{N}}
be a minimizing sequence, meaning
J^{\varphi}_{T}(u_{k};t^{0},x^{0})\to J^{\star}
.
By definition of
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
, we have
\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\leq M
for all
k\in\mathds{N}
.
Since
L^{\infty}([0,T],\mathds{R}^{m})\subseteq L^{2}([0,T],\mathds{R}^{m})
,
we conclude that
(u_{k})
is a bounded sequence in the Hilbert space
L^{2}
, thus there
exists a function
u^{\star}\in L^{2}([0,T],\mathds{R}^{m})
and a weakly convergent subsequence

u_{k}\rightharpoonup u^{\star}
(which we do not relabel). More precisely,
u_{k}|_{[0,t]}\rightharpoonup u^{\star}|_{[0,t]}
weakly in
L^{2}([0,t],\mathds{R}^{m})
for all
t\in[0,T]
as a
straightforward argument shows. We define
(x_{k}):=(x(\cdot;t^{0},x^{0},u_{k}))\in C([0,T],\mathds{R}^{n})^{\mathds{N}}

as the sequence of associated responses.

Step 1
:
We show that
(x_{k})
is uniformly bounded. By
u_{k}\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
we have

x_{k}(t)\in\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\varphi}
, i.e.,
{\varphi(t)\left\|h(x_{k}(t))-y_{\rf}(t)\right\|<1}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Set
(y_{k}(t),\eta_{k}(t))=\Phi(x_{k}(t))
for
t\in[0,T]
and
k\in\mathds{N}
, where
\Phi:\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{R}^{n}

is a diffeomorphism such that the coordinate transformation
\Phi(x)=(y,\eta)
puts the
system (
1
) into Byrnes-Isidori form (
8
). Since
\varphi
is positive on

[0,T]
, we obtain

\ \forall\,t\in[0,T]:\hskip 10.00002pt\left\|y_{k}(t)\right\|\leq\left\|h(x_{k}(t))-y_{\rf}(t)\right\|+\left\|y_{\rf}(t)\right\|\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\varphi(t)^{-1}+\left\|y_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}=:\tilde{c}_{0}

and
c_{0}:=\tilde{c}_{0}+\left\|\Phi(x^{0})\right\|
is independent of
k
.
Hence, by (
9
) there exists
c_{1}>0
such that

\ \forall\,y\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{m}):\hskip 10.00002pt\|y\|_{\infty}\leq\tilde{c}_{0}\ \implies\ \|\eta(\cdot;0,\eta_{k}(0),y)\|_{\infty}\leq c_{1}.

Extending
y_{k}
to
\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
\|y_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\tilde{c}_{0}
then yields that

\|\eta_{k}(t)\|\leq c_{1}
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Therefore,

x_{k}(t)\in\Phi^{-1}\left(\left\{\ \begin{pmatrix}z_{1}\\
z_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathds{R}^{m}\times\mathds{R}^{n-m}\ \left|\ \vphantom{\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}\\
z_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathds{R}^{m}\times\mathds{R}^{n-m}}\left\|z_{1}\right\|\leq\tilde{c}_{0}\ \wedge\ \left\|z_{2}\right\|\leq c_{1}\ \right.\right\}\right)=:\mathcal{K}

for all
t\in[0,T]
and all
k\in\mathds{N}
, where
\mathcal{K}
is compact and independent of
k
. Hence,
(x_{k})
is uniformly
bounded.

Step 2
:
We show that
(x_{k})
is uniformly equicontinuous. Since the sequence
(u_{k})
is bounded,

M:=\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}
exists. Set
C_{1}:=\max_{x\in\mathcal{K}}\left\|f(x)\right\|
and

C_{2}:=\max_{x\in\mathcal{K}}\left\|g(x)\right\|
, which exist by continuity of
f
and
g
. Now let
\varepsilon>0

and define
\delta:=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(C_{1}+MC_{2})\right\}
. Let
k\in\mathds{N}
and

t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T]
such that
|t_{2}-t_{1}|<\delta^{2}
. Then, using Hölder’s inequality in the third
estimate,

\displaystyle\left\|x_{k}(t_{2})-x_{k}(t_{1})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left\|f(x_{k}(s))\right\|+\left\|g(x_{k}(s))\right\|\left\|u_{k}(s)\right\|\ \text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|+C_{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left\|u_{k}(s)\right\|\ \text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\sqrt{\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|}+C_{2}\sqrt{\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\sqrt{\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|}+C_{2}\sqrt{\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|}M

\displaystyle<\delta(C_{1}+MC_{2})\leq\varepsilon,

which shows that
(x_{k})
is uniformly equicontinuous.

Step 3
:
By the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem
there exists a function
x^{\star}\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathds{R}^{n})
and a uniformly convergent
subsequence
x_{k}\to x^{\star}
(which we do not relabel).
Now we prove that
x^{\star}=x(\cdot;t^{0},x^{0},u^{\star})
, which means to show that

x^{\star}(t)=x^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(x^{\star}(s))+g(x^{\star}(s))u^{\star}(s)\ \text{d}s,\hskip 10.00002ptt\in[0,T].

We have

x_{k}(t)=x^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(x_{k}(s))+g(x_{k}(s))u_{k}(s)\ \text{d}s,\hskip 10.00002ptk\in\mathds{N},\ t\in[0,T],

and since
x_{k}
in particular converges pointwise to
x^{\star}
and the sequence
(f(x_{k}))
is uniformly bounded as
(x_{k})
is uniformly bounded and
f
is continuous, the bounded convergence theorem gives that

\ \forall\,t\in[0,T]:\ \int_{0}^{t}f(x_{k}(s))\ \text{d}s\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{t}f(x^{\star}(s))\ \text{d}s.

Therefore, it remains to show

\ \forall\,t\in[0,T]:\ \int_{0}^{t}g(x_{k}(s))u_{k}(s)\ \text{d}s\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{t}g(x^{\star}(s))u^{\star}(s)\ \text{d}s.

The argument
s
is omitted in the following. Since
g(x^{\star})
is bounded on
[0,T]
, it is an element of
L^{2}([0,T],\mathds{R}^{n\times m})
, thus the weak
convergence of
(u_{k})
implies

\ \forall\,t\in[0,T]:\ \int_{0}^{t}g(x^{\star})u_{k}\ \text{d}s\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{t}g(x^{\star})u^{\star}\ \text{d}s.

Therefore, using Hölder’s inequality in the second estimate we obtain, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!g(x_{k})u_{k}-g(x^{\star})u^{\star}\!\ \text{d}s\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!g(x_{k})u_{k}+g(x^{\star})u_{k}-g(x^{\star})u_{k}-g(x^{\star})u^{\star}\!\ \text{d}s\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\left\|g(x_{k})-g(x^{\star})\right\|\left\|u_{k}\right\|\!\!\!\ \text{d}s+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}g(x^{\star})u_{k}-g(x^{\star})u^{\star}\!\ \text{d}s\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\left\|g(x_{k})-g(x^{\star})\right\|^{2}\!\!\!\ \text{d}s\right)^{\!\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\left(\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\left\|u_{k}\right\|^{2}\!\!\!\ \text{d}s\right)^{\!\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\!\!+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!g(x^{\star})u_{k}-g(x^{\star})u^{\star}\!\!\ \text{d}s\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{m\in\mathds{N}}\!\left\|u_{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}\!\!\underbrace{\left(\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\left\|g(x_{k})-g(x^{\star})\right\|^{2}\!\!\ \text{d}s\!\right)^{\!\frac{1}{2}}\!\!}_{\to 0}\!+\underbrace{\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!g(x^{\star})u_{k}-g(x^{\star})u^{\star}\!\ \text{d}s\right\|}_{\to 0}\!\to\!0.

Step 4
:
We show
\left\|u^{\star}\right\|_{\infty}\leq M
. To this end, define the sets

A_{m}:=\left\{\ t\in[0,T]\ \left|\ \vphantom{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u^{\star}(t)\right\|^{2}\geq M^{2}+\frac{1}{m}\ \right.\right\},\hskip 10.00002ptm\in\mathds{N}.

Let
\chi_{A_{m}}
denote the indicator function of the set
A_{m}
, then, since
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u^{\star}
, we have that

\langle u_{k},\chi_{A_{m}}u^{\star}\rangle_{L^{2}}\to\langle u^{\star},\chi_{A_{m}}u^{\star}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\|\chi_{A_{m}}u^{\star}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

On the other hand, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have that

\langle u_{k},\chi_{A_{m}}u^{\star}\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq\|\chi_{A_{m}}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|\chi_{A_{m}}u^{\star}\|_{L^{2}},

thus

\|\chi_{A_{m}}u^{\star}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\chi_{A_{m}}u^{\star}\|_{L^{2}}^{-1}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\langle u_{k},\chi_{A_{m}}u^{\star}\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|\chi_{A_{m}}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}

and hence

\int_{A_{m}}\|u^{\star}(s)\|^{2}\ \text{d}s\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{A_{m}}\|u_{k}(s)\|^{2}\ \text{d}s.

Since
\|u_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq M
, we then find the following for all
m\in\mathds{N}
and
k\in\mathds{N}
:

\displaystyle\lambda\left(A_{m}\right)=\int_{A_{m}}1\ \text{d}s\leq m\int_{A_{m}}\left\|u^{\star}(s)\right\|^{2}-M^{2}\ \text{d}s\leq m\int_{A_{m}}\left\|u^{\star}(s)\right\|^{2}-\left\|u_{k}(s)\right\|^{2}\ \text{d}s,

where
\lambda
denotes the Lebesgue measure, thus

0\leq\lambda\left(A_{m}\right)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}m\int_{A_{m}}\left\|u^{\star}(s)\right\|^{2}-\left\|u_{k}(s)\right\|^{2}\ \ \text{d}s\leq 0.

Due to the
\sigma
-continuity of
\lambda
we get

\lambda(\left\{\ t\in[0,T]\ \left|\ \vphantom{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u^{\star}(t)\right\|>M\ \right.\right\})=\lambda\left(\bigcup_{m\in\mathds{N}}A_{m}\right)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\lambda(A_{m})=0.

This implies
\left\|u^{\star}\right\|_{\infty}\leq M
.

Step 5
:
We prove
u^{\star}\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
, which means to show
x^{\star}(t)\in\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\varphi}

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Assume there exists
\tau\in(0,T]
with
\varphi(\tau)\left\|h(x^{\star}(\tau))-y_{\rf}(\tau)\right\|\geq 1
.
By continuity of
x^{\star}
,
\varphi
,
h
, and
y_{\rf}
there exists

\widehat{t}:=\min\left\{\ \tau\in(0,T]\ \left|\ \vphantom{\tau\in(0,T]}\ \varphi(\tau)\left\|h(x^{\star}(\tau))-y_{\rf}(\tau)\right\|=1\ \right.\right\}.

\dot{x}^{\star}
is bounded on the interval
[0,T]
since
g
and
f
are
continuous and both
x^{\star}
and
u^{\star}
are bounded.
Hence,
x^{\star}
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
L^{\star}>0
.
Define the continuously differentiable function
\omega:[0,T]\times\mathcal{K}\to\mathds{R},(t,x)\mapsto 1-\varphi(t)^{2}\left\|h(x)-y_{\rf}(t)\right\|^{2}
.
Due to the compactness of
[0,T]
and
\mathcal{K}
,
\omega
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz
constant
L_{\omega}>0
.
We have
\omega(s,x_{k}(s))>0
for all
k\in\mathds{N}
and all
s\in[0,\hat{t}]
because

u_{k}\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
.
Since
w(\widehat{t},x^{\star}(\widehat{t}))=0
, the following holds for all

s\in[0,\widehat{t}]
and all
k\in\mathds{N}
.

\displaystyle\omega(s,x_{k}(s))

\displaystyle=\left|\omega(s,x_{k}(s))\right|=\left|\omega(s,x_{k}(s))-w(\widehat{t},x^{\star}(\widehat{t}))\right|

\displaystyle\leq L_{\omega}\left\|\begin{pmatrix}s-\widehat{t}\\
x_{k}(s)-x^{\star}(\widehat{t})\end{pmatrix}\right\|=L_{\omega}\left\|\begin{pmatrix}s-\widehat{t}\\
x_{k}(s)-x^{\star}(s)+x^{\star}(s)-x^{\star}(\widehat{t})\end{pmatrix}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{\omega}\left|s-\widehat{t}\right|+L_{\omega}\left\|x_{k}(s)-x^{\star}(s)\right\|+L_{\omega}L^{\star}\left|s-\widehat{t}\right|.

The supremum
\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}J^{\varphi}_{T}(u_{k};t^{0},x^{0})<\infty
exists because
J^{\varphi}_{T}(u_{k};t^{0},x^{0})\to J^{\star}
.
Since
\int_{0}^{\widehat{t}}\tfrac{1}{(L_{\omega}+L_{\omega}L^{\star})\left|s-\widehat{t}\right|}\ \text{d}s=\infty
, there exists
\delta>0
with

\int_{0}^{\widehat{t}}\tfrac{1}{(L_{\omega}+L_{\omega}L^{\star})\left|s-\widehat{t}\right|+L_{\omega}\delta}\ \text{d}s-\widehat{t}>\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}J^{\varphi}_{T}(u_{k};t^{0},x^{0})
. Due to the uniform convergence of
x_{k}
to
x^{\star}
, there
exists
K\in\mathds{N}
such that
\left\|x_{k}(s)-x^{\star}(s)\right\|<\delta
for all
k\geq K
and all

s\in[0,\widehat{t}]
. Thus, we arrive, for
k\geq K
, at the following contradiction.

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}J^{\varphi}_{T}(u_{k};t^{0},x^{0})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{T}\ell_{\varphi}(s,x_{k}(s),u_{k}(s))\ \text{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{1-\varphi(s)^{2}\left\|h(x_{k}(s))-y_{\rf}(s)\right\|^{2}}-1+\lambda_{u}\left\|u_{k}(s)\right\|^{2}\ \text{d}s

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\widehat{t}}\frac{1}{\omega(s,x_{k}(s))}-1\ \text{d}s

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\widehat{t}}\frac{1}{(L_{\omega}+L_{\omega}L^{\star})\left|s-\widehat{t}\right|+L_{\omega}\left\|x_{k}(s)-x^{\star}(s)\right\|}\ \text{d}s-\widehat{t}

\displaystyle>\int_{0}^{\widehat{t}}\frac{1}{(L_{\omega}+L_{\omega}L^{\star})\left|s-\widehat{t}\right|+L_{\omega}\delta}\ \text{d}s-\widehat{t}>\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}}J^{\varphi}_{T}(u_{k};t^{0},x^{0}).

Hence,
u^{\star}\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
.

Step 6
:
We show
J^{\varphi}_{T}(u^{\star};t^{0},x^{0})=J^{\star}
.
Let
\tilde{\ell}_{\varphi}:\mathcal{D}^{\varphi}\to\mathds{R},(t,x)\mapsto\tfrac{1}{1-\varphi(t)^{2}\left\|h(x)-y_{\rf}(t)\right\|^{2}}-1
.
For all
k\in\mathds{N}
, we have
\left\|\tilde{\ell}_{\varphi}(\cdot,x_{k}(\cdot))\right\|_{\infty}<\infty

and
\left\|\tilde{\ell}_{\varphi}(\cdot,x^{\star}(\cdot))\right\|_{\infty}<\infty
because
{u_{k},u^{\star}\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})}
.
According to Step 5, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\left\|x^{\star}(t)\right\|\leq\tfrac{1}{\varphi(t)}-\varepsilon
for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Due to the uniform convergence of
x_{k}
to
x^{\star}
, there exists
N\in\mathds{N}
such that

\left\|x_{k}-x^{\star}\right\|_{\infty}<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}
for
k\geq N
. Thus,

\ \forall\,k\geq N\ \forall\,t\in[0,T]:\hskip 10.00002pt\left\|x_{k}(t)\right\|\leq\left\|x_{k}(t)-x^{\star}(t)\right\|+\left\|x^{\star}(t)\right\|<\frac{1}{\varphi(t)}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Hence, the sequence
\left(\tilde{\ell}_{\varphi}(\cdot,x_{k}(\cdot))^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\right)
is uniformly bounded.
Due to the continuity of
\tilde{\ell}
, the bounded convergence theorem gives that

\tilde{\ell}_{\varphi}(\cdot,x_{k}(\cdot))^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\to\tilde{\ell}_{\varphi}(\cdot,x^{\star}(\cdot))^{\tfrac{1}{2}}

strongly and, thus, also weakly in
L^{2}([0,T],\mathds{R})
.
Since
J^{\varphi}_{T}(u_{k};t^{0},x^{0})\to J^{\star}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})}J^{\varphi}_{T}(u;t^{0},x^{0})

and since the
L^{2}
-norm is weakly lower semi-continuous, the following holds.

\displaystyle J^{\varphi}_{T}(u^{\star};t^{0},x^{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\ell_{\varphi}(s,x^{\star}(s),u^{\star}(s))\ \text{d}s=\left\|\tilde{\ell}_{\varphi}(\cdot,x^{\star}(\cdot))^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lambda_{u}\left\|u^{\star}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left\|\tilde{\ell}_{\varphi}(\cdot,x_{k}(\cdot))^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\liminf_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{u}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}J^{\varphi}_{T}(u_{k};t^{0},x^{0})=J^{\star}.

Therefore
J^{\varphi}_{T}(u^{\star};t^{0},x^{0})=\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})}J^{\varphi}_{T}(u;t^{0},x^{0})
.

Step 7
: We show that
J^{\varphi}_{T}(u^{\star};t^{0},x^{0})=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in L^{\infty}([t^{0},t^{0}+T],\mathds{R}^{m}),\\
\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}\leq M\end{subarray}}J^{\varphi}_{T}(u;t^{0},x^{0})
. Since
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})\neq\emptyset
by assumption this follows from Remark
4.4
(ii) and completes the proof.
∎

4.2
Initial and recursive feasibility

In the following we seek to show initial and recursive feasibility of the FMPC
Algorithm
2.9
. For this we need to show that the essential assumption

\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})\neq\emptyset
of Theorem
4.6
is initially (i.e., at
t=t^{0}
) and
recursively (i.e., at
t=t^{0}+\delta n
after
n
steps of Algorithm
2.9
)
satisfied. The main difficulty is to prove the existence of a number
M>0
for which the latter is
satisfied for all initial values within a prescribed bounded set. This is the purpose of the
following results.

First observe that applying the funnel controller (
12
) from Proposition
2.7
to
the system (
1
) ensures that the error evolves strictly within the funnel for any initial
condition
x^{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\varphi}_{t^{0}}
. As stated in Remark
2.8
the funnel
controller is bounded. This bound however depends on the initial value
x^{0}
. This means that for
every
x^{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\varphi}_{t^{0}}
there exists
M>0
such that
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
is non-empty. This raises
the question whether it is possible to find a bound
M
independent of the initial value
x^{0}
. The
following example shows that this is not the case in general.

Example 4.7
.

Consider the two-dimensional linear system

\displaystyle\dot{y}(t)

\displaystyle=\eta(t)+u(t),

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pty(0)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\dot{\eta}(t)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\eta(0)

\displaystyle=\eta^{0},

in Byrnes-Isidori form with constant reference signal
y_{\rf}\equiv 0
and the constant
funnel
\varphi\equiv 1
. Let
M>0
and
T>0
be arbitrary. Although the system satisfies the BIBS condition (
9
) and the initial error
e(0)=y^{0}-y_{\rf}(0)=0
lies within the
funnel for every
\eta^{0}\in\mathds{R}
, there exists
\eta^{0}\in\mathds{R}
such that the error
e
exceeds the
funnel boundaries at time
T
for every
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T],\mathds{R})
with
\|u\|_{\infty}\leq M
. To
see this, choose
\eta^{0}:=M+\frac{2}{T}
, then

\displaystyle e\left(T\right)=y\left(T\right)=\int_{0}^{T}\eta(s)+u(s)\ \text{d}s=T\eta^{0}+\int_{0}^{T}u(s)\ \text{d}s\geq T\eta^{0}-TM=2>1=\frac{1}{\varphi\left(T\right)}.

The example shows that, in general, there exists no
M>0
such that
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
is
non-empty for all
x^{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\varphi}_{t^{0}}
. However, for a bounded set
B\subset\mathcal{D}^{\varphi}_{t^{0}}
of
initial values, it is possible to find a uniform bound
M>0
. Moreover,
M
can be chosen
independently of
T>0
. To show this, we denote by
\mathcal{Y}^{\varphi,y^{0}}_{y_{\rf}}(I)
the set of all
functions in
\mathcal{C}(I,\mathds{R}^{m})
starting at
y^{0}\in\mathds{R}^{m}
and evolving within the funnel on an interval
I\subseteq\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
of the form
I=[a,b)
with
b\in(a,\infty]
or
I=[a,b]
with
b\in(a,\infty)
:

\mathcal{Y}^{\varphi,y^{0}}_{y_{\rf}}(I):=\left\{\ y\in\mathcal{C}(I,\mathds{R}^{m})\ \left|\ \vphantom{y\in\mathcal{C}(I,\mathds{R}^{m})}y(\inf I)=y^{0},\ \ \forall\,t\in I:\ \varphi(t)\left\|y(t)-y_{\rf}(t)\right\|<1\ \right.\right\}.

Lemma 4.8
.

Consider system (
1
) with
(f,g,h)\in\mathcal{N}^{m}
. Let

\varphi\in\mathcal{G}
,
y_{\rf}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{m})
,
t^{0}\in\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
. Then for all bounded sets

B\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
there exists
a compact set
K\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\ \forall\,T>0\ \forall\,(y^{0},\eta^{0})\in B\ \forall\,y\in\mathcal{Y}^{\varphi,y^{0}}_{y_{\rf}}([t^{0},t^{0}+T])\ \forall\,t\in[t^{0},t^{0}+T]:\hskip 10.00002pt(y(t),\eta(t;t^{0},\eta^{0},y))\in K.

(25)

Proof.

Define

N_{t}:=\left\{\ \eta(t;t^{0},\eta^{0},y)\ \left|\ \vphantom{\eta(t;t^{0},\eta^{0},y)}(y^{0},\eta^{0})\in B,\ y\in\mathcal{Y}^{\varphi,y^{0}}_{y_{\rf}}([t^{0},\infty))\ \right.\right\},\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq t^{0}.

By definition of
\mathcal{G}
,
\varphi
is strictly positive and
\inf_{t\geq 0}\varphi(t)>0
.
Therefore,
1/\varphi
is bounded. Since clearly
y_{\rf}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{m})
is bounded,
every function
y\in\mathcal{Y}^{\varphi,y^{0}}_{y_{\rf}}([t^{0},\infty))
is bounded by

\left\|y\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|y-y_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|y_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\tfrac{1}{\varphi}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|y_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}.

Since
B
is bounded it follows from the BIBS
condition (
9
) that the set
N:=\bigcup_{t\geq t^{0}}N_{t}
is also bounded. Furthermore, the set

O:=\bigcup_{t\geq t^{0}}\left\{\ y\in\mathds{R}^{m}\ \left|\ \vphantom{y\in\mathds{R}^{m}}\varphi(t)\left\|y-y_{\rf}(t)\right\|<1\ \right.\right\}

is bounded, too. Then the set
K:=\overline{O\times N}
is compact and by definition of
N
and
O
we find that (
25
) holds.
∎

The following result provides a number
M>0
with
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})\neq\emptyset
for all initial values
x^{0}
from any compact set which satisfies a condition similar to (
25
).

Proposition 4.9
.

Consider system (
1
) with
(f,g,h)\in\mathcal{N}^{m}
. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{G}
,
\ y_{\rf}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{m})
,
T>0
,
t^{0}\in\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
,
x^{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{t^{0}}^{\varphi}
, and
\Phi:\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{R}^{n}

be a diffeomorphism such that the coordinate transformation
\Phi(x)=(y,\eta)
puts the
system (
1
) into Byrnes-Isidori form (
8
). Let
(y^{0},\eta^{0})=\Phi(x^{0})

and
K\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
be a compact set with

\ \forall\,y\in\mathcal{Y}^{\varphi,y^{0}}_{y_{\rf}}([t^{0},t^{0}+T])\ \forall\,t\in[t^{0},t^{0}+T]:\hskip 10.00002pt(y(t),\eta(t;t^{0},\eta^{0},y))\in K.

(26)

If

M\geq G_{\rm max}\left(P_{\rm max}+\left\|\tfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\tfrac{1}{\varphi}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\dot{y}_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}\right),

(27)

where, with
p(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\Gamma(\cdot)
as in (
7
) and (
8
),

\displaystyle P_{\rm max}:=\max_{(y,\eta)\in K}\|p(y,\eta)\|,\hskip 10.00002ptG_{\rm max}:=\max_{(y,\eta)\in K}\|\Gamma(\Phi^{-1}(y,\eta))^{-1}\|,

then
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Step 1
:
We first show the existence of
M>0
satisfying (
27
). Note that
p
and

\Gamma
from (
7
) and (
8
) are continuous and
\Gamma
is pointwise invertible.
Therefore,
P_{\rm max}
and
G_{\rm max}
are well-defined. Furthermore, the essential supremum

\left\|\dot{y}_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}
is finite, because
y_{\rf}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{m})
. Since
\varphi
is
an element of
W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R})
, always positive and
\inf_{t\geq 0}\varphi(t)>0
the
reciprocal
\psi:=1/\varphi
is an element of
W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R})
, too, and in particular

\dot{\psi}
is bounded. Thus,
M
can be chosen as in (
27
).

Step 2
:
We construct a control function
u
and show that
u\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
.
To this end, define
e(t):=y(t)-y_{\rf}(t)
and observe that, since

x^{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{t^{0}}^{\varphi}
, we have
\varphi(t^{0})\left\|e(t^{0})\right\|<1
.
The application of the output feedback

u(t)=\Gamma\big(\Phi^{-1}(y(t),\eta(t))\big)^{-1}\left(-p(y(t),\eta(t))+\varphi(t^{0})e(t^{0})\dot{\psi}(t)+\dot{y}_{\rf}(t)\right)

to the system (
8
) leads to a closed-loop system. If this initial value problem is
considered on the interval
[t^{0},t^{0}+T]
, then there exists a unique maximal solution

(y,\eta):[t^{0},\omega)\to\mathds{R}^{n}
with
\omega\in(t^{0},t^{0}+T]
and if
(y,\eta)
is bounded, then
\omega=t^{0}+T
, cf.
[
31
, § 10, Thm. XX]
. Then we find for all
t\in[t^{0},\omega)
that

\displaystyle\left\|e(t)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{t^{0}}^{t}\dot{y}(s)-\dot{y}_{\rf}(s)\ \text{d}s+e(t^{0})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{t^{0}}^{t}p(y(s),\eta(s))+\Gamma\big(\Phi^{-1}(y(s),\eta(s))\big)u(s)-\dot{y}_{\rf}(s)\ \text{d}s+e(t^{0})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{t^{0}}^{t}\varphi(t^{0})e(t^{0})\dot{\psi}(s)\ \text{d}s+e(t^{0})\right\|=\left\|\varphi(t^{0})e(t^{0})\left(\psi(t)-\psi(t^{0})\right)+e(t^{0})\right\|

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\varphi(t^{0})\left\|e(t^{0})\right\|}_{<1}\psi(t)<\psi(t).

This means, the tracking error
e
remains within the
funnel, i.e.,
(y(t),\eta(t))\in\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\varphi}
for all
t\in[t^{0},\omega)
. Thus,
y
is uniformly bounded by

\left\|y\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|y-y_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|y_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\psi\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|y_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}.

Since the error remains within the funnel, the output
y
, defined on
[t^{0},\omega)
,
can be extended to an element
\tilde{y}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\varphi,y^{0}}_{y_{\rf}}([t^{0},t^{0}+T])
and so by assumption (
26
) we have

\ \forall\,t\in[t^{0},\omega):\hskip 10.00002pt(y(t),\eta(t;t^{0},\eta^{0},y))\in K.

Therefore,
(y,\eta)
is bounded and hence
\omega=t^{0}+T
and, with the same arguments,
(y,\eta)
has a continuous extension to
[t^{0},t^{0}+T]
. Furthermore, by definition of
u
it is clear that
\|u\|_{\infty}\leq M
and hence
u\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
, which completes the proof.
∎

The result of Proposition
4.9
essentially guarantees initial feasibility of
Algorithm
2.9
for all initial values from a given bounded set, which we will
summarize in the following theorem. To further obtain recursive feasibility we need to ensure that,
after the application of a control
u
from
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
over an interval
[t^{0},t]
, the set of
controls corresponding to the new state value, namely
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t,x(t;t^{0},x^{0},u))
, is non-empty as well.

Theorem 4.10
.

Consider system (
1
) with
(f,g,h)\in\mathcal{N}^{m}
. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{G}
,
\ y_{\rf}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{m})
,
t^{0}\in\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
, and
B\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\varphi}_{t^{0}}
be a bounded set.
Then, there exists
M>0
such that

\displaystyle\ \forall\,x^{0}\in B\ \forall\,T>0:\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})\neq\emptyset

(28)

and, furthermore,

\displaystyle\ \forall\,x^{0}\in B\ \forall\,T_{1},T_{2}>0\ \forall\,u\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T_{1}}(M,t^{0},x^{0})\ \forall\,t\in[t^{0},t^{0}+T_{1}]:\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T_{2}}(M,t,x(t;t^{0},x^{0},u))\neq\emptyset.

(29)

Proof.

Let
\Phi:\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{R}^{n}
be a diffeomorphism such that the coordinate transformation
\Phi(x)=(y,\eta)
puts
the system (
1
) into Byrnes-Isidori form (
8
). Fix
x^{0}\in B
and set
(y^{0},\eta^{0}):=\Phi(x^{0})
. According to Lemma
4.8
there exists a
compact set
K
such that (
25
) holds. In particular,
K
satisfies (
26
) for every
T>0
. Therefore, Proposition
4.9
yields that there exists
M>0
, independent of
x^{0}
, such that
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})\neq\emptyset
for all
T>0
, which shows (
28
).

If, for any
T_{1}>0
an arbitrary but fixed control function
u\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T_{1}}(M,t^{0},x^{0})
is
applied to the system (
1
), then the output
y
of the system (i.e.,
y(\cdot):=h(x(\cdot;t^{0},x^{0},u))
) evolves within the funnel and
is therefore an element of
\mathcal{Y}^{\varphi,y^{0}}_{y_{\rf}}([t^{0},t^{0}+T_{1}])
. By (
25
),
this implies
\Phi(x(t;t^{0},x^{0},u))\in K
for all
t\in[t^{0},t^{0}+T_{1}]
. If, for any

\widehat{t}\in[t^{0},t^{0}+T_{1}]
, the system is considered on the interval
[\widehat{t},\widehat{t}+T_{2}]
with
T_{2}>0
and the current state
x(\widehat{t};t^{0},x^{0},u)
of the system as initial
value, then the prerequisites for Proposition
4.9
are still met on the interval

[\widehat{t},\widehat{t}+T_{2}]
, i.e.,
K
satisfies (
26
) in the sense

\ \forall\,\tilde{y}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\varphi,\widehat{y}}_{y_{\rf}}([\widehat{t},\widehat{t}+T_{2}])\ \forall\,t\in[\widehat{t},\widehat{t}+T_{2}]:\hskip 10.00002pt(\tilde{y}(t),\eta(t;\widehat{t},\widehat{\eta},\tilde{y}))\in K,

where
(\widehat{y},\widehat{\eta}):=\Phi(x(\widehat{t};t^{0},x^{0},u))\in K
. To see this, observe that for any

\tilde{y}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\varphi,\widehat{y}}_{y_{\rf}}([\widehat{t},\widehat{t}+T_{2}])
there exists
\bar{y}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\varphi,y^{0}}_{y_{\rf}}([t^{0},\widehat{t}+T_{2}])
with
\bar{y}|_{[\widehat{t},\widehat{t}+T_{2}]}=\tilde{y}
and

\bar{y}|_{[t^{0},\widehat{t}]}=y
(
\bar{y}
is continuous since
y(\widehat{t})=\widehat{y}
) and we have

\eta(t;t^{0},\eta^{0},\bar{y})=\eta(t;\widehat{t},\widehat{\eta},\tilde{y})
for
t\in[\widehat{t},\widehat{t}+T_{2}]
,
thus the assertion follows from (
26
). Therefore,
Proposition
4.9
can again be applied and yields
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T_{2}}(M,\widehat{t},x(\widehat{t};t^{0},x^{0},u))\neq\emptyset
, which completes the proof.
∎

Example 4.11
.

We revisit Example
3.1
and calculate a number
M>0
satisfying (
28
) and (
29
) to illustrate Theorem
4.10
for the linear case. Consider the system (
16
)
in Byrnes-Isidori form with
(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},A_{4})\in\mathds{R}^{m\times m}\times\mathds{R}^{m\times(n-m)}\times\mathds{R}^{(n-m)\times m}\times\mathds{R}^{(n-m)\times(n-m)}
and
t^{0}\in\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
.
Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{G}
,
y_{\rf}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{m})
and define
\psi:=1/\varphi
. Further, assume that
A_{4}
is Hurwitz, i.e., all of its eigenvalues have a negative real part.
Then there exist
\alpha>0
,
\beta\geq 1
such that

\ \forall\,x\in\mathds{R}^{n-m}\ \forall\,\text{$t\geq t^{0}$}:\hskip 10.00002pt\left\|\mathrm{e}^{A_{4}(t-t^{0})}x\right\|\leq\beta\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha(t-t^{0})}\left\|x\right\|.

Let

be an arbitrary, but fixed bounded set. We show that for

B:=\left\{\ y\in\mathds{R}^{m}\ \left|\ \vphantom{y\in\mathds{R}^{m}}\varphi(t^{0})\left\|y-y_{\rf}(t^{0})\right\|<1\ \right.\right\}\times N\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\varphi}_{t^{0}}

\displaystyle M:=\left\|\Gamma^{-1}\right\|\left(\left(\left\|A_{1}\right\|+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\left\|A_{2}\right\|\left\|A_{3}\right\|\right)\left(\left\|\psi\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|y_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}\right)+\beta\left\|A_{2}\right\|\sup_{\eta^{0}\in N}\left\|\eta_{0}\right\|+\left\|\dot{\psi}\right\|_{\infty}\!\!+\left\|\dot{y}_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}\right)

the conditions (
28
) and (
29
) are
satisfied. One can see that
M
is chosen according to inequality (
27
) with
a more accurate estimate for
P_{\max}
.

To this end, let
T>0
and
(y^{0},\eta^{0})\in B
be arbitrary and denote

e(t):=y(t)-y_{\rf}(t)
. Since
the initial value is inside the funnel, we have
\varphi(t^{0})\left\|e(t^{0})\right\|<1
.
If the output feedback

is applied to the system (
16
), then clearly a unique global
solution
(y,\eta):[t^{0},\infty)\to\mathds{R}^{n}
exists and, as in the proof of
Proposition
4.9
, we may calculate that
\left\|e(t)\right\|<\psi(t)
for all
t\geq t^{0}
. As a consequence
(y(t),\eta(t))\in\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\varphi}
for all
t\geq t^{0}
, and
y
is uniformly
bounded by

\left\|y\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|y-y_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|y_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\psi\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|y_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}.

Therefore, for
t\geq t^{0}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\eta(t;t^{0},\eta^{0},y)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathrm{e}^{A_{4}(t-t^{0})}\eta^{0}+\int_{t^{0}}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{A_{4}(t-s)}A_{3}y(s)\ \text{d}s\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathrm{e}^{A_{4}(t-t^{0})}\right\|\left\|\eta^{0}\right\|+\int_{t^{0}}^{t}\left\|\mathrm{e}^{A_{4}(t-s)}\right\|\left\|A_{3}\right\|\left\|y(s)\right\|\ \text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\beta\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha(t-t^{0})}\sup_{\eta^{0}\in N}\left\|\eta^{0}\right\|+\int_{t^{0}}^{t}\beta\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha(t-t^{0})}\left\|A_{3}\right\|\left(\left\|\psi\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|y_{\rf}\right\|_{\infty}\right)\ \text{d}s

As a consequence we see that
\|u\|_{\infty}\leq M
and thus

\ \forall\,T>0:\ u\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},(y^{0},\eta^{0}))\neq\emptyset

and (
28
) is satisfied.
If any
u\in\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},(y^{0},\eta^{0}))
is applied to the
system (
16
) and the system is then considered for any

\widehat{t}\in[t^{0},t^{0}+T]
and
\widehat{T}>0
on the interval
[\widehat{t},\widehat{t}+\widehat{T}]

and it can be similarly shown that the feedback control

\widehat{u}(t):=\Gamma^{-1}\left(-A_{1}y(t)-A_{2}\eta(t)+\varphi(\widehat{t})e(\widehat{t})\dot{\psi}(t)+\dot{y}_{\rf}(t)\right)

leads to an element of
\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{\widehat{T}}(M,\widehat{t},x(\widehat{t};t^{0},x^{0},u))
, by
which
M
satisfies (
29
). Here we like to emphasize that the
estimate for
\eta
needs to be carried out in terms of
t^{0}
, i.e., for
\widehat{y}(t):=h(x(t;\widehat{t},x(\widehat{t};t^{0},x^{0},u),\widehat{u}))
denoting the output on

[\widehat{t},\widehat{t}+\widehat{T}]
and
\widehat{\eta}=\eta(\widehat{t};t^{0},x^{0},u)
we have
that

\displaystyle\eta(t;\widehat{t},\widehat{\eta},\widehat{y})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{A_{4}(t-\widehat{t})}\widehat{\eta}+\int_{\widehat{t}}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{A_{4}(t-s)}A_{3}\widehat{y}(s)\ \text{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{A_{4}(t-t^{0})}\eta^{0}+\int_{t^{0}}^{\widehat{t}}\mathrm{e}^{A_{4}(t-s)}A_{3}y(s)\ \text{d}s+\int_{\widehat{t}}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{A_{4}(t-s)}A_{3}\widehat{y}(s)\ \text{d}s

as for

.

.

Proof of Theorem
2.10
.

Step 1
:
According to Theorem

, there exists
M>0

) and (

).

be an arbitrary initial value and
T\geq\delta
.

\mathcal{U}^{\varphi}_{T}(M,t^{0},x^{0})\neq\emptyset

,

5
Conclusion

In the present paper we have shown that the FMPC scheme proposed in
[
3
]
,
which solves the problem of tracking a reference signal within a prescribed performance funnel, is
initially and recursively feasible for an arbitrary finite prediction horizon when applied to
nonlinear multi-input multi-output systems with relative degree one and stable internal dynamics
(in the sense of a BIBS condition). By exploiting concepts from funnel control and using a new
“funnel-like” stage cost function, feasibility is achieved without any need for additional

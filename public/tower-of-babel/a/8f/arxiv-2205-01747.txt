PBW filtration and monomial bases for Demazure modules in types A and C
George Balla, Ghislain Fourier, and Kunda Kambaso

Abstract
. We characterise the symplectic Weyl group elements such that the FFLV basis is compatible with the PBW filtration on symplectic Demazure modules, extending type A results by the second author. Surprisingly, the number of such elements depends not on the type A or C of the Lie algebra but on the rank only. Keywords : PBW filtration; FFLV basis; Demazure modules



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex simple, finite-dimensional Lie algebra. For a dominant integral weight
\lambda
, let
V(\lambda)
denote the simple, finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module of highest weight
\lambda
and let
v_{\lambda}\in V(\lambda)
be a fixed highest weight vector. Let
\text{gr }V(\lambda)
denote the associated graded module with respect to the PBW filtration on
V(\lambda)
. These degenerate modules have been studied extensively in the last decade, mainly also due to the induced degenerate flag variety, defined by Feigin in
[
Fei12
]
as a highest weight orbit of an action of a degenerate group on
\text{gr }V(\lambda)
. One of the achievements of this framework so far is a “new” monomial basis of
\text{gr }V(\lambda)
and hence of
V(\lambda)
for
\mathfrak{sl}_{n},\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}

[
FFL11
,
FFL11a
]
. These monomials are naturally assigned with lattice points in a normal polytope
P(\lambda)\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}
,
N
the number of positive roots, known as the FFLV polytope.

We seek to understand if this basis is compatible with Demazure modules. The second author has provided in
[
Fou16
]
the compatibility for Demazure modules in type
A
corresponding to a class of Weyl group elements that avoid the patterns
4231
and
2413
. These are called triangular Weyl group elements. Formulated differently, a particular face of the FFLV polytope indexes a basis of the graded Demazure modules for triangular Weyl group elements. The PBW-degenerated Schubert varieties in that context were also studied. The main goal of the current paper is to extend these results to a more general setting that also includes Demazure modules and Schubert varieties in type
C
.

Let
R^{-}
denote the set of negative roots of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. We call a subset
A\subset R^{-}
,
FFLV-admissible
if it is closed under taking sums inside
R^{-}
and stable for a
join
operation (see Definition
3.1
). This join operation allows for a straightening law on monomials. For example, the sets corresponding to the triangular subsets studied in
[
Fou16
]
are admissible but admissibility is not restricted to triangular subsets, as for example, in type
{\tt A}
every single element set is admissible. We consider a submodule of
V(\lambda)
corresponding to an FFLV-admissible set
A
, which we denote by
V_{A}(\lambda)
, generated through
v_{\lambda}
by the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
defined by
A
. It turns out that the induced PBW filtration is compatible with the one on
V(\lambda)
(Proposition
5.2
). Let
\text{gr }V_{A}(\lambda)
denote the associated graded submodule.

Our first interest is in constructing a monomial basis for
V_{A}(\lambda)
and
\text{gr }V_{A}(\lambda)
. For this, we will consider the following faces of the polytope
P(\lambda)
: for
A\subset R^{-}
, let
P_{A}(\lambda)
denote the face of
P(\lambda)
obtained by setting the coordinates
s_{\beta}=0
for all
\beta\notin A
. Let
S_{A}(\lambda)
denote the set of lattice points in
P_{A}(\lambda)
. We prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
or
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
and let
A\subset R^{-}
be FFLV-admissible, then:

(a)

for all
\lambda,\mu
, dominant integral weights,
S_{A}(\lambda)+S_{A}(\mu)=S_{A}(\lambda+\mu)
.

(b)

the set
S_{A}(\lambda)
parametrizes a monomial basis of the PBW-graded submodule
\text{gr }V_{A}(\lambda)\subset\text{gr }V(\lambda)
and hence of
V_{A}(\lambda)
.

Our approach in proving part (b) of the above theorem relies on having defining relations for the modules
\text{gr }V(\lambda)
as constructed in
[
FFL11
,
FFL11a
]
(see Theorem
5.7
).
A direct consequence is the following and explains our interest in considering FFLV-admissible subsets: if
A\subset R^{-}
is not FFLV-admissible and
\lambda
is regular, then
S_{A}(\lambda)
doesn’t parametrize (in a “natural” way) a monomial basis of
\text{gr }V_{A}(\lambda)
(Lemma
5.8
). We should note, that a Kogan face construction as in
[
Kog00
,
KST12
,
BF15
]
is not applicable, as in contrast to that case, the coordinates are assigned positive roots and not simple roots (with respect to a reduced expression of the longest Weyl group element).

We would also like to note that the polytopes constructed in
[
Kam21
]
parametrizing monomial bases for submodules studied there, are in general not faces of the FFLV polytope
P(\lambda)
in types
A
and
C
. On the other hand, monomial bases similar to those discussed here have been constructed in
[
Mak19
]
for orthogonal modules in type
B
. However, our approach is not applicable in that situation, since defining relations for type
B
are still missing.

It turns out that the submodules
V_{A}(\lambda)
are favourable in the sense of
[
FFL17a
]
, so we obtain flat PBW-degenerated symplectic Schubert varieties which degenerate further into toric varieties (Proposition
5.11
). Moreover, we consequently describe a monomial basis for the homogeneous coordinate rings of the PBW-degenerate Schubert varieties labelled by PBW-semistandard tableaux (Proposition
5.17
).

Our paper is organised as follows: in Section
2
, we recall basic notation on Lie algebras while in Section
3
,
we introduce FFLV-admissible subsets and show that they are compatible with Dynkin automorphisms. In Section
4
, we describe the correspondence between FFLV-admissible subsets and triangular Weyl group elements and in Section
5
we describe the monomial basis for our submodules and give the geometric interpretation in terms of PBW-degenerated Schubert varieties.

2.
Preliminaries: notation on Lie algebras

We follow
[
Hum12
]
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the simple, complex Lie algebra of classical type
{\tt A}_{n}
,
{\tt B}_{n}
,
{\tt C}_{n}
or
{\tt D}_{n}
.
We fix a triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-}
. Having fixed this decomposition, we denote by
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
, the set of simple roots,
R^{+}
and
R^{-}
, the sets of positive roots and negative roots respectively.

•

For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
(of type
A
n
) and
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
, we abbreviate
\alpha_{i,j}:=\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}.
Then
R_{{\tt A}_{n}}^{+}=\{\alpha_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq j\leq n\}
.

•

For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}
(of type
B
n
),
\alpha_{i,j}
as above and
\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}:=\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j-1}+2\alpha_{j}+\cdots+2\alpha_{n}
, we have
R_{{\tt B}_{n}}^{+}=\{\alpha_{i,j},\alpha_{k,\overline{l}}\mid 1\leq i\leq j\leq n,\,\,1\leq k<l\leq n\}
.

•

For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
(of type
C
n
),
\alpha_{i,j}
as above and
\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}:=\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j-1}+2\alpha_{j}+\cdots+2\alpha_{n-1}+\alpha_{n}
(with
\alpha_{i,n}=\alpha_{i,\overline{n}}
), one has
R_{{\tt C}_{n}}^{+}=\{\alpha_{i,j},\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}\mid 1\leq i\leq j\leq n\}
.

•

Finally, for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
(of type
D
n
),
\alpha_{i,j}
as before,
\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}:=\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{n-2}+\alpha_{j}+\cdots+\alpha_{n},
the set of positive roots is given by
R_{{\tt D}_{n}}^{+}=\{\alpha_{i,j},\alpha_{k,\overline{l}}\mid 1\leq i\leq j\leq n-1,\,\,1\leq k<l\leq n\}
.

For an element
\beta=\sum a_{i}\alpha_{i}\in\sum\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
we denote
\text{supp }\beta=\{i\mid a_{i}\neq 0\}
and further say
\alpha
and
\beta
are linked, if
\text{supp }\alpha+\beta
defines a connected subdiagram of the Dynkin diagram.

For
\beta\in R^{+}
, let
e_{\beta}
and
f_{\beta}
denote fixed basis elements in the root spaces of
\mathfrak{g}
of weights
\beta
and
-\beta
respectively. We will use short notation
e_{i,j}
resp.
f_{i,\overline{j}}
for
e_{\beta}
and
f_{\beta}
where
\beta=\alpha_{i,j}
or
\beta=\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}
. For
\beta\in R^{+}
,
h_{\beta}
will denote the corresponding co-root.

Let the set of (dominant) integral weights of
\mathfrak{g}
be denoted by
P
(resp.
P^{+}
). The fundamental weights are denoted
\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}\}
, they generate the monoid
P^{+}
.
For
\lambda\in P^{+}
, we denote by
V(\lambda)
, the simple, finite-dimensional highest weight module of highest weight
\lambda
, with a highest weight vector
v_{\lambda}
.

For Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
, let
U(\mathfrak{a})
denote the universal enveloping algebra. Let
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{l})
be an ordered basis of
\mathfrak{a}
, then the PBW filtration on
U(\mathfrak{a})
is defined as

U(\mathfrak{a})_{s}:=\langle z_{i_{1}}\cdots z_{i_{r}}\mid r\leq s,z_{i_{j}}\in\mathfrak{a}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}.

The famous PBW theorem states that the associated graded algebra is the symmetric algebra
S(\mathfrak{a})
. On a cyclic
U(\mathfrak{a})
-module
M
with generator
m\in M
, one has the induced filtration
M_{s}:=U(\mathfrak{a})_{s}\cdot m
and the associated graded module
\text{gr }M
is a cyclic module for
S(\mathfrak{n}^{-})
.

We denote by
W
, the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
, it is generated by the simple reflections
s_{i}
corresponding to the simple roots
\alpha_{i}
.
The Weyl group acts on the weights in
P
and on the weights of
V(\lambda)
, leaving the dimension of the weight spaces invariant.
For
w\in W
, the extremal weight space of weight
w(\lambda)
in
V(\lambda)
is therefore one-dimensional and we denote
v_{w(\lambda)}
a basis element in this weight space.

Let
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
be a Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
The Demazure module corresponding to
w\in W
is defined to be the
\mathfrak{b}
-module
V_{w}(\lambda):=U(\mathfrak{b})\cdot v_{w(\lambda)}
generated by the element
v_{w(\lambda)}
.
Notice that when
w
is the longest element in
W
, the vector space
V_{w}(\lambda)
coincides with
V(\lambda)
.

Now let
\mathfrak{g}
be of type
X
and
\tau
an automorphism of the Dynkin diagram,
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
be the fixed point algebra of type
X^{\tau}.

Let
R_{X}^{-}
and
R_{X^{\tau}}^{-}
denote negative roots of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
respectively. In particular, we will consider the following automorphisms and fixed point algebras. The automorphism of type:

(a)

{\tt A}_{2n-1}
, and corresponding fixed point algebra which is of type
C
n
.

(b)

{\tt D}_{n+1}
, and corresponding fixed point algebra being of type
B
n
.

We introduce two partial orders on
R^{-}
. The first one is classical:

\beta<\gamma:\Leftrightarrow\gamma-\beta\in\sum\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{i}.

Let
\delta\in R^{-}+R^{-}
, then

\mathcal{P}(\delta):=\begin{cases}(\delta,0)\quad\text{ if }-\delta\text{ is the highest root of a Dynkin subdiagram, }\\
\{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in(R^{-}\cup\{0\})^{2}\mid\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\delta\}\,\,\text{ else}.\end{cases}

We define a partial order
\leq
on
\mathcal{P}(\delta)
by

(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\leq(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}):\Leftrightarrow\gamma_{1}\leq\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\leq\gamma_{2}.

For clarity, we include here a short example of the above partial order on
\mathcal{P}(\delta)
. In type
A
3
, consider the pair of negative roots
(\alpha_{1,2},\alpha_{2,3})
, then one has
(\alpha_{1,3},\alpha_{2,2})\in\mathcal{P}(\alpha_{1,2}+\alpha_{2,3})
, and
(\alpha_{1,2},\alpha_{2,3})<(\alpha_{1,3},\alpha_{2,2})
. Consider type
C
3
and the pair
(\alpha_{1,\overline{2}},\alpha_{1,3})
, then
(\alpha_{1,\overline{2}},\alpha_{1,3})<(\alpha_{1,\overline{1}},\alpha_{2,3})\in\mathcal{P}(\alpha_{1,\overline{2}}+\alpha_{1,3})
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in R^{-}\times R^{-}
, then we define the
join
of
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
:

\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}:=\{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\mid(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{P}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})\mid(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})<(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\}.

By abuse of notation, we denote
\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}
also the set of roots in
\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}
.

Example 2.2
.

To explain the join of two roots, we work it out for types
A
and
C
:

(1)

Suppose
\beta_{1}=\alpha_{i_{1},i_{2}},\beta_{2}=\alpha_{j_{1},j_{2}}
with
i_{1}\leq j_{1},i_{2},j_{2}\in I
, then:

\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}=\begin{cases}\emptyset&\text{ if }i_{1}=j_{1}\text{ or }i_{2}<j_{1}-1\text{ or }j_{2}\leq i_{2},\\
\{(\alpha_{i_{1},j_{2}},\alpha_{j_{1},i_{2}})\}&\text{ if }j_{1}-1\leq i_{2}<j_{2}.\end{cases}

(2)

Suppose
\beta_{1}=\alpha_{i_{1},\overline{i}_{2}},\beta_{2}=\alpha_{j_{1},\overline{j}_{2}}
with
i_{1}\leq j_{1},i_{2},j_{2}\in I
, then:

\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}=\begin{cases}\emptyset&\text{ if }i_{2}<j_{1},\\
\{(\alpha_{i_{1},\overline{j}_{1}},\alpha_{j_{2},\overline{i}_{2}}),(\alpha_{i_{1},\overline{j}_{2}},\alpha_{j_{1},\overline{i}_{2}})\}&\text{ if }j_{1}\leq j_{2}<i_{2},\\
\{(\alpha_{i_{1},\overline{j}_{1}},\alpha_{i_{2},\overline{j}_{2}})\}&\text{ if }j_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq j_{2}.\\
\end{cases}

(3)

For
\beta_{1}=\alpha_{i_{1},i_{2}},\beta_{2}=\alpha_{j_{1},\overline{j}_{2}}
with
i_{1}\leq j_{1},i_{2},j_{2}\in I
, one has:

\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}=\begin{cases}\emptyset&\text{ if }i_{1}\leq i_{2}<j_{1}-1\leq j_{2},\\
\{(\alpha_{i_{1},\overline{j}_{1}},\alpha_{j_{2},i_{2}}),(\alpha_{i_{1},\overline{j}_{2}},\alpha_{j_{1},i_{2}})\}&\text{ if }j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq i_{2},\\
\{(\alpha_{i_{1},\overline{j}_{2}},\alpha_{j_{1},i_{2}}),(\alpha_{i_{1},\overline{j}_{1}},0)\}&\text{ if }j_{1}\leq i_{2}=j_{2}-1,\\
\{(\alpha_{i_{1},\overline{j}_{2}},\alpha_{j_{1},i_{2}})\}&\text{ if }j_{1}\leq i_{2}<j_{2}-1,\\
\{(\alpha_{i_{1},\overline{j}_{2}},0)\}&\text{ if }i_{1}\leq i_{2}=j_{1}-1\leq j_{2}.\end{cases}

(4)

Lastly, for
\beta_{1}=\alpha_{i_{1},i_{2}},\beta_{2}=\alpha_{j_{1},\overline{j}_{2}}
with
j_{1}<i_{1},i_{2},j_{2}\in I
, we have:

\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}=\begin{cases}\emptyset&\text{ if }i_{1}\leq i_{2}<j_{2}-1,\\
\{(\alpha_{j_{1},\overline{i}_{1}},\alpha_{j_{2},i_{2}})\}&\text{ if }i_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq i_{2}\text{ or }j_{1}\leq j_{2}<i_{1}\leq i_{2},\\
\{(\alpha_{j_{1},\overline{i}_{1}},0)\}&\text{ if }i_{1}\leq i_{2}=j_{2}-1.\end{cases}

The second order on
R^{-}
is defined as follows:

Let
\alpha=\sum a_{i}\alpha_{i},\beta=\sum b_{i}\alpha_{i}\in R^{-}
. Let
s=\text{min}\{i\mid a_{i}\neq 0\}
. We define
\alpha\succ\beta
if

\beta=\alpha+\alpha_{s}\text{ and }a_{s}=1

or

\beta=\alpha-\alpha_{j}\text{ for some }j\geq s.

The transitive closure of these cover relations defines an order
\prec
on
R^{-}
.

Example 2.3
.

Two examples to explain the order:

(1)

In type

\alpha_{i,j}\succ\alpha_{k,\ell}\Leftrightarrow i\leq k,j\leq\ell

(2)

and

are non-comparable with respect to

or

from

,

]

A Dyck path

is a sequence of positive roots

such that

•

is a simple root

•

3.
FFLV-admissible sets and fixed point subalgebras

We will introduce the main combinatorial object of our study. We will explain in Section
5

Definition 3.1
.

A subset
A\subseteq R^{-}
is called
FFLV-admissible
(short

(1)

\mathfrak{n}_{A}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in A}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is a Lie subalgebra.

(2)

For all pairs
\alpha\succeq\beta\in A
one has
\alpha\oplus\beta\in A
.

We note, that if
A
is admissible, then

\mathfrak{g}_{A}:=\mathfrak{h}\bigoplus_{\alpha\in A}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

is a Lie subalgebra in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
A\subset R^{-}
be admissible and
S\subset A
. Let
\text{supp }S=\bigcup_{\alpha\in S}\text{supp }\alpha
and

be a connected component. Then
\theta_{S_{i}}
, the highest root of the Dynkin subdiagram of

belongs to

.

Proof.

The statement follows for roots of the form
\alpha_{i,j}
with

straight from the definition. Now, let
\beta\in A
and suppose
\beta\oplus\beta\neq\emptyset
. Let

\theta_{S}\in\beta\oplus\beta
.

3.1.
Fixed point subalgebras and admissible sets

We discuss here how admissible sets and fixed point sets of Dynkin automorphisms are compatible. Recall the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of type
X
and the corresponding fixed point algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
of type
X^{\tau}
for a fixed Dynkin automorphism
\tau
. We have two notions of admissibility for
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
: the first one is given by Definition
3.1
. For the second one, we denote for a
\tau
-invariant
A\subset R^{-}_{X}
the set of orbits by
A^{\tau}\subset R^{-}_{X^{\tau}}
. Recall that every subset of
R^{-}_{X^{\tau}}
is of the form
A^{\tau}
for a unique
A\subset R^{-}_{X}
.

Definition 3.4
.

We call
A^{\tau}\subset R^{-}_{X^{\tau}}

induced admissible
if
A\subset R^{-}_{X}
is admissible.

The following proposition gives the equivalence of the two notions:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
A^{\tau}\subset R_{X^{\tau}}^{-}
, then
A^{\tau}
is admissible if and only if
A^{\tau}
is induced admissible.

Proof.

Let
A=\tau(A)
be admissible and
\beta_{1}^{\tau},\beta_{2}^{\tau}\in A^{\tau}
with
\beta_{1}^{\tau}\succeq\beta_{2}^{\tau}
. We choose minimal representatives
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
with respect to
\succ
. Suppose
(\gamma_{1}^{\tau},\gamma_{2}^{\tau})\in\beta_{1}^{\tau}\oplus\beta_{2}^{\tau}
, e.g.
\gamma_{1}^{\tau}+\gamma_{2}^{\tau}=\beta_{1}^{\tau}+\beta_{2}^{\tau}
and
\gamma_{1}^{\tau}\leq\beta_{1}^{\tau},\beta_{2}^{\tau}\leq\gamma_{2}^{\tau}
. We choose minimal representatives
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
, then

\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}\text{ and }\gamma_{1}\leq\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\leq\gamma_{2}

and hence
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}
.
A
is admissible and and
\tau
invariant, so
(\gamma_{1}^{\tau},\gamma_{2}^{\tau})\in\beta_{1}^{\tau}\oplus\beta_{2}^{\tau}
.

Suppose
A=\tau(A)
and
A^{\tau}
is admissible. Similarly as before, one shows for
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in A
that
\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}\subset A
if both are the minimal or both are the maximal elements in their orbit. We are left with
\beta_{1}
minimal and
\beta_{2}
maximal,
\beta_{1}\succ\beta_{2}
and suppose
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}
. Then
\gamma_{1}^{\tau}+\gamma_{2}^{\tau}=\beta_{1}^{\tau}+\beta_{2}^{\tau}
and
\gamma_{1}^{\tau}\leq\beta_{1}^{\tau},\beta_{2}^{\tau}\leq\gamma_{2}^{\tau}
as those properties are true for
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
. Since
A^{\tau}
is admissible,
\{\gamma_{1}^{\tau},\gamma_{2}^{\tau}\}\in A^{\tau}
and so
\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\}\in A
.
∎

3.2.
Saturated sets

Definition 3.6
.

For a subset
S\subset R^{-}
, we define the
join closure

\overline{S}^{\oplus}
of
S
to be the minimal subset in
R^{-}
, containing
S
and for any two roots
\alpha,\beta\in\overline{S}^{\oplus}
,
\alpha\oplus\beta\subset S
.

This is certainly well-defined and a non-trivial subset, for example in type
{\tt A}
, if
S=\{\alpha\}
, then
S=\overline{S}^{\oplus}
while the closure of the set of all (negative) simple roots is
R^{-}
. Admissible subsets are by definition closed under join.

We consider the join closure of particular subsets:

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
and
\mathbf{p}=(\alpha_{i_{1},j_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{s},j_{s}})
a Dyck path, such that consecutive roots are linked. Then

\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}=\{\alpha_{i_{k},j_{\ell}}\mid 1\leq k,\ell\leq n\}.

Proof.

Let
1\leq k<n
, then

\alpha_{i_{k},j_{k}}\oplus\alpha_{i_{k+1},j_{k+1}}=\{\alpha_{i_{k},j_{k+1}},\alpha_{j_{k},i_{k+1}}\}.

Iterating this procedure gives the proposed set. We are left to show, that this is closed under join: consider
\alpha_{i_{k},j_{\ell}},\alpha_{i_{r},j_{t}}
with
i_{k}\leq i_{r}
and
i_{k},i_{r}\in\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\},j_{\ell},j_{t}\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{s}\}
. Then by Example
2.2
we see that

\alpha_{i_{k},j_{\ell}}\oplus\alpha_{i_{r},j_{t}}=\emptyset

unless
i_{r}-1\leq j_{\ell}<j_{t}
, while in this case

\alpha_{i_{k},j_{\ell}}\oplus\alpha_{i_{r},j_{t}}=\{\alpha_{i_{k},j_{t}},\alpha_{j_{\ell},i_{r}}\}

which is in the proposed set.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
and
\mathbf{p}
be a Dyck path, such that consecutive roots are linked, then

\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}=(\overline{T_{\mathbf{p}}}^{\oplus})^{\tau}\text{ where }T_{\mathbf{p}}=\bigcup_{\alpha^{\tau}\in\mathbf{p}}\{\gamma\in\alpha^{\tau}\}\subset R^{-}_{{\tt A}_{2n-1}}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha^{\tau},\beta^{\tau}\in R^{-}
, then we can read off from Example
2.2
:

\alpha^{\tau}\oplus\beta^{\tau}=\{\gamma_{1}\oplus\gamma_{2}\mid\gamma_{1}\in\alpha^{\tau},\gamma_{2}\in\beta^{\tau}\}^{\tau}.

Applying this to
\mathbf{p}
yields

(\overline{T_{\mathbf{p}}}^{\oplus})^{\tau}\subseteq\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}

since by construction
\tau(\overline{T_{\mathbf{p}}}^{\oplus})=\overline{T_{\mathbf{p}}}^{\oplus}
.

We are left to show that the left hand side is closed under join.
So let
\alpha^{\tau},\beta^{\tau}\in(\overline{T_{\mathbf{p}}}^{\oplus})^{\tau}
, then the first equation in the proof shows that
\alpha^{\tau}\oplus\beta^{\tau}\subset(\overline{T_{\mathbf{p}}}^{\oplus})^{\tau}
, because
\overline{T_{\mathbf{p}}}^{\oplus}
is closed under join.
∎

The following will be a crucial observation in the proof of the main Theorem
5.7
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1},\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
and let
\mathbf{p}
be a Dyck path, such that consecutive roots are linked. Let
\alpha,\beta\in\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}
and suppose
\gamma=\alpha-\beta\in R^{+}
. Then

(\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}+\gamma)\cap R^{-}\subset\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}.

Proof.

For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
and
\mathbf{p}=(\alpha_{i_{1},j_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{s},j_{s}})
,
\gamma
could be only one of the following

\{\alpha_{i_{k},i_{\ell}-1},\alpha_{j_{k}-1,j_{\ell}}\mid k<\ell\}.

Observing, that
\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}
is the set of all roots starting in
i_{k}
and ending in
j_{\ell}
completes the proof in this case.

Turning to
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
, we again observe that
\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}
is admissible and hence induced admissible. Any difference of two roots in
\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}
is in fact the orbit of a difference between two roots in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2n}
. We can therefore reduce the stability of
\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}
to the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-case.
∎

Triangular Weyl group elements

In this section, we consider particular subsets
A\subset R^{+}
. Let
w\in W
, then

We are aiming to classify the
w\in W
, such that
A_{w}
is admissible.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{i}:=i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}
with
i_{j}\in\{1,\ldots,4\}
pairwise distinct. We call
\tau\in S_{N}

\mathbf{i}
-avoiding (

such that

.

4.1.
Type

The case of admissible sets originating from Weyl group elements in type

]

]
and

]

.

Let
w\in S_{n+1}
, then

is called

if

does not contain patterns of the form

and

.

We should note here some interesting facts on triangular elements in type

:

(1)

The number of triangular elements is given by the sequence A032351 in OEIS,
[

]
. The first few elements suggest, that the number might be close to

but this is not true for large

(2)

There are interesting elements in

introduced in

.

(3)

The “smallest” non-triangular element is

(4)

and

]
.

The following can be found in

.

Let

4.2.
Type
C

We turn to the symplectic case and it’s Weyl group
W_{{\tt C}_{n}}
. We consider the symmetric group
S_{2n}
with the involutive element

w_{0}:\{1,\ldots,2n\}\longrightarrow\{1,\ldots,2n\}\;,\;i\mapsto 2n+1-i.

With the help of
w_{0}
we define an involution on
S_{2n}
by

\sigma:S_{2n}\longrightarrow S_{2n}\;,\;\tau\mapsto w_{0}\circ\tau\circ w_{0}.

The symplectic Weyl group
W_{{\tt C}_{n}}
is generated by
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
subject to the relations

s_{i}^{2}=1,(s_{i}s_{j})^{2}=1\text{ for }|i-j|>1,(s_{i}s_{i+1})^{3}=1\text{ for }i<n-1,(s_{n-1}s_{n})^{4}=1.

This symplectic Weyl group is isomorphic to the subgroup of
\sigma
-invariant elements in
S_{2n}
, that is
\tau=w_{0}\circ\tau\circ w_{0}
, via the following identification
\iota

s_{i}\mapsto t_{i}t_{2n-i}\text{ for }i\leq n-1;s_{n}\mapsto t_{n}.

Again we are interested in the
w\in W_{{\tt C}_{n}}
such that
A_{w}
is admissible. Our goal is

Lemma 4.4
.

There is a bijection from the set
\{w\in W_{{\tt C}_{n}}\mid A_{w}\text{ is admissible }\}
to the set
\{w\in W_{{\tt A}_{n}}\mid A_{w}\text{ is admissible }\}
.

Before giving the proof, we use the lemma to define

Definition 4.5
.

An element
w\in W_{{\tt C}_{n}}
is called
symplectic triangular
if
\iota(w)
is triangular for type
A
.

Due to the facts on triangular elements, we deduce

Corollary 4.6
.

The number of symplectic triangular elements is given by the sequence A032351 in OEIS,
[
OEI
]
.

One could ask whether there is also bijection of the admissible sets of
R_{{\tt C}_{n}}
to the admissible sets of
R_{{\tt A}_{n}}
. In turns out, that there are
8
admissible subsets in type
{\tt C}_{2}
and only
7
admissible subsets in type
{\tt A}_{2}
; in type
{\tt A}_{2}
all Weyl group elements are triangular while in type
{\tt C}_{2}
, the list of symplectic triangular Weyl group elements is

\text{id},s_{1},s_{2},s_{1}s_{2},s_{2}s_{1}s_{2},s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}s_{2}.

In type
{\tt A}_{2}
, there is one admissible set which is not obtained from a Weyl group element, namely
\{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\}
, while in type
{\tt C}_{2}
, there are two
\{\alpha_{1,\bar{1}}\}
and
\{\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1,\bar{1}}\}
.

We turn to the proof of Lemma
4.4
, which has three steps:

Proposition 4.7
.

Fix a pattern
\mathbf{i}\subset\{1,\ldots,4\}
. There is a mapping between the elements from
S_{2n}
that avoid
\mathbf{i}
, and the elements from
S_{n+1}
that avoid
\mathbf{i}
.

Proof.

Let
\tau=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{2n})\in S_{2n}
and define
\tilde{\tau}
to be the sequence where all
j>n+1
are replaced by
n+1
. Let
\underline{\tau}
denote the sequence where all
n+1
in
\tilde{\tau}
are deleted except the leftmost. Then
\underline{\tau}\in S_{n+1}
. It follows immediately, that if
\tau
avoids the pattern
\mathbf{i}
, then
\underline{\tau}
avoids
\mathbf{i}
as well.
∎

In the next step, we prove the surjectivity:

Proposition 4.8
.

Consider a pattern
\mathbf{i}^{*}:=i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}
with
i_{j}\in\{1,\ldots,4\}
pairwise distinct such that
i_{1}>i_{2},i_{4}
and
i_{3}>i_{2},i_{4}
. Then there is a surjection between the elements in
S_{2n}
that are
\sigma
-invariant and avoid the pattern
\mathbf{i}^{*}
and the elements in
S_{n+1}
that avoid the pattern
\mathbf{i}^{*}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\tau\in S_{2n}
is invariant under
\sigma
and avoids
\mathbf{i}^{*}
. Let
\underline{\tau}
be the pattern constructed from
\tau
as in the proof of Proposition
4.7
. Let
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<j_{3}<j_{4}\leq n+1
be such that
\underline{\tau}(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3},j_{4})
is the pattern
\mathbf{i}^{*}
. Then either
j_{4}<n+1
, in which case this
\tau
would have the same pattern, which is a contradiction or
j_{4}=n+1
and we set
n+1\leq s\leq 2n
the leftmost element in
\tau
greater or equals
n+1
. Then
\tau(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3},s)
is the pattern
\mathbf{i}^{*}
. We conclude that
\underline{\tau}
avoids the pattern
\mathbf{i}^{*}
.
∎

For the bijection of Lemma
4.4
, it remains to prove the injectivity:

Proof.

Let
w\in S_{n+1}
be an element that avoids the patterns
3142
and
4231
. We will construct the preimage of this element in the following way. Consider the position of
n+1
in the presentation of
w
. Let
k
denote the number of elements left of
n+1
in this presentation. Let
j
be the element at position
i<n+1
. Construct an element
w^{\prime}
as follows. We leave every element
j
at position
i
and every element of the form
2n+1-j
(left of
n+1
), we put at position
2n+1-i
. Now we are left with the element
n+1
and the elements to its right. These are mapped in the following way. The ordering of the elements
1,\ldots,n
determines the ordering of
n+1,\ldots,2n
, because of the involution. Now replace all
n+1
by the elements
n+1,\ldots,2n
which are not placed before, in that order. These are
n-k
elements. So the elements to the right of
n+1
are just shifted by
n-k
to the right.
∎

From Proposition
3.5
and Lemma
4.4
, we can deduce the following analogue to Proposition
4.3
:

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
w\in W_{{\tt C}_{n}}
, then
w^{-1}(R^{+})\cap R^{-}
is admissible if and only if
w
is symplectic triangular.

5.
Application

We discuss in this section the application we have in mind for defining admissible sets. For this we will recall Demazure modules, the PBW filtration and the FFLV monomial bases for simple, finite-dimensional modules.

5.1.
Submodules for admissible sets

We fix
\mathfrak{g}
and an admissible set
A\subset R^{-}
, then
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{A}^{-}
is a subalgebra and hence we can consider
U(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{A})
as a subalgebra in
U(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-})
. We further fix a dominant, integral weight
\lambda
and a highest weight vector
v_{\lambda}\in V(\lambda)
. We define

V_{A}(\lambda):=U(\mathfrak{n}_{A}^{-})\cdot v_{\lambda}.

By construction, this is a
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{A}
-module but in fact, it can be extended to a module for

\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{A}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{A}

where
\mathfrak{n}_{A}^{+}
is the maximal subalgebra in
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
such that the above is invariant under
\mathfrak{n}_{A}^{+}
.

In the special case
A=A_{w}
, one has

V_{A}(\lambda)\cong V_{w}(\lambda)

via the twist
w:U(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{A}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{A})\longrightarrow U(\mathfrak{b})
. We want to analyse the modules
V_{A}(\lambda)
and especially asking for a monomial basis, e.g. a set of monomials in
U(\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{A})
. Note, that in the special case of Demazure modules there are monomial bases known, while we explain in the next section that we are interested in a monomial basis defined by a homogeneous lexicographic order and such bases for Demazure modules are not known in general.

5.2.
PBW filtration and sub objects

For a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
, we explain briefly that the PBW filtration on
U(\mathfrak{a})
is compatible with the filtration on the universal enveloping algebra of a subalgebra. In the case of submodules, one has to ask for mild assumptions which are satisfied in all relevant cases.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{a}
be a subalgebra, then
U(\mathfrak{b})_{s}=U(\mathfrak{a})_{s}\cap U(\mathfrak{b})
, i.e., the PBW filtration is compatible with subalgebras.

Proof.

It is clear, that
U(\mathfrak{b})_{s}\subset U(\mathfrak{a})_{s}\cap U(\mathfrak{b})
. We fix an ordered basis
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})
of
\mathfrak{b}
and extend with
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{t})
to a basis of
\mathfrak{a}
.
We denote the induced basis of
U(\mathfrak{b})
by
\{x_{I}\mid I\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{s}\}
.

U(\mathfrak{a})
is a free
U(\mathfrak{b})
-module with basis given by ordered monomials in
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{t})
and hence a basis of
U(\mathfrak{a})
is given by
\{x_{I}y_{J}\mid I\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{s},J\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{t}\}
.

Suppose now
p\in U(\mathfrak{b})_{r}\setminus U(\mathfrak{b})_{r-1}
, then
p=\sum_{I}c_{I}x_{I}
with
|I|\leq r
and at least one
I
with
|I|=r
. Suppose
p\in U(\mathfrak{a})_{r-1}
, then
p=\sum_{I,J}c_{I,J}x_{I}y_{J}
with
|I|+|J|\leq r-1
and by construction there is
c_{I,J}\neq 0
for some
J
with
|J|\neq 0
. But the freeness of
U(\mathfrak{a})
and the equality
\sum_{I}c_{I}x_{I}=\sum_{I,J}c_{I,J}x_{I}y_{J}
provide a contradiction. This implies that
U(\mathfrak{b})_{s}=U(\mathfrak{a})_{s}\cap U(\mathfrak{b})
.
∎

We turn to PBW filtration of sub-modules, say
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{a}
a subalgebra and
V
a cyclic
\mathfrak{a}
-module with generator
v
. Here one can apply the PBW filtration to

V_{\mathfrak{b}}:=U(\mathfrak{b})\cdot v\subset V.

In contrast to the case of subalgebras, this filtration is in general not compatible with the filtration on
V
. Let
\mathfrak{a}=\langle x,y,z\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
be the abelian three dimensional Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{b}=\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
.
We consider the
U(\mathfrak{a})
-module defined by the ideal
(x^{2},y^{2},z^{2},xz,yz,xy-z)
. Then the degree vector of
\text{gr }V
is
(1,3,0,\ldots)
.
While the submodule through
1
generated by
\mathfrak{b}
has degree vector
(1,2,1,0\ldots)
.
We see, that if the vanishing ideal is not homogeneous, the PBW filtration is not compatible. One needs a few more properties to have the PBW filtration on modules compatible with submodules.

Let for the moment
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple, finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra with triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-}
and let
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{n}^{-}
be a subalgebra such that
\mathfrak{b}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R^{-}}\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
. Let
\lambda
be a dominant integral weight for
\mathfrak{g}
and
V(\lambda)
the irreducible highest weight module with highest weight
\lambda
,
v_{\lambda}
a highest weight generator.

Proposition 5.2
.

With this setup, one has
\text{gr }V_{\mathfrak{b}}(\lambda)\subset\text{gr }V(\lambda)
, the PBW filtration on
V(\lambda)
is compatible with the filtration on the subalgebra.

Proof.

We follow here the proof in
[
CFF21
]
. The PBW filtration on
V(\lambda)
is induced by the filtration on each weight space
V_{\mu}(\lambda)
and for the associated graded module, it is enough to compare the degree of each monomial in root vectors. Now let
0\neq f_{\beta_{1}}\cdots f_{\beta_{s}}\cdot v_{\lambda}\in V_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda)
with
f_{\beta_{i}}\in\mathfrak{b}
. Suppose there exists
f_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots f_{\gamma_{r}}\cdot v_{\lambda}=f_{\beta_{1}}\cdots f_{\beta_{s}}\cdot v_{\lambda}
for some
r<s
. From
[
OH02
]
, we deduce that there are two kind of exchange relations on roots. The first one is homogeneous and for our purpose, we can ignore it here. The second is not homogeneous and reads as
\alpha+\beta=\gamma
for some roots. Suppose now there are
i,j,k
with
\beta_{i}+\beta_{j}=\gamma_{k}
. But this implies that
f_{\gamma_{k}}\in\mathfrak{b}
, since
\mathfrak{b}
is a subalgebra. Hence we deduce that
f_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots f_{\gamma_{r}}\in U(\mathfrak{b})
and hence the PBW filtrations are compatible.
∎

5.3.
The FFLV bases

In
[
FFL11
]
and
[
FFL11a
]
, monomial bases of simple, finite-dimensional modules for complex Lie algebras of type
A
and
C
were introduced:

Let
\lambda=\sum m_{i}\omega_{i}\in P^{+}
, then for type
A
(resp. type
C
), the following polytope has been defined in
[
FFL11
]
(resp.
[
FFL11a
]
):

P(\lambda)=\left\{(x_{\alpha})\in\mathbb{R}^{\sharp R^{+}}_{\geq 0}\mid\forall\;\text{Dyck paths }\mathbf{p},\sum_{\alpha_{\in}\mathbf{p}}x_{\alpha}\leq m_{e(p)}-m_{s(p)}\right\}

We denote
S(\lambda)=P(\lambda)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{\sharp R^{+}}
, the lattice points.
In the mentioned paper it is proved that

\left\{\prod_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}^{s_{\alpha}}\cdot v_{\lambda}\mid\mathbf{s}\in S(\lambda)\right\}

is (for any chosen order in the monomials) a basis of
V(\lambda)
. This basis share an interesting property as we will explain here:

Theorem 5.3

(
[
FFL11
,
FFL11a
]
)
.

The following is a basis of
\text{gr }V(\lambda)
:

\left\{\prod_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}^{s_{\alpha}}\cdot v_{\lambda}\mid\mathbf{s}\in S(\lambda)\right\}.

There are several monomial bases known for
V(\lambda)
, such as the basis constructed by Gelfand-Tsetlin
[
GT50
]
and its generalization
[
Mol06
]
, Lusztig bases
[
Lus90
]
, string bases
[
Lit98
,
BZ92
]
but none of these bases is compatible with the PBW filtration.

An important property of the assigned polytopes is the following

Theorem 5.4

(
[
FFL11
,
FFL11a
]
)
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
, then
P(\lambda)
is a lattice polytope and moreover:

P(\lambda)+P(\mu)=P(\lambda+\mu)\text{ and }S(\lambda)+S(\mu)=S(\lambda+\mu),

where
+
denotes the Minkowski sum.

5.4.
Monomial bases for submodules

We turn to admissible sets
A\subset R^{-}
and the natural projection of the polytopes
P(\lambda)
. We define

\pi_{A}:\mathbb{R}^{|R^{+}|}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{|A|},\;e_{\alpha}\mapsto\begin{cases}e_{\alpha}&\text{ if }-\alpha\in A,\\
0&\text{ else, }\end{cases}

and

P_{A}(\lambda):=\pi_{A}(P(\lambda))\text{ which implies }S_{A}(\lambda)=\pi_{A}(S(\lambda)).

As we are considering faces of the polytope
P(\lambda)
, we have by Theorem
5.4
:

Corollary 5.5
.

For all
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
and every admissible subset
A
one has

P_{A}(\lambda)+P_{A}(\mu)=P_{A}(\lambda+\mu)\text{ and }S_{A}(\lambda)+S_{A}(\mu)=S_{A}(\lambda+\mu).

We list a few useful relations:

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
1\leq i\leq n
and let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
or
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
. Let
1<k\leq i\leq j<\ell\leq n
(resp.
\ell<n
for type
{\tt C})
, then one has the following relation

(f_{1,j}f_{k,\ell}+cf_{1,\ell}f_{k,j})\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}=0\in V(\omega_{i}).

In type
{\tt C}
, there are more relations that will be needed in the following:

(1)

for
1<k\leq\ell,j\leq n
, one has

(f_{1,j}f_{k,\bar{\ell}}+c_{1}f_{1,\bar{\ell}}f_{k,j}+c_{2}f_{1,\bar{k}}f_{\ell,j})\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}=0\in V(\omega_{i})

for some
c_{r}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, where
f_{\ell,j}=1
for
\ell=j+1
and
0
for
\ell>j+1
.

(2)

for
1<k\leq\ell<j\leq n
, one has

(f_{1,\bar{j}}f_{k,\bar{\ell}}+c_{1}f_{1,\bar{\ell}}f_{k,\bar{j}}+c_{2}f_{1,\bar{k}}f_{\ell,\bar{j}})\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}=0\in V(\omega_{i})

for some
c_{r}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
one has
f_{1,\ell}^{2}\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}=0
, resp. for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
one has
f_{1,\ell-1}\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}=0
and
f_{1,\bar{1}}^{2}\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}=0\in V(\omega_{i})
.
Certainly, for any monomial
\mathbf{m}\in U(\mathfrak{g})
:

\mathbf{m}.f_{1,\bar{1}}^{2}\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}=0.

•

For the first relation, one sets
\mathbf{m}=e_{j+1,\ell}e_{1,k-1}
.

•

For the second relation, one sets
\mathbf{m}=e_{1,k-1}e_{j+1,\overline{j+1}}e_{1,\ell-1}e_{1,j}
.

•

For the third relation, one sets
\mathbf{m}=e_{1,k-1}e_{1,j-1}e_{1,\ell-1}
.

The computation is straightforward and can be found in
[
FFL11
,
FFL11a
]
.
∎

We give an equivalent statement of the proposition. For
\alpha\succeq\beta\in R^{-}
one has

f_{\alpha}f_{\beta}\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}+\sum_{\mathbf{\gamma}}c_{\mathbf{\gamma}}f_{\gamma_{1}}f_{\gamma_{2}}\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}=0.

where the sum is over
\mathbf{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{P}(\alpha+\beta)
with
(\alpha,\beta)\leq(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
.

The main motivation for the paper is the following:

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be of type
A
or
C
,
\lambda\in P^{+}
,
A\subset R^{-}
admissible, then

\left\{\prod_{\alpha\in A}f_{\alpha}^{s_{\alpha}}\cdot v_{\lambda}\mid\mathbf{s}\in S_{A}(\lambda)\right\}

is a basis of
\text{gr }V_{A}(\lambda)
and hence (for any chosen ordering in the monomials) a basis for
V_{A}(\lambda)
.

A remark before the proof: a proof for the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-case has been provided in
[
Fou16
]
and then Proposition
3.5
allows to reduce the study for
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
to
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda=\sum m_{i}\omega_{i}\in P^{+}
. Since
S_{A}(\lambda)\subset S(\lambda)
,

\left\{\prod_{\alpha\in A}f_{\alpha}^{s_{\alpha}}\cdot v_{\lambda}\mid\mathbf{s}\in S_{A}(\lambda)\right\}

is linearly independent, due to Theorem
5.3
. It remains to prove, that this set spans
V_{A}(\lambda)
. Clearly,

\left\{\prod_{\alpha\in A}f_{\alpha}^{s_{\alpha}}\cdot v_{\lambda}\mid\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{|A|}\right\}

is a spanning set and we have to prove a straightening law to reduce to
S_{A}(\lambda)
. Let
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{|A|}
and suppose there is a Dyck path
\mathbf{p}\subset A
, such that consecutive roots are linked and

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbf{p}}s_{\alpha}>m_{s(\mathbf{p})}+\ldots+m_{e(\mathbf{p})}.

We will show that
f^{\mathbf{s}}\cdot v_{\lambda}
is in the span of

\{f^{\mathbf{s}}\cdot v_{\lambda}\mid\mathbf{s}\in S_{A}(\lambda)\}.

First, we consider
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
, then
\mathbf{p}=(\alpha_{i_{1},j_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{s},j_{s}})
with
i_{1}\leq\ldots\leq i_{s},j_{1}\leq\ldots\leq j_{s}
. We have seen in Proposition
3.7
, that the full subgrid
\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}
of roots
\alpha_{i_{k},j_{\ell}}
is contained in
A
.

In
[
FFL11
]
, a homogeneous order
<
on
\mathbb{Z}^{|R^{-}|}
is defined, we consider its restriction to
\mathbb{Z}^{|A_{\mathbf{p}}|}
. It is shown using operators from
\{e_{\beta}\mid\beta\in R^{+}\}
applied on
f_{\alpha_{i_{1},j_{s}}}^{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbf{p}}s_{\alpha}}\cdot v_{\lambda}(=0\in V(\lambda))
that

f^{\mathbf{s}}\cdot v_{\lambda}=\sum_{\mathbf{t}<\mathbf{s}}c_{\mathbf{t}}f^{\mathbf{t}}\cdot v_{\lambda}

for some
c_{\mathbf{t}}
. A further inspection of the proof in
[
FFL11
]
shows, that the needed operators
e_{\beta}
are exactly given by
\beta=\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}
with
\gamma_{i}\in\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}
. We conclude from Proposition
3.9
, that each
\mathbf{t}
on the right hand side is supported on
\overline{\mathbf{p}}^{\oplus}\subset A
. Concluding, for each violated Dyck path we have a straightening law with respect to
<
. Iterating this procedure is a finite process, showing that

\{f^{\mathbf{s}}\cdot v_{\lambda}\mid\mathbf{s}\in S_{A}(\lambda)\}

is a spanning set for
V_{A}(\lambda)
.

We turn to
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
. It turns out, that the same argument as for
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
is valid here, thanks to Proposition
3.5
. The homogeneous order for
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
is naturally extended to
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
in
[
FFL11a
]
.

Similarly as above, we just have to show that for each Dyck path
\mathbf{p}
, there is a set of differential operators in
U(\mathfrak{n}^{+})
that are acting on a given subset
A^{\tau}_{\mathbf{p}}
and to generate a straightening law.
[
FFL11a
]
provides the straightening law and we have to check, whether this is supported on
A
only. But this follows as for
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
from Proposition
3.8
and, with a close inspection of the proof in
[
FFL11a
]
, from Proposition
3.9
.
∎

With the following lemma, we justify the discussion of admissible sets. Reasonably, we restrict ourselves to
A\subset R^{-}
such that
\mathfrak{n}_{A}^{-}
is a Lie subalgebra.

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose
A\subset R^{-}
is not admissible, then there exists
i\in I
such that the lattice points in the face
P_{A}(\omega_{i})
do not parametrize a basis of
V_{A}(\omega_{i})
.

Proof.

Suppose
A
is not admissible, then there exists
\beta_{1}\succ\beta_{2}
such that
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}
but
\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\}\notin A
. Suppose
\text{supp }\beta_{1}\cap\text{supp }\beta_{2}=\emptyset
but
\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}\neq\emptyset
, then
\beta_{1}\oplus\beta_{2}=\{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}\}
, but this is in
A
since
\mathfrak{n}_{A}^{-}
is a Lie subalgebra. In both cases, there exists
i
with

f_{\beta_{1}}f_{\beta_{2}}\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}\neq 0\text{ and }f_{\gamma_{1}}f_{\gamma_{2}}\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}\neq 0.

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
are non-comparable with respect to
\succ
, and hence, due to the definition of Dyck paths,
e_{\gamma_{1}}+e_{\gamma_{2}}\in S(\omega_{i})\setminus S_{A}(\omega_{i})
. Proposition
5.6
on the other hand implies, that
f_{\beta_{1}}f_{\beta_{2}}\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}
is a nontrivial linear combination involving
f_{\gamma_{1}}f_{\gamma_{2}}\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}
. Since
S(\omega_{i})
parametrizes a linearly independent subset

f_{\beta_{1}}f_{\beta_{2}}\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}\notin\text{ span }\left\{\prod_{\alpha\in A}f_{\alpha}^{s_{\alpha}}\cdot v_{\omega_{i}}\mid\mathbf{s}\in S_{A}(\omega_{i})\right\}.

∎

5.5.
Geometric interpretation

In this section, we are giving a geometric interpretation of the results on monomial bases. The main motivation is due to the admissible sets of the form
A_{w}
for some
w\in W
.
In this context, the module
V_{A}(\lambda)
is isomorphic to the Demazure module
V_{w}(\lambda)
and one obtains an action of
\mathfrak{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
on
\text{gr }V_{w}(\lambda)
(resp.
w^{-1}(\mathfrak{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h})
on
\text{gr }V_{A}(\lambda)
.

5.5.1.
Favourable modules and flat degenerations

Briefly explained is a favourable module
M
, for the general setup on favourable modules we refer to
[
FFL17a
]
. Let
M
be a cyclic finite-dimensional complex vector space acted upon by a complex algebraic unipotent group
\mathbb{U}
satisfying certain conditions.
First, this module is cyclic for
U(\mathfrak{n})
, the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{n}
, the nilpotent Lie algebra corresponding to
\mathbb{U}
.
For a fixed basis of
\mathfrak{n}
and a fixed homogeneous ordering
\leq
on monomials in this basis, we obtain a filtration of
M
and the associated graded space has one-dimensional leafs only. The monomials of the basis of
\text{gr }M
are called essential
[
FFL17
]
and we denote
\text{es }M\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}_{\geq 0}
the set of the exponents of these monomials.
M
is called favourable if

•

there exists a convex polytope
P(M)\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}
such that its set of lattice points,
S(M)
, coincides with the set of essential multi-exponents
es(M)
.

•

for all
n\geq 1
, consider
U(\mathfrak{n})\cdot(m^{\otimes n})\subset M\otimes\cdots\otimes M
and demand
\dim U(\mathfrak{n})\cdot(m^{\otimes n})=|nS(M)|
.

In the following, we show that the submodule
V_{A}(\lambda)\subset V(\lambda)
is favourable. For a fixed admissible subset
A
, let
{\operatorname*{G}}_{A}
denote the corresponding connected, simply connected Lie group of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{A}
. Notice that
{\operatorname*{G}}_{A}
is a unipotent subgroup of
{\operatorname*{G}}
, the connected, simply connected Lie group of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. We have:

Lemma 5.9
.

For
A
an admissible set,
V_{A}(\lambda)
is a favourable
{\operatorname*{G}}_{A}
-module for
\lambda\in P^{+}
regular.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
Fou16
, Lemma 5]
. It suffices to consider the case
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
. Let the roots in
R^{+}
be ordered as follows:

\displaystyle\alpha_{1,\overline{1}}\succ\alpha_{1,\overline{2}}\succ\alpha_{2,\overline{2}}\succ\cdots\succ\alpha_{1,\overline{n}}\succ\cdots\succ\alpha_{n,\overline{n}}\succ\alpha_{1,n-1}\succ\cdots\succ\alpha_{n-1,n-1}\succ\cdots\succ\alpha_{1,1}.

We consider the restriction of this order on
A
. By
[
FFL17a
, Theorem 11.8]
, choosing the above order and taking the induced homogeneous reverse lexicographic order on monomials in
S(\mathfrak{n}^{-})
, implies that the
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
-module
V(\lambda)
is favourable. In particular, we have
S(\lambda)=es(V(\lambda))
. Let
\textsf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
, with
s_{\alpha}=0
for all
\alpha\notin A
. Suppose
s
is essential for
V(\lambda)
. By definition, and through the trivial embedding
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\#A}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
, it follows that
s
is essential for
V_{A}(\lambda)
. It therefore follows that
S_{A}(\lambda)\subset es(V_{A}(\lambda))
. The opposite inclusion
es(V_{A}(\lambda))\subset S_{A}(\lambda)
holds by reason of dimension, which gives the equality.

Furthermore, we have an injective homomorphism of modules
V_{A}(\lambda+\mu)\hookrightarrow V_{A}(\lambda)\otimes V_{A}(\mu)
, obtained by restricting the embedding
V(\lambda+\mu)\hookrightarrow V(\lambda)\otimes V(\mu)
. From this, and by Corollary
5.5
, we have that for all
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
:

\dim U(\mathfrak{n}_{A}^{-})\cdot v_{\lambda}\otimes v_{\mu}=|S_{A}(\lambda+\mu)|=|S_{A}(\lambda)+S_{A}(\mu)|.

The proof is completed by considering the case
\lambda=\mu
, and extending by induction to the general case.
∎

We denote the projective varieties

X_{A}(\lambda):=\overline{{\operatorname*{G}}_{A}\cdot[v_{\lambda}]}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(V(\lambda)).

and

X_{A}^{a}(\lambda):=\overline{{\operatorname*{G}}^{a}_{A}\cdot[v_{\lambda}]}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}\left(\text{gr }V_{A}(\lambda)\right).

where
{\operatorname*{G}}^{a}_{A}
denotes
|A|
copies of the additive group
\mathbb{G}_{a}
, which is a Lie group associated with the Lie algebra of the vector space
\mathfrak{n}_{A}^{-}
with a trivial bracket. Note that the latter and hence
{\operatorname*{G}}^{a}_{A}
act on
\text{gr }M
.

Definition 5.10
.

For
A
admissible and
\lambda\in P^{+}
regular, we call
X_{A}^{a}(\lambda)
the PBW degenerate variety corresponding to
X_{A}(\lambda)
.

While the definition is reasonable for all types, the following proposition depends on the existence of a suitable basis.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be of type
{\tt A,C}
. Let
\lambda\in P^{+}
be regular, and
A
be an admissible set, then the variety
X_{A}(\lambda)
degenerates flatly into the PBW degenerate variety
X_{A}^{a}(\lambda)
and further into a toric variety.

Proof.

It follows from
[
FFL17a
, Theorem 8.1]
since the module
V_{A}(\lambda)
is favourable (according to Lemma
5.9
).
∎

In view Proposition

5.5.2.
PBW-semistandard tableaux bases for coordinate rings

Feigin introduced in

[

to type

. Within our setup, it is natural to describe the subset of those tableaux parametrizing a basis of the homogeneous coordinate ring of the PBW degenerate Schubert variety

For a Young diagram
Y_{\lambda}
corresponding to a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 0)

denote the length of the

A

{\tt A}

is a filling of the Young diagram

of shape
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 0)

T_{r,c}\in\{1,\ldots,n+1\}

•

if

, then
T_{r,c}=r
,

•

if
r_{1}<r_{2}
and
T_{r_{1},c}\neq r_{1}

,

•

for any
c>1
and any
r
, there exists
r^{\prime}\geq r
such that
T_{r^{\prime},c-1}\geq T_{r,c}
.

A
type
{\tt C}
PBW-semistandard tableau
is a filling of the Young diagram
Y_{\lambda}
of shape
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 0)
, with numbers
T_{r,c}\in\{1,\ldots,n,\overline{n},\ldots,\overline{1}\}
satisfying all the three properties above and the following extra property:

•

if
T_{r,c}=r
, and
\exists\,\,r^{\prime}
such that
T_{r^{\prime},c}=\overline{r}
, then
r^{\prime}<r
.

Proposition 5.14

(
[
Fei12
,
Bal22
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be of type
{\tt A,C}
. For
\lambda=\sum_{k=1}^{n}m_{k}\omega_{k}
a dominant integral weight, the set
\{\prod_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}^{s_{\alpha}}\cdot v_{\lambda}\mid\mathbf{s}\in S(\lambda)\}
is in a weight preserving one-to-one correspondence with the set of PBW-semistandard tableaux of shape
\lambda
.

The idea for the proof of the above proposition is the following. If a root vector
f_{\alpha_{i,j}}
appears in
\prod_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}^{s_{\alpha}}\cdot v_{\lambda}
, with
\mathbf{s}\in S(\lambda)
, then there is a box in the
i
-th row of the corresponding tableau containing the entry
j+1
. Additionally for type
C
, if there is a root vector of the form
f_{\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}}
in
\prod_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}^{s_{\alpha}}\cdot v_{\lambda}
, with
\mathbf{s}\in S(\lambda)
, there should be an entry
\overline{j}
in some box in the
i
-th row. Details on how these boxes are arranged to form the PBW-semistandard tableaux can be found in
[
Bal22
]
.

. We define

.

-permissible type

PBW-semistandard tableau
of shape

, that satisfies the following extra condition:

for all

.

An
A
-permissible type
{\tt C}
PBW-semistandard tableau
of shape
\lambda
is a type
{\tt C}
PBW-semistandard tableau of shape
\lambda

(b)

for all

for all

r=i
.

The following is an analogue of Proposition
5.14
for admissible sets.

Proposition 5.17
.

For an admissible set
A
, and for
\lambda=\sum_{k=1}^{n}m_{k}\omega_{k}
a dominant integral weight, the set
\{\prod_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}^{s_{\alpha}}\cdot v_{\lambda}\mid\mathbf{s}\in S_{A}(\lambda)\}
is in a weight preserving one-to-one correspondence with the set of
A
-permissible PBW-semistandard tableaux of shape
\lambda
.

Proof.

We claim that the bijection of Proposition
5.14
still holds when restricted to the case of an admissible set
A
. Indeed, suppose
\alpha_{i,j}\notin A
with
j\geq\mu_{c}
and
r=i
. Then clearly
T_{r,c}\neq j+1
in the corresponding tableau. And likewise for any
\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}\notin A
, it follows that
T_{r,c}\neq\overline{j}
for all columns
c
and rows
r
such that
r=i
. This together with Theorem
5.7
imply the result.
∎

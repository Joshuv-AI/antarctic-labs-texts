Satake equivalence for Hodge modules on affine Grassmannians
Roman Fedorov

Abstract
. For a reductive group G G we equip the category of G 𝒪 G_{\mathcal{O}} -equivariant polarizable pure Hodge modules on the affine Grassmannian Gr G \Gr_{G} with a structure of neutral Tannakian category. We show that it is equivalent to a twisted tensor product of the category of representations of the Langlands dual group and the category of pure polarizable Hodge structures.



1.
Introduction and Main Results

We fix a complex reductive group
G
. Consider the affine Grassmannian
\Gr_{G}
defined as the étale sheafification of the functor sending a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
R
to the quotient
G\bigl(R((t))\bigr)/G(R[[t]])
, where
R((t)):=R[[t]](t^{-1})
. Let
G_{\mathcal{O}}
be the algebraic group of infinite type representing the functor
R\mapsto G(R[[t]])
, that is, the group of jets. Clearly,
G_{\mathcal{O}}
acts on
\Gr_{G}
.

It is well-known that the affine Grassmannian is a strict ind-projective ind-scheme, that is, we can write

(1)

\Gr_{G}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\Gr_{G}^{(l)},

where
(\Gr_{G}^{(l)})
is an inductive systems of projective schemes with morphisms being closed embeddings. We may assume that the schemes
\Gr_{G}^{(l)}
are
G_{\mathcal{O}}
-equivariant.

1.1.
Recollection on the Geometric Satake Equivalence

The first work in the direction of geometrizing the Satake isomorphism was Lusztig’s paper
[
Lus
]
. Independently, Drinfeld understood that geometrizing the Satake isomorphism is crucial for the geometric Langlands correspondence. Following Drinfeld’s suggestion, Ginzburg in
[
Gin
]
proved the characteristic zero case of the Geometric Satake equivalence. We follow
[
MV
]
, where Mirkovic and Vilonen generalized the above results.

In more details, Mirkovic and Vilonen equip the category
\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
with a convolution product and a commutativity constraint for this product, making it into a symmetric monoidal category. They also construct a fiber functor making
\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
into a neutral Tannakian category. Thus, according to Tannakian formalism (see for example
[
DM
, Thm. 2.11]
), there is an affine group
\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
over
\mathbb{Q}
such that we have an equivalence of categories

\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})=\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}(\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}).

Here
\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}
stands for the category of finite dimensional representations on
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces. The main result of
[
MV
]
shows that
\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is split reductive and is Langlands dual to
G
. The main goal of our paper is to “upgrade” this theorem from perverse sheaves to pure polarizable Hodge modules.

Recently the geometric Satake equivalence was upgraded to the motivic setup in
[
RS
]
. It seems plausible that some of our results can be obtained from Hodge realization of the results in loc. cit. It is, however, important to have a direct construction. Our result is also, in a sense, simpler, as it does not use the theory of motives.

1.2.
Geometric Satake Equivalence for pure polarizable Hodge modules

For a complex variety
Y
(=reduced separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
) one has the category
\MH(Y,n)=\MH(Y,\mathbb{Q},n)
of pure polarizable rational Hodge modules of weight
n
(cf.
[
Sai1
,
Sai2
]
). In Section
2
we extend the notion of Hodge modules to ind-schemes and to the equivariant setting so that we have the category
\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G},n)
of
G_{\mathcal{O}}
-equivariant pure polarizable Hodge modules of weight
n
on
\Gr_{G}
. This is a semisimple abelian
\mathbb{Q}
-linear category. Consider the graded category

\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G}):=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G},n).

We equip this category with a structure of neutral Tannakian category in Section
3
.

Let
\MH(pt,n)
stand for the category of polarizable Hodge structures of weight
n
(which is the same as the polarizable Hodge modules of weight
n
on a point, thus the notation; see Section
2.1.3
) and set

\MH(pt):=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\MH(pt,n).

Recall that we have the Tate twist, which is a degree
-2
autoequivalence of
\MH(pt)
.

Our main goal in this paper is to identify the category
\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
as a “twisted” tensor product. We will need two constructions.

1.2.1.
Adding the square root of the Tate twist

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category with an auto-equivalence
T
. We define the new category
\mathcal{C}[\sqrt{T}]:=\mathcal{C}^{+}\times\mathcal{C}^{-}
to be the product of two copies of
\mathcal{C}
. This decomposition is viewed as a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
grading. If
\mathcal{F}
is an object of
\mathcal{C}
, we write
\mathcal{F}^{\pm}
for
\mathcal{F}
viewed as an object of
\mathcal{C}^{\pm}\subset\mathcal{C}[\sqrt{T}]
.

We define an autoequivalence
\sqrt{T}
of
\mathcal{C}[\sqrt{T}]
by

\sqrt{T}(\mathcal{F}^{+})=\mathcal{F}^{-},\quad\sqrt{T}(\mathcal{F}^{-})=T(\mathcal{F})^{+}.

Note that
T=(\sqrt{T})^{2}
. We will also write
\mathcal{F}(n/2)
for
\sqrt{T}^{n}(\mathcal{F})
.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{C}^{i}
is a graded additive category and that
T
has degree
-2
. Then the category
\mathcal{C}[\sqrt{T}]
also has a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading defined by
\mathcal{C}[\sqrt{T}]^{i}:=(\mathcal{C}^{+})^{i}\times(\mathcal{C}^{-})^{i+1}
.
Note that
\sqrt{T}
has degree
-1
.

Assume now that
\mathcal{C}
is a monoidal category and
T
is compatible with the monoidal structure in the sense that we have natural isomorphisms

T(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{G})=T(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{F}\otimes T(\mathcal{G})

for any objects
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
. Then we define a monoidal structure on
\mathcal{C}[\sqrt{T}]
by

\mathcal{F}^{+}\otimes\mathcal{G}^{+}:=(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{G})^{+},\quad\mathcal{F}^{+}\otimes\mathcal{G}^{-}:=(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{G})^{-},\quad\mathcal{F}^{-}\otimes\mathcal{G}^{+}:=(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{G})^{-},\quad\mathcal{F}^{-}\otimes\mathcal{G}^{-}:=(T(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{G}))^{+}

The product is compatible with
\sqrt{T}
. If
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded, then this product is compatible with gradings.

1.2.2.
Tensor product of semisimple categories

Recall that for abelian semisimple
\mathbb{Q}
-linear categories
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
one can define their tensor product
\mathcal{C}_{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}_{2}
(see, e.g.
[
BK
, Def. 1.1.15]
). The objects of this category are formal expressions
\oplus_{i=1}^{n}V_{1,i}\boxtimes V_{2,i}
, where
V_{j,i}
are objects of
\mathcal{C}_{j}
; the morphisms are given by

\Hom(\oplus_{i=1}^{n}V_{1,i}\boxtimes V_{2,i},\oplus_{j=1}^{m}V^{\prime}_{1,j}\boxtimes V^{\prime}_{2,j}):=\oplus_{i,j}\Hom(V_{1,i},V^{\prime}_{1,j})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\Hom(V_{2,i},V^{\prime}_{2,j}).

The tensor product of neutral Tannakian categories is again a neutral Tannakian category.

1.2.3.

Recall that
\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is the split reductive group over
\mathbb{Q}
Langlands dual to
G
. Fix a Borel subgroup
\check{B}_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and a split maximal torus
\check{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\check{B}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. The sum of all positive coroots of
\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
with respect to
\check{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\check{B}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a cocharacter
2\rho\colon\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}}\hookrightarrow\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

The simple objects of
\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
correspond to the dominant weights of
\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. If
\lambda
is such a weight, we call the corresponding representation even or odd depending on the parity of
2\rho(\lambda)
. Thus, we equip the category
\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
with a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
grading. Now we can formulate our first main result.

Theorem 1
.

The category
\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
has a natural structure of a neutral Tannakian category. There is a canonical equivalence of Tannakian categories compatible with the gradings and the fiber functors

\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})=\left(\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\boxtimes\MH(pt)[\sqrt{T}]\right)^{+},

where
\MH(pt)[\sqrt{T}]
is an instance of the construction defined in Section
1.2.1
,
T
is the Tate twist; the superscript
+
stands for the even component of the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded category.

We note that if
-1
is in the kernel of
2\rho\colon\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}}\hookrightarrow\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, then the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grading on
\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is trivial, and the previous theorem becomes

\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})=\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\boxtimes\MH(pt).

1.3.
Geometric Satake Equivalence for Tate’s Hodge modules

Let
\Tate^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
be the strictly full subcategory of
\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
whose objects are isomorphic to direct sums of Tate twists of IC Hodge modules on the
G_{\mathcal{O}}
-orbits in
\Gr_{G}
. This is a semisimple monoidal subcategory of
\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
, see Proposition
4.3
below. We can give a more direct Tannakian description of this category.

We can form a twisted product

\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\times^{\mu_{2}}\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}}:=(\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\times\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}})/((2\rho\times\iota)(\mu_{2})),

where
\iota\colon\mu_{2}\to\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}}
is the standard embedding. This is Deligne’s modified Langlands dual group.

Theorem 2
.

There is a canonical equivalence of Tannakian categories compatible with the gradings and the fiber functors

\Tate^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})\simeq\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}(\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\times^{\mu_{2}}\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}}).

This should be compared with
[
RS
, Thm. C]
.

1.4.
Geometric Satake equivalence for mixed Hodge modules

Similarly to the above, one can define the category
\MHM^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
of
G_{\mathcal{O}}
-equivariant mixed Hodge modules on
\Gr_{G}
. In Section
5
we sketch the proof of the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

The category
\MHM^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
has a natural structure of a neutral Tannakian category. There is a canonical equivalence of Tannakian categories compatible with the fiber functors

\MHM^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})\simeq\left(\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\boxtimes\MHM(pt)[\sqrt{T}]\right)^{+},

where, as before,
T
is the Tate twist and the superscript
+
stands for the even component of the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded category.

We note that the category
\MHM(pt)
is not semisimple. However, the tensor product of two
\mathbb{Q}
-linear categories also makes sense if only one of the categories is semisimple. Thus, the right hand side of the above equivalence of categories makes sense.

1.5.
Future directions

Consider the cotangent stack

\St_{G}:=T^{*}(G_{\mathcal{O}}\backslash\Gr_{G}),

which we call the
Steinberg Stack
. Here the stack
G_{\mathcal{O}}\backslash\Gr_{G}
is the quotient of
\Gr_{G}
by the action of
G_{\mathcal{O}}
. Note that
\St_{G}
is
not
an ind-algebraic stack, since some points have infinite-dimensional automorphism groups. However, one can show that it is a quotient of an ind-scheme by
G_{\mathcal{O}}
. In a subsequent work we will construct
a functor

\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}(\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\times^{\mu_{2}}\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}})\to\Coh(\St_{G}),

where
\Coh(\bullet)
is the abelian category of coherent sheaves. We will equip
\Coh(\St_{G})
with a monoidal structure and show that the functor is uniquely defined by certain axioms, provided that the quotient of
G
by its radical is of adjoint type. We call this functor the
semi-classical Satake functor
. We expect that this functor will play a crucial role in the local Langlands duality for Hitchin systems. Let us sketch the construction of this functor.

Assume first that
Y
is a separated scheme smooth over
\mathbb{C}
. Then
\MH(Y)
is a strictly full subcategory of the category of
\mathcal{D}
-modules on
Y
with good filtrations (see Section
2.1.2
below). Since the associated graded of a filtered
\mathcal{D}
-module is a coherent sheaf on the cotangent bundle, we get a functor
\gr\colon\MH(Y)\to\Coh(T^{*}Y)
. This can be extended to ind-schemes and to the equivariant setting so that we get a functor

\gr\colon\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})\to\Coh(\St_{G}).

The semi-classical Satake functor is defined as the composition

\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}(\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\times^{\mu_{2}}\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Tate^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})\hookrightarrow\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})\xrightarrow{\gr}\Coh(\St_{G}).

The biggest difficulty in defining this functor is that
\Gr_{G}
is not a limit of smooth projective schemes.

1.6.
Notation and conventions

We will be working with left
\mathcal{D}
-modules. We will freely work with perverse sheaves on ind-schemes. The category of perverse sheaves of vector spaces over
\mathbb{Q}
on
Y
, where
\mathbb{Q}
is a field, is denoted by
\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)
.

We call an affine group scheme over
\mathbb{Q}
a group for brevity.

We write
\simeq
for an isomorphism and simply
=
for a canonical isomorphism.

1.7.
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
we recall all the statements we need about Hodge modules. We extend the definition of Hodge modules to the case of ind-schemes and to the equivariant setup.

In Section
3
we equip the category
\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
with a structure of neutral Tannakian category. In Section
4
we prove Theorems
1
and
2
. Finally, in Section
5
we sketch a proof of Theorem
3
.

2.
Hodge modules on ind-schemes

The categories of Hodge modules on varieties have been defined and studied by M. Saito in
[
Sai1
,
Sai2
]
. In this section we extend this to the case of ind-separated ind-schemes with ‘nice’ actions of affine groups. We formulate all the statements we will need later and prove most of them (leaving a few simpler statements to the reader). It seems that some of the results about pure polarizable Hodge modules (especially the results related to the inverse images) can only be proved with the help of mixed Hodge modules, so we will use mixed Hodge modules in the proofs as well.

2.1.
Hodge modules on schemes

We briefly recall M. Saito’s theory of Hodge modules.

2.1.1.
Filtered
\mathcal{D}
-modules with rational structures on smooth schemes

Let
Y
be a separated scheme smooth over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
denote the sheaf of differential operators on
Y
with the usual filtration
\mathcal{D}^{\leq p}_{Y}
by the degree. Let
M
be a
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-module. Recall that an increasing filtration
F_{\bullet}
of
M
by
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-coherent submodules is
good
if it is separated and exhaustive, it satisfies
\mathcal{D}^{\leq p}_{Y}\cdot F_{q}M\subset F_{p+q}M
, and there is
q_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that for all
p\geq 0
and
q\geq q_{0}
we have
\mathcal{D}^{\leq p}_{Y}\cdot F_{q}M=F_{p+q}M
.

Let
\FM_{rs}(\mathcal{D}_{Y})
be the category of quadruples
(M,F_{\bullet},\mathcal{M},\alpha)
, where
M
is a holonomic
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-module with regular singularities,
F_{\bullet}
is a good filtration on
M
,
\mathcal{M}\in\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)
, and
\alpha
is an isomorphism
\alpha\colon\DR(M)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
(
\DR
is the de Rham functor).

Since
\DR
is an equivalence of categories, we see that
\FM_{rs}(\mathcal{D}_{Y})
can be defined equivalently as the category of pairs
(\mathcal{M},F_{\bullet})
, where
\mathcal{M}\in\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)
,
F_{\bullet}
is good filtration of
\DR^{-1}(\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
). It is clear that
\FM_{rs}(\mathcal{D}_{Y})
is an additive
\mathbb{Q}
-linear category. We define the functor
\mathrm{Rat}\colon\FM_{rs}(\mathcal{D}_{Y})\to\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)
by
\mathrm{Rat}(M,F_{\bullet},\mathcal{M},\alpha)=\mathcal{M}
. We see that
\mathrm{Rat}
is faithful.

If
j\colon U\to Y
is an open embedding, we have an obvious restriction functor

(2)

j^{*}\colon\FM_{rs}(\mathcal{D}_{Y})\to\FM_{rs}(\mathcal{D}_{U}).

On the other hand, let
i\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
be a closed embedding of smooth varieties with connected
Y^{\prime}
. Following
[
Sai1
, Intro.]
, we define the direct image functor

(3)

i_{*}\colon\FM_{rs}(\mathcal{D}_{Y^{\prime}})\to\FM_{rs}(\mathcal{D}_{Y})

by

i_{*}(M,F_{\bullet},\mathcal{M},\alpha):=(i_{*}M,{{}^{\prime}\!F}^{\bullet},i_{*}\mathcal{M},i_{*}(\alpha)).

We are using the fact that
\DR
commutes with
i_{*}
. The filtration
{}^{\prime}\!F_{\bullet}
is defined as follows. Recall that
i_{*}M=i_{ab,*}(\mathcal{D}_{Y\leftarrow Y^{\prime}}\otimes_{\mathcal{D}_{Y^{\prime}}}M)
, where
i_{ab,*}
is the direct image for sheaves of abelian groups. Then

{{}^{\prime}\!F}^{p}i_{*}M:=i_{ab,*}\left(\sum_{q+\nu\leq p-n}F_{\nu}\mathcal{D}_{Y\leftarrow Y^{\prime}}\otimes F_{q}M\right),

where
n
is the codimension of
Y^{\prime}
in
Y
. It is clear that the restriction functor for the composition of open embeddings is the composition of restriction functors, and the direct image functor for the composition of closed embeddings is the composition of direct image functors. It is also clear that
i_{*}
commutes with restriction to open subsets (this is a particular case of the base change). We define the support of a filtered
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-module with a rational structure by

\supp(M,F_{\bullet},\mathcal{M},\alpha):=\supp(M)=\supp(\mathcal{M}).

Thus
\supp(M,F_{\bullet},\mathcal{M},\alpha)
is a reduced closed subscheme of
Y
.

2.1.2.
Polarizable Hodge modules on separated schemes of finite type

In
[
Sai1
]
for every smooth over
\mathbb{C}
separated scheme
Y
Saito defines the category
\MH(Y,\mathbb{Q},n)^{p}
of polarizable Hodge module of weight
n
. This is a strictly full subcategory of
\FM_{rs}(\mathcal{D}_{Y})
. More precisely, Saito works with an analogue of
\FM_{rs}(\mathcal{D}_{Y})
, where the
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-modules are only assumed to be holonomic. However, in retrospect all
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-modules underlying polarizable Hodge modules have regular singularities
[
Sai1
, p. 858 and Rem. 5.1.18]
.

Convention
. We will only work with rational polarizable Hodge modules, so we denote
\MH(Y,\mathbb{Q},n)^{p}
by
\MH(Y,n)
and sometimes skip the adjective ‘polarizable’.

Let now
Y
be a not necessarily smooth reduced separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. In
[
Sai1
, Sect. 5.3.12]
(see also
[
Sai2
, Sect. 2.1]
) Saito extends the definition of
\MH(Y,n)
to this case as follows. Let
Y=\bigcup_{i}U_{i}
be a Zariski cover and
U_{i}\to V_{i}
be closed embeddings into smooth schemes. Then a Hodge module on
Y
is a collection
\mathcal{F}_{i}
of Hodge modules on
V_{i}
such that
\supp(\mathcal{F}_{i})\subset U_{i}
together with isomorphisms on intersections satisfying the cocycle condition. The category
\MH(Y,n)
does not depend on the cover and the embeddings because Hodge modules on smooth separated schemes satisfy the Zariski descent and Kashiwara’s equivalence.

Remark
.

Polarizable Hodge modules satisfy Kashiwara’s equivalence according to
[
Sai1
, Lemme 5.1.9, Lemme 5.2.11]
. It follows implicitly from
[
Sai1
, Sect. 5.3.12]
that they satisfy Zariski descent. The reason is, however, not quite clear. In fact, Zariski descent follows from definitions for Hodge modules, but it is not obvious that a locally polarizable module is polarizable. This, however, follows from
[
Sai2
, Thm. 3.21]
after reducing to the case of a module with strict support (cf. also Proposition
2.1
below).

To summarize, for a reduced separated
\mathbb{C}
-scheme of finite type, we have the category of Hodge modules; the Hodge modules satisfy the Zariski descent and Kashiwara’s equivalence. In particular, the restriction functors (
2
) to open subsets of smooth schemes preserve the subcategories of Hodge modules and the definition of these functors easily extends to the case of singular schemes. The same applies to the direct image functors (
3
).

We can easily extend the definition of Hodge modules to non-reduced schemes by setting
\MH(Y,n):=\MH(Y_{red},n)
. Kashiwara’s equivalence and the Zariski locality extend to this case. One also extends the definition of the functor
\mathrm{Rat}\colon\MH(Y,n)\to\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)
to not necessarily reduced schemes. Note that the support of a Hodge module is always reduced.

Remark
.

We can also extend the definition of Hodge modules to not necessarily separated schemes locally of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
using Zariski locality. However, we will not need it. In fact, all our schemes will be quasi-projective. Thus, we leave the generalization to the reader.

2.1.3.
Hodge modules with strict support and polarizable variations of Hodge structures

Let
Y
be any separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
Z\subset Y
be an integral closed subscheme. Recall from
[
Sai2
, Sect. 2.1]
that
\mathcal{F}\in\MH(Y,n)

has strict support

Z
if
\supp\mathcal{F}=Z
and
\mathrm{Rat}(\mathcal{F})
is an IC-sheaf (that is,
\mathrm{Rat}(\mathcal{F})
has neither quotient objects nor subobjects supported on a proper subscheme of
Z
, cf. also
[
Sai1
, p. 3]
). Denote the full subcategory of Hodge modules of weight
n
with strict support
Z
by
\MH_{Z}(Y,n)
. If
Z
is an integral separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
we set
\MH_{Z}(n):=\MH_{Z}(Z,n)
.

Let
U
be an integral smooth scheme. Recall that a rational variation of Hodge structures (VHS for short) of weight
n
on
U
is a pair
(\mathcal{S},F_{\bullet})
, where
\mathcal{S}
is a locally constant sheaf of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces,
F_{\bullet}
is a filtration of
\mathcal{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathcal{O}_{U}
satisfying Griffiths transversality condition and such that for all
u\in U
the pair
(\mathcal{S}_{u},(F_{\bullet})_{u})
is a Hodge structure of weight
n
. We note that in this case
\mathcal{S}[\dim U]
is a perverse sheaf and
\DR^{-1}(\mathcal{S}[\dim U]\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C})=(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathcal{O}_{U},\nabla)
, where
\nabla
is the connection whose sheaf of flat sections is
\mathcal{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
. Then
F_{\bullet}
is a good filtration of
(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathcal{O}_{U},\nabla)
and we see that a VHS is a particular case of a filtered
\mathcal{D}
-module with rational structure.

Denote by
\VHS(U,n)
the category of polarizable variations of Hodge structure of weight
n
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
with quasi-unipotent local monodromies. If
W\subset U
is an open subscheme, then we have a restriction functor

\VHS(U,n)\to\VHS(W,n).

For an integral separated scheme
Z
of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
, let
\VHS_{gen}(Z,n)
denote the direct limit of categories
\VHS(U,n)
, where
U
ranges over smooth open non-empty subschemes of
Z
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
Z
be an integral separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. Then there is a canonical equivalence of categories

2.2.
Hodge modules on ind-schemes

All the ind-schemes we consider are strict ind-separated ind-schemes of ind-finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. That is, we work with étale sheaves

representable as

, where

are separated schemes of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
, and for

the morphism

is a closed embedding. Recall that any morphism

\mathbb{C}

We define

,

. By Kashiwara’s equivalence, this category does not depend on the presentation of

as a limit of schemes. Explicitly, a Hodge module on

is a pair

, where
\mathcal{F}
is a Hodge module on

We note that the definitions of the functor of restriction to an open subscheme and of the direct image under a closed embedding extend easily to the case of ind-schemes. One also extends the definition of the functor
\mathrm{Rat}
to the case of ind-schemes.

If

is a scheme viewed as an ind-scheme, then

defined above is the same as

discussed in Section

We easily extend the notion of support to Hodge modules on ind-schemes. We emphasize that the support of a Hodge module is a reduced subscheme of
Y
(not an ind-subscheme). The notion of a Hodge module with strict support also extends to the case of ind-schemes. Kashiwara’s equivalence gives a canonical identification
\MH_{Z}(Y,n)=\MH_{Z}(n)
, whenever
Z
is an integral subscheme of
Y
. We have the following decomposition

(4)

\MH(Y,n)=\bigoplus_{Z\subset Y}\MH_{Z}(Y,n)\simeq\bigoplus_{Z\subset Y}\MH_{Z}(n),

where the direct sum is over integral closed subschemes of
Y
. Indeed, if
Y
is a smooth scheme, then this follows from
[
Sai1
, p. 852–853]
. If
Y
can be embedded into a smooth scheme, this follows from Kashiwara’s equivalence. Thus, if
Y
is any reduced scheme, then the decomposition exists locally. But the decomposition of a Hodge module into the direct sum of modules with strict support is unique because there are no morphisms between Hodge modules with different strict supports. Thus the global decomposition follows from the local one. Finally, the case of ind-schemes is reduced to the case of reduced schemes using again Kashiwara’s equivalence.

(

that there is a unique isomorphism

2.5.

The direct image of complexes of mixed Hodge modules on schemes is defined in

Proposition 2.14
.

Hodge modules satisfy descent for smooth schematic surjective morphisms.

Proof.

Let

Lemma 3.8

3.5.
Rigidity

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an object of
\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
. Recall from
[
BK
, Def. 2.1.1]
that an object
\mathcal{F}^{\vee}
is the left dual of
\mathcal{F}
if there are morphisms
e_{\mathcal{F}}\colon\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\star\mathcal{F}\to\delta_{1}
(called the counit) and
i_{\mathcal{F}}\colon\delta_{1}\to\mathcal{F}\star\mathcal{F}^{\vee}
(called the unit) such that the composition

\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{i_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\Id_{\mathcal{F}}}\mathcal{F}\star\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\star\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{\Id_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes e_{\mathcal{F}}}\mathcal{F}

is equal to
\Id_{\mathcal{F}}
and the composition

\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\Id_{\mathcal{F}^{\vee}}\otimes i_{\mathcal{F}}}\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\star\mathcal{F}\star\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\Id_{\mathcal{F}^{\vee}}\otimes e_{\mathcal{F}}}\mathcal{F}^{\vee}

is equal to
\Id_{\mathcal{F}^{\vee}}
.
Right dual objects are defined similarly. Our goal is to show that
\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
is a rigid monoidal category, that is, every object has a left and a right dual.

Our first observation is that a monoidal functor obviously preserves duals. The functor
\MH(pt)\to\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
sending
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathrm{IC}_{0}(\mathcal{H})
is easily seen to be monoidal, and the category of pure polarizable Hodge structures is rigid. Thus every object of the form
\mathrm{IC}_{0}(\mathcal{H})
has duals.

Secondly, for
\lambda\in X_{+}
set
\lambda^{\prime}:=w_{0}(-\lambda)\in X_{+}
, where
w_{0}
is the longest element of the Weyl group of
G
. Set
\mathcal{F}:=\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:=\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
. We claim that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(i)
, where
i=\dim\Gr^{\lambda}
, is the dual of
\mathcal{F}
.

Indeed, first of all, we have
G_{\mathcal{O}}\cdot(t^{-\lambda}\cdot G_{\mathcal{O}})=\Gr^{\lambda^{\prime}}
. Now it follows from
[
MV
, (11.10)]
that we have
\mathrm{Rat}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(i))\simeq(\mathrm{Rat}(\mathcal{F}))^{\vee}
(note that for perverse sheaves we can trivialize the Tate twists). Next, we claim that the injective map

(12)

\Hom(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(i)\star\mathcal{F},\delta_{1})\hookrightarrow\Hom(\mathrm{Rat}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(i)\star\mathrm{Rat}(\mathcal{F}),\mathrm{Rat}(\delta_{1}))

is an isomorphism. Indeed, since the RHS is equal to
\Hom(\mathrm{Rat}(\mathcal{F}),\mathrm{Rat}(\mathcal{F}))
, it is a 1-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{Q}
, so it is enough to show that the LHS is non-zero. However, we know that
\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\star\mathcal{F}
is the direct sum of Hodge modules isomorphic to
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
, where
\mathcal{H}
are pure polarizable Hodge structures. Applying
\mathrm{Rat}
, and noting once again that the RHS of (
12
) is a 1-dimensional vector space, we see that there is exactly one summand with
\lambda=0
in this sum, and the rank of the corresponding Hodge structure
\mathcal{H}
is one. Thus
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{0}(j)
is Tate’s Hodge structure.

It remains to compare weights: we have

\dim\Gr^{\lambda^{\prime}}=(2\rho,\lambda^{\prime})=(w_{0}^{-1}(2\rho),-\lambda)=(-2\rho,-\lambda)=\dim\Gr^{\lambda}.

Thus,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(i)\star\mathcal{F}
has weight
\dim\Gr^{\lambda^{\prime}}-2\dim\Gr^{\lambda}+\dim\Gr^{\lambda}=0
, so
j=0
. Thus the LHS of (
12
) is non-zero, so (
12
) is an isomorphism. We define the counit
e_{\mathcal{F}}
as the preimage of the counit for
\mathrm{Rat}(\mathcal{F})
under this isomorphism.

We define the unit
i_{\mathcal{F}}
similarly. It follows from the fact that
\mathrm{Rat}
is faithful and monoidal that
e_{\mathcal{F}}
and
i_{\mathcal{F}}
satisfy the above conditions for the unit and the counit. The existence of a right dual is proved similarly.

One checks that the dual of
\mathcal{F}_{1}\star\mathcal{F}_{2}
is
\mathcal{F}_{2}^{\vee}\star\mathcal{F}_{1}^{\vee}
.
Thus, according to (
11
), for every
\lambda\in X_{+}
and for every pure polarizable Hodge structure
\mathcal{H}
, the Hodge module
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
has a dual. Finally, every object is the direct sum of the objects isomorphic to
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
, so every object has a dual and the category is proved to be rigid.

\boxtimes

∎

Using the base change and Proposition

:

a_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{1})\otimes a_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{2})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}|_{0}=a_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{1}\star\mathcal{F}_{2}).

Applying the cohomology functor and using the Künneth decomposition (which follows from Proposition
2.3
(i)) we get an injective graded homomorphism

a_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{1})\otimes a_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{2})\hookrightarrow a_{+}(\mathcal{F}_{1}\star\mathcal{F}_{2}).

Since the global cohomology functor from
[
MV
]
is monoidal, the graded dimensions are equal, so this homomorphism is an isomorphism.
∎

4.2.
The category of Tate’s Hodge modules

Recall that
\mathcal{H}_{0}
stands for the trivial 1-dimensional Hodge structure. The following proposition is crucial for the proofs of Theorems
1
,
2
, and
3
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\Tate^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
be the strictly full subcategory of
\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
whose objects are isomorphic to the direct sums of
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0}(i))
, where
\lambda
ranges over
X_{+}
and
i
ranges over
\mathbb{Z}
.

(i) For
\mathcal{F}\in\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
the Hodge structure
a_{+}(\mathcal{F})
is the direct sum of Tate twists of the trivial Hodge structure if and only if
\mathcal{F}\in\Tate^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
.

(ii) The category
\Tate^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
is closed under the convolution.

Proof.

(i) Using (
11
) and Lemma
4.1
, we see that

a_{+}(\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}))=a_{+}(\mathrm{IC}_{0}(\mathcal{H})\star\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0}))=\mathcal{H}\otimes a_{+}(\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0})).

Now the ‘only if’ direction follows easily from Proposition
3.1
and faithfulness of
a_{+}
.

For the ‘if’ direction, let
j_{\lambda}\colon\Gr^{\lambda}\to\Gr
be the locally closed embedding and
a_{\lambda}\colon\Gr^{\lambda}\to pt
be the constant morphism. Consider the mixed Hodge module
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}:=H^{0}((j_{\lambda})_{!}a_{\lambda}^{\dagger}\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{Hdg}})\in\MHM(\Gr_{G})
. Recall that
a\colon\Gr_{G}\to pt
is the constant morphism. We need a lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
i\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
H^{2i+1}(a_{*}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda})=0
and
H^{2i}(a_{*}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda})
is a Tate twist of a trivial Hodge structure.

Proof.

Let
N\subset G
be the unipotent radical of the Borel subgroup
B
. Let
\nu\in X_{*}
be a co-character and let
S_{\nu}:=N_{\mathcal{K}}\cdot(t^{\nu}G_{\mathcal{O}})
be a semi-infinite Schubert cell (here
t^{\nu}
is as in Section
3.1
). Let
\rho
be the half sum of positive roots of
G
with respect to
B
. Recall from
[
MV
, Thm. 3.2]
that the intersection
S_{\nu}\cap\Gr^{\lambda}
is of pure dimension
\rho(\nu+\lambda)
as long as it is non-empty.

Let
a_{\nu,\lambda}\colon S_{\nu}\cap\Gr^{\lambda}\to pt
be the projection to the point. Consider the top degree cohomology with compact support

H_{c}^{2\rho(\nu+\lambda)}(S_{\nu}\cap\Gr^{\lambda},\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{Hdg}}):=H^{2\rho(\nu+\lambda)}((a_{\nu,\lambda})_{!}a_{\nu,\lambda}^{\dagger}\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{Hdg}})\in\MHM(pt).

Repeating the arguments of
[
MV
, Sect. 3]
(see especially Prop. 3.10 in loc. cit.), we identify
H^{i}(a_{*}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda})
with

\bigoplus_{\nu\in X_{*},2\rho(\nu)=i}H_{c}^{2\rho(\nu+\lambda)}(S_{\nu}\cap\Gr^{\lambda},\mathbb{Q}).

In particular, we see that
H^{i}(a_{*}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda})=0
for odd
i
.

It remains to show that
H_{c}^{2\rho(\nu+\lambda)}(S_{\nu}\cap\Gr^{\lambda},\mathbb{Q})
is a Tate twist of a trivial Hodge structure. In fact, the top cohomology with compact support is always Tate. One way to show this is to reduce to the case when the scheme is irreducible and to note that the top cohomology is 1-dimensional in this case. ∎

We return to the proof of Proposition
4.3
. Since
a_{*}=a_{!}
preserves weights, we have
\gr^{0}(a_{*}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda})=a_{*}(\gr^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}))
. Now the previous lemma implies that
a_{*}(\gr^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}))
is the direct sum of Tate twists of trivial Hodge structures.

Note that
\gr^{0}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\in\MH^{0}(\Gr_{G})
and it follows from semisimplicity that
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
is
its direct summand. Thus
a_{*}\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
is a direct summand of
a_{*}(\gr^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}))
and the claim follows.

(ii) Since
a_{+}
is monoidal (Proposition
4.1
), part (ii) follows from part (i).
∎

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
1

We will construct an equivalence

\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})[\sqrt{T}]=\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Gr_{G})\boxtimes\MH(pt)[\sqrt{T}].

The theorem will follow by applying the usual geometric Satake equivalence
\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Gr_{G})=\Rep\mathbb{Q}(\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}})
and taking the even components of the both sides.

Consider the functor
F\colon\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Gr_{G})\to\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})[\sqrt{T}]
sending
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}
to
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{+}(\frac{1}{2}\dim\Gr^{\lambda})
. We claim that this is a monoidal functor. Indeed, take
\lambda,\mu\in X_{*}
. By Proposition
4.3
,
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0})(\frac{1}{2}\dim\Gr^{\lambda})\star\mathrm{IC}_{\mu}(\mathcal{H}_{0})(\frac{1}{2}\dim\Gr^{\mu})
is the direct sum of objects of the form
\mathrm{IC}_{\nu}(\mathcal{H}_{0})(i)
, where
\nu\in X_{*}
and
i\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. However, this object has weight zero, and the statement follows.

Next, we have an obvious embedding
G\colon\MH(pt)[\sqrt{T}]\to\MH^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})[\sqrt{T}]
. We claim that
F\boxtimes G
is an equivalence of categories. According to (
11
) and the commutativity constraints, we have

\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})^{+}=\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{+}\left(\frac{1}{2}\dim\Gr^{\lambda}\right)\star\mathrm{IC}_{0}(\mathcal{H})^{+}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\dim\Gr^{\lambda}\right).

Similarly,

\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})^{-}=\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{+}\left(\frac{1}{2}\dim\Gr^{\lambda}\right)\star\mathrm{IC}_{0}(\mathcal{H})^{-}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\dim\Gr^{\lambda}\right).

We see that by Proposition
3.1
(i), the functor is essentially surjective. The fact that the functor is fully faithful follows from the definitions and Proposition
3.1
(ii). ∎

4.4.
Proof of Theorem
2

Consider the functor
F\colon\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Gr_{G})\to\Tate^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})[\sqrt{T}]
sending
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}
to
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{+}(\frac{1}{2}\dim\Gr^{\lambda})
. As in the proof of Theorem
1
, we check that this functor is monoidal.

Consider the category
\Rep\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}}
. Its irreducible objects are in a natural bijection with
\mathbb{Z}
, and we equip the category with the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
parity grading. Consider the functor

G\colon\Rep\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}}\to\Tate^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})[\sqrt{T}]

sending the representation with weight
i\in\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathrm{IC}(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{+}(i/2)
. As in the proof of Theorem
1
, we check that
F\boxtimes G
is an equivalence of categories. Thus, using the usual geometric Satake, we get

\Tate^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})[\sqrt{T}]=\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Gr_{G})\boxtimes\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}}=\Rep_{\mathbb{Q}}(\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\times\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}}).

We note that the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grading on this category corresponds to a central embedding
\mu_{2}\to\check{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\times\mathbf{G_{m,\mathbb{Q}}}
. It is easy to see that this embedding is exactly
2\rho\times\iota
. The theorem follows. ∎

5.
Mixed Hodge modules

In this Section we sketch a proof of Theorem
3
. In Section
2.3
we defined the category
\MHM(Y)
of mixed Hodge modules on an ind-scheme
Y
with an exact faithful functor
\MHM(Y)\to\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)
. We note that we have smooth descents for mixed Hodge modules, proof being the same as that of Proposition
2.14
.

5.1.
Equivariant mixed Hodge modules

Let, as in Section
2.10
,
H
be a connected pro-algebraic group acting nicely on an ind-scheme
Y
. In this case, using smooth descent for morphisms of mixed Hodge modules, we can define the category
\MHM^{H}(Y)
of equivariant mixed Hodge modules. Similarly to Proposition
2.16
, we show that the forgetful functor
\MHM^{H}(Y)\to\MHM(Y)
is fully faithful and we identify
\MHM^{H}(Y)
with a full subcategory of
\MHM(Y)
. Finally, we have an analogue of Lemma
2.17
.

Recall that an object of
\MHM(Y)
has a weight filtration. It follows from
[
Sai2
, Lemma 2.25]
that for an object in
\MHM^{H}(Y)
this is a filtration by equivariant submodules so that the associated graded is in
\MH^{H}(Y)
.

5.2.
G_{\mathcal{O}}
-equivariant mixed Hodge modules on
\Gr_{G}

By the above, we can consider the category
\MHM^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
. We want to describe the objects. Using the weight filtration and Proposition
3.1
, we see that the simple objects are pure Hodge modules of the form
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
, where
\lambda\in X_{+}
and
\mathcal{H}
is an irreducible Hodge structure.

The definition of
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
, given in Section
3.1
, generalizes to the mixed Hodge structures
\mathcal{H}
as follows:
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}):=\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
. (The original definition cannot be used because we do not define intermediate extensions for mixed Hodge modules.)

Proposition 5.1
.

Every object of
\MHM^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
can be uniquely written as the direct sum of subobjects isomorphic to
\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
for some distinct co-characters
\lambda\in X_{+}
and some mixed Hodge structures
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in X_{+}
,
\lambda\neq\mu
, let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
be mixed Hodge structures. It is enough to show that

\Ext^{1}(\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}),\mathrm{IC}_{\mu}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}))=0
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\Gr^{\mu}\subset\overline{\Gr}^{\lambda}
. Let
j\colon\Gr^{\mu}\to\overline{\Gr}^{\lambda}
be the locally closed embedding. It is enough to show that
j^{*}\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
lives in cohomological degrees
\leq-2
. Since
\mathrm{Rat}
is exact and faithful, it is enough to show that
j^{*}\mathrm{IC}_{\lambda}
lives in perverse cohomological degrees
\leq-2
. This is explained in
[
Gai
, Sect. 2.1.3]
.
∎

We define the convolution of mixed Hodge modules similarly to the convolution of pure Hodge modules.

The constructions of rigidity, commutativity constraint, unit object, and the fiber functor are the same as in the pure case with minor modifications, thus
\MHM^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})
becomes a neutral Tannakian category.

Finally, as in the proof of Theorem
1
we construct an equivalence of categories.

\MHM^{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\Gr_{G})[\sqrt{T}]=\Perv_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Gr_{G})\boxtimes\MHM(pt)[\sqrt{T}].

and the theorem follows. ∎

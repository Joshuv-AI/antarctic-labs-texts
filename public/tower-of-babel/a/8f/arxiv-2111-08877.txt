Global stability dynamics of the timelike extremal hypersurfaces in Minkowski space
Weiping Yan, Weijia Li

Abstract
This paper aims to study the relationship between the timelike extremal hypersurfaces and the classical minimal surfaces. This target also gives the long time dynamics of timelike extremal hypersurfaces in Minkowski spacetime ℝ 1 + M \mathbb{R}^{1+M} with the dimension 2 ≤ M ≤ 7 2\leq M\leq 7 . In this dimension, the stationary solution of timelike extremal hypersurface equation is the solution of classical minimal surface equation div ⁡ ( ∇ u 1 + | ∇ u | 2 ) = 0 , ∀ x ∈ ℝ M , {\rm div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\right)=0,\hskip 11.74988pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}, which only admits the hyperplane solution by Bernstein theorem. We prove that this hyperplane solution as the stationary solution of timelike extremal hypersurface equation is asymptotic stablely by finding the hidden dissipative structure of linearized equation. Here we overcome that the vector field method (based on the energy estimate and bootstrap argument) is lose effectiveness due to the lack of time-decay of solution for the linear perturbation equation. Meanwhile, a global well-posed result of linear damped wave with variable time-space coefficients is established. Hence, our result construct a unique global timelike non-small solution near the hyperplane. Key Words : Timelike minimal surface; The hyperplane; Asymptotic stability 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification : 53A10; 37L15; 35L05; 35A01;35B35

1.2
Main result

In the present paper, we aim to investigate the dynamical behavior around the stationary solution of equation (
1.2
) (i.e. answer the problem (i)).
We supplement equation (
1.2
) with an initial data

u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),~~u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x).

(1.5)

Since the minimal surface equation (
1.3
) only admits the hyperplane solution, we can denote it by

u_{s}(x)=A\cdot x+B,~~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{M},~~2\leq M\leq 7,

(1.6)

where
A
and
B
denote two constant vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{M}
, and we require that
A
is non-zero vector. According to the classification of solution, (
1.6
) is the spacelike solution of the timelike minimal surface equation (
1.2
).

We state the main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let the dimension
2\leq M\leq 7
. The hyperplane solution (
1.6
) of
the timelike extremal hypersurfaces equation (
1.2
) is asymptotic stable in Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{M})
for any
s\geq 1
, that is,
there exist a small positive constant
\varepsilon
, if the initial data (
1.5
) satisfies

u_{0}(x)=u_{s}(x)+w_{0}(x),\hskip 11.74988ptu_{1}(x)=w_{1}(x),

where smooth functions
w_{0}(x)
and
w_{1}(x)
are supported in
\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\Big||x|\leq 1\Big\}
, and

\|w_{0}(x)\|_{H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{M})}+\|w_{1}(x)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{M})}<\varepsilon,

then the timelike extremal hypersurfaces equation (
1.2
) admits a unique global solution
u(t,x)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{M})
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|u(t,x)-u_{s}(x)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{M})}=0.

Remark 1.1
.

We remark that our result also build up a global well-posedness result for the the timelike extremal hypersurfaces equation (
1.2
).
The uniqueness global solution takes the form

u(t,x)=u_{s}(x)+w(t,x),

with

\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\|w(t,x)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{M})}\lesssim\varepsilon.

Meanwhile,
w(t,x)
decays in time with
polynomial
form.

Furthermore, it holds

\displaystyle 1+|\nabla u|^{2}-u_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+|\nabla u_{s}(x)+\nabla w|^{2}-|w_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle 1+|\nabla u_{s}(x)|^{2}+O(\varepsilon)>0.

Therefore, we construct a unique global timelike non-small solution near the hyperplane.

Remark 1.2
.

For the dimension
M\geq 8
, theorem 1.1 also holds. But we are more interested in the stability of non-hyperplane solution of minimal surface equation for the dimension
M\geq 8
,
for example, the stability of Simons cone.

1.3
Sketch of the proof

Equation (
1.2
) can be rewritten as

\Big(1+|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}-u_{t}^{2}\Big)^{-{3\over 2}}\mathcal{F}(u)=0,\hskip 11.74988pt\forall(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{M},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{tt}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}u_{t}\partial_{t}|\nabla u|^{2}

(1.7)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left(\partial^{2}_{x_{k}}u(|\nabla u|^{2}-u_{t}^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x_{k}}u(\partial_{x_{k}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\partial_{x_{k}}u_{t}^{2})\right).

Thus finding the solution of (
1.2
) is equivalent to solve the equation

(1+|\nabla u|^{2})u_{tt}-(1+|\nabla u|^{2}-u_{t}^{2})\Delta u-\frac{1}{2}u_{t}\partial_{t}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\partial_{x_{k}}u\partial_{x_{k}}(|\nabla u|^{2}-u_{t}^{2})=0,~~

(1.8)

where
\forall(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{M}
, and the operator
\Delta=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\partial^{2}_{x_{k}}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator.

From (
1.8
), the stationary equation of it is

-(1+|\nabla u|^{2})\Delta u+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\partial_{x_{k}}u\partial_{x_{k}}|\nabla u|^{2}=0,

(1.9)

which is equivalent to the minimal surface equation (
1.3
). Thus to prove theorem 1.1, we only need to consider the perturbation equation from (
1.8
)-(
1.9
).
More precisely, we set the solution of (
1.8
) having the form

u(t,x)=u_{s}(t,x)+w(t,x),\hskip 11.74988pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{M},~t>0,

where
u_{s}(t,x)
given in (
1.6
) is the solution of (
1.9
), then we substitute it into (
1.8
) to get the perturbation equation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}w:=

\displaystyle\Big(1+|A+\nabla w|^{2}\Big)w_{tt}-\Big(1+|A+\nabla w|^{2}-w_{t}^{2}\Big)\Delta w-\frac{1}{2}w_{t}\partial_{t}\Big((\nabla w+2A)\cdot\nabla w\Big)

(1.10)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\Big(\partial_{x_{k}}w+A_{k}\Big)\partial_{x_{k}}\Big((\nabla w+2A)\cdot\nabla w-w_{t}^{2}\Big)=0,

which is a quasilinear wave equation, and the linear equation of it is

w_{tt}-\Delta w+(1+|A|^{2})^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{M}A_{k}A_{k^{\prime}}\partial_{x_{k}}\partial_{x_{k^{\prime}}}w=0.

(1.11)

We supplement it with an initial data

w(t,x)|_{t=0}=w_{0}(x),\hskip 11.74988ptw_{t}(t,x)|_{t=0}=w_{1}(x),

then utilizing the energy inequality given in Proposition 6.3.2 of the book of Hörmander
[
11
]
, it holds

\|w_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{M})}+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\|\partial_{x_{k}}w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{M})}\leq 2\Big(\|w_{1}(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{M})}+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\|\partial_{x_{k}}w(t,x)|_{t=0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{M})}\Big),

from which, the bootstrap argument (based on the energy estimate) loses efficacy due to
the absence of time-decay of solution for the linear wave equation (
1.11
). But if we choose a function
w^{(0)}(t,x)
to be satisfies

-(A+\nabla w^{(0)})\cdot\nabla w^{(0)}_{t}+w^{(0)}_{t}\Delta w^{(0)}>0,\hskip 11.74988pt\forall t>T^{*}>0,

then
we linearize nonlinear equation (
1.10
) at it to get the following linear damped wave equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(0)}_{w^{(0)}}h:=

\displaystyle\Big(1+|A+\nabla w^{(0)}|^{2}\Big)h_{tt}-\Big(1+|A+\nabla w^{(0)}|^{2}-(w^{(0)}_{t})^{2}\Big)\Delta h

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\Big(\partial_{x_{k}}w^{(0)}+A_{k}\Big)\Big(\nabla w^{(0)}+A\Big)\cdot\nabla\partial_{x_{k}}h+2\Big(-(A+\nabla w^{(0)})\cdot\nabla w^{(0)}_{t}+w^{(0)}_{t}\Delta w^{(0)}\Big)h_{t}

\displaystyle+\Big[{1\over 2}\nabla\Big(|\nabla w^{(0)}|^{2}-2(w^{(0)}_{t})^{2}+2A\cdot\nabla w^{(0)}\Big)+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\Big(\partial_{x_{k}}w^{(0)}+A_{k}\Big)\nabla\partial_{x_{k}}w^{(0)}

\displaystyle+2\Big(\nabla w^{(0)}+A\Big)\Big(w^{(0)}_{tt}-\Delta w^{(0)}\Big)\Big]\cdot\nabla h-2w^{(0)}_{t}\Big(\nabla w^{(0)}+A\Big)\cdot\nabla h_{t}=0,\hskip 11.74988ptt>T^{*}>0,

where the positive constant

is the local existence time of solution for above linear wave equation. It is not a damped linear wave equation for
t\in[0,T^{*}]
.
The proof of local existence of it can be followed from the book of Sogge
[
21
]
.

It gives a possible way to get a time-decay solution of linear wave equation. But the function
w^{(0)}(t,x)
is not a solution of equation (
1.10
), there must be an error term denoted by

E^{(0)}:=\mathcal{L}(w^{(0)}),

meanwhile, the function
w^{(0)}(t,x)
should be chosen to make the error term small, i.e.

E^{(0)}=\mathcal{L}(w^{(0)})\sim\varepsilon,~~in~some~function~space,

where

is called as the initial error term. In order to construct the solution of nonlinear equation (

, where

\mathcal{L}^{(0)}_{w^{(0)}}h^{(1)}=E^{(0)}.

Forward this idea, we get the

\mathcal{L}^{(0)}_{w^{(m-1)}}h^{(m)}=E^{(m-1)},\hskip 11.74988pt\forall m\in\mathbb{N},

At last, the most important thing is to prove

and the error term

\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}E^{(m)}(t,x)=\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{L}(w^{(\infty)})=0.

Here we should notice that there is loss of derivatives in each iteration step due to the quasilinear terms in (

for more details on this operator) to smooth the linearized equation at each iteration step.
Therefore, we construct the solution

Above method is called as Nash-Moser iteration scheme.
It has been used in

We refer the readers to

4.5
), we derive

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{N_{m}}h^{(m)}\|_{H^{k_{m-1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{m}^{k_{m-1}-1}\|h^{(m)}\|_{H^{1}}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{m}^{k_{m-1}-1}\|\Pi_{N_{m}}E^{(m-1)}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{m}^{2(k_{m-1}-1)}\|\Pi_{N_{m}}E^{(m-1)}\|_{H^{k_{m-1}}}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon^{2^{m-2}},

which combined with (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) yields

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{N_{m}}E^{(m)}\|_{H^{k_{m}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{m}^{4}\|h^{(m)}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{m}^{4}\Big(\|\Pi_{N_{m}}E^{(m-1)}\|_{H^{1}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{m}^{4}\Big(\|\Pi_{N_{m-1}}E^{(m-1)}\|_{H^{k_{m-1}}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{0}^{(4+\alpha_{m+1})m+2(4+\alpha_{m+2})(m-1)}\Big(\|\Pi_{N_{m-2}}E^{(m-2)}\|_{H^{k_{m+2}}}\Big)^{2^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ldots,

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(N_{0}^{64+k-\bar{k}}\|\Pi_{N_{1}}E^{(0)}\|_{H^{k_{2m}}}\Big)^{2^{m}}.

We choose a sufficiently small positive constant
\varepsilon_{0}
such that

0<N_{0}^{64+k-\bar{k}}\|\Pi_{N_{1}}E^{(0)}\|_{H^{\bar{k}}}<\varepsilon^{2}.

Thus, by (
4.7
) we have

\|\Pi_{N_{m}}E^{(m)}\|_{H^{k_{m}}}<\varepsilon^{2^{m}},

so, the error term goes to
0
as
m\rightarrow\infty
, that is,

\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\|\Pi_{N_{m}}E^{(m)}\|_{H^{k_{m}}}=0.

On the other hand, note that
N_{m}=N_{0}^{m}
, by (
4.5
)-(
4.6
). It follows that

\|w^{(m)}\|_{H^{k_{m}}}\lesssim\|w^{(m-1)}\|_{H^{k_{m}}}+\|h^{(m)}\|_{H^{k_{m}}}\lesssim\varepsilon.

This means that
w^{(m)}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
. Hence we conclude that (
4.4
) holds.

Therefore, the nonlinear equation (
3.2
) with the zero initial data and the boundary condition
(
3.4
)
admits a global Sobolev solution

w^{(\infty)}(t,x)=w^{(0)}(t,x)+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}h^{(m)}(t,x),

and we use (
3.6
) to get

w^{(0)}(0,x)=\partial_{t}w^{(0)}(t,x)|_{t=0}=0.

Next, we discuss the case of small non-zero initial data.

We introduce the auxiliary function

\overline{w}(t,x)=w(t,x)-e^{-t^{2}}w_{0}(x)-te^{-t}w_{1}(x),\hskip 11.74988pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}.

Thus, the initial data reduces to

\overline{w}(0,x)=0,\hskip 11.74988pt\partial_{t}\overline{w}(0,x)=0,

and equations (
1.8
) are transformed into equations of
\overline{w}
.

Thus, we can follow the above iteration scheme to construct a global Sobolev solution

\overline{w}
.
Furthermore, the global Sobolev solution of equations (
1.8
) with a small non-zero initial data takes the form

\overline{w}(t,x)+e^{-t^{2}}w_{0}(x)+te^{-t}w_{1}(x)
, and this solution is uniqueness due to the uniqueness of each iteration step
h^{(m)}(t,x)
.
This completes the proof.

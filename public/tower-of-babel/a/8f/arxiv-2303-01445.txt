Abstract
. We construct harmonic weak Maass forms that map to cusp forms of weight k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 with rational coefficients under the ξ \xi -operator. This generalizes work of the first author, Griffin, Ono, and Rolen, who constructed distinguished preimages under this differential operator of weight 2 2 newforms associated to rational elliptic curves using the classical Weierstrass theory of elliptic functions. We extend this theory and construct a vector-valued Jacobi–Weierstrass ζ \zeta -function which is a generalization of the classical Weierstrass ζ \zeta -function.



1.
Introduction

Harmonic weak Maass forms are real-analytic generalizations of classical modular forms with applications in combinatorics, number theory, as well as representation theory and physics (see for instance
[
BFOR17
]
and the references therein). They were first introduced by Bruinier and the second author
[
BF04
]
in the context of theta lifts. A
harmonic weak Maass form of weight
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
for a congruence subgroup
\Gamma
of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is a smooth function
F:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
that transforms like a classical modular form under
\Gamma
, but which is harmonic rather than holomorphic (see Section
2.1
for a precise definition). If
F
has poles in
\mathbb{H}
it is called
polar harmonic weak Maass form
.

Harmonic weak Maass forms are inherently connected to deep number-theoretic questions. A beautiful example is Zwegers’ work
[
Zwe02
]
who showed that Ramanujan’s mock theta functions are holomorphic parts of harmonic weak Maass forms. Such functions are also called mock modular forms
[
Zag09
]
. Harmonic weak Maass forms also appear prominently in the Kudla program and numerous other places in mathematics.

The
\xi
-operator

\xi_{k}=-2iv^{k}\overline{\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\tau}}},\ \ \tau=u+iv,

plays a crucial role in relating the theory of classical modular forms to harmonic weak Maass forms.
It defines a surjective map from the space
H_{k}
of harmonic weak Maass forms of weight
k
to the space
S_{2-k}
of cusp forms of weight
2-k
.
The image under the
\xi
-operator is called the
shadow
of the corresponding harmonic weak Maass form resp. mock modular form.
In particular, there are infinitely many preimages in the space
H_{k}
for any given cusp form in
S_{2-k}
, yet they are surprisingly difficult to construct. The quest for distinguished preimages under the
\xi
-operator is a fundamental question, and as cusp forms are omnipresent in number theory, the construction of distinguished preimages offers new routes to tackle related problems.

There are various approaches to this problem: Bruinier
[
Bru02
]
and Bringmann and Ono
[
BO07
]
showed that certain real-analytic Poincaré series, originally introduced by Niebur
[
Nie74
]
, map to the classical exponential type Poincaré series of dual weight under the
\xi
-operator, provided that they converge, e.g. when the weight is negative. The coefficients of cuspidal Poincaré series are however not easy to handle, both practically and theoretically.

Another approach to the problem uses the
non-holomorphic Eichler integral

g^{*}(\tau)=\int_{-\overline{\tau}}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{f(-\overline{z})}}{(-i(z-\tau))^{k}}dz

for a cusp form
g
of weight
2-k
, as proposed and used first in work by Andrews, Rhoades, and Zwegers
[
ARZ13
]
as well as Dabholkar, Murthy, and Zagier
[
DMZ23
]
. Using a suitable auxiliary holomorphic cusp form
h
and holomorphic projection, they construct a harmonic weak Maass form
F(\tau)
of weight
k
such that
\xi_{k}F=cg
for some constant
c
.
This approach is particularly well-suited when
g
is a unary theta function, since then the Fourier coefficients of the holomorphic projection can be evaluated explicitly (see also
[
MOR21
,
MMR22
]
).

Recently, Ehlen, Li, and Schwagenscheidt
[
ELS22
]
gave an explicit procedure to find a
good
preimage of a CM modular form using a certain theta lift. It is known from previous work by Bruinier, Ono, and Rhoades
[
BOR08
]
that such good preimages always exist and in particular that their holomorphic parts have algebraic Fourier coefficients at
\infty
. Using their explicit construction, Ehlen, Li, and Schwagenscheidt could pinpoint the exact algebraic number field containing these Fourier coefficients.

In this work we focus on the geometric approach and generalize the work of Griffin, Ono, Rolen, and the first author
[
AGOR15
]
. They construct harmonic weak Maass forms whose shadows are given by newforms of weight
2
with rational coefficients, generalizing previous work by Guerzhoy
[
Gue15
]
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and let
G_{E}
be the corresponding newform of weight
2
for a congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
via the modularity theorem. Over
\mathbb{C}
,
E
is isomorphic to a torus
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda_{E}
, with the isomorphism given by

\wp^{\prime}(z)^{2}=4\wp(z)^{3}-g_{2}\wp(z)+g_{3},

where
\wp
is the Weierstrass
\wp
-function for the lattice
\Lambda_{E}
, and
g_{2},g_{3}
are the normalized Eisenstein series for the lattice
\Lambda_{E}
.

The
Weierstrass
\zeta
-function

(1.1)

\zeta_{\Lambda_{E}}(z)=\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda_{E}\setminus\{0\}}\left(\frac{1}{z-\omega}+\frac{1}{\omega}+\frac{z}{\omega^{2}}\right),

is not itself invariant under shifts by lattice points, but has a well-known non-analytic completion
\zeta_{\Lambda_{E}}^{*}(z)
which is indeed invariant under
\Lambda_{E}
. This completion allows one to construct a canonical preimage
Z_{E}
of the modular form
G_{E}
under the operator
\xi_{0}
by taking

Z_{E}(\tau)=\zeta_{\Lambda_{E}}^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{E}(\tau)),

where
\mathcal{E}_{E}(\tau)=\int_{\tau}^{\infty}G_{E}(t)dt
is the holomorphic Eichler integral. The
error to modularity
of the Eichler integral is determined by the lattice, i.e.
\mathcal{E}_{E}(\gamma\tau)=\mathcal{E}_{E}(\tau)+\omega
, for
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(N)
and for some
\omega\in\Lambda_{E}
, giving the
\Gamma_{0}(N)
-invariance of
Z_{E}(\tau)
. We recall the details of this construction in Section
3
.

These preimages
Z_{E}
can be computed very efficiently and are also of theoretical interest. In
[
AGOR15
]
, they were used to obtain a criterion for the vanishing of critical
L
-derivatives of quadratic twists of
E
. Another application in the context of vertex operator algebras can be found in
[
BM21
]
.

In the current work we generalize the above construction to newforms
f
with rational Fourier coefficients of weight
k\geq 2
, for a congruence subgroup
\Gamma\subset\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, with
\Gamma_{1}(N)\subset\Gamma
for some integer
N
. Our construction involves vector-valued forms. For simplicity we restrict to the easiest case in the introduction and focus on describing the central ideas of our work.

We start by noting that a modular form
f
of weight
k\geq 2
gives a vector-valued modular form
f:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
of weight
2
by taking

(1.2)

G(\tau)=cf(\tau)(\tau e_{1}+e_{2})^{k-2},

where
(e_{1},e_{2})
is a fixed basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
is the
(k-2)
-th symmetric power of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, which can be realized as the space of homogeneous polynomials in
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
of degree
k-2
, and
c
is a constant. Note that this construction of vector-valued modular forms goes back to classic work of Kuga and Shimura
[
KS60
]
. For further details, see Section
2.3
.

The construction involves two central elements: a vector-valued generalization of the Weierstrass
\zeta
-function
\widehat{\zeta}
and the polynomial Eichler integral
\mathcal{E}_{f}
.

In the simplest case (see Section
6
), we define the multivariable function
\zeta:\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}^{k-1}\rightarrow\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
by

(1.3)

\zeta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})=\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z_{j})c_{j}e_{1}^{j}e_{2}^{k-2-j},\ \ c_{j}={k-2\choose j},\ \ \Lambda_{\tau}=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau.

It has a natural completion

(1.4)

\widehat{\zeta}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})=\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}\zeta^{*}_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z_{j})c_{j}e_{1}^{j}e_{2}^{k-2-j},

where
\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}^{*}
is the completion of the classical Weierstrass
\zeta
-function. Our first main result is the general construction of
Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-functions

\widehat{\zeta}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The function
\widehat{\zeta}
is a (non-holomorphic) Jacobi form of weight
1
and index
0
, invariant under the lattice
\Lambda_{\tau}^{k-1}
.

To construct the higher degree generalization
\widehat{\zeta}
of the completed Weierstrass
\zeta
-function we follow Rolen
[
Rol16
]
. The key point is to write the
\zeta
-function as the logarithmic derivative of the Weierstrass
\sigma
-function which in turn is essentially given by a multiple of Jacobi’s theta function. This definition of the
\sigma
-function can be generalized to higher degree by giving an analogous construction using a Jacobi theta function of lattice index.
Replacing the logarithmic derivative by a suitable weight raising operator we obtain the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function.

We then use the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function to construct the natural preimages of
f
by evaluating it at the polynomial Eichler integral
\mathcal{E}_{f}:\mathbb{H}\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
,

(1.5)

\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(X_{1},X_{2}))=\int_{\tau}^{\infty}f(t)(tX_{1}+X_{2})^{k-2}dt,

for
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
,
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
(X_{1},X_{2})=(e_{1},e_{2})M
is the change of basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and
f
is a cusp form of weight
k
.

To realize this, we first extend the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function to include a possible change of basis for the space
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, given by
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, as follows

(1.6)

\widehat{\zeta}_{M}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})=\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}\zeta^{*}_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z_{j})X_{1}^{j}X_{2}^{k-2-j},\ \ (X_{1},X_{2})=(e_{1},e_{2})M.

This gives the vector-valued Jacobi form
\widehat{\zeta}:\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}^{k-1}\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
where we write
\widehat{\zeta}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z},M)=\widehat{\zeta}_{M}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}).

Fixing further the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, we take the natural isomorphism
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{k-1}
, which gives us the most general Jacobi–Weierstrass function
\widehat{\zeta}:\mathbb{H}\times\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2}).
This form was carefully defined to account for the various actions of the subgroup
\Gamma
on the upper-half plane
\mathbb{H}
and
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
(compare Section
5
).

Finally, we define the vector-valued form
F:\mathbb{H}\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
by taking

(1.7)

F(\tau,M)=\widehat{\zeta}_{M}(\Lambda_{f},\mathcal{E}(\tau,(e_{1},e_{2})M^{-1})),

where
\Lambda_{f}
is the lattice corresponding to
f
(see Section
2.4
, in particular Remark
2.3
).

We note that
F
can also be written as a vector-valued form

F:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow V,

valued in the infinite-dimensional vector space
V=\mathrm{Functions}\left\{\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\right\}
, by sending
F(\tau)
to the function
M\rightarrow F(\tau,M)
in
V
.

The main result of the paper is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

The function
F:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow V
is a vector-valued polar harmonic weak Maass form of weight
0
for
\Gamma
, whose image under
\xi_{0}
is given by

G(\tau)=\frac{2\pi i}{\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{f})}f(\tau)(\tau e_{1}+e_{2})^{k-2}.

Note that in the case of
k=2
the functions we obtain through our construction are in fact constant in the
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-variable, therefore we recover the results from
[
AGOR15
]
. The poles of
F
are explicitly computed in Proposition
6.3
.

Remark 1.3
.

The naive approach of fixing a basis
(e_{1},e_{2})
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and plugging in the Eichler integral directly
\widehat{\zeta}(\Lambda_{f},\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(e_{1},e_{2})))
loses the
\Gamma
-invariance. In general we do not have a compatibility between the action of
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
on the outside and on the inside

\widehat{\zeta}\left(\Lambda_{f},\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right)\neq\gamma\cdot\widehat{\zeta}\left(\Lambda_{f},\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\gamma\tau,\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right).

By controlling the change of basis with the extra variable
M\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
we insure the
\Gamma
-invariance of the vector-valued form
F
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the notion of harmonic weak Maass forms, give the necessary background on representation theory, and introduce the vector-valued Eichler integral. In Section
3
we review the construction of Weierstrass harmonic weak Maass forms in the case of weight
2
. The generalization to higher degree of the completed Weierstrass
\zeta
-function is given in Section
4
. Section
5
contains our main result, namely the construction of the vector-valued Jacobi–Weierstrass harmonic weak Maass form
F:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow V
. In Section
6
we compute the Laurent expansion of the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function and the poles of the polar harmonic weak Maass form
F
. In Section
7
we provide two examples of our construction.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper we let
\Gamma
be a congruence subgroup of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
\Gamma_{1}(N)\subset\Gamma
for some positive integer
N
, and
\Gamma_{1}(N)=\{\gamma\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\gamma\equiv\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&*\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mod N\}
. We call
f
a newform for
\Gamma
if it is a newform for
\Gamma_{1}(N)
.

2.1.
Harmonic weak Maass forms

By
\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
we denote the metaplectic group consisting of pairs
(\gamma,\phi)
, where
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\phi:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic function with
\phi(\tau)^{2}=c\tau+d
. We let
\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
be the inverse image of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
under the covering map
\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

A twice continuously differentiable function
F:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a
harmonic weak Maass form of weight
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
for
\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
if it satisfies

(1)

\Delta_{k}F=0
, where
\Delta_{k}=-v^{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v^{2}}\right)+ikv\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial u}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\right)
is the hyperbolic weight
k
Laplace operator and we write
\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{H}
.

(2)

F(\tau)|_{k}(\gamma,\phi):=\phi(\tau)^{-2k}F(\gamma\tau)=F(\tau)
for
(\gamma,\phi)\in\mathrm{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

(3)

There is a Fourier polynomial
P_{F}(\tau)=\sum_{n\leq 0}a^{+}(n)q^{n}
, called the principal part of
F
, such that

F(\tau)-P_{F}(\tau)=O(e^{-\varepsilon v})

as
v\to\infty
, uniformly in
u
, for some
\varepsilon>0
. A similar conditions holds at all cusps.

We define harmonic weak Maass forms of integral weight
k\in\mathbb{Z}
transforming with respect to the group
\Gamma
accordingly. If
F
has poles on
\mathbb{H}
it is called
polar harmonic weak Maass form
.

2.2.
The Jacobi theta function

Let
L\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{g}
be an even lattice with positive definite inner product defined through
\left(v,w\right):=v^{t}G_{L}w
, where
G_{L}
is the Gram matrix of
L
, a symmetric matrix with integer entries and even entries on the diagonal, and let
V=L\otimes\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{g}
with quadratic form
Q(v)=\left(v,v\right)/2
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
. We define the Jacobi theta function by

\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})=\sum_{\ell\in L}e^{\pi i(\ell,\ell)\tau}e^{2\pi i(\ell,\boldsymbol{z})}.

The following two transformation properties of
\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})
are well-known

(2.1)

\displaystyle\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})

\displaystyle=e^{-\pi i\tau(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{m})}e^{-2\pi i(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{m})}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}),\ \ \boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\theta\left(\tau+1,\boldsymbol{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}).

Under the additional assumption that
L
is unimodular, see e.g.
[
Boy15
]
, it follows from Poisson summation that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\theta\left(-\frac{1}{\tau},\frac{\boldsymbol{z}}{\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=(\tau/i)^{g/2}e^{2\pi i\frac{Q(\boldsymbol{z})}{\tau}}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}).

These functional equations imply that
\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})
is a Jacobi form of weight
g/2
and index
\frac{1}{2}G_{L}
in the sense of
[
BK93
]
for the full Jacobi group
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}^{g})^{2}
. If
L
is not unimodular,
\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})
is a Jacobi form for some suitable congruence subgroup
\mathcal{J}_{L}=\Gamma_{L}\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}^{g})^{2}
of the full Jacobi group (compare
[
Boy15
, Corollary 3.34, 3.35]
).

We follow Böcherer–Kohnen
[
BK93
]
and define the
slash operator
by

\displaystyle(\phi|_{k,mL}A)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})

\displaystyle:=\phi\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\frac{\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n}}{c\tau+d}\right)(\sqrt{c\tau+d})^{-2k}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad e^{2\pi im\left(-c\frac{2Q(\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})}{(c\tau+d)}+\left(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{m}\right)\tau+2\left(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{z}\right)\right)},

where
A=\left[\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right),\sqrt{c\tau+d}\right),(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{n})\right]\in\MP_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}^{g})^{2}
and
k,m\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
.
Then the condition that
\theta
is a Jacobi form for the Jacobi subgroup
\mathcal{J}_{L}
is equivalent to

(2.4)

\theta|_{g/2,1/2}A=\theta,\ \ \text{ for }A\in\mathcal{J}_{L}.

As in
[
BRR15
]
we now define a weight raising operator for Jacobi forms by

(2.5)

Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}+2\pi i\frac{\Imm((G_{L}\boldsymbol{z})_{j})}{\Imm(\tau)},

where
\boldsymbol{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{g})\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
.
As we will show in Proposition
4.6
, we have

(2.6)

Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}\theta=(Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}\theta)|_{g/2+1,1/2}A

for
A=\left[\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right),\sqrt{c\tau+d}\right),(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{n})\right]\in\mathcal{J}_{L}
and
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
.

2.3.
A little bit of representation theory

We let
W(=\mathbb{C}^{2})
be the standard complex representation of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and let
\langle\,,\,\rangle
be the usual symplectic form on
W
with standard basis
e_{1},e_{2}
. This defines an integral structure on
W
. We let
W_{m}=\Sym^{m}W
be the irreducible representation of dimension
m+1
of highest weight
m
. Weight vectors are (multiples of)
e_{1}^{j}e_{2}^{m-j}
with corresponding weight
j
with respect to the standard rationally split torus
S=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}t&0\\
0&t^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\,:\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Here we write
v^{k}
for the symmetric tensor
v\bullet v\bullet\cdots\bullet v
.

Note that
W_{m}
is self-dual, i.e.
W_{m}\simeq W_{m}^{*}
, where the isomorphism is induced by the symplectic form on
W
. We will not distinguish between
W_{m}
and
W_{m}^{*}
.

The action of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
W_{k-2}=\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
is given by acting on the basis on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, i.e.

M\circ(e_{1},e_{2}):=\begin{pmatrix}e_{1}&e_{2}\end{pmatrix}M=\begin{pmatrix}ae_{1}+ce_{2}&be_{1}+de_{2}\end{pmatrix},\,\text{ for }M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

We recall the usual action of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of the upper-half plane
\mathbb{H}
by fractional linear transformations
M\tau=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}
for
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Moreover, we denote by
j(M,\tau)=c\tau+d
the factor of automorphy.

We then have

(2.7)

M^{-1}\circ((M\tau)e_{1}+e_{2})^{k-2}=j(M,\tau)^{2-k}(\tau e_{1}+e_{2})^{k-2}

for
M\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

For a modular form
f
of weight
k
for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma
, this directly implies that the holomorphic
1
-form on
\mathbb{H}
given by

\eta_{f}:=f(\tau)d\tau\otimes(\tau e_{1}+e_{2})^{k-2}

satisfies
\gamma^{-1}\circ\eta_{f}(\gamma\tau)=\eta_{f}(\tau)
for
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

2.4.
Eichler integrals

Throughout this section let
f
be a cusp form of weight
k
for some finite index subgroup
\Gamma
of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and let
(X_{1},X_{2})
be a basis for
W
. We define the
Eichler integral of
f
by

(2.8)

\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(X_{1},X_{2}))=\int_{\tau}^{\infty}f(t)(tX_{1}+X_{2})^{k-2}dt.

It defines a function
\mathcal{E}_{f}:\mathbb{H}\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, where the basis
(X_{1},X_{2})
above corresponds to a matrix
M\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
(X_{1},X_{2})=M\circ(e_{1},e_{2})
. Note that we recover the usual (scalar-valued) Eichler integral by identifying
W_{m}
with the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
m
in two variables
X,Y
and by replacing
X_{1}
by
1
and
X_{2}
by
\tau
.

For
\gamma\in\Gamma
we consider

\mathcal{E}_{f}(\gamma\tau,\gamma^{-1}\circ(X_{1},X_{2}))=\int_{\gamma\tau}^{\infty}f(t)\left[\gamma^{-1}\circ(tX_{1}+X_{2})^{k-2}\right]dt,\,\gamma\in\Gamma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f
be a cusp form of weight
k
for
\Gamma
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{f}(\gamma\tau,\gamma^{-1}\circ(X_{1},X_{2}))=\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(X_{1},X_{2}))-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}{k-2\choose\ell}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}\int_{\gamma^{-1}\infty}^{\infty}f(t)t^{\ell}dt.

for
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
(X_{1},X_{2})
a basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Proof.

Identity (
2.7
) implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{f}(\gamma\tau,\gamma^{-1}\circ(X_{1},X_{2}))

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma\tau}^{\infty}f(t)\cdot\left[\gamma^{-1}\circ(tX_{1}+X_{2})^{k-2}\right]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau}^{\gamma^{-1}\infty}f(t)(tX_{1}+X_{2})^{k-2}dt.

We split the integral

\int_{\tau}^{\gamma^{-1}\infty}f(t)(tX_{1}+X_{2})^{k-2}dt=\int_{\tau}^{\infty}f(t)(tX_{1}+X_{2})^{k-2}dt-\int_{\gamma^{-1}\infty}^{\infty}f(t)(tX_{1}+X_{2})^{k-2}dt

and use the binomial expansion to obtain the result.
∎

We now recall some facts about modular symbols. Further details and background may be found for instance in Chapters
8
and
10
in
[
Ste07
]
. Let
\mathbb{M}_{k}
denote the module of
modular symbols
, i.e. the
\mathbb{Z}
-module generated by symbols
P\{\alpha,\beta\}
, where
P\in\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]_{k-2}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k-2
with integer coefficients and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
are cusps, modulo the relations obtained from

\{\alpha,\beta\}+\{\beta,\gamma\}+\{\gamma,\alpha\}=0

and all torsion.
The group
\Gamma
acts from the left on modular symbols by combining the usual action on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
via Möbius transformations and on homogeneous polynomials defined by

(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right),P)\mapsto P(dX-bY,-cX+aY).

We denote by
\mathbb{M}_{k}(\Gamma)
the module of modular symbols modulo all torsion and all relations obtained from
x-\gamma.x
where
x\in\mathbb{M}_{k}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Every cusp form
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma)
defines a pairing

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Phi_{f}:\mathbb{M}_{k}(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{C},\ \ (f,P\{\alpha,\beta\})\mapsto\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}f(t)P(t,1)dt,

which is consistent with the action of Hecke operators. If
f
is a normalized newform with integral coefficients, then the image
\Phi_{f}(\mathbb{M}_{k}(\Gamma))
is a lattice
\Lambda_{f}
in
\mathbb{C}
.

Remark 2.3
.

We write

(2.10)

\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(X_{1},X_{2}))=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\mathcal{E}_{\ell,f}(\tau)c_{\ell}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell},

with
c_{\ell}={k-2\choose\ell}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\ell,f}(\tau)=\int_{\tau}^{\infty}f(t)t^{\ell}dt
. If
f
has integral coefficients, the considerations above imply that

(2.11)

\mathcal{E}_{\ell,f}(\gamma\tau)-\mathcal{E}_{\ell,f}(\tau)\in\Lambda_{f}.

For
k=2
and
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}(N)
, the lattice
\Lambda_{f}
coincides with the period lattice of the rational elliptic curve associated to
f
.

We now choose the basis
\{c_{\ell}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}\}_{0\leq\ell\leq k-2}
of
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. In these coordinates the Eichler integral is given as

(2.12)

\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(X_{1},X_{2}))=(\mathcal{E}_{0,f}(\tau),\dots,\mathcal{E}_{k-2,f}(\tau))_{(X_{1},X_{2})}.

For later convenience, we reformulate Lemma
2.2
in a slightly different fashion.

Lemma 2.4
.

The Eichler integral
\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(X_{1},X_{2}))
is
\Lambda_{f}^{k-1}
-invariant under the
\Gamma
-action, meaning that for
\gamma\in\Gamma
we have

(\mathcal{E}_{0,f}(\gamma\tau),\dots,\mathcal{E}_{k-2,f}(\gamma\tau))_{\gamma^{-1}\circ(X_{1},X_{2})}=(\mathcal{E}_{0,f}(\tau),\dots,\mathcal{E}_{k-2,f}(\tau))_{(X_{1},X_{2})}+\omega,

with
\omega=(\lambda_{0},\dots,\lambda_{k-1})_{(X_{1},X_{2})}
and each
\lambda_{i}\in\Lambda_{f}
.

A straightforward computation gives the Fourier expansion of the coefficients
\mathcal{E}_{f,\ell}(\tau)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For a cusp form
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma)
with a Fourier expansion
f(\tau)=\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}
we have

\mathcal{E}_{f,\ell}(\tau)=\int_{\tau}^{\infty}f(t)t^{\ell}dt=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{n}}{2\pi n}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{\ell!}{(\ell-j)!}\left(\frac{i}{2\pi n}\right)^{j}\tau^{\ell-j}\right)q^{n}.

As is also apparent directly from the definition,
\mathcal{E}_{f,\ell}
is not 1-periodic except when
\ell=0
. In general the Fourier coefficients of
\mathcal{E}_{f,\ell}
are polynomials in
\tau
.

2.5.
Change of basis

We note that each basis
(X_{1},X_{2})
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
gives rise to a basis

\{c_{\ell}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}\}_{0\leq\ell\leq k-2}
of
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Let
\boldsymbol{z}=(z_{0},\ldots_{,}z_{k-2})\in\mathbb{C}^{k-1}
. We write

(2.13)

(\boldsymbol{z})^{\mathrm{t}}_{(X_{1},X_{2})}=(z_{0},\ldots,z_{k-1})^{\mathrm{t}}_{(X_{1},X_{2})}=\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k-2}z_{\ell}c_{\ell}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}

for the corresponding element in
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
.
Then the action of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is given by

M\circ(\boldsymbol{z})^{\mathrm{t}}_{(e_{1},e_{2})}:=(\boldsymbol{z})^{\mathrm{t}}_{M\circ(e_{1},e_{2})}.

This gives a change of basis from the standard basis
\{c_{\ell}e_{1}^{\ell}e_{2}^{k-2-\ell}\}_{0\leq\ell\leq k-2}
of
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
to the basis
\{c_{\ell}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}\}_{0\leq\ell\leq k-2}
, where
(X_{1},X_{2})=M\circ(e_{1},e_{2})
. We describe this change of basis explicitly.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then

(\boldsymbol{z})^{\mathrm{t}}_{M\circ(e_{1},e_{2})}=N(M)(\boldsymbol{z})^{\mathrm{t}}_{(e_{1},e_{2})}

for a matrix
N(M)\in\GL_{k-1}(\mathbb{Z})
, whose entries
(N_{\ell t})_{0\leq\ell,t\leq k-2}
are given by

N_{\ell t}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq t\\
0\leq\ell-i\leq k-2-t\end{subarray}}{\ell\choose i}{k-2-\ell\choose t-i}a^{i}c^{t-i}b^{\ell-i}d^{k-2-t-\ell+i}.

Proof.

We note that

(\boldsymbol{z})^{\mathrm{t}}_{M\circ(e_{1},e_{2})}=\sum\limits_{t=0}^{k-2}z_{t}c_{t}(ae_{1}+ce_{2})^{t}(be_{1}+de_{2})^{k-2-t}.

By using the binomial theorem twice and rearranging the sums we obtain the stated formula.

The claim that
N(M)\in\GL_{k-1}(\mathbb{Z})
follows easily from the properties mentioned in Remark
2.7
below: for each
M\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
N(M)
has all integer entries and it is a group homomorphism, which implies that
N(M)^{-1}=N(M^{-1})
has integer entries as well, proving the claim.
∎

We note that this formula is already contained in classical work by Kuga-Shimura from the late 1950’s
[
KS60
]
.

Remark 2.7
.

For
M,M_{1},M_{2}\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and the notation as in Lemma
2.6
, we note the following properties

\displaystyle N(M_{1}M_{2})

\displaystyle=N(M_{2})N(M_{1}),

\displaystyle N(M)^{\mathrm{t}}

\displaystyle=N(M^{\mathrm{t}}),

\displaystyle N(M)^{-1}

\displaystyle=N(M^{-1}).

Since
N(M)\in\GL_{k-1}(\mathbb{Z})
by Lemma
2.6
we immediately obtain the following observation.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
M\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and the notation be as in Lemma
2.6
. Then we have

N(M)\Lambda_{f}^{k-1}=\Lambda_{f}^{k-1}.

Here, we view an element of
\Lambda_{f}^{k-1}
as a column vector.

3.
Weierstrass mock modular forms

We recall the classical construction of Weierstrass mock modular forms of Guerzhoy
[
Gue15
]
and Alfes–Griffin–Ono–Rolen
[
AGOR15
]
.

3.1.
The completed Weierstrass
\zeta
-function

In this section we review Rolen’s
[
Rol16
]
construction of Eisenstein’s completion of the Weierstrass
\zeta
-function.

We let
\Lambda_{\tau}=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
for
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and define for
z\in\mathbb{C}
the classical
Weierstrass
\sigma
-function

\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z)=z\,\prod_{w\in\Lambda_{\tau}\setminus\{0\}}\left(1-\frac{z}{w}\right)\exp\left(\frac{z}{w}+\frac{z^{2}}{2w^{2}}\right).

Its logarithmic derivative is the
Weierstrass
\zeta
-function

\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z)=\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z)}{\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z)}.

Moreover, we need the following classical identity (cf. Theorem 3.9 of
[
Pol03
]
) relating the Weierstrass
\sigma
-function to the standard Jacobi theta function

(3.1)

\vartheta(\tau,z)=-2\pi\eta(\tau)^{3}\exp\left(-\frac{\eta_{1}z^{2}}{2}\right)\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z).

Here,
\vartheta(\tau,z)
is a Jacobi form of weight
1/2
and index
1/2
given by

\vartheta(\tau,z)=\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}}e^{\pi in^{2}\tau+2\pi in\left(z+\frac{1}{2}\right)},

\eta_{1}
is the quasi-period defined by

\eta_{1}=\eta_{1}(\tau):=\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z+1)-\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z),

and
\eta(\tau)=q^{1/24}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})
is the Dedekind
\eta
-function.

Then we find that

\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\vartheta(\tau,z)}{\vartheta(\tau,z)}=\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z)-\eta_{1}z.

As
\vartheta(\tau,z)
is a holomorphic function in
z
, one may consider its derivative. However, this is no longer a Jacobi form.
We therefore replace the derivative
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
by the canonical raising operator
Y_{+}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}
on Jacobi forms and apply it to the Jacobi theta function

Y_{+}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}(\vartheta(\tau,z))=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\vartheta(\tau,z)+2\pi i\frac{\Imm(z)}{\Imm(\tau)}\vartheta(\tau,z)

which then is a Jacobi form of weight
3/2
and index
1/2
(compare Section
2.2
). Considering the corresponding analogue of the logarithmic derivative we obtain

(3.2)

\frac{Y_{+}^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}(\vartheta(\tau,z))}{\vartheta(\tau,z)}=\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z)-\eta_{1}z+\frac{2\pi i\Imm(z)}{\Imm(\tau)},

which is a real-analytic Jacobi form of weight
1
and index
0
. In particular, it is an elliptic function in
z
. Using the relation
G_{2}=\eta_{1}
for the weight
2
Eisenstein series, normalized to have constant term
2\zeta(2)
, one can show that the function

\zeta^{*}_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z)=\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z)-zG^{*}_{2}(\tau)-\pi\frac{\overline{z}}{\Imm(\tau)}

is a doubly-periodic function with respect to the lattice
\Lambda_{\tau}
. Here,
G_{2}^{*}(\tau)=G_{2}(\tau)-\pi/\Imm(\tau)
is the non-holomorphic completion of the weight
2
Eisenstein series.

We note that

\Imm(\tau)=\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{\tau}).

3.2.
Weierstrass mock modular forms

The completed Weierstrass
\zeta
-function can be used to produce harmonic weak Maass forms of weight
0
. We let
f
be a newform of weight
2
for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
with rational Fourier coefficients. In the notation of Section
2.4
,
\Lambda_{f}
is the associated lattice and
\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{f}(n)}{n}q^{n}
is the Eichler integral of
f(\tau)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{f}(n)q^{n}
. In particular, we have

\mathcal{E}_{f}(\gamma\tau)=\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau)+\omega,\,\omega\in\Lambda_{f},

for
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(N)
. We then define the Weierstrass form by

Z_{f}(\tau)=\zeta^{*}_{\Lambda_{f}}(\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau)).

The following theorem was proven in
[
Gue15
]
and
[
AGOR15
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume the notation and hypotheses above. The following are true:

(1)

The poles of the holomorphic part
Z_{f}^{+}(\tau)
of
Z_{f}(\tau)
are precisely those points for which
\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau)\in\Lambda_{f}
.

(2)

If
Z_{f}^{+}(\tau)
has poles in
\mathbb{H}
, then there is a canonical modular function
M_{f}(\tau)
with algebraic coefficients on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
for which
Z_{f}^{+}(\tau)-M_{f}(\tau)
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{H}
.

(3)

The function
Z_{f}(\tau)-M_{f}(\tau)
is a harmonic weak Maass form of weight
0
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.

(4)

We have that

\xi_{0}(Z_{f}(\tau))=-\frac{2\pi i}{\mathrm{vol}(\Lambda_{f})}f(\tau).

4.
Vector-valued Jacobi–Weierstrass forms

We first construct the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function in Section
4.1
, which is a higher degree analogue of the Weierstrass
\zeta
-function. In Section
4.2
we construct its completion based on Rolen’s approach and in Section
4.3
we define a vector-valued analogue.

4.1.
Elliptic functions of higher degree

Generalizing (
3.1
) we define a higher degree analogue of the Weierstrass
\sigma
-function. We let
\Lambda_{\tau}=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
for
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
. We define the
Jacobi–Weierstrass
\sigma
-function
by

(4.1)

\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(\boldsymbol{z})=e^{-u(\tau)Q(\boldsymbol{z})}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}),

where
u(\tau)
is a function in
\tau
defined in (
4.7
).

Note that for
g=1
the above definition differs from the classical definition of the Weierstrass
\sigma
-function by a (non-zero) constant factor depending on the lattice. This is however of no further importance here.

Let
\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}
. Using the properties of the Jacobi theta function from (
2.1
), we directly see that

(4.2)

\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})=e^{-u(\tau)(Q(\boldsymbol{z+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n}})-Q(\boldsymbol{z}))}e^{-\pi i\tau(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{m})}e^{-2\pi i(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{m})}\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(\boldsymbol{z})

for
\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}
.
We now define higher degree analogues of the
\wp
- and
\zeta
-function.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
. We define the
Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function
by

(4.3)

\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}(\boldsymbol{z})=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\log\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau},}(\boldsymbol{z}),\,1\leq j\leq g,

and the
Jacobi–Weierstrass
\wp
-function
by

(4.4)

\wp_{ji}(\boldsymbol{z})=\wp_{\Lambda_{\tau},ji}(\boldsymbol{z})=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}(\boldsymbol{z}),\,1\leq i,j\leq g.

We first show that
\wp_{ji}
is invariant under the lattice
\Lambda_{\tau}^{g}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
1\leq i,j\leq g
, the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\wp
-function
\wp_{ji}
is
\Lambda_{\tau}^{g}
-invariant, i.e. it holds that

(4.5)

\wp_{ji}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})=\wp_{ji}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}),

for
\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}
.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{g}),\,\boldsymbol{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{g})\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}
.
Using the transformation properties of the higher degree
\sigma
-function (compare (
4.2
)) we see that

\frac{\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}\right)(\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})}{\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})}=\frac{\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}\right)(\boldsymbol{z})}{\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(\boldsymbol{z})}+(-u(\tau)(G_{L}(\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n}))_{j}-2\pi i(G_{L}\boldsymbol{m})_{j}),

where, as earlier (see Section
2.2
),
G_{L}
denotes the Gram matrix of the lattice
L
used to define the theta series.
This implies that

(4.6)

\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}(\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})-\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}(\boldsymbol{z})=-u(\tau)(G_{L}(\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n}))_{j}-2\pi i(G_{L}\boldsymbol{m})_{j}.

The right hand side of the equation above is independent of
\boldsymbol{z}
and depends only on the lattice, thus

\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}(\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})-\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}(\boldsymbol{z})=0.

This implies the desired invariance of
\wp_{ji}
. ∎

The independence of
\boldsymbol{z}
of the difference in (
4.6
) justifies the following definition.

Definition 4.4
.

We define the
quasi-periods
to be

\eta_{jl,1}(\tau)=\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}(\boldsymbol{z}+e_{l})-\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}(\boldsymbol{z})=0,\,\text{if }j\neq l,

(4.7)

\eta_{jj,1}(\tau)=\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}(\boldsymbol{z}+e_{j})-\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}(\boldsymbol{z})=:u(\tau).

Here,
e_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}
denotes the
i
-th unit vector.

Remark 4.5
.

This generalizes the definition of the quasi-periods in the classical setting, where we have

\eta_{1}(\tau)=\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z+1)-\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z).

4.2.
The completion of the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function

In this section we complete the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function such that it is lattice invariant. This generalizes the construction in (
3.2
). Let
\Lambda_{\tau}=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
for
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
. We define

(4.8)

\zeta_{z_{j}}^{*}(\boldsymbol{z})=\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}^{*}(\boldsymbol{z})=\frac{(Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}\theta)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}{\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})},\,\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{g}.

Proposition 4.6
.

The function
\zeta^{*}_{z_{j}}(\boldsymbol{z})
is a (non-holomorphic) Jacobi form of weight
1
and index
0
for the Jacobi subgroup
\mathcal{J}_{L}
(see Section
2.2
). In particular,
\zeta^{*}_{z_{j}}(\boldsymbol{z})
is invariant under the lattice
\Lambda_{\tau}^{g}
.

Proof.

We first note that to show that
\zeta^{*}_{z_{j}}(\boldsymbol{z})
is a Jacobi form of weight
1
, it is enough to show the identity (
2.6
). To check that
\zeta^{*}_{z_{j}}(\boldsymbol{z})
is elliptic, we first compute the partial derivative
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}
of (
2.1
), namely

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\theta\right)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})

\displaystyle=e^{-\pi i\tau(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{m})}e^{-2\pi i(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{m})}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\theta\right)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})

\displaystyle\quad-2\pi i(G_{L}\boldsymbol{m})_{j}e^{-\pi i\tau(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{m})}e^{-2\pi i(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{m})}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}).

Adding
2\pi i\frac{(\Imm(G_{L}(\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})))_{j}}{\Imm(\tau)}e^{-\pi i\tau(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{m})}e^{-2\pi i(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{m})}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})
and using that
\frac{\Imm(G_{L}(\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n}))}{\Imm(\tau)}=G_{L}\boldsymbol{m},

we get

(4.9)

(Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}\theta)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})=e^{-\pi i\tau(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{m})}e^{-2\pi i(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{m})}(Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}\theta)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}).

Dividing by
\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})=e^{-\pi i\tau(\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{m})}e^{-2\pi i(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{m})}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})
, we obtain

\frac{(Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}\theta)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})}{\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}+\boldsymbol{m}\tau+\boldsymbol{n})}=\frac{(Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}\theta)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}{\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}

which finishes the proof that
\zeta^{*}_{z_{j}}(\boldsymbol{z})
is elliptic.

Now we compute the action under the matrix
B=[\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right),\sqrt{c\tau+d}]\in\mathcal{J}_{L}
. It is enough to show identity (
2.6
),which is equivalent to showing

(c\tau+d)^{-g/2-1}e^{-\pi ic\frac{(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{z})}{c\tau+d}}(Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}\theta)\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\frac{\boldsymbol{z}}{c\tau+d}\right)=Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}\theta,

We compute the two components of the action of
Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}+2\pi i\frac{\Imm(G_{L}\boldsymbol{z})_{j}}{\Imm\tau}
. The transformation property (
2.4
) implies that

\theta\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\frac{\boldsymbol{z}}{c\tau+d}\right)=(c\tau+d)^{g/2}e^{\pi ic\frac{(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{z})}{c\tau+d}}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}).

We differentiate and obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\theta\right)\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\frac{\boldsymbol{z}}{c\tau+d}\right)

\displaystyle=(c\tau+d)^{g/2}2\pi ic(G_{L}\boldsymbol{z})_{j}e^{\pi ic\frac{(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{z})}{c\tau+d}}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(c\tau+d)^{g/2+1}e^{\pi ic\frac{(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{z})}{c\tau+d}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\theta\right)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}).

Multiplying by
(c\tau+d)^{-g/2-1}e^{-\pi ic\frac{(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{z})}{c\tau+d}}
yields

(4.10)

2\pi ic(G_{L}\boldsymbol{z})_{j}(c\tau+d)^{-1}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})+\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\theta\right)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}).

For the second part, we have to multiply the term

(c\tau+d)^{-g/2-1}e^{-\pi ic\frac{(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{z})}{c\tau+d}}\theta\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\frac{\boldsymbol{z}}{c\tau+d}\right)=(c\tau+d)^{-1}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})

by
2\pi i(\Imm\frac{(G_{L}\boldsymbol{z})_{j}}{c\tau+d})/(\Imm\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d})=(c\tau+d)\frac{\Imm(G_{L}\boldsymbol{z})_{j}}{\Imm\tau}-c(G_{L}\boldsymbol{z})_{j}
, resulting in

(4.11)

2\pi i\frac{\Imm(G_{L}\boldsymbol{z})_{j}}{\Imm\tau}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})-2\pi ic(G_{L}\boldsymbol{z})_{j}(c\tau+d)^{-1}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}).

Adding (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) we get
Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}\theta
, as desired. This finishes the proof.
∎

We obtain a similar description of the completed Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function as in the degree
1
case.

Lemma 4.7
.

The completed Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function can be written as

\zeta_{z_{j}}^{*}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})=\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}(\boldsymbol{z})-u(\tau)z_{j}+2\pi i\frac{\Imm((G_{L}\boldsymbol{z})_{j})}{\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{\tau})}.

Proof.

Using the definition of the weight raising operator
Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}
we find

\frac{(Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2L}\theta)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}{\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}=\frac{(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\theta)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}{\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}+2\pi i\frac{\Imm((G_{L}\boldsymbol{z})_{j})}{\Imm(\tau_{j})}.

In order to compute the logarithmic derivative of the Jacobi theta-function, we note that

\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(\boldsymbol{z})=c(\tau)e^{-u(\tau)Q(\boldsymbol{z})}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\theta\right)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})-z_{j}u(\tau)c(\tau)e^{-u(\tau)Q(\boldsymbol{z})}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}),

which implies

\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(\boldsymbol{z})}{\sigma_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(\boldsymbol{z})}=e^{-u(\tau)Q(\boldsymbol{z})}\frac{(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\theta)(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}{\sigma(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}-z_{j}u(\tau)e^{-u(\tau)Q(\boldsymbol{z})}\frac{\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}{\sigma(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}.

Thus, using the definition (
4.1
) of the Jacobi-Weierstrass
\sigma
-function, we find

(4.12)

\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}{\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}=\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau},j}(\boldsymbol{z})+z_{j}u(\tau).

∎

4.3.
Vector-valued Jacobi–Weierstrass forms

We now fix
g=k-2
and introduce vector-valued Jacobi–Weierstrass forms. Let
z\in\mathbb{C}^{k-1}
and
M\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Let
(e_{1},e_{2})
denote the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
We recall the notation from Section
2.5
and write
(\boldsymbol{z})_{(e_{1},e_{2})}
for the element in
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
corresponding to
\boldsymbol{z}
.

Definition 4.8
.

Let the notation be as above.
We define the vector-valued
Jacobi–Weierstrass form
by

(\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}(\boldsymbol{z}))_{(X_{1},X_{2})}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}\zeta^{*}_{z_{j}}(\boldsymbol{z})c_{j}X_{1}^{j}X_{2}^{k-2-j}

with
c_{j}=\binom{k-2}{j}
as before, and
(X_{1},X_{2})=M\circ(e_{1},e_{2})
.

Under the isomorphism
\mathbb{C}^{k-1}\simeq\Sym^{k-1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
coming from the standard basis, we can write
\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}
as
\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}((\boldsymbol{z})_{(e_{1},e_{2})})_{(X_{1},X_{2})}
. It defines a function
\widehat{\zeta}:\,\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, where
\widehat{\zeta}((\boldsymbol{z})_{(e_{1},e_{2})},M)=\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}((\boldsymbol{z})_{(e_{1},e_{2})})_{M\circ(e_{1},e_{2})}
. In the definition we suppressed the dependence on the lattice
\Lambda_{\tau}=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
,
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
. Fixing a basis
(X_{1},X_{2})
, we have the following result that is an immediate consequence of Proposition
4.6

Theorem 4.9
.

The function
(\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}})_{(X_{1},X_{2})}
is a (non-holomorphic) Jacobi form of weight
1
and index
0
, invariant under the lattice
\Lambda_{\tau}^{k-1}
.

Remark 4.10
.

Note that there are two sets of coordinates involved in the definition of the Jacobi–Weierstrass form. First we have the element in
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
corresponding to
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{k-2}
in terms of the standard basis. Second, the Jacobi–Weierstrass function itself is an element of
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
in terms of the basis
(X_{1},X_{2})
.

We now consider directional derivatives of the Jacobi–Weierstrass form. Recall that

(\boldsymbol{z})^{\mathrm{t}}_{(e_{1},e_{2})}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}z_{\ell}c_{\ell}e_{1}^{\ell}e_{2}^{k-2-\ell}.

We then define the directional derivative in the direction
N(M)(\boldsymbol{z})^{\mathrm{t}}_{(e_{1},e_{2})}
by

\zeta_{(N(M)\boldsymbol{z})_{i}}(\boldsymbol{z})=\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial(N(M)\boldsymbol{z})_{i}}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}{\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}.

Here,
N(M)
is the matrix defined in Lemma
2.6
and we write
(N(M)\boldsymbol{z})_{i}
for the
i^{th}
term in the vector
N(M)\boldsymbol{z}
. We consider the global Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function

(4.13)

\left(\zeta_{M^{-1}}(\boldsymbol{z})\right)_{(e_{1},e_{2})}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\zeta_{(N(M)\boldsymbol{z})_{\ell}}(\boldsymbol{z})c_{\ell}e_{1}^{\ell}e_{2}^{k-2-\ell},

as well as the global completed Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function

(4.14)

\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{M^{-1}}(\boldsymbol{z})\right)_{(e_{1},e_{2})}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\zeta^{*}_{(N(M)\boldsymbol{z})_{\ell}}(\boldsymbol{z})c_{\ell}e_{1}^{\ell}e_{2}^{k-2-\ell}.

We occasionally use the notation
\zeta_{N(M)\boldsymbol{z}}
and
\widehat{\zeta}_{N(M)\boldsymbol{z}}
when we want to emphasize the indices. To shorten notation we will also write
\widehat{\zeta}_{M^{-1}}(\boldsymbol{z})
for
\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{M^{-1}}(\boldsymbol{z})\right)_{(e_{1},e_{2})}
in some places.

We note that in this notation
\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}(\boldsymbol{z})=\widehat{\zeta}_{Id}(\boldsymbol{z})
and we show that the global directional derivative corresponds to a change of coordinates of
\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}
.

Lemma 4.11
.

For
M\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{k-1}
and
(X_{1},X_{2})
a basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, we have

\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{M}(\boldsymbol{z})\right)_{(X_{1},X_{2})}=\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}(\boldsymbol{z})\right)_{M\circ(X_{1},X_{2})}.

Proof.

We first consider the non-completed version of Definition
4.8
, i.e.

\left(\zeta_{M}(\boldsymbol{z})\right)_{(X_{1},X_{2})}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\zeta_{(N(M^{-1})\boldsymbol{z})_{\ell}}(\boldsymbol{z})c_{\ell}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}.

By definition this equals (in vector notation)

\frac{1}{\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial(N(M^{-1})\boldsymbol{z})_{0}}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}),\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial(N(M^{-1})\boldsymbol{z})_{k-2}}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})\right)^{\mathrm{t}}_{(X_{1},X_{2})}.

A short calculation using multivariable calculus yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial(N(M^{-1})\boldsymbol{z})_{0}}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}),\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial(N(M^{-1})\boldsymbol{z})_{k-2}}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})\right)^{\mathrm{t}}_{(X_{1},X_{2})}

\displaystyle\quad=N(M)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z}),\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k-2}}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})\right)^{\mathrm{t}}_{(X_{1},X_{2})}.

Therefore, we have

\left(\zeta_{M}(\boldsymbol{z})\right)_{(X_{1},X_{2})}=N(M)\left(\zeta_{\boldsymbol{z}}(\boldsymbol{z})\right)_{(X_{1},X_{2})}.

Plugging this into the completion of the Jacobi–Weierstrass function yields the desired result.
∎

5.
Vector-valued Jacobi–Weierstrass as polar harmonic weak Maass forms

In this section we define the higher weight analogue of Weierstrass harmonic weak Maass forms. Throughout, we let
f
be a newform of weight
k
for
\Gamma
with rational Fourier coefficients, with
\Gamma_{1}(N)\subset\Gamma
for some integer
N
. We will use the Eichler integral
\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(X_{1},X_{2}))
and the lattice
\Lambda_{f}
associated to
f
from Section
2.4
.

By
V
we denote the infinite-dimensional vector space

V=\mathrm{Functions}\{\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\}.

The action of
\Gamma
on
V
is given by

(5.1)

\rho(\gamma)F(M)=\gamma\circ F(\gamma^{-1}M),

where
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Here
\Gamma
acts on
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
by matrix multiplication and on
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
by acting on the basis as described in Section
2.5
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and
M\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
We define the
vector-valued Jacobi–Weierstrass form
by

(5.2)

F(\tau):=\left[M\mapsto\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{M}(\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,M^{-1}\circ(e_{1},e_{2})))_{(e_{1},e_{2})}\right)\right].

We write

F(\tau,M)=\widehat{\zeta}_{M}(\Lambda_{f},\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,M^{-1}\circ(e_{1},e_{2})))_{(e_{1},e_{2})},

for the image of
F
in
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and drop the dependence on
\Lambda_{f}
in the definition of
\widehat{\zeta}
.

Remark 5.2
.

The function
F
depends on the chosen basis of
\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
in two places. We note two important changes of coordinates. By Lemma
4.11
we see

(5.3)

F(\tau,M)=\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,M^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right)\right)_{M\circ(e_{1},e_{2})}.

This can also be taken as the definition of the function
F(\tau,M)
.

Moreover, we have a second change of coordinates inside the function given by changing the basis for the Eichler integral. As by definition we have
\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,M^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)=N(M^{-1})\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(e_{1},e_{2}))
it follows that

(5.4)

F(\tau,M)=\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{M}\left(N(M^{-1})\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right)\right)_{(e_{1},e_{2})}.

We now prove that
F
defines a vector-valued polar harmonic weak Maass form of weight
0
that maps to
f
with values in a coefficient system under
\xi_{0}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

The function
F(\tau)
is a vector-valued polar harmonic weak Maass form of weight
0
for
\Gamma
with respect to the representation
\rho
and its image under
\xi_{0}
is given by

G(\tau)=\frac{2\pi i}{\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{f})}f(\tau)\ G_{L}\cdot(\tau e_{1}+e_{2})^{k-2}.

Here
G_{L}
is the Gram matrix corresponding to the lattice
L
which is implicitly used in the construction of the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function
\widehat{\zeta}
. For
\boldsymbol{z}=(z_{0},\dots,z_{k-2})^{t}
written in the basis
(X_{1},X_{2})
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, the action of
G_{L}=(g_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq k-2}
is given by

(5.5)

G_{L}\cdot(\boldsymbol{z})_{(X_{1},X_{2})}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}g_{\ell i}z_{i}\right)c_{\ell}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}z_{i}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}g_{\ell i}c_{\ell}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}\right).

We note that for the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{k-1}
with the standard inner product, the theorem simplifies to

G(\tau)=\frac{2\pi i}{\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{f})}f(\tau)(\tau e_{1}+e_{2})^{k-2}.

Remark 5.4
.

(1)

The function
G:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
is a holomorphic vector-valued modular form of weight
2
.

(2)

For the special case of weight
k=2
it is possible to construct a modular function
M
with algebraic Fourier coefficients which eliminates all poles of
F
on the upper half-plane (see
[
AGOR15
]
resp. Theorem
3.1
). Then,
F+M
is a harmonic weak Maass forms whose shadow is the newform
f
. The proof given in loc. cit. uses the proof for the well-known fact that
j(\tau)
and
j(N\tau)
generate the field of modular functions for the group
\Gamma_{0}(N)
(see e g.
[
Cox13
, Theorem 11.9]
). To the authors’ knowledge, there is, however, no systematic theory of modular functions with respect to the (infinite-dimensional) representation
\rho
from (
5.1
) which would allow to prove an analogous result in general.

We prove Theorem
5.3
in several steps. We first show the
\Gamma
-invariance of each one of the terms
\widehat{\zeta}_{M}\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,M^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right)
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let the notation be as above. For
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have

\widehat{\zeta}_{M}\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,(\gamma^{-1}M)^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right)=\widehat{\zeta}_{M}\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\gamma\tau,M^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right).

Proof.

We write
(X_{1},X_{2})=M^{-1}\circ(e_{1},e_{2})
and note that

(\gamma^{-1}M)^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)=\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)M^{-1}\gamma=\left(X_{1},X_{2}\right)\gamma.

By definition of the change of basis matrix
N(\cdot)
, for the Eichler integral we have

\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,\gamma\circ\left(X_{1},X_{2}\right)\right)=N(\gamma)\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(X_{1},X_{2}))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(X_{1},X_{2}))=N(M^{-1})\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(e_{1},e_{2})).

Moreover, from Lemma
2.4
we have

(5.6)

\mathcal{E}_{f}(\gamma\tau,(X_{1},X_{2}))=\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,\gamma\circ(X_{1},X_{2}))-\omega,

where
\omega\in\Lambda_{f}^{k-1}
with
\Lambda_{f}^{k-1}
written in the basis
\gamma\circ(X_{1},X_{2})=(e_{1},e_{2})M^{-1}\gamma
, thus it is an element of
N(M^{-1}\gamma)\Lambda_{f}^{k-1}
.
By Lemma
2.8
we know that
N(M^{-1}\gamma)\Lambda_{f}^{k-1}=\Lambda_{f}^{k-1}
. Therefore we obtain

\mathcal{E}_{f}(\gamma\tau,M^{-1}\circ(e_{1},e_{2}))-\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(\gamma^{-1}M)^{-1}\circ(e_{1},e_{2}))\in\Lambda_{f}^{k-1}.

Since
\widehat{\zeta}_{M}
is
\Lambda_{f}^{k-1}
-invariant from Proposition
4.6
, this gives the result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

We first prove that
F
is invariant under the action of
\Gamma
. The harmonicity (away from possible poles) will follow from the fact that
\xi_{0}(F)
is a holomorphic cusp form (with values in a coefficient system), since the Laplacian equals
\Delta_{k}=-\xi_{2-k}\circ\xi_{k}
.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
. For
M\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
, we need to show that

F(\gamma\tau,M)=\rho(\gamma)F(\tau,M),

where the action
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\End(V)
was defined in (
5.1
), thus

\rho(\gamma)F(\tau,M)=\gamma\circ\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{(\gamma^{-1}M)}\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,\left((\gamma^{-1}M)^{-1}\right)\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right)\right)_{(e_{1},e_{2})}.

By Lemma
4.11
this equals

\gamma\circ\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,\left(\gamma^{-1}M\right)^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right))\right)_{(e_{1},e_{2})\gamma^{-1}M}

and this expression simplifies to

\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,\left(\gamma^{-1}M\right)^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right))\right)_{(e_{1},e_{2})\gamma\gamma^{-1}M}=\widehat{\zeta}_{M}\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,\left(\gamma^{-1}M\right)^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right))\right)_{(e_{1},e_{2})}.

Finally, we use Lemma
5.5
and obtain that

\rho(\gamma)F(\tau,M)=\widehat{\zeta}_{M}\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\gamma\tau,M^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right)_{(e_{1},e_{2})}=F(\gamma\tau,M).

Now we will compute the image of
F(\tau,M)
under
\xi_{0}=-2i\overline{\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\tau}}}
. This gives a function

g(\tau,M)=\xi_{0}F(\tau,M),

with
g:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow V
. We will compute
g
explicitly and show that it is indeed constant on
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. We write

(5.7)

F(\tau,M)=\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,M^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right)_{(Y_{1},Y_{2})},

with
(Y_{1},Y_{2})=M\circ(e_{1},e_{2})
and consider each of the coordinates
\zeta_{z_{\ell}}^{*}
in the basis
(Y_{1},Y_{2})
. By Lemma
4.7
we obtain

\zeta_{z_{\ell}}^{*}(\boldsymbol{z})=\zeta_{z_{\ell}}(\boldsymbol{z})-u(\tau_{f})z_{\ell}+2\pi i\frac{\Imm((G_{L}\boldsymbol{z})_{\ell})}{\Imm(\tau_{f})}.

Writing
\Imm(z)=(z-\overline{z})/2i
and plugging in
\boldsymbol{z}=\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,M^{-1}\circ(e_{1},e_{2})))
we see that

\overline{\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\tau}}\zeta_{z_{\ell}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,M^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right)}=-\frac{\pi}{\Imm(\tau_{f})}\overline{\left(G_{L}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\tau}}\overline{\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,M^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)}\right)}_{\ell}.

As
G_{L}
accounts for changing the basis from (
5.5
)

G_{L}\cdot(\boldsymbol{z})_{(Y_{1},Y_{2})}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}z_{i}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}g_{\ell i}c_{\ell}Y_{1}^{\ell}Y_{2}^{k-2-\ell}\right),

it is enough to compute each component
\overline{\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\tau}}\left(\overline{\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,M^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)}\right)}_{\ell}
, where

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,M^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right)_{\ell}=\int_{\tau}^{\infty}f(t)(dt-b)^{\ell}(-ct+a)^{k-2-\ell}dt,

with
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. This gives us

\overline{\frac{\partial}{\overline{\partial\tau}}\overline{\left(\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,M^{-1}\circ\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right)_{\ell}}}=f(\tau)(d\tau-b)^{\ell}(-c\tau+a)^{k-2-\ell}

and with the action of
G_{L}
we get

\displaystyle\xi_{0}F(\tau,M)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi i}{\Imm(\tau_{f})}G_{L}\cdot\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}f(\tau)(d\tau-b)^{\ell}(-c\tau+a)^{k-2-\ell}c_{\ell}Y_{1}^{\ell}Y_{2}^{k-2-\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi i}{\Imm(\tau_{f})}f(\tau)\ G_{L}\cdot\left((dY_{1}-cY_{2})\tau+(-bY_{1}+aY_{2})^{k-2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi i}{\Imm(\tau_{f})}f(\tau)\ G_{L}\cdot\left(M^{-1}\circ(Y_{1}\tau+Y_{2})^{k-2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi i}{\Imm(\tau_{f})}f(\tau)\ \left(G_{L}\cdot(e_{1}\tau+e_{2})^{k-2}\right).

The last expression is independent of
M
. The holomorphicity is obvious and the modularity follows from (
2.7
). Moreover, we see that
F(\tau)
is harmonic.

It remains to show that
F
grows at most linear exponentially when
\tau
approaches a cusp of
\Gamma
. For this let
\sigma\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
be arbitrary. The same computation as in the proof of Lemma
2.2
shows that

\mathcal{E}_{f}(\sigma\tau,M^{-1}\circ(e_{1},e_{2}))=\mathcal{E}_{f|\sigma}(\tau,M^{-1}\sigma\circ(e_{1},e_{2}))+C

for a certain constant vector
C
. Since
f
is a cusp form and
f|\sigma
yields the Fourier expansion of
f
at the cusp
\sigma\infty
by definition, it vanishes exponentially as
\tau\to\infty
. Since
\theta
is holomorphic in all elliptic variables, it follows that the partial logarithmic derivatives of
\theta
all have at most a finite order pole at the divisor of
\theta
. But this implies that, as
\tau\to\infty
, the analytic part of
F(\sigma.\tau,M)
grows at most linear exponentially if
C
lies in the divisor of
\theta
and is bounded otherwise. This shows the claim, as the non-analytic part is clearly bounded.
∎

6.
Laurent expansion and poles

In the definition of the Jacobi theta function we allowed an arbitrary positive definite integral bilinear form on
\mathbb{Z}^{g}
. Especially when computing examples (see Section
7
), it is most convenient to take the standard bilinear form, but allow for a non-trivial characteristic. In what follows we define

(6.1)

\displaystyle\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})=\sum_{n\in\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}\right)}e^{\pi i\left(\sum_{i}n_{i}^{2}\right)\tau+2\pi i\sum_{i}n_{i}z_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{g}\vartheta(\tau,z_{i}).

In this setting we immediately find from (
4.8
) that

\zeta_{z_{j}}^{*}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})=\frac{Y_{+,z_{j}}^{g/2,1/2}\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}{\theta(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})}=\frac{Y_{+,z_{j}}^{1/2,1/2}\vartheta(\tau,z_{j})}{\vartheta(\tau,z_{j})}=\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}^{*}(z_{j}),

where
\zeta^{*}
here denotes the completion of the usual
g=1
Weierstrass
\zeta
-function.

Similarly, the directional derivatives occurring in the definition of the function
F
in (
5.2
) can be expressed explicitly as

\widehat{\zeta}_{(N(M^{-1})\boldsymbol{z})_{j}}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})=\sum_{i}N(M)_{ij}\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}^{*}(z_{i}),

which follows directly from the definition of the directional derivative, where
N(M)=\{N(M)_{ij}\}_{0\leq i,j\leq k-2}
.

We recall the Laurent expansion of the completed Weierstrass
\zeta
-function

\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}^{*}(z)=\frac{1}{z}-\sum_{n\geq 1}G_{2n+2}(\Lambda_{\tau})z^{2n+1}-S(\Lambda_{\tau})z-\frac{\pi}{\Imm(\tau)}\overline{z},

where
G_{2n}(\Lambda)=\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\omega^{-2n}
is the classical Eisenstein series of weight
2n
and
S(\Lambda_{\tau})=\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda_{\tau}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{\omega^{2}|\omega|^{2}}
.

For the Laurent expansion of the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function

\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})=\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\zeta^{*}_{\Lambda_{\tau}}(z_{\ell})e_{1}^{\ell}e_{2}^{k-2-\ell}c_{\ell}

we then obtain the following result

Lemma 6.1
.

With the standard choice of lattice, the Laurent expansion of the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function
\zeta_{\boldsymbol{z}}
is given by

\zeta_{\boldsymbol{z}}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\frac{1}{z_{\ell}}e_{1}^{\ell}e_{2}^{k-2-\ell}c_{\ell}+\sum_{n\geq 1}G_{2n+2}(\Lambda_{\tau})\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}z_{\ell}^{2n+1}e_{1}^{\ell}e_{2}^{k-2-\ell}c_{\ell}.

Moreover, the Laurent expansion of its completion is given by

\widehat{\zeta}_{\boldsymbol{z}}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})=\zeta_{\boldsymbol{z}}(\tau,\boldsymbol{z})-S(\Lambda_{\tau})\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}z_{\ell}e_{1}^{\ell}e_{2}^{k-2-\ell}c_{\ell}-\frac{\pi}{\Imm(\tau)}\overline{z_{\ell}}e_{1}^{\ell}e_{2}^{k-2-\ell}c_{\ell}.

We note that when plugging in
\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(e_{1},e_{2}))
, we get the poles of the vector-valued function
F(\tau,Id):\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\Sym^{k-2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, for
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
such that

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\tau,(e_{1},e_{2}))\in\Lambda_{f}.

The theory easily extends to general
M\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
for

\zeta_{M^{-1}}(\Lambda_{\tau},N(M^{-1})\boldsymbol{z})=\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\zeta_{\Lambda_{\tau}}((N(M^{-1})\boldsymbol{z})_{\ell})X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}c_{\ell},

where
(X_{1},X_{2})=M\circ(e_{1},e_{2})
, and its completed version.

Lemma 6.2
.

The Laurent expansion of the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function
\zeta_{M^{-1}}(\Lambda,(\boldsymbol{z})_{M^{-1}})
is given by

\zeta_{M^{-1}}(\Lambda,(\boldsymbol{z})_{M^{-1}})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\frac{1}{(\boldsymbol{z})_{M^{-1},\ell}}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}c_{\ell}+\sum_{k\geq 0}G_{k-2}(\Lambda)\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}(\boldsymbol{z})_{M^{-1},\ell}^{k-1}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}c_{\ell},

where
(X_{1},X_{2})=M\circ(e_{1},e_{2})
, and
(\boldsymbol{z})_{M^{-1},\ell}=(N(M^{-1})(\boldsymbol{z}))_{l}
is the
\ell^{th}
component of the vector
N(M^{-1})(\boldsymbol{z})
. We get a similar expansion for the completion

\widehat{\zeta}_{M^{-1}}(\Lambda,(\boldsymbol{z})_{M^{-1}})=\zeta_{M^{-1}}(\Lambda,(\boldsymbol{z})_{M^{-1}})+S(\Lambda_{\tau})\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}z_{\ell}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}c_{\ell}-\frac{\pi}{\Imm(\tau)}\overline{z_{\ell}}X_{1}^{\ell}X_{2}^{k-2-\ell}c_{\ell}.

We recall that
F(\tau,M)=\widehat{\zeta}_{M^{-1}}\left(\Lambda_{f},N(M^{-1})\mathcal{E}_{f}\left(\tau,\left(e_{1},e_{2}\right)\right)\right)
. Therefore, we can compute the poles of the Jacobi–Weierstrass
\zeta
-function for the standard choice of symmetric form on
\mathbb{Z}^{k-1}
explicitly.

Proposition 6.3
.

Under the conditions above, the polar harmonic weak Maass form
F:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow V
has poles of order
1
at the values
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
for which there exists
M\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
0\leq\ell\leq k-1
such that

(N(M^{-1})\mathcal{E}_{f}(\tau,(e_{1},e_{2})))_{\ell}\in\Lambda_{f}.

7.1.
A Weierstrass form for
\Delta

Consider the Ramanujan
\Delta

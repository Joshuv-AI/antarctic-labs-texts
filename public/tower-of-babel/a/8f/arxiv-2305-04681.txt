Phenomenology of a minimal extension of the standard model with a family-dependent gauge symmetry
Duong Van Loi, Cao H. Nam, Phung Van Dong

Abstract
We consider a gauge symmetry extension of the standard model given by S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) C ⊗ S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L ⊗ U ​ ( 1 ) X ⊗ U ​ ( 1 ) N ⊗ ℤ 2 SU(3)_{C}\otimes SU(2)_{L}\otimes U(1)_{X}\otimes U(1)_{N}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{2} with minimal particle content, where X X and N N are family dependent but determining the hypercharge as Y = X + N Y=X+N , while ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} is an exact discrete symmetry. In our scenario, X X (while N N is followed by X − Y X-Y ) and ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} charge assignments are inspired by the number of fermion families and the stability of dark matter, as observed, respectively. We examine the mass spectra of fermions, scalars, and gauge bosons, as well as their interactions, in presence of a kinetic mixing term between U ​ ( 1 ) X , N U(1)_{X,N} gauge fields. We discuss in detail the phenomenology of the new gauge boson and the right-handed neutrino dark matter stabilized by ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} conservation. We obtain parameter spaces simultaneously satisfying the recent CDF W W -boson mass, electroweak precision measurements, particle colliders, as well as dark matter observables, if the kinetic mixing parameter is not necessarily small.

a/b
.

IV
Boson sector

In what follows, we diagonalize the scalar and gauge boson sectors in order to obtain relevant physical mass spectra.

IV.1
Scalar sector

Firstly, we consider the scalar potential in Eq. (
12
). The necessary conditions for this potential to be bounded from below as well as to yield a desirable vacuum structure are

\lambda_{1,2}>0,\hskip 14.22636pt\lambda_{3}>-2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}},\hskip 14.22636pt\mu_{1,2}^{2}<0,\hskip 14.22636pt|\mu_{1}|\ll|\mu_{2}|.

(28)

To obtain the potential minimum and physical scalar spectrum, we expand the scalar fields around their VEVs as follows:

\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi^{+}_{1}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(v+S_{1}+iA_{1})\end{pmatrix},\hskip 14.22636pt\chi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\Lambda+S_{2}+iA_{2}).

(29)

Substituting these fields into the scalar potential in Eq. (
12
), we derive conditions for the potential minimum, such as

\Lambda^{2}=\frac{2(\lambda_{3}\mu_{1}^{2}-2\lambda_{1}\mu_{2}^{2})}{4\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}^{2}},\hskip 14.22636ptv^{2}=\frac{2(\lambda_{3}\mu_{2}^{2}-2\lambda_{2}\mu_{1}^{2})}{4\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}^{2}}.

(30)

Using the potential minimum conditions, we obtain physical scalar fields,

\phi=\begin{pmatrix}G^{+}_{W}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(v+c_{\xi}H+s_{\xi}H^{\prime}+iG_{Z_{1}})\end{pmatrix},\hskip 14.22636pt\chi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\Lambda-s_{\xi}H+c_{\xi}H^{\prime}+iG_{Z_{2}}),

(31)

where
G_{W}\equiv\phi_{1}
,
G_{Z_{1}}\equiv A_{1}
, and
G_{Z_{2}}\equiv A_{2}
are the massless Goldstone bosons eaten by
W
,
Z_{1}
, and
Z_{2}
gauge bosons (see below), respectively. The field
H\equiv c_{\xi}S_{1}-s_{\xi}S_{2}
is identical to the SM Higgs boson, while
H^{\prime}\equiv s_{\xi}S_{1}+c_{\xi}S_{2}
is a new Higgs boson associate to
U(1)_{X}\otimes U(1)_{N}
symmetry breaking down to
U(1)_{Y}
. Their masses are given by

\displaystyle m^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}v^{2}+\lambda_{2}\Lambda^{2}-\sqrt{(\lambda_{1}v^{2}-\lambda_{2}\Lambda^{2})^{2}+\lambda_{3}^{2}v^{2}\Lambda^{2}}\simeq\left(2\lambda_{1}-\frac{\lambda_{3}^{2}}{2\lambda_{2}}\right)v^{2},

(32)

\displaystyle m^{2}_{H^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}v^{2}+\lambda_{2}\Lambda^{2}+\sqrt{(\lambda_{1}v^{2}-\lambda_{2}\Lambda^{2})^{2}+\lambda_{3}^{2}v^{2}\Lambda^{2}}\simeq 2\lambda_{2}\Lambda^{2},

(33)

which are obviously that
m_{H}
is at the weak scale, while
m_{H^{\prime}}
is at the
\Lambda
scale. The Higgs and new Higgs, i.e.
S_{1}
-
S_{2}
, mixing angle,
\xi
, is defined by

\tan(2\xi)\equiv t_{2\xi}=\frac{\lambda_{3}v\Lambda}{\lambda_{2}\Lambda^{2}-\lambda_{1}v^{2}}\simeq\frac{\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{2}}\frac{v}{\Lambda},

(34)

which is small due to
v\ll\Lambda
.

IV.2
Gauge sector

Concerning gauge bosons, they acquire masses via the scalar kinetic term,
\sum_{S}(D^{\mu}S)^{\dagger}(D_{\mu}S)
, when the symmetry breaking occurs. For charged gauge bosons, we obtain

W^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(A_{1}\mp iA_{2}),

(35)

to be a physical field by itself, with mass

m^{2}_{W}=\frac{1}{4}g^{2}v^{2},

(36)

which is identical to that of the SM, given that
v=246
GeV.

For neutral gauge bosons
(A_{3},B,C)
, the fields
B
and
C
are generically not orthogonal and normalized due to the kinetic mixing term. Let us change to the canonical basis
(A_{3},\hat{B},\hat{C})
via a nonunitary transformation,
(A_{3},\hat{B},\hat{C})^{T}=U_{\delta}(A_{3},B,C)^{T}
, where

U_{\delta}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&\delta\\
0&0&\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

(37)

The mass matrix of neutral gauge bosons in the canonical basis,
(A_{3},\hat{B},\hat{C})
, is given by

M_{0}^{2}=\frac{g^{2}}{4}\begin{pmatrix}v^{2}&0&-\frac{t_{N}v^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}}\\
0&16t_{X}^{2}x^{2}\Lambda^{2}&-\frac{16t_{X}t_{N}x^{2}(1+\delta t)\Lambda^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}}\\
-\frac{t_{N}v^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}}&-\frac{16t_{X}t_{N}x^{2}(1+\delta t)\Lambda^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}}&\frac{t_{N}^{2}[v^{2}+16x^{2}(1+\delta t)^{2}\Lambda^{2}]}{1-\delta^{2}}\end{pmatrix},

(38)

where
t_{X}=g_{X}/g
,
t_{N}=g_{N}/g
, and
t=g_{X}/g_{N}
. This mass matrix has a zero eigenvalue (i.e., the photon mass) with a corresponding eigenstate (i.e., the photon field) to be

A=s_{W}A_{3}+c_{W}(s_{\theta}\hat{B}+c_{\theta}\hat{C}),

(39)

where the Weinberg’s angle and the mixing angle
\theta
are defined via
\tan
functions,

\tan(\theta_{W})\equiv t_{W}=\frac{t_{X}}{\sqrt{1+2\delta t+t^{2}}},\hskip 14.22636pt\tan(\theta)\equiv t_{\theta}=\frac{1+\delta t}{t\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}}.

(40)

We define the SM
Z
boson and the new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
orthogonal to
A
, such as

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{W}A_{3}-s_{W}(s_{\theta}\hat{B}+c_{\theta}\hat{C}),

(41)

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\theta}\hat{B}-s_{\theta}\hat{C}.

(42)

In the new basis
(A,Z,Z^{\prime})
, the mass matrix
M^{2}_{0}
is changed to

M^{2}=U^{T}_{W}M_{0}^{2}U_{W}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&m^{2}_{Z}&m^{2}_{ZZ^{\prime}}\\
0&m^{2}_{ZZ^{\prime}}&m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix},

(43)

where

U_{W}=\begin{pmatrix}s_{W}&c_{W}&0\\
c_{W}s_{\theta}&-s_{W}s_{\theta}&c_{\theta}\\
c_{W}c_{\theta}&-s_{W}c_{\theta}&-s_{\theta}\end{pmatrix},

(44)

and

\displaystyle m^{2}_{Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{2}}{4c^{2}_{W}}v^{2},

(45)

\displaystyle m^{2}_{ZZ^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{2}}{4c_{W}}t_{W}t_{\theta}v^{2},

(46)

\displaystyle m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{2}}{4}t^{2}_{W}\left[t^{2}_{\theta}v^{2}+\frac{16t^{2}x^{2}(1-\delta^{2})\Lambda^{2}}{c^{4}_{\theta}}\right].

(47)

At this stage, the photon field
A
is decoupled as a physical field, while the two states
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
still mix by themselves via the
2\times 2
mass matrix, as given.

Finally, diagonalizing this
2\times 2
mass matrix, we obtain two physical states,

Z_{1}=c_{\varphi}Z-s_{\varphi}Z^{\prime},\hskip 14.22636ptZ_{2}=s_{\varphi}Z+c_{\varphi}Z^{\prime},

(48)

with corresponding masses,

\displaystyle m^{2}_{Z_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[m^{2}_{Z}+m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}-\sqrt{(m^{2}_{Z}-m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}})^{2}+4m^{4}_{ZZ^{\prime}}}\right]\simeq m^{2}_{Z}-\frac{m^{4}_{ZZ^{\prime}}}{m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}},

(49)

\displaystyle m^{2}_{Z_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[m^{2}_{Z}+m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}+\sqrt{(m^{2}_{Z}-m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}})^{2}+4m^{4}_{ZZ^{\prime}}}\right]\simeq m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}.

(50)

The
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing angle is given by

t_{2\varphi}=\frac{s_{W}s_{2\theta}v^{2}}{(s_{W}^{2}s^{2}_{\theta}-c^{2}_{\theta})v^{2}+16c_{W}^{2}t_{X}^{2}x^{2}\Lambda^{2}}\simeq\frac{t\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}(1+\delta t)}{8s_{W}x^{2}(1+2\delta t+t^{2})^{2}}\frac{v^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}.

(51)

The above approximations apply due to the fact that
v\ll\Lambda
. Notice that the mixing effect between
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
comes from two sources, the gauge symmetry breaking by
v,\Lambda
and the kinetic mixing term by
\delta
. Particularly, this mixing effect completely disappears when
\delta=-1/t=-g_{N}/g_{X}
; or in other words, two contributions to the mixing are canceled out.

Let us stress that the field
Z_{1}
has a mass approximating that of the SM, called the SM
Z
-like boson, whereas the field
Z_{2}
is a new gauge boson with a large mass at
\Lambda
scale. In addition, the
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing angle is small due to
v\ll\Lambda
. The observable effects in experiment concern only a shift in the
Z
mass and the
Z
couplings with SM particles, which are all suppressed by
(v/\Lambda)^{2}
. Because of this suppression, the electroweak precision measurements on the
Z
mass and the
Z
couplings may be satisfied for a finite
\delta
and
t=g_{X}/g_{N}
.

Last, but not least, let us summarize the relation between the original gauge states and the physical eigenstates as
(A_{3},B,C)^{T}=U_{\delta}^{-1}U_{W}U_{\varphi}(A,Z_{1},Z_{2})^{T}
, where

U_{\varphi}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&c_{\varphi}&s_{\varphi}\\
0&-s_{\varphi}&c_{\varphi}\end{pmatrix}.

(52)

VI
Experimental constraints

In the model under consideration, the SM
Z
boson mixes with the new neutral gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
through the kinetic mixing and the symmetry
breaking, which gives rise to interesting phenomena. Notice that in the original framework with two Higgs doublets, some bounds were given, but suppressing the kinetic mixing effect
VanDong:2022cin
. In this section, we will deliver a full analysis to various new physics processes and relevant constraints.

VI.1
W
-boson mass deviation

Recently, the CDF collaboration has reported a new result of
W
-boson mass,
m_{W}|_{\mathrm{CDF}}=80.5335\pm 0.0094
GeV
CDF:2022hxs
, which is
7\sigma
above the SM prediction of
m_{W}|_{\mathrm{SM}}=80.357\pm 0.006
GeV
ParticleDataGroup:2020ssz
. This may be a significant indication of new physics beyond the SM.

In present model, because of the
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing, the observed
Z_{1}
-boson mass is reduced in comparison with the SM
Z
-boson mass, while the mass of
W
boson is retained. This gives rise to a positive contribution to the Peskin-Takeuchi
T
-parameter at tree-level,

\alpha T=\rho-1=\frac{m^{2}_{W}}{c^{2}_{W}m^{2}_{Z_{1}}}-1=\frac{m^{2}_{Z}}{m^{2}_{Z_{1}}}-1\simeq\frac{m^{4}_{ZZ^{\prime}}}{m^{2}_{Z}m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}}\simeq\frac{(1+\delta t)^{2}}{16x^{2}(1+2\delta t+t^{2})^{2}}\frac{v^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}.

(63)

Since the mass of
Z_{1}
boson is already fixed, the positive value of
\alpha T
dominantly enhances the mass of
W
boson (cf.
Peskin:1991sw
;
Strumia:2022qkt
), such as

\Delta m^{2}_{W}=\frac{c^{4}_{W}m^{2}_{Z}}{c^{2}_{W}-s^{2}_{W}}\alpha T\simeq\frac{g^{2}c_{W}^{2}(1+\delta t)^{2}}{64c_{2W}x^{2}(1+2\delta t+t^{2})^{2}}\frac{v^{4}}{\Lambda^{2}}.

(64)

Taking the central values of
W
mass from the CDF experiment and the SM prediction, as well as using
v=246
GeV,
s^{2}_{W}=0.231
and
\alpha=1/128
, we obtain

\Lambda\simeq 1.68\times\left|\frac{1+\delta t}{x(1+2\delta t+t^{2})}\right|\text{ TeV}.

(65)

VI.2
Total
Z_{1}

The

-

with SM fermions, thus causing a shift of the total

(66)

where

and

are the vector and axial-vector couplings predicted by the SM, while
\Delta_{\nu_{L}}
,
\Delta^{V}_{f}
, and
\Delta^{A}_{f}
are the coupling deviations predicted by the present model, evaluated by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\nu_{L}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{(1+\delta t)[1+\delta t+2x(1+2\delta t+t^{2})]}{16x^{2}(1+2\delta+t)^{2}t^{2}}\frac{v^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}},

(67)

\displaystyle\Delta^{V}_{f}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{(1+\delta t)\{[T_{3}(f)-2Q(f)](1+\delta t)+2X(f)(1+2\delta t+t^{2})\}}{16[T_{3}(f)-2s^{2}_{W}Q(f)]x^{2}(1+2\delta+t)^{2}t^{2}}\frac{v^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}},

(68)

\displaystyle\Delta^{A}_{f}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{(1+\delta t)^{2}}{16x^{2}(1+2\delta+t)^{2}t^{2}}\frac{v^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}.

(69)

\displaystyle\frac{m_{Z}}{6\pi}\left(\frac{g}{2c_{W}}\right)^{2}\left\{\sum_{f}N_{C}(f)\left[\left(g_{V}^{Z}(f)\right)^{2}\Delta^{V}_{f}+\left(g_{A}^{Z}(f)\right)^{2}\Delta^{A}_{f}\right]+\frac{3\Delta_{\nu_{L}}}{2}\right\}

(70)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta m_{Z}}{12\pi}\left(\frac{g}{2c_{W}}\right)^{2}\left\{\sum_{f}N_{C}(f)\left[\left(g_{V}^{Z}(f)\right)^{2}+\left(g_{A}^{Z}(f)\right)^{2}\right]+\frac{3}{2}\right\},

Comparing the experimental measurement of the total

VI.4
LEPII

Because the
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing is strongly suppressed by
v^{2}/\Lambda^{2}
, we can omit this mixing effect for the processes above the
Z
pole, hereafter. Namely, taking
\varphi\to 0
, thus
Z_{2}\to Z^{\prime}
, results in
Z^{\prime}
to be a physical field.
With the mass at TeV scale,
Z^{\prime}
is certainly not produced in electron-positron collisions at LEPII experiment
ALEPH:2006bhb
. However, it can indirectly modify observables, which deviate from the SM predictions.

One of the important processes at LEPII is the pair production of leptons
e^{+}e^{-}\to\bar{f}f
with
f=e,\mu,\tau

ALEPH:2013dgf
. Such processes can be induced by the
Z^{\prime}
exchange, which is described by the effective Lagrangian as follows

\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}}=\frac{1}{1+\delta_{ef}}\frac{g^{2}}{c^{2}_{W}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\sum_{i,j=L,R}C_{i}^{Z^{\prime}}(e)C_{j}^{Z^{\prime}}(f)\bar{e}_{i}\gamma_{\mu}e_{i}\bar{f}_{j}\gamma^{\mu}f_{j},

(74)

where
\delta_{ef}=1(0)
for
f=e(f\neq e)
and
C_{L,R}^{Z^{\prime}}(f)=\frac{1}{2}[g_{V}^{Z_{2}}(f)\pm g_{A}^{Z_{2}}(f)]
. Considering the case of dimuon in final state, we have

\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}}\supset\frac{g^{2}[C_{L}^{Z^{\prime}}(e)]^{2}}{c^{2}_{W}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\bar{e}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}e_{L}\bar{\mu}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}\mu_{L}+(LR)+(RL)+(RR),

(75)

where the last three terms differ from the first one only in chiral structures.

Taking a typical bound for the models like ours
Carena:2004xs
,

\frac{g^{2}[C_{L}^{Z^{\prime}}(e)]^{2}}{c^{2}_{W}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}<\frac{1}{(6\text{ TeV})^{2}},

(76)

we obtain

\Lambda\gtrsim 3\times\left|\frac{0.5(1+\delta t)+x(1+2\delta t+t^{2})}{x(1+2\delta t+t^{2})}\right|\text{ TeV}.

(77)

LHC

At the LHC, the new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
can be resonantly produced via quark fusion
\bar{q}q\to Z^{\prime}

(78)

\displaystyle L_{q\bar{q}}(m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}})=\int^{1}_{\frac{m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}}{s}}\frac{dx}{xs}\left[f_{q}(x,m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}})f_{\bar{q}}\left(\frac{m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}}{xs},m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}\right)+f_{q}\left(\frac{m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}}{xs},m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}\right)f_{\bar{q}}(x,m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}})\right],

(79)

q

(80)

while the total

\displaystyle\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{g^{2}m_{Z^{\prime}}}{48\pi c^{2}_{W}}\sum_{f}N_{C}(f)\left\{[g^{Z_{2}}_{V}(f)]^{2}+[g^{Z_{2}}_{A}(f)]^{2}\right\}

(81)

\displaystyle+\frac{g^{2}m_{Z^{\prime}}}{32\pi c^{2}_{W}}(C^{Z_{2}}_{\nu_{L}})^{2}+\frac{m_{Z^{\prime}}}{192\pi m^{2}_{Z}}(C_{Z^{\prime}ZH})^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1,2,3}\frac{g^{2}m_{Z^{\prime}}}{96\pi c^{2}_{W}}(C^{Z_{2}}_{\nu_{R}})^{2}\left(1-\frac{4m^{2}_{\nu_{iR}}}{m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}}\right)^{3/2}\Theta\left(\frac{m_{Z^{\prime}}}{2}-m_{\nu_{iR}}\right),

VI.6
Non-DM right-handed neutrino signal at colliders

Before closing the section, we would like to comment on the production and discovery potential of the two right-handed neutrinos
\nu_{1,2R}
which govern type-I seesaw neutrino mass generation, apart from the dark matter
\nu_{3R}
that is separately studied in the next section.

Firstly, the neutrinos
\nu_{1,2R}
can be produced at high energy colliders through the processes mediated by the SM bosons
W,Z
, and
H
(see, e.g.,
Antusch:2016ejd
), and the new bosons
Z^{\prime},H^{\prime}
(see, e.g.,
Das:2017deo
;
Das:2018tbd
). However, since
\nu_{1,2R}
are a SM-singlet possessing only a
h^{\nu}
coupling to leptons, contribution to the production rate in case of
W,Z,H
, and
H^{\prime}
exchanges is only through mixing effects, hence being very suppressed by the small mixing parameters,
{\varphi}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})
,
{\xi}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-2})
,
\epsilon\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-6})
, as well as the small coupling
h^{\nu}\sim 10^{-5}
. That said, the neutrinos
\nu_{1,2R}
are dominantly produced in pair due to the
Z^{\prime}
exchange, say
\sigma(pp\to Z^{\prime}\to NN)
at LHC and
\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to Z^{\prime}\to NN)
at ILC for
N=\nu_{1,2R}
, because both
\nu_{1,2R}
and SM fermions have non-zero new gauge charges that set the
Z^{\prime}
force. Although the production rate in this case is suppressed by the
Z^{\prime}
mass at TeV, it opens up a possibility of discovering these heavy neutrinos in the high-luminosity experiments
Das:2022rbl
.

Next, once produced these heavy neutrinos can decay into the SM particles such as
l^{\pm}W^{\mp},\nu_{L}Z
, and
\nu_{L}H
through their mixings with SM neutrinos. Because of Majorana nature of
N
, the characteristic signal in experiment might be a same-sign dilepton or a trilepton plus jets (associated with quarks) and/or missing energy (associated with neutrinos) in final state. For instance,
\sigma(pp\to Z^{\prime}\to NN\to l^{\pm}\l^{\pm}W^{\mp}W^{\mp})
is either followed by both
W
’s decay to quarks
W\to ud
or followed by a
W
decay to quarks
W\to ud
and another
W
decay to leptons
W\to l\nu_{L}
. Alternatively, because the
N
-
\nu_{L}
mixing is very small, one of two heavy neutrinos can be long-lived, enough to be explored by the displaced vertex search experiments
Das:2019fee
. A detailed study of all such processes is interesting but out of the scope of this work, which would be published elsewhere.

VII
Majorana dark matter

In this section, we will show that the third right-handed neutrino
\nu_{3R}
is a viable dark matter candidate based on the dark matter relic density and direct dark matter detection experiments. In our scenario, the dark matter candidate
\nu_{3R}
is stable and impossibly decaying to ordinary particles because of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
conservation. The idea of a right-handed neutrino dark matter is old, but in our model it takes a new life since it communicates with normal matter via
Z^{\prime},H^{\prime}
portals, the fields of family-dependent abelian dynamics. Additionally, since the
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
and
H
-
H^{\prime}
mixings are very small, given that
v\ll\Lambda
, we can omit extra contributions of ordinary particles due to the mixings. Also, since
H^{\prime}
only couples to ordinary particles through a mixing with
H
, the
\nu_{3R}
candidate interacts with SM particles dominantly through the new gauge boson portal
Z^{\prime}
.

VII.1
Dark matter scattering off nuclei

Direct dark matter detection experiments are mostly aimed at observing elastic scatters of dark matter particles on nucleons of heavy nuclei in a large detector. Furthermore, this scattering can be described, at the microscopic level, starting from effective interactions between the dark matter particle and the SM quarks. In our scenario, such interactions are dominantly contributed by the
t
-channel exchange of the new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
, as described by the following effective Lagrangian
Barger:2008qd

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{g^{2}}{4c^{2}_{W}}\frac{g_{A}^{Z^{\prime}}(\nu_{R})}{m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}}\bar{\nu}_{3R}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\nu_{3R}\bar{q}\gamma^{\mu}[g_{V}^{Z^{\prime}}(q)-g_{A}^{Z^{\prime}}(q)\gamma_{5}]q,

(82)

where the vector and axial-vector couplings of
Z^{\prime}
to quark
q
, say
g_{V,A}^{Z^{\prime}}(q)
, can be directly obtained from Table
3
, while the axial-vector coupling of
Z^{\prime}
to
\nu_{3R}
is
g_{A}^{Z^{\prime}}(\nu_{R})\equiv C_{\nu_{R}}^{Z_{2}}
. Note that the dark matter candidate
\nu_{3R}
is a self-conjugate Majorana fermion, so it cannot have a vector-vector coupling in the above effective Lagrangian. From this effective Lagrangian, we obtain the spin-dependent (SD) scattering cross section of the dark matter candidate
\nu_{3R}

\sigma_{\mathrm{SD}}=(2!)^{2}\frac{3}{\pi}\frac{g^{4}}{16c^{4}_{W}}\frac{m^{2}_{\text{DM-N}}}{m^{4}_{Z^{\prime}}}\left[g_{A}^{Z^{\prime}}(\nu_{R})\right]^{2}\left[g_{A}^{Z^{\prime}}(u)\lambda_{u}^{\mathrm{N}}+g_{A}^{Z^{\prime}}(d)(\lambda_{d}^{\mathrm{N}}+\lambda_{s}^{\mathrm{N}})\right]^{2},

(83)

where the factor
(2!)^{2}
is due to two counts of Wick contraction for the Majorana field
\nu_{3R}
. In addition,
m_{\text{DM-N}}=m_{\nu_{3R}}m_{\mathrm{N}}/(m_{\nu_{3R}}+m_{\mathrm{N}})
is the reduced mass of the dark matter-nucleon system,
m_{\mathrm{N}}
is the mass of neutron, and

VIII
Conclusion

In this work, we have considered a simple extension of the SM with minimal particle content, based on the symmetry
SU(3)_{C}\otimes SU(2)_{L}\otimes U(1)_{X}\otimes U(1)_{N}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, inspired by the observed fermion family number and the dark matter stability. In addition to the SM particles, the model contains only a scalar singlet and three right-handed neutrinos as the new fields. We have shown that three right-handed neutrinos are just enough to explain both the smallness of active neutrino masses and the dark matter observables. The phenomenology of the new gauge boson has been investigated through the mixing of it with the SM
Z
-like boson. We have obtained the parameter spaces that simultaneously satisfy the recent CDF result for
W
-boson mass, electroweak precision measurements, particle colliders, as well as dark matter observables, if the kinetic mixing parameter between the
U(1)_{X,N}
gauge bosons is not necessarily small. In contrast to
VanDong:2022cin
that requires at least two Higgs doublets, this work shows that only the standard model Higgs doublet presented is sufficient for recovering all phenomenological aspects when the kinetic mixing effect is included.

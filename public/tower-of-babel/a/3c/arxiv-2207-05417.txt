Upper Bounds on Maximum Lengths of Singleton-Optimal Locally Repairable Codes
Shu Liu, Tingyi Wu, Chaoping Xing, Chen Yuan

Abstract
. A locally repairable code is called Singleton-optimal if it achieves the Singleton-type bound. Such codes are of great theoretic interest in the study of locally repairable codes. In the recent years there has been a great amount of work on this topic. One of the main problems in this topic is to determine the largest length of a q q -ary Singleton-optimal locally repairable code for given locality and minimum distance. Unlike classical MDS codes, the maximum length of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes are very sensitive to minimum distance and locality. Thus, it is more challenging and complicated to investigate the maximum length of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes. In literature, there has been already some research on this problem. However, most of work is concerned with some specific parameter regime such as small minimum distance and locality, and rely on the constraint that ( r + 1 ) | n (r+1)|n and recovery sets are disjoint, where r r is locality and n n is the code length. In this paper we study the problem for large range of parameters including the case where minimum distance is proportional to length. In addition, we also derive some upper bounds on the maximum length of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes with small minimum distance by removing this constraint. It turns out that even without the constraint we still get better upper bounds for codes with small locality and distance compared with known results. Furthermore, based on our upper bounds for codes with small distance and locality and some propagation rule that we propose in this paper, we are able to derive some upper bounds for codes with relatively large distance and locality assuming that ( r + 1 ) | n (r+1)|n and recovery sets are disjoint.

Introduction

Modern distributed storage systems have great demand for erasure coding based schemes with good storage efficiency in order to cope with the explosion in the amount of data stored online. Locally Repairable Codes (LRCs) have emerged as the codes of choice for many such scenarios and have been implemented in a number of large scale systems e.g., Microsoft Azure
[

and Hadoop

. The topic has attracted many researchers and a great amount of work has been done in the literature

.

A block code is called a locally repairable code with locality
r
if every symbol in the encoding is a function of

r
other symbols. This enables recovery of any single erased symbol in a local fashion by downloading at most
r
other symbols. On the other hand, one would like the code to have a good minimum distance to enable recovery of many erasures in the worst-case. Locally repairable codes have been the subject of extensive study in recent years.
Locally repairable codes offer a good balance between efficient erasure recovery in the typical case in distributed storage systems where a single node fails (or becomes temporarily unavailable due to maintenance or other causes), and still allowing recovery of the data from a larger number of erasures and thus safeguarding the data in more worst-case scenarios.

Codes with information locality property were also studied in
[
7
,
8
]
. In this paper we focus on linear codes. For an
[n,k,d]
-linear code with locality
r
, we denote it by
[n,k,d;r]
(see the definition of locally repairable codes in Section 2). An
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code must obey various upper bounds such as the Singleton-type bound
[
12
]
, the Cadambe-Mazumdar bound
[
5
]
, etc. A locally repairable code achieving any of these upper bounds is called optimal. Among these bounds, the Singleton-type bound is neat and independent of alphabet size. Precisely speaking, for a linear locally repairable code
{\mathcal{C}}
of length
n
with
k
information symbols and locality
r
, the minimum distance
d({\mathcal{C}})
of
{\mathcal{C}}
is upper bounded by

(1)

d({\mathcal{C}})\leqslant n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+2.

The bound (
1
) is called the Singleton-type bound for locally repairable codes and it was first proved in
[
8
]
. A linear code achieving the above Singleton-type bound is called Singleton-optimal.

A code achieving the classical Singleton bound is called a Maximal Distance Separable (MDS) code. There is a well-known Main MDS Conjecture stating that a
q
-ary nontrivial MDS code has length at most
q+2
. This conjecture is believed to be true widely and the conjecture has been proved when the ground field has a prime order
[
1
]
. The Main MDS Conjecture is very neat in the sense that the maximum length of a nontrivial MDS code depends only on
q
and is independent of minimum distance. Unlike the Main MDS Conjecture, the maximum length of a Singleton-optimal locally repairable code is very sensitive to minimum distance and locality. This makes the problem more challenging and complicated.
The current paper will focus on study of maximum lengths of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes, namely we will drive some upper bounds.

1.1.
Known results

One natural question is how large the maximum length of a Singleton-optimal locally repairable code could be. There is already some research on this problem though it is still far from complete. The problem was first studied in the paper by Guruswami et al.
[
9
]
where they considered the cases of relatively small distance

and locality

(r+1)|n
and recovery sets are disjoint, where

Proposition 1.1

(see
[

)
.

Assume that

-locally repairable code with

if

The above result was further improved in
[

proved the following.

Proposition 1.2

-locally repairable code with

n\geqslant\Omega(dr^{2})

Apart from the above bounds, there are various constructions of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes in the literature. A class of codes constructed earlier and known as pyramid codes
[

are shown to be codes that
are Singleton-optimal. In
[

[

only produces a specific value of the length

The first breakthrough construction was given in
[
22
]
. This construction naturally generalizes Reed-Solomon construction which relies on the alphabet of cardinality comparable to the code length
n
. The idea behind the construction is very nice. The only shortcoming of this construction is restriction on locality
r
. Namely,
r+1
must be a divisor of either
q-1
or
q

r+1
is equal to a product of a divisor of
q-1
and a divisor of
q

q
is the code alphabet. There are also some existence results given in
[
19
]
and
[
22
]
with less restriction on locality

. But both results require large alphabet which is an exponential function of the code length.
This construction was extended via automorphism group of rational function fields by Jin, Ma and Xing
[
14
]
and it turns out that there are more flexibility on locality and the code length can be
q+1
.

Recently, there are also some constructions of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes. For instance, the construction in
[
13
]
provides Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes with distance
d=5
and
6
. By using elliptic curves, some Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes with distance proportional to length are constructed in
[
15
]
. For
(d,r)=(6,2)

are constructed in
[

. In addition, a class of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes with a large range of distance and locality are presented in
[
9

[
26

provides three families of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes based on matrix-product codes. The reader may refer to these papers for the detail.

2.1.
Some basic notations and results for classical block codes

Definition 1
.

The support of a vector

{\rm{supp}}({\bf u})=\{i\in[n]:u_{i}\neq 0\},

The Hamming weight

then

(4)

\displaystyle n\geqslant\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left\lceil{\frac{d}{q^{i}}}\right\rceil.

Now, we introduce some propagation rules
[
16
]
of classical block codes that will be used in this paper.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose there is an
[n,k,d]
-linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}.
Then

(
i
)

there exists an
[n-s,k,d-s]
-linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for any
1\leqslant s\leqslant d-1;

(
ii
)

there exists an
[n-s,k-s,d]
-linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for any
1\leqslant s\leqslant k-1.

Then, we recall the Singleton defect of an
[n,k,d]
-linear code
\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 2
.

The Singleton defect of an
[n,k,d]
-linear code
\mathcal{C}
is defined to be
s(\mathcal{C})=n-k+1-d.

(i)

A code
\mathcal{C}
with
s(\mathcal{C})=0
is called
maximum distance separable
(MDS). Note that MDS codes with dimension
k\in\{0,1,n-1,n\}
are called
trivial
. Then we have the following well-know conjecture.

Main MDS Conjecture
[
17
]
:
For a nontrivial
[n,k]
-MDS code we have that
n\leqslant q+2
if
q
is even and
k=3
or
k=q-1,
and
n\leqslant q+1
otherwise. So, the length
n
of a MDS code is always upper bounded by
q+2.

(ii)

A code
\mathcal{C}
with
s(\mathcal{C})=1
is called
an almost MDS
(AMDS) code.
Let
\mu(r,q)
denote the maximum length
n
for which there exists an
[n,n-r-1,r+1]
code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then we have

\mu(2,q)=q^{2}+q+1

[
25
]
. Furthermore, since
\mu(r,q)\leqslant\mu(r-1,q)+1
, we have
\mu(3,q)\leqslant q^{2}+q+2

[
25
]
.

Remark 1
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a
q
-ary nontrivial
[n,k,d\geqslant n-k]
-linear code, then
d=n-k
or
n-k+1
. If
d=n-k
, then
n\leqslant\mu(n-k-1,q)
. If
d=n-k+1
, we can get an
[n,k,d=n-k]
-almost MDS code by setting the last coordinate to be
0
. Hence, we also have
n\leqslant\mu(n-k-1,q)
.

2.2.
Some basic notations and results for locally repairable codes

Informally speaking, if every coordinate of a given codeword of a block code with locality
r
can be recovered by accessing at most
r
other coordinates of this codeword. The formal definition of a locally repairable code with locality
r
is given as follows.

Definition 3
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be a
q
-ary block code of length
n
. For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
, define
{\mathcal{C}}(i,\alpha):=\{{\bf c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in{\mathcal{C}},\;c_{i}=\alpha\}
. For a subset
I\subseteq\{1,2,\cdots,n\}\setminus\{i\}
, we denote by
{\mathcal{C}}_{I}(i,\alpha)
the projection of
{\mathcal{C}}(i,\alpha)
on
I
.
Then
{\mathcal{C}}

is called a locally repairable code with locality
r
if, for every
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
, there exists a subset

I_{i}\subseteq\{1,2,\cdots,n\}\setminus\{i\}
with
|I_{i}|\leqslant r
such that
{\mathcal{C}}_{I_{i}}(i,\alpha)
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{I_{i}}(i,\beta)
are disjoint for any
\alpha\neq\beta
.

One can have an equivalence definition as the following.

Definition 4
.

A
q
-ary code of length
n
is called a locally recoverable code with locality
r
if for any
i\in[n]
, there exists a subset
R\subset[n]\setminus\{i\}
of size
r
such that for any
{\bf u},{\bf v}\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
{\bf u}_{R\cup\{i\}}={\bf v}_{R\cup\{i\}}
iff
{\bf u}_{R}={\bf v}_{R}
. For the sake of convenience,
R\cup\{i\}
is called a recovery set of
i
in the paper.

Thus, apart from the usual parameters: length, rate and minimum distance, the locality of a locally repairable code plays a crucial role. In this paper, we always consider locally repairable codes that are linear over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus, a
q
-ary locally repairable code of length
n
, dimension
k
, minimum distance
d
and locality
r
is said to be an
[n,k,d]_{q}
-locally repairable code with locality
r
, denoted by
[n,k,d;r]
or
[n,k,d;r]_{q}
if we want to emphasize the code alphabet size
q
.

If we ignore minimum distance of a
q
-ary locally repairable code, then there is a constraint on the rate
[
8
]
, namely,

\frac{k}{n}\leqslant\frac{r}{r+1}.

However, if we also care about global error correction, we have to take minimum distance into account. In this case, the parameters have the constraint given in (
1
).

The following result can be found in
[
9
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
n,k,d,r
be positive integers with
(r+1)|n
. If the Singleton-type bound (
1
) is achieved, then

n-k=\frac{n}{r+1}+d-2-\left\lfloor\frac{d-2}{r+1}\right\rfloor.

Lemma 2.5
.

Given a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code, the dimension
k
is completely determined by
n,d,r
.

Proof.

Suppose we have two Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes
[n,k_{1},d;r]
and
[n,k_{2},d;r]
with
1\leqslant k_{1}<k_{2}
. Then we have

d=n-k_{1}-\left\lceil\frac{k_{1}}{r}\right\rceil+2>n-k_{2}-\left\lceil\frac{k_{2}}{r}\right\rceil+2=d.

This is a contradiction.
∎

For linear codes, the following lemma establishes a connection between the locality and the dual code
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
. It was proved in
[
9
]
that recovery sets are determined by codewords with specific support in the dual code. Precisely we have Lemma
2.6
.

Lemma 2.6

(see
[
9
]
)
.

A subset
R\subset[n]
with
i\in R
is a recovery set at
i
for a
q
-ary linear code of length
n
if and only if there exists a codeword
{\bf c}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
such that
i\in{\rm supp}({\bf c})\subset R
.

For a linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
and
r\geqslant 1
, we define the set

\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{C}}(r):=\{{\rm supp}({\bf c}):\;{\bf c}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp},\;|{\rm supp}({\bf c})|\leqslant r+1\}.

The following result is a straightforward corollary of Lemma
2.2
.

Corollary 2.7
.

A linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
has locality
r
if and only if
[n]=\cup_{I\in\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{C}}(r)}I
.

Finally, we introduce the disjoint recovery sets.

Definition 5
.

Let
\ell
be an integer. We say that a linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
with locality
r
has disjoint recovery sets if there exist subsets
\{I_{1},I_{2},\cdots,I_{\ell}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{C}}(r)
that form a partition of
[n]
.

3.
Upper Bounds for Locally Repairable Codes

In this section, we focus on upper bounds for code length when
d=\Omega(n)
and
d=o(n)
, respectively. We also provide an upper bound for general minimum distance
d
and locality
r
.

Throughout the paper, we assume that parameters of a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable codes satisfy

(5)

r<k,\quad{n\geqslant 2(r+1)}\quad{\rm and}\quad d\geqslant 3.

To start, we construct a parity-check matrix
H
of an
[n,k,d;r]
-linear locally repairable code
\mathcal{C}
with a specific form.

Based on the Lemma
2.6
, we can choose a basis of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
as follows: there exists a vector
{\bf u}_{1}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
such that
i_{1}=1\in{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{1})
and
|{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{1})|\leqslant r+1
. Choose
i_{2}\in[n]\setminus{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{1})
, then there exists a vector
{\bf u}_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
such that
i_{2}\in{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{2})
and
|{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{2})|\leqslant r+1
. Choose
i_{3}\in[n]\setminus({\rm supp}({\bf u}_{1})\cup({\bf u}_{2}))
, then there exists a vector
{\bf u}_{3}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
such that
i_{3}\in{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{3})
and
|{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{3})|\leqslant r+1
.
Continue in this fashion until we get
{\bf u}_{1},{\bf u}_{2},\dots,{\bf u}_{\ell}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
satisfying:

(i)

|{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{i})|\leqslant r+1
for all
i\in[\ell]
;

(ii)

\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{i})=[n]
;

(iii)

{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{i})\nsubseteq\cup_{j=1}^{i-1}{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{j})
for all
i\in\{2,3,\dots,\ell\}
.

From the above condition (iii), it is clear that
{\bf u}_{1},{\bf u}_{2},\dots,{\bf u}_{\ell}
are linearly independent. Thus,
\ell\leqslant n-k
and we can extend to a basis
{\bf u}_{1},{\bf u}_{2},\dots,{\bf u}_{n-k}
of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
. Denote by
H_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell\times n}
and
H_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(n-k-\ell)\times n}
, respectively the matrix whose rows consists of
{\bf u}_{1},{\bf u}_{2},\dots,{\bf u}_{\ell}
and
{\bf u}_{\ell+1},{\bf u}_{\ell+2},\dots,{\bf u}_{n-k}
, respectively. Denote by
H
the matrix

(6)

H=\begin{pmatrix}H_{1}\\
H_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that the number of rows of
H_{1}
is
\ell
. We denote by
h
the number of rows of
H_{2}
. Then

(7)

h+\ell=n-k.

Based on the above construction of a parity-check matrix
H
, we have the following inequalities for parameters involved.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

H\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(n-k)\times n}
be a parity-check matrix of
\mathcal{C}
given in (
6
). We have

(8)

\frac{k}{r}<\frac{n}{r+1}\leqslant\ell\leqslant n-k.

Proof.

We write
H=(h_{ij})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(n-k)\times n},1\leq i\leq n-k,1\leq j\leq n
. Since
{\bf u}_{1},{\bf u}_{2},\dots,{\bf u}_{\ell}
are linearly independent, so it is clear
\ell\leqslant n-k
. Consider the first
\ell
rows of
H,
each rows contains at most
r+1
nonzero elements, so the total number of nonzero elements within the first
\ell
rows of
H
is at most
\ell(r+1)
. On the other hand, for every
j\in[n]
there is
i\in[\ell]
such that the element
h_{ij}\neq 0.
This means that within the first
\ell
rows, there are at least
n
nonzero elements, so
n\leqslant\ell(r+1)
. Therefore, we have

\ell+k\leqslant n\leqslant\ell(r+1).

By the assumption
d\geqslant 3
and the Singleton-type bound, we have

3\leqslant d\leqslant n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+2\leqslant n-k-\frac{k}{r}+2,

i.e.,
\frac{k}{r}<\frac{n}{r+1}
.
Hence, we have

\frac{k}{r}<\frac{n}{r+1}\leqslant\ell\leqslant n-k.

∎

We shall construct a parity-check matrix
H_{I}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with a parity-check matrix
H=(h_{ij})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(n-k)\times n}
given in (
6
).

Let
I
be a subset of
[\ell],
define the set
J_{I}=\{j\in[n]:h_{ij}\neq 0{~\rm~for~some}~i\in I\}.
Denote by
H_{I}
the submatrix obtained from
H
by removing all the rows of
H
indexed by
I
and all the columns of
H
indexed by
J_{I}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{I}
be the linear code with
H_{I}
as a parity-check matrix, then
\mathcal{C}_{I}
is a
q
-ary
[n_{I}\geqslant n-|I|(r+1),k_{I}\geqslant k-r|I|,d_{I}\geqslant d]
-linear code.

Proof.

As each row indexed by
i\in I
has at most
r+1
nonzero elements, thus we delete at most
|I|(r+1)
columns and hence the length
n_{I}
is at least
n-|I|(r+1)
. The dimension
k_{I}
of
{\mathcal{C}}_{I}
is
n_{I}-{\rm rank}(H_{I})\geqslant n_{I}-(n-k-|I|)\geqslant k-r|I|
.

Now, we prove the minimum distance of
\mathcal{C}_{I}
should be at least
d.
Note that
H_{I}
can be obtained in two steps: (i) deleting the columns of
H
indexed by
J_{I}
to get the remaining matrix
H^{*}
. Then it is clear that any
d-1
columns of
H^{*}
are linearly independent. (ii) deleting zero rows of
H^{*}
gives
H_{I}
. Since we delete all zero entries of
H
, so the linear independence is unchanged, i.e.,
any
d-1
columns of
H_{I}
are still linearly independent. Thus, we get the desired lower bound on minimum distance.
∎

3.1.
Upper bound on code length when
d=\Omega(n)

Throughout this subsection, we assume that parameters of a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code satisfies
r\geqslant 2
.
Recall that an
[n,k,d]
-linear code is called trivial if
k=0,1,n-1
or
n
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code. Let
I
be a subset of
[\ell]
with
|I|=\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil-2
(note that such an
I
can be chosen as
\ell\geqslant\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil>\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil-2\geqslant 0
).
If
d\geqslant 3
, then
{\mathcal{C}}_{I}
is a nontrivial
[n_{I},{k_{I}=n_{I}-d},d_{I}\geqslant d]
-linear code.

Proof.

The number of rows of
H_{I}
is
n-k-|I|=n-k-\left(\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil-2\right)=d
and any
d-1
rows of
H_{I}
are linearly independent. We conclude that
{\mathcal{C}}_{I}
is an
[n_{I},k_{I}=n_{I}-d,d_{I}\geqslant d]
-linear code.

Next we show that
{\mathcal{C}}_{I}
is nontrivial. Based on Lemma
3.2
, we have
k_{I}\geqslant k-r|I|=k-r\left(\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil-2\right)\geqslant k-r\left(\frac{k}{r}+1-2\right)=r\geqslant 2
. Furthermore, we have

k_{I}=n_{I}-{\rm rank}(H_{I})\leqslant n_{I}-(d-1)\leqslant n_{I}-2.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

[
17
]
If there is a
q
-ary
[n,{k=n-d},\geqslant d]
-linear code
\mathcal{C}
with
k\geqslant 2
, then
d\leqslant 2q
.

Proof.

Suppose
d>2q
. Applying the Grismer bound in (
4
) to the code gives

n\geqslant\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left\lceil\frac{d}{q^{i}}\right\rceil\geqslant d+3+(k-2)=d+k+1\geqslant n+1.

This is a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

If there exists a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code with
d=\Omega(n)
, then
n=O(q)
. More precisely, if
d=\lambda n
for some
\lambda<1
, then
n\leqslant\frac{2q}{\lambda}
.

Proof.

As
d=\Omega(n)
, there exists a real
\lambda>0
such that
d=\lambda n
. Choose a subset
I
of
[\ell]
with
|I|=\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil-2
. Applying Lemma
3.4
to the code
{\mathcal{C}}_{I}
given in Lemma
3.3
, we get
d\leqslant 2q
. Thus, we have

n\leqslant\frac{d}{\lambda}\leqslant\frac{2}{\lambda}\times q.

The proof is completed.
∎

Next we give a sufficient condition under which
n\leqslant q+O(1)
in Theorem
3.7
.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code, then
{\mathcal{C}}_{I}
is an MDS code if
|I|=\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil-1
. Furthermore, if
k\not\equiv 1\pmod{r}
and
d\geqslant 3
, then
{\mathcal{C}}_{I}
is a nontrivial MDS code.

Proof.

Firstly, we show that
{\mathcal{C}}_{I}
is an MDS code if
|I|=\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil-1
. Note that the parity-check matrix
H_{I}
of
{\mathcal{C}}_{I}
has
n-k-|I|=n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+1=d-1
rows. Since any
d-1
columns of
H_{I}
is linearly independent, so
{\mathcal{C}}_{I}
is an MDS code.

Assume that
k\equiv s\pmod{r}
with
0\leqslant s\leqslant r-1
. From Lemma
3.2
, we have
k_{I}\geqslant k-r|I|.
If
s=0
, i.e.,
r|k
, then
k_{I}\geqslant k-r|I|=k-(k-r)=r>1
. If
s\neq 0
, then
s\geqslant 2
and we have
k_{I}\geqslant k-r|I|=k-(k+r-s-r)=s>1
. Furthermore, as
k_{I}=n_{I}-(n-k-|I|)
, we have
n_{I}-k_{I}=n-k-|I|=d-1\geqslant 2
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Suppose that the Main MDS conjecture holds. Assume that there exists a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code
\mathcal{C}
, if
k\not\equiv 1\pmod{r}
then the length of
\mathcal{C}
is bounded by

n\leqslant q+1+k+\frac{k}{r}.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairabel code. Choose a subset
I
of
[\ell]
with
|I|=\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil-1
.
By the Main MDS conjecture
[
17
]
, and Lemmas
3.2
and
3.6
, we have

q+2\geqslant n_{I}\geqslant n-|I|(r+1)>n-\frac{k}{r}(r+1)=n-k-\frac{k}{r}.

This gives
n\leqslant q+1+k+\frac{k}{r}
.
∎

By Theorem
3.7
, we have following results.

Corollary 3.8
.

Suppose that the Main MDS conjecture holds. If there exists a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code with
k\not\equiv 1\pmod{r}
, then we have

(i)

if
k
is a constant, then
n\leqslant q+O(1)
.

(ii)

if
d=\lambda n
for some
\lambda<1
, then
n\leqslant\frac{q}{\lambda}+O(1).

Proof.

If
k
is a constant, then by Theorem
3.7
, we have
n\leqslant q+1+k+\frac{k}{r}=q+O(1)
.
On the other hand,
if
d=\lambda n
, then by the Singleton-type bound in (
1
), we have

d=\lambda n=n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+2=n-k-\frac{k}{r}+O(1),

i.e.,
k=\frac{(1-\lambda)r}{r+1}n+O(1)
. By Theorem
3.7
, we have

n\leqslant q+1+\left(1+\frac{1}{r}\right)k=q+\left(1+\frac{1}{r}\right)\times\frac{(1-\lambda)r}{r+1}n+O(1)=q+(1-\lambda)n+O(1).

This gives the desired result.
∎

Remark 2
.

From
[
15
]
, there exists a Singleton-optimal
[n=(r+1)m,k=1+r(t-1),d=n-(t-1)(r+1);r]
-locally repairable code for
r=2,3,5,7,11,23
and
1\leqslant t<m=\lfloor\frac{q+2\sqrt{q}-r-2}{r+1}\rfloor
. Thus,
n=q+2\sqrt{q}+O(1)
when
k\equiv 1\pmod{r}
. Hence, to have the upper bound
n\leqslant{q}+O(1)
, the condition
k\not\equiv 1\pmod{r}
given in part (i) of Corollary
3.8
is indeed necessary.

Remark 3
.

Compared with Theorem
3.5
, part (ii) of Corollary
3.8
gives a better upper bound
n\leqslant{q}+O(1)
if
d=\Omega(n)
.

3.2.
Upper bound on code length when
d=o(n)

In
[
9
]
, the authors derived an upper bound on maximum length of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes by assuming that
(r+1)|n
and recovery sets are disjoint. In this subsection, we generalize the upper bound given in
[
9
]
by removing the condition that
(r+1)|n
and recovery sets are disjoint.

Theorem 3.9
.

If there exists a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code with
rd=o(n)
, then we have

n\leqslant\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1+o(1))\frac{r+1}{r}\times\frac{d-1}{4(q-1)}q^{4r(d-1-\varepsilon)/((d-t)(r+1))}&\mbox{if $d\equiv 1,2\pmod{4}$}\\
(1+o(1))\frac{r+1}{r}\times\frac{d-1}{4(q-1)}q^{4r(d-2-\varepsilon)/((d-t)(r+1))}&\mbox{if $d\equiv 3,4\pmod{4}$},\end{array}\right.

where
t=\{d\pmod{4}\}
and
\varepsilon=\frac{\{k\pmod{r}\}}{r}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}
is a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code attaining the Singleton bound with
d=o\left(\frac{n}{r}\right)
. Let
k\equiv-s\pmod{r}
with
0\leqslant s\leqslant r-1
, i.e.,
s=r-\{k\pmod{r}\}
. Then we have

d=n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+2=n-k-\frac{k+s}{r}+2.

This gives

(9)

\frac{r+1}{r}\times\frac{k}{n}=1-\frac{d}{n}+\frac{1}{n}\left(2-\frac{s}{r}\right).

Put
f(n)=\frac{d}{n}-\frac{1}{n}\left(2-\frac{s}{r}\right)
. As
d\geqslant 3,
we have
f(n)>0
.
Rewrite
f(n)
into the following identity

(10)

nf(n)=d-1-\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon=1-\frac{s}{r}=\frac{\{k\pmod{r}\}}{r}
and hence
\frac{1}{r}\leqslant\varepsilon\leqslant 1
.

By (
9
) and (
10
), we have

(11)

~n-k=n-(1-f(n))\frac{r}{r+1}n=\frac{n}{r+1}+f(n)\frac{r}{r+1}n.

Let
H
be a parity-check matrix of
\mathcal{C}
given in the form of (
6
). Define
h=n-k-\ell
, by (
11
) we have

\ell+h=n-k=\frac{n}{r+1}+f(n)\frac{r}{r+1}n.

By (
8
), we know that
\ell\geqslant\frac{n}{r+1}
. Thus, we may write

(12)

~\ell=\frac{n}{r+1}+g(n)

for some
g(n)
with
0\leqslant g(n)\leqslant f(n)\frac{r}{r+1}n
, then

\frac{n}{r+1}+g(n)+h=\frac{n}{r+1}+f(n)\frac{r}{r+1}n.

Put
c(n)=f(n)\frac{r}{r+1}n-g(n)
,
so
h=c(n)\geqslant 0
.

Now we shall construct a
q
-ary locally repairable code with a specific form, so that we can apply Hamming bound to get the desired results.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
q
-ary
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code with parity-check matrix
H
in (
6
).
To start, we define two sets

A=\{i\in[n]:\;\mbox{the $i$-th column of $H_{1}$ has exactly one nonzero element}\}

and

B=\{i\in[n]:\;\mbox{the $i$-th column of $H_{1}$ has at least two nonzero elements}\}.

Put
a=|A|
and
b=|B|
. Then we have

(i)

a+b=n
;

(ii)

(r+1)\ell-b\geqslant n
.

Thus, by (
12
) we have

(13)

~\frac{b}{n}\leqslant\frac{\ell(r+1)}{n}-1=(r+1)\times\frac{g(n)}{n}.

Through (
13
), we get

a=n-b\geqslant n-(r+1)g(n).

Next, we construct aforementioned
q
-ary locally repairable code in
3
steps.

Step
1.

Denote by
L_{1}
be the submatrix of
H
obtained by deleting all columns indexed by
B
. By multiplying nonzero elements for each column of
L_{1}
, we may assume that
L_{1}
has the following form

(14)

L_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\begin{array}[]{c|c|ccc|c}{\bf 1}&\mathbf{0}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&{\bf 1}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\mathbf{0}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&{\bf 1}\end{array}\\
\huge{L_{2}}\\
\end{array}\right),

where each
{\bf 1}
is the all-one vector of length at most
r+1
and total number
\ell_{1}
of all-one vectors are upper bounded by
\ell
; and
L_{2}
is the
h\times a
submatrix of
H_{2}
indexed by
B
(recall that
h=n-k-\ell
). It is clear that
L_{1}
is an
(n-k)\times a
matrix.

Step
2.

For each block of
L_{1}
, we do column operations by subtracting the first column of this block, we get a matrix of the following form

(15)

L_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c|ccc|c}1,{\bf 0}&\mathbf{0}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&1,{\bf 0}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\mathbf{0}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&1,{\bf 0}\\
{\bf h}_{1},K_{1}&{\bf h}_{2},K_{2}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&{\bf h}_{\ell_{1}},K_{\ell_{1}}\end{array}\right),

where
{\bf h}_{i}
is a column vector of length
{h}
and
K_{i}
is a matrix of
{h}
rows and at most
r
columns.

Step
3.

Put

K=(K_{1},K_{2},\dots,K_{\ell_{1}}).

Then
K
has
{h}
rows and
a-\ell_{1}
columns. Since any
d-1
columns of
H
are linear independent, we have that any
\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor
columns of
L_{3}
are linearly independent.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{K}
be the linear code with
K
as a parity-check matrix, then
{\mathcal{C}}_{K}
has length
a-\ell_{1}
, dimension at least
a-\ell_{1}-{h}
and distance at least
\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor+1
.

Note that by (
10
),
{h}=c(n)=f(n)\frac{r}{r+1}n-g(n)\leqslant f(n)\frac{r}{r+1}n=\frac{r}{r+1}(d-1-\varepsilon)
. Let
t=\{d\pmod{4}\}
and put
d_{1}=(d-t)/4
.

Case 1:
t=1
or
2
. Then
\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor+1=2d_{1}+1
. In this case, we get a
q
-ary
[a-\ell_{1},\geqslant a-\ell_{1}-{h},\geqslant 2d_{1}+1]
-linear code
{\mathcal{C}}_{K}
. Applying the Hamming bound in (
3
) to
{\mathcal{C}}_{K}
gives

q^{a-\ell_{1}-{h}}\leqslant q^{\dim({\mathcal{C}}_{K})}\leqslant\frac{q^{a-\ell_{1}}}{\sum_{i=0}^{d_{1}}{a-\ell_{1}\choose i}(q-1)^{i}}.

This gives
{a-\ell_{1}\choose d_{1}}(q-1)^{d_{1}}\leqslant q^{h}
. As
{a-\ell_{1}\choose d_{1}}\geqslant\left(\frac{a-\ell_{1}}{d_{1}}\right)^{d_{1}}
, we get
\left(\frac{a-\ell_{1}}{d_{1}}\right)^{d_{1}}(q-1)^{d_{1}}\leqslant q^{h}
. Hence,

(16)

a-\ell\leqslant a-\ell_{1}\leqslant\frac{d_{1}}{q-1}q^{{h}/d_{1}}\leqslant\frac{d-1}{4(q-1)}q^{4r(d-1-\varepsilon)/((d-t)(r+1))}.

By (
10
) and
rd=o(n)
, we have
rf(n)=o(1)
. Thus, we have
rg(n)=o(n)
as
\frac{rg(n)}{n}\leqslant rf(n)\times\frac{r}{r+1}
.
Hence, we have

a-\ell\geqslant n-(r+1)g(n)-\frac{n}{r+1}-g(n)=\frac{r}{r+1}\times n-(r+2)g(n)=\frac{r}{r+1}\times n+o(n).

Then, we get

n\leqslant(1+o(1))\frac{r+1}{r}\times\frac{d-1}{4(q-1)}q^{4r(d-1-\varepsilon)/((d-t)(r+1))}.

Case 2:
t=3
or
4
. Then
\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor+1=2d_{1}+2
. In this case, we get a
q
-ary
[a-\ell_{1},\geqslant a-\ell_{1}-{h},\geqslant 2d_{1}+2]
-linear code
{\mathcal{C}}_{K}
. By the propagation rule, there exists an
[a-\ell_{1}-1,q-\ell_{1}-{h},2d_{1}+1]
-linear code.
In a similar way, applying the Hamming bound in (
3
) to
{\mathcal{C}}_{K}
gives

n\leqslant(1+o(1))\frac{r+1}{r}\times\frac{d-1}{4(q-1)}q^{4r(d-2-\varepsilon)/((d-t)(r+1))}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

If there is a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d=5;r]
-locally repairable code, then we have

(i)

n=O(1)
if
r=1
;

(ii)

n=O(q)
if
r=2
or
3
and assume that the Main MDS conjecture holds;

(iii)

n=O(r)
if
r\geqslant 4
,
k\equiv 0,-1
or
-2\pmod{r}
;

(iv)

n=O(q^{2})
if
r\geqslant 4
and
r=o(n)
.

Proof.

We follow the notations and approach given in the proof of Theorem
3.9
. Let
k\equiv-s\pmod{r},0\leqslant s\leqslant r-1
. Then, we have

5=n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+2=n-k-\frac{k+s}{r}+2,

i.e.,

k=\frac{r}{r+1}n-\frac{s}{r+1}-\frac{3r}{r+1}.

By (
8
) and (
6
), we can obtain

\frac{n}{r+1}+h\leqslant\ell+h=n-k=\frac{n}{r+1}+\frac{s+3r}{r+1},

and

(20)

h\leqslant\frac{s+3r}{r+1}=2+\frac{s+r-2}{r+1}.

Since
0\leqslant s\leqslant r-1,
so

(21)

\ell\leqslant\frac{n}{r+1}+\frac{s+3r}{r+1}\leqslant\frac{n}{r+1}+\frac{4r-1}{r+1}.

Recall that
a+b=n
and
(r+1)\ell-b\geqslant n
in the proof of Theorem
3.9
. This gives
a\geqslant 2n-(r+1)\ell
.
Consider the code
{\mathcal{C}}_{K}
with length
a-\ell_{1}
(refer to the proof of Theorem
3.9
again), then we have

(22)

a-\ell_{1}\geqslant a-\ell\geqslant 2n-(r+2)\ell\geqslant 2n-(r+2)\left(\frac{n}{r+1}+\frac{4r-1}{r+1}\right).

Case 1:

r=1
. In this case, we have
s=0
. By (
22
), the length of
\mathcal{C}_{K}
should be at least
\frac{n-9}{2}.
If
n<9
, then we have
n=O(1)
. Now assume that
n\geqslant 9
. By (
20
), we have
h\leqslant 1.5
, i.e,
h\leqslant 1
. By (
22
),
a-\ell_{1}\geqslant\frac{n-3}{2}\geqslant 3
. As
{\mathcal{C}}_{K}
gives an
[a-\ell_{1},a-\ell_{1}-h,3]
-linear code which is beyond the classical Singleton bound, this is a contradiction. The contradiction implies that
n<9
if there exists a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d=5;1]
-locally repairable code.

Case 2:

r=2
or
3
. In this case, by (
20
), we have
h<3
, i.e.,
h\leqslant 2
. First of all, if
h=0
or
1
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{K}
is linear code exceeding the classical Singleton bound. As we proved in Case 1, we must have
n=O(1)
. Now assume that
h=2
, then
{\mathcal{C}}_{K}
is an
[a-\ell_{1},a-\ell_{1}-2,3]
-linear code which achieves the classical Singleton bound. By the Main MDS conjecture
[
17
]
, we have
a-\ell_{1}\leqslant q+2
. Thus, we have

2n-(r+2)\ell\leqslant a-\ell\leqslant a-\ell_{1}\leqslant q+2
. Combining this with (
21
), we get

n\leqslant\frac{r+1}{r}\times(q+2)+\frac{(r+2)(4r-1)}{r}=O(q).

Next we assume that
r\geqslant 4
.
The average length of each block of the matrix
L_{1}
defined in (
14
) is

\frac{a}{\ell_{1}}\geqslant\frac{a}{\ell}\geqslant\frac{2n}{\ell}-(r+1)\geqslant\frac{2n(r+1)}{n+4r-1}-(r+1).

Thus, we have
\frac{a}{\ell_{1}}\geqslant\frac{2n(r+1)}{n+4r-1}-(r+1)\geqslant 4

if
n\geqslant\frac{(r+5)(4r-1)}{r-3}
.

Case 3:

r\geqslant 4
and
k\equiv 0,-1
or
-2\pmod{r}
, i.e.,
0\leqslant s\leqslant 2
. In this case, we have
h<3
, i.e.,
h\leqslant 2
. Choose a block
M
of the largest length within the matrix
L_{1}
defined in (
14
), then the number of columns of
M
is at least
4
. Furthermore, we know that any
4
columns of
M
are linearly independent. This is a contradiction since
M
has at most three nonzero rows. The contradiction implies that
n<\frac{(r+5)(4r-1)}{r-3}=O(r)
.

Case 4:

r\geqslant 4
and
k\not\equiv 0,-1
or
-2\pmod{r}
, i.e.,
3\leqslant s\leqslant r-1
. In this case, we have
h<4
, i.e.,
h\leqslant 3
. If
h\leqslant 2
, then we claim that
n=O(r)
as proved in Case 3. This is impossible as
r=o(n)
. Now we assume that
h=3
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}_{K}
is a
[a-\ell_{1},a-\ell_{1}-3,3]
-almost MDS code. By
[
25
]
, we have
a-\ell_{1}\leqslant q^{2}+q+1
. Combining this with (
21
), we get

n\leqslant\frac{r+1}{r}\times(q^{2}+q+2)+\frac{(r+2)(4r-1)}{r+1}=O(q^{2}).

This completes the proof.

∎

columns of

are linearly independent. This is a contradiction since

The last case follows from Theorem

Theorem 3.13
.

Assume that there is a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code. If
s+2<d<r+2
, where
k\equiv-s\pmod{r}
with
0\leqslant s\leqslant r-1
, then we have
n<\frac{(d+r)(r-1+(d-2)r)}{r-d+2}
. In particular,
n=O(1)
if both
r
and
d
are constant.

Proof.

Suppose
n\geqslant\frac{(d+r)(r-1+(d-2)r)}{r-d+2}
.
We follow the notations and approach given in the proof of Theorem
3.9
.
Let
k\equiv-s\pmod{r},0\leqslant s\leqslant r-1
. Then, we have

d=n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+2=n-k-\frac{k+s}{r}+2,

i.e.,

k=\frac{r}{r+1}n-\frac{s}{r+1}-\frac{(d-2)r}{r+1}.

By (
8
) and (
6
), we can obtain

\frac{n}{r+1}+h\leqslant\ell+h=n-k=\frac{n}{r+1}+\frac{s+(d-2)r}{r+1},

and

(27)

h\leqslant\frac{s+(d-2)r}{r+1}=d-3+\frac{s+r-d+3}{r+1}.

So, we have

(28)

\ell\leqslant\frac{n}{r+1}+\frac{s+(d-2)r}{r+1}.

The average length of each block of the matrix
L_{1}
defined in (
14
) is

\frac{a}{\ell_{1}}\geqslant\frac{a}{\ell}\geqslant\frac{2n}{\ell}-(r+1)\geqslant\frac{2n(r+1)}{n+s+(d-2)r}-(r+1)\geqslant d-1

if
n\geqslant\frac{(d+r)(r-1+(d-2)r)}{r-d+2}
.

Since
d<r+2
, we have
h\leqslant d-3
by (
27
). Choose a block
M
of the largest length within the matrix
L_{1}
defined in (
14
), then the number of columns of
M
is at least
d-1
. Furthermore, we know that any
d-1
columns of
M
are linearly independent. This is a contradiction since
M
has at most
d-2
nonzero rows. This contradiction implies that
n<\frac{(d+r)(r-1+(d-2)r)}{r-d+2}.
This proof is completed.
∎

4.
Improved upper bounds with a constraint

In
[
9
]
and
[
6
]
, the authors studied upper bounds on length of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes under the constraint that
(r+1)|n
and recovery sets are disjoint. In this section, we assume this constraint as well to obtain some improved upper bounds.

Our main idea is to introduce some propagation rules and then reduce the problem to determine upper bounds on lengths of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes with small minimum distance. To make our propagation rules work well, we always assume that
n=\Omega(dr^{2})
in this section. With this assumption, we may assume that
r^{2}+2r<n-d
. Note that with assumption that
(r+1)|n
and
n=\Omega(dr^{2})
, it was proved in
[
9
]
that recovery sets are disjoint.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
n=\Omega(dr^{2})
, then a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code must have a recovery set of size
r+1
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code. Suppose that every recovery set has size at most
r
.
If
r=1
, then
k=\frac{n-d}{2}+1\geqslant 1
. Since
r<k,
the locality
r
of
{\mathcal{C}}
is also
0
, this forces that
{\mathcal{C}}
is the trivial code
\{{\bf 0}\}
. This gives a contradiction.

Now assume that
r\geqslant 2
. Since
{\mathcal{C}}
is a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code, so

(29)

~n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+2=d.

In this case, the locality of
\mathcal{C}
is
r-1,
by the Singleton bound (
1
) and (
29
), we have

n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+2=d\leqslant n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r-1}\right\rceil+2,

this gives
\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil\geqslant\left\lceil\frac{k}{r-1}\right\rceil
.

Combining the inequality
2r+r^{2}<{n-d}
and identity
n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+2=d
gives
k\geqslant r^{2}+r
.
Write
k=ur+v
with
1\leqslant v\leqslant r
, so
u\geqslant r
. It can be rewritten as
k=u(r-1)+u+v
.
Thus we have

\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil=u+1<u+2\leqslant\left\lceil\frac{k}{r-1}\right\rceil.

This gives a contradiction and the proof is complete.
∎

Based on Lemma
2.3
, let us have a propagation rule.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with disjoint recovery sets. Then, there exists a Singleton-optimal

[n-(r+1),k-a,d-r-1+a;r]
-locally repairable code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with disjoint recovery sets if
\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{k-a}{r}\right\rceil
, where
0\leqslant a\leqslant r+1
is an integer.

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{C}}
has disjoint recovery sets, without loss of generality, we may assume
\{n,n-1,\ldots,n-r\}
is a recovery set for indices
n-r,\ldots,n
. We first shorten code
{\mathcal{C}}
by removing the last
a
columns from its parity-check matrix. Denote the resulting code by
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
. It is clear that
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}=[n_{1},k_{1},d_{1};r_{1}]
has length
n_{1}=n-a
, dimension
k_{1}\geqslant k-a
, minimum distance
d_{1}\geqslant d
and locality
r_{1}=r
. Furthermore,
\{n-r,n-r+1,\ldots,n-a\}
is one of its disjoint recovery set. We then puncture the last
r+1-a
indices from
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
to obtain a linear code
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}=[n_{2},k_{2},d_{2};r_{2}]
. It is clear that
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
still has locality
r_{2}=r
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
has code length
n_{2}=n-(r+1)
, dimension
k_{2}\geq k-a
and minimum distance
d_{2}\geq d-(r+1)+a
. By taking a subcode of
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
, we may assume that
k_{2}=k-a
. Observe that

d_{2}\geq d-(r+1)+a=(n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+2)-(r+1)+a=n_{2}-k_{2}-\left\lceil\frac{k_{2}}{r}\right\rceil+2.

Note that in the last step, we use the fact that
\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{k-a}{r}\right\rceil
.
∎

In literature, there has been already some work on upper bounds on lengths of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes with small distances
[
9
]
and
[
6
]
. However, it is more difficult to upper bound lengths of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes with large distances.
Lemma
4.2
provides a way to upper bound lengths of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes with larger distance via Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes with smaller distances.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with disjoint recovery sets. If
d\equiv b\pmod{r+1}
and
n=\Omega(dr^{2})
, then there exists a Singleton-optimal

[n-d+b,k,b;r]
-locally repairable code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with disjoint recovery sets.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
4.2
to
{\mathcal{C}}
by letting
a=0
and we obtain a Singleton-optimal
[n-(r+1),k,d-(r+1);r]
-locally repairable code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with disjoint recovery sets.
Note that there still exists a recovery set of size
r+1
in
{\mathcal{C}}
as
r^{2}+2r<{n-d}
. After we do the same operations for
\frac{d-b}{r+1}
times, we obtain the desired result.
∎

4.2.

We now proceed to discuss the case where

r+1
divides
n
.

Based on Lemma
4.2
, we are able to derive a stronger upper bound.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d\geq r+2;r]
-locally repairable code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with disjoint recovery sets such that
(r+1)|n
,
d\equiv 1,2,3,4,5\pmod{r+1}
. Then,
n=O(q^{2})
.

Proof.

Note that our setting is
r>3
. The case
r\leq 3
is proved in our previous subsection.
Let
d\equiv a\pmod{r+1}
with
a=1,2,3,4,5
. By the Singleton-type bound in Lemma
2.4
, we have

k=\frac{rn}{r+1}-\left(d-2-\left\lfloor\frac{d-2}{r+1}\right\rfloor\right).

This implies
k\equiv 0\pmod{r}
when
a=1
and
k\equiv r-a+2\pmod{r}
otherwise. If
k\equiv 0\pmod{r}
, then
5-a<r
and we have
{\lceil\frac{k}{r}\rceil}=\lceil\frac{k-(5-a)}{r}\rceil
. If
k\equiv r-a+2\pmod{r}
, then
r-a+2-(5-a)=r-3\geq 1
. This implies that

{\lceil\frac{k}{r}\rceil}=\lceil\frac{k-(5-a)}{r}\rceil
.
Therefore, by Lemma
4.2
we obtain a Singleton-optimal
[n-(r+1),k-(5-a),d-(r+1)+5-a;r]
-locally repairable code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with disjoint recovery sets and
(r+1)|n
. Since
d-(r+1)+5-a\equiv 5\pmod{r+1}
, by Corollary
4.3
and Proposition
1.1
, we have
n-(r+1)=O(q^{2})
. The proof is completed.
∎

We now proceed to prove an upper bound for
d\pmod{r+1}>5
. Note that this implicitly assumes
r+1>5
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a Singleton-optimal
[n,k,d\geq r+2;r]
-locally repairable code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with disjoint recovery sets such that
(r+1)|n
. If
d\equiv 4t-i\pmod{r+1}

with
1\leqslant t\leqslant r/4
and
i\in\{-1,0,1,2\}
, then
n=O(q^{3-\frac{1}{t}})
.

Proof.

The proof is almost the same as Theorem
4.6
.
Let
d\equiv a\pmod{r+1}
with
a=4t-i
. By the Singleton-type bound in Lemma
2.4
, we have

k=\frac{rn}{r+1}-\left(d-2-\left\lfloor\frac{d-2}{r+1}\right\rfloor\right).

This implies
k\equiv r-a+2\pmod{r}
as
a\geq 2
.
One can easily verify that
\lceil\frac{k}{r}\rceil=\lceil\frac{k-(i+1)}{r}\rceil
as
r-a+2-(i+1)=r+1-4t\geq 1
.
Therefore, by Lemma
4.2
we obtain a Singleton-optimal
[n-(r+1),k-(i+1),d-(r+1)+i+1;r]
-locally repairable code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with disjoint recovery sets and

. Since

4.3
and Proposition
1.1
, the proof is completed.

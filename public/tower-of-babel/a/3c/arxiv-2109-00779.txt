Transmit Design for Joint MIMO Radar and Multiuser Communications with Transmit Covariance Constraint
Xiang Liu, Tianyao Huang, Yimin Liu

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the design of a multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) transmitter which simultaneously functions as a MIMO radar and a base station for downlink multiuser communications. In addition to a power constraint, we require the covariance of the transmit waveform be equal to a given optimal covariance for MIMO radar, to guarantee the radar performance. With this constraint, we formulate and solve the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) balancing problem for multiuser transmit beamforming via convex optimization. Considering that the interference cannot be completely eliminated with this constraint, we introduce dirty paper coding (DPC) to further cancel the interference, and formulate the SINR balancing and sum rate maximization problem in the DPC regime. Although both of the two problems are non-convex, we show that they can be reformulated to convex optimizations via the Lagrange and downlink-uplink duality. In addition, we propose gradient projection based algorithms to solve the equivalent dual problem of SINR balancing, in both transmit beamforming and DPC regimes. The simulation results demonstrate significant performance improvement of DPC over transmit beamforming, and also indicate that the degrees of freedom for the communication transmitter is restricted by the rank of the covariance.

II
Joint transmit design problems

Consider a joint transmitter which simultaneously functions as a
MIMO
radar transmitter and a base station for downlink multiuser communications.
In the transmitter, radar and communications share transmit signal, whose expression is given in Sec.
II-A
, following
[
16
]
.
Considering the radar performance, we introduce a transmit covariance constraint to the transmit signal in Sec.
II-B
.
With this radar constraint, we formulate the general transmit beamforming optimizations for communications in Sec.
II-C
, and extend the optimizations to the
DPC

.

II-A

Shared transmit signal

The transmitter is equipped with a transmit array with
M
antennas and sends independent communication symbols to
K
users, where
K\leq M
.
The average transmit power is
P
.
The transmit signal
\bm{x}(n)
for the shared transmit array is generated by the joint linear precoding scheme in
[
16
]
.
In particular,
\bm{x}(n)
is the sum of linear precoded radar waveforms and communication symbols, given by

\bm{x}(n)=\bm{W}_{r}\bm{s}(n)+\bm{W}_{c}\bm{c}(n),\ n=0,\ldots,N-1,

(1)

where
N
is the number of samples.
Here,
\bm{s}(n)=[s_{1}(n),\ldots,s_{M}(n)]^{T}
includes
M
orthogonal radar waveforms, and the
M\times M
matrix
\bm{W}_{r}
is the precoding matrix for radar
[
11
]
.
The orthogonality of radar waveforms means that
(1/N)\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\bm{s}(n)\bm{s}^{H}(n)=\bm{I}_{M}
.
The
K
parallel communication symbols to the users are contained in
\bm{c}(n)=\left[c_{1}(n),\ldots,c_{K}(n)\right]^{T}
, precoded by the
M\times K
matrix
\bm{W}_{c}
.

Following
[
25
,
2
,
16
]
, we rely on the following conditions to the communications symbols and radar waveforms:

(a)

The communication symbols to different users are mutually independent, have zero mean, and are normalized to have unit average power. Therefore,
\mathbf{E}(\bm{s}(n)\bm{s}^{H}(n))=\bm{I}_{M}
.

(b)

The radar waveforms and communication symbols are statistically independent.

Given
\{\bm{s}(n)\}
and
\{\bm{c}(n)\}
, transmit design for joint MIMO radar and communication becomes designing
\bm{W}_{r}
and
\bm{W}_{c}
.

II-B

Transmit covariance constraint for radar

The radar is monostatic so that the communication signals can also be used for target detection because they are completely known at the radar receiver.
Unlike phased array radars,
MIMO
radars transmit independent or partially correlated signals from the array elements.
The performance of
MIMO
radar highly depends on its transmit covariance

\bm{R}=\mathbf{E}\Big\{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\bm{x}(n)\bm{x}^{H}(n)\Big\}.

(2)

It was shown that the transmit beam pattern
[
18
]
, the angular estimation accuracy
[
21
,
20
]
and the detection performance of radars
[
21
]
is determined by
\bm{R}
.
Substituting (
1
) into (
2
),
\bm{R}
is given by

\bm{R}=\bm{W}_{r}\bm{W}_{r}^{H}+\bm{W}_{c}\bm{W}_{c}^{H}.

(3)

Given the average transmit power
P
,
\bm{R}
should obey
\mathrm{tr}(\bm{R})=P
.
To guarantee the radar performance, in a solely
MIMO
radar without communications, the transmit covariance is optimized, yielding
\bm{R}_{o}
, under a power constraint as in
[
18
,
20
,
19
]
.

Then in the joint design considered in this paper,
\bm{W}_{c}
and
\bm{W}_{r}
are constrained so that the obtained
\bm{R}
in (
3
) equals to
\bm{R}_{o}
. Hence, the joint radar and communications system achieves the optimal radar performance as the solely radar system without communications.
In the following, we design
\bm{W}_{c}
and
\bm{W}_{r}
by transmit beamforming and
DPC
, respectively, to optimize the communication performance under this constraint.

This approach is different from existing precoder design methods in
[
2
,
16
,
17
]
where they sacrifice the radar performance to achieve the desired
SINRs
for communications. Particularly in their methods,
\bm{W}_{c}
and
\bm{W}_{r}
are constrained to meet the minimum requirements on
SINRs
, and are optimized to improve the radar performance.

II-C

Transmit beamforming for multiuser communications

For downlink multiuser communication, transmit beamforming is performed to increase the signal power at intended users and reduce interference to non-intended users
[
22
,
25
]
.
Here, a vector
Gaussian broadcast channel (GBC)

[
41
]
is considered in which each user is equipped with a single receive antenna.
The channel is denoted by a
K\times M
matrix
\bm{H}
.
The channel output of the
GBC
is given by
[
16
]

\bm{r}(n)=\bm{H}\bm{x}(n)+\bm{v}(n)=\bm{H}\bm{W}_{c}\bm{c}(n)+\bm{H}\bm{W}_{r}\bm{s}(n)+\bm{v}(n).

(4)

Here, the
k
-th elements of
\bm{r}(n)
represents the received signal at the
k
-th user, and
\bm{v}(n)
is complex
AWGN
whose covariance is
\sigma^{2}\bm{I}_{K}
. For convenience, we let
\sigma^{2}=1
in the sequel.

In (
4
), each user receives the mixture of its own signals, the interference from other users, the radar signal and the noise.
Let
\bm{F}=\bm{H}\bm{W}_{c}
and
\bm{G}=\bm{H}\bm{W}_{r}
.
For the
k
-th user, the sum power of received signal, including both desired signal and interference is
\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\bm{F}_{k,i}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\bm{G}_{k,i}|^{2}=[\bm{H}\bm{R}_{o}\bm{H}^{H}]_{k,k}
, and the power of desired signal is
|\bm{F}_{k,k}|^{2}
.
Then, in the transmit beamforming regime, the
SINR
at the
k
-th user is given by
[
2
,
16
]

\mathrm{SINR}_{k}=\frac{|\bm{F}_{k,k}|^{2}}{\sum_{i\neq k}|\bm{F}_{k,i}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\bm{G}_{k,i}|^{2}+1},

(5)

for
k=1,\ldots,K
.
In the regime of
DPC
, the interference is treated differently. Hence, the definition of
SINR
is different, as will be introduced later in
II-D
.

Our goal is to maximize a utility function
f(\mathrm{SINR}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{SINR}_{K})
which is increasing in the
SINRs

[
25
]
, with the transmit covariance constraint.
The optimization problem is stated as

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{W}_{c},\bm{W}_{r}}\

\displaystyle\ f(\mathrm{SINR}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{SINR}_{K})

(6a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}\

\displaystyle\ \bm{R}_{o}=\bm{W}_{r}\bm{W}_{r}^{H}+\bm{W}_{c}\bm{W}_{c}^{H}.

(6b)

There are some common utility functions in existing works.
For
SINR
balancing, the utility functions is the worst
SINR
of the users, given by
[
24
]

f(\mathrm{SINR}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{SINR}_{K})=\min_{1\leq k\leq K}\ \mathrm{SINR}_{k}.

(7)

For sum rate maximization, the utility function is
[
24
,
25
]

f(\mathrm{SINR}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{SINR}_{K})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k}).

(8)

We then reformulate the optimization problems with respect to
\bm{F}
.
Letting
\bm{R}_{h}=\bm{H}\bm{R}_{o}\bm{H}^{H}
, it can be shown that the constraint in (
6b
) is equivalent to
[
42
]

\bm{F}\bm{F}^{H}\preceq\bm{R}_{h}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\bm{R}_{h}&\bm{F}\\
\bm{F}^{H}&\bm{I}_{K}\end{array}\right]\succeq 0,

(9)

which is convex.
Meanwhile, we let
s_{k}=([\bm{R}_{h}]_{k,k}+1)^{1/2}
, simplifying the
SINR
at the
k
-th as

\mathrm{SINR}_{k}=\frac{|\bm{F}_{k,k}|^{2}}{s_{k}^{2}-|\bm{F}_{k,k}|^{2}},\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(10)

Introducing the slack variables
\gamma_{k}\leq\mathrm{SINR}_{k}
, for
k=1,\ldots,K
, we reformulate (
6
) into an optimization with respective to
\bm{F}
and
\bm{\gamma}=[\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{K}]^{T}
:

\max_{\bm{F},\bm{\gamma}}\ f(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{K}),\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \eqref{eq-ff}\ \mathrm{and}\ |\bm{F}_{k,k}|\geq\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{k}}{1+\gamma_{k}}}s_{k},\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(11)

After solving (
11
), the optimum of the original problem in (
6
) can be computed by

\bm{W}_{c}=\bm{R}_{o}^{1/2}(\bm{H}\bm{R}_{o}^{1/2})^{\dagger}\bm{F},\ \bm{W}_{r}=(\bm{R}_{o}-\bm{W}_{c}\bm{W}_{c}^{H})^{1/2}.

(12)

For
SINR
balancing, the solver for (
11
) will be provided in Sec.
III
.

II-D

Dirty paper coding for multiuser communications

The numerical results in Sec.
VI-B
and in
[
16
]
indicate that the achievable
SINRs
with (
6
) and (
11
) may be low with the transmit covariance constraint, and some trade-off designs
[
2
,
8
,
16
]
were proposed to improve the
SINR
by relaxing the constraint.
To further improve the
SINRs
, one can perform non-linear precoding techniques, which eliminate the interference by encoding the communication signals to adapt the interference.
In particular, we consider
DPC

[
30
]
, which reveals that the capacity of an
AWGN
channel corrupted by interference equals to the capacity of an interference-free
AWGN
channel if the interference is known at the transmitter.

DPC
was applied to
GBC
to eliminate the effect of inter-user interference
[
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
, and was shown to be able to achieved the capacity region of
MIMO

GBC

[
35
]
.

We apply
DPC
to the
GBC
in (
4
) by serially encoding the source signal of each user.
The encoding operations are conducted in the order
\{1,\ldots,K\}
.
When performing
DPC
for the
k
-th user,
\{c_{1}(n)\},\ldots,\{c_{k-1}(n)\}
are already encoded while
\{c_{k+1}(n)\},\ldots,\{c_{K}(n)\}
are not encoded yet.
Thus, the interference from the
1,\ldots,(k-1)
-th user is known while the interference from the
k+1,\ldots,K
-th user is unknown at the transmitter.
The radar interference is also known at the transmitter.
Therefore, the effective
SINR
at the
k
-th user in the
DPC
regime is
[
32
]

\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{dpc}}=\frac{|\bm{F}_{k,k}|^{2}}{\sum_{i>k}|{\bm{F}}_{k,i}|^{2}+1},

(13)

for
k=1,\ldots,K
.

Comparing (
5
) and (
13
), it is observed that
DPC
improves the
SINR
compared with transmit beamforming by eliminating the interference.
It is worth noting that when
\bm{R}_{h}
is non-singular, one can perform
zero forcing (ZF)

DPC
, namely completely cancel the interference, by computing a lower triangular

\bm{F}
via the Cholesky decomposition
[
40
]
of
\bm{R}_{h}
, while
ZF
transmit beamforming is generally not applicable, since it requires
\bm{R}_{h}
be a diagonal matrix
[
16
]
.

Similarly, we maximize the utility function in the
DPC
regime.
The optimization problem is stated as

\max_{\bm{W}_{c},\bm{W}_{r}}\ \ f(\mathrm{SINR}_{1}^{\mathrm{dpc}},\ldots,\mathrm{SINR}_{K}^{\mathrm{dpc}}),\ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \eqref{eq-max-bf-b}.

(14)

We note that the
SINRs
in the
DPC
regime also explicitly depend on
\bm{F}
.
As in the transmit beamforming regime, we can reformulate (
14
) into an optimization with respect to
\bm{F}
.
Introducing the slack variables
\gamma_{k}\leq\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{dpc}}
, for
k=1,\ldots,K
, (
14
) is equivalent to

\max_{\bm{F},\bm{\gamma}}\ f(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{K}),\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \eqref{eq-ff}\ \mathrm{and}\ \frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}|\bm{F}_{k,k}|^{2}\geq\sum_{i>k}|{\bm{F}}_{k,i}|^{2}+1,\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(15)

In Sec.
IV
and
V
, we will consider the two criteria,
SINR
balancing and sum rate maximization, for
DPC
, repectively.
Prior to these
DPC
approaches, we first discuss below in Sec.
III
the
SINR
balancing in the transmit beamforming regime as a benchmark.

III
SINR balancing for transmit beamforming

In this section, we provide optimization methods to solve the SINR balancing problem in the transmit beamforming regime.
The optimization problem is (
11
) with the target function given by

f(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{K})=\min_{1\leq k\leq K}\,\gamma_{k}
.
We will first reformulate it into a linear conic optimization that can be effectively solved by on-the-shelf optimization solvers, shown in Sec.
III-A
.
Later in Sec.
III-B
, we will further provide a more efficient, dual program based method.

III-A

Conic optimization solution

While the
SINR
constraint in (
11
) are non-convex, we can convert it to a convex one, and hence reformulate (
11
) to a linear conic optimization.

Defining the balanced
SINR

\gamma=\min_{1\leq k\leq K}\,\gamma_{k}
, we reformulate the optimization into

\max_{\bm{F},\gamma}\ \gamma,\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \eqref{eq-ff}\ \mathrm{and}\ |\bm{F}_{k,k}|\geq\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}s_{k},\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(16)

Note that for a feasible
\bm{F}
to (
16
), rotating its
k
-th column by a scalar phase factor
e^{j\theta_{k}}
does not violate the feasibility
[
38
,
25
]
.
Therefore, we only need to consider
\bm{F}
with real diagonal elements.
Introducing a new variable
t=\sqrt{\gamma/(1+\gamma)}
, (
16
) is equivalent to the following:

\max_{\bm{F},t}\ t,\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \eqref{eq-ff}\ \mathrm{and}\ \Re\{\bm{F}_{k,k}\}\geq ts_{k},\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(17)

This is now solvable by linear conic programming.

The scale of (
17
) can be further reduced when
\bm{R}_{h}
is singular.
We let
r\leq K
be the rank of
\bm{R}_{h}
, and write the eigen decomposition of
\bm{R}_{h}
as
\bm{R}_{h}=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}\bm{U}^{H}
, where
\bm{\Sigma}_{r}
is a
r\times r
diagonal matrix and
\bm{U}^{H}\bm{U}=\bm{I}_{r}
.
Following the constraint in (
9
), we let
\bm{F}=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}\bm{F}_{u}
. Then (
17
) becomes an optimization with respect to
\bm{F}_{u}
:

\max_{\bm{F}_{u},t}\ t,\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \bm{F}_{u}\bm{F}_{u}^{H}\preceq\bm{I}_{r},\ \Re\{\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\bm{f}_{k}\}\geq ts_{k},\ k=1,\ldots,K,

(18)

where
\bm{f}_{k}
is the
k
-th column in
\bm{F}_{u}
and
\bm{u}_{k}^{H}
is the
k
-th row in
\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}
.
In (
18
), the dimension of
\bm{F}_{u}
reduces to
r\times K
if
r<K
.

III-B

Dual program solution

Here, we propose a simpler iteration method for (
18
) based on its Lagrange dual program. Define the Lagrange function
[
29
]
of (
18
) by

\mathcal{L}_{1}(\bm{F}_{u},t,\bm{Y},\bm{d})=t+\mathrm{tr}\left\{\bm{Y}(\bm{I}_{r}-\bm{F}_{u}\bm{F}_{u}^{H})\right\}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}\left(\Re\{\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\bm{f}_{k}\}-ts_{k}\right),

(19)

where
\bm{Y}\succeq 0
and
\bm{d}=[d_{1},\ldots,d_{K}]^{T}\geq 0
are associated dual variables.
We first derive the dual of (
18
) by maximizing the Lagrange function with respect to
\bm{F}_{u}
and
t
, and then show that the dual problem is equivalent to

\min_{\bm{d}\geq 0}\mathrm{tr}\Big\{(\bm{D}\bm{D}^{H})^{1/2}\Big\},\ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \bm{s}^{T}\bm{d}=1,

(20)

where
\bm{D}=\left[d_{1}\bm{u}_{1},\ldots,d_{K}\bm{u}_{K}\right]
and
\bm{s}=[s_{1},\ldots,s_{K}]^{T}
.
The derivations are given in Appendix
A
.

Generally, solving (
20
) can be simpler than (
18
) as the variable is only a
K
dimensional vector.
The constraints in (
20
) are all linear, and the objective function is actually the nuclear norm
[
43
]
of
\bm{D}
, which is convex in
\bm{d}
.
Nuclear norm minimization with linear constraints can be solved by optimization softwares such as CVX
[
44
,
45
]
.

We also propose a gradient projection based method to solve (
20
).
The key step is to calculate the descend direction at a point
\bm{d}
, denoted by
\bm{\Delta}_{d}(\bm{d})
, under the constraint.
Let
h(\bm{d})
denote the objective function in (
20
), which is differential if
\bm{d}
is strictly feasible, i.e.
\bm{d}>0
and
\bm{s}^{T}\bm{d}=1
.
The gradient of
h(\bm{d})
, denoted by
\nabla h(\bm{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
, is given by

[\nabla h(\bm{d})]_{k}=d_{k}^{-1}[(\bm{D}^{H}\bm{D})^{1/2}]_{k,k},

(21)

for
k=1,\ldots,K
.
Considering the constraints, we compute
\bm{\Delta}_{d}(\bm{d})
from a projection operation
[
46
]
:

\bm{\Delta}_{d}(\bm{d})=\mathcal{P}_{\Omega_{1}}\left(\bm{d}-\nabla h(\bm{d})\right)-\bm{d},

(22)

where
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega_{1}}(\cdot)
is the orthogonal projection onto the constraint set
\Omega_{1}=\{\bm{d}\,|\,\bm{d}\geq 0,\ \bm{s}^{T}\bm{d}=1\}
.
The projection does not have a close form expression.
Nevertheless, it can be computed by at most
K
loops.
The details on the computation of the projection is omitted here, and is given in Appendix
B
.

With the obtained
\bm{\Delta}_{d}(\bm{d})
from above loops,
gradient projection is performed via the following iterations:

\bm{d}^{(\ell+1)}:=\bm{d}^{(\ell)}+\alpha^{(\ell)}\bm{\Delta}_{d}(\bm{d}^{(\ell)}),\ \ell=0,1,\ldots

(23)

Here, the step size
\alpha^{(\ell)}\in(0,1)
can be determined by backtracking search
[
29
]
.
The initial value
\bm{d}^{(0)}
should be strictly feasible.
The iterations in (
23
) can be stopped if
\|\bm{\Delta}_{d}(\bm{d}^{(\ell)})\|_{2}
is small enough.

Then we compute
t
and
\bm{F}_{u}
for the primal problem.
Let
L
be the number of performed iterations at convergence.
With strong duality,
t
is given by
t:=h(\bm{d}^{(L)})
.
From the
Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT)
conditions
[
29
]
, it holds that
\bm{Y}\bm{F}_{u}=\frac{1}{2}\bm{D}
at the optimum of (
18
) and its dual, where
\bm{Y}=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{D}\bm{D}^{H})^{1/2}
according to Appendix
A
.
Thus, we compute
\bm{F}_{u}
via

\bm{F}_{u}:=\big[\bm{D}^{(L)}(\bm{D}^{(L)})^{H}\big]^{-1/2}\bm{D}^{(L)},

(24)

where
\bm{D}^{(L)}=\big[d_{1}^{(L)}\bm{u}_{1},\ldots,d_{K}^{(L)}\bm{u}_{K}\big]
.

IV
SINR balancing for DPC

In this section, we provide optimization methods to solve the
SINR
balancing problem in the
DPC
regime.
Like (
16
), we define the balanced
SINR

\gamma=\min_{1\leq k\leq K}\,\gamma_{k}
, and then the optimization is expressed as a problem with respect to
\bm{F}
and
\gamma
:

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{F},\gamma}\

\displaystyle\ \gamma,\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \bm{F}\bm{F}^{H}\preceq\bm{R}_{h},

(25a)

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{\gamma}|\bm{F}_{k,k}|^{2}\geq\sum_{i>k}|{\bm{F}}_{k,i}|^{2}+1,\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(25b)

In Sec.
IV-A
, we provide a power minimization based solution, which is solvable with some on-the-shelf optimization toolboxes. Later in Sec.
IV-B
, we give a more efficient solution based on its dual problem.

IV-A

Solution via power minimization

Unlike the
SINR
balancing for transmit beamforming, (
25
) is nonconvex since the constraint in (
25b
) is nonconvex.
Despite its non-convexity, (
25
) can be solved with a polynomial time complexity. Firstly, we formulate the corresponding power minimization problem
[
23
,
26
,
38
]
, which is a linear conic optimization, solvable with a polynomial time complexity. Based on the results, the optimizer of the original problem is then obtained with bisection search, also solvable with a polynomial time complexity.

To formulate the power minimization problem, we assume that
\gamma
is given. We then
adjust the transmit power by a scaling factor
\lambda\geq 0
, and thus the transmit power and covariance becomes
\lambda P
and
\lambda\bm{R}_{o}
, respectively.
Regarding
\lambda
as a variable, the minimal transmit power problem seeks for the minimal
\lambda
under the
SINR
constraints, given by

\displaystyle\min_{\lambda,\bm{F}}\

\displaystyle\ \lambda,\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \bm{F}\bm{F}^{H}\preceq\lambda\bm{R}_{h},

(26a)

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{\gamma}|\bm{F}_{k,k}|^{2}\geq\sum_{i>k}|{\bm{F}}_{k,i}|^{2}+1,\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(26b)

Let
\lambda^{*}(\gamma)
be the optimal value of (
26
).

The minimal transmit power problem is a solvable linear conic optimization.
In (
26
), the constraint in (
26a
) can be recast to a semi-definite constraint as in (
9
).
Similar to (
17
), we replace
|\bm{F}_{k,k}|
by
\Re\{\bm{F}_{k,k}\}
in (
26b
), and the constraint becomes second order cone constraints.
Therefore, the optimization in (
26
) can be effectively solved by linear conic solvers with a polynomial time complexity.

The relationship between the original problem (
25
) and (
26
) is built from this observation: The
SINR

\gamma
is achievable if and only if
\lambda^{*}(\gamma)\leq 1
, namely the minimal transmit power to achieve the
SINR

\gamma
is less than
P
. Note that
\lambda^{*}(\gamma)
increases monotonically in
\gamma
.
Therefore, the optimal
\gamma
in (
25
), denoted by
\gamma_{o}^{*}
, should satisfy
\lambda^{*}(\gamma_{o}^{*})=1
, and can be found by a bisection search
[
26
]
, which can be finished in polynomial time.

Similar to (
18
), (
26
) can be reformulated to an optimization with respect to
\bm{F}_{u}
.
Let
r=\mathrm{rank}(\bm{R}_{h})
,
\bm{R}_{h}=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}\bm{U}^{H}
be the eigen decomposition of
\bm{R}_{h}
, and
\bm{F}=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}\bm{F}_{u}
.
Then (
26
) is reformulated to

\displaystyle\min_{\lambda,\bm{F}_{u}}\

\displaystyle\ \lambda,\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \bm{F}_{u}\bm{F}_{u}^{H}\preceq\lambda\bm{I}_{r},

(27a)

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{\gamma}|\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\bm{f}_{k}|^{2}\geq\sum_{i>k}|\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\bm{f}_{i}|^{2}+1,\ k=1,\ldots,K,

(27b)

where
\bm{f}_{k}
is the
k
-th column in
\bm{F}_{u}
and
\bm{u}_{k}^{H}
is the
k
-th row in
\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}
.
When
r<K
, the dimension of
\bm{F}_{u}
is less than that of
\bm{F}
.

Although both linear conic optimization and bisection search are solvable and have polynomial time complexity, below we provide a more efficient approach for (
25
).

IV-B

Dual program solution

This section derives the dual problem of (
25
), and presents a gradient projection based method to solve it.

IV-B
1
Formulation of the dual problem

The optimal value of (
25
) is equal to that of the following dual program:

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{Y}\succeq 0}\min_{\bm{d}\geq 0,\gamma>0}\ \gamma,\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}

\displaystyle\geq 1,

(28a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\bm{Y})

\displaystyle=1,

(28b)

\displaystyle\bm{Y}+\sum_{i<k}d_{i}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{H}

\displaystyle\succeq\frac{1}{\gamma}d_{k}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H},\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(28c)

The proof starts from the Lagrange dual problem of the power minimization in (
27
), and derives the dual problems of (
27
) and (
25
), sequentially, as will be detailed in Appendix
C
.

Note that (
28
) is still nonconvex, since the constraints in (
28c
) are bilinear.
We propose an iterative method to solve it, summarized later in Sec.
IV-B4
, where the inner optimization problem is solved by fixed-point method and the outer is solved by gradient decent.

IV-B
2
Fixed-point iterations for inner minimization

Under a given
\bm{Y}
, the inner minimization of (
28
) is stated as

\min_{\bm{d}\geq 0,\gamma>0}\ \gamma,\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}\geq 1\ \mathrm{and}\ \eqref{eq-dual-cond-b}.

(29)

Define the index set
\mathcal{I}(\bm{Y})=\{k\,|\,\bm{u}_{k}\notin\mathcal{C}(\bm{Y}),\ 1\leq k\leq K\}
.
The following equations should hold at the optimum of (
29
):

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}=1,\ d_{k}=0\ \mathrm{for}\ k\in\mathcal{I}(\bm{Y}),

(30a)

\displaystyle\gamma=d_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H}(\bm{Y}+\sum_{i<k}d_{i}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{H})^{\dagger}\bm{u}_{k}\ \mathrm{for}\ k\notin\mathcal{I}(\bm{Y}).

(30b)

For the case of
k\notin\mathcal{I}(\bm{Y})
, according to
[
26
,
38
]
we compute
\{d_{k}\}
by the fixed-point iterations given in Algorithm
1
, which converges rapidly in practice. After
\{d_{k}\}
is obtained,
\gamma
can be computed via (
30b
).

Algorithm 1

Fix-point algorithm to solve (
29
)

1:

Initialization:
\{d_{k}>0\}
.

2:

repeat

3:

Compute
\hat{d}_{k}
from (
30b
), for all
k\notin\mathcal{I}(\bm{Y})
:

\hat{d}_{k}\leftarrow\Big[\bm{u}_{k}^{H}(\bm{Y}+\sum_{i<k}d_{i}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{H})^{\dagger}\bm{u}_{k}\Big]^{-1}.

4:

Store the value of
\{d_{k}\}
, for all
k\notin\mathcal{I}(\bm{Y})
:
d_{k}^{\prime}\leftarrow d_{k}
.

5:

Normalize
\{d_{k}\}
so that their sum is
1
:

d_{k}\leftarrow\frac{\hat{d}_{k}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\hat{d}_{k}},\ \forall k\notin\mathcal{I}(\bm{Y}).

6:

until

\sum_{k\notin\mathcal{I}(\bm{Y})}|d_{k}-d_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}<\varepsilon
.

6:

Solution of
d_{k}
, for
k\notin\mathcal{I}(\bm{Y})
.

IV-B
3
Gradient derivation for the outer minimization

Writing the optimal value of (
29
) as
\gamma_{o}(\bm{Y})
, we reformulate (
28
) into an optimization with respect to
\bm{Y}
:

\min_{\bm{Y}\succeq 0}\ \gamma_{o}(\bm{Y}),\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \eqref{eq-dual-cond-a}.

(31)

We observe that, when
\bm{Y}\succ 0
,
\gamma_{o}(\bm{Y})
is a convex and differential function.
Therefore, (
31
) can be solved via gradient projection.

We first give the expression of the gradient
\nabla\gamma_{o}(\bm{Y})
below, which we note that is non-trivial because there is no analytical expression of the objective function
\gamma_{o}(\bm{Y})
. For readability, we leave the derivation in Appendix
D
.
We define

\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{*}(\bm{Y})=(\bm{Y}+\sum_{i<k}d_{i}^{*}(\bm{Y})\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{H})^{-1}\bm{u}_{k},

(32)

where
{d_{k}^{*}(\bm{Y})}
is the solution of (
29
) and (
30
), and a
K\times K
lower triangular matrix
\bm{A}
by

\bm{A}_{k,i}=-d_{k}^{*}(\bm{Y})|\bm{u}_{i}^{H}\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{*}(\bm{Y})|^{2},\ \bm{A}_{k,k}=\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{*}(\bm{Y}),

(33)

for
1\leq k\leq K
and
1\leq i<k
.
Letting
\bm{a}=(\bm{A}^{-1})^{T}\bm{1}_{K}
, we have

\nabla\gamma_{o}(\bm{Y})=-\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}d_{k}^{*}(\bm{Y})\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{*}(\bm{Y})[\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{*}(\bm{Y})]^{H}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}},

(34)

where
a_{k}
is the
k
-th element in
\bm{a}
.

Under the constraint (
28b
), the descend direction at a point
\bm{Y}\succ 0
is computed from a projection operation
[
46
]
:

\bm{\Delta}_{y}(\bm{Y})=\mathcal{P}_{\Omega_{2}}\left(\bm{Y}-\nabla\gamma_{o}(\bm{Y})\right)-\bm{Y}.

(35)

where
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega_{2}}(\cdot)
is the orthogonal projection onto the constraint set
\Omega_{2}=\{\bm{Y}\,|\,\bm{Y}\succeq 0,\ \mathrm{tr}(\bm{Y})=1\}
.
The projection does not have a close form expression, but can be computed by at most
K
loops; See Appendix
E
.

IV-B
4
Summary of the iterations for the dual problem

With the initial value
\bm{Y}^{(0)}
, which should be strictly feasible, namely
\bm{Y}^{(0)}\succ 0
and
\mathrm{tr}(\bm{Y}^{(0)})=1
, we update
\bm{Y}
and
\bm{d}
by the following iterations for
\ell=0,1,\ldots

(a)

Update
\bm{Y}
via
\bm{Y}^{(\ell+1)}:=\bm{Y}^{(\ell)}+\alpha^{(\ell)}\bm{\Delta}_{y}(\bm{Y}^{(\ell)}).

(b)

Update
\bm{d}
and
\gamma
via (
30
), yielding
\bm{d}^{(\ell+1)}:=\bm{d}^{*}(\bm{Y}^{(\ell+1)})
and
\gamma^{(\ell+1)}:=\gamma_{o}(\bm{Y}^{(\ell+1)})
.

Here, the step size
\alpha^{(\ell)}\in(0,1)
can be determined by backtracking search
[
29
]
.
The iterations of gradient descend can be stopped if
\|\bm{\Delta}_{y}(\bm{Y}^{(\ell)})\|_{F}
is small enough.
Let
L
be the number of iterations at convergence.
After (
28
) is solved, the balanced
SINR
is given by
\gamma:=\gamma^{(L)}
.

IV-B
5
The solution for the primal problem

With the solution of
\gamma_{o}
obtained by the above dual problem, we then compute the solution of the primal problem in (
25
). In this regard, we first compute the optimum
\bm{F}_{u}
for (
27
). Then, the optimal
\bm{F}
in (
25
) is given by
\bm{F}:=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}\bm{F}_{u}
.

The relationship between the dual and primal problems is stated as follow. Given
\gamma=\gamma_{o}^{*}
, the optimum of (
28
) should also be the solution of the dual problem of (
27
). The dual of (
27
) is provided in (
65
) of the Appendix
C
.
From the
KKT
conditions, the optimal solution of (
27
) and (
65
) obey

\big(\bm{Y}+\sum_{i<k}d_{i}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{H}\big)\bm{f}_{k}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}d_{k}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\bm{f}_{k},\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(36)

From (
36
), we compute
\bm{F}_{u}
by
\bm{f}_{k}:=\sqrt{b_{k}}\tilde{\bm{f}}_{k}
, where

\tilde{\bm{f}}_{k}:=\big(\bm{Y}^{(L)}+\sum_{i<k}d_{i}^{(L)}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{H}\big)^{\dagger}\bm{u}_{k},

and
b_{k}
is a real factor, for
k=1,\ldots,K
.
The factors
\{b_{k}\}
can be determined by solving the following
K

SINR
equations:

\frac{1}{\gamma^{(L)}}\left|\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\bm{f}}_{k}\right|^{2}b_{k}-\sum_{i>k}\left|\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\tilde{\bm{f}}_{i}\right|^{2}b_{i}=1,\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(37)

V
Sum rate maximization for DPC

In this section, we solve the sum rate maximization in the DPC regime, given by

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{F},\bm{\gamma}}\

\displaystyle\ \sum_{k=1}^{K}\log(1+\gamma_{k}),\ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \bm{F}\bm{F}^{H}\preceq\bm{R}_{h}\ \mathrm{and}

(38a)

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}|\bm{F}_{k,k}|^{2}\geq\sum_{i>k}|{\bm{F}}_{k,i}|^{2}+1,\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(38b)

This problem is from (
15
) with the target function being the sum rate.
Since the
SINR
constraint is non-convex, sum rate maximization is non-convex and hard to solve.

We solve (
38
) via the well-known downlink-uplink duality
[
33
,
32
,
38
]
, which introduces a dual uplink
multiple access channel (MAC)
that has the same achievable rate region as the downlink
GBC
.
Then the problem becomes the sum rate maximization for the
MAC
. From the duality, we are able to compute the optimal
\bm{F}
via an equivalent convex optimization.

V-A

Optimization reformulation based on downlink-uplink duality

This section first introduces the signal model of the dual uplink
MAC
with respect to the original downlink model, given in Sec.
V-A1
.
Based on such dual uplink
MAC
, we formulate the sum rate maximization problem in Sec.
V-A2
.
Then in Sec.
V-A3
, we illustrate the downlink-uplink duality by showing that the uplink
MAC
and the downlink
GBC
have the same achievable rate region.
Based on the duality, Sec.
V-A4
provides the solutions to the original downlink transmit design problem.

V-A
1
Dual uplink
MAC
model

Consider a uplink
MAC
, in which
K
users simultaneously transmit to a base station with
r
antennas.
Each user is equipped with a single transmit antenna.
The channel is
\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}\bm{U}^{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times K}
. The received signal is

\bm{y}_{\mathrm{ul}}=\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}\bm{U}^{H}\bm{x}_{\mathrm{ul}}+\bm{v}_{\mathrm{ul}},

(39)

where
\bm{x}_{\mathrm{ul}}=[x_{\mathrm{ul},1},\ldots,x_{\mathrm{ul},K}]^{T}
includes the transmit signal of the users, and
\bm{v}_{\mathrm{ul}}
is additive Gaussian noise that has uncertain covariance
\bm{Y}
constrained by
\mathrm{tr}(\bm{Y})=1
, analogy to (
28b
).
The transmit power of the
k
-th user, denoted by
d_{k}
, for
k=1,\ldots,K
, should be optimized, under the sum power constraint
\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}\leq 1
.

For the
k
-th user, the receiver applies a linear filter
\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}
, and the output is

\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{H}\bm{y}_{\mathrm{ul}}=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{H}\bm{u}_{i}x_{\mathrm{ul},i}+\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{H}\bm{v}_{\mathrm{ul}}.

(40)

Corresponding to the
DPC
strategy in the downlink regime, we use successive cancellation with the reverse order
\{K,\ldots,1\}

[
32
]
so that the signal from the
k+1,\ldots,K
-th user can be subtracted when decoding for the
k
-th user.
Therefore, the
SINR
for the
k
-th user is

\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{mac}}=\frac{d_{k}|\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{H}\bm{u}_{k}|^{2}}{\sum_{i<k}d_{i}|\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{H}\bm{u}_{i}|^{2}+\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}^{H}\bm{Y}\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}}.

(41)

The sum rate is then given by
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{mac}})
.
Below, we formulate the sum rate maximization for the
MAC
with respect to the filters
\{\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}\}
, the transmit power
\bm{d}:=[d_{1},\dots,d_{K}]^{T}
, and the noise covariance
\bm{Y}
.

V-A
2
Sum rate maximization formulation

The maximum sum rate is defined with regard to the worst case of noise: seeking for the noise variance
\bm{Y}
that most worsens the sum rate. Therefore, we only need to consider non-singular
\bm{Y}
, i.e.,
\bm{Y}\succ 0
, because the sum rate can be infinity when
\bm{Y}
is singular.

When
\bm{Y}
is non-singular, the
minimum mean square error (MMSE)
filter that maximizes the output
SINR
for the
k
-th user is given by
[
38
]

\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}=(\bm{Y}+\sum_{i<k}d_{i}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{H})^{-1}\bm{u}_{k},\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(42)

Correspondingly, the achieved
SINR
for the
k
-th user in (
41
) becomes

\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{mac}}=d_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H}(\bm{Y}+\sum_{i<k}d_{i}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{H})^{-1}\bm{u}_{k},\ k=1,\ldots,K,

(43)

and further the sum rate is written as

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{mac}})=\log\big|\bm{Y}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\big|-\log\big|\bm{Y}\big|.

(44)

Now we write the sum rate maximization for the dual
MAC
as

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{Y}\succeq 0}

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{d}\geq 0}\ \log\big|\bm{Y}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\big|-\log\big|\bm{Y}\big|

(45a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}\ \mathrm{tr}(\bm{Y})=1,\ \sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}=1.

(45b)

In (
45
), all the constraints are convex.
The objective function, denoted by
g(\bm{Y},\bm{d})
, is convex in
\bm{d}
and is concave in
\bm{Y}
.
Therefore, (
45
) is a convex-concave saddle point problem.
Solutions to this problem will be discussed later in Sec.
V-B
.

V-A
3
Downlink-uplink duality

The downlink-uplink duality is established from the power minimization problem and its dual.
In (
27
), we assign individual
SINR
thresholds to the users, and the power minimization becomes:

\min_{\lambda,\bm{F}_{u}}\ \lambda,\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \bm{F}_{u}\bm{F}_{u}^{H}\preceq\lambda\bm{I}_{r},\ \frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}|\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\bm{f}_{k}|^{2}\geq\sum_{i>k}|\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\bm{f}_{i}|^{2}+1,\ k=1,\ldots,K,

(46)

where
\gamma_{k}
is the given
SINR
for the
k
-th user, for
k=1,\ldots,K
.
Correspondingly, the Lagrange dual problem becomes:

\max_{\bm{Y}\succeq 0}\ \max_{\bm{d}\geq 0}\ \sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k},\ \ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \eqref{eq-dual-cond-a},\ \mathrm{and}\ \bm{Y}+\sum_{i<k}d_{i}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{H}\succeq\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}d_{k}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H},\ k=1,\ldots,K.

(47)

We denote the optimal value of (
46
) and (
47
) by
\lambda^{*}(\bm{\gamma})
and
\lambda^{*}_{\mathrm{dual}}(\bm{\gamma})
, respectively, where
\bm{\gamma}=[\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{K}]^{T}
.

Recall that for the
GBC
, the
SINRs

\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{K}
are achievable if and only if
\lambda^{*}(\bm{\gamma})\leq 1
.
Meanwhile, we note that the inner maximization in (
47
) is equivalent to
[
38
]

\min_{\bm{d}\geq 0,\{\hat{\bm{f}}_{k}\}}\ \sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k},\ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{mac}}\geq\gamma_{k},\ k=1,\ldots,K,

(48)

which finds the minimal transmit power of the
MAC
to achieve the
SINRs
under a given
\bm{Y}
.
Further, the optimal value of the outer maximization in (
47
) is the worst-case minimal transmit power under all possible
\bm{Y}
constrained by (
28b
).
Therefore,
\lambda^{*}_{\mathrm{dual}}(\bm{\gamma})
gives the minimal transmit power to achieve the
SINRs

\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{K}
in the
MAC
.

It is worth noting that the maximized sum rate in the

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, we consider the transmit design of a joint
MIMO
radar and downlink multiuser communications system, in which the communication performance is optimized under a transmit covariance constraint from radar.
In particular, we formulate the
SINR
balancing problem in both transmit beamforming and
DPC
regimes, and the sum rate maximization in the
DPC
regime.
Further, we proposed methods to solve these problems via convex optimization.
Despite the low complexity of transmit beamforming, the achievable
SINR
via transmit beamforming may be low even if the transmit
SNR
is high.
As the theoretically optimal scheme for multiuser precoding,
DPC
has a impressive performance gain over transmit beamforming, with increased complexity for encoding and optimization.
In the simulations, it is observed that the degrees of freedom for the communication transmitter is restricted by the rank of the transmit covariance.

\lambda_{\mathrm{dual}}^{*}(\gamma_{o}^{*})=1
.
In other words,
\gamma_{o}^{*}
is the minimal
\gamma\geq 0
so that
\lambda_{\mathrm{dual}}^{*}(\gamma)\geq 1
.
Note that
\lambda_{\mathrm{dual}}^{*}(\gamma)\geq 1
means there exists a pair of feasible solution
\bm{Y},\bm{d}
in (
65
) satisfying
\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}\geq 1
.
Therefore,
\gamma_{o}^{*}
is equal to the optimal value of the dual problem in (
28

To derive the gradient, we note that

Appendix E
Computation of the projection onto
\Omega_{2}

The projection of a Hermitian matrix
\bm{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
onto
\Omega_{2}
is

\arg\min_{{\bm{X}}}\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{Y}-{\bm{X}}\|_{F}^{2},\ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \mathrm{tr}({\bm{X}})=1,\ {\bm{X}}\succeq 0.

(70)

To solve (
70
), we write the eigen decomposition of
\bm{Y}
as
\bm{Y}=\bm{V}\bm{\Sigma}_{y}\bm{Y}^{H}
, where
\bm{V}
is a
r\times r
unitary matrix and
\bm{\Sigma}_{y}
is diagonal.
Letting
\bm{\Sigma}_{x}=\bm{V}^{H}{\bm{X}}\bm{V}
, the optimization in (
70
) is reformulated to

\min_{\bm{\Sigma}_{x}}\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{\Sigma}_{y}-\bm{\Sigma}_{x}\|_{F}^{2},\ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \mathrm{tr}({\bm{\Sigma}}_{x})=1,\ {\bm{\Sigma}}_{x}\succeq 0.

(71)

It can be observed that
\bm{\Sigma}_{x}
should be diagonal at the optimum.
We let
y_{k}
and
x_{k}
be the
(k,k)
-th elements in
\bm{\Sigma}_{y}
and
\bm{\Sigma}_{x}
, respectively.
Then (
71
) is equivalent to

\min_{\bm{x}}\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{y}-\bm{x}\|_{2}^{2},\ \mathrm{s.t.}\ \bm{1}_{r}^{T}\bm{x}=1,\ \bm{x}\geq 0.

(72)

where
\bm{x}=[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]^{T}
and
\bm{y}=[y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}]^{T}
.
Here, (
72
) has the same form as the optimization in (
59
), and can be solved with no more than
K
loops.
Once the optimal
\bm{x}
is obtained, the projection is given by
\bm{V}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{x})\bm{V}^{H}
.

Appendix F
Proof for Theorem
1

In this proof, we verify that
\bm{Y}^{*}
and
\bm{d}^{*}
meet the
KKT
condition of (
45
), which is stated as

\displaystyle({\bm{Y}}^{*}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}^{*}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H})^{-1}-[\bm{Y}]^{*-1}+\nu\bm{I}_{r}=\bm{0},

(73a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}({\bm{Y}^{*}})=1,\ {\bm{Y}}^{*}\succ 0,

(73b)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{j}^{H}({\bm{Y}}^{*}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}^{*}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H})^{-1}\bm{u}_{j}-\eta+\varphi_{j}=0,

(73c)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}^{*}=1,\ d^{*}_{j}\geq 0,\ \varphi_{j}\geq 0,\ \varphi_{j}d_{j}^{*}=0,

(73d)

for
j=1,\ldots,K
.
Here,
\nu
,
\eta
and
\{\varphi_{k}\}
are the dual variables associated with the constraints
\mathrm{tr}(\bm{Y})=1
,
\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}=1
and
\bm{d}\geq 0
, respectively, and their values are given by

\eta=\bm{1}_{K}^{T}\bm{\phi},\ \nu=\eta,\ \varphi_{k}=\eta\Big(1-\bm{u}_{k}^{H}{\bm{Z}}^{*}\bm{u}_{k}\Big),

(74)

for
k=1,\ldots,K
.

In the following, we check the conditions in (
73
) one by one.
First, we point out an important relationship between
\bm{d}^{*}
,
{\bm{Y}}^{*}
and
{\bm{Z}}^{*}
.
From (
52
), one has

(1/\eta)[{\bm{Z}}^{*}]^{-1}=\bm{Y}^{*}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}^{*}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H}.

(75)

Then

\displaystyle({\bm{Y}}^{*}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}^{*}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H})^{-1}-[\bm{Y}^{*}]^{-1}=\eta{\bm{Z}}^{*}-[\bm{Y}^{*}]^{-1}=-\nu\bm{I}_{r},

i.e. (
73a
) holds, and

\bm{u}_{j}^{H}({\bm{Y}}^{*}+\sum_{ki=1}^{K}d_{k}^{*}\bm{u}_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H})^{-1}\bm{u}_{j}=\eta\bm{u}_{j}^{H}\bm{Z}^{*}\bm{u}_{j}=\eta-\varphi_{k},

i.e. (
73c
) holds.
In (
73b
),
{\bm{Y}}^{*}\succ 0
is trivial, and

\mathrm{tr}({\bm{Y}}^{*})=\frac{1}{\eta}\mathrm{tr}((\bm{Z}^{*}+\bm{I}_{r})^{-1})=\frac{r}{\eta}-\frac{1}{\eta}\mathrm{tr}((\bm{Z}^{*}+\bm{I}_{r})^{-1}\bm{Z}^{*}).

(76)

Multiply the left and right side of (
52

\mathrm{tr}((\bm{Z}^{*}+\bm{I}_{r})^{-1}\bm{Z}^{*})=r-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\phi_{k}\bm{u}_{k}^{H}\bm{Z}^{*}\bm{u}_{k}.

Substituting (

73b

Finally we show that (

The first three conditions in (

According to (
52

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}d_{j}^{*}=\phi_{j}(1-\bm{u}_{j}^{H}{\bm{Z}}^{*}\bm{u}_{j})=0.

(79)

Therefore (
73d
) holds and the proof is completed.

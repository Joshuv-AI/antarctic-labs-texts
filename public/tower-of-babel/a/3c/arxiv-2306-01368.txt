Logarithmic Prismatic Cohomology via Logarithmic THH
Federico Binda, Tommy Lundemo, Doosung Park, Paul Arne Østvær

Abstract
. Inspired by Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze’s work on THH {\rm THH} , we define Nygaard-completed log prismatic cohomology based on log topological Hochschild homology via filtrations on log THH {\rm THH} and its variants. Moreover, of independent interest, we describe log THH {\rm THH} for quasiregular semiperfectoid pre-log rings as a 1 1 -parameter deformation of ordinary, non-logarithmic Hochschild homology.



1.
Introduction

The work of Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze
[
3
]
provides filtrations on variants of topological Hochschild homology,
in analogy with the motivic filtrations on algebraic
K
-theory
[
12
]
,

[
25
]
,

[
41
]
.
Control over the graded pieces allows one to define new integral cohomology theories,
such as prismatic and syntomic cohomology, for
p
-adic schemes.
These are computable invariants:
Antieau–Krause–Nikolaus
[
1
]
reduces the computation of topological cyclic homology
(and hence algebraic
K
-theory
[
33
,
11
]
)
of rings like
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n}
to the computation of prismatic cohomology,
for which they provide an algorithm.

An example more in the spirit of the present paper is the original arithmetic application of
[
3
]
,
in which étale, crystalline, and de Rham cohomology are exhibited as specializations of a common
cohomology theory
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
.
The results of this paper provide a first step in establishing matching comparison results in the context of
logarithmic geometry using techniques from homotopy theory (see
[
22
]
for a different approach). The interest in such results is justified because a much wider class of objects behaves
“as if they were smooth” in logarithmic geometry.
Standard examples are varieties with semi-stable reduction.

1.1.
Log topological Hochschild homology

We begin by quickly reviewing the construction of (Nygaard-complete) prismatic cohomology, following
[
3
]
.
It is a
p
-adic cohomology theory built in several steps,
starting with the case of
quasiregular semiperfectoid
rings: this is a convenient class of rings that contains perfectoid rings, but also the tensor products of perfections of smooth algebras over a field of positive characteristic.
In this case,
one defines

\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{S}:=\pi_{0}{\rm TC}^{-}(S;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}),

where
{\rm TC}^{-}(S;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}):={\rm THH}(S;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{hS^{1}}
is the homotopy fixed points of the
natural
S^{1}
-action on topological Hochschild homology
[
16
]
.
One feature of quasiregular semiperfectoid rings is that
{\rm THH}(S;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
is
even
,
i.e., its odd homotopy groups vanish,
and
\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{S}
is complete for the filtration of the degenerate homotopy fixed point
spectral sequence

E_{2}^{s,t}=H^{s}(S^{1},\pi_{t}{\rm THH}(S;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}))\implies\pi_{t-s}{\rm TC}^{-}(S;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}).

By definition, this is the
Nygaard filtration
on
\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{S}
.
The relationship between this construction and the site-theoretic definition of prismatic cohomology

{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{S}
is the content of
[
4
, Theorem 13.1]
,
where Bhatt and Scholze exhibit
\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{S}
as the completion of
{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{S}
with respect to an
independently defined Nygaard filtration, thus justifying a posteriori the terminology. The
unfolding
equivalence of
[
3
, Proposition 4.31]
extends the definition of
\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{A}
from quasiregular semiperfectoid rings to quasisyntomic rings.

The key construction carried out in this paper is that of a logarithmic version of the Nygaard-complete
prismatic cohomology
\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{S}
of
[
3
]
.
For this,
we shall replace topological Hochschild homology with
log topological Hochschild homology

in the sense of
[
37
]
.

Let us review the basics of this construction.
A
pre-log ring

(A,M,\alpha)
consists of a commutative ring
A
,
a commutative monoid
M
,
and a map of commutative monoids
\alpha\colon M\to(A,\cdot)
.
In this paper,
pre-log rings will play the role of affine schemes in logarithmic geometry
[
21
]
;
we refer to
[
6
, Section 7.4]
for further discussion on this point.
The
log
{\rm THH}
of
(A,M)
, which we review in Section
3
,
is a commutative
A
-algebra in spectra
[
37
, Definition 8.11]
.
As we explain in Section
3.8
,
log
{\rm THH}
carries a cyclotomic structure in the sense of Nikolaus–Scholze
[
30
]
,
and hence we obtain definitions of log
{\rm TC}^{-},{\rm TP}
, and
{\rm TC}
.

One goal of this paper is to define logarithmic analogs of some key notions in
[
3
]
,
such as quasisyntomic and quasiregular semiperfectoid,
that allow us to define Nygaard-completed log prismatic cohomology in a manner analogous to that of
loc. cit.

While this leads to analogs of the results of
[
3
]
, it also sheds new light on logarithmic
{\rm THH}
.
As we explain in further detail in Section
1.5
,
the results of this paper show that log
{\rm THH}
can be thought of as a
1
-parameter deformation of
ordinary, non-logarithmic Hochschild homology for quasiregular semiperfectoid pre-log rings.

1.2.
A log variant of a result of Antieau

Perhaps the easiest example of a filtration in the spirit of
[
3
]
is the

Hochschild–Kostant–Rosenberg-filtration

{\rm Fil}_{\rm HKR}^{\bullet}{\rm HH}(A/R)
on ordinary
Hochschild homology
{\rm HH}(A/R)
,
with graded pieces given by the derived exterior powers of the algebraic cotangent complex
{\mathbb{L}}_{A/R}
.
We obtained in
[
6
]
a similar filtration for log Hochschild homology and Gabber’s cotangent complex.
These constructions are briefly reviewed in Section
2
.

Let
R\to A
be a map of commutative rings.
Generalizing a result of Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze
[
3
, Theorem 1.17]
from the
p
-complete setting,
Antieau
[
2
]
constructed a filtration on periodic cyclic homology
{\rm HP}(A/R)
with graded pieces
given by shifts of the Hodge-completed derived de Rham complex
\widehat{\Omega}_{A/R}
.

Antieau’s approach is appealing due to its generality; the only necessary input specific to the case of (the
S^{1}
-Tate construction of) Hochschild homology are

(1)

the
{\rm HKR}
-theorem, which gives rise to the complete, exhaustive filtration
F^{\bullet}_{\rm HKR}{\rm HC}^{-}(A/R)
, called the
HKR-filtration
, on negative cyclic homology;

(2)

the compatibility of the differential in the resulting chain complexes
[
2
, Example 2.4]

0\xrightarrow{}\Omega^{n}_{A/R}\xrightarrow{}\Omega^{n+1}_{A/R}\xrightarrow{}\cdots

with the de Rham differential; and

(3)

fpqc descent for Hochschild homology and its variants, in order to extend the result from the affine case to any qcqs
R
-scheme.

Due to our earlier work
[
6
]
and the results of the present paper, we now have analogs of all of the above facts in the context of logarithmic geometry. More specifically, if
(R,P)\to(A,M)
is a map of pre-log rings, then

(1)

there is an HKR-theorem for log Hochschild homology
{\rm HH}((A,M)/(R,P))
, which gives rise to the complete, exhaustive
HKR-filtration

F^{\bullet}_{\rm HKR}{\rm HC}^{-}((A,M)/(R,P))

on log negative cyclic homology (
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
, see Theorem
2.21
);

(2)

the chain complex resulting from the construction of
[
2
, Example 2.4]
is of the form

0\to\Omega^{n}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}\xrightarrow{}\Omega^{n+1}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}\xrightarrow{}\cdots

and the differential is the log de Rham differential (Proposition
2.23
); and

(3)

a descent property for log Hochschild homology and its variants
allows us to extend the result from the “log affine” case under suitable hypotheses
(Proposition
2.24
).

We refer to
[
6
, Section 7.4]
for further discussion of the globalization procedure since we only construct
the filtration in the “log affine” affine case for the present exposition.
Defining derived log de Rham cohomology as in
[
5
, Section 6]
,
Antieau’s argument applies to obtain the following result,
whose proof we also summarize at the end of Section
2
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(R,P)\to(A,M)
be a map of pre-log rings. The log negative and periodic cyclic homologies

{\rm HC}^{-}((A,M)/(R,P)),\quad{\rm HP}((A,M)/(R,P))

admit functorial, complete, decreasing filtrations with graded pieces equivalent to

\widehat{L\Omega}^{\geq n}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}[2n],\quad\widehat{L\Omega}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}[2n],

respectively. Each filtered piece is itself equipped with a compatible filtration, which induces the Hodge filtration on the graded pieces under the equivalences above.

The filtrations on
{\rm HC}^{-}((A,M)/(R,P))
and
{\rm HP}((A,M)/(R,P))
are exhaustive if the Gabber cotangent complex
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
has
{\rm Tor}
-amplitude in
[-1,0]
.

We will give a full, independent proof of Theorem
1.3
in the
p
-complete setting in Section
5
, following the proof strategy of
[
3
, Section 5]
.

1.4.
The log quasisyntomic site

When constructing filtrations on variants of log
topological
Hochschild homology,
it is convenient to work with the log quasisyntomic site for pre-log rings.
Its definition and main properties are discussed in Section
4.8
.
For
(A,M)
a log quasisyntomic pre-log ring, the log quasisyntomic covers turns
the category of log quasisyntomic
(A,M)
-algebras into a site.
This construction is inspired by and analogous to the non-log situation in
[
3
]
.

There is a convenient basis for the topology on the log quasisyntomic site given by

log quasiregular semiperfectoid rings
(see Definition
4.12
).
As in the non-log setting,
there exists a surjection from some perfectoid ring onto the underlying commutative ring of
every log quasiregular semiperfectoid ring
(S,Q)
.
In addition, the commutative monoid
Q
is semiperfect;
its
p
-power map is surjective.

Examples of log quasisyntomic morphisms include
morphisms that are smooth and lci in the eyes of the Gabber cotangent complex. Under integrality hypotheses, this recovers the usual notion of log smooth, see e.g.
[
6
, Section 4]
.
A version of the log quasisyntomic site is also independently pursued in the work of
Koshikawa–Yao
[
22
]
: up to minor choices of technical conventions, the constructions agree.

1.5.
Deforming log
{\rm THH}
to ordinary Hochschild homology

One important step in constructing the filtrations of
[
3
]
is analyzing the topological Hochschild homology of
quasiregular semiperfectoid rings
S
. In turn, this is achieved using the cofiber sequence

(1.1)

{\rm THH}(S;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})[2]\xrightarrow{u}{\rm THH}(S;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{}{\rm HH}(S/R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

of
[
3
, Theorem 6.7]
, exhibiting
{\rm THH}
as a
1
-parameter deformation of
relative

{\rm HH}
, where
u\in\pi_{2}{\rm THH}(R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
is a generator for
\pi_{*}{\rm THH}(R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\cong R[u]
,
with
R\to S
a chosen surjection from some perfectoid ring
R
.

If
(S,Q)
is a log quasiregular semiperfectoid ring,
there exists a surjection
R\to S
,
where
R
is a perfectoid ring.
The conditions on the underlying monoid
Q
imply that the canonical map from its
tilt

Q^{\flat}
to
Q

is a surjection.
In particular,
the canonical map
R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle\to S
is a surjection from a perfectoid ring,
which can be used to define
u
in the cofiber sequence (
1.1
).
Here
R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle
is a standard notation for the
p
-complete version of the monoid ring

R[Q^{\flat}]
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(S,Q)
be a log quasiregular semiperfectoid pre-log ring. There is a cofiber sequence

{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})[2]\xrightarrow{u}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{}{\rm HH}(S/R\langle Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\rangle;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}),

where
Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\to Q
is the repletion (or exactification) of the map
Q^{\flat}\to Q
.

The above result serves as evidence that we have captured the correct notions in the logarithmic context. Indeed, as we will see in the proof, it is equivalent to the log analog

{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})[2]\xrightarrow{u}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{}{\rm HH}((S,Q)/R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

of the cofiber sequence (
1.1
), which is in fact a cofiber sequence for any algebra over a perfectoid ring.

More importantly, Theorem
1.6
lets us view
{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
as a
1
-parameter deformation
of
ordinary
, non-logarithmic Hochschild homology relative to the base ring
R\langle Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\rangle
which
need not be a perfectoid ring.
In some sense,
the failure of this ring being perfectoid measures the difference between the theory developed here and its
non-logarithmic analog, see Remark
6.6
.

The techniques leading to Theorem
1.6
appear throughout the paper:
See, for instance,
Proposition
4.20
, Proposition
5.9
,
and in particular Proposition
6.7
, which gives a filtration on log
{\rm THH}

with graded pieces described in terms of the
ordinary, non-logarithmic cotangent complex.
We refer to Examples
4.21
and
4.22
for an example of the repletion procedure
in this context.

There are several variants of topological Hochschild homology, such as log THH in
[
37
]
and the approaches to
\rm THH
using Waldhausen categories by Hesselholt–Madsen
[
14
]
and Blumberg–Mandell
[
7
]
. Among these, Rognes’ approach is unique in the sense that it manipulates the
output
of the cyclic bar construction (by means of repletion) as opposed to its input. This has also served as the main philosophical obstacle in relating log THH to
K
-theoretic invariants. For this reason, we find it very appealing that the repletion procedure appears in the
input
of Hochschild homology in the cofiber term in Theorem
1.6
.

Variants of the commutative ring
R\langle Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\rangle
naturally appear in other contexts.
For example, if
S
is a quasiregular semiperfect ring,
by
[
3
, Theorem 8.17]
there is a natural isomorphism

\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{S}\cong\widehat{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm crys}(S)

relating the Nygaard-complete prismatic cohomology
\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{S}
and the
(Nygaard-completion of the
p
-adic completion of) the divided power envelope of Fontaine’s
period map
W(S^{\flat})\to S
.
The above is the key step in the crystalline comparison of
[
3
, Theorem 1.2(3)]
.
In future work,
Diao–Yao
[
10
]
defines
{\mathbb{A}}_{\rm crys}(S,Q)
as
the divided power envelope of
W(S^{\flat})\langle Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\rangle\to S
.
For this reason,
the techniques leading to Theorem
1.6
will be useful in establishing a crystalline comparison
in our context.
Moreover, as explained in Remark
7.4
,
this should also prove useful in comparing our construction with the future work of Koshikawa–Yao,
in which log prismatic cohomology is developed using Koshikawa’s log prismatic site
[
23
]
.
We hope to relate our construction to the Nygaard-completion of Koshikawa–Yao’s derived log prismatic cohomology.

1.7.
The log filtrations

The log variants of quasiregular semiperfectoid and quasisyntomic enjoy many formal properties similar to those of their non-log counterparts considered in
[
3
]
. For example, there is an
unfolding
equivalence, which identifies sheaves defined on log quasiregular semiperfectoid pre-log rings with sheaves defined on the log quasisyntomic site. See Theorem
4.30
. We denote the latter site by
{\rm lqsyn}
.
For example, the functor
(S,Q)\mapsto\pi_{0}{\rm TC}^{-}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
is a sheaf defined on log quasiregular semiperfectoid rings, and hence unfolds to a sheaf on
{\rm lqsyn}
.

In analogy with the definition of
\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{A}
in
[
3
]
,
we define the
Nygaard-complete log prismatic cohomology
of a log quasisyntomic pre-log ring
(A,M)
by

\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)}:=R\Gamma_{{\rm lqsyn}}((A,M),\pi_{0}{\rm TC}^{-}((-,-);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})).

The homotopy fixed points spectral sequence computing the group

\pi_{0}{\rm TC}^{-}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
gives rise to a
complete filtration
{\rm Fil}_{N}^{\geq\bullet}\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)}
of
\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)}
.
Moreover, variants of topological Hochschild homology applied to log quasiregular semiperfectoid rings enjoy evenness properties analogous to those of
[
3
]
, and so we will make similar use of the double-speed Postnikov filtration (indicated by
\tau_{\geq 2*}
).

The following result is our logarithmic analog of
[
3
, Theorem 1.12]
,
which we state in full for ease of reference. The statement makes use of Breuil–Kisin twists
\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)}\{n\}
, see Remark
6.9
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
(A,M)
be a quasisyntomic pre-log ring.

(1)

Locally on
{\rm lqSyn}_{(A,M)}
, we have that
{\rm THH}((-,-);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}),{\rm TC}^{-}((-,-);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
and
{\rm TP}((-,-);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
are concentrated in even degrees.

(2)

The filtrations

\displaystyle{\rm Fil}^{*}{\rm THH}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}):=R\Gamma_{\rm lqsyn}((A,M),\tau_{\geq 2*}{\rm THH}((-,-);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}))

\displaystyle{\rm Fil}^{*}{\rm TC}^{-}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}):=R\Gamma_{\rm lqsyn}((A,M),\tau_{\geq 2*}{\rm TC}^{-}((-,-);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}))

\displaystyle{\rm Fil}^{*}{\rm TP}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}):=R\Gamma_{\rm lqsyn}((A,M),\tau_{\geq 2*}{\rm TP}((-,-);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}))

are complete, exhaustive, decreasing filtrations.

(3)

The associated graded of the filtrations in part (2) participate in natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle{\rm gr}^{n}{\rm THH}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\cong{\rm Fil}_{N}^{n}\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)}\{n\}[2n],

\displaystyle{\rm gr}^{n}{\rm TC}^{-}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\cong{\rm Fil}_{N}^{\geq n}\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)}\{n\}[2n],

\displaystyle{\rm gr}^{n}{\rm TP}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\cong\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)}\{n\}[2n].

(4)

The cyclotomic Frobenius and canonical maps

\varphi_{p},{\rm can}\colon{\rm TC}^{-}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\to{\rm TP}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

descend to the associated graded,
and hence we obtain a filtration on log topological cyclic homology
{\rm TC}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
.
Writing
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(n)(A,M)
for the associated graded, we have

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(n)(A,M)={\rm hofib}(\varphi_{p}-{\rm can}\colon{\rm Fil}^{\geq n}_{N}\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)}\{n\}\to\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)}\{n\}).

1.10.
Conventions

Most of the results in this paper are stated for ordinary (commutative) rings, but it is possible to generalize them for derived or animated rings in many cases. When necessary, we will model animated rings using simplicial commutative rings in order to apply the general framework of derived logarithmic geometry as developed in
[
39
]
(and used in
[
6
]
).

Given a commutative ring
R
, we write
\mathrm{GL}_{1}(R)
for the multiplicative group of invertible elements of
R
, while for a commutative monoid
M
we write
{\rm GL}_{1}(M)
for its submonoid of invertible elements.

2.
Logarithmic Hochschild homology

We begin by reviewing some of the results of
[
6
]
. In addition, we describe a useful base change property of the Gabber cotangent complex (Lemma
2.15
), essentially due to Rognes. We also prove that the circle action on log Hochschild homology is compatible with the log de Rham differential (Proposition
2.23
).

2.1.
Pre-log rings

A
pre-log ring

(R,P,\beta)
is the datum of commutative ring
R
, a commutative monoid
P
and a map
\beta\colon P\to(R,\cdot)
of commutative monoids to the underlying multiplicative monoid of
R
. It is a
log ring
if the canonical map
\beta^{-1}({\rm GL}_{1}(R))\to{\rm GL}_{1}(R)
is an isomorphism, and a pre-log ring
(R,P,\beta)
gives rise to a log ring
(R,P^{a},\beta^{a})
by defining
P^{a}
to be the (non-derived) pushout of the diagram
P\xleftarrow{}\beta^{-1}({\rm GL}_{1}(R))\xrightarrow{}{\rm GL}_{1}(R)
. We call
(R,P^{a},\beta^{a})
the
logification
of
(R,P,\beta)
.

A
morphism

(f,f^{\flat})\colon(R,P,\beta)\to(A,M,\alpha)
of pre-log rings consists of a ring map
f
and a monoid map
f^{\flat}
such that the obvious diagram commutes. A monoid map
P\to M
is
strict
if
P/{\rm GL}_{1}(P)\to M/{\rm GL}_{1}(M)
is an isomorphism
[
31
, Definition I.4.1.1]
. If
f^{\flat}\colon P\to M
is strict, then the logification of the pre-log structure
M\to(A,\cdot)
is isomorphic to that of
P\xrightarrow{f^{\flat}}M\xrightarrow{}(A,\cdot)
.

Given a commutative monoid
M
, we shall denote by
\gamma\colon M\to M^{\rm gp}
the canonical map to its group completion
M^{\rm gp}
.

2.2.
Animated pre-log rings

Let
{\rm Poly}_{(R,P)}
denote the category of
polynomial

(R,P)
-algebras, that is, those of the form
(R[x_{1},\dots,x_{m},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}],P\oplus\langle y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\rangle)
. In analogy with the definition of animated commutative
R
-algebras (see e.g.,
[
9
, Section 5.1.4]
), we define the category of
animated
(R,P)
-algebras

{\rm PreLog}_{(R,P)}^{\rm ani}
to be that of finite product-preserving functors

{\rm Poly}_{(R,P)}^{\rm op}\to{\rm Ani}

to the
\infty
-category of anima. The category
{\rm PreLog}_{(R,P)}^{\rm ani}
is the
\infty
-category underlying the simplicial model category of simplicial pre-log rings of
[
39
, Section 3]
. The latter model is used in
[
6
]
to
model various functors involving
{\rm PreLog}_{(R,P)}^{\rm ani}
.

As specified in our conventions, all (relative) coproducts involving monoids and (pre-log) rings should be considered in the derived (or animated) sense.

2.3.
Integral monoid maps are flat

A (discrete) commutative monoid
P
is
integral
if the canonical map
P\to P^{\rm gp}
is injective, and a map
P\to M
of integral monoids is
integral
if the monoid
\pi_{0}(M\oplus_{P}N)
is integral for any map
P\to N
with
N
integral. If
P\to M
is an integral map of integral monoids, then the truncation map

(2.1)

M\oplus_{P}N\to\pi_{0}(M\oplus_{P}N)

is an equivalence for any map of commutative monoids
P\to N
, see
[
5
, Proposition 4.9]
.

2.4.
Faithfully flat morphisms of pre-log rings

We now introduce the class of morphisms of pre-log rings that will play the role of faithfully flat morphisms of ordinary commutative rings. To motivate the following definition, let us record two observations:

(1)

It is reasonable to impose a flatness condition on monoid maps
P\to M
in terms of the truncation map (
2.1
) being an equivalence: Indeed, this is always satisfied for integral maps of integral monoids.

(2)

Invariants of pre-log rings tend to pass through an exactification procedure (Section
2.10
), ensuring that the output lives in a category only dependent on the underlying commutative ring. For example, we consider the log cotangent complex (Section
2.7
)
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
an
A
-module, as opposed to an object of some hypothetical category of modules over the pre-log ring
(A,M)
. This suggests that it suffices to impose a notion of faithfulness on the underlying commutative rings: This point will become very clear in the proof of Theorem
2.9
.

Based on this, we introduce the following definition:

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(f,f^{\flat})\colon(R,P)\to(A,M)
be a map of pre-log rings. We say that

(1)

f^{\flat}
is
flat
if (
2.1
) is an equivalence for all monoid maps
P\to N
;

(2)

(f,f^{\flat})
is
homologically log flat
if both
f
and
f^{\flat}
are flat; and

(3)

(f,f^{\flat})
is
homologically log faithfully flat
if
f
is faithfully flat and
f^{\flat}
is flat.

We refer to the resulting topology as the
homologically log flat
or
hlf
topology. Note that (
1
) is taken from
[
5
, Definition 4.8]
.

Remark 2.6
.

Our first name for the above topology was “log fpqc”. This comes with some disadvantages: It clashes with the terminology of Molcho–Temkin
[
29
]
and differs from the terminology of Koshikawa–Yao
[
22
]
for the same notion. As recorded in e.g.,
[
6
, Example 4.9]
, there are classically log flat morphisms that fail to be homologically log flat, while the discrepancy disappears for integral maps. We, therefore, choose to borrow Koshikawa–Yao’s terminology for the above notion, which they attribute to Ofer Gabber.

2.7.
The logarithmic cotangent complex

We now review the Gabber cotangent complex. One reference is Olsson
[
32
, Section 8]
. The exposition here is closer in spirit to that of
[
6
, Section 3]
or
[
39
, Section 4]
. We refer to these sources for proof of the statements made here.

Let
(f,f^{\flat})\colon(R,P)\to(A,M)
be a map of pre-log rings. The
module of relative log differentials

\Omega^{1}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
is the
A
-module

\frac{\Omega^{1}_{A/R}\oplus\pi_{0}(A\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(M^{\rm gp}/P^{\rm gp}))}{{\rm d}\alpha(m)\sim\alpha(m)\otimes\gamma(m)},

where
\alpha\colon M\to(A,\cdot)
is the structure map and
\gamma\colon M\to M^{\rm gp}
is the canonical map from
M
to its group completion
M^{\rm gp}
.

This extends levelwise to a functor

\Omega^{1}_{(-,-)/(R,P)}\colon{\rm sPreLog}_{(R,P)/}\to{\rm sMod}_{R}

from the category of simplicial pre-log
(R,P)
-algebras to that of simplicial
R
-modules. It is left Quillen, and its total derived functor is the
Gabber cotangent complex

{\mathbb{L}}_{(-,-)/(R,P)}
. The associated functor of
\infty
-categories is equivalent to the left Kan extension of the functor
\Omega^{1}_{(-,-)/(R,P)}
defined only on polynomial pre-log
(R,P)
-algebras
(R[x_{1},\dots,x_{m},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}],\langle y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\rangle)
to all animated pre-log
(R,P)
-algebras. If the base pre-log ring has the trivial pre-log structure we omit the pre-log structure from the notation: That is, we set
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/R}:={\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,\{1\})}
.

This construction enjoys the following properties:

(1)

For polynomial
(R,P)
-algebras

(A,M):=(R[x_{1},\dots,x_{m},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}],P\oplus\langle y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\rangle)

there is a canonical identification

{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}=\Omega^{1}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}A\{{\rm d}x\}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}A\{{\rm dlog}(y)\},

and the resulting map
\Omega^{1}_{A/R}\to\Omega^{1}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
sends generators of the form
{\rm d}y
to
y{\rm dlog}(y)
.

(2)

If
f^{\flat}
is strict, then the canonical map
{\mathbb{L}}_{A/R}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
is an equivalence.

(3)

If
(C,K)
arises as the homotopy pushout of

(B,N)\xleftarrow{(g,g^{\flat})}(R,P)\xrightarrow{(f,f^{\flat})}(A,M),

then the canonical map
C\otimes_{B}{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(R,P)}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{(C,K)/(A,M)}
is an equivalence.

(4)

There is a canonical equivalence
\mathbb{L}_{(\mathbb{Z}[M],M)/(\mathbb{Z}[P],P)}\cong\mathbb{Z}[M]\otimes{Z}(M^{\mathrm{gp}}/P^{\mathrm{gp}})
, and a cofiber sequence of
A
-modules

A\otimes{Z}(M^{\mathrm{gp}}/P^{\mathrm{gp}})\to\mathbb{L}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}\to\mathbb{L}_{A/R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[P]}\mathbb{Z}[M]}

(5)

For a composite
(R,P)\xrightarrow{}(A,M)\xrightarrow{}(B,N)
, there is a cofiber sequence

B\otimes_{A}{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(R,P)}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(A,M)}

of
B
-modules.

(6)

If
(R,P)\to(A,M)
is an
integral
log smooth morphism, then the truncation map

{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}\to\Omega^{1}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}

is an equivalence. See
[
6
, Proposition 4.6]
for a precise relationship between the notion of log smoothness in classical log geometry and its derived counterpart.

2.8.
Homologically log flat descent for the cotangent complex

By
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
, the wedge powers of the (non-logarithmic) cotangent complex satisfy flat descent. Using the HKR theorem, this is the key input in proving flat descent for topological Hochschild homology.
For the analogous statement in the log setting, we use the notion of homologically log faithfully flat of Definition
2.5
:

Theorem 2.9
.

Consider a base pre-log ring
(R,P)
, and let
(A,M)\to(B,N)
be a homologically log faithfully flat map of
(R,P)
-algebras. Then

\wedge^{i}{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}\simeq{\rm lim}(\wedge^{i}{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(R,P)}\rightrightarrows\wedge^{i}{\mathbb{L}}_{(B\otimes_{A}B,N\oplus_{M}N)/(R,P)}\cdots).

Proof.

We follow the steps of
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
, and proceed by induction on
i
, where the case
i=0
is just faithfully flat descent for rings. The argument for
i=1
will, as spelled out below, be as in
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
, and this implies the result for higher
i
by the filtrations considered in
[
19
, Section V.4]
.

Let
(B^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})
denote the Cech nerve of the map
(A,M)\to(B,N)
. We can apply the transitivity sequence of
2.7
.(
5
) to the composite
(R,P)\to(A,M)\to(B^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})
to obtain a cosimplicial cofiber sequence

B^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{(B^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})/(R,P)}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{(B^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})/(A,M)}.

Since
A\to B
is faithfully flat, fpqc descent implies that the induced map

{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}\to{\rm lim}(B^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)})

is an isomorphism. Again by the transitivity sequence, it suffices to prove that the totalization of
{\mathbb{L}}_{(B^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})/(A,M)}
vanishes. For this, we show that each
\pi_{i}{\mathbb{L}}_{(B^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})/(A,M)}
vanishes (the cosimplicial direction of
(B^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})
is still running). Since
A\to B
is faithfully flat, it suffices to prove that (the right-hand side of)

(2.2)

B\otimes_{A}\pi_{i}{\mathbb{L}}_{(B^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})/(A,M)}\simeq\pi_{i}(B\otimes_{A}{\mathbb{L}}_{(B^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})/(A,M)})

vanishes. Descent for
B
together with the base change property
2.7
.(
3
) of the Gabber cotangent complex (using again that
A\to B
is faithfully flat) implies that

B\otimes_{A}{\mathbb{L}}_{(B^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})/(A,M)}\xrightarrow{\simeq}(B\otimes_{A}B^{\bullet})\otimes_{B^{\bullet}}{\mathbb{L}}_{(B^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})/(A,M)}\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\mathbb{L}}_{(B\otimes_{A}B^{\bullet},N\oplus_{M}N^{\bullet})/(B,N)}.

But now
(B,N)\to(B\otimes_{A}B^{\bullet},N\oplus_{M}N^{\bullet})
is the Cech nerve of a map with a section, hence a cosimplicial homotopy equivalence. This shows that the right-hand side of (
2.2
) is equivalent to
\pi_{i}{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(B,N)}\simeq 0
, which concludes the proof.
∎

2.10.
Repletion

The key ingredient to the definition of log Hochschild homology pursued by Rognes is the notion of
repletion
, or
exactification
, of a map of monoids. This operation was introduced by Kato in
[
21
]
in the geometric context and was revisited and generalized to the context of topological log structures by Rognes
[
37
]
. We shall use Rognes’ terminology throughout.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
(C,K)\to(A,M)
be a map of pre-log rings with the property that
K^{\rm gp}\to M^{\rm gp}
is surjective.

(1)

The
repletion

K^{\rm rep}\to M
is the pullback of
K^{\rm gp}\to M^{\rm gp}
along
M\to M^{\rm gp}
.

(2)

The
replete base change

C^{\rm rep}
is the base change
C\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[K]}{\mathbb{Z}}[K^{\rm rep}]
.

Note that, in general,
C\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[K]}{\mathbb{Z}}[K^{\rm rep}]
may not be discrete. By definition, the resulting map
K^{\rm rep}\to M
is exact. Since the monoids involved are integral, it is strict by

[
31
, Proposition 4.2.1(5)]
.

Remark 2.12
.

Note that the exactification has a very concrete geometric interpretation in terms of affine blow-ups of the corresponding toric variety. For closed embeddings, the exactification procedure is the passage to a blow-up, after which the morphism becomes strict.
We view the passage from
(C,K)
to
(C^{\rm rep},K^{\rm rep})
as a means of “cashing out” the pre-log structure on
C
(relative to that on
M
), allowing us to work with the resulting commutative ring
C^{\rm rep}
while still retaining information from the log structure. If
M
is trivial, this corresponds precisely to passing to the open complement of the log structure. The following example supports this philosophy and motivates the definition of log Hochschild homology pursued in
[
6
]
.

Example 2.13
.

Let
(R,P)\to(A,M)
be a map of pre-log rings. There is a factorization

(2.3)

\pi_{0}(A\otimes_{R}A,M\oplus_{P}M)\to\pi_{0}((A\otimes_{R}A)^{\rm rep},(M\oplus_{P}M)^{\rm rep})\to(A,M)

of the (underived) diagonal
\pi_{0}(A\otimes_{R}A,M\oplus_{P}M)\to(A,M)
. The indecomposables (i.e., conormal) of the underlying map
\pi_{0}(A\otimes_{R}A)^{\rm rep}\to A
is the module
\Omega^{1}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
of relative log differentials
[
20
, Proposition 4.2.8(ii)]
. In the derived setting, we instead obtain the Gabber cotangent complex
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
as the (derived) conormal of the log diagonal

(A\otimes_{R}A,M\oplus_{P}M)\to((A\otimes_{R}A)^{\rm rep},(M\oplus_{P}M)^{\rm rep})\to(A,M);

this is the content of
[
6
, Proposition 3.17]
. Note that, geometrically, the diagonal embedding
X=\mathrm{Spec}(A)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(A\otimes_{R}A)=X\times_{S}X
is not an exact (equivalently, strict) closed immersion, where
X
is seen as a log scheme with log structure induced by
M
, and
X\times_{S}X
is a log scheme with log structure induced by
\pi_{0}(M\oplus_{P}M)
. One achieves strictness precisely by passing to the replete base change that models an affine chart for a suitable blow-up. See
[
18
, 2.16]
.

see

2.16.
Logarithmic Hochschild homology

Let
R\to A
be a map of commutative rings. The
Hochschild homology

{\rm HH}(A/R)
of
A
relative to
R
can be defined as the tensor
S^{1}\otimes_{R}A
in simplicial commutative
R
-algebras. Writing
S^{1}
as the homotopy pushout of
*\xleftarrow{}*\sqcup*\xrightarrow{}*
, we find that
{\rm HH}(A/R)=A\otimes_{A\otimes_{R}A}A
.

Motivated by Example
2.13
, we gave in
[
6
, Definition 5.3]
the following:

Definition 2.17
.

Let
(R,P)\to(A,M)
be a map of pre-log rings. The
logarithmic Hochschild homology
of
(A,M)
relative to
(R,P)
is the pushout

{\rm HH}((A,M)/(R,P)):=A\otimes_{(A\otimes_{R}A)^{\rm rep}}A

of simplicial commutative
A
-algebras.

Remark 2.18
.

In algebro-geometric terms, the description of Hochschild homology as the iterated coproduct
A\otimes_{A\otimes_{R}A}A
exhibits
{\rm HH}(A/R)
as the (derived) self-intersections of the diagonal
A\otimes_{R}A\to A
. In the language of Kato–Saito
[
20
, Section 4]
, Definition
2.17
exhibits
{\rm HH}((A,M)/(R,P))
as the (derived) self-intersections of the (derived) log diagonal
(A\otimes_{R}A)^{\rm rep}\to A
, see Example
2.13
. Note that in
[
6
]
, we used the notation
\log\HH
instead of
\HH
to denote logarithmic Hochschild homology.

Remark 2.19
.

We record some key properties of log Hochschild homology here:

(1)

In analogy with the transitivity formula
{\rm HH}(B/A)\simeq A\otimes_{{\rm HH}(A/R)}{\rm HH}(B/R)
for a composite
R\to A\to B
, there is an equivalence

{\rm HH}((B,N)/(A,M))\simeq A\otimes_{{\rm HH}((A,M)/(R,P))}{\rm HH}((B,N)/(R,P))

for a composite
(R,P)\to(A,M)\to(B,N)
of pre-log rings. This is the content of
[
6
, Proposition 5.4]
.

(2)

The formulation of Definition
2.17
coincides with that pursued by Rognes
[
37
]
. In particular, for a map of commutative monoids
P\to M
, we can form the simplicial tensor
S^{1}\oplus_{P}M
, and the repletion
S^{1}\oplus_{P}^{\rm rep}M
of the augmentation
S^{1}\oplus_{P}M\to M
. Then
{\rm HH}((A,M)/(R,P))
can equivalently be described as the pushout of the diagram

(2.5)

{\rm HH}(A/R)\xleftarrow{}A\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M]}{\mathbb{Z}}[S^{1}\oplus_{P}M]\xrightarrow{}A\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M]}{\mathbb{Z}}[S^{1}\oplus_{P}^{\rm rep}M]

of derived
A
-algebras; this is the content of
[
6
, Proposition 1.4]
.

(3)

Log Hochschild homology is invariant under passing to the associated log ring:
There are equivalences

{\rm HH}((A,M)/(R,P))\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\rm HH}((A,M^{a})/(R,P))\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\rm HH}((A,M^{a})/(R,P^{a}));

this can be deduced from Theorem
2.21
below and the analogous statement for the Gabber cotangent complex
[
32
, Theorem 8.16]
. In particular, this implies that log Hochschild recovers classical Hochschild homology for strict morphisms, as in this case, the canonical map

{\rm HH}(A/R)\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\rm HH}((A,P)/(R,P))\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\rm HH}((A,M)/(R,P))

is an equivalence by logification invariance. This implies that there is an equivalence
{\rm HH}((A,M)/(R,P))\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\rm HH}((A,N)/(R,P))
for strict maps
M\to N
.

2.22.

Let

is a simplicial commutative ring. Rognes’ formulation (the right-hand side of the displayed equivalence) is more convenient
for discussing the circle action on logarithmic Hochschild homology.

The operator

satisfies the formulas of
[

Since the map
{\rm HH}({\mathbb{Z}}[M]/{\mathbb{Z}}[P])\to{\rm HH}(({\mathbb{Z}}[M],M)/({\mathbb{Z}}[P],P))

of

The element

\displaystyle B(1,(\gamma(m)^{-1},\gamma(m)))

3.
Logarithmic topological Hochschild homology

We now review the notion of logarithmic
topological
Hochschild homology, as first introduced by Rognes
[
37
]
. As we shall only be concerned with pre-log ring spectra whose underlying “commutative monoid” is an ordinary commutative monoid, the theory simplifies significantly. In particular, we do not use the machinery of
[
38
,
40
,
35
,
36
]
to handle “graded
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-spaces” to capture homotopy classes in non-zero degrees.

3.1.
Definition and first properties

For our purposes, a
pre-log ring spectrum

(A,M)
consists of an
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-ring
A
, a commutative monoid
M
, and a map
{\mathbb{S}}[M]\to A
of
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-rings.

The following is a variant of the definition of log topological Hochschild homology pursued in the second-named author’s thesis
[
27
,
28
]
, and is also closely related to
[
37
, Section 13]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(R,P)\to(A,M)
be a map of pre-log ring spectra. The
log topological Hochschild homology

{\rm THH}((A,M)/(R,P))
is defined as the pushout of the diagram

A\xleftarrow{}(A\otimes_{R}A)\otimes_{{\mathbb{S}}[M\oplus_{P}M]}{\mathbb{S}}[(M\oplus_{P}M)^{\rm rep}]\xrightarrow{}A

of
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-rings.

If
(R,P)=({\mathbb{S}},\{1\})
with structure map the identity,
we omit it from the notation and simply write
\THH(A,M)
.

Remark 3.3
.

The definition is motivated by its relationship with the (spectral) log cotangent complex in
[
27
]
, in analogy with Example
2.13
.
Note that if
(R,P)\to(A,M)
is a map of ordinary pre-log rings, this recovers the log Hochschild homology
{\rm HH}((A,M)/(R,P))
introduced in Definition
2.17
(it is enough to spell out the replete base change).

As in the case of ordinary topological Hochschild homology, we have the following transitivity property:

Proposition 3.4
.

[
27
, Lemma 5.4]
Let
(R,P)\to(A,M)\to(B,N)
be maps of pre-log ring spectra. There is a natural equivalence

{\rm THH}((B,N)/(A,M))\simeq A\otimes_{{\rm THH}((A,M)/(R,P))}{\rm THH}((B,N)/(R,P))

of
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-rings.

We shall mostly use the following consequence:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(A,M)
be an ordinary pre-log ring. There is a natural equivalence

{\rm HH}(A,M)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{{\rm THH}({\mathbb{Z}})}{\rm THH}(A,M)

of
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-rings.

In particular, there is a canonical map

(3.1)

{\rm THH}(A,M)\to{\rm HH}(A,M)

of
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-rings, obtained by extension of scalars along the augmentation
{\rm THH}(\mathbb{Z})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

The map (
3.1
) induces an isomorphism

\pi_{i}{\rm THH}(A,M)\to\pi_{i}{\rm HH}(A,M)

for
i\leq 2
.

Proof.

This follows Bökstedt’s computation of
\pi_{*}{\rm THH}({\mathbb{Z}})

[
8
]
(in particular, its vanishing in degrees
1
and
2
) and the
{\rm Tor}
-spectral sequence

E^{2}_{p,q}={\rm Tor}_{p}^{\pi_{*}{\rm THH}({\mathbb{Z}})}({\mathbb{Z}},\pi_{*}{\rm THH}(A,M))_{q}\implies\pi_{*}{\rm HH}(A,M)

obtained from Corollary
3.5
.
∎

Finally, we will need to refer to Rognes’ formulation of log topological Hochschild homology:

Proposition 3.7
.

There is a natural equivalence

{\rm THH}(A,M)\simeq{\rm THH}(A)\otimes_{{\mathbb{S}}[B^{\rm cyc}(M)]}{\mathbb{S}}[M\times_{M^{\rm gp}}B^{\rm cyc}(M^{\rm gp})]

of
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-ring spectra.

Proof.

This can be deduced from
[
37
, Section 13]
, or by applying
[
27
, Proposition 5.8]
to the class of pre-log ring spectra considered here.
∎

3.8.
Cyclotomic structure

The description of Proposition

gives a convenient way to characterize the circle action on log topological Hochschild homology. As explained below, this gives a cyclotomic structure on absolute log topological Hochschild homology.

Let

that topological Hochschild homology of the spherical monoid ring

By

We define the Frobenius on log topological Hochschild homology

Remark 3.11

The construction given above of the cyclotomic structure on log topological Hochschild homology is dependent upon the fact that we are working on (pre)-log ring spectra of the form

4.
The log quasisyntomic site

We now pursue a logarithmic version of the quasisyntomic site introduced in
[
3
]
. A similar theory has been developed by Koshikawa–Yao
[
22
]
. They pursue a log version of the log prismatic cohomology developed in
[
4
]
; in particular, they do not discuss the Nygaard-complete version of log prismatic cohomology considered here.

4.1.
Recollection on quasisyntomic rings

Let us briefly recall the notion of quasisyntomic ring from
[
3
, Section 4]
and the basic properties of the resulting site. As in
loc. cit.
all rings will be implicitly
p
-complete and with bounded
p^{\infty}
-torsion. By definition, a ring
A
is
quasisyntomic
if it has bounded
p^{\infty}
-torsion and
{\mathbb{L}}_{A/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
has
p
-complete
{\rm Tor}
-amplitude in
[-1,0]
.

Definition 4.2

(
[
3
, Definition 4.10]
)
.

Let
f\colon A\to B
be a map of commutative rings. We say that
f
is
quasisyntomic
if

(1)

A\to B
is
p
-completely flat, i.e.,
B\otimes_{A}A/p
is concentrated in degree
0
and is a flat
A/p
-module, and

(2)

{\mathbb{L}}_{B/A}
has
p
-complete
{\rm Tor}
-amplitude in
[-1,0]
.

If, in addition, the underlying map
f\colon A\to B
of commutative rings is
p
-completely faithfully flat (that is,
B\otimes_{A}A/p
is also a faithfully flat
A/p
-module), we say that
f
is a
quasisyntomic cover
.

Definition 4.3

(
[
3
, Definition 4.20]
)
.

The ring
S
is
quasiregular semiperfectoid
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

S
is quasisyntomic;

(2)

S
admits a map from a perfectoid ring;

(3)

S/pS
is semiperfect; that is, its Frobenius is surjective.

We shall write
{\rm QRSPerfd}
for the resulting category.

Recall from
[
3
, Lemma 4.27]
that
{\rm QRSPerfd}^{\rm op}
admits the structure of a site and that the collection of sheaves on
{\rm QSyn}^{\rm op}
identify with the collection of sheaves on
{\rm QRSPerfd}^{\rm op}
(with values in any presentable
{\infty}
-category
{\cal C}
): This is the content of
[
3
, Proposition 4.31]
. The resulting equivalence is denoted

{\rm Shv}_{\cal C}({\rm QRSPerfd}^{\rm op})\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\rm Shv}_{\cal C}({\rm QSyn}^{\rm op}),\quad F\mapsto F^{\sqsupset}

and the sheaf
F^{\sqsupset}
is called the

of
F
.

4.4.
Complete monoid rings

We shall adopt the following convention regarding monoid rings: For any commutative monoid
M
, we let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\langle M\rangle
denote the
p
-completion of the monoid ring
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[M]
. For a different
p
-complete base ring
R
,
we shall write
R\langle M\rangle
for the
p
-complete tensor product
R\widehat{\otimes}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\langle M\rangle
.

4.5.
(Semi)perfect monoids

Fix a prime number
p
. Given any commutative monoid
M
, we write

F_{M}\colon M\to M,\quad m\mapsto m^{p}

for its
p
-power map.

Definition 4.6
.

We call a monoid

(1)

perfect
if
F_{M}
is an isomorphism, and

(2)

semiperfect
if
F_{M}
is surjective.

For example, the additive monoid
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is semiperfect, but not perfect. This definition gives rise to notions of direct and inverse limit perfections:

M_{\rm perf}:={\rm colim}(M\xrightarrow{F_{M}}M\xrightarrow{F_{M}}\cdots),\quad M^{\flat}:={\rm lim}(\cdots\xrightarrow{F_{M}}M\xrightarrow{F_{M}}M).

Observe that a monoid is semiperfect precisely when the canonical map from its
tilt

M^{\flat}\to M
is surjective.

The following elementary observation is essential to us:

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
R
be a perfectoid ring and let
P
be a perfect monoid.
Then the
p
-complete monoid ring
R\langle P\rangle
is a perfectoid ring.

Proof.

We make use of the equivalence between the category of perfect prisms and the category of perfectoid rings provided by
[
4
, Theorem 3.10]
.
Let
(A_{\rm inf}(R^{\flat}),(d))
be the perfect prism with
A_{\rm inf}(R^{\flat})/(d)\cong R
. We claim that this gives rise to a perfect prism
(A_{\rm inf}(R^{\flat})\widehat{\otimes}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\langle P\rangle,(d))
. Indeed, the Frobenius on the
(p,d)
-complete
\delta
-ring
A_{\rm inf}(R^{\flat})\widehat{\otimes}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\langle P\rangle
remains bijective since
P
is perfect, and its mod
d
reduction is
R\widehat{\otimes}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\langle P\rangle=:R\langle P\rangle
, as desired.
∎

4.8.
Quasisyntomic pre-log rings

The following are generalizations of the notions of quasismooth and quasisyntomic ring maps to the logarithmic setting. Recall that we assume all rings to be
p
-complete. We say that a pre-log ring
(A,M)
with
M
integral is
log quasismooth
if
A
has bounded
p^{\infty}
-torsion and
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
is
p
-completely flat, while
(A,M)
is
log quasisyntomic
if
A
has bounded
p^{\infty}
-torsion and
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
has
p
-complete
{\rm Tor}
-amplitude in
[-1,0]
.

Definition 4.9

(Cfr.
[
3
, Definition 4.10(2, 3)]
)
.

Let
(f,f^{\flat})\colon(A,M)\to(B,N)
be an integral map of integral pre-log rings whose underlying rings have bounded
p^{\infty}
-torsion.

(1)

We say that
(f,f^{\flat})
is
log quasismooth
if

(i)

A\to B
is
p
-completely flat, and

(ii)

{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(A,M)}
is
p
-completely flat.

If, in addition, the underlying map
f\colon A\to B
of commutative rings is
p
-completely faithfully flat, we say that
(f,f^{\flat})
is a
log quasismooth cover
.

(2)

We say that
(f,f^{\flat})
is
log quasisyntomic
if

(i)

A\to B
is
p
-completely flat, and

(ii)

{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(A,M)}
has
p
-complete
{\rm Tor}
-amplitude in
[-1,0]
.

If, in addition, the underlying map
f\colon A\to B
of commutative rings is
p
-completely faithfully flat, we say that
(f,f^{\flat})
is a
log quasisyntomic cover
.

Remark 4.10
.

The transitivity sequence of the Gabber cotangent complex implies that log quasismooth and log quasisyntomic are properties that are closed under composition. As the composition of faithfully maps are faithfully flat,
the same goes for log quasismooth and quasisyntomic covers.

Similarly, the flat base change property of the Gabber cotangent complex implies that these classes of maps are closed under base change; here we use that the completed derived base change along a
p
-completely flat map coincides with the ordinary completed base change as soon as all rings in question have bounded
p^{\infty}
-torsion, as observed in the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 4.16(2)]
.

4.11.
Quasiregular semiperfectoid pre-log rings

We shall use the following notion of quasiregular semiperfectoid in the log setting:

Definition 4.12
.

Let
(S,Q)
be an integral pre-log ring. We say that
(S,Q)
is
log quasiregular semiperfectoid
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

the pre-log ring
(S,Q)
is log quasisyntomic;

(2)

S
admits a map from a perfectoid ring;

(3)

S/pS
and
Q
are semiperfect.

We shall write
{\rm lQRSPerfd}
for the resulting category.

Remark 4.13
.

Throughout the rest of the paper, we shall freely use the following elementary observation to pass between monoid rings of the form
R[P]
and their
p
-complete variants
R\langle P\rangle
:
If
R
is
p
-complete of bounded
p^{\infty}
-torsion, then the monoid ring
R[P]
is also of bounded
p^{\infty}
-torsion, as it is free over
R
.
Consequently, the cotangent complex
{\mathbb{L}}_{R\langle P\rangle/R[P]}
vanishes after derived
p
-completion, by derived reduction mod
p
, derived base change, and derived Nakayama.

Remark 4.14
.

We can always arrange for a log quasiregular semiperfectoid ring to receive a surjection from a “pre-log perfectoid ring” with underlying monoid the tilt
Q^{\flat}
, e.g., by replacing the surjection
R\to S
by
R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle\to S
.

Recall from
[
3
, Remark 4.21]
that if
S
is quasiregular semiperfectoid, then the cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}_{S/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
has
p
-complete Tor amplitude in degree
-1
. The same holds for their log counterparts:

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
(S,Q)
be in
{\rm lQRSPerfd}
.
Then the Gabber cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}_{(S,Q)/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
has
p
-complete Tor amplitude in degree
-1
.

Proof.

For every
x\in S
,
we have
x=y^{p}+pz
for some
y,z\in S
since
S/pS
is semiperfect,
which implies that
dx=pdy^{p-1}+pdz
, that is,
\Omega^{1}_{(S/pS)/\mathbb{F}_{p}}=0
.
Moreover, for every
q\in Q

we have
q=pr
for some
r\in Q
since
Q
is semiperfect,
which implies that
d\log q=pd\log r
.
Hence the multiplication map

\cdot p\colon\Omega_{(S,Q)/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{1}\to\Omega_{(S,Q)/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{1}

is surjective.
Since
\Omega_{(S,Q)/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{1}\cong\pi_{0}(\mathbb{L}_{(S,Q)/\mathbb{Z}_{p}})
,
we obtain by base change.

\pi_{0}(\mathbb{L}_{(S,Q)/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\otimes_{S}S/pS)\cong 0.

Since
(S,Q)
is log quasisyntomic by condition (1), we find that
{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
has
p
-complete
{\rm Tor}
-amplitude in degree
-1
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
(S,Q)
be in
{\rm lQRSPerfd}
,
and let
R\to S
be a map with
R
a perfectoid ring.
then
\mathbb{L}_{(S,Q)/R}
has
p
-complete Tor amplitude in degree
-1
.

Proof.

From the transitivity sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to R\to(S,Q)
, we obtain after (derived) base change to
S/pS
a cofiber sequence

\mathbb{L}_{R/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\otimes_{R}S/pS\xrightarrow{\alpha_{(S,Q)}}\mathbb{L}_{(S,Q)/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\otimes_{S}S/pS\to\mathbb{L}_{(S,Q)/R}\otimes_{S}S/pS

The first two terms have
p
-complete Tor amplitude in degree
-1
by
[
3
, Proposition 4.19(2)]
and Lemma
4.15
. This immediately implies that
\pi_{i}(\mathbb{L}_{(S,Q)/R}\otimes_{S}S/pS\otimes_{S/pS}M)=0
for any discrete
S/pS
-module
M
,
i\geq 3
and
i\leq 0
. We are then left to show that the same holds for
\pi_{2}
, that is, it remains to show that
\beta_{(S,Q)}:=\pi_{1}(\alpha_{(S,Q)})
is pure in the sense that it is injective after tensoring with any discrete
S/pS
-module (we borrow the terminology from
[
3
, Lemma 4.25]
). The term
\mathbb{L}_{R/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
is the ordinary (non-log) cotangent complex, and after
p
-completion,
it coincides with
\ker(\theta_{R})/\ker(\theta_{R})^{2}=R[1]
by
[
3
, Example 4.24]
. Thus
we can describe
\beta_{(S,Q)}
as

\ker(\theta_{R})/\ker(\theta_{R})^{2}\otimes_{R}S/pS\to\pi_{1}(\mathbb{L}_{(S,Q)/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\otimes_{S}S/pS),

and the source is a finite free
S/pS
-module with formation compatible with base changes in
S
.
By Lemma
4.15
,
the target is a flat
S/pS
-module.
By the criterion provided by
[
3
, Lemma 4.26]
it is enough to show that
\beta_{(S,Q)}\otimes_{S/pS}k
is injective for all perfect fields
k
with a map
S/pS\to k
(note that in loc. cit. there is no perfectness assumption on
k
, but we can reduce to this case).
By functoriality,

\beta_{(S,Q)}\otimes_{S/pS}k
factors
\beta_{(k,Q)}
.
Furthermore,

\beta_{(k,Q)}
factors
\beta_{(k,Q_{\mathrm{perf}})}
.
Hence it remains to show that
\beta_{(k,Q_{\mathrm{perf}})}
is injective.

In this case,

k
is perfectoid.
By
[
3
, Lemma 3.14]
,
we have
\mathbb{L}_{k/R}\simeq 0
.
There is a cocartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{Z}[Q_{\mathrm{perf}}]/\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[Q_{\mathrm{perf}}]}k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}_{k/R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}_{(\mathbb{Z}[Q_{\mathrm{perf}}],Q_{\mathrm{perf}})/\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[Q_{\mathrm{perf}}]}k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}_{(k,Q_{\mathrm{perf}})/R},}

see
[
6
, Eq. 3.3]
.
Together with Lemma
4.17
below, we deduce that
\mathbb{L}_{(k,Q_{\mathrm{perf}})/R}
vanishes.
This means that
\beta_{(k,Q_{\mathrm{perf}})}
is an isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
P
be a perfect monoid.
Then the modules

\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{Z}[P]/\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{L}_{(\mathbb{Z}[P],P)/\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}

vanish.

Proof.

This is essentially an observation taken from
[
5
]
. For the statement about the ordinary cotangent complex, the flat base change equivalence

\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{Z}[P]/\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}={\mathbb{L}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}[P]/{\mathbb{Z}}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[P]}{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[P]\simeq{\mathbb{L}}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[P]/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}

allows us to conclude by
[
5
, Corollary 3.8]
. The proof for the statement about the Gabber cotangent complex is analogous, using instead
[
5
, Corollary 7.11]
.
∎

In particular, we have:

Corollary 4.18
.

Let
(R,P)
be a pre-log ring with
R
a perfectoid ring and
P
a perfect monoid. Then the canonical map

{\mathbb{L}}_{R/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\xrightarrow{}{\mathbb{L}}_{(R,P)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}

is an equivalence after
p
-completion.

Proof.

By the transitivity sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to R\to(R,P)
, it suffices to prove that
{\mathbb{L}}_{(R,P)/R}
vanishes after
p
-completion. This follows from Lemma
4.17
and the description of
{\mathbb{L}}_{(R,P)/R}
as the pushout of the diagram

{\mathbb{L}}_{R/R}\xleftarrow{}R\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[P]}{\mathbb{L}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[P]/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\xrightarrow{}R\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[P]}{\mathbb{L}}_{({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[P],P)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}

of
R
-modules.
∎

Remark 4.19
.

In light of Corollary
4.18
, we shall typically consider perfectoid rings
R
with trivial log structure.

As pointed out in Remark
4.14
, if
(S,Q)
is a log quasiregular semiperfectoid ring, there is a surjection to
S
from a perfectoid ring of the form
R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle
. Let
Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\to Q
denote the repletion of the surjection
Q^{\flat}\to Q
. The following observation will be crucial in the sequel:

Proposition 4.20
.

With notation as in the previous paragraph, there is a canonical equivalence

{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle}\simeq{\mathbb{L}}_{S/R\langle Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\rangle}

of
S
-modules after
p
-completion.

We stress that the right-hand side of the equivalence is an ordinary, non-logarithmic cotangent complex. The reader already familiar with the material of
[
3
]
may wonder why Proposition
4.20
does not imply that our resulting theory of Nygaard-complete log prismatic cohomology coincides with the ordinary, non-logarithmic variant. The point is that the ring
R\langle Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\rangle
need not be a perfectoid ring (and it is not, in general: this happens when the map

is already exact). See also Remark
6.6
below.

Proof of Proposition
4.20
.

There are equivalences

{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle}\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/(R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle,Q^{\flat})}\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/(R\langle Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\rangle,Q^{\flat,\rm rep})}\xleftarrow{\simeq}{\mathbb{L}}_{S/R\langle Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\rangle}

after derived
p
-completion by Corollary
4.18
, the
p
-complete variant of replete base change (Lemma
2.15
), and the fact that the resulting map
Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\to Q
is strict. .
∎

Example 4.21
.

With a small abuse of notation, write
\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{N}
for the perfect submonoid of
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
consisting of all elements of the form
\frac{a}{p^{n}}
for

. Let

be a perfectoid ring. Then the

-complete monoid ring

is still a perfectoid ring, and comes equipped with a canonical pre-log structure

(4.1)

\frac{1}{p}{\mathbb{N}}\oplus\frac{1}{p}{\mathbb{N}}\to(R\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}},y^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle,\cdot).

Consider now the pushout
\frac{1}{p}{\mathbb{N}}\oplus_{\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{p}{\mathbb{N}}

(4.2)

\frac{1}{p}{\mathbb{N}}\oplus_{\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{p}{\mathbb{N}}\to(R\langle\frac{1}{p}{\mathbb{N}}\oplus_{\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{p}{\mathbb{N}}\rangle,\cdot)\cong(R\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}},y^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle/(x-y),\cdot)

which we claim to be a log quasiregular semiperfectoid ring. Let us write
(S,Q)
for the pre-log ring determined by (
4.2
), and
\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle,\langle y^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle
for the two copies of
\frac{1}{p}{\mathbb{N}}

As conditions (2) and (3) in Definition
4.12
are clear, it only remains to show that
(S,Q)
is log quasisyntomic, that is, we want to show that
{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
has
p
-complete
{\rm Tor}
-amplitude in
[-1,0]
.

Using the transitivity sequence

S\otimes_{R\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle}{\mathbb{L}}_{R\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\xrightarrow{}{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\xrightarrow{}{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/R\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle}

and the fact that
{\mathbb{L}}_{R\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
has
p
-complete
{\rm Tor}
-amplitude in degree
-1
(with derived
p
-completion
R\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle[1]
, since
R\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle
a perfectoid ring), we find that it suffices to prove that

has
p
-complete
{\rm Tor}
-amplitude in
[-1,0]
in order to draw the same conclusion about
{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
.

For this, we observe that there are equivalences

{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/R\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle}\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/(R\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle,\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle)}\xleftarrow{\simeq}S\otimes_{R\langle y^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle}{\mathbb{L}}_{(R\langle y^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle,\langle y^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle)/(R\langle t\rangle,\langle t\rangle)}

after

-completion. The first equivalence is due to Corollary

(and the transitivity sequence), while the second is an application of base change for the Gabber cotangent complex since

can be realized as the pushout of the diagram

(R\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle,\langle x^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle)\xleftarrow{}(R\langle t\rangle,\langle t\rangle)\xrightarrow{}(R\langle y^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle,\langle y^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle).

It thus suffices to prove that

{\mathbb{L}}_{(R\langle y^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle,\langle y^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle)/(R\langle t\rangle,\langle t\rangle)}\xrightarrow{\simeq}(R\langle y^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle\otimes_{R\langle t\rangle}{\mathbb{L}}_{(R\langle t\rangle,\langle t\rangle)/R})[1]

arising from the transitivity sequence of the Gabber cotangent complex associated to the composite

4.24.

{\rm lQRSPerfd}^{\rm op}

Analogously to

Lemma 4.25
.

The category
{\rm lQRSPerfd}^{\rm op}
with log quasisyntomic covers forms a site.

Proof.

We only need to show that the class of log quasisyntomic covers is closed under pullbacks in
\mathrm{lQRSPerfd}^{\rm op}
.
For this,
let

be a log quasisyntomic cover in
\mathrm{lQRSPerfd}

(A,M)\to(A^{\prime},M^{\prime})

Then

Since

is log quasisyntomic,
then so is
(B^{\prime},N^{\prime})
. Moreover,

receives a map from a perfectoid ring (since

∎

4.26.
Existence of log quasisyntomic covers

We now aim to prove that every log quasisyntomic
(A,M)
admits a cover by a log quasiregular semiperfectoid ring:

.

Let
(A,M)
be in
{\rm lQSyn}
. Then there exists a log quasisyntomic cover
(A,M)\to(S,Q)
, where

is log quasiregular semiperfectoid ring.

Proof.

There exists a free
p
-complete algebra

and surjections

and

.
As in the proof of

, Lemma 4.28]
, we obtain the quasisyntomic cover

by formally adjoining all

-th roots of

and

for all

in the

-complete sense.
Observe that

is a perfectoid ring.

We impose the pre-log structures

and

sending the

-th unit vector to

for all

.
Since

In particular,

-completely flat,

.
Consider now the cocartesian square defining

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{Z}[P_{\infty}]/\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[P_{\infty}]}F_{\infty}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}_{F_{\infty}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}_{(\mathbb{Z}[P_{\infty}],P_{\infty})/\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[P_{\infty}]}F_{\infty}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}_{(F_{\infty},P_{\infty})/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}.}

We already know

(in fact, it is a perfectoid ring).
Together with Lemma
4.17
, we see that

has

-complete Tor amplitude in
[-1,0]
.
From the transitivity triangle for
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to(F,P)\to(F_{\infty},P_{\infty})

\mathbb{L}_{(F_{\infty},P_{\infty})/(F,P)}
has

-complete Tor amplitude in
[-1,0]
.

The map

is integral by
[

, Proposition I.4.6.7]
since
\mathbb{Z}[P]\to\mathbb{Z}[P_{\infty}]
is flat.
Furthermore,

is
p
-completely faithfully flat over

.
It follows that
(F,P)\to(F_{\infty},P_{\infty})
is a log quasisyntomic cover.
Let
(A,M)\to(S,Q)
be the

-complete base change of

along

,
where

.
The integrality of

ensures that

is integral.
Since

,

.
Furthermore,

.

.
∎

Let

be in
{\rm lQRSPerfd}

6.
Logarithmic
{\rm THH}
over perfectoid bases

We now work towards the proof of Theorem

by establishing analogs of the results of
[
3
, Section 6]
in the log setting. As one consequence we obtain Proposition

{\rm THH}
of object in

6.1.
Log
{\rm THH}
of log quasismooth rings

We work with a perfectoid base
R
. Let
u
be a generator of
\ker{\theta_{R}}/\ker\theta_{R}^{2}=R[1]
, where
\theta_{R}
is again Fontaine’s map. As observed in Lemma
4.16
, this gives a trivialization of
(\mathbb{L}_{R/\mathbb{Z}_{p}})^{\wedge}_{p}
, and the HKR Theorem gives then an isomorphism
\ker{\theta_{R}}/\ker\theta_{R}^{2}\cong\pi_{2}\HH(R;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.
By
[
3
, Theorem 6.1]
, this extends to an isomorphism of graded rings
\pi_{*}{\rm THH}(R;\mathbb{Z}_{p})\cong R[u]
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
(A,M)
be an
R
-algebra. There is an
S^{1}
-equivariant cofiber sequence

{\rm THH}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})[2]\xrightarrow{u}{\rm THH}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{}{\rm HH}((A,M)/R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}),

and hence corresponding cofiber sequences on homotopy fixed points and Tate constructions.

Proof.

This follows by applying
-\otimes_{{\rm THH}(R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}{\rm THH}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
to the cofiber sequence

{\rm THH}(R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})[2]\xrightarrow{u}{\rm THH}(R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{}{\rm HH}(R/R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

of
[
3
, Theorem 6.7]
and Proposition
3.4
.
∎

By Lemma
3.6
, the canonical map

{\rm THH}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{}{\rm HH}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

is an isomorphism in degrees less than or equal to two. In particular, we may mimic
[
3
, Construction 6.8]
to obtain a map

(6.1)

(\Omega^{*}_{(A,M)/{\mathbb{Z}}})^{\wedge}_{p}\xrightarrow{}\pi_{*}{\rm THH}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

of graded
A
-algebras. The following is then a version of
[
3
, Corollary 6.9]
,
with an analogous proof:

Corollary 6.3
.

The map (
6.1
) linearizes to a map

(\Omega^{*}_{(A,M)/{\mathbb{Z}}})^{\wedge}_{p}\otimes_{R}\pi_{*}{\rm THH}(R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{}\pi_{*}{\rm THH}((A,M);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

which is an isomorphism if
(A,M)
is log quasismooth. ∎

From this, we formally obtain an analog of
[
3
, Corollary 6.10]
: The functor
{\rm THH}((-,-);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
on pre-log
R
-algebras whose underlying ring is
p
-complete admits a filtration with graded pieces shifts of wedge powers of the log cotangent complex
({\mathbb{L}}_{(-,-)/R})^{\wedge}_{p}
; the sum of the degree of the wedge power and the degree shift is even.

This, in particular, gives rise to a proof of Theorem
1.6
,
the setting and statement we recall here.

Let
(S,Q)
be a log quasiregular semiperfectoid pre-log ring and let
R
be a perfectoid ring surjecting to
S
. Consider the surjection
R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle\to S
, where
Q^{\flat}
is the tilt of
Q
.

Theorem 6.4
.

With notation as in the previous paragraph, there is a cofiber sequence

{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})[2]\xrightarrow{u}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{}{\rm HH}(S/R\langle Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\rangle;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}).

Proof.

Applying Lemma
6.2
to the case at hand, we obtain a cofiber sequence

{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})[2]\xrightarrow{u}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{}{\rm HH}((S,Q)/R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}).

Now we apply Proposition
5.9
to the cofiber term
{\rm HH}((S,Q)/R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
to identify it with the ordinary Hochschild homology
{\rm HH}(S/R\langle Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\rangle;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, which gives the desired result.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

To make the slogan that log topological Hochschild homology is a one-parameter deformation of ordinary Hochschild homology slightly more precise, observe that

\{\tau_{\leq n}{\rm THH}(R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\otimes_{{\rm THH}(R\langle Q^{\flat}\rangle;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\}

is a
weak Postnikov tower
in the sense of
[

, which can be proved using the techniques therein. If lifting problems along this tower could be phrased in terms of (logarithmic) deformation theory, one could attempt to use this to establish a universal property for logarithmic topological Hochschild homology, as alluded to in the introduction.

Remark 6.6
.

Note that if
Q^{\flat}\to Q
was already exact, then

would be a perfectoid ring, and by
[

,

would deform to the non-log version

. In particular, the canonical map
{\rm THH}(S;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\to{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
is an equivalence in this case.

Proposition 6.7
.

With the notation of the previous paragraph, the log topological Hochschild homology
{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
admits a complete descending filtration with graded pieces

\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq n\\
i-n\ \mathrm{even}\end{subarray}}(\wedge^{i}_{S}{\mathbb{L}}_{S/R\langle Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\rangle})^{\wedge}_{p}[n],

where
Q^{\flat,\rm rep}\to Q
denotes the repletion of the canonical map
Q^{\flat}\to Q
.

Proof.

Since

is a perfectoid ring, the analog of
[

, Corollary 6.10]
described above applies to give a filtration with graded pieces

\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq n\\

Now the result follows from Proposition
4.20
.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

Note that the isomorphism

7.
Filtrations and log prismatic cohomology

We now aim to prove Theorem
1.8
from the introduction.

7.1.
Evenness of log
{\rm THH}
of quasiregular semiperfectoids

Let
R
be a perfectoid ring. As a consequence of our work so far, we obtain analogs of
[
3
, Theorems 7.1 and 7.2]
with similar proofs. For the convenience of the reader, we choose to spell this out here:

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
(S,Q)
be in
{\rm lQRSPerfd}_{R}
.

(1)

The homotopy groups
\pi_{*}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
are concentrated in even degrees.

(2)

Multiplication by
u\in\pi_{2}{\rm THH}(R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
induces an injection

\pi_{2i-2}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{u}\pi_{2i}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}).

(3)

Consider
\pi_{0}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})[u^{\pm 1}]
as a filtered commutative
R
-algebra, equipped with an increasing filtration. Then there is a canonical isomorphism

(\Gamma_{S}^{*}(\pi_{1}{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/R})^{\wedge}_{p})^{\wedge}_{p}\cong{\rm gr}_{*}(\pi_{0}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})[u^{\pm 1}])

of graded rings. On the left-hand side, the first
p
-completion is of an
S
-module, the second of a graded ring.

(4)

Each even homotopy group
\pi_{2i}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
is
p
-completely flat over
S
.

Proof.

We first prove (1). As remarked after the proof of Corollary
6.3
, the log topological Hochschild homology
{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
admits a complete filtration with graded pieces a sum of shifts of wedge powers of the log cotangent complex
({\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/R})^{\wedge}_{p}
; moreover, the sum of the degree of the shift and the wedge power is even. By Lemma
5.2
, each wedge power
(\wedge_{S}^{i}{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/R})^{\wedge}_{p}
has
p
-complete
{\rm Tor}
-amplitude in (homological) degree
i
, and hence it lives in degree
i
by
[
3
, Lemma 4.7]
. Hence the associated graded of the complete filtration of
{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
lives in even degrees, which proves (1).

We now prove (2) and (3). Consider the cofiber sequence

{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})[2]\xrightarrow{u}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{}{\rm HH}((S,Q)/R);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

of Lemma
6.2
. The associated long exact sequence splits into short exact sequences

0\xrightarrow{}\pi_{2i-2}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{u}\pi_{2i}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\xrightarrow{}\pi_{2i}{\rm HH}((S,Q)/R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\to 0

since both
\pi_{*}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
and
\pi_{*}{\rm HH}((S,Q)/R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
live in even degrees. This proves (2), and part (3) follows from the isomorphism
\pi_{2i}{\rm HH}((S,Q)/R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\cong\pi_{i}(\wedge_{S}^{i}{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/R})^{\wedge}_{p}
from Corollary
5.3
, as well as the isomorphism
\pi_{i}(\wedge_{S}^{i}{\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/R})^{\wedge}_{p}\cong(\Gamma_{S}^{i}\pi_{1}({\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/R})^{\wedge}_{p})^{\wedge}_{p}
(note that the right-hand side is discrete since the cotangent complex is a
p
-completely flat module after
p
-completion).

Since
(\Gamma_{S}^{i}\pi_{1}({\mathbb{L}}_{(S,Q)/R})^{\wedge}_{p})^{\wedge}_{p}
is
p
-completely flat (see
[
3
, Proof of Theorem 7.1]
), this also proves part (4).
∎

7.3.
Definition of Nygaard-complete log prismatic cohomology

As a consequence of Theorem
7.2
, for a log quasiregular semiperfectoid
R
-algebra
(S,Q)
, the homotopy fixed points and Tate spectral seqences computing
\pi_{*}{\rm TC}^{-}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
and
\pi_{*}{\rm TP}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
degenerate. This equips the
Nygaard-complete log prismatic cohomology

\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(S,Q)}:=\pi_{0}{\rm TC}^{-}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\cong\pi_{0}{\rm TP}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

with a complete, descending filtration
{\rm Fil}_{N}^{\geq\bullet}\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(S,Q)}
, that we call the
logarithmic Nygaard filtration
. The associated graded
{\rm gr}_{N}^{i}\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(S,Q)}
identifies with log topological Hochschild homology

{\rm gr}_{N}^{i}\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(S,Q)}\cong\pi_{2i}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}),

and the
i
th filtration level
{\rm Fil}^{\geq 2i}_{N}\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(S,Q)}
identifies with
\pi_{2i}{\rm TC}^{-}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, where the inclusion to
\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(S,Q)}:=\pi_{0}{\rm TC}^{-}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
identifies with multiplication by
v^{i}\in\pi_{-2i}{\rm TC}^{-}(R;{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
. As in
[
3
, Theorem 7.2(4), (5)]
, the cyclotomic Frobenius from log
{\rm TC}^{-}
to log
{\rm TP}
gives rise to divided Frobenii

\varphi_{S,i}\colon{\rm Fil}^{\geq i}_{N}\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(S,Q)}\to\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(S,Q)},

and there is a natural isomorphism
\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(S,Q)}/\xi\cong\widehat{L\Omega}_{(S,Q)/R}
.

The above paragraph summarizes the log version of
[
3
, Theorem 7.2]
.

Remark 7.4

(Relationship with Koshikawa–Yao)
.

Let
S
be a quasiregular semiperfectoid ring. Recall that Bhatt–Scholze
[
4
]
defines prismatic cohomology
{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{S}
via the prismatic site.
They prove
[
4
, Theorem 13.1]
that a canonical comparison map
{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{S}\to\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{S}
exhibits the target as the Nygaard completion of the source, where the Nygaard filtration is defined directly on
{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{S}
.

Koshikawa
[
23
]
introduced the log prismatic site. In work with Yao
[
KY
]
, they define a certain Nygaard filtration on (a derived variant of) log prismatic cohomology
{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{(S,Q)}
for
(S,Q)
a log quasiregular semiperfectoid pre-log ring. We now review one key idea for their setup and explain how it translates into the present paper’s language.

Recall that, if
S
is a quasiregular semiperfectoid ring, then the prismatic cohomology
{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{S/A_{\rm inf}(R)}:={{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{S/(A_{\rm inf}(R),{\rm ker}(\theta))}
is initial
[
4
, Proposition 7.10]
, which gives rise to a comparison map
{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{S}\to\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{S}
.

In the logarithmic setting, we may consider the object
{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{(S,Q)/A_{{\rm inf}}(R)}
, and try to define and study a Nygaard filtration on it in hopes of obtaining an analog of Bhatt–Scholze’s comparison result.

For this, it seems that Koshikawa–Yao are using techniques similar to our Proposition
4.20
, Proposition
5.9
, and Theorem
6.4
to describe their log prismatic cohomology as ordinary prismatic cohomology, at the price of working with a non-perfect base prism. For this reason, we believe that our Theorem
6.4
and the surrounding techniques will be the key ingredient in comparing our Nygaard-complete log prismatic cohomology with that considered by Koshikawa–Yao, and we hope to elaborate upon this in the future.

7.5.

The following is analogous to
[
3
, Construction 7.4, Proposition 7.5]
. Let
R
be a perfectoid ring and
(S,Q)
be a log quasiregular semiperfectoid
R
-algebra. By Theorem
7.2
, the
S
-module
\pi_{2i}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
admits a finite filtration with graded pieces shifts of wedge powers of the log cotangent complex

. In particular, Theorem

implies that the presheaf

{\rm lQRSPerfd}_{R}\to D(R),\quad(S,Q)\mapsto\pi_{2i}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

is a sheaf. By unfolding, we obtain a sheaf

{\rm lQSyn}_{R}\to D(R),\quad(S,Q)\mapsto\pi_{2i}{\rm THH}((S,Q);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{\sqsupset},

and so we get the following analog of

:

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
(A,M)

Sample application: the de Rham comparison

In future work, we intend to prove a log analog of
[
4
, Theorem 13.1]
relating our construction with that of
[
22
]
. From this, we can transport the various comparison results relating log prismatic cohomology to log de Rham, log crystalline, and log étale cohomology from those of Koshikawa–Yao. This will provide an extension of the results in
[
4
]
to the bad reduction (semistable) case.

As a reality-check, we demonstrate how to obtain the log de Rham comparison (up to a Frobenius twist, see Remark
8.2
) directly from our setup, as in
[
3
, Section 11]
. Let us keep the notation of
[
3
, Section 11]
. We fix a discretely valued extension
K
of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. We let
{\cal O}_{K}
be its ring of integers,
k
its residue field, and we fix
\bar{\omega}
a uniformizer of
{\cal O}_{K}
. We write

for the
p
-adic completion of

. There is a canonical map
W(k)[[z]]\to{\cal O}_{K}
sending

to the uniformizer
\bar{\omega}
, whose kernel is generated by an Eisenstein polynomial

.

Recall that an integral map of integral monoids is of

if its relative Frobenius is exact. If

is a pre-log ring with

an adic ring, classically complete with respect to a finitely generated ideal

, we shall write (following

for the corresponding log formal scheme.

be an integral pre-log ring and assume that

is log smooth of Cartier type over

. Scalar extension along

induces an isomorphism

\widehat{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}_{(A,M)/((W(k)[[z]],\langle z\rangle)}\otimes_{W(k)[[z]]}{\cal O}_{K}\simeq(\Omega_{(A,M)/({\cal O}_{K},\langle\pi\rangle)})^{\wedge}_{p}

of
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-

-algebras.

We refer to
[

for material on log formal schemes.

Remark 8.2
.

Theorem

8.3.
Log cyclotomic bases

As in
[
3
, Section 11]
, we will need to work with the relative version of logarithmic
{\rm THH}
. As recorded in Lemma
3.4
, we have the transitivity formula

{\rm THH}((A,M)/(R,P))\simeq R\otimes_{{\rm THH}(R,P)}{\rm THH}(A,M).

For this relative
{\rm THH}
to inherit a cyclotomic structure from its absolute building blocks, we therefore need the following compatibility condition (cf.
[
13
, Definition 3.2.1]
):

Definition 8.4
.

A
log cyclotomic base
is a pre-log ring spectrum
(R,P)
together with a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm THH}((R,P);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm THH}((R,P);{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{tC_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R^{tC_{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{p}}

of
S^{1}
-equivariant
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-rings.

Lemma 8.5
.

The pre-log ring spectrum
({\mathbb{S}}[z],\langle z\rangle)
is a log cyclotomic base.

Proof.

This is similar to
[
3
, Proposition 11.3]
, using the description of the circle action on the replete bar construction
B^{\rm rep}(\langle z\rangle)
provided in
[
37
, Proposition 3.21]
. This is spelled out below.

Recall (from e.g.
[
37
, Proposition 3.21]
) that the cyclic bar construction
B^{\rm cyc}(\langle z\rangle)
decomposes as

B^{\rm cyc}(\langle z\rangle)\simeq*\sqcup(\bigsqcup_{j\geq 1}S^{1}(j));

i.e. as the subobject of
S^{1}\times{\mathbb{Z}}
given by the union of
S^{1}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}
and the zero element
(0,1)
. For the replete bar construction, there is another circle in “degree zero”, that is,

B^{\rm rep}(\langle z\rangle)\simeq\bigsqcup_{j\geq 0}S^{1}(j)=S^{1}\times{\mathbb{N}}.

The circle action extends in the natural manner:
t.(s,n)=(t^{n}s,n)
. In particular, the action on the degree zero circle
S^{1}(0)=S^{1}\times\{0\}
is trivial.

By Construction
3.9
, the replete Frobenius
\varphi_{p}\colon{\mathbb{S}}[B^{\rm rep}(\langle z\rangle)]\to{\mathbb{S}}[B^{\rm rep}(\langle z\rangle)]^{tC_{p}}
factors through
{\mathbb{S}}[\psi_{p}]\colon{\mathbb{S}}[B^{\rm rep}(\langle z\rangle)]\to{\mathbb{S}}[B^{\rm rep}(\langle z\rangle)^{hC_{p}}]
, where
\psi_{p}
is given by
(s,n)\mapsto(s^{p},pn)
. In particular, the resulting diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}[B^{\rm rep}(\langle z\rangle)]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}[B^{\rm rep}(\langle z\rangle)]^{tC_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}[z]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}[z]^{tC_{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces z\mapsto z^{p}}

is a commutative diagram of
S^{1}
-equivariant
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-rings. The only part of this statement which is not covered by
[
3
, Proposition 11.3]
is that its “degree zero” part

(8.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}[S^{1}(0)]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}[S^{1}(0)]^{tC_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{S}}^{tC_{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{p}}

commutes, which is formal.
∎

Remark 8.6
.

Commutativity of the diagram (
8.1
) is closely related to the “spherical log point”
({\mathbb{S}},\langle z\rangle)
(with structure map
{\mathbb{S}}[z]\to{\mathbb{S}},z\mapsto 0

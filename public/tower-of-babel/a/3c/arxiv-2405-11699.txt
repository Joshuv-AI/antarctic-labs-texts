Fixed-parameter tractability of canonical polyadic decomposition over finite fields
Jason Yang

Abstract
We present a simple proof that finding a rank- R R canonical polyadic decomposition of a 3-dimensional tensor over a finite field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} is fixed-parameter tractable with respect to R R and 𝔽 \mathbb{F} . We also show a nontrivial upper bound on the time complexity of this problem.

Introduction

Given a
n_{0}\times n_{1}\times n_{2}
tensor (multidimensional array)
\mathcal{T}
, a rank-
R
canonical polyadic decomposition (CPD) is a triplet of
factor matrices

A,B,C
with shapes
R\times n_{0},\ R\times n_{1},\ R\times n_{2}
respectively, such that (s.t.)

\forall 0\leq i<n_{0},\ 0\leq j<n_{1},\ 0\leq k<n_{2}:\mathcal{T}_{i,j,k}=\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}A_{r,i}B_{r,j}C_{r,k}.

This equation can also be written as
\mathcal{T}=\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}A_{r,:}\times B_{r,:}\times C_{r,:}
, where
M_{r,:}
denotes the
r
-th row of
M
and
\times
denotes the outer product (defined in Section
1.1
).

CPD, also called parallel factor analysis (PARAFAC), plays a central role in the field of fast matrix multiplication, as finding an asymptotically fast algorithm for this problem is equivalent to finding a low-rank CPD of a specific 3-dimensional tensor
[
2
]
.
Although normally these CPDs should be over
\mathbb{R}
, there has been significant progress in recent years that restricts CPDs to be over a finite field, especially
\mathbb{F}_{2}

[
4
]

[
6
]

[
7
]

[
1
]
, with some of these CPDs having been lifted to the integers (so that they also work over
\mathbb{R}
).
This trend motivates us to restrict our attention to CPDs over finite fields.

Our objective is to solve rank-
R
CPD for an arbitrary tensor
\mathcal{T}
.

Solving
means that we must detect whether or not a rank-
R
CPD of
\mathcal{T}
exists, and return such a CPD if it exists.
We require an algorithm for this problem to give an exact (not approximate) CPD, to work for all possible
\mathcal{T}
, and to be guaranteed to be correct; to our knowledge, no previous work on CPD
[
4
]

[
6
]

[
10
]

[
9
]

[
3
]

[
8
]
satisfies all conditions at once, and most previous work only considers CPDs over
\mathbb{R}
.

We present a simple proof that solving rank-
R
CPD is fixed-parameter tractable with respect to (w.r.t.) the rank
R
and ground field
\mathbb{F}
. This result is formally stated as Theorem
1
and proven in Section

.
Additionally, this is close to the best result we can hope for, since if
R

Theorem 1
.

For whole number
R
and finite field
\mathbb{F}
, determining whether a rank-
R
CPD over
\mathbb{F}
of a
n_{0}\times n_{1}\times n_{2}
tensor exists, and constructing such a CPD if it exists, can be done in
O(f(|\mathbb{F}|,R)+\mathrm{poly}(n_{0},n_{1},n_{2},R))

Proof of fixed-parameter tractability

Given an input tensor
\mathcal{T}\in\mathbb{F}^{n_{0}\times n_{1}\times n_{2}}
, construct a basis of its
i
-slices
\mathcal{T}_{i,:,:}
. Explicitly, we apply row reduction on the flattened tensor
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\vdots\\
\hline\cr\mathrm{vec}\left(\mathcal{T}_{i,:,:}\right)\\
\hline\cr\vdots\end{array}\right]_{i}
, creating a matrix
\Gamma\in\mathrm{GL}\left(n_{0},\ \mathbb{F}\right)
s.t.
\Gamma\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}=\mathrm{rref}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\right)
.
Removing the all-zero rows of
\mathrm{rref}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\right)
and unflattening yields a
R_{0}\times n_{1}\times n_{2}
tensor
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, where
R_{0}=\mathrm{rank}\left(\widetilde{T}\right)
, satisfying the property
\forall i^{\prime}:\sum_{i}\Gamma_{i^{\prime},i}\mathcal{T}_{i,:,:}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{i^{\prime},:,:}&i^{\prime}<R_{0}\\
O&\mathrm{else}\end{cases}
.

Using the invertibility of
\Gamma
, one can convert a rank-
R
CPD for
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
into one for
\mathcal{T}
and vice versa, so solving rank-
R
CPD for
\mathcal{T}
is equivalent to solving rank-
R
CPD for
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
:

\forall i,j,k:\sum_{0\leq r<R}A_{r,i}B_{r,j}C_{r,k}=\mathcal{T}_{i,j,k}\Longrightarrow\forall i^{\prime},j,k:\sum_{0\leq r<R}\left(\sum_{0\leq i<n_{0}}\Gamma_{i^{\prime},i}A_{r,i}\right)B_{r,j}C_{r,k}=\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{i^{\prime},j,k}

\forall i^{\prime},j,k:\sum_{0\leq r<R}A^{\prime}_{r,i^{\prime}}B_{r,j}C_{r,k}=\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{i^{\prime},j,k}\Longrightarrow\forall i,j,k:\sum_{0\leq r<R}\left(\sum_{0\leq i^{\prime}<R_{0}}\left(\Gamma^{-1}\right)_{i,i^{\prime}}A^{\prime}_{r,i^{\prime}}\right)B_{r,j}C_{r,k}=\mathcal{T}_{i,j,k}.

The upshot of this process is that the existence of a rank-
R
CPD of
\mathcal{T}
implies that
R_{0}\leq R
,
since having
\mathcal{T}=\sum_{0\leq r<R}A_{r,:}\times B_{r,:}\times C_{r,:}
implies
\forall i:\mathcal{T}_{i,:,:}\in\mathrm{span}\left(\left\{B_{r,:}\times C_{r,:}\right\}_{0\leq r<R}\right)
.
Thus, if we detect
R_{0}>R
, we immediately know there is no solution and can terminate.

We can apply this basis extraction along the other two dimensions,
first constructing
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{R_{0}\times R_{1}\times n_{2}}
s.t.
\left\{\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}_{:,j^{\prime},:}\right\}_{j^{\prime}}
is a basis of
\left\{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{:,j,:}\right\}_{j}
,
then constructing
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{R_{0}\times R_{1}\times R_{2}}
s.t.
\left\{\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{:,:,k^{\prime}}\right\}_{k^{\prime}}
is a basis of
\left\{\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}_{:,:,k}\right\}_{k}
.
Using a similar argument as above, we have
R_{1}\leq R
and
R_{2}\leq R
(otherwise we terminate), and solving rank-
R
CPD of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime\prime}
is equivalent to solving rank-
R
CPD for
\mathcal{T}
.

Since
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime\prime}
has length
\leq R
across each dimension, we can simply use brute force to solve rank-
R
CPD for it, without spending an amount of time that depends on
n_{0},n_{1},n_{2}
.
Doing so takes
\mathcal{F}\left(R(R_{0}+R_{1}+R_{2}),\ 0\right)\in\mathcal{F}\left(3R^{2},\ 0\right)
time and
O(R(R_{0}+R_{1}+R_{2}))\in O(R^{2})
space.

Finally, constructing
\mathcal{T}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime\prime}
and converting a CPD for
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime\prime}
into one for
\mathcal{T}
can be done with several row reductions and matrix products, each of which takes polynomial time and space, so we are done.

One can extend this proof to show that rank-
R
CPD over an arbitrary
D
-dimensional tensor is fixed-parameter tractable w.r.t. the rank
R
, ground field
\mathbb{F}
, and tensor dimension
D
.

Tighter time complexity

The proof in Section
2
naturally corresponds to an algorithm for rank-

CPD. Here we present tighter bounds on the time complexity for this algorithm. For simplicity, we sometimes upper-bound

with
n:=\mathrm{max}\left(n_{0},n_{1},n_{2}\right)
and
R_{0},R_{1},R_{2}
with

3.1
Pre- and post-processing

Preprocessing, i.e. constructing
\mathcal{T}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime\prime}
and their corresponding change-of-basis matrices
\Gamma\in\mathrm{GL}\left(n_{0},\ \mathbb{F}\right)

\Gamma^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GL}\left(n_{1},\ \mathbb{F}\right)

\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{GL}\left(n_{2},\ \mathbb{F}\right)

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}

Instead of naïvely running row-reduction, we notice that our algorithm only has to run to completion if each of the flattenings of
\mathcal{T}

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}
have rank

. We can thus add early termination to row-reduction to get the following result:

Lemma 1
.

Given a matrix
M\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times n}
, and integer

, we can do the following in
O((\mathrm{min}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)+1)(m+1)(m+n))
time, where
r:=\mathrm{rank}\left(M\right)
:

•

If
r\leq r^{\prime}
: return some
C,J\in\mathrm{GL}\left(m,\ \mathbb{F}\right)
and
F\in\mathbb{F}^{r\times n}
s.t.
M=C_{:,:r}F
and
J=C^{-1}
.

•

Else: return nothing

We give a proof in Appendix
A.1
. Using this result allows preprocessing to be done in
O((R+1)n^{3})
time.

Postprocessing, i.e. converting a rank-
R
CPD of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime\prime}
(if it exists) to a rank-
R
of
\mathcal{T}
, involves multiplying each factor matrix of the CPD of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime\prime}
with
\leq R
many columns of the corresponding change-of-basis matrix (e.g. when converting from
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
to
\mathcal{T}
in the previous section, we calculate

O(R^{2}n)

Thus, we can replace the

term in Theorem

with
O((R+1)n^{3})
; for fixed

) if

.

Row reduction

4

C_{0}
is a solution,
CX^{-1}=C_{0}
. Thus,
C=C_{0}X
and
F=X^{-1}F_{0}
.
∎

Since invertible matrices form a group, it follows that all pairs of full-rank factorizations of
M
are reachable from each other via the transformation
(C,F)\mapsto(CX,X^{-1}F)
, so the lemma stays true when
(C_{0},\ F_{0})
is an arbitrary full-rank factorization of
M
, not just the one where
F_{0}=\mathrm{rref}\left(M\right)_{:r,:}
.

Lastly, we bound the number of
m\times n
matrices with rank
\leq 1
and enumerate them as efficiently as possible (up to
\mathrm{poly}(R)
factors).

Lemma 7
.

There are
\mathcal{F}\left(m+n,\ 1\right)
many matrices
M\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times n}
s.t.
\mathrm{rank}\left(M\right)\leq 1
,
and enumerating all of them takes
\mathcal{F}\left(m+n,\ 1\right)
time.

Proof.

WLOG
\mathrm{rank}\left(M\right)=1
, otherwise
M=O
(only one possibility).
Then there is exactly one way of expressing
M
as
u\times v
with the requirement that
u
is canonically normalized, so we can iterate over all such
u\in\mathbb{F}^{m}
and all nonzero
v\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
in
\frac{|\mathbb{F}|^{m}-1}{|\mathbb{F}|-1}\left(|\mathbb{F}|^{n}-1\right)=\mathcal{F}\left(m+n,\ 1\right)
time.
∎

Note that the bound on the number of such
M
is a corollary of Lemma
6
.

A.3.1
Rank-
r
factorization summands of a rank-
r
matrix

We now prove Lemma
2
.
Using the same matrices as defined in Lemma
6
, we have that for any
i
,
C_{:,i}F_{i,:}=C_{0}\left(X_{:,i}(X^{-1})_{i,:}\right)F_{0}
. Since the paranthesized part is a
r\times r
matrix of rank
\leq 1
, by Lemma
7
the whole expression has
\mathcal{F}\left(2r,\ 1\right)
many possibilities.

A.3.2
Rank-
r
factorization summands of a rank-
(r-1)
matrix

To prove Lemma
3
, we further derive a rank bound on the factors of a matrix factorization.

Lemma 8
.

Let
(U,\ V)
be a rank-
r
factorization of
M\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times n}
and
q:=\mathrm{rank}\left(M\right)
. Then
\mathrm{rank}\left(U\right)+\mathrm{rank}\left(V\right)\leq r+q
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
,
\exists F\in\mathrm{GL}\left(m,\ \mathbb{F}\right)
s.t.
FM=\mathrm{rref}\left(M\right)
, so
(FM)_{q:,:}=(FU)_{q:,:}V=O
.
Transposing this equation shows that each column of
((FU)_{q:,:})^{\intercal}
is in
\mathrm{nullspace}\left(V^{\intercal}\right)
, so
\mathrm{rank}\left(((FU)_{q:,:})^{\intercal}\right)=\mathrm{rank}\left((FU)_{q:,:}\right)=\mathrm{dim}\left(\mathrm{nullspace}\left(V^{\intercal}\right)\right)
.
By the rank-nullity theorem,
\mathrm{dim}\left(\mathrm{nullspace}\left(V^{\intercal}\right)\right)=r-\mathrm{rank}\left(V^{\intercal}\right)=r-\mathrm{rank}\left(V\right)
.
Adding back the first
q
rows of
FU
, we have that
\mathrm{rank}\left(FU\right)\leq r+q-\mathrm{rank}\left(V\right)
. Since left- (or right-) multiplying by an invertible matrix does not change matrix rank,
\mathrm{rank}\left(U\right)+\mathrm{rank}\left(V\right)\leq r+q
.
∎

When
q=r-1
, we get a stronger result:

Lemma 9
.

Let
(C,\ F)
be a rank-
r
factorization of
M\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times n}
.
Suppose
\mathrm{rank}\left(M\right)=r-1
, and let
(C_{0},\ F_{0})
be a rank-
(r-1)
factorization of
M
.
Then
(\exists G\in\mathbb{F}^{(r-1)\times r}\textrm{ s.t. }C=C_{0}G)
or
(\exists H\in\mathbb{F}^{r\times(r-1)}\textrm{ s.t. }F=HF_{0})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
8
,
\mathrm{rank}\left(C\right)+\mathrm{rank}\left(F\right)\leq 2r-1
. Since
\mathrm{rank}\left(C\right)\geq r-1
and
\mathrm{rank}\left(F\right)\geq r-1
, we must have
\mathrm{rank}\left(C\right)=r-1
or
\mathrm{rank}\left(F\right)=r-1
.

If
\mathrm{rank}\left(C\right)=r-1
, then there exists a rank-
(r-1)
factorization
(C^{\prime},\ A)
of
C
. Then
(C^{\prime},\ AF)
is a rank-
(r-1)
factorization of
M
, so by Lemma
6

\exists X\in\mathrm{GL}\left(r-1,\ \mathbb{F}\right)
s.t.
(C^{\prime},\ AF)=(C_{0}X,\ X^{-1}F_{0})
, and thus
C=C_{0}(XA)
.

Otherwise,
\mathrm{rank}\left(F\right)=r-1
, and a similar result can be obtained for
F
by transposing the previous argument.
∎

If such
G
exists, then for any
i
,
C_{:,i}F_{i,:}=C_{0}(G_{:,i}F_{i,:})
; otherwise,
C_{:,i}F_{i,:}=(C_{:,i}H_{i,:})F_{0}
. In each case, the paranthesized part is a matrix of rank
\leq 1
, with shape
(r-1)\times n
and
m\times(r-1)
respectively. By Lemma
7
,
C_{:,i}F_{i,:}
has a total of
\mathcal{F}\left(m+r-1,\ 1\right)+\mathcal{F}\left(n+r-1,\ 1\right)
many possibilities.

Note that when
q=r-2
, we can no longer upper-bound the number of possibilities of
C_{:,i}F_{i,:}
for any individual
i
beyond the naïve bound of
\mathcal{F}\left(m+n,\ 1\right)
, since if
(U,\ V)
is a full-rank factorization of
M
, then
\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}U&u&u\end{array}\right],\ \left[\begin{array}[]{c}V\\
\hline\cr v\\
\hline\cr-v\end{array}\right]\right)
is a rank-
r
factorization of
M
for arbitrary vectors
u\in\mathbb{F}^{m\times 1},\ v\in\mathbb{F}^{1\times n}
. However, it may still be possible to give a nontrivial bound on the number of possibilities of lists
\left[C_{:,i}F_{i,:}\right]_{0\leq i<\ell}
w.r.t.
\ell
for
\ell>1
.

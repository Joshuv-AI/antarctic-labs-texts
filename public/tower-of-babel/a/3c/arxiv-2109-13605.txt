Algebraic properties of the information geometry’s fourth Frobenius manifold
N. Combe, P. Combe, H. Nencka

Abstract
Recently, it has been shown that within the statistical manifold, related to exponential families, there exists a submanifold having a Frobenius manifold structure. This appears as the fourth class of Frobenius manifolds. It has a structure of a projective manifold over a rank two Frobenius algebra 𝔄 \mathfrak{A} , being the algebra of paracomplex numbers and generated by 1 , ε 1,\varepsilon such that ε 2 = 1 \varepsilon^{2}=1 . This last result is a key step towards an algebraization of the results concerning the manifold of probability distributions and thus offers a new perspective on it. In this paper, we prove that the fourth Frobenius manifold is decomposed into a pair of symmetric totally geodesic pseudo-Riemannian submanifolds, each of which correspond to a module over an ideal of 𝔄 \mathfrak{A} . This pair of ideals are orthogonal idempotents. The symmetry is obtained under the Peirce mirror. Mathematics Subject Classification 53B12, 62B11, 60D99

The fourth Frobenius manifolds

For the reader’s convenience, we recall in this section the definition of Frobenius manifolds and related to notions.

2.1
Frobenius manifolds

Let us start with the following data:

(M;\circ:TM\otimes TM\to TM;TM^{f}\subset TM;g:S^{2}(TM)\to O_{M}),

(1)

where

•

M
is a manifold;

•

\circ
is a multiplication operation on the tangent space
TM
to
M
;

•

TM^{f}
is a subspace of
TM

of linear spaces of pairwise commuting vector fields;

•

g
is an even symmetric pairing (i.e. here a non-degenerate Riemannian metric) with
O_{M}
a so-called structure sheaf.

A Frobenius manifold is a manifold equipped with an associativity axiom and potentiality axiom (see
[
10
]
p.19). These properties can be summarised as having a family of local potentials
\Phi
(sections of
O_{M}
) such that for any local flat tangent fields
X,Y,Z
we have :

g(X\circ Y,Z)=g(X,Y\circ Z)=(XYZ)\Phi.

If such a structure exists, then (super)commutativity and associativity of the multiplication
\circ
follows naturally, and we say that the family defines a Frobenius manifold.

Alternative description of the associativity condition

It is possible to encapsulate the associativity relation of the Frobenius’ structure within the Witten–Dijkgraaf–Verlinde–Verlinde (WDVV) highly non-linear PDE system:

\forall a,b,c,d:\underset{ef}{\sum}\varPhi_{abe}g^{ef}\varPhi_{fcd}=(-1)^{a(b+c)}\underset{ef}{\sum}\varPhi_{bce}g^{ef}\varPhi_{fad}.

(2)

Notice that the (WDVV) system expresses
a flatness condition of the manifold
(i.e. vanishing of the curvature),

Frobenius structure and flatness

The structure of Frobenius manifold is deeply related to the notion of flat connections, as we well see.
Let us introduce the Levi–Civita connection
\nabla_{0}:\mathcal{T}_{M}\to\Omega^{1}_{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{M}}\mathcal{T}_{M}
,
uniquely defined by the horizontality of
\mathcal{T}^{f}_{M}
.

The Levi–Civita connection
\nabla_{0}
can be further on deformed into a pencil of connections
\nabla_{\lambda}

depending on an even parameter
\lambda
. The respective covariant derivative is given by the following equation:

\nabla_{\lambda,X}(Y):=\nabla_{0,X}(Y)+\lambda X\circ Y.

This pencil of connections is referred to as the

of our pre–Frobenius manifold.
Now, by
[
10
]
, Theorem 1.4, one can prove that:

Theorem 2.1
(
[
10
]
, Theorem 1.4)

\nabla_{\lambda,X}(Y)
is flat if and only if the manifold is Frobenius.

2.3
Paracomplex structures

In
[
6
]
it turned out that a very important algebra to consider, in the context of statistical manifolds, is the algebra of paracomplex numbers. This real algebra is the key towards understanding many statements concerning the fourth Frobenius manifold, in a concise and more algebraic way.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be the unital and bi-dimensional algebra, generated by
1
and
\varepsilon
where
\varepsilon^{2}=1
, verifying the following relations:

e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=\sum_{k}C^{k}_{ij}e_{k}\quad\text{with}\quad C^{k}_{ij}=C^{k}_{ji}.

The algebra
\mathfrak{A}
is known as the spin factor algebra or the algebra of paracomplex numbers.

The structure constants

are:

C_{11}^{1}=C_{12}^{2}=C_{22}^{1}=\,1,

the other structure constants are null.

Let us change the basis such that the new generators are defined by:

e_{-}=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2},\quad e_{+}=\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}.

These generators have the following relations:

e_{-}\circ e_{-}=e_{-},\ e_{+}\circ e_{+}=e_{+},e_{-}\circ e_{+}=0,

e_{-}+e_{+}=1,\ e_{-}-e_{+}=\varepsilon.

we call this new basis a canonical basis. Notice that this new basis highlights the existence of a pair of idempotents i.e.
e_{-}^{2}=e_{-}
and
e_{+}^{2}=e_{+}
.

Remark
This semi-simple algebra is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}
.
As a set, it can be identified to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
but not as an algebra.

3.1
A brief survey on Peirce’s reflections

Peirce’s decomposition arises in the context of associative rings and also Jordan algebras (see section 6.1 in
[
12
]
). A Peirce decomposition of an associative algebra (or ring) is a decomposition of this algebra (or ring) as a sum of eigenspaces of commuting idempotent elements.

Given an idempotent of an algebra
A
(i.e. an element
a\in A
that verifies
a^{2}=a
), there exist right, left and two-sided Peirce decompositions, which are respectively defined as:

A=Aa+A(1-a),

A=aA+(1-a)A,

A=aAa+aA(1-a)+(1-a)Aa+(1-a)A(1-a).

The latter writes
A
as the direct sum of
aAa,aA(1-a),(1-a)Aa
and
(1-a)A(1-a).

In a more general setting, for associative rings, a decomposition
\sum_{i}a_{i}=1
of the unit into mutually

orthogonal idempotent elements
, (i.e. verifying the condition
a_{i}a_{j}=a_{j}a_{i}=0
) produces a decomposition of the algebra into Peirce spaces:

A=\sum_{i,j}a_{i}Aa_{j}.

Remark 2

We can recover the structure of the entire algebra by analysing the structure of the individual pieces and how they are reassembled to form
A
.

Jordan algebras have similar Peirce decompositions. We call Jordan algebra a commutative algebra
J
where the following Jordan identity holds for all
x,y
in
J
:

x\bullet(x^{2}\bullet y)=x^{2}\bullet(x\bullet y).

Remark 3

Note that any associative algebra
A
over the reals gives rise to a Jordan algebra
J
under
quasi-multiplication: the product
x\bullet y:=\frac{1}{2}(xy+yx)
is clearly commutative, and satisfies the Jordan identity.

The only difference with the case above being that the “off- diagonal” Peirce spaces
J_{ij}
were
(i\neq j)
behave like the sum

A_{ij}+A_{ji}
of two associative Peirce spaces, so that we have symmetry
J_{ij}=J_{ji}
. For
n=2
the decomposition is determined by a single idempotent
a=a_{1}
with its complement
a^{\prime}:=1-a
, and can be described more simply.

In particular, we have te following Peirce Decomposition Theorem.

Theorem 3.1
(Peirce decomposition)

An idempotent
a
in a Jordan algebra
J
determines a Peirce decomposition of the algebra into the direct sum of Peirce subspaces:

J=J_{2}\oplus J_{1}\oplus J_{0}\quad J_{k}:=J_{k}(a):=\{x\,|\,V_{a}x=kx\},\quad\text{($V$ is a linear space)}.

The diagonal Peirce subalgebras
J_{2}=U_{a}J,

J_{0}=U_{1-a}J
are principal inner ideals.

More generally, a decomposition
1=a_{1}+\dots+a_{n}
of the unit of a Jordan algebra
J
into a sum of
n
supplementary orthogonal idempotents leads to the following Peirce decomposition of the algebra into the direct sum of Peirce subspaces:

J=\oplus_{i\leq j}J_{ij}.

Note that the diagonal Peirce subalgebras are the inner ideals

J_{ii}:=U_{a_{i}}(J)=\{x\in J\,|\,a_{i}\bullet x=x\}

and the off-diagonal Peirce spaces are

J_{ij}=J_{ji}:=U_{a_{i},a_{j}}(J)=\{x\in J\,|\,a_{i}\bullet x=a_{j}\bullet x=\frac{1}{2}x\}\quad(i\neq j).

Because of the symmetry
J_{ij}=J_{ji}
the multiplication rules for these subspaces are a bit less uniform to describe, but basically the product of two Peirce spaces vanishes unless two of the indices can be linked.

The notion of Peirce reflection (or Peirce mirror) arises very naturally. Let
J
be a unital Jordan algebra. Consider a pair of orthogonal idempotents
a,a^{\prime}
and put
u=a-a^{\prime}
. Then, it is easy to check that
u
is an involution. Now,
if
u
is an involution in a unital Jordan algebra
J
, then the involution
U_{u}
is an involutionary automorphism of
J
, i.e.
U_{u}^{2}=1_{J}
(see lemma 6.10 in
[
12
]
).
Then the involution defined as
U_{u}=E_{2}-E_{1}+E_{0}
has a description as a Peirce reflection in terms of the Peirce projections
E_{i}(a)
(see p.238
[
12
]
for more details).

We will use this Peirce decomposition construction and reflection on the Frobenius algebra
\mathfrak{A}
of paracomplex numbers and then study the consequences for the module over this algebra.

A
paracomplex manifold
is a real manifold
M
endowed with a paracomplex structure
\mathfrak{K}
that admits an atlas of paraholomorphic coordinates (which are functions with values in the algebra
\mathfrak{A}=\mathbb{R}+\varepsilon\mathbb{R}
defined above), such that the transition functions are paraholomorphic.

Explicitly, this means the existence of local coordinates
(z_{+}^{\alpha},z_{-}^{\alpha}),\,\alpha=1\dots,m
such that
paracomplex decomposition of the local tangent fields is of the form

T^{+}M=span\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{+}^{\alpha}},\,\alpha=1,...,m\right\},

T^{-}M=span\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{-}^{\alpha}}\,,\,\alpha=1,...,m\right\}.

Such coordinates are called
adapted coordinates
for the paracomplex structure
\mathfrak{K}
.

By abuse of notation, we write
\partial_{z}
instead of
\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\alpha}}
.

We associate with any adapted coordinate system
(z_{+}^{\alpha},z_{-}^{\alpha})
a paraholomorphic coordinate system
z^{\alpha}
by

z^{\alpha}\,=\,\frac{z_{+}^{\alpha}+z_{-}^{\alpha}}{2}+\varepsilon\frac{z_{+}^{\alpha}-z_{-}^{\alpha}}{2},\alpha=1,...,m.

We define the paracomplex tangent bundle as the
\mathbb{R}
-tensor product
T^{\mathfrak{A}}M=TM\otimes\mathfrak{A}
and we extend the endomorphism
\mathfrak{K}
to a
\mathfrak{A}
-linear endomorphism of
T^{\mathfrak{A}}M
. For any
p\in M
, we have the following decomposition of
T_{p}^{\mathfrak{A}}M
:

T_{p}^{\mathfrak{A}}M=T_{p}^{1,0}M\oplus T_{p}^{0,1}M\,

where

T_{p}^{1,0}M=\{v\in T_{p}^{\mathfrak{A}}M|\mathfrak{K}v=\varepsilon v\}=\{v+\varepsilon\mathfrak{K}v|v\in E^{2m}\},

T_{p}^{0,1}M=\{v\in T_{p}^{\mathfrak{A}}M|\mathfrak{K}v=-\varepsilon v\}=\{v-\varepsilon\mathfrak{K}v|v\in E^{2m}\}

are the eigenspaces of
\mathfrak{K}
with eigenvalues
\pm\varepsilon
.
The following paracomplex vectors

\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{+}^{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha}}+\varepsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\alpha}}\right),\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial{z}_{-}^{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha}}-\varepsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\alpha}}\right)

form a basis of the spaces
T_{p}^{1,0}M
and
T_{p}^{0,1}M
.

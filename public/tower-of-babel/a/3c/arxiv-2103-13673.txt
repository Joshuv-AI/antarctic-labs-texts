1.
Introduction

The classical heat equation
\partial_{t}u=\Delta u
describes the heat diffusion in homogeneous media. If we change the order of differentiation in time variable, then the equation becomes

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+f(t,x),

and it describes diffusion of particles (
\alpha\in(0,1)
) or propagation of waves (
\alpha\in(1,2)
) in inhomogeneous media (see e.g.
[
23
,
24
,
21
,
22
]
).

In this article, we prove the unique solvability and maximal regularity theory for equation (
0.1
) with zero initial condition in Sobolev spaces with Muckenhoupt weights. Precisely, we prove that for any
p,q\in(1,\infty)
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
w_{1}=w_{1}(x)\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
w_{2}=w_{2}(t)\in A_{q}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists a unique solution satisfying

\||\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u|+|u|+|u_{x}|+|u_{xx}|\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T)}\leq N\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T)},

where the norm in
\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T)
is defined by

\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T)}:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(1-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}f(t,x)|^{p}w_{1}(x)dx\right)^{q/p}w_{2}(t)dt\right)^{1/q},

and
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the class of Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
weights defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The class of Muckenhoupt weights is the optimal class of weights for Hardy-Littlewood maximal functions to have boundedness in
L_{p}
spaces with weight (see
[
27
,
11
]
). Also many types of Fourier multipliers role as a bounded operator in
L_{p}
space with Muckenhoupt weight (see e.g.
[
31
,
18
,
9
]
). These facts make it possible to develop theories for Sobolev spaces with Muckenhoupt weights (see e.g.
[
12
,
26
,
8
]
). Also see e.g.
[
10
]
for embedding theorems in Sobolev space with
A_{p}
weight. In this article, we prove general interpolation inequalities in Sobolev spaces with Muckenhoupt weights (Theorem
3.7
and Remark
3.8
). Precisely, we prove that for
\theta\in(0,1)
,
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
,
p_{0},p_{1}\in(1,\infty)
,
w_{0}\in A_{p_{0}}
,
w_{1}\in A_{p_{1}}
,

\|u\|_{H^{\gamma}_{p}(w)}\leq\|u\|^{1-\theta}_{H^{\gamma_{0}}_{p_{0}}(w_{0})}\|u\|^{\theta}_{H^{\gamma_{1}}_{p_{1}}(w_{1})},

where

\gamma=(1-\theta)\gamma_{0}+\theta\gamma_{1},\quad\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},\quad w^{1/p}=w^{\frac{(1-\theta)}{p_{0}}}_{0}w^{\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}}_{1}.

The above relation is widely studied in the literature. In
[
20
]
, the author characterized complex interpolation spcaces of weighted Sobolev spaces when the given weight satisfies polynomial growth condition, which covers the results for unweighted Sobolev spaces in
[
2
]
. For abstract function spaces with weight, complex interpolation was given in e.g.
[
25
,
19
]
, where the weights are types of power functions. For example, if
X
is a UMD space,
w_{\mu}(t)=t^{(1-\mu)p}
(
\mu\in(1/p,1])
, and
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\geq 0
, then

[H^{\gamma_{0}}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\mu};X),H^{\gamma_{1}}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)]_{[\theta]}=H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\mu};X),

where
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\mu};X)
is
X
-valued weighted Sobolev space defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Here we emphasize that
w_{\mu}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
25
, Lemma 2.8]
). Comparing the above-mentioned ones, our result only requires that the given weights are in
A_{p}
class, not the exact growth or shape of weights. Also, when
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{1}=1
, and
p_{0}=p_{1}
, then the condition for weights
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
was relaxed in
[
4
]
so that they need not be in
A_{p}
.

An
L_{q}(L_{p})
-estimation without weight for abstract parabolic Volterra equations like

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(c_{0}u+\int_{-\infty}^{t}k_{1}(t-s)u(s,x)ds\right)=a^{ij}u_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)+f(t,x)

was introduced in
[
3
,
29
]
for
a^{ij}=\delta^{ij}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and

\frac{2}{\alpha q}+\frac{d}{p}<1,

where
c_{0}\geq 0
and
k_{1}(t)\geq ct^{-\alpha}
. The results of
[
3
,
29
]
are based on semi group theory, and similar approach is used in
[
35
]
for general

under the conditions that
p=q>1
,
a^{ij}
are uniformly continuous in
(t,x)
, and

\alpha\not\in\left\{\frac{2}{2p-1},\frac{2}{p-1}-1,\frac{1}{p},\frac{3}{2p-1}\right\}.

For (
0.1
) the algebraic restriction on
\alpha,d,p
and
q
are dropped in
[
14
]
under the condition that
a^{ij}
are uniformly continuous in
x
and piecewise continuous in
t
. The result in
[
14
]
is obtained by using Calderón-Zygmund theorem, and estimation of fundamental solution. The condition for coefficients are significantly relaxed in
[
6
]
for the case
p=q
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
so that coefficients are measurable in
t
and have small mean oscillation in
x
.

For weighted mixed norm estimate, see
[
13
]
for the case
a^{ij}=\delta^{ij}
. The result of
[
13
]
is based on solution representation by using kernel, and the authors control the mean oscillation of the solution and its derivative to find weighted mixed estimation. Quite recently, weighted mixed norm estimation under the same condition in
[
6
]
is proved in
[
7
]
. However, the results of
[
7
,
13
]
only cover the case
\gamma=0
. Also the result of
[
13
]
only covers the case
a^{ij}=\delta^{ij}
, and the result of
[
7
]
does not cover the range of
\alpha\in(1,2)
.

Our result is an extension of
[
7
,
13
]
in the sense that we give general regularity theory in
L_{q}(L_{p})
spaces with Muckenhoupt weights for equations with variable coefficients, and we cover the full range of
\alpha\in(0,2)
without any restrictions on
\alpha,d,p,q
. Our approach depends on a perturbation argument used in
[
14
,
16
]
which is a standard approach if one starts from the result of equations with constant coefficients. The main difficulty arises here, and we deal with this as follows.

First, since we consider maximal regularity theory for equations with variable coefficients in weighted space, we need to prove pointwise multiplier theorem like

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(1-\Delta)^{k/2}(au)(x)|^{p}w_{1}(x)dx\leq N|a|^{p}_{C^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(1-\Delta)^{k/2}u(x)|^{p}w_{1}(x)dx.

If
k
is not a nonnegative integer, then we cannot obtain this by product rule. To handle this, we construct complex interpolation for Sobolev space with weight by using Fourier multiplier theorems and abstract interpolation theory. Second, the perturbation argument in
[
14
,
16
]
uses uniform localization theorem for Sobolev space in
[
15
]
. Hence, we need to prove the corresponding theorem for Sobolev space with weight.

This article is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce basic definitions and facts related to fractional calculus, and Sobolev spaces with Muckenhoupt weights. Also, we present our main result, Theorem

2.11
. In Section 3, we prove properties of interpolation spaces of Sobolev spaces with weights, and uniform localization theorem for these spaces. In Section 4, we prove the main theorem.

2.
Main result

We first introduce some definitions and facts related to the fractional calculus. For more details, see e.g.
[
5
,
28
,
30
,
32
]
.
For
\alpha>0
and
\varphi\in L_{1}((0,T))
, the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order
\alpha
is defined by

I^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi(t):=(I^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi)(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\varphi(s)ds,\quad t\leq T.

By Jensen’s inequality, for any
p\in[1,\infty]
,

\|I^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi\|_{L_{p}((0,T))}\leq N(\alpha,p,T)\|\varphi\|_{L_{p}((0,T))}.

It is also easy to check that if
\varphi
is bounded then
I^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi(t)
is a continuous function satisfying
I^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi(0)=0
.

Let
n
be the integer such that
n-1\leq\alpha<n
. If
\varphi
is
(n-1)
-times differentiable, and
(\frac{d}{dt})^{n-1}I_{t}^{n-\alpha}\varphi
is absolutely continuous on
[0,T]
, then the Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative
D_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi
and the Caputo fractional derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi
are defined as follows.

D^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi(t):=(D^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi)(t):=(I_{t}^{n-\alpha}\varphi)^{(n)}(t),

(2.1)

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi(t):=D^{\alpha}_{t}\left(\varphi(t)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\varphi^{(k)}(0)\right)(t).

Obviously,
D^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi=\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi
if
\varphi(0)=\varphi^{(1)}(0)=\cdots=\varphi^{(n-1)}(0)=0
.
It is easy to show that, for any
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
,

I^{\alpha+\beta}_{t}\varphi(t)=I^{\alpha}_{t}I^{\beta}_{t}\varphi(t),\quad D^{\alpha}_{t}D^{\beta}_{t}\varphi=D^{\alpha+\beta}\varphi,

and

D^{\alpha}_{t}I^{\beta}_{t}\varphi=\begin{cases}D^{\alpha-\beta}_{t}\varphi&\mbox{ if }\alpha>\beta\\
I^{\beta-\alpha}_{t}\varphi&\mbox{ if }\alpha\leq\beta\end{cases}

Furthermore, if
\varphi
is sufficiently smooth (say,
\varphi\in C^{n}([0,T]))
and
\varphi(0)=\cdots=\varphi^{(n-1)}(0)=0
, then

(2.2)

I^{\alpha}_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi(t):=I^{\alpha}_{t}(\partial^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi)(t)=\varphi(t),\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T].

Consequently, if
\varphi\in C^{2}([0,T])
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
, then
\partial^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi=f
is equivalent to

(2.3)

\varphi(t)-\varphi(0)-1_{\alpha>1}\varphi^{\prime}(0)t=I^{\alpha}_{t}f(t),\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T].

Now we introduce the class of weights used in this article.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. We write
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if
w(x)
is a nonnegative locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

[w]_{p}:=\sup_{B}\left(-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{B}w(x)dx\right)\left(-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{B}w(x)^{-1/(p-1)}dx\right)^{p-1}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{B}w(x)dx=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)dx.

If
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
w
is said to be an
A_{p}
weight.

Remark 2.2
.

The necessary and sufficient condition for Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
to be bounded in
L_{p}(wdx)
is
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see
[
27
]
or
[
11
, Theorem 9.1.9]
). Therefore, if one uses an approach based on sharp and maximal functions, then it is natural to consider
L_{p}
-spaces with
A_{p}
weights for full generality.

Remark 2.3
.

(i) From the definition of
[w]_{p}
, we can easily check that for any
c>0
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the functions
w^{c}(x)=w(cx)
, and
w_{y}(x)=w(x-y)
are in
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
[w^{c}]_{p}=[w_{y}]_{p}=[w]_{p}
.

Also for
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for any
d\times d
orthogonal matrix
Q
, it is easy to see that
w_{Q}(x)=w(Qx)\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
[w_{Q}]_{p}=[w]_{p}
. Therefore, for any symmetric
d\times d
matirx
\Sigma
with positive eigenvalues, one can check that
w_{\Sigma}(x)=w(\Sigma x)\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
[w]_{p}\sim[w_{\Sigma}]_{p}
.

(ii) The class
A_{p}
is increasing as

increases, and it holds that

A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\bigcup_{q\in(1,p)}A_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

More precisely, for any
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, one can find

, which depends on

, and
[w]_{p}
such that

.

Let

,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and

. For

and

and we denote

by the class of tempered distributions satisfying

\|u\|_{H^{\gamma}_{p}(w_{1})}=\|(1-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}u\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}<\infty,

where the operator

is defined by

(1-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((1+|\xi|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\mathcal{F}u\right).

The action of

on

, which is denoted by

(u,\phi):=((1-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}u,(1-\Delta)^{-\gamma/2}\phi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}u(1-\Delta)^{-\gamma/2}\phi dx.

Also we define

\mathbb{L}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T)=L_{q}((0,T),w_{2}dt;L_{p}(w_{1})),

and

\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T)=L_{q}((0,T),w_{2}dt;H^{\gamma}_{p}(w_{1})).

We omit

The norms of these function spaces are defined in a natural way. For example,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T)}

\displaystyle:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}^{q}w_{2}(t)dt\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(t,x)|^{p}w_{1}(x)dx\right)^{q/p}w_{2}(t)dt\right)^{1/q}.

\displaystyle=\|h-h^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|h-\eta\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}+\|\eta-\eta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}+\|\eta^{\varepsilon}-h^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq N\delta+\|\eta-\eta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})},

where

, and

such that

. By letting

, since

is arbitrary we have the desired result.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
1<q<\infty
,
0<T<\infty
, and let

. Then for any

depends only on

Proof.

For the case

\displaystyle|I^{\alpha}_{t}f(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}|t-s|^{\alpha-1}|f(s)|ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}|t|^{\alpha-1}|f(s)|ds\leq\frac{T^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\frac{2}{2t}\int_{0}^{2t}|f(s)|ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2T^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathcal{M}f(t),

where

is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function defined as

\mathcal{M}f(t)=\sup_{t\in(a,b)}-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{a}^{b}|f(s)|ds.

Extend
f=0
ouside of

such that

,
\phi(t)=1
for

\phi_{n}(t)=0
for

or

\displaystyle\|I^{\alpha}_{t}f\|_{L_{q}((0,T),w_{2}dt)}

\displaystyle=\|I^{\alpha}_{t}(f\phi_{n})\|_{L_{q}((0,T),w_{2}dt)}

\displaystyle\leq T^{\alpha}N\|\mathcal{M}(f\phi_{n})\|_{L_{q}((0,T),w_{2}dt)}\leq T^{\alpha}N\|\mathcal{M}(f\phi_{n})\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R},w_{2}dt)}

\displaystyle\leq T^{\alpha}N\|f\phi_{n}\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R},w_{2}dt)}

\displaystyle\leq T^{\alpha}N\|f\phi_{n}\|_{L_{q}((-1/n,T+1/n),w_{2}dt)}.

Letting

Lemma 2.7

(iii) If

3.
Interpolation and Uniform localization theorem of weighted Sobolev spaces

First of all, we introduce Littlewood-Paley operator. Let
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a space of Schwartz functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathcal{D}
be a space of tempered distribution, and let
\Psi
be a radial function in
\mathcal{S}
whose Fourier transform

has support in the strip

\hat{\Psi}(\xi)>0
for

(3.1)

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\Psi}(2^{-j}\xi)=1\quad\textrm{for}\quad\xi\neq 0.

Define
\Psi_{j}
and
\Psi_{0}
by

(3.2)

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\hat{\Psi}(2^{-j}\xi)\quad j=\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots,

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}_{0}(\xi)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\hat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi).

The following lemma is a weighted version of the Littlewood-Paley inequality. For the proof, see e.g.
[
18
, Theorem 1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
w_{1}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For any

, it holds that

(3.3)

\begin{gathered}\left\|\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\Psi_{j}\ast f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}\leq N_{1}\|f\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}\leq N_{2}\left\|\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\Psi_{j}\ast f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})},\end{gathered}

where the constant

and

depend only on

, and
\Psi
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}
, and let
\Phi_{j}(x)=2^{jd}\Phi(2^{j}x)
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Define
K_{j}(x,y)=\Phi_{j}(x-y)
. Then it holds that

\begin{gathered}\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|K_{j}(x,y)|^{2}\right|^{1/2}\leq\frac{N}{|x-y|^{d}},\\
\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|K_{j}(x,y)-K(x^{\prime},y)|^{2}\right|^{1/2}\leq\frac{N|x-x^{\prime}|}{(|x-y|+|x^{\prime}-y|)^{d+1}},\end{gathered}

provided that

, where the constant
N
depends only on
d
, and
\Phi
.

Proof.

Take
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
j_{0}=j_{0}(x,y)
, the smallest integer so that

. This implies that

(3.4)

1<2^{j_{0}}|x-y|\leq 2.

Note that due to the choice of
j_{0}
, we have (
3.4
). From this, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|K_{j}(x,y)|^{2}\right|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{j<j_{0}}|K_{j}(x,y)|^{2}\right|^{1/2}+\left|\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}|K_{j}(x,y)|^{2}\right|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq N(d,\Phi)\left|\sum_{j<j_{0}}2^{2jd}\right|^{1/2}+N(d,\Phi)\left|\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}\left|2^{jd}\frac{1}{|2^{j}(x-y)|^{a}}\right|^{2}\right|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq N(d,\Phi)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}}+N(d,\Phi)2^{j_{0}(d-a)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{a}}

\displaystyle\leq N(d,\Phi)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d}},

where

, and the last inequality holds due to (

). Note that since
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}
, one can choose such

.

Now take

satisfying
|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max{\{|x-y|,|x^{\prime}-y|\}}
and let
j_{1}
be the smallest integer so that

. Like (
3.4
), we get

(3.5)

1<2^{j_{1}}(|x-y|+|x^{\prime}-y|)\leq 2.

Observe that by the mean value theorem,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|K_{j}(x,y)-K_{j}(x^{\prime},y)|^{2}\right|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg||x-x^{\prime}||\nabla\Phi_{j}(\theta(x,x^{\prime},y))|\bigg|^{2}\right|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{j<j_{1}}\bigg|2^{j(d+1)}|x-x^{\prime}||\nabla\Phi(2^{j}\theta(x,x^{\prime},y))|\bigg|^{2}\right|^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{j\geq j_{1}}\bigg|2^{j(d+1)}|x-x^{\prime}||\nabla\Phi(2^{j}\theta(x,x^{\prime},y))|\bigg|^{2}\right|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq N(d,\Phi)|x-x^{\prime}|2^{(d+1)j_{1}}+N(d,\Phi)\left|\sum_{j\geq j_{1}}\bigg|\frac{2^{j(d+1)}|x-x^{\prime}|}{|2^{j}\theta(x,x^{\prime},y)|^{d+b}}\bigg|^{2}\right|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq N(d,\Phi)|x-x^{\prime}|2^{(d+1)j_{1}}+N(d,\Phi)\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|2^{j_{1}(1-b)}}{|\theta(x,x^{\prime},y)|^{d+b}},

where
b>1
(recall that since
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}
we can choose such
b
), and
\theta(x,x^{\prime},y)
is a point in the line segment connecting
x-y
, and
x^{\prime}-y
. Note that there exist
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in(0,1)
so that

\begin{gathered}\theta(x,x^{\prime},y)=(x-y)+\lambda(x-x^{\prime}),\\
\theta(x,x^{\prime},y)=(x^{\prime}-y)+\lambda^{\prime}(x-x^{\prime}).\end{gathered}

Since
|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max{\{|x-y|,|x^{\prime}-y|\}}
, one can check that

(3.6)

\frac{1}{4}(|x-y|+|x^{\prime}-y|)\leq|\theta(x,x^{\prime},y)|\leq(|x-y|+|x^{\prime}-y|).

Therefore, by (
3.5
) we get

\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|K_{j}(x,y)-K_{j}(x^{\prime},y)|^{2}\right|^{1/2}\leq N(d,\Phi)\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{(|x-y|+|x^{\prime}-y|)^{d+1}}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

By applying the above lemmas, we get the following Littlewood-Paley characterization for

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
w_{1}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the following hold;

(i) If
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p}(w_{1})
, then there is a constant

(3.10)

\begin{gathered}\|\Psi_{0}\ast u\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}+\|\big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|2^{\gamma j}\Psi_{j}\ast u|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}\leq N\|u\|_{H^{\gamma}_{p}(w_{1})}.\end{gathered}

(ii) Also if
u
is a tempered distribution so that the left hand side of (
3.10

u\in H^{\gamma}_{p}(w_{1})
, and there is a constant
N=N(d,p,\gamma,w_{1},\Psi)
so that

(3.11)

\begin{gathered}\|u\|_{H^{\gamma}_{p}(w_{1})}\leq N\left(\|\Psi_{0}\ast u\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}+\|\big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|2^{\gamma j}\Psi_{j}\ast u|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}\right)\end{gathered}

Proof.

(i) Suppose that
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p}(w_{1})
. Note that for
j\geq 2

2^{\gamma j}\hat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)\hat{u}(\xi)=|2^{-j}\xi|^{-\gamma}\hat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)|\xi|^{\gamma}\hat{u}(\xi)=|2^{-j}\xi|^{-\gamma}\hat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)|\xi|^{\gamma}(1-\hat{\Psi}_{0}(\xi))\hat{u}(\xi)

since the support of
\hat{\Psi}_{0}
does not intersect the support of
\hat{\Psi}_{j}
.
Therefore, by defining

\tilde{u}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left\{|\cdot|^{\gamma}(1-\hat{\Psi}_{0})\hat{u}\right\},

we get

\|\big(\sum_{j\geq 2}|2^{\gamma j}\Psi_{j}\ast u|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}=\|\big(\sum_{j\geq 2}|\Psi_{\gamma,j}\ast\tilde{u}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})},

where
\hat{\Psi}_{\gamma,j}(\xi)=|2^{-j}\xi|^{-\gamma}\hat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)
. By Remark
3.4
(i), we have

\|\big(\sum_{j\geq 2}|2^{\gamma j}\Psi_{j}\ast u|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}\leq N\|\tilde{u}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}.

Note that

\displaystyle\tilde{u}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\cdot|^{\gamma}(1-\hat{\Psi}_{0})\hat{u})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\frac{|\cdot|^{\gamma}(1-\hat{\Psi}_{0})}{(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\gamma/2}}(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\hat{u}\right).

Applying Theorem
3.5
to the multiplier

we have

\|\big(\sum_{j\geq 2}|2^{\gamma j}\Psi_{j}\ast u|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}\leq N\|\tilde{u}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}\leq N\|u\|_{H^{\gamma}_{p}(w_{1})}.

Similarly for

\begin{gathered}2^{\gamma}\hat{\Psi}_{1}(\xi)\hat{u}(\xi)=2^{\gamma}\frac{\hat{\Psi}(\frac{1}{2}\xi)}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\gamma/2}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\hat{u}(\xi),\\
\hat{\Psi}_{0}(\xi)\hat{u}(\xi)=\frac{\hat{\Psi}_{0}(\xi)}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\gamma/2}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\hat{u}(\xi),\end{gathered}

and it holds that

\begin{gathered}\|2^{\gamma}\Psi_{1}\ast u\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}\leq N\|u\|_{H^{\gamma}_{p}(w_{1})},\\
\|\Psi_{0}\ast u\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}\leq N\|u\|_{H^{\gamma}_{p}(w_{1})}\end{gathered}

by Theorem
3.5
. Hence, we have (
3.10
).

(ii) Now suppose that
u\in\mathcal{D}
satisfies

\|\Psi_{0}\ast u\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}+\|\big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|2^{\gamma j}\Psi_{j}\ast u|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}<\infty.

Note that

(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\hat{u}=\hat{\Psi}_{0}(\xi)(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\hat{u}+(1-\hat{\Psi}_{0}(\xi))(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\hat{u}=:\hat{u}_{0}+\hat{u}_{1}.

\Pi_{0}\in\mathcal{S}

Since

satisfies the condition in Theorem
3.5

\|u_{0}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}\leq N\|\Psi_{0}\ast u\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}.

Let
\Pi_{1}
be a smooth function so that
\hat{\Pi}_{1}=0
in the neighborhood of the origin and equals 1 on the support of

\hat{u}_{1}(\xi)=|\xi|^{-\gamma}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\hat{\Pi}_{1}(\xi)(1-\hat{\Psi}_{0}(\xi))|\xi|^{\gamma}\hat{u}(\xi).

Since

\|u_{1}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}\leq N\|\tilde{u}_{1}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\cdot|^{\gamma}(1-\hat{\Psi}_{0})\hat{u}).

Let
\Pi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
such that
\hat{\Pi}_{2}=1
on the support of
\hat{\Psi}
and
\hat{\Pi}_{2}=0

\mathcal{F}\tilde{u}_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\xi|^{\gamma}\hat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)\hat{\Pi}_{2}(2^{-j}\xi)\hat{u}(\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\gamma j}\hat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)\hat{\Theta}_{j}(\xi)\hat{u}(\xi),

\hat{\Theta}_{j}(\xi)=\hat{\Theta}(2^{-j}\xi)

\tilde{u}_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Theta_{j}\ast(2^{\gamma j}\Psi_{j}\ast u)

\|\tilde{u}_{1}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}\leq N\|\big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|2^{\gamma j}\Psi_{j}\ast u|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}(w_{1})}.

3.11

Now we give an interpolation theorem of weighted Sobolev spaces.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
1<p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
,
w^{0}_{1}\in A_{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

w^{1}_{1}\in A_{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\gamma=(1-\theta)\gamma_{0}+\theta\gamma_{1},\quad w_{1}=(w^{0}_{1})^{p(1-\theta)/p_{0}}(w^{1}_{1})^{p\theta/p_{1}},

(3.12)

[H^{\gamma_{0}}_{p_{0}}(w^{0}_{1}),H^{\gamma_{1}}_{p_{1}}(w^{1}_{1})]_{[\theta]}=H^{\gamma}_{p}(w_{1}),

where the space
[H^{\gamma_{0}}_{p}(w^{0}_{1}),H^{\gamma_{1}}_{p}(w^{1}_{1})]_{[\theta]}

Proof.

For
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, define
l^{\mu}_{2}
as follows;

l^{\mu}_{2}:=\{a=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots):\|a\|_{l^{\mu}_{2}}:=(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{\mu j}|a_{j}|^{2})^{1/2}<\infty\}.

By Theorem
3.6
(i) if we define
(\mathcal{I}u)_{j}=\Psi_{j}\ast u
(
j=0,1,2,\dots

1<q<\infty

w\in A_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\mathcal{I}u\in L_{q}(l^{\mu}_{2};wdx)
. This implies that

\mathcal{I}[H^{\gamma_{0}}_{p_{0}}(w^{0}_{1})+H^{\gamma_{1}}_{p_{1}}(w^{1}_{1})]\subset[L_{p_{0}}(l^{\gamma_{0}}_{2};w^{0}_{1}dx)+L_{p_{1}}(l^{\gamma_{1}}_{2};w^{1}_{1}dx)].

Now define a map
\mathcal{P}
on
L_{p_{0}}(l_{2}^{\gamma_{0}};w^{0}_{1}dx)+L_{p_{1}}(l_{2}^{\gamma_{1}};w^{1}_{1}dx)
by

\mathcal{P}u=(\Psi_{0}+\Psi_{1})\ast u_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(\Psi_{j-1}+\Psi_{j}+\Psi_{j+1})\ast u_{j}.

Since

(3.13)

\begin{gathered}\Psi_{0}=\Psi_{0}\ast(\Psi_{0}+\Psi_{1}),\\
\Psi_{j}=\Psi_{j}\ast(\Psi_{j-1}+\Psi_{j}+\Psi_{j+1}),\quad j=1,2,\dots,\\
\Psi_{i}\ast\Psi_{j}=0\quad\forall\,|i-j|\geq 2,\end{gathered}

it follows that

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}\ast\mathcal{P}u

\displaystyle=\Psi_{0}\ast u_{0}+(\Psi_{0}+\Psi_{1})\ast\Psi_{0}\ast u_{1},

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}\ast\mathcal{P}u

\displaystyle=(\Psi_{j-1}+\Psi_{j})\ast\Psi_{j}\ast u_{j-1}

\displaystyle+\Psi_{j}\ast u_{j}+(\Psi_{j}+\Psi_{j+1})\ast\Psi_{j}\ast u_{j+1}\quad j=1,2,\dots.

Therefore, we have for
i=0,1

\displaystyle\|(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{\gamma_{i}j}\Psi_{j}\ast\mathcal{P}u|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{L_{p_{i}}(w^{i}_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\||\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\Psi_{j}\ast 2^{\gamma_{i}j}u_{j}|^{2}|^{1/2}\|_{L_{p_{i}}(w^{i}_{1})}

\displaystyle+2^{-\gamma_{i}}\|(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|(\Psi_{j+1}+\Psi_{j})\ast\Psi_{j}\ast 2^{\gamma_{i}(j+1)}u_{j+1}|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{L_{p_{i}}(w^{i}_{1})}

\displaystyle+2^{\gamma_{i}}\|(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|(\Psi_{j-1}+\Psi_{j})\ast\Psi_{j}\ast 2^{\gamma_{i}(j-1)}u_{j-1})^{2}|^{1/2}\|_{L_{p_{i}}(w^{i}_{1})}.

3.9
), then it follows that

\|(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{\gamma_{i}j}\Psi_{j}\ast\mathcal{P}u|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{L_{p_{i}}(w^{i}_{1})}\leq N\|u\|_{L_{p_{i}}(l^{\gamma_{i}}_{2},w^{i}_{1})}\quad i=0,1,

\mathcal{P}u\in[H^{\gamma_{0}}_{p_{0}}(w^{0}_{1})+H^{\gamma_{1}}_{p_{1}}(w^{1}_{1})]
by Theorem
3.6
(ii). Also, one can check that
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{I}
is the indentity map defined on
H^{\mu}_{q}(w)
, and on
[H^{\gamma_{0}}_{p_{0}}(w^{0}_{1})+H^{\gamma_{1}}_{p_{1}}(w^{1}_{1})]
by using (
3.13
).

On the other hand, by
[
33
, Theorem 1.18.5]
, and
[
2
, Theorem 5.6.3]
we have

\begin{gathered}\quad[L_{p_{0}}(A_{0};w^{0}_{1}dx),L_{p_{1}}(A_{1};w^{1}_{1}dx)]_{[\theta]}=L_{p}([A_{0},A_{1}]_{[\theta]};w_{1}dx),\\
[l^{\gamma_{0}}_{2},l^{\gamma_{1}}_{2}]_{[\theta]}=l^{\gamma}_{2},\end{gathered}

where
A_{0}
, and
A_{1}

[L_{p_{0}}(l^{\gamma_{0}}_{2};w^{0}_{1}dx),L_{p_{1}}(l^{\gamma_{1}}_{2};w^{1}_{1}dx)]_{[\theta]}=L_{p}(l^{\gamma}_{2},w_{1}).

[
2

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{I}:[H^{\gamma_{0}}_{p_{0}}(w^{0}_{1}),H^{\gamma_{1}}_{p_{1}}(w^{1}_{1})]_{[\theta]}\to[L_{p_{0}}(l^{\gamma_{0}}_{2};w^{0}_{1}dx),L_{p_{1}}(l^{\gamma_{1}}_{2};w^{1}_{1}dx)]_{[\theta]},\\

\displaystyle H^{\gamma}_{p}(w_{1})

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
w_{1}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
w_{2}\in A_{q}(\mathbb{R})

.

(i) For any

\tilde{u}\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,\gamma+2}_{q,p,0}(w_{2},w_{1},\tilde{T})
such that
u(t)=\tilde{u}(t)
for all

, and

(4.3)

\|\tilde{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,\gamma+2}_{q,p,0}(w_{2},w_{1},\tilde{T})}\leq N\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,\gamma+2}_{q,p,0}(w_{2},w_{1},T)},

(ii) Let

\bar{u}(t):=u(T+t)-v(T+t)\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,\gamma+2}_{q,p,0}(w_{2,T},w_{1},\tilde{T}-T)

By Lemma
2.7

\gamma=0

(i) By Theorem
4.1
, the equation

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}\tilde{u}=\Delta\tilde{u}+(\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u-\Delta u)1_{t\leq T},\quad t\leq\tilde{T}

has a unique solution
\tilde{u}\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}_{q,p,0}(w_{2},w_{1},\tilde{T})

4.3

\bar{u}=u-\tilde{u}

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}\bar{u}=\Delta\bar{u}\quad t\leq T,

for

(ii) Take sequences
u_{n},v_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

u_{n}

and

2.8

\bar{u}_{n}=u_{n}(T+t,x)-v_{n}(T+t,x),

we have
\bar{u}_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

\bar{u}_{n}(0,x)=0,\quad 1_{\alpha>1}\partial_{t}\bar{u}_{n}(0,x)=0\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Moreover, since

converges to
\bar{u}
in
\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,\gamma+2}_{q,p}(w_{2,T},w_{1},\tilde{T}-T)
, we have the desired result. The lemma is proved.

Lemma 4.3
.

,

,

2.11

\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}\leq N_{0}\left(\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}+\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+1}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}\right)\quad\forall\,t\leq T_{0},

, and the constant

From (
4.4
) and (
3.14
) for any

and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}

\displaystyle\leq N_{0}\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}+\varepsilon N_{0}\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}

\displaystyle+N(N_{0},\varepsilon)\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}.

for

satisfies (
2.11

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}

\displaystyle\leq 2N_{0}\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}+N(N_{0})\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}.

\displaystyle\leq N_{1}t^{\alpha}(\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}+\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)})

Proof of Theorem
2.11

Due to Theorem
4.1
we only prove the a priori estimate. Moreover, by the definition of the norm
\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,\gamma+2}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T)}
we only prove the a priori estimate with
\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T)}
in place of
\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,\gamma+2}_{q,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T)}
.

Step 1.

p=q
,
a^{ij}=a^{ij}(t)
are independent of
x
and
b^{i}=c=0
.

Suppose that
u\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,\gamma+2}_{p,p,0}(w_{2},w_{1},T)
satisfies (
2.11
). If we let
a^{ij}_{0}=a^{ij}(0)
, then
u
satisfies

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u=a^{ij}_{0}u_{x^{i}x^{j}}+f+(a^{ij}-a^{ij}_{0})u_{x^{i}x^{j}}.

Hence, for each
t\leq T
, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}+\|(a^{ij}-a^{ij}_{0})D^{2}u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}\right)

\displaystyle=N\left(\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}+\|(a^{ij}-a^{ij}_{0})(1-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}D^{2}u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}+\sup_{0<s<t}|a^{ij}(s)-a^{ij}_{0}|\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}\right),

where the constant
N
is independent of
t
. Due to the uniform continuity of
a^{ij}
there exists a
T_{0}
such that if
t<T_{0}
, then the
N\sup_{0<s<t}|a^{ij}(s)-a^{ij}_{0}|<1/2
. Therefore, by Lemma
4.3
there is
t_{0}\leq T_{0}
such that

(4.6)

\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}\leq N\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}

for all
t<t_{0}
. Take an integer

such that
T/l<\frac{1}{2}t_{0}
and let
T_{k}=kT/l
. Suppose that for
k\leq l
we have (
4.6
) with
T_{k}
in place of

. Take
\tilde{u}
from Lemma
4.2
(i) corresponding to

and
\tilde{T}=T_{k+1}
. Set

\bar{u}
satisfies

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}\bar{u}=a^{ij}\bar{u}_{x^{i}x^{j}}+\bar{f}\quad t\leq T_{k+1}-T_{k}

\bar{f}(t,x)=f(T_{k}+t,x)-\partial^{\alpha}_{t}\tilde{u}(T_{k}+t,x)+a^{ij}\tilde{u}(T_{k}+t,x).

Therefore, by the above result and Lemma
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{p,p}(w_{2,T_{k}},w_{1},T_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq N\|\bar{f}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w_{2,T_{k}},w_{1},T_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T_{k+1})}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T_{k+1})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T_{k+1})}+\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T_{k})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T_{k+1})},

where
w_{2,T_{k}}(t)=w_{2}(T_{k}+t)
and the last inequality holds due to the assumption that the a priori estimate holds for

. Hence, by (
4.3
) we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T_{k+1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T_{k})}+\|u(T_{k}+\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma+2}_{p,p}(w_{2,T_{k}},w_{1},T_{k+1}-T_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq N\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},T_{k+1})}.

Step 2.

p=q
,
a^{ij}=a^{ij}(t,x)

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u_{r}=a^{ij}_{r0}(u_{r})_{x^{i}x^{j}}+f_{r}+(a^{ij}_{r}-a^{ij}_{0})(u_{r})_{x^{i}x^{j}}\quad t\leq r^{-2/\alpha}T,

3.9
, for any

\displaystyle\|(u_{r})_{xx}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w^{r}_{2},w^{r}_{1},t)}

\displaystyle\leq N_{0}\|f_{r}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w^{r}_{2},w^{r}_{1},t)}

\displaystyle+N_{0}\sup_{t}|a^{ij}_{r0}-a^{ij}_{r}|_{B^{|\gamma|}}\|(u_{r})_{xx}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w^{r}_{2},w^{r}_{1},t)},

Therefore, if there is a

and

\sup_{(t,x)}|a^{ij}(t,x)-a^{ij}(t,0)|+1_{\gamma\neq 0}r^{|\gamma|\wedge 1}\delta^{-1}\leq\varepsilon_{1}+r^{|\gamma|\wedge 1}\delta^{-1}<\frac{1}{2}N_{0},

for each

, we have

\|(u_{r})_{xx}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w^{r}_{2},w^{r}_{1},t)}\leq 2N_{0}\|f_{r}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w^{r}_{2},w^{r}_{1},t)},

and by change of variables we have

\|u_{xx}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}\leq 2N_{0}\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}_{p,p}(w_{2},w_{1},t)}

for any

In the current article we classify locally all homogeneous
real

(2,3,5)

(2,3,5)

Our method is standard: Any homogenous
(2,3,5)
distribution
(M,\mathbf{D})
can be encoded in an
algebraic model

(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k};\mathfrak{d})

\mathbf{D}

\mathfrak{k}
is the isotropy subalgebra of a point

\dim\mathfrak{k}=\dim\geq 1

is the subspace corresponding to
\mathbf{D}_{u}

(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{k}\otimes\mathbb{C};\mathfrak{d}\otimes\mathbb{C})
is a complex algebraic model, and conversely the given real algebraic model can be recovered from an appropriate anti-involution
\phi\colon\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathbb{C}\to\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathbb{C}
admissible in the sense that it preserves the filtration
\mathfrak{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}\subset\mathfrak{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}\subset\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathbb{C}

2
The geometry of
\boldsymbol{(2,3,5)}
distributions

A
(2,3,5)
distribution
is a tangent
2
-plane distribution
\mathbf{D}
on a
5
-manifold
M
satisfying the genericity condition

\displaystyle[\mathbf{D},[\mathbf{D},\mathbf{D}]]=TM.

Here, for distributions
\mathbf{E},\mathbf{E}^{\prime}
on
M
,
[\mathbf{E},\mathbf{E}^{\prime}]:=\coprod_{u\in M}[\mathbf{E},\mathbf{E}^{\prime}]_{u}
, where
[\mathbf{E},\mathbf{E}^{\prime}]_{u}\subset T_{u}M
is the vector subspace
\{[X,Y]_{u}\colon X\in\Gamma(\mathbf{E}),Y\in\Gamma(\mathbf{E}^{\prime})\}
. Implicit in the notation
[\mathbf{D},[\mathbf{D},\mathbf{D}]]
is the condition that
[\mathbf{D},\mathbf{D}]
has constant rank; for a
(2,3,5)
distribution,
\rank{}[\mathbf{D},\mathbf{D}]=3
. We will work in both the smooth and complex categories. In both cases we will always assume that
M
is connected.

2.1
Monge (quasi-)normal form

Any ordinary differential equations of the form

\displaystyle z^{\prime}(x)=F(x,y,y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime},z)

(2.1)

defines a
2
-plane distribution on the (partial) jet space
J^{2,0}\big(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F}^{2}\big)\cong\mathbb{F}^{5}
(
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
). We can prolong any solution
(x,y(x),z(x))
to a curve
(x,y(x),y^{\prime}(x),y^{\prime\prime}(x),z^{\prime}(x))
in
\mathbb{F}^{5}
, and by construction any such curve is tangent to the
2
-plane distribution
\mathbf{D}_{F}\subset T\mathbb{F}^{5}
defined in the respective jet coordinates
(x,y,p,q,z)
as the common kernel of the canonical jet
1
-forms
{\rm d}y-p\,{\rm d}x
and
{\rm d}p-q\,{\rm d}x
and the
1
-form
{\rm d}z-F(x,y,p,q,z)\,{\rm d}x
defined by (
2.1
). Conversely, the projection to
xyz
-space of any integral curve of this distribution to which the pullback of
dx
vanishes nowhere defines a solution of this o.d.e. The distribution
\mathbf{D}_{F}
is spanned by

\displaystyle\partial_{q}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad D_{x}:=\partial_{x}+p\partial_{y}+q\partial_{p}+F(x,y,p,q,z)\partial_{z}

– the latter is the total derivative – and computing directly shows that
\mathbf{D}_{F}
is a
(2,3,5)
distribution iff
\partial_{q}^{2}F
vanishes nowhere.

Goursat showed that, in fact, every
(2,3,5)
distribution arises locally this way and hence can be specified locally by some function
F
of five variables. Such an o.d.e. (or, by slight abuse of terminology, the function
F
itself) is called a
Monge normal form
of the distribution.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
18
, Section 76]
)
.

Let
(M,\mathbf{D})
be a real (complex)
(2,3,5)
distribution and fix a point
u\in M
. There is a neighborhood
U\subseteq M
of
u
, a diffeomorphism
(
biholomorphism
)

\Psi\colon U\to V\subset J^{2,0}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F}^{2})
, and a smooth (complex-analytic) function
F\colon V\to\mathbb{F}
for which
T\Psi\cdot\mathbf{D}|_{U}=\mathbf{D}_{F}
.

3
Homogeneous distributions

3.1
Infinitesimal symmetries

An
infinitesimal symmetry
of a
(2,3,5)
distribution
(M,\mathbf{D})
is a vector field
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathbf{D})
whose (local) flow preserves
\mathbf{D}
, or equivalently for which
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\eta\in\Gamma(\mathbf{D})
for all
\eta\in\Gamma(\mathbf{D})
. We denote the Lie algebra of infinitesimal symmetries, called the
(
infinitesimal
)
symmetry algebra
, by
\mathfrak{aut}(\mathbf{D})
, and we say that
(M,\mathbf{D})
is
infinitesimally homogeneous
if
\mathfrak{aut}(\mathbf{D})
acts infinitesimally transitively, that is, if
\{\xi_{u}\colon\xi\in\mathfrak{aut}(\mathbf{D})\}=T_{u}M
for all
u\in M
. This article concerns (infinitesimally)
multiply transitive
homogeneous distributions, that is, infinitesimally homogeneous distributions for which the isotropy subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}_{u}<\mathfrak{aut}(\mathbf{D})
of infinitesimal symmetries vanishing at any
u\in M
is nontrivial, or equivalently, for which
\dim\mathfrak{aut}(\mathbf{D})\geq 6
.

3.2
Algebraic models for homogeneous distributions

Fix a homogeneous
(2,3,5)
(real or complex) distribution
(M,\mathbf{D})
with transitive symmetry algebra
\mathfrak{h}:=\mathfrak{aut}(\mathbf{D})
, fix a point
u\in M
, and denote by
\mathfrak{k}<\mathfrak{h}
the subalgebra of vector fields in
\mathfrak{h}
vanishing at
u
and by
\mathfrak{d}\subset\mathfrak{h}
the subspace
\mathfrak{d}:=\{\xi\in\mathfrak{h}\colon\xi_{u}\in\mathbf{D}_{u}\}
. Then,
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
,
[\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{d}]\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
(we call this property
\mathfrak{k}
-invariance
), and
\dim(\mathfrak{d}/\mathfrak{k})=2
. The fact that
\mathbf{D}
is a
(2,3,5)
distribution implies the genericity condition
\mathfrak{d}+[\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d}]+[\mathfrak{d},[\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d}]]=\mathfrak{h}
. We call the triple
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k};\mathfrak{d})
a (real or complex)
algebraic model
(of
(M,\mathbf{D})
).

Given an algebraic model, we can reconstruct
\mathbf{D}
up to local equivalence: For any groups
H
,
K
with
K<H
and respectively realizing
\mathfrak{h}
,
\mathfrak{k}
(for the groups that occur in the classification, we can choose
K
to be a closed subgroup of
H
), invoke the canonical identification
T_{\id\cdot K}(H/K)\cong\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{k}
to take
\mathbf{D}\subset T(H/K)
to be the distribution with fibers
\mathbf{D}_{h\cdot K}=T_{\id\cdot K}L_{h}\cdot(\mathfrak{d}/\mathfrak{k})
, where
L_{h}\colon H/K\to H/K
is the map
L_{h}\colon g\cdot K\mapsto(hg)\cdot K
; by
\mathfrak{k}
-invariance this definition is independent of the coset representative
h
, and by genericity
\mathbf{D}
is an
H
-invariant
(2,3,5)
distribution. Via the above identification,
[\mathbf{D},\mathbf{D}]_{\id\cdot K}=(\mathfrak{d}+[\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d}])/\mathfrak{k}
.
1
1

1

Conversely, a triple
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k};\mathfrak{d})
, where
\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{k}<\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie subalgebra, and
\mathfrak{d}\subset\mathfrak{h}
is a subspace for which
\mathfrak{h}\supset\mathfrak{k}
and
\dim(\mathfrak{d}/\mathfrak{k})=2
, together satisfying the
\mathfrak{k}
-invariance and genericity conditions, determines up to local equivalence a homogeneous distribution via this construction. The symmetry algebra of this distribution may be strictly larger that
\mathfrak{h}
, however; for example, for
\mathbf{D.6}_{\lambda}
we have
\dim\mathfrak{h}=6
, but for the excluded value
\lambda=9
, the resulting distribution
\Delta
is flat, so
\dim\mathfrak{aut}(\Delta)=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}=14
.

We declare two algebraic models
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k};\mathfrak{d}),(\mathfrak{h}^{\prime},\mathfrak{k}^{\prime};\mathfrak{d}^{\prime})
to be
equivalent
iff there is a Lie algebra isomorphism
\alpha\colon\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
satisfying
\alpha(\mathfrak{k})=\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}
and
\alpha(\mathfrak{d})=\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
. Unwinding definitions shows that equivalent algebraic models determine locally equivalent distributions.

5.1
Real forms of complex algebraic models

Given a real algebraic model
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k};\mathfrak{d})
, the triple
(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{k}\otimes\mathbb{C};\mathfrak{d}\otimes\mathbb{C})
is a complex algebraic model; we call the latter the
complexification
of the former.

Conversely, suppose that we have a complex algebraic model
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k};\mathfrak{d})
. Recall that a
real form
of
\mathfrak{h}
is the fixed point Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{\phi}
of an anti-involution
\phi\colon\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
, that is, a complex-antilinear map satisfying
\phi^{2}=\id_{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\phi([x,y])=[\phi(x),\phi(y)]
for all
x,y,\in\mathfrak{h}
. In analogy to
[
16
, Section 3]
we call
\phi

admissible
iff it preserves
\mathfrak{k}
and
\mathfrak{d}
, in which case
(\mathfrak{h}^{\phi},\mathfrak{k}^{\phi};\mathfrak{d}^{\phi})
is a real algebraic model, where
\mathfrak{k}^{\phi}:=\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{h}^{\phi}
and
\mathfrak{d}^{\phi}:=\mathfrak{d}\cap\mathfrak{h}^{\phi}
. By construction its complexification is
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k};\mathfrak{d})
, so we call
(\mathfrak{h}^{\phi},\mathfrak{k}^{\phi};\mathfrak{d}^{\phi})
a
real form
of
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k};\mathfrak{d})
.

We say that two admissible anti-involutions
\phi
,
\psi
are
equivalent
if
\psi=\alpha\circ\phi\circ\alpha^{-1}
for some
admissible
automorphism
\alpha
of
\mathfrak{h}
, that is, one preserving
\mathfrak{k}
and
\mathfrak{d}
. Two admissible anti-involutions are equivalent iff they determine equivalent real algebraic models (and hence locally equivalent real homogeneous distributions), so to classify the latter one can classify the former. Not all
\mathfrak{h}
admit admissible anti-involutions, and hence not all complex algebraic models admit real forms.

We thus classify the multiply transitive homogeneous real distributions as follows: Up to local equivalence there is only a single real flat distribution and hence only a single real form of
\mathbf{O}
. For the submaximal case we appeal to the existing classification
[
17
, Theorem 2]
of real submaximal distributions.

This leaves the classification of real forms of the complex algebraic models
\dim\mathfrak{h}=6
. For each such model
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k};\mathfrak{d})
(see Table

\displaystyle\mathfrak{k}=\spanOperator\{{e_{6}}\},\qquad\mathfrak{d}=\spanOperator\{{e_{4},e_{5}}\}\oplus\mathfrak{k},\qquad\mathfrak{d}+[\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d}]=\spanOperator\{{e_{3}}\}\oplus\mathfrak{d},

An important subclass of

\Sigma_{2}

x_{2}^{\prime}(t)=\Phi(t)\cdot x_{1}^{\prime}(t)

(E_{a}(t))
along

2

\mathbf{D}
to the open set of points

(2,3,5)

(\Sigma_{2},cg_{2})

It is immediate that a pair of surfaces

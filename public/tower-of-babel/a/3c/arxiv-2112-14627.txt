On the other hand, the compactness threshold is usually related to the best fractional critical Sobolev constant

\displaystyle S:=\inf\limits_{u\in D^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{{{{\left|{u(x)-u(y)}\right|}^{p}}}}{{{{\left|{x-y}\right|}^{N+sp}}}}dxdy}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p_{s}^{\ast}}\right)^{p/p_{s}^{\ast}}}.

(ii) The Lagrange-Euler functional of problem (

(iii) The non-Hilbert structure of the fractional Sobolev space

u_{n}:=u_{\varepsilon_{n}}\in\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon_{n}}
such that
J_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n})=c_{\varepsilon_{n}}
,
J^{\prime}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{n})=0
and

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}\setminus{\Lambda_{\varepsilon_{n}}})}\geq a.

(6.10)

From Lemma
5.2
, there exists
\{y_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
v_{n}=u_{n}(\cdot+y_{n})\to v
in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
and
\varepsilon_{n}y_{n}\to y_{0}
for
y_{0}\in\Lambda
such that
V(y_{0})=V_{0}
.

Now, if we choose
r>0
such that
B_{r}(y_{0})\subset B_{2r}(y_{0})\subset\Lambda
, we can see that
B_{\frac{r}{\varepsilon_{n}}}\left(\frac{y_{0}}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)\subset\Lambda_{\varepsilon_{n}}
. Then, for any
y\in B_{\frac{r}{\varepsilon_{n}}}(y_{n})
, it holds
|y-\frac{y_{0}}{\varepsilon_{n}}|\leq|y-y_{n}|+|y_{n}-\frac{y_{0}}{\varepsilon_{n}}|<\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}(r+o_{n}(1))<\frac{2r}{\varepsilon_{n}}
for
n
sufficiently large. Hence, for there values of
n
we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\setminus{\Lambda_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\setminus{B_{\frac{r}{\varepsilon_{n}}}(y_{n})}

(6.11)

for any
n
big enough. Using Lemma
6.1
, we can see that

\displaystyle v_{n}(x)\to 0\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ |x|\to\infty\ \ \mbox{uniformly}\ \mbox{in}\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

(6.12)

Therefore, there exists
R>0
such that
v_{n}(x)<a
for
|x|\geq R

\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
, from which
u_{n}(x)=v_{n}(x-y_{n})<a
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(y_{n})
and
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
. On the other hand, by (
6.11
), there exists
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
n\geq\nu
, it holds
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\setminus{\Lambda_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\setminus{B_{\frac{r}{\varepsilon_{n}}}(y_{n})}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(y_{n})
, which gives
u_{n}(x)<a
,
\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\setminus{\Lambda_{\varepsilon_{n}}}
. This last fact contradicts (
6.10
) and thus (
6.9
) is verified.

Now, let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a nonnegative solution to (
3.4
), since
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
6.9
) for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, it follows from the definition
g
that
u_{\varepsilon}
is a solution to (
3.4
), and
v_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}(x/\varepsilon)
is a solution to (
1.1
) for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
.

Finally, we study the behavior of maximum points of solutions to problem (
3.4
). Taking
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0^{+}
and consider a sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}\subset\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon_{n}}
of solution to
(\ref{1.2})
. We first notice that, by
(G_{1})
, there exists
\gamma\in(0,a)
such that

\displaystyle g(\varepsilon_{n}x,t)t=f(t)t+t^{p_{s}^{*}}\leq\frac{V_{1}}{K}t^{p}\ \ \text{for}\ \text{any}\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\ \ 0\leq t\leq\gamma.

(6.13)

Arguing as before, we can find
R>0

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon_{n}}|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{N}}\setminus{B_{R}(y_{n})})}<\gamma.

(6.14)

Moreover, up to subsequence, we may assume that

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon_{n}}|_{L^{\infty}({B_{R}(y_{n})})}\geq\gamma.

(6.15)

Indeed, if (
6.15
) does not hold, in view of (
6.14
) we can see that
|u_{\varepsilon_{n}}|_{\infty}<\gamma
. Then, from
\left\langle{{J^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}({u_{\varepsilon_{n}}}),{u_{\varepsilon_{n}}}}\right\rangle=o_{n}(1)
and (
6.13
)

\|u_{\varepsilon_{n}}\|_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{p}\leq\|u_{\varepsilon_{n}}\|_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{p}+b[u_{\varepsilon_{n}}]_{s,p}^{\theta p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(\varepsilon_{n}x,u_{\varepsilon_{n}})u_{\varepsilon_{n}}dx\leq\frac{V_{1}}{K}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{p},

which yields
\|u_{\varepsilon_{n}}\|_{\varepsilon_{n}}=0
, and this is a contradiction. Hence, (
6.15
) holds true. In the light of (
6.14
) and (
6.15
), we can deduce that the maximum points
p_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
of
u_{\varepsilon_{n}}
belongs to
B_{R}(y_{n})
. Thus,
p_{n}=y_{n}+q_{n}
for some
q_{n}\in B_{R}
. Consequently,
\eta_{\varepsilon_{n}}={\varepsilon_{n}}y_{n}+\varepsilon_{n}q_{n}
is the maximum point of
v_{\varepsilon_{n}}=u_{\varepsilon_{n}}(x/{\varepsilon_{n}})
, we conclude that the maximum point
\eta_{\varepsilon_{n}}
of
v_{\varepsilon_{n}}
is given by
\eta_{\varepsilon_{n}}:=\varepsilon_{n}y_{n}+\varepsilon_{n}q_{n}
. Since
\{q_{n}\}\subset B_{R}
is bounded and
\varepsilon_{n}y_{n}\to y_{0}
with
V(y_{0})=V_{0}
, from the continuity of
V
, we can deduce that
\mathop{\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}V(\eta_{\varepsilon_{n}})=V(y_{0})=V_{0}
.

Next, we give a decay estimate for
v_{\varepsilon_{n}}
. For this purpose, using Lemma
7.1
in
[
17
]
, we can find a continuous positive function
w
and a constant
C>0
such that for large
|x|>R_{0}
it holds that

\displaystyle 0<w(x)\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{N+sp}}

(6.16)

and

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}w+\frac{V_{1}}{2(a+bA_{1}^{(\theta-1)p})}w^{p-1}\geq 0\ \ \rm{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{1}},

(6.17)

for some suitable
R_{1}>0
, and
A_{1}>0
is such that
a+b[v_{n}]_{s,p}^{(\theta-1)p}\leq a+bA_{1}^{(\theta-1)p}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Using
(G_{1})
and (
6.12
), we can find
R_{2}>0
sufficiently large such that

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}{v_{n}}+\frac{V_{1}}{2(a+bA_{1}^{(\theta-1)p})}{v_{n}}^{p-1}\leq

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}{v_{n}}+\frac{V_{1}}{2(a+b[v_{n}]_{s,p}^{(\theta-1)p})}{v_{n}}^{p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(a+b[v_{n}]_{s,p}^{(\theta-1)p})}\left[g(\varepsilon_{n}x+\varepsilon_{n}y_{n},v_{n})-(V(\varepsilon_{n}x+\varepsilon_{n}y_{n})-\frac{V_{1}}{2}){v_{n}}^{p-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(a+b[v_{n}]_{s,p}^{(\theta-1)p})}\left[g(\varepsilon_{n}x+\varepsilon_{n}y_{n},v_{n})--\frac{V_{1}}{2}{v_{n}}^{p-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0,\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus{B_{R_{2}}}.

(6.18)

Thanks to the continuity of
v_{n}
and
w
. there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\psi_{n}:=v_{n}-C_{1}w\leq 0\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ |x|=R_{3},

where

.

Taking
\phi_{n}=\max\{\psi_{n},0\}\in W^{s,p}(B_{R}^{c})
as a test function in (
6
) and using (
6.17
) with
\tilde{w}=C_{1}w
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{{|{v_{n}}(x)-{v_{n}}(y){|^{p-2}}({v_{n}}(x)-{v_{n}}(y))({\phi_{n}}(x)-{\phi_{n}}(y))}}{{|x-y{|^{N+sp}}}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\frac{V_{1}}{2(a+bA_{1}^{(\theta-1)p})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}^{p-1}\phi_{n}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\mathcal{G}_{n}(x,y)}{{|x-y{|^{N+sp}}}}({\phi_{n}}(x)-{\phi_{n}}(y))dxdy+\frac{V_{1}}{2(a+bA_{1}^{(\theta-1)p})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[v_{n}^{p-1}-{{\tilde{w}}^{p-1}}]\phi dx,

(6.19)

where
{\mathcal{G}_{n}(x,y)}:={|{v_{n}}(x)-{v_{n}}(y){|^{p-2}}({v_{n}}(x)-{\tilde{w}}(y))}-{|{\tilde{w}}(x)-{\tilde{w}}(y){|^{p-2}}({\tilde{w}}(x)-{\tilde{w}}(y))}
. Therefore, if we prove that

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\mathcal{G}_{n}(x,y)}{{|x-y{|^{N+sp}}}}({\phi_{n}}(x)-{\phi_{n}}(y))dxdy\geq 0,

(6.20)

it follows from (

0\geq\frac{V_{1}}{2(a+bA_{1}^{(\theta-1)p})}\int_{v_{n}\geq{\tilde{w}}}[v_{n}^{p-1}-{\tilde{w}}^{p-1}](v_{n}-{\tilde{w}})dx\geq 0,

which implies that

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x|\geq R_{3}\ \ and\ \ v_{n}\geq{\tilde{w}}\}=\emptyset.

To obtain our purpose, we first note that for all

\displaystyle|(v_{n}-{\tilde{w}})^{+}(x)-(v_{n}-{\tilde{w}})^{+}(y)|^{2},

which gives

Therefore,
\psi_{n}\leq 0

, which implies that

\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon_{n}}(x)=

\displaystyle{u_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)=v_{n}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}}-y_{n}\right)\leq C_{1}w\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}}-y_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{C}{1+|\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}}-y_{n}|^{N+sp}}

Expandable Local and Parallel Two-Grid Finite Element Scheme for the Stokes Equations
Yanren Hou, Feng Shi, Haibiao Zheng

Abstract
In this paper, we present a novel local and parallel two-grid finite element scheme for solving the Stokes equations, and rigorously establish its a priori error estimates. The scheme admits simultaneously small scales of subproblems and distances between subdomains and its expansions, and hence can be expandable. Based on the a priori error estimates, we provide a corresponding iterative scheme with suitable iteration number. The resulting iterative scheme can reach the optimal convergence orders within specific two-grid iterations ( O ⁡ ( | ln ⁡ H | 2 ) O(|\ln H|^{2}) in 2-D and O ⁡ ( | ln ⁡ H | ) O(|\ln H|) in 3-D) if the coarse mesh size H H and the fine mesh size h h are properly chosen. Finally, some numerical tests including 2-D and 3-D cases are carried out to verify our theoretical results. ’

Introduction

Due to the limiting of computer resources, two-grid finite element methods/nonlinear Galerkin schemes
[
1
,
2
]
and domain
decomposition methods
[
3
]
are popular and powerful tools for numerical simulations of linear and nonlinear PDEs nowadays. Such as two-grid/two-level post-processing schemes for incompressible flow, we refer

[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
and the references therein for details.

In the past decades, a local and parallel two-grid finite element
method for elliptic boundary value problems was initially proposed
in
[
10
]
. The scheme firstly solves the elliptic equation on a
coarse mesh to get an initial lower frequency
guess of the solution. Then the whole computational domain
is divided into a series of disjoint subdomains
\{D_{j}\}
and the
driven term of the error equation, namely the residual term, is split into
several parts defined only on such small subdomains. Finally the global error equation can be
transferred into a series of subproblems with local driven terms.
Since the solution of higher frequency to each subproblem
decays very fast apart from the support of the local driven term, by suitablely
expanding each
D_{j}
to the domain
\Omega_{j}\supset D_{j}

and imposing the homogeneous boundary condition on
\partial\Omega_{j}
,
each subproblem can be approximated in a localized version defined
in the corresponding expanded domain
\Omega_{j}
. The most attractive feature of the scheme is that
not any communication is required between local fine grid subproblems,
which makes the scheme a highly effective parallel scheme.
Such local and parallel two-grid scheme can be found in
[
11
]
and has been extended to the
Stokes equations in
[
12
]
. Error estimates derived in
[
10
,
12
]
show that the approximate solutions in such schems can reach the optimal convergence orders in
both
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms.

However, according to
[
13
]
, the error
estimates are limited by the usage of the superapproximation
property of finite element spaces, which makes the error constant appeared in

[
10
,
12
]
to be a form of
O(t^{-1})
,
where
t
denotes the distance between
\partial D_{j}
and

\partial\Omega_{j}
. To obtain the optimal error orders, usually
t=O(1)
is required, which means the distance of
\partial\Omega_{j}
and
\partial D_{j}
is almost a constant. Therefore one can not expect
\Omega_{j}
to be arbitrary small. This will prevent the corresponding scheme
from utilization in large parallel computer systems. In the previously mentioned local and parallel schemes,
computational results for each
\Omega_{j}
are usually removed
outside
D_{j}
, and are simply pasted together to form the final
approximation, in which discontinuity may appear along the boundaries of different

D_{j}
. Instead, based on the method of partition of unity
[
14
]
, a local and parallel two-grid scheme is proposed for second order linear
elliptic equations
[
15
,
16
]
and is also extended to dealing with the Stokes equations and Navier-Stokes equations
[
17
,
18
]
. Although the partition of unity method can guarantee that
the global approximation is continuous, usage of superposition principle causes a crucial requirement that the distance
t
should be

O(1)
. To overcome such defects, some research on
linear elliptic equations has been done by the first author and his collaborator in
[
19
]
by
iterative method.

In this paper, based on the basic idea presented in
[
10
,
12
]
, as an important extension of the idea in
[
19
]
, we construct a local and parallel two-grid iterative
scheme for solving the Stokes equations, in which the scale of each subproblem is

O(H)
, just requiring that
\mbox{diam}(D_{j})=O(H)
and
t=O(H)
(much smaller than
O(1)
).
Since only a small overlapping in each two adjacent subproblems while
H

tends to zero, the scale of each subproblem can be arbitrary small as
O(H)
, that’s a main reason why we
call the scheme an expandable local and parallel two-grid scheme. Meanwhile, in each cycle of two-grid iteration, to guarantee a better
L^{2}
error estimate, we adopt a coarse grid correction.
Another main contribution
in this paper is that, to yield the globally
continuous velocity in
\Omega
, we use the principle of superposition based on a partition of unity to generate a series of local
and independent subproblems. In particular, for patches of given
size, to obtain a similar approximate accuracy as the one from the standard Galerkin
method on the fine mesh, we carry out rigorous analysis, and through the a priori
error estimate of the scheme, we show that a few number of iterations of order
O(|\ln H|^{2})
in 2-D and

O(|\ln H|)
in 3-D is only needed, respectively. Similar technique has
been successfully applied for adaptive schemes with some a posterior error estimates in
[
20
,
21
]
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we will introduce the model problem and some preliminary materials.
In Section 3, we will present our expandable local and parallel scheme for the Stokes equations. The a priori
error estimates of the scheme are derived and then suggested iterative scheme is presented in Section 4.
Some numerical experiments including 2-D and 3-D examples are carried out in a parallel computer system to support our
theoretical analysis in Section 5. Finally we give some conclusions in Section
5
6.

Consider the following Stokes equations in a smooth bounded convex domain

\Omega\subset R^{d}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}+\nabla p=\mathbf{f},&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0,&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\mathbf{u}=0,&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1)

For a given bounded domain

We denote by

. Therefore, we have

Hereafter, we always use boldface characters to denote vector valued functions or spaces, for instance,

For simplicity, we will also use the symbols

Furthermore, let us denote

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{X}}(D)={\boldsymbol{H}}^{1}(D),\quad{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}(D)={\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}^{1}(D),\quad M(D)=L^{2}(D)

\displaystyle Q(D)=L_{0}^{2}(D)=\{q\in L^{2}(D):\;\int_{D}q=0\},

\displaystyle a_{D}({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{v}})=\nu(\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}},\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}})_{D},\;\;d_{D}(p,{\boldsymbol{v}})=-(p,\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}})_{D}\;\;\forall{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{\boldsymbol{X}}(D),\;p\in M(D).

By these notations, when

,

If we further introduce the bilinear form

B([{\boldsymbol{u}},p],[{\boldsymbol{v}},q])=({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}})\quad\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}},q]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}\times Q.

(2)

Assume that
T^{H}(\Omega)=\{\tau_{\Omega}^{H}\}

{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{H}\times Q^{H}

Given

on

3
Local and Parallel Two-Grid Scheme

In this section, we will introduce our local and parallel two-grid scheme for solving Stokes equations. Firstly, we define the error functions by

\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}={\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0},\quad\hat{p}=p-p_{H}\in Q,

where
[{\boldsymbol{u}},p]
and
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}]
are solutions to (
2
) and (
3
), respectively.
Then
[\hat{\boldsymbol{u}},\hat{p}]
satisfies the error equation

B([\hat{\boldsymbol{u}},\hat{p}],[{\boldsymbol{v}},q])=({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}})-B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}],[{\boldsymbol{v}},q])\quad\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}},q]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}\times Q.

(5)

For any given
partition of unity of
\Omega
, namely
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
with
N\geq 1
an integer,

\Omega\subset\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{N}\mbox{supp }\phi_{j}
and

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{j}\equiv 1
on
\Omega
, we denote
D_{j}=\mbox{supp}\,\phi_{j}
and always assume
that
D_{j}
aligns with
T^{H}(\Omega)
. With this patition of unity,
the error equation (
5
) can be rewritten as:

B([\hat{\boldsymbol{u}},\hat{p}],[{\boldsymbol{v}},q])=({\boldsymbol{f}},\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{j}{\boldsymbol{v}})-B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}],\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{j}[{\boldsymbol{v}},q])\quad\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}},q]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}\times Q.

Clearly, by the principle of superposition, this equation is equivalent
to the summation of the following
N
-subproblems:

B([\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{j},\hat{p}^{j}],[{\boldsymbol{v}},q])=({\boldsymbol{f}},\phi_{j}{\boldsymbol{v}})-B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}],\phi_{j}[{\boldsymbol{v}},q])\quad\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}},q]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}\times Q.

(6)

Namely,
[\hat{\boldsymbol{u}},\hat{p}]=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}[\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{j},\hat{p}^{j}]
. The most important feature for such subproblems is that
all the
subproblems are mutually independent when
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}]
is known. Meanwhile
each subproblem is globally defined with homogeneous
Dirichlet boundary condition for the velocity, however is driven by
right-hand-side term with very small compact support, namely
D_{j}
.
As is
pointed out in
[
21
]
(used also in
[
19
]
) for elliptic problems, solution
\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{j}
to each subproblem may decay very fast away from
D_{j}
.
So for the present Stokes problems, we will localize each subproblem to a small extension domain of
D_{j}
, namely
\Omega_{j}
, and impose homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition for the velocity and discretize the subproblem on some triangulation
T^{h}(\Omega_{j})
for finite element approximation.

Remark 3.1
.

For each
\Omega_{j}
, here we give a much natural choice.
The
partition of unity functions
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
of
\Omega
is selected as the piecewise linear Lagrange basis functions
associated with the coarse triangulation
T^{H}(\Omega)
, where

N
is the number of all vertices in
T^{H}(\Omega)
including the
boundary ones. For each vertex
x_{j}
of
T^{H}(\Omega)
, we can
denote
D_{j}=\mbox{supp }\phi_{j}
, and construct
\Omega_{j}
by one layer extension of
D_{j}
in
T^{H}(\Omega)
, namely

\Omega_{j}=\bigcup_{x_{i}\in D_{j}}D_{i}.

We refer
[
17
]
for more details. Clearly, we know the scale esimates

\mbox{diam}(D_{j}),\;\mbox{dist}(\partial D_{j},\partial\Omega_{j})\approxeq H.

(7)

Generally, we need assume that each
D_{j}
and its extension domain
\Omega_{j}

are aligned with
T^{H}(\Omega)
. For each local domain
\Omega_{j}
, its regular triangulation is denoted by
T^{h}(\Omega_{j})=\{\tau^{h}_{\Omega_{j}}\}
,
with mesh scale over all
j
as
h=\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\left\{\max_{\,\tau^{h}_{\Omega_{j}}\in T^{h}(\Omega_{j})}\{\mbox{diam}(\tau^{h}_{\Omega_{j}})\}\right\}
. As to the natural choices of partition of unity functions and subdomains as discussed in Remark
3.1
, we can simply select local fine mesh
T^{h}(\Omega_{j})
as the limitation of the global regular triangulation of
\Omega
,
T^{h}(\Omega)=\{\tau^{h}_{\Omega}\}
, namely,
T^{h}(\Omega_{j})=T^{h}(\Omega)|_{\Omega_{j}}
, which will be used in the rest of this paper.

Base on the fine mesh triangulations
T^{h}(\Omega_{j})
and
T^{h}(\Omega)
for each
\Omega_{j}
and
\Omega
, we can introduce corresponding finite element spaces
{\boldsymbol{X}}^{h}(\Omega_{j})
,
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}(\Omega_{j})

and
{\boldsymbol{X}}^{h}
,
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}
, similarly defined as
{\boldsymbol{X}}^{H}
and

{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{H}
previously. We also introduce

M^{h}(\Omega_{j})
,
Q^{h}(\Omega_{j})
,
M^{h}
and
Q^{h}
for the pressure accordingly. Noting that
any function in
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}(\Omega_{j})
can be extended to a function in

{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}
through zero value assignment over
\Omega\backslash\Omega_{j}
, we regard

{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}(\Omega_{j})
as a subspace of
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}
in the sense of such
zero extension. In addition, we always assume that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\boldsymbol{X}}^{H}\subset{\boldsymbol{X}}^{h},\quad{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{H}\subset{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}=\bigcup\limits_{1\leq j\leq N}{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}(\Omega_{j}),\\
M^{H}\subset M^{h},\quad M^{H}|_{\Omega_{j}}\subset M^{h}(\Omega_{j}).\end{array}\right.

(8)

Besides, we define discontinuous finite
element spaces associated with the pressure

M_{j}^{h}=\{q_{h}:\;q_{h}|_{\Omega_{j}}\in M^{h}(\Omega_{j}),\;q_{h}|_{\Omega\backslash\Omega_{j}}\in M^{h}(\Omega\backslash\Omega_{j})\},\quad Q^{h}_{j}=M_{j}^{h}\cap L_{0}^{2}(\Omega).

Clearly,
M^{h}\subset\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{N}M_{j}^{h}\subset M_{H}^{h}=\{q_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega):q_{h}|_{\tau_{H}}\in M^{h}(\tau_{H})\}
. Then for
Q_{H}^{h}=M_{H}^{h}\cap L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, we have that
Q^{h}\subset Q_{H}^{h}
and the finite element pair
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}\times Q_{H}^{h}
is stable (see

[
24
]
).

Right now, we can present the localized finite element approximation to error equation (
6
) as follows:
find
[\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{j}_{H,h},\hat{p}^{j}_{H,h}]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}(\Omega_{j})\times Q^{h}(\Omega_{j})
such that
\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}(\Omega_{j})\times Q^{h}(\Omega_{j})

B_{\Omega_{j}}([\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{j}_{H,h},\hat{p}^{j}_{H,h}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}])=({\boldsymbol{f}},\phi_{j}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})_{\Omega_{j}}-B_{\Omega_{j}}([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}],\phi_{j}[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}]),

(9)

in which
\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{j}_{H,h}
and
\hat{p}^{j}_{H,h}
are defined on
\Omega_{j}
. In the rest, we always use the same symbols, that is
[\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{j}_{H,h},\hat{p}^{j}_{H,h}]
, to denote their zero extension over
\Omega\backslash\Omega_{j}
. In such sense,
[\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{j}_{H,h},\hat{p}^{j}_{H,h}]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}\times Q_{j}^{h}(\Omega)
.
Then we construct totally

\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{j}_{H,h}\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h},\quad\hat{p}_{H,h}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\hat{p}_{H,h}^{j}\in Q_{H}^{h},

and the intermediate approximate solution

[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{H,h}]=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}+\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{H}+\hat{p}_{H,h}].

(10)

Since this approximation
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{H,h}]
is derived by solving a
series of subproblems on local fine mesh with homogeneous boundary conditions, its high frequency error
may be suppressed apparently. To balance the lower and higher frequency errors,
a smooth step associated with the intermediate
approximation
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{H,h}]
is resorted through coarse grid
correction as follows: find
[{\boldsymbol{E}}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{H},E_{p}^{H}]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{H}\times Q^{H}
such that

\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{H},q_{H}]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{H}\times Q^{H}

B([{\boldsymbol{E}}_{u}^{H},E_{p}^{H}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{H},q_{H}])=({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{H})-B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{H,h}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{H},q_{H}]).

(11)

To this end, an expected more accurate approximation than the coarse grid approximation is obtained,

[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{H}^{h}]=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h}+{\boldsymbol{E}}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{H},p_{H,h}+E_{p}^{H}]=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}+\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h}+{\boldsymbol{E}}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{H},p_{H}+\hat{p}_{H,h}+E_{p}^{H}].

(12)

In summary, we propose our local and parallel
two-grid scheme for solving (
1
) in the following.

Local and parallel two-grid scheme:

Step 0.

Deriving
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}]
by solving (
3
);

Step 1.

Solving the equation (
9
) to get
\{[\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h}^{j},\hat{p}_{H,h}^{j}]\}_{j=1}^{N}
for each
j
, then constructing
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{H,h}]
by formula (
10
);

Step 2.

Deriving
[{\boldsymbol{E}}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h},E_{p}^{h}]
by
solving (
11
) and finally constructing

[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{H}^{h}]

by (
12
).

4
Theoretical Analysis and Suggested Local and Parallel Two-Grid Iterative Scheme

As the basic step for analyzing the scheme above, we follow the idea discussed in
[
19
]
, and extend the local sub-problem
(
9
) to the original domain
\Omega
. For this purpose, we denote

\Gamma=\partial\Omega
,

\Gamma_{j}=\partial\Omega_{j}\backslash\Gamma
, and introduce a trace
space
{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{j})={\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)|_{\Gamma_{j}}
on

\Gamma_{j}
, which can be defined by interpolation (for example, see

[
26
]
)

{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}(\Gamma_{j}),{\boldsymbol{H}}^{1}(\Gamma_{j})]_{\frac{1}{2}},&\mbox{when}\,\Gamma_{j}\,\mbox{is a closed curve or surface},\\[0.0pt]
[{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}(\Gamma_{j}),{\boldsymbol{H}}_{0}^{1}(\Gamma_{j})]_{\frac{1}{2}},&\mbox{when}\,\Gamma_{j}\,\mbox{is a non-closed curve or surface}.\end{array}\right.

We also denote by

{\boldsymbol{H}}_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{j})={\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}(\Omega)|_{\Gamma_{j}}\subset{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{j})
,
a finite dimensional trace space, equipped with the same
norm as in
{\boldsymbol{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{j})
, and its dual space by

{\boldsymbol{H}}_{h}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{j})=({\boldsymbol{H}}_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{j}))^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim H(\sum\limits_{\tau_{\Omega}^{H}\subset D_{j}}[H^{-2}\|D(I_{H}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\|_{\tau_{\Omega}^{H}}^{2}+H^{-2}\|D(I_{H}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\|_{\tau_{\Omega}^{H}}^{2}])^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{D_{j}},

\displaystyle\|\nabla\hat{I}_{H}[\phi_{j}\hat{I}_{H}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}]\|_{D_{j}}\lesssim\|D(\phi_{j}\hat{I}_{H}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\|_{D_{j}}\lesssim\|D\phi_{j}\hat{I}_{H}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{D_{j}}+\|\phi_{j}D(\hat{I}_{H}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\|_{D_{j}}\lesssim\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{D_{j}}.

Combining these estimates immediately yield

\|\nabla\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{j}\|_{\Omega}+\|\hat{p}_{H}^{j}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H})\|_{D_{j}}+\|p-p_{H}\|_{D_{j}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Now we can state the main theorem concerning the approximate accuracy of solutions derived by the scheme
(
9
)-(
12
) as follows.

Theorem 6
.

Assume that A1, A2, A3 and (
4
) hold and
[{\boldsymbol{u}},p]\in{\boldsymbol{H}}^{r+1}(\Omega)\times{\boldsymbol{H}}^{r}(\Omega)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim H^{\alpha_{d}}(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}\|_{\Omega}),

(26)

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim H(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}),

(27)

\displaystyle\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p-p_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{r}+H^{\alpha_{d}}(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H})\|_{\Omega}+\|p-p_{H}\|_{\Omega}),

(28)

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{r+1}+H(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p-p_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}),

(29)

where
\alpha_{d}>0
is defined in Lemma
4
.

Proof.

We know from (
11
) that

\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{E}}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{H}\|_{\Omega}+\|E_{p}^{H}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H,h}\|_{\Omega}.

Since

[{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p-p_{H}^{h}]=[{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p-p_{h}]+[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{h}-p_{H,h}]+[{\boldsymbol{E}}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{H},E_{p}^{H}],

and

\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h^{r},

the conclusions (
26
) and (
28
) follows immediately due to Lemma
4

and Lemma
5
and the triangle inequality.

To estimate the
L^{2}
-norms of the velocity, we firstly introduce a Stokes projection

P_{H}=[P_{H}^{\boldsymbol{u}},P_{H}^{p}]
defined from
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}\times Q
onto
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{H}\times Q^{H}
:
for given
[{\boldsymbol{w}},r]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}\times Q
, find
[P_{H}^{\boldsymbol{u}}{\boldsymbol{w}},P_{H}^{p}r]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{H}\times Q^{H}
such that

B([{\boldsymbol{v}}_{H},q_{H}],[{\boldsymbol{w}}-P_{H}^{\boldsymbol{u}}{\boldsymbol{w}},r-P_{H}^{p}r])=0,\quad\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{H},q_{H}]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{H}\times Q^{H}.

In view of the definitions of
\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h}
and
\hat{p}_{H,h}
, and

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{j}=1
in
\Omega
, adding up all the
equations of (
13
) over
j
with
\mu=0
gives

\displaystyle B([\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},\hat{p}_{H,h}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}])=({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})-B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}])

(30)

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{j}}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{j}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}ds,\quad\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}\times Q^{h}.

Since
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{H,h}]=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}+\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{H}+\hat{p}_{H,h}]
, then we have

B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{H,h}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}])=({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{j}}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{j}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}ds,\quad\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}\times Q^{h}.

On the another aspect, by the definition of the Stokes projection

P_{H}
above, we can rewrite (
11
) associated with the coarse mesh
correction as

\displaystyle B([{\boldsymbol{E}}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{H},E_{p}^{H}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}])=({\boldsymbol{f}},P_{H}^{\boldsymbol{u}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})-B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{H,h}],[P_{H}^{\boldsymbol{u}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},P_{H}^{p}q_{h}]).

Combining these two equations leads to

\displaystyle B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{H}^{h}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}])=({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})+({\boldsymbol{f}},P_{H}^{\boldsymbol{u}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})-B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{H,h}],[P_{H}^{\boldsymbol{u}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},P_{H}^{p}q_{h}])

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{j}}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{j}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}ds,\quad\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}\times Q^{h}.

Finally, we obtain
\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}\times Q^{h}

B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{H}^{h}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}])=({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{j}}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{j}(I-P_{H}^{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}ds,

and
\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}^{h}\times Q^{h}

B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}])=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\Gamma_{j}}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{j}(I-P_{H}^{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}ds.

(31)

From this error equation about
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}]
, and by virtue of
Lemma
1
,
4
and
5
, we can easily get

\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim H^{\alpha_{d}}(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H})\|_{\Omega}+\|p-p_{H}\|_{\Omega}).

Then we can derive the first result by using the triangle
inequality.

Next, we also use the
Aubin-Nitsche duality argument to show
L^{2}-
error estimate of the velocity. By
A3
, for

{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h}\in{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}(\Omega)
, there exists

[{\boldsymbol{w}},r]\in{\boldsymbol{H}}^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
safistying

B([{\boldsymbol{v}},q],[{\boldsymbol{w}},r])=({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},{\boldsymbol{v}}),\quad\forall[{\boldsymbol{v}},q]\in{\boldsymbol{X}}_{0}(\Omega)\times Q,

and

\|{\boldsymbol{w}}\|_{2,\Omega}+\|r\|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}.

If taking
[{\boldsymbol{v}},q]=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}]
here and in view of
(
31
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}=B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}],[{\boldsymbol{w}},r])

\displaystyle\qquad=B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}],[{\boldsymbol{w}}-P_{H}^{\boldsymbol{u}}{\boldsymbol{w}},r-P_{H}^{p}r]).

Then we can proceeds the estimate as

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}\lesssim(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega})\|\nabla(I-P_{H}^{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{w}}\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h})\|_{\Omega}\|(I-P_{H}^{p})r\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim H(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega})(\|{\boldsymbol{w}}\|_{2,\Omega}+\|r\|_{1,\Omega})

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim H(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega})\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega},

which immediately yields

\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim H(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}).

By using the triangle inequality again, we prove

L^{2}-
error estimate for the velocity.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

By the results in Theorem
6
above, especially (
26
) and (
27
), we can clearly improve the convergence orders by introducing a
two-grid iteration. Actually, we can verify that all the lemmas and theorems above still hold when
replacing
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}]
by
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{H}^{h}]
.
This suggests us to introducing the
following two-grid iterative scheme with a iteration number of K.

K=[\alpha_{d}^{-1}+0.5]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}O(|\ln H|^{2}),&d=2,\\
O(|\ln H|),&d=3.\end{array}\right.

(32)

Local and parallel two-grid iterative scheme:

Step 0.

Setting
k=0
; Deriving
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}]
by solving (
3
) and denoting by
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{0,h},p_{H}^{0,h}]=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}]
;

Step 1.

For
k\geq 0
, solving the equations (
9
) with
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}]=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{k,h},p_{H}^{k,h}]
to get
\{[\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h}^{j},\hat{p}_{H,h}^{j}]\}_{j=1}^{N}
for each
j
, which are
denoted as
\{[\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h}^{k+1,j},\hat{p}_{H,h}^{k+1,j}]\}_{j=1}^{N}
. Then we construct
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h}^{k+1},p_{H,h}^{k+1}]
by formula (
10
);

Step 2.

Deriving
[{\boldsymbol{E}}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{k+1},E_{p}^{k+1}]
by
solving (
11
) with
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h},p_{H,h}]=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h}^{k+1},p_{H,h}^{k+1}]
, and constructing

[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{k+1,h},p_{H}^{k+1,h}]=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H,h}^{k+1}+{\boldsymbol{E}}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{k+1},p_{H,h}^{k+1}+E_{p}^{k+1}].

Step 3.

Checking whether
k+1>K
, if yes, terminating the iteration and deriving
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{H}^{h}]=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{k+1,h},p_{H}^{k+1,h}]
; otherwise, letting
k:=k+1
and turning to Step 1.

Theorem 7
.

Suppose
[{\boldsymbol{u}},p]\in{\boldsymbol{H}}^{r+1}(\Omega)\times H^{r}(\Omega)
, the final approximation
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{H}^{h}]
for the iterative scheme with
K
defined by (
32
) has the following error bounds

\displaystyle\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p-p_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h^{r}+H^{r+1},

(33)

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h^{r+1}+H^{r+2}.

(34)

Proof.

Note that, the Ritz-Projection of
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{H}^{h}]
onto the corresponding course grid finite element spaces is nothing but the standard Galerkin approximation,
see (
31
), when selecting
[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}]=[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{H},q_{H}]
, we have

\displaystyle B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{h}-p_{H}^{h}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{H},q_{H}])=0,

then,

\displaystyle B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{H}^{h}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{H},q_{H}])=B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{H},q_{H}])=({\boldsymbol{f}},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{H})=B([{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H},p_{H}],[{\boldsymbol{v}}_{H},q_{H}]).

Then, we can update
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{0,h},p_{H}^{0,h}]
by
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{1,h},p_{H}^{1,h}]
,
and execute the previous local and parallel two-grid iterative scheme (without Step 0) again to get
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{2,h},p_{H}^{2,h}]
, then follow (
26
) and (
27
) to derive the error estimates of the form, equivalently, there exists a mesh independent constant

c>0
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{2,h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{2,h}\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim H^{\alpha_{d}}(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{1,h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{1,h}\|_{\Omega})

\displaystyle\lesssim H^{2\alpha_{d}}(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{0,h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{0,h}\|_{\Omega}),

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{2,h}\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim H(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{2,h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{2,h}\|_{\Omega}).

Therefore, cyclically executing our two-grid iterative scheme with updating data by a iteration of
K
times, we can finally derive

\displaystyle\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{K,h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{K,h}\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim H^{K\alpha_{d}}(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{0,h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{0,h}\|_{\Omega})

\displaystyle\lesssim H^{K\alpha_{d}}(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}\|_{\Omega}),

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{K,h}\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim H(\|\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{K,h})\|_{\Omega}+\|p_{h}-p_{H}^{K,h}\|_{\Omega}).

By the choice of
K
within (
32
) and the triangle inequality, since
[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{h},p_{H}^{h}]=[{\boldsymbol{u}}_{H}^{K,h},p_{H}^{K,h}]
now, we finally get (
33
) and (
34
).

∎

.

By the estimates in Theorem
7
, we can clearly know that in order to get the optimal convergence orders of the velocity - pressure pair in the sense of

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we have designed an expandable local and parallel two-grid finite element iterative scheme based on superposition
principle for the Stokes problem. The optimal convergence orders of the scheme are analyzed and obtained within suitable two-grid
iterations while numerical tests in 2D and 3D are carried out to show the flexible and high
efficiency of the scheme. The extension of the scheme to the time-dependent problems or nonlinear problems, e.g.,
Navier-Stokes equations, will be our further work.

Restricted Phase Space Thermodynamics of NED-AdS Black Holes
Mozib Bin Awal 1, Prabwal Phukon 1,2

Abstract
We study the Restricted Phase Space Thermodynamics (RPST) of magnetically charged Anti de Sitter (AdS) black holes sourced by nonlinear electrodynamics(NED). The first law and the corresponding Euler relation are examined using the scaling properties. While the mass is homogeneous in the first order, the intensive variables are observed to follow zeroth order homogeneity. We use numerical and graphical techniques to find the critical points of the various thermodynamic quantities. By utilizing the re-scaling properties of the equation of states, we study the thermodynamic processes using different pairs of variables. From our analysis, we infer that although the RPS thermodynamics of NED-AdS black hole resembles those of RN-AdS, Kerr-AdS, Kerr-Sen-Ads black holes in most of its aspects, hinting at a possible universality, there exists one particular μ − C \mu-C process that differs in its behaviour from its counterparts in earlier reported works.

II
NED-AdS black hole and the RPS formalism

Let us begin with the action of the NED-AdS black holes
[
39
]
,

I=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{R-2\Lambda}{16\pi G}+\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{L})\right)

(2)

where
\Lambda=-3/l^{2}
is the cosmological constant,
l
is the AdS radius and
G
is the Newton’s constant. The lagrangian
[
40
]
the NED-AdS black holes is written as

\mathcal{L}(\text{$\mathcal{F}$})=-\frac{\mathcal{F}}{4\pi\text{cosh}^{2}\left(a\sqrt[4]{2\mathcal{|F|}}\right)}

(3)

where
a
is called the coupling constant and
\mathcal{F}=F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}/4
is the field invariant. We analyse the spacetime with spherical symmetry which has the line element squared,

ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{1}{f(r)}dr^{2}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})

(4)

We will exclusively examine magnetized black holes as being electrically charged black holes, for NED that has Maxwell weak-field limit, results in singularities
[
40
]
. The metric function in equation (
4
) can be expressed as,

f(r)=1-\frac{2MG}{r}+\frac{Q^{2}G}{br}\tanh\left(\frac{b}{r}\right)+\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}

(5)

Here, for simplicity, the parameter
b=a\sqrt{q}
is used. The mass of the black hole can be found out in terms of horizon radius by putting
f(r_{+})=0
. This gives,

M=\frac{Q^{2}\tanh\left(\frac{b}{r_{+}}\right)}{2b}+\frac{r_{+}^{3}}{2Gl^{2}}+\frac{r_{+}}{2G}

(6)

Using the metric function, the Hawking temperature is calculated and it xomes out to be,

T=\frac{l^{2}r_{+}^{2}+3r_{+}^{4}-Gl^{2}Q^{2}\text{sech}^{2}\left(\frac{b}{r_{+}}\right)}{4\pi l^{2}r_{+}^{3}}

(7)

Having the temperature and mass, the entropy of the black hole can be easily figured out which comes out to be a very simple expression,

S=\frac{\pi r_{+}^{2}}{G}

(8)

Additionally, the magnetic potential
\Phi
is given by,

\Phi=\frac{q}{b}\tanh\left(\frac{b}{r_{+}}\right)

(9)

Similar to the works in
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
, we introduce the additional pair of thermodynamic variables for the RPST formalism,

C=\frac{l^{2}}{G},\quad\mu=\frac{M-TS-\hat{\Phi}\hat{Q}}{C}

(10)

In the above equation (
10
),
\hat{Q}
is termed as the rescaled electric charge and
\hat{\Phi}
is it’s corresponding rescaled electric potential. These two quantities are expressed as

\hat{Q}=\frac{Ql}{\sqrt{G}},\quad\hat{\Phi}=\frac{\Phi\sqrt{G}}{l}

(11)

here, we wish to make a couple of comments about the chemical potential
\mu
. First, all the thermodynamic variables written above, always need to be positive, apart from the chemical potential
\mu
. Secondly, the chemical potential can be identified as an independent thermodynamic quantity via the of AdS/CFT dictionary
[
41
,
42
,
43
]
.

As mentioned earlier, the RPST formalism is different from Visser’s proposed formalism using AdS/CFT in that, in the former we keep the AdS length,
l
fixed and so its variation vanishes. Taking this into account and utilising the thermodynamic quantities above, it is evident that the first law of thermodynamics is valid.,

dM=TdS+\hat{\Phi}d\hat{Q}+\mu dC

(12)

which is clearly free from the
\mathcal{P}d\mathcal{V}
term. Also, equation (
10
) implies that the Euler-like relation is followed,

M=TS+\hat{\Phi}\hat{Q}+\mu C

Equation (

) and (

[

,
28
,
29

, we should keep in mind that even though the

of the microscopic degrees of freedom of black hole in the bulk. Alternatively, we may put forward new rules to the AdS/CFT dictionary, viz,
\mu_{\text{CFT}}=\mu_{\text{bulk}}
and

. However, for ease of notation, we drop the subscripts and just keep the original
(\mu,C)
instead of
(\mu_{\text{bulk}},N_{\text{bulk}})

(\mu,C)
variable pair in the bulk, when re-scaled as:
\mu\rightarrow\lambda^{-1}\mu
and
C\rightarrow\lambda C
,
\lambda
being an arbitrary non-zero constant, keeps the first law and the Euler-like relation stays intact.

In this section we write the expressions of the thermodynamic quantities such as mass, temperature etc. in terms of the extensive variables
S,\hat{Q}
and

in terms of

G=\frac{l^{2}}{C},\quad r_{+}=\left(\frac{SG}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}

Using these two equations and the rescaled electric charge and potential in equation (

(17)

\mu=\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial C}\right)_{S,\hat{Q}}=\frac{\hat{Q}^{2}\left[\pi^{2}b\text{sech}^{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}b}{\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}S}{C}}}\right)-\frac{2\pi^{3/2}l\sqrt{S}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}b\sqrt{C}}{l\sqrt{S}}\right)}{\sqrt{C}}\right]+bS(\pi C-S)}{4\pi^{3/2}bC^{2}\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}S}{C}}}

Therefore, we have expressed the intensive variables as functions of the extensive variables
S
,
\hat{Q}
and
C
. We can observe that, with the rescaling
S\rightarrow\lambda S
,
\hat{Q}\rightarrow\lambda\hat{Q}
and

. However,

,
\hat{\Phi}
and

in equation (
16
), (
17
) and (

,
\hat{\Phi}
and
\mu
in
S,\hat{Q},C
in zeroth order. At this point, we may remind ourselves that mathematically, the functions which are homogeneous in the zeroth order are by definition intensive. Considering all these arguments and the first law (
12
) and it’s corresponding Euler-like relation (
13
), we can write the Gibbs-Duhem relation as,

d\mu=-\mathcal{S}dT-\hat{\mathcal{Q}}d\hat{\Phi}

with

are homogeneous functions of

and

Moreover, the homogeneity properties of the intensive variables including the internal energy must also be obeyed. RPST formalism satisfies all these relations and homogeneity properties which were absent in the previous formalisms of black hole thermodynamics.

It is evident that equations (

(T,S)
,
(\hat{\Phi},\hat{Q})

IV
Thermodynamic Processes

The goal of this section is to analyse the various thermodynamic processes of the NED-AdS black hole in RPS formalism. In the previous section we discussed from the equation of states that the intensive variables are functions of three extensive variables. So, it will be a very complicated task to analyse all the allowed thermodynamic processes. Therefore we will only focus on some of the special kinds of processes by keeping two of independent variables constant. We will consider simple, conventional processes having only one pair of canonically conjugate variables where one is intensive and the other is extensive. However, in the following sections, we will see that even the simple processes can lead to complicated results and one must resort to alternative methods such as numerical or graphical techniques for the solution of the equations. The Reissner-Nordström-AdS black hole is analysed in Ref
[
27
]
, and in this case one gets the exact analysis. It is required to take approximations in the slow rotating limits to get the analytic results for Kerr-AdS black holes. For Kerr-Sen-Ads black holes
[
29
]
, the authors used numerical techniques for the analysis. In our case also we adopt some graphical and numerical techniques to study the thermodynamic processes.

IV.1
Critical Points and
T-S

The study of the
T-S

T-S

\hat{Q}

Now, we aim to find the critical points of the

\hat{Q}

T-S

\left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial S}\right)_{\hat{Q},C}=0,\quad\left(\frac{\partial^{2}T}{\partial S^{2}}\right)_{\hat{Q},C}=0

(19)

To solve these, we employ the equation of state (
16
). This set of equation becomes extremely complicated and is not possible to find an analytic solution. Therefore, we use graphical and numerical technique. In doing so, we will get the approximate critical points.

We know, by dimensional analysis, that we must have,

S_{c}=p_{1}C,\quad\hat{Q}_{c}=p_{2}C

(20)

where
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are some coefficients. We need to keep in mind such scalings while employing numerical or graphical techniques. After solving we get the critical values as

S_{c}\approx 0.4947C,\quad\hat{Q}_{c}\approx 0.1754C

Accordingly, the critical temperature and the Helmholtz free energy,
F=M-TS
comes out to be

T_{c}\approx 0.2584l^{-l},\quad F_{c}\approx 0.1398l^{-l}C

At these critical points, the black hole undergoes a phase transition. For instance, in Figure
1
we observe that above the critical point
T_{c}
there are three black hole phases and below the critical point only one phase exists. This is analogous to the liquid gas phase transition in classical thermodynamics. The specific heat at constant value of charge is given by

\mathbf{C}=T\frac{dS}{dT}

Which comes out to be

\mathbf{C}=\frac{2S\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}S}{C}}\left[S(\pi C+3S)-\pi^{2}\hat{Q}^{2}\text{sech}^{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}b}{\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}S}{C}}}\right)\right]}{S(3S-\pi C)\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}S}{C}}-\pi^{2}\hat{Q}^{2}\left(2\sqrt{\pi}b\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}b}{\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}S}{C}}}\right)-3\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}S}{C}}\right)\text{sech}^{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}b}{\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}S}{C}}}\right)}

(21)

The specific heat should diverge at the critical points. Putting the calculated values of the critical points in the denominator of equation

we find it becomes approximately zero (approximately because the critical values are not exact but numerically calculated. It comes out of the order of

).

Some approaches have been suggested to probe black hole phase trnasitions by examining Quasinormal Modes (QMNs)
[
44
,
45
,
46

A QMN frequency has both real and imaginary part describing oscillation and damping time respectively, hence they are referred to as the characteristic sounds of the black hole. These are believed to be potentially detected in the gravitational wave detector

48

Specifically, the modes completely change when we go from a small black hole phase to a large black hole phase. Hence it is conclusive that the information of phase transition could be extracted from the gravitational waves.

V
Conclusions

We investigated the thermodynamics of NED-AdS black holes within the RPS framework. We studied the thermodynamics with the introduction of a new pair of thermodynamics variables,
(\mu,C)
and keeping the AdS radius fixed. In this context, the Newton’s constant is treated as a variable. In the RPS formalism, we observed that the first law aligns with the conventional description of ordinary thermodynamics, where the mass can be thought of as the internal energy. The Euler relation and the Gibbs-Duhem relation also hold perfectly. We then calculated the approximate critical values of temperature, free energy, entropy and the rescaled electric charge. Critical points are also found to occur with the usual Maxwell theory. The analysis is done using the RN-AdS black hole system
[
27
]
. However, we have observed that the numerical values of the critical points are totally different in the case when a black hole is sourced by non linear electrodynamics. Hence, we may conclude that the introduction of non linear electrodynamics alters the critical points. These critical points are associated with changes in the behaviour of the black hole, such as transitions between different thermodynamic phases (e.g., small and large black hole phases). So, addition of non linear electrodynamics should affect those behaviour. In this work we have demonstrated how some of the thermodynamic processes show different behaviour from that of the usual maxwell theory, while some processes remain the same. Using the critical points, we studied the iso-
e
-charge processes from the plots of temperature with entropy. It shows a first order phase transition reminiscent of Van der Waals behaviour for some non-vanishing subcritical value,
0<\hat{Q}<\hat{Q_{c}}
. The isovoltage process on the
T-S
plane contains a single minimum and the free energy does not go to zero anywhere suggesting that a Hawking-Page transition is not present in this case. The constant
(S,\hat{Q})
process have a single maximum on the
\mu-C
plane and there exists one Hawking-Page transition point. While, no Hawking-Page points are observed in the fixed
(S,\hat{\Phi})
process, which is confirmed by the
\mu-T
process. This is in contrast with the universality proposed for the
\mu-C
process in the existing literature
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
. The
\mu-C
processes need further exploration for their full understanding. We plan to do so in our future works.

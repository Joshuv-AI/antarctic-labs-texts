Cauchy Problem of Stochastic Kinetic Equations Thanks: This work is supported by NNSFC grant of China (No. 11731009) and the DFG through the CRC 1283 “Taming uncertainty and profiting from randomness and low regularity in analysis, stochastics and their applications”.
Xiaolong Zhang, Xicheng Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper we establish the optimal regularity estimates for the Cauchy problem of stochastic kinetic equations with random coefficients in anisotropic Besov spaces. As applications, we study the nonlinear filtering problem for a degenerate diffusion process, and obtain the existence and regularity of conditional probability densities under few assumptions. Moreover, we also show the well-posedness for a class of super-linear growth stochastic kinetic equations driven by velocity-time white noises, as well as a kinetic version of Parabolic Anderson Model with measure as initial values. Keywords : Stochastic kinetic equations, Anisotropic Besov spaces, Itô-Wentzell’s formula, filtering problem AMS 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary: 60H15, 35R60.



1.
Introduction

Let
\{(W^{k}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0},\ k\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of independent one-dimensional standard Brownian motions
defined on some stochastic basis
(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},\mathbb{P};({\mathscr{F}}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0})
.
Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be the
({\mathcal{F}}_{t})
-predictable
\sigma
-algebra over
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\Omega
, and

{\mathbb{M}}^{d}_{sym}
the set of all
d\times d
-symmetric positive-definite matrices.
Given
{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
-measurable processes

(a,b,\sigma):\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to({\mathbb{M}}^{d}_{sym},\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\ell^{2}),

we introduce the following operators: for a function
u
of
(x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
,

{\mathscr{L}}_{v}u:=\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot\nabla^{2}_{v}u)+b\cdot\nabla_{v}u,\ \ {\mathscr{M}}^{k}_{v}u:=\sigma^{k}\cdot\nabla_{v}u,\ \ k\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
\nabla_{v}
(resp.
\nabla_{x}
) stands for the gradient operator that acts only on the variable
v
(resp.
x
).
In this paper we consider the following stochastic kinetic equation (SKE for short) of Itô’s type:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u=\big[{\mathscr{L}}_{v}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u+f\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\big[{\mathscr{M}}^{k}_{v}u+g^{k}\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},

(1.1)

as well as its adjoint form

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u=\big[{\mathscr{L}}^{*}_{v}u-v\cdot\nabla_{x}u+f\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\big[({\mathscr{M}}^{k}_{v})^{*}u+g^{k}\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},

(1.2)

subject to the initial condition
u(0,\omega,x,v)=u_{0}(\omega,x,v)
being
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}
-measurable,
where the asterisk stands for the adjoint operator, the unknown
u
is a function of
(t,\omega,x,v)
, and
(x,v)
represents the position and velocity,
the nonhomogenous or free terms

(f,g):\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to({\mathbb{R}},\ell^{2})

are
{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
-measurable processes.
Here and below we use the usual Einstein convention for summation: An index appearing in a product
will be summed automatically. Notice that SPDEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are highly degenerate in
x
-direction.

When
\sigma
,
g
are zero and
a,b,f
do not depend on
\omega
, PDEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are degenerate deterministic
equations and called kinetic Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov equations in the literature
since they are just the associated forward and backward Kolmogorov equations of the following SDE

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=V_{t}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,\ \ {\mathord{{\rm d}}}V_{t}=b(t,X_{t},V_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sqrt{2a}(t,X_{t},V_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B_{t},

(1.3)

where
B
is a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion.
Some backgrounds of nonrandom PDEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are referred to
[
17
,
27
,
31
]
and references therein.
We mention that the two-sides estimates of the distribution density of SDE (
1.3
) (also called heat kernel of
{\mathscr{L}}_{v}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}
)
were studied by Delarue and Menozzi
[
8
]
.
The Schauder estimates for deterministic kinetic equation (
1.1
) were established in
[
19
,
20
,
25
]
,
and the maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimates in
(x,v)
were obtained in
[
3
]
(see also
[
5
]
,
[
12
]

and
[
11
]
for nonlocal versions).
Moreover, through studying PDE (
1.1
) with rough drift
b
,
the strong and weak well-posedness of SDE (
1.3
) with irregular drift
b
was also studied in
[
4
,
35
,
30
]
.

On the other hand, SKE (
1.1
) has a close connection with the filtering problem associated with SDE (
1.3
).
In fact, let us consider the following simple stochastic Langevin equation

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=V_{t}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,\quad{\mathord{{\rm d}}}V_{t}={\mathord{{\rm d}}}B_{t}-{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},

where
B
and
W
are two independent Brownian motions. In this model,
W
is regarded as the observable noise,

B
is the hidden noise,
(X,V)
stands for the position and velocity of particles, whose distribution needs to be predicted.
Let
\pi(t,\omega,x,v)
be the conditional probability density of
{\mathbb{P}}((X_{t},V_{t})\in\cdot|{\mathscr{F}}^{W}_{t})
, i.e.,
for any
\varphi\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\pi(t,\omega,x,v)\varphi(x,v){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}v={\mathbb{E}}(\varphi(X_{t},V_{t})|{\mathscr{F}}^{W}_{t})(\omega),

where
{\mathscr{F}}^{W}_{t}
is the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\{W_{s},s\leqslant t\}
.
It is well known that (see
[
26
]
)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x})\pi=\Delta_{v}\pi{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\nabla_{v}\pi{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}.

(1.4)

This is just a model equation of SKE (
1.1
).
Note that the nonlinear filtering problem of SDE (
1.3
) is naturally related to SKE (
1.2
) (see Section 6 below).

The study of SPDEs has a long history since the earlier work of Pardoux
[
23
]
about the SPDEs in Hilbert spaces,
Krylov and Rozovskii
[
13
]
about the SPDEs in the framework of Gelfand triple, and Walsh
[
29
]
about the study of stochastic wave equations.
Nowadays, the theory of SPDEs has been greatly developed, and there are vast literatures, see, for examples,
the monographs
[
6
,
26
,
18
,
15
]
, etc..
Here we only mention parts of the related work.
In the case of smooth coefficients, Rozovsky and Lototsky
[
26
]
systematically developed a complete linear
L^{2}
-theory about
parabolic SPDEs, especially, addressed the application in nonlinear filtering problems (see also
[
33
]
for a non-smooth, degenerate
L^{2}
-theory).
A complete
L^{p}
-theory about semi-linear SPDEs was established by Krylov
[
15
]
.
Here by a theory, according to Krylov’s words
[
15
]
, we mean not only results that, for
f,g^{k}
belonging to a space
{\mathbb{S}}
,
the solution belongs to some stochastic spaces
{\mathbb{S}}^{\prime}
, but also that every element of
{\mathbb{S}}^{\prime}

can be obtained as a solution for certain
f,g^{k}
belonging to the same
{\mathbb{S}}
.
In other words, we have a bijection
F:{\mathbb{S}}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{\prime}
between
f,g^{k}
and the solutions.
In
[
21
]
,
Mikulevicius obtained the Schauder estimates for nondegenerate SPDEs when the leading coefficient
a

is nonrandom and
\sigma=0
. The full Schauder theory for nondegenerate second order SPDEs
with random coefficients was established recently by Du and Liu
[
9
]
.
The main difficulty, when we consider the random leading coefficients, is that the direct Duhamel formulation
is no longer applicable due to the non-adaptedness of the integrands in the stochastic integrals since we have random kernels in this case.
In
[
9
]
, the authors adopt Campanato’s energy characterization of Hölder spaces to overcome this difficulty.

The aim of this paper is to establish the optimal regularity estimates for SKE (
1.1
)
in anisotropic Besov spaces under some Hölder regularity assumptions on
a,b,\sigma
.
Our optimal regularity estimates are not only for nondegenerate velocity component
v
, but also for degenerate
position component
x
. As discussed above, when
a
is nonrandom, by Duhamel’s formulation, and using completely
the same argument developed in
[
11
]
, one can establish a satisfactory Schauder theory.
However, for random leading coefficient
a
, the method adopted by Du and Liu
[
9
]
, if it is not impossible,
seems hard to be used for SKE (
1.1
).
We mention that when
d=1
, Pascucci and Pesce
[
24
]
obtained the Schauder estimates for SKE (
1.1
)
by using Itô-Wentzell’s formula to reduce SKE (
1.1
) into a deterministic PDE
with random coefficients. Thus the price to pay is that the coefficients have to be at least
C^{3}
-differentiable in
x,v
.
Moreover, we are also interested in the following nonlinear SKE with super-linear growth coefficient in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u=[\Delta_{v}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+|u|^{\gamma+1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}B,\ \gamma\in[0,\tfrac{1}{8}),

(1.5)

where
{\mathord{{\rm d}}}B
stands for the velocity-time white noise.
In particular, it includes a kinetic version of continuous Parabolic Anderson Model (abbreviated as PAM) when
\gamma=0
and
u\geqslant 0
.
We referred to
[
2
]
and references therein for some background about PAM.
In the nondegenerate case, i.e.,
u
does not depend on
x
,
the global well-posedness to the above super-linear growth SKEs with Dirichlet boundary conditions for
\gamma\in[0,\frac{1}{2})

was first established by Mueller
[
22
]
. His proof is based on the large deviation estimates and only for positive solutions.
While in
[
15
, Section 8.4]
, Krylov provides a quite different and more direct proof, and still for positive solutions,
where the key observation is that for any bounded stopping time
\tau
,

{\mathbb{E}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}u(\tau,v){\mathord{{\rm d}}}v\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}u(0,v){\mathord{{\rm d}}}v.

Here we aim to show the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions
and the optimal regularity of
u
in
t,x,v
to nonlinear SKE (
1.5
). As above, the key point is to show

{\mathbb{E}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|u(\tau,x,v)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}v\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|u(0,x,v)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}v.

See Lemma
7.9
below.

Now we describe the strategies of proving the optimal Besov regularity to SKE (
1.1
).
First of all, we study the model equation

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u=[\Delta_{v}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u+f]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+g^{k}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t}.

(1.6)

Using some tools from
[
11
]
, we establish the optimal regularity estimates of the solution
in anisotropic Besov spaces with respect to the time and spatial-velocity variables by Duhamel’s formula.
We would like to emphasize that the semigroup
P_{t}
associated with
\Delta_{v}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}

behaves unlike the Gaussian heat semigroup. One has to consider the transport semigroup
\Gamma_{t}f(x,v):=f(x+tv,v)
.
The non-commutativity
\Gamma_{t}P_{t}\not=\Gamma_{t}P_{t}
brings us many difficulties.
Moreover, although
P_{t}
is a strongly continuous semigroup in
L^{p}
-space,
there seems not exist a good characterization for the domain of
\Delta_{v}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}
in
L^{p}
-space.
Thus, SKE (
1.6
) does not fall into the abstract framework studied in
[
6
]
. We have to carefully handle
the anisotropicity caused by degenerate term
v\cdot\nabla_{x}
.
Next, by a generalized
Itô-Wentzell’s formula (
[
15
,
16
]
),
we transform SKE (
1.1
) with random but constant
a,\sigma
into model equation (
1.6
), and then
obtain the regularity estimates for (
1.1
), where the key point is that we need to work in Besov spaces
with finite integrability exponent.
Finally, we shall use the freezing coefficient argument to derive the optimal regularity estimates for variable random coefficients
a,\sigma
.
In order to make the perturbation term can be absorbed by the freezing term, we introduce a new localized
anisotropic Besov norm
|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}}
in (
2.18
) below. Unlike
L^{\infty}\not\subset L^{p}
for
p<\infty
,
one obvious advantage of using this localized norm is that for any
s>0
,

|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}}\leqslant C|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p^{\prime};\theta}},\ \ p^{\prime}\geqslant p.

Thus our initial value can be a constant. Such a norm was also used in
[
36
]
.
It is noticed that if the coefficients
a,b,\sigma
and
f,g
,
initial value
u_{0}
do not depend on the position variable
x
, then
SKEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) naturally reduce to the classical SPDEs studied in
[
15
]
.
To the authors’ knowledge, even in this classical case, our Besov estimates are also new.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we introduce some anisotropic function spaces, and prepare some useful results for later use.
In Section 3, we establish the optimal regularity estimates for random constant coefficients case,
namely,
a
and
\sigma^{k}
are independent of space and velocity variables. In Sections 4 and 5, we prove our main results
(see Theorems
5.2
,
5.3
,
5.5
).
In Section 6, we apply our results to the filtering problem of degenerate diffusion processes, and show the existence and regularity of
conditional density processes, which satisfies a nonlinear SPDE in classical sense under some assumptions.
In Section 7, we also show a well-posedness result for a class of nonlinear SKEs driven by velocity-time white noises, and obtain
the optimal Besov regularity, which seems to be new even in the classical nondegenerate case (cf.
[
29
,
15
]
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Vector-valued anisotropic Besov and Hölder spaces

In this subsection we introduce the vector-valued anisotropic Hölder and Besov spaces and their basic properties for later use.
Let
m=(m_{1},\cdots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n}=N
and
\theta=(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n})\in[1,\infty)^{n}

be fixed. We denote
\theta\cdot m=\theta_{1}m_{1}+\cdots+\theta_{n}m_{n}
.
For
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
,
we introduce the following distance in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by

|x-y|_{\theta}:=\sum^{n}_{i=1}|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{1/\theta_{i}},\quad x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}.

Let
{\mathbb{B}}
be a Banach space. For
p\in[1,\infty]
, let
{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}}):=L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N},{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x;{\mathbb{B}})

be the usual vector-valued
L^{p}
-space over
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. For
h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and a map
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{B}}
, the first order difference operator is defined by

\delta^{(1)}_{h}f(x):=f(x+h)-f(x),

and for
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
M
-order difference operator is defined recursively by

\delta^{(M)}_{h}f(x):=\delta^{(1)}_{h}\delta^{(M-1)}_{h}f(x).

By induction, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\delta^{(M)}_{h}f(x)=\sum^{M}_{k=0}(-1)^{M-k}{M\choose k}f(x+kh),\quad h\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(2.1)

where
\binom{M}{k}
is the binomial coefficient.

Definition 2.1

(Vector-valued anisotropic Hölder spaces)
.

For
s\in(0,\infty)
, the
{\mathbb{B}}
-valued anisotropic Hölder space is defined by norm

\|f\|_{\mathbf{C}^{s}_{\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}:=\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}+[f]_{\mathbf{C}^{s}_{\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}<\infty,

where

[f]_{\mathbf{C}^{s}_{\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}:=\sup_{h}\|\delta^{([s]+1)}_{h}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}/|h|^{s}_{\theta}.

Here
[s]
denotes the greatest integer less than
s
.

In order to introduce the anisotropic Besov space, we need a symmetric
nonnegative
C^{\infty}-
function
\phi^{\theta}_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with

\phi^{\theta}_{0}(\xi)=1\ \mathrm{for}\ \xi\in B^{\theta}_{1}\ \mathrm{and}\ \phi^{\theta}_{0}(\xi)=0\ \mathrm{for}\ \xi\notin B^{\theta}_{2},

where for
r>0
and
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,

B^{\theta}_{r}(y):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x-y|_{\theta}\leqslant r\},\ \ B^{\theta}_{r}:=B^{\theta}_{r}(0).

For
\xi=(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\phi^{\theta}_{j}(\xi):=\phi^{\theta}_{0}(2^{-\theta j}\xi)-\phi^{\theta}_{0}(2^{-\theta(j-1)}\xi),

where

2^{-\theta j}\xi:=(2^{-\theta_{1}j}\xi_{1},\cdots,2^{-\theta_{n}j}\xi_{n}).

By the very definition, one sees that for
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\phi^{\theta}_{j}(\xi)=\phi^{\theta}_{1}(2^{-\theta(j-1)}\xi)\geqslant 0
and

\mathrm{supp}\,\phi^{\theta}_{j}\subset B^{\theta}_{2^{j+1}}\setminus B^{\theta}_{2^{j-1}},\quad\sum^{n}_{j=0}\phi^{\theta}_{j}(\xi)=\phi^{\theta}_{0}(2^{-n}\xi)\to 1,\quad n\to\infty.

For
f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})
, let
\hat{f}
be the Fourier transform of
f
defined by

\hat{f}(\xi):=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\mathrm{e}^{-{\rm i}\xi\cdot x}f(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x,\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

and
\check{f}
the Fourier inverse transform of
f
defined by

\check{f}(x):=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}\xi\cdot x}f(\xi){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\xi,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be the space of all Schwartz functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{B}})
the space of all continuous linear operators from

{\mathcal{S}}
to
{\mathbb{B}}
, called vector-valued distribution space.
Note that
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
is just the tempered distribution space.
In a natural way,
{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})\subset{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{B}})
, and we can extend the Fourier
transform to the element in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{B}})
by duality. For given
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the block operator
\mathcal{R}^{\theta}_{j}
is defined on
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{B}})
by (see
[
1
]
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\theta}_{j}f(x):=(\phi^{\theta}_{j}\hat{f})\check{\ }(x)=\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}*f(x)=2^{\theta\cdot m(j-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{1}(2^{\theta(j-1)}y)f(x-y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y,

(2.2)

where the convolution is understood in the distributional sense. In particular, by the symmetry of
\phi^{\theta}_{j}
, we have

\langle{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f,g\rangle=\langle f,{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}g\rangle,\ \ f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},\ g\in{\mathcal{S}},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
stands for the dual pair between
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Definition 2.2

(Vector-valued anisotropic Besov spaces)
.

For
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, the
{\mathbb{B}}
-valued anisotropic Besov space is defined by

\mathbf{B}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}}):=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{B}}):\|f\|_{\mathbf{B}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}:=\sup_{j\geqslant 0}\big(2^{sj}\|\mathcal{R}^{\theta}_{j}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\big)<\infty\right\}.

When
\theta=(1,\cdots,1)
, we shall simply write

\mathbf{B}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{B}}):=\mathbf{B}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}}),\ {\mathcal{R}}_{j}:={\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j},\ \phi_{j}:=\phi^{\theta}_{j}.

When
{\mathbb{B}}={\mathbb{R}}
, we shall simply write
\mathbf{B}^{s}_{p;\theta}:=\mathbf{B}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Remark 2.3
.

In the literature, there are usually two subscripts
p,q
in the definition of Besov spaces, where

p
stands for the integrability of spatial variables and
q
denotes the
\ell^{q}
-norm of frequency index
j
.
Since we only use the Besov space of
q=\infty
, in our definition, we take
q=\infty
and drop it for simplicity.

Remark 2.4
.

In application below, we usually take
{\mathbb{B}}={\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}:=L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
.
In this case, by Fubini’s theorem, for any
s<s^{\prime}
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}^{s^{\prime}}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})\subset{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\mathbf{B}^{s}_{p;\theta})\subset\mathbf{B}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}).

(2.3)

Indeed, by definition and Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\|f\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\mathbf{B}^{s}_{p;\theta})}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\|f(\omega,\cdot)\|^{p}_{\mathbf{B}^{s}_{p;\theta}}={\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{j\geqslant 0}2^{sjp}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f(\omega,\cdot)\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{sjp}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f(\omega,\cdot)\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}\right)=\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{sjp}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{(s-s^{\prime})jp}\|f\|^{p}_{\mathbf{B}^{s^{\prime}}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}=1/(1-2^{(s-s^{\prime})p})\|f\|^{p}_{\mathbf{B}^{s^{\prime}}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})},

and

\displaystyle\|f\|^{p}_{\mathbf{B}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\geqslant 0}2^{sjp}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\leqslant{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{j\geqslant 0}2^{sjp}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f(\omega,\cdot)\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}\right)=\|f\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\mathbf{B}^{s}_{p;\theta})}.

We recall the following result whose proof is completely the same as in

[
1
]
*p.52, Lemma 2.1. We omit the details.

Lemma 2.5

(Bernstein’s type inequalities)
.

For any
1\leqslant p\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
i=1,\cdots,n
, there is a constant

C=C(\theta,m,p,q,k,i)>0

such that for all

,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}_{x_{i}}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim_{C}2^{j(\theta_{i}k+(\theta\cdot m)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}))}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})},

(2.4)

where
\nabla^{k}_{x_{i}}
denotes the

-order gradient with respect to

.

As easy consequences of the above lemma, we have

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
1\leqslant p\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
i=1,\cdots,n
.

(i)

If
0<\theta_{i}k+(\theta\cdot m)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})<s
, then for some

,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}_{x_{i}}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})},

(2.5)

and for
s^{\prime}=s+\theta_{i}k+(\theta\cdot m)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}_{x_{i}}f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{q;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s^{\prime}}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

(2.6)

(ii)

For any

, there is a
C=C(\theta,m,p,s,s_{1},s_{2})>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|^{(s-s_{1})/(s_{2}-s_{1})}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s_{2}}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}\|f\|^{(s_{2}-s)/(s_{2}-s_{1})}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s_{1}}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

(2.7)

Proof.

(i) Noting that
f=\sum_{j}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f
, by Bernstein’s inequality (

), we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}_{x_{i}}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\|\nabla^{k}_{x_{i}}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\leqslant\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{j(\theta_{i}k+(\theta\cdot m)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}))}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{j(\theta_{i}k+(\theta\cdot m)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}))-js}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

Thus we get (
2.5
). Estimate (
2.6
) is direct by (
2.4
) and
{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\nabla^{k}_{x_{i}}=\nabla^{k}_{x_{i}}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}
.

(ii) It follows by definition.
∎

The following lemma is elementary.

Lemma 2.7
.

For any
0<\beta<\alpha<\infty
, it holds that for all
\lambda>0
,

\sum_{j\geqslant 0}(\lambda 2^{\alpha j}\wedge 1)2^{-\beta j}\leqslant\left(\frac{2^{\beta}}{\ln 2}\int^{\infty}_{0}(r^{\alpha}\wedge 1)r^{-\beta-1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right)\lambda^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geqslant 0}(\lambda 2^{\alpha j}\wedge 1)2^{-\beta j}\leqslant 2^{\beta}\int^{\infty}_{0}(\lambda 2^{\alpha s}\wedge 1)2^{-\beta s}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=\frac{2^{\beta}\lambda^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}}{\ln 2}\int^{\infty}_{\lambda^{1/\alpha}}(r^{\alpha}\wedge 1)r^{-\beta-1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

which gives the desired estimate.
∎

Now we show the following important characterization of anisotropic Besov space
{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})

(cf.
[
28
]
).

Lemma 2.8
.

For any
s\in(0,\infty)
and
p\in[1,\infty]
,
there exists a constant
C=C(\theta,m,p,s)\geqslant 1
such that for all
f\in{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}\asymp_{C}\sup_{h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Big(|h|^{-s}_{\theta}\|\delta^{([s]+1)}_{h}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\Big)+\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}.

(2.8)

In particular,
{\mathbf{C}}^{s}_{\theta}({\mathbb{B}})={\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{\infty;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})
and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}\asymp_{C}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s/\theta_{1}}_{p;x_{1}}({\mathbb{B}})}+\cdots+\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s/\theta_{n}}_{p;x_{n}}({\mathbb{B}})},

(2.9)

where for
i=1,\cdots,n
,

\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;x_{i}}({\mathbb{B}})}:=\sup_{j\geqslant 0}\left[2^{sj}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{x_{i}}_{j}f(x)\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{B}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right],

and for
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{x_{i}}_{j}f(x):=\mathcal{R}_{j}f(\cdots,x_{i-1},\cdot,x_{i+1},\cdots)(x_{i}).

(2.10)

Proof.

(i) For simplicity, we set
M:=[s]+1
. We first prove that for any
h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,

\|\delta^{(M)}_{h}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim|h|^{s}_{\theta}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

By definition, without loss of generality, it suffices to prove that for
h_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m_{1}}
,

\displaystyle\|\delta^{(M)}_{{\bf h}_{1}}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim|h_{1}|^{s/\theta_{1}}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})},

(2.11)

where
{\bf h}_{1}:=(h_{1},0,\cdots,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
Since
{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\delta^{(M)}_{{\bf h}_{1}}f=\delta^{(M)}_{{\bf h}_{1}}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f
, by (
2.1
)
with Taylor’s expansion and Bernstein’s inequality (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\delta^{(M)}_{{\bf h}_{1}}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim|h_{1}|^{M}\|\nabla^{M}_{x_{1}}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim|h_{1}|^{M}2^{(M\theta_{1}-s)j}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})},

and also,

\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\delta^{(M)}_{{\bf h}_{1}}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{M}{M\choose k}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\leqslant 2^{M}2^{-sj}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

Hence,

\|\delta^{(M)}_{{\bf h}_{1}}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\leqslant\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\delta^{(M)}_{{\bf h}_{1}}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\Big[(|h_{1}|^{M}2^{(M\theta_{1}-s)j})\wedge 2^{-sj}\Big]\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})},

which gives (
2.11
) by Lemma
2.7
.

(ii) For
j\geqslant 1
, since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(h){\mathord{{\rm d}}}h=(2\pi)^{d/2}\phi^{\theta}_{j}(0)=0
, by (
2.1
) and the change of variable, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(h)\delta^{(M)}_{h}f(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}h

\displaystyle=\sum^{M}_{k=0}(-1)^{M-k}{M\choose k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(h)f(x+kh){\mathord{{\rm d}}}h

\displaystyle=\sum^{M}_{k=1}(-1)^{M-k}{M\choose k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(h)f(x+kh){\mathord{{\rm d}}}h

\displaystyle=\sum^{M}_{k=1}(-1)^{M-k}{M\choose k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi^{\theta}_{j}(k\cdot)^{\check{}}(h)f(x+h){\mathord{{\rm d}}}h.

In particular, if we define for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

\phi^{\theta,M}_{j}(\xi):=(-1)^{M+1}\sum^{M}_{k=1}(-1)^{M-k}{M\choose k}\phi^{\theta}_{j}(k\xi),

then

(-1)^{M+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(h)\delta^{(M)}_{h}f(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}h=[\phi^{\theta,M}_{j}]^{\check{}}*f(x)=:\mathcal{R}^{\theta,M}_{j}f(x),

and for
j\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta,M}_{j}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(h)|\,\|\delta^{(M)}_{h}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}h

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(h)||h|^{s}_{\theta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}h\right)\sup_{h}\frac{\|\delta^{(M)}_{h}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}}{|h|^{s}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-sj}\sup_{h}\Big(|h|^{-s}_{\theta}\|\delta^{(M)}_{h}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\Big).

(2.12)

On the other hand, noting that

\phi^{\theta,M}_{j}(\xi)=\phi^{\theta,M}_{0}(2^{-\theta j}\xi)-\phi^{\theta,M}_{0}(2^{-\theta(j-1)}\xi)

and

\phi^{\theta,M}_{0}(\xi)=1\mbox{ for $\xi\in B^{\theta}_{1/M}$ and }\phi^{\theta,M}_{0}(\xi)=0\mbox{ for $\xi\notin B^{\theta}_{2}$},

we have

\mathrm{supp}\ \phi^{\theta,M}_{j}\subset B^{\theta}_{2^{j+1}}\setminus B^{\theta}_{(2^{j-1})/M}.

Hence, for any
i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with
|j-i|>\log_{2}M+2=:\gamma
,

\mathcal{R}^{\theta,M}_{j}\mathcal{R}^{\theta}_{i}f(x)=0.

Moreover, noting that for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\phi^{\theta,M}_{j}(\xi)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{M+1}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}(-1)^{M-k}{M\choose k}\phi^{\theta}_{j}(k\xi)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{M+1}\sum^{M}_{k=1}(-1)^{M-k}{M\choose k}=1,

we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{i}f=\sum_{j\geqslant 0}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{i}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta,M}_{j}f=\sum_{|j-i|\leqslant\gamma}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{i}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta,M}_{j}f.

Therefore, for
i\geqslant\gamma+1
, by (
2.12
),

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{i}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{|i-j|\leqslant\gamma}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{i}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta,M}_{j}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim\sum_{|i-j|\leqslant\gamma}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta,M}_{j}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|i-j|\leqslant\gamma}2^{-sj}\sup_{h}\Big(|h|^{-s}_{\theta}\|\delta^{(M)}_{h}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-si}\sup_{h}\Big(|h|^{-s}_{\theta}\|\delta^{(M)}_{h}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\Big).

For
i<\gamma+1
, we always have

\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{i}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}({\mathbb{B}})}.

Thus we obtain another side estimate and complete the proof.
∎

Definition 2.14

(Generalized Itô’s process)
.

Let

,

\displaystyle\langle u(t\wedge\tau),\varphi\rangle=\langle u(0),\varphi\rangle+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\langle f(s),\varphi\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\langle g^{k}(s),\varphi\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}.

(2.21)

Let

Lemma 2.15

(Itô-Wentzell’s formula)
.

Let

,
g\in\mathbf{D}(\ell^{2})
and

be a stopping time,

X
an Itô’s process. Suppose (

satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}w(t,x)&=\Big[\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{ik}_{t}\sigma^{jk}_{t}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w(t,x)+b_{t}\cdot\nabla w(t,x)\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\\
&\quad+\Big[f(t,x+X_{t})+\partial_{i}g^{k}_{t}(x+X_{t})\sigma^{ik}_{t}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\\

3.
Model equations

Let
W_{t}
be a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion. Define

(X_{t},V_{t}):=\left(\sqrt{2}\int^{t}_{0}W_{s}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,\sqrt{2}W_{t}\right)

and the kinetic semigroup

\displaystyle P_{t}f(x,v):={\mathbb{E}}f(x+tv+X_{t},v+V_{t})=(\Gamma_{t}p_{t})*(\Gamma_{t}f)(x,v),

(3.1)

where
p_{t}
is the density of
(X_{t},V_{t})
given by

p_{t}(x,v)=\left(\frac{2\pi t^{4}}{3}\right)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{3|x|^{2}+|3x-2tv|^{2}}{4t^{3}}\right),

and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}f(x,v):=f(x+tv,v).

(3.2)

It is easy to see that for any
\lambda>0
,

p_{\lambda t}(x,v)=\lambda^{-2d}p_{t}(\lambda^{-3/2}x,\lambda^{-1/2}v)

and for
f\in{\mathcal{S}}
, by Itô’s formula,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P_{t}f=[\Delta_{v}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}]P_{t}f,

(3.3)

which also holds for
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{B}})
in the distributional sense by duality.

In the remainder of this paper, we take the parameters in Subsection 2.1 as

N=2d,\ n=2,\ m_{1}=m_{2}=d,\ \theta=(3,1).

For notational simplicity, we use
z=(x,v)
to denote a generic point in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
, and
for a Banach space
{\mathbb{B}}
, and for
T>0
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we write

{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{T}({\mathbb{B}}):=L^{p}([0,T];{\mathbb{B}}),\ \ {\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z,\omega}({\mathbb{B}}):={\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}({\mathbb{B}}))={\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})).

The operator
\Gamma_{t}
in (
3.2
) will play a crucial role below. Note that it is in general not a bounded operator in
{\mathbf{B}}^{s}_{p;\theta}
,
but we obviously have

\|\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}}=\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}}.

First of all we have the following estimate about the heat kernel

that is similar to
[

]
*Lemma 5.1.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
l\geqslant 0
, there is a constant
C=C(d,l)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}p_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}\lesssim_{C}(t4^{j})^{-l},\ \ \forall j\in{\mathbb{N}},\ t>0.

(3.4)

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}p_{t}(x,v)=t^{-2d}p_{1}(t^{-\frac{3}{2}}x+t^{-\frac{1}{2}}v,t^{-\frac{1}{2}}v).

Let
\hbar:=t^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{-j}
. Denote the left hand side of (
3.4
) by
{\mathscr{I}}
.
By the change of variable, we have

\displaystyle{\mathscr{I}}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{v})p_{1}(x-\hbar^{3}\bar{x}+v-\hbar\bar{v},v-\hbar\bar{v}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}\right|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}v.

We define the following operators: for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\Lambda^{m}f(x,v):=(\Delta_{x}+\Delta_{v}^{3})^{m}f(x,v),

and for
\hat{f}\in{\mathcal{S}}
with
0\notin
support of
\hat{f}
,

\widehat{\Lambda^{-m}f}(\xi,\eta):=(|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{6})^{-m}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta).

Let

H(\bar{x},\bar{v},x,v):=p_{1}(x-\hbar^{3}\bar{x}+v-\hbar\bar{v},v-\hbar\bar{v}).

Since
\phi^{\rm\theta}_{1}(\xi,\eta)\subset B^{\theta}_{4}\setminus B^{\theta}_{1}
, for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathscr{I}}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\Lambda^{-m}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{v})\Lambda^{m}H(\cdot,\cdot,x,v)(\bar{x},\bar{v}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}\right|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}v

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}|\Lambda^{-m}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{v})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\left|\Lambda^{m}H(\cdot,\cdot,x,v)\right|(\bar{x},\bar{v}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}v.

By the chain rule and elementary calculations, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\bar{x},\bar{v}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\left|\Lambda^{m}H(\cdot,\cdot,x,v)\right|(\bar{x},\bar{v}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}v\lesssim{\hbar}^{6m}.

Since
\Lambda^{-m}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{1}
is a Schwartz function, we thus have

{\mathscr{I}}\lesssim{\hbar}^{6m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}|\Lambda^{-m}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{v})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}\lesssim{\hbar}^{6m}.

Moreover, it is easy to see that

{\mathscr{I}}\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}|\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{1}(\bar{x},\bar{v})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}\lesssim 1.

The desired estimate now follows by the above two estimates.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

(i) For any
p\in[1,\infty]
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

and

, there is a constant

such that for all

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim_{C}2^{-j\alpha}h_{l,\alpha}(t4^{j})\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})},

(3.5)

where

(ii) Let
p\in[1,\infty]
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
r\in[1,\infty)
,
q\in[r,\infty]
and

and
\kappa^{\prime}\geqslant\kappa

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa^{\prime}-1}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t-s}f(s)\|^{r}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/r}\\
&\qquad\lesssim_{C}2^{-(\alpha+2(\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{\kappa}{q}))j}\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}\|f(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q}.\end{split}

(3.6)

Proof.

(i) By definition (
3.1
), we have (see
[
11

j\geqslant 1
, then by Young’s inequality, we have for any

and

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}p_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}\sum_{\ell\in{\Theta}^{t}_{j}}\|\Gamma_{t}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{\ell}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{C2}}}{{\lesssim}}(t4^{j})^{-l}\sum_{\ell\in{\Theta}^{t}_{j}}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{\ell}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim(t4^{j})^{-l}\sum_{\ell\in{\Theta}^{t}_{j}}2^{-\alpha\ell}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t4^{j})^{-l}2^{-j\alpha}\big(1+t4^{j}\big)^{|\alpha|}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})},

where the last step is due to
[
11
, Lemma 6.7]
. By the arbitrariness of

\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\leqslant 2^{-j\alpha}(1\wedge(t4^{j})^{-l})\big(1+t4^{j}\big)^{|\alpha|}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

If
j=0

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{0}P_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{0}\Gamma_{t}p_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}\sum_{\ell\in{\Theta}^{t}_{0}}\|\Gamma_{t}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{\ell}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim\sum_{\ell\in{\Theta}^{t}_{0}}2^{-\alpha\ell}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+t\big)^{|\alpha|}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}\leqslant T^{l}(1\wedge t^{-l})\big(1+t\big)^{|\alpha|}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

Thus we obtain (
3.5
) for
\alpha\not=0

\alpha=0

(ii) Denote the left hand side of (

) by

. For
q\in(r,\infty]
,
let

with

)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{I}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j\alpha}\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa^{\prime}-1}h^{r}_{l,\alpha}((t-s)4^{j})\|f(s)\|^{r}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|f(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q}.

For the first integral denoted by

, by the change of variable we have

\displaystyle{\mathscr{I}}_{0}

\displaystyle=4^{-(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{1-\kappa}{q}-\frac{1-\kappa^{\prime}}{r})j}\left(\int^{t4^{j}}_{0}h^{q^{\prime}}_{l,\alpha}(s)s^{\frac{q^{\prime}(1-\kappa)}{q}-\frac{q^{\prime}(1-\kappa^{\prime})}{r}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4^{-(\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{\kappa}{q})j}\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}(1\wedge s^{-lq^{\prime}})(1+s)^{|\alpha|q^{\prime}}s^{\frac{q^{\prime}(1-\kappa)}{q}-\frac{q^{\prime}(1-\kappa^{\prime})}{r}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}

In particular, for

large enough, the last integral is finite. Thus,

For

, it is similar. The proof is complete.

.

For any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\kappa\geqslant 0

Proof.

Let
T>0
.

,

\displaystyle\|P_{t}f-\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}t^{\frac{\alpha-\gamma}{2}}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

(3.17)

Proof.

(i) Write
f_{R}:=\chi^{R}_{0}f
. Let
{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}
be defined as in (
2.10
). Note that

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{t}{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}(x,v)-{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}(x,v)\|_{\mathbb{B}}\leqslant t|v|\int^{1}_{0}\|\nabla_{x}{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}\|_{\mathbb{B}}(x+stv,v){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Since
f_{R}
has support in
B^{\theta}_{2R}
, by Bernstein’s inequality (
2.4
), we have

\|\Gamma_{t}{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}-{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\leqslant 2Rt\|\nabla_{x}{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim t2^{j}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})},

and also,

\|\Gamma_{t}{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}-{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\leqslant 2\|{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{t}f_{R}-f_{R}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\|\Gamma_{t}{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}-{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim\sum_{j\geqslant 0}((t2^{j})\wedge 1)\|{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f_{R}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\geqslant 0}((t2^{j})\wedge 1)2^{-\frac{\alpha}{3}j}\|f_{R}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha/3}_{p;x}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim t^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}\|f_{R}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha/3}_{p;x}({\mathbb{B}})},

where the last step is due to Lemma
2.7
.
For (
3.13
), noting that by (
2.8
),

\|\delta^{(1)}_{h}(\Gamma_{t}f_{R})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}=\|\delta^{(1)}_{\Gamma_{t}h}f_{R}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim|\Gamma_{t}h|^{\alpha}_{\theta}\|f_{R}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})},

where for
h=(h_{1},h_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
,
\Gamma_{t}h=(h_{1}+th_{2},h_{2})
,
we have

\displaystyle\|\delta^{(1)}_{h}(\Gamma_{t}f_{R}-f_{R})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\delta^{(1)}_{h}\Gamma_{t}f_{R}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}+\|\delta^{(1)}_{h}f_{R}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(|\Gamma_{t}h|^{\alpha}_{\theta}+|h|^{\alpha}_{\theta})\|f_{R}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

Moreover, by (
3.12
) we also have

\|\delta^{(1)}_{h}(\Gamma_{t}f_{R}-f_{R})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\leqslant 2\|\Gamma_{t}f_{R}-f_{R}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim t^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}\|f_{R}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha/3}_{p;x}({\mathbb{B}})}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\delta^{(1)}_{h}(\Gamma_{t}f_{R}-f_{R})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}\wedge(|\Gamma_{t}h|^{\alpha}_{\theta}+|h|^{\alpha}_{\theta}))\|f_{R}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{T}t^{\frac{\alpha-\gamma}{3}}|h|^{\gamma}_{\theta}\|f_{R}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

Thus we obtain (
3.13
) by (
2.8
).

(ii) Note that by (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\|P_{t}f-f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\Gamma_{t}p_{t}*\Gamma_{t}f-\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}+\|\Gamma_{t}f-f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle=\|p_{t}*f-f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}+\|\Gamma_{t}f-f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})},

which in turn yields (
3.14
).
Moreover, for
\gamma<\alpha
, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{t}f-f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\geqslant 0}2^{\gamma j}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}(P_{t}f-f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\leqslant\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{\gamma j}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}(P_{t}f-f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}.

Since
\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim 2^{-\alpha j}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}
, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\|P_{t}f-f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{\gamma j}\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}(P_{t}f-f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{DX31}}}{{=}}0.

(iii) Note that by (
2.8
),

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{t}f(\cdot+\bar{x},\cdot+\bar{v})-\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle=\|f(\cdot+\bar{x}+t\bar{v},\cdot+\bar{v})-f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(|\bar{x}+t\bar{v}|^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}+|\bar{v}|^{\alpha})\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

(3.18)

Thus, by definition we have

\displaystyle\|P_{t}f-\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\Gamma_{t}p_{t}(\bar{x},\bar{v})\|\Gamma_{t}f(\cdot-\bar{x},\cdot-\bar{v})-\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\Gamma_{t}p_{t}(\bar{x},\bar{v})(|\bar{x}+t\bar{v}|^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}+|\bar{v}|^{\alpha})\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}p_{t}(\bar{x},\bar{v})(|\bar{x}|^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}+|\bar{v}|^{\alpha}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

For (
3.17
),
set
\hbar:=t^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{-j}
. Since
\|\Gamma_{t}p_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}=1
, we have for
j\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t}f-{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}=\|(\Gamma_{t}p_{t})*({\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f)-{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\Gamma_{t}p_{t}(\bar{x},\bar{v})\big\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f(\cdot-\bar{x},\cdot-\bar{v})-{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\big\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\Gamma_{t}p_{t}(\bar{x},\bar{v})\Big(|\bar{x}|\|\nabla_{x}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}+|\bar{v}|\|\nabla_{v}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\Big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim(t^{3/2}2^{3j}+t^{1/2}2^{j})\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}=(\hbar^{-3}+\hbar^{-1})\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}.

Moreover, we clearly have

\|({\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}p_{t})*(\Gamma_{t}f)-{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\leqslant 2\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}.

Thus

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t}f-{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim((\hbar^{-3}+\hbar^{-1})\wedge 1)\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}.

On the other hand, for
j\geqslant 1
, noting that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f(x,v)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(\bar{x},\bar{v})[\Gamma_{t}f(x-\bar{x},v-\bar{v})-\Gamma_{t}f(x,v)]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v},

by (
3.18
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(\bar{x},\bar{v})\|\Gamma_{t}f(\cdot-\bar{x},\cdot-\bar{v})-\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(\bar{x},\bar{v})(|\bar{x}+t\bar{v}|^{\alpha/3}+|\bar{v}|^{\alpha})\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}

\displaystyle\lesssim((t2^{-j})^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}+2^{-\alpha j})\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}=t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\hbar^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}+\hbar^{\alpha})\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}.

Therefore, for
j\geqslant 1
and
\gamma\in(0,\tfrac{\alpha}{3}]
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t}f-{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}((\hbar^{-3}+\hbar^{-1})\wedge 1)(\hbar^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}+\hbar^{\alpha})\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\hbar^{\gamma}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})}=2t^{\frac{\alpha-\gamma}{2}}2^{-\gamma j}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{B}})},

which together with

\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{0}P_{t}f-{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{0}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim((t^{\frac{3}{2}}+t^{\frac{1}{2}})\wedge 1)\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{0}\Gamma_{t}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}\lesssim t^{\frac{\alpha-\gamma}{2}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{B}})}

yields (
3.17
).
∎

3.2.
Simple model equations

We consider the following simple model equation:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u=[\nu\Delta_{v}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u+f]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+g^{k}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},\ \ u(0)=u_{0},

(3.19)

where
\nu>0
and
f\in\mathbf{D}^{1}
,
g\in\mathbf{D}^{2}(\ell^{2})
, and
u_{0}
is an
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}
-measurable
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
-valued random variable.

Definition 3.5
.

An
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
-valued predictable process
u
is called a distribution solution of
the above SKE if for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\langle u(t),\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{0},\varphi\rangle+\int^{t}_{0}\langle u(s),\nu\Delta_{v}\varphi-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\langle f(s),\varphi\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int^{t}_{0}\langle g^{k}(s),\varphi\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s},\ {\mathbb{P}}-a.s.

We have the following regularity estimate for this model equation.

Theorem 3.6
.

For any
p\geqslant 2
,
q\in[2,\infty]
,
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\kappa\in(0,1]
,
\alpha\leqslant\beta+2(1-\frac{\kappa}{q})
and
T>0
,
there is a constant
C=C(T,d,p,q,\kappa,\alpha,\beta,\nu)>0
such that
for any distribution solution
u
of SKE (
3.19
) and
t\in(0,T]
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|u(t)\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+2(1-\frac{\kappa}{q})}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim_{C}t^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}-1+\frac{\kappa}{q}}\|u_{0}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\\
&\qquad+\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}\Big[\|f(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+\|g(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q},\end{split}

(3.20)

and also,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|\!|\!|u(t)|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+2(1-\frac{\kappa}{q})}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim_{C}t^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}-1+\frac{\kappa}{q}}|\!|\!|u_{0}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\\
&\qquad+\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}\Big[|\!|\!|f(s)|\!|\!|^{q}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+|\!|\!|g(s)|\!|\!|^{q}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q}.\end{split}

(3.21)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
\nu=1
in equation (
3.19
).
Let
P_{t}
be defined by (
3.1
)
and
\bar{u}:=P_{t}u_{0}
. Since
\partial_{t}\bar{u}=\Delta_{v}\bar{u}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{u}
, one sees that

\tilde{u}=u-\bar{u}
solves

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{u}=[\Delta_{v}\tilde{u}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{u}+f]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+g^{k}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},\ \ \tilde{u}(0)=0.

Thus by (
3.7
), we may assume
u_{0}\equiv 0
.

(i) By Duhamel’s formula, we have in the distributional sense,

\displaystyle u(t)=\int^{t}_{0}P_{t-s}f(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int^{t}_{0}P_{t-s}g^{k}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}=:I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t).

For
I_{1}(t)
, by (
3.6
) with
(r,\alpha)=(1,\beta)
and
{\mathbb{B}}:={\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}I_{1}(t)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z,\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t-s}f(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(\beta+2(1-\frac{\kappa}{q}))j}\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}\|f(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q}.

For
I_{2}(t)
, by BDG’s inequality and Minkowskii’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}I_{2}(t)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z,\omega}}^{p}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left\|\int^{t}_{0}{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t-s}g^{k}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}\right\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t-s}g(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}(\ell^{2})}^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t-s}g(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{p/2}.

(3.22)

By (
3.6
) with
(r,\alpha)=(2,\beta+1)
and
{\mathbb{B}}:={\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2})
, we obtain

\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}I_{2}(t)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim 2^{-(\beta+2(1-\frac{\kappa}{q}))j}\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}\|g(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q}.

Combining the above calculations, we obtain (
3.20
).

(ii) Let
\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}
be as in (
3.9
) and define

u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}(t,\omega,z):=u(t,\omega,z)\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}(t,z).

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}=[\Delta_{v}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}+F^{\delta}_{z_{0}}]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+g^{k}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},

(3.23)

where

F^{\delta}_{z_{0}}:=f\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}-2\nabla_{v}u\cdot\nabla_{v}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}-((v-v_{0})\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}+\Delta_{v}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}})u.

Note that by (
2.14
),

\displaystyle\sup_{z_{0}}\|F^{\delta}_{z_{0}}(s)\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim|\!|\!|f(s)|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+|\!|\!|u(s)|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{1+\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}.

By (
3.20
), we have

\displaystyle|\!|\!|u(t)|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+2(1-\frac{\kappa}{q})}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}=\sup_{z_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\|u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}(t)\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+2(1-\frac{\kappa}{q})}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}\sup_{z_{0}}\Big[\|F^{\delta}_{z_{0}}(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+\|g\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}\Big[|\!|\!|u(s)|\!|\!|^{q}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{1+\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+|\!|\!|f(s)|\!|\!|^{q}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+|\!|\!|g(s)|\!|\!|^{q}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q}.

In particular, taking
q=2
,
\kappa=1
and then by Gronwall’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle|\!|\!|u(t)|\!|\!|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\Big[|\!|\!|f(s)|\!|\!|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+|\!|\!|g(s)|\!|\!|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Substituting this into the above inequality we obtain (
3.21
).
∎

The following stochastic convolution lemma provides the existence of a continuous version of the solution in time variable,
and also will be used to show the boundedness of
\|u(\omega,\cdot)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T,z}}
in the probability sense.

Lemma 3.7
.

(Stochastic convolutions)
Let
p\geqslant 2
,
T\geqslant 1
and
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
\alpha<\beta+1-\frac{2}{p}
.
For any
g\in{\mathbf{D}}(\ell^{2})\cap{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{T}({\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2})))
, there is a continuous version

\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}
of

[0,T]\ni t\mapsto{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t):=\int^{t}_{0}P_{t-s}g^{k}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}\in{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}
so that
for some
C=C(p,T,\alpha,\beta)>0
and any stopping time
\tau
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau]}\left\|\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t)\right\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}}\right)\lesssim_{C}\int^{T}_{0}\|g(s\wedge\tau)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

(3.24)

Proof.

We use the factorization method in
[
7
]
. Noting that

\int^{t}_{s}(t-r)^{\kappa-1}(r-s)^{-\kappa}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\kappa\pi)},\ \ 0\leqslant s<t<\infty,\ \kappa\in(0,1),

by stochastic Fubini’s theorem (cf.
[
16
]
), we have for each
t\in(0,T)
,

{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t)=\int^{t}_{0}P_{t-s}g^{k}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}=\frac{\sin(\kappa\pi)}{\pi}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}P_{t-s}G(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=:\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t),\ {\mathbb{P}}-a.s.

where

G(t):=\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\kappa}P_{t-s}g^{k}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}.

We show the above defined
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}
has the desired property.
Since
\alpha<\beta+1-\frac{2}{p}
, one can choose
\kappa,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
small enough so that

\displaystyle\alpha=\beta+1-\tfrac{2}{p}-\tfrac{2\kappa}{p}-\varepsilon.

(3.25)

By Minkowskii’s inequality and (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t)\right\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t-s}G(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\alpha j}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|G(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha-2(\kappa-\frac{1}{p})}_{p;\theta}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/p},

which implies that for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\left\|\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t)\right\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}}\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\|G(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha-2(\kappa-\frac{1}{p})}_{p;\theta}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\leqslant\int^{T}_{0}\|G(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha-2(\kappa-\frac{1}{p})}_{p;\theta}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

From this a priori estimate, as in
[
7
, Lemma 1]
, one sees that
t\mapsto\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t)
is continuous
as long as the last integral is finite a.s.. In particular,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t)\right\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}}\right)\lesssim\int^{T}_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\|G(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha-2(\kappa-\frac{1}{p})}_{p;\theta}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{CT1}}}{{\lesssim}}\int^{T}_{0}\|G(t)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha-2(\kappa-\frac{1}{p})+\varepsilon}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{FU2}}}{{=}}\int^{T}_{0}\|G(t)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+1-2\kappa-\frac{2\kappa}{p}}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t.

(3.26)

On the other hand, by BDG’s inequality and (
3.6
) again, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}G(t)\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-2\kappa}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t-s}g(s)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}(\ell^{2})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-2\kappa}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}P_{t-s}g(s)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(\beta+2(\frac{1-2\kappa}{2}-\frac{\kappa}{p}))pj}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}\|g(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Hence,

\|G(t)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+2(\frac{1-2\kappa}{2}-\frac{\kappa}{p})}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}\|g(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Substituting this into (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t)\right\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{T}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}\|g(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int^{T}_{0}(T-s)^{\kappa}\|g(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{T^{\kappa}}{\kappa}\int^{T}_{0}\|g(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Finally, for any stopping time
\tau
, since
\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t\wedge\tau)=\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g(\cdot\wedge\tau)}(t\wedge\tau)
a.s., we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau]}\left\|\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t)\right\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g(\cdot\wedge\tau)}(t)\right\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\alpha}_{p;\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int^{T}_{0}\|g(s\wedge\tau)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

The proof is complete.
∎

4.
SKEs with constant random coefficients

In this section we consider the following SKE with constant random coefficients:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u=[\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot\nabla^{2}_{v}u)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u+f]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+[\sigma^{k}\cdot\nabla_{v}u+g^{k}]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},\ u(0)=u_{0},

(4.1)

where
u_{0}:\Omega\to{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
is
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}
-measurable,
f\in{\bf D}^{1}
,
g\in{\bf D}^{2}(\ell^{2})
and

(a,\sigma):{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\Omega\to({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes\ell^{2})

Suppose that for some
c_{0},c_{1}>0
and all
(t,\omega)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\Omega
,

\displaystyle 2a(t,\omega)-\sigma^{\cdot k}\sigma^{\cdot k}(t,\omega)\geqslant c_{0}{\mathbb{I}}

\displaystyle\|a(t,\omega)\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}+\|\sigma(t,\omega)\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes\ell^{2}}\leqslant c_{1}.

Definition 4.1
.

We call a predictable process

a distribution solution of SKE (
4.1
)
if for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
and
t\geqslant 0

\displaystyle\langle u(t),\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{0},\varphi\rangle+\int^{t}_{0}[\langle u,\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot\nabla^{2}_{v}\varphi)\rangle-\langle u,v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\rangle+\langle f,\varphi\rangle]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}[\langle g^{k},\varphi\rangle-\langle u,\sigma^{k}\cdot\nabla_{v}\varphi\rangle]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}.

We use Krylov’s trick
[
15
]

to give a representation for the solution of SKE (
4.1
) in terms of the solution of the model equation (
3.19
).
Let

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}:=(2a-\sigma\sigma^{*}-c_{0}{\mathbb{I}})^{1/2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{f}:=f-\sigma^{k}\cdot\nabla_{v}g^{k}.

(4.5)

Let
(B^{k})_{k=1,\cdots,d}
be another Brownian motion that is independent of
W

Define

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{u}=\Big[\tfrac{c_{0}}{2}\Delta_{v}\bar{u}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{u}+\bar{f}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\bar{g}^{k}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},\ \bar{u}(0)=u_{0},

where

\bar{g}(t,x,v):=g\left(t,x+\int^{t}_{0}({\mathcal{I}}_{s}+\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,v-{\mathcal{I}}_{t}-\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{t}\right).

Let

By Itô-Wentzell’s formula (see Lemma
2.15
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}w

\displaystyle=\Big[\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{a}\cdot\nabla^{2}w)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}w+\tilde{f}+\sigma^{k}\cdot\nabla_{v}g^{k}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle\quad+(g^{k}+\sigma^{k}\cdot\nabla_{v}w){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t}+(\tilde{\sigma}^{k}\cdot\nabla_{v}w){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B^{k}_{t},

where

\tilde{a}:=\tfrac{1}{2}(\tilde{\sigma}\tilde{\sigma}^{*}+{\sigma}{\sigma}^{*})+c_{0}{\mathbb{I}}/2.

Now we define

u(t,x,v):={\mathbb{E}}(w(t,x,v)|{\mathscr{F}}^{W}_{t}),

where

is the
\sigma
-algebra generated by

and
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}

By (

) and (

Note that (cf.
[
26

{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\xi^{k}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}\Big|{\mathscr{F}}^{W}_{t}\right)=\int^{t}_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\xi^{k}(s)|{\mathscr{F}}^{W}_{s}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}

In particular, one sees that
u
solves SKE (
4.1
). Now by Theorem
3.6
and Fubini’s theorem, we have

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p\geqslant 2
,

,

&\qquad+\left(\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\kappa-1}\Big[\|f(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+\|g(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q}.\end{split}

Thus we obtain (
4.6
).
∎

), we obtain

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}(t)\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+2(1-\frac{1}{q})}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim_{C}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}(u_{0}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}(0,\cdot))\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+2(1-\frac{1}{q})}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\int^{t}_{0}\Big[\delta^{\beta}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+\|{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2^{-\gamma j}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|u^{2\delta}_{z_{0}}(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{2+\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\int^{t}_{0}\Big[\|{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}f^{\delta}_{z_{0}}(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+\|{\mathcal{R}}^{x}_{j}g^{\delta}_{z_{0}}(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q},

which further implies by definition and (
5.1
) that for any
q\in[2,\infty]
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|\!|\!|u(t)|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma,\beta+2(1-\frac{1}{q})}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim_{C}|\!|\!|u_{0}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma,\beta+2(1-\frac{1}{q})}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\\
&\quad+\left(\int^{t}_{0}\Big[\delta^{\beta}|\!|\!|u(s)|\!|\!|^{q}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma,2+\beta}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+C_{\delta}|\!|\!|u(s)|\!|\!|^{q}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma,1}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/q}+{\bf K}_{T},\end{split}

(5.16)

where

{\bf K}_{T}:=|\!|\!|f|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\gamma,\beta}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})))}+|\!|\!|g|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\gamma,1+\beta}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2})))}.

In particular, letting
q=\infty
and
\delta
be small enough, we get

\displaystyle|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{t}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\gamma,2+\beta}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}|\!|\!|u_{0}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma,\beta+2}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{t}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\gamma,1}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}+{\bf K}_{T}.

(5.17)

Substituting this into (
5.16
) and taking
q=2
, we have

\displaystyle|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{t}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\gamma,1+\beta}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}|\!|\!|u_{0}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma,\beta+2}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+\left(\int^{t}_{0}|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{s}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma,1+\beta}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/2}+{\bf K}_{T}.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we get

|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma,1+\beta}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim|\!|\!|u_{0}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma,\beta+2}_{p;x,\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+{\bf K}_{T}.

Substituting this into (
5.17
), we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

5.3.
Solvability of adjoint equation (

)

In this subsection we study the adjoint equation (
1.2
), and assume

(H
{}^{\beta}_{1}
)
′

We suppose that for some
\beta\in(0,1)
and
c_{2}>0
,

\|a\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T,\omega}({\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\theta})}+\|b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T,\omega,z}}+\|\sigma\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T,\omega}({\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\theta}(\ell^{2}))}\leqslant c_{2},\ T>0.

We also introduce the following notion about the solution of SKE (
1.2
).

Definition 5.4
.

Let
T>0
,
\beta\in(0,1)
and
p\in[2,\infty)
.
A predictable process
u\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))

\displaystyle\langle u(t),\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{0},\varphi\rangle+\int^{t}_{0}[\langle u,{\mathscr{L}}_{v}\varphi+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\rangle+\langle f,\varphi\rangle]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}[\langle u,{\mathscr{M}}^{k}_{v}\varphi\rangle+\langle g^{k},\varphi\rangle]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}.

We have the following result.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
T>0
,
p\in[2,\infty)
,
\beta\in(0,1)
and

u_{0}\in\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}),\ \ f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})),\ \ g\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta-1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))).

Under
(H
0
)
and
(H
{}^{\beta}_{1}
)
′
,
there is a unique weak solution
u
to SKE (
1.2
) so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}&\lesssim_{C}|\!|\!|u_{0}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+|\!|\!|f|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}+|\!|\!|g|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta-1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2})))},\end{split}

(5.18)

where the constant
C=C(d,p,q,\beta,T,c_{0},c_{1},c_{2})>0
.

Proof.

We only prove the a priori estimate (
5.18
).
We follow the proof of Theorem
5.2
and use the same notations therein.
It is the same reason as in the proof of Theorem
5.2
that we may assume
u_{0}\equiv 0
.
By Definition
5.4
, one sees that

u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}
is a weak solution of the following freezing SKE:

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}=\Big[\mathrm{tr}(a_{z_{0}}\cdot\nabla^{2}_{v}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}})-v\cdot\nabla_{x}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}+\tilde{f}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\Big[\sigma^{k}_{z_{0}}\cdot\nabla_{v}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}+\tilde{g}^{k}\Big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{f}

\displaystyle:=\partial^{2}_{v_{i}v_{j}}((a^{ij}-a^{ij}_{z_{0}})u^{\delta}_{z_{0}})-2\partial_{v_{i}}(a^{ij}u)\partial_{v_{j}}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}+\mathord{{\rm div}}_{v}(bu^{\delta}_{z_{0}})

\displaystyle\quad-\big[\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot\nabla^{2}_{v}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}})+(v-v_{0})\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}+b\cdot\nabla_{v}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}\big]u+f\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}},

and

\displaystyle\tilde{g}

\displaystyle:=\mathord{{\rm div}}_{v}((\sigma-\sigma_{z_{0}})u^{\delta}_{z_{0}})-\sigma\cdot\nabla_{v}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}u+g\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}.

For simplicity of notations, if we set

\bar{a}:=(a-a_{z_{0}})\chi^{2\delta}_{z_{0}},\ \ \bar{\sigma}:=(\sigma-\sigma_{z_{0}})\chi^{2\delta}_{z_{0}},\ \bar{h}:=\sigma\cdot\nabla_{v}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}},

and

\bar{c}:=-\big(\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot\nabla^{2}_{v}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}})+(v-v_{0})\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}+b\cdot\nabla_{v}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}\big),

then due to
\chi^{2\delta}_{z_{0}}=1
on the support of
\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}
, we can write

\displaystyle\tilde{f}=\partial^{2}_{v_{i}v_{j}}(\bar{a}^{ij}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}})-2\partial_{v_{i}}(a^{ij}u^{2\delta}_{z_{0}})\partial_{v_{j}}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}+\mathord{{\rm div}}_{v}(bu^{\delta}_{z_{0}})+\bar{c}\,u^{2\delta}_{z_{0}}+f\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}

and

\displaystyle\tilde{g}=\mathord{{\rm div}}_{v}(\bar{\sigma}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}})-\bar{h}\,u^{2\delta}_{z_{0}}+g\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}.

For any
p\in[2,\infty)
and
q\in(2,\infty]
, by Theorem
4.2
, there is a constant
C=C(T,d,p,q,\beta,c_{0},c_{1})>0

such that for all
\delta\in(0,1)
and
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-\frac{2}{q}}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}&\lesssim_{C}\|\tilde{f}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2})))}.\end{split}

(5.19)

We estimate each term of the right hand side.
By Bernstein’s inequality and (
2.13
),

\displaystyle\|\partial^{2}_{v_{i}v_{j}}(\bar{a}^{ij}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}})\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim\|\bar{a}u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim\delta^{\beta}\|u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+C_{\delta}\|u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})},

and by (
2.14
),

\displaystyle\|\partial_{v_{i}}(a^{ij}u^{2\delta}_{z_{0}})\partial_{v_{j}}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{v_{i}}(a^{ij}u^{2\delta}_{z_{0}})\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\|\partial_{v_{j}}\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbf{C}}^{2}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a^{ij}u^{2\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim\|u^{2\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})},

and

\|\mathord{{\rm div}}_{v}(bu^{\delta}_{z_{0}})\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim\|bu^{\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\leqslant\|b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{z,\omega}}\|u^{\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}.

Since
\bar{c}
is bounded, we also have

\|\bar{c}u^{2\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim\|\bar{c}u^{2\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim\|u^{2\delta}_{z_{0}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim\delta^{\beta}|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+C_{\delta}|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+|\!|\!|f|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}.

Similarly, one can show that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{g}\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}\lesssim\delta^{\beta}|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+C_{\delta}|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+|\!|\!|g|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}.

Substituting these two estimates into (
5.19
), and taking supremum in
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
, we obtain
that for any
q\in[2,\infty]
,

\displaystyle|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-\frac{2}{q}}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}\delta^{\beta}|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}+C_{\delta}|\!|\!|u|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}

\displaystyle\quad+|\!|\!|f|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}+|\!|\!|g|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2})))}.

The remaining proof is completely the same as in Theorem
5.2
.
∎

6.
Applications in nonlinear filtering problems

Fix
d,d_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
(\tilde{B}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
and
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be two independent
d
and
d_{1}
-dimensional Brownian motions
on some stochastic basis
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P};(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0})
.
Let

(\tilde{b},\tilde{\sigma},\sigma,\hat{b}):{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\to({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}},{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})

and

\hat{\sigma}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}

be bounded Borel measurable functions.
Consider the following SDE of Itô’s type:

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\!\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X_{t}\\
V_{t}\\
Y_{t}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}V_{t},\\
\tilde{b}(t,U_{t})\\
\hat{b}(t,U_{t})\end{array}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0,&0,&0\\
0,&\tilde{\sigma}(t,U_{t}),&\sigma(t,U_{t})\\
0,&0,&\hat{\sigma}(t,Y_{t})\end{array}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\!\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\tilde{B}_{t}\\
B_{t}\end{array}\right),

(6.1)

where

U_{t}:=(X_{t},V_{t},Y_{t})=:(Z_{t},Y_{t})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}},

Z_{t}
stands for the
unobservable
signal and
Y_{t}
denotes the
observable
signal,

U_{0}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable random variable.
Let
{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}
be the
{\mathbb{P}}
-completed
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\{Y_{s},s\leqslant t\}
, which represents the observation information.
In application, we want to predict
Z_{t}
in terms of
{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}
. More precisely, we want to calculate the conditional
distribution of
Z_{t}
under
{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}
:

\Pi_{t}(\omega,A):={\mathbb{P}}(Z_{t}\in A|{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}).

This is usually called the filtering problem (cf.
[
26
]
).

Throughout this section we suppose that

(\bf{A}_{1})

\Pi_{0}(\omega,{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z)=\pi_{0}(\omega,z){\mathord{{\rm d}}}z
with

\pi_{0}\in\cap_{p\geqslant 2}\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
.

(\bf{A}_{2})

\tilde{b},\tilde{\sigma},\sigma,\hat{b},\hat{\sigma}
are bounded and Lipschitz in
(x,v,y)
and uniformly in
t
.

(\bf{A}_{3})

\tilde{\sigma}
and
\hat{\sigma}
are non-singular, and for some
K>0
and all
t,x,v,y
,

\|\tilde{\sigma}^{-1}(t,x,v,y)\|_{{\mathbb{M}}^{d}_{sym}}+\|\hat{\sigma}^{-1}(t,y)\|_{{\mathbb{M}}^{d_{1}}_{sym}}\leqslant K.

Under the above assumptions, it is well-known that SDE (
6.1
) admits a unique strong solution.
Let

\displaystyle\tilde{h}(t,z,y)

\displaystyle:=(\hat{\sigma}^{-1}\hat{b})(t,z,y),\ \bar{b}(t,z,y):=(\tilde{b}-\sigma\tilde{h})(t,z,y)

and

W_{t}=B_{t}+\int^{t}_{0}\tilde{h}(s,U_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Then we can write SDE (
6.1
) as

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X_{t}\\
V_{t}\\
Y_{t}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}V_{t},\\
\bar{b}(t,U_{t})\\
0\end{array}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0,&0,&0\\
0,&\tilde{\sigma}(t,U_{t}),&\sigma(t,U_{t})\\
0,&0,&\hat{\sigma}(t,Y_{t})\end{array}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\!\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\tilde{B}_{t}\\
W_{t}\end{array}\right).

Define

\displaystyle\rho_{t}:=\exp\left\{\int^{t}_{0}\tilde{h}(s,U_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\|\tilde{h}(s,U_{s})\|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\}.

(6.2)

Fix
T>0
. Since
\tilde{h}
is bounded, by Itô’s formula, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(\rho^{-1}_{T}|{\mathscr{F}}_{t})=\rho^{-1}_{t},\ \ t\in[0,T].

(6.3)

Thus, by Girsanov’s theorem, under the new probability measure

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\omega):=\rho^{-1}_{T}(\omega)\mathbb{P}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\omega),

(6.4)

(W_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is still a
d_{1}
-dimensional Wiener process and independent of
\tilde{B}
.
Moreover, since
\hat{\sigma}
is invertible, it is easy to see that (see
[
26
, Lemma 6.2]
)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}={\mathscr{F}}^{W}_{t}\vee{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{0}.

(6.5)

This is the crucial point for deriving the density equation of
\Pi_{t}(\omega,\cdot)
. In other words, by Girsanov’s technique, the observation
information is transformed into Brownian filtration information.

Now we let

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Sigma(t,\omega,z)&:=\sigma\left(t,z,Y_{t}(\omega)\right),\ \ \tilde{\Sigma}(t,\omega,z):=\tilde{\sigma}\left(t,z,Y_{t}(\omega)\right),\\
b(t,\omega,z)&:=\bar{b}\left(t,z,Y_{t}(\omega)\right),\ \ h(t,\omega,z):=(\hat{\sigma}^{-1}\hat{b})(t,z,Y_{t}(\omega)),\end{split}

(6.6)

and

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{v}\varphi:=\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot\nabla^{2}_{v}\varphi)+b\cdot\nabla_{v}\varphi,\ \ {\mathscr{M}}_{v}\varphi:=\Sigma\cdot\nabla_{v}\varphi+h\varphi,

(6.7)

where
a:=\frac{1}{2}(\Sigma\Sigma^{*}+\tilde{\Sigma}\tilde{\Sigma}^{*})
.
Consider the following linear SKE:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u=[{\mathscr{L}}^{*}_{v}u-v\cdot\nabla_{x}u]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathscr{M}}^{*}_{v}u{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\ \ u(0)=\pi_{0},

(6.8)

where
{\mathscr{L}}^{*}_{v}
and
{\mathscr{M}}^{*}_{v}
stand for the adjoint operators of
{\mathscr{L}}_{v}
and
{\mathscr{M}}_{v}
, respectively.
Under
{\bf(A_{1})}
-
{\bf(A_{3})}
, by Theorem
5.5
, there exists a unique generalized solution

\displaystyle u\in\cap_{p\geqslant 2}{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})),\quad T>0,

(6.9)

to the above SKE. We first show the following result.

Lemma 6.1
.

For each
t\geqslant 0
,
R>0
and
\beta^{\prime}<\beta
, it holds that for
{\mathbb{P}}
-almost all
\omega
,

\displaystyle\chi^{R}_{0}(\cdot)u(t,\omega,\cdot)\in\cap_{p\geqslant 2}{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta^{\prime}}_{p;\theta},

(6.10)

where
\chi^{R}_{0}
is the cutoff function in (
2.17
), and for any bounded measurable
\varphi
,

\displaystyle\langle u(t,\omega),\varphi\rangle=\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}}\left(\varphi(Z_{t})\rho_{T}|{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}\right)(\omega),

(6.11)

where
\rho_{T}
is defined by (
6.2
).

Proof.

First of all, for each
R,t>0
, by (
6.9
) and (
2.3
), we have

\chi^{R}_{0}u(t)\in\cap_{p\geqslant 2}{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})\subset\cap_{p\geqslant 2}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}({{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta^{\prime}}_{p;\theta}),

which in turn gives (
6.10
).
Let

(b_{\varepsilon},\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon},\Sigma_{\varepsilon},h_{\varepsilon})=(b,\tilde{\Sigma},\Sigma,h)*\varrho_{\varepsilon},

where
(\varrho_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
is a family of mollifiers in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
.
Let
({\mathscr{L}}^{\varepsilon}_{v},{\mathscr{M}}^{\varepsilon}_{v})
be defined as in (
6.7
) in terms of the above mollifying coefficients.
Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the unique solution of the following SKE:

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u_{\varepsilon}=[({\mathscr{L}}^{\varepsilon}_{v})^{*}u_{\varepsilon}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}u_{\varepsilon}]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+({\mathscr{M}}^{\varepsilon}_{v})^{*}u_{\varepsilon}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\ \ u_{\varepsilon}(0)=\pi_{0}.

By (
5.18
), there is a constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle|\!|\!|u_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}\lesssim_{C}|\!|\!|\pi_{0}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}.

(6.12)

Let

U_{\varepsilon}:=u_{\varepsilon}-u,\ F_{\varepsilon}:=({\mathscr{L}}^{\varepsilon}_{v}-{\mathscr{L}}_{v})^{*}u_{\varepsilon},\ G_{\varepsilon}:=({\mathscr{M}}^{\varepsilon}_{v}-{\mathscr{M}}_{v})^{*}u_{\varepsilon}.

Then one sees that

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}U_{\varepsilon}=[({\mathscr{L}}_{v})^{*}U_{\varepsilon}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}U_{\varepsilon}+F_{\varepsilon}]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+[({\mathscr{M}}_{v})^{*}U_{\varepsilon}+G_{\varepsilon}]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\ \ U_{\varepsilon}(0)=0.

By (
5.18
), we have

\displaystyle|\!|\!|U_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}\lesssim|\!|\!|F_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}+|\!|\!|G_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta-1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2})))}.

(6.13)

Here and below, the implicit constant in
\lesssim
is independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
Noting that

F_{\varepsilon}=\partial^{2}_{v_{i}v_{j}}((a^{ij}_{\varepsilon}-a^{ij})u_{\varepsilon})+\partial_{v_{i}}((b^{i}_{\varepsilon}-b^{i})u_{\varepsilon}),

by Bernstein’s inequality (
2.4
), (
2.13
) and (
6.12
), we have

\displaystyle|\!|\!|F_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta-2}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\!|\!|(a_{\varepsilon}-a)u_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+|\!|\!|(b_{\varepsilon}-b)u_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta-1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a_{\varepsilon}-a\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{t,\omega}({\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}_{\theta})}|\!|\!|u_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+\|b_{\varepsilon}-b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{t,\omega,z}}|\!|\!|u_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\varepsilon^{1-\beta}+\varepsilon)|\!|\!|u_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1-\beta},

where in the third inequality we have used that for
|z-z^{\prime}|\leqslant 1
,

\displaystyle|(a_{\varepsilon}-a)(t,\omega,z)-(a_{\varepsilon}-a)(t,\omega,z^{\prime})|\lesssim\|a_{\varepsilon}-a\|_{\infty}^{1-\beta}\|a\|^{\beta}_{\rm Lip}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\beta}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1-\beta}\ |z-z^{\prime}|^{\beta}_{\theta}.

Similarly we have

\displaystyle|\!|\!|G_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta-1}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\!|\!|(\Sigma_{\varepsilon}-\Sigma)u_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+|\!|\!|(h_{\varepsilon}-h)u_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1-\beta}.

Thus, by (
6.13
),

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}|\!|\!|U_{\varepsilon}|\!|\!|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))}=0.

(6.14)

Let
Z^{\varepsilon}:=(X^{\varepsilon},V^{\varepsilon})
solve the following SDE

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{\varepsilon}_{t}=V^{\varepsilon}_{t}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,\ \ {\mathord{{\rm d}}}V^{\varepsilon}_{t}=b_{\varepsilon}(t,Z^{\varepsilon}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}(t,Z^{\varepsilon}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{B}_{t}+\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(t,Z^{\varepsilon}_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B_{t},\ Z^{\varepsilon}_{0}=Z_{0}.

Since
b_{\varepsilon},\Sigma_{\varepsilon},\tilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}
are bounded and Lipschitz continuous in
z
uniformly in
(t,\omega,\varepsilon)
,
it is by now standard to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathbb{E}}|Z^{\varepsilon}_{t}-Z_{t}|^{2}=0.

(6.15)

Now by
[
26
, p.201, Theorem 5.6]
, we have
for any
\varphi\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
,

\displaystyle\langle u_{\varepsilon}(t,\omega),\varphi\rangle=\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}}\left(\varphi(Z^{\varepsilon}_{t})\rho^{\varepsilon}_{t}|{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}\right)(\omega),\quad{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.

(6.16)

where

\rho^{\varepsilon}_{t}:=\exp\left\{\int^{t}_{0}h_{\varepsilon}(s,Z^{\varepsilon}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\|h_{\varepsilon}(s,Z^{\varepsilon}_{s})\|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\}.

By (
6.14
) and (
6.15
) and taking limits for (
6.16
), we get

\displaystyle\langle u(t,\omega),\varphi\rangle=\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}}\left(\varphi(Z_{t})\rho_{t}|{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}\right)(\omega),\quad{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.

(6.17)

On the other hand, for any
A\in{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}
, by (
6.4
) and (
6.3
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}}\left({\mathbf{1}}_{A}\varphi(Z_{t})\rho_{t}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left({\mathbf{1}}_{A}\varphi(Z_{t})\rho_{t}\rho^{-1}_{T}\right)={\mathbb{E}}\left({\mathbf{1}}_{A}\varphi(Z_{t}){\mathbb{E}}(\rho_{t}\rho^{-1}_{T}|{\mathscr{F}}_{t})\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left({\mathbf{1}}_{A}\varphi(Z_{t})\right)={\mathbb{E}}\left({\mathbf{1}}_{A}\varphi(Z_{t})\rho_{T}\rho^{-1}_{T}\right)={\mathbb{E}}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}}\left({\mathbf{1}}_{A}\varphi(Z_{t})\rho_{T}\right),

which means that

\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}}\left(\varphi(Z_{t})\rho_{t}|{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}\right)=\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}}\left(\varphi(Z_{t})\rho_{T}|{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}\right).

Substituting this into (
6.17
), we obtain (
6.11
).
∎

We now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 6.2
.

Under
{\bf(A_{1})}
-
{\bf(A_{3})}
, for each
t>0
, the conditional distribution
\Pi_{t}(\omega,\cdot)
has a continuous density

\pi_{t}(\omega,\cdot)
for
{\mathbb{P}}
-almost all
\omega
, and

\displaystyle\pi_{t}(\omega,\cdot)=u(t,\omega,\cdot)/\|u(t,\omega,\cdot)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}},

(6.18)

where
u
is given in (
6.9
).
Moreover,
\pi_{t}
solves the following nonlinear SKE:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\pi_{t}(\varphi)=\pi_{t}({\mathscr{L}}_{v}\varphi+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+(\pi_{t}({\mathscr{M}}_{v}\varphi)-\Pi_{t}(h_{t})(\pi_{t}(\varphi))){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{W}_{t},

(6.19)

for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
, where
\bar{W}_{t}:=W_{t}+\int^{t}_{0}\Pi_{s}(h_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s
.

Proof.

Let
T>0
. Note that by (
6.2
),

\displaystyle\rho_{t}=\exp\left\{\int^{t}_{0}\tilde{h}(s,U_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\|\tilde{h}(s,U_{s})\|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\}

satisfies

\displaystyle\rho_{t}=1+\int^{t}_{0}\tilde{h}(s,U_{s})\rho_{s}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}.

Taking the conditional expectation with respect to
{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}
, by
[
26
, p.28, Theorem 1.15]
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}}(\rho_{t}|{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t})

\displaystyle=1+{\mathbb{E}}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\tilde{h}(s,U_{s})\rho_{s}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}|{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\int^{t}_{0}{\mathbb{E}}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}}\left(\tilde{h}(s,U_{s})\rho_{s}|{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{s}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}

\displaystyle=1+\int^{t}_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\tilde{h}(s,U_{s})|{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{s}\right){\mathbb{E}}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}}\left(\rho_{s}|{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{s}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s},

which together with
\Pi_{t}(h_{t})=\mathbb{E}\left(h_{t}(Z_{t})\mid{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t}\right)={\mathbb{E}}(\tilde{h}(t,U_{t})|{\mathscr{F}}^{Y}_{t})
yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{T}}\left(\rho_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\int^{t}_{0}\Pi_{s}(h_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\|\Pi_{s}(h_{s})\|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\}.

(6.20)

Now, by Bayes’ formula about the conditional expectations (cf.
[
26
, p.218, Lemma 6.1]
) and (
6.11
), we have

\displaystyle\Pi_{t}(\varphi)=\mathbb{E}\left(\varphi(Z_{t})\mid\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}\right)=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}\left(\varphi(Z_{t})\rho_{T}\mid\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}\right)}{\mathbb{E}^{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}\left(\rho_{T}\mid\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}\right)}=\frac{\langle u(t),\varphi\rangle}{\langle u(t),1\rangle}=\left\langle\frac{u(t)}{\langle u(t),1\rangle},\varphi\right\rangle.

By the arbitrariness of
\varphi
, we get (
6.18
). The continuity of
\pi_{t}(\omega,\cdot)
follows by (
6.10
) and the
Sobolev embedding.

Moreover, by (
6.16
) and (
6.20
),

\langle u(t),1\rangle=\exp\left\{\int^{t}_{0}\Pi_{s}(h_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\|\Pi_{s}(h_{s})\|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\}

and

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\langle u(t),\varphi\rangle=\langle u(t),{\mathscr{L}}_{t}\varphi\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\langle u(t),{\mathscr{M}}_{t}\varphi\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}.

Thus, by Itô’s formula,

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\pi_{t}(\varphi)=

\displaystyle\left[\pi_{t}(\mathscr{L}_{t}\varphi)+\left(\pi_{t}(\mathscr{M}_{t}\varphi)-\pi_{t}(\varphi)\right)\|\Pi_{t}(h_{t})\|^{2}\right]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle+\left(\pi_{t}(\mathscr{M}_{t}\varphi)-\pi_{t}(\varphi)\Pi_{t}(h_{t})\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}{W}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{t}(\mathscr{L}_{t}\varphi){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\left(\pi_{t}(\mathscr{M}_{t}\varphi)-\pi_{t}(\varphi)\Pi_{t}(h_{t})\right)({\mathord{{\rm d}}}{W}_{t}+\Pi_{t}(h_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t).

From these, we obtain (
6.21
) and complete the proof.
∎

7.
SKEs driven by velocity-time white noises

Let
\{B(t,v),(t,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}\}
be a Brownian sheet of time and velocity variables,
whose distribution derivative in
t,v
is usually considered as velocity-time white noise. Let

(f,g)(t,\omega,x,v,u):{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})

be
{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
-measurable functions. We consider the following nonlinear SKE driven by
B
,

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u=\big[\Delta_{v}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u+f(u)\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+g(u){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B(t,v),\ \ u(0)=u_{0},

(7.1)

where the stochastic integral is understood in the sense of Walsh
[
29
]
.
Here we have suppressed the variables
(t,\omega,x,v)
of
f,g
. We introduce the following notion about the solution to the above SKE.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
p\geqslant 2
and
T>0
. We call a predictable process
u\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))
being
a weak solution of SKE (
7.1
) if for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle u(t),\varphi\rangle&=\langle u_{0},\varphi\rangle+\int^{t}_{0}\big[\langle u,\Delta_{v}\varphi-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\rangle+\langle f(u),\varphi\rangle\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\\
&\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(x,v)g(s,x,v,u(s,x,v)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B(s,v)\ \ a.s.\end{split}

(7.2)

In order to use our previous results to study SKE (
7.1
),
we shall use the following representation of
B(t,v)
(cf.
[
15
, Section 8.2]
),

\displaystyle B(t,v)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\int^{v}_{0}\eta_{k}(r){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right)W^{k}_{t},

(7.3)

where
\{\eta_{k},k\in{\mathbb{N}}\}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
is an orthogonormal basis of
{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{v}({\mathbb{R}})
.
In particular, for any measurable adapted processes
\xi(s,v)
with
\int^{T}_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\|\xi(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{v}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s<\infty
,

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\xi(s,v){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B(s,v)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int^{T}_{0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi(s,v)\eta_{k}(v){\mathord{{\rm d}}}v\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}.

(7.4)

For
N=1,\cdots,\infty
and a function
h
, we introduce the following notations:

{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(h):=(h\eta_{1},\cdots,h\eta_{N},0,\cdots),\ \ {\mathbb{G}}(h):={\mathbb{G}}_{\infty}(h).

By this notation and (
7.4
), we can write SKE (
7.1
) as

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u=\big[\Delta_{v}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u+f(u)\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathbb{G}}^{k}(g(u)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},\ \ u(0)=u_{0}.

(7.5)

We have the following important lemma.

Lemma 7.2
.

For any
p\geqslant 2
and
r\in[1,p]
, there is a constant
C=C(p,r)>0
such that
for all
N=1,\cdots,\infty
and
h\in{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}({\mathbb{L}}^{r}_{z})
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(h)\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}\leqslant C\|h\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}({\mathbb{L}}^{r}_{z})},

(7.6)

where
\beta:=4(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r})-\frac{1}{2}
. Moreover, for any
\beta^{\prime}<\beta
, we also have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\|{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(h)-{\mathbb{G}}(h)\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta^{\prime}}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}=0.

(7.7)

Proof.

Let
h\in{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}({\mathbb{L}}^{r}_{z})
.
Recall (
2.2
). By Parsaval’s identity, we have for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(h)(x,v)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(x-\bar{x},v-\bar{v})h(\bar{x},\bar{v})\eta_{k}(\bar{v}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(x-\bar{x},v-\bar{v})h(\bar{x},\bar{v}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}\right)\eta_{k}(\bar{v}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(x-\bar{x},v-\bar{v})h(\bar{x},\bar{v}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}\right|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(\bar{x},\bar{v})h(x-\bar{x},v-\bar{v}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}\right|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}.

Let
q,s\in[1,\infty]
be defined by

\tfrac{1}{q}+\tfrac{1}{r}=\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{p},\ \ \tfrac{1}{s}+\tfrac{1}{r}=1+\tfrac{1}{p}.

Since
p\geqslant 2
, by Minkowskii’s inequality and Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(h)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}(\ell^{2})}^{p}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}(h\eta_{\cdot})(\cdot,v)\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}(\ell^{2})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}v

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(\bar{x},\bar{v})h(\cdot-\bar{x},v-\bar{v}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{x}\right\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}\right)^{p/2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}v

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\|\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}(\cdot,\bar{v})\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{s}_{x}}\|h(\cdot,v-\bar{v})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{r}_{x}}^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\bar{v}\right)^{p/2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}v

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{v}({\mathbb{L}}^{s}_{x})}^{p}\|h\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{r}_{v}({\mathbb{L}}^{r}_{x})}\lesssim 2^{p(4-\frac{3}{s}-\frac{1}{q})j}\|h\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{r}_{z}}=2^{-p\beta j}\|h\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{r}_{z}},

(7.8)

where the last inequality is due to the scaling property of
\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{j}
and
\check{\phi}^{\theta}_{1}\in{\mathcal{S}}
. Thus,
by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\|{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(h)\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\geqslant 0}2^{\beta j}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(h)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\geqslant 0}2^{\beta j}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(h)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}(\ell^{2}))}\lesssim\|h\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}({\mathbb{L}}^{r}_{z})}.

Moreover, for
\beta^{\prime}<\beta
, by the dominated convergence theorem we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\|{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(h)-{\mathbb{G}}(h)\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta^{\prime}}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{j\geqslant 0}2^{\beta^{\prime}j}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}({\mathbb{G}}_{N}(h)-{\mathbb{G}}(h))\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{\beta^{\prime}j}\lim_{N\to\infty}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}({\mathbb{G}}_{N}(h)-{\mathbb{G}}(h))\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}(\ell^{2}))},

which converges to zero since for each
x,v
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\theta}_{j}({\mathbb{G}}_{N}(h)-{\mathbb{G}}(h))(x,v)\|_{\ell^{2}}=0.

The proof is complete.
∎

7.1.

{}^{s}_{f,g}

Lemma 7.4

{}^{s}_{f,g}

\displaystyle|\!|\!|f(u)-f(u^{\prime})|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+|\!|\!|{\mathbb{G}}(g(u)-g(u^{\prime}))|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}\lesssim_{C_{0}}|\!|\!|u-u^{\prime}|\!|\!|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})},

\displaystyle\|f(u)-f(u^{\prime})\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}+\|{\mathbb{G}}(g(u)-g(u^{\prime}))\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}\lesssim_{C_{0}}\|u-u^{\prime}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega})}.

Proof.

We only prove the first one.
Let
r\in[1,p]
be defined by
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{s}
. Fix
\delta>0

z_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Let
\chi^{\delta}_{z_{0}}

Example 7.7
.

Here we provide an example for (

Suppose that

Proof.

Define

f_{\varepsilon}:=f*\rho_{\varepsilon}
. Then

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u_{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{v}u_{\varepsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u_{\varepsilon}+[\rho_{\varepsilon}*,v\cdot\nabla_{x}]u+f_{\varepsilon}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathbb{G}}^{k}_{N}(\xi u)*\rho_{\varepsilon}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},

where

[\rho_{\varepsilon}*,v\cdot\nabla_{x}]u:=\rho_{\varepsilon}*(v\cdot\nabla_{x}u)-v\cdot\nabla_{x}(u*\rho_{\varepsilon}).

Let
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,\infty)
be a smooth convex smooth function with bounded derivative of all orders greater than
1
.
By Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\psi(u_{\varepsilon}(t))

\displaystyle=\psi(u_{\varepsilon}(0))+\int^{t}_{0}\psi^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})(\Delta_{v}u_{\varepsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u_{\varepsilon}+[\rho_{\varepsilon}*,v\cdot\nabla_{x}]u+f_{\varepsilon}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\psi^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\|{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(\xi u)*\rho_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int^{t}_{0}\psi^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})({\mathbb{G}}^{k}_{N}(\xi u)*\rho_{\varepsilon}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}.

Let
\chi
be a nonnegative smooth function with compact support. Multiplying both sides by
\chi
and then
integrating on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and noting that

\displaystyle\int\psi^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})(\Delta_{v}u_{\varepsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u_{\varepsilon})\chi

\displaystyle=-\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})|\nabla_{v}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\chi+\int(\Delta_{v}\chi-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi)\psi(u_{\varepsilon}),

we obtain that for any bounded stopping time
\tau
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int\psi(u_{\varepsilon}(\tau))\chi

\displaystyle\leqslant\int\psi(u_{\varepsilon}(0))\chi+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\!\!\int(\Delta_{v}\chi-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi)\psi(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\quad+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\!\!\int\psi^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})([\rho_{\varepsilon}*,v\cdot\nabla_{x}]u+f_{\varepsilon})\chi

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\!\!\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\|{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(\xi u)*\rho_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}\chi.

Here and below we drop the integral variables
{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}v{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s
for simplicity.
Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
and by Fatou’s lemma and the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\int\psi(u(\tau))\chi&={\mathbb{E}}\int\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\psi(u_{\varepsilon}(\tau))\chi\leqslant\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathbb{E}}\int\psi(u_{\varepsilon}(\tau))\chi\\
&\leqslant\int\psi(u(0))\chi+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\!\!\int(\Delta_{v}\chi-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi)\psi(u)\\
&+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\!\!\int\psi^{\prime}(u)f\chi+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\!\!\int\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)\|{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(\xi u)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}\chi.\end{split}

(7.26)

Now we take

\psi(r)=\psi_{\delta}(r):=(r+\sqrt{r^{2}+\delta})/2,\ \ \delta>0.

Clearly,

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\psi_{\delta}(r)=r^{+},\ \ \psi^{\prime\prime}_{\delta}(r)\geqslant 0,\ \ r^{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}_{\delta}(r)\leqslant\sqrt{\delta},\ \ |\psi^{\prime}_{\delta}(r)|\leqslant 1.

Thus by (
7.26
),

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int\psi_{\delta}(u(\tau))\chi

\displaystyle\leqslant\int\psi_{\delta}(u(0))\chi+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\!\!\int(\Delta_{v}\chi-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi)\psi_{\delta}(u)

\displaystyle\quad+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int f^{+}\chi+\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\!\!\int\|{\mathbb{G}}_{N}(\xi)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}\chi.

Letting
\delta\downarrow 0
, we get for any bounded stopping time
\tau
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int u^{+}(\tau)\chi\leqslant\int u^{+}(0)\chi+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\!\!\int u^{+}(\Delta_{v}-v\cdot\nabla_{x})\chi+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int f^{+}\chi.

(7.27)

Now, let
\chi:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to[0,1]
be a nonnegative smooth function with

\chi(x,v)=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1,&\ \ |x|^{1/3}+|v|\leqslant 1,\\
0,&\ \ |x|^{1/3}+|v|\geqslant 2.\end{aligned}\right.

For
R\geqslant 1
, let

\chi_{R}(x,v):=\chi(R^{-3}x,R^{-1}v).

By the chain rule, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\Delta_{v}\chi_{R}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi_{R})(x,v)&=R^{-2}(\Delta_{v}\chi-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi)(R^{-3}x,R^{-1}v)\\
&\leqslant\|\Delta_{v}\chi-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\infty}R^{-2}\chi_{2R}(x,v).\end{split}

(7.28)

Thus, for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, by (
7.27
) with
\chi_{2^{n}R}
in place of
\chi
, we get for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int u^{+}(t)\chi_{2^{n}R}\leqslant\int u^{+}_{0}+C_{0}(2^{n}R)^{-2}{\mathbb{E}}\int^{t}_{0}\int u^{+}\chi_{2^{n+1}R}+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{T}_{0}\int f^{+},

where
C_{0}:=\|\Delta_{v}\chi-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\infty}
.
Let
H_{n}(t):={\mathbb{E}}\int u^{+}(t)\chi_{2^{n}R}
. The above inequality means that

H_{n}(t)\leqslant\int u^{+}_{0}+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{T}_{0}\int f^{+}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+C_{0}(2^{n}R)^{-2}\int^{t}_{0}H_{n+1}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

By iteration, we get for any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle H_{0}(t)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int u^{+}_{0}+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{T}_{0}\int f^{+}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\left(1+\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{(C_{0}R^{-2}t)^{n}}{2^{n(n-1)}n!}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(C_{0}R^{-2})^{N}}{2^{N(N-1)}}\int^{t}_{0}\!\!\int^{s_{1}}_{0}\!\!\!\cdots\!\!\int^{s_{N-1}}_{0}H_{N}(s_{N}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{N}\cdots{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{1}.

Since by Hölder’s inequality and the assumption, for
q=\frac{p}{p-1}\in(1,2]
,

H_{N}(t)\leqslant{\mathbb{E}}\|u(t)\|_{p}\|\chi_{2^{N}R}\|_{q}\leqslant C_{1}(2^{N}R)^{\frac{4}{q}},

where
C_{1}
does not depend on
N,R,t
, we have

\frac{(C_{0}R^{-2})^{N}}{2^{N(N-1)}}\int^{t}_{0}\!\!\int^{s_{1}}_{0}\!\!\!\cdots\!\!\int^{s_{N-1}}_{0}H_{N}(s_{N}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{N}\cdots{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{1}\leqslant\frac{C_{1}(2^{N}R)^{\frac{4}{q}}(C_{0}R^{-2}t)^{N}}{2^{N(N-1)}N!}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
. Hence,

{\mathbb{E}}\int u^{+}(t)\chi_{R}=H_{0}(t)\leqslant\left(\int u^{+}_{0}+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{T}_{0}\int f^{+}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\mathrm{e}^{C_{0}R^{-2}t}.

Letting
R\to\infty
, we obtain that for any
t\in[0,T]
,

{\mathbb{E}}\int u^{+}(t)\leqslant\int u^{+}_{0}+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{T}_{0}\int f^{+}.

Finally, by this estimate and (
7.27
), (
7.28
) again,

{\mathbb{E}}\int u^{+}(\tau)\chi_{R}\leqslant\int u^{+}(0)\chi_{R}+\frac{\|\Delta_{v}\chi-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\infty}}{R^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\!\!\int u^{+}+{\mathbb{E}}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int f^{+}\chi_{R},

which implies the desired result by taking limits
R\to\infty
.
∎

Remark 7.10
.

Suppose
f^{-}\equiv 0
. If
u(0)\geqslant 0
, then
u(t)\geqslant 0
a.s. by (
7.24
). This in turn implies the nonnegativity of the solution to SKE (
7.22
) provided
u_{0}\geqslant 0
.

Remark 7.11
.

Estimate (
7.24
) is also used to derive the local uniqueness of SKE (
7.1
) under
(H
{}^{\infty}_{f,g}
)
.
More precisely, let
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
be two solutions of SKE (
7.1
) before stopping time
\tau\leqslant T

with the same initial values, i.e., for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

\displaystyle\langle u_{i}(t),\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{0},\varphi\rangle+\int^{t}_{0}\big[\langle u_{i},\Delta_{v}\varphi-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\rangle+\langle f(u_{i}),\varphi\rangle\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\langle{\mathbb{G}}^{k}(g(u_{i})),\varphi\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s},\ \ t\leqslant\tau,\ \ a.s.,\ \ i=1,2.

Then it holds that

u_{1}(t)=u_{2}(t),\ \ \forall t\leqslant\tau,\ \ a.s.

Indeed, let
U:=u_{1}-u_{2}
. Then
U
solves the following SKE: for
t\leqslant\tau
,

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}U=[\Delta_{v}U+v\cdot\nabla_{x}U+\xi_{0}U]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathbb{G}}^{k}(\xi_{1}U){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},\ U(0)=0,

where
\xi_{0}:=(f(u_{1})-f(u_{2}))/(u_{1}-u_{2})
and
\xi_{1}:=(g(u_{1})-g(u_{2}))/(u_{1}-u_{2})
. Here we use the convention
\frac{0}{0}:=0.

Under
(H
{}^{\infty}_{f,g}
)
,
\xi_{0}
and
\xi_{1}
are bounded predictable. Using (
7.24
), we have

{\mathbb{E}}\|U(t\wedge\tau)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}\leqslant{\mathbb{E}}\int^{t\wedge\tau}_{0}\|\xi_{0}U(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\leqslant\|\xi_{0}\|_{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\int^{t}_{0}\|U(s\wedge\tau)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

By Gronwall’s inequality,
U(t\wedge\tau)=0
for any
t\in[0,T]
. We would like to point out that the local uniqueness seems not be an obvious consequence of
the global uniqueness since for
u^{\tau}(t):=u(t\wedge\tau)
, we only have

\partial_{t}u^{\tau}={\mathbf{1}}_{t\leqslant\tau}\big[\Delta_{v}u^{\tau}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u^{\tau}+f(u^{\tau})\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathbb{G}}^{k}(g(u^{\tau})){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t\wedge\tau},

which has a random degenerate coefficient in front of the Laplacian operator.

Remark 7.12
.

By (
7.24
) and
[
14
, Theorem III 6.8]
or
[
34
, Lemma 2.7]
, when
f=0
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}^{1/2}\right)\leqslant 2\|u_{0}\|^{1/2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}.

(7.29)

Now we are in a position to give

Proof of Theorem
7.8
.

For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we define

g_{m}(u):=(|u|\wedge m)^{\gamma+1}.

Clearly,
g_{m}
is global Lipschitz. By Theorem
7.5
, the following SKE has a unique weak solution
u_{m}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathbf{B}}^{\frac{1}{2}-4\gamma}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}))

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u=\big[\Delta_{v}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathbb{G}}^{k}(g_{m}(u)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},\ \ u(0)=u_{0}.

(7.30)

From the definition, one sees that
u_{m}
is also a weak solution of the following linearized equation:

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}w=\big[\Delta_{v}w+v\cdot\nabla_{x}w\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathbb{G}}^{k}(\xi_{m}w){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},\ \ w(0)=u_{0},

where

\xi_{m}(t,x,v):=g_{m}(u_{m}(t,x,v))/u_{m}(t,x,v),\ \ \tfrac{0}{0}:=0.

Since
|\xi_{m}|\leqslant m^{\gamma}
, by (
7.29
), we have

{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{m}(t)\|^{1/2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}\right)\leqslant 2\|u_{0}\|^{1/2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}.

In the following we shall use this a priori estimate to derive that for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{m}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\omega:\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{m}(t,\omega,\cdot)\|_{C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\geqslant R\right)=0.

(7.31)

For
S>0
, define a stopping time

\tau^{S}_{m}:=\inf\Big\{t\geqslant 0:\|u_{m}(t,\cdot)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}\geqslant S\Big\}.

Noting that

|\xi_{m}(t,\omega,z)|\leqslant|u_{m}(t,\omega,z)|^{\gamma},

we have for
s=1/\gamma
,

\|\xi_{m}(t\wedge\tau^{S}_{m},\omega,\cdot)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{s}_{z}}\leqslant S^{\gamma}.

On the other hand, by Duhamel’s formula, we also have

u_{m}(t)=P_{t}u_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}P_{t-s}{\mathbb{G}}^{k}(\xi_{m}u_{m}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{s}.

Let
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{p}+\gamma
and
\beta=4(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r})-\frac{1}{2}=-\frac{1}{2}-4\gamma
.
For any
\delta\in(0,\beta+1-\frac{2}{p})
, by (
3.7
), (
3.24
), (
7.6
) and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau^{S}_{m}]}\|u_{m}(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\delta}_{p;\theta}}\right)\lesssim\|u_{0}\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta+1-\frac{2}{p}}_{p;\theta}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\|{\mathbb{G}}((\xi_{m}u_{m})(\cdot\wedge\tau^{S}_{m}))(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p;\theta}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}(\ell^{2}))}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim 1+\int^{t}_{0}\|(\xi_{m}u_{m})(s\wedge\tau^{S}_{m})\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}({\mathbb{L}}^{r}_{z})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 1+S^{\gamma p}\int^{t}_{0}\|u_{m}(s\wedge\tau^{S}_{m})\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{\omega}({\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{z})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,

which implies by Gronwall’s inequality that

\sup_{m}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau^{S}_{m}]}\|u_{m}(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\delta}_{p;\theta}}\right)\leqslant C_{1}=C_{1}(S).

Since
p>\frac{12}{1-8\gamma}
, one can choose
\delta\in(\frac{4}{p},\beta+1-\frac{2}{p})=(\frac{4}{p},\frac{1}{2}-4\gamma-\frac{2}{p})
so that by the above estimate and (
2.5
) with
q=\infty
,

\sup_{m}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau^{S}_{m}]}\|u_{m}(t)\|^{p}_{C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\right)\leqslant C_{2}=C_{2}(S).

By Chebychev’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\omega:\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{m}(t,\omega,\cdot)\|_{C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\geqslant R\right)\leqslant{\mathbb{P}}(\tau^{S}_{m}\leqslant T)

\displaystyle\qquad+{\mathbb{P}}\left(\omega:\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau^{S}_{m}]}\|u_{m}(t,\omega,\cdot)\|_{C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\geqslant R\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\frac{1}{S^{1/2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{m}(t,\cdot)\|^{1/2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}\right)+\frac{1}{R}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau^{S}_{m}]}\|u_{m}(t)\|^{p}_{C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\frac{2\|u_{0}\|^{1/2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{z}}}{S^{1/2}}+\frac{C_{2}}{R},

which yields (
7.31
) by firstly letting
R\to\infty
and then
S\to\infty
.

Finally, by (
7.31
), we can construct a unique solution of (
7.22
) as in
[
15
]
. Indeed, for given
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we define

\tau_{m}^{n}:=\inf\Big\{t>0:\|u_{m}(t)\|_{C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\geqslant n\Big\}.

Since for
m>n
,
u_{m}
and
u_{n}
satisfy the same equation (
7.30
) with coefficient
g_{n}
before
\tau^{n}_{m}
.
By the local uniqueness (see Remark
7.11
), we have
u_{m}|_{[0,\tau^{n}_{m})}=u_{n}|_{[0,\tau^{n}_{m})}
. Hence,

\tau^{m}_{m}\geqslant\tau^{n}_{m}\geqslant\tau^{n}_{n},\ \ a.s.,

and we can define without ambiguity

u(t,x,v):=u_{m}(t,x,v),\ \ t\leqslant\tau^{m}_{m}.

Clearly,
u
is a unique weak solution of (
7.22
) before
\tau^{m}_{m}
. By (
7.31
), one sees that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(\tau^{m}_{m}\leqslant T)

\displaystyle\leqslant{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{m}(t)\|_{C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\geqslant m\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{n}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{C_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\geqslant m\right)\to 0

as
m\to\infty
. The proof is complete.
∎

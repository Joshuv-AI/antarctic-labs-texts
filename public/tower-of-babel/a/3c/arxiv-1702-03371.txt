Finite groups with permutable Hall subgroups
Xia Yin, Nanying Yang

Abstract
Let σ = { σ i | i ∈ I } \sigma=\{\sigma_{i}|i\in I\} be a partition of the set of all primes ℙ \mathbb{P} and G G a finite group. A set ℋ {\cal H} of subgroups of G G is said to be a complete Hall σ \sigma -set of G G if every member ≠ 1 \neq 1 of ℋ {\cal H} is a Hall σ i \sigma_{i} -subgroup of G G for some i ∈ I i\in I and ℋ \cal H contains exactly one Hall σ i \sigma_{i} -subgroup of G G for every i i such that σ i ∩ π ⁡ ( G ) ≠ ∅ \sigma_{i}\cap\pi(G)\neq\emptyset . In this paper, we study the structure of G G assuming that some subgroups of G G permutes with all members of ℋ {\cal H} .

D=\langle(W_{2})^{V^{x}}\rangle\cap\langle(V^{x})^{W_{2}}\rangle

is
subnormal in
G
by
[
14
, 1.1.9(2)]
. But
D\leq\langle W_{2},V^{x}\rangle\leq M
,

11
, Ch. A, 14.3]
, which implies that
[W_{2},V^{x}]=1
.
Thus
V^{x}\leq C_{G}(W_{2})
for all

. It follows that

H\leq(W_{3})^{G}\leq N_{G}(W_{2})
and therefore
W_{2}\leq C_{G}(H)=H

H\neq C_{G}(H)
.

Finally, let
D=G\times G
,
A^{*}=\{(g,g)|g\in G\}
,

and

. Then
C\leq C_{D}(R)
,

is a minimal normal subgroup of

and the factors

and

are
(A^{*}R)
-isomorphic.
Moreover,

where

and

RC/C
a minimal normal subgroup of

. As

, we see that

|A^{*}R/C|<|G|
. On the other hand, by Lemma 3.1, the hypothesis
holds for

, so the choice of

implies that
A\simeq A^{*}/C

is either a
\sigma_{i}
-group, where

p\not\in\sigma_{i}
, or a
(\sigma_{i}\cup\sigma_{j})
-group for some different
\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{j}

such that
p\in\sigma_{i}
.
This contradiction completes the proof of the necessity.

Sufficiency.
Assume that this is false and let

be a
counterexample of minimal order. Then
G
has a complete Hall set

{\cal W}=\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{t}\}

of type
\sigma
such that for some

W_{i}W_{j}\neq W_{j}W_{i}
. Let
R
be a minimal normal subgroup of

(1)

, so

is a unique minimal

.

It is clear that the hypothesis holds for

G/R\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\sigma}
by the choice of

. If

has a minimal

, then we also have

. Hence

is isomorphic to some subgroup of

. It follows from Lemma 3.1 that

. This contradiction shows that we
have Claim (1).

(2)
The hypothesis holds for any subgroup
E
of
G
.

Let
H/K
be any chief factor of
G
of order divisible by
p
such

. Then

is either a
\sigma_{i}
-group, where

, or a

-group for some different
\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{j}

. Let

-group or a

-group. Therefore the hypothesis holds for

of some chief series of

(3)
R

Since

of

. Assume that

, so

Final contradiction for sufficiency.

In view of Claims (1) and (3), there is a

.
Hence

\sigma_{k}

This contradiction completes the proof of the sufficiency.

Finally we prove that
\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma}
is saturated if and only if
|\sigma|\leq 2.

It is clear

. Now we show that for any
\sigma
such

Indeed, since
|\sigma|>2
, there are primes

\sigma_{i}
,
\sigma_{j}
and
\sigma_{k}
in

\sigma

,

and

and

-module

,

\mathbb{F}_{p}C_{r}

\mathbb{F}_{q}H
-module

Let

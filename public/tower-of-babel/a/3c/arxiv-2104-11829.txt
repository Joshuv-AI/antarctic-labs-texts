Nonlinear Stochastic parabolic partial differential equations with a monotone operator of the Ladyzenskaya-Smagorinsky type, driven by a Lévy noise
Phuong Nguyen 1,2 , Krutika Tawri 1 and Roger Temam 1

Abstract
. The aim of this article is to show the global existence of both martingale and pathwise solutions of stochastic equations with a monotone operator, of the Ladyzenskaya-Smagorinsky type, driven by a general Lévy noise. The classical approach based on using directly the Galerkin approximation is not valid. Instead, our approach is based on using appropriate approximations for the monotone operator, Galerkin approximations and on the theory of martingale solutions.

1.
Introduction

In this article we study the existence and uniqueness of global martingale and pathwise solutions to stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) with a nonlinear monotone operator driven by a Lévy noise, not necessarily square-integrable. As explained below from the physical point of view, the two types of equations that we consider are firstly the equations of Ladyzenskaya-Smagorinsky type, that are essentially the Navier Stokes equations with a viscosity depending on the gradient of velocity. The second type of equations that we consider are parabolic equations with a linear elliptic part and a nonlinearity of polynomial type. Such equations were already considered in
[
14
]
,
[
20
]
with restrictions on the degree of the polynomials but here polynomials of arbitrary degrees can be considered. Such equations appear in biology in e.g. population dynamics.

The equation that we consider is written in the abstract form

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}u(t)+[\gamma Au(t)+B(u(t),u(t))+F(u(t))]\mathrm{d}t\\
\hskip 36.135pt=G(u(t-))\mathrm{d}W+\int_{E_{0}}K(u(t-),\xi)\mathrm{d}\widehat{\pi}(t,\xi)+\int_{E\setminus E_{0}}\mathcal{K}(u(t-),\xi)\mathrm{d}\pi(t,\xi),\\
u(0)=u_{0},\end{cases}

where
u
takes values in a real, separable Hilbert space
H
. On the left-hand side of (
1.1
),
A
is a linear operator on
H
, generally unbounded,
B
is a bilinear form of the type often encountered in fluid dynamics and
F
is a monotone operator on a reflexive Banach space
X
which is continuously and densely embedded into
H
(see e.g.
[
8
]
and below). In Section
2
we introduce the precise assumptions on
F
. The terms on the right-hand side of (
1.1
) are stochastic integrals that represent different effects of a Lévy noise, which is fitting in stochastic models of fluid dynamics as a way to represent interactions that occur at random times. The term
G(u)\mathrm{d}W
has effects that are continuous in time on the system, whereas the two terms
\int_{E_{0}}K(u(t-),\xi)\mathrm{d}\widehat{\pi}(t,\xi)
,
\int_{E\setminus E_{0}}\mathcal{K}(u(t-),\xi)\mathrm{d}\pi(t,\xi),
influence the system discretely in time.

Interest in SPDEs with Lévy noise has been growing in the recent years, see e.g.,
[
12
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
42
]
,
[
43
]
. In
[
13
]
and
[
52
]
the authors use processes with jumps to model phase transitions in bursts of wind that contribute to the dynamics of El Niño. In
[
48
]
the authors use processes with jumps to model phase transitions in precipitation dynamics.
Models based on SPDEs with Lévy noise have been used to describe observational records in paleoclimatology in
[
21
]
, with the jump events of the Lévy noise proposed as representing abrupt triggers for shifts between glacial and warm climate states.
See also
[
24
]
for a comparison of Wiener noise and Lévy noise in modeling climate phenomena.

Our main results are Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.4
which establish the existence and uniqueness of martingale and pathwise solutions to the equation (
1.1
) (see also Definitions
3.1
and
3.2
). We use the classical Galerkin approximation method to prove Theorem
3.3
. However a traditional application of the Galerkin method fails as the projectors
P_{n}
, defined in (
4.1
), may not necessarily be projectors in the Banach space
X
associated with
F
(see Section
2
). Hence we approximate the monotone operator
F
in (
1.1
) by a family of monotone operators
F^{R}
acting in another Hilbert space, continuously and densely embedded in
H
, so as to recover certain useful properties when
R\rightarrow\infty
. The a priori estimates for (
1.1
) also suggest the need to truncate the bilinear term
B
in the original system into
B_{\widetilde{R}}
(see equations (
4.2
)), then pass to the limit
\widetilde{R}\rightarrow\infty
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
3
we state the main results. In Section
2
we layout the functional analytical and probabilistic framework and the assumptions on the terms appearing in (
1.1
). In Section
4
we introduce the Galerkin system for the approximation equations (
4.2
). We establish a priori estimates in Section
4.1
and tightness of the laws of the Galerkin approximations in Section
4.3
. Then we use the Skorohod convergence theorem to obtain a sequence, same in distribution, that converges a.s. on a
new probability space and pass to the limit to obtain a global martingale solution to the truncated
equation (
4.2
) in Section
4.4
. We then pass to the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
and
\widetilde{R}\rightarrow\infty
in Sections
5
and
6
respectively to obtain a global martingale solution to (
1.1
). In Section
7
we establish pathwise uniqueness for the equation (
1.1
) and then obtain a global pathwise solution to (
1.1
) using a well-known argument by Gyöngy and Krylov that generalizes to infinite dimensions the classical Yamada–Watanabe theorem. In Section
8
we describe examples that fit the setting of our analysis.

2.
Mathematical framework

The following is a general framework which we will adapt to the actual problem under consideration.
We consider a separable, real Hilbert space
V\subset H
, such that the embedding is dense and compact. Similarly,
X
is a reflexive Banach space continuously and densely embedded into
H
. We may thus define the Gelfand inclusions

(2.1)

\displaystyle V\subset H\equiv H^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime},

(2.2)

\displaystyle X\subset H\equiv H^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}.

where
V^{\prime}
,
X^{\prime}
and

are the dual spaces of

,
X
and

respectively.
In fact, we will strengthen (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) by requiring that
V\cap X
is dense in
V
and
X
, so that, for
V^{\prime}+X^{\prime}

denoting the dual of
V\cap X
, we have

(2.3a)

V\cap X\text{}\subset X\subset H\subset X^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime}+X^{\prime},

and

(2.3b)

V\cap X\text{}\subset V\subset H\subset V^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime}+X^{\prime}.

We denote by
(\cdot,\cdot)
,
|\cdot|
,
((\cdot,\cdot))
, and
\|\cdot\|
the norms and inner products of
H
, and
V
respectively.

The duality pairing between

and

will be denoted by

For the purpose of the abstract analysis in this paper, in continuation with
[
49
]
, and keeping our primary examples in mind, we will first assume that, for some open set

,

and

are closed subspaces of
(H^{1}(\mathcal{O}))^{d}
and
(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))^{d}
respectively and that

, for some

and an integer

. However slightly different settings can be introduced depending on the properties of the monotone operator (see Remark
2.1
and Section
8.4
).

We now give the precise assumptions on each of the terms appearing in (
1.1
), beginning with the linear term
A
. In our analysis we shall assume that
\gamma>0
in (
1.1
). However we show in Section
8.2
(see Remark
8.4
), how the case
\gamma=0
is handled. We assume that
A:D(A)\subset V\rightarrow H
is an unbounded, densely defined, bijective, linear operator such that
(Av,w)=((v,w))
for all
v,w\in D(A)
. Under these conditions on
A
, the space
D(A)
is complete under the Hilbertian norm
|v|_{D(A)}:=|Av|
. When
H
is a subspace of
(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))^{d}
and
V
a subspace of
(H^{1}(\mathcal{O}))^{d}
, the role of
A
can be played by the negative Laplacian associated with suitable boundary conditions. It is clear that for suitable boundary conditions, the operator
A
is symmetric. The operator
A
can be viewed as a linear continuous operator from
V
to
V^{\prime}
via

(2.4)

\langle Av,w\rangle:=((v,w)),\qquad\textnormal{for all }v,w\in V.

Furthermore, since we assume that
V
is compactly embedded in
H
, it follows that
A^{-1}
is a compact operator on
H
. Since
A
is assumed to be symmetric and surjective we easily see that
A^{-1}
is self-adjoint. By the spectral theory for positive compact operators, there exists an orthonormal basis
\left(w_{k}\right)_{k=1}^{\infty}
of
H
made of eigenvectors of
A
. The sequence
\left(w_{k}\right)_{k=1}^{\infty}
lies in
D(A)
and forms an orthogonal basis in
D(A),V,V^{\prime}
. Furthermore, each
w_{k}
is an eigenvector of
A
whose corresponding eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
is positive and
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Note that

D(A^{1/2}):=\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}w_{k}\in H:\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{2}\lambda_{k}<\infty\Big\}

is nothing but the space
V
. Under the conditions given above, one can show that the embedding
D(A)\subset D(A^{1/2})=V
is compact (see e.g.
[
37
]
,
[
51
]
).

Next we assume that
B\colon V\times D(A)\to V^{\prime}
is bilinear and continuous. Furthermore, we assume that
B
maps the subspace
D(A)\times D(A)
continuously into
H
and that
B
possesses the cancellation property

(2.5)

\langle B(v,w),w\rangle=0,\qquad\textnormal{for all }v\in V\textnormal{ and }w\in D(A).

We also assume that

(2.6)

\begin{split}\begin{cases}|{B(v,w)}|_{D(A)^{\prime}}&\lesssim|v|\|w\|\qquad\textnormal{for all }v\in H,w\in V,\\
&\lesssim{\|v\||w|}\qquad\textnormal{for all }v\in V,w\in H,\end{cases}\end{split}

and,

(2.7)

\displaystyle|{B(v,v)}|_{V^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|v|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|^{\frac{3}{2}}\qquad\textnormal{for all }v\in V.

In Section
7.1
,
we also use the following assumption:

(2.8)

\displaystyle|B(v,v)|_{X^{\prime}}\lesssim|v|\|v\|\qquad\textnormal{for all }v\in V.

We will often write

.

For certain subspaces
V
and
H

(H^{1}(\mathcal{O}))^{d}
and
(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))^{d}
respectively, we have in mind for

the bilinear function

, corresponding to the nonlinear terms in the Navier-Stokes equations (see e.g.
[
51
]
).

Remark 2.1
.

The framework above, which we will use till the end of Section
8.3
, covers the main example that we have in view, namely the Ladyzenskaya-Smagorinsky equations and equations of the same type. We will see in Section

of the noise terms in equation (

. In this paper, we assume that

(see

We denote by

. We assume that

on a measurable space

. The intensity measure of

. In equation (

such that

is independent of

. Under this condition it is possible to construct a filtration

on

is an

is an

satisfies the usual conditions (as explained in e.g.

which, informally, gives us the rule for the decomposition of integration with respect to

Now, let

denote the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators from
{U_{0}}
to

equipped with the

.

Then for the coefficients

G\colon H\rightarrow L_{2}(U_{0},H)
and

•

And for some

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left\|G(v)\right\|^{2}_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}+\int_{E_{0}}|K(v,\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\leq\rho(1+|v|^{2})\qquad\textnormal{for all }v\in H,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left\|G(v)-G(w)\right\|^{2}_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}+\int_{E_{0}}|K(v,\xi)-K(w,\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\leq\rho|v-w|^{2}\quad\textnormal{for all }v,w\in H.

Note that the above conditions and Lemma 3.2 from

3.
Existence results

3.1.
The main results

Galerkin scheme

We describe here the Galerkin approximation of the problem, then derive moment estimates, estimates in fractional Sobolev spaces, tightness of the Galerkin approximation and then pass to the limit to obtain the existence of solutions in Theorem

To construct the Galerkin scheme for equation (

\displaystyle(F^{R}(v),\varphi)

4.1.
Moment estimates

In order to construct a solution to the system (

and then pass to the limit

Lemma 4.1
.

(
L^{2}
-regularity)

Suppose that F satisfies the conditions (
2.13
)-(
2.15
), so that the family of operators
F^{R}
satisfies (FR1)-(FR4). Then there exists a constant
C(T,\rho,c_{1},c_{5})>0
, independent of
n
and in fact independent of
R
and
\widetilde{R}
such that for every
n\geq 1
:

(4.10)

\displaystyle{\bf E}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|u^{n}(s)|^{2}+(\gamma+c_{1}){\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|u^{n}(s)\right\|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\leq C{\bf E}|u_{0}|^{2}=:K_{1}(u_{0},T,\rho,c_{1},c_{5}).

Proof.

We begin by applying the Itô formula to the process in (
4.9
). To be precise, here we use the Itô formula in the case of solutions to SDEs with Lévy noise as stated in Theorem 2.19 in
[
14
]
and the special case in which
\psi(u)=|u|^{2}
as stated in Corollary 1 in
[
14
]
. See also Theorem I.3.1 in
[
32
]
and
[
38
]
for the Itô formula in the general context of semi-martingales. We obtain

(4.11)

\begin{split}&|{u^{n}}|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(Au^{n}(s),u^{n}(s))\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}(P_{n}B_{\widetilde{R}}(u^{n}(s)),u^{n}(s))\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}(P_{n}F^{R}(u^{n}(s)),u^{n}(s))\mathrm{d}s\\
&=|u^{n}_{0}|^{2}+\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E_{0}}2(u^{n}(s),P_{n}K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)\mathrm{d}\widehat{\pi}(s,\xi))+\int_{0}^{t}2(u^{n}(s),P_{n}G(u^{n}(s-))\mathrm{d}W(s))\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|P_{n}G(u^{n}(s))\right\|_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E_{0}}|{P_{n}K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\pi(s,\xi).\end{split}

Here we made use of the cancellation property
(B_{\widetilde{R}}(u,u),u)=0
. Now we use the equicoercivity result (
4.7
) along with the equation (
2.4
), we take the supremum in time over
[0,r]
for a fixed
r\in[0,T]
and then take expectation of both sides; we find

(4.12)

\displaystyle{\bf E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}|u^{n}(t)|^{2}]+

\displaystyle 2{\bf E}\int_{0}^{r}{\left((\gamma+c_{1})\left\|u^{n}(s)\right\|^{2}-c_{5}\right)}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq{\bf E}[|u^{n}_{0}|^{2}]+{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}[I(t)+J(t)+K(t)+L(t)],

where

\displaystyle I(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\|P_{n}G(u^{n}(s))\right\|_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle J(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E_{0}}|{P_{n}K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\pi(s,\xi),

\displaystyle K(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(u^{n}(s),P_{n}G(u^{n}(s-))\mathrm{d}W(s)),

\displaystyle L(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E_{0}}(u^{n}(s),P_{n}K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)\mathrm{d}\widehat{\pi}(s,\xi)).

We estimate the terms
I(t)
and
J(t)

based on the assumption (
2.9
) along with Young’s inequality as follows

\displaystyle{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|P_{n}G(u^{n}(s))\right\|_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|G(u^{n}(s))\right\|_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle={\bf E}\int_{0}^{r}\left\|G(u^{n}(s))\right\|_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

(4.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim{\bf E}\int_{0}^{r}\rho(|u^{n}(s)|^{2}+1)\mathrm{d}s.

Similarly for
J(t)
we use the isometry property (see e.g. Theorem 2.16 in
[
14
]
) to obtain,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E_{0}}|P_{n}K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\pi(s,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E_{0}}|K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\pi(s,\xi)

\displaystyle={\bf E}\int_{(0,r]}\int_{E_{0}}|K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}s

(4.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim{\bf E}\int_{(0,r]}\rho(|u^{n}(s)|^{2}+1)\mathrm{d}s.

Note that in (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) we used the fact that
P_{n}
is a projector on
H
. The next two martingale terms

and
L(t)
are treated by using the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality (see e.g.
[
45
]
) and Young’s inequality. Observe that for some constant
C(\rho)>0
independent of

we obtain

\displaystyle{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}\left|\int_{0}^{t}(u^{n}(s),P_{n}G(u^{n}(s-))\mathrm{d}W(s))\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim{\bf E}\left(\int_{0}^{r}(u^{n}(t),P_{n}G(u^{n}(t-)))^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\bf E}\left(\int_{0}^{r}\left\|P_{n}G(u^{n}(t))\right\|^{2}_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}|u^{n}(t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}|u^{n}(t)|\left(\int_{0}^{r}\left\|G(u^{n}(t))\right\|^{2}_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which implies for
K(t)
that,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}\left|\int_{0}^{t}(u^{n}(s),P_{n}G(u^{n}(s-))\mathrm{d}W(s))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}|u^{n}(t)|^{2}+C(\rho){\bf E}\int_{0}^{r}\left(1+|u^{n}(s)|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s.

Similarly using the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, (see Proposition 2 pg. 5650 in
[
14
]
) we obtain for the term
L(t)
:

\displaystyle{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}\left|\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E_{0}}(u^{n}(s),P_{n}K(u^{n}(s-),\xi))\mathrm{d}\widehat{\pi}(s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim{\bf E}\left(\int_{(0,r]}\int_{E_{0}}|u^{n}(s)|^{2}|P_{n}K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\pi(s,\xi)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{1}{4}{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}|{u^{n}(t)}|^{2}+C{\bf E}\bigg[\int_{(0,r]}\int_{E_{0}}|K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\pi(s,\xi)\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\frac{1}{4}{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}|{u^{n}(t)}|^{2}+C{\bf E}\bigg[\int_{(0,r]}\int_{E_{0}}|K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)ds\bigg]

(4.15)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{1}{4}{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq r}|u^{n}(t)|^{2}+C(\rho){\bf E}\int_{0}^{r}\left(1+|u^{n}(s)|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s.

Collecting all relations from (
4.13
) through (
4.15
) and applying in (
4.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|u^{n}(s)|^{2}+{(\gamma+c_{1})}{\bf E}\int_{0}^{r}\left\|u^{n}(s)\right\|^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 144.54pt\leq\penalty\ {\bf E}|u^{n}_{0}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|u^{n}(s)|^{2}+C(\rho){\bf E}\int_{0}^{r}(1+|u^{n}(s)|^{2})\mathrm{d}s.

Multiplying both sides by 2 and using the fact that

, we obtain for some constant

independent of

(4.16)

\displaystyle\bf{\displaystyle E}\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|u^{n}(s)|^{2}+{2(\gamma+c_{1})}\int_{0}^{r}{\bf E}\left\|u^{n}(s)\right\|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\leq\penalty\ C\left({\bf E}|u_{0}|^{2}+{\int_{0}^{r}\sup_{0\leq t\leq s}{\bf E}|u^{n}(t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s}\right)+C.

The proof is complete after applying the Grönwall lemma to
{\bf E}\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|u^{n}(s)|^{2}
.
∎

4.2.
Estimates in fractional Sobolev spaces

To obtain compactness, we will use a particular characterization of the fractional Sobolev space
{W^{\alpha,m}(0,T;Z)}
where
Z
is a Banach space.

Given
m>1
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
W^{\alpha,m}(0,T;Z)
is the Sobolev space consisting of the
v\in L^{m}(0,T;Z)
such that the (Gagliardo) seminorm
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|v(t)-v(s)|^{m}}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha m}}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s
is finite. This space is then endowed with the norm

(4.17)

\displaystyle\|v\|^{m}_{W^{\alpha,m}(0,T;Z)}=\|v\|_{L^{m}(0,T;Z)}^{m}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|v(t)-v(s)|^{m}}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha m}}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s.

We denote
W^{\alpha,2}=:H^{\alpha}
. Now we state the following lemma:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}_{0}\subset\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{Y}_{1}
be Banach spaces,
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
reflexive with compact embedding of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
in
\mathcal{Y}
. Let
s,m>1
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
be given. Let

\mathcal{G}
be the space

\mathcal{G}=L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{Y})\cap L^{s}(0,T;\mathcal{Y}_{0})\cap W^{\alpha,m}(0,T;\mathcal{Y}_{1}),

endowed with the natural norm. Then the embedding of
\mathcal{G}
in
L^{r}(0,T;\mathcal{Y})
is compact,
\forall 1\leq r<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
q=\min(m,s)>1
and define
\mathscr{G}_{q}=L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{Y}_{0})\cap W^{\alpha,q}(0,T;\mathcal{Y}_{1})
. Then it is shown in
[
50
]
(Section 13.3 Theorem 13.2) and
[
25
]
(Theorem 2.1) that the injection of
\mathscr{G}_{q}
in
L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{Y})
is compact. Now if
\{v_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{G}
that converges to
v
weakly in
\mathscr{G}_{q}
and strongly in
L^{q}(0,T;\mathcal{Y})
, then up to the extraction of a subsequence,
v_{k}(t)\rightarrow v(t)
strongly in
\mathcal{Y}
for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
. Since
v_{k}
is also bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{Y})
, using the Vitali convergence theorem, we have that
v_{m}\rightarrow v
strongly in
L^{r}(0,T;\mathcal{Y})
for every
r\in[1,\infty)
.
∎

In what follows, we will apply the above Lemma with
\mathcal{Y}_{0}=V
,
\mathcal{Y}_{1}=D(A)^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{Y}=H
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
s=m=2
. We will use the above lemma again in Sections
5
and
6
as well.

We are now in position to show that the approximating sequence
\{u^{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in the space
L^{2}(\Omega;H^{\alpha}(0,T;D(A)^{\prime}))

for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. For that purpose, we recall the following proposition essentially borrowed from
[
25
]
:

Proposition 4.4

For every
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and

(4.19)

{\bf E}\left\|\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E_{0}}P_{n}K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)\mathrm{d}\widehat{\pi}(s,\xi)\right\|^{\beta}_{W^{\alpha,\beta}(0,T;H)}\leq C.

Using Lemma 2.1 in
[

]

) and Lemma

(4.20)

\displaystyle\penalty\ {\bf E}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}P_{n}G(u^{n}(s))\mathrm{d}W(s)\right\|^{\beta}_{W^{\alpha,\beta}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|P_{n}G(u^{n})\right\|^{\beta}_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}\mathrm{d}s

A continuity result similar to Lemma 2.1 in
[

]
holds for the stochastic integral with respect to a compensated Poisson random measure. For more details of the case

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left\|\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E_{0}}P_{n}K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)\mathrm{d}\widehat{\pi}(s,\xi)\right\|^{\beta}_{W^{\alpha,\beta}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E_{0}}|P_{n}K(u^{n}(s-),\xi)|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\left({\bf E}\int_{0}^{T}(1+|u^{n}(s)|^{2})\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\leq C.

Proposition 4.6

The proof follows standard procedure (see e.g.

However in order to obtain a candidate solution that is càdlàg we will need to establish tightness of the laws of

Let

7.1.
Pathwise uniqueness

In this section we will prove the uniqueness of martingale solutions, existence of which has been established in the previous section.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
u_{1}
and
u_{2}

be two martingale solutions of (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) relative to the same stochastic basis
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},{\bf P},W,\pi)
. Then
u_{1},u_{2}
are indistinguishable, that is if
u_{1}(0)=u_{2}(0)
a.s. then we have

(7.1)

{\bf P}[u_{1}(t)=u_{2}(t),\forall t\geq 0]=1.

Proof.

Letting
u=u_{1}-u_{2}
we write the equations (
3.1
) satisfied by
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
and obtain by difference the equation satisfied by
u
. We then obtain, by
applying the Itô formula using the function
|u|^{2}
to the difference equation:

\displaystyle|u(t)|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle(\gamma(Au(s),u(s))+(F(u_{1}(s))-F(u_{2}(s)),u(s))+2(B(u_{1}(s))-B(u_{2}(s)),u(s)))\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip-21.68121pt=\int_{0}^{t}|G(u_{1}(s))-G(u_{2}(s))|_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}([G(u_{1}(s))-G(u_{2}(s))]\mathrm{d}W(s),u)

\displaystyle+2\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E_{0}}(u(s),K(u_{1}(s-),\xi)-K(u_{2}(s-),\xi))\mathrm{d}\widehat{\pi}(s,\xi)

(7.2)

\displaystyle+\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E_{0}}|K(u_{1}(s-),\xi)-K(u_{2}(s-),\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\pi(s,\xi).

Here we recall that our assumption (
2.11
) gives:

(7.3)

(F(u_{1})-F(u_{2}),u)\geq 0.

Next we derive upper bounds for the term
B
. Observe that due to the bilinearity of
B
and the assumptions (
2.5
) and (
2.8
) we have

\displaystyle(B(u_{1},u_{1})-B(u_{2},u_{2}),u)

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2}\left(B(u_{1}+u_{2},u_{1}-u_{2})+B(u_{1}-u_{2},u_{1}+u_{2})\right),u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((B(u_{1}+u_{2},u),u)+(B(u,u_{1}+u_{2}),u)\right)

\displaystyle=-(B(u,u_{1}),u)

(7.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim|u|\|u_{1}\|_{X}\|u\|.

We continue our analysis by introducing the stopping time

(7.5)

\tau^{M}=\inf\{t\geq 0:\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{1}\|^{p}_{X}\mathrm{d}s>M\},\quad M>0.

Then inserting (
7.4
) and the monotonicity condition (
7.3
) into equation (
7.2
), taking the supremum in time over
[0,t\wedge\tau^{M}]
, and finally taking expectations, we obtain

\displaystyle{\bf E}\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau^{M}]}|u(s)|^{2}

\displaystyle+\gamma{\bf E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\|u(s)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{2}{\gamma}{\bf E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\left(|u(s)|^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{X}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|u(s)\|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+2{\bf E}\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau^{M}]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{s}(G(r,u_{1}(r))-G(r,u_{2}(r))\mathrm{d}W(r),u)\bigg|

\displaystyle+{\bf E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}||G(u_{1}(s))-G(u_{2}(s))||_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+2{\bf E}\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau^{M}]}\bigg|\int_{(0,s]}\int_{E_{0}}(K(r,u_{1}(r-),\xi)-K(r,u_{2}(r-),\xi),u(r))\mathrm{d}\widehat{\pi}(r,\xi)\bigg|

(7.6)

\displaystyle+{\bf E}\int_{(0,t\wedge\tau^{M}]}\int_{E_{0}}|K(u_{1}(s-),\xi)-K(u_{2}(s-),\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\pi(s,\xi).

By using the Lipschitz growth assumption (
2.10

\displaystyle{\bf E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}||G(u_{1}(s))-G(u_{2}(s))||_{L_{2}(U_{0},H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 93.95122pt+{\bf E}\int_{(0,t\wedge\tau^{M}]}\int_{E_{0}}|K(u_{1}(s-),\xi)-K(u_{2}(s-),\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\pi(s,\xi)

(7.7)

\displaystyle\hskip 93.95122pt\leq\rho{\bf E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(1+|u(s)|^{2})\mathrm{d}s.

By utilizing the BDG inequality along with the hypothesis (

\displaystyle{\bf E}\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau^{M}]}

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{s}([G(r,u_{1}(r))-G(r,u_{2}(r))]\mathrm{d}W(r),u)\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim{\bf E}\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}(G(r,u_{1}(r))-G(r,u_{2}(r)),u)^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\bf E}\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}|u(s)|^{2}|G(u_{1}(s))-G(u_{2}(s))|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}{\bf E}\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau^{M}]}|u(s)|^{2}+C{\bf E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\rho|u(s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

\displaystyle{\bf E}

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau^{M}]}\bigg|\int_{(0,t]}\int_{E_{0}}(u(s),K(u_{1}(s-),\xi)-K(u_{2}(s-),\xi))\mathrm{d}\widehat{\pi}(s,\xi)\bigg|

(7.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}{\bf E}\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau^{M}]}|u(s)|^{2}+C{\bf E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}\rho|u(s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

Collecting the estimates (

(7.10)

\displaystyle{\bf E}\sup_{s\in[0,t\wedge\tau^{M}]}|u(s)|^{2}\leq C(\rho,T,\gamma){\bf E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{M}}|u(s)|^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{X}^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

We now apply the stochastic Grönwall lemma (see e.g. Lemma 5.3 in

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|u(t)|^{2}\right)=0.

Since

7.2.
Pathwise solution

In this section we apply the well known result by Gyöngy and Krylov in
[
28
]
that extends the Yamada–Watanabe theorem (cf.

]
) to infinite dimension. This theorem states that the existence of martingale solutions and the pathwise uniqueness imply the existence of a pathwise solution to (

We can now finish the proof of Theorem

First using the following theorem in
[
28
]
, it can be shown that
\bar{\bar{u}}^{\widetilde{R}}
, the sequence of solutions to (
5.1

with respect to the original stochastic basis

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Polish space. A sequence of
{\mathcal{E}}
-valued random variables
\{\mathscr{Y}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, defined on te same probability space, converges in probability if and only if for every subsequence of joint probability laws of
\{\mathscr{Y}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, given by
\{\mu_{m_{l}},\mu_{n_{l}}\}_{l\geq 0}
, there exists a further subsequence that converges weakly to a probability measure
\mu
such that

(7.13)

\displaystyle\mu(\{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}\times{\mathcal{E}}:x=y\})=1.

In preparation for using this theorem, define

(7.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}:=H\times\mathcal{Z}_{1}\times H\times\mathcal{Z}_{1}\times C([0,T];U)\times\mathcal{N}^{\#*}_{[0,\infty)\times E},

where
\mathcal{N}^{\#*}_{[0,\infty)}
denotes the space of counting measures on
[0,\infty)\times E
that are
finite on bounded sets.
Now, consider two subsequences

]

[

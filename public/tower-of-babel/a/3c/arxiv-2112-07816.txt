Square integrals of the logarithmic derivatives of Selberg’s zeta functions in the critical strip
Yasufumi Hashimoto

Abstract
In our previous work ( https://doi.org/10.1002/mana.202000268 , Math. Nachr., 2021), we proposed an upper bound of the logarithmic derivative of Selberg’s zeta function for the modular groups in the critical strip. The present paper studies the growth of its square integral for the modular group, co-compact arithmetic groups derived from indefinite quaternion algebras and their subgroups.

1
Introduction and the main theorem

Let
H:=\{x+y\sqrt{-1}\hskip 3.0pt|\hskip 3.0ptx,y\in\mathbb{R},y>0\}
be the upper half plane
and
\Gamma
a discrete subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma\backslash H)<\infty
.
Denote by
\mathrm{Prim}(\Gamma)
the set of primitive hyperbolic conjugacy classes
of
\Gamma
and
N(\gamma)
the square of the larger eigenvalue of
\gamma
.
The Selberg zeta function for
\Gamma
is defined by

\displaystyle Z_{\Gamma}(s):=\prod_{\gamma\in\mathrm{Prim}(\Gamma),n\geq 0}(1-N(\gamma)^{-s-n}),\qquad\mathrm{Re}{s}>1.

It is well known that
Z_{\Gamma}(s)
is analytically continued to the whole complex plane
as a meromorphic function
and has a functional equation between the values at
s
and
1-s
(see, e.g.
[
11
]
).
In the present paper, we study the growth of
Z_{\Gamma}(s)

in the critical strip
\{0<\mathrm{Re}{s}<1\}
as
|\mathrm{Im}{s}|\to\infty
.

For the Riemann zeta function

\zeta(s):=\prod_{p}(1-p^{-s})^{-1},

(\mathrm{Re}{s}>1),

it has been known there exists a constant
a\geq 0
satisfying

\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+iT\right)\ll_{\epsilon}T^{a+\epsilon},\quad\text{as}\quad T\to\infty.

Note that
a\leq\frac{32}{205}
was proven
[
12
]

and
a=0
has been expected as the Lindelöf conjecture.
For the Selberg zeta function,
the following bounds have been given for for
1/2<\sigma<1
(see e.g.
[
11
,
13
,
3
]
).

\displaystyle\log{Z_{\Gamma}(\sigma+iT)},\quad\frac{Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\sigma+iT)}{Z_{\Gamma}(\sigma+iT)}\ll_{\epsilon}T^{2-2\sigma+\epsilon},\quad\text{as}\quad T\to\infty.

(1.1)

The bound above means that
the growth of
Z_{\Gamma}(s)
is (presently bounded to be) exponential of
|\mathrm{Im}{s}|
,
which is quite different to the Riemann zeta function.
This fact often causes difficulties
when analyzing the Selberg zeta functions,
and then sharpening the estimate (
1.1
) has been desirable.

In our previous work
[
9
]
,
we improved (
1.1
) for
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
to

\displaystyle\frac{Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\sigma+iT)}{Z_{\Gamma}(\sigma+iT)}\ll_{\epsilon}\begin{cases}\displaystyle T^{\frac{19}{9}-\frac{20}{9}\sigma+\epsilon},&(\frac{1}{2}<\sigma\leq\frac{5}{8}),\\
\displaystyle T^{\frac{52}{27}(1-\sigma)+\epsilon},&(\frac{5}{8}<\sigma<1),\end{cases}\quad\text{as}\quad T\to\infty

(1.2)

by using the expression of the Selberg zeta function for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

in terms of indefinite binary quadratic forms.
On the other hand, Aoki
[
1
]
studied the square integral

\int_{1}^{T}\left|\frac{Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\sigma+it)}{Z_{\Gamma}(\sigma+it)}\right|^{2}dt

and proved that it appears in the pair correlation formulas
for the non-trivial zeros of the Selberg zeta functions
under the assumption that

\Gamma
is co-compact torsion free
and
Z_{\Gamma}(s)
does not have real zeros in the critical strip.
He also proved that there exists a constant
C_{\Gamma}(\sigma)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}\left|\frac{Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\sigma+it)}{Z_{\Gamma}(\sigma+it)}\right|^{2}dt\sim C_{\Gamma}(\sigma)T,\quad\text{as}\quad T\to\infty

(1.3)

when
\frac{17}{20}<\sigma<1
and

\Gamma
is a co-compact arithmetic group derived from an indefinite division quaternion algebra,
defined as follows:
Let
a,b
be square free and relatively prime positive integers and

B:=\mathbb{Q}+\mathbb{Q}\alpha+\mathbb{Q}\beta+\mathbb{Q}\alpha\beta
the quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}

with
\alpha^{2}=a
,
\beta^{2}=b
,
\alpha\beta=-\beta\alpha
.
Suppose that
B
is division and
fix a maximal order
\mathcal{O}
of
B
.
Then the group
\mathcal{O}^{1}
consisting
of
q_{0}+q_{1}\alpha+q_{2}\beta+q_{3}\alpha\beta\in\mathcal{O}

with
q_{0}^{2}-q_{1}^{2}a-q_{2}^{2}b+q_{3}^{2}ab=1

can be identified with
a co-compact discrete subgroup
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathcal{O}}

of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
by the map

\displaystyle q_{0}+q_{1}\alpha+q_{2}\beta+q_{3}\alpha\beta\mapsto

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}q_{0}+q_{1}\sqrt{a}&q_{2}\sqrt{b}+q_{3}\sqrt{ab}\\
q_{2}\sqrt{b}-q_{3}\sqrt{ab}&q_{0}-q_{1}\sqrt{a}\end{pmatrix}.

While (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are for different
\Gamma
’s,
the asymptotic formula (
1.3
) gives a better estimate
than the square integral of the right hand side of (
1.2
)
in the region
\{\frac{17}{20}<\sigma<1\}
.
It was proven by
Landau’s formula for square integrals of Dirichlet series
(see Section 233–226 in
[
16
]
) based on the fact that
the error term of the prime geodesic theorem for the corresponding quaternion
\Gamma

are bounded as follows (see
[
15
,
18
]
).

\displaystyle\#\{\gamma\in\mathrm{Prim}(\Gamma)\hskip 3.0pt|\hskip 3.0ptN(\gamma)<x\}-\int_{2}^{x}\frac{dt}{\log{t}}\ll_{\epsilon,\Gamma}x^{\frac{7}{10}+\epsilon},\quad\text{as}\quad x\to\infty.

The range
\frac{17}{20}<\sigma<1
can be extended
if the error term estimation of the prime geodesic theorem will be improved.
For the modular group, the exponent
\frac{7}{10}
of the error term
was improved to
\frac{25}{36}
, and to
\frac{2}{3}

if the Lindelöf conjecture
for the Dirichlet
L
function holds
[
21
,
4
]
.
By using Landau’s formula, one can improve the range of
\sigma
for (
1.3
)
to
\frac{5}{6}<\sigma<1
,
if the exponent
\frac{2}{3}
will be given also for quaternion
\Gamma
’s.

In the present paper, we improve and generalize (
1.3
) as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a (not necessarily congruence) subgroup of the modular group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

or a co-compact arithmetic group
\Gamma_{\mathcal{O}}
.
Then, for
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}\left|\frac{Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\sigma+it)}{Z_{\Gamma}(\sigma+it)}\right|^{2}dt\ll_{\epsilon,\Gamma}T^{\max(0,5-6\sigma+\epsilon)},\quad\text{as}\quad T\to\infty.

Especially, when
\frac{5}{6}<\sigma<1
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}\left|\frac{Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\sigma+it)}{Z_{\Gamma}(\sigma+it)}\right|^{2}dt\sim C_{\Gamma}(\sigma)T,\quad\text{as}\quad T\to\infty

for some constant
C_{\Gamma}(\sigma)>0
depending on
\Gamma
and
\sigma
.

In the theorem above, the range
\frac{5}{6}<\sigma<1

is available without the assumption of the Lindelöf conjecture.
To prove the theorem,
we first state an explicit formula (Proposition
2.1
)
to describe
Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(s)/Z_{\Gamma}(s)

as a sum of
\gamma\in\mathrm{Prim}(\Gamma)

and take its square integral.
In the process of estimating the square integral,
we use an upper bound (Lemma
3.1
) of the number
m_{\Gamma}(n)

which is almost the same as the number of
\gamma\in\mathrm{Prim}(\Gamma)
with the same
N(\gamma)
,
i.e. the multiplicity in the length spectrum on
\Gamma\backslash H
.
Since its multiplicity is more informative than the error term of the prime geodesic theorem,
Theorem
1.1
gives an improvement of the contribution of (
1.3
).

2
Explicit formula

To prove Theorem
1.1
,
we first state the following explicit formula for the logarithmic derivative of
Selberg’s zeta function.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma\backslash H)<\infty
,

s=\sigma+iT\in\mathbb{C}
with
1/2<\sigma<1
and

\varphi_{s}(x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathrm{Prim}(\Gamma),j\geq 1\\
N(\gamma)^{j}<x\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{\Gamma}(\gamma^{j})\left(1-\frac{N(\gamma)^{j}}{x}\right)N(\gamma)^{-js}

for
x>0
, where
\displaystyle\Lambda_{\Gamma}(\gamma^{j}):=\frac{\log{N(\gamma)}}{1-N(\gamma)^{-j}}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(s)}{Z_{\Gamma}(s)}=

\displaystyle\varphi_{s}(x)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{1}{2}<\rho\leq 1\\
Z_{\Gamma}(\rho)=0\end{subarray}}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{(\rho-s)(1+\rho-s)}+O_{\epsilon}\left(T^{1+\epsilon}x^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma+\epsilon}\right),\quad\text{as}\quad T,x\to\infty

for any
\epsilon>0
,
where
\{1/2<\rho\leq 1\}
is the set of (at most a finite number of) real zeros of
Z_{\Gamma}(s)
.

Proof.

For
V=T^{3}
and
\epsilon>0
, let
C
be the rectangle
with the corners
1+\epsilon-iV
,
1+\epsilon+iV
,
1/2+\epsilon+iV
,
1/2+\epsilon-iV
,
and

\displaystyle J:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial C}\frac{Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(z)}{Z_{\Gamma}(z)}\frac{x^{z-s}}{(z-s)(z-s+1)}dz,

where the integral is in an anti-clockwise direction.
It is known that the singular points of
Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(z)/Z_{\Gamma}(z)
in
C

are a single pole at
z=1
and
at most a finite number of single poles on the real line.
Then, due to the residue theorem, we have

\displaystyle J=\frac{Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(s)}{Z_{\Gamma}(s)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{1}{2}<\rho\leq 1\\
Z_{\Gamma}(\rho)=0\end{subarray}}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{(\rho-s)(1+\rho-s)}.

(2.1)

Next, divide
J
by

\displaystyle 2\pi iJ=\int_{\partial C}=

\displaystyle\int_{1+\epsilon-i\infty}^{1+\epsilon+i\infty}-\int_{1+\epsilon-i\infty}^{1+\epsilon-iV}-\int_{1+\epsilon+iV}^{1+\epsilon+i\infty}+\int_{1+\epsilon+iV}^{1/2+\epsilon+iV}+\int_{1/2+\epsilon+iV}^{1/2+\epsilon-iV}+\int_{1/2+\epsilon-iV}^{1+\epsilon-iV}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle J_{1}-J_{2}-J_{3}+J_{4}+J_{5}+J_{6}.

Due to (
1.1
), we can bound
J_{2},\dots,J_{6}
by

\displaystyle J_{2},J_{3}

\displaystyle\ll_{\epsilon}\int_{V}^{\infty}|u|^{\epsilon}\frac{x^{1-\sigma}}{(u-T)^{2}+1}du\ll V^{-1+\epsilon}x^{1-\sigma},

(2.2)

\displaystyle J_{4},J_{6}

\displaystyle\ll_{\epsilon}V^{1+\epsilon}x^{1-\sigma}(V-T)^{-2}\ll V^{-1}x^{1-\sigma},

(2.3)

\displaystyle J_{5}

\displaystyle\ll_{\epsilon}\int_{-V}^{V}|u|^{1+\epsilon}\frac{x^{1/2-\sigma}}{(u-T)^{2}+1}du\ll_{\epsilon}T^{1+\epsilon}x^{1/2-\sigma}.

(2.4)

The remaining term
J_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}J_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathrm{Prim}(\Gamma),j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{\Gamma}(\gamma^{j})N(\gamma)^{-js}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1-s+\epsilon-i\infty}^{1-s+\epsilon+i\infty}\frac{(x/N(\gamma)^{j})^{z}}{z(z+1)}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathrm{Prim}(\Gamma),j\geq 1\\
N(\gamma)^{j}<x\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{\Gamma}(\gamma^{j})\left(1-\frac{N(\gamma)^{j}}{x}\right)N(\gamma)^{-js}=\varphi_{s}(x).

(2.5)

The proposition follows from (2.1)–(2.5) immediately. ∎

3
Length spectrum

Since
N(\gamma)^{j/2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{tr}{\gamma^{j}}+\sqrt{(\mathrm{tr}{\gamma^{j}})^{2}-4}\right)
,
the function
\varphi_{s}(x)

can be expressed by

\displaystyle\varphi_{s}(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathrm{Tr}{(\Gamma)}\\
n<X\end{subarray}}m_{\Gamma}(n)\Lambda(n,x)\left(1-\frac{\epsilon(n)^{2}}{x}\right)\epsilon(n)^{-2s}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathrm{Tr}{(\Gamma)}\\
n<X\end{subarray}}m_{\Gamma}(n)\bar{\Lambda}(n,x)\epsilon(n)^{-2s},

(3.1)

where

\displaystyle X:=

\displaystyle x^{1/2}+x^{-1/2},\hskip 96.0pt\epsilon(n):=\frac{1}{2}(n+\sqrt{n^{2}-4}),

\displaystyle\bar{\Lambda}(n):=

\displaystyle\frac{2\log{\epsilon(n)}}{1-\epsilon(n)^{-2}},\hskip 96.0pt\bar{\Lambda}(n,x):=\frac{2\log{\epsilon(n)}}{1-\epsilon(n)^{-2}}\left(1-\frac{\epsilon(n)^{2}}{x}\right),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\Gamma):=

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{tr}{\gamma^{j}}\hskip 3.0pt|\hskip 3.0pt\gamma\in\mathrm{Prim}(\Gamma),j\geq 1\},\qquad m_{\Gamma}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathrm{Prim}(\Gamma),j\geq 1\\
\mathrm{tr}{\gamma^{j}}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{j}.

In this section, we study
\mathrm{Tr}(\Gamma)
and
m_{\Gamma}(n)
,
which gives a variant of the length spectrum on
\Gamma\backslash H
,
to understand
\varphi_{s}(x)
in detail.
When
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, it is known that
\mathrm{Tr}(\Gamma)=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 3}
and

m_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(n)
is written in the term of
primitive indefinite binary quadratic forms (see e.g.
[
7
,
20
]
),
since there is a deep connection between
\mathrm{Prim}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
and
equivalence classes of binary quadratic forms.

Let

Q(x,y)=[a,b,c]:=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}

be a binary quadratic form over
\mathbb{Z}
with
a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\gcd(a,b,c)=1
,
and
D=D(Q):=b^{2}-4ac
the discriminant of
[a,b,c]
.
We call that two quadratic forms
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are equivalent (
Q\sim Q^{\prime}
)
if there exists
g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
Q(x,y)=Q^{\prime}\big((x,y).g\big)
.
Denote by
h(D)
the number of equivalence classes of the quadratic forms of given
D=b^{2}-4ac
.
It is known that, if
D>0
, then there are infinitely many positive solutions
(t,u)

of the Pell equation
t^{2}-Du^{2}=4
.
Put
(t_{j},u_{j})=(t_{j}(D),u_{j}(D))
the
j
-th positive solution of
t^{2}-Du^{2}=4

and
\epsilon_{j}(D):=\frac{1}{2}(t_{j}(D)+u_{j}(D)\sqrt{D})
.
Note that
\epsilon_{1}(D)
is called the fundamental unit of
D
in the narrow sense,
and it holds that
\epsilon_{j}(D)=\epsilon_{1}(D)^{j}
.
For a quadratic form
Q=[a,b,c]
and a positive solution
(t,u)
of
t^{2}-Du^{2}=4
, let

\displaystyle\gamma\big(Q,(t,u)\big):=\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{t+bu}{2}&-cu\\
au&\displaystyle\frac{t-bu}{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

(3.2)

Conversely, for
\gamma=(\gamma_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we put

\displaystyle\begin{split}&t_{\gamma}:=\gamma_{11}+\gamma_{22},\quad u_{\gamma}:=\gcd{(\gamma_{21},\gamma_{11}-\gamma_{22},-\gamma_{12})},\\
&a_{\gamma}:=\frac{\gamma_{21}}{u_{\gamma}},\quad b_{\gamma}:=\frac{\gamma_{11}-\gamma_{22}}{u_{\gamma}},\quad c_{\gamma}:=-\frac{\gamma_{12}}{u_{\gamma}},\\
&Q_{\gamma}:=[a_{\gamma},b_{\gamma},c_{\gamma}],\quad D_{\gamma}:=\frac{t_{\gamma}^{2}-4}{u_{\gamma}^{2}}=b_{\gamma}^{2}-4a_{\gamma}c_{\gamma}.\end{split}

(3.3)

It is known that (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) give a one-to-one correspondence between
equivalence classes of primitive indefinite binary quadratic forms with
D>0

and primitive hyperbolic conjugacy classes of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

(see
[
20
]
and Chap. 5 in
[
7
]
).
Then
m_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(n)
is described as follows
[
20
,
8
]
.

\displaystyle m_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(n)=\sum_{u\in U(n)}\frac{1}{j_{n,u}}h\left(D_{n,u}\right),

(3.4)

where
\displaystyle D_{n,u}:=\frac{n^{2}-4}{u^{2}}
,

U(n):=\{u\geq 1\hskip 3.0pt|\hskip 3.0ptu^{2}\mid n^{2}-4,D_{n,u}\equiv 0,1\bmod{4}\}
and

j=j_{u,n}\geq 1
is an integer with

\epsilon_{j}\left(D_{n,u}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(n+\sqrt{n^{2}-4}\right)
.

According to Arakawa-Koyama-Nakasuji’s work
[
2
]
,
we see that
\mathrm{Tr}{(\Gamma)}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 3}

and
m_{\Gamma}(n)
can be expressed as follows when
\Gamma
is quaternion.

\displaystyle m_{\Gamma}(n)=\sum_{u\in U(n)}\frac{1}{j_{n,u}}h\left(D_{n,u}\right)\lambda_{\Gamma}(D_{n,u}),

(3.5)

where the constant
\lambda_{\Gamma}(D)\geq 0
is bounded by
the constant depending on
\Gamma
, not on
D

(see
[
2
]
for
\lambda_{\Gamma}(D)
in detail).

Furthermore, when
\Gamma
is a subgroup of
\bar{\Gamma}=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
or
\Gamma_{\mathcal{O}}
with

[\bar{\Gamma},\Gamma]<\infty
,
we see that
\mathrm{Tr}{(\Gamma)}\subset\mathrm{Tr}{(\bar{\Gamma})}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 3}

and we can obtain the following formula from
Venkov-Zograf’s formula
[
22
]
.

\displaystyle m_{\Gamma}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathrm{Prim}(\bar{\Gamma}),j\geq 1\\
\mathrm{tr}{\gamma^{j}}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{j}\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(\mathrm{Ind}_{\Gamma}^{\bar{\Gamma}}1\right)(\gamma^{j})\right).

Since
0\leq\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(\mathrm{Ind}_{\Gamma}^{\bar{\Gamma}}1\right)(\gamma^{j})\right)\leq[\bar{\Gamma}:\Gamma]
,
we have

\displaystyle 0\leq m_{\Gamma}(n)\leq[\bar{\Gamma}:\Gamma]m_{\bar{\Gamma}}(n).

(3.6)

Due to (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
),
we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

When
\Gamma
is a subgroup of the modular group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
or a quaternion
\Gamma_{\mathcal{O}}
,
we have

\displaystyle m_{\Gamma}(n)\ll_{\epsilon,\Gamma}n^{1+\epsilon},\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(3.7)

Proof.

According to (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
),
we see that
0\leq m_{\Gamma}(n)\leq\alpha_{\Gamma}m_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(n)
holds
for some constant
\alpha_{\Gamma}
.
Then checking (
3.7
) only for
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is enough to prove Lemma
3.1
.

For the discriminant
D>0
,
the following class number formula holds (see e.g.
[
6
]
).

h(D)=\frac{\sqrt{D}}{\log{\epsilon_{1}{(D)}}}L(1,\chi_{D}),

where
L(z,\chi_{D}):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\frac{D}{n}\right)n^{-z}
(
\mathrm{Re}{z}\geq 1
)
is the Dirichlet
L
function.
It is well-known that

L(1,\chi_{D})\ll_{\epsilon}D^{\epsilon}
as
D\to\infty
,
and
\epsilon_{1}(D)\geq\epsilon_{1}(5)=\frac{1}{2}(3+\sqrt{5})
.
We thus obtain

\displaystyle m_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(n)\ll_{\epsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\geq 1\\
u^{2}\mid n^{2}-4\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{n^{2}-4}{u^{2}}\right)^{1/2+\epsilon}\ll_{\epsilon}n^{1+\epsilon},\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(3.8)

∎

Remark that (
3.7
) is not far from the best possible upper bound of
m_{\Gamma}(n)
.
In fact, the asymptotic formula

\displaystyle\sum_{n<x}m_{\Gamma}(n)^{k}\sim c_{\Gamma}^{(k)}\frac{x^{k+1}}{(\log{x})^{k}},\quad\text{as}\quad x\to\infty

has been given for some constant
c_{\Gamma}^{(k)}>0

when
\Gamma
is quaternion and
k=2
,
and when
\Gamma
is a congruence subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and any
k\geq 2

[
5
,
19
,
17
,
8
]
.
This means that the growth of
m_{\Gamma}(n)
is averagely close to
\displaystyle\frac{n}{\log{n}}

and then (
3.7
) works well in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

4
Proof of Theorem
1.1

According to Proposition
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}\left|\frac{Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\sigma+it)}{Z_{\Gamma}(\sigma+it)}\right|^{2}dt=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}|\varphi_{\sigma+it}(x)|^{2}dt+O(T^{-1}x^{2-2\sigma})+O(T^{2}x^{1-2\sigma+\epsilon}).

(4.1)

We now study the first term in the right hand side.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}|\varphi_{\sigma+it}(x)|^{2}dt=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
3\leq n_{1},n_{2}<X\end{subarray}}m(n_{1})m(n_{2})\bar{\Lambda}(n_{1},x)\bar{\Lambda}(n_{2},x)\epsilon(n_{1})^{-2\sigma}\epsilon(n_{2})^{-2\sigma}\frac{1}{T}\left|\int_{1}^{T}\left(\frac{\epsilon(n_{1})}{\epsilon(n_{2})}\right)^{-2it}dt\right|.

Divide the sum in the right hand side above by

\sum_{n_{1}=n_{2}}+\sum_{n_{1}\neq n_{2}}=:S_{1}+S_{2}
.
The first sum
S_{1}
is as follows.

\displaystyle S_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq n<X\end{subarray}}m_{\Gamma}(n)^{2}\bar{\Lambda}(n,x)^{2}\epsilon(n)^{-4\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq n<X\end{subarray}}m_{\Gamma}(n)^{2}\bar{\Lambda}(n)^{2}\epsilon(n)^{-4\sigma}+x^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq n<X\end{subarray}}m_{\Gamma}(n)^{2}\bar{\Lambda}(n)^{2}\left(\frac{\epsilon(n)^{2}}{x}-2\right)\epsilon(n)^{2-4\sigma}.

Since
m_{\Gamma}(n)\ll n^{1+\epsilon}
,

\epsilon(n)=n+O(n^{-1})
and
|\bar{\Lambda}(n)|\ll\log{n}
,
we have

\displaystyle S_{1}\ll_{\epsilon}\sum_{3\leq n<X}n^{2-4\sigma+\epsilon}+x^{-1}\sum_{3\leq n<X}n^{4-4\sigma+\epsilon}\ll_{\epsilon}X^{\max(0,3-4\sigma+\epsilon)}\ll x^{\max(0,\frac{3}{2}-2\sigma+\epsilon)}

and, especially when
\sigma>3/4
, it converges as
x\to\infty
by

\lim_{x\to\infty}S_{1}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 3\end{subarray}}m_{\Gamma}(n)^{2}\bar{\Lambda}(n)^{2}\epsilon(n)^{-4\sigma}.

The remaining part of the proof is the estimation of
S_{2}
.
Since

\int_{1}^{T}\left(\frac{\epsilon(n_{1})}{\epsilon(n_{2})}\right)^{-2it}dt\ll\frac{1}{\left|\log{\frac{\epsilon(n_{1})}{\epsilon(n_{2})}}\right|}\ll\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}-n_{1}},&(n_{1}<n_{2}<2n_{1}),\\
1,&(n_{2}\geq n_{1}),\end{cases}

we have

\displaystyle S_{2}\ll

\displaystyle T^{-1}\sum_{3\leq n_{1}<X}m_{\Gamma}(n_{1})\bar{\Lambda}(n_{1},x)\epsilon(n_{1})^{-2\sigma}\sum_{n_{1}<n_{2}<X}m_{\Gamma}(n_{2})\bar{\Lambda}(n_{2},x)\epsilon(n_{2})^{-2\sigma}\frac{1}{\left|\log{\frac{\epsilon(n_{1})}{\epsilon(n_{2})}}\right|}

\displaystyle\ll_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle T^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\in\mathrm{Tr}(\Gamma)\\
2<n_{1}<X\end{subarray}}n_{1}^{1-2\sigma+\epsilon}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{2}\in\mathrm{Tr}(\Gamma)\\
n_{1}<n_{2}<2n_{1}\end{subarray}}n_{2}^{1-2\sigma+\epsilon}\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}-n_{1}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{2}\in\mathrm{Tr}(\Gamma)\\
2n_{1}\leq n_{2}<X\end{subarray}}n_{2}^{1-2\sigma+\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle T^{-1}X^{4-4\sigma+\epsilon}\ll T^{-1}x^{2-2\sigma+\epsilon}.

(4.2)

We thus obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}\left|\frac{Z^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\sigma+it)}{Z_{\Gamma}(\sigma+it)}\right|^{2}dt\ll_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle x^{\max(0,\frac{3}{2}-2\sigma+\epsilon)}+T^{-1}x^{2-2\sigma}+T^{2}x^{1-2\sigma+\epsilon}.

The main theorem immediately follows from the above with
x=T^{3}
,
and the constant
C_{\Gamma}(\sigma)
for
\frac{5}{6}<\sigma<1
is given by

C_{\Gamma}(\sigma)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 3\end{subarray}}m_{\Gamma}(n)^{2}\bar{\Lambda}(n)^{2}\epsilon(n)^{-4\sigma}.

∎

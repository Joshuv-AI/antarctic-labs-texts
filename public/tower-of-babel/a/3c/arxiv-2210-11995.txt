(48)

\displaystyle\hskip 75.0001pt+4\text{Re}[\bar{C}_{+}C_{+}^{*}-\bar{C}_{-}C_{-}^{*}]\sinh(y\Gamma t)\Big]\,,

3.3
Time-integrated observables in the photon-pole approximation

For incoherent production (for instance at LHCb, but also
Z
-factories like FCCee or CEPC), it proves interesting to consider the time-integrated observables
S_{i}
and
A_{i}
, defined by integrating over time the numerator and the denominator defining each ratio, i.e.

X=\frac{N}{D}\,,\qquad\mathrm{with}\qquad\langle X\rangle_{\rm inc}=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ N}{\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ D}\,.

(49)

As discussed in Refs.
[
46
,
47
]
, the same integral would range from
-\infty
to
\infty
in the case of a coherent production, washing away mixing-induced terms. This does not occur in the case of an incoherent production, allowing one to probe mixing-induced terms through time-integrated measurements. Due to the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
singular behaviour of the branching ratio in the denominator defining
S_{i}
and
A_{i}
,

\small\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ \frac{d(\Gamma+\bar{\Gamma})}{dq^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{\Gamma}\alpha_{-1/2}|N^{\prime}|^{2}\frac{2}{1-y^{2}}\left[[|C_{+}|^{2}+|C_{-}|^{2}+|\bar{C}_{+}|^{2}+|\bar{C}_{-}|^{2}]+y\cdot 2\text{Re}[\bar{C}_{+}C_{+}^{*}-\bar{C}_{-}C_{-}^{*}]\right]\,,

(50)

the observables corresponding associated with subleading numerators
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1},\epsilon^{0})
turn out to vanish in the photon-pole approximation

\langle S_{i}\rangle_{\mathrm{inc}}\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\langle A_{i}\rangle_{\mathrm{inc}}\to 0\qquad\text{for}\quad i\in\{1c,2c,4,5,6s,6c,7,8\}\,,

(51)

whereas the non-vanishing observables are
S_{1s}
,
S_{2s}
,
S_{3}
and
S_{9}
, as well as
A_{1s}
,
A_{2s}
,
A_{3}
and
A_{9}
, with the corresponding expressions collected in Appendix

8

8

We recall that

are accessible through an untagged measurement at LHCb, but that is not possible for
A_{1s},A_{2s},A_{3},S_{9}
.
. By using these expressions, we can show that the considered transverse asymmetries at low

will be

\displaystyle\langle A_{T}^{(2)}\rangle_{\rm inc}

\displaystyle\frac{[|C_{+}|^{2}+|\bar{C}_{+}|^{2}-|C_{-}|^{2}-|\bar{C}_{-}|^{2}]+y\cdot 2\text{Re}[\bar{C}_{+}C_{+}^{*}+\bar{C}_{-}C_{-}^{*}]}{[|C_{+}|^{2}+|\bar{C}_{+}|^{2}+|C_{-}|^{2}+|\bar{C}_{-}|^{2}]+y\cdot 2\text{Re}[{\bar{C}_{+}C_{+}^{*}-\bar{C}_{-}C_{-}^{*}]}}\,,

3.4
Constraints on
{\cal C}_{7}
and
{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}
near the SM point

We can derive further simplified expressions in the vicinity of the SM. We will assume that we can neglect the
c\bar{c}
contributions
h_{\pm}(0)
and that the NP corrections to
{\cal C}_{7^{(^{\prime})}}
may carry weak phases but do not modify the SM hierarchy (i.e. we do not consider scenarios with very large right-handed currents). We can then expand in
\eta=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
, counting

y\sim 1/x\sim{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}/{\cal C}_{7}=\mathcal{O}(\eta)
and keep only
\mathcal{O}(\eta)
contributions. We obtain:

\displaystyle\langle S_{1s}\rangle_{\rm inc}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\frac{\alpha_{-1/3}}{\alpha_{-1/2}}\,,\qquad\qquad\langle S_{2s}\rangle_{\rm inc}\sim\frac{1}{4}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{-1/2}}\,,

(55)

\displaystyle\langle S_{3}\rangle_{\rm inc}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{-1/2}}\frac{1}{(\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}+(\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}}

(56)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}]\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}]+\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}]\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}]+\frac{y}{2}[(\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}-(\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}]\right]\,,

\displaystyle\langle S_{9}\rangle_{\rm inc}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{-1/2}}\frac{1}{x}\frac{(\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}-(\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}}{(\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}+(\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}}\,,

(57)

and

\displaystyle\langle A_{1s}\rangle_{\rm inc}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\langle A_{2s}\rangle_{\rm inc}\sim 0\,,

(58)

\displaystyle\langle A_{3}\rangle_{\rm inc}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{-1/2}}\frac{1}{x}\frac{\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}]\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}]}{(\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}+(\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}}\,,

(59)

\displaystyle\langle A_{9}\rangle_{\rm inc}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{-1/2}}\frac{\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}]\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}]-\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}]\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}]-y\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}]\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}]}{(\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}+(\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}}\,.

\displaystyle\langle A_{T}^{(2)}\rangle_{\rm inc}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}+(\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}}

(61)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}]\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}]+\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}]\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}]+\frac{y}{2}[(\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}-(\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}]\right]\,,

\displaystyle\langle A_{T}^{(\text{Re});CP}\rangle_{\rm inc}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle 0\,,

(62)

\displaystyle\langle A_{T}^{(\text{Im});CP}\rangle_{\rm inc}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}+(\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}}

(63)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big[\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}]\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}]-\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}]\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}]-y\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}]\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}]\Big]\,.

Within this limited framework, we already see that

•

Mixing effects do not impact
\langle S_{1c}\rangle_{\rm inc}
,
\langle S_{2c}\rangle_{\rm inc}
,
\langle A_{1c}\rangle_{\rm inc}
,
\langle A_{2c}\rangle_{\rm inc}
, which are linked to
s
and
h
coefficients probed directly by the time-evolution of the
B_{s}\to\phi\gamma
decay rate.

•

The contributions induced via mixing compete with the mixing-independent part for
\langle S_{3}\rangle_{\rm inc}
and

\langle A_{9}\rangle_{\rm inc}
, or equivalently
\smash{\langle A_{T}^{(2)}\rangle_{\rm inc}}
and

\smash{\langle A_{T}^{(\text{Im});CP}\rangle_{\rm inc}}
. Therefore, one should be careful in the interpretation of
\smash{\langle A_{T}^{(2)}\rangle_{\rm inc}}
and
\smash{\langle A_{T}^{(\text{Im});CP}\rangle_{\rm inc}}
to consider the non-negligible mixing-induced effects to obtain the correct constraints on
{\cal C}_{7}
and
{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}
. In particular, this effect is already important for
\langle A_{T}^{(2)}\rangle_{\rm inc}
in the SM.

•

Mixing effects become the dominant contributions (suppressed by
1/x
) in
\langle A_{3}\rangle_{\rm inc}
and
\langle S_{9}\rangle_{\rm inc}
, or equivalently
\smash{\langle A_{T}^{(2);CP}\rangle_{\rm inc}}
and
\smash{\langle A_{T}^{(\text{Im})}\rangle_{\rm inc}}
, as discussed in Appendix
D
. Their deviations from zero would provide information on the imaginary part of
{\cal C}_{7}
and
{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}
that is complementary to other quantities. More specifically, comparing Eqs. (
93
)-(
95
) in Appendix
D
and
Eqs. (
61
)-(
63

\langle A_{T}^{(2)}\rangle_{\rm inc}
and
\langle A_{T}^{(\text{Im});CP}\rangle_{\rm inc}
in this limit.

The difference between
A_{T}^{(2)}
for
B_{s}\to\phi ee
and
B\to K^{*}ee

(and similarly
A_{T}^{(\text{Im});CP}
)
can provide additional constraints on
{\cal C}_{7}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{T}^{(2)}&\equiv\langle A_{T}^{(2)}\rangle_{\rm inc}(B_{s}\to\phi ee)-\langle A_{T}^{(2)}\rangle_{\rm inc}(B\to K^{*}ee)\\[2.5pt]
&=y\dfrac{(\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}-(\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}}{(\text{Re}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}+(\text{Im}[{\cal C}_{7}])^{2}}\,,\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{T}^{(\text{Im});CP}&\equiv\langle A_{T}^{(\text{Im});CP}\rangle_{\rm inc}(B_{s}\to\phi ee)-\langle A_{T}^{(\text{Im});CP}\rangle_{\rm inc}(B\to K^{*}ee)\\[2.5pt]

and
\smash{\langle A_{T}^{(2);CP}\rangle_{\rm inc}}

Clearly, the above interpretation changes if one allows significantly larger NP contributions to
{\cal C}_{7}
and/or
{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}

\smash{\langle A_{T}^{(2)}\rangle_{\rm inc}}
and
\smash{\langle A_{T}^{(\text{Im});CP}\rangle_{\rm inc}}

It also suggests that
\smash{\langle A_{T}^{(2);CP}\rangle_{\rm inc}}
and
\smash{\langle A_{T}^{(\text{Im})}\rangle_{\rm inc}}

\langle A_{3}\rangle_{\rm inc}

{\cal C}_{7}

5
Conclusion

In the context of
b\to s\ell\ell
anomalies, it is particularly important to analyse the related
b\to s\gamma
decay
which are expected to be particularly sensitive to New Physics contributions modifying the short-distance Wilson coefficients
{\cal C}_{7}
and
{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}
, describing this decay in the Low-Energy Effective Field Theory at the scale of the
b
-quark mass. Among several decays modes and approaches, one can use
B_{s}\to\phi ee
at low
q^{2}
, in a kinematic range where the photon pole dominates. In this range, the longitudinal amplitude gets suppressed as well as many angular observables, compared to the angular observables involving only transverse amplitudes (i.e.,
J_{i}
with
i=1s,2s,3,9
).

The presence of
B_{s}-\bar{B}_{s}
mixing affects the theoretical description of the angular observables that can be determined from this mode. A time-dependent modulation arises and involves interference terms between mixing and decay which are sensitive to the relative moduli and phase of
{\cal C}_{7}
and
{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}
. Therefore, these interference terms open an interesting window to constrain not only the size of the Wilson coefficients, but also their (weak) phases. We exploited the results of Ref.
[
46
]
in the photon-pole dominance approximation to determine simplified expressions of these interference terms, associated with the time dependence of the angular coefficients
J_{1s}
,
J_{2s}
,
J_{3}
and
J_{9}
. In the photon-pole approximation, the time-dependent terms associated with
J_{1s}
and
J_{2s}
contain the same information as the CP asymmetries that can be built for
B_{s}\to\phi\gamma
, whereas the other angular observables feature mixing-induced time-dependence that can be exploited to probe complementary combinations of Wilson coefficients.

These interference terms can be extracted through a time-dependent analysis at Belle II. We also demonstrated that they affect time-integrated observables at LHCb and, more generally, at any collider producing
b\bar{b}
pairs incoherently. Within the approximation where the photon-pole dominates and assuming that NP does not alter the overall hierarchy between
{\cal C}_{7}
and
{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}
, we showed that the time-integrated value of
A_{T}^{(2)}
and

A_{T}^{(\text{Im});CP}

receive terms of similar size from mixing-independent and mixing-induced terms, whereas the other usual transverse asymmetries are not significantly affected. Moreover, we discussed briefly the three time-integrated transverse asymmetries that can be measured in
d\Gamma-d\bar{\Gamma}
, and we pointed out that two of them, corresponding to
A_{T}^{(2);CP}
and
A_{T}^{(\text{Im})}
,
are dominated by neutral-meson mixing at low
q^{2}
and thus require a specific interpretation in terms of the Wilson coefficients of the low-energy effective field theory.

Finally, we have considered several NP scenarios involving weak phases for
{\cal C}_{7}
and/or
{\cal C}_{7^{\prime}}
. We showed that
A_{T}^{(2)}
and
\smash{A_{T}^{(\text{Im});CP}}
measured at low
q^{2}
could indeed provide interesting constraints, and that it was important to take into account mixing-induced contributions for the foreseen time-integrated measurements at LHCb.
In particular, mixing effects are noticeable for
\smash{A_{T}^{(2)}}
already within the SM.
The
\smash{A_{T}^{(\text{Re});CP}}
transverse asymmetry has not been considered in this discussion, as it is mainly sensitive weak phases in
{\cal C}_{10^{(^{\prime})}}
. Even though we have not discussed this third asymmetry in detail given our choices of NP scenarios, it is worth stressing that such a measurement would provide complementary information to
\smash{A_{T}^{(2)}}
and
\smash{A_{T}^{(\text{Im});CP}}
.

Given that LHCb has already performed angular analyses of
B_{s}\to\phi\mu\mu
, as well as
B\to K^{*}ee
at low
q^{2}
, one may hope that the angular analysis of
B_{s}\to\phi ee
at low
q^{2}
will be soon available in a time-integrated form, requiring
to include the effects of neutral-meson mixing for their theoretical interpretation. On a longer term, Belle II could measure directly the time dependence of this decay to extract the modulation terms
s_{i}
and
h_{i}
, which are not affected by strong phases. Both types of observables could provide very useful additional information on
b\to s\gamma
decays, helping us to determine if they are significantly affected by New Physics contributions and if they can contribute to the ongoing debate on the nature of the
b
-quark anomalies.

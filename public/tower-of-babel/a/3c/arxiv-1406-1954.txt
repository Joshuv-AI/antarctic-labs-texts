Notes on sphere-based universal extra dimensions
Hideto Dohi, Takuya Kakuda, Kenji Nishiwaki,Kin-ya Oda, and Naoya Okuda

Abstract
We review the six dimensional universal extra dimension models compactified on the sphere S 2 S^{2} , the orbifold S 2 / Z 2 S^{2}/Z_{2} , and the projective sphere, which are based on the spontaneous compactification mechanism on the sphere. In particular, we spell out the application of the Newman-Penrose eth-formalism on these models with some technical details on the derivation of the Kaluza-Klein modes and their interactions, and revisit the problem in the existence of the zero mode of U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} additional gauge boson required for the spontaneous compactification. We also explain the theoretical background on the vacuum stability argument for the upper bound on the ultraviolet cutoff scale.

2.1
Spontaneous compactification

Let us first review the spontaneous compactification mechanism on two-sphere
S^{2}

[
39
]
. The starting metric ansatz is:

\displaystyle{\text{d}s}^{2}

\displaystyle=g_{MN}\text{d}z^{M}\text{d}z^{N}=\eta_{\mu\nu}\text{d}x^{\mu}\text{d}x^{\nu}+R^{2}\left({\text{d}\theta}^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,{\text{d}\phi}^{2}\right),

(1)

where
\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}\right)_{\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3}=\diag\left(-1,1,1,1\right)
.
Throughout this paper, lower case greek indices
\mu,\nu,\dots
run for
0,1,2,3
, while upper case roman ones
M,N,\dots
for
0,1,2,3,\theta,\phi
.

By this metric, the vacuum Einstein equation cannot be satisfied except for a trivial solution with
R\to\infty
. In order to stabilize the radius
R
, Randjbar-Daemi, Salam and Strathdee have introduced a classical monopole configuration of a
U(1)_{X}
gauge field
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{M}\text{d}z^{M}
in the
S^{2}
extra dimensions
[
63
,
39
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{{N\atop S}}

\displaystyle={n\over 2g_{X}}\left(\cos\theta\mp 1\right)\text{d}\phi,

(2)

where the integer
n
is the monopole number and
g_{X}
is the
U(1)_{X}
coupling constant.
Throughout this paper, superscripts
N
and
S
stand for north and south charts containing
\theta=0
and
\pi
, respectively, and correlate with
\pm
signs when indicated.
The transition function for the
U(1)_{X}
gauge field is

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{S}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=U_{t}\left(\mathcal{X}^{N}_{\phi}-{i\over g_{X}}\partial_{\phi}\right)U^{\dagger}_{t},

(3)

with
U_{t}=e^{-in\phi}
.

The field strength
\mathcal{X}_{MN}:=\partial_{M}\mathcal{X}_{N}-\partial_{N}\mathcal{X}_{M}
reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\theta\phi}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{X}_{\phi\theta}=-{n\over 2g_{X}}\sin\theta,

others

\displaystyle=0.

(4)

The energy momentum tensor
T_{MN}=-{1\over 4}g_{MN}F_{KL}F^{KL}+F_{MK}F_{N}{}^{K}
becomes

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-{n^{2}\over 8g_{X}^{2}R^{4}}\eta_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle T_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle={n^{2}\over 8g_{X}^{2}R^{2}},

\displaystyle T_{\phi\phi}

\displaystyle={n^{2}\over 8g_{X}^{2}R^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta,

others

\displaystyle=0.

(5)

Under this monopole configuration, the Einstein equation can be satisfied by tuning the cosmological constant, and the radius is fixed to be

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle={\sqrt{8\pi G_{6}}\left|n\right|\over 2g_{X}},

(6)

where
G_{6}
is the six dimensional (6D) Newton constant.

As the volume of the extra dimension is
4\pi R^{2}
, the 4D Planck scale and gauge coupling become
M_{P}=1/\sqrt{8\pi G}=\sqrt{4\pi}R/\sqrt{8\pi G_{6}}
(
=2.4\times 10^{18}\,\text{GeV}

g_{X4}=\left|n\right|/2RM_{P}

g_{X4}\sim 10^{-15}\left|n\right|

2.2
Newman-Penrose eth formalism

Let us review the Newman-Penrose eth-formalism to get the spin-weighted spherical harmonics
[
67
]
.
Consider a rotation by an angle
\alpha
of an orthonormal basis of the tangent space at a point on
S^{2}
.
A quantity
\eta
has spin weight
s
if it transforms under the rotation as

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=e^{is\alpha}\eta.

(7)

If
\eta
has a spin weight
s
, its complex conjugate
\bar{\eta}
has spin weight
-s
.
A product of two quantities with spin weights
s
and
s^{\prime}
has the spin weight
s+s^{\prime}
.
A derivative of a quantity with a definite spin weight may not have a well-defined spin weight.
However if
\eta
has a spin weight
s
, the following quantities have well-defined spin weights

\displaystyle\text{\dh}\eta

\displaystyle=-\left(\sin\theta\right)^{s}\left[\left(\bm{e}_{\theta}+i\bm{e}_{\phi}\right)\cdot\bm{L}\right](\sin\theta)^{-s}\eta=-\left[{\partial\over\partial\theta}+i\csc\theta{\partial\over\partial\phi}-s\cot\theta\right]\eta,

(8)

\displaystyle\overline{\text{\dh}}\eta

\displaystyle=+\left(\sin\theta\right)^{-s}\left[\left(\bm{e}_{\theta}-i\bm{e}_{\phi}\right)\cdot\bm{L}\right](\sin\theta)^{s}\eta=-\left[{\partial\over\partial\theta}-i\csc\theta{\partial\over\partial\phi}+s\cot\theta\right]\eta,

(9)

where

\displaystyle\bm{L}

\displaystyle:=-i\left(-\bm{e}_{\phi}{\partial\over\partial\theta}+\bm{e}_{\theta}\csc\theta{\partial\over\partial\phi}\right),

(10)

is the angular momentum operator.
ð
and
\overline{\text{\dh}}
are read “eth” and “eth bar”, respectively.
One can find that
\text{\dh}\eta
has spin weight
s+1
and
\overline{\text{\dh}}\eta
has
s-1
.
That is,
ð
(
\overline{\text{\dh}}
) raise (lower) the spin weight by unity.

Using eth(-bar) operators, spherical harmonic
Y_{jm}
can be generalized to the spin-weighted spherical harmonics:

\displaystyle{}_{s}Y_{jm}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\sqrt{{\left(j-s\right)!\over\left(j+s\right)!}}\,\text{\dh}^{s}\,Y_{jm}&\text{for $0\leq s\leq j$},\\
\left(-1\right)^{s}\sqrt{{\left(j+s\right)!\over\left(j-s\right)!}}\,\overline{\text{\dh}}^{-s}\,Y_{jm}&\text{for $-j\leq s\leq 0$}.\end{cases}

(11)

The (spin-weighted) spherical harmonics
Y_{jm}
(
{}_{s}Y_{jm}
) has a spin weight
0
(
s
).
The concrete form of spin-weighted spherical hamonics is

\displaystyle{}_{s}Y_{jm}

\displaystyle=\left(-1\right)^{m}\sqrt{{2j+1\over 4\pi}\left(j+m\right)!\,\left(j-m\right)!\,\left(j+s\right)!\,\left(j-s\right)!}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{k=\mathrm{max}\{0,\,-m-s\}}^{\mathrm{min}\{j-s,\,j-m\}}{\left(-1\right)^{k}\left(\sin\frac{1}{2}\theta\right)^{m+s+2k}\left(\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta\right)^{2j-m-s-2k}\over k!\left(j-m-k\right)!\,\left(j-s-k\right)!\,\left(m+s+k\right)!}e^{im\phi}.

(12)

We see that this expression for
{}_{s}Y_{jm}
properly reduces to the ordinary spherical harmonics
Y_{jm}
for
s=0
.
Note that for a fixed
s
,
\left\{{}_{s}Y_{jm}\right\}
form a complete orthonormal basis on sphere
S^{2}
and any function with spin weight
s
defined on it can be expanded by them.
The inner product of two spin-weighted spherical harmonics satisfies the following orthonormality condition:

\displaystyle\int\text{d}\Omega\,\left[{}_{s}Y_{jm}(\theta,\phi)\right]^{*}\,{}_{s}Y_{j^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}},

(13)

where
\text{d}\Omega:=\sin\theta\,\text{d}\theta\,\text{d}\phi
. Following relations are useful:

\displaystyle\left[{}_{s}Y_{jm}(\theta,\phi)\right]^{*}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(m+s)}\ {}_{-s}Y_{j,-m}(\theta,\phi),

(14)

\displaystyle{}_{s}Y_{jm}(\pi-\theta,\phi+\pi)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j}\ {}_{-s}Y_{jm}(\theta,\phi)=(-1)^{j-s+m}\ \left[{}_{s}Y_{j,-m}(\theta,\phi)\right]^{*},

(15)

\displaystyle{}_{s}Y_{jm}(\pi-\theta,-\pi)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j-s}\ {}_{s}Y_{j,-m}(\theta,\phi),

(16)

\displaystyle{}_{s}Y_{jm}(0,\phi)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{for $m\neq-s$},\\
(-1)^{-s}\sqrt{{2j+1\over 4\pi}}e^{-is\phi}&\text{for $m=-s$},\end{cases}

(17)

\displaystyle{}_{s}Y_{jm}(\pi,\phi)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{for $m\neq s$},\\
(-1)^{j}\sqrt{{2j+1\over 4\pi}}e^{is\phi}&\text{for $m=s$}.\end{cases}

(18)

In particular for the
s=0
mode,

\displaystyle\int\text{d}\Omega\,Y_{jm}(\theta,\phi)\,Y_{j^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\delta_{m+m^{\prime}},

(19)

where
\delta_{m+m^{\prime}}
follows the notation:

\displaystyle\delta_{M}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&(M=0),\\
0&(M\neq 0).\end{cases}

(20)

Each renormalizable interaction term in the Lagrangian consists of three or four fields, a KK-expanded 4D interaction includes three or four spin-weighted spherical harmonics.
When calculating three and four point interactions, we use, respectively,

\displaystyle\int\,\text{d}\Omega\,{}_{s_{1}}Y_{j_{1}m_{1}}(\theta,\phi)\,{}_{s_{2}}Y_{j_{2}m_{2}}(\theta,\phi)\,{}_{s_{3}}Y_{j_{3}m_{3}}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle\quad=\sqrt{\frac{(2j_{1}+1)(2j_{2}+1)(2j_{3}+1)}{4\pi}}\begin{pmatrix}j_{1}&j_{2}&j_{3}\\
m_{1}&m_{2}&m_{3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}j_{1}&j_{2}&j_{3}\\
-s_{1}&-s_{2}&-s_{3}\end{pmatrix},

(21)

\displaystyle\int\,\text{d}\Omega\,{}_{s_{1}}Y_{j_{1}m_{1}}(\theta,\phi)\,{}_{s_{2}}Y_{j_{2}m_{2}}(\theta,\phi)\,{}_{s_{3}}Y_{j_{3}m_{3}}(\theta,\phi)\,{}_{s_{4}}Y_{j_{4}m_{4}}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{J=\lvert j_{1}-j_{2}\rvert}^{j_{1}+j_{2}}\frac{(-1)^{m_{1}+m_{2}+s_{1}+s_{2}}(2J+1)}{4\pi}\sqrt{(2j_{1}+1)(2j_{2}+1)(2j_{3}+1)(2j_{4}+1)}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\begin{pmatrix}j_{1}&j_{2}&J\\
m_{1}&m_{2}&-(m_{1}+m_{2})\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}j_{1}&j_{2}&J\\
-s_{1}&-s_{2}&s_{1}+s_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\begin{pmatrix}J&j_{3}&j_{4}\\
m_{1}+m_{2}&m_{3}&m_{4}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}J&j_{3}&j_{4}\\
-(s_{1}+s_{2})&-s_{3}&-s_{4}\end{pmatrix},

(22)

where all
j
,
m
and
s
are integers
and

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}j_{1}&j_{2}&j_{3}\\
m_{1}&m_{2}&m_{3}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2j_{3}+1}}(-1)^{j_{1}-j_{2}-m_{3}}\braket{j_{1}j_{2};m_{1}m_{2}|j_{3},-m_{3}},

(23)

is the Wigner’s
3j
symbol with
\braket{j_{1}j_{2};m_{1}m_{2}|j_{3},-m_{3}}
being the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient.
Further details can be found in Ref.
[
68
,
69
]
.

We also write
\text{\dh}_{s}
(
\overline{\text{\dh}}_{s}
) when we want to make explicit the spin weight
s
of the quantity on which the eth(-bar) operator act.
For quantities
\eta_{s}
and
\kappa_{s^{\prime}}
with spin weights
s
and
s^{\prime}
, respectively, we have

\displaystyle\text{\dh}_{s+s^{\prime}}\left(\eta_{s}\kappa_{s^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\eta_{s}\text{\dh}_{s^{\prime}}\kappa_{s^{\prime}}+\kappa_{s^{\prime}}\overline{\text{\dh}}_{s}\eta_{s}.

(24)

Let us define the following “K-operator”:

\displaystyle\textbf{K}_{s}

\displaystyle:=-\overline{\text{{\dh}}}_{s+1}\,\text{\dh}_{s}+s(s+1)=-\text{\dh}_{s-1}\,\overline{\text{{\dh}}}_{s}+s(s-1)

\displaystyle=-(\csc\theta\,\partial_{\theta}\sin\theta\,\partial_{\theta}+\csc^{2}\theta\,\partial^{2}_{\phi}+2is\csc\theta\cot\theta\,\partial_{\phi}-s^{2}\csc^{2}\theta),

(25)

which satisfies

\displaystyle\textbf{K}_{s}\,{}_{s}Y_{jm}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=j(j+1)\,{}_{s}Y_{jm}(\theta,\phi),

(26)

for
-{j}\leq m\leq{j}
.
More explicitly,

\displaystyle\text{\dh}_{s+1}\text{\dh}_{s}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\theta}^{2}-\left(2s+1\right)\cot\theta\,\partial_{\theta}+2i\csc\theta\,\partial_{\theta}\partial_{\phi}-2i\left(s+1\right)\csc\theta\cot\theta\,\partial_{\phi}

\displaystyle\quad-\csc^{2}\theta\,\partial_{\phi}^{2}+s\csc^{2}\theta+s\left(s+1\right)\cot^{2}\theta,

\displaystyle\overline{\text{{\dh}}}_{s-1}\overline{\text{{\dh}}}_{s}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\theta}^{2}+\left(2s-1\right)\cot\theta\,\partial_{\theta}-2i\csc\theta\,\partial_{\theta}\partial_{\phi}-2i\left(s-1\right)\csc\theta\cot\theta\,\partial_{\phi}

\displaystyle\quad-\csc^{2}\theta\,\partial_{\phi}^{2}-s\csc^{2}\theta+s\left(s-1\right)\cot^{2}\theta,

\displaystyle\overline{\text{{\dh}}}_{s+1}\text{\dh}_{s}

\displaystyle=\csc\theta\,\partial_{\theta}\sin\theta\,\partial_{\theta}+\csc^{2}\theta\,\partial^{2}_{\phi}+2is\csc\theta\cot\theta\,\partial_{\phi}-s^{2}\csc^{2}\theta+s(s+1),

\displaystyle\text{\dh}_{s-1}\overline{\text{{\dh}}}_{s}

\displaystyle=\csc\theta\,\partial_{\theta}\sin\theta\,\partial_{\theta}+\csc^{2}\theta\,\partial^{2}_{\phi}+2is\csc\theta\cot\theta\,\partial_{\phi}-s^{2}\csc^{2}\theta+s(s-1).

(27)

We also define generalized eth, eth-bar, and K operators for later use:

\displaystyle\text{\dh}_{s}^{\chi}\,\eta_{s}

\displaystyle:=-e^{-i(s+1)\chi}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+i\csc\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}-s\cot\theta\right]\left(e^{is\chi}\eta_{s}\right),

(28)

\displaystyle\overline{\text{{\dh}}}_{s}^{\chi}\,\eta_{s}

\displaystyle:=-e^{-i(s-1)\chi}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}-i\csc\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}+s\cot\theta\right]\left(e^{is\chi}\eta_{s}\right),

(29)

\displaystyle{\textbf{K}^{\chi}_{s}}

\displaystyle:=-\overline{\text{{\dh}}}^{\chi}_{s+1}\,\text{\dh}^{\chi}_{s}+s(s+1)=-\text{\dh}^{\chi}_{s-1}\,\overline{\text{{\dh}}}^{\chi}_{s}+s(s-1)

\displaystyle=-e^{-is\chi}\left(\csc\theta\,\partial_{\theta}\sin\theta\,\partial_{\theta}+\csc^{2}\theta\,\partial^{2}_{\phi}+2is\csc\theta\cot\theta\,\partial_{\phi}-s^{2}\csc^{2}\theta\right)e^{is\chi},

where

\displaystyle\textbf{K}^{\chi}_{s}\left({}_{s}Y_{jm}(\theta,\phi)\,e^{-is\,\chi(\theta,\phi)}\right)

\displaystyle=j(j+1)\left({}_{s}Y_{jm}(\theta,\phi)\,e^{-is\,\chi(\theta,\phi)}\right).

2.3
Six dimensional gauge theory

In general, the 6D action for a gauge field
\hat{A}=\hat{A}_{M}\,\text{d}z^{M}=\hat{A}_{M}^{a}T^{a}\,\text{d}z^{M}
is written as

\displaystyle S_{A}

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{6}z\,\sqrt{-g}\,\left[-{1\over 2}\tr\left(\hat{F}_{MN}\hat{F}^{MN}\right)\right],

(32)

where indices
M
,
N
, …are raised and lowered by the metric (
1
) and

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{MN}

\displaystyle=\partial_{M}\hat{A}_{N}-\partial_{N}\hat{A}_{M}+ig_{A}[\hat{A}_{M},\hat{A}_{N}],

(33)

with
g_{A}
being the 6D gauge coupling constant.
Throughout this paper, “
\tr
” is replaced by
1/2
for a
U(1)
case.
Note that since we are working in a torsion free space we have
\nabla_{M}\hat{A}_{N}-\nabla_{N}\hat{A}_{M}=\partial_{M}\hat{A}_{N}-\partial_{N}\hat{A}_{M}
, where
\nabla_{M}\hat{A}_{N}=\partial_{M}\hat{A}_{N}-\Gamma^{L}{}_{MN}\hat{A}_{L}
is the general covariant derivative.

Following the background field method, we separate the gauge field into the classical and quantum parts
\mathcal{A}
and
A
, respectively,
\hat{A}_{M}=\mathcal{A}_{M}+A_{M}
.
5
5

5

Throughout this paper, curly and normal letters denote a classical background and a quantum fluctuation, respectively.

We also define the classical field strength
\mathcal{F}_{MN}:=\partial_{M}\mathcal{A}_{N}-\partial_{N}\mathcal{A}_{M}+ig_{A}[\mathcal{A}_{M},\mathcal{A}_{N}]
and the background-covariant derivative
\mathcal{D}_{M}:=\partial_{M}+ig_{A}[\mathcal{A}_{M},\ \cdot\ ]
so that

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{MN}=\mathcal{F}_{MN}+\mathcal{D}_{M}A_{N}-\mathcal{D}_{N}A_{M}+ig_{A}[A_{M},A_{N}].

(34)

The actions linear and quadratic in the quantum part
A_{M}
become

\displaystyle S^{\text{linear}}_{A}

\displaystyle=-\int\text{d}^{6}z\sqrt{-g}\,\tr\left[\mathcal{F}^{MN}\left(\mathcal{D}_{M}A_{N}-\mathcal{D}_{N}A_{M}\right)\right],

(35)

\displaystyle S^{\text{quad}}_{A}

\displaystyle=-\int\text{d}^{6}z\sqrt{-g}\,\tr\left[ig_{A}\,\mathcal{F}^{MN}[A_{M},A_{N}]+{1\over 2}\left(\mathcal{D}^{M}A^{N}-\mathcal{D}^{N}A^{M}\right)\left(\mathcal{D}_{M}A_{N}-\mathcal{D}_{N}A_{M}\right)\right].

(36)

We see that the linear term vanishes for a pure gauge configuration
\mathcal{F}^{MN}=0
.
In contrast, the monopole configuration (
2
) gives
\mathcal{F}^{\theta\phi}\neq 0
, and more careful treatment is necessary.
We will come back to this point in Section
3.3
.

For the gauge fixing action,

\displaystyle S_{f}

\displaystyle=-\int\text{d}^{6}z\sqrt{-g}\,{1\over\xi}\,\tr\left(ff\right),

(37)

we choose the following gauge fixing function

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=g^{\mu\nu}\left(\nabla_{\mu}A_{\nu}+ig_{A}[\mathcal{A}_{\mu},A_{\nu}]\right)+\xi\left[g^{\theta\theta}\left(\nabla_{\theta}A_{\theta}+ig_{A}[\mathcal{A}_{\theta},A_{\theta}]\right)+g^{\phi\phi}\left(\nabla_{\phi}A_{\phi}+ig_{A}[\mathcal{A}_{\phi},A_{\phi}]\right)\right]

Six-dimensional UED models on sphere

As shown above, the compactification on two-sphere automatically yields the chiral fermion zero mode.

On the other hand, the latter possibility is considered in Ref.
[

[

.

5
Vacuum stability constraint

The vacuum stability leads to the most stringent upper bound on the ultraviolet cutoff scale
\Lambda
of the UED models
[
46
]
.
Since the idea is only briefly sketched in Ref.
[
46
]
, we clarify the argument more in detail here.

In
D
space-time dimensions, the Higgs action is written as

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int\sqrt{-g}\,\text{d}^{D}x\left[-\left(D_{M}H\right)^{\dagger}D^{M}H-V(H)\right],

(120)

with

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=m^{2}\left|H\right|^{2}+{\hat{\lambda}\over\Lambda^{D-4}}\left|H\right|^{4}+{\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}\over\Lambda^{2D-6}}\left|H\right|^{6}+\cdots,

(121)

where the hatted
\hat{\lambda}
,
\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}
, …are dimensionless coupling constants.
We note that
\hat{\lambda}
contains linear (quadratic) divergence in the 5D (6D) model.
At the one-loop level:

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\lambda_{B,1}\,\Lambda+b\ln{\mu\over\Lambda}+c&(D=5),\\
\lambda_{B,2}\,\Lambda^{2}+\lambda_{B,1}\,\Lambda+b\ln{\mu\over\Lambda}+c&(D=6),\end{cases}

(122)

where the mass dimension of the field and the bare couplings is
\left[H\right]=\left(D-2\right)/2
and
\left[\lambda_{B,n}\right]=-n
, respectively.

The zero mode Higgs
h
is constant in the extra dimension if neglect the electroweak symmetry breaking effects, and hence

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle={h\over\sqrt{\text{vol}}}+\cdots,

(123)

where
vol
is the volume of the extra dimension(s), with mass dimension
\left[\text{vol}\right]=4-D
.
Therefore the 4D potential for the zero mode is

\displaystyle V_{\text{4D}}

\displaystyle=m^{2}\left|h\right|^{2}+{\hat{\lambda}\over\left(\text{vol}\right)\Lambda^{D-4}}\left|h\right|^{4}+{\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}\over\left(\text{vol}\right)^{2}\Lambda^{2D-6}}\left|h\right|^{6}+\cdots.

(124)

We see that the four dimensional Higgs quartic coupling
\lambda_{\text{4D}}
is given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{\text{4D}}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle={\hat{\lambda}(\Lambda)\over\left(\text{vol}\right)\Lambda^{D-4}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\lambda_{B,1}\over\text{vol}}+{c\over\left(\text{vol}\right)\Lambda}&\text{for $D=5$,}\\
\displaystyle{\lambda_{B,2}\over\text{vol}}+{\lambda_{B,1}\over\left(\text{vol}\right)\Lambda}+{c\over\left(\text{vol}\right)\Lambda^{2}}&\text{for $D=6$.}\end{cases}

(125)

The left hand side can be estimated from the low energy inputs through the 4D renormalization group running, that is, from the running coupling
\lambda(\mu)
at the scale
\mu=\Lambda
.

Even though we can never know the bare coupling
\lambda_{B,i}
from the low energy data, what matters for the stability of the potential is the quantity
\hat{\lambda}
, which can be evaluated within the low energy (KK reduced) 4D effective theory.
13
13

13

If one likes the bottom-up approach, the bare quantity can be regarded as totally unphysical. If one is interested in the ultraviolet completion, then the bare quantity itself becomes of interest, together with the specification of the regularization scheme. What we argue here is that no matter which viewpoint one takes, the stability argument can be done solely by the running coupling
\lambda_{\text{4D}}(\mu)
, which is reliable up to the cutoff scale
\mu<\Lambda
.

The bare constants are screened from the effective potential in the low energy theory at the scales
\mu<\Lambda
; see e.g. Appendix B in Ref.
[
78
]
.

If we have negative
\lambda_{\text{4D}}(\mu)
at some scale
\mu
, then it necessarily requires the higher dimensional terms
\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}
etc. suppressed by
\Lambda\sim\mu
, in order to avoid the unbounded potential.
Therefore we can read off the cutoff
\Lambda
from the scale where the running quartic coupling
\lambda_{4D}(\mu)
becomes negative.

Summary

We have presented a review on the 6D UED models compactified on sphere, namely on
S^{2}
,
S^{2}/Z_{2}
, and PS.
We have spelled out the basic techniques to treat the fields on sphere in terms of the Newman-Penrose eth formalism. KK expansion of the scalar, spinor, and vector fields are given. We have reviewed how the various fields are projected on the

We have critically reconsidered the

S^{2}

models need a modification.
We have explained the conceptual background of our previous work on the vacuum stability bound.

Six-dimensional Bulk Dirac mass and The Higgs Mechanism

Finally, we consider the bulk Dirac mass term.
Even in the 6D case, spinors with plus and minus 6D chiralities
\Psi_{+}
and
\Psi_{-}
can have a Dirac mass if both of them have equal charges to each other for all the unbroken gauge interactions, similar to the four-dimensional case:

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle:=-\int\text{d}^{6}z\sqrt{-g}M_{\Psi}\left(\overline{\Psi_{+}}\Psi_{-}+\overline{\Psi_{-}}\Psi_{+}\right)

\displaystyle=-\int d^{4}x\sum_{j=j_{\text{min}}}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-j}^{j}M_{\Psi}\left(\overline{\psi^{jm}_{+,4D}}\psi^{jm}_{-,4D}+\overline{\psi^{jm}_{-,4D}}\psi^{jm}_{+,4D}\right).

(165)

We can diagonalize the mass matrix of the KK-modes,

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{4D\,jm}_{\text{mass}}

\displaystyle=-\left(\overline{\psi^{jm}_{+,4D}}\ \overline{\psi^{jm}_{-,4D}}\right)\begin{pmatrix}im_{j}\gamma^{\underline{5}}&M_{\Psi}\\
M_{\Psi}&im_{j}\gamma^{\underline{5}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi^{jm}_{+,4D}\\
\psi_{-,4D}^{jm}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=-\left(\overline{\psi^{jm}_{1,4D}}\ \overline{\psi^{jm}_{2,4D}}\right)\begin{pmatrix}-\sqrt{m_{j}^{2}+M^{2}_{\Psi}}&\\
&+\sqrt{m_{j}^{2}+M^{2}_{\Psi}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi^{jm}_{1,4D}\\
\psi_{2,4D}^{jm}\end{pmatrix},

(166)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\psi^{jm}_{+,4D}\\
\psi^{jm}_{-,4D}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}e^{{\pi\over 4}i\gamma^{\underline{5}}}\cos\alpha_{j}&-e^{{\pi\over 4}i\gamma^{\underline{5}}}\sin\alpha_{j}\\
e^{-{\pi\over 4}i\gamma^{\underline{5}}}\sin\alpha_{j}&e^{-{\pi\over 4}i\gamma^{\underline{5}}}\cos\alpha_{j}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi^{jm}_{1,4D}\\
\psi^{jm}_{2,4D}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\tan 2\alpha_{j}:=-\frac{M_{\Psi}}{m_{j}}.

We have obtained mass eigen-values
\pm\sqrt{{m_{j}}^{2}+M^{2}_{\Psi}}

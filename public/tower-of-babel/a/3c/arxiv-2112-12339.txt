Abstract
. We establish that three well-known and rather different looking conjectures about Dirichlet characters and their (weighted) sums, (concerning the Pólya-Vinogradov theorem for maximal character sums, the maximal admissible range in Burgess’ estimate for short character sums, and upper bounds for L ⁡ ( 1 , χ ) L(1,\chi) and L ⁡ ( 1 + i ​ t , χ ) L(1+it,\chi) ) are more-or-less “equivalent”. We also obtain a new mean value theorem for logarithmically weighted sums of 1-bounded multiplicative functions.

Introduction

1.1.
Conjectures about weighted sums of Dirichlet characters

Let
\chi
be a primitive character mod
q>1
and

S(\chi,N):=\sum_{n\leqslant N}\chi(n)\text{ for all }N\geqslant 1.

The three most widely-used, unconditionally proved estimates about characters sums are:

•

The
Pólya-Vinogradov
theorem:

M(\chi):=\max_{1\leqslant N\leqslant q}|S(\chi,N)|\leqslant c_{1}\sqrt{q}\log q

for some explicit
c_{1}>0
;

•

Burgess’s theorem
(
[
4
]
,
[
17
]
): For
N\geqslant q^{c_{2}}
(and
q

cube-free
)

|S(\chi,N)|=o(N)

for any
c_{2}\geqslant\tfrac{1}{4}
; and

•

The Dirichlet
L
-function
L(s,\chi)
at
s=1
satisfies

|L(1,\chi)|=\bigg|\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{\chi(n)}{n}\bigg|\leqslant c_{3}\log q

for some explicit
c_{3}>0
. One can also show that for any fixed
T>0
, there exists a constant
c_{3}(T)>0
such that if

then

|L(1+it,\chi)|\leqslant c_{3}(T)\log q
.

The Riemann Hypothesis for

implies that one can take any

but this has resisted unconditional proof. One unlikely but currently plausible ‘obstruction’ to establishing this unconditionally is the possibility that
\chi(p)=1
for all primes
p\leqslant q^{c}
, in which case
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\gg c
, or indeed if
\chi
is
1
-pretentious for the primes up to
q
.

1

Inspired by connections highlighted in
[

,

,

]

we show that improving any one of these bounds will, more-or-less, improve the others.

Theorem 1
.

The following statements are equivalent:

•

There exists
\kappa_{1}>0
such that there are infinitely many primitive characters
\chi\pmod{q}
for which
M(\chi)\geqslant\kappa_{1}\sqrt{q}\log q
;

•

There exists
\kappa_{3}>0
such that there are infinitely many odd primitive characters
\psi\pmod{r}
for which

|L(1,\psi)|\geqslant\kappa_{3}\log r
.

This follows from a more precise connection:

Corollary 1
.

Suppose that
\chi
is a primitive character mod
q
. We have

M(\chi)\gg\sqrt{q}\log q
if and only if there exists a primitive character

\xi\pmod{\ell}
with
\xi(-1)=-\chi(-1)
and
\ell\ll 1
for which
|L(1,\psi)|\gg\log q
, where
\psi
is the primitive (odd) character that induces
\chi\bar{\xi}
.

In other words we prove that if
M(\chi)\gg\sqrt{q}\log q
then
\chi
is
\xi
-pretentious for some
\xi
of bounded conductor, and we will also establish a converse theorem.

Next we relate large
S(\chi,N)
-values with large
L(1+it,\chi)
-values:

Theorem 2
.

The following statements are equivalent:

•

There exists
\kappa_{2}>0
such that there are infinitely many primitive characters
\chi\pmod{q}

for which there is an integer
N\in[q^{\kappa_{2}},q]
such that

|S(\chi,N)|\geqslant\kappa_{2}N
;

•

There exists
\kappa_{3}>0
and
T>0
such that there are infinitely many primitive characters
\chi\pmod{q}
for which there exists

such that
|L(1+it,\chi)|\geqslant\kappa_{3}\log q
.

If we restrict attention here to characters of bounded order then one can take
t=0
.
The precise connection is given in the following result.

Proposition 1
.

Fix
c>0
. Let
\chi
be a primitive character mod
q
.
There exists

with
|t|\ll 1
for which
|L(1+it,\chi)|\geqslant c\log q

if and only if there exist
\kappa=\kappa(c)>0
and
x\in[q^{\kappa},q]
for which
|S(\chi,x)|\gg_{c}x
.
If
\chi^{k}=\chi_{0}
for some

then we may take

.

In other words we prove that if
|S(\chi,N)|\gg N
for some
N>q^{\kappa}
and
\kappa>0
then
\chi
is
n^{it}
-pretentious for some bounded real number

We can combine these results: If

and

,

\chi
is both

-pretentious, which implies that

-pretentious, where

Therefore such a putative character is the only obstruction to improving at least one of our three famous results unconditionally (that is, being able to take any

1.2.
Logarithmic averages of multiplicative functions

We prove our results on sums of characters by viewing characters as examples of multiplicative functions that take their values on the unit disk

\mathbb{U}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leqslant 1\}
. Halász’s theorem, which we will discuss in detail below, bounds the mean value of
f(n)
for
n
up to
x
in terms of how “pretentious”
f
is. In particular, if
f
is real-valued then
Hall and Tenenbaum
[
16
]
showed that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n)\ll x\,e^{-\tau\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}}\text{ where }\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}=\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{1-\text{Re}(f(p))}{p},

and

\tau=0.3286\ldots=-\cos\theta\text{ where }\theta\in(0,\pi)\text{ satisfies }\sin\theta-\theta\cos\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}.

They gave an example where one attains equality in (
1.1
) (up to the inexplicit constant).

We give an analogous result for logarithmic averages of the form
\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n)/n
though, as discovered in
[
13
]
, we do not need to restrict attention to real-valued
f
. Here we let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
be such that

\int_{0}^{1}|e(\theta)-\lambda|d\theta=2-\lambda,

so that
\lambda=0.8221\dots
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
be a multiplicative function.

(a)
We have

(1.2)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{f(n)}{n}\ll(\log x)(1+\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2})e^{-\lambda\,\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}}+\log\log x.

(b)
The exponent
\lambda
is “best possible” in a result of this kind, since there exists
a multiplicative function
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
such that

(1.3)

\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{f(n)}{n}\bigg|\asymp(\log x)e^{-\lambda\,\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)^{2}}.

The
\lambda
in the bound (
1.2
) improves on the
\tfrac{1}{2}
in the bound given in Lemma 4.3 of
[
13
]
.

We deduce Proposition
2
(b) from Theorem
3
which establishes asymptotics for

\sum_{n\leqslant N}f(n)/n
for a class of multiplicative functions
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
for which

f(p)=g(\tau\log p)
for each prime
p
for some fixed small real
\tau
, where
g(t)
is a
1
-periodic function with “well-behaved” Fourier coefficients.

More details, as well as other new results on this topic, will be discussed in section
3
.

2.
Connections between different sums of characters

2.1.
Large character sums

Pólya gave the following Fourier expansion (see e.g., Lemma 1 of
[
21
]
) for character sums: for
\alpha\in[0,1)

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant\alpha q}\chi(n)=\frac{g(\chi)}{2\pi i}\sum_{1\leqslant|n|\leqslant q}\frac{\bar{\chi}(n)}{n}\bigg(1-e(-n\alpha)\bigg)+O(\log q),

where
e(t):=e^{2\pi it}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
g(\chi):=\sum_{a\pmod{q}}\chi(a)e(\tfrac{a}{q})
is the Gauss sum. When
\chi
is primitive we know that
|g(\chi)|=\sqrt{q}
and

(2.2)

\sum_{1\leqslant|n|\leqslant q}\frac{\bar{\chi}(n)}{n}=(1-\chi(-1))L(1,\bar{\chi})+o(1),

and so to estimate the left-hand side of (
2.1
) for any
\alpha
we are left to estimate the sums

\sum_{n\leqslant q}\frac{\bar{\chi}(n)e(\pm n\alpha)}{n}.

Note that (
2.2
) is large if and only if
\chi
is an odd character and
L(1,\chi)
is large.

Fix
\tfrac{2}{\pi}<\Delta<1
and let
R_{q}:=\exp(\tfrac{(\log q)^{\Delta}}{\log\log q})
.
For any
\alpha\in[0,1)
we may obtain an approximation
|\alpha-\tfrac{b}{m}|<\tfrac{1}{mR_{q}}
, with
(b,m)=1
and
m\leqslant R_{q}
, by Dirichlet’s theorem.

If
r_{q}:=(\log q)^{2-2\Delta}(\log\log q)^{4}<m\leqslant R_{q}
then we say that
\alpha
is on a
minor arc
. By straightforward modifications to the proof of Lemma 6.1 of
[
13
]
, for such
\alpha
we get

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant q}\frac{\chi(n)e(n\alpha)}{n}\ll\log\log q+\frac{(\log r_{q})^{3/2}}{\sqrt{r_{q}}}\log q+\log R_{q}=o((\log q)^{\Delta}).

If
m\leqslant r_{q}
then we say that
\alpha
is on a
major arc
. Let
N:=\min\{q,\tfrac{1}{|m\alpha-b|}\}
,
so that
R_{q}\leqslant N\leqslant q
. By Lemma 6.2 of
[
13
]
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant|n|\leqslant q}\frac{\chi(n)e(n\alpha)}{n}=\sum_{1\leqslant|n|\leqslant N}\frac{\chi(n)e(n\tfrac{b}{m})}{n}+O(\log\log q).

Therefore, if
M(\chi)\gg\sqrt{q}(\log q)^{\Delta}
then
either
\chi
is odd and
L(1,\chi)\gg(\log q)^{\Delta}
, or

M(\chi)=|S(\chi,\alpha q)|
where
\alpha
lies on a major arc.
The following proposition provides more detailed information in these cases.

Proposition 3
.

Fix
\tfrac{2}{\pi}<\Delta<1
and let
\chi
be a character mod
q
. We have

M(\chi)\gg\sqrt{q}(\log q)^{\Delta}

if and only if there is a primitive character

\xi\pmod{\ell}
with
\xi(-1)=-\chi(-1)
and
\ell\leqslant(\log q)^{2-2\Delta}(\log\log q)^{4}
such that

\max_{1\leqslant N\leqslant q}\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant N}\frac{(\chi\bar{\xi})(n)}{n}\bigg|\gg\frac{\phi(\ell)}{\sqrt{\ell}}(\log q)^{\Delta}.

More precisely, in this case we have

(2.5)

M(\chi)\sim\tau_{\chi,\xi}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{q\ell}}{\pi\phi(\ell)}\cdot\max_{1\leqslant N\leqslant q}\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant N}\frac{(\chi\bar{\xi})(n)}{n}\bigg|

where
\tau_{\chi,1}\in[\tfrac{1}{2},3]
and
\tau_{\chi,\xi}=\max\{1,|1-(\chi\bar{\xi})(2)|\}
if
\xi\neq 1
.

Throughout,
N_{q}
denotes an integer value of
N
that maximizes the right-hand side of (
2.5
).

Using results from the next subsection, we will deduce Corollary
1
by showing that
if
M(\chi)\gg\sqrt{q}\log q
then for
\psi=\chi\bar{\xi}
we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle|L(1,\psi)|\gg\log q\text{ if and only if }\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant N_{q}}\frac{\psi(n)}{n}\bigg|\gg\log q.

However, there is not necessarily a correspondence between these two sums when they are slightly smaller.
For example, if
\psi(p)=1
for all
p\leqslant N:=\exp((\log q)^{\tau})
and

\psi(p)=-1
for all
\exp((\log q)^{\tau})<p\leqslant q^{\delta}

where
\tfrac{1}{2}<\tau<1
and
\delta>0
is some small fixed constant, then assuming

is non-exceptional (see (
4.3
)),

\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant N}\frac{\psi(n)}{n}\bigg|\asymp(\log q)^{\tau}\text{ while }|L(1,\psi)|\asymp\prod_{p\leqslant q}\bigg|1-\frac{\psi(p)}{p}\bigg|^{-1}\asymp(\log q)^{2\tau-1},

which is much smaller. Moreover this (purported) example shows why

that
N_{q}=q
and that the largest sum is
\sim|L(1,\psi)|
.

Following an idea from
[

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\

3.
Halász’s Theorem and beyond

For multiplicative functions
f,g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
and
x\geqslant 2
, we define the
pretentious distance

\mathbb{D}(f,g;x):=\bigg(\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{1-\text{Re}(f(p)\bar{g}(p))}{p}\bigg)^{1/2}.

It is well-known that
\mathbb{D}
satisfies the triangle inequality:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(f,h;x)\leqslant\mathbb{D}(f,g;x)+\mathbb{D}(g,h;x)\text{ for all }f,g,h:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}.

With
2\leqslant y\leqslant x
we also write
\mathbb{D}(f,g;y,x)
to work only with the primes in
(y,x]
.
We say that
f
is
g
-pretentious
(for the primes up to
x
) if

\mathbb{D}(f,g;x)\ll 1
; so if
f
is
g
-pretentious then
f(p)\approx g(p)
frequently for
p\leqslant x
.

3.1.
Halász’s theorem

For
T>0
,
x\geqslant 2
and a multiplicative function
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
, we also define

M(f;x,T):=\min_{|t|\leqslant T}\mathbb{D}(f,n^{it};x)^{2}.

We let
t=t(f;x,T)
be a real number in this range where we get equality.
Halász’s Theorem (see e.g.,
[
12
, Thm. 1]
) states that if
1\leqslant T\leqslant\log x
then, for
M=M(f;x,T)
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n)\ll(1+M)e^{-M}x+\frac{x}{T}.

If
f(n)=n^{it}
with
|t|\leqslant T
then
M=0
, which reflects the fact that

|\sum_{n\leqslant x}n^{it}|\sim x/|1+it|
.

Halász’s theorem shows that
\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n)\bigg|
is
o(x)
if
f
is not
n^{it}
-pretentious for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Elementary estimates for
\zeta(s)
to the right of the
1
-line imply that

(3.3)

\mathbb{D}(1,n^{it};x)^{2}=\begin{cases}\log(1+|t|\log x)+O(1)&\text{ if }|t|\leqslant 100;\\
\log\log x+O(\log\log|t|)&\text{ if }|t|\geqslant 100.\end{cases}

This shows that
1
is
not

n^{it}
-pretentious unless
|t|\ll\tfrac{1}{\log x}
.
Therefore if
|t_{1}|,|t_{2}|\leqslant T:=(\log x)^{O(1)}
then
n^{it_{1}}
cannot be
n^{it_{2}}
-pretentious unless
|t_{1}-t_{2}|\log x\ll 1
.

If
t(f;x,T)
is not unique, say
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
both yield equality above, then (
3.1
) implies that

\mathbb{D}(n^{it_{1}},n^{it_{2}};x)\leqslant\mathbb{D}(f,n^{it_{1}};x)+\mathbb{D}(f,n^{it_{2}};x)=2\mathbb{D}(f,n^{it_{1}};x)
. In particular if

f
is
n^{it_{1}}
-pretentious then
f
is not
n^{it_{2}}
-pretentious unless
t_{2}=t_{1}+O(\tfrac{1}{\log x})
.

3.2.
Halász-type bounds for logarithmically weighted sums

If
f
is real-valued then we might expect that
t(f;x,T)=0
but there are examples where this is not so
(which lead to the “best possible examples” in Hall and Tenenbaum’s estimate (
1.1
)).
The examples
f(n)=n^{it}
show that there cannot be an upper bound in terms of
\mathbb{D}(f,1;x)
alone for arbitrary
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
, though such bounds are given in
[
16
]
for
f
belonging to certain restricted families of multiplicative functions (most importantly those of bounded order).

In this article we will need bounds for the logarithmically weighted sums
\sum_{n\leqslant x}f(n)/n
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
x\geqslant 3
.
Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
be a multiplicative function with

M=M(f;x,1)
, and let
t\in[-1,1]
minimize the expression

\tau\mapsto\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{2-\text{\rm Re}((1+f(p))p^{-i\tau})}{p},\quad\tau\in[-1,1].

If
|t|\leqslant\tfrac{1}{\log x}
then

(3.4)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{f(n)}{n}\ll(1+M)e^{-M}\log x+\log\log x.

If
|t|\geqslant\tfrac{1}{\log x}
then

(3.5)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{f(n)}{n}\ll\frac{1}{|t|}(1+M+\log(|t|\log x))e^{-M}+\log\log x.

The bound (
3.5
) improves upon Theorem 2.4 in
[
8

.

Lemma 3
.

Fix
C>0
and let

.
Let

be chosen such that

satisfies

.
Let

and
\chi_{2}=\chi_{1}\xi
be characters with modulus in

, where

is an odd character.
Suppose that

Suppose that

has order

We note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\chi_{2},n^{it_{2}};q)^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\mathbb{D}(\chi_{2},n^{it_{2}};q)^{2}+\mathbb{D}(\chi_{1},n^{it_{1}};q)^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\log\log q-\frac{1}{2}\text{Re}\bigg(\sum_{p\leqslant q}\frac{\chi_{1}(p)p^{-it_{1}}+\chi_{2}(p)p^{-it_{2}}}{p}\bigg)+O(1)

\displaystyle=\log\log q-\frac{1}{2}\text{Re}\bigg(\sum_{p\leqslant q}\frac{\chi_{1}(p)p^{-it_{1}}(1+\xi(p)p^{-it})}{p}\bigg)+O(1)

(6.4)

\displaystyle\geqslant\log\log q-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\leqslant q}\frac{|1+\xi(p)p^{-it}|}{p}+O(1)

) we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leqslant q}\frac{|1+\xi(p)p^{-it}|}{p}=\frac{4}{\pi}\bigg(\log\log q-\sum_{1\leqslant|d|\leqslant D}\frac{(-1)^{d}}{4d^{2}-1}S_{d}\bigg)+O(1),

where

and

If

divides

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\leqslant q}\frac{1}{p^{1+idt}}+O\bigg(\sum_{p|m}\frac{1}{p}\bigg)=\sum_{p\leqslant q}\frac{1}{p^{1+idt}}+O(\log\log\log\log q).

\displaystyle=\log(\min\{\log q,\tfrac{2\pi}{|dt|}\})+O(1)

\displaystyle=\log(\min\{\log q,\tfrac{1}{|t|}\})+O(\log\log\log q).

\geqslant 1

We deduce that if

divides
d

.

If

, a non-principal character mod

For

Since

\displaystyle\int_{Y}^{q}\frac{d\psi(y,\alpha)}{y^{1+iT}\log y}+O\bigg(\frac{1}{Y}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\psi(y,\alpha)}{y^{1+iT}\log y}\right]_{Y}^{q}+(1+iT)\int_{Y}^{q}\frac{\psi(y,\alpha)}{y^{2+iT}\log y}dy+O\bigg(\frac{1}{(\log Y)^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{(\log Y)^{2}}+T\int_{Y}^{q}\frac{1}{y(\log y)^{2}}dy\ll 1.

We conclude that

.

Putting these estimates into (

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leqslant q}\frac{|1+\xi(p)p^{-it}|}{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi}\log\log q+2\bigg(\gamma_{k}-\frac{2}{\pi}\bigg)\log(\min\{\log q,\tfrac{1}{|t|}\})

\displaystyle+O(\log\log\log q+\log\log Y),

and

Changing

Now since

;
the result follows since the middle term is

∎

\delta(\alpha-1)=\alpha^{k}(2-\alpha)
so that
1<\alpha<2
. We claim that

|h(2^{m})|\leqslant\kappa\alpha^{m}
for all
m\geqslant 0
, where
\kappa:=\max_{0\leqslant m\leqslant k}|h(2^{m})|/\alpha^{m}
. This is trivially true for
m\leqslant k
; otherwise for
m>k
we have (as
h(1)=1,h(2)=0
)

\displaystyle|h(2^{m})|

\displaystyle=\bigg|f(2^{m})-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}f_{t}(2^{m-j})h(2^{j})\bigg|\leqslant 2+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\neq m-k\end{subarray}}^{m-1}|h(2^{j})|+(1-\delta)|h(2^{m-k})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\neq m-k\end{subarray}}^{m-1}\kappa\alpha^{j}+(1-\delta)\kappa\alpha^{m-k}<\kappa\alpha^{m}\bigg(\sum_{i\geqslant 1}\alpha^{-i}-\delta\alpha^{-k}\bigg)=\kappa\alpha^{m}

as
2\leqslant\kappa+\kappa\alpha
, by induction, using the definition of
\alpha
.

Finally we wish to determine whether the Euler factor of
H(1+i\ell t)
at
2
equals
0
. This equals the
Euler factor for
f
at
1
divided by the Euler factor for
f_{t}
at
1
.
Since each
|f_{t}(2^{k})|\leqslant 1
the denominator is bounded; since
|f(2^{k})|\leqslant 1

we have
\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\frac{f(2^{k})}{2^{k(1+i\ell t)}}=0
if and only if
f(2^{k})=-2^{ik\ell t}
for all
k\geqslant 1
.

∎

7.2.
Our specific example

We now use Theorem
3
to construct a multiplicative function
f
satisfying the conclusion of Proposition
2
(b).
We will use the auxiliary 1-periodic function

g(u)=\frac{e(u)-\lambda}{|e(u)-\lambda|}=\frac{|e(u)-\lambda|}{e(-u)-\lambda}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{n}e(nu).

We see that
g
takes values on
S^{1}
with
g(0)=1
. We will verify the following properties of
\{g_{n}\}_{n}
in the appendix (section
A.1
), which shows that
g
satisfies the assumptions required to apply Theorem
3
.

Lemma 5
.

For the
\{g_{n}\}_{n}
defined just above we have:

(a)

g_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
n
,

(b)

|g_{n}|\ll\ (\tfrac{2\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}})^{|n|}\leqslant 0.99^{|n|}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(c)

g_{-n}<g_{n}
for all
n\geqslant 1
,

(d)

g_{1}=0.7994\dots
, and there is
\delta>0
such that
g_{n}\leq g_{1}-\delta
or all
n\neq 0,1
, and

(e)

g_{0}<g_{1}-1
.

Deduction of Proposition
2
(b).

Let
x
be large. Let
t\in[\tfrac{1}{\log\log x},1]
be small, and set
y_{t}:=e^{\tfrac{1}{t}}
and
f=f_{t}
.
For small enough
t
, Theorem
3
yields

(7.7)

\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{f(n)}{n}\bigg|\asymp t^{-1}(t\log x)^{g_{1}-1}\asymp\log x\exp\bigg((g_{1}-2)\sum_{y_{t}<p\leqslant x}\frac{1}{p}\bigg).

Using the definition of
\lambda
,

\displaystyle 2-\lambda=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|e(u)-\lambda|}{e(-u)-\lambda}(e(-u)-\lambda)du=\int_{0}^{1}g(u)e(-u)du-\lambda\int_{0}^{1}g(u)du=g_{1}-\lambda g_{0},

so that
g_{1}-2=\lambda(g_{0}-1)
. By partial summation and the prime number theorem we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(f,1;y_{t},x)^{2}=\sum_{y_{t}<p\leqslant x}\frac{1-\text{Re}(g(\tfrac{t}{2\pi}\log p))}{p}

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(1-g(u)\bigg)du\bigg)\log\bigg(\frac{\log x}{\log y_{t}}\bigg)+O(1)

\displaystyle=(1-g_{0})\sum_{y_{t}<p\leqslant x}\frac{1}{p}+O(1).

Combining these last few observations we deduce that

\bigg|\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{f(n)}{n}\bigg|\asymp\log x\exp\bigg(\lambda(g_{0}-1)\sum_{y_{t}<p\leqslant x}\frac{1}{p}\bigg)\asymp\log x\exp\bigg(-\lambda\ \mathbb{D}(f,1;y_{t},x)^{2}\bigg).

Finally, since
g
is Lipschitz and
g(0)=1
,

|g(\tfrac{t}{2\pi}\log p)-1|\ll t\log p\text{ for all }p\leqslant y_{t},

and so by Mertens’ theorem,

\mathbb{D}(f,1;y_{t})^{2}=\sum_{p\leqslant y_{t}}\frac{1-\text{Re}(g(\tfrac{t}{2\pi}\log p))}{p}\ll t\sum_{p\leqslant y_{t}}\frac{\log p}{p}\ll 1.

Combining these last two estimates, (
1.3
) follows.
∎

A.1.
On the Fourier Coefficients of
g

Proof of Lemma

.

(a) Since

, a term-by-term comparison of the Fourier series of each shows that

and thus

for each

(b) We will prove a numerically sharper bound, which will be used in part (d).
Note that

g(u)=\frac{e(u)-\lambda}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}\cdot\bigg(1-\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}}(e(u)+e(-u))\bigg)^{-1/2}.

As
2\lambda<0.99(1+\lambda^{2})
, Taylor expanding the bracketed expression gives

\displaystyle g(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{e(u)-\lambda}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\binom{-1/2}{j}(-1)^{j}\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}}\bigg)^{j}\sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant j}\binom{j}{i}e((2i-j)u)

\displaystyle=\frac{e(u)-\lambda}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e(nu)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geqslant|n|\\
2|(n+j)\end{subarray}}\binom{j}{(j+n)/2}2^{-2j}\binom{2j}{j}\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}}\bigg)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e(mu)\frac{h_{m-1}-\lambda h_{m}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}},

where we have set

(A.1)

\displaystyle h_{n}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geqslant|n|\\
2|(j+n)\end{subarray}}2^{-2j}\binom{2j}{j}\binom{j}{(j+|n|)/2}\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}}\bigg)^{j}=\sum_{l\geqslant 0}\binom{|n|+2l}{|n|+l}\binom{2(|n|+2l)}{|n|+2l}\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{4(1+\lambda^{2})}\bigg)^{|n|+2l}.

It follows that

g_{n}=\frac{h_{n-1}-\lambda h_{n}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}.

We use Stirling’s approximation in the form
\sqrt{2\pi n}(n/e)^{n}\leqslant n!\leqslant e^{\tfrac{1}{12}}\sqrt{2\pi n}(n/e)^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
(see
[
22
]
), and bound
\binom{j}{(j+|n|)/2}
by a central binomial coefficient as
\binom{j}{(j+|n|)/2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2^{\nu}}\binom{j+\nu}{(j+\nu)/2}
, where
\nu=1_{2\nmid j}
. When
n\neq 0
this gives

2^{-2j}\binom{2j}{j}\binom{j}{(j+|n|)/2}\leqslant 2^{-2j}\bigg(\tfrac{e^{\tfrac{1}{12}}}{\sqrt{\pi j}}2^{2j}\bigg)\bigg(\tfrac{e^{\tfrac{1}{12}}}{2^{\nu}\sqrt{\pi(j+\nu)/2}}2^{j+\nu}\bigg)\leqslant\frac{e^{\tfrac{1}{6}}\sqrt{2}}{\pi j}2^{j},\text{ if }2|(j+n).

Setting

, we find using the first expression for
h_{n}
in (

) that

0\leqslant h_{m}\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geqslant|m|\\
2|(j+m)\end{subarray}}\frac{e^{\tfrac{1}{6}}\sqrt{2}}{\pi j}\,2^{j}\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}}\bigg)^{j}\leqslant\frac{e^{\tfrac{1}{6}}\sqrt{2}}{(1-c^{2})\pi}\,\frac{c^{|m|}}{|m|},\quad m\neq 0.

Using the second expression for
h_{n}
in (
A.1

|n|\geqslant 1

\displaystyle h_{n-1}-\lambda h_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\geqslant 0}\binom{|n|-1+2l}{|n|-1+l}\binom{2(|n|-1+2l)}{|n|-1+2l}\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{4(1+\lambda^{2})}\bigg)^{|n|-1+2l}\bigg(1-\frac{2|n|-1+4l}{2(|n|+l)}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}\bigg)

Together with the previous bound, it follows that when

(A.2)

\displaystyle|g_{n}|\leqslant\frac{e^{\tfrac{1}{6}}\sqrt{2}}{(1-c^{2})\pi\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}\bigg(1-\frac{2|n|-1}{2|n|}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}\bigg)\frac{c^{|n|-1}}{|n|-1},

(c) We deduce from (

(A.3)

\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}g_{n}=\begin{cases}h_{n-1}-\lambda h_{n}&\text{ if }n\geqslant 1,\\

To see this, note that

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{2(1+\lambda^{2})}\bigg)^{2}\sum_{l\geqslant 0}\binom{n-1+2l}{n-1+l}\binom{2(n-1+2l)}{n-1+2l}\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{4(1+\lambda^{2})}\bigg)^{n-1+2l}\frac{(2n+4l)^{2}-1}{(n+l+1)(n+l)}

\displaystyle\leqslant 16\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{2(1+\lambda^{2})}\bigg)^{2}\sum_{l\geqslant 0}\binom{n-1+2l}{n-1+l}\binom{2(n-1+2l)}{n-1+2l}\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{4(1+\lambda^{2})}\bigg)^{n-1+2l},

(d) Since

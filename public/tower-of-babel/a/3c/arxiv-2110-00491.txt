Dynamic Models of Spherical Parallel Robots for Model-Based Control Schemes
Ali Hassani, Abbas Bataleblu, S. A. Khalilpour, Hamid D. Taghirad, Philippe Cardou

Abstract
In this paper, derivation of different forms of dynamic formulation of spherical parallel robots (SPRs) is investigated. These formulations include the explicit dynamic forms, linear regressor, and Slotine-Li (S-L) regressor, which are required for the design and implementation of the vast majority of model-based controllers and dynamic parameters identification schemes. To this end , the implicit dynamic of SPRs is first formulated using the principle of virtual work in task-space , and then by using an extension, their explicit dynamic formulation is derived. The dynamic equation is then analytically reformulated into linear and S-L regression form with respect to the inertial parameters, and by using the Gauss-Jordan procedure, it is reduced to a unique and closed-form structure. Finally, to illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed method, two different SPRs, namely, the ARAS-Diamond, and the 3- R RR, are examined as the case studies. The obtained results are verified by using the MSC-ADAMS ® software, and are shared to interested audience for public access.

and gravity terms. The common inverse dynamic controller (IDC),

Dynamic Analysis

2.1
Preliminary Definitions

In this section, preliminary definitions for the
dynamic formulation of SPRs are given.
The structure of these
robots consists of several links and a moving platform with pure
rotational motion about the CR point. The origin of the base
coordinate system is set at the CR point, as shown in the schematic
of
Figure 1
.

Figure 1:
View of a SPR

Throughout this paper, the
i
and
p
indices refer to each link
and to the moving platform, respectively. Moreover,

\boldsymbol{q}=\begin{bmatrix}q_{1}&...&q_{j}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
denotes the
actuated joint variables,
while
\chi
represents
independent generalized parameters of the moving platform. In SPRs,
the moving platform has pure rotation about the CR point, and
therefore,
\chi
may be considered as
\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}=\boldsymbol{\theta}=\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
.
In addition, to
describe the orientation of the moving platform in the task space
coordinate, different approaches are reported in the literature. In
this paper, Euler angles and the spherical base coordinate system
are adopted.
Besides,
S(\boldsymbol{a})
denotes the

3\times 3
skew-symmetric matrix generated from the vector

\boldsymbol{a}=[a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}]^{T}
.

2.2
The Principle of Virtual Work for Parallel Robots

The principle of virtual work for a PR with several
links and a moving platform, may be stated as

[
9
]
:

\delta W={\delta\boldsymbol{q}}^{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}+{\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}_{p}}^{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left({\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}_{i}}^{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}\right)=0,

(1)

where
\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}_{p}=\left[\delta{\boldsymbol{x}_{p}}^{T},\delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{p}}^{T}\right]^{T}
and
\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}_{i}=\left[\delta{\boldsymbol{x}_{i}}^{T},\delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}}^{T}\right]^{T}

represent the virtual displacements of the CGs of the moving
platform and of link
i
, respectively.
Furthermore,
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{p}
and

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}
denote the inertial wrenches respectively applied at the
moving platform CG and the
i
th
link CG.
Moreover,
k
indicates the number of links. These inertial
wrenches take the form:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{p}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{f}_{p}\\
\boldsymbol{n}_{p}\end{bmatrix}=-\begin{bmatrix}m_{p}\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{a}}_{p}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\boldsymbol{g}_{0}}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\boldsymbol{f}_{G_{d}}}\\
\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{p}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}+~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{p}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\boldsymbol{n}_{G_{d}}}\end{bmatrix},

(2a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{f}_{i}\\
\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\end{bmatrix}=-\begin{bmatrix}m_{i}\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{a}}_{i}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\boldsymbol{g}_{0}}\right)\\
\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{i}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{i}+~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\times\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{i}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\right)\right)\end{bmatrix},

(2b)

in which
{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{a}}_{p}
denotes the linear acceleration of
CG of the moving platform, and
{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{p}
demotes the
inertia matrix of the moving platform about its CG and expressed in
the base coordinate system. Moreover,
{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
and

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}
respectively represent the angular velocity
and acceleration of the moving platform. Similar to this notation,
the same is applied to each link’s inertial force with index
i
.

In addition,

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{G_{d}}=\left[{\boldsymbol{f}_{G_{d}}}^{T},{\boldsymbol{n}_{G_{d}}}^{T}\right]^{T}
is an
external disturbance wrench applied on the moving platform CG.

By choosing the
\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}=\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}_{p}
as the independent
generalized virtual displacements of the PR, virtual displacement of
links,
\delta\boldsymbol{q}
, may be related to the virtual displacement of
the moving platform,
\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}
, by the manipulator Jacobian

\boldsymbol{J}=\left[{\boldsymbol{J}_{v}}^{T},{\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega}}^{T}\right]^{T}
as:

\delta\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{J}~\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}.

(3)

Furthermore, the virtual displacement of the CG of link
i
,
\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}_{i}
, may be related to
\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}
by a Jacobian
matrix defined for each link and denoted by

\boldsymbol{J}_{i}=\left[{\boldsymbol{J}_{v_{i}}}^{T},{\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}}^{T}\right]^{T}
as:

\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}_{i}=\boldsymbol{J}_{i}~\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}.

(4)

Substituting (
3
) and (
4
) into
equation of (
1
) results in:

{\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}}^{T}\left(\boldsymbol{J}^{T}\boldsymbol{\tau}+\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\boldsymbol{J}_{i}}^{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}\right)\right)=0.

(5)

Finally, the implicit dynamics formulation of PRs may be
represented as follows:

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}={\boldsymbol{J}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\tau}=-\left(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\boldsymbol{J}_{i}}^{T}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}\right)\right).

(6)

In general,
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}=\left[{\boldsymbol{f}}^{T},{\boldsymbol{n}}^{T}\right]^{T}=\boldsymbol{J}^{T}\boldsymbol{\tau}
is defined as a mapping of the actuator
forces
\boldsymbol{\tau}
into the space of moving platform wrenches.

Therefore, the Jacobian matrix is a squared matrix if the robot is
fully parallel with no redundancy in actuators. Accordingly, in
non-singular configurations, actuator forces may uniquely be derived
as
\boldsymbol{\tau}=\boldsymbol{J}^{-T}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}
.

As mentioned before, this formulation is based on the kinematic
analysis of the CG of each link and of the moving platform. However,
if external wrenches (
2
) are applied to an
arbitrary point
\mathcal{A}
distinct from the CG, the external
wrenches equation may be rewritten according to this arbitrary
point. It may be proved that the principle of virtual work for any
arbitrary point
\mathcal{A}
in PRs is derived as

[
56
]
:

\delta W={\delta\boldsymbol{q}}^{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}+{\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}}^{T}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left({\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}}^{T}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}\right)=0,

(7)

in which,
\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}=\left[\delta{\boldsymbol{x}_{{\mathcal{A}_{p}}}}^{T},\delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}}^{T}\right]^{T}
and
\delta\boldsymbol{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\chi$}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}=\left[\delta{\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}}^{T},\delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}}^{T}\right]^{T}
indicate virtual
displacements of any arbitrary point
\mathcal{A}
of the moving
platform and each link, respectively. Therefore, inertial forces of the
moving platform and each link with respect to this arbitrary point
may be derived as:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{f}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\\
\boldsymbol{n}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\end{bmatrix}=-\begin{bmatrix}m_{p}\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}\times~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}+~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}\right)+\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}-\boldsymbol{g}_{0}\right)\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\boldsymbol{f}_{d}}\\
\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}+~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)+m_{p}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}\times\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}-\boldsymbol{g}_{0}\right)\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\boldsymbol{n}_{d}}\end{bmatrix},

(8a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{f}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}\\
\boldsymbol{n}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}\end{bmatrix}=-\begin{bmatrix}m_{i}\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{i}\times~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}+~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\times\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\times~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}\right)+\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}-\boldsymbol{g}_{0}\right)\right)\\
\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{i}+~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\times\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\right)+m_{i}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}\times\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{a}_{\mathcal{A}}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{g}_{0}\right)\right)\end{bmatrix},

(8b)

in which,
{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}
denotes the
acceleration of point
\mathcal{A}_{p}
in the base coordinate system.
The vector
{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{p}}
, points from the arbitrary point

\mathcal{A}_{p}
to the origin of the base coordinate system. It must
be noted that equal notation applies to the inertial forces of each
link with the index of
i
.
Moreover, we define

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{f}}_{d}=\left[{\boldsymbol{f}_{d}}^{T},{\boldsymbol{n}_{d}}^{T}\right]^{T}
as the external
disturbance wrench applied on the moving platform at the arbitrary
point
\mathcal{A}_{p}
.

Therefore, PRs’ dynamic formulation may be derived analogously to
the process mentioned in equations of (
3
) and
(
4
). The only difference, however, is the kinematic
analysis, which is based on the arbitrary points
\mathcal{A}_{i}
and

\mathcal{A}_{p}
, for each link and the moving platform,
respectively. Thus, in the following, this general formulation is
used to derive a simple form of the SPR dynamics.

2.3
Implicit Dynamic Analysis of Spherical Parallel Robots

As mentioned earlier, in SPRs, the moving platform undergoes pure
rotations about the CR point. Accordingly, we propose expressing the
dynamic formulations in a coordinate system with its origin at the
CR. Hence, by selecting
\mathcal{A}_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{p}
as a fixed
point at the origin of the base coordinate system, all links and the
moving platform experience pure rotations about the CR point. As a
result, the virtual linear displacement of points of

\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
\mathcal{A}_{p}
always
remains zero. This implies that, the substitution of

\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}
and
\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}
into Eq. (
7
)
leads to
\delta\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega}~\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}
and
\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}~\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}
. Therefore, the dynamic
formulation of SPRs may be accomplished as follows, which would be
interpreted as the implicit dynamic formulation of SPRs:

{\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\tau}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\boldsymbol{n}+\boldsymbol{n}_{d}=\boldsymbol{n}_{p}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\boldsymbol{n}_{i}}~,~\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

(9)

in which,
{\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\tau}={\boldsymbol{n}+\boldsymbol{n}_{d}}
represents the mapping of the
actuator forces
\boldsymbol{\tau}
on moving platform moments.
Moreover,
m
denotes
the number of generalized task space variables, and the
\boldsymbol{n}_{p}
and
\boldsymbol{n}_{i}

are defined as:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{n}_{p}=~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}+S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\right)-m_{i}~S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}\right)\boldsymbol{g}_{0},

(10a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{n}_{i}={\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}}^{T}\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{i}+S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\right)\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\right)-m_{i}~S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}\right)\boldsymbol{g}_{0}\right),

(10b)

where,
S(\boldsymbol{a})
represents the
3\times 3
skew-symmetric matrix of
vector
\boldsymbol{a}
and the
following mapping may be
used for deriving
S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}\right)
and,
likewise,
S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}\right)
:

S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}\right)=~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}~S\left({}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}\right)~{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}}^{T}.

(11)

in which,
{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}
denotes the
rotation matrix of each link, respect to base coordinate system, and

{}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}
is the center of gravity (CG) position of
link
i
respect to its CG.

2.4
Explicit Dynamic Analysis of Spherical Parallel Robots

As stated before, the orientation of the moving platform of SPRs
might be determined by their generalized coordinate, while its
derivatives represent the angular velocity and the angular
acceleration as
\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}={{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}
, and

\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}={{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}}
. In addition, the angular
velocity and acceleration of each link may be expressed as:

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}},

(12a)

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{i}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}+\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{i}}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}.

(12b)

In order to derive the explicit form of the dynamic formulations
employing the principle of virtual work, substitute
(
12
) into the implicit dynamic formulation of
(
9
).

With some manipulations
, the explicit dynamic formulation for SPRs is written as:

{\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\tau}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\boldsymbol{n}+\boldsymbol{n}_{d}}=\boldsymbol{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}+\boldsymbol{C}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}})\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}+\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),

(13)

in which,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})={{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\left({\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}}^{T}~{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}\right),

(14a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{C}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}})=S(\boldsymbol{\omega})~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}{\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}}^{T}\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{i}}+S\left(\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}\right)~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}\right),

(14b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=-m_{p}~S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}\right)\boldsymbol{g}_{0}-m_{i}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}{\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}}^{T}~S(^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i})~\boldsymbol{g}_{0}.

(14c)

In order to use the CG position of each link in the body coordinate
system instead of the base coordinate system, we substitute
(
11
) into (
14c
), which yields:

\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=-m_{i}\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{p}~S\left({}^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}\right){{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{p}}^{T}}\right)\boldsymbol{g}_{0}-m_{i}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}{\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}}^{T}\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}~S\left({}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}\right){{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}}^{T}}\right)\boldsymbol{g}_{0}.

(15)

The dynamic matrices of the explicit dynamic formulation of SPRs
obtained in equation (
13
) satisfy two important
properties, which might be used in the design of model-based
controllers
[
54
]
:

Property 1
. Matrix
\boldsymbol{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
is positive definite
in all configurations.

Property 2
. The matrix

\left[\boldsymbol{\dot{M}}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}})-2\boldsymbol{C}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}})\right]
is
skew–symmetric.

The first property may be easily proved, considering the quadratic
structure of its expression in Eq. (
14a
). Proving the
second property is less straight forward. First, the time derivative
of
\boldsymbol{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
may be computed as:

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\dot{M}}=&\left(S({{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}})~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}+~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}~{S({{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}})}^{T}\right)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}{\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{i}}}^{T}~{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}\\
&+{\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}}^{T}\left(S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\right)~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}+~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i}\right)^{T}\right)\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}+{\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}}^{T}~{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{i}}.\end{split}

(16)

Therefore, it can be easily verified that:

\left(\boldsymbol{\dot{M}}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}})-2\boldsymbol{C}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}})\right)^{T}+\left(\boldsymbol{\dot{M}}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}})-2\boldsymbol{C}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}})\right)=0.

(17)

3
Regressor Analysis

3.1
Slotine-Li Regressor

In the S-L adaptive controller, the control effort in the task-space is
defined as
[
28
]
:

\boldsymbol{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}_{r}+\boldsymbol{C}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}})\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r}+\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\theta})-\boldsymbol{K}\boldsymbol{s}=\boldsymbol{Y}_{S}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r},\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}_{r})\boldsymbol{\pi}-\boldsymbol{K}\boldsymbol{s},

(18)

in which, the error is defined as
\boldsymbol{e}=\boldsymbol{\theta}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{d}
, whereas

\boldsymbol{\theta}_{d}
denotes the desired trajectory in the task-space.
Moreover, the reference velocity of
\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r}
is defined as

\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r}=\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{d}-\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{e}
, and
\boldsymbol{s}=\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}-\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r}=\boldsymbol{\dot{e}}+\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{e}
indicates the sliding surface.
Furthermore,
\boldsymbol{K}
and
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}
represent symmetric positive
definite gains while,

\boldsymbol{Y}_{S}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r},\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}_{r})
is known as the
Slotine-Li regressor.

In order to derive this regressor for SPRs,
\boldsymbol{M}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
,
\boldsymbol{C}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}})
, and

\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
are substituted into Eq. (
18
). By this
means, the following relation is derived:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{n}_{S}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\boldsymbol{n}_{d}}=\boldsymbol{n}_{S_{p}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\boldsymbol{n}_{S_{i}},

(19a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{n}_{S_{p}}=\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right){\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}}_{r}+\left(S\left({{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}\right)~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right){\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}}_{r}-m_{p}~S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}\right)\boldsymbol{g}_{0},

(19b)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\boldsymbol{n}_{S_{i}}={\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}}^{T}\left(\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}\right)\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}_{r}+\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{i}}+S\left(\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}\right)~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}\right)\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r}+\right.{\cdot}\cdot\cdot\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\left.-m_{i}~S(^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i})~\boldsymbol{g}_{0}\right).\end{split}

(19c)

Analogous to Eq. (
12
), it is clear that

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r_{i}}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r}
and

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r_{i}}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}_{r}+\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{i}}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r}
. Furthermore, for SPRs,
it may be considered that
\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r}={{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r}}
and
\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}_{r}={{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r}}
. Hence, the following relation is derived by substituting
these formulas into Eq. (
21
):

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{n}_{S_{i}}={\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}}^{T}\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r_{i}}+~S(^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i})\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r_{i}}\right)-m_{i}~S(^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i})~\boldsymbol{g}_{0}\right),

(20a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{n}_{S_{p}}=\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right){{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r}}+\left(S\left({{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}\right)~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right){{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r}}-m_{p}~S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}\right)\boldsymbol{g}_{0}.

(20b)

To derive the S-L regressor from equation (
20
),
one may use

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r_{i}}=~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r_{i}}
,

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r_{i}}=~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r_{i}}
,

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}=~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}}^{T}
,
and Eq. (
11
)
. Therefore, different parts of equation of
(
20a
) will be rewritten as follows:

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r_{i}}=~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}\left(~{}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r_{i}}\right),

(21a)

\displaystyle S(^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i})\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r_{i}}\right)=~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}\left(S(^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i})\left({}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r_{i}}\right)\right),

(21b)

\displaystyle-m_{i}~S(^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i})~\boldsymbol{g}_{0}=-m_{i}~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}~S\left({}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}\right)~{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}}^{T}~\boldsymbol{g}_{0}.

(21c)

Considering
{}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{g}_{0}}=~{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}}^{T}~\boldsymbol{g}_{0}
, and
using
S\left({}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}\right)~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{g}_{0}}=-S\left({}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{g}_{0}}\right)~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}
,

Eq. (
21c
) will be rewritten as:

-m_{i}~S(^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i})~\boldsymbol{g}_{0}=~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}~S\left({}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{g}_{0}}\right)\left(m_{i}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}\right).

(22)

A similar process may be applied to the derivation of the

S-L
regression form of the moving platform
dynamic formulation. Notice that

{}^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r}={{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}}_{p}}^{T}~{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r}
,
{}^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r}={{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{p}}^{T}~{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r}
,

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}=~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{p}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}~{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{p}}^{T}
,
and
{}^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{g}_{0}}={{{}^{0}}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{p}}^{T}~\boldsymbol{g}_{0}
. Therefore,
the dynamic formulation of the SPRs with respect to the robot’s

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{n}_{S_{i}}={\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}}^{T}~{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}\left({}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r_{i}}+S(^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{i})\left({}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r_{i}}\right)+S\left({}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{g}_{0}}\right)\left(m_{i}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{i}\right)\right),

(23a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{n}_{S_{p}}=~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}_{p}}\left({}^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}~^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r}\right)+S\left({{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}\right){{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}_{p}}}\left({}^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}~^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r}\right)+S\left({}^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{g}_{0}}\right)\left(m_{p}~^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}\right).

(23b)

On the other hand, in order to transform the first and second terms of
equations of (

) and (

)
to linear forms, it may be proved that

[

,
56
]
:

\displaystyle{{}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r_{i}}=~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\tilde{\dot{\omega}}}}_{r_{i}}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\bar{I}}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}},

(24a)

\displaystyle{{}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r_{i}}=~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\tilde{\omega}}}_{r_{i}}~^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\bar{I}}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}},

(24b)

in which,
{}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\bar{I}}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}
and
{}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\tilde{\omega}}}_{i}
are
defined as:

{}^{i}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\bar{I}}}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}=[I_{xx\mathcal{A}_{i}},I_{xy\mathcal{A}_{i}},I_{xz\mathcal{A}_{i}},I_{yy\mathcal{A}_{i}},I_{yz\mathcal{A}_{i}},I_{zz\mathcal{A}_{i}}],

(25b)

Now, substitute (
24

). The

{\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\tau}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\boldsymbol{n}_{S}+\boldsymbol{n}_{d}}=\boldsymbol{Y}_{S}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r},\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}_{r})\boldsymbol{\pi},

(26)

in which,
the S-L regressor
\boldsymbol{Y}_{S}
is defined as
follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Y}_{S}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{Y}_{S_{p}}&,\boldsymbol{Y}_{{S}_{i}}&,...&,\boldsymbol{Y}_{S_{n}}\end{bmatrix},

(27a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Y}_{S_{p}}=\begin{bmatrix}{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{p}~S\left({}^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{g}_{0}}\right)&,\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{p}~^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\tilde{\dot{\omega}}}}_{r}+S\left({{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}\right)~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{p}~^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\tilde{\omega}}}_{r}\right)\end{bmatrix},

(27b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\pi}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\pi}_{p}&\boldsymbol{\pi}_{i}&...&\boldsymbol{\pi}_{n}\end{bmatrix}^{T}~,~\in\mathbb{R}^{9k},

(28a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\pi}_{p}=\begin{bmatrix}m_{p}~^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}&{}^{p}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\bar{I}}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\end{bmatrix}^{T},

(28b)

(28c)

.

3.2
Unique Regressor Reduction

Careful examinations of regressor forms stated in
Eq. (
27
) reveal the stringent requirement of
order reduction to eliminate the zero or linearly dependent elements
in the regressor. In practice, although using a reduced regressor in
adaptive model-based controllers does not improve the performance of the controller, it significantly decreases the required real-time computational process. On the other hand, in the calibration schemes
in which linear regression form of robot dynamics is used, it is
inevitable to use a reduced regressor to avoid having a
rank-deficient observation matrix and to make elements of the
inertial parameters fully identifiable.

For this means, we want to eliminate the zero or
linearly dependent elements in the obtained regressor.

For this purpose, the method mentioned in
Ref.
[
57
]
, which has a unique solution, is
used.
To this end, one may represent the observation matrix of SPRs
as:

\boldsymbol{W}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}},\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}})=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{Y}(t_{1})^{T}&...&\boldsymbol{Y}(t_{k})^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}~,~\in\mathbb{R}^{jm\times 9k},

(29)

in which
j
represents the number of observations.

If
j
is sufficiently large, the condition
jm\geq 9k
shall be met for the system to be observable
. Hence, the
reduced regressor matrix
\boldsymbol{Y}_{r}
and the
BIPs

\boldsymbol{\pi}_{r}
shall be found such that to preserve the full rank
p
of
the observation matrix
W
, in order for the system to be
observable. In such a case, it may be proved that there is a unique
transformation matrix
\boldsymbol{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times 9k}
and a matrix

\boldsymbol{S}
, spanning the row space of the observation matrix such that
there exist a generalized inverse
\boldsymbol{B}^{\dagger}=\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{B}^{T}\left(\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{B}^{T}\right)^{-1}
.

In order to derive the
\boldsymbol{B}
matrix, it suffices
to calculate the reduced-row echelon form of
\boldsymbol{W}
, which is the
upper triangular matrix
\boldsymbol{B}_{E}
. By eliminating the zero rows of

\boldsymbol{B}_{E}
, the matrix
\boldsymbol{B}
is derived.
By this means,
\boldsymbol{Y}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times p}
and
\boldsymbol{\pi}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
are derived as:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Y}_{r}=\boldsymbol{Y}\boldsymbol{B}^{\dagger},

(30a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\pi}_{r}=\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{\pi}.

(30b)

It should be noted that, the
\boldsymbol{B}_{E}
matrix is
only a function of the kinematic parameters of the robot, and has
numerical values. Thus, to calculate it,
rref
command in MATLAB
can be used for the above mentioned calculations.

as
:

\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{3}}=\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{1}}+\begin{bmatrix}\textbf{0}_{3\times 1}&-\left(\dot{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{b}}+s\cdot\boldsymbol{\dot{\hat{b}}}\right)\end{bmatrix},~~\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{4}}=\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{2}}+\begin{bmatrix}\textbf{0}_{3\times 1}&-\left(\dot{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{c}}+s\cdot\boldsymbol{\dot{\hat{c}}}\right)\end{bmatrix}.

(41)

the 3-

.
The kinematic

]

4.2.1
Robot Geometry

For kinematic analysis of the 3-
R
RR spherical parallel manipulator,
the orientation of the moving platform is described using
ZYX
fixed-body
Euler angles. Therefore,
\boldsymbol{\theta}=[\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}]^{T}
are
considered as the task space variables,

Thus, the rotation matrix of the moving
platform may be derived as
{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{p}=\boldsymbol{R}_{z}(\theta_{1})\boldsymbol{R}_{y}(\theta_{2})\boldsymbol{R}_{x}(\theta_{3})
.

In the 3-
R
RR manipulator, unit vectors
\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}
,

\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}
, and
\boldsymbol{\hat{w}}_{i}
, for
\rm i=1,2,3
, all intersect at the CR point, which is defined as the origin of the base
coordinate system. Therefore, the unit vector of the primary and the
intermediate revolute joints can be formulated as:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}=\boldsymbol{R}_{z}(\lambda_{i})\boldsymbol{R}_{x}(\gamma-\pi)[0,0,1]^{T},

(42a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\hat{w}}_{i}=\boldsymbol{R}_{z}(\lambda_{i})\boldsymbol{R}_{x}(\gamma-\pi)\boldsymbol{R}_{z}(q_{i})\boldsymbol{R}_{x}(\alpha_{1_{i}})[0,0,1]^{T}.

(42b)

Moreover,
\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}
may be derived as follows:

\displaystyle{{\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}}_{i}}^{*}=\boldsymbol{R}_{z}(\eta_{i})\boldsymbol{R}_{x}(-\beta)[0,0,1]^{T},

(43a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}=~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}_{p}~{{\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}}_{i}}^{*},

(43b)

in which,
{\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}}^{*}
represents the unit vector
{\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}}
in the initial
configuration of the moving platform.

4.2.2
Differential Kinematic Analysis

Before beginning the Jacobian analysis of the 3-
R
RR
manipulator, it should be noted that by choosing the Euler angles as a way
to describe the moving platform orientation, the angular velocity of the moving platform would not be equal to the rate of change of the Euler
angles,
\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}=[\dot{\theta}_{1},\dot{\theta}_{2},\dot{\theta}_{3}]^{T}
. Instead,
this relationship between these two vectors would be defined as:

{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}=\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}},

(44)

in which,
\boldsymbol{E}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})
relates the
rates of the Euler angles to the angular velocity of the moving platform. In the
ZYX
fixed body Euler angles, this matrix is given by:

\boldsymbol{E}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\sin(\theta_{1})&\cos(\theta_{1})\cos(\theta_{2})\\
0&\cos(\theta_{1})&\sin(\theta_{1})\cos(\theta_{2})\\
1&0&-\sin(\theta_{2})\end{bmatrix}.\\

(45)

Therefore, the angular velocity of the moving platform may be
derived as the sum of its successive angular rotations as:

{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}=\dot{q}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}+\dot{\psi}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{w}}_{i}+\dot{\zeta}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}.

(46)

In this equation,
\dot{q}_{i}
and
\dot{\psi}_{i}
are derived by dot-multiplying both
sides of the equation of (
46
) in
\left(\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}\times\boldsymbol{\hat{w}}_{i}\right)
and
\left(\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}\times\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}\right)
, respectively, which
results in:

\displaystyle\dot{q}_{i}=\frac{\left(\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}\times\boldsymbol{\hat{w}}_{i}\right)}{\left(\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}\times\boldsymbol{\hat{w}}_{i}\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}}\cdot{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}~{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}~\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}}~~,~~\rm for~~i=1,2,3,

(47a)

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}_{i}=\frac{\left(\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}\times\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}\right)}{\left(\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}\times\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{w}}_{i}}\cdot{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}~{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{p_{i}}}~\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}}~~,~~\rm for~~i=1,2,3.

(47b)

Now, in order to derive the Jacobian
matrices
from equations of
(
47a
) and (
47b
), the following
parameters are defined for the sake of simplification:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}_{1_{i}}=\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}\times\boldsymbol{\hat{w}}_{i},

(48a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}_{2_{i}}=\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}\times\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i},

(48b)

\displaystyle h_{i}=\left(\boldsymbol{\hat{v}}_{i}\times\boldsymbol{\hat{w}}_{i}\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}=\boldsymbol{p}_{1_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}.

(48c)

Finally, using equations of (
44
),
(
47
), and (
48
), each row
of the input-output Jacobian and the passive Jacobian matrices are derived as:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}~={h_{i}}^{-1}~{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1_{i}}}^{T}~\boldsymbol{E},~~\rm{for}~~i=1,2,3,

(49a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{J}_{{\omega_{p}}_{i}}={h_{i}}^{-1}~{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2_{i}}}^{T}~\boldsymbol{E},~~\rm{for}~~i=1,2,3.

(49b)

4.2.3
Jacobian Analysis of Links

The angular velocity of the
proximal and distal links
of each
kinematic chain may be written as:

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{1_{i}}=\dot{q}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}~{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{1_{i}}}~\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}},

(50a)

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{2_{i}}=~^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{1_{i}}+\dot{\psi}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{w}}_{i}~{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{2_{i}}}~\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}}.

(50b)

Therefore, using equations (
47a
) and
(
47b
), each row of the Jacobian matrix of the
proximal and distal links are formulated as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{1_{i}}}={\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}}~\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}},

(51a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{2_{i}}}=\boldsymbol{J}_{1_{i}}+{\boldsymbol{\hat{w}}_{i}}~\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{p_{i}}}.

(51b)

4.2.4
Acceleration Analysis

In order to derive the acceleration terms in the proposed dynamic
formulation, it is necessary to obtain the time derivative of
\dot{q}_{i}

and
\dot{\psi}_{i}
. Thus, the time derivative of each row of the
corresponding Jacobian matrices
\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega}
and
\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{p}}
is computed symbolically as:

\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{i}}=\frac{{\boldsymbol{\dot{p}}_{1_{i}}}^{T}\boldsymbol{E}+{\boldsymbol{p}_{1_{i}}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}{h_{i}}-\frac{{\boldsymbol{p}_{1_{i}}}^{T}\boldsymbol{E}~\dot{h}_{i}}{{h_{i}}^{2}},

(52)

\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{{\omega_{p}}_{i}}=\frac{{\boldsymbol{\dot{p}}_{2_{i}}}^{T}\boldsymbol{E}+{\boldsymbol{p}_{2_{i}}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\dot{E}}}{h_{i}}-\frac{{\boldsymbol{p}_{2_{i}}}^{T}\boldsymbol{E}~\dot{h}_{i}}{{h_{i}}^{2}},

(53)

In these equations, the method of calculating the time derivatives of vectors of

\boldsymbol{p}_{1_{i}}
and
\boldsymbol{p}_{2_{i}}
, and the value of scalar
{h}_{i}
is given in Appendix
B
. Therefore, the time
derivative of the Jacobian matrix of the proximal and the distal links may
be represented by:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{1_{i}}}=\boldsymbol{\hat{u}}_{i}~\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{i}},

(54a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{2_{i}}}=\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{1_{i}}}+\left(\boldsymbol{\dot{\hat{w}}}_{i}~\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{p_{i}}}+\boldsymbol{\hat{w}}_{i}~\boldsymbol{\dot{J}}_{\omega_{p_{i}}}\right),

(54b)

in which,
\boldsymbol{\dot{\hat{w}}}_{i}
is given in the Appendix
B
.

4.2.5
Dynamic Formulations

As mentioned earlier, the angular velocity of the moving platform is
related to the Euler angles rate with
{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}=\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}
. In the
Jacobian analysis of each link, the effect of the
\boldsymbol{E}
matrix has been
considered. Thus, the terms related to the dynamics of the moving platform
given in Eq. (
10a
), are rewritten as follows:

{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{J}}_{\omega}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\tau}=\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right){{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}}+S\left({{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}\right)\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right){{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}-m_{i}~S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}\right)\boldsymbol{g}_{0},

(55)

in which each row of the Jacobian matrix
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{J}}_{\omega}}
is defined by
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{J}}_{\omega}}_{i}={h_{i}}^{-1}~{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1_{i}}}^{T}
. Although the Eq. (
55
) has an explicit form, it is written
based on
{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}}
and
{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}}
terms, while explicit dynamics
should be in terms of
\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}
and
\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}
to have the same
force distribution as the dynamics of each link. As a result, using
the properties of
{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}=\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}
and
{{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}}=\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}+\boldsymbol{\dot{E}}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}
, and multiplying
\boldsymbol{E}^{T}
to the left
side of the Eq. (
55
), the explicit dynamic of
the moving platform based on
\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}
and
\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}
, may be
written as follows:

{\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega}}^{T}\boldsymbol{\tau}=\boldsymbol{M}_{p}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}+\boldsymbol{C}_{p}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\theta})\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}+\boldsymbol{g}_{p}(\boldsymbol{\theta}).

(56)

In this representation, each row of the Jacobian matrix

\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{i}}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{J}}_{{\omega}_{i}}\boldsymbol{E},~\rm{for}~i=1,2,3
, is equal
to each row of the Jacobian matrix of the whole manipulator, given
in equation (
49a
). Moreover, the explicit dynamic of
the moving platform may be represented by the following matrices:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{M}_{p}=\boldsymbol{E}^{T}\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right)\boldsymbol{E},

(57a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{C}_{p}=\boldsymbol{E}^{T}\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right)\boldsymbol{\dot{E}}+\boldsymbol{E}^{T}\left(S(\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}})\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right)\right)\boldsymbol{E},

(57b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{g}_{p}=-\boldsymbol{E}^{T}\left(m_{i}~S\left({}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{p}\right)\boldsymbol{g}_{0}\right).

(57c)

The same process must be followed for the derivation of the
corresponding terms related to the moving platform in the

regressors
.
Therefore, it is
enough to use
{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{r}=\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r}
and

{}^{0}\mathrm{\boldsymbol{\dot{\omega}}}_{r}=\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{\ddot{\theta}}_{r}+\boldsymbol{\dot{E}}\boldsymbol{\dot{\theta}}_{r}
and
multiply the left side of equation (
27b
)
by
\boldsymbol{E}^{T}
.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, different forms of dynamic
formulations of spherical parallel robots (SPRs) including explicit
dynamics, linear and Slotine-Li (S-L) regressors were formulated to
be used in the design of model-based controllers and dynamic
identification schemes.

To this end, the implicit
dynamic of SPRs was first formulated using the principle of virtual
work in task-space, and then by an extension, their explicit dynamic formulation was derived.

The linear and S-L
regressor forms of SPRs were then obtained using explicit dynamic,
and by using the Gauss-Jordan procedure, regressor forms are reduced
to a unique and closed-form structure.
Finally, to verify the
proposed formulations,
two SPR case studies
,
namely, the ARAS-Diamond robot and the 3-
R
RR spherical
manipulator examined. The results of the explicit dynamics, linear
regression form of robots’ dynamics, S-L regressor, and the
corresponding reduced form regressors were verified by comparing
them with those obtained by
MSC-ADAMS
®
package. The proposed method opens the possibility of implementing a wide range of
adaptive model-based controllers and regressor-based identification
schemes on SPRs.

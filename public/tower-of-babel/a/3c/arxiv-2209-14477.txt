Use of indicator functions to enumerate cross-array designs without direct product structure
Satoshi Aoki, Masayuki Noro

Abstract
Use of polynomial indicator functions to enumerate fractional factorial designs with given properties is first introduced by Fontana, Pistone and Rogantin (2000) for two-level factors, and generalized by Aoki (2019) for multi-level factors. In this paper, we apply this theory to enumerate cross-array designs. For the experiments of several control factors and noise factors, use of the cross-array designs with direct product structure is widespread as an effective robust strategy in Taguchi method. In this paper, we relax this direct product structure to reduce the size of the designs. We obtain 24 24 -runs cross-array designs without direct product structure with some desirable properties for 6 6 control factors and 3 3 noise factors, each with two-levels, instead of 32 32 -runs design that is widely used. Keywords: Computational algebraic statistics, Cross-array designs, Fractional factorial designs, Gröbner bases, Indicator functions, Taguchi methods.

2
Constructing fractions using indicator functions

In this section, we summarize the theory on the indicator function of
designs and its use to construct designs.
The arguments are based on the theory of the interpolatory
polynomials on designs, which is one of the first applications of
Gröbner bases to statistics by
[
6
]
.
The arguments in this section are given in
[
1
]
in general
settings. We give the arguments in the setting of the cross-array designs in
this section.

Let
x_{1},.\ldots,x_{p}
be
p
control factors. Let
A_{j}\subset\mathbb{Q}
be a level set of a control factor
x_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,p
,
where
\mathbb{Q}
denotes the field of rational numbers. A full
factorial design of the control factors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}
is
D_{x}=A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{p}
. A subset of
D_{x}
,
F_{x}\subset D_{x}
, is
called a fractional factorial design of the control factors.
We also define similar materials for
q
noise factors
y_{1},\ldots,y_{q}
,
i.e., let
B_{j}\subset\mathbb{Q}
be a level set of a noise factor

y_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,q
,
D_{y}=B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{q}
be a full
factorial design, and
F_{y}\subset D_{y}
be a fractional factorial design
for the noise factors
y_{1},\ldots,y_{q}
.

For fractional factorial designs
F_{x}\subset D_{x}
and
F_{y}\subset D_{y}
,

F_{x}\times F_{y}
is a cross-array design with a direct product
structure.
In the theory of Taguchi method, use of direct product type
cross-array designs
F_{x}\times F_{y}

is recommended for

regular
fractional factorial designs
F_{x}
and
F_{y}
.
In this paper, we consider
general fractional factorial designs of
F_{x}\times D_{y}
for a regular
fractional factorial design
F_{x}\subset D_{x}
as follows.
We assume that the levels of the
r

(r<p)

control factors
x_{p-r+1},\ldots,x_{p}
are determined from the
levels of the remaining
p-r

(=s)
control factors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}
by

x_{s+j}=g_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}),\ j=1,\ldots,r,

(1)

where
g_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
for
j=1,\ldots,r
.
Under this relation, the fractional factorial design

\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s},g_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}),\ldots,g_{r}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}))\ |\ (x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})\in A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{s}\}\subset D_{x}

is called a regular fractional factorial design of
D_{x}
with the
defining relation
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}
.

Remark 2.1
.

In the textbooks such as
[
8
]

or
[
7
]
, the regular fractional factorial design is
mainly explained for two or three level factors. For two-level cases,
which we also consider in this paper, it is common to define such
as
x_{4}=g_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=x_{1}x_{2}
for the level
\{-1,1\}
. For
three-level cases, it is also common to define by “mod
3
operation”
such as

x_{4}=x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}\ (\mbox{mod}\ 3)

for the level set
\{0,1,2\}
.
Though our definition of the regular designs by (
1
)
seems different from the conventional definition given in the textbooks
in this field, our definition is more general and holds for factors
with different number of levels. Note that it is also possible to
represent the above
x_{4}
by the polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
as
the interpolation function on
\{0,1,2\}^{3}
with the response

x_{4}
.

We write the full factorial design of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}
as

D_{x}^{*}=A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{s}
and consider fractional factorial
designs of
D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}
in this paper. Now we introduce an
indicator function on
D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
4
]
)
.

The indicator function of
F\subset D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}

is a response function
f
on
D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}
satisfying

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s},y_{1},\ldots,y_{q})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\mbox{if}\ (x_{1},\ldots,x_{s},y_{1},\ldots,y_{q})\in F,\\
0,&\mbox{if}\ (x_{1},\ldots,x_{s},y_{1},\ldots,y_{q})\in(D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y})\setminus F.\end{array}\right.

To show the existence, uniqueness and construction of the polynomial indicator
function below,
we prepare the set
L\subset\mathbb{Z}^{s+q}_{\geq 0}
by

L=\left\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{s},b_{1},\ldots,b_{q})\ \left|\ \begin{array}[]{l}0\leq a_{i}\leq|A_{i}|-1,\ i=1,\ldots,s,\\
0\leq b_{j}\leq|B_{j}|-1,\ j=1,\ldots,q\end{array}\right.\right\},

where
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be the set of nonnegative integers, and

|A_{i}|
and
|B_{j}|
be the cardinality of
A_{i}
and
B_{j}
, respectively.
By ordering the points of
D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}
, we write

D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}=\{\bm{{\bm{d}}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{{\bm{d}}}_{m}\},

where
m=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{s}|A_{j}|\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{q}|B_{j}|\right)
is the size of
D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}
, and

\bm{{\bm{d}}}_{i}=(x_{i1},\ldots,x_{is},y_{i1},\ldots,y_{iq}),\ i=1,\ldots,m,

where
x_{ij}
and
y_{ij}
are the levels of the factors
x_{j}
and
y_{j}

in the
i
th design point (i.e.,
i
th run), respectively.
For each
\bm{{\bm{c}}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{s},b_{1},\ldots,b_{q})\in L
, write the
monomial as

\bm{{\bm{d}}}_{i}^{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}=x_{i1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{is}^{a_{s}}y_{i1}^{b_{1}}\cdots y_{iq}^{b_{q}}

for
i=1,\ldots,m
. By ordering the elements of
L
, we define a model
matrix of
D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}
by

X=\left[\bm{{\bm{d}}}_{i}^{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}\right]_{i=1,\ldots,m;\ \bm{{\bm{c}}}\in L}.

Note that
X
is called a design matrix in Definition 26 of

[
5
]
, and is an
m\times m
nonsingular
matrix (Theorem 26 of
[
5
]
).
For the variables
\bm{{\bm{z}}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s},y_{1},\ldots,y_{q})
and
\bm{{\bm{c}}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{s},b_{1},\ldots,b_{q})\in L
, we also write the monomial as

\bm{{\bm{z}}}^{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{s}^{a_{s}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots y_{q}^{b_{q}},

and write the indicator function of
F\subset D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}
as

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s},b_{1},\ldots,b_{q})=f(\bm{{\bm{z}}})=\sum_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}\in L}\theta_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}\bm{{\bm{z}}}^{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}.

Then the
m\times 1
column vector
\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}=\left[\theta_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}\right]_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}\in L}
is given by
\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}=X^{-1}\bm{{\bm{u}}}
, where
\bm{{\bm{u}}}=\left[u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\right]^{T}\in\{0,1\}^{m}
is
an
m\times 1
column vector of a response on
D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}=\{\bm{{\bm{d}}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{{\bm{d}}}_{m}\}

satisfying

u_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\mbox{if}\ \bm{{\bm{d}}}_{i}\in F,\\
0,&\mbox{if}\ \bm{{\bm{d}}}_{i}\in(D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y})\setminus F.\end{array}\right.

(2)

See Theorem 26 of
[
5
]
for detail.
An important fact is that the set
\{\bm{{\bm{z}}}^{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}\ |\ \bm{{\bm{c}}}\in L\}
is a
basis of
\mathbb{Q}[\bm{{\bm{z}}}]/I(D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y})
as a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector
space, where
I(D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y})
is the design ideal of
D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}
, i.e., the
set of polynomials in

\mathbb{Q}[\bm{{\bm{z}}}]
which are
0
at every point of
D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}
. This fact guarantees the existence and the uniqueness of the
polynomial indicator function of
F\subset D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}
. See Theorem
15 of
[
5
]
for detail.

The fractional factorial designs with given properties can be
characterized as the zero points of the
0
-dimensional ideal defined
for the coefficients of the corresponding indicator function. Let
{\cal G}
be the reduced Gröbner basis of
I(D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y})
for some
monomial order on
\bm{{\bm{z}}}
. Then a polynomial
f(\bm{{\bm{z}}})=\sum_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}\in L}\theta_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}\bm{{\bm{z}}}^{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}
is an indicator function of some fractional
factorial design of
D_{x}^{*}\times D_{y}
if and only if the system of
the algebraic equations

\theta_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}=\mu_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}},\ \ \bm{{\bm{c}}}\in L

(3)

hold, where
\sum_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}\in L}\mu_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}\bm{{\bm{z}}}^{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}
is the standard form
of
\sum_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}_{1}\in L}\sum_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}_{2}\in L}\theta_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}_{1}}\theta_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}_{2}}\bm{{\bm{z}}}^{\bm{{\bm{c}}}_{1}+\bm{{\bm{c}}}_{2}}
with respect to

{\cal G}
(Proposition 3.1 of
[
1
]
).
Therefore, if we give some properties for the fractional factorial
designs as
k
constraints
in the form of
C\bm{{\bm{u}}}=\bm{{\bm{h}}}
for the corresponding response
vector
\bm{{\bm{u}}}
of (
2
), where

is a

matrix and

\bm{{\bm{h}}}
is a

column vector,
we can define a polynomial ideal of
\mathbb{Q}[\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}]
by

I=\left<\{\theta_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}}-\mu_{\bm{{\bm{c}}}},\ \bm{{\bm{c}}}\in L\},CX\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}-\bm{{\bm{h}}}\right>,

(4)

and the variety defined by the polynomial ideal

V(I)=\{\bm{{\bm{\theta}}}\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}\ |\ f(\bm{{\bm{\theta}}})=0,\ \forall f\in I\},

corresponds to the set of the coefficients of the indicator functions
of the fractional factorial designs with the given properties.
Note that
V(I)
is finite, i.e.,

is a
0
-dimensional ideal. See

[
1
]
and

[
2
]
for detail.

In this paper, we consider using the indicator functions to
enumerate cross-array designs with some desirable properties. Although
the theory of the indicator functions to enumerate designs is already given
in previous papers, the computational feasibility becomes a problem in
practice. In this paper, to avoid calculation for
2^{9}=512

in a naive approach, we define the ideal for

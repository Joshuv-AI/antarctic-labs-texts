Unital algebras being Morita equivalent to weighted Leavitt path algebras
Roozbeh Hazrat, Tran Giang Nam

Abstract
. In this article, we describe the endomorphism ring of a finitely generated progenerator module of a weighted Leavitt path algebra L 𝗄 ​ ( E , w ) L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w) of a finite vertex weighted graph ( E , w ) (E,w) . Contrary to the case of Leavitt path algebras, we show that a (full) corner of a weighted Leavitt path algebra is, in general, not isomorphic to a weighted Leavitt path algebra. However, using the above result, we show that for every full idempotent ϵ \epsilon in L 𝗄 ​ ( E , w ) L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w) , there exists a positive integer n n such that 𝕄 n ​ ( ϵ ​ L 𝗄 ​ ( E , w ) ​ ϵ ) \mathbb{M}_{n}(\epsilon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)\epsilon) is isomorphic to the weighted Leavitt path algebra of a weighted graph explicitly constructed from ( E , w ) (E,w) . We then completely describe unital algebras being Morita equivalent to weighted Leavitt path algebras of vertex weighted graphs. In particular, we characterize unital algebras being Morita equivalent to sandpile algebras. 1.
Introduction

The notion of abelian sandpile models was invented in
1987
by Bak, Tang and Wiesenfeld
[
11
]
. While defined by a simple local rule, it produces self-similar global patterns that call for an explanation. The models have been used to describe phenomena such as forest fires, traffic jams, stock market fluctuations, etc. The book of Bak
[
12
]
describes how events in nature apparently follow this type of behaviour. In
[
15
]
Dhar systematically associated finite abelian monoid and groups to sandpile models, now called respectively
sandpile monoids
and
sandpile groups
, and championed the use of them as an invariant which proved to capture many properties of the model. These algebraic structures constitute one of the main themes of the subject and they have been extensively studied (see, e.g.,
[
22
,
10
,
14
]
and references there). The abelian sandpile model was independently discovered in
1991
by combinatorialists Björner, Lovász and Shor
[
13
]
, who called it
chip-firing
. Indeed, in the last two decades the subject has been enriched by an exhilarating interaction of numerous areas of mathematics, including statistical physics, combinatorics, free boundary PDE, probability, potential theory, number theory and group theory; refer to Levine and Peres’s recent survey on the subject
[
19
]
in more details.

In a different realm, the notion of Leavitt path algebras
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
associated to directed graphs
E
were introduced by Abrams and Aranda Pino in
[
1
]
, and independently Ara, Moreno, and Pardo in
[
8
]
. These are a generalisation of algebras introduced by William Leavitt in 1962
[
18
]
as a “universal” algebra
A
of type
(1,n)
, so that
A\cong A^{n}
as right
A
-modules, where
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. It was established quite early on in the theory that Leavitt path algebras only produce rings of ranks
(1,n)
. In
[
18
]
Leavitt further constructed rings of type
(m,n)
, where
1<m<n
and showed that these algebras surprisingly are domains. When
m\geq 2
, this universal ring is not realizable as a Leavitt path algebra. With this in mind, the notion of
weighted
Leavitt path algebras
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
associated to weighted graphs
(E,w)
were introduced by the first author in
[
16
]
(see
[
21
]
for a nice overview of this topic). The weighted Leavitt path algebras provide a natural context in which all of Leavitt’s algebras (corresponding to any pair
m,,n\in\mathbb{N}
) can be realised as a specific example.

The study of the commutative monoid
\mathcal{V}(A)
of isomorphism classes of finitely generated projective left modules of a unital ring
A
(with operation
\oplus
) goes back to the work of Grothendieck and Serre. For a Leavitt path algebra
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
, the monoid
\mathcal{V}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))
has received substantial attention since the introduction of the topic. Furthermore, the monoid
\mathcal{V}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))
has been described completely by the first author in
[
16
]
, and subsequently by Preusser in
[
20
]
.
In
[
4
]
Abrams and the first author established that these monoids could be naturally related to the sandpile monoids of graphs. This relationship allows us to associate an algebra, a sandpile algebra (see
[
4
, Definition 6.11]
), to the theory of sandpile models, thereby opening up an avenue by which to investigate sandpile models via the structure of weighted Leavitt path algebras (particularly the sandpile algebras), and vice versa.

In this article, we investigate the relations between these objects by characterizing unital algebras which are Morita equivalent to sandpile algebras. More precisely, we completely describe unital algebras which are Morita equivalent to weighted Leavitt path algebras of finite vertex weighted graphs–containing the class of sandpile algebras as a special example.

The article is organized as follows. In Section 2, for the reader’s convenience, we collect some basic definitions and facts on weighted graphs and weighted Leavitt path algebras, as well as provide a description of the quotient of a weighted Leavitt path algebra by an ideal generated a set of vertices (Theorem
2.10
) and the matrix ring over a weighted Leavitt path algebra (Theorem
2.14
).

In Section 3, based on Theorem
2.10
and the structure of the monoid
\mathcal{V}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))
established by the first author in
[
16
, Theorem 5.21]
, we completely describe the endomorphism ring of a projective and finitely generated generator for the category of left modules over the weighted Levitt path algebra
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
of a finite vertex weighted graph
(E,w)
(Theorem
3.7
). Consequently, this yields that for every full idempotent
\epsilon
in
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
, there exists a positive integer
n
such that
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\epsilon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)\epsilon)
is isomorphic to the weighted Leavitt path algebra of a finite vertex weighted graph explicitly constructed from
(E,w)
(Corollary
3.9
). Also, we show that a corner of a weighted Leavitt path algebra of a finite vertex weighted graph is, in general, not a weighted Leavitt path algebra (Example
3.10
). We should mention every corner of a Leavitt path algebra of a finite graph is always isomorphic to a Leavitt path algebra (see
[
6
, Theorem 3.15]
), and a corner of a Leavitt path algebra of an arbitrary graph need not in general be isomorphic to a Leavitt path algebra (see
[
3
, Example 2.11 and Corollary 3.8]
).
As a consequence of Theorem
2.14
and
3.9
, we obtain that a unital
\mathsf{k}
-algebra
A
is Morita equivalent to the weighted Leavitt path algebra
L_{K}(E,w)
of a finite vertex weighted graph
(E,w)
if and only if there exists a positive integer
n
such that
\mathbb{M}_{n}(A)
is isomorphic to the weighted Leavitt path algebra of a finite vertex weighted graph explicitly constructed from
(E,w)
(Theorem
3.13
). In particular, we completely describe unital algebras being Morita equivalent to sandpile algebras (Corollary
3.14
).

Throughout we write
\mathbb{N}
for the set of non-negative integers, and
\mathbb{N}^{+}
for the set of positive integers.

2.
Weighted Leavitt path algebras

In this section, we give a description of the quotient of a weighted Leavitt path algebra by an ideal generated a set of vertices (Theorem
2.10
) and the matrix ring over a weighted Leavitt path algebra (Theorem
2.14
).

A
(directed) graph
is a quadruple
E=(E^{0},E^{1},s,r)
, where
E^{0}
and
E^{1}
are sets and
s,r:E^{1}\rightarrow E^{0}
are maps. The elements of
E^{0}
are called

vertices
and the elements of
E^{1}

edges
. If
e
is an edge, then
s(e)
is called its
source
and
r(e)
its
range
. If
v
is a vertex and
e
an edge, we say that
v

emits

e
if
s(e)=v
, and
v

receives

e
if
r(e)=v
. A graph
E
is called
row-finite
if any vertex in
E
emits a finite number (possibly zero) of edges. The graph
E
is called
finite
if
E^{0}
and
E^{1}
are finite sets.
A vertex is called a
sink
if it emits no edges.
A vertex is called
regular
if it is not a sink and does not emit infinitely many edges. The subset of
E^{0}
consisting of all the regular vertices is denoted by
E^{0}_{\operatorname{reg}}
. A vertex is called a
course
if it receives no edges.

A
path

p
in
E
is a sequence
p=e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{n}
of edges in
E
such that
r(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. In this case, we say that the path
p
starts at the vertex
s(p):=s(e_{1})
and and ends at the vertex
r(p):=r(e_{n})
, and has
length

|p|:=n
. We assign the length zero to vertices. A
closed path
(based at
v
) is a path
p
such that
s(p)=r(p)=v
. A
cycle
(based at
v
) is a closed path
p=e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{n}
based at
v
such that
s(e_{i})\neq s(e_{j})
for any
i\neq j
. A cycle
c
is called a
loop
if
|c|=1
.

A subset
H\subseteq E^{0}
is said to be
hereditary
if
for any
e\in E^{1}
,
s(e)\in H
implies
r(e)\in H
.
For a hereditary subset
H
of
E
, we define the quotient graph
E/H
as follows:

(E/H)^{0}=E^{0}\setminus H^{0}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ (E/H)^{1}=\{e\in E^{1}\;|\ r(e)\notin H\}.

The source and range maps of
E/H
are the source and range maps restricted from the graph
E
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
weighted graph
is a pair
(E,w)
, where
E
is a graph and
w:E^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{+}
is a map. If
e\in E^{1}
, then
w(e)
is called the
weight
of
e
. A weighted graph
(E,w)
is called
row-finite
if the graph
E
is row-finite. A weighted graph
(E,w)
is called
finite
if the graph
E
is finite.

For each regular vertex
v
in a weighted graph
(E,w)
we set
w(v):=\max\{w(e)\mid e\in s^{-1}(v)\}
. This gives a map (called
w
again)
w:E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{+}
.

A row-finite weighted graph
(E,w)
is called a
vertex weighted graph
if for any
v\in E^{0}_{\operatorname{reg}}
,
w(e)=w(e^{\prime})
for all
e,e^{\prime}\in s^{-1}(v)
.

A vertex weighted graph is called a
balanced weighted graph
if
w(v)=|s^{-1}(v)|
for all
v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0}
.

It is worth mentioning the following note.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
(E,w)
be a row-finite weighted graph and
H
a hereditary subset of
E^{0}
. We form the quotient graph
E/H
. Then, there is a natural way to define a weight function
w_{r}
on
E/H
by restricting the weight function on
E
to
E/H
.

We next recall the notion of weighted Leavitt path algebras. These are algebras associated to row-finite weighted graphs. We refer the reader to
[
16
]
and
[
21
]
for a detailed analysis of these algebras.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(E,w)
be a row-finite weighted graph and
\mathsf{k}
a field. The free
\mathsf{k}
-algebra generated by
\{v,e_{i},e_{i}^{*}\mid v\in E^{0},e\in E^{1},1\leq i\leq w(e)\}
subject to relations

(i)

uv=\delta_{uv}u,\text{ where }u,v\in E^{0}
,

(ii)

s(e)e_{i}=e_{i}=e_{i}r(e),~r(e)e_{i}^{*}=e_{i}^{*}=e_{i}^{*}s(e),\text{ where }e\in E^{1},1\leq i\leq w(e)
,

(iii)

\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}e_{i}e_{j}^{*}=\delta_{ij}v,\text{ where }v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0}\text{ and }1\leq i,j\leq w(v)
,

(iv)

\sum_{1\leq i\leq w(v)}e_{i}^{*}f_{i}=\delta_{ef}r(e),\text{ where }v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0}\text{ and }e,f\in s^{-1}(v)
,

is called the
weighted Leavitt path algebra
of
(E,w)
over
\mathsf{k}
, and denoted
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
, where
\delta
is the Kronecker delta. In relations (iii) and (iv) we set
e_{i}
and
e_{i}^{*}
to be zero whenever
i>w(e)
.

Note that if the weight of each edge is
1
, then
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
reduces to the usual Leavitt path algebra
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
of the graph
E
. It is easy to see that the mappings given by
v\longmapsto v
for all
v\in E^{0}
,
e_{i}\longmapsto e^{*}_{i}
and
e^{*}_{i}\longmapsto e_{i}
for all
e\in E^{1}
and
1\leq i\leq w(e)
, produce an involution on the
\mathsf{k}
-algebra
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
. Also, if the weighted graph
(E,w)
is finite, then
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
is a unital ring having identity
1_{L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)}=\sum_{v\in E^{0}}v
(see
[
16
, Proposition 5.7 (2)]
). Moreover,
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
has the following property: if
\mathcal{A}
is a
\mathsf{k}
-algebra generated by a family of elements
\{a_{v},b_{e},c_{e^{*}}\mid v\in E^{0},e\in E^{1},1\leq i\leq w(e)\}
satisfying the relations analogous to (1) - (4) in Definition
2.3
, then there is a unique
\mathsf{k}
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi:L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}
given by
\varphi(v)=a_{v}
,
\varphi(e)=b_{e}
and
\varphi(e^{*})=c_{e^{*}}
. We will refer to this property as the Universal Property of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
.

In
[
17
]
Hazrat and Preusser found a Gröbner-Shirshov basis of a weighted Leavitt path algebra. We will represent again this result in Theorem
2.4
below. To do so, we need to recall some notions. Given a row-finite weighted graph
(E,w)
let
s(e_{i}):=s(e)
,
r(e_{i})=r(e)
,
s(e^{*}_{i})=r(e)
and
r(e^{*}_{i})=s(e)
for all
e\in E^{1}
and
1\leq i\leq w(e)
. Let
\langle X\rangle
denote the set of all nonempty words over
X:=\{v,e_{i},e^{*}_{i}\mid v\in E^{0},e\in E^{1},1\leq i\leq w(e)\}
. A word
p\in\langle X\rangle
is called a
generalized path
in
(E,w)
if either
p=x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
for some
n\geq 1
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\{e_{i},e_{i}^{*}\mid e\in E^{1},1\leq i\leq w(e)\}
such that
r(x_{k})=s(x_{k+1})
for all
1\leq k\leq n-1
or
p=x_{1}
for some
x_{1}\in E^{0}
.

For every
v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0}
fix an
\alpha^{v}\in s^{-1}(v)
such that
w(\alpha^{v})=w(v)
. The words

\alpha^{v}_{i}(\alpha^{v}_{j})^{*}
(
v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0},\ 1\leq i,j\leq w(v)
) and
e_{1}^{*}f_{1}
(
v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0},\ e,f\in s^{-1}(v)
)

are called
forbidden
. A generalised path is called
normal
if none of its subword is forbidden.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
17
, Theorem 16]
)
.

Let
(E,w)
be a row-finite weighted graph and
\mathsf{k}
a field. Then the weighted Leavitt path algebra
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
has a basis consisting of normal generalized paths.

Let
(E,w_{E})
be a row-finite weighted graph. A
weighted subgraph

X
of the weighted graph
X=(X^{0},X^{1},r_{X},s_{X},w_{X})
such that
X^{0}\subseteq E^{0}
,
X^{1}\subseteq E^{1}
, and
r_{X},s_{X}
are respectively the restrictions of
s_{E},r_{E}
on
X^{1}
and
w_{X}(e)=w_{E}(e)
for all
e\in X^{1}
. A weighted subgraph
X
is called
complete
if
s^{-1}_{X}(v)=s^{-1}_{E}(v)
for all
v\in X^{0}_{\operatorname{reg}}
.

As a consequence of Theorem
2.4
, we have the following useful note.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field,
(E,w)
a row-finite weighted graph and
(X,w)
a complete weighted subgraph of
(E,w)
. Then the map
L_{\mathsf{k}}(X,w)\longrightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
, defined by
v\longmapsto v
for all
v\in X^{0}
,
e_{i}\longmapsto e_{i}
and
e^{*}_{i}\longmapsto e^{*}_{i}
for all
e\in X^{1}
and
1\leq i\leq w(e)
, is an injective homomorphism of
\mathsf{k}
-algebras.

Proof.

By the Universal Property of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(X,w)
, there exists a
\mathsf{k}
-algebra homomorphism
\phi:L_{\mathsf{k}}(X,w)\longrightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
such that
\phi(v)=v
for all
v\in X^{0}
,
\phi(e_{i})=e_{i}
and
\phi(e^{*}_{i})=e^{*}_{i}
for all
e\in X^{1}
and
1\leq i\leq w(e)
. Since
(X,w)
is a complete weighted subgraph of
(E,w)
, it follows that every normal generalized path in
X
is also a normal generalized path in
E
. From this note and Theorem
2.4
, we immediately obtain that
\phi
is injective, thus finishing the proof. ∎

For any unital ring
R

\mathcal{V}(R)
denotes the set of isomorphism classes (denoted by
[P]
) of finitely generated projective left

\mathcal{V}(R)

[P]+[Q]=[P\oplus Q]

for any isomorphism classes

and

. On the other hand, for any row-finite vertex weighted graph
(E,w)
the monoid

is defined as follows. Denote by
\mathbb{F}_{E}
the free abelian

, and define relations on
\mathbb{F}_{E}
by setting

(1)

for every
v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0}
. Let
\sim_{E}
be the congruence relation on
\mathbb{F}_{E}
generated by these relations. Then

is defined to be the quotient monoid

Theorem 2.6

(
[

[

Let

.

.

Let
(E,w)

.

The following theorem provides a description of the quotient of a weighted Leavitt path algebra by an ideal generated a set of vertices, which is a generalization of

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field,
(E,w)
a row-finite weighted graph, and
H
a hereditary and saturated subset of
E^{0}
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)
There exists an isomorphism of
\mathsf{k}
-algebras

\bar{\phi}:L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)/I(H)\longrightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(E/H,w_{r}),

where
I(H)
is the ideal of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
generated by
H
.

(2)

I(H)\cap E^{0}=H
.

Proof.

(1) We define the elements
\{p_{v}\mid v\in E^{0}\}
and
\{s_{e_{i}},s_{e^{*}_{i}}\mid e\in E^{1},1\leq i\leq w(e)\}
of

,

to any of the newly added vertices which yield

, we have as above that

may be viewed as an element of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
. Then, using the previous observation that
p_{i}^{y}(p_{i}^{y})^{*}=v_{i}^{y}
in

, we conclude that
\varphi(s^{\prime})=p_{i}^{y}(p_{i}^{y})^{*}v_{i}^{y}sv_{j}^{z}p_{j}^{z}(p_{j}^{z})^{*}=v_{i}^{y}sv_{j}^{z}
, and thus
\varphi
is surjective.

We now define
\Phi
to be the
\mathsf{k}
-space isomorphism between the two matrix rings induced by applying each of the
\varphi_{(n_{v_{i}},y),(n_{v_{j}},z)}
componentwise. We need only show that these componentwise isomorphisms respect the corresponding matrix multiplications. But to do so, it suffices to show that the maps behave correctly in each component. That is, we need only show, for each
m_{\ell}
(
1\leq\ell\leq t
) and each
x
(
1\leq x\leq m_{v_{\ell}}
), that

\varphi_{(n_{v_{i}},y),(n_{v_{j}},z)}(v_{i}rv_{\ell})\cdot\varphi_{(m_{v_{\ell}},x),(m_{j},z)}(v_{\ell}r^{\prime}v_{j})=\varphi_{(n_{v_{i}},y),(n_{v_{j}},z)}(v_{i}rv_{\ell}r^{\prime}v_{j}).

But this is immediate, as
(p_{\ell}^{x})^{*}p_{\ell}^{x}=v_{\ell}
for each

and
1\leq x\leq m_{v_{\ell}}

We note that in
[
6
, Theorem 3.15]
Abrams and the second author proved that every nonzero corner of the Leavitt path algebra of a finite graph is isomorphic to a Leavitt path algebra. However, this is, in general, not true for the case of weighted Leavitt path algebras.

Example 3.10
.

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field and
(E,w)
the following weighted graph

\textstyle{v_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{e}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{v_{2},}

where
w(e)=w(f)=2
. We then have that
v_{1}
is a full idempotent in
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
(since
v_{2}=f^{*}_{1}f_{1}+f^{*}_{2}f_{2}
) and
(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{1})^{2}\cong L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{1}\oplus L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{2}
. By Theorem
3.7
, it follows that
\mathbb{M}_{2}(v_{1}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{1})\ \cong\ L_{\mathsf{k}}(E^{+}(1,1),w^{+})=L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
.

Assume that
v_{1}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{1}
is a weighted Leavitt path algebra. Since
v_{1}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{1}
is Morita equivalent to
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
, it follows that
\mathcal{V}(v_{1}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{1})\cong\mathcal{V}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))
. By Theorem
2.6
,
\mathcal{V}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))\cong M_{(E,w)}
, and so

\mathcal{V}(v_{1}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{1})\cong M_{(E,w)}=\langle u,v\rangle/\langle 2v=v+u\rangle.

It is easy to see that
M_{(E,w)}
is an infinite commutative monoid. On the other hand, by
[
17
, Proposition 40]
, it follows that
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
has a local valuation
\upsilon:L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{-\infty\}
such that
0
is the only element satisfying
\upsilon(0)=-\infty
. Let
x
and
y
be two elements in
v_{1}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{1}
. We then have
\upsilon(xy)=\upsilon(x)+\upsilon(y)
(see
[
17
, Definition 3.7]
). Now if
xy
is zero, and
x
or
y
is not zero, then the equality
\upsilon(xy)=\upsilon(x)+\upsilon(y)
would not happen. This shows that
v_{1}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{1}
is a domain. It is obvious that
v_{1}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{1}
is not a commutative ring. Then, by
[
17
, Theorem 41]
, we have that
\mathcal{V}(v_{1}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{1})\cong\langle v\rangle/\langle nv=mv\rangle
which is either a finite cyclic monoid or a free cyclic monoid, a contradiction. Thus
v_{1}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)v_{1}
is not a weighted Leavitt path algebra.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
(E,w)
be a row-finite weighted graph and
v\in E^{0}
a source. We form the
source elimination
weighted graph
(E_{\setminus v},w_{r})
of
(E,w)
as follows:
E_{\setminus v}
denotes the graph obtained from
E
by deleting
v
and all the edges in
E
emitting from
v
, and
w_{r}
is the restriction of the weight function
w
to
E_{\setminus v}
.

The following lemma is to extend
[
5
, Proposition 1.4]
and
[
9
, Lemma 4.3]
to the case of weighted Leavitt path algebras.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field,
(E,w)
a finite weighted graph and
v\in E^{0}
a source and not a sink with
w(v)=1
. Then
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
is Morita equivalent to
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E_{\setminus v},w_{r})
.

Proof.

Since
(E_{\setminus v},w_{r})
is a complete weighted subgraph of
(E,w)
and by Corollary
2.5
, the map
\phi:L_{\mathsf{k}}(E_{\setminus v},w_{r})\longrightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
, defined by
u\longmapsto u
for all
u\in E^{0}\setminus\{v\}
,
e_{i}\longmapsto e_{i}
and
e^{*}_{i}\longmapsto e^{*}_{i}
for all
e\in E^{1}\setminus s^{-1}(v)
and
1\leq i\leq w(e)
, is an injective homomorphism of
\mathsf{k}
-algebras.

Let
\epsilon=\phi(1_{L_{\mathsf{k}}(E_{\setminus v},w_{r})})=\sum_{u\in E^{0},u\neq v}u
. We claim that
\phi(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E_{\setminus v},w_{r}))=\epsilon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E_{\setminus v},w_{r})\epsilon
by following the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 4.3]
. It is obvious that

\phi(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E_{\setminus v},w_{r}))\subseteq\epsilon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)\epsilon
.

Conversely, let
p=x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
be a generalized path in
(E,w)
such that
s(x_{1})\neq v
and
r(x_{n})\neq v
, where
x_{i}\in\{e_{i},e^{*}_{i}\mid e\in E^{1},1\leq i\leq w(e)\}
. Since
v
is a source,
p
cannot pass through
v
, and so
p
is a generalized path in
E_{\setminus v}
. Consequently,
p=\phi(p)\in\phi(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E_{\setminus v},w_{r}))
, showing the claim.

We next show that
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)=L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)\epsilon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
. It suffices to prove that
v
is in
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)\epsilon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
. Since
w(v)=1
, it follows that
w(e)=1
for all
e\in s^{-1}(v)
. Since
r(e)\in L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)\epsilon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
for all
e\in s^{-1}(v)
,
e_{1}=e_{1}r(e)\in L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)\epsilon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
for all
e\in s^{-1}(v)
. We then have

v=\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}e_{1}e^{*}_{1}\in L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)\epsilon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w),

which yields
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)=L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)\epsilon L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
. Therefore,
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
is Morita equivalent to
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E_{\setminus v},w_{r})
, thus finishing the proof.
∎

We are now in a position to give the second main result of this article, describing a unital
\mathsf{k}
-algbera being Morita equivalent to a weighted Leavitt path algebra.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
(E,w)
be a finite vertex weighted graph,
\mathsf{k}
a field, and
A
a unital
\mathsf{k}
-algebra. Then
A
is Morita equivalent to
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
if and only if there exist a positive integer
n
and a hair extension
(E^{+},w^{+})
of
(E,w)
such that

\mathbb{M}_{n}(A)\ \cong\ L_{\mathsf{k}}(E^{+},w^{+}).

Proof.

Assume that
A
is Morita equivalent to
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
. We then have
A\cong\epsilon\mathbb{M}_{m}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))\epsilon
for some
m\geq 1
and a full idempotent
\epsilon\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))
. By Theorem
2.14
,
\mathbb{M}_{m}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))\cong L_{\mathsf{k}}(\mathbb{M}_{m}E,\bar{w})
as
\mathsf{k}
-algebras. Since
(\mathbb{M}_{m}E,\bar{w})
is a finite vertex weighted graph and by Corollary
3.9
, there exist a positive integer
n
and a hair extension
(E^{+},w^{+})
of
(\mathbb{M}_{m}E,\bar{w})
(consequently, a hair extension of
(E,w)
) such that
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\epsilon L_{\mathsf{k}}(\mathbb{M}_{m}E,\bar{w})\epsilon)\ \cong\ L_{\mathsf{k}}(E^{+},w^{+}).
This implies that
\mathbb{M}_{n}(A)\ \cong\ L_{\mathsf{k}}(E^{+},w^{+}).

Conversely, suppose there exist a positive integer
n
and a hair extension
(E^{+},w^{+})
of
(E,w)
such that

\mathbb{M}_{n}(A)\ \cong\ L_{\mathsf{k}}(E^{+},w^{+}).
Since
A
is Morita equivalent to
\mathbb{M}_{n}(A)
, it follows that
A
is Morita equivalent to
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E^{+},w^{+})
. We note that all newly added vertices which yield
E^{+}
are sources and regular vertices of weight
1
. So we may apply the source elimination process by step-by-step at these vertices. At each step, the Morita equivalence of the corresponding weighted Leavitt path algebras is preserved by Lemma
3.12
. Hence
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E^{+},w^{+})
is Morita equivalent to
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
, as desired.
∎

In
[
4
, Theorem 6.1]
Abrams and the first author showed a nice connection between sandpile momoids and weighted Leavitt path algebras that any conical sandpile monoid
\text{SP}(E)
of a directed sandpile graph
E
can be realised as the
\mathcal{V}
-monoid of a weighted Leavitt path
algebra
L_{\mathsf{k}}(F,w)
, where
F
is an explicitly constructed subgraph of
E
. Motivated by this result, they introduced sandpile algebras. As a consequence of Theorem
3.13
, we next describe unital algebras being Morita equivalent to sandpile algebras. To do so, we need to recall some useful notions.

A finite graph
E
is called a
sandpile graph
if
E
has a unique sink (denote it by
s
), and for every
v\in E^{0}
there exists a path
p
with
s(p)=v
and
r(p)=s
. Any sandpile graph
E
may be given the structure of a balanced weighted graph
(E,w)
by assigning
w(v)=|s^{-1}(v)|
for all
v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0}
. A sandpile graph
E
is called
conical
if any vertex not connected to a cycle has weight one. A unital algebra
A
over a field
\mathsf{k}
is called a
sandpile algebra
if there
exists a conical sandpile graph
E
for which

A\cong L_{\mathsf{k}}(E/S_{E},w_{r}),

where
S_{E}
is the set of all vertices in
E
which do not connect to any cycle in
E
, and
w_{r}
is the restriction of the weight function
w
to
E/S_{E}
(see
[
4
, Definition 6.11]
).

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field and
A
a unital
\mathsf{k}
-algebra. Then
A
is Morita equivalent to a sandpile
\mathsf{k}
-algebra if and only if there exist a positive integer
n
, a conical sandpile graph
E
and a hair extension
(G,w_{G})
of the vertex weighted graph
(E/S_{E},w_{r})
such that

\mathbb{M}_{n}(A)\cong L_{\mathsf{k}}(G,w_{G}).

Proof.

The statement immediately follows from Theorem
3.13
.
∎

Lucas-Euler Relations using Balancing and Lucas-Balancing Polynomials


Abstract
We establish some new combinatorial identities involving Euler polynomials and balancing (Lucas-balancing) polynomials. The derivations use elementary techniques and are based on functional equations for the respective generating functions. From these polynomial relations, we deduce interesting identities with Fibonacci and Lucas numbers, and Euler numbers. The results must be regarded as companion results to some Fibonacci-Bernoulli identities, which we derived in our previous paper.

1
Motivation and preliminaries

In 1975, Byrd
[
1
]
derived the following identity relating Lucas numbers to Euler numbers:

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{n/2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{2k}\left(\frac{5}{4}\right)^{k}L_{n-2k}E_{2k}=2^{1-n}.

(1)

In
[
14
]
, Wang and Zhang obtained a more general result valid for
j\geq 1
as follows

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{n/2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{2k}\left(\frac{5}{4}\right)^{k}\!F_{j}^{2k}L_{j(n-2k)}E_{2k}=2^{1-n}L_{j}^{n}.

(2)

Castellanos
[
2
]
found

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2n}{2k}2^{-2k-1}L_{2(n-k)j}L_{j}^{2k}E_{2k}=\Big(\frac{5}{4}\Big)^{n}F_{j}^{2n},

(3)

which expresses even powers of Fibonacci numbers in terms of Lucas and Euler numbers.

Here, as usual, Fibonacci and Lucas numbers satisfy the recurrence
u_{n}=u_{n-1}+u_{n-2}
,
n\geq 2
,
with initial conditions
F_{0}=0
,
F_{1}=1
and
L_{0}=2
,
L_{1}=1
, respectively,
whereas Euler numbers
(E_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are given by the power series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{\cosh{z}}.

Fibonacci and Lucas numbers are entries
A000045
and
A000032
in the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences
[
13
]
, respectively.

The Lucas-Euler pair may be regarded as the twin of the Fibonacci-Bernoulli pair.
In the last years, there has been a growing interest in deriving new relations for these two pairs of sequences.
Zhang and Ma
[
17
]
proved a relation between Fibonacci polynomials and Bernoulli numbers
(B_{n})_{n\geq 0}
defined by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{z}{e^{z}-1}.

The following identity is a special case of their result:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}5^{\frac{n-k}{2}}F_{k}B_{n-k}=n\beta^{n-1},

where
\beta=(1-\sqrt{5})/2
, or, equivalently,

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{2k}5^{k}F_{n-2k}B_{2k}=\frac{nL_{n-1}}{2}.

(4)

See also
[
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
for other results in this direction.
Recently, Frontczak
[
5
]
, Frontczak and Goy
[
7
]
, and Frontczak and Tomovski
[
8
]
proved
some generalizations of existing results. For instance, from
[
7
]
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}(\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}F_{jk}B_{n-k}=nF_{j}\beta^{j(n-1)},

(5)

which holds for all
j\geq 1
and generalizes (
4
) to an arithmetic progression, and

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{n\choose 2k}(20^{k}-5^{k})F_{2j}^{2k}L_{2j(n-2k)}B_{2k}=\frac{5n}{2}F_{2j}F_{2j(n-1)}.

(6)

Note, since
B_{2n+1}=0
for
n\geq 1
, from (
5
) we get Kelinsky’s formula
[
9
]

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor}{n\choose 2k}5^{k}F_{j}^{2k}F_{j(n-2k)}B_{2k}=\frac{n}{2}F_{j}L_{j(n-1)}.

In this paper, we present new identities linking Lucas numbers to Euler numbers (polynomials).
The results stated are polynomial generalizations of (
1
) and are complements of the recent discoveries
from
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
.

Throughout the paper, we will work with different kind of polynomials of a complex variable
x
:
Euler polynomials
(E_{n}(x))_{n\geq 0}
, Bernoulli polynomials
(B_{n}(x))_{n\geq 0}
, balancing polynomials
(B_{n}^{*}(x))_{n\geq 0}
,
and Lucas-balancing polynomials
(C_{n}(x))_{n\geq 0}
.

Euler and Bernoulli polynomials are famous mathematical objects and are fairly well understood.
They are defined by
[
3
, Chapter 24]

H(x,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{ze^{xz}}{e^{z}-1}\qquad(|z|<2\pi)

(7)

and

I(x,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{2e^{xz}}{e^{z}+1}\qquad(|z|<\pi).

(8)

The numbers
B_{n}(0)=B_{n}
are the famous Bernoulli numbers. Bernoulli numbers are rational numbers starting
with
B_{0}=1,B_{1}=-1/2,B_{2}=1/6,B_{4}=-1/30
and so on. Also, as already mentioned,
B_{2n+1}=0
for
n\geq 1
.
Euler numbers
E_{n}
are obtained from
I(1/2,2z)
that is

E_{n}=2^{n}E_{n}(1/2).

(9)

In contrast to Bernoulli numbers, Euler numbers are integers where
E_{0}=1,E_{2}=-1,E_{4}=5
and
E_{2n+1}=0
for
n\geq 0
.
Explicit formulas for the polynomials are

B_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}B_{k}x^{n-k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad E_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{E_{k}}{2^{k}}\Big(x-\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{n-k}.

Euler polynomials can be expressed in terms of Bernoulli polynomials via

E_{n}(x)=\frac{2}{n+1}\Big(B_{n+1}(x)-2^{n+1}B_{n+1}\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)\Big).

Particularly,

E_{n}(0)=\frac{2\,(1-2^{n+1})}{n+1}B_{n+1}.

(10)

Balancing polynomials are of younger age and are introduced in the next section.

2
Balancing and Lucas-Balancing Polynomials

Balancing polynomials
B_{n}^{*}(x)
and Lucas-balancing polynomials
C_{n}(x)
are generalizations of
balancing and Lucas-balancing numbers
[
4
]
. These polynomials satisfy the recurrence

w_{n}(x)=6xw_{n-1}(x)-w_{n-2}(x)
,
\geq 2
, but with the respectively initial conditions

B_{0}^{*}(x)=0
,
B_{1}^{*}(x)=1
and
C_{0}(x)=1
,
C_{1}(x)=3x
. The explicit formulas for these polynomials are

B^{*}_{n}(x)=\frac{\lambda^{n}(x)-\lambda^{-n}(x)}{2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad C_{n}(x)=\frac{\lambda^{n}(x)+\lambda^{-n}(x)}{2},

where
\lambda(x)=3x+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}
. Consult the papers
[
4
,
6
,
10
,
11
]
for more information about these polynomials. The numbers
B_{n}^{*}(1)=B_{n}^{*}
and
C_{n}(1)=C_{n}
are called balancing and Lucas-balancing numbers, respectively. These numbers are indexed in
[
13
]
under entries
A001109
and
A001541
.

Balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials possess interesting properties. They are related to Chebyshev polynomials by simple scaling
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
. The exponential generating functions for balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials are derived in
[
4
,
6
]
. Here, however, we will only need the results from
[
6
]
: Let
b_{1}(x,z)
and
b_{2}(x,z)
be the exponential generating functions
of odd and even indexed balancing polynomials, respectively. Then

\displaystyle b_{1}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{2n+1}^{*}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}}{\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\bigl(3x\sinh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\cosh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)\bigr)

(11)

and

b_{2}(x,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{2n}^{*}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}}{\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\sinh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z).

(12)

Similarly, the exponential generating functions for Lucas-balancing polynomials are found to be

\displaystyle c_{1}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{2n+1}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}\bigl(3x\cosh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\sinh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)\bigr)

(13)

and

c_{2}(x,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{2n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}\cosh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z).

(14)

Connections between Bernoulli polynomials
B_{n}(x)
and balancing polynomials
B_{n}^{*}(x)

have been established in the recent papers
[
5
,
7
]
. They are interesting,
as they instantly give relations between Bernoulli numbers and Fibonacci and Lucas numbers.
The links are the following evaluations
[
4
]

B_{n}^{*}\Big(\omega_{s}\frac{L_{s}}{6}\Big)=\omega_{s}^{n-1}\frac{F_{sn}}{F_{s}}\,,\qquad C_{n}\Big(\omega_{s}\frac{L_{s}}{6}\Big)=\omega_{s}^{n}\frac{L_{sn}}{2},

(15)

where
\omega_{s}=1
, if
s
is even, and
\omega_{s}=i=\sqrt{-1}
, if
s
is odd.
These links will be used to prove our results.

3
Relations between Euler and Balancing

(Lucas-Balancing) Polynomials

We start with the following result involving even indexed balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials.

Theorem 1
.

For each
n\geq 1
and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor}{n-1\choose 2k-1}C_{2(n-2k)}(x)\big(144x^{2}(9x^{2}-1)\big)^{k}E_{2k-1}(0)=12x(1-9x^{2})B_{2n-2}^{*}(x).

(16)

Proof.

Since
\tanh{z}=1-\frac{2}{e^{2z}+1}
, from (
8
) we get

I(0,12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)=1-\tanh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)

and, by (
12
) and (
14
),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k}

\displaystyle C_{2k}\bigl(12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})^{n-k}E_{n-k}(0)+C_{2n}(x)\Big)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=c_{2}(x,z)I(0,12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)

\displaystyle=e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}\bigl(\cosh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)-\sinh(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)\bigr)

\displaystyle=c_{2}(x,z)-\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}b_{2}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigl(C_{2n}(x)-\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}B_{2n}^{*}(x)\bigr)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}C_{2(n-k)}\bigl(12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})^{k}E_{k}(0)=C_{2n}(x)-\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}B_{2n}^{*}(x).

Since
E_{2n-1}=0
for
n\geq 1
, after some algebra we have (
16
).
∎

Corollary 2
.

For each
n\geq 1
and
j\geq 1
,

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor}{n-1\choose 2k-1}5^{k-1}F_{2j}^{2k-1}L_{2j(n-2k)}E_{2k-1}(0)=-F_{2j(n-1)}.

(17)

Proof.

Evaluate (
16
) at the
x=\omega_{j}L_{j}/6
and use the links from (
15
).
To simplify recall that
L_{n}^{2}-5F_{n}^{2}=(-1)^{n}4
and
F_{2n}=F_{n}L_{n}
.
∎

Using (
10
), we can write (
17
) as

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor}{n-1\choose 2k-1}\frac{20^{k}-5^{k}}{k}F_{2j}^{2k-1}L_{2j(n-2k)}B_{2k}=5F_{2j(n-1)},

which is easily reduced to (
6
).

We also have the following interesting identity.

Theorem 3
.

For each
n\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, we have the relation

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor}{n\choose 2k}C_{2(n-2k)}(x)\big(36x^{2}({9x^{2}-1})\big)^{k}E_{2k}=\bigl(18x^{2}-1\bigr)^{n}.

(18)

Proof.

The result is a consequence of the fact that

c_{2}(x,z)I(1/2,12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)=e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}.

∎

Corollary 4
.

For each
n\geq 0
and
j\geq 1
,

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{n\choose 2k}\Bigl(\frac{5}{4}\Bigr)^{k}F^{2k}_{2j}L_{2j(n-2k)}E_{2k}=2^{1-n}L_{2j}^{n}.

(19)

Proof.

Evaluate (
18
) at the point
x=\omega_{j}L_{j}/6
and use the links from (
15
).
When simplifying you will also need the formula
L_{n}^{2}-L_{2n}=(-1)^{n}2
.
∎

Interestingly, if
j=1/2
from (
19
) we obtain Byrd’s result (
1
).
Also, when
j=1
and
j=2
, from (
19
) we obtain the following Lucas-Euler relations:

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{n\choose 2k}\Big(\frac{5}{4}\Big)^{k}L_{2(n-2k)}E_{2k}=2\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)^{n}

and

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{n\choose 2k}\Big(\frac{45}{4}\Big)^{k}L_{4(n-2k)}E_{2k}=2\Big(\frac{7}{2}\Big)^{n},

(20)

respectively. The first example appears as equation (31) in
[
5
]
.

A different expression for the sum on the left of (
18
) is stated next.

Theorem 5
.

For each
n\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{n\choose 2k}C_{2(n-2k)}(x)\big(36x^{2}(9x^{2}-1)\big)^{k}E_{2k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\big(C_{2k}(x)-

\displaystyle\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}B_{2k}^{*}(x)\big)(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})^{n-k}.

(21)

Proof.

We use the identity
I(1/2,2z)=e^{z}(1-\tanh z)
, from which the functional equation follows

c_{2}(x,z)I(1/2,12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)=e^{(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})z}\big(c_{2}(x,z)-\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}b_{2}(x,z)\big).

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}C_{2k}(x)(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})^{n-k}E_{n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}

\displaystyle\big(C_{2k}(x)-\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}B_{2k}^{*}(x)\big)(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})^{n-k},

that is equivalent to (
5
).
∎

Theorem 6
.

For each
n\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, it is true that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}C_{2(n-k)}(x)(12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})^{k}E_{k}(x)=\big(18x^{2}-1+6x(2x-1)\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\big)^{n}.

(22)

Proof.

The functional relation
{\cosh(z/2)}I(x,z)=e^{(x-1/2)z}
produces immediately

c_{2}(x,z)I(x,12x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z)=e^{(18x^{2}-1+6x(2x-1)\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})z}.

Comparing the coefficients of
z
in the power series expansions on both sides gives the identity.
∎

When
x=1/2
, then we recover (
20
), by (
9
).

4
Other Special Polynomial Identities

The following result appears as Theorem 13 in
[
7
]
:
For each
n\geq 0
,
j\geq 1
, and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}F_{jk}(\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}B_{n-k}(x)=nF_{j}\big((\sqrt{5}x+\beta)F_{j}+F_{j-1}\big)^{n-1}

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}F_{jk}(-\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}B_{n-k}(x)=nF_{j}\big((\alpha-\sqrt{5}x)F_{j}+F_{j-1}\big)^{n-1},

where
\alpha=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
is the golden ratio and
\beta=(1-\sqrt{5})/2=-1/\alpha
.

Now, we present the analogue result for the Lucas-Euler pair:

Theorem 7
.

The following polynomial identity is valid for all
n\geq 0
,
j\geq 1
, and
x\in\mathbb{C}
:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}L_{jk}(\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}E_{n-k}(x)=2\big((\sqrt{5}x+\beta)F_{j}+F_{j-1}\big)^{n}

(23)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}L_{jk}(-\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}E_{n-k}(x)=2\big((\alpha-\sqrt{5}x)F_{j}+F_{j-1}\big)^{n}.

(24)

Proof.

Let
L(z)
be the exponential generating function for
(L_{jn})_{n\geq 0},j\geq 1
. Then, using the Binet formula for
L_{n}
we get

L(z)=2e^{(1/2F_{j}+F_{j-1})z}\cosh\Big(\frac{\sqrt{5}F_{j}}{2}z\Big).

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}L_{jk}(\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}E_{n-k}(x)\Big)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=L(z)I(x,\sqrt{5}F_{j}z)

\displaystyle=2\,e^{((x-1/2)\sqrt{5}F_{j}+1/2F_{j}+F_{j-1})z}

\displaystyle=2\,e^{((\sqrt{5}x+\beta)F_{j}+F_{j-1})z}.

This proves the first equation. The second follows upon replacing
x
by
1-x

and using
E_{n}(1-x)=(-1)^{n}E_{n}(x)
and
\alpha-\beta=\sqrt{5}
.
∎

Note that the relations (
23
) and (
24
) provide a generalization of (
19
).
To see this, notice that they can be written more compactly as

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}L_{jk}(\pm\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}E_{n-k}(x)=2^{1-n}\big(L_{j}\pm\sqrt{5}F_{j}(2x-1)\big)^{n}.

(25)

Now, if
x=1/2
, we get

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\big(\pm\sqrt{5}F_{j}\big)^{n-k}2^{k}L_{jk}E_{n-k}=2L_{j}^{n},

which is equivalent to (
19
). We also mention the nice and curious identities

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\big(\pm\sqrt{5}F_{j}\big)^{n-k}L_{jk}E_{n-k}(\alpha)=2(\pm 1)^{n}L_{j\pm 1}^{n},

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\big(\pm\sqrt{5}F_{j}\big)^{n-k}L_{jk}E_{n-k}(\beta)=2(\mp 1)^{n}L_{j\mp 1}^{n},

which can be deduced from (
25
) and
5F_{n}=L_{n+1}+L_{n-1}
.

We conclude this presentation with the following interesting corollary.

Corollary 8
.

Let
n
,
j
and
q
be integers with
n
,
j\geq 1
and
q
odd. Then it holds that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}(\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}\big(q^{-(n-k)}-1\big)L_{jk}E_{n-k}(0)=2q^{-n}\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}(-1)^{r}\big(r\alpha^{j}+(q-r)\beta^{j}\big)^{n}.

Proof.

The known multiplication formula for Euler polynomials for odd
q

[
3
, Chapter 24]

q^{n}\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}(-1)^{r}E_{n}\Big(x+\frac{r}{q}\Big)=E_{n}(qx)

yields

\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}(-1)^{r}E_{n}\Big(\frac{r}{q}\Big)=\big(q^{-n}-1\big)E_{n}(0).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}L_{jk}(\sqrt{5}F_{j})^{n-k}\big(q^{-(n-k)}-1\big)E_{n-k}(0)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}(-1)^{r}\Big(\Big(\sqrt{5}\,\frac{r}{q}+\beta\Big)F_{j}+F_{j-1}\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=2q^{-n}\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}(-1)^{r}\big(\sqrt{5}rF_{j}+q(\beta F_{j}+F_{j-1})\big)^{n}

\displaystyle=2q^{-n}\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}(-1)^{r}\big(r\alpha^{j}+(q-r)\beta^{j}\big)^{n}.

∎

The special instances for
j=1
, and
q=3
and
q=5
, respectively, take the form

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}L_{k}(\sqrt{5})^{n-k}\big(3^{-(n-k)}-1\big)E_{n-k}(0)=2\cdot 3^{-n}\sqrt{5}F_{2n}

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}L_{k}(\sqrt{5})^{n-k}\big(5^{-(n-k)}-1\big)E_{n-k}(0)=\left.\begin{cases}2\cdot 5^{(1-n)/2}(F_{2n}-F_{n}),&\mbox{if $n$ is even};\\
2\cdot 5^{-n/2}(L_{2n}-L_{n}),&\mbox{if $n$ is odd}.\end{cases}\right.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have documented identities relating Euler numbers (polynomials) to
balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials. We have also derived a general identity involving
Euler polynomials and Lucas numbers in arithmetic progression.
All results must be seen as companion results to the
Fibonacci-Bernoulli pair from
[
7
]
. In the future, we will work
on more identities connecting Bernoulli/Euler numbers (polynomials) with Fibonacci/Lucas numbers (polynomials).

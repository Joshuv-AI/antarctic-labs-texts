Logarithmic cotangent bundles, Chern-Mather classes, and the Huh-Sturmfels Involution conjecture
Laurentiu G. Maxim, Jose Israel Rodriguez, Botong Wang, Lei Wu

Abstract
. Using compactifications in the logarithmic cotangent bundle, we obtain a formula for the Chern classes of the pushforward of Lagrangian cycles under an open embedding with normal crossing complement. This generalizes earlier results of Aluffi and Wu-Zhou. The first application of our formula is a geometric description of Chern-Mather classes of an arbitrary very affine variety, generalizing earlier results of Huh which held under the smooth and schön assumptions. As the second application, we confirm an involution formula relating sectional maximum likelihood (ML) degrees and ML bidegrees, which was conjectured by Huh and Sturmfels in 2013.

1.
Introduction

Maximum likelihood estimation in statistics leads to the problem of finding the critical points of a likelihood function on an algebraic variety. The number of critical points of a general likelihood function is called the
maximum likelihood (ML) degree
of the algebraic variety. It is well-known that the ML degree is closely related to the topology of the variety. More precisely, if the variety is smooth, its ML degree can be computed as the Euler characteristic of an open subset of the variety, and if the variety is singular, the ML degree can be computed as the Euler characteristic of a certain constructible function which measures the complexity of singularities (see
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
and Corollary
1.7
).

Some natural generalizations of the ML degree are the ML bidegrees and the sectional ML degrees. In
[
12
]
, Huh and Sturmfels conjectured that the ML bidegrees and the sectional ML degrees of a variety determine each other under some involution formulas, and proved the case when the variety is smooth and Schön. The main goal of this paper is to confirm their Involution Conjecture in full generality.

If a variety is smooth and Schön, Huh proved that its Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson (CSM) classes determine the ML bidegrees (
[
11
, Theorem 2]
, see also
[
12
, page 101]
). So the conjecture follows in this case from Aluffi’s involution formula which relates the Euler characteristics of general linear sections of a projective variety to its CSM class (
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
). To generalize Huh’s result to arbitrary varieties, we need to establish a similar relation between CSM classes and the ML bidegrees, which we achieve in Theorem
1.6
. In fact, we show that the correct substitute for the CSM classes of a smooth very affine variety are the Chern-Mather classes, i.e., the CSM class associated to the local Euler obstruction function. The key step in the proof of Theorem
1.6
is a new geometric formula computing the CSM classes of any constructible function on a smooth quasi-projective variety (see Theorem
1.1
). Our geometric formula involves logarithmic conic Lagrangian cycles, and reduces to earlier works of Ginsburg (
[
9
, Theorem 3.2]
).

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect relevant background material on constructible functions, constructible sheaves and their characteristic cycles, we give a brief overview of the microlocal interpretation of MacPherson’s Chern classes, and recall Aluffi’s involution formula relating CSM classes to sectional Euler characteristics.

2.1.
Constructible sheaves. Constructible functions. Characteristic cycles

Denote by
D^{b}_{c}(X)
the bounded derived category of
\mathbb{C}
-constructible complexes (with respect to some stratification) on the smooth complex algebraic variety
X
. By associating characteristic cycles to constructible complexes on
X
(e.g., see
[
6
, Definition 4.3.19]
or
[
13
, Chapter IX]
), one gets a functor

CC:K_{0}(D^{b}_{c}(X))\longrightarrow L(X)

on the Grothendieck group of
\mathbb{C}
-constructible complexes, where
L(X)
is the free abelian group spanned by the irreducible conic Lagrangian cycles in the cotangent bundle
T^{*}X
. Recall that any element of
L(X)
is of the form
\sum_{k}n_{k}\cdot T^{*}_{Z_{k}}X
, for some
n_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
Z_{k}
closed irreducible subvarieties of
X
. Here, if
Z
is a closed irreducible subvariety of
X
with smooth locus
Z_{\reg}
, its conormal bundle
T^{*}_{Z}X
is defined as the closure in
T^{*}X
of

T^{*}_{Z_{\reg}}X:=\{(z,\xi)\in T^{*}X\mid z\in Z_{\reg},\ \xi\in T^{*}_{z}X,\ \xi|_{T_{z}Z_{\rm reg}}=0\}.

One can then define a group isomorphism

T:L(X)\longrightarrow Z(X)

to the group
Z(X)
of algebraic cycles on
X
by:

\sum_{k}n_{k}\cdot T^{*}_{Z_{k}}X\longmapsto\sum_{k}(-1)^{\dim Z_{k}}n_{k}Z_{k}.

Let
F(X)
be the group of algebraically constructible functions on
X
, i.e., the free abelian group generated by indicator functions
1_{Z}
of closed irreducible subvarieties
Z
of
X
. There is a unique linear map

\chi:F(X)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}

called the
Euler characteristic
, defined on generators by
\chi(1_{Z}):=\chi(Z).

An important example of a constructible function on
X
is the MacPherson
local Euler obstruction
function
Eu_{Z}
of an irreducible subvariety
Z
of
X
, see
[
14
]
. The local Euler obstruction function is a measure of the singularities of
Z
, and it takes the value
1
on the smooth locus
Z_{\rm reg}
.

The relation between constructible complexes and constructible functions is made explicit by the following construction.
To any constructible complex
{\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot}\in D^{b}_{c}(X)
, one associates a constructible function
\chi_{st}({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot})\in F(X)
by taking stalkwise Euler characteristics, i.e.,

\chi_{st}({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot})(x):=\chi({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot}_{x})

for any
x\in X
. For example,
\chi_{st}(i_{!}\mathbb{C}_{Z})=1_{Z}
, for
Z
a closed irreducible subvariety of
X
. Note that if
\varphi=\chi_{st}({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot})
, then
\chi(\varphi)=\chi(X,{\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot})
.

Since the Euler characteristic is additive with respect to distinguished triangles, one gets an induced group homomorphism (in fact, an epimorphism)

\chi_{st}:K_{0}(D^{b}_{c}(X))\longrightarrow F(X).

Moreover, since the class map
D^{b}_{c}(X)\to K_{0}(D^{b}_{c}(X))
is onto,
\chi_{st}
is already an epimorphism on
D^{b}_{c}(X)
.

If
Z
is a closed subvariety of
X
, we may regard the function
Eu_{Z}
as being defined on all of

by setting
Eu_{Z}(x)=0
for
x\in X\setminus Z
. In particular, one may consider the group homomorphism

(1)

Eu:Z(X)\longrightarrow F(X)

defined on an irreducible cycle

by the assignment
Z\mapsto Eu_{Z}
, and then extended by
\mathbb{Z}
-linearity. A well-known result (e.g., see
[
6

and the references therein) states that the homomorphism

Eu:Z(X)\to F(X)

is an isomorphism.

The Euler obstruction function appears in the formulation of the

, which in the notations of this section asserts the existence of the following commutative diagram (e.g., see

and the references therein):

(2)

\scriptstyle{CC}
\scriptstyle{\chi_{st}}
\textstyle{F(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

In particular, one can associate a characteristic cycle to any constructible function
\varphi\in F(X)
by the formula

CC(\varphi):=T^{-1}\circ Eu^{-1}(\varphi).

For example, if
Z
is a closed irreducible subvariety of

, one has:

(3)

CC(Eu_{Z})=(-1)^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Z}\cdot T^{*}_{Z}X.

Note also that

CC({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot})=CC(\chi_{st}({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot}))

It is well known (e.g., see
[

) that all the usual functors in sheaf theory, which respect the corresponding category of constructible complexes of sheaves, induce by the epimorphism

well-defined group homomorphisms on the level of constructible functions. Moreover, if

which also implies for the induced group homomorphisms of complex algebraically constructible functions the equality

Here we note that

1_{Z}\longmapsto\left(y\longmapsto\chi(f^{-1}(y)\cap Z),\ \ y\in Y\right).

Finally, by (

T^{*}X
(with support in a certain subvariety, if needed).

2.2.
CSM classes. Microlocal interpretation

We work in the complex algebraic context, with
A_{*}
the Chow group and
H_{*}
the Borel-Moore homology.

In
[
14
]
, MacPherson extended the definition of Chern classes to singular complex algebraic varieties. More precisely, he defined a natural transformation

c_{*}:F(-)\longrightarrow A_{*}(-)

from the functor of constructible functions (with proper morphisms) to Chow (or Borel-Moore) homology, such that if
X
is a smooth variety then
c_{*}(1_{X})=c(TX)\cap[X]
. Here,
c(TX)
denotes the total (cohomology) Chern class of the tangent bundle
TX
, and
[X]
is the fundamental class.
For any locally closed irreducible subvariety

Z
of a complex algebraic variety
X
, the class

c_{SM}(Z):=c_{*}(1_{Z})\in A_{*}(X)

is usually referred to as the
Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson (CSM) class
of
Z
in
X
. Similarly,
the class

c_{Ma}(Z):=c_{*}(Eu_{Z})\in A_{*}(X)

is called the
Chern-Mather class
of
Z
, where we regard
Eu_{Z}
as a constructible function on
X
by setting the value zero on
X\setminus Z
.

Results of Ginsburg
[
9
]
and Sabbah
[
17
]
showed that McPherson’s Chern class transformation
c_{*}
factors through the group of conic Lagrangian cycles in the cotangent bundle. This construction was revisited more recently in
[
2
]
, as well as in
[
5
]
, also in the equivariant context.

In this section, we recall the construction of MacPherson’s Chern class transformation in terms of characteristic cycles, following the approach of
[
2
,
5
]
.

Let
X
be a smooth complex algebraic variety, and let
E
be a rank
r
vector bundle on
X
. Let
\overline{E}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}(E\oplus\mathbf{1})
be the projective bundle, which is a fiber-wise compactification of
E
(here
\mathbf{1}
denotes the trivial line bundle on
X
). Then
E
may be identified with the open complement of
\mathbb{P}(E)
in
\overline{E}
. Let
\pi:E\to X
and
\bar{\pi}:\overline{E}\to X
be
the projections, and let
\xi:=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\overline{E}}(1))
. Pullback via
{\bar{\pi}}
realizes
A_{*}(\overline{E})
as a
A_{*}(X)
-module. Moreover, as shown in
[
8
, Theorem 3.3]
, each
\alpha\in A_{i}(\overline{E})
can be uniquely written as:

(5)

\alpha=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\xi^{j}\cap{\bar{\pi}}^{*}\alpha_{j},

for
\alpha_{j}\in A_{i-r+j}(X)
.

An irreducible conic
r
-dimensional subvariety (
r={\rm rank}\ E
)
\Lambda\subset E

determines an
r
-dimensional cycle
\overline{\Lambda}
in
\overline{E}
and, by formula (
5
), knowledge of
[\overline{\Lambda}]\in A_{r}(\overline{E})
is equivalent to knowledge of a collection of
r+1
classes on
X
. We denote these classes by

c_{0}^{E}(\Lambda),\ldots,c_{r}^{E}(\Lambda)

with
c_{j}^{E}(\Lambda)\in A_{j}(X)
, and call them the
Chern classes of
\Lambda
. The terminology is justified by the following result (see
[
2
, Lemma 4.3]
,
[
5
, Proposition 3.3]
and the references therein), applied to the cotangent bundle
T^{*}X
and elements of
L(X)
:

Proposition 2.1
.

For any constructible function
\varphi\in F(X)
, the Chern classes of the characteristic cycle
CC(\varphi)
equal the signed MacPherson Chern classes of
\varphi
, namely:

(6)

c_{j}^{T^{*}X}\left(CC(\varphi)\right)=(-1)^{j}\cdot c_{j}(\varphi)\in A_{j}(X),\ \ j=0,\ldots,\dim(X),

where
c_{j}(\varphi)
denotes the
j
-th component of MacPherson’s Chern class
c_{*}(\varphi)
.

Remark 2.2
.

The signs appearing in (
6
) may of course be already absorbed in the definition of the
c_{j}(\Lambda)
. This is in fact the way Ginzburg describes these classes in the Appendix of
[
9
]
.

For future reference, let us introduce the following notation for the
signed MacPherson Chern classes
appearing in Proposition
2.1
. For
\varphi\in F(X)
, set:

(7)

\check{c}_{*}(\varphi)=\sum_{j\geq 0}\check{c}_{j}(\varphi):=\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}{c}_{j}(\varphi).

In particular, if
\varphi=1_{Z}
or
\varphi=Eu_{Z}
, we get a corresponding
signed CSM class

\check{c}_{SM}(Z)
and, respectively, a
signed Chern-Mather class

\check{c}_{Ma}(Z)
of a
locally closed irreducible subvariety
Z\subset X
.

There is an alternative way of recovering the Chern classes of lagrangian cycles, which makes use of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant Chow groups.

Consider the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the vector bundle
E
by fiberwise dilation, and the trivial action on
\mathbf{1}
. This induces a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\overline{E}
, such that the inclusion
E\subset\overline{E}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant (and the trivial action on
\mathbb{P}(E)
). The natural projection
\pi:E\to X
is equivariant, where
\mathbb{C}^{*}
acts trivially on
X
.
Let
A_{*}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(E)
and
A_{*}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(X)
be the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant Chow groups of
E
and
X
, respectively. Since
\mathbb{C}^{*}
acts trivially on
X
, we have an isomorphism

A_{*}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(X)\cong A_{*}(X)[t],

where
t:=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{\infty}}(-1))
. Moreover, the inclusion
i:X\hookrightarrow E
of the zero section induces isomorphisms (e.g., see
[
5
, Lemma 2.5]
):

i^{*}=(\pi^{*})^{-1}:A_{*}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(E)\longrightarrow A_{*-r}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(X),

with
r={\rm rank}\ E
.

An
r
-dimensional
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant cycle
\Lambda
in
E
determines as above an
r
-dimensional cycle
\overline{\Lambda}
in
\overline{E}
. By comparing the class
[\Lambda]
of
\Lambda
in the equivariant Chow group
A_{*}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(E)\overset{i^{*}}{\cong}A_{*-r}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(X)
with the class
[\overline{\Lambda}]
in the ordinary Chow group
A_{*}(\overline{E})
, one gets the following identification of
[
5
, Proposition 2.7]
:

(8)

i^{*}([\Lambda])|_{t\mapsto 1}=c_{0}^{E}(\Lambda)+c_{1}^{E}(\Lambda)\ldots+c_{r}^{E}(\Lambda).

Formula (
8
) yields immediately the following.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\phi:E_{1}\to E_{2}
be a bundle map of rank
r
vector bundles over
X
. If
\Lambda
is an
r
-dimensional
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant cycle in
E_{2}
such that the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant subset
\phi^{-1}(\Lambda)
in

is also pure

-dimensional, then

c^{E_{1}}_{j}(\phi^{*}\Lambda)=c^{E_{2}}_{j}(\Lambda),

for each
j=0,\ldots,r
.

2.3.
Aluffi’s inversion formula

Let
X
be a locally closed set in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, so that the function
1_{X}
is constructible on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Then

c_{SM}(X):=c_{*}(1_{X})=\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{j}[\mathbb{P}^{j}]\in A_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n}).

Let

\gamma_{X}(\mathsf{t}):=\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{j}\mathsf{t}^{j}

be the polynomial of degree
\leq n
obtained from
c_{SM}(X)
by replacing
\mathbb{P}^{j}
by
\mathsf{t}^{j}
. Let
X_{j}=X\cap L_{n-j}
, where
L_{n-j}
is a generic linear subspace of codimension
j
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Consider the generating polynomial of degree
\leq n
of the Euler characteristics of these sections, defined as

\chi_{X}(\mathsf{t}):=\sum_{j\geq 0}\chi(X_{j})\cdot(-\mathsf{t})^{j}.

In
[
4
]
, Aluffi showed that for any locally closed set
X
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, the polynomials
\gamma_{X}(\mathsf{t})
and
\chi_{X}(\mathsf{t})
carry precisely the same information.
In order to formulate the result from loc. cit., consider the following linear transformation:

p(\mathsf{t})\longmapsto\mathcal{I}(p):=\frac{\mathsf{t}\cdot p(-\mathsf{t}-1)+p(0)}{\mathsf{t}+1},

and note that if
p(\mathsf{t})
is a polynomial, then
\mathcal{I}(p)
is a polynomial of the same degree. Furthermore,
\mathcal{I}
is an
involution
, whose effect is to perform a sign-reversing symmetry about
\mathsf{t}=-1/2
of the non-constant part of
p
. The main result of
[
4
]
is the following:

Theorem 2.4
.

For every locally closed set
X
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, the involution
\mathcal{I}
interchanges
\gamma_{X}(\mathsf{t})
and
\chi_{X}(\mathsf{t})
, i.e.,

\gamma_{X}=\mathcal{I}(\chi_{X}),\ \ \chi_{X}=\mathcal{I}(\gamma_{X}).

More generally, consider a constructible function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Assuming that

c_{*}(\varphi)=\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{j}[\mathbb{P}^{j}]\in A_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n}),

we define

\gamma_{\varphi}(\mathsf{t})\coloneqq\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{j}\mathsf{t}^{j}.

Let
\varphi_{j}
be the restriction of
\varphi
to a generic codimension
j
linear subspace
L_{n-j}
, and we consider
\varphi_{j}
as a constructible function on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with support contained in
L_{n-j}
. Then we define

\chi_{\varphi}(\mathsf{t})\coloneqq\sum_{j\geq 0}\chi(\varphi_{j})\cdot(-\mathsf{t})^{j}.

Since the constructible functions of the form
1_{X}
for irreducible closed subvarieties
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
form a basis of
F(\mathbb{P}^{n})
, i.e., the abelian group of all constructible functions on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, Theorem
2.4
can be reformulated as the following corollary.

Corollary 2.5
.

For any constructible function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, the involution
\mathcal{I}
interchanges
\gamma_{\varphi}(\mathsf{t})
and
\chi_{\varphi}(\mathsf{t})
, i.e.,

\gamma_{\varphi}=\mathcal{I}(\chi_{\varphi}),\ \ \chi_{\varphi}=\mathcal{I}(\gamma_{\varphi}).

3.
Characteristic cycles and the universal graph embedding

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
.

In
[
9
, Section 3]
, Ginsburg gave explicit pushforward formula for conic Lagrangian cycles on an open embedding of the complement of a hypersurface. The formula uses explicitly the defining equation of the hypersurface. First, we modify Ginsburg’s construction to obtain a global formula, which does not depend on the choice of local defining equations.

Let
X
be a smooth complex algebraic variety and let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{r}D_{i}
be a sum of effective divisors. Let
U=X\setminus D
.
For each
i
, denote the total space of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
by
\mathbf{L}_{i}
, and denote the bundle map by
\pi_{i}:\mathbf{L}_{i}\to X
.
Since
D_{i}
is effective, there is a tautological section of
\mathbf{L}_{i}
defining the divisor
D_{i}
, which we denote by
\mathbf{s}_{i}:X\to\mathbf{L}_{i}
.

Let
\mathbf{E}
be the total space of the vector bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{r})
. Then,

\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{L}_{1}\times_{X}\mathbf{L}_{2}\times_{X}\cdots\times_{X}\mathbf{L}_{r},

and we denote the bundle map by
\pi:\mathbf{E}\to X
. Putting the sections
\mathbf{s}_{i}
together gives a section of
\mathbf{E}
:

\mathbf{s}\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathbf{E},\quad x\mapsto(\mathbf{s}_{1}(x),\mathbf{s}_{2}(x),\dots,\mathbf{s}_{r}(x)).

Pulling back the zero section
X\hookrightarrow\mathbf{L}_{i}
through the (vector bundle) morphism
\mathbf{E}\to\mathbf{L}_{i}
gives a smooth divisor on
\mathbf{E}
, which we denote by
\mathbf{F}_{i}
. Let
\mathbf{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbf{F}_{i}
. The following lemma is tautological.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the above notations,
\mathbf{F}
is a simple normal crossing divisor on
\mathbf{E}
. Moreover,
\mathbf{s}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{F}_{i}=D_{i}
and
\mathbf{s}^{*}\mathbf{F}=D
.

Next, we consider the local picture. Suppose that on some open set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq X
, there exist regular functions
f_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,r
, such that the zero locus of each
f_{i}
is equal to
D_{i}
. Notice that the section
\mathbf{s}_{i}
of
\mathbf{L}_{i}
is defined as the section “
\mathbf{1}
” of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
. Thus,
\frac{1}{f_{i}}\cdot\mathbf{1}
defines a nonzero section of
\mathbf{L}_{i}
on
\mathcal{U}
, and
(\frac{1}{f_{1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{f_{r}})
gives a local trivialization
\mathbf{E}|_{\mathcal{U}}\simeq\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}
. Under this trivialization, the section
\mathbf{s}
can be written as the graph-embedding:

\mathbf{s}\colon\mathcal{U}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{C}^{r},\quad x\mapsto(x,f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{r}(x)).

Let
\underline{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
be the coordinates of the factor
\mathbb{C}^{r}
, and we write
\mathbb{C}^{r}_{\underline{t}}
to emphasize the chosen coordinates.
Under the local trivialization
\mathbf{E}|_{\mathcal{U}}\simeq\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}
, we have the induced local trivializations
for the logarithmic cotangent bundle

T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})|_{\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}}\simeq T^{*}\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}_{\underline{t}}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}_{\underline{s}}

where
\mathbb{C}^{r}_{\underline{t}}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}_{\underline{s}}
is the total space of the logarithmic cotangent bundle of
\mathbb{C}^{r}_{\underline{t}}
with respect to the coordinate divisors, and
\underline{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
with each
s_{i}
corresponding to the logarithmic tangent vector
-t_{i}\partial_{t_{i}}
.

Using the section
\mathbf{s}:X\to\mathbf{E}
, we can identify
X
as the closed subvariety
\mathbf{s}(X)
of
\mathbf{E}
, and hence open subvarieties of
X
as a locally closed subvarieties of
\mathbf{E}
. By the above trivialization, we have

(9)

T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})|_{\mathcal{U}}=\mathbf{s}^{*}(T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})|_{\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}})\simeq T^{*}\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{C}_{\underline{s}}^{r}.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq T^{*}U
be an irreducible conic Lagrangian cycle. Following
[
9
, §2.1]
, locally on
\mathcal{U}^{\circ}\coloneqq\mathcal{U}\setminus D
, we can define an
(n+r)
-cycle in
T^{*}\mathcal{U}^{\circ}\times\mathbb{C}_{\underline{s}}^{r}
:

(10)

\Lambda^{\sharp}|_{\mathcal{U}^{\circ}}\coloneqq\left\{\left(x,\xi+\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}d\log f_{i}(x),s\right)\middle|(x,\xi)\in\Lambda\textup{ and }s_{i}\not=0\textup{ for all }i\right\}.

It follows from the next lemma that the definition of
\Lambda^{\sharp}|_{\mathcal{U}^{\circ}}
does not depend on the choice of the functions
f_{i}
and the local cycles glue together to a global
(n+r)
-cycle

\Lambda^{\sharp}\subseteq T^{*}U\times\mathbb{C}^{r}\simeq T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})|_{U}.

Moreover, it also follows from the next lemma that
\Lambda^{\sharp}
is a conic cycle.

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the above notations, let
V=\mathbf{E}\setminus\mathbf{F}
. Pulling back one-forms defines a surjective bundle map

q:U\times_{V}T^{*}V=T^{*}V|_{U}\rightarrow T^{*}U.

Then, over the open set
\mathcal{U}^{\circ}=\mathcal{U}\setminus D
, we have an equality of algebraic cycles

\Lambda^{\sharp}|_{\mathcal{U}^{\circ}}=(q^{-1}\Lambda)|_{\mathcal{U}^{\circ}}.

Proof.

Recall that, locally on
\mathcal{U}^{\circ}
, the section
\mathbf{s}:X\to\mathbf{E}
is given by

\mathbf{s}(x)=(x,f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{r}(x)).

Thus, as the dual map of
q
, the pushfoward map on tangent bundle is of the form

(11)

q^{\vee}:TU\to\mathbf{s}^{*}TV=TV|_{X},\quad v\mapsto v+\sum_{i=1}^{r}{df_{i}(v)}\mathbf{s}^{*}(\partial_{t_{i}})

for any tangent vector
v
in
TU
. Locally on
\mathcal{U}^{\circ}
, under the above coordinate system,

T^{*}V=T^{*}U\times T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{r}=T^{*}U\times\mathbb{C}^{r}_{\underline{t}}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}_{\underline{s}}.

Then as the dual map of (
11
), the bundle map
q
at a fiber
T_{x}^{*}V
(
x\in U
) is of the form

(12)

q:T^{*}V|_{U}\rightarrow T^{*}U,\quad\xi+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}dt_{i}\mapsto\xi+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}df_{i},

where
\xi\in T_{x}^{*}U
.

Now, we show that
\Lambda^{\sharp}|_{\mathcal{U}^{\circ}}\subset(q^{-1}\Lambda)|_{\mathcal{U}^{\circ}}
, or equivalently,
q(\Lambda^{\sharp}|_{\mathcal{U}^{\circ}})\subset\Lambda
. Since
\Lambda
is conic, for any point
x\in\mathcal{U}^{\circ}
and
v\in T^{*}_{x}U
such that the pairing between
v
and any element in
\Lambda\cap T^{*}_{x}U
is zero, by (
10
) we need to show that

q\left(\xi+\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}d\log f_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}(-d\log t_{i})\right)(v)=0

for any
\xi\in\Lambda\cap T^{*}_{x}U
.
In fact, by (
12
), we have

\displaystyle q\left(\xi+\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}d\log f_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}(-d\log t_{i})\right)(v)=

\displaystyle\left(\xi+\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}d\log f_{i}\right)(v)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}\frac{df_{i}}{t_{i}}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi(v)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}\left(\frac{df_{i}}{f_{i}}(v)-\frac{df_{i}}{t_{i}}(v)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

where the second last equality follows from the fact that the image of
\mathbf{s}:U\to V
is cut out by equations
t_{i}=f_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
and the last equality follows from the assumption that the pairing between
v
and any element in
\Lambda\cap T^{*}_{x}U
is zero.

Therefore, we have proved the inclusion that
\Lambda^{\sharp}|_{\mathcal{U}^{\circ}}\subset(q^{-1}\Lambda)|_{\mathcal{U}^{\circ}}
. Since
\Lambda
is irreducible, the inclusion is between two irreducible closed
(n+r)
-cycles in
T^{*}\mathcal{U}^{\circ}\times\mathbb{C}_{\underline{s}}^{r}
. Hence the inclusion must be an equality, that is,
\Lambda^{\sharp}|_{\mathcal{U}^{\circ}}=(q^{-1}\Lambda)|_{\mathcal{U}^{\circ}}
.
∎

The above lemma shows that the global cycle
\Lambda^{\sharp}\subset T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})|_{U}
is well-defined, and

(13)

\Lambda^{\sharp}=q^{-1}\Lambda.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
D=\sum_{i=1}^{r}D_{i}
is a simple normal crossing divisor on
X
, then the pullback map of logarithmic forms,
p
, is a surjective map of vector bundles over
X
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{s}^{*}T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})=T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})|_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}(X,D)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

In particular, as restriction of
p
, the pullback map
q:T^{*}V|_{U}\rightarrow T^{*}U
is also a surjective map of vector bundles.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that
\mathbf{s}
induces an embedding of log pairs
\mathbf{s}:(X,D)\to(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})
. In other words, the image of
\mathbf{s}:X\to\mathbf{E}
intersects the divisor
\mathbf{F}
transversally in the stratified sense.

More precisely, we will show that there are (analytic) local coordinates of
\mathbf{E}
, such that
\mathbf{F}
is defined by the product of a subset of the coordinates and the image of
\mathbf{s}:X\to\mathbf{E}
is cut out by a disjoint subset of coordinates. Then the desired statement follows immediately.

Choose a small analytic neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of a given point
y\in X
, such that there exist holomorphic functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
on
\mathcal{U}
defining the divisors
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
.
Let
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a set of coordinates of
\mathcal{U}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
y\in D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{r_{0}}
and
y\notin D_{i}
for
i>r_{0}
. Since
D
is simple normal crossing, we can assume that
f_{i}=x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r_{0}
.
Then, locally, the embedding
\mathbf{s}|_{\mathcal{U}}:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbf{E}|_{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}_{\underline{t}}
is given by

x\mapsto(x,f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{r}(x))=(x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{r_{0}},f(x_{r_{0}+1}),\ldots,f(x_{r})).

The variables
(x,\underline{t})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
is a set of coordinates of
\mathbf{E}|_{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}_{\underline{t}}
. Thus,

(14)

\big(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r_{0}},x_{r_{0}+1},\ldots,x_{r},t_{1}-x_{1},\ldots,t_{r_{0}}-x_{r_{0}},t_{r_{0}+1}-f_{r_{0}+1},\ldots,t_{r}-f_{r}\big)

also form a set of coordinates of
\mathbf{E}|_{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}_{\underline{t}}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is centered at
y
with
y\notin D_{i}
for
i>r_{0}
, we can assume that
\mathcal{U}
does not intersect
D_{i}
for
i>r_{0}
. Now, the image of
\mathbf{s}|_{\mathcal{U}}
is cut out by

t_{1}-x_{1}=0,\cdots,t_{r_{0}}-x_{r_{0}}=0,t_{r_{0}+1}-f_{r_{0}+1}=0,\cdots,t_{r}-f_{r}=0,

and the divisor
\mathbf{F}
in
\mathbf{E}|_{\mathcal{U}}
is defined by
t_{1}\cdots t_{r_{0}}=0
. Therefore, the set of coordinates (
14
) satisfy the desired property.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Suppose
D
is simple normal crossing but not smooth. If we take
\mathbf{E}
to be the line bundle

\mathcal{O}_{X}
on
X
, then we can similarly define an irreducible divisor
\mathbf{F}
of
\mathbf{E}
, the graph embedding
\mathbf{s}:X\to\mathbf{E}
, and the projection

p:T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})|_{X}\to T^{*}(X,D).

However, in this case, the map

will not be surjective. For example, assume
\dim X=2
and
(x_{1},x_{2})
are locally coordinates of

such that locally

is defined by
x_{1}x_{2}=0
. One can easily see that the image of

on the fiber
T^{*}(X,D)|_{(0,0)}
is 1-dimensional and spanned by
d\log(x_{1}x_{2})
.
Therefore, it is necessary to introduce a variable
t_{i}
to each irreducible component
D_{i}
.

Similar to the construction in
[
9
, Section 3]
, we define
\overline{\Lambda^{\sharp}}
to be the closure of
\Lambda^{\sharp}
inside
\mathbf{s}^{*}T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})=T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})|_{X}
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq T^{*}U
be a conic Lagrangian cycle.
If
D=\sum_{i=1}^{r}D_{i}
is simple normal crossing, then

\overline{\Lambda^{\sharp}}=p^{-1}(\overline{\Lambda}^{\log}),

where
\overline{\Lambda}^{\log}
is the closure of
\Lambda
inside
T^{*}(X,D)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
, we have the following cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}V|_{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})|_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}(X,D),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

where

and both horizontal maps are surjective maps of vector bundles. Since

is closed and it contains

, the inclusion

follows. On the other hand, both

and

. Hence, they must be equal to each other, that is,
\overline{\Lambda^{\sharp}}=p^{-1}(\overline{\Lambda}^{\log})
.
∎

For the rest of this section, we assume that the divisor

is simple normal crossing.
Consider the following diagram of vector bundles on

,

(15)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}\mathbf{E}|_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})|_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}(X,D),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{\mathbf{E}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

where both

and

are defined by considering a one-form as a logarithmic one form, the map

is the pullback map of one-forms by
\mathbf{s}
, the map

.

Since our local description of
\Lambda^{\sharp}
in (

, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.6

(
[

, Theorem 3.2]
, see also
[

, Theorems 3.4]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot}
be any constructible complex on
U
, with characteristic cycle
CC({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot})=\sum_{k}n_{k}\Lambda_{k}
. Then, as cycles,

CC(Rj_{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot}))=\sum_{k}n_{k}\iota^{*}\overline{\Lambda^{\sharp}_{k}}.

Proof.

Given an irreducible conic Lagrangian cycle
\Lambda\subset T^{*}U
, by (
9
),

T^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})|_{\mathcal{U}}\cong T^{*}\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}_{\underline{s}}.

Under this trivialization, our definition of
\Lambda^{\sharp}
is the same as the total space of
\Lambda^{\sharp}_{s}
in
[
7
, Equation (3.3)]
. Moreover,
\iota
is a map of vector bundles over
X
, and under the above trivialization

\iota:T_{x}^{*}X\to T_{x}^{*}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F}),\quad\xi\to(\xi,0,\ldots,0),

for any
x\in\mathcal{U}
. Thus, the image of
\iota
is exactly cut out by equations
s_{1}=\cdots=s_{r}=0
. Therefore, our
\iota^{*}\overline{\Lambda^{\sharp}}
is exactly equal to
\lim_{{\underline{s}}\to(0,\ldots,0)}\Lambda_{\underline{s}}^{\sharp}
, as defined in
[
7
]
. Therefore, the desired equality follows from
[
7
, Theorem 3.4]
.
∎

We are ready to prove the first main theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot}
be a constructible complex on
U
, and assume that its characteristic cycle is of the form
CC({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot})=\sum_{k}n_{k}\Lambda_{k}
. By Corollary
3.5
and Theorem
3.6
,

CC(Rj_{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot}))=\sum_{k}n_{k}\iota^{*}p^{*}\left(\overline{\Lambda_{k}}^{\,\log}\right).

By the commutative diagram (
15
), since
p^{\prime}\circ\pi^{*}=\id
, we have
\iota^{*}p^{*}=\phi^{*}
and the above equation becomes

CC(Rj_{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot}))=\sum_{k}n_{k}\phi^{*}\left(\overline{\Lambda_{k}}^{\,\log}\right).

By Lemma
2.3

we have:

c_{*}^{T^{*}X}\left(\phi^{*}\left(\overline{\Lambda_{k}}^{\,\log}\right)\right)=c_{*}^{T^{*}(X,D)}\left(\overline{\Lambda_{k}}^{\,\log}\right).

Therefore,

c^{T^{*}X}_{*}\left(CC(Rj_{*}({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot}))\right)=c^{T^{*}(X,D)}_{*}\left(\sum_{k}n_{k}\overline{\Lambda_{k}}^{\,\log}\right)=c^{T^{*}(X,D)}_{*}\Big(\overline{CC({\mathcal{F}}^{\centerdot})}^{\log}\Big),

and we have finished the proof.
∎

First, we apply Theorem
1.1
to provide a dictionary between the class of the closure of the total space of critical points
\mathfrak{X}(Z)\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
and the Chern-Mather class
c_{Ma}(Z)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

To apply Theorem
1.1
, we let
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
with homogeneous coordinates
[p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},p_{+}]
. Let
D_{i}=\{p_{i}=0\}
for
0\leq i\leq n
,
D=\sum_{i=0}^{n}D_{i}
and
U=X\setminus D=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
.

First of all, we notice that the logarithmic cotangent bundle
E\coloneqq T^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n},D)
is a trivial rank
n
vector bundle. Thus, we can identify the compactification
\overline{E}
with
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
, with the first factor being the base and the second being the fiber. Given a very affine variety
Z\subset U=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
, we have by definition that

(16)

\mathfrak{X}^{\circ}(Z)=T^{*}_{Z_{\textrm{reg}}}(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}.

Let
\Lambda=T^{*}_{Z}(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
be the closure of
T^{*}_{Z_{\textrm{reg}}}(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
in
T^{*}(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
. Then
\Lambda
is a conic Lagrangian cycle in
T^{*}(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
.
Taking the closure of (
16
) in
E=T^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n},D)
, we have an equality of algebraic cycles:

\mathfrak{X}(Z)\cap E=\overline{\Lambda}^{\log}.

The closure of
\mathfrak{X}(Z)\cap E
in
\overline{E}
is exactly
\mathfrak{X}(Z)
. Hence, by (
5
), if

c_{*}^{T^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n},D)}(\overline{\Lambda}^{\log})=\sum_{i=0}^{d}v_{i}[\mathbb{P}^{i}]\in A_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n}),

with
d=\dim Z
, then

[\mathfrak{X}(Z)]=\sum_{i=0}^{d}v_{i}[\mathbb{P}^{i}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-i}]\in A_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}).

Next consider
Eu_{Z}
as a constructible function on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, with value equal to zero outside
Z
. In view of (
4
), this corresponds to the pushforward of
\Lambda
under the open inclusion

j:(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Let
\phi:T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{n}\to T^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n},D)
be the natural bundle map appearing in diagram (
15
). Then we have by Theorem
1.1
, Proposition
2.1
, Lemma
2.3
and (
3
) the following equalities in
A_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
:

\displaystyle c_{*}^{T^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n},D)}(\overline{\Lambda}^{\log})=

\displaystyle c_{*}^{T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\phi^{*}\overline{\Lambda}^{\log})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{d}\cdot c_{*}^{T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{n}}(CC(Eu_{Z}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{d}\cdot\check{c}_{Ma}(Z).

This is equivalent to saying that

c_{Ma}(Z)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{d-i}v_{i}[\mathbb{P}^{i}]\in A_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n}),

which finishes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

The next result, Theorem
1.6
, is a stepping stone towards proving the Huh-Sturmfels conjecture of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

First, we consider the compactification
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
, with homogeneous coordinates
[p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},p_{+}]
such that
x_{i}=\frac{p_{i}}{p_{+}}
. Then the hyperplane
{\mathcal{H}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is defined by
p_{+}-p_{1}-\cdots-p_{n}=0
, or equivalently,
p_{0}=0
with
p_{+}=p_{0}+\cdots+p_{n}
.

Let
U=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}\setminus\mathcal{H}
, and let
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Then the boundary divisor
D=X\setminus U
is equal to the union of all coordinate hyperplanes and
\mathcal{H}
, which is a simple normal crossing divisor. Notice that for any
{{u}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, the holomorphic 1-form

d\log l_{{u}}=u_{1}d\log x_{1}+\cdots+u_{n}d\log x_{n}+u_{0}d\log(1-x_{1}-\cdots-x_{n})

on
U
extends to a logarithmic
1
-form on
X
, that is, a global section of
\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)
. Moreover, since the mixed Hodge structure on
H^{1}(U,\mathbb{Q})
is of
(1,1)
-type, there is a natural isomorphism

H^{1}(U,\mathbb{C})\cong H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)),

and hence
\dim H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D))=n+1
. On the other hand, the
1
-forms
d\log l_{{u}}
form a vector space of dimension
n+1
. Thus, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the
1
-forms
d\log l_{{u}}
and the global sections of
\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)
. Under this correspondence,
H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D))
has a natural basis, given by

d\log x_{1},\,\ldots,\,d\log x_{n},\,\,\text{and}\,\,d\log(1-x_{1}-\cdots-x_{n}).

Since the vector bundle
\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)
is globally generated, the evaluation map

H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D))\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)

is surjective. Identifying
H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D))
with
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
using the above basis, we denote the corresponding map on the total spaces by

\rho:X\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\to T^{*}(X,D).

The likelihood correspondence
\mathcal{L}_{Y}
is defined as the closure in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
of

\mathcal{L}_{Y}^{\circ}\coloneqq\left\{({p},{u})\in Y^{\circ}_{\textrm{reg}}\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\mid{p}\text{ is a critical point of }l_{{u}}|_{Y^{\circ}_{\textrm{reg}}}\right\},

where
Y^{\circ}=Y\setminus{\mathcal{H}}
. Thus, by definition, we have

\mathcal{L}_{Y}^{\circ}=\rho^{-1}\left(T^{*}_{Y^{\circ}_{\textrm{reg}}}U\right).

Taking closure in
X\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1}=\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, we have

(17)

\mathcal{L}_{Y}\cap(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1})=\rho^{-1}\left(\overline{T^{*}_{Y^{\circ}}U}^{\log}\right).

Since
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is a trivial vector bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, its fiber-wise compactification is equal to
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
. Since
\mathcal{L}_{Y}\cap(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1})
is a conic subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
and its closure in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
is equal to
\mathcal{L}_{Y}
, we get by (
5
) that, if

c_{*}^{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1}}\big(\mathcal{L}_{Y}\cap(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1})\big)=\sum_{i=0}^{\dim Y}\alpha_{i}[\mathbb{P}^{i}]\in A_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n}),

then

[\mathcal{L}_{Y}]=\sum_{i=0}^{\dim Y}\alpha_{i}[\mathbb{P}^{i}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1-i}]\in A_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n+1}).

Now, considering
Eu_{Y^{\circ}}
as a constructible function on
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
with value zero outside
Y^{\circ}
and denoting its signed Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson class by
\check{c}_{Ma}(Y^{\circ})\in A_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle c_{*}^{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1}}\big(\mathcal{L}_{Y}\cap(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n+1})\big)=

\displaystyle c_{*}^{T^{*}(X,D)}\left(\overline{T^{*}_{Y^{\circ}}U}^{\log}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\dim Y}\cdot\check{c}_{Ma}(Y^{\circ}),

where the first equality follows from (
17
) and Lemma
2.3
, and the second equality follows from Theorem
1.1
, Proposition
2.1
and (
3
). Finally, combining the above three displayed equations, we can conclude Theorem
1.6
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the Huh-Sturmfels conjecture.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
\varphi=Eu_{Y^{\circ}}
, considered as a constructible function on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. By Theorem
1.6
and Corollary
1.7
, respectively, we have

B_{Y}(\mathsf{p},\mathsf{u})=(-1)^{\dim Y}\gamma_{\varphi}\left(-\frac{\mathsf{u}}{\mathsf{p}}\right)\mathsf{p}^{n},\quad\text{and}\quad S_{Y}(\mathsf{p},\mathsf{u})=(-1)^{\dim Y}\chi_{\varphi}\left(\frac{\mathsf{u}}{\mathsf{p}}\right)\mathsf{p}^{n},

with
\gamma_{\varphi}
and
\chi_{\varphi}
as defined in Section
2.3
.
By Corollary
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle S_{Y}(\mathsf{p},\mathsf{u})=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\dim Y}\chi_{\varphi}\left(\frac{\mathsf{u}}{\mathsf{p}}\right)\mathsf{p}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\dim Y}\frac{\frac{\mathsf{u}}{\mathsf{p}}\gamma_{\varphi}(-\frac{\mathsf{u}}{\mathsf{p}}-1)+\gamma_{\varphi}(0)}{\frac{\mathsf{u}}{\mathsf{p}}+1}\mathsf{p}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\dim Y}\frac{{\mathsf{u}}\gamma_{\varphi}(-\frac{\mathsf{u}+\mathsf{p}}{\mathsf{p}})+\mathsf{p}\gamma_{\varphi}(0)}{{\mathsf{u}}+\mathsf{p}}\mathsf{p}^{n}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{u}}{\mathsf{u}+\mathsf{p}}B_{Y}(\mathsf{p},\mathsf{u}+\mathsf{p})+\frac{\mathsf{p}}{\mathsf{u}+\mathsf{p}}B_{Y}(\mathsf{p},0),

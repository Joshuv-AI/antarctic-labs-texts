1.
Introduction

We study the arithmetic of an interesting family of genus two curves and their Jacobians. Combining explicit methods with techniques from geometry of numbers, we give bounds on the average number of rational points in the quadratic twist families of these curves and abelian surfaces, as well as lower bounds on the size of related Tate-Shafarevich groups.

1.1.
Results

Our first result is an explicit rational parameterization of the moduli space
\mathcal{H}_{3}
of genus two curves
C
, equipped with
\sqrt{3}
-multiplication
\iota\colon{\mathbb{Z}}[\sqrt{3}]\hookrightarrow\End(J)
on its Jacobian
J={\mathrm{Jac}}(C)
and a full
\sqrt{3}
-level structure
\epsilon\colon{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}\times\mu_{3}\simeq J[\sqrt{3}]
.
Our parameterization uses the polynomials
G_{1},G_{2},H_{1},H_{2},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[a,b,c][x]
given in Section
3
; see also
[
9
]
. The
G_{i}(x)
are cubic,
H_{i}(x)
are quadratic, and
\lambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[a,b,c]
are constants.

Let
k
be a field and assume
\mathrm{char}\,k\neq 2,3
. For non-zero
(a,b,c)\in k^{3}
, define the hyperelliptic curve
C_{a,b,c}
over
k
with affine model

y^{2}=G_{1}(x)^{2}+\lambda_{1}H_{1}(x)^{3},

obtained by specializing
G_{1},\lambda_{1},
and
H_{1}
at
(a,b,c)
. The isomorphism class of
C_{a,b,c}
depends only on the class
(a\colon b\colon c)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{2}(k)
.
The ratio

(\scalebox{0.70}[1.0]{$-$}3)\cdot\dfrac{G_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{1}H_{1}^{3}}{G_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{2}H_{2}^{3}}

is a square in the field of fractions of
{\mathbb{Z}}[a,b,c][x]
, hence
C_{a,b,c}
is isomorphic to

\scalebox{0.70}[1.0]{$-$}3y^{2}=G_{2}(x)^{2}+\lambda_{2}H_{2}(x)^{3}.

Outside of the vanishing locus
\Delta
of the discriminant of the defining sextic (see (
7

11

C=C_{a,b,c}
is smooth and has genus 2. Its Jacobian
J=J_{a,b,c}
is the abelian surface over
k
parameterizing equivalence classes of degree-zero divisors on
C
. There are two rational subgroups of order 3 on
J
, defined as follows. Let
\kappa
be a canonical divisor on
C
, having degree 2. Let
x_{1}
and
x_{1}^{\prime}
be the roots of
H_{1}(x)
in an algebraic closure
k^{\mathrm{alg}}
of

y_{1}=G_{1}(x_{1})
and
y_{1}^{\prime}=G_{1}(x_{1}^{\prime})
. Then the divisor
D_{1}=(x_{1},y_{1})+(x_{1}^{\prime},y_{1}^{\prime})-\kappa
has order 3 and represents a point in
J(k)
. It follows that the cyclic subgroup
\langle D_{1}\rangle
corresponds to a subgroup scheme
{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}\subset J
over
k
. Similarly, let
x_{2}
and
x_{2}^{\prime}
be the roots of
H_{2}(x)
, and set

y_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\scalebox{0.70}[1.0]{$-$}3}}G_{2}(x_{2})\mbox{ and }y_{2}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\scalebox{0.70}[1.0]{$-$}3}}G_{2}(x_{2}^{\prime}).

Then
D_{2}=(x_{2},y_{2})+(x_{2}^{\prime},y_{2}^{\prime})-\kappa
has order 3 in
J(k(\sqrt{\scalebox{0.70}[1.0]{$-$}3}))
, and
\langle D_{2}\rangle
corresponds to a subgroup scheme
\mu_{3}\subset J
over
k
. We establish the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

The assignment
(a:b:c)\mapsto J_{a,b,c}
realizes
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\setminus\Delta
as an open subset of the moduli space of principally polarized abelian surfaces with real multiplication by
\sqrt{3}
defined over
k
and full
\sqrt{3}
-level structure
{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}\times\mu_{3}\simeq J[\sqrt{3}]

Moduli spaces of abelian surfaces with special endomorphism rings are well-studied, particularly through their closely associated Hilbert modular surfaces. See for instance
[
29
,
20
]
for a classification of the discriminants that lead to rational surfaces, or the work of Elkies and Kumar
[
17
]
, covering many non-rational Hilbert modular surfaces as well. Results with explicit moduli interpretations, in the sense that a model of the associated universal
genus
2
curve is provided, are relatively rare. To our knowledge the parametrization provided here is new.

As a first application of Theorem
1.1
we prove a result about the growth of Tate-Shafarevich groups in quadratic twist families of abelian surfaces. If
A
is an abelian surface over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and

is a squarefree integer, we write

for the quadratic twist of

corresponding to the quadratic character
\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})/{\mathbb{Q}})\to\{\pm 1\}

\Sha(A_{d})[\ell]
denote the
\ell

\Sha(A_{d})

Delaunay-type conjectures
[
12
]
predict that for any

, there should be infinitely many

such that
\Sha(A_{d})[\ell]\neq 0
; in fact, the natural density of the set
\{d\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,\colon\Sha(A_{d})[\ell]\neq 0\}
should be positive
[
4
]
*Conj. 1.1.
This has been verified for certain twist families of elliptic curves for
\ell=3

[
4
,
1
]
and

(
[

]
).

The third author gave examples with
\dim A>1
(and
A
simple) in
[

, by studying quotients of the prime level modular Jacobian

with a point of order 3. For such

, one can access properties of their Néron models despite not having explicit equations, via Mazur’s study of the Eisenstein ideal. However, it seems likely that there are only finitely many such

of given dimension.

Our next theorem verifies
[
4
, Conj. 1.1]
for infinitely many abelian surfaces
A
, namely the quotients
A_{a,b,c}:=J_{a,b,c}/\langle D_{1}\rangle
. In fact, we show that for most of these

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix

and order points in

by height. Then for

\liminf_{X\to\infty}\dfrac{\#\{|d|\leq X\colon\#\Sha(A_{d})[3]\geq 3^{r}\}}{\#\{|d|\leq X\}}>0.

Remark 1.3

The same result holds for the surfaces
B_{a,b,c}:=J_{a,b,c}/\langle D_{2}\rangle
as well.

We say

if the lim inf in Theorem

As a second application of our explicit models for the curves
C=C_{a,b,c}
, we bound the average Mordell-Weil rank of the Jacobians

. Combining this with the Chabauty-Kim method, we obtain statistical results on the number of rational points on the curves
C_{d}
as well. Our methods apply to any of the curves

. As an example, we carry out the full analysis for the (simple) Jacobian of minimal root conductor in our family.

Theorem 1.4
.

The Jacobian
J
of the curve
C_{1,2,\scalebox{0.70}[1.0]{$-$}1}\colon y^{2}=8x^{5}-3x^{4}-2x^{3}-7x^{2}+4x+20

has root conductor
2\cdot 31
. The average
{\mathbb{Z}}[\sqrt{3}]
-rank of
J_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})
is at most
1.203
. At least
23.3\%
of twists have rank
0
and at least another
41.6\%
of twists have
{\mathbb{Z}}[\sqrt{3}]
-rank at most
1
. At least
20.8\%
of the latter twists have
{\mathbb{Z}}[\sqrt{3}]
-rank exactly equal to
1
.

From these rank results, we deduce uniform bounds on the size of
\#C_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})
, for many

.
This is clear when
\mathrm{rk}\,J_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})=0
, because then
C_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})
can only contain Weierstrass points (for

\mathrm{rk}\,J_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})>0

does not apply, since the real multiplication implies
\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}\,J_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})\geq 2=g(C_{d})

[
2

on the Chabauty-Kim method.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
C=C_{1,2,\scalebox{0.70}[1.0]{$-$}1}
, and consider integers

ordered by
|d|
. For at least

of such

, and for at least another

of

we have
\#C_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})\leq 154133

This seems to be the first non-trivial example of a genus 2 curve

can be uniformly bounded for a majority of

. We say “non-trivial” since

has rational Weierstrass points (so there are no local obstructions) and admits no maps to elliptic curves (even over

is surely far from optimal, but should improve as the Chabauty-Kim method is further refined.

1.2.
Methods and outline

Theorem
1.1
is proved in two steps. In Sections
2
and
3
, we give an explicit parameterization of genus 2 Jacobians
J
with isotropic
({\mathbb{Z}}/3\times\mu_{3})
-level structure, by proving a twisted version of a result of the first two authors and Testa
[
8
]
. Such Jacobians come equipped with a
(3,3)
-isogeny
\pi\colon J\to J^{\prime}
, and the surface
\mathcal{H}_{3}
is the locus where
\pi=\hat{\pi}
(and hence
J=J^{\prime}
and
\pi=[\sqrt{3}]
). This turns out to be a rational surface, and we explicitly write out the universal curve
C_{a,b,c}
over an open part of the moduli space.

The defining equation for
C_{a,b,c}
is cumbersome, but most of the geometry is encoded in the ten discriminant factors, which are quite simple and display many symmetries; see (
7
) and (
11
) in Section
3
. The non-smooth locus
\Delta=0
is a union of genus 0 curves (four lines and six conics) in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. By explicit computations in the generic fiber, we determine the reduction type of
C_{a,b,c}
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
away from the 18 residue disks lying over the points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
where these genus 0 curves intersect (Theorem
4.5
).
Using the explicit equations, we determine when the 3-torsion points
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
reduce to the identity component in the Néron model of the Jacobian, which allows us to compute the respective Tamagawa ratios
c_{p}(A)/c_{p}(J)
and
c_{p}(B)/c_{p}(J)
, listed in Table
2
. We find that up to some symmetries, the discriminant factors exactly correspond to the possible values of the two Tamagawa ratios.

The Tamagawa ratios are needed to study the Selmer groups of the 3-isogenies
\phi_{d}\colon J_{d}\to A_{d}
and
\psi_{d}\colon J_{d}\to B_{d}
. Using an analysis of local Selmer ratios and Poitou-Tate duality, we give a simple criterion in terms of
(a\colon b\colon c)
for the set
\{d\colon\#\Sha(A_{d})[3]\geq 3^{r}\}
to have positive density (Theorem
6.5
); here
A=A_{a,b,c}
. We show that when there are enough primes dividing specific discriminant factors, then there are many twists
d
such that
\Sel(\phi_{d})
is large, whereas
\Sel(\psi_{d})
is small on average. This forces
\Sha(A_{d})[3]
to be large, not just on average, but for many individual
d
as well. This method builds on the third author’s work
[
26
]
and his work with Bhargava, Klagsbrun, and Lemke Oliver
[
3
]
. To conclude that the criterion holds for
100\%
of
(a\colon b\colon c)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
, we invoke Erdos-Kac type results for values of multivariable polynomials
[
16
,
22
]
. This proves Theorem
1.2
, which can be thought of as a higher-dimensional version of
[
4
, Thms. 1.2-1.4]
.

In Section
7
, we prove Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
, and show how to prove explicit statistical results for
\mathrm{rk}\,J_{d}({\mathbb{Q}}),\,\#C_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})
, and
\#\Sha(A_{d})[3]
, for a fixed curve
C=C_{a,b,c}
in our family. The tools here are the geometry-of-numbers techniques of
[
26
,
3
,
4
]
, results of Balakrishnan-Dogra
[
2
]
, and a recent result of Castella-Gross-Li-Skinner
[
11
]
in Iwasawa theory.

Finally, we recall that our formulas for Tamagawa ratios are only valid away from the 18 points of intersection in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. Over these 18 degenerate points, the Tamagawa ratios could in principle be computed for specific
(a\colon b\colon c)
but giving general formulas seems harder. One issue is that
J_{a,b,c}
may fail to be semistable at
p
in this locus, even for
p
large. Recall that an abelian variety
A
over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
with full
\ell
-level structure is automatically semistable if
p\neq\ell>2
, by a theorem of Raynaud
[
19
, Exposé IX, 4.7]
. Similarly, one can show that if
A
has
{\mathbb{Z}}[\sqrt{\ell}]
-multiplication and full
\sqrt{\ell}
-level structure, then
J
is semistable if
p\neq\ell>3
. Our curves show that the inequality
\ell>3
is sharp. Computing Tamagawa ratios when
J
has additive reduction is quite subtle, since
A
and
B
are not themselves Jacobians. Fortunately, additive reduction on
\mathcal{H}_{3}
only appears in codimension two, so this is not an issue in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

4.2.

Recall that

are parameterized by

If

The torus character can be read off from the tangent cone of one of the nodes of

The other bit of data we will need is the component group

Possible reduction types of hyperelliptic curves, and in particular of curves of genus

Proposition 4.4
.

Let

To ease notation we set

The relevant observation here is that after a coordinate change, we can assume that

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose that

and that

has good reduction. In fact,

Proof.

We use
[
14

to have good reduction in terms of a

determined by the six roots of

Write

for the algebraic closure of

and write
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{alg}}
for its ring of integers over
\mathcal{O}
, with

the extension of the valuation. We endow

with a logarithmic distance induced by

of

For a hyperelliptic curve

with a set of six points

The criterion

is that the splitting field of

is unramified, that all proper subclusters of

have odd cardinality, and that all clusters of cardinality at least

have even depth.

We parametrize

by setting

. We obtain

f_{s,t,s}(x)=((8s^{3}-t^{3})x-3s)(s^{2}(4s^{3}+t^{3})x^{2}-(4s^{3}+t^{3})x+s),

We particularly see that

has three roots with negative valuation, so the naive model does not have stable reduction. We also see that

where

have degrees

respectively. Let us write
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
for the points corresponding to the roots of

\alpha_{4},\alpha_{5}
for the roots of

, and
\alpha_{6}
for the root of

.

. From Proposition
3.4
we know that

admits a Richelot isogeny, so by
[
7

It follows that the splitting field of

is unramified. Since this is true for

We see that

are subclusters of depth

, and

with center

has at least three children of odd cardinality,

does not have potentially totally toric reduction

, Thm. 1.8(8)]
. Since

has real multiplication, it must therefore have potentially good reduction. By
[

, Thm. 1.8(6)]

have odd cardinality. This implies that

are pairwise equidistant. From (

so we see that the depth of

is

, which is indeed even.

Finally, by
[

has bad reduction. Since its Jacobian has good reduction, it follows that its Jacobian reduces to a product of elliptic curves.
∎

5.
Ratios of Tamagawa numbers

In this section, we compute certain ratios of Tamagawa numbers associated to the curve
C=C_{a,b,c}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, where
p
is a prime of bad reduction. To begin, we continue with the setting and notation of Section
4
, working over a more general local field
k
.

If
A/k
is an abelian variety, its Tamagawa number is
c(A)=\#\Phi_{A}(\bar{k})
,
where
\Phi_{A}
is the component group of
A
, as defined in (
12
). The specialization map

\mathrm{sp}\colon A(k)\longrightarrow\Phi_{A}(\bar{k})

is defined by extending
P\in A(k)
to
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
and then specializing to the special fiber of the Néron model
\mathcal{A}
.

Now let
J=J_{a,b,c}=\mathrm{Jac}(C)
and let
k
be a finite extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
for some prime
p
. By construction we have
\langle D_{1}\rangle\times\langle D_{2}\rangle\simeq({\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}})\times\mu_{3}\subset J[3]
. Write

A=J/\langle D_{1}\rangle\text{ and }B=J/\langle D_{2}\rangle

for the corresponding quotients over
k
.

The goal of this section is to compute the ratios
c(A)/c(J)
and
c(B)/c(J)
. Depending on the reduction type of
C
, this could be a rather subtle question. For the proof of Theorem
1.2
, it will be enough to consider cases of semistable reduction. With this in mind, we give a general criterion for computing Tamagawa ratios of
\ell
-isogenous abelian varieties with totally split toric reduction.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
J
be an abelian variety over
k
with split totally toric reduction. Let
\langle P\rangle\subset J[\ell]
be a
k
-rational subgroup of prime order
\ell\neq p
, and let
A=J/\langle P\rangle
. Assume
P\in J(k^{\mathrm{alg}})
is defined over an unramified extension
F/k
. Then

c(A)/c(J)=\begin{cases}\ell&\mbox{if }\mathrm{sp}_{F}(P)=0\\
1/\ell&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Tamagawa numbers of abelian varieties with split totally toric reduction are unchanged by unramified base change, so we may assume
F=k
. By the theory of
p
-adic uniformization there is an analytic isomorphism
J\simeq T/\Lambda
, where
T=\mathbb{G}_{m,k}^{g}
and
\Lambda=\langle q_{1},\ldots,q_{g}\rangle\subset k^{\times g}
is a full rank multiplicative lattice.

If
\mathrm{sp}(P)\neq 0
, then
c(A)/c(J)=1/\ell
; the proof is exactly as in
[
26
, Prop. 6.6]
. Note that the condition
\mathrm{sp}(P)\neq 0
is equivalent to saying that we may choose the basis
\{q_{i}\}
of
\lambda
such that
A\simeq T/\langle q_{1}^{1/\ell},q_{2},\ldots,q_{g}\rangle
.

If
\mathrm{sp}(P)=0
, then
P
belongs to the subgroup

\mu_{\ell}^{g}\hookrightarrow(T/\Lambda)[\ell]\simeq J[\ell].

Note that the quotient
J/\mu_{\ell}^{g}
is isomorphic
T/\langle q_{1}^{\ell},\ldots,q_{g}^{\ell}\rangle
via the map

(x_{1},\ldots x_{g})\mapsto(x_{1}^{\ell},x_{2}^{\ell},\ldots,x_{g}^{\ell}).

In the other direction, the natural
\ell^{g}
-isogeny

J/\mu_{\ell}^{g}\simeq T/\langle q_{1}^{\ell},\ldots,q_{g}^{\ell}\rangle\to T/\langle q_{1},\ldots,q_{g}\rangle=J

factors as a sequence of
g
different
\ell
-isogenies whose kernels map non-trivially into the component group. So
c(J/\mu_{\ell}^{g})=\ell^{g}c(J)
, by what we’ve already proven. To prove that
c(A)/c(J)=\ell
in this case, we use the fact that
c(A)/c(J)
is either
\ell
or
1/\ell
, for any
\ell
-isogeny
J\to A

[
21
, Lem. 2.3]
. Since the total effect of the
g
different
\ell
-isogenies is to multiply the Tamagawa number by
\ell^{g}
, it follows that for each subgroup
\langle P\rangle\subset\mu_{\ell}^{g}
, we have
c(J/\langle P\rangle)=\ell c(J)
, since we may choose
J\to J/\langle P\rangle
to be the first isogeny in the chain.
∎

We also deduce a formula for the Tamagawa ratio of the quadratic twists
J_{d}
, for

.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let

be as above and suppose
\mathrm{char}\,\bar{k}

.
∎

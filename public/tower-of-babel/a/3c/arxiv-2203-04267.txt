A bijective proof of a generalization of the non-negative crank–odd mex identity
Isaac Konan

Abstract
. Recent works of Andrews–Newman and Hopkins–Sellers unveil an interesting relation between two partition statistics, the crank and the mex. They state that, for a positive integer n n , there are as many partitions of n n with non-negative crank as partitions of n n with odd mex. In this paper, we give a bijective proof of a generalization of this identity provided by Hopkins, Sellers and Stanton. Our method uses an alternative definition of the Durfee decomposition, whose combinatorial link to the crank was recently studied by Hopkins, Sellers, and Yee.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
State of art

An
integer partition
is a finite non-increasing sequence of positive integers. It then has the form
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s})
with
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{s}\geq 1
. The terms
\lambda_{i}
are called the parts of
\lambda
, and we denote by
\ell(\lambda)=s
and
|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{s}
respectively the length and the weight of the partition
\lambda
. For example,
\ell(\emptyset)=|\emptyset|=0
. For a non-negative integer
n
, an integer partition with weight
n
is commonly called a partition of
n
. For example, the partition of
3
are
(3),(2,1)
and
(1,1,1)
, respectively with length
1,2
and
3
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of integer partitions. For
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{P}
,
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is the complementary of
\mathcal{F}
, i.e.
\overline{\mathcal{F}}=\{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}:\lambda\notin\mathcal{F}\}
. In the remainder of the paper, the term “partition” stands for an integer partition, and
\sharp A
denotes the number of elements in the set
A
.

In a 1988 paper
[
1
]
, Andrews and Garvan formally provide a definition of Dyson’s crank, a partition statistic introduced by Dyson in
[
4
]
to combinatorially explain a divisibility property of partitions.
Let
\lambda
be a partition. Set
\omega(\lambda)=\sharp\{i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell(\lambda)\}:\lambda_{i}=1\}
, the number of occurrences of
1
as part of
\lambda
, and set
\eta(\lambda)=\sharp\{i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell(\lambda)\}:\lambda_{i}>\omega(\lambda)\}
the number of parts greater than
\omega(\lambda)
. The crank of
\lambda
, denoted
crank(\lambda)
, is defined by the relation

crank(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \omega(\lambda)=0\,,\\
\eta(\lambda)-\omega(\lambda)\ \ \text{if}\ \ \omega(\lambda)>0\,.\end{cases}

(1.1)

In particular, set
crank(\emptyset)=0
. One can easily check that
-|\lambda|\leq crank(\lambda)\leq|\lambda|
.
Given integers
m,n
with
-n\leq m\leq n
,
C(m,n)
denotes the number of partitions of weight
n
and crank
m
, with the exception that
C(1,1)=-C(0,1)=1
. In
[
5
]
, Garvan explicitly provides the generating function for the crank.

Theorem 1.1

(Garvan)
.

We have

(x-1)q+\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}x^{crank(\lambda)}q^{|\lambda|}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{m=-n}^{n}C(m,n)x^{m}q^{n}=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(qx,qx^{-1};q)_{\infty}}

(1.2)

where
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{t};q)_{\infty}=\prod_{k\geq 0}\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1-a_{i}q^{k})
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Given integers
n\geq m\geq 0
, we have
C(m,n)=C(-m,n)
.

Recent works involved the use of a new partition statistic, the
minimal excludant
or
mex
, defined as the smallest positive integer which is not a part of the partition.
For
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
, set
mex(\lambda)
to be the mex of
\lambda
. For example,
mex(\emptyset)=mex((5,3,2,2))=1
. A curious yet interesting connection between the mex and the crank arose from the works of Andrews–Newman
[
2
]
and Hopkins–Sellers
[
6
]
, who independently found the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

At fixed weight, the number of partitions with non-negative crank is equal to the number of partitions with odd mex.

In
[
7
]
, Hopkins, Sellers and Stanton provide a broad generalization of Theorem
1.3
by introducing a notion generalizing the mex. For
j\geq 0
, and
\lambda
a partition containing the part
j
,
mex_{j}(\lambda)
is the smallest integer greater than
j
which is not a part of
\lambda
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, at fixed weight greater than
1
, the number of partitions
\lambda
with a part
j
such that
mex_{j}(\lambda)-j
is odd is equal to the number of partitions with crank at least equal to
j
.

The case
j=0
of the above theorem implies Theorem
1.3
as
mex_{0}(\lambda)=mex(\lambda)
and
0
can be seen as a fictitious part of all partitions.

The aim of this paper is to provide a purely bijective proof Theorem
1.4
. This generalization will derive from a key result related the Durfee decomposition of partitions.

1.2.
Statement of results

We first extend the generalization of the notion of mex to all partitions.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. For
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
, the
j
-mex of
\lambda
, denoted
mex_{j}(\lambda)
, is the smallest integer greater than
j
which is not a part of
\lambda
.
For example, we have
mex_{j}(\emptyset)=j+1
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

mex_{0}(5,3,2,2)=1,mex_{1}(5,3,2,2)=mex_{2}(5,3,2,2)=mex_{3}(5,3,2,2)=4,mex_{4}(5,3,2,2)=6\,,

and finally
mex_{j}(5,3,2,2)=j+1
for
j\geq 5
. Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{j}
the set of partitions with a
j
-mex parity different from
j
. With the previous examples, we have that
\emptyset\in M_{j}
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and

(5,3,2,2)\in\mathcal{M}_{0}\cap\mathcal{M}_{1}\cap\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2}\cap\mathcal{M}_{3}\cap\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}\cap\bigcap_{j\geq 5}\mathcal{M}_{j}\,.

In the remainder of the paper, for all
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
, we set
\lambda_{0}=\infty
and
\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)+1}=0
, so that the sequence
(\lambda_{0},\cdots,\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)+1})
remains non-increasing. A partition then becomes a non-increasing sequence starting from
\infty
and ending by
0
. For example, the partition
\emptyset
is associated to the sequence
(\infty,0)
with
\emptyset_{0}=\infty
and
\emptyset_{1}=0
. For
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, denote by
\mathcal{P}_{j}
the set of partitions which do not have
j
as part. Conversely,
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j}
is the set of partitions with a part
j
. A rephrasing of Theorem
1.4
is then the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

For
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, at fixed weight greater than
1
, the number of partitions in
\mathcal{M}_{j}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j}
is equal to the number of partitions with crank at least equal to
j
.

The bijective proof of Theorem
1.6
that we provide in this paper, was deeply inspired by the work of Hopkins, Sellers, and Yee, who described in
[
8
]
combinatorial relations that link the crank and the Durfee decomposition of a partition. Our work is based on a simple yet subtle definition related the very notion of Durfee decomposition.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
. The function
i\mapsto\lambda_{i}-i
is then decreasing on
\{0,\ldots,\ell(\lambda)+1\}
, with
\lambda_{0}-0=\infty
and
\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)+1}-(\ell(\lambda)+1)<0
. Therefore, for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, there exists a unique integer
d_{j}^{\lambda}\in\{0,\ldots,\ell(\lambda)\}
such that
\lambda_{d_{j}^{\lambda}}-d_{j}^{\lambda}\geq j>\lambda_{d_{j}^{\lambda}+1}-(d_{j}^{\lambda}+1)
. Formally written,

d_{j}^{\lambda}=\max\{i\in\{0,\ldots,\ell(\lambda)\}:\lambda_{i}-i\geq j\}\,.

(1.3)

For example,
d_{j}^{\emptyset}=0
, and for
\lambda=(5,3,2,2)
,

d_{0}^{\lambda}=d_{1}^{\lambda}=2,\,d_{2}^{\lambda}=d_{3}^{\lambda}=d_{4}^{\lambda}=1\text{ and }d_{j}^{\lambda}=0\text{ for }j\geq 5\,.

We also denote by
\mathcal{F}_{j}
the set of partitions
\lambda
such that
j\notin\{\lambda_{i}-i:i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell(\lambda)\}\}
, which is equivalent to saying that
\lambda_{d_{j}^{\lambda}}-d_{j}^{\lambda}>j>\lambda_{d_{j}^{\lambda}+1}-(d_{j}^{\lambda}+1)
. Conversely,
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j}
is the set of partitions
\lambda
satisfying
\lambda_{d_{j}^{\lambda}}=d_{j}^{\lambda}+j
.

Remark 1.8
.

For
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, as
\lambda_{d_{j+1}^{\lambda}+2}-(d_{j+1}^{\lambda}+2)<j<\lambda_{d_{j+1}^{\lambda}}-d_{j+1}^{\lambda}
when
d_{j+1}^{k}<\ell(\lambda)
, we always have that
d_{j}^{\lambda}-d_{j+1}^{\lambda}\in\{0,1\}
. Therefore, the sequence
(d_{j}^{\lambda})_{j\geq 0}
is non-increasing while the sequence
(d_{j}^{\lambda}+j)_{j\geq 0}
is non-decreasing.

Remark 1.9
.

The Durfee decomposition of a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)})
is the triplet
(d_{0}^{\lambda},\mu,\nu)
with
(\mu,\nu)=[(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{d_{0}^{\lambda}}),(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{d_{0}^{\lambda}})]
such that, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,d_{0}^{\lambda}\}
,
\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}-i
and
\nu_{i}=\sharp\{u\in\{1,\ldots,\ell(\lambda)\}:\lambda_{u}\geq i\}-i
. Inversely, for any triplet
(t,\mu,\nu)
such that
\mu
and
\nu
are increasing sequences of
t
non-negative integers, we associate the partition
\lambda
with length
\nu_{1}+1
and

\begin{cases}\lambda_{i}=\mu_{i}+i\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for}\ \ 1\leq i\leq t\\
\lambda_{i}=\sharp\{u:\nu_{u}+u\geq i\}\ \ \text{for}\ \ t+1\leq i\leq\nu_{1}+1\,.\end{cases}

We note
\lambda\equiv(t,\mu,\nu)
, and one can check that
|\lambda|=t+\sum_{u=1}^{t}\mu_{u}+\nu_{u}
. In particular,
\emptyset\equiv(0,\emptyset,\emptyset)
.

Observe that, for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
\mathcal{F}_{j}
can be equivalently defined as the set of partitions
\lambda\equiv(d_{0}^{\lambda},\mu,\nu)
such that
j
is not in
\mu
.

We now provide an intermediate result that plays a fundamental role in the bijective proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 1.10
.

For
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, at fixed weight, the number of partitions of
\mathcal{M}_{j}
is equal to the number of partitions in
\mathcal{F}_{j}
.

Corollary 1.11
.

For
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, at fixed weight, the number of partitions of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{j}
is equal to the number of partitions in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j}
.

Using the following reformulation of a result provided by Hopkins, Sellers and Stanton in
[
7
]
, we derive the generalization of Theorem
1.3
from Theorem
1.10
and Corollary
1.2
.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Then, at fixed weight greater than
1
, there are as many partitions in
\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j}
as partitions with crank at most equal to
-j
.

By adding a part
j
to the partitions in
\mathcal{M}_{j}
and
\mathcal{F}_{j}
when
j>0
, Theorem
1.12
implies that, at fixed weight, there are as many partitions in
\mathcal{M}_{j}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j}
as partitions in
\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j}
. Then, by Theorem
1.10
, at fixed weight greater than
1
, there are as many partitions in
\mathcal{M}_{j}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j}
as partitions with crank at most equal to
-j
. We finally obtain Theorem
1.4
from Corollary
1.2
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. We first provide in Section
2
a simple analytic proof of Theorem
1.10
. Then, in Sections
3
and
4
, a bijection for Theorem
1.10
is given, so as the proof of its well-definedness. After that, in Section
5
, we give a direct bijective proof of Corollary
1.11
in the spirit of the bijection of Section
3
.
In Section
6
, Theorem
1.12
is proved bijectively. Finally, in Section
8
, we provide the full scope of bijective proof of Theorem
1.4
with a bijection for Corollary
1.2
given in Section
7
.

2.
Analytic proof of Theorem
1.10

For
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, set
\Delta_{j,k}=(j+k,\ldots,j+1)
the partition consisting of
k
consecutive integers ending by
j+1
, and
\Delta_{j,0}=\emptyset
. Then,
|\Delta_{j,k}|=\frac{k(k+1)}{2}+jk
.
Hence, by Definition
1.5
, the set of partitions with
j
-mex equal to
j+k+1
can be associated to
\{\Delta_{j,k}\}\times\mathcal{P}_{j+k+1}
, i.e.
mex_{j}(\lambda)=j+k+1
if and only if there exists a unique partition, without a part
j+k+1
, whose parts are exactly those of
\lambda
except once the parts
j+1,\ldots,j+k
.
We then have

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}x^{mex_{j}(\lambda)}y^{\ell(\lambda)}q^{|\lambda|}=\frac{x^{j+1}}{(qy;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{k\geq 0}(xy)^{k}(1-yq^{j+k+1})q^{|\Delta_{j,k}|}\,.

Using the above relation with
x=y=1
and the sum run over
k
even, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}q^{|\lambda|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{k\geq 0}(1-q^{j+2k+1})\cdot q^{|\Delta_{j,2k}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}q^{|\Delta_{j,k}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}q^{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}+jk}\,.

Also, by the Jacobi triple product,

(-x;q)_{\infty}(-x^{-1}q;q)_{\infty}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\cdot\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k}x^{k}q^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\,,

and by Remark
1.9
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}q^{|\lambda|}

\displaystyle=[x^{0}]\left[(-x;q)_{\infty}(-x^{-1}q;q)_{\infty}\right]\cdot\frac{1}{(1+xq^{j})}

\displaystyle=[x^{0}]\left[\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\cdot\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k}x^{k}q^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\right]\cdot\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}x^{k}q^{jk}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\cdot\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}q^{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}+kj}\,.

In conclusion,

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}q^{|\lambda|}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}q^{|\lambda|}
.

3.
Bijection for Theorem
1.10

In this section, we provide two inverse maps for the bijective proof of Theorem
1.10
. In our bijections, we will apply some transformations on pairs of partitions in
\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,k}\}\times\mathcal{P}
. One has to keep in mind that
\mathcal{P}
can be trivially associated to
\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{P}=\{\emptyset\}\times\mathcal{P}
. Finally, we identify
\mathcal{M}_{j}
to
\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}_{j+2k+1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{j}
to
\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j}
. Two maps will then be constructed,

\Phi_{j}:\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}_{j+2k+1}\to\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j}

and

\Psi_{j}:\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j}\to\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}_{j+2k+1}\,,

in such a way that the partitions in the pairs have their weight and their length sums conserved during the process. By abuse of notation, if
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{j}
is identified to the pair
(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)
, we write
\Phi_{j}(\lambda)=\nu\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
such that
\Phi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu))=(\Delta_{j,0},\nu)
. The same convention stands for
\Psi_{j}:\mathcal{F}_{j}\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{j}
.

3.1.
From
\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}_{j+2k+1}
to
\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j}

Let
\phi_{j}
be the map defined on

\left(\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}\right)\setminus\left(\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j}\right)=\left(\bigsqcup_{k\geq 1}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j+2k}\right)\sqcup\left(\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2k}\right)

as follows.

(1)

For
k\geq 0
and
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2k}
, do the transformation

\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}\mapsto\lambda_{1}+1,\ldots,\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1}+1,1+j+2k\,.

This means that, in
\lambda
, we delete the part
\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}=d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+j+2k
, add
1
to the
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1
largest
finite
parts and add a part
1+j+2k+1
. We then obtain a partition
\mu
, and set
\phi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))=(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)
. Observe that
|\mu|=|\lambda|
and
\ell(\mu)=\ell(\lambda)
, so that the weight and length sums are conserved. Moreover, the transformation does not involved parts less than
1+j+2k
, so that the parts at most equal to
j
are conserved.

(2)

For
k\geq 1
and
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j+2k}
, do the transformation

\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}\mapsto\Delta_{j,2k-2},\lambda_{1}-1,\ldots,\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}-1,d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+j+2k,j+2k-1\,.

This means that, in
\Delta_{j,2k}
, we deleted the parts
j+2k,j+2k-1
, and in
\lambda
, we subtract
1
to the
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}
largest finite parts and add the parts
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+j+2k
and
j+2k-1
to obtain a partition
\mu
. We finally set
\phi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))=(\Delta_{j,2k-2},\mu)
. Note that
|\mu|=|\lambda|+(j+2k)+(j+2k-1)
, and
\ell(\mu)=\ell(\lambda)+2
, so that the weight and length sums are conserved. In addition, The parts at most equal to
j
are conserved.

For all
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}_{j+2k+1}
,
iterate the map
\phi_{j}
as long as it is possible. The iteration stops as soon as we reach a pair in
\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j}
. We then set
\Phi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))=\phi_{j}^{u}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))\in\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j}
. We finally observe that the transformations occur only on parts greater than
j
. Therefore, if
\Phi_{j}
is well-defined, it then induces a map from
\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\mathcal{P}_{j+2k+1}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j}\right)
to
\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\left(\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j}\right)
.

Example 3.1
.

For
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\Phi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\emptyset))=(\Delta_{j,0},\Delta_{j,2k})\,,

as
\phi_{j}^{u}((\Delta_{j,2k},\emptyset))=(\Delta_{j,2k-2u},\Delta_{j+2k-2u,2u})
for
0\leq u\leq k
.

3.2.
From
\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j}
to
\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}_{j+2k+1}

Let
\psi_{j}
be the map defined on

\left(\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}\right)\setminus\left(\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}_{j+2k+1}\right)=\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}

as follows. Let
k\geq 0
.

(1)

For
(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2k+1}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)
, do the transformation

\Delta_{j,2k},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}},1+j+2k\mapsto\Delta_{j,2k+2},\mu_{1}+1,\ldots,\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}-1}+1\,.

This means that, in
\Delta_{j,2k}
, we add the parts
1+j+2k,j+2k+2
, and in
\mu
, we add
1
to the
d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}-1
largest finite parts and delete the parts
\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}}=d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+1+j+2k
and
1+j+2k
to obtain a partition
\lambda
. We finally set
\psi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu))=(\Delta_{j,2k+2},\lambda)
. Note that
|\lambda|=|\mu|-(j+2k+2)-(j+2k+1)
, and
\ell(\lambda)=\ell(\mu)-2
.

(2)

For
(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\mathcal{F}_{j+2k+1}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)

\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}},1+j+2k\mapsto\mu_{1}-1,\ldots,\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}}-1,d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+1+j+2k\,.

This means that, in
\mu
, we deleted the part
1+j+2k
, subtract
1
to the
d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}
largest finite parts and add the part
d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+1+j+2k
to obtain a partition
\lambda
. We finally set
\psi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu))=(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)
. Observe that
|\lambda|=|\mu|
and
\ell(\lambda)=\ell(\mu)
.

For all
(\Delta_{j,0},\mu)\in\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j}
,

. We then set
\Psi_{j}((\Delta_{j,0},\mu))=\psi_{j}^{u}((\Delta_{j,0},\mu))\in\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}_{j+2k+1}

\Phi_{j}
, if

to
\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\mathcal{P}_{j+2k+1}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j}\right)

4.
Proof of the well-definedness of the bijection

The maps
\Phi_{j}
and
\Psi_{j}
preserve the weight and length sums of the pair of partitions, as they result from iterations of
\phi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
which have this property. To prove that
\Phi_{j}
and
\Psi_{j}
describe inverse bijections, we first prove that
\phi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
are inverse each other, then prove the well-definedness of
\Phi_{j}
and
\Psi_{j}
and conclude.

4.1.
The maps
\phi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
are inverse each other

We first prove that
\phi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
are inverse each other with the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

For all
k\geq 0
,
\phi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
describe inverse bijections between

(1)

\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2k}
and
\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\mathcal{F}_{1+j+2k}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)
,

(2)

\{\Delta_{j,2k+2}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j+2k+2}
and
\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1+j+2k}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
k,j\geq 0
.

(1)

The maps
\phi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
describe inverse bijections between
\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2k}
and

\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\mathcal{F}_{1+j+2k}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)
.

(a)

For
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2k}
,
set
\ell(\lambda)\geq t\geq d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}
such that
\lambda_{t}\geq 1+j+2k>\lambda_{t+1}
. By the first case of Section
3.1
,
\mu
consists of the parts

\begin{cases}\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}+1\ \ \text{ for }\ \ 1,\leq i<d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}\,,\\
\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}\ \ \ \ \text{ for }\ \ d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}\leq i<t\,,\\
\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{ for }\ \ t<i\leq\ell(\lambda)\,,\\
\mu_{t}=1+j+2k\,.\end{cases}

Since
1+j+2k
is a part of
\mu
, we have that
\mu\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{1+j+2k}
.
Moreover,
\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}\geq\mu_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}
, and

\displaystyle\mu_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1}-(d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1}-(d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1)+1

\displaystyle>\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}-d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+1

\displaystyle=1+j+2k

\displaystyle>\mu_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}-d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}\,,

so that
d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}=d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1
and
\mu\in\mathcal{F}_{1+j+2k}
. Thus,

\phi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\mathcal{F}_{1+j+2k}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)\,.

As
d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}=d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1
, by applying the second case of Section
3.2
on
(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)
, it is straightforward that
\psi_{j}(\phi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)))=(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)
.

(b)

Let
(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\mathcal{F}_{1+j+2k}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)
.
Similarly to the previous case, as

\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+1}\leq 1+d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+j+2k\leq\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}}-1\,,

we observe that the part
\lambda_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+1}=1+d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+j+2k
so that
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}=d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+1
and
\lambda\in\mathcal{F}_{j+2k}
. Then,

\psi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu))\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2k}

. Using the first case of Section
3.1
on
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)
with
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}=d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+1
, it is straightforward that
\phi_{j}(\psi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)))=(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)
.

(2)

The maps
\phi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
describe inverse bijections between
\{\Delta_{j,2k+2}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j+2k+2}
and

\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1+j+2k}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)
.

(a)

Let
(\Delta_{j,2k+2},\lambda)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k+2}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j+2k+2}
.
Note that

\lambda_{d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}}-d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}>j+2k+2>\lambda_{d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}+1}-(d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}+1)\,.

Then, the partition
\mu
consists of the parts
\lambda_{1}-1,\ldots,\lambda_{d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}}-1,\lambda_{d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell}
and the parts
d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}+j+2k+2,1+j+2k
. Since
1+j+2k
is a part
\mu
,
\mu\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{1+j+2k}
. Moreover,
\lambda_{d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}}-1\geq d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}+j+2k+2\geq\lambda_{d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}}
and
d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}+j+2k+2>1+j+2k
, so that
\mu_{d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}+1}=d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}+j+2k+2
. Hence,
d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}=d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}+1
and
\mu\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1+j+2k}
, and

\phi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k+2},\lambda))\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1+j+2k}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)\,.

Finally, by using the first case of Section
3.2
on
(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)
with
d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}=d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}+1
, we retrieve the fact that
\psi_{j}(\phi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k+2},\lambda)))=(\Delta_{j,2k+2},\lambda)
.

(b)

Let
(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1+j+2k}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)
.
Using the first case of Section
3.2
, we have that
\lambda
consists of the parts
\mu_{1}+1,\ldots,\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}-1}+1
, and the parts
\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+1},\ldots,\mu_{\ell(\mu)}
except
1+j+2k
. Moreover,
\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}-1}+1-(d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}-1)>\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}}-d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+1=2+j+2k>\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+1}-d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}
, so that
d_{j+2k+2}^{\lambda}=d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}-1
and
\lambda\in\mathcal{F}_{j+2k+2}
. Hence,

\psi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu))\in\{\Delta_{j,2k+2}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j+2k}\,.

We prove similarly to the previous case that
\phi_{j}(\psi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)))=(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)
.

∎

4.2.
Well-definedness of
\Phi_{j}

By Proposition
4.1
,
\phi_{j}
is injective, and this implies that a pair is not fixed by
\phi_{j}
if and only if its iterations are not fixed. Hence, it suffices to check that
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}_{1+j+2k}
is not fixed by
\phi_{j}
, and that we reach
\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j}
after a finite number of iterations of
\phi_{j}
. In this regard, we state the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2k}
. Then,
\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}=d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+j+2k
, and
\lambda_{1}\geq 1+j+2k
.

(1)

If
\lambda_{1}=1+j+2k
, then
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}=1
and
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)
is fixed by
\phi_{j}
. Inversely, all the pairs fixed by
\phi_{j}
have the form
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)
with
\lambda_{1}=1+j+2k
.

(2)

If
\lambda_{1}>1+j+2k
, then
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}\geq 2
and, by setting
u=\sharp\{i\geq d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}:\lambda_{i}=d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+j+2k\}
, we have that

\phi_{j}^{0}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)),\ldots,\phi_{j}^{u-1}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2k}\,,

and
\phi_{j}^{u}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j+2k}
.

We first prove the well-definedness of
\Phi_{j}
assuming that the above proposition is true. Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{1+j+2k}
. Since
1+j+2k
is not a part of
\lambda
,
\lambda_{1}\neq 1+j+2k
and by Proposition
4.2
,

(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)
is not fixed by
\Phi_{j}
. Hence, its iterations are not fixed by
\phi_{j}
. We can then use the second case of Proposition
4.2
and deduce the existence of non-negative integers
u_{l}
that counts the numbers of iterations of
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)
respectively in
\{\Delta_{j,2l}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2l}
. More precisely, we have for all
l\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
,

\phi_{j}^{k-l+u+\sum_{t=l+1}^{k}u_{t}}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))\in\{\Delta_{j,2l}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2l}\text{ for }0\leq u<u_{l}\text{ and }\phi_{j}^{k-l+\sum_{t=l}^{k}u_{t}}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))\in\{\Delta_{j,2l}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j+2l}\,.

Since these iterations are not fixed, by setting
n=|\lambda|+|\Delta_{j,2k}|
, we have by definition of
u_{l}
in Proposition
4.2
that

u_{l}+1\leq\frac{n}{2+j+2l}\leq\frac{n}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{l+1}
. Therefore, there is at most

\frac{n}{2}(1+\log(k+2))
iterations, hence finite. Moreover,

\Phi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))=\phi_{j}^{k+\sum_{t=0}^{k}u_{t}}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))\in\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j}

so that
\Phi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))
is well-defined.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Let
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2k}
.
Recall that
\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}=d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+j+2k
and set
\phi_{j}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))=(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)
.

(1)

As
\lambda_{1}\geq 1+j+2k
, the fact that
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}
is unique implies that
\lambda_{1}=1+j+2k
if and only if
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}=1
. The fact that
\phi_{j}(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)=(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)
is trivial, as
\mu
is obtained by deleting
\lambda_{1}
, adding
1+j+2k
, and not adding
1
to any other part. Inversely, only the pairs of
\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2k}
can be fixed by
\phi_{j}
, and when
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}\geq 2
,
\mu_{1}=\lambda_{1}+1>\lambda_{1}
so that
\phi_{j}(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)\neq(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)
. Hence, the only pairs fixed by
\phi_{j}
have the form
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda)
with
\lambda_{1}=1+j+2k
.

(2)

When
\lambda_{1}>1+j+2k
,
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}\geq 2
. Since
\mu_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1}>\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}\geq\mu_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}
, we then have that
\mu_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1}-(d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1)>j+2k\geq\mu_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}-{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}
. Hence,
\mu\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{j+2k}
if and only if
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}=d_{j+2k}^{\mu}
and
\mu_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}=d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+j+2k>1+j+2k
. This occurs only if
\mu_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}=\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+1}=d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+j+2k
.
Thus, by formally setting
(\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda^{(w)})=\phi_{j}^{w}((\Delta_{j,2k},\lambda))
for
w\in\{0,\ldots,u\}
and
\ell(\lambda)\geq t\geq d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+u-1
such that
\lambda_{t}\geq 1+j+2k>\lambda_{t+1}
, we have that

\begin{cases}\lambda_{i}^{(w)}=\lambda_{i}+w\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{ if }\ \ 1\leq i\leq d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1\,,\\
\lambda_{i}^{(w)}=\lambda_{i+w}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{ if }\ \ d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}\leq i\leq t-w\,,\\
\lambda_{i}^{(w)}=1+j+2k\ \ \ \text{ if }\ \ t-w+1\leq i\leq t\,,\\
\lambda_{i}^{(w)}=\lambda_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{ if }\ \ t+1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)\,,\end{cases}

as we recursively obtain that
d_{j+2k}^{\lambda^{(w)}}=d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}
and
\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}^{(w)}=\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+w}=d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+j+2k
for all
w\in\{0,\ldots,u-1\}
. Moreover,
\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}^{(u)}
is either
\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+u}
when
t\geq d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+u
, or
1+j+2k
when
t=d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}+u-1
. In all cases,
\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}}^{(u)}-d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}<j+2k<\lambda_{d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1}^{(u)}-(d_{j+2k}^{\lambda}-1)-u
, so that
\lambda^{(u)}\in\mathcal{F}_{j+2k}
.

∎

4.3.
Well-defined of
\Psi_{j}

As before, we only need to check that
(\Delta_{j,0},\lambda)\in\{\Delta_{j,0}\}\times\mathcal{F}_{j}
is not fixed by
\psi_{j}
, and that we reach
\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}_{j+2k+1}
after a finite number of iterations of
\psi_{j}
. The following propositions helps in that purpose.

Proposition 4.3
.

For
(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\mathcal{F}_{1+j+2k}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)
, we have
\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}}-d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}>1+j+2k>\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+1}-(d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}+1)
.

(1)

If
d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}=0
, then
\mu_{1}=1+j+2k
and
(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)
is fixed by
\psi_{j}
. Inversely, all the pairs fixed by
\psi_{j}
have the form
(\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)
with
\mu_{1}=1+j+2k
.

(2)

If
d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}\geq 1
, set
u=\mu_{d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}}-d_{1+j+2k}^{\mu}-(1+j+2k)
, and
v
the number occurrences of
1+j+2k
in
\mu
.

(a)

If
v>u
, then

\psi_{j}^{0}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)),\ldots,\psi_{j}^{u-1}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu))\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\mathcal{F}_{1+j+2k}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)\,,

and
\psi_{j}^{u}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu))\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1+j+2k}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)
.

(b)

If
v\leq u
, then

\psi_{j}^{0}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu)),\ldots,\psi_{j}^{v-1}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu))\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\left(\mathcal{F}_{1+j+2k}\cap\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{j+2k+1}\right)\,,

and
\psi_{j}^{v}((\Delta_{j,2k},\mu))\in\{\Delta_{j,2k}\}\times\mathcal{P}_{j+2k+1}
.

To prove the well-definedness of
\Psi_{j}
, we first observe that, for
\mu\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
,
\mu_{1}\neq j+1
, and by Proposition
4.3
,
(\Delta_{j,0},\mu)
is not fixed by
\psi_{j}
. Thus, as
\psi_{j}
is injective, its iterations are not fixed by
\psi_{j}
. Moreover, for
l
such that
|\Delta_{j,2l}|=l(1+2j+2l))>|\mu|
, these iterations do not reach
\{\Delta_{j,2l}\}\times\mathcal{P}
since
\psi_{j}
conserves the weight sum of the pair. Let then
k
be the largest
l
such that
\{\Delta_{j,2l}\}\times\mathcal{P}
is reached by the iterations. By Proposition
4.3
, for
0\leq l\leq k
,
w_{l}
the number of iterations of
(\Delta_{j,0},\mu)
by
\psi_{j}
in
\{\Delta_{j,2l}\}\times\mathcal{P}
is at most equal to one plus the number of occurrences of
1+j+2l
, hence at most equal to
\frac{|\mu|}{1+j+2l}
. We then have in total at most
\frac{|\mu|(2+\log(k+1))}{2}
iterations. Finally, at

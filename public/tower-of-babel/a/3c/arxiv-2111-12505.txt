Cross-Sections for t ​ t ¯ \text{t}\bar{\text{t}} H production with the Top Quark MS ¯ \overline{\text{MS}} Mass
Maria Aldaya Martin, Sven-Olaf Moch, Andrej Saibel

Abstract
We study the impact of the top quark mass renormalized in the MS ¯ \overline{\text{MS}} scheme on the t ​ t ¯ \text{t}\bar{\text{t}} H production cross-sections as an alternative to theory predictions with the conventionally used pole mass scheme. The differential cross-sections at next-to-leading order in perturbative QCD with stable top quarks show a moderate decrease in scale uncertainties for the MS ¯ \overline{\text{MS}} mass renormalization scheme compared to the on-shell one. The shape of the differential distributions is not affected much, the largest differences being observed in the invariant mass distributions of the t ​ t ¯ \text{t}\bar{\text{t}} and the t ​ t ¯ \text{t}\bar{\text{t}} H systems.

Introduction

A Higgs-like particle was observed by the ATLAS
Aad:2012tfa
and
CMS
Chatrchyan:2012ufa
collaborations at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) in the year 2012.
To confirm that this particle with spin 0 and mass
m_{\text{H}}=125.10\pm 0.14

GeV

PDG2020
is indeed the Higgs boson of the Standard Model (SM),
studies of all properties of the newly discovered particle need to be conducted.
To achieve this goal, all production and decay modes of the Higgs-like particle are investigated.
Especially the study of the Yukawa coupling is of great interest, since the observation of the Higgs-Yukawa coupling confirms the prediction of the SM, in which the masses of the fermions are generated by interactions with the Higgs field.
Conversely, significant deviations from the SM predicted cross-sections would be an indicator for new physics beyond the SM.

Since the top quark is by far the heaviest known elementary particle and the strength of the Yukawa coupling is proportional to the mass of the fermions, the searches for physics processes involving the production of top quark pairs with an associated Higgs boson (
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
) are most promising for the discovery of the coupling.
The observation of
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
production and, therefore, the confirmation of the existence of the top-Higgs Yukawa coupling was claimed by both ATLAS
bib:ObservationttHATLAS
and
CMS
bib:ObservationttHCMS
in the year 2018.
This achievement was made possible by a statistical combination of the results of the data analyses for the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
production with different channels of the Higgs boson decay, i.e., to di-bosons,
\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
,
\gamma\gamma
, and
\text{b}\bar{\text{b}}
-pairs.

The uncertainties originating from systematic effects in the combined measurements of both collaborations surpass the statistical uncertainties arising from the limited size of the recorded data samples already now.
Looking further into the future, the discovery of
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
leads to an era of precise measurements probing the coupling with larger data sets and improved analysis techniques.
In particular, the High Luminosity LHC (HL-LHC)
ApollinariG.:2017ojx
will allow for precise differential cross-section measurements of
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
production.
Systematic uncertainties of the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H

This motivates studies which aim at quantifying and reducing these uncertainties.

Currently available theory calculations for the

H

2
Theoretical framework

The on-shell mass of a heavy quark, defined through the pole of the propagator, is a well-defined concept in perturbative quantum field theory, but it has its disadvantages.
In the pole mass scheme, the particles are assumed to be free, asymptotic states, an assumption not well justified for quarks in nature due to confinement.
Furthermore, the pole mass exhibits poor convergence in the perturbative expansion, having an intrinsic uncertainty in the order of
\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}

Beneke:1994sw
;
Bigi:1994em
.

Short-distance mass, such as heavy quark masses renormalized in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme do not have such limitations and can, thus, be used for predictions of differential cross-sections to reduce uncertainties.
For
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
cross-sections, both total
Langenfeld:2009wd
and differential
Dowling:2013baa
;
Garzelli:2020fmd
, it has been shown that the
\overline{\text{MS}}
renormalization scheme for the top quark mass improves the apparent convergence of the perturbative expansion along with a reduced scale dependence. These improvements lead to smaller total theory uncertainties and, therefore, to predictions which are desirable for the usage in cross-section measurements and comparisons of the experimental results to theory.

The computation of differential cross-sections for
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
production
proceeds in analogy to the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
case, see e.g.,
Langenfeld:2009wd
.
Starting from an expansion in powers of the strong coupling
\alpha_{\text{s}}

up to NLO in QCD,
the (differential) cross-section with the on-shell mass
m_{\text{t}}^{\text{pole}}

can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma\left(m_{\text{t}}^{\text{pole}}\right)}{dX}=\left(\frac{\alpha_{\text{s}}}{\pi}\right)^{2}\,\frac{d\sigma^{(0)}\left(m_{\text{t}}^{\text{pole}}\right)}{dX}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{\text{s}}}{\pi}\right)^{3}\,\frac{d\sigma^{(1)}\left(m_{\text{t}}^{\text{pole}}\right)}{dX}+O\left(\alpha_{\text{s}}^{2}\right)\,,

(1)

where
\sigma^{(0)}
is the Born cross-section,
\sigma^{(1)}
the NLO contribution, and
X
the observable in which the differential cross-section is calculated.

The conversion of the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
production cross-sections in the pole mass renormalization scheme to the
\overline{\text{MS}}
mass one proceeds as follows.
The on-shell top quark mass
m_{\text{t}}^{\text{pole}}
is rewritten in terms of the
\overline{\text{MS}}
mass
m({\mu_{R}})
using standard formulae

\displaystyle m_{\text{t}}^{\text{pole}}=m({\mu_{R}})\left(1+\frac{\alpha_{\text{s}}}{\pi}d_{1}+O\left(\alpha_{\text{s}}^{2}\right)\right)\,,

(2)

where the perturbative expansion in
\alpha_{\text{s}}
on the right hand side is truncated at NLO for brevity.
Currently, the conversion in Eq. (
2
) is known to
four-loop order
Gray:1990yh
;
Melnikov:2000qh
;
Chetyrkin:1999qi
;
Marquard:2016dcn
. The NLO coefficient reads

\displaystyle d_{1}=\frac{4}{3}+\ln\left(\frac{\mu_{R}^{2}}{m({\mu_{R}})^{2}}\right)\,.

(3)

The differential cross-section in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme is then
obtained by inserting the relation between the pole and the
\overline{\text{MS}}
masses
in Eq. (
2
) and expanding in
\alpha_{\text{s}}
(see e.g.,
Dowling:2013baa
),

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma\left(m({\mu_{R}})\right)}{dX}=\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\right)^{2}\frac{d\sigma^{(0)}\left(m({\mu_{R}})\right)}{dX}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\right)^{3}\left\{\frac{d\sigma^{(1)}\left(m({\mu_{R}})\right)}{dX}+d_{1}m({\mu_{R}})\frac{d}{dm_{\text{t}}}\left.\left(\frac{d\sigma^{(0)}\left(m_{\text{t}}\right)}{dX}\right)\right|_{m_{\text{t}}=m({\mu_{R}})}\right\}+O\left(\alpha_{s}^{2}\right)\,,

(4)

where
\sigma^{(0)}
and
\sigma^{(1)}
, i.e., the first and the second term,
correspond to the ones in Eq. (
1
), only with
m({\mu_{R}})
replacing
m_{\text{t}}^{\text{pole}}
.
The mass derivative of the Born cross-section evaluated at
m_{\text{t}}=m({\mu_{R}})
, i.e., the third term,
has to be calculated separately in order to obtain the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
cross-section at NLO with the top quark mass in
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme.
For a given differential cross-section this can be done numerically.
To that end, we use
MG5_aMC@NLO

Alwall:2014hca
(version 2.6.5)
to obtain the Born level differential cross-sections varying
the top quark mass (and the Yukawa coupling accordingly)
in a range between
m_{\text{t}}=150

GeV
and
m_{\text{t}}=175

GeV
in steps of
\Delta m_{\text{t}}=0.5

GeV
.

The calculations in our study
Saibel:2021the
are performed using the
MMHT2014
PDF sets
Harland-Lang:2014zoa
at NLO with
\alpha_{\text{s}}(m_{\text{Z}})=0.118
.
The
\overline{\text{MS}}
mass of the top quark at the scale
m_{\text{t}}
is chosen to be
m_{\text{t}}(m_{\text{t}})=163.2

GeV
, while the corresponding pole mass is
m_{\text{t}}^{\text{pole}}=172.5

GeV
.
The mass of the Higgs boson is chosen to be
m_{\text{H}}=125.0

GeV
.
The running of
\alpha_{\text{s}}
and of the top quark mass
m({\mu_{R}})
are calculated with five light flavors with the help of the
RunDec program
Chetyrkin:2000yt
;
Schmidt:2012az
.
The nominal value for the
\mu_{R}
and the
\mu_{F}
scales in the calculations is chosen to be
\mu_{0}=2m_{\text{t}}+m_{\text{H}}
.
When the mass of the top quarks is varied for the calculation of the mass derivative, the scales are also adjusted.
The scale uncertainties are estimated through variations of
\mu_{R},\mu_{F}\in[0.5\mu_{0},\,2\mu_{0}]
and the value of the top quark
\overline{\text{MS}}
mass
m_{\text{t}}(m_{\text{t}})
is kept unchanged between the scale variations.

For the NLO cross-sections in the pole mass scheme, i.e.

in Eq. (

3
Results and Discussion

Here we present the results for
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
differential cross-sections
with the top quark
\overline{\text{MS}}
mass at NLO and compare them with calculations in the pole mass scheme with
m_{\text{t}}^{\text{pole}}=172.5

GeV
.
We study the behavior of the top quark and the Higgs boson separately, as well as those of systems of particles.
Therefore, the distributions we study are
the transverse momentum
p_{\text{T}}
and the rapidity
y
of the top quarks
and the Higgs boson, as well as the invariant mass of the top quark pair and the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
system.

The results are shown as the differential cross-sections, as well as
two ratio graphs to facilitate comparisons.
The first ratio compares the shapes of the differential cross-sections
in the two mass renormalization schemes.
For the second ratio, the scale uncertainties are normalized to the cross-section prediction in each bin for the two mass renormalization schemes.
Thus, the second ratio compares the relative scale uncertainties.
If the scale variations calculated in the interval

[0.5\mu_{0},\,2\mu_{0}]
point in the same direction in a given bin, then the larger deviation from the nominal cross-section
is taken as the uncertainty.
The values in each bin of all differential cross-sections
considered in this Section are listed in App.
A
.

4
Summary

The impact of the top quark
\overline{\text{MS}}
mass on differential distributions in
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
production is an interesting topic for future differential
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
analyses at high precision, in particular at the HL-LHC.
We have presented the first ever studies of the impact of the
top quark mass in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme on the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
production cross-sections.
Our work compares fixed-order NLO QCD predictions with stable top quarks in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
and the pole mass scheme for differential cross-sections as a function of
p_{\text{T}}
and
y
of the top quark and the Higgs boson, as well as the invariant mass of the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
and the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
systems.

The overall impact of the top quark mass in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme on the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
production is found to be small.
The behavior of the Higgs boson is barely affected by the
change of the quark mass renormalization scheme.
However, for the top quark differences in the shape of the
p_{\text{T}}
and
y
distributions are visible in comparisons between the
\overline{\text{MS}}
and the pole mass schemes.
Additionally, the scale uncertainties in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme are slightly reduced in the low-
p_{\text{T}}
region of the top quark
p_{\text{T}}
distribution.
The largest corrections due to the effects of the top quark
\overline{\text{MS}}
mass are observed in the invariant mass distributions of the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
and the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
systems.
Over a wide range of the distributions, a moderate decrease in scale uncertainties for the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme is seen.
However, also the expected shortcomings of the
\overline{\text{MS}}
mass at the threshold of the
M_{\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}\text{H}}
distribution become apparent.
In summary, it can be confirmed that no major systematic effects due to the choice of the quark mass renormalization scheme were neglected in the experimental analyses leading to the observation of the
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
production in the year 2018.

Overall, the numerical approach for the estimation of the Born-level mass derivative is advantageous since it enables also the calculation of other differential cross-sections within the same framework, e.g.
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
,
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}\text{Z}
,
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
,
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}\gamma
*, etc.,
without requiring any analytical expressions for the derivative of the Born cross-sections.
On the other hand, those calculations have to be performed for a range of values of the top quark mass at a high numerical precision and, therefore, the approach is computationally expensive.
For this reason, the approach can be improved by incorporating the analytical expressions for the mass derivative of the Born cross-sections.

The present studies focus on the NLO QCD radiative corrections. Further improvements of the cross-section predictions
due to the effect of resummed threshold logarithms, Coulomb corrections or the complete NNLO QCD corrections
will become important as the precision of differential measurements for
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
production increases.
The studies can also be improved by incorporating the electroweak corrections to
\text{t}\bar{\text{t}}
H
production and the
renormalization of the top quark mass in the future.
The shortcomings of the
\overline{\text{MS}}
mass at the production threshold can be overcome by considering a low-scale short-distance mass, such as the MSR mass.
We leave theses aspects for future studies.

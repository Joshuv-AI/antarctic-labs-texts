Moiré Fractional Chern Insulators II: First-principles Calculations and Continuum Models of Rhombohedral Graphene Superlattices
Jonah Herzog-Arbeitman, Yuzhi Wang, Jiaxuan Liu, Pok Man Tam, Ziyue Qi, Yujin Jia, Dmitri K. Efetov, Oskar Vafek, Nicolas Regnault, Hongming Weng, Quansheng Wu, B. Andrei Bernevig, Jiabin Yu

Abstract
The experimental discovery of fractional Chern insulators (FCIs) in rhombohedral pentalayer graphene twisted on hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) has preceded theoretical prediction. Supported by large-scale first principles relaxation calculations at the experimental twist angle of 0.77 ∘ 0.77^{\circ} , we obtain an accurate continuum model of n = 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 n=3,4,5,6,7 layer rhombohedral graphene-hBN moiré systems. Focusing on the pentalayer case, we analytically explain the robust | C | = 0 , 5 |C|=0,5 Chern numbers seen in the low-energy single-particle bands and their flattening with displacement field, making use of a minimal two-flavor continuum Hamiltonian derived from the full model. We then predict nonzero valley Chern numbers at the ν = − 4 , 0 \nu=-4,0 insulators observed in experiment. Our analysis makes clear the importance of displacement field and the moiré potential in producing localized ”heavy fermion” charge density in the top valence band, in addition to the nearly free conduction band. Lastly, we study doubly aligned devices as additional platforms for moiré FCIs with higher Chern number bands.

Introduction

Fractional Chern insulators (FCIs) have long been defined as topologically ordered states arising from partial filling of a Chern insulator
Neupert et al. 2011
;
Sheng et al. 2011
;
Regnault and Bernevig 2011

These FCI states have been shown to exhibit a fractional quantum anomalous Hall effect in transport experiments. Importantly, experiments show deviations from the typical FCI phase diagram caused by the moiré potential, which favors competing states at different fillings
Yu et al. 2023a
, and allows many different phases and phase transitions to be observed in the same device tuned by filling and displacement field. This offers an unprecedented opportunity to make and check theoretical predictions

;

Mao et al. 2023
. In a prior publication

II
First Principles Results for moiré Structures

Lu et al. 2023

). Doing degenerate perturbation theory, we see these null modes are connected at order
t_{1}^{n}
, so the low energy bands can be seen (App.
A.2
) to form a high-degree node
E_{\pm}(\mathbf{k})=\pm(v_{F}|k|)^{n}/t_{1}^{n-1}+\dots
. Their eigenstates are also solvable to order
O(|k|^{n+1})
. It is convenient to write them in the chiral basis
Koshino 2010
with the holomorphic/anti-holomorphic form (up to normalization)

\displaystyle\hbox{}[\psi_{A}]_{l\alpha}(k)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{-v_{F}k}{t_{1}}\Big)^{l}\delta_{\alpha,A},\quad[\psi_{B}]_{l\alpha}(\bar{k})=\Big(\frac{-v_{F}\bar{k}}{t_{1}}\Big)^{\bar{l}}\delta_{\alpha,B}

(9)

with
l=0,\dots,n-1
,
\bar{l}=n-1-l
, and
\alpha=A,B
to index the sublattice. These states are sublattice polarized, exchanged under the combined space-time inversion symmetry
D[\mathcal{I}\mathcal{T}]=\delta_{l+l^{\prime},n-1}[\sigma_{1}]_{\alpha\beta}K
(whose center is the middle of the third layer, i.e.,
l=2
for
n=5
), and decay exponentially from one side to the other with a momentum-dependent length scale set by
v_{F}|k|/t_{1}
. This perturbation theory is valid for
v_{F}|k|/t_{1}\ll 1
, and we can gauge its validity by picking
|k|=|\mathbf{q}_{1}|
which is the largest momentum in the first moiré BZ. We find that
v_{F}|\mathbf{q}_{1}|/t_{1}\leq 0.65
for
\theta\leq 1^{\circ}
, demonstrating that the states in Eq. (
9
) are valid. This basis provides a powerful tool for exposing the low-energy features of the model when the moiré potential and displacement field are added.

In particular, the observation of FCIs and correlated Chern insulators occurs at large displacement field
D

Lu et al. 2023
described by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hbox{}[H_{D}]_{l\alpha,l^{\prime}\beta}=V(l-\frac{n-1}{2})\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{\alpha\beta}

(10)

where
V\propto-|e|d_{0}D
is the inter-layer potential difference which is proportional to the displacement field, electric charge
-|e|
, and the inter-layer distance
d_{0}\sim 3.33\text{\AA}
. The proportionality constant depends on the effective screening which is not directly computed in this work, although attempts have been made to estimate it from experiment
Yang et al. 2022
, resulting in an effective screening constant
\epsilon_{r}\sim 5.5
in trilayer devices. For this reason, we use
V
in this work.

Collecting the chiral states into the column matrix
\Psi(\mathbf{k})=[\psi_{A}(k),\psi_{B}(\bar{k})]/\sqrt{\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{k})}
where
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{k})=|\psi_{A}(k)|^{2}=|\psi_{B}(\bar{k})|^{2}
is the normalization, we find (for now setting
v_{3}=v_{4}=t_{2}=V_{ISP}=0
)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\dagger}(\mathbf{k})(H_{\text{K}}+H_{D})\Psi(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{F}^{n}}{t_{1}^{n-1}}\begin{pmatrix}0&\bar{k}^{n}\\
k^{n}&0\end{pmatrix}

(11)

\displaystyle+V\Big(\frac{1-n}{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{2m\leq n}\big(v_{F}|k|/t_{1}\big)^{2m}\Big)\sigma_{3}

which is a
2\times 2
matrix in Pauli form (see App.
A.4
for a derivation.) Two essential features are revealed. Firstly, at
V=0
, the Hamiltonian describes the well-known high degree node with a
n\pi
Berry curvature monopole. Turning on
V
splits the node, breaking the
n\pi
monopole and distributing the Berry curvature
F(\mathbf{k})
to the valence bands (opposite to the conduction bands) according to

\displaystyle F(|k|)=-\frac{n^{2}(v_{F}^{n}/t_{1}^{n-1})^{2}}{2}\frac{|k|^{2(n-1)}\Delta}{\big((v_{F}^{n}/t_{1}^{n-1})^{2}|k|^{2n}+\Delta^{2}\big)^{3/2}}

(12)

where
\Delta=V\frac{n-1}{2}
is the gap at
k=0
, the graphene
K
point. Integrating
F(|k|)
over all
\mathbf{k}
gives
-n\pi\text{ sign}(V)
, corresponding to a half-quantized Chern number
C=-\text{ sign}(V)n/2
for the valence bands. Introducing a moiré potential will open gaps at the boundary of the first moiré BZ, isolating the highest valence band and the lowest conduction band (see Fig.
7
for a schematic of gap openings driven by displacement field and the moiré potential).

For
n=5
and large
V>0
, we will show by symmetry and confirm numerically in Sec.
IV.2
that the Chern number of the conduction band is
C=5
, while the valence band is trivial with
C=0
, and has the symmetry representation of an atomic limit. This means the remaining conduction and valence bands each carry a half-quantized
C=-5/2
, which we check numerically. The
K^{\prime}
valley is related by time-reversal has opposite Berry curvature, so the total Chern number at charge neutrality is zero. However, we predict a nonzero valley Chern number of

\displaystyle C_{V}=\sum_{s=\uparrow,\downarrow}\frac{C_{\text{K},s}-C_{\text{K}^{\prime},s}}{2}=-5,

(13)

including spin, at the charge neutrality point for large
V>0
. The valley Chern number may be measured in multi-terminal transport experiments
Wang et al. 2022
, or throughout gap closings in the Hofstadter spectrum
Burg et al. 2020
;
Herzog-Arbeitman et al. 2020
which is accessible up to one flux due to the large moiré unit cell
Das et al. 2022
;
Herzog-Arbeitman et al. 2022a
. Since the highest valence band band below charge neutrality also has
C=0
, we predict a valley Hall effect with
C_{V}=5
at
\nu=-4
(
i.e.
, the Fermi energy in between the top and second top valence band in each valley/spin) as well in the non-interacting limit.

The second important effect of
V
is to flatten the bands. The full
\mathbf{k}

\sigma_{3}

) is crucial for explaining this effect since the constant term

. In App.

(14)

Using the experimental twist angle, we estimate that

meV results in the optimally flattened band, corresponding to a top-to-bottom potential difference of

This completes our discussion of R
n
G. We now consider the addition of a moiré potential.

III.2
Moiré Coupling

The form of the moiré coupling can be derived from the Bistrizter-MacDonald two-center approximation
Moon and Koshino 2014
by keeping only the lowest harmonic terms.
We note that the lowest-harmonic approximation for moiré coupling between R
n
G and hBN is not necessarily quantitatively accurate here due to the large gap of hBN, which makes its low-energy dispersion flattish.
Nevertheless, we can still use the lowest-harmonic moiré coupling between R
n
G and hBN to derive the form of the effective moiré potential after integrating out hBN, since it is reasonable to keep only the lowest-harmonic terms in the effective moiré potential since it only couples graphene degrees of freedom. (See App.
A
for details.)
Therefore, in this part, we will still use the lowest harmonic approximation

Under the lowest-harmonic approximation, the moiré coupling between the lowest graphene and hBN reads

\displaystyle T(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}e^{i\mathbf{q}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{r}}T_{j},\quad T_{j+1}=w\begin{pmatrix}1&e^{i\frac{2\pi j}{3}}\\
e^{-i\frac{2\pi j}{3}}&1\end{pmatrix}

(15)

where the single moiré coupling parameter
w
results from the assumption of equal hoppings between the carbon and hBN orbitals at all positions of the moiré lattice.
The assumption of equal hoppings is reasonable due to the exponentially accurate layer and sublattice polarization of the low energy states in Eq. (
9
), as we now show.

The most general
C_{3}
-allowed moiré coupling matrix takes the form

\displaystyle T_{j}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}t_{B,C_{A}}&e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{3}j}t_{B,C_{B}}\\
e^{i\frac{2\pi}{3}j}t_{N,C_{A}}&t_{N,C_{B}}\end{pmatrix}

(16)

where
C_{\alpha}
are the carbon orbitals on the bottom layer and
B,N
are the hBN orbitals beneath. However, we check that — because the low-energy states have exponentially small weight on the
C_{B}
atoms on the bottom layer — one can set
t_{B,C_{B}}=t_{N,C_{B}}=0
with negligible change to the band structure, as shown in Fig.
8
. This is because the second column of
T_{j}
has very small overlap on the low-energy bands, so it can be set to zero without incurring error.

In both plots we have shifted the chemical potential to the minimum of the conduction band and boosted both valleys to the moiré
\Gamma_{M}
point.

With
t_{B,C_{B}}=t_{N,C_{B}}=0
, we can now integrate out the hBN and get the effective coupling via the second order perturbation theory.
As a result, we get

\displaystyle V_{eff,\xi}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=-T^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}^{\xi}H_{BN}\sigma_{1}^{\xi}}T(\mathbf{r})

(17)

\displaystyle=\big(V_{0}+2V_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\cos(\mathbf{g}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{r}+\psi_{\xi})\big)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

which couples only to the active
A
sublattice as only
A
sublattice is active in the bottom layer, and the parameters are given by (with
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}
, see App.
A.3
)

\displaystyle V_{0}

\displaystyle=-3\begin{pmatrix}|t_{B,C_{A}}|^{2}&|t_{N,C_{A}}|^{2}\end{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}^{\xi}\begin{pmatrix}V_{B}^{-1}\\
V_{N}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

(18)

\displaystyle V_{1}e^{i\psi}

\displaystyle=-\begin{pmatrix}|t_{B,C_{A}}|^{2}&\omega^{*}|t_{N,C_{A}}|^{2}\end{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}^{\xi}\begin{pmatrix}V_{B}^{-1}\\
V_{N}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\ .

Therefore, although there are two independent parameters
t_{B,C_{A}}
and
t_{N,C_{A}}
in Eq. (
16
), they only contribute to one complex parameter in the non-uniform part of the moiré potential after integrating out hBN.
The form of the potential is similar to the one proposed in Ref.
Wu et al. 2019b
for twisted transitional metal dichalcogenides. We have found it convenient to define
\mathbf{g}_{j}=R(\frac{2\pi}{3}(j-1))(\mathbf{q}_{2}-\mathbf{q}_{3})
for
j=1,2,3
related by
C_{3}
symmetry. This simplified model agrees with Eq. (
15
) when the latter is also restricted to the active
A
sublattice only, as we have shown numerically holds to good approximation in Fig.
8
. Thus, Eq. (
17
) shows that although the most general form of the potential contains many more parameters than the equal amplitude case of Eq. (
15
), Eq. (
15
) is sufficient to fit the data. Thus, we arrive at the effective hBN potential

\displaystyle V_{\xi}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=-T^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})\sigma_{1}^{\xi}H_{BN}^{-1}\sigma_{1}^{\xi}T(\mathbf{r})

(19)

\displaystyle=V_{0}+\left[V_{1}e^{i\psi_{\xi}}\sum_{j=1}^{3}e^{i\mathbf{g}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega^{-j}\\
\omega^{j+1}&\omega\end{pmatrix}+h.c.\right]

which couples only to the bottom graphene layer.

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we studied the single-particle band structure and topology of R
n
G/hBN and hBN/R
n
G/hBN for
n=3,\dots,7

\sim 10

We then adapted the continuum model proposed in Ref.
Jung et al. 2014

This model has size of
2n\times 2n
at each position, but we showed that a minimal
2\times 2

Using this model to study the experimentally relevant R5G/hBN, we find the Chern numbers of the lowest conduction and highest valence bands are 0 or 5, and we analytically explain them and their robustness against parameter tuning. We found that for R5G/hBN, a large displacement field pointing away from hBN leads to nearly free conduction electrons, while the lowest valence band forms an isolated Wannierizable band.
For a large displacement field pointing toward hBN, the conduction bands of R5G/hBN are topologically trivial, but nevertheless show nontrivial quantum geometry.

p_{z}
carbon orbitals on the two sublattice sites:

\displaystyle h_{1}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=-t_{0}\begin{pmatrix}0&\sum_{j}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}_{j}}\\
\sum_{j}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}_{j}}&0\end{pmatrix}

(33)

where
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1}=(0,a_{G}/\sqrt{3}),\boldsymbol{\delta}_{2}=C_{3}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\delta}_{3}=C_{3}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{2}
and
a_{G}=0.246
nm is the graphene lattice constant. The Dirac points are located at

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}=2\pi\left(\frac{2}{3a_{G}},0\right),\quad\mathbf{K}^{\prime}=-\mathbf{K}\ ,

(34)

which are related by time-reversal. We will focus on the low-energy states near the
\mathbf{K}
point, which we refer to as the
K
valley. Expanding the Hamiltonian and defining
v_{F}=\frac{3}{2}t_{0}a_{G}
, we find

\displaystyle h_{1}(\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=v_{F}\mathbf{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}+\dots\ .

(35)

We now consider R
n
G where each layer is shifted by
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1}
relative to the one below, resulting in a
2n\times 2n
Hamiltonian whose basis is layer
\otimes

sublattice. Expanding in the
K
-valley, this model takes the form

\displaystyle H_{n}(\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}v_{F}\mathbf{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}&t^{\dagger}(\mathbf{k})&\\
t(\mathbf{k})&\ddots&t^{\dagger}(\mathbf{k})\\
&t(\mathbf{k})&v_{F}\mathbf{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\\
\end{pmatrix}+H_{ISP}+H_{t_{2}},\qquad t(\mathbf{k})=\begin{pmatrix}-v_{4}k&t_{1}\\
-v_{3}\bar{k}&-v_{4}k\end{pmatrix},\quad k,\bar{k}=k_{x}\pm ik_{y}

(36)

where
t(\mathbf{k})
is the inter-layer coupling matrix. The inter-layer AB hopping is
t_{1}
, and the next-nearest inter-layer hoppings yield the effective velocity terms
v_{3}
(inter-layer AB coupling) and
v_{4}
(inter-layer AA/BB coupling), see Fig.
6
in the main text. The term
H_{ISP}
, which reads

[H_{ISP}]_{ll^{\prime}}=V_{ISP}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}|l-\frac{n+1}{2}|\ ,

(37)

with
l=1,\dots,n
, describes the differences in the local chemical environment on the internal versus external graphene layers due to an effective inversion-symmetric potential. The value
V_{ISP}/c=5\text{meV}/\text{\AA}
is fit from the DFT on pristine pentalayer graphene and reflects the higher chemical potential of the outer layers, where
c
is the interlayer distance between graphene layers.
Finally,

[H_{t_{2}}]_{ll^{\prime}}=\delta_{l,2+l^{\prime}}t_{2}\frac{\sigma_{1}-i\sigma_{2}}{2}

(38)

is a coupling between
A
and
B
carbon orbitals 2 layers apart. Although this coupling is small (
t_{2}=-7
meV) it is important to include it in the
n=3
case, where
t_{2}
directly couples the zero energy states at
\mathbf{k}=0
(see Fig.
25
a), and opens up a gap there. For consistency, we include it for all number of layers.
We now derive the values of these couplings.

To do so, we employ a generalized Slater-Koster (SK) approach which parameterizes the hoppings between any two orbitals by their SO(3) character (spherical harmonic) and the distance between them
\mathbf{r}
(the so-called two-center approximation). We use the following form of the SK hopping between
p_{z}
orbitals with parameters fit to match ab-initio:

\displaystyle t_{SK}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=V_{pp\pi}(1-\frac{z^{2}}{r^{2}})e^{q_{\pi}(1-r/a_{\pi})}/(1+e^{(r-r_{c})/l_{c}})+V_{pp\sigma}\frac{z^{2}}{r^{2}}e^{q_{\sigma}(1-r/a_{\sigma})}/(1+e^{(r-r_{c})/l_{c}}),\quad r=|\mathbf{r}|,\ z=\hat{z}\cdot\mathbf{r}

(39)

and the parameters are

\displaystyle V_{pp\pi}

\displaystyle=-2810\text{meV},\ V_{pp\sigma}=480\text{meV},\ l_{0}=3.364\text{\AA},\ q_{\pi}=3.1451,\ a_{\pi}=1.418\text{\AA},

(40)

\displaystyle q_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=7.428,\ a_{\sigma}=3.349\text{\AA},\ r_{c}=6.14\text{\AA},l_{c}=0.265\text{\AA}\ .

This parameterization is fit to the DFT calculation of the pristine R
n
G for
n=3,4,5,6,7
. The resulting graphene tight-binding model parameters can be computed by performing the SK sums

\displaystyle h_{l\alpha,l^{\prime}\beta}(\mathbf{p})=\sum_{\mathbf{R}}t(\mathbf{r}_{l,\alpha}-\mathbf{r}_{l^{\prime},\beta}-\mathbf{R})e^{-i(\mathbf{r}_{l,\alpha}-\mathbf{r}_{l^{\prime},\beta}-\mathbf{R})\cdot\mathbf{p}}

(41)

which converges exponentially in
\mathbf{R}
.
We note that
v_{3}=v_{4}
in the SK two-center approximation since the nearest-neighbor distance between the AB and AA/BB inter-layer orbitals is the same. Throughout this work, we keep
v_{3}=v_{4}
since we find that allowing them to differ does not noticeably improve the fits.

This completes our derivation of the microscopic rhombohedral graphene Hamiltonian. In the next section, we study its symmetries and low-energy spectrum.

A.2
Band Flattening with Displacement Field in Rhombohedral Graphene

The rhombohedral band structure is tunable in experiment by displacement field, creating an inter-layer potential
V
. In this subsection, we study the behavior of the R
n
G bands in
V
to understand the flattening of the low energy spectrum.

In order to first understand the essential physics, we set
v_{3}=v_{4}=0
and
t_{2}=0
(and we will restore them for a full analysis in the main text, as well as in App.
A.4
), in which case the Hamiltonian is fully isotropic and the spectrum is a function of
|\mathbf{k}|
only. Explicitly, the model in this limit, which is called
h_{n}(\mathbf{k})
, reads

\displaystyle\hbox{}[h_{n}(\mathbf{k})]_{ij}=v_{F}\delta_{ij}\mathbf{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}+t_{1}\delta_{i,j+1}\sigma^{+}+t_{1}\delta_{i,j-1}\sigma^{-}

(42)

which has the chiral symmetry
\Sigma h_{n}(\mathbf{k})\Sigma^{\dagger}=-h_{n}(\mathbf{k})
with
\Sigma=\mathds{1}\otimes\sigma_{3}
where
\mathds{1}
is the identity on the
n
layers. The other important symmetry obtained by this model is
SO(2)
rotation, which takes the form

\displaystyle h_{n}(R_{\theta}\mathbf{k})=D_{\theta}h_{n}(\mathbf{k})D^{\dagger}_{\theta},\qquad[D_{\theta}]_{ll^{\prime}}=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}e^{i\theta(l-1-\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor)}e^{-i\theta\sigma_{3}/2}

(43)

corresponding to the angular momenta
-n/2,-n/2+1,-n/2+1,-n/2+2,\dots,n/2
. Of course, the realistic model with
v_{3}\neq 0
only possesses
C_{3}
symmetry, which we obtain from
D[C_{3}]=-D_{\frac{2\pi}{3}}
(with the
-1
phase determined by requiring
D[C_{3}]^{3}=\mathds{1}
). There is also spacetime-inversion symmetry
D_{ll^{\prime}}[\mathcal{I}\mathcal{T}]=\delta_{l,-l^{\prime}}\sigma_{1}\mathcal{K}
which is intra-valley because inversion and time-reversal both flip the valley. This symmetry is broken by the displacement field. While
C_{2z}\mathcal{T}
is not broken by the displacement field, it is broken by stacking structure of the R
n
G for
n>1
.

We start in the
v_{3}=v_{4}=0
and
t_{2}=0
limit where we can expose some simple analytical results. The
SO(2)
symmetry requires that the spectrum
E_{n}(\mathbf{k})
depends only on
|\mathbf{k}|
. Then we can expand the characteristic polynomial of
h_{n}(\mathbf{k})
in
|\mathbf{k}|
to find that

\displaystyle E_{n}(\mathbf{k})=\pm\frac{(v_{F}|\mathbf{k}|)^{n}}{t_{1}^{n-1}},\quad\pm t_{1}+\dots{}

(44)

where the
\pm t_{1}
eigenvalues are
(n-1)
-fold degenerate and correspond to bonding/anti-bonding inter-layer dimers hybridized by
t_{1}
. By direct substitution, one can verify that the eigenspace of the
O(|k|^{n})
eigenvalues is spanned by the holomorphic states (up to normalization):

\displaystyle\psi_{A}(k)

\displaystyle=\{1,0,(-v_{F}k/t_{1}),0,(-v_{F}k/t_{1})^{2},0,\dots\}+O(k^{n})

(45)

\displaystyle\psi_{B}(\bar{k})

\displaystyle=\{0,(-v_{F}\bar{k}/t_{1})^{n-1},0,(-v_{F}\bar{k}/t_{1})^{n-2},\dots,0,1\}+O(\bar{k}^{n})

or in components
[\psi_{A}(k)]_{A_{l}}=(-v_{F}k/t_{1})^{l-1}
and
[\psi_{B}(\bar{k})]_{B_{l}}=(-v_{F}\bar{k}/t_{1})^{n-l}
. Here
k=k_{x}+ik_{y}=|k|e^{i\phi},\bar{k}=k_{x}-ik_{y}=|k|e^{-i\phi}
are the usual holomorphic coordinates, and
\psi_{A,B}
are sublattice polarized since they diagonalize the chiral operator
\Sigma
, which is sublattice diagonal. Given that
v_{F}|k|/t_{1}<1
,
\psi_{A}(k)
has its maximum weight on the top layer and
\psi_{B}(\bar{k})
on the bottom, and they exponentially decay away from the top and bottom layers respectively. Indeed,
v_{F}|\mathbf{q}_{1}(\theta)|/t_{1}\sim 0.5
at
\theta=0.767^{\circ}
(
\mathbf{q}_{1}(\theta)
is the relevant BZ scale at the experimental moiré angle) so that the perturbation theory will be qualitatively valid across the first moiré BZ. We gather the chiral states (which we note are not
eigenstates
) into the column vector
\Psi(\mathbf{k})=[\psi_{A}(k),\psi_{B}(\bar{k})]/|\psi_{A}(k)|
(where
|\psi_{A}(k)|=|\psi_{B}(k)|
is the normalization). From Eq.
42
, it can readily be verified that

\displaystyle\Psi^{\dagger}(\mathbf{k})h_{n}(\mathbf{k})\Psi(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{F}^{n}}{t_{1}^{n-1}}\begin{pmatrix}0&\bar{k}^{n}\\
k^{n}&0\end{pmatrix}+O(|k|^{n+1})

(46)

which directly shows the
\pm|k|^{n}
dispersion from coupling the chiral modes. Now we add a displacement field
[H_{V}]_{ll^{\prime}}=(l-(n+1)/2)V\delta_{ll^{\prime}}
(
l=1,2,...,n
) with inter-layer potential difference
V
. We find

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{k})\big(h_{n}(\mathbf{k})+H_{V}\big)\mathcal{U}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{F}^{n}}{t_{1}^{n-1}}\begin{pmatrix}0&\bar{k}^{n}\\
k^{n}&0\end{pmatrix}+V\Big[\frac{1-n}{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{2m\leq n}\big(\frac{v_{F}|k|}{t_{1}}\big)^{2m}\Big]\sigma_{3}

(47)

The closed form expression for the projection of the displacement field term can be found in Eq. (
80
). This Hamiltonian is in Pauli form and can be immediately diagonalized to yield (setting
n=5
)

\displaystyle E_{\pm}(k)=\pm\sqrt{|v_{F}k|^{10}/t_{1}^{8}+V^{2}(2-v_{F}^{2}|k|^{2}/t_{1}^{2}-v_{F}^{4}|k|^{4}/t_{1}^{4})^{2}}

(48)

which compares very well with the numerically diagonalized energies shown in Fig.
17
. The most important feature of Eq. (
48
) is its non-monotonicity appearing from the
-v_{F}^{2}|k|^{2}/t_{1}^{2}-v_{F}^{4}|k|^{4}/t_{1}^{4}
terms. Hence at small
\mathbf{k}
, the energies will initially decrease, while at large
\mathbf{k}
they must approach infinity. Thus we can define a flatness condition

\displaystyle E_{+}(\mathbf{k}=0)=2V=E_{+}(\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{q}_{1}(\theta))

(49)

set by the scale of the moiré momentum. Here we used that at
\mathbf{k}=0
, the full
2n\times 2n
Hamiltonian can be diagonalized to yield
E(\mathbf{0})=2V
(assuming
t_{2}=0
).

We can now obtain an estimate for the critical
V
that satisfies the flatness condition in Eq. (
49
).
Using Eq. (
48
) to evaluate
E(\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{q}_{1}(\theta))
is straightforward, but it results in a high order polynomial equation to solve for the critical
V
. To get an analytical solution, we keep only the lowest
O(k^{2})
terms to capture the non-monotonicity and highest
O(k^{10})
terms to capture the large
\mathbf{k}
behavior. This approximation is validated in Fig.
17
. Then we find

\displaystyle E_{+}(\mathbf{k})\sim\sqrt{(2V)^{2}+|v_{F}k|^{10}/t_{1}^{8}-(2V)^{2}|v_{F}k|^{2}/t_{1}^{2}}

(50)

leading to the flat-band condition being

\displaystyle v_{F}^{2}|\mathbf{q}_{1}(\theta)|^{2}=t_{1}\sqrt{2Vt_{1}}\ .

(51)

While
v_{3},v_{4},t_{2},V_{ISP}
will modify this result (see App.
A.4
for their effect), it serves to identify a maximally flattened region tuned by
D
field, at least at the single-particle level.

Figure 17:
Dispersion
\pm E(\mathbf{k})
of the
10\times 10
pentalayer Hamiltonian is shown in black, in comparison with the analytical energies of the projected model of Eq. (
47
) (blue) at
V=10
meV. The red curve is the asymptotic approximation
\sqrt{(2V)^{2}+|v_{F}k|^{10}/t_{1}^{8}-4V^{2}|v_{F}k|^{2}/t_{1}^{2}}
for
\theta=0.767^{\circ}
.

A.3
Moiré Hamiltonian of R
n
G/hBN and hBN/R
n
G/hBN

In this section, we derive the moiré Hamiltonian of the superlattices formed by R
n
G and hBN. This Hamiltonian has three parts: the intra-layer Hamiltonians for R
n
G and hBN, and the moiré coupling between the two as caused by their lattice mismatch and relative twist. We will derive the form of the moiré coupling from one graphene layer to the hBN, and then build the full Hamiltonian for the variety of possible configurations and encapsulations shown in Fig.
20
.

In the experiment
Lu et al. 2023
, the R
n
G is encapsulated by two hBNs; however, only one of them is twisted at a small angle and thus nearly aligned, while the other does not contribute to the electronic structure of the system. To model this case, we consider the configuration where R
n
G is on top of one nearly-aligned hBN (R
n
Gg/hBN) without any hBN on the other side.
We will also consider the case where R
n
G is encapsulated by two nearly-aligned hBN that generate the same moiré pattern (hBN/R
n
G/hBN).

The
\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{p}
Hamiltonian of R
n
G is discussed in Eq. (
36
). We approximate the hBN Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle H_{BN,\xi}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}^{\xi}\begin{pmatrix}V_{B}&\\
&V_{N}\end{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}^{\xi},\qquad V_{B}=3352\text{meV},\quad V_{N}=-1388\text{meV}

(52)

for the stacking configuration
\xi=0,1
(see Main Text) where carbon-A,B is nearly vertically aligned with B,N or N,B respectively in the AA region. (See Fig.
2
in Main Text.) Here
V_{B}
and
V_{N}
are the chemical potentials for boron and nitrogen, respectively. Here we have neglected the
\mathbf{k}
-dependence of the hBN altogether, which is acceptable because
V_{B},V_{N}\sim 1000
meV, and corrections from hBN dispersion will not affect the low energy graphene bands, which are near chemical potential
0
.

The R
n
G/hBN devices have the following Hamiltonians in the
K
valley:

\displaystyle H_{\text{K},\xi=0}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}H_{\text{K}}(-i\boldsymbol{\nabla})&\tilde{T}_{b}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})\\
\tilde{T}_{b}(\mathbf{r})&H_{BN}\\
\end{pmatrix},\qquad H_{\text{K},\xi=1}(\mathbf{r})=\begin{pmatrix}H_{\text{K}}(-i\boldsymbol{\nabla})&\tilde{T}_{b}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})\\
\tilde{T}_{b}(\mathbf{r})&\sigma_{1}H_{BN}\sigma_{1}\\
\end{pmatrix}

(53)

describing the two possible stackings of the bottom hBN layer on the graphene. They are exchanged by a
C_{2}
rotation of the hBN, but the models are not symmetry-related because R
n
G is not
C_{2}
-symmetry (it is inversion symmetric, which exchanges the top and bottom layers). The moiré coupling
\tilde{T}(\mathbf{r})
acts only on the bottom layer, meaning

\displaystyle\hbox{}[\tilde{T}_{b}(\mathbf{r})]_{l}=\delta_{l,0}T_{b}(\mathbf{r})

(54)

where
l=0,\dots,n-1
indexes the layers of R
n
G where
l=0
is the layer that is closest to hBN.
T_{b}(\mathbf{r})
is a
2\times 2
matrix as shown in Eq. (
72
).

Secondly, we discuss the hBN/R
n
G/hBN models

\displaystyle H_{\text{K},\xi_{b},\xi_{t}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{1}^{\xi_{t}}H_{BN}\sigma_{1}^{\xi_{t}}&\tilde{T}_{t}(\mathbf{r})&\\
\tilde{T}_{t}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})&H_{\text{K}}(-i\boldsymbol{\nabla})&\tilde{T}_{b}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})\\
&\tilde{T}_{b}(\mathbf{r})&\sigma_{1}^{\xi_{b}}H_{BN}\sigma_{1}^{\xi_{b}}\\
\end{pmatrix},\qquad(\xi_{t},\xi_{b})=(1,1),(1,0)

(55)

which differ in the relative orientation of the N and B stackings on top and bottom.
H_{\text{K},\xi_{b}=1,\xi_{t}=1}
is strongly inversion asymmetric since the low-energy modes couple to different hBN orbitals on opposite sides, recalling that the bottom layer A-sublattice and top layer B-sublattice are the dominant orbitals in the chiral basis (see Eq. (
45
)). In contrast,
H_{\text{K},\xi_{b}=1,\xi_{t}=0}
is exactly inversion symmetric (nearly symmetric if the inversion center of two hBN deviates slightly from that of the R
n
G), since the bottom layer A-sublattice and top layer B-sublattice are aligned with the same atom. The moiré couplings
[\tilde{T}_{t}(\mathbf{r})]_{l}=\delta_{l,n-1}T_{t}(\mathbf{r}),\tilde{T}_{b}(\mathbf{r})=\delta_{l,0}T_{b}(\mathbf{r})
again connect hBN to the nearest graphene layer only.

We will now derive the inter-layer moiré coupling
T_{t,b}(\mathbf{r})
using the Bistritzer-MacDonald (BM) two-center approximation
Bistritzer and MacDonald 2011
following the appendices of Ref.
Song et al. 2019
. We consider the coupling between graphene layer
l
with orbitals

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l},\qquad\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l}=\alpha\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1}+l(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1}+d_{0}\hat{z})

(56)

where
\mathbf{R}
indexes the graphene unit cells,
\alpha=0,1
corresponds to the positions of the carbon
A,B
sublattices (see Fig.
6
a of the Main Text),
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1}
is defined under Eq. (
33
), and
l=0,\dots,n-1
labels the rhomobohedrally stacked layers which are spaced
d_{0}\sim 3.36\text{\AA}
apart in the rigid structure. The hBN layers have orbitals at

\displaystyle M(\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{r}_{\beta,l^{\prime}})

(57)

where
M=1+i\theta\sigma_{2}+\mathcal{E}+\dots
,
l^{\prime}
corresponds to the top and bottom encapsulated layers, and
\beta
here labels the B and N orbitals in the top/bottom layers. The twist angle
\theta
can be tuned in device construction and, in this work, we take
\mathcal{E}=\epsilon\sigma_{0}
(
\epsilon>0
) to describe the enlarged lattice constant of hBN
a_{hBN}=(1+\epsilon)a_{G}
.

The inter-layer Hamiltonian hopping hBN onto graphene is given by

\displaystyle H^{inter}_{\alpha l,\beta l^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\mathbf{R},\mathbf{R}^{\prime}}e^{-i(\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l})\cdot\mathbf{p}+i(M(\mathbf{R}^{\prime}+\mathbf{r}_{\beta,l^{\prime}}))\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\braket{\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l}|H|M(\mathbf{R}^{\prime}+\mathbf{r}_{\beta,l^{\prime}})}

(58)

where
H
is the underlying microscopic Hamiltonian. We emphasize that
\mathbf{R},\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
are lattice vectors in the unrotated graphene layer. To proceed, we assume that the matrix element of the Hamiltonian is only dependent on the distance between orbitals (the “two-center” approximation) leading to

\displaystyle\braket{\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l}|H|M(\mathbf{R}^{\prime}+\mathbf{r}_{\beta,l^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in BZ}\sum_{\mathbf{G}}t_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}e^{i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\big(\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l}-(M(\mathbf{R}^{\prime}+\mathbf{r}_{\beta,l^{\prime}}))\big)}

(59)

and
t_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}
is the momentum-space matrix element for the hopping between the orbitals labeled by
\alpha,\beta
,
\mathcal{N}
is the number of graphene unit cells with area
\Omega
. Here we have made the SK approximation

\displaystyle t_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}r\,e^{-i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\cdot\mathbf{r}}t_{SK}(\mathbf{r})

(60)

which treats the boron-carbon and nitrogen-carbon hoppings identically, since both are
p_{z}
-
p_{z}
orbital overlaps. Although this is an approximation, we argue in the Main Text that the resulting form of the Hamiltonian is general enough to fit the band structure obtained from the large-scale numerical calculations. (See App.
E
.) Plugging this expression into the inter-layer Hamiltonian and using
M\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{k}=(M\mathbf{r})^{T}\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{r}\cdot M^{T}\mathbf{k}

gives

\displaystyle H^{inter}_{\alpha l,\beta l^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in BZ}\sum_{\mathbf{G}}\ t_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}e^{i\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l}\cdot(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{p})-i\mathbf{r}_{\beta,l^{\prime}}\cdot M^{T}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})}\sum_{\mathbf{R},\mathbf{R}^{\prime}}e^{i\mathbf{R}\cdot(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{p})-i\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\cdot M^{T}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})}

(61)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in BZ}\sum_{\mathbf{G}}\ t_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}e^{i\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l}\cdot(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{p})-i\mathbf{r}_{\beta,l^{\prime}}\cdot M^{T}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})}\sum_{\mathbf{G}_{1},\mathbf{G}_{2}}\delta_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{p},\mathbf{G}_{1}}\delta_{M^{T}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}),\mathbf{G}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{G}_{1},\mathbf{G}_{2}}\frac{t_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{G}_{1}}}{\Omega}e^{i\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l}\cdot\mathbf{G}_{1}-i\mathbf{r}_{\beta,l^{\prime}}\cdot\mathbf{G}_{2}}\delta_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{G}_{1},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}+M^{-T}\mathbf{G}_{2}}\ .

Note that
\mathbf{G}_{1},\mathbf{G}_{2}
are the graphene lattice vectors. We now use the fact that the momentum space coupling
t_{\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{G}}
is rapidly decaying to cutoff the sum over lattice vectors. Let us consider what terms couple to the
\mathbf{K}
point of the top layer where
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p}
. The terms that contribute are
\mathbf{G}=0,C_{3}\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{K},C^{2}_{3}\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{K}
(all of the same magnitude due to
C_{3}
), because all others are outside the first BZ and are suppressed. We have

\displaystyle H^{inter}_{\alpha l,\beta l^{\prime}}(\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{\mathbf{K}}}{\Omega}\sum_{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}\Big(e^{-i\mathbf{r}_{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{G}^{\prime}+i\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l}\cdot\mathbf{0}}\delta_{\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}+M^{-T}\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}

(62)

\displaystyle+e^{-i\mathbf{r}_{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{G}^{\prime}+i\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l}\cdot(C_{3}\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{K})}\delta_{C_{3}\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}+M^{-T}\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+e^{-i\mathbf{r}_{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{G}^{\prime}+i\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l}\cdot(C^{2}_{3}\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{K})}\delta_{C^{2}_{3}\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}+M^{-T}\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}\Big)\ .

Now we consider the
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
sum. We take
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=M^{-T}\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
.
Owing to the large gap of hBN, the momentum corrections to the gap is small at the scale of moiré reciprocal lattice vectors.
Therefore, it is not necessarily legitimate to restrict
\delta\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
to be small, and a faithful microscopic calculation must take higher moiré harmonics into account since they are not rapidly cut off by the hBN dispersion.
We leave the derivation of the form of the higher harmonics to future work; in this work, we will explicitly provide the form for the first harmonic terms for concreteness.
The delta function in the first term of Eq. (
62
) enforces

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p}

\displaystyle=M^{-T}\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p}^{\prime}+M^{-T}\mathbf{G}^{\prime}

(63)

\displaystyle\delta\mathbf{p}-\delta\mathbf{p}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=M^{-T}\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{K}+M^{-T}\mathbf{G}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\equiv M^{-T}\mathbf{G}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}_{1}\ .

For the first harmonics, we have
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}=0
. Repeating this for second and third terms, we eventually get

\displaystyle H^{inter}_{\alpha l,\beta l^{\prime}}(\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p},M^{-T}\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p}^{\prime})

(64)

\displaystyle+e^{i(\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l}-\mathbf{r}_{\beta})\cdot(C_{3}\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{K})}\delta_{\delta\mathbf{p},\delta\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}_{2}}

\displaystyle+e^{i(\mathbf{r}_{\alpha,l}-\mathbf{r}_{\beta})\cdot(C^{2}_{3}\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{K})}\delta_{\delta\mathbf{p},\delta\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}_{3}}\Big)+...\ ,

where “
...

Using Eq.

for

in the graphene and

\displaystyle H^{inter}_{\text{bottom}}(\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p},M^{-T}\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}T^{\dagger}_{j}\delta_{\delta\mathbf{p},\delta\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}_{j}}+\dots,\qquad T^{\dagger}_{j}

\displaystyle=t_{\mathbf{K}}(d\hat{z})\begin{pmatrix}1&e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{3}(j-1)}\\
e^{i\frac{2\pi}{3}(j-1)}&1\end{pmatrix}

(65)

for all
n
, the total number of layers, and “
...
” contains all the higher harmonics.

to emphasize its dependence on the inter-layer distance. We check that in the relaxed structures, the average value of

to

Next in hBN/R
n
G/hBN devices, we compute the coupling between the top layers. We will assume that the top and bottom hBN layers are perfectly aligned so that there is no super moiré pattern formed. There now appear phase factors in the

\displaystyle H^{inter}_{\text{top}}(\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p},M^{-T}\mathbf{K}+\delta\mathbf{p}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}e^{i\frac{2\pi(j-1)}{3}(n-1)}T^{\dagger}_{j}\delta_{\delta\mathbf{p},\delta\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}_{j}}+...\ ,

(66)

where “…” contains all the higher harmonics.
In position space these term can be written

\displaystyle T_{b}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}e^{i\mathbf{q}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{r}}T_{j}+...,\qquad T_{t}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{j=1}^{3}e^{-i\frac{2\pi(j-1)}{3}(n-1)}e^{i\mathbf{q}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{r}}T_{j}+...\ ,

where “…” contains all the higher harmonics.
The two-center approximation results in all carbon-hBN hoppings have equal amplitude for the first harmonics. This is similar to how, in twisted bilayer graphene, the

e^{i\frac{2\pi}{3}j}t_{B,C_{B}}&t_{N,C_{B}}\end{pmatrix}\ .

(68)

are relevant (i.e., the first row of

The model Eq. (

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}H_{G}&T^{\dagger}\\

\psi_{hBN}\end{pmatrix}

out in components yields

\displaystyle\left(H_{G}+T^{\dagger}(E-H_{hBN})^{-1}T\right)\psi_{G}=E\psi_{G}\ .

A.4
Effective Model

Here we derive the effective model that incorporates the inter-layer AB hopping (
t_{1}
), the next-nearest inter-layer AB hoppings (yielding the effective velocity terms
v_{3}
), the inter-layer AA/BB coupling (
v_{4}

t_{2}
). The full Hamiltonian
H_{n}
for R
n
G is defined in Eq. (
36
), and upon projecting onto the two low-energy states (with respect to the charge neutrality) defined in Eq. (
45
), we obtain (for
n\geq 3
)

h^{\text{eff}}_{n}(\mathbf{k})=\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{k})H_{n}\mathcal{U}(\mathbf{k})=\begin{pmatrix}H_{0}(\mathbf{k})+V_{3}(\mathbf{k})&\bar{k}^{n-3}(\beta\bar{k}^{3}+\gamma|k|^{2}+\delta)\\
k^{n-3}(\beta k^{3}+\gamma|k|^{2}+\delta)&H_{0}(\mathbf{k})-V_{3}(\mathbf{k})\end{pmatrix},

(78)

where
k=k_{x}+ik_{y},\bar{k}=k_{x}-ik_{y}
is the complex coordinate for the relative momentum
\mathbf{k}=(k_{x},k_{y})
in the
\mathbf{K}
-valley, and

H_{0}(\mathbf{k})=\alpha|k|^{2}+V_{ISP}\mathcal{F}_{n}(\frac{v_{F}|k|}{t_{1}}),\quad\text{and}\quad V_{3}(\mathbf{k})=V\mathcal{G}_{n}(\frac{v_{F}|k|}{t_{1}})

(79)

with the exact projection giving the following closed form results

\mathcal{F}_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{2\left(x-x^{n}\right)^{2}}{\left(x^{2}-1\right)\left(x^{2n}-1\right)}+n-1\right),\;\;\text{for odd $n$}\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(n-\frac{\left(x^{2}+1\right)\left(x^{n}-1\right)}{\left(x^{2}-1\right)\left(x^{n}+1\right)}\right),\;\;\text{for even $n$}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{G}_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n\left(x^{2n}+1\right)}{x^{2n}-1}+\frac{x^{2}+1}{1-x^{2}}\right).

(80)

To be consistent in the perturbation theory, we keep terms up to

in the expansion of
\mathcal{F}_{n}(x)
and
\mathcal{G}_{n}(x)
, which is what we used in the main text (Eq. (

(81)

=&\delta_{i,j}2v_{F}v_{4}|k|^{2}/t_{1}+\mathcal{O}(|k|^{2n}),\end{split}

(82)

where

are at larger distances and will be neglected in our first-order degenerate perturbation analysis. Notice that
\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{M}-(\mathbf{q}_{1}-\mathbf{q}_{2})=C_{3}\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{M}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{M}-(\mathbf{q}_{1}-\mathbf{q}_{3})=C^{2}_{3}\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{M}
, we obtain the following 6
\times
6 Hamiltonian

H^{\text{eff}}_{n}(\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{M})=\begin{pmatrix}h^{\text{eff}}_{n}(\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{M})+V_{0}\sigma_{11}&V_{1}e^{i\psi_{\xi}}\sigma_{11}&V_{1}e^{-i\psi_{\xi}}\sigma_{11}\\
V_{1}e^{-i\psi_{\xi}}\sigma_{11}&h^{\text{eff}}_{n}(C_{3}\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{M})+V_{0}\sigma_{11}&V_{1}e^{i\psi_{\xi}}\sigma_{11}\\
V_{1}e^{i\psi_{\xi}}\sigma_{11}&V_{1}e^{-i\psi_{\xi}}\sigma_{11}&h^{\text{eff}}_{n}(C^{2}_{3}\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{M})+V_{0}\sigma_{11}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\sigma_{11}\equiv(\sigma_{0}+\sigma_{3})/2\ .

(92)

The
C_{3}
operator that commutes with
H^{\text{eff}}_{n}(\tilde{\mathbf{K}}_{M})
takes the form

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix}\otimes D_{\text{eff}}[C_{3}],

(93)

which can be used to diagonalize
H^{\text{eff}}_{n}
into three 2
\times
2 blocks that correspond to symmetry eigenvalues
\omega^{j}
(
j=0,1,2
). The highest energy in each of these symmetry sectors are found to be

\displaystyle\epsilon^{(c)}_{j}(\tilde{K}_{M})

\displaystyle=f_{0}+V_{1}\cos(\psi_{\xi}-(j+1)\frac{2\pi}{3})+\sqrt{f^{2}_{+}+\Big(\frac{V_{0}}{2}+V_{3}(|\tilde{K}_{M}|)+V_{1}\cos(\psi_{\xi}-(j+1)\frac{2\pi}{3})\Big)^{2}}

(94a)

where
f_{0}=H_{0}(|\tilde{K}_{M}|)+V_{0}/2
, see Eq. (
80
), and we define

f_{\pm}^{2}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\tilde{K}_{M}\right\rvert}^{4}\big[\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\tilde{K}_{M}\right\rvert}^{6}\beta^{2}+(|\tilde{K}_{M}|^{2}\gamma+\delta)^{2}\pm 2\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\tilde{K}_{M}\right\rvert}^{3}\beta(|\tilde{K}_{M}|^{2}\gamma+\delta)\cos 3\theta_{K}\big],\quad\text{with}\quad\theta_{K}=\tan^{-1}\frac{\tilde{K}_{M,y}}{\tilde{K}_{M,x}}.

(95)

Notice that
f_{\pm}
is always non-negative. A similar analysis for the
\tilde{K}_{M}^{\prime}
point leads to

\displaystyle\epsilon^{(c)}_{j}(\tilde{K}_{M}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=f_{0}+V_{1}\cos(\psi_{\xi}+(j+1)\frac{2\pi}{3})+\sqrt{f^{2}_{-}+\Big(\frac{V_{0}}{2}+V_{3}(|\tilde{K}_{M}^{\prime}|)+V_{1}\cos(\psi_{\xi}+(j+1)\frac{2\pi}{3})\Big)^{2}}

(96a)

We consider certain parameter regime where it is easy to identify the energy of the lowest conduction band at
\tilde{K}_{M}
and
\tilde{K}_{M}^{\prime}
: (I) with negative (or small positive) displacement field such that
\frac{V_{0}}{2}+V_{3}(|\tilde{K}_{M}|)>0

\min\{\cos(\psi_{\xi}-2\pi/3),\cos(\psi_{\xi}+2\pi/3),\cos\psi_{\xi}\}

, one can Taylor expand the square root and realize that the lowest conduction state corresponds to maximizing
|\frac{V_{0}}{2}+V_{3}+V_{1}\cos(\psi_{\xi}+\frac{2\pi j}{3})|
. Since
\frac{V_{0}}{2}+V_{3}
acquires a large negative value in this case, the lowest conduction state again corresponds to choosing
\min\{\cos(\psi_{\xi}-2\pi/3),\cos(\psi_{\xi}+2\pi/3),\cos\psi_{\xi}\}

f_{\pm}\sim 20
meV for experimental twist angle
\theta=0.77^{\circ}
, while
V_{0},V_{1}\sim 5
meV as determined by our band-structure fitting analysis.

Eq. (

\psi_{\xi=0}=223.5^{\circ}

\psi_{\xi=1}=16.5^{\circ}

\eta_{\text{K}}=\begin{cases}\omega^{*},\quad\text{for}\quad\xi=0\\
\omega,\quad\text{for}\quad\xi=1\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\eta_{\text{K}^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}\omega^{*},\quad\text{for}\quad\xi=0\\
1,\quad\text{for}\quad\xi=1\end{cases}.

(97)

Irrespective of the value of
\psi_{\xi}
(and hence the stacking configuration), we consistently have
\eta_{K}\eta_{\text{K}^{\prime}}=\omega
.
Altogether, using the relationship between point-group symmetry eigenvalues and Chern number
Fang et al. 2012

\exp(\frac{2i\pi C_{c}}{3})=\begin{cases}1,\quad\text{for}\quad\frac{V_{0}}{2}-2V>0,\\
\omega^{*},\quad\text{for}\quad\frac{V_{0}}{2}-2V<0.\end{cases}

(98)

Lattice structure and Structural relaxation

There are two types of structures R
n
G/hBN.
The two structures are distinguished by their stackings in the AA regions of the moiré structure. (See the example of zero twist in Fig.
20
(a),(b).)
In one structure, the carbon A/B in the bottom layer of graphene is aligned with nitrogen/boron in the AA region, which we call
\xi=1
configuration; the other

\xi=0

The symmetries of the
\xi=0,1

n
G as hBN breaks inversion and nonzero small twist angles breaks mirrors.
Only the three-fold rotation
C_{3}
(with axis perpendicular to the sample) can be preserved for all twist angles.
The
\xi=0,1

C_{2}
rotation (about the axis perpendicular to the sample) on the hBN only.

We also consider hBN/R
n
G/hBN structures with hBN nearly aligned on top and on bottom.
We choose the alignments of two hBNs such that there is only one moiré pattern and
C_{3}
symmetry (with axis perpendicular to the sample) is preserved.
The stackings of the top and bottom hBNs (relative to graphene) are labeled by
\xi_{t},\xi_{b}
, respectively.
For simplicity, we in this work only study
(\xi_{b},\xi_{t})=(1,1)
(Fig.
20
(c)) and
(\xi_{b},\xi_{t})=(1,0)
(Fig.
20
(d)).
We do not need to study
(\xi_{b},\xi_{t})=(0,0)

(\xi_{b},\xi_{t})=(1,1)

C_{2}
rotation.
We note that
(\xi_{b},\xi_{t})=(1,0),(0,1)
can restore the inversion symmetry if the two hBNs are related by inversion and their inversion center is aligned with that of the R
n
G; we choose
(\xi_{b},\xi_{t})=(1,0)
as a representative as this type of structure.
In our DFT calculation for
(\xi_{b},\xi_{t})=(1,0)

C

Appendix D
Valley-Resolved Band Structure

In superlattices form by R
n
G and hBN, the valleys of R
n
G emerge as a good quantum number in the low energy part of bands. Inherited from the gapless Dirac dispersion band structure at
\text{K},\text{K}^{\prime}
valleys in untwisted case, the low energy bands of R
n
G-hBN superlattice either belongs to
K
valley or
K^{\prime}
valley. Thus, We can construct an operator to distinguish the valley degree of freedom with eigenvalue
+1
for the states at one valley and eigenvalue
-1
for the states at the other valley, which is in the same spirit as the spin-z operator in basis
\{\ket{\uparrow},\ket{\downarrow}\}
.

The valley operator for Dirac fermion model — single layer graphene(SLG) in real space reads
Lopez-Bezanilla and Lado 2020

\mathcal{V}_{z}^{Gr}=\frac{i}{3\sqrt{3}}\sum_{\ll i,j\gg,s}\eta_{ij}\sigma_{z}^{i\,j}c^{\dagger}_{i,s}c_{j,s}

(99)

where
\ll i,j\gg
denotes the next-nearest-neighbor(NNN) hopping term,
s
denote spin,
\eta_{ij}=\pm 1
for clockwise or counterclockwise hopping and
\sigma_{z}
is in the sublattice degree of freedom, meaning that
\sigma_{z}^{i\,j}=1
if both
i,j
are on the
A
sublattice,
\sigma_{z}^{i\,j}=-1
if both
i,j
are on the
B
sublattice and
\sigma_{z}^{i\,j}=0
otherwise. The schematic diagram of valley operator in real space for an unit cell is shown in Fig.
31
(a). The factor
\eta_{ij}\sigma_{z}^{ij}
forms the valley flux, which distinguishes the NNN hopping in
K
direction and
K^{\prime}
direction. To specify the valley flux, we compare it with Haldane’s local magnetic flux(Fig.
31
(b),(c)). While the latter holds the same flux with both sublattice A and B, valley flux means sublattice A/B holds opposite local magnetic flux. After performing Fourier transformation, the valley operator of single layer graphene in sublattice is proportional to the identity matrix in reciprocal space,
\mathcal{V}_{z}^{Gr}(k)=\left(\begin{matrix}f(k)&\\
&f(k)\end{matrix}\right)
.
f(k)
is an odd function in Brillouin Zone
f(k)=-f(-k)
, which satisfies
\bra{\psi(K)}\mathcal{V}_{z}(K)\ket{\psi(K)}=1
and
\bra{\psi(K^{\prime})}\mathcal{V}_{z}(K^{\prime})\ket{\psi(K^{\prime})}=-1
(Fig.
31
(d),(e)).

Since valley operator in real space only contains intra-layer hopping term, it can be easily generalised to R

\mathcal{V}_{z}=\sum_{l}\mathcal{V}_{z}^{l}
, where
l
denotes the layer index. And for twisted hBN-R
n
G system,
valley operator of each layer can be obtained numerically by considering all the NNN hopping term multiplied by valley flux factor
\eta_{ij}\sigma_{z}^{ij}
for the atoms in superlattice and this layer. The process to obtain real space valley operator for moiré system corresponds to valley band folding in momentum space.

To identify the valley quantum number of the energy bands in the momentum space, we solve the valley operator of hBN-R
n
G
\mathcal{V}_{z}
in reciprocal space. Using the average value of
\mathcal{V}_{z}(k)
under the eigenvectors of
H(k)
, we can distinguish the valley components of each state.
And in the low energy region, it satisfies
\bra{\psi(k)}\mathcal{V}_{z}(k)\ket{\psi(k)}\simeq 1
for
K
valley and
\bra{\psi(k)}\mathcal{V}_{z}(k)\ket{\psi(k)}\simeq-1
for
K^{\prime}
valley. Based on the eigenvalue of each state, we can make a bipartition for bands with different valley components and obtain the valley-resolved bands. The calculation steps for valley-resolved bands are shown in Fig.

(f). The calculations are implemented in WannierTools package
Wu et al. 2018
. The valley-resolved bands of hBN-R
n
G(n=3,4,5,6,7) are shown in Figs.

,

,

,

and

.

86

continuum model.
Without loss of generality, we focus on the

valley.

We discuss the
2n\times 2n
continuum model first.
From SK method, we can directly derive the parameter values for R
n
G (
n\geq 3

Regarding to the moiré potential parameters in Eq. (

) and Eq. (

,

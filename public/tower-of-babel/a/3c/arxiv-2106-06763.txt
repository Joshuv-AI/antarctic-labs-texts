Abstract
. We describe a general method of constructing nonlocal recursion operators for symmetries of PDEs. As an example, the cotangent equation to the 3D rdDym equation u y ​ t = u x ​ u x ​ y − u y ​ u x ​ x u_{yt}=u_{x}u_{xy}-u_{y}u_{xx} for which two mutually inverse operators are found. The exposition includes a rigorous criterion to check the hereditary property.

Introduction

The rdDym equation (
r
th dispersionless Dym equation)

u_{yt}=u_{x}u_{xy}-u_{y}u_{xx}

(1)

belongs to the class of linearly degenerate equations
[
8
]
and
arose in the Martínez Alonso-Shabat hierarchy
[
16
]
. It is
Lax integrable and its symmetry properties were studied
in
[
1
,
3
]
. Recursion operators for
symmetries of Equation (
1
) was found by O. Morozov
in
[
19
]
.

In
[
22
]
, V. Ovsienko considered (in slightly different
notation) the system

\begin{array}[]{rcl}v_{yt}&=&2(v_{y}u_{xx}-v_{x}u_{xy})+u_{x}v_{xy}-u_{y}v_{xx}-2(u_{y}u_{xx}+2u_{x}u_{xy}),\\[2.0pt]
u_{yt}&=&u_{x}u_{xy}-u_{y}u_{xx},\end{array}

(2)

which is a two-component extension of the rdDym equation (another
two-component generalization of (
1
) was studied
in
[
20
]
). Treating (
2
) as the Euler equation on
the Virasoro algebra, its bi-Hamiltonian structure was established.

System (
2
) may be also considered in the following way. Recall
(see
[
12
,
13
]
) that to any equation

\mathscr{E}=\{F[u]=0\}
in unknowns
u
its cotangent
equation
\mathscr{T}^{*}\mathscr{E}

F[u]=0,\qquad\ell_{F}^{*}[u,p]=0

is naturally associated,
\ell_{F}^{*}
is the operator adjoint to the
linearisation of
F
(here and below
[u]
,
[u,p]
, etc. denotes the
variables together with their derivatives up to certain
order). Then (
2
) may be understood as the cotangent equation
of (
1
), with
v
instead of
p
, ‘deformed’ by the Lagrangian term

\frac{\delta L}{\delta u}\ \text{ with }\ L=u_{y}u_{x}^{2}.

System (
2
) is an Euler-Lagrange equations.

Lagrangian deformations of some linearly degenerate equations were studied
in
[
2
]
. In particular, the system

\begin{array}[]{rcl}v_{yt}&=&2(v_{y}u_{xx}-v_{x}u_{xy})+u_{x}v_{xy}-u_{y}v_{xx}-2(2ku_{xy}u_{x}+(ku_{y}+l)u_{xx}),\\[2.0pt]
u_{yt}&=&u_{x}u_{xy}-u_{y}u_{xx},\end{array}

(3)

which is considered below, is the deformation of the cotangent to rdDym
equation with the deforming Lagrangian
L=(ku_{y}+l)u_{x}^{2}
,
k
,

l\in\mathbb{R}
. System (
2
) is obtained from (
3
)
when
k=1
,
l=0
. We find below recursion operators for symmetries
of (
3
).

Remark 1

(courtesy O.I. Morozov)
.

Actually, both parameters
k
and
l
are fake in a sense: the change of
variables
v\mapsto v-2kxu_{x}-ku+ly
kills them and transforms
System (
3
) to the pure cotangent equation of (

). In
particular, the Ovsienko system (
2
) also reduces to the cotangent
equation. Though the computations in the general case are not much harder
that in a particular one, the result look more complicated. For that reason
we set

everywhere below, i.e., the equation we study below will
be of the form

\begin{array}[]{rcl}v_{yt}&=&2(v_{y}u_{xx}-v_{x}u_{xy})+u_{x}v_{xy}-u_{y}v_{xx},\\[2.0pt]
u_{yt}&=&u_{x}u_{xy}-u_{y}u_{xx}.\end{array}

(4)

There exist several approaches to construct recursion operators (see, for

[
21
,
25
]
). Our method is based on the
technology described in
[
13
]
and originating

[
11
]
. It is based on geometrical interpretation of

[
17

14
]

1.
Preliminaries and notation

The exposition here is based on
[
5
,
13
,
14
]
.

Let
\pi\colon E\to M
be a vector bundle over a smooth manifold
M
,

\dim M=n
,
\rank\pi=m
, and
\pi_{\infty}\colon J^{\infty}(\pi)\to M
be the
bundle of its infinite jets. Consider an infinitely prolonged differential
equation
\mathscr{E}\in J^{\infty}(\pi)
and assume that

\mathscr{E}=\{F=0\}
, where
F\in\Gamma(\pi_{\infty}^{*}(\xi))
, where

\xi\colon N\to M
is another vector bundle. Any
\mathscr{E}
is naturally
endowed with an
n
-dimensional Frobenius integrable
distribution
\mathscr{C}
(the Cartan distribution) that defines its
geometry. The Cartan distribution is
\pi_{\infty}
-horizontal and,
consequently, defines a flat connection
in
\pi_{\infty}\colon\mathscr{E}\to M
.

A morphism of equations
f\colon\tilde{\mathscr{E}}\to\mathscr{E}
is a
smooth map, such that

f_{*}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{\theta})\subset\mathscr{C}_{f(\theta)}
for
any
\theta\in\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
. A morphism is called a (differential)
covering if it is a submersion and the
restriction
\left.f_{*}\right|_{\mathscr{C}_{\theta}}
of its differential to any
Cartan plane is an isomorphism. Two coverings
f_{i}\colon\mathscr{E}_{i}\to\mathscr{E}
,
i=1
,
2
, are called equivalent if there exists a
diffeomorphism
g\colon\mathscr{E}_{1}\to\mathscr{E}_{2}
that preserves the
Cartan distributions and such that
f_{1}=f_{2}\circ g
.

Denote by
\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{F}(\mathscr{E})
the algebra of smooth
functions on
\mathscr{E}
. A (higher infinitesimal) symmetry of
\mathscr{E}

is a
\pi_{\infty}
-vertical derivation
S\colon\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{F}
(a
vector field on
\mathscr{E}
) that preserves the Cartan distribution. There
exists a one-to-one correspondence between symmetries and solutions of the
equation

\ell_{\mathscr{E}}(\varphi)=0,

where
\ell_{\mathscr{E}}
is the restriction of the linearisation

\ell_{F}\colon\varkappa\to P
to
\mathscr{E}
and
\varkappa
denotes the module of
sections
\Gamma(\pi_{\infty}^{*}(\pi))
. A conservation law of
\mathscr{E}
is a

\pi_{\infty}
-horizontal form
\omega\in\Lambda_{h}^{n-1}(\mathscr{E})
closed
with respect to the horizontal de Rham differential
d_{h}
. Trivial
conservation laws are those of the form
\omega=d_{h}\rho
,
\rho\in\Lambda^{n-2}(\mathscr{E})
.

Given a covering
\tau\colon\tilde{\mathscr{E}}\to\mathscr{E}
, symmetries
and conservation laws of
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
are called nonlocal
for
\mathscr{E}
. Let
S\colon\mathscr{F}(\mathscr{E})\to\mathscr{F}(\tilde{\mathscr{E}})
be a
\pi_{\infty}
-vertical derivation. It is
called a
\tau
-shadow if

\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{X}\circ S=S\circ\mathscr{C}_{X}

for any vector field
X
on
M
. We say that a symmetry
\tilde{S}
is a lift
of the shadow
S
if
\left.\tilde{S}\right|_{\mathscr{E}}=S
.

Let an equation
\mathscr{E}
be given by
\{F=0\}
. Then the
system
\mathscr{T}\mathscr{E}

F[u]=0,\qquad\ell_{F}[u,q]=0

is called the tangent equation of
\mathscr{E}
, while the projection

\mathrm{t}\colon[u,q]\mapsto[u]
is the tangent covering
over
\mathscr{E}
. It must be stressed that
q
is considered as an odd
variable of degree
1
. Sections of
\mathrm{t}
that preserve the Cartan
distributions are identified with symmetries of
\mathscr{E}
and consequently
Bäcklund auto-transformations
\mathscr{R}_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}
of the form

\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau}
\scriptstyle{\tau^{\prime}}
\textstyle{\mathscr{T}\mathscr{E}}
\textstyle{\mathscr{T}\mathscr{E}\hbox to0.0pt{,\hss}}

(5)

where
\tau
,
\tau^{\prime}
are coverings, are naturally interpreted as recursion
operators for symmetries of
\mathscr{E}
.

To construct these operators, we use a scheme consisting of two steps. In the
first step, we are looking for two-component conservation laws
\omega

of
\mathscr{T}\mathscr{E}
linear on the fibers of the
projection
\mathrm{t}
. Provided such a law was found, we consider the
covering
\tau=\tau_{\omega}
associated with it and, as the second step, try
to find
\tau_{\omega}
-shadows (also linear on fibers both of
\mathrm{t}

and
\tau_{\omega}
). If such a shadow
S
exists, it delivers the
covering
\tau^{\prime}=\tau_{S}
and the desired recursion
operator
\mathscr{R}_{\tau_{\omega},\tau_{S}}
.

Finally, few words about the type of equations that are the subject of our
study. Assume
\mathscr{E}=\{F=0\}
,
F\in P
, and consider the operator

\ell_{F}^{*}\colon\hat{P}\to\hat{\varkappa}
formally adjoint to
\ell_{F}
, where

\hat{\bullet}=\hom(\bullet,\Lambda_{h}^{n})
. Then the
system
\mathscr{T}^{*}\mathscr{E}

\ell_{F}^{*}[u,p]=0,\qquad F[u]=0

(6)

is said to be the cotangent equation of
\mathscr{E}
, while the projection

\mathrm{t}^{*}\colon\mathscr{T}^{*}\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}
,
(u,p)\mapsto(u)
, is called the cotangent covering. Similar to the case of the tangent
equation, the variable
p
is odd. System (
6
) is always an
Euler-Lagrange equation with the Lagrangian density
L=\sum p^{j}F^{j}
and we
are interested in equations

\ell_{F}^{*}[u,p]+G[u,p]=0,\qquad F[u]=0,

(‘Lagrangian deformations’ of (
6
)), such that (a) they are Lagrangian
as well and (b) they possess a nontrivial recursion
operator. Equation (
2
) is exactly of this type. Note that for
Euler-Lagrange equations
\mathscr{T}\mathscr{E}

and
\mathscr{T}^{*}\mathscr{E}
coincide, since
\ell_{F}=\ell_{F}^{*}
in this
case.

Coordinates

Let
\mathscr{U}\subset M
be a coordinate neighborhood with local coordinates

x=(x^{1},\dots,x^{n})
and
E\supset\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{U})\simeq\mathscr{U}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a trivialization of
\pi
with fiber-wise
coordinates
u=(u^{1},\dots,u^{m})
. Then adapted coordinates
u_{\sigma}^{j}

arise on
J^{\infty}(\pi)
, where
u_{\sigma}^{j}
correspond to the partial
derivative
\partial^{\left|\sigma\right|}u^{j}/\partial x^{\sigma}
. The Cartan
distribution is spanned by the total derivatives

D_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}+\sum u_{\sigma i}^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{\sigma}^{j}},

while the Cartan connection takes
\partial/\partial x^{i}

to
D_{i}
. Equation
\mathscr{E}
is given by the system of relations
F^{l}(x,\dots,u_{\sigma}^{j},\dots)=0
,
l=1,\dots,r
. To restrict necessary objects
to
\mathscr{E}
, one needs to choose internal coordinates on
\mathscr{E}

(cf.
[
18
]
) and express these objects in terms of internal
coordinates. In particular, the Cartan distribution on
\mathscr{E}
is got by
rewriting the operators
D_{i}
in internal coordinates.

The coordinate presentation of the linearisation operator acts on
\varphi=(\varphi^{1},\dots,\varphi^{m})
by

\ell_{F}(\varphi)=\sum_{\sigma,j}\frac{\partial F^{l}}{\partial u_{\sigma}^{j}}D_{\sigma}(\varphi^{j}),

where
D_{\sigma}
is the composition of the total derivatives corresponding to
the multi-index
\sigma
, while for the adjoint one we have

\ell_{F}^{*}(\psi)=\sum_{\sigma,l}(-1)^{\left|\sigma\right|}D_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\partial F^{l}}{\partial u_{\sigma}^{j}}\psi^{l}\right),

where
\psi=(\psi^{1},\dots,\psi^{r})
. Thus, the tangent equation
of
\mathscr{E}
is

\sum_{\sigma,j}\frac{\partial F^{l}}{\partial u_{\sigma}^{j}}q_{\sigma}^{j}=0,\quad F(x,\dots,u_{\sigma}^{j},\dots)=0,\qquad q=(q^{1},\dots,q^{m}),

while while the cotangent one is given by

\sum_{\sigma,l}(-1)^{\left|\sigma\right|}D_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\partial F^{l}}{\partial u_{\sigma}^{j}}p^{l}\right)=0,\quad F(x,\dots,u_{\sigma}^{j},\dots)=0,\qquad p=(p^{1},\dots,p^{r}).

Without loss of generality, we can define two-component conservation laws as
the forms

\omega=(X_{1}\,dx^{1}+X_{2}\,dx^{2})\,dx^{3}\wedge\dots\wedge\,dx^{n},\qquad D_{1}(X_{2})=D_{2}(X_{1}),

where
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are smooth functions on
\mathscr{E}
. Triviality
of
\omega
means existence of a potential
Y
, such that
D_{i}(Y)=X_{i}
,
i=1
,
2
.

A covering structure in a locally trivial vector bundle
\tau\colon W\to\mathscr{E}
is determined by the system of pair-wise commuting vector fields

\tilde{D}_{i}=D_{i}+X_{i},\qquad i=1,\dots,n,

(7)

where
X_{i}=\sum X_{i}^{\alpha}\partial/\partial w^{\alpha}
are
\tau
-vertical
fields and
w^{\alpha}
are coordinates in the fiber of
\tau
(nonlocal
variables). The equalities
[\tilde{D}_{i},\tilde{D}_{j}]=0
amount to the fact
that the system

w_{x^{i}}^{\alpha}=X_{i}^{\alpha},\qquad i=1,\dots,n,\quad\alpha=1,\dots,\rank\tau,

is compatible modulo
\mathscr{E}
. Note that (
7
) allows to lift any
differential operator
\Delta
in total derivatives from
\mathscr{E}
to an
operator
\tilde{\Delta}
on the covering equation.

Let
\omega
be a two-component conservation law like above. Then one can
construct the covering
\tau_{\omega}
as follows. In the case
\dim M=2
we
set

w_{x^{1}}=X_{1},\qquad w_{x^{2}}=X_{2},

and
\rank\tau_{\omega}=1
. When
\dim M>2
, we introduce infinite number of
nonlocal variables
w^{\sigma}
, where
\sigma
is a multi-index consisting of
integers
2,\dots,n
,
\left|\sigma\right|\geq 0
, and set

w_{x^{i}}^{\sigma}=\begin{cases}D_{\sigma}(X_{i}),&i=1,2,\\
w^{\sigma i},&i>2.\end{cases}

Obviously, we obtain covering structures in both cases.

Consider an
m
-component function
S=(S^{1},\dots,S^{m})
. It defines a

\tau
-shadow if and only if

\tilde{\ell}_{\mathscr{E}}(S)=0,

where
\tilde{\ell}_{\mathscr{E}}
is the natural lift of the
operator
\ell_{\mathscr{E}}
to the covering equation (see above). Let
now
S
be a
\tau
-shadow, where
\tau=\tau_{\omega}
in
Diagram (
5
). Denote by
p_{\sigma}
the fiber coordinates in the left
copy of
\mathscr{T}\mathscr{E}
and by
\bar{p}_{\sigma}
the same coordinates
in the right one and set
\bar{p}_{\sigma}^{j}=\tilde{D}_{\sigma}(S^{j})
,
where
\tilde{D}_{\sigma}
is the composition of total derivatives in
W
. This
gives the covering
\tau^{\prime}=\tau_{S}
. The desired recursion operator is
obtained, when we set

where
S_{l}^{j,\sigma}
,
S_{\sigma}^{j}
are smooth functions on
\mathscr{E}
.

Conservation laws

There exist four conservation laws on

of order
\leq 2
and linear with respect to the variables

\displaystyle\omega_{1}

\displaystyle=(X_{1}\,dx+T_{1}\,dt)\wedge\,dy,\quad\omega_{2}=(X_{2}\,dx+T_{2}\,dt)\wedge\,dy,

\displaystyle=(X_{3}\,dx+Y_{3}\,dy)\wedge\,dt,\quad\omega_{4}=(X_{4}\,dx+Y_{4}\,dy)\wedge\,dt,

3.
Conclusions

The method used here to construct recursion operators for symmetries of
Equation (
4
) seems to be of a universal nature. In particular, it
allows to test rigorously the hereditary property of nonlocal recursion
operators. It would be interesting to apply it to other two-component
extensions of linearly degenerate equations constructed
in
[
2
]
, as well as to the Dunajski
equation
[
7
]
, etc.

It would be also interesting to describe (similar to how it was done
in
[
3
]
) the algebra of nonlocal symmetries (and the
corresponding coverings) under the action of recursion operators described
above.

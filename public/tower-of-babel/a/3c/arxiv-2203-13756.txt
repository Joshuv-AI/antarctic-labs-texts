Min-Max Polarization for Certain Classes
of Sharp Configurations on the Sphere

Abstract
We consider the problem of finding an N N -point configuration on the sphere S d ⊂ ℝ d + 1 S^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1} with the smallest absolute maximum value over S d S^{d} of its total potential. The potential induced by each point 𝐲 {\bf y} in a given configuration at a point 𝐱 ∈ S d {\bf x}\in S^{d} is f ⁡ ( | 𝐱 − 𝐲 | 2 ) f\left(\left|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\right|^{2}\right) , where f f is continuous on [ 0 , 4 ] [0,4] and completely monotone on ( 0 , 4 ] (0,4] , and | 𝐱 − 𝐲 | \left|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\right| is the Euclidean distance between points 𝐱 {\bf x} and 𝐲 {\bf y} . We show that any sharp point configuration ω ¯ N \overline{\omega}_{N} on S d S^{d} , which is antipodal or is a spherical design of an even strength is a solution to this problem. We also prove that the absolute maximum over S d S^{d} of the potential of any such configuration ω ¯ N \overline{\omega}_{N} is attained at points of ω ¯ N \overline{\omega}_{N} .

\begin{split}&q(t_{i})=g(t_{i}),\ \ i=1,\ldots,m+1,\ \ q^{\prime}(t_{i})=g^{\prime}(t_{i}),\ \ i=2,\ldots,m,\\
&\text{and, if}\ \ \nu=1,\ we\ additionally\ assume\ that\ \ q^{\prime}(t_{1})=g^{\prime}(t_{1}).\end{split}

(11)

Then

q(t)\geq g(t),\ \ t\in[-1,1].

(12)

If, in addition, the derivative
g^{(L)}
is strictly convex on
(-1,1)
, the inequality in (
12
) is strict for
t\in[-1,1]\setminus\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{m+1}\}
.

Proof.

The assertion of the lemma is trivial when
L=0
; i.e., when
m=1
and
\nu=0
. Therefore, we assume that
L\geq 1
.
Inequality (
12
) holds trivially for
t=t_{1},\ldots,t_{m+1}
. We choose arbitrary
x\in[-1,1]\setminus\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{m+1}\}
and denote

h(t):=q(t)+b(t-t_{1})^{1+\nu}(t-t_{m+1})\prod_{i=2}^{m}(t-t_{i})^{2},

where the constant
b
is chosen so that
h(x)=g(x)
. Observe that the polynomial
h
also satisfies interpolation conditions (
11
). The function
\tau(t):=g(t)-h(t)
has at least
m+2
distinct zeros in
[-1,1]
at least
m-1+\nu
of which have multiplicity two and are located in
(-1,1)
. By the Rolle’s theorem, the derivative
\tau^{\prime}
has at least
2m+\nu=L+2
distinct zeros in
(-1,1)
, which implies that the derivative
\tau^{(L)}
has at least three distinct zeros in this interval. The polynomial
h^{(L)}
has degree at most two with the coefficient of
t^{2}
being
\frac{(L+2)!}{2}b
. It interpolates the convex function
g^{(L)}
at at least three distinct points in
(-1,1)
. Then
b\geq 0
. Since
(x-t_{1})^{1+\nu}>0
both for
\nu=0
and
\nu=1
, we have

g(x)=h(x)=q(x)+b(x-t_{1})^{1+\nu}(x-t_{m+1})\prod_{i=2}^{m}(x-t_{i})^{2}\leq q(x),

(13)

which proves (
12
).
If
g^{(L)}
is strictly convex on
(-1,1)
, then
b\neq 0
(otherwise
g^{(L)}
would be interpolated by the polynomial
h^{(L)}
of degree at most one at three distinct points). Therefore,
b>0
and the inequality in (
13
) is strict for
x\in[-1,1]\setminus\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{m+1}\}
.
∎

Denote

w_{d}(t):=\gamma_{d}(1-t^{2})^{d/2-1},

where the constant
\gamma_{d}
is chosen so that
w_{d}
is a probability density on
[-1,1]
. We will need the following special case of the Funk-Hecke formula

\int_{S^{d}}g({\bf x}\cdot{\bf y})\ \!d\sigma_{d}({\bf x})=\int_{-1}^{1}g(t)w_{d}(t)\ \!dt,\ \ \ {\bf y}\in S^{d},

(14)

where
g
is any integrable function on
[-1,1]
with the weight
w_{d}
.

We next recall the following property of spherical designs.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
n\geq 0
, and
\omega_{N}=\{{\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{N}\}
be a spherical
n
-design on
S^{d}
. Then for every polynomial
Q\in\mathbb{P}_{n}
, the sum

J_{Q}({\bf z}):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}Q\left({\bf z}\cdot{\bf x}_{i}\right)

is constant over
{\bf z}\in S^{d}
.

Proof.

The assertion of Lemma
3.2
is trivial when
n=0
.
Let
n\geq 1
and
{\bf z}\in S^{d}
be arbitrary. Since the polynomial
p({\bf x})=Q({\bf z}\cdot{\bf x})
has degree at most
n
, using (
14
) we have

\begin{split}J_{Q}({\bf z})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}Q\left({\bf z}\cdot{\bf x}_{i}\right)=N\int_{S^{d}}Q({\bf z}\cdot{\bf x})\ \!d\sigma_{d}({\bf x})=N\int_{-1}^{1}Q(t)w_{d}(t)\ \!dt,\end{split}

which is independent of
{\bf z}
.
∎

To establish the uniqueness in Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
, we need the following result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
n\geq 0
, and
\omega_{N}\subset S^{d}
be a
2n
-design. Then for every point
{\bf z}\in S^{d}
, the set
D({\bf z},\omega_{N})
contains at least
n+1
distinct elements.

Proof.

The assertion of the lemma is trivial for
n=0
. Let
n\geq 1
and
assume to the contrary that there is a point
{\bf z}\in S^{d}
with
D({\bf z},\omega_{N})
containing
k\leq n
distinct elements. Denote them by
t_{1}<\ldots<t_{k}
and let
p(t):=(t-t_{1})^{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot(t-t_{k})^{2}
. Since
p
vanishes at each dot product
{\bf z}\cdot{\bf x}_{i}
and
\omega_{N}
is a
2n
-design, using the Funk-Hecke formula (
14
) we have

0=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}p({\bf z}\cdot{\bf x}_{i})=N\int_{S^{d}}p({\bf z}\cdot{\bf x})\ \!d\sigma_{d}({\bf x})=N\int_{-1}^{1}p(t)w_{d}(t)\ \!dt>0.

This contradiction proves the assertion of the lemma.
∎

For the proof of Theorem
2.3
, we also need the following statement.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
d,m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\overline{\omega}_{N}
be a strongly
m
-sharp configuration on
S^{d}
. Then no dot product between vectors in
\overline{\omega}_{N}
equals
-1
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that one of the dot products between vectors in
\overline{\omega}_{N}
equals
-1
. Let
-1=\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\ldots<\tau_{m}<1
be all values of the dot product between distinct vectors in
\overline{\omega}_{N}
. Denote by
p(t)=(t+1)(t-\tau_{2})^{2}\cdots(t-\tau_{m})^{2}(t-1)
and let
{\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{N}
be points in
\overline{\omega}_{N}
. Since the polynomial
h({\bf x})=p({\bf x}_{1}\cdot{\bf x})
has degree
2m
and
\overline{\omega}_{N}
is a
2m
-design, using the Funk-Hecke formula (
14
), we have

0=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}p({\bf x}_{1}\cdot{\bf x}_{i})=N\int\limits_{S^{d}}p({\bf x}_{1}\cdot{\bf x})\ \!d\sigma_{d}({\bf x})=N\int\limits_{-1}^{1}p(t)w_{d}(t)\ \!dt<0.

This contradiction shows that no dot product between vectors in
\overline{\omega}_{N}
is
-1
.
∎

Finally, we establish a general statement used in
the proofs of Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
d,m\geq 1
and that a function
g:[-1,1]\to[-\infty,\infty)
and a set
A
consisting of numbers
-1\leq t_{1}<\ldots<t_{m+1}\leq 1
are arbitrary. Suppose also that
q
is a polynomial such that
q(t_{i})=g(t_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,m+1
, and

q(t)\geq g(t),\ \ t\in[-1,1].

(15)

If
\omega_{N}=\{{\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{N}\}\subset S^{d}
is a spherical
L
-design, where
L\geq{\rm deg}\ \!q
, and
{\bf y}\in S^{d}
is any point such that
D({\bf y},\omega_{N})\subset A
, then the potential

p^{g}({\bf x},\omega_{N}):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}g({\bf x}\cdot{\bf x}_{i}),\ \ \ {\bf x}\in S^{d},

attains its absolute maximum over
S^{d}
at the point
{\bf y}
.

If, in addition, the inequality in (
15
) is strict for every
t\in[-1,1]\setminus A
, then any point
{\bf z}\in S^{d}
such that
D({\bf z},\omega_{N})\not\subset A
is not a point of absolute maximum of
p^{g}(\ \!\cdot\ \!,\omega_{N})
on
S^{d}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
, since
\omega_{N}
is a spherical
L
-design, the sum
\sum_{i=1}^{N}q({\bf x}\cdot{\bf x}_{i})
is constant over
{\bf x}\in S^{d}
.
Since
D({\bf y},\omega_{N})\subset A
and
q
interpolates
g
on
A
, taking into account inequality (
15
), for arbitrary
{\bf x}\in S^{d}
, we have

p^{g}({\bf x},\omega_{N})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}g({\bf x}\cdot{\bf x}_{i})\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}q({\bf x}\cdot{\bf x}_{i})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}q({\bf y}\cdot{\bf x}_{i})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}g({\bf y}\cdot{\bf x}_{i})=p^{g}({\bf y},\omega_{N}),

(16)

which shows that
{\bf y}
is a point of absolute maximum.

Assume, in addition, that inequality (
15
) is strict for every
t\in[-1,1]\setminus A
. For any
{\bf z}\in S^{d}
with
D({\bf z},\omega_{N})\not\subset A
, there is an index
k
such that
{\bf z}\cdot{\bf x}_{k}\notin A
and, hence,
g({\bf z}\cdot{\bf x}_{k})<q({\bf z}\cdot{\bf x}_{k})
. Then the inequality in (
16
) is strict for
{\bf x}={\bf z}
; that is
{\bf z}
is not a point of absolute maximum of
p^{g}(\cdot,\omega_{N})
.
∎

Proof of Theorems
2.3
and

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
{\bf y}\in\omega_{N}
be arbitrary point and let
-1<\tau_{1}<\ldots<\tau_{m}<\tau_{m+1}=1
be the distinct values of dot products between points in
\omega_{N}
(we have
\tau_{1}>-1
in view of Lemma
3.4
). By assumption, the function
g(t)=f(2-2t)
has a convex derivative of order
2m-1
on
(-1,1)
. Let
Q\in\mathbb{P}_{{2m}}
be the polynomial such that
Q(\tau_{i})=g(\tau_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,m+1
, and
Q^{\prime}(\tau_{i})=g^{\prime}(\tau_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,m
. By Lemma
3.1
(with
\nu=1
) we obtain that
Q(t)\geq g(t)
,
t\in[-1,1]
. Since
\omega_{N}
is a
2m
-design and
D({\bf y},\omega_{N})\subset\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{m+1}\}
, by Lemma
3.5
the potential

p_{f}({\bf x},\omega_{N})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(\left|{\bf x}-{\bf x}_{i}\right|^{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}g({\bf x}\cdot{\bf x}_{i})=p^{g}({\bf x},\omega_{N})

attains its absolute maximum over
S^{d}
at
{\bf y}
.

If
f^{(2m-1)}
is strictly concave then
g^{(2m-1)}
is strictly convex. Lemma
3.1
implies that
Q(t)>g(t)
for

. Let
{\bf z}\in S^{d}\setminus\omega_{N}
be arbitrary. By Lemma

, the set

contains at least
m+1
distinct values and does not contain
\tau_{m+1}=1
(since

). Then
D({\bf z},\omega_{N})
is not contained in
\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{m+1}\}
. By Lemma
3.5
, the potential
p^{g}(\cdot,\omega_{N})=p_{f}(\cdot,\omega_{N})
does not attain its absolute maximum at
{\bf z}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Let
{\bf y}\in\omega_{N}
be arbitrary point and let
-1=\tau_{1}<\ldots<\tau_{m}<\tau_{m+1}=1
be distinct values of the dot product between vectors in
\omega_{N}
. By assumption, the function
g(t)=f(2-2t)
has a convex derivative of order
2m-2
on
(-1,1)
. Let
Q\in\mathbb{P}_{{2m-1}}
be the polynomial such that
Q(\tau_{i})=g(\tau_{i})
,

, and
Q^{\prime}(\tau_{i})=g^{\prime}(\tau_{i})
,

. By Lemma
3.1
(with

) we obtain that

,

. Since
\omega_{N}
is a
(2m-1)
-design, by Lemma
3.5
, the potential

p_{f}({\bf x},\omega_{N})=p^{g}({\bf x},\omega_{N})

attains its absolute maximum over

at
{\bf y}
.

If
f^{(2m-2)}
is strictly convex then so is

. Lemma

implies that

for

. Let
{\bf z}\in S^{d}\setminus\omega_{N}

with

contains at least

. It also does not contain

(if it did, then
-{\bf z}
would be in

5
Proof of Theorem
2.5

We use the following well-known result from
[
15
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
f:(0,4]\to\mathbb{R}
be completely monotone, and let
\mathcal{C}\subset S^{d}
be a
sharp configuration. If
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subset S^{d}
is any subset
satisfying
\#\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\#\mathcal{C}
, then

\sum\limits_{{\bf x},{\bf y}\ \!\!\in\ \!\!\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\ \!{\bf x}\neq{\bf y}}f\left(|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2}\right)\geq\sum\limits_{{\bf x},{\bf y}\ \!\!\in\ \!\!\mathcal{C},\ \!{\bf x}\neq{\bf y}}f\left(|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|^{2}\right).

(17)

If
f
is strictly completely monotone, then equality in (
17
) implies that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is also a sharp configuration and the same distances occur in
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Without a loss of generality, we can assume that
f
is completely monotone on
(0,4]
(otherwise we add an appropriate contstant to
f
). The derivative
f^{(2m-1)}
is concave on
(0,4)
and the derivative
f^{(2m-2)}
is convex on
(0,4)
. If
\overline{\omega}_{N}=\{{\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{N}\}
is strongly
m
-sharp for some
m\geq 1
, we apply Theorem
2.3
. If
\overline{\omega}_{N}
is an
m
-sharp antipodal configuration for some
m\geq 1
, we apply Theorem
2.4
. In both cases, we conclude that the potential
p_{f}(\cdot,\overline{\omega}_{N})
achieves its absolute maximum over
S^{d}
at points of
\overline{\omega}_{N}
. Let
\omega_{N}=\{{\bf y}_{1},\ldots,{\bf y}_{N}\}
be an arbitrary configuration (multiset) on
S^{d}
. By Theorem
5.1
,

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{j=1\atop j\neq i}^{N}f\left(\left|{\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{j}\right|^{2}\right)\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{j=1\atop j\neq i}^{N}f\left(\left|{\bf y}_{i}-{\bf y}_{j}\right|^{2}\right)

(18)

whenever
{\bf y}_{i}\neq{\bf y}_{j}
for
i\neq j
in
\omega_{N}
.
Inequality (
18
) still holds if
{\bf y}_{i}={\bf y}_{j}
for some
i\neq j
, since in this case there is a sequence of
N
-point configurations on
S^{d}
where points with different indices are distinct that converges to
\omega_{N}
.
For any fixed index
\ell
, we now have

\begin{split}Q_{f}(\overline{\omega}_{N};S^{d})&=\max\limits_{{\bf x}\in S^{d}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}f\left(\left|{\bf x}-{\bf x}_{i}\right|^{2}\right)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}f\left(\left|{\bf x}_{\ell}-{\bf x}_{i}\right|^{2}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}f\left(\left|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{i}\right|^{2}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{i=1\atop i\neq j}^{N}f\left(\left|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{i}\right|^{2}\right)+Nf(0)\right).\end{split}

From (
18
) we have

\begin{split}Q_{f}(\overline{\omega}_{N};S^{d})&\leq\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{i=1\atop i\neq j}^{N}f\left(\left|{\bf y}_{j}-{\bf y}_{i}\right|^{2}\right)+Nf(0)\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}f\left(\left|{\bf y}_{j}-{\bf y}_{i}\right|^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}p_{f}({\bf y}_{j},\omega_{N})\\
&\leq\max\limits_{{\bf x}\in S^{d}}p_{f}({\bf x},\omega_{N})=Q_{f}(\omega_{N};S^{d}),\end{split}

(19)

which completes the proof of (
8
).

Assume now that
f
is strictly completely monotone on
(0,4]
(after possibly adding a constant). Then equality in (
8
) implies equality between the energies of
\overline{\omega}_{N}
and
\omega_{N}
in (
18
). If
{\bf y}_{i}\neq{\bf y}_{j}
for all
i\neq j
, Theorem
5.1
immediately implies that
\omega_{N}
is sharp and the same distances occur in
\omega_{N}
and
\overline{\omega}_{N}
.

It remains to show that when
{\bf y}_{i}={\bf y}_{j}
for some
i\neq j
, the configuration
\omega_{N}
is not energy minimizing.
Denote by
-1\leq t_{1}<\ldots<t_{m}<1
the dot products that occur between distinct points in
\overline{\omega}_{N}
. The function
g(t):=f(2-2t)
is continuous on
[-1,1]
and strictly absolutely monotone on
[-1,1)
; that is,
g^{(k)}>0
on
(-1,1)
for every
k\geq 0
.
Let
p\in\mathbb{P}_{2m-1}
be the Hermite interpolating polynomial for
g
at
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}
. Then (cf., e.g.,
[
17
, Theorem 3.5.1]
)

g(t)\geq p(t),\ \ t\in[-1,1],

(20)

and the inequality in (
20
) is strict for
t\neq t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}
. Since one of the dot products occuring in
\omega_{N}
equals
1
and
g(1)>p(1)
, we have

\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}f(\left|{\bf y}_{i}-{\bf y}_{j}\right|^{2})=\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}g({\bf y}_{i}\cdot{\bf y}_{j})>\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}p({\bf y}_{i}\cdot{\bf y}_{j}).

(21)

Then (see the proof in
[
15
, Sections
2-6
]
)

\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}p({\bf y}_{i}\cdot{\bf y}_{j})\geq\sum\limits_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}{p({\bf x}_{i}\cdot{\bf x}_{j})}=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}{g({\bf x}_{i}\cdot{\bf x}_{j})}=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}{f(\left|{\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{j}\right|^{2})},

which combined with (
21
) shows that
\omega_{N}
is not energy minimizing. Then the first inequality in (
19
) is strict and, hence,
\omega_{N}
does not satisfy the equality in (
8
).
∎

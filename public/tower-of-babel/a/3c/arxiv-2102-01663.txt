Interpolated family of non group-like simple integral fusion rings of Lie type
Zhengwei Liu, Sebastien Palcoux, Yunxiang Ren

Abstract
. This paper is motivated by the quest of a non-group irreducible finite index depth 2 2 maximal subfactor. We compute the generic fusion rules of the Grothendieck ring of Rep ⁡ ( PSL ⁡ ( 2 , q ) ) \mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)) , q q prime-power, by applying a Verlinde-like formula on the generic character table. We then prove that this family of fusion rings ( ℛ q ) (\mathcal{R}_{q}) interpolates to all integers q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 , providing (when q q is not prime-power) the first example of infinite family of non group-like simple integral fusion rings. Furthermore, they pass all the known criteria of (unitary) categorification. This provides infinitely many serious candidates for solving the famous open problem of whether there exists an integral fusion category which is not weakly group-theoretical. We prove that a complex categorification (if any) of an interpolated fusion ring ℛ q \mathcal{R}_{q} (with q q non prime-power) cannot be braided, and so its Drinfeld center must be simple. In general, this paper proves that a non-pointed simple fusion category is non-braided if and only if its Drinfeld center is simple; and also that every simple integral fusion category is weakly group-theoretical if and only if every simple integral modular fusion category is pointed.

Next §

2.
Preliminaries

This section recalls some basic notions about fusion rings (we refer to
[
14
, Chapter 3]
). We then show that the data of a commutative fusion ring is equivalent to the data of a formal table satisfying some assumptions.

A
fusion ring

\mathcal{F}
of
rank

r
is defined by a finite set
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\}
called its
basis
, and
fusion rules
on
\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{B}
:

b_{i}b_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}N_{i,j}^{k}b_{k}

with
N_{i,j}^{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, satisfying axioms slightly extending the group axioms:

•

Associativity.

b_{i}(b_{j}b_{k})=(b_{i}b_{j})b_{k}
,

•

Neutral.

b_{1}b_{i}=b_{i}b_{1}=b_{i}
,

•

Dual.

\forall i\ \exists!i^{*}
with
N_{i,k}^{1}=N_{k,i}^{1}=\delta_{i^{*},k}
,

•

Frobenius reciprocity.

N_{ij}^{k}=N_{i^{*}k}^{j}=N_{kj^{*}}^{i}
.

The dual structure
*
induces an antihomomorphism of algebra, providing a structure of
*
-algebra on
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{B}
defined by
b_{i}^{*}=b_{i^{*}}
, and by Frobenius-Perron theorem, there is a unique
*
-homomorphism
d:\mathbb{C}\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{C}
with
d(\mathcal{B})\subset(0,\infty)
. The number
\mathrm{FPdim}(b_{i}):=d(b_{i})
is called the
Frobenius-Perron dimension
of
b_{i}
, and
\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{F}):=\sum_{i}d(b_{i})^{2}
is the Frobenius-Perron dimension of
\mathcal{F}
. The ordered sequence
(\mathrm{FPdim}(b))_{b\in\mathcal{B}}
is called the
type
of
\mathcal{F}
. The fusion ring
\mathcal{F}
is called of
Frobenius type
if
\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{F})}{\mathrm{FPdim}(b_{i})}
is an algebraic integer for all
i
. It is called
integral
if
\mathrm{FPdim}(b_{i})
is an integer for all
i
. It is
commutative
if
b_{i}b_{j}=b_{j}b_{i}
for all
i,j
. The fusion matrices
M_{i}:=(N_{i,j}^{k})_{k,j}
provides a representation of
\mathcal{F}
because
M_{i}M_{j}=\sum_{k}N_{ij}^{k}M_{k}
, moreover
M_{i}^{*}=M_{i^{*}}
and
\mathrm{FPdim}(b_{i})=\|M_{i}\|
. In the commutative case,
M_{i}M_{j}=M_{j}M_{i}
for all
i,j
, in particular
M_{i}M_{i}^{*}=M_{i}^{*}M_{i}
, in other words, the fusion matrices are pairwise commutative and normal, so are simultaneously diagonalizable.

Definition 2.1

(Eigentable)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a commutative fusion ring. Let
(M_{i})
be its fusion matrices, and let
(D_{i})=(diag(\lambda_{i,j}))
be their simultaneous diagonalization. The
eigentable
of
\mathcal{F}
is the table given by
(\lambda_{i,j})
.

Note that the eigentable of the Grothendieck ring of
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, with
G
finite group, is the character table of
G
.

Theorem 2.2

(Reconstruction)
.

Let
(\lambda_{i,j})
be a formal
r\times r
table. Consider

•

the space of functions from
\{1,\dots,r\}
to
\mathbb{C}
with some inner product
\langle f,g\rangle
,

•

the functions
(\lambda_{i})
defined by
\lambda_{i}(j)=\lambda_{i,j}
,

•

the pointwise multiplication
(fg)(i)=f(i)g(i)
,

•

the multiplication operator
M_{f}:g\mapsto fg
, and
M_{i}:=M_{\lambda_{i}}
,

and assume that

•

\langle\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
,

•

for all
i
there is
j
(automatically unique, denoted
i^{*}
) such that
M_{i}^{*}=M_{j}
,

•

M_{1}
is the identity,

•

N_{i,j}^{k}:=\langle\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}\rangle
is a nonnegative integer for all
i,j,k
.

Then
(N_{i,j}^{k})
are the structure constants of a commutative fusion ring and
(\lambda_{i,j})
is its eigentable. Moreover, every eigentable of a commutative fusion ring satisfies all the assumptions above.

Proof.

By assumption, the set
\{\lambda_{i}\ |\ i=1,\dots,r\}
is an orthonormal basis of the
r
-dimensional space it generates, so this space must be the
r
-dimensional space of functions from
\{1,\dots,r\}
to
\mathbb{C}
. It follows that for all
i,j
,
\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}
decomposes into a linear sum of
\lambda_{k}
, and so by construction

\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}=\sum_{k}N_{i,j}^{k}\lambda_{k}.

We can now show that
(N_{i,j}^{k})
are the structure constants of a commutative fusion ring:

•

Commutativity.
\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}=\lambda_{j}\lambda_{i}
because the multiplication is pointwise and
\mathbb{C}
is commutative,

•

Associativity.
(\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j})\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{i}(\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k})
because the multiplication is pointwise and
\mathbb{C}
is associative,

•

Frobenius reciprocity.
N_{i,j}^{k}=\langle M_{i}\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}\rangle=\langle\lambda_{j},M_{i}^{*}\lambda_{k}\rangle=\overline{\langle M_{i}^{*}\lambda_{k},\lambda_{j}\rangle}=\overline{N_{i^{*},k}^{j}}=N_{i^{*},k}^{j}=\dots=N_{k,j^{*}}^{i}
,

•

Neutral.
N_{1,i}^{k}=N_{i,1}^{k}=\langle\lambda_{i}\lambda_{1},\lambda_{k}\rangle=\langle\lambda_{i},\lambda_{k}\rangle=\delta_{i,k}
,

•

Dual.
N_{i,j}^{1}=N_{i^{*},1}^{j}=\delta_{i^{*},j}
.

Finally, consider the function
\delta_{i}:j\mapsto\delta_{i,j}
. Then
M_{i}\delta_{j}=\lambda_{i,j}\delta_{j}
. It follows that
(diag(\lambda_{i,j}))
is a simultaneous diagonalization of
(M_{i})
. So
(\lambda_{i,j})
is an eigentable of the commutative fusion ring defined by
(N_{i,j}^{k})
.

Reciprocally, let
(\lambda_{i,j})
be the eigentable of a commutative fusion ring. Define
(\lambda_{i})
as above and the inner product by
\langle\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
. Then all the assumptions in the statement of the theorem follows easily.
∎

Definition 2.3

(Formal codegrees)
.

The
formal codegrees
of a commutative fusion ring
\mathcal{F}
with eigentable
(\lambda_{i,j})
are

\mathfrak{c}_{j}:=\sum_{i}|\lambda_{i,j}|^{2}.

Theorem 2.4

(Schur orthogonality relations)
.

Following the notations of Definition
2.3
:

\sum_{i}\lambda_{i,j}\overline{\lambda_{i,j^{\prime}}}=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\mathfrak{c}_{j}\ \text{ and }\ \sum_{j}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{c}_{j}}\lambda_{i,j}\overline{\lambda_{i^{\prime},j}}=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Observe that the map
i\mapsto\lambda_{i,j}
induces a character on
\mathcal{F}
, so the first relation follows from
[
14
, Lemma 8.14.1]
. Next, let
U
be the matrix
(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}_{j}}}\overline{\lambda_{i,j}})
. The first relation means that
U^{*}U=id
, i.e.
U
is an isometry. But in the finite dimensional case, an isometry is unitary, so
UU^{*}=id
also, which means that:

\sum_{j}\frac{\overline{\lambda_{i,j}}}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}_{j}}}\frac{\lambda_{i^{\prime},j}}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}_{j}}}=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}.

The second relation follows.
∎

Corollary 2.5

(Inner product)
.

In Theorem
2.2
, the inner product can always be taken of the form

\langle f,g\rangle:=\sum_{s}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{c}_{s}}f(s)\overline{g(s)}.

Corollary 2.6

(Verlinde-like formula)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a commutative fusion ring with basis
(b_{i})
, fusion rules
(N_{i,j}^{k})
, eigentable
(\lambda_{i,j})
and formal codegrees
(\mathfrak{c}_{j})
. Then

N_{i,j}^{k}=\langle b_{i}b_{j},b_{k}\rangle=\sum_{s}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{c}_{s}}\lambda_{i,s}\lambda_{j,s}\overline{\lambda_{k,s}}=\sum_{s}\frac{\lambda_{i,s}\lambda_{j,s}\overline{\lambda_{k,s}}}{\sum_{l}|\lambda_{l,s}|^{2}}.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let
g
be an element of
G
, let
Cl(g)
be its conjugacy class and let
C_{G}(g)
be its centralizer. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem
|G|=|Cl(g)|\times|C_{G}(g)|
. By
[
24
, Theorem 2.18]
, the formal codegree is
|C_{G}(g)|
, whereas (by definition) the class size is
|Cl(g)|
.

Now the conjugacy classes form a partition of
G
, so
\sum_{i}|G|/\mathfrak{c}_{i}=|G|
, which provides the Egyptian fraction
\sum_{i}1/\mathfrak{c}_{i}=1
. It generalizes as follows:

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a commutative fusion ring and let
(\mathfrak{c}_{i})
be its formal codegrees. Then
\sum_{i}1/\mathfrak{c}_{i}=1
.

Proof.

Apply Corollary
2.5
to
f=g=\lambda_{1}
.
∎

In particular,
\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{F})=\mathfrak{c}_{1}=\sum_{i}\mathfrak{c}_{1}/\mathfrak{c}_{i}
, so we may see
(\mathfrak{c}_{1}/\mathfrak{c}_{i})
as
formal class sizes
(and see §
7.3
).

4.
Interpolated fusion rings

The generic character table of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)
as provided by GAP can be interpolated to
q
non prime-power. In this section, we show that this interpolated formal table is in fact the character table of an interpolated “
\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)
”, more precisely, we will prove that the application of the Verlinde-like formula (Corollary
2.6
) to these formal tables provides a family of fusion rings (
\mathcal{R}_{q}
) which are exactly the Grothendieck rings of
\mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))
when
q
is a prime-power, but otherwise, new non group-like simple integral fusion rings, whose eigentables are exactly these formal tables.

Theorem 4.1
.

The generic character table of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)
, interpolated to every integer
q\geq 2
, is the eigentable of an integral fusion ring, called the
interpolated fusion ring

\mathcal{R}_{q}
. Moreover, if
q\geq 4
then it is simple, and if
q
is not a prime-power then it is not group-like.

Proof.

The rules displayed in Theorems
4.3
,
4.6
,
4.7
(and computed in §
8
from Tables
3.1
,
3.2
,
3.3
) satisfy all the axioms of a fusion ring by applying Theorem
2.2
together with Propositions
8.12
,
8.35
,
8.57
(with the inner product as in Corollary
2.5
), and admit these tables as eigentables, again by Theorem
2.2
. To show that
\mathcal{R}_{q}
is simple if
q\geq 4
, just observe that every non-trivial simple object generates all ones, so that there is no proper non-trivial fusion subring. Finally, it is not group-like when
q
is not a prime-power by Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For
q
non prime-power, the interpolated fusion ring
\mathcal{R}_{q}
is not the Grothendieck ring of some
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
with
G
a finite group.

Proof.

Assume that for
q
non prime-power (and so
\geq 6
), there exists such a finite group
G
(obviously non-abelian). The interpolated fusion ring
\mathcal{R}_{q}
being simple, the Grothendieck ring of
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
and
G
itself must be so. So by the classification of finite simple groups,
G
must be of Lie type, alternating or sporadic.

Firstly, assume that
G
is of Lie type, so defined over a finite field of characterisitc
p
. By
[
23
]
, the character values for the Steinberg representation (complex, irreducible and non-trivial) on an element
g
equals, up to a sign, the order of a
p
-Sylow subgroup of the centralizer of
g
if
p
does not divide the order of
g
, and zero otherwise (in particular all integral). But it never fit with
\mathcal{R}_{q}
. Let us show why for
q
even (the odd case is similar). Consider Table
3.1
. The Steinberg representation can neither be the one of degree
1
(because it is non-trivial), nor the one of degree
q
(because the degree is a character value and
q
is not prime-power, whereas the order of
p
-Sylow is so). There remain the ones of degree
q\pm 1
. Assume it is of degree
q-1
(the proof for degree
q+1
is similar). Let
c\in\{1,\dots,\frac{q}{2}\}
be the parameter of the Steinberg character, and consider an element
g
with character value
-\zeta_{q+1}^{kc}-\zeta_{q+1}^{-kc}
. Its class size is
q(q-1)
, so the order of its centralizer is
q+1
, which is coprime with
q-1
, but the order of
g
divides the order of its centralizer and
q-1
must be a power of
p
(by assumption), so
p
does not divide the order of
g
. Then its character value is the order of a
p
-Sylow subgroup of the centralizer, up to a sign. It follows that
\zeta_{q+1}^{kc}+\zeta_{q+1}^{-kc}=2\cos(\frac{2\pi kc}{q+1})=\pm 1
, i.e.
\frac{kc}{q+1}\equiv\pm\frac{1}{3}
or
\pm\frac{1}{6}\mod 1
, for all
k\in\{1,\dots,\frac{q}{2}\}
. Take
k=1
and multiply by
3
, we get that
\frac{3c}{q+1}\equiv 0
or
\frac{1}{2}\mod 1
. But
q\geq 6
, so we can directly take
k=3
to get
\frac{3c}{q+1}\equiv\pm\frac{1}{3}
or
\pm\frac{1}{6}\mod 1
, contradiction.

Secondly, assume that
G=A_{n}
. It is well-known that for
n\geq 7
, there is a single non-trivial irreducible representation of smallest degree
n-1
(see
[
21
]
for more details about this folklore result). This contradicts with the type of
\mathcal{R}_{q}
where the smallest non-trivial degree is not unique. So
n=5,6
, but
A_{5}\simeq\mathrm{PSL}(2,4)
and
A_{6}\simeq\mathrm{PSL}(2,9)
, both of Lie type.

Finally, assume that
G
is sporadic. The following GAP computation

gap> L:=["M11","M12","M22","M23","M24","J1","J2","J3","J4","Co1","Co2","Co3",
"Fi22","Fi23","Fi24’","Suz","HS","McL","He","HN","Th","B","M","ON","Ly","Ru","Tits"];;

7

shows that the number of different character degrees is greater than
6
, whereas it is less than
6
for
\mathcal{R}_{q}
, contradiction.
∎

5.
General results on simple fusion categories

This section proves general results on simple fusion categories (always assumed over
\mathbb{C}
). It proves that a non-pointed simple fusion category is non-braided if and only if its Drinfeld center is simple. In particular, a simple fusion category Morita equivalent to a simple fusion category with a noncommutative Grothendieck ring, is non-braided and has a simple Drinfeld center. This section also provides a nice reformulation of the weakly group-theoretical conjecture in the simple case, namely, it proves that every simple integral fusion category is weakly group-theoretical if and only if every simple integral modular fusion category is pointed. We checked by computer assistance that every simple integral modular fusion category of rank up to
12
is pointed, see
[
1
]
. We refer to
[
14
]
for the notions of degenerate, braided, symmetric, modular fusion categories, Müger centralizer and Drinfeld center. The beginning of the proof of
[
17
, Theorem 9.12]
leads us to the following result. Its detailed proof is due to Victor Ostrik.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-pointed simple fusion category over
\mathbb{C}
. Then the Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
is simple if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is non-braided.

Proof.

One way is well-known: if
\mathcal{C}
is braided (without further assumption) then
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
has a subcategory equivalent to
\mathcal{C}
, so is not simple.
Assume now that
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
is not simple, so it contains a proper non-trivial subcategory
\mathcal{B}
. The image of
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathcal{C}
under the forgetful functor
F:\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}
is some subcategory
\mathcal{D}
of
\mathcal{C}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is simple, then
\mathcal{D}
is
\mathrm{Vec}
or
\mathcal{C}
.

If
\mathcal{D}=\mathrm{Vec}
then the functor
F_{|B}:B\to Vec
is central as a restriction of the central functor
F
, so it coincides with the braided tensor functor
\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{Z}(\mathrm{Vec})=\mathrm{Vec}
. Then
B
is symmetric (because
F
is faithful) and Tannakian by
[
14
, Definition 9.9.16]
. So
\mathcal{C}
has a non-trivial grading by
[
17
, Proposition 2.9]
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is simple, it must be pointed, contradiction.
Next, if
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{C}
, then the functor
\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
is surjective. So by
[
9
, Theorem 3.12]
, the functor

∎

The following result should be well-known to experts:

.

A simple spherical braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
(over

, for some finite simple group

Because

[

is symmetric, and by

]
and simplicity,

as braided fusion category (see

), with

∎

6.
No braided complex categorification

The interpolated fusion rings
(\mathcal{R}_{q})
pass all the known categorification criteria (checked in §
7
). This leads to wonder whether some ones can be categorified (with
q
non prime-power). We could naively expect that the F-symbols can be interpolated too, which is not clear, at least without modification. What is true is that the symmetric structure cannot be interpolated, so something else could be broken somewhere. What about the braiding structure? In this section we prove that a complex categorification (if any) of an interpolated fusion ring
\mathcal{R}_{q}
(with
q
non prime-power) cannot be braided, and so its Drinfeld center must be simple. The following result is partially due to an anonymous referee.

Corollary 6.1
.

For
q
non prime-power, the interpolated fusion ring
\mathcal{R}_{q}
admits no braided complex categorification.

Proof.

Assume that it admits a braided complex categorification
\mathcal{C}
, then we can apply Corollary
5.4
, and by Lemma
4.2
,
\mathcal{C}
must be modular. Thus by Theorem
5.9
, pseudo-unitarity and integrality, for every simple object
x
then
\mathrm{FPdim}(x)^{2}
divides
\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
; but
\mathrm{FPdim}(x_{q,1})=q
whereas
\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=q(q^{2}-1)
or
q(q^{2}-1)/2
, contradiction.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

Assume the existence of
q
non prime-power such that
\mathcal{R}_{q}
admits a complex categorification
\mathcal{C}_{q}
. Then by Corollary
6.1
and Theorem
5.1
, the Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{q})
is simple. Contrariwise, if
q
is a prime-power then
\mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,q))
is braided so its Drinfeld center is not simple.

In this section we check seven categorification criteria on the interpolated fusion rings

7.1.
Schur product criterion

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a commutative fusion ring with eigentable
(\lambda_{i,j})
and
\lambda_{i,1}=\max_{j}(|\lambda_{i,j}|)
. Here is the commutative Schur product criterion:

Theorem 7.1

(Corollary 8.5 in
[
38
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{F}
admits a unitary categorification then for all triple
(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})
we have

\sum_{i}\frac{\lambda_{i,j_{1}}\lambda_{i,j_{2}}\lambda_{i,j_{3}}}{\lambda_{i,1}}\geq 0.

Note that Theorem
7.1
is the corollary of a (less tractable) noncommutative version
[
38
, Proposition 8.3]
, which is an application of the quantum Schur product theorem on subfactors
[
35
, Theorem 4.1]
. See also a new proof in
[
18
]
.

Lemma 7.2
.

If
\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{i}=0
and
|a_{n}|\geq 1
then
|x|<1+\max_{i}|a_{i}|
.

Proof.

We can assume
|x|>1
(otherwise we are done). Let
m:=\max_{i}|a_{i}|
. We have
a_{n}x^{n}=-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}x^{i}
, so
|a_{n}x^{n}|=|\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}x^{i}|
and then by triangle inequality

|a_{n}||x|^{n}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}|a_{i}||x|^{i}\leq m\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}|x|^{i}=m\frac{|x|^{n}-1}{|x|-1}<m\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x|-1},

but
|a_{n}|\geq 1
, thus

|x|^{n}<\frac{m}{|a_{n}|}\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x|-1}\leq m\frac{|x|^{n}}{|x|-1},

It follows that
1<\frac{m}{|x|-1}
, so
|x|-1<m
and
|x|<m+1
.
∎

Proposition 7.3
.

All the fusion rings of the interpolated family (
\mathcal{R}_{q}
) pass Schur product criterion.

Proof.

According to the calculations in §
8
and the fact that the variables
k
and

play the same role in the tables of §

, it is clear that we are reduced to consider rational functions and to show that they are non-negative. Let first compute one example of such rational funtion, say for
q
even and

given by the last column of Table
3.1
(then we will see a general argument). As for the proof of Lemma
8.1
:

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{c=1}^{q/2}\prod_{i=1}^{3}(-\zeta_{q+1}^{k_{i}c}-\zeta_{q+1}^{-k_{i}c})-\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{q+1}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\epsilon_{i}=\pm 1}\sum_{c=1}^{q}(\zeta_{q+1}^{\sum_{i=1}^{3}\epsilon_{i}k_{i}})^{c}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{q+1}\frac{1}{2}(-1)=\frac{2q^{2}+q-2}{2q(q+1)}&\mbox{if}&\gcd(q+1,\sum_{i=1}^{3}\epsilon_{i}k_{i})<q+1\\
1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{q+1}\frac{1}{2}(q)=\frac{q^{2}-2}{2q(q+1)}&\mbox{else}&\end{array}\right.

In general, we already know that these rational functions are non-negative for

such that

and

that the polynomial

7.6.
Zero spectrum criterion

Here is a general categorification criterion over every field. It corresponds to the existence of a pentagon equation of the form
xy=0
, with

(see examples as
\mathcal{F}_{660}
in
[
36
]

Theorem 7.13

(see
[
36
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fusion ring with fusion rules
(N_{i,j}^{k})
. If there are indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{9}
, such that

(1)

\displaystyle N_{i_{4},i_{1}}^{i_{6}},\ N_{i_{5},i_{4}}^{i_{2}},\ N_{i_{5},i_{6}}^{i_{3}},

\displaystyle\ N_{i_{7},i_{9}}^{i_{1}},\ N_{i_{2},i_{7}}^{i_{8}},\ N_{i_{8},i_{9}}^{i_{3}}\neq 0,

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{k}N_{i_{4},i_{7}}^{k}N_{i_{5}^{*},i_{8}}^{k}N_{i_{6},i_{9}^{*}}^{k}

\displaystyle=0,

(3)

\displaystyle N_{i_{2},i_{1}}^{i_{3}}

\displaystyle=1,

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{k}N_{i_{5},i_{4}}^{k}N_{i_{3},i_{1}^{*}}^{k}

\displaystyle=1~\text{or}~\sum_{k}N_{i_{2},i_{4}^{*}}^{k}N_{i_{3},i_{6}^{*}}^{k}=1~\text{or}~\sum_{k}N_{i_{5}^{*},i_{2}}^{k}N_{i_{6},i_{1}^{*}}^{k}=1,

\displaystyle\sum_{k}N_{i_{2},i_{7}}^{k}N_{i_{3},i_{9}^{*}}^{k}

\displaystyle=1~\text{or}~\sum_{k}N_{i_{8},i_{7}^{*}}^{k}N_{i_{3},i_{1}^{*}}^{k}=1~\text{or}~\sum_{k}N_{i_{2}^{*},i_{8}}^{k}N_{i_{1},i_{9}^{*}}^{k}=1.

then
\mathcal{F}
cannot be categorified at all, i.e. it is not the Grothendieck ring of a fusion category, over any field.

Let us take the index
1
for the trivial object.

Lemma 7.14
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fusion ring, and assume the existence of indices

such that (
1
) and (
2
) hold. Then

, for all

Proof.

Assume that there is

. We will reach a contradiction. Let write the details for

(the other cases are similar): assume that

, by (

) and the neutral axiom of fusion ring,

and

; in addition,

or

).
∎

.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fusion ring such that there is

for all

. Then

9.2.
Lübeck’s trick

Here is a trick (given by Frank Lübeck) to display the character table directly on GAP for
q
not necessarily a prime-power, by removing the prime-power restriction as follows:

gap> gt02 := CharacterTableFromLibrary("SL2even");;
gap> gt02.domain := function(q) return q mod 2 = 0; end;;
gap> gt1 := CharacterTableFromLibrary("PSL2even");;
gap> gt1.domain := function(q) return q mod 4 = 1; end;;
gap> gt3 := CharacterTableFromLibrary("PSL2odd");;
gap> gt3.domain := function(q) return q mod 4 = 3; end;;

Then we can display as usual for every integer
q
using the following command:

gap> Display(CharacterTable("PSL",2,q));

In addition, we can get the fusion ring for every
given
integer
q
by applying the Schur orthogonality relations with the following function:

gap> FusionPSL2:=function(q)
> Ch:=CharacterTableWithSortedCharacters(CharacterTable("PSL",2,q));;
> irr:=Irr(Ch);; n:=Size(irr);;
> return List([1..n],i->List([1..n],j->List([1..n],k->ScalarProduct(irr[i]*irr[j],irr[k]))));
> end;;

Fluid-plate interaction under periodic forcing
Aday Celik 1 and Mads Kyed 2,3 1 Fachbereich Mathematik Technische Universität Darmstadt, Germany celik@mathematik.tu-darmstadt.de 2 Faculty of Science and Engineering Waseda University, Japan kyed@aoni.waseda.jp 3 Fachbereich Maschinenbau Flensburg University of Applied Sciences, Germany mads.kyed@hs-flensburg.de

Abstract
The motion of a thin elastic plate interacting with a viscous fluid is investigated. A periodic force acting on the plate is considered, which in a setting without damping could lead to a resonant response. The interaction with the viscous fluid provides a damping mechanism due to the energy dissipation in the fluid. Moreover, an internal damping mechanism in the plate is introduced. In this setting, we show that the periodic forcing leads to a time-periodic (non-resonant) solution. We employ the Navier-Stokes and the Kirchhoff-Love plate equation in a periodic cell structure to model the motion of the viscous fluid and the elastic plate, respectively. Maximal L q \mathrm{L}^{q} regularity for the linearized system is established in a framework of time-periodic function spaces. Existence of a solution to the fully nonlinear system is subsequently shown with a fixed-point argument.

Introduction

The interaction of an incompressible viscous fluid with a thin elastic structure is investigated.
We consider a three-dimensional fluid filled container with an elastic plate as part of its boundary.
Our aim is to obtain a good understanding of the damping effects on the elastic structure in such a setting.
To this end, we introduce a periodic force, which in a setting without damping could lead to onset of resonance in the structure.
We develop a framework in which the damping effects under periodic forcing can be quantified, and employ the framework to show
how damping prevents resonance when the periodic force is sufficiently restricted in magnitude.

In order to simplify the mathematical analysis, we consider a simple geometry in which the container’s stress free configuration is a cuboid with its bottom face an elastic plate.
We assume the motion of the viscous fluid is governed by the Navier-Stokes equations, and the motion of the plate by the Kirchoff-Love plate equations.
To further simplify the analysis, we impose periodic boundary conditions on the lateral faces of the cube.
In this setting we can study the linearized equations of motion directly in Fourier space, where we are able to quantify the damping effects in a precise manner.
From this characterization we are able to establish time-periodic a priori estimates, which we utilize to show that a solution to the nonlinear coupled fluid-structure system is necessarily time-periodic, that is, non-resonant, when the time-periodic force is sufficiently small.

In order to show the full mathematical potential of our technique, we have chosen to include an additional damping term in the structure equations that regularizes the system and enables us to establish comprehensive
\mathrm{L}^{q}
estimates of maximal regularity type. Without this internal damping, our approach only yields a priori estimates in an
\mathrm{L}^{2}
framework. We do not go further into this case in the current article. Further research is also required to extend our results to include more physically reasonable boundary conditions and geometries.

We denote by

\displaystyle\omega:=(0,L)\times(0,L)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

a square that represents a flat elastic plate in its stress free configuration, and consider the cuboid

\displaystyle\Omega\coloneqq\omega\times(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

as container for the viscous fluid. More precisely, when no outer forces act on the system, the fluid filled cuboid container
\Omega
with an elastic bottom face represents the stress free configuration of the fluid-structure system. As customary in fluid-structure problems, we utilize the stress free configuration
\Omega
as the
reference configuration
and will refer to it as such.

Two
{\mathcal{T}}
-time-periodic outer body forces are introduced. A force

\displaystyle f:\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad f(t+{\mathcal{T}},\cdot)=f(t,\cdot)

(1.1)

that acts on the fluid, and a force

\displaystyle h:\mathbb{R}\times\omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad h(t+{\mathcal{T}},\cdot)=f(t,\cdot)

(1.2)

that acts in normal direction on the plate (tangential forces are neglected in the Kirchoff-Love plate model). Here,
\mathbb{R}
denotes the time axis. We consider generic forces formulated as functions on the reference configuration.

With outer forces acting on the system, the dynamics of the elastic structure is described by the displacement

\displaystyle\eta:\mathbb{R}\times\omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

of the plate in normal direction
-e_{3}
. The
current configuration
of the container at time
t
is then

\displaystyle\Omega_{\eta}(t)\coloneqq\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,\lvert\,x^{\prime}\in\omega,\,-\eta(t,x^{\prime})<x_{3}<1\}.

A canonical mapping that takes the reference configuration into the current configuration at time
t
is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\eta}(t)\colon\Omega\to\Omega_{\eta}(t),\qquad x\mapsto\left(x^{\prime},x_{3}-(1-x_{3})\eta(t,x^{\prime})\right).

(1.3)

Provided
\eta
is sufficiently small,
\varphi_{\eta}(t)
is a bijection with inverse given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\eta}^{-1}(t)\colon\Omega_{\eta}(t)\to\Omega,\qquad x\mapsto\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x_{3}+\eta(t,x^{\prime})}{1+\eta(t,x^{\prime})}\right).

The dynamics of the incompressible viscous fluid flow is described in terms of its Eulerian velocity field and pressure term

\displaystyle u:\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,\{t\}\times\Omega_{\eta}(t)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3},\qquad\qquad p:\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,\{t\}\times\Omega_{\eta}(t)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

respectively. We assume the fluid is Newtonian with Cauchy stress tensor given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}(u,p):=\mu_{f}\big(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\top}\big)-pI.

Letting
\nu_{t}
denote the outer normal of the container’s current configuration
\Omega_{\eta}(t)
,
we can thus express the surface force exerted by the fluid in normal direction
-e_{3}
on the structure in reference configuration coordinates as

\displaystyle e_{3}\cdot\big((\mathrm{T}(u,p)\nu_{t})\circ\varphi_{\eta}\big)_{|x_{3}=0}.

The Kirchoff-Love plate equation (with damping) govern the motion of the elastic structure:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\eta+\Delta^{\prime\,2}\eta-\mu_{s}\Delta^{\prime}\partial_{t}\eta

\displaystyle=h+e_{3}\cdot\big((\mathrm{T}(u,p)\nu_{t})\circ\varphi_{\eta}\big)_{|x_{3}=0}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}\times\omega.

(1.4)

The term
\mu_{s}\Delta^{\prime}\partial_{t}\eta
with
\mu_{s}>0
introduces an internal Kelvin-Voigt type damping in the structure. We use
\Delta^{\prime}
to denote the Laplacian with respect to the coordinates of the plate
\omega
.

The Navier-Stokes equations govern the motion of the incompressible viscous fluid:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\mu_{f}\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p&=f_{\eta}&&\text{in }\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,\{t\}\times\Omega_{\eta}(t),\\

the kinematic viscosity constant.

We assume a no-slip boundary condition for the fluid velocity on both the top and bottom (elastic) face of the container:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &u=0&&\text{on }\mathbb{R}\times\omega\times\{1\},\\
&u\circ\varphi_{\eta}=-\partial_{t}\eta\,e_{3}&&\text{on }\mathbb{R}\times\omega\times\{0\}.\end{aligned}

(1.6)

We assume periodic boundary conditions on the lateral faces of the container, that is,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\eta(\cdot,x_{1}+L,\cdot)=\eta(\cdot,x_{1},\cdot),&&\eta(\cdot,\cdot,x_{2}+L)=\eta(\cdot,\cdot,x_{2}),\\
&u(\cdot,x_{1}+L,\cdot,\cdot)=u(\cdot,x_{1},\cdot,\cdot),&&u(\cdot,\cdot,x_{2}+L,\cdot)=u(\cdot,\cdot,x_{2},\cdot).\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\eta(t,x^{\prime})\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{\prime}=0.

\displaystyle\lVert u_{k}\rVert_{1,2}+\lVert\eta_{k}\rVert_{2,2}+\lVert k\eta_{k}\rVert_{1,2}\leq C_{4}\big(\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{2}+\lVert h_{k}\rVert_{2}\big)

Proof.

The sesquilinear form

∎

\mathrm{L}^{q}
estimates. To this end, we need the following uniqueness property:

Lemma 3.4

(Uniqueness)

Let

A solution to

\mathcal{X}^{q}({\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega)

Proof.

It suffices to consider a solution

to (

) with homogeneous right-hand side

We employ a duality argument. Let

By the same argument that leads to Lemma

, existence of a solution
(w,\pi,\zeta)\in\mathcal{X}^{q^{\prime}}_{\bot}({\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega)
to the dual of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}^{2}\zeta+\Delta^{\prime\,2}\zeta+\Delta^{\prime}\partial_{t}\zeta&=\psi-e_{3}\cdot\big(\mathrm{T}(w,\pi)e_{3}\big)_{|x_{3}=0}&&\text{in }{\mathbb{T}}\times\mathbb{T}_{0}^{2},\\

\Div w&=0&&\text{in }{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega,\\
w(t,x^{\prime},0)&=-\partial_{t}\zeta(t,x^{\prime})e_{3}&&\text{on }{\mathbb{T}}\times\mathbb{T}_{0}^{2},\\

Proof.

Remark 3.6
.

As a key observation in the proof above, we recognize, quantified in the formula (
3.20
), the damping effect both the viscous fluid and the internal damping has on the displacement
\eta
of the plate.
Indeed, without the coupling of the viscous fluid and the introduction of internal damping the representation formula would read

\displaystyle\eta=\mathscr{F}^{-1}_{{\mathbb{T}}\times\mathbb{T}_{0}^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{\big(1-\delta_{\frac{2\pi}{{\mathcal{T}}}\mathbb{Z}}(k)\big)\big(1-\delta_{(\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z})^{2}}(\xi^{\prime})\big)}{{\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert}^{4}-k^{2}}\widehat{F}\Bigg].

(3.23)

The additional terms

\displaystyle-\frac{k^{2}}{{\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert}}+ik\bigg({\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert}+\sqrt{{\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert}^{2}+ik}\bigg)\quad\text{and }\quad ik{\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert}^{2}

in the denominator in (

Observe that the multiplier in (
3.23

{\lvert(k,\xi^{\prime})\rvert}\rightarrow\infty

The decay rate of the multiplier in (
3.20
) as
{\lvert(k,\xi^{\prime})\rvert}\rightarrow\infty
dictates the order of the a priori estimates that can be established
for
\eta
in terms of the data, and can thus be interpreted as a quantification of the damping effects.

\displaystyle\qquad+\lVert\tilde{\mathrm{R}}_{d}\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{T}};\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega))\cap\mathrm{W}^{1,q}({\mathbb{T}};\mathrm{\dot{W}}^{-1,q}(\Omega))}+\lVert h\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{T}}\times\mathbb{T}_{0}^{2})}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}(\varepsilon+r^{2}+r^{3}).

Choosing
r=\sqrt{\varepsilon}
and
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, we have

c_{1}(\varepsilon+r^{2}+r^{3})\leq r
, in which case
\mathcal{F}
becomes a self-mapping
on
B_{r}
.
To complete the proof, it remains to show that
\mathcal{F}
is a contraction.
For this purpose we utilize Theorem
4.1
with
m=2
,
M_{t}=1
,
M_{x}=0
,
\alpha=1
and subsequently
m=2
,
M_{t}=0
,
M_{x}=0
,
\alpha=2
to deduce

\displaystyle\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}\times\mathbb{T}_{0}^{2})}+\lVert\partial_{t}\eta\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}\times\mathbb{T}_{0}^{2})}\leq c_{2}\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathrm{W}^{2,4,q}({\mathbb{T}}\times\mathbb{T}_{0}^{2})}\leq c_{2}\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathrm{S}^{q}({\mathbb{T}}\times\mathbb{T}_{0}^{2})}.

We thus conclude from (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle\Bigl\lVert\nabla u\partial_{t}\eta\frac{1}{1+\eta}-\nabla v\partial_{t}\zeta\frac{1}{1+\zeta}\Bigr\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega)}\leq c_{3}\varepsilon\lVert(u,p,\eta)-(v,\mathfrak{p},\zeta)\rVert_{\mathcal{X}^{q}},

and similarly

\displaystyle\Bigl\lVert\frac{\partial_{x_{3}}p}{1+\eta}\begin{pmatrix}\rho\nabla^{\prime}\eta\\
-\eta\end{pmatrix}-\frac{\partial_{x_{3}}\mathfrak{p}}{1+\zeta}\begin{pmatrix}\rho\nabla^{\prime}\zeta\\
-\zeta\end{pmatrix}\Bigr\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega)}\leq c_{4}\varepsilon\lVert(u,p,\eta)-(v,\mathfrak{p},\zeta)\rVert_{\mathcal{X}^{q}}.

The additional terms in

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\mathrm{R}}_{f}(u,p,\eta)-\tilde{\mathrm{R}}_{f}(v,\mathfrak{p},\zeta)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega)}\leq c_{5}\varepsilon\lVert(u,p,\eta)-(v,\mathfrak{p},\zeta)\rVert_{\mathcal{X}^{q}}.

A straightforward calculation yields

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\mathrm{R}}_{d}(u,p,\eta)-\tilde{\mathrm{R}}_{d}(v,\mathfrak{p},\zeta)\rVert_{\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{T}};\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega))\cap\mathrm{W}^{1,q}({\mathbb{T}};\mathrm{\dot{W}}^{-1,q}(\Omega))}\leq c_{6}\varepsilon\lVert(u,p,\eta)-(v,\mathfrak{p},\zeta)\rVert_{\mathcal{X}^{q}}.

Collecting the estimates deduced for

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{F}(u,p,\eta)-\mathcal{F}(v,\mathfrak{p},\zeta)\rVert_{\mathcal{X}^{q}}\leq c_{8}\varepsilon\lVert(u,p,\eta)-(v,\mathfrak{p},\zeta)\rVert_{\mathcal{X}^{q}}.

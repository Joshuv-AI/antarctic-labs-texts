Abstract
Given a class of (undirected) graphs 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , we say that a Feedback Arc Set (FAS for short) F F is a 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -FAS if the graph induced by the edges of F F (forgetting their orientations) belongs to 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . We show that deciding if a tournament has a 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -FAS is NP -complete when 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is the class of all forests. We are motivated by connections between 𝒞 \mathcal{C} - FAS and structural parameters of tournaments, such as the dichromatic number, the clique number of tournaments, and the strong Erdős-Hajnal property.

Let us motivate the

Given a tournament

For example, a tournament version

Let

Conversely, by taking an ordering built from a

\dic(T)=\min\,\big\{\chi(T^{\prec}):\mbox{$\prec$ is a total order of $V(D)$}\big\}

(1)

Now, it is straightforward that a tournament

This problem has been proved to be NP-complete for every

Inspired by (

A tournament

We think the

-

As explained in the introduction, when

.

4.1
[

]
.

Given a tournament

, what is the complexity of deciding if
T
has a triangle-free FAS?

On the same flavour, Aboulker et al.
[

]
proved that an approximation version of the above problem is polynomial. More precisely, they prove that there is a constant

such that one can decide in poly-time if, given a tournament
T
,
\diomega(T)\geq 3
, or
\diomega(T)\leq c
. It is thus natural to conjecture the following.

There a function

In
[

]
, it is proved that a tournament has a forest-ordering if and only if it has a tree-ordering. (For the curious reader, the proof is easy: simply start from a forest-ordering, runs through vertices from left to right, and when two consecutive vertices

The

The

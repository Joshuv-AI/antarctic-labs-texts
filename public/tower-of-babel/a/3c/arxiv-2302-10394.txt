Introduction

During the last years, the subject of variable exponent differential equations and problems with nonstandard growth structure
has drawn a lot of attention in the mathematical world (e.g.

), motivated by their abstract mathematical formulations, extensions, and beauty,
but even more, by their multiple types of applications. Among the list of applications,
we have elastic materials

]
, image restoration

,

,

,

]
, electrorheological fluids
[

,

[

]
, mathematical biology
[

]
, dielectric breakdown, and electrical
resistivity and polycrystal plasticity
[

]
(among others). Thus one can say that allowing variations in an exponent
p
of a function space
or a differential equation produce a big wave of interest, which keeps growing as the theory continues being mastered and applied to particular models.

Adding anisotropy to the exponents opens the opportunity for an increase in the scale of applications, but it also adds major complications,
and these models are less known, and less investigated. Here we treat problems that are cast in the framework of the anisotropic nonstandard structures
given by vectorial functions

\overset{\rightarrow}{r}:D\rightarrow[1,\infty]^{d};\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\overset{\rightarrow}{r}(\cdot):=\left(r_{1}(\cdot),r_{2}(\cdot),\dots,r_{d}(\cdot)\right),

where
r_{i}:D\rightarrow[1,\infty]
are measurable functions (
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
). We then denote

r_{M}(\cdot)=\max\{r_{1}(\cdot),\ldots,r_{d}(\cdot)\},\quad r_{m}(\cdot)=\min\{r_{1}(\cdot),\ldots,r_{d}(\cdot)\}.

(1.1)

Before we amplify the discussion about anisotropic function spaces and differentia equations and their importance in the field, let us
formulate explicitly the problem under consideration in this paper.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
be a bounded domain with the (anisotropic)
W^{1,\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)}
-extension property
(see Definition
1
), and with Lipschitz boundary

\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
, for
N\geq 3
, and let
p_{i}(\cdot),\,q_{j}(\cdot)
be
Lipschitz continuous functions over
\overline{\Omega}
, such that
1<p^{-}_{i}:=\textrm{ess}\displaystyle\inf_{\overline{\Omega}}p_{i}(x)\leq p^{+}_{i}:=\textrm{ess}\displaystyle\sup_{\overline{\Omega}}p_{i}(x)<\infty
and
1<q^{-}_{j}:=\textrm{ess}\displaystyle\inf_{\overline{\Omega}}q_{j}(x)\leq q^{+}_{j}:=\textrm{ess}\displaystyle\sup_{\overline{\Omega}}q_{j}(x)<\infty
,
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
. Since
\Omega
is bounded and has Lipschitz boundary, we stress that
\Gamma

becomes a
(N-1)
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold; this plays a crucial role.
We are concerned with the solvability of the generalized quasi-linear parabolic equation formally given by

\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}\,=\,\Delta_{\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)}u(t,x)-\alpha|u(t,x)|^{p_{M}(\cdot)-2}u(t,x),\,\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{for each}\,\,\,(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\Omega,

(1.2)

with an initial condition
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, and dynamical Wentzell boundary conditions

\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}|_{{}_{\Gamma}}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\partial_{x_{i}}u(t,x)|^{p_{i}(\cdot)-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u(t,x)\;\nu_{i}-\Delta_{{}_{\overset{\rightarrow}{q}(\cdot),\Gamma}}u(t,x)+\beta|u(t,x)|^{q_{M}(\cdot)-2}u(t,x)\,=\,0,\,\,\,\,\,(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\Gamma,

(1.3)

for
\alpha\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\beta\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
with
\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Omega}\alpha(x)\geq\alpha_{0}

and
\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Gamma}\beta(x)\geq\beta_{0}
for some constants
\alpha_{0},\,\beta_{0}>0
, for

\nu_{i}
the
i
-th component of the outward unit normal at
\Gamma
, where

\Delta_{\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)}u:=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}\left(|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p_{i}(\cdot)-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u\right)

denotes the (anisotropic)
\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)
-Laplace operator,
and

\Delta_{{}_{\overset{\rightarrow}{q}(\cdot),\Gamma}}u:=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\displaystyle\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}\left(|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}u|^{q_{j}(\cdot)-2}\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}u\right)

represents the (anisotropic)

\overset{\rightarrow}{q}(\cdot)
-Laplace-Beltrami operator, where

\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}u
denotes the directional derivative of
u
along the tangential directions
\tau_{j}
at each point on
\Gamma
.

The above model equation (
1.2
) with boundary conditions (
1.3
) is regarded as an
anisotropic partial differential equation
, since
the growth of the operator with respect to each
i
-th partial derivative is governed by
different powers (see for example
[
4
, Chapter 1, Section 4.2]
). As pointed out in
[
14
]
,
very often, in many relevant applications, the materials and phenomena have an important

anisotropy
, presenting different properties in different directions, in contrast to the more usual isotropy property. Anisotropy leads to
mathematical models presenting peculiar constitutive laws which correspond to material’s physical or mechanical
properties with different behaviour according the directions. The spectrum of fields in which such situations arise is
broad; it integrates areas, such as: computer science (e.g. image processing), physics (e.g. atmospheric radiative transfer), chemistry (e.g.
materials science
[
52
]
), geophysics and geology, engineering (e.g. wastewater reactors
[
22
]
), or neuroscience (among others).
Commonly, this anisotropy comes as a diffusion operator described by three principal different directional
coefficients. This is the case, for example, of thermal and electrical conductivity in heterogeneous
media, or of the study of crystals (see
[
36
]
). Moreover, many of the recent innovations
in the field of electroceramics have exploited the anisotropy of nonlinearities modelling different material properties
such as electric field, mechanical stress or temperature (see for example
[
52
, Chapter 15]
). We also mention here that it is
well-known that homogenization techniques generate anisotropic diffusion limit problems (e.g.
[
48
,
69
]
).

The study of anisotropic boundary value problems involving variable exponents is less known in the mathematical community.
Some existence and regularity results for particular classes of Neumann-type or Robin-type anisotropic elliptic problem (with variable exponents) on
smooth domains can be found in
[
12
,
30
,
33
,
44
,
45
]
. However, the realization of the corresponding
parabolic problem with nonstandard anisotropic structure has been considered mainly for the Dirichlet problem; the realization
of a Robin problem involving nonstandard anisotropic structure has been developed in
[
13
]
, but the dynamical
problem has not been investigated, up to the present time.

On the other hand, Wentzell boundary conditions (also referred as pure Wentzell boundary conditions), are viewed as a
kind of “dynamical” boundary condition, and the physical interpretation
of this kind of boundary condition was given by Goldstein
[
41
]
. Moreover, Wentzell boundary conditions arise in many applications, such as
phase-transition phenomena, fluid diffusion, heat flow subject to nonlinear cooling on the boundary, suspended transport energy,
fermentation, population dynamics, and climatology, among many others (see
[
6
,
23
,
31
,
38
,
39
,
54
]

and the references therein), and have been investigated and used by several authors
(e.g.
[
3
,
55
,
62
,
66
,
67
]
, among others). However, the realization of Wentzell-type problems
of nonstandard growth structure is a new subject, first considered by Vélez - Santiago
[
65
]
.

In the present paper, we deal with a general class of time-dependent Wentzell-type evolution equations involving the anisotropic Laplace operator

\Delta_{\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)}
in the interior of the region, while its boundary is governed by a second-order anisotropic Laplace-Beltrami
operator
\Delta_{{}_{\overset{\rightarrow}{q}(\cdot),\Gamma}}
. Our goal consists in establishing the well-posedness and ultracontractivity
bounds for the parabolic problem (
1.2
) with dynamical boundary conditions
(
1.3
). In fact, we first show that for each measurable function
r(\cdot)
over
\overline{\Omega}
with
1\leq r^{-}\leq r^{+}<\infty
,
the subdifferential associated with the Wentzell functional
\Phi_{\sigma}
(see Section
3
)
generates an order-preserving non-expansive nonlinear
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{T_{\sigma}(t)\}

over
\mathbb{X\!}^{\,r(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega}):=L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega)\times L^{r(\cdot)}(\Gamma)
. Consequently, for each

\mathbf{u}_{0}:=(u_{0},u_{0}|_{{}_{\Gamma}})\in\mathbb{X\!}^{\,r(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega})
, the function
\mathbf{u}(t):=T_{\sigma}(t)\mathbf{u}_{0}

turns out to be the unique strong solution to problem (
1.2
) with boundary conditions (
1.3
). Then, under the additional assumption

\min\{p^{-}_{m},q^{-}_{m}\}\geq 2
, we prove that the nonlinear Wentzell semigroup
\{T_{\sigma}(t)\}
satisfies a Hölder-type ultracontractivity
property, in the sense that there exist constants
C_{1},\,C_{2},\,\kappa>0
, and
\gamma\in(0,1]
, such that

|\|T_{\sigma}(t)\mathbf{u}_{0}-T_{\sigma}(t)\mathbf{v}_{0}\||_{{}_{\infty}}\,\leq\,C_{1}\,e^{C_{2}t}t^{-\kappa}|\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{v}_{0}\||^{\gamma}_{{}_{r(\cdot),s(\cdot)}}

(1.4)

for each
\mathbf{u}_{0},\,\mathbf{v}_{0}\in\mathbb{X\!}^{\,r(\cdot),s(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega})
and for all
t>0
, where

r:\Omega\rightarrow[2,\infty]
and
s:\Gamma\rightarrow[2,\infty]
are measurable functions. The constants
C_{1},\,C_{2}
,
\kappa
, and
\gamma
,
have been explicitly calculated. In particular, the ultracontractivity property shows that solutions of problem (
1.2
) with
boundary conditions (
1.3
) are globally bounded.

The above general model equation brings several important novelties. First, problems
involving the anisotropic Laplace-Beltrami operator correspond to a completely new subject in the field. In fact, the appearance of such anisotropic operator
is only known in the recent work
[
24
]
(for an elliptic boundary value problem), but it has never appeared in a parabolic-type
differential equation. The framework in the present paper not only motivates the study of anisotropic problems over the interior of a domain,
but also the realization of anisotropic parabolic-type differential equations over Riemannian manifolds. This is a subject never considered
before, up to the present time.

Hölder-type ultracontractivity results for nonlinear semigroups have been established in
[
18
,
19
,
68
]

(for the constant isotropic case
p=q
),
where elegant methods have been developed, which rely on logarithmic Sobolev inequalities (a subject motivated by the work of Davies
[
21
]
).
In
[
13
]
, the authors presented for the first time ultracontracitvity results for a class of anisotropic Robin problems with variable exponents.
However, the authors found a mistake in
[
13
, proof of Lemma 10]
(which allowed for the remaining of the proofs to run as in
the previous cases in the literature), which has happened as a consequence of absence of a positive constant at the last
inequality of
[
13
, proof of Lemma 10]
. It turns out that such missing positive constant may bring complications in the subsequent results,
so one needs a way to avoid it, or try another approach. Fortunately, in our present situation we
were able to find the way to correct this mistake, by keeping the problem in the anisotropic structure
(instead of reducing it to
p_{m}(\cdot)
-gradient term; see the last inequality of
[
13
, proof of Lemma 10]
), which complicates
substantially several subsequent calculations. Nevertheless, we were able to develop a technical procedure to deal with the anisotropy
and furthermore, the fact that we have two (possibly independent) vector fields exponents acting on the interior and boundary, respectively.
A more detailed explanation is provided in Remark
5
.
In
[
65
]
, the results in
[
68
]
were extended to the variable exponent isotropic case
p(\cdot)=q(\cdot)
, by establishing
(
1.4
); however, the explicit value of the constants in (
1.4
) (with the exception of
\gamma
) were not achieved, and in addition
some key typos in the proofs of key lemmas in
[
65
]
were corrected.
It is also important to outline the works in
[
9
,
10
]
, where the authors developed Hölder-type ultracontractivity bounds for
nonlinear semigroups over compact Riemannian manifolds. However, ultracontractivity properties associated with semigroups generated by nonlinear
anisotropic-type operators have never been considered before (over Riemannian manifolds), even in the constant case. This will be an important result which we
will investigate in this paper. Furthermore, we will obtain ultracontractivity properties, not only on the interior of the domain, but
also at the boundary, which in our case is regarded as a compact Riemannian manifold. This opens the idea of having a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{T_{\sigma}(t)\}

over
\mathbb{X\!}^{\,r(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega})
associated with problem (
1.2
) with boundary conditions (
1.3
), which could
behave as an order pair

T_{\sigma}(t)=\left(T_{\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)}(t),T_{\overset{\rightarrow}{q}(\cdot)}(t)\right),

where
\{T_{\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)}(t)\}
is a semigroup associated with a differential equation involving the

\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)
-Laplace operator
\Delta_{\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)}
, and
\{T_{\overset{\rightarrow}{q}(\cdot)}(t)\}

is a semigroup associated with a differential equation (over a Riemannian manifold) involving the
\overset{\rightarrow}{q}(\cdot)
-Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{{}_{\overset{\rightarrow}{q}(\cdot),\Gamma}}
. The validity of the above
equality is unknown, up for the present time; however, we will show that the Wentzell semigroup generated in this paper will possess some properties exhibiting a sort
of “splitting” behavior into interior and boundary operators.

Another important point to consider, is the fact that the boundary value problem (
1.2
) with (
1.3
) deals with multiple variables, in
the sense that it contains an interior vector field
\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)
, and a boundary vector field
\overset{\rightarrow}{q}(\cdot)
. These two
vectorial functions are in general not equal, and unrelated each other. We comment that Gal and Warma
[
40
]
were the first ones to consider a
Wentzell-type quasi-linear boundary value problem involving two (constant) exponents
p\neq q
, but in their case they only consider an elliptic-type
differential equation, in which
p\neq q
, but
q
is related to
p
in some way. This is no longer the case in the present situation. In

[
24
]
, the authors recently generalized the results in
[
40
]
to a class of elliptic anisotropic boundary value problems
where
\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)\neq\overset{\rightarrow}{q}(\cdot)
are unrelated each other. Here we turn our attention into the corresponding
time-dependent problem with dynamical boundary conditions, which has interest on its own, since the methods employed in the parabolic case are
completely different than the ones applied in the elliptic case.

The organization of the paper is the following. Section
2
is divided into five parts, in which we outline the main definitions, known tools,
and known results, which will be applied in the subsequent section. We will present an overview on all the machinery related to variable exponent isotropic
and anisotropic function spaces, relative capacity, and the corresponding embedding results.
Some useful analytical tools are provided, and at the end, we give an overview on nonlinear semigroups. Then, section
3
concerns the
realization and global regularity theory for problem (
1.2
) with boundary conditions (
1.3
), and it is divided in two main parts.
In the first subsection, we show that the boundary value problem (
1.2
) with (
1.3
) is well-posed over

\mathbb{X\!}^{\,r(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega})
for any measurable function
r:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
with
r^{+}<\infty
.
We also show that the associated Wentzell
C_{0}
-semigroup is order-preserving and non-expansive over
\mathbb{X\!}^{\,r(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega})
.
In the second part, we establish an Hölder-type ultracontractivity result for the Wentzell semigroup generated in the first part. In particular,
we establish the validity of the inequality (
1.4
), where the constants in (
1.4
) are explicitly given. We also discuss a particular
case in which we get a Lipschitz ultracontractivity property, namely, the fulfillment of (

. At the end, we add an Appendix A,

).

.

Let us pass to the (isotropic) Sobolev space with variable exponent, that is,

W^{1,r(\cdot)}({\Omega})=\left\{u\in L^{r(\cdot)}({\Omega})\mid\,\partial_{x_{i}}u\in L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega),\,\,i\in\{1,\dots,N\}\right\},

where
\partial_{x_{i}}u
,
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, represent the partial derivatives of
u
with respect to
x_{i}
in the weak sense.
This space, endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,r(\cdot)}({\Omega})}=\|u\|_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}+\|\nabla u\|_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}},

(2.7)

is a separable and reflexive Banach space
(see
[
47
, Theorem 3.1]
) whenever
r\in{\mathcal{P}}({\Omega})
with
1<r^{-}\leq r^{+}<\infty
.
For more classical properties of the variable exponent
Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces, refer to
[
27
,
34
,
47
,
51
]
, among others.

Next, we introduce the anisotropic space
W^{1,\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
, where
\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot):\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the vectorial function

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot)=\left(r_{1}(\cdot),\cdots,r_{N}(\cdot)\right)

and
r_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
with
1<r_{i}^{-}\leq r_{i}^{+}<\infty
for all
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}
. The
anisotropic variable exponent Sobolev space
is defined by

\displaystyle W^{1,\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot)}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{r_{M}(\cdot)}(\Omega)\mid\partial_{x_{i}}u\in L^{r_{i}(\cdot)}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\right\}

(2.8)

endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1,\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{{}_{r_{M}(\cdot),\Omega}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{{}_{r_{i}(\cdot),\Omega}}.

(2.9)

On the other hand, for
s\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
,
we define the variable exponent Sobolev space at the boundary
W^{1,s(\cdot)}(\Gamma)

as the completion of the space
C^{1}(\Gamma)
with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{{}_{W^{1,q(\cdot)}(\Gamma)}}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\mid\Theta_{{}_{q(\cdot),\Gamma}}(u/\lambda)+\Theta_{{}_{q(\cdot),\Gamma}}(|\nabla_{{}^{\Gamma}}u|/\lambda)\leq 1\right\},

where we recall that
\nabla_{{}^{\Gamma}}u:=(\partial_{x_{\tau_{1}}}u,\ldots,\partial_{x_{\tau_{N-1}}}u)
denotes
the tangential gradient at
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
(also called the Riemannian gradient of
u
),
for
\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}u
the directional derivative of
u
along the tangential directions
\tau_{j}
at each point on
\Gamma
.
For a treatment of this space for
q\in(1,\infty)
constant, we refer to
[
43
,
46
]
. Then proceeding as above,
given
\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot):\Gamma\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
a measurable vectorial function, one can define
the corresponding anisotropic Sobolev space over
\Gamma
by:

\displaystyle W^{1,\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot)}(\Gamma)=\left\{u\in L^{s_{M}(\cdot)}(\Gamma)\mid\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}u\in L^{s_{j}(\cdot)}(\Gamma)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad j\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}\right\}

(2.10)

endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1,\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot)}(\Gamma)}:=\|u\|_{{}_{s_{M}(\cdot),\Gamma}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}u\|_{{}_{s_{j}(\cdot),\Gamma}}.

(2.11)

Then one can deduce in the same way as in the variable exponent Lebesgue spaces that

W^{1,\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot)}(\Gamma)
are both Banach spaces, which are reflexive,
provided that
1<r^{-}_{m}\leq r^{+}_{M}<\infty
and
1<s^{-}_{m}\leq s^{+}_{M}<\infty
(e.g.
[
32
]
).

Now, we define the space

\mathcal{V}_{\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot),\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot)}:=\left\{u\in W^{1,\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot)}(\Omega)\mid u|_{{}_{\Gamma}}\in W^{1,\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot)}(\Gamma)\right\}.

(2.12)

Then, one have that the space
\mathcal{V}_{\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot),\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot)}
is a Banach space with respect to the norm

\|\cdot\|_{{}_{\mathcal{V}_{\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot),\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot)}}}:=\|\cdot\|_{{}_{W^{1,\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}+\|\cdot\|_{{}_{W^{1,\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot)}(\Gamma)}}.

(2.13)

Finally, we define our target function space by

\mathbf{V}_{\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot),\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot)}:=\left\{\mathbf{u}:=(u,u|_{{}_{\Gamma}})\mid u\in\mathcal{V}_{\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot),\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot)}\right\},\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{endowed with the norm:}\,\,\,\,\,|\|\mathbf{u}\||_{{}_{\mathbf{V}_{\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot),\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot)}}}:=\|u\|_{{}_{\mathcal{V}_{\overrightarrow{r}(\cdot),\overrightarrow{s}(\cdot)}}}.

(2.14)

The next definitions will play a crucial role on identifying the class of domains under consideration in this paper.
We start by introducing to the mathematical literature the notion of anisotropic
p(\cdot)
-extension domains.

Definition 1
.

Let
r_{i}\in C(\mathbb{R\!}^{N}\,)
, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. A domain

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
is called an
anisotropic
r(\cdot)
-extension domain
,
if
\Omega
has the
W^{1,\overset{\rightarrow}{r}(\cdot)}
-
extension property
,
that is, if there exists a bounded linear operator

S:W^{1,\overset{\rightarrow}{r}(\cdot)}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{1,\overset{\rightarrow}{r}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R\!}^{N})
such that
Su=u
a.e. on
\Omega
.
In the case when
r_{i}(\cdot)=r(\cdot)
over
\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, we
call
\Omega
a
r(\cdot)
-
extension domain
.

There are many examples of domains satisfying the statement of Definition
1
(for the case when
r_{1}=r_{2}=\ldots=r_{N}=
constant; see for example

[
63
, Example 6.7]
), and it is
well-known that Lipschitz domains are
r(\cdot)
-extension domains for all “sufficiently smooth”
r(\cdot)
with
1\leq r^{-}\leq r^{+}\leq\infty
.
However, in the present case, as we are dealing with
anisotropic Sobovev spaces, the situation becomes more critical, namely because for many domains, the extension operator may
“mix” the derivatives in such way that the extended function no more belongs to the required anisotropic Sobolev space
(see for instance
[
42
]
). So in order for a domain to be an anisotropic
r(\cdot)
-extension domain, we are assuming
that the situation previously mentioned does not occur, that is, we are requiring that the extended function may lie in the corresponding
anisotropic Sobolev space without going through the “mixing” problem mentioned above. Consequently, the class of domains with the anisotropic property
in Definition
1
is smaller than the one considered in
[
63
]
. Known examples of anisotropic
r(\cdot)
-extension domains include rectangular domains, rectangular-like domains, domains with the semirectangular restriction, and
admissible domains (in the sense of
[
57
]
); see for instance
[
32
,
42
,
57
]
. In particular, it is unknown (up to the present time) whether
bounded Lipschitz domains are anisotropic
r(\cdot)
-extension domain, even for a smooth vector field
\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)
. Thus throughout
the body of the paper, we will assume this condition (in addition to the preliminary assumption over the domain
\Omega
).

Remark 1
.

If
p_{i}\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})
, then it is known that each
p_{i}
can be extended to a function
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R\!}^{N})

with
\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{i}=p^{+}_{i}
, and
\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{i}=p^{-}_{i}
(in fact this holds even for log-Hölder continuous functions; see for instance
[
25
]
).
Then for such case, one can regard an anisotropic
p(\cdot)
-extension domain as an order pair
(\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot),\Omega)
possessing an extension property in the following way:

\bullet

\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot)=(p_{1}(\cdot),\ldots,p_{{}_{N}}(\cdot))
can be extended to a vectorial function
\overset{\rightarrow}{\mathfrak{p}}(\cdot)=(\mathfrak{p}_{1}(\cdot),\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{{}_{N}}(\cdot))
.

\bullet

There exists a bounded extension operator from
W^{1,\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
into
W^{1,\overset{\rightarrow}{\mathfrak{p}}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R\!}^{N})

such that the derivatives may not get mixed under the extension operator.

In
[
32
,
42
]
, the authors show that a rectangular domain satisfies all the conditions in the formulation given in Remark
1
.

2.2.
Sobolev embedding theorems, and inequalities

The following embedding results will be frequently applied in the subsequent sections. We recall that for now on we will assume
that
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, for
N\geq 2
. We begin by stating the following know results.

Theorem 1
.

(see
[
13
]
)
Let
{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) be a bounded
p_{m}(\cdot)
-extension domain, for

p_{m}(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})
with
1<p^{-}_{m}\leq p^{+}_{m}<N
,
and suppose that
\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})^{N}
.

(a)

If
r(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}(\overline{{\Omega}})
is such that
r^{-}>1
, and

r(\cdot)\,\leq\,\displaystyle\frac{Np_{m}(\cdot)}{N-p_{m}(\cdot)}:=p^{\star}_{m}(\cdot)\,,\qquad\textrm{over}\,\,\overline{\Omega},

(2.15)

then the interior embedding
W^{1,\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is continuous. Moreover,
if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

r(x)\,\leq\,p^{\ast}_{m}(\cdot)-\epsilon,\qquad\forall\,x\in\overline{\Omega},

then the embedding
W^{1,\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is compact.

(b)

Assume now that the boundary
\Gamma
of
\Omega
is an upper
d
-set with respect to a measure
\mu
on
\Gamma
,
for
d\in(N-p^{-}_{m},N)
. If
s(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}(\overline{{\Omega}})
is such that
s^{-}>1
, and

s(x)\,\leq\,\displaystyle\frac{dp_{m}(\cdot)}{N-p^{-}_{m}}\,,\qquad\forall\,x\in\overline{\Omega},

then the trace embedding
W^{1,\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{s(\cdot)}(\Gamma)
is continuous. Furthermore,
if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

s(x)\,\leq\,\displaystyle\frac{dp_{m}(\cdot)}{N-p^{-}_{m}}-\epsilon,\qquad\forall\,x\in\overline{\Omega},

Then the trace embedding
W^{1,\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{s(\cdot)}(\Gamma)
is compact.

The next embedding result for the variable exponent case has been established over
general compact Riemannian manifolds by Gaczkowski and Górka
[
37
]
. In the case of a bounded Lipschitz domain,
clearly
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
is a compact Riemannian manifold, and if
q\in C^{0,1}(\Gamma)
with
1\leq q^{-}\leq q^{+}<\infty
,
a simpler proof has been developed in
[
24
]
.

Theorem 2
.

(see
[
24
,
37
]
)
Given
N\geq 3
and
q\in C^{0,1}(\Gamma)
with
1\leq q^{-}\leq q^{+}<N-1
,
let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\Gamma
. Then there exists a linear continuous mapping

W^{1,q(\cdot)}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow L^{{}^{\frac{(N-1)q(\cdot)}{N-1-q(\cdot)}}}(\Gamma)
and a constant
c_{3}>0
such that

\|u\|_{{}_{\frac{(N-1)q(\cdot)}{N-1-q(\cdot)},\Gamma}}\,\leq\,c_{3}\|u\|_{{}_{W^{1,q(\cdot)}(\Gamma)}},\indent\,\textit{for all}\,\,\,u\in W^{1,q(\cdot)}(\Gamma).

(2.16)

Also, if
s\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
fulfills
1\leq s(x)\leq(N-1)q(x)(N-1-q(x))^{-1}
for all
x\in\Gamma
, then
the linear mapping
W^{1,q(\cdot)}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow L^{{}^{\frac{(N-1)q(\cdot)}{N-1-q(\cdot)}}}(\Gamma)
is continuous.
Furthermore, if
\tilde{q}\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
is such that

q(x)\leq\tilde{q}(x)\,\,\,\textit{for a.e.}\,\,\,x\in\Gamma\indent\textit{and}\indent\textrm{ess}\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Gamma}\left\{\frac{(N-1)q(x)}{N-1-q(x)}-\tilde{q}(x)\right\}>0,

then the embedding
W^{1,q(\cdot)}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow L^{\tilde{q}(\cdot)}(\Gamma)
is compact.

Now, the anisotropic case follows easily by applying Hölder’s inequality, together with Theorem
2
.

Theorem 3
.

(see
[
24
]
)
Let
{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
(
N\geq 3
) be a bounded
q_{m}(\cdot)
-extension domain and be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\Gamma
, for

q_{i}\in C(\Gamma)
with
q_{i}^{-}>1
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
, with
q_{m}(\cdot)
log-Hölder continuous
over
\Gamma
with
q_{m}^{+}<N-1
. If
q\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Gamma)
with
q^{-}>1
satisfies the condition

q(\cdot)\,\leq\,\displaystyle\frac{(N-1)q_{m}(\cdot)}{N-1-q_{m}(\cdot)}:=q_{m}^{\partial}(\cdot),\qquad\textrm{over}\,\,\Gamma,

(2.17)

then there is a continuous embedding

W^{1,\overset{\rightarrow}{q}(\cdot)}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow L^{q(\cdot)}({\Gamma}).

Moreover, if
\tilde{q}\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
is such that

q(x)\leq\tilde{q}(x)\,\,\,\textit{for a.e.}\,\,\,x\in\overline{\Omega}\indent\textit{and}\indent\textrm{ess}\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Gamma}\left\{q^{\partial}(x)-\tilde{q}(x)\right\}>0,

then the embedding
W^{1,q(\cdot)}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow L^{\tilde{q}(\cdot)}(\Gamma)
is compact.

Remark 2
.

The conclusions of Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
are clearly valid, if one
replace the Sobolev spaces by the space
\mathcal{V}_{\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot),\overrightarrow{q}(\cdot)}
defined by (
2.12
).

Remark 3
.

The conclusions of Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
are valid under a more general setting. In fact,
one can easily show that both theorems hold if one replaces the functions
p^{\star}_{m}(\cdot)
and
q_{m}^{\partial}(\cdot)
by the functions

\hat{p}_{{}_{M,m}}(\cdot):=\max\left\{p_{m}^{\star}(\cdot),\,p_{M}(\cdot)\right\}\indent\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\,\,\,\indent\check{q}_{{}_{M,m}}(\cdot):=\max\left\{q_{m}^{\partial}(\cdot),\,q_{M}(\cdot)\right\}.

We conclude this subsection by providing an anisotropic logarithmic inequality, which will be of use later on.
We recall that a similar inequality
has been obtained by Warma
[
68
, Lemma 2.5]
for the constant isotropic case, and the generalization to the isotropic nonstandard case
runs similarly; cf.
[
65
, Lemma 3.2.1.]
. The result is unknown for the anisotropic case.

Theorem 4
.

Given
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
a bounded Lipschitz domain (
N>2
),
let
(\overset{\rightarrow}{p},\,p^{-}_{m})\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})^{N+1}
with
1<p^{-}_{m}<N
, and let

(\overset{\rightarrow}{q},\,q^{-}_{m})\in C^{0,1}(\Gamma)^{N}
with
1<q^{-}_{m}<N-1
.

(a)

If
u\in W^{1,\overset{\rightarrow}{p}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is nonnegative with

\psi_{{}_{\Omega}}(u^{p_{m}(x)}):=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u^{p_{m}(x)}\,dx\,=\,1,

(2.18)

then for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a constant
C^{\prime}_{\epsilon}>0
such that for every
\epsilon_{1}>0
, one has

\psi_{{}_{\Omega}}(u^{p_{m}(x)}\log(u))\,\leq\,\frac{1}{M_{1}}\left(-\log(\epsilon_{1})+C^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\epsilon_{1}\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{{}_{p_{i}(\cdot),\Omega}}+\epsilon_{1}C^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\epsilon\right),

(2.19)

for some constant
M_{1}>0
.

(b)

If
u\in W^{1,\overset{\rightarrow}{q}(\cdot)}(\Gamma)
is nonnegative with

\psi_{{}_{\Gamma}}(u^{q_{m}(x)}):=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}u^{q_{m}(x)}\,dx\,=\,1,

(2.20)

then for every
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
there exists a constant
C^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon^{\prime}}>0
such that for every
\epsilon_{2}>0
, one has

\psi_{{}_{\Gamma}}(u^{q_{m}(x)}\log(u))\,\leq\,\frac{1}{M_{2}}\left(-\log(\epsilon_{2})+C^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon^{\prime}}\epsilon_{2}\displaystyle\sum^{N-1}_{j=1}\|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}u\|_{{}_{q_{j}(\cdot),\Gamma}}+\epsilon_{2}C^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon^{\prime}}\epsilon^{\prime}\right),

(2.21)

for some constant
M_{2}>0
.

Proof.

We first prove part (b). Put
s_{m}(\cdot):=\frac{q_{m}(\cdot)[q_{m}(\cdot)-1]}{N-1-q_{m}(\cdot)}
. Then by Jensen’s inequality one has

\psi_{{}_{\Gamma}}(u^{q_{m}(x)}\log(u))\,\leq\,\displaystyle\frac{1}{s^{-}_{m}}\log\left(\psi_{{}_{\Gamma}}(u^{q_{m}^{\partial}(x)})\right)\,\leq\,\displaystyle\frac{1}{s^{-}_{m}\hat{q}_{m}^{\partial}}\log\left(\|u\|_{{}_{q_{m}^{\partial}(\cdot),\Gamma}}\right)

\indent\indent\indent\indent\indent\indent\indent\indent\indent\,\leq\,\frac{1}{s^{-}_{m}\hat{q}_{m}^{\partial}}\left(-\log(\epsilon_{2})+C_{{}_{\Gamma}}\epsilon_{2}\|u\|_{{}_{q_{m}^{\partial}(\cdot),\Gamma}}\right),

(2.22)

for every
\epsilon_{2}>0
and for some constant
C_{{}_{\Gamma}}>0
, where

\hat{q}_{m}^{\partial}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{(N-1)q^{+}_{m}}{N-1-q^{+}_{m}},\,\indent\,\textrm{if}\,\,\,\,\|u\|_{{}_{q_{m}^{\partial}(\cdot),\Gamma}}>1,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{(N-1)q^{-}_{m}}{N-1-q^{-}_{m}},\,\indent\,\textrm{if}\,\,\,\,\|u\|_{{}_{q_{m}^{\partial}(\cdot),\Gamma}}\leq 1.\\[4.30554pt]
\end{array}\right.

Now, from Theorem
3
and (
2.20
) one has

C_{\epsilon}\|u\|_{{}_{q_{m}^{\partial}(\cdot),\Gamma}}\,\leq\,\displaystyle\sum^{N-1}_{j=1}\|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}u\|_{{}_{q_{j}(\cdot),\Gamma}}+\epsilon,

(2.23)

for all
\epsilon>0
, and for some constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
. Therefore,
combining (
2.22
) and (
2.23
) yield the inequality (
2.21
). Now part (a)
follows in the exact way, with the help of Theorem
1
. The proof is complete.

2.4.
Basics concepts about the relative
p(\cdot)
-capacity

In this part we will summarize the necessary facts regarding the theory of the relative
p(\cdot)
-capacity, and its application
to measure theory. Recall that we will assume that
p\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})
with
1<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<\infty
(unless specified
differently). A complete treatment of this topic and its applications has been recently given in
[
64
]
(under much
more general settings on the domain and on
p(\cdot)
; see also
[
65
, Appendix B]
).

Definition 2
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
and let
r\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R\!}^{N})
be such that

1\leq r^{-}\leq r^{\ast}<\infty
. The
r(\cdot)
-capacity
of
E
is defined by

\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{r(\cdot)}(E):=\displaystyle\inf_{u\in S_{r(\cdot)}(E)}\left\{\Theta_{{}_{r(\cdot),\mathbb{R\!}^{N}}}(u)+\Theta_{{}_{r(\cdot),\mathbb{R\!}^{N}}}(|\nabla u|)\right\},

where

S_{r(\cdot)}(E):=\left\{u\in W^{1,r(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R\!}^{N})\mid u\geq 0\,\,\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\,\,u\geq 1\,\,\,\,\textrm{in an open set containing}\,\,\,E\right\}.

Definition 3
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
be an open set, and let
\widetilde{W}^{1,r(\cdot)}(\Omega)

denote the closure in
W^{1,r(\cdot)}(\Omega)
of the space
W^{1,r(\cdot)}(\Omega)\cap C_{c}(\overline{\Omega})
.
The
relative
r(\cdot)
-capacity
of
E
with respect to
\Omega
is defined by

\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}(E):=\displaystyle\inf_{u\in S_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}(E)}\left\{\Theta_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}(u)+\Theta_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}(|\nabla u|)\right\},

where

S_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}(E):=\left\{u\in\widetilde{W}^{1,r(\cdot)}(\Omega)\mid\,\exists\,\,\,U\subseteq\mathbb{R\!}^{N}\,\,\,\textrm{open},\,\,\,\,E\subseteq U\,\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\,u\geq 1\,\,\,\textrm{a.e. on}\,\,\,\Omega\cap U\right\}.

Observe that if
\Omega=\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
, then
\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}(E)=\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{r(\cdot)}(E)
for each
E\subseteq\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
.

Definition 4
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
be an open set, and let
E\subseteq\overline{\Omega}
.

(a)

E
is said to be
\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}
-
polar
, if
\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}(E)=0
.

(b)

We say that a property holds
\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}
-
quasi everywhere
(abbreviated
r(\cdot)
-q.e.) on
E
,
if there exists a
\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}
-polar set
D
such that the property holds over
E\setminus D
.

(c)

A function
u
is called
\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}
-
quasi continuous
on
E
, if for all
\epsilon>0
, there
exists a open set
U\subseteq\overline{\Omega}
such that
\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{{}_{r(\cdot),\Omega}}(U)\leq\epsilon
and

u|_{{}_{E\setminus U}}
is continuous.

The following are important results, which we state here.

Theorem 5
.

(see
[
64
,
65
]
)
Given
p\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})
with
1\leq p^{-}\leq p^{+}\leq\infty
,
if
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
is a bounded
W^{1,p(\cdot)}
-extension domain, then there exists a constant
c_{{}_{\Omega}}=c(\Omega)>0
such that

\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{p(\cdot)}(E)\,\leq\,c_{{}_{\Omega}}\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{{}_{p(\cdot),\Omega}}(E)^{{}^{\hat{p}/\bar{p}}}\,\leq\,c_{{}_{\Omega}}\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{p(\cdot)}(E)^{{}^{\hat{p}/\bar{p}}}

(2.29)

for every set
E\subseteq\overline{\Omega}
, where
\hat{p}
and
\bar{p}
are positive constants.

Corollary 1
.

(see
[
64
,
65
]
)
Under the assumptions of Theorem
5
, let
E\subseteq\overline{\Omega}
. Then
E
is

\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{{}_{p(\cdot),\Omega}}
-polar if and only if
E
is

\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{{}_{p(\cdot),\mathbb{R\!}^{N}}}

2.5.
Basic concepts for nonlinear semigroups

Now we put together some definitions and results on nonlinear semigroups. Indeed, let
H
be a Hilbert space, and let

\varphi:H\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper, convex, lower semicontinuous functional with effective domain
D(\varphi):=\{u\in H\mid\varphi(u)<\infty\}
. Clearly
D(\varphi)\subseteq H
is convex. Then the subdifferential
\partial\varphi
of
\varphi
is defined by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}D(\partial\varphi):=\left\{u\in D(\varphi)\mid\exists\,w\in H,\,\,\,\varphi(v)-\varphi(u)\geq\langle w,v-u\rangle_{{}_{H}},\,\,\,\textrm{for all}\,\,\,v\in H\right\},\\
\partial\varphi(u):=\left\{w\in H\mid\varphi(v)-\varphi(u)\geq\langle w,v-u\rangle_{{}_{H}},\,\,\,\textrm{for all}\,\,\,v\in H\right\},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{}_{H}}
denotes the inner product on
H
. The following classical result is fundamental.

Theorem 6
.

(see
[
50
]
)
The subdifferential
\partial\varphi
is a maximal monotone operator. Moreover,

\overline{D(\varphi)}=\overline{D(\partial\varphi)}
. The subdifferential
\partial\varphi
generates a (nonlinear)
C_{0}
-semigroup

\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
on
\overline{D(\varphi})
in the following sense: for each
u_{0}\in\overline{D(\varphi)}
,
the function
u:=T(\cdot)u_{0}
is the unique strong solution of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}u\in C(\mathbb{R\!}_{\,+};H)\cap W^{1,\infty}_{loc}((0,\infty);H)\,\,\,\textit{and}\,\,\,u(t)\in\partial\varphi\,\,\,\textit{a.e.},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\partial\varphi(u)\,=\,0\,\,\,\,\textit{a.e. on}\,\,\,\mathbb{R\!}_{\,+},\\
u(0,x)\,=u_{0}(x).\end{array}\right.

In addition, the subdifferential
\partial\varphi
generates a (nonlinear) semigroup
\{\tilde{T}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
on
H
, where for every
t\geq 0
,

\tilde{T}(t)
is the composition of the semigroup
T(t)
on
\overline{D(\varphi)}
with the projection on the convex set
\overline{D(\varphi)}
.

Definition 6
.

Let
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a (nonlinear) semigroup on a Hilbert lattice
H
with ordering
\leq
,
let
X
be a locally compact metric space, and
\nu
a Borel regular measure on
X
.

(a)

\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is said to be
order-preserving
, if

T(t)u\,\leq\,T(t)v\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{for all}\,\,\,t\geq 0,\,\,\textrm{whenever}\,\,\,u,\,v\in H,\,\,u\leq v.

(b)

\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is called
non-expansive
over
L^{q(\cdot)}(X,d\nu)
(
q\in\mathcal{P}(X)
), if

\indent\indent\|T(t)u-T(t)v\|_{{}_{q(\cdot),X}}\,\leq\,\|u-v\|_{{}_{q(\cdot),X}},

for every
t\geq 0

and
u,\,v\in L^{2}(X,d\nu)\cap L^{q(\cdot)}(X,d\nu)
.

(c)

\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is said to be
submarkovian
, if
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is non-expansive over
L^{\infty}(X,d\nu)
.

The next two well-known results characterize the order-preserve property and the submarkovian property of the
functional
\varphi
, respectively.

Proposition 1
.

(see
[
20
]
)  Let
\varphi:\;H\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper, convex,
lower semicontinuous functional on a real Hilbert lattice
H
, with effective domain
D(\varphi)
. Let

\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be the (nonlinear) semigroup on
H
generated by
\partial\varphi
. Then the following
assertions are equivalent.

(i)

The semigroup
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is order preserving.

(ii)

For all

one has

\varphi\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(u+u\wedge v\right)\right)+\varphi\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(v+u\vee v\right)\right)\,\leq\,\varphi(u)+\varphi(v),

where
u\wedge v:=\inf\{u,v\}
and
u\vee v:=\sup\{u,v\}
.

Proposition 2
.

(see
[
20
,
53
]
)  Let
\varphi:L^{2}(X,d\nu)\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper,
convex, lower semicontinuous functional. Let

be the (nonlinear) semigroup on
L^{2}(X,d\nu)
generated by

A=\partial\varphi
. Assume that
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is order preserving. Then, the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

The semigroup
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is submarkovian.

(ii)

For all

and

\varphi\bigg(v+g_{\alpha}(u,v)\bigg)+\varphi\bigg(u-g_{\alpha}(u,v)\bigg)\,\leq\,\varphi(u)+\varphi(v),

with
u^{+}:=\sup\{u,0\}
, and
u^{-}:=\sup\{-u,0\}
,

(iii)

The operator

is accretive on
L^{\infty}(X,d\nu)
, that is,

\|u-v\|_{{}_{\infty,X}}\,\leq\,\|(I-\eta A)(u-v)\|_{{}_{\infty,X}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\textit{for all}\,\,\,u,\,v\in D(A)\,\,\,\textit{and}\,\,\,\eta>0.

The next definition brings the notion of ultracontractivity for nonlinear semigroups. If the semigroup is linear, then
the classical ultracontractivity property in the sense of
[

coincide with the Lipschitz-ultracontractivity

Definition 7
.

Let

be a locally compact metric space, and

a Borel regular measure on

.

,
and

\|T(t)u-T(t)v\|_{{}_{\infty}}\,\leq\,c_{1}\,t^{-\alpha}\|u-v\|^{\gamma}_{{}_{2}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\indent\,\textrm{for all}\,\,\,\,u,\,v\in L^{2}(X,d\nu)\,\,\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\,\,t\in(0,1].

3.
The parabolic problem

In this section we turn our attention of the well-posedness of the quasi-linear parabolic problem with variable exponents
and pure Wentzell boundary conditions, on bounded anisotropic
p(\cdot)
-extension domains with Lipschitz boundaries.

3.1.
The Cauchy problem over
\mathbb{X\!}^{\,r(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega})

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
be a bounded anisotropic
p(\cdot)
-extension domain with Lipschitz boundary,
for
N\geq 3
, and let
(\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot),\,\overrightarrow{q}(\cdot))\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})^{N}\times C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})^{N-1}

be such that
1<p^{-}_{m}\leq p^{+}_{M}<\infty
and
1<q^{-}_{m}\leq q^{+}_{M}<\infty

Given
\alpha\in L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\beta\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
with
\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\alpha(x)\geq\alpha_{0}

and
\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Gamma}\beta(x)\geq\beta_{0}
for some constants
\alpha_{0},\,\beta_{0}>0
, and

\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{X\!}^{\,r(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega})
for
r\in\mathcal{P}(\overline{\Omega})
with

1\leq r^{-}\leq r^{+}\leq\infty
, we want to investigate the
well-posedness of the quasi-linear parabolic equation with fully dynamical anisotropic Wentzell boundary conditions, formally defined by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}u_{t}-\Delta_{\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot)}u+\alpha|u|^{p_{M}(\cdot)-2}u\,=\,0\indent\indent\indent\indent\indent\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\indent\indent\textrm{in}\,\,\,\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u_{t}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p_{i}(\cdot)-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u\;\nu_{i}-\Delta_{{}_{\overrightarrow{q}(\cdot),\Gamma}}u+\beta|u|^{q_{M}(\cdot)-2}u\,=\,0\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{on}\,\,\,\Gamma\times(0,\infty),\\
u(0,x)\,=\,u_{0}(x)\indent\indent\indent\indent\indent\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\indent\indent\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\indent\indent\indent\indent\indent\,\,\textrm{in}\,\,\,\overline{\Omega},\\
\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

To begin with the discussion, we define the functional
\Phi_{\sigma}:\mathbb{X\!}^{\,2}(\overline{\Omega})\rightarrow[0,\infty]
by

\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{E}_{{}_{\Omega}}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})+\mathcal{E}_{{}_{\Gamma}}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}),\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{if}\,\,\mathbf{u}\in D(\Phi_{\sigma}),\\[6.45831pt]
+\infty,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{if}\,\,\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{X\!}^{\,2}(\overline{\Omega})\setminus D(\Phi_{\sigma}),\\
\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

with effective domain

D(\Phi_{\sigma}):=\mathbf{V}_{\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot),\overrightarrow{q}(\cdot)}\cap\mathbb{X\!}^{\,2}(\overline{\Omega}),

where we recall that
\mathbf{V}_{\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot),\overrightarrow{q}(\cdot)}
is defined by (

\mathcal{E}_{{}_{\Omega}}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}):=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\left(\frac{|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p_{i}(x)-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u\partial_{x_{i}}v}{p_{i}(x)}\right)\,dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha\frac{|u|^{p_{M}(x)-2}uv}{p_{M}(x)}\,dx

\mathcal{E}_{{}_{\Gamma}}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}):=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\displaystyle\sum^{N-1}_{j=1}\left(\frac{|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}u|^{q_{j}(x)-2}\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}u\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}v}{q_{j}(x)}\right)\,d\sigma+\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\beta\frac{|u|^{q_{M}(x)-2}uv}{q_{M}(x)}\,d\sigma,

for each
\mathbf{u},\,\mathbf{v}\in D(\Phi_{\sigma})
. We start by establishing the following three results, whose arguments run in a

Lemma 1
.

The functional
\Phi_{\sigma}

lower semicontinuous functional on
\mathbb{X\!}^{\,2}(\overline{\Omega})

Proof.

It is clear that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{u}_{n}=(u,w)
in
\mathbb{X\!}^{\,2}(\overline{\Omega})

If

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}_{n})<+\infty

Since

Thus, passing to subsequences if necessary, we may assume that

Next, consider the Banach space

valid for all
a,\,b\in\mathbb{R\!}^{N}
and for all

s\in[1,\infty)
, the above calculation implies that

\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{g}_{u,v})+\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{h}_{u,v})\leq\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})+\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{v}).

Thus, a direct application of Proposition
1
asserts that
\left\{T_{\sigma}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is order preserving.

Next, given
\xi>0
, we put

\displaystyle g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}}:=\left[\dfrac{1}{2}\left(u-v+\xi\right)_{+}-\left(u-v-\xi\right)_{-}\right],

(3.6)

and observe that

\displaystyle g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lclcl}u-v,&\mbox{ if }&|u-v|\leq\xi\\
&&\\
\dfrac{u-v-\xi}{2},&\mbox{ if }&u-v<-\xi\\
&&\\
\dfrac{u-v+\xi}{2},&\mbox{if}&u-v>\xi.\end{array}\right.

Again, it is clear that
\mathbf{g}_{{}_{u,v,\xi}}\in D(\Phi_{\sigma})
.
Then, we first deal with the terms involving partial derivatives. In fact, we have:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{i}}(v+g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}})\right|^{p_{i}(x)}}{p_{i}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\Omega\cap\left\{|u-v|\leq\xi\right\}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{i}}(v+g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}})\right|^{p_{i}(x)}}{p_{i}(x)}dx+\int\limits_{{\Omega\cap\left\{|u-v|\geq\xi\right\}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{i}}(v+g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}})\right|^{p_{i}(x)}}{p_{i}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\Omega\cap\left\{|u-v|\leq\xi\right\}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{i}}u\right|^{p_{i}(x)}}{p_{i}(x)}dx+\int\limits_{{\Omega\cap\left\{u-v<-\xi\right\}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{i}}\left(\frac{u+v-\xi}{2}\right)\right|^{p_{i}(x)}}{p_{i}(x)}dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\Omega\,\cap\,\left\{u-v>-\xi\right\}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{i}}\left(\frac{u+v+\xi}{2}\right)\right|^{p_{i}(x)}}{p_{i}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\Omega\,\cap\,\left\{|u-v|\leq\xi\right\}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{i}}u\right|^{p_{i}(x)}}{p_{i}(x)}dx+\int\limits_{{\Omega\,\cap\,\left\{u-v<-\xi\right\}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{i}}\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)\right|^{p_{i}(x)}}{p_{i}(x)}dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\Omega\,\cap\,\left\{u-v>\xi\right\}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{i}}\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)\right|^{p_{i}(x)}}{p_{i}(x)}dx.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}(u-g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}})\right|^{q_{j}(x)}}{q_{j}(x)}d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\Gamma\,\cap\,\left\{|u-v|\leq\xi\right\}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}v\right|^{q_{j}(x)}}{q_{j}(x)}d\sigma+\int\limits_{{\Gamma\,\cap\,\left\{u-v<-\xi\right\}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)\right|^{q_{j}(x)}}{q_{j}(x)}d\sigma

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\Gamma\,\cap\,\left\{u-v>\xi\right\}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)\right|^{q_{j}(x)}}{q_{j}(x)}d\sigma.

Adding the two last results and applying the inequality (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{i}}(v+g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}})\right|^{p_{i}(x)}}{p_{i}(x)}dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}(u-g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}})\right|^{q_{j}(x)}}{q_{j}(x)}d\sigma\,\leq\,\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{i}}u\right|^{p_{i}(x)}}{p_{i}(x)}dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\dfrac{\left|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}v\right|^{q_{j}(x)}}{q_{j}(x)}d\sigma.

(3.13)

For the terms not involving the partial derivatives, given
\xi>0
, we put

\displaystyle\lambda_{\xi}:=\chi_{{}_{\left\{u\neq v\right\}}}\dfrac{g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}}}{u-v}.

(3.14)

Then
\lambda_{\xi}
satisfies the condition
0\leq\lambda_{\xi}\leq 1,
with

\displaystyle v+g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}}=\lambda_{\xi}u+(1-\lambda_{\xi})v\qquad\mbox{ and }u-g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}}=\lambda_{\xi}v+(1-\lambda_{\xi})u.

(3.15)

Since the maps
t\longmapsto|t|^{p_{M}(\cdot)}
and
t\longmapsto|t|^{q_{M}(\cdot)}
are convex, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\alpha(x)}{p_{{}_{M}}(x)}\left(|v+g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}}|^{p_{{}_{M}}(x)}+|u-g_{u,v,\xi}|^{p_{M}(x)}\right)dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\dfrac{\beta(x)}{q_{M}(x)}\left(|v+g_{{}_{u,v,\xi}}|^{q_{{}_{M}}(x)}+|u-g_{u,v,\xi}|^{q_{{}_{M}}(x)}\right)d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda_{\xi}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\alpha(x)}{p_{{}_{M}}(x)}\left(|u|^{p_{{}_{M}}(x)}+|v|^{q_{M}(x)}\right)dx+(1-\lambda_{\xi})\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\alpha(x)}{p_{{}_{M}}(x)}\left(|u|^{p_{{}_{M}}(x)}+|v|^{p_{{}_{M}}(x)}\right)dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda_{\xi}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\beta(x)}{q_{{}_{M}}(x)}\left(|u|^{q_{{}_{M}}(x)}+|v|^{q_{M}(x)}\right)d\sigma+(1-\lambda_{\xi})\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\beta(x)}{q_{{}_{M}}(x)}\left(|u|^{q_{{}_{M}}(x)}+|v|^{q_{{}_{M}}(x)}\right)d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\alpha(x)}{p_{{}_{M}}(x)}\left(|u|^{p_{{}_{M}}(x)}+|v|^{p_{{}_{M}}(x)}\right)dx+\int_{\Gamma}\dfrac{\beta(x)}{q_{{}_{M}}(x)}\left(|u|^{q_{{}_{M}}(x)}+|v|^{q_{{}_{M}}(x)}\right)d\sigma.

Combining (
3.13
) and this last calculation yields

\displaystyle\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g}_{{}_{u,v,\xi}})+\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{g}_{{}_{u,v,\xi}})\,\leq\,\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})+\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{v}),

(3.16)

and thus
\left\{T_{\sigma}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is non-expansive on
\mathbb{X\!}^{\,\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
by virtue of Proposition
2
.
By
[
15
, Theorem 1]
and
[
49
, Corollary 3]
we see that
\left\{T_{\sigma}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
can be extended to a non-expansive semigroup on

\mathbb{X\!}^{\,r(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega})
for every
r\in\mathcal{P}(\overline{\Omega})
with
1\leq r^{-}\leq r^{+}\leq\infty
.
To see the strong continuity of
\left\{T_{\sigma}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
over
\mathbb{X\!}^{\,r(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega})

(for
r\in\mathcal{P}(\overline{\Omega})
with
1\leq r^{-}\leq r^{+}<\infty
), first take

\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{X\!}^{\,2}(\overline{\Omega})\cap\mathbb{X\!}^{\,\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, let
\hat{r}:=\min\{2,r^{-}\}
, and let

a(x):=\frac{\hat{r}}{r(x)}\left(\frac{r^{+}-r(x)}{r^{+}-\hat{r}}\right),\,\,b(x):=\frac{r^{+}}{r(x)}\left(\frac{r(x)-\hat{r}}{r^{+}-\hat{r}}\right)
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.
Then by
[
47
, Corollary 2.2]
and Hölder’s inequality (if necessary) we obtain that for
t>0
,

|\|T_{\sigma}(t)\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\||_{{}_{r(\cdot)}}\,\leq\,c|\|T_{\sigma}(t)\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\||^{\gamma_{1}}_{{}_{2}}\,|\|T_{\sigma}(t)\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\||^{\gamma_{2}}_{{}_{\infty}}\,\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\rightarrow 0^{+}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,0,

(3.17)

where

\gamma_{1}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}a^{+},\,\indent\,\textrm{if}\,\,\,\,|\|T_{\sigma}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\||_{{}_{2}}>1,\\
a^{-},\,\indent\,\textrm{if}\,\,\,\,|\|T_{\sigma}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\||_{{}_{2}}\leq 1,\\
\end{array}\right.\,\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\gamma_{2}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}b^{+},\,\indent\,\textrm{if}\,\,\,\,|\|T_{\sigma}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\||_{{}_{r^{+}}}>1,\\
b^{-},\,\indent\,\textrm{if}\,\,\,\,|\|T_{\sigma}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\||_{{}_{r^{+}}}\leq 1.\\
\end{array}\right.

Finally, for a general
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{X\!}^{\,r(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega})
, fix
\epsilon>0
and choose

\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{X\!}^{\,2}(\overline{\Omega})\cap\mathbb{X\!}^{\,\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

|\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\||_{{}_{r(\cdot)}}<\epsilon/3
. Then for a sufficiently small
t>0
, we get from (
3.17
) that

|\|T_{\sigma}(t)\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\||_{{}_{r(\cdot)}}\,\leq\,|\|T_{\sigma}(t)\mathbf{u}-T_{\sigma}(t)\mathbf{v}\||_{{}_{r(\cdot)}}+|\|T_{\sigma}(t)\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}\||_{{}_{r(\cdot)}}+|\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\||_{{}_{r(\cdot)}}<\epsilon.

(3.18)

Since
\epsilon>0
was chosen arbitrary, the inequality (
3.18
) implies that
\left\{T_{\sigma}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}

is a
C_{0}
-semigroup over
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{X\!}^{\,r(\cdot)}(\overline{\Omega})
, and this completes the proof.

\Box

Remark 4
.

The results of this section can be established inthe same way under more general assumptions on the vector fields
\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot)
and
\overrightarrow{q}(\cdot)
. In fact, for this part
it suffices to have
\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}(\overline{\Omega})^{N}
and
\overrightarrow{q}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}(\Gamma)^{N-1}
, where

\mathcal{P}^{\log}(\overline{\Omega})
denotes the set of functions
u\in\mathcal{P}(\overline{\Omega})

such that the function

is globally log-Hölder continuous, that is,
if there exist constants
c_{1},\,c_{2}>0
and a constant
\alpha\in\mathbb{R\!}\,
such that

|v(x)-v(y)|\,\leq\,\frac{c_{1}}{\log(e+1/|x-y|)}\indent\,\textrm{and}\indent\,|v(x)-\alpha|\,\leq\,\frac{c_{2}}{\log(e+|x|)}

for all
x,\,y\in\overline{\Omega}
.

3.2.
Ultracontractivity of semigroups

In this section we develop ultracotractivity properties for the nonlinear semigroup
\left\{T_{\sigma}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
,
under some additional conditions over
\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot)
and
\overrightarrow{q}(\cdot)
(we are assuming all the conditions in subsection
3.1
).
Cipriani and Grillo
[
18
,
19
]
developed some key
tools to develop ultracontractivity properties for Dirichlet-type nonlinear semigroups. Such methods were generalized and extended
to a dynamical Robin-Wentzell type problem by Warma
[
68
]
, and these were extended further in
[
65
]
to the case of isotropic
Robin-Wentzell and pure Wentzell differential equations with nonstandard growth conditions (for
p(\cdot)=q(\cdot)
).
In our problem, we will follow the approach in
[
65
,
68
]
; however,
in our case we are dealing with anisotropic operators involving different unrelated vector fields, one defined in the interior, and the other at the boundary.
These facts bring new difficulties, which require substantial modifications in the arguments previously applied by multiple authors.
Consequently, we provide complete proofs to all the results in this subsection.

We begin with three technical lemmas which are needed in order to establish our desired ultracontractivity bounds.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\left\{T_{\sigma}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
be the submarkovian
C_{0}
-semigroup on

\mathbb{X\!}^{\,2}(\overline{\Omega})
generated by
\partial\Phi_{\sigma}
. Given
t\geq 0
and
\mathbf{u}_{0},\,\mathbf{v}_{0}\in\mathbb{X\!}^{\,\infty}(\overline{\Omega})

constant

such that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{v}(t)\||^{r}_{{}_{r}}\,\leq\,-C_{1}r\left((r-1)\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(t)-v(t)|^{r-2}\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}|\partial_{x_{i}}(u(t)-v(t))|^{p_{i}(x)}\,dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha|u(t)-v(t)|^{r+p_{M}(x)-2}dx\right)+

\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-C_{1}r\left((r-1)\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|u(t)-v(t)|^{r-2}\displaystyle\sum^{N-1}_{j=1}\left|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}(u(t)-v(t))\right|^{q_{j}(x)}\,d\sigma+\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\beta|u(t)-v(t)|^{r+q_{M}(x)-2}d\sigma\right).

(3.19)

Proof.

Given
t\geq 0
and
\mathbf{u}_{0},\,\mathbf{v}_{0}\in\mathbb{X\!}^{\,\infty}(\overline{\Omega})

and

For

, we define the function

by

Clearly
G_{r}
is differentiable for almost all

Setting
\mathbf{w}(t):=\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{v}(t)
, using the fact that
\mathbf{u}(t),\,\mathbf{v}(t)

solve the Eq. (
3.4

3.1

with the respective initial data
\mathbf{u}_{0}
and
\mathbf{v}_{0}
, taking into account (
3.3

2.25
), we obtain the following calculation.

G^{\prime}_{r}(t)=r\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(t)-v(t)|^{r-1}(u^{\prime}(t)-v^{\prime}(t))\textrm{sgn}(u(t)-v(t))\,dx

=-r\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|w(t)|^{r-1}[\partial\Phi_{\sigma}(u(t))-\partial\Phi_{\sigma}(v(t))]\textrm{sgn}(w(t))\,dx

=r\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|w(t)|^{r-1}\left(\Delta_{\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot)}(u(t))-\alpha|u(t)|^{p_{M}(x)-2}u(t)\right)\textrm{sgn}(w(t))\,dx-r\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|w(t)|^{r-1}\left(\Delta_{\overrightarrow{p}(\cdot)}(v(t))-\alpha|v(t)|^{p_{M}(x)-2}v(t)\right)\textrm{sgn}(w(t))\,dx+

-r\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|w(t)|^{r-1}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\partial_{x_{i}}u(t)|^{p_{i}(\cdot)-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u\;\nu_{i}-\Delta_{{}_{\overrightarrow{q}(\cdot),\Gamma}}u(t)+\beta|u(t)|^{q_{M}(x)-2}u(t)\right)\textrm{sgn}(w(t))\,d\sigma+

+r\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|w(t)|^{r-1}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\partial_{x_{i}}v(t)|^{p_{i}(\cdot)-2}\partial_{x_{i}}v(t)\;\nu_{i}-\Delta_{{}_{\overrightarrow{q}(\cdot),\Gamma}}v(t)+\beta|v(t)|^{q_{M}(x)-2}v(t)\right)\textrm{sgn}(w(t))\,d\sigma

=-r(r-1)\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|w(t)|^{r-2}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(|\partial_{x_{i}}u(t)|^{p_{i}(\cdot)-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u-|\partial_{x_{i}}v(t)|^{p_{i}(x)-2}\partial_{x_{i}}v(t)\right)\partial_{x_{i}}w(t)\,dx+

-r\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|w(t)|^{r-2}\left(\alpha|u(t)|^{p_{M}(x)-2}u(t)-\alpha|v(t)|^{p_{M}(x)-2}v(t)\right)(w(t))\,dx+

-r(r-1)\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|w(t)|^{r-2}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}u(t)|^{q_{j}(\cdot)-2}\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}u-|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}v(t)|^{q_{j}(x)-2}\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}v(t)\right)\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}w(t)\,d\sigma+

\indent\indent\indent\indent-r\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|w(t)|^{r-2}\left(\beta|u(t)|^{q_{M}(x)-2}u(t)-\beta|v(t)|^{q_{M}(x)-2}v(t)\right)(w(t))\,d\sigma

\leq\,-C_{1}r\left((r-1)\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|w(t)|^{r-2}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\partial_{x_{i}}w(t)|^{p_{i}(x)}\,dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha|w(t)|^{r-2}|w(t)|^{p_{M}(x)}\,dx\right)+\indent\,\,

-C_{1}r\left((r-1)\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|w(t)|^{r-2}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}|\partial_{x_{\tau_{j}}}w(t)|^{q_{j}(x)}\,d\sigma+\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\beta|w(t)|^{r-2}|w(t)|^{q_{M}(x)}\,d\sigma\right),

for some constant
C_{1}>0
. Therefore, (
3.19
) is valid, as asserted.

\Box

For the rest of the subsection, we will assume that

Remark 5
.

In
[
13
]
, the authors established for the first time an ultracontractivity-type bound for nonlnear semigroups associated to an anisotropic
boundary value problem. To develop such bound for anisotropic differential equations, in the last two
inequalities of
[
13
, proof of Lemma 10]
, it was argued that
-\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p_{i}(x)}\leq-c\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p_{m}(x)}
for some constant

and a.e.
x\in\Omega
, and this was used to obtain the fulfillment of the
rest of the results following the approach as in
[
65
]
. Unfortunately, the correct inequality reads as

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p_{i}(x)}\leq-c_{\epsilon}\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p_{m}(x)}+\epsilon\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{for a.e.}\,\,x\in\Omega,\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{for each}\,\,\epsilon>0,\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{and for some constant}\,\,c_{\epsilon}>0.

and it may not be valid for
\epsilon=0
. Furthermore, the presence of a positive constant in the above correct inequality is sufficient
to hinder the subsequent results, so a different approach needed to be followed. Consequently, in the above lemmas, we
kept the anisotropy intact, and instead derived an alternative way to deduce the appropriate estimations and modifications
to obtain desirable conclusions in the key technical lemmas, which form a basis for the proofs of the main results of the section.
In particular, the methods employed in this paper provide a way to fix the error used in the derivation of ultracontractivity bounds in
[
13
]
.

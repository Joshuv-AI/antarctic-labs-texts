The influence of an external magnetic field on cold plasma oscillations
Olga S. Rozanova*, Eugeniy V. Chizhonkov

Abstract
. For a system of equations describing one-dimensional nonlinear oscillations in a magnetoactive plasma, we study the effect of a constant magnetic field on the breaking of oscillations. For the nonrelativistic case, a criterion for the formation of a finite-dimensional singularity is obtained in terms of the initial data. It is shown that the enhancement of the magnetic field basically leads to an expansion of the class of initial data providing the global smoothness of the solution. The nature of the singularities of the solutions is illustrated by numerical examples.

Introduction

Fully ionized plasma is a highly nonlinear medium in which even relatively small initial collective displacements of particles can lead to oscillations and large-amplitude waves. In the absence of dissipation, their evolution can lead to the appearance of a singularity of the electron density
[
9
]
. This effect is commonly called
breaking
of oscillations and/or waves. As shown in
[
27
]
,
singularity, i.e. the infinity of electron density in the Euler description of the motion of a medium is equivalent to the intersection of electron trajectories in its Lagrangian description. From a mathematical point of view, the breaking process means a blow up (the formation of infinite gradients of solution).

The complexity and variety of problem formulations increases sharply if we consider the dynamics of a
magnetoactive
plasma, i.e. plasma placed in an external magnetic field. Even in the case of small disturbances, i.e. when considering only linear waves, a special classification is introduced in classical textbooks and monographs (see, for example,
[
3
]
,
[
13
]
). In particular, waves propagating in the longitudinal and transverse directions with respect to the vector of a given magnetic field are distinguished. Already in a cold magnetoactive plasma, there are five oscillation branches propagating strictly along the external magnetic field, four of them describing transverse waves and one longitudinal waves
[
4
]
,
[
13
]
. It should be noted that, in the general case, linear longitudinal and transverse waves are not independent, as in a isotropic (unmagnetized) plasma
[
3
]
. It is for this reason that the modeling of nonlinear oscillations and waves in a magnetoactive plasma is of particular interest. Numerical modeling serves as the main research tool here, since the possibilities of the analytical and asymptotic approaches are severely limited due to the cumbersomeness and complexity of the equations describing the plasma dynamics. For example, we can mention the monograph
[
5
]
, or more recent paper
[
12
]
devoted to the numerical simulation of oscillations in cold plasma, as well as wake waves excited by a short high-power laser pulse.

Exact analytical results for the equations of cold plasma are very rare. However, they are the main theoretical value for the analysis of the acceleration of electrons in the wake wave of a powerful laser pulse
[
11
]
. In
[
20
]
, for the first time when using Eulerian variables, a necessary and sufficient condition for breaking plane one-dimensional oscillations in the nonrelativistic case was obtained; detailed proofs are given in
[
22
]
. Subsequently, the results of
[
20
]
were generalized to the case of accounting for electron-ion collisions
[
21
]
.

In this paper, electrostatic oscillations of a magnetoactive plasma are considered with emphasis on the non-relativistic case. The methods described here make it possible to analyze in the relativistic case, but the results are rather cumbersome and we do not include them in this work in order not to overload the presentation.
It should be noted that earlier statements similar in meaning were previously considered only in the physical literature (see, for example,
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
and the references therein), where methods and formulations of results differ significantly from ours.

The work is organized as follows. In Section
1
, a system of equations is derived that describes one-dimensional oscillations of an electron plasma in a constant magnetic field. In Section
2
, for the solution of the Cauchy problem for this system in the nonrelativistic approximation we obtain a blow up criterion in terms of the initial data. Hence follows the regularizing effect of a constant magnetic field in the general case. In Section
3
, we analyze subclasses of solutions, including traveling waves, for which the number of equations can be reduced. In Section
4
, for the purpose of numerical simulation of oscillations, we construct an algorithm is constructed in Lagrangian variables, which is closely related to the method of characteristics for the original differential setting. Numerical experiments clearly illustrate both the dynamics of smooth solutions and the formation of a gradient catastrophe.
Additional calculations confirm the stabilizing effect of the external magnetic field. In the conclusion, the results of the research are systematized.

1.
Statement of the problem on plasma oscillations

We consider plasma as a non-relativistic electron liquid, neglecting recombination effects and ion motion. Then, in vector form, the system of hydrodynamic equations describing it, together with Maxwell’s equations, will have the form:

(1)

\ \begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\partial n\over\partial t}+\mbox{div}\,(n{\bf V})=0\,,\quad\displaystyle{\partial{\bf V}\over\partial t}+\left({\bf V}\cdot\nabla\right){\bf V}=\frac{e}{m}\,\left({\bf E}+\displaystyle{1\over c}\left[{\bf V}\times{\bf B}\right]\right),\\
\displaystyle{1\over c}\frac{\partial{\bf E}}{\partial t}=-\displaystyle{4\pi\over c}en{\bf V}+{\rm rot}\,{\bf B}\,,\quad\displaystyle{1\over c}\frac{\partial{\bf B}}{\partial t}=-{\rm rot}\,{\bf E}\,,\quad\mbox{div}\,{\bf B}=0\,,\end{array}

where

e,m
— charge and mass of the electron (here the electron charge has a negative sign:
e<0
),

c
— speed of light;

n,{\bf V}
— density and velocity of
electrons;

{\bf E},{\bf B}
— vectors of electric and magnetic fields.
The system of equations (
1
) is one of the simplest models of plasma, which is often called the equations of hydrodynamics of ”cold” plasma;
it is well known and described in sufficient detail in textbooks and monographs (see, for example,
[
3
]
,
[
13
]
).

Since the complete system is very difficult to study, physicists traditionally use various simplifications to study the effect of an external magnetic field. They come from the assumption of the smallness of various parameters, expansion in a series and retention of physically significant terms. One of these models is a system that allows one to take into account the effects of an external magnetic field, which appeared, apparently, for the first time, in
[
8
]
,
see also
[
9
]
, and has since emerged in various physical contexts, e.g.
[
15
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
. The solutions of this system are called in
[
8
]

nonlinear zero-temperature Bernstein modes.

The system has the following form:

(2)

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{\partial n\over\partial t}+\displaystyle{\partial\over\partial x}\left(n\,V_{1}\right)=0,\quad\displaystyle{\partial V_{1}\over\partial t}+V_{1}\displaystyle{\partial v_{1}\over\partial x}=\frac{e}{m}\left[E_{1}+\displaystyle{1\over c}\,V_{2}B_{0}\right],\\
\displaystyle{\partial V_{2}\over\partial t}+V_{1}\displaystyle{\partial V_{2}\over\partial x}=-\displaystyle{e\over m}\,V_{1}B_{0},\\
\displaystyle{\partial E_{1}\over\partial t}=-4\,\pi\,e\,n\,V_{1}.\end{array}

Here

{\bf V}=(V_{1}(x,t),V_{2}(x,t),0),\quad{\bf E}(x,t)=(E_{1}(x,t),0,0),

where
(x,y,z)
are the Cartesian coordinate.
It immediately implies that

{\rm rot}\,{\bf E}=0
(the electric field is irrotational), in other words, the respective oscillations are electrostatic. The magnetic field does not depend on time and space in the model, it is directed along the axis
z
,
{\bf B}(x,t)=(0,0,B_{0})
,
B_{0}\equiv{\rm const}
, see Fig.
1
.

Figure 1.
The components of solution
V_{1},V_{2},E_{1}
and their dependence on coordinates

Despite its apparent simplicity, system (
2
) allows one to describe many interesting nonlinear effects.
We emphasize that it is not a direct consequence of (
1
), but uses additional assumptions.
System (
2
) is often called the equations of
upper hybrid
oscillations of cold magnetoactive plasma (see, for example,
[
9
]
) in the sense that it describes the dynamics of high Fourier harmonics and can be studied independently. The hybridity is understood as a special type of perturbation of the plasma frequency by an external magnetic field.

For convenience, we introduce the dimensionless quantities

\begin{array}[]{c}\rho=k_{p}x,\quad\theta=\omega_{p}t,\quad{\hat{V}_{1}}=\displaystyle{V_{1}\over c},\quad{\hat{V}_{2}}=\displaystyle{V_{2}\over c},\\
{\hat{E}_{1}}=-\,\displaystyle{e\,E_{1}\over m\,c\,\omega_{p}},\quad{\hat{N}}=\displaystyle{n\over n_{0}},\quad{\hat{B}}_{0}=-\,\displaystyle{e\,B_{0}\over m\,c\,\omega_{p}},\end{array}

where
\omega_{p}=\left(4\pi e^{2}n_{0}/m\right)^{1/2}
is the plasma frequency,
n_{0}
is the value of the unperturbed electron
density,
k_{p}=\omega_{p}/c
.
In the new variables, the system (
2
) takes the form

(3)

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{\partial{\hat{N}}\over\partial\theta}+\displaystyle{\partial\over\partial\rho}\left({\hat{N}}\,{\hat{V}}_{1}\right)=0,\\
\displaystyle{\partial{\hat{V}}_{1}\over\partial\theta}+{\hat{V}}_{1}\displaystyle{\partial{\hat{V}}_{1}\over\partial\rho}=-{\hat{E}}_{1}-{\hat{V}}_{2}{\hat{B}}_{0},\quad\displaystyle{\partial{\hat{V}}_{2}\over\partial\theta}+{\hat{V}}_{1}\displaystyle{\partial{\hat{V}}_{2}\over\partial\rho}={\hat{V}}_{1}{\hat{B}}_{0},\\
\displaystyle{\partial{\hat{E}}_{1}\over\partial\theta}={\hat{N}}\,{\hat{V}}_{1}.\end{array}

From equations

\displaystyle{\partial{\hat{N}}\over\partial\theta}+\displaystyle{\partial\over\partial\rho}\left({\hat{N}}\,{\hat{V}}_{1}\right)=0,\quad\displaystyle{\partial{\hat{E}}_{1}\over\partial\theta}={\hat{N}}\,{\hat{V}}_{1}

we have

\displaystyle{\partial\over\partial\theta}\left[{\hat{N}}+\displaystyle{\partial\over\partial\rho}{\hat{E}}_{1}\right]=0.

This relationship is valid both in the absence of plasma oscillations and in their presence. Therefore, under the traditional assumption of a uniform background charge density of stationary ions, this implies a simpler expression for the electron density
{\hat{N}}(\rho,\theta)
:

(4)

{\hat{N}}(\rho,\theta)=1-\displaystyle{\partial{\hat{E}}_{1}(\rho,\theta)\over\partial\rho}.

Formula (
4
) is a special case of the Gauss theorem
[
9
]
, which in differential dimensional form is

\mbox{div}\,{\bf E}=4\,\pi\,e(n-n_{0}).

Using (
4
) in (
3
), we arrive at the equations describing plane one-dimensional electrostatic plasma
oscillations. For convenience, we remove the ”hat” from all dimensionless components of solution and obtain

(5)

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{\partial V_{1}\over\partial\theta}+V_{1}\displaystyle{\partial V_{1}\over\partial\rho}=-E_{1}-B_{0}\,V_{2},\quad\displaystyle{\partial V_{2}\over\partial\theta}+V_{1}\displaystyle{\partial V_{2}\over\partial\rho}=B_{0}\,V_{1},\\
\displaystyle{\partial E_{1}\over\partial\theta}+V_{1}\displaystyle{\partial E_{1}\over\partial\rho}=V_{1}.\end{array}

We equip the equations (
5
) with the initial conditions:

(6)

V_{1}(\rho,0)=V_{1}^{0}(\rho),\quad V_{2}(\rho,0)=V_{2}^{0}(\rho),\quad E(\rho,0)=E^{0}(\rho),\quad\rho\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Completing the formulation of the problems, we recall the electron density formula useful for what follows:

(7)

{N}(\rho,\theta)=1-\displaystyle{\partial{E}(\rho,\theta)\over\partial\rho}.

System (
5
) is of hyperbolic type. It is well known that for such systems, there exists, locally in time, a unique solution to the Cauchy problem of the same class as the initial data. It is also known that for such systems the loss of smoothness by the solution occurs according to one of the following scenarios: either the solution components themselves go to infinity during a finite time, or they remain bounded, but their derivatives
[
6
]
turn to infinity. The latter possibility is realized, for example, for homogeneous conservation laws, which include the equations of gas dynamics, where the appearance of a singularity corresponds to the formation of a shock wave (a gradient catastrophe,
[
24
]
).

2.
A criterion for the formation of a singularity of the Cauchy problem

Theorem 1
.

For the existence of a
C^{1}
smooth
\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{1+B_{0}^{2}}}
- periodic solution

(V_{1}(\theta,\rho),\,V_{2}(\theta,\rho),\,E(\theta,\rho))
of the problem (
5
), (
6
),
(V_{1}^{0},V_{2}^{0},E^{0})\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
,
is necessary and sufficient that at any point
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
the condition

(8)

\Delta= \left((V_{1}^{0})^{\prime}\right)^{2}+2\,(E^{0})^{\prime}+2B_{0}\,(V_{2}^{0})^{\prime}-B_{0}^{2}-1<0.

If the opposite inequality (
8
) holds at least at one point
\rho_{0}
, then the derivatives of the solution turn to infinity in a finite time.

Proof. Along the characteristics, the system (
5
) has the form

(9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{dV_{1}\over d\theta}=-E_{1}-B_{0}\,V_{2},\quad\displaystyle{dV_{2}\over d\theta}=B_{0}\,V_{1},\quad\displaystyle{dE_{1}\over d\theta}=V_{1},\quad\displaystyle{d\rho\over d\theta}=V_{1}.

This implies
V_{1}^{2}+V_{2}^{2}+E^{2}=V_{1}^{2}(0)+V_{2}^{2}(0)+E^{2}(0)=\rm const
along each characteristic
\rho=\phi(\theta)
, starting from the point
(\rho_{0},0)
, and the solution remains bounded. Thus, the loss of smoothness by the solution can only be associated with the infinity of the derivatives.

Note that for a technical reason, to avoid further complication, we require a greater smoothness for the initial data. We denote
q_{1}=\partial_{\rho}V_{1}
,
q_{2}=\partial_{\rho}V_{2}
,
e=\partial_{\rho}E
, differentiate (
5
) with respect to
\rho
and get

(10)

\displaystyle{dq_{1}\over d\theta}=-q_{1}^{2}-B_{0}q_{2}-e,\quad\displaystyle{dq_{2}\over d\theta}=-q_{1}q_{2}+B_{0}q_{1},\quad\displaystyle{de\over d\theta}=(1-e)q_{1},

which together with (
9
) forms an extended system.

The positiveness of the density implies
e<1
, see (
7
), therefore, below we consider only this part of the phase space. The case
e=1
corresponding to vacuum points must be considered separately.

System (
10
) does not depend on (
9
),
it is integrable. Indeed, the last two equations imply

(11)

q_{2}=B_{0}+C_{1}(e-1),\quad C_{1}=\frac{q_{2}(\rho_{0},0)-B_{0}}{e(\rho_{0},0)-1}.

Further, taking into account (
11
), from the first and third equations (
10
) we obtain

(12)

\displaystyle C_{2}(e-1)^{2}=q_{1}^{2}+2(e-1)(B_{0}C_{1}+1)+B_{0}^{2}+1,

\displaystyle C_{2}=\frac{q^{2}_{1}(\rho_{0},0)+2(e(\rho_{0},0)-1)(B_{0}C_{1}+1)+B_{0}^{2}+1}{(1-e(\rho_{0},0))^{2}}.

From (
12
), in particular, it follows that
q_{2}
can go to infinity only together with
e
.

On the plane
(e,q_{1})
, the algebraic equation (
12
) defines a second-order curve that is bounded (an ellipse) if
C_{2}
, given as (
12
), is negative.
If we substitute here the value of
C_{1}
from (
11
), then we get the condition
(
8
).

Otherwise, the curve (
12
), the projection of the phase curve of the system (
10
) onto the plane
(e,q_{1})
, is a parabola or hyperbola, and the derivatives
(e,q_{1})
go to infinity on it.

Let us show that this happens in a finite time.
Indeed, from (
10
), (
12
) it follows that
e
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle{de\over d\theta}=\pm(e-1)\sqrt{C_{2}(e-1)^{2}-2(e-1)(B_{0}C_{1}+1)-B_{0}^{2}-1},

therefore

(13)

\pm\int\limits_{e(\theta_{*})}^{e(\theta^{*})}\displaystyle{de\over(e-1)\sqrt{C_{2}(e-1)^{2}-2(e-1)(B_{0}C_{1}+1)-B_{0}^{2}-1}}=\theta^{*}-\theta_{*},

where
(\theta_{*},e(\theta_{*}))
is the starting point of integration, and
(\theta^{*},e(\theta^{*}))
is the final one.
If the time for going to infinity is infinite, then
\theta^{*}=\infty
,
e(\theta^{*})=\pm\infty
, and the integral on the left side of (
13
) must diverge. However, it is easy to see that it is convergent. The resulting contradiction completes the proof for the case
e<1
.

If
e=1
, then system (
10
) is reduced to

\displaystyle{dq_{1}\over d\theta}=-q_{1}^{2}-B_{0}q_{2}-1,\quad\displaystyle{dq_{2}\over d\theta}=-q_{1}q_{2}+B_{0}q_{1},

its first integral under the assumption
q_{2}\neq B_{0}
has the form

q_{1}^{2}+2B_{0}q_{2}-B_{0}^{2}+1=C_{3}(q_{2}-B_{0})^{2}.

This curve is defined on the phase plane
(q_{1},q_{2})
under the condition
C_{3}<0
, which coincides with (
8
) if we substitute in the last
e=1
.

Finally, in the case
e=1
,
q_{2}=B_{0}
, system (
10
) reduces to one equation

\displaystyle{dq_{1}\over d\theta}=-q_{1}^{2}-B_{0}^{2}-1,

the solution of which for any initial data turns to infinity in a finite time. It is easy to see that in this case condition (
8
) cannot be met.

Formula (
13
) allows calculating the period
T
of revolution along the phase trajectory for
C_{1}<0
. Namely,

\frac{T}{2}=\int\limits_{e_{-}}^{e_{+}}\displaystyle{de\over(e-1)\sqrt{C_{2}(e-1)^{2}-2(e-1)(B_{0}C_{1}+1)-B_{0}^{2}-1}},

where
e_{\pm}
is the smaller and larger root of the equation
C_{2}(e-1)^{2}-2(e-1)(B_{0}C_{1}+1)-B_{0}^{2}-1=0
.
Standard calculations show that
T=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{1+B_{0}^{2}}}
for all
C_{1}<0
and
C_{2}
.
The same result follows from (
9
), since for smooth solutions

\displaystyle{d^{2}V_{1}\over d\theta^{2}}+(1+B_{0}^{2})V_{1}=0.

Thus, the theorem is completely proved.

3.
Subclasses of solutions and traveling waves in the nonrelativistic approximation

3.1.
First integrals and subclasses of solutions

As shown in
[
22
]
, in the case
B_{0}=0
,
V_{2}=0
, there is a subclass of solutions distinguished by the condition

E^{2}(\rho_{0},0)+V_{1}^{2}(\rho_{0},0)=K
, where
K
is a constant common to all points of
\rho_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. In other words, the first integral of equation (
9
) was assumed to be equal to the same constant along all characteristics.

In our case, there are three equations, two functionally independent first integrals. They are, for example,

(14)

V_{2}-B_{0}E_{1}=K_{1},\quad V_{1}^{2}+V_{2}^{2}+E_{1}^{2}=K_{2}.

It is easy to see that for the case
B_{0}=0
,
V_{2}=0
, the integral
K_{2}
corresponds to
K
from
[
22
]
.

In addition, any differentiable function of
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
is also a first integral. This gives great opportunities for reducing the dimension of the system (reducing to two or one equations).

A simple example is a system distinguished by the condition
K_{1}=0
, that is

\displaystyle{\partial V_{1}\over\partial\theta}=-(1+B_{0}^{2})E_{1},\quad\displaystyle{\partial E_{1}\over\partial\theta}=V_{1},

the solution with data (
6
) for which has the form

\begin{array}[]{c}E_{1}(\rho,\theta)=E_{0}(\rho)\,\cos(\omega\,\theta),\quad V_{1}(\rho,\theta)=-\,E_{0}(\rho)\,\omega\,\sin(\omega\,\theta),\end{array}

with
\omega=\sqrt{1+B_{0}^{2}}.

Remark 4
.

It should be noted that the statement about the stabilizing effect of the magnetic field may not be valid for certain subclasses of solutions. For example, for the subclass considered above, distinguished by the condition
K_{1}=0
, from (
8
) we get

\Delta=q_{1}^{2}+(2e-1)(1+B_{0}^{2}),

and at the points where
e>\frac{1}{2}
, increasing
B_{0}
will not make
\Delta
negative. However, this situation differs from the one described in Remark
2
, since the condition
K_{1}=0
implies that
B_{0}
participates in the initial data and we cannot
increase
B_{0}
fixing the initial data.

3.2.
Traveling waves

For the equations of cold plasma, solutions in the form of waves traveling with constant velocity (quasi-stationary solutions) are well known to physicists
[
1
,
2
]
. In our work
[
22
]
for the case
B_{0}=0
we constructed a subclass of solutions for which the first integral is constant.

For the system (
5
), traveling waves can also be constructed. On such solutions both functionally independent first integrals are constant and for their initial data the condition
\Delta<0
from (
8
) is necessarily satisfied.

Let us
V_{1}(\theta,\rho)={\mathcal{V}}_{1}(\xi)
,
V_{2}(\theta,\rho)={\mathcal{V}}_{2}(\xi)
,

E(\theta,\rho)={\mathcal{E}}(\xi)
,
\xi=\rho-w\theta
,
w=\rm const
. From (
5
) we get the system

(15)

(-w+{\mathcal{V}}_{1}){\mathcal{V}}_{1}\,^{\prime}=-{\mathcal{E}}-B_{0}{\mathcal{V}}_{1},\quad(-w+{\mathcal{V}}_{1}){\mathcal{E}}\,^{\prime}={\mathcal{V}}_{1},\quad(-w+{\mathcal{V}}_{1}){\mathcal{V}}_{2}\,^{\prime}=B_{0}{\mathcal{V}}_{1}.

We choose the first integrals as

(16)

{\mathcal{V}}_{2}-B_{0}{\mathcal{E}}=K_{1},\quad{\mathcal{V}}_{1}^{2}+{\mathcal{V}}_{2}^{2}+{\mathcal{E}}^{2}=K_{2},

whence follows

{\mathcal{V}}_{1}^{2}+(1+B_{0}^{2})\left({\mathcal{E}}+\frac{K_{1}B_{0}}{1+B_{0}^{2}}\right)^{2}=K_{2}+\frac{K_{1}^{2}B_{0}^{2}}{1+B_{0}^{2}}=K_{3}\,(>0)

and

{\mathcal{E}}=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+B_{0}^{2}}}\sqrt{{K_{3}}-{\mathcal{V}}_{1}^{2}}-\frac{K_{1}B_{0}}{1+B_{0}^{2}}.

From (
15
), (
16
) we get the equation for the traveling wave profile:

(17)

{\mathcal{V}}_{1}\,^{\prime}=\frac{\pm\sqrt{1+B_{0}^{2}}\sqrt{K_{3}-{\mathcal{V}}_{1}^{2}}}{-w+{\mathcal{V}}_{1}}.

Since

{\mathcal{V}}_{1}^{2}\leq K_{3}\,(>0),

then under the condition

w^{2}>K_{3}

the denominator of (
17
) preserves the sign and the function
{\mathcal{V}}_{1}
at each half-period increases or decreases in
\xi
,
changing between
{\mathcal{V}}_{\pm}=\pm{K_{3}}
.
The period
X
can be calculated as

X=\frac{2}{\sqrt{1+B_{0}^{2}}}\,{\rm sign}\,w\int\limits_{-\sqrt{K_{3}}}^{\sqrt{K_{3}}}\frac{-w+\eta}{\sqrt{K_{3}-\eta^{2}}}\,d\eta=\frac{2\pi|w|}{\sqrt{1+B_{0}^{2}}}.

The profile
{\mathcal{V}}_{1}
can be found implicitly as

\left(\sqrt{1+B_{0}^{2}}\xi+c\right)^{2}=\left(w\arcsin\frac{{\mathcal{V}}_{1}}{\sqrt{K_{3}}}+\sqrt{K_{3}-{\mathcal{V}}_{1}^{2}}\right)^{2},

c=w\arcsin\frac{{\mathcal{V}}_{1}(0)}{\sqrt{K_{2}}}+\sqrt{K_{3}-({\mathcal{V}}_{1}(0))^{2}}
.

4.
Numerical illustrations

The approach to the analysis of characteristics used to obtain analytical results can be transformed into a high-precision approximate method for calculating nonrelativistic oscillations of a magnetoactive cold plasma. The specificity of the method lies in the identification of the function of displacement of particles (electrons) in Lagrangian variables with a function that characterizes the electric field in Euler variables. In fact, the method presented below is a method of characteristics; however, it is convenient to present its construction in terms of the Lagrangian
descriptions of the medium, i.e. using the concepts of particles and their trajectories.

Note that the simulated medium is represented by charged particles located in the background field formed by stationary ions. Therefore, for each particle there is an ”equilibrium” position when the background field is canceled and which is convenient to take as the value of its Lagrangian coordinate. In this case, the function of displacement relative to the equilibrium position is responsible for the formation of the electric field:

(18)

\rho(\rho^{L},\theta)=\rho^{L}+R(\rho^{L},\theta),

where
\rho^{L}
is the ”equilibrium” position of the particle when it does not contribute to the formation of the electric field,
R(\rho^{L},\theta)
is its displacement function, which generates the electric field at the point of the trajectory
\rho(\rho^{L},\theta)
. According to
[
10
]
(see also
[
5
]
), in the flat one-dimensional case, there is a simple relationship between the field and the displacement

(19)

R(\rho^{L},\theta)=E(\rho,\theta)\equiv E(\rho^{L}+R(\rho^{L},\theta),\theta),

which makes the functions of the electric field and displacement on the particle trajectory indistinguishable.
Note that this approach additionally makes it possible not to solve simultaneously two identical differential equations with different initial conditions (to determine the Euler trajectory of a particle and the electric field along it).
In this case, the formal transition from the Euler coordinates
(\rho,\theta)
to the Lagrangian coordinates
(\rho^{L},\tau)
is carried out by the usual transformation

\tau\equiv\theta,\quad\rho^{L}=\rho-\int\limits_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}V_{1}(\rho^{L},\tau^{\prime})\,,

but instead of
\tau
, the old notation
\theta
is used.

4.1.
Numerical algorithm

To find a numerical solution on the line
\rho\in(-\infty,\infty)
, we define an arbitrary grid at the initial moment of time
\theta=0
:

\rho_{1}(0)<\rho_{2}(0)<\dots<\rho_{M}(0),

consisting of
M

\rho_{k}(\theta=0),\;1\leq k\leq M,

Moreover, for each particle from (

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{d\,V_{1}(\rho^{L}_{k},\theta)\over d\,\theta}=-R(\rho^{L}_{k},\theta)-B_{0}V_{2}(\rho^{L}_{k},\theta),\quad\displaystyle{d\,V_{2}(\rho^{L}_{k},\theta)\over d\,\theta}=B_{0}V_{1}(\rho^{L}_{k},\theta),\\

Then we use (
18

At the node
\rho_{k}
for
\theta=0
the value

is defined, namely,
\rho_{k}=\rho^{L}_{k}+E^{0}(\rho_{k})

with the number

\begin{array}[]{c}V_{1}(\rho^{L}_{k},\theta=0)=V_{1}^{0}(\rho_{k}),\quad V_{2}(\rho^{L}_{k},\theta=0)=V_{2}^{0}(\rho_{k}),\\

and the equilibrium value of the Lagrangian coordinate

The solution of (

\rho_{k}(\theta)=\rho^{L}_{k}+R(\rho^{L}_{k},\theta),\quad k=1,2,\dots,M,

If the solution has sufficient smoothness in the variable

Let us stress that the accuracy of the obtained approximation is determined exclusively by the smoothness of the solution.

Note also that the stability of the time integration of equations (

22

\rho_{k+1}(\theta)-\rho_{k}(\theta)>0\quad\forall k=1,2,\dots,M-1.

Summarizing the above description of the numerical algorithm, we emphasize that to solve the original problem (
5

\rho_{k}(\theta^{n}),\quad k=1,2,\dots,M,

\theta^{n}=\theta^{n-1}+\tau_{n},\;n=1,2,\dots,
belonging to trajectories of particles. This is quite enough for the study of most problems. However, it is quite possible that situations arise when it is required to determine the solution at the given Euler points
(\rho,\theta)

In this case, at the moment of time
\theta^{n}

\rho

A summary of the blow up effect in the framework of the non-relativistic hydrodynamic model of cold plasma is as follows. Let us fix arbitrary initial data. The initial spatial distribution of the electron density

5.
Conclusion

In the present work, the effect of an external magnetic field on plane nonrelativistic nonlinear plasma electrostatic oscillations generated by a powerful laser pulse is investigated analytically and numerically. A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a smooth solution of the corresponding hyperbolic system of equations is obtained.
It is shown that with an increase in the intensity of the external magnetic field, the set of initial data corresponding to a globally smooth solution extends. To analyze the regularizing effect of an external field, we analyzed the subclasses of solutions to the problem on which the reduction of the number of equations is allowed, including traveling waves.
For the purpose of numerical simulation, an algorithm is constructed in Lagrangian variables, which is closely related to the method of characteristics for the original differential setting.

Numerical experiments clearly illustrate both the dynamics of smooth solutions and the formation of a gradient catastrophe. Additionally, calculations of the stabilizing effect of an external magnetic field are presented. The results obtained can be used to simulate upper hybrid oscillations at relativistic electron velocities, i.e. to study the influence of an external magnetic field on the breaking effect of multi-period plasma oscillations. In addition, it is possible to extend the results to the case of taking into account electron - ion collisions.

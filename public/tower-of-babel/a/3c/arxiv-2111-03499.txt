Justification of the KP-II approximation in dynamics of two-dimensional FPU systems
Nikolay Hristov, Dmitry E. Pelinovsky

Abstract
. Dynamics of the Fermi–Pasta–Ulam (FPU) system on a two-dimensional square lattice is considered in the limit of small-amplitude long-scale waves with slow transverse modulations. In the absence of transverse modulations, dynamics of such waves, even at an oblique angle with respect to the square lattice, is known to be described by the Korteweg–de Vries (KdV) equation. For the three basic directions (horizontal, vertical, and diagonal), we prove that the modulated waves are well described by the Kadomtsev–Petviashvili (KP-II) equation. The result was expected long ago but proving rigorous bounds on the approximation error turns out to be complicated due to the nonlocal terms of the KP-II equation and the vector structure of the FPU systems on two-dimensional lattices. We have obtained these error bounds by extending the local well-posedness result for the KP-II equation in Sobolev spaces and by controlling the error terms with energy estimates. The bounds are useful in the analysis of transverse stability of solitary and periodic waves in two-dimensional FPU systems due to many results available for the KP-II equation.



1.
Introduction

A Fermi-Pasta-Ulam (FPU) system is formed by particles connected
to their nearest neighbours by nonlinear springs. If the particles are
organized in a one-dimensional chain, we can label the position variable
of the
j
-th particle by
q_{j}
. If we
denote the corresponding velocity variable by
p_{j}
, then the FPU system is generated by the Hamiltonian function in the form

H(p,q)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2}p_{j}^{2}+V(q_{j+1}-q_{j}),

(1.1)

where
V(q)
is the potential energy. A useful approximation
to dynamics of small-amplitude long-scale waves in the FPU chain
with smooth
V
satisfying
V^{\prime}(0)=0
,
V^{\prime\prime}(0)>0
, and
V^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)\neq 0

is given by the Korteweg–de Vries (KdV) equation, which is a remarkable
model due to integrability, stability of periodic and solitary waves, and global existence of solutions in Sobolev spaces (see
[
10
]
for review).

Bounds on the approximation error between solutions of the FPU system and the KdV equation were obtained by G. Schneider and C.E. Wayne
in a conference proceeding
[
30
]
as an exercise related to
the justification technique the
authors had developed for the water wave problem
[
31
]
.
The same approximation appeared to be very useful in the context of
stability of FPU solitary waves and was studied comprehensively
in the series of papers by G. Friesecke and R. L. Pego
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
.
It was also justified in the context of normal forms and KAM theory
for metastability and recurrency of the FPU systems
[
2
,
29
]
.

More recently, the same approximation but with other
versions of the KdV equation was justified for FPU systems with Hertzian potentials
[
8
]
and with pure anharmonic powers
[
25
]
. In
[
25
]
, the KdV approximation was extended to logarithmically long time scales provided the global dynamics of the generalized KdV equation is well defined in Sobolev spaces of higher regularity.
The KdV approximation was also used in the context of periodic
waves and for FPU systems with nonlocal interactions
[
12
,
23
]
.
Higher-order corrections to the KdV approximation were studied in
[
20
]
,
where it was shown that the second-order corrections are spanned by
two members of the integrable KdV hierarchy whereas the third-order corrections
can only be spanned by three members of the KdV hierarchy under a constraint on parameters of the FPU system. Review of results on nonlinear waves in one-dimensional FPU chains can be found in
[
36
]
.

The purpose of this work is to consider dynamics of the two-dimensional FPU systems and to justify the two-dimensional KdV approximation given by the
Kadomtsev–Petviashvili (KP-II) equation.
Similar to the KdV equation,
the KP-II equation is remarkable due to integrability
[
39
]
, stability of periodic and solitary waves
[
22
,
27
]
, and global existence of solutions in Sobolev spaces
[
21
,
28
]
.

The first formal derivation of the KP-II equation was performed in
[
9
]
for the scalar extension of the two-dimensional FPU systems with the total energy of the form

H=\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{2}p_{j,k}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(q_{j+1,k}-q_{j,k})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}(q_{j,k+1}-q_{j,k})^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\alpha(q_{j+1,k}-q_{j,k})^{3},

(1.2)

where
\varepsilon^{2}
is a small parameter of slow transverse modulations
in the
k
-direction of the dominant wave propagating in the
j
direction and
\alpha
is the parameter for the cubic interaction potential. A similar scalar model was considered in the recent work
[
19
]
, where the KP-II equation in the periodic domain was rigorously justified (among other integrable models) as the normal form for metastability phenomenon in two-dimensional rectangular lattices.
Existence of two-dimensional solitary waves
[
6
]
and breathers (space-localized and time-periodic solutions)
[
37
,
38
]
was also studied
in the scalar two-dimensional FPU lattices. Extensions of the scalar two-dimensional FPU models that include harmonic interactions between the first and second nearest neighbors were considered in
[
1
,
24
]
.
Applications of the scalar FPU models to the two-dimensional square-packed granular arrays were studied numerically and experimentally in
[
26
]
, where propagation of a modulated one-dimensional solitary wave was observed.
Periodic triangular lattices were compared with square lattices
in the numerical study in
[
3
,
4
]
, where it was shown that non-square lattices do not have integrable approximations leading to the energy localization phenomenon.

The scalar problem can not be realized mechanically in the dynamics of
particles organized in a two-dimensional square lattice and connected by the nonlinear springs. On the other hand, in the context of mechanical models of the elasticity theory, the long-wave reductions were used to derive the one-dimensional KdV equation (rather than the two-dimensional KP-II equation)
from the vector two-dimensional FPU systems.

A two-dimensional square FPU lattice was considered in
[
11
]
with additional springs along the diagonals and a pair of potential functions, one for the horizontal and vertical displacements and the other one for the diagonal displacements. Existence of small-amplitude supersonic longitudinal solitary waves propagating along the horizontal direction was proven in
[
11
]
. Surprisingly, the result holds even when the potential functions are quadratic,
due to the geometric nonlinearity of the lattice. Nonlinear couplings were included in the consideration of the same model in
[
7
]
, where the solitary wave propagated under an arbitrary angle with respect to the square lattice.

Another work can be found in
[
32
]
, where propagation
of rings in two-dimensional lattices was analyzed in the linear approximation
and compared rigorously with the approximation of the linearized KdV equation (rather than with the linearized KP-II equation).

Compared to the previous works, we prove validity of the KP-II approximation for dynamics of transversely modulated small-amplitude long-scale waves in a vector FPU system on a two-dimensional square lattice.

The potential energy of a single spring between two particles in the horizontal direction is defined by

V(r,s)=\frac{1}{2}(c_{1}^{2}r^{2}+c_{2}^{2}s^{2})+\frac{1}{3}\alpha_{1}r^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{2}rs^{2},

where
(r,s)
are the relative displacements of the two particles in the
(x,y)
coordinates,
(c_{1}^{2},c_{2}^{2})
are some coefficients of the quadratic interaction potential, and
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
are some coefficients of the cubic interaction potential. The cubic terms are chosen in such a way that the potential energy of the horizontal spring is symmetric with respect to the sign of vertical relative displacements of the particles. Due to the symmetry between horizontal and vertical springs, the total energy of the two-dimensional FPU lattice takes the form

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\dot{x}_{j,k}^{2}+\dot{y}_{j,k}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}V(x_{j+1,k}-x_{j,k},y_{j+1,k}-y_{j,k})+V(y_{j,k+1}-y_{j,k},x_{j,k+1}-x_{j,k}).

(1.3)

As is discussed in
[
17
]
, the model with horizontal and vertical springs may not capture all properties of elastic materials and diagonal springs are required to describe structural stability of some materials. The KP-II approximation in the square lattices with additional diagonal springs should be possible, but computations of coefficients will become more complicated.

Next we present the main result for the propagation of nonlinear waves in the horizontal direction. We will seek a continuous approximating function of the form

\displaystyle x_{j,k}=\varepsilon X\left(\xi,\eta,\tau\right)+\text{error},

(1.4)

with
\xi=\varepsilon(j-c_{1}t)
,
\eta=\varepsilon^{2}k
, and
\tau=\varepsilon^{3}t
. We will show that
x_{j,k}
satisfy the equations of motion with small error controllable in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
if
X\left(\xi,\eta,\tau\right)
solves the following KP-II equation

\displaystyle 2c_{1}\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\tau}X+\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{12}\partial_{\xi}^{4}X+2\alpha_{1}\left(\partial_{\xi}X\right)\left(\partial^{2}_{\xi}X\right)+c_{2}^{2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}X=0.

(1.5)

For justification analysis, it is more convenient to solve the FPU system in strain variables,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{j,k}^{(1)}:=x_{j+1,k}-x_{j,k},\\
u_{j,k}^{(2)}:=x_{j,k+1}-x_{j,k},\\
v_{j,k}^{(1)}:=y_{j+1,k}-y_{j,k},\\
v_{j,k}^{(2)}:=y_{j,k+1}-y_{j,k},\end{array}\right.

which are defined by the relative displacements between adjacent particles,
and introduce the amplitude function
A\left(\xi,\eta,\tau\right)
in the form

\displaystyle x_{j+1,k}-x_{j,k}=\varepsilon^{2}A\left(\xi,\eta,\tau\right)+\text{error}.

(1.10)

The reason for the different scaling in (
1.4
) and (
1.10
) is that we can formally consider the relationship between the function
A
and
X
through a Taylor expansion

A\left(\xi,\eta,\tau\right)=\partial_{\xi}X\left(\xi,\eta,\tau\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon),

(1.11)

so that the KP-II equation (
1.5
) can be rewritten in the form

\displaystyle 2c_{1}\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\tau}A+\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{12}\partial_{\xi}^{4}A+2\alpha_{1}\partial_{\xi}\left(A\partial_{\xi}A\right)+c_{2}^{2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A=0,

(1.12)

Associated with a smooth solution to the KP–II equation (
1.12
) at a given time
\tau
, for which both
A\in H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
and
\partial_{\xi}^{-1}A\in H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
with sufficiently large
s
,
we define the anti-derivative by

\partial_{\xi}^{-1}A(\xi,\eta):=\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}A(\xi^{\prime},\eta)d\xi^{\prime}.

The solution to the KP-II equation (
1.12
) is required to have enough regularity so that
\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\tau}^{2}A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\right)
and
\partial_{\xi}^{-2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}\partial_{\tau}A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\right)
with
s\geq 3
. Existence of such solutions is proven in Lemma
1
(Section
2
). Since
A(\varepsilon(j-c_{1}t),\varepsilon^{2}k,\varepsilon^{3}t)

is estimated in the
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
norm over
(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we will also need the bound on the
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
norm of these terms by their
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
norm, this bound is obtained in Lemma
2
(Section
2
).

The following theorem formulates the main result which justifies the KP-II approximation (
1.12
) for the horizontal propagation of nonlinear waves.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+9}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\right)
be a solution to the KP-II equation
(
1.12
)
with fixed
s\geq 0
, whose initial data

A(\xi,\eta,0)=A_{0}
satisfies

A_{0}\in H^{s+9}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right),\qquad\partial_{\xi}^{-2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A_{0}\in H^{s+9}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)

and

\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}^{2}\left[\partial_{\xi}^{-2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A_{0}+A_{0}^{2}\right]\in H^{s+3}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right).

Then there are constants
C_{0},C_{1},\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
if the initial conditions of the two-dimensional FPU system satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\lVert u^{(1)}_{\text{in}}-\varepsilon^{2}A_{0}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert u^{(2)}_{\text{in}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert\dot{x}_{\text{in}}+\varepsilon^{2}c_{1}A_{0}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}\\
&+\left\lVert v^{(1)}_{\text{in}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert v^{(2)}_{\text{in}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert\dot{y}_{\text{in}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}\leq C_{0}\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{2}}\end{split}

(1.13)

then the solution to the two-dimensional FPU system satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\lVert u^{(1)}(t)-\varepsilon^{2}A\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert u^{(2)}(t)\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert\dot{x}(t)+\varepsilon^{2}c_{1}A\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}\\
&+\left\lVert v^{(1)}(t)\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert v^{(2)}(t)\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert\dot{y}(t)\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}\leq C_{1}\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{2}},\end{split}

(1.14)

for
t\in[-\tau_{0}\varepsilon^{-3},\tau_{0}\varepsilon^{-3}]
.

Remark 1
.

Extending this result to
\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{2}}
is difficult as the next order of the asymptotic expansion has terms which are not removed by seeking solutions to the KP-II equation alone. They could be removed by seeking a function of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\lVert u^{(1)}_{\text{in}}-\varepsilon^{2}A_{0}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert u^{(2)}_{\text{in}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert\dot{x}_{\text{in}}+\varepsilon^{2}c_{1}^{*}A_{0}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}\\

.

2.
Preliminary results

Consider the Cauchy problem for the normalized KP-II equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{\tau}A+\partial_{\xi}\left(A^{2}\right)+\partial_{\xi}^{3}A+\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A=0,\quad t>0,\\
A|_{t=0}=A_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

The normalized KP-II equation differs slightly from (
1.12
) and (
1.16
), in the choice of constants. However, the constants in the KP-II equation can be changed, as long as each constant is positive, through a scaling of its variables.

Global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
2.1
) was established
in
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{T}^{2}\right)
or
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
with any
s\geq 0
in
[
5
]
, provided that the initial data satisfies
the constraint

\int_{\mathbb{R}}A_{0}\left(\xi,\eta\right)d\xi=0,\quad\mbox{\rm for every }\eta.

(2.2)

The result was proven by combining local well-posedness and conservation laws, namely conservation of the
L^{2}
norm along the solution.

Local well-posedness result was extended in
[
34
]
to Sobolev spaces of the type
H^{s_{1},s_{2}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
, with
s_{1}>-\frac{1}{4}
and
s_{2}\geq 0
, the global result was also obtained by the conservation laws provided that
s_{1}\geq 0
. It was further shown in
[
33
]
that the zero mean constraint (
2.2
) can be dropped in the local-wellposedness result.

We will use the following local well-posedness result
[
18
]
(see
[
35
]
for earlier work).

Proposition 1
.

[
18
]

For any
A_{0}\in H^{s+6}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
such that

\partial_{\xi}^{-2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A_{0}\in H^{s+3}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
with fixed
s\geq 0
, there exists
\tau_{0}>0
such that the Cauchy problem (
2.1
) admits a unique solution

\displaystyle A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+6}\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+3}\right)\cap C^{2}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s}\right)

Lemma 1
.

For any
A_{0}\in H^{s+9}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)

\partial_{\xi}^{-2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A_{0}\in H^{s+6}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)

the Cauchy problem (

) admits a unique solution

\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+8}\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+5}\right)\cap C^{2}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+2}\right)

Proof.

Assume that
A
solves the Cauchy problem (
2.1
) and set

D:=\partial_{\xi}^{-2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A
. By Proposition
1
, since the initial data satisfies
A_{0}\in H^{s+9}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
and
\partial_{\xi}^{-2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A_{0}\in H^{s+6}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
with
s\geq 0
, the KP-II equation has a solution

A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+9}\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+6}\right)\cap C^{2}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+3}\right)

(2.3)

such that
\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+8}\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+5}\right)
. Taking
\partial_{\eta}^{2}\left(\cdot\right)
of the KP-II equation yields

\partial_{\xi}^{2}\partial_{\tau}D+\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\eta}^{2}\left(A^{2}\right)+\partial_{\xi}^{5}D+\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\eta}^{2}D=0.

Setting
\tilde{D}:=D+A^{2}
and taking

2.3

Taking the time derivative of the integral equation (

\frac{d}{d\tau}S(\tau-s)=-\frac{d}{ds}S(\tau-s),

and integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}D(\tau)=

\displaystyle S(\tau)\left[\Omega\tilde{D}_{0}+2A_{0}\partial_{\tau}A_{0}+\partial_{\xi}^{3}\left(A^{2}_{0}\right)\right]-2A(\tau)\partial_{\tau}A(\tau)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}S\left(\tau-s\right)\partial_{s}\left[\partial_{s}\left(A^{2}\right)+\partial_{\xi}^{3}\left(A^{2}\right)\right](s)ds.

\Omega\tilde{D}_{0}\in H^{s+3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

2.3

\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}\partial_{\tau}^{2}A

which is achieved by computing the time derivatives of the KP-II equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}^{2}A

\displaystyle=-\left(2\partial_{\xi}\left(A\partial_{\tau}A\right)+\partial_{\xi}^{3}\partial_{\tau}A+\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}^{2}\partial_{\tau}A\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}^{3}A

\displaystyle=-\left(2\partial_{\xi}\left(\left(\partial_{\tau}A\right)^{2}+A\partial_{\tau}^{2}A\right)+\partial_{\xi}^{3}\partial_{\tau}^{2}A+\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}^{2}\partial_{\tau}^{2}A\right)

It follows from (

C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s}\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\tau}^{2}A=-\left(2A\partial_{\tau}A+\partial_{\xi}^{2}\partial_{\tau}A+\partial_{\tau}D\right),

\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\tau}^{2}A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+3}\right)

\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}A\in C^{2}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+2}\right)

Proposition 2
.

[
8
]

Let
u_{j}=U(\varepsilon j)
, where

. There is a constant
C>0
such that for every

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})},\qquad\forall U\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right).

(2.7)

The following lemma generalizes Proposition
2.7

for two-dimensional square lattices.

Lemma 2
.

Let
u_{j,k}=U(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}k)
, where
U\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with fixed

C_{s}>0
that depends on

\varepsilon\in(0,1]
we have

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})}\leq C_{s}\varepsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\qquad\forall U\in H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right).

(2.8)

Proof.

We use the discrete Fourier transform defined by

\hat{u}\left(\theta,\phi\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}u_{j,k}e^{-i(j\theta+k\phi)}

with the inverse transform given by

u_{j,k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\hat{u}\left(\theta,\phi\right)e^{i(j\theta+k\phi)}d\theta d\phi.

(2.9)

Since
U\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
s>1
, the Fourier transform of
U
denoted by
\hat{U}
can be defined through the standard formula.
We represent

\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{j,k}&=U(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}k)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{U}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})e^{i(\varepsilon j\tilde{p}+\varepsilon^{2}k\tilde{q})}d\tilde{p}d\tilde{q}\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\varepsilon^{3}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{U}\left(\frac{p}{\varepsilon},\frac{q}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)e^{i(jp+kq)}dpdq\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\varepsilon^{3}}\sum_{(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{(2n-1)\pi}^{(2n+1)\pi}\int_{(2m-1)\pi}^{(2m+1)\pi}\hat{U}\left(\frac{p}{\varepsilon},\frac{q}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)e^{i(jp+kq)}dpdq\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\varepsilon^{3}}\sum_{(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\hat{U}\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi m}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)e^{i(j\theta+k\phi)}d\theta d\phi.\end{split}

(2.10)

For any finite subset
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,m)\in\Lambda}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\hat{U}\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi m}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right|d\theta d\phi\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\hat{U}\left(\frac{p}{\varepsilon},\frac{q}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right|dpdq,

hence

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\hat{U}\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi m}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right|d\theta d\phi\leq\|\hat{U}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
\|U\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}<\infty
if
U\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
s>1
.
Then we can interchange summation and integration in (
2.10
) by the Fubini-Tonelli theorem. Comparing (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) yields

\displaystyle\hat{u}\left(\theta,\phi\right)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\sum_{(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\hat{U}\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi m}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

Parseval’s identity yields

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\varepsilon^{6}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\sum_{(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\hat{U}\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi m}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right|^{2}d\theta d\phi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\varepsilon^{6}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n_{1},m_{1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
(n_{2},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\end{subarray}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\hat{U}\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi m_{1}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n_{1}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\varepsilon^{6}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n_{1},m_{1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
(n_{2},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\end{subarray}}}\times\left|\hat{U}\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi m_{2}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n_{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right|d\theta d\phi.

Denote
\left\langle x,y\right\rangle_{2}=\sqrt{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}
. Inserting the weights
\left\langle\frac{\pi m_{1}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\pi n_{1}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rangle^{-2s}_{2}
and
\left\langle\frac{\pi m_{2}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\pi n_{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rangle^{-2s}_{2}
, then applying Young’s inequality,
ab\leq\frac{1}{2}a^{2}+\frac{1}{2}b^{2}
, yields

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\varepsilon^{6}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n_{1},m_{1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
(n_{2},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\end{subarray}}\left\langle\frac{\pi m_{1}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\pi n_{1}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rangle^{-2s}_{2}\left\langle\frac{\pi m_{2}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\pi n_{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rangle^{-2s}_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\right.\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\frac{\pi m_{1}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\pi n_{1}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rangle^{4s}_{2}\left|\hat{U}\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi m_{1}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n_{1}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right|^{2}d\theta d\phi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left.\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\frac{\pi m_{2}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\pi n_{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rangle^{4s}_{2}\left|\hat{U}\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi m_{2}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n_{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right|^{2}d\theta d\phi\right).

Hence, by symmetry of coefficients, we obtain:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\varepsilon^{6}}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(n_{1},m_{1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left\langle\frac{\pi m_{1}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\pi n_{1}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rangle^{-2s}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(n_{2},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left\langle\frac{\pi m_{2}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\pi n_{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rangle^{2s}_{2}\left|\hat{U}\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi m_{2}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n_{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right|^{2}d\theta d\phi\right).

When
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
the double series in first term in
\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
converges for

by the integral test, hence
\exists C_{s}>0
so that

\sum_{(n_{1},m_{1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left\langle\frac{\pi m_{1}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\pi n_{1}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rangle^{-2s}_{2}<C_{s}^{2}.

The second term in
\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
is related to the
H^{s}
norm of

given by,

\displaystyle\|U\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\langle\tilde{p},\tilde{q}\right\rangle^{2s}_{2}\left|\hat{U}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\right|^{2}d\tilde{p}d\tilde{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\varepsilon^{3}}\sum_{(n_{2},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left\langle\frac{\theta+2\pi m_{2}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n_{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rangle^{2s}_{2}

\displaystyle\phantom{=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\varepsilon^{3}}\sum_{(n_{2},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}}\times\left|\hat{U}\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi m_{2}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n_{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right|^{2}d\theta d\phi.

Since

\left\langle\frac{\pi m_{2}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\pi n_{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rangle^{2s}_{2}\leq\left\langle\frac{\theta+2\pi m_{2}}{\varepsilon},\frac{\phi+2\pi n_{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rangle^{2s}_{2},\quad\forall\theta,\phi\in[-\pi,\pi],

the second term in
\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
is bounded above by
(2\pi)^{2}\varepsilon^{3}\|U\|^{2}_{H^{s}}
. Hence, we obtain

\|u\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{-3}C_{s}^{2}\|U\|^{2}_{H^{s}}
which yields
(
2.8
).
∎

) and (
3.8
) into (
3.4
) and expanding each
W^{(j)}
in Taylor series, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\varepsilon^{2}\left(-c_{1}\partial_{\xi}U^{(1)}\right)+\varepsilon^{3}\left(-c_{1}\partial_{\xi}U^{(2)}\right)+\varepsilon^{4}\left(-c_{1}\partial_{\xi}U^{(3)}+\partial_{\tau}U^{(1)}\right)\\
&\qquad=\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{\eta}W^{(0)}+\varepsilon^{3}\partial_{\eta}W^{(1)}+\varepsilon^{4}\left(\partial_{\eta}W^{(2)}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}W^{(0)}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5}).\end{split}

(3.9)

Grouping terms by their orders in powers of
\varepsilon
and using the values for
W_{\varepsilon}
found in (
3.6
), we obtain a sequence of equations with their relevant solutions:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}):\quad-c_{1}\partial_{\xi}U^{(1)}=-c_{1}\partial_{\eta}A\\
&\phantom{O(\varepsilon^{2}):-c_{1}\partial_{\eta}U=\partial_{\eta}W^{(0)}}\quad\implies U^{(1)}=\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}A\\
&\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}):\quad-c_{1}\partial_{\xi}U^{(2)}=\frac{c_{1}}{2}\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\eta}A\\
&\phantom{O(\varepsilon^{3}):\frac{c_{1}}{2}\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\eta}U=\partial_{\eta}W^{(1)}}\quad\implies U^{(2)}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\eta}A\\
&\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}):\quad-c_{1}\partial_{\xi}U^{(3)}+\partial_{\tau}U^{(1)}=\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}\partial_{\tau}A-\frac{c_{1}}{12}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\partial_{\eta}A-\frac{c_{1}}{2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A\\
&\phantom{O(\varepsilon^{4}):\partial_{\eta}W^{(2)}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}W^{(0)}}\quad\implies U^{(3)}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A+\frac{1}{12}\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\eta}A\end{split}

(3.10)

Substituting (
3.10
) into (
3.8
) yields

U_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}A-\varepsilon^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\eta}A\right)+\varepsilon^{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A+\frac{1}{12}\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\eta}A\right).

(3.11)

Substituting decomposition (
3.2
) into the first and second equations of system (
3.1
) yield equations

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{U}_{j,k}^{(1)}&=W_{j+1,k}-W_{j,k}+Res_{j,k}^{U^{(1)}},\\
\dot{U}^{(2)}_{j,k}&=W_{j,k+1}-W_{j,k}+Res^{U^{(2)}}_{j,k},\end{split}

(3.12)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}Res_{j,k}^{U^{(1)}}&:=c_{1}\varepsilon\partial_{\xi}A-\varepsilon^{3}\partial_{\tau}A+W_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi+\varepsilon,\eta\right)-W_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta\right),\\
Res_{j,k}^{U^{(2)}}&:=c_{1}\varepsilon\partial_{\xi}{U_{\varepsilon}}-\varepsilon^{3}\partial_{\tau}{U_{\varepsilon}}+W_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta+\varepsilon^{2}\right)-W_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta\right).\end{split}

If expansions (
3.7
) and (
3.11
) are used, the residual terms have the formal order of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5})
. The third and fourth equations of system (
3.1
) have not been changed:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{V}_{j,k}^{(1)}=Z_{j+1,k}-Z_{j,k},\\
\dot{V}^{(2)}_{j,k}=Z_{j,k+1}-Z_{j,k}.\end{split}

(3.13)

Finally, the last two equations of system (
3.1
) can be rewritten explicitly as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{W}_{j,k}&=c_{1}^{2}\left[U^{(1)}_{j,k}-U^{(1)}_{j-1,k}\right]+c_{2}^{2}\left[U^{(2)}_{j,k}-U^{(2)}_{j,k-1}\right]\\
&\quad+\alpha_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\left[2AU^{(1)}_{j,k}-2A\left(\xi-\varepsilon,\eta\right)U^{(1)}_{j-1,k}+\left(U_{j,k}^{(1)}\right)^{2}-\left(U_{j-1,k}^{(1)}\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\quad+\alpha_{2}\varepsilon^{2}\left[U_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta\right)V^{(2)}_{j,k}-U_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta-\varepsilon^{2}\right)V^{(2)}_{j,k-1}+U^{(2)}_{j,k}V^{(2)}_{j,k}-U^{(2)}_{j,k-1}V^{(2)}_{j,k-1}\right]\\
&\quad+\alpha_{2}\varepsilon^{2}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(V^{(1)}_{j,k}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(V^{(1)}_{j-1,k}\right)^{2}\right]+Res^{W}_{j,k}\\
\dot{Z}_{j,k}&=c_{2}^{2}\left[V_{j,k}^{(1)}-V_{j-1,k}^{(1)}\right]+c_{1}^{2}\left[V_{j,k}^{(2)}-V_{j,k-1}^{(2)}\right]\\
&\quad+\alpha_{2}\varepsilon^{2}\left[U_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta\right)U_{j,k}^{(2)}-U_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta-\varepsilon^{2}\right)U_{j,k-1}^{(2)}+\frac{1}{2}\left(U_{j,k}^{(2)}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(U_{j,k-1}^{(2)}\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\quad+\alpha_{2}\varepsilon^{2}\left[A\left(\xi,\eta\right)V_{j,k}^{(1)}-A\left(\xi-\varepsilon,\eta\right)V_{j-1,k}^{(1)}+V^{(1)}_{j,k}U^{(1)}_{j,k}-V^{(1)}_{j-1,k}U^{(1)}_{j-1,k}\right]\\
&\quad+\alpha_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\left[\left(V^{(2)}_{j,k}\right)^{2}-\left(V^{(2)}_{j,k-1}\right)^{2}\right]+Res^{Z}_{j,k}\end{split}

(3.14)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}Res^{W}_{j,k}:=&c_{1}\varepsilon\partial_{\xi}W_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{3}\partial_{\tau}W_{\varepsilon}+c_{1}^{2}\left[A\left(\xi,\eta\right)-A\left(\xi-\varepsilon,\eta\right)\right]\\
&+c_{2}^{2}\left[U_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta\right)-U_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta-\varepsilon^{2}\right)\right]+\alpha_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\left[A\left(\xi,\eta\right)^{2}-A\left(\xi-\varepsilon,\eta\right)^{2}\right],\\
Res^{Z}_{j,k}:=&\frac{\alpha_{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left[U_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta\right)^{2}-U_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta-\varepsilon^{2}\right)^{2}\right].\end{split}

Expanding each term in
Res^{W}_{j,k}
by using expansions (
3.7
) and (
3.11
) yields the following formal expansion

\displaystyle Res^{W}_{j,k}=\quad

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{3}\left[2c_{1}\partial_{\tau}A+\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{12}\partial_{\xi}^{3}A+c_{2}^{2}\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A+\alpha_{1}\partial_{\xi}\left(A^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{4}\left[c_{1}\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\tau}A+\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{24}\partial_{\xi}^{4}A+\frac{c_{2}^{2}}{2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\partial_{\xi}^{2}\left(A^{2}\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5}).

If the function
A
is a solution of the KP-II equation (
1.12
), the residual term
Res^{W}
has the formal order of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5})
. It is also clear from expansion (
3.11
) that
Res^{Z}
has the formal order of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{6})
.

3.2.
Step 2. Residual terms

The residual terms are handled by using Taylor’s theorem.
If
A
is defined in Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with sufficiently large
s
, then we can estimate the residual terms in the
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
norm by an application of Lemma
2
. Since all residual terms have the formal order of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5})
, we can obtain the
bound of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{2}})
on the residual terms in the
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
norm.

The following lemma gives us estimates of the
\ell^{2}
-norm for the residual terms in equations (
3.12
) and (
3.14
). No residual terms appear in equations (
3.13
).

Lemma 3
.

Let
A\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},H^{s})
be a solution to the KP-II equation (
1.12
) with
s\geq 9
. There is a positive constant
C

that depend on
A
such that for all
\varepsilon\in\left(0,1\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert Res^{U^{(1)}}_{j,k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert Res^{U^{(2)}}_{j,k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert Res^{W}_{j,k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}+\left\lVert Res^{Z}_{j,k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{2}}.

(3.15)

Proof.

By construction, all terms in
Res_{j,k}^{U^{(1)}}
below the formal order of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5})
vanish. From the Taylor’s theorem for
W_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi+\varepsilon,\eta\right)-W_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta\right)
, the nonzero terms
at
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5})
are given by the integrals:

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{5}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\xi}^{5-l}W^{(l)}\left(\varepsilon\left(j+r\right),\varepsilon^{2}k\right)\frac{\left(1-r\right)^{5-l-1}}{\left(5-l-1\right)!}dr,\quad 0\leq l\leq 3.

In view of corrections for
W^{(l)}
in (
3.7
), the error is given by a linear combination of the following two terms:

\varepsilon^{5}\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\partial_{\xi}^{5}A\left(\varepsilon\left(j+r\right),\varepsilon^{2}k\right)\right|,\quad\varepsilon^{5}\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\partial_{\xi}^{2}\partial_{\tau}A\left(\varepsilon\left(j+r\right),\varepsilon^{2}k\right)\right|.

Using Lemma
2
, there is a constant
C_{s}>0
such that the
\ell^{2}
norm of the residual term
Res^{U^{(1)}}
is bounded by

\left\lVert Res^{U^{(1)}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}\leq C_{s}\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{2}}\left(\|A\|_{H^{s+5}}+\|\partial_{\tau}A\|_{H^{s+2}}\right),

for
s>1
.

Similarly, all terms in
Res_{j,k}^{U^{(2)}}
below the formal order of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5})
vanish. From the Taylor’s theorem for
W_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta+\varepsilon^{2}\right)-W_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi,\eta\right)
and the corrections for
W_{\varepsilon}
and
U_{\varepsilon}
given by (
3.7
) and (
3.11
), the error is given by a linear combination of the following six terms:

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{5}\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A\left(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}(k+r)\right)\right|,\quad

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{5}\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\partial_{\eta}\partial_{\tau}A\left(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}(k+r)\right)\right|,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{6}\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\partial_{\eta}^{3}A\left(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}(k+r)\right)\right|,\quad

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{6}\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\partial_{\xi}^{2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A\left(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}(k+r)\right)\right|,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{6}\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}^{2}\partial_{\tau}A\left(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}(k+r)\right)\right|,\quad

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{6}\left|\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\eta}\partial_{\tau}A\left(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}k\right)\right|.

Using Lemma
2
, there is a constant
C_{s}>0
such that the
\ell^{2}
norm of the residual term
Res^{U^{(2)}}
is bounded for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
by

\left\lVert Res^{U^{(2)}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}\leq C_{s}\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{2}}\left(\|A\|_{H^{s+4}}+\|\partial_{\tau}A\|_{H^{s+2}}+\|\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}\partial_{\tau}A\|_{H^{s+1}}\right),

for
s>1
.

If
A\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},H^{s})
is a solution to the KPII equation (
1.12
)
with
s\geq 9
, all terms in
Res_{j,k}^{W}
below the formal order of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5})
vanish. Expanding all terms in
Res_{j,k}^{W}

shows that the error is given by a linear combination of the following eight terms:

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{5}\left|\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\tau}^{2}A\left(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}k\right)\right|,\quad\varepsilon^{5}\left|\partial_{\xi}^{2}\partial_{\tau}A\left(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}k\right)\right|,\quad\varepsilon^{6}\left|\partial_{\tau}^{2}A\left(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}k\right)\right|,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{5}\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\partial_{\xi}^{5}A\left(\varepsilon(j+r),\varepsilon^{2}k\right)\right|,\quad\varepsilon^{5}\sup_{r_{1},r_{2}\in[0,1]}\left|A\left(\varepsilon(j+r_{1}),\varepsilon^{2}k\right)\partial_{\xi}^{3}A\left(\varepsilon(j+r_{2}),\varepsilon^{2}k\right)\right|,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{5}\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}^{3}A\left(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}(k+r)\right)\right|,\quad\varepsilon^{5}\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\partial_{\xi}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A\left(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}(k+r)\right)\right|,

and

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{6}\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\left|\partial_{\eta}^{3}A\left(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}(k+r)\right)\right|.

Using Lemma
2
and the relation (
2.6
) for
\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\tau}^{2}A
, there is a constant
C_{s}>0
such that the
\ell^{2}
norm of the residual term
Res^{W}
is bounded for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
by

\displaystyle\left\lVert Res^{W}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{2}}\left(\|\partial_{\tau}^{2}A\|_{H^{s}}+\|\partial_{\tau}A\|_{H^{s+2}}+\|\partial_{\xi}^{-2}\partial_{\eta}^{2}\partial_{\tau}A\|_{H^{s}}+\|A\|_{H^{s+5}}+\|\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}A\|_{H^{s+2}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+\|A\|_{H^{s}}\|A\|_{H^{s+3}}+\|A\|_{H^{s}}\|\partial_{\tau}A\|_{H^{s}}\right),

for
s>1
.

The final residual term
Res^{Z}_{j,k}
is estimated from
the expansion (
3.11
). The error is given by

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{6}\sup_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},r_{4}\in[0,1]}\left(|\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}A(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}(k+r_{1}))|+\varepsilon|\partial_{\eta}A(\varepsilon(j+r_{2}),\varepsilon^{2}k)|\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left(|\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}^{2}A(\varepsilon j,\varepsilon^{2}(k+r_{3}))|+\varepsilon|\partial_{\eta}^{2}A(\varepsilon(j+r_{4}),\varepsilon^{2}k)|\right)

and is controlled in the
\ell^{2}
norm by using the bound

\left\lVert Res^{Z}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}\leq C_{s}\varepsilon^{\frac{9}{2}}\left(\left\lVert\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}A\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{s+1}}+\left\lVert A\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{s+2}}\right),

where
C_{s}>0
,
s>1
, and
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

Combining all four bounds together and using the fact that
a solution
A\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R},H^{s})
to the KP-II equation (
1.12
) with
s\geq 9
enjoys the estimates of Lemma
1
, we obtain
the bound (
3.15
).
∎

3.3.
Step 3. Energy estimates

In order to control the growth of the approximation error from solutions to system (
3.12
), (
3.13
), and (
3.14
), we will introduce the following energy function,

\displaystyle\begin{split}E(t)=&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}W_{j,k}^{2}+Z_{j,k}^{2}+c_{1}^{2}\left(U_{j,k}^{(1)}\right)^{2}+c_{2}^{2}\left(U_{j,k}^{(2)}\right)^{2}+c_{1}^{2}\left(V_{j,k}^{(1)}\right)^{2}+c_{2}^{2}\left(V_{j,k}^{(2)}\right)^{2}\\
&+\alpha_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\left[2A\left(U_{j,k}^{(1)}\right)^{2}+\frac{2}{3}\left(U_{j,k}^{(1)}\right)^{3}+\frac{2}{3}\left(V_{j,k}^{(2)}\right)^{3}\right]\\
&+\alpha_{2}\varepsilon^{2}\left[A\left(V_{j,k}^{(1)}\right)^{2}+U_{j,k}^{(1)}\left(V_{j,k}^{(1)}\right)^{2}+\left(U_{j,k}^{(2)}\right)^{2}V_{j,k}^{(2)}+2U_{\varepsilon}U_{j,k}^{(2)}V_{j,k}^{(2)}\right].\end{split}

(3.16)

The
\varepsilon
-dependent terms of
E(t)
are chosen from the condition that the growth rate
E^{\prime}(t)
along the solution of system (
3.12
), (
3.13
), and (
3.14
) does not contain terms of the formal orders
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
and
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})
(see Lemma
5
below).

The following lemma establishes coercivity of the energy
E(t)
with respect to the
\ell^{2}
norm of the perturbations as long as the perturbations are not large in the
\ell^{2}
norm.

Lemma 4
.

Let
A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\right)

and
\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s-1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\right)
with
s>3
and assume that
E(t)\leq E_{0}
for some
\varepsilon
-independent constant
E_{0}>0
for every
t\in[-\tau_{0}\varepsilon^{-3},\tau_{0}\varepsilon^{-3}]
.
There exists some constants
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
K_{0}>0
that depend on
A
such that

\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert Z\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert U^{(1)}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert U^{(2)}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert V^{(1)}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert V^{(2)}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\leq 2K_{0}E(t),

(3.17)

for each
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\varepsilon_{0}\right)
and
t\in[-\tau_{0}\varepsilon^{-3},\tau_{0}\varepsilon^{-3}]
.

Proof.

It follows from the decomposition (
3.11
) and Sobolev’s embedding
of
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
into
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s>1
that if
A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\right)

and
\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{\eta}A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s-1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\right)
with
s>3
, then there exists
a constant
C_{0}>0
that depends on
A
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}]}\left(\|A(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|U_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\right)\leq C_{0}.

By using (
3.16
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for
U^{(2)}_{j,k}V^{(2)}_{j,k}
, we derive the following lower bound for
E(t)
:

\displaystyle 2E(t)\geq

\displaystyle\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert Z\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(c_{1}^{2}-2\alpha_{1}\varepsilon^{2}C_{0}-\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\|U^{(1)}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\right)\left\lVert U^{(1)}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(c_{2}^{2}-\alpha_{2}\varepsilon^{2}C_{0}-\alpha_{2}\varepsilon^{2}\|V^{(2)}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\right)\left\lVert U^{(2)}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(c_{1}^{2}-\alpha_{2}\varepsilon^{2}C_{0}-\alpha_{2}\varepsilon^{2}\|U^{(1)}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\right)\left\lVert V^{(1)}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(c_{2}^{2}-\alpha_{2}\varepsilon^{2}C_{0}-\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\|V^{(2)}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\right)\left\lVert V^{(2)}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

For fixed
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, we use the bound
\|U\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq\|U\|_{\ell^{2}}

and choose
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
K_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle c_{1}^{2}-2\alpha_{1}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}C_{0}-\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{1}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}(2K_{0}E_{0})^{1/2}\geq{\rm min}(1,K_{0}^{-1}),

\displaystyle c_{2}^{2}-\alpha_{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}C_{0}-\alpha_{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}(2K_{0}E_{0})^{1/2}\geq{\rm min}(1,K_{0}^{-1}),

\displaystyle c_{1}^{2}-\alpha_{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}C_{0}-\alpha_{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}(2K_{0}E_{0})^{1/2}\geq{\rm min}(1,K_{0}^{-1}),

\displaystyle c_{2}^{2}-\alpha_{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}C_{0}-\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{1}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}(2K_{0}E_{0})^{1/2}\geq{\rm min}(1,K_{0}^{-1}),

which is always possible if
\varepsilon_{0}
is sufficiently small. This gives the bound (
3.17
) with
K_{0}
redefined as
\max(1,K_{0})
.
∎

The following lemma uses the coercivity of the energy
E(t)
in Lemma
4
to establish the rate at which it may grow in time along the solutions of system (
3.12
), (
3.13
), and (
3.14
). We will be able to use this in Step 4, along with a Gronwall lemma argument of Lemma
6
below, in order to get a bound on the size of the energy quantity. This will in turn gives a bound on how far solutions of the KP-II equation (
1.12
) drift away from solutions of the FPU system (
3.1
) and hence will complete the proof of Theorem
1
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
A\in C^{0}\left(\left[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\right],H^{s+9}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\right)
be a solution to the KP-II equation (
1.12
) with
s\geq 0
in the class of functions of Lemma
1
and assume that
E(t)\leq E_{0}
for some
\varepsilon
-independent constant
E_{0}>0
for every
t\in[-\tau_{0}\varepsilon^{-3},\tau_{0}\varepsilon^{-3}]
. There exist some constants
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
K_{0}>0
that depend on
A
such that

\left|E^{\prime}(t)\right|\leq K_{0}\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{2}}E(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\varepsilon^{3}E(t)\right),

(3.18)

for each
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
t\in[-\tau_{0}\varepsilon^{-3},\tau_{0}\varepsilon^{-3}]
.

Proof.

By differentiating
E(t)
, defined by (
3.16
), in time
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\dot{W}_{j,k}W_{j,k}+\dot{Z}_{j,k}Z_{j,k}+c_{1}^{2}\dot{U}_{j,k}^{(1)}U_{j,k}^{(1)}+c_{2}^{2}\dot{U}_{j,k}^{(2)}U_{j,k}^{(2)}+c_{1}^{2}\dot{V}_{j,k}^{(1)}V_{j,k}^{(1)}+c_{2}^{2}\dot{V}_{j,k}^{(2)}V_{j,k}^{(2)}

\displaystyle+\alpha_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\left[\dot{A}\left(U_{j,k}^{(1)}\right)^{2}+2A\dot{U}_{j,k}^{(1)}U_{j,k}^{(1)}+\dot{U}_{j,k}^{(1)}\left(U_{j,k}^{(1)}\right)^{2}+\dot{V}_{j,k}^{(2)}\left(V_{j,k}^{(2)}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\alpha_{2}\varepsilon^{2}\left[\dot{U_{\varepsilon}}U_{j,k}^{(2)}V_{j,k}^{(2)}+\frac{1}{2}\dot{U}_{j,k}^{(1)}\left(V_{j,k}^{(1)}\right)^{2}+U_{j,k}^{(1)}\dot{V}_{j,k}^{(1)}V_{j,k}^{(1)}+\frac{1}{2}\left(U_{j,k}^{(2)}\right)^{2}\dot{V}_{j,k}^{(2)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\dot{U}_{j,k}^{(2)}U_{j,k}^{(2)}V_{j,k}^{(2)}+U_{\varepsilon}\dot{U}_{j,k}^{(2)}V_{j,k}^{(2)}+U_{\varepsilon}U_{j,k}^{(2)}\dot{V}_{j,k}^{(2)}+\frac{1}{2}\dot{A}\left(V_{j,k}^{(1)}\right)^{2}+A\dot{V}_{j,k}^{(1)}V_{j,k}^{(1)}\right],

where the dot denotes derivative in
t
and is applied with the chain rule to
A
and
U_{\varepsilon}
that depends on
(\varepsilon(j-c_{1}t),\varepsilon^{2}k,\varepsilon^{3}t)
.
Substituting equations of motion (
3.12
), (
3.13
), and (
3.14

5.
Conclusion

We have proved here the validity of the KP–II approximation for dynamics of transversely modulatied small-amplitude long-scale waves in a vector FPU system on a two-dimensional square lattice. The justification was performed for horizontal and vertical propagations of the waves and, under some restrictions on parameters of the FPU system, for the diagonal propagation. While the general algorithm of the justification analysis is well understood by now,
the technical details of the justification analysis have been developed for the first time in the vector FPU systems, to the best of our knowledge.

This research opens up new directions. First, it is interesting to see
if the justification analysis can be generalized for the vector FPU mass–spring systems with diagonal springs and for the wave propagation under an arbitrary angle with respect to the square lattice. Second, in terms of applications
of the FPU models to the graphene materials, it is important to consider
other two-dimensional models such as hexagonal lattices. Finally, known properties of the KP-II equation can be applied to study other problems of the nonlinear dynamics of small-amplitude waves in the two-dimensional FPU lattices
such as the linear and nonlinear stability of periodic and solitary waves with respect to transverse modulations.

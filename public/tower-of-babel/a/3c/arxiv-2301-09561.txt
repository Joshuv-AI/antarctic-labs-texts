Homological full-and-faithfulness of comodule inclusion and contramodule forgetful functors
Leonid Positselski

Abstract
. In this paper we consider a conilpotent coalgebra C C over a field k k . 1.
Introduction

In this paper we work with coassociative, counital coalgebras over
a field
k
.
For any such coalgebra
C
, the dual
k
-vector space
C^{*}
is
naturally an associative, unital algebra over
k
.
One has to choose between two opposite ways of defining
the multiplication on
C^{*}
.
We prefer the notation in which any left
C
-comodule becomes
a left
C^{*}
-module, and any right
C
-comodule becomes
a right
C^{*}
-module.

It is known, at least, since 1960s that the resulting exact functor

\Upsilon\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is fully
faithful
[
44
, Section 2.1]
.
Following the terminology of the book
[
44
]
,
C^{*}
-modules coming
from
C
-comodules are often called “rational” in the literature.
The essential image of the functor
\Upsilon
is a
hereditary
pretorsion class
in
C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
: this means that the full subcategory

\Upsilon(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}})
is closed under subobjects, quotient objects,
and infinite coproducts in
C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
However, this full subcategory
need not
be closed under
extensions.
In other words,
\Upsilon(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}})
is
not
always a torsion
class or a Serre subcategory in
C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
A module extension of two comodules need not be a comodule.

When is the essential image of
\Upsilon
closed under extensions in

C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
?
There is a vast body of literature on this topic, including
the papers
[
41
,
43
,
15
,
4
,
3
,
46
,
10
]
.
In this paper we discuss further questions going in this direction,
under an additional assumption.
The assumption is that the coalgebra
C
is
conilpotent
.
The conilpotent coalgebras were called “pointed irreducible” in
the terminology of
[
44
]
.
For a conilpotent coalgebra
C
, the full subcategory
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}

is closed under extensions in
C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
if and only if the coalgebra

C
is
finitely cogenerated

[
41
, Corollary 2.4 and
Section 2.5]
,
[
43
, Theorem 4.6]
,
[
15
, Corollary 21]
,

[
4
, Theorem 2.8]
,
[
3
, Proposition 3.13 and
Corollary 3.14]
,
[
10
, Lemma 1.2 and Theorem 4.8]
.
If
C
is not finitely cogenerated, then there is a two-dimensional

C^{*}
-module which is not a
C
-comodule, but an extension of two
one-dimensional
C
-comodules.

Let
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
be two abelian categories, and
\Phi\colon\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
be a fully faithful exact functor.
Then the essential image of
\Phi
is a full subcategory closed under
kernels and cokernels in
\mathsf{A}
.
The full subcategory
\Phi(\mathsf{B})
is closed under extensions in
\mathsf{A}
if
and only if the functor
\Phi
induces an isomorphism

(1)

\Phi\colon\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{B}}(X,Y)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{A}}(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))

for all objects
X
,
Y\in\mathsf{B}
.
Generally speaking, for a fully faithful exact functor
\Phi
,
the map (
1
) is a monomorphism, but not necessarily
an isomorphism.

Thus, the following question is a natural extension of the question
about extension closedness of
\Upsilon(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}})
in
C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
Put
\mathsf{B}=C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
,
\mathsf{A}=C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
, and
\Phi=\Upsilon
.
Consider the induced maps on the Ext spaces

(2)

\Phi\colon\Ext^{i}_{\mathsf{B}}(X,Y)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext^{i}_{\mathsf{A}}(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y)).

When is the map (
2
) in isomorphism for
all
X
,
Y\in\mathsf{B}
?
Generally speaking, for an exact functor of abelian categories

\Phi\colon\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
, all one can say is that
the map (
2
) is a monomorphism for
i=n+1
and
all
X
,
Y\in\mathsf{B}
whenever it is an isomorphism for
i=n
and
all
X
,
Y\in\mathsf{B}
.

Let
C
be a conilpotent coalgebra over a field
k
.
Then the one-dimensional
k
-vector space
k
has a unique left

C
-comodule structure (and a unique right
C
-comodule structure)
provided by the unique coaugmentation of
C
.
In this context, we show that the maps (
2
) are
isomorphisms for
\Phi=\Upsilon
and all
1\leq i\leq n
if and only if
the
k
-vector spaces
\Ext^{i}_{C}(k,k)
(computed in the abelian
category of left or right
C
-comodules) are finite-dimensional
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
In particular,
\Ext^{1}_{C}(k,k)
is the vector space of cogenerators of
a conilpotent coalgebra
C
; so
C
is finitely cogenerated if and only
if
\Ext^{1}_{C}(k,k)
is finite-dimensional.

Alongside with the abelian categories of left and right

comodules
over a coalgebra
C
, there are much less familiar,
but no less natural abelian categories of left and right

C
-contramodules

[
24
]
.
Endowing the dual vector space
C^{*}
to a coalgebra
C
with
the natural algebra structure in which any left
C
-comodule is a left

C^{*}
-module and any right
C
-comodule is a right
C^{*}
-module,
one also obtains a natural left
C^{*}
-module structure on any left

C
-contramodule.
So there is an exact forgetful functor
\Theta\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.

The functor
\Theta
is
not
in general fully faithful.
It was shown in the paper
[
30
, Theorem 2.1]
that that
the functor
\Theta
is fully faithful for any finitely cogenerated
conilpotent coalgebra
C
.
In this paper we demonstrate a counterexample proving the (much easier)
converse implication: if a conilpotent coalgebra
C
is
not

finitely cogenerated, then the functor
\Theta
is
not
fully
faithful.

More generally, the maps (
2
) are
isomorphisms for
\Phi=\Theta
and all
0\leq i\leq n-1
(for separated
contramodules
Y
; and also for
0\leq i\leq n-2
and arbitrary
Y
) if
and only if the
k
-vector spaces
\Ext^{i}_{C}(k,k)
are
finite-dimensional for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
Summarizing the assertions for comodules and contramodules, let us
state the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
C
be a conilpotent coalgebra over a field
k
and
n\geq 1
be
an integer.
Then the following five conditions are equivalent:

(i)

the map

\Ext^{i}_{C}(L,M)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext^{i}_{C^{*}}(L,M)

induced by the inclusion functor
\Upsilon\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

is an isomorphism for all left
C
-comodules
L
and
M
, and
all
1\leq i\leq n
;

(ii)

the map

\Ext^{i}_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext^{i}_{C^{*}{}^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)

induced by the inclusion functor
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}C^{*}
is
an isomorphism for all right
C
-comodules
L
and
M
, and
all
1\leq i\leq n
;

(iii)

the map

(3)

\Ext^{C,i}(P,Q)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext^{i}_{C^{*}}(P,Q)

induced by the forgetful functor
\Theta\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

is an isomorphism for all left
C
-contramodules
P
, all

separated
left
C
-contramodules
Q
, and all
0\leq i\leq n-1
;

(iv)

the
k
-vector space
\Ext^{i}_{C}(k,k)
is finite-dimensional
for all
1\leq i\leq n
;

(v)

the
k
-vector space
\Ext^{C,i}(k,k)
is finite-dimensional
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

If any one of the equivalent conditions
(i–v)
holds, then
the map (
3
) is an isomorphism for
all

left
C
-contramodules
P
and
Q
and all
0\leq i\leq n-2
.

Notice a curious cohomological dimension shift in comparison between
the assertions about the comodule and contramodule inclusion/forgetful
functors.
Let
n\geq 1
be the minimal integer for which the vector space

\Ext^{n}_{C}(k,k)
is infinite-dimensional.
Then the map
\Ext^{n}_{C}(k,k)\longrightarrow\Ext^{n}_{C^{*}}(k,k)

induced by the functor
\Upsilon
is injective, but not surjective.
In fact, the dimension cardinality of the vector space

\Ext^{n}_{C^{*}}(k,k)
is larger than that of
\Ext^{n}_{C}(k,k)
in this case:

\Ext^{n}_{C^{*}}(k,k)
is as large as the double dual vector space

\Ext^{n}_{C}(k,k)^{**}
to
\Ext^{n}_{C}(k,k)
.

At the same time, denoting by
T
an infinite-dimensional

k
-vector space endowed with the trivial
C
-contramodule structure,
the map
\Ext^{C,n}(T,k)\longrightarrow\Ext_{C^{*}}^{n}(T,k)
induced by
the functor
\Theta
is
not injective
(for the integer
n

as in the previous paragraph).
Consequently, there exists a projective
C
-contramodule
P
such
that the map
\Ext^{C,n-1}(P,k)\longrightarrow\Ext_{C^{*}}^{n-1}(P,k)
is
injective, but not surjective.
For
n>1
this, of course, means that
\Ext_{C^{*}}^{n-1}(P,k)\neq 0
,
while
\Ext^{C,n-1}(P,k)=0
as it should be.
Notice that both the contramodules
P
and
k
are separated.

Let us emphasize that
we do not know
whether the equivalent
conditions (i–v) of
Theorem
1.1
imply
bijectivity of the maps (
3
) for arbitrary
(nonseparated) contramodules
Q
and
i=n-1
.
This remains an open question.

Returning to the discussion of a fully faithful exact functor

\Phi\colon\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
, it is clear that the maps of Ext
groups (
2
) induced by
\Phi
are isomorphisms if
and only if the induced triangulated functor between the bounded derived
categories
\Phi^{\mathsf{b}}\colon\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{b}}(\mathsf{B})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{b}}(\mathsf{A})
is fully
faithful.
Now assume that there are enough injective objects in the abelian
category
\mathsf{A}
and the functor
\Phi\colon\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
has
a right adjoint.
Then the functor
\Phi^{\mathsf{b}}
is fully faithful if and only if
the similar functor between the bounded below derived categories

\Phi^{+}\colon\mathsf{D}^{+}(\mathsf{B})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{+}(\mathsf{A})
is fully faithful.

Dually, assume that there are enough projective objects in
\mathsf{A}
and
the functor
\Phi\colon\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
has a left adjoint.
Then the functor
\Phi^{\mathsf{b}}
is fully faithful if and only if
the similar functor between the bounded above derived categories

\Phi^{-}\colon\mathsf{D}^{-}(\mathsf{B})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{-}(\mathsf{A})
is fully
faithful
[
31
, Proposition 6.5]
.

In the situation at hand, there are enough projective and injective
objects in the categories of modules over associative rings.
The comodule inclusion functor

\Upsilon\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
has a right adjoint functor

\Gamma\colon C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
, while the contramodule forgetful
functor
\Theta\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
has a left adjoint functor

\Delta\colon C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
Consequently,
Theorem
1.1
implies
the following result about full-and-faithfulness of induced
triangulated functors.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any conilpotent coalgebra
C
over a field
k
, the following
eight conditions are equivalent:

(i)

the triangulated functor

\Upsilon^{\mathsf{b}}\colon\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{b}}(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{b}}(C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}})

induced by the comodule inclusion functor
\Upsilon\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is fully faithful;

(ii)

the triangulated functor

\Upsilon^{+}\colon\mathsf{D}^{+}(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{+}(C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}})

induced by the comodule inclusion functor
\Upsilon

is fully faithful;

(iii)

the triangulated functor

\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{b}}({\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C)\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{b}}({\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}C^{*})

induced by the comodule inclusion functor
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}C^{*}

is fully faithful;

(iv)

the triangulated functor

\mathsf{D}^{+}({\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C)\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{+}({\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}C^{*})

induced by the comodule inclusion functor is fully faithful;

(v)

the triangulated functor

\Theta^{\mathsf{b}}\colon\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{b}}(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{b}}(C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}})

induced by the contramodule forgetful functor

\Theta\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is fully faithful;

(vi)

the triangulated functor

\Theta^{-}\colon\mathsf{D}^{-}(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{-}(C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}})

induced by the contramodule forgetful functor
\Theta

is fully faithful;

(vii)

the
k
-vector space
\Ext^{n}_{C}(k,k)
is finite-dimensional
for all
n\geq 0
;

(viii)

the
k
-vector space
\Ext^{C,n}(k,k)
is finite-dimensional
for all
n\geq 0
.

Let us discuss the condition of finite-dimensionality of the Ext
spaces
\Ext_{C}^{i}(k,k)
for all
i\geq 0
in some detail.
One can consider the special case when the coalgebra
C
is positively
graded with finite-dimensional components; so
C
is the graded dual
coalgebra to a positively graded algebra
A=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}A_{m}

with
\dim A_{m}<\infty
for all
m\geq 0
and
A_{0}=k
.
Then one has
\Ext_{C}^{i}(k,k)\simeq\bigoplus_{j=i}^{\infty}\Ext_{A}^{i,j}(k,k)
and
\Ext_{A}^{i}(k,k)\simeq\prod_{j=i}^{\infty}\Ext_{A}^{i,j}(k,k)
, where
i
is the usual cohomological grading on
the Ext spaces, while the
internal grading

j
is induced by
the grading on
A
(cf.
[
17
, Section 1 of Chapter 1]
,

[
35
, Section 2.1]
, and
[
19
, Section 2]
).

Assume further that
A
is multiplicatively generated by
A_{1}
with
relations of degree
2
; so
A
is a quadratic algebra with
finite-dimensional components over
k
.
Then the vector spaces
\Ext_{C}^{i}(k,k)
or
\Ext_{A}^{i}(k,k)
still
need
not
be finite-dimensional; in fact,
\Ext_{C}^{3}(k,k)
or
(equivalently)
\Ext_{A}^{3}(k,k)
can be infinite-dimensional
already
[
1
, Theorem 7.6]
,
[
7
]
,

[
17
, Section 6 of Chapter 6]
.

Nevertheless, for a (
homogeneous
)
Koszul

k
-algebra
A

with finite-dimensional grading components
[
40
,
17
]
and the graded
dual coalgebra
C
, the vector spaces
\Ext_{C}^{i}(k,k)
and

\Ext_{A}^{i}(k,k)
are, of course, finite-dimensional for all
i\geq 0
.
More generally, for a finitely cogenerated conilpotent coalgebra
C
,
all the vector spaces
\Ext_{C}^{i}(k,k)
are finite-dimensional whenever
the Ext-algebra
\Ext_{C}^{*}(k,k)
is generated by
\Ext_{C}^{1}(k,k)
.
This includes the important particular case when the Ext-algebra

\Ext_{C}^{*}(k,k)
is Koszul (but the coalgebra
C
need not be graded)
described in
[
39
, Main Theorem]
and
[
26
, Sections 5–6]
.
Here we keep assuming that the coalgebra
C
is finitely cogenerated.

With these important special cases in mind, we call a conilpotent
coalgebra
C

weakly finitely Koszul
if the vector space

\Ext_{C}^{n}(k,k)
is finite-dimensional for every
n\geq 0
.
This terminology goes back to
[
35
, Sections 5.4 and 5.7]
.

Let us emphasize that
we do not know
whether the induced
triangulated functors between the unbounded derived categories

\Upsilon^{\varnothing}\colon\mathsf{D}(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\mathsf{D}(C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}})\quad\text{and}\quad\Theta^{\varnothing}\colon\mathsf{D}(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\mathsf{D}(C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}})

are fully faithful under the equivalent conditions of
Theorem
1.2

(or even for a graded coalgebra
C
graded dual to a Koszul algebra

A
with finite-dimensional components) in general.
However, the triangulated functors
\Upsilon^{\varnothing}
and

\Theta^{\varnothing}
are known to be fully faithful for any finitely
cogenerated conilpotent
cocommutative
coalgebra
C
.
This is a particular case of
[
25
, Theorems 1.3 and 2.9]

(applied to the complete Noetherian commutative local
k
-algebra

R=C^{*}
with its maximal ideal
I=\mathfrak{m}
).

In this connection, it should first of all be mentioned that all
finitely cogenerated conilpotent cocommutative coalgebras
C
are
weakly finitely Koszul since they are Artinian, and consequently,
co-Noetherian
[
29
, Section 2]
.
Quite generally, any left or right co-Noetherian conilpotent coalgebra
is weakly finitely Koszul (and so is any finitely cogenerated left or
right cocoherent coalgebra).
But this does not seem to be enough.
The proofs of
[
25
, Theorems 1.3 and 2.9]
are essentially based
on the observation that
the right adjoint functor
\Gamma
to

\Upsilon
and the left adjoint functor
\Delta
to
\Theta
have
finite homological dimensions
(cf.
[
28
, Theorem 6.4]

and
[
31
, Proposition 6.5]
).
We cannot think of any general noncommutative versions of these
properties, proved in
[
18
]
and
[
25
]
using commutative
Koszul complexes.

2.
Preliminaries on Coalgebras, Comodules, and Contramodules

Unless otherwise mentioned, all
coalgebras
,
comodules
,
and
contramodules
in this paper are presumed to be coassociative
and counital; all
coalgebra homomorphisms
are presumed to
preserve the counit.
Dually, all
rings
,
algebras
, and
modules
are
presumed to be associative and unital.

A (coassociative, counital)
coalgebra

C
over a field
k
is
a
k
-vector space endowed with
k
-linear maps of

comultiplication

\mu\colon C\longrightarrow C\otimes_{k}C
and
counit

\epsilon\colon C\longrightarrow k
satisfying the usual coassociativity and
counitality axioms (which can be obtained by writing down the definition
of an associative, unital
k
-algebra in the tensor notation and
inverting the arrows).
We suggest the books
[
44
,
16
]
as the standard reference sources
on coalgebras over a field.
The present author’s surveys
[
24
, Section 1]
,

[
36
, Section 3]
can be used as additional reference sources.

Let
C
be a coalgebra over
k
.
A
right
C
-comodule

N
is a
k
-vector space endowed with
a
k
-linear map of
right coaction

\nu\colon N\longrightarrow N\otimes_{k}C

satisfying the usual coassociativity and counitality axioms (which can
be obtained by inverting the arrows in the definition of a module over
an associative, unital algebra).
Similarly, a
left
C
-comodule

M
is a
k
-vector space
endowed with a
k
-linear map of
left coaction

\nu\colon M\longrightarrow C\otimes_{k}M
satisfying the coassociativity and counitality axioms.

A
left
C
-contramodule
(see
[
5
, Section III.5]
,

[
20
, Sections 0.2.4 and 3.1.1, and Appendix A]
,

[
24
, Sections 1.1–1.6]
, and
[
36
, Section 8]
) is
a
k
-vector space
P
endowed with a
k
-linear map of
left
contraaction

\pi\colon\Hom_{k}(C,P)\longrightarrow P
satisfying the following

contraassociativity
and
contraunitality
axioms.
Firstly, the two maps
\Hom(C,\pi)
and
\Hom(\mu,P)\colon\Hom_{k}(C\otimes_{k}C,\>P)\simeq\Hom_{k}(C,\Hom_{k}(C,P))\longrightarrow\Hom_{k}(C,P)

must have equal compositions with the contraaction map
\pi\colon\Hom_{k}(C,P)\longrightarrow P
,

\Hom_{k}(C\otimes_{k}C,\>P)\simeq\Hom_{k}(C,\Hom_{k}(C,P))\,\rightrightarrows\,\Hom_{k}(C,P)\longrightarrow P.

Secondly, the composition of the map
\Hom(\epsilon,P)\colon P\longrightarrow\Hom_{k}(C,P)
with the map
\pi\colon\Hom_{k}(C,P)\longrightarrow P
must be equal
to the identity endomorphism of
P
,

P\longrightarrow\Hom_{k}(C,P)\longrightarrow P.

Here the
k
-vector space isomorphism
\Hom_{k}(C\otimes_{k}C,\>P)\simeq\Hom_{k}(C,\Hom_{k}(C,P))
is obtained as a particular case of
the adjunction isomorphism
\Hom_{k}(U\otimes_{k}V,\>W)\simeq\Hom_{k}(V,\Hom_{k}(U,W))
, which holds for any vector spaces
U
,
V
,
and
W
.

The definition of a
right
C
-contramodule
is similar, with
the only difference that the isomorphism
\Hom_{k}(C\otimes_{k}C,\>P)\simeq\Hom_{k}(C,\Hom_{k}(C,P))
arising as a particular case of
the identification
\Hom_{k}(V\otimes_{k}U,\>W)\simeq\Hom_{k}(V,\Hom_{k}(U,W))

is used.

For any right
C
-comodule
N
and any
k
-vector space
V
,
the vector space
\Hom_{k}(N,V)
has a natural left
C
-contramodule
structure.
The left contraaction map

\pi\colon\Hom_{k}(C,\Hom_{k}(N,V))\simeq\Hom_{k}(N\otimes_{k}C,\>V)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{k}(N,V)

is constructed by applying the functor
\Hom_{k}({-},V)
to the right
coaction map
\nu\colon N\longrightarrow N\otimes_{k}C
, i. e.,
\pi=\Hom_{k}(\nu,V)
.

Let
C^{\mathrm{op}}
denote the opposite coalgebra to
C
(i. e., the same
vector space endowed with the same counit and the left-right opposite
comultiplication to that in
C
).
Similarly, given a ring
A
, we denote by
A^{\mathrm{op}}
the opposite ring.
Then right
C
-comodules are the same things as left

C^{\mathrm{op}}
-comodules, and vice versa.

We will use the notation
\Hom_{C}({-},{-})
for the vector spaces of
morphisms in the category of left
C
-comodules
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
and
the notation
\Hom_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}({-},{-})
for the vector spaces of
morphisms in the category of right
C
-comodules
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C
.
The vector spaces of morphisms in the left and right contramodule
categories
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
and
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Contra--}}}C
will be denoted by

\Hom^{C}({-},{-})
and
\Hom^{C^{\mathrm{op}}}({-},{-})
.
Similarly, the Yoneda Ext spaces are denoted by

\Ext_{C}^{*}({-},{-})
in
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
, by
\Ext_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}^{*}({-},{-})

in
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C
, and by
\Ext^{C,*}({-},{-})
in
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

The category of left
C
-comodules
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
is a locally finite
Grothendieck abelian category.
Any
C
-comodule is the union of its finite-dimensional
subcomodules
[
44
, Propositions 2.1.1–2.1.2 and Corollary 2.1.4]
,

[
36
, Lemma 3.1(b)]
.
The forgetful functor
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\longrightarrow k{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Vect}}}
from the category of

C
-comodules to the category of
k
-vector spaces is exact and
preserves the infinite coproducts (but
not

the infinite products).
So the coproduct (and more generally, filtered direct limit) functors
are exact in
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
, while the functors of infinite product are
usually
not
exact.

Left
C
-comodules of the form
C\otimes_{k}V
and right
C
-comodules
of the form
V\otimes_{k}C
, where
V
ranges over the
k
-vector spaces,
are called the
cofree

C
-comodules.
For any left
C
-comodule
L
, the vector space of
C
-comodule
morphisms
L\longrightarrow C\otimes_{k}V
is naturally isomorphic to the vector
space of
k
-linear maps
L\longrightarrow V
,

\Hom_{C}(L,\>C\otimes_{k}V)\simeq\Hom_{k}(L,V).

Hence the cofree comodules are injective (as objects of

C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
or
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C
).
A
C
-comodule is injective if and only if it is a direct summand of
a cofree one.

The category of left
C
-contramodules
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is a locally
presentable abelian category with enough projective objects.
The forgetful functor
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow k{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Vect}}}
is exact and preserves
the infinite products (but
not
the infinite coproducts).
Hence the functors of infinite product are exact in
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
, while
the coproduct functors are usually
not
exact.

Left
C
-contramodules of the form
\Hom_{k}(C,V)
, where
V\in k{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Vect}}}
,
are called the
free

C
-contramodules.
For any left
C
-contramodule
Q
, the vector space of

C
-contramodule morphisms
\Hom_{k}(C,V)\longrightarrow Q
is naturally
isomorphic to the vector space of
k
-linear maps
V\longrightarrow Q
,

\Hom^{C}(\Hom_{k}(C,V),Q)\simeq\Hom_{k}(V,Q).

Hence the free contramodules are projective (as objects of

C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
).
A
C
-contramodule is projective if and only if it is a direct
summand of a free one.

Let us introduce a simplified version of the
Sweedler
notation

[
44
, Section 1.2]
,
[
16
, Notation 1.4.2]
for
the comultiplication in
C
.
Given an element
c\in C
, we write

\mu(c)=c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)}\in C\otimes_{k}C.

Following the convention in
[
20
,
24
,
36
]
(which is
opposite to the convention in
[
44
,
16
]
), we define
the associative algebra structure on the dual vector space

C^{*}=\Hom_{k}(C,k)
to a coalgebra
C
by the formula

(fg)(c)=f(c_{(2)})g(c_{(1)})\qquad\text{for all $f$, $g\in C^{*}$ and $c\in C$}.

The counit on
C
induces a unit in
C^{*}
in the obvious way.

Then, for any left
C
-comodule
M
, the composition

C^{*}\otimes_{k}M\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC^{*}\otimes_{k}C\otimes_{k}M\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muM

of the map induced by the coaction map
\nu\colon M\longrightarrow C\otimes_{k}M

and the map induced by the pairing map
C^{*}\otimes_{k}C\longrightarrow k

endows
M
with a left
C^{*}
-module structure.
Similarly, for any right
C
-comodule
N
, the composition

N\otimes_{k}C^{*}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muN\otimes_{k}C\otimes_{k}C^{*}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muN

endows
N
with a right
C^{*}
-module structure.
Finally, for any left
C
-contramodule
P
, the composition

C^{*}\otimes_{k}P\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{k}(C,P)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muP

of the natural embedding of vector spaces
C^{*}\otimes_{k}P\rightarrowtail\Hom_{k}(C,P)
and the contraaction map
\pi\colon\Hom_{k}(C,P)\longrightarrow P

endows
P
with a left
C^{*}
-module structure.

We have constructed the
comodule inclusion functors

\displaystyle\Upsilon\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}

\displaystyle\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}},

\displaystyle{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C

\displaystyle\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}C^{*}

and the
contramodule forgetful functor

\Theta\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}.

The comodule inclusion functors (for a coalgebra
C
over a field
k
)
are always fully faithful (see
[
44
, Propositions 2.1.1–2.1.2
and Theorem 2.1.3(e)]
for a discussion).
The contramodule forgetful functor is
not
fully faithful in
general, as we will see in Example
8.2

below.

3.
Conilpotent Coalgebras and Minimal Resolutions

What we call
conilpotent
coalgebras (in the terminology going
back to
[
39
, Section 3.1]
,
[
19
, Section 4.1]
) were called
“pointed irreducible” coalgebras in
[
44
, Section 8.0]
.
We refer to
[
36
, Sections 3.3–3.4]
for an introductory
discussion.

Let
D
be a coalgebra without counit over a field
k
.
For every
n\geq 1
, there is the uniquely defined
iterated
comultiplication map

\mu^{(n)}\colon D\longrightarrow D^{\otimes n+1}
.
The coalgebra
D
is said to be
conilpotent
if for every

d\in D
there exists
n\geq 1
such that
\mu^{(n)}(d)=0

in
D^{\otimes n+1}
.
Clearly, one then also has
\mu^{(m)}(d)=0
for all
m\geq n
.

A
coaugmentation

\gamma
of a coalgebra
C
is a homomorphism
of (counital) coalgebras
\gamma\colon k\longrightarrow C
.
So the composition
\epsilon\gamma\colon k\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow k
must be
the identity map.

Given a coaugmented coalgebra
(C,\gamma)
, the cokernel

D=C/\gamma(k)
of the map
\gamma
has a unique coalgebra structure
for which the natural surjection
C\longrightarrow D
is a homomorphism of
noncounital coalgebras.
A coaugmented coalgebra
C
us called
conilpotent
if
the noncounital coalgebra
D
is conilpotent (in the sense of
the definition above).

Obviously, no nonzero noncounital coalgebra homomorphisms
k\longrightarrow D

exist for a conilpotent noncounital coalgebra
D
.
Consequently, a conilpotent coaugmented coalgebra
(C,\gamma)
admits
no other coaugmentation but
\gamma
.

The following result is a version of Nakayama lemma for conilpotent
noncounital coalgebras.

Lemma 3.1
.

(a)
Let
D
be a conilpotent noncounital coalgebra and
M\neq 0

be a noncounital left
D
-comodule.
Then the coaction map
M\longrightarrow D\otimes_{k}M
is
not

injective.

(b)
Let
D
be a conilpotent noncounital coalgebra and
P\neq 0

be a noncounital left
D
-contramodule.
Then the contraaction map
\Hom_{k}(D,P)\longrightarrow P
is
not

surjective.

Proof.

Part (a): for the sake of contradiction, assume that the coaction map

\nu\colon M\longrightarrow D\otimes_{k}M
is injective.
Then the iterated coaction map
\nu^{(n)}\colon M\longrightarrow D^{\otimes n}\otimes_{k}M

is also injective for every
n\geq 1
.

Pick a nonzero element
x\in M
, and write
\nu(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{r}\otimes y_{r}
for some
d_{i}\in D
and
y_{i}\in M
.
Choose
n\geq 1
such that
\mu^{(n)}(d_{i})=0
in
D^{\otimes n+1}

for every
1\leq i\leq r
.
Then
\nu^{(n+1)}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu^{(n)}(d_{i})\otimes y_{i}=0
in

D^{\otimes n+1}\otimes_{k}M
, a contradiction.

The proof of part (b) is a bit more involved; it can be found
in
[
20
, Lemma A.2.1]
.
For a discussion of generalizations and other versions of
the comodule and contramodule Nakayama lemmas,
see
[
24
, Lemma 2.1]
.
∎

Let
E
be a subcoalgebra in a coalgebra
C
.
Then in any left
C
-comodule
M
there exists a unique maximal
subcomodule whose
C
-comodule structure arises from
an
E
-comodule structure.
We denote this subcomodule, which can be computed as the kernel of
the composition of maps
M\longrightarrow C\otimes_{k}M\longrightarrow C/E\otimes_{k}M
,
by
{}_{E}M\subset M
.
The similar subcomodule of a right
C
-comodule
N
will be
denoted by
N_{E}\subset N
.

Dually, any left
C
-contramodule
P
admits a unique maximal
quotient contramodule whose
C
-contramodule structure arises from
an
E
-contramodule structure.
We denote this quotient contramodule, which can be computed as
the cokernel of the composition of maps
\Hom_{k}(C/E,P)\longrightarrow\Hom_{k}(C,P)\longrightarrow P
, by
{}^{E}\!P\twoheadleftarrow P
.
A further discussion of the functors
M\longmapsto{}_{E}M
and

P\longmapsto{}^{E}\!P
can be found in
[
29
, Section 2]

or
[
36
, Section 8.4]
.

For any right
C
-comodule
N
, any subcoalgebra
E\subset C
,
and any
k
-vector space
V
, there is a natural isomorphism of
left
E
-contramodules

(4)

{}^{E}\!\Hom_{k}(N,V)\simeq\Hom_{k}(N_{E},V),

where the left contramodule structure on the space of linear maps
from a right comodule to a vector space is constructed as explained
in Section
2
.
The natural isomorphism (
4
)
follows immediately from the constructions of the functors

M\longmapsto{}_{E}M
and
P\longmapsto{}^{E}\!P
above.

In this section, we will be interested in the particular case when

(C,\gamma)
is a coaugmented (eventually, conilpotent) coalgebra
and
E=\gamma(k)\subset C
.
In this case, we put
{}_{\gamma}M={}_{E}M
and
{}^{\gamma}\!P={}^{E}\!P
.
The similar notation for a right
C
-comodule
N
is

N_{\gamma}=N_{E}
, and for a right
C
-contramodule
Q
it is

Q^{\gamma}=Q^{E}
.
Endowing the one-dimensional
k
-vector space
k
with
the (left and right)
C
-comodule and
C
-contramodule structures
defined in terms of
\gamma
, one has natural isomorphisms of

k
-vector spaces

{}_{\gamma}M\simeq\Hom_{C}(k,M)\quad\text{and}\quad\Hom_{k}({}^{\gamma}\!P,k)\simeq\Hom^{C}(P,k).

One can further compute the vector space
{}^{\gamma}\!P
as
the
contratensor product

k\odot_{C}P

(see
[
24
, Section 3.1]
for the definition), but we will not
need to use this fact.

Lemma 3.2
.

(a)
Let
(C,\gamma)
be a conilpotent coaugmented coalgebra
and
M\neq 0
be a left
C
-comodule.
Then
{}_{\gamma}M\neq 0
.

(b)
Let
(C,\gamma)
be a conilpotent coaugmented coalgebra
and
P\neq 0
be a left
C
-contramodule.
Then
{}^{\gamma}\!P\neq 0
.

Proof.

This is an equivalent restatement of
Lemma
3.1
for
D=C/\gamma(k)
.
∎

For a conilpotent coalgebra
C
, the subcomodule
{}_{\gamma}M
of
a
C
-comodule
M
can be also described as the socle (i. e.,
the maximal semisimple subcomodule) of
M
, and the quotient
contramodule
{}^{\gamma}\!P
of a
C
-contramodule
P
can be
described as the cosocle (i. e., the maximal semisimple quotient
contramodule) of
P
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(C,\gamma)
be a conilpotent coaugmented coalgebra. Then

(a)
a morphism of left
C
-comodules
f\colon L\longrightarrow M
is
injective if and only if the induced map of vector spaces

{}_{\gamma}f\colon{}_{\gamma}L\longrightarrow{}_{\gamma}M
is injective;

(b)
a morphism of left
C
-contramodules
f\colon P\longrightarrow Q
is
surjective if and only if the induced map of vector spaces

{}^{\gamma}\!f\colon{}^{\gamma}\!P\longrightarrow{}^{\gamma}Q
is surjective.

Proof.

Part (a): if the map
f
is injective, then so is
the map
{}_{\gamma}f
, since
{}_{\gamma}N
is a vector subspace in
N

for every left
C
-comodule
N
.
Conversely, the functor
N\longmapsto{}_{\gamma}N
is left exact; so
if
K=\ker(f)\in C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
, then
{}_{\gamma}K=\ker({}_{\gamma}f)
.
Now if
{}_{\gamma}K=0
, then
K=0
by
Lemma
3.2
(a).
The proof of part (b) is similar (or rather, dual-analogous in
the sense of
[
36
, Section 8.2]
).
The functor
P\longmapsto{}^{\gamma}\!P
is right exact, etc.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(C,\gamma)
be a conilpotent coaugmented coalgebra. Then

(a)
a morphism of injective left
C
-comodules
f\colon I\longrightarrow J

is an isomorphism if and only if the induced map of vector spaces

{}_{\gamma}f\colon{}_{\gamma}I\longrightarrow{}_{\gamma}J
is an isomorphism;

(b)
a morphism of projective left
C
-contramodules

f\colon P\longrightarrow Q
is an isomorphism if and only if the induced map of
vector spaces
{}^{\gamma}\!f\colon{}^{\gamma}\!P\longrightarrow{}^{\gamma}Q
is
an isomorphism.

Proof.

Part (a): if the map
{}_{\gamma}f
is injective, then the morphism
f

is injective by Lemma
3.3
(a).
Now if the
C
-comodule
I
is injective, then
f
is a split
monomorphism in
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
.
Therefore,
{}_{\gamma}\coker(f)=\coker({}_{\gamma}f)
, and
Lemma
3.2
(a) tells us that
f
is an isomorphism
whenever
{}_{\gamma}f
is.
The proof of part (b) is dual-analogous.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(C,\gamma)
be a conilpotent coaugmented coalgebra. Then

(a)
for any left
C
-comodule
M
there exists a cofree
left
C
-comodule
J
together with an injective
C
-comodule morphism

f\colon M\longrightarrow J
such that the induced map
{}_{\gamma}f\colon{}_{\gamma}M\longrightarrow{}_{\gamma}J
is an isomorphism of
k
-vector spaces;

(b)
for any left
C
-contramodule
Q
there exists a free
left
C
-contramodule
P
together with a surjective
C
-contramodule
morphism
f\colon P\longrightarrow Q
such that the induced map
{}^{\gamma}\!f\colon{}^{\gamma}\!P\longrightarrow{}^{\gamma}Q
is an isomorphism of
k
-vector spaces.

Proof.

Part (a): put
V={}_{\gamma}M
and
J=C\otimes_{k}V
.
Then the injective map
V\longrightarrow J
induced by the map
\gamma\colon k\longrightarrow C
is a
C
-comodule morphism
M\supset{}_{\gamma}M\longrightarrow J
.
Since the cofree comodules are injective, this morphism can be
extended to a
C
-comodule morphism
f\colon M\longrightarrow J
.
The morphism
f
is injective by Lemma
3.3
(a).
The comodule
J
can be also described as an injective envelope of
the comodule
M
in the comodule category
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
.
The proof of part (b) is dual-analogous; the contramodule
P
can be
also described as a projective cover of the contramodule
Q
in
contramodule category
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
(cf.
[
32
, Example 12.3]
).
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
C
be a conilpotent (coaugmented) coalgebra. Then

(a)
a
C
-comodule is injective if and only if is cofree;

(b)
a
C
-contramodule is projective if and only if it is
free.

Proof.

Generally speaking, over a coalgebra
C
over a field
k
,
the injective comodules are the direct summands of the cofree ones, and
the projective contramodules are the direct summands of the free ones
(see Section
2
).
In the situation at hand with a conilpotent coalgebra
C
,
the assersion of part (a) follows straighforwardly from
Lemmas
3.4
(a)
and
3.5
(a).
Part (b) follows from Lemmas
3.4
(b)
and
3.5
(b).
∎

Let
K^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
be a complex of left
C
-comodules.
We will say that the complex
K^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is
minimal
if
the differential in the complex of vector spaces
{}_{\gamma}K^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}

vanishes.
Similarly, a complex of left
C
-contramodules
Q_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is said to be

minimal
if the differential in the complex of vector spaces

{}^{\gamma}Q_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
vanishes.

Let
M
be a
C
-comodule.
An injective coresolution
M\longrightarrow J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
of the comodule
M
is
said to be
minimal
if the complex of comodules
J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}

is minimal.
Dually, a projective resolution
Q_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow P
of a
C
-contramodule

P
is said to be
minimal
if the complex of contramodules

Q_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is minimal.

Proposition 3.7
.

The following assertions hold for any conilpotent coalgebra
C
.

(a)
Any
C
-comodule
M
admits a minimal injective
coresolution. A minimal injective coresolution of
M
is unique up to
a (nonunique) isomorphism.

(b)
Any
C
-contramodule
P
admits a minimal projective
resolution. A minimal projective resolution of
P
is unique up to
a (nonunique) isomorphism.

Proof.

Part (a): to construct a minimal injective coresolution of
M
, all
one needs to do is to iterate the construction of
Lemma
3.5
(a).
Pick an injective
C
-comodule
J^{0}
together with an injective
morphism of
C
-comodules
M\longrightarrow J^{0}
such that the induced map

{}_{\gamma}M\longrightarrow{}_{\gamma}J^{0}
is an isomorphism.
Then the induced map
{}_{\gamma}J^{0}\longrightarrow{}_{\gamma}(J^{0}/M)
is zero.
Pick an injective
C
-comodule
J^{1}
together with an injective
morphism of
C
-comodules
J^{0}/M\longrightarrow J^{1}
such that the induced
map
{}_{\gamma}(J^{0}/M)\longrightarrow{}_{\gamma}J^{1}
is an isomorphism, etc.

To prove uniqueness, let
M\longrightarrow I^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
and
M\longrightarrow J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
be two
minimal injective coresolutions of
M
.
Then there exists a morphism of complexes of
C
-comodules
f\colon I^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
making the triangular diagram
M\longrightarrow I^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
commutative.
Now we have
{}_{\gamma}I^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}=\Hom_{C}(k,I^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})
and
{}_{\gamma}J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}=\Hom_{C}(k,I^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})
.
The map of complexes
\Hom_{C}(k,f)\colon\Hom_{C}(k,I^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})\longrightarrow\Hom_{C}(k,J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})
induces an isomorphism of the cohomology spaces,
as both the complexes compute
\Ext_{C}^{*}(k,M)
.
Since both the complexes
{}_{\gamma}I^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
and
{}_{\gamma}J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
have
vanishing differentials by assumption, it follows that
\Hom_{C}(k,f)={}_{\gamma}f
is a termwise isomorphism of complexes of vector spaces.
Applying Lemma
3.4
(a), we conclude
that
f
is a termwise isomorphism of complexes of
C
-comodules.

The proof of part (b) is dual-analogous.
∎

4.
The Left and Right Comodule and Contramodule Ext Comparison

Let
(C,\gamma)
be a coaugmented coalgebra over a field
k

(as defined in Section

).
Then the one-dimensional

-vector space

can be endowed with
left and right
C
-comodule and

-contramodule structures
defined in terms of

.
We recall the notation
\Ext_{C}^{*}({-},{-})
for the Ext spaces
in the category of left
C
-comodules

and

\Ext_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}^{*}({-},{-})
for the Ext spaces in the category of

C
-comodules
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C
, as well as the notation

\Ext^{C,*}({-},{-})
for the Ext spaces in the category of left

C
-contramodules
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

The condition that the vector spaces
\Ext_{C}^{n}(k,k)
be
finite-dimensional plays a key role in this paper.
In this section we explain that this condition is left-right symmetric:
it holds for a coalgebra
C
if and only if it holds for
C^{\mathrm{op}}
.
Furthermore, the vector space
\Ext_{C}^{n}(k,k)
is finite-dimensional
if and only if the vector space
\Ext^{C,n}(k,k)
is.
The following proposition is certainly essentially well-known; we spell
out the details here for the sake of completeness of the exposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(C,\gamma)
be a coaugmented coalgebra over
k
.
Then there are natural isomorphisms of Ext spaces

\Ext_{C}^{n}(k,k)\simeq\Ext_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}^{n}(k,k)\qquad\text{for all\/ $n\geq 0$}.

Moreover, in fact, the Ext-algebras
\Ext_{C}^{*}(k,k)
and

\Ext_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}^{*}(k,k)
are naturally opposite to each other
(as graded algebras),

\Ext_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}^{*}(k,k)\simeq\Ext_{C}^{*}(k,k)^{\mathrm{op}}.

The module analogue of Proposition
4.1

is well-known: for any augmented associative algebra
A
over
a field
k
, there are natural isomorphisms of
k
-vector spaces

\Ext^{n}_{A}(k,k)\simeq\Tor^{A}_{n}(k,k)^{*}\simeq\Ext^{n}_{A^{\mathrm{op}}}(k,k)
, making

\Ext_{A^{\mathrm{op}}}^{*}(k,k)
the graded algebra with the opposite
multiplication to
\Ext_{A}^{*}(k,k)
.

First proof.

Put
C_{+}=C/\gamma(k)
.
For any left
C
-comodule
M
, the reduced cobar coresolution

0\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muM\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC\otimes_{k}M\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC\otimes_{k}C_{+}\otimes_{k}M\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC\otimes_{k}C_{+}\otimes_{k}C_{+}\otimes_{k}M\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\dotsb

is an injective coresolution of
M
in the abelian category
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
.
Applying the functor
\Hom_{C}(k,{-})
, we obtain a cobar complex

(5)

M\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC_{+}\otimes_{k}M\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC_{+}\otimes_{k}C_{+}\otimes_{k}M\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\dotsb

computing the vector spaces
\Ext_{C}^{*}(k,M)
.
In particular, the vector spaces
\Ext_{C}^{*}(k,k)
are computed by
the cobar complex

(6)

k\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC_{+}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC_{+}\otimes_{k}C_{+}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC_{+}\otimes_{k}C_{+}\otimes_{k}C_{+}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\dotsb

[
39
, Section 1.1]
,
[
19
, Section 2.1]
.

Reordering the tensor factors inside every term of the complex in
the opposite way identifies
the complex (
6
) for a coalgebra
C
with
the similar complex for the opposite coalgebra
C^{\mathrm{op}}
.
This suffices to prove the first assertion of the proposition.

To prove the second assertion, one needs to observe that the obvious
(free associative) multiplication on the cobar
complex (
6
) induces a multiplication on its
cohomology spaces that is precisely opposite to the composition
multiplication on
\Ext_{C}^{*}(k,k)
.
Moreover, the obvious left action of
the DG-algebra (
6
) on
the complex (
5
) induces a left action
of the cohomology algebra of (
6
) on
the cohomology of (
5
) corresponding to
the natural graded right module structure over
\Ext_{C}^{*}(k,k)

on
\Ext^{*}_{C}(k,M)
.
Thus the isomorphism
\Ext_{C}^{*}(k,k)\simeq\Ext_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}^{*}(k,k)

obtained by comparing the cobar complexes (
6
)
for
C
and
C^{\mathrm{op}}
makes the multiplication on the latter Ext-algebra
opposite to the one on the former one.

This argument was sketched in
[
19
, Section 2.4]
.
An alternative proof of the first assertion of the proposition was
suggested in
[
19
, Section 2.5]
.
∎

Second proof.

Let
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--comod}}}
and
{\operatorname{\mathsf{comod--}}}C
denote the abelian categories of

finite-dimensional
left and right
C
-comodules, respectively.
One makes two observations, which, taken together, imply both
the assertions of the proposition.

Firstly, the fully faithful, exact inclusions of abelian categories

C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--comod}}}\longrightarrow C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
and
{\operatorname{\mathsf{comod--}}}C\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C
induce
isomorphisms on the Ext spaces.
Moreover, the triangulated functors between bounded above derived
categories
\mathsf{D}^{-}(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--comod}}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{-}(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}})
and

\mathsf{D}^{-}({\operatorname{\mathsf{comod--}}}C)\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{-}({\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C)
induced by these inclusions
of abelian categories are fully faithful.
This is a particular case of a well-known general result for abelian
or even exact categories (see
[
12
, opposite version of
Proposition 1.7.11]
,
[
13
, opposite version of Theorem 13.2.8]
,

[
14
, opposite version of Theorem 12.1(b)]
,
or
[
37
, Proposition 2.1]
).
The point is that for any finite-dimensional
C
-comodule
L
,
any
C
-comodule
M
, and any surjective
C
-comodule morphism

M\longrightarrow L
, there exists a finite-dimensional
C
-subcomodule

M^{\prime}\subset M
such that the composition
M^{\prime}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow L

is surjective.

Secondly, for any finite-dimensional right
C
-comodule
N
, the dual
vector space
N^{*}
is naturally a finite-dimensional left
C
-comodule.
Here it helps to notice that for any such
N
there exists
a finite-dimensional subcoalgebra
E\subset C
such that
the
C
-comodule structure on
N
arises from an
E
-comodule
structure
[
36
, Lemma 3.1(c)]
.
Consequently, there is a natural anti-equivalence of abelian categories

N\longmapsto N^{*}\colon({\operatorname{\mathsf{comod--}}}C)^{\mathsf{op}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC{\operatorname{\mathsf{--comod}}}

taking the right
C
-comodule
k
to the left
C
-comodule
k
.
This anti-equivalence induces the desired anti-isomorphism of
the Ext algebras.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
C
be a coalgebra over a field
k
.
Let
M\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C
be a right
C
-comodule and
L\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{comod--}}}C
be
a finite-dimensional right
C
-comodule.
Then the vector space of left
C
-contramodule homomorpisms

M^{*}\longrightarrow L^{*}
is naturally isomorphic to the double dual vector
space to the vector space of right
C
-comodule homomorphisms

L\longrightarrow M
,

(7)

\Hom^{C}(M^{*},L^{*})\simeq\Hom_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)^{**}.

This isomorphism identifies the map
\Hom_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)\longrightarrow\Hom^{C}(M^{*},L^{*})
induced by the contravariant functor of vector
space dualization
N\longmapsto N^{*}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C\longrightarrow C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}

with the natural inclusion
\Hom_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)\longrightarrow\Hom_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)^{**}
of a vector space
\Hom_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)

into its double dual vector space.

Proof.

Notice that for any
k
-vector space
V
and any finite-dimensional

k
-vector space
W
there is a natural isomorphism of
k
-vector
spaces

(8)

\Hom_{k}(V^{*},W^{*})\simeq\Hom_{k}(W,V)^{**},

where
V^{*}=\Hom_{k}(V,k)
and
W^{*}=\Hom_{k}(W,k)
.
The isomorphism (
8
)
identifies the map
\Hom_{k}(W,V)\longrightarrow\Hom_{k}(V^{*},W^{*})
induced
by the contravariant functor
U\longmapsto U^{*}\colon k{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Vect}}}\longrightarrow k{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Vect}}}

with the natural inclusion
\Hom_{k}(W,V)\longrightarrow\Hom_{k}(W,V)^{**}

of a vector space
\Hom_{k}(W,V)
into its double dual vector space.

Now, for any two right
C
-comodules
L
and
M
, the vector space

\Hom_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)
is the kernel of (the difference of) a natural
pair of maps
\Hom_{k}(L,M)\rightrightarrows\Hom_{k}(L,\>\allowbreak M\otimes_{k}\penalty C)
,

(9)

0\longrightarrow\Hom_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)\longrightarrow\Hom_{k}(L,M)\,\rightrightarrows\Hom_{k}(L,\>M\otimes_{k}C).

For any two left
C
-contramodules
P
and
Q
, the vector space

\Hom^{C}(P,Q)
is the kernel of (the difference of) a natural pair of
maps
\Hom_{k}(P,Q)\rightrightarrows\Hom_{k}(\Hom_{k}(C,P),Q)
,

(10)

0\longrightarrow\Hom^{C}(P,Q)\longrightarrow\Hom_{k}(P,Q)\,\rightrightarrows\Hom_{k}(\Hom_{k}(C,P),Q).

In particular, for
P=M^{*}
and
Q=L^{*}
, we have

(11)

0\longrightarrow\Hom^{C}(M^{*},L^{*})\longrightarrow\Hom_{k}(M^{*},L^{*})\,\rightrightarrows\Hom_{k}(\Hom_{k}(C,M^{*}),L^{*}).

It remains to use the natural
isomorphism (
8
) in
order to show that the double dual vector space functor

U\longmapsto U^{**}
takes the rightmost pair of parallel morphisms
in the sequence (
9
) to the rightmost pair of
parallel morphisms in
the sequence (
11
)
for a finite-dimensional
C
-comodule
L
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(C,\gamma)
be a coaugmented coalgebra over
k
.
Then the vector space
\Ext^{C,n}(k,k)
is naturally isomorphic to
the double dual vector space to the vector space

\Ext_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}^{n}(k,k)
,

\Ext^{C,n}(k,k)\simeq\Ext^{n}_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(k,k)^{**}\qquad\text{for all\/ $n\geq 0$}.

This isomorphism identifies the map
\Ext_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}^{n}(k,k)\longrightarrow\Ext^{C,n}(k,k)
induced by the exact contravariant functor of vector
space dualization
N\longmapsto N^{*}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C\longrightarrow C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
with
the natural inclusion
\Ext^{n}_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(k,k)\longrightarrow\Ext^{n}_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(k,k)^{**}
of a vector space
\Ext^{n}_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(k,k)

into its double dual vector space.

Proof.

This observation goes back to the discussion
in
[
20
, Section A.1.2]
.
The point is that the functor
N\longmapsto N^{*}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C\longrightarrow C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
takes injective right
C
-comodules to projective
left
C
-contramodules, which makes it easy to compute the induced
map of the Ext spaces.

For any coalgebra
C
and any right
C
-comodule
M
, pick
an injective coresolution

(12)

0\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muM\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muJ^{0}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muJ^{1}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muJ^{2}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\dotsb

of
M
in the abelian category
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C
.
Applying the dual vector space functor
\Hom_{k}({-},k)

to the complex (

), we obtain
the complex

(13)

0\mskip 1.5mu\leftarrow\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{k}(M,k)\mskip 1.5mu\leftarrow\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{k}(J^{0},k)\mskip 1.5mu\leftarrow\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{k}(J^{1},k)\mskip 1.5mu\leftarrow\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\mskip 1.5mu\dotsb,

which is a projective resolution of the left
C
-contramodule

M^{*}=\Hom_{k}(M,k)
.

Now let
L
be a another right
C
-comodule.
Then one can compute the vector spaces
\Ext^{n}_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)

as the cohomology spaces of the complex obtained by applying
the functor
\Hom_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,{-})
to
the coresolution (
12
).
Similarly, one can compute the vector spaces
\Ext^{C,n}(M^{*},L^{*})

as the cohomology spaces of the complex obtained by applying
the functor
\Hom^{C}({-},L^{*})
to
the resolution (

).
Taking into account Lemma

,
we obtain a natural isomorphism of complexes

inducing a natural isomorphism of the cohomology spaces

for all right
C
-comodules
M
, all finite-dimensional right

C
-comodules

, and all integers

.
Moreover, it follows from the second assertion of the lemma that
the map
\Ext^{n}_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)\longrightarrow\Ext^{C,n}(M^{*},L^{*})
induced by
the functor
N\longmapsto N^{*}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Comod--}}}C\longrightarrow C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}

agrees with the obvious map
\Ext^{n}_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)\longrightarrow\Ext^{n}_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(L,M)^{**}
.
∎

5.
Weakly Finite Koszulity Implies Comodule Ext Isomorphism

The aim of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
C
be a conilpotent coalgebra over a field
k
and
n\geq 0

be an integer such that the vector space
\Ext^{i}_{C}(k,k)
is
finite-dimensional for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
Then the map of Ext spaces

\Ext_{C}^{i}(L,M)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext_{C^{*}}^{i}(L,M)

induced by the inclusion functor
\Upsilon\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is an isomorphism for all left
C
-comodules
L
and
M

and all
0\leq i\leq n
.

We start with a lemma describing some vector spaces of morphisms in
the category of left
C^{*}
-modules for a coalgebra
C
.
A right
C
-comodule
N
is said to be
finitely cogenerated

if it is a subcomodule of a cofree
C
-comodule
V\otimes_{k}C
with
a finite-dimensional vector space of cogenerators
V
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
C
be a coalgebra over
k
, let
N
be a right
C
-comodule,
and let
U
be a
k
-vector space.
Then there is a natural monomorphism of
k
-vector spaces

\eta_{N,U}\colon N\otimes_{k}U\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{C^{*}}(N^{*},\>C\otimes_{k}U),

which is an isomorphism whenever
N
is a finitely cogenerated
injective
C
-comodule.
Here the left
C^{*}
-module structure on
N^{*}=\Hom_{k}(N,k)
is
obtained by dualizing the right
C^{*}
-module structure on
N

(or equivalently, by applying the forgetful functor to the natural
left
C
-contramodule structure on
N^{*}
), while the left

C^{*}
-module structure on
C\otimes_{k}U
comes from the cofree left

C
-comodule structure.

Proof.

The map
\eta_{N,U}
is defined by the formula

\eta_{N,U}(x\otimes u)(f)=f(x_{(0)})x_{(1)}\otimes u,

for all
x\in N
,
u\in U
, and
f\in N^{*}
.
Here
\nu(x)=x_{(0)}\otimes x_{(1)}\in N\otimes_{k}C
is a notation for
the right
C
-coaction map
\nu\colon N\longrightarrow N\otimes_{k}C
.

To prove the isomorphism assertion for a finitely cogenerated
injective
C
-comodule
N
, it suffices to consider the case

N=V\otimes_{k}C
, where
\dim_{k}V<\infty
.
In this case,
N^{*}=C^{*}\otimes_{k}V^{*}
is a free left
C^{*}
-module,
so
\Hom_{C^{*}}(N^{*},\>C\otimes_{k}U)=\Hom_{k}(V^{*},\>C\otimes_{k}U)=V\otimes_{k}C\otimes_{k}U=N\otimes_{k}U
.

To prove that
\eta_{N,U}
is an injective map for any
C
-comodule

N
, consider the
k
-linear map

\Hom_{C^{*}}(N^{*},\>C\otimes_{k}U)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{k}(N^{*},U)

induced by the counit map
\epsilon\colon C\longrightarrow k
.
Notice that the counit is
not
a
C
-comodule morphism, and
consequently not a
C^{*}
-module morphism, but only a linear map of

k
-vector spaces.
The composition

N\otimes_{k}U\overset{\eta}{\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu}\Hom_{C^{*}}(N^{*},\>C\otimes_{k}U)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{k}(N^{*},U)

is the natural injective map of
k
-vector spaces
N\otimes_{k}U\longrightarrow\Hom_{k}(N^{*},U)
(defined for any two vector spaces
U
and
W=N
).
Since the composition is a monomorphism, so is
the map
\eta=\eta_{N,U}
.
∎

Let
C=(C,\gamma)
be a coaugmented coalgebra.
For every
m\geq 0
, denote by
F_{m}C\subset C
the kernel of
the composition
C\longrightarrow C^{\otimes m+1}\longrightarrow(C/\gamma(k))^{\otimes m+1}
,
where
\mu^{(m)}\colon C\longrightarrow C^{\otimes m+1}
is the iterated comultiplication
map and

is the natural
surjection.
So one has
F_{-1}C=0
and
F_{0}C=\gamma(k)\subset C
.
One can check that
F
is a comultiplicative filtration on
C
,
that is,

\mu(F_{m}C)\subset\sum\nolimits_{p+q=m}F_{p}C\otimes_{k}F_{q}C\subset C\otimes_{k}C

for all
m\geq 0

[
39
, Section 3.1]
.

Let
L
be a left
C
-comodule.
Denote by
F_{m}L\subset L
the full preimage of the subspace

under the coaction map
\nu\colon L\longrightarrow C\otimes_{k}L
.
So one has
F_{-1}L=0
and
F_{0}L={}_{\gamma}L
(in the notation of
Section
3
).
One can check that
F
is a comultiplicative filtration on
L

compatible with the filtration
F
on
C
, that is,

\nu(F_{m}L)\subset\sum\nolimits_{p+q=m}F_{p}C\otimes_{k}F_{q}L\subset C\otimes_{k}L

for all
m\geq 0

[
19
, Section 4.1]
.

In other words, this means that
F_{m}L
is a
C
-subcomodule of

for every

and
the successive quotient

-comodules

are trivial (i. e., the coaction of

in them is
induced by the coaugmentation

In fact, one has
F_{m}L/F_{m-1}L={}_{\gamma}(L/F_{m-1}L)\subset L/F_{m-1}L
for every

.

By the definition, a coaugmented coalgebra
C
is conilpotent if and
only if
C=\bigcup_{m=0}^{\infty}F_{m}C
(i. e., the increasing filtration

F
on
C

In this case, it follows that

, i. e.,
the increasing filtration

on any
C
-comodule is exhaustive
as well.

Proof of
Theorem
5.1
.

The argument resembles the proofs of the comodule Ext comparison
theorems in
[

, Sections 5.4 and 5.7]
(see
specifically
[
35
, Theorem 5.21]
).
Notice first of all that the functor
\Upsilon
is always fully

[

According to Lemma

from

for all left

-comodules
L
, all injective

-comodules

, and all integers

.

According to the discussion preceding this proof, the

-comodule

Using the Eklof lemma

Let

\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muV_{n-1}\otimes_{k}C\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muV_{n}\otimes_{k}C\overset{\tau_{n}}{\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu}V_{n+1}\otimes_{k}C\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\dotsb

Applying the dual vector space functor

6.
Comodule Ext Isomorphism Implies Weakly Finite Koszulity

The aim of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
C
be a conilpotent coalgebra over a field
k
and
n\geq 1

be the minimal integer for which the vector space
\Ext_{C}^{n}(k,k)
is
infinite-dimensional.
Then the map
\Ext^{n}_{C}(k,k)\longrightarrow\Ext^{n}_{C^{*}}(k,k)
induced by
the comodule inclusion functor
\Upsilon\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

is injective, but
not
surjective.
In fact, if
\lambda
is the dimension cardinality of the
k
-vector
space
\Ext^{n}_{C}(k,k)
and
\kappa
is the cardinality of
the field
k
, then the dimension cardinality of

\Ext^{n}_{C^{*}}(k,k)
is equal to
\kappa^{\kappa^{\lambda}}
.

The following lemma computing the dimension cardinality of the dual
vector space is a classical result.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
V
be an infinite-dimensional vector space of dimension
cardinality
\lambda
over a field
k
of cardinality
\kappa
.
Then the dimension cardinality of the dual
k
-vector space

V^{*}=\Hom_{k}(V,k)
is equal to
\kappa^{\lambda}
.

Proof.

This is
[
11
, Section IX.5]
.
∎

Let
C
be a conilpotent coalgebra.
Then the vector space
\Ext^{1}_{C}(k,k)
can be interpreted as
the
space of cogenerators
of the coalgebra
C

(we refer to
[
26
, Lemma 5.2]
for a discussion).
So we will say that a conilpotent coalgebra
C
is
finitely
cogenerated
if the vector space
\Ext^{1}_{C}(k,k)
is finite-dimensional.

For any coaugmented coalgebra
(C,\gamma)
, the dual linear map

\gamma^{*}\colon C^{*}\longrightarrow k
to the coaugmentation
\gamma\colon k\longrightarrow C

defines an augmentation on the algebra
C^{*}
.
Accordingly, the one-dimensional vector space
k
can be endowed
with left and right
C^{*}
-module structures provided by
the augmentation
\gamma^{*}
.
These
C^{*}
-module structures on
k
can be also viewed as coming
from the left and right
C
-comodule structures on
k
induced
by the coaugmentation
\gamma
.
The same module structures also come from the left and right

C
-contramodule structures on
k
induced by
\gamma
.

We start with a discussion of the case
n=1
in

6.1
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
C
be a conilpotent coalgebra over a field
k
.
Then there is a natural
k
-vector space monomorphism

\Ext_{C}^{1}(k,k)^{**}\,\rightarrowtail\,\Ext_{C^{*}}^{1}(k,k).

The dimension cardinality of the vector space
\Ext_{C^{*}}^{1}(k,k)

is equal to that of the vector space
\Ext_{C}^{1}(k,k)^{**}
.

Proof.

Notice first of all the isomorphism
\Ext_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}^{*}(k,k)\simeq\Ext_{C}^{*}(k,k)
provided by
Proposition
4.1
.
Furthermore, let
A
be an augmented
k
-algebra with
an augmentation
\alpha\colon A\longrightarrow k
and the augmentation ideal

A_{+}=\ker(\alpha)\subset A
.
Then the vector space
\Ext^{1}_{A}(k,k)\simeq\Tor_{1}^{A}(k,k)^{*}\simeq\Ext^{1}_{A^{\mathrm{op}}}(k,k)
is computed as the kernel of the
k
-linear map

A_{+}^{*}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu(A_{+}\otimes_{k}A_{+})^{*}

dual to the multiplication map
A_{+}\otimes_{k}A_{+}\longrightarrow A_{+}
.
In particular, the vector space
\Ext^{1}_{C^{*}}(k,k)
is
naturally isomorphic to the kernel of the map

(19)

C_{+}^{**}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu(C_{+}^{*}\otimes_{k}C_{+}^{*})^{*}

dual to the multiplication map
C_{+}^{*}\otimes_{k}C_{+}^{*}\longrightarrow C_{+}^{*}

(where
C_{+}=C/\gamma(k)
).

On the other hand, from the cobar complex (
6
)
one can immediately see that the vector space
\Ext_{C}^{1}(k,k)

is naturally isomorphic to the kernel of the comultiplication map

C_{+}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC_{+}\otimes_{k}C_{+}.

Hence the double dual vector space
\Ext_{C}^{1}(k,k)^{**}
is
naturally isomorphic to the kernel of the map

(20)

C_{+}^{**}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu(C_{+}\otimes_{k}C_{+})^{**}.

Notice that
(C_{+}^{*}\otimes_{k}C_{+}^{*})
is naturally a subspace
in
(C_{+}\otimes_{k}C_{+})^{*}
; hence
(C_{+}^{*}\otimes_{k}C_{+}^{*})^{*}
is a quotient
space of
(C_{+}\otimes_{k}C_{+})^{**}
.
One can also observe that the cokernel of the map

Comparing the maps (

)
and (

).

To compute the dimension cardinality, put

, and
notice that
C
is a subcoalgebra of the cofree conilpotent (tensor)

cospanned by

[
26
, Lemma 5.2]
(see
[

, Sections 2.3 and 3.3]
for
an introductory discussion).
Hence, if

is infinite-dimensional, then the dimension cardinalities
of

and
C_{+}
are equal to each other.
Now

is a subspace in
\Ext^{1}_{C^{*}}(k,k)
, which is in
turn a subspace in
C_{+}^{**}
.
Thus the dimension cardinalities of all the three vector spaces

,
\Ext^{1}_{C^{*}}(k,k)
, and

are equal to

If

5.1

(for

).
∎

7.
Weakly Finite Koszulity Implies Contramodule Ext Isomorphism

Before stating the main theorem of this section, let us have
a discussion of
separated
and
nonseparated
contramodules.

Let
C=(C,\gamma)
be a coaugmented coalgebra and
P
be a left

C
-contramodule.
For every
m\geq 0
, denote by
F^{m}P\subset P
the image of the subspace

\Hom_{k}(C/F_{m-1}C,P)\subset\Hom_{k}(C,P)
under the contraaction
map
\pi\colon\Hom_{k}(C,P)\longrightarrow P
.
So one has
F^{0}P=P
and
F^{1}P=\ker(P\twoheadrightarrow{}^{\gamma}\!P)

(in the notation of Sections
3

and
5
).
One can check that
F
is a contramultiplicative decreasing filtration
on
P
compatible with the increasing filtration
F
on
C
, that is

\pi(\Hom_{k}(C/F_{q-1}C,F^{p}P))\subset F^{p+q}P

for all
p
,
q\geq 0
.

In other words, this means that
F^{m}P
is a
C
-subcontramodule of
P

for every
m\geq 0

and
the successive quotient
C
-contramodules

F^{m}P/F^{m+1}P
are trivial (i. e., the contraaction of
C
in them
is induced by the coaugmentation
\gamma
).
In fact, one has
F^{m}P/F^{m+1}P={}^{\gamma}(F^{m}P)
for every
m\geq 0
.

The problem with the decreasing filtration
F
on
P
is that it is

not
in general separated (not even when
C
is a conilpotent
coalgebra); see the counterexample in
[
24
, Section 1.5]

(going back to
[
42
, Example 2.5]
,
[
48
, Example 3.20]
,
and
[
20
, Section A.1.1]
).
The next lemma explains how this problem can be partially rectified
(for a conilpotent coalgebra
C
).

We say that a vector space
P
endowed with a decreasing filtration
F

is
separated
if the natural map to the projective limit

\lambda_{P,F}\colon P\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\varprojlim\nolimits_{m\geq 1}P/F^{m}P

is injective, and that
P
is
complete
if the map
\lambda_{P,F}

is surjective.
A contramodule
P
over a conilpotent coalgebra
C
is said to be

separated
(respectively,
complete
) if it is separated
(resp., complete) with respect to its natural decreasing filtration
F

constructed above.

Notice that, for any subcontramodule
Q
in a contramodule
P
, one has

F^{m}Q\subset F^{m}P
for all
m\geq 0
.
Thus any subcontramodule of a separated contramodule is separated.
For a free contramodule
P=\Hom_{k}(C,V)
, one has

F^{m}P=\Hom_{k}(C/F_{m-1}C,V)
.
Hence all free (and therefore, all projective) contramodules over
a conilpotent coalgebra are separated and complete.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
C
be a conilpotent coalgebra. Then

(a)
any
C
-contramodule is complete;

(b)
any
C
-contramodule can be presented as the quotient
contramodule of a separated contramodule by a separated subcontramodule.

Proof.

Part (b) holds because any contramodule is a quotient of
a free contramodule, which is separated, and any subcontramodule of
a separated contramodule is separated.
For part (a), see
[
20
, Lemma A.2.3]
.
∎

The aim of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
C
be a conilpotent coalgebra over a field
k
and
n\geq 1

be an integer such that the vector space
\Ext^{i}_{C}(k,k)
is
finite-dimensional for all

Then the map of the Ext spaces

(29)

\Ext^{C,i}(P,Q)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext_{C^{*}}^{i}(P,Q)

-contramodules

The map (

all

-contramodules

The following lemma, describing some tensor products of

-modules

Lemma 7.3

Let
C
be a coalgebra over

-comodule,
and let

-module structure on

) contain
the annihilator of some finite-dimensional vector subspace of
C

(with respect to the pairing map

)

[

.

For any coalgebra
C
over a field

, the contramodule forgetful
functor
\Theta\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\allowbreak\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
has a left
adjoint functor
\Delta\colon C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
Given a left

-module

To construct the functor

-modules by the rule

Functoriality of the category object corepresenting a (given

-modules.
Then there is always a unique way to extend an abelian category-valued

-modules.
Simply put, to compute the contramodule

-modules

is the cokernel of
the contramodule map
\Delta(f)\colon\Delta(F^{\prime})\longrightarrow\Delta(F^{\prime\prime})
.
∎

10.
Co-Noetherian and Cocoherent Conilpotent Coalgebras

The aim of this section is to explain that certain Noetherianity
or coherence-type conditions on a conilpotent coalgebra
C
imply
weakly finite Koszulity.
In particular, all
cocommutative
conilpotent coalgebras are
weakly finitely Koszul; this fact will be relevant for the discussion
in the next Section

.

Let
C
be a coalgebra over a field
k

A left
C
-comodule is said to be
finitely cogenerated
if it
can be embedded as a subcomodule into a cofree
C
-comodule
C\otimes_{k}V

with a finite-dimensional space of cogenerators

.
A coalgebra

is said to be
left co-Noetherian

quotient comodule of a finitely cogenerated left
C
-comodule is
finitely cogenerated
[
47
,
9
,
29
]
.
A coalgebra

is said to be

if it is
Artinian as an object of the category of left
C
-comodules

C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
, that is, any descending chain of left coideals in
C

terminates
[
9
]
,
[
29
, Section 2]
.
(Here by a
left coideal
in

one means a left subcomodule
in the left
C
-comodule

.)

Lemma 10.1
.

(a)
A coalgebra

is left Artinian if and only if it is
left co-Noetherian
and
the maximal cosemisimple subcoalgebra
of
C
is finite-dimensional.
In particular, a conilpotent coalgebra is left Artinian if and
only if it is left co-Noetherian.

(b)
A coalgebra

is left Artinian if and only if its
dual algebra
C^{*}
is right Noetherian.

Proof.

See
[
9
, Proposition 1.6 of the published version,
or Proposition 2.5 of the
arXiv
version]

or
[
29
, Lemmas 2.6 and 2.10(b), Example 2.7, and the general
discussion in Section 2]
.
∎

Proposition 10.2
.

Any left or right co-Noetherian conilpotent coalgebra is weakly
finitely Koszul.

Proof.

The weak finite Koszulity property of a conilpotent coalgebra is
left-right symmetric by Proposition
4.1
;
so it suffices to consider the case of a left co-Noetherian conilpotent
coalgebra
C
.
Then it follows easily from the co-Noetherianity that every finitely
cogenerated left
C
-comodule
L
has a coresolution
J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
by finitely
cogenerated cofree
C
-comodules

,

.
In particular, this applies to all finite-dimensional left

C
-comodules
L
; so the left
C
-comodule
k
has such
a coresolution
J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
.
It remains to compute the spaces
\Ext^{n}_{C}(k,k)
as

H^{n}(\Hom_{C}(k,J^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}))
and notice that the vector space

\Hom_{C}(k,J^{n})={}_{\gamma}J^{n}
is finite-dimensional for every

.
∎

A left
C
-comodule is said to be
finitely copresented
if it
can be obtained as the kernel of a morphism of cofree
C
-comodules

C\otimes_{k}V\longrightarrow C\otimes_{k}U
with finite-dimensional vector spaces of
cogenerators
V
and
U
.
A coalgebra
C
is said to be
left cocoherent
if every
finitely cogenerated quotient comodule of a finitely copresented left

C
-comodule is finitely copresented
[

, Section 2]
.

Lemma 10.3
.

Let

be a conilpotent coalgebra over

.
Then

(a)
a left
C
-comodule

is finitely cogenerated if and
only if the vector space

is finite-dimensional;

(b)
a finitely cogenerated left
C
-comodule

is
finitely copresented if and only if the vector space
\Ext_{C}^{1}(k,L)

is finite-dimensional;

(c)
in particular, the left
C
-comodule

is finitely
copresented if and only if the coalgebra

is finitely cogenerated.

Proof.

Part (a) follows from
Lemma
3.5
(a).
To prove the “if” assertion in part (b), one can use
Proposition
3.7
(a) together with
Corollary
3.6
(a) to the effect
that for any left
C
-comodule

there exists a left exact

-comodules

with

The “only if” assertion in part (b) is deduced from the fact that

.
In particular, the left

-comodule

∎

Proposition 10.4
.

Any finitely cogenerated left or right cocoherent conilpotent
coalgebra is weakly finitely Koszul.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case when

is left cocoherent.
Then it follows from the cocoherence and the same fact that

) that
any finitely copresented left
C
-comodule

has a coresolution

by finitely cogenerated cofree

-comodules

,

.
If

is finitely cogenerated, then this applies to

(by Lemma

the left
C
-comodule
k
has such a coresolution

.
The argument finishes exactly in the same way as the proof of
Proposition

.
∎

Remark 10.5
.

A coalgebra version of the Morita equivalence theory was developed
by Takeuchi
[
45
]
.
The notions of co-Noetherianity and co-coherence for coalgebras are

not
Morita–Takeuchi invariant (neither is conilpotence, of
course; while Artinianity of coalgebras
is
invariant under
the Morita–Takeuchi equivalence).

The co-Noetherianity and cocoherence properties have Morita–Takeuchi
invariant versions, which are called
quasi-co-Noetherianity

and
quasi-cocoherence
in
[
33
, Section 3]
,

[
30
, Sections 5.1–5.4]
.
(The former property was called “strict quasi-finiteness”
in
[
9
]
.)
The key definition, going back to Takeuchi
[
45
]
, is that of
a “quasi-finite comodule”, called
quasi-finitely cogenerated

in the terminology of
[
33
,
30
]
.
A left
C
-comodule
L
is called quasi-finitely cogenerated if
the vector space
\Hom_{C}(K,L)
is finite-dimensional for any
finite-dimensional left
C
-comodule
K
.

One can see from
[
29
, Lemma 2.2(e)]
that over a coalgebra
C

with finite-dimensional maximal cosemisimple subcoalgebra (in
particular, over a conilpotent coalgebra
C
) the classes of
finitely cogenerated and quasi-finitely cogenerated comodules coincide.
Consequently, a conilpotent coalgebra is quasi-co-Noetherian in
the sense of
[
33
,
30
]
if and only if it is co-Noetherian, and
a conilpotent coalgebra is quasi-cocoherent in the the sense
of
[
33
,
30
]
if and only if it is cocoherent.
That is why we were not concerned with the quasi-co-Noetherianity
and quasi-cocoherence properties in this section, but only with
the co-Noetherianity and cocoherence properties.

11.
Cocommutative Conilpotent Coalgebras

Let
V
be a
k
-vector space.
We refer to
[
36
, Sections 2.3 and 3.3]
for an introductory
discussion of the
cofree conilpotent
(
tensor
) coalgebra

\Ten(V)=\bigoplus\nolimits_{n=0}^{\infty}V^{\otimes n}

cospanned by a vector space
V
.
The
cofree conilpotent cocommutative
(
symmetric
)
coalgebra
\Sym(V)
is defined as the subcoalgebra

\Sym(V)=\bigoplus\nolimits_{n=0}^{\infty}\Sym^{n}(V)\subset\Ten(V),

where
\Sym^{n}(V)\subset V^{\otimes n}
is the vector subspace of all
symmetric tensors in
V^{\otimes n}
.
One can easily see that
\Sym(V)
is the maximal cocommutative
subcoalgebra in
\Ten(V)
; in fact, for any cocommutative coalgebra
C

over
k
and coalgebra homomorphism
f\colon C\longrightarrow\Ten(V)
, the image
of
f
is contained in
\Sym(V)
.

Let
C
be a conilpotent coalgebra over
k
.
Assume that
C
is finitely cogenerated; so the vector space

V=\Ext^{1}_{C}(k,k)=\ker(C_{+}\to C_{+}\otimes_{k}C_{+})
is finite-dimensional.
Choose a
k
-linear projection
g\colon C_{+}\longrightarrow V
onto the vector
subspace
V\subset C_{+}
.
Then, according to
[
26
, Lemma 5.2(a)]
, the map
g
extends
uniquely to coalgebra homomorphism
f\colon C\longrightarrow\Ten(V)
.
Moreover, by
[
26
, Lemma 5.2(b)]
, the map
f
is injective.

Assume additionally that
C
is cocommutative.
Then, following the discussion above, the image of the map
f
is
contained in the subcoalgebra
\Sym(V)\subset\Ten(V)
.
Hence
C
is a subcoalgebra in
\Sym(V)
.

Choose a
k
-vector space basis
x_{1}^{*}
, …,
x_{m}^{*}
in
the vector space
V
.
Then
x_{1}
, …,
x_{m}
is a basis in the dual vector space
V^{*}
.
The choice of such bases identifies the
k
-vector space dual

k
-algebra
\Sym(V)^{*}
to the symmetric coalgebra
\Sym(V)
with
the algebra of formal power series in the variables
x_{1}
, …,
x_{m}
,

\Sym(V)^{*}\simeq k[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]].

Accordingly, the
k
-algebra
C^{*}
dual to
C
is a quotient algebra
of the algebra of formal power series by an ideal

J\subset k[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]]
,

C^{*}\simeq k[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]]/J.

Denote by
s_{1}
, …,
s_{m}\in C^{*}
the images of the elements

x_{1}
, …,
x_{m}
under the surjective
k
-algebra homomorphism

k[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]]\longrightarrow C^{*}
dual to the injective coalgebra map

C\longrightarrow\Sym(V)
.
We have shown that
C^{*}
is a complete Noetherian commutative local
ring with the maximal ideal
I=(s_{1},\dotsc,s_{m})\subset C^{*}

generated by the elements
s_{1}
, …,
s_{m}\in C^{*}
.
Indeed, the ring of formal power series
k[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]]
is
a complete Noetherian commutative local ring; hence so is any (nonzero)
quotient ring of
k[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]]
.

Corollary 11.1
.

All finitely cogenerated cocommutative conilpotent coalgebras are
co-Noetherian.
Consequently, all such coalgebras are weakly finitely Koszul.

Proof.

Let
C
be a finitely cogenerated cocommutative conilpotent coalgebra
over
k
.
Then the algebra
C^{*}
is Noetherian, as explained above.
By Lemma
10.1
(a–b), it follows that
the coalgebra
C
is co-Noetherian.
Now Proposition
10.2
tells us that
C
is
weakly finitely Koszul.
∎

Corollary
11.1
says that the condition
of Theorem
1.2
(vii) is
satisfied for any finitely cogenerated cocommutative conilpotent
coalgebra
C
.
Consequently, the triangulated functors
\Upsilon^{+}\colon\mathsf{D}^{+}(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{+}(C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}})
and
\Theta^{-}\colon\mathsf{D}^{-}(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{-}(C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}})
are fully faithful.
In the rest of this section, our aim is to show, using the results of
the paper
[
25
]
, that the triangulated functors

\Upsilon^{\varnothing}\colon\mathsf{D}(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}(C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}})
and

\Theta^{\varnothing}\colon\mathsf{D}(C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}(C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}})
between
the unbounded derived categories are actually fully faithful in this
case, too.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
I\subset R
be an ideal.
An
R
-module
M
is said to be
I
-torsion
if for every
pair of elements
s\in I
and

there exists an integer

such that
s^{n}x=0
in
M
.
Equivalently,

is
I
-torsion if and only if for every
s\in I

one has
R[s^{-1}]\otimes_{R}M=0
.
Here
R[s^{-1}]
is the ring obtained by inverting formally the element

s\in R
(or equivalently, by localizing
R
at the multiplicative
subset
S=\{1,s,s^{2},s^{3},\dotsc\}\subset R
spanned by
s
).

An
R
-module
P
is said to be
an
I
-contramodule

[
27
,
25
]
if for every element

s\in I
one has

\Hom_{R}(R[s^{-1}],P)=0=\Ext^{1}_{R}(R[s^{-1}],P).

It is important to notice here that the projective dimension of
the
R
-module

can never exceed
1

[
27
, proof of Lemma 2.1]
.

The full subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-tors}}}}
of all
I
-torsion

-modules is closed under submodules, quotients, extensions,
and infinite direct sums in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
, as one can easily
see
[
27
, Theorem 1.1(b)]
.
The full subcategory
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-ctra}}}}
of all
I
-contramodule

-modules is closed under kernels, cokernels, extensions,
and infinite products in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

[

, Proposition 1.1]
,
[

, Theorem 1.2(a)]
.
Consequently, both the categories
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-tors}}}}
and

are abelian, and the inclusion functors

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-tors}}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
and
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-ctra}}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

Theorem 11.2
.

Let
C
be a finitely cogenerated cocommutative conilpotent coalgebra
over a field
k
, and let
I\subset C^{*}
be the maximal ideal of
the complete Noetherian commutative local ring
C^{*}
.
Then

(a)
the essential image of the comodule inclusion functor

\Upsilon\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
coincides with the full subcategory
of
I
-torsion
C^{*}
-modules
C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-tors}}}}\subset C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
,
so the functor
\Upsilon
induces a category equivalence

C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\simeq C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-tors}}}}
;

(b)
the contramodule forgetful functor
\Theta\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is fully faithful, and its essential image coincides
with the full subcategory of
I
-contramodule
C^{*}
-modules

C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-ctra}}}}\subset C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
, so the functor
\Theta

induces a category equivalence
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\simeq C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{I{\operatorname{\mathsf{-ctra}}}}
.

Proof.

The dual vector space
W^{*}
to any (discrete infinite-dimensional)
vector space
W
is naturally endowed with a pro-finite-dimensional
(otherwise known as linearly compact or pseudocompact) topology.
The annihilators of finite-dimensional vector subspaces in
W
are
precisely all the open vector subspaces in
W^{*}
, and they form
a base of neighborhoods of zero in
W^{*}
.
In the case of a coalgebra
C
, the vector space
C^{*}
with its
pro-finite-dimensional topology is a topological algebra.

A left
C^{*}
-module
N
is said to be
discrete
(or “rational”
in the terminology of
[
44
]
) if, for every element
x\in N
,
the annihilator of
x
is an open left ideal in
C^{*}
.
Equivalently, this means that the action map
C^{*}\times N\longrightarrow N

is continuous in the pro-finite-dimensional topology on
C^{*}
and
the discrete topology on
N
.
The essential image of the comodule inclusion functor
\Upsilon\colon C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
consists precisely of all the discrete

C^{*}
-modules
[
44
, Propositions 2.1.1–2.1.2]
.
(Cf. the proof of
Theorem
1.2

in Section
9
.)
These assertions hold for any coalgebra
C
over
k
.

In the case of a finitely cogenerated cocommutative conilpotent
coalgebra
C
, the surjective map
k[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]]\longrightarrow C^{*}

is open and continuous, because it is obtained by applying the dual
vector space functor
W\longmapsto W^{*}=\Hom_{k}(W,k)
to the inclusion
of discrete vector spaces (coalgebras)
C\longrightarrow\Sym(V)
.
The resulting pro-finite-dimensional topology on the algebra of formal
power series
k[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]]
is the usual (adic) topology of
the formal power series.
One easily concludes that a
C^{*}
-module is discrete if and only if
it is discrete over
k[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]]
.
By the definition of the adic topology on a formal power series ring

k[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]]
, a module over this ring is discrete if and
only if it is a torsion module for the maximal ideal

(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m})\subset k[[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]]
.
Hence a
C^{*}
-module
N
is discrete if and only if it is a torsion
module for the ideal
I=(s_{1},\dotsc,s_{m})\subset C^{*}
.
One can also observe that the pro-finite-dimensional topology on

C^{*}
coincides with the
I
-adic topology, since this holds for
the coalgebra
\Sym(V)
.
This proves part (a).

To prove part (b), one needs to use the concept of a
contramodule
over a topological ring
as an intermediate step.
Without going into the (somewhat involved) details of this definition,
which can be found in
[
22
, Section 2.1]
,

[
24
, Section 2.1]
, or
[
32
, Sections 2.5–2.7]
,
let us say that the category of left
C
-contramodules is naturally
equivalent (in fact, isomorphic) to the category of left contramodules
over the topological ring
C^{*}
.
This equivalence agrees with the natural forgetful functors acting
from the categories of left
C
-contramodules and left

C^{*}
-contramodules to the category of left
C^{*}
-modules.
We refer to
[
22
, Section 1.10]
or
[
24
, Section 2.3]

for the details of the proof of this assertion.

The contramodule forgetful functor
C{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow C^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is
fully faithful by Theorem
7.4
.
In the special case of
cocommutative
coalgebras
C
, one can
also obtain this result as a particular case of the following
theorems, which provide more information.
By
[
22
, Theorem B.1.1]
or
[
24

The following lemma tells how far one can go computing the Ext groups
with a resolution which is only
initially
projective.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be an abelian category and

0\longleftarrow X\longleftarrow P_{0}\longleftarrow P_{1}\longleftarrow P_{2}\longleftarrow\dotsb

be an exact sequence in
\mathsf{A}
.
Then for every object
Y\in\mathsf{A}
there are natural maps of abelian
groups

(47)

H^{n}(\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(P_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}},Y))\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{A}}(X,Y)

defined for all
n\geq 0
.
The map (
47
) is an isomorphism
whenever the objects
P_{i}
are projective in
\mathsf{A}
for all

0\leq i\leq n-1
.

Proof.

The point of the lemma is that one does
not
need the objects

P_{n}
or
P_{n+1}
(but only the objects
P_{0}
, …,
P_{n-1}
) to be
projective in order to compute
\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{A}}(X,Y)
using a resolution

P_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
of an object
X\in\mathsf{A}
.
The proof is standard.
Denote by
X_{i}
the image of the differential
P_{i}\longrightarrow P_{i-1}

(so
X=X_{0}
).
Then the connecting homomorphisms in the long exact sequences of
groups
\Ext^{*}_{\mathsf{A}}({-},Y)
related to the short exact sequences

0\longrightarrow X_{i+1}\longrightarrow P_{i}\longrightarrow X_{i}\longrightarrow 0
in
\mathsf{A}
provide
natural maps of abelian groups

H^{n}(\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(P_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}},Y))=\coker(\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(P_{n-1},Y)\to\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{n},Y))\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{n-1},Y)\\
\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext^{2}_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{n-2},Y)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\dotsb\longrightarrow\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{1},Y)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{0},Y),

whose composition is the desired
map (
47
).
Furthermore, the map
H^{n}(\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(P_{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}},Y))\allowbreak\longrightarrow\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{n-1},Y)
is an isomorphism whenever

\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{A}}(P_{n-1},Y)=0
; the map

\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{n-1},Y)\longrightarrow\Ext^{2}_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{n-2},Y)
is
an isomorphism whenever
\Ext^{1}_{\mathsf{A}}(P_{n-2},Y)=0=\Ext^{2}_{\mathsf{A}}(P_{n-2},Y)
,
etc.; and the map
\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{1},Y)\longrightarrow\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{0},Y)

is an isomorphism whenever
\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{A}}(P_{0},Y)=0=\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{A}}(P_{0},Y)
.
∎

The next lemma is essentially well-known, but we give it a more
precise formulation than it usually receives.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
be abelian categories, and let

\Phi\colon\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
be an exact functor.
Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and
Y\in\mathsf{B}
be a fixed object.

(a)
Assume that the map of groups
\Ext^{n-1}

\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{B}}(X,Y)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{A}}(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))

induced by the functor
\Phi
is an isomorphism for all objects

X\in\mathsf{B}
.
Then the map of groups
\Ext^{n}

\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{B}}(X,Y)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{A}}(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))

induced by the functor
\Phi
is injective for all objects
X\in\mathsf{B}
.

(b)
More generally, let
0\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow T\longrightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence in
\mathsf{B}
.
Assume that the map
\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{B}}(P,Y)\longrightarrow\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{A}}(\Phi(P),\Phi(Y))
is surjective, while the map

\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{B}}(X,Y)\longrightarrow\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{A}}(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))

is injective.
Then the intersection of the kernel of the map

\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{B}}(T,Y)\longrightarrow\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{B}}(P,Y)
induced by the epimorphism

P\longrightarrow T
with the kernel of the map
\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{B}}(T,Y)\longrightarrow\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{A}}(\Phi(T),\Phi(Y))
induced by the functor
\Phi
is
the zero subgroup in
\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{B}}(T,Y)
.

Proof.

This observation goes back, at least,
to
[
2
, Remarque 3.1.17(i)]
.
Part (b) is provable by a straightforward diagram chase of
the commutative diagram of a morphism of long exact sequences

\textstyle{\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{B}}(P,Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{B}}(X,Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{B}}(T,Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{B}}(P,Y)}
\textstyle{\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{A}}(\Phi(P),\Phi(Y))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Ext^{n-1}_{\mathsf{A}}(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{A}}(\Phi(T),\Phi(Y))}

induced by the functor
\Phi
.

To deduce (a) from (b), let
T\in\mathsf{B}
be an object and

\beta\in\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{B}}(T,Y)
be an extension class annihilated by the map

\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{B}}(T,Y)\longrightarrow\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{A}}(\Phi(T),\Phi(Y))
.
Choose an epimorphism

in
\mathsf{B}
such that
\beta

is annihilated by the induced map

Then part (b) implies that

The final series of lemmas concerns abelian categories with enough

Lemma A.5
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}

\mathsf{B}

\mathsf{B}_{\mathsf{inj}}\subset\mathsf{B}

all such injective objects.
Let

be an integer.

(1)

X

and integers

(2)

Proof.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(2) Condition (2) is clearly the particular
case of (1) for
Y=J\in\mathsf{B}_{\mathsf{inj}}
.

(2)
\Longrightarrow
(1) For
i=0
, the map of Ext groups
in question is an isomorphism by the assumption that the functor

\Phi
is fully faithful.
For
1\leq i\leq n

.

Y\in\mathsf{B}

0\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow Y^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0
in
\mathsf{B}
with
J\in\mathsf{B}_{\mathsf{inj}}

Then the exact functor
\Phi
induces a commutative diagram of

\textstyle{\Ext^{i-1}_{\mathsf{B}}(X,J)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Ext^{i-1}_{\mathsf{B}}(X,Y^{\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Ext^{i}_{\mathsf{B}}(X,Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Microscopic Mechanism of Pair-, Charge- and Spin-Density-Wave Instabilities in Interacting 𝑫 D -Dimensional Fermi Liquids
Dmitry Miserev, 1∗ Herbert Schoeller, 2 Jelena Klinovaja, 1 and Daniel Loss

Abstract
We present an analytic theory unraveling the microscopic mechanism of instabilities within interacting D D -dimensional Fermi liquid. Our model consists of a D D -dimensional electron gas subject to an instantaneous electron-electron interaction of a finite range exceeding the average inter-particle distance. Pair, charge and spin susceptibilities are evaluated via the one-loop renormalization group theory and via the bosonization approach, giving identical results. In case of a repulsive interaction, we identify an intrinsic Fermi liquid instability towards insulating spin/charge density wave order when the interaction coupling strength reaches a universal critical value. If both electron and hole pockets of the same size are present, the ground state is an excitonic insulator at arbitrarily small repulsive interaction. If the interaction is attractive, the ground state is a singlet non-BCS superconductor with a uniform condensate. In case if both electron and hole Fermi surfaces are present, we predict an instability towards the inter-pocket pair-density-wave ordering at the critical coupling. This prediction lends strong theoretical support to the pair-density-wave scenario of superconductivity in cuprate materials. Due to its simple and universal nature, presented microscopic mechanism of intrinsic instabilities of interacting D D -dimensional Fermi liquids constitutes a solid theoretical ground for understanding quantum phase transitions in a variety of quantum materials, from ultraclean semiconductor quantum wells to high-temperature superconductors.

II
Free static susceptibilities

We consider a zero-temperature, spin-degenerate,
D
-dimensional electron gas,
D>1
, with two spherical Fermi surfaces, the electron pocket of radius
k_{e}
centered at momentum
\bm{K}
and the hole pocket of radius
k_{h}
centered at momentum
-\bm{K}
, see Fig.
1
.
If all Fermi surfaces are electron-like (or hole-like), the inter-pocket susceptibilities exhibit the same singularities as the intra-pocket ones.
The electrons interact via a forward-scattering density-density interaction with a finite range
R_{0}
that is much greater than the inter-particle distance, i.e.
R_{0}\gg\lambda_{e}=2\pi/k_{e},\lambda_{h}=2\pi/k_{h}
[see Eq. (
1
)].
The long-distance asymptotics of the free electron,
G_{e}(\tau,\bm{r})
, and the free hole,
G_{h}(\tau,\bm{r})
, Green’s functions at large distances
r=|{\bm{r}}|\gg\lambda_{e},\lambda_{h}
, where
\bm{r}
is the
D
-dimensional position coordinate, and large imaginary times
\tau\gg 1/(k_{h}v_{h}),1/(k_{e}v_{e})
, where
v_{e}>0
and
v_{h}>0
are the electron and the hole Fermi velocities, respectively, follows from the general dimensional reduction procedure
Miserev et al. 2023
,

\displaystyle G_{e}(\tau,\bm{r})=e^{i\bm{K}\cdot\bm{r}}\sum\limits_{\nu_{e}}\frac{e^{i\nu_{e}\left(k_{e}r-\vartheta\right)}}{\left(\lambda_{e}r\right)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}g_{\nu_{e}}(\tau,r),

(2)

\displaystyle G_{h}(\tau,\bm{r})=e^{-i\bm{K}\cdot\bm{r}}\sum\limits_{\nu_{h}}\frac{e^{i\nu_{h}\left(k_{h}r-\vartheta\right)}}{\left(\lambda_{h}r\right)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}g_{\nu_{h}}(\tau,r),

(3)

where

\nu_{e},\nu_{h}\in\{\pm 1\}
are the chiral indices,
\vartheta=\pi(D-1)/4
is the semiclassical phase,
g_{\nu_{e}}(\tau,x)
and
g_{\nu_{h}}(\tau,x)
are effective one-dimensional (1D) slowly-varying Green’s functions,

{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\displaystyle g_{\nu_{e}}(\tau,x)&=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{i\nu_{e}x-v_{e}\tau},\\
\displaystyle g_{\nu_{h}}(\tau,x)&=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{-i\nu_{h}x-v_{h}\tau}.\end{array}}

(4)

Note that the hole-like origin of the hole pocket manifests itself in the chirality sign in
g_{\nu_{h}}(\tau,x)
.

The free static susceptibilities are defined in the standard way,

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\chi^{(0)}_{P,ab}(\bm{r})=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}G_{a}(\tau,\bm{r})G_{b}(\tau,\bm{r})\,d\tau,

(5)

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\chi^{(0)}_{C/S,ab}(\bm{r})=-2\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}G_{a}(\tau,\bm{r})G_{b}(-\tau,-\bm{r})\,d\tau,

(6)

where
a,b\in\{e,h\}
and the subscripts

,
C
, and
S
label pair, charge, and spin susceptibilities, respectively.
The free charge and spin susceptibilities are the same for a spin-degenerate electron gas, where the factor of 2 in Eq. (

The intra-pocket pair susceptibilities
\chi^{(0)}_{P,{ee}}(\bm{r})

The intra-pocket pair susceptibility features

\displaystyle\chi^{(0)}_{P,{aa}}(\bm{r})=\frac{e^{\pm 2i\bm{K}\cdot\bm{r}}}{2\pi v_{a}\lambda_{a}^{D-1}r^{D}},

(7)

\displaystyle\chi^{(0)}_{P,{eh}}(r)=\frac{\cos\left(Q_{eh}^{+}r-2\vartheta\right)}{\pi(v_{e}+v_{h})\left(\lambda_{e}\lambda_{h}\right)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}r^{D}},

(8)

where
a\in\{e,h\}
, and
+2\bm{K}
(
-2\bm{K}
) in Eq. (
7

ee

(hh)

The intra-pocket pair susceptibilities

probing the zero-momentum pairing, are logarithmically divergent at

, where
\chi^{(0)}_{P,{aa}}(\bm{Q})
is the Fourier transform of Eq. (
7

{\bm{r}}
.
Here,
\bm{Q}=\pm 2\bm{K}
is due to the choice of origin of the corresponding pocket, see Fig.
1

The Fourier transform
\chi^{(0)}_{P,{eh}}(\bm{Q})
of the inter-pocket pair susceptibility in Eq. (
8

that is not singular at

The intra-pocket charge and spin susceptibilities exhibit long-range harmonics at

\displaystyle\chi_{C/S,{aa}}^{(0)}(r)=\frac{\cos\left(2k_{a}r-2\vartheta\right)}{\pi v_{a}\lambda_{a}^{D-1}r^{D}},

\displaystyle\chi_{C/S,{eh}}^{(0)}(\bm{r})=\frac{2e^{2i\bm{K}\cdot\bm{r}}\cos\left(Q_{eh}^{-}r\right)}{\pi(v_{e}+v_{h})\left(\lambda_{e}\lambda_{h}\right)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}r^{D}},

where
a\in\{e,h\}
and
\chi_{C/S,{he}}^{(0)}(\bm{r})=\chi_{C/S,{eh}}^{(0)}(-\bm{r})

III
Renormalization group

Next, we consider interaction corrections to the susceptibilities emerging from a forward-scattering density-density interaction, see Eq. (
1
).
Following the recipe of calculation used in Ref.
Hutchinson et al. 2024
, which is based on a dimensional reduction technique
Miserev et al. 2023
, we find the first-order interaction corrections to the susceptibilities, see App.
A
,

\displaystyle\chi_{P,{ab}}^{(1)}(\bm{r})=-\chi_{P,{ab}}^{(0)}(\bm{r})\gamma_{ab}\ln\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right),

(11)

\displaystyle\chi_{C/S,{ab}}^{(1)}(\bm{r})=\chi_{C/S,{ab}}^{(0)}(\bm{r})\gamma_{ab}\ln\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right),

(12)

where
a,b\in\{e,h\}
and
r\gg R_{0}
.
Three dimensionless coupling constants are introduced here,

\displaystyle\gamma_{ee}=\frac{2V_{2}}{\pi v_{e}},\hskip 3.0pt\gamma_{hh}=\frac{2V_{2}}{\pi v_{h}},\hskip 3.0pt\gamma_{eh}=\frac{4V_{2}}{\pi(v_{e}+v_{h})}.

(13)

The notation
V_{2}
for the interaction matrix element is taken from Ref.
Hutchinson et al. 2024
,

\displaystyle V_{2}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}V(r)\,dr.

(14)

The logarithmic corrections to the susceptibilities originate from vertex corrections, see Fig.
2
A, and indicate the emergence of singularities.
In case of a repulsive interaction (
\gamma_{ab}>0
), both inter-pocket and intra-pocket charge and spin susceptibilities are logarithmically enhanced, see Eq. (
12

11

The situation is opposite for an attractive interaction (
\gamma_{ab}<0

In order to construct the one-loop RG, we also require second-order interaction corrections.
Leading second-order diagrams for the inter-pocket pair susceptibility are shown in Fig.
2
B,C, all other diagrams are subleading in
D\geq 1
, see Apps.
B
,
C
, and
D

Leading logarithmic contributions to charge and spin susceptibilities also originate from the vertex corrections that are diagrammatically similar to the ones shown in Fig.
2

Deferring details of the calculations to App.

, we provide here only the final results, accurate to leading logarithmic order,

\displaystyle\chi_{P/C/S,{ab}}^{(2)}(\bm{r})=\chi_{P/C/S,{ab}}^{(0)}(\bm{r})\frac{\gamma_{ab}^{2}}{2}\ln^{2}\!\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right),

(15)

where, again,
a,b\in\{e,h\}
and
r\gg R_{0}
.

The sum of diagrams up to second order for each susceptibility takes the following form:

\displaystyle\chi(\bm{r})=\chi^{(0)}(\bm{r})F\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}},\gamma_{0}\right),

(16)

\displaystyle F(\rho,\gamma_{0})=1+\gamma_{0}\ln\rho+\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{2}\ln^{2}\!\rho,

(17)

where
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{ab}
for
\chi_{C/S,ab}(\bm{r})

\gamma_{0}=-\gamma_{ab}
for
\chi_{P,ab}(\bm{r})
.

\displaystyle\chi(\bm{r})=\chi^{(0)}(\bm{r})\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right)^{\!{\gamma_{0}}}.

\chi_{P,ab}(\bm{r})

;
Castro Neto and Fradkin 1994
;

;
Metzner et al. 1998
;
Kopietz
;

;
Delacrétaz et al. 2022

and

According to the bosonization

Castro Neto and Fradkin 1994
;
Fröhlich et al. 1995
;
Metzner et al. 1998
;
Kopietz
;

;

;
Miserev et al. 2023

\displaystyle\tilde{V}(i\omega,q)=\left[V^{-1}(q)-\delta\Pi_{0}(i\omega,q)\right]^{-1},

(19)

where

is the
D
-dimensional Fourier transform of the bare interaction

For this reason, the matrix element

;
Gangadharaiah et al. 2005
;

;

;

.

in Eq. (

\displaystyle\tilde{V}(i\omega,q)\approx V(q),

(20)

where
q<1/R_{0}
.
In contrast to

\propto\gamma^{2}
corrections to the critical exponents of the 1D susceptibilities

;
Sólyom 1973
;
Dzyaloshinskiǐ and Larkin 1974
;

;
Giamarchi 2003

According to Ref.
Miserev et al. 2023

-dimensional Fourier transform of

20

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{1\rm D}(i\omega,q)\approx\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\,e^{-iqx}V(x)\approx 2V_{2},

(21)

where
q
is the 1D momentum transfer,
|q|<1/R_{0}
, and
V_{2}
is defined in Eq. (

).
Together with the RPA approximation [see Eq. (

V_{1\rm D}(i\omega,q)
,

\displaystyle\hskip-17.0ptV_{1\rm D}(i\omega,q)\approx 2V_{2}\left[1+2\sum\limits_{a\in\{e,h\}}\gamma_{aa}\frac{\omega^{2}}{\omega^{2}+(v_{a}q)^{2}}\right]^{-1}\hskip-3.0pt,

(22)

where
\gamma_{aa}
is defined in Eq. (
13
), and
v_{a}
is the Fermi velocity of pocket
a
.
Here, we used the following form of the dynamic component of the 1D polarization operator,

\displaystyle\delta\Pi_{0}^{(1\rm D)}(i\omega,q)=\sum\limits_{a\in\{e,h\}}\frac{2}{\pi v_{a}}\frac{\omega^{2}}{\omega^{2}+(v_{a}q)^{2}}.

(23)

In contrast to the conventional Tomonaga-Luttinger model
Tomonaga 1950
;
Luttinger 1963
;
Mattis and Lieb 1965
, the 1D bare interaction given by Eq. (
22
) is frequency-dependent.
In this paper we aim to calculate critical exponents of
D
-dimensional susceptibilities in linear order in
\gamma_{ab}
which corresponds to a one-loop RG approximation.
The dynamic part of
V_{1\rm D}(i\omega,q)
contributes to
\gamma_{ab}^{2}
corrections and can therefore be neglected for
\gamma_{ab}\ll 1
,

\displaystyle V_{1\rm D}(i\omega,q)\approx 2V_{2}.

(24)

The full bosonization treatment accounting for the frequency-dependent part of
V_{1\rm D}(i\omega,q)
is left for future study.

We stress that the approximation given by Eq. (
24
) is valid for correlation functions whose critical exponents scale linearly with
\gamma_{ab}
for
\gamma_{ab}\ll 1
.
In particular, this approximation is not suitable for fermion Green’s functions that require an exact treatment of
V_{1\rm D}(i\omega,q)
.
For this reason, investigation of the fermion Green’s function is deferred to future studies.

The general dimensional reduction procedure constructed in Ref.
Miserev et al. 2023
allows us to map
D
-dimensional susceptibilities onto 1D susceptibilities,

\displaystyle\chi_{P,aa}(\bm{r})=\frac{2e^{\pm 2i\bm{K}\cdot\bm{r}}}{\left(\lambda_{a}r\right)^{D-1}}\chi_{P,aa}^{(1\rm D)}(r),

(25)

\displaystyle\chi_{P,eh}(\bm{r})=\frac{2\cos\left(Q_{eh}^{+}r-2\vartheta\right)}{(\lambda_{e}\lambda_{h})^{\frac{D-1}{2}}r^{D-1}}\chi_{P,eh}^{(1\rm D)}(r),

(26)

\displaystyle\chi_{C/S,aa}(\bm{r})=\frac{4\cos\left(2k_{a}r-2\vartheta\right)}{\left(\lambda_{a}r\right)^{D-1}}\chi_{C/S,aa}^{(1\rm D)}(r),

(27)

\displaystyle\chi_{C/S,eh}(\bm{r})=\frac{4e^{2i\bm{K}\cdot\bm{r}}\cos\left(Q_{eh}^{-}r\right)}{(\lambda_{e}\lambda_{h})^{\frac{D-1}{2}}r^{D-1}}\chi_{C/S,eh}^{(1\rm D)}(r),

(28)

where susceptibilities with the superscript
(1D)
correspond to conventional 1D susceptibilities,
+2\bm{K}
(
-2\bm{K}
) in Eq. (
25
) corresponds to
a=e
(
a=h
).
We point out that the pocket index plays the role of a pseudo-spin for 1D susceptibilities, all oscillatory factors are taken into account explicitly, see Eqs. (
25
)–(
28
).

The bosonization procedure under the approximation given by Eq. (
24
) is applied to the 1D susceptibilities and follows a standard 1D bosonization path, e.g., see Refs.
von Delft and Schoeller 1998
;
Giamarchi 2003
, which results in the following scaling behavior,

\displaystyle\chi_{P,ab}(\bm{r})\approx\chi_{P,ab}^{(0)}(\bm{r})\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right)^{\!\!-\gamma_{ab}},

(29)

\displaystyle\chi_{C/S,ab}(\bm{r})\approx\chi_{C/S,ab}^{(0)}(\bm{r})\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right)^{\!\gamma_{ab}},

(30)

where the critical exponents are expanded to linear order in the coupling constants.
The derivation of Eqs. (
29
) and (
30
) is given in App.
F
.
As expected, the bosonization approach exactly agrees with the one-loop RG result given by Eq. (
18
).
We note that the correct treatment of
\propto\gamma_{ab}^{2}
and higher-order corrections to the critical exponents would require proper treatment of the dynamic part of the effective 1D bare interaction, see Eq. (
22
). This will be done elsewhere.

We stress that any finite temperature
T>0
cuts the power-law singularities at any coupling strength, see App.
F
.
This behavior is qualitatively different from the BCS-like mean-field mechanisms providing finite critical temperatures at any coupling.
Instead, quite remarkably, the behavior found here is reminiscent of 1D Luttinger liquids, where forward scattering alone is not enough to stabilize long-range order.
Studies of possible stabilization mechanisms for the relevant orders is deferred to future work.

V
Instabilities of the Fermi liquid

In this section we derive the analytical form of the instabilities which signal a breakdown of the Fermi liquid state. First we analyze repulsive interactions,
\gamma_{ab}>0
.
In this case only the charge and spin susceptibilities develop relevant singularities, see Eq. (
30
).
Taking the
D
-dimensional Fourier transform (see App.
G
), we find the singular behavior of these susceptibilities in momentum space,

\displaystyle\hskip-17.0pt\chi_{C/S,aa}(\delta Q)=\frac{\cos\left[\frac{\pi}{2}\delta\gamma_{aa}+\vartheta\,\mathrm{sgn}(\delta Q)\right]\Gamma\left(\delta\gamma_{aa}\right)}{\pi v_{a}\left[2R_{0}\lambda_{a}\right]^{\gamma_{c}}\left|\delta Q\,R_{0}\right|^{\delta\gamma_{aa}}},

(31)

\displaystyle\hskip-17.0pt\chi_{C/S,eh}(\delta Q)

\displaystyle\hskip 17.0pt=\frac{2\cos\left[\frac{\pi}{2}\delta\gamma_{eh}-\vartheta\,\mathrm{sgn}(\delta Q)\right]\Gamma\left(\delta\gamma_{eh}\right)}{\pi(v_{e}+v_{h})\left|R_{0}(\lambda_{e}-\lambda_{h})\right|^{\gamma_{c}}\left|\delta Q\,R_{0}\right|^{\delta\gamma_{eh}}},

(32)

\displaystyle\hskip-17.0pt\gamma_{c}=\frac{D-1}{2},

(33)

where

\delta\gamma_{ab}=\gamma_{ab}-\gamma_{c}
,
\mathrm{sgn}(x)
returns the sign of
x
, and
\Gamma(x)
is the Euler gamma function.
In Eq. (
31
),
\delta Q=Q-2k_{a}\ll 1/R_{0}\ll k_{a}
, while in Eq. (
32
)
\bm{Q}=2\bm{K}+\bm{q}
and
\delta Q=q-Q_{eh}^{-}\ll\mathrm{min}\{1/R_{0},Q_{eh}^{-}\}
, where
Q_{eh}^{-}=|k_{e}-k_{h}|
.
Here, we point out that Eqs. (
31
), (
32
) capture only the non-analytic part of the susceptibilities, the analytic part does not contribute to the long-distance asymptotics given by Eq. (
30
).

For
\gamma_{ab}<\gamma_{c}
charge and spin susceptibilities in Eqs. (
31
), (
32
) are finite, supporting the FL ground state at weak coupling.
However, at
\gamma_{ab}>\gamma_{c}
the susceptibilities diverge at
\delta Q=0
.
At the quantum critical point,
\delta\gamma_{ab}\to 0
, the critical charge and spin susceptibilities develop the following non-analytic behavior as function of
\delta Q
:

\displaystyle\hskip-17.0pt\chi_{C/S,aa}(\delta Q)=-\frac{2\cos\vartheta\ln|\delta QR_{0}|+\pi\sin\vartheta\,\mathrm{sgn}(\delta Q)}{2\pi v_{a}\left[2R_{0}\lambda_{a}\right]^{\gamma_{c}}},

(34)

\displaystyle\hskip-17.0pt\chi_{C/S,eh}(\delta Q)=-\frac{2\cos\vartheta\ln|\delta QR_{0}|-\pi\sin\vartheta\,\mathrm{sgn}(\delta Q)}{\pi(v_{e}+v_{h})\left|R_{0}\left(\lambda_{e}-\lambda_{h}\right)\right|^{\gamma_{c}}},

(35)

where all finite analytic contributions at
\delta Q=0
are neglected as they do not lead to singularities at
\delta Q=0
.
In the two-dimensional (2D) case,
\vartheta=\pi/4
, and the leading singularity is logarithmic with asymmetric peak structure due to the
\mathrm{sgn}(\delta Q)
-term, see Eqs. (
34
), (
35
).
Plots of the 2D charge and spin susceptibilities
\chi_{C/S,aa}(\delta Q)
for different values of
\gamma_{aa}
are shown in Fig.
3
, where all plots are defined up to a constant shift due to regular analytic corrections.

In the three-dimensional (3D) case we get
\vartheta=\pi/2
, which results in a discontinuity
\propto\mathrm{sgn}(\delta Q)
of the 3D charge and spin susceptibilities, see Fig.
4
.
For
\gamma_{ab}>\gamma_{c}
a logarithmic singularity
\propto\delta\gamma_{ab}\ln|\delta QR_{0}|\mathrm{sgn}(\delta Q)
develops, see green dashed curve in Fig.
4
.

The singularity in the intra-pocket charge and spin susceptibilities for
\gamma_{aa}>\gamma_{c}
signals an instability of the FL towards the formation of non-uniform insulating CDW/SDW orderings with spatial oscillations with wave vector
2k_{a}
.
On the other hand, the singular behavior of the electron-hole (inter-pocket) component of the charge and spin susceptibilities for
\gamma_{eh}>\gamma_{c}
signals the formation of an exciton condensate
Jérome et al. 1967
;
Khveshchenko 2001
;
Herbut 2006
;
Drut and Lähde 2009
oscillating in real space with the wave vector
\bm{Q}_{eh}=2\bm{K}+\bm{q}_{eh}
, where
q_{eh}=Q_{eh}^{-}
.
As singularities in both intra- and inter-pocket charge and spin susceptibilities are of the same kind, the ground state is a mixture of
2k_{a}
CDW/SDW and
\bm{Q}_{eh}=2\bm{K}+\bm{q}_{eh}
excitonic orders.

In the fine-tuned case of same-size electron and hole pockets,
k_{e}=k_{h}\equiv k_{F}
, the electron-hole charge and spin susceptibilities
\chi_{C/S,eh}(\bm{r})\propto e^{2i\bm{K}\cdot\bm{r}}/r^{D-\gamma_{eh}}
develop the following power-law singularity (see App.
G
):

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0pt\chi_{C/S,eh}(q)=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{D}{2}-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{\gamma_{eh}}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{D-\gamma_{eh}}{2}\right)(v_{e}+v_{h})\lambda_{F}^{D-1}}\left|\frac{2}{qR_{0}}\right|^{\gamma_{eh}}\!,

(36)

where
\bm{Q}=2\bm{K}+\bm{q}
,
\lambda_{F}=2\pi/k_{F}
,
q\ll 1/R_{0}\ll k_{F}
.
In this case, the FL is destroyed for arbitrarily small coupling
\gamma_{eh}>0
and the ground state is an excitonic insulator
Jérome et al. 1967
;
Khveshchenko 2001
;
Herbut 2006
;
Drut and Lähde 2009
.
The power-law singularity in Eq. (
36
) at
q\to 0
is due to the instantaneous nature of the Coulomb interaction, see Eq. (
1
), which invalidates a standard mean-field approach
Jérome et al. 1967
;
Khveshchenko 2001
.

In case of attractive interactions,
\gamma_{ab}<0
, only pair susceptibilities are relevant, see Eq. (
29
),

\displaystyle\hskip-17.0pt\chi_{P,aa}(q)=\frac{\pi^{\frac{D}{2}-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{|\gamma_{aa}|}{2}\right)}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{D-|\gamma_{aa}|}{2}\right)v_{a}\lambda_{a}^{D-1}}\left|\frac{2}{qR_{0}}\right|^{|\gamma_{aa}|}\!,

(37)

\displaystyle\hskip-17.0pt\chi_{P,eh}(\delta Q)=\frac{\cos\left[\frac{\pi}{2}\delta\gamma_{eh}+\vartheta\,\mathrm{sgn}(\delta Q)\right]\Gamma\left(\delta\gamma_{eh}\right)}{\pi(v_{e}+v_{h})\left[R_{0}(\lambda_{e}+\lambda_{h})\right]^{\gamma_{c}}\left|\delta Q\,R_{0}\right|^{\delta\gamma_{eh}}},

(38)

where
\delta\gamma_{eh}=|\gamma_{eh}|-\gamma_{c}
.
In Eq. (
37
),
\bm{Q}=\pm 2\bm{K}+\bm{q}
and
q\ll 1/R_{0}\ll k_{a}
, where the plus (minus) sign corresponds to
a=e
(
a=h
).
In Eq. (
38
),
\delta Q=Q-Q_{eh}^{+}\ll 1/R_{0}\ll Q_{eh}^{+}
, where
Q_{eh}^{+}=k_{e}+k_{h}
.
As follows from Eq. (
37
), the FL is destroyed for arbitrarily small
\gamma_{aa}<0
, and the emerging ground state is a uniform singlet
(
s
-wave) superconductor.
In contrast, a singularity in
\chi_{P,eh}(\delta Q)
develops only for finite
|\gamma_{eh}|>\gamma_{c}
, and therefore, this singularity cannot compete with the much stronger power-law singularity in
\chi_{P,aa}(q)
.

In order to highlight the
Q=Q_{eh}^{+}
singularity of the electron-hole pair susceptibility, see Eq. (
38
), we consider a finite Zeeman spin splitting of the Fermi surfaces caused by a magnetic field
\bm{B}
.
In this case, the intra-pocket pair susceptibility acquires an oscillatory factor
\propto\cos(\Delta k_{a}r)
, where
\Delta k_{a}=g_{a}\mu_{B}B/v_{a}
is the spin splitting of the
a^{\mathrm{th}}
Fermi surface,
g_{a}
is the
g
-factor of the
a^{\mathrm{th}}
Fermi surface,
\mu_{B}
is the Bohr magneton.
A similar oscillatory factor emerges within the Fulde-Ferrel-Larkin-Ovchinnikov (FFLO) mechanism of BCS superconductivity
Fulde and Ferrell 1964
;
Larkin and Ovchinnikov 1965
.
However, in our case the standard mean-field approach
Fulde and Ferrell 1964
;
Larkin and Ovchinnikov 1965
is no longer valid, and the corresponding intra-pocket pair susceptibility develops a non-BCS power-law singularity,

\displaystyle\chi_{P,aa}(\delta Q)=\frac{\cos\left[\frac{\pi}{2}\delta\gamma_{aa}-\vartheta\,\mathrm{sgn}(\delta Q)\right]\Gamma\left(\delta\gamma_{aa}\right)}{2\pi v_{a}\left[2R_{0}\lambda_{a}\right]^{\gamma_{c}}\left|\delta Q\,R_{0}\right|^{\delta\gamma_{aa}}},

(39)

where
\delta\gamma_{aa}=|\gamma_{aa}|-\gamma_{c}
,
v_{a}=(v_{a}^{\uparrow}+v_{a}^{\downarrow})/2
is the average Fermi velocity of the spin-split pocket
a^{\mathrm{th}}
,
\lambda_{a}=(\lambda_{a}^{\uparrow}+\lambda_{a}^{\downarrow})/2
is the average Fermi wavelength,

\bm{Q}=\pm 2\bm{K}+\bm{q}
, and
\delta Q=q-\Delta k_{a}\ll\mathrm{min}\{1/R_{0},\Delta k_{a}\}
.
In this case, both intra-pocket and inter-pocket pair susceptibilities develop the same singularity for
|\gamma_{ab}|>\gamma_{c}
.
Therefore, the ground state is a mixture of the singlet superconducting order oscillating in real space
\propto e^{\pm 2i\bm{K}\cdot\bm{r}}\cos(\Delta k_{a}r)
with small wave vector
\Delta k_{a}=|k_{a}^{\uparrow}-k_{a}^{\downarrow}|
, and a non-uniform PDW order with a much larger wave vector
|\bm{Q}_{PDW}|=Q_{eh}^{+}
.
Here, we point out that the spin-singlet PDW order oscillates with wave vectors
k_{e}^{\uparrow}+k_{h}^{\downarrow}
,
k_{e}^{\downarrow}+k_{h}^{\uparrow}
, while the spin-triplet order oscillates with
k_{e}^{\uparrow}+k_{h}^{\uparrow}
,
k_{e}^{\downarrow}+k_{h}^{\downarrow}
wave vectors, which could be used to identify the fraction of spin-triplet superconducting condensate in real materials.
We stress that the PDW order predicted here requires the presence of both electron and hole Fermi surfaces.

Finally, we would like to point out that there exist two dualities that are interchangeable under
\gamma_{ab}\to-\gamma_{ab}
: (i) duality between the intra-pocket charge/spin susceptibilities and the electron-hole pair susceptibility; (ii) duality between the electron-hole charge/spin susceptibilities and the intra-pocket pair susceptibility.
However, we stress that these dualities are no longer exact if higher-order corrections to critical exponents are taken into account.

As our model is not material specific, the behavior uncovered here is universal for all
D
-dimensional metals,
D>1
, with a finite-range interaction.
We note that effects of strong repulsive interactions can be measured in the FL state for
\gamma_{ab}<\gamma_{c}
via local STM measurements of the charge and spin Friedel oscillations decaying via the anomalous power-law
\propto 1/r^{D-\gamma_{ab}}
.
PDW correlations in materials containing both electron and hole pockets can be measured via STM with a superconducting tip
Ruan et al. 2018
.

VI
Discussion and future directions

The findings presented here for FL instabilities in dimensions
D>1
constitute a solid theoretical ground for unraveling the mechanisms behind quantum phase transitions observed in a variety of quantum materials
Agterberg et al. 2020
;
Sato et al. 2001
;
Doiron-Leyraud et al. 2007
;
Helm et al. 2009
;
Jang et al. 2017
;
Li et al. 2019
;
Fournier et al. 1998
;
Hücker et al. 2014
;
Sato et al. 2017
;
Mokashi et al. 2012
;
Hossain et al. 2020
;
Melnikov et al. 2019
;
Shashkin and Kravchenko 2021
;
Cao et al. 2018a
;
Sharpe et al. 2019
;
Xie et al. 2019
;
Wong et al. 2020
;
Jiang et al. 2019
;
Lu et al. 2019
;
Yankowitz et al. 2019
;
Cao et al. 2018b
;
Oh et al. 2021
;
Park et al. 2021
;
Arora et al. 2020
;
Wang et al. 2020
;
Shen et al. 2020
;
Rickhaus et al. 2021
;
Su et al. 2023
.

In the single-pocket case, we identify singularities in the
2k_{F}
harmonics of charge and spin susceptibilities for a repulsive interaction at the critical coupling
\gamma=\gamma_{c}
(see Eq. (
31
) and Figs.
3
,
4
), signaling a phase transition from FL to a strongly correlated CDW/SDW insulator.
We believe that this mechanism of metal-to-insulator transition is relevant to experimental observations in ultraclean semiconductor quantum wells at very low electron densities
Mokashi et al. 2012
;
Hossain et al. 2020
;
Melnikov et al. 2019
;
Shashkin and Kravchenko 2021
.
If the interaction is attractive, a uniform spin-singlet pair susceptibility exhibits a non-BCS power-law singularity at
Q\sim 0
[see Eq. (
37
)], indicating a superconducting quantum phase transition.
This mechanism of superconductivity is beyond the standard BCS or Eliashberg treatment based on a mean-filed approach
Éliashberg 1960
;
Chubukov et al. 2020
.
If the spin degeneracy of the Fermi surface is removed by a finite Zeeman splitting, we find that the leading harmonic in the pair susceptibility is of FFLO type [see Eq. (
39
)] with a non-BCS singularity emerging at the critical coupling
|\gamma|=\gamma_{c}
.
Hence, our theory describes the
case of non-BCS superconductors which can be relevant for systems with attractive interactions mediated by fast bosons with typical frequency
\omega_{0}\gtrsim v_{F}/R_{0}
.
This may correspond to interactions with energetic optical phonons or with collective electron modes (plasmons and spinons)
Takada 1978
;
Sharma et al. 2020
;
Grankin and Galitski 2023
;
Abanov et al. 2003
.
We believe that this mechanism is also relevant to photon-mediated superconductivity in cavity-coupled electron systems
Schlawin et al. 2019
.

If both electron and hole pockets are present, additional instabilities within the electron-hole channel are possible.
In case of a repulsive interaction, we find that the electron-hole charge and spin susceptibilities develop a singularity at the critical coupling
\gamma_{eh}=\gamma_{c}
, see Eq. (
32
).
If the electron and the hole pockets are of the same size, the singularity develops at arbitrarily small coupling
\gamma_{eh}>0
, see Eq. (
36
).
This corresponds to a phase transition to an excitonic insulator observed in a variety of layered materials
Lu et al. 2017
;
Ma et al. 2021
;
Gu et al. 2022
;
Jia et al. 2022
.

The two-pocket model with attractive interaction provides a unique mechanism of PDW superconductivity which could be relevant to describe strong electron correlations in cuprate materials
Agterberg et al. 2020
;
Berg et al. 2009a
;
Berg et al. 2009b
;
Agterberg and Garaud 2015
;
Wang et al. 2018
.
We stress that both electron and hole pockets are required for the PDW superconductivity.
This could be the case for cuprate superconductors due to substantial experimental evidence that points towards a potential Lifshitz transition, resulting in the reconstruction of the Fermi surface into a few small electron and hole pockets near the optimal doping in cuprates
Doiron-Leyraud et al. 2007
;
Helm et al. 2009
;
Jang et al. 2017
;
Li et al. 2019
, which lends support to our model.
Attractive interactions in cuprate materials may originate from coupling to high-energy optical phonons.
The finite interaction range
R_{0}
in this case has the same electrostatic origin as the Thomas-Fermi screening.
We point out that the phonon energy scale drops out from our model for large phonon frequencies
\omega_{0}\gtrsim v_{F}/R_{0}
, which corresponds to the limit of instantaneous interaction, see Eq. (
1
).
This condition could potentially be satisfied for small electron and hole pockets in cuprate materials.
Therefore, we believe that our model could potentially provide a theoretical basis to describe high-temperature superconductivity in cuprate materials.

The superconducting order observed in MAG and double-bilayer graphene

Lu et al. 2019
;
Yankowitz et al. 2019
;
Cao et al. 2018b
;
Oh et al. 2021
;
Park et al. 2021
;
Arora et al. 2020
;
Su et al. 2023

is also associated with a Lifshitz transition resulting in reconstructed Fermi surfaces containing both electron and hole pockets
Arora et al. 2020
;
Laksono et al. 2018
;
Liu et al. 2018
.
This could potentially signal the PDW origin of superconductivity in these strongly correlated quantum materials as well.
It might be possible to change the interaction range
R_{0}
in these materials in a controlled way by gates, which affect the screening, and thereby test our predictions.

The mechanism of FL instabilities uncovered here is not material-specific but rather universal for interacting electron systems in dimensions
D>1
.
Therefore, our model and theory could also be relevant to experimental observations of quantum phase transitions in other materials
Wang et al. 2020
;
Shen et al. 2020
;
Rickhaus et al. 2021
;
Su et al. 2023
.

Further directions of study encompass
bosonization techniques to calculate higher-order corrections to the critical exponents that are required to evaluate the electron spectral function.
Quantum critical behavior of finite-temperature susceptibilities when the interaction coupling is greater than the critical value, points to strange-metal physics which is typical for quantum critical systems
Park et al. 2008
;
Knebel et al. 2008
;
Doiron-Leyraud et al. 2009
;
Legros et al. 2019
;
Cao et al. 2020
.
A detailed theoretical description of these effects is currently under way and left for a subsequent study.
Mechanisms stabilizing long-range orders at finite temperatures will also be considered elsewhere.

VII
Conclusions

We proposed and analyzed a realistic microscopic model universally describing superconducting and insulating instabilities of interacting
D
-dimensional Fermi liquids observed in a large variety of quantum materials, from ultraclean semiconductor quantum wells at very low electron densities to high-temperature superconductors.
In case of a single Fermi surface, we predict CDW/SDW instabilities at the critical value of repulsive interaction coupling and non-BCS singlet superconductivity for an attractive interaction.
In case if both electron and hole pockets are present, we find an instability towards excitonic insulators if the interaction is repulsive, while towards
k_{e}+k_{h}
PDW superconductivity if the interaction is attractive with a coupling strength that is greater than a critical universal value.
Stabilization mechanisms of long-range orders at finite temperature, as well as their melting and possible formation of the strange-metal state at high temperatures is deferred to subsequent studies.

In the Appendices, we provide technical details on the first- and second-order susceptibility diagram calculations, renormalization group construction,

-dimensional Fourier transforms, and bosonization.
As the calculations for all susceptibility diagrams are quite similar, we provide here the details for the inter-pocket pair susceptibility only.
In order to calculate the Feynman diagrams, we employ the dimensional reduction technique
Miserev et al. 2023
, which greatly simplifies the evaluations since
D
-dimensional integrals are reduced to effective 1D integrations (with some exceptions, see below).
Dimension-reduced representations for the susceptibility diagrams can be derived in full analogy with the general derivations presented in Ref.
Miserev et al. 2023
.
For example, the long-distance asymptotics of the static inter-pocket pair susceptibility takes the following form, [see also Eq. (
26
)]

\displaystyle\hskip-12.0pt\chi_{P,eh}(\bm{r})=\sum\limits_{\nu_{e},\nu_{h}}\frac{e^{ir(\nu_{e}k_{e}+\nu_{h}k_{h})-i\vartheta(\nu_{e}+\nu_{h})}}{(\lambda_{e}\lambda_{h})^{\frac{D-1}{2}}r^{D-1}}\chi_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}(r),

(40)

where
\nu_{e},\nu_{h}\in\{\pm 1\}
are the chiral indices,
\vartheta=\pi(D-1)/4
is the semiclassical phase, the function
\chi_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}(r)
is represented by the dimension-reduced susceptibility diagrams, the index
(1D)
introduced in Eq. (
26
) is suppressed here for brevity.
In Eq. (
26
), it is already taken into account that only the
\nu_{e}=\nu_{h}
components exhibit non-analytic behavior.
We show this directly in Apps.
A
,
B
.
We emphasize that polarization effects must be treated within the original
D
dimensions
Miserev et al. 2023
.
This is important for the dynamic screening contribution in the second-order diagrams considered below.
More complicated multiloop diagrams do not appear within the first two lowest orders
Miserev et al. 2023
, except the second-order Aslamazov-Larkin diagrams for charge and spin susceptibilities, see Fig.
6
B, which, however, vanish in electron gas with forward-scattering density-density interaction.

The first-order diagram contributing to the leading logarithmic order corresponds to the vertex correction, see Fig.
2
A.
The required calculations are similar to the ones shown in Ref.
Hutchinson et al. 2024
, so we only provide a few key steps here.
The leading logarithmic first-order correction to

\chi_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}(r)
is represented by the 1D analog of the vertex-correction diagram in Fig.
2
A,

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0pt\chi^{(1)}_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}(r)=-\int d\tau\int d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}\,U(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi-\xi_{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 23.0pt\times g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi_{1})g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi-\xi_{2})g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi_{2}),

(41)

where
\xi=(\tau,r)
and
\xi_{i}=(\tau_{i},x_{i})
. Here,
\tau_{i}
is the imaginary time,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
is the 1D spatial coordinate,
d\xi_{i}=d\tau_{i}dx_{i}
,
U(\xi)=U(\tau,|x|)=\delta(\tau)V(|x|)
is the electron-electron interaction, and
g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi)
and
g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi)
are the 1D electron and hole Green’s function, respectively [see Eq. (
4
)]. As we are interested in the asymptotics at large distances,
r\gg R_{0}\gg\lambda_{e},\lambda_{h}
, the interaction
U(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})
is effectively local on this scale (while still being non-local on the scale of the inter-particle distance) and we can approximate
\xi_{2}\approx\xi_{1}
in Eq. (
41
),

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0pt\chi_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}^{(1)}(r)=-2V_{2}\int d\tau\int d\xi_{1}\,

\displaystyle\hskip 23.0pt\times g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi-\xi_{1})g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi_{1})g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi-\xi_{1})g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi_{1}),

(42)

where
V_{2}
is given by Eq. (
14
).
We point out that the dimensional reduction is suitable for all interactions with the range
R_{0}\gg\lambda_{e},\lambda_{h}
.
This procedure can not be applied to zero-range contact interactions.
Integration over
\tau
immediately shows that only terms with
\nu_{e}=\nu_{h}
contribute.
Otherwise, the poles of
g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi-\xi_{1})
and
g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi-\xi_{1})
for
\nu_{e}\neq\nu_{h}
are located on the same complex half-plane and the integral over
\tau
vanishes.
After performing elementary contour integrations over
\tau
and
\tau_{1}
, we find,

\displaystyle\chi_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}^{(1)}(r)=-\frac{\gamma_{eh}\delta_{\nu_{e},\nu_{h}}}{8\pi v_{+}}\int\frac{dx_{1}}{|x_{1}||x_{1}-r|},

(43)

where
v_{+}=v_{e}+v_{h}
,
\gamma_{eh}
is the dimensionless coupling constant [see Eq. (
13
)], and
\delta_{\nu_{e},\nu_{h}}
is the Kronecker symbol.
The integral over
x_{1}
diverges at short distances
|x_{1}|\lesssim R_{0}
,
|x_{1}-r|\lesssim R_{0}
because we neglected details of the interaction on short scales
\sim R_{0}
.
This ultraviolet divergence is therefore cured by restricting the integration to the interval
x_{1}\in(-\infty,-R_{0})\cup(R_{0},r-R_{0})\cup(r+R_{0},+\infty)
, where
r\gg R_{0}
,

\displaystyle\int\frac{dx_{1}}{|x_{1}||x_{1}-r|}\approx\frac{4}{r}\ln\left|\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right|.

(44)

Substituting this result back into Eq. (
41
), we find,

\displaystyle\chi_{P,eh}^{(1)}(\bm{r})=-\chi_{P,eh}^{(0)}(r)\gamma_{eh}\ln\left|\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right|.

(45)

Again, this asymptotics is valid at
r\gg R_{0}\gg\lambda_{e},\lambda_{h}
.

The first-order corrections to the intra-pocket charge and spin susceptibilities calculated in Ref.
Hutchinson et al. 2024
are dual to the corresponding corrections to the inter-pocket pair susceptibility upon the transformation
v_{h}\to v_{e}
,
\nu_{h}\to-\nu_{e}
and
\tau\to-\tau
in the hole Green’s function.
The same transformation reveals the duality between the inter-pocket charge and spin susceptibilities and the intra-pocket pair susceptibility.

Appendix B
Box and cross-box diagrams

The leading second-order diagrams, see Fig.
2
B,C, correspond to insertion of the box and cross-box scattering amplitude diagrams into the inter-pocket pair susceptibility.
The same insertions into the charge and spin susceptibilities also produce leading logarithmic second-order diagrams that are calculated similarly to the pair susceptibility box and cross-box diagrams shown in Fig.
2
B,C.
Corresponding contributions to
\chi_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}(r)
, see Eq. (
40
), are represented by 1D diagrams.
Applying the locality condition for large-distance correlations at
r\gg R_{0}
, we find,

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\chi_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}^{(b)}(r)=4V_{2}^{2}\int d\tau\int d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}\,g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi-\xi_{1})g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.0pt\times g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi_{2})g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi-\xi_{1})g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi_{2}),

(46)

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\chi_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}^{(cb)}(r)=4V_{2}^{2}\int d\tau\int d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}\,g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi-\xi_{1})g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.0pt\times g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi_{2})g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi-\xi_{2})g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi_{1}),

(47)

where, again,
\xi=(\tau,r)
,
\xi_{i}=(\tau_{i},x_{i})
, and the superscripts
(b)
and
(cb)
stand for the box and cross-box diagram, respectively.

The box diagram can be integrated in a fashion that is similar to the first-order vertex correction.
First, we integrate over
\tau
and find that terms only with
\nu_{e}=\nu_{h}
contribute.
After integrating over
\tau
,
\tau_{1}
, and
\tau_{2}
, which are simple contour integrals, we find,

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\chi_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}^{(b)}(r)=\frac{\gamma_{eh}^{2}\delta_{\nu_{e},\nu_{h}}}{32\pi v_{+}}\int\frac{dx_{1}dx_{2}}{|x_{2}||x_{2}-x_{1}||x_{1}-r|},

(48)

where, again, we have to cut out the interval
(-R_{0},R_{0})
near each short-distance singularity in Eq. (
48
).
The integral over
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
can be calculated via a partial fraction expansion of the integrand, yielding

\displaystyle\int\frac{dx_{1}dx_{2}}{|x_{2}||x_{2}-x_{1}||x_{1}-r|}=\frac{12}{r}\ln^{2}\left|\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right|,

(49)

where
r\gg R_{0}
, and we keep only the
\log^{2}
contribution.
Therefore, the box diagram gives the following contribution,

\displaystyle\chi_{P,eh}^{(b)}(\bm{r})=\frac{3}{4}\gamma_{eh}^{2}\ln^{2}\left|\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right|\chi_{P,eh}^{(0)}(r),

(50)

where
r\gg R_{0}
.

The cross-box diagram in Fig.
2
C is more complicated, see Eq. (
47
).
In order to integrate over
\xi_{2}
, we separate products of 1D Green’s functions into sums, for example,

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0ptg_{\nu_{e}}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi_{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 45.0pt=g_{\nu_{e}}\left(\xi_{1}\right)\left[g_{\nu_{e}}\left(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\right)+g_{\nu_{e}}\left(\xi_{2}\right)\right],

(51)

and similar for hole Green’s functions.
Then, the integral over
\xi_{2}
is especially straightforward,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\chi_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}^{(cb)}(r)=-2V_{2}\int d\tau\int d\xi_{1}\,g_{\nu_{e}}\left(\xi-\xi_{1}\right)g_{\nu_{e}}\left(\xi_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 38.0pt\times g_{\nu_{h}}\left(\xi-\xi_{1}\right)g_{\nu_{h}}\left(\xi_{1}\right)\mathcal{I}_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}(\xi_{1},\xi),

(52)

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{I}_{+}(\xi_{1},\xi)=\frac{\gamma_{eh}}{4}\left\{\ln\left[\left(\zeta_{h}-\zeta_{1h}\right)\left(\zeta_{e}-\zeta_{1e}\right)\zeta_{1e}\zeta_{1h}\frac{v_{e}v_{h}}{v_{+}^{2}R_{0}^{4}}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 38.0pt\left.-\ln\left[\frac{\zeta_{e}\zeta_{h}}{R_{0}^{2}}\right]\right\},

(53)

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\mathcal{I}_{-}(\xi_{1},\xi)=\frac{V_{2}}{\pi(v_{e}-v_{h})}\ln\left[\frac{v_{h}\zeta_{h}}{v_{e}\zeta_{e}}\frac{\left(\zeta_{e}-\zeta_{1e}\right)\zeta_{1e}}{\left(\zeta_{h}-\zeta_{1h}\right)\zeta_{1h}}\right],

(54)

where we introduced the following complex variables,

\displaystyle\zeta_{e}=x+i\nu_{e}v_{e}\tau,\hskip 5.0pt\zeta_{h}=x-i\nu_{h}v_{h}\tau,

(55)

\displaystyle\zeta_{1e}=x_{1}+i\nu_{e}v_{e}\tau_{1},\hskip 5.0pt\zeta_{1h}=x_{1}-i\nu_{h}v_{h}\tau_{1}.

(56)

Here,
\mathcal{I}_{+}(\xi_{1},\xi)
[
\mathcal{I}_{-}(\xi_{1},\xi)
] corresponds to
\mathcal{I}_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}(\xi_{1},\xi)
at
\nu_{e}=\nu_{h}
(
\nu_{e}=-\nu_{h}
).
Note that Eq. (
52
) has the same structure as the first-order vertex correction, see Eq. (
42
), but being multiplied by the factor
\mathcal{I}_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}(\xi_{1},\xi)
.
As here we are interested in the leading logarithmic order, terms of order unity are not shown in
\mathcal{I}_{\nu_{e},\nu_{h}}(\xi_{1},\xi)

In case of
\nu_{e}=-\nu_{h}

\mathcal{I}_{-}(\xi_{1},\xi)

This is best seen if
\mathcal{I}_{-}(\xi_{1},\xi)
is expanded in a sum of logarithms and then the integrations are performed for each logarithmic term separately.

gives zero after the shift
\xi_{1}\to\xi-\xi_{1}
after which the logarithm transforms into
\ln(\zeta_{1e})
which is independent of
\tau

The integral over
\tau

\nu_{e}=-\nu_{h}

The same is true for the other logarithmic terms in Eq. (

In case of
\nu_{e}=\nu_{h}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\chi_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}^{(cb)}(r)=\delta_{\nu_{e},\nu_{h}}\left(\chi_{1}^{(cb)}(r)+\chi_{2}^{(cb)}(r)\right),

(57)

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\chi_{1}^{(cb)}(r)=\frac{-\gamma_{eh}^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}\!\int\!\frac{d\tau_{1}dx_{1}}{\zeta_{1e}\zeta_{1h}}\ln\left(\!\frac{v_{e}v_{h}\zeta_{1e}\zeta_{1h}}{v_{+}^{2}R_{0}^{2}}\!\right)\!\frac{1}{|x_{1}-r|},

(58)

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\chi_{2}^{(cb)}(r)=\frac{\gamma_{eh}^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}\int\frac{d\tau}{\zeta_{e}\zeta_{h}}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{\zeta_{e}\zeta_{h}}{R_{0}^{2}}\right),

(59)

where
v_{+}=v_{e}+v_{h}
.
Here, we separated parts that contain logarithms depending on
\zeta_{1e}
and
\zeta_{1h}
[first term in Eq. (
53

\zeta_{e}
and
\zeta_{h}
[second term in Eq. (
53
)].
As the logarithms in
\chi_{1}^{(cb)}(r)
are independent of
\tau

\tau
was also performed in Eq. (
58

As the logarithm-terms in
\chi^{(cb)}_{2}(r)
are independent of
\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\int\frac{d\xi_{1}}{\zeta_{1e}\zeta_{1h}(\zeta_{1e}-\zeta_{e})(\zeta_{1h}-\zeta_{h})}=\frac{4\pi\ln\left(\zeta_{e}\zeta_{h}/R_{0}^{2}\right)}{\zeta_{e}\zeta_{h}v_{+}}.

\tau_{1}

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\tau_{1}}{2\pi}\frac{\ln(\zeta_{1e}\zeta_{1h})}{\zeta_{1e}\zeta_{1h}}=\frac{1}{|x_{1}|v_{+}}\ln\left(\frac{x_{1}^{2}v_{+}^{2}}{v_{e}v_{h}}\right).

The remaining integral over

after which we find the simple relation

\displaystyle\chi_{1}^{(cb)}(r)=-\chi^{(b)}(r),

(62)

\chi^{(b)}(r)

.
Therefore, the remaining contribution

\tau

59

\displaystyle\chi_{2}^{(cb)}(r)=\frac{\gamma_{eh}^{2}}{4\pi v_{+}r}\ln^{2}\left|\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right|.

.

\xi_{2}

\displaystyle\chi^{(v)}_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}(r)=-\frac{\gamma_{eh}^{2}\delta_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}}{8\pi v_{+}r}\ln\left(\frac{rv_{+}}{\sqrt{v_{e}v_{h}}}\right).

,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\chi_{+}^{(scr)}(r)=-\sum\limits_{a,\nu_{a}}\!\int\!\frac{V_{0}^{2}\,d\tau}{\pi^{2}\lambda_{a}^{D-1}}g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi)g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi)\!\int\!\frac{d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}}{|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{D-1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 39.0pt\times\frac{g_{\nu_{e}}(\xi_{1})}{(\zeta_{1a}-\zeta_{2a})^{2}}\left[g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi_{2})+g_{\nu_{h}}(\xi-\xi_{2})\right],

\tau

\tau_{1}

\tau_{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-17.0pt\chi_{+}^{(scr)}(r)=-\frac{V_{0}^{2}}{2\pi^{3}v_{e}v_{h}v_{+}r}\sum\limits_{a}\frac{1}{v_{a}^{2}\lambda_{a}^{D-1}}\!\int\limits_{0}^{r}\!\!\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\frac{dx_{1}dx_{2}}{|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{D-1}}\!\left\{\!\left[\frac{x_{1}}{v_{e}}+\frac{x_{2}}{v_{h}}+\frac{|x_{1}-x_{2}|}{v_{a}}\right]^{-2}\!\!\!+\left[\frac{x_{1}}{v_{h}}+\frac{x_{2}}{v_{e}}+\frac{|x_{1}-x_{2}|}{v_{a}}\right]^{-2}\!\right\}.

(77)

It does not matter which integration interval corresponds to which variable, due to the symmetry between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
.
The factor
1/|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{D-1}
in Eq. (
77
) is responsible for a dramatic suppression of this diagram in
D>1
, which scales
\propto 1/r^{D}
.
In contrast, all other diagrams for
\chi_{\nu_{e}\nu_{h}}(r)
which we calculated before scale as
1/r
modulo powers of logarithms.
The
\propto 1/r^{D}
scaling of
\chi_{+}^{(scr)}(r)
is evident after rescaling
x_{1}\to ru_{1}
,
x_{2}\to ru_{2}
, the remaining integral over
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
is convergent after appropriate regularization of the kernel
1/|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{D-1}
.
The ultraviolet divergence near
x_{1}=x_{2}
originates from the semiclassical asymptotics of the Green’s function [see Eqs. (
2
), (
3
)] and cannot be applied if
|x_{1}-x_{2}|\lesssim\lambda_{e},\lambda_{h}
.
The diagram in Fig.
7
B is irrelevant in
D>1
for the same reason.
We note that the diagram in Fig.
7
B is subleading even if

(not considered here) and does not affect susceptibilities within one-loop RG
Menyhard and Sólyom 1973
;
Sólyom 1973
.

Appendix E
Renormalization group

In this section we derive the RG flow equation for the coupling constant following a multiplicative RG procedure.
We begin with the observation that the susceptibilities take the following form,

\displaystyle\chi(r)=\chi^{(0)}(r)F\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}},\gamma_{0}\right),

(78)

where, again,
\chi^{(0)}(r)
is the free susceptibility,
R_{0}
the finite range of the interaction, and
\gamma_{0}
the interaction coupling.
However, from a theoretical point of view, we could choose a different value of the cut-off, say
R_{0}^{\prime}
, yet we expect the form-factor
F
to retain the same dependence on
r
, just with a new value of the cut-off
R^{\prime}_{0}
and, possibly, new value of the coupling constant.
This observation corresponds to the following scaling symmetry of the form-factor
Menyhard and Sólyom 1973
;
Sólyom 1973
:

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}},\gamma(R_{0})\right)=ZF\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}^{\prime}},\gamma\left(R_{0}^{\prime}\right)\right),

(79)

where
\gamma(R_{0}^{\prime})
is the coupling associated with the new cut-off
R_{0}^{\prime}
and
\gamma(R_{0})=\gamma_{0}
is our original (bare) coupling.
The multiplicative factor
Z
does not depend on
r
.
We can get rid of this factor by taking the logarithm of Eq. (
79
)
and differentiate it by
r
,

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0ptr\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\ln F\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}},\gamma\left(R_{0}\right)\right)=r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\ln F\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}^{\prime}},\gamma\left(R_{0}^{\prime}\right)\right).

(80)

Note that
R_{0}
and
R_{0}^{\prime}
are independent parameters.
In particular, we can choose
R_{0}^{\prime}\to r
which establishes the flow equation for the form-factor,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\ln F\left(\rho,\gamma\left(R_{0}\right)\right)}{\partial\ln\rho}=\lim\limits_{y\to 1}\frac{\partial\ln F\left(y,\gamma(r)\right)}{\partial\ln y},

(81)

where we introduced the notations
y=r/R_{0}^{\prime}
,
\rho=r/R_{0}
is the dimensionless coordinate, and the limit
R_{0}^{\prime}=r
is taken.
Note that the right-hand side of Eq. (
81
) just gives us the first coefficient in the Taylor series expansion of
\ln F(y,\gamma)
with respect to
\ln y
.
This coefficient can be calculated within the perturbation theory that provides us with this Taylor series expansion.
For example, for the pair susceptibility we find,

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0pt\frac{\chi_{P,eh}(r)}{\chi_{P,eh}^{(0)}(r)}\approx 1-\gamma_{eh}\ln\left|\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right|+\frac{\gamma_{eh}^{2}}{2}\ln^{2}\left|\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right|+\dots

(82)

In particular, this means that to the linear order in
\ln y
the logarithmic form-factor in our case has the following expansion,

\displaystyle\ln F(y,\gamma)=1+\gamma\ln y+\frac{\beta(\gamma)}{2}\ln^{2}y+\dots,

(83)

where
\beta(\gamma)
is the RG
\beta
-function that we discuss in a moment.
Therefore, it is convenient to introduce the coupling constant such that it straight away corresponds to the coefficient of the
\ln y
term in
\ln F(y,\gamma)
, i.e. the flow equation for the form-factor in our case takes the form,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\ln F\left(\rho,\gamma\left(R_{0}\right)\right)}{\partial\ell}=\gamma(r),

(84)

where
\rho=r/R_{0}
,
\ell=\ln\rho
.
We point out that for the inter-pocket pair susceptibility the coupling constant
\gamma=-\gamma_{eh}
.
The formal dependence of the coupling constant on the coordinate
r
in Eq. (
84
) is due to the limit
R_{0}^{\prime}\to r
.
The coupling constant is a function of the cut-off (not of the coordinate
r
).

In order to obtain the flow equation for the coupling constant, we just act on both sides of Eq. (
79
) by the operator
\partial^{2}/\partial\ell^{2}
, and use the same limit
R_{0}^{\prime}=r
to obtain

\displaystyle\hskip-17.0pt\frac{\partial^{2}\ln F\left(\rho,\gamma\left(R_{0}\right)\right)}{\partial\ell^{2}}=\lim\limits_{y\to 1}\frac{\partial^{2}\ln F(y,\gamma(r))}{\partial(\ln y)^{2}}=\beta(\gamma(r)).

(85)

Using the definition of our coupling constant, we find the flow equation,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\gamma(R_{0}^{\prime})}{\partial\ell}=\beta(\gamma),

(86)

where
\ell=\ln(R_{0}^{\prime}/R_{0})
.
Here, we emphasize again that the coupling constant is a function of the cut-off
R_{0}^{\prime}
and not of the coordinate
r
.

The Taylor expansion of the pair susceptibility form-factor given by Eq. (
82
) yields us a vanishing
\beta
-function,
\beta(\gamma)=0
, in leading order.
This means that the coupling does not flow, i.e.
\gamma(R_{0}^{\prime})=\gamma(R_{0})
.
The only reason why we get
\beta(\gamma)=0
is because we were only taking into account terms
\propto\gamma^{2}\ell^{2}
.
However,
\ell^{2}
terms are also produced in higher orders of the perturbation expansion, for example, in third-order diagrams we can have
\gamma^{3}\ell^{2}
contributions.
This means that here we have proven the following relation:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\gamma(R_{0}^{\prime})}{\partial\ell}=\mathcal{O}\left(\gamma^{3}\right).

(87)

In order to make a conclusive statement on how the coupling constant runs, a two-loop RG is required, i.e. one has to consider all third-order diagrams with accuracy
\gamma^{3}\ell^{2}
which is generally challenging (and not considered here).

Here we present the leading-order one-loop RG behavior.
In this case we find that the coupling constant does not flow which immediately solves the flow equation for the susceptibility form-factor,

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}},\gamma\right)=\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right)^{\!\gamma}.

(88)

This establishes one of the main results of this work [see Eq. (
18
)] allowing us to draw significant conclusions on the instabilities in an interacting electron gas in
D
dimensions.

We also would like to demonstrate the importance of the cross-box diagram shown in Fig.
2
C in the present context.
For this, we consider the susceptibility form-factor in absence of this cross-box diagram and obtain,

\displaystyle F_{Cooper}(\rho,\gamma)=1+\gamma\ln\rho+\frac{3}{4}\gamma^{2}\ln^{2}\rho+\dots,

(89)

where the subscript “Cooper” indicates that only the Cooper ladder diagrams, see Fig.
2
B, are retained in the susceptibility via the RG procedure.
This corresponds to
\beta(\gamma)=\gamma^{2}/2
with the Cooper-pole singularity of the running coupling constant,

\displaystyle\gamma_{Cooper}(R_{0}^{\prime})=\frac{\gamma_{0}}{1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}\ell}\,,

(90)

where
\ell=\ln(R_{0}^{\prime}/R_{0})
.
In turn, this results in the double-pole structure of the susceptibility form-factor,

\displaystyle F_{Cooper}(\rho,\gamma)=\frac{1}{\left[1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}\ell\right]^{2}}\,,

(91)

where
\ell=\ln\rho
,
\rho=r/R_{0}
.
Here,
\gamma_{0}=-\gamma_{ab}
for the pair susceptibility and
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{ab}
for charge and spin susceptibilities.
Presence of the pole in the susceptibility form-factor provides a finite energy scale corresponding to a finite gap at any
\gamma_{0}>0
.
The cross-box diagram completely removes the pole and yields the power-law singularity that corresponds to quantum critical behavior discussed in the main text.

Appendix F
Bosonization

In this Appendix we show that a standard bosonization calculation in strict 1D gives the same result for the scaling of the susceptibilities in leading order as the RG approach. In this calculation we neglect the dynamic part of the effective 1D bare interaction [see Eq. (
22
)] since it plays no role for the scaling in leading order, as was shown in App.
D
.

The effective 1D susceptibilites introduced in Eqs. (
25
-
28
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{C/S,ab}(x)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=\int d\tau\langle T(\psi_{+a\uparrow}\psi_{-b\uparrow}^{\dagger})(\xi)(\psi_{+a\uparrow}\psi_{-b\uparrow}^{\dagger})^{\dagger}(0)\rangle,

(92)

\displaystyle\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{P,ab}(x)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=\int d\tau\langle T(\psi_{+a\uparrow}\psi_{-b\uparrow})(\xi)(\psi_{+a\uparrow}\psi_{-b\uparrow})^{\dagger}(0)\rangle\,,

(93)

where
\xi=(\tau,x)
,
T
is the time-ordering operator, and, as before,
a=b\in\{e,h\}
(
a=e
,
b=h
) correspond to the intra-pocket (inter-pocket) case.
The fermion field operator
\psi_{\nu a\sigma}(x)
corresponds to the slowly varying part of the right (
\nu=+
) and left (
\nu=-
) movers with band index
a\in\{e,h\}
and spin index
\sigma\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}
.
Due to the spin degeneracy, we choose all fermion fields with spin
\sigma=\uparrow

We also use the convention that the chirality index of holes is opposite to the right/left moving index, see Eq. (
4
).

Using conventional bosonization
von Delft and Schoeller 1998
;
Giamarchi 2003
, we express slowly varying right/left moving field operators in the following form (the Klein factors are discarded for simplicity)

\displaystyle\psi_{\nu a\sigma}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi R_{0}}}\,e^{i\nu\sqrt{4\pi}\varphi_{\nu a\sigma}(x)}\,,

(94)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\nu a\sigma}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{q\neq 0}\left(\frac{1}{2L|q|}\right)^{1/2}\theta(\nu q)

\displaystyle\times\left(e^{iqx}a_{q,a\sigma}+e^{-iqx}a_{q,a\sigma}^{\dagger}\right)e^{-R_{0}|q|/2}\,.

(95)

Here,
R_{0}
is the short distance cutoff given by the range of the forward scattering,
L
is the length of the system,
q=2\pi n/L
are the discrete momenta for periodic boundary conditions, and
a_{q,a\sigma}
,
a_{q,a\sigma}^{\dagger}
denote bosonic operators.

The bosonized Hamiltonian with forward scattering only reads

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0ptH=\sum\limits_{\nu a\sigma}v_{a}\int dx\,\left(\partial_{x}\varphi_{\nu a\sigma}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{V_{2}}{\pi}\int dx\,\left(\sum_{\nu a\sigma}\partial_{x}\varphi_{\nu a\sigma}\right)^{2}\,.

(96)

Introducing canonically conjugate fields
\varphi_{a\sigma}=\varphi_{+a\sigma}+\varphi_{-a\sigma}
and
\Pi_{a\sigma}=-\partial_{x}\vartheta_{a\sigma}
, where
\vartheta_{a\sigma}=\varphi_{+a\sigma}-\varphi_{-a\sigma}
, together with the charge and spin fields
\varphi^{C}_{a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{\sigma}\varphi_{a\sigma}
,
\varphi^{S}_{a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{\sigma}\sigma\varphi_{a\sigma}
(and analogously for
\vartheta^{C/S}_{a}
and
\Pi^{C/S}_{a}
), we find the spin-charge separated form for
H
:

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0ptH=H_{C}+H_{S},

(97)

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0ptH_{C}=\sum\limits_{a}\frac{v_{a}}{2}\int dx\,\left[\left(\partial_{x}\varphi_{a}^{C}\right)^{2}+\left(\Pi_{a}^{C}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0pt+\frac{2V_{2}}{\pi}\int dx\,\left(\sum\limits_{a}\partial_{x}\varphi_{a}^{C}\right)^{2}\,,

(98)

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0ptH_{S}=\sum\limits_{a}\frac{v_{a}}{2}\int dx\,\left[\left(\partial_{x}\varphi_{a}^{S}\right)^{2}+\left(\Pi_{a}^{S}\right)^{2}\right]\,.

(99)

The spin part is independent of the interaction and contributes only to the free part of the susceptibilities. Therefore, we consider in the following only the charge part.

After a straightforward diagonalization, we can write the charge part in the following form of free bosons with linear dispersion relation,

\displaystyle H_{C}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{a}\frac{\tilde{v}_{a}}{2}\int dx\,\left[\left(\partial_{x}\tilde{\varphi}_{a}\right)^{2}+\left(\tilde{\Pi}_{a}\right)^{2}\right]\,,

(100)

where
\tilde{v}_{a}=\bar{v}/g_{a}
are renormalized velocities and the new canonically conjugate fields
\tilde{\varphi}_{a}
and
\tilde{\Pi}_{a}=-\partial_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}_{a}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{a}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{b}\frac{1}{\sqrt{g_{a}}}\,U_{ab}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{b}}}\,\varphi^{C}_{b}\,,

(101)

\displaystyle\tilde{\vartheta}_{a}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{b}\sqrt{g_{a}}\,U_{ab}\,\sqrt{\alpha_{b}}\,\vartheta^{C}_{b}\,.

(102)

Here, the following notations are introduced,

\displaystyle\alpha_{a}=\frac{v_{a}}{\bar{v}},\hskip 5.0pt\bar{v}=\frac{1}{2}\left(v_{e}+v_{h}\right)\,,

(103)

\displaystyle g_{a}=\left(\alpha^{2}+2\gamma_{eh}+a\lambda\right)^{-1/2}\,,

(104)

\displaystyle\lambda=\sqrt{\delta\alpha^{2}\left(1+\gamma_{eh}\right)+4\alpha_{+}\alpha_{-}\gamma_{eh}^{2}}\,,

(105)

where
\alpha^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{+}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2})
,
\delta\alpha=\alpha_{+}-\alpha_{-}
. The unitary matrix
U
diagonalizes the matrix
A
defined as follows,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}+2\gamma_{eh})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)+

\displaystyle+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\delta\alpha(1+\gamma_{eh})&2\gamma_{eh}\sqrt{\alpha_{+}\alpha_{-}}\\
2\gamma_{eh}\sqrt{\alpha_{+}\alpha_{-}}&-\delta\alpha(1+\gamma_{eh})\end{array}\right)\,,

such that

\displaystyle UAU^{\dagger}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda_{+}&0\\
0&\lambda_{-}\end{array}\right)\,,

with
\lambda_{\pm}=\alpha^{2}+2\gamma_{eh}\pm\lambda
.

The susceptibilities then follow straightforwardly from the diagonalized bosonic operators.
As we are only interested in the critical exponents within linear order in the coupling constant, we can express the charge bosonic fields as follows:

\displaystyle\varphi^{C}_{a}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{\gamma_{aa}}{2}\right)\tilde{\varphi}_{a}+a\gamma_{eh}\frac{\alpha_{a}}{\delta\alpha}\tilde{\varphi}_{\bar{a}}\,,

(112)

\displaystyle\vartheta^{C}_{a}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{aa}}{2}\right)\tilde{\vartheta}_{a}+a\gamma_{eh}\frac{\alpha_{\bar{a}}}{\delta\alpha}\tilde{\vartheta}_{\bar{a}}\,.

(113)

Furthermore, in leading order we can use
\tilde{v}_{a}\approx v_{a}
since these corrections only influence prefactors but not the exponents.

The intra-pocket charge/spin susceptibility is then given by the following vacuum averages,

\displaystyle\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{C/S,aa}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi R_{0})^{2}}\int d\tau\langle Te^{i\sqrt{2\pi}\varphi^{C}_{a}(\xi)}e^{-i\sqrt{2\pi}\varphi^{C}_{a}(0)}\rangle

\displaystyle\times\langle Te^{i\sqrt{2\pi}\varphi^{S}_{a}(\xi)}e^{-i\sqrt{2\pi}\varphi^{S}_{a}(0)}\rangle\,.

(114)

The intra-pocket pair susceptibility is given by a similar equation with
\varphi^{C/S}_{a}\rightarrow\vartheta^{C/S}_{a}
.
Using Eqs. (
112
), (
113
), we find the intra-pocket susceptibility scaling in leading order

\displaystyle\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{C/S,aa}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi R_{0})^{2}}\int d\tau\left(\frac{R_{0}}{|x+iv_{a}\tau|}\right)^{2-\gamma_{aa}}\,,

(115)

\displaystyle\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{P,aa}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi R_{0})^{2}}\int d\tau\left(\frac{R_{0}}{|x+iv_{a}\tau|}\right)^{2+\gamma_{aa}}\,.

(116)

After performing the integral over
\tau
, we then find the static susceptibilities,

\displaystyle\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{C/S,aa}(x)

\displaystyle=\chi_{aa}^{(0)}(x)\left|\frac{x}{R_{0}}\right|^{\gamma_{aa}}\,,

(117)

\displaystyle\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{P,aa}(x)

\displaystyle=\chi_{aa}^{(0)}(x)\left|\frac{x}{R_{0}}\right|^{-\gamma_{aa}}\,,

(118)

where
\chi_{aa}^{(0)}(x)=1/(4\pi v_{a}|x|)
is the noninteracting susceptibility and the cut-off
R_{0}
is adjusted such that dressed and free susceptibilities are equal at

The inter-pocket charge and spin susceptibilities are calculated in a similar fashion,

\displaystyle\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{C/S,eh}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi R_{0})^{2}}\int d\tau\langle Te^{i\sqrt{\pi/2}\,\Phi^{C}_{+}(\xi)}e^{-i\sqrt{\pi/2}\,\Phi^{C}_{+}(0)}\rangle

\displaystyle\times\langle Te^{i\sqrt{\pi/2}\,\Phi^{S}_{+}(\xi)}e^{-i\sqrt{\pi/2}\,\Phi^{S}_{+}(0)}\rangle\,.

(119)

The inter-pocket pair susceptibility corresponds to
\Phi^{C/S}_{+}\rightarrow\Phi^{C/S}_{-}
, where

\displaystyle\Phi^{C/S}_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{a}\left(\varphi^{C/S}_{a}+a\vartheta^{C/S}_{a}\right)\,,

(120)

\displaystyle\Phi^{C/S}_{-}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{a}\left(a\varphi^{C/S}_{a}+\vartheta^{C/S}_{a}\right)\,.

(121)

Using Eqs. (
112

\displaystyle\Phi^{C}_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{a}\left(1+\frac{\gamma_{aa}}{2}-a\gamma_{eh}\frac{\alpha_{\bar{a}}}{\delta\alpha}\right)\tilde{\varphi}_{a}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{a}a\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{aa}}{2}+a\gamma_{eh}\frac{\alpha_{a}}{\delta\alpha}\right)\tilde{\vartheta}_{a}\,,

(122)

\displaystyle\Phi^{C}_{-}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{a}a\left(1+\frac{\gamma_{aa}}{2}+a\gamma_{eh}\frac{\alpha_{\bar{a}}}{\delta\alpha}\right)\tilde{\varphi}_{a}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{a}\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{aa}}{2}-a\gamma_{eh}\frac{\alpha_{a}}{\delta\alpha}\right)\tilde{\vartheta}_{a}\,.

(123)

\displaystyle\hskip-25.0pt\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{C/S,eh}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi R_{0})^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\times\int d\tau\left[\frac{R_{0}^{2}}{(x+iv_{e}\tau)(x-iv_{h}\tau)}\right]^{1-\gamma_{eh}/2}\,,

(124)

\displaystyle\hskip-25.0pt\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{P,eh}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi R_{0})^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0pt\times\int d\tau\left[\frac{R_{0}^{2}}{(x+iv_{e}\tau)(x-iv_{h}\tau)}\right]^{1+\gamma_{eh}/2}\,,

(125)

which, after integration over
\tau

\displaystyle\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{C/S,eh}(x)

\displaystyle=\chi_{eh}^{(0)}(x)\left|\frac{x}{R_{0}}\right|^{\gamma_{eh}}\,,

(126)

\displaystyle\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{P,eh}(x)

\displaystyle=\chi_{eh}^{(0)}(x)\left|\frac{x}{R_{0}}\right|^{-\gamma_{eh}}\,,

(127)

with

Finally, we note that all results can be easily generalized to finite temperature

.
This corresponds to the following transformation in Eqs. (

), (

), (
124
), (
125

\displaystyle x\pm iv_{a}\tau\rightarrow\frac{\beta v_{a}}{\pi}\sinh\left[\frac{\pi}{\beta v_{a}}\left(x\pm iv_{a}\tau\right)\right]\,,

where
\beta=1/T
is the inverse temperature, the Boltzmann constant

We point out that all zero-temperature singularities in the relevant susceptibilities for

which we discussed in the main text are cut by any finite temperature.
This behavior is qualitatively different from the BCS-like mechanisms of instabilities which provide finite energy scales at
|\gamma_{ab}|>\gamma_{c}
corresponding to finite critical temperatures.
In our situation, a forward-scattering interaction cannot stabilize any long-range order at any finite temperature, which is similar to 1D Luttinger liquids, where additional irrelevant interactions are necessary to stabilize long-range orders.

Below, we provide the temperature scaling of static susceptibilities.
All 1D static susceptibilities given by Eqs. (

), (

), (

), (

, see the conformal transformation given by Eq. (

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{ab}(r)=\frac{T^{2}}{4v_{a}v_{b}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{v_{a}v_{b}}}{\pi TR_{0}}\right)^{\gamma_{0}}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\times\!\int\limits_{0}^{\beta}\!\frac{d\tau}{\left[\sinh\!\left(\frac{\pi Tr}{v_{a}}+i\pi T\tau\right)\sinh\!\left(\frac{\pi Tr}{v_{b}}-i\pi T\tau\right)\!\right]^{1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}}},

\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{ab}
for charge and spin susceptibilities,
\gamma_{0}=-\gamma_{ab}

In order to take the integral over

\tau

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{ab}(r)=\frac{T}{v_{a}v_{b}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{v_{a}v_{b}}}{2\pi TR_{0}}\right)^{\gamma_{0}}

(130)

where

R_{T}

\displaystyle R_{T}=\frac{v_{a}v_{b}}{\pi(v_{a}+v_{b})T}.

(131)

The integral over
S_{\rho}
in Eq. (

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{ab}(r)=\frac{T}{\pi v_{a}v_{b}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{v_{a}v_{b}}}{2\pi TR_{0}}\right)^{\gamma_{0}}e^{-\frac{r}{R_{T}}\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}\right)}\sin\left(\frac{\pi\gamma_{0}}{2}\right)

(132)

where we rescaled

.
Finally, the integral over

\hskip-20.0pt{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c-b)}

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0pt\times\int\limits_{0}^{1}du\,u^{b-1}(1-u)^{c-b-1}\left(1-zu\right)^{-a}.

(133)

Hence, the finite-temperature 1D susceptibilities take the following form:

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0pt\chi^{(1\rm D)}_{ab}(r)=\frac{e^{-\frac{r}{R_{T}}\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}\right)}}{\pi(v_{a}+v_{b})R_{T}}\left(\frac{R_{T}}{\tilde{R}_{0}}\right)^{\gamma_{0}}

\displaystyle\hskip 25.0pt\times\,_{2}F_{1}\!\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2},1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2};1;e^{-\frac{2r}{R_{T}}}\right).

(134)

Here, we introduced an adjusted cut-off,

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{0}=R_{0}\frac{2\sqrt{v_{a}v_{b}}}{v_{a}+v_{b}}.

(135)

It is clear from Eq. (
134
) that the finite-temperature 1D static susceptibility is localized on the thermal scale
r\lesssim R_{T}
.
At zero-temperature, we found that the
D
-dimensional susceptibilities converged at
r\sim 1/\delta Q
, where
\delta Q
is the distance from the corresponding anomaly, see Sec.
V
.
In case of finite temperature, we can set
\delta Q=0
, and the corresponding convergence of
D
-dimensional Fourier transforms is provided on the scale
r\sim R_{T}
.
Therefore, the temperature scaling of the
D
-dimensional susceptibilities can be obtained by the substitution,

\displaystyle\delta Q\to\frac{1}{R_{T}}\propto T.

(136)

Therefore, the finite-temperature static susceptibilities remain finite at any
T>0
.
However, at
\gamma_{0}>\gamma_{c}
this temperature scaling is singular if
T\to 0
which agrees with the non-Fermi-liquid behavior observed in many strongly correlated electron systems.

Appendix G
Fourier transforms

Here we provide the
D
-dimensional Fourier transforms of oscillatory functions with power-law amplitudes,

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0pt\int d\bm{r}\,e^{-i\bm{Q}\cdot\bm{r}}\frac{\cos\left(Q_{0}r-\phi\right)}{r^{D-\gamma}}\approx\left[\frac{2\pi}{Q_{0}}\right]^{\gamma_{c}}

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\times\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\gamma-\gamma_{c}\right)+\left(\phi-\vartheta\right)\,\mathrm{sgn}\left(\delta Q\right)\right)\frac{\Gamma(\gamma-\gamma_{c})}{|\delta Q|^{\gamma-\gamma_{c}}},

(137)

where
\phi
is an arbitrary phase,
\gamma_{c}=(D-1)/2
is given by Eq. (
33
),

\vartheta=\pi(D-1)/4
is the semiclassical phase,
\delta Q=Q-Q_{0}\ll Q_{0}
,
\textrm{sgn}(x)
returns the sign of
x
.
Equation (
137
) is the asymptotic expansion near
Q=Q_{0}
that only takes into account the non-analytic
\propto|\delta Q|^{\gamma_{c}-\gamma}
contribution, any analytic part can be safely discarded.
Next, we show a few steps of the derivation.

The angular integral over the
D
-dimensional solid angle
d\bm{n}
can be expressed in terms of the Bessel function
J_{\mu}(z)
,

\displaystyle\int d\bm{n}\,e^{-i\bm{Q}\cdot\bm{r}}=2\pi\left(\frac{2\pi}{Qr}\right)^{\frac{D}{2}-1}J_{\frac{D}{2}-1}\left(Qr\right).

(138)

Here we are interested in the asymptotic limit when
Q\approx Q_{0}
, so we consider the sector of large distances where
Qr\gg 1
,

\displaystyle\int d\bm{n}\,e^{-i\bm{Q}\cdot\bm{r}}\approx\left(\frac{2\pi}{Qr}\right)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}2\cos\left(Qr-\vartheta\right).

(139)

From the product
\cos\left(Qr-\vartheta\right)\cos\left(Q_{0}r-\phi\right)
we consider only the slowest
Q-Q_{0}
harmonic that is sensitive to the long-distance limit, whereas the
Q+Q_{0}
harmonic is discarded.
The integral over
r
can be analytically continued to the Euler gamma function as follows,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{\gamma_{c}-\gamma+1}}e^{i\delta Qr}=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma-\gamma_{c})}{|\delta Q|^{\gamma-\gamma_{c}}}e^{\frac{i\pi}{2}(\gamma-\gamma_{c})\mathrm{sgn}\left(\delta Q\right)},

(140)

where the Wick rotation was performed, such that on the rotated contour we have
r=iz/\delta Q
, where
z\in(0,+\infty)
.
The formal short-distance singularity of the integral in Eq. (
140
) at
r\to 0
in case if
\gamma<\gamma_{c}
is cured by the short-distance cut-off.
The ultraviolet divergence in this case just corresponds to the standard Fermi-liquid renormalizations coming from the short distances
r\sim\lambda_{F}
.
In contrast, the singularity for
\delta Q\to 0
if
\gamma>\gamma_{c}
indicates a strong infrared divergence of the integral at large distances that is only cut by finite
\delta Q
.

The Fourier transform of power-law functions without an oscillatory factor is the following,

\displaystyle\hskip-28.0pt\int d\bm{r}\,\frac{e^{-i\bm{Q}\cdot\bm{r}}}{r^{D-\gamma}}=\left[\frac{2}{Q}\right]^{\gamma}\frac{\pi^{\frac{D}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{D-\gamma}{2}\right)}.

(141)

Equation (
141
) is exact at
\gamma>0
.
It is straightforwardly derived using Eq. (
138
) and the following integral representation of the Bessel function,

\displaystyle\hskip-7.0ptJ_{\mu}(u)=\frac{u^{\mu}}{2^{\mu}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\mu+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int\limits_{-1}^{1}ds\,\left(1-s^{2}\right)^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}e^{isu},

(142)

where
\mu>-1/2
.
This integral representation is used to calculate the following integral,

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}du\,u^{\gamma-\frac{D}{2}}J_{\frac{D}{2}-1}(u)=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)}{2^{\frac{D}{2}-\gamma}\Gamma\left(\frac{D-\gamma}{2}\right)}.

(143)

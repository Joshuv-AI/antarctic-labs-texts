A Two Stepsize SQP Method for Nonlinear Equality Constrained Stochastic Optimization
Michael J. O’Neill

Abstract
We develop a Sequential Quadratic Optimization (SQP) algorithm for minimizing a stochastic objective function subject to deterministic equality constraints. The method utilizes two different stepsizes, one which exclusively scales the component of the step corrupted by the variance of the stochastic gradient estimates and a second which scales the entire step. We prove that this stepsize splitting scheme has a worst-case complexity result which improves over the best known result for this class of problems. In terms of approximately satisfying the constraint violation, this complexity result matches that of deterministic SQP methods, up to constant factors, while matching the known optimal rate for stochastic SQP methods to approximately minimize the norm of the gradient of the Lagrangian. We also propose and analyze multiple variants of our algorithm. One of these variants is based upon popular adaptive gradient methods for unconstrained stochastic optimization while another incorporates a safeguarded line search along the constraint violation. Preliminary numerical experiments show competitive performance against a state of the art stochastic SQP method. In addition, in these experiments, we observe an improved rate of convergence in terms of the constraint violation, as predicted by the theoretical results.

Introduction

We propose a new algorithm for solving equality constrained optimization problems in which the objective function is the expectation of a stochastic funciton. Formally, we consider the optimization problem

\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\min}\quad f(x)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad c(x)=0\quad\text{with}\quad f(x)=\mathbb{E}[F(x,\omega)],

The method we design is based on Sequential Quadratic Optimization (SQP) methods, a popular class of algorithms that has seen significant interest in recent years for solving stochastic equality constrained optimization problems, beginning with the influential work of
[

At each iteration, these algorithms generate a search direction by solving a quadratic optimization problem defined in terms of a stochastic gradient estimate subject to a linearization of the constraints and then produce a new iterate by moving along this search direction. For stochastic SQP methods, the chosen step length is generally scaled in such a way as to control the variance of the stochastic gradient estimates, in a manner similar to stepsizes for stochastic gradient methods in unconstrained optimization. Our algorithm takes a different approach and directly utilizes the orthogonal step decomposition of SQP methods

Using this observation, we propose a method which employs two different stepsizes: one which controls the variance of the stochastic gradient estimates and scales only the tangential component and a second stepsize which scales the entire search direction.

Unfortunately, the complexity result we prove for our initial algorithm requires certain choices of the stepsizes based on potentially difficult to estimate parameters of the problem (such as Lipschitz constants and a reasonable setting of the merit parameter). To remedy this, we propose a variant of our method which incorporates stepsizes inspired by adaptive gradient methods for unconstrained stochastic optimization

25

. Specifically, we build upon the methodolgy commonly known as Adagrad-Norm, which estimates a stepsize using the prior stochastic gradient estimates. We show that we can generate both of the stepsizes used by our algorithm under this framework and derive a worst-case complexity result for this variant of our method of the order
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon_{\ell}^{-4})

\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon_{c}^{-1})

These numerical experiments also demonstrate faster convergence in constraint violation when compared with previously proposed stochastic SQP methods, providing confirmation of our theoretical results.

1.1
Notation

We adopt the notation that
\|\cdot\|
denotes the
\ell_{2}
-norm for vectors and the vector-induced
\ell_{2}
-norm
for matrices. The set of
nonnegative integers is denoted as
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\dots,\}
and we denote the positive real numbers by
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

Given
\phi:\mathbb{R}{}\to\mathbb{R}{}
and
\varphi:\mathbb{R}{}\to[0,\infty)
, we write
\phi(\cdot)=\mathcal{O}(\varphi(\cdot))
to indicate that
|\phi(\cdot)|\leq c\varphi(\cdot)
for some
c\in(0,\infty)
. Similarly, we write
\phi(\cdot)=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi(\cdot))
to indicate that
|\phi(\cdot)|\leq c\varphi(\cdot)|\log^{\bar{c}}(\cdot)|
for some
c\in(0,\infty)
and
\bar{c}\in(0,\infty)
. In this manner, one finds that
\mathcal{O}(\varphi(\cdot)|\log^{\bar{c}}(\cdot)|)\equiv\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi(\cdot))
for any
\bar{c}\in(0,\infty)
.

The algorithm that we analyze is iterative, generating in each realization a sequence
\{x_{k}\}
. We also append the iteration number to other quantities corresponding an iteration, e.g.,
f_{k}:=f(x_{k})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

1.2
Assumptions and Background

Throughout, we require the following assumptions on
f
and
c
:

Assumption 1

The objective function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuously differentiable and bounded below by
f_{\text{\rm low}}\in\mathbb{R}{}
and the corresponding gradient function
\nabla f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is bounded and Lipschitz continuous with constant
L\in(0,\infty)
. The constraint function
c:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}

(
where
m\leq n
)
and the corresponding Jacobian function
J:=\nabla c^{\top}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
are bounded, each gradient function
\nabla c_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
\gamma_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, and the singular values of
J\equiv\nabla c^{\top}
are bounded below and away from zero.

Under this assumption both the gradient of
f
and the constraint violation are bounded in norm by constants. We denote these constants as
\|\nabla f(x)\|\leq\kappa_{g}
and
\|c_{k}\|_{1}\leq\kappa_{c}
.

Defining the Lagrangian
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
corresponding to (
1
) by
\ell(x,y):=f(x)+c(x)^{\top}y
, first-order primal-dual stationarity conditions for (
1
), which are necessary for optimality under Assumption
1
, are given by

0=\left[\begin{matrix}\nabla_{x}\ell(x,y)\\
\nabla_{y}\ell(x,y)\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\nabla f(x)+\nabla c(x)y\\
c(x)\end{matrix}\right].

(3)

We note that the complexity measure (
2
) is simply an approximate version of these optimality conditions.

As stated above, our algorithm generates a search direction at iteration
k
by solving the following quadratic optimization problem:

\underset{p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\min}\ f_{k}+g_{k}^{T}p+\frac{1}{2}p^{T}H_{k}p\ \ \text{subject to}\ \ c_{k}+J_{k}p=0,

(4)

where
g_{k}
is the current stochastic gradient estimate. It is well known that this is equivalent to solving the “Newton SQP system”:

\left[\begin{matrix}H_{k}&J_{k}^{T}\\
J_{k}&0\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}p_{k}\\
y_{k}\end{matrix}\right]=-\left[\begin{matrix}g_{k}\\
c_{k}\end{matrix}\right].

(5)

In order to ensure the solution of this sub-problem is unique, we require the following assumption on
H_{k}
.

Assumption 2

The sequence
\{H_{k}\}
is bounded norm by
\kappa_{H}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. In addition, there exists a constant
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the matrix
H_{k}
has the property that
u^{T}H_{k}u\geq\zeta\|u\|^{2}
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
J_{k}u=0
.

In order to analyze our algorithm, we utilize the
\ell
-1 merit function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
:

\phi(x,\tau)=\tau f(x)+\|c(x)\|_{1}.

(6)

In the above equation,
\tau
is the merit parameter which balances between the function value and constraint violation. For the analysis, we also use the following local model of the merit function
l:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined as

l(x,\tau,d)=\tau(f(x)+\nabla f(x)^{T}d)+\|c(x)+\nabla c(x)^{T}d\|_{1}.

(7)

In addition, we consider the reduction in the model for a direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
c(x)+\nabla c(x)^{T}d=0
which is
\Delta l:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined as

\begin{split}\Delta l(x,\tau,d)&:=l(x,\tau,0)-l(x,\tau,d)\\
&=-\tau\nabla f(x)^{T}d+\|c(x)\|_{1}.\end{split}

(8)

We wish to stress here that unlike previous work, we do not attempt to estimate a good value of
\tau
. We choose to avoid this as previous work relied upon strong assumptions (such as uniformly bounded stochastic gradients
[
4
]
or sub-Gaussian stochastic gradients
[
12
]
) in order to prove their results. By choosing to relegate the merit function and parameter exclusively to the analysis, we are able to avoid overcomplicating the analysis and adding unnecessary assumptions.

Algorithm and Basic Properties

Recall that at each iteration, a search direction
p_{k}
is computed as the solution of (
5
). We assume that this step computation is performed in such a way that the orthogonal decomposition

p_{k}=u_{k}+v_{k}\ \text{where}\ u_{k}\in\text{Null}(J_{k})\ \text{and}\ v_{k}\in\text{Range}(J_{k}^{T}),

(9)

is known
2
2

2

This is not necessary in certain circumstances, see Remark
1

, does not depend on the current stochastic gradient estimate
g_{k}
. Unlike prior work, we do not directly use
p_{k}
as our search direction. Instead, we rescale the tangential component,
u_{k}
, in order to generate our search direction
d_{k}
as follows,

d_{k}=\beta_{k}u_{k}+v_{k},

(10)

where
\beta_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Then, we find the next iterate
x_{k+1}
by setting
x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k}
for some
\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. This procedure is formalized in Algorithm

.

.

The choice of
\beta_{k}
is crucial to ensure convergence of our algorithm, as it controls the variance of the stochastic gradient estimates and plays a similar role as the stepsize in stochastic gradient methods. As such, it is natural to consider
\beta_{k}
to be quite small. Indeed, to ensure our convergence result, we set
\beta_{k}=O(1/\sqrt{K})
, where
K
is the total number of iterations we intend to perform. On the other hand,
\alpha_{k}
does
not
need to control the error in the stochastic gradients and thus may be set independent of
K
. Thus,
v_{k}
, which is the component of
d_{k}
that drives the algorithm towards constraint satisfaction, is only scaled by a stepsize which is independent of
K
. This is the key insight that leads to our improved complexity.

Algorithm
1
is written generically, without specifying how to choose the stepsizes
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
. We consider two variants for choosing these stepsizes in Section
3
and analyze their behavior. First, in Section
3.1
, we consider the case where
\beta_{k}
is defined by a pre-specified sequence and

\alpha_{k}\in[\nu,\nu+\theta\beta_{k}],

(11)

where
\nu\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. This case is essentially equivalent to the standard stochastic gradient regime with a pre-specified stepsize sequence (modulo the relaxtion of
\alpha_{k}
into a range, which was originally suggested for stochastic SQP methods in
[
4
]
). For this method, we prove the complexity result foreshadowed in Section
1
. However, this result only holds under certain conditions on
\nu
which depend on the Lipschitz constants of the gradient of
f
and Jacobian of
c
as well as a good estimate of the merit parameter
\tau
. Unfortunately, it may not be reasonable to estimate these parameters a-priori.

To remedy this, in Section
3.2
we analyze a version of Algorithm
1
which utilizes adaptive stepsizes based on Adagrad-Norm, which is a popular approach for choosing stepsizes in the stochastic gradient literature
[
14
,
17
,
25
]
. In this case, some additional logarithmic factors appear in the final complexity result, but this approach does not require any knowledge of the Lipschitz constants or the merit parameter.

In addition, in both of the cases we analyze in Section
3
,
\alpha_{k}
may be chosen from a specific range. In Section
4
, we describe a safeguarded line search procedure which can be used to determine
\alpha_{k}
. We take adavantage of the assumption that the constraints are deterministic and design a line search which only relies on the constraint violation and does not include stochastic gradient information when computing an
\alpha_{k}
. In addition, we provide a fully specified algorithm in Algorithm
2
that combines this linesearch procedure with an adaptive lower bound based on the Adagrad-Norm stepsizes developed in Section

.

Remark 1

Under the condition that
H_{k}
preserves the null space of
J_{k}
(i.e. for any
u\in\text{Null}(J_{k}),H_{k}u\in\text{Null}(J_{k})

p_{k}
by simply rescaling the matrix
H_{k}
by
\beta_{k}^{-1}
and directly use the computed direction as

. This additional requirement is necessary when using rescaling in order to prove a result similar to Lemma

\beta_{k}^{-1}

\tau_{\min}
that is independent of a

\beta_{k}
, thus causing serious issues in the final complexity result. For the sake of generality, we don’t consider this rescaling approach, though when
H_{k}
preserves the nullspace of
J_{k}
our results still hold, albeit with potentially different constant factors.

A direct consequence of the previous lemma is

\Delta l(x_{k},\tau_{\min},d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})\geq\tau_{\min}\beta_{k}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+\sigma\|c_{k}\|_{1},

(16)

) and first order stationarity, we prove the following lemma, which shows that the quadratic term can be lower bounded in terms of the gradient of the Lagrangian at
x_{k}
for a specific Lagrange multipler.

Lemma 4
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
hold. Then,

(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\geq\zeta\kappa_{H}^{-2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}-(1+2\kappa_{u})\zeta\kappa_{v}\|c_{k}\|_{1}.

Proof.

By Assumption
2
,

(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\geq\zeta\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}\geq\zeta\kappa_{H}^{-2}\|H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}.

Then, by (
12
) and Lemmas
1
and
2
,

\displaystyle\|H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+H_{k}v_{k}-H_{k}v_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\|H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+H_{k}v_{k}\|^{2}-2v_{k}^{T}H_{k}H_{k}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+v_{k})+\|H_{k}v_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}-2v_{k}^{T}H_{k}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-\|H_{k}v_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}-(1+2\kappa_{u})\kappa_{H}^{2}\kappa_{v}\|c_{k}\|_{1},

which proves the result.

Thus, given these results, we can see that the convergence rate in terms of the gradient of the Lagrangian should be directly related to the choice of
\beta_{k}
while convergence in the constraint violation will be largely independent of this stepsize (provided it is chosen to sufficiently control the noise in
g_{k}
). This is in contrast to the results in
[
12
]
, where the norm of the constraint violation is multiplied by
\beta_{k}
and is the root cause of the improvement in the complexity result for the constraint violation that we prove in the sequel.

We finish this subsection with the following generic descent lemma.

Lemma 5
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
hold. Then, with
\tau_{\min}
defined as in (
14
),

\displaystyle\phi(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},\tau_{\min})-\phi(x_{k},\tau_{\min})

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\alpha_{k}\Delta l(x_{k},\tau_{\min},d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|u_{k}\|^{2}

(17)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\kappa_{v}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)+4)\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}).

Proof.

By L-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f(x)
and
\Gamma
-Lipschitz continuity of
J_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},\tau_{\min})-\phi(x_{k},\tau_{\min})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}d_{k}+\|c_{k}+\alpha_{k}J_{k}d_{k}\|_{1}-\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|d_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+|1-\alpha_{k}|\|c_{k}\|_{1}-\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|d_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}),

where the equality follows from
J_{k}d_{k}=-c_{k}
.

Then, when
\alpha_{k}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\phi(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},\tau_{\min})-\phi(x_{k},\tau_{\min})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+(1-\alpha_{k})\|c_{k}\|_{1}-\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|d_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-\alpha_{k}\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|d_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})

On the other hand, when
\alpha_{k}>1
,

\displaystyle\phi(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},\tau_{\min})-\phi(x_{k},\tau_{\min})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+(\alpha_{k}-1)\|c_{k}\|_{1}-\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|d_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-\alpha_{k}\|c_{k}\|_{1}+2(\alpha_{k}-1)\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|d_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-\alpha_{k}\|c_{k}\|_{1}+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|d_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})

where the second inequality follows by
\alpha_{k}>1
.

Therefore, in either case, we have

\displaystyle\phi(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},\tau_{\min})-\phi(x_{k},\tau_{\min})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-\alpha_{k}\|c_{k}\|_{1}+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|d_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}).

Then, using the orthogonal decomposition
d_{k}=\beta_{k}u_{k}+v_{k}
, Lemma
1
, and (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\phi(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},\tau_{\min})-\phi(x_{k},\tau_{\min})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-\alpha_{k}\|c_{k}\|_{1}+2\alpha_{k}^{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)(\beta_{k}^{2}\|u_{k}\|^{2}+\|v_{k}\|^{2})+\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})

\displaystyle\leq-\alpha_{k}\Delta l(x_{k},\tau_{\min},d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|u_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\kappa_{v}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)+4)\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}),

proving the result.

2.2
Stochastic Assumptions and Properties

In order to analyze the convergence of our algorithm, let
\mathcal{F}_{k}
denote the natural filtration adapted to Algorithm
1
and let
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\cdot]=\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{k}]
. Under these definitions, we have the following assumption on our stochastic gradient estimates,
g_{k}
.

Assumption 3

There exists
M\in\mathbb{R}{}_{>0}
such that, for all
k
, one finds

\mathbb{E}_{k}[g_{k}]=\nabla f(x_{k})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathbb{E}_{k}[\|g_{k}-\nabla f(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}]\leq M.

(18)

This assumption is largely standard in the stochastic gradient literature. We note that relaxing the uniformly bounded variance assumption to an assumption which allows the variance to grow with the norm of the gradient of
f
(such as in
[
9
]
) is no more general under Assumption
1
since
\|\nabla f(x)\|\leq\kappa_{g}
.

Under Assumption
3
, we have the following properties.

Lemma 6
.

Let Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
hold. Then,
\mathbb{E}_{k}[u_{k}]=u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}
,
\mathbb{E}_{k}[y_{k}]=y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}
,

\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|u_{k}\|^{2}]\leq\zeta^{-1}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+\zeta^{-2}M\ \text{and}\ \mathbb{E}_{k}[\|u_{k}-u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}]\leq\zeta^{-2}M.

In addition, when
\beta_{k}
is measurable to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
,
\mathbb{E}_{k}[d_{k}]=d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}
, and

\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|]\leq\beta_{k}\zeta^{-1}\sqrt{M}.

Proof.

The first two claims follow directly by the statement of
[
4
, Lemma 3.8]
and the third follows directly by the proof of
[
4
, Lemma 3.9]
combined with Assumption
2
. To prove the fourth result, let
Z_{k}
be an orthogonal basis for the null space of
J_{k}
(which, by Assumption
1
is a matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times(n-m)})
and let
u_{k}=Z_{k}w_{k}
and
u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}=Z_{k}w_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}
. Then, by (
5
), it follows that

Z_{k}w_{k}=-Z_{k}(Z_{k}^{T}H_{k}Z_{k})^{-1}Z_{k}^{T}(g_{k}+H_{k}v_{k}).

Similarly,

Z_{k}w_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}=-Z_{k}(Z_{k}^{T}H_{k}Z_{k})^{-1}Z_{k}^{T}(\nabla f(x_{k})+H_{k}v_{k}),

so that

u_{k}-u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}=Z_{k}(Z_{k}^{T}H_{k}Z_{k})^{-1}Z_{k}^{T}(\nabla f(x_{k})-g_{k})

and thus, by Assumptions
2
and
3
,

\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|u_{k}-u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|Z_{k}(Z_{k}^{T}H_{k}Z_{k})^{-1}Z_{k}^{T}\|^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})-g_{k}\|^{2}]\leq\zeta^{-2}M.

When
\beta_{k}
is measurable to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
, it follows that

\mathbb{E}_{k}[d_{k}]=\beta_{k}\mathbb{E}_{k}[u_{k}]+v_{k}=\beta_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+v_{k}=d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}.

For the final result, we have that

d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}=\beta_{k}(u_{k}-u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})=\beta_{k}Z_{k}(Z_{k}^{T}H_{k}Z_{k})^{-1}Z_{k}^{T}(\nabla f(x_{k})-g_{k}).

Thus, by Assumptions
2
and
3
, as well as Jensen’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|]\leq\beta_{k}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|Z_{k}(Z_{k}^{T}H_{k}Z_{k})^{-1}Z_{k}^{T}\|\|\nabla f(x_{k})-g_{k}\|]\leq\beta_{k}\zeta^{-1}\sqrt{M}.

3
Convergence Analysis

In this section, we derive our main convergence results for two variants of Algorithm
1
, which differ on how
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
are chosen at each iteration.

3.1
Covergence with Pre-specified Stepsize Sequences

Throughout this subsection, we analyze Algorithm
1
when
\{\beta_{k}\}
is a pre-specified sequence and
\alpha_{k}
lies a pre-specified range, i.e.,

\{\beta_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0},\quad\quad\alpha_{k}\in[\nu,\nu+\theta\beta_{k}],\ \ \forall k,

(19)

for some
\nu\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

Under this stepsize scheme, we prove a preliminary result about the final term that appears in Lemma
5
.

Lemma 7
.

Let Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
hold. Then,

\mathbb{E}_{k}[\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})]\leq\beta_{k}^{2}\theta\tau_{\min}\kappa_{g}\zeta^{-1}\sqrt{M}.

Proof.

Let
\xi_{k}\in[0,1]
be the random variable such that
\alpha_{k}=\nu+\xi_{k}\theta\beta_{k}
. Then, by Lemma
6
and the fact that
\nu
and
\beta_{k}
are measurable to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}[(\nu+\xi_{k}\theta\beta_{k})\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}[\xi_{k}\theta\beta_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{k}[\theta\beta_{k}\tau_{\min}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|\|d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|]

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{k}^{2}\theta\tau_{\min}\kappa_{g}\zeta^{-1}\sqrt{M}.

Now, we are ready to derive our first main result.

Theorem 8
.

Let Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
hold. Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
, let
\{\beta_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be a pre-specified sequence such that
\beta_{k}\leq\kappa_{\beta}
holds for all
k
, let
\alpha_{k}\in[\nu,\nu+\theta\beta_{k}]
, for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
\nu\in(0,\sigma/(2\kappa_{v}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)+4)]
, and let
\tau_{\min}
be defined as in Lemma
3
. Let

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{(\nu+\theta\kappa_{\beta})^{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)(\zeta^{-1}\kappa_{H}\kappa_{u}^{2}+\zeta^{-2}M)}{2}

(20)

\displaystyle\quad+\theta(\theta\kappa_{c}(\kappa_{v}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)+4)+\tau_{\min}\kappa_{g}\zeta^{-1}\sqrt{M}).

Then, for any
K\in\mathbb{N}
,

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}\tau_{\min}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}]\\
\leq\tau_{\min}(f(x_{0})-f_{\text{\rm low}})+\|c_{0}\|_{1}+\kappa_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\beta_{k}^{2}.\end{split}

(21)

Proof.

Taking the conditional expectation on both sides of (
17
) and applying the results of Lemma
2
, Lemma
6
, and Lemma
7
(noting that
\beta_{k}
is measurable to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\phi(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},\tau_{\min})]-\phi(x_{k},\tau_{\min})

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\mathbb{E}_{k}[\alpha_{k}\Delta l(x_{k},\tau_{\min},\nabla f(x_{k}),d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})]+\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|u_{k}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\kappa_{v}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)+4)\|c_{k}\|_{1}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{k}[\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})]

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\alpha_{k}\Delta l(x_{k},\tau_{\min},\nabla f(x_{k}),d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})+(\nu^{2}+\theta^{2}\beta_{k}^{2})(\kappa_{v}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)+4)\|c_{k}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)(\zeta^{-1}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+\zeta^{-2}M)+\theta\beta_{k}^{2}\tau_{\min}\kappa_{g}\zeta^{-1}\sqrt{M}

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\alpha_{k}\Delta l(x_{k},\tau_{\min},\nabla f(x_{k}),d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})+\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)(\zeta^{-1}\kappa_{H}\kappa_{u}^{2}+\zeta^{-2}M)

\displaystyle\quad\ +\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\beta_{k}^{2}\theta(\theta\kappa_{c}(\kappa_{v}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)+4)+\tau_{\min}\kappa_{g}\zeta^{-1}\sqrt{M})

\displaystyle\quad=-\alpha_{k}\Delta l(x_{k},\tau_{\min},\nabla f(x_{k}),d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})+\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\beta_{k}^{2}\kappa_{1},

where the final inequality follows by
\nu\leq\alpha_{k}
.

Now, by (
16
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\phi(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},\tau_{\min})]-\phi(x_{k},\tau_{\min})

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\alpha_{k}\Delta l(x_{k},\tau_{\min},\nabla f(x_{k}),d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})+\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\beta_{k}^{2}\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\alpha_{k}(\beta_{k}\tau_{\min}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+\sigma\|c_{k}\|_{1})+\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\beta_{k}^{2}\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle\quad=-\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}\tau_{\min}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}+\beta_{k}^{2}\kappa_{1}.

Taking the total expectation of this inequality, rearranging and summing from
k=0,\dots,K-1
,

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}\tau_{\min}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}]\\
\leq\phi(x_{0},\tau_{\min})-\mathbb{E}[\phi(x_{K},\tau_{\min})]+\kappa_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\beta_{k}^{2}.\end{split}

Due to Assumption
1
, we have,

-\mathbb{E}[\phi(x_{K},\tau_{\min})]=-\mathbb{E}[\tau_{\min}f(x_{K})+\|c_{K}\|_{1}]\leq-\tau_{\min}f_{\text{\rm low}},

so that

\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}\tau_{\min}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}]\leq\phi(x_{0},\tau_{\min})-\tau_{\min}f_{\text{\rm low}}+\kappa_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\beta_{k}^{2},

which proves the result.

Corollary 9
.

For any
K\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, let
\beta_{k}:=\beta=\eta/\sqrt{K}
for all
k\in[0,K-1]
where
\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, let
\kappa_{1}
be defined in (
20
) and let

\kappa_{2}:=\tau_{\min}(f(x_{0})-f_{\text{\rm low}})+\|c_{0}\|_{1}+\eta^{2}\kappa_{1}.

(22)

Then, under the conditions of Theorem
8
, we have

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\|c_{k}\|_{1}]\leq\frac{2\kappa_{2}}{\nu\sigma K},

(23)

and

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{\kappa_{H}^{2}\kappa_{2}}{\tau_{\min}\zeta\nu\eta\sqrt{K}}+\frac{2\zeta(1+2\kappa_{u})\kappa_{v}\kappa_{H}^{2}\kappa_{2}}{\nu\sigma K}.

(24)

Finally, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\begin{split}\underset{k\in[0,K-1]}{\min}\ \tau_{\min}\zeta\kappa_{H}^{-2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma\sqrt{K}}{2\eta}\|c_{k}\|_{1}\\
\leq\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\nu\eta\delta\sqrt{K}}+\frac{2(1+2\kappa_{u})\zeta\tau_{\min}\kappa_{v}\kappa_{2}}{\sigma\delta K}.\end{split}

(25)

Proof.

By Theorem
8
, the definition of
\beta_{k}
, and
\nu\leq\alpha_{k}
, it follows that

\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\|c_{k}\|_{1}]\leq\frac{2(\tau_{\min}(f(x_{0})-f_{\text{\rm low}})+\|c_{0}\|_{1}+\kappa_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\beta_{k}^{2})}{\nu\sigma}\leq\frac{2\kappa_{2}}{\nu\sigma}.

(26)

Dividing both sides of this inequality by
K
yields the first result.

Now, by Theorem
8
and Lemma
4
as well as
\alpha_{k}\leq\nu
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\nu\beta_{k}\tau_{\min}\zeta\kappa_{H}^{-2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}+\frac{\nu\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}-\nu\tau_{\min}\beta_{k}(1+2\kappa_{u})\zeta\kappa_{v}\|c_{k}\|_{1}]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})+\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\tau_{\min}(f(x_{0})-f_{\text{\rm low}})+\|c_{0}\|_{1}+\kappa_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\beta_{k}^{2}.

(27)

Rearranging this inequality and using
\beta_{k}=\eta/\sqrt{K}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{\kappa_{H}^{2}\kappa_{2}\sqrt{K}}{\nu\eta\tau_{\min}\zeta}+(1+2\kappa_{u})\kappa_{v}\kappa_{H}^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\|c_{k}\|_{1}].

Dividing through by
K
and applying (
23
), it follows that

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{\kappa_{H}^{2}\kappa_{2}}{\nu\eta\tau_{\min}\zeta\sqrt{K}}+\frac{2(1+2\kappa_{u})\tau_{\min}\kappa_{v}\kappa_{H}^{2}\kappa_{2}}{\nu\sigma\tau_{\min}K},

which proves the second result.

To prove the final result, by (
27
),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\nu\beta_{k}\tau_{\min}\zeta\kappa_{H}^{-2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}+\frac{\nu\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\tau_{\min}(f(x_{0})-f_{\text{\rm low}})+\|c_{0}\|_{1}+\kappa_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\beta_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}[\nu\beta_{k}\tau_{\min}(1+2\kappa_{u})\zeta\kappa_{v}\|c_{k}\|_{1}].

Applying the definition of
\beta
, multiplying through by
\frac{1}{\nu\beta K}
, and using (
23
),

\begin{split}\frac{1}{K}\mathbb{E}[\tau_{\min}\zeta\kappa_{H}^{-2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma\sqrt{K}}{2\eta}\|c_{k}\|_{1}]\\
\leq\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\nu\eta\sqrt{K}}+\frac{2(1+2\kappa_{u})\zeta\tau_{\min}\kappa_{v}\kappa_{2}}{\sigma K}\end{split}

and thus

\begin{split}\underset{k\in[0,K-1]}{\min}\ \mathbb{E}[\tau_{\min}\zeta\kappa_{H}^{-2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2\beta}\|c_{k}\|_{1}]\\
\leq\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\nu\sqrt{K}}+\frac{2(1+2\kappa_{u})\zeta\tau_{\min}\kappa_{v}\kappa_{2}}{\nu\sigma K}.\end{split}

Applying Markov’s inequality, it follows that with probability at least
1-\delta
that

\begin{split}\underset{k\in[0,K-1]}{\min}\ \tau_{\min}\zeta\kappa_{H}^{-2}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma\sqrt{K}}{2\eta}\|c_{k}\|_{1}\\
\leq\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\nu\delta\sqrt{K}}+\frac{2(1+2\kappa_{u})\zeta\tau_{\min}\kappa_{v}\kappa_{2}}{\sigma\delta K},\end{split}

which proves the final result.

From the result of Corollary
9
, we can easily derive our worst-case complexity results, as promised in Section
1
. It should be clear that in terms of the constraint violation, by (
23
), the maximum number of iterations until
\mathbb{E}[\|c_{k}\|_{1}]
falls below
\epsilon_{c}
is at most
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{c}^{-1})
. Similarly, by Jensen’s inequality and (
24
), the maximum number of iterations until
\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|]\leq\epsilon_{\ell}
is
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\ell}^{-4})
. Finally, if one is interested in a combined result, we obtain the same
\mathcal{O}(K^{-1/2})
convergence rate as
[
12
]
, however, our convergence is in terms of a much stronger measure with respect to the constraint violation
\|c_{k}\|_{1}
, which is scaled by an additional factor of
\sqrt{K}
. Thus, we expect much faster convergence with respect to the constraint violation than the algorithm in
[
12
]
without harming the convergence rate in terms of the gradient of the Lagrangian.

3.2
Convergence with Adaptive Stepsizes

Now, we analyze the case where
\beta_{k}
and
\alpha_{k}
are set adaptively, in a manner inspired by Adagrad-Norm
[
25
]
. Specifically, at each iteration
k
, let

b_{k}^{2}=b_{k-1}^{2}+\|u_{k}\|^{2},\quad q_{k}^{2}=q_{k-1}^{2}+\|c_{k}\|_{1},

(28)

and

\beta_{k}=\frac{\eta}{b_{k}},\quad\quad\alpha_{k}\in\left[\frac{\nu}{q_{k}},\frac{\nu}{q_{k}}+\min\left\{\frac{\theta}{b_{k}},\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\right\}\right],

(29)

for some constants
\eta>0
and
\nu>0
. We note here that the additional term at the upper end of the range for
\alpha_{k}
is due to our adaptive setting of
\beta_{k}
using
b_{k}
, which is sufficient to control the stochasticity in
g_{k}
, but may be insufficient to control second order terms involving the constraint violation. We remedy this situation via the inclusion of the
\theta/q_{k}
term. In addition, we remark that
q_{k}
can be set in many different ways, such as using
\|v_{k}\|^{2}
or
\|c_{k}\|_{2}
in place of
\|c_{k}\|_{1}
. These other strategies may lead to longer stepsizes, which could have important practical implications, however, we choose to use
\|c_{k}\|_{1}
as it obtains the best constant factors in the convergence analysis among the relevant choices.

Throughout this section, since
\beta_{k}
is dependent on
g_{k}
, we redefine
d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}
as

d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}:=v_{k}+\beta_{k-1}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}},

(30)

so that it remains measurable to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
. We note that under this re-definition, the results of Lemmas
3
and
5
still hold.

Our subsequent analysis relies on the following lemma, which we give without proof as it is a well-known result in the adaptive gradient literature (see for example,
[
24
, Lemma 10]
).

Lemma 10
.

Let
\{a_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
be a series of non-negative real numbers with
a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Then,

\sum_{k=1}^{T}\frac{a_{k}}{\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}}\leq\log\left(\sum_{k=0}^{T}a_{k}\right)-\log(a_{0})

(31)

In order to prove convergence of our algorithm, the key issue posed by the adaptive stepsizes is the final term in (
17
), which requires a more detailed analysis than in Lemma
7
as
\beta_{k}
is no longer measurable to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
and
d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}
has been redefined in (
30
). We give a bound on this term in the following lemma.

Lemma 11
.

Let Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
hold and let

\kappa_{3}:=\zeta^{-1}(\kappa_{H}\kappa_{u}^{2}+\zeta^{-1}M)

(32)

and

\kappa_{4}:=\max\left\{\zeta^{-1}\kappa_{H}^{2},\beta_{-1}(1+2\kappa_{u})\kappa_{H}^{2}\kappa_{v}\tau_{\min}/\sigma\right\}

(33)

Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\alpha_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{k}\Bigg[\frac{\alpha_{k}\beta_{k-1}}{2}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2\tau_{\min}}\|c_{k}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{3\eta^{2}\kappa_{3}\kappa_{4}(\nu+\theta)^{2}}{2q_{-1}b_{-1}}+\frac{3\kappa_{4}\theta^{2}(\eta^{2}+\beta_{-1}^{2}\kappa_{3})}{2\eta\nu}+\frac{3\zeta^{-1}M\kappa_{4}\theta^{2}\beta_{-1}}{2q_{-1}b_{-1}^{2}}\right)\frac{\|u_{k}^{2}\|}{b_{k}^{2}}\Bigg].

(34)

Proof.

By the definition of
d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\alpha_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})\right]=\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\alpha_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(\beta_{k}u_{k}-\beta_{k-1}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})\right].

Let
\xi_{k}\in[0,1]
be the random variable such that
\alpha_{k}=\frac{\nu}{q_{k}}+\xi_{k}\min\{\frac{\theta}{b_{k}},\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\}
. Then, by Lemma
6
and the fact that
\beta_{k-1}
,
\nu
, and
q_{k}
are measurable to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\alpha_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(\beta_{k}u_{k}-\beta_{k-1}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\left(\frac{\nu}{q_{k}}+\xi_{k}\min\left\{\frac{\theta}{b_{k}},\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\right\}\right)\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(\beta_{k}u_{k}-\beta_{k-1}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}\Big[\frac{\nu}{q_{k}}(\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1})\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}u_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\xi_{k}\min\left\{\frac{\theta}{b_{k}},\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\right\}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(\beta_{k}u_{k}-\beta_{k-1}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}\Big[\frac{\nu}{q_{k}}(\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1})(\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}u_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\xi_{k}\min\left\{\frac{\theta}{b_{k}},\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\right\}(\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}(\beta_{k}u_{k}-\beta_{k-1}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}\Big[\frac{\nu}{q_{k}}(\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1})(\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}u_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\xi_{k}\min\left\{\frac{\theta}{b_{k}},\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\right\}\beta_{k}(\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}(u_{k}-u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})

\displaystyle\quad+\xi_{k}\min\left\{\frac{\theta}{b_{k}},\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\right\}(\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1})(\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}\Big[\left(\frac{\nu}{q_{k}}+\xi_{k}\min\left\{\frac{\theta}{b_{k}},\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\right\}\right)(\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1})(\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}u_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\xi_{k}\min\left\{\frac{\theta}{b_{k}},\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\right\}\beta_{k}(\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}(u_{k}-u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})

\displaystyle\quad+\xi_{k}\min\left\{\frac{\theta}{b_{k}},\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\right\}(\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1})(\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-u_{k})\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{k}\Big[\frac{\nu+\theta}{q_{k}}|\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1}|\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|\|u_{k}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\min\left\{\frac{\theta}{b_{k}},\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\right\}\beta_{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|\|u_{k}-u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|

(35)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}|\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1}|\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-u_{k}\|\Big],

where the third equality follows by
u_{k},u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\in\text{Null}(J_{k})
and the inequality by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
\xi_{k}\leq 1
.

Now, we focus on the first term in (
35
),

|\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1}|=\frac{\eta}{b_{k-1}}-\frac{\eta}{b_{k}}=\frac{\eta\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{b_{k-1}b_{k}(b_{k}+b_{k-1})}\leq\frac{\eta\|u_{k}\|}{b_{k-1}b_{k}},

(36)

where the inequality follows by
\|u_{k}\|\leq b_{k}
. Therefore, applying Young’s inequality, we have, for any
\lambda_{1}>0
measurable to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\nu+\theta}{q_{k}}|\beta_{k-1}-\beta_{k}|\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|\|u_{k}\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\eta\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{(\nu+\theta)\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{q_{k}b_{k-1}b_{k}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}}{2b_{k-1}q_{k}\lambda_{1}}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|u_{k}\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\eta\left(\nu+\theta\right)^{2}\lambda_{1}\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{2q_{k}b_{k-1}b_{k}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta\kappa_{3}\beta_{k-1}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}}{2q_{k}\lambda_{1}}+\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\eta(\nu+\theta)^{2}\lambda_{1}\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{2q_{k}b_{k-1}b_{k}^{2}}\right]

(37)

where the final inequality follows by Assumption
2
as well as the results of Lemma
2
and Lemma
6
.

Now, for the second term in (
35
), by Young’s inequality, for any
\lambda_{2}>0
measurable to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\beta_{k}\min\left\{\frac{\theta}{b_{k}},\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\right\}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|\|u_{k}-u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2q_{k}b_{k}\lambda_{2}}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}}{2}\|u_{k}-u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}\right].

(38)

Working with the last term in this inequality, since
q_{k}
and
\beta_{k-1}
are measurable to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
, by Lemma
6
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}}{2}\|u_{k}-u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\beta_{k}^{2}(\|u_{k}\|^{2}+\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}-2u_{k}^{T}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\beta_{k}^{2}(\|u_{k}\|^{2}+\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}-2u_{k}^{T}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})+\beta_{k-1}^{2}(2u_{k}^{T}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-2\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\beta_{k}^{2}\|u_{k}\|^{2}+2|\beta_{k}^{2}-\beta_{k-1}^{2}|\|u_{k}\|\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|-\beta_{k-1}^{2}\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\beta_{k}^{2}\|u_{k}\|^{2}+2\eta^{2}\frac{\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{b_{k-1}^{2}b_{k}^{2}}\|u_{k}\|\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|-\beta_{k-1}^{2}\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\beta_{k}^{2}\|u_{k}\|^{2}+2\eta^{2}\frac{\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{b_{k-1}^{2}b_{k}}\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|-\beta_{k-1}^{2}\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}\right]

(39)

where the first inequality follows by
\beta_{k}\leq\beta_{k-1}
and the second inequality follows by
\|u_{k}\|\leq b_{k}
. Dealing with the second term in this inequality, again applying Young’s inequality and using
\beta_{k-1}=\eta/b_{k-1}
, by Lemmas
2
and
6
as well as Assumption
2
,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{2\beta_{k-1}^{2}\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{b_{k}}\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}\eta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\beta_{k-1}^{2}\|u_{k}\|^{2}\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}}{\kappa_{3}}+\frac{\beta_{k-1}^{2}\kappa_{3}\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{b_{k}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\beta_{k-1}^{2}\zeta^{-1}(\kappa_{H}\kappa_{u}^{2}+\zeta^{-1}M)\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}}{\kappa_{3}}+\frac{\beta_{k-1}^{2}\kappa_{3}\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{b_{k}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\beta_{k-1}^{2}\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}+\frac{\beta_{k-1}^{2}\kappa_{3}\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{b_{k}^{2}}\right],

so that the first term cancels with the last in (
39
).

Now, for the final term in (
35
), by (
36
), applying Young’s inequality for some
\lambda_{3}>0
that is measurable to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
, by Lemma
6
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}|\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1}|\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-u_{k}\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\theta}{q_{k}}\frac{\beta_{k-1}\|u_{k}\|}{b_{k-1}b_{k}}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-u_{k}\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\beta_{k-1}}{2\lambda_{3}q_{k}}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}\|u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}-u_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\theta^{2}\lambda_{3}\beta_{k-1}\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{2q_{k}b_{k-1}^{2}b_{k}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\zeta^{-1}M\beta_{k-1}}{2\lambda_{3}q_{k}}\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}+\frac{\theta^{2}\lambda_{3}\beta_{k-1}\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{2q_{k}b_{k-1}^{2}b_{k}^{2}}\right]

Therefore, combining (

37

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\alpha_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(\beta_{k}u_{k}-\beta_{k-1}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{k}\Bigg[\left(\frac{\eta\kappa_{3}\beta_{k-1}}{2q_{k}\lambda_{1}}+\frac{1}{2q_{k}b_{k}\lambda_{2}}+\frac{\zeta^{-1}M\beta_{k-1}}{2q_{k}\lambda_{3}}\right)\|\nabla f(x_{k})+J_{k}^{T}y_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}\eta(\nu+\theta)^{2}}{2q_{k}b_{k-1}}+\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}(\eta^{2}+\beta_{k-1}^{2}\kappa_{3})}{2}+\frac{\lambda_{3}\theta^{2}\beta_{k-1}}{2q_{k}b_{k-1}^{2}}\right)\frac{\|u_{k}^{2}\|}{b_{k}^{2}}\Bigg]

Applying Lemma
4
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\alpha_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(\beta_{k}u_{k}-\beta_{k-1}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{k}\Bigg[\left(\frac{\eta\kappa_{3}\beta_{k-1}}{2q_{k}\lambda_{1}}+\frac{1}{2q_{k}b_{k}\lambda_{2}}+\frac{\zeta^{-1}M\beta_{k-1}}{2q_{k}\lambda_{3}}\right)\big(\zeta^{-1}\kappa_{H}^{2}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}

\displaystyle+(1+2\kappa_{u})\kappa_{H}^{2}\kappa_{v}\|c_{k}\|_{1}\big)+\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}\eta(\nu+\theta)^{2}}{2q_{k}b_{k-1}}+\frac{\lambda_{2}\theta^{2}(\eta^{2}+\beta_{k-1}^{2}\kappa_{3})}{2}+\frac{\lambda_{3}\theta^{2}\beta_{k-1}}{2q_{k}b_{k-1}^{2}}\right)\frac{\|u_{k}^{2}\|}{b_{k}^{2}}\Bigg].

Choosing
\lambda_{1}=\frac{3\eta\kappa_{3}\kappa_{4}}{\nu}

\lambda_{2}=\frac{3\kappa_{4}}{\nu\eta}
, and
\lambda_{3}=\frac{3\zeta^{-1}M\kappa_{4}}{\nu}

\nu/q_{k}\leq\alpha_{k}

proves the result.

Now, we are prepared to present the first main result of this subsection.

Theorem 12
.

Let Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
hold.
Let

\kappa_{5}:=\frac{(\nu+\theta)^{2}(\kappa_{v}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)+4)}{2}.

(40)

\displaystyle\kappa_{6}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\eta^{2}(\nu+\theta)^{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)}{2q_{-1}^{2}}+\frac{3\eta^{2}\kappa_{3}\kappa_{4}(\nu+\theta)^{2}}{2q_{-1}b_{-1}}

(41)

\displaystyle\quad\ +\frac{3\kappa_{4}\theta^{2}(\eta^{2}+\beta_{-1}^{2}\kappa_{3})}{2\eta\nu}+\frac{3\zeta^{-1}M\kappa_{4}\theta^{2}\beta_{-1}}{2q_{-1}b_{-1}^{2}}.

Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\beta_{k-1}}{2}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{\min}(f_{-1}-f_{\min})+\|c_{-1}\|_{1}+\kappa_{5}\log(1+\kappa_{c}K/q_{-1}^{2})

(42)

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa_{6}(1+\log(\zeta^{-1}(\kappa_{H}\kappa_{u}^{2}+\zeta^{-1}M)K/b_{-1}^{2})).

In addition,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[q_{K-1}]

\displaystyle\leq q_{-1}+\frac{2}{\nu\sigma}(\tau_{\min}(f_{-1}-f_{\min})+\|c_{-1}\|_{1}+\kappa_{5}\log(1+\kappa_{c}K/q_{-1}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa_{6}\log(1+\zeta^{-1}(\kappa_{H}\kappa_{u}^{2}+\zeta^{-1}M)K/b_{-1}^{2}).

Proof.

By Lemma
5
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\phi(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},\tau_{\min})]-\phi(x_{k},\tau_{\min})

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\mathbb{E}_{k}[\alpha_{k}\Delta l(x_{k},\tau_{\min},d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})]+\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|u_{k}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}}{2}(\kappa_{v}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)+4)\|c_{k}\|_{1}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{k}[\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\nabla f(x_{k})^{T}(d_{k}-d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})].

To prove the result, we need to bound the final three terms. Starting with the first of these, we have that

\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}}{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)\|u_{k}\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\eta^{2}(\nu+\theta)^{2}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)}{2q_{-1}^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{b_{k}^{2}}\right],

where the inequality follows due to the definition of
\alpha_{k}
and
q_{k}\geq q_{-1}
. For the next term,

\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}(\kappa_{v}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)+4)}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}\leq\frac{(\nu+\theta)^{2}(\kappa_{v}(\tau_{\min}L+\Gamma)+4)}{2q_{k}^{2}}\|c_{k}\|_{1}=\frac{\kappa_{5}\|c_{k}\|_{1}}{q_{k}^{2}}.

Now, applying the result of Lemma
11
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\phi(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},\tau_{\min})]-\phi(x_{k},\tau_{\min})

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\mathbb{E}_{k}[\alpha_{k}\Delta l(x_{k},\tau_{\min},d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})]+\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\beta_{k-1}}{2}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}\right]+\frac{\kappa_{5}\|c_{k}\|_{1}}{q_{k}^{2}}+\kappa_{6}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{b_{k}^{2}}\right].

Then, applying (
16
) (where we note that under the re-definition of
d_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}
in (
30
),
\beta_{k}
is replaced by
\beta_{k-1}
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\phi(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},\tau_{\min})]-\phi(x_{k},\tau_{\min})

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\beta_{k-1}}{2}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\kappa_{5}\|c_{k}\|_{1}}{q_{k}^{2}}+\kappa_{6}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{b_{k}^{2}}\right].

Next, taking the total expectation of this inequality and summing for all
k=0,\dots,K-1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\beta_{k-1}}{2}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\phi(x_{-1},\tau_{\min})-\mathbb{E}[\phi(x_{K},\tau_{\min})]+\mathbb{E}\left[\kappa_{5}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{\|c_{k}\|_{1}}{q_{k}^{2}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\kappa_{6}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{b_{k}^{2}}\right].

By the definition of
\phi
and Assumption
1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\phi(x_{-1},\tau_{\min})-\mathbb{E}[\phi(x_{K},\tau_{\min})]

\displaystyle=\tau_{\min}f_{-1}+\|c_{-1}\|_{1}-\mathbb{E}[\tau_{\min}f_{K}-\|c_{K}\|_{1}]

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{\min}(f_{-1}-f_{\min})+\|c_{-1}\|_{1}.

Now, applying Lemma
10
twice, by Assumption
1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{\alpha_{k}\tau_{\min}\beta_{k-1}}{2}(u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}})^{T}H_{k}u_{k}^{\text{\rm true}}+\frac{\alpha_{k}\sigma}{2}\|c_{k}\|_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{\min}(f_{-1}-f_{\min})+\|c_{-1}\|_{1}+\kappa_{5}\log(1+\kappa_{c}K/q_{-1}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa_{6}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(\frac{b_{-1}^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{b_{-1}^{2}}\right)\right].

Using Jensen’s inequality, the tower rule, and the results of Lemma
2
and Lemma
6
,

4
Safeguarded Line Search

The convergence analysis in Section
3
specifies proper ranges for
\alpha_{k}
in Algorithm
1
in order to ensure convergence, but does not provide any recommendations on how to choose
\alpha_{k}
in this range. Commonly, in other stochastic SQP methods, the procedure used to set
\alpha_{k}
incorporates the merit parameter
\tau_{k}
, which is adaptively estimated at each iteration. However, the estimation of
\tau_{k}
may be highly inaccurate and noisy due to only having stochastic access to the gradient of
f
. For this reason, we do not attempt rely on the stochastic gradient information in order to choose
\alpha_{k}
and instead solely utilize the constraints.

Consider first the case where
\alpha_{k}
satisfies
\alpha_{k}\in[\nu,\nu+\theta\beta_{k}]
as it does in the analysis in Section
3.1
. Then, we can find an
\alpha_{k}
in this range through a safeguarded backtracking procedure. Starting from
\hat{\alpha}_{k}=\nu+\theta\beta_{k}
, we backtrack until

\|c(x_{k}+\hat{\alpha}_{k}d_{k})\|_{1}\leq(1-\xi\hat{\alpha}_{k})\|c_{k}\|_{1},

(50)

holds for some
\xi\in(0,1)
where, when (
50
) fails to hold for
\hat{\alpha}_{k}
, we set
\hat{\alpha}_{k}=\rho\hat{\alpha}_{k}
for some
\rho\in(0,1)
. However, as we cannot guarantee termination, we safeguard this linesearch by ceasing the search procedure if
\hat{\alpha}_{k}
ever falls below
\nu
. When (
50
) holds for some
\hat{\alpha}_{k}\geq\nu
, we set
\alpha_{k}=\hat{\alpha}_{k}
. On the other hand, if (
50
) fails to hold prior to
\hat{\alpha}_{k}<\nu
, we instead set
\alpha_{k}=\nu
. Thus, this procedure is guaranteed to output an
\alpha_{k}
in the specified range and therefore the convergence results of Section
3.1
hold. In addition, on any step where (
50
) is satisfied for
\hat{\alpha}_{k}\geq\nu
, we have confirmation of sufficient decrease in the constraint violation. Finally, we note that the number of backtracking steps at any iteration
k
is at most
\log(\nu/(\nu+\theta\beta_{k}))/\log(\rho)
due to terminating the backtracking as soon as
\hat{\alpha}_{k}<\nu
.

Unfortunately, the convergence theory only holds for the previous procedure under certain conditions on
\nu
. To relax these conditions, we once again turn to the one of the adaptive stepsize rules of Section
3.2
. In particular, we consider the case where
\beta_{k}
is chosen as a pre-specified sequence and the lower bound for
\alpha_{k}
is chosen in a manner similar to that of (
29
). As we use a slight modification of this stepsize, we give the full procedure (which is the algorithm used in the computational results of Section
5
) in Algorithm
2
.

The backtracking procedure in Algorithm
2
is very similar to the one described above, with a few minor differences. In particular, we set the lower bound adaptively, using the stepsize rule in Section
3.2
. In addition, unlike in Section
3.2
, we only update the lower bound when the backtracking procedure fails to satisfy the sufficient decrease condition prior to reducing
\hat{\alpha}_{k}
below the lower bound. The logic for this is simple; if the lower bound was reached, then it is probably too large and should be reduced. On the other hand, when the sufficient decrease condition is satisfied at iteration
k
, we keep the lower bound as it was at the start of this iteration, since it is already sufficiently small to find a good steplength in terms of reducing the constraint violation.

\alpha_{k}>1

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose and analyze a new SQP method for equality constrained optimization with a stochastic objective function. The algorithm uses a stepsize splitting scheme in order to improve upon the worst-case complexity of recently proposed stochastic SQP methods. We show that the proposed method matches the rate of convergence of a determinstic SQP method in terms of constraint violation and obtains the optimal rate for a stochastic method in terms of the gradient of the Lagrangian.

There are number of possible directions of future research. Fundamentally, this stepsize splitting scheme can be incorporated into any of the previously proposed stochastic SQP methods in the literature, including those for rank deficient Jacobians, inequality constraints, and inexact subproblem solutions. Designing new algorithms for these cases and deriving a worst-case complexity analysis are potential direction of future work.

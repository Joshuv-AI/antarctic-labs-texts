On a Blaschke-Santaló-type inequality for r r -ball bodies Keywords and phrases: Euclidean space, intrinsic volume, isoperimetric inequality, isodiametric inequality, Brunn-Minkowski inequality, intersections of congruent balls, r r -ball body, Blaschke-Santaló-type inequalities. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 52A20, 52A22.
Károly Bezdek

Abstract
Let 𝔼 d \mathbb{E}^{d} denote the d d -dimensional Euclidean space. The r r -ball body generated by a given set in 𝔼 d \mathbb{E}^{d} is the intersection of balls of radius r r centered at the points of the given set. The author [Discrete Optimization 44/1 (2022), Paper No. 100539] proved the following Blaschke-Santaló-type inequality for r r -ball bodies: for all 0 < k < d 0<k<d and for any set of given d d -dimensional volume in 𝔼 d \mathbb{E}^{d} the k k -th intrinsic volume of the r r -ball body generated by the set becomes maximal if the set is a ball. In this note we give a new proof showing also the uniqueness of the maximizer. Some applications and related questions are mentioned as well.



1
Introduction

We denote the Euclidean norm of a vector
\mathbf{p}
in the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}^{d}
,
d>1
by
|\mathbf{p}|:=\sqrt{\left<\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}\right>}
, where
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
is the
standard inner product. Let
A\subset\mathbb{E}^{d}
be a compact convex set, and

1\leq k\leq d
. We denote the
k
-th intrinsic volume of
A
by
{\rm V}_{k}\left(A\right)
. It is well known that
{\rm V}_{d}\left(A\right)
is the
d
-dimensional
volume of
A
,
2{\rm V}_{d-1}\left(A\right)
is the surface area of
A
, and
\frac{2\omega_{d-1}}{d\omega_{d}}{\rm V}_{1}\left(A\right)
is equal to the mean width of
A
, where
\omega_{d}
stands for the volume of a
d
-dimensional unit ball, that is,
\omega_{d}=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{d}{2})}
. (For a focused overview on intrinsic volumes see
[
8
]
). In this note, for simplicity
{\rm V}_{k}\left(\emptyset\right)=0
for all
1\leq k\leq d
. The closed Euclidean ball of radius
r
centered at
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{E}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{p},r]:=\{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{E}^{d}\ |\ |\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}|\leq r\}
.

Definition 1
.

For a set
\emptyset\neq X\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{d}
,
d>1
and
r>0
let the
r
-ball body

X^{r}
generated by
X
be defined by
X^{r}:=\bigcap_{\mathbf{x}\in X}\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{x},r]
.

We note that either
X^{r}=\emptyset
, or
X^{r}
is a point, or
{\rm int}(X^{r})\neq\emptyset
. Perhaps not surprisingly,
r
-ball bodies of
\mathbb{E}^{d}
have already been investigated in a number of papers however, under various names such as “überkonvexe Menge” (
[
12
]
), “
r
-convex domain” (
[
6
]
), “spindle convex set” (
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
), “ball convex set” (
[
11
]
), “hyperconvex set” (
[
7
]
), and “
r
-dual set” (
[
2
]
).
r
-ball bodies satisfy some basic identities such as
\left((X^{r})^{r}\right))^{r}=X^{r}
and
(X\cup Y)^{r}=X^{r}\cap Y^{r}
, which hold for any
X\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{d}
and
Y\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{d}
. Clearly, the operation is order-reversing namely,
X\subseteq Y\subseteq\mathbb{M}^{d}
implies
Y^{r}\subseteq X^{r}
.
In this note we investigate volumetric relations between
X^{r}
and
X
in
\mathbb{E}^{d}
. First, recall the theorem of Gao, Hug, and Schneider
[
8
]
stating that for any convex body of given volume in
\mathbb{S}^{d}
the volume of the spherical polar body is maximal if the convex body is a ball. The author has proved the following Euclidean analogue of their theorem in
[
2
]
. Let
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{E}^{d}
,
d>1
be a compact set of volume
V_{d}(\mathbf{A})>0
and
r>0
. If
\mathbf{B}\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{d}
is a ball with
V_{d}(\mathbf{A})=V_{d}(\mathbf{B})
, then

V_{d}(\mathbf{A}^{r})\leq V_{d}(\mathbf{B}^{r}).

(1)

As the theorem of Gao, Hug, and Schneider
[
8
]
is often called a spherical counterpart of the Blaschke–Santaló inequality, therefore one may refer to (
1
) as a Blaschke–Santaló-type inequality for
r
-ball bodies in
\mathbb{E}^{d}
. Next recall that (
1
) has been extended by the author to spherical as well as hyperbolic spaces (
[
2
]
) and then to intrinsic volumes (
[
4
]
) proving the following Blaschke–Santaló-type inequality for intrinsic volumes of
r
-ball bodies in
\mathbb{E}^{d}
without precise equality condition, which we included here. (See
[
3
]
for the core ideas behind Theorem
1
and Theorem 3.1 in
[
13
]
for a randomized version.)

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{E}^{d}
,
d>1
be a compact set of volume
V_{d}(\mathbf{A})>0
and
r>0
. If
\mathbf{B}\subset\mathbb{E}^{d}
is a ball with
V_{d}(\mathbf{A})=V_{d}(\mathbf{B})
, then

V_{k}(\mathbf{A}^{r})\leq V_{k}(\mathbf{B}^{r})

(2)

holds for all
1\leq k\leq d
with equality if and only if
\mathbf{A}
is congruent (i.e., isometric) to
\mathbf{B}
.

Fodor, Kurusa, and Vígh
[
7
]
have proved the following inequality for
k=d
, which we extended to other intrinsic volumes as well. Corollary
2
follows from Theorem
1
, the homogeneity (of degree
k
) of
k
-th intrinsic volume, and from the observation that
f(x)=x^{k}(r-x)^{k}
,
0\leq x\leq r
has a unique maximum value at
x=\frac{r}{2}
for any
d>1
,
1\leq k\leq d
, and
r>0
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
d>1
,
1\leq k\leq d
,
r>0
, and
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{E}^{d}
be an
r
-ball body. Set
P_{k}(\mathbf{A}):=V_{k}(\mathbf{A})V_{k}(\mathbf{A}^{r})
. Then

P_{k}(\mathbf{A})\leq P_{k}\left(\mathbf{B}^{d}\left[\mathbf{o},\frac{r}{2}\right]\right)

(3)

with equality if and only if
\mathbf{A}
is congruent to
\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{o},\frac{r}{2}]
.

As a further application we mention that Theorem
1
has been used in
[
4
]
(see also
[
2
]
) to prove the long-standing Kneser-Poulsen conjecture for uniform contractions of intersections of sufficiently many congruent balls. We close this section with the following complementary question to Theorem
1
, which seems to be new and open, and can be regarded as a Mahler-type problem for
r
-ball bodies.

Problem 3
.

Let
d>2
,
1\leq k\leq d
, and
0<v<r^{d}\omega_{d}=V_{d}(\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{o},r])
. Find the minimum of
V_{k}(\mathbf{A}^{r})
for all
r
-ball bodies
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{E}^{d}
of given volume
v=V_{d}(\mathbf{A})
.

Remark 4
.

Problem
3
for
d=2
can be answered as follows. Let
0<v<\pi r^{2}
. Then the minimum of
V_{1}(\mathbf{A}^{r})
(resp.,
V_{2}(\mathbf{A}^{r})
) for all
r
-disk domains
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{E}^{2}
of given area
v=V_{2}(\mathbf{A})
is attained only for
r
-lenses, which are intersections of two disks of radius
r
.

In the rest of this note we give a short proof for Theorem
1
(which uses the Brunn-Minkowski inequality and the isoperimetric inequality instead of the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality applied in
[
4
]
) and derive Remark
4
.

2
Proof of Theorem
1

Clearly, if
\mathbf{B}^{r}=\emptyset
, then
\mathbf{A}^{r}=\emptyset
and (
2
) follows. Similarly, it is easy to see that if
\mathbf{B}^{r}
is a point in
\mathbb{E}^{d}
, then (
2
) follows. Hence, we may assume that
\mathbf{B}^{r}=\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{o},R]
and
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{o},r-R]
with
0<R<r
, where
\mathbf{o}
denotes the origin in
\mathbb{E}^{d}
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\emptyset\neq K\subset\mathbb{E}^{d}
,
d>1
and
r>0
. Then the
r
-ball convex hull

{\rm conv}_{r}K
of
K
is defined by

{\rm conv}_{r}K:=\bigcap\{\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{x},r]\ |\ K\subseteq\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{x},r]\}.

Moreover, let the
r
-ball convex hull of
\mathbb{E}^{d}
be
\mathbb{E}^{d}
. Furthermore, we say that
K\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{d}
is
r
-ball convex
if
K={\rm conv}_{r}K
.

Remark 5
.

We note that clearly,
{\rm conv}_{r}K=\emptyset
if and only if
K^{r}=\emptyset
. Moreover,
\emptyset\neq K\subset\mathbb{E}^{d}
is
r
-ball convex if and only if
K
is an
r
-ball body.

We need Lemma 5 of
[
2
]
stated as

Lemma 6
.

If
\emptyset\neq K\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{d}
,
d>1
and
r>0
, then
K^{r}=({\rm conv}_{r}K)^{r}
.

Now, via Lemma
6
we may assume that
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{E}^{d}
is an
r
-ball body of volume
V_{d}(\mathbf{A})>0
and
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{o},r-R]
with
0<R<r
such that
V_{d}(\mathbf{A})=V_{d}(\mathbf{B})
.
Next, recall Proposition 2.5 of
[
3
]
which we state as

Lemma 7
.

Let
d>1
and
r>0
. If
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{E}^{d}
is an
r
-ball body, then
\mathbf{A}+(-\mathbf{A}^{r})=\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{o},r]
, where
+
denotes the Minkowski sum.

Thus, the Brunn-Minkowski inequality for intrinsic volumes (
[
9
]
, Eq. (74)) and Lemma
7
imply

V_{k}(\mathbf{A})^{\frac{1}{k}}+V_{k}(\mathbf{A}^{r})^{\frac{1}{k}}=V_{k}(\mathbf{A})^{\frac{1}{k}}+V_{k}(-\mathbf{A}^{r})^{\frac{1}{k}}\leq V_{k}(\mathbf{A}+(-\mathbf{A}^{r}))^{\frac{1}{k}}=V_{k}(\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{o},r])^{\frac{1}{k}}

(4)

with equality if and only if (
\mathbf{A}
and
-\mathbf{A}^{r}
are homothetic, i.e.,)
\mathbf{A}
is congruent to
\mathbf{B}
, where
1\leq k\leq d
. Finally, (
4
), the isoperimetric inequality for intrinsic volumes stating that among convex bodies of given volume the balls have the smallest
k
-th intrinsic volume (
[
14
]
, Section 7.4), and the homogeneity (of degree
k
) of
k
-th intrinsic volume imply

V_{k}(\mathbf{A}^{r})^{\frac{1}{k}}\leq V_{k}(\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{o},r])^{\frac{1}{k}}-V_{k}(\mathbf{A})^{\frac{1}{k}}\leq V_{k}(\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{o},r])^{\frac{1}{k}}-V_{k}(\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{o},r-R])^{\frac{1}{k}}

=V_{k}(\mathbf{B}^{d}[\mathbf{o},R])^{\frac{1}{k}}=V_{k}(\mathbf{B}^{r})^{\frac{1}{k}},

with
V_{k}(\mathbf{A}^{r})^{\frac{1}{k}}=V_{k}(\mathbf{B}^{r})^{\frac{1}{k}}
if and only if
\mathbf{A}
is congruent to
\mathbf{B}
, where
1\leq k\leq d
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.

3
Proof of Remark
4

Let
0<v<\pi r^{2}
. Let
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{E}^{2}
be an
r
-disk domain of area
v=V_{2}(\mathbf{A})
. Lemma
7
implies that

V_{1}(\mathbf{A})+V_{1}(\mathbf{A}^{r})=\pi r.

(5)

Next, according to the reverse isoperimetric inequality of
r
-disk domains, which has been proved by Borisenko and Drach
[
5
]
(see
[
7
]
for a different proof without uniqueness of the extremal set), we have that

V_{1}(\mathbf{A})\leq V_{1}(\mathbf{L}),

(6)

where
\mathbf{L}
denotes the
r
-lens of area
v
in
\mathbb{E}^{2}
. Moreover, equality holds in (
6
) if and only if
\mathbf{A}
is conguent to
\mathbf{L}
. Thus, (
5
) and (
6
) imply that

\pi r-V_{1}(\mathbf{L})\leq V_{1}(\mathbf{A}^{r})
. It follows via (
5
) that

V_{1}(\mathbf{L}^{r})=\pi r-V_{1}(\mathbf{L})\leq V_{1}(\mathbf{A}^{r})

(7)

with equality if and only if
\mathbf{A}
is congruent to
\mathbf{L}
. Finally, observe that (
6
) is equivalent to the statement that if
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
is an
r
-disk domain and
\mathbf{L}^{\prime}
is an
r
-lens with
V_{1}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})=V_{1}(\mathbf{L}^{\prime})
, then

V_{2}(\mathbf{L}^{\prime})\leq V_{2}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})

(8)

with equality if and only if
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
is conguent to
\mathbf{L}^{\prime}
. Hence, (
7
) combined with (
8
) yields

V_{2}(\mathbf{L}^{r})\leq V_{2}(\mathbf{A}^{r})

(9)

with equality if and only if
\mathbf{A}
is congruent to
\mathbf{L}
. This completes the proof of Remark
4
.

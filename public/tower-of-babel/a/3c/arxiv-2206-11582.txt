Wrapped sutured Legendrian homology and unit conormal of local 2-braids
Côme Dattin

Abstract
We extend the sutured framework to the case of Legendrians with boundary. Using ideas from Lagrangian Floer theory, we define the cylindrical and the wrapped sutured Legendrian homologies of a pair of sutured Legendrians. They fit together into an exact sequence, and the exact triangle is invariant along an Legendrian isotopy fixed at the boundary. For a single Legendrian, we also define a wrapped version of its Chekanov-Eliashberg dga. Our main example of sutured Legendrian is obtained via the unit conormal construction : a submanifold N ⊂ M N\subset M , such that ∂ N ⊂ ∂ M \partial N\subset\partial M , induces a sutured Legendrian Λ N ⊂ S ​ T ∗ ​ M \Lambda_{N}\subset ST^{*}M , thus we get smooth invariants of manifolds with boundary. As a simple application, we show that if the conormals of two local 2-braids are isotopic (as Legendrians with fixed boundary), then the braids are equivalent.



In the literature, contact manifolds are usually assumed to be either closed
[
Par19
]
, or the contactisation of some Liouville domain
[
EES07
]
. A few years ago the notion of
sutured contact manifold
, which generalise both of those settings, was introduced by Colin-Ghiggini-Honda-Hutchings in
[
CGHH11
]
, where they define the contact homology of such manifolds. In this paper we introduce the notion of
sutured Legendrian
and, adapting ideas from Lagrangian Floer theory, we define the
cylindrical
and the
wrapped sutured Legendrian homology
of a pair. They fit together into an exact sequence, and the induced exact triangle yields an invariant of sutured Legendrians with fixed boundary.

We illustrate this construction with an example obtained from the smooth geometry. Starting with a submanifold
N\subset M
, such that
\partial N\subset\partial M
, we construct a sutured contact manifold from
ST^{*}M
, as in
[
Dat22
]
. Then the unit conormal of
N
induces a sutured Legendrian, thus the previous exact triangle yields an invariant of
N
, seen as a submanifold with fixed boundary.

As a simple application, we show that if two local 2-braids have unit conormals which are isotopic as Legendrians with fixed boundary, then the two braids are equivalent. This can be seen as a relative version of the analogous result for knots from
[
She19
]
[
ENS17
]
(see also
[
Dat22
]
for a different point of view), in a much simpler case. The general result, which requires more technology, is only sketched and will be the subject of subsequent work.

Sutured setting

Sutured contact manifold.

Originally introduced in the topological world by Gabai
[
Gab83
]
as a tool to study 3-manifolds, the definition was adapted to the contact setting in
[
CGHH11
]
, as a way to handle contact manifolds with convex boundary. Let us briefly present those objects.

Loosely speaking, a (balanced, completed) sutured contact manifold
(V,\xi=\ker\lambda)
is a non-compact contact manifold which, at infinity, looks like the contactisation of some Liouville domain
(W,\beta)
:

(V,\lambda)\simeq(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times W,dt+\beta)\qquad\qquad\text{ away from a compact.}

More precisely, a sutured contact manifold
(V,\xi)
is a compact manifold with boundary and corners, such that

\partial V=W_{+}\cup[-1,1]\times\Gamma\cup W_{-},

where
W_{\pm}
are two Liouville domains with common contact boundary
\Gamma
. Moreover the contact structure must satisfy some properties on a neighbourhood of the boundary, see
section

2.1
for a detailed definition.
The contact manifold
(\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma})=(\partial W_{\pm},\beta_{\pm})
is called the
suture
, and can be seen as a codimension 2 contact submanifold in
(V,\xi)
.
Following
[
CGHH11
]
, we can complete
V
into a non-compact contact manifold, and if
(V,\xi)
is
balanced
, ie
(W_{+},\beta_{+})\simeq(W_{-},\beta_{-})
, we recover the previous loose description.

Note that, contrary to the compact case, we specify a contact form, and not only the contact structure, because we want some control at infinity. As a consequence of those constraints, it is proven in
[
CGHH11
]
that the contact homology of such a manifold is well-defined (after picking a well-behaved almost complex structure).

Let us give some examples : first, while the trivialisation of a Liouville domain is a trivial case, applying any operation which only affects a compact (for example surgeries) yields a sutured contact manifold.
As a second source of examples, it is shown in
[
CGHH11
]
that given a contact manifold with convex boundary, as defined in
[
Gir91
]
, one can construct a sutured contact manifold, and vice-versa. In particular, the complement of a standard neighbourhood of a Legendrian (in a closed contact manifold) yields a sutured contact manifold.
Finally, and as alluded to above, the unit bundle of a manifold
M
with boundary has convex boundary. Thus, applying the previous construction, we get a a sutured contact manifold which is, at infinity, the contactisation of
T^{*}(\partial M)
. In particular, the suture is
ST^{*}(\partial M)
, see
section

2.5
for more details (as well as
[
Dat22
]
).

Sutured Legendrian.

We now add a Legendrian submanifold to the picture, which is also non-compact but will behave nicely at infinity. Roughly speaking, a sutured Legendrian
\Lambda\subset(V,\lambda)
is a Legendrian which, at infinity, is
cylindrical
and
centered
:

(\Lambda,V,\lambda)\simeq(\mathbb{R}\times W,\{0\}\times\Lambda_{\Gamma},dt+\beta)\qquad\qquad\text{ away from a compact,}

where
\Lambda_{\Gamma}\subset\Gamma
is a Legendrian submanifold, which will also be denoted
\partial\Lambda
. One should think about this definition as generalising the notion of exact cylindrical Lagrangian in a Liouville domain. Indeed, any such submanifold lift to a sutured Legendrian in the contactisation.
As mentioned above, the main source of example will be applying the unit conormal construction to a submanifold
N\subset M
with boundary
\partial N\subset\partial M
, such that
N
intersect
\partial M
transversely. We get a sutured Legendrian
\Lambda_{N}
, so that

(\Lambda_{N},ST^{*}M)\simeq(\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda_{\partial N},\mathbb{R}\times T^{*}(\partial M))\qquad\qquad\text{ away from a compact,}

where
\Lambda_{N}\subset ST^{*}(\partial M)\simeq\partial T^{*}(\partial M)
is the unit conormal of
\partial N
in
\partial M
.

Main results

We first adapt the convex-sutured construction from
[
CGHH11
]
to the Legendrian case : given a Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda\subset(V,\xi)
, such that

\bullet

\Lambda
is transverse to the boundary of
V
,

\bullet

\partial V
is W-convex (meaning that both its positive and negative region admit a Weinstein structure),

\bullet

there is a contact vector field
X
, transverse to the boundary, whose associated dividing set contains
\partial\Lambda
,

we construct a sutured contact manifold containing a sutured Legendrian. Thus all invariants of sutured Legendrian defined below will be invariants of Legendrians inside contact manifolds with convex boundary (such that
\partial\Lambda
is in the dividing set).

Sutured Legendrian homologies

We define several invariants of sutured Legendrian, mimicking constructions from Lagrangian Floer theory. The main difference with
[
CGHH11
]
is that the Legendrian is non-compact, and that Reeb chords will not be contained in a compact. To constrain the relevant holomorphic curves, we rely on several maximum principles for curves with Lagrangian boundary conditions, where both the contact manifolds and the Legendrians inducing the boundary condition are non-compact. Nevertheless, for a well-behaved almost complex structure, we can still control the curves so that Gromov’s compactness theorem holds
[
Gro85
]
.

Cylindrical sutured homology.

Given a sutured Legendrian
\Lambda\subset(V,\lambda)
, consider the dga
\mathcal{L}C(\Lambda,V,\lambda)
generated by Reeb chords, or the complex
LC(\Lambda,V,\lambda)
if some additional assumptions are satisfied (we assume that the contact form is hypertight so we can ignore Reeb orbits). The differential counts holomorphic disks in the symplectisation of
V
, and with boundary on
\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda
. Although both
\Lambda
and
V
are non-compact, this indeed defines a differential, and the homology is an invariant of the sutured Legendrian.

Theorem 1
.

The homology
\mathcal{L}H(\Lambda,V,\lambda)
is well-defined and independent of the choices. Moreover it is invariant under sutured Legendrian isotopy.

We emphasise that, although the boundary
\partial\Lambda
can move during the isotopy, the sutured Legendrian must stay cylindrical (and in a
t
-level) at infinity.

Wrapped sutured homology.

This version is only defined for a pair
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}\subset(V,\lambda)
of disjoint sutured Legendrians (here we assume that they are both without contractible chord). Pushing the first one by a contact vector field induced by a function quadratic at infinity (and vanishing on the compact part), we get a Legendrian
\Lambda_{0}^{\mathcal{W}}
which is not sutured anymore. Note that when
V
is a contactisation, and
\Lambda_{0}
is the lift of an exact Lagrangian
L_{0}
, the wrapped Legendrian
\Lambda_{0}^{\mathcal{W}}
can be seen as the lift of the Lagrangian obtained by pushing
L
via an Hamiltonian quadratic at infinity.

With this procedure, we have created Reeb chords going from
\Lambda_{0}
to
\Lambda_{1}
, which correspond to chords between the boundaries in the suture :

\mathcal{C}(\Lambda_{0}^{\mathcal{W}},\Lambda_{1};V,\lambda)=\mathcal{C}(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1};V,\lambda)\cup\mathcal{C}(\partial\Lambda_{0},\partial\Lambda_{1};\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma}).

Denote
\mathcal{W}LC(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1};V,\lambda)
the complex generated by those chords, where the differential counts holomorphic strips in the symplectisation of
V
, with boundary on
\mathbb{R}\times(\Lambda_{0}^{\mathcal{W}}\cup\Lambda_{1})
. While chords can now form a non-compact set, this still defines a differential, and the homology is invariant in the following sense

Theorem 2
.

The homology
\mathcal{W}LH(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1};V,\lambda)
is well-defined and independent of the choices. Moreover it is invariant under a sutured Legendrian isotopy
(\Lambda_{0}^{u},\Lambda_{1}^{u})_{u\in[0,1]}
such that

\bullet

for all
u
,
\Lambda_{0}^{u}
and
\Lambda_{1}^{u}
are disjoints ;

\bullet

\Lambda_{0}^{u}
is cylindrical, included in the level
\{t=0\}
;

\bullet

\Lambda_{1}^{u}
is cylindrical, included in the level
\{t=t_{u}\leq 0\}
, with
t_{u}=0
for
u\in\{0,1\}
.

We emphasise that, since
\Lambda_{0}
can now pass below
\Lambda_{1}
during the isotopy, a
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-family of Reeb chords can appear in the non-compact part (for some
u\neq 0,1
).

Wrapping the Chekanov-Eliashberg dga.

For a single sutured Legendrian
\Lambda\subset(V,\lambda)
, we can also define a wrapped theory by adopting a different strategy. By modifying the contact form into
\tilde{\lambda}
, we create two families of Reeb chords, in cancelling positions, and once again each family is in bijection with the chords of the boundary
\mathcal{C}(\partial\Lambda,\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma})
. Picking an appropriate almost complex structure, we construct a holomorphic foliation in the symplectisation (near those families), which implies that one of those families form a sub-dga.

Theorem 4
.

Consider a sutured Legendrian
\Lambda\subset(V,\lambda)
, and assume that
(V,\lambda)
is S-sutured and
(\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma})
is hypertight. Then there is an inclusion of dga

\mathcal{L}C(\partial\Lambda;\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}C(\Lambda;V,\tilde{\lambda}),

where the homology of the boundary has
H_{1}(\Lambda)
coefficients, induced by
\partial\Lambda\subset\Lambda
.
In particular, an augmentation on
\mathcal{L}C(\Lambda;V,\lambda)
induces an augmentation on the dga of the boundary.

\Lambda^{u}
of sutured Legendrians fixed on the boundary yields a commutative diagram

The wrapped Chekanov-Eliashberg dga is obtained via quotienting by the generators of this sub-dga (when the boundary
\partial\Lambda\subset(\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma})
is hypertight). Although no actual wrapping happens in that construction, this quotient corresponds, at least morally, to adding chords at infinity which cancel out with the generators of the sub-dga.

Invariant of local 2-braids

As mentioned previously, the unit conormal of a submanifold
N\subset M
, with boundary
\partial N\subset\partial M
, is a rich source of sutured Legendrians. For example, the conormal of a braid
B\subset[-1,1]\times\Sigma
, where
\Sigma
is a fixed surface, is a collection of cylinders, whose boundaries are fibers in
\Gamma=ST^{*}(\Sigma\sqcup\Sigma)
.
For very simple braids (namely, the braid has only 2 strands and projects to a small disk in
\Sigma
), the previous exact triangle is a complete invariant :

Theorem 5
.

If
B,B^{\prime}\subset[-1,1]\times\Sigma
are two local 2-braids, such that
\Lambda_{B}
and
\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}
are isotopic as sutured Legendrians with fixed boundary, then the braids are equivalent :

\Lambda_{B}\sim\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\text{ rel }\partial\Rightarrow B\sim B^{\prime}.

Note that, if one allows more general isotopies, the unit conormals become equivalents :

\bullet

as smooth manifolds with fixed boundary, two strands can cross each other, so we have
\Lambda_{B}\overset{C^{\infty}}{\sim}\Lambda_{B^{\prime}}\text{ rel }\partial
;

\bullet

as sutured Legendrians with moving boundary, we can unbraid everything so the conormals are again isotopic.

To prove this result, we compute the exact sequence of the pair of sutured Legendrians, and use homotopical restrictions to show that this is invariant in the sense of
3
. The computation relies on the fact that, because our braids are local, we can compute holomorphic curves in the neighbourhood of a constant strand, which is contactomorphic to
J^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times S^{1})
. In that 1-jet space, the sutured Legendrians become conical at infinity, and wrapping corresponds to a modification creating critical points, which is reminiscent of the construction in
[
PR20
]
.

Relation to other works

Lagrangian Floer homology.

The sutured setting is a straight forward generalisation of Floer theory. Indeed, any exact immersed Lagrangian in a Liouville domain
(W,\beta)
lifts to a sutured Legendrian, and intersection points lifts to Reeb chords. Moreover, by
[
DR16
]
the holomorphic curves in
W
and in the symplectisation of
V=\mathbb{R}\times W
are also in bijection, so the (wrapped) sutured Legendrian homology of a pair of hypertight Legendrians
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}\subset V
is isomorphic (on the chain level) to the (wrapped) positive Lagrangian Floer homology of their Lagrangians projections
L_{0},L_{1}\subset W
:

LC(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1};V)\simeq FC^{+}(L_{0},L_{1};W)\qquad\mathcal{W}LC(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1};V)\simeq\mathcal{W}FC^{+}(L_{0},L_{1};W).

Note that an holomorphic curve in
W
going from a intersection point of positive action, to one of negative action, lifts to a banana curve in the symplectisation (ie a curves with two positive asymptotics). Thus to recover the full Lagrangian Floer homology from the sutured setting, one should consider a Rabinowitz-type complex, generated by chords going from
\Lambda_{0}
to
\Lambda_{1}
as well as from
\Lambda_{1}
to
\Lambda_{0}
, and where the differential counts those bananas.

Conical Legendrians.

In
[
PR20
]
, sutured Legendrians in the contactisation of a standard ball
(D^{2n},\beta)
, where the Liouville vector field is outgoing, are studied using the contactomorphism
(\mathbb{R}\times D^{2n},dt+\beta)\simeq(\mathbb{R}\times T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n},dt-p\cdot dq)
. This turns a sutured Legendrian into the 1-jet of a conical function (or, more generally, a mutisection). By adding critical points in cancelling position, and studying Morse flow trees, they obtain the map from
4
.

The unit conormal of a knot.

As stated previously, our result for braids is a relative version of an analogous theorem for knots, proved in
[
She19
]
[
ENS17
]
: given two knots
K,K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, if
\Lambda_{K}\simeq\Lambda_{K^{\prime}}
as Legendrians in
ST^{*}\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then
K\simeq K^{\prime}
(up to mirror). The strategy of those papers is to use Legendrian invariants to recover a classical complete invariant (namely, the group knot with its peripheral subgroup). We also mention another point of view due to the author
[
Dat22
]
, based on an idea of
[
CGHH11
]
. For
K\subset M
an hyperbolic knot, consider the sutured contact manifold obtained by removing a standard neighbourhood of
\Lambda_{K}\subset ST^{*}M
, and applying the convex-sutured construction. Then the (sutured) Legendrian homology of a unit fiber, with its product structure, recover the knot group
\pi_{1}(M\setminus K)
.

Sutured manifolds and stops.

If we restrict to balanced sutured manifolds, there is a correspondence with the stopped perspective introduced in
[
Syl19
]
[
GPS19
]
[
AE21
]
. More precisely, one can go from one of the following object to the other

\bullet

a balanced sutured contact manifold, whose horizontal boundaries are both
(W,\beta)
;

\bullet

a contact manifold with (smooth) convex boundary, whose positive and negative regions are both
(W,\beta)
, and with dividing set contactomorphic to the suture ;

\bullet

a closed contact manifold containing a stop
W
, ie a submanifold of codimension
1
which is a Liouville domain.

The equivalence between the first two objects is due to
[
CGHH11
]
. For a more detailed discussion regarding the relation with the stopped point of view, we refer to
[
Dat22
, §2.4]
.

Contact manifolds and Liouville cobordisms

We recall some classical definitions from contact and symplectic geometry. All objects are assumed smooths.

1.1
Symplectic and contact manifolds

A
symplectic manifold

(W,\omega)
is an even-dimensional manifold
W^{2n}
, oriented, with a closed 2-form
\omega
such that
\omega^{n}>0
. In other words
\omega
is non-degenerate : for any
v\in T_{x}W,(\iota_{v}\omega)_{x}\neq 0
.

A
contact manifold

(V,\xi)
an odd-dimensional manifold
V^{2n+1}
, oriented, endowed with a
contact structure

\xi
, ie a cooriented hyperplane field maximally non-integrable. In other words there exists a 1-form
\lambda
such that
\ker\lambda=\xi
, and satisfying the contact condition
\lambda\wedge d\lambda^{n}>0
. Such a form will be called a contact form.
Note that if
\lambda_{0}
is a contact form for
\xi
, all other contact forms can be written
\lambda=f\lambda_{0}
, with
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}
.

Its
Reeb vector field

R_{\lambda}
is defined by
i_{R_{\lambda}}d\lambda=0
and
\lambda(R_{\lambda})=1
. Note that if
f\lambda
is another contact form, the Reeb vector fields are related by

R_{f\lambda}=\frac{1}{f^{2}}\left(fR_{\lambda}+v\right)

where
v\in\xi
is such that
\iota_{v}d\lambda_{\restriction\xi}=df_{\restriction\xi}
.

If
(V,\lambda)
is contact, its
symplectisation
is

\text{Symp}(V)=(\mathbb{R}\times V,\omega=d(e^{s}\lambda)).

We also define the positive symplectisation
\text{Symp}_{+}=\{s\geq 0\}
and the negative symplectisation
\text{Symp}_{-}=\{s\leq 0\}
.
If
(W,\omega=d\beta)
is an exact symplectic manifold, ie
\omega=d\beta
for some
1
-form
\beta
, its
contactisation
is

\text{Cont}(W)=(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times W,\lambda=dt+\beta).

Similarly, the
S^{1}
-contactisation of
W
is
\text{Cont}_{S^{1}}(W)=(S^{1}_{\theta}\times W,d\theta+\beta)
.

.

Proposition 1.3

(Standards neighbourhood)
.

Consider a point
p\in(V,\xi)
. There exists a neighbourhood of
p
and coordinates
(z,x_{i},y_{i})
such that
\xi=\ker(dz-x_{i}dy_{i})
.

Let
\Lambda\subset(V,\xi)
be a Legendrian. Then a neighbourhood of

is contactomorphic to a neighbourhood of the zero section
0_{\Lambda}=\{0\}\times\Lambda\subset(J^{1}(\Lambda),\xi_{\text{st}})
.

Let

be a Lagrangian. Then a neighbourhood of

is symplectomorphic to a neighbourhood of the zero section

.

Proposition 1.4

(Moser for Legendrians)
.

[

Let
\Lambda_{t}\subset(V,\xi_{t})
be a path of Legendrians. Then there exists a path of diffeomorphisms
\phi_{t}

\phi_{t}^{*}\xi_{t}=\xi_{0}\text{ and }\phi_{t}^{*}(\Lambda_{t})=\Lambda_{0}\ (\text{ie }\phi_{t}(\Lambda_{0})=\Lambda_{t}).

1.2
Liouville domains

A
Liouville domain

(W,\beta)
is a manifold endowed with a 1-form such that :

\bullet

W
is a compact manifold (with boundary) ;

\bullet

(W,d\beta)
is symplectic ;

\bullet

the Liouville vector field
Y
, defined by
\iota_{Y}d\beta=\beta
, is positively transverse to the boundary.

A
Liouville cobordism

(W,\beta)
is a manifold endowed with a 1-form such that

\bullet

(W,d\beta)
is symplectic.

\bullet

The boundary of
W
splits into
\partial W=\partial_{+}W\sqcup\partial_{-}W
, such that the Liouville vector field is positively (resp. negatively) transverse to
\partial_{+}W
(resp.
\partial_{-}W
).

We will then say that
W
is a Liouville cobordism from
(\partial_{+}W,\beta_{\restriction\partial_{+}W})
to
(\partial_{-}W,\beta_{\restriction\partial_{-}W})
. In particular a Liouville domain is a cobordism from
(\partial W,\beta_{\restriction\partial W})
to the emptyset.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
(W,\beta)
be a Liouville cobordism. Then the boundary
(\partial_{+}W,\beta_{\restriction\partial_{+}W})
is contact, and there exists a neighbourhood

(\mathcal{N}(\partial_{+}W),\beta)\simeq\big((-\varepsilon,0]_{r}\times\partial W,e^{r}\beta_{\restriction\partial W}\big)

In other words, a neighbourhood of the positive boundary is symplectomorphic to (a neighbourhood of the boundary of) the negative symplectisation of
\partial_{+}W
. Similarly, a neighbourhood of the negative boundary is symplectomorphic to a positive symplectisation.

The
completion
of a Liouville cobordism
(W,\beta)
, denoted
(\hat{W},\hat{\beta})
, is obtained by gluing the positive (resp. negative) symplectisation of
\partial_{+}W
(resp.
\partial_{-}W
) via the previous coordinates :
\hat{W}=W\cup(\mathbb{R}^{\pm}_{r}\times\partial_{\pm}W,e^{r}\beta_{\restriction\partial_{\pm}W})
.

Remark 1.6
.

[
Cou12
]
The symplectisations of discincts contact manifolds can sometimes be symplectomorphic, thus it might be more natural the directly work with completed manifolds.

Examples 1.7
.

The disk
(D^{2n},-x_{i}dy_{i})
is a Liouville domain, its Liouville vector field is radial and its completion is
\mathbb{R}^{2}n
.
For a smooth manifold
M
, its cotangent
(D^{*}M,-p\cdot dq)
is a Liouville domain, its Liouville vector field is
p.\partial_{p}
, and its completion
T^{*}M
.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
L\subset(W,\beta)
be a Lagrangian immersed in a Liouville cobordism. It is exact if it satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions :

\bullet

\beta_{\restriction TL}
is an exact 1-form, ie
\beta_{\restriction TL}=df
, and
f
is constant at near the boundary

\bullet

L
lifts to a Legendrian in the contactisation of
W
.

The boundary of such a Lagrangian are Legendrians submanifolds
\partial_{\pm}\subset\partial_{\pm}W
, and
L
will be called an exact Lagrangian cobordism from
\partial_{+}L
to
\partial_{-}L
.

1.3
From a Legendrian isotopy to an exact cobordism

Let
(\lambda_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
be a path of contact forms on a manifold
V
. Then there exists a Liouville cobordism from
(V,C\lambda_{1})
to
(V,\lambda_{0})
, for
C\in\mathbb{R}
big enough. To construct it, choose a function
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{s}\rightarrow[0,1]
increasing, vanishing for
s<<0
and evaluating to
1
for
s>>0
. Then if
\psi^{\prime}
is small enough, the manifold

(W,\beta)=(\mathbb{R}_{s}\times V,e^{s}\lambda_{\psi(s)})

is a Liouville domain. Indeed,

d\beta=e^{s}(d\lambda_{\psi(s)}+ds\wedge\lambda_{\psi(s)}+\psi^{\prime}(s)ds\wedge\partial_{s}\lambda_{\psi(s)})

and the symplectic condition is open.

More generally, if
\Lambda_{s}\subset(V,\lambda_{s}),s\in[0,1]
is a path of Legendrians, we can construct an exact Lagrangian cobordism :

Lemma 1.9
.

There exists a Liouville cobordism
(W,\beta)
, as well as an exact Lagrangian cobordism
L\subset(W,\beta)
, going from
\Lambda_{S}\subset(V,C\lambda_{S})
to
\Lambda_{0}\subset(V,\lambda_{0})
(for
C
big enough).

Proof.

Contrary to the case of contact forms, the graph of a Legendrian isotopy does
not
directly define a Lagrangian.
However we can modify the Liouville form to make it work, as in
[
Ekh08
, Appendix A]
: if
\Lambda_{s}\subset(V,\lambda)
is induced by the flow of
X_{s}
, associated to
f_{s}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, then the graph of this path

L=\{(s,x),x\in\Lambda_{s}\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{s}\times V

in an exact Lagrangian for the Liouville form

\beta=e^{s}(\lambda_{s}-df)

where
f(s,x)=f_{s}(x)
. Once again one might have to slow down the isotopy so that this is indeed a Liouville Form. Note we have

\beta_{\restriction TL}=d(e^{s}f)
.
∎

Remark 1.10
.

Another construction is also possible, using Hamiltonian vector fields, see
[
Cha10
]

[
CCDR19
]
for more details. Consider the cobordism
(\mathbb{R}\times V,e^{s}\mu_{s}\lambda_{0})
, where
\mu_{s}
is given by
1.2
(as previously, one might have to slow down the isotopy to get a Liouville form). Choose a time-dependent Hamiltonian
H_{u}
, vanishing at
-\infty
and given by
e^{s}f_{u}(x)
for
s\gg 0
. Then
\phi_{H}(\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda_{0})
is the desired exact cobordism.

1.4
Stein and Weinstein manifolds

A
Weinstein manifold

(W,\beta,f)
is a Liouville domain, with a Morse function
f
such that :

\bullet

the Liouville vector field
Y
is a pseudo-gradient of
f
:
\forall x\notin\text{Crit}(f),df_{x}(Z(x))>0
;

\bullet

the boundary of
W
is a regular level of
f
.

Note that
f
can be extended to the completion
\hat{W}
without adding critical points.

The skeleton of the manifold, defined by
\text{Core}(W)=\underset{t>0}{\cap}\phi_{Y}^{-t}(W)
,
is then the union of the
Y
-stables manifolds of the critical points of
f
, which are isotropic and consequently of dimension smaller than
n
(see
[
CE12
]
and
[
EG91
]
).

Remark 1.11
.

Although the skeleton is defined for any Liouville domain, it can a priori be of codimension 1 as noticed in
[
McD91
]
.

The pair
(\hat{W},J_{0})
is a
Stein manifold
if
J_{0}
is an integrable complex structure, ie if
(W,J_{0})
can be properly embedded in
(\mathbb{C}^{N},i)
. It is equivalent to the existence of a function
\phi:\hat{W}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
which is proper, bounded below, and strictly pluri-sub-harmonic, ie such that
\Delta_{J}\phi:=-d(d\phi\circ J)
is a symplectic form.

In particular
\beta=-d\phi\circ J
is a Liouville form, however
J
might not be adapted in the sens of
section

3.2
, because the level
\phi
are a priori different from the levels of the Liouville vector field, see
[
CGHH11
, §3.2]
.

Remark 1.12
.

A Stein manifold is automatically Weinstein :
\beta=-d\phi\circ J_{0}
is a Liouville form, and the corresponding Liouville vector field is the negative gradient of
\phi
. Conversely, Weinstein manifolds can be deformed into Stein manifolds, see
[
CE12
, Theorème 13.9]
.

Example 1.13
.

[
CE13
]
The cotangent
(T^{*}M,\beta=-p\cdot dq+dh,\mathcal{L}=(p-\nabla g,0),f=h(q)+|p|^{2})
is a Weinstein manifold, where
h
is a small Morse function on
M
.

2
Sutured Legendrians and convex hypersurfaces

Sutured manifolds were first introduced by Gabai
[
Gab83
]
as a tool to study 3-manifolds, and later extended to the contact setting by Colin-Ghiggini-Hutchings-Honda
[
CGHH11
]
. In this section we introduce the notion of sutured Legendrian, which further generalise this construction.

2.1
Sutured contact manifolds

Definition 2.1
.

[
CGHH11
]

A
sutured contact manifold
is a collection
(V,\Gamma,\lambda,\mathcal{N}_{0},\psi)
where ;

\bullet

the pair
(V,\ker\lambda)
is a compact, oriented, contact
(2n+1)
-manifold with corners ;

\bullet

\Gamma
is
(2n-1)
-submanifold included in
\partial V
;

\bullet

\mathcal{N}_{0}
is a neighbourhood of
\Gamma
, and
\psi
is a diffeomorphism

\mathcal{N}_{0}\overset{\psi}{\simeq}(-\varepsilon,0]_{\tau}\times[-1,1]_{t}\times\Gamma

providing coordinates
1
1

1

As in
[
CGHH11
]
this product is actually oriented as
[-1,1]_{t}\times(-\varepsilon,0]_{\tau}\times\Gamma
, but we want to think about
t
(resp.
\tau
) as the vertical (resp. horizontal) direction.
;

satisfying the following conditions :

1.

the boundary of
V
splits into

\partial V\simeq R_{+}\underset{\{1\}\times\Gamma}{\cup}[-1,1]\times\Gamma\underset{\{1\}\times\Gamma}{\cup}R_{-},

where the corners are exactly the gluing loci ;

2.

in coordinates,
\Gamma=\{\tau=t=0\}
and

\partial V\cap\mathcal{N}_{0}=\{t=1\}\cup\{\tau=0\}\cup\{t=-1\}
,
where once again the corners of
V
are exactly the gluing loci ;

3.

the pair
(R_{+},\lambda_{\restriction R_{+}})
(resp.
(R_{-},\lambda_{\restriction R_{-}})
), oriented as the boundary of
V
(resp. with reversed orientation), is a Liouville domain ;

4.

on
\mathcal{N}_{0}
, we have
\lambda=C.dt+e^{\tau}\lambda_{\Gamma}
, where
\lambda_{\Gamma}
is a contact form on
\Gamma
(independent of
t
and
\tau
, and without term in
dt
and
d\tau
).

The Liouville forms
\lambda_{\restriction R_{\pm}}
will be denoted
\beta_{\pm}
,
and the Reeb vector field of
(V,\lambda)
(resp.
(\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma})
will be
R
(resp.
R_{\Gamma}
).
When the collection is unambiguous, a sutured manifold will only be noted
(V,\Gamma,\lambda)
.

We list some consequences from this definition which will be used afterwards, for more precise proofs we refer to
[
CGHH11
, §2]
.

First of all, on the neighbourhood
\mathcal{N}_{0}
, the Reeb vector field is
C^{-1}\partial_{t}
and the contact structure splits into

\ker\lambda=\left<\partial_{\tau},CR_{\Gamma}-e^{\tau}\partial_{t}\right>\oplus\ker\lambda_{\Gamma}.

The conditions
item

3
and
item

4
imply that
(R_{\pm},\beta_{\pm})
are Liouville domains : since the Liouville vector field is
\partial_{\tau}
, it is outgoing, hence
R_{\pm}
have no negative boundary.

Moreover the condition
item

3
imply that the Reeb vector field of
\lambda
is positively (resp. negatively) transverve to
R_{+}
(resp.
R_{-}
). Using its flow we obtain neighbourhoods of
R_{\pm}
contactomorphic to
((1-\varepsilon,1]_{t}\times R_{+},Cdt+\beta_{+})
and
([-1,-1+\varepsilon]_{t}\times R_{-},Cdt+\beta_{-})
, extending the coordinate
t
to a neighbourhood of all of
\partial V
. Note that the coordinate
\tau
can be recovered by integrating the Liouville vector field on each
t
-level, since on
\mathcal{N}_{0}
it is
\partial_{\tau}
.

Remark 2.2
.

If there exists an exact symplectomorphism
(R_{+},\beta_{+})\simeq(-R_{-},\beta_{-})
, the sutured contact manifold will be called
balanced
. It could also be described as a manifold contactomorphic to the symplectisation of
(R_{+},\beta_{+})
near the boundary (or away from a compact, if working with completed manifolds, see
section

3.1
).

2.2
Sutured Legendrians

Definition 2.3
.

A
sutured Legendrian
is an embedded Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda\subset(V,\lambda,\Gamma)
which is :

\bullet

centered, ie its boundary
\partial\Lambda
is included in the suture
\{\tau=t=0\}
;

\bullet

cylindrical, ie on
\mathcal{N}_{0}
we have
\Lambda=(-\varepsilon,0]\times\{0\}\times\partial\Lambda
for
\varepsilon
small enough.

Note that the second condition implies that the boundary
\partial\Lambda
is a Legendrian submanifold of
(\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma})
.
We refer to
figure

for a depiction of the situation.

Remark 2.4
.

\Lambda
could also be called a
Legendrian filling
of
(\partial\Lambda,\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma})
. We can also relax this definition to allow for several
t
-levels near the boundary, but forbidding Reeb chords in the boundary (ie the projection of

to

Those definitions extend Lagrangian fillings in the following way : for

lifts to a sutured Legendrian

2.3
Convex hypersurfaces

Definition 2.5
.

Consider
(V,\xi)
a contact manifold. A vector fields
X
is
contact
if it satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions :

\bullet

the flow of
X
preserves
\xi
, ie
(\phi_{X}^{t})^{*}\xi=\xi
;

\bullet

for any 1-form
\lambda
of kernel
\xi
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{X}\lambda=f\lambda
, where
f\in C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R})
;

\bullet

there exists a 1-form
\lambda
of kernel
\xi
such that
\mathcal{L}_{X}\lambda=f\lambda
, where
f\in C^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(V,\xi)
be a contact manifold. There is a bijection the contact vector fields and the functions
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(V,\mathbb{R})
. If we choose an adapted contact form, a bijection is given by

\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\mapsto X_{\varphi}=\varphi R_{\lambda}+v,

where
v\in\xi
is such that
(\iota_{v}d\lambda)_{\restriction\xi}=d\varphi_{\restriction\xi}
.

Remark 2.7
.

\bullet

If
\tilde{\varphi}
is a
C^{1}
-perturbation of
\varphi
, then
X_{\tilde{\varphi}}
is
C^{0}
-close from
X_{\varphi}
;

\bullet

X_{\varphi}
is a Reeb vector field iff
\varphi>0
;

\bullet

We can interpolate "in time" (if
X
and
Y
are contact vector fields,
(1-t)X+tY
is contact), and "in space" on disjoints closed subsets (if
X
and
Y
are two contact vector fields on those subsets, we can interpolate between the corresponding functions).

An hypersurface
\Sigma\subset(V,\xi)
is
convex
if there exist a contact vector field
X
transverse to
\Sigma
. A direct consequence is that the set

\Gamma_{X}=\{x\in\Sigma,X_{x}\in\xi_{x}\}

is a contact submanifold of codimension
2
, dividing
\Sigma
into two Liouville domains
R_{\pm}
. This configuration is actually a characterisation of a convex hypersurface :

Lemma 2.8
.

[
CGHH11
, Lemme 2.2]

Let
\Sigma^{2n}\subset(V^{2n+1},\xi)
be a closed hypersurface, without boundary.
\Sigma
is convex if and only if there exists an orientation of
\Sigma
, a submanifold
\Gamma^{2n-1}\subset\Sigma
and a contact form
\lambda
of kernel
\xi
such that

\bullet

(\Gamma,\xi\cap T\Gamma)
is a contact manifold, oriented such that the contact form is positive.

\bullet

\Gamma
splits
\Sigma
alternating parts
R_{\pm}
, such
R_{+}
induces on
\Gamma
the previous orientation (ie
\Gamma=\partial R_{+}=-\partial R_{-}
).

\bullet

The Reeb vector field associated to
\lambda
is positively (resp. negatively) transverse to
R_{\pm}
.

A contact form satisfying those conditions will be called adapted to the convex boundary.

Note that the last condition is equivalent to requiring that
(R_{+},\lambda)
and
(-R_{-},\lambda)
, where
R_{+}
is oriented as
\Sigma
and
R_{-}
is endowed with the opposite orientation, are Liouville domains.

As a corollary of the proof, we get a normalisation of the contact structure on a neighbourhood of the convex hypersurface.

Corollary 2.9
.

[
CGHH11
, Corollary 2.5]
If
X
is a contact vector field transverse to
\Sigma
, there exists a contact form
\lambda
of kernel
\xi
and coordinate functions

,
\tau:\mathcal{N}(\Gamma_{X})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that :

\bullet

,
\Sigma=\{t=0\}
and
\Gamma=\{t=\tau=0\}
;

\bullet

on

where

(and has no term

) ;

\bullet

on
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)

,

,

We also define some additional notion, which restricts which kind of Liouville domain can appear as part of the boundary :

Consider a convex hypersurface

2.4

Let
(V,\xi)
be a contact manifold containing a convex hypersurface
\Sigma

\Lambda\subset(V,\xi)

\Sigma

Lemma 2.11
.

If
\Sigma
is W-convex for a contact form
\lambda

\Lambda

\Sigma

\Lambda\pitchfork\Sigma\subset\Gamma_{X}

Proof.

First note that generically, the intersection
\Lambda_{\Sigma}:=\Lambda\cap\Sigma

\Lambda_{\Sigma}
in a neighbourhood of the suture using the Liouville vector field

n

Hence the flow
\phi_{Z}

\Lambda_{\Sigma}

\Gamma_{X}
, and we now need to perturb

\phi_{Z}\Lambda\cap\Sigma\subset\Gamma_{\tilde{X}}
. We will then have
\Lambda_{\Sigma}\subset\Gamma_{\phi_{Z}^{*}\tilde{X}}

For this purpose we use the coordinates obtained in the previous paragraph : on
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)

\lambda=\tau dt+g(\tau)\lambda_{\Gamma}

\{t=\tau=0\}

We choose a contact vector field

\phi_{Z}\Lambda\cap\Sigma\subset\Gamma_{\tilde{X}}

is determined by the function

Conormal of a manifold with boundary.

3.1
Completions

We first present the construction of

Let
(V,\Gamma,\mathcal{N}_{0}(\Gamma),\lambda)
be a sutured contact manifold. We complete it into a non-compact contact manifold
(V^{*},\lambda^{*},\Lambda^{*})
in the following way :

\bullet

On

\bullet

To summarize, we have

V^{*}=V\cup\text{Cont}_{\pm}(R_{\pm})\cup\text{Cont}(\text{Symp}_{+}(\Gamma)).

Let

(on each fiber). If

(in other words

The space of admissible almost complex structure on

is non-empty and contractible, because they are in bijection with the metrics.

In a symplectisation.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(Y,\xi)
be contact,

its symplectisation, and

\bullet

is invariant by

-translation ;

\bullet

,

preserves
\xi
;

\bullet

There exists a form
\lambda
,
\xi
-adapted, such

maps

to

;

\bullet

J_{\restriction\xi}
is

-admissible.

Note that the last condition doesn’t depend on the choice of

. If we want to precise the used contact form we will say that

is

is fully determined by

In a cobordism.

Let
(W,\beta)
be a Liouville cobordism from

to

, and

an admissible almost complex structure. We will say that

is adapted if there exists a compact

and

We take the definitions of

\bullet

3.3
Holomorphic curves and energies

We now define the moduli spaces which will be used to construct our invariants. Let us first recall some definitions.

Chords and orbits.

Given a contact manifold
(V,\lambda)
, a
Reeb orbit
is a closed trajectory of the Reeb vector field, and their set will be denoted
\mathcal{P}(V,\lambda)
. The
action
of an orbit
\gamma
is
\mathcal{A}(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma}\lambda
, which is also the length of the trajectory (parameterised using the Reeb vector field). A Reeb orbit
\gamma
is called
non-degenerate
if the linearized return map of the Reeb flow, restricted to
\xi_{\gamma}
, has no fixed vector.

Given a Legendrian
\Lambda\subset(V,\lambda)
, a
Reeb chord
is a trajectories of the Reeb vector field going from the Legendrian to itself, and their set will be denoted
\mathcal{C}(\Lambda;V,\lambda)
. Given a second Legendrian
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset(V,\lambda)
, the set a chords going from
\Lambda
to
\Lambda^{\prime}
will be denoted
\mathcal{C}(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};V,\lambda)
.
The
action
of a chord
c
is once again
\mathcal{A}(c)=\int_{c}\lambda
, and a chord
c
of action
T
will be called non-degenerate if

(d\phi_{R}^{T})(T_{c(0)}\Lambda)\pitchfork T_{c(T)}\Lambda.

Definition 3.7
.

A sutured contact manifold will be called non degenerate if all Reeb orbits are non-degenerate.

A sutured Legendrian
\Lambda\subset(V,\lambda,\Gamma)
will be called

\bullet

non-degenerate if all Reeb orbits and chords are non-degenerate (in
(V,\lambda)
) ;

\bullet

totally non-degenerate if it is non-degenerate, and all Reeb chords of
\mathcal{C}(\partial\Lambda;\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma})
are non-degenerate.

A sutured contact manifold will be called hypertight if all Reeb orbits are non-contractibles.

A sutured Legendrian
\Lambda\subset(V,\lambda,\Gamma)
will be called relatively hypertight if all Reeb chords are non-contractible.

Given a sutured contact manifold, the set of adapted contact forms which are non-degenerate is a countable intersection of dense open sets.
Similarly, given a sutured Legendrian
\Lambda\subset(V,\lambda,\Gamma)
, the set of adapted contact forms so that
\Lambda
is totally non-degenerate is also a countable intersection of dense open sets.

Remark 3.8
.

It is also possible to fix the contact form, and instead to perturb the Legendrian. Once again the set of perturbations so that the Legendrian is totally non-degenerate is a countable intersection of dense open sets.

Holomorphic curves.

Consider a Liouville cobordism
(W,\beta)
(which could simply be a symplectisation), with an adapted complex structure
J
. Assume that we have a (embedded) Lagrangian cobordism
L\subset(W,\beta)
, of boundary
\Lambda_{\pm}\subset\partial_{\pm}W
.

An
holomorphic curve
is a map
F:(S,j)\rightarrow(W,J)
defined on a Riemann surface, such that
dF\circ j=J\circ dF
and
F(\partial S)\subset L
.

Let
g

4.2
Other points of view

We present some others constructions geometrically similar, but whose implementation is more difficult.

Wrapping the contact form.
We can perturb

to make it

, in bijection with

is
not

-holomorphic anymore.

Reduced energy

We could also work with cylindrically completed Legendrians, using the usual contact form. The differential should now count more general curves, whose energy might be infinite, but with finite "reduced energy", defined by integrating

on

Counting Floer curves.
Alternatively we could also count curves satisfying a Floer equation. Indeed Floer strips between two Lagrangians, asymptotic to 1-periodic trajectories of an Hamiltonian vector field

Cancelling Reeb orbits.
Finally, one could make appear the Reeb chords (and orbits) of the dividing set, by creating two families of Reeb orbits in cancelling position, as in

4.5
"Wrapping" the Legendrian dga

We now briefly explain how to adapt our constructions to the Chekanov-Eliashberg dga. Let us first recall the classical definition, which still holds in the sutured setting.
Consider a sutured Legendrian
\Lambda\subset(V,\lambda,\Gamma)
, and assume that
\lambda
is hypertight to avoid dealing with Reeb orbits. Following
[
Che97
]
, its Chekanov-Eliashberg dga is defined by picking a tailored almost complex structure

, and counting holomorphic curves (with several negative punctures) in
\mathbb{R}\times V^{*}
, with boundary in
\mathbb{R}\times\Lambda^{*}

More precisely, denote
\mathcal{L}C(\Lambda,V,\lambda,J)
the unital differential graded
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-algebra, generated by the Reeb chords
\mathcal{C}(\Lambda,V,\lambda)
and the homological variables
\mathbb{Z}[H_{1}(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})]
(the chords do not commute with the homological elements), graded as in
section

4.1
.
The differential is defined on Reeb chords by counting holomorphic curves

\partial c=\underset{|w|=|c|-1}{\sum}\#\mathcal{M}(c;w;V^{*},\Lambda^{*};J)\ w,

and extended by the Leibniz rule
\partial(ab)=\partial a.b+(-1)^{|a|}a.\partial b

As in
section

4.1.1
, this is indeed a differential, and the homology is independent of the choices made along the way. More generally, a path of sutured Legendrians
\Lambda^{u}\subset(V,\xi^{u})
induces a quasi-isomorphism between the dgas (in the sense of
[
EO17
, Lemma 5.6]
), which only depends on the homotopy class of
(\xi^{u},\Lambda^{u})
.

We now explain how to get a wrapped version of this dga. We first modify the contact form near the suture, as in
[
CGHH11
, §4.2]
: on the neighbourhood

\mathcal{N}\simeq(-1,0]_{\tau}\times[-1,1]_{t}\times\Gamma,

take
\tilde{\lambda}=f(\tau,t)dt+g(\tau,t)\lambda_{\Gamma}
, such that

\bullet

the contact condition is satisfied :
g^{n-1}(g\partial_{\tau}f-f\partial_{\tau}g)>0
;

\bullet

\partial_{\tau}f\geq 0
;

\bullet

near the boundary

and
g=e^{\tau}
;

\bullet

g
presents two cancelable critical points : a maximum at
(-2\varepsilon,0)
and a saddle point at
(-\varepsilon,0)
.

The Reeb vector field is directed by
X_{g}+\partial_{\tau}fR_{\Gamma}
, where
X_{g}
is the Hamiltonian vector field associated to

, defined by
\iota_{X_{g}}d\tau\wedge dt=dg
, see
figure

6

With this modification we have created two families of Reeb orbits, one for each critical point of

, which correspond to orbits of
(\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma})
(we also create other orbits around the maximum, but their action can be taken as big as needed). However there is also a grading shift happening, because for orbits the definition involves the dimension of the ambient manifold. For orbits over the saddle point, the Conley-Zehnder index is unchanged and the dimension increased, so the resulting degree increases by one. Similarly, the degree of an orbit above the maximum increases by two. To avoid dealing with this phenomena, we now assume that
(\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma})
is hypertight (for this construction this hypothesis is actually important, and not only a simplifying assumption).

The sutured Legendrian
\Lambda\subset(V,\lambda,\Gamma)
stays Legendrian during this process,
and we have again created two families of Reeb chords, corresponding to chords of
\mathcal{C}(\Lambda_{\Gamma},\Gamma,\lambda_{\Gamma})
(as well as other long chords which can again be ignored by an action-filtration argument). For a chord

in
\Gamma

(resp.

) the induced chord above the saddle point (resp. maximum). The degrees of the chords are given by :

|\check{c}|_{V}=|c|_{\Gamma}\qquad\qquad|\hat{c}|_{V}+1=|c|_{\Gamma}.

Thus, and contrary the case of Reeb orbits, the degree is unchanged for the family of chords corresponding to the saddle point (compare also to
[

]

5.4
Sketch in the general case

We quickly present the strategy to handle the general case, which will be treated in a forthcoming paper. Given a pure braid
B
with
n
strands in
\Sigma
, our goal is to construct an automorphism of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma\setminus\{x_{1}...x_{n}\})
(up to inner automorphism). This is a complete invariants by Dehn–Nielsen–Baer theorem, see
[
FM11
, Theorem 8.1]
.

The main idea was mentioned in
section

2.5
: given two Legendrians
\Lambda
and
\Lambda_{0}
in a closed, compact contact manifold
(V,\xi)
, the Legendrian contact homology of
\Lambda_{0}
in

V_{\Lambda}=V\setminus\mathcal{N}(\Lambda)

is an invariant of the (ordered) pair of Legendrians. From another point of view, it is the contact homology of
\Lambda_{0}
in
(V,\xi)

stopped
at
\Lambda
.

This construction should extend to the sutured setting : consider a sutured Legendrian
\Lambda\subset(V,\lambda,\Gamma)
. After removing a neighbourhood, we get a "2-sutured" contact manifold
V_{\Lambda}
, with two kinds of boundary. We can complete it so that, at infinity, it looks like the contactisation of some
Liouville sector
(for the appropriate notion of sector). Moreover, the exact sequence from
section

4.4
should be modified as follows. If
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}
are two other sutured Legendrians in
(V,\lambda,\Gamma)
, we should get an inclusion as before

LC(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1};V_{\Lambda})\hookrightarrow WLC(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1};V_{\Lambda}),

whose quotient is the exterior complex. For simple situations (notably when we don’t need to augment the chords), the exterior homology should be isomorphic to the stopped homology of the boundary :

LC^{\text{ext}}(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1};V_{\Lambda})\simeq LC(\partial\Lambda_{0},\partial\Lambda_{1};\Gamma_{\partial\Lambda}),

and the exact sequence is an invariant (in the sense of
section

4.4
) under isotopy of
(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1},\Lambda)
fixed at the boundary (note that the construction from
section

4.5
might be more adapted).

We now take for sutured contact manifold the unit bundle

V=U(I\times\Sigma)
,
and for sutured Legendrian stop the conormal
\Lambda=\Lambda_{B}
, which is a collection of
n
cylinders. Finally
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}
are the conormals of two push-off of the first strand.

The boundary of
\Lambda_{B}
are unit fibers in the suture
\Gamma=U\Sigma\sqcup U\Sigma
, and by definition the complement of the unit fiber over
x\in\Sigma
is
U(\Sigma\setminus\{x\})
. Thus, the exterior homology is the relative Legendrian homology of two unit fibers in
U(\Sigma\setminus\{x_{1}...x_{n}\})
, in other words

LH^{\text{ext}}(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1};V_{\Lambda})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(\Sigma\setminus\{x_{1}...x_{n}\})]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(\Sigma\setminus\{x_{1}...x_{n}\})].

Moreover if we add the product structure obtained by counting pair of pants (notice that we can identify the chords of
\Lambda_{i}
with the mixed chords from
\Lambda_{0}
to
\Lambda_{1}
), we can upgrade this to a ring isomorphism.

On the other hand, using techniques from
[
Dat22
]
, we should prove that, to compute Legendrian homology, we can replace
V_{\Lambda}=U(I\times\Sigma)\setminus\Lambda_{B}
by
U(I\times\Sigma\setminus B)
. Hence, using the homotopical restrictions on the differential, we get the exact sequence

\rightarrow C_{0}\rightarrow C\rightarrow C_{-}[1]\oplus C_{+}[1]\rightarrow,

where
C=C_{\pm}=C_{0}=\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(\Sigma\setminus\{x_{1}...x_{n}\})]
, and the boundary map will be induced by the classical map raised by the braid
f_{B}\in\text{Aut}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma\setminus\{x_{1}...x_{n}\}))
. As previously, we can recover this map using the invariance of the exact triangle.

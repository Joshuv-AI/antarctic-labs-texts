Abstract
. Given a finitely generated subgroup H H of a free group F F , we present an algorithm which computes g 1 , … , g m ∈ F g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\in F , such that the set of elements g ∈ F g\in F , for which there exists a non-trivial H H -equation having g g as a solution, is, precisely, the disjoint union of the double cosets H ⊔ H ​ g 1 ​ H ⊔ ⋯ ⊔ H ​ g m ​ H H\sqcup Hg_{1}H\sqcup\cdots\sqcup Hg_{m}H . Moreover, we present an algorithm which, given a finitely generated subgroup H ⩽ F H\leqslant F and an element g ∈ F g\in F , computes a finite set of elements of H ∗ ⟨ x ⟩ H*\langle x\rangle that generate (as a normal subgroup) the “ideal” I H ​ ( g ) ⊴ H ∗ ⟨ x ⟩ I_{H}(g)\unlhd H*\langle x\rangle of all “polynomials” w ⁡ ( x ) w(x) , such that w ⁡ ( g ) = 1 w(g)=1 . 1.
Introduction

The following is a basic question studied in classical algebra: given a
field extension

L/K
(
L
over
K
) and an element
\alpha\in L
, is
\alpha

algebraic
over
K
, i.e., does there exist a non-trivial polynomial with coefficients in
K
, say
p(x)\in K[x]
, such that
p(\alpha)=0
, or, otherwise, is
\alpha

transcendental
over
K
? Moreover, when
\alpha
is algebraic over
K
, it is interesting to study the set of all polynomials over
K
annihilating
\alpha
, namely
I_{K}(\alpha)=\{p(x)\in K[x]\mid p(\alpha)=0\}\subseteq K[x]
. As is well known,
I_{K}(\alpha)
is a principal ideal of
K[x]
,
I_{K}(\alpha)=m_{\alpha}(x)K[x]
, whose unique monic generator
m_{\alpha}(x)
is called the
minimal polynomial
of
\alpha
over
K
. An interesting algorithmic problem is then to compute
m_{\alpha}(x)
in terms of
\alpha
.

Analogous questions can be asked and studied in the context of group theory, where the answers and algorithms turn out to be much more complicated. Here, we start with an
extension of groups

H\leqslant G
and an element
g\in G
. The analog of the “ring of polynomials over
K
” is the free product
H*\langle x\rangle\simeq H*\mathbb{Z}
, and the analog of the “ideal of polynomials annihilating
\alpha
” is the normal subgroup
I_{H}(g)\unlhd H*\langle x\rangle
of all “polynomials”
w(x)\in H*\langle x\rangle
, such that
w(g)=1
. In the present paper, after some general considerations, we concentrate on extensions of free groups,
H\leqslant F
, where
H
is finitely generated, and analyze both the set of elements in
F
being algebraic over
H
, and the set of equations over
H
satisfied by a given
g\in F
. We give the precise definitions below.

Let
G
be a group and
H\leqslant G
. A univariate
equation over
H
(or an
H
-equation
, for short) is a “polynomial equation” of the form

(1.1)

w(x)=1,

where
w(x)\in H*\langle x\rangle
, the free product of
H
and the free abelian group of rank 1 generated by the
variable

x
. That is,
w(x)
is an expression of the form

(1.2)

w(x)=h_{0}x^{\varepsilon_{1}}h_{1}x^{\varepsilon_{2}}\cdots h_{d-1}x^{\varepsilon_{d}}h_{d},

where
\varepsilon_{i}=\pm 1
and
h_{i}\in H
. We assume that the equation is
non-trivial
, i.e.,
w(x)\in H*\langle x\rangle\setminus\{1\}
, and that it is in
reduced form
, i.e.,
\varepsilon_{i}=\varepsilon_{i+1}
whenever
h_{i}=1
, for
i=1,\ldots,d-1
. The
degree
of the equation
w(x)=1
(or of the polynomial
w(x)
), when written in reduced form, is
d=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\varepsilon_{i}|
. The equation is
balanced
if
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\varepsilon_{i}=0
. It is standard to group together into higher exponents the various possible consecutive occurrences of
x
with trivial elements in between; for example,
h_{1}x^{2}h_{2}x^{-2}
stands for
h_{1}x1xh_{2}x^{-1}1x^{-1}
, a balanced equation of degree 4. An element
g\in G
is a
solution
to equation (
1.1
) if
w(g)=1
, that is, the element of
G
resulting in substituting
g
for
x
in the expression (
1.2
) is the trivial group element.

The area of Group Theory studying equations and their solutions is known as
algebraic geometry over groups
; see
[
3
]
: the elements of
H*\langle x\rangle
(or, more generally, of
H*\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
in the case of multivariate equations) are the analog of non-commutative polynomials. Following the language from
[
3
]
, a normal subgroup
I\unlhd H*\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
is called an
ideal
, and the set of its common
zeros
(i.e., the subset of
G^{n}
of common solutions to the set of equations
\{w(x)=1\mid w(x)\in I\}
) is an
algebraic set
. There is a vast body of literature about the general problem of solving equations (univariate or multivariate) in a group
G
. A typical question is to decide, given an equation (or a system of equations), whether it has a solution in
G
or not and, in the positive case, to describe the set of all such solutions and its structure. Two of the main results in this direction are Makanin’s and Razborov’s theorems, which analyze the case of free groups: the first one (see Makanin
[
10
]
) solves the decidability part of the problem, while the second one (see Razborov
[
17
,
18
]
) provides a kind of compact algorithmic description of
all
solutions, in case they exist. These are two very deep results, with intricate proofs, and having numerous important applications.

Here, we restrict ourselves to univariate equations and to free groups. In this particular case, the description of solution sets is much simpler: Bormotov, Gilman and Myasnikov
[
4
]
use reduction techniques from formal language theory to show that the solution set to a single equation of degree greater than 1 is either the whole group or a finite union of sets of the form
\{g_{1}g_{2}^{n}g_{3}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. They also give a polynomial time algorithm for computing this solution set.

In contrast, we adopt here a kind of dual point of view. Instead of studying the solution set to a given equation in a group
G
, we fix a subgroup
H\leqslant G
and are interested in the set of all solutions in
G
of
all
possible equations over
H
. In addition, given an element
g\in G
, we study the set of equations over
H
satisfied by
g
, namely the ideal
I_{H}(g)=\{w(x)\in H*\langle x\rangle\mid w(g)=1\}\unlhd H*\langle x\rangle
: Is it trivial or not? And, if not, can we compute a finite set of generators for it (as a normal subgroup)? We shall answer and give algorithms for these and related questions, when the ambient group
G
is a free group.

The fundamental notion here is that of dependence of an element
g\in G
on a subgroup
H\leqslant G
, first introduced in Rosenmann
[
21
]
:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a group,
H\leqslant G
a subgroup, and
g\in G
an element. We say that
g
is
dependent on
H
if there exists a non-trivial
H
-equation
w(x)=1
that is satisfied by
g
. More generally,
g
is dependent on a subset
S\subseteq G
if it is dependent on the subgroup
H=\langle S\rangle
. We denote by
\mathrm{dep}_{G}(H)
the set of elements in
G
that depend on
H
, and by
\mathrm{Dep}_{G}(H)=\langle\mathrm{dep}_{G}(H)\rangle
, the subgroup they generate, called the
dependence
subgroup of
H
. When there is no risk of confusion, we shall delete the subscript
G
from the notation.

Remark 1.2
.

The notion of dependence, as given above, naturally suits free groups (as defined in
[
21
]
and as studied here). However, other possible definitions suit better in other contexts. For example, when
G
is an abelian group and
1\neq H\leqslant G
then every
g\in G
satisfies the equation
hxh^{-1}x^{-1}=1
(for each
h\in H
) and so, it is dependent on
H
. Here, we may define an element
g
to be dependent on
H
if it satisfies an equation of the form
x^{n}h=1
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,
h\in H
. When
G
is free abelian and
H
is finitely generated then this definition is equivalent to
\mathrm{rk\,}(\langle H,g\rangle)\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H)
. As seen below (Proposition
2.1
), the latter is also equivalent to Definition
1.1
in the case of free groups.

Remark 1.3
.

Clearly,
H\subseteq\mathrm{dep}_{G}(H)\subseteq\mathrm{Dep}_{G}(H)\leqslant G
but, in general,
\mathrm{dep}_{G}(H)
is not a subgroup. Also, when
H\leqslant K\leqslant G
, we have
\mathrm{dep}_{K}(H)=K\cap\mathrm{dep}_{G}(H)
and
\mathrm{Dep}_{K}(H)\leqslant K\cap\mathrm{Dep}_{G}(H)
, where the inclusion may be strict. We shall see examples of these situations in the free context; see Examples
2.2
.

Examples 1.4
.

(i)

If
g\in H
then
g
is dependent on
H
, satisfying the equation
g^{-1}x=1
(also
gx^{-1}=1
) of degree 1.

(ii)

If
g\notin H
but
H^{g}\cap H\neq 1
then
g
is dependent on
H
, satisfying the balanced equation
x^{-1}hxh^{\prime-1}=1
of degree 2, where
h,h^{\prime}\in H
are such that
g^{-1}hg=h^{\prime}
. It follows that if
\{1\}\neq H\unlhd G
is a nontrivial normal subgroup of
G
then
\mathrm{dep}(H)=G
. In particular, this is the case when
\{1\}\neq H
is a subgroup of the center
Z(G)
of
G
. It follows also that
N_{G}(H)\subseteq\mathrm{dep}(H)
, where
N_{G}(H)=\{g\in G\mid g^{-1}Hg=H\}
is the normalizer of
H
in
G
.

(iii)

If
H
is of finite index in
G
then
\mathrm{dep}(H)=G
: any
g\in G
satisfies the equation
x^{k}h^{-1}=1
, where
k\geqslant 1
and
h\in H
are such that
g^{k}=h
.

(iv)

\mathrm{dep}(\{1\})
is, precisely, the set of torsion elements of
G
. This follows from the fact that the only equations of positive degree over the trivial subgroup are those of the form
x^{n}=1
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.

Clearly, the notion of “
g
being dependent on
H
” is the group theory analog of “
\alpha
being algebraic over the field
K
”. At this point, we can already observe a relevant initial difference with classical field theory: the sum and product of two algebraic elements over
K
are also algebraic over
K
while, here, the product of two dependent elements on a subgroup
H
may very well not be dependent on
H
; see Example
2.2
(i). So, one can expect the structure of the set
\mathrm{dep}(H)
to be more complicated than its analog in the classical situation, where the algebraic elements over
K
just form an intermediate field between
K
and
F
. Here,
\mathrm{dep}(H)
is not even a subgroup but, still, in the free group case, it will not be hard to understand its structure; see Theorem
4.1
below.

In order to describe the set of elements
\mathrm{dep}(H)
, the following easy observation will be important:
\mathrm{dep}(H)
is always the disjoint union of several (maybe infinite)
(H,H)
-double cosets:

Observation 1.5
.

Let
G
be a group and let
H\leqslant G
. If
g\in\mathrm{dep}(H)
then all the elements of the double coset
HgH
depend on
H
as well. Moreover, they all satisfy an
H
-equation of the same minimal degree.

Proof.

Let
w(x)=h_{0}x^{\varepsilon_{1}}h_{1}x^{\varepsilon_{2}}\cdots h_{d-1}x^{\varepsilon_{d}}h_{d}=1
be an
H
-equation satisfying
w(g)=1
. Then, for every
h,h^{\prime}\in H
, we have that the new equation

w^{\prime}(x)=h_{0}(h^{-1}xh^{\prime-1})^{\varepsilon_{1}}h_{1}(h^{-1}xh^{\prime-1})^{\varepsilon_{2}}\cdots h_{d-1}(h^{-1}xh^{\prime-1})^{\varepsilon_{d}}h_{d}=1

satisfies
w^{\prime}(hgh^{\prime})=1
, showing that
hgh^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dep}(H)
. Since (after appropriate cancellations)
w^{\prime}(x)=1
has the same or smaller degree than
w(x)=1
, it follows that all the elements of
HgH
satisfy an
H
-equation of the same minimal degree.
∎

The following is another elementary result. Its converse, however, is not true in general (see Example
2.2
(iii) below).

Observation 1.6
.

Let
H\leqslant G
,
g\in G
, and
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. If
g^{k}\in G
is dependent on
H
then
g
is dependent on
H
as well.

Proof.

If
h_{0}x^{\varepsilon_{1}}h_{1}x^{\varepsilon_{2}}\cdots h_{d-1}x^{\varepsilon_{d}}h_{d}=1
is a non-trivial
H
-equation satisfied by
g^{k}
then,
h_{0}x^{k\varepsilon_{1}}h_{1}x^{k\varepsilon_{2}}\cdots h_{d-1}x^{k\varepsilon_{d}}h_{d}=1
is a non-trivial
H
-equation satisfied by
g
.
∎

Two related notions are those of a dependence-closed subgroup and the dependence closure operator. The latter appeared first in Rosenmann
[
21
]
and it coincides with the notion of elementary-algebraic extension closure from Miasnikov–Ventura–Weil
[
15
]
.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
H\leqslant G
. The subgroup
H
is called
dependence-closed
if
\mathrm{Dep}(H)=H
, i.e., if the only elements
g\in G
which are solutions to non-trivial
H
-equations are those
g\in H
. For example, any free factor of
G
is clearly dependence-closed.

When
H
is not dependence-closed then
\mathrm{dep}(H)
, the set of
all
dependent elements
on
H
, strictly contains
H
. But then, when constructing the subgroup
\mathrm{Dep}(H)
generated by these elements, new elements may emerge, which depend on
\mathrm{Dep}(H)
but not on
H
. We can then iterate this process of executing the dependence operator and ask ourselves whether the process finally stabilizes, that is, do we reach a dependence-closed subgroup after finally many steps, or can the process continue indefinitely? In Proposition
7.3
, we show that when
H
is a finitely generated subgroup of a free group then the process indeed stabilizes after finitely many steps. However, the number of steps may be arbitrary large even in the case of a subgroup of a free group of rank
2
, as shown in Example
7.1
, but it is still bounded by the total length of the generators of
H
(see Proposition
7.3
).

Definition 1.8
.

Let the ascending sequence of subgroups
H_{0}\leqslant H_{1}\leqslant H_{2}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant G
be defined by
H_{0}=H
and
H_{i}=\mathrm{Dep}(H_{i-1})=\mathrm{Dep}^{i}(H)
, for
i\geqslant 1
. We define the
dependence closure
of
H
to be the subgroup

\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H)=\bigcup_{i\geqslant 0}H_{i}\leqslant G.

If this ascending sequence stabilizes after finitely many steps, we define the
dependence length
of
H
to be
\mathrm{depl}(H)=\min\{j\geqslant 0\mid H_{j}=\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H)\}
; otherwise, we let
\mathrm{depl}(H)=\infty
.

Observation 1.9
.

For any subgroup
H\leqslant G
,
\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H)
is the smallest dependence-closed subgroup of
G
containing
H
.

Proof.

Let
g\in G
be dependent on
\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H)
, i.e.,
g
is a solution to some non-trivial
\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H)
-equation
w(x)=h_{0}x^{\varepsilon_{1}}h_{1}\cdots x^{\varepsilon_{d}}h_{d}=1
. Since
h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{d}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H)
, there exists
m\geqslant 0
, such that
h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{d}\in\mathrm{Dep}^{m}(H)
. Thus,
w(x)=1
is also a non-trivial
\mathrm{Dep}^{m}(H)
-equation and hence,
g\in\mathrm{Dep}^{m+1}(H)\leqslant\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H)
. Therefore,
\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H)
is dependence-closed. The rest of the statement is clear.
∎

The following two propositions are straightforward, and we leave their proofs to the reader.

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
H\leqslant K\leqslant G
. If
H
is dependence-closed in
K
, and
K
is dependence-closed in
G
then
H
is dependence-closed in
G
.
∎

Proposition 1.11
.

Let
H\leqslant G
be a dependence-closed subgroup of a group
G
. Then:

(i)

H
is malnormal in
G
:
H^{g}\cap H=1
for every
g\in G\setminus H
;

(ii)

H
is pure in
G
(also called root-closed, radical-closed): if
g^{n}\in H
,
n\neq 0
, then
g\in H
;

(iii)

if
H\leqslant K\leqslant G
and
H\neq K
then
|K:H|=\infty
;

(iv)

if
K
is a free factor of
H
then
K
is dependence-closed in
G
;

(v)

if
K\leqslant G
is also dependence-closed in
G
then so is
H\cap K
.
∎

In the following and along the rest of the paper, when referring to morphisms, we adopt the notation with arguments on the left:
x\mapsto(x)\varphi=x\varphi
, and compositions denoted accordingly:
x\mapsto x\varphi\mapsto x\varphi\phi
.

Let us now look at
H
-equations from a different perspective. When
H\leqslant G
and
g\in G
, we can consider the group homomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi_{H,g}\colon H*\langle x\rangle

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle G,

(1.3)

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle h,\;\forall h\in H,

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle g,

which is well defined by the universal property of free products. Then,

(w(x))\varphi_{H,g}=w(g).

So, in this setting,
g
is a solution to
w(x)=1
if and only if
w(x)\in\ker\varphi_{H,g}
. Thus, the set of
H
-equations satisfied by
g
is exactly

\{w(x)=1\mid w(x)\in\ker\varphi_{H,g}\},

and
\ker\varphi_{H,g}
is a normal subgroup of
H*\langle x\rangle
. Hence, a natural way to describe these

-equations is by giving a set of generators for the ideal
I_{H}(g)=\ker\varphi_{H,g}
as a normal subgroup of
H*\langle x\rangle
. This is what we do in the free case: we show that

is always finitely generated (as normal subgroup) and we provide an algorithm computing a set of generators for it; see Theorem
5.2
.

1.1.
Organization of the paper

After this section, where the basic notions and definitions corresponding to dependence on subgroups in general groups have been introduced, we restrict ourselves to free groups
F
for the rest of the paper. In Section
2
we exhibit basic results for free groups. In Section
3
we present a brief description of the well-known Stallings techniques for studying subgroups of a free group, which play a central role in the paper. Then, in Section
4
, we examine closely the way we can use Stallings foldings in order to provide an explicit description of the set of elements that depend on a finitely generated subgroup
H\leqslant F
. This set is proved to have the form of a finite disjoint union of double cosets
\mathrm{dep}(H)=Hg_{0}H\sqcup Hg_{1}H\sqcup\cdots\sqcup Hg_{n}H
, where
g_{0}=1
and the
g_{i}
’s are computable and generate
\mathrm{Dep}(H)
; see Theorem
4.1
. In Section
5
we present two alternative algorithms for computing a finite set of normal generators for the ideal (i.e., normal subgroup)
I_{H}(g)\unlhd H*\langle x\rangle
consisting of those polynomials
w(x)
over
H
satisfying
w(g)=1
, for a given fixed element
g\in F
; see Theorem
5.2
. The first algorithm is based on Nielsen transformations and the second one on Stallings graph-theory techniques providing, respectively, an algebraic and a geometric reason (in a way, complementary to one another) for such equations to exist. Finally, in Section
7
we study further the notion of a dependence-closed subgroup, and the corresponding closure operator within the context of free groups
F
, including an example of a subgroup
H\leqslant F_{2}
of an arbitrarily long dependence length. We close the paper highlighting a few interesting questions for further research.

[
23
]
. A year later, in the process of writing the present final version, we have been informed of a recent further development in this interesting topic: by applying as well Stallings techniques, Dario Ascari

2.
Dependence in free groups

For the rest of the paper we concentrate on free groups and study the above notions in this setting, both from the algebraic and the algorithmic points of view. We adopt the following notation: a free group is generally denoted by
F
; also by
F_{r}
, if we want to emphasize its rank,
\mathrm{rk\,}(F_{r})=r
; and by
F(A)
, if we want to emphasize a basis (a set of free generators)
A\subseteq F
for it. The notations
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F
and
H\leqslant_{\textsf{ff}}F
mean that
H
is a finitely generated, respectively, a free factor of
F
.

The next proposition follows from works by Nielsen and Schreier (see Rosenmann
[
21
, Lemma 1.1]
, or Miasnikov–Ventura–Weil
[
15
, Prop. 3.13]
) and provides equivalent definitions for dependence of elements in the setting of free groups.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
H\leqslant F
and
g\in F
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

g
does not depend on
H
;

(ii)

the morphism
\varphi_{H,g}\colon H*\langle x\rangle\to F,h\mapsto h,x\mapsto g
is injective;

(iii)

H
is a proper free factor of
\langle H,g\rangle
;

(iv)

H
is contained in a proper free factor of
\langle H,g\rangle
;

(v)

\mathrm{rk\,}(\langle H^{\prime},g\rangle)=\mathrm{rk\,}(H^{\prime})+1
for every finitely generated subgroup
H^{\prime}\leqslant H
;

(vi)

\mathrm{rk\,}(\langle H^{\prime},g\rangle)>\mathrm{rk\,}(H^{\prime})
for every finitely generated subgroup
H^{\prime}\leqslant H
. ∎

In view of Proposition
2.1
, it is quite easy to decide, given
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F
and
g\in F
, whether
g
is dependent on
H
or not: we only need to compute the rank of
\langle H,g\rangle
. In case
g
is dependent on
H
, we can even find an explicit non-trivial
H
-equation
w(x)=1
, such that
w(g)=1
, by a brute force algorithm, enumerating all possible
H
-equations and checking for which one is
g
a solution. In the following sections we tackle more challenging problems:

(i)

in Section
4
we provide an effective algorithm which, given
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F
, computes a finite set of generators for
\mathrm{Dep}(H)
;

(ii)

in Section
5
we present two independent algorithms (both faster and more interesting than just the brute force one) which, given
g\in\mathrm{dep}(H)
, results in a representation of
all
the
H
-equations having
g
as a solution.

Examples 2.2
.

Let
F_{2}=F(\{a,b\})
be a free group of rank 2.

(i)

For
H=\langle a^{2},b^{2}\rangle
, we have
a,b\in\mathrm{dep}(H)
(being solutions to the
H
-equations
x^{2}a^{-2}=1
and
x^{2}b^{-2}=1
, respectively) and so,
\mathrm{Dep}(H)=F_{2}
. However,

since
\{a^{2},b^{2},ab\}
is a freely independent subset of
F_{2}
. It follows that
\mathrm{dep}(H)
is not a subgroup and thus, it is strictly contained in
\mathrm{Dep}(H)
.

(ii)

When
H=\langle a^{2},b^{2}\rangle
and
K=\langle a^{2},b^{2},ab\rangle\leqslant F_{2}
then
H\leqslant_{\textsf{ff}}K\leqslant F_{2}
. It follows that
\mathrm{Dep}_{K}(H)=H
and
K\cap\mathrm{Dep}_{F_{2}}(H)=K\cap F_{2}=K
, so that the inclusion
\mathrm{Dep}_{K}(H)\leqslant K\cap\mathrm{Dep}_{F_{2}}(H)
is strict.

(iii)

For
H=\langle b,aba^{-1}\rangle\leqslant F_{2}
, we have
a\in\mathrm{dep}(H)
, whereas
a^{r}\not\in\mathrm{dep}(H)
for
|r|\geqslant 2
.

(iv)

Let
F
be a free group and
g\in F
not being a proper power. Then, for every

,
\mathrm{dep}(\langle g^{r}\rangle)=\mathrm{Dep}(\langle g^{r}\rangle)=\langle g\rangle
. In particular,
\langle g^{r}\rangle
is dependence-closed if and only if
r=\pm 1
, i.e., the dependence-closed cyclic subgroups are, precisely, the maximal ones.

(v)

Let
F
be a free group,
g\in F
, and
H,K\leqslant F
, such that

and
H<K
(strict inclusion). Then,
\mathrm{Dep}(K)=F
because

is dependent on
K
.

When
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F
then
\mathrm{Dep}(H)\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F
and, moreover,
\mathrm{rk\,}(\mathrm{Dep}(H))\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H)
. This follows from results in Rosenmann
[

and Miasnikov–Ventura–Weil

; for completeness, we offer here a direct and elementary proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
H_{0}\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}H_{1}\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}H_{2}\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}\cdots
be a non-decreasing sequence of finitely generated subgroups of a free group
F
, satisfying
\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})\geqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i+1})
, for
i\geqslant 0
. Let
H=\bigcup_{i\geqslant 0}H_{i}
. Then
\mathrm{rk\,}(H)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})
and, furthermore, there exists
m
, such that
H=H_{i}
for all
i\geqslant m
.

Proof.

Let
r_{i}=\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})
, for each
i\geqslant 0
, and let
r_{H}=\mathrm{rk\,}(H)
. The sequence
(r_{i})_{i\geqslant 0}
of natural numbers is monotone decreasing and therefore eventually constant: there exists
r\geqslant 0
and
m^{\prime}\geqslant 0
such that
r_{i}=r
, for all
i\geqslant m^{\prime}
. Suppose now that
r_{H}>r
. It follows that there exists
H^{\prime}\leqslant_{\textsf{ff}}H
of rank

. But then,
H^{\prime}\leqslant H_{m^{\prime\prime}}
, for some
m^{\prime\prime}
, and, in fact,
H^{\prime}\leqslant_{\textsf{ff}}H_{i}
, for all
i\geqslant m^{\prime\prime}
, which is in contradiction to
\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})=r
for all
i\geqslant m^{\prime}
. Therefore,
r_{H}\leqslant r
and there exists
m
, such that
H_{m}
contains a generating set of
H
, which implies that
H=H_{i}
, for all
i\geqslant m
, and
r_{H}=r
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F
. Then,
\mathrm{rk\,}(\mathrm{Dep}(H))\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H)
.

Proof.

Being finitely generated,
H
is contained in a finitely generated free factor of
F
(even in the case that
F
is not countably generated). Therefore, there are only countably many elements in
F
depending on
H
, say
(g_{i})_{i\geqslant 1}
. Let
(H_{i})_{i\geqslant 0}
be the sequence of ascending subgroups of
F
defined by
H_{0}=H
and
H_{i+1}=\langle H_{i},g_{i+1}\rangle
, for
i>0
. Since
g_{i+1}\in\mathrm{dep}(H)
then
g_{i+1}\in\mathrm{dep}(H_{i})
and hence, by Proposition
2.1
(vi),
\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i+1})\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})
, for each

. The result then follows by Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
H,K\leqslant G
. Then,

(i)

\mathrm{Dep}(H\cap K)\leqslant\mathrm{Dep}(H)\cap\mathrm{Dep}(K)
and the inclusion may be strict;

(ii)

\mathrm{Dep}(\langle H,K\rangle)\geqslant\langle\mathrm{Dep}(H),\mathrm{Dep}(K)\rangle
and the inclusion may be strict.

Proof.

(i). The inclusion is immediate by definition. As an example for a strict inclusion, let
F=F(\{a,b,c\})
,
H=\langle a^{-1}ba,b\rangle
and
K=\langle a^{-1}ca,c\rangle
. Then
H\cap K=1
and so,
\mathrm{Dep}(H\cap K)=1
. However,
\mathrm{Dep}(H)=\langle a,b\rangle
,
\mathrm{Dep}(K)=\langle a,c\rangle
, hence
\mathrm{Dep}(H)\cap\mathrm{Dep}(K)=\langle a\rangle
.

(ii) The inclusion is clear. As an example for a strict inclusion, let
F=F(\{a,b\})
,
H=\langle a^{2}b\rangle
and
K=\langle a^{4}b\rangle
. By Example
2.2
(iv),
\mathrm{Dep}(H)=H
and
\mathrm{Dep}(K)=K
. But
\langle H,K\rangle=\langle a^{2},b\rangle
and so,
\mathrm{Dep}(\langle H,K\rangle)=F
, which strictly contains
\langle a^{2},b\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Dep}(H),\mathrm{Dep}(K)\rangle
.
∎

3.
Stallings graph-theory techniques

Let
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\}
be an alphabet of
r
letters, let
A^{\pm}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a_{1}^{-1},\ldots,a_{r}^{-1}\}
be its formal involutive closure, and let
F(A)
be the free group on
A
(formally,
F(A)
is the free monoid
(A^{\pm})^{*}
on
A^{\pm}
, modulo the equivalence relation generated by the elementary reductions
a_{i}a_{i}^{-1}\sim a_{i}^{-1}a_{i}\sim 1
). In 1983, elaborating on previous ideas by several authors, Stallings
[
25
]
established the notion of so-called
Stallings
A
-automata
: oriented graphs (allowing loops and parallel edges) with labels from
A^{\pm}
at the edges, being
involutive
(i.e., for every edge
e
from
p
to
q
with label
a\in A
there is an edge
e^{-1}
from
q
to
p
labelled
a^{-1}
;

and
e^{-1}
are said to be
inverse
to each other), with a selected vertex called the
basepoint
(denoted here
\bp
), and being connected,
deterministic
(there are no two different edges with the same label coming from, or into, the same vertex) and
trim
(every vertex appears in some reduced closed path through the basepoint). A (nontrivial)
path
of
length

n\geqslant 1
is a concatenation of edges
\gamma=e_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots e_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
, such that
\tau e_{i}^{\varepsilon_{i}}=\iota e_{i+1}^{\varepsilon_{i+1}}
(the terminal vertex of an edge is the initial vertex of the following edge), for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. The label of
\gamma
is
\ell(\gamma)=(\ell(e_{1}))^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots(\ell(e_{n}))^{\varepsilon_{n}}
, the product of the labels of the edges, understood as an element of
F(A)
. The path is
reduced
if it has no backtracking, i.e., if
\varepsilon_{i}=\varepsilon_{i+1}
whenever
e_{i}=e_{i+1}
. Note, that in the deterministic case, a path
\gamma
is reduced if and only if its label
\ell(\gamma)
is a reduced word of
(A^{\pm})^{*}
. A path beginning and ending at vertex
p
is called a
closed path at
p
(a
p
-path
, for short). For later convenience, given a vertex
p
in a deterministic
A
-automaton
\Gamma
and given a reduced word
u\in F(A)
, we define
pu
to be the end of the unique possible path in
\Gamma
labelled
u
and starting at
p
; in case no such path exists,
pu
remains undefined.

For the sake of brevity, we may only mention and depict the positive subautomaton of
\Gamma
(i.e., that formed by the edges labelled by letters in
A
), denoted
\Gamma^{+}
, bearing in mind that, for each of such edges,
\Gamma
also contains its inverse (even if not depicted or mentioned). The

of a vertex in
\Gamma
is the number of edges of
\Gamma^{+}
incident to it.

With this notion, Stallings
[
25
]
established a bijection between the set of (free) subgroups of
F(A)
, and the set of isomorphism classes of Stallings

-automata.

Theorem 3.1

(Stallings,
[
25
]
)
.

The following is a bijection:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}St\colon\{H\leqslant F(A)\}&\longrightarrow&\{\mbox{isom. classes of Stallings $A$-automata}\}\\
H&\mapsto&\Gamma_{A}(H),\\
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)&\mapsfrom&\Gamma.\end{array}

Furthermore,
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F_{A}
if and only if
\Gamma_{A}(H)
is finite; in this case, both directions are computable. ∎

The map to the left consists of reading the
language

\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)\leqslant F(A)
of a given Stallings
A
-automaton
\Gamma
, i.e., the set of labels of reduced
\bp
-cycles (cycles that contain the basepoint) in
\Gamma
. As for the map to the right, it assigns to each subgroup

its

, being the core of the

of

of

.
One can think of the Schreier coset graph of

In this paper, the important part of the bijection in Theorem

In order to compute the map to the left, assuming that

and so,

).

In order to compute the map to the right, we need to use a key result in this setting:
“for every
H\leqslant F(A)
there exists a unique
A
-automaton (up to isomorphism) which is deterministic, trim, and has language
H
”
; it is called the
Stallings
A
-automaton
for

, and is denoted
\Gamma_{A}(H)
. Given a set

of reduced words on
A
, we can construct the Stallings
A
-automaton
\Gamma_{A}(H)
for the subgroup
H=\langle h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\rangle\leqslant F(A)
by applying the following procedure. First, for each
h_{i}
,

, we form the circular graph (called
petal
) whose

h_{i}
(or its inverse if traveled in the opposite direction). Then, we form the
wedge
of these circuits by identifying the
n

\bp
. The resulting
A
-automaton is called the

, denoted
\mathcal{F}(W)
. Note, that
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}(W))=H
. In order to gain determinism, we apply a finite series of

: whenever two edges sharing the same initial (resp., terminal) vertex have the same label, we identify them into a single edge, together with their terminal (resp., initial) vertices. When the two terminal as well as the two initial vertices are the same, the elementary folding is called

(or

) (see Figure
1

(or
homotopic
) (see Figure
1
(a),(b)), and the first Betti number remains unchanged. Note, that in a closed folding we identify two edges, whereas in an open folding we identify two edges as well as two vertices. In both cases, the language of the automaton remains unchanged. Of course, after performing an elementary folding, new possible foldings may emerge, but since at each step the number of edges strictly decreases, the process terminates after a finite number of steps. By construction, the resulting
A
-automaton is deterministic and has language
H
. It is also easy to see that it is trim (each vertex of the flower automaton, except possibly for the basepoint, has two incident edges of different labels, and they remain incident to the vertex throughout all the folding process). By the previous key result, the output of this process must be
\Gamma_{A}(H)
. In particular, this implies that the result of the folding process is independent of the specific sequence of foldings applied, and even of the set of generators for

we started with: it only depends on the subgroup

(and on the ambient basis

\Gamma

, and

. The natural map

induces an epimorphism
\varphi\colon\pi(\Gamma,\bp)\twoheadrightarrow\pi(\Gamma^{\prime},\bp)

An explicit description of the set of dependent elements

\Gamma_{0}\curvearrowright\Gamma_{1}\curvearrowright\Gamma_{2}\curvearrowright\cdots\curvearrowright\Gamma_{s}=\Gamma_{A}(\langle H,g\rangle)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}g\text{ is dependent on }H&\Leftrightarrow&\mathrm{rk\,}(\langle H,g\rangle)\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H)\\
&\Leftrightarrow&b_{1}(\Gamma_{A}(\langle H,g\rangle))\leqslant b_{1}(\Gamma_{0})-1\\
&\Leftrightarrow&\eqref{eq:cascade}\text{ contains at least one closed folding.}\end{array}

if and only if
g\in H
, or
g
goes into case (iii) with at least a closed folding occurring in the second part of the process. The above discussion leads us to an explicit description of
all
elements dependent on
H
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
F(A)
be the free group with basis
A
. Given a finite set of generators of total length
n
of a subgroup
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F(A)
, one can algorithmically compute in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{3}\log^{*}(n))
a finite set
\{g_{0}=1,g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\}
of elements of
F(A)
that depend on
H
, and such that
\mathrm{dep}(H)
is the disjoint union of the corresponding double cosets,
\mathrm{dep}(H)=H\sqcup Hg_{1}H\sqcup\cdots\sqcup Hg_{m}H
.

Proof.

Since
H
is finitely generated, there exists a finite subset
A_{0}\subseteq A
(computable in linear time), such that
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F(A_{0})\leqslant_{\textsf{ff}}F(A)
; moreover,
\mathrm{dep}_{F(A)}(H)=\mathrm{dep}_{F(A_{0})}(H)
. Thus, without loss of generality, we may assume that
A
is finite, say
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\}
.

First, we construct the finite Stallings
A
-automaton
\Gamma_{A}(H)
. Then, for each pair of distinct vertices
p,q\in V=V\Gamma_{A}(H)
, we form the graph
\Gamma_{A}(H)/(p=q)
by identifying these two vertices, and then we perform elementary foldings until reaching a deterministic
A
-automaton, say
\Gamma_{p=q}
. Let

(4.2)

R=\big\{(p,q)\in V\times V\mid p\neq q,\,\,b_{1}(\Gamma_{p=q})\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H)\big\}\subseteq V\times V\setminus\Delta,

where
\Delta=\{(p,p)\mid p\in V\}
is the diagonal of
V\times V
. In other words,
R
is the set of pairs
(p,q)\in V\times V\setminus\Delta
, for which the sequence of elementary foldings towards
\Gamma_{p=q}
contains at least one which is closed. Note, that
(p,q)\in R
implies that
(q,p)\in R
and also
(pa_{i}^{\varepsilon},qa_{i}^{\varepsilon})\in R
, for all
a_{i}\in A
and
\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}
, for which both
pa_{i}^{\varepsilon}
and
qa_{i}^{\varepsilon}
are defined.

Clearly, the discussion above tells us that

(4.3)

\mathrm{dep}(H)=H\sqcup\bigcup_{(p,q)\in R}S_{(p,q)},

where
S_{(p,q)}
is the set of elements
g\in F(A)
that fall into case (iii), that is,

S_{(p,q)}=\{g=g_{+}g_{-}^{-1}\mid g_{+}=\ell(\gamma_{p}),g_{-}=\ell(\gamma_{q}),\iota\gamma_{p}=\iota\gamma_{q}=\bp,\,\,\tau\gamma_{p}=p,\,\,\tau\gamma_{q}=q\}.

It only remains to observe that each of these sets
S_{(p,q)}
is the double coset
S_{(p,q)}=Hg_{(p,q)}H
, for some (any) representative
g_{(p,q)}\in S_{(p,q)}
. Since
H
is finitely generated,
\Gamma_{A}(H)
and
R
are finite, and so, the union in (
4.3
) is also finite. In addition, the coset representatives
g_{(p,q)}
are computable. Moreover, we can algorithmically check whether
HuH=HvH
, i.e.,
uH\cap Hv\neq\emptyset
, by using a solution to the coset intersection problem for free groups; see
[
5
]
. So, by writing
g_{(\bp,\bp)}=1
and
S_{(\bp,\bp)}=H
, and keeping in the set
\{g_{(p,q)}\mid(p,q)\in R\}
only one representative of each double coset, we obtain the required elements
g_{0}=1,g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}
, such that:

\mathrm{dep}(H)=\bigcup_{(p,q)\in R\sqcup\{(\bp,\bp)\}}S_{(p,q)}=H\sqcup Hg_{1}H\sqcup\cdots\sqcup Hg_{m}H.

If
n
denotes the total length of the input generators,
\Gamma_{A}(H)
can be computed in time
\mathcal{O}(n\log^{*}(n))
; see Touikan
[
27
]
. This
A
-automaton contains at most
n
vertices, and so,
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
pairs
(p,q)
. For each such pair
(p,q)
, we can check in time
\mathcal{O}(n\log^{*}(n))
whether, after identifying
p
with
q
, the elementary foldings towards
\Gamma_{p=q}
contain a closed folding. This makes a total time
\mathcal{O}(n^{3}\log^{*}(n))
to compute the set
R
.

Finally, the process of removing from
R
the excessive pairs of vertices, so that no two pairs determine the same double coset, can be done in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
. In order to do it, we first compute a spanning tree
T
of
\Gamma_{A}(H)
(in time
\mathcal{O}(n)
). Then, for each
p\in V\Gamma_{A}(H)
, we attach to
\Gamma_{A}(H)
a “hair”, labelled
\ell(T[\bp,p])
, that ends at the basepoint
\bp
. After making the necessary elementary foldings, we obtain a “hairy”
A
-automaton, denoted
\Gamma^{\prime}
, with at most
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
vertices. Next, we compute the pull-back of
\Gamma^{\prime}
with
\Gamma_{A}(H)
, which takes time
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
(see
[
5
]
). For each pair of vertices
p,q\in V\Gamma_{A}(H)
, let
u=\ell(T[\bp,p])
and
v=\ell(T[\bp,q])
. Then,
HuH=HvH
if and only if
uH\cap Hv\neq\emptyset
if and only if
(\bp u^{-1},\bp)
and
(\bp,q)
belong to the same connected component of the pull-back.

Altogether, the whole process can be done in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{3}\log^{*}(n))
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The algorithm for computing the representatives
g_{i}
of the double cosets whose union is
\mathrm{dep}(H)
can also be done using Nielsen transformations. Suppose that
u=\ell(T[\bp,p])
and
v=\ell(T[\bp,q])
, as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
. Let
S=W\cup\{g\}
, where
W
is a basis of
H
and
g=uv^{-1}
. Then, an occurrence of a closed elementary folding, after the identification of
p
with
q
, is equivalent to checking whether performing a sequence of Nielsen transformations to
S
leads to one of the generators being reduced to the trivial group element.

In fact, an identification of two edges of the same initial vertex and the same label
a
is equivalent to the reduction of a word in which the letter
a^{\varepsilon}
is followed by
a^{-\varepsilon}
.

Similar to performing Nielsen transformations but using a different representation is the following. We can represent each group element
g=uv^{-1}
as a
binomial

u-v
in the group ring
\mathbb{Z}_{2}F
of the free group
F
over the ring of integers modulo 2. Then, the analog of a Nielsen transformation is a reduction à la Gröbner bases, using, say, a
shortlex
order (comparing monomials by their length and using a lexicographic order for a tie-breaker); see
[
20
, Sec. 7]
. The advantage of this method is that it allows, in some cases, the generalization of theorems and algorithms from groups to group rings. On the other hand, Stallings graph-theory techniques, besides the new perspective and results they brought to the field of subgroups of free groups, show, in many cases, a simplified and unified approach: for example, the graph representation of the subgroup
H\leqslant F
does not depend on the generators of
H
but only on the chosen basis for
F
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Given a finite set of generators of total length
n
for a subgroup
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F(A)
, a generating set for
\mathrm{Dep}(H)
can be algorithmically computed in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{3}\log^{*}(n))
.

Proof.

Clearly, if
\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{s}\}
are the given generators for
H
, then
\mathrm{Dep}(H)=\langle h_{1},\ldots,h_{s},g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\rangle
, where
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}
are the elements computed in Theorem
4.1
. The result then follows.
∎

5.
Equations for a dependent element

Let
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F(A)
and let
g\in F(A)
be an element dependent on
H
. In this section we present two algorithms for computing a finite set of polynomials that generate
I_{H}(g)
(the ideal of all polynomials
w(x)
, such that
w(g)=1
) as a normal subgroup of
H*\langle x\rangle
. The first one is based on Nielsen transformations and the second one on Stallings graph theory techniques. Let us start with the easy case
g\in H
.

Observation 5.1
.

For
g\in H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F(A)
, we have
I_{H}(g)=\,\ll g^{-1}x\gg\unlhd H*\langle x\rangle
.

Proof.

Clearly,
g^{-1}x\in I_{H}(g)
. Let now
w(x)=1
be a reduced
H
-equation satisfying
w(g)=1
. We will show, by induction on the degree
d
of
w(x)
, that
w(x)
is in the normal subgroup
\ll g^{-1}x\gg\,=\,\ll gx^{-1}\gg
. For
d=1
, we have
w(x)=h_{0}x^{\varepsilon_{1}}h_{1}
and
h_{0}g^{\varepsilon_{1}}h_{1}=1
. So,
g^{-\varepsilon_{1}}=h_{1}h_{0}
and

w(x)=h_{0}x^{\varepsilon_{1}}h_{1}=h_{1}^{-1}(h_{1}h_{0}x^{\varepsilon_{1}})h_{1}=h_{1}^{-1}(g^{-\varepsilon_{1}}x^{\varepsilon_{1}})h_{1}\in\,\ll g^{-1}x\gg.

Assume now that the statement holds for degree
d-1
and let

w(x)=h_{0}x^{\varepsilon_{1}}h_{1}x^{\varepsilon_{2}}\cdots h_{d-1}x^{\varepsilon_{d}}h_{d}=1

be a reduced
H
-equation of degree
d
and satisfying
w(g)=1
. Then,
g
clearly satisfies the
H
-equation

w^{\prime}(x)=h_{0}x^{\varepsilon_{1}}h_{1}x^{\varepsilon_{2}}\cdots x^{\varepsilon_{d-1}}(h_{d-1}g^{\varepsilon_{d}}h_{d})=1

of degree
d-1
, and so, by the induction hypothesis,
w^{\prime}(x)\in\,\ll g^{-1}x\gg
. It follows that

w(x)=(h_{0}x^{\varepsilon_{1}}h_{1}x^{\varepsilon_{2}}\cdots x^{\varepsilon_{d-1}}h_{d-1}g^{\varepsilon_{d}}h_{d})(h_{d}^{-1}g^{-\varepsilon_{d}}x^{\varepsilon_{d}}h_{d})=w^{\prime}(x)(g^{-\varepsilon_{d}}x^{\varepsilon_{d}})^{h_{d}},

which also belongs to
\ll g^{-1}x\gg
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F(A)
be of rank
n
and let
1\neq g\in F(A)
be dependent on
H
, such that
\mathrm{rk\,}(\langle H,g\rangle)=m\leqslant n
. Then, there exists an algorithm that, given a finite set of generators for
H
, computes a set of
n+1-m
normal generators of the ideal
I_{H}(g)\unlhd H*\langle x\rangle
:

I_{H}(g)=\,\ll w_{1}(x),\ldots,w_{n-m+1}(x)\gg.

Proof (using Nielsen transformations).

Let
\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\}
be a set of free generators of
H
. As in (
1
), we consider the epimorphism

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\varphi_{H,g}\colon H*\langle x\rangle&\twoheadrightarrow&\langle H,g\rangle\leqslant F(A)\\
h_{1}&\mapsto&h_{1},\\
&\vdots&\\
h_{n}&\mapsto&h_{n},\\
x&\mapsto&g.\end{array}

By applying a sequence of Nielsen transformations, the tuple
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n},g)
can be transformed into the tuple
(1,\ldots,1,b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
, consisting of
n-m+1
trivial elements, followed by a basis
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}
of
\langle H,g\rangle
:

(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n},g)\looparrowright\cdots\looparrowright(1,\ldots,1,b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}).

Applying the same sequence of transformations to the tuple
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n},x
) results in a new basis
\{w_{1}(x),\ldots,w_{n+1}(x)\}
of the free group
H*\langle x\rangle
. The epimorphism
\varphi_{H,g}
, expressed in this new basis, is

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\varphi_{H,g}\colon H*\langle x\rangle&\twoheadrightarrow&\langle H,g\rangle\\
w_{1}(x)&\mapsto&1,\\
&\vdots&\\
w_{n-m+1}(x)&\mapsto&1,\\
w_{n-m+2}(x)&\mapsto&b_{1},\\
&\vdots&\\
w_{n+1}(x),&\mapsto&b_{m}.\end{array}

Since
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}
is a basis of
\langle H,g\rangle
, the restriction of
\varphi_{H,g}
to
\langle w_{n-m+2}(x),\ldots
,
w_{n+1}(x)\rangle
is injective, thus the kernel of
\varphi_{H,g}
is

\ker\varphi_{H,g}=\,\ll w_{1}(x),\ldots,w_{n-m+1}(x)\gg,

the normal subgroup of
H*\langle x\rangle
, normally generated by
w_{1}(x),\ldots,w_{n-m+1}(x)
.
∎

Proof (using Stallings techniques).

An alternative way to obtain a set of normal generators for the ideal
I_{H}(g)
is through Stallings techniques. The basic case
g\in H
was already covered in Observation
5.1
: in this case
\langle H,g\rangle=H
,
m=n
,
n-m+1=1
, and
I_{H}(g)
is normally generated by
g^{-1}x
.

So, let us assume that
g
is dependent on
H
and
g\notin H
, i.e., case (iii) in the above discussion. Let
W=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\}
be a free basis for
H
and let
\mathcal{F}(W\cup\{g\})
be the corresponding flower automaton. We will now perform a series of elementary foldings towards the deterministic
A
-automaton
\Gamma_{A}(\langle H,g\rangle)
, in an order that suits our needs. At the first stage we only fold the elements of
W
and leave the petal
g
as it is, obtaining the automaton
\Gamma_{0}
, as in (
4.1
), which is the wedge of
\Gamma_{A}(H)
with the petal labelled
g
:

\mathcal{F}(W\cup\{g\})\curvearrowright\cdots\curvearrowright\Gamma_{0}.

Note that, up to now, all the foldings are open because
W
is a free basis for
H
. In a second stage, we integrate
g
in
\Gamma_{A}(\langle H,g\rangle)
through a series of elementary foldings as in (
4.1
), but doing
only open foldings
, until no more possible open foldings are available: first we fold the maximal initial and terminal segments of the petal labelled
g
until getting the automaton
\Gamma_{A}(H)/p=q
, and then we keep applying
open
foldings until no more are available; denote
\Gamma_{p,q}
the resulting automaton:

\Gamma_{0}\curvearrowright\cdots\curvearrowright\Gamma_{A}(H)/(p=q)\curvearrowright\cdots\curvearrowright\Gamma_{p,q}.

The automaton
\Gamma_{p,q}
is not yet necessarily deterministic: it could contain several closed foldings to be done. But note that, after doing all of them, no more identifications of vertices are happening, no new foldings are introduced and so, we get directly the final deterministic output

\Gamma_{p,q}\,\widehat{\,\curvearrowright}\,\,\Gamma_{A}(\langle H,g\rangle).

Let us introduce notation for this final step:
\Gamma_{p,q}
contains, say
r\geqslant 0
, blocks of edges (possibly loops) parallel to each other, namely
\{e_{i,0},e_{i,1},\ldots,e_{i,k_{i}}\}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
, with
\iota e_{i,0}=\iota e_{i,1}=\cdots=\iota e_{i,k_{i}}
, and
\tau e_{i,0}=\tau e_{i,1}=\cdots\tau e_{i,k_{i}}
, and
\ell(e_{i,0})=\ell(e_{i,1})=\cdots=\ell(e_{i,k_{i}})
. This final step, denoted
\widehat{\,\curvearrowright}
, aggregates

\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}=n-m+1

closed foldings, and consists simply on identifying each block into a single edge; alternatively, we can look at it as deleting the edges
e_{i,1},\ldots,e_{i,k_{i}}
and leaving only
e_{i,0}
from each block. The whole process of folding looks like this

(5.1)

\mathcal{F}(W\cup\{g\})\curvearrowright\cdots\curvearrowright\Gamma_{0}\curvearrowright\cdots\curvearrowright\Gamma_{A}(H)/(p=q)\curvearrowright\cdots\curvearrowright\Gamma_{p,q}\widehat{\,\curvearrowright}\,\Gamma_{A}(\langle H,g\rangle),

Next, we will show how to construct
n-m+1
non-trivial
H
-equations
w_{i,j}(x)=1
satisfied by
g
for each of the edges
e_{i,j}
,
i=1,\ldots,r
,
j=1,\ldots,k_{i}
. Then, we will prove that the polynomials
w_{i,j}(x)
generate the ideal
I_{H}(g)
as a normal subgroup of
H*\langle x\rangle
.

For each
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
, let
\gamma_{i}
be a reduced path in
\Gamma_{p,q}
from
\bp
to
\iota e_{i,0}=\cdots=\iota e_{i,k_{i}}
, which does not contain any of the edges
e_{i,0},\ldots,e_{i,k_{i}}
(or their inverses) (if this is not possible, we define
e_{i,0},\ldots,e_{i,k_{i}}
to be in the reverse direction, and then take a path
\gamma_{i}
as above - this is always possible since every vertex lies on a reduced
\bp
-cycle). For each
i=1,\ldots,r
and for each
j=1,\ldots,k_{i}
, let now
\gamma_{i,j}
be the reduced non-trivial
\bp
-cycle

\gamma_{i,j}=\gamma_{i}e_{i,j}e_{i,0}^{-1}\gamma_{i}^{-1}.

Let
a^{\varepsilon}
be the label of
e_{i,j}
. Then, the label of
\gamma_{i,j}
is

\ell(\gamma_{i,j})=\ell(\gamma_{i})a^{\varepsilon}a^{-\varepsilon}\ell(\gamma_{i})^{-1}=1;

this is capturing the algebraic essence of the closed folding given by the pair of edges
e_{i,0}
and
e_{i,j}
. In the next stage, we lift
\gamma_{i,j}
all the way up to
\mathcal{F}(W\cup\{g\})
by unfolding the open foldings in a reverse order of (
5.1
) to obtain a
\bp
-cycle
\widehat{\gamma}_{i,j}
.
The
\bp
-cycle
\widehat{\gamma}_{i,j}
in
\mathcal{F}(W\cup\{g\})
is non-trivial and (topologically) reduced. So, it spells a non-trivial, reduced word, denoted
w_{i,j}(g)
, on
W\cup\{g\}
, but reads the trivial element
\ell(\widehat{\gamma}_{i,j})=\ell(\gamma_{i,j})=1
. Replacing the occurrences of
g
by
x
(and of
g^{-1}
by
x^{-1}
) in
w_{i,j}(g)
, results in a reduced, non-trivial polynomial
w_{i,j}(x)
, satisfying
w_{i,j}(g)=1
(note, that
x
must occur in
w_{i,j}(x)
at least once, because
W
is a free basis of
H
).

Let us rename the
n-m+1
polynomials
w_{i,j}(x)
obtained this way by
w_{1}(x),\ldots,

w_{n-m+1}(x)
. We finish the proof by showing that they generate normally the ideal
I_{H}(g)
. So, let
w(x)\in I_{H}(g)
be an arbitrary reduced polynomial over
H
, satisfying
w(g)=1
. Then,
w(x)
corresponds to a reduced
\bp
-cycle
\gamma
in the flower
A
-automaton
\mathcal{F}(W\cup\{g\})
. Note, that the condition
w(g)=1
means that
\ell(\gamma)=1
. We project
\gamma
down the open foldings in the cascade (
5.1
) until getting a reduced
\bp
-cycle
\gamma^{\prime}
in
\Gamma_{p,q}
, satisfying
\ell(\gamma^{\prime})=1
. We are going to prove our goal
w(x)\in\,\ll w_{1}(x),\ldots,w_{n-m+1}(x)\gg
by induction on the number of occurrences, say
s
, of the duplicated edges
e_{i,j}
,
i=1,\ldots,r
,
j=1,\ldots,k_{i}
, in
\gamma^{\prime}
. If
s=0
then the same path
\gamma^{\prime}
is, in fact, also a reduced
\bp
-cycle in
\Gamma_{A}(\langle H,g\rangle)
because the closed foldings do not affect it. But
\Gamma_{A}(\langle H,g\rangle)
is a deterministic
A
-automaton and therefore
\gamma^{\prime}
is the trivial path. We claim that this implies that
\gamma
is also the trivial path in
\mathcal{F}(W\cup\{g\})
. The reason is that all the foldings on the way from
\mathcal{F}(W\cup\{g\})
to
\Gamma_{p,q}
are open, and after an unfolding of an open folding, a trivial path consisting of a single point remains a single point (which may not be the case when unfolding and reducing a closed folding). It follows that
w(x)\,\in\,\ll~w_{1}(x),\ldots,w_{n-m+1}(x)~\gg
. This proves the base of the induction.

Suppose now that the result holds for equations determining paths with
s
occurrences of the duplicated edges
e_{i,j}
and let
w(x)\in I_{g}(H)
be an equation determining a path
\gamma
with
s+1
such occurrences. Then,
\gamma
can be written as
\gamma=\alpha e_{i,j}^{\varepsilon}\beta
, with
j\geqslant 1
and
\alpha
containing no duplicated edge
e_{i,j}
,
i=1,\ldots,r
,
j=1,\ldots,k_{i}
(in either direction). Next, we decompose
\gamma
in the following form:

\gamma=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha e_{i,j}\beta=(\alpha\gamma_{i}^{-1})(\gamma_{i}e_{i,j}e_{i,0}^{-1}\gamma_{i}^{-1})(\gamma_{i}\alpha^{-1})(\alpha e_{i,0}\beta),&\text{ if }\varepsilon=1;\\
\alpha e_{i,j}^{-1}\beta=(\alpha e_{i,0}^{-1}\beta)(\beta^{-1}\gamma_{i}^{-1})(\gamma_{i}e_{i,0}e_{i,j}^{-1}\gamma_{i}^{-1})(\gamma_{i}\beta),&\text{ if }\varepsilon=-1.\end{array}\right.

Lifting
\gamma
up to
\mathcal{F}(W\cup\{g\})
, we get

\widehat{\gamma}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\widehat{\gamma_{i}\alpha^{-1}})^{-1}\widehat{\gamma}_{i,j}(\widehat{\gamma_{i}\alpha^{-1}})(\widehat{\alpha e_{i,0}\beta}),&\text{ if }\varepsilon=1;\\
(\widehat{\alpha e_{i,0}^{-1}\beta})(\widehat{\gamma_{i}\beta})^{-1}\widehat{\gamma}_{i,j}^{-1}(\widehat{\gamma_{i}\beta}),&\text{ if }\varepsilon=-1,\end{array}\right.

which, when translated back into
H
-polynomials, means that
w(x)
equals the product of a conjugate of one of the polynomials
w_{1}(x)^{\pm},\ldots,w_{n-m+1}(x)^{\pm}
and the polynomial corresponding to
(\widehat{\alpha e_{i,0}^{\varepsilon}\beta})
, say
w^{\prime}(x)
. Observe that
w^{\prime}(g)=1
as well, and that the corresponding path has
s
occurrences of duplicated edges (since the first one,
e_{i,j}^{\varepsilon}
, was replaced by
e_{i,0}^{\varepsilon}
). By the induction hypothesis, we have
w^{\prime}(x)\in\,\ll w_{1}(x),\ldots,w_{n-m+1}(x)\gg
and therefore, so does
w(x)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Given
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F
, one can effectively compute an integer
d
, such that each
g\in F
dependent on
H
satisfies an equation over
H
of degree at most
d
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
, we can compute elements
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\in F
dependent on
H
, so that
\mathrm{dep}(H)=H\sqcup Hg_{1}H\sqcup\cdots\sqcup Hg_{m}H
. Now, by Theorem
5.2
, we can effectively compute an
H
-equation
w_{i}(x)=1
of degree, say,
d_{i}
for each
g_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
. Since, by Observation
1.5
, all the elements of the form
hg_{i}h^{\prime}\in Hg_{i}H
satisfy an
H
-equation of degree less than or equal to
d_{i}
, it follows that each dependent element satisfies an equation of degree at most
d=\max\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}\}
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

We have seen that when
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F
then the set of elements that depend on
H
is of the form
\mathrm{dep}(H)=H\sqcup Hg_{1}H\sqcup\cdots\sqcup Hg_{m}H
. Let

J=\bigcup_{i=0}^{m}I_{H}(g_{i})\subseteq H*\langle x\rangle,

where
g_{0}=1
. Then, the set of
H
-polynomials which are annihilated by some element dependent on
H
is the union, over the set of all automorphisms of
H*\langle x\rangle
that fix

6.
Compressed subgroups

Let us look at some special classes of subgroups of a finitely generated free group
F
and see whether they are preserved under the dependence operator. These classes form a chain with respect to inclusion, where at the top of the chain are compressed subgroups and at the bottom free factors; see Figure

.

A subgroup
H\leqslant F
is called
inert
if
\mathrm{rk\,}(H\cap K)\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(K)
, for every
K\leqslant F
, and it is called
compressed
when
\mathrm{rk\,}(H)\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(K)
, for every
H\leqslant K\leqslant F
(i.e., the same property required only for those subgroups
K
containing
H
); see
[
6
]
. Of course, every inert subgroup of
F
is compressed, and it is an open problem whether compressed subgroups in free groups are inert. It is also clear that compressed subgroups of
F
cannot have rank larger than that of the ambient group
F
.

A subgroup
H\leqslant F_{r}
is an
echelon
subgroup if there exists an ordered basis
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\}
of
F_{r}
, such that
0\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})-\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i-1})\leqslant 1
for
i=1,\ldots,r
, where
H_{0}=\{1\}
and
H_{i}=H\cap\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}\rangle
,
i=1,\ldots,r
(we also say then that
H
is in
echelon form w.r.t.
A
); see
[
22
]
. Note, that the inequality
0\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})-\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i-1})
always holds because
H_{i-1}\leqslant_{\textsf{ff}}H_{i}
. Clearly, every free factor of
F
is an echelon subgroup.

The family of echelon subgroups is a subclass of a broader class, called
1-gen-endo
, which was shown by Rosenmann
[
22
]
to be inert. A 1-gen-endo subgroup
H
is formed through a finite number of transformations. We start with a basis
B
of a free group
F
and let
H_{1}
be the subgroup generated by the elements of

except for some

that is replaced by some element

. Suppose now that at step
i
we have a subgroup
H_{i}\leqslant F
. Then, in step
i+1
, we form
H_{i+1}
by replacing some element
h_{j}
of a basis

of
H_{i}
by an element
h^{\prime}_{j}\in H_{i}
. That is,
H_{i+1}
is the image of the
H_{i}
-endomorphism which sends
h_{j}
to

and all other basis elements are mapped to themselves. We stop after finitely-many steps and the resulting subgroup is
H
. It is easy to see that every echelon subgroup can be formed through a finite number of such transformations.

We also note, that the class of inert subgroups is still larger since e.g. every subgroup of rank 2 is inert, and there are subgroups of rank 2 that are not 1-gen-endo subgroups.

Let
\mathrm{Aut}(F)
be the group of automorphisms of
F
. A subgroup
H\leqslant F
is called

if

; see
[

]
. Clearly, this class includes free factors of

.
In Dicks–Ventura

it was proved that 1-auto-fixed subgroups of finitely generated free groups are inert (recently, this has been generalized to 1-endo-fixed subgroups by Antolín–Jaikin
[

). Also, an explicit description of 1-auto-fixed subgroups of free groups was given in Martino–Ventura
[

, and from this it follows that 1-auto-fixed subgroups are in echelon form w.r.t. an appropriate ambient basis; see also
[

]
.

6.1.
Free factors

We start with the simplest class, that of free factors of

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
F_{1}=F(A_{1})

F_{2}=F(A_{2})
, where

It is clear that

. The Stallings automaton

of

is the disjoint union of

and

. We claim that

Clearly,

since each of the pairs

and in

results in a rank reduction after identifying

6.2.
The dependence subgroup of an 1-auto-fixed subgroup

In the following example we show that the dependence subgroup of

, for

, is not necessarily fixed by
\alpha
. This does not exclude the possibility of
\mathrm{Dep}(H)
being fixed by another automorphism, as is indeed the case in this example.

Example 6.2
.

Let
F=F({a,b})
and let
\alpha\colon F\to F
be the automorphism defined by

,

. Then,
H=\mathrm{Fix}(\alpha)=\langle a,bab^{-1}\rangle
, and
\mathrm{Dep}(H)=F
is not fixed by

6.3.
The dependence subgroup of an echelon subgroup

For the class of echelon subgroups, we show that it is closed under the dependence operator.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
F(A)
be the free group on
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\}
, and let
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F(A)
be an echelon subgroup w.r.t.
A
. Then,
\mathrm{Dep}(H)
is also an echelon subgroup w.r.t.
A
.

Proof.

Let
F_{0}=\{1\}
and
F_{i}=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}\rangle\leqslant_{\textsf{ff}}F
, for
i=1,\ldots,r
. Also, let
H_{i}=H\cap F_{i}\leqslant_{\textsf{ff}}H
and
D_{i}=\mathrm{Dep}(H)\cap F_{i}\leqslant_{\textsf{ff}}\mathrm{Dep}(H)
, for
i=0,\ldots,r
. Since
F_{i-1}\leqslant_{\textsf{ff}}F_{i}
then
H_{i-1}\leqslant_{\textsf{ff}}H_{i}
,
D_{i-1}\leqslant_{\textsf{ff}}D_{i}
and
\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i-1})\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})
,
\mathrm{rk\,}(D_{i-1})\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(D_{i})
, for
i=1,\ldots,r
. Moreover, observe that when
\mathrm{rk\,}(D_{i})=\mathrm{rk\,}(D_{i-1})
then
D_{i}=D_{i-1}
and this implies that
H_{i}=H_{i-1}
. Now, suppose that
H
is in echelon form w.r.t.
A
, i.e.,
0\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})-\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i-1})\leqslant 1
, for each
i
. Then
\mathrm{rk\,}(H)
equals the number of times that
\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})=\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i-1})+1
. Since an equality in ranks,
\mathrm{rk\,}(D_{i})=\mathrm{rk\,}(D_{i-1})
, implies
H_{i}=H_{i-1}
, we deduce that
\mathrm{rk\,}(D_{i})-\mathrm{rk\,}(D_{i-1})\geqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})-\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i-1})
, for each
i=1,\ldots,r
. Therefore,

\mathrm{rk\,}(\mathrm{Dep}(H))=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\mathrm{rk\,}(D_{i})-\mathrm{rk\,}(D_{i-1}))\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})-\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i-1}))=\mathrm{rk\,}(H).

But, by Proposition
2.4
,
\mathrm{rk\,}(\mathrm{Dep}(H))\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H)
. Hence, the above inequality is an equality and thus, for each
i=1,\ldots,r
,

0\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(D_{i})-\mathrm{rk\,}(D_{i-1})=\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i})-\mathrm{rk\,}(H_{i-1})\leqslant 1.

Thus,
\mathrm{Dep}(H)
is in echelon form w.r.t
A
.
∎

6.4.
The dependence subgroup of a compressed subgroup

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
F
be a free group and let
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F
be a compressed subgroup. Then,
\mathrm{Dep}(H)
is compressed, with
\mathrm{rk\,}(\mathrm{Dep}(H))=\mathrm{rk\,}(H)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4
,
\mathrm{rk\,}(\mathrm{Dep}(H))\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H)
, but since
H\leqslant\mathrm{Dep}(H)
and
H
is compressed then
\mathrm{rk\,}(\mathrm{Dep}(H))\geqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H)
. It follows that
\mathrm{rk\,}(\mathrm{Dep}(H))=\mathrm{rk\,}(H)
. Let now
K
be an arbitrary subgroup with
\mathrm{Dep}(H)\leqslant K\leqslant F
. Then, since
H\leqslant K
and
H
is compressed, we have

\mathrm{rk\,}(K)\geqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H)=\mathrm{rk\,}(\mathrm{Dep}(H)),

which proves that
\mathrm{Dep}(H)
is compressed.
∎

Despite the existence of these arbitrarily long dependence sequences, the dependence length,
\mathrm{depl}(H)
, of a finitely generated subgroup
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F
is finite, and its dependence closure,

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F(A)
and let
n
be the total length of the given generators of
H
. Then

(i)

\mathrm{depl}(H)\leqslant n
and is computable;

(ii)

\mathrm{rk\,}(\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H))\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H)
and one can effectively compute a basis for
\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H)
in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{4}\log^{*}(n))
.

Proof.

(i) Consider the ascending sequence
H\leqslant\mathrm{Dep}^{1}(H)\leqslant\mathrm{Dep}^{2}(H)\leqslant\cdots
of subgroups of
F(A)
. At each step, when passing from
\mathrm{Dep}^{i}(H)
to
\mathrm{Dep}^{i+1}(H)
, we identify at least one pair of vertices of the automaton, which does not result in an increase of rank. When no such pair exits anymore, the resulting dependence-closed subgroup is
\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H)
. Since the sum of the lengths of the generators is
n
, the process consists of at most
n
steps, i.e.,
\mathrm{depl}(H)\leqslant n
,

(ii) The fact that
\mathrm{rk\,}(\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H))\leqslant\mathrm{rk\,}(H)
follows from Proposition
2.4
and from Lemma
2.3
. By Corollary
4.3
, given a set of generators for
\mathrm{Dep}^{i}(H)
, we can compute a basis for
\mathrm{Dep}^{i+1}(H)
in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{3}\log^{*}(n))
. Since the dependence length is at most
n
, a basis for
\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H)
can be computed in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{4}\log^{*}(n))
.

In fact, we can compute a set of generators for
\widehat{\mathrm{Dep}}(H)
directly from
\Gamma_{A}(H)
instead of computing the generators for all the intermediate subgroups
\mathrm{Dep}^{i}(H)
, and so, accelerate the process. Simply, whenever two vertices
p
and
q
are identified in an automaton
\Gamma
and, after performing all the needed foldings, the new automaton
\Gamma^{\prime}
is of degree at most that of
\Gamma
, then we continue the identification of next pairs of elements in
\Gamma^{\prime}
instead of
\Gamma
.
∎

When we are just interested in the question of deciding whether the subgroup
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F(A)
is dependence-closed then we only need to check if
\mathrm{Dep}(H)=H
.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
H\leqslant_{\textsf{fg}}F(A)
and let
n
be the total length of the given generators of
H
. Then, it is algorithmically decidable in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{3}\log^{*}(n))
whether
H
is dependence-closed or not.

Proof.

In order to decide whether
\mathrm{Dep}(H)\neq H
, we only need to check (in the worst case) all pairs of distinct vertices
(p,q)
of the automaton
\Gamma_{A}(H)
and see whether, after identifying
p
with
q
, the following elementary foldings are all open. By Corollary
4.3
, this can be done in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{3}\log^{*}(n))
.
∎

Open problems

We gather here some questions that we think are interesting for further research.

Question 8.1

(answered by Dario Ascari
[
2
]
)
.

Given

and an element

dependent on

Question 8.2
.

Is the class of 1-auto-fixed subgroups closed under the dependence operator? And the class of 1-gen-endo subgroups? and that of inert subgroups?

Question 8.3
.

Can one extend the definitions and results in this paper to other classes of groups beside free groups? What are the “right” definitions of dependence in these cases (see Remark
1.2
)?

Question 8.4
.

Is there a finitely presented group
G
where (some of) the above questions
are algorithmic unsolvable? In the affirmative case, construct such a group.

Question 8.5
.

Can one develop a similar theory for multi-variate equations over a subgroup

? Here, also, one has to find the “right” definition of dependence.

The arithmetic of tame quotient singularities in dimension 2 2
Giulio Bresciani

Abstract
. Let k k be a field, X X a variety with tame quotient singularities and X ~ → X \tilde{X}\to X a resolution of singularities. Any smooth rational point x ∈ X ⁡ ( k ) x\in X(k) lifts to X ~ \tilde{X} by the Lang-Nishimura theorem, but if x x is singular this might be false. For certain types of singularities the rational point is guaranteed to lift, though; these are called singularities of type R \mathrm{R} . This concept has applications in the study of the fields of moduli of varieties and yields an enhanced version of the Lang-Nishimura theorem where the smoothness assumption is relaxed. We classify completely the tame quotient singularities of type R \mathrm{R} in dimension 2 2 ; in particular, we show that every non-cyclic tame quotient singularity in dimension 2 2 is of type R \mathrm{R} , and most cyclic singularities are of type R \mathrm{R} too.

Corollary 12
.

1.2.

Since we are assuming

If

Let

If

Let

be the associated variety of the singularity
[

, §6.4]

Remark 16
.

Another way of constructing singularities with particular arithmetic properties, e.g. being non-liftable, is to use a theorem of J. Kollár

which classifies twisted forms of a singularity in terms of Galois cohomology. While for cyclic singularities in dimension

2.
\mathrm{R}_{2}
-groups in characteristic
0

Once we have a complete classification of
\mathrm{R}
-singularities in dimension

\mathrm{R}_{2}
by checking every finite subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
. We do this in every characteristic for abelian groups, and in characteristic

for non-abelian ones. Since most finite groups are
\mathrm{R}_{2}
, our point of view will be that of searching for groups

of type

.

Proposition 17
.

Let

be a finite abelian group and

or a prime not dividing

. Then

is

in characteristic

if and only if

for some

.

Proof.

The representation
(x,y)\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}\zeta_{2ab}^{2bx+y}&0\\
0&\zeta_{2ab}^{y}\end{pmatrix}
of

generates a singularity of type

, hence

is
\neg\mathrm{R}_{2}
by Theorem
4
.

On the other hand, assume that
G
has a faithful representation

generating a singularity which is not of type
\mathrm{R}
. Since
G
is abelian, up to conjugation we may assume that the matrices of

are diagonal. We may write

for some

, we want to show that

is even. By cardinality reasons,
C_{a}\times C_{a}\subseteq G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K)
is the set of all diagonal matrices whose order is finite and divides

. Because of this,
C_{a}\times C_{a}
is contained in the subgroup

of

generated by pseudoreflections. Since the associated singularity is not of type

, the index of

is even by Theorem

. The index of

divides

, which is thus even, as desired.
∎

We now study non-abelian groups of type

in characteristic

, which essentially goes back to Klein, and of which Cohen
[
Coh76
]
gives a modern account. Furthermore, we use Shephard and Todd’s
[

]
classification of finite subgroups of

generated by pseudoreflections. Again, Cohen
[

]

∎

5.2.1.

Since

It is easy to see that the class
\omega

in

Representation ring of Levi subgroups versus cohomology ring of flag varieties III
Shrawan Kumar and Jiale Xie

Abstract
. For any reductive group G G and a parabolic subgroup P P with its Levi subgroup L L , the first author [Ku2] introduced a ring homomorphism ξ λ P : Rep λ − poly ℂ ⁡ ( L ) → H ∗ ​ ( G / P , ℂ ) \xi^{P}_{\lambda}:\Rep^{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda-\poly}(L)\to H^{*}(G/P,\mathbb{C}) , where Rep λ − poly ℂ ⁡ ( L ) \Rep^{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda-\poly}(L) is a certain subring of the complexified representation ring of L L (depending upon the choice of an irreducible representation V ⁡ ( λ ) V(\lambda) of G G with highest weight λ \lambda ). In this paper we study this homomorphism for G = SO ⁡ ( 2 ​ n ) G=\SO(2n) and its maximal parabolic subgroups P n − k P_{n-k} for any 2 ≤ k ≤ n − 1 2\leq k\leq n-1 (with the choice of V ⁡ ( λ ) V(\lambda) to be the defining representation V ⁡ ( ω 1 ) V(\omega_{1}) in ℂ 2 ​ n \mathbb{C}^{2n} ). Thus, we obtain a ℂ \mathbb{C} -algebra homomorphism ξ n , k D : Rep ω 1 − poly ℂ ⁡ ( SO ⁡ ( 2 ​ k ) ) → H ∗ ​ ( OG ⁡ ( n − k , 2 ​ n ) , ℂ ) \xi_{n,k}^{D}:\Rep^{\mathbb{C}}_{\omega_{1}-\poly}(\SO(2k))\to H^{*}(\OG(n-k,2n),\mathbb{C}) . We determine this homomorphism explicitly in the paper. We further analyze the behavior of ξ n , k D \xi_{n,k}^{D} when n n tends to ∞ \infty keeping k k fixed and show that ξ n , k \xi_{n,k} becomes injective in the limit. We also determine explicitly (via some computer calculation) the homomorphism ξ λ P \xi^{P}_{\lambda} for all the exceptional groups G G (with a specific ‘minimal’ choice of λ \lambda ) and all their maximal parabolic subgroups except E 8 E_{8} .

Introduction

This is a follow-up of the papers
[
Ku2
]
and
[
KR
]
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
with Borel subgroup
B
and maximal torus
T\subset B
. Let
P
be a standard parabolic subgroup with Levi subgroup
L
containing
T
. Let
W
(resp.
W_{L}
) be the Weyl group of
G
(resp.
L
). Let
V(\lambda)
be an irreducible almost faithful representation of
G
with highest weight
\lambda
, i.e.,
\lambda
is a dominant integral weight and the corresponding map
\rho_{\lambda}:G\to\GL(V(\lambda))
has finite kernel. Then, Springer defined an adjoint-equivariant regular map with Zariski dense image from the group to its Lie algebra,
\theta_{\lambda}:G\to\mathfrak{[}g]
, which depends on
\lambda
(cf.
[
BR
]
).

Using the Springer morphism
\theta_{\lambda}
, Kumar
[
Ku2
]
defined the
\lambda
-polynomial representation ring
\text{Rep}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda-\text{poly}}(L)
, which is a subring of the complexified representation ring
\text{Rep}^{\mathbb{C}}(L)
. For
G=\GL(n)
and
V(\lambda)
the defining representation
V(\omega_{1})=\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the ring
\Rep_{\omega_{1}-\poly}(G):=\Rep^{\mathbb{C}}_{\omega_{1}-\poly}(G)\cap\Rep(G)

coincides with the standard polynomial representation ring
\Rep_{\poly}(G)
of
\GL(n)
.

Kumar
[
Ku2
]

also defined a surjective
\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism

(1)

\xi^{P}_{\lambda}:\text{Rep}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda-\text{poly}}(L)\to H^{*}(G/P,\mathbb{C}).

This map generalizes (cf.
[
Ku2
, Theorem 8]
) the classical ring homomorphism

\phi_{n,r}:\text{Rep}_{\text{poly}}(\text{GL}(r))\to H^{*}(\text{Gr}(r,n),\mathbb{Z}),

where
\text{Gr}(r,n)
is the Grassmannian of
r
-planes in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which is also the quotient
\text{GL}(n)/P_{r}
for the maximal parabolic subgroup
P_{r}
of
\text{GL}(n)
obtained from deleting the
r
-th node in the Dynkin diagram of

\SL(n)
.

In a subsequent work, Kumar–Rogers
[
KR
]
explicitly determined the homormorphism
\xi^{P}_{\omega_{1}}
for the classical groups
G
of types
B
and
C
and the maximal parabolic subgroups, where
\omega_{1}
is the first fundamental weight. Moreover, in these cases, they further analysed the map
\xi^{P}_{\omega_{1}}
in the limit described below:

For type
C_{n}
, i.e.,
G=\text{Sp}(2n)
(the symplectic group) and any positive integer
0<k<n
, consider the standard maximal parabolic subgroup
P^{C}_{n-k}
corresponding to the
(n-k)
-th node of the Dynkin diagram of
\text{Sp}(2n)
, and let
L^{C}_{n-k}
be its Levi subgroup. Then, the quotient
\text{Sp}(2n)/P^{C}_{n-k}
is the isotropic Grassmannian
\IG(n-k,2n)
, and we have

L^{C}_{n-k}\simeq\text{GL}(n-k)\times\text{Sp}(2k).

Thus, following (
1
), we get a ring homomorphism

\xi^{P^{C}_{n-k}}_{\omega_{1}}:\text{Rep}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\omega_{1}-\text{poly}}(L^{C}_{n-k})\to H^{*}(\text{IG}(n-k,2n),\mathbb{C}).

Restricting
\xi^{P^{C}_{n-k}}_{\omega_{1}}
to the component
\text{Sp}(2k)
, we get a ring homomorphism

\xi^{C}_{n,k}:\text{Rep}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\omega_{1}-\text{poly}}(\text{Sp}(2k))\to H^{*}(\text{IG}(n-k,2n),\mathbb{C}).

Define the stable cohomology ring

\mathbb{H}^{*}(\text{IG}_{k},\mathbb{C}):=\varprojlim_{n}H^{*}(\text{IG}(n-k,2n),\mathbb{C})

as the inverse limit under a certain embedding of
\IG(n-k,2n)\hookrightarrow\IG(n+1-k,2(n+1))
(cf.
[
KR
, Definition 15]
). Then, the homomorphisms
(\xi^{C}_{n,k})_{n>k}
combine to give a ring homomorphism

\xi^{C}_{k}:\text{Rep}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\omega_{1}-\text{poly}}(\text{Sp}(2k))\to\mathbb{H}^{*}(\text{IG}_{k},\mathbb{C}).

Kumar–Rogers proved that the homomorphism
\xi_{k}^{C}
is injective
[
KR
, Theorem 16]
(however it is not surjective). They also obtained parallel results for the classical groups of type
B
.

The aim of this paper is to complete the above results for the even orthogonal groups
\text{SO}(2n)
, and also the exceptional groups of type
G_{2},F_{4},E_{6}
and
E_{7}
. In principle, we can also handle
E_{8}
, but the results in this case are too long and complicated to reproduce here.

For

and

(n-k)
-dimensional isotropic subspaces in

\text{SO}(2n)/P^{D}_{n-k}

(n-k)
-th node of the Dynkin diagram of type

. Let

Thus, following (

Restricting

to the component

We determine this ring homomorphism explicitly in Theorem

Define the stable cohomology ring

\mathbb{H}^{*}(\text{OG}_{k},\mathbb{C}):=\varprojlim_{n}H^{*}(\text{OG}(n-k,2n),\mathbb{C})

as the inverse limit under the embedding

3.8
and Remark
3.9

The above ring homomorphism
\xi^{D}_{k}:\text{Rep}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\omega_{1}-\text{poly}}(\text{SO}(2k))\to\mathbb{H}^{*}(\text{OG}_{k},\mathbb{C})

However, it is not surjective.

and

Recall the

Let

3.
Injectivity Result for the even orthogonal Groups

In this section, we take
n\geq 4
to avoid some trivial cases.

Let
V=\mathbb{C}^{2n}
be equipped with the non-degenerate quadratic form
\langle\,,\,\rangle
so that its matrix
(\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle)_{1\leq i,j\leq 2n}
in the standard basis

is given by the antidiagonal matrix

with all its antidiagonal entries equal to

A Minkowski-type inequality for capillary hypersurfaces in a half-space
Guofang Wang, Liangjun Weng, Chao Xia

Abstract
. In this article, we investigate a flow of inverse mean curvature type for capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space. We establish the global existence of solutions for this flow and demonstrate that it converges smoothly to a spherical cap as time tends to infinity. As a result, we derive a new Minkowski-type inequality for star-shaped and mean convex capillary hypersurfaces for the whole range of contact angle θ ∈ ( 0 , π ) \theta\in(0,\pi) .

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\,|\,x_{n+1}>0\}

(n\geq 2)
be the Euclidean upper half-space and
\Sigma
an embedded compact hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
with boundary
\partial\Sigma
lying in
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. Such a hypersurface is called a

capillary hypersurface
, if

\Sigma
intersects with
\partial{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}^{n+1}
at a constant contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
along
\partial\Sigma
. In this paper, we consider a family of embedded capillary hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}:=x(M,t)\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, where
M
is an
n
-dimensional compact orientable smooth manifold with boundary
\partial M
, satisfies the following inverse mean curvature type flow

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}(\partial_{t}x)^{\perp}&=&\left(\frac{n\left(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)}{H}-\langle x,\nu\rangle\right)\nu,&\hbox{ in }M\times[0,T),\\
\langle\nu,e\rangle&=&-\cos\theta&\hbox{ on }\partial M\times[0,T),\end{array}\right.

where
\nu
is the unit outward normal of

\Sigma_{t}
and
e:=(0,\cdots,0,-1)
.

The introduction of flow (
1
) is motivated by the idea of Guan-Li
[
9
,
10
]
(see also
[
2
,
4
,
11
]
) for establishing the isoperimetric-type inequality for quermassintegral and by the Minkowski formulas for capillary hypersurfaces in
[
25
]
. For more description of Guan-Li’s idea for free boundary or capillary hypersurfaces, we refer to
[
18
,
20
,
21
,
25
,
27
]
and references therein.

For a capillary hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, we denote
\widehat{\Sigma}
the bounded domain in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
enclosed by
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
and
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. The boundary of
\widehat{\Sigma}
consists of two parts: one is
\Sigma
and the other, which
will be denoted by
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
, lies on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
.
Both have a common boundary, namely
\partial\Sigma
. The capillary area functional (cf.
[
6
]
) is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=|\Sigma|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|.

For a hypersurface with boundary (not necessarily capillary) in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, the relative (or capillary) isoperimetric inequality says that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{|\widehat{\Sigma}|^{\frac{n}{n+1}}}\geq\frac{\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta,1}})}{|\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta,1}}|^{\frac{n}{n+1}}}=(n+1)\mathbf{b}_{\theta}^{\frac{1}{n+1}},

with equality holding if and only if
\widehat{\Sigma}
is a capillary spherical cap.
Here we use
\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}
to denote a capillary spherical cap lying entirely in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
by

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}:=\Big\{x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\big||x-r\cos\theta e|=r\Big\},\penalty\ r\penalty\ \in(0,\infty),

which is a portion of the sphere of radius
r
and centered at
r\cos\theta e
. For notation simplicity, we denote

(n+1)\mathbf{b}_{\theta}:=\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta,1}}).

One can check easily that
\mathbf{b}_{\theta}
is the volume of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta,1}}.

In
[
25
]
, we introduced a family of quermassintegrals for capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space, which can be regarded as a natural generalization of quermassintegrals for closed hypersurfaces. Among them the second quermassintegral is given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{2,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\frac{1}{n}\left(\int_{\Sigma}HdA-\cos\theta\sin\theta|\partial\Sigma|\right).

The main objective of this paper is to establish an optimal isoperimetric type inequality between
\mathcal{V}_{2,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
and the capillary area functional
\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
under star-shaped and mean convex assumption, which is a capillary counterpart of the classical Minkowski-type inequality for closed hypersurfaces. For the latter, we refer to Schneider’s seminal book on convex bodies
[
22
]
and Guan-Li
[
9
]
.

We return to flow (
1
). A key feature of flow (
1
) is that it preserves the capillary area functional
\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}})
while it monotonically decreases
\mathcal{V}_{2,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}})
(cf.
[
25
, Proposition 3.1]
). It is easy to check that a family of stationary solutions of flow (
1
) is given by capillary spherical caps
\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}
.
Our main result in this article is the following long-time existence and convergence for flow (
1
) under the assumption of star-shapedness and mean convexity. This can be viewed as a capillary counterpart of the results of Gerhardt
[
7
]
and Urbas
[
24
]
for closed hypersurfaces.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume the initial hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
is a star-shaped and strictly mean convex capillary hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}

(n\geq 2)
with a contact angle
\theta\in(0,{{\pi}})
. Then flow (
1
) starting from
\Sigma_{0}
exists for all time with uniform
C^{\infty}
-estimates. Moreover, it converges smoothly to a uniquely determined capillary spherical cap
\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}
, as
t\to+\infty
.

As an application, we obtain the following Minkowski-type inequality for star-shaped and mean convex capillary hypersurfaces in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
for the whole angle range
{\theta\in(0,\pi)}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}

(n\geq 2)
be a star-shaped and mean convex capillary hypersurface with a contact angle
{\theta\in(0,\pi)}
, then

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}HdA\geq n(n+1)^{\frac{1}{n}}\mathbf{b}_{\theta}^{\frac{1}{n}}(|\Sigma|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+\sin\theta\cos\theta|\partial\Sigma|.

Equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a capillary spherical cap.

Note that (
1.6
) is equivalent to

(1.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{V}_{2,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}\geq\frac{\mathcal{V}_{2,\theta}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta,1}})}{\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta,1}})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}=n(n+1)^{\frac{1}{n}}\mathbf{b}_{\theta}^{\frac{1}{n}}.

From (
1.7
) one can see easily that the functional
\frac{\mathcal{V}_{2,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}
achieves its minimum at spherical caps.

When
\theta=\pi/\penalty 2
, Theorem
1.2
follows from the result of Guan-Li
[
9
, Theorem 2]
for closed hypersurfaces by using a
reflection argument. It is clear that if
\theta\not=\pi/\penalty 2
the reflection argument does not work.

Under the stronger geometric condition that
\Sigma
is convex, Theorem
1.2
has been proved for contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi/\penalty 2]
in
[
12
]
, where the authors proved actually more general geometric inequalities, the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities, which were initiated and conjectured in
[
25
]
. We emphasize that the results in
[
25
]
and
[
12
]
crucially depend on the restriction
\theta\leq\pi/2
, which was used to obtain
C^{2}
estimates.
We observed in
[
19
]
that for convex capillary hypersurfaces, all the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities with the whole range of the contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
follow actually from the classical Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for non-smooth convex bodies and provided a proof in a smooth setting with a Robin-type boundary condition. The proof follows closely the original idea in the theory of convex geometry with necessary modifications on the boundary.

It remains to be asked if the convexity condition could be weakened for these Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for capillary hypersurfaces. This paper is the first one in this direction.
We remark that without the star-shaped assumption, the Minkowski inequality for closed mean convex hypersurfaces remains an open problem, despite numerous efforts made to solve it, see
[
1
,
8
,
13
]
for instance.
It is also natural to expect that (
1.6
) holds for general mean convex capillary hypersurfaces. Especially a capacity method used in
[
1
]
would be expected to be useful by a necessary modification. We also mention several works on the Minkowski-type inequality in warped product spaces
[
3
,
26
,
20
]
, as well as anisotropic Minkowski-type inequality
[
28
]
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, it is crucial to show that flow (
1
) preserves the star-shapedness and strictly mean convex. For the star-shapedness, we prove a uniform lower bound for a new test function

\bar{u}:=\frac{\langle x,\nu\rangle}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle},

which is a variant of the support function
\langle x,\nu\rangle
. In fact, it can be viewed as the anisotropic support function (see e.g.
[
28
]
) with respect to
{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta,1}
.
Another key point is the uniform lower and upper bounds for
H
. For this, instead of
H
we consider

P=\bar{u}H.

To get the curvature estimate, we employ a similar method in
[
17
]
and
[
28
]
with a few modifications to take care of the Robin boundary condition, which explore the specific divergence structure of mean curvature, to directly obtain Schauder’s estimate for the radial function for star-shaped hypersurfaces. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
. For the proof of Theorem
1.2
, it follows from the monotonicity property
\mathcal{V}_{2,\theta}
along flow (
1
) and the convergence result in Theorem
1.1
.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, some relevant evolution equations are collected and we prove the preservation of the star-shapedness and obtain uniform a priori estimates for flow (
1
). Section
3
is dedicated to demonstrating the long-time existence and global convergence of flow (
1
), specifically, we prove Theorem
1.1
. As a result, we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Mean convexity and star-shapedness

2.1.
Evolution equations

Let
\Sigma_{t}
be a family of smooth, embedded hypersurfaces with capillary boundary in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, given by the embeddings
x(\cdot,t):M\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, which evolve by the general flow.

(2.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}x=f\nu+\mathcal{T},

with
\mathcal{T}\in T\Sigma_{t}
and a general velocity function
f
. For a fixed

, we choose
\mathcal{T}|_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}=f\cot\theta\mu
with
\mu
being the unit outward conormal of
\partial\Sigma_{t}
in
\Sigma_{t}
, which implies that the restriction of
x(\cdot,t)
on
\partial M
is contained in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We refer to
[
25
, Section 2]
for further discussions. Along flow (
2.1
), we have the following evolution equations for the induced metric
g_{ij}
, the volume element
d\mu_{t}
, the unit outward normal
\nu
, the second fundamental form
(h_{ij})
, the Weingarten curvature tensor
(h^{i}_{j})
and the mean curvature
H
of the hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}:=x(M,t)\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
. (cf.
[
27
, Proposition 2.11]
for a proof)

Proposition 2.1

(
[
27
]
)
.

Along flow (
2.1
), there holds

(1)

\partial_{t}g_{ij}=2fh_{ij}+\nabla_{i}\mathcal{T}_{j}+\nabla_{j}\mathcal{T}_{i}
.

(2)

\partial_{t}d\mu_{t}=(fH+\dive(\mathcal{T}))d\mu_{t}
.

(3)

\partial_{t}\nu=-\nabla f+h(e_{i},\mathcal{T})e_{i}
.

(4)

\partial_{t}h_{ij}=-\nabla^{2}_{ij}f+fh_{ik}h_{j}^{k}+\nabla_{\mathcal{T}}h_{ij}+h_{j}^{k}\nabla_{i}\mathcal{T}_{k}+h_{i}^{k}\nabla_{j}\mathcal{T}_{k}.

(5)

\partial_{t}h^{i}_{j}=-\nabla^{i}\nabla_{j}f-fh_{j}^{k}h^{i}_{k}+\nabla_{\mathcal{T}}h^{i}_{j}.

(6)

\partial_{t}H=-\Delta f-|h|^{2}f+\langle\nabla H,\mathcal{T}\rangle
.

In above,

and

2.2.
Star-shapedness and Mean convexity

We turn to the study of the flow.
In this subsection, we show that the star-shapedness and the mean convexity are preserved along flow (
1
). In order to prove this, the key is a choice of a suitable test function.

In the following we study flow (
1
), i.e, namely we consider

For convenience, we introduce the parabolic operator with respect to flow (

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}:=\partial_{t}-\frac{n(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{H^{2}}\Delta-\left\langle\mathcal{T}+x-\frac{n\cos\theta}{H}e,\nabla\right\rangle.

Let
\Sigma_{0}
be an initial capillary hypersurface which is star-shaped and mean convex.
The star-shapedness of

implies that there exist

where

enclosed by
\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}
and

Following the argument in
[

is a stationary solution of flow (

).

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
t\in[0,T)
, along flow (
1
), there holds

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r_{2}}}\setminus\ \widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r_{1}}},

where
\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}
defined by (
1.4
) and

Next, we show that the star-shapedness is preserved along flow (
1
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Sigma_{0}
be a star-shaped and strictly mean convex hypersurface with capillary boundary in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
and
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, then there exists
c_{0}>0
depending only on
\Sigma_{0}
, such that the solution
\Sigma_{t}
of flow (
1
) satisfies

(2.3)

\displaystyle\langle x,\nu\rangle(p,t)\geq c_{0},

for all
(p,t)\in M\times[0,T)
.

Proof.

A direct computation gives

\displaystyle\langle x,\nu\rangle_{;ij}=h_{ij}+h_{ij;k}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle-(h^{2})_{ij}\langle x,\nu\rangle.

Using Proposition
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle x,\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f\nu+\mathcal{T},\nu\rangle+\langle x,-\nabla f+h(e_{i},\mathcal{T})e_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{H}-\langle x,\nu\rangle+h(x^{T},\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle-\langle x,e_{k}\rangle\left(\frac{n\cos\theta\langle h_{ki}e_{i},e\rangle}{H}-(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)\frac{nH_{;k}}{H^{2}}-\langle x,h_{ki}e_{i}\rangle\right),

which yields

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\langle x,\nu\rangle=\Big[\big(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\big)\frac{n|h|^{2}}{H^{2}}-1\Big]\langle x,\nu\rangle.

It is easy to see

\displaystyle\langle\nu,e\rangle_{;kl}=h_{kl;s}\langle e_{s},e\rangle-(h^{2})_{kl}\langle\nu,e\rangle.

Combining it with

\displaystyle\langle e,\nabla f\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\langle e,e_{i}\rangle\left(\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{H}\right)_{;i}-\langle e,e_{i}\rangle\langle x,\nu\rangle_{;i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n\cos\theta}{H}h(e^{T},e^{T})-nH^{-2}(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)\langle\nabla H,e\rangle-h(x^{T},e^{T}),

we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle\nu,e\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla f,e\rangle+h(e_{i},\mathcal{T})\langle e_{i},e\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n\cos\theta}{H}h(e^{T},e^{T})+nH^{-2}(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)\langle\nabla H,e\rangle+h(x^{T},e^{T})+h(\mathcal{T},e^{T}).

It follows

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\langle\nu,e\rangle=(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)nH^{-2}|h|^{2}\langle\nu,e\rangle.

Now we introduce the function mentioned in the introduction

(2.6)

\displaystyle\bar{u}:=\frac{\langle x,\nu\rangle}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}.

One can check that it satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\bar{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}\mathcal{L}u-\frac{\langle x,\nu\rangle}{(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)^{2}}\mathcal{L}(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)

\displaystyle+\frac{2n(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{H^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{\langle x,\nu\rangle_{;i}(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)_{;i}}{(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)^{2}}

\displaystyle-\langle x,\nu\rangle\frac{(\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)_{;i}(\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)_{;i}}{(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)^{3}}\Bigg].

Combining with equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we get

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\bar{u}=\frac{\langle x,\nu\rangle}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}\left(\frac{n|h|^{2}}{H^{2}}-1\right)+\frac{2n}{H^{2}}{\bar{u}}_{;i}(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)_{;i}.

On
\partial M
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\langle x,\nu\rangle=\langle x,h(\mu,\mu)\mu\rangle=\cos\theta h(\mu,\mu)\langle x,\overline{\nu}\rangle=\cot\theta h(\mu,\mu)\langle x,\nu\rangle

and

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\big(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\big)=-\sin\theta h(\mu,\mu)\langle\nu,e\rangle.

Altogether yields

(2.8)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\bar{u}=0,\ \text{ on }\ \partial M.

Since

(2.9)

\displaystyle n|h|^{2}\geq H^{2},

(

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\bar{u}\geq 0,\quad\text{ mod }\nabla\bar{u},

which, together with

by the maximum principle.
Since

, then

\displaystyle\langle x,\nu\rangle=(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)\cdot\bar{u}\geq c_{0}>0,

for some positive constant

, which depends only on
\Sigma_{0}
. ∎

If

3.
Long-time existence and convergence

The short-time existence of flow (
1
) is well-known. Let
T^{*}
be the maximal time of the existence of a smooth solution to (
1
)
in the class of star-shaped and strictly mean convex hypersurfaces. In order to establish the long-time existence of flow (
1
), we need to obtain the uniform height estimate, the gradient estimate, and the second order derivative estimate (or curvature estimates) for the solution of flow (
1
). Then the long-time existence and the uniform
C^{\infty}
-estimates follow from the standard nonlinear parabolic PDE theory with a strictly oblique boundary condition (cf.
[
5
,
14
,
23
]
).

Assume that a capillary hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
is star-shaped with respect to the origin.
One can reparametrize it as a graph over
\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
. Namely,
there exists a positive function
\rho
defined on
\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
such that

\displaystyle\Sigma=\left\{\rho(X)X|X\in\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}\right\},

where
X:=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
is a local coordinate of
\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
.

We denote
\nabla^{0}
the Levi-Civita connection on
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
with respect to the standard round metric
\sigma:=g_{{}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}}
,
\partial_{i}:=\partial_{X_{i}}
,
\sigma_{ij}:=\sigma(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})
,
\rho_{i}:=\nabla^{0}_{i}\rho
, and
\rho_{ij}:=\nabla^{0}_{i}\nabla^{0}_{j}\rho
. The induced metric
g
on
\Sigma
is given by

\displaystyle g_{ij}=\rho^{2}\sigma_{ij}+\rho_{i}\rho_{j}=e^{2\varphi}\left(\sigma_{ij}+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}\right),

where
\varphi(X):=\log\rho(X)
. Its inverse
g^{-1}
is given by

\displaystyle g^{ij}=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\left(\sigma^{ij}-\frac{\rho^{i}\rho^{j}}{\rho^{2}+|\nabla^{0}\rho|^{2}}\right)=e^{-2\varphi}\left(\sigma^{ij}-\frac{\varphi^{i}\varphi^{j}}{v^{2}}\right),

where
\rho^{i}:=\sigma^{ij}\rho_{j}
,
\varphi^{i}:=\sigma^{ij}\varphi_{j}

The unit outward normal vector field on

We also have for the mean curvature,

(3.1)

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{e^{\varphi}v}\left[n-\left(\sigma^{ij}-\frac{\varphi^{i}\varphi^{j}}{v^{2}}\right)\varphi_{ij}\right].

Next, we derive the capillary boundary condition in (

\varphi
. We use the polar coordinate in

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=r\cos\beta,\quad|x^{\prime}|=r\sin\beta.

Then

\displaystyle e_{n+1}=\partial_{x_{n+1}}=\cos\beta\partial_{r}-\frac{\sin\beta}{r}\partial_{\beta}.

In these coordinates the standard Euclidean metric is given by

It follows that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\langle\nu,e_{n+1}\rangle=\frac{1}{v}\left(\cos\beta+\sin\beta\nabla_{\partial_{\beta}}^{0}\varphi\right).

Along
\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
, i.e.,
\beta=\frac{\pi}{2}

\displaystyle e=-e_{n+1}=\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\beta},

then

\displaystyle-\cos\theta=\langle\nu,e\rangle=\left\langle\frac{1}{v}\left(\partial_{r}-r^{-1}\nabla^{0}\varphi\right),\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\beta}\right\rangle=-\frac{\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi}{v},

that is,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi=\cos\theta\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{0}\varphi|^{2}}.

3.1.
Gradient estimates

First of all, we give the estimate of

Proposition 3.1
.

If

\displaystyle\min_{\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}}\frac{nv_{0}}{e^{\varphi_{0}}H_{0}}\left[1-\frac{\cos\theta}{v}\left(\cos\beta+\sin\beta\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi_{0}\right)\right]\leq\dot{\varphi}+1

where

and

Proof.

Assume

admits the maximum value at some point

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}(p_{0},t_{0})=\max_{\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}\times[0,T]}\dot{\varphi}.

We claim that
t_{0}=0
. If not, we have
t_{0}>0
. A
direct computation gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\dot{\varphi}}{\partial t}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}G^{ij}\dot{\varphi}_{ij}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}G^{k}\dot{\varphi}_{k}\quad\text{in}\quad\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}\times[0,T].

The maximum principle yields that
p_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
. Choosing an orthonormal frame around
p_{0}
such that

\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}
are the tangential vector fields
on
\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
and
e_{n}:=\partial_{\beta}
the unit normal along
\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}\subset\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
. Then we have

(\dot{\varphi})_{\alpha}(p_{0},t_{0})=0
. Combining it with the boundary equation in (
3
) yields that

\displaystyle(\dot{\varphi})_{n}(p_{0},t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\big(\cos\theta\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{0}\varphi|^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\varphi_{i}(\varphi_{i})_{t}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{0}\varphi|^{2}}}\cos\theta=\frac{\varphi_{n}{(\dot{\varphi})}_{n}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{0}\varphi|^{2}}}\cos\theta=\cos^{2}\theta{(\dot{\varphi})}_{n},

Since
|\cos\theta|<1
, we obtain
({\dot{\varphi}})_{n}(p_{0},t_{0})=0
, which is
a contradiction to the Hopf boundary lemma. Therefore
t_{0}=0
, as a result,

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}\leq\max_{\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}}\dot{\varphi_{0}}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}\geq\min_{\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}}\dot{\varphi_{0}}.

Both, together with

\displaystyle G=\frac{nv}{e^{\varphi}H}\left[1-\frac{\cos\theta}{v}\left(\cos\beta+\sin\beta\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi\right)\right]-1,

give the desired estimate. Hence we complete the proof.
∎

\displaystyle e^{\varphi}H=\frac{n}{1+\dot{\varphi}}\left[\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{0}\varphi|^{2}}-{\cos\theta}\left(\cos\beta+\sin\beta\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi\right)\right].

Hence the conclusion (
3.17
) follows from the Hölder estimates for
|\nabla^{0}\varphi|,\dot{\varphi}
and
\varphi
in Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
3.4
, while the Hölder estimate for
\varphi
follows trivially from Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
.
∎

With the preparation above, we obtain the full second-order and high-order derivative estimates for
\varphi
.

Proposition 3.6
.

If
\varphi
solves (
3
), then for every
t_{0}\in(0,T)
,
there exists some
\gamma>0
such that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{C^{2+\gamma,1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}\times[0,T])}\leq C(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{C^{2+\alpha}(\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}})},n,\gamma),

and

(3.19)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{C^{2l+\gamma,l+\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}\times[t_{0},T])}\leq C(\|\varphi(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{C^{2l+\alpha}(\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}})},n,\gamma),

for any
2\leq l\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

To get the Schauder estimates (
3.18
), we rewrite the evolution equation for
\varphi
with respect to the induced metric
g
, which is uniformly controlled (due to the uniform estimate for
\varphi
and
\nabla^{0}\varphi
, see Proposition
3.2
). Noticing that
v:=\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{0}\varphi|^{2}}
and

\displaystyle e^{\varphi}vH=n-\left(\sigma^{ij}-\frac{\varphi^{i}\varphi^{j}}{v^{2}}\right)\varphi_{ij}=n-e^{2\varphi}\Delta_{g}\varphi,

we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{nv}{e^{\varphi}H}\left[1-\frac{\cos\theta}{v}\left(\cos\beta+\sin\beta\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi\right)\right]-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{n}{e^{2\varphi}H^{2}}(n-e^{2\varphi}\Delta_{g}\varphi)\left[1-\frac{\cos\theta}{v}\left(\cos\beta+\sin\beta\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{2nv}{e^{\varphi}H}\left[1-\frac{\cos\theta}{v}\left(\cos\beta+\sin\beta\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi\right)\right]-1.

It follows

(3.20)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{H^{2}}\left[1-\frac{\cos\theta}{v}\left(\cos\beta+\sin\beta\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi\right)\right]\Delta_{g}\varphi

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2nv}{e^{\varphi}H}-\frac{n^{2}}{e^{2\varphi}H^{2}}\right)\left[1-\frac{\cos\theta}{v}\left(\cos\beta+\sin\beta\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi\right)\right]-1,

which is a linear, uniformly parabolic equation with Hölder coefficients, due to Proposition
3.5
and Proposition
3.3
. Moreover, the Neumann boundary value condition in (
3
) satisfies the strictly oblique property (due to
|\cos\theta|<1
) and its coefficient has uniform Hölder bounds (due to again Proposition
3.3
). Hence (
3.18
) follows from the linear parabolic PDE theory with a strictly oblique boundary value problem (cf.
[
16
, Chapter V, Theorem 4.9 and Theorem 4.23]
, see also
[
14
, Chapter IV, Theorem 5.3]
).

Next, we show (
3.19
). By differentiating both sides of (
3.20
) with respect to
t
and

\partial_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq n
, respectively, one can easily
get evolution equations of
\dot{\varphi}
and
\nabla^{0}_{j}\varphi
respectively, which,
using the estimate (
3.18
), can be treated as uniformly
parabolic PDEs on the time interval
[t_{0},T]
. At the initial time

t_{0}
, all compatibility conditions are satisfied and the initial
function
\varphi(\cdot,t_{0})
is smooth, which implies the

C^{3+\gamma,(3+\gamma)/2}
-estimate for
\nabla^{0}_{j}\varphi
and the

C^{2+\gamma,(2+\gamma)/2}
-estimate for
\dot{\varphi}
. So, we have the

C^{4+\gamma,(4+\gamma)/2}
-estimate for
\varphi
. From
[
16
, Chapter 4,
Theorem 4.3, Exercise 4.5]
and the above argument, it is not
difficult to know that the constants are independent of the time
T
. Higher
regularity of (
3.19
) can be proven by induction over
2\leq l\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence we complete the proof.
∎

With the estimates given above, the long-time existence of inverse mean curvature type flow (
3
) or (
1
) follows directly. That is.

Corollary 3.7
.

If
\Sigma_{0}
is star-shaped and strictly mean convex capillary hypersurface with a contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, then the solution of flow (
1
) exists for all time with a uniform
C^{\infty}
-estimates.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
by using the above uniform a priori estimates.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Following the parabolic theory with a strictly oblique boundary value condition (cf.
[
5
,
16
,
23
]
), we know that the solution of equation (
1
) exists for all time with a uniform
C^{\infty}
-estimate. And the convergence result of Theorem
1.1
can be proved in a similar way as
[
25
, Proposition 4.12]
, by adopting the monotonicity property of quermassintegrals along flow (
1
).
∎

Finally, by applying the convergence result of flow (
1
) and the monotonicity of
\mathcal{V}_{2,\theta}
, i.e., Theorem
1.1
and
[
25
, Proposition 3.1]
, one can complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
for the strictly mean convex case. When
\Sigma
is only mean convex, we use the approximation argument, as described in
[
9
]
to prove Theorem
1.2
. The equality characterization in Theorem
1.2

,

]

Packets of Serre weights for generic locally reducible two-dimensional Galois representations
Misja F.A. Steinmetz

Abstract
. Suppose K / 𝐐 p K/\mathbf{Q}_{p} is finite and r ¯ : G K → GL 2 ​ ( 𝐅 ¯ p ) \overline{r}\colon G_{K}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}) is a reducible Galois representation. In this paper we prove that we can use the results from [ Ste22 ] to obtain a decomposition of the set of Serre weights W ⁡ ( r ¯ ) W(\overline{r}) into a disjoint union of at most ( e + 1 ) f (e+1)^{f} ‘packets’ of weights (where f f is the residue degree and e e the ramification degree of K K ) under the assumption that r ¯ \overline{r} is weakly generic. Thereby, we improve on results of [ DS15 ] which give a similar decomposition, by rather different methods, under the assumption that r ¯ \overline{r} is strongly generic. We show that our definition of weak genericity is optimal for the results of this paper to hold when e = 1 e=1 . However, we expect that for e = 2 e=2 one of the main results of this paper still holds under weaker hypotheses than the ones in this paper.



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime. In
[
Ser87
]
Serre conjectured that an irreducible, odd, mod
p
Galois representation
\overline{\rho}\colon G_{\mathbf{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
must arise as the reduction of the Galois representation attached to a modular form. Moreover, Serre gives a precise recipe for the weight and level of the modular form in terms of
\overline{\rho}
. Generalisations of Serre’s conjecture to the case of Hilbert modular forms over a totally real number field
F
and representations
\overline{\rho}\colon G_{F}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
have been the subject of much research in the last decade. Initally,
[
BDJ10
]
gave a generalisation for such
F
under the assumption that
p
is unramified in
F
. This assumption was later removed by
[
Sch08
]
,
[
Gee11
]
and
[
BLGG13
]
. Similarly to the original conjecture, these conjectures attach a set of weights
W(\overline{\rho})
to the representation for which
\overline{\rho}
is conjectured to be modular. The so-called weight part of the conjecture was resolved in
[
GLS15
]
building on work of
[
BLGG13
]
,
[
GK14
]
and
[
New13
]
. This says that if the representation is modular, then it must be modular for a weight in
W(\overline{\rho})
. A general aspect of these conjectures is that
\sigma\in W(\overline{\rho})
is defined as
\sigma=\otimes_{v\mid p}\sigma_{v}
with
\sigma_{v}\in W(\overline{\rho}|_{G_{F_{v}}})
, for all places
v\mid p
of
F
, where
F_{v}
is the completion of
F
at
v
. Therefore, we may, and will, work completely locally.

Suppose then that
K/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
is a finite extension of residue degree
f
and ramification degree
e
. Given
\overline{r}\colon G_{K}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, we would like to study the set of Serre weights
W(\overline{r})
associated to
\overline{r}
. Since
W(\overline{r})
is explicitly defined when
\overline{r}
is irreducible (see Defn.
2.3
), we will assume
\overline{r}
is reducible

\overline{r}\sim\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{1}&c_{\overline{r}}\\
0&\chi_{2}\end{pmatrix},

for characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\colon G_{K}\to\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}^{\times}
. The class
c_{\overline{r}}
is an element of
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}[G_{K}]}(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})=H^{1}(G_{K},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}(\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1})
.

In fact, if
c_{\overline{r}}=0
, then
W(\overline{r})
is also explicitly defined (see Defn.
2.3
). If this is not the case, then
W(\overline{r})
is defined as follows. Given a Serre weight
\sigma\in W(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})
(see Defn.
2.3
), we usually define a subspace
L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\subseteq H^{1}(G_{K},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}(\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1})
in terms of reductions of crystalline representations of specified Hodge–Tate weights (see Defn.
3.1
). Then we define
\sigma\in W(\overline{r})
if and only if
c_{\overline{r}}\in L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
. Unfortunately, the definition of
L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
in terms of
p
-adic Hodge theory is neither explicit nor computable. The approach of
[
DDR16
]
,
[
CEGM17
]
and
[
Ste22
]
to make this explicit is to use local class field theory and the Artin–Hasse exponential to define explicit basis elements
c_{i,j}
of
H^{1}(G_{K},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}(\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1})
for
0\leq i<f
and
0\leq j<e
; an alternative approach to making the subspace
L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
explicit using Kummer theory can be found in
[
BS22
]
. In
[
Ste22
]
an indexing set
J_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is defined (see Defn.
3.7
) and it is proved that
L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=\mathrm{Span}(\{c_{i,j}\mid(i,j)\in J_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\})
. Unfortunately, the definition of
J_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is combinatorial and not as straightforward as one would have hoped. The first main result of this paper is that under the assumption that
\overline{r}
is weakly generic (see Hypo.
4.1
), the definition of
J_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
can be greatly simplified.

Theorem A
.

Suppose
\overline{r}
is weakly generic and
\sigma\in W(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})
. Then there exists an
\ell\in\mathbf{Z}^{f}
(depending on
\overline{r}
and
\sigma
) with
\ell_{i}\in[0,e]
, for all
i
, such that

L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=\mathrm{Span}(\{c_{i,j}\mid j<\ell_{i}\}).

This is Thm.
4.4
below. The proof of this theorem positively resolves Conjecture 7.4.8 from
[
Ste20
]
. Then we go on to use this result to prove a decomposition of
W(\overline{r})
. For each
w\in\mathbf{Z}^{f}
with
w_{i}\in[0,e]
for all
i
, we define a subset
P_{w}\subseteq W(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})
that is typically of size
2^{f-\delta_{w}}
for
\delta_{w}=|\{i\mid w_{i}=0\text{ or }w_{i}=e\}|
. Order the set of all such
w
by the natural product ordering, i.e. define
\leq
by

(w_{0},w_{1},\dotsc,w_{f-1})\leq(w_{0}^{\prime},w_{1}^{\prime},\dotsc,w_{f-1}^{\prime})\text{ if and only if }w_{i}\leq w_{i}^{\prime}\text{ for all $i$.}

Theorem B
.

Suppose
\overline{r}
is weakly generic. Then we have

W(\overline{r})=\coprod_{w\leq w^{\mathrm{max}}}P_{w}

for some
w^{\mathrm{max}}
depending on
\overline{r}
.

In other words, this theorem says that the weights come in packets and these packets respect the natural ordering on the indices
w
. This is Thm.
4.10
below.

In §
6
we show that our definition of weak genericity is optimal for the results of this paper to hold when
e=1
. We also give a short explanation of why we expect that Thm. A will still hold under weaker assumptions when
e=2
.

The paper
[
DS15
]
inspired us to write this paper and we have certainly borrowed from their clear style and exposition. However, we use completely different methods to obtain similar results and, indeed, the techniques used here were not yet available when
[
DS15
]
was written. Moreover, our genericity hypothesis is weaker than that used in
[
DS15
]
. To distinguish the two cases we have referred to their hypothesis as strongly generic and to our hypothesis as weakly generic (see Hypo.
4.1
). In particular, Thm.
4.10
and Prop.
4.7
recover the main local result of
[
DS15
]
when
\overline{r}
is strongly generic. In §
6
we have included a short discussion of cases in which the results of this paper apply, but that cannot be proved using the methods of
[
DS15
]
. This shows that our different approach has resulted in more than could have been achieved by the methods of
[
DS15
]
, even if the authors had opted to use more difficult combinatorics. Our weakening of the generic hypothesis does come at the expense of no longer having a easy expression for the size of
P_{w}
(cf. Prop.
4.7
and Prop.
4.8
). A more involved expression for
|P_{w}|
may exists also in the weakly generic case – this essentially boils down to a good understanding of when
(J,x)
is not maximal for
\sigma
– but we have chosen not to pursue this direction for brevity.

We believe that it should be possible to use the techniques
[
BS22
]
to recover the results of this paper, but we have not attempted to do this.

1.2.
Notation

We will write
K/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
for a finite extension with residue degree
f
and ramification degree
e
. Denote its residue field by
k
. We fix a choice of uniformiser
\pi\in K
. We also fix an algebraic closure
\overline{K}
and a
(p^{f}-1)
-th root
\pi^{1/(p^{f}-1)}\in\overline{K}
of
\pi
. Let
\omega\colon G_{K}\to k^{\times}
denote the character defined by letting
\omega(g)
equal the image of
\frac{g(\pi^{1/(p^{f}-1)})}{\pi^{1/(p^{f}-1)}}
in
k^{\times}
. The restriction of
\omega
to the inertia subgroup
I_{K}
of
G_{K}
is independent of the choice of
\pi^{1/(p^{f}-1)}
. For any
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, we obtain a character
\omega_{\tau}\colon G_{K}\to\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}^{\times}
as
\omega_{\tau}:=\tau\circ\omega
. Unless otherwise stated we write
\varphi\colon k\to k
for the
p
-th power map
\varphi(x)=x^{p}
(and similarly for other finite fields).

A Serre weight
\sigma
is an isomorphism class of irreducible
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
-representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(k)
. Recall that these are represented by

\sigma_{a,b}=\bigotimes_{\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}\left(\mathrm{det}^{b_{\tau}}\otimes_{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}}k^{2}\right)\otimes_{k,\tau}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}

for uniquely determined integers
a_{\tau},b_{\tau}
satisfying
b_{\tau},a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}\in[0,p-1]
and not all
b_{\tau}
equal to
p-1
. When convenient we may relax this restriction to
b_{\tau}\in\mathbf{Z}
, while still requiring
a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}\in[0,p-1]
, for all
\tau
and use the identification
\sigma_{a,b}\cong\sigma_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}
if and only if
a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}=a_{\tau}^{\prime}-b_{\tau}^{\prime}
for all
\tau
and
\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}b_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}p^{i}\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}b_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}^{\prime}p^{i}\bmod{(p^{f}-1)}
for any
\tau
.

We write
\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}
for the
p
-adic cyclotomic character. Suppose
V
is a Hodge–Tate representation of
G_{K}
on a
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
-vector space. For each
\kappa\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}(K,\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})
, the
\kappa
-Hodge Tate weights
\mathrm{HT}_{\kappa}(V)
is the multiset of integers containing
i
with multiplicity

\dim_{\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}\left(V\otimes_{\kappa,K}\widehat{\overline{K}}(-i)\right)^{G_{K}},

where
\widehat{\overline{K}}(i)
denotes the completed algebraic closure of
K
with the twisted
G_{K}
-action
g(a)=\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)^{i}g(a)
. Thus,
\mathrm{HT}_{\kappa}(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}})=\{1\}
for every
\kappa
.

For any tuple
a=(a_{\tau})_{\tau}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
, we define

\Omega_{\tau,a}:=\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}p^{i}a_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}.

2.
Explicit Serre weights in the semisimple case

Let us begin by defining the set of Serre weights in terms of crystalline lifts.

Definition 2.1
.

For a Serre weight
\sigma=\sigma_{a,b}
, we will say a tuple of integers
\tilde{\sigma}=(\tilde{a}_{\kappa},\tilde{b}_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}(K,\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})}
is a lift of
\sigma
if, for each
\tau\colon k\to\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
, there is an indexing

\left\{\kappa\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}(K,\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})\mid\kappa|_{k}=\tau\right\}=\{\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\dotsc,\tau_{e-1}\}

such that

(\tilde{a}_{\kappa},\tilde{b}_{\kappa})=\begin{cases}(a_{\tau}+1,b_{\tau})&\text{ if }\kappa=\tau_{0};\\
(1,0)&\text{ if }\kappa=\tau_{i}\text{ for }i>0.\end{cases}

We say a crystalline representation of
G_{K}
on a finite free
\overline{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}
-module
V
has Hodge type
\sigma
if there exists a lift
\tilde{\sigma}=(\tilde{a}_{\kappa},\tilde{b}_{\kappa})
of
\sigma
such that

\mathrm{HT}_{\kappa}(V)=(\tilde{a}_{\kappa},\tilde{b}_{\kappa})

for every
\kappa\colon K\to\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose
\overline{r}\colon G_{K}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
is a continuous representation. We write
W^{\mathrm{cr}}(\overline{r})
for the set of Serre weights
\sigma_{a,b}
for which there exists a crystalline representation of
G_{K}
on a finite free
\overline{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}
-module
V
with Hodge type
\sigma_{a,b}
such that
V\otimes_{\overline{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}\cong\overline{r}
.

2.1.
The semisimple case

Unfortunately,
W^{\mathrm{cr}}(\overline{r})
is neither explicit nor computable. When
\overline{r}
is semisimple, there is a simple explicit equivalent definition. Let us recall this definition here.

For any character
\chi\colon G_{K}\to\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}^{\times}
, there exist integers
(n_{\tau})_{\tau}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
such that

\chi|_{I_{K}}=\prod_{\tau}\omega_{\tau}^{n_{\tau}}

and these are unique if we require
n_{\tau}\in[1,p]
for all
\tau
and
n_{\tau}<p
for at least one
\tau
. Since
\omega_{\tau}^{p}=\omega_{\tau\circ\varphi}
, we can write
\chi|_{I_{K}}=\omega_{\tau}^{\Omega_{\tau,n}}
with
\Omega_{\tau,n}
defined as in §
1.2
.

Let
K_{2}
denote the unique unramified extension of
K
of degree 2 with residue field
k_{2}
and let
\pi\colon\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k_{2},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
denote the natural projection induced by restriction to
k
. For
\lambda\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k_{2},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, let
\omega_{\lambda}
denote the fundamental character of
I_{K_{2}}=I_{K}
corresponding to
\lambda
. Following
[
GLS15
]
, we give the definition
W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r})
for semisimple
\overline{r}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose
\overline{r}\colon G_{K}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
is continuous and semisimple. We define the set of Serre weights
W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r})
as follows.

(1)

If
\overline{r}
is irreducible, then
\sigma_{a,b}\in W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r})
if there is a subset
J\subset\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k_{2},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
and integers
x_{\tau}\in[0,e-1]
, for all
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, such that

\overline{r}|_{I_{K}}\cong\begin{pmatrix}\prod_{\lambda\in J}\omega_{\lambda}^{a_{\pi(\lambda)}+1+x_{\pi(\lambda)}}\prod_{\lambda\notin J}\omega_{\lambda}^{b_{\pi(\lambda)}+e-1-x_{\pi(\lambda)}}&0\\
0&\prod_{\lambda\notin J}\omega_{\lambda}^{a_{\pi(\lambda)}+1+x_{\pi(\lambda)}}\prod_{\lambda\in J}\omega_{\lambda}^{b_{\pi(\lambda)}+e-1-x_{\pi(\lambda)}}\end{pmatrix}

and the restriction
\pi\colon J\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
is a bijection.

(2)

If
\overline{r}
is the direct sum of two characters, then
\sigma_{a,b}\in W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r})
if there exists a subset
J\subset\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
and integers
x_{\tau}\in[0,e-1]
, for all
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, such that

\overline{r}|_{I_{K}}\cong\begin{pmatrix}\prod_{\tau\in J}\omega_{\tau}^{a_{\tau}+1+x_{\tau}}\prod_{\tau\notin J}\omega_{\tau}^{b_{\tau}+x_{\tau}}&0\\
0&\prod_{\tau\notin J}\omega_{\tau}^{a_{\tau}+e-x_{\tau}}\prod_{\tau\in J}\omega_{\tau}^{b_{\tau}+e-1-x_{\tau}}\end{pmatrix}.

Theorem 2.4

(Gee–Liu–Savitt, Wang)
.

If
\overline{r}
is semisimple, then
W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r})=W^{\mathrm{cr}}(\overline{r})
.

Proof.

For
p>2
this is
[
GLS15
, Thm. 4.1.6]
and for
p=2
this is
[
Wan17
, Thm. 5.4]
.
∎

3.
Explicit Serre weights in the non-semisimple case

Having treated the semisimple case in the previous section, we will now assume
\overline{r}
is reducible. Then
\overline{r}
is of the form

\overline{r}\sim\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{1}&*\\
0&\chi_{2}\end{pmatrix}

for characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\colon G_{K}\to\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}^{\times}
. The results of
[
GLS15
]
show that we have an inclusion
W^{\mathrm{cr}}(\overline{r})\subset W^{\mathrm{cr}}(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})
. However, this is rarely an equality. To make
W^{\mathrm{cr}}(\overline{r})
explicit we need to specify a condition on the extension class of
\overline{r}
which determines whether
\sigma\in W^{\mathrm{cr}}(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})
lies in
W^{\mathrm{cr}}(\overline{r})
or not. Note that, for any representation
\overline{\rho}\colon G_{K}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
of the form

\overline{\rho}\sim\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{1}&c_{\overline{\rho}}\\
0&\chi_{2}\end{pmatrix},

the extension class
c_{\overline{\rho}}
is an element of
H^{1}(G_{K},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}(\chi))
, where
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose
\sigma=\sigma_{a,b}
is a Serre weight. Write
L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
for all the extension classes in
H^{1}(G_{K},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}(\chi))
arising as the reduction of a crystalline representation
r
of
G_{K}
on a finite free
\overline{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}
-module
V
of Hodge type
\sigma
which are of the form

r\sim\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\chi}_{1}&*\\
0&\tilde{\chi}_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
\tilde{\chi}_{1}
and
\tilde{\chi}_{2}
are crystalline lifts of
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
, respectively.

Theorem 3.2

(Gee–Liu–Savitt, Wang)
.

Suppose that
\overline{r}\sim\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{1}&c_{\overline{r}}\\
0&\chi_{2}\end{pmatrix}
. We have that
\sigma\in W^{\mathrm{cr}}(\overline{r})
if and only if

(1)

\sigma\in W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})
and

(2)

c_{\overline{r}}\in L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
.

Moreover, whenever
\sigma\in W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})
, we have that
L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is a subspace.

Proof.

This is the main local result of
[
GLS15
]
for
p>2
and follows from
[
Wan17
]
for
p=2
.
∎

The approach of
[
DDR16
]
,
[
CEGM17
]
and
[
Ste22
]
is to make the set of extensions
L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
explicit using local class field theory and the Artin–Hasse exponential. Let us briefly recall this approach.

3.1.
Explicit basis elements

We will start by constructing an explicit basis of
H^{1}(G_{K},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}(\chi))
. Recall that we defined the characters
\omega_{\tau}
, for
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, in §
1.2
. Writing
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}
, we have

\chi=\psi\prod_{\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}\omega_{\tau}^{n_{\tau}}

for an unramified character
\psi
and integers
n_{\tau}\in[1,p]
for all
\tau
. We require that
n_{\tau}<p
for at least one
\tau
so that the
n_{\tau}
are uniquely determined. Write
\Omega_{\tau,n}=\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}p^{i}n_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}
. Since
\omega_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}=\omega_{\tau}^{p}
, it follows that
\chi|_{I_{K}}=\omega_{\tau}^{\Omega_{\tau,n}}|_{I_{K}}
for all
\tau
. Write
f^{\prime}
for the smallest integer
i>0
such that
n_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}=n_{\tau}
for all
\tau
. Note that
f^{\prime}\mid f
and let
f^{\prime\prime}=f/f^{\prime}
.

For any
j\geq 0
, write
W_{j}^{\prime}
for the set of integers
m\in\mathbf{Z}
satisfying
\frac{jp(p^{f}-1)}{p-1}<m<\frac{(j+1)p(p^{f}-1)}{p-1}
,
p\nmid m
and there exists a
\tau
such that
m\equiv\Omega_{\tau,n}\bmod{(p^{f}-1)}
. Then
W_{j}^{\prime}
has cardinality
f^{\prime}
(see
[
Ste22
, Rem. 3.4]
). Let
W^{\prime}:=\cup_{j=0}^{e-1}W_{j}^{\prime}
and
W:=W^{\prime}\times\{0,\dotsc,f^{\prime\prime}-1\}.
Then
W
has cardinality
ef
. For any
m\in W^{\prime}
, we have an embedding
\tau_{m}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
such that
m\equiv\Omega_{\tau_{m},n}\bmod{(p^{f}-1)}
. To any
\alpha=(m,k)\in W
, we may attach an embedding
\tau_{\alpha}:=\tau_{m}\circ\varphi^{-kf^{\prime}}
. Notice that
m\equiv\Omega_{\tau_{\alpha},n}\bmod{(p^{f}-1)}
.

Write
\varpi:=\pi^{1/(p^{f}-1)}
with
\pi^{1/(p^{f}-1)}
as in §
1.2
. We define
M:=L(\varpi)
, where
L/K
is a fixed unramified extension of degree prime-to-
p
such that
\psi|_{G_{L}}
is trivial. Note that
\chi|_{G_{M}}
is also trivial. Write
l
for the residue field of
M
and
\mathcal{O}_{M}
for its ring of integers. Moreover, let
\lambda_{\tau,\psi}
denote a basis element of the 1-dimensional space
(l\otimes_{k,\tau}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})^{\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)=\psi}
. Lastly, let
E(X):=\exp(\sum_{n\geq 0}X^{p^{n}}/p^{n})\in\mathbf{Z}_{p}[[X]]
. For any
\alpha\in M
with positive valuation, we define a map

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\alpha}\colon l\otimes_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{O}_{M}^{\times}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}

\displaystyle a\otimes b

\displaystyle\mapsto E([a]\alpha)\otimes b,

where
[a]
is a Teichmüller lift of
a\in l
. For any
\alpha=(m,k)\in W
, we define

u_{\alpha}:=\varepsilon_{\varpi^{m}}(\lambda_{\tau_{\alpha},\psi})\in\mathcal{O}_{M}^{\times}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}.

Furthermore, if
\chi
is trivial (respectively, cyclotomic) we refer to

Ste22

for the definitions of

that the isomorphisms of local class field theory give

the
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
-dual

of

and similarly for

(respectively,

(respectively,

Proposition 3.3
.

The set
\{c_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in W\}
together with

(respectively,

Proof.

.∎

3.2.
Explicit sets of extensions

Let us now show how to use our explicit basis elements to get to an explicit definition of
L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
. These are results from
[

]
,
[

]
and

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\sigma=\sigma_{a,b}
be a Serre weight. Then we define
\mathcal{S}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\sigma)
to equal the set of pairs
(J,x)
with
J\subset\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
and
x\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}

\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}}|_{I_{K}}\cong\begin{pmatrix}\prod_{\tau\in J}\omega_{\tau}^{a_{\tau}+1+x_{\tau}}\prod_{\tau\notin J}\omega_{\tau}^{b_{\tau}+x_{\tau}}&0\\
0&\prod_{\tau\notin J}\omega_{\tau}^{a_{\tau}+e-x_{\tau}}\prod_{\tau\in J}\omega_{\tau}^{b_{\tau}+e-1-x_{\tau}}\end{pmatrix}.

Definition 3.5
.

Suppose
(J,x)\in\mathcal{S}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\sigma)
. We define
s(J,x)\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}

s(J,x)=\begin{cases}a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}+1+x_{\tau}&\text{if $\tau\in J$;}\\
x_{\tau}&\text{if $\tau\notin J$.}\end{cases}

We impose an ordering
\preceq
on
\mathcal{S}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\sigma)
by stipulating that, for all
(J,x),(J^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},r)

(J,x)\preceq(J^{\prime},x^{\prime})\;\text{if and only if}\;\Omega_{\tau,s(J^{\prime},x^{\prime})-s(J,x)}\in(p^{f}-1)\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}\text{ for all $\tau$.}

Proposition 3.6
.

If
\mathcal{S}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\sigma)
is non-empty, then it contains a unique maximal element
(J^{\mathrm{max}},x^{\mathrm{max}})
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
GLS15
, Lem. 5.3.3]
– see also
[
BS22
, Prop. 6.5]
and its proof.

Definition 3.7
.

Suppose
\sigma
is a Serre weight and
\mathcal{S}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\sigma)
is non-empty with maximal element
(J,x)=(J^{\mathrm{max}},x^{\mathrm{max}})
. Let
s=s(J,x)
as in Defn.
3.5
and
t_{\tau}=a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}+e-s_{\tau}
for all
\tau
. For each

\mathcal{I}_{\tau}:=\begin{cases}[0,s_{\tau}-1]&\text{ if }\tau\notin J;\\
\{t_{\tau}\}\cup[r_{\tau},s_{\tau}-1]&\text{ if }\tau\in J.\end{cases}

Moreover, for each
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})

\xi_{\tau}:=(p^{f}-1)s_{\tau}+\Omega_{\tau,s-t}.

We let
J_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{AH}}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
denote the subset of all
\alpha=(m,k)\in W
such that there exist
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
,

and

such that

(1)

p^{j}m=\xi_{\tau}-d(p^{f}-1)
and

(2)

\tau_{\alpha}=\tau\circ\varphi^{j}
.

Remark 3.8
.

We note that
s_{\tau}=s_{\tau\circ\varphi^{if^{\prime}}}
for any
\tau
and
0\leq i<f^{\prime\prime}
; hence, similarly for
t
and
\xi

(m,k)\in J_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{AH}}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
implies
(m,i)\in J_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{AH}}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
for all

.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\sigma=\sigma_{a,b}
be a Serre weight. We define
L_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{AH}}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
to be the span of

\{c_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in J_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{AH}}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\}

together with
c_{\mathrm{un}}
(respectively,
c_{\mathrm{tr}}
) if
\chi
is trivial (respectively, if
\chi
is cyclotomic,
\chi_{2}
is unramified and

for all
\tau
).

This explicit definition of the set of extensions allows us to give an explicit definition of the set of Serre weights associated to
\overline{r}
in the non-semisimple case. The following definition is first found in
[

]
for

Definition 3.10
.

Suppose that

4.
The main result

In this section will state the main local result of this paper. Recall that
\overline{r}\colon G_{K}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
is a continuous reducible representation of the form

\overline{r}\sim\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{1}&*\\
0&\chi_{2}\end{pmatrix}

for
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\colon G_{K}\to\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}^{\times}
continuous characters. Denote the quotient by
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}
and write
\chi=\psi\prod_{\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}\omega_{\tau}^{n_{\tau}}
for
\psi
an unramified character and
n_{\tau}\in[1,p]
for all
\tau
with at least one
n_{\tau}<p
. Note that this uniquely defines
n\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
.

Hypothesis 4.1

(Generic Hypothesis)
.

We will say that
\overline{r}
(or
\chi
) is weakly generic if
n_{\tau}\in[e,p-e]
for all
\tau
. We will say that
\overline{r}
(or
\chi
) is strongly generic if
n_{\tau}\in[e,p-1-e]
for all
\tau
.

Note that weak genericity implies that
e<p/2
if
p
is odd and
e=1
if
p=2
, whereas strong genericity implies that
e\leq(p-1)/2
(hence,
p>2
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
\chi
is weakly generic. For
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
and
0\leq j<e
, write

m_{\tau,j}:=\Omega_{n,\tau}+j(p^{f}-1).

Then
W_{j}^{\prime}=\left\{m_{\tau,j}\mid\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\right\}
.

Proof.

This is
[
Ste20
, Lem. 7.4.2]
.
∎

Definition 4.3
.

Suppose
\sigma=\sigma_{a,b}
is a Serre weight and
\mathcal{S}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\sigma)
is non-empty with maximal element
(J,x)=(J^{\mathrm{max}},x^{\mathrm{max}})
. We define the dimension vector
\ell=(\ell_{\tau})_{\tau}\in[0,e]^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
as follows: for
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, we define

\ell_{\tau}:=\begin{cases}x_{\tau}&\text{if $\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\notin J$;}\\
x_{\tau}+1&\text{if $\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\in J$.}\end{cases}

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose
\sigma
is a Serre weight. Let
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
,
0\leq j<e
and
0\leq k<f^{\prime\prime}
. If
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}
is weakly generic, then

(m_{\tau,j},k)\in J_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{AH}}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\;\text{if and only if}\;j<\ell_{\tau}.

We will prove this theorem in §
5
. Now we will show how to use Theorem
4.4
to obtain a decomposition of the set of Serre weights.

Definition 4.5
.

We let
\mathcal{W}
denote the set of tuples
(w_{\tau})_{\tau}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
such that
0\leq w_{\tau}\leq e
for all
\tau
. We impose
\mathcal{W}
with the usual product partial ordering, i.e. we define
\leq
on
\mathcal{W}
by

(w_{\tau})_{\tau}\leq(w_{\tau}^{\prime})_{\tau}\text{ if and only if }w_{\tau}\leq w_{\tau}^{\prime}\text{ for all $\tau$.}

Definition 4.6
.

For
w=(w_{\tau})_{\tau}\in\mathcal{W}
, write

L_{w}:=\mathrm{Span}(\{c_{\alpha}\mid\alpha=(m_{\tau,j},k)\text{ with }0\leq j<e-w_{\tau}\})

unless
\chi
is trivial, in which case we additionally include
c_{\mathrm{un}}
in this span. We define a subset of Serre weights
P_{w}
via

\sigma\in P_{w}:\iff\sigma\in W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})\text{ and }L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=L_{w}.

Proposition 4.7
.

Suppose
\overline{r}
is strongly generic and
w=(w_{\tau})_{\tau}\in\mathcal{W}
. Then we have that
|P_{w}|=2^{f-\delta_{w}}
, where

\delta_{w}=|\{\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\mid w_{\tau}=0\text{ or }w_{\tau}=e\}|,

unless we are in one of the following exceptional cases. If
\chi
is cyclotomic and
w_{\tau}=0
for all
\tau
or
\chi^{-1}
is cyclotomic and
w_{\tau}=e
for all
\tau
, then
|P_{w}|=2
.

This proposition is analogous to
[
DS15
, Prop. 3.8(2)]
, although they use a different definition of the ‘packets’ of weights
P_{w}
. We will give a proof using some of the combinatorics developed in §
5
, hence we will defer the proof until then.

Proposition 4.8
.

Suppose
\overline{r}
is weakly generic, but not strongly generic. Let
w=(w_{\tau})_{\tau}\in\mathcal{W}
. Then we have that
0\leq|P_{w}|\leq 2^{f-\delta_{w}}
, where

\delta_{w}=|\{\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\mid w_{\tau}=0\text{ or }w_{\tau}=e\}|.

Since the proof of this proposition is similar to the proof of Prop.
4.7
, we will also postpone its proof until §
5
.

Definition 4.9
.

We will say
\overline{r}
is très ramifiée if
c_{\overline{r}}\notin L_{0}
.

See
[
Ste22
, Chap. 4]
, in particular Prop. 4.1.2, for the relationship of this definition to other definitions of très ramifiée. We remark that
c_{\overline{r}}\in L_{0}
always holds unless
\chi
is cyclotomic,
\chi_{2}
is unramified and
a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}=p-1
for all
\tau
. In the latter case,
L_{0}\subseteq H^{1}(G_{K},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}(\chi))
forms a codimension 1 subspace and
\overline{r}
is très ramifiée precisely when it is not contained in this subspace.

Theorem 4.10
.

Suppose
\overline{r}
is weakly generic. We have

W(\overline{r})=\coprod_{w\leq w^{\mathrm{max}}}P_{w}

for some
w^{\mathrm{max}}\in\mathcal{W}
depending on
\overline{r}
, unless
\overline{r}
is très ramifiée in which case
W(\overline{r})=\{\sigma_{a,b}\}
where
\chi_{2}|_{I_{K}}=\prod_{\tau}\omega_{\tau}^{b_{\tau}}
and
a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}=p-1
for all
\tau
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\overline{r}
is not très ramifiée. It follows from the inclusion
W(\overline{r})\subset W(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})
, Thm.
3.11
and Thm.
4.4
that
W(\overline{r})\subseteq\coprod_{w\in\mathcal{W}}P_{w}
. Conversely, let
c_{\overline{r}}
denote the extension class associated to
\overline{r}
and write
\mathcal{W}_{\overline{r}}
for all
w\in\mathcal{W}
such that
c_{\overline{r}}\in L_{w}
. (In other words, all
w\in\mathcal{W}
such that
P_{w}\subseteq W(\overline{r})
.) Since
\overline{r}
is not très ramifiée,
c_{\overline{r}}
lies in
L_{0}
so
\mathcal{W}_{\overline{r}}
is non-empty. Take
w^{\mathrm{max}}:=\max_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{\overline{r}}}\{w\}
. Then

L_{w^{\mathrm{max}}}=\bigcap_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{\overline{r}}}L_{w}.

Hence,
c_{\overline{r}}\in L_{w^{\mathrm{max}}}
. Furthermore,
w\leq w^{\mathrm{max}}
implies
c_{\overline{r}}\in L_{w}
. It follows that

W(\overline{r})=\coprod_{w\leq w^{\mathrm{max}}}P_{w}.

Now suppose that
\overline{r}
is très ramifiée. Then
c_{\overline{r}}
does not lie in the codimension one subspace
L_{0}
of
H^{1}(G_{K},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}(\chi))
. It follows from
[
BS22
, Cor. 6.2]
that
\sigma_{a,b}\in W(\overline{r})
with
\sigma_{a,b}
as in the statement of the theorem. On the other hand, if
\sigma=\sigma_{a,b}
is a Serre weight with
r_{\tau}=a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}<p-1
for some
\tau
, then it follows from
[
Ste20
, Thm. 4.1.1]
that
c_{\overline{r}}\notin L_{\sigma}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
. Therefore,
\sigma\notin W(\overline{r})
.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
4.4

In this section we will prove Theorem
4.4
and Propositions
4.7
and
4.8
. Most of the proof of Thm.
4.4
will consist of delicate combinatorial arguments. We will start with a Lemma giving an explicit solution to a congruence.

5.1.
Explicitly solving a congruence

Suppose
J\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
and
c\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
such that
c_{\tau}\in[1,p-1]
for all
\tau
. Recall the definition

(-1)^{\tau\notin J}:=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }\tau\notin J\text{;}\\
1&\text{if }\tau\in J.\end{cases}

In this subsection we would like to study solutions
r\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
to the congruence

\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}(-1)^{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}\notin J}r_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}p^{i}\equiv\Omega_{\tau,c}\pmod{p^{f}-1}

satisfying
r_{\tau}\in[1,p]
for all
\tau
. Such a solution always exists, but need not be unique for a given
(J,c)
. Let us give an explicit definition of a solution
r
to this congruence.

Remark 5.1
.

In
[
DS15
]
the authors restrict themselves to strongly generic representations for which the above congruence needs to be solved only when
c_{\tau}\in[1,p-2]
for all
\tau
. This is a far easier combinatorial problem and may have been one of the reasons why the authors adopted this more restrictive definition of genericity.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\delta_{J}\colon\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}\times\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\to\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
be defined as follows. Fix
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
and let us write
(y_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}=\delta_{J}((x_{\sigma})_{\sigma},\tau)
. If
1\leq x_{\tau}\leq p
, then we set
y_{\sigma}:=x_{\sigma}
for all
\sigma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
. Otherwise, we have two special cases.

(1)

If
x_{\tau}\leq 0
, then we set
y_{\tau}:=x_{\tau}+p
and we define

y_{\tau\circ\varphi}:=\begin{cases}x_{\tau\circ\varphi}-1&\text{if }\tau\circ\varphi\notin J;\\
x_{\tau\circ\varphi}+1&\text{if }\tau\circ\varphi\in J.\end{cases}

We define
y_{\sigma}:=x_{\sigma}
for all remaining embeddings
\sigma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\setminus\{\tau,\tau\circ\varphi\}
.

(2)

If
x_{\tau}>p
, then we set
y_{\tau}:=x_{\tau}-p
and we define

y_{\tau\circ\varphi}:=\begin{cases}x_{\tau\circ\varphi}-1&\text{if }\tau\circ\varphi\notin J;\\
x_{\tau\circ\varphi}+1&\text{if }\tau\circ\varphi\in J.\end{cases}

We define
y_{\sigma}:=x_{\sigma}
for all remaining embeddings
\sigma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\setminus\{\tau,\tau\circ\varphi\}
.

Definition 5.3
.

Suppose
J\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
and
c\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
such that
c_{\tau}\in[1,p-1]
for all
\tau
. We define
r(J,c)\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
recursively by the following process. Define
y_{0}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
as

y_{0,\tau}:=\begin{cases}c_{\tau}&\text{ if }\tau\in J,\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\in J;\\
c_{\tau}+1&\text{ if }\tau\in J,\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\notin J;\\
p-c_{\tau}&\text{ if }\tau\notin J,\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\in J;\\
p-1-c_{\tau}&\text{ if }\tau\notin J,\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\notin J.\end{cases}

Note that
y_{0,\tau}\geq 0
for all
\tau
. If
y_{0,\tau}>0
for all
\tau
, then we set
r(J,c)=y_{0}
. Otherwise, we fix
\tau_{0}
such that
y_{0,\tau_{0}}=0
. For
\kappa=1,\dotsc,f
, we then define
y_{\kappa}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
recursively as
y_{\kappa}=\delta_{J}(y_{\kappa-1},\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa-1})
. We set
r(J,c)=y_{f}
.

Remark 5.4
.

For notational purposes, when
y_{0,\tau}>0
for all
\tau
we may assume when necessary that
y_{\kappa}=y_{0}
for all
\kappa=1,\dotsc,f
.

Remark 5.5
.

We will prove in Prop.
5.7
that
r(J,c)
is independent of the choice of
\tau_{0}
in its definition in the context in which we will need it.

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose
J\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
and
c\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
such that
c_{\tau}\in[1,p-1]
for all
\tau
. Then
r=r(J,c)\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
(defined using any appropriate choice of
\tau_{0}
if necessary) solves the congruence

\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}(-1)^{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}\notin J}r_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}p^{i}\equiv\Omega_{\tau,c}\pmod{p^{f}-1}

and satisfies
r_{\tau}\in[1,p]
for all
\tau
.

Proof.

We adopt the same notation as in Defn.
5.3
. It follows immediately from the definitions that
y_{0}
solves the congruence and
y_{0,\tau}\in[0,p]
for all
\tau
. Thus, the lemma is proved if
y_{0,\tau}>0
for all
\tau
. Therefore, we may assume we have fixed a choice
\tau_{0}
such that
y_{0,\tau_{0}}=0
. Then
c_{\tau_{0}}=p-1
and
\tau_{0},\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{-1}\notin J
.

For any
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, define
v^{\tau}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
via
v^{\tau}_{\tau}=p
and

v^{\tau}_{\tau\circ\varphi}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if $\tau,\tau\circ\varphi\in J$ or $\tau,\tau\circ\varphi\notin J$;}\\
1&\text{if $\tau\in J$, $\tau\circ\varphi\notin J$ or $\tau\notin J$, $\tau\circ\varphi\in J$,}\end{cases}

and let
v^{\tau}_{\tau^{\prime}}=0
for all
\tau^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
. We see easily that adding
\pm v^{\tau}
, for any
\tau
, to
y_{0}
does not change the congruence. For
\kappa=0,\dotsc,f-1
, we claim that
y_{\kappa+1}=\delta_{J}(y_{\kappa},\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa})
leaves
y_{\kappa}
invariant, adds
v^{\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}
to
y_{\kappa}
or subtracts
v^{\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}
from
y_{\kappa}
implying the congruence remains unchanged. We note that
y_{\kappa,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}\leq 0
implies that
y_{0,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}=0
or
y_{\kappa,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}=y_{0,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}-1
. Either case implies
\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}\notin J
. Applying Case (
1
) of Defn.
5.2
, we see that if
y_{\kappa,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}\leq 0
, then
\delta_{J}(y_{\kappa},\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa})
changes
y_{\kappa}
by adding
v^{\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}
. On the other hand,
y_{\kappa,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}>p
implies that
y_{\kappa,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}=y_{0,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}+1
which only occurs when
\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}\in J
. Hence, applying Case (
2
) of Defn.
5.2
, we see that if
y_{\kappa,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}>p
, then
\delta_{J}(y_{\kappa},\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa})
changes
y_{\kappa}
by subtracting
v^{\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{\kappa}}
.

Now we will prove that
r_{\tau}\in[1,p]
for all
\tau
. For
\kappa=1,\dotsc,f
, we claim inductively that
y_{\kappa,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{i}}\in[1,p]
for all
0\leq i<\kappa
. Since
y_{1,\tau_{0}}=p
, the claim is true for
\kappa=1
. Suppose it is true for
\kappa=n
. To prove the claim for
\kappa=n+1
, we note that
y_{n+1,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{i}}=y_{n,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{i}}
for all
0\leq i<n
, except that, possibly, when
n=f-1
, we have that
y_{f,\tau_{0}}\neq y_{f-1,\tau_{0}}
. In the latter case, since
\tau_{0}\notin J
and
y_{f-1,\tau_{0}}=y_{1,\tau_{0}}=p
, we have that
y_{f,\tau_{0}}=p-1\in[1,p]
, so we may disregard this case. Otherwise, to complete the proof we must show that
y_{n+1,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{n}}\in[1,p]
. Since
y_{0,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{n}}\in[0,p]
, it is clear that
y_{n,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{n}}\in[-1,p+1]
. If
y_{n,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{n}}\in\{-1,0\}
, then
y_{n+1,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{n}}\in\{p-1,p\}
. If
y_{n,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{n}}=p+1
, then
y_{n+1,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{n}}=1
. If
y_{n,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{n}}\in[1,p]
, then
y_{n+1,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{n}}=y_{n,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{n}}
. In all cases,
y_{n+1,\tau_{0}\circ\varphi^{n}}\in[1,p]
which completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Unique Serre weight in the weakly generic case

Suppose
\overline{r}\sim\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{1}&*\\

\chi_{1},\chi_{2}:G_{K}\to\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}^{\times}

for

with

, for all
\tau
, and

for at least one
\tau

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose
\overline{r}
is weakly generic and
\sigma_{a,b}\in W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})
. Let
(J,x)=(J_{\mathrm{max}},x_{\mathrm{max}})
be the unique maximal element of
\mathcal{S}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\sigma_{a,b})
. Define
c_{\tau}:=n_{\tau}+e-1-2x_{\tau}\in[1,p-1]
for all
\tau
. Write
r=r(J,c)\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
as in Defn.
5.3
.

Then
a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}+1=r_{\tau}
for all
\tau
except when
J=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
,

and

for all

for all
\tau

Furthermore,

is independent of the choice of
\tau_{0}
in its definition.

Proof.

It follows from the assumption
(J,x)\in\mathcal{S}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\sigma_{a,b})
and
\chi|_{I_{K}}=\prod_{\tau}\omega_{\tau}^{n_{\tau}}
that

\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}(-1)^{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}\notin J}(a_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}-b_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}+1)p^{i}\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}(n_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}+e-1-2x_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}})p^{i}\pmod{p^{f}-1}.

Therefore, if
c_{\tau}=n_{\tau}+e-1-2x_{\tau}
and
r_{\tau}=a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}+1
for all
\tau
, then
c_{\tau}\in[1,p-1]
and
r_{\tau}\in[1,p]
for all
\tau
. Furthermore,
r
solves the congruence

(1)

\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}(-1)^{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}\notin J}r_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}p^{i}\equiv\Omega_{\tau,c}\pmod{p^{f}-1}.

This congruence may not always have a unique solution

satisfying
r_{\tau}\in[1,p]
for all
\tau
. When it does have a unique solution it follows from Lem.
5.6
that
r=r(J,c)
.

Therefore, suppose we have
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})}
satisfying the congruence with
r_{\tau},r_{\tau}^{\prime}\in[1,p]
for all
\tau
. Then

\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}(-1)^{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}\notin J}(r_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}-r_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}^{\prime})p^{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{f}-1}.

Since
r_{\tau}-r_{\tau}^{\prime}\in[-(p-1),(p-1)]
for all
\tau
, this implies that either
r=r^{\prime}
or, possibly after interchanging

and
r^{\prime}
, that

(2)

r_{\tau}=\begin{cases}p&\text{if }\tau\in J;\\
1&\text{if }\tau\notin J,\end{cases}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274ptr_{\tau}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\tau\in J;\\
p&\text{if }\tau\notin J.\end{cases}

Suppose
J\neq\varnothing,\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
and

r_{\tau}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\tau\in J;\\
p&\text{if }\tau\notin J.\end{cases}.

We claim this contradicts the maximality of
(J,x)
. In this case we may fix
\kappa\in J
such that
\kappa\circ\varphi^{-1}\notin J
. Then define
J^{\prime}
by removing
\kappa
from
J
and adding
\kappa\circ\varphi^{-1}
. Let
x^{\prime}=x
. Now define
s_{\tau}=x_{\tau}
if
\tau\notin J
and
s_{\tau}=r_{\tau}+x_{\tau}
if
\tau\in J
and similarly for
s^{\prime}
using
(J^{\prime},x^{\prime})
so that

and
s^{\prime}=s(J^{\prime},x^{\prime})
. Then it follows that
s_{\kappa}^{\prime}-s_{\kappa}=-1
,
s_{\kappa\circ\varphi^{-1}}^{\prime}-s_{\kappa\circ\varphi^{-1}}=p
and
s_{\tau}^{\prime}=s_{\tau}
for all
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\setminus\{\kappa,\kappa\circ\varphi^{-1}\}
. It follows that
\Omega_{\kappa,s^{\prime}-s}=(p^{f}-1)
and
\Omega_{\tau,s^{\prime}-s}=0
for all
\tau\neq\kappa
. This contradicts the maximality of
(J,x)
according to Prop.
3.6
.

We are left with showing the following: if congruence (
1
) has multiple solutions, then either we are in one of the exceptional cases of the proposition or
r(J,c)
produces the first of the two solutions of (
2
), i.e. the solution corresponding to
(J_{\mathrm{max}},x_{\mathrm{max}})
. For any subset
J\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, let
\mathbf{1}_{J}\colon\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\to\{0,1\}
denote the characteristic function of
J
and let
J^{\mathrm{c}}
denote the complement of
J
in
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
. The above shows congruence (
1
) has a unique solution unless

\Omega_{\tau,c}\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}(\mathbf{1}_{J}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i})p-\mathbf{1}_{J^{\mathrm{c}}}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i}))p^{i}\pmod{p^{f}-1}.

Suppose this congruence is satisfied. Then

\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}(c_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}-\mathbf{1}_{J}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i-1})+\mathbf{1}_{J^{\mathrm{c}}}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i}))p^{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{f}-1}.

Since
c_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}-\mathbf{1}_{J}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i-1})+\mathbf{1}_{J^{\mathrm{c}}}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i})\in[0,p]
for all
i
, it follows that the sum

S:=\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}(c_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}-\mathbf{1}_{J}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i-1})+\mathbf{1}_{J^{\mathrm{c}}}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i}))p^{i}

is equal to
0
or
p^{f}-1
(resp.
0
,
2^{f}-1
or
2(2^{f}-1)
) if
p>2
(resp.
p=2
).

Firstly, suppose

. If
S=0
, then
c_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}-\mathbf{1}_{J}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i-1})+\mathbf{1}_{J^{\mathrm{c}}}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i})=0
for all
i
. It is clear that this implies

,

and
x_{\tau}=e-1
for all
\tau
. Now suppose
S=p^{f}-1
. If the top coefficient in
S
were
p
, then
S\geq p^{f}
. If the top coefficient of
S
were
\leq p-2
, then

. So we find that
c_{\tau\circ\varphi^{f-1}}-\mathbf{1}_{J}(\tau\circ\varphi^{f-2})+\mathbf{1}_{J^{\mathrm{c}}}(\tau\circ\varphi^{f-1})=p-1
. Continuing like this we find that
c_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}-\mathbf{1}_{J}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i-1})+\mathbf{1}_{J^{\mathrm{c}}}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i})=p-1
for all

. From this it follows that

for all
\tau
in the definition of

(see Defn.
5.3

and

. Hence,

gives the solution of (

in this case. On the other hand, if

and
x_{\tau}=0
, for all
\tau

. Note that in either of the two exceptional cases we have that

for all
\tau
, so that the other solution is given by

for all
\tau

Now suppose

, which implies

,
n_{\tau}=1
and

, for all
\tau

for all
\tau

for all
\tau
. Lastly, assume

. As before this implies
1-\mathbf{1}_{J}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i-1})+\mathbf{1}_{J^{\mathrm{c}}}(\tau\circ\varphi^{i})=1
for all

. Rewriting gives
\mathbf{1}_{J}(\tau)=\mathbf{1}_{J^{\mathrm{c}}}(\tau\circ\varphi)
, for all
\tau
, so that
J\neq\varnothing,\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})

for all
\tau

It is easily checked that in the exceptional cases

for all

in the definition of

so that this definition is not dependent on any choices, except when

Proposition 5.10
.

For all
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, we have that
t_{\tau}\in\mathcal{I}_{\tau}\text{ if and only if }t_{\tau}<r_{\tau}
.

Proof.

Fix
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
. If
t_{\tau}<r_{\tau}
, then it follows from the definition that
t_{\tau}\in\mathcal{I}_{\tau}
. Therefore, suppose that
t_{\tau}\geq r_{\tau}
. We will show that
t_{\tau}\geq s_{\tau}
proving that
t_{\tau}\notin\mathcal{I}_{\tau}
.

Note that
\tau\notin J
. We want to apply Prop.
5.7
. Let us deal with the exceptional cases first. Since we may exclude
J=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
, suppose
J=\varnothing
,
n_{\kappa}=p-1-e
,
x_{\kappa}=0
and
r_{\kappa}=p
for all
\kappa
. Then
t_{\tau}=p-1+e
and
s_{\tau}=0
, so it is clear that
t_{\tau}\geq s_{\tau}
.

Therefore, it follows from Prop.
5.7
that
r=r(J,x)
. Since
\tau\notin J
, we see that

y_{0,\tau}=\begin{cases}p-e-n_{\tau}+1+2x_{\tau}&\text{if $\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\in J$;}\\
p-e-n_{\tau}+2x_{\tau}&\text{if $\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\notin J$.}\end{cases}

Since
p-e-n_{\tau}\geq 0
, we find
y_{0,\tau}\geq 2x_{\tau}

y_{0,\tau}\leq p-1

r_{\tau}=y_{f,\tau}\geq y_{0,\tau}-1

r_{\tau}\geq 2x_{\tau}-1
.

We note that
t_{\tau}\geq s_{\tau}
if and only if
r_{\tau}+e-1-x_{\tau}\geq x_{\tau}
. Rewriting gives
r_{\tau}\geq 2x_{\tau}+1-e
. Therefore, the proposition follows immediately from

when

so that
t_{\tau}=r_{\tau}>0=s_{\tau}
.
∎

Proposition 5.11
.

For all
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
and all

, we have that

v_{p}(\xi_{\tau}-c(p^{f}-1))>0

if and only if either
c=t_{\tau}
or
t_{\tau}=0
,
s_{\tau}=p-1+e
and
r_{\tau}=p
for all
\tau
and

.

Fix
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
. Since
\xi_{\tau}\equiv-t_{\tau}\bmod p
, it follows immediately that
\xi_{\tau}-t_{\tau}(p^{f}-1)\equiv 0\bmod p
. It is also clear that
t_{\tau}=0
and
c=p
implies that
\xi_{\tau}-c(p^{f}-1)\equiv 0\bmod p
. This finishes the if-direction of the proposition.

For the other direction, let us treat the cases
t_{\tau}\geq r_{\tau}
and
t_{\tau}<r_{\tau}
separately. Suppose
t_{\tau}\geq r_{\tau}
. Then
t_{\tau}>0
and
t_{\tau}\notin\mathcal{I}_{\tau}
by Prop.
5.10
. Therefore, we need to show
v_{p}(\xi_{\tau}-c(p^{f}-1))=0
for all
c\in\mathcal{I}_{\tau}
. Note that
\mathcal{I}_{\tau}=[0,s_{\tau}-1]=[0,x_{\tau}-1]
, so we may assume without loss of generality that
x_{\tau}>0
. Together with
\tau\notin J
this excludes both exceptional cases from Prop.
5.7
so that
r=r(J,x)
. Since
\tau\notin J
, we find that
\xi_{\tau}-c(p^{f}-1)\equiv-(r_{\tau}-x_{\tau}+e-1-c)\bmod{p}
. In the proof of Prop.
5.10
we have proved already that
r_{\tau}\geq 2x_{\tau}+1-e
. Therefore,
r_{\tau}-x_{\tau}+e-1-c\geq x_{\tau}-c\geq 1
since

. To bound
r_{\tau}
from above, we use

such that

y_{0,\tau}=\begin{cases}p-e-n_{\tau}+1+2x_{\tau}&\text{if $\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\in J$;}\\
p-e-n_{\tau}+2x_{\tau}&\text{if $\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\notin J$.}\end{cases}

Since

. Therefore,

b_{\tau}=p-e+x_{\tau}\geq p-e>p-e-1\geq n_{\tau}-x_{\tau}\geq b_{\tau}^{\prime},

contradicting
b=b^{\prime}
. Hence,
J=J^{\prime}
. Then it follows immediately from
b=b^{\prime}
and
J=J^{\prime}
that
x=x^{\prime}
, proving the claim.

Thus, we have proved that there is a bijection

W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})\to\{(J,x)\mid J\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\text{ and integers }x_{\tau}\in[0,e-1]\text{ for $\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})$}\}

unless either (resp. both)
\chi^{\pm 1}
is cyclotomic in which case the map is
2-
to
-1
for a single weight (resp. precisely two weights). Therefore,

|W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})|=\begin{cases}e^{f}2^{f}&\text{ if $\chi^{\pm 1}$ are not cyclotomic;}\\
e^{f}2^{f}+1&\text{ if either $\chi^{\pm 1}$ is cyclotomic;}\\
e^{f}2^{f}+2&\text{ if both $\chi^{\pm 1}$ are cyclotomic.}\end{cases}

Now fix
w\in\mathcal{W}
. It follows from Thm.
4.4
that
\sigma(J,x)\in P_{w}
if and only if the dimension vector
\ell
(as in Defn.
4.3
) corresponding to
\sigma(J,x)
satisfies
\ell_{\tau}=e-w_{\tau}
for all
\tau
. Since
\ell
corresponding to
\sigma(J,x)
satisfies

\ell_{\tau}=\begin{cases}x_{\tau}&\text{ if $\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\notin J$;}\\
x_{\tau}+1&\text{ if $\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\in J$,}\end{cases}

the only imposed conditions on
J
are: if
w_{\tau}=e
, then
\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\notin J
, and if
w_{\tau}=0
, then
\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\in J
. It is clear that
x
is uniquely determined by
J
and
w
. Therefore, we get
2^{f-\delta_{w}}
non-exceptional weights in
P_{w}
.

Considering the exceptional weights, it is easily seen that if
\chi
is cyclotomic,
J=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
and
x_{\tau}=e-1
for all
\tau
, then
\sigma^{\prime}(J,x)\in P_{w}
for
w_{\tau}=0
for all
\tau
. On the other hand, if
\chi^{-1}
is cyclotomic,
J=\varnothing
and
x_{\tau}=0
for all
\tau
, then we see
\sigma^{\prime}(J,x)\in P_{w}
for
w_{\tau}=e
for all
\tau
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Prop.
4.8
.

Suppose that
\overline{r}
is weakly generic, but not strongly generic. In particular, note that this excludes the exceptional cases of Prop.
5.7
.

Suppose
\sigma=\sigma_{a,b}\in W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})
. It follows from Defn.
2.3
and Prop.
3.6
that there exists a unique maximal
(J,x)
such that

\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}}|_{I_{K}}\cong\begin{pmatrix}\prod_{\tau\in J}\omega_{\tau}^{a_{\tau}+1+x_{\tau}}\prod_{\tau\notin J}\omega_{\tau}^{b_{\tau}+x_{\tau}}&0\\
0&\prod_{\tau\notin J}\omega_{\tau}^{a_{\tau}+e-x_{\tau}}\prod_{\tau\in J}\omega_{\tau}^{b_{\tau}+e-1-x_{\tau}}\end{pmatrix}.

Moreover, it follows from Prop.
5.7
that
a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}+1=r_{\tau}
, for all
\tau
, where
r_{\tau}=r(J,x)
. We immediately see that
\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}b_{\tau\circ\varphi^{i}}p^{i}\bmod{(p^{f}-1)}
is uniquely defined given
(J,x)
and
r_{\tau}=a_{\tau}-b_{\tau}+1
for all
\tau
. If we write
\chi_{2}|_{I_{K}}=\prod_{\tau}\omega_{\tau}^{n_{2,\tau}}
, then
b
may be explicitly given by

b_{\tau}=\begin{cases}n_{2,\tau}+x_{\tau}-e+1&\text{if $\tau\in J$;}\\
n_{2,\tau}+x_{\tau}-e+1-r_{\tau}&\text{if $\tau\notin J$.}\end{cases}

Hence,
\sigma
is uniquely defined by
(J,x)
. Therefore, we have just proved that there is an injective map

\vartheta\colon W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})\hookrightarrow\{(J,x)\mid J\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})\text{ and integers }x_{\tau}\in[0,e-1]\text{ for $\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})$}\}

sending
\sigma
to the unique maximal
(J,x)
as above. Therefore,
|W^{\mathrm{exp}}(\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}})|\leq e^{f}2^{f}
. (The crucial difference with the strongly generic case being that this injective map may not be surjective.)

Now fix
w\in\mathcal{W}
and suppose
(J,x)\in\mathrm{Im}(\vartheta)
corresponds with a
\sigma\in P_{w}
. Exactly as in the proof of Prop.
4.7
, we find that if
w_{\tau}=e
, then
\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\notin J
, and if
w_{\tau}=0
, then
\tau\circ\varphi^{-1}\in J
. As before, we see that
x
is uniquely determined by
J
and
w
. Therefore, we get at most
2^{f-\delta_{w}}
weights in
P_{w}
.
∎

6.
A remark on genericity

Suppose
\overline{r}\colon G_{K}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
reducible as before with
\overline{r}^{\mathrm{ss}}\cong\chi_{1}\oplus\chi_{2}
. Write
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}|_{I_{K}}=\prod_{\tau}\omega_{\tau}^{n_{\tau}}
with
n_{\tau}\in[1,p]
for all
\tau
. Thm.
4.10
was proved in
[
DS15
]
under the assumption that
\overline{r}
was strongly generic (see Hypo.
4.1
). However, calculations in
[
Ste22
]
suggested to us that Thm.
4.4
should be true under the weaker condition that
\overline{r}
was only weakly generic (see Hypo.
4.1
) which led to this paper. Let us briefly discuss here what evidence there is for these various hypotheses being optimal.

It follows from
[
DS15
, §5]
that their techniques no longer apply in the case
f=2
,
e=1
and
(n_{\tau_{0}},n_{\tau_{1}})=(p-1,b)
with
b\in[1,p-2]
for some choice
\{\tau_{0},\tau_{1}\}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}(k,\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
. However, the authors there remark that indeed it follows from
[
CD11
]
that Thm.
4.10
should still hold in this case despite the techniques of
[
DS15
]
no longer applying. Since this example is weakly, but not strongly generic, the examples in
[
DS15
, §5]
therefore show that the methods of
[
DS15
]
would not have sufficed in the weakly generic case (even if the authors’ of
[
DS15
]
had used more complicated combinatorics).

When
e=1
it follows from
[
DDR16
, §7.1]
(and its proof in
[
CEGM17
]
) that Thm.
4.4
is true if and only if
n_{\tau}<p-1
for all
\tau
, i.e. precisely if and only if
\overline{r}
is weakly generic. This shows that Hypo.
4.1
is optimal for Thm.
4.4
to hold when
e=1
. On the other hand, the calculations in
[
DDR16
, §8.1–8.4]
show that Thm.
4.10
should still hold when
e=1
,
f=2
and
\overline{r}
is not weakly generic. Although it follows from these calculations that one of the packets of weights
P_{w}
with
\sum_{\tau}w_{\tau}=1
will be empty and the other one will have cardinality 2. This contradicts Prop.
4.8
and shows a crucial distinction between the weakly and non-generic cases for
f=2
and
e=1
.

In
[
Ste20
, §7.5]
some conjectural formulae for
J_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{AH}}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
are presented for
e=2
and arbitrary
f
– see, for example, Conj. 7.5.12 in
[
Ste20
]
. These formulae suggest that when
e=2
the correct condition on
\overline{r}
for Thm.
4.4
to hold should be
n_{\tau}\in[2,p-1]
for all
\tau
; whereas weak genericity requires
n_{\tau}\in[2,p-2]
for all
\tau
when
e=2
. Therefore, these calculations suggest that there are situations in which a version of Thm.
4.4
may be true under weaker assumptions than the ones made in this paper.

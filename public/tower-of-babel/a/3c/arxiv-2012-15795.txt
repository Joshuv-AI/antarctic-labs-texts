Abstract
. The Lalanne–Kreweras involution is an involution on the set of Dyck paths which combinatorially exhibits the symmetry of the number of valleys and major index statistics. We define piecewise-linear and birational extensions of the Lalanne–Kreweras involution. Actually, we show that the Lalanne–Kreweras involution is a special case of a more general operator, called rowvacuation, which acts on the antichains of any graded poset. Rowvacuation, like the closely related and more studied rowmotion operator, is a composition of toggles. We obtain the piecewise-linear and birational lifts of the Lalanne–Kreweras involution by using the piecewise-linear and birational toggles of Einstein and Propp. We show that the symmetry properties of the Lalanne–Kreweras involution extend to these piecewise-linear and birational lifts.

The starting point of our work is a certain well-known involution on the set of Dyck paths. A
Dyck path of semilength

\mathbb{Z}^{2}

\operatorname{Cat}(n)\coloneqq\frac{1}{n+1}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{2n}{n}
.

A
valley
in a Dyck path is a down step which is immediately followed by an up step. Although not obvious, it is true that the number of Dyck paths in
\operatorname{Dyck}_{n}
with
k
valleys is the same as the number with
(n-1)-k
valleys, for all
k
. The
Lalanne–Kreweras involution
is an involution on
\operatorname{Dyck}_{n}
which combinatorially exhibits this symmetry: it sends a Dyck path with
k
valleys to one with
(n-1)-k
valleys.

A related statistic to number of valleys is major index. The
major index
of a Dyck path is the sum of the positions of its valleys. Major index is an important statistic because the
q

\operatorname{Cat}(n;q)\coloneqq\frac{1}{[n+1]_{q}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{2n}{n}_{q}
is the generating function for Dyck paths in
\operatorname{Dyck}_{n}
according to their major indices. Again, although not obvious, major index is symmetrically distributed: there are as many Dyck paths in
\operatorname{Dyck}_{n}
with major index
k
as with major index
n(n-1)-k
. And again, the Lalanne–Kreweras involution combinatorially exhibits this symmetry: it sends a Dyck path with major index
k
to one with major index
n(n-1)-k
.

We prefer to describe the Lalanne–Kreweras involution using the language of partially ordered sets (posets). For
\mathsf{P}

\operatorname{Dyck}_{n+1}

\operatorname{maj}(A)\coloneqq\sum_{[i,j]\in A}(i+j)

A set of intervals
A=\{[i_{1},j_{1}],\ldots,[i_{k},j_{k}]\}
is an antichain of
\mathsf{A}^{n}
if and only if:

•

1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n
,

•

1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}\leq n

•

and

The

\operatorname{LK}\colon\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})\to\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})

\operatorname{LK}(A)\coloneqq\{[i^{\prime}_{1},j^{\prime}_{1}],\ldots,[i^{\prime}_{m},j^{\prime}_{m}]\}

\displaystyle\{i^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<i^{\prime}_{m}\}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[n]\setminus\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\},

\displaystyle\{j^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<j^{\prime}_{m}\}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[n]\setminus\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}.

The

\times

\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathsf{P}}

\mathsf{P}
), then its tropicalization will be defined on, e.g.,
\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{P}}
.

Our piecewise-linear and birational extensions of the Lalanne–Kreweras involution are the maps
\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{PL}}
and
\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{B}}

.

•

•

\displaystyle\prod_{[i,j]\in\mathsf{A}^{n}}\pi([i,j])\cdot\prod_{[i,j]\in\mathsf{A}^{n}}\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{PL}}(\pi)([i,j])

Observe how the second bulleted item in
1.1
is the birational analog of the fact that the Lalanne–Kreweras involution exhibits the symmetry of the antichain cardinality and major index statistics. Thus, our piecewise-linear and birational extensions retain the key features of

Recently, there has been a great deal of interest in studying piecewise-linear and birational extensions of constructions from algebraic combinatorics. Indeed, these piecewise-linear and birational maps are at the core of the growing subfield of dynamical algebraic combinatorics
[
35
]
. Our work fits squarely into this research program.

The combinatorial operator whose piecewise-linear and birational lifts have received the most attention is rowmotion.
Rowmotion
,
\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{A}}\colon\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{P})\to\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{P})
, is the invertible operator on the set of antichains of a poset
\mathsf{P}
defined by

\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{A}}(A)\coloneqq\nabla(\{x\in\mathsf{P}\colon\textrm{$x\not\leq y$ for any $y\in A$}\})

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{P})
, where
\nabla(X)
denotes the set of minimal elements of
X
.

It is known
[
7
,
20
]
that rowmotion can alternatively be defined as a composition of toggles.
Toggles
are certain simple, local involutions which “toggle” the status of an element in a set when possible. This toggle perspective turns out to be very useful for analyzing the behavior of rowmotion. Moreover, in 2013 Einstein and Propp
[
12
]
introduced piecewise-linear and birational extensions of the toggles, and, with these, piecewise-linear and birational extensions of rowmotion.

Our first step towards defining the piecewise-linear and birational extensions of the Lalanne–Kreweras involution is to show that
\operatorname{LK}\colon\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})\to\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
can be written as a composition of toggles. Actually, we show that
\operatorname{LK}
is a special case of a more general construction.

For any graded poset
\mathsf{P}
,
rowvacuation
,
\operatorname{Rvac}_{\mathcal{A}}\colon\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{P})\to\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{P})
, is another map on antichains defined as a certain composition of toggles. Rowmotion and rowvacuation are “partner” operators in exactly the same way that promotion and evacuation are “partner” operators. We recall that promotion and evacuation are two operators on the set of linear extensions of a poset which were first defined and studied by Schützenberger
[
37
]
. The same basic facts about promotion and evacuation hold for rowmotion and rowvacuation: rowvacuation is always an involution, just like evacuation is; rowvacuation conjugates rowmotion to its inverse, just like evacuation does for promotion; etc. This connection explains the name “rowvacuation.”

We show that, in the case
\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{A}^{n}
, rowvacuation is precisely the Lalanne–Kreweras involution. This gives us natural candidates for
\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{PL}}
and
\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{B}}
, where we simply replace the toggles in the definition of rowvacuation with their piecewise-linear and birational extensions. General properties of the toggles imply that these
\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{PL}}
and
\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{B}}
remain involutions.

Then the final thing is to establish the piecewise-linear and birational analogs of the fact that the Lalanne–Kreweras involution exhibits the symmetry of the antichain cardinality and major index statistics. Results of this kind have also been a focus of recent research in dynamical algebraic combinatorics. More precisely, if
\varphi
is an invertible operator acting on a combinatorial set
X
, and
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is some statistic on
X
, then we say that
f
is
homomesic
with respect to the action of
\varphi
on
X
if the average of
f
along every
\varphi
-orbit is equal to the same constant.

With this terminology, we can say that the antichain cardinality and major index statistics are homomesic under the Lalanne–Kreweras involution. In fact, there is a broader collection of homomesies for
\operatorname{LK}
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, define
h_{i}\colon\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})\to\mathbb{Z}
by

h_{i}(A)\coloneqq\#\{j\colon[i,j]\in A\}+\#\{j\colon[j,i]\in A\}.

It is easily seen that
h_{i}(A)+h_{i}(\operatorname{LK}(A))=2
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
, i.e., that the average of
h_{i}
along any
\operatorname{LK}
-orbit is
1
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\#A

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(h_{1}(A)+h_{2}(A)+\cdots+h_{n}(A)),

\displaystyle\operatorname{maj}(A)

\displaystyle=h_{1}(A)+2\cdot h_{2}(A)+\cdots+n\cdot h_{n}(A).

Any linear combination of homomesies is again a homomesy. Thus, the antichain cardinality and major index homomesies for
\operatorname{LK}
follow from the
h_{i}
homomesies.

We show that the (piecewise-linear and birational analogs of) the
h_{i}
homomesies extend to
\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{PL}}
and
\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{B}}
. We do this via a careful analysis of a certain embedding of the triangle-shaped poset
\mathsf{A}^{n}
into the rectangle poset
\mathsf{[n+1]}\times\mathsf{[n+1]}
. From now on we will not separately emphasize the antichain cardinality and major index statistics, and instead focus on the more general
h_{i}
statistics.

2.1.

Rowmotion

Evidently,

2.3.
Rowvacuation

While rowmotion and toggling can be defined on any poset, our next action, rowvacuation, is defined only on graded posets.

For
0\leq i\leq r
, set

\mathbf{t}_{i}\coloneqq\prod\limits_{p\in\mathsf{P}_{i}}t_{p}.

The
order filter rank toggle

\mathbf{t}_{i}
is well-defined because toggles of elements of the same rank commute. Some immediate properties of these rank toggles are recorded in the next proposition.

Proposition 2.8
.

For
0\leq i,j\leq r
,

•

\mathbf{t}_{i}^{2}=1
;

•

\mathbf{t}_{i}\mathbf{t}_{j}=\mathbf{t}_{j}\mathbf{t}_{i}
if
|i-j|>1
.

Clearly,
\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathbf{t}_{0}\mathbf{t}_{1}\cdots\mathbf{t}_{r-1}\mathbf{t}_{r}
. This “row-by-row” (“rank-by-rank”) description of rowmotion is why it is called “rowmotion.” Rowvacuation is also built out of these rank toggles.

Definition 2.9
.

Order filter rowvacuation
is the map
\operatorname{Rvac}_{\mathcal{F}}\colon\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{P})\to\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{P})
defined as the following composition of rank toggles

\operatorname{Rvac}_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq(\mathbf{t}_{r})(\mathbf{t}_{r-1}\mathbf{t}_{r})\cdots(\mathbf{t}_{1}\mathbf{t}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{t}_{r-1}\mathbf{t}_{r})(\mathbf{t}_{0}\mathbf{t}_{1}\mathbf{t}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{t}_{r-1}\mathbf{t}_{r}).

Order filter dual rowvacuation
,
\operatorname{DRvac}_{\mathcal{F}}\colon\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{P})\to\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{P})
, is

\operatorname{DRvac}_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq(\mathbf{t}_{0})(\mathbf{t}_{1}\mathbf{t}_{0})\cdots(\mathbf{t}_{r-1}\cdots\mathbf{t}_{2}\mathbf{t}_{1}\mathbf{t}_{0})(\mathbf{t}_{r}\mathbf{t}_{r-1}\cdots\mathbf{t}_{2}\mathbf{t}_{1}\mathbf{t}_{0}).

We use
\mathsf{P}^{*}
to denote the
dual poset
to a poset
\mathsf{P}
. There is an obvious duality
{}^{*}\colon\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{P})\to\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{P}^{*})
between the order filters of
\mathsf{P}
and of
\mathsf{P}^{*}
; namely,
F^{*}\coloneqq\Theta(F)
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{P})
. We could alternatively define dual rowvacuation by setting
\operatorname{DRvac}_{\mathcal{F}}(F)\coloneqq\operatorname{Rvac}_{\mathcal{F}}(F^{*})^{*}
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{P})
. This explains the “dual” in the name “dual rowvacuation.”

The following are the basic properties relating rowmotion and rowvacuation which hold for all graded posets:

Proposition 2.10

(c.f.
[
19
]

2.6.
Piecewise-linear lifts

Now we explain the extensions of our actions on
\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{P})
and
\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{P})
from the combinatorial to the piecewise-linear (PL) realm.

Let
\widehat{\mathsf{P}}
denote the poset obtained from
\mathsf{P}
by adjoining a new minimal element
\widehat{0}
and a new maximal element
\widehat{1}
. Then define the following affine spaces of real-valued functions on
\widehat{\mathsf{P}}
or
\mathsf{P}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\pi\in\mathbb{R}^{\widehat{\mathsf{P}}}\colon\pi(\widehat{0})=0,\pi(\widehat{1})=\kappa\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\pi\in\mathbb{R}^{\widehat{\mathsf{P}}}\colon\pi(\widehat{0})=\kappa,\pi(\widehat{1})=0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\pi\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{P}}\}.

Here
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
is a parameter. When its values is clear from context, we will use the shorthands
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}(\mathsf{P})
,
\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{PL}}(\mathsf{P})
, and
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}(\mathsf{P})
. Observe that these three spaces are all basically the same (and we often implicitly identify them all with
\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{P}}
by forgetting the values at
\widehat{0}
and
\widehat{1}
); but we think of them separately as the piecewise-linear analogs of
\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{P})
,
\mathcal{J}(\mathsf{P})
, and
\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{P})
.

There are some important polytopes which live in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{1}(\mathsf{P})
,
\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{1}(\mathsf{P})
, and
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{1}(\mathsf{P})
. Namely:

•

the
order polytope

\mathcal{O}\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{P})\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{1}(\mathsf{P})
, where

\mathcal{O}\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{P})\coloneqq\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{1}(\mathsf{P})\colon\pi(x)\leq\pi(y)\textrm{ whenever $x\leq y\in\widehat{\mathsf{P}}$}\right\};

•

the
order-reversing polytope

\mathcal{O}\mathcal{R}(\mathsf{P})\subseteq\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{1}(\mathsf{P})
, where

\mathcal{O}\mathcal{R}(\mathsf{P})\coloneqq\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{1}(\mathsf{P})\colon\pi(x)\geq\pi(y)\textrm{ whenever $x\leq y\in\widehat{\mathsf{P}}$}\right\};

•

the
chain polytope

\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{P})\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{1}(\mathsf{P})
, where

\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{P})\coloneqq\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{1}(\mathsf{P})\colon 0\leq\sum_{x\in C}\pi(x)\leq 1\textrm{ for any chain $C\subseteq\mathsf{P}$}\right\}.

Recall that a
chain
of
\mathsf{P}
is a subset
C\subseteq\mathsf{P}
in which any two elements are comparable. Some of the inequalities in the above descriptions of these polytopes are redundant; for example, the facets of the order polytope
\mathcal{O}\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{P})
are given by

\pi(x)\leq\pi(y)\textrm{ whenever $x\lessdot y\in\widehat{\mathsf{P}}$},

and the facets of the chain polytope
\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{P})
are given by

\displaystyle 0\leq\pi(x)

\displaystyle\textrm{ for all $x\in\mathsf{P}$},

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in C}\pi(x)\leq 1

\displaystyle\textrm{ for any \emph{maximal} chain $C\subseteq\mathsf{P}$}.

Here by
maximal chain
we mean a maximal by inclusion chain.

We identify each subset
S\subseteq\mathsf{P}
with its indicator function. With this identification in mind, Stanley
[
38
]
showed that:

•

the vertices of
\mathcal{O}\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{P})
(resp. of
\mathcal{O}\mathcal{R}(\mathsf{P})
) are the
F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{P})
(resp.
J\in\mathcal{J}(\mathsf{P})
),

•

the vertices of
\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{P})
are the
A\in\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{P})
.

This explains how we transfer information from the piecewise-linear realm to the combinatorial realm: we
specialize
(i.e., restrict) to the vertices of these polyotpes.

We proceed to explain the piecewise-linear lifts of rowmotion and toggling introduced by Einstein and Propp
[
12
]
. In many cases we use exactly the same notation as in the combinatorial realms for these piecewise-linear maps, and let context distinguish them. Of course, the PL extensions specialize to their combinatorial analogs.

We first explain the PL analog of the definition of rowmotion as the composition of three bijections. These bijections are:

•

complementation

\Theta\colon\mathbb{R}^{\widehat{\mathsf{P}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\widehat{\mathsf{P}}}
, with
(\Theta\pi)(x)\coloneqq\kappa-\pi(x)
for all
x\in\widehat{\mathsf{P}}
;

•

up-transfer

\Delta\colon\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})
, with

(\Delta\pi)(x)\coloneqq\pi(x)-\max\left\{\pi(y)\colon x\lessdot y\in\widehat{\mathsf{P}}\right\}

for all
x\in\mathsf{P}
;

•

down-transfer

\nabla\colon\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})
, with

(\nabla\pi)(x)\coloneqq\pi(x)-\max\left\{\pi(y)\colon y\lessdot x\in\widehat{\mathsf{P}}\right\}

for all
x\in\mathsf{P}
.

Evidently,
\Theta^{-1}=\Theta
. Also, note that

\displaystyle(\Delta^{-1}\pi)(x)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\pi(y_{1})+\pi(y_{2})+\cdots+\pi(y_{k})\colon x=y_{1}\lessdot y_{2}\lessdot\cdots\lessdot y_{k}\lessdot\widehat{1}\in\widehat{\mathsf{P}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\pi(x)+\max\left\{(\Delta^{-1}\pi)(y)\colon x\lessdot y\in\widehat{\mathsf{P}}\right\}

for all
x\in\mathsf{P}
, and similarly,

\displaystyle(\nabla^{-1}\pi)(x)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\pi(y_{1})+\pi(y_{2})+\cdots+\pi(y_{k})\colon\widehat{0}\lessdot y_{1}\lessdot y_{2}\lessdot\cdots\lessdot y_{k}=x\in\widehat{\mathsf{P}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\pi(x)+\max\left\{(\nabla^{-1}\pi)(y)\colon y\lessdot x\in\widehat{\mathsf{P}}\right\}

for all
x\in\mathsf{P}
.

Complementation is an involution that maps
\mathcal{O}\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{P})
to
\mathcal{O}\mathcal{R}(\mathsf{P})
and vice versa. Down-transfer (which is equivalent to Stanley’s “transfer map”
[
38
]
) maps
\mathcal{O}\mathcal{P}(\mathsf{P})
to
\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{P})
, while up-transfer maps
\mathcal{O}\mathcal{R}(\mathsf{P})
to
\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{P})
.

Definition 2.20
.

PL order filter rowmotion
, denoted
\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathrm{PL}}:\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}(\mathsf{P})\to\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}(\mathsf{P})
, is given by
\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathrm{PL}}\coloneqq\Theta\circ\Delta^{-1}\circ\nabla
.

Definition 2.21
.

PL antichain rowmotion
, denoted
\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{PL}}:\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}(\mathsf{P})\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}(\mathsf{P})
, is given by
\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{PL}}\coloneqq\nabla\circ\Theta\circ\Delta^{-1}
.

Next, we go over the toggling description of these maps.

Definition 2.22
.

Let
p\in\mathsf{P}
. Then the
PL order filter toggle
at
p
is the map
t_{p}\colon\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})\to\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})
defined by

(t_{p}\pi)(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\pi(x)&\textrm{if $x\neq p$};\\
\max\left\{\pi(y)\colon y\lessdot x\in\widehat{\mathsf{P}}\right\}+\min\left\{\pi(y)\colon x\lessdot y\in\widehat{\mathsf{P}}\right\}-\pi(x)&\textrm{if $x=p$},\end{cases}

for all
x\in\mathsf{P}
.

Definition 2.23
.

Let
p\in\mathsf{P}
. Let
\operatorname{MC}_{p}(\mathsf{P})
denote the set of all maximal chains
C\subseteq\mathsf{P}
with
p\in C
. Then the
PL antichain toggle
at

\tau_{p}\colon\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})
defined by

for all

These PL toggles are again involutions and have the same commutativity properties as their combinatorial analogs. An important observation is that (when

2.7.
Birational lifts

Now we do everything again in the birational realm. The idea is to
detropicalize
the above piecewise-linear maps, that is, everywhere replace
\max
with addition, and addition with multiplication. (Strictly speaking, detropicalization is not well-defined because more identities are satisfied by
(\max,+)
than by
(+,\times)

\mathbb{K}

\mathsf{P}

\mathbb{K}
is an arbitrary field of characteristic zero, and treat these expressions as rational maps (i.e., defined outside of a Zariski closed set); this is done in
[

14

[
12

\mathbb{R}_{>0}

So define the following sets of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-valued functions on
\widehat{\mathsf{P}}
or
\mathsf{P}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{B}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\pi\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\widehat{\mathsf{P}}}\colon\pi\big(\widehat{0}\big)=1,\pi\big(\widehat{1}\big)=\kappa\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{B}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\pi\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\widehat{\mathsf{P}}}\colon\pi\big(\widehat{0}\big)=\kappa,\pi\big(\widehat{1}\big)=1\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{B}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{P})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\pi\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathsf{P}}\right\}.

Here
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a parameter; we use
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{B}}(\mathsf{P})
,
\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{B}}(\mathsf{P})
,
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{B}}(\mathsf{P})
when its value is clear from context. Also we often implicitly identify all of these sets with

by forgetting the values at

and

It is well-known that “birational identities tropicalize to PL identities,” although, as mentioned, not vice versa. What this means for us is that if some subtraction-free birational identity holds on all of
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{B}}(\mathsf{P})
then its
tropicalization
– the result of replacing addition by
\max

(and similarly for the other birational/PL spaces). See
[

We first explain the birational analog of the definition of rowmotion as the composition of three bijections. These bijections are:

•

complementation

\Theta\colon\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\widehat{\mathsf{P}}}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\widehat{\mathsf{P}}}

for all
x\in\mathsf{P}

for all

4.
Homomesies for the Lalanne–Kreweras involution

In this section we prove the homomesy results for the piecewise-linear and birational Lalanne–Kreweras involution. For conciseness we work exclusively at the birational level.

Recall from
Section

1

h_{i}\colon\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}
, for
1\leq i\leq n

h_{i}(A)\coloneqq\#\{j\colon[i,j]\in A\}+\#\{j\colon[j,i]\in A\}.

And recall that the antichain cardinality and major index statistics are linear combinations of the

\#A=\frac{1}{2}(h_{1}(A)+h_{2}(A)+\cdots+h_{n}(A));\quad\quad\operatorname{maj}(A)=h_{1}(A)+2h_{2}(A)+\cdots+nh_{n}(A).

Any linear combination of homomesies is again a homomesy; so in terms of understanding the homomesies of

We define the birational analogs

Theorem 4.1

The statistics

are all multiplicatively
\kappa

\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{B}}
on
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{B}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{A}^{n})

). Nonetheless, the products along the diagonals still work out exactly as claimed in
4.13
.

{proof}

[Proof of
4.13
]
For (a) and (b): as mentioned in
4.14
, it is not hard to see that we in fact have
\nabla\pi([i,j])=\nabla\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}\pi(n+2-i,j+1)
for all
[i,j]\in\mathsf{A}^{n}
. This immediately yields the claim.

For (c) and (d): here we need to be a little more careful. It is easy to see that (c) and (d) are equivalent via
\operatorname{Flip}
; so let us prove (c). Set
\pi^{\prime}:=\operatorname{Rvac}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathrm{B}}\pi
. We compute

\prod_{i\leq j\leq n}\hskip-5.0pt\nabla\operatorname{Rvac}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathrm{B}}\pi([i,j])=\hskip-5.0pt\prod_{i\leq j\leq n}\hskip-5.0pt\nabla\pi^{\prime}([i,j])=\frac{\prod_{i\leq j\leq n}\pi^{\prime}([i,j])}{\prod_{i\leq j\leq n-1}\big(\pi^{\prime}([i,j])+\pi^{\prime}([i+1,j+1])\big)}.

Then, denoting
Z\coloneqq\prod_{i\leq j\leq n}\nabla\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}\pi(n+1-j,i+1)
, we compute

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\frac{2\kappa\cdot\prod_{i+1\leq j\leq n-1}\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}\pi(n+1-j,i+1)}{\prod_{i\leq j\leq n-1}\big(\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}\pi(n+1-j,i)+\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}\pi(n-j,i+1)\big)\cdot\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}\pi(1,i)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\kappa\cdot\prod_{i\leq j\leq n-1}\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}\pi(n-j,i+1)}{\prod_{i\leq j\leq n-1}\big(\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}\pi(n+1-j,i)+\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}\pi(n-j,i+1)\big)\cdot\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}\pi(1,i)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\kappa\cdot\prod_{i\leq j\leq n-1}\big(\kappa\pi^{\prime}([i+1,j+1])^{-1}\big)}{\prod_{i\leq j\leq n-1}\big(\kappa\pi^{\prime}([i,j])^{-1}+\kappa\pi^{\prime}([i+1,j+1])^{-1}\big)\cdot\kappa\pi^{\prime}([i,n])^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\cdot\prod_{i\leq j\leq n-1}\pi^{\prime}([i+1,j+1])^{-1}}{\prod_{i\leq j\leq n-1}\big(\pi^{\prime}([i,j])^{-1}+\pi^{\prime}([i+1,j+1])^{-1}\big)\cdot\pi^{\prime}([i,n])^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\cdot\prod_{i\leq j\leq n-1}\big(\pi^{\prime}([i,j])\;\pi^{\prime}([i+1,j+1])\big)\cdot\pi^{\prime}([i,n])}{\prod_{i\leq j\leq n-1}\big(\pi^{\prime}([i,j])+\pi^{\prime}([i+1,j+1])\big)\prod_{i\leq j\leq n-1}\pi^{\prime}([i+1,j+1])}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\cdot\prod_{i\leq j\leq n}\pi^{\prime}([i,j])}{\prod_{i\leq j\leq n-1}\big(\pi^{\prime}([i,j])+\pi^{\prime}([i+1,j+1])\big)}

\displaystyle=2\cdot\prod_{i\leq j\leq n}\nabla\operatorname{Rvac}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathrm{B}}\pi([i,j]),

where to go from the fourth to fifth lines we used the identity
\frac{1}{a^{-1}+b^{-1}}=\frac{ab}{a+b}
, and to get the last line we used
\nabla\pi^{\prime}([i,j])=\frac{\pi^{\prime}([i,j])}{\pi^{\prime}([i-1,j])+\pi^{\prime}([i,j+1])}
for all
[i,j]\in\mathsf{A}^{n}
.

We can now prove
4.1
.

{proof}

[Proof of
4.1
]
So let
\pi\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{B}}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
and let
1\leq i\leq n
. Set
\widetilde{\pi}:=\nabla^{-1}(\pi)
. Applying parts (a) and (d) of
4.13
to

\displaystyle 2\hskip-5.0pt\prod_{i\leq j\leq n}\hskip-5.0pt\pi([i,j])\hskip-5.0pt\prod_{1\leq j\leq i}\hskip-5.0pt\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{B}}\pi([j,i])

\displaystyle=\hskip-5.0pt\prod_{i\leq j\leq n}\hskip-5.0pt\nabla\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}\widetilde{\pi}(n+2-i,j+1)\hskip-5.0pt\prod_{1\leq j\leq i}\hskip-5.0pt\nabla\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}\widetilde{\pi}(n+2-i,j)

where in the last equality we used

2\prod_{i\leq j\leq n}\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{B}}\pi([i,j]))\prod_{1\leq j\leq i}\pi([j,i])=2\kappa.

Hence,

.

The embedding

\iota^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\mathsf{B}}\colon\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{B}^{\prime n})\to\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{A}^{2n})
.

{proof}

This is straightforward.

For
1\leq i\leq 2n-1
, define
h^{\mathrm{PL}}_{i}\colon\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}(\mathsf{B}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}

h^{\mathrm{PL}}_{i}(\pi)\coloneqq\sum_{i\leq j\leq 2n-1}\pi(\langle\operatorname{Flip}\rangle[i,j])+\sum_{1\leq j\leq i}\pi(\langle\operatorname{Flip}\rangle[j,i]).

Note that
h^{\mathrm{PL}}_{i}=h^{\mathrm{PL}}_{2n-i}
. Define
h^{\mathrm{PL}}_{i}\colon\mathsf{B}^{\prime n}\to\mathbb{R}
, for

Corollary 5.2
.

The statistics
h^{\mathrm{PL}}_{i}\colon\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{B}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}

. The same is true for the

{proof}

For

(\nabla\iota^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\mathsf{B}}\nabla^{-1}\pi)([i,j])=\pi(\langle\operatorname{Flip}\rangle[i,j]),

.

.

The statistic

Observe that for any

.

The embedding

.

The statistics

In this section we will consider some enumeration related to the operators we have been studying. Specifically, we will count fixed points of elements of

\mathsf{A}^{n}

\operatorname{Flip}

\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n+1}{\lfloor(n+1)/2 \rfloor}
.

Affirming a conjecture of Bessis and Reiner

[

]

-Catalan polynomial
\operatorname{Cat}(n+1;q)
. This means that the numbers of fixed points of elements of
\langle\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle
acting on

are given by plugging roots of unity into
\operatorname{Cat}(n+1;q)

\operatorname{Cat}(n+1;q)

\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{A}}^{i}
has a nice product formula for any

\operatorname{Flip}

\operatorname{Cat}(n+1;q\coloneqq-1)=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{n+1}{\lfloor(n+1)/2 \rfloor}
.

in the order filter toggle group of
\mathsf{A}^{n}
.

For considerations of space, we will not go through all the details of the proof of
Lemma

6.3
here (and anyways it is a relatively straightforward computation); but let us state two propositions which aid in the proof.

Proposition 6.4
.

If
k
and
n
have the same parity, then
\operatorname{Flip}\circ\mathbf{t}_{k}
is conjugate to
\operatorname{Flip}
in the order filter toggle group of
\mathsf{A}^{n}
.

Proposition 6.5
.

For
0\leq k\leq n-1
, define
d_{k}\colon\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{A}^{n})\to\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
by

d_{k}\coloneqq(\mathbf{t}_{0}\mathbf{t}_{1}\mathbf{t}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{t}_{k})(\mathbf{t}_{0}\mathbf{t}_{1}\mathbf{t}_{2}\cdots\mathbf{t}_{k-1})\cdots(\mathbf{t}_{0}\mathbf{t}_{1})(\mathbf{t}_{0}).

If
k
has the same parity as
n
, then
\operatorname{Flip}\circ d_{k}
is conjugate to
\operatorname{Flip}
in the order filter toggle group of
\mathsf{A}^{n}
.

In summary, for any element of
\langle\operatorname{LK},\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle
acting on
\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
, there is a nice product formula for its number of fixed points.

Remark 6.6
.

For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, the set
\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}^{\mathsf{P}}\cap\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{P})
of rational points in the chain polytope with denominator dividing
m
is a finite set. In fact, a result of Proctor
[
31
,
32
]
(namely, the enumeration of “plane partitions of staircase shape”) implies that when
\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{A}^{n}
the cardinality of this set is

\#\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}^{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\cap\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{A}^{n})=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\frac{i+j+2m}{i+j}.

Observe how the case
m=1
recaptures the product formula for the Catalan number.

Since
\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}^{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\cap\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
is preserved by the PL antichain toggles
\tau_{p}
(when
\kappa=1
, which we will assume from now on), it caries an action of
\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{PL}}
and
\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{PL}}
. We could therefore ask for formulas counting fixed points of elements of
\langle\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{PL}},\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{PL}}\rangle
acting on this set of points.

Extending the CSP results of Armstrong–Stump–Thomas, it is conjectured (see
[
17
, Conj. 4.28]

[
18
, Conj. 5.2]
) that
\langle\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{PL}}\rangle
acting on
\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}^{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\cap\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
exhibits cyclic sieving with the sieving polynomial being the so-called “
q
-multi-Catalan number.” But this remains unproven.

Lemma

6.3
extends directly to the PL level (and in fact to the birational level as well). Thus, the number of fixed points of
\operatorname{Rvac}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{PL}}\circ\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{PL}}
acting on
\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}^{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\cap\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
is the same as the number of points in this set fixed by
\operatorname{Flip}
. By another result of Proctor
[
30
]
(enumerating “plane partitions of shifted trapezoidal shape”) this number is known to be

\#\left\{\pi\in\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}^{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\cap\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{A}^{n})\colon\operatorname{Flip}(\pi)=\pi\right\}=\hskip-8.0pt\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq\lceil n/2\rceil}\hskip-8.0pt\frac{i+j-1+m}{i+j-1}\hskip-8.0pt\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\hskip-8.0pt\frac{i+j+m}{i+j}.

As for counting fixed points of
\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{PL}}
acting on
\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}^{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\cap\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
, we have no idea what the answer should be.

7.
Future directions

In this final section we discuss some possible future directions for research.

7.1.
Rowvacuation for other posets

Our work here suggests that it may be interesting to study rowvacuation for other graded posets. It especially makes sense to study rowvacuation on those posets which are known to have good behavior of rowmotion. Prominent examples of such posets include the
minuscule posets
and
root posets
. As explained in
[
19
]
, work of Grinberg–Roby
[
14
]
and Okada
[
26
]
implies that rowvacuation of a minuscule poset
\mathsf{P}
has a very simple description in terms of a canonical anti-automorphism of
\mathsf{P}
. Meanwhile, in
[
9
]
, Defant and the second author study rowvacuation for the classical type root posets. Additionally, as discussed in
[
19
]
, there are a handful of other families of posets which have good rowmotion behavior, and it might be worth looking at rowvacuation for these.

7.2.
Combinatorics of the
\mathsf{A}^{n}
into
\mathsf{[n+1]}\times\mathsf{[n+1]}
embedding

By tropicalizing the embedding
\iota^{\mathrm{B}}_{\mathsf{A}}
, we obtain an embedding

\iota^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\mathsf{A}}\colon\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\kappa}(\mathsf{A}^{n})\to\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{PL}}_{2\kappa}(\mathsf{[n+1]}\times\mathsf{[n+1]}).

In particular (with
\kappa=1
) we have
\iota^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\mathsf{A}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{A}^{n}))\subseteq 2\cdot\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{[n+1]}\times\mathsf{[n+1]})
. We can ask where the vertices of
\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
are sent under
\iota^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\mathsf{A}}
. In fact, the image of the vertices of
\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
under
\iota^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\mathsf{A}}
is a set that can be naturally identified with the
321
-avoiding permutations in the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}
. In this way,
\iota^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\mathsf{A}}
provides a bijection between Dyck paths (i.e.,
\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
) and
321
-avoiding permutations. There are many known such bijections; it is not hard to see that
\iota^{\mathrm{PL}}_{\mathsf{A}}
is precisely the
Billey–Jockusch–Stanley bijection

[
4
,
6
,
13
]
. Furthermore, since
\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
caries an action of rowmotion, we can use this Billey–Jockusch–Stanley bijection to define an action of rowmotion on the set of
321
-avoiding permutations. Rowmotion on
321
-avoiding permutations is studied in upcoming work of Adenbaum and Elizalde
[
1
]
.

7.3.
Invariants

A natural thing to do when studying any operator is to try to find functions that are invariant under the operator. For example, these invariant functions can separate orbits. However, for the operators studied in dynamical algebraic combinatorics, it is quite hard in practice to find nontrivial invariant functions. This is because, loosely speaking, these operators “move things around a lot.” Indeed, this is a major reason there has been so much focus on finding homomesies for these operators: there is a precise sense in which invariant functions and
0
-mesies are dual to one another (see
[
34
, §2.4]
and
[
33
]
).

Nonetheless, there actually
is
a very interesting function on antichains in
\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
which is invariant under both rowmotion and the Lalanne–Kreweras involution. This invariant appears in a paper of Panyushev
[
28
]
, but he attributes it to Oksana Yakimova and calls it the OY-invariant.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
A
be an antichain in
\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
and
F\coloneqq\nabla^{-1}(A)
be the order filter generated by
A
. Then we define the
OY-invariant

\mathcal{Y}(A)
of
A
to be

\mathcal{Y}(A)\coloneqq\sum\limits_{e\in A}\big(\#\nabla(F\setminus\{e\})-\#A+1\big).

Theorem 7.2

(
[
28
, Theorem 3.2, Proposition 3.6]
)
.

The function
\mathcal{Y}\colon\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})\to\mathbb{Z}
is invariant under both
\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\operatorname{LK}
.

Can
\mathcal{Y}
be generalized to the birational realm to a function that is invariant under
\operatorname{Row}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{B}}
and
\operatorname{LK}^{\mathrm{B}}
? We will now explain how we think it can.

First, for each
[i,j]\in\mathsf{A}^{n}
and
A\in\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
, define

\mathcal{Y}_{[i,j]}(A)=\begin{cases}\#\nabla(\nabla^{-1}(A)\setminus\{[i,j]\})-\#A+1&\textrm{if $[i,j]\in A$},\\
0&\textrm{if $[i,j]\not\in A$}.\end{cases}

Then note that

\mathcal{Y}=\sum_{[i,j]\in\mathsf{A}^{n}}\mathcal{Y}_{[i,j]}.

In the next definition we give the detropicalized version of these statistics. For conciseness we omit the proof, but one can show that the map
\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{B}}_{[i,j]}
defined below is equivalent to
\mathcal{Y}_{[i,j]}
when tropicalized and restricted to the combinatorial realm.

Definition 7.3
.

Let
\pi\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{B}}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
. Define the
birational OY-invariant

\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{B}}
of
\pi
as

\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{B}}(\pi)\coloneqq\prod\limits_{[i,j]\in\mathsf{A}^{n}}\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{B}}_{[i,j]}(\pi).

Here for
[i,j]\in\mathsf{A}^{n}
we define
\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{B}}_{[i,j]}(\pi)\coloneqq LR
, with
L
and
R
described below.

•

If
i=1
, set
L\coloneqq 1
.
If
i\geq 2
, then consider the subposet
\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{L}}
of
\mathsf{A}^{n}
consisting of elements
e
such that
e
is greater than or equal to either
[i-1,i-1]
or
[i,i]
AND
e
is less than or equal to either
[i-1,j-1]
or
[i,j]
. Also consider the subposet
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{L}}=\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{L}}\setminus\{[i,j]\}
.
Then set

L\coloneqq\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1}\lessdot\cdots\lessdot v_{j-i+1}\\
\textrm{ a maximal chain in $\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{L}}$}\end{subarray}}\pi(v_{1})\pi(v_{2})\cdots\pi(v_{j-i+1})}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1}\lessdot\cdots\lessdot v_{j-i+1}\\
\textrm{ a maximal chain in $\mathsf{P}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{L}}$}\end{subarray}}\pi(v_{1})\pi(v_{2})\cdots\pi(v_{j-i+1})}.

•

If
j=n
, set
R\coloneqq 1
.
If
j\leq n-1
, then consider the subposet
\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{R}}
of
\mathsf{A}^{n}
consisting of elements
e
such that
e
is greater than or equal to either
[j,j]
or
[j+1,j+1]
AND
e
is less than or equal to either
[i,j]
or
[i+1,j+1]
. Also consider the subposet
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{R}}=\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{R}}\setminus\{[i,j]\}.

Then set

R\coloneqq\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1}\lessdot\cdots\lessdot v_{j-i+1}\\
\textrm{ a maximal chain in $\mathsf{P}_{\mathsf{R}}$}\end{subarray}}\pi(v_{1})\pi(v_{2})\cdots\pi(v_{j-i+1})}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1}\lessdot\cdots\lessdot v_{j-i+1}\\
\textrm{ a maximal chain in $\mathsf{P}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{R}}$}\end{subarray}}\pi(v_{1})\pi(v_{2})\cdots\pi(v_{j-i+1})}.

The function

Remark 7.6
.

At the combinatorial level, it appears that

is invariant not just under

and

τ \tau -cluster morphism categories of factor algebras
Maximilian Kaipel

Abstract
. We take a novel lattice-theoretic approach to the τ \tau -cluster morphism category 𝔗 ⁡ ( A ) \mathfrak{T}(A) of a finite-dimensional algebra A A and define the category via the lattice of torsion classes tors ⁡ A \tors A . Using the lattice congruence induced by an ideal I I of A A we establish a functor F I : 𝔗 ⁡ ( A ) → 𝔗 ⁡ ( A / I ) F_{I}:\mathfrak{T}(A)\to\mathfrak{T}(A/I) . If tors ⁡ A \tors A is finite, F I F_{I} is a regular epimorphism in the category of small categories and we characterise when F I F_{I} is full and faithful. The construction is purely combinatorial, meaning that the lattice of torsion classes determines the τ \tau -cluster morphism category up to equivalence.

2.
Background

Throughout,
K
is a field and
A
a finite-dimensional
K
-algebra. The category of finitely-generated (right) modules is denoted by
\mods A
. A
torsion class
is a full subcategory of
\mods A
which is closed under taking factor modules and extensions
[
Dic66
]
. Dually, a
torsion-free
class is a full subcategory of
\mods A
which is closed under taking submodules and extensions. A full subcategory of
\mods A
is called
wide
if it is closed under kernels, cokernels and extensions. Both torsion classes and wide subcategories of
\mods A
form partially ordered sets (posets) under inclusion and we denote these posets by
\tors A
and
\wide A
respectively. We now recall the definition of lattices, for a textbook reference see for example
[
Grä02
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
L
be a poset.

(1)

L
is called a
join-semilattice
if there exists a unique minimal common upper bound, the
join

x\lor y\in L
of
x
and
y
for all
x,y\in L
.

(2)

L
is called a
meet-semilattice
if there exists a unique maximal common lower bound, the
meet

x\land y\in L
of
x
and
y
for all
x,y\in L
.

A join-semilattice (resp. meet-semilattice)
L
is called
complete
if every subset
S=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}\subseteq L
admits a unique maximal upper bound
\bigvee S\coloneqq x_{1}\lor\dots\lor x_{r}
(resp. a unique maximal lower bound
\bigwedge S\coloneqq x_{1}\land\dots\land x_{r}
).

Definition 2.2
.

A poset
L
which is both a join-semilattice and a meet-semilattice is called a
lattice
. A lattice is
complete
if it is both a complete join-semilattice and a complete meet-semilattice.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
Grä02
, Lem. 14]

The following coincide for a poset
L
:

(1)

L
is a complete join-semilattice.

(2)

L
is a complete meet-semilattice.

(3)

L
is complete lattice.

This makes the following easy to see.

Example 2.4
.

The partially ordered sets
\tors A
and
\wide A
are complete meet-semilattices with meet given by intersection. By
Lemma

2.3
they are therefore complete lattices. For some special properties of the lattice
\tors A
see for example
[
DIR+23
, Thm. 1.3]

Definition 2.5
.

Let
L
be a join-semilattice (resp. meet-semilattice), a
join-subsemilattice
(resp.
meet-subsemilattice
)
K
is a subset
K\subseteq L
such that for all
x,y\in K
we have
x\lor y\in K
(resp.
x\land y\in K
). A subset
K
of a lattice
L
is a
sublattice
if it is both a join-subsemilattice and meet-subsemilattice.

Definition 2.6
.

An equivalence relation
\equiv
on a complete lattice
L
is called a
complete lattice congruence
if for an indexing set
I
and families
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I},\{y_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq L
the following holds:

x_{i}\equiv y_{i}\text{ for all }i\in I\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\bigvee\{x_{i}:i\in I\}\equiv\bigvee\{y_{i}:i\in I\}\text{ and }\bigwedge\{x_{i}:i\in I\}\equiv\bigwedge\{y_{i}:i\in I\}.

Given the corresponding set of partitions
\Phi_{\equiv}
of an equivalence relation
\equiv
, define the
quotient lattice

L/\equiv
as the lattice whose elements are elements of
\Phi
and such that for
C_{1},C_{2}\in\Phi
the element
C_{1}\lor C_{2}
is the equivalence class
C_{3}\in\Phi
containing
x\lor y
for some
x\in C_{1}
and
y\in C_{2}
. The meet
C_{1}\land C_{2}
is defined dually.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}
be complete lattices. A map
\eta:L_{1}\to L_{2}
is a
morphism of complete lattices
if
\eta(\bigvee_{L_{1}}S)=\bigvee_{L_{2}}\eta(S)
and
\eta(\bigwedge_{L_{1}}S)=\bigwedge_{L_{2}}\eta(S)
for all
S\subseteq L_{1}
.

The following result will play an essential role throughout this article and act as an example of the notions defined above.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
DIR+23
, Thm. 5.12]

For any ideal
I\in\ideal A
the map
\overline{(-)}_{I}:\mathcal{T}\mapsto\overline{(\mathcal{T})}_{I}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}\cap\mods A/I

and the induced equivalence relation

For convenience we write

and
\overline{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq\overline{(\mathcal{T})}_{I}
when no confusion about

may arise. We can describe lattice congruences in the following way.

.

Let

be a complete lattice congruence on

, then the equivalence classes

If

2.1.
\tau
-tilting theory

Let
\tau
denote the Auslander-Reiten translation of
\mods A
. In
[
AIR14
]
the authors introduce
\tau
-tilting theory as a simultaneous generalisation of classical tilting theory to higher projective dimensions and a completion of classical tilting theory from the viewpoint of mutation. Denote by
\proj A
the full subcategory of
\mods A
consisting of projective modules and for

denote by

the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable direct summands of

.

Definition 2.10
.

[
AIR14
, Def. 0.3]

A pair
(M,P)\in\mods A\times\proj A
is called
\tau
-rigid
if
\Hom_{A}(M,\tau M)=0
and
\Hom_{A}(P,M)=0
. It is called
\tau
-tilting if additionally
|M|+|P|=|A|
.

For a module
M
denote by
\Fac M
the full subcategory of
\mods A
consisting of factor modules of
M
and by
M^{\perp}\coloneqq\{X\in\mods A:\Hom_{A}(M,X)=0\}
and dually for
{}^{\perp}M
. It was shown in
[
AS81
, Thm. 5.10]
that
\Fac M
is a torsion class if
M
is
\tau
-rigid. Torsion classes arising in this way are called
functorially-finite
and conversely each functorially-finite torsion class arises as
\Fac M
of some
\tau
-rigid module
M\in\mods A
by
[
AIR14
, Prop. 1.1, Prop. 1.2]
. We denote the collection of functorially-finite torsion classes by
\ftors A
. More precisely, by
[
AIR14
, Thm. 2.7]
we have bijection between
\tau
-tilting pairs and
\ftors A
, which induced poset structure on the set of
\tau
-tilting pairs. By
[
IRTT15
]

\ftors A
forms a complete lattice if and only if
\ftors A=\tors A
if and only if there are only finitely many
\tau
-rigid pairs. An algebra
A
satisfying these equivalent conditions is called
\tau
-tilting finite
.

Proposition 2.11
.

[
AIR14
, Cor. 2.13]

Every
\tau
-rigid pair
(M,P)
gives rise to two torsion classes
\Fac M\in\ftors A
and
{}^{\perp}\tau M\cap P^{\perp}\in\ftors A
. Moreover,
\Fac M\subseteq{}^{\perp}\tau M\cap P^{\perp}
.

More generally, given a full subcategory

, we denote by
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{W})\coloneqq\Filt(\Fac\mathcal{S})
the smallest torsion class containing

, see
[

, Lem. 3.1]
.

.

An interval

\mathcal{U}=\Fac M

\tau
-rigid pair

-perpendicular wide subcategory

. For this reason we restrict ourselves to the case when
A
is
\tau
-tilting finite, which implies
\ftors A=\tors A
by
[
DIJ19
, Thm. 3.8]
.

In this case,
\tau
-perpendicular intervals and join-intervals (and meet-intervals) all coincide. In order to distinguish between intervals of the same shape but which correspond to distinct subcategories we need to recover the brick labelling of
\tors A
combinatorially, since the brick labelling determines the corresponding
\tau
-perpendicular subcategory by
[
DIR+23
, Thm. 4.16]
and
[
AP22
, Prop. 5.3]
.

Let
L
be a lattice, an element
j\in L
is called
completely join-irreducible
if there does not exist a subset
S\subseteq L
such that
j=\bigvee S
and
j\not\in S
. A join-irreducible element
j\in L
covers exactly one other element
j_{*}
. Take
x\lessdot y
then the set
\{t\in L:t\lor x=y\}\subseteq L
has a minimum element
\ell\in L
which is completely join-irreducible and satisfies
\ell_{*}\leq x
by
[
RET21
, Lem. 3.7]
. This inspires the following definition, similar to
[
RET21
, Rmk. 3.8]
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
L
be a completely semidistributive lattice. The
join-irreducible labelling
of
L
associates to each arrow
x\leftarrow y\subseteq\Hasse(L)
the unique completely join-irreducible element
j
which is minimal in the set
\{t\in L:x\lor t=y\}\subseteq L
.

Lemma 4.5
.

[
DIR+23
, Thm. 3.11]

Let
L
be an abstract lattice isomorphic to
\tors A
, then the join-irreducible labelling on
L
corresponds to the brick labelling of
\tors A
. In other words, the brick labelling is determined combinatorially by the underlying lattice structure.

Remark 4.6
.

This fact was used in
[
Eno23
]
to recover the posets of wide and so-called ICE-closed subcategories of
\mods A
from the lattice theoretic information of
\tors A
. And we have taken inspiration for the formulation of the following theorem from
[
Eno23
, Thm. A]
.

For an interval
[x,y]\subseteq L
write
\jirrc[x,y]
for the set of completely-join irreducible elements
j\in L
arising as labels of cover relations of
x\leq a\lessdot b\leq y
. The following is the main result of this section and follows the same idea as the previous remark.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
L
be an abstract finite lattice isomorphic to
\tors A
for some finite-dimensional algebra
A
. Then we can define
\mathfrak{T}(A)
combinatorially from
L
without using any algebraic information of
A
or
\mods A
. More precisely, we have an equivalence of categories
\mathfrak{T}(L)\cong\mathfrak{T}(A)
, where
\mathfrak{T}(L)
is defined to be the category:

•

whose objects are equivalence classes of join-intervals
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]=[\mathcal{U},\bigvee_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{U}_{i}]\subseteq L
under the equivalence

[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]\sim[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]

whenever
\jirrc[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]=\jirrc[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]
. In this case there exists an isomorphism
\phi_{[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}][\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]}:[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]\to[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]
preserving the join-irreducible labels (in
L
) of the arrows in the Hasse diagrams;

•

and whose morphisms are given by equivalence classes of morphisms in the poset category
\itv(L)
of intervals of
L
partially-ordered by reverse containment. More precisely

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathfrak{T}(L)}([\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]_{\sim},[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]_{\sim})=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}]\in[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]_{\sim}\\
[\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}]\in[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]_{\sim}\end{subarray}}\Hom_{\itv(L)}([\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}],[\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}])

under the equivalence relation
f_{[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}][\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1}]}\sim f_{[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}][\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{2}]}

whenever

\phi_{[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}][\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]}([\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1}])=[\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{2}].

Proof.

Denote by
\psi:L\to\tors A
the isomorphism of complete lattices. Since
L
is finite it follows that
\tors A
is finite and hence
\ftors A=\tors A
by
[
DIJ19
, Thm. 3.8]
. Let
[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]
and
[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]
be two distinct join-intervals of
L
satisfying
\jirrc[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]=\jirrc[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]
. By
Proposition

4.3
and
Lemma

4.5
, this is equivalent to saying that
\psi([\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}])
and
\psi([\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}])
are
\tau
-perpendicular intervals of
\tors A
such that
\brick(\psi([\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]))=\brick(\psi([\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]))
. Moreover, by
[
DIR+23
, Lem. 3.10]
we have

\psi(\mathcal{U}_{1})^{\perp}\cap\psi(\mathcal{T}_{1})=\Filt(\brick(\psi([\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}])))=\Filt(\brick(\psi([\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}])))=\psi(\mathcal{U}_{2})^{\perp}\cap\psi(\mathcal{T}_{2}).

It follows that
[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]\sim_{\mathfrak{T}(L)}[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]
implies
\psi([\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}])\sim_{\mathfrak{T}(A)}\psi([\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}])
. Moreover, then
Theorem

3.1
and
Proposition

3.2
imply that there is a brick label-preserving isomorphism
\varphi:\psi([\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}])\to\psi([\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}])
. By
Lemma

4.5

\varphi
lifts to a join-irreducible-label-preserving isomorphisms

\phi_{[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}][\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]}\coloneqq\psi^{-1}\varphi\psi:[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]\to[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}].

Let
[\mathcal{V}_{i},\mathcal{S}_{i}]\subseteq L
be join-intervals such that
[\mathcal{V}_{i},\mathcal{S}_{i}]\subseteq[\mathcal{U}_{i},\mathcal{T}_{i}]
for
i=1,2
. Then
f_{[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}][\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1}]}\sim_{\mathfrak{T}(L)}f_{[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}][\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{2}]}
means
\phi_{[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}][\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]}([\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1}])=[\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{2}]
which implies

\psi([\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1}])\cap(\psi(\mathcal{U}_{1})^{\perp}\cap\psi(\mathcal{T}_{1}))=\psi([\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{2}])\cap(\psi(\mathcal{U}_{2})^{\perp}\cap\psi(\mathcal{T}_{2}))

as
\varphi
factors through
\tors(\psi(\mathcal{U}_{1})^{\perp}\cap\psi(\mathcal{T}_{1}))=\tors(\psi(\mathcal{U}_{2})^{\perp}\cap\psi(\mathcal{T}_{2}))
. Thus

f_{[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}][\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1}]}\sim_{\mathfrak{T}(L)}f_{[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}][\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{2}]}\Longrightarrow f_{\psi([\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}])\psi([\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1}])}\sim_{\mathfrak{T}(A)}f_{\psi([\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}])\psi([\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{2}])}.

Conversely, let
[\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}]
and
[\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}]
be
\tau
-perpendicular intervals of
\tors A
such that
(\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime})^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime})^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}
, then
Theorem

3.1
and

Proposition

3.2
imply that there exists a brick-label preserving isomorphism
\varphi^{\prime}:[\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}]\to[\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}]
which lifts to a join-irreducible-preserving isomorphism

\phi_{[\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}][\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}]}^{\prime}\coloneqq\psi^{-1}\varphi^{\prime}\psi:\psi^{-1}([\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime})]\to\psi^{-1}([\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}]).

From
Lemma

4.5
it follows that
\jirrc(\psi^{-1}([\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}]))=\jirrc(\psi^{-1}([\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}]))
. Therefore

[\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}]\sim_{\mathfrak{T}(A)}[\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\psi^{-1}([\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}])\sim_{\mathfrak{T}(L)}\psi^{-1}([\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}]).

Consequently the objects of
\mathfrak{T}(A)
and
\mathfrak{T}(L)
are in bijection. Now let
[\mathcal{V}_{i}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}]
be
\tau
-perpendicular intervals such that
[\mathcal{V}_{i}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}]\subseteq[\mathcal{U}_{i}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{i}^{\prime}]
for
i=1,2
. Then
f_{[\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}][\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}]}\sim_{\mathfrak{T}(A)}f_{[\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}][\mathcal{V}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\prime}]}
means
[\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}]\cap((\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime})^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime})=[\mathcal{V}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\prime}]\cap((\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime})^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime})
. Since
\varphi^{\prime}
factors through
\tors((\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime})^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime})=\tors((\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime})^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime})
, it follows that

\phi_{[\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}][\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}]}([\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}])=[\mathcal{V}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\prime}].

Therefore
\phi_{[\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}][\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}]}^{\prime}\psi^{-1}([\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}])=([\mathcal{V}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\prime}])
, which imples

f_{[\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}][\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}]}\sim_{\mathfrak{T}(A)}f_{[\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}][\mathcal{V}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\prime}]}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f_{\psi^{-1}([\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}])\psi^{-1}([\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}])}\sim_{\mathfrak{T}(L)}f_{\psi^{-1}([\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\prime}])\psi([\mathcal{V}_{2}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\prime}])}.

Hence morphisms sets of
\mathfrak{T}(A)
and
\mathfrak{T}(L)
are in bijection and the construction of the
\mathfrak{T}(A)
and
\mathfrak{T}(L)
coincide. As a consequence, the categories
\mathfrak{T}(A)
and
\mathfrak{T}(L)
are equivalent.
∎

As an immediate consequence we get the following.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
A
be
\tau
-tilting finite and
B
be
\tau
-tilting equivalent to
A
. Then there exists an equivalence of categories
\mathfrak{T}(A)\cong\mathfrak{T}(B)
.

Proof.

Since
A
is
\tau
-tilting finite
\tors A=\ftors A\cong\ftors B
and therefore
B
is
\tau
-tilting finite. Then both are isomorphic to an abstract finite lattice
L\cong\ftors A
and we get
\mathfrak{T}(A)\cong\mathfrak{T}(L)\cong\mathfrak{T}(B)
from
Theorem

4.7
.
∎

As an alternative definition, see
[
BM21b
, Prop. 11.7]
,
signed
\tau
-exceptional sequences
can be defined as factorisations of morphisms in
\mathfrak{T}(A)
into irreducible morphisms. As a consequence of
Theorem

4.7
, we get the following.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
A
be
\tau
-tilting finite and
B
be
\tau
-tilting equivalent to
A
. Then there exists a bijection

\{\text{signed $\tau$-exceptional sequences of $\mods A$}\}\longleftrightarrow\{\text{signed $\tau$-exceptional sequences of $\mods B$}\}.

Proof.

The fully faithful functor of the equivalence of categories
\mathfrak{T}(A)\cong\mathfrak{T}(B)
of
Corollary

4.8
induces a bijection between
\Hom
-sets of
\mathfrak{T}(A)
and
\mathfrak{T}(B)
, which consequently gives a bijection between all factorisations of morphisms in the
\Hom
-sets and thus all signed
\tau
-exceptional sequences corresponding with those.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

Recently, a mutation for
\tau
-exceptional sequences was defined
[
BHM24
]
. This mutation generalises that of exceptional sequences of hereditary algebras
[
CB93
,
Rin94
]
. if
A
and
B
are two finite-dimensional algebras such that
\tors A\cong\tors B
and both are finite, then there is a bijection between
\tau
-exceptional sequences of
\mods A
and
\mods B
by
[

. However, the mutation of

-exceptional sequences heavily relies on signed

-exceptional sequences. Since
Corollary

shows that these are in bijection as well, it seems plausible that the mutation of

-exceptional sequences may be determined by the lattice of torsion classes. This could provide further inside into the problem of transitivity of the mutation, the understanding of which is still limited to two special cases, see
[
BHM24
,
BKT25
]
.

5.
Factor algebras and lattice congruences

We now use the lattice-theoretic definition of the
\tau
-cluster morphism category to gain new insights into its structure and its properties. In particular, as illustrated in
Example

8.1
this approach appears to be the most natural one to study factor algebras of
A
by an ideal
I
. An intuitive reason for this is that
\tors A\cap\mods A/I=\tors A/I
but the same behaviour is generally not exhibited by
\tau
-rigid pairs or wide subcategories. More precisely, recall from
Theorem

2.8
that an ideal
I\in\ideal A
induces a surjective morphism of lattices
\tors A\twoheadrightarrow\tors A/I
given by
\mathcal{T}\mapsto\mathcal{T}\cap\mods A/I
. The following result describes the lattice congruence
\Phi_{I}
on
\tors A
in terms of the brick labelling.

Theorem 5.1
.

[
DIR+23
, Thm. 5.15]

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional algebra and
I
an ideal.

(1)

An arrow
q\in\Hasse(\tors A)
is not contracted by
\Phi_{I}
if and only if its brick label
B_{q}
is in
\mods A/I
. In this case, it has the same label in
\Hasse(\tors A)
and
\Hasse(\tors A/I)
.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{T}\in\tors A
, then
\mathcal{U}\equiv_{\Phi_{I}}\mathcal{T}
if and only if,
IB\neq 0
for all bricks
B\in\mathcal{U}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}
.

To understand the behaviour of intervals under the surjective lattice morphism
\tors A\twoheadrightarrow\tors A/I
given by intersecting with
\mods A/I
we need the following two immediate results. Throughout the following, fix an ideal
I\in\ideal A
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{U}\in\tors A
. We have
(\mathcal{U}\cap\mods A/I)^{\perp_{A/I}}=\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mods A/I
.

Proof.

To show the inclusion
\supseteq
, take
X\in\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mods A/I
. Assume for a contradiction, there exists
Y\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mods A/I
such that
\Hom_{A/I}(Y,X)\neq 0
. Since
Y\in\mathcal{U}
it follows immediately that
X\not\in\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}
, a contradiction.

To show
\subseteq
, take
X\in(\mathcal{U}\cap\mods A/I)^{\perp_{A/I}}\subseteq\mods A/I
. We need to show that
X\in\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}
. Assume for a contradiction that there exists
0\neq f\in\Hom_{A}(Y,X)
with
Y\in\mathcal{U}
. By the assumption on
X
, it follows that
Y\not\in\mods A/I
. However,
\image f
is a submodule of
X
, and thus lies in
\mods A/I
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{U}
is a torsion class and thus closed under quotients, we have
\image f\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mods A/I
. Hence there should not be a non-zero morphism
\image f\to X
. Since
f\neq 0
implies
\image f\neq 0
, this is a contradiction and we have shown equality.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]\subseteq\tors A
. We have
\overline{\mathcal{U}}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{T}\cap\mods A/I
.

Proof.

Clearly
\overline{\mathcal{U}}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{T}}=(\mathcal{U}\cap\mods A/I)^{\perp_{A}}\cap(\mathcal{T}\cap\mods A/I)=\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{T}\cap\mods A/I
by
Lemma

5.2
.
∎

The following is a simple observation but since wide subcategories play a central role in this paper we include it for convenience.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a wide subcategory of
\mods A
, then
\mathcal{W}\cap\mods A/I
is a wide subcategory of
\mods A/I
.

Proof.

Let
L,M,N\in\mods A/I
lie in a short exact sequence
0\to L\to M\to N\to 0
with
L,N\in\mathcal{W}\cap\mods A/I\subseteq\mods A
. Since
L,N\in\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mods A
, we get
M\in\mathcal{W}
since
\mathcal{W}
is wide. Hence
M\in\mathcal{W}\cap\mods A/I
as required. Similarly, let
f:M\to N
be a morphism with
M,N\in\mathcal{W}\cap\mods A/I
, then since
M,N\in\mathcal{W}
, we have
\ker f,\coker f\in\mathcal{W}
. Moreover
\ker f\in\mods A/I
and
\coker f\in\mods A/I
since
\mods A/I
is a full abelian subcategory of
\mods A
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
be a wide interval of
\tors A
, then
\overline{[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}
is a wide interval of
\tors A/I
.

Proof.

By assumption
\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{T}
is a wide subcategory of
\mods A
, then by
Corollary

5.3
we have
\overline{\mathcal{U}}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{T}\cap\mods A/I
which is a wide subcategory of
\mods A/I
by
Lemma

5.4
.
∎

The following is the starting point for connecting
\mathfrak{T}(A)
and
\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
.

Proposition 5.6
.

If
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
is a
\tau
-perpendicular interval of
\tors A
, then
\overline{[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}
is a
\tau
-perpendicular interval of
\tors A/I
.

Proof.

Let
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
be a
\tau
-perpendicular interval, in particular, it is wide. By
Corollary

5.5
we have that
\overline{[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}
is a wide interval of
\tors A/I
. By
[
DIR+23
, Thm. 1.3]
and
Example

2.4
the lattice
\tors A
is a complete lattice and by
Theorem

2.8
the lattice congruence
\Phi_{I}
is a complete lattice congruence. By
Proposition

2.9
, we have
\pi_{\downarrow}^{\Phi_{I}}(\tors A)\cong\tors/\Phi_{I}\cong\tors A/I
. By the same result, congruence classes of
\Phi_{I}
are intervals and in this case
[
Rea16
, 9.47, Prop. 9-5.8]
implies that
\pi_{\downarrow}^{\Phi_{I}}(\tors A)
is a join-subsemilattice of
\tors A
.

By
[
Rea16
, Rmk. 9-5.9]
this means that
\pi_{\downarrow}^{\Phi_{I}}(x\lor y)=\pi_{\downarrow}^{\Phi_{I}}x\lor\pi_{\downarrow}^{\Phi_{I}}y
holds. Since
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
is
\tau
-perpendicular, by
Proposition

4.3
there are
s
arrows
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\to\mathcal{U}\}_{i=1}^{s}
in
\Hasse([\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}])
such that
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{U}_{1}\lor\dots\lor\mathcal{U}_{s}
. From the join-subsemilattice property, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{\downarrow}^{\Phi_{I}}(\mathcal{T})=\pi_{\downarrow}^{\Phi_{I}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\lor\dots\lor\mathcal{U}_{s})=\pi_{\downarrow}^{\Phi_{I}}\mathcal{U}_{1}\lor\dots\lor\pi_{\downarrow}^{\Phi_{I}}\mathcal{U}_{r}\cong\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{1}\lor\dots\lor\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{r}.

Since
\overline{\mathcal{U}}\in\ftors A/I
by
[
DIR+23
, Prop. 5.6b]
,
Proposition

4.3
implies that the interval
[\overline{\mathcal{U}},\overline{\mathcal{T}}]
is
\tau
-perpendicular as required.
∎

This implies that we may extend the lattice isomorphisms between the
\tau
-perpendicular intervals of
Theorem

3.1
to their quotients in a natural way.

Proposition 5.7
.

We have the following commutative diagram extending
Theorem

3.1
.

\mods A/I

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{W}\coloneqq\mathcal{W}_{(M_{1},P_{1})}=\mathcal{W}_{(M_{2},P_{2})}
. By

We are now able to establish a functor between the categories.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let

. There exists a functor

, that is,

Proof.

F_{I}
maps
\tau
-perpendicular intervals to
\tau
-perpendicular intervals by

5.6
which also implies that

is well-defined on objects since

\displaystyle[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]\sim[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}_{1}=\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}_{2}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}_{1}\cap\mods A/I=\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}_{2}\cap\mods A/I

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\overline{[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]}\sim\overline{[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]}

5.3
. It is clear that containment of intervals is preserved by

\displaystyle=[f_{\overline{[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}][\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}]}}]\circ[f_{\overline{[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}][\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}}]

We are interested in understanding the categorical properties of the functor

.

Lemma 5.9
.

The functor
F_{I}
is faithful if and only if the restriction
\overline{(-)}:\ftors A\to\ftors A/I
is injective.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
. Let
[f_{[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}][\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]}],[f_{[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}][\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]}]\in\Hom_{\mathfrak{T}(A)}([\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]_{\sim},[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]_{\sim})
be distinct morphisms, which is to say that either
\mathcal{U}_{1}\neq\mathcal{U}_{2}
or
\mathcal{T}_{1}\neq\mathcal{T}_{2}
. Then applying
F_{I}
gives

[f_{\overline{[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]},\overline{[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]}}]
and
[f_{\overline{[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]},\overline{[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]}}]
in
\Hom_{\mathfrak{T}(A/I)}({\overline{[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]}_{\sim},\overline{[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}}_{\sim})
,
which coincide if and only if
\overline{[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]}=\overline{[\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]}
. So both
\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{1}=\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{2}
,
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{1}=\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{2}
. which would imply that
\overline{(-)}:\ftors A\to\ftors A/I
is not injective. Thus
F_{I}
is injective on
\Hom
-sets, hence faithful.

(\Rightarrow)
. Let
F_{I}
be faithful and assume for a contradiction that
\overline{(-)}:\ftors A\to\ftors A/I
is not an injection.
There exists a distinct morphism
[f_{[0,\mods A][\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}]}]:[0,\mods A]_{\sim}\to[\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}]_{\sim}
for every functorially-finite torsion class
\mathcal{T}\in\ftors A
, see also
Remark

3.8
. If
\overline{(-)}
is not injective, then
\mathcal{T}_{1}\cap\mods A/I=\mathcal{T}_{2}\cap\mods A/I
for two distinct
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\in\ftors A
. However, then the distinct morphisms

[f_{[0,\mods A][\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]}]\neq[f_{[0,\mods A][\mathcal{T}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2}]}]

of
\mathfrak{T}(A)
have the same image under

and hence
F_{I}
would not be faithful.
∎

Lemma 5.14

Let

\tors A\cong\tors A_{1}\times\tors A_{2},\quad\text{and}\quad\ftors A\cong\ftors A_{1}\times\ftors A_{2}.

Proof.

It is well-known that we have a
\mods A\cong\mods A_{1}\times\mods A_{2}
. This implies that the inverse bijections are given by

\displaystyle\tors A

\displaystyle\cong\tors A_{1}\times\tors A_{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle\mapsto(\mathcal{T}\cap\mods A_{1},\mathcal{T}\cap\mods A_{2})

\displaystyle\add(\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup\mathcal{T}_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsfrom(\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}).

If
\mathcal{T}\in A
is a torsion class, then it is clear that
\mathcal{T}\cap\mods A_{i}\in\tors A_{i}
for
i=1,2
since both terms of the intersection are closed under extensions and quotients. In particular, if
\mathcal{T}
is functorially-finite then

is functorially-finite, for

[

implies closure under extensions. Because

it follows that
\add(\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup\mathcal{T}_{2})
is functorially-finite, since any module

admits left and right

-approximations, which are simply given by a

-approximation of the direct summand of

which is in
\mods A_{1}
, and a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
-approximation of the direct summand of
M
which is in
\mods A_{2}
. One sees directly that these are inverse assignments and order-preserving.

Lemma 5.15

Let

Denote the lattice isomorphism by

-module and

Applying

We can now describe when the functor
F_{I}:\mathfrak{T}(A)\to\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
is full. To avoid further technicalities involving isomorphisms between infinite lattices of functorially-finite torsion classes like in

4.2
(3), we restrict ourselves to the finite case for the following result.

Proposition 5.16
.

Let
A
be
\tau
-tilting finite. The functor
F_{I}
is full if and only if
\tors A\cong\tors A/I\times\tors B
for some finite-dimensional algebra
B
.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
. It follows from (the proof of)
Lemma

5.15
that we can write
A\cong C\times B
where
C
is a quotient algebra of
A
by an ideal generated by the primitive orthogonal idempotent such that
\tors C\cong\tors A/I
. By
Theorem

4.7
we have an equivalence
G:\mathfrak{T}(C)\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
. Moreover, we have a sequence of surjective algebra morphisms
A\twoheadrightarrow C\twoheadrightarrow A/I
and hence the lattice congruences on
\tors A
induced by
A\twoheadrightarrow C
and
A\twoheadrightarrow A/I
coincide. Furthermore, the functor
F_{I}
factors through the equivalence, i.e.
F_{I}=G\circ F_{C}
, where
F_{C}:\mathfrak{T}(A)\to\mathfrak{T}(C)
is induced by the surjection
A\twoheadrightarrow C
. Since the composition of full functors is full, it is sufficient to show that
F_{C}
is full.

Let
[\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}]_{\sim},[\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}]_{\sim}\in\mathfrak{T}(A)
be such that there is a pair of representatives
[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]\in[\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}]_{\sim}
and
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]\in[\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}]_{\sim}
such that
[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]\supseteq[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
. We need to show that the induced map

\Hom_{\mathfrak{T}(A)}([\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]_{\sim},[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]_{\sim})\to\Hom_{\mathfrak{T}(C)}([\mathcal{V}\cap\mods C,\mathcal{S}\cap\mods C]_{\sim},[\mathcal{U}\cap\mods C,\mathcal{T}\cap\mods C]_{\sim})

is surjective. Thus take an arbitrary morphism
f_{[\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1}][\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]}\in\mathfrak{T}(C)
in the codomain, where
[\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1}]\sim[\mathcal{V}\cap\mods C,\mathcal{S}\cap\mods C]
and
[\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]\sim[\mathcal{U}\cap\mods C,\mathcal{T}\cap\mods C]
. Define

[\widehat{\mathcal{U}},\widehat{\mathcal{T}}]\coloneqq[\add(\mathcal{U}_{1}\cup(\mathcal{U}\cap\mods B)),\add(\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup(\mathcal{T}\cap\mods B))],

which is an interval of
\tors A
because both terms are additive closures of a torsion classes in
\mods B
and a torsion class in
\mods C
. Since
\mods A\cong\mods B\times\mods C

Lemma

5.14
.Then, the simple objects of the wide subcategories satisfy

\displaystyle\simp(\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle=(\simp(\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{T})\cap\mods C)\cup(\simp(\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{T})\cap\mods B)

\displaystyle=\simp(\mathcal{U}_{1}^{\perp_{C}}\cap\mathcal{T}_{1})\cup(\simp(\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{T}\cap\mods B))

\displaystyle=\simp(\widehat{\mathcal{U}}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{T}}),

where the second equality follows from
Corollary

5.3
. By
[
DIR+23
, Thm. 4.16(a)]
this

\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{T}=\widehat{\mathcal{U}}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{T}}
and thus
[\widehat{\mathcal{U}},\widehat{\mathcal{T}}]_{\sim}=[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]_{\sim}\in\mathfrak{T}(A)
. Similarly we define

[\widehat{\mathcal{V}},\widehat{\mathcal{S}}]\coloneqq[\add(\mathcal{V}_{1}\cup(\mathcal{V}\cap\mods B)),\add(\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup(\mathcal{S}\cap\mods B))]

and obtain

. Then, under

, the morphism
[f_{[\widehat{\mathcal{V}},\widehat{\mathcal{S}}][\widehat{\mathcal{U}},\widehat{\mathcal{T}}]}]\in\Hom_{\mathfrak{T}(A)}([\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]_{\sim},[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]_{\sim})
gets mapped to
[f_{[\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1}][\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}]}]\in\mathfrak{T}(C)
as required. Therefore the induced map between
\Hom
-sets is surjective. Hence
F_{C}
is full, and so is
F_{I}=G\circ F_{C}
.

(\Rightarrow)
. Assume

is full. If
\tors A\cong\tors A/I

\mathcal{T}_{1}\xleftarrow{B}\mathcal{T}_{2}\subseteq\Hasse(\tors A)
be an arrow contracted by the lattice congruence. In particular since

is full, the following induced map of
\Hom
-sets is surjective:

\Hom_{\mathfrak{T}(A)}([0,\mods A]_{\sim},[\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}]_{\sim})\to\Hom_{\mathfrak{T}(A/I)}([0,\mods A/I]_{\sim},[0,0]_{\sim}).

\mathcal{X}\in\tors A/I
be any torsion class, then the corresponding morphism
[f_{[0,\mods A/I][\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}]}]\in\mathfrak{T}(A/I)

\mathcal{T}_{3}\xleftarrow{B}\mathcal{T}_{4}\in\Hasse([\pi_{\downarrow}\mathcal{X},\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{X}])

, we deduce that

. Let

\Phi_{I}^{-1}(0)\subseteq\tors A
, the other lies in the congruence class
\Phi_{I}^{-1}(\Filt_{A/I}\{S_{2}\})\subseteq\tors A
. The two halves are connected by the two arrows labelled by
S_{2}
.

Let
0\to S_{2}\to M\to S_{1}\to 0
be a non-split short exact sequence in
\mods A
, so in particular
M\not\in S_{2}^{\perp_{A}}
. Now,
M
is a brick by
[
DIR+23
, Lem. 4.26]
and arises as a label of an arrow in the polygon
[0,\Filt_{A}\{S_{1},S_{2}\}]\subseteq\tors A
by
[
DIR+23
, Thm. 4.21(b)]
. More precisely
M
is a label in the half of the polygon lying in
\Phi_{I}^{-1}(0)=[0,\pi_{\uparrow}0]
since
M\not\in S_{2}^{\perp_{A}}
. However, from
Eq.

5.2
it follows that
M
must arise as a label of some arrow in
\Phi_{I}^{-1}(\Filt_{A/I}\{S_{2}\})=[\Filt_{A}\{S_{2}\},\pi_{\uparrow}\Filt_{A/I}\{S_{2}\}]
, which is a contradiction, since this requries
M\in\Filt_{A}\{S_{2}\}^{\perp_{A}}=S_{2}^{\perp_{A}}
by definition of the brick-labelling. Thus
\Ext_{A}^{1}(S_{1},S_{2})=0
.

Let
0\to S_{1}\to M\to S_{2}\to 0
be a non-split short exact sequence in
\mods A
. Like in the previous paragraph, it follows that
M
arises as a label of an arrow in the half of the polygon
[0,\Filt_{A}\{S_{1},S_{2}\}]\subseteq\tors A
, which is contained in
\Phi_{I}^{-1}(\Filt_{A/I}\{S_{2}\})
. However, by
Eq.

5.2
it must also arise in
\Phi_{I}^{-1}(0)
. Since
M\not\in S_{1}^{\perp_{A}}
,
M
cannot label an arrow above
\Filt_{A}\{S_{1}\}
in
\Hasse(\tors A)
. Consequently, there must exist another simple module
S_{3}\in\mods A
such that
M\in\brick[\Filt_{A}\{S_{3}\},\pi_{\uparrow}0]
. As
\Filt_{A}\{S_{3}\}\in\Phi_{I}^{-1}(0)
it follows that
S_{3}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
. In other words, there exists an arrow
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\xleftarrow{M}\mathcal{T}\subseteq\Hasse([\Filt_{A}\{S_{3}\},\pi_{\uparrow}0])
labelled by
M
. Because
M\in\mathcal{T}
it follows that
S_{2}\in\Fac M\subseteq\mathcal{T}
, and moreover that
S_{2}\not\in(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})^{\perp_{A}}
, as otherwise there would be two bricks
M
and
S_{2}
in the intersection
(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{T}
, which is a contradiction by
[
DIR+23
, Thm. 3.3(b)]
. Any morphism to the simple module
S_{2}
is an epimorphism, and since torsion classes are closed under quotients, it follows that
S_{2}\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. As a consequence the join
\Filt_{A}\{S_{2}\}\lor\Filt_{A}\{S_{3}\}=\Filt_{A}\{S_{2},S_{3}\}
is contained in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. In particular, there exists an arrow
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}\xleftarrow{S_{2}}\Filt_{A}\{S_{2},S_{3}\}
in
\Hasse(\tors A)
for some
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}
containing
\Filt_{A}\{S_{3}\}
. Combining these observations, it follows that

0\subseteq\Filt_{A}\{S_{3}\}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\Filt_{A}\{S_{2},S_{3}\}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\subseteq\pi_{\uparrow}0.

In conclusion, the interval
[0,\pi_{\uparrow}0]
contains arrows labelled by
S_{2}
which are not contracted by
\Phi_{I}
. Hence the equivalence class
[0]
of
\Phi_{I}
is not an interval, a contradiction to
Proposition

2.9
. Therefore
\Ext_{A}^{1}(S_{2},S_{1})=0
.

In conclusion, we obtain
A\cong A/\langle\epsilon_{1}\rangle\times A/\langle\epsilon_{2}\rangle
, and clearly
\tors A/\langle\epsilon_{2}\rangle\cong\tors A/I
as required.
∎

\tau

2.9

\tau

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
I\in\ideal A
. For every
\tau
-perpendicular interval
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]\subseteq\tors A/I

\tau
-perpendicular interval
[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}]\subseteq[\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U},\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{T}]\subseteq\tors A

\overline{[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}]}=[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}=\iota(\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\mathcal{T})
, where
\iota

5.1
. Moreover, it is given by

[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}]=[\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U},\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U}\lor\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{W})],

where
\mathcal{W}=\iota(\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\mathcal{T})
.

Proof.

Let
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
be a
\tau
-perpendicular interval of
\tors A/I
whose corresponding wide subcategory is given by
\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\mathcal{T}=\Filt_{A/I}\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}\in\wide A/I
for some
1\leq k\leq|A|
. By
Theorem

5.11
, it is generated by some semibrick
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}\in\sbrick A
. This implies that

(6.1)

\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\mathcal{T}=(\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U})^{\perp_{A}}\cap\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{T}\cap\mods A/I\subseteq(\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U})^{\perp_{A}}\cap\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{T}

by using
Corollary

5.3
to obtain the equality. Since
(\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U})^{\perp_{A}}
and
\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{T}
are a torsion-free and a torsion class of
\mods A
, respectively, they are closed under extensions. As a consequence the lifted
\tau
-perpendicular subcategory
\mathcal{W}=\Filt_{A}\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}\in\wide A
satisfies
\mathcal{W}\subseteq(\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U})^{\perp_{A}}\cap\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{T}
, as
\mathcal{W}=\Filt_{A}\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}
consists of iterated extensions of modules contained in
(\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U})^{\perp_{A}}\cap\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{T}
.

By the dual of
[
Rea16
, Prop. 9-5.10]
and
Proposition

2.9
, the intersection with
\mods A/I
induces a bijection from the elements
\mathcal{V}\in\tors A
covering
\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U}
in
\tors A
to the elements
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
covering
\mathcal{U}
in
\tors A/I
. Thus, using
Proposition

3.2
and
Theorem

5.1
, there is a brick-label preserving bijection between arrows

(6.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U}\lor\mathrm{T}_{A}(S_{i})\xrightarrow{S_{i}}\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U}\text{ in }\Hasse([\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U},\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{T}])\subseteq\Hasse(\tors A)\right\}_{i=1}^{k}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\left\{\mathcal{U}\lor\mathrm{T}_{A/I}(S_{i})\xrightarrow{S_{i}}\mathcal{U}\text{ in }\Hasse([\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}])\subseteq\Hasse(\tors A/I)\right\}_{i=1}^{k},

which are labelled by the bricks
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}
generating
\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{T}
by
[
DIR+23
, Thm. 4.16]
.

It is easy to see that
\mathrm{T}_{A}(\mathcal{W})=\mathrm{T}_{A}(S_{1})\lor\dots\lor\mathrm{T}_{A}(S_{k})
, so that
Proposition

4.3
gives the following
\tau
-perpendicular interval by taking the join of all atoms:

[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}]\coloneqq[\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U},\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U}\lor\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{W})]\subseteq\tors A.

By
[
DIR+23
, Thm. 4.16]
this intervals satisfies
\mathcal{A}^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{W}
. Since both
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{T}
and
\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U}\subseteq\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{T}
, the interval is such that
\overline{[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}]}\subseteq[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]\subseteq\tors A/I
. By
Corollary

5.3
we have

\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{T}}=\Filt_{A}\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}\cap\mods A/I=\Filt_{A/I}\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}.

Hence
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\mathcal{T}
and by
Theorem

3.1
there is a lattice isomorphism between
\overline{[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}]}
and
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
. Since
\overline{[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}]}\subseteq[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
it follows that
\overline{[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}}]}=[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
.
∎

Using

, define a map of
\tau

(6.3)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{i}:\tauint(\tors A/I)

\displaystyle\to\tauint(\tors A)

\displaystyle[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]=[\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U},\pi_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U}\lor\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{W})].

Example 6.2
.

The map of
\tau
-perpendicular intervals
\mathfrak{i}:\tauint(\tors A/I)\to\tauint(A)
of

6.3
is not inclusion-preserving. For example, take the surjective algebra morphism

. Then the inclusion
[\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix}),\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix})]\subseteq[0,\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix})]
of
\tau
-perpendicular intervals of
\tors K^{2}
maps to
[\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
2\end{smallmatrix}),\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
2\end{smallmatrix})]\not\subseteq[0,\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix})]
.

Nonetheless, there is a way of resolving this problem by restricting the lattice congruence to the desired interval.

Proposition 6.3
.

For every inclusion of
\tau
-perpendicular intervals
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]\subseteq[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]
in
\tors A/I
, there exists a
\tau
-perpendicular interval
\overline{\mathfrak{i}}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]\subseteq\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]
such that
(\overline{\mathfrak{i}}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}])\cap\mods A/I=[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
and its corresponding wide subcategory is
\iota(\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\mathcal{T})
. The intervals are such that

F_{I}([f_{(\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}])(\overline{\mathfrak{i}}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}])}])=[f_{[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}][\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}],

consequently every morphism of
\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
lies in the essential image of
F_{I}:\mathfrak{T}(A)\to\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}\in\tors A/I
be such that
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]\subseteq[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]
and both are
\tau
-perpendicular intervals of
\tors A/I
. By definition of
\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
every non-zero morphism in
\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
is of the form
[f_{[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}][\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}]\in\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
. Consider the representative
f_{[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}][\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}
of its equivalence class. Using the map
\mathfrak{i}
of
Eq.

6.3
we obtain a
\tau
-perpendicular interval
\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]
of
\tors A
such that
F_{I}((\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}])_{\sim})=[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]_{\sim}
. However, the
\tau
-perpendicular interval
\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
may not be contained in
\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]
, see
Example

6.2
.

Nonetheless, we may consider the restriction of the lattice congruence
\Phi\coloneqq\Phi_{I}
to the interval lattice
\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]
, which we denote by
\overline{\Phi}|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}}
. By the dual of
[
Rea16
, Lem. 9-5.7]
, the interval
\overline{[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]}=[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]
of
\tors A/I
, which contains
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
, is isomorphic to the quotient lattice
\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]/(\Phi|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}})
. Let
\mathcal{W}\coloneqq\iota(\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\mathcal{T})
and let
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}
be the relative simple modules of
\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\mathcal{T}
, so that
\mathcal{W}=\Filt_{A}\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}
by
Theorem

5.11
.

Restricting
Proposition

2.9
to
[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]
, we find that the preimage of
\mathcal{U}
under
\overline{\Phi}|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}}
is an interval of
[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]
which we denote by
[(\overline{\pi}|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}})_{\downarrow}(\mathcal{U}),(\overline{\pi}|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}})_{\uparrow}(\mathcal{U})]\subseteq\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}]
. Let
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}=(\overline{\pi}|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}})_{\uparrow}(\mathcal{U})\in\tors A
. Similar to the proof of
Lemma

6.1
, we apply the dual of
[
Rea16
, Prop. 9-5.10]
and
Proposition

3.2
to obtain a brick-label preserving bijection

(6.4)

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\lor\mathrm{T}_{A}(S_{i})\xrightarrow{S_{i}}\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\text{ in }\Hasse([(\overline{\pi}|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}})_{\uparrow}\mathcal{U},(\overline{\pi}|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}})_{\uparrow}\mathcal{T}])\subseteq\Hasse(\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}])\right\}_{i=1}^{k}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\left\{\mathcal{U}\lor\mathrm{T}_{A/I}(S_{i})\xrightarrow{S_{i}}\mathcal{U}\text{ in }\Hasse([\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}])\subseteq\Hasse([\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}])\right\}_{i=1}^{k},

which are labelled by the bricks
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}
generating
\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\mathcal{T}
by
[
DIR+23
, Thm. 4.16]
. Since
\mathfrak{T}(\mathcal{W}_{1})=\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{T}(S_{i})
it follows from
Proposition

4.3
that

(6.5)

\overline{\mathfrak{i}}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]\coloneqq[(\overline{\pi}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}})_{\uparrow}(\mathcal{U}),(\overline{\pi}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}})_{\uparrow}(\mathcal{U})\lor\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{W})]

is a
\tau
-perpendicular interval of
\tors A
such that the corresponding wide subcategory is
\mathcal{W}
by
[
DIR+23
, Thm. 4.16]
. Write
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(\overline{\phi}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}})_{\uparrow}\mathcal{T}
. Because
(\overline{\Phi}|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}})^{-1}(\mathcal{U})\subseteq\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{U})
and
(\overline{\Phi}|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}})^{-1}(\mathcal{T})\subseteq\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{T})

\overline{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{U}

\overline{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{T}

Corollary

5.3
that therefore
(\overline{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}})^{\perp_{A}}\cap\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\cap\mods A/I=\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{A/I}}\cap\mathcal{T}

6.1
in the proof of

6.1

(\overline{\mathfrak{i}}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}])\cap\mods A/I\subseteq[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]

Again, analogous to the proof of

6.1

[f_{(\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}])(\overline{\mathfrak{i}}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}])}].

∎

Recall that an epimorphism
e

\mathcal{C}

Corollary 6.4
.

The smallest subcategory of
\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
containing the image of

is
\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
itself. Thus
F_{I}
is an extremal epimorphism in the category
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}
.

Proof.

It follows immediately from
Proposition

6.3
that the image of

is
\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
itself. The functor
F_{I}
of

5.8
is an extremal epimorphism in
\mathcal{C}
at by
[
BBP99
, Thm. 3.4]
.

Moreover, we say that a functor
G:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}

reflects composition
if given two morphisms
f
and
g
in
\mathcal{A}
such that
G(f)\circ G(g)
is defined in
\mathcal{B}
, there exist morphisms
f^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{A}
such that
F(f)=G(f^{\prime})
,
G(g)=G(g^{\prime})
and
f^{\prime}\circ g^{\prime}
is defined in
\mathcal{B}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

The functor
F_{I}:\mathfrak{T}(A)\to\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
reflects composition. Moreover, if

, where the maps of
\tau
-perpendicular intervals are as in

6.3
and

6.5
.

By

3.6
we may assume

[f_{[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}][\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}]\mapsto[f_{(\mathfrak{i}[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{S}])(\overline{\mathfrak{i}}_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{S}}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}])}]

is not well-defined on composition. Take for example the morphism
[f_{[0,\mods A][\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix}),\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix})]}]
of
\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
which gets mapped to
[f_{[0,\mods A][\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
2\end{smallmatrix}),\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
2\end{smallmatrix})]}]\in\mathfrak{T}(A)
under the assignment. However, the morphism may be decomposed as

\displaystyle[f_{[0,\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix})][\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix}),\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix})]}]\circ[f_{[0,\mods A][0,\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix})]}]

\displaystyle=[f_{[0,\mods A][\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix}),\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix})]}]

\displaystyle=[f_{[\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix}),\mods A][\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix}),\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix})]}]\circ[f_{[0,\mods A][\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix}),\mods A]}]

However, the composition of the images of the left-hand side is
[f_{[0,\mods A][\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix}),\Fac(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix})]}]
, which is different to the composition of the images of the left-hand side

.

7.
Classifying spaces and picture groups

The original motivation for the (
\tau
-)cluster morphism category comes from the study of cohomology groups of the
picture group

[
ITW16
]
. This group is the fundamental group of an associated topological space, called the
picture space
which is simply the classifying space
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{T}(A)
of the (
\tau
)-cluster morphism category
\mathfrak{T}(A)
, see
[
HI21b
,
IT17
]
. Therefore, if
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{T}(A)
is a
K(\pi,1)
space, that is, a space whose only non-trivial homotopy group is in degree 1, then we may relate the cohomology groups of the picture group with those of the space.

The
classifying space

\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}
of a topological category
\mathcal{C}
was first explicitly studied in
[
Seg68
]
, where it is defined as the geometric realisation of the nerve of the category. The nerve of the category is a simplicial set whose 0-simplices are identity morphisms and whose
k
-simplices are chains of
k
composable non-identity morphisms. It was shown in
[
IT17
]
and
[
HI21b
]
that for the (
\tau
)-cluster morphism category
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{T}(A)
is actually a CW-complex with a particularly nice structure. As a consequence we are able to obtain the following.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
A
be
\tau
-tilting finite and
I\in\ideal A
, then the classifying space
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
is a quotient space of
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{T}(A)
and the quotient map is induced by
F_{I}
from
Theorem

5.8
.

Proof.

The
\tau
-cluster morphism category
\mathfrak{T}(A)
is a cubical category
[
HI21b
, Thm. 2.14]
, see also
[
Kai25
, Thm. 3.16]
. This means that every morphism can be seen as (the diagonal of) a cube, whose edges correspond to factorisations of the morphism into irreducible ones. It is shown in
[
HI21b
, Prop. 4.7]
that the classifying space
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{T}(A)
of the
\tau
-cluster morphism category is a CW-complex with one
k
-cell
e([\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]_{\sim})
for each equivalence class of wide intervals, where
k
is the number of isomorphism classes of (relative) simple modules of
\mathcal{U}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}
. This cell is the union of factorisation cubes of morphisms
[f_{[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}][\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}]}]
for
\mathcal{X}\in[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
. Given an ideal
I\in\ideal A
, define an equivalence
\asymp
relation on factorisation cubes by setting

[f_{[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}][\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}]]}]\asymp[f_{[\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}][\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{X}^{\prime}]}]

whenever
F_{I}
applied to these morphisms coincides. Since factorisation cubes are simply geometric realisations of morphisms, this identification coincides with the generalised congruence
\asymp_{I}
, as defined in
[
BBP99
, Sec. 3]
, on
\mathfrak{T}(A)
induced by the functor
F_{I}
. By
[
BBP99
, Cor. 3.11]
there is a monomorphism
(\mathfrak{T}(A)/\asymp)\to\mathfrak{T}(A/I))
in
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{at}
from the quotient category
\mathfrak{T}(A)/\asymp
as defined in
[
BBP99
, Sec. 3.9]
. Since
F
is an extremal epimorphism by
Corollary

6.4
, it follows that
(\mathfrak{T}(A)/\asymp)\cong\mathfrak{T}(A/I)
. Clearly,
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{T}(A)/\asymp)
is a quotient space of
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{T}(A)
and the result follows.
∎

The standard approach taken in
[
IT17
,
IT22
,
HI21a
,
HI21b
,
Kai25
]
to show that
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{T}(A)
is a
K(\pi,1)
space is to use three sufficient conditions translated from cube complexes
[
Gro87
]
to cubical categories
[
Igu14
]
. The first condition is the existence of faithful functor to a groupoid with one object (a so-called
faithful group functor
). While the existence of such a faithful group functor is conjectured to exist for an arbitrary algebra, see
[
HI21b
, Conj. 5.10]
, its existence is currently known only for the following three classes of algebras:

•

Hereditary algebras of finite or tame type
[
IT22
, Thm. 3.7]
;

•

K
-stone algebras,
[
HI21a
, Thm. 5.9]
;

•

Algebras whose
g
-vector fan is a finite hyperplane arrangement
[
Kai25
, Thm. 6.20]
.

As an immediate consequence of
Corollary

4.8
we can extend these faithful functors to
\tau
-tilting finite algebras
A
having isomorphic lattice of torsion classes to one of the above families.

In general, the picture group captures the structure of the lattice of torsion-classes and thus of maximal green sequences. For hereditary algebras, it was shown in
[
IT21
]
that positive expressions for a certain (Coxeter) group element are in bijection with maximal green sequences. The definition of the picture group in
[
HI21b
, Prop. 4.4]
is readily adapted to the general case as follows.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional algebra. The
picture group

G(A)
is defined by having generators

\{X_{S}:S\in\brick A\text{ and }\Filt_{A}\{S\}\text{ is $\tau$-perpendicular}\}\cup\{g_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{T}\in\ftors A\}

with a relation
g_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}=X_{S}g_{\mathcal{T}_{2}}
whenever there is an arrow
\mathcal{T}_{1}\xrightarrow{S}\mathcal{T}_{2}
in
\Hasse(\ftors A)
and the relation
g_{0}=e
.

We conclude this section with a group-theoretic analogue of
Proposition

7.1

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
A
be
\tau
-tilting finite and
I\in\ideal A
. Then there is a surjective group homomorphism
G(A)\to G(A/I)
induced by
\overline{(-)}:\tors A\to\tors A/I
.

Proof.

Define a map
\phi:G(A)\to G(A/I)
given by

X_{S}\mapsto\begin{cases}X_{S}&\text{ if }S\in\brick A/I,\\
e&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad g_{\mathcal{T}}\mapsto g_{\overline{\mathcal{T}}}.

Since
\mods A/I
is a full subcategory of
\mods A
, we identify
\brick A/I
with
\{S\in\brick A:IS=0\}\subseteq\brick A
, similar to
[
DIR+23
, Sec. 5.2]
. Therefore
\phi
induces a surjection on the generators
X_{S}\in G(A/I)
. Similarly by
Theorem

2.8
the map
\overline{(-)}:\tors A\to\tors A/I
is surjective and hence
\phi
is surjective on the generators
g_{\overline{\mathcal{T}}}
. To show that the group relations
g_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}=X_{S}g_{\mathcal{T}_{2}}
are preserved we distinguish between two cases: If
S\in\brick A/I
, the corresponding arrow of
\Hasse(\tors A)
is not contracted, by
Theorem

5.1
, and the group relation becomes

\phi(g_{\mathcal{T}_{1}})=g_{\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{1}}=X_{S}g_{\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{2}}=\phi(X_{S})\phi(g_{\mathcal{T}_{2}}).

And if
S\not\in\brick A/I
, then
\phi(X_{S})=e
and the corresponding arrow of
\Hasse(\tors A)
is contracted, so
\overline{\mathcal{T}_{1}}=\overline{\mathcal{T}_{2}}
and the group relation becomes

\phi(g_{\mathcal{T}_{1}})=g_{\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{1}}=g_{\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{2}}=eg_{\overline{\mathcal{T}_{2}}}=\phi(X_{S})\phi(g_{\mathcal{T}_{2}}).

Hence
\phi
is a well-defined group homomorphism and surjective.
∎

\tau
-perpendicular interval of
\tors A

[\Fac_{B}(\begin{smallmatrix}1\end{smallmatrix}),\Fac_{B}(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\

\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
2\end{smallmatrix}
is support
\tau
-tilting in
\mods B
but not in
\mods A
, where it is a regular module.

It was shown in
[
IT22
, Thm. 2.5]
that
\mathfrak{T}(A)
satisfies the pairwise compatibility property since
A
is hereditary of finite type. The quotient
A/\langle\gamma_{4}\gamma_{2}\rangle
is gentle with no loops and 2-cycles, thus
[
HI21a
, Thm. 4.1]
implies that
A/I
does not have the pairwise compatibility condition since there exists a vertex of valency greater than 2. Hence taking quotients does not preserve the pairwise compatibility condition. Moreover,
A
is a quotient of the preprojective algebra
\Pi_{D_{4}}
of type
D_{4}
which was shown in
[
BH22b
, Thm. 4]
to not have the pairwise compatibility property, so taking quotient also does not preserve the failure of the pairwise compatibility property. In total we have a sequence of algebra epimorphisms:

\Pi_{D_{4}}\twoheadrightarrow D_{4}\twoheadrightarrow D_{4}/\langle\gamma_{4}\gamma_{2}\rangle\twoheadrightarrow K^{4}

where
K^{4}
is the semisimple algebra on 4 vertices, which does satisfy the pairwise compatibility property.

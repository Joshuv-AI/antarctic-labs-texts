On Products of Δ \Delta -sets
Rodrigo Carvalho, Vinicius Rodrigues

Abstract
. We prove the consistency of the existence of a Q Q -set whose square is not a Δ \Delta -set and that if there is a Δ \Delta -set, then there exists a Δ \Delta -set whose all finite powers are Δ \Delta -sets. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 54B10, 54D15, 03E35, 54H05. Keywords: Δ \Delta -sets, Q Q -sets, product spaces, special subsets of the reals.



1.
Introduction

\Delta
-sets and
Q
-sets are well-studied classes of special uncountable subsets of reals which have strong and interesting properties of category.

The history of
Q
-sets goes back to Hausdorff
[
5
]
, Sierpinski
[
15
]
and Rothberger
[
14
]
. A
Q
-set is a subspace of the real line - or some other Polish space - whose every subspace is a relative
G_{\delta}
(see e.g.
[
9
]
).
Q
-sets are related to the Normal Moore Space Problem for the separable case: there exists an uncountable non-metrizable Moore normal space if, and only if there exists a
Q
-set (see e.g.
[
6
]
). The existence of
Q
-sets is independent of ZFC - one readily sees that the existence of a
Q
-set implies
2^{\omega_{1}}=\mathfrak{c}
, and every uncountable subset of the reals of size less than
\mathfrak{p}
is a
Q
-set
[
1
]
.

The study of
\Delta
-sets started with G. M. Reed and E. van Douwen
[
13
, p. 176]
and is related to the study of the dividing line between the class of normal spaces and the class of countably paracompact spaces, thus, of Dowker spaces. A vanishing sequence is a countable decreasing sequence of sets with empty intersections. We say that an uncountable subset
X
of the reals- or some other Polish space -
is a
\Delta
-set if for every vanishing sequence
(F_{n}:n\in\omega)
of subsets of
X
, there exists a vanishing sequence of open sets (equivalently,
G_{\delta}
sets) of
X

(U_{n}:n\in\omega)
such that for every
n\in\omega
,
F_{n}\subseteq U_{n}
. We call this an open expansion of the vanishing sequence. T. Przymusinski has proved that there exists an uncountable non-metrizable Moore normal space if, and only if there exists a
\Delta
-set
[
11
]
.

Clearly, every
Q
-set is a
\Delta
-set. In
[
7
]
, it is claimed that it is consistent that there exists a
\Delta
-set that is not a
Q
-set. However, the proof is considered to be somewhat unclear and alternative proofs are looked for
[
8
]
. It is not known if the existence of
\Delta
-sets implies that
2^{\omega_{1}}=2^{\omega}
.

In this paper, we prove that two classical results regarding the productivity of
Q
-sets also hold for
\Delta
-sets. A natural question is whether the finite product of
Q
-sets or
\Delta
-sets is a
Q
-set or
\Delta
-set.

In
[
3
]
, Fleissner claimed to have proven the consistency of the existence of is a
Q
-set whose square is not a
Q
-set. However, Miller observed that Fleissner’s proof is flawed
[
10
]
, and J. Brendle subsequently fixed the proof
[
2
]
. In Section
2
, we modify Brendle’s argument to prove the consistency of the existence of a
Q
-set whose square is not a
\Delta
-set.

In
[
12
]
, T. Przymusinski proved that the existence of a
Q
-set implies the existence of a
Q
-set whose all finite powers are countably compact. Such a
Q
-set is called a “strong
Q
-set”. In Section
3
we prove that the existence of a
\Delta
-set implies the existence of a
strong

\Delta
-set, that is, of a
\Delta
-set whose all finite powers are
\Delta
-sets.

\bar{f}(\rho_{0},\rho_{1})=\begin{cases}f^{*}(\rho_{0},\rho_{1}),&\text{if }(\rho_{0},\rho_{1})\in\dom f^{*},\\
f(\rho_{0},\rho_{1}),&\text{if }(\rho_{0},\rho_{1})\in\dom f\setminus\dom f^{*}.\end{cases}

•

\dom\bar{p}=\dom p\cup\dom p^{*}
.

•

\bar{p}(\xi)=\begin{cases}\tilde{p}(\xi),&\text{if }\xi\neq\alpha,\\
\tilde{p}(\alpha^{\prime}),&\text{if }\xi=\alpha.\par\end{cases}

•

\dom\bar{q}=\dom q\cup\dom q^{*}
.

•

\bar{q}(\gamma)=\begin{cases}q(\gamma),&\text{if }\gamma\in\dom q\setminus\dom q^{*},\\
q^{*}(\gamma),&\text{if }\gamma\in\dom q^{*}\setminus\dom q,\\
q(\gamma)\cup q^{*}(\gamma)&\text{if }\gamma\in\dom q^{*}\cap\dom q.\end{cases}

Such a triple
\bar{t}
is well defined, but we must verify that
\bar{t}\in\mathbb{P}
and that
\bar{t}\leq t,t^{*}
.
Notice that as
\alpha\neq\beta
,
\bar{p}(\beta)=\tilde{p}(\beta)
and that
\bar{p}(\alpha)=\tilde{p}(\alpha^{\prime})
, as intended.

Thus, recursively, we show that for every
\gamma\leq\kappa^{+}
,
(\bar{f},\bar{p},\bar{q}|\gamma)\in\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}
and extends
(f,p,q|\gamma)
and
(f^{*},p^{*},q^{*}|\gamma)
. The limit step is trivial, so we show how to verify this for
\gamma=0
and for the successor step.

Step
\gamma=0
:
(\bar{f},\bar{p},\emptyset)
is clearly in
\mathbb{P}_{0}
. If
(\rho_{0},\rho_{1})\in\dom f\cap\dom f^{*}
, we have that
f^{*}(\rho_{0},\rho_{1})=\tilde{f}(\rho_{0},\rho_{1})=f(\rho_{0},\rho_{1})
, so
f\cup f^{*}\subseteq\bar{f}
.

For every
\xi\in\dom p^{*}
,
\xi\neq\alpha
, so
p^{*}(\xi)\subseteq\tilde{p}(\xi)=\bar{p}(\xi)
. Now suppose
\xi\in\dom p
. We must show that
p(\xi)\subseteq\bar{p}(\xi)
.

Case 1:
if
\xi\neq\alpha
, we have
\bar{p}(\xi)=\tilde{p}(\xi)\supseteq p(\xi)
.

Case 2:
if
\xi=\alpha
, we have
\bar{p}(\xi)=\bar{p}(\alpha)=\tilde{p}(\alpha^{\prime})\supseteq p^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime})=p^{\prime}(\phi_{t^{\prime}}\circ\phi_{t}^{-1}(\alpha))=\tau_{\phi_{t}^{-1}(\alpha)}=p(\alpha)
.

Step
\gamma+1
: Assume that
(\bar{f},\bar{p},\bar{q}|\gamma)\in\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}
extends
(f,p,q|\gamma)
and
(f^{*},p^{*},q^{*}|\gamma)
.

We will show that for all
s\in 2^{<\omega}
,
\xi\in\kappa
and
n\in\omega
, if
(s,0,n),(\xi,1,n)\in\bar{q}(\gamma)
, we have
\xi\in\dom\bar{p}
and
\bar{p}(\xi)\perp s
. This will guarantee that
(\bar{f},\bar{p},\bar{q}|_{\gamma+1})\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then it easily follows that it is in
\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}
as well.

Our claim is trivial if
\gamma\in\dom q\setminus\dom q^{*}
or
\dom q^{*}\setminus\dom q
. So assume
\gamma\in\dom q\cap\dom q^{*}\subseteq X
. Then
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Again, this is trivial if both triples are in
q(\gamma)
, or in
q^{*}(\gamma)
. So we have only three cases:

Case A:

(\xi,1,n)\in q^{*}(\gamma)
and
(s,0,n)\in q(\gamma)
. In this case,
\xi\in\dom p^{*}
, so
\xi\neq\alpha
, therefore
\bar{p}(\xi)=\tilde{p}(\xi)\perp s
as
(s,0,n),(\xi,1,n)\in\tilde{q}(\gamma)
.

Case B:

(\xi,1,n)\in q(\gamma)\setminus q^{*}(\gamma)
,
(s,0,n)\in q^{*}(\gamma)
. We break this case into two subcases:

Subcase B1:

\xi=\alpha
. In this case,
(\xi,1,n)=(\alpha,1,n)\in q(\gamma)
, so
(\phi_{t^{\prime}}\circ\phi_{t}^{-1}(\alpha),1,n)=(\alpha^{\prime},1,n)\in q^{\prime}(\gamma)\subseteq\tilde{q}(\gamma)
. Also,
(s,0,n)\in\tilde{q}(\gamma)\supseteq q^{*}(\gamma)
. Thus,
\bar{p}(\alpha)=\tilde{p}(\alpha^{\prime})
is incompatible with
s
.

Subcase B2:

\xi\neq\alpha
. In this case,
(s,0,n),(\xi,1,n)\in\tilde{q}(\gamma)\supseteq q^{*}(\gamma)\cup q(\gamma)
, and
\bar{p}(\xi)=\tilde{p}(\xi)
. Thus,
\bar{p}(\xi)=\tilde{p}(\xi)
is incompatible with
s
.

∎

3.
On the existence of strong
\Delta
-sets

In this section, we prove that the existence of
\Delta
-sets implies the existence of strong
\Delta
-sets, that is, of
\Delta
-sets whose all finite powers are also
\Delta
-sets. We start by fixing the following notation:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a collection of sets. We define
\mathcal{A}_{u}
,
\mathcal{A}_{i}
,
\mathcal{A}_{\delta}

\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
as the collection of all finite unions, finite intersections, countable unions and finite intersections of
\mathcal{A}
, respectively. If all elements of
\mathcal{A}
are contained in some set
Y
which is clear from the context,
\mathcal{A}^{c}
is the collection of all complements of members of
\mathcal{A}
with respect to
Y
.

If
(\mathcal{A}_{i}:i\leq r)
is a sequence of collection of sets,
\bigotimes_{i\leq r}\mathcal{A}_{i}=\{\prod_{i\leq r}A_{i}:\forall i\leq r\,A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}\}
.

Definition 3.2

(
[
16
]
)
.

Given a countable family of subsets of
\omega_{1}
,
\mathcal{A}=(A_{n}:n\in\omega)
, the characteristic function for the family
\mathcal{A}
is
\chi_{\mathcal{A}}:\omega_{1}\rightarrow 2^{\omega}
given by, for every
\alpha\in\omega_{1}
and
n\in\omega

\chi_{\mathcal{A}}(\alpha)(n)=\chi_{A_{n}}(\alpha)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

In the conditions of the definition above, for any
A_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
the set
\chi_{\mathcal{A}}[A_{n}]
is a clopen subspace of
\chi_{\mathcal{A}}[\omega_{1}]
. Moreover, the function
\chi_{\mathcal{A}}
is injective if, and only if
\mathcal{A}
separates points, that is, if for every
\alpha,\beta\in\omega_{1}
there exists
n\in\omega
such that
\alpha\in A_{n}
and
\beta\notin A_{n}
.

Indeed it is immediate that
\chi_{\mathcal{A}}[A_{n}]=\{f\in 2^{\omega}:f(n)=1\}\cap\chi_{\mathcal{A}}[\omega_{1}]
and therefore is a clopen.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
r\in\omega
and
i\leq n
. A relation
f\subset\omega_{1}^{r+1}
is said to be an
i
-function into
\omega_{1}^{r}
if it is a function with respect to the
i
-th coordinate, that is, if for every
\alpha\in\omega_{1}
,
|\pi_{i}^{-1}[\{\alpha\}]\cap f|=1

If
f
is an
i
-function into
\omega_{1}^{r}
, for every
\alpha\in\omega_{1}
, the unique
r
-tuple
(\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{r-1})
such that
(\beta_{j})_{j<i}^{\frown}(\alpha)^{\frown}(\beta_{j})_{i\leq j<r}\in f
is denoted by
f(\alpha)
, and such a
\beta_{j}
is denoted by
f(j,\alpha)
.

The next two lemmas state useful properties of this class of objects for our purposes.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
r\in\omega
,
i\leq r
and
f
an
i
-function into
\omega_{1}^{r}
. There exists a countable collection
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega_{1})
such that
f\in(\bigotimes_{j\leq r}\mathcal{B})_{u\delta}
.

Proof.

Fix an injective family
(x_{\beta}:\beta\in\omega_{1})
of real numbers. Then for every
\alpha
,
f(\alpha)=\vec{\beta}=(\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{r-1})
iff
\forall q\in Q\,\forall j<r
,
q<x_{f(j,\alpha)}\leftrightarrow q<x_{\beta_{j}}
. Thus:

First consider the case where
i=0
to ease our notation, the general case can be verified by “shifting the coordinates” on any
i
-function to obtain an
0
-function. We have that

g=\bigcap_{j<r}\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb{Q}}\pi_{0}^{-1}[\{\alpha<\omega_{1}:q\geq x_{f(j,\alpha)}\}]\cap\pi_{j+1}^{-1}[\{\beta<\omega_{1}:q<x_{\beta}\}]

\cup\pi_{0}^{-1}[\{\alpha<\omega_{1}:q\geq x_{f(j,\alpha)}\}]\cap\pi_{j+1}^{-1}[\{\beta<\omega_{1}:q<x_{\beta}\}]

Taking
\mathcal{B}
to be the collection of the sets used to write the intersections above we only need to verify that
f=g
to conclude the lemma.

Indeed let
(\alpha,\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{r-1})\in f
. For all
j<r
and
q\in\mathbb{Q}
, since either
q<f(j,\alpha)=\beta_{j-1}
or
f(j,\alpha)=\beta_{j-1}\leq q
, it is immediate that
(\alpha,\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{r-1})\in g
. Now, if
(\alpha,\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{r-1})\notin f
, there are
j<r
and
q\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
f(j,\alpha)<q<\beta_{j-1}
or
f(j,\alpha)>q>\beta_{j-1}
. Hence
(\alpha,\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{r-1})\notin g
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Given
r<\omega
. There exists
(f^{i}_{n}:n\in\omega,i\leq r)
a collection of
i
-functions into
\omega_{1}^{r}
such that
\omega_{1}^{r+1}=\bigcup_{i\leq r}\bigcup_{n\in\omega}f^{i}_{n}
.

Proof.

Define, for each
i<r
,
f^{i}_{n}(\alpha)
such that the set
\{f^{i}_{n}(\alpha):n\in\omega\}=(\alpha+1)^{r}
. The union of the functions is a subset of
\omega_{1}^{r+1}
by Definition
3.4
. Now, given
x=(x_{0},\dots,x_{r})\in\omega_{1}^{r+1}
, let
j\leq r
be such that
x_{j}\geq x_{i}
for all

. Since
\bar{x}=(x_{0},\dots,\hat{x_{j}},\dots,x_{r})\in(x_{j}+1)^{r}
, we must have
n\in\omega
such that
f^{j}_{n}(x_{j})=\bar{x}
, therefore
x\in f^{j}_{n}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

If there exists an uncountable
\Delta
-set, then there exists a countable subalgebra
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
of

which separates points such that for every vanishing sequence

Every subset of a

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.8
.

If there exists an uncountable
\Delta
-set, then there is an uncountable strong
\Delta
-set.

Proof.

Since there exists an uncountable
\Delta
-set, there exists a countable set
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
of subsets of
\omega_{1}
as in Lemma
3.7
.

For each
r<\omega
and
i\leq r

(f^{ri}_{n}:n\in\omega)
be a collection of
i

\omega_{1}^{r+1}
such that
\omega_{1}^{r+1}=\bigcup_{i\leq r}\bigcup_{n\in\omega}f^{r,i}_{n}
as seen in Lemma
3.6
.

For each
r<\omega
, let
g_{n}^{ri}=f_{n}^{ri}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{n^{\prime}<n}\bigcup_{i^{\prime}\leq r}f_{n^{\prime}}^{ri^{\prime}}\cup\bigcup_{i^{\prime}<i}f_{n}^{ri^{\prime}}\right)
, a partial
i
-function. By the Lemma
3.5
, for each
n,r<\omega
and
i\leq r
there exists a countable collection
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{ri}
of subsets of
\omega_{1}
such that
f_{n}^{ri}\in(\mathcal{B}_{n}^{ri}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n}^{ri})_{u\delta}
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be the countable algebra generated by the countable collection
\bigcup_{r\in\omega}\bigcup_{i\leq r}\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\mathcal{B}_{n}^{ri}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, Then, for every
n,r\in\omega
,and
i\leq r
,
g_{n}^{ri}
are intersections of a member of
(\bigotimes_{i\leq r}\mathcal{A})_{u\delta}
and a member of
(\mathcal{[}\bigotimes_{i\leq r}\mathcal{A}]^{c})_{i\sigma i}
.

Consider
\vec{\mathcal{A}}=(A_{n}:n\in\omega)
to be an enumeration of
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\sigma=\chi_{\vec{\mathcal{A}}}:\omega_{1}\rightarrow 2^{\omega}
as in Definition
3.2
and
X=\sigma[\omega_{1}]
. For each
r\in\omega
, let
\sigma_{r}:\omega_{1}^{r+1}\rightarrow(2^{\omega})^{r+1}
be given by
\sigma_{r}(\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{r})=(\sigma(\alpha_{0}),\dots,\sigma(\alpha_{r}))
. Let
X=\sigma[\omega_{1}]
.

Claim:
For every
r,n\in\omega
and
i\leq n
,
\sigma_{r}[g_{n}^{ri}]
is an
F_{\sigma}
of
X^{r+1}
.

Proof.

Fix
n
. We prove that
\sigma_{r}[g_{n}^{ri}]
is a
G_{\delta}^{ri}
and an
F_{\sigma}
.

Write
g_{n}^{ri}=Q_{0}\cap Q_{1}
, where
Q_{0}\in(\bigotimes_{i\leq r}\mathcal{A})_{u\delta}
and
Q_{1}=(\mathcal{[}\bigotimes_{i\leq r}\mathcal{A}]^{c})_{i\sigma i}
.

It suffices to show that
\sigma_{r}[Q_{0}]
is closed and that
\sigma_{r}[Q_{1}]
is open, since we are working with subspaces of
(2^{\omega})^{r}
.

\sigma_{r}[Q_{0}]
is closed:
write
Q_{0}=\bigcap_{l\in\omega}\bigcup_{j<l}\prod_{i^{\prime}\leq r}A_{i^{\prime}jl}
, where each
A_{i^{\prime}jl}
is in
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\sigma_{r}[Q_{0}]=\bigcap_{l\in\omega}\bigcup_{j<l}\prod_{i^{\prime}\leq r}\sigma[A_{i^{\prime}jl}]
. By Lemma
3.3
, each
\sigma[A_{i^{\prime}jl}]
is a clopen subset of
X
, so this is a closed set.

\sigma_{r}[Q_{1}]
is open:
write
Q_{1}=\bigcap_{k<p}\bigcup_{l\in\omega}\omega_{1}^{r+1}\setminus\bigcap_{j<l}\prod_{i^{\prime}\leq r}A^{k}_{i^{\prime}jl}
, where each

is in
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\sigma_{r}[Q_{1}]=\bigcap_{i<p}\bigcup_{l\in\omega}\bigcap_{j<l}X^{r+1}\setminus\prod_{i^{\prime}\leq r}\sigma[A^{k}_{i^{\prime}jl}]
. Again each
\sigma[A^{k}_{i^{\prime}jl}]
is a clopen subset of
X
, so this is an open set.
∎

Fix
r<\omega
. We show that
X^{r+1}
is a
\Delta
-set. Let
(K_{m}:m\in\omega)
be a vanishing sequence in
X^{r+1}
, and
F_{m}=\sigma_{r}^{-1}[K_{m}]
. For every
m,n\in\omega
, let
X^{ni}_{m}=\pi_{i}[g_{n}^{ri}\cap F_{m}]
. Now we have:

•

g_{n}^{ri}\cap F_{m}=\pi_{i}^{-1}[X^{ni}_{m}]\cap g_{n}^{ri}
;

•

For each
n\in\omega
and
i\leq n
,
(X^{ni}_{m}:m\in\omega)
vanishes;

•

For each
n\in\omega
and
i\leq n
,
\exists(G^{ni}_{m}:m\in\omega)
vanishing expansion of
(X^{ni}_{m}:n\in\omega)
in
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
.

Then, for each
m\in\omega

F_{m}=\bigcup_{i\leq r}\bigcup_{n\in\omega}F_{m}\cap g^{ri}_{n}=\bigcup_{i\leq r}\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\pi_{i}^{-1}[X^{ni}_{m}]\cap g_{n}^{ri}

\subseteq\bigcup_{i\leq r}\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\pi_{i}^{-1}[G^{ni}_{m}]\cap g_{n}^{ri}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}W_{m}

Claim:

(W_{m}:m\in\omega)
is vanishing.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction it is not. Fix
\vec{\alpha}\in\bigcap_{m\in\omega}W_{m}
. There exists a unique member of
(g_{n}^{ri}:n\in\omega,i\leq r)
such that
\vec{\alpha}
is inside of it. Then
\vec{\alpha}\in\bigcap_{m\in\omega}\pi_{i}^{-1}[G^{ni}_{m}]\cap g_{n}^{ri}
. Thus,
\vec{\alpha}(i)\in\bigcap_{m\in\omega}G_{m}^{k}=\emptyset

∎

Thus, we have that:

K_{m}\subseteq\sigma_{r}[W_{m}]=\bigcup_{i\leq r}\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\pi_{i}^{-1}[\sigma[G^{ni}_{m}]]\cap\sigma_{r}[g_{n}^{ri}].

We have that
\sigma_{r}[W_{m}]
is vanishing. As

is a partition of
\omega_{1}^{r+1}
, its complement is:

\bigcup_{i\leq r}\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\sigma_{r}[g_{n}^{ri}]\setminus\pi^{-1}_{i}[\sigma[G^{ni}_{m}]],

which is clearly an

as each

is open for each

,

.
∎

Is there a consistent example of a

-set

such that

is a

Some natural attempts at adapting the forcing from Section

If

(Q/\Delta)
-set such that

is also a

-set, then must

In this sense Theorem

guarantees that the existence of a strongly

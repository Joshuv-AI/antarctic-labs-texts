On the subinvariance of uniform domains in metric spaces Thanks: The research was partly supported by NNSF of China
Yaxiang Li, Manzi Huang, Xiantao Wang ∗, Qingshan Zhou

Abstract
. Suppose that X X and Y Y are quasiconvex and complete metric spaces, that G ⊂ X G\subset X and G ′ ⊂ Y G^{\prime}\subset Y are domains, and that f : G → G ′ f:G\to G^{\prime} is a homeomorphism. Our main result is the following subinvariance property of the class of uniform domains: Suppose both f f and f − 1 f^{-1} are weakly quasisymmetric mappings and G ′ G^{\prime} is a quasiconvex domain. Then the image f ⁡ ( D ) f(D) of every uniform subdomain D D in G G under f f is uniform. The subinvariance of uniform domains with respect to freely quasiconformal mappings or quasihyperbolic mappings is also studied with the additional condition that both G G and G ′ G^{\prime} are locally John domains.

1.
Introduction and main results

The quasihyperbolic metric (briefly, QH metric) was introduced by Gehring and
his students Palka and Osgood in the 1970’s
[
12
,
13
]
in the setting of Euclidean spaces

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

(n\geq 2).
Since its first appearance, the quasihyperbolic metric has become
an important tool in the geometric function theory of Euclidean spaces, especially, in the study of quasiconformal and quasisymmetric mappings. From late 1980’s onwards, Väisälä has developed the theory of (dimension) free quasiconformal mappings (briefly, free theory) in Banach spaces, which is based on properties of the quasihyperbolic metric
[
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
,
41
]
. The main advantage of this approach over generalizations based on the conformal modulus (see
[
16
]
and references therein) is that it does not make use of volume integrals, thus allowing the study of quasiconformality in infinite dimensional Banach spaces.

The class of quasisymmetric mappings on the real axis was first introduced by Beurling and
Ahlfors
[
4
]
, who found a way to obtain a quasiconformal extension of a quasisymmetric self-mapping of the
real axis to a self-mapping of the upper half-plane. This idea
was later generalized by Tukia and Väisälä, who studied quasisymmetric
mappings between metric spaces
[
32
]
. In
1998, Heinonen and Koskela
[
18
]
proved a remarkable result, showing that the concepts of quasiconformality and
quasisymmetry are quantitatively equivalent in a large class of metric spaces, which
includes Euclidean spaces. Also, Väisälä proved the quantitative equivalence between free quasiconformality and
quasisymmetry of homeomorphisms between two Banach spaces. See
[
41
, Theorem 7.15]
. Against this background, it is not surprising that the study of quasisymmetry in metric spaces has recently attracted significant attention.

The main goal of this paper is to establish the subinvariance of uniform domains in suitable metric spaces with respect to quaisymmetric mappings, freely quasiconformal mappings and quasihyperbloic mappings. We start by recalling some basic definitions.
Through this paper, we always assume that
X
and
Y
are metric spaces. We follow the notations and terminology of
[
17
,
18
,
25
,
33
,
41
]
.

Definition 1
.

A homeomorphism
f
from
X
to
Y
is said to be

(1)

if there is a constant
H<\infty
such that

(1.1)

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{L_{f}(x,r)}{l_{f}(x,r)}\leq H

for all

;

(2)

if there is a constant
H<\infty
such that

(1.2)

\frac{L_{f}(x,r)}{l_{f}(x,r)}\leq H

for all

,

L_{f}(x,r)=\sup_{|y-x|\leq r}\{|f(y)-f(x)|\}

and

l_{f}(x,r)=\inf_{|y-x|\geq r}\{|f(y)-f(x)|\}
.

Here and in what follows, we always use
|x-y|
to denote the distance between

and

.

Definition 2

A homeomorphism

from

to

(1)

(2)

|x-a|\leq|x-b|\;\;\mbox{ implies}\;\;|f(x)-f(a)|\leq H|f(x)-f(b)|

,

of points in

.

.

The following observations follow immediately from Definitions

(1)

(2)

2.1.
Quasihyperbolic metric, solid arcs and short arcs

In this subsection, we start with the definition of quasihyperbolic metric. If
X
is a connected metric space and
G\varsubsetneq X
is a non-empty open set,
then it follows from
[
25
, Remark 2.2]
that the boundary of
G
satisfies
\partial G\not=\emptyset
.
Suppose
\gamma\subset G
denotes a rectifiable arc or a path, its
quasihyperbolic length
is the number:

\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma}\frac{|dz|}{\delta_{G}(z)},

where
\delta_{G}(z)
denotes the distance from
z
to
\partial G
.

For each pair of points
x
,
y
in
G
, the
quasihyperbolic distance

k_{G}(x,y)
between
x
and
y
is defined in the following way:

k_{G}(x,y)=\inf\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma),

where the infimum is taken over all rectifiable arcs
\gamma

joining
x
to
y
in
G
.

Suppose
X
is quasiconvex and
G\subsetneq X
. If
\gamma
is a rectifiable curve in
G
connecting
x
and
y
, then (see, e.g., the proof of Theorem
2.7
in
[
25
]
)

\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma)\geq\log\Big(1+\frac{\ell(\gamma)}{\min\{\delta_{G}(x),\delta_{G}(y)\}}\Big)

and thus,

k_{G}(x,y)\geq\log\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\min\{\delta_{G}(x),\delta_{G}(y)\}}\Big).

Gehring and Palka
[
13
]
introduced the quasihyperbolic metric of
a domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. For the basic properties of this metric we refer to
[
12
]
. Recall that a curve
\gamma
from
x
to

y
is a
quasihyperbolic geodesic
if

\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma)=k_{G}(x,y)
. Each subcurve of a quasihyperbolic
geodesic is obviously a quasihyperbolic geodesic. It is known that a
quasihyperbolic geodesic between any two points in a Banach space
X
exists if the
dimension of
X
is finite, see
[
12
, Lemma 1]
. This is not
true in arbitrary metric spaces (cf.
[
36
, Example 2.9]
).

Let us recall a result which is useful for the discussions later on.

Lemma B.

(
[
23
, Lemma 2.4]
) Let
X
be a
c
-quasiconvex metric space and let
G\subsetneq X
be a domain. Suppose that
x
,
y\in G
and either
|x-y|\leq\frac{1}{3c}\delta_{G}(x)
or
k_{G}(x,y)\leq 1
. Then

(2.1)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{|x-y|}{\delta_{G}(x)}<k_{G}(x,y)<3c\frac{|x-y|}{\delta_{G}(x)}.

Here, we say that
X
is
c-quasiconvex

(c\geq 1)
if each pair of points
x
,
y\in X
can be joined by an arc
\gamma
with length
{\ell}(\gamma)\leq c|x-y|
.

Definition 4
.

Suppose
\gamma
is an arc in a domain
G\varsubsetneq X
and
X
is a rectifiably connected metric space. The arc may be closed, open or half open. Let
\overline{x}=(x_{0},

\ldots,

x_{n})
,

\gamma
.
For
h\geq 0
, we say that
\overline{x}
is

k_{G}(x_{j-1},x_{j})\geq h
for all

. Let
\Phi_{k}(\gamma,h)

denote the family of all

-coarse sequences of
\gamma
. Set

s_{k}(\overline{x})=\sum^{n}_{j=1}k_{G}(x_{j-1},x_{j})

and

\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma,h)=\sup\{s_{k}(\overline{x}):\overline{x}\in\Phi_{k}(\gamma,h)\}

with the agreement that
\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma,h)=0
if

\Phi_{k}(\gamma,h)=\emptyset
. Then the number
\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma,h)
is the

-coarse quasihyperbolic length
of
\gamma

If

is

-quasiconvex, then
\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma,0)=\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma)
(see, e.g.,
[

and

Definition 5

Let
G

. An arc
\gamma\subset G

is
(\nu,h)
-solid
with

\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma[x,y],h)\leq\nu\;k_{G}(x,y)

An arc
\gamma\subset G
with endpoints

\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma)\leq k_{G}(x,y)+\varepsilon.

Obviously, by the definition of

, we know that for every

, there exists an arc
\gamma\subset G
such that
\gamma
is
\varepsilon
-short, and it is easy to see that every subarc of an

\varepsilon

Remark 5
.

For any pair of points
x
and

in a proper domain
G

In order to overcome this disadvantage, in this paper, we will exploit the substitution of “quasigeodesics” replaced by “short arcs”. The class of short arcs has been introduced when Väisälä studied properties of Gromov hyperbolic spaces
[

(see also

Although, there is no implication between the class of quasigeodesics and the one of short arcs, we will prove that, under certain geometric assumptions, every short arc is a double cone arc.

By a slight modification of the method used in the proof of
[
37

, we get the following result.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
X
is a
c

\gamma

-solid arc in

\mu_{1}=\mu_{1}(c,\nu)

{\operatorname{diam}}(\gamma)\leq\max\{\mu_{1}|x-y|,2r(e^{h}-1)\},

where “

Proof.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\delta_{G}(y)\geq\delta_{G}(x)=r
. Denoting

and applying Lemma ??, we get

k_{G}(x,y)\leq 3ct/r.

Let
u\in\gamma
. To prove this lemma, it suffices to show that there exists a constant
\mu_{1}=\mu_{1}(c,\nu)
such that

(2.2)

|u-x|\leq\max\big\{\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}|x-y|,r(e^{h}-1)\big\}.

To this end, we consider two cases. The first case is:
k_{G}(u,x)\leq h
. Under this assumption, it is easy to see that

(2.3)

|u-x|\leq(e^{k_{G}(u,x)}-1)\delta_{G}(x)\leq r(e^{h}-1).

For the remaining case:
k_{G}(u,x)>h
, we choose a sequence of successive points of
\gamma

x=x_{0}
,
\ldots
,

such that

k_{G}(x_{j-1},x_{j})=h\;\;\;{\rm for}\;\;\;j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}

Then

(n-1)h\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}k_{G}(x_{j-1},x_{j})\leq\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma,h)\leq\nu k_{G}(x,y)\leq 3c\nu t/r,

Let

. Then

\frac{|u-x|}{t}\leq\frac{e^{6c\nu s}-1}{s}.

Obviously,
the function
g(s)=\frac{1}{s}\big(e^{6c\nu s}-1\big)
is increasing in

(0,\frac{1}{3c}]
and
\lim_{s\to 0}{\frac{e^{6c\nu s}-1}{s}}=6c\nu
. Letting

\mu_{1}=6c(e^{2\nu}-1)

gives

(2.4)

|u-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu_{1}t.

It follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) that (
2.2
) holds, and hence the proof of the lemma is complete.

Proof.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\min\{\delta_{G}(x),\delta_{G}(y)\}=\delta_{G}(x)

Therefore,

as required.∎

microscopic conservation laws for the derivative Nonlinear Schrödinger equation
Xingdong Tang, Guixiang Xu

Abstract
. Compared with macroscopic conservation law for the solution of the derivative nonlinear Schrödingger equation (DNLS) with small mass in [ 21 ] , we show the corresponding microscopic conservation laws for the Schwartz solutions of DNLS with small mass. The new ingredient is to make use of the logarithmic perturbation determinant introduced in [ 34 , 35 ] to show one-parameter family of microscopic conservation laws of the A ⁡ ( κ ) A(\kappa) flow and the DNLS flow, which is motivated by [ 11 , 19 , 20 ] .

We now recall some Hamiltonian mechanics background. (
1.1
) is Hamiltonian equation with respect to the following Poisson structure:

\{F,G\}:=\int\tfrac{\delta F}{\delta r}\partial_{x}\bigl(\tfrac{\delta G}{\delta q}\bigr)+\tfrac{\delta F}{\delta q}\partial_{x}\bigl(\tfrac{\delta G}{\delta r}\bigr)\,{\rm{d}}x,

(1.5)

where the operators
\tfrac{\delta}{\delta q}
and
\tfrac{\delta}{\delta r}
denote the functional Fréchet derivatives. Any Hamiltonian
H(q,r,t)
generates a flow via the equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\begin{bmatrix}q\\
r\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}\partial_{x}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\delta H}{\delta q}\\
\frac{\delta H}{\delta r}\end{bmatrix}.

(1.6)

Correspondingly, (
1.1
) is the Hamiltonian flow associated to

H_{\mathrm{DNLS}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}-{i\mkern 1.0mu}qr^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}q^{2}r^{2}\,{\rm{d}}x,

(1.7)

where
r=-\bar{q}
. Two other important Hamiltonian quantities for (
1.1
) are

M:=\int qr\,{\rm{d}}x,\quad E_{\mathrm{DNLS}}:=\int q^{\prime}r^{\prime}-\frac{3}{2}{i\mkern 1.0mu}q^{2}rr^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}q^{3}r^{3}\,{\rm{d}}x.

(1.8)

Conservations of
M
,
H_{\mathrm{DNLS}}
and
E_{\mathrm{DNLS}}
is due to gauge, space translation and time translation invariance of (
1.1
), the commutativity of
H_{\mathrm{DNLS}}
and
E_{\mathrm{DNLS}}
is based on the fact that they are completely integrable, which means the existence of an infinite family of commuting flows for (
1.1
). We can refer to
[
1
]

[
21
]
for more details. Based on the recent breakthrough in KdV, mKdV and NLS by B. Harrop-Griffiths, R. Killip and M. Visan in
[
11
,
19
]
, the commuting flow approximation will be the robust method for showing global well-posedness theory of (
1.1
) in the lower regularity space. We can also refer to
[
22
,
30
]
and reference thereein.

2.
Some notation and preliminary estimates

In this paper, we take
r=-\bar{q}
and choose

s\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}),

and all implicit constants can depend on
s
. We denote
\mathscr{S}
the Schwartz function, and introduce the notation

B_{\delta}:=\left\{q\in H^{s}:\|q\|_{H^{s}}\leq\delta\right\}.

(2.1)

which can be ensured by scaling argument under the assumption that the mass
\|q\|_{L^{2}}
is small enough.

We use the inner product on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
as follows

\langle f,g\rangle=\int\overline{f(x)}g(x)\,dx,

which also gives the dual product between
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
and
H^{-s}(\mathbb{R})
. In addtion, If
F:\mathscr{S}\to\mathbb{C}
is
C^{1}
, we have

\tfrac{d\ }{d\theta}\Big|_{\theta=0}F(q+\theta f)=\bigl\langle\bar{f},\tfrac{\delta F}{\delta q}\bigr\rangle-\bigl\langle f,\tfrac{\delta F}{\delta r}\bigr\rangle.

(2.2)

The Fourier transform is defined by

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\xi)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi x}f(x)\,dx,\quad\text{whence}\quad\widehat{fg}(\xi)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}[\hat{f}*\hat{g}](\xi).

2.1.
Sobolev spaces

For complex
\kappa
with
|\kappa|\geq 1
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
we define the norm

\|q\|_{H^{\sigma}_{\kappa}}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(4|\kappa|^{4}+\xi^{2}\right)^{\sigma}|\hat{q}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

and write
H^{\sigma}:=H^{\sigma}_{1}
.

For
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
, simple calculation yields the Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}}\bigl\|(|\xi|^{2}+4|\kappa|^{4})^{-\frac{2s+1}{4}}\bigr\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim|\kappa|^{-2s}\|f\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}}.

(2.3)

Consequently, we have the following algebra property of
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}
space:

\|fg\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}}\lesssim|\kappa|^{-2s}\|f\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}}\|g\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}}.

(2.4)

By duality and the fractional product rule in
[
4
]
, Sobolev embedding, and (
2.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|qf\|_{H^{s-1/2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle|\kappa|^{-2s}\|q\|_{H^{s-1/2}}\|f\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}}.

(2.5)

2.2.
Operator estimates and Trace

For
0<\sigma<1
and
i\kappa^{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
|\kappa|\geq 1
we define the operator
(i\kappa^{2}\mp\partial)^{-\sigma}
using the Fourier multiplier
(i\kappa^{2}\mp i\xi)^{-\sigma}
where, for
\arg z\in(-\pi,\pi]
, we define

z^{-\sigma}=|z|^{-\sigma}e^{-i\sigma\arg z}.

(2.6)

Therefore for all
{i\mkern 1.0mu}\kappa^{2}\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\kappa|\geq 1
we have

\left((i\kappa^{2}\mp\partial)^{-\sigma}\right)^{*}=(i\kappa^{2}\pm\partial)^{-\sigma},

and

\left\|(i\kappa^{2}\mp\partial)^{-\sigma}\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq|\kappa|^{-2\sigma}.

We denote
\mathfrak{I}_{p}
the Schatten class of compact operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
whose singular
numbers are
l^{p}
summable.
\mathfrak{I}_{p}
is complete and is an embedded subalgebra of bounded operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, we have

\mathfrak{I}_{p}\subset\mathfrak{I}_{q},\text{~~for~~}p\leq q.

(2.7)

Let us recall some facts about the class
\mathfrak{I}_{p}
that we will use repeatedly in Section
3
: An operator A on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is Hilbert–Schmidt class (
\mathfrak{I}_{2}
) if and only if it admits an integral kernel
a(x,y)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})
, and

\|A\|^{2}_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\|A\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}|a(x,y)|^{2}\,\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}y.

The product of two Hilbert–Schmidt operators is trace class,; Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\tr(AB):=

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}a(x,y)b(y,x)\,{\rm{d}}y\,{\rm{d}}x=\tr(BA),

\displaystyle|\tr(AB)|

\displaystyle\leq\|AB\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{1}}\leq\|A\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}\|B\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}

The class
\mathfrak{I}_{p}
forms a two-sided ideal in the algebra of
bounded operators on
L^{2}
; indeed, for any bounded operators
B,C
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\|BAC\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{p}}\leq\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|A\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{p}}\|C\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

We can refer to
[
8
,
35
]
for more details.

The following estimates are the elementary estimates in this paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1/2)
,
{i\mkern 1.0mu}\kappa^{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\|(\partial+{i\mkern 1.0mu}\kappa^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\kappa q(\partial-{i\mkern 1.0mu}\kappa^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|q\|_{L^{2}},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|(\partial+{i\mkern 1.0mu}\kappa^{2})^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\kappa q(\partial-{i\mkern 1.0mu}\kappa^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\kappa|\|q\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}},

(2.9)

and

\displaystyle\|(\partial+i\kappa^{2})^{-s-\frac{1}{2}}f(\partial-i\kappa^{2})^{-s-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\kappa|^{-2s}\|f\|_{H^{-s-\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}}.

(2.10)

Proof.

The estimate (
2.8
) can be proved by
[
20
, Lemma 4.1]
. For (
2.9
), it suffices to consider the case
{i\mkern 1.0mu}\kappa^{2}=1
by scaling argument. By Plancherel’s Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|(1-\partial)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}q(1+\partial)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathfrak{I}_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\tr\left\{(1-\partial^{2})^{-\alpha}q(1-\partial^{2})^{-\beta}\bar{q}\right\}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\hat{q}(\xi-\eta)|^{2}\,}{\left(1+\xi^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-s}\left(1+\eta^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}d\eta\,d\xi,

Note that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\left(1+(\xi+\eta)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-s}\left(1+\eta^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,d\eta\lesssim(4+\xi^{2})^{s-\frac{1}{2}},

we can obtain the estimate (
2.9
).

Lastly, we can obtain (
2.10
) by duality and (
2.4
) as follows

\biggl|\int_{\mathbb{R}}fgh\,dx\biggr|\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{-s-\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}}\|gh\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}}\lesssim|\kappa|^{-2s}\|f\|_{H^{-s-\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}}\|g\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}}\|h\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{\kappa}}.

This completes the proof.

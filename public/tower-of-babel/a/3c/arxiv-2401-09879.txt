One-loop partition functions in T ​ T ¯ T\overline{T} -deformed AdS 3


Abstract
We study the geometry of T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} -deformed BTZ black hole and find it can be regarded as a quotient of hyperbolic space. We then consider the massive scalar field propagating in the T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} -deformed BTZ black hole background. The one-loop partition function of scalar field is calculated using the heat kernel method and the Wilson spool proposal. These two methods give consistent result which implies the Wilson spool proposal still holds under T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} deformation. Moreover, we also calculate the one-loop partition function of graviton in T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} -deformed BTZ black hole. We find the deformed one-loop partition functions are modified in a simple way, which corresponds to a replacement of the modular parameter. The result precisely matches the large c c expansion of T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} -deformed CFT partition function. These results provide a further check about the correspondence between T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} -deformed CFT 2 and AdS 3 with mixed boundary condition.

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}+\mathcal{L}}{r^{2}-\mathcal{L}}e^{-2\sqrt{\mathcal{L}}(\theta^{\prime}+it^{\prime})},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}+\mathcal{L}}{r^{2}-\mathcal{L}}e^{2\sqrt{\mathcal{L}}(\theta^{\prime}-it^{\prime})},

(2.10)

One-loop partition function of scalar field

In this section, we consider the massive scalar field propagating in the
T\bar{T}
-deformed BTZ black hole background. The total action becomes

\displaystyle S=-\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{3}x\sqrt{g}(R+2)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\phi(-\Delta+m^{2})\phi+B

(3.1)

where
B

\displaystyle Z=\int[\mathcal{D}g_{\mu\nu}]e^{-I_{\mathrm{EH}}[g_{\mu\nu}]}Z_{\mathrm{scalar}}[g_{\mu\nu}],

(3.2)

where

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{scalar}}[g_{\mu\nu}]=\int[\mathcal{D}\phi]e^{-S_{\mathrm{m}}[g_{\mu\nu},\phi]}.

(3.3)

We can not be able to work out the gravitational path integral exactly.

In the semi-classical limit
G\to 0
, only the Einstein-Hilbert action and the boundary term contribute to the partition function. Under the saddle point approximation, the partition function can be calculated by sum over all the gravitational saddles. The on-shell Euclidean action becomes the energy of AdS
3
times the euclidean time
[

\displaystyle Z_{0}=e^{kM\beta_{0}}=|q|^{-2k},\quad k=\frac{1}{4G}.

(3.4)

For the
T\bar{T}
-deformed BTZ black hole saddles, We can obtain the similar result. In this case, the Euclidean action with boundary term would give the
T\bar{T}
-deformed spectrum
[
20
]

T\bar{T}
-deformed partition function is

\displaystyle Z_{0}=e^{kQ\beta}=e^{\frac{k\beta}{2\lambda}\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}\lambda}}\right)},

(3.5)

which is agree with the result in
[
37
]

3.1
Heat kernel method

The heat kernel method is a powerful technique to calculate the path integral. In terms of the heat kernel, we can write the determinant as an integral

\displaystyle\log Z_{\mathrm{scalar}}^{\text{1-loop}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{3}x\sqrt{g}K(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}),

(3.6)

where the heat kernel should satisfy the differential equation

\displaystyle(\partial_{s}-\Delta+m^{2})K(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=0,

(3.7)

with the initial condition

\displaystyle K(0,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\delta(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

(3.8)

For more details about the heat kernel method see
[
64
,
63
]
. One can also turn to Appendix
A
for a brief introduction to heat kernel in AdS
3
. The key point is to obtain the heat kernel then work out the integral to get the one-loop partition function.

The information about the manifold is encoded in the Laplace operator
\Delta
as well as the heat kernel. For the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}_{3}
, which is the maximally symmetric space, we expect the heat kernel
K^{\mathbb{H}_{3}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
depends on
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
only through the length of geodesics. Then the heat kernel for hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}_{3}
should be

\displaystyle K^{\mathbb{H}_{3}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=K^{\mathbb{H}_{3}}(s,\sigma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))=\frac{e^{-(m^{2}+1)s-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4s}}}{(4\pi s)^{3/2}}\frac{\sigma}{\sinh\sigma},

(3.9)

where the length of geodesics can be calculated by

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\cosh^{-1}(1+u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})),

(3.10)

\displaystyle u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{2yy^{\prime}}.

(3.11)

Now we are working in the
T\bar{T}
-deformed BTZ black hole, which turns out to be a quotient of hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}_{3}/\mathbb{Z}
in section
2
. The heat kernel for
\mathbb{H}_{3}/\mathbb{Z}
can be constructed from the one for
\mathbb{H}_{3}
by the method of images
[
64
,
63
]
. The result turns out to be

\displaystyle K^{\mathrm{dBTZ}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}K^{\mathbb{H}_{3}}\left(s,\sigma(\mathbf{x},\gamma^{n}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))\right),

(3.12)

where the
\gamma
is the generators of discrete group of isometry which act on the space leads to the shift of coordinates

\displaystyle\gamma^{n}(r,\theta,t)=(r,\theta+2\pi n,t+n\beta).

(3.13)

Therefore, the heat kernel on
\mathbb{H}_{3}/\mathbb{Z}
is sum over all the geodesics winding arbitrary times around the quotient space.

For the
T\bar{T}
-deformed BTZ, in terms of
(r,\theta,t)

\displaystyle\cosh\sigma_{n}=1+\left(1+\frac{r^{2}(1-\lambda Q)}{2Q}+\frac{Q}{2r^{2}(1-\lambda Q)}\right)\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{2n\pi^{2}}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}\lambda}}\right),

(3.14)

where we have used

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{0}\leq\sigma_{n}<+\infty,\quad\sigma_{n}^{0}=\frac{4n\pi^{2}}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}\lambda}}.

Then the integral measure of the

-deformed BTZ black hole metric becomes

\displaystyle d^{3}x\sqrt{g}=\frac{Q(1-\lambda Q)}{1-2\lambda Q}\left[\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{2n\pi^{2}}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}\lambda}}\right)\right]^{-1}\sinh\sigma_{n}d\sigma_{n}d\theta dt.

For

\displaystyle K^{\mathbb{H}_{3}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x})=K^{\mathbb{H}_{3}}(s,0)=\frac{e^{-(1+m^{2})s}}{(4\pi s)^{3/2}},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s}\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}K^{\mathbb{H}_{3}}(s,0)=\frac{(m^{2}+1)^{3/2}}{6\pi}\mathrm{VoL}(\text{dBTZ}).

\displaystyle K^{\mathbb{H}_{3}}(s,\sigma_{n})=\frac{e^{-(1+m^{2})s-\frac{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{4s}}}{(4\pi s)^{3/2}}\frac{\sigma_{n}}{\sinh\sigma_{n}}.

The integral of heat kernel becomes

(3.20)

In the last step, we have used (

\beta

Finally, the one-loop partition function of scalar field in

3.2
Wilson spool method

It is well-known that 3D general relativity has no local degrees of freedom, which is purely topological and can be formulated as a Chern-Simons theory
[
75
]
. In the case of AdS
3
gravity, the relevant isometry group is
SO(2,2)\simeq SL(2,\mathbb{R})\times SL(2,\mathbb{R})
. Then Einstein-Hilbert action can be written as the difference of two copies
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
Chern-Simons theories

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{EH}}[e,\omega]

\displaystyle=I_{CS}[A]-I_{CS}[\bar{A}],

(3.26)

where the Chern-Simons action is

\displaystyle I_{CS}[A]

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\text{Tr}\left(A\wedge dA+\frac{2}{3}A\wedge A\wedge A\right),\quad k=\frac{1}{4G}.

(3.27)

The gauge fields
A
and
\bar{A}
are valued in
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
, which are the linear combination of gravitational vielbein and spin connection

\displaystyle A=\left(\omega^{a}+e^{a}\right)L_{a},\quad\bar{A}=\left(\omega^{a}-e^{a}\right)L_{a}.

(3.28)

The
L_{a}
are
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
generators, they satisfy the commutation relations

\displaystyle[L_{a},L_{b}]=(a-b)L_{a+b},\quad a,b\in\{0,\pm 1\}.

(3.29)

The non-zero components of non-degenerate bilinear form are given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(L_{0}L_{0})=\dfrac{1}{2},\quad\operatorname{Tr}(L_{-1}L_{1})=\operatorname{Tr}(L_{1}L_{-1})=-1.

(3.30)

Variation of the action leads to the equations of motion

\displaystyle F\equiv dA+A\wedge A=0,\quad\bar{F}\equiv d\bar{A}+\bar{A}\wedge\bar{A}=0,

(3.31)

which are equivalent to the gravitational field equation and torsion free equation. The AdS
3
metric can also be recovered from the gauge fields

\displaystyle g_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\Big[(A_{i}-\bar{A}_{i})(A_{j}-\bar{A}_{j})\Big].

(3.32)

Recently, it was proposed that the partition function of massive scalar field in AdS
3
is described by the Wilson spool in Chern-Simons formulation, which is a collection of Wilson loops winding around closed paths of the background
[
67
,
68
]
.
In terms of the Chern-Simons formulation, the one-loop partition function of AdS
3
can be obtained using the Wilson spool proposal

\displaystyle Z^{\text{1-loop}}_{\mathrm{scalar}}[g_{\mu\nu}]=\exp\left(\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{W}_{j}[A,\bar{A}]\right),

(3.33)

where the Wilson spool is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{j}[A,\bar{A}]=i\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha}{\alpha}\frac{\cos\alpha/2}{\sin\alpha/2}\mathrm{Tr}_{R_{j}}\left(\mathcal{P}e^{\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\oint A}\right)\mathrm{Tr}_{R_{j}}\left(\mathcal{P}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\oint\bar{A}}\right).

(3.34)

Here
j
labels a lowest-weight representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
related to the mass of the bulk scalar field by (
3.23
). The contour of integral for AdS
3
is given by
\mathcal{C}=2\mathcal{C}_{+}
with
\mathcal{C}_{+}
running upwards along the imaginary
\alpha
axis to the right of zero
2
2

2

Since the
T\bar{T}
-deformed BTZ black hole is still AdS
3
solution, we choose the contour for AdS
3
in this paper. However, the contour of integral for dS
3
is given by
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{-}\cup\mathcal{C}_{+}
, which is the union of contours running upwards along the imaginary
\alpha
axis both to the left and right of zero.
. This contour integral becomes a sum over poles implements a sum over Wilson loops with arbitrary windings. Then the Wilson spool gives the partition function of scalar fields coupling with Chern-Simons theory. This proposal has been verified in AdS
3
and dS
3

[
67
,
68
]
.

We now turn to the
T\bar{T}
-deformed BTZ black hole, which can be obtained from the BTZ black hole through a coordinate transformation. The AdS
3
gravity with mixed boundary condition can also described by two copies of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
Chern-Simons theory but with a non-trivial boundary term
[
19
,
20

. In Chern-Simons formulation, the deformed gauge connection reads

\displaystyle A_{r}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}L_{0},\quad A_{\theta}=r(1-\lambda Q)L_{1}-\frac{1}{r}QL_{-1},\quad A_{t}=K\Big(r(1-\lambda Q)L_{1}-\frac{1}{r}QL_{-1}\Big),

(3.35)

\displaystyle-\bar{A}_{r}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}L_{0},\quad\bar{A}_{\theta}=\frac{1}{r}QL_{1}-r(1-\lambda Q)L_{-1},\quad\bar{A}_{t}=\bar{K}\Big(\frac{1}{r}QL_{1}-r(1-\lambda Q)L_{-1}\Big),

(3.36)

where

\displaystyle K=-\bar{K}=\frac{1}{1-2\lambda Q}.

(3.37)

Since the
T\bar{T}
-deformed BTZ black hole geometry is filled in
r-t
plane, the
t
-cycle is bulk contractible while the
\theta

(A,\bar{A})
around
\theta

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\exp\left(\oint_{\gamma_{\theta}}A\right)

\displaystyle=u^{-1}e^{2\pi hL_{0}}u,

(3.38)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\exp\left(\oint_{\gamma_{\theta}}\bar{A}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{u}^{-1}e^{2\pi\bar{h}L_{0}}\bar{u},

(3.39)

where

\displaystyle h=-\bar{h}=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{Q(1-\lambda Q)}=\frac{2\pi}{\beta^{2}+\sqrt{4\pi^{2}\lambda}}.

(3.40)

So that the Wilson spool becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{j}[a_{L},a_{R}]=i\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha}{\alpha}\frac{\cos\alpha/2}{\sin\alpha/2}\chi_{j}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}h\right)\chi_{j}\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\bar{h}\right),

(3.41)

where the characters are given by

\displaystyle\chi_{j}(z)=\operatorname{Tr}_{R_{j}}\left(e^{2\pi zL_{0}}\right)=\frac{e^{\pi z(2j-1)}}{2\sinh(-\pi z)}.

(3.42)

The integral in the Wilson spools can be calculated by taking the residues of the poles. We deform the contour to the
right and pick the residues of the poles at
\alpha=2n\pi,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
for each
\mathcal{C}_{+}

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{j}[a_{L},a_{R}]=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\chi_{j}(n\pi h)\chi_{j}(-n\pi\bar{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[n\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{2n\pi^{2}}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}\lambda}}\right)\right]^{-1}\exp\left(\frac{-4n\pi^{2}(2j-1)}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\log\prod_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\left(1-q_{\lambda}^{l+j}\bar{q}_{\lambda}^{l^{\prime}+j}\right)^{-1},

(3.43)

We then have

\displaystyle Z^{\text{1-loop}}_{\mathrm{scalar}}[g_{\mu\nu}]=\exp\left(\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{W}_{j}[A,\bar{A}]\right)=\prod_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\left(1-q_{\lambda}^{l+j}\bar{q}_{\lambda}^{l^{\prime}+j}\right)^{-1}.

(3.44)

This is the same result we have obtained using the heat kernel method. Therefore, the Wilson spool proposal still holds for the
T\bar{T}
-deformed AdS
3

From the calculation we find the Wilson spool is more convenient since we do not need to treat more details about the length of geodesics and the periodicity of
\theta
and
t

The method of image is becomes sum over all the Wilson loops which exactly corresponds to the Wilson spool proposal. Moreover, the Wilson spool also gives the character expansion of the one-loop partition function.

In addition, the gauge-fixing procedure also introduces a Fadeev-Popov ghost field, which in this case is a complex valued vector field
\phi_{\mu}

\displaystyle S=I_{\mathrm{EH}}[g_{\mu\nu}]+S_{\mathrm{trace}}[\phi]+S_{\mathrm{traceless}}[\phi_{\mu\nu}]+S_{\mathrm{ghost}}[\phi_{\mu}],

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{32\pi G}\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\left[\frac{1}{12}\phi\left(-\Delta+4\right)\phi\right],

\displaystyle-\log\det\Delta^{(1)}

While the operator

5
Conclusion and discussion

In this work, we consider the AdS
3
with mixed boundary condition, whose solution can be obtained by a field-dependent coordinate transformation. We found the
T\bar{T}
-deformed BTZ black hole can be regarded as a quotient of hyperbolic space. Based on this fact, we studied the scalar field propagating in
T\bar{T}
-deformed BTZ black hole background. The one-loop partition function for scalar field in
T\bar{T}
-deformed BTZ black hole was calculated using the heat kernel method and Wilson spool method. We also computed the one-loop partition function for graviton by considering the tensor perturbation of
T\bar{T}
-deformed BTZ black hole. We found the one-loop partition function is deformed in a simple way, which is just a replacement of the modular parameter. These result coincide with the
T\bar{T}
-deformed CFT partition function in a special double scaling limit of large central charge and small deformation parameter. Including the tree level contribution, the full
T\bar{T}
-deformed gravity partition function also have a similar form with the undeformed one, which agree with the asymptotic symmetry group associated with the mixed boundary condition is a state-dependent Virasoro algebra.

For the scalar one-loop partition function, we found the Wilson spool proposal is more convenient. However, for the gravity one-loop partition function, we only used the heat kernel method since we do not have the Wilson spool proposal for the
U(1)
gauge field and traceless symmetric tensor field. From the heat kernel calculations we found the Wilson spool proposal
[
67
]
may be generalized to the gauge field by considering an appropriate representation of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
. It would be interesting to generalize the Wilson spool proposal to the higher spin fields in AdS
3
.

It is worth noting that the one-loop partition function is not modular invariant since we just consider the
T\bar{T}
-deformed BTZ black hole saddle. To obtain the full modular invariant partition function we have to sum over all the saddles including BTZ black hole, thermal AdS and BTZ black hole with conical singularity. In present, we do not know how to obtain the deformed thermal AdS and the deformed BTZ black hole with conical singularity. For the undeformed case, the thermal AdS partition function can be obtained by the modular transformation. For the
T\bar{T}
-deformed case, the modular invariant turns to be related to the transformation of the deformation parameter
[
77
,
78
]
. Using the modular transformation with associated transformation of deformation parameter, we can obtain contributions from other saddles.

The one-loop partition is also related to the correlation functions in the context of AdS
3
/CFT
2

[
79
]
. The one-loop partition function can be written into a summation of correlation functions. The one-loop partition function formula provides us with a result to infer the two-point function and higher point function in
T\bar{T}
-deformed CFTs.

Appendix A
Heat kernel method

The heat kernel method is used to calculate the path integral. Here we give a brief introduction about this method. We start from the massive free field theory, whose action is given by

\displaystyle S(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{3}x{\sqrt{g}}\phi(\Delta+m^{2})\phi.

(A.1)

Then the partition function can be written as a path integral form

\displaystyle Z=\int[\mathcal{D}\phi]e^{-S(\phi)}.

(A.2)

This is a Gaussian integral, which can be written as

\displaystyle\log Z=-\frac{1}{2}\log\det(\Delta+m^{2})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\log\lambda_{n},

(A.3)

where the
\lambda_{n}
is the eigenvalue of operator. The most straightforward procedure is to find a complete basis of orthogonal normalized eigenfunctions

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\psi_{n}(\mathbf{x})\psi_{n}(\mathbf{x})=\delta^{3}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),\quad

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\psi_{n}(\mathbf{x})\psi_{m}(\mathbf{x})=\delta_{nm},

(A.4)

which obeys
(\Delta+m^{2})\psi_{n}=\lambda_{n}\psi_{n}
.
The heat kernel is constructed by

\displaystyle K(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\sum_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\psi_{n}(\mathbf{x})\psi_{n}(\mathbf{y}).

(A.5)

One can verify the heat kernel satisfies the differential equation

\displaystyle(\partial_{s}+\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}+m^{2})K(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=0,

(A.6)

with the initial condition

\displaystyle K(0,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\delta(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

(A.7)

In practice, we may obtain the heat kernel as the unique solution to the differential equation with initial condition. In terms of the heat kernel, the sum over the eigenvalues of Laplace operator can be written as a integral

\displaystyle\log Z_{\mathrm{scalar}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{3}x\sqrt{g}K(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}).

(A.8)

This method can also be used for the spinors, vector fields and symmetric traceless tensor fields, etc.

For the
U(1)
gauge field, the heat kernel is a bi-tensor
K_{\mu\nu^{\prime}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
, which satisfies the differential equation

\displaystyle\partial_{s}K_{\mu\nu^{\prime}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})+(\Delta^{(1)})_{\mu}^{\nu}K_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=0

with the initial condition

\displaystyle K_{\mu\nu^{\prime}}(0,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=g_{\mu\nu^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x})\delta^{3}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}).

(A.9)

The partition function for gauge field can be calculated as

\displaystyle\log Z_{\mathrm{gauge}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{3}x\sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu^{\prime}}K_{\mu\nu^{\prime}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}).

(A.10)

For the traceless symmetric tensor field, the heat kernel is
K_{\mu\nu,\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
, which satisfies the differential equation

\displaystyle\partial_{s}K_{\mu\nu,\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})+(\Delta^{(2)})_{\mu\nu}^{\rho\sigma}K_{\rho\sigma,\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=0

with the initial condition

\displaystyle K_{\mu\nu,\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(0,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\left(g_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}g_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}+g_{\mu\nu^{\prime}}g_{\nu\mu^{\prime}}-\frac{2}{3}g_{\mu\nu}g_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\right)\delta^{3}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}).

(A.11)

Since the field is traceless, the heat kernel should be traceless as well

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu,\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=g^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}K_{\mu\nu,\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=0

(A.12)

The partition function for gauge field can be calculated as

\displaystyle\log Z_{\mathrm{traceless}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{3}x\sqrt{g}g^{\mu\mu^{\prime}}g^{\nu\nu^{\prime}}K_{\mu\nu,\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

(A.13)

In this method, the key point is to find the heat kernel in various manifold. We then show two examples of the manifold
\mathcal{M}
, which is helpful in this paper.

Hyperbolic Space

We use the following metric on Hyperbolic Space
\mathbb{H}_{3}

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{dy^{2}+d\xi d\bar{\xi}}{y^{2}},

(A.14)

where
y>0
and
\xi
is a complex coordinate. Since the hyperbolic space is maximally symmetric, we expect the heat kernel
K(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
just depends on
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
only through the length of geodesics

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\cosh^{-1}(1+u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})),

(A.15)

\displaystyle u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}{2yy^{\prime}}.

(A.16)

In terms of the length of geodesic, the Laplace operator becomes

\displaystyle\Delta=\partial_{\sigma}^{2}+2\coth\sigma\partial_{\sigma}.

(A.17)

In this case, it is straightforward to solve the differential equation (
A.6
) and obtain the heat kernel

\displaystyle K^{\mathbb{H}_{3}}(s,\sigma)=\frac{e^{-(m^{2}+1)s-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4s}}}{(4\pi s)^{3/2}}\frac{\sigma}{\sinh\sigma}.

(A.18)

The heat kernel for the
U(1)
gauge field and the traceless symmetric tensor field in
\mathbb{H}_{3}
also depends on the length of geodesics. The complete expression for the heat kernel is rather complicated, we prefer to write down the trace of these two heat kernels. For the
U(1)
gauge field, we have

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu^{\prime}}K_{\mu\nu^{\prime}}^{\mathbb{H}_{3}}(s,\sigma)=-3\left(F(t,\sigma)+\frac{1}{\sinh\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}S(t,\sigma)\right)

(A.19)

where

\displaystyle F(s,\sigma)

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4s}}}{(4\pi s)^{3/2}}\frac{\sigma}{\sinh\sigma},

(A.20)

\displaystyle S(s,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4s}}}{\sinh\sigma}\sqrt{s}\int_{0}^{1}d\xi e^{-s(1-\xi)^{2}}\sinh\sigma\xi.

(A.21)

For the traceless symmetric tensor field, the heat kernel becomes more complicated, which can be found in the literatures
[
63
,
64
]
. We do not write down the concrete expression here.

BTZ black hole

The BTZ black hole is described by the quotient space geometry of hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}_{3}/\mathbb{Z}
. The quotient space can be obtained by the identification

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}^{n}(\rho,\theta,t)\to(\rho,\theta+2\pi n,t+n\beta_{0}).

(A.22)

where the
\gamma_{0}
is the generators of discrete group of isometry. We also expect the heat kernel
K(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
just depends on the length of geodesics between
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
. The heat kernel on
\mathbb{H}_{3}/\mathbb{Z}
can be obtained from the one on
\mathbb{H}_{3}
by the method of images

\displaystyle K^{\mathbb{H}_{3}/\mathbb{Z}}(s,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}K^{\mathbb{H}_{3}}\left(s,\sigma_{n}\right),\quad\sigma_{n}\equiv\sigma(\mathbf{x},\gamma_{0}^{n}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))

(A.23)

Therefore, the heat kernel on
\mathbb{H}_{3}/\mathbb{Z}
is sum over all the geodesics winding arbitrary times around the quotient space. This method can also be used for the heat kernel for gauge field and traceless symmetric tensor field.

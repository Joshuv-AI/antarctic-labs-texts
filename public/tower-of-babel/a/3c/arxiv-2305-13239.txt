Abstract
We consider the performance of Glauber dynamics for the random cluster model with real parameter q > 1 q>1 and temperature β > 0 \beta>0 . Recent work by Helmuth, Jenssen and Perkins detailed the ordered/disordered transition of the model on random Δ \Delta -regular graphs for all sufficiently large q q and obtained an efficient sampling algorithm for all temperatures β \beta using cluster expansion methods. Despite this major progress, the performance of natural Markov chains, including Glauber dynamics, is not yet well understood on the random regular graph, partly because of the non-local nature of the model (especially at low temperatures) and partly because of severe bottleneck phenomena that emerge in a window around the ordered/disordered transition. Nevertheless, it is widely conjectured that the bottleneck phenomena that impede mixing from worst-case starting configurations can be avoided by initialising the chain more judiciously. Our main result establishes this conjecture for all sufficiently large q q (with respect to Δ \Delta ). Specifically, we consider the mixing time of Glauber dynamics initialised from the two extreme configurations, the all-in and all-out, and obtain a pair of fast mixing bounds which cover all temperatures β \beta , including in particular the bottleneck window. Our result is inspired by the recent approach of Gheissari and Sinclair for the Ising model who obtained a similar-flavoured mixing-time bound on the random regular graph for sufficiently low temperatures. To cover all temperatures in the RC model, we refine appropriately the structural results of Helmuth, Jenssen and Perkins about the ordered/disordered transition and show spatial mixing properties “within the phase”, which are then related to the evolution of the chain.

Introduction

Glauber dynamics is a well-studied Markov chain that is widely used to sample from spin systems such as the Ising and Potts models. A particularly appealing feature of the chain is that it is usually very simple to implement; yet, establishing whether convergence to the equilibrium distribution is fast turns out to be significantly harder, and typically requires an in-depth understanding of the underlying distribution. Here, we focus on studying the performance of Glauber dynamics for the random-cluster representation of the classical Potts model on the random regular graph, where the behaviour of the chain is underpinned by various phenomena that distinguish it from classical spin systems and pose new challenges in the analysis.

We begin with a few definitions. For real numbers
q,\beta>0
and a graph

, the
random cluster model
on
G
with parameters
q
and
\beta

\mathcal{F}:E\rightarrow\{0,1\}

\mathcal{F}
, we say that edges mapped to
1

are

to denote the set of edges

\Out(\mathcal{F})

\mathcal{F}(e)=0

|\mathcal{F}|

For integer values of

We will be primarily interested in sampling from the so-called

Our goal is to obtain a fast algorithm for the RC model using Glauber dynamics on the random regular graph. There are two key obstacles that arise, especially at low temperatures (large

The random regular graph is a particularly interesting testbed in this front since it exhibits all the relevant phase transition phenomena and has also been used as the main gadget in hardness reductions
[
14

Sketch of proof of Theorem

Let
\Delta\geq 5
be an integer.
Consider any sufficiently large
q
and any

\beta\geq\beta_{c}
.
For sufficiently large

, choose a “radius”

and let

.
Fix a vertex

.
We wish to show, with sufficiently high probability, that
\|{\pi_{\mathcal{B}_{r}^{+}(v)}(e\mapsto\cdot)-\pi^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G}(e\mapsto\cdot)}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq{1}/{(100|E|)}
.

Our goal is essentially to
construct a coupling of
\mathcal{F}^{+}\sim\pi_{\mathcal{B}^{+}_{r}(v)}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{ord}}\sim\pi^{\mathrm{ord}}
, such that
\Pr(\mathcal{F}^{+}(e)\neq\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{ord}}(e))
is sufficiently small. In order to construct the coupling, we take advantage of the fact that
G[B_{r}(v)]
is locally tree-like. In fact, we identify a suitable subgraph of
G[B_{r}(v)]
without cycles and restrict the coupling to this subgraph.

Consider a breadth-first search from
v
in
G[B_{r}(v)]
.
Let
T_{0}
be the rooted tree consisting of all forward edges in this breadth-first search.
All other edges in
B_{r}(v)
are called “excess edges”.
W.h.p., since
G\sim\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}
, there are at most
K
excess edges in
B_{r}(v)
for some absolute constant
K>0
. In particular,
since
G
is locally tree-like,
we can identify integers
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
satisfying
r\geq r_{1}>r_{2}\geq 0
such that

E(B_{r_{1}}(v))\setminus E(B_{r_{2}}(v))
contains no excess edges and
r_{1}-r_{2}\geq r/(2K)=\Omega(r)
.
The fact that
r_{1}-r_{2}=\Omega(r)
ensures that

B_{r_{1}}(v)\setminus B_{r_{2}}(v)
is a sufficiently large subgraph of
G
, and the coupling focuses on this subgraph.

In order to describe the coupling process we need a small amount of notation.
A
partial configuration

\mathcal{F}
is a map from the edges of
G
to the set
\{0,1,*\}
.
In-edges and out-edges (those that are mapped to
1

0
) are “revealed” and edges that are mapped to

A refinement of a partial configuration is obtained by revealing more edges.
We use
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
to denote the fact that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
refines
\mathcal{F}
.

In the coupling, we generate a sequence of edge subsets
F_{0}\subseteq F_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq E

such that, after iteration
i
, the edges in
F_{i}
are revealed.
We also construct two sequences of partial configurations

\mathcal{F}_{0}^{+}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{1}^{+}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{+}
and

\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\mathrm{ord}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{1}^{\mathrm{ord}}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{ord}}
, maintaining the invariant that the revealed edges in
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\mathrm{ord}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{+}
are exactly the edges in
F_{i}
.
The coupling will have the crucial property that
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{ord}}\sim\pi^{\mathrm{ord}}
and
\mathcal{F}^{+}\sim\pi_{\mathcal{B}_{r_{1}}^{+}(v)}

•

The process starts with iteration
i=0
. The initial set
F_{0}
of revealed edges is all edges except those in
E(B_{r_{1}}(v))
.
In
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\mathrm{ord}}
these revealed edges are sampled from the projection
\pi^{\mathrm{ord}}_{F_{0}}
of
\pi^{\mathrm{ord}}
onto
F_{0}
.
It is likely that the configuration
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\mathrm{ord}}
has at least
(1-\eta)|E|
in-edges. If not,
then the coupling terminates (unsuccessfully), generating
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{ord}}
and
\mathcal{F}^{+}
from the right distributions. We will show that the probability of this unsuccessful termination is low.
On the other hand, if
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\mathrm{ord}}
has a least
(1-\eta)|E|
in-edges, then we are off to a good start.
All configurations refining
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\mathrm{ord}}
are in
\Omega^{\mathrm{ord}}
, so the projection of
\pi
and
\pi^{\mathrm{ord}}

At this point
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{+}
is taken to be the configuration with revealed edges

where all revealed edges are in-edges.

•

After iteration
i=0

until an edge is revealed whose distance from

is at most

or until the in-edges in
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\mathrm{ord}}
induce a giant component, and this giant component contains all vertices on the boundary of

\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\mathrm{ord}}
contains all vertices on the boundary of

.
This is a good situation because the conditional distribution of

\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\mathrm{ord}}
and the conditional distribution of
\mathcal{F}^{+}

\mathcal{F}_{i}^{+}

\Pr(\mathcal{F}^{+}(e)\neq\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{ord}}(e))
is sufficiently small.

•

The process at iteration

is as follows.

W_{i}
is taken to be the set of all vertices on the boundary of
F_{i}
whose components
(induced by the in-edges in
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\mathrm{ord}}
) are all small.
By “boundary” we mean that vertices in
W_{i}
are adjacent to revealed edges, and to unrevealed edges.
If
W_{i}

be generated by the process. Then at least one of the following conditions holds.

\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{ord}}
and
\mathcal{F}^{+}
agree on the edges that are incident to
v
.

2.

|\In(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{ord}})\setminus E(B_{r_{1}}(v))|<(1-\eta)|E|
.

3.

\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{ord}}
contains a polymer of size at least
\frac{r}{400\Delta(1+K)}-1
.

To complete the proof of Theorem
5
, we show that items 2 and 3 are unlikely.
The proof that item 2 is unlikely, Lemma
45
, follows from the slack specified in Equation (
2
) of Lemma
3
.
The proof that item 3 is unlikely, Lemma
28
, follows from an analysis on the size of polymers by adapting appropriately the cluster expansion techniques of
[
21
]
(a bit of extra work is needed there to capture the
1/\beta
dependence in the size of the polymer, see Lemma
28
).

The proof of WSM for the disordered phase (Theorem
6
) follows a similar strategy but the details are substantially simpler (due to a more straightforward notion of polymers), the argument can be found in Section
6
.

4.1
The RC model on expanders in the ordered regime

We use the following Lemma from
[

]
.

Lemma 22

(
[
21
, Lemma 8]
)
.

Let
\Delta\geq 5
,
\delta\in(0,1/2)
, and
\zeta\in(0,1/2)
be reals. Suppose that
G=(V,E)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Delta,\delta}
satisfies
|V|\geq 360/(\zeta\delta)
and

\zeta|E|\leq|A|\leq(1-\zeta)|E|
. Then

{c(A)}/{|V|}+{|A|}/{|E|}\leq 1-\zeta/40

where

is the number of connected components in

.

Remark 23
.

[
21
, Lemma 8]
is proved for the special case of
\zeta=\min(1/100,\delta/5)
, however the proof works for any positive
\zeta<1/2
.

Lemma 24

(
[
21
, Theorems 1 and 2]
)
.

Let
\Delta\geq 5
be an integer. Fix
\delta\in(0,1/2)
satisfying Lemma
16
. Let
\eta=\min\{\delta/5,1/100\}
.
Let
q_{0}\geq e^{21\Delta/\eta}
be sufficiently large that
Theorems 1 and 2 of
[
21
]
hold. There is a positive number
\zeta<\eta
such that
w.h.p. for
G\sim\mathcal{G}_{n,\Delta}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mbox{for $\beta\geq\beta_{c}$},

\displaystyle\pi_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}}\Big(|\In(\mathcal{F})|\leq(1-\zeta)|E|\Big)=\mathrm{e}^{-\Omega(n)},

\displaystyle\mbox{for $\beta\leq\beta_{c}$},

\displaystyle\pi_{G}^{\mathrm{dis}}\Big(|\In(\mathcal{F})|\geq\zeta|E|\Big)=\mathrm{e}^{-\Omega(n)}.

Proof.

We prove the first bound, the second is similar.
Choose
\zeta=20\Delta/\log(q_{0})
. Note that
\zeta<\eta
, as desired.
Let
\Omega^{\prime}=\{\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^{\mathrm{ord}}:|\In(\mathcal{F})|\leq(1-\zeta)|E|\}
and let

Z^{\prime}=\sum_{\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^{\prime}}w_{G}(\mathcal{F})=\sum_{\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^{\prime}}q^{c(\mathcal{F})}(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{|\In(\mathcal{F})|}.

Let
z=\max\{q,(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{\Delta/2}\}
. Since
Z\geq z^{n}
every
\mathcal{F}\in\Omega
has

w_{G}(\mathcal{F})/Z\leq z^{c(\mathcal{F})+2|\In(\mathcal{F})|/\Delta-n}=z^{n(c(\mathcal{F})/n+|\In(\mathcal{F})|/|E|-1)}.

Since
\zeta<\eta<1/2
, for any

\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^{\prime}
,

\zeta|E|\leq(1-\eta)|E|\leq|\In(\mathcal{F})|\leq(1-\zeta)|E|
.
By Lemma
16
, w.h.p.,
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\Delta,\delta}
.
By Lemma
22
, for
n\geq 360/(\zeta\delta)
, we have

c(\mathcal{F})/n+|\In(\mathcal{F})|/|E|\leq 1-\zeta/40
so

Z^{\prime}/Z\leq 2^{n\Delta/2}z^{n(-\zeta/40)}
, which gives

Z^{\prime}/Z=\mathrm{e}^{-\Omega(n)}
since

q>\mathrm{e}^{20\Delta/\zeta}
.
To finish, we will show that
Z/Z^{\mathrm{ord}}=O(1)
, so
that
\frac{Z^{\prime}}{Z^{\mathrm{ord}}}=\frac{Z^{\prime}}{Z}\times\frac{Z}{Z^{\mathrm{ord}}}=\mathrm{e}^{-\Omega(n)}O(1)=\mathrm{e}^{-\Omega(n)}
.
If
\beta>\beta_{c}
, item 3 of
[
21
, Theorem 1]
says that there are positive numbers
n_{0}
and
\xi
such that, for
n\geq n_{0}
,

(1/n)\log((Z-Z^{\mathrm{ord}})/Z)\leq-\xi
.
So for
n\geq n_{0}
,
Z^{\mathrm{ord}}/Z\geq 1-e^{-\xi n}
, which is at least
1/2
for
n\geq 1/\xi
.
If
\beta=\beta_{c}
,
then items 1 and 3 of
[
21
, Theorem 2]
show that

Z^{\mathrm{ord}}/Z
converges in distribution to a random variable
Q/(Q+1)
where (say)
Q\geq q/2
for
q\geq q_{0}
. So for sufficiently large
q
,
Z^{\mathrm{ord}}/Z=O(1)
.
∎

4.2
Disordered polymers

We will use
disordered polymers
from
[
21
, Section 2.3]
.
Given a graph
G=(V,E)
and a configuration
\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^{\mathrm{dis}}
, a
disordered polymer
of
\mathcal{F}
is a connected component of
G[\In(\mathcal{F})]
.
Since
\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^{\mathrm{dis}}
, each such connected component contains at most
\eta|E|
edges.

Each disordered polymer
\gamma=(V(\gamma),E(\gamma))
has an associated weight given by
w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{dis}}:=q^{1-|V(\gamma)|}(e^{\beta}-1)^{|E(\gamma)|}
. We observe the following relation between the weight of a disordered configuration and the weights of its polymers.

Observation 25
.

For
\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^{\mathrm{ord}}
, let
\Gamma^{\mathrm{dis}}(\mathcal{F})
be the set of disordered polymers of
\mathcal{F}
. Then

w_{G}(\mathcal{F})=q^{n}\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{dis}}(\mathcal{F})}w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{dis}}.

This observation follows from the fact each vertex of
G
is contained in some polymer of
\mathcal{F}
, each in-edge of
\mathcal{F}
is an edge of some polymer of
\mathcal{F}
, and each polymer of
\mathcal{F}
corresponds to one component of
G[\In(\mathcal{F})]
.

Based on the work by Helmuth, Jenssen and Perkins
[
21
, Proposition 11]
, we can upper bound the probability that
\mathcal{F}\sim\pi^{\mathrm{dis}}
contains a large polymer.

Lemma 26
.

Let
\Delta\geq 5
and
K\geq 0
be integers,
\delta\in(0,1/2)
be real. Let
C
be a constant. Then, for all sufficiently large
q
, the following holds for all
\beta\leq\log(q^{2.1/\Delta}+1)
. Let
n
be sufficiently large. For
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\Delta,\delta,K}
with
n=|V(G)|
, for
s:=C\log n
,

\pi^{\mathrm{dis}}_{G}\big(\mbox{$\mathcal{F}$ contains a polymer with at least $s$ edges}\big)\leq n^{-3/2}/2.

Proof.

Let
q
and
n
be large enough so that Proposition 11 from
[
21
]
applies, and so in particular
[
21
, Equation (13)]
holds, and also that
s>1
and
q\geq e^{4\Delta(\frac{5}{2C}-1)}
.

For a disordered configuration
\mathcal{F}
, let
\Gamma^{\mathrm{dis}}(\mathcal{F})
be the set of disordered polymers of
\mathcal{F}
.

First we will bound, for a particular polymer
\gamma
with
|E(\gamma)|\geq 1
,
\Pr(\gamma\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{dis}}(.))
. Suppose
\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^{\mathrm{dis}}
has
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{dis}}(\mathcal{F})
. By Observation
25
,

w_{G}(\mathcal{F})=q^{n}\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{dis}}(\mathcal{F})}w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{dis}}
.
Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be the (disordered) configuration defined by
\In(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\In(\mathcal{F})\backslash E(\gamma)
. Since
E(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
,
\mathcal{F}\neq\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. By construction,

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\mathrm{dis}}
. Since vertices in
\gamma
are not incident to edges in
\In(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
,
\Gamma^{\mathrm{dis}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=(\Gamma^{\mathrm{dis}}(\mathcal{F})\setminus\{\gamma\})\cup_{v\in\gamma}\{v\}
.
A polymer
\gamma^{\prime}
consisting of a single vertex has weight
w_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{dis}}=1
.
Thus,
w_{G}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=w_{G}(\mathcal{F})/w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{dis}}
.
Let
\Omega_{\gamma}
be the set of all disordered configurations containing
\gamma
as a polymer.
Then

Z^{\mathrm{dis}}\geq\sum_{\mathcal{F}\in\Omega_{\gamma}}(w_{G}(\mathcal{F})+w_{G}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))=(1+1/w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{dis}})\sum_{\mathcal{F}\in\Omega_{\gamma}}w_{G}(\mathcal{F})
.
Thus, the probability that
\mathcal{F}\sim\pi^{\mathrm{dis}}
contains the polymer
\gamma
is at most
\frac{1}{1+1/w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{dis}}}\leq w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{dis}}
.

Next, for
v\in V(G)

let
p_{v}
be the probability that
\mathcal{F}\sim\pi_{G}^{\mathrm{dis}}
has a polymer with at least
s
edges containing the vertex
v
vertex
v
. By a union bound,
p_{v}\leq P_{v}:=\sum_{\gamma\ni v:|E(\gamma)|\geq s}w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{dis}}
.
Let
\rho=\log(q)/(4\Delta)
.
By
[
21
, Equation (13)]
,

\sum_{\gamma\ni v:|\gamma|>1}e^{(1+\rho)|E(\gamma)|}w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{dis}}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}.

Thus also

e^{(1+\rho)s}P_{v}=e^{(1+\rho)s}\sum_{\gamma\ni v:|E(\gamma)|\geq s}w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{dis}}\leq\sum_{\gamma\ni:|E(\gamma)|\geq s}e^{(1+\rho)|E(\gamma)|}w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{dis}}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}.

Hence
P_{v}\leq(1/2)e^{-(1+\rho)s}
. By a union bound over all vertices, the probability in the statement of the lemma is at most
\sum_{v}P_{v}\leq(n/2)2^{-(1+\rho)s}=(1/2)n^{1-C(1+\rho)}
. The lemma follows by requiring
q
to be sufficiently large with respect to
\Delta
and
C
that
C(1+\rho)-1\geq 3/2
.

∎

4.3
Ordered polymers

We use the definition of
ordered polymers
from
[
21
, Section 2.4.2]
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph of max degree
\Delta
with
n=|V(G)|
and
m=|E(G)|
. For a set of edges
A\subseteq E
, Let
\mathcal{B}_{0}(A)=A
, and inductively for
i=0,1,2,\dots
define
\mathcal{B}_{i+1}(A)
to be the set of all edges such that they are either in
\mathcal{B}_{i}(A)
or edges that are incident to a vertex that has at least
{5\Delta}/{9}
incident edges in
\mathcal{B}_{i}(A)
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(A)=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{B}_{i}(A)
. It is shown in
[
21
, Lemma 12]
that
|\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(A)|\leq 10|\mathcal{B}_{0}(A)|
.

An ordered polymer is a connected subgraph
\gamma=(V(\gamma),E(\gamma))
of
G
together with an edge configuration
\ell:E(\gamma)\rightarrow\{0,1\}
subject to: (i)
\Out(\ell)\leq\eta m
, and (ii)
\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\Out(\ell))=E(\gamma)
. We let
E_{u}(\gamma)
be the set of unoccupied edges
\Out(\ell)
of the polymer and
c^{\prime}(\gamma)
be the number of components in the graph
G[E\backslash E_{u}(\gamma)]
with fewer than
n/2
vertices. The size of
\gamma
is defined as
|E_{\gamma}|
, whereas the weight of
\gamma
is defined as
w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ord}}:=q^{c^{\prime}(\gamma)}(e^{\beta}-1)^{-E_{u}(\gamma)}
.

Given a (partial) configuration
\mathcal{F}
, the set of ordered polymers of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted by
\Gamma(\mathcal{F})
, consists of the connected components of
G[\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\Out(\mathcal{F}))]
, each with the labelling on the edges induced by the corresponding assignment in
\mathcal{F}
.

We will use a couple of polymer properties from
[
21
]
. First, we note the following connection between the weight of a configuration
\mathcal{F}
to the weight of its polymers
\Gamma(\mathcal{F})
.

Lemma 27
.

Fix
\Delta\geq 5
,
K\geq 0
and
\delta\in(0,1/2)
. Let
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\Delta,\delta,K}
and
\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^{\mathrm{ord}}
. Then

w_{G}(\mathcal{F})=q(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{|E|}\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathcal{F})}w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ord}}
.

Proof.

Note that
\Out(\mathcal{F})=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathcal{F})}E_{u}(\gamma)
, so
(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{|E|-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathcal{F})}|E_{u}(\gamma)|}=(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{|\In(\mathcal{F})|}.

Hence, by
[
21
, Lemma 21]
,
q^{\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathcal{F})}c^{\prime}(\gamma)}=q^{c(G[\In(\mathcal{F})])-1}
, so the result follows.
∎

Lemma 28
.

Let
\Delta\geq 5
and
K\geq 0
be integers,
\delta\in(0,1/2)
be real. Then, for all sufficiently large
q
, the following holds for all
\beta\geq\log(q^{1.9/\Delta}+1)
. For
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\Delta,\delta,K}
with
n=|V(G)|
, for
s:={2000}\log(n)/\beta
,

\pi^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G}\big(\mbox{$\mathcal{F}$ contains a polymer with size at least $s$}\big)\leq 2n^{-3/2}.

Proof.

This proof closely follows the proof of Proposition 15 from
[
21
]
.
Suppose that
q
is large enough so that
q^{1.9/\Delta}\geq(2\mathrm{e}\Delta)^{400}
and fix arbitrary
\beta\geq\log(q^{1.9/\Delta}+1)
.

Consider an arbitrary ordered polymer
\gamma
. For any configuration
\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^{\mathrm{ord}}
with
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathcal{F})
, by
Lemma
27
, it holds that
w_{G}(\mathcal{F})\leq q(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{|E|}w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ord}}
.
Note that for any
\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^{\mathrm{ord}}
such that
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathcal{F})
, there is a configuration
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
with
\Gamma(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\Gamma(\mathcal{F})\setminus\{\gamma\}
(obtained by setting
\In(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\In(\mathcal{F})\cup E_{u}(\gamma)
), which therefore has weight
w_{G}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=w_{G}(\mathcal{F})/w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ord}}
.

Let
\Omega_{\gamma}
be the set of all ordered configurations with a polymer
\gamma
. We can lower bound
Z^{\mathrm{ord}}
by
\sum_{\mathcal{F}\in\Omega_{\gamma}}(w_{G}(\mathcal{F})+w_{G}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))=(1+\frac{1}{w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ord}}})\sum_{\mathcal{F}\in\Omega_{\gamma}}w_{G}(\mathcal{F})
. Therefore,

\pi^{\mathrm{ord}}(\gamma\in\Gamma(\cdot))=\frac{\sum_{\mathcal{F}\in\Omega_{\gamma}}w_{G}(\mathcal{F})}{Z^{\mathrm{ord}}}\leq\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ord}}}}\leq w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ord}},

so we conclude that the probability that any fixed polymer
\gamma
is a polymer of
\mathcal{F}\sim\pi^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G}
is at most
w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ord}}
.

Let
u
be a vertex in
V(G)
and denote by
\Gamma_{u}
the set of polymers containing
u
. Let
p_{u}
be the probability that
u
is contained in a polymer of size at least
s
for
\mathcal{F}\sim\pi^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G}
. By a union bound over the polymers in
\Gamma_{u}
,
We have that
p_{u}\leq P_{u}
, where

P_{u}:=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{u};\ |E(\gamma)|\geq s}w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ord}}

By
[
21
, Lemma 14]
,

c^{\prime}(\gamma)\leq 9|E_{u}(\gamma)|/(5\Delta)
. The bound on
\beta
ensures that

w_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ord}}=q^{c^{\prime}(\gamma)}(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{-|E_{u}(\gamma)|}\leq(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{(\frac{9}{5\Delta}\cdot\frac{\Delta}{1.9}-1)|E_{u}(\gamma)|}\leq(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{-|E_{u}(\gamma)|/20}.

Hence we get

P_{u}\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{u};\ |E(\gamma)|\geq s}(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{-|E_{u}(\gamma)|/20}.

By
[
21
, Lemma 12]
, for any ordered polymer
\gamma
,
|E(\gamma)|\leq 10|E_{u}(\gamma)|
. Thus

P_{u}\leq\sum_{k\geq s/10}\ \ \sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{u};\ |E_{u}(\gamma)|=k}(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{-|E_{u}(\gamma)|/20}.

By
[
21
, Proof of Proposition 15]
, the number of polymers with
k
unoccupied edges containing a particular vertex is at most
(2\mathrm{e}\Delta)^{10k}
, hence

P_{u}\leq\sum_{k\geq s/10}\big((2\mathrm{e}\Delta)^{10}(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{-1/20}\big)^{k}.

By the lower bounds on
\beta
and
q
, we have
\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1\geq q^{1.9/\Delta}\geq(2\mathrm{e}\Delta)^{400}
so

P_{u}\leq\sum_{k\geq s/10}(\mathrm{e}^{\beta}-1)^{-k/40}\leq\sum_{k\geq s/10}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta k/80}.

The last sum is a geometric series with ratio
\mathrm{e}^{-\beta/80}<1/2
, so it is upper bounded by twice its largest term. Since
s=\tfrac{2000}{\beta}\log n
, it follows that
P_{u}\leq 2/n^{5/2}
.
By a union bound over the vertices of
G
, the probability that there is a polymer of
\mathcal{F}\sim\pi^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G}
which has size at least
s
is therefore at most
2/n^{3/2}
, as required.
∎

Furthermore, we can prove the following lemma based on the proof of
[

Lemma 29

Fix
\Delta\geq 5
,

and

Let

For

\kappa^{\prime}

\mathcal{F}

\kappa^{\prime}

We start with the proof of Item

Consider a non-giant component
\kappa^{\prime}
of

be the set of vertices in
\kappa^{\prime}

\mathcal{F}

For contradiction, assume that
\tau

By Corollary

|\kappa^{\prime}|\leq\delta|V|

The definition of
\tau

is in
\tau
.

Before proving Item

\kappa^{\prime}

\tau
be the set of vertices in

that contain vertices in

, proving Item

.

To finish, we prove Item

.
First we bound the size of
\kappa^{\prime}
. Since
\mathcal{F}\in\Omega^{\mathrm{ord}}
,

.
Since

,

is at most

. Thus,
|\tau|\leq\delta n+10|S|/\Delta

Next let

and let

be a subset of

Then

\displaystyle|E(\tau,V\backslash\tau)|

\displaystyle\geq|E(U,V\setminus\tau)|

\displaystyle\geq|E(U,V\setminus U)|-|E(U,\tau\setminus U)|

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{5}{9}\Delta|U|-\rho\Delta=\tfrac{5}{9}\Delta|\tau|-14\rho\Delta/9.

By the definition of

\mathcal{F}
.
Since

is a component of

,
there are at most
|S|
edges in
\In(\mathcal{F})
between
\tau

and

.
Thus the number of edges in

that are in polymers is at least
|E(\tau,V\backslash\tau)|-|S|

This is at least

\tfrac{5}{9}\Delta|\tau|-14\rho\Delta/9-|S|\geq\tfrac{5}{9}\Delta|\tau|-149|S|/9-14\Delta/9\geq\tfrac{5}{9}\Delta|\tau|-45\Delta|S|/9

where the last inequality follows from

,
|S|\geq 1
.

Averaged over all vertices

the number of edges in

that are in polymers and
are incident to
w

is at least
\tfrac{5}{9}\Delta-45\Delta|S|/(9\tau)
.
We may assume

is non-empty, otherwise Item
2
directly follows.
Thus, there is a vertex

such that the number of edges in
E(\tau,V\setminus\tau)
that are in polymers and
are incident to
w

is at least
\tfrac{5}{9}\Delta-45\Delta|S|/(9\tau)
.
Since this number is an integer, the number of edges in

that are in polymers and
are incident to

is at least

Suppose for contradiction that

is less than

. On the other hand, if the first term,

is not an integer, then its non-integer part is at least

Either way,

5.2
The coupling process

From now on, we assume that

to couple edges incident to

, and two sequences of partial configurations

get revealed. It will also hold, for each

To facilitate this process of revealing the edges, the following definitions will be helpful.

7.1
The log-Sobolev constant and mixing time inequality

Let
\mu
be a distribution supported on a set
\Omega
. For a function
g:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, let

\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu}[g]:=\mathbf{E}_{\mu}[g\log g]-\mathbf{E}_{\mu}[g]\log\mathbf{E}_{\mu}[\log g],

with the convention
0\log 0:=0
. Moreover, for a reversible Markov chain
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\Omega
with transition matrix
P\in\mathbb{R}^{\Omega\times\Omega}_{\geq 0}
, let
\mathcal{E}(g,g):=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\omega,\omega^{\prime}}\mu(\omega)P(\omega,\omega^{\prime})(g(\omega)-g(\omega^{\prime}))^{2}
be the Dirichlet form for
P
. The standard log-Sobolev constant of the chain is then defined as

\alpha(P):=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}g:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\\
\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu}[g]\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sqrt{g},\sqrt{g})}{\mathrm{Ent}_{\mu}[g]}.

The following well-known connection between the log-Sobolev constant and the distance from stationarity can be found, e.g., in
[
4
, Fact 6.1]
. Let
\mu_{\min}=\min_{\omega\in\Omega}.\mu(\omega)
. Then, for any
\gamma<\alpha(P)
, it holds that

\mbox{$\max_{X_{0}}$}\,\mathrm{dist}_{\mathrm{TV}}(X_{t},\mu)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma t/2}\big(\log\tfrac{1}{\mu_{\min}}\big)^{1/2}.

(8)

Appendix A
Mixing on tree-like graphs for the wired RC dynamics

In this section, we prove Lemma
9
which we restate here for convenience.

Lemma
9
.

Let
\Delta\geq 3
be an integer, and
q,K>1
,
\beta>0
be reals. There exists
\hat{C}>0
such that the following holds for every
\Delta
-regular graph
G=(V,E)
and any integer
r\geq 1
.

Suppose that
\rho\in V
is such that
G[B_{r}(\rho)]
is
K
-treelike. Then, with
n=|B_{r}(\rho)|
, the mixing time of the wired RC dynamics on
B_{r}(\rho)
is
\leq\hat{C}n^{3}(q^{4}\mathrm{e}^{\beta})^{\Delta r}
.

Proof.

We use a canonical paths argument, following largely
[
25
, Proof of Lemma 8]
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a
\Delta
-regular graph and
\rho\in V
be such that
G[B_{r}(\rho)]
is
K
-treelike. Let
n=|B_{r}(\rho)|
,
m=|E(B_{r}(\rho)|
. Let
\hat{\pi}=\hat{\pi}_{B_{r}(\rho)}
be the stationary distribution of the wired RC dynamics on
B_{r}(\rho)
.

Consider a BFS tree
T
for
G[B_{r}(\rho)]
starting from
\rho
, let
L
be the set of the leaves of
T
and let
\mathcal{E}=E\backslash E(T)
be the set of excess edges. Note that the height of
T
is at most
r
,
S_{r}(\rho)\subseteq L
and there are at most
2K
leaves of
T
at depth less than
r
(by the
\Delta
-regularity of
G
, each such leaf must be incident to an excess edge of
G
), so
|L\backslash S_{r}(\rho)|\leq 2K
. For a vertex
u
, we let
T_{u}
be the subtree of
T
rooted at
u
, and
\mathrm{Anc}(u)
be the ancestors of
u
in
T
(i.e., the vertices on the path from the root
\rho
to
u
, including
u
).

Consider next a depth-first-search traversal of the tree
T
starting from
\rho
, and let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
be the order in which the vertices of
T
were first visited. We write
v_{i}<v_{j}
whenever
i<j
; moreover, for a subset of vertices
S
and a vertex
u
, we write
S<u
to denote that for each
w\in S
it holds that
w<u
. Since the tree
T
has depth
r
, at any stage of the DFS traversal there are at most
r
vertices which are visited but not fully explored (note that a leaf that is visited is automatically explored). Since the degree of any vertex is
\leq\Delta
, it follows that

(a)

for
i=1,\ldots,n
, there are at most
\Delta r
tree edges with one endpoint in
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\}
and the other in
\{v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
(from the DFS traversal).

(b)

for an arbitrary vertex
u
in
T
and any vertex
w
which is not an ancestor of
u
(i.e.,
w\notin\mathrm{Anc}(u)
), we have from the DFS traversal either that
V(T_{w})<u
or
V(T_{w})>u
.

We order the edges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}

in
G[\mathcal{B}_{r}(\rho)]
(including excess edges)
in lexicographic order in terms of the order of the vertices. So edges with an endpoint
v_{1}
come first (ordered by the other endpoint) and so on.

For two configurations
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:E\rightarrow\{0,1\}
that differ on the edges
e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k}}
(with
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}
), define the path
\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
of configurations
\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\mathcal{F}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{1}\cdots\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{k}
by defining, for
j=0,\ldots,k
,
\mathcal{F}_{j}
to agree with
\mathcal{F}
on the edges
\{e_{i_{j+1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k}}\}
and to agree with
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
on the edges
\{e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{j}}\}
. Note that
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}_{k}=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and both
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and hence
\mathcal{F}_{j}
as well agree on
E\backslash\{e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k}}\})
. Then, the relaxation time of the chain, see for example
[
23
, Chapter 5]
, is bounded by

\tau\leq m\max_{(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}:E\rightarrow\{0,1\};\\
(\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\end{subarray}}\frac{\hat{\pi}(\mathcal{A})\hat{\pi}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})}{\hat{\pi}(\mathcal{F})\Pr(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})},

where the maximum is over all pairs of configurations
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
that differ on a single edge and the summation is over all pairs of configurations
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
whose corresponding
\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
includes the transition
\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
as part of the path.

Consider an arbitrary pair of configurations
\kappa=(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
that differ at a single edge
f=\{v_{j},v_{j^{\prime}}\}
for some
j<j^{\prime}
and suppose further that
f=e_{\ell}
.
For configurations
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
such that
(\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
define the configuration
g_{\kappa}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})

g_{\kappa}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})

\mathcal{A}
on
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-1}\}
and with
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}

\{e_{\ell},\ldots,e_{m}\}

(13)

From the fact that
(\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, we also have that:

\mbox{$\mathcal{A}$ agrees with $\mathcal{F}$ on $\{e_{\ell},\ldots,e_{m}\}$},\ \ \mbox{$\mathcal{A}^{\prime}$ agrees with $\mathcal{F}^{\prime}$ on $\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-1}\}$.}

(14)

It follows that the map
g_{\kappa}(\cdot,\cdot)
is injective, i.e., given its value
\mathcal{F}^{*}
and the configurations
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, there is a unique pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
such that
\mathcal{F}^{*}=g_{\kappa}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
and
(\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})

For a configuration
\mathcal{X}:E(B_{r}(\rho))\rightarrow\{0,1\}

denote the number of components in

that do not include any of the vertices in

, cf. Footnote
5
.
Let

and

.
The main step in the proof is to show that:

\displaystyle|\In(\mathcal{A})|+|\In(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})|-|\In(\mathcal{F})|-|\In(g_{\kappa}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}))|\leq s,

(15)

\displaystyle|\hat{c}(\mathcal{A})+\hat{c}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})-\hat{c}(\mathcal{F})-\hat{c}(g_{\kappa}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}))|\leq t.

(16)

Assuming these for the moment, we have that

\frac{\hat{\pi}(\mathcal{A})\hat{\pi}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})}{\hat{\pi}(\mathcal{F})\hat{\pi}\big(g_{\kappa}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\big)}\leq q^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\beta s}.

Recall that
p=1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}

and that for

it holds that
\hat{p}\in(p/q,p)
. Therefore,
for the RC dynamics, we have that
\Pr(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\geq\frac{\min\{p/q,(1-p)\}}{m}\geq(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta})/(qm\mathrm{e}^{\beta})

.
Using this and the injectivity of

\tau\leq\frac{m^{2}q^{t+1}\mathrm{e}^{\beta(s+1)}}{(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}:E\rightarrow\{0,1\};\\

The mixing time is at most

\tau(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\log(\min_{\mathcal{X}}\hat{\pi}(\mathcal{X})^{-1}))

Since

\min_{\mathcal{X}}\hat{\pi}(\mathcal{X})\geq 2^{-n}q^{-n}e^{-\beta m}

,

n^{3}\Delta^{2}q^{t+1}\mathrm{e}^{\beta(s+1)}\tfrac{1+\log(q)+\beta\Delta}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}

so

\hat{C}:=2\Delta^{3}(q\mathrm{e}^{\beta})^{12K+3}/{(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta})}

It remains to show (
15
) and (
16
). For convenience let
\mathcal{F}^{*}=g_{\kappa}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
.
We will shortly prove the following facts for an arbitrary edge
e=\{u,w\}

1.

if
\mathcal{A}(e)=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(e)
, then
\mathcal{A}(e)=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(e)=\mathcal{F}(e)=\mathcal{F}^{*}(e)
.

2.

if
\mathcal{A}(e)\neq\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(e)

\big(\mathcal{A}(e),\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(e)\big)=\big(\mathcal{F}^{*}(e),\mathcal{F}(e)\big)
when

and
\big(\mathcal{A}(e),\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(e)\big)=\big(\mathcal{F}(e),\mathcal{F}^{*}(e)\big)

Indeed, for Item

e
with
\mathcal{A}(e)=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(e)

and suppose first that
\mathcal{A}(e)=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(e)=1

\mathcal{F}(e)=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(e)=1

\mathcal{F}^{*}(e)=1

on the edges where the latter agree). The proof for the second case

13

14

e=\{u,w\}<e_{\ell}
, while
u,w>v_{j}
gives that
e>e_{\ell}
.

Let
W_{j}
be the tree edges which are incident to a vertex in
\mathrm{Anc}(v_{j})

|W_{j}|\leq\Delta r
.
From Item (
a
), these are the only tree edges that can have one endpoint in
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{j}\}
and the other among

\mathcal{E}

1
and
2

\mathcal{A}(e)+\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(e)=\mathcal{F}(e)+\mathcal{F}^{*}(e),

(17)

which establishes (
15

To show (
16

To show this, first note that
\hat{\mathcal{A}},\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime},\hat{\mathcal{F}},\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{*}

and, further, for any edge

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}\{v\in V^{*}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})\}+\mathbf{1}\{v\in V^{*}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})\}=\mathbf{1}\{v\in V^{*}(\hat{\mathcal{F}})\}+\mathbf{1}\{v\in V^{*}(\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{*})\}.

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}\{e\in E^{*}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})\}+\mathbf{1}\{e\in E^{*}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})\}=\mathbf{1}\{e\in E^{*}(\hat{\mathcal{F}})\}+\mathbf{1}\{e\in E^{*}(\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{*})\}.

) is true for any

for each
\mathcal{X}\in\{\hat{\mathcal{A}},\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime},\hat{\mathcal{F}},\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{*}\}
by construction of the tweaked configurations. So, consider

.

T_{u}
. Denote by

the set of all such paths.

, we have that

and that

is not ancestor of
v_{j}

. It follows from (

) that for every edge

we have

, so

, proving (

) for

. An analogous argument applies when

and, using (

) again,
v\in V^{*}(\hat{\mathcal{A}})\Leftrightarrow v\in V^{*}(\hat{\mathcal{F}})
,
v\in V^{*}(\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})\Leftrightarrow v\in V^{*}(\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{*})
, finishing the proof of (

). The proof of (

) is very similar, after noting that for
\mathcal{X}\in\{\hat{\mathcal{A}},\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime},\hat{\mathcal{F}},\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{*}\}
and an edge

it holds that

iff
\mathcal{X}(e)=1
and

.

This completes the proof of (

), and hence the proof of (

) and (

) as well, concluding therefore the lemma.

.

Observation 51

Let

\mathcal{A}\in\Omega_{F}
,
\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}(\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A})=\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}(\mathcal{F}_{2}\cup\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a partial configuration in
\Omega_{F}
. Consider the components of

G[\In(\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A})]
and
G[\In(\mathcal{F}_{2}\cup\mathcal{A})]
.

•

The components of
G[\In(\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A})]
containing only vertices of
V_{R}(\mathcal{F}_{1})\backslash\partial\mathcal{F}_{1}
do not depend on the partial configuration
\mathcal{A}
. Let
C_{1}
be the number of these components. Similarly, the components of
G[\In(\mathcal{F}_{2}\cup\mathcal{A})]
containing only vertices of
V_{R}(\mathcal{F}_{2})\backslash\partial\mathcal{F}_{2}
do not depend on
\mathcal{A}
. Let
C_{2}
be the number of these components.

•

The components
of
G[\In(\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A})]

containing only vertices of
V\backslash V_{R}(\mathcal{F}_{1})
depend on
\mathcal{A}
but not on
F
,
so the same components are in
G[\In(\mathcal{F}_{2}\cup\mathcal{A})]
.

•

Since
\xi(\mathcal{F}_{1})=\xi(\mathcal{F}_{2})
, there is one-to-one correspondence between components of
G[\In(\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A})]
that contain vertices in
\partial\mathcal{F}_{1}
and components of
G[\In(\mathcal{F}_{2}\cup\mathcal{A})]
containing vertices in
\partial\mathcal{F}_{2}
. Any two components that correspond to each other induce the same component on
F
.

Thus, for any
\mathcal{A}\colon F\rightarrow\{0,1,*\}
,
the number of components in
G[\In(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{F}_{1})]
minus
C_{1}
is equal to the number of components in
G[\In(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2})]
minus
C_{2}
.
Hence
w_{G}(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{F}_{1})
and
w_{G}(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2})
differ by a constant multiplicative factor, from which the result follows.
∎

Cor
53
gives information about
marginals in the case where
R(\mathcal{F}_{1})=R(\mathcal{F}_{2})
and
\In(\mathcal{F}_{1})\subseteq\In(\mathcal{F}_{2})
. The proof follows from Observation
52
.

Observation 52
.

Let
f
be an edge in
E
. Consider partial configurations
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
with
R(\mathcal{F}_{1})=R(\mathcal{F}_{2})=E\backslash\{f\}
and
\In(\mathcal{F}_{1})\subseteq\In(\mathcal{F}_{2})
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{1}\sim\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}\sim\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}
. Then
\Pr(f\in\In(\mathcal{A}_{1}))\leq\Pr(f\in\In(\mathcal{A}_{2}))
.

Proof.

First, observe that if
G[\In(\mathcal{F}_{1})]
has two components connected by
f
then the vertices in these components are connected in
G[\In(\mathcal{F}_{2})\cup\{f\}]
.

For
j\in\{0,1\}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{i,j}
be
\mathcal{F}_{i}\cup\{f\mapsto j\}
. Then, by the observation,
c(\mathcal{F}_{2,0})-c(\mathcal{F}_{2,1})\leq c(\mathcal{F}_{1,0})-c(\mathcal{F}_{1,1})
. It follows that

\displaystyle\Pr(f\in\In(\mathcal{A}_{1}))

\displaystyle=\frac{\Pr(\mathcal{F}_{1,1})}{\Pr(\mathcal{F}_{1,1})+\Pr(\mathcal{F}_{1,0})}=\frac{(e^{\beta}-1)^{|\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1}(1)|+1}q^{c(\mathcal{F}_{1,1})}}{(e^{\beta}-1)^{|\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1}(1)|+1}q^{c(\mathcal{F}_{1,1})}+(e^{\beta}-1)^{|\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1}(1)|}q^{c(\mathcal{F}_{1,0})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(e^{\beta}-1)q^{c(\mathcal{F}_{1,1})-c(\mathcal{F}_{1,0})}}{(e^{\beta}-1)q^{c(\mathcal{F}_{1,1})-c(\mathcal{F}_{1,0})}+1}\leq\frac{(e^{\beta}-1)q^{c(\mathcal{F}_{2,1})-c(\mathcal{F}_{2,0})}}{(e^{\beta}-1)q^{c(\mathcal{F}_{2,1})-c(\mathcal{F}_{2,0})}+1}

\displaystyle=\Pr(f\in\In(\mathcal{A}_{2}))

where the inequality follows from
c(\mathcal{F}_{1,1})-c(\mathcal{F}_{1,0})\leq c(\mathcal{F}_{2,1})-c(\mathcal{F}_{2,0})
.
∎

Corollary 53

Proof.

Use
[
18

. It says that if a RCM measure is
1
-monotonic, then the measure is monotonic, i.e.
\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}(A)\leq\pi_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}(A)

52
says that
\pi
is

A

f

\mathcal{F}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}_{2}^{\prime}
,
f\in\In(\mathcal{F}_{1}^{\prime})

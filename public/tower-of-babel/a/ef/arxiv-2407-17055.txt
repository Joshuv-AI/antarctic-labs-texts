1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

The Bloch–Kato conjecture, which relates the dimensions of Selmer groups of geometric
p
-adic Galois representations to the order of vanishing of their
L
-functions, is one of the outstanding open problems of number theory. One of the most successful approaches to this problem so far has been via the method of Euler systems: norm-compatible families of global cohomology classes for Galois representations, which serve to give upper bounds for Selmer groups. These are related to
L
-functions by so-called
explicit reciprocity laws
, relating the localisation of the Euler system classes at
p
to the values of the
L
-function. This can be used as a criterion for the non-vanishing of Euler systems, a crucial input in applications to the Bloch–Kato conjecture (and related problems such as the Iwasawa Main Conjecture).

One strategy for proving such explicit reciprocity laws, introduced in the seminal paper
[
BDP13
]
in the context of Heegner points, proceeds as follows. Supposing we already have an Euler system for some Galois representation
V
, we aim to carry out the following steps:

(1)

Construct a
p
-adic
L
-function, for some
p
-adic family of Galois representations specialising to
V
, whose values in a suitable interpolation range are critical values of the corresponding complex
L
-functions.

(2)

Relate values of this
p
-adic
L
-function at suitable points
outside
the range of interpolation to Bloch–Kato logarithms of Euler system classes.

(3)

Via analytic continuation, deduce a relation between Euler systems and
p
-adic
L
-values at points
inside
the interpolation range, where the
p
-adic
L
-function specialises to the complex
L
-value.

This strategy was successfully carried out for the Euler system of Beilinson–Flach elements (attached to a Rankin–Selberg convolution of modular forms) in
[
BDR15
,
KLZ17
]
; and more recently in the
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
setting in
[
LPSZ21
,
LZ20a
]
. In the present paper we focus on the
Euler system of Asai–Flach classes
, introduced in
[
LLZ18
]
, which is an Euler system for the Asai, or twisted tensor, Galois representation associated to a Hilbert modular form for a real quadratic field. (These Galois representations are naturally essentially self-dual, factoring through the orthogonal group
\operatorname{GO}(4)
. Conversely, it follows from the Fontaine–Mazur conjecture that all 4-dimensional, irreducible, essentially self-dual geometric representations of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
with distinct Hodge–Tate weights arise either as Rankin–Selberg convolutions, as spin representations of
\operatorname{GSp}(4)
, or as Asai representations of quadratic Hilbert modular forms, so we have Euler system constructions in all cases.)

In this setting, step (1) of the above strategy was carried out in the prequel paper
[
GLZ23
]
, building on the higher Hida theory for Hilbert modular groups developed in
[
Gro21
]
. The goal of the present paper is to carry out Steps (2) and (3). As applications, we prove one inclusion of the cyclotomic Iwasawa Main Conjecture and the Bloch–Kato conjecture for the Asai
L
-functions of Hilbert modular forms over real quadratic fields (under various technical hypotheses, see below).

Main results

The main results of this paper are the following:

.

•

•

the Galois representation satisfies a “big image” condition (see
Assumption

13.1.1
),

•

the local Euler factor

does not vanish at

Then:

•

(

13.3.4
) For each

For more details of the notations and the definitions of the objects involved, we refer to the body of the paper.

We briefly survey the main steps towards

Theorem B

For each

with

p
-adic

The proof of this theorem relies on the use of higher Coleman theory (as developed in

\lx@sectionsign

\mathfrak{p}_{1}
-ordinary locus in

Although the statement of

involves only a single automorphic representation

p

\underline{\Pi}

\Pi

Theorem C

(
Theorem

11.5.1
)
.

There exists a 3-variable

\underline{\Pi}
, interpolating all critical values of the

\underline{\Pi}
.

This strengthens a theorem proved in
[
GLZ23
]

p
-adic
L

k_{1}
and

fixed (but without requiring ordinarity at
\mathfrak{p}_{2}

On the other hand, Sheth
[
She25a
]
has shown that the Euler system construction of
[

]
also extends to such families; and

gives a relation between these objects at a set of points which is Zariski-dense in the parameter space. In order to derive from this an explicit reciprocity law relating the Euler system and

p
-adic Eichler–Shimura comparison isomorphism, relating étale and de Rham cohomology, “interpolates in Hida families” in a suitable sense (analogous to results of Ohta
[
Oht00
]
and Andreatta–Iovita–Stevens

\GL_{2}/\mathbb{Q}

\mathcal{U}
is a small neighbourhood in weight space over which
\underline{\Pi}
is defined: one module
\mathcal{S}_{w_{1}}(\underline{\Pi})

\underline{\Pi}

\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathbb{M}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}})
which is
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}
of a rank-1 subquotient of the Asai Galois representation of
\underline{\Pi}

\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-module isomorphism having all of these “pointwise” isomorphisms as specialisations. What we actually prove is something a little weaker:

Theorem D

(
Theorem

12.4.3
)
.

There exists an isomorphism between the above modules after base-extending to
\operatorname{Frac}\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
, interpolating the Eichler–Shimura isomorphisms for all but finitely many classical points. Moreover, the composite of this isomorphism and Perrin-Riou’s regulator map sends the Euler system to the
p
-adic
L

Note that the proofs of both halves of the theorem are intertwined: we

the Euler system and regulator formula in order to derive the existence of the meromorphic Eichler–Shimura isomorphism.

as long as

2.
Conventions

Throughout this paper
F
denotes a real quadratic field, of discriminant
D
, and we fix an enumeration of the embeddings
F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
as
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
.

2.1.
Algebraic groups

As in
[
LLZ18
]
, define
G=\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\GL_{2})
, and
H=\GL_{2}
, with
\iota:H\hookrightarrow G
the natural embedding. We write
B_{H}
and
B_{G}
for the upper-triangular Borel subgroups. The Weyl group of
G
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
, with generators
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
(corresponding to the
\sigma_{i}
).

Recall that if
V
is an algebraic representation of
B_{H}
, then
V
gives rise to a vector bundle on the Shimura variety
Y_{H}
(for any sufficiently small level group), endowed with an action of Hecke correspondences. There are two possible normalisations for this functor, and we normalise so that the defining 2-dimensional representation of
H
(restricted to
B_{H}
) maps to the relative de Rham
cohomology
sheaf of the universal elliptic curve
\mathcal{E}\to Y_{H}
(rather than the relative homology, which is the other convention in use). There is an analogous construction for
G
as long as we restrict to representations trivial on the norm-one subgroup
\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&\\
&x\end{smallmatrix}\right):N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(x)=1\}
of
Z_{G}
, cf.
[
LLZ18
, §3.2c]
.

2.2.
Algebraic representations and the Clebsch–Gordan map

For the group
G
, we shall always work with representations over fields containing
F
. Given two representations
V,V^{\prime}
of
\GL_{2}
, we write
V\boxtimes V^{\prime}
for the tensor product of
V,V^{\prime}
, endowed with a
G
-action as the tensor product of the actions via
\sigma_{1}
on
V
and via
\sigma_{2}
on
V^{\prime}
.

Given integers
k_{1},k_{2}\geqslant 0
, and
0\leqslant j\leqslant\min\{k_{1},k_{2}\}
, let

V_{G}\coloneqq\Sym^{k_{1}}W_{G}\boxtimes\Sym^{k_{2}}W_{G}\qquad\text{and}\qquad V_{H}\coloneqq\Sym^{t}W_{H},\quad t=k_{1}+k_{2}-2j,

where
W_{H}
and
W_{G}
denote the defining 2-dimensional representations of
\GL_{2}/\mathbb{Q}
and
\GL_{2}/F
respectively.

The representation
V_{H}
has a canonical basis
(v^{a}w^{t-a})_{0\leqslant a\leqslant t}
, where
v,w
are the two standard basis vectors of
W_{H}
; and is equipped with a decreasing
B_{H}
-stable weight filtration
\Fil^{n}V_{H}=\langle\{v^{a}w^{t-a}:a\geqslant n\}\rangle
. Similarly,
V_{G}
is equipped with a bi-filtration (a decreasing filtration indexed by
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
) arising from the weight vector filtrations on each factor.

There is a non-zero morphism of
H
-representations (the
Clebsch–Gordan map
), unique up to scalars,

\CG^{[k_{1},k_{2},j]}:V_{H}\to V_{G}\otimes\det{}^{-j}.

See (
9.1
) below for explicit formulae. If we equip the one-dimensional representation
\det^{-j}
with a filtration concentrated in degree
-j
, then this map respects the filtrations, and hence induces a map on the graded pieces.

3.
Setup: Hilbert modular forms

3.1.
Notations

Let
J\in\{G,H\}
. For a neat open subgroup
K
of
J(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})
, write
Y_{J}(K)
for the Shimura variety of
J
of level
K
,
X_{J}(K)
for a toroidal compactification, and
D_{J}=X_{J}(K)-Y_{J}(K)
for the boundary divisor.

A
weight
for
G
will mean a tuple of integers
(k_{1},k_{2};w)
with
w=k_{1}=k_{2}\bmod 2
. If
\mu
is a weight, we have a locally-free sheaf
\omega^{\mu}
on
X_{G,K}/F
, whose sections are Hilbert modular forms of weight
\mu
.

Note 3.1.1
.

As an abstract sheaf
\omega_{\mu}
does not depend on
w
; but the action of Hecke operators on it does. With our conventions, if
\mathfrak{q}
is a prime of
F
which is trivial in the narrow ray class group modulo
Z_{G}\cap K
, the action of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}&\\
&\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
on
R\Gamma\left(X_{G,K}/F,\omega^{\mu}\right)
is multiplication by
(\#\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{q})^{w}
.

3.2.
Automorphic representations of
G

Definition 3.2.1
.

Let
\Pi
be the unitary cuspidal automorphic representation of
G(\mathbb{A})
generated by a holomorphic Hilbert modular newform of weight
(k_{1}+2,k_{2}+2)
, for some integers
k_{1},k_{2}\geqslant 0
with
k_{1}=k_{2}\bmod 2
. We assume that
\Pi
has level
\mathfrak{N}\trianglelefteqslant\mathcal{O}_{F}
, so if
U_{1}(\mathfrak{N})
denotes the open compact subgroup
\{g\in G\left(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}\right):g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\star&\star\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\bmod\mathfrak{N}\}
, then the space of
U_{1}(\mathfrak{N})
-invariants of
\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}
(the new subspace of
\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}
) is one-dimensional.

Remark 3.2.2
.

We shall assume throughout this paper that
\mathfrak{N}
does not divide
6\operatorname{disc}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}
, so that
U_{1}(\mathfrak{N})
is
sufficiently small
in the sense of
[
LLZ18
, Definition 2.2.1]
. The case of small levels can be dealt with in the usual fashion by introducing an auxiliary full level structure at some prime away from
p
; we leave the details to the interested reader.

We define
a_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\circ}(\Pi)
, for each ideal
\mathfrak{n}\trianglelefteqslant\mathcal{O}_{F}
, to be the Hecke eigenvalues of the new vector of
\Pi
, normalised in the analytic fashion, so that
|a_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\circ}(\Pi)|\leqslant 2
for each prime
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{N}
. For each such prime
\mathfrak{q}
we let
\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\circ}
and
\beta_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\circ}
be the Satake parameters of
\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}}
(so that
|\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\circ}|=|\beta_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\circ}|=1
and
a_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\circ}(\Pi)=\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\circ}+\beta_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\circ}
).

By a rationality theorem due to Shimura
[
Shi86
, Proposition 1.2]
, if we choose
w
such that
(k_{1},k_{2},w)
is a weight, then there is a number field
L\subset\mathbb{C}
depending only on
\Pi
which contains the quantities
N(\mathfrak{n})^{(w+1)/2}a_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\circ}(\Pi)
for all ideals
\mathfrak{n}
. We can (and do) assume that
L
also contains the images of the embeddings
F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, which is automatic if
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
.

For each integer
n\geqslant 1
, we write

a_{n}(\Pi)=n^{(k_{1}+k_{2}+2)/2}a_{n\mathcal{O}_{F}}^{\circ}(\Pi)\in\mathcal{O}_{L}.

3.3.
The Asai
L
-function

Definition 3.3.1
.

We define the
imprimitive Asai
L
-series
of
\Pi
to be the function

L^{\mathrm{imp}}_{\As}(\Pi,s)=L_{(N)}(\chi,2s-k_{1}-k_{2}-2)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}}\frac{a_{n}(\Pi)}{n^{s}},

where
\chi
is the restriction to
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
of the central character of
\Pi
, and
(N)
denotes omitting the Euler factors at the primes dividing
N=\mathfrak{N}\cap\mathbb{Z}
.

Note 3.3.2
.

(1)

The imprimitive
L
-function
L^{\mathrm{imp}}_{\As}(\Pi,s)
may differ by finitely many Euler factors from the “true” Asai
L
-series

whose local factors are defined using the local Langlands correspondence.

(2)

Our normalisations are slightly different here from
[
GLZ23
]
, in which we used the analytic normalisation (with the centre at
s=\tfrac{1}{2}
) which is more convenient for zeta-integral computations; the
L^{\mathrm{imp}}_{\As}(\Pi,s)
here is
L^{\mathrm{imp}}_{\As}(\Pi,s-\tfrac{k_{1}+k_{2}+2}{2})
in the notation of
op.cit.
. On the other hand, our normalisations
are
compatible with
[
LLZ18
, §5.1]
, and are also consistent with the conventional normalisation for Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions in
[
KLZ17
]
and elsewhere (if we interpret Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions as “Asai
L
-functions for the degenerate quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{Q}
”).

(3)

The critical values of
L_{\As}(\Pi,s)
are the integers satisfying
k_{2}+2\leqslant s\leqslant k_{1}+1
if
k_{1}>k_{2}
, and similarly if
k_{1}<k_{2}
. (There are no critical values if

5.3.
Partial Frobenii

We may identify

, as a Frobenius module (forgetting the filtration), with the rigid cohomology of the special fibre of

at
p
.

The operators
\varphi_{i}
induce commuting linear operators on

; and it follows from the “partial Eichler–Shimura” comparison result proved in

(\varphi_{i}-\alpha_{i})(\varphi_{i}-\beta_{i})=0\quad\text{on }D_{p}^{\As}(\Pi).

One checks easily that the partial Frobenii satisfy

is the Poincaré duality form. This identifies the
\beta_{i}
-generalised eigenspace with the dual of that for

. Hence, if
\alpha_{i}\neq\beta_{i}
, the
\varphi_{i}=\alpha_{i}
and
\varphi_{i}=\beta_{i}
eigenspaces are both 2-dimensional, and each is isotropic with respect to

Remark 5.3.1
.

We are using a slightly different normalisation of the partial Frobenii here from
[
LZ23
]
: the
\varphi_{i}
here is

in the notation of
op.cit.
, where

is an auxiliary choice of integers such that

is independent of

of

, while
\alpha_{i}
here is

, the action of Frobenius (given by the functoriality of rigid cohomology) cannot be ‘seen’ via this description.

For proper schemes such as
\mathbb{X}_{G}
, there is no difference between Gros fp-cohomology and the usual fp-cohomology, so there is an extension-by-zero map

\widetilde{R\Gamma}_{\fp,c}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})\to R\Gamma_{\fp,c}(\mathbb{X}_{G},\mathcal{V}_{G}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j}).

Proposition 7.2.3
.

There exists a class

\tilde{\eta}_{\fp}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})}\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})

with the following properties:

•

Its image in
H^{2}_{\fp,c}(\mathbb{X}_{G},\mathcal{V}_{G}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})
is the
\eta_{\fp}
of
Proposition

6.2.3
.

•

Its image in
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{dR},c}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle)
is the class
(\eta^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},0)
of
Proposition

7.1.1
.

Remark 7.2.4
.

The reader may be relieved to hear that the class
\tilde{\eta}_{\fp}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})}
is “the ultimate among liftings of
\eta
”: all other variants of
\eta
will be images of this one.

Proof.

From the mapping-fibre definition of Gros fp-cohomology we have a long exact sequence

\dots\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{rig},c}(X_{G,0}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle)\to\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})\to\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{dR},c}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle)\to\dots,

in which the boundary map is
P(\varphi)\circ\iota
. Moreover, this is compatible under extension-by-zero with the corresponding sequence for fp-cohomology of
\mathbb{X}_{G}
.

We have seen that the image of
(\eta^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},0)
under
\iota
is the class
\eta_{\mathrm{dR}}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})}
, which is annihilated by
P(\varphi)
. Hence it lifts to
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c}
. This lift is unique up to the image of an element of
H^{1}_{\mathrm{rig},c}(X_{G,0}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle)
; but from
Proposition

5.5.3
it follows that this group has trivial
\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}
-eigenspace for the prime-to-
p
Hecke operators, so there is a unique Hecke-equivariant lifting of
(\eta^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},0)
to Gros-fp cohomology. The image of this class under extension-by-0 is therefore a Hecke-equivariant lifting of
\eta_{\mathrm{dR}}
to fp-cohomology of
\mathbb{X}_{G}
, so it must be
\eta_{\fp}
.
∎

Now as shown in Note
4.3.1
we have
\iota^{-1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}})=\mathcal{X}_{H}^{\mathrm{ord}}
, so we deduce the following:

Corollary 7.2.5
.

The pairing (
6.1
) is equal to
\left\langle\iota^{[j],*}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{\fp}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})}\right),\widetilde{\mathrm{Eis}}^{t,\mathrm{ord}}_{\syn}\right\rangle
, where
\widetilde{\mathrm{Eis}}^{t,\mathrm{ord}}_{\syn}
is the image of
\mathrm{Eis}^{t}_{\syn}
in the Gros fp-cohomology of
\mathcal{X}_{H}^{\mathrm{ord}}
.∎

Here we define
\iota^{[j],*}
for classes in Gros fp-cohomology using the quasi-isomorphism from the BGG complex to the full de Rham complex. We shall give explicit formulae in

below, but first we need to give an explicit form for

\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}}\subset\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})}
.

Restricting to the fully-ordinary locus

8.1.
Cohomology with partial compact support

We now consider the cohomology of the fully ordinary locus
X_{G,0}^{\mathrm{ord}}
. Since the complement
X_{G,0}-X_{G,0}^{\mathrm{ord}}
is the disjoint union of a closed subvariety
X_{G,0}^{(1-\mathrm{ss})}
and the open subvariety
X_{G,0}^{(2-\mathrm{ss})}\cap X_{G,0}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})}
, we can apply the formalism of
[
LZ20a
, §13]
to define “cohomology of
\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}}
with compact support towards
\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ss})}
” (with coefficients in any abelian sheaf on
\mathcal{X}_{G}
). We write this as
R\Gamma_{c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G},-)
. By construction, this comes equipped with a restriction map

R\Gamma_{c}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},-\right)\to R\Gamma_{c1}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},-\right),

which fits into a triangle whose third term is the compactly-supported cohomology of
\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})}\cap\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(2-\mathrm{ss})}
. In particular, by the same argument as
Proposition

5.5.3
, we have isomorphisms

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{rig},c1}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle\right)[\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}]\longleftarrow R\Gamma_{\mathrm{rig},c}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle\right)[\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}]\longrightarrow R\Gamma_{\mathrm{rig},c}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle\right)[\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}].

The advantage of working with
\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}}
is that both
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
have liftings.

Notation 8.1.1
.

Write
\eta^{\mathrm{ord}}\in H^{1}_{c1}\Big(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\omega^{(-k_{1},k_{2}+2)}(-D_{G})\Big)
for the image of
\eta^{(1-\mathrm{ord})}
under the above restriction map.

.

Over the ordinary locus, we have commuting liftings of both
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
, which both act on
R\Gamma_{c1}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\omega^{(\dots)}\right)
; and the operator
T_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
decomposes as

T_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}=U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}+\varphi_{2},

with
U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}\circ\varphi_{2}=p^{k_{2}+1}\langle\mathfrak{p}_{2}\rangle
.

Corollary 8.1.3
.

The class
P(\varphi)\cdot\eta^{\mathrm{ord}}
lies in the kernel of the Hecke operator
U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
.

Proof.

We have
\varphi=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}
.
The result follows easily from
Theorem

4.1.4
and Note
8.1.2
, using the fact that
\eta^{(1-\mathrm{ord})}
is a

-eigenvector.
∎

8.2.
The Poznan spectral sequence

We now recall a spectral sequence (introduced in
[
LZ20a
]
) relating Gros fp-cohomology to coherent cohomology. Here Gros fp-cohomology is defined in the same way as for the
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
-ordinary locus above, but now with
c1
-support.

Definition 8.2.1
.

We define groups
\mathscr{C}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})
, for
m,n\geqslant 0
, by

\mathscr{C}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}(\dots)=H^{n}_{c1}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},(\tau_{\geqslant 1}\BGG)^{m}(-D_{G})\right)\oplus H^{n}_{c1}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\BGG^{m-1}(-D_{G})\right);

and we define differentials
\mathscr{C}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}(\dots)\to\mathscr{C}^{m+1,n}_{\fp,c1}(\dots)
by

(x,y)\mapsto\left(\nabla x,P(\varphi/p^{n})\iota(x)-\nabla y\right),

where
\iota
is the inclusion of
\tau_{\geqslant 1}\BGG^{\bullet}
into
\BGG^{\bullet}
, and
\nabla
the differential of the BGG complex.

Note that
\mathscr{C}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}
is zero for
m\leqslant 0
(this is obvious for
m\leqslant-1
, and holds for
m=0
since the truncated BGG complex vanishes in degree 0). It is also zero for
n\leqslant 0
, since
H^{0}_{c1}
vanishes for locally-free sheaves.

Proposition 8.2.2
.

There is a first-quadrant spectral sequence, the
Poznań spectral sequence
, with

{}^{\Pz}E_{1}^{mn}=\mathscr{C}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j}),

and the differentials on the
E_{1}
page given by the formula above. This spectral sequence abuts to the Gros fp-cohomology
\widetilde{H}^{m+n}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})
.

Proof.

Choose double complexes computing
\widetilde{R\Gamma}_{\mathrm{dR},c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle)
and
R\Gamma_{\mathrm{rig},c1}(X_{G,0}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle)
, respectivly, in such a way that
P_{1+j}(\varphi)
extens to a map of double complexes. Then
\widetilde{H}^{m+n}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})
is computed by the total complex of the associated mapping fibre. The Poznań spectral sequence is one of the spectral sequences associated to this triple complex.
∎

Definition 8.2.3
.

We define a
coherent fp-pair
(of degree
(m,n)
, twist
1+j
and
c1
-support) to be an element of the kernel of the differential
\mathscr{C}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}\to\mathscr{C}^{m+1,n}_{\fp,c1}
; we write the group of these as
\mathscr{Z}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})
.

Thus an fp-pair is a pair of elements

x\in H^{n}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\tau_{\geqslant 1}\BGG^{m}(-D_{G})),\qquad y\in H^{n}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\BGG^{m-1}(-D_{G}))

which satisfy

(8.1)

\nabla(x)=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla(y)=P(p^{-1-j}\varphi)\iota(x).

Note 8.2.4
.

Given
x
, the equation (
8.1
) does not determine the element
y
uniquely: it is determined up to an element of
H^{n}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\BGG^{m-1}(-D_{G}))^{\nabla=0}
.

Proposition 8.2.5
.

For
0\leqslant j\leqslant\min\{k_{1},k_{2}\}
, the spectral sequence gives rise to an isomorphism

\alpha_{G,\fp}:\mathscr{Z}^{1,1}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j}).

Proof.

Since the
E_{1}
page of the spectral sequence is zero for
m\leqslant 0
, the term
{}^{\Pz}E_{2}^{1,1}
is the kernel of the differential on
{}^{\Pz}E_{1}^{1,1}
, which is the group of fp-pairs. Since this is the only nonzero term with
m+n=2
, and the incoming and outgoing differentials at
{}^{\Pz}E_{r}^{1,1}
are trivially zero for all
r\geqslant 1
, we conclude that
{}^{\Pz}E_{2}^{1,1}
coincides with
\widetilde{H}^{2}
of the abutment, as required.
∎

Corollary 8.2.6
.

Every cohomology class in
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})
can be uniquely represented by a coherent fp-pair of degree
(1,1)
.

Proof.

Since the
E_{1}
terms of the spectral sequence are supported in the region
m,n\geqslant 1
, there are no other terms of total degree 2 except
(m,n)=(1,1)
; and clearly
E_{2}^{(1,1)}=E_{\infty}^{(1,1)}
since the differentials on the
E_{2}
page and beyond land outside this region.
∎

An exactly analogous argument shows that for the truncated de Rham cohomology groups
\widetilde{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{dR},c1}
(the hypercohomology of
\tau_{\geqslant 1}\BGG^{\bullet}(-D_{G})
), we have an isomorphism

H^{1}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\BGG^{1}(-D_{G}))\xrightarrow{\ \alpha_{G,\mathrm{rig}}\ }\widetilde{H}_{\mathrm{dR},c1}^{2}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle,1+j).

Lemma 8.2.7
.

Let
0\leqslant j\leqslant\min\{k_{1},k_{2}\}
. We have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Z}^{1,1}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\BGG^{1}(-D_{G}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{H}_{\mathrm{dR},c1}^{2}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle,1+j)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{G,\fp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{G,\mathrm{rig}}}

where the vertical arrows are the natural projection maps.

Proof.

Clear from the constructions.
∎

Notation 8.3.1
.

Define

\tilde{\eta}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\fp}\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{G}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})

There exists a uniquely determined class

which is independent of

, such that

, and such that

-eigenspace for the Hecke operators away from

.

The existence of

and Lemma

Now, if

is another element such that

Lemma 8.3.3
.

The element

has the following properties:

(8.2)

\varphi_{1}\cdot\xi=\alpha_{1}\,\xi\qquad\text{and}\qquad U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}\cdot\xi=0.

We first show the latter statement. We deduce from Corollary

But as observed above, this space is zero. The former statement follows analogously, by considering the element

.

We now lift these classes from the BGG complex to the full de Rham complex:

Definition 8.3.4
.

(1)

Write
\dot{\xi}
for the image of

in

.

Similarly, for

The element

\varphi_{1}(\dot{\xi})=\alpha_{1}\,\dot{\xi}\qquad\text{and}\qquad U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(\dot{\xi})=0.

.

We have

U_{p}\circ\iota^{*}(\dot{\xi})=0.

As maps

Since

9.
Expression via coherent cohomology

9.1.
Relating the BGG and de Rham complexes

We need to recall some formulae relating the de Rham and BGG complexes for
\GL_{2}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. The BGG complex for weight
k\geqslant 0
is given by
\left[\omega^{-k}\xrightarrow{\Theta}\omega^{k+2}\right]
, where
\Theta
is a differential operator given in terms of
q
-expansions by

\Theta=\tfrac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\theta^{k+1},\qquad\theta=q\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}q}.

(Cf.
[
TX16
, Remark 2.17]
for example.) The map
\BGG^{\bullet}\to\DR^{\bullet}
is given as follows.

•

In degree 1, it is given by the tensor product of the natural inclusion
\omega^{k}=\Fil^{k}(\Sym^{k}\mathcal{W}_{H})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}_{H}
and the Kodaira–Spencer isomorphism
\omega^{2}\cong\Omega^{1}(D)
.

•

In degree 0, a section
s
of
\omega^{-k}
is mapped to the unique section of
\Sym^{k}\mathcal{W}_{H}
whose image in
\Sym^{k}\mathcal{W}_{H}/\Fil^{0}\cong\omega^{-k}
is
s
, and whose image under the differential
\nabla
lands in
\omega^{k}\otimes\Omega^{1}(D)
.

We now recall (and somewhat reformulate) some results from §4 of
[
KLZ20
]
giving a completely explicit description of these maps. We write
\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{H}
for the ordinary locus
as a classical rigid space
(not a dagger space), i.e. neglecting overconvergence. Passing to the Igusa tower
\mathscr{I\!G}_{H}
(the canonical
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
-covering of
\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{H}
parametrising ordinary elliptic curves with a trivialization of their formal group), we obtain a canonical section
v
of
\omega
, corresponding to the invariant differential form
\tfrac{dT}{T}
on the Tate curve
\mathbb{G}_{m}/q^{\mathbb{Z}}
; note that
\varphi^{*}(v)=pv
. If we let
w
be the unique section such that
\nabla(v)=u\otimes\xi
, where
\xi
the local basis of
\Omega^{1}_{\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{H}}(D)
corresponding to
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}q}{q}
, then
v
and
w
are a basis of sections of
\mathcal{W}_{H}
over the Igusa tower, with
w
spanning the unit-root subspace (we have
\varphi^{*}(w)=w
) and
\nabla w=0
. Hence we obtain a basis of sections
(v^{a}w^{k-a})_{0\leqslant a\leqslant k}
of
\Sym^{k}\mathcal{W}_{H}
in which the actions of
\nabla
and
\varphi
are completely explicit.

In these coordinates, the map from
\omega^{-k}
to
\Sym^{k}\mathcal{W}_{H}
sends a section
g
of
\omega^{-k}
to the section

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\tfrac{(-1)^{i}}{i!}\theta^{i}(g)\cdot v^{i}w^{k-i}.

One verifies easily that the image of this under
\nabla
is
\theta^{k+1}(g)v^{k}\otimes\xi
, as expected. (Note that it is not
a priori
obvious that such a sum is overconvergent if
g
is, since neither
\theta
nor the local bases
v^{i}w^{k-i}
have any meaning outside the ordinary locus.)

9.2.
The Eisenstein class as a coherent fp-pair

Recall that the
\GL_{2}
Eisenstein class
\mathrm{Eis}^{t}_{\syn,N}
lies in
H^{1}_{\syn}(\mathbb{Y}_{H},\mathcal{V}_{H}(1+t))
, where
\mathbb{Y}_{H}
is the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-model of the modular curve
Y_{1}(N)
, and
\mathcal{V}_{H}
is the sheaf corresponding to the
t
-th symmetric power of the standard representation.

If we restrict to the open subscheme
\mathbb{Y}_{H}^{\mathrm{ord}}
given by removing the supersingular points of the special fibre (so
\mathbb{Y}_{H}^{\mathrm{ord}}
has the same generic fibre as
\mathbb{Y}_{H}
), and work with Gros syntomic cohomology, then the Poznan spectral sequence applied to
\mathcal{X}_{1}(N)^{\mathrm{ord}}
gives the following explicit description:

Proposition 9.2.1
.

The Poznan spectral sequence gives rise to an isomorphism

\alpha_{H,\syn}:\mathscr{Z}^{1,0}_{\syn}(\mathcal{X}_{H}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}_{H}(t))\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\syn}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{H},\mathcal{V}_{H}(t)).

Explicitly, a class
x\in\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\syn}(\mathcal{X}_{1}(N)^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}_{H},1+t)
is given by a coherent syntomic pair
(x_{0},x_{1})
, where

x_{0}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{1}(N)^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}),\qquad x_{1}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{1}(N)^{\mathrm{ord}},\Fil^{t}\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}\langle D\rangle),\qquad\nabla x_{0}=(1-p^{-1-t}\varphi)x_{1}.

Remark 9.2.2
.

Applying the Poznan speactral sequence to Gros fp-cohomology of
\mathcal{X}_{1}(N)^{\mathrm{ord}}
with compact support, we obtain an isomorphism

\alpha_{H,\fp}:\mathscr{Z}^{1,1}_{\fp,c}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{H},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{H}\rangle,2;P_{1+j})\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{H},\mathcal{V}\langle-D_{H}\rangle,2;P_{1+j}).

The sheaf
\Fil^{t}\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}\langle D\rangle
is simply
\omega^{t+2}
, so
x_{1}
is an overconvergent
p
-adic modular form of weight
t+2
; via this description,
\varphi
acts on overconvergent forms as
p^{t+1}\langle p\rangle V_{p}
, where
\langle p\rangle
is the diamond operator for
p\bmod N
.

We let
\widetilde{\mathrm{Eis}}^{t,\mathrm{ord}}_{\syn,N}
be the image of
\mathrm{Eis}^{t}_{\syn,N}
in this group; we shall now write down an explicit representing pair. The following is a reformulation of Theorem 4.5.7 of
[
KLZ20
]
; the formulations differ because we are using symmetric powers here rather than symmetric tensors (the basis vector
v^{[r,s]}
of
op.cit.
corresponds to
\tfrac{v^{s}w^{r}}{r!s!}
in our present notation) and because we use a slightly different notation for Eisenstein series following
[
Kat04
]
.

Proposition 9.2.3
.

The Eisenstein class
\widetilde{\mathrm{Eis}}^{t,\mathrm{ord}}_{\syn,N}
is represented by the coherent syntomic pair
\left(\epsilon_{0}^{t},\epsilon_{1}^{t}\right)
whose restrictions to
\mathscr{X}_{H}^{\mathrm{ord}}
are

\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle=-N^{k}\sum_{u=0}^{t}\frac{(-1)^{t-u}}{u!}\theta^{u}\left(E^{-t,\mathrm{ord}}_{0,1/N}\right)\cdot v^{t-u}w^{u},

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}^{t}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{N^{t}}{t!}F^{t+2}_{0,1/N}\cdot v^{t}\otimes\xi.

Here
F^{t+2}_{0,1/N}
is the algebraic Eisenstein series with
q
-expansion

\zeta(-1-t)+\sum_{n>0}q^{n}\sum_{d|n}(\tfrac{n}{d})^{t+1}(\zeta_{N}^{d}+(-1)^{t}\zeta_{N}^{-d}),

and
E^{-t,\mathrm{ord}}_{0,1/N}
is an ordinary
p
-adic Eisenstein series of weight

.

∎

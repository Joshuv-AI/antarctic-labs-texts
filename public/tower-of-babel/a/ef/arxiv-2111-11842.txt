A short noncomplex proof for the solution set of homogeneous second order linear differential equations with constant coefficients
Patrik Lundström

Abstract
. We provide a short proof, not utilizing complex numbers, for the solution set of homogeneous second order linear differential equations with constant coefficients.



We argue that the solution set
of second order linear differential equations
with constant
coefficients
should be presented using hyperbolic
functions in the distinct real root case:

Theorem.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Put

\alpha=a/2
and
\beta=\sqrt{|a^{2}/4-b|}
.
All solutions to
y^{\prime\prime}+ay^{\prime}+by=0
are given by
(1)
y=e^{-\alpha x}(C_{1}\cosh(\beta x)+C_{2}\sinh(\beta x))
, if
a^{2}/4>b
,
(2)
y=e^{-\alpha x}(C_{1}+C_{2}x)
,
if
a^{2}/4=b
, and
(3)
y=e^{-\alpha x}(C_{1}\cos(\beta x)+C_{2}\sin(\beta x))
,
if
a^{2}/4<b
,
for arbitrary
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.

There are many reasons for doing so.
First of all, this presentation
highlights the beautiful symmetry between (1) and (3).
Secondly, if we in (1) and (3) use the
first order approximations
\cosh(\beta x)\approx 1
,

\sinh(\beta x)\approx\beta x
,

\cos(\beta x)\approx 1
and

\sin(\beta x)\approx\beta x
, for small
\beta
,
then (2) can be seen, qualitatively,
as a limiting case of both (1) and (3), as
\beta\to 0
.
Thirdly, and perhaps most importantly,
utilizing a condensed version of a clever trick by
Dobos
[
1
]
, and here expanding
it to to the real case also,
one can construct
a short coherent elementary
noncomplex proof of (1)-(3).

Namely, it is a straighforward calculation to show that the expressions
in (1), (2) and (3) indeed are
solutions to the differential equation.
Now suppose that
y
is an arbitrary solution and put

z=e^{\alpha x}y
.
Then
z^{\prime}=\alpha z+e^{\alpha x}y^{\prime}
and thus

z^{\prime\prime}=\alpha^{2}z+2\alpha e^{\alpha x}y^{\prime}+e^{\alpha x}y^{\prime\prime}=e^{\alpha x}((a^{2}/4)y+ay^{\prime}+y^{\prime\prime})
.

(1) Let
a^{2}/4>b
. Then

z^{\prime\prime}-\beta^{2}z=e^{\alpha x}((a^{2}/4)y+ay^{\prime}+y^{\prime\prime}-(a^{2}/4-b)y)=e^{\alpha x}(y^{\prime\prime}+ay^{\prime}+by)=0

and hence

(-z\sinh(\beta x)+z^{\prime}\cosh(\beta x)/\beta)^{\prime}=0

and

(-z^{\prime}\sinh(\beta x)/\beta+z\cosh(\beta x))^{\prime}=0

which implies that

-z\sinh(\beta x)+z^{\prime}\cosh(\beta x)/\beta=C_{2}

and

-z^{\prime}\sinh(\beta x)/\beta+z\cosh(\beta x)=C_{1}

for some
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore

C_{1}\cosh(\beta x)+C_{2}\sinh(\beta x)=z\cosh^{2}(\beta x)-z\sinh^{2}(\beta x)=z=e^{\alpha x}y

and thus finally we get that

y=e^{-\alpha x}z=e^{-\alpha x}(C_{1}\cosh(\beta x)+C_{2}\sinh(\beta x))
.

(2) Suppose that
a^{2}/4=b
. Then

z^{\prime\prime}=e^{\alpha x}((a^{2}/4)y+ay^{\prime}+y^{\prime\prime})=e^{\alpha x}(y^{\prime\prime}+ay^{\prime}+by)=0
.
Hence

z=C_{1}+C_{2}x
for some
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}

and therefore we get that

y=e^{-\alpha x}z=e^{-\alpha x}(C_{1}+C_{2}x)
.

(3) Let
a^{2}/4<b
. Then

z^{\prime\prime}+\beta^{2}z=e^{\alpha x}((a^{2}/4)y+ay^{\prime}+y^{\prime\prime}+(b-a^{2}/4)y)=e^{\alpha x}(y^{\prime\prime}+ay^{\prime}+by)=0

and hence

(z\sin(\beta x)+z^{\prime}\cos(\beta x)/\beta)^{\prime}=0

and

(-z^{\prime}\sin(\beta x)/\beta+z\cos(\beta x))^{\prime}=0

which implies that

z\sin(\beta x)+z^{\prime}\cos(\beta x)/\beta=C_{2}

and

-z^{\prime}\sin(\beta x)/\beta+z\cos(\beta x)=C_{1}

for some
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore

C_{1}\cos(\beta x)+C_{2}\sin(\beta x)=z\cos^{2}(\beta x)+z\sin^{2}(\beta x)=z=e^{\alpha x}y

and thus finally we get that

y=e^{-\alpha x}z=e^{-\alpha x}(C_{1}\cos(\beta x)+C_{2}\sin(\beta x))
.
∎

Note that the solutions in (1)
above
of course easily can
be rewritten in the familiar form

y=e^{-\alpha x}(C_{1}\cosh(\beta x)+C_{2}\sinh(\beta x))=D_{1}e^{r_{1}x}+D_{2}e^{r_{2}x}
where

D_{1}=(C_{1}+C_{2})/2
,
D_{2}=(C_{1}-C_{2})/2
and

r_{1}=-\alpha+\beta
and
r_{2}=-\alpha-\beta

are the two roots of the characteristic equation

r^{2}+ar+b=0
.

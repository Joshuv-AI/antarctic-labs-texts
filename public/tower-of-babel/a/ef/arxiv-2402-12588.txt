Local and local-to-global Principles for zero-cycles on geometrically Kummer K ​ 3 K3 surfaces
Evangelia Gazaki*, Jonathan Love**

Abstract
. Let X X be a K ​ 3 K3 surface over a p p -adic field k k such that for some abelian surface A A isogenous to a product of two elliptic curves, there is an isomorphism over the algebraic closure of k k between X X and the Kummer surface associated to A A . Under some assumptions on the reduction types of the elliptic curve factors of A A , we prove that the Chow group A 0 ​ ( X ) A_{0}(X) of zero-cycles of degree 0 0 on X X is the direct sum of a divisible group and a finite group. This proves a conjecture of Raskind and Spiess and of Colliot-Thélène and it is the first instance for K ​ 3 K3 surfaces when this conjecture is proved in full. This class of K ​ 3 K3 ’s includes, among others, the diagonal quartic surfaces. In the case of good ordinary reduction we describe many cases when the finite summand of A 0 ​ ( X ) A_{0}(X) can be completely determined. Using these results, we explore a local-to-global conjecture of Colliot-Thélene, Sansuc, Kato and Saito which, roughly speaking, predicts that the Brauer-Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to Weak Approximation for zero-cycles. We give examples of Kummer surfaces over a number field F F where the ramified places of good ordinary reduction contribute nontrivially to the Brauer set for zero-cycles of degree 0 0 and we describe cases when an unconditional local-to-global principle can be proved, giving the first unconditional evidence for this conjecture in the case of K ​ 3 K3 surfaces.



1.
Introduction

For a smooth projective variety
Y
over a field
k
we consider the Chow group of zero-cycles
\CH_{0}(Y)
. This group has a filtration

\CH_{0}(Y)\supset A_{0}(Y)\supset T(Y)\supset 0,

where
A_{0}(Y):=\CH_{0}(Y)^{\deg=0}
is the subgroup of cycles of degree
0
and
T(Y)=A_{0}(Y)^{\alb_{Y}=0}
is the kernel of the Albanese map

\alb_{Y}:A_{0}(Y)\rightarrow\Alb_{Y}(k).

The group
\CH_{0}(Y)
is in general extremely hard to compute, even when
Y
is a surface, due mainly to how little information we have on the structure of the Albanese kernel. When
k
is a large transcendental field like
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and the variety
Y
has positive geometric genus, it follows by works of Mumford (
[
Mum68
]
) and Bloch (
[
Blo75
]
) that
T(Y)
is very large; in particular its maximal divisible subgroup is nontrivial.
The question that is of interest to us in this article is what can be said about the non-divisible part of the Chow group when
k
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
or
\mathbb{Q}
. In
[
GL21
,
GH21
,
Gaz22
,
Gaz23
]
the first author and her collaborators obtained local and local-to-global results for certain classes of abelian varieties. In this article we extend these results to a special class of
K3
surfaces, those that are
geometrically of Kummer type
. That is, over the algebraic closure
\overline{k}
the surface
Y_{\overline{k}}:=Y\otimes_{k}\overline{k}
becomes isomorphic to the Kummer surface
\Kum(A)
associated to an abelian surface
A
over
\overline{k}
.

1.1.
Results over
p
-adic fields

For varieties defined over a finite extension
k
of the
p
-adic field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

the motivating question is the following conjecture due to Raskind and Spiess, motivated by earlier considerations of Colliot-Thélène.

Conjecture 1.1
.

(
[
CT95
, 1.4(d,e,f)]
,
[
RS00
, Conjecture 3.5.4]
) Let
Y
be a smooth projective and geometrically connected variety over
k
. The kernel
T(Y)
of the Albanese map of
Y
is the direct sum of its maximal divisible subgroup and a finite group.

S. Saito and K. Sato (
[
SS10
, Theorem 0.3]
) obtained a breakthrough result showing the weaker property that the subgroup
A_{0}(Y)
is the direct sum of a group divisible by any integer
m
coprime to
p
and a finite group. Moreover, they showed that the finite piece vanishes in the case of good reduction (
[
SS10
, Corollary 0.10]
).

The full conjecture is only known in limited cases by the work of Raskind and Spiess (
[
RS00
, Theorem 1.1]
) for certain products of curves whose Jacobians have split semi-ordinary reduction and by the first author and her collaborators for products of elliptic curves with at most one having supersingular reduction (
[
GL21
, Theorem 1.2]
).
In
section 2
we prove this conjecture for large infinite collections of geometrically Kummer
K3
surfaces, by pushing forward the information from the abelian surface. Note that for a
K3
surface
Y
the Albanese variety is zero, and hence
T(Y)=A_{0}(Y).
Our first theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

(cf.
subsection 2.3
) Let
k
be a finite extension of the
p
-adic field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
X
be a
K3
surface over
k
. Suppose there exists a finite extension
K/k
such that the base change
X_{K}:=X\otimes_{k}K
becomes isomorphic to the Kummer surface
\Kum(A)
associated to an abelian surface
A
over
K
.

(i)

Suppose that there exists an isogeny
\phi:E_{1}\times E_{2}\to A
from a product of elliptic curves at most one of which has potentially good supersingular reduction. Then
subsection 1.1
is true for both
A
and
X
.

(ii)

Suppose that the abelian surface
A
is geometrically simple and it has split semi-ordinary reduction. Then the group
A_{0}(X)
is the direct sum of its maximal divisible subgroup and a torsion group.

See
subsection 2.3
for the definition of split semi-ordinary reduction.
To our knowledge this is the first piece of evidence for
subsection 1.1
for
K3
surfaces. Verifying the conjecture for abelian surfaces isogenous to products of elliptic curves is also new and produces infinitely many new examples of Jacobians of genus
2
curves for which
subsection 1.1
is true (see
subsection 2.3
). A weaker result was obtained in
[
Gaz23
]
.

An important special class of geometrically Kummer
K3
surfaces are the diagonal quartics, which are hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{3}
given by an equation of the form

D:x_{0}^{4}+a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+a_{2}x_{2}^{4}+a_{3}x_{3}^{4}=0,

with
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in k^{\times}
. Mizukami (
[
Miz78
]
) showed that any such surface becomes isomorphic over an explicit finite extension to the Kummer surface
\Kum(A)
of an abelian surface isogenous to a self-product
C\times C
of a CM elliptic curve (see
subsection 2.4
).

The following Corollary can be thought of as the
p
-analog of
[
SS10
, Corollary 0.10]
. For simplicity we state it here only for Kummer surfaces and diagonal quartics.

Corollary 1.3
.

(cf.
subsection 3.1
,
subsection 3.1
) Let
k
be a finite
unramified
extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, where
p
is an odd prime. Let
X=\Kum(E_{1}\times E_{2})
be the Kummer surface associated to a product of two elliptic curves with good ordinary or almost ordinary reduction (cf.
subsection 2.3
). Then the group
A_{0}(X)
is divisible. If moreover
p\equiv 1\mod 4
, then the same is true for the diagonal quartic surface
D
assuming that
D
has good reduction.

A similar result was obtained in
[
GH21
, Theorem 1.4]
for products of elliptic curves. Such divisibility results are essential for studying Weak Approximation over number fields.

If we remove the unramified assumption, the result is in general very far from being true. Suppose that
X=\Kum(E_{1}\times E_{2})
satisfying the reduction hypotheses of
subsection 1.1
. We denote by
A_{0}(X)_{\nd}
the non-divisible summand of
A_{0}(X)
, which by our theorem is a finite group. Our methods show that we have a surjection

T(E_{1}\times E_{2})_{\nd}\{2^{\prime}\}\twoheadrightarrow A_{0}(X)_{\nd}\{2^{\prime}\}

of the subgroups consisting of elements of order coprime to
2
.
In
section 3
we describe many cases when this map is an isomorphism. The key ingredient is an isomorphism between the transcendental Brauer groups of
E_{1}\times E_{2}
and
X
obtained in
[
SZ12
, Theorem 2.4]
(see
subsection 3.2
). Our results show:

1.

In all reduction cases there is a finite extension
K/k
over which the group
A_{0}(X_{K})_{\nd}\{2^{\prime}\}
is nontrivial, and it can have order divisible by arbitrarily large powers of
p
.

2.

In the case of good ordinary reduction
A_{0}(X)_{\nd}\{2^{\prime}\}
can often be fully computed.

1.2.
Weak Approximation for zero-cycles

We next focus on local-to-global principles for zero-cycles. We are interested in the following conjecture, which informally states that the Brauer-Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to Weak Approximation for zero-cycles. This is due to Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc for geometrically rational varieties and to Kato and Saito for general varieties.

Conjecture 1.4
.

(
[
CTS81
, Conjecture A]
,
[
KS86
, Section 7]
, see also
[
Wit12
, Section 1.1]
) Let
Y
be a smooth projective and geometrically connected variety over a number field
F
. Let
\Omega
(resp.
\Omega_{f}
) be the set of places (resp. finite places) of
F
. The following complex is exact

(1.5)

\widehat{\CH_{0}(Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(Y)}\rightarrow\Hom(\Br(Y),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

Here for an abelian group
M
we denoted by
\widehat{M}=\varprojlim_{n}M/n
the profinite completion of
M
. The adelic Chow group
\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(Y)
is essentially
\prod_{v\in\Omega_{f}}\CH_{0}(Y_{v})
with a small
2
-torsion contribution from the infinite real places.

Exactness of (
1.5
) would imply exactness of the degree zero piece,

(1.6)

\widehat{A_{0}(Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{A_{0,\mathbf{A}}(Y)}\rightarrow\Hom(\Br(Y)/\Br(F),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),

and if
Y(F)\neq\emptyset
, the converse is also true. In this article we focus on the complex (
1.6
).

Definition 1.7
.

We define the Brauer set for zero-cycles of degree
0
and denote it
(\widehat{A_{0,\mathbf{A}}(Y)})^{\Br}
to be the subgroup of the adelic group
\widehat{A_{0,\mathbf{A}}(Y)}
consisting of elements that are orthogonal to the Brauer group.

Suppose that
\Pic(Y\otimes_{F}\overline{F})
is finitely generated and torsion-free and that the quotient
\Br(Y)/\Br(F)
is finite. Two important classes of varieties that satisfy these assumptions are rationally connected varieties and
K3
surfaces. The former have geometric genus zero, a property which in general implies that both the global and the local Chow groups are small. In fact, it follows by
[
KS03
]
that
A_{0}(Y_{v})=0
for every finite place
v
of good reduction.
For this class of varieties there are far reaching results regarding the validity of
subsection 1.2
due to Liang (
[
Lia13
]
) and Harpaz, Wittenberg (
[
HW18
]
), who used the fibration method to reduce the question to the one for rational points.
On the other hand, if
Y
is a

K3
surface, then
H^{2}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})\neq 0
, which forces all the local Chow groups
\CH_{0}(Y_{v})
to be enormous.
Ieronymou (
[
Ier21
, Theorem 1.2]
) used the fibration method for
K3
surfaces to “approximate modulo
n
”, assuming that the Brauer-Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to Weak Approximation for rational points on every finite extension
L/F
(see
section 4
for the precise statement).
The following questions arise when
Y
is a
K3
surface.

Question 1.8
.

Does
(\widehat{A_{0,\mathbf{A}}(Y)})^{\Br}
consist of genuine local zero-cycles?

Note that if
subsection 1.1
is true for
Y_{v}
for every finite place
v
, then the entire adelic group
\widehat{A_{0,\mathbf{A}}(Y)}
will consist of genuine zero-cycles.

Question 1.9
.

Which places contribute nontrivial components in
(\widehat{A_{0,\mathbf{A}}(Y)})^{\Br}
? Are they only finitely many?

subsection 1.1
suggests that only places of bad reduction and ramified places of good reduction might contribute nontrivially, although there is still an infinite set of good reduction places for which we don’t have enough information. Such a finiteness expectation is also supported by general expectations about zero-cycles over number fields combined with the finiteness of the Brauer group
\Br(Y)/\Br(F)
(see
section 4
).

Question 1.10
.

Do the places of good reduction contribute to the Brauer set?

Question 1.11
.

Can we obtain any unconditional evidence for
subsection 1.2
for
K3
surfaces without assuming anything about the arithmetic of rational points?

In
section 4
we give answers to all these questions and obtain evidence for
subsection 1.2
for geometrically Kummer
K3
surfaces by considering one prime at a time. Namely, fixing a prime number
p
, one can explore the exactness of the complex

(1.12)

\varprojlim\limits_{n}A_{0}(Y)/p^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\prod_{v\in\Omega}\varprojlim\limits_{n}A_{0}^{\prime}(Y_{v})/p^{n}\rightarrow\Hom(\Br(Y)\{p\}/\Br_{0}(Y)\{p\},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

For the definition of
A_{0}^{\prime}(Y_{v})
see
section 4
.
Exactness of the complex (
1.12
) for every prime
p
will imply exactness of (
1.6
).
Our first theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.13
.

(cf.
section 4
)
Let
X
be a
K3
surface over a number field
F
. Suppose there exists a finite extension
K/F
over which
X
becomes isomorphic to the Kummer surface associated to an abelian surface
A
over
K
admitting an isogeny
E_{1}\times E_{2}\xrightarrow{\phi}A
from a product of elliptic curves.
Then there exists an infinite set
T
of rational primes
p
for which the following are true:

(a)

For every
p\in T
the middle term of the complex (
1.12
) is the following finite group

\prod_{v|p}A_{0}(X_{v})_{\nd}\{p\}.

In particular, it consists of genuine local zero-cycles.

(b)

Suppose that for every finite extension
L/F
the set
X(L)
is dense in the Brauer set
X(\mathbf{A}_{L})^{\Br(X_{L})}
. Then for every
p\in T
the complex (
1.12
) is exact.

(c)

For all but finitely many
p\in T
the middle term of the complex (
1.12
) vanishes and for these primes the density assumption can be dropped.

Part (b) of the above theorem can be thought of as a “passing to the limit” version of the result of Ieronymou for geometrically split Kummer
K3
surfaces (see
subsection 2.3
).

In
subsection 4.1
we construct examples of Kummer surfaces for which the ramified places of good ordinary reduction contribute nontrivially to the Brauer set for zero-cycles of degree
0
, which gives an affirmative answer to
subsection 1.2
. This shows that the behavior for
K3
surfaces is quite different from the one for rationally connected varieties.

Proposition 1.14
.

(cf.
section 4
) Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
such that
E\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has complex multiplication by the full ring of integers of a quadratic imaginary field
K
. Let
A=E\times E
and
X=\Kum(A)
. Let
p\geq 5
be a prime that splits completely in
K
and is coprime to the conductor
\mathfrak{n}
of
E
. Suppose that the reduction
\overline{E_{p}}
of
E
modulo
p
satisfies
|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p
. Then there exists a finite extension
L/K
of degree
p-1
totally ramified at the places of
K
lying above
p
such that for the Kummer surface
X_{L}
the following are true:

(i)

The Brauer group
\Br(X_{L})\{p\}/\Br_{0}(X_{L})\{p\}
vanishes.

(ii)

The middle term of the complex (
1.12
) becomes
\displaystyle\prod_{v|p}A_{0}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}\{p\}=(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{2}
.

This proposition follows easily by an analogous result we proved in
[
Gaz22
, Theorem 1.6]
for the product
A=E\times E
and by the explicit computations we carry out in
subsection 3.2
. In
section 4
we give examples of pairs
(E,p)
of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
and primes
p
satisfying the assumptions of the proposition for each of the quadratic imaginary fields
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
with
D=-3,-11,-19,-43,-67,-163
.

We close this article by addressing
subsection 1.2
in the situation of
subsection 1.2
. The question becomes whether we can unconditionally lift the group
\displaystyle\prod_{v|p}A_{0}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}\{p\}=(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{2}
to global zero-cycles.
We show that this question can be reduced to the corresponding one for the abelian surface
A=E\times E
, for which in
[
Gaz22
, Theorem 1.7]
we described sufficient conditions for elliptic curves with positive rank over
\mathbb{Q}
that guarantee such a lifting. This allows us to find explicit examples of Kummer surfaces satisfying a nontrivial local-to-global principle, in the sense that global zero-cycles need to be constructed. This gives the first
unconditional evidence
for
subsection 1.2
for
K3
surfaces.
The following example is deduced by a case study carried out in the Appendix of
[
Gaz22
]
written by A. Koutsianas.

Example 1.15
.

Consider the family of elliptic curves given by the Weierstrass equations
\{E_{n}:y^{2}=x^{3}-2+7n,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and the prime
p=7
. Note that for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
the pair
(E_{n},p)
satisfies the assumptions of
subsection 1.2
. A SAGE computation shows that for about
87\%
of elliptic curves with rank
1
in the family
E_{n}
with
n\in[-5000,5000]
the Kummer surface
X_{n}=\Kum(E_{n}\times E_{n})
satisfies unconditionally a local-to-global principle for zero-cycles of degree
0
after extending to the appropriate degree
6
extension of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})
.

2.
The local Conjecture

In this section we will prove
subsection 1.1
. We start with some general results about zero-cycles on Kummer surfaces.
For most of this section unless otherwise mentioned
k
will be an arbitrary base field.

2.1.
Background and notation

Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety over
k
. The Chow group
\CH_{0}(Y)
of zero-cycles has a filtration

\CH_{0}(Y)\supset A_{0}(Y)\supset T(Y)\supset 0,

where
A_{0}(Y)=\CH_{0}(Y)^{\deg=0}
is the kernel of the degree map,
\CH_{0}(Y)\xrightarrow{\deg}\mathbb{Z}
, and
T(Y)=A_{0}(Y)^{\alb_{Y}=0}
is the kernel of the Albanese map,
A_{0}(Y)\xrightarrow{\alb_{Y}}\Alb_{Y}(k)
.
When
Y
is a surface, it follows by
[
CTC79
, Proposition 6.3]
that the group
A_{0}(Y)
is a birational invariant. This is a key ingredient in obtaining results for Kummer surfaces.

Example 2.1
.

Let
Y
be a
K3
surface over
k
. Then
\Alb_{Y}=0
, and hence
A_{0}(Y)=T(Y)
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
k
. Then
\Alb_{A}=A
and the Albanese map has the following simple description

\displaystyle\alb_{A}:

\displaystyle A_{0}(A)\to A(k)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in A}n_{x}[x]\mapsto\sum_{x\in A}n_{x}N_{k(x)/k}(x),

where
N_{k(x)/k}:A(k(x))\to A(k)
is the usual norm map on abelian varieties.
Here we are abusing notation: for a closed point
x\in A
, we also denote by
x
the induced
k(x)
-rational point of
A
.

2.2.
Kummer surfaces

Let
A
be an abelian surface over
k
. Let
A[2]
be its subgroup of
2
-torsion elements. Let
A^{\prime}:=\Bl_{A[2]}A
be the surface obtained by
A
by blowing-up the subscheme
A[2]
and let
\rho:A^{\prime}\to A
be the corresponding birational morphism. The preimage of
A[2]
is a
1
-dimensional scheme
V\subseteq A^{\prime}
that splits over
\bar{k}
into
16
rational curves (one lying over each
\bar{k}
-point of
A[2]
), and
\rho
induces an isomorphism

\rho:A^{\prime}-V\xrightarrow{\simeq}A-A[2].

Let
X_{0}:=A/\langle-1\rangle
be the quotient of
A
by the negation involution. We have a degree two cover
\pi:A\to X_{0}
. The singular locus of
X_{0}
is
\pi(A[2])
, which splits into sixteen singularities over
\bar{k}
. Let
X
be the surface obtained from
X_{0}
by blowing up the singular locus. The variety
X:=\Kum(A)
is a
K3
surface that is known as
the Kummer surface
associated to
A
. We will denote by
W
the preimage of
\pi(A[2])

. It follows by the universal property of blow-ups that
\pi
induces a degree

\pi^{\prime}:A^{\prime}\to X

(see
[

We consider the Chow group of zero-cycles
\CH_{0}(X)
.
The proper morphism
\pi:A^{\prime}\to X

\pi_{\star}:A_{0}(A^{\prime})\to A_{0}(X).

Moreover, the birational morphism
\rho

[

\rho_{\star}:A_{0}(A)\xrightarrow{\simeq}A_{0}(A^{\prime}).

\Alb_{A^{\prime}}=\Alb_{A}=A

inducing an injection
H^{2d}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(Y,\mu_{n}^{\otimes d})\hookrightarrow\Hom(H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(Y,\mu_{n}),\mathbb{Z}/n)=\Hom(H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(Y,\mu_{n}),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. Precomposing with the cycle class map we obtain a homomorphism

\CH_{0}(Y)/n\to\Hom(H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(Y,\mu_{n}),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

The Kummer sequence
1\to\mu_{n}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\xrightarrow{n}\mathbb{G}_{m}\to 1
on
Y_{\text{\'{e}t}}
induces a short exact sequence

0\to\Pic(Y)/n\to H^{2}(Y,\mu_{n})\to\Br(Y)_{n}\to 0.

Applying the exact functor
\Hom(-,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
gives an exact sequence

0\to\Hom(\Br(Y)_{n},\mathbb{Q}/Z)\to\Hom(H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(Y,\mu_{n}),\mathbb{Q}/Z)\to\Hom(\Pic(Y)/n,\mathbb{Q}/Z)\to 0.

The pairing
\CH_{0}(Y)/n\times\Pic(Y)/n\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
induced by the cycle class map and the Kummer sequence is zero, which implies that the homomorphism
\CH_{0}(Y)/n\to\Hom(H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(Y,\mu_{n}),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
factors through
\Hom(\Br(Y)_{n},\mathbb{Q}/Z)
. The induced homomorphism

\CH_{0}(Y)/n\to\Hom(\Br(Y)_{n},\mathbb{Q}/Z)

coincides with the map
\varepsilon_{n}^{Y}
induced by the Brauer-Manin pairing. It then follows by the non-degeneracy of the Saito pairing that
c_{n}^{Y}
is injective if and only if
\varepsilon_{n}^{Y}
is.

We note that the Brauer group of a variety is contravariant functorial. For a morphism
f:Y\to Z
of smooth projective varieties we will denote by
f^{\star}:\Br(Z)\to\Br(Y)
the induced pullback map and by
\widehat{f^{\star}}
the dual homomorphism,
\Hom(\Br(Y),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\widehat{f^{\star}}}\Hom(\Br(Z),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. The following lemma about functoriality of the Brauer-Manin pairing is standard. We include a proof for completeness.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
f:Y\to Z
be a proper morphism between smooth projective varieties over
k
. For every
n\geq 1
there is a commutative diagram

(3.8)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\CH_{0}(Y)/n}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(\Br(Y)_{n},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\CH_{0}(Z)/n}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(\Br(Z)_{n},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon_{n}^{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\star}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{f^{\star}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon_{n}^{Z}}

where the maps
\varepsilon_{n}^{Y},\varepsilon_{n}^{Z}
are induced by the Brauer-Manin pairing.

Proof.

The lemma will follow if we show

\langle f_{\star}([y]),\alpha\rangle_{Z}=\langle[y],f^{\star}(\alpha)\rangle_{Y},

for every closed point
y\in Y
and all
\alpha\in\Br(Z)
. The right hand side equals

\langle[y],f^{\star}(\alpha)\rangle_{Y}=\Cor_{k(y)/k}(\iota_{y}^{\star}(f^{\star}(\alpha))=\Cor_{k(f(y))/k}(\Cor_{k(y)/k(f(y))}((f\circ\iota_{y})^{\star}(\alpha))).

The left hand side equals

\langle f_{\star}([y]),\alpha\rangle_{Z}=\Cor_{k(f(y))/k}([k(y):k(f(y))]\iota^{\star}_{f(y)}(\alpha)).

Thus, it suffices to show
\Cor_{k(y)/k(f(y))}((f\circ\iota_{y})^{\star}(\alpha))=[k(y):k(f(y))]\iota^{\star}_{f(y)}(\alpha)
. This follows immediately by the factoring
f\circ\iota_{y}=\pi_{k(y)/k(f(y))}\circ\iota_{f(y)}
, where
\pi_{k(y)/k(f(y))}:\Spec(k(y))\to\Spec(k(f(y))
is the projection. Namely, the pullback
\pi_{k(y)/k(f(y))}^{\star}:\Br(k(f(y)))\to\Br(k(y))
coincides with the restriction map
\res_{k(y)/k(f(y))}
of Galois cohomology and we have an equality
\Cor_{k(y)/k(f(y))}\circ\res_{k(y)/k(f(y))}=\cdot[k(y):k(f(y))]
.

∎

For a smooth projective variety
Y
over
k
the Hochshild-Serre spectral sequence gives a filtration of the Brauer group

\Br(Y)\supseteq\Br_{1}(Y)\supseteq\Br_{0}(Y)\supset 0,

where
\Br_{0}(Y):=\img(\Br(k)\to\Br(Y))
are the constants and
\Br_{1}(Y)=\ker(\Br(Y)\to\Br(Y_{\overline{k}}))
is the
algebraic Brauer group
. The quotient
\Br(Y)/\Br_{1}(Y)
is known as the
transcendental Brauer group
of
Y
. It follows easily that the Brauer-Manin pairing induces a homomorphism

A_{0}(Y)/n\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_{n}^{Y}}\Hom(\Br(Y)_{n}/\Br_{0}(Y)_{n},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

Proposition 3.9
.

Consider the set-up (
3.2
). Suppose that the Néron-Severi group
\NS(A_{\overline{k}})
has trivial
\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
-action. Then for every integer
n\geq 1
there is a commutative diagram induced by the Brauer-Manin pairing

(3.10)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T(A)/n}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(\Br(A)_{n}/\Br_{1}(A)_{n},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{0}(X)/n}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(\Br(X)_{n}/\Br_{0}(X)_{n},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\star}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\pi^{\star}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon_{n}^{X}}

where the top map is obtained by factoring
\varepsilon_{n}^{A}
through
\Hom(\Br(A)_{n}/\Br_{1}(A)_{n},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
.
Moreover, if
n
is odd and the map
\varepsilon_{n}^{A}
is injective, then the map
\pi_{\star}
is an isomorphism, and the maps
\widehat{\pi^{\star}}
and
\varepsilon_{n}^{X}
are injective.

Proof.

It follows by
[
Gaz22
, Prop. 2.16]
that when the Néron-Severi group
\NS(A_{\overline{k}})
has trivial
\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
-action, the homomorphism
A_{0}(A)/n\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_{n}^{A}}\Hom(\Br(A)_{n}/\Br_{0}(A)_{n},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
when restricted to
T(A)/n
factors through the transcendental piece
\Hom(\Br(A)_{n}/\Br_{1}(A)_{n},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
. Moreover, Skorobogatov and Zharin (
[
SZ12
, Theorem 2.4]
) showed that for every integer
n\geq 1
the pullback
\pi^{\star}:\Br(X)\to\Br(A)
induces an injection

\pi^{\star}:\Br(X)_{n}/{\Br_{1}(X)_{n}}\hookrightarrow\Br(A)_{n}/\Br_{1}(A)_{n},

which is an isomorphism when

is odd. By composing with the natural quotient map, we obtain a map

\pi^{\star}:\Br(X)_{n}/\Br_{0}(X)_{n}\to\Br(A)_{n}/\Br_{1}(A)_{n}

which is a surjection when

is odd.
This shows that the diagram (
3.10
) is well-defined and its commutativity follows from the one of (
3.8
).

From now on assume that
n
is odd and
\varepsilon_{n}^{A}
is injective. As mentioned above, the map
\pi^{\star}
is surjective, and hence its dual
\widehat{\pi^{\star}}
is injective. Thus, the composition
\widehat{\pi^{\star}}\varepsilon_{n}^{A}
is injective, forcing the pushforward
\pi_{\star}
to be injective as well. Notice that we have equalities

T(A)/n=T(A)_{\nd}\{2^{\prime}\}/n,\;A_{0}(X)/n=A_{0}(X)_{\nd}\{2^{\prime}\}/n,

and hence by
subsection 2.2
the map

is also surjective.
It follows that
\pi_{\star}
is an isomorphism and
\varepsilon_{n}^{X}
is injective.

∎

Remark 3.11
.

Even if
\varepsilon_{n}^{A}
is not injective, we can note the following consequence of the proof: if for some odd

there exists
z\in T(A)
and
\alpha\in\Br_{n}(A)
with
\langle z,\alpha\rangle\neq 0
, then
\pi_{\star}(z)
is not contained in
n\cdot A_{0}(X)
.

As we saw in the beginning of this subsection, injectivity of the homomorphism
\varepsilon_{n}^{A}
is equivalent to injectivity of the cycle class map

\CH_{0}(A)/n\xrightarrow{c_{n}^{A}}H^{4}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mu_{n}^{\otimes 2}).

For a product
A
of elliptic curves the Albanese kernel
T(A)
is a direct summand of the Chow group
\CH_{0}(A)
, and hence if
c_{n}
is injective, then so is its restriction to the Albanese kernel. It is easy to see that the converse is also true (see for example
[
Yam05
, Proposition 2.4]
). Using the relation between
T(A)
and the Somekawa

-group

attached to

obtained in
[
RS00
, Theorem 2.2, Corollary 2.4.1]
, studying the map

has been reduced to the study of the generalized Galois symbol (cf.
[
Som90
, Proposition 1.5]
)

K(k;E_{1},E_{2})/n\xrightarrow{s_{n}}H^{2}(k,E_{1}[n]\otimes E_{2}[n]).

With these identifications, there are many situations where the injectivity of the cycle class map
c_{n}
has been verified. Using these results together with
subsection 3.2
we show below that for all possible reduction types included in (
3.2
) we can find large enough finite extensions

over which the group
A_{0}(X_{K})_{\nd}
is nontrivial.

Corollary 3.12
.

Consider the set-up (
3.2
) with

. Let
n\geq 1
. There is a finite extension
K/k
such that the cycle class map

c_{p^{n}}^{X}:A_{0}(X_{K})/p^{n}\rightarrow H^{4}(X_{K},\mu_{p^{n}}^{\otimes 2})

is injective, and the group

is isomorphic to

\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
or
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})^{2}
depending on the reduction type of
E_{1},E_{2}
.

Proof.

Consider a finite extension
K/k
such that the Néron-Severi group
\NS(A_{\overline{k}})
has trivial
\Gal(\overline{k}/K)
-action, both elliptic curves have split semistable reduction and

. The first assumption is possible because
\NS(A_{\overline{k}})
is a finitely generated abelian group. Depending on the reduction type of

it follows by
[
Yam05
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[

, Theorem 4.2]
that the cycle class map

is injective. Hiranouchi in
[

]
in fact showed that the group

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
if the two elliptic curves have the same reduction type (either good ordinary or split multiplicative reduction), and

if the reduction types are different. It then follows by

that the same holds for the Kummer surface,
A_{0}(X_{K})/p^{n}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
or
A_{0}(X_{K})/p^{n}\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})^{2}
.

∎

The issue is that in general the extension

considered above depends on the integer

.
In the case when both elliptic curves have good ordinary or split multiplicative reduction we can say something much stronger, obtaining a result independent of

is injective for every

.

Fix an extension

as above and

∎

.

Let

∎

The wild ramification assumption is very often satisfied. Namely, if we start with a product

Let

4.
Weak Approximation for zero-cycles

In this section we discuss applications of the local results obtained in sections (
2
) and (
3
) to local-to-global principles for zero-cycles. Throughout this section we will be working over an algebraic number field
F
. We will denote by
\Omega
the set of all places of
F
and by
\Omega_{f}
the set of all finite places. For a place
v\in\Omega
we will denote by
F_{v}
the completion of
F
at
v
. Moreover, for a variety
X
over
F
we will denote by
X_{v}:=X\otimes_{F}F_{v}
the base change to
F_{v}
. For a finite extension
K/F
we will denote by
\Omega_{K}
the set of places of
K
.

For a smooth projective variety
Y
over
F
the
adelic Chow group

\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(Y)
is defined to be the infinite product
\displaystyle\prod_{v\in\Omega}\CH_{0}^{\prime}(Y_{v})
, where
\CH_{0}^{\prime}(Y_{v})=\CH_{0}(Y_{v})
for every finite place
v
, while at infinite places
\CH_{0}^{\prime}(Y_{v})
is the cokernel of the norm (push-forward) map

N_{\overline{F}_{v}/F_{v}}:\CH_{0}(Y_{v}\otimes_{F_{v}}\overline{F}_{v})\to\CH_{0}(Y_{v}).

It follows that
\CH_{0}^{\prime}(Y_{v})=0
for all infinite complex places, while for the infinite real places the group
\CH_{0}^{\prime}(Y_{v})
is
2
-torsion (see
[
CT95
, Théorème 1.3]
). We define
A_{0,\mathbf{A}}(Y)
and
A_{0}^{\prime}(Y_{v})
in a similar way.

For each finite place
v\in\Omega_{f}
we consider the Brauer-Manin pairing (cf.
subsection 3.2
)

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{v}:\CH_{0}(Y_{v})\times\Br(Y_{v})\to\Br(F_{v})\simeq\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

Such a pairing can be defined also for every real place
v
(by embedding
\Br(\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2
to
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
), and in this case the group
N_{\overline{F}_{v}/F_{v}}(\CH_{0}(Y_{v}\otimes_{F_{v}}\overline{F}_{v}))
lies in the left kernel of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{v}
.

The local pairings induce a global pairing,

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(X)\times\Br(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},

defined by
\langle(z_{v})_{v},\alpha\rangle=\sum_{v}\langle z_{v},\iota_{v}^{\star}(\alpha)\rangle_{v}
, where
\iota_{v}^{\star}
is the pullback of
\iota_{v}:X_{v}\to X
.
The short exact sequence of global class field theory,

0\rightarrow\Br(F)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in\Omega}\Br(F_{v})\xrightarrow{\sum\inv_{v}}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0,

implies that the group
\CH_{0}(X)
lies in the left kernel of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

We are interested in the following conjecture due to Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc for geometrically rational varieties and to Kato and S. Saito for general smooth projective varieties.

Conjecture 4.1
.

(
[

, Conjecture A]
,
[

, Section 7]
, see also
[

, Section 1.1]

Y
be a smooth, projective, geometrically connected variety over a number field
F
. The following complex is exact:

(4.2)

\widehat{\CH_{0}(Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(Y)}\rightarrow\Hom(\Br(Y),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

For a general
K3
surface
X/F
Ieronymou (
[

]
) obtained the following result, which is a first step towards

.

Theorem 4.3
.

(

) Let

be a
K3

F
. Suppose that for all finite extensions

the set

is dense in

\{z_{v}\}_{v\in\Omega}
be a family of local zero-cycles of constant degree orthogonal to
\Br(X)
. Then for every

there exists a zero-cycle
z\in\CH_{0}(X)
of the same degree such that

in
\CH_{0}(X_{v})/n
for all

.

In what follows we focus on cycles of degree
0
. For a variety
Y
over
F

section 4
would imply that the following complex is exact

(4.4)

\widehat{A_{0}(Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{A_{0,\mathbf{A}}(Y)}\rightarrow\Hom(\Br(Y)/\Br_{0}(Y),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

It is easy to see that if

In this section we want to strengthen

1.

Fixing a prime

2.

Can we obtain any unconditional results?

To elaborate more on the first question, if we fix a prime number

\varprojlim\limits_{n}A_{0}(Y)/p^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\prod_{v\in\Omega}\varprojlim\limits_{n}A_{0}^{\prime}(Y_{v})/p^{n}\rightarrow\Hom(\Br(Y)\{p\}/\Br_{0}(Y)\{p\},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

Note that proving exactness of complex (

) for every prime

. Our first result is the following.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
X
be a geometrically split Kummer
K3

F
.
There exists an infinite set
T

for which the following are true:

(a)

For every
p\in T

\prod_{v|p}A_{0}(X_{v})_{\nd}\{p\}.

(b)

L/F

(c)

For all but finitely many

Proof.

The proof will be along the lines of
[

K/F

Let

\Lambda_{K}
is a finite set. Consider the following finite set of places of

\Lambda=\{v\in\Omega_{f}:\exists w\in\Lambda_{K}\text{ such that }w|v\}.

Condition 1:

Remark 4.8
.

The key point in the above proof that allowed us to pass to the limit for

in the result of Ieronymou was the

and
subsection 3.1
.
In

of elliptic curves over a number field

for all unramified places of good reduction. The cases we are missing are the places where both elliptic curves have good supersingular reduction. If we could prove this,

for Kummer surfaces associated to products of elliptic curves in its greatest generality, since any such

would reduce the problem to proving exactness of the complex (

•

primes

of bad reduction places,

•

primes that ramify in

Thus, one could try to find explicit examples of Kummer surfaces over

that satisfy the conjecture unconditionally.
We close this remark by noting that the expectation

for a Kummer surface

and for all (but finitely many) unramified places of good reduction is also motivated by the Bloch-Beilinson conjectures. For a general smooth projective variety

over a number field Bloch (

Thus, the complex

4.1.
The role of good reduction places

In this section, building from the main results in
[
Gaz22
]
, we will construct explicit examples of Kummer surfaces to show:

•

The places of good ordinary reduction can contribute nontrivially to the Brauer set for zero-cycles of degree
0
, as long as we pass to a suitable finite ramified extension.

•

Obtain unconditional evidence for the exactness of the complex (
4.5
), even in cases when there are nontrivial local families of zero-cycles that are orthogonal to the Brauer group.

We start with the following lemma, which follows by an easy diagram chasing.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
A_{i}\xrightarrow{f_{i}}B_{i}\xrightarrow{g_{i}}C_{i}
be complexes of abelian groups for
i=1,2
and suppose there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{2}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{2}}

(i)

If
\beta
is surjective,
\gamma
is injective, and the top complex is exact, then the bottom complex is exact.

(ii)

If
\alpha
is surjective,
\beta
is injective, and the bottom complex is exact, then the top complex is exact.

Proof.

First suppose
\beta
is surjective,
\gamma
is injective, and
A_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}B_{1}\xrightarrow{g_{1}}C_{1}
is exact. Let
b_{2}\in\ker(g_{2})
. Since
\beta
is surjective, there exists
b_{1}\in B_{1}
such that
\beta(b_{1})=b_{2}
. Then
\gamma(g_{1}(b_{1}))=0
, and
\gamma
is injective, which yields
b_{1}\in\ker(g_{1})=\img(f_{1})
. Thus we may write
b_{1}=f_{1}(a_{1})
for some
a_{1}\in A_{1}
. Commutativity yields
f_{2}(\alpha(a_{1}))=\beta(f_{1}(a_{1}))=\beta(b_{1})=b_{2}
. Hence
b_{2}\in\img(f_{2})
as required.

Next assume that
\alpha
is surjective,
\beta
is injective, and that
A_{2}\xrightarrow{f_{2}}B_{2}\xrightarrow{g_{2}}C_{2}
is exact. Let
b_{1}\in\ker(g_{1})
. Then
g_{2}(\beta(b_{1}))=0
, and hence
\beta(b_{1})\in\ker(g_{2})=\img(f_{2})
. Write
\beta(b_{1})=f_{2}(a_{2})
for some
a_{2}\in A_{2}
. Since
\alpha
is surjective, we can find
a_{1}\in A_{1}
such that
a_{2}=\alpha(a_{1})
. Then
\beta(f_{1}(a_{1}))=f_{2}(\alpha(a_{1}))=f_{2}(a_{2})=\beta(b_{1})
. Since
\beta
is injective we conclude that
b_{1}=f_{1}(a_{1})
, and hence
b_{1}\in\img(f_{1})
as required.

∎

Lemma 4.13
.

Consider the above set-up. For every prime number

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T(A)/p^{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{v|p}T(A_{v})_{\nd}\{p\}}

Notation 4.14
.

For an elliptic curve

Proposition 4.15
.

Let

be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
such that

has complex multiplication by the full ring of integers

of a quadratic imaginary field

the following are true:

(i)

The Brauer group
\Br(X_{L})\{p\}/\Br_{0}(X_{L})\{p\}
vanishes.

(ii)

4.5
) becomes
\displaystyle\prod_{v|p}A_{0}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}\{p\}=(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{2}

We recall the following facts about elliptic curves over

with CM (for more details we refer to
[
Gaz22
, Section 2]

The field

is one of the nine quadratic imaginary fields with class number

. That is,

is a PID. Let

be a prime that splits completely in

with

coprime to
\mathfrak{n}

for some prime element

of

has good ordinary reduction at

can be chosen so that the endomorphism

From now on assume that

and that

(b)

Remark 4.19

The assumption needed on the point

[
Gaz22

P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}=\widehat{P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\oplus\overline{P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}},

where
P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}

P

E(\mathbb{Q})\hookrightarrow E(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\twoheadrightarrow E(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/p,

\widehat{P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}

\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})/p

\overline{P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}

\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})=\overline{E_{p}}[p]

[
Gaz22
, Remark 4.8]

p

Gaz22

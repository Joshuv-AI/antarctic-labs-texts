Weighted Sylvester sums on the Frobenius set in more variables
Takao Komatsu, Yuan Zhang

Abstract
Let a 1 , a 2 , … , a k a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k} be positive integers with gcd ⁡ ( a 1 , a 2 , … , a k ) = 1 \gcd(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})=1 . Let NR = NR ⁡ ( a 1 , a 2 , … , a k ) {\rm NR}={\rm NR}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}) denote the set of positive integers nonrepresentable in terms of a 1 , a 2 , … , a k a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k} . The largest nonrepresentable integer max ⁡ NR \max{\rm NR} , the number of nonrepresentable positive integers ∑ n ∈ NR 1 \sum_{n\in{\rm NR}}1 and the sum of nonrepresentable positive integers ∑ n ∈ NR n \sum_{n\in{\rm NR}}n have been widely studied for a long time as related to the famous Frobenius problem. In this paper by using Eulerian numbers, we give formulas for the weighted sum ∑ n ∈ NR λ n ​ n μ \sum_{n\in{\rm NR}}\lambda^{n}n^{\mu} , where μ \mu is a nonnegative integer and λ \lambda is a complex number. We also examine power sums of nonrepresentable numbers and some formulae for three variables. Several examples illustrate and support our results. Keywords: Frobenius problem, weighted sums, Sylvester sums, Eulerian numbers



1
Introduction

Given positive integers
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
with
\gcd(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})=1
, it is well-known that all sufficiently large
n
can be represented as a nonnegative integer combination of
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
.
The
Frobenius Problem
is to determine the largest positive integer that is NOT representable as a nonnegative integer combination of given positive integers that are coprime (see
[
15
]
for general references). This number is denoted by
g(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
and often called Frobenius number. The Frobenius Problem has been also known as the
Coin Exchange Problem
(or Postage Stamp Problem / Chicken McNugget Problem), which has a long history and is one of the problems that has attracted many people as well as experts.

Let
n(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
be the number of positive integers with no nonnegative integer representation by
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
. It is sometimes called Sylvester number.

According to Sylvester, for positive integers
a
and
b
with
\gcd(a,b)=1
, we have

\displaystyle g(a,b)

\displaystyle=(a-1)(b-1)-1\quad{\rm\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{sy1884}{}{}]}}\,,

\displaystyle n(a,b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(a-1)(b-1)\quad{\rm\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{sy1882}{}{}]}}\,.

There are many kinds of problems related to the Frobenius problem. The problems for the number of solutions (e.g.,
[
22
]
) and the sum of integer powers of values the gaps in numerical semigroups (e.g.,
[
4
,
8
,
7
]
) are some of the interesting ones.
One of other famous problems is about the so-called
Sylvester sums

s(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})=\sum_{n\in{\rm NR}(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})}n

(see, e.g.,
[
15
, §5.5]
,
[
26
]
and references therein), where
{\rm NR}(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
denotes the set of positive integers without nonnegative integer representation by
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
. In addition, we denote by
{\rm R}(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
the set of positive integers with nonnegative integers representation by
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
.
Brown and Shiue
[
4
]
found the exact value of
s(a,b)
for positive integers
a
and
b
with
\gcd(a,b)=1
,

s(a,b)=\frac{1}{12}(a-1)(b-1)(2ab-a-b-1)\,.

(1)

Rødseth
[
17
]
generalized Brown and Shiue’s result by giving a closed form for

s_{\mu}(a,b):=\sum_{n\in{\rm NR}(a,b)}n^{\mu}\,,

where
\mu
is a positive integer.

When
k=2
, there exist beautiful closed forms for Frobenius numbers, Sylvester numbers and Sylvester sums, but
when
k\geq 3
, exact determination of these numbers is difficult.
The Frobenius number cannot be given by closed formulas of a certain type (Curtis
[
6
]
). The problem to determine
g(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
is NP-hard under Turing reduction (see, e.g., Ramírez Alfonsín
[
15
]
). Nevertheless, the Frobenius number for some special cases are calculated (e.g.,
[
13
,
16
,
18
]
). One convenient formula is by Johnson
[
10
]
. One analytic approach to the Frobenius number can be seen in
[
2
,
11
]
.

Obtaining closed forms for general case are hopeless for
k\geq 3
. Several formulae for Frobenius numbers, Sylvester numbers and Sylvester sums have been considered under special cases. For example, some formulas for the Frobenius number in three variables can be seen in
[
24
]
.

In fact, by introducing the Apéry set below, it is easy to obtain the formulas for
g(A)
,
n(A)
and
s(A)
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
a_{1}=\min(A)
.

Definition 1
.

For positive integers
A=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}\}
with
\gcd(A)=1
and
a_{1}=\min(A)
we denote by

{\rm Ape}(A)={\rm Ape}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})=\{m_{0},m_{1},\dots,m_{a_{1}-1}\}\,,

the Apéry set of
A
, where
m_{i}
is the least positive integer of
R(A)
satisfying
m_{i}\equiv i\pmod{a_{1}}

(1\leq i\leq a_{1}-1)
.
Note that
m_{0}
is defined to be
0
.

Lemma 1
.

We have

\displaystyle g(A)

\displaystyle=\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq a_{1}-1}m_{i}\right)-a_{1}\,,\quad{\rm\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{bs62}{}{}]}}

\displaystyle n(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{a_{1}-1}m_{i}-\frac{a_{1}-1}{2}\,,\quad{\rm\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{se77}{}{}]}}

\displaystyle s(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2a_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{a_{1}-1}m_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{a_{1}-1}m_{i}+\frac{a_{1}^{2}-1}{12}\,.\quad{\rm\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{tr08}{}{}]}}

Note that the third formula appeared with a typo in
[
23
]
, and it has been corrected in
[
14
,
25
]
.

In this paper, we are interesting in giving a formula for the weighted sum

s_{\mu}^{(\lambda)}(A):=\sum_{n\in{\rm NR}(A)}\lambda^{n}n^{\mu}\,,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. More precisely, if
\lambda\neq 1
and
\lambda^{a_{1}}\neq 1
, then

\displaystyle s_{\mu}^{(\lambda)}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\mu}\frac{(-a_{1})^{n}}{(\lambda^{a_{1}}-1)^{n+1}}\binom{\mu}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left\langle n\atop n-j\right\rangle\lambda^{ja_{1}}\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}m_{i}^{\mu-n}\lambda^{m_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{\mu+1}}{(\lambda-1)^{\mu+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu}\left\langle\mu\atop\mu-j\right\rangle\lambda^{j}

(see Theorem
1
below). Here, the Eulerian number
\left\langle n\atop l\right\rangle
is the number of permutations of
1,2,\dots,n
in which exactly
l
elements are greater than the previous element (see, e.g.,
[
9
, §6.2]
,
[
19
, A008292]
).
If
\lambda=1
, then

s_{\mu}^{(1)}(A)=\sum_{\kappa=0}^{\mu}\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa+1}\binom{\mu}{\kappa}\binom{\kappa+1}{j}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{\kappa+1}a_{1}^{\kappa-j}B_{\kappa-j+1}\sum_{i=1}^{a_{1}-1}(m_{i}-i)^{j}m_{i}^{\mu-\kappa}\,,

where
B_{n}
is the Bernoulli number (see Theorem
5
below).

When
k=2
, the general formula for weighted sums is given in
[
12
]
. In fact, the weighted power sums can be expressed in terms of the Apostol-Bernoulli numbers.
When
k>2
, no general closed form for the functions
g
,
n
and
s
have been discovered. However, for some special cases or conditional cases, convenient forms have been known. Whenever an additional condition
a|{\rm lcm}(b,c)
holds, the following results are known in
[
25
]
.

Lemma 2
.

Set
l_{1}:={\rm lcm}(a,b)
and
l_{2}:={\rm lcm}(a,c)
. Then,

\displaystyle g(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=l_{1}+l_{2}-(a+b+c)\,,

\displaystyle n(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(l_{1}+l_{2}-(a+b+c)+1\bigr)\,,

\displaystyle s(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}\bigl(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+3(abc+ab+bc+ca)

\displaystyle\qquad-3(a+b+c)(l_{1}+l_{2})+2(l_{1}^{2}+l_{2}^{2})-1\bigr)\,.

With the same condition
a|{\rm lcm}(b,c)
, we obtain the explicit formula

\displaystyle s^{(\lambda)}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\frac{l_{1}(\lambda^{l_{2}}-1)+l_{2}(\lambda^{l_{1}}-1)+(l_{1}+l_{2}-a-b-c)(\lambda^{l_{1}}-1)(\lambda^{l_{2}}-1)}{(\lambda^{a}-1)(\lambda^{b}-1)(\lambda^{c}-1)}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(\lambda^{l_{1}}-1)(\lambda^{l_{2}}-1)}{(\lambda^{a}-1)(\lambda^{b}-1)(\lambda^{c}-1)}\left(\frac{a}{\lambda^{a}-1}+\frac{b}{\lambda^{b}-1}+\frac{c}{\lambda^{c}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda-1)^{2}}\,,

where
\lambda\neq 1
with
\lambda^{a}\neq 1
,
\lambda^{b}\neq 1
and
\lambda^{c}\neq 1
(see Theorem
6
below).

Main results

In this section, we consider the power sums of nonrepresentable numbers

s_{\mu}^{(\lambda)}(A):=\sum_{n\in{\rm NR}(A)}\lambda^{n}n^{\mu}\,,

where
\mu
is a positive integer, and
A:=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}
is the set of positive integers with
\gcd(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})=1

First, assume that
\lambda^{a_{1}}\neq 1
.

Eulerian numbers
\left\langle n\atop m\right\rangle
appear in the generating function

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{n}x^{k}=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{n+1}}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left\langle n\atop m\right\rangle x^{m+1}\quad(n\geq 1)

with
0^{0}=1
and
\left\langle 0\atop 0\right\rangle=1
(
[
5
, p.244]
),
and have an explicit formula

\left\langle n\atop m\right\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{m+1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{k}(m-k+1)^{n}

(
[
5
, p.243]
). From the definition, we have

\left\langle n\atop k\right\rangle=\left\langle n\atop n-k-1\right\rangle\quad(0\leq k\leq n-1),

in particular, we have

\left\langle n\atop 0\right\rangle=\left\langle n\atop n-1\right\rangle=1\quad\hbox{and}\quad\left\langle n\atop n\right\rangle=0\quad(n\geq 1)\,.

For
1\leq i\leq a_{1}-1
, let
m_{i}
be defined in Definition
1
. Then,
m_{i}-a_{1}
is the largest positive integer in
{\rm NR}(A)
among those congruent to

. Hence, there exists a nonnegative integer
\ell_{i}
such that

m_{i}-a_{1},m_{i}-2a_{1},\dots,m_{i}-\ell_{i}a_{1}\in{\rm NR}(A)

with
m_{i}-\ell_{i}a_{1}>0
and
m_{i}-(\ell_{i}+1)a_{1}<0
.
We want to obtain

s_{\mu}^{(\lambda)}(A)=\sum_{n\in{\rm NR}(A)}\lambda^{n}n^{\mu}=\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\lambda^{m_{i}-ja_{1}}(m_{i}-ja_{1})^{\mu}\,.

(2)

Since
m_{i}\equiv i\pmod{a_{1}}
and
1\leq i\leq a_{1}-1
, we get
\ell_{i}=(m_{i}-i)/a_{1}
.
Let
\lambda\neq 1
and
\lambda^{a_{1}}\neq 1

Then, the weighted sum of elements in
{\rm NR}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\lambda^{m_{i}-ja_{1}}(m_{i}-ja_{1})^{\mu}\,.

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{m_{i}-ja_{1}}j^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{m_{i}}}{(1-\lambda^{-a_{1}})^{n+1}}\sum_{h=0}^{n-1}\left\langle n\atop h\right\rangle\lambda^{-(h+1)a_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{m_{i}}}{(\lambda^{a_{1}}-1)^{n+1}}\sum_{h=0}^{n-1}\left\langle n\atop h\right\rangle\lambda^{(n-h)a_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{m_{i}}}{(\lambda^{a_{1}}-1)^{n+1}}\sum_{h=1}^{n}\left\langle n\atop n-h\right\rangle\lambda^{ha_{1}}\,.

So, for
n\geq 0
, we get

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{m_{i}-ja_{1}}j^{n}=\frac{\lambda^{m_{i}}}{(\lambda^{a_{1}}-1)^{n+1}}\sum_{h=0}^{n}\left\langle n\atop n-h\right\rangle\lambda^{ha_{1}}\,.

Hence, for
\ell_{i}=(m_{i}-i)/a_{1}

\displaystyle s_{\mu}^{(\lambda)}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\lambda^{m_{i}-ja_{1}}(m_{i}-ja_{1})^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\lambda^{m_{i}-ja_{1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{n}m_{i}^{\mu-n}j^{n}(-a_{1})^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{n}m_{i}^{\mu-n}(-a_{1})^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{m_{i}-ja_{1}}j^{n}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{n}m_{i}^{\mu-n}(-a_{1})^{n}\sum_{j=\ell_{i}+1}^{\infty}\lambda^{m_{i}-ja_{1}}j^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}\sum_{n=1}^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{n}m_{i}^{\mu-n}(-a_{1})^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{m_{i}-ja_{1}}j^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}m_{i}^{\mu}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{m_{i}-ja_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{n}m_{i}^{\mu-n}(-a_{1})^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{i-ja_{1}}\left(j+\frac{m_{i}-i}{a_{1}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}\sum_{n=1}^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{n}m_{i}^{\mu-n}(-a_{1})^{n}\frac{\lambda^{m_{i}}}{(\lambda^{a_{1}}-1)^{n+1}}\sum_{h=1}^{n}\left\langle n\atop n-h\right\rangle\lambda^{ha_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}m_{i}^{\mu}\frac{\lambda^{m_{i}}}{\lambda^{a_{1}}-1}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{n}m_{i}^{\mu-n}(-a_{1})^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{i-ja_{1}}\left(j+\frac{m_{i}-i}{a_{1}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\mu}\frac{(-a_{1})^{n}}{(\lambda^{a_{1}}-1)^{n+1}}\binom{\mu}{n}\sum_{h=0}^{n}\left\langle n\atop n-h\right\rangle\lambda^{ha_{1}}\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}m_{i}^{\mu-n}\lambda^{m_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i=0}^{a_{1}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{i-ja_{1}}(i-ja_{1})^{\mu}\,.

Since the last term is equal to

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-k}(-k)^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\mu+1}}{(1-\lambda^{-1})^{\mu+1}}\sum_{m=0}^{\mu-1}\left\langle\mu\atop m\right\rangle\lambda^{-(m+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\mu+1}}{(\lambda-1)^{\mu+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu}\left\langle\mu\atop\mu-j\right\rangle\lambda^{j}\,,

we have the desired result.

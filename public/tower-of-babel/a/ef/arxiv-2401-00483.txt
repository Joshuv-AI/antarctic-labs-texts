Magnetic properties at various fillings of the quasiflat band in a fermionic two-leg ladder model
Paban Kumar Patra, Yixuan Huang, Hridis K. Pal

Abstract
A recent study has demonstrated that a fermionic two-leg ladder model, threaded by a flux and characterized by a spatially varying interleg hopping term, gives rise to a quasiflat low-energy band. This band exhibits an unusual ground state at half filling in the presence of interaction—a ferromagnetic Mott insulator. In this paper, we extend the study of this model to other fillings of the quasiflat band and explore the magnetic properties of the ground state at these fillings. In particular, we study four fillings: one-quarter, three-quarters, slightly above half filling (half filling plus two electrons), and slightly below half-filling (half filling minus two electrons). Incorporating interaction within the Hubbard model and using the Density Matrix Renormalization Group method to find the ground states, we find that the spin-spin correlation is ferromagnetic at fillings less than half, similar to that observed at half filling, but is antiferromagnetic beyond half filling. Interestingly, these results hold only when mixing between the lowest quasiflat band and the next-to-lowest dispersive band is negligible; once mixing between the two bands is facilitated by increasing the interaction strength, the correlation becomes ferromagnetic above half filling as well. Additionally, by reducing the strength of the interaction in comparison to the bandwidth, a transition from the ferromagnetic to the antiferromagnetic state is observed in all the cases.



I
Introduction

The interplay of reduced dimension and strong correlations often leads to interesting ground states in quantum many-body systems. The simplest fermionic model studied in this regard is the Hubbard model in one dimension (1D) with nearest-neighbor hopping
t
and onsite interaction
U
. By changing the two parameters, one can easily tune the interaction strength with respect to the bandwidth, and study its effect on the ground state properties. The 1D Hubbard model has been studied extensively over the past decades across the parameter space of
t
and
U
, and a variety of interesting phases have been discovered, both in the charge and the spin sectors
Lieb and Wu 2003
;
Giamarchi 2003
;
Shiba and Pincus 1972
;
Essler et al. 2005
;
Frahm and Korepin 1990
. An interesting exact result in the spin sector is due to Lieb and Mattis
Lieb and Mattis 1962
which asserts that the ground state has the smallest possible spin at any filling, thus precluding the appearance of ferromagnetism. Nevertheless, certain models that incorporate beyond-nearest-neighbor hopping terms at the noninteracting level (thereby circumventing Lieb and Mattis’s theorem) can give rise to ferromagnetism
Tasaki 1992
;
Tasaki 1995
;
Mielke 1991
;
Daul and Noack 1998
. An essential feature of these models is that they all host a low-energy flat band at the noninteracting level and the effect of interaction is nonperturbative.

In a recent work
Huang et al. 2020
, a one-dimensional model supporting a low-energy quasiflat band was proposed. The model, at the noninteracting level, consists of a two-leg ladder pierced by a constant flux with a spatially varying interleg hopping term. In a certain parameter regime, the bandwidth of the lowest-energy band becomes very small leading to a quasiflat band. The model does not employ any beyond-nearest-neighbor hopping terms and is qualitatively distinct from those mentioned earlier. The effect of interaction was studied at half filling of the quasiflat band
Huang et al. 2020
and the ground state was found to be that of a ferromagnetic Mott insulator, instead of an antiferromagnetic one as found conventionally
Giamarchi 2003
;
Fazekas 1999
.

In light of the above findings, it is natural to ask whether the unusual features of the ground state persist at fillings other than half filling of the quasiflat band. We address the question in this work by studying the spin-spin correlation within the Hubbard model at one-quarter, three-quarters, slightly above half, and slightly below half fillings of the quasiflat band. Our key findings are as follows: (1) For values of
U
where band mixing can be ignored, the ground state exhibits ferromagnetic correlations at fillings less than half, akin to half filling, but displays antiferromagnetic correlation above half filling. (2) However, at larger values of
U
allowing for mixing between the lowest quasiflat band and the next-to-lowest band, the correlation becomes ferromagnetic above half filling as well, i.e., the ground state exhibits ferromagnetism irrespective of the filling. Additionally, whenever the ground state is ferromagnetic, on reducing the strength of the interaction with respect to the bandwidth, the spin-spin correlation undergoes a transition to the antiferromagnetic state.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
, we introduce our model and describe the method we employ to calculate the ground state. In Section
III
we present our main results—the spin-spin correlation at various fillings of the quasiflat band. In Section
IV
, we discuss various features of our findings. Our concluding remarks are presented in Section
V
.

We consider a two-leg ladder model, as shown in Fig.
1
, with the following single-particle Hamiltonian:

H_{0}=-t\sum_{j,\sigma,\lambda}c_{j+1\sigma\lambda}^{\dagger}c_{j\sigma\lambda}+\sum_{j,\sigma}\gamma_{j}c_{j\sigma 2}^{\dagger}c_{j\sigma 1}+\mathrm{h.c.}.

(1)

Here,
c_{j\sigma\lambda}^{\dagger}
(
c_{j\sigma\lambda}
) represents the creation (annihilation) operator for an electron at site j with spin
\sigma=\downarrow,\uparrow
and leg index
\lambda=1,2
. The parameters
t
and
\gamma_{j}
describe nearest-neighbor hopping within a leg and between two legs, respectively. Importantly, while
t
is the same at all sites,
\gamma_{j}
depends on the site index; we parameterize it as

\gamma_{j}=\gamma_{0}\left(1+g\ \mathrm{cos}\frac{2\pi j}{J}\right)e^{i\frac{2\pi j}{J}},

(2)

where
\gamma_{0}
and
g
are real constants. The imaginary exponent implies a constant flux piercing through each plaquette of the ladder and the modulating prefactor implies that the interleg hopping strength varies along the length of the ladder resulting in a supercell. The resultant band structure consists of
2J
Bloch bands. A curious feature is that the lowest-energy Bloch band becomes quasiflat in some regime of the parameters
\gamma_{0}
and
g
. The next-to-lowest band, however, remains dispersive. This is illustrated in Fig.
1
. In Fig.
1
, we plot the bandwidth of the two bands as well as the band gap between them as a function of
\gamma_{0}
. In the plots, we have used
t=1
,
g=0.1
and
J=6
, which we keep fixed throughout the paper unless otherwise stated. The bandwidth of the lowest band becomes minimum when
\gamma_{0}=0.49
, leading to a bandwidth
\delta_{BW}=7.5\times 10^{-3}
and a bandgap
\Delta_{BG}=4.89\times 10^{-2}
, while the next-to-lowest band remains dispersive. The parameter
\gamma_{0}
can be changed to tune the bandwidth of the lowest band and will be used as a proxy for the latter in the remainder of this paper.

We next add interaction to (
1
):

H_{\mathrm{int}}=U\sum_{j,\lambda}n_{j\uparrow\lambda}n_{j\downarrow\lambda}.

(3)

The parameter
U
represents the onsite Hubbard interaction strength with
n_{j\sigma}
indicating the number of electrons at site
j
with spin
\sigma
. We use the finite Density Matrix Renormalization Group (DMRG) technique
White 1992
;
White 1993
;
Schollwöck 2011
with open boundary conditions to find the ground state in the presence of interactions at various fillings of the lowest quasiflat band. The calculations are conducted using the ITensor library
Fishman et al. 2022a
;
Fishman et al. 2022b
. To mitigate finite-size effects, we have investigated systems of different sizes, reaching a maximum of
2L=2\times 192
sites and a cutoff error below
10^{-9}
with bond dimensions up to 3000 (see Appendix
A
for further details). The results presented below, unless otherwise stated, are for
2L=2\times 144
sites. The energy difference between the two iterations remains lower than
10^{-7}
throughout the final sweeps. Total charge and
S_{z}
conservation methods are employed to enhance convergence.

Once the ground state is calculated, we study its magnetic properties by computing the average spin-spin correlation
\langle\overrightarrow{S}_{i\lambda}\cdot\overrightarrow{S}_{j\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle
, where
\overrightarrow{S}_{i\lambda}
is the total spin at site
i
on leg
\lambda
. We consider both the intraleg (
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
and
i\neq j
) as well as the interleg (
i=j
and
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
) correlations.

III
Results

To put our results in context and for ease of comparison, we first summarize the results at half filling of the quasiflat band found earlier in Ref.
Huang et al. 2020
. With
U=0.4
such that
\braket{U}/\delta_{BW}\approx 1.17
and
\braket{U}/\Delta_{BG}\approx 0.18
, the main findings are as follows (see Figs. 2 and 3 in Ref.[
Huang et al. 2020
]). In the charge sector, a density wave is found with the same periodicity as the supercell size, i.e.,
J=6
. A charge gap is observed which decreases with decreasing
U
and goes to zero
U\approx 0.1
signalling a nonzero-
U
metal-insulator (Mott) transition. In the spin sector, the spin-spin correlation is positive across all sites, for both intraleg and interleg cases, indicating a ferromagnetic ground state. However, as the bandwidth is increased by changing
\gamma_{0}
, the spin-spin correlation changes from ferromagnetic to antiferromagnetic. In comparison with the standard 1D Hubbard model at half filling, differences are observed in both charge and spin sectors: the metal-insulator transition occurs at a nonzero
U
as opposed to a zero value and the spin-spin correlation is ferromagnetic in the strongly correlated regime as opposed to being antiferromagnetic.

We now study other fillings of the quasiflat band to inquire whether they also exhibit such departures. Away from half filling, within only the onsite Hubbard approximation, the charge gap is expected to be zero and the question of a metal-insulator transition does not arise—supporting evidence is presented in Appendix
B
. Therefore, we focus exclusively on the spin sector and investigate the spin-spin correlation, both intraleg and interleg, at these fillings.

III.1
One-quarter filling

We first study the case of one-quarter filling of the lowest band. This occurs at the value
n=12/288
. We choose
U=1

\braket{U}/\delta_{BW}\approx 1.7

\braket{U}/\Delta_{BG}\approx 0.26

\gamma_{0}=0.49

The intraleg and interleg spin-spin correlations are presented in Figs.

2

IV
Discussion

A simple two-leg ladder model with only nearest-neighbor hopping and onsite interaction is not qualitatively different from a standard 1D Hubbard model as regards the magnetic properties of the ground state. It has been extensively studied in the past and a ferromagnetic ground state has not been reported
Giamarchi 2003
. Similarly, a two-leg ladder model with flux with constant interleg hopping have also been investigated before, and no sign of ferromagnetic correlations has been found
Carr et al. 2006
;
Narozhny et al. 2005
. Our results indicate that the model studied in this work is fundamentally different from other ladder models considered before and the quasiflat band in this model is unconventional. To the best of our knowledge, there is no existing theory that adequately explains our results.

Despite the lack of a comprehensive theoretical understanding, we can attempt to gain some insights into the contributing factors. There are two aspects in our ladder model that set it apart from the conventional scenario: the presence of flux and a periodically varying interleg hopping parameter. To discern the significance of each feature in the emergence of ferromagnetism, we systematically eliminate them, one at a time, and reevaluate spin-spin correlations, focusing exclusively on the half-filled case. For proper comparison, we choose the hopping parameters and the interaction strength such that
\delta_{BW}
and
\langle U\rangle/\delta_{BW}
are the same in all the cases. First, we switch off the periodicity in the interleg hopping but retain the flux, i.e., we use
\gamma_{j}=\gamma_{0}\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{2\pi j}{J}}
in Eq.
1
. Using the parameters
\gamma_{0}=0.49
and
t=0.002
to achieve a bandwidth
\delta_{BW}\approx 0.0075
, and choosing
U=0.07
such that
\langle U\rangle/\delta_{BW}\approx 1.17
, we find the spin-spin correlation to be antiferromagnetic, as shown in Fig.
6
. Next, we remove the flux and maintain only the periodicity in the interleg hopping, i.e. we use
\gamma_{j}=\gamma_{0}(1+g\cos\frac{2\pi j}{J})
in Eq.
1
. Setting
\gamma_{0}=22
and
t=1
gives us the same bandwidth as before, and using
U=0.15
to ensure that
\langle U\rangle/\delta_{BW}
is similar to the previous case, the spin-spin correlation is again calculated and found to be antiferromagnetic, as seen in Fig.
6
. Both these observations are in contrast to the emergence of a ferromagnetic spin-spin correlation when both features are present at the noninteracting level. This is shown in Fig.
6
where the parameters
t=1
,
\gamma_{0}=0.49
, and
U=0.4
have been used to achieve the same values for
\delta_{BW}
and
\langle U\rangle/\delta_{BW}
as in the previous two cases. In the first two cases, we have also tried higher values of
U
—up to an order of magnitude more—but no qualitative change is observed. This unequivocally confirms that ferromagnetism in our model arises from a combination of both flux and periodicity in the interleg hopping at the noninteracting level, with both features being equally vital.

V
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have numerically studied the magnetic properties at various fillings of the quasiflat band in a two-leg ladder model with flux and periodic interleg hopping. Specifically, we have computed the spin-spin correlation within the Hubbard model at one-quarter, three-quarters, slightly above half, and slightly below half fillings of the quasiflat band. Three key observations emerge from our findings.

First, in the regime when interactions do not induce substantial band mixing, the nature of the spin-spin correlations is manifestly different below and above half filling—it is ferromagnetic in the former (and qualitatively similar to what is observed at half filling) but antiferromagnetic in the latter. Second, at higher interaction strength, when the lowest two bands are allowed to mix, this distinction vanishes and the ground state becomes ferromagnetic at all fillings of the lowest band. And third, ferromagnetism survives only when the interaction is strong. Regardless of the filling, as the system is made less interacting—either effectively by increasing the bandwidth or explicitly by increasing the interaction strength—the system loses its ferromagnetic character and transitions into an antiferromagnetic state. Thus, the unusual ferromagnetic property is nonperturbative.

It is evident that the ladder model explored in this work, particularly the quasiflat band it manifests at low energy, opens up a novel avenue for investigating interaction-driven phases and quantum magnetism in one-dimensional systems. Furthermore, our model could be simulated using cold atoms in fermionic flux ladders
Mancini et al. 2015
, providing a practical means to experimentally study the unusual features uncovered in this work and possibly other ones yet to be found.

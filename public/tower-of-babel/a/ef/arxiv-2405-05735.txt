Resolution of 1-foliation singularities on surfaces and threefolds
Quentin Posva

Abstract




Abstract.
We consider resolution of singularities for
1
-foliations on varieties of dimension at most three in positive characteristic. We prove that such singularities can be completely resolved if we allow tame regular Deligne–Mumford stacks as underlying spaces. If one restricts to underlying varieties, we show that
1
-foliations singularities can be simplified into multiplicative ones.

1.
Introduction

On a normal variety
X
over a field of positive characteristic
p>0
, a
1
-foliation is a saturated sub-sheaf of
T_{X}
that is closed under Lie brackets and
p
-th powers. It follows from their definition that
1
-foliations are uniquely determined by their generic stalks, and so it is relatively easy to produce examples. However they generally acquire complicated singularities at special points, and a desingularization statement was previously known only in the special case
p=2=\dim

[
Hir99
]
. If
p>2
, then straightforward computations show that already on surfaces we cannot hope to resolve the singularities of the
1
-foliation germ
x\partial_{x}+\lambda y\partial_{y}
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0,1\}
on
(\mathbb{0}\in\mathbb{A}^{2}_{x,y})
while preserving the regularity of the underlying surface (see for example
[
Tzi17
, Remark 4.8]
). But these
1
-foliations have rather simple singularities (see
[
Pos24
, §§4–5]
), and one can wonder if they are the only obstruction to a full desingularization statement. One purpose of this article is to show that it is indeed the case, at least for
1
-foliations of corank
1
on surfaces and threefolds:

Theorem 1

(
Theorem 4.0.1
and
Theorem 5.1.1
)
.

Let
X
be a normal variety of dimension
d\leq 3
over an algebraically closed field
k
of positive characteristic, and
\mathcal{F}
be a
1
-foliation on
X
. Then there exists a projective birational morphism
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
such that if
s\in\Sing(f^{*}\mathcal{F})
, then there exists étale coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime},s}^{\text{h}}
such that
f^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime},s}^{\text{h}}
is generated by derivations of the form

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\quad\text{or}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\ \text{ with }\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}.

In the case
\dim X=2
, the starting point of the proof is a result due to Giraud
[
Gir83
]
which asserts that a given section
u\in\Gamma(X^{(1)},\mathcal{O}_{X^{(1)}})
, after a sequence of blow-ups, can be given everywhere étale-locally the normal form

u=v^{p}+\mathbb{x}^{\mathbb{a}}

where
\mathbb{x}
is a
p
-basis and
\mathbb{a}\neq\mathbb{0}
modulo
p
. This allows us to describe the normalization of the
p
-cyclic cover
X^{(1)}[\sqrt[p]{u}]
as a ring of constants for derivations of the desired type (
Proposition 2.2.2
). Using Jacobson’s correspondence
[
Pos24
, Theorem 2.5.12]
, it follows that the
1
-foliation on
X
defining the sub-extension
K(X^{(1)})\left[\sqrt[p]{u}\right]\subset K(X)
is étale-locally generated by derivations of the desired form. This settles the surface case.

The corank
1
case on threefolds is proved similarly using Cossart’s generalization of Giraud’s result
[
Cos87a
]
. We actually need a stronger statement than the main result of
[
Cos87b
]
, but which follows quite easily from the same arguments. In the
Appendix A
we outline Cossart’s methods and explain why they imply the result that we need.

The Jacobson correspondence allows one to deduce some results about the corank
2
case on threefolds, but if we want to ensure regularity of the underlying scheme we are faced with the problem of lifting derivations along resolutions of linearly reductive quotient singularities. We work out the surface case in
Proposition 2.3.5
, relying on earlier work of Wahl and Hirokado, but we leave the threefold case open to an interested reader. See
Remark 5.2.2
for more details. Nevertheless, as we will see below, there is a nicer approach to the corank
2
case.

We can go further than
Theorem 1
. It has been realized during recent years that stacks and weighted blow-ups provide powerful tools for resolution of singularities, simplifying and streamlining the resolution algorithms in characteristic
0
(see
[
ATWo23
]
for a survey). Following this trend, one may ask whether weighted blow-ups can help with the resolution of
1
-foliation singularities. Now, local calculations show that weighted blow-ups are exactly the missing piece to obtain a full desingularization for
1
-foliations on surfaces! More precisely, to resolve
x\partial_{x}+\lambda y\partial_{y}
one blow-ups
(x,y)
with weights
(1,\tilde{\lambda})
, where
\tilde{\lambda}\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
reduces to
\lambda
modulo
p
. This settles at once the surface case.

The threefold case is more delicate, since the singular locus of the
1
-foliation is in general one-dimensional and the étale coordinates given by
Theorem 1
are not natural in any helpful way. Nonetheless, if we work with a given set of coordinates, then there is a simple algorithm that resolves the singularities of
1
-foliations of corank
1
with two steps of weighted blow-ups, and one only has to prove that the Rees algebras defining these blow-ups are independent of the coordinates: this is shown in
Claim 5.1.7
.

Once this is done, the corank
2
should in principle follow at once through the Jacobson correspondence. However at this stage we are dealing with
1
-foliations on Deligne–Mumford stacks, so some foundational work has to be taken care off. We do so in

: everything follows from the schematic counterparts and from the properties of étale morphisms.

Putting everything together, we obtain:

Remark 1.0.2

(Resolution of foliations in characteristic
0
in dimension
\leq 3
)
.

Similar resolution statements for foliations on complex varieties are known (
1

1

), with the caveat that a larger range of final singularities must be allowed. Thus in the light of

In dimension
2
, resolution of complex foliation singularities is a well-known theorem of Seidenberg. It guarantees that we can simplify the singularities of a line foliation
\mathcal{F}
on a surface
S
until at each
s\in\Sing(\mathcal{F})
with local coordinates

we can write

\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S,s}=\mathcal{O}_{S,s}\cdot(a(x,y)\partial_{x}+b(x,y)\partial_{y})

with the property that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
of the Jacobian matrix

are not both zero, and that their ratio does not belong to
\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
. Furthermore, the Poincaré–Dulac theorem asserts that if the ratio does not belong to
\mathbb{Q}
, then
\mathcal{F}\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{S,s}
is generated by
\lambda_{1}\bar{x}\partial_{\bar{x}}+\lambda_{2}\bar{y}\partial_{\bar{y}}
for some formal coordinates
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{S,s}
. Not every final foliation singularities is formally linearizable (e.g. the saddle-node); but as noticed by McQuillan
[
McQ08
, Proposition II.1.3]
, and in accordance with our
Theorem 1
, if a complex derivation has
p
-closed reduction for all-but-finitely-many primes
p
then it is formally linearizable.

In dimension
3
, the complex corank
1
case is due to Cano
[
Can04
]
. His methods are similar in spirit to those of Cossart
[
Cos87a
]
, and so our

The corank
2

is more delicate. By
[

]
, one can always obtain only “log elementary” singularities using smooth blow-ups. But as explained in
[

we cannot always obtain elementary singularities (equivalently log canonical singularities, see
[

]
or
[

[

that the situation is largely clarified if one works with regular Deligne–Mumford stacks as underlying spaces and weighted blow-ups as modifications: in this set-up one can simplify foliations until they only have canonical singularities. See also
[

for an alternative proof and an extended discussion on that topic.

Remark 1.0.3

(Resolution of vector fields in positive characteristic)
.

Our methods rely heavily on Jacobson’s correspondence, and thus give no insight into the resolution of non-

1.1.
Digression:
1
-foliations and formal groupoids

A desingularization statement is usually the first step for a Minimal Model Program of some sort. We shall comment briefly on the topic of the MMP for (
1
-)foliations.

From the point of view of birational geometry, a general
1
-foliation in positive characteristic is not be well-behaved
[
Ber24
]
: the cone theorem, the base-point freeness theorem and the existence of Mori fiber spaces might all fail. Thus there is no hope to obtain an MMP for
1
-foliations, while in characteristic
0
the MMP for foliations has been developed on surfaces
[
Bru15
,
McQ08
,
Men00
]
and (in most cases) on threefolds

[
McQ08
,
CS21
,
CS20
,
SS22
]
, and even in greater generality for foliations induced by fibrations
[
CS25
,
CHLX23
]
.

In characteristic
0
, there are several equivalent points of view on foliations: a first order datum
\mathcal{F}\subset T_{X/k}
determines a sub-algebra
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\subset\Diff_{X/k}
, which in turn can be seen as an infinitesimal equivalence relation on
X
. In positive characteristic, all these notions are different and
1
-foliations (that is, first order data) are the less structured ones. So one can expect that more constrained objects behave better from the perspective of birational geometry.

In an effort to develop a characteristic-free study of foliations, McQuillan introduces in
[
McQ22
]
the notion of
formal groupoids
, which encapsulates the idea of infinitesimal equivalence relation. A formal groupoid on
X
is an infinitesimal groupoid
g\colon\mathfrak{F}\to X\times_{k}X
in the category of formal schemes such that, generically over
X
, both projections
p_{i}\circ g
are formally smooth and
g
factors through an embedding into the completion
\widehat{\Delta}
of
X\times X
along its diagonal. This notion leads to a satisfactory MMP for (suitable mildly singular) formal groupoids on surfaces
[
McQ22
, §§5-6]
. So formal groupoids appear to be more suited to birational geometry than
1
-foliations.

However, apart from smooth dominant morphisms, we currently lack a good source of examples of midly singular formal groupoids in positive characteristic. Any
\mathfrak{F}\rightrightarrows X
determines a
1
-foliation by truncation to the first order (see
[
Bon23
, §4.5]
for a clear explanation of this procedure), but not every
1
-foliation can be integrated into a formal groupoid. The obstruction already appears for regular
1
-foliations
\mathcal{F}
: one has to, at the very least, extend
\mathcal{F}
into a sub-algebra
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\subset\Diff_{X/k}
of the sheaf of Grothendieck differential operators with the property that
\gr(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is isomorphic to the symmetric divided power algebra
\Gamma(\mathcal{F})
(cf.
[
McQ22
, 1.6]
). This is possible locally at any point
s\in X\setminus\Sing(\mathcal{F})
: for then by
[
Pos24
, Lemma 2.4.5]
there are local coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in\mathcal{O}_{X,s}
such that
\mathcal{F}
is generated by the
\partial_{x_{1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{r}}
, and one can integrate
\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X,s}
into the sub-algebra of
\Diff(\mathcal{O}_{X,s})
generated by the operators
\frac{1}{a!}\frac{\partial^{a}}{\partial x_{i}^{a}}

(i\leq r,a\geq 1)
. The problem is that these extensions are not unique: see
[
Gra23
, Example 5.7]
for an extension of
\partial_{y}\in\Der_{k}(k(x,y))
into an algebra
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
of differential operators that is not generated by
\{\frac{1}{a!}\frac{\partial^{a}}{\partial y^{a}}\mid a\geq 1\}
, but which nonetheless satisfies
\gr(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\cong\Gamma(\mathcal{F}^{1})
.

A natural question is whether we can generalize our resolution theorems to the setting of formal groupoids. We will not investigate it here, but we make the following remark.

.

2.3.
Lifts of derivations along resolutions

There is no difficulty in pulling back foliations along birational morphisms (see
[

In this section we work over an algebraically closed field

In case of blow-ups of regular varieties at regular centers, the transformation rules for derivations are easy to understand:

Example 2.3.1

(Pullback along smooth blow-ups)

We explain how foliations pullback along smooth blow-ups on the affine space.

(a)

Let
S\to\mathbb{A}^{2}_{x,y}
be the blow-up of the origin. One chart of the blow-up is given by

k[x,y]\longrightarrow k[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}],\quad(x,y)\mapsto(x^{\prime},x^{\prime}y^{\prime}).

Then

\partial_{x}(y^{\prime})=-y/x^{2}=-y^{\prime}/x^{\prime}

This implies that
\partial_{x}=\partial_{x^{\prime}}-(y^{\prime}/x^{\prime})\partial_{y^{\prime}}
.
Similar computations show that

\partial_{y}=x^{\prime}\partial_{y^{\prime}}

A simple way to summarize these identities is the following:

(2.3.1.a)

\begin{cases}x\partial_{x}&=x^{\prime}\partial_{x^{\prime}}-y^{\prime}\partial_{y^{\prime}},\\
y\partial_{y}&=y^{\prime}\partial_{y^{\prime}}.\end{cases}

As the differentials
\partial_{x}
and

pullback to

on this chart. To describe pullbacks of foliations, we just have to saturate the sub-module generated by the pullbacks of the vector fields in

. The transformation rules on the other chart are symmetric.

(b)

Let

be the blow-up of the origin. One chart of the blow-up is given by

k[x,y,z]\longrightarrow k[x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}],\quad(x,y,z)\mapsto(x^{\prime},x^{\prime}y^{\prime},x^{\prime}z^{\prime}).

After calculations similar to the ones above, we find the transformation rules

(2.3.1.b)

\begin{cases}x\partial_{x}&=x^{\prime}\partial_{x^{\prime}}-y^{\prime}\partial_{y^{\prime}}-z^{\prime}\partial_{z^{\prime}},\\
y\partial_{y}&=y^{\prime}\partial_{y^{\prime}},\\
z\partial_{z}&=z^{\prime}\partial_{z^{\prime}}.\end{cases}

The situation on the two other charts are obtained by permuting the roles of the coordinates.

(c)

More generally, the reader will check that if

\pi^{*}\partial_{y_{1}}=\partial_{y_{1}}-\sum_{i=2}^{r}\frac{y_{i}}{y_{1}}\partial_{y_{i}},\quad\pi^{*}\partial_{x_{i}}=\frac{1}{y_{1}}\partial_{y_{i}}\ (1<i\leq r),\quad\pi^{*}\partial_{x_{j}}=\partial_{y_{j}}\ (j>r).

2.4.
Weighted blow-ups

Stacks will enter the picture via weighted blow-ups, which we quickly review now. We refer to

Let

is a finitely generated graded sub-

of

such that

and

for every

\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action, and one can form the

\Bl_{I_{\bullet}}\Spec(A)=\left[\Spec_{A}(I_{\bullet})\setminus V(I_{+})/\mathbb{G}_{m}\right].

It is a tame algebraic stack. The natural morphism

is proper
[
QR22
, Proposition 1.6.1]
, but usually not projective (see
[
QR22
, Corollary 1.6.2]
).

An important instance of this construction is the following. For

and

let
(x,d)
be the graded ideal of

defined by

(x,d)=A\oplus(x)t\oplus\dots\oplus(x)t^{d}\oplus(x^{2})t^{d+1}\oplus\dots

or more succinctly by

. Given a collection
\mathbb{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
of elements of

and positive integers

be the Rees algebra defined by

Notice that if

for every

and so

is the usual blow-up of

along

.

Weighted blow-ups commute with flat extensions
[

, we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V^{\prime}/\mathcal{F}_{V^{\prime}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U/\mathcal{F}_{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V^{\prime(1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{\prime(1)}}

.

Proof.

Next we generalize

Proposition 3.3.1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{X}
is regular, and let
\mathcal{F}\subset T_{\mathcal{X}/k}
be a
1
-foliation. Then
\mathcal{F}
is regular if and only if
\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{F}
is regular.

Proof.

If

as above, then

and

are regular. Thus by
[

, Lemma 2.5.10]
, the quotients

and

are regular if and only if
\mathcal{F}_{U}
and
\mathcal{F}_{V}
are regular. Since the two morphisms

is regular if and only if

is regular. Therefore we obtain:

is regular if and only if

and

are regular, if and only if
\mathcal{F}_{U}
is regular, if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is regular.
∎

Lemma 3.3.2

Suppose that

Let

4.1.
Functorial resolution for two-dimensional foliated Deligne–Mumford stacks

In this sub-section, we extend the methods of
Theorem 4.0.1
and
Theorem 4.0.3
to obtain a functorial resolution theorem for
1
-foliations on two-dimensional Deligne–Mumford stacks. We let
k
denote an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
.

At first glance, it seems that one can obtain a resolution in three steps: first by resolving the underlying stack, then by blowing-up the non-multiplicative singularities of the
1
-foliation, and finally by performing a weighted blow-up along the multiplicative singularities. However, the last step is not completely functorial, as shown by the following example.

Example 4.1.1

(Non-uniqueness of weighted blow-ups)
.

Consider the
1
-foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
S=\Spec(k\llbracket x,y\rrbracket)
generated by
x\partial+\lambda y\partial_{y}
where
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Another generator of
\mathcal{F}
is given by
\lambda^{-1}x\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}
. So according to the computations of
Claim 4.0.4
, we can resolve
\mathcal{F}
by blowing-up either
\mathfrak{I}=(x,1)+(y,\Lambda)
or
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}=(x,\Lambda^{\prime})+(y,1)
where
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
respectively lift
\lambda
and
\lambda^{-1}
. But in general
\mathfrak{I}\neq\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}
, and consequently
\Bl_{\mathfrak{I}}S\not\cong\Bl_{\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}}S
.

For example, suppose that
p=5
and that
\lambda=3
. Then
\Lambda=3
and
\Lambda^{\prime}=2
. The element
x^{3-3}y^{\lceil 3/\Lambda\rceil}=y
belongs to
\mathfrak{I}_{3}
. However it does not belong to
\mathfrak{I}^{\prime}_{3}=(x^{3},x^{2}y,xy,y^{2})

.

In this example, the issue is that the two different generators of

induce different endomorphisms of the cotangent space

essentially shows that if two generators of

-foliations on two-dimensional Deligne–Mumford stacks.

A.1.5.
Stability

We record the following result:

Claim A.1.1
.

Let
\phi\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})
. Then the ideals
\mathcal{J}
,
\mathcal{H}
,

, the invariants

and

.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

∎

A.3.
The algorithm

We keep the notations of
Section A.1.1
.
Recall that we wish to find a modification
X(n)
with
\nu(X(n))=0
. The previous sub-sections show that, by working inductively, it suffices to solve the following problem: given a modification
X(i)
with
\dim\Sing_{\nu(X(i))}X(i)\leq 1
, find a sequence of permissible blow-ups
X(i+n)\to X(i)
such that
\nu(X(i+n))<\nu(X(i))
.

Note that if a permissible blow-up
X(i+1)\to X(i)
satisfies
\nu(X(i+1))=\nu(X(i))
, then we still have
\Sing_{\nu(X(i+1))}X(i+1)

[
Cos87b
, II.C.0.3]
.

Cossart designs an intricate inductive proof in order to show that this problem can be solved. The main additional invariant is the function
\kappa
, defined from every point of any modification
X(i)
and taking values in
\{0,1,\dots,7\}
(and being sometimes decorated by an additional letter). Following Cossart, we first define characterize the cases
\kappa\in\{0,1\}
before succinctly describing the algorithm of the proof.

A.3.1.
\kappa=0

For a closed point
x\in\Sing X(i)
, we set
\kappa(x)=0
if
[
Cos87b
, Definition II.C.1]
:
x
is isolated in
\Sing_{\nu(x)}X(i)
, and there is a sequence of permissible blow-ups

X(i+n)\to\dots\to X(i)

such that:

(a)

the set
Y(i+t)\subset X(i+t)
of points that are
\nu
-closed to
x
is finite,

(b)

X(i+t+1)\to X(i+t)
is the blow-up of
Y(i+t)
,

(c)

Y(i+n)
is empty.

These are, of course, the easiest singularities to resolve.
It is clear that:

Claim A.3.1
.

Let
\phi\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})
. Then the condition
\kappa=0
is left unchanged by the substitution
f\rightsquigarrow\phi^{p}f
.

A.3.2.
\kappa=1

For a given point
x\in\Sing X(i)
, let
\mathcal{C}(x)
be the set of closed irreducible curves
C
through
x
with
\alpha(C)\geq\nu(x)
and
(\nu(C),m(C),\alpha(C),\sigma(C))
maximal for the lexicographic order. Then the condition
\kappa(x)=1
indicates the success of an algorithm defined using
\nu
, permissibility and cardinality of
\mathcal{C}(\bullet)

[
Cos87b
, II.C.2]
.

This is the generic case: we have
\kappa=1
on a open dense open subset of the dimension one components of
\Sing_{\nu(X(i))}X(i)

[
Cos87b
, Proposition C.2.3]
.

Claim A.3.2
.

Let
\phi\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})
. Then the condition
\kappa=1
is left unchanged by the substitution
f\rightsquigarrow\phi^{p}f
.

Proof.

In view of what we have explained, this follows from
Claim A.1.1
and
Claim A.2.1
.
∎

A.3.3.
Good and bad points

Assume that we have defined the conditions
\kappa\in\{0,\dots,q\}
with
q\leq 7
. Let
x\in X(i)
with
\kappa(x)=q
. We say that
x
is a
good
point if the algorithm
[
Cos87b
, II.C.3,“Algorithme du point bon”]
is a success. This algorithm is defined in terms of permissibility, cardinality of
\mathcal{C}(\bullet)
,
\nu
and
\kappa\leq q
; its success indicates, roughly speaking, that we have a sequence of blow-ups of curves such that eventually no point is
(\nu,\kappa)
-close to
x
. If this algorithm fails, then
x
is a
bad
point.
Obviously,

Claim A.3.3
.

Let
\phi\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})
. Then the condition “
x
is a good point” is left unchanged by the substitution
f\rightsquigarrow\phi^{p}f
, assuming the conditions
\kappa\in\{0,\dots,\kappa(x)\}
are.

Cossart’s strategy is to prove that bad points become good upon several rounds of blow-ups, and that further blow-ups make
(\nu,\kappa)
drop (for the lexicographic order). In view of the definition of
\kappa=0
, this leads to the desired drop of
\nu
.

A.3.4.
The blow-up procedure

The sequence of permissible blow-ups used to make
\nu(X(i))
drop is specified in
[
Cos87b
, Algorithme II.C.5.3]
. The blow-ups centers are selected using the notions of dimension, permissibility, good and bad points,
\nu
,
m
,
\alpha
,
\sigma
,
\kappa
and
\mathcal{C}
.

After reducing to
\dim\Sing_{\nu(X(i))}X(i)\leq 1
, the algorithm blow-ups points and curves. Let us note that we blow-up curves not contained in
\Sing_{\nu(X(i))}X(i)
only once we have achieved
\dim\Sing_{\nu(X(i))}X(i)=0
. We need to ensure that if we blow-up a curve

to improve the situation at a point

, then this will not mess up the situation at another point of

: this is shown in
[
Cos87b
, Proposition I.C.5.1]
(using only the tools introduced above).

We claim now that the conditions
\kappa\in\{2,\dots,7\}
are invariant under

for

. We illustrate this in several important cases (including

and the “joyful case”), and leave the remaining ones to the reader.

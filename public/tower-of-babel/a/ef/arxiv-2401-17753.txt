Twisted tensor products of field algebras
Ezio Vasselli

Abstract
Let 𝒜 \mathcal{A} be a C*-algebra, 𝒉 {\boldsymbol{h}} a Hilbert space and 𝒞 𝒉 \mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{h}} the CAR algebra over 𝒉 {\boldsymbol{h}} . We construct a twisted tensor product of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} by 𝒞 𝒉 \mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{h}} such that the two factors are not necessarily one in the relative commutant of the other. The resulting C*-algebra may be regarded as a generalized CAR algebra constructed over a suitable Hilbert 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -bimodule. As an application, we exhibit a class of fixed-time models where a free Dirac field (giving rise to the 𝒞 𝒉 \mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{h}} factor) in general is not relatively local to a free scalar field (which yields the 𝒜 \mathcal{A} factor). In some of the models, gauge-invariant combinations of the two (not relatively local) fields form a local observable net. Mathematics Subject Classification. 46L06, 46L08, 81T05 Keywords. C*-algebras, Hilbert modules, Fock spaces, quantum field theory

Introduction

In quantum field theory, a common way to construct a field system is to perform
the tensor product of Fock spaces and define for each factor the corresponding free field.
This method is used in both exactly solvable models and perturbative theory,
as a preliminary step to perform Wick or time-ordered products
and get the corresponding Wightman or Green functions.
At the algebraic level the typical construction is given by the spatial tensor product

\mathcal{F}\doteq\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{h}}\otimes\mathcal{A}
,
where
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{h}}
is the CAR algebra over a Hilbert space
{\boldsymbol{h}}
(generalized free Fermi field)
and
\mathcal{A}
is a CCR algebra (generalized free Bosonic field).

The starting point of the present paper is the remark that we may regard
\mathcal{F}

as the C*-algebra generated by a Fermi field
\psi
intended in a broader sense,
defined in terms of creation and annihilation operators living in a
fermionic Fock bimodule
\EuFrak{F}_{-}(\EuFrak h)
.
Here,
\EuFrak h\doteq{\boldsymbol{h}}\otimes\mathcal{A}
is the free Hilbert bimodule
carrying the
trivial left action
uniquely determined by the relations

wA=Aw
,
w\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
.
Adopting this point of view,
\mathcal{A}
appears as part of the target space for
the ”non-commutative anticommutator function” of
\psi
.

This suggests a strategy to escape from tensor products and possibly produce
interesting models. The idea is that of considering
Hilbert bimodules carrying a non-trivial left action,
with the aim of constructing field systems where the Bosonic components do not
necessarily commute with the fermionic ones.
This is what happens, for example, in QED,
where (in positive gauges) charged fields cannot be relatively local to
the electromagnetic field
[
14
]
.

It is aim of the present paper to provide an algebraic machinery to
construct field C*-algebras having the above mentioned property.
We start by reviewing generic Hilbert bimodules, their Fock bimodules
and the corresponding GNS Hilbert spaces obtained by applying states of
\mathcal{A}
§
2
.
On these spaces there are well-defined annihilation and creation operators,
and the ”zero particle space” is the GNS space of
\mathcal{A}

instead of
{\mathbb{C}}
as in usual Fock space.
Annihilation operators –while they perform the usual operation
of annihilating states defined by
\EuFrak h
– modify (without annihilating)
states in the GNS space of
\mathcal{A}
.
Thus in the present paper the term
annihilation operator
should be intended
in this broader sense.

To define fermionic spaces, one should introduce a permutation symmetry on Fock bimodules.
Since in general this is an impossible task,
we focus on free Hilbert bimodules
for which it is possible to define a permutation symmetry in the obvious way §
3
.
This does not necessarily lead to the trivial construction
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{h}}\otimes\mathcal{A}
,
because the crucial property to avoid it is non-triviality of the left
\mathcal{A}
-action.
Having defined our fermionic Fock space, we face the fact that antisymmetry

f\otimes_{-}g\ =\ -g\otimes_{-}f\ \ \ ,\ \ \ f,g\in\EuFrak h\,,

(1.1)

does not hold in full generality. The reason relies on commutation properties of
\mathcal{A}
:
namely, there are suitable support C*-subalgebras
\mathcal{A}(f),\mathcal{A}(g)
of
\mathcal{A}
,
and a sufficient condition for (
1.1
) holding true is that

[\mathcal{A}(f)\,,\,\mathcal{A}(g)]\ =\ 0

(1.2)

together with the fact that the left
\mathcal{A}(g)
-action on
f
is trivial
and analogously for
\mathcal{A}(f)
on
g
, Remark
3.3
.
In this case we say that
f
and
g
are
mutually free
.

An other consequence of the fact that we are dealing with Hilbert modules
rather than Hilbert spaces is given by non-stability of the fermionic Fock module
both under the action of creation operators and the left
\mathcal{A}
-action.
Both these problems are solved by considering a particular class of left
\mathcal{A}
-actions
that we call
\mathcal{G}
-twists
, where
\mathcal{G}
is a group of unitary generators of
\mathcal{A}

(Lemma
3.4
).

\mathcal{G}
-twists are given by group morphisms
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{U}({\boldsymbol{h}})

that are used to perturb the trivial left
\mathcal{A}
-action, Def.
3.2
.
We exhibit two classes of C*-algebras that naturally admit
\mathcal{G}
-twists:
the first is given by Weyl algebras (giving rise to generalized free Bosonic fields),
Example
3.1
;
the second, Example
3.2
, is the universal C*-algebra of the electromagnetic field

[
8
]
.

Finally in §
4
we construct our generalized CAR algebra having as input the
free module
\EuFrak h
and the twist
u
, Theorem
4.3
.
Adding as a third ingredient a suitable conjugation
\kappa
acting on
{\boldsymbol{h}}
we obtain what
we call a
Dirac triple over
\mathcal{A}
, from which we construct a Dirac field (
4.16
)
and the corresponding field algebra (
4.18
).
It is this field algebra that defines the desired twisted tensor product of
\mathcal{A}
by
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{h}}
.

As an application we exhibit a family of models in a fixed-time formulation,
depending on a tempered distribution
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
that defines the twist. In our models,
a free Dirac field (generating the C*-algebra
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{h}}
) and a free scalar field
(generating a Weyl C*-algebra
\mathcal{W}
) give rise to the following situations:
(1)
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{h}}
and
\mathcal{W}
do not commute, but are relatively local (when
\sigma
is the Dirac delta);
(2)
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{h}}
and
\mathcal{W}
are not relatively local (for
\sigma
having support with non-empty interior);
(3)
\mathcal{W}
is not relatively local to
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{h}}
, but is in the commutant of the fixed-point
algebra of
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{h}}
under the gauge action (when
\sigma
is the Lebesgue measure).

A discussion of our results, work in progress and perspectives are given in the final section §
5
.

To use the symbol

.

Hilbert modules.

A
(right Hilbert)
\mathcal{A}
-module
is a complex vector space
\EuFrak h

carrying a right
\mathcal{A}
-module action and endowed with a
\mathcal{A}
-valued, strictly positive and
right
\mathcal{A}
-linear scalar product
{\left\langle\right.}\cdot,\cdot{\left.\right\rangle}
,
which induces the norm
\|{\left\langle\right.}\cdot,\cdot{\left.\right\rangle}\|
under which
\EuFrak h
is a Banach space.
It is worth noting that then

A{\left\langle\right.}f,g{\left.\right\rangle}\,=\,({\left\langle\right.}g,f{\left.\right\rangle}A^{*})^{*}\,=\,({\left\langle\right.}g,fA^{*}{\left.\right\rangle})^{*}\,=\,{\left\langle\right.}fA^{*},g{\left.\right\rangle}\,,

(2.1)

for all
f,g\in\EuFrak h
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
.
An
\mathcal{A}
-module
\EuFrak h
defines the C*-algebra
\EuFrak{B}(\EuFrak h)
of linear operators

T:\EuFrak h\to\EuFrak h
such that there is
T^{*}\in\EuFrak{B}(\EuFrak h)
with

{\left\langle\right.}T^{*}f,g{\left.\right\rangle}={\left\langle\right.}f,Tg{\left.\right\rangle}
,
f,g\in\EuFrak h
.
This implies that
T\in\EuFrak{B}(\EuFrak h)
is right
\mathcal{A}
-linear,
i.e.

T(fA)=(Tf)A
, and bounded.
The C*-algebra of compact operators is given by the closed ideal
\EuFrak{K}(\EuFrak h)\subseteq\EuFrak{B}(\EuFrak h)

generated by the elementary operators

|f{\left.\right\rangle}{\left\langle\right.}g|h\doteq f{\left\langle\right.}g,h{\left.\right\rangle}
,
h\in\EuFrak h
.
We say that
\EuFrak h
is a
Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule
whenever there is a *-morphism

\lambda:\mathcal{A}\to\EuFrak{B}(\EuFrak h)
,
called the left action (Hilbert bimodules are also known as
C*-correspondences

[
2
]
).
In the present paper we shall assume that
\lambda
is faithful and non-degenerate,
and following a standard notation we will write

Af\,\equiv\,\lambda(A)f\ \ \ ,\ \ \ \forall f\in\EuFrak h\,,\,A\in\mathcal{A}\,.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})
be a state. Then
{\left\langle\right.}f,g{\left.\right\rangle}_{\omega}\doteq\omega({\left\langle\right.}f,g{\left.\right\rangle})

is a scalar product and defines the Hilbert space
\EuFrak h^{\omega}
whose elements

f^{\omega}\in\EuFrak h^{\omega}
are defined in correspondence with
f\in\EuFrak h
.
We have the representation

\pi^{\omega}:\EuFrak{B}(\EuFrak h)\to\mathcal{B}(\EuFrak h^{\omega})\ \ \ ,\ \ \ T^{\omega}f^{\omega}\,\doteq\,(Tf)^{\omega}\,.

(2.2)

The argument to prove that (
2.2
) is well-defined is standard,
so we give just a sketch.
Let
T\in\EuFrak{B}(\EuFrak h)
and
v\in\EuFrak h
such that
v^{\omega}=0
,
i.e.

{\left\langle\right.}v,v{\left.\right\rangle}_{\omega}=0
;
then
\|T\|^{2}-T^{*}T
is a positive element of
\EuFrak{B}(\EuFrak h)
and this
implies that
{\left\langle\right.}v,(\|T\|^{2}-T^{*}T)v{\left.\right\rangle}\in\mathcal{A}
is positive for any
v\in\EuFrak h
.
As a consequence

0\ =\ \|T\|^{2}{\left\langle\right.}v,v{\left.\right\rangle}_{\omega}\ \geq\ {\left\langle\right.}v,T^{*}Tv{\left.\right\rangle}_{\omega}\ =\ {\left\langle\right.}Tv,Tv{\left.\right\rangle}_{\omega}\ \geq\ 0\,,

implying
(Tv)^{\omega}=0
,
and
T^{\omega}
is a well-defined linear operator on
\mathcal{B}(\EuFrak h^{\omega})
with norm
\leq\|T\|
.
Note that (
2.2
) restricts to the representation of the left
\mathcal{A}
-action

\pi^{\omega}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}(\EuFrak h^{\omega})\ \ \ ,\ \ \ A^{\omega}f^{\omega}\,\doteq\,(Af)^{\omega}\,.

(2.3)

We call (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) the GNS-representations induced by
\omega
.

Example 2.1
.

We set
\EuFrak h\doteq\mathcal{A}
with right (left)

\mathcal{A}
-module action given by right (left) multiplication and scalar product

{\left\langle\right.}f,g{\left.\right\rangle}\doteq f^{*}g
,
f,g\in\EuFrak h
.
If
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})
, then it is readily seen that (
2.3
) is the
GNS representation in the usual sense. For future use, we write
v_{A}^{\omega}

to indicate the GNS vector corresponding to
A\in\mathcal{A}
, so that

\|v_{A}^{\omega}\|^{2}=\omega(A^{*}A)
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\EuFrak h\doteq{\boldsymbol{h}}\mathcal{A}
denote the free right Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module,
with right action

vB\doteq w\otimes AB

and scalar product

{\left\langle\right.}v,v^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}\doteq A^{*}A^{\prime}{\left\langle\right.}w,w^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}
,
where
v\doteq w\otimes A
,
v^{\prime}\doteq w^{\prime}\otimes A^{\prime}
,
w,w^{\prime}\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
,
A,A^{\prime},B\in\mathcal{A}
.
If

\lambda:\mathcal{A}\to\EuFrak{B}(\EuFrak h)\simeq\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{h}})\otimes\mathcal{A}

is a *-morphism, then we have the left
\mathcal{A}
-action
Af\doteq\lambda(A)f
.
Given
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})
, the Hilbert space
\EuFrak h^{\omega}

is isomorphic to
{\boldsymbol{h}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{h}}^{\omega}
, where
{\boldsymbol{h}}^{\omega}

is the GNS space of
\omega
.

The Fock bimodule.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\EuFrak h
denote a Hilbert bimodule.
We consider the tensor product of complex vector spaces
\EuFrak h^{\odot n}
,
that we endow with the scalar product

{\left\langle\right.}v,w{\left.\right\rangle}\,\doteq\,{\left\langle\right.}v_{n}\,,\,{\left\langle\right.}v_{n-1}\,,\,{\left\langle\right.}\ldots{\left\langle\right.}v_{1},w_{1}{\left.\right\rangle}\ldots{\left.\right\rangle}w_{n-1}{\left.\right\rangle}\,w_{n}{\left.\right\rangle}\,,

(2.4)

where
v\doteq v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{n}
and
w
are in
\EuFrak h^{\odot n}
.
Note that the term
{\left\langle\right.}v_{n-1},\ldots w_{n-1}{\left.\right\rangle}
appearing in the previous expression
belongs to
\mathcal{A}
, so it makes sense to consider its left action on
w_{n}
,
and analogously for the nested terms
{\left\langle\right.}v_{n-k},\ldots w_{n-k}{\left.\right\rangle}
,
k=2,\ldots,n-1
.
The completion obtained by (
2.4
) is denoted by
\EuFrak h^{n}
and is called
the
internal
n
-fold tensor product
: it is endowed with the
\mathcal{A}
-module actions

Av\doteq Av_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{n}\ \ \ ,\ \ \ vA\doteq v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{n}A\,,

and therefore it forms a Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule. By construction, it turns out

\ldots\otimes v_{k}\otimes Av_{k+1}\otimes\ldots\,=\,\ldots\otimes v_{k}A\otimes v_{k+1}\otimes\ldots\ \ \ ,\ \ \ \forall A\in\mathcal{A}\,.

(2.5)

Remark 2.1
.

A delicate point of the tensor product of Hilbert bimodules is that
an elementary tensor
v\in\EuFrak h^{\odot n}
may have norm zero even when the all the factors

v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
do not. For example, if
\mathcal{A}=C(X)
is commutative,

\EuFrak h
is the module of sections of a vector bundle
E\to X
, and
Af=fA

is defined by pointwise multiplication for all
A\in C(X)
and
f\in\EuFrak h
,
then
{\left\langle\right.}v,v{\left.\right\rangle}={\left\langle\right.}v_{1},v_{1}{\left.\right\rangle}{\left\langle\right.}v_{2},v_{2}{\left.\right\rangle}=0

for
v\doteq v_{1}\otimes v_{2}
and
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(v_{1})\cap{\mathrm{supp}}\,(v_{2})=\emptyset
.

The direct sum of Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-bimodules
\EuFrak h,\EuFrak h^{\prime}
is defined in the obvious way,
and yields the
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule
\EuFrak h\oplus\EuFrak h^{\prime}
.
Thus we define the
Fock
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule

\EuFrak{F}(\EuFrak h)\,\doteq\,\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\EuFrak h^{n}\,,

(2.6)

with
\EuFrak h^{0}\doteq\mathcal{A}
and
\EuFrak h^{1}\doteq\EuFrak h
; any
v\in\EuFrak{F}(\EuFrak h)
is a sequence

v=\{v^{n}\in\EuFrak h^{n}\}
.
Let now
f\in\EuFrak h
,
n\geq 1
, and
v\doteq v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{n}\in\EuFrak h^{n}
. We set

\left\langle f\right|v\,\doteq\,{\left\langle\right.}f,v_{1}{\left.\right\rangle}v_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{n}\,,

(2.7)

obtaining a right
\mathcal{A}
-linear operator

\left\langle f\right|:\EuFrak h^{n}\to\EuFrak h^{n-1}
.
We define the annihilation operator

(a(f)v)^{n-1}\,\doteq\,\sqrt{n}\,\,\left\langle f\right|v^{n}\ \ \ ,\ \ \ n\geq 1\,,

(2.8)

with domain

\,{\mathrm{Dom}}\,(a(f))\doteq\EuFrak{F}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)\doteq\left\{v\in\EuFrak{F}(\EuFrak h)\,:\,\exists\sum_{n}n{\left\langle\right.}v^{n},v^{n}{\left.\right\rangle}\in\mathcal{A}\right\}
.
Next we define the creation operator

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(a^{*}(f)v)^{n+1}\,\doteq\,\sqrt{n+1}\,\,f\otimes v^{n}\ \ \ ,\ \ n\geq 1\,,\\
(a^{*}(f)v)^{1}\doteq fv^{0}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \,,\ \ n=0\,,\end{array}\right.

(2.9)

for
v\in\,{\mathrm{Dom}}\,(a^{*}(f))\doteq\EuFrak{F}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)
. Note that
v^{0}\in\mathcal{A}
, so the expression

fv^{0}\in\EuFrak h
makes sense. Moreover, if
v^{n}=0
for all
n\geq 1
then

a(f)v=0
, so that
\mathcal{A}=\EuFrak h^{0}
is in the kernel of
a(f)
. We have

a(f)a^{*}(g)\,=\,(n+1){\left\langle\right.}f,g{\left.\right\rangle}\ \ \ ,\ \ \ a^{*}(g)a(f)\,=\,ng\otimes\left\langle f\right|\ \ \ ,\ \ \ f,g\in\EuFrak h\,.

(2.10)

The above notation suggests that
a^{*}(f)
is the adjoint of
a(f)
and,
actually, for elementary tensors
v\in\EuFrak h^{n}
,
w\in\EuFrak h^{n-1}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}{\left\langle\right.}v,a^{*}(f)w{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\ \sqrt{n}\,{\left\langle\right.}v,f\otimes w{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\\
\sqrt{n}\,{\left\langle\right.}v_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{n}\,,\,{\left\langle\right.}v_{1},f{\left.\right\rangle}w{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\ \sqrt{n}\,{\left\langle\right.}{\left\langle\right.}f,v_{1}{\left.\right\rangle}v_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{n}\,,\,w{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\\
{\left\langle\right.}a(f)v\,,\,w{\left.\right\rangle}\,.\end{array}

(2.11)

Thus
a^{*}(f)
behaves as the adjoint of
a(f)
on the common domain
\EuFrak{F}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)
.
Note that
a^{*}(f)
and
a(f)
are unbounded, thus we should be careful
when we use the term
adjoint
.
Yet, since we are interested in the fermionic case
where it will be shown that the creation and annihilation operators are bounded,
we prefer to not discuss this point here.

Hilbert spaces in Hilbert modules.

A
Hilbert space in
\EuFrak h
is given by a closed vector subspace
{\boldsymbol{h}}\subset\EuFrak h

such that

{\left\langle\right.}w,w^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}\in{\mathbb{C}}{\bf 1}
,
\forall w,w^{\prime}\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
.
The proof of the following result is trivial, therefore it is omitted.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{h}}\subset\EuFrak h
be a Hilbert space.
Then there is an injective linear mapping
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}})\to\EuFrak{F}(\EuFrak h)

preserving the scalar product,
where
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}})
is the Fock space in the usual sense.

GNS-Hilbert spaces of the Fock bimodule.

Let now
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})
and
\EuFrak h^{n,\omega}
denote the Hilbert space
obtained by completion of
\EuFrak h^{n}
under the scalar product
{\left\langle\right.}\cdot,\cdot{\left.\right\rangle}_{\omega}
.
Moreover, let
\EuFrak{F}^{\omega}(\EuFrak h)
denote the Hilbert space obtained by the analogous completion of
\EuFrak{F}(\EuFrak h)
.
Basic properties of this Hilbert space are resumed in the following result.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\EuFrak h
be a Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule and
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})
.
With the above notation, there is a decomposition

\EuFrak{F}^{\omega}(\EuFrak h)\,\simeq\,\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\EuFrak h^{n,\omega}\,.

(2.12)

The component
\EuFrak h^{0,\omega}
is the usual GNS Hilbert space of
\omega
,
and there is a representation

\hat{\pi}^{\omega}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}(\EuFrak{F}^{\omega}(\EuFrak h))\ \ ,\ \ \hat{\pi}^{\omega}\,=\,\oplus_{n}\pi^{n,\omega}\,,

(2.13)

where each
\pi^{n,\omega}
is defined as in (
2.3
) and

\pi^{0,\omega}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}(\EuFrak h^{0,\omega})
is the usual GNS representation.
Given a Hilbert space
{\boldsymbol{h}}\subset\EuFrak h
,
the closed vector subspace of
\EuFrak{F}^{\omega}(\EuFrak h)
spanned
by elements of the type
(wA)^{\omega}
,
w\in{\boldsymbol{h}}^{n}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
is a Hilbert space isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}})\otimes\EuFrak h^{0,\omega}
,
where
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}})
is the Fock space in the usual sense,
so that there is an embedding

\iota:\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}})\otimes\EuFrak h^{0,\omega}\to\EuFrak{F}^{\omega}(\EuFrak h)\ \ ,\ \ \iota(w\otimes v_{A}^{\omega})\,\doteq\,(wA)^{\omega}\,.

(2.14)

Proof.

The direct sum decomposition (
2.12
) is obvious.
About
\EuFrak h^{0,\omega}
, by definition it is given by the completion of
\EuFrak h^{0}=\mathcal{A}

under the scalar product
\omega(A^{*}A^{\prime})
,
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
, so it is the GNS-space of
\omega
.
The decomposition (
2.13
) trivially follows by the previous points,
as well as the fact that

is the GNS representation of
\omega
.
Finally, the embedding (
2.14
) follows by the fact that,
given elementary tensors

,

,

{\left\langle\right.}wA,w^{\prime}A^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\ A^{*}\,\prod_{k}{\left\langle\right.}w_{k},w^{\prime}_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}\,A^{\prime}\,,

having used the fact that

{\left\langle\right.}w_{n},{\left\langle\right.}w_{n-1},{\left\langle\right.}\ldots{\left\langle\right.}w_{1},w^{\prime}_{1}{\left.\right\rangle}\ldots{\left.\right\rangle}w^{\prime}_{n-1}{\left.\right\rangle}w^{\prime}_{n}{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\ \prod_{k}{\left\langle\right.}w_{k},w^{\prime}_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}

(recall that
{\left\langle\right.}w_{k},w^{\prime}_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}\in{\mathbb{C}}

∎

Remark 2.4

(Non-Fock nature of

Remark 2.5
.

Let

Creation and annihilation operators in
\EuFrak{F}^{\omega}(\EuFrak h)
.

Let

(2.17)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(a^{*,\omega}(f)v^{\omega})^{n+1}\,\doteq\,\sqrt{n+1}\,\,(f\otimes v^{n})^{\omega}\ \ \ ,\ \ n\geq 1\,,\\

(a^{*,\omega}(f)v)^{1}\,\doteq\,(fv^{0})^{\omega}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \,\,,\ \ n=0\,,\end{array}\right.

where

3
Twisted module actions and permutation symmetry

In the present section we study the permutation symmetry on tensor powers of Hilbert bimodules
or, to be honest, the factors that in general prevent the realization of this property.
These factors are easily identified and are
non-commutativity of
\mathcal{A}
and the fact that in general

Af\neq fA
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
f\in\EuFrak h
.
Anyway even with the above limitations we shall give a version of the fermionic Fock space
for free Hilbert bimodules,
and define the corresponding restrictions of the creation and annihilation operators.
The tool used to perform these constructions is the one of
twist
,
namely a ”perturbation” of the trivial left
\mathcal{A}
-module action
defined using a group of unitary generators of
\mathcal{A}
.
We remark that thanks to the Kasparov stabilization theorem
[
5
]

the choice of considering only free bimodules is not a severe requirement.

Let us now consider an elementary tensor
v=v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{n}\in\EuFrak h^{n}
.
Given a permutation
\varrho\in{\mathbb{P}}(n)
, we would like to define the operator

U_{\varrho}v\ \doteq\ v_{\varrho(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{\varrho(n)}\,.

(3.1)

It is obvious that the above operator is well-defined on the algebraic elementary
tensors
v_{1}\odot\ldots\odot v_{n}
, thus the question is what happens when one
applies the norm induced by
\mathcal{A}
.
Here we immediately encounter a problem, illustrated by the following example.
Consider a Hilbert bimodule
\EuFrak h
of the type Example
2.1
with
\EuFrak h=\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{O}_{2}
,
the Cuntz algebra generated by mutually orthogonal isometries
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
;
we take
v_{1}\doteq\psi_{1}
,
v_{2}\doteq\psi_{2}^{*}
and evaluate the scalar products

{\left\langle\right.}v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\,,\,v_{1}\otimes v_{2}{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\ {\left\langle\right.}v_{2},{\left\langle\right.}v_{1},v_{1}{\left.\right\rangle}v_{2}{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\ \psi_{2}\psi_{1}^{*}\psi_{1}\psi_{2}^{*}\ =\ \psi_{2}\psi_{2}^{*}\,,

{\left\langle\right.}v_{2}\otimes v_{1}\,,\,v_{2}\otimes v_{1}{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\ {\left\langle\right.}v_{1},{\left\langle\right.}v_{2},v_{2}{\left.\right\rangle}v_{1}{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\ \psi_{1}^{*}\psi_{2}\psi_{2}^{*}\psi_{1}\ =\ 0\,.

This example shows that the above operators
U_{\varrho}
in general are ill-defined,
because they map zero norm algebraic elementary tensors into tensors with norm
\neq 0
.
This also spoils the preservation of compositions of permutations in (
3.1
).
For example, given the flip
\varrho(1)=2
,
\varrho(2)=1
, in the situation
of the previous example we find

\|U_{\varrho}(v_{2}\otimes v_{1})\|=\|v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\|\neq 0\ \ \ ,\ \ \ U_{\varrho}U_{\varrho}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2})=0\ \ \ ,\ \ \ U_{\varrho\varrho}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2})=v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\,.

Free modules.

In the language adopted in the present paper, a Hilbert bimodule
\EuFrak h

is free if, and only if, there is a Hilbert space
{\boldsymbol{h}}\subset\EuFrak h

such that

\EuFrak h\ =\ {\boldsymbol{h}}\mathcal{A}\ \doteq\ {\mathrm{closed\ span}}\{wA\,:\,w\in{\boldsymbol{h}}\,,\,A\in\mathcal{A}\}\,.

(3.2)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\EuFrak h
be a free Hilbert bimodule. Then there is an isomorphism of right
Hilbert modules
\EuFrak{F}(\EuFrak h)\simeq\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}})\mathcal{A}
and, given a state

\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})
, there is an isomorphism of Hilbert spaces

\EuFrak{F}^{\omega}(\EuFrak h)\simeq\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}})\otimes\EuFrak h^{0,\omega}

where
\EuFrak h^{0,\omega}
is the usual GNS Hilbert space.
The left
\mathcal{A}
-module action
\lambda
defines by evaluation the representation

\lambda^{\omega}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}})\otimes\EuFrak h^{0,\omega})\,.

(3.3)

Proof.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

v=v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{n}\in\EuFrak h^{n}
;
then by (
3.2
) we may write
v_{i}=w_{k}A_{k;i}
,
where
\{w_{k}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
{\boldsymbol{h}}
,
A_{k;i}\doteq{\left\langle\right.}w_{k},v_{i}{\left.\right\rangle}\in\mathcal{A}
,
and the (possibly infinite) sum on the repeated index
k
is performed.
Using the above decomposition, it is easily seen that
v\in{\boldsymbol{h}}^{n}\mathcal{A}

and we conclude that
\EuFrak h^{n}\subseteq{\boldsymbol{h}}^{n}\mathcal{A}
.
Since the opposite inclusion is obvious, we have
\EuFrak h^{n}={\boldsymbol{h}}^{n}\mathcal{A}
,
and this proves that
\EuFrak h^{n}\simeq{\boldsymbol{h}}^{n}\mathcal{A}
implying
\EuFrak{F}(\EuFrak h)\simeq\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}})\mathcal{A}
.
Thus
\EuFrak{F}^{\omega}(\EuFrak h)\simeq\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}})\otimes\EuFrak h^{0,\omega}
by Proposition
2.3
.
∎

We shall see in the following sections that
in general
\lambda^{\omega}(\mathcal{A})
is not in the commutant of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}}))

in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}})\otimes\EuFrak h^{0,\omega})
;
moreover, operators of the type
\lambda^{\omega}(A)
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
mix vectors of
\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}})
and
\EuFrak h^{0,\omega}

in the sense that, given the GNS-vector
\Omega\doteq v_{\bf 1}^{\omega}\in\EuFrak h^{0,\omega}
,
typically we will find

\lambda^{\omega}(A)(v\otimes\Omega)=\sum_{k}v_{k}\otimes v^{0}_{k}

with
v_{k}\neq v
and
v^{0}_{k}\neq\Omega
.
In contrast, let us define the left
\mathcal{A}
-module action

A(wB)\ \doteq\ wAB\ \ \ ,\ \ \ w\in{\boldsymbol{h}}\,,\,A,B\in\mathcal{A}

(3.4)

(in standard notation,
A(w\otimes B)\doteq w\otimes AB
).
We call (
3.4
) the
trivial left action
.
At the level of Fock space, it does not induce a mixing because

\lambda(A)=1\otimes A
,
and clearly
\lambda^{\omega}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}({\boldsymbol{h}}))
commute for any

\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})
.

Twists and left actions.

The following notion concerns a class of left actions
well-behaved with respect to the permutation symmetry,
obtained by twisting the trivial one by means of a group action.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\mathcal{A}
be a group generating
\mathcal{A}
as C*-algebra
and
\EuFrak h={\boldsymbol{h}}\mathcal{A}
a free bimodule.
Then the left
\mathcal{A}
-action
\lambda
is said to be
\mathcal{G}
-
twisted
whenever
there is a group morphism
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{U}({\boldsymbol{h}})
,
that we call the
\mathcal{G}
-
twist
, such that

\lambda(\gamma)w\equiv\gamma w\,=\,(u_{\gamma}w)\gamma\ \ ,\ \ \forall\gamma\in\mathcal{G}\,,\,w\in{\boldsymbol{h}}\,.

(3.5)

We say that the
\mathcal{G}
-twist is
trivial
whenever
u
is the
trivial representation.

For reader’s convenience we check the consistence of the previous definition by verifying
that the r.h.s. of (
3.5
) defines an adjointable
(and as a consequence
\mathcal{A}
-linear and bounded) operator.
We write

wA,w^{\prime}A^{\prime}\in\EuFrak h
for
w,w^{\prime}\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
,
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
,
and compute

{\left\langle\right.}wA,\gamma w^{\prime}A^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\ A^{*}{\left\langle\right.}w,u_{\gamma}w^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}\gamma A^{\prime}\ =\ (\gamma^{*}A)^{*}{\left\langle\right.}u_{\gamma}^{*}w,w^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}A^{\prime}\ =\ {\left\langle\right.}\gamma^{*}wA,w^{\prime}A^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}

(note that
{\left\langle\right.}w,u_{\gamma}w^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}\in{\mathbb{C}}
);
thus (
3.5
) has adjoint
\gamma^{*}
as expected.
We remark that since
\mathcal{G}
generates
\mathcal{A}
the left action
\lambda
is
determined by
u
. Despite that,
u
may have a kernel even when
\lambda
does not
(as shall be evident in the following example, where
\mathcal{A}
can be simple).
Finally we note that in the previous definition we
did not assume strong continuity of
u
, so in general it is not
a unitary representation; therefore we say that
u
is a

unitary morphism
.

Example 3.1

(Weyl algebras)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a real vector space with a symplectic form
\eta

Let

.

,

Since

\begin{array}[]{lcl}W_{s}^{\lambda}W_{s^{\prime}}^{\lambda}wB&=&(u(W_{s})u(W_{s^{\prime}})w)\,W_{s}W_{s^{\prime}}B\ =\\

Permutation symmetry.

Lemma

allows to define a permutation symmetry in the obvious way,
by extending the one defined on

U_{\varrho}(wA)\,\doteq\,w_{\varrho}A\ \ \ ,\ \ \ \varrho\in{\mathbb{P}}(n)\,,\,w\in{\boldsymbol{h}}^{n}\,,\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\,,

(3.9)

where
w_{\varrho}\in{\boldsymbol{h}}^{n}
is the vector transformed under the usual permutation symmetry in Fock space.
Of course, to apply the previous definition we must express a tensor of the type

v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{n}
,
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\EuFrak h
,
in terms of tensors of the type

(w_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes w_{n})A
,
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
.
By definition
U_{\varrho}
is unitary on
\EuFrak{F}(\EuFrak h)
in the sense of right Hilbert modules,
thus we get the spectral projection
P_{-}\doteq\oplus_{n}P_{-}^{n}\in\EuFrak{B}(\EuFrak{F}(\EuFrak h))
,

P_{-}^{n}\doteq(n!)^{-1}\sum_{\varrho}\epsilon_{\varrho}U_{\varrho}
,
where
\epsilon_{\varrho}
is the sign of
\varrho\in{\mathbb{P}}(n)

We write
\EuFrak{F}_{-}(\EuFrak h)\doteq P_{-}\EuFrak{F}(\EuFrak h)

\EuFrak{F}_{-}(\EuFrak h)
is a

-module, and that it is free with

\EuFrak{F}_{-}(\EuFrak h)\simeq\mathcal{F}_{-}({\boldsymbol{h}})\otimes\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{F}_{-}({\boldsymbol{h}})
is the ordinary fermionic Fock space.
We have the decomposition

\EuFrak{F}_{-}(\EuFrak h)\ \doteq\ \bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\EuFrak h^{n}_{-}\ \simeq\ \bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\,({\boldsymbol{h}}^{n}_{-}\mathcal{A})\,,

(3.10)

where
\EuFrak h^{n}_{-}\doteq P_{-}^{n}\EuFrak h^{n}

.

and
\EuFrak h^{1}_{-}=\EuFrak h\simeq{\boldsymbol{h}}\mathcal{A}
.

Remark 3.3

(The Pauli principle)
.

In spite of the simplicity of our definition
some care is needed to handle antisymmetric tensors.
For example, if
f,g\in\EuFrak h
then it is not ensured that

P_{-}(f\otimes g)=-P_{-}(g\otimes f)
,
unless
f
and
g
belong to
{\boldsymbol{h}}
.
In particular, the Pauli principle

P_{-}(f\otimes f)\ =\ 0\ \ \ ,\ \ \ f\in\EuFrak h\,,

(3.11)

does not hold in general, and
a priori
its validity is ensured
only for
f\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
.
A more general class of examples for which the Pauli principle holds is the following.
Given
f\in\EuFrak h
, we define the
support

\mathcal{A}(f)\,\doteq\,C^{*}\{{\left\langle\right.}v,f{\left.\right\rangle}\in\mathcal{A}\,:\,v\in{\boldsymbol{h}}\}\,\subseteq\mathcal{A}\,.

(3.12)

The support has the property that if
f=f_{h}A_{h}
, where
\{f_{h}\}\subset{\boldsymbol{h}}

is an orthonormal set and
A_{h}\in\mathcal{A}
, then
A_{h}={\left\langle\right.}f_{h},f{\left.\right\rangle}\in\mathcal{A}(f)

for all
h\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Given
f,g\in\EuFrak h
, we write

f\bowtie g\ \ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\Leftrightarrow}}\ \ fB^{\prime}=B^{\prime}f\ \ ,\ \ gB=Bg\ \ ,\ \ \left[B,B^{\prime}\right]\ =\ 0\,,

(3.13)

for all
B\in\mathcal{A}(f)\,,\,B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(g)
.
In this case we say that
f
and
g
are
mutually free
, and we find

\begin{array}[]{lcl}P_{-}(f\otimes g)&=&P_{-}(f_{h}\otimes A_{h}g)\ =\ P_{-}(f_{h}\otimes g)A_{h}\ =\\
&=&P_{-}(f_{h}\otimes g_{k})\,A^{\prime}_{k}A_{h}\ =\ -P_{-}(g_{k}\otimes f_{h})\,A^{\prime}_{k}A_{h}\ =\\
&=&-P_{-}(g_{k}\otimes f_{h})\,A_{h}A^{\prime}_{k}\ =\ -P_{-}(g_{k}\otimes f)\,A^{\prime}_{k}\ =\\
&=&-P_{-}(g\otimes f)\,,\end{array}

so that

P_{-}(f\otimes g)\ =\ -P_{-}(g\otimes f)\ \ ,\ \ f\bowtie g\,.

(3.14)

We conclude that validity of the Pauli principle is related to commutation properties
of
\mathcal{A}
(
locality
, when
\mathcal{A}
is the C*-algebra of a Haag-Kastler net),
and properties of the left
\mathcal{A}
-action, that is, the eventuality that
it is trivial on the involved vectors of
\EuFrak h
and elements of
\mathcal{A}
.
Note for
f\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
we have
\mathcal{A}(f)={\mathbb{C}}
so that

f,g\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
implies
f\bowtie g
.

Fermionic creation and annihilation operators.

To define and handle fermionic creation and annihilation operators
we make some remarks on elementary tensors, using the attention needed in the
case of Hilbert bimodules.

We start by noting that we may arrange order
n
permutations by considering
for any
k=1,\ldots,n
the set of those permutations that bring
the
k
-th object at first position.
Thus for any elementary tensor
v=wA\in\EuFrak h^{n}
,
w\in{\boldsymbol{h}}^{n}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
we may write

v_{-}\ \doteq\ P_{-}^{n}v\ =\ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\,w_{k}\otimes w^{(k)}_{-}A\,\in\EuFrak h^{n}_{-}\ ,

(3.15)

\displaystyle w^{(k)}_{-}\,\doteq\,\ldots\otimes_{-}\hat{w}_{k}\otimes_{-}\ldots=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{\varrho\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n-1,k}}\epsilon_{\varrho}\,w_{\varrho(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes w_{\varrho(n)}\,\in{\boldsymbol{h}}^{n-1}_{-}\,,

where the notation
\hat{w}_{k}
indicates that
w_{k}
does not appear in the tensor
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{n-1,k}
is understood as the permutation group of the set

\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{k\}
; the term
(-1)^{k-1}
in (
3.15
)
is the sign of the transposition bringing
w_{k}
at first position.
The above expression makes manifest that in general
\EuFrak{F}_{-}(\EuFrak h)
is not stable under
the left
\mathcal{A}
-action, because the terms

Bw_{k}=\sum_{i}w_{i}B_{ik}
,
B_{ik}\doteq{\left\langle\right.}w_{i},Bw_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}\in\mathcal{A}
,
typically belong to
\EuFrak h
and induce a mixing in the tensor product.
The point is that in general
Bw_{k}
does not belong to
{\boldsymbol{h}}
,
thus we must perform the operations of the proof of Lemma
3.1
to get a tensor of
the form
Bv_{-}=\sum_{i}w^{\prime}_{i}A^{\prime}_{i}
with
w^{\prime}_{i}\in{\boldsymbol{h}}^{n}
.
It is after this operation that we can apply the projection
P_{-}^{n}
and get

P_{-}^{n}Bv_{-}=\sum_{i}w^{\prime}_{i,-}A_{i}
.

In the sequel, we shall write
\EuFrak h_{\circ}\subseteq\EuFrak h
for the
vector space spanned by (finite linear combinations of) vectors of the type
w\gamma
,
w\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
,

\gamma\in\mathcal{G}
. By construction
\EuFrak h_{\circ}
is dense in
\EuFrak h
.

We now establish some properties of antisymmetric tensors
in case of a
\mathcal{G}
-twist. The most important is that, in spite of the previous
remark, the fermionic space is stable both under the
\mathcal{G}
-action and antisymmetric
tensor products by vectors in
\EuFrak h_{\circ}
.

Lemma 3.4

(Twists and permutation symmetry)
.

Let
\EuFrak h={\boldsymbol{h}}\mathcal{A}
be a free Hilbert bimodule with
\mathcal{G}
-twist

u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{U}({\boldsymbol{h}})
. Then the following properties hold:

1.

For any
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\mathcal{A}
it turns out

\gamma\EuFrak h^{n}_{-}\in\EuFrak h^{n}_{-}
,
so that
\EuFrak{F}_{-}(\EuFrak h)
is stable under the left module action by
\mathcal{G}
.

2.

Let
g=g_{i}\gamma_{i}\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}
with
g_{i}\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
,

\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{G}
. Then for any tensor
w_{-}A\in\EuFrak h^{n}_{-}
with

w_{-}\doteq w_{1}\otimes_{-}\ldots\otimes_{-}w_{n}\in{\boldsymbol{h}}_{-}^{n}
,

A\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

P_{-}^{n+1}(g\otimes w_{-}A)\ =\ g_{i}\otimes_{-}(u_{\gamma_{i}}w_{1})\otimes_{-}\ldots\otimes_{-}(u_{\gamma_{i}}w_{n})\cdot\gamma_{i}A\ \in\EuFrak h^{n}_{-}\,,

(3.16)

so
\EuFrak{F}_{-}(\EuFrak h)
is stable under antisymmetric tensor product
by elements of
\EuFrak h_{\circ}
;

3.

For
g=g_{i}\gamma_{i}\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}
it turns out

P_{-}^{n+1}(g\otimes w_{-}A)\ =\ \frac{1}{n}\,\left(g\otimes w_{-}A-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}w_{k}\otimes P_{-}^{n}(g\otimes w^{(k)}_{-}A)\right)\,.

(3.17)

Proof.

Point 1
. Let
w_{-}A\in\EuFrak h^{n}_{-}
with
w=w_{1}\otimes_{-}\ldots\otimes_{-}w_{n}
an elementary antisymmetric tensor.
Then for any permutation
\varrho
it turns out

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\gamma w_{\varrho(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes w_{\varrho(n)}&=&(u_{\gamma}w_{\varrho(1)})\otimes\ldots\otimes(u_{\gamma}w_{\varrho(n)})\gamma\,.\end{array}

Thus, defining
u_{\gamma}^{-}\doteq P_{-}^{n}\cdot\otimes^{n}u_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{U}({\boldsymbol{h}}_{-}^{n})

we find that
\gamma w_{-}A=(u_{\gamma}^{-}w_{-})\gamma A
belongs to
\EuFrak h^{n}_{-}

as claimed.

Point 2
. With the notation of the previous point, with
g=g_{i}\gamma_{i}
we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}g\otimes w_{-}A&=&\sum_{\varrho}\epsilon_{\varrho}\,g_{i}\otimes\gamma_{i}w_{\varrho(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes w_{\varrho(n)}A\,=\\
&=&\sum_{\varrho}\epsilon_{\varrho}\,g_{i}\otimes(u_{\gamma_{i}}w_{\varrho(1)})\otimes\ldots\otimes(u_{\gamma_{i}}w_{\varrho(n)})\gamma_{i}A\,=\\
&=&g_{i}\otimes(u_{\gamma_{i}}w_{1})\otimes_{-}\ldots\otimes_{-}(u_{\gamma_{i}}w_{n})\cdot\gamma_{i}A\,.\end{array}

Thus, using the fact that
P_{-}^{n}
is a right
\mathcal{A}
-linear operator, we conclude that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}P_{-}^{n}(g\otimes w_{-}A)&=&P_{-}^{n}(g_{i}\otimes((u_{\gamma_{i}}w_{1})\otimes_{-}\ldots\otimes_{-}(u_{\gamma_{i}}w_{n}))\,\gamma_{i}A\,=\\
&=&g_{i}\otimes_{-}(u_{\gamma_{i}}w_{1})\otimes_{-}\ldots\otimes_{-}(u_{\gamma_{i}}w_{n})\,\gamma_{i}A\,.\end{array}

Point 3
. We have, by applying (
3.15
) and (
3.16
),

\begin{array}[]{lcl}P_{-}^{n+1}(g\otimes w_{-}A)&=&(g_{i}\otimes_{-}u_{\gamma_{i}}^{-}w_{-})\gamma_{i}A\,=\\
&=&1/n\left(g_{i}\otimes u_{\gamma_{i}}^{-}w_{-}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\,w_{k}\otimes(g_{i}\otimes_{-}u_{\gamma_{i}}^{-}w^{(k)}_{-})\right)\gamma_{i}A\,=\\
&=&1/n\left(g_{i}\gamma_{i}\otimes w_{-}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\,w_{k}\otimes(g_{i}\gamma_{i}\otimes_{-}w^{(k)}_{-})\right)A\,=\\
&=&1/n\left(g\otimes w_{-}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\,w_{k}\otimes(g\otimes_{-}w^{(k)}_{-})\right)A\,.\end{array}

∎

Now, by (
3.15
), given
f\in\EuFrak h
we have

{\left\langle\right.}f|v_{-}\ =\ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k}(\pm 1)^{k-1}{\left\langle\right.}f,w_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}\,w^{(k)}_{-}A\,\in\EuFrak h^{n-1}\,,

(3.18)

thus in general we cannot say that
{\left\langle\right.}f|v_{-}\in\EuFrak h^{n-1}_{-}
. Yet we have the following property:

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that there is a
\mathcal{G}
-twist on
\EuFrak h
and let
f\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}
.
Then
{\left\langle\right.}f|v_{-}\in\EuFrak h^{n-1}_{-}
for all
v_{-}=w_{-}A\in\EuFrak h^{n}_{-}
,
and
\EuFrak{F}_{-}(\EuFrak h)
is stable under the action of the operator
{\left\langle\right.}f|
.

Proof.

Starting from (
3.18
) and writing
f=f_{i}\gamma_{i}
,
f_{i}\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
,

\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{G}
, we find

\begin{array}[]{lcl}{\left\langle\right.}f|v_{-}&=&1/n\sum_{k}(\pm 1)^{k-1}{\left\langle\right.}f_{i},w_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}\,\gamma_{i}^{*}w^{(k)}_{-}A\,=\\
&=&1/n\sum_{k}(\pm 1)^{k-1}{\left\langle\right.}f_{i},w_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}\,(u_{\gamma_{i}}^{*}w_{1})\otimes_{-}\ldots\otimes_{-}(u_{\gamma_{i}}^{*}w_{n})\,\gamma_{i}^{*}A\,.\end{array}

Since
{\left\langle\right.}f_{i},w_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, the last term belongs to
\EuFrak h^{n-1}_{-}
as claimed.
∎

The next computations will allow to evaluate the anti-commutation relations.
By (
3.17
), for
f\in\EuFrak h
and
g=g_{i}\gamma_{i}\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}
we get

{\left\langle\right.}f|P_{-}^{n+1}(g\otimes w_{-}A)\ =\ \frac{1}{n}\,\left({\left\langle\right.}f,g{\left.\right\rangle}w_{-}A-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}{\left\langle\right.}f,w_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}P_{-}^{n}(g\otimes w^{(k)}_{-})A\right)

(3.19)

and

P_{-}^{n+1}(g\otimes{\left\langle\right.}f|w_{-}A)\ =\ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k}(-1)^{k-1}P_{-}^{n}(g\otimes{\left\langle\right.}f,w_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}\,w^{(k)}_{-})A\,.

(3.20)

About the last equality, we note that due to the antisymmetrization operator
P_{-}^{n}

the factor
{\left\langle\right.}f,w_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}\in\mathcal{A}
appears in
i
-th position of the involved elementary tensor
for any
i=1,\ldots,n
.
This implies that
(
3.20
) may differ from the sum at r.h.s. of (
3.19
),
because in general
{\left\langle\right.}f,w_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}
cannot freely shift on the left of the
involved elementary tensor.

Let now
P_{-}\doteq\oplus_{n}P_{-}^{n}
, where
P_{-}^{0}
and
P_{-}^{1}
are the identity.
We introduce the notation
\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)\doteq\EuFrak{F}_{-}(\EuFrak h)\cap\EuFrak{F}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)
,
and for any
f\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}
define the fermionic annihilation and creation operators

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}(f):\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)\to\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)\ \ \ ,\ \ \ {\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}(f)\,\doteq\,a(f)\restriction\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)\,,\\
\\
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(f):\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)\to\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)\ \ ,\ \ {\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(f)\,\doteq\,P_{-}a^{*}(f)\restriction\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)\,.\end{array}\right.

(3.21)

Note that the property

{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}(f)\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)\,\subseteq\,\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)\ \ ,\ \ {\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}(f)\,=\,P_{-}{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}(f)\ \ ,\ \ \forall f\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}\,,

(3.22)

tacitly understood in (
3.21
)
is a consequence of the hypothesis that there is a
\mathcal{G}
-twist
and Lemma
3.5
.
Also note that for the moment we do not know whether the fermionic creation and annihilation operators
are bounded, thus in (
3.21
) we make use of the domain
\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)
.
In the following result we give an interpretation of the twist
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{U}({\boldsymbol{h}})

as an obstacle to make the creation and annihilation operators commute with elements of
\mathcal{A}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
v\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}
.
Then

\gamma{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(v)={\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(u_{\gamma}v)\gamma

and

\gamma{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}(u_{\gamma}^{*}v)={\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}(v)\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
w_{-}A\in\EuFrak h_{-}^{n}
,
w_{-}\in{\boldsymbol{h}}_{-}^{n}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
.
By the argument of the proof of Lemma
3.4
, Point 1, we find

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\gamma{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(v)w_{-}A&=&\gamma P_{-}(v\otimes w_{-})A\ =\ (u_{\gamma}v\otimes_{-}u_{\gamma}^{-}w_{-})\gamma A\ =\\
&=&{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(u_{\gamma}v)(u_{\gamma}^{-}w_{-})\gamma A\ =\ {\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(u_{\gamma}v)\gamma w_{-}A\,.\end{array}

With an analogous argument the claim about
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}(v)
is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
f\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}
.
Then
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(f)
is the adjoint of
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}(f)
over the domain
\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)
.

Proof.

By applying (
3.21
) and (
3.22
) we find

{\left\langle\right.}v,{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}(f)v^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\ {\left\langle\right.}v,a(f)P_{-}v^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\ {\left\langle\right.}P_{-}a^{*}(f)v,v^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\ {\left\langle\right.}{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(f)v,v^{\prime}{\left.\right\rangle}\,,

for all
v,v^{\prime}\in\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)
. This proves the Lemma.
∎

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})
. We define
\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\omega}(\EuFrak h)
as the Hilbert space
obtained by evaluation of
\EuFrak{F}_{-}(\EuFrak h)
over
\omega
.
In the previous lines we proved that

{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}(f)
and
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(f)
are well-defined,
\mathcal{A}
-linear
and one the adjoint of the other in the sense of Hilbert modules (on
\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)
);
thus, by the argument used to construct
a^{\omega}(f)
and
a^{*,\omega}(f)
, we define

{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{\omega}(f)\ \ \ ,\ \ \ {\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*,\omega}(f)\ \ \ ,\ \ \ f\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}\,,

as the evaluations of
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}(f)
and
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(f)
over
\omega
.
The domain of these operators is clearly given by
\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#,\omega}(\EuFrak h)
,
defined by evaluation of vectors in
\EuFrak{F}_{-}^{\#}(\EuFrak h)
.

4
CARs and fermionic fields

In the present section we study the anti-commutation relations that our fermionic
creation and annihilation operators fulfill, and define the corresponding Dirac field
assuming the presence of a suitable conjugation.
We bring on the light some features
that in general prevent anticommutators to be expressed only in
terms of the given
\mathcal{A}
-valued scalar product and, primarily, make anticommutators
non-local in the sense that they do not vanish even when the involved ”spinors” are orthogonal.
In fact, not surprisingly, to get anticommutators of the usual form
besides orthogonality we must require
mutual freeness Rem.
3.3
.

We proceed by maintaining the assumptions of the previous section,
so that
\EuFrak h={\boldsymbol{h}}\mathcal{A}
is free and there is a
\mathcal{G}
-twist

u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{U}({\boldsymbol{h}})
,
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\mathcal{A}
.
Before computing our anti-commutation relations,
for convenience we give a notion of mutual freeness explicitly designed to handle
vectors in
\EuFrak h_{\circ}
.
Let
f=f_{i}\gamma_{i}
and
g=g_{h}\gamma_{h}\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}
,
with
f_{i},g_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
,
\gamma_{i},\gamma^{\prime}_{h}\in\mathcal{G}
.
We write
f\bowtie_{\circ}g
whenever for all
i,h
it turns out

[\gamma_{i}\,,\,\gamma^{\prime}_{h}]\,=\,0\ \ ,\ \ u_{\gamma_{i}}g_{h}\,=\,g_{h}\ \ ,\ \ u_{\gamma^{\prime}_{h}}f_{i}\,=\,f_{i}\,.

(4.1)

Lemma 4.1
.

If
f,g\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}
and
f\bowtie_{\circ}g
, then
f\bowtie g
.

Proof.

As a preliminary step we note that any generator
{\left\langle\right.}w,f{\left.\right\rangle}
of
\mathcal{A}(f)
,
defined for
w\in{\boldsymbol{h}}
, is a linear combination in
\{\gamma_{i}\}
,
so
\mathcal{A}(f)
is contained in the C*-algebra generated by
\{\gamma_{i}\}
.
Thus the hypothesis
f\bowtie_{\circ}g
and (
4.1
) imply

[A,B]=0
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}(f)
,
B\in\mathcal{A}(g)
;
moreover,

\gamma_{i}g=(u_{\gamma_{i}}g_{h})\gamma_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{h}=(u_{\gamma_{i}}g_{h})\gamma^{\prime}_{h}\gamma_{i}=g\gamma_{i}
,
and analogously for
f
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{h}
. This implies
f\bowtie g
,
as claimed.
∎

Anticommutators of creation operators.

Let
f\bowtie_{\circ}g\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}
and

v_{-}=w_{-}A\in\EuFrak h_{-}^{n}
,
w_{-}\in{\boldsymbol{h}}_{-}^{n}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
.
Writing
f=f_{i}\gamma_{i}
,
g=g_{h}\gamma^{\prime}_{h}
we find

\begin{array}[]{lcl}{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(f){\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(g)w_{-}A&=&P_{-}^{n+2}(f\otimes P_{-}^{n+1}(g\otimes w_{-}))A\ =\\
&=&P_{-}^{n+2}(f_{i}\otimes\gamma_{i}P_{-}^{n+1}(g_{h}\otimes u^{\prime}_{h}w_{-}))\gamma^{\prime}_{h}A\ =\\
&=&P_{-}^{n+2}(f_{i}\otimes P_{-}^{n+1}(g_{h}\otimes u_{i}u^{\prime}_{h}w_{-}))\gamma_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{h}A\ =\\
&=&-P_{-}^{n+2}(g_{h}\otimes P_{-}^{n+1}(f_{i}\otimes u_{i}u^{\prime}_{h}w_{-}))\gamma_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{h}A\ =\\
&=&-P_{-}^{n+2}(g_{h}\otimes P_{-}^{n+1}(f_{i}\otimes\gamma_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{h}w_{-}))A\ =\\
&=&-P_{-}^{n+2}(g_{h}\otimes P_{-}^{n+1}(f\otimes\gamma^{\prime}_{h}w_{-}))A\ =\\
&=&-P_{-}^{n+2}(g\otimes P_{-}^{n+1}(f\otimes w_{-}))A\ =\\
&=&-{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(g){\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(f)w_{-}A\,.\end{array}

In the previous computation, for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we defined the antisymmetric tensor powers

u_{i}\doteq u(\gamma_{i})^{-}
,
u^{\prime}_{h}\doteq u(\gamma^{\prime}_{h})^{-}\in\mathcal{U}({\boldsymbol{h}}_{-}^{n})
;
moreover, we used (
3.16
) and the fact that expressions of the type

P_{-}^{n+2}(f_{i}\otimes P_{-}^{n+1}(g_{h}\otimes u_{i}u^{\prime}_{h}w_{-}))

are ordinary antisymmetric tensor powers on Hilbert space, so the Pauli principle applies
and the minus sign appears when we exchange
f_{i}
and
g_{h}
.
We conclude that

[{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(f)\,,\,{\boldsymbol{a}}_{-}^{*}(g)]_{+}\,=\,0\ \ ,\ \ f\bowtie_{\circ}g\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}\,.

(4.2)

Note that the need to assume
f\bowtie_{\circ}g
to make the above anticommutators vanish
is an aspect of non-validity of the Pauli principle in full generality, Remark
3.3
.

Anticommutators of annihilation operators.

It should not be a surprise that passing to the adjoint of (
4.2
)
the anticommutators of annihilation operators vanish. Anyway it is instructive to perform
the explicit computations, both to understand the interplay of
elements of
\mathcal{G}
with the relation
\bowtie_{\circ}

and to keep in evidence details that may be useful to approach (unbounded)
bosonic annihilation operators.

We maintain the hypothesis

f\bowtie_{\circ}g\in\EuFrak h_{\circ}
and the notation for the orthonormal decompositions

f=f_{i}\gamma_{i}
,
g=g_{h}\gamma^{\prime}_{h}
.
As a first step, we further apply (
3.15
) and get

w_{-}\,=\,\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\left(\sum_{k<h}(-1)^{k+h-1}w_{k}\otimes w_{h}\otimes w^{(h,k)}+\sum_{k>h}(-1)^{k+h}w_{h}\otimes w_{k}\otimes w^{(h,k)}\right)

(4.3)

=\,\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{k<h}(-1)^{k+h-1}(w_{k}\otimes_{-}w_{h})\otimes w^{(h,k)}\,,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \,\,\,\,

(4.4)

where

w^{(h,k)}\,\doteq\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\ldots\hat{w}_{k}\otimes_{-}\ldots\otimes_{-}\hat{w}_{h}\ldots\ \ ,\ \ k<h\,,\\
\ldots\hat{w}_{h}\otimes_{-}\ldots\otimes_{-}\hat{w}_{k}\ldots\ \ ,\ \ k>h\,;\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

in (
4.3
) we used the fact that for
k<h
the term
w_{h}

needs a cyclic permutation with order
h-2
to shift on the left of the tensor

w_{1}\otimes\ldots\hat{w}_{k}\ldots\otimes w_{n}
, whilst for
k>h
the order is
h-1
.
Using elementary properties of symmetric tensors, we find

w^{(h,k)}=-w^{(k,h)}
,
h\neq k
.
After these preparations, we get

\displaystyle{\left\langle\right.}g|{\left\langle\right.}f|v_{-}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{k<h}(-1)^{k+h-1}{\left\langle\right.}f\otimes g\,,\,w_{k}\otimes_{-}w_{h}{\left.\right\rangle}\,w^{(h,k)}A

\displaystyle=\,\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{k<h}(-1)^{k+h-1}{\left\langle\right.}f\otimes g\,,\,w_{k}\otimes_{-}w_{h}{\left.\right\rangle}\,w^{(h,k)}A\,.

We analyze in details the terms

\begin{array}[]{l}{\left\langle\right.}f\otimes g\,,\,w_{h}\otimes_{-}w_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}\ =\\
{\left\langle\right.}g_{h}\gamma^{\prime}_{h}\,,\,{\left\langle\right.}f_{i}\gamma_{i},w_{h}{\left.\right\rangle}\,w_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}-{\left\langle\right.}g_{h}\gamma^{\prime}_{h}\,,\,{\left\langle\right.}f_{i}\gamma_{i},w_{k}{\left.\right\rangle}\,w_{h}{\left.\right\rangle}\,=\\

5
Conclusions

In the present paper we presented a construction based on Hilbert bimodules,
in which the spatial tensor product of a CAR algebra by a C*-algebra
is replaced by a twisted product.
This allows to construct field systems with non-trivial commutation relations, as in
(
4.19
) and (
4.24
).
The technical obstacles concerning tensor products and (the absence of)
permutation symmetry in Hilbert bimodules have been overcome by introducing
the notion of twist, which yields a class of left actions for which
these drawbacks are under control

3

3

We remark that the same technique may be used

in such a scenario, the construction in
[
18

\mathcal{A}

action in the sense of the present paper.

The models presented in the previous section are elementary,
yet they pose questions that in our opinion deserve to be discussed.
For example, the model 1 exhibits Weyl unitaries that induce local gauge transformations,
thus in regular representations of
\mathcal{W}

In the model 2.1,

In this regard, the model 2.2 provides a simple illustration of the fact that this

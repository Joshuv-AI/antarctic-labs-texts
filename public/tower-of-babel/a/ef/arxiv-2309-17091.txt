Abstract
. Our work is motivated by the connection established between Lorentzian polynomials and the Dressian in the seminal work of Brändén and Huh on Lorentzian polynomials. We analyze this relation for the class of positroids, and are able to show that in this case, we can relate a multiaffine homogenous stable polynomial to it. Additionally, we also highlight that a conjecture for matroids posed by Brändén and Huh is true when considered over the class of Rayleigh matroids which strictly contain the class of positroids. We collect these findings along with other results for further exploration.

1.
Introduction

The combinatorics associated with electrical networks carries a lot of really intricate details related to matroids, an initial documentation of which can be found in
[
25
]
. In subsequent studies
[
11
]
the relation between matroids and the geometry of polynomials is described. In recent work
[
8
]
with the introduction of the notion of

it has been demonstrated that highly intrinsic properties concerning matroids can be obtained via findings that are based on the geometry of polynomials. For example, the well-known Mason conjecture for matroids can be resolved by methods developed in
[
8

14

q
-state Potts model partition function
[
7
]
.

In
[
11
]
the authors are interested in studying the
half-plane property
for multivariate polynomials. For matroids, this is equivalent to the case when the basis generating polynomial is
stable
. The authors in
[
11
]
also discuss the various examples of matroids that satisfy the half-plane matroids: uniform matroids, sixth-root of unity matroids, and all matroids of rank and corank two satisfy the half-plane property. Moreover, in recent work
[
16
]
the authors provide a complete classification of which matroids on at most
8
elements have the half-plane property. These investigations have also now forayed into asking slightly refined formulations, for example, the
Rayleigh
property for polynomials which captures negative dependence correlation between elements of the bases in a matroid. In
[
10
]
the authors introduce the notion of
Rayleigh
matroid, which is the class of matroids whose basis generating polynomials is Rayleigh. They also provide examples of matroids which are Rayleigh: regular matroids and all matroids of rank three are Rayleigh.

In this paper, our emphasis is on the class of
positroids
, which in essence captures positivity in the class of matroids. In one of the first results, we build on the work done in
[
24
]
in which they show that a certain multi affine homogenous polynomial over
\mathbb{C}
which
represents
a point in the Grassmannian is stable if and only if this point represents a nonegative point which corresponds to a positroid. We extend this result over real and real closed fields, and use this to refine the characterization of points in the Dressian of a positroid in the form of this
result

Theorem 1
.

Let
V
represent a nonegative point in

and let
\mathcal{M}
be a positroid of rank

on

elements with bases

that corresponds to

. Let

represent the nonzero

of

, which also satisfy the quadratic Plücker relations. Then for

, the multi affine homogenous stable polynomial

This result is sort of intermediate between the weak half-plane property and strong half-plane property; in the sense that in the case of the strong plane property, the polynomial being considered is fixed and it is the basis generating polynomial of the matroid, whereas, in the case of the weak half-plane property, it guarantees only the existence of a stable polynomial whose support is the bases of the matroid.

Additionally, we also share an observation to highlight that Conjecture 3.12 in
[
8
]
is true when considered over the class of Rayleigh matroids

[

The following conditions are equivalent for any non-empty

Essentially, Conjecture 3.12

We conclude with an extension of results relating Lorentzian polynomials and Dressians to the case of Flag Dressians and we also list some problems which we aim to work on in the future.

2.
Preliminaries

We try to introduce the basic notions in this section which we use in our subsequent sections. We refer the reader to
[
22

A
matroid

\mathcal{M}
of rank
k

E
is a nonempty collection
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\binom{E}{k}
of
k

E

of
\mathcal{M}
, that satisfies the basis exchange axiom:
For any
I,J\in\mathcal{B}
and

b\in J
such that
I\setminus\{a\}\cup\{b\}\in\mathcal{B}
.

A matroid is called

if it can be represented by columns of a matrix over some field
\mathbb{K}
. We index the columns of a
k\times n
matrix by the set
[n]
.

The generating function of a matroid
\mathcal{M}
with bases
\mathcal{B}
(also referred to as the basis enumeration polynomials in
[
18
]
) is defined as the polynomial

(1)

M(x)=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}x^{B}

A slightly general notion of

of a subset

f_{J}=\sum_{\alpha\in J}\frac{x^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\quad\text{where}\quad\alpha!=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}!

and we see that these two notions agree when
J\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}
.

The

\mathbb{K}^{n}

.

An element

is understood as a collection of

. Let

be a
k\times n
-matrix consisting of column vectors

. We call

. This defines a matroid

whose bases are the
k

such that
\text{det}(A[I])\neq 0
. It is important to note that this is independent of the choice of

, and only depends on

. Here,

denotes the determinant of the

submatrix of

A

Definition 4

We now discuss the background of the definition of stable polynomials and how they are related to the study of Lorentzian polynomials. In earlier works, the definition of stable polynomials is referred to as the

[

]
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\{x\in\mathbb{C}\>\>|\>\>\text{Re}x>0\}

Definition 7

A polynomial

In this case,

.

Remark 8
.

In

Let

be the set of degree

. We refer the reader to

is the limit of

[

]

Lorentzian polynomials are a generalization of stable polynomials, i.e., all stable polynomials are Lorentzian, moreover, any homogeneous stable polynomial is a constant multiple of a Lorentzian polynomial
[
8
]
.

In
[
11
]
the authors envisaged that for large classes of matroids, the

are stable. Subsequent studies also concentrate on certain relaxations of this class, the most prominent of them being

polynomials,

Definition 9
.

Let

be a fixed positive real number, and let
f
be a polynomial in
\mathbb{R}[w_{1},\cdots,w_{n}]
.

is called

\partial^{\alpha}f(x)\>\partial^{\alpha+e_{i}+e_{j}}f(x)\leq c\>\partial^{\alpha+e_{i}}f(x)\>\partial^{\alpha+e_{j}}f(x)\quad\text{for all}\>\>i,j\in[n],\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}

When

each variable, the

f(x)\partial^{i}\partial^{j}f(x)\leq c\>\>\partial^{i}f(x)\partial^{j}f(x)\>\>\text{for all distinct}\>\>i,j\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\>\>\text{and}\>\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}

A multi-affine polynomial

f(x)\partial^{i}\partial^{j}f(x)\leq c\>\>\partial^{i}f(x)\partial^{j}f(x)\>\>\text{for all distinct}\>\>i,j\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\>\>\text{and}\>\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

A multi-affine polynomial is stable if and only if it is strongly Rayleigh
[
6

. Based on the definition of Rayleigh polynomials, the notion of

[

]
was introduced, where a matroid

is a Rayleigh matroid if its basis generating polynomial is Rayleigh. Similarly, there also exists the class of

, where a matroid is a

if its basis generating polynomial is strongly Rayleigh. It is straightforward to see from
[

We now define notions related to tropical geometry that appear in our discussion and we refer the reader to
[
17
]
for further details. Tropical geometry is the study of vanishing sets of polynomials defined over the

semiring
\mathbb{T}=\{\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\},\text{max}=\oplus,+=\odot\}
. A

\mathbb{T}
with the binary operations replaced by
\oplus
and
\odot
.
\text{Trop}(f)
denotes the

associated with

which is the collection of all points where the maxima is achieved at least twice.

The

\text{TropGr}(k,n)
is the intersection of the tropical hypersurfaces

, where

ranges over all elements of the

which is generated by the

, and therefore it is also a tropical variety
[
17
]
. The

is the intersection of the tropical hypersurfaces
\text{Trop}(f)
, where

ranges over all three-term Plücker relations, that generate the

and hence it possesses the structure of a tropical prevariety
[
17
]
. The underlying matroid for the definitions of the tropical Grassmannian and Dressian is the

\mathcal{U}_{k,n}
. However, the notion of Dressian has been extended to arbitrary matroids with the idea of the

[
21
]
.

Remark 10
.

We acknowledge that in some instances, all Plücker relations (not necessarily only the 3-term Plücker relations) are considered for the definition of the Dressian. However, by the work in
[

]
we know that these two notions coincide over the tropical hyperfield and hence our definition would remain consistent for our discussion.

3.

(iii)

\mu

Definition 28

A matroid

It is clear from the definition that Rayleigh matroids are balanced. In

Equivalently the Rayleigh condition can be restated in terms of probability distribution in the following way

for

\text{Pr}(i\in B,j\in B)\>\>\text{Pr}(i\not\in B,j\not\in B)\leq\text{Pr}(i\in B,j\not\in B)\>\>\text{Pr}(j\in B,i\not\in B)

\text{Pr}(i\in B,j\in B)\>\>\text{Pr}(i\not\in B,j\not\in B)=0.04355

\text{Pr}(i\in B,j\not\in B)\>\>\text{Pr}(j\in B,i\not\in B)=0.04298

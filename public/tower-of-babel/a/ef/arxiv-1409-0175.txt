Study of formality for the Heisenberg algebra
Olivier Elchinger

Abstract
In this paper, we compute the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra with values in its universal enveloping algebra. We also compute the Schouten brackets on cochains and cohomology level in order to write the formality equations. It turns out that there is no formality, and that the perturbed L ∞ L_{\infty} structure on the cohomology has non-trivial terms in infinitely many degrees.



Keywords
: Heisenberg Lie algebra, Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology, formality equations.

2010 AMS Subject Classification
: 17-08, 17B56, 16E45

Introduction

Deformation of structure theories is useful to formalize quantum physics. The algebraic structure considered by classical mechanics is the associative commutative algebra of smooth functions over a symplectic, or more generally, a Poisson manifold. Deformation quantization consists to construct an associative non-commutative multiplication (more precisely a
\star
-product) on the formal series in
\hbar
(which represents Planck’s constant) with coefficients in this algebra, which encodes the Poisson bracket in the first order. The Poisson structure is then called the classical limit and the deformation is the
\star
-product. This point of view, initiated in 1978 in
[
BFF+78
]
, tries to consider quantum mechanics as a deformation from classical mechanics. This pioneering work raises the fundamental questions about the existence and the uniqueness of a deformation quantization for a given Poisson manifold.

The first results treated the case of symplectics manifolds. The general case of Poisson manifolds was solved by Kontsevich in 1997 in
[
Kon03
]
. He deduced the result by proving a much more general statement, which he called « formality conjecture ». Endowed with the Gerstenhaber bracket, the Hochschild complex of the algebra of smooth functions over a Poisson manifold admits a graded Lie algebraic structure by shift, which controls the deformations of the Poisson bracket. Kontsevich shows that this complex is linked with its cohomology — which therefore controls the same deformations — by a
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism, called a formality map. Using this formality map, he shows of to deform the commutative product of functions in a
\star
-product.

Konstevich shows in particular that the formality criterion is true for symmetric algebras over a finite-dimensional vector space are formal. Bordemann and Makhlouf have examined in
[
BM08
]
a slight generalization to the case of universal enveloping algebras. They showed (implicit in Kontsevich’s work) that the formality for a Lie algebra is equivalent to those of its universal enveloping algebra. They also proved that there is formality for the universal enveloping algebra of an affine Lie algebra. These methods were used in
[
BMP05
]
and give informations about the rigidity of universal enveloping algebras.

In
[
Elc12
]
, we detailed the definitions of formality for associative and Lie algebras, and we studied the formality equations for the associative free algebra, and for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(3)
. It turns out that in these cases, there is no formality in Kontsevich’s sense, but one has to add an extra term to the graded Lie bracket on the cohomology to obtain a perturbed formality map.

This work is part of this line of research. We study here the formality for the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra. Although the Lie bracket is more simple than the one of
\mathfrak{so}(3)
, the cohomology is more important, and therefore the formality equations contains more terms. Actually, to obtain a perturbed formality map in this case, we have to add infinitely many terms to the Schouten bracket on the cohomology.

The paper is organised as follows. In
Section 1
, we set the notations and recall the graded Lie structure (Schouten bracket) on the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of a Lie algebra and its cohomology. We also give the definition of formality for this complex and write down the formality equations. In
Section 2
, we compute the cohomology of the Heisenberg algebra.
Section 3
and
Section 4
are devoted to the computation of the Schouten brackets on cochain level and cohomology, in order to solve the formality equations in
Section 5
.

I wish to thank gratefully Mr Makhlouf who showed me his first computations of the cohomology for the Heisenberg algebra and for his advice to pursue the study of the formality.

1
Formality of Lie algebras

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ])
be a Lie
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. We consider the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential complex
(C_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g}),\delta_{CE})
with values in
\Sym\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle C^{0}\coloneqq C_{CE}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq\Sym\mathfrak{g}\qquad C^{k}\coloneqq C_{CE}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq\Hom\big({\bigwedgeop\displaylimits}^{k}\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g}\big)\quad\text{for $k\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}$}

(1.1)

\displaystyle\delta_{CE}^{k}:C^{k}\to C^{k+1}

and its cohomology

\displaystyle H_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{k\in\mathbb{N}}H_{CE}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})

where for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the
k^{\mathrm{th}}
Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology group with coefficients in
\Sym\mathfrak{g}
is

H^{k}\coloneqq H_{CE}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq\frac{\Kr(\delta_{CE}^{k})}{\Img(\delta_{CE}^{k-1})}\coloneqq Z^{k}/B^{k}

(1.2)

The coboundary operator
\delta_{CE}
equals
[\pi,\penalty\ ]_{s}
, where
\pi=[\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ]\in\Hom\big({\bigwedgeop\displaylimits}^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}\big)
is the Lie bracket from
\mathfrak{g}
and
[\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ]_{s}
is the Schouten bracket on the complex
C_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})
.

As the Gerstenhaber bracket, the Schouten bracket defines a graded Lie bracket on the shifted space
C_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})[1]
. Therefore the graded antisymmetry and graded Jacobi identities: for
\xi,\eta,\zeta\in C_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})[1]
we have

\displaystyle[\eta,\xi]_{s}=-(-1)^{|\eta||\xi|}[\xi,\eta]_{s},

(1.3)

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\xi||\zeta|}[[\xi,\eta]_{s},\zeta]_{s}+(-1)^{|\eta||\xi|}[[\eta,\zeta]_{s},\xi]_{s}+(-1)^{|\zeta||\eta|}[[\zeta,\xi]_{s},\eta]_{s}=0,

(1.4)

degrees being taken in
C_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})[1]
.

On the other hand there is the pointwise exterior multiplication
\wedge
which makes
C_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})
a graded commutative algebra. The Schouten bracket and the exterior multiplication are compatible by the graded Leibniz rule

[\xi,\eta\wedge\zeta]_{s}=[\xi,\eta]_{s}\wedge\zeta+(-1)^{|\xi|(|\eta|+1)}\eta\wedge[\xi,\zeta]_{s},

(1.5)

(if
\eta
is of degree
|\eta|
in
C_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})[1]
then it is of degree
|\eta|+1
in
C_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})
).

Throughout the computations, we will use the fact that
C_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})
is an
\Sym\mathfrak{g}
-module
i.e.
for
\xi\in C_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g}),\ f\in\Sym\mathfrak{g}
,
f\wedge\xi=f\xi
. We also give some examples of Schouten brackets which will be used later.

Example 1.1
.

The space
C^{1}\coloneqq C_{CE}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})
is the space of vectors fields,
i.e.
the derivations of
\Sym\mathfrak{g}=C^{0}
. For
f,g\in\Sym\mathfrak{g},\ X,Y\in C^{1}

\displaystyle[f,g]_{s}=0,

\displaystyle[X,f]_{s}=X(f)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\partial_{i}f,

\displaystyle[X,Y]_{s}=[X,Y]=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\partial_{i}Y-\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}\partial_{i}X,

with in particular

\displaystyle[f\partial_{i},g\partial_{j}]=f(\partial_{i}g)\partial_{j}-g(\partial_{j}f)\partial_{i}.

The Schouten bracket
[\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ]_{s}
on the shifted complex
C[1]\coloneqq C_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})[1]
induces a bracket
[\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ]_{s}^{\prime}
on the shifted cohomology complex
H[1]\coloneqq H_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})[1]
, which is also a graded Lie algebra.

We consider
\overline{d}
the unique coderivation of
\Sym(H[2])
induced by
[\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ]^{\prime}_{s}[1]
and
\overline{\delta_{CE}+D}
, the unique coderivation of
\Sym(C[2])
induced by
\delta_{CE}+[\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ]_{s}[1]
. They are also differentials, equipping respectively
(H[1],\overline{d})
and
(C[1],\overline{\delta_{CE}+D})
of structures of
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Definition 1.2
.

The Chevalley-Eilenberg complex
C[1]
associated to the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ])
is said to be

if there is a

-quasi-isomorphism
\Phi:\Sym(H[2])\to\Sym(C[2])
,
i.e.

(\Phi\otimes\Phi)\circ\Delta_{\Sym{}H[2]}=\Delta_{\Sym{}C[2]}\circ\Phi\qquad\textrm{et}\qquad\overline{\delta_{CE}+D}\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ\overline{d},

(1.6)

such that the restriction
\Phi_{1}
of
\Phi
to
C[2]
is a section. The map
\Phi
is called a

.

Even if there is no formality map between
\Sym(H[2])
and
\Sym(C[2])
, we can always modify the

structure induced by

and by induction build the components of the differential

and of the

-morphism

. This construction was done by Bordemann et al. in
[
BGH+05
, A.4, Proposition A.3]
. This result can also be obtained by a more general point of view using the

for contraction of differential complexes. This was done by induction by Huebschmann for dg-Lie and

-algebras in
[
Hue10

Hue11
]
, and Bordemann gives in
[

]

The obtained

morphism
\Phi=\overline{\varphi}
and new differential
\overline{d}
satisfy the equation

\Phi\circ\overline{d}=\overline{\delta_{CE}+D}\circ\Phi,

(1.7)

which writes, after projection on
C[2]
and using that

,

\varphi\circ(d\tilde{\star}id_{\Sym(H[2])})=\delta_{CE}\circ\varphi+\frac{1}{2}D_{2}\circ\varphi\tilde{\star}\varphi.

(1.8)

Since

, evaluating on

gives

\begin{split}\sumop\displaylimits_{a=2}^{k+1}&\sumop\displaylimits_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\dotsb<i_{a}\leqslant k+1}\nu_{a}(y_{1},\dotsc,y_{k+1})\\
&\cdotp\varphi_{k+2-a}(d_{a}(y_{i_{1}}\bullet\dotsc\bullet y_{i_{a}})\bullet y_{1}\bullet\dotsc\bullet\widehat{y_{i_{1}}}\bullet\dotsc\bullet\widehat{y_{i_{a}}}\bullet\dotsc\bullet y_{k+1})\\

+\frac{1}{2}&\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{k}\sumop\displaylimits_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\dotsb<i_{a}\leqslant k+1}\nu_{a}(y_{1},\dotsc,y_{k+1})\\
&\cdotp D_{2}(\varphi_{a}(y_{i_{1}}\bullet\dotsc\bullet y_{i_{a}})\bullet\varphi_{k+1-a}(y_{1}\bullet\dotsc\bullet\widehat{y_{i_{1}}}\bullet\dotsc\bullet\widehat{y_{i_{a}}}\bullet\dotsc\bullet y_{k+1}).\end{split}

(1.9)

The maps
\varphi_{k}
are of degree
0
, and the signs

\nu_{a}(y_{1},\dotsc,y_{k+1})=\prodop\displaylimits_{r=1}^{a}(-1)^{|y_{i_{r}}|(|y_{1}|+\dotsb+\widehat{|y_{i_{1}}|}+\dotsb+\widehat{|y_{i_{r-1}}|}+\dotsb+|y_{i_{r}-1}|)}

However, care of signs must be taken when working with the shifted maps. Denoting by

2
Cohomology of the Heisenberg algebra

From now on, we take
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}_{3}
the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra

\mathfrak{h}_{3}=<x,y,z\ \ |\ \ [x,y]=z>

or, writing the Lie bracket as a bivector
[\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ]=\pi=z\partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{y}\in\Hom\big({\bigwedgeop\displaylimits}^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}\big)
.

Since
\dim\mathfrak{g}=3
, the degree zero part
C^{0}=\Sym\mathfrak{g}
of the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex
C_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})
identifies with
\mathbb{K}[x,y,z]
. Moreover, the cohomology
H_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})
is concentrated only in degrees
0,1,2,3
. Indeed,
C^{k}=\Hom\big({\bigwedgeop\displaylimits}^{k}\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g}\big)=\{0\}
for
k\geqslant 4
, because
\partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{y}\wedge\partial_{z}\wedge\partial_{t}=0
for
t\in\{x,y,z\}
.

In the following computations, we mostly use the three relations satisfied by the Schouten bracket and the exterior multiplication
\wedge
, namely the graded antisymmetry (
1.3
), the graded Jacobi identity (
1.4
) and the graded Leibniz rule (
1.5
).

2.0
Degree
0

In degree
0
, we have
H_{CE}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})=\Sym\mathfrak{g}^{\mathfrak{g}}=\{f\in\Sym\mathfrak{g},\ \delta_{CE}^{0}(f)=0\}
. Let
f\in\Sym\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{K}[x,y,z]
,

\delta_{CE}^{0}(f)=[\pi,f]_{s}=[z\partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{y},f]_{s}=z\partial_{y}f\partial_{x}-z\partial_{x}f\partial_{y},

so
\delta_{CE}^{0}(f)=0\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{aligned} z\partial_{y}f&=0\\
-z\partial_{x}f&=0\end{aligned}\right.\ \Leftrightarrow f(x,y,z)=\varphi(z)
is a function of
z
only.

Thus
H_{CE}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})=\mathbb{K}[z]
.

2.1
Degree
1

The
1
-cocycles are
Z^{1}=\{X:\mathfrak{g}\to\Sym\mathfrak{g},\ \delta_{CE}^{1}(X)=0\}

X\in C^{1}=\Hom(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})

X=X_{1}\partial_{x}+X_{2}\partial_{y}+X_{3}\partial_{z}

\displaystyle\delta_{CE}^{1}(X)=[\pi,X]_{s}

\displaystyle=[z\partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{y},X_{1}\partial_{x}+X_{2}\partial_{y}+X_{3}\partial_{z}]_{s}

\displaystyle=\left(z\partial_{x}X_{1}+z\partial_{y}X_{2}-X_{3}\right)\partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{y}+z\partial_{y}X_{3}\ \partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{z}-z\partial_{x}X_{3}\ \partial_{y}\wedge\partial_{z},

z\partial_{y}X_{3}&=0\\
-z\partial_{x}X_{3}&=0\end{aligned}\right..

The last two equations imply that
X_{3}(x,y,z)=\varphi(z)
is a function of
z
only. The first equation shows then that
X_{3}=\varphi(z)=z\psi(z)
is a multiple of
z
; and can be rewritten as

\partial_{x}X_{1}+\partial_{y}X_{2}=\psi.

(2.1)

Setting

\left\{\begin{gathered}X_{1}=-\partial_{y}g+Y_{1}\psi\\
X_{2}=\partial_{x}g+Y_{2}\psi\end{gathered}\right.,

g\in\Sym\mathfrak{g}

) in

is equivalent to solve in

\partial_{x}Y_{1}+\partial_{y}Y_{2}=1.

(2.2)

The solutions of (

) are of the form
\left\{\begin{aligned} Y_{1}&=ax+K_{1}(y,z)\\
Y_{2}&=(1-a)y+K_{2}(x,z)\end{aligned}\right.,

with

, and a good choice of the function

\left\{\begin{aligned} X_{1}&=-\partial_{y}g+ax\psi\\
X_{2}&=\partial_{x}g+(1-a)y\psi\end{aligned}\right..

We obtained the following expression for
1
-cocycles

X=\left(ax\psi(z)-\partial_{y}g\right)\partial_{x}+\big((1-a)y\psi(z)+\partial_{x}g\big)\partial_{y}+z\psi(z)\partial_{z}.

(2.3)

The
1
-coboundaries are
B^{1}=\left\{X:\mathfrak{g}\to\Sym\mathfrak{g},\ \exists\ F\in\Sym\mathfrak{g},\ X=\delta_{CE}^{0}(F)\right\}
. Since we have
\delta_{CE}^{0}(F)=z\partial_{y}F\partial_{x}-z\partial_{x}F\partial_{y}

Writing

W=\partial_{y}g\partial_{x}-\partial_{x}g\partial_{y}=\underbrace{\partial_{y}g_{0}(x,y)\partial_{x}-\partial_{x}g_{0}(x,y)\partial_{y}}_{\in\ Z^{1}\setminus B^{1}}+\delta_{CE}^{0}(g_{1}).

Since the
z

z
-part. We also use the following notations:

. The expression (

with

. (The element

Let

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-a\right)xy\varphi_{1}(z)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\left(ax\varphi_{0}-\partial_{y}g_{0}+\dfrac{1}{2}xz\varphi_{1}(z)\right)\partial_{x}+\left((1-a)y\varphi_{0}+\partial_{x}g_{0}+\dfrac{1}{2}yz\varphi_{1}(z)\right)\partial_{y}+z\varphi(z)\partial_{z}+\delta_{CE}^{0}(k)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\varphi=\psi\qquad\text{and}\qquad g_{0}-h_{0}=(a-b)xy\varphi_{0},

2.2
Degree

The

\displaystyle\xi+\delta_{CE}^{1}(X)+\delta_{CE}^{1}(Y)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{y}(g_{0}-zh_{0})\ \partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{z}+\partial_{x}(g_{0}-zh_{0})\ \partial_{y}\wedge\partial_{z}

H_{CE}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})=\left\{[\xi]:\mathfrak{g}\wedge\mathfrak{g}\to\Sym\mathfrak{g},\ \xi=X_{g}\wedge\partial_{z}\right\},

(2.6)

with

\displaystyle\xi=\delta_{CE}^{2}(\phi)\Leftrightarrow\xi_{123}\ \partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{y}\wedge\partial_{z}=z\left(\partial_{x}\phi_{13}+\partial_{y}\phi_{23}\right)\partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{y}\wedge\partial_{z}

4

We will now compute the various Schouten brackets

5
Formality equations

In this section, we will explicit the formality equations (
1.9
) for the first orders. In particular, we will obtain non-trivial terms
d_{k}
of arity
k=3
and higher showing that the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the Heisenberg algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{3}
is not formal in Kontsevich sense,
i.e.
the
L_{\infty}
structure on the cohomology is not induced by the Schouten bracket
d_{2}=[\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ]^{\prime}_{s}[1]
only.

Note that in equations (
1.9
), we work with elements
y_{i}\in H[2]
so the degrees are shifted again.

The maps
\varphi_{k}:\Sym^{k}(H[2])\to C[2]
are of degree zero, so for
i_{1},\dotsc,i_{n}\in\text{\textlbrackdbl}-2,1\text{\textrbrackdbl}

\varphi_{k}(H[2]^{i_{1}}\bullet\dotsb\bullet H[2]^{i_{n}})\subset C[2]^{i_{1}+\dotsb+i_{n}}.

Sometimes, we will consider the maps
\varphi_{k}:H[2]^{\otimes k}\to C[2]
having source in the tensorial power instead of the symmetric algebra of the space, and write
\varphi_{k}(H[2]^{i_{1}},\dotsc,H[2]^{i_{n}})
with periods to separate arguments.

Since the cohomology is non-trivial in degrees
0,1,2,3
, we can always choose
k
elements in
H[2]
of various degrees such that the image by the map
\varphi_{k}
is in the non-trivial part of
C[2]
, it suffice to choose
k
numbers in
\text{\textlbrackdbl}-2,1\text{\textrbrackdbl}
such that their sum is again in
\text{\textlbrackdbl}-2,1\text{\textrbrackdbl}
. The situation here is more complicated that for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(3)
(see
[
Elc12
]
), since in this case we have terms
\varphi_{k}
, and hence terms
d_{k}
of all arities greater than two.

5.0
Order
k=0

For
k=0
and
y_{1}\in H[2]
, we have

0=\delta_{CE}(\varphi_{1}(y_{1}))

(5.1)

which express that
\varphi_{1}
is a section from the cohomology into the cocycles. We choose
\varphi_{1}=\operatorname{inc}
, the inclusion given by the choice of representatives of classes given in
Section 2
.

5.1
Order
k=1

For
k=1
and
y_{1}\bullet y_{2}\in\Sym^{2}(H[2])

\varphi_{1}(d_{2}(y_{1}\bullet y_{2}))=\delta_{CE}\varphi_{2}(y_{1}\bullet y_{2})+D(\varphi_{1}(y_{1})\bullet\varphi_{1}(y_{2})).

(5.2)

This equation shows that the inclusion
\varphi_{1}
is not a morphism of the graded Lie algebras
(H[2],d_{2})\to(C[2],D)
, since there is the coboundary
\delta_{CE}\varphi_{2}(y_{1}\bullet y_{2})
. We will explicit the term
\varphi_{2}
and higher terms to obtain an
L_{\infty}
-morphism between these two
L_{\infty}
-algebras.
To simplify the expressions, we will omit the inclusion
\varphi_{1}
in the remaining computations. The preceding equation rewrites

d_{2}(y_{1}\bullet y_{2})=\delta_{CE}\varphi_{2}(y_{1}\bullet y_{2})+D(y_{1}\bullet y_{2}).

Let

and also

\delta_{CE}\varphi_{2}(\varphi,\psi)=0

\varphi_{2}(\varphi,\psi)=0.

Let

(5.5)

Let
y_{1}=\varphi\in H^{0}
and

, with

and

(5.6)

Let

For

&+\varphi_{2}(d_{2}(y_{1}\bullet y_{2})\bullet y_{3})+(-1)^{|y_{2}||y_{3}|}\varphi_{2}(d_{2}(y_{1}\bullet y_{3})\bullet y_{2})+(-1)^{|y_{1}|(|y_{2}|+|y_{3}|)}\varphi_{2}(d_{2}(y_{2}\bullet y_{3})\bullet y_{1})\\

(5.14)

Recall that the maps
\delta_{CE}
,
D
and
d_{k}
are of degree
1
and the maps
\varphi_{k}
are of degree
0
. Thus, the left-hand side and the right-hand side of the equation are of degree one more than the sum of the degrees of the three elements
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}
.

We will keep the same notation as before for elements of various degrees in
H[2]
, without specifying further the types of the functions used (of
x
and
y
, of
z
only, of
x,y,z
).
Although we work with double-shifted spaces, we keep the original degree for the Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator, so

\delta_{CE}^{k}:C^{k}=C_{CE}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g})\to C^{k+1}=C_{CE}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},\Sym\mathfrak{g}).

By graded symmetry, it will be enough to study the cases
-2\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant k\leqslant 1
, where
i,j,k
are the degrees of
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\in H[2]
. We note
\omega\coloneqq\partial_{x}\wedge\partial_{y}\wedge\partial_{z}
.

Let
y_{1}=\varphi,y_{2}=\psi\in H^{0}
and
P\omega\in H^{3}
. The equation (
5.14
) rewrites

d_{3}(\varphi\bullet\psi\bullet P\omega)-2(Pz\varphi^{\prime}_{z}\psi^{\prime}_{z})_{1}=\delta_{CE}\varphi_{3}(\varphi\bullet\psi\bullet P\omega)+2P\varphi^{\prime}_{z}\psi^{\prime}_{z}

The elements of this equation are of degree
|\varphi|+|\psi|+|P\omega|+1=-2-2+1+1=-2
and since
\delta_{CE}
is of degree
+1
, the element
\varphi_{3}(\varphi\bullet\psi\bullet P\omega)\in C[2]^{-3}=C^{-1}
has image in
C^{0}
, so here
\delta_{CE}=\delta_{CE}^{-1}:C^{-1}\to C^{0}
. Of course,
C^{-1}=\{0\}
, so we get

\varphi_{3}(\varphi\bullet\psi\bullet P\omega)=0\quad\text{and}\quad d_{3}(\varphi\bullet\psi\bullet P\omega)=4P\varphi^{\prime}_{z}\psi^{\prime}_{z}.

Even if we had chosen other expressions for
\varphi_{2}(\varphi,P\omega)
and
\varphi_{2}(\varphi,X_{g}\wedge\partial_{z})
adding cocycles, there is no possibility to cancel the constant part in
z
coming from the function
P
. Therefore, this case alone shows that the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the Heisenberg algebra is not formal.

y_{1}=\varphi\in H^{0}

ON CERTAIN REGULAR NICELY DISTANCE–BALANCED GRAPHS
Blas Fernández, Štefko Miklavič, Safet Penjić

Abstract
A connected graph Γ \Gamma is called nicely distance–balanced , whenever there exists a positive integer γ = γ ⁡ ( Γ ) \gamma=\gamma(\Gamma) , such that for any two adjacent vertices u , v u,v of Γ \Gamma there are exactly γ \gamma vertices of Γ \Gamma which are closer to u u than to v v , and exactly γ \gamma vertices of Γ \Gamma which are closer to v v than to u u . Let d d denote the diameter of Γ \Gamma . It is known that d ≤ γ d\leq\gamma , and that nicely distance-balanced graphs with γ = d \gamma=d are precisely complete graphs and cycles of length 2 ​ d 2d or 2 ​ d + 1 2d+1 . In this paper we classify regular nicely distance-balanced graphs with γ = d + 1 \gamma=d+1 .

Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a finite, undirected, connected graph with diameter
d

V(\Gamma)
and

denote the vertex set and the edge set of

For

and

For a pair of adjacent vertices

W_{u,v}=\{x\in V(\Gamma)\mid d(x,u)<d(x,v)\}.

We say that

is

(DB for short) whenever
for an arbitrary pair of adjacent vertices

and

|W_{u,v}|=|W_{v,u}|.

The investigation of distance-balanced graphs was initiated in 1999 by Handa
[
8
]
, although the name

was coined nine years later by Jerebic, Klavžar and Rall
[
10
]
. The family of distance-balanced graphs is very rich and its study is interesting from various purely graph-theoretic aspects where one focuses on particular properties of such graphs such as symmetry
[
11
]
, connectivity
[
8
,
13
]
or complexity aspects of algorithms related to such graphs
[
6
]
. However, the balancedness property of these graphs makes them very appealing also in areas such as mathematical chemistry and communication networks. For instance, the investigation of such graphs is highly related to the well-studied Wiener index and Szeged index (see
[
2
,
9
,
10
]
) and they present very desirable models in various real-life situations related to (communication) networks
[
2
]
. It turns out that these graphs can be characterized by properties that at first glance do not seem to have much in common with the original definition from
[
10
]
. For example, in
[
3
]
it was shown that the distance-balanced graphs coincide with the
self-median
graphs, that is graphs for which the sum of the distances from a given vertex to all other vertices is independent of the chosen vertex. Other such examples are
equal opportunity graphs
(see
[
2
]
for the definition). In
[
2
]
it is shown that even order distance-balanced graphs are also equal to opportunity graphs.

The notion of nicely distance-balanced graphs appears quite naturally in the context of DB graphs. We say that
\Gamma
is
nicely distance–balanced
(NDB for short) whenever there exists a positive integer
\gamma=\gamma(\Gamma)
,
such that for an arbitrary pair of adjacent vertices
u
and
v

\Gamma

holds. Clearly, every NDB graph is also DB, but the opposite is not necessarily true. For example, if

is an odd positive integer, then the prism graph on

Assume now that
\Gamma
is NDB. Let us denote the diameter of

by

[
12
]

and NDB graphs with

or

. Therefore, we will concentrate our study to the class of regular graphs (recall that
\Gamma
is said to be regular with valency

). Our main result is the following theorem.

Let

is isomorphic to one of the following graphs:

the Möbius ladder graph on eight vertices (with

x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}
and
x_{1},x_{0},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}
, we get that

and

are adjacent, and that

. As

, vertex

must have a neighbour, which is at distance
3
from
x_{1}
and at distance
4
from
w
. As
x_{4},y_{3}
and
u_{1}
ar all at distance
3
from

, this implies that

has a neighbour

, which is not adjacent with

(and is therefore different from

). Note that since

is at distance
3
from
x_{0}
, it is adjacent with

. As

,
v
has a neighbour
z_{2}\neq z
in
D^{3}_{3}
. Pick now a vertex
t\in D^{4}_{4}
(observe that

has even order). If

is adjacent with

or with

, then

, a contradiction. If

is adjacent with

or

, then

, a contradiction. This finally proves that

.

Proposition 5.4

Let
\Gamma
denote a regular NDB graph with valency
k=3
, diameter
d=4
and
\gamma=\gamma(\Gamma)=d+1=5
. With the notation above,
\Gamma
is triangle-free.

Proof.

Pick an edge
xy\in E(\Gamma)
and let
D^{i}_{j}=D^{i}_{j}(x,y)
. If either
D^{4}_{3}
or
D^{3}_{4}
is nonempty, then Propositions
5.2
and
5.3
together with Lemma

imply that
|D^{1}_{2}|=|D^{2}_{1}|=2
. As
\Gamma
is
3
-regular, the set

is empty, and so

is not contained in any triangle.

Assume next that

. If the edge

is contained in a triangle, then

and

both contain at most one vertex, and so

and

could contain at most two vertices as
\Gamma
is
3
-regular. We thus have

. The result follows.

Proposition 5.5

Let
\Gamma
denote a regular NDB graph with valency
k=3
, diameter
d\geq 3
and
\gamma=\gamma(\Gamma)=d+1
. Then
d\neq 4
.

Proof.

Towards a contradiction suppose that

, and so
\gamma=5
. Assume the notation from the first paragraph of this subsection, and note that Propositions
5.2
and
5.3
imply that
\ell=2
. By Lemma

we have
|D^{2}_{1}|=2
. Let
u_{1},y_{2}
denote the vertices of
D^{2}_{1}
. Note that
D^{1}_{1}
is empty. We also observe that by Proposition
3.1
(i) either
u\in\Gamma(x_{3})
, or
x_{3}
has a neighbour in

. We consider these two cases separately.

Case 1:

u
and
x_{3}
are adjacent. Then
\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},u\}=W_{x_{3},x_{4}}
, and so neither
x_{2}
nor
u
have neighbours in
D^{2}_{1}
. Since
\Gamma
is triangle-free, there exists
w\in\Gamma(x_{2})\cap D^{2}_{2}
, and
w
has a neighbour in
D^{2}_{1}
(by definition of the set

). We may assume without loss of generality that

. Note that

, and so

as well, as otherwise

is not balanced. It follows that there exists a common neighbour
z
of
w
and
x_{4}
, and it is clear that

.

Similarly we find that
u
has a neighbour
w_{1}\in D^{2}_{2}
, and as

, we have that
w_{1}\neq w
. Note that
\{x_{2},x_{1},x_{0},w,y_{2}\}=W_{x_{2},x_{3}}
, and so
d(x_{3},u_{1})=3
(otherwise

, a contradiction). Note however that

is only possible if
w_{1}
and
u_{1}
are adjacent. A similar argument as above shows that
w_{1}
and
x_{4}
must have a common neighbour
z_{1}\in D^{3}_{3}
. If
z_{1}=z
, then
\{z,w,w_{1},y_{2},u_{1},x_{0}\}\subseteq W_{z,x_{4}}
, a contradiction. Therefore
z_{1}\neq z
, and it is now clear that
D^{2}_{2}=\{w,w_{1}\}
,
D^{3}_{3}=\{z,z_{1}\}
. If there exists
t\in D^{4}_{4}
, then
t
is adjacent to either
z
or
z_{1}
, but none of these two possible edges is balanced, and so
D^{4}_{4}=\emptyset
. If
z
(
z_{1}
, respectively) has a neighbour in
D^{4}_{3}
, then
x_{2}x_{1}
(
ux_{1}
, respectively) is not balanced, a contradiction. As
\Gamma
is triangle-free,
z
and
z_{1}
both have a neighbour in
D^{3}_{2}
. Assume now for a moment that there exists a vertex
y_{4}\in D^{4}_{3}
. In this case
\gamma=5
forces that there is a unique vertex in
D^{3}_{2}
, which is therefore adjacent to both
z
and
z_{1}
, to
y_{4}
and to at least one of
y_{2},u_{1}
, contradicting

. It follows that
D^{4}_{3}=\emptyset
. Let us denote the neighbours of
z
and
z_{1}
in
D^{3}_{2}
by
v
and
v_{1}
respectively. Note that as
zx_{4}
and
z_{1}x_{4}
are balanced, we have that
W_{z,x_{4}}=\{z,w,v,y_{2},x_{0}\}
and
W_{z_{1},x_{4}}=\{z_{1},w_{1},v_{1},u_{2},x_{0}\}
. It follows that
v
and
v_{1}
must be adjacent to
y_{2}
and
u_{1}
, respectively, and so

. As

, also
v
and
v_{1}
are adjacent. It is now easy to see that
\Gamma
is not NDB with
\gamma=5
(for example, edge
x_{1}u
is not balanced). This shows that
u
and
x_{3}
are not adjacent.

Case 2:

x_{3}
has a neighbour
w
in
D^{3}_{3}
. As
\Gamma
is triangle-free,
x_{2}
has a neighbour
z
in
D^{2}_{1}\cup D^{2}_{2}
, and
w\not\sim x_{4}
. If

, then

, a contradiction. This yields that

. If

, then again

, a contradiction. Therefore,

and
x_{4}
have a common neighbour

, and
w_{1}\neq w
as

. But now
\{x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},z,w,w_{1}\}\subseteq W_{x_{2},x_{1}}
, a contradiction. This finishes the proof.

5.2
Case
d=3

In this subsection we consider the case
d=3
. We start with the following proposition.

Proposition 5.6

Let
\Gamma
denote a regular NDB graph with valency
k=3
, diameter
d=3
and
\gamma=4
. Then for every edge
x_{0}x_{1}
of
\Gamma
we have that
|D^{1}_{2}(x_{1},x_{0})|=|D^{2}_{1}(x_{1},x_{0})|=2
.

Proof.

Pick an edge
x_{0}x_{1}
of
\Gamma
and let
D^{i}_{j}=D^{i}_{j}(x_{1},x_{0})
. Observe first that that
|D^{1}_{2}|\leq 2
as
k=3
. By Proposition
4.4
we have that
D^{2}_{3}\neq\emptyset
, and so pick
x_{3}\in D^{2}_{3}
. Note that
x_{1}
and
x_{3}
have a common neighbour
x_{2}\in D^{1}_{2}
. Assume to the contrary that
|D^{1}_{2}|=1
, and so
|D^{2}_{3}|=2
,
|D^{1}_{1}|=1=|D^{2}_{1}|
. Let us denote the unique vertex of
D^{2}_{1}
by
y_{2}
(note that
y_{2}
has two neighbors, say
y_{3}
and
u_{1}
in
D^{3}_{2}
), the unique vertex of
D^{1}_{1}
by
w
, and the unique vertex of
D^{2}_{3}\setminus\{x_{3}\}
by
u
(note that
\Gamma(x_{2})=\{x_{1},x_{3},u\}
). Note that
w
has a neighbour
v
in
D^{2}_{2}
, and that
D^{2}_{2}=\{v\}
.

Assume first that
u
and
x_{3}
are not adjacent. Then
W_{x_{2},x_{3}}=\{x_{2},u,x_{1},x_{0}\}
, and so
w
is at distance
2
from
x_{3}
(otherwise
w\in W_{x_{2},x_{3}}
). It follows that
x_{3}
is adjacent with
v
. Similarly we show that
u
is adjacent with
v
. As none of the neighbours of
v
is contained in
D^{3}_{3}
, every vertex from
D^{3}_{3}
must be adjacent to either
u
or
x_{3}
, and so
D_{3}^{3}\cup\{x_{2},x_{3},u\}\subseteq W_{x_{2},x_{1}}
. It follows that
|D^{3}_{3}|\leq 1
. As
\Gamma
is a cubic graph, it must have an even order, which gives us
D^{3}_{3}=\emptyset
. This shows that both
u
and
x_{3}
have a neighbour in
D^{3}_{2}
. But now
\{y_{2},y_{3},u_{1},x_{3},u\}\cup D^{3}_{2}\subseteq W_{y_{2},x_{0}}
, a contradiction.

Therefore,
u
and
x_{3}
must be adjacent, and they have a common neighbour
x_{2}
. Let
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
denote the third neighbour of
u
and
x_{3}
, respectively. If
z_{1}=z_{2}
then
ux_{3}
is not balanced, and so we have that
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
. Furthermore, as
\{x_{2},x_{3},u\}\subseteq W_{x_{2},x_{1}}
, not both of

are contained in

. Therefore, either
z_{1}
or
z_{2}
is equal to

. Without loss of generality assume that

. But then

forces
W_{x_{2},u}=\{x_{2},x_{1},x_{0}\}
, a contradiction. This shows that
|D^{1}_{2}|=2
, and by Lemma

also
|D^{2}_{1}|=2
.

Corollary 5.7

Let
\Gamma
denote a regular NDB graph with valency
k=3
, diameter
d=3
and
\gamma=4
. Then
\Gamma
is triangle-free and
D^{3}_{3}(x,y)=\emptyset
for every edge
xy
of
\Gamma

Proof.

Pick an arbitrary edge
xy
of
\Gamma
and let
D^{i}_{j}=D^{i}_{j}(x,y)
. By Proposition
4.4
we get that the sets
D^{1}_{2}
,
D^{2}_{1}
,
D^{2}_{3}
and
D^{3}_{2}
are all nonempty. Furthermore, by Proposition
5.6
and Lemma

we have that
|D^{1}_{2}|=|D^{2}_{1}|=2
and
|D^{3}_{2}|=|D^{2}_{3}|=1
(recall that

). Since

, it follows that

. This shows that

is triangle-free.

We next assert the set

is empty. Suppose to the contrary there exists

and let

denote a neighbour of

. Assume first that

. Since

, there exist vertices

and

which are neighbours of

. We thus have

, contradicting

. This shows that

. Therefore
z
is adjacent to both vertices which are in
D^{3}_{2}
and
D^{2}_{3}
. As

has three neighbours, none of which is in

, and as

, it follows that

has a neighbour

. But by the same argument as above,

must be adjacent to both vertices in
D^{3}_{2}
and

, contradicting the fact that

is triangle-free.

Theorem 5.8

Let
\Gamma
denote a regular NDB graph with valency

, diameter

. Then

By Theorem

, it is shown that the number of connected

, but only 5 of them have diameter

6
Case

Let

. Recall that by Theorem

. In this section we first show that case

Lemma 6.1

Let
\Gamma
denote a regular NDB graph with valency
k=4
, diameter
d=4
and
\gamma=\gamma(\Gamma)=d+1
. Pick vertices
x_{0},x_{4}
of
\Gamma
such that

and
x_{4}
. Let

. Moreover,

and

.

Assume first that
\ell=4
. By Proposition
3.1
(i), vertex
x_{3}
has a neighbour
z
in
D_{3}^{3}
. Now

has two neighbours in

(ii).

Assume now that

. By Proposition
3.1
(i)
x_{3}
does not have neighbours in

, and so by Proposition
3.2
(ii) we get that
x_{3}
and

are adjacent, and that
x_{3}
has a neighbour
z
in

. By Proposition
3.2
(ii) vertex
x_{2}
has no neighbours in

, and so

has a neighbour

in

is adjacent to both

, and so

has no neighbours in

. It follows that

has a neighbour in

, and by Proposition
3.2
(ii), this neighbour is

. But now the edge

is not balanced, a contradiction.

This shows that
\ell=2
. By Proposition
3.1
(i), vertex
x_{3}
has either one or two neighbours in

. If
x_{3}
has two neighbours in

(i) vertex

has no neighbours in

is adjacent to the unique vertex

. But now we have that

has exactly one neighbour in

(i) vertex

has no neighbours in

. Consequently

, and so

and

.

and

have exactly one neighbour in

\Gamma
denote a regular NDB graph with valency

, diameter

.

. Pick vertices

, and so

|D^{1}_{1}|=2

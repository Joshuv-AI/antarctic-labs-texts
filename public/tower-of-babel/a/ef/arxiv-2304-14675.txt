Integral curves and Jacobian Conjecture
Jean-Yves Charbonnel

Abstract
. In this note, we are interested in the Jacobian Conjecture. Following the results of L.M. Dru z ˙ \dot{\rm z} kowski, we consider some vector fields depending on a certain étale polynomial map. From results of semialgebraic geometry with the consideration of some integral curves of these vector fields, we deduce that an étale polynomial endomorphism is a polynomial automorphism.

1.
Introduction

In this note, we are interested in the jacobian conjecture, that is: for
n
positive
integer, an étale polynomial endomorphism of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is a polynomial automorphism.
Some results are known for a long time. For example, an étale polynomial endomorphism
is an automorphism if it is proper or birational
[
Bass 82
, Theorem 2.1]
. According to
the results of L.M. Dru
\dot{\rm z}
kowski, it is sufficient to prove that the following
conjecture is true.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
{F}_{1}{,\ldots,\hskip 1.7624pt}{F}_{n}
in the dual of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
Denote by
\Phi
the polynomial endomorphism of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
:

1.1.
Sketch of proof

Let
n
be a positive integer and
{F}_{1}{,\ldots,\hskip 1.7624pt}{F}_{n},\Phi
as in
Conjecture
1.1
. Suppose that
\Phi
is étale. For
x
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, let

be the vector field on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,

y\longmapsto\Phi^{\prime}(y)^{-1}(x)

with
\Phi^{\prime}(y)
the differential of
\Phi
at

\gamma
is an integral curve
of
X_{x}
with initial datum
\gamma(0)=y

t
in the interval of
definition of
\gamma

\Phi\raisebox{0.85358pt}{\scriptsize$\circ$}\gamma(t)=\Phi(y)+tx.

The key step of the proof is the following proposition:

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
x
be in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
\gamma_{x}
the maximal integral curve of
X_{x}
with
initial datum
\gamma_{x}(0)=0
. Then
{\mathbb{R}}
is the interval of definition of

\gamma_{x}

For
x
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, let
\varphi_{x}
be the map from
{\mathbb{C}}
to
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
such that

\varphi_{x}(0):=0\hskip 11.74988pt\text{and}\hskip 11.74988pt\varphi_{x}(t):=\gamma_{ux}(|t|)\hskip 11.74988pt\text{with}\hskip 11.74988ptu:=\frac{t}{|t|},

for all

in
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. The second step is the following result:

Proposition 1.3
.

For all
x
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,
\varphi_{x}
is a polynomial map such that

\Phi\raisebox{0.85358pt}{\scriptsize$\circ$}\varphi_{x}(t)=tx
for all

in
{\mathbb{C}}
.

We deduce from this proposition that the map

). Let

is stable under finite union and finite intersection. For

For

2.1.

According to

For

For

.

For some positive integer

Proof.

,

Theorem 7.5
.

The polynomial endomorphism

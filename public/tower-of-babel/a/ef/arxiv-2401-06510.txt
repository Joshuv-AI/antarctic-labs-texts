Every spectrum is the K-theory of a stable ∞ \infty -category
Maxime Ramzi, Vladimir Sosnilo, Christoph Winges

Abstract
. We prove that any spectrum is equivalent to the nonconnective K-theory of a stable ∞ \infty -category. We use these results to construct a stable ∞ \infty -category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} with a bounded t-structure such that K ⁡ ( 𝒞 ) \operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}) is not equivalent to K ⁡ ( 𝒞 ♡ ) \operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}) , disproving a conjecture of Antieau, Gepner, and Heller.



Introduction

0.1.

Throughout the history of mathematics it has been observed that important numerical invariants of mathematical objects admit
categorifications
. For example, the Euler characteristics and the Betti
numbers of a space are categorified by the homology, the Hilbert polynomial of a scheme is categorified by the coherent
cohomology, and various knot-theoretic invariants are categorified by Khovanov homology
[
Kho00
]
and Knot-Floer
homology
[
OS04
]
.
More precisely, categorifying an invariant valued in some abelian group
A
means finding a stable
\infty
-category

\mathcal{C}_{A}
with
\operatorname{K}_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{A})\simeq A
such that the given invariant lifts to functor valued in
\mathcal{C}_{A}
. In this situation,
\mathcal{C}_{A}
is thought of as a categorification of
A
.
In
[
Kho16
]
Mikhail Khovanov asked several questions about the extent to which categorification is
possible. In particular, he asked whether it is possible to categorify the rational numbers, that is, to find a stable

\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{Q}}
with
\operatorname{K}_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{Q}})\simeq\mathbf{Q}
. On the level of abelian groups, this question was eventually answered in the paper

[
BGH+19
]
. More generally, for a given stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
and a set of primes
S\subset\mathbf{Z}
they
construct a stable
\infty
-category
S^{-1}\mathcal{C}
satisfying

\operatorname{K}(S^{-1}\mathcal{C})\simeq S^{-1}\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}).

In this paper we show that every spectrum admits a categorification.

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.1
)
.

For every spectrum
M
there exists a small idempotent complete stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}_{M}
such that

\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}_{M})\simeq M.

Here
\operatorname{K}
denotes the nonconnective K-theory spectrum. Moreover, the construction of
\mathcal{C}_{M}
is functorial in
M
.

One could also view this result as a variant of Thomason’s theorem that says that every connective spectrum can be
recovered as the group completion of the underlying monoid of a symmetric monoidal 1-category

[
Tho95
]
.

To prove this theorem, we first show that several natural constructions one may perform
with the K-theory spectrum can be naturally lifted to constructions on the categorical level. These include:

(1)

suspensions (Proposition
3.7
);

(2)

loops (Theorem
3.5
);

(3)

cofibers of maps induced by functors (Proposition
3.7
).

The rest of the proof uses the Dundas–McCarthy formula for computing

\mathrm{THH}(\mathbf{S};M)\simeq M
(Theorem
2.8
) and carefully performs all the constructions involved in it at the
categorical level.

0.2.

One of the most fundamental results about algebraic K-theory is the
theorem of the heart
.
In Barwick’s formulation
[
Bar15
]
, it says that for any small stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
with a bounded t-structure, there is a canonical equivalence

\operatorname{K}^{\mathrm{cn}}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}))\simeq\operatorname{K}^{\mathrm{cn}}(\mathcal{C}).

Here
\operatorname{K}^{\mathrm{cn}}
stands for connective K-theory. Antieau, Gepner, and Heller observed that this equivalence
can be extended to nonconnective K-theory, as long as the heart
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
is a noetherian abelian
category
[
AGH19
, Theorem 1.3]
. Their Conjecture C states that the
nonconnective theorem of the heart
holds in general, i.e. the map

u:\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}))\to\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C})

is always an equivalence, without any assumptions on the heart. This was also asked by
Burklund and Levy
[
BL23
, Question 1.4]
. As the main application of our results, we show that it is not
true.

Theorem B

(
5.4.1
)
.

The nonconnective theorem of the heart is false: there exists a small stable
\infty
-category with a bounded t-structure such that the map
u
is not an equivalence.

The construction of a counterexample is
surprisingly easy, once we have Theorem
A
. We start
by building a stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
whose K-theory has sufficiently nontrivial
chromatic
behaviour.
Given such an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
we consider a new small stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{A}c(\mathcal{C})
that admits a
bounded t-structure
1
1

1

This construction is an adaptation of the construction of Neeman

[
Nee21
, Section 1]
to the more
general setting.
(see Section
5
), and whose K-theory spectrum happens to be chromatically similar to
\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C})
.
This immediately yields a counterexample, as the chromatic behaviour of
\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}))
is always very
simple.

Using the same example we also show that the nonconnective K-theory of a stable

\infty
-category with a bounded t-structure is not
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-invariant (Remark
5.5
).

1.
Reminder on localizing invariants

In our exposition we mostly follow the terminology of
[
BGT13
]
and
[
Hoy17
]
. Note, however,
that we consider a
slightly more general notion of a localizing invariant (as in
[
LT19
]
or
[
BKRS22
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a diagram in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\infty}

\mathcal{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}\mathcal{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}

is a
Karoubi–Verdier sequence
if

f
is fully faithful, the composite
g\circ f
is trivial and the induced functor

\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{C}

is an equivalence up to idempotent completion.

We say that it is a
Verdier sequence
if
g
is essentially
surjective and the essential image of
f
is closed under retracts.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a stable
\infty
-category. A functor

E:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{E}

is said to be a
localizing invariant
if for any Karoubi-Verdier sequence
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
in

\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
the sequence

E(\mathcal{A})\to E(\mathcal{B})\to E(\mathcal{C})

is a fiber sequence.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a cocomplete stable
\infty
-category. We say that a functor

E:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{E}

is
finitary
if it preserves filtered colimits. For a cocomplete stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}
we denote the subcategory of
\operatorname{Fun}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},\mathcal{D})
consisting of finitary localizing invariants
by
\operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{fin}}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},\mathcal{D})
.

Example 1.4
.

The nonconnective K-theory functor
\operatorname{K}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Spt}
is a finitary localizing invariant.

Theorem 1.5

(Blumberg, Gepner & Tabuada
[
BGT13
, Theorem 8.7]
)
.

There exists a functor

\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}

initial among finitary localizing invariants with values in a presentable stable
\infty
-category.

The
\infty
-category
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is naturally presentably symmetric monoidal and the functor

\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}
admits a symmetric monoidal enhancement upon considering the symmetric monoidal
structure of
[
BZFN10
, 4.1.2]
on
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
(see also
[
BGT13
, Section 3.1]
).
This structure allows us to identify the nonconnective K-theory naturally in this setting:

Theorem 1.6

(Blumberg, Gepner & Tabuada
[
BGT13
, Theorem 9.8]
)
.

The unit
\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is compact and for any stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
there is a natural
equivalence

\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbf{1},\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{C}))\simeq\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}).

The following easy corollary of Theorem
1.6
will come in handy later in the paper.

Corollary 1.7
.

Any natural equivalence
\operatorname{K}\to\operatorname{K}
is either homotopic to
\mathrm{id}
or
-\mathrm{id}
.

Proof.

By Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
we have an equivalence of rings

\pi_{0}\mathrm{Nat}(\operatorname{K},\operatorname{K})\simeq\pi_{0}\operatorname{K}(\mathbf{S})\simeq\mathbf{Z}.

The result follows since the only units on the right-hand side are 1 and -1.
∎

Reminder on the Dundas–McCarthy theorem

In this section, we remind the reader of a special case of a nonconnective variant of the Dundas–McCarthy theorem. To state this result, we need to introduce twisted endomorphism
\infty
-categories.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a small stable
\infty
-category. By a
\mathcal{C}
-
bimodule
we simply mean an exact functor
\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C}).

Remark 2.2
.

A
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule
T
gives rise to a colimit-preserving functor

T:\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})\to\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C}).

We often abuse the notation and denote the latter by the same letter.

Example 2.3
.

If
M

R

R

M\otimes_{R}-:\mathbf{Perf}_{R}\to\operatorname{Ind}(\mathbf{Perf}_{R})
defines
a
\mathbf{Perf}_{R}

Definition 2.4

(
[
NS18
, Definition II.1.4]

.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a small stable
\infty
-category, and
T:\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})
a
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule.
The
\infty
-category

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})\times\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(s,t)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\text{\char 136\relax},T)}

Remark 2.5

When

3.
Categorification of spectra

The goal of this section is proving the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
M
be a spectrum. Then there exists a small idempotent complete stable
\infty
-category

\mathcal{C}_{M}
such that

\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}_{M})\simeq M.

Moreover, the assignment
M\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{M}
is functorial.

3.2.
Reminder on the Calkin construction

For a small stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
we denote by

\mathrm{Calk}(\mathcal{C})
the
\omega_{1}
-small Calkin category of
\mathcal{C}
, i.e. the idempotent completion of the Verdier quotient of the Yoneda embedding

3.3.
Categorifying suspensions and loops

Observe that
\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})^{\omega_{1}}
admits an Eilenberg swindle and, in particular,

\operatorname{K}(\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})^{\omega_{1}})\simeq 0

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a small idempotent complete stable
\infty

\operatorname{K}(\mathrm{Calk}(\mathcal{C}))\simeq\Sigma\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}).

Theorem 3.5

There exists a functor

\operatorname{K}(\Gamma\mathcal{C})\simeq\Omega\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}).

4.
Bounded t-structures and the theorem of the heart

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category.
A pair of full subcategories
(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0},\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant 0})
is said to be a
t-structure
if the following
axioms hold:

(1)

\Sigma\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}
and
\Omega\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant 0}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant 0}
;

(2)

for
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}
and
y\in\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant 0}
we have
\pi_{0}\Maps_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma x,y)=0
;

(3)

for
x\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a fiber sequence

x_{\geqslant 1}\to x\to x_{\leqslant 0}

such that
x_{\geqslant 1}\in\Sigma\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}
and
x_{\leqslant 0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant 0}
.

The
heart
of a t-structure on
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}:=\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant 0}
.

An exact functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
of stable
\infty
-categories equipped with t-structures is called t-exact
if
F(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0})\subset\mathcal{D}_{\geqslant 0}
and
F(\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant 0})\subset\mathcal{D}_{\leqslant 0}
.

We set
\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant n}=\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant n}=\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant 0}
.

A t-structure on
\mathcal{C}
is called bounded if

\mathcal{C}=\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mathbf{N}}(\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant-n}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant n})
.

4.1.1.
Basic properties

(1)

It formally follows from the definition that the choice of a fiber sequence in (3) is unique up to
equivalence and is functorial in
x
.

(2)

The subcategories
\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}
determine each other as the corresponding
categorical orthogonals, that is, we have:

\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}=\{x\in\mathcal{C}:\pi_{0}\Maps_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma x,y)=0\text{ for any }y\in\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant 0}\},

\mathcal{C}_{\leqslant 0}=\{x\in\mathcal{C}:\pi_{0}\Maps_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma y,x)=0\text{ for any }y\in\mathcal{C}_{\geqslant 0}\}.

(3)

The heart of a t-structure is always an abelian category.

(4)

A stable
\infty
-category with a bounded t-structure is automatically idempotent complete

[
AGH19
, Corollary 2.14]
.

Example 4.2
.

The
\infty
-category of spectra admits a t-structure with
\mathrm{Spt}_{\geqslant 0}
being the subcategory
of connective spectra and
\mathrm{Spt}_{\leqslant 0}
the subcategory of coconnective spectra. The fact that
the axiom (3) is satisfied reflects
the existence of Postnikov towers of spectra. This t-structure
is not
bounded. The heart of this
t-structure consists of Eilenberg–MacLane spectra of abelian groups, and is equivalent to
\mathcal{A}b
.

Example 4.3
.

Generalizing the previous example, for a connective ring spectrum
R
,
the
\infty
-category of right
R
-module spectra
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}
admits a t-structure with
(\mathrm{Mod}_{R})_{\geqslant 0}

being the subcategory of connective
R
-module spectra.

Example 4.4
.

Given a small abelian category
A
, the bounded derived category
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)
admits a bounded t-structure
with
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)_{\geqslant 0}
consisting of complexes
x
with
H_{i}(x)=0
for
i<0
and similarly for

\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)_{\leqslant 0}
. The heart of this t-structure is equivalent to
A
.

The following result shows that the bounded t-structure of Example
4.4
is in some sense
a universal one.

Proposition 4.5

(
[
AGH19
, Proposition 3.26]
)
.

For any small stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
equipped with a bounded t-structure,
there is a natural t-exact functor

\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit})\to\mathcal{C}

that induces an equivalence on the hearts of the t-structures.

4.6.
K-theoretic properties of t-structures

Proposition
4.5
yields a canonical map

\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}))\to\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C})

for any small stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
endowed with a bounded t-structure.
In
[
AGH19
]
Antieau, Gepner, and Heller formulated the following conjectures:

Conjecture 4.7

(Conjecture B)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a small stable
\infty
-category equipped with a bounded t-structure.
Then the K-theory spectrum of
\mathcal{C}
is connective.

Conjecture 4.8

(Conjecture C)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a small stable
\infty
-category equipped with a bounded t-structure.
Then the map

\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}))\to\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C})

is an equivalence of spectra.

Remark 4.9
.

Both conjectures were proved in
[
AGH19
]
for t-structures whose heart is
a

abelian category. Besides, they showed that
\pi_{-1}\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C})=0
without any extra

[

a version of

It is also worth pointing out that Conjecture

version of K-theory. This is known as the theorem of the heart and was
proved by Clark Barwick
[

and a triangulated version of it had been proved even before

,
Nee99
,

]

In particular, Conjecture

would follow from Conjecture

.

However, Conjecture

in its full generality turned out to be false.
In
[

]
Neeman constructed an explicit stable

-category

with a t-structure such that

\pi_{-2}\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{A})
is not zero. Until now Conjecture

has remained open.

4.10.

In this paper we show that Conjecture

To do this, we will need to investigate some general

Let

The stable

Corollary 4.16

Let

5.

Recall from (

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\mathrm{fin}}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{padd}}_{\infty}}

\infty

For any stable

\operatorname{Fun}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Fun}_{\mathrm{ex}}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fin}},\mathcal{D})\simeq\operatorname{Fun}_{\times}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}).

Thus

For any small stable
\infty

Proof.

Following
[

\mathcal{A}
we denote by

the smallest subcategory

and closed under finite colimits. This is a prestable

Now consider the case

Moreover, the induced functor

By

\mathcal{D}^{\omega}(\mathcal{C};H)
of a stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
associated with an
adapted

homology theory
H
on
\mathcal{C}
. It is a prestable
\infty
-category with finite limits.
They construct a left and right exact functor of prestable
\infty
-categories

A_{\infty}^{\omega}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{D}^{\omega}(\mathcal{C};H)

which is a localization and whose kernel consists of bounded objects (see
[
PP23
, Theorem 5.34(4)]
). After applying
(-)^{\mathrm{fin}}
this yields a t-exact Verdier localization functor such
that the
kernel consists of t-bounded objects.
In case
H
is the trivial homology theory valued in the trivial abelian category, we have
\mathcal{D}^{\omega}(\mathcal{C};H)=\mathcal{C}
and deduce the claim of Theorem
5.1
.

Now we can finally show Theorem
B
.

5.4.1.
Construction of the counterexample

For any stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
we have a fiber sequence

\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{A}c(\mathcal{C}))\to\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fin}})\to\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}).

For any spectrum
M
we can apply this to
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{M}
from Theorem
3.1
. We now have
a fiber sequence

\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{A}c(\mathcal{C}))\to\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fin}})\to M.

Theorem
4.17
says that the spectrum
\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fin}})
is
\operatorname{K}(\mathbf{Z})
-local.
Thus, for any
M
that is not
\operatorname{K}(\mathbf{Z})
-local,
\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{A}c(\mathcal{C}))
is not
\operatorname{K}(\mathbf{Z})
-local.
Such spectra exist: we can pick
M=K(2)
by Example
4.14
.
Since
\mathcal{A}c(\mathcal{C}_{M})
is endowed with a bounded t-structure,
Conjecture
4.8
fails by Corollary
4.16
.

Remark 5.5
.

The formula

\mathcal{C}\mapsto\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathbf{Perf}_{\mathbf{S}[\Delta^{\bullet}]})

defines a localizing invariant
\mathrm{KH}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Spt}
(see
[
Sos
, 1.14.2]
and also

[
LT19
, Definition 3.13]
).
By
[
CK
, Corollary 5.1.9]
(see also
[
Kha19
, Theorem A]
) we have

\mathrm{KH}(\mathbf{S})\simeq\mathrm{KH}(\mathbf{Z})\simeq\mathrm{K}(\mathbf{Z}),

which gives a
\mathrm{K}(\mathbf{Z})
-module structure on
\mathrm{KH}(\mathcal{C})
for any
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
.
Now the construction in (
5.4.1
) shows that there exists a stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
endowed
with a bounded t-structure such that
\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{C})
is not equivalent to
\mathrm{KH}(\mathcal{C})
.
In particular, the maps in the colimit defining
\mathrm{KH}
are not all equivalences and so, there exists an
integer
n>0
such that the map

\mathrm{K}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{K}(\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathbf{Perf}_{\mathbf{S}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]})

is
not
an equivalence. This shows that the
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-invariance in the sense of

[
BL23
, Theorem 1.6]
does not hold for nonconnective algebraic K-theory.

Sublinearly Morse Boundary of CAT ⁡ ( 0 ) \operatorname{CAT}(0) admissible groups
Hoang Thanh Nguyen, Yulan Qing

Abstract
. We show that if G G is an admissible group acting geometrically on a CAT ⁡ ( 0 ) \operatorname{CAT}(0) space X X , then G G is a hierarchically hyperbolic space and its κ \kappa -Morse boundary ( ∂ κ G , ν ) (\partial_{\kappa}G,\nu) is a model for the Poisson boundary of ( G , μ ) (G,\mu) where ν \nu is the hitting measure associated to the random walk driven by μ \mu ( with mild assumptions of μ \mu ).

1.
Introduction

Sublinearly Morse boundaries are recently constructed for all finitely generated groups
[
QRT19
,
QRT20
]
. It is a metrizable topological space that is a group invariant. Similar Gromov boundary of hyperbolic spaces, sublinearly Morse boundaries are particularly illuminating in revealing features of groups that contains hyperbolic-like features
[
IZ21
,
MQZ20
,
QZ21
,
Zal20
]
. One of these feature centers around asymptotic behavior of random walk on the associated groups. In several important classes of groups, such right-angled Artin groups, relative hyperbolic groups, and the mapping class groups of surfaces of finite type, an appropriately chosen sublinear function yields a sublinearly Morse boundary that serves as a topological model for the Poisson boundaries(with mild assumptions) of the group.

In this paper we continue to prove the connection between a geometric boundary and random walk boundary for a new class of groups:
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
admissible groups.

\operatorname{CAT}(0)
admissible groups is a particular type of graph of groups that generalizes graph manifolds. A compact, orientable, irreducible 3-manifold with empty or tori boundary is called a graph manifold if it is obtained by gluing finitely many Seifert manifolds with orientable hyperbolic base orbifolds where the gluing maps between the Seifert components do not identify (unoriented) Seifert fibers up to homotopy.
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
admissible group was first introduced by Croke-Kleiner
[
CK02
]
as key counterexamples to the nice properties of Gromov boundary when the hyperbolicity assumption is dropped. This property is further studied in
[
Qin16
]
.

Roughly speaking, a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
admissible group models the JSJ structure of nonpositively curved graph manifolds where the Seifert fibration is replaced by the following central extension of a general hyperbolic group:

(1)

1\to Z(G_{v})=\mathbb{Z}\to G_{v}\to H_{v}\to 1

In this paper, we also refer to
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
admissible groups as CKA groups (CKA stands for Croke-Kleiner admissible). This class of groups serves as a simple algebraic means to produce interesting groups from an arbitrary finite collection of
n

\operatorname{CAT}(0)
hyperbolic groups.

A basic example
. Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be two torsion-free hyperbolic groups which act geometrically on
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
spaces
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
respectively. Then
G_{i}=H_{i}\times\langle t_{i}\rangle
(with
i=1,2
) acts geometrically on the
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space
Y_{i}=X_{i}\times\mathbb{R}
. Any primitive hyperbolic element
h_{i}
in
H_{i}
gives a totally geodesic torus
T_{i}
in the quotient space
Y_{i}/G_{i}
with basis
([h_{i}],[t_{i}])
. We rescale
Y_{i}
so that the lengths of
[h_{1}]
and of
[t_{2}]
are the same, and the lengths of
[t_{1}]
and
[h_{2}]
are the same. Let
f\colon T_{1}\to T_{2}
be an isometry that flips the
\mathbb{Z}
-axis with the axis of an loxodromic element in
H_{i}
. Let
M
be the space obtained by gluing
Y_{1}
to
Y_{2}
by the isometry

. One can show that there exists a metric on
M
such that with respect to this metric,
M
is a locally
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space (see e.g.
[
BH99
, Proposition 11.6]
). By the Cartan-Hadamard Theorem, then the universal cover
\widetilde{M}
with the induced length metric from

is a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space, and thus
\pi_{1}(M)
is a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
admissible group and
\pi_{1}(M)\curvearrowright\widetilde{M}
is a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
action.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
admissible group. The following holds.

(1)

G
is a hierarchically hyperbolic space with respect to a word metric.

(2)

Let
\mu
be a finitely supported, non-elementary probability measure on
G
. Then for
\kappa(t)=\log^{p}(t)
, where
p
is the complexity of the HHS. Then almost every sample path
(w_{n})
converges to a point in
\partial_{\kappa}G
; and the
\kappa
-Morse boundary
(\partial_{\kappa}G,\nu)
is a model for the Poisson boundary of
(G,\mu)
where
\nu
is the hitting measure associated to the random walk driven by
\mu
.

The key step to show Theorem
A
involves proving that
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
admissible groups acts geometrically on hierarchically hyperbolic spaces. Hierarchically hyperbolic spaces are introduced axiomatically, modeling the Masur–Minsky deconstruction of mapping class groups
[
MM00
]
. A hierarchically hyperbolic space (HHS) consists of a metric space
X
together with a partially ordered set of
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces. There are projections maps from
X
to each of the hyperbolic spaces and from the hyperbolic spaces to each other. In some sense, this set of hyperbolic spaces form a tree-like metric that can be used to measure distances in
X
. Many interesting groups are quasi-isometric to hierarchically hyperbolic spaces, examples of classes of groups include but are not limited to:

•

hyperbolic groups and relative hyperbolic groups

•

Mapping class groups of orientable surfaces of finite type.

•

Fundamental groups of compact three-manifolds with no Nil or Sol in their prime decomposition.

•

Groups that act properly and cocompactly on a special
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
cube complex, and more
generally any cubical group which admits a factor system.

It is established in
[
BHS19
]
that all graph manifolds are a hierarchically hyperbolic space. To show this, we have Kapovich-Leeb
[
KL98
]
to show that all graph manifolds are quasi-isometric to flip graph manifolds, and the combination theorem of
[
BHS19
]
shows that the fundamental group of a flip graph manifold is HHS. When we lose the manifold structure and instead we look at graph of groups and in the setting of
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
admissible groups, it is unknown whether all fundamental group of graph of hyperbolic-by-Z groups are quasi-isometric to the ones whose gluing map is a flip isometry(here flip roughly means the same as in the basic example). In Theorem
A
, we drop both the assumption of flip and also the assumption of the space being a manifold.

Lastly, an intermediate step in the proof of Theorem
A
depends on the following fact:

Theorem B
.

Let
\mu
be a non-elementary, finitely supported measure on
G
and assume that
G
acts geometrically on an hierarchically hyperbolic space. Then for
\kappa(r):=\log^{p}(r)
, where
p
is the complexity of the hierarchy, the
\kappa
-Morse boundary is a topological model for the Poisson boundary of
(G,\mu)
.

The claim is proven for all mapping class groups and is remarked in
[
QRT20
]
. We include the proof for hierarchically hyperbolic spaces in the appendix of this paper.

4.1.
Convergence to the
\kappa
-Morse boundary

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
. We say that
\mu
is

if the semigroup generated by its support
contains two loxodromic elements with disjoint fixed sets in

Let us recall some useful facts on random walks on any HHS group.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\mu
be a finitely supported, non-elementary probability measure on an HHS group
G
. Then:

(1)

For almost every sample path
\omega=(w_{n})
, the sequence
(w_{n})_{S}
converges to a point
\xi_{\omega}
in the Gromov boundary of
\mathcal{C}S
.

(2)

Moreover, there exists

such that

\mathbb{P}\left(d_{S}({\mathfrak{o}},w_{n})\geq ln\right)\geq 1-c^{n}

for any
n
.

(3)

Further, for any
k>0
there exists
C>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(d_{S}(w_{n},\gamma_{\omega})\geq C\log n\right)\leq n^{-k}

for any
n
, where
\gamma_{\omega}=[{\mathfrak{o}},\xi_{\omega})_{S}
.

Claim (2) is proven by Maher (
[
Mah10
]
,
[
Mah12
]
), while (1) and (3) are proven by Maher-Tiozzo in
[
MT18
]
.
Also, exactly the same proof as in
[
QRT19
, Theorem A.17]
yields for HHS groups that for finitely supported, non-elementary probability measure on
G
.
Then for any
k>0
there exists
C>0
such that for all
n
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{Y}d_{Y}({\mathfrak{o}},w_{n})\geq C\log n\right)\leq Cn^{-k},

where the supremum is taken over all (proper) elements of
\Lambda
of
S
.
As a consequence, for almost every sample path there exists

such that for all

\sup_{Y}d_{Y}({\mathfrak{o}},w_{n})\leq C\log n.

It follows that almost every sample path converges to a point in the
\kappa
-Morse boundary of the HHS group,
where
\kappa(r)=\log^{p}(r)
. We now complete the proof of Theorem

Proof.

Since almost every sample path sublinearly tracks a

so we can apply Theorem

ℤ 2 − \mathbb{Z}_{2}- Graded Lie Algebra of Quaternions and Superconformal Algebra in D = 4 D=4 dimensions
Bhupendra C. S. Chauhan, Pawan Kumar Joshi, B.C. Chanyal

Abstract
In the present discussion, we have studied the ℤ 2 − \mathbb{Z}_{2}- g ​ r ​ a ​ d ​ i ​ n ​ g grading of quaternion algebra ( ℍ ) (\mathbb{H}) . We have made an attempt to extend the quaternion Lie algebra to the graded Lie algebra by using the matrix representations of quaternion units. The generalized Jacobi identities of ℤ 2 − g ​ r ​ a ​ d ​ e ​ d \mathbb{Z}_{2}-graded algebra then result in symmetric graded partners ( N 1 , N 2 , N 3 ) (N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}) . The graded partner algebra ( ℱ ) (\mathcal{F}) of quaternions ( ℍ ) (\mathbb{H}) thus has been constructed from this complete set of graded partner units ( N 1 , N 2 , N 3 ) (N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}) , and N 0 = C N_{0}=C . Keeping in view the algebraic properties of the graded partner algebra ( ℱ ) (\mathcal{F}) , the ℤ 2 − g ​ r ​ a ​ d ​ e ​ d \mathbb{Z}_{2}-graded superspace ( S l , m ) (S^{l,m}) of quaternion algebra ( ℍ ) (\mathbb{H}) has been constructed. It has been shown that the antiunitary quaternionic supergroup U ​ U a ​ ( l , m , ℍ ) UU_{a}(l;m;\mathbb{H}) describes the isometries of ℤ 2 − g ​ r ​ a ​ d ​ e ​ d \mathbb{Z}_{2}-graded superspace ( S l , m ) (S^{l,m}) . The Superconformal algebra in D = 4 D=4 dimensions is then established, where the bosonic sector of the Superconformal algebra has been constructed from the quaternion algebra ( ℍ ) (\mathbb{H}) and the fermionic sector from the graded partner algebra ( ℱ ) (\mathcal{F}) .

Quaternion Algebra
(\mathbb{H})
(Definition):

According to the celebrated Hurwitz theorem
[
7

11
]

there exist four normed division algebras:
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{H}
,
and
\mathbb{O}
, respectively, named as the algebras of real numbers,
complex numbers, quaternions, and octonions. The quaternion algebra

(\mathbb{H})
is the second largest normed division algebra, which
is non-commutative but associative. Any element of this quaternion
algebra
(\mathbb{H})
is called a quaternion, which is expressed
over the field of real numbers as

\displaystyle Q=

\displaystyle q^{0}e_{0}+q^{1}e_{1}+q^{2}e_{2}+q^{3}e_{3}.\quad\quad\quad(Q\in\mathbb{H}).

(1)

Where
q^{0}
and
q^{j}\,\>(\forall j=1,2,3)
are the real numbers
and the quaternion basis elements
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
satisfy the
following multiplication rules:

\displaystyle e_{i}e_{j}=

\displaystyle-\delta_{ij}+\in_{ijk}e_{k}\;\;\;\;(\forall i,j,k=1\,to\,3).

(2)

The
\in_{ijk}
is a Levi-Civita tensor, which is totally antisymmetric
and has a value of
+1
for the permutations:
(ijk)=(123),(231),(312)
.

The quaternion conjugate is defined by
\overline{Q}=q^{0}e_{0}-q^{1}e_{1}-q^{2}e_{2}-q^{3}e_{3}\>(q\in\mathbb{H}).

It is to be noted that the quaternion conjugation operation satisfies
the following composition rule:

\displaystyle\overline{(Q_{1}Q_{2})}=

\displaystyle\overline{Q}_{2}\overline{Q}_{1}\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>(\forall Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathbb{H}).

(3)

The norm of a quaternion is positive-definite and computed as

\displaystyle N(Q)=

\displaystyle Q\overline{Q}=\overline{Q}Q=\left|Q\right|=(q^{0})^{2}+(q^{1})^{2}+(q^{2})^{2}+(q^{3})^{2}\geq 0.

(4)

Quaternion is the second-highest normed division algebra; the norm
of quaternion satisfies the following multiplication rule:

\displaystyle N(Q_{1})N(Q_{2})=

\displaystyle N(Q_{1}Q_{2})\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>(\forall Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathbb{H}).

(5)

Following the eq. (
4
), the inverse of a quaternion is defined
by
Q^{-1}=\frac{\overline{Q}}{\left|Q\right|}
. Meanwhile, the inverse
of the multiplication of two quaternions has the following property:

\displaystyle(Q_{1}Q_{2})^{-1}=

\displaystyle Q_{1}^{-1}Q_{2}^{-1}\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>(\forall Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathbb{H}).

(6)

A
2\times 2
quaternionic matrix is defined as

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}h_{1}&h_{2}\\
h_{3}&h_{4}\end{array}\right]\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>(\forall h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}\in\mathbb{H}).

Where
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},
and
h_{4}
are the elements of quaternion
algebra
(\mathbb{H}).
The Hermitian conjugate of this quaternionic
matrix is defined as

\displaystyle H^{\dagger}=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\overline{h}_{1}&\overline{h}_{3}\\
\overline{h}_{2}&\overline{h}_{4}\end{array}\right],

where
\overline{h}_{1},\overline{h}_{2},\overline{h}_{3},\overline{h}_{4}

are the quaternion conjugates of
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}

The Hermitian conjugation operation in quaternionic matrices satisfies
the following composition rule:

\displaystyle(H_{1}H_{2})^{\dagger}=

\displaystyle H_{2}^{\dagger}H_{1}^{\dagger}.

(11)

In a

real matrix representation, the quaternion basis

[
20

\displaystyle e_{1}=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&1&0&0\\
-1&0&0&0\\

These basis elements satisfy the following commutation algebra:

\displaystyle[e_{i},e_{j}]=

\displaystyle 2\in_{ijk}e_{k}\quad\quad\quad(\forall i,j,k=1\,to\,3).

\displaystyle{}[e_{p},[e_{q},e_{r}]]+[e_{q},[e_{r},e_{p}]]+[e_{r},[e_{p},e_{q}]]=

\displaystyle 0\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\>\>(\forall p,q,r=1\,to\,3).

(24)

A first order in time wave equation modeling nonlinear acoustics
Barbara Kaltenbacher, Pascal Lehner

Abstract
In this paper we focus on a small amplitude approximation of a Navier-Stokes-Fourier system modeling nonlinear acoustics. Omitting all third and higher order terms with respect to certain small parameters, we obtain a first order in time system containing linear and quadratic pressure and velocity terms. Subsequently, the well-posedness of the derived system is shown using the classical method of Galerkin approximation in combination with a fixed point argument. We first prove the well-posedness of a linearized equation using energy estimates and then the well-posedness of the nonlinear system using a Newton-Kantorovich type argument. Based on this, we also obtain global in time well-posedness for small enough data and exponential decay. This is in line with semigroup results for a linear part of the system that we provide as well.

Introduction

Nonlinear acoustics has recently found much interest in the physics, engineering and mathematics community. This is driven by a multitude of applications of high intensity (focused) ultrasound ranging from medical therapy and imaging to industrial cleaning and welding, requiring simulation and optimization tools that need to be well based on a mathematical analysis of the underlying system and its models. In fact, also modeling itself is a highly active field of research.
For a recent review on the mathematics of nonlinear acoustics with some (but not complete, due to ongoing activities) references we refer to
[
12
]
.
Details on modeling can be found, for example, in the by now standard physics references
[
4
,
11
]
.

The first order in time system to be studied here derives from the mass conservation, momentum balance and entropy equation, complemented by appropriate constitutive equations relating pressure and temperature to density and entropy.
The decisive difference between classical fluid mechanics, where the above mentioned balance equations arise as well, and nonlinear acoustics studied here, is the typical incompressibility assumption there, while a typical assumption in acoustics is irrotationality of the particle velocity as well as its vanishing mean or background value. While recently also compressibility in fluid mechanics has been studied intensively, see., e.g.,
[
3
]
and the references therein, we derive and analyze a system that is more targeted at nonlinear acoustics and in particular contains less unknowns.

While the mathematical literature for second order in time nonlinear wave equations is meanwhile quite extensive, much less work exists so far for first order in time systems; we particularly refer to
[
5
,
24
]
.
The quest for such first order formulations arises from their usefulness for the development of efficient numerical schemes, in particular adaptive space-time discretizations, where first order systems have already been proven to serve as good starting points for discontinuous Galerkin methods in case of linear acoustics, see, e.g.,
[
6
,
20
]
.
There the authors rely on the linearity of the underlying equations, while a more general approach can be encountered in the field of fluid dynamics. There adaptive space-time discontinuous Galerkin methods for nonlinear Navier-Stokes systems are considered, see
[
9
]
and the references therein.
Again, the fact that the system we study here is tailored to nonlinear acoustics and only contains the velocity and the pressure as variables is supposed to allow for increased efficiency of numerical schemes to be derived for them.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.

In Section
2
, we derive a first order in time system from the above mentioned system of balance equations and constitutive relations, skipping higher than quadratic terms and using the substitution corollary that allows to insert linearized relations into nonlinear terms – a procedure known in nonlinear acoustics as Blackstock’s scheme, cf.
[
1
,
18
]
and see also Remark
2.3
.
Here we base the approximation on a non-dimensionalization of the physical quantities and consideration of its asymptotics for small Mach numbers.

Section
3
studies mathematical well-posedness of the initial boundary value problem for this system of PDEs; that is, we prove existence and uniqueness of solutions and provide estimates on a certain regularity level, depending on the smoothness of the initial data and forcing functions, that need to be small enough to enable local in time well-posedness – a fact that is also known from second order wave equation models of nonlinear acoustics and that results from potential degeneracy of the equations. We also prove global in time well-posedness and exponential decay.

A short appendix type Section
4
provides some basic facts on the semigroup pertaining to a linear part of the system.

Lemma 3.2

The Fréchet derivative of

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{u_{1}}[h]-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{u_{2}}[h]\|_{Y}\leq 2C_{\mathcal{B}}|\varepsilon|\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\tilde{X}}\|h\|_{\tilde{X}}

Proof.

We check that each term is Fréchet differentiable. For the linear terms this is clear. For the quadratic terms we can use the bilinearity in combination with Lemma

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathcal{F}(u+h)-\mathcal{F}(u)-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{u}[h]\|_{Y}}{\|h\|_{X}}\leq C_{\mathcal{B}}|\varepsilon|\|h\|_{X}\to 0\text{ as }\|h\|_{X}\to 0.

(181)

\mathcal{B}[u_{1},h]+\mathcal{B}[h,u_{1}]-\mathcal{B}[u_{2},h]-\mathcal{B}[h,u_{2}]=\mathcal{B}[u_{1}-u_{2},h]+\mathcal{B}[h,u_{1}-u_{2}]

.
∎

Note that with

Proof of Theorem
3.1

The proof is split into several steps and uses the classical method of Galerkin approximation, see cf., eg.,

3.1.1
Galerkin Approximation

For the following Galerkin method we make use of the family of eigenfunctions
\{\sigma^{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of the Dirichlet Laplacian
-\Delta:H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0}\to L^{2}
.
We can normalize
\{\sigma^{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that it is an orthogonal basis of
H^{1}_{0}
and an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}
.

For any fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
we define
\sigma_{m}:=(\sigma^{1},\dots,\sigma^{m})^{\top}\in(H^{2}\cap H_{0}^{1})^{m}
and define the solution ansatz as

\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{m}(t):=D_{m}(t)\sigma_{m}\in U_{m}:=(span\{\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{m}\})^{n}\end{split}

For a suitable choice of the coefficient matrix
D:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
we want to solve the projection of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{u_{*}}[u_{m}]=f
onto the finite dimensional subspace
U_{m}
.
That is, we are looking for a function
D_{m}
such that
u_{m}
according to (
3.1.1
) solves

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{P}_{U_{m}}(\partial_{t}u_{m}(t)-\mathbb{A}u_{m}(t)+\mathbb{B}[u_{m}(t),u_{*}(t)]+\mathbb{B}[u_{*}(t),u_{m}(t)]-f(t))&=0\\
u_{m}(0)&=\mathbf{P}_{U_{m}}(u_{0})\end{split}

for a.e
t\in[0,T]
, where
\mathbf{P}_{U_{m}}
denotes the component wise projection from
L^{2}
onto
U_{m}
.
Note that the boundary conditions are automatically satisfied due to the choice of the ansatz functions.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
T\in(0,\infty)
,
\gamma,\delta\in L^{\infty}
,
({f},u_{0})\in Y
and
u_{*}\in X
.
Then there exists a unique absolutely continuous function
D_{m}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
that solves (
3.1.1
) with (
3.1.1
).

Proof.

For the sake of notation, in the following we consider the vectorized version
d_{m}
of
D_{m}
defined as

\displaystyle d_{m}:=(D_{m}^{1,1},\dots,D_{m}^{1,m},\dots,D_{m}^{n,1},\dots D_{m}^{n,m})^{\top}=(d^{1},\dots,d^{nm})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{nm}.

(192)

Testing the
l
-th equation of (
3.1.1
) with
\sigma^{j}
for each
l\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
gives
the following initial value problem for an
(nm)
-dimensional nonhomogeneous linear ODE with time dependent coefficients

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{d}_{m}(t)&=M_{m}(t)d_{m}(t)+b_{m}(t)\\
d_{m}(0)&=\int_{\Omega}((u_{0})_{1}\sigma^{1},\dots,(u_{0})_{1}\sigma^{m},\dots,(u_{0})_{n}\sigma^{1},\dots,(u_{0})_{n}\sigma^{m})^{\top}\end{split}

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}M^{i,j}_{m}(t)&:=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{l}=(l-1)m+1}^{lm}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\delta_{k_{l}j}\int_{\Omega}(\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{B}[u_{*}(t),\cdot]-\mathbb{B}[\cdot,u_{*}(t)])_{\chi(i)}(\sigma^{k_{1}\bmod m}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!,\ldots,\sigma^{k_{n}\bmod m})\sigma^{i\bmod m}\\
b_{m}^{i}(t)&:=\int_{\Omega}{{f}(t)_{\chi(i)}}\sigma^{i\bmod m},\end{split}

where
i\in\{1,...,nm\}
and
\chi(i)=\left\lceil\frac{i}{m}\right\rceil
with
\left\lceil\cdot\right\rceil
denoting the ceil function that rounds up to the next integer. Here we use entry wise integration if the integral sign is in front of a vector.

Indeed, using the linearity of
\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{B}[u_{*}(t),\cdot]-\mathbb{B}[\cdot,u_{*}(t)]
in each component we get

\displaystyle(M_{m}(t)d_{m}(t))_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{nm}M^{i,j}_{m}(t)d^{j}_{m}(t)

(193)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{l}=(l-1)m+1}^{lm}\int_{\Omega}(\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{B}[u_{*}(t),\cdot]-\mathbb{B}[\cdot,u_{*}(t)])_{\chi(i)}(\sigma^{k_{1}\bmod m}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!,\ldots,\sigma^{k_{n}\bmod m})\sigma^{i\bmod m}d^{k_{l}}_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{l}=(l-1)m+1}^{lm}\int_{\Omega}(\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{B}[u_{*}(t),\cdot]-\mathbb{B}[\cdot,u_{*}(t)])_{\chi(i)}(d_{m}^{k_{1}}\sigma^{k_{1}\bmod m}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!,\ldots,d_{m}^{k_{n}}\sigma^{k_{n}\bmod m})\sigma^{i\bmod m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{B}[u_{*}(t),\cdot]-\mathbb{B}[\cdot,u_{*}(t)])_{\chi(i)}(\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{m}d_{m}^{k_{1}}\sigma^{k_{1}\bmod m}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!,\ldots,\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{k_{n}=(n-1)m+1}^{nm}d_{m}^{k_{n}}\sigma^{k_{n}\bmod m})\sigma^{i\bmod m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{B}[u_{*}(t),\cdot]-\mathbb{B}[\cdot,u_{*}(t)])_{\chi(i)}(u_{m})\sigma^{i\bmod m},

which corresponds to testing the vectorized version of (
3.1.1
).

For the ODE in (
3.1.1
) we have the following properties.

i) System (
3.1.1
) is linear in

, thus clearly also Lipschitz continuous.

ii)

can be estimated using Hölder’s inequality and
\|\sigma^{i\bmod m}\|_{{{{L}^{2}}}}=1

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|b^{i}_{m}(t)|dt\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|{f}_{\chi(i)}(t)\|_{{{{L}^{2}}}}\|\sigma^{i\bmod m}\|_{{{{L}^{2}}}}dt\leq\|{f}\|_{L^{1}(T;(L^{2})^{n})}\leq\sqrt{T}\|{f}\|_{L^{2}(T,(L^{2})^{n})},

(194)

so that

, where we use

.
Similarly, for
M_{m}
and
\ThisStyle{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.46pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.33pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.2pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr$\SavedStyle k$\crcr}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
\sigma^{\ThisStyle{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.46pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.33pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.2pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr$\SavedStyle k$\crcr}}}}=(\sigma^{k_{1}},\ldots,\sigma^{k_{n}})

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|M_{m}^{i,j}(t)|dt\leq\sum_{|\ThisStyle{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.46pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.33pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.2pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr$\SavedStyle k$\crcr}}}|_{\infty}\leq n}\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathbb{A}_{\chi(i)}(\sigma^{\ThisStyle{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.46pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.33pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.2pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr$\SavedStyle k$\crcr}}}})\|_{{{{L}^{2}}}}+\|\mathbb{B}_{\chi(i)}[\sigma^{\ThisStyle{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.46pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.33pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.2pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr$\SavedStyle k$\crcr}}}},u_{*}(t)]\|_{{{{L}^{2}}}}+\|\mathbb{B}_{\chi(i)}[u_{*}(t),\sigma^{\ThisStyle{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.46pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.33pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.2pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr$\SavedStyle k$\crcr}}}}]\|_{{{{L}^{2}}}}dt

(195)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\ThisStyle{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.46pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.33pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.2pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr$\SavedStyle k$\crcr}}}|_{\infty}\leq n}C_{\mathbb{A}}T\|\sigma^{\ThisStyle{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.46pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.33pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.2pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr$\SavedStyle k$\crcr}}}}\|_{{({H}^{2})^{n}}}+2\max\{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{4}\}C_{R}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{*}(t)\|_{{({H}^{2})^{n}}}\|\sigma^{\ThisStyle{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.46pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.33pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.2pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr$\SavedStyle k$\crcr}}}}\|_{{({H}^{2})^{n}}}dt

\displaystyle\leq C(U_{m},\mu,\eta,\gamma,\delta,\varepsilon,T)(1+\|u_{*}\|_{X}),

where we have estimated the

terms like in the proof of Lemma

and write
|\ThisStyle{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.46pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.33pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr\kern 0.2pt$\SavedStyle k$\cr$\SavedStyle k$\crcr}}}|_{\infty}=\max_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}}k_{i}
. Here

.
This allows us to conclude that

and

are in

and

respectively, so that

defines a Carathréodory function.

iii) Moreover, we have the following growth condition for

\displaystyle|M_{m}(t)d+b_{m}(t)|\leq C|M_{m}(t)||d|+|b(t)|,

(196)

where we have

for some

By
[

we obtain existence of a unique absolutely continuous solution

.
∎

5
Conclusion

In this paper we have shown that a first order in time quadratic wave equation can be obtained from a small amplitude approximation. We have provided the derivation of such a system from the Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations in Section
2
and proven its well-posedness with Dirichlet boundary conditions in Section
3
.
An extension of the analysis to the practically relevant but technically somewhat more complicated Neumann and/or impedance boundary case will be subject of future research.

Since Fourier’s law of heat conduction, that underlies the entropy equation (
2
) used here, is known to lead to nonphysical infinite speed of propagation, we plan to also investigate the use of the Maxwell-Cattaneo law and fractional versions thereof. An analysis of the resulting second order in time nonlinear wave equations can be found in, e.g.,
[
2
,
13
,
14
]
.
However, the derivation and analysis of pressure-velocity systems like the one studied here (but with the leading time derivative probably differing from one) is yet to be done.

From a theoretical and also practical viewpoint the limit as
\eta,\,\varepsilon\to 0
of (
2.2
) is of interest as well; that is, convergence to solutions of the system with
\eta=\varepsilon=0
in appropriate function spaces in the spirit of, e.g.,
[
2
,
14
]
.
This requires energy estimates that are uniform with respect to vanishing
\eta
,
\varepsilon
as well as investigations on their appropriate interplay when taking limits to still allow global in time well-posedness.

The modeling and analysis results of this paper will serve as a basis for the development and analysis of numerical solution methods, in particular for adaptive space-time Galerkin methods. These are able to adequately treat shock-like scenarios and allow high parallelizability as well as
hp
-adaptivity in space-time.

1.
Introduction

The study of minimal submanifolds of a given ambient space plays a central role in differential geometry. This has a long, interesting history and has attracted the interests of profound mathematicians for many generations. The famous Weierstrass-Enneper representation formula, for minimal surfaces in Euclidean
3
-space, brings
complex analysis
into play as a useful tool for the study of these beautiful objects.

This was later generalised to the study of minimal surfaces in much more general ambient manifolds via
harmonic conformal immersions
. The next result follows from the seminal paper
[
6
]
of Eells and Sampson from 1964. For this see also Proposition 3.5.1 of
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\phi:(M^{m},g)\to(N,h)
be a smooth conformal map between Riemannian manifolds. If
m=2
then
\phi
is harmonic if and only if the image is minimal in
(N,h)
.

This result has turned out to be very useful in the construction of minimal surfaces in Riemannian symmetric spaces of various types. For this we refer to
[
5
]
,

[
7
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
, just to name a few.

In their work
[
1
]
from 1981, Baird and Eells have shown that complex-valued harmonic morphisms from Riemannian manifolds are useful tools for the study of minimal submanifolds of codimension two.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
1
]

Let
\phi:(M,g)\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a complex-valued harmonic morphism from a Riemannian manifold. Then every regular fibre of
\phi
is a minimal submanifold of
(M,g)
of codimension two.

This can be seen as dual to the above mentioned generalisation of the Weierstrass-Enneper representation. Harmonic morphisms are the much studied
horizontally conformal harmonic
maps. For an introduction to the theory we recommend the book
[
2
]
, by Baird and Wood, and the regularly updated online bibliography
[
11
]
.

2.
The Main Results

The recent work
[
12
]
introduces a method for constructing minimal submanifolds of Riemannian manifolds via
submersions
, see Theorem
4.1
. Then this scheme is employed to provide compact examples in several important cases. The main ingredients for this new procedure are the so called
complex-valued eigenfunctions
on the Riemannian ambient space. These are functions which are eigen both with respect to the classical
Laplace-Beltrami
and the so called
conformality
operator, see Section
3
.

In the current study we continue the investigation and apply the above mentioned method to the classical Riemannian symmetric spaces

\text{\bf SU}(n)/\mathbf{SO}(n),\ \text{\bf Sp}(n)/\text{\bf U}(n),\ \mathbf{SO}(2n)/\text{\bf U}(n)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \text{\bf SU}(2n)/\text{\bf Sp}(n).

\mathcal{F}_{1}=\{\phi_{a}^{-1}(\{0\})\ |\ [a]\in{\mathbb{C}}P^{n-1}\}

.

3.
Eigenfunctions and Eigenfamilies

Let
(M,g)
be an
m
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
T^{{\mathbb{C}}}M
be the complexification of the tangent bundle
TM
of
M
. We extend the metric
g
to a complex bilinear form on
T^{{\mathbb{C}}}M
. Then the gradient
\nabla\phi
of a complex-valued function
\phi:(M,g)\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a section of
T^{{\mathbb{C}}}M
. In this situation, the well-known complex linear
Laplace-Beltrami operator
(alt.
tension field
)
\tau
on
(M,g)
. In local coordinates this satisfies

\tau(\phi)=\Div(\nabla\phi)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(g^{ij}\,\sqrt{|g|}\,\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}\right).

For two complex-valued functions
\phi,\psi:(M,g)\to{\mathbb{C}}
we have the following well-known fundamental relation

\tau(\phi\,\psi)=\tau(\phi)\,\psi+2\,\kappa(\phi,\psi)+\phi\,\tau(\psi),

where the complex bilinear
conformality operator

\kappa
is given by

\kappa(\phi,\psi)=g(\nabla\phi,\nabla\psi).

Locally this satisfies

\kappa(\phi,\psi)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}g^{ij}\cdot\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x_{j}}.

Definition 3.1
.

[
13
]

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold. Then a complex-valued function
\phi:M\to{\mathbb{C}}
is said to be a
(\lambda,\mu)
-eigenfunction
if it is eigen both with respect to the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
i.e. there exist complex numbers
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that

\tau(\phi)=\lambda\cdot\phi\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(\phi,\phi)=\mu\cdot\phi^{2}.

A set
\mathcal{E}=\{\phi_{i}:M\to{\mathbb{C}}\ |\ i\in I\}
of complex-valued functions is said to be a
(\lambda,\mu)
-eigenfamily
on
M
if there exist complex numbers
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{E}
we have

\tau(\phi)=\lambda\cdot\phi\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(\phi,\psi)=\mu\cdot\phi\,\psi.

For the standard odd-dimensional round spheres we have the following eigenfamilies based on the classical real-valued spherical harmonics.

Example 3.2
.

[
12
]

Let
S^{2n-1}
be the odd-dimensional unit sphere in the standard Euclidean space
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
and define
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}:S^{2n-1}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

\phi_{j}:(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\mapsto\frac{z_{j}}{\sqrt{|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{2}}}.

Then the tension field
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
on
S^{2n-1}
satisfy

\tau(\phi_{j})=-\,(2n-1)\cdot\phi_{j}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(\phi_{j},\phi_{k})=-\,1\cdot\phi_{j}\cdot\phi_{k}.

For the standard complex projective space
{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
we have a complex multi-dimensional eigenfamily, in a similar way.

Example 3.3
.

[
12
]

Let
{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
be the standard
n
-dimensional complex projective space. For a fixed integer
1\leq\alpha<n+1
and some
1\leq j\leq\alpha<k\leq n+1
define the function
\phi_{jk}:{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

\phi_{jk}:[z_{1},\dots,z_{n+1}]\mapsto\frac{z_{j}\cdot\bar{z}_{k}}{z_{1}\cdot\bar{z}_{1}+\cdots+z_{n+1}\cdot\bar{z}_{n+1}}.

Then the tension field
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
on
{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
satisfy

\tau(\phi_{jk})=-\,4(n+1)\cdot\phi_{jk}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(\phi_{jk},\phi_{lm})=-\,4\cdot\phi_{jk}\cdot\phi_{lm}.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\pi:(\hat{M},\hat{g})\to(M,g)
be a harmonic Riemannian submersion between Riemannian manifolds. Further let
\phi:(M,g)\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a smooth function and
\hat{\phi}:(\hat{M},\hat{g})\to{\mathbb{C}}
be the composition
\hat{\phi}=\phi\circ\pi
. Then the corresponding tension fields
\tau
and conformality operators
\kappa
satisfy

\tau(\hat{\phi})=\tau(\phi)\circ\pi\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(\hat{\phi},\hat{\psi})=\kappa(\phi,\psi)\circ\pi.

]
.

.

\hat{\phi}:(\hat{M},\hat{g})\to{\mathbb{C}}

\hat{\phi}=\phi\circ\pi

is a

,

4.
Minimal Submanifolds via Eigenfunctions

The recent paper
[
12
]
provides a new application of complex-valued eigenfunctions. This is a method for constructing minimal submanifolds of codimension two.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
12
]

Let
\phi:(M,g)\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a complex-valued eigenfunction on a Riemannian manifold, such that
0\in\phi(M)
is a regular value for
\phi
. Then the fibre
\phi^{-1}(\{0\})
is a minimal submanifold of
M
of codimension two.

The main aim of our work is to apply Theorem
4.1
in several of the interesting cases when the Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is one of the classical compact symmetric spaces.

The next result, from Riedler and Siffert’s paper
[
16
]
supplies us with a straightforward way of checking whether an eigenfunction, on a compact and connected Riemannian manifold, attains the required value
0\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
16
]

Let
(M,g)
be a compact and connected Riemannian manifold and let
\phi:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a
(\lambda,\mu)
-eigenfunction not identically zero. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

\lambda=\mu
.

(2)

\rvert\phi\rvert^{2}
is constant.

(3)

\phi(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in M.

As an obvious consequence we have the following.

Corollary 4.3
.

If
\phi:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex-valued
(\lambda,\mu)
-eigenfunction on a compact and connected Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
such that
\lambda\neq\mu,
then there exists
x\in M
such that
\phi(x)=0
.

5.
The General Linear Group
\mathbf{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})

In this section we now turn our attention to the concrete Riemannian matrix Lie groups embedded as subgroups of the complex general linear group.

The group of linear automorphisms of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is the complex general linear group
\mathbf{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}\,|\,\det z\neq 0\}
of invertible
n\times n
matrices with its standard representation

z\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}z_{11}&\cdots&z_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
z_{n1}&\cdots&z_{nn}\end{bmatrix}.

Its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of left-invariant vector fields on
\mathbf{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
can be identified with
{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
i.e. the complex linear space of
n\times n
matrices. We equip
\mathbf{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with its natural left-invariant Riemannian metric
g
induced by the standard Euclidean inner product
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\times\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
satisfying

g(Z,W)\mapsto\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{e}\,\trace\,(Z\cdot\bar{W}^{t}).

For
1\leq i,j\leq n
, we shall by
E_{ij}
denote the element of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
satisfying

(E_{ij})_{kl}=\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}

and by
D_{t}
the diagonal
matrices
D_{t}=E_{tt}.
For
1\leq r<s\leq n
, let
X_{rs}
and

Y_{rs}
be the matrices satisfying

X_{rs}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(E_{rs}+E_{sr}),\ \ Y_{rs}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(E_{rs}-E_{sr}).

For the real vector space
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
we then have the canonical orthonormal basis
\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{B}\cup i\mathcal{B}
, where

\mathcal{B}=\{Y_{rs},X_{rs}\,|\,1\leq r<s\leq n\}\cup\{D_{t}\,|\,t=1,2,\dots,n\}.

Let
G
be a classical Lie subgroup of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
inheriting the induced left-invariant Riemannian metric, which we shall also denote by
g
. In the cases considered in this paper,
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}\cap\mathfrak{g}
will be an orthornormal basis for the subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. By employing the Koszul formula for the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
on
(G,g)
, we see that for all
Z,W\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}
we have

\displaystyle g(\hbox{$\nabla$\kern-3.00003pt\lower 4.30554pt\hbox{$Z$}\kern-1.00006pt{$Z$}},W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g([W,Z],Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{e}\,\trace\,((WZ-ZW)\bar{Z}^{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{e}\,\trace\,(W(Z\bar{Z}^{t}-\bar{Z}^{t}Z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

If
Z\in\mathfrak{g}
is a left-invariant vector field on
G
and
\phi:U\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a local complex-valued function on
G
then the
k
-th order derivatives
Z^{k}(\phi)
satisfy

Z^{k}(\phi)(p)=\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}\bigl(\phi(p\cdot\exp(sZ))\bigr)\Big|_{s=0},

This implies that the tension field
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
on
G
fulfill

\tau(\phi)=\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}}\bigl(Z^{2}(\phi)-\hbox{$\nabla$\kern-3.00003pt\lower 4.30554pt\hbox{$Z$}\kern-1.00006pt{$Z$}}(\phi)\bigr)=\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}}Z^{2}(\phi),

\kappa(\phi,\psi)=\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}}Z(\phi)\cdot Z(\psi),

where
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the orthonormal basis
\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}\cap\mathfrak{g}
for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

6.
The Special Orthogonal Group
\mathbf{SO}(n)

In this section we introduce the basic tools, related to the special orthogonal group
\mathbf{SO}(n)
, needed for our later constructions. The classical Lie group
\mathbf{SO}(n)
is given by

\mathbf{SO}(n)=\{x\in\mathbf{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})\ |\ x\cdot x^{t}=I_{n},\ \det x=1\}.

For this we use its standard
n
-dimensional representation on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with

x\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}x_{11}&\cdots&x_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
x_{n1}&\cdots&x_{nn}\end{bmatrix}.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(n)
of
\mathbf{SO}(n)
is the set of real skew-symmetric matrices

\mathfrak{so}(n)=\{X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})\ |\ X+X^{t}=0\}

and for this we have the canonical orthonormal basis

\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=\{Y_{rs}\ |\ 1\leq r<s\leq n\}.

The gradient
\nabla\phi
of a complex-valued function
\phi:\mathbf{SO}(n)\to{\mathbb{C}}
is an element of the complexified tangent bundle
T^{\mathbb{C}}\mathbf{SO}(n)
. This satisfies

\nabla\phi=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}}Y(\phi)\cdot Y.

We now consider the functions
x_{j\alpha}:\mathbf{SO}(n)\to{\mathbb{C}}
with
x_{j\alpha}:x\mapsto e_{j}\cdot x\cdot e_{\alpha}^{t}
. For any tangent vector
Y\in B_{\mathfrak{u}(n)}
we have

Y(x_{j\alpha}):x\mapsto e_{j}\cdot x\cdot Y\cdot e_{\alpha}^{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{jk}\cdot Y_{k\alpha}.

This implies that

(6.1)

Y_{rs}(x_{j\alpha})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(x_{jr}\cdot\delta_{\alpha s}-x_{js}\cdot\delta_{\alpha r}).

7.
The Unitary Groups
\text{\bf U}(n)
and
\text{\bf SU}(n)

The unitary group
\text{\bf U}(n)
is the compact subgroup of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
given by

\text{\bf U}(n)=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}\,|\,z\cdot\bar{z}^{t}=I\}.

For its standard complex representation
\text{\bf U}(n)\to\mathbf{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
we use the notation

z\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}z_{11}&\cdots&z_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
z_{n1}&\cdots&z_{nn}\end{bmatrix}.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(n)
of
\text{\bf U}(n)
consists of the skew-Hermitian matrices i.e.

\mathfrak{u}(n)=\{Z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}\ |\ Z+\bar{Z}^{t}=0\}.

The left-invariant metric on
\mathbf{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
induces the standard biinvariant Riemannian metric
g
on
\text{\bf U}(n)
, with

g(Z,W)=\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{e}\trace(Z\cdot\bar{W}^{t}).

The canonical orthonormal basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(n)
is then given by

\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{u}(n)}=\{Y_{rs},iX_{rs}|\ 1\leq r<s\leq n\}\cup\{iD_{t}|\ t=1,\dots,n\}.

The special unitary group
\text{\bf SU}(n)
is the subgroup of
\text{\bf U}(n)
satisfying

\text{\bf SU}(n)=\{z\in\text{\bf U}(n)\ |\ \det(z)=1\}

with Lie algebra

\mathfrak{su}(n)=\{Z\in\mathfrak{u}(n)\ |\ \trace Z=0\}.

For the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(n)
of
\text{\bf U}(n)
we have the orthogonal decomposition

\mathfrak{u}(n)=\mathfrak{su}(n)\oplus\mathfrak{m},

where the one dimensional subspace
\mathfrak{m}
is generated by the unit vector

E=i\,I_{n}/\sqrt{n}.

8.
The Quaternionic Unitary Group
\text{\bf Sp}(n)

The group
\text{\bf Sp}(n)
is the intersection of the unitary group
\text{\bf U}(2n)
and the standard representation of the quaternionic general linear group
\mathbf{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n\times 2n}
given by

(8.1)

(z+jw)\mapsto q=\begin{bmatrix}z&w\\
-\bar{w}&\bar{z}\end{bmatrix}.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}(n)
of
\text{\bf Sp}(n)
satisfies

\mathfrak{sp}(n)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}Z&W\\
-\bar{W}&\bar{Z}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2n\times 2n}\ \Big|\ Z^{*}+Z=0,\ W^{t}-W=0\right\}.

We now introduce the following notation for the elements of the orthonormal basis
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{sp}(n)}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}(n)
:

Y^{a}_{rs}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}Y_{rs}&0\\
0&Y_{rs}\end{bmatrix},X^{a}_{rs}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}iX_{rs}&0\\
0&-iX_{rs}\end{bmatrix},

X^{b}_{rs}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&iX_{rs}\\
iX_{rs}&0\end{bmatrix},X^{c}_{rs}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&X_{rs}\\
-X_{rs}&0\end{bmatrix},

D^{a}_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}iD_{t}&0\\

iD_{t}&0\end{bmatrix},D^{c}_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&D_{t}\\

Here
1\leq r<s\leq n
and
1\leq t\leq n
.

9.
The Symmetric Space
\text{\bf SU}(n)/\mathbf{SO}(n)

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
2.1
and thereby construct a new multi-dimensional family of compact minimal submanifolds of the homogeneous quotient manifold
\text{\bf SU}(n)/\mathbf{SO}(n)
, which carries the structure of a compact Riemannian symmetric space. It is well-known that the natural projection
\pi:\text{\bf SU}(n)\to\text{\bf SU}(n)/\mathbf{SO}(n)
from the special unitary group
\text{\bf SU}(n)
is a Riemannian submersion. This means that we can apply Corollary
3.5
in this situation.

From the work
[
14
]
, of Gudmundsson, Siffert and Sobak, we have the following construction of eigenfunctions.

Proposition 9.1
.

[
14
]

For
n\geq 2
, let
a
be a non-zero element of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
A\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
with
A=a^{t}a
. Further let
\phi_{a}:\text{\bf SU}(n)/\mathbf{SO}(n)\to{\mathbb{C}}
be the complex-valued function induced by its
\mathbf{SO}(n)
-invariant lift
\hat{\phi}_{a}:\text{\bf SU}(n)\to{\mathbb{C}}
given by

\hat{\phi}_{a}(z)=\trace(Azz^{t}).

Then
\phi_{a}
is an eigenfunction on
\text{\bf SU}(n)/\mathbf{SO}(n)
with eigenvalues
\lambda
and
\mu
satisfying

\lambda=-\frac{2(n^{2}+n-2)}{n}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mu=-\frac{4(n-1)}{n}.

Proof.

(Theorem
2.1
)
For the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(n)
of
\text{\bf SU}(n)
we have the orthogonal decomposition
\mathfrak{su}(n)=\mathfrak{so}(n)\oplus i\mathfrak{p}
where the subspace

\mathfrak{p}=\{X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}\ |\ X=X^{t}\ \text{and}\ \trace X=0\}

is generated by the elements of

\{X_{rs},\ D_{rs}\ |\ 1\leq r<s\leq n\}.

This means that if a point
z\in\text{\bf SU}(n)
is a critical point for
\hat{\phi}:\text{\bf SU}(n)\to{\mathbb{C}}
then, for all
1\leq r<s\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle X_{rs}(\hat{\phi}_{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\trace(X_{rs}^{t}\cdot z^{t}\cdot A\cdot z+z^{t}\cdot A\cdot z\cdot X_{rs})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\trace((X_{rs}^{t}+X_{rs})\cdot z^{t}\cdot a^{t}\cdot a\cdot z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\trace(a\cdot z\cdot X_{rs}\cdot z^{t}\cdot a^{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{2}\,(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}z_{kr})(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}z_{ks})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Hence, for any choice
1\leq r<s\leq n
, either

(9.1)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}z_{kr}=0\ \ \text{or}\ \ \sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}z_{ks}=0.

Now there exists an integer
1\leq t\leq n
such that

(9.2)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}z_{kt}\neq 0,

otherwise the rows of the matrix
z\in\text{\bf SU}(n)
would be linearly dependent, which is impossible. It then follows from statement (
9.1
) that if
m\neq t
, then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}z_{km}=0.

Similarly, we see that at a critial point
z\in\text{\bf SU}(n)
we have

D_{rs}(\phi)=0
for all
1\leq r<s\leq n
. This is equivalent to

(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}z_{kr}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}z_{ks})\cdot(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}z_{kr}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}z_{ks})=0.

Applying our previous findings, we see that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}z_{kt}=0,

which contradicts equation
(
9.2
).
We conclude that the eigenfunction
\phi_{a}:\text{\bf SU}(n)/\mathbf{SO}(n)\to{\mathbb{C}}
has no critical points. According to Corollary
4.3
, the origin
0\in{\mathbb{C}}
lies in the image of
\phi_{a}.
It then immediately follows from Theorem
4.1
that the fibre
\phi_{a}^{-1}(\{0\})
is minimal.
∎

10.
The Symmetric Space
\text{\bf Sp}(n)/\text{\bf U}(n)

In this section we prove Theorem
2.2
, which yields a new multi-dimensional family of compact minimal submanifolds of the Riemannian symmetric space
\text{\bf Sp}(n)/\text{\bf U}(n)
. We first recall that the natural projection
\pi:\text{\bf Sp}(n)\to\text{\bf Sp}(n)/\text{\bf U}(n)
from the quaternionic unitary group
\text{\bf Sp}(n)
is a Riemannian submersion and thus we may now employ Corollary
3.5
.

Here we use the standard representation of the quaternionic unitary group
\text{\bf Sp}(n)
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
given by equation (
8.1
). Then we identify the unitary group with following subgroup of
\text{\bf Sp}(n)

\text{\bf U}(n)\cong\Big\{\begin{bmatrix}x&y\\
-y&x\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n\times 2n}\ \Big|\ xx^{t}+yy^{t}=I_{n},\ \ xy^{t}=yx^{t}\Big\}.

For the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}(n)
of
\text{\bf Sp}(n)
we have the orthogonal decomposition
\mathfrak{sp}(n)=\mathfrak{u}(n)\oplus\mathfrak{p}
where the subspace
\mathfrak{p}
is generated by the elements of its following orthonormal basis

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl\{X_{rs}^{a}=\frac{i}{2}\begin{bmatrix}X_{rs}&0\\
0&-X_{rs}\end{bmatrix},D_{t}^{a}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}D_{t}&0\\
0&-D_{t}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\qquad X_{rs}^{b}=\frac{i}{2}\begin{bmatrix}0&X_{rs}\\

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\Big|\ 1\leq r<s\leq n,\ 1\leq t\leq n\Bigr\}.

Let
\sigma:\text{\bf Sp}(n)\to\text{\bf Sp}(n)
be the map given by
\sigma(q)=q\,q^{t}
. Then it is easily seen that
\sigma
is
\text{\bf U}(n)
-invariant i.e. for all

and

\sigma(qz)=qzz^{t}q^{t}=qq^{t}=\sigma(q).

The following result on eigenfunctions on the quotient space

can be found in
[
14
]
.

Proposition 10.1
.

[
14
]

Let
a
be a non-zero element of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
and
A\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2n\times 2n}
with
A=a^{t}a
. Further let
\phi_{a}:\text{\bf Sp}(n)/\text{\bf U}(n)\to{\mathbb{C}}
be the complex-valued function induced by its
\text{\bf U}(n)
-invariant lift
\hat{\phi}_{a}:\text{\bf Sp}(n)\to{\mathbb{C}}

\hat{\phi}_{a}(q)=\trace(Aqq^{t}).

Then
\phi_{a}
is an eigenfunction on
\text{\bf Sp}(n)/\text{\bf U}(n)
with eigenvalues

\lambda=-2(n+1)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mu=-2.

Proof.

(Theorem
2.2
)
Let us assume that
q\in\text{\bf Sp}(n)
is a critical point for the function
\hat{\phi}_{a}
i.e. that the gradient

\nabla\hat{\phi}_{a}(q)=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}}X(\hat{\phi}_{a}(q))\cdot X=0.

By setting
D_{t}^{a}(\phi_{a})=0
and
D_{t}^{b}(\phi_{a})=0
, for all

, we obtain the following relations

(10.1)

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}q_{kt}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}q_{k,n+t}\right)=0

and

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}q_{kt}\right)^{2}-\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}q_{k,n+t}\right)^{2}=0,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}q_{kt}=0\ \ \text{or}\ \ \sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}q_{k,n+t}=0.

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}q_{kt}=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}q_{k,n+t}=0,

4.3
that the origin

lies in the image of

. Theorem
4.1
implies that the fibre

of codimension two.
∎

11.
The Symmetric Space
\mathbf{SO}(2n)/\text{\bf U}(n)

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
2.3
, which immediately provides us with a new multi-dimensional family of compact minimal submanifolds of the Riemannian symmetric space
\mathbf{SO}(2n)/\text{\bf U}(n)
. As the natural projection
\pi:\mathbf{SO}(2n)\to\mathbf{SO}(2n)/\text{\bf U}(n)
from the special orthogonal group
\mathbf{SO}(2n)
is a Riemannian submersion, we can now apply Corollary
3.5
.

For the unitary group
\text{\bf U}(n)
we have a well-known natural group monomorphism

\psi:\text{\bf U}(n)\to\mathbf{SO}(2n),\ \ \text{with}\ \ \psi:z=x+iy\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}x&-y\\
y&x\end{pmatrix},

embedding it into the special orthogonal group
\mathbf{SO}(2n)
. Here
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
are the real and imaginary parts of
z\in\text{\bf U}(n)
. In this section we identify
\text{\bf U}(n)
with the image
\psi(\text{\bf U}(n))
which clearly is a subgroup of
\mathbf{SO}(2n)
.

For the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(2n)
we have the orthogonal decomposition

\mathfrak{so}(2n)=\mathfrak{u}(n)\oplus\mathfrak{p},

where
\mathfrak{u}(n)
is the Lie algebra of
\text{\bf U}(n)
as a subgroup of
\mathbf{SO}(2n)
. A natural orthonormal basis for the vector space
\mathfrak{p}
is given by

\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\left\{Y_{rs}^{a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}Y_{rs}&0\\
0&-Y_{rs}\end{pmatrix},Y_{rs}^{b}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&Y_{rs}\\
Y_{rs}&0\end{pmatrix}\ \Big|\ 1\leq r<s\leq n\right\}.

Let
I_{n}
denote the
n\times n
identity matrix and
J_{n}
be the standard complex structure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
given by

J_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0\end{bmatrix}.

Then consider the map
\sigma:\mathbf{SO}(2n)\to\mathbf{SO}(2n)
satisfying

\sigma(x)=x\cdot J_{n}\cdot x^{t}.

A simple calculation shows that this map is
\text{\bf U}(n)
-invariant i.e. for any
y\in\text{\bf U}(n)
and
x\in\mathbf{SO}(2n)
we have

\sigma(xy)=xyJ_{n}y^{t}x^{t}=xJ_{n}x^{t}=\sigma(x).

This shows that for any skew-symmetric matrix
A\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2n\times 2n}
the function
\hat{\phi}_{A}:\mathbf{SO}(2n)\to{\mathbb{C}}
, given by

\hat{\phi}_{A}(x)=\trace(A\sigma(x))=\trace(x^{t}AxJ_{n}),

is
\text{\bf U}(n)
-invariant and hence induces a map
\phi_{A}:\mathbf{SO}(2n)/\text{\bf U}(n)\to{\mathbb{C}}
on the the quotient space. Note that in this case the skew-symmetry condition can be assumed without loss of generality, since
\sigma(x)
is skew-symmetric.

We now have the following slightly improved version of Proposition 5.1. of
[
14
]
.

Proposition 11.1
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
a,b
be two linearly independent elements of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
satisfying the condition

(a,a)(b,b)-(a,b)^{2}=0.

Proof.

(Theorem
2.3

From the definition

\hat{\phi}_{a,b}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\sum_{t=1}^{n}\big((a,x_{n+t})(b,x_{t})-(a,x_{t})(b,x_{n+t})\big).

Since

4.3

0=(u,x_{r})(b,x_{n+s})+(u,x_{n+r})(b,x_{s})-(u,x_{s})(b,x_{n+r})-(u,x_{n+s})(b,x_{r}),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle(u,x_{r})(b,x_{n+s})+(u,x_{n+r})(b,x_{s})-(u,x_{s})(b,x_{n+r})-(u,x_{n+s})(b,x_{r}),

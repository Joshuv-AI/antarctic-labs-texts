Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials for B ~ 2 \widetilde{B}_{2}
Karina Batistelli, Aram Bingham , and David Plaza

Abstract
\justify In their seminal paper [ KL79 ] Kazhdan and Lusztig define, for an arbitrary Coxeter system ( W , S ) (W,S) , a family of polynomials indexed by pairs of elements of W W . Despite their relevance and elementary definition, the explicit computation of these polynomials is still one of the hardest open problems in algebraic combinatorics. In this paper we explicitly compute Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials for a Coxeter system of type B ~ 2 \widetilde{B}_{2} .

Introduction

\justify

Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials lie at the intersection of representation theory, geometry and algebraic combinatorics. Their relevance derives from the fact that they elegantly express the answers to many difficult problems in the aforementioned areas. Despite their straightforward definition (through a recursive algorithm involving only elementary operations) and enormous efforts made by several authors over the past forty years, in general the explicit computation of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials remains elusive.

In this paper we compute Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials in type
\widetilde{B}_{2}
; for relevant background and related theory we refer the reader to
[
EMTW20
]
. In order to present our results we introduce some notation. The main protagonist in this paper is the affine Weyl group of type
\widetilde{B}_{2}

W

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we describe geometrically the lower intervals in the Bruhat order of the elements of the big region and the thick region. We specify the region corresponding to each lower interval inductively and obtain closed formulas for their sizes.
In Section 3 we introduce the Hecke algebra of

Our main results lie in Sections 4 and 5, which provide explicit formulas for the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis elements of elements in the big region and the thick region respectively. Finally, in Section 6 we conjecture the explicit formulas of the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis elements corresponding to the elements of the thin region.

Lemma 2.1
.

The set
\leq\theta(m,0)
is the square

defined as the smallest square satisfying the following properties:

•

•

Lemma 2.4

3
Multiplicative formulas in the Hecke algebra

In this section we introduce the Hecke algebra of the affine Weyl group of type
\widetilde{B}_{2}
. We also collect several multiplicative identities that will be key in order to obtain formulas for Kazhdan-Lusztig basis elements in the forthcoming sections.

3.1
The Hecke algebra of type
\widetilde{B}_{2}

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the Hecke algebra of
W
. It is the
{\cal{A}}:=\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
-algebra with generators
\mathbf{H}_{s_{0}}
,
\mathbf{H}_{s_{1}}
and
\mathbf{H}_{s_{2}}
and relations
\mathbf{H}_{s_{i}}^{2}=(v^{-1}-v)\mathbf{H}_{s_{i}}+1
,

\mathbf{H}_{s_{0}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{1}}=\mathbf{H}_{s_{1}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{0}},\qquad\mathbf{H}_{s_{0}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{2}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{0}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{2}}=\mathbf{H}_{s_{2}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{0}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{2}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{0}}\,\mbox{ and }\,\mathbf{H}_{s_{1}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{2}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{1}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{2}}=\mathbf{H}_{s_{2}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{1}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{2}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{1}}.

(3.1)

Given a reduced expression
s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\ldots s_{i_{k}}
of an element
w\in W
we define
\mathbf{H}_{w}:=\mathbf{H}_{s_{i_{1}}}\mathbf{H}_{s_{i_{2}}}\ldots\mathbf{H}_{s_{i_{k}}}
. It is well-known that
\mathbf{H}_{w}
does not depend on the choice of a reduced expression. The set
\{\mathbf{H}_{w}\}_{w\in W}
forms an
\cal{A}
-basis of
\mathcal{H}
, which is called the standard basis. It is easy to see that each generator of
\mathcal{H}
is invertible and therefore all the elements of the standard basis are invertible. There is a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear involution
d:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
which is determined by
d(v)=v^{-1}
and
d(\mathbf{H}_{w})=\mathbf{H}_{w^{-1}}^{-1}
. An element invariant under
d
is called
self-dual
.

There is another basis
\{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{w}\}_{w\in W}
called the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis whose elements are uniquely determined by two conditions: They are self-dual and

{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{w}=\mathbf{H}_{w}+\sum_{x<w}h_{x,w}(v)\mathbf{H}_{x},

(3.2)

for some polynomials
h_{x,w}(v)\in v\mathbb{Z}[v]
. These polynomials are the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials.

There is a recursive algorithm to compute this basis. Indeed, if
\mu(x,w)
denotes the coefficient of
v
in
h_{x,w}(v)
then

{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{w}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s}={\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{ws}+\sum_{xs<x<w}\mu(x,w){\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{x},

(3.3)

for all pairs
(w,s)\in W\times S
such that
w<ws
. Also, we have

{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{w}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s}=(v+v^{-1}){\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{w}

(3.4)

for all pairs
(w,s)\in W\times S
such that
w>ws
.

We recall the elements defined in the introduction for any
w\in W
,

\mathbf{N}_{w}=\sum_{x\leq w}v^{l(w)-l(x)}\mathbf{H}_{x}.

(3.5)

Definition 3.1
.

Given
w\in W
and
X\in\mathcal{H}
we denote by
G_{w}(X)
the coefficient of
\mathbf{H}_{w}
in
X
when it is written in terms of the standard basis. That is,

X=\sum_{w\in W}G_{w}(X)\mathbf{H}_{w}.

(3.6)

We also define the
content
of
X
by

c(X):=\sum_{w\in W}G_{w}(X)(1)\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.7)

Note that this implies that
c(\mathbf{N}_{w})=\left\lvert w\right\rvert
and also that
c(X{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s})=2c(X)
for any
X\in\mathcal{H}
and any
s\in S
, since

\mathbf{H}_{w}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{H}_{ws}+v\mathbf{H}_{w},&\text{if }w<ws;\\
\mathbf{H}_{ws}+v^{-1}\mathbf{H}_{w},&\text{if }w>ws.\end{cases}

(3.8)

Definition 3.2
.

Let
w\in W
. An element
H\in\mathcal{H}
is called
triangular of height

w
if
G_{w}(H)=1
and
G_{x}(H)=0
for
x\not\leq w
. Furthermore, a triangular element
H
of height
w
is called
monotonic
if
G_{x}(H)\in\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}]
for all
x\in W
and

G_{y}(H)-v^{l(x)-l(y)}G_{x}(H)\in\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}],

(3.9)

for all
y\leq x\leq w
.

A trivial example of a monotonic element is any
\mathbf{N}_{w}
. We also know that
{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{w}
is always monotonic (see
[
BM01
,
Pla17
]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
H\in\mathcal{H}
be a monotonic element of height
w
. Suppose that
ws>w
. Then,
H{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s}
is monotonic of height
ws
.

Proof.

It is clear that
H{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s}
is triangular of height
ws
. On the other hand, (
3.8
) shows that

G_{x}(H{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}vG_{x}(H)+G_{xs}(H),&\mbox{if }xs>x;\\
v^{-1}G_{x}(H)+G_{xs}(H),&\mbox{if }xs<x.\end{array}\right.

(3.10)

Using this, a simple case analysis shows that the monotonicity of
H
implies the monotonicity of
H{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s}
. ∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(W,S)
be an arbitrary Coxeter system. Let
w\in W
and
s\in S
such that
s\in D_{R}(w)
. Then,
\mathbf{N}_{w}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s}=(v+v^{-1})\mathbf{N}_{w}
.

Proof.

Since
ws<w
we can use the Lifting Property
[
BB06
, Proposition 2.2.7]
to conclude that multiplication on the right by
s
induces a permutation of the lower interval
[e,w]
. Therefore the result follows by (
3.10
).
∎

Definition 3.5
.

Let
p
and
q
in
\mathcal{A}
. We write
p\geq q
if
p-q\in\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}]
. Then for
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}
, we write
X\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}Y
if
G_{w}(X)\geq G_{w}(Y)
for all
w\in W
.

Notice that
X\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}Y
and
c(X)=c(Y)
implies
X=Y
. We claim no originality in this observation and refer to
[
LP20
]
for its first application in the context of computation of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials. In general, it is not an easy task to prove an inequality of the form
X\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}Y
. However, there are certain situations where we can make some simplifications. For instance, let us suppose that

Y=\sum_{w\in Z}p_{w}(v)\mathbf{N}_{w}

(3.11)

where
Z
is a finite subset of
W
and
p_{z}(v)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}p_{z}^{i}v^{i}\in\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}]
. For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
we define

Y_{i}=\sum_{w\in Z}p_{w}^{i-\ell(w)}\mathbf{N}_{w}.

(3.12)

Since the elements
Y_{i}
lie in different degrees (in the sense that
G_{u}(Y_{i})
and
G_{u}(Y_{j})
are monomials of different degree if
i\neq j
) in order to prove
X\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}Y
it is enough to show
X\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}Y_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. We stress that
Y_{i}=0
for all but finitely many integers. Henceforth, we refer to this simplification as “degree reasons.”

In order to prove inequalities of the form
X\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}Y_{i}
we can make some extra reductions if
X
is monotonic. For example, if
X
is monotonic and
Y_{i}
is made of just one
\mathbf{N}
-element, i.e.
Y_{i}=cv^{k}\mathbf{N}_{w}
, then in order to prove
X\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}Y_{i}
it is enough to check
G_{w}(X)\geq cv^{k}
. In contrast, if
Y_{i}
is made of two or more
\mathbf{N}
-elements then additional analysis of the relationship between the elements involved in
Y_{i}
in the Bruhat order is required.

We conclude this section with a useful result that allows us to rule out the occurrence of terms in the sum in (
3.3
). A proof can be found in
[
KL79
, (2.3.f)]
or
[
BB06
, Proposition 5.1.9]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
x,w\in W
such that
\mu(x,w)\neq 0
. Suppose that
l(w)-l(x)>1
. Then

D_{R}(w)\subseteq D_{R}(x)\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad D_{L}(w)\subseteq D_{L}(x).

In particular, if
s\not\in D_{R}(w)
and
{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{x}
appears in the expansion of
{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{w}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s}
when written in terms of the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis, then
D_{R}(w)\cup\{s\}\subseteq D_{R}(x)
. Similarly, if
s\not\in D_{L}(w)
and
{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{x}
appears in the expansion of
{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{w}
when written in terms of the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis, then
D_{L}(w)\cup\{s\}\subseteq D_{L}(x)
.

3.2
Multiplication formulas for
\mathbf{N}_{w}

In this section we collect several multiplicative formulas involving elements
\mathbf{N}_{w}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
m
and
n
be positive integers. Then,

\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}=\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)t_{m}}+v\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}}.

(3.13)

Proof.

First, let us prove that the contents on both sides of (
3.13
) coincide.
On the left side, Corollary
2.5
shows that

c(\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}})=2c(\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)})=\left\lvert\theta(m,n)\right\rvert=16(m^{2}+2m+4mn+2n^{2}+2n+1).

On the right side, Lemma
2.6
implies that

c(\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)t_{m}}+v\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}})=\left\lvert\theta(m,n)t_{m}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}\right\rvert=16(m^{2}+2m+4mn+2n^{2}+2n+1).

(3.14)

Thus we only need to show that

\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)t_{m}}+v\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}}.

(3.15)

By degree reasons it is enough to check

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)t_{m}},

(3.16)

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}v\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}}.

(3.17)

Furthermore, by the monotonicity of
\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}
ensured by Lemma
3.3
, inequalities (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) will follow from the inequalities

\displaystyle G_{\theta(m,n)t_{m}}(\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}})

\displaystyle\geq 1,

(3.18)

\displaystyle G_{\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}})

\displaystyle\geq v,

(3.19)

respectively. Inequality (
3.18
) is clear. On the other hand, a direct computation shows that

G_{\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}}(v^{2}\mathbf{H}_{\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}})=v,

(3.20)

which proves (
3.19
). The lemma is proved.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

and

or

is negative, for any

Lemma 3.9
.

and

be positive integers. We have

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{\theta(0,n)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{0}}

Furthermore,

For all

\mathbf{N}_{s_{0}\theta(0,0)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{0}}=\mathbf{N}_{s_{0}\theta(0,0)s_{0}}+v^{2}\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}s_{0}}
.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let

be a positive integer. We have

\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}s_{2}s_{0}\theta(m,0)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}=\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}s_{2}s_{0}\theta(m,0)t_{m}}+v\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}s_{2}s_{0}\theta(m-1,0)t_{m}^{\prime}}.

(3.25)

The following lemma is proved using essentially the same argument as the one used in the proof of Lemma
3.7
. This, however, is the first time so far in which we have a case where we must show

with

consisting of two or more
\mathbf{N}
-terms, which leads some extra subtleties in the analysis.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
m
and
n
be positive integers. Then,

\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{2}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}=2\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}+\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m+1,n)}+\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,n+1)}+\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m+1,n-1)}+\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,n)}.

(3.26)

Proof.

Let us denote by
L
and
R
the left-hand and right-hand side of (

), respectively. A repeated application of Corollary
2.5
shows that

c(L)=c(R)=64(m^{2}+2m+4mn+2n^{2}+2n+1).

(3.27)

Therefore, to finish the proof we only need to show that
L\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}R
. Lemma
3.7
implies

R=2\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)t_{m}}+2v\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}}+\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m+1,n)}+\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,n+1)}+\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m+1,n-1)}+\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,n)}.

(3.28)

Hence, by degree reasons, it is enough to check

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m+1,n)}

(3.29)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,n)}

(3.30)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,n+1)}+2\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,n)t_{m}}

(3.31)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m+1,n-1)}+2v\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}}

(3.32)

We now notice that
L
is monotonic of height

by Lemma

The above inequality together with the monotonicity of

proves (

We now prove (

for some positive integers

and

and

\begin{array}[]{rl}L&\overset{\mathbf{H}}{\geq}c_{1}v^{i}\mathbf{N}_{x},\\

Let us apply this general principle to prove (

). By the diagrammatic description of lower intervals given in §

we have

(\leq\theta(m-1,n+1))\cap(\leq\theta(m,n)t_{m})=(\leq\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}s_{2}t_{m}).

(3.36)

Therefore, we only need to prove

G_{\theta(m-1,n+1)}(L)\geq 1,\qquad G_{\theta(m,n)t_{m}}(L)\geq 2\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad G_{\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}s_{2}t_{m}}(L)\geq 3v.

).

\begin{split}G_{\theta(m-1,n+1)}(v\mathbf{H}_{\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime}s_{2}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{2}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}})&=1\\

The proof of (

(\leq\theta(m+1,n-1))\cap(\leq\theta(m-1,n)t_{m}^{\prime})=(\leq\theta(m-1,n))\cup(\leq\theta(m,n-1)t_{m}).

(3.38)

This description of the lower intervals allows one to apply the discussion leading up to (

); we leave the details to the reader.∎

Proposition 3.15
.

. We have

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m,0)}({\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{2}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}-2{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m+1,0)}+\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,1)}+(1+v^{2})\mathbf{N}_{\theta(m-1,0)},

Lemma 4.2

Theorem 4.5
.

For all
(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
we have
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m,n)}={\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{\theta(m,n)}
.

Proof.

By definition of
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m,n)}
it is clear that
G_{\theta(m,n)}(\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m,n)})=1
and that
G_{x}(\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m,n)})\in v\mathbb{N}[v]
for all
x<\theta(m,n)
. Therefore, in order to prove the theorem it is enough to show that
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m,n)}
is self-dual. To do this we proceed by induction on
m+n
. The result is clear for the pair
(0,0)
since
{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{\theta(0,0)}=\mathbf{N}_{\theta(0,0)}=\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(0,0)}
. We fix
u\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m,n)}
is self-dual for all pairs
(m,n)
such that
m+n\leq u
. By Lemma
4.3
we know that

\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m,n)}({\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{2}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}-2{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}})=\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m+1,n)}+\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m-1,n+1)}+\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m+1,n-1)}+\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m-1,n)}.

(4.18)

We notice that
{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{2}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}-2{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{t_{m}}
is self-dual. Also, the terms
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m,n)}
,
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m-1,n+1)}
,
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m+1,n-1)}
and
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m-1,n)}
are self-dual by our inductive hypothesis. We can thus conclude that
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(m+1,n)}
is self-dual as well. This shows that
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(a,b)}
is self-dual for all the pairs
(a,b)
with
a+b=u+1
and
a\neq 0
.

It remains to show that
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(0,u+1)}
is self-dual. By Lemma
4.4
we know that

\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(0,u)}({\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{0}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{2}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{1}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{2}}-2{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{0}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{2}}+1-(v+v^{-1})^{2})=\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(0,u+1)}+\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(2,u-1)}+\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(0,u-1)}.

(4.19)

We notice that
{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{0}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{2}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{1}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{2}}-2{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{0}}{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}_{s_{2}}+1-(v+v^{-1})^{2}
is self-dual and that
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(0,u-1)}
is also self-dual by our inductive hypothesis. Although the self-duality of
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(2,u-1)}
is not covered by our inductive hypothesis, we have already proved its self-duality since
(2,u-1)
is a pair
(a,b)
with
a\neq 0
. As before, we conclude that
\hat{{\underline{\mathbf{H}}}}_{\theta(0,u+1)}
is self-dual as well, and the theorem is proved.
∎

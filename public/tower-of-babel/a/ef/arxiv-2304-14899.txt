F_{m}\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
. But
G_{m}\setminus\displaystyle\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}{K_{j}}\in\mathcal{S}
and so by upper semicontinuity of
\{\mathcal{N}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
relative to
\mathcal{S}
,
\displaystyle\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}(G_{n}\setminus\displaystyle\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}{K_{j}})\notin\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
. Consequently, by (ii) of Definition
2.1
,
\displaystyle\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}{F_{n}}\notin\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
. Hence
[f_{n}]\not\to_{n}[f]
(w.r.t
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
).

Thus from what we have deduced above, it follows that

THEOREM
2.8
:
If
\mathcal{S}
is a
\sigma
-algebra on
X
admissible with respect to a
\omega
-system
\{\mathcal{N}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
which is upper semicontinuous relative to
\mathcal{S}
, then the
\sigma
-ideal
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
is uniformizable.

REMARKS:
We observed in
[12]
, that condition (*) does not imply countable chain condition (c.c.c). But in our case, in order to show that
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}/\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
satisfies (*) (Theorem
2.6
), we need countable chain condition in
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
as a part of our hypothesis. However, countable chain condition may be relaxed by virtue of upper semicontinuity. In fact, in proving Theorem
2.8
, we do not even need the full force of admissibility.

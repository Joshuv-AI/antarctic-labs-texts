More on semipositive tensor and tensor complementarity problem
R. Deb, A. K. Das

Abstract
In recent years several classes of structured matrices are extended to classes of tensors in the context of tensor complementarity problem. The tensor complementarity problem is a class of nonlinear complementarity problem where the involved functions are special polynomials defined by a tensor. Semipositive and strictly semipositive tensors play an important role in the study of the tensor complementarity problem. The article considers some important properties of semipositive tensor. We establish invariance property of semipositive tensor. We prove necessary and sufficient conditions for a tensor to be semipositive tensor. A relation between even order row diagonal semipositive tensor and its majorization matrix is proposed. Keywords: Tensor complementarity problem, semipositive tensor, semimonotone matrix, null vector, majorization matrix. AMS subject classifications: 90C33, 47H60, 15A69, 46G25.

At the outset, we define semipositive tensor along with an example. Subsequently, we establish the invariance property of semipositive tensor with respect to diagonal matrix as well as permutation matrix.

[

Here we give an example of a semipositive tensor.

example 3.1
:

Let

such that
m_{1211}=1,\;m_{1233}=2,\;m_{1323}=-1,\;m_{1223}=-3,\;m_{1232}=4,\;m_{2211}=1,\;m_{2223}=-3,\;m_{2322}=5,\;m_{3232}=-1,\;m_{3322}=-3,\;m_{3313}=-2
and all other entries of
\mathscr{M}
are zero. For
u=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\\
u_{3}\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
we have
\mathscr{M}u^{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{1}^{2}u_{2}+u_{2}u_{3}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}u_{3}\\

-2u_{1}u_{3}^{2}-4u_{2}^{2}u_{3}\end{array}\right).

Then

theorem 3.1
:

Let
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
and
D\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a positive diagonal matrix. Then
\mathscr{M}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor if and only if
D\mathscr{M}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor.

Proof.

Let
D=diag(d_{1},...,d_{n})
be a positive diagonal matrix. Then the diagonal entries
d_{i}>0,\;\forall\;i\in I_{n}.
Suppose
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
is a (strictly) semipositive tensor. Then for
0\neq u\geq 0\;\exists
an index
l
such that
u_{l}>0
and
(\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{l}\geq 0\;(>0).
We show that
D\mathscr{M}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor. Since
d_{l}>0
we obtain
(D\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{l}=d_{l}(\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{l}\geq 0\;(>0).
Thus
D\mathscr{M}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor.

Conversely, suppose
D\mathscr{M}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor. Now
D
is positive diagonal matrix so
D^{-1}
exists and
D^{-1}
is again a positive diagonal matrix. Therefore
D^{-1}(D\mathscr{M})=\mathscr{M}
is a (strictly) semipostive tensor.
∎

corollary 3.1
:

Let
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
be a semipositive tensor and
D\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a nonnegative diagonal matrix. Then
D\mathscr{M}
is semipositive tensor.

remark 3.1
:

The converse of the above corollary is not true in general.

Clearly for

theorem 3.2
:

Let
P\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a permutation matrix also let
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}.
Then the tensor
\mathscr{M}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor if and only if
P\mathscr{M}P^{T}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
be a (strictly) semipositive tensor. Let

P\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a permutation matrix such that for any vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(Pv)_{k}=v_{\sigma(k)}
where
\sigma
is a permutation on the set of indices
I_{n}.
Let
0\neq u\geq 0
where
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Let
v=P^{T}u.
Then
v\geq 0.
As
\mathscr{M}
is a (strictly) semipositive tensor
\exists\;l
such that
v_{l}>0
and
(\mathscr{M}v^{r-1})_{l}\geq 0\;(>0).
Thus
(P\mathscr{M}P^{T}u^{r-1})_{\sigma(l)}=(\mathscr{M}P^{T}u^{r-1})_{l}=(\mathscr{M}v^{r-1})_{l}\geq 0\;(>0).
Also
u_{\sigma(l)}=(P^{T}u)_{l}=v_{l}>0.
Therefore
P\mathscr{M}P^{T}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor.

Conversely, suppose
P\mathscr{M}P^{T}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor. We have
\mathscr{M}=P(P\mathscr{M}P^{T})P^{T}
, since
P
is a permutation matrix. Hence by the forward part of the proof
\mathscr{M}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor.
∎

Now we prove the necessary and sufficient conditions for a tensor to be semipositive tensor.

theorem 3.3
:

Let
\mathscr{M},\mathscr{N}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
where
\mathscr{M}
is a (strictly) semipositive tensor and
\mathscr{N}\geq\mathscr{O}
. Then
\mathscr{M}+\mathscr{N}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor. Then for
0\neq u\geq 0\;\exists\;l\in I_{n}
such that
u_{l}>0
and
(\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{l}\geq 0\;(>0).
Let
\mathscr{N}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
where
\mathscr{N}\geq\mathscr{O}.
Then for
0\neq u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
we have
(\mathscr{N}u^{r-1})_{i}\geq 0,\;\forall\;i\in I_{n}.
Therefore
((\mathscr{M}+\mathscr{N})u^{r-1})_{l}=(\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{l}+(\mathscr{N}u^{r-1})_{l}\geq 0\;(>0).
Hence,
\mathscr{M}+\mathscr{N}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor.
∎

Here we discuss a necessary and sufficient condition for (strictly) semipositive tensor.

theorem 3.4
:

Let
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
be a tensor such that all its proper principal subtensors are (strictly) semipositive tensor.
\mathscr{M}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor if and only if for all diagonal tensors
\mathscr{D}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
with
\mathscr{D}>\mathscr{O}\;(\geq\mathscr{O})
, the tensor
\mathscr{M}+\mathscr{D}
does not have a positive null vector.

Proof.

Let there be a diagonal tensor
\mathscr{D}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
with
\mathscr{D}>\mathscr{O}\;(\geq\mathscr{O})
such that
\mathscr{M}+\mathscr{D}
has a positive null vector. i.e., There exists
0<u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle(\mathscr{M}+\mathscr{D})u^{r-1}=0

\displaystyle\implies\mathscr{M}u^{r-1}+\mathscr{D}u^{r-1}=0

\displaystyle\implies\mathscr{M}u^{r-1}=-\mathscr{D}u^{r-1}<0\;(\leq 0),

which shows that
\mathscr{M}
is not (strictly) semipositive tensor.

Conversely, suppose
\mathscr{M}
is not (strictly) semipositive tensor. We show that there exists a diagonal tensor
\mathscr{D}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
with
\mathscr{D}>\mathscr{O}\;(\geq\mathscr{O})
such that the tensor
\mathscr{M}+\mathscr{D}
has a positive null vector. Since
\mathscr{M}
is not (strictly) semipositive tensor (but all proper principal subtensors are),
\exists\;u>0
such that
\mathscr{M}u^{r-1}<0\;(\leq 0).
Now we construct diagonal tensor
\mathscr{D}
with diagonal entries being defined as
d_{i...i}=-\frac{(\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{i}}{u_{i}^{r-1}}.
Then
\mathscr{D}>\mathscr{O}\;(\geq\mathscr{O})
and
\mathscr{M}u^{r-1}=-\mathscr{D}u^{r-1}.
Therefore
\mathscr{M}u^{r-1}+\mathscr{D}u^{r-1}=(\mathscr{M}+\mathscr{D})u^{r-1}=0.
Thus
\mathscr{M}+\mathscr{D}
has a positive null vector, since
u>0.

∎

Here we propose a necessary and sufficient condition for a semipositive tensor to be a strictly semipositive tensor.

theorem 3.5
:

A tensor
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
is semipositive tensor if and only if for every
\delta>0,
the tensor
\mathscr{M}+\delta\mathscr{I}
is strictly semipositive tensor.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
be a semipositive tensor. The for every
0\neq u\geq 0,\;\exists
an index
l
such that
u_{l}>0
and
(\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{l}\geq 0.
Therefore
[(\mathscr{M}+\delta\mathscr{I})u^{r-1}]_{l}=(\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{l}+\delta u^{r-1}_{l}>0.
Thus
\mathscr{M}+\delta\mathscr{I}
is a strictly semipositive tensor.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathscr{M}+\delta\mathscr{I}
is a strictly semipositive tensor for each
\delta>0.
For arbitrarily chosen
0\neq u\geq 0
consider a sequence
\{\delta_{k}\}
, where
\delta_{k}>0
and
\delta_{k}
converges to zero. Then for each
k,\;\exists\;l_{k}\in I_{n}
such that
u_{l_{k}}>0
and
[(\mathscr{M}+\delta\mathscr{I})u^{r-1}]_{l_{k}}>0.
This implies
\exists\;l\in I_{n}
such that
u_{l}>0
and
(\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{l}+\delta_{k}u^{r-1}_{l}=[(\mathscr{M}+\delta_{k}\mathscr{I})u^{r-1}]_{l}>0
for infinitely many
\delta_{k}.
Since
\delta_{k}\rightarrow 0
when
k\rightarrow\infty
we conclude that
\mathscr{M}u^{r-1}_{l}\geq 0.
Hence
\mathscr{M}
is semipositive tensor.
∎

Let
I
denote the identity matrix of order
n
and
[0,I]
denote all
n\times n
diagonal matrices whose diagonal entries are in
[0,1].
Now we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for strictly semipositive tensor.

theorem 3.6
:

Let
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
and
r
is even.
\mathscr{M}
is strictly semipositive tensor if and only if
\forall\;G\in[0,I]
the tensor
G\mathscr{I}+(I−G)\mathscr{M}
has no nonzero nonnegative null vector.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a strictly semipositive tensor and
\forall\;G\in[0,I],\;\exists\;0\neq u\geq 0
which is a null vector of the tensor
G\mathscr{I}+(I−G)\mathscr{M}.
i.e.
(G\mathscr{I}+(I−G)\mathscr{M})u^{r-1}=0.
Let
v=\mathscr{M}u^{r-1}.
As
\mathscr{M}
is strictly semipositive tensor
\exists\;l\in I_{n}
such that
u_{l}>0
and
v_{l}=(\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{l}>0.
Now

\displaystyle[G\mathscr{I}+(I-G)\mathscr{M}]u^{r-1}=0

\displaystyle\implies g_{ll}u_{l}^{r-1}+(1-g_{ll})(\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{l}=0

\displaystyle\implies g_{ll}(u_{l}^{r-1}-v_{l})=-v_{l}.

Note that if
u_{l}^{r-1}=v_{l}
then
v_{l}=0
which contradicts the fact that
v_{l}>0.
Therefore
u_{l}^{r-1}-v_{l}\neq 0
and
g_{ll}=\frac{-v_{l}}{u_{l}^{r-1}-v_{l}}.
Now if
u_{l}^{r-1}-v_{l}>0
then we have
-v_{l}\geq 0,
since
g_{ll}\geq 0.
Therefore
v_{l}\leq 0,
which is again a contradiction, since
v_{l}>0
.
Let
u_{l}^{r-1}-v_{l}<0.
Now since
g_{ll}\leq 1,
we have
\frac{-v_{l}}{u_{l}^{r-1}-v_{l}}\leq 1\implies u_{l}^{r-1}-v_{l}\leq-v_{l}\implies u_{l}\leq 0
(since
r
is even) which contradicts the fact that
u_{l}>0.
Hence no nonzero nonnegative null vector exists.

Conversely, let
\mathscr{M}
be not a strictly semipositive tensor. Then
\exists\;0\neq u\geq 0
such that
u_{i}(\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{i}\leq 0\;\forall\;i\in I_{n}.
Let
v=\mathscr{M}u^{r-1}.
Then for
u_{l}>0
we have either
v_{l}=0
or
v_{l}<0.
Note that if
u_{l}=0,
then there are three choices for
v_{l}
, they are
v_{l}=0,\;v_{l}>0\text{ or }v_{l}<0.
Now we define diagonal a matrix
G
with the diagonal elements as

g_{ii}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\text{if }u_{i}>0,\;v_{i}=0\\
\frac{-v_{i}}{u_{i}^{r-1}-v_{i}}&\text{if }u_{i}>0,\;v_{i}<0\\
0&\text{if }u_{i}=0,\;v_{i}=0\\
1&\text{if }u_{i}=0,\;v_{i}\lessgtr 0\end{array}\right..

Note that if
u_{i}>0
and
v_{i}<0,
then
0\leq\frac{-v_{i}}{u_{i}^{r-1}-v_{i}}\leq 1.
If not, then there are two possibilities either
\frac{-v_{i}}{u_{i}^{r-1}-v_{i}}<0
or
\frac{-v_{i}}{u_{i}^{r-1}-v_{i}}>1.
Now
\frac{-v_{i}}{u_{i}^{r-1}-v_{i}}<0
implies
v_{i}>0
, since
u_{i}^{r-1}-v_{i}>0.
This leads to a contradiction.
Again if
\frac{-v_{i}}{u_{i}^{r-1}-v_{i}}>1
then we have
-v_{i}>u_{i}^{r-1}-v_{i}\implies u_{i}^{r-1}<0,
which is again a contradiction. Thus we have
0\leq g_{ii}\leq 1
and
[(I-G)\mathscr{M}u^{r-1}]_{i}=(1-g_{ii})v_{i}=-g_{ii}u_{i}^{r-1}=-(G\mathscr{I}u^{r-1})_{i}.
Therefore
[G\mathscr{I}+(I-G)\mathscr{M}]u^{r-1}=0.
i.e. There exists a diagonal matrix
G
with diagonal entries from
[0,1]
such that the tensor
[G\mathscr{I}+(I-G)\mathscr{M}]
has a nonzero nonnegative null vector.
∎

Here we establish a relation between an even order row diagonal (strictly) semipositive tensor and its majorization matrix.

theorem 3.7
:

Let
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
be a row diagonal tensor of even order.
\mathscr{M}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor if and only if
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a (strictly) semimonotone matrix.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
be an even order row diagonal tensor. i.e.,
r
is even. Let
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be the majorization matrix of the tensor
\mathscr{M}.
Then for
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have
\mathscr{M}u^{r-1}=\tilde{\mathscr{M}}\mathscr{I}u^{r-1}=\tilde{\mathscr{M}}u^{[r-1]}.
Let
\mathscr{M}
be a (strictly) semipositive tensor. We prove that
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}
is (strictly) semimonotone matrix. Now for
0\neq v\geq 0,

v^{[\frac{1}{r-1}]}
exists uniquely with
0\neq v^{[\frac{1}{r-1}]}\geq 0,
since
r
is even. Let
u=v^{[\frac{1}{r-1}]}.
Since
\mathscr{M}
is a (strictly) semipositive tensor, for
0\neq u\geq 0\;\exists\;l
such that
u_{l}>0
and
(\mathscr{M}u^{r-1})_{l}=(\tilde{\mathscr{M}}u^{[r-1]})_{l}\geq 0\;(>0)
This implies
u_{l}^{r-1}>0
and
(\tilde{\mathscr{M}}u^{[r-1]})_{l}\geq 0\;(>0).
i.e., For
0\neq v\geq 0\;\exists\;l
such that
v_{l}=u_{l}^{r-1}>0
and
(\tilde{\mathscr{M}}v)_{l}=(\tilde{\mathscr{M}}u^{[r-1]})_{l}\geq 0\;(>0).
This implies that
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}
is a (strictly) semimonotone matrix.

Conversely, let
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}
be a (strictly) semimonotone matrix. Since
\mathscr{M}=\tilde{\mathscr{M}}\mathscr{I},
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

(\mathscr{M}x^{r-1})_{i}=(\tilde{\mathscr{M}}x^{[r-1]})i,\;\forall\;i\in I_{n}.

(3.1)

We choose
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
0\neq x\geq 0
and construct the vector
y=x^{[r-1]}.
Then
0\neq y\geq 0.
Since
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}
is a (strictly) semimonotone matrix
\exists\;t\in I_{n}
such that
y_{t}>0
and
(\tilde{\mathscr{M}}y)_{t}\geq 0\;(>0).
i.e., For
0\neq x\geq 0\;\exists\;t\in I_{n}
such that
x^{r-1}_{t}>0
and
(\mathscr{M}x^{r-1})_{t}\geq 0\;(>0)
by equation
3.1
. Therefore for
0\neq x\geq 0\;\exists\;t\in I_{n}
such that
x_{t}>0
(since
r
is even) and
(\mathscr{M}x^{r-1})_{t}\geq 0\;(>0).
Hence
\mathscr{M}
is (strictly) semipositive tensor.
∎

Not all even order semipositive tensors are row diagonal. Here we give an example of a semipositive tensor which is not row diagonal tensor.

example 3.3
:

Let
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{4,3}
be such that
m_{1122}=2,\;m_{1131}=2,\;m_{2211}=1,\;m_{2112}=-4,\;m_{2322}=-1,\;m_{3232}=-1,\;m_{3322}=1,\;m_{3313}=3
and all other entries of
\mathscr{M}
are zero. Then for
u=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\\
u_{3}\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
we have
\mathscr{M}u^{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2u_{1}u_{2}^{2}+2u_{1}^{2}u_{3}\\
-3u_{1}^{2}u_{2}-u_{2}^{2}u_{3}\\
3u_{1}u_{3}^{2}\end{array}\right).

The tensor
\mathscr{M}
is a semipositive tensor of even order but
\mathscr{M}
is not a row diagonal tensor.

Conclusion

In this article, we establish some tensor theoretic properties of semipositive tensor and strictly semipositive tensor. We show invariance property of (strictly) semipositive tensor with respect to diagonal matrix and permutation matrix. We show that
\mathscr{M}\in\mathbb{T}_{r,n}
is semipositive tensor if and only if for every
\delta>0,
the tensor
\mathscr{M}+\delta\mathscr{I}
is strictly semipositive tensor. We propose a necessary and sufficient conditions for (strictly) semipositive tensor. Furthermore, we prove that an even order row diagonal tensor is (strictly) semipositive tensor if and only if its majorization matrix is (strictly) semimonotone matrix.

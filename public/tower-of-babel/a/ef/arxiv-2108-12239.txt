Geometric Models for (Temporally) Attributed Description Logics
Camille Bourgaux 1, Ana Ozaki 2, Jeff Z. Pan 3

Abstract
In the search for knowledge graph embeddings that could capture ontological knowledge, geometric models of existential rules have been recently introduced. It has been shown that convex geometric regions capture the so-called quasi-chained rules. Attributed description logics (DL) have been defined to bridge the gap between DL languages and knowledge graphs, whose facts often come with various kinds of annotations that may need to be taken into account for reasoning. In particular, temporally attributed DLs are enriched by specific attributes whose semantics allows for some temporal reasoning. Considering that geometric models and (temporally) attributed DLs are promising tools designed for knowledge graphs, this paper investigates their compatibility, focusing on the attributed version of a Horn dialect of the DL-Lite family. We first adapt the definition of geometric models to attributed DLs and show that every satisfiable ontology has a convex geometric model. Our second contribution is a study of the impact of temporal attributes. We show that a temporally attributed DL may not have a convex geometric model in general but we can recover geometric satisfiability by imposing some restrictions on the use of the temporal attributes.

Introduction

Knowledge graph embeddings
are
popular latent representations of knowledge graphs (KG). In the search for KG embeddings that could capture ontological knowledge (i.e., schema of KG),
geometric models of existential rules have been recently introduced
[
13
]
.
Such models have several advantages. Notably, they ensure that facts which are valid in the embedding are logically consistent and deductively closed w.r.t. the ontology, and they can also be used to find plausible missing ontology rules.
It has been shown that convex geometric regions capture the so-called quasi-chained rules, a fragment
of first-order Horn logic.
Attributed description logics (DL) have been defined to bridge the gap between DL ontology languages and KG,
whose facts often come with various kinds of annotations that may need to be taken into account for reasoning.
In particular, they were introduced as a formalism for dealing with the
meta-knowledge present in KG, such as temporal validity, provenance, language, and others
[
16
,
17
,
8
]
.
As time is of primary interest in KG, attributed DLs have been enriched with temporal attributes,
whose semantics allows for some temporal reasoning over discrete time
[
22
]
.
Considering that geometric models and (temporally) attributed DLs are promising tools designed for KG, this paper investigates their compatibility, focusing on the attributed version of a Horn dialect of the DL-Lite family.

Our contributions are as follows:

•

We adapt the notion of geometric models for ((temporally) attributed) DLs; in particular, we use an arbitrary linear map to combine the individual geometric interpretations instead of restricting ourselves to vector concatenation, and define satisfaction of concept or role inclusions directly based on geometric inclusion relationship between the regions that interpret the concepts or roles.

•

We show that every satisfiable attributed DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathit{horn}}
ontology has a convex geometric model but
there are satisfiable
temporally
attributed DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathit{horn}}
ontologies without such a model.

•

We exhibit restrictions on the use of temporal attributes that guarantee that temporally attributed
DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathit{horn}}
ontologies have a convex geometric model.

We define attributed DLs and geometric models in Section
2
.
Then, in Section
3
, we study the relationship between satisfiability
and the existence of convex geometric models in DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathit{horn}}
.
We then extend our analysis for temporally attributed DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathit{horn}}
in Section
4
.
In Section

and we conclude in Section
6
.
Omitted proofs are given in the appendix.

2.2
Geometric Models

We now define the geometric interpretations of attributed relations.
Let

(Geometric Interpretation)

,

•

Moreover, for all

and

,

then
\eta(E@S)\subseteq\eta(E@T)
.
We say that
\eta
is

if, for every

, every ground

S\in\mathbf{S}

\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}_{2}\in\eta(E@S)

(1-\lambda)\boldsymbol{v}_{1}+\lambda\boldsymbol{v}_{2}\in\eta(E@S)

The interpretation of ground complex concept or role expressions is as follows.
Assume all specifiers occurring in expressions below are ground,
R\in\mathsf{N_{R}}
,
P
is a role, and

are basic concepts.
Then,

•

\eta(R^{-}@S)\coloneqq\{f(\delta,\delta^{\prime})\mid f(\delta^{\prime},\delta)\in\eta(R@S)\}

•

\eta(\neg P)\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{2\cdot m}\setminus\eta(P)
,

•

,

•

\eta(\bigsqcap_{i=1}^{n}B_{i})\coloneqq\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\eta(B_{i})

We may omit “of

3
Satisfiability and Convex Geometric Models

We start by recalling some definitions and
results on geometric interpretations of an ontology
containing existential rules
[
13
]
.
An existential rule is an expression of the form
B_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge B_{n}\rightarrow\exists X_{1},\dots,X_{j}.H
where
n\geq 0
, the
B_{i}
’s and
H
are atoms built from sets of predicates, constants and variables, and the
X_{i}
’s are variables. A negative constraint is a rule whose head is
\bot
. An existential rule or negative constraint is
quasi-chained
if for all
1\leq i\leq n
,
|({\sf vars}(B_{1})\cup\dots\cup{\sf vars}(B_{i-1}))\cap{\sf vars}(B_{i})|\leq 1
, where
{\sf vars}(B)
denotes the variables that occur in
B
.
It is easy to see that a DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathit{horn}}
ontology without negative role inclusions can be translated into a quasi-chained ontology. Negative role inclusions are not quasi-chained: their translation to rules is indeed of the form
P_{1}(x,y)\wedge P_{2}(x,y)\rightarrow\bot
where the body atoms share two variables.
A (standard) model
\mathcal{M}
of an existential rules ontology
\mathcal{K}
is a set of facts that contains all facts from
\mathcal{K}
and satisfies all existential rules from
\mathcal{K}
. In this setting, for every fact
\alpha
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{M}
iff
\mathcal{M}\models\alpha
.

Given a set
\mathcal{R}
of relation names and a set
X
of constants and labelled nulls, a
m
-dimensional geometric interpretation
\eta
of
(\mathcal{R},X)
assigns to each
k
-ary relation
R
from
\mathcal{R}
a region
\eta(R)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k.m}
and to each object
o
from
X
a vector
\eta(o)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Tuples of individuals are interpreted using vectors concatenation, which plays the role of the linear map

we use to interpret a pair of individuals:
for every
R\in\mathcal{R}
and
o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\in X

\eta\models R(o_{1},\dots,o_{k})
if
\eta(o_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus\eta(o_{k})\in\eta(R)
.
The authors define

\Phi(\eta)=\{R(o_{1},\dots,o_{k})\mid R\in\mathcal{R},o_{1},\dots,o_{k}\in X,\eta\models R(o_{1},\dots,o_{k})\}.

Proposition 3 in
[
13
]
states that if
\mathcal{K}
is a quasi-chained ontology and
\mathcal{M}
is a finite model of

, then
\mathcal{K}
has a convex geometric model
\eta
such that

.
This transfers to the DL setting as follows. Let
\mathcal{O}
be a quasi-chained DL ontology and
\mathcal{J}
be a finite

\mathcal{O}
such that
\Delta^{\mathcal{J}}=\mathsf{N_{i}}

for every

(which implies that for every concept

Then
\mathcal{O}
has a convex geometric model
\eta
such that

\{E(\boldsymbol{t})\mid\eta\models E(\boldsymbol{t})\}=\{E(\boldsymbol{t})\mid\mathcal{J}\models E(\boldsymbol{t})\},

where
E\in\mathsf{N_{C}}\cup\mathsf{N_{R}}

and
\boldsymbol{t}
is a tuple with the arity of
E
. In the following, we may similarly
write

to refer to an atom of the form

or

Theorem 3.1

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a satisfiable DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H},@}_{\mathit{horn}}
ontology
without negative role inclusions.

\mathcal{O}
has a convex geometric model.

Proof
(Sketch)

We use grounding to translate

Since

]
to get a convex

.

4
Adding Time

In this section, we discuss the ability of convex geometric models to capture temporally attributed DLs.
We show that we need to restrict the expressivity of the temporal ontology to get a convex geometric model.

We introduce temporally attributed DLs by defining temporally attributed DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathit{horn}}
, called DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H},\mathbb{T},@}_{\mathit{horn}}
,
as in
[
22
]
.
The description logic DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H},\mathbb{T},@}_{\mathit{horn}}
is defined as a multi-sorted version of DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H},@}_{\mathit{horn}}
,
where time points and intervals are seen as datatypes.

Time points
are elements of
\mathsf{N_{T}}
, and
time intervals
are elements of

\mathsf{N^{2}_{T}}
.
These sets and the set of
(abstract) individual names

\mathsf{N_{I}}
are mutually disjoint.
Time points are represented in a discrete manner by natural numbers, and we assume that
elements of
\mathsf{N_{T}}
(
\mathsf{N^{2}_{T}}
) are (pairs of) numbers.
A pair of numbers
k,\ell
in
\mathsf{N^{2}_{T}}
is denoted
[k,\ell]
.

The annotation
names:
{\sf time},{\sf before},{\sf after},{\sf until},{\sf since},{\sf during},{\sf between}\in\mathsf{N_{a}}
are called
temporal attributes
and have their own semantics.
Basically,
{\sf time}
is used to mark a point in time and
{\sf before}
and
{\sf after}
refer to
some
point in the past and in the future, respectively. The temporal attributes
{\sf until}
and
{\sf since}
refer to
all
points in the past and all point in the future (e.g.
{\sf since}

2020
the KR conference became an annual event).
Finally,
{\sf during}
is an interval which represents
a period of time (it refers to all points in the interval) and
{\sf between}
is an interval
of uncertainty for when an event happened.
The
value type
of
{\sf time},{\sf before},{\sf after},{\sf until},{\sf since}
is
\mathsf{N_{T}}
, while
the value type of
{\sf during},{\sf between}
is
\mathsf{N^{2}_{T}}
.
We write
{\sf valtype}(a)
to refer to the value type of
the annotation name
a
. Object variables are now taken from pairwise disjoint sets
{\sf Var}(\mathsf{N_{a}})
,

{\sf Var}(\mathsf{N_{T}})
, and
{\sf Var}(\mathsf{N^{2}_{T}})
.

Annotation set specifiers are defined as in Section
2.1
with
the difference that for each

a_{i}\in\mathsf{N_{a}}
and each
v_{i}
in attribute value pair
a_{i}:v_{i}

we require compatibility between the value type of its attribute, that is:

•

v_{i}\in{\sf valtype}(a_{i})\cup{\sf Var}({\sf valtype}(a_{i}))
,
or

•

v_{i}=[v,w]
with
{\sf valtype}(a_{i})=\mathsf{N^{2}_{T}}

and
v,w
in
\mathsf{N_{T}}\cup{\sf Var}(\mathsf{N_{T}})
, or

•

v_{i}=X.b
with
X\in\mathsf{N_{U}}
,
b\in\mathsf{N_{a}}
, and
{\sf valtype}(a_{i})={\sf valtype}(b)
.

A
time-sorted interpretation

\mathcal{I}=({\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\sf i},\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\sf a},\cdot^{\mathcal{I}}})

is an interpretation with a domain
\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\sf a}
that is a disjoint union of

\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{A}\cup\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{T}\cup\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{2T}
, where
\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{A}

is the
abstract domain of annotations
,

\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{T}
(the
temporal domain
) is
a finite
or infinite interval,
and
\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{2T}=\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{T}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{T}
.
We interpret individual names in
\mathsf{N_{i}}
as elements in
\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\sf i}
;
annotation names in
\mathsf{N_{a}}
as elements in
\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{A}
;
time points
t\in\mathsf{N_{T}}
as
t^{\mathcal{I}}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{T}
;
and intervals
[t,t^{\prime}]\in\mathsf{N^{2}_{T}}
as
[t,t^{\prime}]^{\mathcal{I}}=(t^{\mathcal{I}},t^{\prime\mathcal{I}})\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{2T}
.
A pair
({\delta,\epsilon})\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{A}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\sf a}
is
well-typed
,
if:

1.

\delta=a^{\mathcal{I}}
for an attribute ‘
a
’ of value type
\mathsf{N_{T}}

and
\epsilon\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{T}
; or

2.

\delta=a^{\mathcal{I}}
for an attribute ‘
a
’ of value type
\mathsf{N^{2}_{T}}
and
\epsilon\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{2T}
; or

3.

\delta=a^{\mathcal{I}}
for an attribute ‘
a
’ of value type
\mathsf{N_{a}}
and
\epsilon\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{A}
.

Let
\Phi^{\mathcal{I}}
be the set of all finite sets of well-typed pairs.
The function
\cdot^{\mathcal{I}}
maps concept names
A\in\mathsf{N_{C}}
to
A^{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\sf i}\times\Phi^{\mathcal{I}}
and role names

R\in\mathsf{N_{R}}
to
R^{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\sf i}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\sf i}\times\Phi^{\mathcal{I}}
.
The semantics of terms is given by variable assignments, which
for a time-sorted interpretation
\mathcal{I}
is defined as a function
\mathcal{Z}
that maps

•

set variables
X\in\mathsf{N_{U}}
to finite binary relations
\mathcal{Z}(X)\in\Phi^{\mathcal{I}}
, and

•

object variables
x\in{\sf Var}(\mathsf{N_{I}})\cup{\sf Var}(\mathsf{N_{T}})\cup{\sf Var}(\mathsf{N^{2}_{T}})

to elements
\mathcal{Z}(x)\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{I}\cup\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{T}\cup\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{2T}
(respecting their types).

For (set or object) variables
x
, we define
x^{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{Z}}\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}(x)
, and
for abstract individuals, time points, or time intervals

, we define

.
The semantics of specifiers is as in Section
2.1

with the difference that values can also be time points and intervals:

•

[a\kern 0.6pt{:}\,v]^{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{Z}}\coloneqq\{\{({a^{\mathcal{I}},v^{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{Z}}})\}\}
,
with
v\in\mathsf{valtype}(a)\cup{\sf Var}(\mathsf{valtype}(a))
;

•

[a\kern 0.6pt{:}\,[v,w]]^{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{Z}}\coloneqq\{\{({a^{\mathcal{I}},(v^{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{Z}},w^{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{Z}})})\}\}
,
with
\mathsf{valtype}(a)=\mathsf{N^{2}_{T}}
,
and

v,w\in\mathsf{N_{T}}\cup{\sf Var}(\mathsf{N_{T}})
.

We are now ready to formally define the semantics of temporal attributes.

Consider a temporal domain

A temporal

(Geometric Model)

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a DL-Lite

An

-geometric interpretation

of

-dimensional

-geometric interpretations and

Example
1
shows that even if temporal specifiers are only of the form
{\sf time}
and
{\sf during}
, convex geometric models may not exist for satisfiable DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H},\mathbb{T},@}_{\mathit{horn}}
ontologies.

Example 1

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=

\displaystyle\{\exists R@[{\sf during}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,[1,2]]\sqsubseteq A,\exists R@[{\sf time}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,1]\sqcap A\sqsubseteq\bot,

\displaystyle R(a,a)@[{\sf time}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,1],R(b,b)@[{\sf time}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,1],R(a,b)@[{\sf time}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,2],R(b,a)@[{\sf time}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,2]\}

and let
\eta
be a convex
f
-geometric model of
\mathcal{O}
.
Let
\delta=0.5\eta(a)+0.5\eta(b)
. By the convexity of
\eta(R@[{\sf time}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,1])
and
\eta(R@[{\sf time}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,2])
,
we have that

\displaystyle f(\delta,\delta)\in\eta(R@[{\sf time}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,1])\text{ and }f(\delta,\delta)\in\eta(R@[{\sf time}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,2]),

so
\mathcal{I}(\eta,\mathsf{\mathsf{N}^{\star}_{\mathnormal{\mathcal{O}}}})\models R(\delta,\delta)@[{\sf time}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,1]
and
\mathcal{I}(\eta,\mathsf{\mathsf{N}^{\star}_{\mathnormal{\mathcal{O}}}})\models R(\delta,\delta)@[{\sf time}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,2]
.
It follows that
\mathcal{I}(\eta,\mathsf{\mathsf{N}^{\star}_{\mathnormal{\mathcal{O}}}})\models R(\delta,\delta)@[{\sf during}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,[1,2]]
.
Since
\mathcal{I}(\eta,\mathsf{\mathsf{N}^{\star}_{\mathnormal{\mathcal{O}}}})\models R@[{\sf during}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,[1,2]]\sqsubseteq A
, we also have that
\mathcal{I}(\eta,\mathsf{\mathsf{N}^{\star}_{\mathnormal{\mathcal{O}}}})\models A(\delta)
.
Hence
\mathcal{I}(\eta,\mathsf{\mathsf{N}^{\star}_{\mathnormal{\mathcal{O}}}})\not\models\exists R@[{\sf time}\kern 0.6pt{:}\,1]\sqcap A\sqsubseteq\bot
. This means that

\mathcal{I}(\eta,\mathsf{\mathsf{N}^{\star}_{\mathnormal{\mathcal{O}}}})
is not a model of
\mathcal{O}
.
\triangleleft

To overcome this problem, we introduce a restriction on the specifiers allowed on roles.
We introduce
atemporal specifiers
. An atemporal specifier is a specifier
S
that can only be interpreted as a set
S^{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq\Phi^{\mathcal{I}}
of matching annotation sets that do not contain any temporal attribute.

To show that convex geometric models can capture some DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H},\mathbb{T},@}_{\mathit{horn}}
ontologies, we use concept inclusions with conjunctions on the left-hand side, which can be expressed in DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathit{horn}}
.
The following example shows that adding conjunction in role inclusions may however lead to satisfiable ontologies not having a convex model, even for plain DLs.

Example 2

Assume role conjunctions are allowed in the ontology.
Let

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{O}=\{R_{1}\sqcap R_{2}\sqsubseteq R_{3},\exists R_{1}\sqsubseteq A,\exists R_{3}\sqcap A\sqsubseteq\bot,R_{1}(a,a),R_{1}(b,b),R_{2}(a,b),R_{2}(b,a)\}\end{array}

and let
\eta
be a convex
f
-geometric model of
\mathcal{O}
.
Let
\delta=0.5\eta(a)+0.5\eta(b)
. By the convexity of
\eta(R_{1})
and
\eta(R_{2})
, we have that

\displaystyle f(\delta,\delta)\in\eta(R_{1})\text{ and }f(\delta,\delta)\in\eta(R_{2}),\text{ hence }f(\delta,\delta)\in\eta(R_{1}\sqcap R_{2}).

Then since
\eta
is a model of
R_{1}\sqcap R_{2}\sqsubseteq R_{3}
,
f(\delta,\delta)\in\eta(R_{3})
so
\delta\in\eta(\exists R_{3})
. Moreover, since
\eta
is a model of
\exists R_{1}\sqsubseteq A
, and
\delta\in\eta(\exists R_{1})
,
\delta\in\eta(A)
.
Hence
\eta\not\models\exists R_{3}\sqcap A\sqsubseteq\bot
so
\eta
is not a model of
\mathcal{O}
.

\triangleleft

We now state the main result of this section, which
states that, under certain conditions, satisfiable
DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H},\mathbb{T},@}_{\mathit{horn}}
ontologies
have a convex geometric model.
The need of Condition (i) is already illustrated by Example
1

whereas Condition (ii) ensures that
the underlying logic is Horn (that is, it does not have disjunctions which
can be expressed with the temporal attributes
{\sf before}
,
{\sf after}
and
{\sf between}
).

Theorem 4.1

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a satisfiable DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H},\mathbb{T},@}_{\mathit{horn}}
ontology without negative role inclusions and such that (i) all specifiers attached to a role
in
\mathcal{O}
are atemporal, and (ii)
{\sf before}
,
{\sf after}
and
{\sf between}
do not occur in
\mathcal{O}
.
Then
\mathcal{O}
has a convex geometric model.

5
Related Work

Traditionally, most KG embedding models are time-unaware.
These models embed both entities and relations in a low-dimensional
latent space based on some regularities of target KG.
They can be used as approximate reasoning methods
[
24
,
23
]

for completing KG without using the schema. Typical
embedding models include the translation based models, such as TransE
[
7
]
and
bilinear models, such as
ComplEx
[
28
]
and SimplE
[
15
]
.
From the expressiveness perspective, TransE and DisMult have been shown to be not fully expressive; however, CompleEx and
SimplE are fully expressive. Gutierrez-Basulto and Schockaert
[
13
]

use geometric models to study the compatibility between TBox/ontology and KG embeddings. They show that bilinear models
(inc. ComplEx and SimplE) cannot strictly represent relation subsumption rules. Wiharja et al.
[
29
]
show
that many well known KG embeddings based on KG completion methods are not impressive, when schema aware correctness is
considered, despite good performance reported in silver standard based evaluations.
Currently, more and more applications are involving dynamic KG, where
knowledge in practice is time-variant and consists of sequences of observations. For example, in
recommendation systems based on KG, new items and new user actions appear in real time.
Accordingly, temporal KG embedding models incorporate time information into their node and relation representations.
We next discuss how temporal
information is taken into account in KG embeddings and how
it has been used in combination with classical DLs.

Temporal Knowledge Graph Embeddings.

Temporal KG embedding models can be seen as extensions of static KG embedding models.
A basic approach is to collapse the dynamic graph into a static graph by aggregating the temporal observations over time
[
18
]
.
This approach, however, may lose large amounts of information. An alternative approach is to
give more weights to snapshots that are more recent
[
27
]
.
Another alternative approach to aggregating temporal observations is to apply decomposition methods to dynamic graphs.
The idea is to model a KG as an order 4 tensor and decompose it using CP or Tucker, or other decomposition methods to obtain entity, relation, and timestamp embeddings
[
11
]
.
In addition to aggregation based approaches, there are approaches extending static KG embedding, such as TransE,
by adding a timestamp embedding into the score function
[
14
,
20
]
.
Jiang et al.
[
14
]
only use such timestamps to maintain temporal order,
while using Integer Linear Programming to encode the temporal consistency information as constraints.
Ma et al.
[
20
]
extend several models (Tucker, RESCAL, HolE, ComplEx, DistMult) by adding a timestamp embedding to their score functions. These models may not work well when the number of timestamps is large. Furthermore, since they only learn embeddings for observed timestamps, they cannot generalize to unseen timestamps.
Dasgupta et al.
[
10
]
fragments a temporally-scoped input KG
into multiple static subgraphs with each subgraph corresponding to a timestamp.
There are also approaches of applying random walk models for temporal KG.
E.g., Bian et al
[
6
]
use metapath2vec to generate random walks on both the initial KG and the updated nodes and re-compute the embeddings for these nodes. These approaches mainly leverage the temporal aspect of dynamic graphs to reduce the computations. However, they may fail at capturing the evolution and the temporal patterns of the nodes.
Another natural choice for modeling temporal KG is by extending sequence models to graph data. E.g., García-Duran
[
12
]
extend TransE and DistMult by combining the relation and timestamp through a character LSTM, so as to learn representations for time-augmented KG facts that can be used in conjunction with existing scoring functions for link prediction.
Ma et al.
[
20
]
argue that temporal KG embeddings could also be used as models for cognitive episodic memory (facts we remember and can recollect) and for semantic memory (current facts we know) can be generated from episodic memory by a marginalization operation.

Temporal Description Logics.

In the DL literature, there are several approaches for representing and reasoning temporal
information
[
19
,
32
,
5
]
.
Schmiedel
[
26
]
was the first to propose an extension of the description logics (the
\mathcal{FLENR^{-}}
DL in this case) with an interval-based temporal logic, with the temporal quantifier
at
, the existential and universal temporal quantifiers
sometime
and
alltime
.
Artale and Franconi
[
2
,
3
]
considered a class of interval-based temporal description logics by reducing the expressivity to keep the property of decidability of the logic proposed by Schmiedel
[
26
]
.
Schild proposed
\mathcal{ALCT}

[
25
]
, extending
\mathcal{ALC}
with point-based modal temporal connectives from tense logic
[
9
]
, including existential future (
\lozenge
), universal future (
\square
), next instant (
\bigcirc
),until (
\mathcal{U}
), reflexive until (
U
). Wolter and Zakharyaschev studied the
\mathcal{ALC_{M}^{-}}
DL and showed that it is decidable in the class of linear, discrete and unbounded temporal structures
[
30
]
. They also showed that the
\mathcal{ALC_{M}}
DL (extending the
\mathcal{ALC_{M}^{-}}
DL with global roles) is undecidable
[
31
]
.
Temporal operators can be used in a temporal ABox as well, allowing the use of next instant (
\bigcirc\varphi
)
and until (
\varphi\mathcal{U}\psi
) with ABox assertions
[
4
]
.
Ozaki et al.
[
21
,
22
]
propose temporally attributed DLs,
which allows the use of absolute temporal information in both TBoxes and ABoxes.
They show that the satisfiability of ground
\mathcal{ELH^{\mathbb{T}}_{@}}
ontologies is ExpTime-complete, and
that the satisfiability of ground
\mathcal{ELH^{\mathbb{T}}_{@}}
ontologies without the
temporal attributes
between
,
before
and
after
is PTime-complete.

6
Conclusion

We investigate how geometric models can (or cannot) be used to capture rules about annotated data expressed in the formalism of attributed DLs. We show that every satisfiable attributed DL-Lite
{}^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathit{horn}}
ontology has a convex geometric model and that this is also the case when allowing the use of temporal attributes under some restrictions.
There is still a long way to make this result practical
since we still require an
embedding technique that would construct such a model.
In this direction, we highlight the work of Abboud et al.
[
1
]
, where relations are mapped
to convex regions in the format of hyper-rectangles.

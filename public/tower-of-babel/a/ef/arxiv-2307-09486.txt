Products of Three k − k- Generalized Lucas Numbers as Repdigits
Alaa ALTASSAN, Murat ALAN

Abstract
Let k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 and let ( L n ( k ) ) n ≥ 2 − k (L_{n}^{(k)})_{n\geq 2-k} be the k − k- generalized Lucas sequence with certain initial k k terms and each term afterward is the sum of the k k preceding terms. In this paper, we find all repdigits which are products of arbitrary three terms of k − k- generalized Lucas sequences. Thus, we find all non negative integer solutions of Diophantine equation L n ( k ) ​ L m ( k ) ​ L l ( k ) = a ⁡ ( 10 d − 1 9 ) L_{n}^{(k)}L_{m}^{(k)}L_{l}^{(k)}=a\left(\dfrac{10^{d}-1}{9}\right) where n ≥ m ≥ l ≥ 0 n\geq m\geq l\geq 0 and 1 ≤ a ≤ 9 . 1\leq a\leq 9.

Introduction

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. The
k-
generalized Lucas sequence or, for simplicity, the
k-
Lucas sequence is defined by the recurrence relation as

L_{n}^{(k)}=L_{n-1}^{(k)}+\cdots+L_{n-k}^{(k)}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\geq 2

with the initial condition
L_{0}^{(k)}=2

L_{1}^{(k)}=1

L_{2-k}^{(k)}=\cdots=L_{-1}^{(k)}=0

For

this sequence is the classical Lucas sequence, and in this case we omit the superscript
(k)
in the notation.

Recall that, a positive integer is called a repdigit if it has only one distinct digit in its decimal expansion. These are the numbers of the form
a{(10^{d}-1)/9}
for some
d\geq 1
and
1\leq a\leq 9.
In recent years, many authors have worked on problems involving relations between terms of some binary recurrence sequences and repdigits
[

[

. In fact, in
[

[

, the authors extend this result to the
k-
Lucas sequences. Recently, in
[
12
]
, the authors determined all repdigits which are products of two Lucas numbers. In this paper, we continue to search in this line and extend some previous works, by examining the product of arbitrary three
k-
Lucas numbers and repdigits. More precisely, we find all repdigits which are product of three
k-
Lucas numbers. Our main result is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1

If the Diophantine equation

L_{n}^{(k)}L_{m}^{(k)}L_{l}^{(k)}=a\left(\dfrac{10^{d}-1}{9}\right),\quad d\geq 2\quad n\geq m\geq l\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad 1\leq a\leq 9

(1)

has solutions in non-negative integers, then

\displaystyle(k,n,m,l,a,d)\in

\displaystyle\{(2,5,0,0,4,2),(2,5,1,0,2,2),(2,5,1,1,1,2),(2,5,2,0,6,2),

\displaystyle(2,5,2,1,3,2),(2,5,2,2,9,2),(2,5,3,0,8,2),(2,5,3,1,4,2),

\displaystyle(2,5,4,1,7,2),(4,5,0,0,8,2),(4,5,1,0,4,2),(4,5,1,1,2,2),

\displaystyle(4,5,2,1,6,2)\}.

Corollary 2

If the product of any two
k-
Lucas numbers is a repdigit with at least two digits then this product is one of the following up to order of the factors:

\displaystyle L_{5}^{(2)}L_{0}^{(2)}=11\cdot 2=22,\quad

\displaystyle\quad L_{5}^{(2)}L_{1}^{(2)}=11\cdot 1=11,\quad

\displaystyle\quad L_{5}^{(2)}L_{2}^{(2)}=11\cdot 3=33,

\displaystyle L_{5}^{(2)}L_{3}^{(2)}=11\cdot 4=44,\quad

\displaystyle\quad L_{5}^{(2)}L_{4}^{(2)}=11\cdot 7=77,\quad

\displaystyle\quad L_{5}^{(4)}L_{0}^{(4)}=22\cdot 2=44,

\displaystyle L_{5}^{(4)}L_{1}^{(4)}=22\cdot 1=22,\quad

\displaystyle\quad L_{5}^{(4)}L_{2}^{(4)}=22\cdot 3=66.

In particular, we state the following result for the Lucas sequence.

Corollary 3

The only repdigits that can be written as products of three Lucas numbers are 2, 1, 3, 4, 7, 11, 22, 33, 44, 66, 77, 88 and 99 .

\bullet

h(\theta_{1}\pm\theta_{2})\leq h(\theta_{1})+h(\theta_{2})+\log 2

\bullet

h(\theta_{1}\theta_{2}^{\pm 1})\leq h(\theta_{1})+h(\theta_{2})

\bullet

h(\theta^{s})=\lvert s\rvert h(\theta),

Theorem 4
(Matveev’s Theorem,

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t}

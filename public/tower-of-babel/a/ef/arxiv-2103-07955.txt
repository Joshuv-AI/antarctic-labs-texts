Abstract
. For each odd prime power q q , we construct an infinite sequence of rational functions f ​ ( X ) ∈ 𝔽 q ​ ( X ) f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X) , each of which is exceptional in the sense that for infinitely many n n the map c ↦ f ⁡ ( c ) c\mapsto f(c) induces a bijection of ℙ 1 ​ ( 𝔽 q n ) \mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}) . Moreover, each of our functions f ⁡ ( X ) f(X) is indecomposable in the sense that it cannot be written as the composition of lower-degree rational functions in 𝔽 q ​ ( X ) \mathbb{F}_{q}(X) . A rational function

Exceptional polynomials have found applications to algebraic geometry

It seems likely that exceptional rational functions will find similar applications.

In this paper we construct a new family of exceptional rational functions. In order to explain how this family relates
to previously known families, we now recall some known results about exceptional rational functions.

Lemma 1.1
.

For any
g,h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
, the composition
g(h(X))
is exceptional if and only if both

g(X)
and
h(X)
are exceptional.

Remark
.

The difficult part of Lemma
1.1
is showing that if
g(x)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}})
for infinitely many
m
and

h(x)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
for infinitely many

then there are infinitely many
\ell
for which both
g(x)
and

h(x)
permute
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}})

is an easy consequence of
[

[

In light of Lemma

pieces, which are defined as follows.

Definition 1.2
.

For any field

f(X)\in K(X)
of degree at least
2
is called

if
f(X)

cannot be written as
g(h(X))
with

each of degree at least
2

In this terminology, Lemma
1.1
has the following immediate consequence.

Corollary 1.3
.

A rational function
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
of degree at least
2
is exceptional if and only if it is a composition of
indecomposable exceptional rational functions.

Corollary
1.3
plays a crucial role in the theory of exceptional rational functions, since it reduces the study
of exceptional rational functions to the study of indecomposable exceptional rational functions. The known results
about indecomposable exceptional
polynomials
are surveyed in
[
53
]
. In short, up to composing on both sides
with degree-one polynomials, there are seven known families of indecomposable exceptional polynomials, and it is
expected that there are no others
[

.
It is known that any as-yet-unknown indecomposable exceptional polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
must satisfy several restrictive

[

,
29
,
30
,
32
,

]
: for instance, its degree must be
p^{\ell}
where
\ell\geq 4
and

is the

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We emphasize that by contrast there are thousands of known families of indecomposable
permutation polynomials.
Thus, exceptional polynomials are vastly more rare and more difficult to find
than permutation polynomials. Moreover, since there are known methods to construct many classes of permutation polynomials from a single exceptional polynomial, one may view exceptional polynomials as the fundamental objects which are the ultimate reason for the existence of a huge collection of permutation polynomials. Much less is known about indecomposable exceptional rational functions than polynomials.
Besides linear changes of variables of polynomials, the only known indecomposable exceptional rational functions
are Rédei functions (which are quadratic twists of

)
[

]

[

In this paper we present a new family of indecomposable exceptional rational functions. These include rational functions
of arbitrarily large degree, but they are not members of any of the known families of such functions. The description
of our rational functions involves the following polynomials.

Definition 1.4
.

For

and any positive integer

E_{r}\Bigl(X+\frac{a}{X},a\Bigr)=\frac{X^{r+1}-(a/X)^{r+1}}{X-a/X};

explicitly, we have

(1.6)

E_{r}(X,a)=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}\binom{r-i}{i}(-a)^{i}X^{r-2i}.

For proofs that the polynomial in (

We can now state the main result of this paper. Here if

Let

When

2.
Monodromy groups

In this section we recall the known Galois-theoretic framework for studying exceptional rational functions. Throughout this
section,
q
is a power of a prime
p
, and
x
is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a nonconstant
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
is
separable
if the field extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(f(x))
is separable,
and we say that
f(X)
is inseparable otherwise.

Separable functions have the following simple characterizations
[
18
, Lemma 2.2]
:

Lemma 2.2
.

A nonconstant
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
is inseparable if and only if its derivative
f^{\prime}(X)
equals
0
, or equivalently if and only
if
f(X)=g(X^{p})
for some
g(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
.

Since plainly the only inseparable indecomposable exceptional rational functions are
\mu\circ X^{p}
with
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)

of degree one, we restrict our attention to the separable case. Here the main tool is as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
be separable, let
\Omega
be the Galois closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(f(x))
, and let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
be
the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in
\Omega
. The
arithmetic monodromy group
of
f(X)
is
A:=\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q}(f(x)))
,
viewed as a group of permutations of the set
\Lambda
of left cosets of
J:=\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x))
in
A
. The

geometric monodromy group
of
f(X)
is
G:=\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}(f(x)))
, viewed as a group of permutations of
\Lambda
.

These monodromy groups have the following properties.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
be separable of degree
n
. Then, in the notation of the above definition,

(1)

A
and
G
are transitive subgroups of
\Sym(\Lambda)\cong S_{n}
, and
G
is a normal subgroup of
A
with
A/G

cyclic of order
k
.

(2)

f(X)
is indecomposable over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
J
is a maximal proper subgroup of
A
.

(3)

f(X)
is indecomposable over
\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}
if and only if the group
H:=\Gal(\Omega/\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}(x))
is a maximal proper
subgroup of
G
.

(4)

For any field
K
containing
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the field extension
K(x)/K(f(x))
has degree
n
.

(5)

f(X)
is exceptional if and only if
J
and
H
have exactly one common orbit on
\Lambda
.

Proof.

This result is classical. For proofs of items (1)–(4) in this setting, see
[
18
, Lemma 2.4]
. Item (5) is proved in
[
9
,
31
]
.

We have

(3.2)

E_{r}\Bigl(\frac{X}{\sqrt{a}},1\Bigr)=\frac{E_{r}(X,a)}{\sqrt{a}^{r}}

and

(3.3)

\Bigl(\frac{X}{\sqrt{a}}\Bigr)^{2}-4=\frac{1}{a}(X^{2}-4a).

Dividing the
(r+1)/2
-th power of (
3.2
) by the
(r^{2}-r)/4
-th power of (
3.3
) yields

f_{0}\Bigl(\frac{X}{\sqrt{a}}\Bigr)=f(X)\sqrt{a}^{(r^{2}-r)/2-(r^{2}+r)/2}=\frac{f(X)}{\sqrt{a}^{r}},

so that

f(v^{\prime})=\sqrt{a}^{r}f_{0}(v)=\sqrt{a}^{r}t=t^{\prime}.\qed

(4.2)

In this section we determine the ramification of the rational function

3.1

Proposition 5.1
.

Each
d\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q})\setminus\{0,\infty\}
has
(r^{2}+r)/2
preimages under

f^{-1}(\infty)
has size
3
, and contains one point with ramification index
r
and two with ramification index
(r^{2}-r)/4
. The set
f^{-1}(0)
has size
r
, and all its points have ramification index
(r+1)/2
. The inertia group of
u=c
relative to the extension
\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}(u)/\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}(t^{\prime})

\begin{array}[]{c@{\mathrel{}{}}l@{}}1&\qquad\text{ if }c\in\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{r}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{r^{2}}\\[3.0pt]

\frac{r^{2}-r}{2}&\qquad\text{ if }c\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{r}).\end{array}

This group is cyclic in the second case, and in the third case is a Borel subgroup of
\PSL_{2}(q)
(i.e., it is conjugate to the group of upper-triangular matrices).
For each
c
, the second ramification group in the lower numbering is trivial, and the first ramification group is the unique Sylow
p
-subgroup of the inertia group.

Proof.

Recall that
t^{\prime}=g(u)
where

g(X)=\sqrt{a}^{r}\frac{(X^{r^{2}}-X)^{(r+1)/2}}{(X^{r}-X)^{(r^{2}+1)/2}}.

Since

\gcd((X^{r^{2}}-X)^{(r+1)/2},(X^{r}-X)^{(r^{2}+1)/2})=(X^{r}-X)^{(r+1)/2},

it follows that the

\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
, each of which has ramification index
(r^{2}-r)/2
, and likewise the

-preimages of

are the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{r^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{r}

(r+1)/2
. If

ramified at an element

would be a zero of

\displaystyle\sqrt{a}^{-r}g^{\prime}(X)(X^{r}-X)^{r^{2}+1}=

\displaystyle-\frac{r+1}{2}(X^{r^{2}}-X)^{(r-1)/2}(X^{r}-X)^{(r^{2}+1)/2}

\displaystyle+\frac{r^{2}+1}{2}(X^{r}-X)^{(r^{2}-1)/2}(X^{r^{2}}-X)^{(r+1)/2}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\frac{r+1}{2}(X^{r^{2}}-X)^{(r-1)/2}(X^{r}-X)^{(r^{2}-1)/2}h(X)

but this is impossible since

\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}(v^{\prime})/\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}(t^{\prime})

have multiplicity

We now compute the inertia groups and higher ramification groups in

with

and

. The first ramification group at

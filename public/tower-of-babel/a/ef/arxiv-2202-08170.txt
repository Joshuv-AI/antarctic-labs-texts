Faithfulness of generalised Verma modules for Iwasawa algebras
Stephen Mann

Abstract
We prove faithfulness of infinite-dimensional generalised Verma modules for Iwasawa algebras corresponding to split simple Lie algebras with a Chevalley basis. We use this to prove faithfulness of all infinite-dimensional highest-weight modules in the case of type A 2 A_{2} . In this case we also show that all prime ideals of the corresponding Iwasawa algebras are annihilators of finite-dimensional simple modules.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation and main results

Suppose
G
is a profinite group and
K
is a complete discretely valued field. Then the Iwasawa algebra
KG
is a completion of the group algebra
K[G]
. Iwasawa algebras are useful for studying the representation theory of compact p-adic Lie groups since there is a relationship between modules over the Iwasawa algebra and representations of the group, see
[
15
, Theorem 3.5]
. Iwasawa algebras are also being used to study problems in Number Theory, see for instance
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
.

When studying any ring, an important problem is to study the prime spectrum. It is an ongoing project to classify the prime ideals of Iwasawa algebras, see for instance
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
10
]
. Results so far suggest that for noncommutative Iwasawa algebras the prime ideals are fairly sparse. This paper continues in this direction in the case of certain noncommutative Iwasawa algebras in characteristic
0
.

This paper makes progress towards answering Question B from
[
1
]
, which if answered positively can be used to show that all non-zero prime ideals of the relevant Iwasawa algebras arise as annihilators of finite-dimensional simple modules. Specifically, we aim to study the Iwasawa algebras of uniform pro-p groups corresponding to simple Lie algebras with a Chevalley basis. However, for our main result it is helpful to make a more general statement for reductive groups for the purpose of an inductive proof.

Let
p
be an odd prime. Let
F
be a finite field extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, and
K
be a discretely valued field extension of
F
with ring of integers
R
. Suppose
G
is an
F
-uniform analytic pro-p group with corresponding Lie algebra
L_{G}=p^{n+1}\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattice of a split simple
F
-Lie algebra and has a Chevalley basis.

We then have a corresponding Iwasawa algebra
KG
, which embeds in a completion
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
of the universal enveloping algebra of
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
.
We then can consider a class of

Specifically, we define an affinoid highest weight module for

to be a module of the form
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})}M
, where

is a highest-weight module for
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
with a highest weight
\lambda
satisfying the condition
\lambda(p^{n}\mathfrak{h}_{R})\subseteq R
. (This condition on

will in fact be zero otherwise).

The study of affinoid highest-weight modules has applications for studying the prime ideals of

. In
[
1
]
, two questions are asked.

Question A
: does every primitive ideal of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
with
K

Question B
: is every affinoid highest-weight module that is infinite-dimensional over
K
faithful as a

-module?

Given positive answers to both of these questions, in the case where
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,

has uniformiser
p
and

is sufficiently large relative to the root system of

, we can prove that every non-zero prime ideal of

arises as the annihilator of a simple finite-dimensional module. This is seen in §
8
. Question A has been answered positively in
[
16
]
.

This paper makes progress towards a positive answer to question B. Specifically, it was shown in
[
1
]
that affinoid Verma modules are faithful for
KG
. In this paper, we extend this to generalised Verma modules.

Theorem 1

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{F}

1.3
Set-up

Throughout, we fix a deformation parameter
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Let
\Phi
be a root system with a fixed set of simple roots
\Delta
and corresponding positive system
\Phi^{+}
. Let
p
be an odd prime with the property that
p
does not divide the determinant of the Cartan matrix of any root subsystem of
\Phi
.

Let
F
be a finite field extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with a ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Let
K
be a complete discretely valued field extension of
F
with ring of integers
R
and a uniformiser
\pi
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattice of a split reductive
F
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
, such that
\mathfrak{g}
has the form
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{s}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
, where
\mathfrak{a}
is the centre of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{s,F}
is split semisimple. Assume
\mathfrak{g}_{s}
has a Chevalley basis
\{e_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}\cup\{h_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Delta\}
and Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{s}
spanned by the
h_{\alpha}
. Write
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{s}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
.

Let
G
be an
F
-uniform group with associated
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebra
L_{G}=p^{n+1}\mathfrak{g}
, and with corresponding Iwasawa algebras
RG
and
KG
. Then as discussed in §
2.5

KG
embeds in a completed enveloping algebra
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
.

Now, for any
I\subseteq\Delta
and any
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
with
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
and
h_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
for all
\alpha\in I
,
there is a corresponding subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}
defined in §
2.3
. In the case where
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, this is simply the parabolic subalgebra corresponding to
I
. If
\lambda:\mathfrak{h}_{K}\rightarrow K
is a weight such that
\lambda(h_{\alpha})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for all
\alpha\in I
, then we define a corresponding generalised Verma module
M_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)
. In the case where
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, this corresponds to the usual meaning of a generalised Verma module.

Definition 1.3.1
.

An
affinoid generalised Verma module
for
KG
is a
KG
-module of the form
\widehat{{M}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)}=\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})}M_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)
, where
M_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)
is a generalised Verma module as above, and
\lambda
satisfies the condition
\lambda(p^{n}\mathfrak{h}_{R})\subseteq R
.

Definition 1.3.2
.

We say that the subset
I\subseteq\Delta
is a
totally proper
subset if in the Dynkin diagram of
\Phi
with nodes
\Delta
, no connected component is contained entirely in
I
.

We can now state our main theorem, which is a more general version of theorem
1
that applies to the reductive case.

Theorem 1.3.3
.

Any affinoid generalised Verma module
\widehat{{M}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)}
such that
I\subset\Delta
is totally proper is faithful as a
KG
-module.

We note that the condition that
I
is totally proper is in fact necessary. For example, if
I=\Delta
then
\widehat{{M}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)}
is finite-dimensional and so is certainly not faithful over
KG
. Also note that the main case of interest is the case where
\mathfrak{g}_{K}
is simple, in which our definition of a generalised Verma module is the same as the usual definition. In this case the condition that
I
is totally proper just means that
I
is a proper subset of
\Delta
, which is equivalent to the condition that the generalised Verma module is infinite-dimensional over
K
.

Our proof strategy is by induction on the size of the set
I
. Although we are most interested in the case where
\mathfrak{g}_{K}
is simple, the more general statement is necessary for our induction proof.

•

In §
3
we carry out a reduction to the semisimple case. Specifically, given
M_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)
we associate an affinoid generalised Verma module
\widehat{M_{I}(\mu)}
for
\mathfrak{g}_{s}
. We then show that for
\widehat{{M}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)}
to be faithful over
KG
, it is sufficient that
\widehat{{M}_{I}(\mu)}
is faithful over
KG_{s}
, where
G_{s}
is the subgroup of
G
corresponding to
p^{n+1}\mathfrak{g}_{s}
.

•

This is enough to prove the base case for our induction. The case where
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple and
I=\emptyset
is precisely the case of an affinoid Verma module, which is proved in
[
1
, Theorem 5.4]
.

•

In §
4
we restrict to the semisimple case. We use the duality functor on category
\mathcal{O}
together with Corollary
2.6.2
(which is our version of
[
1
, Theorem 5.3]
) to show that for an affinoid generalised Verma module to be faithful over
KG
, it is sufficient that it is faithful over
RL
, with
L
the corresponding Levi subgroup.

•

Looking at the structure of an affinoid generalised Verma module over the Levi subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}
, we note that it contains a family of infinitely many finite-dimensional modules. In §
5
we argue that any element of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{l})_{n,K}}
annihilating all of these finite-dimensional modules must also annihilate some induced module for
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{l})_{n,K}}
.

•

This induced module has the required form for us to apply the inductive hypothesis. This motivates stating the theorem for reductive Lie algebras, because
\mathfrak{l}
will be reductive but not semisimple.

2.1
Preliminaries about Lie algebras

Whenever
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra over a commutative ring
A
and
B
is an
A
-algebra, we will write
\mathfrak{g}_{B}
for the
B
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{B}=B\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{g}
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is an

-Lie algebra, we say an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is an

-lattice of

of

We now make a statement of the PBW theorem, which is well known in the case of Lie algebras over a field, but also holds for free Lie algebras over commutative rings.

Theorem 2.1.1
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}
is a free, finite rank Lie algebra over a commutative ring

, with a basis
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
. Then
U(\mathfrak{g})
has a free
A
-basis
\{x_{1}^{s_{1}}\dots x_{m}^{s_{m}}\mid s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}\}
.

Proof.

5
, 2.1.12]

∎

For some results we will want to consider highest-weight modules over Lie algebras that are not semisimple. To this end, we establish a more general setting to define highest-weight modules.

Firstly, suppose
\mathfrak{a}
is an abelian
K
-Lie algebra and
M
is a
U(\mathfrak{a})
-module.

Definition 2.1.2
.

•

Suppose
\lambda:\mathfrak{a}_{K}\rightarrow K
is a
K
-linear map. We say

is a
weight vector
of weight
\lambda
if
a\cdot v=\lambda(a)v
for all
a\in\mathfrak{a}_{K}
.

•

Given
\lambda:\mathfrak{a}_{K}\rightarrow K
we write
M_{\lambda}
for the
K
-subspace of

consisting of all
\lambda
-weight vectors.

•

We say
M
is a
weight module
if

has a
K
-basis consisting of weight vectors.

Definition 2.1.3
.

We say an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has a
triangular decomposition
if there are free, finite-rank
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie subalgebras
\mathfrak{n}^{+},\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{n}^{-}
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

\mathfrak{h}
is abelian. Given this, we can consider weight vectors and weight modules over
U(\mathfrak{h}_{K})
.

2.

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-}
as an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-module.

3.

Suppose
\mathfrak{n}
is either
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
or
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
. Then
[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{n}]\subseteq\mathfrak{n}
. Moreover, the
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-linear maps
[h,-]:\mathfrak{n}\rightarrow\mathfrak{n}
for
h\in\mathfrak{h}
are simultaneously diagonalisable. Note that this condition together with Theorem
2.1.1
imply that
U(\mathfrak{n}_{K})
is a weight module with
h\in\mathfrak{h}_{K}
acting by
[h,-]
.

4.

Suppose
\mathfrak{n}
is either
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
or
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
. Suppose
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-basis of weight vectors for
\mathfrak{n}
with corresponding weights
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}:\mathfrak{h}_{K}\rightarrow K
. Then for any
c_{1},\dots,c_{r}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
not all zero we have
\sum c_{i}\alpha_{i}\neq 0
. In particular, for any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{K}^{*}
, the subspace
U(\mathfrak{n}_{K})_{\lambda}
is finite-dimensional.

Note for instance that the usual definition of a triangular decomposition for a semisimple Lie algebra meets these conditions.

Now, if
M
is a
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-module, we say a weight vector
x\in M
is a
highest-weight vector if

e\cdot x=0
for all
e\in\mathfrak{n}^{+}
. We say
M
is a
highest-weight module
if
M
is generated by a highest-weight vector.

Now restrict to the case where
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
is a split semisimple
F
-Lie algebra with root system
\Phi
.

Definition 2.1.4
.

We say that an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-basis
\{h_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Delta\}\cup\{e_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}
for
\mathfrak{g}

is a
Chevalley basis
if the following relations are satisfied:

•

[h_{\alpha},h_{\beta}]=0
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Delta
,

•

[h_{\alpha},e_{\beta}]=\langle\beta,\alpha^{\lor}\rangle e_{\beta}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta,\beta\in\Phi^{+}
,

•

[h_{\alpha},f_{\beta}]=-\langle\beta,\alpha^{\lor}\rangle f_{\beta}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta,\beta\in\Phi^{+}
,

•

[e_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}]=h_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
.

•

Suppose we write
e_{-\alpha}=f_{\alpha}
whenever
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
. Then for
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi
, we have
[e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}]=C_{\alpha,\beta}e_{\alpha+\beta}
for
C_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
, where
C_{\alpha,\beta}\neq 0
if and only if
\alpha+\beta\in\Phi
, and where
C_{\alpha,\beta}=C_{-\beta,-\alpha}
for any
\alpha,\beta
.

This is analagous to the concept of a Chevalley basis for a semisimple Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
, which is known to always exist (see for instance
[
8
, §25.2]
). In our setting we will assume that a Chevalley basis exists. For example we could take
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{m+1}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
for some
m
, which has a Chevalley basis.

We now look at some subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
. Given a subset
I\subseteq\Delta
, we can then define a corresponding root subsystem
\Phi_{I}=\mathbb{Z}I\cap\Phi
. Then we have the following subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
:

Definition 2.1.5
.

•

\mathfrak{h}
will denote the Cartan subalgebra spanned by
\{h_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Delta\}
.

•

\mathfrak{p}_{I}
is the parabolic subalgebra spanned by
\{h_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Delta\}\cup\{e_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}\cup\{f_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{I}^{+}\}
,

•

\mathfrak{l}_{I}
is the Levi subalgebra spanned by
\{h_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Delta\}\cup\{e_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{I}^{+}\}
,

•

\mathfrak{g}_{I}
is the semisimple subalgebra spanned by
\{h_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in I\}\cup\{e_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{I}^{+}\}
.

Note that
\mathfrak{p}_{I}
and
\mathfrak{l}_{I}
have triangular decompositions, so we can define highest-weight modules over them although they are not semisimple.

2.2
Completions

For now, we take
\mathfrak{g}
to be any
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebra which is free of finite rank as an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-module.

Definition 2.2.1
.

We fix a descending filtration of
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
as follows: first take
\Gamma_{0}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})=U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})
. Then take
\Gamma_{i}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})=\pi^{i}\Gamma_{0}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Define
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
to be the completion of
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
with respect to this filtration, which is itself a filtered ring with a filtration
\Gamma_{i}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
.

Lemma 2.2.2
.

Suppose we have an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-basis
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
for
\mathfrak{g}
. Write
(p^{n}x)^{s}=(p^{n}x_{1})^{s_{1}}\dots(p^{n}x_{n})^{s_{n}}
for
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}
, and use the notation
|s|=\underset{i}{\sum}s_{i}
. Then as a
K
-vector space, we can identify
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
with the ring of convergent power series:

\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}=\{\underset{s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}}{\sum}C_{s}(p^{n}x)^{s}\mid C_{s}\in K,C_{s}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }|s|\rightarrow\infty\}.

Proof.

The PBW theorem (Theorem
2.1.1
) implies that
\Gamma_{0}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
has a free
R
-basis
\{(p^{n}x)^{s}\mid s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}\}
. Then
\Gamma_{i}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
has free
R
-basis
\{\pi^{i}(p^{n}x)^{s}\mid s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}\}
. The result now follows from the definition of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
.

∎

Definition 2.2.3
.

For any finitely generated
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-module
M
, we define the completion of
M
as
\hat{M}=\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})}M
.

Proposition 2.2.4
.

\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
is flat as a right
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-module. In particular, the completion functor
M\mapsto\hat{M}
is an exact functor from finitely generated
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-modules to
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
-modules.

Proof.

First, note that
\Gamma_{0}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}=\{\underset{s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}}{\sum}C_{s}(p^{n}x)^{s}\mid C_{s}\in R,C_{s}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }|s|\rightarrow\infty\}
is the
\pi
-adic completion of
\Gamma_{0}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
. Also note that
\Gamma_{0}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})=U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})
is a left Noetherian ring, because its associated graded ring is a polynomial ring over
R
by the PBW theorem. So by
[
4
, 3.2.3 (iv)]
we can see that
\Gamma_{0}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
is flat as a right
\Gamma_{0}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-module.

Now if
M
is a
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-module,
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})}M\cong\Gamma_{0}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}\otimes_{\Gamma_{0}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})}M
as abelian groups, and so
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
is flat over
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
.
∎

Proposition 2.2.5
.

Suppose
M
is a finitely-generated
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-module with a good filtration
\Gamma_{i}M
with respect to the filtration of
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
(i.e.
\Gamma_{i}=\pi^{i}\Gamma_{0}M
for all
i
and
\Gamma_{0}M
is a finitely generated
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})
-module). Then
\hat{M}
is the completion of
M
with respect to
\Gamma
. Thus
\hat{M}
also has a filtration
\Gamma_{i}\hat{M}
.

Proof.

Write
N
for the completion of
M
with respect to
\Gamma
. Then
N
has a filtration
\Gamma
, with
\Gamma_{j}N=\varprojlim_{i\geq j}\Gamma_{i}M=\varprojlim_{i\geq j}\pi^{i}\Gamma_{0}M
.

So
\Gamma_{0}N
is the
\pi
-adic completion of
\Gamma_{0}M
, a finitely generated
\Gamma_{0}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-module. Then by
[
4
, 3.2.3 (iii)]
, the natural map
\Gamma_{0}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}\otimes_{\Gamma_{0}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})}\Gamma_{0}M\rightarrow\Gamma_{0}N

Now note that
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})}M=K\otimes_{R}(\Gamma_{0}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}\otimes_{\Gamma_{0}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})}\Gamma_{0}M)
and

. Since
K
is flat over
R
(being the field of fractions of
R
), we obtain an isomorphism
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})}M\rightarrow N
.

∎

From now on, we will restrict ourselves to filtered modules in the following category.

Definition 2.2.6
.

The
category

\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
is the category of all finitely generated filtered
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-modules
(M,\Gamma)
such that
\Gamma
is a good filtration, and such that
\Gamma_{0}M
is free as an
R
-module. The morphisms are filtered module homomorphisms. We will write
M\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
if there exists a filtration for
M
such that
(M,\Gamma)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Lemma 2.2.7
.

Suppose
(M,\Gamma)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
. Then the filtration
\Gamma
is separated. This has the following consequences:

•

M
embeds as a
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-submodule of
\hat{M}
,

•

the topology of
\hat{M}
induced by the filtration is Hausdorff.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{B}
is a free
R
-basis for
\Gamma_{0}M
. We can describe the filtration of
M
as follows:
\Gamma_{i}M=\bigoplus_{v\in\mathcal{B}}\pi^{i}Rv
, so
\underset{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{\bigcap}\Gamma_{i}M=0
.
∎

Let
M\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
. Given a free
R
-basis
\mathcal{B}
for
\Gamma_{0}M
, every element of
\hat{M}
can be uniquely described as a sum
\underset{v\in\mathcal{B}}{\sum}C_{v}v
with
v\in M
, which is convergent in the sense that for any
i
,
C_{v}\in\pi^{i}R
for all but finitely many
v
. Moreover, every such convergent sum describes an element of
\hat{M}
. We can then describe
M\subseteq\hat{M}
as the subset of all the finite sums.

Lemma 2.2.8
.

1.

Suppose
M
is a
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-module which is finite-dimensional over
K
. Then the embedding
M\subseteq\hat{M}
is a bijection, and so
M
can be considered as a
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
-module.

2.

Suppose
M\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
and
N\leq M
is a finite-dimensional
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-submodule. Then
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}\cdot N\subseteq N
in
M
.

Proof.

1.

Suppose
M
has a
K
-basis
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
. Then define a good filtration
\Gamma
on
M
by
\Gamma_{i}M=\pi^{i}R\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
. So
(M,\Gamma)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
. Then our description of
\hat{M}
and
M
above means that both
\hat{M}
and
M
consist of all sums
\sum a_{i}x_{i}
with
a_{i}\in K
, that is,
\hat{M}=M
.

2.

By exactness of the completion functor (Proposition
2.2.4
), we can see that the embedding
N\subseteq M
of
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-modules extends to an embedding
\hat{N}\subseteq\hat{M}
of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
-modules. But since
\hat{N}=N
as above, this means that
N\subseteq\hat{M}
is a
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
-submodule.

∎

The following result will be useful when considering generalised Verma modules, which we will see are locally finite over the corresponding parabolic subalgebra.

Proposition 2.2.9
.

Suppose
(M,\Gamma)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathfrak{p}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
M
is a locally finite
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})
-module. Then
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{p})_{n,K}}\cdot M\subseteq M
inside
\hat{M}
.

Proof.

Let
w\in M
, and we claim that
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{p})_{n,K}}\cdot w\subseteq M
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a free
R
-basis for
\Gamma_{0}M
.

Firstly, consider
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})\cdot w
. Each element of
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})\cdot w
can be described as a finite linear combination of elements of
\mathcal{B}
, and moreover
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})\cdot w
is finite-dimensional by local finiteness. So we can choose
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\in\mathcal{B}
such that
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})\cdot w\subseteq K\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}\subseteq M
. Then any element of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{p})_{n,K}}\cdot w
is the limit of a sequence of elements of
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})\cdot w\subseteq K\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
, and hence also lies in
K\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}\subseteq M
.
∎

When proving that an element of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
annihilates
\hat{M}
, it can be useful to use the following result, which comes from
M
being a dense subspace in
\hat{M}
in the topology of the filtration
\Gamma
.

Lemma 2.2.10
.

Let
(M,\Gamma)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
. Suppose
x\in\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
such that
x\cdot M=0
in
\hat{M}
. Then
x\cdot\hat{M}=0
.

Proof.

Let
v\in\hat{M}
. Say
v=\underset{i\geq j}{\sum}v_{i}
where
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
v_{i}\in\Gamma_{i}M
for all
i
. Also say
x\in\Gamma_{m}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
(using the filtration on
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
coming from the filtration on
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
).

Then
x\cdot v=x\cdot v-x\cdot\underset{j\leq i\leq k}{\sum}v_{i}=x\cdot\underset{i>k}{\sum}v_{i}\in\Gamma_{m+k+1}\hat{M}
for each
k\geq j
. So
x\cdot v=0
since
\Gamma
is separated by Lemma
2.2.7
.

∎

For the remainder of the section we want to specialise further. So now assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is a free, finite-rank
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebra with a triangular decomposition in the sense of Definition
2.1.3
.

We can now define a category of modules in our setting analogous to the usual concept of category
\mathcal{O}
for split semisimple Lie algebras.

Definition 2.2.11
.

The
category

\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{g})
is the full subcategory of all
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-modules
M
satisfying:

•

M
is a weight module,

•

M
is finitely generated over
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
,

•

M
is locally finite over
U(\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{K})
.

The
category

\mathcal{O}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
is the full subcategory of
\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{g})
consisting of modules
M
such that all weights
\lambda
of
M
satisfy
\lambda(p^{n}\mathfrak{h}_{R})\subseteq R
.

In particular,
\mathcal{O}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
contains any highest-weight module of weight
\lambda
such that
\lambda(p^{n}\mathfrak{h}_{R})\subseteq R
. The following results are standard facts about category
\mathcal{O}
.

Lemma 2.2.12
.

Suppose
M\in\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{g})
. Then:

•

M
is generated by a finite set of weight vectors,

•

the weight spaces of
M
are finite-dimensional.

Proof.

Since
M\in\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{g})
,
M
is finitely generated. Moreover,
M
is a weight module. So let
T
be a finite generating set for
M
, then let
S
be the set of all weight components of elements of
T
. Thus
S
is a finite generating set for
M
consisting of weight vectors.

Now, since
M
is locally finite over
U(\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{K})
, we have that
U(\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{K})S
is finite-dimensional and spanned by weight vectors. Say
S^{\prime}
is a
K
-basis for
U(\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{K})S
consisting of weight vectors. Then
S^{\prime}
generates
M
.

Moreover, by Theorem
2.1.1
,
M=U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})S=U(\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{K})U(\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{K})U(\mathfrak{h}_{K})S
. Since the elements of
S
are weight vectors,
M=U(\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{K})U(\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{K})S=U(\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{K})S^{\prime}
. By Definition
2.1.3
, the weight spaces of
U(\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{K})
are finite-dimensional. Since
S^{\prime}
is finite, the weight spaces of
M
are finite-dimensional: if we write
\lambda_{v}
for the weight of
v\in S^{\prime}
, then
M_{\lambda}=\underset{v\in S^{\prime}}{\sum}U(\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{K})_{\lambda-\lambda_{v}}v
for any weight
\lambda
.
∎

We now want to show that any
M\in\mathcal{O}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
has a filtration
\Gamma
such that
(M,\Gamma)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
, so that our results for
\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
remain applicable.

Proposition 2.2.13
.

Suppose
M
is a
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
-module in category
\mathcal{O}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
. Let
S
be a finite set of weight vectors generating
M
. Then
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})S
is free as an
R
-module, with a free
R
-basis consisting of weight vectors.

Proof.

We have
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})S=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{K}^{*}}U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})S\cap M_{\lambda}
since the elements of
S
are weight vectors, and hence
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})S
is spanned by weight vectors. So it is sufficient to show the weight spaces of
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})S
are free over
R
.

Now, writing
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
for the
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
spanned by the
f_{\alpha}
, we have
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})S=U(p^{n}\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{R})U(p^{n}\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{R})U(p^{n}\mathfrak{h}_{R})S
by the PBW theorem.

The assumption that all weights
\lambda
of
M
satisfy
\lambda(p^{n}\mathfrak{h}_{R})\subseteq R
ensures
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{h}_{R})S=RS
, so
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})S=U(p^{n}\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{R})U(p^{n}\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{R})S
.

By assumption, both
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
have free
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-bases of weight vectors, say
e_{1},\dots,e_{r}
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{r^{\prime}}
respectively. So by Theorem
2.1.1
,
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{R})S
is spanned as an
R
-module by the set of weight vectors
S^{\prime}=\{(p^{n}e_{1})^{i_{1}}\dots(p^{n}e_{r})^{i_{r}}s\mid i_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},s\in S\}
. But note that since
U(\mathfrak{n}_{K}^{+})
has finite-dimensional weight spaces (by our definition of a triangular decomposition), only finitely many monomials
(p^{n}e_{1})^{i_{1}}\dots(p^{n}e_{r})^{i_{r}}
can have the same weight. Therefore only finitely many elements of
S^{\prime}
can have the same weight.

But since
M
is locally finite over
U(\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{K})
, elements of
S^{\prime}
can only have finitely many different weights, and so
S^{\prime}
has only finitely many non-zero elements. So
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})S=U(p^{n}\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{R})S^{\prime}
with
S^{\prime}
a finite set of weight vectors.

Now, fix a weight
\lambda
of
M
. Then
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})S\cap M_{\lambda}
is spanned by the set of all
(p^{n}f_{1})^{i_{1}}\dots(p^{n}f_{r^{\prime}})^{i_{r}}s^{\prime}
with
i_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
of weight
\lambda
. There are only finitely many such elements since the weight spaces of
U(\mathfrak{n}^{-}_{K})
are finite-dimensional and since
S^{\prime}
is finite, so
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})S\cap M_{\lambda}
is finitely generated over
R
.

Now,
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})S\cap M_{\lambda}
is a finitely generated torsion-free module over a principal ideal domain
R
, and so is free of finite rank over
R
.

∎

Corollary 2.2.14
.

Suppose
M\in\mathcal{O}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
. Then there is a good filtration
\Gamma
for
M
such that
(M,\Gamma)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
, and also
\Gamma_{0}M
has a free
R
-basis of weight vectors.

Proof.

Let
S
be a finite generating set of
M
consisting of weight vectors, which exists by Lemma
2.2.12
.

Define
\Gamma_{i}M=\Gamma_{i}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})S
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, so
\Gamma
is a good filtration for
M
. Moreover, by Proposition
2.2.13
,
\Gamma_{0}M=U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})S
has a free
R
-basis of weight vectors.

∎

If a basis
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
for
\mathfrak{g}
consists of weight vectors, (e.g. a Chevalley basis when
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple), then we can describe weight components of
y\in\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
extending the concept of weight components in
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
.

Definition 2.2.15
.

If
y=\sum C_{s}(\pi x)^{s}\in\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
and
\lambda
is a weight, then the
\lambda
-
weight component
is the convergent sum of all
C_{s}(\pi x)^{s}
which have weight
\lambda
. We also say that
y
has weight
\lambda
if all weight components of
y
with weight not equal to
\lambda
are zero. We then say
y
is a
weight vector
if
y
has weight
\lambda
for some
\lambda
.

Lemma 2.2.16
.

Suppose
M\in\mathcal{O}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
. Suppose
x\in\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
is a weight vector. Then
x\cdot M\subseteq M
inside
\hat{M}
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a finite generating set of weight vectors of
M
, which exists by Lemma
2.2.12
. Let
\Gamma
be the corresponding good filtration, so
\Gamma_{i}M=\Gamma_{i}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})S
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a free
R
-basis for
\Gamma_{0}M
consisting of weight vectors (which exists by Proposition
2.2.13
).

Suppose
w\in M
. Then
w
has finitely many non-zero weight components, since
M
is a weight module. Say
W
is the set of all weights of components of
w
. Say
x
has weight
\lambda
, and let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be the set of all elements of
\mathcal{B}
with weights lying in
\lambda+W
. Note that since the weight spaces of
M
are finite-dimensional by Lemma
2.2.12
, only finitely many elements of
\mathcal{B}
can have the same weight and therefore
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is finite.

Now we can write
x=\lim_{i}x_{i}
where
x_{i}\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
have weight
\lambda
. Then all weight components of
x_{i}\cdot w
have weights in
\lambda+W
, and therefore
x_{i}\cdot w\in K\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. Therefore
x\cdot w=\lim_{i}x_{i}\cdot w\in K\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subseteq M
.
∎

Lemma 2.2.17
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{O}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
. Suppose
x\in\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}}\hat{M}
. Then all weight components of
x
lie in
\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}}\hat{M}
.

Proof.

Say
x=\underset{\lambda}{\sum}x_{\lambda}
as a convergent sum, where
x_{\lambda}\in\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
has weight
\lambda
. We claim
x_{\lambda}\cdot M=0
for each
\lambda
, which by Lemma
2.2.10
will imply
x_{\lambda}\in\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}}\hat{M}
.

Let
v\in M
be a weight vector of weight
\mu
. Then
0=x\cdot v=\underset{\lambda}{\sum}x_{\lambda}\cdot v
. Then the
(\lambda+\mu)
-weight component of
x\cdot v
is
x_{\lambda}\cdot v
for each
\lambda
, so
x_{\lambda}\cdot v=0
for all
\lambda
. Thus indeed each
x_{\lambda}
annihilates
M\subseteq\hat{M}
.
∎

We will now claim that any
M\in\mathcal{O}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
is diagonalisable over
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{h})_{n,K}}
. Suppose
\lambda:p^{n}\mathfrak{h}_{R}\rightarrow R
, then
\lambda
defines a filtered homomorphism
\lambda:U(\mathfrak{h}_{K})\rightarrow K
(with the filtration
\pi^{i}R
on
K
). So this extends to a filtered homomorphism
\lambda:\widehat{U(\mathfrak{h})_{n,K}}\rightarrow K
.

Lemma 2.2.18
.

Suppose
M\in\mathcal{O}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
and
v\in M
has weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{K}^{*}
such that
\lambda(p^{n}\mathfrak{h}_{R})\subseteq R
. Then for any
x\in\widehat{U(\mathfrak{h})_{n,K}}
we have
x\cdot v=\lambda(x)v
in
\hat{M}
.

Proof.

Say
x=\underset{i\geq j}{\sum}x_{i}
where
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x_{i}\in\Gamma_{i}U(\mathfrak{h}_{K})
for each
i
. Say
v\in\Gamma_{m}M
, where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then for each
k\geq j
, we have:

\begin{split}x\cdot v-\lambda(x)v&=\underset{j\leq i\leq k}{\sum}x_{i}\cdot v+\underset{i>k}{\sum}x_{i}\cdot v-\lambda(\underset{j\leq i\leq k}{\sum}x_{i})v-\lambda(\underset{i>k}{\sum}x_{i})v\\
&=\underset{i>k}{\sum}x_{i}\cdot v-\lambda(\underset{i>k}{\sum}x_{i})v\in\Gamma_{m+k+1}\hat{M}.\end{split}

So
x\cdot v=\lambda(x)v.

∎

2.3
Induced modules

In this section, we look at modules induced from finite-dimensional modules. Firstly, we want to confirm that the modules relevant to us are in category
\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
given an appropriate filtration. This allows us to apply the results of §
2.2
.

Proposition 2.3.1
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
as
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-modules, where both
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{n}
are free, finite rank
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebras. Suppose
(V,\Gamma)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})
such that
\Gamma_{0}V
has finite rank over
R
. Let
M=U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})}V
. Then:

1.

\Gamma
induces a filtration on
M
such that
(M,\Gamma)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
,

2.

M
is locally finite as a
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})
-module, which in particular means it can be considered as a (locally finite)
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{p})_{n,K}}
-module.

Proof.

Define
\Gamma_{i}M=U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})\Gamma_{i}V
. Then
\underset{i}{\bigcup}\Gamma_{i}M=U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})V=M
. Moreover,
\Gamma_{i}M=\pi^{i}U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})\Gamma_{0}V=\pi^{i}\Gamma_{0}M
, and
\Gamma_{0}M
is finitely generated over
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{R})
(e.g. by any finite generating set for
\Gamma_{0}V
as a
U(p^{n}\mathfrak{p}_{R})
-module). So
\Gamma
is a good filtration for
M
.

Given a finite free
R
-basis
B
for
\Gamma_{0}V
and a free
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-basis
y_{1},\dots,y_{r}
for
\mathfrak{n}
, the PBW theorem implies that
\Gamma_{0}M
has free
R
-basis
\{(p^{n}y_{1})^{s_{1}}\dots(p^{n}y_{r})^{s_{r}}v\mid x_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},v\in B\}
. So
(M,\Gamma)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
. This proves the first part.

In order to prove the second part, we claim that the
K
-vector spaces
\Omega_{i}M=K\{x_{1}\dots x_{j}v\mid 0\leq j\leq i,x_{1},\dots,x_{j}\in\mathfrak{g},v\in V\}
are in fact
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})
-modules. The proposition then follows since each
\Omega_{i}M
is finite-dimensional, and
\underset{i}{\bigcup}\Omega_{i}M=M
.

We proceed by induction. Firstly,
\Omega_{0}M=V
is a
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})
-module. Now assume
\Omega_{i}M
is a
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})
-module, and show
\Omega_{i+1}M
is a
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})
-module.

Let
y\in U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})
,
x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
v\in V
. Then
y\cdot x_{1}\dots x_{i+1}v=[y,x_{1}]x_{2}\dots x_{i+1}v+x_{1}yx_{2}\dots x_{i+1}v
.

By definition,
[y,x_{1}]x_{2}\dots x_{i+1}v\in\Omega_{i+1}M
. By our induction assumption,
yx_{2}\dots x_{i+1}v\in\Omega_{i}M
and hence
x_{1}yx_{2}\dots x_{i+1}\in\Omega_{i+1}M
. So
y\cdot x_{1}\dots x_{i+1}v\in\Omega_{i+1}M
.

We now conclude that every
\Omega_{i}M
is a
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})
-module, and therefore
M
is locally finite over
U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})
.
M
now can be considered as a
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{p})_{n,K}}
-module by Proposition
2.2.9
.

∎

Now note that in the case where
\mathfrak{p}
has a triangular decomposition and
V\in\mathcal{O}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})
, we know there is a filtration
\Gamma
for
V
such that
(V,\Gamma)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})
. So the above result implies that
M=U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})}V
has a filtration
\Gamma
such that
(M,\Gamma)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
.

We now look at the specific induced modules that we want to study.

For the rest of the section, let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{s}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
be an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattice of a split reductive
F
-Lie algebra, with root system
\Phi
, a fixed set of simple roots
\Delta
,
\mathfrak{a}
the centre of
\mathfrak{g}
, and a Chevalley basis
\{e_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}\cup\{h_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Delta\}
for
\mathfrak{g}_{s}
. Say
\mathfrak{h}_{s}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{s}
.

Suppose
I\subseteq\Delta
and
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
is a subalgebra such that
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
and
h_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
for all
\alpha\in I
. Suppose
\lambda:\mathfrak{h}_{K}\to K
is a weight such that
\lambda(h_{\alpha})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for all
\alpha\in I
.

We then have a corresponding subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{F}\{e_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{F}\{f_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{I}^{+}\}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
.

Note that the parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}_{I}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{s}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{p}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}
as a Lie algebra. Specifically, let
f:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{s}
be the projection map with kernel
\mathfrak{a}
, which is a Lie algebra homomorphism. Then
f
restricts to an isomorphism
f:\mathfrak{p}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{p}_{I}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{I}
be the semisimple subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{s}
corresponding to
I
. That is,
\mathfrak{g}_{I}
is spanned over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
by
\{e_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{I}^{+}\}\cup\{h_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in I\}
. Then let
V
be the unique irreducible highest-weight
U(\mathfrak{g}_{I,K})
-module of weight
\lambda|_{\mathfrak{h}_{I,K}}
. The existence and uniqueness of
V
is a standard fact, see for instance
[
5
, 7.1.11, 7.1.13]
. Then
V
is finite-dimensional because
\lambda(h_{\alpha})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for all
\alpha\in I
, see for instance
[
5
, 7.2.6]
. Moreover,
V
can be made into a
U(\mathfrak{p}_{I,\mathfrak{h^{\prime}},K})
-module as follows.

Firstly, let
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}=\underset{\alpha\in I}{\bigcap}\ker\alpha\leq\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}_{K}
, so
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}
is central in
U(\mathfrak{g}_{I,K}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K})
. We can also see that
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}_{K}=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{I,K}
. This holds because if
h\in\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{I,K}
then
0=\alpha(h)=\langle h,h_{\alpha}^{\lor}\rangle
for all
\alpha\in I
, which means
(h,h_{\alpha})=0
for all
\alpha\in I
and hence
h=0
since the
h_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in I
span
\mathfrak{h}_{I,K}
. So
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{I,K}=0
. Moreover by standard linear algebra
\dim_{K}\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}=\dim_{K}\underset{\alpha\in I}{\bigcap}\alpha\geq\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}_{K}-|I|
, which means indeed
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}_{K}=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{I,K}
.

By Theorem
2.1.1
, we can see that
U(\mathfrak{g}_{I,K}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K})\cong U(\mathfrak{g}_{I,K})\otimes_{K}U(\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K})
as a
K
-module, and moreover since
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}
is central, this is also an isomorphism of
K
-algebras. We can then define a
K
-algebra homomorphism
U(\mathfrak{g}_{I,K}+\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K})\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{g}_{I,K})
by mapping each
h\in\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}
to
\lambda(h)
. Then inflate
V
along this homomorphism to make
V
into a
U(\mathfrak{g}_{I,K}+\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K})
-module, which is a highest-weight module of weight
\lambda
.

Then note that the nilpotent subalgebra
\mathfrak{n}_{I}=\mathcal{O}_{F}\{e_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\setminus\Phi_{I}^{+}\}
satisfies
\mathfrak{p}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime},K}=\mathfrak{g}_{I,K}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{I,K}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}
. This is because by definition
\mathfrak{p}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime},K}=\mathcal{O}_{F}\{e_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}_{K}\oplus\{f_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{I}^{+}\}=(\mathcal{O}_{F}\{e_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{I}^{+}\}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{I,K}\oplus\{f_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{I}^{+}\})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{F}\{e_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\setminus\Phi_{I}^{+}\}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}=\mathfrak{g}_{I,K}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{I,K}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}
.

Also
\mathfrak{n}_{I}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{p}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime},K}
. This is because if
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\setminus\Phi_{I}^{+}
and
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
, then
\alpha+\beta\notin\Phi_{I}^{+}
since
\alpha
and hence
\alpha+\beta
has a non-zero coefficient of some simple root not in
I
. Thus
[e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}]
(which is either
0
or a scalar multiple of
e_{\alpha+\beta}
) lies in
\mathfrak{n}_{I}
. Similarly if
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\setminus\Phi_{I}^{+}
and
\beta\in\Phi_{I}^{+}
then
\alpha-\beta
has a positive coefficient of some element of
\Delta\setminus I
and so
[e_{\alpha},f_{\beta}]\in\mathfrak{n}_{I}
.

Inflate
V
along the projection
\mathfrak{p}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime},K}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{I,K}+\mathfrak{h}^{\prime\prime}_{K}
with kernel
\mathfrak{n}_{I,K}
, and we make
V
into an irreducible highest-weight
U(\mathfrak{p}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime},K})
-module of weight
\lambda
such that all
e_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\setminus\Phi_{I}^{+}
act as
0
.

Definition 2.3.2
.

The
generalised Verma module
corresponding to
I
and
\lambda
is
M_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{p}_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}})}V
.

Suppose
\mathcal{B}
is a
K
-basis for
V
. Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
be the elements of
\{f_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi\setminus\Phi_{I}\}
. Let
x_{r+1},\dots,x_{s}
be an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-basis for
\mathfrak{a}
. Then by the PBW theorem,
M_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)
has basis
\{x^{t}v\mid t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s},v\in\mathcal{B}\}
. Here,
x^{t}
denotes
x_{1}^{t_{1}}\dots x_{s}^{t_{s}}
.

In the case where
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
is split semisimple (meaning that
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
must equal
\mathfrak{h}
) we simply write
M_{I}(\lambda)
for
M_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)
, which agrees with the notation of
[
7
]
.

2.4
F
-uniform groups

Suppose
G
is a uniform pro-p group in the sense of
[
6
, Definition 4.1]
. Then
G
has an associated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebra
L_{G}
. The elements of this Lie algebra are the elements of
G
. The structure is described in full detail in
[
6
, §4]
, but we can describe the operations as follows:

•

a\cdot g=g^{a}
for
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
g\in G
,

•

g+h=\lim_{i\to\infty}(g^{p^{i}}h^{p^{i}})^{p^{-i}}
for
g,h\in G
,

•

[g,h]=\lim_{i\to\infty}(g^{-p^{i}}h^{-p^{i}}g^{p^{i}}h^{p^{i}})^{p^{-2i}}
for
g,h\in G
.

Any uniform pro-p group can be described using a minimal topological generating set as follows.

Proposition 2.4.1
.

Let
G
be a uniform pro-p group. Then if
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
is a topological generating set for
G
of minimal size, the map
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})\mapsto x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\dots x_{r}^{\lambda_{r}}
is a homeomorphism from
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}
to
G
.

Proof.

[
6
, Theorem 4.9]

∎

We can then relate minimal topological generating sets of
G
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-bases of
L_{G}
.

Proposition 2.4.2
.

A subset
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
of
G
is a minimal topological generating set of
G
if and only if it is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis for
L_{G}
.

Proof.

[
6
, Theorem 4.17]
gives that any minimal topological generating set for
G
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis for
L_{G}
. Adapting the proof of this theorem, we can show the converse also holds.

From
[
6
, Corollary 4.15]
, the multiplicative subgroup
G_{2}=G^{p}
of
G
is also an additive subgroup of
G
, and the additive cosets of
G_{2}
are the same as the multiplicative cosets. By
[
6
, Proposition 4.16]
,
(G,+)
is a uniform pro-p group with the same dimension as
G
. By
[
6
, Lemma 3.4]
,
G_{2}
is the Frattini subgroup of both
G
and
(G,+)
.

Suppose
X
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis for
L_{G}
. Then
X
topologically generates
(G,+)
. By
[
6
, Proposition 1.9]
, the image of
X
in
G/G_{2}
generates
G/G_{2}
(which is the same group whether we use the multiplication or the addition operation on
G
). Therefore
X
topologically generates
G
, and is minimal since the dimensions of
G
,
(G,+)
as uniform groups are the same.

∎

Now make the further assumption that
G
is an
F
-analytic group. Following
[
13
, Remark 2.2.5]
, we can identify
L_{G}
with a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-submodule of the
F
-Lie algebra
\text{Lie}(G)
, via the exponential map.

Definition 2.4.3
.

A uniform pro-p group
G
is an
F
-uniform group
if
G
is an
F
-analytic group, and moreover
L_{G}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-submodule of
\text{Lie}(G)
.

Definition 2.4.4
.

We say an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is powerful if
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\subseteq p\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 2.4.5
.

The assignment
G\mapsto L_{G}
is an isomorphism of categories from the category of
F
-uniform groups to the category of free, finite rank, torsion-free, powerful
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebras. The inverse is given by
L\mapsto(L,*)
where
x*y=\log(\exp(x)\exp(y))
.

Proof.

Follows from
[
6
, 9.10]
.
∎

We will use this result repeatedly to show the existence of groups corresponding to certain
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebras.

Example 2.4.6
.

Given any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the group corresponding to
p^{n+1}\mathfrak{sl}_{m+1}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
is
\ker(SL_{m+1}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\rightarrow SL_{m+1}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/p^{n+1}\mathcal{O}_{F}))
.

2.5
Iwasawa algebras

Given any profinite group
G
, we can define the Iwasawa algebras
RG
and
KG
as follows.

Definition 2.5.1
.

•

RG=\varprojlim_{N\unlhd_{o}G}R[G/N]
.

•

KG=K\otimes_{R}RG
.

Now suppose
G
is an
F
-uniform group. We can then describe the structure of
RG
and
KG
as follows.

Proposition 2.5.2
.

Let
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
be a minimal topological generating set for
G
. Write
b^{s}=(x_{1}-1)^{s_{1}}\dots(x_{r}-1)^{s_{r}}\in RG
for
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}
. Then
RG
can be described as follows:

RG=\{\underset{s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}}{\sum}C_{s}b^{s}\mid C_{s}\in R\}

It follows from this that we can describe
KG
as:

KG=\{\underset{s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}}{\sum}C_{s}b^{s}\mid C_{s}\in K,\text{ the }C_{s}\text{ bounded as }|s|\rightarrow\infty\}

Proof.

A standard fact about Iwasawa algebras, for example
[
14
, §28]
.
∎

Now assume
\mathfrak{g}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebra such that
L_{G}=p^{n+1}\mathfrak{g}
. We wish to embed
KG
in the completed enveloping algebra
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
. In the setting of the introduction, this allows us to consider completed highest-weight modules for
\mathfrak{g}_{K}
as
KG
-modules. To this end, we make the following claims inspired by
[
2
, Theorem 10.4]
.

We write
id
for the identity map
G\rightarrow L_{G}=p^{n+1}\mathfrak{g}
. This allows us to describe a mapping of
G
into
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
as follows.

Proposition 2.5.3
.

The map
x\mapsto\exp(\text{id}(x))
defines an injective continuous homomorphism from
G
to the multiplicative group
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}^{\times}
(with the subspace topology on
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}^{\times}
given by the filtration on
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
).

Proof.

Note that for any
y\in p^{n+1}\mathfrak{g}
,
\exp(y)=\sum\frac{y^{j}}{j!}
defines an element of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
. So
x\mapsto\exp(\text{id}(x))
does define a map
G\rightarrow\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
. Moreover, Theorem
2.4.5
implies that
x*y=\log(\exp(x)\exp(y))
defines an operation on
G
that coincides with the group operation. This implies that
x\mapsto\exp(\text{id}(x))
is a homomorphism. Moreover, it is clear that for any
y\in p^{n+1}\mathfrak{g}\setminus 0
we have
\exp(y)\neq 1
in
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
and so the map is injective.

For the continuity, we choose a minimal topological generating set
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
for
G
. By
[
6
, Theorem 4.9]
this choice gives a homeomorphism
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}\rightarrow G
via
a\mapsto x_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots x_{r}^{a_{r}}
. So we can consider the topology of
G
as being given by the filtration
G_{i}=\{x_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots x_{r}^{a_{r}}\mid p^{i}|a_{j}\text{ for all }j\}
. Also note that
\exp(p^{n+1}\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\Gamma_{1}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
, so we can see that
\exp(G_{i})\subseteq\Gamma_{rp^{i}}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
. Therefore
x\mapsto\exp(x)
is indeed continuous.
∎

Corollary 2.5.4
.

There is an embedding of
K
-algebras
KG\rightarrow\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
given by
x\mapsto\exp(\text{id}(x))
for
x\in G
.

Proof.

Using
[
14
, Corollary 19.3]
, the map
x\mapsto\exp(\text{id}(x))
extends to an
R
-homomorphism
RG\mapsto\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
, and hence to a
K
-homomorphism
\phi:KG\rightarrow\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
.

Now we check that
\phi
is injective. To do this, we fix a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
for
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
p^{n+1}x_{1},\dots,p^{n+1}x_{r}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis for
L_{G}
and hence a topological generating set for
G
by Proposition
2.4.2
. Then the elements
b^{s}=(p^{n+1}x_{1}-1)^{s_{1}}\dots(p^{n+1}x_{r}-1)^{s_{r}}
(as in Proposition
2.5.2
) satisfy:

\begin{split}\phi(b^{s})&=(e^{p^{n+1}x_{1}}-1)^{s_{1}}\dots(e^{p^{n+1}x_{r}}-1)^{s_{r}}\\
&=p^{(n+1)|s|}x_{1}^{s_{1}}\dots x_{r}^{s_{r}}+\text{ (higher degree terms)}.\end{split}

Now suppose
0\neq x=\underset{s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}}{\sum}C_{s}b^{s}\in KG
satisfies
\phi(x)=0
. Suppose
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is minimal such that some
s
with
|s|=m
satisfies
C_{s}\neq 0
. Then
0=\phi(x)=\underset{|s|=m}{\sum}C_{s}x_{1}^{s_{1}}\dots x_{m}^{s_{m}}+\text{higher degree terms}
. Lemma
2.2.2
now implies that all
C_{s}
with
|s|=m
are zero, a contradiction. So
\phi
is injective.

∎

This proof now implies the following result.

Corollary 2.5.5
.

Given a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
for
\mathfrak{g}
, we can identify
KG
with the following subalgebra of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
:

KG=\{\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}}C_{s}(e^{p^{n+1}x_{1}}-1)^{s_{1}}\dots(e^{p^{n+1}x_{r}}-1)^{s_{r}}\mid C_{s}\in K\text{ are bounded}\}.

2.6
A faithfulness result for Iwasawa algebras

The following result is a modification of
[
1
, Theorem 5.3]
.

Theorem 2.6.1
.

Let
G
be an
F
-uniform group with associated
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebra
L_{G}=p^{n+1}\mathfrak{g}
. Let
N,P
be
F
-uniform subgroups with associated
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebras
L_{N}=p^{n+1}\mathfrak{n},L_{P}=p^{n+1}\mathfrak{p}
respectively such that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
.

Suppose
\hat{M}
is a
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
-module, such that
\hat{M}
is free as a
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{n})_{n,K}}
-module by restriction with a basis
\mathcal{B}
, and that
U(\mathfrak{n}_{K})\mathcal{B}
is a faithful locally finite
RP
-module. Then
\hat{M}
is faithful as a
KG
-module.

Proof.

Let
g_{1},\dots,g_{r}
be a minimal topological generating set for
G
such that
g_{1},\dots,g_{s}
is a topological generating set for
N
and
g_{s+1},\dots,g_{n}
is a topological generating set for
P
(this can be done due to Proposition
2.4.2
).

Also let
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
be an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-basis for
\mathfrak{g}
such that
x_{1},\dots,x_{i}
and
x_{i+1},\dots,x_{m}
are bases for
\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{p}
respectively.

Write
b^{\alpha}=(g_{1}-1)^{\alpha_{1}}\dots(g_{r}-1)^{\alpha_{r}}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}
, and
x^{\beta}=x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\dots x_{m}^{\beta_{m}}
for
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
.

Let
\zeta\in\Ann_{RG}\hat{M}
. Then
\zeta
has the form
\zeta=\underset{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}}{\sum}C_{\alpha}b^{\alpha}
for some
C_{\alpha}\in R
. So we can write
\zeta=\underset{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}}{\sum}b^{\alpha}\zeta_{\alpha}
for some
\zeta_{\alpha}\in RP
.

Let
v\in U(\mathfrak{n}_{K})\mathcal{B}
. Then since
U(\mathfrak{n}_{K})\mathcal{B}
is locally finite as an
RP
-module,
RPv
is contained in the
R
-span of a set
\pi^{-r}\{x^{\beta}w\mid\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{i},|\beta|\leq k,w\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\}
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is a finite subset of
\mathcal{B}
.

So for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
we can write
\zeta_{\alpha}\cdot v=\underset{|\beta|\leq k,w\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}{\sum}D_{\alpha,\beta,w}x^{\beta}w
for some
D_{\alpha,\beta,w}\in R
, uniformly bounded in
\alpha,\beta
and
w
.

Then we have :

\begin{split}0&=\zeta\cdot v\\
&=\sum_{\alpha}b^{\alpha}\zeta^{\alpha}\cdot v\\
&=\sum_{\alpha}b^{\alpha}\sum_{\beta,w}D_{\alpha,\beta,w}x^{\beta}w\\
&=\sum_{w}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}D_{\alpha,\beta,w}b^{\alpha}x^{\beta}w.\end{split}

Since
\mathcal{B}
is a free basis for
\hat{M}
over
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{n})_{n,K}}
, we can see that
\underset{\alpha,\beta}{\sum}D_{\alpha,\beta,w}b^{\alpha}x^{\beta}=0
for each
w
. So now fix a
w\in\mathcal{B}
.

Now, from the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 5.3]
, the multiplication map
KN\otimes_{K}U(\mathfrak{n}_{K})\rightarrow\widehat{U(\mathfrak{n})_{n,K}}
is injective. So
\underset{\alpha,\beta}{\sum}D_{\alpha,\beta,w}b^{\alpha}\otimes x^{\beta}=0
. Since the
x^{\beta}\in U(\mathfrak{n}_{K})
are linearly independent, this means
\underset{\alpha}{\sum}D_{\alpha,\beta,w}b^{\alpha}=0
for any
\beta
, and so all
D_{\alpha,\beta,w}=0
since the
b^{\alpha}
are linearly independent in
KN
.

Thus
\zeta_{\alpha}\cdot v=0
for each
\alpha
and each
v\in U(\mathfrak{n}_{K})\mathcal{B}
. Since
U(\mathfrak{n}_{K})\mathcal{B}
is faithful over
RP
, it now follows that each
\zeta_{\alpha}=0
. So
\zeta=0
.

∎

The main setting in which we want to use this result is for induced modules, as in the following corollary.

Corollary 2.6.2
.

Let
G
be an
F
-uniform group with associated
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebra
L_{G}=p^{n+1}\mathfrak{g}
. Let
N,P
be
F
-uniform subgroups with associated
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebras
L_{N}=p^{n+1}\mathfrak{n},L_{P}=p^{n+1}\mathfrak{p}
respectively such that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})
be finite-dimensional over
K
. Take
M=U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{p}_{K})}V
. Suppose
M
is a faithful
RP
-module. Then
\hat{M}
is a faithful
KG
-module.

Note that the condition that
V\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})
is for instance met if
\mathfrak{p}
has a triangular decomposition and
V\in\mathcal{O}_{n}(\mathfrak{p})
by Corollary
2.2.14
. Also note that Proposition
2.3.1
implies that
M
can indeed be considered as an
RP
-module.

Proof.

Taking a
K
-basis
\mathcal{B}
for
V
,
\mathcal{B}
is a generating set for
M
over
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
. This set is a free
U(\mathfrak{n}_{K})
-basis for
M
, so we can define a corresponding good filtration
\Gamma_{i}M
for
M
. Given a free
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-basis
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
for
\mathfrak{n}
,
\Gamma_{0}M
has free
R
-basis
\{(p^{n}x)^{s}v\mid s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r},v\in\mathcal{B}\}
where
(p^{n}x)^{s}
denotes
(p^{n}x_{1})^{s_{1}}\dots(p^{n}x_{r})^{s_{r}}
.

So
\hat{M}
can be described as
\{\underset{s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r},v\in\mathcal{B}}{\sum}C_{s,v}(p^{n}x)^{s}v\mid C_{s,v}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }|s|\rightarrow\infty\}
, and in particular is a free
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{n})_{n,K}}
-module with finite basis
\mathcal{B}
. Then
M=U(\mathfrak{n}_{K})\mathcal{B}
is a locally finite
RP
-module by Proposition
2.3.1
. So if
M
is faithful over
RP
, then
\hat{M}
is faithful over
KG
by Theorem
2.6.1
.

∎

3
Reduction to the semisimple case

3.1
Set-up

For this section, let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{s}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
be an
\mathcal{O}_{F}

3.2
First step

Lemma 3.2.1
.

U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}_{K}+\mathfrak{a}_{K})}V

-quotient of

In general, suppose

.
∎

Corollary 3.2.2

\Ann_{RP^{\prime}}M_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)\subseteq\Ann_{RP^{\prime}}U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}_{K}+\mathfrak{a}_{K})}V

Proof.

Note that

The image of the annihilator under

Now we claim that

Lemma 3.3.1

Now consider the algebra isomorphism

4.2
Completions and the duality functor

We now want to see how the duality functor interacts with our completions.

Lemma 4.2.1
.

Suppose
M\in\mathcal{O}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
and
x\in\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}
is a weight vector. Then for any
\phi\in M^{\lor}
,
x\cdot\phi\in M^{\lor}
is given by
(x\cdot\phi)(v)=\phi(\tau(x)v)
for
v\in M
.

Proof.

Firstly, note that
x\cdot\phi
can be considered as an element of
M^{\lor}
by Lemma
2.2.16
.

Both sides of the equality are linear in
\phi
, so we can assume
\phi
is a weight vector. Say
\phi
has weight
\mu
and
x
has weight
\nu
, then

has weight
\mu+\nu
. So for

in any weight space of

other than

, we have
(x\cdot\phi)(v)=0=\phi(\tau(x)v)
. So it is sufficient to check the equality for

. Fix some

.

Fix an ordering of
\Phi^{+}
, and write
e^{s}=\underset{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}{\prod}e_{\alpha}^{s_{\alpha}}
and
f^{s}=\\
underset{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}{\prod}f_{\alpha}^{s_{\alpha}}
for

(with the order of the multiplication subject to the chosen ordering of

). Then using Lemma

we can write

and the sum is over all

such that

has weight
\nu
. Then

.

Since

is locally finite over

, for all but finitely many

. Moreover, for each fixed

has weight
\nu
. Therefore there are only finitely many pairs

such that

has weight
\nu
and

Similarly, since

is locally finite over

, there are only finitely many pairs

such that

has weight
\nu
and

So let

be the set of all the pairs

with

has weight

So

Now, whenever

&=\sum_{(s,t)\in\mathcal{S}}\phi(H_{s,t}\cdot f^{s}e^{t}v)\\
&=\phi(\sum_{(s,t)\in\mathcal{S}}H_{s,t}f^{s}e^{t}v)\\

5
Annihilator of a collection of finite-dimensional modules

5.1
Motivation and outline

Motivated by Theorem
4.1.1
, we want to show that an affinoid generalised Verma module
M_{I}(\lambda)
is faithful over the Iwasawa algebra
RL
, where
L
corresponds to
p^{n+1}\mathfrak{l}
with
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{l}_{I}
.

To this end, we look at a family of infinitely many finite-dimensional
U(\mathfrak{l}_{K})
-submodules of
M_{I}(\lambda)
. Note that any finite-dimensional
RL
-module is certainly not faithful. But we want to show that the simultaneous annihilator in
RL
of our family of finite-dimensional submodules is contained in the annihilator of some induced module for
\mathfrak{l}
.

More specifically, the family of finite-dimensional modules we look at will be the irreducible highest-weight
U(\mathfrak{l}_{K})
-modules
L(\lambda-\underset{\alpha\in\Delta\setminus I}{\sum}c_{\alpha}\alpha)
for
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Denote
L_{\lambda,c}=L(\lambda-\underset{\alpha\in\Delta\setminus I}{\sum}c_{\alpha}\alpha)
for any
c\in(p^{-n}R)^{\Delta\setminus I}
. Write
I^{o}=\{\beta\in I\mid\alpha(h_{\beta})=0\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Delta\setminus I\}
, which we can consider as the interior of
I
in the Dynkin diagram with nodes
\Delta
. Also write
M_{I,\lambda,c}
for the generalised Verma module

. Note that for any
\beta\in I^{o}
and any
c
we have
(\lambda-\sum c_{\alpha}\alpha)(h_{\beta})=\lambda(h_{\beta})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, so we can define such a generalised Verma module.

The main points of our argument will then be as follows.

1.

We will use a density argument to show that if
x\in\widehat{U(\mathfrak{l})_{n,K}}
annihilates all
L_{\lambda,c}
for
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then

annihilates all
L_{\lambda,c}
for
c_{\alpha}\in p^{-n}R
.

2.

We will define surjective
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{l})_{n,K}}
-homomorphisms
\phi_{c}:\widehat{M_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)}\rightarrow\widehat{M_{I,\lambda,c}}
for

. Then if
x\in\underset{c\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\Delta\setminus I}}{\bigcap}\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{l})_{n,K}}}L_{\lambda,c}
and

, we can see that
\phi_{c}(x\cdot v)=x\phi_{c}(v)=0
for all
c
such that
M_{I,\lambda,c}
is irreducible.

3.

We show that if
w\in\widehat{M_{I,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\lambda)}
and
\phi_{c}(w)=0
for all
c
such that
M_{I,\lambda,c}
is irreducible, then

. This will involve looking at a certain set of
c
for which an irreducibility criterion is met, then using a density argument.

The result we aim to prove in this subsection is as follows. For any

Lemma 5.4.4

Suppose

Proof.

By

Note that

Say

Proposition 7.0.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattice of a split semisimple
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-Lie algebra with root system
\Phi
and such that
\mathfrak{g}

\lambda:\mathfrak{h}_{K}\rightarrow K

such that
\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\lor}\rangle\notin\mathbb{N}_{0}

Then
\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}}\widehat{L(\lambda)}\subseteq\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}}\widehat{L(s_{\alpha}\cdot\lambda)}

Firstly, we prove the following lemma. Write

for the highest-weight vectors of

Lemma 7.0.2
.

Suppose
x\in\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}}\widehat{L(\lambda)}
has weight
0
. Then
x\cdot v_{s_{\alpha}\cdot\lambda}=0
in
\widehat{M(s_{\alpha}\cdot\lambda)}
.

Proof.

By our assumption (from the introduction) that
p
does not divide the determinant of the Cartan matrix of
\Phi
, we have an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-basis
\{h^{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Delta\}
dual to
\Delta
, that is, with
\beta(h^{\gamma})=\delta_{\beta,\gamma}
for
\beta,\gamma\in\Delta
.

We consider the action of

on
f_{\alpha}^{[s]}v_{\lambda}
, where
f_{\alpha}^{[s]}=\frac{1}{s!}f_{\alpha}^{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We write
a=\lambda(h_{\alpha})\notin\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Claim:
for any
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have
e_{\alpha}\cdot f_{\alpha}^{[s]}v_{\lambda}=(a+1-s)f_{\alpha}^{[s-1]}v_{\lambda}
.

Proof of claim:

\begin{split}e_{\alpha}\cdot f_{\alpha}^{[s]}v_{\lambda}&=\frac{1}{s!}\text{ad}(e_{\alpha})(f_{\alpha}^{s})v_{\lambda}\\
&=\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}f_{\alpha}^{s-1-i}\text{ad}(e_{\alpha})(f_{\alpha})f_{\alpha}^{i}v_{\lambda}\\
&=\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}f_{\alpha}^{s-1-i}h_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}^{i}v_{\lambda}\\
&=\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}f_{\alpha}^{s-1-i}(\lambda-i\alpha)(h_{\alpha})f_{\alpha}^{i}v_{\lambda}\text{ (because }f_{\alpha}^{i}v_{\lambda}\text{ has weight }\lambda-i\alpha\text{)}\\
&=\frac{1}{s!}\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}(a-2i)f_{\alpha}^{s-1}v_{\lambda}\\
&=\frac{1}{s!}(sa-s(s-1))f_{\alpha}^{s-1}v_{\lambda}\left(\text{ because }\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}i=\frac{1}{2}s(s-1)\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{(s-1)!}(a+1-s)f_{\alpha}^{s-1}v_{\lambda}\\
&=(a+1-s)f_{\alpha}^{[s-1]}v_{\lambda}.\end{split}

As a consequence of this claim, we can see that
e_{\alpha}^{s}f_{\alpha}^{[s]}v_{\lambda}=(a+1-s)(a+2-s)\dots av_{\lambda}
is a non-zero multiple of
v_{\lambda}
, which means that the image of
f_{\alpha}^{[s]}
in
L(\lambda)
is non-zero.

Now apply Lemma
2.2.2
and the fact that
\lambda(p^{n}\mathfrak{h}_{R})\subseteq R

as a convergent sum of monomials of the form:

To see this, firstly note that if

annihilates both all

is a highest-weight vector. We can also see that

has weight

, which is not the weight of any non-zero element of

for some

.

Using the fact that

has weight

, we can write
x=\underset{t\geq 0}{\sum}f_{\alpha}^{t}e_{\alpha}^{t}Q_{t}(\lambda(h^{\alpha})-h^{\alpha})+(\text{ monomials in }J)
where the

for all

, and
L(\lambda)=M_{\{\alpha\}}(\lambda)
. Thus
\Ann_{KG}\widehat{L(\lambda)}=0
.

Now, suppose
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{<0}
(so
a\leq-2
since
\lambda
is regular). Then
\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}}\widehat{L(\lambda)}\subseteq\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}}\widehat{L(s_{\alpha}\cdot\lambda)}
by Proposition
7.0.1
, and
s_{\alpha}\cdot\lambda=-(a+2)\omega_{1}+(a+b+1)\omega_{2}
is of the form covered above.

So the result holds for
\lambda
if
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, and also holds if
b\in\mathbb{Z}
by symmetry.

Now suppose
a,b\notin\mathbb{Z}
. If
a+b+2\notin\mathbb{N}
, then
L(\lambda)=M(\lambda)
since condition (**) holds with
I=\emptyset
. So assume
a+b+2\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}}\widehat{L(\lambda)}\subseteq\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n,K}}}\widehat{L(s_{\alpha}\cdot\lambda)}
and
s_{\alpha}\cdot\lambda=-(a+2)\omega_{1}+(a+b+1)\omega_{2}
with
a+b+1\in\mathbb{Z}
. So by the cases above,
\Ann_{KG}\widehat{L(s_{\alpha}\cdot\lambda)}=0
and the result holds for
\lambda
.

Case 3
:
\lambda
is a regular, integral weight.

First, suppose
\lambda=s_{\beta}\cdot\mu
where
\mu
is a dominant integral weight. By
[
7
, Example 9.5]
,
M_{\{\alpha\}}(\mu)
has composition factors
L(\mu)
and
L(\lambda)
. So the maximal submodule of
M_{\{\alpha\}}(\mu)
must be isomorphic to
L(\lambda)
, and
M_{\{\alpha\}}(\mu)/L(\lambda)\cong L(\mu)
.

Now,
\widehat{M_{\{\alpha\}}(\mu)}
is faithful over
KG
by Theorem
1.3.3
. Moreover,
\widehat{L(\lambda)}\subseteq\widehat{M_{\{\alpha\}}(\mu)}
with
\widehat{M_{\{\alpha\}}(\mu)}/\widehat{L(\lambda)}\cong\widehat{L(\mu)}
. Since
\mu
is dominant integral,
L(\mu)
is finite-dimensional. Therefore
\Ann_{KG}\widehat{L(\mu)}\neq 0
, for instance a sufficiently large power of
\exp(p^{n+1}e_{\alpha})-1
will annihilate
\widehat{L(\mu)}
.

Fix
0\neq y\in\Ann_{KG}\widehat{L(\mu)}
So
y\cdot\widehat{M_{\{\alpha\}}(\mu)}\subseteq\widehat{L(\lambda)}
. Suppose
x\in\Ann_{KG}\widehat{L(\lambda)}
. Then
xy\cdot\widehat{M_{\{\alpha\}}(\mu)}=0
. So
xy=0
, and thus
x=0
since
KG
is a domain. So
\Ann_{KG}\widehat{L(\lambda)}=0
.

Now, in general
\lambda
will be linked to a dominant integral weight. So
\lambda
has one of the forms:
s_{\alpha}\cdot\mu,s_{\beta}\cdot\mu,s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}\cdot\mu,s_{\beta}s_{\alpha}\cdot\mu,s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}s_{\alpha}\cdot\mu
.
Using Proposition
7.0.1
we have
\Ann_{KG}\widehat{L(s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}s_{\alpha}\cdot\mu)}\subseteq\Ann_{KG}\widehat{L(s_{\beta}s_{\alpha}\cdot\mu)}\subseteq\Ann_{KG}\widehat{L(s_{\alpha}\cdot\mu)}=0
. By symmetry, also
\Ann_{KG}\widehat{L(s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}\cdot\mu)}=\Ann_{KG}\widehat{L(s_{\beta}\cdot\mu)}=0
.

∎

Proof of theorem
2
.

Suppose
M
is an infinite-dimensional highest-weight module for
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K})
with highest-weight
\lambda
, where
\lambda(p^{n}\mathfrak{h}_{R})\subseteq R
. Then
M
is a quotient of the Verma module
M(\lambda)
.

The composition factors of
M(\lambda)
all have the form
L(\mu)
for some weights
\mu\in\lambda-\mathbb{N}_{0}\Delta
, which also satisfy
\mu(p^{n}\mathfrak{h}_{R})\subseteq R
. Therefore all composition factors of
M
also have this form.

Since
M
is infinite-dimensional,
M
has a composition factor of the form
L(\mu)
which is infinite-dimensional. Then by exactness of the completion functor (Proposition
2.2.4
),
\hat{M}
has a subquotient of the form
\widehat{L(\mu)}
. By Theorem
7.0.4
this composition factor is faithful over
KG
, therefore
\hat{M}
is faithful over
KG
.
∎

8
Application to prime ideals of Iwasawa algebras

For this section we assume
\mathfrak{g}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattice of a split simple
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebra with root system
\Phi
, and a fixed choice of root basis
\Delta
, such that
\mathfrak{g}
has a Chevalley basis and a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
. We make the assumption that
R
has uniformiser
p
(since this is assumed in
[
2
, §10]
). Assume
G
is a uniform pro-p group with
L_{G}=p^{n+1}\mathfrak{g}
, that
p
does not divide the determinant of the Cartan matrix of any root subsystem of
\Phi
, and
p
is a very good prime for
\Phi
in the sense of
[
2
, §6.8]
. (In particular, for any given
\Phi
, all sufficiently large primes meet these conditions, while for type
A_{2}
these conditions are met precisely when
p>3
).

We also take
\mathbf{G}
to be a split simple, connected, simply connected affine algebraic group over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
such that
G\leq\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is an open subgroup and the Lie algebra associated to
\mathbf{G}
is
\mathfrak{g}
. For instance if
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, we take
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{SL_{3}}
and
G=\ker(SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rightarrow SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
.

Theorem 8.0.1
.

Suppose we can give a positive answer to question B in the case of
\mathfrak{g}
, for any deformation parameter
n
and for any choice of the field
K
.

Then any prime ideal of
KG
is the annihilator of an irreducible finite-dimensional module.

Note that this theorem together with theorem
2
will imply theorem
3
.

We start with the following lemma, which is a general result in ring theory.

Lemma 8.0.2
.

Suppose
A
is a unital
K
-algebra and
P
is a non-zero prime ideal of
A
of finite codimension in
A
. Then
P
is the annihilator of an irreducible finite-dimensional
A
-module.

Proof.

Let
M=A/P
, an
A
-module which is finite-dimensional over
K
and which satisfies
\Ann_{A}M=P
. Then
M
has a composition series, say
0=M_{0}<M_{1}<\dots<M_{r}=M
. Let
L_{j}=M_{j}/M_{j-1}
for
1\leq j\leq r
, so each
L_{j}
is an irreducible finite-dimensional
A
-module. Let
I_{j}=\Ann_{A}L_{j}
for each
j
. So
P\subseteq I_{j}
for each
j
.

Then
I_{1}\dots I_{r}\subseteq\Ann_{A}M=P
, so since
P
is prime, we have
I_{j}\subseteq P
for some
j
, thus
I_{j}=P
, and the result follows.

∎

Suppose
P
is a non-zero prime ideal of
KG
. If
P
has finite codimension in
KG
, it follows from the lemma above that
P
is the annihilator of an irreducible finite-dimensional module. So now assume
KG/P
is infinite-dimensional, we then wish to show that
P=0
. We write
P_{m}=KG^{p^{m}}\cap P
for any
m
.

Proposition 8.0.3
.

For sufficiently large
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}
-module
M=(\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}\ast G/G^{p^{m}})\otimes_{KG}KG/P
is infinite-dimensional. Moreover,
P_{m}\subseteq\Ann_{KG^{p^{m}}}M
and
M
is a cyclic
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}
-module.

Proof.

We use
[
2
, §10.13]
. From the lemma in §10.13, the canonical dimension
d(M)
of a finitely generated
KG
-module (defined in
[
2
, §2.5]
) satisfies
d(M)=0
if and only if
M
is finite-dimensional over
K
. Now applying the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 10.13]
we can see that for sufficiently large
m
, the module
M=(\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}\ast G/G^{p^{m}})\otimes_{KG}KG/P
satisfies
d(M)=d(KG/P)
. In particular,
M
is infinite-dimensional since
KG/P
is infinite-dimensional.

Now we want to show that
P_{m}
annihilates
M
. By
[
2
, Proposition 10.6(b)]
, we can see that
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}\ast G/G^{p^{m}}\cong\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}\otimes_{RG^{p^{m}}}RG=\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}\otimes_{KG^{p^{m}}}KG
. Therefore
M\cong(\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}\otimes_{KG^{p^{m}}}KG)\otimes_{KG}KG/P\cong\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}\otimes_{KG^{p^{m}}}KG/P
as
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}
-modules.

Now consider the natural surjection
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}\rightarrow M
of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}
-modules. This induces a filtration of
M
with respect to the filtration
\Gamma_{i}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}
on
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}
(as defined in §
2.2
). We know
KG^{p^{m}}
is a dense subring of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}
with respect to the filtration
\Gamma
, and therefore the image of
KG^{p^{m}}
in
M
is a dense subset of
M
.

Now
P_{m}
annihilates the image of
KG^{p^{m}}
in
M=\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}\otimes_{KG^{p^{m}}}KG/P
, and therefore by the density annihilates
M
.

Finally, since there is a surjection
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}\rightarrow M
of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}
-modules,
M
is a cyclic module as required.

∎

Now we have this result, fix some
m
such that the
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}
-module
M=(\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}\ast G/G^{p^{m}})\otimes_{KG}KG/P
is infinite-dimensional.

Proposition 8.0.4
.

There is an infinite-dimensional
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}
-quotient
M^{\prime}
of
M
such that the action of the centre
Z(\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}})
on
M^{\prime}
is locally finite.

Proof.

By applying
[
2
, Proposition 9.4]
, there is a quotient
M^{\prime}
of
M
such that
d(M^{\prime})>0
(which means
M^{\prime}
is infinite-dimensional) and such that
M^{\prime}
is locally finite over
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{nm,K}^{\mathbf{G}}}
. Now, by
[
1
, Theorem C]
,
Z(\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}})=\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{nm,K}^{\mathbf{G}}}
and the result follows.
∎

So now take
M^{\prime}
as in the proposition above. Since
P_{m}
annihilates
M
, we can see that
P_{m}
also annihilates
M^{\prime}
. Also
M
is a cyclic
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{mn,K}}
-module, so is
M^{\prime}
.

Proposition 8.0.5
.

There is a finite field extension
K^{\prime}
of
K
, with ramification index
e
, uniformiser
\pi^{\prime}
and ring of integers
R^{\prime}
, together with a weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{K^{\prime}}
such that
\lambda(\pi^{nme}\mathfrak{h}_{R^{\prime}})\subseteq R^{\prime}
, and an infinite-dimensional cyclic
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{nme,K^{\prime}}}
-module
N
with central character
\chi_{\lambda}
such that
P_{me}
annihilates
N
.

Proof.

The module
N
is constructed in the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 9.5]
.
N
is obtained by first taking a quotient of
M^{\prime}
, and then applying a base change. This means that since
P_{m}
annihilates
M^{\prime}
,
P_{me}
annihilates
N
. Also since
M^{\prime}
is cyclic, so is
N
.
∎

Now take
K^{\prime}
,
N
and
\lambda
as in the proposition. Write
n^{\prime}=nme
. We want to use
[
16
, Theorem B]
, which applies to primitive ideals with central character. So to this end we need to show that
N
has an infinite-dimensional irreducible subquotient.

Lemma 8.0.6
.

\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}
is a (both left and right) Noetherian ring.

Proof.

In
[
2
, 5.16]
it is observed that
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}
is an almost commutative affinoid
K^{\prime}
-algebra and in
[
2
, 3.8]
it is observed that almost commutative affinoid
K^{\prime}
-algebras are Noetherian.
∎

Lemma 8.0.7
.

Suppose
L
is a non-zero finite-dimensional
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K^{\prime}})
-module and
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d>\dim_{K^{\prime}}L
. Then
L^{d}
cannot be a cyclic
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K^{\prime}})
-module.

Proof.

Suppose
(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in L^{d}
. Then
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in L
are
K^{\prime}
-linearly dependent since
d>\dim_{K^{\prime}}L
, so choose
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\in K^{\prime}
not all zero such that
\sum a_{i}x_{i}=0
. Then for any
y\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{K^{\prime}})
we have
\sum a_{i}yx_{i}=0
.

Choose any
0\neq v\in L
and any
i
with
a_{i}\neq 0
. Then the element of
L^{d}
with
i
-th entry
v
and all other entries
0
cannot be contained in
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K^{\prime}})(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
.
∎

Lemma 8.0.8
.

There exists
D\in\mathbb{N}
such that all finite-dimensional quotients of
N
have dimension at most
D
.

Write
W
for the Weyl group of
\Phi
, and
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\alpha
. Then given a weight
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}_{K^{\prime}}^{*}
, recall the dot action of
w\in W
on
\mu
is given by
w\cdot\mu=w(\mu+\rho)-\rho
.

We say a weight
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}_{K^{\prime}}^{*}
is dominant integral if
\mu(h_{\alpha})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
. It is a standard fact in Lie algebra representation theory (see for instance
[
5
, 7.2.6]
) that the irreducible highest-weight module
L(\mu)
with highest-weight
\mu
is finite-dimensional if and only if
\mu
is dominant integral.

Proof of Lemma
8.0.8
.

Suppose
N^{\prime}
is a finite-dimensional quotient of
N
. Then since
N
is a cyclic
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}
-module and has central character
\chi
, it follows that
N^{\prime}
is also a cyclic
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}
-module and has central character
\chi
.

Note that any generator for
N^{\prime}
over
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}
also generates
N^{\prime}
over
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K^{\prime}})
: if
v\in N^{\prime}
such that
N^{\prime}=\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}v
, then
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K^{\prime}})v
is a
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}
-submodule of
N^{\prime}
by Proposition
2.2.9
and hence
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K^{\prime}})v=\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}v=N^{\prime}
.

Now by Weyl’s Theorem (see for instance
[
17
, Theorem 7.8.11]
),
N^{\prime}
is a direct sum of simple modules. Moreover, by
[
5
, 7.2.2]
any simple finite-dimensional
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K^{\prime}})
-module has the form
L(\mu)
for some weight
\mu
. So
N^{\prime}
is a direct sum of modules of the form
L(\mu)
with
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}_{K^{\prime}}^{*}
, and with
\mu
dominant integral.

By
[
7
, Theorem 1.10(b)]
, for any two weights
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
we have
\chi_{\mu_{1}}=\chi_{\mu_{2}}
if and only if
\mu_{1}=w\cdot\mu_{2}
for some
w\in W
.

Moreover, there is at most one weight
\mu
such that
\mu=w\cdot\lambda
for some
w\in W
and
\mu
is dominant integral. To see this, suppose
\nu
is a dominant integral weight and
w\in W
such that
w\cdot\nu
is also dominant integral. Then for any
\alpha\in\Phi
we have
\langle w\cdot\nu+\rho,\alpha^{\lor}\rangle=\langle\nu+\rho,w^{-1}(\alpha)^{\lor}\rangle\in\mathbb{N}
and hence
w^{-1}(\alpha)\in\Phi^{+}
. So
w^{-1}(\Phi^{+})=\Phi^{+}
, which implies
w^{-1}=1
.

Thus there is at most one weight
\mu
such that
\chi_{\mu}=\chi
and
L(\mu)
is finite-dimensional. If no such
\mu
exists than the only finite-dimensional quotient of
N
is
0
, and we are done. So assume
\mu
satisfies
\chi_{\mu}=\chi
and
L(\mu)
is finite-dimensional, and let
d=\dim_{K^{\prime}}L(\mu)
.

Then
N^{\prime}
is a direct sum of copies of
L(\mu)
. We know
N^{\prime}
is a cyclic
U(\mathfrak{g}_{K^{\prime}})
-module, so by Lemma
8.0.7

N^{\prime}
is a copy of at most
d
copies of
L(\mu)
. Thus
\dim_{K^{\prime}}N^{\prime}\leq d^{2}
, and we are done.

∎

Corollary 8.0.9
.

N
has an infinite-dimensional irreducible subquotient.

Proof.

Since
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}
is a Noetherian ring and
N
is a finitely generated
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}
-module, it follows that
N
is a Noetherian
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}
-module.

Now by Lemma
8.0.8
, choose
N^{\prime}
to be a finite-dimensional quotient of
N
of maximal dimension. Say
N^{\prime}=N/N_{1}
, where
N_{1}\leq N
is an infinite-dimensional submodule. Then
N_{1}
is a Noetherian
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}
-module since
N
is Noetherian.

Now since
N_{1}
is Noetherian we can choose a maximal submodule
N_{2}
of
N_{1}
. Then
N_{1}/N_{2}
is infinite-dimensional since otherwise
N/N_{2}
would be a finite-dimensional quotient of
N
with larger dimension than
N^{\prime}
. So
N_{1}/N_{2}
is an infinite-dimensional irreducible subquotient of
N
.
∎

Now let
N_{1}
be an infinite-dimensional irreducible subquotient of
N
. Then
P_{me}\subseteq KG^{p^{me}}\subseteq K^{\prime}G^{p^{me}}
annihilates
N_{1}
, and
\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}}N_{1}
is a primitive ideal of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}
with central character
\chi_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 8.0.10
.

There exists a weight
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}_{K^{\prime}}
with
\mu(p^{n^{\prime}}\mathfrak{h}_{R^{\prime}})\subseteq R^{\prime}
such that
L(\mu)
is infinite-dimensional and
\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}}N_{1}=\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{nme,K^{\prime}}}}\widehat{L(\mu)}
.

Proof.

It follows from
[
16
, Theorem B]
(applied to the group scheme
\mathbf{G}(R^{\prime})
) that there is a weight
\mu
such that
\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}}N_{1}=\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}}\widehat{L(\mu)}
. Moreover, since
N_{1}
is infinite-dimensional, it follows that
\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}}N_{1}
must have infinite codimension in
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}
. Therefore
\Ann_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})_{n^{\prime},K^{\prime}}}}\widehat{L(\mu)}
also has infinite codimension, and so
\widehat{L(\mu)}
must be infinite-dimensional. Therefore
L(\mu)
is infinite-dimensional.
∎

We can now conclude the proof of Theorem
8.0.1
: given a positive answer to question B,
\widehat{L(\mu)}
is faithful as a
K^{\prime}G^{p^{me}}
-module. We can then see that
P_{me}=0
since
P_{me}\subseteq K^{\prime}G^{p^{me}}
annihilates
N_{1}
and hence annihilates
\widehat{L(\mu)}
. It then follows from the argument in
[
1
, §5.7]
that
P=0
.

.

2.
Humble Extensions

Before introducing humble extensions, we begin this section with a simple observation concerning finitely generated extensions.

Proposition 2.1
:
Suppose that
{\mathcal{M}}\prec{\mathcal{N}}
and that
a
generates
{\mathcal{N}}
over
{\mathcal{M}}
. Let
{\mathcal{M}}\prec{\mathcal{N}}_{0}\preccurlyeq{\mathcal{N}}
, where
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
is finitely generated over
{\mathcal{M}}
. Then there is
b\leq a
that generates
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
over
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof
. Let
a_{0}
generate
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
over
{\mathcal{M}}
. Then there is an
{\mathcal{M}}
-definable function
t_{0}:M\longrightarrow M
such that
{\mathcal{N}}\models a_{0}=t_{0}(a)
. Using recursion in
{\mathcal{M}}
, define the function
t:M\longrightarrow M
so that

t(x)=\begin{cases}x,\mbox{ if }t_{0}(z)\neq t_{0}(x)\mbox{ for all }z<x,\\
t(z),{\mbox{ where }}z=\min\{y<x:t_{0}(y)=t_{0}(x)\},\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Clearly,
t
is
{\mathcal{M}}
-definable and
{\mathcal{M}}\models\forall x[t(x)\leq x]
. Let
b=t^{\mathcal{N}}(a)
, so that
{\mathcal{N}}\models b\leq a
. We will show that
b
generates
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
over
{\mathcal{M}}
. This can be accomplished by showing that there are
{\mathcal{M}}
-definable functions
t_{1},t_{2}
such that

{\mathcal{N}}\models t_{1}(a_{0})=b\wedge t_{2}(b)=a_{0}.

Note that
t_{1},t_{2}
need not be total, but it should be that
a_{0}\in\dom(t^{\mathcal{N}}_{1})
and
b\in\dom(t^{\mathcal{N}}_{2})
.

Let
R,R_{0}
be the ranges of
t,t_{0}
, respectively. Let
t_{1}:R\longrightarrow M
and
t_{2}:R_{0}\longrightarrow M
be such that

(
*
)

t_{1}(t(x))=t_{0}(x)\mbox{ and }t_{2}(t_{0}(x))=t(x)

for each
x\in M
.
Observe that both
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
are well defined since for any
x,y\in M
,
t(x)=t(y)
iff
t_{0}(x)=t_{0}(y)
. Thus, both
t_{1},t_{2}
are
{\mathcal{M}}
-definable. It follows from
(*)
that

t_{1}^{\mathcal{N}}(b)=t_{1}^{\mathcal{N}}(t^{\mathcal{N}}(a))=t^{\mathcal{N}}_{0}(a)=a_{0}

and

t^{\mathcal{N}}_{2}(a_{0})=t^{\mathcal{N}}_{2}(t^{\mathcal{N}}_{0}(a))=t^{\mathcal{N}}(a)=b,

thereby proving that
b
generates
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
over
{\mathcal{M}}
. ∎

Suppose that
{\mathcal{M}}
is nonstandard. Thus,
\omega
is the
standard
cut of
{\mathcal{M}}
. In this section, we will be concerned with elementary extensions
{\mathcal{N}}\succ{\mathcal{M}}
that are generated over
{\mathcal{M}}
by an element
c
that fills the standard cut of
{\mathcal{M}}
, meaning that
\omega=\{a\in M:{\mathcal{N}}\models a<c\}
. We refer to such extensions as a
humble
extensions. If
{\mathcal{N}}
is a humble extension of
{\mathcal{M}}
, then
{\mathcal{M}}\prec_{\sf cf}{\mathcal{N}}
and
|N|=|M|
.

Corollary 2.2
:
Suppose that
{\mathcal{N}}
is a humble extension of
{\mathcal{M}}
, and
{\mathcal{M}}\prec{\mathcal{N}}_{0}\preccurlyeq{\mathcal{N}}
, where
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
is finitely generated over
{\mathcal{M}}
. Then
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
is a humble extension of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof
. Let
a\in N
fill the standard cut of
{\mathcal{M}}
and generate
{\mathcal{N}}
over
{\mathcal{M}}
. Proposition 2.1 implies that there is
b\leq a
that generates
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
over
{\mathcal{M}}
. Obviously,
b
also fills the standard cut of
{\mathcal{M}}
. ∎

Recall that the standard system of nonstandard
{\mathcal{M}}
is
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})=\cod({\mathcal{M}}/\omega)
, which is a Boolean subalgebra of
{\mathcal{P}}(\omega)
and which contains all finite and cofinite subsets of
\omega
. A filter
{\mathcal{F}}
of
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
is
free
(or
Fréchet
) if it contains all cofinite subsets of
\omega
. An ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
of
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
is nonprincipal iff it is free.

Humble extensions of a nonstandard
{\mathcal{M}}
can be obtained in the following way. Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a nonprincpal ultrafilter of
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
. Then
{\mathcal{U}}
determines a complete 1-type
p(x)

over
{\mathcal{M}}
as follows: if
\theta(x)
is an
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{M}})
-formula, then
\theta(x)\in p(x)
iff there is
A\in{\mathcal{U}}
such that
{\mathcal{M}}\models\theta(a)
for all
a\in A
. Let
{\mathcal{N}}
be generated over
{\mathcal{M}}
by an element
c
that realizes
p(x)
. This
{\mathcal{N}}
is a humble extension of
{\mathcal{M}}
generated by
c
, which fills the standard cut.

Alternatively, the extension
{\mathcal{N}}
can be viewed as a kind of ultrapower, the well known construction of which we now describe. Let
T
be the set of
{\mathcal{M}}
-definable functions
t:M\longrightarrow M
. Define the equivalence relation
\sim
on
T
so that if
t_{1},t_{2}\in T
, then
t_{1}\sim t_{2}
iff
\{n<\omega:t_{1}(n)=t_{2}(n)\}\in{\mathcal{U}}
. Let
[t]
be the equivalence class to which
t
belongs.
By a mixture of theorems of Skolem
[
9
]
and Loś
[
5
]
, there is
{\mathcal{N}}
such that
N=\{[t]:t\in T\}
and for any
{\mathcal{L}}
-formula

\varphi(x_{0},\dots,x_{j-1})
and
t_{0},\ldots,t_{j-1}\in T
,

{\mathcal{N}}\models\varphi([t_{0}],\ldots,[t_{j-1}]){\mbox{ iff }}\{n<\omega:{\mathcal{M}}\models\varphi(t_{0}(n),\ldots,t_{j-1}(n))\}\in{\mathcal{U}}.

By identifying
a\in M
with
[t_{a}]
, where
t_{a}:M\longrightarrow M
is the
{\mathcal{M}}
-definable function that is constantly
a
, we get that
{\mathcal{M}}\prec{\mathcal{N}}
. Thus, we use the suggestive notation

{\mathcal{N}}={\mathcal{M}}^{\omega}/{\mathcal{U}}

when
{\mathcal{N}}
is constructed in either of these ways. Notice that
[id_{M}]
generates
{\mathcal{N}}
over
{\mathcal{M}}
, where
id_{M}:M\longrightarrow M
is the identity function.

Conversely, suppose that
{\mathcal{N}}
is a humble extension of
{\mathcal{M}}
generated over
{\mathcal{M}}
by
c
, which fills the standard cut. Let

{\mathcal{U}}_{c}=\{X\cap\omega\in\ssy(M):X\in\Def({\mathcal{M}})\mbox{ and }a\in X^{\mathcal{N}}\}.

Then,
{\mathcal{U}}_{c}
is a nonprincipal ultrafilter of
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
and
{\mathcal{M}}^{\omega}/{\mathcal{U}}_{c}\cong{\mathcal{N}}
.
In fact, there is an isomorphism
f
such that if
t:M\longrightarrow M
is
{\mathcal{M}}
-definable, then
f([t])=t^{\mathcal{N}}(c)
, so that, in particular,
f([id_{M}])=c
.

Summarizing:

Proposition 2.3
:
Suppose that
{\mathcal{M}}
is nonstandard.

(1)

If
{\mathcal{U}}
is a nonprincipal ultrafilter of
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
, then
{\mathcal{N}}={\mathcal{M}}^{\omega}/{\mathcal{U}}

is a humble extension of
{\mathcal{M}}
generated by
[id_{M}]
.

(2)

If
{\mathcal{N}}
is a humble extension of
{\mathcal{M}}
generated by

over
{\mathcal{M}}
, then there is an isomorphism

that fixes

.

∎

The well known Rudin-Keisler quasi-order
\leq_{\sf RK}
on ultrafilters over
\omega
has a natural generalization to those ultrafilters of
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
. Recall its definition: If
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}}
are ultrafilters over
\omega
, then
{\mathcal{U}}\leq_{\sf RK}{\mathcal{V}}
iff there is
f:\omega\longrightarrow\omega
such that whenever
A\in{\mathcal{U}}
, then
f^{-1}(A)\in{\mathcal{V}}
. We generalize
\leq_{\sf RK}
from
{\mathcal{P}}(\omega)
to
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
.

If
{\mathcal{U}}
,
{\mathcal{V}}
are ultrafilters of
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
, then we define
{\mathcal{U}}\leq_{\sf RK}^{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{V}}
if there is an
{\mathcal{M}}
-definable
t:M\longrightarrow M
such that
whenever
X\in\Def({\mathcal{M}})
and
X\cap\omega\in{\mathcal{U}}
, then
t^{-1}(X)\cap\omega\in{\mathcal{V}}
. For such a
t
, we will say that
t

demonstrates
that
{\mathcal{U}}\leq_{\sf RK}^{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{V}}
. One easily checks that
\leq_{\sf RK}^{\mathcal{M}}
is a quasi-order
-
in other words, if
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{W}}
are ultrafilters of
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
and

{\mathcal{U}}\leq^{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf RK}{\mathcal{V}}\leq^{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf RK}{\mathcal{W}}
, then
{\mathcal{U}}\leq^{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf RK}{\mathcal{W}}
. Moreover, if
s
demonstrates that
{\mathcal{U}}\leq^{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf RK}{\mathcal{V}}
and
t
demonstrates that
{\mathcal{V}}\leq^{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf RK}{\mathcal{W}}
, then

s\circ t
demonstrates that
{\mathcal{U}}\leq^{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf RK}{\mathcal{W}}
.

By the next proposition, there is a simpler definition of
\leq^{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf RK}
if
({\mathbb{N}},\ssy({\mathcal{M}}))\models{\sf ACA_{0}}
.

Proposition 2.4
:
If
{\mathcal{M}}
is nonstandard,

and

,
{\mathcal{V}}
are ultrafilters of

(1)

(2)

, coded in
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
, such that

.

Remark
: Note that

iff
\omega
is a

cut of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof
. Suppose that
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}}
are ultrafilters of
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
, where
{\mathcal{M}}
is nonstandard and
({\mathbb{N}},\ssy({\mathcal{M}}))\models{\sf ACA_{0}}
.
.

(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
: Suppose that
{\mathcal{U}}\leq_{\sf RK}^{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{V}}
. Let
t:M\longrightarrow M
demonstrate that
{\mathcal{U}}\leq_{\sf RK}^{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{V}}
. Let
f=t\cap\omega^{2}
, so that
f:D\longrightarrow\omega
for some
D\subseteq\omega
.
Since
({\mathbb{N}},\ssy({\mathcal{M}}))\models{\sf ACA_{0}}
, then
D\in\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
and

. We claim that

.

If

, then
\omega\backslash R\in{\mathcal{U}}
. Let

be such that

. Then

, which is a contradiction showing that
R\in{\mathcal{U}}
.

In order to prove (2), suppose that

, intending to show that

. Let
X\in\Def({\mathcal{M}})
be such that

. It suffices to show that

. For a contradiction, suppose that

. Since

, then

.
But
t^{-1}(M\backslash X)\cap\omega=f^{-1}(\omega\backslash A)\in{\mathcal{V}}\supseteq f^{-1}(R\backslash A)
, so
t^{-1}(M\backslash X)\cap\omega\in{\mathcal{V}}
. This implies
t^{-1}(X)\cap\omega\not\in{\mathcal{V}}
, which, in turn, implies that

, a contradiction.

Of course, this

may not have
\omega
as its domain. If that is the case, replace it with an extension of itself that is coded in
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
having domain of
\omega
.

(2)\Longrightarrow(1)
: (This implication does not require that
({\mathbb{N}},\ssy({\mathcal{M}}))\models{\sf ACA}_{0}
.) Let
f
be as in
(2)
. Since
f
is coded in
\ssy({\mathcal{M}})
, there is an
{\mathcal{M}}
-definable

such that

. Let

t=\{\langle x,y\rangle\in M^{2}:y=\min(\{0\}\cup\{z\in M:\langle x,z\rangle\in T\})\}.

Then

and
t:M\longrightarrow M
. To see that

,
suppose that

is such that

. Then,

. ∎

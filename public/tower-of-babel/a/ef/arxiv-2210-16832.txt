Rooted tree maps for multiple L L -values from a perspective of harmonic algebras
Hideki Murahara, Tatsushi Tanaka, Noriko Wakabayashi

Abstract
. In this paper, we show the image of rooted tree maps themselves forms a subspace of the kernel of the evaluation map of multiple L L -values. For its proof, we define the diamond product as a modified harmonic product and find its properties. We also show that τ \tau -conjugate rooted tree maps are their antipodes.

Introduction

Let
\mu_{r}
be the set of
r
th roots of unity.
For an index set
(\mathbf{k};\mathbf{s})=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{l};s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})
with

L

[
1

L(\mathbf{k};\mathbf{s})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{m>m_{1}>\cdots>m_{l}>0}\frac{s_{1}^{m_{1}-m_{2}}\cdots s_{l-1}^{m_{l-1}-m_{l}}s_{l}^{m_{l}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{l}^{k_{l}}}.

If
r=1
, this is nothing but the multiple zeta value (abbreviated as MZV).
The MZVs and the MLVs are well-studied in the last three decades.

The index set
(\mathbf{k};\mathbf{s})
is often identified by the word
\mathbf{z}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{s}}:=z_{k_{1},s_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{l},s_{l}}

\mathbb{Q}
-linear map

defined by

and

. (
\mathcal{A}_{r}^{0}
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}_{r}
generated by admissible words, detailed in the next section.)

It is known that the coproduct

The counit

is defined by vanishing on

and
\hat{\mathbb{I}}(\mathbb{I})=1

Let

and

2.1
Construction of a set associated with an alternating map

Let
X=\{u_{1},\cdots,u_{n}\}
be a basis for
U
and
u_{1}<u_{2}<\dots<u_{n}
be an ordering on
X
. Clearly
Y=\{A(u_{i},u_{j})\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n\}

spans
V
. We define a set
\mathcal{Y}=\{\{i,j\}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n,i\neq j\}
.
The total order on
X
induces a total order on the set
\mathcal{Y}
defined as
\{i,j\}<\{r,s\}
, whenever

1.

\max\{u_{i},u_{j}\}<\max\{u_{r},u_{s}\}
or

\max\{u_{i},u_{j}\}=\max\{u_{r},u_{s}\}=u_{a}
and
\{u_{i},u_{j}\}\setminus\{u_{a}\}<\{u_{r},u_{s}\}\setminus\{u_{a}\}

The order relation on the set

\begin{array}[]{cccccc}&\{1,2\}\\
&\{1,3\}&\{2,3\}\\
&\{1,4\}&\{2,4\}&\{3,4\}\\
&\cdots\\
&\{1,n\}&\{2,n\}&\{3,n\}&\cdots&\{n-1,n\}.\\
\end{array}

Note that

if
\{i,j\}
is in one of the rows above

or
\{i,j\}
appears to the left of
\{r,s\}
in the same row. Now we construct a set

associated with the alternating map. We begin the construction of

by choosing the least element

of
\mathcal{Y}
such that

or

\mathcal{B}_{1}=\{\{{i_{1}},{j_{1}}\}\}
and

. We define
\mathcal{B}_{k+1}
and

inductively as follows: Let

\mathcal{Y}
such that

is not in the span of

. Again, without loss of generality, we assume that

. Define
{\mathcal{B}_{k+1}:=\mathcal{B}_{k}\cup\{\{{i_{k+1}},j_{k+1}\}\}}

{B_{k+1}:=B_{k}\cup\{A(u_{i_{k+1}},u_{j_{k+1}})\}}

. Let
\mathcal{B}
be the set obtained in the last step, that is,

. Note that the construction of

2.5
Estimating the size of
W(\mathcal{B})

In this section, our aim is to estimate a lower bound for
\dim\text{Im}{(\Psi)}.
Towards this aim, we state our next proposition.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
U
and
V
be finite-dimensional
F
vector spaces with
\dim U=n
and
\dim V=m
. If
\mathcal{B}
is the set constructed in Section
2.1
, then the number of linearly independent elements in
W(\mathcal{B})
is at least
{n\choose 3}-{r\choose 3}-\binom{t}{2}
, where
r
and
t
are non-negative integers satisfying
{n\choose 2}-m={r\choose 2}+t
with
0\leq t<r
. In particular,
\dim\operatorname{Im}{\Psi}\geq{n\choose 3}-{r\choose 3}-{t\choose 2}.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.7
, we have that
W(\mathcal{B})
is linearly independent in
V\otimes U
, and
|\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})|=|W(\mathcal{B})|
. Thus, it is enough to estimate the size of
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})
. Since
\{a,b\}\in\mathcal{B}
implies that
a\neq b
, we obtain that
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})
is a subset of
{T=\{\{a,b,c\}\mid 1\leq a,b,c\leq n,c\notin\{a,b\},a\neq b\}}
. Noting that
T
has size
\binom{n}{3}
, we get that
|\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})^{c}|={n\choose 3}-|\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})|
. Thus, an upper bound on the size of
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})^{c}
gives a lower bound on the size of
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})
. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})^{c}=\{\{a,b,c\}\in T\mid\{a,b\}\notin\mathcal{B},\{a,c\}\notin\mathcal{B},\{b,c\}\notin\mathcal{B}\}.

To estimate the size of
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})^{c}
, we consider the complement of the graph given in Definition
2.1
. The number of edges of this graph is
{n\choose 2}-m
. Observe that
|\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})^{c}|
is the number of triangles in this graph. By
[
16
, Theorem 2.5]
, the number of triangles in the graph is at most
{r\choose 3}+\scalebox{.55}{$\dbinom{{n\choose 2}-m-{r\choose 2}}{2}$}={r\choose 3}+\binom{t}{2}
. Thus,
{n\choose 3}-{r\choose 3}-\binom{t}{2}
is a lower bound for the size of
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})
.
∎

The next proposition shows that the above estimate can be improved when

is not a tree of height one.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let

and

be finite-dimensional

and
\dim V=m
. If the set

constructed in Section

. In particular,

Proof.

Let
\{i_{1},i_{2}\}
be any element in
\mathcal{B}
. We consider two cases:

Case 1
: For any
\{k,l\}\in\mathcal{B}
,
\{k,l\}\cap\{i_{1},i_{2}\}\neq\varnothing
.

For any
\{k,l\}\in\mathcal{B}
, we have that

or
i_{2}\in\{k,l\}
. Therefore, we obtain
{\mathcal{B}=\{\{i_{1},i_{2}\},\{i_{1},a_{1}\},\dots,\{i_{1},a_{r}\},\{i_{2},b_{1}\},\dots,\{i_{2},b_{s}\}\}}
for some

and

Since

, we have
r+s+1=m
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is not a tree of height one, we have

and

. Define
\mathcal{W}_{0}=\{\{i_{1},i_{2},c\}\mid c\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i_{1},i_{2}\}\}
. For
1\leq i\leq r
, define

. Similarly for

, define
\mathcal{W}_{j}^{2}=\{\{i_{2},b_{j},c\}\mid c\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i_{1},i_{2},b_{1},\dots,b_{j}\}\}

are contained in
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})
, and

,
|\mathcal{W}_{i}^{1}|=n-(i+2)
and
|\mathcal{W}_{j}^{2}|=n-(j+2)
.
Moreover, they are pairwise disjoint.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})|

\displaystyle\geq|\mathcal{W}_{0}|+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}|\mathcal{W}_{i}^{1}|+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}|\mathcal{W}_{i}^{2}|=(n-2)+\sum\limits_{i=3}^{r+2}(n-i)+\sum\limits_{i=3}^{s+2}(n-i)

\displaystyle=(n-2)+\sum\limits_{i=3}^{r+2}(n-i)+(n-3)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s-1}(n-(3+i)).

Since
r\geq 1,
we have
n-(3+i)\geq n-(r+2+i)
. Therefore

\displaystyle|\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})|

\displaystyle\geq(n-2)+\sum\limits_{i=3}^{r+2}(n-i)+(n-3)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s-1}(n-(r+2+i))

\displaystyle=(n-2)+(n-3)+\sum\limits_{i=3}^{r+2+(s-1)}(n-i)

\displaystyle=(n-2)+(n-(m+1))+(m-2)+\sum\limits_{i=3}^{r+s+1}(n-i)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=2}^{r+s+1}(n-i)+(n-(m+1))+(m-2).

Since

, the proof is now complete.

Case 2:
There exists
\{r,s\}\in\mathcal{B}
, with

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{\{i_{1}^{j},i_{2}^{j}\}\mid 1\leq j\leq m\}

and
i_{1}^{2}=r,\ i_{2}^{2}=s
. For each

, our aim is to define

such that
\mathcal{W}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{W}_{j}
are contained in
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})
and they are pairwise disjoint. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{\{i_{1}^{1},i_{2}^{1},c\}\mid c\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i_{1}^{1},i_{2}^{1}\}\}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}

\displaystyle=\{\{i_{1}^{2},i_{2}^{2},c\}\mid c\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i_{1}^{2},i_{2}^{2}\}\}.

Note that
|\mathcal{W}_{1}|=|\mathcal{W}_{2}|=n-2
, and that they are pairwise disjoint. We will now inductively define
\mathcal{W}_{j}
for
3\leq j\leq m
. Observe that if

for all

. Hence,
\mathcal{W}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{W}_{m}
are pairwise disjoint. Our aim is to estimate the size of
\mathcal{W}_{j}
for
j\geq 3
. First note that

The next lemma is standard and we omit its proof.

Lemma 3.1

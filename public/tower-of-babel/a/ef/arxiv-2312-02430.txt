Almost-Sure Safety Guarantees of Stochastic Zero-Control Barrier Functions Do Not Hold footnoteinfo
Oswin So, Andrew Clark, Chuchu Fan

Abstract
The 2021 paper “Control barrier functions for stochastic systems” provides theorems that give almost sure safety guarantees given stochastic zero control barrier function (ZCBF). Unfortunately, both the theorem and its proof is invalid. In this letter, we illustrate on a toy example that the almost sure safety guarantees for stochastic ZCBF do not hold and explain why the proof is flawed. Although stochastic reciprocal barrier functions (RCBF) also uses the same proof technique, we provide a different proof technique that verifies that stochastic RCBFs are indeed safe with probability one. Using the RCBF, we derive a modified ZCBF condition that guarantees safety with probability one. Finally, we provide some discussion on the role of unbounded controls in the almost-sure safety guarantees of RCBFs, and show that the rate of divergence of the ratio of the drift and diffusion is the key for whether a system has almost sure safety guarantees.

Flaw of the Proof

The main flaw in the proof comes from an implicit assumption when applying mathematical induction. Let
\theta\in(0,h(x_{0})]
and define the following sequence of stopping times
\eta_{i}
and
\zeta_{i}
for
i=0,1,\dots

\displaystyle\eta_{0}

\displaystyle=0,

(6)

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}

\displaystyle=\inf\{t:h(x_{t})>\theta\}

(7)

\displaystyle\eta_{i}

\displaystyle=\inf\{t:h(x_{t})<\theta,t>\zeta_{i-1}\},\quad i=1,2,\dots

(8)

\displaystyle\zeta_{i}

\displaystyle=\inf\{t:h(x_{t})>\theta,t>\eta_{i-1}\},\quad i=1,2,\dots.

(9)

Next, define the random process

The flaw then comes from the following statement:

We will first prove by induction that
h(x_{t})\geq U_{t}
and
U_{t}\leq\theta
.

While the intention is to prove that this property holds for all
t\geq 0
, this turns out to not be the case. In particular, the mathematical induction is performed by showing that this property holds for all closed intervals
[\eta_{i},\zeta_{i}]
and
[\zeta_{i},\eta_{i+1}]
. However, unless the union of these intervals covers
[0,\infty)
, i.e.,

\lim_{i\to\infty}\eta_{i}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\zeta_{i}=\infty,

(11)

the property does not hold for all
t\geq 0
, and the proof is invalid.

Proof Counterexample for Uncontrolled Brownian Motion

In the case of (uncontrolled) Brownian motion, it can be shown that, except for

Suppose

6
Unbounded controls are insufficient for almost-sure safety

While one might be led to believe that the ability of RCBFs to maintain safety with probability one is due to the fact that the controls can be unbounded, we show in the following section that this is not sufficient.
To better understand
how
an RCBF guarantees safety, we take
h(x)=1/B(x)
as in the previous section. By rearranging the Modified ZCBF constraint (
35
), we obtain

\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{t}}{\tilde{\sigma}_{t}^{2}}\geq h^{-1}-\tilde{\sigma}^{-2}\alpha_{3}(h).

(39)

In other words, as
h\downarrow 0
,
h^{-1}\to\infty
, and thus the ratio
\tilde{\mu}/\tilde{\sigma}^{2}
also becomes unbounded.
However, more importantly, the ratio
\tilde{\mu}/\tilde{\sigma}^{2}
diverges
faster
than
h^{-1}
.
Some works mention that the ability of RCBFs to maintain safety with probability one is due to the fact that the controls can be unbounded
[
18
]
.
However, as we show in the following lemma, the
rate
of divergence of
\tilde{\mu}/\tilde{\sigma}^{2}
as
h\downarrow 0
is the deciding factor.

Lemma 12
.

Let the scalar stochastic process
h
satisfy the SDE

\mathinner{\mathrm{d}h}_{t}=\tilde{\mu}_{t}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}t}+\tilde{\sigma}_{t}\mathinner{\mathrm{d}W}_{t},

(40)

for
h\in(0,\infty)
, and suppose that the ratio
\tilde{\mu}/\tilde{\sigma}^{2}
satisfies

\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{t}}{\tilde{\sigma}_{t}^{2}}=\gamma h^{-p}

(41)

for any
\gamma\geq 0
and
p>0
. Define the stopping time
T=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}=0\}
.
Then,

1.

If
p\in[0,1)
and
\tilde{\sigma}
is bounded from below, then

\Pr(T<\infty)>0.

(42)

2.

If
p=1
and
\gamma\geq 1/2
, or
p>1
, then

\Pr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\inf_{t\geq 0}h_{t}>0}}\right)=1\text{ and }\Pr(T=\infty)=1.

(43)

Hence, if we take
\tilde{\sigma}
constant and
\tilde{\mu}_{t}=h^{-1/2}
, then
there with some nonzero probability, there exists some
t\geq 0
such that
x_{t}
exits
\interior(C)
and
h_{t}=0
.
In some sense, the stochastic RCBF asks for the slowest polynomial divergence rate of
\tilde{\mu}/\tilde{\sigma}^{2}
that still guarantees safety with probability one.

7
Conclusion

In this letter, we have revealed a flaw in the almost-sure safety guarantees of stochastic ZCBFs as constructed in
[
5
, Theorem 4]
, and provided a new proof technique to show that stochastic RCBFs are indeed safe with probability one.
The appeal of ZCBFs as a way to guarantee safety almost surely without the need for unbounded drift has attracted much attention in literature
[
10
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
6
]
. Unfortunately, these claims are not valid. Some works may already suspect this to be the case, as in
[
18
]
, which references only the safety guarantees of stochastic RCBFs but not stochastic ZCBFs.
We hope this letter can inspire the community to revisit previous claims made about stochastic ZCBFs and potentially construct new methods that build upon the new proof techniques and insights presented.

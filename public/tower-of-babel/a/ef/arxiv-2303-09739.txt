New results about the Inverse Eigenvalue Problem of a Graph
Roberto C. Díaz, Ana I. Julio

Abstract
All graphs considered are simple and undirected. The Inverse Eigenvalue Problem of a Graph G G (IEP-G) aims to find all possible spectra for matrices whose ( i , j ) − (i,j)- entry, for i ≠ j i\neq j , is nonzero precisely when i i is adjacent to j j . A cluster in a graph G G is a pair of vertex subsets ( C , S ) (C,S) , where C C is a maximal set of cardinality | C | ≥ 2 |C|\geq 2 of independent vertices sharing the same set S S of | S | |S| neighbors. Let G G be a connected graph on n n vertices with a cluster ( C , S ) (C,S) and H H be a graph of order | C | |C| . Let G ⁡ ( H ) G(H) be the connected graph obtained from G G and H H when the edges of H H are added to the edges of G G by identifying the vertices of H H with the vertices in C C . In this paper, we construct a symmetric matrix with associated complete graph, which satisfies some interesting properties. This result is applied to obtain new sufficient conditions on the IEP-G, when G G is a graph of order n n having a clique of order k k and a cluster ( C , S ) (C,S) , where | C | = n − k |C|=n-k and | S | = r ≤ k |S|=r\leq k , as well as, for the graph G ⁡ ( K n − k ) G(K_{n-k}) , being G G as before. In particular, when G G is a graph obtained from K n K_{n} by deleting a single edge e k ∈ E ⁡ ( K n ) e_{k}\in E(K_{n}) , we establish a necessary and sufficient condition on the IEP-G. Several illustrative example are given. The constructive nature of our results generate algorithmic procedures that always allow one to compute a solution matrix.

\varphi(j)=\sum\limits_{s=j}^{n-1}\frac{\lambda_{n-(\phi(s)-1)}-\lambda_{n-(\phi(s)-2)}}{s}\neq-a_{1,n},\quad\text{for all}\quad j=2,\ldots,n-1.

Thus,
a_{1,j}=a_{1,n}+\varphi(j)\neq 0
, for all
j=2,\ldots,n-1
and by (
11
),
A\in S(K_{n})
.

iii.
Since
R{\bf e}_{1}={\bf e}
,
R^{-1}{\bf e}={\bf e}_{1}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle A{\bf e}=(RDR^{-1}){\bf e}=RD{\bf e}_{1}=\lambda_{1}R{\bf e}_{1}=\lambda_{1}{\bf e}.

Thus,
A\in\mathcal{CS}_{\lambda_{1}}
. Similarly, we observe that
R{\bf e}_{k}={\bf x}_{k}
, where

\displaystyle{\bf x}^{T}_{k}=(1,1,\ldots,1,-(n-k+1),\underset{(k-2)\text{ zeros}}{\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}}),\quad k=2,\ldots,n.

Hence,
R^{-1}{\bf x}_{k}={\bf e}_{k}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle A{\bf x}_{k}=(RDR^{-1}){\bf x}_{k}=RD{\bf e}_{k}=\lambda_{k}R{\bf e}_{k}=\lambda_{k}{\bf x}_{k}.

Thus,
(\lambda_{k},{\bf x}_{k})
is a eigenpair for
A
.

iv.

\displaystyle a_{n,n}=\mathbf{G}_{n}D\mathbf{H}_{n}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1},-(n-1)\lambda_{2},\underset{(n-2)\text{ zeros}}{\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}})\mathbf{H}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{n}+\frac{(n-1)\lambda_{2}}{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})+\lambda_{2}.

The proof is complete.

\Box

Theorem 3.2

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
having a clique of order
k
and a cluster
(C,S)
, where
|C|=n-k
and
|S|=r\leq k
. For any list
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}
of real numbers with at least
n-k+1
distinct elements such that at least two of them have algebraic multiplicity greater than or equal to
2
, there exists a matrix
M\in\mathcal{S}(G)
with spectrum
\Lambda
.

Proof
Let us write
\Lambda=\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2}
, where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}=\{\lambda_{1,1},\lambda_{1,2},\ldots,\lambda_{1,n-k+1}\},

is a list whose elements are arranged in strictly decreasing order. We choose
n-k
real numbers
\mu_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,n-k
, satisfying

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,1}>\mu_{1}>\lambda_{1,2}>\mu_{2}>\lambda_{1,3}>\cdots>\lambda_{1,n-k}>\mu_{n-k}>\lambda_{1,n-k+1},

such that
a=\left(\lambda_{1,1}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-k}(\mu_{j}-\lambda_{1,j+1})\right)\notin\Lambda_{2}
. From Lemma
1
and Remark
2.1
, we can construct a bordered matrix
A=\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}&{\bf b}\\
{\bf b}^{T}&a\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{S}(S_{n-k+1})
with spectrum
\Lambda_{1}
, where
A_{1}=diag\{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n-k}\}
and
a=trace(A)-trace(A_{1})
. Now, we consider the list
\Gamma=\Lambda_{2}\cup\{a\}
. Since
\Lambda
has at least two distinct elements with algebraic multiplicity greater than or equal to
2
and
a\notin\Lambda_{2}
, we obtain that
\Gamma
has at least three distinct eigenvalues. Taking into account the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we construct the diagonal matrix
D
whose diagonal entries are the elements of
\Gamma
, where
a
is its
(k-r+2,k-r+2)-
th position. Thus, from Theorem
3.1
(
iii.
), we get that the matrix
B=RDR^{-1}
has an eigenvector
{\bf u}
with
(k-r)-
zeros entries associated to
a
. Finally, Lemma
2
allows us conclude that

\displaystyle M=\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}&\mathbf{b}\mathbf{u}^{T}\\
\mathbf{u}\mathbf{b}^{T}&B\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{S}(G),

and has spectrum
\Lambda
.

\Box

Remark 3.1

1.

If in the previous theorem the list
\Lambda
has more than
n-k+1
distinct elements, then the condition that
\Lambda
has at least two of elements with algebraic multiplicity greater than or equal to
2
is not necessary.

2.

When
G
is a star, Theorem
3.2
is consistent with Remark
2.1
and allows us to construct immediately an
n\times n
bordered matrix solution.

An interesting conclusion derived from the previous theorem is the following result:

Corollary 3.1

Let

. Any list
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}
with at least three distinct real numbers is the spectrum of a matrix
M\in\mathcal{S}(G)
. Also, when

,
M\in\mathcal{S}(G)
if and only if
\sigma(M)
has exactly three distinct elements.

Proof
The proof follows taking into account Theorem
3.2
and Proposition
2.4
.

The relative isoperimetric inequality for minimal submanifolds in the Euclidean space
Lei Liu, Guofang Wang and Liangjun Weng

Abstract
. In this paper, we mainly consider the relative isoperimetric inequalities for minimal submanifolds in ℝ n + m \mathbb{R}^{n+m} . We first provide, following Cabré [ 5 ] , an ABP proof of the relative isoperimetric inequality proved in Choe-Ghomi-Ritoré [ 10 ] , by generalizing ideas of restricted normal cones given in [ 9 ] . Then we prove a relative isoperimetric inequalities for minimal submanifolds in ℝ n + m \mathbb{R}^{n+m} , which is optimal when the codimension m ≤ 2 m\leq 2 . In other words we obtain a relative version of isoperimetric inequalities for minimal submanifolds proved recently by Brendle [ 3 ] . When the codimension m ≤ 2 m\leq 2 , our result gives an affirmative answer to an open problem proposed by Choe in [ 8 ] , Open Problem 12.6. As another application we prove an optimal logarithmic Sobolev inequality for free boundary submanifolds in the Euclidean space following a trick of Brendle in [ 4 ] .

1.
Introduction

The classical isoperimetric inequality states

(1.1)

\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{|\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\geq\bigg(\frac{|\Omega|}{|\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\bigg)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

for a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and equality holds if and only if
\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{n}
, the unit ball, which plays a fundamental role in mathematics.
Its origin goes back to antiquity, known as Dido’s problem. It is a longstanding open problem if (
1.1
) holds for domains
in a minimal submanifold
M^{n}
in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
, which goes back at least to Carleman
[
11
]
. For domains in an area-minimizing
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
, Almgren solved this open problem affirmatively
in
[
2
]
. See also a proof for
n=2
in
[
18
]
.
There have been many other results on this problem, especially when
M
is a minimal surface. Here we just
refer to a survey of Choe
[
8
]
and references therein. Till the recent work of Brendle, this problem is widely open. In
[
3
]
Brendle
achieved a breakthrough on this problem and proved

Theorem 1.1

(
Brendle
[
3
]
)
.

Given
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}

(m\geq 1)
a compact
n
-dimensional submanifold with boundary
\partial M
, then

(1.2)

\frac{|\partial M|+\int_{M}|H|dv}{|\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\geq b_{n,m}\bigg(\frac{\mbox{}|M|}{\mbox{}|\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\bigg)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

where
b_{n,m}
is defined by

(1.3)

b_{n,m}=\begin{cases}\bigg(\frac{(n+m)|\mathbb{B}^{n+m}|}{m|\mathbb{B}^{n}||\mathbb{B}^{m}|}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n}},&\hbox{ if }m>2,\\
1,&\hbox{ if }m=1\hbox{ or }2,\end{cases}

with equality for
m\leq 2
if and only if
M
is a round ball. Here
H
is the mean curvature of
M
,
|\partial M|
and
|M|
are the area and the volume of
\partial M
and
M
respectively.

Theorem
1.1

implies that the above longstanding open problem has an affirmative answer if the codimension is not bigger than
2
,
while gives an explicit constant
b_{n,m}
for the general case.
Inequality (
1.2
)
implies a Michael-Simon and Allard inequality
[
16
]
,
[
1
]
with an optimal positive constant, at least in the case of codimension
m\leq 2
.
See
[
3
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in the so-called the relative isoperimetric inequality for

n
-dimensional minimal submanifolds in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. Set
N:=n+m
.
Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with a smooth boundary

S=\partial\mathcal{C}
. Let
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an
n
-dimensional submanifold with codimension
m=N-n
. Its boundary
\partial M

consists of two smooth pieces
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
, where
\Gamma\subset\partial\mathcal{C}
. Denote their common boundary by
\partial\Sigma
, which may be empty.
Let
\nu
be the outer unit normal vector field of
\partial M\subset M

and
\nu_{S}
the outer unit normal vector field of
S
. We assume that
\nu=-\nu_{S}
along
\Gamma
, i.e.
M
intersects
S
from outside orthogonally, and call such a submanifold
a
partially free boundary submanifold
with free boundary
\Gamma
and relative boundary (or fixed, or Dirichlet boundary)
\Sigma
.
When
\Sigma=\emptyset
, we call
M
a
free boundary submanifold
.
In some literature,
S
is also called a support
hypersurface.
The relative isoperimetric inequality concerns the relation between the area of the relative boundary
\Sigma
,
|\Sigma|
and the volume
of
M
,
|M|
.

We prove the following relative isoperimetric inequality for submanifolds in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
, which is a relative version of the result of Brendle
[
3
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}

(m\geq 1)
be a partially free boundary submanifold
with relative boundary
\Sigma
and free boundary
\Gamma
on a convex support hypersurface
S
. We have

(1.4)

\frac{|\Sigma|+\int_{M}|H|dv}{|\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\geq\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n}}b_{n,m}\bigg(\frac{\mbox{}|M|}{\mbox{}|\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\bigg)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

where
b_{n,m}
is defined in (
1.3
) and
H
is the mean curvature vector of
M
.
Moreover, when
m\leq 2
, equality holds if and only if

M
is a flat half
n
-ball.

Inequality (
1.4
) also implies a relative version of Michael-Simon and Allard inequality. See Section
3
.

As a Corollary, we obtain the optimal relative isoperimetrical inequality for minimal submanifolds
in the Euclidean space, provided that the codimension
m:=N-n\leq 2
.
Namely we solve the open problem which was proposed by Choe, Open problem 12.6 in
[
8
]
, if the codimension is not bigger than
2
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
M^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}

(m\leq 2)
be a partially free boundary minimal submanifold
with relative boundary
\Sigma
and free boundary
\Gamma
on a convex support hypersurface
S
. We have

(1.5)

\frac{|\Sigma|}{|\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\geq\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n}}\bigg(\frac{\mbox{}|M|}{\mbox{}|\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\bigg)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

moreover, equality holds if and only if

M
is a flat half
n
-ball.

Inequality (
1.5
) is equivalent to

\frac{|\Sigma|}{|\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n-1}|}\geq\bigg(\frac{\mbox{}|M|}{\mbox{}|\mathbb{B}_{+}^{n}|}\bigg)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

where
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}
is a unit half ball and
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}
is a unit half sphere. Either the condition of intersecting
M
with
S
orthogonally
or the convexity of
S
is necessarily, since it is
easy to find a counterexample if without one of these conditions.
There have been a lot of work on the relative isoperimetric inequality on minimal submanifolds,
especially on minimal surface by Choe and his school. See again the nice survey of Choe
[
8
]
and references therein. An Almgren type result was proved recently
by Krummel
[
15
]
, namely, the relative isoperimetric inequality holds when

is area-minimizing with partially free boundary on a convex hypersurface.

When
M=\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,
N=n
, one can view
\Omega
as a minimal submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with codimension
0
.
In this case, Corollary
1.3
is the relative isoperimetric inequality
proved by Choe, Ghomi and Ritoré.

Theorem 1.4

(
Choe-Ghomi-Ritoré
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\Omega=M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain outside a convex body
\mathcal{C}
with its
boundary
\partial\Omega
consisting of two smooth pieces
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
,
where
\Gamma\subset\partial\mathcal{C}
. Then

(1.6)

\frac{|\Sigma|}{|\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\geq\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n}}\bigg(\frac{|\Omega|}{|\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\bigg)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

moreover, equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a flat half
n
-ball.

It is this relative isoperimetric inequality and other results for minimal surfaces obtained by Choe and his colleagues
that motivated Choe to propose the above open problem.

There are a lot of proofs of the classical isoperimetric inequality (
1.1
). Here we just mention one proof for smooth domains,
an ABP proof, which was first given by Cabré. See
[
5
,
6
]
for the proof and various interesting applications.
We remark that in this paper we are interested in the smooth case.
Brendle’s method to prove Theorem

is a clever extension of the ABP proof of Cabré.
One could naturally ask whether there is an ABP proof for the relative isoperimetric inequality of Choe-Ghomi-Ritoré
[
10
]
, namely, Theorem
1.4
? In this paper we
firstly give an ABP proof for the relative isoperimetric inequality, by modifying interesting ideas, the restricted normal cones,
given in another paper of Choe-Ghomi-Ritoré
[
9
]
. The original proof of the relative isoperimetric inequality in
[
10
]
relies also crucially on

[
9
]
.
The key is Proposition

below, which generalizes Proposition
2.1
proved by Choe-Ghomi-Ritoré
[

]
.
With this ABP technique, or precisely Proposition

, we then generalize the results of Brendle in
[

]
to the relative case.
It is interesting to see that with Proposition

we can also generalize another interesting result, an optimal logarithmic Sobolev inequality for
submanifolds in the Euclidean space, proved recently by Brendle in
[
4
]
to obtain an optimal logarithmic Sobolev inequality for free boundary submanifolds.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
M
be a compact
n
-dimensional free boundary submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with support hypersurface
S
, which is a boundary of a convex body
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then for any positive function
f
on

, there holds

(1.7)

\int_{M}f\bigg(\log f+n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi)-\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f^{2}}-|H|^{2}\bigg)dv\leq\bigg(\int_{M}fdv\bigg)\log\bigg(2\int_{M}fdv\bigg),

where
H
is the mean curvature vector of

.

The logarithmic Sobolev inequality for the Euclidean space was first proved by Gross
[
14
]
. Ecker generalized it to any submanifold in the Euclidean space

]
with a non-optimal

[

It is well known that the isoperimetric inequalities, Michael-Simon and Allard inequality and the logarithmic Sobolev inequality are very useful in differential
geometry and geometric analysis, especially in the study of minimal submanifolds and curvature (and inverse curvature) flows for closed submanifolds. We believe that
our inequalities are also very
useful for the related problems with boundary, especially curvature flows of submanifolds (or hypersurfaces) with free boundary, on which
there has been recently a lot of work.

2.
A new proof of the reletive isoperimetric inequality

In this section, in order to well present
our methods and ideas, we firstly give a new proof for the relative isoperimetric inequality of domains, that is, Theorem

1.4
. As mentioned in the Introduction, Cabré gave a simply proof of the classical isoperimetric inequality, by using
the technique introduced by Alexandrov, Bakelman, and Pucci to establish the ABP estimate.
One could naturally ask if there is a similar proof for the relative isoperimetric inequality of Choe-Ghomi-Ritoré
[
10
]
. With this aim in mind: we generalize Cabré’s idea to provide
a new proof of the relative isoperimetric inequality. Namely we provide a boundary version of his proof. However, the boundary causes some big problems.
We modify the ideas in another paper of Choe-Ghomi-Ritoré
[
9
]

to handle these extra difficulties arising from the boundary.
It is interesting to see that our proof can be used to generalize the result
of Brendle’s isoperimetric inequality for minimal submanifold in the next Section.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with a smooth boundary

S=\partial\mathcal{C}
. Let
M:=\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain outside
\mathcal{C}
with its
boundary
\partial\Omega
consisting of two smooth pieces
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
,
where
\Gamma\subset\partial\mathcal{C}
. Both
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
can be non-connected and
\Gamma
is closed and hence compact.
Their common boundary is denoted by
\partial\Sigma
. Let
\nu
be the unit outward normal vector field of
\partial\Omega

and
\nu_{S}
be the unit outward normal vector field of
S
. It is clear that
\nu=-\nu_{S}
along
\Gamma
. (Note that in this Section
we consider the case of codimension
0
.
In the higher codimensional case,
\nu=-\nu_{S}
along
\Gamma
is the free boundary condition.)

We assume first that

(2.1)

\Sigma\hbox{ intersects }S\hbox{ orthogonally.}

This is equivalent to that
\Sigma\hbox{ intersects }\Gamma\hbox{ orthogonally.}
(For the general case, we will use a simply approximation argument to reduce to this
case. See the proof below.)
Under this assumption we consider the following problem

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Delta u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\Sigma|}{|\Omega|},\quad\hbox{ in }\Omega,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,\quad\hbox{ in }\Sigma,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\quad\hbox{ in }\Gamma\backslash\partial\Sigma.

The existence of a weak solution is easy to show. Due to the Neumann condition on
\Gamma
and the orthogonality in (
2.1
) one can show that

u\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{\infty}_{loc}(\overline{\Omega}\backslash\partial\Sigma)
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Without loss of generality, by scaling, we may assume that

\frac{|\Sigma|}{|\Omega|}=N.

Now we define its lower contact set, as in the ABP method, by

\Gamma_{+}:=\{x\in\Omega\,|\,u(y)\geq u(x)+\langle\nabla u(x),y-x\rangle\quad\forall\,y\in\Omega\}.

If we can prove that

(2.5)

|\nabla u(\Gamma_{+})|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\mathbb{B}^{N}|,

then we can follow the proof of Cabré
[
5
]
. See at the end of this section.
One might hope that

\nabla u(\Gamma_{+})\hbox{ contains a half unit ball,}

which obviously implies (
2.5
). Unfortunately, this is in general not true.
To overcome this difficulty, for any
\rho\in(0,\infty)
, we introduce the level sets

\Gamma^{\rho}_{+}:=\{x\in\Gamma_{+}\,|\,|\nabla u(x)|<\rho\}\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\partial\Gamma^{\rho}_{+}:=\{x\in\Gamma_{+}\,|\,|\nabla u(x)|=\rho\}.

We want to prove

(2.6)

|\nabla u(\partial\Gamma^{\rho}_{+})|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}(\rho)|,\qquad\qquad\forall\rho\in(0,1).

Then (
2.5
) is clearly follows from (
2.6
). Our idea to prove (
2.6
) is motivated by
a new concept,
the restricted normal cone
, which is introduced by Choe-Ghomi-Ritoré in
[
9
]
.

Let us first introduce the (unit)
normal cone
, which is the standard concept. For any subset
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and any point
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the
(unit) normal cone of
X
at
p
is defined by

N_{p}X:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\,|\,\langle x-p,\xi\rangle\leq 0,\quad\forall\,x\in X\}.

Set

NX:=\cup_{p\in X}N_{p}X.

If we have further a map
\sigma:X\to\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, we define the
restricted normal cone
of
X
at
p\in X
(with respect to
\sigma
) as in
[
9
]

N_{p}X/\penalty\sigma:=N_{p}X\cap{H_{\sigma(p)}},

and set

NX/\penalty\sigma:=\cup_{p\in X}N_{p}X/\penalty\sigma.

Here
H_{\eta}
(
\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
) is the half space defined by

H_{\eta}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,|\,\langle y,\eta\rangle\geq 0\}.

The following interesting Proposition was proved in
[
9
]

Proposition 2.1

(
Choe-Ghomi-Ritoré
[
9
]
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a compact set which is disjoint from the relative interior of its convex hull.
Suppose there exists a continuous mapping
\sigma:X\to\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
such that
\sigma(p)\in N_{p}X
for all
p\in X.
Then,

(2.7)

|NX/\penalty\sigma|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}|.

Remark 2.2
.

For the later use, we need to consider the normal cone and the restricted normal cone of
length
\rho\in(0,\infty)
. The normal cone of
length
\rho\in(0,\infty)
of
X
at
p
is defined
by

N^{\rho}_{p}X:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}(\rho)\,|\,\langle x-p,\xi\rangle\leq 0,\quad\forall\,x\in X\},

where
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}(\rho)
is the sphere of radius
\rho
centered at the origin.
One can similarly define
N^{\rho}X
and
N^{\rho}X/\penalty\sigma

for a function
\sigma(p)\in N_{p}X
. Due to the scaling invariance of the condition
\langle x-p,\xi\rangle\leq 0
for
\xi
, it
is trivial to see that
N^{\rho}X=\rho NX.
Hence,
under the same assumptions as in Proposition
2.1
we have

(2.8)

|N^{\rho}X/\penalty\sigma|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}(\rho)|=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{N-1}|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}|.

The scaling invariance is clearly not true for the generalized normal cone, which we will now define.

Now we generalize the concept of the normal cone and the restricted normal cone to our case as follows.
For any subset
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, any point
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and any function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, we introduce the

generalized normal cone
of length
\rho
of
X
at
p
with respect to
u
defined by

N^{u,\rho}_{p}X:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}(\rho)\,|\,\langle x-p,\xi\rangle\leq u(x)-u(p),\quad\forall\,x\in X\}

and set

N^{u,\rho}X:=\cup_{p\in X}N^{u,\rho}_{p}X.

Here, for the simplicity of notation, we omit the superscript
\rho
in the following.
If we have further a map
\sigma:X\to\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, we define the
generalized restricted normal cone
of
X
at
p\in X
with respect to
u

N^{u}_{p}X/\penalty\sigma:=N^{u}_{p}X\cap{H_{\sigma(p)}}

and set

N^{u}X/\penalty\sigma:=\cup_{p\in X}N^{u}_{p}X/\penalty\sigma.

When
u
is a constant function, then the both definitions are certainly the same. But when
u
is not a constant function,
there are at least two big differences, which prevent us to directly use the ideas in
[
9
]
to prove an analogous inequality to (
2.7
) or (
2.8
).
The first difference is that
the condition

\langle x-p,\xi\rangle\leq u(x)-u(p)

is not scaling invariance. This causes that

N^{u}_{p}X
might be not spherical convex, while
N_{p}X
is. The latter is crucial for the validity of (
2.7
).
The second difference, which is also crucial in the later application, is that
\nu(p)\in N_{p}\Gamma
, when
\Gamma
is a set lying on the convex hypersurface,
but
\nu(p)\in N^{u}_{p}\Gamma
is in general not true.

To overcome these difficulties, instead of
X
we consider its graph of function
u

\tilde{X}:=\{(x,u(x))\,|\,x\in X\},

in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. For
x\in X
, we set
\tilde{x}=(x,u(x))\in\tilde{X}
. It is important to remark that if
X
is disjoint from the relative interior
of its convex hull, so is
\tilde{X}
.
We consider the normal cone
N^{\tilde{\rho}}\tilde{X}=\cup_{\tilde{p}\in\tilde{X}}N^{\tilde{\rho}}_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{X}
of length
\tilde{\rho}=\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}

by viewing
\tilde{X}
as a subset in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
,
i.e.

N^{\tilde{\rho}}_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{X}=\{\bar{\xi}:=(\xi,\xi_{N+1})\in\mathbb{S}^{N}(\tilde{\rho})\,|\,\langle\tilde{x}-\tilde{p},\bar{\xi}\rangle\leq 0,\quad\forall\tilde{x}\in\tilde{X}\}.

In this way we embed
N^{u}_{p}X
into
N^{\tilde{\rho}}_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{X}
, with
\tilde{\rho}:=\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}
.
For the simplicity of notation we also omit the superscript
\tilde{\rho}
, if there is no confusion.
We have the following simple observation

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
\rho\in(0,\infty)
and
\tilde{\rho}=\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}
, we have

(2.9)

\xi\in N^{u}_{p}X\Longleftrightarrow(\xi,-1)\in N_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{X}.

As a result, we can identify
N^{u}_{p}X
with
N_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{X}\cap\{\xi_{N+1}=-1\}.

Proof.

The Lemma follows trivially from that

\langle x-p,\xi\rangle\leq u(x)-u(p),

holds for all
x\in X
, which
is equivalent to

\langle\tilde{x}-\tilde{p},\bar{\xi}\rangle\leq 0,

holds for all
\tilde{x}\in\tilde{X}
with

\bar{\xi}:=(\xi,-1).

Here, also as above, we use the convention that
\tilde{x}:=(x,u(x))
and
\tilde{p}:=(p,u(p))
.
∎

Now we state our generalization of Proposition
2.1
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a compact set which is disjoint from the relative interior of its convex hull and
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function.
Suppose there exists a continuous mapping
\sigma:X\to\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
such that

\bar{\sigma}:=\tilde{\rho}\cdot(\sigma(p),0)\in N_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{X}\subset\mathbb{S}^{N}(\tilde{\rho})
for all
p\in X.
Then,

(2.10)

|N^{u}X/\penalty\sigma|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}(\rho)|.

Remark 2.5
.

The assumption on the map
\sigma
is crucial. In the application
\nu(p)\in N^{u}\Gamma
is in general not true, however it is
easy to see that
\tilde{\rho}(\nu(p),0)\in N_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{\Gamma}
, and hence Proposition
2.4
can be applied.

To prove Proposition
2.4
, the following observation is simple, but crucial to us.

Lemma 2.6
.

Under the same assumptions as in Proposition
2.4
, it holds

(2.11)

|N^{u}_{p}X/\penalty\sigma|\geq\frac{1}{2}|N^{u}_{p}X|,

for any
p\in X
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\sigma(p)=(1,0,\cdots,0)\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}

and
\bar{\sigma}=\tilde{\rho}(1,0,\cdots,0,0)\in N_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{X}.

By Lemma 4.1 in
[
9
]
, we know that the normal cone
N_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{X}
is a convex spherical set on
\mathbb{S}^{N}(\tilde{\rho})
.
By Lemma
2.3
for any point
\xi\in N^{u}_{p}X
, we have
(\xi,-1)\in N_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{X}\subset\mathbb{S}^{N}(\tilde{\rho})
. Hence the geodesic segment connecting
\bar{\sigma}
and
(\xi,-1)
lies entirely in
N_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{X}
.
Set

\displaystyle\frac{|\Sigma|+\int_{M}|H|dv}{|\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\geq\big(\frac{N|\mathbb{B}^{N}|}{2m|\mathbb{B}^{n}||\mathbb{B}^{m}|}\big)^{\frac{1}{n}}\Big(\frac{|M|}{{|\mathbb{B}^{n}|}}\Big)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

One can check easily that when
m=2
,
{(n+2)|\mathbb{B}^{n+2}|}=2{|\mathbb{B}^{n}||\mathbb{B}^{2}|}
holds. Hence we have proved the relative isoperimetric inequality for
m\geq 2
.

If

\Sigma
and
\Gamma
are not connected, i.e., their common boundary is an empty set. Then the above proof works without any change.

If
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
are connected and
M
does not satisfy Assumption (
3.2
), one can use an
approximation argument to construct a submanifold
M_{\varepsilon}
satisfying (
3.2
) such that
the area and the volume of
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
and
M_{\varepsilon}
resp. are close to the area and the volume of
\Sigma
and
M
respectively as small as we want.
Then we obtain (
1.4
) for
M_{\varepsilon}
as above. By taking
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain (
1.4
) for a general
M
.

Now we consider the case that
m=2
and equality holds.
Assume that
M^{n}
is a compact free boundary submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
such that

(3.13)

\frac{|\Sigma|+\int_{M}|H|dv}{|\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}|}=\bigg(\frac{(n+2)|\mathbb{B}^{n+2}|}{4|\mathbb{B}^{n}||\mathbb{B}^{2}|}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n}}\bigg(\frac{\mbox{}|M|}{\mbox{}|\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\bigg)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}=\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{n}}\bigg(\frac{\mbox{}|M|}{\mbox{}|\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\bigg)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

where we have used that
{(n+2)|\mathbb{B}^{n+2}|}=2{|\mathbb{B}^{n}||\mathbb{B}^{2}|}
in the last equality.
Such a submanifold
M
is called
the relative isoperimetric regions
, which is by the relative isoperimetric inequality (
1.4
)
a stationary point of functional (
5.1
) below.
It can be proved that Assumption (
3.2
) holds for
M
, namely the relative boundary
\Sigma
interests
S
orthogonally. See Proposition
5.1
in the Appendix.
Hence we can follow the above argument to define a function
u
and obtain
the isoperimetric inequality. Since for
M
we have equality, all inequalities in the above proof are equalities.
In particular, we conclude that
\det(\text{Jac}\Phi)\cdot\chi_{A}=1
a.e. in
M
and
|U|=|M|
. From Lemma
3.1
, we obtain that

\nabla^{2}u(x)-\langle\Pi_{x},y\rangle=g_{x}
for a.e. in
\Omega
. Since
u\in C^{2}(M)
, we have

\nabla^{2}u(x)-\langle\Pi_{x},y\rangle=g_{x}
for all
(x,y)\in\Omega
. Since
g_{x}
and
y
are independent, it follows that
\nabla^{2}u(x)=g_{x}
for all
x\in M
and
\Pi\equiv 0
on
M
,
and
M
is contained in an
n
-dimensional flat space
P=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is clear that we are now in the case of codimension
0

and the argument given at the end of last section implies that
M
is a flat half
n
-ball. Hence we complete the proof.
∎

As a direct Corollary, we have a boundary version of
Michael-Simon and Allard inequality
[
16
]
,
[
1
]
with an optimal positive constant, at least in the case of codimension
m\leq 2
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
M^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}

(m\geq 1)
be a partially free boundary submanifold
with relative boundary
\Sigma
and free boundary
\Gamma
on a convex support hypersurface
S
. For any non-negative smooth function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
vanishing on the relative boundary

\Sigma
, we have

(3.14)

\frac{\int_{M}(|\nabla f|+|H|f)dv}{|\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\geq\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n}}b_{n,m}\bigg(\frac{\mbox{}\int_{M}f^{\frac{n}{n-1}}dv}{\mbox{}|\mathbb{B}^{n}|}\bigg)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Proof.

Since the superlevel set
\{f\geq s\}
is a partially free boundary submanifold, one can
apply Theorem
1.2
to
\{f\geq s\}
and then follow
completely the proof of Brendle in
[
3
]
.
∎

4.
A logarithmic Sobolev inequality for a free boundary submanifold

As another application, we prove the following logarithmic Sobolev inequality for a free boundary submanifold.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
M
be a compact
n
-dimensional free boundary submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with support hypersurface
S
, which is a boundary of a convex body
\mathcal{C}
.
Then for any positive function
f
on
M
, there holds

(4.1)

\int_{M}f\bigg(\log f+n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi)-\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f^{2}}-|H|^{2}\bigg)dv\leq\bigg(\int_{M}fdv\bigg)\log\bigg(2\int_{M}fdv\bigg),

where
H
is the mean curvature vector of
M
.

This is a boundary version of Brendle’s logarithmic Sobolev inequality for compact submanifolds in
[
4
]
.
Remember that by definition a free boundary submanifold
M
has no relative boundary and has a free boundary
\Gamma\subset S
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\int_{M}fdv=1
. As in
[
4
]
, we set

\int_{M}f\bigg(\log f-\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f^{2}}-|H|^{2}\bigg)dv=-\alpha.

Assume that
M
is connected. We consider a weighted Laplacian on
M
w.r.t.
f

\Delta_{f}:=f^{-1}\hbox{\rm div}\,(f\nabla u)=\Delta u+f^{-1}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle.

and a Neumann boundary problem

(4.2)

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\displaystyle\Delta_{f}u&=&\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046pt\log f-\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f^{2}}-|H|^{2}+\alpha&\hbox{ in }M,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}&=&0,&\hbox{ on }\Gamma,\\
\end{array}

where
\nu
is the unit outward normal of
\Gamma\subset M
.
One easily checks that this problem has a smooth solution, and it is unique up to a constant. Define as in
[
4
]

\begin{array}[]{rcl}U&:=&\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046pt\{(x,y):x\in M,y\in T^{\perp}_{x}M\},\\
A&:=&\displaystyle\{(x,y)\in U:\nabla^{2}u(x)-\langle\Pi_{x},y\rangle\geq 0\},\end{array}

and a map
\Phi:U\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
by
\Phi(x,y)=\nabla u(x)+y
. We need a Lemma from
[
4
]

Lemma 4.2
.

The Jacobian determinant of
\Phi
satisfies

0\leq e^{-\frac{|\Phi(x,y)|^{2}}{4}}\det\hbox{\rm Jac}\,\Phi(x,y)\leq f(x)e^{-\frac{|2H(x)+y|^{2}}{4}+\alpha-n},\quad\forall(x,y)\in A.

As in the previous Section, we set for any
\rho\in(0,\infty)

\displaystyle\partial A_{\rho}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{(x,y)\in U:|\nabla u|^{2}(x)+|y|^{2}=\rho^{2}\hbox{ and }{\nabla^{2}u(x)-\langle\Pi_{x},y\rangle\geq 0}\}.

It is clear that
A=\cup_{\rho\in(0,\infty)}\partial A_{\rho}.

As above we have a similar

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
\rho\in(0,\infty)
, it holds that

\displaystyle|\Phi(\partial A_{\rho})|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}(\rho)|.

We leave the proof to the interested reader. Now we can prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

On the one hand, by the Proposition
4.3
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\displaystyle(4\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{M}\bigg(\int_{T_{x}^{\perp}M}e^{-\frac{|\Phi(x,y)|^{2}}{4}}\det\text{Jac}\Phi(x,y)\chi_{A}(x,y)dy)\bigg)dv_{x}\\
&\geq&\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046pt(4\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\Phi(A)}e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4}}d\xi=(4\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho\int_{\Phi(\partial A_{\rho})}e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma(\xi)\\
&\geq&\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046pt(4\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}(\rho)|e^{-\frac{|\rho|^{2}}{4}}d\rho\\
&=&\frac{1}{2}(4\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4}}d\xi=\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}

On the other hand, from Lemma
4.2
we can follow the proof in
[
4
]
to obtain
that

\displaystyle(4\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{M}\bigg(\int_{T_{x}^{\perp}M}e^{-\frac{|\Phi(x,y)|^{2}}{4}}\det\text{Jac}\Phi(x,y)\chi_{A}(x,y)dy\bigg)dv_{x}\leq(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{\alpha-n}.

Hence we have

\alpha-n-\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi)\geq-\log 2.

It follows that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\int_{M}f\bigg(\log f+n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi)-\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f^{2}}-|H|^{2}\bigg)dv&=&\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle-\alpha+n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi)\\
&\leq&\log 2\\
&=&\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{M}fdv\bigg)\log\bigg(2\int_{M}fdv\bigg),\end{array}

where we have used the normalization
\int_{M}fdv=1
. When
M
is not connected, the inequality holds also true, see the argument in
[
4
]
. Hence we complete the proof.
∎

The following Corollary may be more interesting in applcations.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
M
be a compact
n
-dimensional free boundary submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with free boundary
\Gamma
on a support hypersurface
S
, which is a boundary of a convex body
\mathcal{C}
.
Let

d\mu:=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{4}}dv,\qquad x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

be the Gaussian measure on
M
centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then for any positive smooth function
\varphi
there holds

(4.3)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046pt\int_{M}\varphi\bigg(\log\varphi-\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}-|H+\frac{(x-x_{0})^{\perp}}{2}|^{2}\bigg)d\mu\\
&&\leq\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{M}\varphi d\mu\bigg)\log\bigg(2\int_{M}\varphi d\mu\bigg)+\int_{\Gamma}\varphi\langle x-x_{0},\nu_{S}\rangle ds(\mu),\end{array}

where
H
is the mean curvature vector of
M
,
x^{\perp}
is the normal part of
x
and
ds(\mu)
is the induced surface measure of
\mu
on
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Wlog we may assume that
x_{0}=0
.
Setting
f=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\varphi

and following the computation given in
[
4
]
, we have

\displaystyle\log\varphi-\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}-|H+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}|^{2}=\log f+n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi)-\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f^{2}}-|H|^{2}-\frac{{\rm div}_{M}\,(fx^{T})}{f}.

where
x^{T}
is the tangential part of
x
.
Noticing
\varphi d\mu=fdv
and integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}\varphi\bigg(\log\varphi-\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}-|H+\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}|^{2}\bigg)d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}f\bigg(\log f+n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi)-\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f^{2}}-|H|^{2}\bigg)dv-\int_{\Gamma}f\langle x^{T},\nu\ \rangle.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{M}fdv\bigg)\log\bigg(2\int_{M}fdv\bigg)-\int_{\Gamma}f\langle x,\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{M}\varphi d\mu\bigg)\log\bigg(2\int_{M}\varphi d\mu\bigg)+\int_{\Gamma}\varphi\langle x,\nu_{S}\rangle ds(\mu),

where we have used (
4.1
) and the free boundary condition
\nu=-\nu_{S}.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

If
\nu_{S}(x)\in N_{x_{0}}\Gamma
,
\forall

, then one can drop the boundary term in (

Appendix

Proposition 5.1
.

Under the assumptions in Theorem
1.2
, if
M
achieves equality in the relative isoperimetric inequality (
1.4

\Sigma
and the free boundary
\Gamma
of

3.2
) holds true.

satisfies

where

Proof.

First of all, we extend

smoothly to a partially free boundary submanifold
\tilde{M}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with free boundary
\tilde{\Gamma}
on

and denote
\tilde{H}
be the mean curvature vector field of
\tilde{M}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Recall that
\Sigma
and

are the relative boundary and free boundary of

.
Then we consider a variation of

in
\tilde{M}
, namely,

such that

, (

be the domain in

enclosed by

and
\tilde{\Gamma}

It is clear that

and free boundary on
the support hypersurface
S
. Define a functional by

(5.1)

\displaystyle J(M_{t}):=\frac{|\Sigma_{t}|+\int_{M_{t}}|\tilde{H}|dv}{|M_{t}|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}},

where
\tilde{H}
is the mean curvature vector field of

\mathbb{R}^{N}

\tilde{M}

\mathbb{R}^{N}

1.2

M_{t}
satisfies the relative isoperimetric inequality. Hence

achieves

Recall that

is the outer unit normal of
\Sigma
in

and denote

in
\Sigma

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}J(M_{t})\cdot|M|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle X,\eta\rangle ds-\int_{\Sigma}h\langle X,\nu\rangle+\int_{\Sigma}|H|_{|_{\Sigma}}\langle X,\nu\rangle-\frac{n-1}{n|M|}\big(|\Sigma|+\int_{M}|H|dv\big)\cdot\int_{\Sigma}\langle X,\nu\rangle,

, Assumption (

∎

Chiral gravitational waves from thermalized neutrinos in the early Universe
and Di-Lun Yang

Abstract
We investigate polarized gravitational waves generated by chiral fermions in the early Universe. In particular, we focus on the contribution from left-handed neutrinos in thermal equilibrium with finite temperature and chemical potential in the radiation dominated era. We compute the correlation functions of gravitational fields pertinent to the Stokes parameter V V characterizing the circular polarization of gravitational waves in the Minkowski and expanding spacetime backgrounds. In the expanding universe, we find that the thermalized neutrinos induce a non-vanishing V V linear to the neutrino degeneracy parameter and wavenumber of gravitational waves in the long wavelength region. While the magnitude of the gravitational waves generated by thermal neutrinos is too small to be detectable by current and planned third generation gravitational wave detectors, their observations by future generation detectors for ultra-high frequency regimes could provide information on the neutrino degeneracy parameter in the early Universe.

The Stokes parameters for electromagnetic and gravitational waves

We here briefly review the Stokes parameters, which were initially proposed for characterizing the polarization of
electromagnetic waves
[
33
,
34
]
, but can also be used to describe the polarization of gravitational waves
[
35
]
.

First, let us for illustration consider an electromagnetic wave moving in the direction of the z-axis,
partially following ref.
[
36
]
.
It suffices to consider only the properties of the electric field
\bm{E}
, as the magnetic field will be simply pointing to the direction perpendicular to
the direction of the wave and
\bm{E}
. The electric field can be generally parametrized as

\displaystyle\bm{E}=\begin{pmatrix}E_{x}\\
E_{y}\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{x}\cos(z-t)\\
A_{y}\cos(z-t+\beta)\\
0\end{pmatrix}

(2.1)

Eliminating the time variable,
E_{x}
and
E_{y}
can be shown to satisfy the equation

\displaystyle\frac{E_{x}^{2}}{A_{x}^{2}}+\frac{E_{y}^{2}}{A_{y}^{2}}-2\frac{E_{x}E_{y}}{A_{x}A_{y}}\cos\beta=\sin^{2}\beta,

(2.2)

which corresponds to a rotated ellipse in the
E_{x}
-
E_{y}
plane. Defining
A_{x}=A\cos\theta
and
A_{y}=A\sin\theta
,
the rotation angle
\alpha
of the ellipse is determined from

\displaystyle\tan 2\alpha=\tan 2\theta\cos\beta,

(2.3)

and the semi-major and semi-minor axes
a
and
b
are given as

\displaystyle a^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{2}}{2}\Bigl(1+\sqrt{1-\sin^{2}2\theta\sin^{2}\beta}\Bigr),

(2.4)

\displaystyle b^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{2}}{2}\Bigl(1-\sqrt{1-\sin^{2}2\theta\sin^{2}\beta}\Bigr).

(2.5)

For
\beta=0
, the wave is linearly polarized, while it is circularly polarized for
\beta=\pi/2
and
\theta=\pi/4
.

The nature of the polarization can be expressed using the Stokes parameters
I
,
Q
,
U
and
V
, which are defined
as

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\equiv a^{2}+b^{2}=A^{2},

(2.6)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle\equiv(a^{2}-b^{2})\cos 2\alpha=A^{2}\cos 2\theta,

(2.7)

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle\equiv(a^{2}-b^{2})\sin 2\alpha=A^{2}\sin 2\theta\cos\beta,

(2.8)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\equiv 2abh=A^{2}h\sin 2\theta\sin\beta,

(2.9)

where
h=\pm 1
parametrizes the direction of rotation for the circularly polarized component of the wave.
It is clear from the above definitions that
I
gives the overall magnitude of the wave,
V
the degree and direction of
its circular polarization, and
Q
and
U
the degree and direction of the linear polarization of the wave.
The four Stokes parameters are not independent, but are related to each other
via

\displaystyle Q^{2}+U^{2}+V^{2}=I^{2}.

(2.10)

Using the complex representation of an electric field,

\displaystyle\bm{E}=\begin{pmatrix}E_{x}\\
E_{y}\\
0\end{pmatrix}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(z-t)},

(2.11)

where
E_{x}
and
E_{y}
are complex and contain relative phases between the
x
and
y
component of the field,
the Stokes parameters can be expressed as

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\equiv|E_{x}|^{2}+|E_{y}|^{2},

(2.12)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle\equiv|E_{x}|^{2}-|E_{y}|^{2},

(2.13)

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle\equiv 2\mathrm{Re}(E_{x}E_{y}^{\ast}),

(2.14)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\equiv-2\mathrm{Im}(E_{x}E_{y}^{\ast}),

(2.15)

which can readily be confirmed to satisfy eq. (
2.10
) and to be consistent with the definitions of eqs. (
2.6
)–(
2.9
)
if one considers
\beta
to be the relative phase of
E_{x}
and
E_{y}
and
\theta
an angle determining the relative size of

|E_{x}|
and
|E_{y}|
. Note that for a non-purely polarized wave, the above definitions should be regarded as expectation values over time of different
components of
E_{x}
,
E_{y}
and their complex conjugates. This can be cast in a concise form using the matrix

\displaystyle\langle E_{i}E_{j}^{\ast}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}I+Q&U-{\rm i}V\\
U+{\rm i}V&I-Q\end{pmatrix},

(2.16)

with
i
,
j=x
,
y
.

In analogy to eq. (
2.16
), the Stokes parameters of gravitational waves can be defined as
[
6
]

\displaystyle\langle h_{i}(\bm{k})h_{j}^{\ast}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\delta(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\begin{pmatrix}I(\bm{k})+Q(\bm{k})&U(\bm{k})-{\rm i}V(\bm{k})\\
U(\bm{k})+{\rm i}V(\bm{k})&I(\bm{k})-Q(\bm{k})\end{pmatrix},

(2.17)

where the subscripts
i
and
j
stand for the
+
and
\times
polarization modes of the
gravitational wave in the transverse-traceless gauge.
The
+
and
\times
components can be rewritten using right- and left-handed modes as

\displaystyle h_{\rm R}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(h_{+}-{\rm i}h_{\times}),

(2.18)

\displaystyle h_{\rm L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(h_{+}+{\rm i}h_{\times}),

(2.19)

which modifies the above matrix to

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k})h_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{\ast}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\delta(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\begin{pmatrix}I(\bm{k})+V(\bm{k})&Q(\bm{k})-{\rm i}U(\bm{k})\\
Q(\bm{k})+{\rm i}U(\bm{k})&I(\bm{k})-V(\bm{k})\end{pmatrix},

(2.20)

with
\lambda
,
\lambda^{\prime}={\rm R}
,
{\rm L}
.
In particular, the
V
parameter is explicitly given as the circular polarization of the gravitational wave:

\displaystyle\langle h_{\rm R}(\bm{k},t)h_{\rm R}^{\ast}(\bm{k}^{\prime},t)\rangle-\langle h_{\rm L}(\bm{k},t)h_{\rm L}^{\ast}(\bm{k}^{\prime},t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})V(\bm{k}).

(2.21)

Starting from the next section, we will for convenience of notation employ
\lambda
,
\lambda^{\prime}=\pm 1
instead of

\lambda
,
\lambda^{\prime}={\rm R}
,
{\rm L}
. Especially,
\lambda

\lambda^{\prime}=+1
should not be confused with the
+
polarization mentioned above.
As we are mostly interested in parity violation components of the gravitational wave spectrum, our focus will be laid on the Stokes parameter
V
, which parametrizes the difference between the magnitudes of the right- and left-handed spectrum.

3.1
General framework

In setting up the general framework to study gravitational waves of differing helicities emitted from left-handed fermionic sources,
we partly follow ref.
[

chemical potential. The key objects that we wish to calculate are helical mode correlators of gravitational
perturbations generated by left-handed neutrinos.

We start with the equation of motion (EOM) for gravitational perturbations
h_{\mu\nu}
in the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) background in the

{\rm d}s^{2}=a(\tau)^{2}({\rm d}\tau^{2}-{\rm d}\bm{x}^{2})

is the conformal time, which is related to the cosmic time

We adopt the transverse-traceless (TT) gauge such that

denote the spatial coordinates. The EOM now reads

2

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\lambda}(\bm{k})\cdot\epsilon_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{*}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}},\quad\epsilon_{\lambda}(\bm{k})\cdot\epsilon_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=-\delta_{\lambda,-\lambda^{\prime}}.

3.3
Thermal corrections

So far, we have closely followed ref.
[
23
]
in our derivation of the correlation function. The purpose of this work, however,
is to study not the effect of vacuum fluctuations of left-handed neutrinos, but to evaluate their thermal contributions to the generation of gravitational waves in the early Universe before decoupling. Despite our focus on neutrinos, the following approach can be applied to chiral fermions in general.

We first introduce the thermal average, which is defined for a generic operator
\hat{O}
as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{O}\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\,\big(\hat{O}\hat{\rho}\big),\quad\hat{\rho}=\frac{1}{Z}{\rm e}^{-\beta\big[(E_{q}-\mu)\hat{n}_{\bm{q}}+(E_{q}+\mu)(\hat{\tilde{n}}_{\bm{q}}-1)\big]}\,,

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\,\left({\rm e}^{-\beta\left[(E_{q}-\mu)\hat{n}_{\bm{q}}+(E_{q}+\mu)(\hat{\tilde{n}}_{\bm{q}}-1)\right]}\right),

(3.27)

where
\hat{n}_{\bm{q}}\equiv(a^{\dagger}_{\bm{q}}a_{\bm{q}})/{\cal V}
and
\hat{\tilde{n}}_{\bm{q}}\equiv(b^{\dagger}_{\bm{q}}b_{\bm{q}})/{\cal V}
with
{\cal V}\equiv(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\bm{0})

denote the normalized density operators for neutrinos and anti-neutrinos and
\beta=1/T
.
For the case of the expanding universe that we will consider later, all dimensionful quantities, such as the physical temperature
T
and chemical potential
\mu
(as well as momenta) will be rescaled by the scale factor
a
as
\bar{T}=aT
and
\bar{\mu}=a\mu
, when taking the thermal average. As
T(\tau)\propto 1/a(\tau)
and
\mu(\tau)\propto 1/a(\tau)
,
\bar{T}
and
\bar{\mu}
are constants as a function of
\tau
. Nonetheless, for notational simplicity, we will use
{\bm{k}}
etc. to denote the rescaled momentum. The physical momentum depending on
\tau
is given by
{\bm{k}}_{\rm phys}(\tau)={\bm{k}}/a(\tau)
. The traces in eq. (
3.27
) are taken for vacuum and one-particle (and anti-particle) states,
while states with more than one particle are forbidden from the Pauli exclusion principle.
Specifically, we define

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\,(\hat{O})=\langle 0|\hat{O}|0\rangle+\int_{\bm{q}}\langle 0|a_{\bm{q}}\hat{O}a_{\bm{q}}^{\dagger}|0\rangle+\int_{\bm{q}}\langle 0|b_{\bm{q}}\hat{O}b_{\bm{q}}^{\dagger}|0\rangle+\frac{1}{\cal V}\int_{\bm{q}}\langle 0|a_{\bm{q}}b_{\bm{q}}\hat{O}b_{\bm{q}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{q}}^{\dagger}|0\rangle

(3.28)

and
\int_{\bm{q}}{\cal V}\equiv 1
.
For this, we find

\displaystyle Z=1+{\rm e}^{-\beta(E_{q}-\mu)}+{\rm e}^{\beta(E_{q}+\mu)}+{\rm e}^{-\beta(E_{q}-\mu)+\beta(E_{q}+\mu)}=\frac{1}{(1-f_{E_{q}-\mu})f_{E_{q}+\mu}}\,,

(3.29)

where
f_{q}\equiv 1/({\rm e}^{\beta q}+1)
.
Furthermore, one can easily confirm that the expectation values of density operators give rise to the Fermi-Dirac distribution functions,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{n}_{\bm{p}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bm{q}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}({\bm{q}-\bm{p}})\frac{1}{Z}{\rm e}^{-\beta(E_{q}-\mu)}\bigl[{\rm e}^{\beta(E_{q}+\mu)}+1\bigr]=\frac{1}{{\rm e}^{\beta(E_{p}-\mu)}+1}\,,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\tilde{n}}_{\bm{p}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bm{q}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}({\bm{q}-\bm{p}})\frac{1}{Z}\bigl[{\rm e}^{-\beta(E_{q}-\mu)}+1\bigr]=\frac{1}{{\rm e}^{\beta(E_{p}+\mu)}+1}\,.

(3.30)

We now replace the vacuum expectation value of eq. (
3.16
) with the trace of eq. (
3.27
), to incorporate the thermal excitations
and the effects of the chemical potential, and look for combinations of operators, which have non-trivial expectation values. This will include the vacuum part that we have already computed in the previous subsection.
As the details of this calculation are rather involved, we relegate them to appendix
A
, and here show only the final result,
which can be divided into the vacuum and thermal parts,

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle\equiv\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\text{vac}}+\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\text{th}},

(3.31)

where
\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\text{vac}}
is given in eq. (
3.2
), which generally
suffers from an ultraviolet divergence and may have to be renormalized by subtracting proper counter terms or by introducing a cutoff
[
23
]
.
For the thermal part, after the rescaling of dimensionful quantities, we have

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\text{th}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta^{3}({\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime}})}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\int{\rm d}\tau^{\prime}{\rm d}\tau^{\prime\prime}\frac{G_{k}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})G_{k^{\prime}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime\prime})}{a(\tau^{\prime})^{2}a(\tau^{\prime\prime})^{2}}\int{\rm d}^{3}p\ p^{2}

(3.32)

\displaystyle\times\Big[\Gamma_{+}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda;\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})f_{|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|-{\bar{\mu}}}+\Gamma_{-}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda;\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})f_{p+\bar{\mu}}

\displaystyle-\Gamma_{0}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda;\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})\big(f_{|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|+\bar{\mu}}+f_{p-\bar{\mu}}\big)\Big],

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\pm}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda;\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{{\rm i}(|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|-p)\tau^{\prime}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(p-|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|)\tau^{\prime\prime}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{\pm}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda),

(3.33)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda;\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}(|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|+p)\tau^{\prime}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(p+|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|)\tau^{\prime\prime}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{0}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda),

(3.34)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}_{\pm}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}\theta\Bigg[1\mp\lambda\cos\theta-(\cos\theta\mp\lambda)\frac{p\cos\theta-k}{|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|}\Bigg],

(3.35)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}_{0}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}\theta\Bigg[1-\lambda\cos\theta+(\cos\theta-\lambda)\frac{p\cos\theta-k}{|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|}\Bigg].

(3.36)

4
Gravitational birefringence from primordial neutrinos

Utilizing the formalism introduced in the previous section, we next explicitly evaluate the Stokes parameter due to primordial neutrinos in thermal equilibrium. As a heuristic study, we first investigate the case of the Minkowski universe in subsection
4.1
. Subsequently, we analyze the more practical case for an expanding universe in the radiation dominated period in subsection
4.2
.

4.1
Minkowski universe

In the Minkowski spacetime, where
\tau=t
,
a(\tau)=1
, and
\mathcal{H}(\tau)=0
, eq. (
3.7
) simply results in

\displaystyle G_{k}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\frac{{\rm d}k_{0}}{2\pi}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}k_{0}(t-t^{\prime})}\frac{1}{(k_{0}+i\epsilon)^{2}-k^{2}}

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(t-t^{\prime})\frac{1}{k}\sin k(t-t^{\prime})\,.

Moreover, the fermionic wave functions reduce to the plane waves,

\displaystyle u(t,p)={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}p\cdot x},\quad v(t,p)={\rm e}^{{\rm i}p\cdot x}.

(4.2)

One can then easily evaluate the integrations with respect to
t^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime\prime}
in eq. (
3.2
) and derive the correlation function in vacuum,

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},t)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta^{3}({\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime}})}{4m_{\rm P}^{4}}\int{\rm d}^{3}p\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\big(p-k\cos\theta+|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|\big)^{2}}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[1-\lambda\cos\theta+(\cos\theta-\lambda)\frac{p\cos\theta-k}{|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|}\Bigg],

where
|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|=\sqrt{p^{2}-2pk\cos\theta+k^{2}}
. The correlation function is in fact independent of
t
since now we work in the static background spacetime.

For the thermal part, by carrying out similar computations, eq. (
3.32
) becomes

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},t)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},t)\rangle_{\text{th}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta^{3}({\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime}})}{4m_{\rm P}^{4}}\int{\rm d}^{3}p\Big[\Pi_{+}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda)f_{|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|-\mu}+\Pi_{-}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda)f_{p+\mu}

(4.4)

\displaystyle-\Pi_{0}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda)\big(f_{|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|+\mu}+f_{p-\mu}\big)\Big]\,,

where

\displaystyle\Pi_{\pm}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\big(p-k\cos\theta-|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|\big)^{2}}\left[1\mp\lambda\cos\theta-(\cos\theta\mp\lambda)\frac{p\cos\theta-k}{|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|}\right]\,,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\Pi_{0}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\big(p-k\cos\theta+|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|\big)^{2}}\left[1-\lambda\cos\theta+(\cos\theta-\lambda)\frac{p\cos\theta-k}{|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|}\right]\,.

(4.6)

When considering the long-wavelength (small
k
) limit, the
p

In this limit, one finds
\Pi_{\pm}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda)
to be the dominant terms.
In particular, we find

\displaystyle\Pi_{+}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\pm 1)\approx\left[\frac{k^{4}}{4p^{2}}\pm\frac{\cos^{2}(\theta/2)k^{5}}{2p^{3}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{6}}{p^{4}}\right)\right]^{-1}\approx\frac{4p^{2}}{k^{4}}\mp\frac{8p\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}{k^{3}}\,,

(4.7)

and can approximate
f_{|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|-\mu}\rightarrow f_{p-\mu}
.

Interestingly, when taking
\mu\gg T
despite being an unrealistic condition in the early Universe, the Fermi-Dirac distribution function reduces to a unit-step function, which leads to
f_{p-\mu}\rightarrow\Theta(\mu-p)
and
f_{p+\mu}\rightarrow\Theta(\mu+p)=0
for
\mu>0
. In such a case, only
\Pi_{+}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\pm 1)
contributes for
k\ll\mu
and eq. (
4.4
) approximately becomes

\displaystyle\langle h_{\pm}(\bm{k},t)h_{\pm}(\bm{k}^{\prime},t)\rangle_{\text{th}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\delta^{3}({\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime}})}{4m_{\rm P}^{4}}\int^{\infty}_{0}{\rm d}p\int^{\pi}_{0}{\rm d}\theta p^{2}\sin\theta\left[\frac{4p^{2}}{k^{4}}\mp\frac{8p\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}{k^{3}}\right]\Theta(\mu-p)

(4.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi\delta^{3}({\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime}})\mu^{4}}{5m_{\rm P}^{4}k^{4}}(4\mu\mp 5k)\,.

Note that the primary contributions for both integrals come from
p=\mu
. The results above are accordingly insensitive to the infrared cutoff of
p
and consistent with the assumption
p\gg k
even though we take
p_{\text{min}}=0
.
The above result furthermore has an IR singularity, behaving as
\sim 1/k^{4}
from small

, which can be traced back to the
infinite ranges of the
t^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime\prime}
integrals in eq. (
3.2

after introducing a finite cutoff to the before-mentioned time integrals. As will be seen in the following section, such a singularity
is indeed absent in the expanding universe case, where all time integral ranges are finite.

In the general case with finite

\mu

\displaystyle\Pi_{+}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda)f_{|{\bm{k}-\bm{p}}|-\mu}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{4p^{2}}{k^{4}}f_{p-\mu}-\frac{4p}{k^{3}}\left(\lambda+\cos\theta\left[1-(1-f_{p-\mu})\frac{p}{T}\right]\right)f_{p-\mu}\,,

\displaystyle\Pi_{-}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda)f_{p+\mu}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{4p^{2}}{k^{4}}f_{p+\mu}+\frac{4p}{k^{3}}(\lambda-\cos\theta)f_{p+\mu}\,,

(4.9)

up to
\mathcal{O}(k^{-3})

k\ll T,\mu
.
From the definition of eq. (
2.21
), we can obtain the Stokes parameter

\displaystyle V(\bm{k})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2m_{\rm P}^{4}}\int^{\infty}_{0}{\rm d}p\int^{\pi}_{0}{\rm d}\theta p^{2}\sin\theta\frac{8p}{k^{3}}(f_{p+\mu}-f_{p-\mu})

(4.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{48\pi T^{4}}{m_{\rm P}^{4}k^{3}}\Big[\rm{Li}_{4}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{-\mu/T}\bigr)-\rm{Li}_{4}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{\mu/T}\bigr)\Big],

where we used

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}{\rm d}pp^{3}f_{p-\mu}=-6T^{4}\rm{Li}_{4}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{\mu/T}\bigr)

(4.11)

with
\mathrm{Li}_{s}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}z^{k}/k^{s}
being the polylogarithm function.
When
\mu\ll T
, eq. (
4.10

\displaystyle V(\bm{k})\approx-\frac{72\pi T^{4}}{m_{\rm P}^{4}k^{3}}\zeta(3)\frac{\mu}{T}+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\frac{\mu}{T}\right)^{2}\right),

(4.12)

where
\zeta(x)
is the Riemann zeta function with
\zeta(3)\simeq 1.202
.
Accordingly, one finds that
V(\bm{k})
vanishes for the
\Pi_{\pm}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda)
terms when
\mu=0
. Even when including the higher-order corrections up to
\mathcal{O}(k^{0})

We, however, find a non-vanishing

for
\mu=0
by including terms up to
\mathcal{O}(k)

\Pi_{0}(\bm{p},\theta;\bm{k},\lambda)

\displaystyle V(\bm{k})\approx-\frac{\pi k}{15m_{\rm P}^{4}}\,,

(4.13)

which shows that

can be non-vanishing even for
\mu=0

A detailed analysis going beyond the long wavelength limit is, however, out of the scope of the present study.

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{th},\,\Gamma_{0},\,\mathrm{LO}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{16\pi}{5}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}p\ p^{4}(f_{p-\bar{\mu}}+f_{p+\bar{\mu}})

\displaystyle\quad\times\Biggl\{\Bigl(\mathrm{ci}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{ci}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)-\frac{1}{\tau p}\sin[(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})p]\cos[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\Bigl(\mathrm{Si}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{Si}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)-\frac{1}{\tau p}\sin[(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})p]\sin[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\Bigr)^{2}\Biggr\}\,,

(4.25)

and

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{th},\,\Gamma_{0},\,\mathrm{NLO}}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{16\pi}{15}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}\lambda k\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}p\ p^{4}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Biggl\{\frac{2}{p}(f_{p-\bar{\mu}}+f_{p+\bar{\mu}})\sin[(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})p]\Bigr(\sin[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\bigl[\mathrm{ci}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{ci}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad-\cos[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\bigl[\mathrm{Si}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{Si}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)\bigr]\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\left[-\frac{2}{p}(f_{p-\bar{\mu}}+f_{p+\bar{\mu}})+\frac{1}{T}f_{p+\bar{\mu}}(1-f_{p+\bar{\mu}})\right]

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl[\Bigl(\mathrm{ci}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{ci}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)-\frac{1}{\tau p}\sin[(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})p]\cos[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\Bigl(\mathrm{Si}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{Si}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)-\frac{1}{\tau p}\sin[(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})p]\sin[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\Bigr)^{2}\biggr]\Biggr\}\,.

(4.26)

As the last step, we compute the remaining integral of
p
.
Let us first consider the
\Gamma_{\pm}
terms which are simpler than
\Gamma_{0}
.
We leave the calculational details to appendix
D
and give here the result for the sum of the
\Gamma_{+}
and
\Gamma_{-}
terms, which reads

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{th},\,\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-}}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{256\pi}{5}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}\left[\log\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{\rm i}}\Bigr)-\frac{\tau-\tau_{\rm i}}{\tau}\right]^{2}\bar{T}^{5}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Biggl\{\mathrm{Li}_{5}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}\bigr)+\mathrm{Li}_{5}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{-\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}\bigr)-\frac{\lambda}{4}\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\Bigl[\mathrm{Li}_{4}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}\bigr)-\mathrm{Li}_{4}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{-\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}\bigr)\Bigr]\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr).

(4.27)

Expanding the above expression in
\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{th},\,\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{96\pi}{5}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}\left[\log\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{\rm i}}\Bigr)-\frac{\tau-\tau_{\rm i}}{\tau}\right]^{2}\bar{T}^{5}\left[5\zeta(5)-\zeta(3)\lambda\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{T}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\Bigr)^{2}\mathrm{,}\,\Bigl(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{T}}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr),

(4.28)

with
\zeta(5)\simeq 1.037
.

To compute the
\Gamma_{0}
term, one needs to integrate eqs. (
4.25
) and (
4.26
), which does not appear to be possible
analytically in general. However, considering the time and temperature regions that are relevant for the radiation dominant period,
some of the functions
\mathrm{Si}(x)
and
\mathrm{ci}(x)
appearing in these equations
can be simplified as follows.
After the change of integration variable from
p
to
y=p/\bar{T}
, the arguments of these functions become either

x=2\tau{\bar{T}}y
or
2\tau_{\rm i}{\bar{T}}y
, while the integral can be expected to be dominated by contributions from the region
y\sim 1
.
Noting that
\tau{\bar{T}}\gg 1
and
\tau_{\rm i}{\bar{T}}\gg 1
in the radiation dominant period (see appendix
E
for details),
the functions
\mathrm{Si}(2\tau{\bar{T}}y)
,
\mathrm{Si}(2\tau_{\rm i}{\bar{T}}y)
,
\mathrm{ci}(2\tau{\bar{T}}y)

and
\mathrm{ci}(2\tau_{\rm i}{\bar{T}}y)
can be approximated using their asymptotic forms

\displaystyle\mathrm{Si}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\cos x}{x}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{x^{2}}\right),

(4.29)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ci}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin x}{x}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{x^{2}}\right).

(4.30)

Within these approximations, the
p
integral in eqs. (
4.25
) and (
4.26
) can be computed as (see appendix
D
for details)

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{th},\,\Gamma_{0}}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{8\pi}{15}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}\frac{\tau-\tau_{\rm i}}{\tau\tau_{\rm i}}\bar{T}^{3}\Biggl\{3\frac{\tau-\tau_{\rm i}}{\tau\tau_{\rm i}}\Bigl[\mathrm{Li}_{3}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}\bigr)+\mathrm{Li}_{3}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{-\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\Bigl[\frac{\tau-\tau_{\rm i}}{\tau\tau_{\rm i}}\mathrm{Li}_{2}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}\bigr)-2\bar{T}{\rm e}^{\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi^{\ast}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}},3,1-2{\rm i}(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})\bar{T}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad-2\bar{T}{\rm e}^{-\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi^{\ast}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{-\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}},3,1-2{\rm i}(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})\bar{T}\bigr)\Bigr]\Biggr\}+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)\,,

(4.31)

where
\Phi^{\ast}(z,s,c)
is the Lerch transcendent function, defined as

\displaystyle\Phi^{\ast}(z,s,c)\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\bigl[(c+k)^{2}\bigr]^{s/2}}\,.

(4.32)

Expanding up to first order in
\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{th},\,\Gamma_{0}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{4\pi}{15}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}\frac{\tau-\tau_{\rm i}}{\tau\tau_{\rm i}}\bar{T}^{3}\Biggl\{9\zeta(3)\frac{\tau-\tau_{\rm i}}{\tau\tau_{\rm i}}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\left(\frac{\tau-\tau_{\rm i}}{\tau\tau_{\rm i}}\left[\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+2\log(2)\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{T}}\right]-\frac{1}{2(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})^{3}\bar{T}^{2}}+\pi^{3}\bar{T}\frac{3+\cosh\bigl[4\pi(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})\bar{T}\bigr]}{\sinh^{3}\bigl[2\pi(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})\bar{T}\bigr]}\right)\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\Bigr)^{2}\mathrm{,}\,\Bigl(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{T}}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)\,.

(4.33)

It is interesting to observe that the difference between the two helicities here does not vanish even for
\mu=0
, which is different from the situation in the
\Gamma_{\pm}
terms [see eq. (
4.28
)].

Let us now add all terms to get the full correlator, which in the small
k/\bar{T}
and small
\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}
expansion
reads

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{th},\,\tau_{\rm i}{\bar{T}}\gg 1}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{8\pi}{15}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}\bar{T}^{5}\Biggl\{180\zeta(5)\left[\log\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{\rm i}}\Bigr)-\frac{\tau-\tau_{\rm i}}{\tau}\right]^{2}+\frac{9}{2}\zeta(3)\frac{(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})^{2}}{\tau^{2}\tau_{\rm i}^{2}\bar{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\Biggl[\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\frac{(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})^{2}}{\tau^{2}\tau_{\rm i}^{2}\bar{T}^{2}}-\frac{1}{4(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})^{2}\tau\tau_{\rm i}\bar{T}^{4}}+\frac{\pi^{3}}{2}\frac{\tau-\tau_{\rm i}}{\tau\tau_{\rm i}\bar{T}}\frac{3+\cosh\bigl[4\pi(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})\bar{T}\bigr]}{\sinh^{3}\bigl[2\pi(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})\bar{T}\bigr]}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{T}}\biggl(-36\zeta(3)\left[\log\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{\rm i}}\Bigr)-\frac{\tau-\tau_{\rm i}}{\tau}\right]^{2}+\log(2)\frac{(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})^{2}}{\tau^{2}\tau_{\rm i}^{2}\bar{T}^{2}}\biggr)\Biggr]\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\Bigr)^{2}\mathrm{,}\,\Bigl(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{T}}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)\,.

(4.34)

One can further clarify the importance of the different terms by considering the
\tau\gg\tau_{\rm i}
limit. Furthermore,
as we concluded in eq. (
E.7
), factors such as
1/(\tau\bar{T})
or
1/\sinh^{3}\bigl[2\pi(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})\bar{T}\bigr]

are strongly suppressed and
1/(\tau_{\rm i}\bar{T})
can in the limit considered here also be regarded as small.
The correlator thus becomes

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{th},\,\tau_{\rm i}{\bar{T}}\gg 1}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{8\pi}{15}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}\bar{T}^{5}\Biggl\{180\zeta(5)\log^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{\rm i}}\Bigr)+\lambda\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\biggl[\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm i}^{2}\bar{T}^{2}}-36\zeta(3)\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{T}}\log^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{\rm i}}\Bigr)\biggr]\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\Bigr)^{2}\mathrm{,}\,\Bigl(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{T}}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)\,.

(4.35)

In terms of the Stokes parameters, the above results can be summarized as

\displaystyle I(\bm{k})

\displaystyle\simeq 192\pi\zeta(5)\frac{1}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}\bar{T}^{5}\log^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{\rm i}}\Bigr)\,,

(4.36)

\displaystyle V(\bm{k})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{192\pi}{5}\frac{1}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}\bar{T}^{4}k\bigg[\frac{\pi^{2}}{432}\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm i}^{2}\bar{T}^{2}}-\zeta(3)\frac{\bar{\mu}}{\bar{T}}\log^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{\rm i}}\Bigr)\bigg]\,.

(4.37)

For
V(\bm{k})
we have kept the leading
1/(\tau_{\rm i}\bar{T})^{2}
term,
having the situation of vanishing chemical potential in
mind, in which case it becomes the leading contribution to the difference between the two helicities.
We will, however, for simplicity ignore this term in the following discussions, as for currently
available estimates of
\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}
the second term will be dominant.

4.3
Comparisons and discussions

In general, the leading-order correlation functions of gravitational fields in the Minkowski and expanding universes in the long wavelength limit are different. In the Minkowski spacetime, the correlation functions diverge at
k\rightarrow 0
. From the contributions of neutrinos both in vacuum and in thermal equilibrium,
the parity-even and parity-odd components of correlation functions become
\mathcal{O}(k^{-4})
and
\mathcal{O}(k^{-3})
, respectively. On the contrary, the correlation functions in the FLRW background are finite at
k\rightarrow 0
. From neutrinos in thermal equilibrium, the parity-even and parity-odd components of correlation functions emerge at
\mathcal{O}(k^{0})
and
\mathcal{O}(k)
, respectively.
Compared with eq. (
4.10
) in the Minkowski spacetime, the
V
parameter in eq. (
4.37
) in the expanding universe is thus much suppressed in the long wavelength regime. Note that both the parity-even and parity-odd components are at
\mathcal{O}(k^{0})
in ref.
[
23
]
in the long wavelength limit, while a different mechanism for the helicity imbalance generated in the inflation period is considered therein.

When focusing on the primary contribution from a non-zero chemical potential with
\tau_{\rm i}\bar{T}\gg 1
and recast the temperature into physical one at
\tau_{\rm i}
,
T_{\rm i}\equiv T(\tau_{\rm i})=\bar{T}/a_{\rm i}
, eq. (
4.37
) becomes

\displaystyle V(\bm{k})\simeq-\frac{192\pi\zeta(3)}{5}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}T_{\rm i}^{4}k\mathcal{N}_{\nu}\xi\log^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{\rm i}}\Bigr)\,,

(4.38)

where
\mathcal{N}_{\nu}=3
is the number of neutrinos and we
define the flavor averaged neutrino degeneracy parameter
\xi
as

\displaystyle\xi\equiv\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{\nu}}\sum_{j}\xi_{j},

(4.39)

where
\xi_{j}\equiv\mu_{j}/T=\bar{\mu}_{j}/\bar{T}
, in which the subscript “
j
” represents the neutrino flavor.
Note that
V(\bm{k})
itself depends on the scale factor through
k=a(\tau)k_{\rm phys}(\tau)
.
In order to make a direct comparison with observations, we may introduce an observable independent of the scale factor,

\displaystyle\Delta\chi({\bm{k}})\equiv\frac{{V}(\bm{k})}{{I}(\bm{k})}=\frac{\langle h_{+}(\bm{k},t)h_{+}(\bm{k}^{\prime},t)\rangle_{\text{th}}-\langle h_{-}(\bm{k},t)h_{-}(\bm{k}^{\prime},t)\rangle_{\text{th}}}{\langle h_{+}(\bm{k},t)h_{+}(\bm{k}^{\prime},t)\rangle_{\text{th}}+\langle h_{-}(\bm{k},t)h_{-}(\bm{k}^{\prime},t)\rangle_{\text{th}}}\,.

(4.40)

From eq. (
4.2
), we find

\displaystyle\Delta\chi({\bm{k}})=-\frac{\zeta(3)}{5\zeta(5)}\xi\frac{k}{\bar{T}}=-\frac{\zeta(3)}{5\zeta(5)}\xi\frac{k_{\rm g}}{T_{\rm g}}\,,

(4.41)

where we take
k_{\rm g}=k_{\rm phys}(\tau_{\rm g})
and
T_{\rm g}=T(\tau_{\rm g})
with
k_{\rm phys}(\tau)=k/a(\tau)
and
\tau_{\rm g}
the conformal time when gravitational waves are generated from the thermal plasma. Note that
\Delta\chi({\bm{k}})
is not suppressed by
m_{\rm P}
and depends on three parameters,
k_{\rm g}
,
T_{\rm g}
and
\xi
;
the parameter
k_{\rm g}
can be related to the momentum (wavenumber) of gravitational waves measured at present time via
k_{\rm g}=a(\tau_{0})k_{\rm phys}(\tau_{0})/a(\tau_{\rm g})
, where
\tau_{0}
denotes the present conformal time and
k_{\rm phy}(\tau_{0})
corresponds to the measured momentum of gravitational waves;
T_{\rm g}
may be chosen as
T_{\rm g}\gtrsim 1\,{\rm MeV}
when neutrinos are still in equilibrium in a primordial plasma during the radiation dominant era;
\xi
is not well constrained, but at least for electron neutrinos known to be small, the recent ref.
[
37
]
for instance obtaining a value of
\xi_{\rm e}=0.05\pm 0.03
(see also the recent ref.
[
38
]
, which reports a similar result and refs.
[
27
,
42
,
41
,
39
]
for earlier estimates). In ref.
[
40
]
, a possible theoretical model that could generate a sufficiently large lepton asymmetry consistent with the estimate in ref.
[
37
]
is also proposed. Yet the exact value of
\xi
is still not fully determined from present observations for all neutrino types. In this work, we assume that the chemical potentials of all neutrino flavors are equilibrated and hence
\xi_{\rm e}=\xi_{\mu}=\xi_{\tau}=\xi

[
39
]
and employ the numerical
value of ref.
[
37
]
quoted above.

The computation for
\Delta\chi({\bm{k}})
above only incorporates the gravitational waves produced from thermalized neutrinos. In practice, we should further include other sources that contribute to
I(\bm{k})
and
V(\bm{k})
. Considering only the gravitational waves produced by matter in thermal equilibrium from a primordial plasma, we may simply multiply
I(\bm{k})
in eq. (
4.36
) by the effective degrees of freedom. Recall that
I(\bm{k})
is dominated by the
\Gamma_{\pm}
terms associated with the integral
I_{1}(k,\pm\bar{\mu},\bar{T})
in eq. (
D.1
) for
\mu\ll T
. We hence simply replace the fermionic distribution function in
I_{1}(k,\pm\bar{\mu},\bar{T})
by its bosonic counterpart to roughly estimate the similar bosonic contributions. In addition, we neglect particle masses, assuming that they are much smaller than the considered temperature scales. Consequently, the effective degrees of freedom for
I(\bm{k})
here can be characterized by

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\rm d.o.f.}=\mathcal{N}_{\rm f}+\frac{16}{15}\mathcal{N}_{\rm b}\,,

(4.42)

due to the relation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}x\frac{x^{4}}{{\rm e}^{x}-1}=\frac{16}{15}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}x\frac{x^{4}}{{\rm e}^{x}+1}\,,

(4.43)

where
\mathcal{N}_{\rm f}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\rm b}
denote the total numbers of fermions and bosons in thermal equilibrium, respectively. For
V(\bm{k})
, the LO contribution arises due to the non-vanishing neutrino degeneracy parameter
\xi
. We eventually obtain a generic expression

\displaystyle\Delta\chi({\bm{k}})=-\frac{\zeta(3)r(T_{\rm g})\mathcal{N}_{\nu}}{5\zeta(5)\mathcal{N}_{\rm d.o.f.}}\frac{k_{\rm phys}(\tau_{0})}{T_{\rm g}}\xi\,.

(4.44)

Here,
r(T_{\rm g})\equiv a(\tau_{0})/a(\tau_{\rm g})
, for which we may utilize
[
43
]

\displaystyle r(T_{\rm g})\simeq 1.25\times 10^{13}\left(\frac{g_{\ast s}(T_{\rm g})}{100}\right)^{1/3}\left(\frac{T_{\rm g}}{\rm GeV}\right)\,,

(4.45)

where
g_{\ast s}(T_{\rm g})
represents effective entropic degrees of freedom at
T_{\rm g}
.
As an example, we choose
T_{\rm g}=200\,{\rm MeV}
just above the QCD phase transition. Accordingly, we take
\mathcal{N}_{\rm f}=50
,
\mathcal{N}_{\rm b}=18
, and
g_{\ast s}(T_{\rm g})\approx 61.75

[
44
]
, which yield
\mathcal{N}_{\rm d.o.f.}\approx 69.2
and
r(T_{\rm g})\approx 2.13\times 10^{12}
. In such a case, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta\chi({\bm{k}})\approx-5.35\times 10^{9}\left(\frac{k_{\rm phys}(\tau_{\rm 0})}{\rm GeV}\right)=-2.21\times 10^{-14}\left(\frac{f}{\rm Hz}\right)\,

(4.46)

by taking
\xi=0.05
,
where we have defined
f\equiv 2\pi k_{\rm phys}(\tau_{\rm 0})
.

Let us next estimate the gravitational wave spectrum emitted from thermalized primordial neutrinos that can be measured at the present time.
For this purpose (and for making a direct comparison to the existing literature simpler), we define the dimensionless power spectra
h^{2}_{I}(\bm{k},\eta)
and

h^{2}_{V}(\bm{k},\eta)
as

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\eta)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{\ast}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi^{5}}{k^{3}}\delta(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}[h^{2}_{I}(\bm{k},\eta)+\lambda h^{2}_{V}(\bm{k},\eta)]\,,

(4.47)

where we have

\displaystyle h^{2}_{I}(\bm{k},\tau)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{12\zeta(5)}{\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}k^{3}\bar{T}^{5}\log^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{\rm i}}\right)\,,

(4.48)

\displaystyle h^{2}_{V}(\bm{k},\tau)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{12\zeta(3)}{5\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}k^{4}\bar{T}^{4}\xi\log^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{\rm i}}\right)\,.

(4.49)

To express the gravitational wave spectrum as a function of frequency
f
at the present time, we use
[
43
]

\displaystyle h^{2}\Omega^{I/V}_{\rm{GW}}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3}\frac{h^{2}}{H_{0}^{2}}f^{2}h_{I/V}^{2}(f)

(4.50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3}10^{-4}\left(\frac{\rm{Mpc}}{\rm{km}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{f}{\rm{Hz}}\right)^{2}h_{I/V}^{2}(f)\,,

where
H_{0}
is the Hubble constant today, which is related to the parameter
h
as

H_{0}=100\,h\,\rm{km}\,\rm{s}^{-1}\,\rm{Mpc}^{-1}
. Furthermore,
h_{I/V}^{2}(f)
and
f
are obtained from

h^{2}_{I/V}(\bm{k},\tau)
at time
\tau
and the momentum
\bm{k}

as
[
45
,
46
,
18
]

\displaystyle h^{2}_{I/V}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\Omega_{m}}{\Omega_{\Lambda}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{g_{\ast}(T)}{g_{\ast}(T_{0})}\right)\left(\frac{g_{\ast s}(T_{0})}{g_{\ast s}(T)}\right)^{4/3}\overline{\left(\frac{3j_{1}(k\tau_{0})}{k\tau_{0}}\right)^{2}}T_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{k_{\mathrm{phys}}}{k_{\mathrm{eq}}}\right)h^{2}_{I/V}(\bm{k},\tau)\,,

(4.51)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a(\tau)}{a(\tau_{0})}\frac{k_{\mathrm{phys}}(\tau)}{2\pi}\,.

(4.52)

Here, the factor
\Omega_{m}/\Omega_{\Lambda}
expresses the ratio of matter and dark energy in the
current universe (
\Omega_{m}=0.3
,
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7
).
g_{\ast}(T)
denotes, similar to the entropic degrees of freedom
g_{\ast s}(T)
, the effective number of relativistic degrees of freedom contributing to the energy density at temperature
T
.
j_{1}(x)=(\sin x/x-\cos x)/x
is the spherical Bessel function and the bar above it
represents the averaging over multiple periods.
Considering its argument
x=k\tau_{0}
, we use
\tau_{0}=1.4\times 10^{3}\,{\rm Mpc}
and
k=k_{\mathrm{phys}}(\tau_{0})/a(\tau_{0})
with
a(\tau_{0})=1
to obtain

\displaystyle k\tau_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 9.1\times 10^{17}\left(\frac{f}{\mathrm{Hz}}\right)\gg 1\,,

(4.53)

for all frequencies
f
relevant for realistic measurements.
Therefore, we can approximate
j_{1}(x)\simeq-\cos x/x
and the barred factor becomes

\displaystyle\overline{\left(\frac{3j_{1}(k\tau_{0})}{k\tau_{0}}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{9}{2}\frac{1}{(k\tau_{0})^{4}}\,.

(4.54)

T_{1}(x)
is the so-called transfer function which describes
the transfer effect of the universe from the radiation dominant
to matter dominant
eras and is obtained by
numerically solving the equation governing
h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)

It can be given as

\displaystyle T^{2}_{1}(x)=1+1.57x+3.42x^{2}.

(4.55)

The momentum

appearing in eq. (
4.51
) is determined by the conformal time

corresponding
to the matter-radiation equality,

with
h=0.7
.
Moreover, the ratio
a(\tau)/a(\tau_{0})

5
Summary and outlook

We have studied the birefringence of gravitational waves triggered by primordial left-handed neutrinos in thermal equilibrium. A generic expression for correlation functions of gravitational fluctuations with different helicities due to chiral fermions at finite temperature and density is derived and further applied to analyze the Stokes parameters of gravitational waves induced by thermalized neutrinos in the early Universe. We investigated both the cases of Minkowski and expanding spacetime backgrounds and obtained a non-vanishing
V
parameter characterizing gravitational birefringence for non-zero neutrino chemical potentials. In the expanding universe, the general expression for the parity-violating parameter
\Delta\chi
is given by eq. (
4.44
).

While we have in this work focused on the thermalized neutrinos in the early Universe, our argument and derivation may be extended to the thermalized neutrino as chiral matter in core-collapse supernovae
[
57
]
, which is expected to produce polarized gravitational waves as well. More generically, chiral gravitational waves from neutrinos or other chiral fermions can also be generated in out of equilibrium situations both in the early Universe and supernovae. To study such situations, one needs to use the radiation transport theory taking into account the effects of chirality of fermions, termed the chiral radiation transport theory
[
58
]
, in which the energy-momentum tensor with chiral corrections is expressed in terms of the distribution function of chiral fermions. These questions are deferred to future work.

integrals

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}{\rm d}\theta\sin^{3}\theta\cos\theta(1-\lambda\cos\theta)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{8}{15}\lambda\,,

(C.12)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}{\rm d}\theta\sin^{5}\theta(\cos\theta-\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{16}{15}\lambda\,,

(C.13)

one gets

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{th},\,\Gamma_{0},\,\mathrm{NLO}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{16\pi}{15}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}\lambda k\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}p\ p^{4}

(C.14)

\displaystyle\times\Biggl\{\frac{2}{p}(f_{p-\bar{\mu}}+f_{p+\bar{\mu}})\sin[(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})p]\Bigr(\sin[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\bigl[\mathrm{ci}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{ci}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)\bigr]

\displaystyle-\cos[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\bigl[\mathrm{Si}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{Si}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)\bigr]\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\left[-\frac{2}{p}(f_{p-\bar{\mu}}+f_{p+\bar{\mu}})+\frac{1}{T}f_{p+\bar{\mu}}(1-f_{p+\bar{\mu}})\right]

\displaystyle\times\biggl[\Bigl(\mathrm{ci}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{ci}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)-\frac{1}{\tau p}\sin[(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})p]\cos[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle+\Bigl(\mathrm{Si}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{Si}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)-\frac{1}{\tau p}\sin[(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})p]\sin[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\Bigr)^{2}\biggr]\Biggr\}\,.

Appendix D
Derivation of eqs. (
4.27
) and (
4.31
)

The remaining
p

4.24
), (
4.25
) and (

D.1
\Gamma_{\pm}

Starting from eq. (
4.24
), we need to evaluate the following integral

\displaystyle I_{1}(k,\pm\bar{\mu},\bar{T})=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}p\ p^{4}\left(1\pm\lambda\frac{k}{p}\right)f_{p\pm\bar{\mu}}\,.

(D.1)

Changing the integration variable from
p
to
y=p/\bar{T}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}yy^{n}\frac{1}{{\rm e}^{y\pm\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}+1}=-n!\mathrm{Li}_{n+1}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{\mp\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}\bigr),

(D.2)

where
\mathrm{Li}_{s}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}z^{k}/k^{s}
is the polylogarithm function, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}(k,\pm\bar{\mu},\bar{T})=-24\bar{T}^{5}\Bigl[\mathrm{Li}_{5}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{\mp\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}\bigr)\pm\frac{\lambda}{4}\frac{k}{\bar{T}}\mathrm{Li}_{4}\bigl(-{\rm e}^{\mp\bar{\mu}/\bar{T}}\bigr)\Bigr],

(D.3)

which leads to eq. (
4.27
).

D.2
\Gamma_{0}
term

As we discussed in the main text, it is possible to recast the functions
\mathrm{Si}(x)
and
\mathrm{ci}(x)
into simpler expressions for the temperature and time regions considered in this work. By making use of eqs. (
4.29
) and (
4.30
), the parts of the integrands involving
\mathrm{Si}(x)
and
\mathrm{ci}(x)
can be simplified as

\displaystyle\Bigl(\mathrm{ci}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{ci}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)-\frac{1}{\tau p}\sin[(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})p]\cos[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle+\Bigl(\mathrm{Si}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{Si}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)-\frac{1}{\tau p}\sin[(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})p]\sin[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})^{2}}{4\tau_{\rm i}^{2}\tau^{2}p^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{(\tau p)^{3}}\Bigr)\,,

(D.4)

\displaystyle\sin[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\bigl[\mathrm{ci}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{ci}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)\bigr]-\cos[(\tau+\tau_{\rm i})p]\bigl[\mathrm{Si}(2\tau p)-\mathrm{Si}(2\tau_{\rm i}p)\bigr]

\displaystyle-\frac{\tau-\tau_{\rm i}}{2\tau_{\rm i}\tau p}\cos[(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})p]+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{(\tau p)^{2}}\Bigr)\,.

(D.5)

Using eq. (
D.4

\displaystyle\langle h_{\lambda}(\bm{k},\tau)h_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{th},\,\Gamma_{0},\,\mathrm{LO}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{5}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})}{m_{\rm P}^{4}}\frac{\tau_{\rm i}^{4}}{a_{\rm i}^{4}}\frac{(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})^{2}}{\tau^{2}\tau_{\rm i}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}p\ p^{2}(f_{p-\bar{\mu}}+f_{p+\bar{\mu}})

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}p\ p^{2}f_{p\pm\bar{\mu}}\sin[2(\tau-\tau_{\rm i})p]

Appendix E
Estimate of
\tau\bar{T}
and
\tau_{\rm i}\bar{T}

We here derive an order-of-magnitude estimate of
\tau\bar{T}
and
\tau_{\rm i}\bar{T}
in the radiation dominated period,
in which the scale factor evolves as (see, e.g., ref.
[
59
]
)

\displaystyle a(t)=At^{1/2},

(E.1)

where

\displaystyle A=\left[\frac{32\pi G}{3}(\epsilon a^{4})\right]^{1/4},

(E.2)

with
\epsilon a^{4}=
constant.
Here,
\epsilon
is the energy density, which is expressed at leading order of the
\mu/T
expansion as

\displaystyle\epsilon=g_{\ast}(T)\frac{\pi^{2}}{30}T^{4}\,,

(E.3)

where
g_{\ast}(T)
is the number of relativistic degrees of freedom at temperature
T
.

From the equations above,
t
can be related to
T
as

\displaystyle t=\left(\frac{45}{16\pi^{3}Gg_{\ast}(T)}\right)^{1/2}\frac{1}{T^{2}}\,.

(E.4)

Furthermore, we can obtain
\tau
from
{\rm d}t=a{\rm d}\tau
and eq. (
E.1
) as

\displaystyle\tau-\tau_{\rm i}=\int^{t}_{t_{\rm i}}\frac{{\rm d}t}{a(t)}=\frac{2}{A}(t^{1/2}-t_{\rm i}^{1/2})\,,

(E.5)

and hence,

\displaystyle\tau\bar{T}-\tau_{\rm i}\bar{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{2025}{256\pi^{6}G^{2}g^{2}_{\ast}(T)}\right)^{1/4}\frac{1}{T}\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{g_{\ast}(T)}T}{\sqrt{g_{*}(T_{\rm i})}T_{\rm i}}\right).

(E.6)

By extracting the
\tau
-dependent component above, we find

\displaystyle\tau\bar{T}=2\left(\frac{2025}{256\pi^{6}G^{2}g^{2}_{\ast}(T)}\right)^{1/4}\frac{1}{T}\sim 10^{20}\left(\frac{T}{\mathrm{GeV}}\right)^{-1}.

(E.7)

We can therefore conclude that
\tau\bar{T}\gg 1
, for relevant temperatures in the radiation dominant period.

Considering next
\tau_{\rm i}\bar{T}
,
based on the
\tau
-independent part in eq. (
E.6
),
we can derive

\displaystyle\tau_{\rm i}{\bar{T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[\frac{2025}{256\pi^{6}G^{2}g^{2}_{\ast}(\tau_{\rm i})}\right]^{1/4}\frac{1}{T(\tau_{\rm i})},

(E.8)

which can further be simplified as

\displaystyle\tau_{\rm i}{\bar{T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3\sqrt{10}}{\pi g^{1/2}_{\ast}(\tau_{\rm i})}\frac{m_{\mathrm{P}}}{T(\tau_{\rm i})}.

(E.9)

The numerical factor in front of
m_{\mathrm{P}}/T(\tau_{\rm i})
is approximately 1 and we hence have

\displaystyle\tau_{\rm i}{\bar{T}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{m_{\mathrm{P}}}{T(\tau_{\rm i})},

(E.10)

which shows that
\tau_{\rm i}{\bar{T}}\gg 1
as long as
T(\tau_{\rm i})\ll m_{\rm P}
.

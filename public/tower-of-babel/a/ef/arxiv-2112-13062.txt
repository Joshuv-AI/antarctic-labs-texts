Categorical idempotents via shifted 0-affine algebras
You-Hung Hsu

Abstract
. We show that a categorical action of shifted 0-affine algebra naturally gives two families of pairs of complementary idempotents in the triangulated monoidal category of triangulated endofunctors for each weight category. Consequently, we obtain two families of pairs of complementary idempotents in the triangulated monoidal category 𝒟 b ​ Coh ​ ( G / P × G / P ) \mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(G/P\times G/P) . As an application, this provides examples where the projection functors of a semiorthogonal decomposition are kernel functors, and we determine the generators of the component categories in the Grassmannians case.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Complementary idempotents

Let
A
be a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with unit
1_{A}
. An element
a\in A
is called an idempotent if
a^{2}=a
. An idempotent
a\in A
naturally induces another idempotent
1_{A}-a
, called its complementary idempotent. In particular, they are mutually orthogonal and give a decomposition of
1_{A}
.

Hogancamp
[
20
]
develops a categorical analog of the above theory of idempotent in the following sense: Given objects
\boldsymbol{T}
,
\boldsymbol{S}
in a triangulated monoidal category
\mathcal{A}
, we call
(\boldsymbol{T},\boldsymbol{S})
a
pair of complementary idempotents
if they satisfy mutual orthogonality and give a decomposition of the monoidal identity
\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathcal{A}}
, see Definition
2.14
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{A}
acts on a triangulated category
\mathcal{M}
by triangulated endofunctors (categorical analog of
A
-modules), then the pair of complementary idempotents
(\boldsymbol{T},\boldsymbol{S})
gives a short semiorthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{M}=\langle\text{Im}\boldsymbol{S},\text{Im}\boldsymbol{T}\rangle
, where
\text{Im}{\sf{F}}
denotes the minimal full triangulated subcategory generated by the class of objects that are essential images of a functor
{\sf{F}}
.

1.2.
Categorical actions

Given a complex semisimple or Kac-Moody Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. There has been much progress in the categorification of
\mathfrak{g}
(or the quantum group
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
) and their representations over the past decade. The notion of lifting their representations from vector spaces to categories is called the
categorical actions
.

For example, when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, a categorical
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-action consists of weight categories
\mathcal{C}(\lambda)
and functors
{\sf{E}}:\mathcal{C}(\lambda)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(\lambda+2)
,
{\sf{F}}:\mathcal{C}(\lambda)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(\lambda-2)
such that

{\sf{E}\sf{F}}|_{\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}\cong{\sf{F}\sf{E}}|_{\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}\bigoplus\text{Id}_{\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}^{\oplus\lambda}\ \text{if}\ \lambda\geq 0,

(1.1)

a similar equivalence exists for
\lambda\leq 0
.

Note that we do not impose any natural transforms in (
1.1
). Understanding the natural transformations between the functors in (
1.1
) is important in higher representation theory. Chuang-Rouquier
[
12
]
gives one answer to such a problem for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and later generalized to (simply-laced) Kac-Moody algebras
\mathfrak{g}
by Khovanov-Lauda
[
25
]
and Rouquier
[
30
]
.

1.3.
Main result

In
[
18
]
, the author defines an algebra called the shifted 0-affine algebra, denoted by
\mathcal{U}=\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
, and defines the notion of
categorical
\mathcal{U}
-action
on certain triangulated 2-categories, see Definition
4.2
and Definition
4.4
. It is proved that there is a categorical
\mathcal{U}
-action on the bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves on
n
-step partial flag varieties, see Proposition
4.5
.

In this article, we use categorical
\mathcal{U}
-action to construct pairs of complementary idempotents in the triangulated monoidal category of triangulated endofunctors of each weight category. We first state the main result of this article.

Theorem A

(Theorem
5.4
, Corollary
5.7
)
.

Given a categorical
\mathcal{U}
-action on
\mathcal{K}
. Then for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, there are pairs of complementary idempotents
({\sf{T}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}},{\sf{S}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}})
in the triangulated monoidal category
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}),\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}))
. Moreover, relations between those functors are given.

In particular, when
\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})=\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))
in which we prove there is a categorical
\mathcal{U}
-action in
[
18
]
, we obtain pairs of complementary idempotents that given by Fourier-Mukai kernels
({\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}},\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}})
in the triangulated monoidal category
\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})\times\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, where the convolution of kernels gives the monoidal structure.

Now we give a brief explanation of the main result. Denote by
\underline{k}=(k_{1},...,k_{n})\vDash N
a composition of
N\geq 2
of
n
parts. Each
\underline{k}
corresponds to a
n
-step partial flag variety below

\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})\coloneqq\{V_{\bullet}=(0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset...\subset V_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{N})\ |\ \mathrm{dim}V_{i}/V_{i-1}=k_{i}\ \text{for}\ \text{all}\ i\}.

(1.2)

We denote
\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X)
to be the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on a variety
X
. The generators
e_{i,r}1_{\underline{k}}
and
f_{i,s}1_{\underline{k}}
with
-k_{i}-1\leq r\leq 0
and
0\leq s\leq k_{i+1}+1
of
\mathcal{U}
act on
\bigoplus_{\underline{k}^{\prime}\vDash N}\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))
by the following correspondence diagram

\textstyle{W^{1}_{i}(\underline{k})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{p_{2}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}+\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})}

where

W^{1}_{i}(\underline{k})\coloneqq\{(V_{\bullet},V_{\bullet}^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})\times\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}+\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})\ |\ V_{j}=V^{\prime}_{j}\ \mathrm{for}\ j\neq i,\ V^{\prime}_{i}\subset V_{i}\}
. Here
\alpha_{i}=(0...0,-1,1,0...0)
is the simple root with the
-1
is in the
i
th position, and
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
are the natural projections.

The generators
e_{i,r}1_{\underline{k}}
act on
\bigoplus_{\underline{k}^{\prime}\vDash N}\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))
by lifting to the following functors

{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\coloneqq p_{2*}(p_{1}^{*}\otimes(\mathcal{V}_{i}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{i})^{r}):\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}+\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))

where we denote
\mathcal{V}_{i}
,
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{i}
to be the tautological bundles on
W^{1}_{i}(\underline{k})
of rank
k_{1}+..+k_{i}
,
k_{1}+..+k_{i}-1
respectively, and thus we form the quotient line bundle
\mathcal{V}_{i}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{i}
. Similarly for the lift of
f_{i,s}1_{\underline{k}}
to
{\sf{F}}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
in the opposite direction. Furthermore,
{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}{\underline{k}}
and
{\sf{F}}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}{\underline{k}}
are Fourier-Mukai transforms with kernels denoted by
\mathcal{E}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
respectively, see Proposition
4.5
for details.

The idea comes from the observation that the categorical Demazure operators can be expressed as generators in the categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
-action and generalized to the
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-action with
n<N
. In more detail, note that we have the full flag variety
\mathrm{Fl}_{(1,1,...,1)}(\mathbb{C}^{N})=G/B
and the partial flag varieties are
G/P_{i}=\mathrm{Fl}_{(1,1,...,1)+\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})=\mathrm{Fl}_{(1,1,...,1)-\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
where
G=\mathrm{SL}_{N}
,
B\subset G
is the standard Borel and
P_{i}\subset G
is the minimal parabolic subgroup corresponds to the simple root
\alpha_{i}
.

There are natural projection
\pi_{i}:G/B\rightarrow G/P_{i}
that induces derived pullback and derived pushforward
\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(G/B)\overset{\pi_{i*}}{\underset{\pi^{*}_{i}}{\rightleftarrows}}\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(G/P_{i})
. Then the categorical Demazure operators are defined by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{T}}_{i}\coloneqq\pi^{*}_{i}\pi_{i*}
. A standard calculation tells us that
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{T}}_{i}
corresponds to a Fourier-Mukai transform, with its kernel given by
\mathcal{T}_{i}=\mathcal{O}_{G/B\times_{G/P_{i}}G/B}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(G/B\times G/B)
, which is the structure sheaf of the Bott-Samelson variety
G/B\times_{G/P_{i}}G/B
for all
i
. From
[
18
, Section 6]
, we have the following isomorphisms of Fourier-Mukai kernels

\mathcal{T}_{i}\cong(\mathcal{E}_{i,0}\ast\mathcal{F}_{i,1}\ast(\Psi^{+}_{i})^{-1})\boldsymbol{1}_{(1,1,...,1)}\cong(\mathcal{F}_{i,0}\ast\mathcal{E}_{i,-1}\ast(\Psi^{-}_{i})^{-1})\boldsymbol{1}_{(1,1,...,1)},

(1.3)

where
\Psi_{i}^{+}
,
\Psi_{i}^{-}
are certain Fourier-Mukai kernels defined in Proposition
4.5
for all
1\leq i\leq N-1
.

Motivating from the idempotent property of Demazure operators, we define
{\sf{T}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{(1,1,...,1)}:={\sf{E}}_{i,0}{\sf{F}}_{i,1}({\sf{\Psi}}^{+}_{i})^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(1,1,...,1)}
for an abstract categorical
\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
-action
\mathcal{K}
. Then it turns out that they satisfy
({\sf{T}}^{\prime}_{i})^{2}\boldsymbol{1}_{(1,1,...,1)}\cong{\sf{T}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{(1,1,...,1)}
(see (
5.4
) for details). This implies that
{\sf{T}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{(1,1,...,1)}
is a weak idempotent (see Definition
2.13
).

To generalize this from categorical
\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
-action to categorical
\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-action with
n<N
, the key observation is to expressing
{\sf{T}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{(1,1,...,1)}
as natural compositions of functors with their left and right adjoints. More precisely, by condition (4) in Definition
4.4
, we have

{\sf{T}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{(1,1,...,1)}\cong{\sf{E}}_{i,0}\boldsymbol{1}_{(1,1,...,1)-\alpha_{i}}({\sf{E}}_{i,0}\boldsymbol{1}_{(1,1,...,1)-\alpha_{i}})^{R}

then we define
{\sf{T}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
by the same way, i.e.,
{\sf{T}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\coloneqq{\sf{E}}_{i,0}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}-\alpha_{i}}({\sf{E}}_{i,0}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}-\alpha_{i}})^{R}
, similarly for its complementary
{\sf{S}}_{i}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
, see (
5.18
) and (
5.20
) for details.

1.4.
Application to semiorthogonal decomposition

Observe that for each
\underline{k}\vDash N
, we have an action of
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}),\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}))
on the weight category
\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})
. From Subsection
1.1
, a pair of complementary idempotents gives a short semiorthogonal decomposition; as a consequence of Theorem
A
, we have the following
\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})=\langle\text{Im}{\sf{S}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}},\ \text{Im}{\sf{T}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\rangle
.

In particular, when the weight categories are
\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})=\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))
, the triangulated monoidal category
\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})\times\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))
acts on
\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))
by Fourier-Mukai transforms. Thus we obtain

\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))=\langle\text{Im}\Phi_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}},\text{Im}\Phi_{{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}}\rangle

(1.4)

for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. This provides concrete examples where the projection functors of a semiorthogonal decomposition are given by Fourier-Mukai kernels; see Proposition
2.12
.

It is natural to ask what explicitly are the component categories in (
1.4
). We answer this question in the case where
n=2
, i.e., partial flag varieties are the Grassmannians
\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)
. Let
\mathcal{V}
be the tautological rank
k
bundle on
\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)
. Then there is a full exceptional collection given by Kapranov
[
23
]

\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,N))=\langle\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in P(N-k,k)},

(1.5)

where we denote by
P(N-k,k)
the set of Young diagrams with at most
k
rows and at most
N-k
columns, and
\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is the associated Schur functor for each
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in P(N-k,k)
.

Since
n=2
, we drop the subscript
i
for the generators in
\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
. Then we have the complementary idempotents
(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)\times\mathrm{Gr}(k,N))
, and we calculate the action of the Fourier-Mukai transform
\Phi_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}}
on the exceptional objects in (
1.5
).

Theorem B

(Theorem
6.3
)
.

For
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{k})\in P(N-k,k)
, we have

\Phi_{{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}}(\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\ \lambda_{1}=N-k\\
\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}&\text{if}\ 0\leq\lambda_{1}\leq N-k-1.\end{cases}

This tells us that the Kapranov exceptional collection is a (categorical) eigenbasis for the weak idempotent
\Phi_{{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}}
. Since
(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)},\ \mathcal{S}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})
is a complementary idempotent, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary A

(Corollary
6.4
)
.

The component categories in (
1.4
) for
\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,N))
are given by

\emph{Im}\Phi_{{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}}=\langle\ \mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}\ \rangle_{0\leq\lambda_{1}\leq N-k-1},\ \emph{Im}\Phi_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}}=\langle\ \mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}\ \rangle_{\lambda_{1}=N-k}.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we provide some background materials for the tools used in the later part of the article.

2.1.
Exceptional collection and semiorthogonal decomposition

In the first subsection, we give the definitions of semiorthogonal decomposition and exceptional collection. We refer the readers to
[
28
]
for a detailed survey.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-linear triangulated category.

Given a class
\mathcal{E}
of objects in
\mathcal{D}
, we denoted
\langle\mathcal{E}\rangle
to be the minimal full triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
containing all objects in
\mathcal{E}
and closed under taking direct summands. Similarly for any sequence of full triangulated subcategories
\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
in
\mathcal{D}
we denote by
\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle
the minimal full triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
which contains all of
\mathcal{A}_{i},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

We begin with the definition of semiorthogonal decompositions.

Definition 2.1
.

A
semiorthogonal decomposition
(
\sf{SOD}
for short) of
\mathcal{D}
is a sequence of full triangulated subcategories
\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
such that

(1)

there is no non-zero Homs from right to left, i.e.

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(A_{i},A_{j})=0
for all
A_{i}\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
,
A_{j}\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{A}_{j})
where
1\leq j<i\leq n
.

(2)

\mathcal{D}
is generated by
\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
, i.e. the smallest full triangulated subcategory containing
\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
is equal to
\mathcal{D}
.

We will use the notation
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle
for a semiorthogonal decomposition of
\mathcal{D}
with components
\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

A standard way to produce a
\sf{SOD}
is to construct a left or right admissible subcategory, which is the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

A full triangulated subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{D}
is called
right admissible
if, for the inclusion functor
i:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
, there is a right adjoint
i^{!}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
, and
left admissible
if there is a left adjoint
i^{*}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
. It is called
admissible
if it is both left and right admissible.

We give a remark.

Remark 2.3
.

A semiorthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle
is called strong if for each
k
, the category
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is admissible in
\langle\mathcal{A}_{k},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle
. If
X
is a smooth projective variety, then any semiorthogonal decomposition of the derived category of coherent sheaves
\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
is strong.

Then the following lemma gives us a way to produce
\sf{SOD}
s.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
10
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rangle
is a
\sf{SOD}
, then
\mathcal{A}
is left admissible and B is right admissible. Conversely, if
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{D}
is left admissible, then
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A},^{\perp}\mathcal{A}\rangle
is a
\sf{SOD}
, and if
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{D}
is right admissible,
then
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{B}^{\perp},\mathcal{B}\rangle
is a
\sf{SOD}
.

The simplest example of an admissible subcategory is the one generated by an exceptional object.

Definition 2.5
.

An object
E\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D})
is called
exceptional
if

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E,E[l])=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&\ \text{if}\ l=0\\
0&\ \text{if}\ l\neq 0.\end{cases}

Then we define the notion of exceptional collections.

Definition 2.6
.

An ordered collection
\{E_{1},...,E_{n}\}
, where
E_{i}\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D})
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, is called an
exceptional collection
if each
E_{i}
is exceptional and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E_{i},E_{j}[l])=0
for all
i>j
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. The collection (sequence) is called
strong
exceptional if in addition

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E_{i},E_{j}[l])=0\ \text{for all}\ i,\ j\ \text{and}\ l\neq 0.

Thus, an exceptional collection
\{E_{1},...,E_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{D}
naturally give rise to a
\sf{SOD}
of
\mathcal{D}

\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A},E_{1},...,E_{n}\rangle

where
\mathcal{A}=\langle E_{1},...,E_{n}\rangle^{\perp}
and
E_{i}
denote the full triangulated subcategory generated by the object
E_{i}
. An exceptional collection is called
full
if the subcategory
\mathcal{A}
is zero.

Next, we define the notion of (right) dual exceptional collection.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\{E_{1},...,E_{n}\}
be an exceptional collection on the triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
. An exceptional collection
\{F_{1},...,F_{n}\}
of objects in
\mathcal{D}
is called
right dual
to
\{E_{1},...,E_{n}\}
if

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F_{i},E_{i}[l])=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&\ \text{if}\ l=0\\
0&\ \text{if}\ l\neq 0\end{cases}

and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F_{i},E_{j}[l])=0
for
i\neq j
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.2.
Fourier-Mukai transforms

In this subsection, we recall the tool of Fourier-Mukai transforms and kernels. The main reference is the book by Huybrechts
[
19
]
.

Let
X
be a smooth and complex projective variety. We will work with the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on
X
, denoted by
\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X)
. Throughout this article, unless we explicitly mention otherwise, all functors between derived categories will be assumed to be derived. For example, we will write
f^{*}
and
\otimes
instead of
Rf^{*}
and
\otimes^{L}
, respectively.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two smooth and complex projective varieties. A
Fourier-Mukai kernel
is an object
\mathcal{P}
of the
bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on
X\times Y
. Given
\mathcal{P}\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X\times Y))
, we may define
the associated
Fourier-Mukai transform
, which is the functor

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(Y)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\mapsto\pi_{2*}(\pi_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{P})

where
\pi_{1}
,
\pi_{2}
are natural projections from
X\times Y
to
X
,
Y
respectively.

We call
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}
the Fourier-Mukai transform with (Fourier-Mukai) kernel
\mathcal{P}
. For convenience, we will just write FM for Fourier-Mukai.

The first property of FM transforms is that they have left and right adjoints that are themselves FM transforms.

Proposition 2.9
.

[
19
, Proposition 5.9]

For
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(Y)
is the FM transform with kernel
\mathcal{P}
, define

\mathcal{P}_{L}=\mathcal{P}^{\vee}\otimes\pi^{*}_{2}\omega_{Y}[\mathrm{dim}Y],\ \mathcal{P}_{R}=\mathcal{P}^{\vee}\otimes\pi^{*}_{1}\omega_{X}[\mathrm{dim}X].

Then

\Phi_{\mathcal{P}_{L}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X),\ \Phi_{\mathcal{P}_{R}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X)

are the left and right adjoints of
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}
, respectively.

The second property is that the composition of FM transforms is also a FM transform.

Proposition 2.10
.

[
19
, Proposition 5.10]

Let
X,Y,Z
be smooth projective varieties over
\mathbb{C}
. Consider objects
\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X\times Y))
and
\mathcal{Q}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(Y\times Z))
. They define FM transforms
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(Y)
,
\Phi_{\mathcal{Q}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(Z)
. We use
\ast
to denote the operation for convolution, i.e.

\mathcal{Q}\ast\mathcal{P}:=\pi_{13*}(\pi_{12}^{*}(\mathcal{P})\otimes\pi_{23}^{*}(\mathcal{Q})).

Then for
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{Q}\ast\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(X\times Z)
, we have
\Phi_{\mathcal{Q}}\circ\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}\cong\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}
.

Remark 2.11
.

Moreover by
[
19
, Remark 5.11]
, we have
(\mathcal{Q}\ast\mathcal{P})_{L}\cong(\mathcal{P})_{L}\ast(\mathcal{Q})_{L}
and
(\mathcal{Q}\ast\mathcal{P})_{R}\cong(\mathcal{P})_{R}\ast(\mathcal{Q})_{R}
.

Finally, we state a result by Kuznetsov, which relates the notion of FM transforms and
\sf{SOD}
s. Since all the varieties we work with are smooth, we decide to state the following result by Kuznetsov in a weak version.

Proposition 2.12
.

[
26
, Theorem 7.1, Theorem 7.3]

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and
\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{m}\rangle
be a
\sf{SOD}
. Let
p_{i}:\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X)
be the projection to the
i
th component. Then there exists
K_{i}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(X\times X))
such that
p_{i}\cong\Phi_{K_{i}}
, which is the FM transform with kernel
K_{i}
.

Now we assume

[

\boldsymbol{T}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathcal{D}}\xrightarrow{\eta}\boldsymbol{S}\xrightarrow{\delta}\boldsymbol{T}[1].

Sometimes we call (

)

. Also, we call

and

The notion of (co)unital idempotents in Definiton

It has been shown (Theorem 4.5 in

We list the following results about complementary idempotents.

[

Let

3.
Affine 0-Heck algebra and its action

In this section, we recall the definition of affine 0-Hecke algebra and its action on the Grothendieck group of full flag variety.

Fix
N\geq 2
be a positive integer. We begin with the definition of affine 0-Hecke algebra.

Definition 3.1
.

The affine 0-Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{N}(0)
is defined to be the unital associative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra generated by the elements
T_{1},...,T_{N-1}
and the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},...,X_{N}^{\pm 1}]
subject to the following relations

T_{i}^{2}=T_{i},

(H01)

T_{i}T_{i+1}T_{i}=T_{i+1}T_{i}T_{i+1},\ T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i},\ \text{if}\ |i-j|\geq 2,

(H02)

T_{i}X_{j}=X_{j}T_{i}\ \text{if}\ j\neq i,i+1,

(H03)

X_{i+1}T_{i}=T_{i}X_{i}+X_{i+1},

(H04)

X_{i}T_{i}=T_{i}X_{i+1}-X_{i+1}.

(H05)

Remark 3.2
.

The affine 0-Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{N}(0)
is a specialization of the affine Hecke algebra at
q=0
. Moreover, the relations (
H04
) and (
H05
) are usually called the Bernstein-Lusztig relations.

Next, we explain there is an action of
\mathcal{H}_{N}(0)
on the Grothendieck group of the full flag variety. Let
G=\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
,
B\subset G
be the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices, and
T\subset G
be the maximal torus of diagonal matrices. Then the full flag variety, defined to be quotient
G/B
, can be identified with the following space

G/B=\{0\subset V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subset...\subset V_{N}=\mathbb{C}^{N}\ |\ \dim V_{k}=k\ \text{for}\ \text{all}\ k\}.

(3.1)

The Weyl group
W
is isomorphic to the symmetric group, denoted by
S_{N}
. Let
\Sigma=\{\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{N-1}\}
to be the set of simple roots with the associated simple reflections
\{s_{1},...,s_{N-1}\}
that generate the Weyl group
W=S_{N}
. Then for each
1\leq i\leq N-1
, there is the minimal parabolic subgroup
P_{i}=B\cup Bs_{i}B
. Similarly, the quotient
G/P_{i}
, which is called a partial flag variety, can be identified with the following space

G/P_{i}=\{0\subset V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subset...\subset V_{i-1}\subset V_{i+1}\subset...\subset V_{N}=\mathbb{C}^{N}\ |\ \dim V_{k}=k\ \text{for}\ k\neq i\}.

(3.2)

There are natural projections
\pi_{i}:G/B\rightarrow G/P_{i}
, which is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration, for
1\leq i\leq N-1
.
They induce natural pullback
\pi^{*}_{i}:K(G/P_{i})\rightarrow K(G/B)
and pushforward
\pi_{i*}:K(G/B)\rightarrow K(G/P_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq N-1
.

Then we obtain the following operators on
K(G/B)
, which are called the Demazure operates, and the construction is originally due to Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand
[
7
]
in cohomology.

\delta_{i}=\pi^{*}_{i}\pi_{i*}:K(G/B)\rightarrow K(G/B).

Furthermore, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.3

(
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
)
.

Those operators satisfy the following

\displaystyle\delta^{2}_{i}=\delta_{i},

\displaystyle\delta_{i}\delta_{i+1}\delta_{i}=\delta_{i+1}\delta_{i}\delta_{i+1}.

More precisely,
\{\delta_{i}\}
generate the 0-Hecke algebra and acting on
K(G/B)
.

We denote
\mathcal{V}_{i}
to be the tautological bundle of rank
i
on
G/B
for
1\leq i\leq N
, then we have the natural line bundles
\mathscr{L}_{i}=\mathcal{V}_{i}/\mathcal{V}_{i-1}
on
G/B
for
1\leq i\leq N
. Let
a_{i}=[\mathscr{L}_{i}]\in K(G/B)
be the class in the Grothendieck group. Then
K(G/B)
has the following presentation, which is the so-called Borel presentation

K(G/B)\cong\mathbb{C}[a_{1}^{\pm},...,a_{N}^{\pm}]/\left\langle e_{i}-{N\choose i}\right\rangle

(3.3)

where
\langle e_{i}-{N\choose i}\rangle
is the ideal generated by
\{e_{i}-{N\choose i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, and
e_{i}
is the
i
-th symmetric polynomial in
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{N}
.

Under the presentation (
3.3
),
\delta_{i}
has the following explicit description

\delta_{i}=\frac{a_{i+1}-a_{i}s_{i}}{a_{i+1}-a_{i}}

(3.4)

where
s_{i}
is the simple reflection that permute
a_{i}
and
a_{i+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq N-1
.

Finally, we define the operators
x_{j}:K(G/B)\rightarrow K(G/B)
to be multiplication by
a_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq N
. Then with the help of the expression (
3.4
), we can verify the following relations.

\displaystyle a_{i}a_{j}=a_{j}a_{i}\ \text{for all}\ i,j,

\displaystyle\delta_{i}a_{j}=a_{j}\delta_{i}\ \text{if}\ j\neq i,i+1,

\displaystyle a_{i+1}\delta_{i}=\delta_{i}a_{i}+a_{i+1},

\displaystyle a_{i}\delta_{i}=\delta_{i}a_{i+1}-a_{i+1}.

Thus we conclude that

Corollary 3.4
.

There is a action of
\mathcal{H}_{N}(0)
on
K(G/B)
where the generators
T_{i}
and
X_{j}
acting by
\delta_{i}
and
a_{j}
, respectively.

4.
Shifted 0-affine algebras and its categorical action

In this section, we recall the definitions of the shifted 0-affine algebra and its categorical action on the derived category of partial flag varieties.

Before we go to the detailed setup and the definition of the shifted 0-affine algebra, we should give the readers some background and motivation for it. The main motivation comes from the study of categorical action or categorification of the quantum group
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
for
\mathfrak{g}
a semisimple or Kac-Moody Lie algebra.

Based on the work
[
8
]
, Beilinson-Lusztig-MacPherson give a geometric model for
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
(more generally
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
). This can be used to construct categorical
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-action.

The weight space
V_{\lambda}
is replaced by the weight category
\mathcal{C}(\lambda)=\mathcal{D}^{b}Con(\mathrm{Gr}(k,N))
, which is the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves on the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)
with
\lambda=N-2k
. The generators
e
,
f
act on them by using the following correspondence diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{Fl}(k-1,k)=\{0\overset{k-1}{\subset}V^{\prime}\overset{1}{\subset}V\overset{N-k}{\subset}\mathbb{C}^{N}\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{p_{2}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Gr}(k-1,N)}

(4.1)

where
\mathrm{Fl}(k-1,k)
is the 3-step partial flag variety and
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
are the natural projections. Then we define

{\sf{E}}:=p_{2*}p^{*}_{1}
and a similar functor
{\sf{F}}
in the opposite direction that can be viewed as a lift of
e
and
f
, respectively. The functors
{\sf{E}}
,
{\sf{F}}
satisfy the defining relations of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, i.e., we have

{\sf{E}\sf{F}}|_{\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}\cong{\sf{F}\sf{E}}|_{\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}\bigoplus\text{Id}^{\oplus\lambda}_{\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}\ \text{if}\ \lambda\geq 0,

similarly for
\lambda\leq 0
.

Remark 4.1
.

In fact, the above action can be upgraded to categorical
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-action if we add the extra structure of homological shift in the derived category.

The shifted 0-affine algebra arises naturally when we replace constructible sheaves with coherent sheaves, i.e., we consider
\mathcal{D}^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,N))
. In the rest of this article, we will omit
\mathrm{Coh}
and use
\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
to denote the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on a variety
X
.

In this setting, there are more functors. Let
\mathcal{V},\ \mathcal{V}^{\prime}
to be the tautological bundles on
\mathrm{Fl}(k-1,k)
of rank
k
,
k-1
respectively. Then there is a natural line bundle
\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
on
\mathrm{Fl}(k-1,k)
. Using the same correspondence (
4.1
), instead of just pulling back and pushing forward directly, we have an extra twist by the line bundles
(\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime})^{r}
where
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. So we have the functors

{\sf{E}}_{r}:=p_{2*}(p_{1}^{*}\otimes(\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime})^{r}):\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,N))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k-1,N))

and similarly
{\sf{F}}_{r}
where
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Then the shifted 0-affine algebra is a
L\mathfrak{sl}_{2}:=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]
-like algebra acting on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
, where
e\otimes t^{r}
and
f\otimes t^{s}
acting via the functors
{\sf{E}}_{r}
,
{\sf{F}}_{s}
respectively for
r,\ s\in\mathbb{Z}
. In
[
18
]
, the author is the first one to study this action in detail.

4.1.
Shifted
q=0
affine algebras

In this section, we define the shifted 0-affine algebras. The presentation we use here is by finite numbers of generators and defining relations. In
[
18
]
, we also give a conjectural presentation defined by generating series and conjecture that the two presentations are equivalent, see conjecture A.2. in
loc. cit.
.

Similarly to the dot version
\dot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
that introduced in
[
8
]
, the shifted 0-affine algebras we introduce below is also an idempotent form. This means that we replace the identity with the direct sum of a system of projectors, one for each element of the weight lattices. They are orthogonal idempotents for approximating the unit element. We refer to part IV in
[
29
]
for details of such modification.

Throughout the rest of this article, we fix a positive integer
N\geq 2
. Let

C(n,N):=\{\underline{k}=(k_{1},...,k_{n})\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}\ |\ k_{1}+...+k_{n}=N\}.

We regard each
\underline{k}
as a weight for
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
via the identification of the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
with the quotient
\mathbb{Z}^{n}/(1,1,...,1)
. We choose the simple root
\alpha_{i}
to be
(0...0,-1,1,0...0)
where the
-1
is in the
i
th position for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Finally, we denote
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
to be the standard pairing. Then we introduce the shifted 0-affine algebra for
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
, which is defined by using finite generators and relations.

Definition 4.2
.

We consider formal symbols of the form
1_{\lambda}x1_{\mu}
(
\lambda,\mu\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}
) and abbreviating
(1_{\lambda_{1}}x_{1}1_{\mu_{1}})...(1_{\lambda_{i}}x_{i}1_{\mu_{i}})=x_{1}...x_{i}1_{\mu_{i}}
if the product is nonzero. Then we define the
shifted 0-affine algebra
, denote by
\mathcal{U}=\dot{\mathrm{\mathbb{U}}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
, to be the associative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra generated by

\bigcup_{\underline{k}\in C(n,N)}\{1_{\underline{k}},1_{\underline{k}+\alpha_{i}}e_{i,r}1_{\underline{k}},\ 1_{\underline{k}-\alpha_{i}}f_{i,s}1_{\underline{k}},\ 1_{\underline{k}}(\psi^{+}_{i})^{\pm 1}1_{\underline{k}},\ 1_{\underline{k}}(\psi^{-}_{i})^{\pm 1}1_{\underline{k}},\ 1_{\underline{k}}h_{i,\pm 1}1_{\underline{k}}\}_{1\leq i\leq n-1}^{-k_{i}-1\leq r\leq 0,\ 0\leq s\leq k_{i+1}+1}

with the following relations

1_{\underline{k}}1_{\underline{l}}=\delta_{\underline{k},\underline{l}}1_{\underline{k}},

(U01)

\{(\psi^{+}_{i})^{\pm 1}1_{\underline{k}},(\psi^{-}_{i})^{\pm 1}1_{\underline{k}},h_{i,\pm 1}1_{\underline{k}}\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n-1,\underline{k}\in C(n,N)\}\ \mathrm{pairwise\ commute},

(U02)

(\psi^{+}_{i})^{\pm 1}\cdot(\psi^{+}_{i})^{\mp 1}1_{\underline{k}}=1_{\underline{k}}=(\psi^{-}_{i})^{\pm 1}\cdot(\psi^{-}_{i})^{\mp 1}1_{\underline{k}},

(U03)

\displaystyle e_{i,r}e_{j,s}1_{\underline{k}}=\begin{cases}-e_{i,s+1}e_{i,r-1}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ j=i\\
e_{i+1,s}e_{i,r}1_{\underline{k}}-e_{i+1,s-1}e_{i,r+1}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ j=i+1\\
e_{i,r+1}e_{i-1,s-1}1_{\underline{k}}-e_{i-1,s-1}e_{i,r+1}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ j=i-1\\
e_{j,s}e_{i,r}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ |i-j|\geq 2\end{cases},

(U04)

\displaystyle f_{i,r}f_{j,s}1_{\underline{k}}=\begin{cases}-f_{i,s-1}f_{i,r+1}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ j=i\\
f_{i,r-1}f_{i+1,s+1}1_{\underline{k}}-f_{i+1,s+1}f_{i,r-1}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ j=i+1\\
f_{i-1,s}f_{i,r}1_{\underline{k}}-f_{i-1,s+1}f_{i,r-1}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ j=i-1\\
f_{j,s}f_{i,r}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ |i-j|\geq 2\end{cases},

(U05)

\displaystyle\psi^{+}_{i}e_{j,r}1_{\underline{k}}=\begin{cases}-e_{i,r+1}\psi^{+}_{i}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ j=i\\
-e_{i+1,r-1}\psi^{+}_{i}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ j=i+1\\
e_{i-1,r}\psi^{+}_{i}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ j=i-1\\
e_{j,r}\psi^{+}_{i}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ |i-j|\geq 2\\
\end{cases},

\displaystyle\psi^{-}_{i}e_{j,r}1_{\underline{k}}=\begin{cases}-e_{i,r+1}\psi^{-}_{i}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ j=i\\
e_{i+1,r}\psi^{-}_{i}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ j=i+1\\
-e_{i-1,r-1}\psi^{-}_{i}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ j=i-1\\
e_{j,r}\psi^{-}_{i}1_{\underline{k}}&\text{if}\ |i-j|\geq 2\\
\end{cases},

5.
Main result: Categorical idempotents

-action gives two families of pair of complementary idempotents for each weight category. The main idea is to generalize the interpretation of the categorified Demazure operators

Remark 5.1
.

Note that even though we have the isomorphism (

Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})

For a Young diagram

[
17

Definition 6.1

Let

be a positive integer and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})
be a sequence of non-increasing positive integers. The
Schur functor

\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
associated to
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is defined as a functor

\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}:\text{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\text{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}

such that for any vector space
V
,
\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}V
coincides with the image of the Young symmetrizer
c_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
in the space of tensors of
V
of rank
n
: i.e.,
\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}V=\text{Im}(c_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|_{V^{\otimes n}})
.

First, for the case where
n=2
, they are just the Grassmannians

\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)=\{0\subset V\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}\ |\ \mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}V=k\}

of
k
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.

Denote
\mathcal{V}
to be the tautological rank
k
bundle on
\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)
and
\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V}
to be the tautological rank
N-k
quotient bundle. For non-negative integers
a,\ b\geq 0
, we denote by
P(a,b)
the set of Young diagrams
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
such that
\lambda_{1}\leq a
and

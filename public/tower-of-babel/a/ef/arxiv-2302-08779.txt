On the convergence result of the gradient-push algorithm on directed graphs with constant stepsize
Woocheol Choi, Doheon Kim, Seok-Bae Yun

Abstract
. Distributed optimization has recieved a lot of interest due to its wide applications in various fields. It consists of multiple agents that connected by a graph and optimize a total cost in a collaborative way. Often in the applications, the graph of the agents is given by a directed graph. The gradient-push algorithm is a fundamental method for distributed optimization for which the agents are connected by a directed graph. Despite of its wide usage in the literatures, its convergence property has not been established well for the important case that the stepsize is constant and the domain is the entire space. This work proves that the gradient-push algorithm with stepsize α > 0 \alpha>0 converges exponentially fast to an O ⁡ ( α ) O(\alpha) -neighborhood of the optimizer if the stepsize α \alpha is less than a specific value. For the result, we assume that each cost is smooth and the total cost is strongly convex. Numerical experiments are provided to support the theoretical convergence result. We also present a numerical test showing that the gradient-push algorithm may approach a small neighborhood of the minimizer faster than the Push-DIGing algorithm which is a variant of the gradient-push algorithm involving the communication of the gradient informations of the agents.

Before discussing the previous results and our results, we recall that the gradient-push algorithm
[
21
]
is described as follows: Given inital data
x_{i}(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
y_{i}(0)=1
for each
1\leq i\leq n
and
t\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, we update

\begin{split}w_{i}(t+1)&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}{x_{j}(t),}\\
y_{i}(t+1)&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}{y_{j}(t),}\\
z_{i}(t+1)&={\frac{w_{i}(t+1)}{y_{i}(t+1)},}\\
x_{i}(t+1)&=w_{i}(t+1)-\alpha(t)\nabla f_{i}(z_{i}(t+1)),\end{split}

(1.2)

where
W_{ij}
are communication weight such that the matrix
W=\{W_{ij}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
is column stochastic and
\alpha(t)>0
is the stepsize. We refer to Section
2
for the full detail.

In the original work
[
23
]
, Nedic and Olshevsky established the convergence result for convex cost functions when the stepsize
\alpha(t)
is given as
\alpha(t)=1/\sqrt{t}
or satisfies the condition
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\alpha(t)=\infty
and
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\alpha(t)^{2}<\infty
. In
[
22
]
, the convergence result was obtained when the cost function is strongly convex, and
\alpha(t)=c/t
for some
c>0
. Recently, the work
[
35
]
established a convergence result for non-convex cost functions. The results are summarized in Table
1
. It is worth mentioning that the convergence results in the previous works
[
21
,
23
,
35
]
assumed that the gradients of the cost functions are uniformly bounded.

We mention that if
W
is given by a doubly-stochastic matrix, then
y_{i}(t)=1
for all
t\geq 0

1\leq i\leq n
. The algorithm (
1.2

[

x_{i}(t+1)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}x_{j}(t)-\alpha(t)\nabla f_{i}(x_{i}(t)).

(1.3)

The work

studied the convergence property of (

Having mentioned the above results, one may ask the following questions on the gradient-push algorithm (
1.2

Question 1
: Can we show that the sequence of (
1.2

Question 2
: Does the gradient-push algorithm with constant stepsize
\alpha>0
converge to an

-neighborhood of the optimizer of (

The aim of this paper is to address these questions. Precsisely, we will show that the sequence of (

\alpha(t)\equiv\alpha>0
is less than a specific value when each cost function is

-neighborhood of the optimizer
x_{*}
provided that
\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}

determined the properties of the cost functions and the communication network.

Given the above result, we recall that variants of the gradient-push algorithm that incorporate the exchange of gradient information with constant step sizes have been shown to converge linearly to the minimizer of (

When comparing the gradient-push algorithm with its variant, the Push-DIging algorithm, the advantage of the Push-DIging algorithm is that it exhibits precise linear convergence. On the other side, the gradient-push algorithm may approach a small neighborhood of the minimizer faster than the Push-DIGing algorithm when we compare the algorithms with stepsizes maximizing the performance (refer to the numerical test in Section

[

In order to achieve the properties of the gradient-push algorithm, we consider two measures:

•

A distance between the average
\bar{w}(t)
of
\{w_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}

A measurement of

We will derive two sequential estimates of the above measures. By analyzing the sequential estimates, we will achieve the boundedness and the convergence properties of the gradient-push algorithm (refer to Sections

For example, we believe the it can be applied for the decentralized TD learning on directed graph

In addition, the framework can be used for obtaining new sharp estimates for the variants containing the online gradient-push algorithm

2.
Gradient-push algorithm and related basic results

In this section, we review the gradient-push algorithm and some preliminary results for obtaining the convergence estimates of the gradient-push algorithm.

We consider a directed graph
G=(V,E)
with the vertex set
V=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
and the edge set
E\subset V\times V
, where the communication between agents are performed. Here
(i,j)\in E
means that there is a directed edge from agent
i
to
j
. We define the set of in-neighbors of agent
i
by

N_{i}^{in}=\{j\in V~\mid~(j,i)\in E\},

and the set of out-neighbors of agent
i
by

N_{i}^{out}=\{j\in V~\mid~(i,j)\in E\}.

Throughout this paper, we assume that
G
satisfies the following two assumptions:

•

(G1) Every vertex of
G
has a self-loop, i.e.,

(i,i)\in E\quad(\Leftrightarrow i\in N_{i}^{in}~\Leftrightarrow~i\in N_{i}^{out}),\quad\forall~i\in V.

•

(G2)
G
is strongly connected, i.e.,
G
contains a directed path from
i
to
j
for every
(i,j)\in V\times V
.

We set
d_{j}=|N_{j}^{out}|
and

W_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\frac{1}{d_{j}}}&\textrm{if}~j\in N_{i}^{in}\quad{(\mbox{or equivalently, }i\in N_{j}^{out}),}\\
0&\textrm{if}~j\notin N_{i}^{in}.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

Let us denote by
w(t)=\mathrm{col}(w_{1}(t),\cdots,w_{n}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{nd}
the column vector obtained by stacking
w_{1}(t),\dots,w_{n}(t)
, and similarly for
x(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{nd}
,
y(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
z(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{nd}
.
Then we may rewrite the gradient-push algorithm (
1.2
) as

\begin{split}w(t+1)&=(W\otimes I_{d}){x(t),}\\
y(t+1)&=W{y(t),}\\
z_{i}(t+1)&=\frac{w_{i}(t+1)}{y_{i}(t+1)},\quad 1\leq i\leq n,\\
x(t+1)&=w(t+1)-\alpha\nabla F(z(t+1)),\end{split}

(2.2)

where we define
\nabla F(x):=\mathrm{col}(\nabla f_{1}(x_{1}),\cdots,\nabla f_{n}(x_{n}))
for any
x=\mathrm{col}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,
I_{d}
is the
d\times d
identity matrix, and
\otimes
is the Kronecker product.
We assume that
y_{i}(0)=1
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Note that the positivity of
y(t)
is ensured by the following inductive argument:

y_{i}(t+1)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}y_{j}(t)\geq W_{ii}y_{i}(t)=\frac{y_{i}(t)}{d_{i}}>0.

(2.3)

In addition, the matrix
W
is column stochastic, by the following relation:

\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{ij}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1_{N_{i}^{in}}(j)}{d_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1_{N_{j}^{out}}(i)}{|N_{j}^{out}|}=1,\quad j=1,\dots,n.

Note that column stochasticity of
W
is equivalent to
1_{n}^{\top}W=1_{n}^{\top}
. Also note that by (
2.1
),
W_{ij}>0
if and only if
(j,i)\in E
. By (G2),
W
is an irreducible matrix, so we can apply the Perron-Frobenius theorem to deduce that
W
has a right eigenvector
\pi=(\pi_{1},\cdots,\pi_{n})^{\top}
associated with the eigenvalue 1, of which all entries are positive and satisfy

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\pi_{j}=1.

(2.4)

Moreover, by (G1),
G
is aperiodic. Since
G
is both strongly connected and aperiodic,
W
is primitive, and it is known that

W^{\infty}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}W^{k}=\pi 1_{n}^{\top}

(2.5)

with convergence speed geometrically fast
[
31
]
.
We denote the Euclidean norm by
\|\cdot\|
and set the matrix norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|A\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
for a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
by

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|A\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|Ax\|}{\|x\|}.

Now we define a weighted inner product as
\langle x,y\rangle_{\pi}=x^{\top}\textrm{diag}(\pi)^{-1}y
and its induced norm
\|x\|_{\pi}=\|\textrm{diag}(\sqrt{\pi})^{-1}x\|
. We also set
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|A\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\pi}
as the matrix norm induced by
\|\cdot\|_{\pi}
for
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, i.e.,

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|A\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\pi}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|Ax\|_{\pi}}{\|x\|_{\pi}}.

It is easy to check that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|A\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\pi}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\textrm{diag}(\sqrt{\pi})^{-1}A\textrm{diag}(\sqrt{\pi})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
. Also we have

\|\cdot\|_{\pi}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi_{a}}}\|\cdot\|\quad\textrm{and}\quad\|\cdot\|\leq\sqrt{\pi_{b}}\|\cdot\|_{\pi},

where
\pi_{a}
and
\pi_{b}
denote the minimum and maximum values of
\pi
, respectively. We state a basic lemma for
W
.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

|||W|||_{\pi}=|||W^{\infty}|||_{\pi}=1.

Proof.

We give a proof of this lemma for the readers’ convenience. Since
\pi
is a right eigenvector of
W
, we have
\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}\pi_{j}=w_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
Using this and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we deduce the following inequality

\begin{split}\|Wx\|_{\pi}^{2}&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}\sqrt{\pi_{j}}\frac{x_{j}}{\sqrt{\pi}_{j}}\Big)^{2}\pi_{i}^{-1}\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}\pi_{j}\Big)\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{\pi_{j}}\pi_{i}^{-1}\Big)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{\pi_{j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{\pi_{j}}=\|x\|_{\pi}^{2},\end{split}

which proves
|||W|||_{\pi}\leq 1
. Also this directly implies that
|||W^{\infty}|||_{\pi}\leq 1
. Combining these inequalities with the fact

W\pi=\pi
yields that

|||W|||_{\pi}=|||W^{\infty}|||_{\pi}=1.

This finishes the proof.
∎

We recall the following result from
[
42
, Lemma 1]
:

Lemma 2.2

(
[
42
]
)
.

Under assumptions
(G1)
and
(G2)
, we have the estimate
\sigma_{W}:=|||W-W^{\infty}|||_{\pi}<1
.

Using this lemma, one may derive the following convergence estimate of
y(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
towards
n\pi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\|y(t)-n\pi\|_{\pi}=\left\|(W-W^{\infty})^{t}(1_{n}-n\pi)\right\|_{\pi}\leq\sigma_{W}^{t}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi},}\quad\forall~t\geq 0.

(2.6)

Combining this with (
2.3
), we find that

\min_{1\leq j\leq n}\min_{t\in\mathbb{N}}y_{j}(t)>0.

(2.7)

We can similarly define a weighted inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{nd}
by

\langle x,y\rangle_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}:=x^{\top}\textrm{diag}(\pi\otimes 1_{d})^{-1}y=x^{\top}(\textrm{diag}(\pi)^{-1}\otimes I_{d})y,

and its induced norm and matrix norm can be defined analogously.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
n\times n
matrix
A
, we have

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|A\otimes I_{d}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|A\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\pi}.

Proof.

We proceed in the following way:

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|A\otimes I_{d}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\textrm{diag}(\sqrt{\pi\otimes 1_{d}})^{-1}(A\otimes I_{d})\textrm{diag}(\sqrt{\pi\otimes 1_{d}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\textrm{diag}(\sqrt{\pi})^{-1}\otimes I_{d})(A\otimes I_{d})(\textrm{diag}(\sqrt{\pi})\otimes I_{d})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|[\textrm{diag}(\sqrt{\pi})^{-1}A\textrm{diag}(\sqrt{\pi})]\otimes I_{d}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\textrm{diag}(\sqrt{\pi})^{-1}A\textrm{diag}(\sqrt{\pi})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|A\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\pi}.

In the fourth inequality, we have used that the matrix norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
is equal to the largest singular value of the matrix.

3.
Main convergence result

Throughout this paper, we assume that the cost functions satisfy the following two assumptions.

•

(F1) The gradient of each function
f_{i}
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
L_{i}>0
for all
1\leq i\leq d
, i.e.,
f_{i}
is
L_{i}
-smooth:

\|\nabla f_{i}(x)-\nabla f_{i}(y)\|\leq L_{i}\|x-y\|\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(3.1)

•

(F2) The total cost
f
is
\beta
-strongly convex with a value
\beta>0
.

Under the above assumptions, there exists a unique minimizer
w_{*}
of the cost
f
.
In order to analyze the convergence property of the algorithm (
1.2
), we consider the following two quantities:

A_{t}:=\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|\quad\textrm{and}\quad B_{t}:=\|w(t)-(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t)\|_{\pi}.

Here
A_{t}
measures the distance between the average of states
\bar{w}(t)
and the optimal point
w_{*}
. Also we note that

(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t)=(\pi 1_{n}^{T}\otimes I_{d})w(t)=n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t).

(3.2)

Therefore
B_{t}
measures how close
w(t)
is to the weighted consensus states
n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)
.
Obtaining convergence properties of
A_{t}
and
B_{t}
will give a convergence estimate of the sequence
w(t)
to
n\pi\otimes w_{*}
in view of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
t\geq 0
, we have

\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes w_{*}\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\leq\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+n\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|.

Proof.

We apply the triangle inequality to find

\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes w_{*}\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\leq\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\|n\pi\otimes(\bar{w}(t)-w_{*})\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}.

(3.3)

By (
2.4
),

\|n\pi\otimes(\bar{w}(t)-w_{*})\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}^{2}=n^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\pi_{j}\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|^{2}=n^{2}\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|^{2}.

Combining this with (
3.3
) gives

\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes w_{*}\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\leq\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+n\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|.

The proof is done.
∎

In order to state the main convergence results, we set the following values:

\delta=\max_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\frac{1}{y_{i}(t)}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\rho=\sigma_{W}(1+\alpha L\delta),

(3.4)

where
\sigma_{W}<1
by Lemma
2.2
.
We also set
\gamma=\frac{\beta L}{\beta+L}
and

\begin{split}\mathbf{D}_{1}&=\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}}+\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi_{j}}}\\
\mathbf{D}_{2}&=L\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}}\|w_{*}\|+\|\nabla F(1_{n}\otimes w_{*})\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}.\end{split}

In the following result, we establish boundedness of the sequence
\{(A_{t},B_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
under a condition on the stepsize
\alpha>0
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
b=\frac{n\gamma}{4L\delta}
. Assume that (F1) and (F2) hold, and that
\alpha>0
satisfies

\alpha\leq\frac{2}{L+\beta}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\alpha<\frac{b(1-\sigma_{W})}{L\sigma_{W}(\delta b+\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}}+\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi_{j}}})}

(3.5)

for all
t\geq 0
. Then the sequence
\{(A_{t},B_{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is uniformly bounded in time, i.e.,

\sup_{t\geq 0}A_{t}<\infty,\quad\sup_{t\geq 0}B_{t}<\infty.

More precisely, take a value
t_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(L\delta/n){\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t_{0}}}\leq\gamma/2
and define
R>0
as

R=\max\Big\{A_{t_{0}},~\frac{B_{t_{0}}}{b},~2\|w_{*}\|,~\frac{\sigma_{W}\alpha\textbf{D}_{2}}{b(1-\sigma_{W})-\alpha L\sigma_{W}(\delta b+\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}}+\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi_{j}}})}\Big\}.

Then we have

A_{t}\leq R\quad\textrm{and}\quad B_{t}\leq bR

for all
t\geq t_{0}
.

We mention that the above boundedness result may hold for a larger range of
\alpha>0
than that guaranteed by Theorem
3.2
. We refer to
[
45
,
12
]
for the discussion of this issue for the decentralized gradient method on undirected graphs.

Now we state the main convergence result for the gradient-push algorithm.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that (F1) and (F2) hold, and that
\alpha>0
satisfies
\alpha\leq\frac{2}{L+\beta}
and the sequence
\{A_{t}\}
is bounded, i.e., there exists
R>0
such that
A_{t}\leq R
for all
t\geq 0
. Set
\bar{R}=L\textbf{D}_{1}R+\textbf{D}_{2}>0
. Then for all
t\geq 0
we have

\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\leq\rho^{t}\|w(0)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(0)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\frac{\alpha\sigma_{W}\bar{R}}{1-\rho}

(3.6)

and

\begin{split}\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|&\leq(1-\gamma\alpha)^{t}\|\bar{w}(0)-w_{*}\|+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}\|w(0)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(0)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}t\Big[\max\{1-\gamma\alpha,\rho\}\Big]^{t-1}\\
&\quad+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}}(\|w_{*}\|+R)t\Big[\max\{1-\gamma\alpha,e^{-c}\}\Big]^{t-1}+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n\gamma}\frac{\sigma_{W}\bar{R}}{1-\rho}.\end{split}

The above result establishes exponentially fast convergence of
w(t)
to an
O\Big(\frac{\alpha}{1-\rho}\Big)
neighborhood of
n\pi\otimes\bar{w}_{*}
. In order to prove the above two results, we will derive two sequential inequalities of
A_{t}
and
B_{t}
in the next two sections. Based on those inequalities, we prove Theorem
3.2
in Section
6
, and then prove Theorem
3.3
in Section
8
.

4.
Weighted Consensus

In this section, we give an estimate of
\|w(t+1)-(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t+1)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}
in terms of
\|w(t)-(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}
and
\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|
. As a preliminary step, we bound a measure of the consensus for the variable
z(t)
in terms of
w(t)
in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have the following estimate

\|z(t)-1_{n}\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\leq\delta\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\Big(\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|+\|w_{*}\|\Big).

for all
t\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

Using the definition of
z(t)
in (
1.2
) and
\delta>0
from (
3.4
), we find

\begin{split}\|z(t)-1_{n}\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}&=\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi_{j}}\Big(\frac{w_{j}(t)}{y_{j}(t)}-\bar{w}(t)\Big)^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\\
&\leq\delta\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi_{j}}(w_{j}(t)-y_{j}(t)\bar{w}(t))^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\\
&=\delta\|w(t)-y(t)\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}.\end{split}

By applying the triangle inequality here, we get

\|z(t)-1_{n}\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\leq\delta\Big(\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\|(n\pi-y(t))\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\Big).

(4.1)

Here we use (
2.6
) to estimate

\begin{split}\|(n\pi-y(t))\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}^{2}&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi_{j}}\Big(n\pi_{j}-y_{j}(t)\Big)^{2}\|\bar{w}(t)\|^{2}\\
&=\|n\pi-y(t)\|_{\pi}^{2}\|\bar{w}(t)\|^{2}\\
&\leq\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}^{2}\sigma_{W}^{2t}\|\bar{w}(t)\|^{2}.\end{split}

(4.2)

Putting this in (
4.1
) yields the following estimate

\begin{split}\|z(t)-&1_{n}\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\leq\delta\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\|\bar{w}(t)\|\\
&\leq\delta\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\Big(\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|+\|w_{*}\|\Big).\end{split}

The proof is done.
∎

We are ready to establish the main estimate of this section.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that (F1) and (F2) hold, and that
\kappa(t)\geq 1
for all
t\geq 0
. Then we have

\begin{split}&\|w(t+1)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t+1)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&\leq\sigma_{W}(1+\alpha L\delta)\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\alpha L\sigma_{W}D_{1}[t]\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|+\alpha\sigma_{W}D_{2}[t],\end{split}

(4.3)

where

\begin{split}D_{1}[t]&=\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}+\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi_{j}}}\\
D_{2}[t]&=L\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\|w_{*}\|+\|\nabla F(1_{n}\otimes w_{*})\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}.\end{split}

Proof.

First we prove (
4.3
). Using the definition (
2.2
) and the relation (
3.2
), we proceed as follows:

\begin{split}&\|w(t+1)-(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t+1)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&=\|((I_{n}-W^{\infty})\otimes I_{d})w(t+1)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&=\|[((I_{n}-W^{\infty})W)\otimes I_{d}]x(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&=\|[((I_{n}-W^{\infty})W)\otimes I_{d}](w(t)-\alpha\nabla F(z(t))){\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}.}\end{split}

(4.4)

We further manipulate this identity to have

\begin{split}&\|w(t+1)-(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t+1)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&=\|[(W-W^{\infty})\otimes I_{d}](w(t)-\alpha\nabla F(z(t)))\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&=\|[(W-W^{\infty})\otimes I_{d}][((I_{n}-W^{\infty})\otimes I_{d})w(t)-\alpha\nabla F(z(t))]\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}},\end{split}

where we have used
W^{\infty}W=WW^{\infty}=(W^{\infty})^{2}=W^{\infty}
in the first and the second equalities. By the triangle inequality and Lemma
2.3
, we estimate (
4.4
) as follows:

\begin{split}&\|w(t+1)-(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t+1)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(W-W^{\infty})\otimes I_{d}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\left[\|((I_{n}-W^{\infty})\otimes I_{d})w(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\alpha\|\nabla F(z(t))\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\right]\\
&={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|W-W^{\infty}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\pi}\left[\|w(t)-(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\alpha\|\nabla F(z(t))\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\right].\end{split}

(4.5)

This together with Lemma
2.2
gives

\begin{split}&\|w(t+1)-(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t+1)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&\leq\sigma_{W}\|w(t)-(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\alpha\sigma_{W}\|\nabla F(z(t))\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}.\end{split}

(4.6)

To estimate
\|\nabla F(z(t))\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}
further, we write

\begin{split}\nabla F(z(t))&=(\nabla F(z(t))-\nabla F(1_{n}\otimes\bar{w}(t)))\\
&\qquad+(\nabla F(1_{n}\otimes\bar{w}(t))-\nabla F(1_{n}\otimes w_{*}))+\nabla F(1_{n}\otimes w_{*}).\end{split}

(4.7)

Using Lemma
4.1
we have

\begin{split}&\|z(t)-1_{n}\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&\leq\delta\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\Big(\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|+\|w_{*}\|\Big).\end{split}

Combining this with (
4.7
) yields

\begin{split}&\|\nabla F(z(t))\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&\leq L\|z(t)-1_{n}\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+L\|1_{n}\otimes\bar{w}(t)-1_{n}\otimes w_{*}\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\|\nabla F(1_{n}\otimes w_{*})\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&\leq L\delta\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+L\Big(\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}+\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi_{j}}}\Big)\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|\\
&\qquad+\|\nabla F(1_{n}\otimes w_{*})\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+L\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\|w_{*}\|.\end{split}

Putting this estimate in (
4.6
) gives the following estimate

\begin{split}&\|w(t+1)-(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t+1)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&\leq\sigma_{W}(1+\alpha L\delta)\|(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t)-w(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\\
&\quad+\alpha L\sigma_{W}\Big(\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}+\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi_{j}}}\Big)\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|\\
&\quad+\alpha\sigma_{W}\Big(L\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\|w_{*}\|+\|\nabla F(1_{n}\otimes w_{*})\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}\Big).\end{split}

The proof is done.
∎

5.
Estimates on the distance between the average and the optimizer

In this section, we find an estimate of
\|\bar{w}(t+1)-w_{*}\|
in terms of
\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|
and
\|w(t)-(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}
. We begin by recalling a classical result in the following lemma (see e.g.
[
6
]
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
f
is
\beta
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth. Then

\langle\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y),~x-y\rangle\geq\frac{L\beta}{L+\beta}\|x-y\|^{2}+\frac{1}{L+\beta}\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|^{2}

holds for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Now we state the main result of this section.

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that (F1) and (F2) hold, and
f
is
\beta
-strongly convex, and that
\kappa(t)\geq 1
for all
t\geq 0
. Let
\gamma=\frac{\beta L}{L+\beta}\in(0,1)
. Then, for
\alpha\in\Big(0,\frac{2}{L+\beta}\Big]
, we have the following estimates

\begin{split}\|\bar{w}(t+1)-w_{*}\|&\leq\Big(1-\gamma\alpha+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\Big)\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|\\
&\quad+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}\|(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t)-w(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\|w_{*}\|.\end{split}

(5.1)

where
w_{*}
is the minimizer of the problem (
1.1
).

Proof.

From (
2.2
) we find

\begin{split}\bar{w}(t+1)&={\frac{1}{n}(1_{n}^{\top}\otimes I_{d})w(t+1)=\frac{1}{n}(1_{n}^{\top}\otimes I_{d})(W\otimes I_{d})x(t)=\frac{1}{n}(1_{n}^{\top}\otimes I_{d})x(t)}\\
&=\frac{1}{n}(1_{n}^{\top}\otimes I_{d})(w(t)-\alpha\nabla F(z(t)))\\
&=\bar{w}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(z_{i}(t))\\
&=\bar{w}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}\left(\frac{w_{i}(t)}{y_{i}(t)}\right).\end{split}

Let us write the above term as

\begin{split}\bar{w}(t+1)-w_{*}&=\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}-\frac{\alpha}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\bar{w}(t))+\frac{\alpha}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\nabla f_{i}(\bar{w}(t))-\nabla f_{i}\left(\frac{w_{i}(t)}{y_{i}(t)}\right)\right).\end{split}

(5.2)

Using Lemma
5.1
, we have

\begin{split}&\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}-\alpha\nabla f(\bar{w}(t))\|^{2}\\
&=\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|^{2}-2\alpha\langle\bar{w}(t)-w_{*},~\nabla f(\bar{w}(t))\rangle+\alpha^{2}\|\nabla f(\bar{w}(t))\|^{2}\\
&\leq\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|^{2}-\frac{2\alpha\beta L}{L+\beta}\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|^{2}-\frac{2\alpha}{L+\beta}\|\nabla f(\bar{w}(t))\|^{2}+\alpha^{2}\|\nabla f(\bar{w}(t))\|^{2}.\end{split}

Since
\alpha\leq\frac{2}{L+\beta}
, the above inequality gives

\begin{split}\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}-\alpha\nabla f(\bar{w}(t))\|^{2}&\leq\Big(1-\frac{2\alpha\beta L}{L+\beta}\Big)\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|^{2}\\
&\leq\Big(1-\frac{\alpha\beta L}{L+\beta}\Big)^{2}\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|^{2}.\end{split}

Hence we have

\left\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}-\frac{\alpha}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\bar{w}(t))\right\|\leq(1-\gamma\alpha)\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|,

(5.3)

where
\gamma=\frac{\beta L}{L+\beta}\in(0,1)
. In order to esimate the right-most term of (
5.2
), we use (
3.1
) to have

\left\|\frac{\alpha}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\nabla f_{i}(\bar{w}(t))-\nabla f_{i}\left(\frac{w_{i}(t)}{y_{i}(t)}\right)\right)\right\|\leq\frac{\alpha L}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\bar{w}(t)-\frac{w_{i}(t)}{y_{i}(t)}\right\|,

(5.4)

whose right-hand side is estimated as

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\|\bar{w}(t)-\frac{w_{i}(t)}{y_{i}(t)}\Big\|&\leq\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi_{i}}\Big\|\bar{w}(t)-\frac{w_{i}(t)}{y_{i}(t)}\Big\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\\
&=\|1_{n}\otimes\bar{w}(t)-z(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}.\end{split}

Gathering the estimates (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) in (
5.2
) we find

\|\bar{w}(t+1)-w_{*}\|\leq(1-\gamma\alpha)\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|+\frac{\alpha L}{n}\|1_{n}\otimes\bar{w}(t)-z(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}.

Combining this with Lemma
4.1
gives the following estimate

\begin{split}\|\bar{w}(t+1)-w_{*}\|&\leq\Big(1-\gamma\alpha+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\Big)\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|\\
&\quad+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}\|(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t)-w(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\|w_{*}\|.\end{split}

The proof is done.
∎

6.
Uniform boundedness of the sequence

Now we shall use the recursive estimates of the previous sections to establish the uniform boundedness property of the sequence
\{w_{k}(t)\}
. For the proof, we recall the estimates of the sequences
A_{t}=\|\bar{w}(t)-w_{*}\|
and
B_{t}=\|(W^{\infty}\otimes I_{d})w(t)-w(t)\|_{\pi\otimes 1_{d}}
given by Propositions
4.2
and
5.2
:

A_{t+1}\leq(1-\gamma\alpha+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}})A_{t}+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}B_{t}+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}{\|w_{*}\|,}

(6.1)

and

\begin{split}B_{t+1}&\leq\sigma_{W}(1+\alpha L\delta)B_{t}+\sigma_{W}(\alpha L)\textbf{D}_{1}A_{t}+\alpha\sigma_{W}\textbf{D}_{2},\end{split}

(6.2)

where
\textbf{D}_{1}=D_{1}[0]
and
\textbf{D}_{2}=D_{2}[0]
.
Now we establish the uniform boundedness result of the sequence
\{(A_{t},B_{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Take a value
t_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for
t\geq t_{0}
we have

(L\delta/n){\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\leq\gamma/2
.
Choose
R>0
as

R=\max\Big\{A_{t_{0}},~\frac{B_{t_{0}}}{b},~2\|w_{*}\|,~\frac{\sigma_{W}\alpha\textbf{D}_{2}}{b(1-\sigma_{W})-\alpha L\sigma_{W}(\delta b+\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}}+\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi_{j}}})}\Big\}.

By an inductive argument, we will show that

A_{t}\leq R\quad\textrm{and}\quad B_{t}\leq bR

(6.3)

for all
t\geq t_{0}
.
Assume that
A_{t}\leq R
and
B_{t}\leq bR
for some
t\geq t_{0}
. Then we use (
6.1
) to deduce

\begin{split}A_{t+1}&\leq(1-\gamma\alpha/2)R+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}bR+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}\|w_{*}\|\\
&=\Big(1-\Big(\gamma/2-(L\delta/n)b\Big)\alpha\Big)R+\frac{\gamma\alpha}{2}\cdot\frac{R}{2}\\
&=R.\end{split}

Next we estimate
B_{t+1}
using (
6.2
) as

\begin{split}B_{t+1}&\leq\sigma_{W}(1+\alpha L\delta)bR+\sigma_{W}\alpha L\textbf{D}_{1}R+\sigma_{W}\alpha\textbf{D}_{2}\\
&\leq\sigma_{W}(1+\alpha L\delta)bR+\sigma_{W}\alpha L\Big(\delta{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}}+\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi_{j}}}\Big)R+\sigma_{W}\alpha\textbf{D}_{2}\\
&\leq bR.\end{split}

The proof is done.
∎

7.
Convergence estimates

In this section, we prove the main convergence result of the gradient-push algorithm stated in Theorem
3.3
. For this aim, we prepare the following two basic lemmas to analyze further the sequential estimates (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) of
A_{t}
and
B_{t}
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume that a sequence
\{Y_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{R}
satisfies

Y_{t+1}\leq(1-\alpha)Y_{t}+C\quad\forall t\geq 0

(7.1)

for some
C>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then we have

Y_{t}\leq(1-\alpha)^{t}Y_{0}+\frac{C}{\alpha}.

Proof.

Using (
7.1
) iteratively, we have

\begin{split}Y_{t}&\leq(1-\alpha)^{t}Y_{0}+C\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}(1-\alpha)^{k}.\\
&\leq(1-\alpha)^{t}Y_{0}+\frac{C}{\alpha},\end{split}

which proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Assume that a sequence
\{Y_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{R}
satisfies

Y_{t+1}\leq(1-\alpha)Y_{t}+C\rho^{t}\quad\forall t\geq 0

(7.2)

for some
C>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\rho\in(0,1)
.
Then we have

Y_{t}\leq(1-\alpha)^{t}Y_{0}+Ct\left(\max\{1-\alpha,\rho\}\right)^{t-1}.

Proof.

Making use of (
7.2
) recursively, we find

\displaystyle Y_{t}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)^{t}Y_{0}+C\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}(1-\alpha)^{k}\rho^{t-1-k}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)^{t}Y_{0}+C\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\left(\max\{1-\alpha,\rho\}\right)^{t-1}

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha)^{t}Y_{0}+Ct\left(\max\{1-\alpha,\rho\}\right)^{t-1}.

The proof is done.
∎

Now we are ready to establish the main convergence result.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Let
R:=\sup_{t\geq 0}A_{t}
. By (
6.2
), we get

\begin{split}B_{t+1}&\leq\sigma_{W}(1+\alpha L)B_{t}+\sigma_{W}(\alpha L)\textbf{D}_{1}R+\alpha\sigma_{W}\textbf{D}_{2}\\
&\leq\rho B_{t}+\alpha\sigma_{W}\bar{R}\quad\textrm{for all}~t\geq 0,\end{split}

where we have let
\rho=\sigma_{W}(1+\alpha L)
and
\bar{R}=L\textbf{D}_{1}R+\textbf{D}_{2}
. Using this we obtain

\begin{split}B_{t}&\leq\rho^{t}B_{0}+\Big(\rho^{t-1}+\rho^{t-2}+\cdots+1\Big)\alpha\sigma_{W}\bar{R}\\
&\leq\rho^{t}B_{0}+\frac{\alpha\sigma_{W}\bar{R}}{1-\rho},\end{split}

which proves (
3.6
). Using this inequality in (
6.1
) we have

\begin{split}A_{t+1}&\leq(1-\gamma\alpha)A_{t}+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}\rho^{t}B_{0}+\alpha^{2}\frac{L\delta}{n}\frac{\sigma_{W}\bar{R}}{1-\rho}+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}(\|w_{*}\|+R).\end{split}

To analyze this inequality, we consider three sequences
A_{t}^{1},A_{t}^{2},A_{t}^{3}
such that for
t\geq 0
,

\begin{split}A_{t+1}^{1}&=(1-\gamma\alpha)A_{t}^{1}+\alpha^{2}\frac{L\delta}{n}\frac{\sigma_{W}\bar{R}}{1-\rho}\\
A_{t+1}^{2}&=(1-\gamma\alpha)A_{t}^{2}+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}\rho^{t}B_{0}\\
A_{t+1}^{3}&=(1-\gamma\alpha)A_{t}^{3}+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}\sigma_{W}^{t}}(\|w_{*}\|+R),\end{split}

with initial values
A_{0}^{1}=A_{0}
,
A_{0}^{2}=0
, and
A_{0}^{3}=0
.
We use Lemma
7.1
and Lemma
7.2
to analyze these sequences to find

\begin{split}A_{t}^{1}&\leq(1-\gamma\alpha)^{t}A_{0}+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n\gamma}\frac{\sigma_{W}\bar{R}}{1-\rho}\\
A_{t}^{2}&\leq\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}B_{0}t\Big[\max\{1-\gamma\alpha,\rho\}\Big]^{t-1}\\
A_{t}^{3}&\leq\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}}(\|w_{*}\|+R)t\Big[\max\{1-\gamma\alpha,e^{-c}\}\Big]^{t-1}.\end{split}

Collecting the above estimates, we get

\begin{split}A_{t}&\leq(1-\gamma\alpha)^{t}A_{0}+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}B_{0}t\Big[\max\{1-\gamma\alpha,\rho\}\Big]^{t-1}\\
&\quad+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n}{\|1_{n}-n\pi\|_{\pi}}(\|w_{*}\|+R)t\Big[\max\{1-\gamma\alpha,e^{-c}\}\Big]^{t-1}+\frac{\alpha L\delta}{n\gamma}\frac{\sigma_{W}\bar{R}}{1-\rho}.\end{split}

The proof is done.
∎

Appendix A
An intuition behind the gradient-push algorithm

In this section, we give an intuition behind the gradient-push
algorithm (
2.2
).

We may rewrite the algorithm (
2.2
) in terms of
w(t)
as

w(t+1)=(W\otimes I_{d})(w(t)-\alpha\nabla F(z(t)).

(A.1)

In order to understand the algorithm (
2.2
) in the above form, we need to figure out why we define
z(t)
as
z_{i}(t)=w_{i}(t)/y_{i}(t)
. First, we prove that regardless of the value of the variable
z(t)
, the variable
w(t)
in (
A.1
) achieves weighted consensus up to an
O(\alpha)
error under a mild boundedness assumption. To be more precise,
we have the following lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume that
\sup_{s\geq 0}\|\nabla F(z(s))\|<\infty
. Then there exist constants
D>0
and
\zeta\in(0,1)
determined by the graph such that

\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|\leq D\zeta^{t}\|w(0)\|-\frac{(\alpha D)\zeta}{1-\zeta}\sup_{s\geq 0}\|\nabla F(z(s))\|

for all
t\geq 0
.

Remark A.2
.

The boundedness assumption in the above lemma is actually guaranteed by the result of Theorem
3.2
under an assumption on the stepsize
\alpha>0
.

Proof.

Using (
A.1
) iteratively, we have

w(t)=W^{t}\otimes I_{d}(w(0))-\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\Big(W^{t-k}\otimes I_{d}\Big)\nabla F(z(k)).

(A.2)

Multiplying
\pi 1_{n}^{T}
in front of the both sides and using the property that
\pi 1_{n}^{T}W=\pi 1_{n}^{T}
, we get the following equation:

\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)=\pi 1_{n}^{T}\otimes I_{d}(w(0))-\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\pi 1_{n}^{T}\otimes I_{d}\nabla F(z(k)).

(A.3)

Combining (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) gives the following identity:

w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)=\Big(W^{t}-\pi 1_{n}^{T}\Big)\otimes I_{d}(w(0))-\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\Big[(W^{t-k}-\pi 1_{n}^{T})\otimes I_{d}\Big]\nabla F(z(k)).

(A.4)

By the property (
2.6
), there exist constants
D>0
and
\zeta>0
such that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|W^{t}-\pi 1_{n}^{T}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq D\zeta^{t}

for all
t\geq 0
. Inserting this estimate in (
A.4
) we deduce

\begin{split}&\|w(t)-n\pi\otimes\bar{w}(t)\|\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt\leq D\zeta^{t}\|w(0)\|-D\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\zeta^{t-k}\|\nabla F(z(k))\|\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt\leq D\zeta^{t}\|w(0)\|-\alpha D\sup_{s\geq 0}\|\nabla F(z(s))\|\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\zeta^{t-k}\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt=D\zeta^{t}\|w(0)\|-\frac{(\alpha D)\zeta}{1-\zeta}\sup_{s\geq 0}\|\nabla F(z(s))\|.\end{split}

The proof is done.
∎

The above lemma shows that
w(t)/n\pi
gets close to
\bar{w}(t)
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, i.e., achieves consensus, up to an error
O(\alpha)
. Now, we multiply
\pi 1_{n}^{T}
to the left of both sides of (
A.1
), to find

\begin{split}\bar{w}(t+1)&=\bar{w}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(z_{i}(t))\\
&=\bar{w}(t)-\alpha\nabla f(\bar{w}(t))+\frac{\alpha}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\nabla f_{i}(\bar{w}(t))-\nabla f_{i}(z_{i}(t))\Big).\end{split}

From this expression, we see that
\{\bar{w}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
follows approximately the gradient descent method of the global cost function
f
provided that
z_{i}(t)
is close enough to
\bar{w}(t)
for each
1\leq i\leq n
. On the other hand, the above lemma yields that
\frac{w_{i}(t)}{n\pi_{i}}
gets close to
\bar{w}(t)
up to an
O(\alpha)
-error.

Therefore, if we want the solution
\bar{w}(t)
to converge to the minimizer
w_{*}
of
f
, the natural choice of
z_{i}(t)
would be
{w_{i}(t)}/n\pi_{i}
if we know
\pi_{i}
in advance. However, it is often the case that the value
\pi_{i}
should be calculated in a distributed manner when a graph of network is given. Having said this, a natural choice of
z_{i}(t)
is given by
z_{i}(t)=w_{i}(t)/y_{i}(t)
with
the balancing constants
y(t)
of (
1.2
) in view of the exponential convergence of
y(t)
to
n\pi
(see (
2.6
)).

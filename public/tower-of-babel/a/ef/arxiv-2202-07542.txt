The ATM implied volatility slope, the (dual) volatility swap, and the (dual) zero vanna implied volatility
Frido Rolloos

Abstract
Exact relationships between the short time-to-maturity ATM implied volatility slope, the (dual) volatility swap, and the (dual) zero vanna implied volatility are given.



1
Assumptions

Various papers have studied the short time behavior of the at-the-money (ATM) implied volatility level and skew (see for example
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
). Here we give another interpretation of the short-time ATM skew for diffusion models with stochastic volatility in terms of volatility swaps.

We will work under the premise that the market implied volatility surface is generated by the following general stochastic volatility (SV) model

S_{T}=S_{t}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{T}\sigma^{2}_{u}\,du+\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}\left(\rho dW_{u}+\bar{\rho}dZ_{u}\right)\right\},

(1)

where
dZ_{u}
and
dW_{u}
are standard Brownian motions,
\bar{\rho}=\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}
. The instantaneous volatility
\sigma_{u}
is assumed to be a positive process that is adapted to the filtration generated by
W_{u}
and independent of the stock price
S_{u}
.

The SV process is assumed to be well-behaved in the sense that vanilla options prices are risk-neutral expectations of the payoff function:

C(S_{t},t,K,T)=E_{t}\left[\left(S_{T}-K\right)_{+}\right]

(2)

The option price
C
can always be expressed in terms of the Black-Merton-Scholes (BS) price with an implied volatility parameter
I
:

C(S_{t},K)=BS(S_{t},K,I)

(3)

The BS price is given by the well-known formula

BS(S_{t},K,I)=S_{t}N(d_{+})-KN(d_{-}),

(4)

where
N\left(d_{\pm}\right)
are normal distribution functions,

d_{\pm}=\frac{\log(S_{t}/K)}{I\sqrt{T-t}}\pm\frac{I\sqrt{T-t}}{2}.

(5)

In what follows we will use the following notations:

•

The log spot price is defined as
x_{t}=\log S_{t}
and the log strike as
k=\log K
.

•

The ATM implied volatility will be denoted as
I_{0}
and the ATM log strike as
k_{0}
.

•

The zero vanna implied volatility
I_{-}
, the zero vanna log strike
k_{-}
, and their duals
I_{+}
and
k_{+}
are defined by

d_{\pm}(I_{\pm},k_{\pm})=0.

(6)

•

The realised volatility over the interval
[t,T]
is

\sigma_{t,T}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{T-t}\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}^{2}\,du}.

(7)

•

The volatility swap price is
E_{t}\left[\sigma_{t,T}\right]
. The dual volatility swap price is
E_{t}\left[e^{x_{T}-x_{t}}\sigma_{t,T}\right]
. Notice that the dual volatility swap is the volatility swap under the share measure.

2
Results

2.1
Approximations

As derived using straightforward Taylor expansion in
[
3
]
there is a simple relationship between (dual) volatility swap prices and (dual) zero vanna implied volatilities:

E_{t}\left[\sigma_{t,T}\right]\approx I_{-},\quad E_{t}\left[e^{x_{T}-x_{t}}\sigma_{t,T}\right]\approx I_{+}.

(8)

Furthermore,

I_{+}-I_{-}\approx\rho\,E_{t}\left[\sigma_{t,T}\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}\,dW_{u}\right].

(9)

It is reasonable to assume that as
T-t\rightarrow 0
the implied volatilities
I_{-}
and
I_{+}
are almost equidistant from the ATM implied volatility
I_{0}
. This is expressed as

I_{-}\approx I_{0}-\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}dk,\quad I_{+}\approx I_{0}+\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}dk,

(10)

and thus

I_{+}-I_{-}\approx 2\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}dk.

(11)

From the definition of
I_{\pm}
it follows that

\displaystyle\ x_{t}-(k_{0}-dk)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}I_{-}^{2}(T-t),

(12)

\displaystyle x_{t}-(k_{0}+dk)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}I_{+}^{2}(T-t).

(13)

Inserting (
10
) into the above and ignoring terms of order
(dk)^{2}
we obtain

2dk\approx I_{0}^{2}(T-t).

(14)

Hence, by making use of (
11
)

I_{0}^{2}(T-t)\,\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}\approx I_{+}-I_{-}

(15)

Remembering that
I_{0}\rightarrow\sigma_{t}
as
T-t\rightarrow 0
, we can also write

\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}\approx\frac{\rho}{\sigma_{t}^{2}(T-t)}\,E_{t}\left[\sigma_{t,T}\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}\,dW_{u}\right].

(16)

2.1.1
Example: lognormal instantaneous variance

Suppose the instantaneous variance is lognormal,

d\sigma^{2}_{u}=\alpha\sigma^{2}_{u}\,dW_{u}.

(17)

According to the integration by parts formula

E_{t}\left[\sigma_{t,T}\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}\,dW_{u}\right]=E_{t}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\left(D^{W}_{u}\sigma_{t,T}\right)\sigma_{u}\,du,\right]

(18)

with
D^{W}_{u}
the Malliavin derivative with respect to
W_{u}
. Now

D^{W}_{u}\sigma_{t,T}=\frac{1}{2\sigma_{t,T}(T-t)}\int_{u}^{T}D^{W}_{u}\sigma_{r}^{2}\,dr.

(19)

For the the lognormal instantaneous variance model
D^{W}_{u}\sigma_{r}^{2}=\alpha\sigma_{r}^{2}
, which for
T-t\rightarrow 0
we can approximate by
\alpha\sigma_{t}^{2}
. Similarly we can approximate
\sigma_{t,T}\approx\sigma_{t}
. Hence,

E_{t}\left[\sigma_{t,T}\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}\,dW_{u}\right]\approx\frac{1}{4}\alpha\sigma^{2}_{t}(T-t).

(20)

By making use of (
16
) the short-time ATM implied volatility slope is

\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}\approx\frac{\rho\alpha}{4}.

(21)

2.2
Exact results

Assuming certain technical conditions are satisfied by the instantaneous volatility process,
[
1
]
prove the following result on the short time to maturity behaviour of the slope of the ATM implied volatility.

Theorem 1
.

Consider the model (
1
). Then

\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{1}{(T-t)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}}\,\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}=\frac{\rho}{2\sigma_{t}^{2}}\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{1}{(T-t)^{H+\frac{3}{2}}}\,E_{t}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}D^{W}_{s}\sigma^{2}_{r}\,drds\right],

(22)

where
H
is the Hurst parameter and
D^{W}_{u}
is the Malliavin derivative with respect to
W_{u}
.

Restricted to the case
H=1/2
, Theorem
1
gives

\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}=\frac{\rho}{2\sigma_{t}^{2}}\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{1}{(T-t)^{2}}\,E_{t}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}D^{W}_{s}\sigma^{2}_{r}\,drds\right].

(23)

We now derive exact relationships between the slope of the ATM implied volatility, volatility swaps under the money market and share measures, and the zero vanna implied volatility and its dual for the case
H=1/2
. The same hypotheses on the instantaneous volatility process as in
[
1
]
are assumed to hold.

Proposition 2
.

\lim_{T\downarrow t}\frac{E_{t}\left[\left(e^{x_{T}-x_{t}}-1\right)\sigma_{t,T}\right]}{\sigma_{t}^{2}(T-t)}=\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}.

(24)

Proof.

Notice that we can write the model (
1
) as

x_{T}=\widetilde{x}_{T}+M_{T},

with

\widetilde{x}_{T}=x_{t}-\frac{\bar{\rho}^{2}}{2}\int_{t}^{T}\sigma^{2}_{u}\,du+\bar{\rho}\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}\,dZ_{u},

and

M_{T}=-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}\int_{t}^{T}\sigma^{2}_{u}\,du+\rho\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}\,dW_{u}.

Thus,

\displaystyle E_{t}\left[e^{x_{T}-x_{t}}\sigma_{t,T}\right]

\displaystyle=E_{t}\left[e^{\widetilde{x}_{T}-x_{t}}e^{M_{T}}\sigma_{t,T}\right]

\displaystyle=E_{t}\left[e^{M_{T}}\sigma_{t,T}\right].

where the second equality is obtained by conditioning on
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{W}
. Since

e^{M_{T}}=1+\rho\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}e^{M_{u}}\,dW_{u},

it follows that

E_{t}\left[\left(e^{x_{T}-x_{t}}-1\right)\sigma_{t,T}\right]=\rho E_{t}\left[\sigma_{t,T}\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}e^{M_{u}}\,dW_{u}\right].

A straightforward application of the integration by parts formula then gives us

E_{t}\left[\left(e^{x_{T}-x_{t}}-1\right)\sigma_{t,T}\right]=\frac{\rho}{2(T-t)}E_{t}\left[\frac{1}{\sigma_{t,T}}\int_{t}^{T}\left(\int_{s}^{T}D^{W}_{s}\sigma^{2}_{r}\,dr\right)e^{M_{s}}\sigma_{s}ds\right].

Comparing this with Theorem
1
it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{T\downarrow t}\frac{E_{t}\left[\left(e^{x_{T}-x_{t}}-1\right)\sigma_{t,T}\right]}{\sigma_{t}^{2}(T-t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho}{2\sigma_{t}^{2}}\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{1}{(T-t)^{2}}\,E_{t}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}D^{W}_{s}\sigma^{2}_{r}\,drds\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}.

∎

Remark 3
.

The quantity
E_{t}\left[\left(e^{x_{T}-x_{t}}-1\right)\sigma_{t,T}\right]
is the covariance between the simple return of the spot price and its realised volatility. Proposition
2
therefore says two things: First of all the slope of the ATM implied volatility contains information about the covariance between the spot and its volatility. Second, although the volatility swap and its dual are model dependent, their normalised difference becomes increasingly model independent as time to maturity decreases.

Proposition 4
.

If the implied volatility skew admits a Taylor series in strike, then

\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{I_{+}-I_{-}}{\sigma_{t}^{2}(T-t)}=\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}.

(25)

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can set
x_{t}=k_{0}=0
. Write

I_{\pm}=I_{0}+k_{\pm}\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}+O\left(k_{\pm}^{2}\right).

From the definitions of
I_{\pm}
and
k_{\pm}
it follows that

\displaystyle I_{+}-I_{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(I_{+}^{2}+I_{-}^{2})(T-t)\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}+O\left((T-t)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=I_{0}^{2}(T-t)\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}+O\left((T-t)^{2}\right).

Since
\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,I_{0}^{2}=\sigma_{t}^{2}
and
\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}
exists,

\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{I_{+}-I_{-}}{T-t}=\sigma_{t}^{2}\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{\partial I_{0}}{\partial k}.

∎

Corollary 5
.

\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{E_{t}\left[\left(e^{x_{T}-x_{t}}-1\right)\sigma_{t,T}\right]}{T-t}=\lim_{T\downarrow t}\,\frac{I_{+}-I_{-}}{T-t}.

(26)

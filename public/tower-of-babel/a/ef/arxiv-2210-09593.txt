1.
Introduction

Let
D
be an
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold with smooth
boundary
\partial D
. We write

(\phi,\lambda)\in\operatorname{Eig}(\Delta)
if
\phi
is a
Dirichlet eigenfunction of
-\Delta
on
D
with eigenvalue

\lambda>0
, i.e.
-\Delta\phi=\lambda\phi
.
We always assume eigenfunctions
\phi
to be normalized in

L^{2}(D)
such that
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}=1
. According to
[
16
]
, there exist two positive constants

c_{1}(D)
and
c_{2}(D)
such that

\displaystyle c_{1}(D)\sqrt{\lambda}\,\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\leqslant c_{2}(D)\sqrt{\lambda}\,\|\phi\|_{\infty},\quad(\phi,\lambda)\in\operatorname{Eig}(\Delta),

(1.1)

where we write

\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}:=\|\,|\nabla\phi|\,\|_{\infty}
for
simplicity. An analogous statement for Neumann eigenfunctions has
been derived by Hu, Shi and Xui
[
9
]
. Subsequently, by methods
of stochastic analysis on Riemannian manifolds, Arnaudon, Thalmaier
and Wang
[
2
]
determined explicit constants
c_{1}(D)
and
c_{2}(D)
in
(
1.1
) for Dirichlet and Neumann
eigenfunctions. From this, together with the uniform estimate of
\phi
(see

[
8
,
7
,
12
]
),

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leq c_{D}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{4}}

for some positive constant
c_{D}
,
the optimal uniform bound of the gradient writes as

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{4}}.

Results of this type have been used to study gradient estimates for unit
spectral projection operators and to give a new proof of
Hörmander’s multiplier theorem, see
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
.

Concerning higher order estimates of eigenfunctions, not much is known.
Very recently, Steinerberger
[
17
]
studied Laplacian
eigenfunctions of
-\Delta
with Dirichlet boundary conditions on bounded
domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary and proved a sharp
Hessian estimate for the eigenfunctions which reads as

\displaystyle\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n+3}{4}}

where

\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}:=\sup\left\{|\operatorname{Hess}\phi(v,v)|(x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ v\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ |v|=1\right\}.

To the best of our knowledge, higher order estimates of eigenfunctions for Euclidean domains
first appeared in
[
6
]
(see Lemma C.1 in the Appendix there
which is easily adapted to cover our situation).

It is a natural question under which geometric assumptions such estimates
extend to compact manifolds (with boundary).
Following the lines of
[
2
]
, one may ask the question
how for the Hessian to derive explicit numerical constants
C_{1}(D)
and
C_{2}(D)
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}(D){\lambda}\,\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}\leqslant C_{2}(D){\lambda}\,\|\phi\|_{\infty},\quad(\phi,\lambda)\in\operatorname{Eig}(\Delta).

(1.2)

Note that for eigenfunctions of the Laplacian, one trivially has

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Hess}\phi|\geq\frac{1}{n}\,|\Delta\phi|=\frac{\lambda}{n}\,|\phi|,

and thus there is always the obvious lower bound

\displaystyle\frac{\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}}{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}\geq\frac{\lambda}{n}.

For this reason, we concentrate in the sequel on upper bounds for

{\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}}/{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}
.

In
[
2
]
a derivative formula for Dirichlet eigenfunctions has been
given from where an upper bound for the gradient of the
eigenfunction could be derived directly.
Let us briefly describe this method. Assume that
X_{t}
is a Brownian motion
on
D\setminus\partial D
with generator
\frac{1}{2}\Delta
, and
write
X_{t}(x)
to indicate the starting point
X_{0}=x
.
Then
X{\bf.}(x)
is defined up to the first hitting time

\tau_{D}=\inf\{t>0\colon X_{t}(x)\in\partial D\}
of the boundary.
For
x\in\partial D
we use the convention that
X{\bf.}(x)
is defined with lifetime

\tau_{D}\equiv 0
; in this case the subsequent statements usually hold automatically.

Suppose that
Q_{t}\colon T_{x}D\rightarrow T_{X_{t}(x)}D
is defined by

\displaystyle\text{D}Q_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}(Q_{t})\,dt,\quad Q_{0}={\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits},

where
\text{D}:=/\!/_{t}\,d\,/\!/_{t}^{-1}
with

/\!/_{t}:=/\!/_{0,t}\colon T_{x}D\rightarrow T_{X_{t}(x)}D
parallel
transport along
X(x)
and
{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}(v)(w)={\operatorname{Ric}}(v,w)
for
v,w\in TD
. Suppose that

(\phi,\lambda)\in\operatorname{Eig}(\Delta)
. Then, for
v\in T_{x}M
and any

k\in C^{1}_{b}([0,\infty);\mathbb{R})
, i.e.,

k
bounded with bounded derivative, the process

\displaystyle k(t)\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}\,\langle\nabla\phi(X_{t}),Q_{t}(v)\rangle-\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}\phi(X_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\langle\dot{k}(s)Q_{s}v,/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle,\quad t\leq\tau_{D}

is a martingale. From this, by taking expectation, a formula
involving
\nabla\phi
can be obtained which allows to derive an upper
bound for
|\nabla\phi|
on
D
by estimating
|\nabla\phi|
on the
boundary
\partial D
and carefully choosing the function
k
. Along
this circle of ideas, our aim is to establish a similar strategy
for the Hessian of an eigenfunction
\phi
.

In view of the fact that
P_{t}\phi=\operatorname{e}^{-\lambda t/2}\phi
where
P_{t}

is the semigroup generated by
\frac{1}{2}\Delta
, we focus first on
martingales which are appropriate for attaining uniform Hessian
estimates of eigenfunctions. Let us start with some background on
Bismut type formulas for second-order derivatives of heat
semigroups. A second-order differential formula for the heat semigroup

P_{t}
was first obtained by Elworthy and Li
[
5
,
13
]
for a
non-compact manifold, however with restrictions on the curvature of
the manifold. An intrinsic formula for
\operatorname{Hess}P_{t}f
has been given by
Stroock
[
18
]
for a compact Riemannian manifold, and a
localized version of such a formula was obtained in
[
1
,
3
]
adopting
martingale arguments. For the Hessian of the Feynman-Kac
semigroup of an operator
\Delta+V
with a potential function
V
on manifolds, we refer the reader to
[
14
,
15
,
19
]
.

For a complete Riemannian manifold
M
without boundary,
an appropriate version of a Bismut-type Hessian formula gives the following estimate
(see
[
3
, Corollary 4.3]
and Lemma
2.2
, or Corollary
3.2
with
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=0
):

\displaystyle\|\operatorname{Hess}P_{t}f\|_{\infty}\leq\left(K_{1}\sqrt{t}+\frac{K_{2}t}{2}\right)\operatorname{e}^{K_{0}t}\,\|f\|_{\infty}+\frac{2}{t}\operatorname{e}^{K_{0}t}\,\|f\|_{\infty}

where

\displaystyle K_{0}:=\sup\left\{-{\rm Ric}(v,v)\colon y\in M,\ v\in T_{y}M,\ |v|=1\right\};

\displaystyle K_{1}:=\sup\left\{|R|(y)\colon y\in M\right\};

(1.3)

\displaystyle K_{2}:=\sup\left\{|({\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}})^{\sharp}(v,w)|(y)\colon y\in M,\ v,w\in T_{y}M,\ |v|=|w|=1\right\}

and

\displaystyle|R|(y):=\sup\left\{\sqrt{\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}R(e_{i},v,w,e_{j})^{2}(y)}:|v|\leq 1,|w|\leq 1\right\}

for an orthonormal base
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
T_{y}M
.

Thus if
f=\phi
and
(\phi,\lambda)\in\operatorname{Eig}(\Delta)
, then

\displaystyle\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}\leq\left(K_{1}\sqrt{t}+\frac{K_{2}t}{2}\right)\operatorname{e}^{(K_{0}+\lambda/2)t}\|\phi\|_{\infty}+\frac{2\operatorname{e}^{(K_{0}+\lambda/2)t}}{t}\,\|\phi\|_{\infty}

for any
t>0
. Letting
t=\frac{1}{K_{0}+\lambda/2}
then yields the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}}{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}\leq\left(K_{1}\sqrt{\frac{2}{2K_{0}+\lambda}}+\frac{K_{2}}{2K_{0}+\lambda}\right)\operatorname{e}+(\lambda+2K_{0})\operatorname{e}.

To carry over such results to (compact) manifolds
D
with boundary, the
influence of the boundary has to be studied. In this paper, we
shall adopt a martingale approach to the Hessian of
Dirichlet eigenfunctions. This approach is based on the construction of a suitable
martingale which builds a relation between
\operatorname{Hess}\phi
and
{\bf d}\phi
and then
to estimate
C_{2}(D)
in (
1.2
) by searching for explicit
constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
such that

\displaystyle\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}\leq C_{1}\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\partial D,\infty}+C_{2}\|\nabla\,\phi\|_{\partial D,\infty}+C_{3}\|\nabla\,\phi\|_{\infty}

(1.4)

where

\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\partial D,\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\partial D}|\operatorname{Hess}\phi|(x)
and

\|\nabla\,\phi\|_{\partial D,\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\partial D}|\nabla\,\phi|(x)
. The final estimate for
|\operatorname{Hess}\phi|
is then
received by combining the last inequality with estimate
(
1.1
) in
[
2
]
.

Let us start with the general principle behind the construction of the relevant
martingale. Let
k\in C_{b}^{1}([0,\infty);\mathbb{R})
and define an
operator-valued process
W^{k}_{t}\colon T_{x}D\otimes T_{x}D\rightarrow T_{X_{t}(x)}D
as
solution to the following covariant Itô equation

\displaystyle{\rm D}W_{t}^{k}(v,w)=R(/\!/_{t}dB_{t},Q_{t}(k(t)v))Q_{t}(w)-\frac{1}{2}({\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}})^{\sharp}(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(w))\,dt-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}(W_{t}^{k}(v,w))\,dt,

with initial condition
W_{0}^{k}(v,w)=0
. Here the operator
{\bf d}^{*}R
is defined by

{\bf d}^{*}R(v_{1},v_{2}):=-\operatorname{tr}\nabla{\!\bf.}\,R(\cdot,v_{1})v_{2}
and thus
satisfies

\displaystyle\langle{\bf d}^{*}R(v_{1},v_{2}),v_{3}\rangle=\langle(\nabla_{v_{3}}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp})(v_{1}),v_{2}\rangle-\langle(\nabla_{v_{2}}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp})(v_{3}),v_{1}\rangle

for all
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in T_{x}D
and
x\in D
. Then the process

\displaystyle M_{t}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}\operatorname{Hess}\phi\big(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(v)\big)+\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}{\bf d}\phi(W_{t}^{k}(v,v))

\displaystyle\quad-\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}{\bf d}\phi(Q_{t}(v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle

(1.5)

is a martingale on
[0,\tau_{D}]
in the sense that
(M_{t\wedge\tau_{D}})_{t\geq 0}
is a globally defined martingale where
\tau_{D}=\inf\{t>0:X_{t}(x)\in\partial D\}
denotes the first hitting time of

X{\bf.}(x)
of the boundary
\partial D
. The martingale property of (
1
) then allows to establish
an inequality of the type (
1.4
) by equating the expectations at time 0 and at time

t\wedge\tau_{D}
. This approach then requires to estimate the boundary values
of
|{\bf d}\phi|
and
|\operatorname{Hess}\phi|
, in order to obtain the wanted upper
bound for
\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}
. To this end, we establish the required
estimates in Lemmas 2.4-2.5 by using the information on the second fundamental form
{\operatorname{II}}
and the second derivative of
N
, where for

X,Y\in T_{x}\partial D
and
x\in\partial D
, the second fundamental form is defined by

\displaystyle{\operatorname{II}}(X,Y)=-\langle\nabla_{X}N,\ Y\rangle.

Finally, let

\displaystyle\ell(t):=\ell_{k,\sigma}(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos{\sqrt{k}t}-\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{k}}\sin{\sqrt{k}t},&k>0,\\
1-\sigma t,&k=0,\\
\cosh{\sqrt{-k}t}-\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{-k}}\sinh{\sqrt{-k}t},&k<0,\end{array}\right.

We state now the first main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
D
be a compact Riemannian manifold with smooth
boundary
\partial D
. Let
K_{0},K_{1},K_{2},\sigma
be non negative constants such that
{\operatorname{Ric}}\geq-K_{0}
,
|R|\leq K_{1}
and
|{\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}|\leq K_{2}
on
D
, and that
|{\operatorname{II}}|\leq\sigma
. Assume that the distance function
\rho_{\partial}
is smooth on the tubular neighborhood
\partial_{r_{1}}D:=\{x\in D:\rho_{\partial}(x)\leq r_{1}\}
of
\partial D
. Let
k,\beta,\gamma
be constants such that
|{\operatorname{Sect}}|\leq k
on
\partial_{r_{1}}D
, and that

\displaystyle|\nabla(\Delta\rho_{\partial})|\leq\beta,\quad|\Delta^{2}\rho_{\partial}|\leq\gamma\quad\mbox{on}\ \partial_{r_{0}}D,

(1.9)

where
r_{0}=r_{1}\wedge\ell^{-1}({1}/{2})
.
Then for any non-trivial

(\phi,\lambda)\in\operatorname{Eig}_{N}(\Delta)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\|\operatorname{Hess}\,\phi\|_{\infty}}{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}\leq C_{\lambda}(D)\lambda

where

\displaystyle C_{\lambda}(D)

\displaystyle\leq 2(n-1)\operatorname{e}\sigma\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\lambda}}\right)+{2\alpha}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}n\sigma r_{0}+\frac{1}{2}}\max\left\{\sqrt{\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{2K_{0}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{\sigma}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{n}{r_{0}}+2\sigma\right)}\,,\,\frac{2\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}n\sigma r_{0}}}{\lambda}\left(\frac{6}{r_{0}}+2\alpha\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\frac{3}{r_{0}}(\alpha^{2}+2\beta)+\frac{6}{r_{0}^{2}}\alpha+\gamma}{\left(\frac{6}{r_{0}}+2\alpha\right)\lambda}+\frac{\alpha}{\frac{6}{r_{0}}+2\alpha}

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\operatorname{e}}\left(\frac{\frac{9}{r_{0}}\alpha+\frac{6}{r_{0}^{2}}+3\beta+\lambda+K_{1}}{\frac{6}{r_{0}}+2\alpha}+\frac{K_{2}}{4\sqrt{\operatorname{e}}\left(\frac{6}{r_{0}}+2\alpha\right)^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}+\left(\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\right)\sqrt{\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{K_{0}}{\lambda^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+4\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}n\sigma r_{0}+\frac{1}{2}}\max\left\{\sqrt{1+\frac{2K_{0}}{\lambda}+\frac{\sigma}{\lambda}\left(\frac{n}{r_{0}}+2\sigma\right)}\,,\,\frac{2\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}n\sigma r_{0}}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(\frac{6}{r_{0}}+2\alpha\right)\right\}

(1.10)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\lambda}}+\left(\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\right)\sqrt{1+\frac{K_{0}}{\lambda}}\right),

for
\alpha=2(n-1)\max\{\sigma,k\}
.

Remark 1.2
.

1) Adopting the estimate above, obviously
C_{\lambda}(D)
is
decreasing in
\lambda
, and hence

C_{\lambda}(D)\leq C_{\lambda_{1}}(D)

where
\lambda_{1}
is the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
-\Delta

which gives

\displaystyle\frac{\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}}{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}\leq C_{\lambda_{1}}(D)\lambda.

2) If the manifold has constant sectional curvature and mean curvature on
\partial_{r_{0}}D
, i.e.
H=\theta,\,{\operatorname{Sect}}=k
on
\partial_{r_{0}}D
, then for
\rho_{\partial}(x)\leq\ell^{-1}(0)\wedge r_{0}
,

\Delta\rho_{\partial}=\frac{\ell^{\prime}_{\theta,(n-1)k}}{\ell_{\theta,(n-1)k}}(\rho_{\partial}).

As a consequence, the upper bound of
|\nabla(\Delta\rho_{\partial})|
and
|\Delta^{2}\rho_{\partial}|
can be calculated explicitly, as

\displaystyle|\nabla(\Delta\rho_{\partial})|(x)\leq 4((n-1)k+\sigma^{2}),\ \ |\Delta^{2}\rho_{\partial}|(x)\leq 8\max\big\{\sigma,\sqrt{(n-1)k}\big\}((n-1)k+\sigma^{2}),

for
\rho_{\partial}(x)\leq i_{0}\wedge\ell^{-1}({1}/{2})
.

For the general case, from the second variation formula of
\rho_{\partial}
(see (
2.9
) below) we see that
further information about
|\nabla{\operatorname{II}}|
,
|\nabla^{2}{\operatorname{II}}|
,
|R|
,
|\nabla R|
and
|\nabla^{2}R|
on
\partial_{r_{0}}D
is needed to derive an upper bound of

|\nabla(\Delta\rho_{\partial})|
and
|\Delta^{2}\rho_{\partial}|
.

Turning now to Hessian estimates for Neumann eigenfunctions, let us
denote by
\operatorname{Eig}_{N}(\Delta)
the set of non-trivial

(\phi,\lambda)
for the Neumann eigenproblem, i.e.,
\phi
is
non-constant,
\Delta\phi=-\lambda\phi
and
N\phi|_{\partial D}=0

for the unit inward normal vector field
N
of
\partial D
. Proceeding along the
previous ideas, the main difference is that we can no longer consider the process only
up to the first hitting the boundary
\partial D
. When constructing the suitable
martingales, the boundary behaviour of the process must be included a priori.
We will use the reflecting Brownian motion as base process to
deal with this question. Due to recent work on Bismut-type Hessian formula for the Neumann
semigroup
[
4
]
, we have the following formula linking
\operatorname{Hess}P_{t}f
and
{\bf d}f
intrinsically:

\operatorname{Hess}{P_{t}f}(v,v)=\mathbb{E}\left[-{\bf d}f(\tilde{Q}_{t}(v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle+{\bf d}f(\tilde{W}_{t}^{k}(v,v))\right],

where
\tilde{Q}
and
\tilde{W}^{k}
are defined in (
3.1
) and (
3
) in Section

3
.
By observing the fact that
P_{t}\phi=\operatorname{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda t}\phi
and estimating
\tilde{Q}{\bm{.}}
and
\tilde{W}{\!\bm{.}}
carefully under suitable curvature conditions, we obtain the following
theorem which gives an upper estimate for
\operatorname{Hess}\phi
of the type (
1.2
)
with an explicit constant
C_{2}(D)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
D
be an
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold with
boundary
\partial D
. Let
K_{0},K_{1},K_{2}
be non-negative constants such that
{\operatorname{Ric}}\geq-K_{0}
,
|R|\leq K_{1}
and

|{\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}|\leq K_{2}
on
D
,and let
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma
be non-negative constants such that

-\sigma_{1}\leq{\operatorname{II}}\leq\sigma
and
|\nabla^{2}N-R(N)|\leq\sigma_{2}
on the
boundary
\partial D
. Assume the distance function
\rho_{\partial}

to the boundary
\partial D
is smooth on
\partial_{r_{1}}D:=\{x\in D:\rho_{\partial}(x)\leq r_{1}\}
and let
k
be constant such that
{\operatorname{Sect}}\leq k
on
\partial_{r_{1}}D
. Then for any non-trivial

(\phi,\lambda)\in\operatorname{Eig}_{N}(\Delta)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\|\operatorname{Hess}\,\phi\|_{\infty}}{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}\leq C_{N,\lambda}(D)\lambda

where

\displaystyle C_{N,\lambda}(D)=

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{K_{1}+2K_{0}+2\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{n}{r_{0}}+2\sigma_{1}\right)}{\lambda}+\frac{K_{2}+2\sigma_{2}\left(\frac{n}{r_{0}}+2\sigma_{1}\right)}{\lambda\sqrt{2\lambda+4K_{0}+4\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{n}{r_{0}}+2\sigma_{1}\right)}}\right)\operatorname{e}^{\frac{3}{2}\sigma_{1}nr_{1}+1}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma_{2}nr_{0}}{2\lambda}\sqrt{2\lambda+4K_{0}+4\sigma_{1}\left(\frac{n}{r_{0}}+2\sigma_{1}\right)}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{3}{2}\sigma_{1}nr_{0}+1}

for
r_{0}=r_{1}\wedge\ell^{-1}(0)
.
Denoting by
\lambda_{1}
the first Neumann eigenvalue of
-\Delta
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}}{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}\leq C_{N,\lambda_{1}}(D)\,\lambda.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we first
show for Dirichlet eigenfunctions

\displaystyle\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}/\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\lambda}(D)\lambda

(1.11)

by verifying that
the process (
1
) is a martingale, in combination with boundary estimates
for

.
Section
3
deals with Neumann eigenfunctions where we give a proof of Theorem

by using Bismut type Hessian formulae for the Neumann semigroup and an estimate of the local time.

2.
Hessian estimates of Dirichlet eigenfunctions

This section is dedicated to the approach
described in the Introduction. In fact, the proof of Theorem
1.1

is also divided into two steps by first showing Theorem
2.11
with some
auxiliary function

, which will be constructed in Section

.

2.1.
Preliminary

We start by defining the fundamental martingale which will serve as basis for our method.

Theorem 2.1
.

On a compact Riemannian manifold
D
with
boundary
\partial D
, let
X{\bf.}(x)
be a Brownian motion starting
from
x\in D
and denote by
\tau_{D}=\inf\{t\geq 0\colon X_{t}(x)\in\partial D\}
its first
hitting time of
\partial D
. Define
Q_{t}
and
W_{t}^{k}
as above where

k\in C_{b}^{1}([0,\infty);\mathbb{R})
. Then,
for
(\phi,\lambda)\in\operatorname{Eig}_{N}(\Delta)
and
v\in T_{x}D
, the process

\displaystyle\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}\operatorname{Hess}\phi\big(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(v)\big)+\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}{\bf d}\phi(W_{t}^{k}(v,v))

\displaystyle\quad-\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}{\bf d}\phi(Q_{t}(v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle

(2.1)

is a martingale on
[0,\tau_{D}]
.

Proof.

Due to the compactness of
D
it is sufficient to check that
(
2.1
) is a local martingale on
[0,\tau_{D})
.
Fixing a time
T>0
, for
v\in T_{x}D
, we let

\displaystyle N_{t}(v,v)=\operatorname{Hess}P_{T-t}\phi(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(v))+({\bf d}P_{T-t}\phi)(W_{t}(v,v)),\quad t\leq T\wedge\tau_{D},

where

\displaystyle W_{t}(v,v)=Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}R\big(/\!/_{r}dB_{r},Q_{r}(v)\big)Q_{r}(v)-\frac{1}{2}Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}({\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}})^{\sharp}\big(Q_{r}(v),Q_{r}(v)\big)\,dr.

Then
N_{t}(v,v)
is a local martingale, see for instance the proof of

[
20
, Lemma 2.7]
with potential
V\equiv 0
.
Since
(\phi,\lambda)\in\operatorname{Eig}(\Delta)
, we know that

P_{T-t}\phi(X_{t})=\operatorname{e}^{-\lambda(T-t)/2}\phi(X_{t})
and thus

\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}\operatorname{Hess}\phi(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(v))+\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}({\bf d}\phi)(W_{t}(v,v))

is also a local martingale.
Furthermore, consider

N^{k}_{t}(v,v):=\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}\operatorname{Hess}\phi(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(v))+(\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}{\bf d}\phi)(W^{k}_{t}(v,v)).

According to the definition of
W^{k}_{t}(v,v)
, resp.
W_{t}(v,v)
, and in
view of the fact that

is a local martingale, it is easy to
see that

\displaystyle\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}\operatorname{Hess}\phi(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(v))+(\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}{\bf d}\phi)(W^{k}_{t}(v,v))-\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{\lambda s/2}\operatorname{Hess}\phi(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),Q_{s}(v))\,ds

is a local martingale as well. From the formula

\displaystyle\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}{\bf d}\phi(Q_{t}(v))={\bf d}\phi(v)+\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{\lambda s/2}(\operatorname{Hess}\phi)(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(v))

it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{\lambda s/2}(\operatorname{Hess}\phi)(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),Q_{s}(v))\,ds-\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}{\bf d}\phi(Q_{t}(v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle

(2.2)

is a local martingale. We conclude that

\displaystyle(\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}\operatorname{Hess}\phi)(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(v))+(\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}{\bf d}\phi)(W^{k}_{t}(v,v))-\operatorname{e}^{\lambda t/2}{\bf d}\phi(Q_{t}(v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle

is a local martingale.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
{\operatorname{Ric}}\geq-K_{0}
,
|R|\leq K_{1}
and
|{\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}|\leq K_{2}
on
D
for non-negative constants

and
K_{2}
.
Let

. For

and
\delta>0

it holds

\displaystyle|Q_{t}|\leq\operatorname{e}^{K_{0}t/2}\quad\text{and}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\big|W_{t}^{k}(v,\dot{k}(t)v)\big|\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\leq\tau_{D}\}}\right]\leq\left(K_{1}\left(\int_{0}^{t}k(s)^{2}\,ds\right)^{1/2}+\frac{K_{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\,ds\right)\,\operatorname{e}^{K_{0}t}\,|\dot{k}(t)|,

(2.4)

where
K_{0},K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are defined as in (
1
).

Proof.

The first inequality follows from the lower Ricci curvature bound condition and the definition of
Q_{t}
.
According to the definition of
W_{t}^{k}
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle W_{t}^{k}(v,v)=

\displaystyle Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{s}^{-1}R(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(k(s)v))Q_{s}(v)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{s}^{-1}({\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}})^{\sharp}(Q_{s}(k(s)v),Q_{s}(v))\,ds.

Note that for
0\leq s\leq t
, the damped parallel transport
Q_{s,t}=Q_{t}Q_{s}^{-1}\colon T_{X_{s}}D\rightarrow T_{X_{t}}D
satisfying

\displaystyle DQ_{t,s}=-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}(Q_{t,s})\,dt,\qquad Q_{s,s}={\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits},

Thus the lower bound of Ricci curvature
-K_{0}
yields

|Q_{s,t}|\leq\operatorname{e}^{K_{0}(t-s)/2}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(|W_{t}^{k}(v,v)|\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\leq\tau_{D}\}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\leq\tau_{D}\}}\big|Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{s}^{-1}R(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(k(s)v))Q_{s}(v)\big|\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\leq\tau_{D}\}}\big|Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{s}^{-1}({\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}})(Q_{s}(k(s)v),Q_{s}(v))\,ds\big|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{e}^{\frac{K_{0}t}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\leq\tau_{D}\}}\big|\operatorname{e}^{-\frac{K_{0}t}{2}}Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{s}^{-1}R(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(k(s)v))Q_{s}(v)\big|^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{K_{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\leq\tau_{D}\}}\big|\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}K_{0}t}\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}K_{0}s}|k(s)|\,ds\big|\right].

(2.5)

Moreover,

\displaystyle d\Big|\operatorname{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}K_{0}t}Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{s}^{-1}R(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(k(s)v))Q_{s}(v)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{e}^{-K_{0}t}\Big\langle R(/\!/_{t}dB_{t},Q_{t}(k(t)v))Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{s}^{-1}R(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(k(s)v))Q_{s}(v)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{e}^{-K_{0}t}\big|R^{\sharp,\sharp}(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(v))\big|_{\rm HS}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-\operatorname{e}^{-K_{0}t}{\operatorname{Ric}}\left(Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{s}^{-1}R(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(k(s)v))Q_{s}(v),Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{s}^{-1}R(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(k(s)v))Q_{s}(v)\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-K_{0}\operatorname{e}^{-K_{0}t}\big|Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{s}^{-1}R(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(k(s)v))Q_{s}(v)\big|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\overset{m}{\leq}\operatorname{e}^{-K_{0}t}\big|R^{\sharp,\sharp}(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(v))\big|_{\rm HS}^{2}\,dt\leq K_{1}^{2}\operatorname{e}^{-K_{0}t}|Q_{t}|^{4}k(t)^{2}\,dt\leq K_{1}^{2}\operatorname{e}^{K_{0}t}k(t)^{2}\,dt,\qquad t\leq\tau_{D}.

Combining this with (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(|W_{t}^{k}(v,v)|\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\leq\tau_{D}\}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}K_{0}t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{K_{0}s}k(s)^{2}\,ds\right)^{1/2}+\frac{K_{2}}{2}\operatorname{e}^{K_{0}t}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\,ds.

We then complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.7

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
K_{0},\sigma
be non-negative constants such that
{\operatorname{Ric}}\geq-K_{0}
,
|{\operatorname{II}}|\leq\sigma
. Suppose that
the distance function
\rho_{\partial}
is smooth on
\partial_{r_{0}}D:=\{x:\rho_{\partial}(x)\leq r_{0}\}
for some constant
r_{0}>0

Then for
x\in\partial D

\displaystyle\big\|\operatorname{Hess}(\phi)\big\|_{\partial D,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)\sigma\,\|N(\phi)\|_{\partial D,\infty}

\displaystyle\quad+\|h\|_{\infty}^{\sigma}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}(K_{0}+\sigma K_{h,\sigma})t}\left(C_{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}+C_{2}\sqrt{t}\right)\|\phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\quad+\|h\|_{\infty}^{\sigma}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}(K_{0}+\sigma K_{h,\sigma})t}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}+C_{3}\sqrt{t}\right)\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\quad+\|h\|_{\infty}^{\sigma}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}(K_{0}+\sigma K_{h,\sigma})t}\sqrt{t}C_{4}\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}

where

and

\displaystyle C_{1}=\|\Delta\rho_{\partial}\|_{\partial_{r_{0}}D},

\displaystyle C_{2}=\|\Delta(\psi(\rho_{\partial}))\Delta\rho_{\partial}+2\psi^{\prime}(\rho_{\partial})|\nabla(\Delta\rho_{\partial})|+\psi(\rho_{\partial})(\lambda\Delta\rho_{\partial}+\Delta^{2}\rho_{\partial})\|_{\partial_{r_{0}}D},

\displaystyle C_{3}=\|\Delta(\psi(\rho_{\partial}))+2\psi^{\prime}(\rho_{\partial})\Delta\rho_{\partial}+\psi(\rho_{\partial})(3|\nabla(\Delta\rho_{\partial})|+\lambda)\,\|_{\partial_{r_{0}}D},

\displaystyle C_{4}=\|2\psi^{\prime}(\rho_{\partial})+2(n-1)\psi(\rho_{\partial})|\operatorname{Hess}\rho_{\partial}|\,\|_{\partial_{r_{0}}D}.

\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},[0,1])
satisfies
\psi(0)=1
,
\psi^{\prime}(0)=0
and
\psi(r)=0
for

Given
x\in\partial D
, let
\{X_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
be an

. Then

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Hess}(\phi)(X_{i},X_{j})|

\displaystyle=|\nabla{\bf d}\phi(X_{i},X_{j})|=|\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla\phi,X_{j}\rangle|

\displaystyle=|X_{i}\langle\nabla\phi,X_{j}\rangle-\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla_{X_{i}}X_{j}\rangle|.

By assumption we have
|{\operatorname{II}}|\leq\sigma
.
If
X_{i},\,X_{j}\in T_{x}\partial D

\langle\nabla\phi,X_{j}\rangle|_{\partial D}=0
and

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Hess}(\phi)(X_{i},X_{j})|=|-N(\phi)\langle N,\nabla_{X_{i}}X_{j}\rangle|\leq\sigma|N(\phi)|.

(2.10)

If
X_{i}=X_{j}=N
, i.e.

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Hess}(\phi)(N,N)|=|N^{2}(\phi)|\leq|HN(\phi)|\leq(n-1)\sigma|N(\phi)|.

on
D
reflected at the boundary. Before going into the details, we make a general remark

\partial D

Remark 2.10

Assuming that the boundary
\partial D
is smooth, let
N
be the unit inward normal vector field
N

{[0,\varepsilon_{0}[}\times\partial D

in

.
This allows to extend

By construction then

If

\partial D
, we extend it to the neighborhood of

as being independent of the real variable in the product

{[0,\varepsilon_{0}[}\times\partial D

.

Let
N
be the extension of the normal vector field to a tubular neighborhood

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=\psi(\rho_{\partial}(x)){\operatorname{div}}(\phi N),\qquad x\in\partial_{r_{0}}D,

(2.14)

\psi(0)=1
,
\psi^{\prime}(0)=0
and
\psi(r)=0

Let now
X_{t}
be the reflecting Brownian motion on
D
and
P_{t}^{N}f(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}[f(X_{t})]
for

According to the Kolmogorov equation,

\displaystyle\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=P_{t}^{N}(\varphi)(x)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}P_{s}^{N}(\Delta\varphi)(x)\,ds.

Taking derivative on both sides of the above equation yields

\displaystyle X_{i}(\varphi)(x)=X_{i}(P_{t}^{N}\varphi)(x)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}X_{i}(P^{N}_{s}\Delta\varphi)(x)\,ds

is tangential to
\partial D

for
x\in\partial D

\displaystyle X_{i}(\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle=X_{i}(\psi(\rho_{\partial}))(x){\operatorname{div}}(\phi N)(x)+\psi(\rho_{\partial}(x))X_{i}({\operatorname{div}}(\phi N))(x)=X_{i}({\operatorname{div}}(\phi N))(x)

\displaystyle=X_{i}N(\phi)(x)+X_{i}(\phi)(x){\operatorname{div}}(N)(x)+\phi(x)X_{i}({\operatorname{div}}(N))(x)

\displaystyle=X_{i}N(\phi)(x).

To deal with the upper bound, we use the Bismut formula established in
[
23
, Theorem 3.2.1]
for the
compact manifold

, which gives

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{t}^{N}f|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}K_{0}t}\mathbb{E}^{x}[\operatorname{e}^{\sigma l_{t}}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\infty},

where

We then conclude that

\displaystyle|X_{i}N(\phi)|(x)

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|_{\infty}^{\sigma}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}(K_{0}+\sigma K_{h,2\sigma})t}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\,\|\varphi\|_{B(x,r_{0})}+\sqrt{t}\,\|\Delta\varphi\|_{B(x,r_{0})}\right].

(2.15)

According to the definition of
\varphi
in (

), we have

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}+\|{\operatorname{div}}(N)\|_{\partial_{r_{0}}D}\|\phi\|_{\infty}

By commutation rules, we calculate

\displaystyle\Delta((\psi(\rho_{\partial})){\operatorname{div}}(\phi N))=

\displaystyle\Delta(\psi(\rho_{\partial})){\operatorname{div}}(\phi N))+2\psi^{\prime}(\rho_{\partial})N({\operatorname{div}}(\phi N))+\psi(\rho_{\partial}))\Delta({\operatorname{div}}(\phi N))

\displaystyle\Delta(\psi(\rho_{\partial}))(\phi{\operatorname{div}}(N)+N(\phi))+2\psi^{\prime}(\rho_{\partial})\Big(\phi N({\operatorname{div}}(N))+N(\phi){\operatorname{div}}(N)+N^{2}(\phi)\Big)

\displaystyle\Delta({\operatorname{div}}(\phi N))

\displaystyle={\operatorname{div}}(\Delta(\phi)N)+{\operatorname{div}}(\phi\Box N)+2{\operatorname{div}}(\nabla_{\nabla\phi}N)-\phi{\operatorname{div}}({\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}(N))-{\operatorname{Ric}}(N,\nabla\phi)

{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}\colon TD\rightarrow TD

\displaystyle=\langle\operatorname{Hess}_{\phi},\nabla N\rangle+\langle\nabla\phi,\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\operatorname{div}}(\nabla_{e_{i}}N)e_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\Delta({\operatorname{div}}(\phi N))

\displaystyle=\phi(-\lambda{\operatorname{div}}(N)+\Delta({\operatorname{div}}(N)))-\lambda N(\phi)+2\langle\operatorname{Hess}(\phi),\nabla N\rangle+2\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla({\operatorname{div}}(N))\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle\Box N,\nabla\phi\rangle-{\operatorname{Ric}}(N,\nabla\phi).

From the fact that

\displaystyle|X_{i}N(\phi)|(x)

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|_{\infty}^{\sigma}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}(K_{0}+2\sigma K_{h,\sigma})t}\left(C_{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}+C_{2}\sqrt{t}\right)\|\phi\|_{\infty}+\|h\|_{\infty}^{\sigma}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}(K_{0}+\sigma K_{h,2\sigma})t}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}+C_{3}\sqrt{t}\right)\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}

where

\displaystyle C_{2}=\|\Delta(\psi(\rho_{\partial}))\Delta\rho_{\partial}+2\psi^{\prime}(\rho_{\partial})|\nabla(\Delta\rho_{\partial})|+\psi(\rho_{\partial})(\lambda\Delta\rho_{\partial}+\Delta^{2}\rho_{\partial})\|_{\partial_{r_{0}}D},

\displaystyle C_{3}=\|\Delta(\psi(\rho_{\partial}))+2\psi^{\prime}(\rho_{\partial})\Delta\rho_{\partial}+\psi(\rho_{\partial})(3|\nabla(\Delta\rho_{\partial})|+\lambda)\,\|_{\partial_{r_{0}}D},

\displaystyle C_{4}=\|2\psi^{\prime}(\rho_{\partial})+2(n-1)\psi(\rho_{\partial})|\operatorname{Hess}\rho_{\partial}|\,\|_{\partial_{r_{0}}D}.

3.
Hessian estimates on Neumann eigenfunctions of Laplacian

We also use a stochastic approach to prove Theorem
1.3
. Let us first recall the
Hessian formulas for the Neumann semigroups, established recently in
[
4
]
.
The reflecting Brownian motion on
D
with generator
\frac{1}{2}\Delta
satisfies the SDE

dX_{t}=/\!/_{t}\circ\,dB_{t}^{x}+\frac{1}{2}N(X_{t})\,dl_{t},\quad X_{0}=x,

where
B_{t}^{x}
is a standard Brownian motion on the Euclidean space
T_{x}D\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We write again
X_{t}=X_{t}(x)
to indicate the starting point
x\in D
(which may be on the boundary
\partial D
).
Here
/\!/_{t}:T_{x}D\rightarrow T_{X_{t}(x)}D
denotes the
\nabla
-parallel
transport along
X_{t}(x)
and

l_{t}
the local time of
X_{t}(x)
supported on
\partial D
.
Note that the reflecting Brownian motion
X_{t}(x)
is defined for all
t\geq 0
.

Suppose that
\tilde{Q}_{t}\colon T_{x}D\rightarrow T_{X_{t}(x)}D
satisfies

\displaystyle\text{D}\tilde{Q}_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{t})\,dt+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla N)^{\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{t})\,dl_{t},\quad\tilde{Q}_{0}={\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}.

(3.1)

For
k\in C^{1}_{b}([0,\infty);\mathbb{R})
define an operator-valued process

\tilde{W}_{t}^{k}\colon T_{x}D\otimes T_{x}D\rightarrow T_{X_{t}(x)}D
as solution to the following covariant Itô equation

\displaystyle\text{D}\tilde{W}_{t}^{k}(v,w)

\displaystyle=R(/\!/_{t}\,dB_{t},\tilde{Q}_{t}(k(t)v))\tilde{Q}_{t}(w)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}({\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}})^{\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{t}(k(t)v),\tilde{Q}_{t}(w))\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{2}N-R(N))^{\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{t}(k(t)v),\tilde{Q}_{t}(w))\,dl_{t}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}(\tilde{W}_{t}^{k}(v,w))\,dt+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla N)^{\sharp}(\tilde{W}_{t}^{k}(v,w))\,dl_{t},

(3.2)

with initial condition
\tilde{W}_{0}^{k}(v,w)=0
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
D
be a compact Riemannian manifold with
boundary
\partial D
. Let
X(x)
be the reflecting Brownian motion on
D
with
starting point
x
(possibly on the boundary) and denote by
P_{t}f(x)=\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t}(x)]

the corresponding Neumann semigroup acting on

Then, for
v\in T_{x}D
,
t\geq 0
and
k\in C^{1}_{b}([0,\infty);\mathbb{R})
,

\operatorname{Hess}{P_{t}f}(v,v)=\mathbb{E}\left[-df(\tilde{Q}_{t}(v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle+df(\tilde{W}_{t}^{k}(v,v))\right].

Estimating
\tilde{W}^{k}
and
\tilde{Q}
explicitly, we can get pointwise bounds
for the Hessian of Neumann eigenfunctions.

Corollary 3.2
.

3.1

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Hess}\phi|(x)\leq

\displaystyle\operatorname{e}^{(\frac{1}{2}\lambda+K_{0})t}\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{e}^{\sigma_{1}l_{t}}]\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}+K_{1}\sqrt{t}+\frac{K_{2}}{2}t\right)\|{\bf d}\phi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma_{2}}{2}\operatorname{e}^{(K_{0}+\frac{\lambda}{2})t}\mathbb{E}\left(\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}l_{t}}\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}l_{s}}\,dl_{s}\right)\|{\bf d}\phi\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

By
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
the Hessian of the semigroup can be estimated as

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Hess}{P_{t}f}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(K_{1}\sqrt{t}+\frac{K_{2}}{2}t+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{e}^{\sigma_{1}l_{t}}\right]\operatorname{e}^{K_{0}t}\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sigma_{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}l_{t}}\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}l_{s}}\,dl_{s}\right)\operatorname{e}^{K_{0}t}\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}.

We complete the proof by observing that
P_{t}\phi=\operatorname{e}^{-\lambda t/2}\phi
.
∎

Combining Theorem
3.2
and Lemma
2.7
, we are now in a position to prove
Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
D
be an
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold with
boundary
\partial D
. Let
K_{0},K_{1},K_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
be non-negative constants such that
{\operatorname{Ric}}\geq-K_{0}
,
|R|\leq K_{1}
and

|{\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}|\leq K_{2}
on
D
, and that

{\operatorname{II}}\geq-\sigma_{1}
and
|\nabla^{2}N-R(N)|\leq\sigma_{2}
on the
boundary
\partial D
. For
h\in C^{\infty}(D)
with

\min_{D}h=1
and
N\log h|_{\partial D}\geq 1
, let

K_{h,\alpha}:=\sup_{D}\{-\Delta\log h+\alpha|\nabla\log h|^{2}\}

with
\alpha
a non-negative constant. Then for any non-trivial

(\phi,\lambda)\in\operatorname{Eig}_{N}(\Delta)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\|\operatorname{Hess}\,\phi\|_{\infty}}{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}\leq C_{N,\lambda}(D)\lambda

where

\displaystyle C_{N,\lambda}(D)=

\displaystyle\operatorname{e}\left(1+\frac{K_{1}+2K_{0}+2\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}}{\lambda}+\frac{K_{2}+2\sigma_{2}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}}{\lambda\sqrt{2\lambda+4K_{0}+4\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}}}\right)\|h\|_{\infty}^{3\sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma_{2}\operatorname{e}}{\lambda}\sqrt{2\lambda+4K_{0}+4\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}}\|h\|_{\infty}^{3\sigma_{1}}\ln\|h\|_{\infty};

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{e}^{\sigma_{1}l_{t}}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{e}^{\sigma_{1}l_{t}}]\leq\|h\|_{\infty}^{2\sigma_{1}}\exp\left(\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}t\right),

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{e}^{\sigma_{1}l_{t}}]\leq\|h\|_{\infty}^{2\sigma_{1}}\exp\left(\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}t\right).

Moreover, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}l_{t}}\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}l_{s}}\,dl_{s}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{e}^{(\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)l_{t}}]-1)}{\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon}\left(\|h\|_{\infty}^{2(\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)}\exp\left((\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)K_{h,2(\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)}t\right)-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon}\left(\|h\|_{\infty}^{2(\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)}\exp\left((\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)K_{h,(\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)}t\right)-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon}\left(\|h\|_{\infty}^{2(\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)}-1\right)+\frac{2}{\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon}\|h\|_{\infty}^{2(\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)}\left[\exp\left((\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)K_{h,2(\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)}t\right)-1\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4\|h\|_{\infty}^{2(\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)}\ln\|h\|_{\infty}+2\|h\|_{\infty}^{2(\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)}\exp\left((\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)K_{h,2(\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)}t\right)K_{h,2(\sigma_{1}+\varepsilon)}t.

Letting
\varepsilon
tend to
0
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}l_{t}}\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}l_{s}}\,dl_{s}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4\|h\|_{\infty}^{2\sigma_{1}}\ln\|h\|_{\infty}+2\|h\|_{\infty}^{2\sigma_{1}}\exp\left(\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}t\right)K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}t.

Therefore, combining this with Theorem
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}}{\|{\bf d}\phi\|_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{e}^{(\frac{1}{2}\lambda+K_{0})t}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}+K_{1}\sqrt{t}+\frac{K_{2}}{2}t\right)\|h\|_{\infty}^{2\sigma_{1}}\exp\left(\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}t\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma_{2}\operatorname{e}^{(\frac{1}{2}\lambda+K_{0})t}\left[2\ln\|h\|_{\infty}+K_{h,\sigma_{1}}t\right]\|h\|_{\infty}^{2\sigma_{1}}\exp\left(\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}t\right)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{e}^{(\frac{1}{2}\lambda+K_{0})t}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}+K_{1}\sqrt{t}+\frac{K_{2}}{2}t\right)\|h\|_{\infty}^{2\sigma_{1}}\exp\left(\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}t\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma_{2}\operatorname{e}^{(\frac{1}{2}\lambda+K_{0})t}\left[2\ln\|h\|_{\infty}+K_{h,\sigma_{1}}t\right]\|h\|_{\infty}^{2\sigma_{1}}\exp\left(\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}t\right).

Let
t=\left(\lambda+2K_{0}+2\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}\right)^{-1}
. Then we get

\displaystyle\frac{\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}}{\|{\bf d}\phi\|_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\frac{K_{1}}{\sqrt{\lambda+2K_{0}+2\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}}}+\sqrt{\lambda+2K_{0}+2\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{K_{2}+2\sigma_{2}K_{h,\sigma_{1}}}{2(\lambda+2K_{0}+2\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}})}+2\sigma_{2}\ln\|h\|_{\infty}\Bigg)\|h\|_{\infty}^{2\sigma_{1}}\sqrt{\operatorname{e}}.

On the other hand, from
[
2
]
, it has already been shown that

\displaystyle\frac{\|{\bf d}\phi\|_{\infty}}{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{e}^{\sigma_{1}l_{t}}]^{1/2}\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}(K_{0}+\lambda)t}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\|h\|_{\infty}^{\sigma_{1}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}(\lambda+\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}+K_{0})t\right).

Let
t=\left(\lambda+K_{0}+\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}\right)^{-1}
. Then we get

\displaystyle\frac{\|{\bf d}\phi\|_{\infty}}{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}\leq\sqrt{\lambda+K_{0}+\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}}\|h\|_{\infty}^{\sigma_{1}}\sqrt{\operatorname{e}}.

We then conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{\|\operatorname{Hess}\phi\|_{\infty}}{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\lambda+K_{1}+2K_{0}+2\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}+\frac{K_{2}+2\sigma_{2}K_{h,\sigma_{1}}}{2\sqrt{\lambda+2K_{0}+2\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sigma_{2}\ln\|h\|_{\infty}\sqrt{\lambda+K_{0}+\sigma_{1}K_{h,2\sigma_{1}}}\Bigg)\|h\|_{\infty}^{3\sigma_{1}}\operatorname{e}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

From the conditions we see that Condition
{\bf(A)}
is satisfied.
Then, the Hessian estimate of Neumann eigenfunctions in Theorem
3.3
remain valid by substituting the
h
defined in (
2.26
). Then under replacing

K_{h,\alpha}\ \mbox{ and }\ \|h\|_{\infty}\

by

{K}_{\alpha}:=\frac{n}{r_{0}}+\alpha\ \mbox{ and }\ \operatorname{e}^{nr_{0}/2}

respectively, the conclusion is just listed in Theorem
1.3
.
∎

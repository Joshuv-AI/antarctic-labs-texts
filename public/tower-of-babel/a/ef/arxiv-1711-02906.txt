On foliations by curves with singularities of positive dimension
Arturo Fernández-Pérez and Gilcione Nonato Costa

Abstract
. We present enumerative results for holomorphic foliations by curves on ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} , n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , with singularities of positive dimension. Some of the results presented improve previous ones due to Corrêa–Fernández-Pérez–Nonato Costa–Vidal Martins [ 2 ] and Nonato Costa [ 11 ] . We also present an enumerative result bounding the number of isolated singularities in a projective subvariety invariant by a holomorphic foliation by curves on ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} with a singularity of positive dimension.

1.
Introduction and statement of results

The purpose of the present paper is to establish numerical relations between the invariants of a foliation by curves
{\mathcal{F}}

on the complex projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and the characteristic classes
of the irreducible components of its singular set
{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}})
. By
numerical invariants of a foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
, we refer to Chern
numbers of its tangent sheaf, the order of vanishing and the
multiplicity of
{\mathcal{F}}
along its singularities. Since any foliation
by curves on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with singular set of positive dimension can be
deformed in to a foliation by curves with isolated singularities

[
16
]
, we address the problem of determining, in terms of
Milnor number, the numerical contribution that each irreducible
component of the singular set offers after being deformed.

More precisely, a holomorphic foliation by curves
{\mathcal{F}}
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, of degree
k\geq 1
, is defined by a morphism

\Phi:\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1-k)\to T\mathbb{P}^{n}

whose singular set
{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}}):=\{p:\Phi(p)=0\}
has codimension at
least two. Outside the singular set of
{\mathcal{F}}
, the morphism
\Phi

induces an integrable distribution
\mathcal{D}
of tangent
directions. By definition, the leaves of
{\mathcal{F}}
are the leaves
induced by
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}\setminus{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}})
. We call

\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{F}}}:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1-k)
the
tangent sheaf
of

{\mathcal{F}}
. It follows from the definition that
{\mathcal{F}}
can be
represented in affine coordinates
(z_{1},...,z_{n})
by a vector field
of the form

X(z)=\sum^{k}_{j=0}X_{j}(z)+g(z)\cdot R(z)

where
R(z)=\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{i=1}z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
,

g\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
is homogeneous of degree
k
and

X_{j}
,
0\leq j\leq k
, is a vector field whose components are
homogenous polynomials of degree
j
.

Let
W
be a projective subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of codimension
d\geq 2
. Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
be the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
along
W
, and
\pi:\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}

the blowup morphism with exceptional divisor

. Here
and subsequently

denotes the strict

{\mathcal{F}}
by
\pi
and

denotes the order of

\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
along

. It is easy to check that
tangent sheaf of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
is

\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{{\mathcal{F}}}}=\pi^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{F}}})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(\ell E).

A holomorphic foliation by curves
{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
it said to be

along

, if

is an invariant set of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
, and
{\rm Sing}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}})
meets

in at most isolated singularities
. Special foliation along curves has been studied for

in

[
11
]
and for
n>3
in
[
2
]
and
[
12
]
.
As we will see below, the notion of speciality of a foliation is
fundamental to the study of the irreducible components of the
singular set of a foliation by curves.

In what follows
\mathcal{W}^{(d)}_{\delta}
and
\sigma_{i}^{(d)}
are
the Wronski (or complete symmetric) and elementary symmetric
functions of degree
\delta
in
d

\mathcal{W}^{(d)}_{\delta}:=\mathcal{W}^{(d)}_{\delta}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})=\sum_{i_{1}+\ldots+i_{d}=\delta}k^{i_{1}}_{1}\ldots k^{i_{d}}_{d}

and

When

\mathbb{P}^{n}
of multidegree

, we will use the symbol

to denote

(1)

\displaystyle\nu({\mathcal{F}},W)=-\deg(W)\sum_{|a|=0}^{n-d}\sum_{m=0}^{n-d-|a|}(-1)^{\delta_{|a|}^{m}}\frac{\varphi_{a}^{(m)}(\ell)}{m!}(\deg({\mathcal{F}}-1)^{m}\sigma_{a_{1}}^{(d)}\tau_{a_{2}}^{(d)}\mathcal{W}_{\delta_{|a|}^{m}}^{(d)},

{\mathcal{F}}
. To complete our notation, we introduce the notion of

of an irreducible component of
W

{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}})

into a foliation by curves

(for some

\mathbb{P}^{n}

is invariant by
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}

is

isolated points
\{p_{i}^{t},i=1,\ldots,s_{t}\}\subset{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{t})

With the above notation, we state the main result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a holomorphic foliation by curves on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
,
of degree
k
. Suppose that its singular set
{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}})
is the
disjoint union of smooth scheme-theoretic complete intersection

of pure codimension

,
respectively, and points

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\mu({\mathcal{F}},p_{i})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}k^{i}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\nu({\mathcal{F}},W_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{r}N({\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{A}_{W_{i}}),

where
N({\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{A}_{W_{i}})
denotes the number (counted with multiplicity) of embedding closed points associated to each
W_{i}
.

It follows easily that the numerical contribution of each
W_{i}
is exactly
N({\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{A}_{W_{i}})-\nu({\mathcal{F}},W_{i})
. In this sense, it can be interpret it as the Milnor number of
{\mathcal{F}}
at
W_{i}
,

(2)

\mu({\mathcal{F}},W_{i}):=N({\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{A}_{W_{i}})-\nu({\mathcal{F}},W_{i}).

In general, for an arbitrary foliation with non-isolated singularities on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
it seems to be difficult to determine effectively
N({\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{A}_{W_{i}})
.
In the final section of the paper, we present a family of foliations
by curves on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
where it is possible to obtain an upper bound to
this number.

On the other hand, to prove Theorem
1
we need prove the following result.

Theorem 2
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a holomorphic foliation by curves on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
,
of degree
k
. Suppose that its singular set
{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}})
is the
disjoint union of smooth scheme-theoretic complete intersection
subvarieties
W_{1},\ldots,W_{r}
of pure codimension
d_{j}\geq 2
,
respectively, and points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}
. If
{\mathcal{F}}
is special
along each

.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\mu({\mathcal{F}},p_{i})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}k^{i}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\nu({\mathcal{F}},W_{i}).

In particular,

\mu({\mathcal{F}},W_{i})=-\nu({\mathcal{F}},W_{i}).

Note that the speciality condition implies that the set of embedding closed points of subvarieties

is empty, for all
i=1,\ldots,r
.
Moreover, in this case,
\mu({\mathcal{F}},W_{i})
is explicitly determined and
depends of the invariants of
{\mathcal{F}}
and
W
. Since we work with
irreducible components of
{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}})
of any dimension, it is
clear that Theorem
2
generalizes Theorem 1 of

[
2
]
and the main result of
[
11
]
.

Now, in the same spirit, the paper also addresses the following question: given a foliation by curves
{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,

, and a smooth algebraic subvariety

.
Assuming that the restriction foliation

The above question was completely solved by Marcio Soares

It is known from

and

of

[
15
]

and the references within. Of course, in the case

our expectation is proving a similar result.

Let
V=Z(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
be a smooth complete intersection on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
be the strict transform of
{\mathcal{F}}
by the blowup

.
Let

. Set

,

and

with

Let

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we focus on some basic definitions and known results of holomorphic foliations by curves on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

2.1.
Milnor number of an isolated singularity

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a holomorphic foliation by curves on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
p
a
singular point of
{\mathcal{F}}
. Without loss of generality we can suppose
that
p=0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
{\mathcal{F}}
is defined, in a neighborhood of
p
,
by a polynomial vector field

X_{p}=\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}P_{j}(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial{z_{n}}},
where

P_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
. The
Milnor number
of
{\mathcal{F}}
at

p
, denoted by
\mu({\mathcal{F}},p)
, is the
\mathbb{C}
-dimension of the local
algebra associated to
X_{p}
,

\mu({\mathcal{F}},p)={\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n,0}}{\displaystyle\langle P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\rangle}

where
\mathcal{O}_{n,0}
is the ring of germs of holomorphic
functions at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. It is well-known that this definition is
independent of the vector field representing
{\mathcal{F}}
at
p
.

We say that
{\mathcal{F}}
is
non-degenerate
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
if
{\mathcal{F}}
has only isolated singularities and the Milnor number of a vector field defining the
foliation around each singularity is 1. The Jouanolou’s foliation
\mathcal{J}_{k}
of degree
k\geq 1
, given by the vector field in affine coordinates
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})

(3)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum^{k}_{j=1}z_{i}z_{1}^{k}-z^{k}_{i+1}\Big)\frac{\partial}{\partial{z}_{i}}+(z_{n}z_{1}^{k}-1)\frac{\partial}{\partial{z}_{n}}

is an example of a non-degenerate foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Now if
{\mathcal{F}}
is non-degenerate foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
then it follows from the classical Baum-Bott formula
[
1
]
that

\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}^{n}}\mu({\mathcal{F}},p)=k^{n}+k^{n-1}+\ldots+k+1.

2.2.
Multiplicity along subvarieties

We describe the multiplicity of
{\mathcal{F}}
along an irreducible variety

W
of pure codimension
{\rm cod}(W)=d
, with
d\geq 2
. From now on we
make the assumption
W\subset{\rm Sing}(\mathcal{F})
. By a holomorphic change of
coordinates,
W
can be locally given as
\{z_{1}=\ldots=z_{d}=0\}
.
In this neighborhood,
{\mathcal{F}}
is described by the vector field

(4)

\mathcal{D}_{{\mathcal{F}}}=\displaystyle P_{1}(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}+\ldots+\displaystyle P_{n}(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{n}}.

Therefore, one may write the local sections as

(5)

P_{i}(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{|a|=m_{i}}z_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots z_{d}^{a_{d}}P_{i,a}(z)

where
a:=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
with
|a|:=a_{1}+\ldots+a_{d}
,

a_{i}\geq 0
and at least one among the
P_{i,a}(z)
does not vanish at

\{z_{1}=\ldots=z_{d}=0\}
. We define the multiplicity of each
function
P_{i}
along
W
as
m_{W}(P_{i})=m_{i}
. As a consequence, the

multiplicity of
{\mathcal{F}}
along
W
is defined as

(6)

m_{W}({\mathcal{F}})={\rm min}\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\}.

In order to simplify our analysis, we use the following Lemma:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a holomorphic foliation by curves,
defined in the neighborhood of
p
by the polynomial vector field

X_{p}=\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}P_{j}(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial{z_{j}}}

where
m_{i}=m_{W}(P_{i})
. Then, by a linear change of coordinates,

{\mathcal{F}}
may be described by the polynomial vector field

Y_{p}=\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}Q_{j}(w)\frac{\partial}{\partial{z_{j}}}

with

m_{W}(Q_{j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m^{\prime}_{W}({\mathcal{F}}),&{\rm for}\quad j=1,\ldots,d\cr m_{W}({\mathcal{F}}),&{\rm for}\quad j=d+1,\ldots,n\end{array}\right.

where
m^{\prime}_{W}({\mathcal{F}})={\rm min}\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\}
.

Proof.

In fact, it is enough to consider the linear
transformation
w=Az
where
A=(a_{ij})\in GL(n,\mathbb{C})
with
a_{ij}=0

for
i=1,\ldots,d
and
j=d+1,\ldots,n
. In this way,
B=(b_{ij})=A^{-1}
has the same properties, i.e.; the subspace
W
given by

\{z_{1}=\ldots=z_{d}=0\}
is an invariant set under this
transformation. Adjusting the coefficients
a_{ij}
, if necessary,
we can admit that

\dot{w}_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{ij}\dot{z}_{j}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{ij}P_{j}(Bw)=Q_{i}(w),&\mathrm{for}\quad i=1,\ldots,d\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\dot{z}_{j}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}P_{j}(Bw)=Q_{i}(w),&\mathrm{for}\quad i=d+1,\ldots,n\end{array}\right.

having the required properties.
∎

along

\displaystyle=P_{j}(\sigma_{1}(u))=\displaystyle\sum_{|a|=q_{j}}u_{1}^{a_{1}}(u_{1}u_{2})^{a_{2}}\cdots(u_{1}u_{d})^{a_{d}}P_{j,a}(\sigma_{1}(u))

But,

Finally, for

for some functions
\widetilde{P_{j}}

2.5.
Chern Class

Let
\pi:\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, be the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
centered
at
W
, with exceptional divisor denoted by
E
. Set

N:=N_{W/\mathbb{P}^{n}}
and
\rho:=\pi|_{E}
. Since
E\cong\mathbb{P}(N)
,
recall that
A(E)
is generated as an
A({\mathcal{C}})
-algebra by the
Chern class

\zeta:=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{N}(-1))

with the single relation

(20)

\zeta^{d}-\rho^{*}c_{1}(N)\zeta^{d-1}+\ldots+(-1)^{d-1}\rho^{*}c_{d-1}(N)\zeta+(-1)^{d}\rho^{*}c_{d}(N)=0.

The normal bundle
N_{E/\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}
agrees with the tautological bundle

\mathcal{O}_{N}(-1)
, and hence

(21)

\zeta=c_{1}(N_{E/\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}).

If
\iota:E\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is the inclusion map, we also get

(22)

\iota_{*}(\zeta^{i})=(-1)^{i}E^{i+1}.

Now, from Porteous Theorem (cf.
[
9
]
,
[
3
]
), writing

c_{j}(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{n})
and
c_{j}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
for
c_{j}(T_{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{n}})
and
c_{j}(T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})
,
respectively, we have

(23)

c(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}^{n}})-\pi^{*}c(\mathbb{P}^{n})=\iota_{*}(\rho^{*}C(T_{W})\cdot\alpha)

where

\alpha=\frac{1}{\zeta}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\big[1-(1-\zeta)(1+\zeta)^{i}\big]\rho^{*}c_{d-i}(N).

In this way, we can rewrite
\alpha
,

(24)

\alpha=\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{d}\sum_{j=0}^{d-m}\bigg[{{d-m}\choose{j}}-{{d-m}\choose{j+1}}\bigg]\zeta^{j}\rho^{*}c_{m}(N)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{i}

where

(25)

\alpha_{i}=\sum_{m=0}^{i}\bigg[{{d-m}\choose{i-m}}-{{d-m}\choose{i-m+1}}\bigg]\zeta^{i-m}\rho^{*}c_{m}(N).

Writing
(\rho^{*}C(W)\cdot\alpha)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\beta_{j}
where

\beta_{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\alpha_{i}\rho^{*}c_{j-i}(T_{W}),

we get,

(26)

c_{j}(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{n})=\pi^{*}c_{j}(\mathbb{P}^{n})+\iota_{*}(\beta_{j-1}).

Now, defining
a=(a_{1},a_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, with
0\leq a_{1}:=m\leq d
,
0\leq a_{2}=j-1-i\leq n-d
and
|a|=a_{1}+a_{2}
, we can rewrite (
26
) in the following way

(27)

c_{j}(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}^{n}})=\pi^{*}c_{j}(\mathbb{P}^{n})+\displaystyle\sum_{|a|=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{j-|a|-1}\Gamma^{j}_{a}\cdot\rho^{*}c_{a_{1}}(N)\rho^{*}c_{a_{2}}(T_{W})E^{j-|a|}

where

(28)

\Gamma^{j}_{a}={{d-a_{1}}\choose{j-|a|-1}}-{{d-a_{1}}\choose{j-|a|}}.

Here we are assuming that

\displaystyle{{p}\choose{q}}=0
if
p<q
or
q<0
.

Now, let
i:W\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a closed embedding of
W
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. From the exact sequence

0\to T_{W}\to i^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\to N\to 0

one has

c(T_{W})=c(i^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})/c(N).

If
W
is an intersection of divisors
D_{1},\ldots,D_{d}
, then

c(N)=\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+c_{1}(i^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(D_{i})))=\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+k_{j}\mathbf{h})=\sum c_{i}(N)

where
\mathbf{h}
is the class of a hyperplane of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
k_{j}

is the
deg(D_{j})
. Therefore,

(29)

c_{i}(N)=\sigma_{i}^{(d)}\cdot{\mathbf{h}}^{i}.

From the projection formula one deduces that

c(T_{W})=\displaystyle\frac{(1+\mathbf{h})^{n+1}}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+k_{j}\mathbf{h})}=\sum c_{i}(T_{W}).

Consequently,

(30)

c_{i}(T_{W})=\tau_{i}^{(d)}\cdot\mathbf{h}^{i}.

Repeatedly applying the equation (
20
), we get

(31)

\zeta^{m}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\rho^{*}(\beta_{m,i}\cdot\mathbf{h}^{m-d+i})\zeta^{d-i}

where

\beta_{m,i}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}^{(d)}\cdot\mathcal{W}_{m-d+i-j}^{(d)}

for
m\geq d
. In fact, it is clear for
m=d
because

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}^{(d)}\cdot\mathcal{W}_{m-j}^{(d)}=0,\quad m>0

and
\beta_{d,i}=(-1)^{i-1}\sigma_{i}^{(d)}
. Assuming that the
induction hypothesis is true for
m\geq d
, we get

\displaystyle\zeta^{m+1}

\displaystyle=\zeta\cdot\zeta^{m}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\rho^{*}(\beta_{m,i}\cdot\mathbf{h}^{m-d+i})\zeta^{d-i+1}

\displaystyle=\rho^{*}(\beta_{m,1}\cdot\mathbf{h}^{m-d+1})\zeta^{d}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\rho^{*}(\beta_{m,i+1}\cdot\mathbf{h}^{m-d+i+1})\zeta^{d-i}.

Again, from (
20
), we yields

\displaystyle\beta_{m+1,i}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i-1}\sigma_{i}^{(d)}\cdot\mathcal{W}_{m-d+1}^{(d)}+\beta_{m,i+1}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}^{(d)}\cdot\mathcal{W}_{m+1-d+i-j}^{(d)}.

In particular,
\beta_{m,1}=\mathcal{W}_{m-d+1}^{(d)}
which results

(32)

\displaystyle\int_{E}\rho^{*}c_{i}(N)\zeta^{n-i-1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-1)^{d-1}\sigma_{i}^{(d)}\mathcal{W}_{n-d-i}^{(d)}\deg(W),&0\leq i\leq n-d\cr 0,&i>n-d.\end{array}\right.

2.6.
Embedded closed points

Let
{\mathcal{F}}ol(\mathbb{P}^{n};k)
be the space of holomorphic foliations by curves of degree
k\geq 1
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
Denote by
N(\mathbb{P}^{n};k)\subset{\mathcal{F}}ol(\mathbb{P}^{n};k)
the set of non-degenerate
foliations of degree
k
. Since the Jouanolou’s foliation given in
(
3
) is non-degenerated, we have that
N(\mathbb{P}^{n};k)
is not
empty. The following result guarantees that a foliation by curves
with singularities of positive dimension can be deformed to a
non-degenerated foliation of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Theorem 2.3

(Soares
[
16
]
)
.

N(\mathbb{P}^{n};k)
is open, dense and connected in
{\mathcal{F}}ol(\mathbb{P}^{n};k)
.

We note that this result can not be used to determine the
Milnor numbers
of an irreducible component of
{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}})
,
because the deformation is not explicit. In order to prove Theorem

1
, we use the notion of
embedded closed points
associated to
W
. In fact, we will see that it is possible to
deform
{\mathcal{F}}
to a foliation by curves
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
(for some
small
0<|t|<\epsilon
) such that, either
W
is
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
-invariant
or else
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
is special along
W
(see for instance Lemma

3.1
). Therefore, these isolated singularities that
appear after this deformation and do not lie in
W
will be called

embedded closed points associated to
W
. More precisely,
the set of embedded closed points of
W
is

(33)

A_{W}:=\{p^{t}_{j}\in{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{t}):\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}p^{t}_{j}\in W,\quad\text{such that}\quad p^{t}_{j}\not\in W\quad\forall t\neq 0\}.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a non-empty subset. Here and subsequently
N({\mathcal{F}},X)
denotes the number of isolated singularities of
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
(counted with multiplicity). Hence,

N({\mathcal{F}},A_{W})=\sum_{q^{t}_{j}\in A_{W}}\mu({\mathcal{F}}_{t},q^{t}_{j}).

Now, suppose that
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is a smooth irreducible complete intersection defined by

V=Z(F_{1},\ldots,F_{n-m})

where
F_{l}\in\mathbb{C}[z_{0},\ldots,z_{n}]
is homogeneous of degree
d_{l}
,
1\leq l\leq n-m
. In the proof of Theorem
3
, we will use the following result due to Soares in

[
14
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume that
V
is invariant by
{\mathcal{F}}
, where
{\mathcal{F}}
is a foliation
by curves of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of degree
k
, non-degenerate along
V
. Then the
number of singularities of
{\mathcal{F}}
in
V
is

N({\mathcal{F}},V)=(d_{1}\ldots d_{n-m})\sum^{m}_{j=0}\left[\sum^{j}_{\delta=0}(-1)^{\delta}\mathcal{W}^{(n-m)}_{\delta}(d_{1}-1,\ldots,d_{n-m}-1)\right]k^{m-j},

where
\mathcal{W}^{(n-m)}_{\delta}
is the Wronski of degree
\delta
and
n-m
variables.

3.
Deformation of a foliation by curves adapted to singular set

We use the following fundamental lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a holomorphic foliation by curves on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, of degree
k
. Suppose that

\mbox{Sing}({\mathcal{F}})=W\cup\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}\}

is a disjoint union where
W=Z(h_{1},\ldots,h_{d})
is a smooth
complete intersection of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
p_{j}
are isolated points. Let

\ell=m_{E}({\mathcal{F}},W)
. Suppose that

V=Z(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})

is
{\mathcal{F}}
-invariant such that
f_{i}\in\mathcal{I}(W)
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Then,
W\subset V
and
there exists a one-parameter family of holomorphic foliations by
curves on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, given by
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\}_{t\in D}
where

D=\{t\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,|t|<\epsilon\}
satisfying:

(i)

{\mathcal{F}}_{0}={\mathcal{F}}
;

(ii)

\deg({\mathcal{F}}_{t})=\deg({\mathcal{F}})
;

(iii)

If
\ell=0
then
{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{t})=\{p_{1}^{t},\ldots,p_{s_{t}}^{t}\}
, if
\ell>0
then
{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{t})=W\cup\{p_{1}^{t},\ldots,p_{s_{t}}^{t}\}
, disjoint union where
p_{i}^{t}
are
isolated points, for any
t\in D\setminus\{0\}
;

(iv)

If
\ell=0
then
W
is
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
-invariant, for any
t\in D\setminus\{0\}
;

(v)

If
\ell>0
then
W\subset{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{t})
,
\ell=m_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{t},W)
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
is special along
W
for any
t\in D\setminus\{0\}
;

(vi)

V
is
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
-invariant, for any
t\in D
.

Proof.

For simplicity we assume that
W
is given in the affine coordinates

(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
by the zeros of the polynomials

h_{1},\ldots,h_{d}
and
V
is given by the zeros of the
polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
, with
f_{i}\in\mathcal{I}(W)
. Now
let us consider the holomorphic function

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}F\ :&\mathbb{C}^{n}\ &\longrightarrow&\mathbb{C}^{n}\\
&z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})&\longmapsto&\bigg(h_{1}(z)\,,\,\ldots\,,\,h_{d}(z)\,,z_{d+1},\ldots,\,z_{n}\bigg).\end{matrix}

By hypotheses we get

\begin{array}[]{cccc}f_{1}=&\lambda_{11}h_{1}&+\ldots+&\lambda_{1d}h_{d}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
f_{m}=&\lambda_{m1}h_{1}&+\ldots+&\lambda_{md}h_{d},\end{array}

where
\lambda_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq d
.
Without loss of generality, since
W
is smooth, we can admit
that
Det(M(z))\neq 0
where
M(z)=\left(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial z_{j}}\right)
is the
d\times d
-minor of the Jacobian matrix
D_{z}F

for
z
in some open set
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Thus, note that
F|_{U}

is a local biholomorphism onto an open set

\widetilde{U}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and restricted to
\widetilde{U}
so one
may fix coordinates
w=F(z)
.

Then the image of
W\cap U
by
F
is the hyperplane of codimension

d
,

H_{d}:=\{(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:w_{1}=\ldots=w_{d}=0\}

while the image of
V\cap U
by
F
is given by

H_{m}:=\{(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{ij}w_{j}=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq m\}.

To continue we describe the push-forward
(F)_{*}{\mathcal{F}}
. We write

\mathcal{D}_{(F)_{*}{\mathcal{F}}}=P_{1}(w)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{1}}+\ldots+P_{n}(w)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{n}}

where

(34)

P_{i}(w)=\sum_{|a|=m_{i}}w_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots w_{d}^{a_{d}}P_{i,a}(w)

with at least one
P_{i,a}(z)
not vanishing in
H_{d}
. Therefore we define
{\mathcal{G}}_{t}
by the vector field

(35)

\mathcal{D}_{{\mathcal{G}}_{t}}=\mathcal{D}_{(F)_{*}{\mathcal{F}}}+t\cdot Y

where

Y=Q_{1}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{1}}+\ldots+Q_{n}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{n}}

with

Q_{j}(w)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{|I|=q_{j}}a_{I,j}w_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots w_{d}^{i_{d}}&\text{if}\qquad 1\leq j\leq d\\
\displaystyle\sum_{|I|=q_{j}}R_{I,j}(w)w_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots w_{d}^{i_{d}}&\text{if}\qquad d+1\leq j\leq n.\end{cases}

where
a_{I,j}\in\mathbb{C}
,
R_{I,j}
are affine linear functions and

(36)

q_{1}=\ldots=q_{d}=q_{d+1}+1=\ldots=q_{n}+1=\ell+1.

One can choose
a_{I,j}
in way
that
H_{m}
are invariants by
Y
( rank-nullity theorem). If
\ell=0

then follows from (
35
) that
H_{d}
and
H_{m}
are
invariant by
{\mathcal{G}}_{t}
for any
t\in D\setminus\{0\}
, indeed

\mathcal{D}_{{\mathcal{G}}_{t}}|_{H_{d}}=\mathcal{D}_{(F)_{*}{\mathcal{F}}}|_{H_{d}}+t\cdot Y|_{H_{d}}=t\cdot Y|_{H_{d}}=t\sum_{j=d+1}^{n}R_{0,j}(w)\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{j}},

because
q_{d+1}=\ldots=q_{n}=0
.

Now, if
\ell>0
then
W\subset{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{G}}_{t})
and
\ell=m_{E}({\mathcal{G}}_{t},W)
for all
t\in D\setminus\{0\}
. Again,
H_{m}
is also invariant by

{\mathcal{G}}_{t}
.

Denoting by
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
the pull-back foliation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{t}
by
F
, we
have that
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
define a one-dimensional singular holomorphic
foliation in
U
. Indeed, this foliation is given by a vector field

\mathcal{D}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{t}}=P^{t}_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}+\ldots+P^{t}_{n}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{n}}

where
P^{t}(z)=(P^{t}_{1}(z),\ldots,P^{t}_{n}(z))
are obtained by the
system

Q^{t}\circ F(z)=D_{z}F(z)\cdot P^{t}(z),

with

Q^{t}=(P_{1}+tQ_{1},\ldots,P_{n}+tQ_{n})
. Solving this system by Cramer’s
rule, we get

P^{t}_{i}(z)=\frac{\det(A^{t}_{i})}{\det(M)}

where one gets
A^{t}_{i}
replacing the
i
-th column of
DF
by the
vector column
Q^{t}\circ F(z)
. In particular,

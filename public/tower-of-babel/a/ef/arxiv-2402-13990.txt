General Theory of Static Response for Markov Jump Processes
Timur Aslyamov, Massimiliano Esposito

Abstract
We consider Markov jump processes on a graph described by a rate matrix that depends on various control parameters. We derive explicit expressions for the static responses of edge currents and steady-state probabilities. We show that they are constrained by the graph topology (i.e. the incidence matrix) by deriving response relations (i.e. linear constraints linking the different responses) and topology-dependent bounds. For unicyclic networks, all scaled current responses are between zero and one and must sum to one. Applying these results to stochastic thermodynamics, we derive explicit expressions for the static response of fundamental currents (which carry the full dissipation) to fundamental thermodynamic forces (which drive the system away from equilibrium).



Introduction.—
Nonequilibrium steady state (NESS) of Markov jump processes describe a plethora of phenomena
Zhang et al. 2012
and understanding their response to external perturbations has crucial implications
Ge et al. 2012
across fields, such as biology
Owen and Horowitz 2023
;
Cornish-Bowden 2013
;
Fell and Cornish-Bowden 1997
;
Murugan et al. 2014
, nanoelectronics
Freitas et al. 2021
;
Moreira et al. 2023
, and deep learning
Sohl-Dickstein et al. 2015
.
When the Markov jump process is produced by thermal noise, near equilibrium, the response is simple and characterized by the dissipation-fluctuation relation (DFR)
Kubo 1966
;
Govern and ten Wolde 2014
. But far-from-equilibrium or for non-thermal processes, the response is significantly more involved; see Refs.
Agarwal 1972
;
Seifert and Speck 2010
;
Prost et al. 2009
;
Altaner et al. 2016
;
Baiesi et al. 2009
;
Baiesi and Maes 2013
;
Hatano and Sasa 2001
;
Falasco et al. 2019
;
Shiraishi 2022
;
Dechant and Sasa 2020
;
Szabó and Fath 2007
;
Chun and Horowitz 2023
;
Gao et al. 2024
.
Recently, for the static response, exact results
Aslyamov and Esposito 2024
;
De Giuli 2023
;
Floyd et al. 2024
and tight bounds
Owen et al. 2020
;
Fernandes Martins and Horowitz 2023
have been derived assuming Arrhenius-like rates.
Moreover, the bounds
Aslyamov and Esposito 2024
only hold for local responses, i.e. when the perturbation and the observable are assigned to the same transition.

In this Letter, we build a general static response theory for any Markov jump processes, which describes both local and nonlocal responses for arbitrary parameterizations of the rate matrix. We identify a broad class of parameterizations that produce two types of linear constraints, which we call the Summation Response Relation (SRR) and Cycle Response Relation (CRR).
The SRR restricts the responses of the edge flux and probability. The form of such constraints does not depend on the topology of the incidence matrix, but the values of the responses involved strongly depend on it. The CRR limits the sum of local responses by the number of fundamental (Schnakenberg
Schnakenberg 1976
) cycles, which are essential topological characteristics. Moreover, the topology defines which static responses among all combinatorial configurations have universal (remarkably simple) bounds.
In unicyclic networks, all responses are bounded; for multicyclic systems, our approach identifies bounded and unbounded responses. In concrete examples considered, the sizes of bounded and unbounded sets are comparable.
Finally, for Markov jump processes describing a system in contact with thermal reservoirs (i.e. stochastic thermodynamics), we derive an explicit expression for the static response of fundamental currents to fundamental thermodynamic forces. The former characterize the full dissipation and the latter drive the system out-of-equilibrium.

Setup.
—We consider a directed graph
\mathcal{G}
with
N
nodes and
N_{e}
edges and a Markov jump process over the discrete set
\mathcal{S}
of the
N
states corresponding to the nodes. Then, the edges
e\in\mathcal{E}
of
\mathcal{G}
define possible transitions with the probability rates encoded in the rate matrix
\mathbb{W}/\tau
. In this description, the jump from
n
to
m
is the edge (arrow)
+e
with the source
s(+e)=n
and the tip
t(+e)=m
. For the reverse transition, we have
-e
with
s(-e)=m
and
t(-e)=n
. Choosing
\tau=1
the non-diagonal elements
W_{nm}=W_{e}\geq 0
become the probabilities per unit of time assigned to the edges
e
.
We assume that the matrix
\mathbb{W}
is irreducible
Van Kampen 1992
and that all transitions are reversible, i.e.
W_{e}\neq 0
only if
W_{-e}\neq 0
.
With the property of diagonal elements
W_{ii}=-\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij}
the described system always exhibits a unique steady-state probability
\bm{\pi}=(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{N})^{\intercal}
that satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}\cdot\bm{\pi}

\displaystyle=\bm{0}\,,

(1)

with the normalization
\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\pi_{i}}=1
. We define the transition current along the edge
e
as
j_{e}\equiv W_{+e}\pi_{s(+e)}-W_{-e}\pi_{s(-e)}
. This definition has a matrix form
\bm{j}=\mathbb{\Gamma}\bm{\pi}
,
where the matrix
\mathbb{\Gamma}
has elements
\Gamma_{ei}\equiv W_{+e}\delta_{is(+e)}-W_{-e}\delta_{is(-e)}
with the Kronecker symbol
\delta
.
In analogy to linear chemical reaction networks
Gunawardena 2012
;
Srinivas et al. 2023
;
Aslyamov et al. 2023
, the rate matrix can be decomposed as
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{S}\mathbb{\Gamma}
, where
\mathbb{S}
is the incidence matrix of the directed graph
\mathcal{G}
with the elements
S_{ie}\equiv\delta_{is(-e)}-\delta_{is(+e)}
.

We introduce a parameterization
\mathbb{W}(\bm{p})
using the vector
\bm{p}=(\dots,p,\dots)^{\intercal}
made of
N_{p}
control parameters
p\in\bm{p}
.
Thus, the steady-state condition (
1
) can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}\cdot\bm{j}(\bm{p})=\bm{0}\,,

(2)

where
\bm{j}(\bm{p})\equiv\bm{j}(\bm{p},\bm{\pi}(\bm{p}))
depends on
\bm{p}
both explicitly due to
\mathbb{\Gamma}(\bm{p})
and implicitly due to
\bm{\pi}(\bm{p})
. The central objects of this work are the static responses of a quantity
q(\bm{p})
(e.g. a probability
\bm{\pi}
or a current
\bm{j}
) to perturbations of the parameters
p
, i.e. the elements of the vector
\nabla_{\bm{p}}q(\bm{p})=(\dots,d_{p}q,\dots)^{\intercal}
.

Our goal will be to obtain
explicit
relations for these responses enabling us to find relations among them.
Many recent works considered edge perturbations where each element of
\bm{p}
acts on a rate associated with a given edge
Aslyamov and Esposito 2024
;
Harunari et al. 2024
;
Zheng and Lu 2024
;
Floyd et al. 2024
;
De Giuli 2023
. But often, perturbing physical quantities implies acting on rates associated with many edges.
In this letter, after an example, we go from generic perturbations to more specific ones, illustrating our findings at every stage using a physical model.

Homogeneous parameterization.—
We first provide a simple illustration of how a given parameterization can give rise to nontrivial relations amongst static responses, let us consider the parameterization
\bm{h}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\dots,h_{p},\dots)^{\intercal}}
of
\mathbb{W}(\bm{h})
such that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}(\alpha\bm{h})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{k}\mathbb{W}(\bm{h})\,,

(3)

where
k
is the positive order of the homogeneous function. The fact that
\mathbb{W}(\alpha\bm{h})\bm{\pi}(\alpha\bm{h})=\alpha^{k}\mathbb{W}(\bm{h})\bm{\pi}(\alpha\bm{h})=0
implies that
\bm{\pi}(\bm{h})=\bm{\pi}(\alpha\bm{h})
since the solution of
Eq.

1
is unique;
\bm{\pi}(\bm{h})
is therefore an homogeneous function of order zero of
\bm{h}
which implies the linear relation (Euler’s theorem for
k=0
)

\displaystyle\sum_{p}h_{p}\frac{d\bm{\pi}}{dh_{p}}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(4)

This in turn implies that the current is a homogeneous function
\bm{j}(\alpha\bm{h},\bm{\pi}(\alpha\bm{h}))=\alpha^{k}\bm{j}(\bm{h},\bm{\pi}(\bm{h}))
, which is equivalent to:

\displaystyle\sum_{p}h_{p}\frac{d\bm{j}}{dh_{p}}

\displaystyle=k\bm{j}\,.

(5)

Equations

4
and
5

are known in metabolic control analysis as summation theorems
Heinrich and Rapoport 1974
;
Kacser et al. 1995
;
Reder 1988
;
Heinrich and Schuster 2012
. In that context, enzyme concentrations play the role of the homogeneous parameters.

Matrix approach to static response.—
We now turn to arbitrary parameterizations. Our strategy is to use
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}=1
, and thus
d_{p}\pi_{N}=-\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}d_{p}\pi_{k}
, to arrive at
N-1
independent equations for others
d_{p}\pi_{k}
with
k\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}\equiv\mathcal{S}\setminus\{N\}
. To solve this linear problem, we introduce the matrix
\mathbb{K}\equiv\hat{\mathbb{S}}\hat{\mathbb{\Gamma}}
, where
\hat{\mathbb{S}}\equiv[S_{ke}]_{\{k,e\}}
and
\hat{\mathbb{\Gamma}}\equiv[\Gamma_{ek}-\Gamma_{eN}]_{\{e,k\}}
are reduced matrices with
k\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}
and
e\in\mathcal{E}
.
In Sec. A of

not

we prove that the matrix
\mathbb{K}
is invertible [see Eq. A5], and that the probability and current response matrices read:

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{\pi}

\displaystyle\equiv[d_{p}\pi_{i}]_{\{i,p\}}=-\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{K}^{-1}\hat{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{J}\\
-\sum_{k}(\mathbb{K}^{-1}\hat{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{J})^{\intercal}_{k}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(6a)

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{j}

\displaystyle\equiv[d_{p}j_{e}]_{\{e,p\}}=\mathbb{P}\mathbb{J}\,.

(6b)

Here,
\sum_{k}(\mathbb{K}^{-1}\hat{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{J})^{\intercal}_{k}
denotes the row that is the sum of all rows of the matrix
\mathbb{K}^{-1}\hat{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{J}
;
\{i,p\}
(resp.
\{e,p\}
) denote the sets of indexes
i\in\mathcal{S}
(resp.
e\in\mathcal{E}
) for rows and
p\in\bm{p}
for columns;
\mathbb{J}\equiv[\partial_{p}j_{e}]_{\{e,p\}}
is the steady state Jacobian

\displaystyle\mathbb{J}=[\pi_{s(+e)}\partial_{p}W_{e}(\bm{p})-\pi_{s(-e)}\partial_{p}W_{-e}(\bm{p})]_{\{e,p\}}\;,

(7)

and the matrix
\mathbb{P}=[P_{ee^{\prime}}]_{\{e,e^{\prime}\}}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\equiv\Big[\delta_{ee^{\prime}}-\sum_{x,x^{\prime}\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}}\hat{\Gamma}_{ex}(\mathbb{K}^{-1})_{xx^{\prime}}S_{x^{\prime}e^{\prime}}\Big]_{\{e,e^{\prime}\}}=\mathbb{I}-\hat{\mathbb{\Gamma}}(\hat{\mathbb{S}}\hat{\mathbb{\Gamma}})^{-1}\hat{\mathbb{S}}\,,

(8)

with
\mathbb{I}
denoting the identity matrix. Matrix
\mathbb{P}
is idempotent [
\mathbb{P}^{2}=\mathbb{P}
] and is known as an oblique projection matrix

.
Since
\hat{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{P}=0
, we define
\mathbb{B}
via

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\equiv\Big[\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{C}}c^{\gamma}_{e}B_{\gamma e^{\prime}}\Big]_{\{e,e^{\prime}\}}=\mathbb{C}\mathbb{B}\,,

(9)

where
\mathbb{C}=(\dots,\bm{c}_{\gamma},\dots)
is the matrix of the fundamental cycles
\bm{c}^{\gamma}
defined as the right null vectors of the incidence matrix
\mathbb{S}\bm{c}^{\gamma}=\bm{0}
(
\mathbb{S}
and
\hat{\mathbb{S}}
share the same
N_{c}
cycles).

Equation

6
is crucial in what follows.
It contains explicit expressions for the responses of all edges to arbitrary perturbations and contains information on how they are related to each other.

Response relations.—
For a vector
\bm{p}
such that
the matrix
\mathbb{J}
in
Eq.

7
is full row rank (
\text{rk}\mathbb{J}=N_{e}
, i.e.
N_{p}\geq N_{e}
), we can always find a right invertible matrix
\mathbb{J}^{+}
such that
\mathbb{J}\mathbb{J}^{+}=\mathbb{I}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{J}^{+}\equiv\mathbb{J}^{\intercal}(\mathbb{J}\mathbb{J}^{\intercal})^{-1}\,.

(10)

Multiplying both sides of
Eqs.

6a
and
6b
by the vector
\mathbb{J}^{+}\bm{j}
, we arrive at

\mathbb{R}^{\pi}\mathbb{J}^{+}\bm{j}=\bm{0}
and
\mathbb{R}^{j}\mathbb{J}^{+}\bm{j}=\mathbb{P}\bm{j}=\mathbb{I}\bm{j}
. In coordinate form, these relations give rise to the SRRs:

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\bm{p}}\phi_{p}d_{p}\pi_{i}

\displaystyle=0\,,\quad\sum_{p\in\bm{p}}\phi_{p}d_{p}j_{e}=j_{e}\,,

(11)

where
\phi_{p}
are elements of the vector

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathbb{J}^{+}\cdot\bm{j}\,.

(12)

Equations (
11
) generalize
Eqs.

4
and
5
beyond homogeneous parameters
with the only difference that the

.

The structure of

The SRR for the current in

Multicycle systems.—
Since the elements of

can always be written using

and noting that it is always possible to define cycles such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{B}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{c}\\
\tilde{\mathbb{C}}\end{pmatrix}=(\overline{\mathbb{B}},\tilde{\mathbb{B}})\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{c}\\

For edge parametrisation, the fact that the scaled responses are bounded [

For edge currents, it is equivalent to finding nondiagonal elements

\displaystyle\#\text{var}-\#\text{ide}=(N_{c}-1)(N-2)+(N_{e}-1-\#\text{ide})=0\,,

which is only possible if

(unicyclic models) or if

(two states models).

9
and

\displaystyle(\mathbb{I}_{c}-\tilde{\mathbb{B}}\tilde{\mathbb{C}})_{ee}

\displaystyle=P_{ee}\,,\quad e\leq N_{c}\,,

(22a)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\mathbb{C}}\tilde{\mathbb{B}})_{ee}

\displaystyle=P_{ee}\,,\quad e>N_{c}\,.

(22b)

This system of
\#\text{ide}
equations allows us to express
\#\text{ide}
elements

as a linear combination of the bounded diagonal elements
\{P_{ee}\}
.
The other elements

are not restricted by the bounds in

Thus, the elements

Thermodynamic responses.—
The rates can be expressed in terms of their symmetric
v_{e}\equiv\ln\sqrt{W_{+e}W_{-e}}=v_{-e}
and antisymmetric
w_{e}\equiv\ln\sqrt{W_{+e}/W_{-e}}=-w_{-e}
parts as
W_{\pm e}=\exp(v_{e}\pm w_{e})
.
For the QDs of
Fig.

1
,
v_{e}=\ln\Gamma_{e}
and
w_{e}=-\ln(1+\exp(\Psi_{e}))
.

This decomposition is used in stochastic thermodynamics (i.e. for open systems undergoing transitions caused by thermal reservoirs) because the antisymmetric part defines the energetics of the system
Rao and Esposito 2018
;
Falasco and Esposito 2023
:

\displaystyle\ln\frac{W_{+e}}{W_{-e}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}E_{i}S_{ie}+\mathcal{F}_{e}\,,\quad\mathcal{F}_{e}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}}X_{e\alpha}f_{\alpha}\,.

(23)

The l.h.s. of
Eq.

23
is the entropy change in the reservoirs caused by a transition
e
. It can always be split, in the r.h.s., as a change in the Massieu potential
E_{i}
of the state
i
and as a non-conservative contribution
\mathcal{F}_{e}=-\mathcal{F}_{-e}
.
The latter can be expressed, in
Eq.

23
, as a sum over a subset of reservoirs
\mathcal{R}
where each term consists
of an amount of conserved quantities exchanged with the reservoirs during a transition
e
,
X_{e\alpha}
, multiplied by a conjugated (fundamental
Rao and Esposito 2018
) thermodynamic force,
f_{\alpha}
made of differences of intensive fields of the reservoirs such as inverse temperatures or chemical potentials
Rao and Esposito 2018
.
Using
Eq.

23
and
Eq.

14
, we find

\displaystyle d_{f_{\alpha}}j_{e}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{E}}d_{f_{\alpha}}\mathcal{F}_{\rho}d_{\mathcal{F}_{\rho}}j_{e}=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{E}}X_{\rho\alpha}P_{e\rho}\partial_{\mathcal{F}_{\rho}}j_{\rho}\;,

(24)

which can be obtained analytically for known
\partial_{\Psi_{\rho}}j_{\rho}
. Since the (fundamental
Rao and Esposito 2018
) currents exchanged with the different reservoirs are the elements of the vector
\bm{I}=\mathbb{X}^{\intercal}j
, their response to the thermodynamic forces read

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{I}\equiv[d_{f_{\alpha^{\prime}}}I_{\alpha}]_{\{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\}}=\mathbb{X}^{\intercal}\mathbb{P}\mathbb{J}(\bm{\mathcal{F}})\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{X}^{\intercal}\mathbb{R}^{j}\mathbb{X}\,,

(25)

where
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
and
\mathbb{J}(\bm{\mathcal{F}})=\text{diag}(\dots,\partial_{\mathcal{F}_{e}}j_{e},\dots)
.

Close to equilibrium,
\mathbb{R}^{I}
reduces to the semi-positive definite Onsager matrix (Sec. F of
not
).
The lack of symmetry of
\mathbb{R}^{I}
can thus be measured experimentally as
|R^{I}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}-R^{I}_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha}|
and used to determine if the system is far from equilibrium.

Let us now assume that
w_{e}
is independent of
v_{e}
. This is relevant, for example, for Arrhenius-like rates
Aslyamov and Esposito 2024
, as well as for electron transfer rates in CMOS transistors
Gopal et al. 2022
or single electron tunneling rates in the wide-band approximation
Sánchez et al. 2019
.

Equation

23
shows that
w_{e}
depends only on the perturbation of the energy and forces, but does not depend on the perturbation of the kinetic parameters.
Such kinetic
v_{e}
and energy (thermodynamic forces)
w_{e}
perturbations will be constrained by
Eq.

11
.
Calculating the partial derivatives
\partial_{w_{e}}j_{e}=\tau_{e}
and
\partial_{v_{e}}j_{e}=j_{e}
, we find
\phi_{e}=j_{e}/\tau_{e}
(resp.
\phi_{e}=1
) for
w_{e}
(resp.
v_{e}
), where
\tau_{e}\equiv W_{e}\pi_{s(+e)}+W_{-e}\pi_{s(-e)}
is the edge traffic.
Inserting
\phi_{e}
into
Eq.

11
, we arrive at the Symmetric and Antisymmetric SRRs

\displaystyle\sum_{e}\frac{j_{e}}{\tau_{e}}d_{w_{e}}\bm{\pi}

\displaystyle=\bm{0}\,,\quad\sum_{e}d_{v_{e}}\bm{\pi}=\bm{0}\,,

(26a)

\displaystyle\sum_{e}\frac{j_{e}}{\tau_{e}}d_{w_{e}}\ln\bm{j}

\displaystyle=\bm{1}\,,\quad\sum_{e}d_{v_{e}}\ln\bm{j}=\bm{1}\,.

(26b)

We note that unlike the antisymmetric parameterisation, the symmetric one is homogeneous
\bm{h}=(\dots,v_{e},\dots)^{\intercal}
as the symmetric RSSs in
Eq.

26
coincide with
Eqs.

4
and
5
.

Appendix A
Derivation of Eq. 6.

Here we derive the matrix expressions (6) for the probability and current responses to an arbitrary parameterisation
\bm{p}
.
The total derivative
d_{p}
of Eq. (2) on
p\in\bm{p}
reads

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}(\partial_{p}\mathbb{\Gamma})\bm{\pi}+\mathbb{S}\mathbb{\Gamma}d_{p}\bm{\pi}=\bm{0}\,.

(27)

Using
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}=1
we find one of the derivatives, say the
N
th one, as
d_{p}\pi_{N}=-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}d_{p}\pi_{i}
. Inserting it in the coordinate form of
Eq.

27
we arrive at:

\displaystyle\footnotesize\sum_{j\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}S_{ie}(\Gamma_{ej}-\Gamma_{eN})d_{p}\pi_{j}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{S}}S_{ie}(\partial_{p}\Gamma_{ek})\pi_{k}\,,

(28)

where
\hat{\mathcal{S}}=\mathcal{S}\setminus\{N\}
denotes the space of
N-1
states, or

\displaystyle\footnotesize\sum_{j\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}}K_{ij}d_{p}\pi_{j}=-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}S_{ie}J_{ep}\,,

(29)

where
J_{ep}=\partial_{p}j_{e}
are elements of the Jacobian defined in Eq. (7) and where we introduced the matrix

\displaystyle\footnotesize\mathbb{K}

\displaystyle=\Big[\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}S_{ie}(\Gamma_{ej}-\Gamma_{eN})\Big]_{i,j\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}}=\hat{\mathbb{S}}\hat{\mathbb{\Gamma}}\,,

(30)

with

and
\hat{\mathbb{\Gamma}}=[\Gamma_{ej}-\Gamma_{eN}]_{e\in\mathcal{E},j\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}}

We proceed with the proof that the matrix
\mathbb{K}
is invertible.
The auxiliary matrix
\mathbb{K}_{N}
coincides with
\mathbb{W}
, except the
N
th row, which is the row of ones.
Therefore
\det\mathbb{K}_{N}=

\displaystyle\footnotesize\det\begin{pmatrix}[W_{m,n}]_{m\in\tilde{S},n\in\mathcal{S}}\\
\bm{1}^{\intercal}\end{pmatrix}=\det\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{K}&[W_{mN}]_{m\in\tilde{S}}\\
\bm{0}^{\intercal}&1\end{pmatrix}=\det\mathbb{K}\,.

(31)

Then, using the results of Ref.

for

, we arrive at:

\displaystyle\det\mathbb{K}=\det\mathbb{K}_{N}=\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{i}\neq 0\,,

(32)

where
\lambda_{i}
are nonzero eigenvalues of the matrix
\mathbb{W}
, which implies that
\mathbb{K}
is invertible.

Multiplying
Eq.

29
by
\mathbb{K}^{-1}
, we find the responses

and rewriting the result in matrix form, we arrive at Eq. (6a).
Similarly, using
Eq.

27
we can calculate the response of the current:

\displaystyle d_{p}\bm{j}

\displaystyle=\partial_{p}\bm{j}+\bm{\Gamma}\partial_{p}\bm{\pi}=\partial_{p}\bm{j}+\Big[\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}}(\Gamma_{ei}-\Gamma_{eN})d_{p}\pi_{i}\Big]_{e\in\mathcal{E}}

(33)

which has the matrix form shown in Eq. (6b).

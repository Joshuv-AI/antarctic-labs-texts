On a sharp upper bound related to the Bellman function of three integral variables of the dyadic maximal operator
Eleftherios N. Nikolidakis

Abstract
We study properties for the sharp upper bound for integral quantities related to the Bellman function of three integral variables of the dyadic maximal operator, that is determined in [ 11 ] .



1
Introduction

The dyadic maximal operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a useful tool in analysis and is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{d}\varphi(x)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{|S|}\int_{S}|\varphi(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u:x\in S,\ S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \text{is a dyadic cube}\right\},

(1.1)

for every
\varphi\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the dyadic cubes are those formed by the grids
2^{-N}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, for
N=0,1,2,\ldots
.

It is well known that it satisfies the following weak type (1,1) inequality

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathcal{M}_{d}\varphi(x)>\lambda\right\}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\left\{\mathcal{M}_{d}\varphi>\lambda\right\}}|\varphi(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u,

(1.2)

for every
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and every
\lambda>0
,
from which it is easy to get the following
L^{p}
-inequality

\|\mathcal{M}_{d}\varphi\|_{p}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\|\varphi\|_{p},

(1.3)

for every
p>1
, and every
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
It is easy to see that the weak type inequality (
1.2
) is the best possible. For refinements of this inequality one can consult
[
6
]
.

It has also been proved that (
1.3
) is best possible (see
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
for general martingales and
[
19
]
for dyadic ones).
An approach for studying the behavior of this maximal operator in more depth is the introduction of the so-called Bellman functions which play the role of generalized norms of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
. Such functions related to the
L^{p}
-inequality (
1.3
) have been precisely identified in
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
12
]
. For the study of the Bellman functions of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
, we use the notation
\Av_{E}(\psi)=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\psi
, whenever
E
is a Lebesgue measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of positive measure and
\psi
is a real valued measurable function defined on
E
. We fix a dyadic cube
Q
and define the localized maximal operator
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{d}\varphi
as in (
1.1
) but with the dyadic cubes
S
being assumed to be contained in
Q
. Then for every
p>1
we let

B_{p}(f,F)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{d}\varphi)^{p}:\Av_{Q}(\varphi)=f,\ \Av_{Q}(\varphi^{p})=F\right\},

(1.4)

where
\varphi
is nonnegative in
L^{p}(Q)
and the variables
f,F
satisfy
0<f^{p}\leq F
. By a scaling argument it is easy to see that (
1.4
) is independent of the choice of
Q
(so we may choose

Q
to be the unit cube
[0,1]^{n}
).
In
[
5
]
, the function (
1.4
) has been precisely identified for the first time. The proof has been given in a much more general setting of tree-like structures on probability spaces.

More precisely we consider a non-atomic probability space
(X,\mu)
and let
\mathcal{T}
be a family of measurable subsets of
X
, that has a tree-like structure similar to the one in the dyadic case (the exact definition can be found in []).
Then we define the dyadic maximal operator associated to
\mathcal{T}
, by

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\varphi(x)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}|\varphi|\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu:x\in I\in\mathcal{T}\right\},

(1.5)

for every
\varphi\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
,
x\in X
.

This operator is related to the theory of martingales and satisfies essentially the same inequalities as
\mathcal{M}_{d}
does. Now we define the corresponding Bellman function of four variables of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}
, by

B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,k)=\sup\left\{\int_{K}\left[\max(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\varphi,L)\right]^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu:\varphi\geq 0,\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=f,\right.\\
\left.\int_{X}\varphi^{p}\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=F,\ K\subseteq X\ \text{measurable with}\ \mu(K)=k\right\},

(1.6)

the variables
f,F,L,k
satisfying
0<f^{p}\leq F
,
L\geq f
,
k\in(0,1]
.
The exact evaluation of (
1.6
) is given in
[
5
]
, for the cases where
k=1
or
L=f
. In the first case the author (in
[
5
]
) precisely identifies the function
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,1)
by evaluating it in a first stage for the case where
L=f
. That is he precisely identifies
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)
(in fact
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)=F\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{F})^{p}
, where
\omega_{p}:[0,1]\to[1,\frac{p}{p-1}]
is the inverse function
H^{-1}_{p}
, of
H_{p}(z)=-(p-1)z^{p}+pz^{p-1}
).

The proof of the above mentioned evaluation relies on a one-parameter integral inequality which is proved by arguments based on a linearization of the dyadic maximal operator. More precisely the author in
[
5
]
proves that the inequality

F\geq\frac{1}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}f^{p}+\frac{(p-1)\beta}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{X}(M_{\mathcal{T}}\varphi)^{p}\;d\mu,

(1.7)

is true for every non-negative value of the parameter
\beta
and sharp for one that depends on
f
,
F
and
p
, namely for
\beta=\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{F})-1
. This gives as a consequence an upper bound for
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)
, which after several technical considerations is proved to be best possible.Then by using several calculus arguments the author in
[
5
]
provides the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,1)
for every
L\geq f
.

Now in
[
12
]
the authors give a direct proof of the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,1)
by using alternative methods. Moreover in the second case, where
L=f
, the author (in
[
5
]
) uses the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)
and provides the evaluation of the more general
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,k)
,
k\in(0,1]
.

Our aim (in the future) is to use the results of
[
11
]
and of this article in order to approach the following Bellman function problem (of three integral variables)

B_{p,q}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,A,F)=\sup\left\{\int_{X}\left(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\varphi\right)^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu:\varphi\geq 0,\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=f,\right.\\
\left.\int_{X}\varphi^{q}\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=A,\ \int_{X}\varphi^{p}\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=F\right\},

(1.8)

where
1<q<p
, and the variables
f,A,F
lie in the domain of definition of the above problem.

In
[
11
]
we proved that whenever
0<\frac{x^{q}}{\kappa^{q-1}}<y\leq x^{\frac{p-q}{p-1}}\cdot z^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}\;\Leftrightarrow\;0<s_{1}^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}\leq s_{2}<1
, (where
s_{1},s_{2})
are defined right below), we find a constant
t=t(s_{1},s_{2})
for which if
h:(0,\kappa]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfies
\int_{0}^{\kappa}h=x
,
\int_{0}^{\kappa}h^{q}=y
and
\int_{0}^{\kappa}h^{p}=z
then

\int_{0}^{\kappa}\bigg(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\bigg)^{p}dt\leq t^{p}(s_{1},s_{2})\cdot\int_{0}^{\kappa}h^{p},

where
t(s_{1},s_{2})=t
is the greatest element of
\big[{1,t(0)}\big]
for which
F_{s_{1},s_{2}}(t)\leq 0
and
F_{s_{1},s_{2}}
is defined in
[
11
]
. Moreover for each such fixed
s_{1},s_{2}

t=t(s_{1},s_{2})=\min\Big\{{t(\beta)\;:\;\beta\in\big[0,\tfrac{1}{p-1}\big]}\Big\}

where
t(\beta)=t(\beta,s_{1},s_{2})
(see:
[
11
]
). That is we found a constant
t=t(s_{1},s_{2})
for which the above inequality is satisfied for all
h:(0,\kappa]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
as mentioned above. Note that
s_{1},s_{2}
depend by a certain way on
x,y,z
, namely
s_{1}=\frac{x^{p}}{\kappa^{p-1}z}
,
s_{2}=\frac{x^{q}}{\kappa^{q-1}y}
and
F_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\cdot)
is given in terms of
s_{1},s_{2}
.

In this article we study further the constant
t(s_{1},s_{2})=t
which is found in
[
11
]
, by providing exact conditions under which the value of
t
is given by either of the two function branches of its definition (see Section 3).

We need to mention that the extremizers for the standard Bellman function
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)
have been studied in
[
7
]
, and in
[
9
]
for the case
0<p<1
. Also in
[
8
]
the extremal sequences of functions for the respective Hardy operator problem have been studied. Additionally further study of the dyadic maximal operator can be seen in
[
10
,
12
]
where symmetrization principles for this operator are presented, while other approaches for the determination of certain Bellman functions are given in
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
. Moreover results related to applications of dyadic maximal operators can be seen in
[
13
]
.

2
Some technical lemmas

We consider
f,A,F
variables such that

f^{q}<A<f^{\frac{p-q}{p-1}}\cdot F^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}\,.\hskip 113.81102pt(*)

We prove

Lemma 2.1
.

If
f,A,F
satisfy the condition
\omega_{q}\big({\frac{f^{q}}{A}}\big)>\omega_{p}\big({\frac{f^{p}}{F}}\big)
then there exists unique
\kappa\in\Big({\big({\frac{f^{q}}{A}}\big)^{\frac{1}{q-1}},1}\Big)
for which the equality:
\omega_{q}\big({\frac{f^{q}}{\kappa^{q-1}A}}\big)=\omega_{p}\big({\frac{f^{p}}{\kappa^{p-1}F}}\big)
, is true.

Proof.

Consider
g:\Big[{\big({\frac{f^{p}}{F}}\big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},1}\Big]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
defined by

g(\kappa)=\kappa^{q-1}\cdot H_{q}\Big({\omega_{p}\big({\tfrac{f^{p}}{\kappa^{p-1}F}}\big)}\Big)\,.

This is obviously well defined. We prove that
g
is strictly increasing on

\Big[{\big({\frac{f^{p}}{F}}\big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},1}\Big]
. We calculate:

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(\kappa)

\displaystyle=(q-1)\,\kappa^{q-2}\,H_{q}\big({\omega_{p}(s(\kappa))}\big)\,+

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\kappa^{q-1}\,\frac{q(q-1)\,\omega_{p}(s(\kappa))^{q-2}\big({1-\omega_{p}(s(\kappa))}\big)}{p(p-1)\,\omega_{p}(s(\kappa))^{p-2}\big({1-\omega_{p}(s(\kappa))}\big)}\,s^{\prime}(\kappa)=

\displaystyle=\kappa^{q-2}\,\bigg[{(q-1)\,H_{q}\big({\omega_{p}(s)}\big)-\frac{q(q-1)}{p}\,\frac{1}{\omega_{p}(s)^{p-q}}\cdot s}\bigg]=

\displaystyle=\kappa^{q-2}\,(q-1)\,\bigg[{H_{q}\big({\omega_{p}(s)}\big)-\frac{q}{p}\,\frac{s}{\omega_{p}(s)^{p-q}}}\bigg]\,,

(2.1)

where
s=s(\kappa)=\frac{f^{p}}{\kappa^{p-1}F}
.

We make the change of variables:
\omega_{p}(s)=t\;\Leftrightarrow\;s=H_{p}(t)
, where
t\in\big[{1,\frac{p}{p-1}}\big)
. Then (
2
) gives:

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(\kappa)

\displaystyle=\kappa^{q-2}\,(q-1)\,\bigg[{H_{q}(t)-\frac{q}{p}\,\frac{H_{p}(t)}{t^{p-q}}}\bigg]=

\displaystyle=\kappa^{q-2}\,(q-1)\,\bigg[{\big({q\,t^{q-1}-(q-1)\,t^{q}}\big)-\frac{q}{p}\,\frac{1}{t^{p-q}}\,\big({p\,t^{p-1}-(p-1)\,t^{p}}\big)}\bigg]=

\displaystyle=\kappa^{q-2}\,(q-1)\,\bigg[{q\,t^{q-1}-(q-1)\,t^{q}-\frac{q}{p}\,\big({p\,t^{q-1}-(p-1)\,t^{q}}\big)}\bigg]=

\displaystyle=\kappa^{q-2}\,(q-1)\,\bigg[{\frac{q}{p}\,(p-1)-(q-1)}\bigg]\,t^{q}=

\displaystyle=\kappa^{q-2}\,(q-1)\,\bigg[{\frac{qp-q-qp+p}{p}}\bigg]\,t^{q}=

\displaystyle=\frac{(q-1)(p-q)}{p}\,\kappa^{q-2}\,\omega_{p}(s(\kappa))^{q}\,.

(2.2)

Thus
g^{\prime}(\kappa)>0\,,\;\forall\,\kappa\in\Big[{\big({\frac{f^{p}}{F}}\big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},1}\Big]
.

Additionally:
g(1)=H_{q}\big({\omega_{p}\big({\tfrac{f^{p}}{F}}\big)}\big)>\frac{f^{q}}{A}
, by the hypothesis on
f,A,F
that is stated in Lemma
2.1
.

Also
g\big({\big({\tfrac{f^{p}}{F}}\big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\big)=\big({\tfrac{f^{p}}{F}}\big)^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}<\frac{f^{q}}{A}
, by condition (
\ast
), at the beginning of this section.

Thus there exists unique
\kappa\in\Big({\big({\frac{f^{q}}{A}}\big)^{\frac{1}{q-1}},1}\Big)
for which

g(\kappa)=\frac{f^{q}}{A}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\frac{f^{q}}{\kappa^{q-1}A}=H_{q}\Big({\omega_{p}\Big({\frac{f^{p}}{\kappa^{q-1}F}}\Big)}\Big)<1\,.

Thus for this
\kappa
, we should also have that
\kappa>\big({\frac{f^{q}}{A}}\big)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}
. Lemma
2.1
now proved.
∎

Conversely, it is easy to prove the following:

Lemma 2.2
.

If
f,A,F
satisfy (
\ast
), and there exists
\kappa\in\Big({\big({\frac{f^{q}}{A}}\big)^{\frac{1}{q-1}},1}\Big)
, for which
\omega_{q}\big({\frac{f^{q}}{\kappa^{q-1}A}}\big)=\omega_{p}\big({\frac{f^{p}}{\kappa^{p-1}F}}\big)
, then
f,A,F
should satisfy:
\omega_{q}\big({\frac{f^{q}}{A}}\big)>\omega_{p}\big({\frac{f^{p}}{F}}\big)
.

Proof.

By the condition on
\kappa
, that is stated in this Lemma, it is implied that
g(\kappa)=\frac{f^{q}}{A}
.

Moreover
since
\kappa<1
we get
g(\kappa)<g(1)
, by the results of the previous Lemma
2.1
. That is

\displaystyle\frac{f^{q}}{A}<g(1)

\displaystyle=H_{q}\bigg({\omega_{p}\Big({\frac{f^{p}}{F}}\Big)}\bigg)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\omega_{q}\Big({\frac{f^{q}}{A}}\Big)>\omega_{p}\Big({\frac{f^{p}}{F}}\Big)\,.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
x,y,z
are variables satisfying:
\frac{x^{q}}{\kappa^{q-1}}\leq y\leq x^{\frac{p-q}{p-1}}\cdot z^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}
. Set
s_{1}=\frac{x^{p}}{\kappa^{p-1}z}
,
s_{2}=\frac{x^{q}}{\kappa^{q-1}y}
, and assume that
\omega_{p}(s_{1})=\omega_{q}(s_{2}):=\gamma
. We now set
t:=\omega_{p}(s_{1})
. Then
F_{s_{1},s_{2}}(t)=0
.

Proof.

By definition (see
[
11
]
)

F_{s_{1},s_{2}}(t_{1})=q\,\big({p\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-1}-(p-1)\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}}\big)\Big({t_{1}^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\Big)-(p-q)\,s_{1}\,\alpha(s_{2})\,,

where
t_{1}\in\big[{1,t(0)}\big]
,
\tau=\tau(t_{1})=\dfrac{p-q}{p}\,\dfrac{t_{1}^{p}-s_{1}}{t_{1}^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}
,
\tau(t(0))=1
, and
\alpha(s_{2})=\frac{\omega_{q}(s_{2})^{q}}{s_{2}}-1
.

Initially we prove that
\tau(t)\leq 1
, when
t=\omega_{p}(s_{1})=\gamma
. For this purpose we need to prove that

\displaystyle\dfrac{p-q}{p}\,\dfrac{\gamma^{p}-s_{1}}{\gamma^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\leq 1\quad

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad(p-q)(\gamma^{p}-s_{1})\leq p\Big({\gamma^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad(p-q)\gamma^{p}-p\,\gamma^{p-q}\leq(p-q)s_{1}-p\,\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}\,,

(2.3)

where
\gamma=\omega_{p}(s_{1})
. Define the function
\varphi(y)=(p-q)\,y^{p}-p\,y^{p-q}\,,\;y\geq 1
. Then
\varphi(y)
is strictly increasing on
[1,+\infty)
. We need to prove (
2
). By hypothesis
t=\omega_{p}(s_{1})=\omega_{q}(s_{2})\;\Rightarrow\;s_{2}=H_{q}\big({\omega_{p}(s_{1})}\big)=H_{q}(t)
. Thus for the proof of (
2
) is enough to justify

\displaystyle(p-q)\,t^{p}-p\,t^{p-q}

\displaystyle\leq(p-q)\,H_{p}(t)-p\,\frac{H_{p}(t)}{H_{q}(t)}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle H_{q}(t)\,\big[{(p-q)\,t^{p}-p\,t^{p-q}}\big]

\displaystyle\leq(p-q)\,H_{p}(t)\cdot H_{q}(t)-p\,H_{p}(t)\,.

The last stated inequality is easily seen to be equivalent to the following:

G(t):=(2pq-p)\,t^{q}-(q-1)p\,t^{q+1}-pq\,t^{q-1}-p\,t+p\leq 0

which is true since
t\geq 1
,
G(1)=0
, and

\displaystyle G(t)=-p(t-1)+pq\,t^{q}-pq\,t^{q+1}+p\,t^{q}(t-1)+pq\,t^{q-1}(t-1)\,.

Then if
t=1\;\Rightarrow\;G(t)=0
, while if
t>1
we get that

\displaystyle G(t)

\displaystyle=-p(t-1)+pq\,t^{q}-pq\,t^{q+1}+p\,t^{q}(t-1)+pq\,t^{q-1}(t-1)

\displaystyle\cong-p+pq(-t^{q})+p\,t^{q}+pq\,t^{q-1}

\displaystyle\cong-1-q\,t^{q}+t^{q}+q\,t^{q-1}

\displaystyle=q\,t^{q-1}-(q-1)\,t^{q}-1

\displaystyle=H_{q}(t)-1<0\,,

since
t>1
.

As we have already seen, when
s_{1}<1\;\Leftrightarrow\;t=\omega_{p}(s_{1})>1
we have that
\tau(t)<1
(this is a consequence of the proof that is already given in
[
11
]
).

Thus
\tau(t)<1=\tau(t(0))\;\Rightarrow\;1<t=\omega_{p}(s_{1})=\gamma<t(0)
.

We finally prove that

\displaystyle F_{s_{1},s_{2}}(t)

\displaystyle=0\quad\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle q\,\big({p\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-1}-(p-1)\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}}\big)\Big({t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=(p-q)\,s_{1}\cdot\alpha(s_{2})

(2.4)

Note that

\displaystyle p\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-1}-(p-1)\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}=

\displaystyle q\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-1}+(p-q)\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-1}-(q-1)\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}-(p-q)\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}=

\displaystyle\big[{q\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-1}-(q-1)\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}}\big]+(p-q)\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-1}\big({1-\omega_{q}(\tau)}\big)=

\displaystyle H_{q}\big({\omega_{q}(\tau)}\big)+(p-q)\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-1}\big({1-\omega_{q}(\tau)}\big)=

\displaystyle\tau+(p-q)\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-1}\big({1-\omega_{q}(\tau)}\big)\,.

Thus for the proof of (
2
) it’s enough to show that:

\displaystyle q(t^{p}-s_{1})+pq\,\big({\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q-1}-\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}}\big)\Big({t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\Big)=p\,s_{1}\cdot\alpha(s_{2})

(2.5)

(
2.5
) is then equivalent to:

\displaystyle q(t^{p}-s_{1})+p\,\big({\tau-\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}}\big)\Big({t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=p\,s_{1}\cdot\alpha(s_{2})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(t^{p}-s_{1})-\Big({t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\Big)\,\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}

\displaystyle=s_{1}\cdot\alpha(s_{2})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{p}{p-q}\,\tau-\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{1}\big({\frac{t^{q}}{s_{2}}-1}\big)}{t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\omega_{q}(\tau)^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{p}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}\,t^{q}}{t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\omega_{q}(\tau)

\displaystyle=t\,.

(2.6)

We prove (
2
). We have: (
2
)
\Leftrightarrow\quad\tau=H_{q}(t)\quad\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{p-q}{p}\,\frac{t^{p}-s_{1}}{t^{p-q}-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}=s_{2}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad p\,s_{2}\,t^{p-q}-(p-q)\,t^{p}=q\,s_{1}\,.

(2.7)

But

\displaystyle\frac{H_{p}(t)}{H_{q}(t)}=\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{p\,t^{p-1}-(p-1)\,t^{p}}{q\,t^{q-1}-(q-1)\,t^{q}}=\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}\quad\Rightarrow

\displaystyle p\,s_{2}\,t^{p-q}=(p-1)s_{2}\,t^{p-q+1}+s_{1}\big({q-(q-1)t}\big)\,.

(2.8)

Thus in view of (
2
), (
2.7
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle(p-q)\,t^{p}=(p-1)s_{2}\,t^{p-q+1}-s_{1}(q-1)t

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(p-q)\,t^{p-1}=(p-1)s_{2}\,t^{p-q}-(q-1)s_{1}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(p-q)\,t^{p-1}-(p-1)\,H_{q}(t)\,t^{p-q}+(q-1)\,H_{p}(t)=0\,,

which is true in view of the definition of
H_{p}(t)
and
H_{q}(t)
. Lemma
2.3
is now proved.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
2.3
we obtain that if

\displaystyle\omega_{p}(s_{1})=\omega_{q}(s_{2})=\gamma>1\,,

(2.9)

then
F_{s_{1},s_{2}}\big({\omega_{p}(s_{1})}\big)=0
, with
1<\gamma=\omega_{p}(s_{1})<t(0)
. Thus (see:
[
11
]
) we must have (in case where (
2.9
) is true) that
t^{\prime}(0)<0
. Indeed if
t^{\prime}(0)\geq 0
(see the comments in the end of Section 4 in
[
11
]
) we should have
F_{s_{1},s_{2}}\big({t(0)}\big)\leq 0
. But we already have that
F_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\gamma)=0
, where
\gamma<t(0)\;\Rightarrow\;F_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\gamma)<F_{s_{1},s_{2}}\big({t(0)}\big)\;\Rightarrow\;F_{s_{1},s_{2}}\big({t(0)}\big)>0
, which is contradiction.

Thus (see
[
11
]
), we have that there exists
\beta\in\big({0,\frac{1}{p-1}}\big)
:

t^{\prime}(\beta)=0
for which

\displaystyle t(\beta)=\min\Big\{{t(\gamma_{1})\;:\;\gamma_{1}\in\big[0,\tfrac{1}{p-1}\big]}\Big\}\,.

(2.10)

But for this
\beta
, we have:
1\leq t(\beta)<t(0)
and

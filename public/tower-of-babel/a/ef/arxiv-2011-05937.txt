Hochschild cohomology of Fermat type polynomials with non–abelian symmetries
Alexey Basalaev, Andrei Ionov

Abstract
. For a polynomial f = x 1 n + ⋯ + x N n f=x_{1}^{n}+\dots+x_{N}^{n} let G f G_{f} be the non–abelian maximal group of symmetries of f f . This is a group generated by all g ∈ GL ⁡ ( N , ℂ ) g\in\mathrm{GL}(N,{\mathbb{C}}) , rescaling and permuting the variables, so that f ⁡ ( 𝐱 ) = f ⁡ ( g ⋅ 𝐱 ) f({\bf x})=f(g\cdot{\bf x}) . For any G ⊆ G f G\subseteq G_{f} we compute explicitly Hochschild cohomology of the category of G G –equivarint matrix factorizations of f f . We introduce the pairing on it showing that it is a Frobenius algebra.



1.
Introduction

Equivariant approach to singularity theory had been under consideration in many different publications.
In particular, it appeared to be useful for such purposes as constructing new moduli spaces and integrable hierarchies in
[
FJR
]
, cohomological field theories in
[
PV
]
and quantum field theories in
[
IV90
]
. One should notice that the certain equivariant approach ideas were applied in the classical singularity theory as well (cf.
[
O
]
). However, the role of equivariant approach to singularity theory is in particular important in mirror symmetry.

Consider a pair
(f,G)
, where
f=f({\bf x})
is a polynomial having only isolated critical points and
G\subseteq\mathrm{GL}(N,{\mathbb{C}})
a group of elements
g
, s.t.
f(g\cdot{\bf x})=f({\bf x})
. In order to put mirror symmetry in a rigorous context, one has to provide the canonical construction of a Frobenius algebra of a pair
(f,G)
.

Diagonal groups

Let
G_{f}^{d}
stand for
the group of maximal diagonal symmetries
of
f
.

G_{f}^{d}:=\left\{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N})\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})^{N}\,\left|\,f(\lambda_{1}x_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}x_{N})=f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\right.\right\}.

For
G\subseteq G_{f}^{d}
the Frobenius algebra of
(f,G)
was considered in
[
K03
,
K09
,
BTW16
,
BTW17
]
. All these publications made an attempt to construct a new object employing some essential ideas — it was widely agreed how the vector space of the pair
(f,G)
should look and it remained to introduce the product structure and the pairing in a canonical manner.
Such a product was given non-uniquely in a wide contex in
[
K03
]
for all polynomials
f
and
G\subseteq G_{f}^{d}
; in
[
K09
]
it was fixed by the particular formula for the special class of
f
, so–called
invertible polynomials
and
G\subseteq G_{f}^{d}\cap{\mathrm{SL}}(N)
; it was introduced axiomatically in
[
BTW16
]
for all polynomials
f
and
G\subseteq G_{f}^{d}
. It was also proved in
[
BTW16
]
that a Frobenius algebra, satisfying such axioms is unique up to isomorphism.

However, there is an essential candidate for Frobenius algebra of
(f,G)
— Hochschild cohomology
{\mathsf{HH}}^{\ast}(\mathrm{MF}(f,G))
of the category of
G
–equivarint matrix factorizations of
f
. The only reason why it was not widely used in mirror symmetry was computational — this cohomology ring was very hard to compute.

In
[
S20
]
D. Shklyarov developed the technique allowing one to compute
{\mathsf{HH}}^{\ast}(\mathrm{MF}(f,G))
systematically. Despite the fact that this Hochschild cohomology is defined for the groups
G
that are not necessarily abelian, Shklyarov’s technique fully works only for
G\subseteq G_{f}^{d}
.
This result of Shklyarov was used in
[
BT2
]
for
f
being invertible polynomial, to show that the construction of
[
BTW16
]
provides the algebra isomorphic to
{\mathsf{HH}}^{\ast}(\mathrm{MF}(f,G))
if
G\subseteq G_{f}^{d}
.

Non-abelian groups

Up to now very few results can be found in literature, concerning the pairs
(f,G)
with a non–abelian symmetry group
G
.
In
[
EGZ18
]
the authors have considered the pairs
(f,G)
topologically from the point of view of Milnor fibre. In
[
PWW
]
the authors considered the A–model vector space for the certain examples of such pairs
(f,G)
.

In particular, for
G
not being diagonal, up to now there was no attempt to investigate a Frobenius algebra of a pair
(f,G)
. We address this question in the current paper. Namely, for fixed positive integers
N
and
n
we consider a polynomial

f=f_{N,n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}):=x_{1}^{n}+\ldots+x_{N}^{n}

together with its group of symmetries
G_{f}=S_{N}\ltimes G_{f}^{d}
, where
S_{N}
acts by permuting the coordinates
x_{\bullet}
. The pairs
(f_{N,n},G_{f})
were considered in the physical context in
[
G91
]
. The certain approach towards the construction of a Frobenius manifold for such singularities was done in
[
I16
]
.

In this paper

We adopt the technique of Shklyarov to compute the basis and product of
{\mathsf{HH}}^{\ast}(\mathrm{MF}(f,G))
for any
G\subseteq G_{f}
. According to this technique one has to consider the Hochschild cochain mixed complex of
({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G,f)
with the coefficients in
{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G
. Denoting its cohomology by
{\mathsf{HH}}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G,f)
, we have
{\mathsf{HH}}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G,f)\cong{\mathsf{HH}}^{\ast}(\mathrm{MF}(f,G))
.
Consider also the Hochschild cochain mixed complex of
({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}],f)
with the coefficients in
{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G
. Its cohomology
{\mathsf{HH}}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}],f;{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G)
has natural
G
–action, so that we have
{\mathsf{HH}}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G,f)\cong\left({\mathsf{HH}}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}],f;{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G)\right)^{G}
. The
\cup
–product of
{\mathsf{HH}}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}],f;{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G)
can be computed combinatorially with the help of HKR isomorphism, found explicitly in
[
S20
]
. However this isomorphism is not
G
–equivariant for non–abelian groups. This makes the problem of finding the
G
–action explicitly complicated.

In this paper for any
G\subseteq G_{f}
we resolve the
\cup
–product of
{\mathsf{HH}}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}],f;{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G)
via the explicit formulae. The latter cohomology ring can be endowed with the bigrading following the ideas of
[
IV90
]
. We show that the
\cup
–product preserves this bigrading. Introducing the certain pairing we show also that
{\mathsf{HH}}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}],f;{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G)
is a Frobenius algebra.

Using the formulae for the
\cup
–product, we compute the
G
–action on
{{\mathsf{HH}}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}],f;{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G)}
explicitly. We show that
{\mathsf{HH}}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G,f)
is a Frobenius algebra too.

Even though we only deal with the polynomials of Fermat type, our approach is applicable to the case of arbitrary invertible polynomials as well. It’s also an interesting question to consider the Hochschild cohomology of invertible polynomials in the context of Berglund-Hübsch-Henningson duality (cf.
[
EGZ18
,
EGZ20
]
). We plan to do this is in a upcoming paper
[
BI2
]
.

We also applied the results of the present paper to generalize the mirror map of
[
K09
]
to the case of Fermat polynomials with non-abelian group of symmetries in
[
BI21
]
.

The paper is organized as follows.
We fix notation and introduce some notions in Section
2
. Section
3
is devoted to the definiton of the phase space
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{f,G}
— a bigraded vector space with the pairing that will be later endowed with the
\cup
–product of Hochschild cohomology ring
{\mathsf{HH}}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}],f;{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G)
. The structure constants of this product are introduced in Section
4
where we explain the technique of Shklyarov. In Section
5
we develop a systematic approach how to compute these structure constants. It takes full Section
6
to compute all structure constants needed. Finally in Section
7
we find the action of
G
on
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{f,G}
. This allows us to consider the
G
–invariants of
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{f,G}
and prove our main theorem showing that
{\mathsf{HH}}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G,f)
is a Frobenius algebra with the pairing introduced in Section
3
.

2.
Preliminary notations

Let
N
and
n
be positive integers. Consider a polynomial

f=f_{N,n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}):=x_{1}^{n}+\ldots+x_{N}^{n}.

Such polynomials are called to be of Fermat type. The set of critical points of
f
consists of
{\bf x}=0
only and it makes sense to consider Jacobian algebra of
f
.

\mathrm{Jac}(f):={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]/(\partial_{x_{1}}f,\dots,\partial_{x_{N}}f).

This is a finite–dimenional
{\mathbb{C}}
–algebra with the canonical product that we denote by
\circ
. Moreover it is a
Frobenius algebra
. Namely there is a non–degenerate symmetric bilinear form
\eta:\mathrm{Jac}(f)\otimes\mathrm{Jac}(f)\to{\mathbb{C}}
, s.t.
\eta(a\circ b,c)=\eta(a,b\circ c)
for all
a,b,c\in\mathrm{Jac}(f)
. We will introduce this pairing explicitly beneath.
In what follows for any polynomial
p=p({\bf x})
we denote by
\lfloor p\rfloor
its class in
\mathrm{Jac}(f)
.

The action of
S_{N}
permuting coordinates on
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
defines an action of
S_{N}
on
G_{f}^{d}
by permutation of diagonal entries. Let
G_{f}
be the group of symmetries
G_{f}=S_{N}\ltimes G_{f}^{d}
with the product
(\sigma^{\prime},h)(\sigma,g)=(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma,\sigma^{-1}(h)g)
. In what follows assume
g\in G_{f}^{d}
being represented by a diagonal matrix
\mathrm{diag}(g_{1},\dots,g_{N})
. Let an element
(\sigma,g)\in G_{f}
act on
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
by
(\sigma,g)(x_{k})=g_{k}x_{\sigma(k)}
.

For any
G\subseteq G_{f}
there are two natural subgroups of
G

\displaystyle G^{s}:=\{(\sigma,\mathrm{id})\}\quad\text{ and }\quad G^{d}:=\{(\mathrm{id},g)\}.

In particular we have
(G_{f})^{s}\simeq S_{N}
and
(G_{f})^{d}\simeq G_{f}^{d}
.
By abuse of notations we also write

\sigma
for an element
(\sigma,\mathrm{id})\in G^{s}
and
g
for an element
(\mathrm{id},g)\in G^{d}
. Note that in this notation we have
(\sigma,g)=\sigma\cdot g
.

Denote by
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})
the element
\sigma\in S_{N}
such that
\sigma(i_{a})=i_{a+1}
,
\sigma(i_{k})=i_{1}
and
\sigma(j)=j
for
j\not\in\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
.
We call an element
(\sigma,g)\in G_{f}
a
cycle of length
k
if
\sigma=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})
and, moreover,
g_{j}=1
for
j\not\in\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
.

Notation 1
.

In what follows denoting length
k
cycles we assume
i_{1}
to be the smallest element in
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
.

Let us fix a
n
-th primitive root of unity
\zeta_{n}:=\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}/n)
. Denote by
t_{i}
the element of
G_{f}^{d}
defined by

t_{i}(x_{j})=\begin{cases}\zeta_{n}\cdot x_{i},\quad&\text{ if }i=j,\\
1\cdot x_{j}\quad&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We call an element
(\sigma,g)\in G_{f}

special
if
g_{1}\ldots g_{N}=1
, i.e. if
g\in\mathrm{SL}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
, and
non–special
otherwise.

2.1.
Fixed loci

For any
u\in G_{f}
denote by
\mathrm{Fix}(u)\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
the linear subspace of the fixed points of
u
, seen as an element of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
. For
u
acting diagonally the set
\mathrm{Fix}(u)
consists of the points with some of the coordinates set to zero. For
u=(\sigma,g)
with
\sigma\neq\mathrm{id}
the set
\mathrm{Fix}(u)
is best seen after the diagonalization of
u
.

Denote
N_{u}:=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Fix}(u)
and
d_{u}\colon=N-N_{u}
.
For each
u\in G_{f}
denote by
I_{u}^{c}
the set of all indices
i
, s.t.
u(x_{i})\neq x_{i}
, and by
I_{u}
the complement of
I_{u}^{c}
in
\{1,\dots,N\}
.
In particular, for
g\in G_{f}^{d}
we get
I_{g}=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{N_{g}}\}
— the set of all indices
j
such that
g_{j}=1
and
I_{\mathrm{id}}=\{1,\dots,N\}
.

The elements
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in G_{f}
will be called
non–intersecting
if
I_{u_{i}}^{c}\cap I_{u_{j}}^{c}=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
.

Remark 2
.

Trying to keep the notation consistent with all the other work done before us we have to note that the notation for
I_{g}
in
[
BTW16
,
BTW17
,
BT2
]
is opposite to
[
S20
]
. We stick to that being historically first.

Decompose further
\mathrm{Fix}(u)=\mathrm{Fix}^{T}(u)\oplus\mathrm{Fix}^{E}(u)
into a
tautological fixed locus

\mathrm{Fix}^{T}(u):=\{x\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}~|~x_{j}=0,j\in I_{(\sigma,g)}^{c}\}
and a
eigen fixed locus

\mathrm{Fix}^{E}(u)
.
For any element
u\in G_{f}
denote by
f^{u}
the restriction of
f
to the fixed locus
\mathrm{Fix}(u)
.

The following example will describe the fixed locus of a special cycle. This example will be used throughout the paper.

Example 3
.

Consider special cycle
(\sigma,g)\in G_{f}
with
{\sigma=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})}
. Recall that in our convention
g
acts trivially on
x_{j}
with
j\not\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
.

For
\zeta_{k}=\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}/k)
consider the change of the variables

\widetilde{x}_{i_{b}}:=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{a=1}^{k}\zeta_{k}^{(1-b)(a-1)}\widetilde{g}_{i_{a}}x_{i_{a}},\quad 1\leq b\leq k,

with
\widetilde{g}_{i_{1}}:=1
and
\widetilde{g}_{i_{a}}:=g_{i_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot g_{i_{a-1}}
for
1\leq a\leq k
.
Then we have

(\sigma,g)(\widetilde{x}_{i_{b}})=\zeta_{k}^{b-1}\widetilde{x}_{i_{b}}.

In particular, the variable
\widetilde{x}_{i_{1}}
is preserved by the action of
(\sigma,g)
. We have:

x_{i_{a}}=\frac{1}{\widetilde{g}_{i_{a}}}\sum_{b=1}^{k}\zeta_{k}^{(b-1)(a-1)}\widetilde{x}_{i_{b}},\quad 1\leq a\leq k,

and also

f^{(\sigma,g)}=k\cdot\widetilde{x}_{i_{1}}^{n}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\not\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}\\
1\leq l\leq N\end{subarray}}x_{l}^{n}.

We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fix}^{T}((\sigma,g))

\displaystyle=\{x\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}|\ x_{i_{1}}=\dots=x_{i_{k}}=0\},

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fix}^{E}((\sigma,g))

\displaystyle=\{x\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}|\ x_{j}=0,\ j\not\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}\text{ and }x_{i_{a}}=t\cdot\widetilde{g}_{i_{a}}^{-1},\ t\in{\mathbb{C}}\}.

(2)

For every
x_{i_{a}}
, considered as a function of
\widetilde{x}_{\bullet}
we have

\displaystyle\left.x_{i_{1}}\right|_{\mathrm{Fix}((\sigma,g))}=\left.\widetilde{g}_{i_{2}}x_{i_{2}}\right|_{\mathrm{Fix}((\sigma,g))}=\ldots=\left.\widetilde{g}_{i_{k}}x_{i_{k}}\right|_{\mathrm{Fix}((\sigma,g))}=\left.\widetilde{x}_{i_{1}}\right|_{\mathrm{Fix}((\sigma,g))},

(3)

\displaystyle\left.\widetilde{x}_{i_{b}}\right|_{\mathrm{Fix}((\sigma,g))}=0\enskip\text{for $b>1$.}

(4)

From this we conclude
\lfloor\left(\widetilde{g}_{i_{p}}x_{i_{p}}\right)^{q}\rfloor=\lfloor\widetilde{x}_{i_{1}}^{q}\rfloor
in
\mathrm{Jac}(f^{(\sigma,g)})
.

In what follows denote

\widetilde{x}_{(\sigma,g)}:=\widetilde{x}_{i_{1}}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{a=1}^{k}\widetilde{g}_{i_{a}}x_{i_{a}}.

The following lemma is obvious.

Lemma 4
.

We have

(1):

If
u\in G_{f}
is a non–special cycle or
u\in G^{d}
. Then
\mathrm{Fix}^{E}(u)=0
.

(2):

If
u\in G_{f}
satisfies
u=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\sigma_{i},g_{i})
for some nonintersecting cycles
(\sigma_{i},g_{i})
. Then

\mathrm{Fix}^{T}(u)=\cap_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Fix}^{T}((\sigma_{i},g_{i}))\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{Fix}^{E}(u)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Fix}^{E}((\sigma_{i},g_{i})).

It follows from the lemma above that for any
u\in G_{f}
,
f^{u}
either vanishes or is a polynomial of Ferma type again. In particular, it defines an isolated singularity in the latter case and it makes sense to consider its Jacobian algebra
\mathrm{Jac}(f^{u})
.

For
f^{u}\equiv 0
we set
\mathrm{Jac}(f^{u}):={\mathbb{C}}\cdot[1]
— the
1
–dimensional
{\mathbb{C}}
–algebra generated by
[1]
.

3.
The phase space

Let
G\subseteq G_{f}
. For any
u\in G
let
\xi_{u}
be a formal letter and
f^{u}
stand for the restriction of
f
to
\mathrm{Fix}(u)
as in section above.
Consider the
{\mathbb{C}}
–vector space

{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{f,G}:=\bigoplus_{u\in G}{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{u}\text{ with }{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{u}:=\mathrm{Jac}(f^{u})\xi_{u}.

(5)

We will denote by
\lfloor\phi\rfloor\xi_{u}
the elements of
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{u}
, where
\lfloor\phi\rfloor\in\mathrm{Jac}(f^{u})
.
The subspace
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{u}
will be called
u
–sector
.

The vector space
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{f,G}
is
G
–graded by its construction. Introduce also the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
grading on it by defining
\lfloor\phi\rfloor\xi_{u}
to be of parity
\overline{0}
if
N-N_{u}\equiv 0\mod 2
and of parity
\overline{1}
if
N-N_{u}\equiv 1\mod 2
.

Following Shklyarov
[
S20
]
we are going to equip this algebra with the product

\displaystyle\cup:{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{f,G}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{f,G}

\displaystyle\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{f,G}

(6)

\displaystyle\lfloor\phi({\bf x})\rfloor\xi_{u}\cup\lfloor\psi({\bf x})\rfloor\xi_{v}

\displaystyle=\lfloor\phi({\bf x})\psi(u({\bf x}))\sigma_{u,v}\rfloor\xi_{uv},\quad\phi({\bf x}),\psi({\bf x})\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}],

(7)

where
\sigma_{u,v}
are some polynomials that will be introduced in Section
4
.

It’s important to note that the product above assumes the classes of polynomials in the different quotient rings.
Special polynomials
\sigma_{u,v}
are needed in order to make this product well-defined. These are given by Hochschild cohomology ring.
In particular, it follows that one may apply Eq. (
3
) and (
4
) on both sides of Eq.(
7
) without any affect to the classes.
We illustrate this property in Remark
31
after computing all
\sigma_{u,v}
.

One notes immediately that
\cup
–product respects the
G
–grading. It also respects the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
–grading. Namely, we have
\sigma_{u,v}=0
if
N-N_{u}+N-N_{v}\not\equiv N-N_{uv}\mod 2
.

3.1.
Pairing

For
(\mathrm{id},g)\in G
we define the polynomial

\mathcal{H}_{(\mathrm{id},g)}:=\prod n(n-1)x_{a}^{n-2},

the product being taken over all
a\in I_{(\mathrm{id},g)}^{c}
.

For a cycle
(\sigma,g)
with
\sigma=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})
we define the polynomial

\mathcal{H}_{(\sigma,g)}:=\begin{cases}(-1)^{k-1}n(n-1)\widetilde{x}_{(\sigma,g)}^{n-2},&\quad\text{ if $g$ is special},\\
\det(g)^{-1}-1&\quad\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

for
\widetilde{x}_{(\sigma,g)}
as in Example
3
.

For general
u\in G
let
u=(\mathrm{id},g_{0})\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\sigma_{i},g_{i})
be its decomposition into non–intersecting cycles.
Denote

p_{i}:=\begin{cases}|\sigma_{i}|-1\quad&\text{if $g_{i}$ is special},\\
|\sigma_{i}|\quad&\text{if $g_{i}$ is non-special}.\end{cases}

Then we set

\mathcal{H}_{u}:=(-1)^{D}\prod_{i=0}^{k}\mathcal{H}_{(\sigma_{i},g_{i})},\quad D=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}p_{i}(p_{i+1}+\dots+p_{k})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}p_{i}p_{j}.

Up to a multiplicative constant we can see
\mathcal{H}_{u}
as a Hessian determinant of
f^{u}
.

For an element
u\in G
there is a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
\eta_{f^{u}}
on
\mathrm{Jac}(f^{u})
defined up to multiplicative constant and endowing it with the structure of Frobenius algebras. We fix this constant by setting

\eta_{f^{u}}(\lfloor 1\rfloor,\lfloor\mathcal{H}_{u}\rfloor)=(n-1)^{N_{u}}.

Moreover,
\eta_{f^{u}}(\lfloor 1\rfloor,-)
vanishes on monomials in
\mathrm{Jac}(f^{u})
not proportional to
\lfloor\mathcal{H}_{u}\rfloor
. These properties define
\eta_{f^{u}}
uniquely.

Consider the direct sum decomposition of
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{f,G}
. We introduce the
{\mathbb{C}}
–bilinear form
\eta_{f,G}
on it by

\eta_{f,G}:=\bigoplus_{u\in G}\eta_{u},

where

\eta_{u}\left(\lfloor\phi^{\prime}\rfloor\xi_{u},\lfloor\phi^{\prime\prime}\rfloor\xi_{u^{-1}}\right)=\eta_{f^{u}}(\lfloor\phi^{\prime}\rfloor,\lfloor\phi^{\prime\prime}\rfloor).

It follows immediately from the definition that
\eta_{f,G}
is non–degenerate. Moreover, it satisfies the following symmetry condition: if
u=(\sigma,g)
then

\eta_{u}\left(\lfloor\phi^{\prime}\rfloor\xi_{u},\lfloor\phi^{\prime\prime}\rfloor\xi_{u^{-1}}\right)=(-1)^{M_{u}}\det(g)\eta_{u^{-1}}\left(\lfloor\phi^{\prime\prime}\rfloor\xi_{u^{-1}},\lfloor\phi^{\prime}\rfloor\xi_{u}\right),

where
M_{u}
is the number of nonspecial cycles in
u
. This condition holds as we have

(-1)^{M_{u}}\det(g)\mathcal{H}_{u}=\mathcal{H}_{u^{-1}}

by construction of
\mathcal{H}_{u}
. In particular
\eta_{f,G}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
–symmetric with
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of
\lfloor\phi\rfloor\xi_{u}
given by parity of
M_{u}
if and only if
G^{d}\subseteq{\mathrm{SL}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
.
We show in Theorem
32
that if
G^{d}\subseteq{\mathrm{SL}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
it endows
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{f,G}
with a structure of Frobenius algebra and in Proposition
45
that it is
G
-invariant.

3.2.
Bigrading

The function
f
assumed in this paper is quasi-homogeneous. This provides the grading of the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]
. In what follows denote it by
\deg
.

For any
u\in G
let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}\in{\mathbb{C}}
be the eigenvalues of the linear transformation
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
given by
{\bf x}\mapsto u{\bf x}
. We may assume
\lambda_{k}=\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}a_{k})

4.
Hochschild cohomology via Koszul complex

In this section we present the technique of Shklyarov
[
S20
]

Define the
N
-th
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded
Clifford algebra

{\mathrm{Cl}}_{N}
as the quotient algebra of

{\mathbb{C}}\langle\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N},{\partial_{\theta_{1}}},\ldots,{\partial_{\theta_{N}}}\rangle

modulo the ideal generated by

\theta_{i}\theta_{j}=-\theta_{j}\theta_{i},\quad{\partial_{\theta_{i}}}{\partial_{\theta_{j}}}=-{\partial_{\theta_{j}}}{\partial_{\theta_{i}}},\quad{\partial_{\theta_{i}}}\theta_{j}=-\theta_{j}{\partial_{\theta_{i}}}+\delta_{ij},

where
\theta_{i}
is of degree
-1
and
{\partial_{\theta_{i}}}
is of degree
1
.
For
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}
write

\partial_{\theta_{I}}:=\prod_{i\in I}\partial_{\theta_{i}},\quad{\theta_{I}}:=\prod_{i\in I}{\theta_{i}},

(10)

where in both cases the multipliers are taken in increasing order of the indices.
The subspaces
{\mathbb{C}}[{\theta}]={\mathbb{C}}[\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N}]
and
{\mathbb{C}}[\partial_{\theta}]={\mathbb{C}}[\partial_{\theta_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{\theta_{N}}]

have the left

-graded

-module structures via the isomorphisms

{\mathbb{C}}[{\theta}]\cong{\mathrm{Cl}}_{N}/{\mathrm{Cl}}_{N}\langle{\partial_{\theta_{1}}},\ldots,{\partial_{\theta_{N}}}\rangle,\quad{\mathbb{C}}[\partial_{\theta}]\cong{\mathrm{Cl}}_{n}/{\mathrm{Cl}}_{n}\langle{{\theta_{1}}},\ldots,{{\theta_{N}}}\rangle.

Write
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N},y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}]
as
{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x},{\bf y}]
and

{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N},y_{1},\ldots,y_{N},z_{1},\dots,z_{N}]
as
{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z}]
.
For each
1\leq i\leq N
, there is a map

\displaystyle\nabla^{{\bf x}\to({\bf x},{\bf y})}_{i}:{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\to{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x},{\bf y}],\qquad\nabla_{i}(p):=\frac{l_{i}(p)-l_{i+1}(p)}{x_{i}-y_{i}}.

(11)

where

and

.
They are called the

\sum_{i=1}^{N}(x_{i}-y_{i})\nabla_{i}(p)=p({\bf x})-p({\bf y}).

(12)

The difference derivatives can be applied consecutively. In particular, we shall use

\nabla_{i}^{{\bf y}\to({\bf y},{\bf z})}\nabla_{j}^{{\bf x}\to({\bf x},{\bf y})}(p)
, which is an element of
{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z}]
.
For any
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra homomorphism
\psi:{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\to{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]

\nabla_{i}^{{\bf x}\to({\bf x},\psi({\bf x}))}(p):=\left.\nabla_{i}^{{\bf x}\to({\bf x},{\bf y})}(p)\right|_{{\bf y}=\psi({\bf x})}\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]
.

4.4.
Brief review of Shklyarov’s technique

In order to prove Theorem
7
Shklyarov makes the following steps.

On the first step (Section 4.1.3) Shklyarov establishes an explicit quasi-isomorphism between the Hochschild complex and mixed Koszul complex. Both complexes are assumed with the coefficients in any ring
M
, in particular, the ring
M={\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G
suits too. Important achievement here is also the formula, connecting the products in the cohomology rings of the two complexes.

On the second step (Section 4.1.6) Shklyarov relates Koszul complex to another mixed complex. Namely, he considers the “Clifford” complex
M[\partial_{\theta_{1}},\dots,\partial_{\theta_{N}}]
with the certain differentials, isomorphic to the Koszul complex. Here
M
is still arbitrary, and the product in cohomology ring of Hochschild complex is related to the product on the Clifford complex via the certain formula. This is exactly the place from where the operators
\exp({{\rm H}}_{f})
and
{\Upsilon}
come.

On the third step (Section 4.2) Shklyarov considers the certain coefficient ring
M={\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]\rtimes G^{d}
with an abelian group
G^{d}
. Here the Clifford complex is decomposed into the dicrect sum over
G^{d}
–elements, each corresponding to a subcomplex. For any
g\in G
the
g
–th subcomplex is computed explicitly. Namely, one of the differentials of the mixed complex vanishes after the certain quasi-isomorphism. This is a place where it is important that
G^{d}
is abelian, however works similarly if
g
is diagonalized. The vanishing of this differential constitutes restriction to the (linear) fixed locus of
g
for the fibre variables. This is exactly the place from where the operator
{{\rm H}}_{f,g}
comes. This step is still applicable for a non–abelian symmetry group with the restriction to the fixed locus being understood linearly.

Subtle point of the three steps above is the
G
–action. The quasi–isomorphism above are only proved to be
G
–equivariant when
G=G^{d}
is abelian. This is the place where can not just compute the
G
–action by the definition and need to derive it from the braided commutativity property.

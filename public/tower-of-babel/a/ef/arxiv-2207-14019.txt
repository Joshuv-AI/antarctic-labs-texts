Online Inference for Mixture Model of Streaming Graph Signals with Non-White Excitation
Yiran He, Hoi-To Wai

Abstract
This paper considers a joint multi-graph inference and clustering problem for simultaneous inference of node centrality and association of graph signals with their graphs. We study a mixture model of filtered low pass graph signals with possibly non-white and low-rank excitation. While the mixture model is motivated from practical scenarios, it presents significant challenges to prior graph learning methods. As a remedy, we consider an inference problem focusing on the node centrality of graphs. We design an expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm with a unique low-rank plus sparse prior derived from low pass signal property. We propose a novel online EM algorithm for inference from streaming data. As an example, we extend the online algorithm to detect if the signals are generated from an abnormal graph. We show that the proposed algorithms converge to a stationary point of the maximum-a-posterior (MAP) problem. Numerical experiments support our analysis.

Our key contributions are:

•

To study graph signals observations based on multiple graphs, we propose a mixture model of graph signals with general non-white excitation.
Moreover, the model supports missing data and general observation model such as the logit model for inference from binary data.

•

We formulate a joint inference and clustering problem via the MAP framework to infer node centrality and cluster observations according to the graphs. We design a batch EM algorithm under a unique low-rank plus sparse prior. We show that the EM algorithm converges to a stationary point at a sublinear rate. The algorithm supports efficient implementation for inference in the mixture model.

•

We develop a novel online EM algorithm based on the stochastic approximation (SA) scheme for streaming data. The algorithm processes each of the incoming observation on-the-fly and features a low memory footprint while delivering similar performances as the batch EM. Our analysis shows that any fixed point of the algorithm is a stationary point of MAP. We also describe an application of the online algorithm to blind anomaly detection.

•

We perform numerical experiments on synthetic and real data from brain and stock markets. The efficacy of the proposed algorithms support our findings.

Compared to the conference version

II-A

MAP Estimation with Reparametrization

This sub-section proposes a reparameterization technique to leverage the signal structure for finding a robust solution to Problem
1
.
We then formulate the maximum-a-priori (MAP) problem which will be the focus for the rest of this paper.

In the absence of knowledge on graph filters, Problem
1
will be ill-defined due to difficulty in extracting
{\bm{v}}_{1}^{(c)}
.
Taking inspirations from
[
17
,
16
]

Assumption 1

The graph filter

\textstyle\eta^{(c)}:=\max_{j=2,\dots,n}|h({\lambda}^{(c)}_{j})|/|h({\lambda}^{(c)}_{1})|<1,

is considered as
weak low pass

\eta^{(c)}\ll 1
, then
{\mathcal{}H}({\bm{A}}^{(c)})
is considered as

.

The naïve spectral clustering essentially utilizes difference in the subspaces

Particularly, the above example demonstrates that it is necessary to jointly consider the signal structure while clustering the graph signals. Our idea is to model and extract the hidden component(s) in
{{\mathcal{}H}}({\bm{A}}^{(c)}){\bm{B}}
that are indicative of the eigen-centrality vector, which thus provides the graph identifiers necessary for clustering. Observe the decomposition:

{{\mathcal{}H}}({\bm{A}}^{(c)}){\bm{B}}=({{\mathcal{}H}}({\bm{A}}^{(c)})-\rho{\bm{I}}){\bm{B}}+\rho{\bm{B}}\equiv{\bm{L}}_{c}+{\bm{B}}_{\rho},

(5)

for any
\rho\geq 0
.
The component
{\bm{L}}_{c}=({\mathcal{}H}({\bm{A}}^{(c)})-\rho{\bm{I}}){\bm{B}}
depends on the shifted graph filter
{\mathcal{}H}({\bm{A}}^{(c)})-\rho{\bm{I}}

that there exists

where the shifted graph filter enjoys a strictly lower low pass ratio, denoted as

For example, with

which can be satisfied for graphs with core-periphery structure
[
45
]
. Consequently, the matrix
{\bm{L}}_{c}
will be approximately rank-one.

Below, we show that the low-rank components
{\bm{L}}_{c}
are distinct for the different graphs that they are associated with.

(\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{(c)}_{1})^{\top}{\bm{v}}^{(c)}_{1}\geq 0

(6)

where
{\bm{V}}^{(c)}_{n-1}
is the last

{\bm{A}}^{(c)}
, and
\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{(c)}_{1},{\bm{q}}^{(c)}_{1}
are the top left, right singular vector of

The right hand side of (

{\mathcal{}O}(\tilde{\eta}^{(c)})

is approximately rank one, we obtain

With the condition

Establishing that

{\bm{B}}

As such, herein we model
{\bm{B}}
to be a sparse matrix which includes the special case of
{\bm{B}}={\bm{I}}
.
We observe that the matrix product
{{\mathcal{}H}}({\bm{A}}^{(c)}){\bm{B}}
admits a ‘low-rank plus sparse’ structure under the said premises.

MAP Estimation.

The above motivates us to explicitly account for the implicit components
{\bm{L}}_{c},{\bm{B}}_{\rho}
during the inference process through a careful re-parameterization. Denote
\Theta:=\big\{\{{\bm{L}}_{c}\}_{c=1}^{C},{\bm{B}}_{\rho},\{P_{c}\}_{c=1}^{C}\big\}
as the set of parameters. We yield the following
structured
MAP estimation problem:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\max_{\Theta\in\mathfrak{T}}&\displaystyle{\mathcal{}L}(\Theta):=\mathbb{E}\left[\log p({\bm{Y}}|\Theta,{\bm{Z}},{\bm{\Omega}})\right]+\log p(\Theta),\end{array}

(7)

where
\mathfrak{T}=\{\Theta:P_{c}\geq 0,~\sum_{c=1}^{C}P_{c}=1\}
and
p(\Theta)
models the prior on
\Theta
with the ‘low-rank plus sparse’ structure of
{\bm{L}}_{c},{\bm{B}}_{\rho}
. A natural choice for the prior distribution is

\textstyle p(\Theta)\propto\exp\big(-\lambda_{S}\|{\bm{B}}_{\rho}\|_{1}-\lambda_{L}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\|{\bm{L}}_{c}\|_{\star}\big),

(8)

where

\lambda_{S},\lambda_{L}\geq 0
are regularization parameters.
Furthermore, the expectation
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
is defined w.r.t. the observation law for
{\bm{Y}},{\bm{Z}},{\bm{\Omega}}
and
the log-likelihood function is given by:

\displaystyle\log p({\bm{y}}|\Theta,{\bm{z}},{\bm{\Omega}})=

\displaystyle\textstyle\log\left(\sum_{c=1}^{C}P_{c}e^{-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}{||{\bm{y}}-{\bm{\Omega}}\odot({\bm{L}}_{c}+{\bm{B}}_{\rho}){\bm{z}}||^{2}}}\right)+{\rm constant},

(9)

which is non-concave due to the nonlinear coupling between
P_{c}
,
{\bm{L}}_{c}
,
{\bm{B}}_{\rho}
, making
direct optimization of (
7
) intractable.

We notice that EM algorithms for classical models such as Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) cannot be directly applied to (
7
). The reason is that (
7
) entails regularization terms for the ‘low-rank plus sparse’ structure. Additionally, the missing data and excitation parameter
{\bm{z}}_{t}
have to be incorporated into the inference process.
In the next section, we concentrate on developing effective algorithms for (
7
) via the EM paradigm.

Remark 2

The poor performance of naïve SC with weak low pass filter can be explained by
[
17
, Lemma 2]
. Under mild conditions, the latter lemma shows:

{\|{\bm{L}}_{c}\|}/{\|{\bm{B}}_{\rho}\|}\lesssim({1-\eta^{(c)}})/({1+\eta^{(c)}}),

(10)

where
{\bm{L}}_{c},{\bm{B}}_{\rho}
are defined in (
5
). Now, if
\eta^{(c)}\approx 1
, we have
{\mathcal{}H}({\bm{A}}^{(c)}){\bm{B}}\approx{\bm{B}}_{\rho}
for any
c=1,\ldots,C
. Consequently, the correlation matrix
{\bm{Y}}^{\top}{\bm{Y}}
does not have the anticipated block structure that is necessary for successful clustering.

III
Batch EM Algorithm

This section develops a customized EM algorithm that is efficient to implement and enjoys desirable theoretical convergence properties.
Particularly, we focus on
batch
data where
m
samples
\{{\sf DP}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{m}
are available all at once.

To begin, let us fix
\widetilde{\Theta}=\big\{\{\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{c}\}_{c=1}^{C},\widetilde{\bm{B}}_{\rho},\{\widetilde{P}_{c}\}_{c=1}^{C}\big\}
and denote the conditional probability mass function for the latent r.v.
w_{t}
as

. The Jensen’s inequality implies the following lower bound on the log-likelihood term in (
7
):

\displaystyle\log p({\bm{y}}_{t}|\Theta,{\bm{z}}_{t},{\bm{\Omega}}_{t})

(11)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\sum_{c=1}^{C}p({\bm{y}}_{t},w_{t}=c|\Theta,{\bm{z}}_{t},{\bm{\Omega}}_{t})\frac{q(w_{t}=c|\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t})}{q(w_{t}=c|\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t})}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{w_{t}\sim q(\cdot|\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t})}\big[\log p({\bm{y}}_{t},w_{t}|\Theta,{\bm{z}}_{t},{\bm{\Omega}}_{t})\big]+\zeta(\widetilde{\Theta}),

where
\zeta(\widetilde{\Theta})
is a function that only depends on the fixed
\widetilde{\Theta}
.
Taking the batch data setting into consideration, the above led us to the batch surrogate optimization problem:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\max_{\Theta}~

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{t=1}^{m}\mathbbm{E}_{w_{t}\sim q(\cdot|\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t})}[\log p({\bm{y}}_{t},w_{t}|\Theta,{\bm{z}}_{t},{\bm{\Omega}}_{t})]

\displaystyle\textstyle-\lambda_{S}\|{\bm{B}}_{\rho}\|_{1}-\lambda_{L}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\|{\bm{L}}_{c}\|_{\star}

(12)

s.t.

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{c=1}^{C}P_{c}=1,P_{c}\geq 0,~c=1,\ldots,C.

Let us take a closer look at the first term in the objective function of (
12
). For
t=1,\ldots,m
, the Bayes’ rule implies

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{w_{t}\sim q(\cdot|\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t})}[\log p({\bm{y}}_{t},w_{t}|\Theta,{\bm{z}}_{t},{\bm{\Omega}}_{t})]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{w_{t}}[\mathbbm{1}(w_{t}=c)\log\mathbbm{P}(w_{t}=c)p({\bm{y}}_{t}|\Theta,{\bm{z}}_{t},{\bm{\Omega}}_{t},w_{t}=c)]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{w_{t}}\big[\mathbbm{1}(w_{t}=c)\{\log(P_{c})+\log p({\bm{y}}_{t}|{{\bm{B}}_{\rho}},{\bm{L}}_{c},{\bm{z}}_{t},{\bm{\Omega}}_{t})\}\big],\end{split}

where we used
\mathbbm{P}(w_{t}=c)=P_{c}
and the dependence on
q(\cdot|\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t})
were omitted for brevity. Moreover,

\displaystyle\log p({\bm{y}}_{t}|{{\bm{B}}_{\rho}},{\bm{L}}_{c},{\bm{z}}_{t},{\bm{\Omega}}_{t})=

(13)

\displaystyle\frac{\langle{\bm{\Omega}}_{t}\odot({\bm{L}}_{c}+{{\bm{B}}_{\rho}}){\bm{z}}_{t}|{\bm{y}}_{t}\rangle}{\sigma^{2}}-\frac{||{\bm{\Omega}}_{t}\odot({\bm{L}}_{c}+{{\bm{B}}_{\rho}}){\bm{z}}_{t}||^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}+\widetilde{\zeta}_{t}(\widetilde{\Theta}).

where
\widetilde{\zeta}_{t}(\widetilde{\Theta}):=\zeta(\widetilde{\Theta})+\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\|{\bm{y}}_{t}\|^{2}
.
The above expressions can be simplified as

\begin{split}&\langle{\bm{\Omega}}_{t}\odot({\bm{L}}_{c}+{{\bm{B}}_{\rho}}){\bm{z}}_{t}|{\bm{y}}_{t}\rangle={\rm Tr}(({\bm{L}}_{c}+{{\bm{B}}_{\rho}})^{\top}\,{\bm{\Omega}}_{t}\odot{\bm{y}}_{t}{\bm{z}}_{t}^{\top}),\\[2.84544pt]
&||{\bm{\Omega}}_{t}\odot({\bm{L}}_{c}+{{\bm{B}}_{\rho}}){\bm{z}}_{t}||^{2}\\
&\textstyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\rm Tr}\big(({\bm{L}}_{c}+{{\bm{B}}_{\rho}})^{\top}{\bm{e}}_{i}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{\top}({\bm{L}}_{c}+{{\bm{B}}_{\rho}})\,\Omega_{t,i}{\bm{z}}_{t}{\bm{z}}_{t}^{\top}\big).\end{split}

Define the conditional probability for the event that the
t
th data tuple is associated to the
c
th graph:

\displaystyle p_{c}(\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t}):=\mathbb{E}_{w_{t}\sim q(\cdot|\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t})}\big[\mathbbm{1}(w_{t}=c)\big]

(14)

\displaystyle=\frac{\widetilde{P}_{c}\exp(\frac{-1}{2\sigma^{2}}||({\bm{y}}_{t}-{\bm{\Omega}}_{t}\odot(\widetilde{\bm{B}}_{\rho}+\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{c}){\bm{z}}_{t})||^{2})}{\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}\widetilde{P}_{c^{\prime}}\exp(\frac{-1}{2\sigma^{2}}||({\bm{y}}_{t}-{\bm{\Omega}}_{t}\odot(\widetilde{\bm{B}}_{\rho}+\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{c^{\prime}}){\bm{z}}_{t})||^{2})}.

and the sufficient statistics:

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle\overline{P}_{c}^{\widetilde{\Theta}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{t=1}^{m}p_{c}(\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t}),

(15)

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle\overline{{\bm{Y}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c}^{\widetilde{\Theta}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{t=1}^{m}p_{c}(\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t})\,{\bm{\Omega}}_{t}\odot{\bm{y}}_{t}{\bm{z}}_{t}^{\top},

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle\overline{{\bm{Z}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c,i}^{\widetilde{\Theta}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{t=1}^{m}p_{c}(\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t})\,\Omega_{t,i}{\bm{z}}_{t}{\bm{z}}_{t}^{\top}.

For any
\widetilde{\Theta}\in\mathfrak{T}
, the lower bound surrogate objective function of (
12

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Theta\,|\,{\widetilde{\Theta}})=\widetilde{\zeta}(\widetilde{\Theta})-\lambda_{S}||{\bm{B}}_{\rho}||_{1}-\sum_{c=1}^{C}\lambda_{L}||{\bm{L}}_{c}||_{\ast}

(16)

\displaystyle+\sum_{c=1}^{C}\Bigg\{\overline{P}_{c}^{\widetilde{\Theta}}\log(P_{c})+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}{\rm Tr}\big(({\bm{L}}_{c}+{\bm{B}}_{\rho})^{\top}\,\overline{{\bm{Y}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c}^{\widetilde{\Theta}}\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr\big(({\bm{L}}_{c}+{\bm{B}}_{\rho})^{\top}{\bm{e}}_{i}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{\top}({\bm{L}}_{c}+{\bm{B}}_{\rho})\overline{{\bm{Z}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c,i}^{\widetilde{\Theta}}\big)\Bigg\},

which is a concave function in
\Theta
and we have defined
\widetilde{\zeta}(\widetilde{\Theta})=(1/m)\sum_{t=1}^{m}\widetilde{\zeta}_{t}(\widetilde{\Theta})
. For any
\Theta,\widetilde{\Theta}\in\mathfrak{T}
, notice that it holds

\textstyle{\mathcal{}L}({\Theta})\geq\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Theta\,|\,{\widetilde{\Theta}}),~~{\mathcal{}L}({\Theta})=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Theta\,|\,{{\Theta}}).

(17)

III-A

Extension to Logit Model

We conclude this section by extending Algorithm
1
to tackling Problem
1
with
binary
graph signals observations. For example, this applies if the latter consists of vote data.
Consider the case without missing data, i.e.,
{\bm{\Omega}}_{t}={\bf 1}
and focus on a logit observation model. The observed data
\{{\sf DP}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{m}=\{{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{m}
satisfy:

\mathbbm{P}(y_{t,j}=Y)=\frac{\exp((\widetilde{y}_{t,j}+b)Y)}{1+\exp(\widetilde{y}_{t,j}+b)},~~Y\in\{0,1\},

(26)

for
j=1,\ldots,n
,
where
b<0
is the bias parameter of the logit model and
\widetilde{y}_{t,j}
is the
j
th element of the vector:

\textstyle\widetilde{\bm{y}}_{t}=\sum_{c=1}^{C}\mathbbm{1}(w_{t}=c){\mathcal{}H}({\bm{A}}^{(c)}){\bm{B}}{\bm{z}}_{t}.

(27)

Similar to Sec.
II-A
, we further adopt the parameterization with
{\mathcal{}H}({\bm{A}}^{(c)}){\bm{B}}={\bm{L}}_{c}+{\bm{B}}_{\rho}
.

The EM algorithm on the above model can be developed similarly as Algorithm
1
. In particular, the derivations up to (
11
) remain valid. Now, denote the conditional probability of the graph identifier
w_{t}
[cf. (
14
)] for the
t
th data tuple as:

\displaystyle p_{c}^{\sf lg}(\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t})=\frac{\widetilde{P}_{c}\Pi_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\exp(y_{t,j}\widetilde{\nu}_{t,j,c})}{1+\exp(\widetilde{\nu}_{t,j,c})}}{\sum_{c^{\prime}=1}^{C}\widetilde{P}_{c^{\prime}}\Pi_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\exp(y_{t,j}\widetilde{\nu}_{t,j,c^{\prime}})}{1+\exp(\widetilde{\nu}_{t,j,c^{\prime}})}},

(28)

where
\widetilde{\nu}_{t,j,c}:=b+{\bm{e}}_{j}^{\top}(\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{c}+\widetilde{\bm{B}}_{\rho}){\bm{z}}_{t}
. We observe that the following surrogate objective function lower bounds the MAP objective function with the logit model (
26
):

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\sf logit}(\Theta\,|\,{\widetilde{\Theta}})={\rm constant}-\lambda_{S}||{\bm{B}}_{\rho}||_{1}-\sum_{c=1}^{C}\lambda_{L}||{\bm{L}}_{c}||_{\ast}

(29)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{m}\sum_{t=1}^{C}\sum_{t=1}^{m}p_{c}^{\sf lg}(\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t})\Bigg\{\log(P_{c})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{t,j}\nu_{t,j,c}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\sum_{t=1}^{m}p_{c}^{\sf lg}(\widetilde{\Theta},{\sf DP}_{t})\sum_{j=1}^{n}\log(1+\exp(\nu_{t,j,c})),

where
\nu_{t,j,c}:=b+{\bm{e}}_{j}^{\top}({\bm{L}}_{c}+{\bm{B}}_{\rho}){\bm{z}}_{t}
is a linear function of the decision variables
{\bm{L}}_{c},{\bm{B}}_{\rho}
.

We observe that (
29
) is a concave function in
\Theta
. To develop the EM algorithm, the
M
-step in Algorithm

can now be replaced by maximizing (
29
) w.r.t.
\Theta
when
\Theta^{k-1}
is given. On the other hand,
E
-step only involves evaluating

according to (
28

Compared to the case with Gaussian observation, the
M
-step involves (
29

m\gg 1
. This is caused by the nonlinear log-likelihood function associated with the logit model (
26
).

Online EM Algorithm

This section considers tackling Problem
1
under
streaming data
. We focus on an online learning process where the data tuple is revealed sequentially. Particularly, at time
t
, we only observe the data tuple

that is generated from the model (

We aim to design an
online
algorithm for the MAP problem (
7

t
th iteration:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\max_{\Theta}~

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\sf DP,W\sim q(\cdot|{\Theta}^{t-1},{\sf DP})}\big[\log p({\bm{Y}},W|\Theta,{\bm{Z}},{\bm{\Omega}})\big]-\Psi(\Theta)

\displaystyle\text{with}~~\Psi(\Theta)

\displaystyle:=\textstyle\lambda_{S}\|{\bm{B}}_{\rho}\|_{1}+\lambda_{L}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\|{\bm{L}}_{c}\|_{\star}

(30)

\displaystyle\textstyle\displaystyle+\epsilon\sum_{c=1}^{C-1}\log(P_{c})+\epsilon\log(1-\sum_{c=1}^{C-1}P_{c}),

where the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{\sf DP}[\cdot]
is taken w.r.t. the random generative model for the data tuple
{\sf DP}=\{{\bm{Y}},{\bm{Z}},{\bm{\Omega}}\}

are regularization parameters.
Compared to (
12

The surrogate objective function of (

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{}L}_{\sf ol}(\Theta;\overline{P}_{c}^{{\Theta}^{t-1}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c}^{{\Theta}^{t-1}},\overline{{\bm{Z}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c,i}^{{\Theta}^{t-1}},\forall c,i):=-\Psi(\Theta)

(31)

\displaystyle\textstyle+\sum_{c=1}^{C}\big\{\overline{P}_{c}^{{\Theta}^{t-1}}\log(P_{c})+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}{\rm Tr}\big(({\bm{L}}_{c}+{\bm{B}}_{\rho})^{\top}\,\overline{{\bm{Y}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c}^{{\Theta}^{t-1}}\big)

\displaystyle\textstyle-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr\big(({\bm{L}}_{c}+{\bm{B}}_{\rho})^{\top}{\bm{e}}_{i}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{\top}({\bm{L}}_{c}+{\bm{B}}_{\rho})\overline{{\bm{Z}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c,i}^{{\Theta}^{t-1}}\big)\big\},

\max_{\Theta}~\mathbb{E}_{\sf DP}[\log p({\bm{Y}}|\Theta,{\bm{Z}},\bm{\Omega})]-\Psi(\Theta),

(35)

If the sufficient statistics
(\overline{P}_{c},\overline{{\bm{Y}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c},\overline{{\bm{Z}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c,i},\forall c,i)
satisfies (
34
), then
\overline{\Theta}\in\Gamma
. Conversely, assume in addition, the maximizer of
\widetilde{\mathcal{}L}_{\sf ol}(\Theta;{P}_{c},{{\bm{Y}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c},{{\bm{Z}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c,i})
is unique for any sufficient statistics [cf. line
6
of
Algorithm

2
]. Then if
\overline{\Theta}\in\Gamma
, the tuple
(\overline{P}_{c},\overline{{\bm{Y}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c},\overline{{\bm{Z}}{\bm{Z}}}_{c,i},\forall c,i)
satisfies (
34
).

The proof, which extends
[
51
,
53
]
to the regularized MAP setting in (
35
), is relegated to Appendix
B
.

The above proposition shows that if the SA recursion (
33
) converges to a fixed point, then such fixed point must lead to the parameter
\overline{\Theta}
stationary to the MAP problem (
35
). The convergence of (
33
) to a fixed point typically requires

\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\beta_{t}=\infty,~\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\beta_{t}^{2}<\infty,

(36)

and additional conditions such as Lipschitz continuity of the population sufficient statistics map (
32
). In the interest of space, the readers are referred to
[
54
,
52
]
for details.
We remark that the stochastic gradient EM algorithm in
[
55
]
is an alternative to
Algorithm

2
. However,
[
55
]
applies stochastic gradient in the parameter (
\Theta
) space, which can be less computationally efficient.

IV-A

Online Joint Inference & Anomaly Detection

We conclude by discussing an application of
Algorithm

2
to
online
joint inference and anomaly detection of graph signals that are not generated from one of the candidate graphs,
G^{(c)}
,
c=1,\ldots,C
, in (
2
).
Detecting if graph signals are originated from an ‘abnormal’ graph is an important task for, e.g., power systems, pathological signal detection, see
[
33
,
30
]
.
While prior works require the normal graph topology to be known a-priori, our goal is to simultaneously perform graph inference through estimating central nodes and detect these abnormal graph signals. In this setting, an
online
algorithm is preferred as we aim to detect anomalies as soon as possible.

At time
t\geq 1
, we consider the graph signal
{\bm{y}}_{t}
(and the latent variable
{\bm{z}}_{t}
) satisfying
{\bm{y}}_{t}=\bm{\Omega}_{t}\odot{\mathcal{}H}({\bm{A}}_{t}){\bm{B}}{\bm{z}}_{t}+{\bm{e}}_{t}
akin to (
2
). Herein,
{\bm{A}}_{t}
denotes the adjacency matrix of a graph
G_{t}
that
{\bm{y}}_{t}
is originated from.
Accordingly,
{\bm{y}}_{t}
is said to be a
normal signal
if
G_{t}\in\{G^{(c)}\}_{c=1}^{C}
; conversely, the signal is said to be
abnormal
if it is generated from an outlier graph
G_{t}\notin\{G^{(c)}\}_{c=1}^{C}
. We define the binary hypothesis classes:

\displaystyle\texttt{(normal)}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{}H}_{0}:G_{t}\in\{{G}^{(c)}\}_{c=1}^{C},

(37)

\displaystyle\texttt{(abnormal)}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{}H}_{1}:G_{t}\notin\{{G}^{(c)}\}_{c=1}^{C}.

We shall work with cases where
{\mathcal{}H}_{1}
occurs with a lower probability than
{\mathcal{}H}_{0}
to allow for successful graph inference. Moreover, under
{\mathcal{}H}_{1}
, the outlier graph is sufficiently different from the normal graphs in terms of its eigencentrality.

Under H
1
, we expect the distance
\min_{c=1,...,C}\|{\bm{y}}_{t}-\bm{\Omega}_{t}\odot{\mathcal{}H}({\bm{A}}^{(c)}){\bm{B}}{\bm{z}}_{t}\|^{2}
to be small under
{\mathcal{}H}_{0}
and large under
{\mathcal{}H}_{1}
. While determining such distance would require knowledge of the normal graphs, we utilize the online EM algorithm and replace the latter using up-to-date estimates. This leads to the online detector: let
\kappa>0
be a user-defined threshold,

\min_{c=1,\dots,C}||{\bm{y}}_{t}-{\bm{\Omega}}_{t}\odot({\bm{L}}^{t-1}_{c}+{\bm{B}}^{t-1}_{\rho}){\bm{z}}_{t}||^{2}\mathop{\lessgtr}_{{\mathcal{}H}_{1}}^{{\mathcal{}H}_{0}}\kappa.

(38)

Note that
{\bm{L}}^{t-1}_{c}+{\bm{B}}^{t-1}_{\rho}\approx{\mathcal{}H}({\bm{A}}^{(c)}){\bm{B}}
and the estimation quality improves as
Algorithm

2
gathers more data samples. We expect the detection performance to improve as
t
grows. Finally, we incorporate the outlier rejection mechanism into
Algorithm

2
by a slight modification; see line
4
.

Remark 3

Compared to
[
33
,
30
]
, our approach requires additional information on the excitation parameter
{\bm{z}}_{t}
. On the other hand, our approach is capable of simultaneous graph learning and abnormal graph detection.

VI
Conclusions

We study a joint graph inference problem on the challenging mixture model of filtered graph signals under general (non-white) excitation, weak low pass graph filters and missing data. We design an efficient algorithm based on EM and develop the latter’s online extension for streaming data. The online algorithm is further applied to abnormal graph signals detection. Efficacy of the proposed algorithms are verified with convergence analysis and numerical experiments.

Appendix A
Proof of Proposition
1

The first part follows directly from the steps of EM algorithm as the latter are majorization-minimization iterations for the regularized log-likelihood. For any
k\geq 0
,

\textstyle{\mathcal{}L}({\Theta}^{k+1})-{\mathcal{}L}({\Theta^{k})}\geq\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Theta^{k+1}\,|\,{\Theta}^{k})-\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(\Theta^{k}\,|\,{{\Theta}}^{k})\geq 0,

(42)

where the last inequality holds since
\Theta^{k+1}
is a maximizer to the surrogate
\widetilde{\mathcal{}L}(\Theta^{k+1}|\Theta^{k})
(
16
) and
\widetilde{\mathcal{}L}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k})={\mathcal{}L}(\Theta^{k})
.

For the second part of the proposition, we observe that as

{\tt D}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1})={\mathcal{}L}(\Theta^{k})-\widetilde{\mathcal{}L}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1})\leq{\mathcal{}L}(\Theta^{k})-{\mathcal{}L}(\Theta^{k-1}),

where the inequality follows from
\widetilde{\mathcal{}L}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1})\geq\widetilde{\mathcal{}L}(\Theta^{k-1}|\Theta^{k-1})={\mathcal{}L}(\Theta^{k-1})
.
Since the MAP problem has bounded objective value
{\mathcal{}L}(\Theta)\leq{\mathcal{}L}^{\star}
. It holds

\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{K_{\sf max}}{\tt D}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1})\leq{\mathcal{}L}^{\star}-{\mathcal{}L}(\Theta^{0}),

(43)

for any
K_{\sf max}\geq 1
.
Now, as the gradient w.r.t.
\Theta
of
{\tt D}(\Theta|\widetilde{\Theta})
is
L
-Lipschitz, it implies

\begin{split}0&\leq{\tt D}\left(\Theta^{k}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{L}}{\nabla}{\tt D}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1})\,|\,\Theta^{k-1}\right)\\
&\leq{\tt D}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1})-\frac{1}{2L}\|{\nabla}{\tt D}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1})\|^{2},\\
&\Longrightarrow\frac{1}{2L}\|{\nabla}{\tt D}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1})\|^{2}\leq{\tt D}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1}).\end{split}

(44)

Summing up both sides from
k=1
to
k=K_{\sf max}
and using (
43
) yields

\min_{k=1,\ldots,K_{\sf max}}\|{\nabla}{\tt D}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1})\|^{2}\leq\frac{2L}{K_{\sf max}}\big({\mathcal{}L}^{\star}-{\mathcal{}L}(\Theta^{0})\big).

(45)

Furthermore, the directional derivative satisfies

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{}L}^{\prime}(\Theta^{k};\Theta-\Theta^{k})\\
&=\langle{\nabla}{\tt D}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1})\,|\,\Theta-\Theta^{k}\rangle+\widetilde{\mathcal{}L}^{\prime}(\Theta^{k};\Theta-\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1}).\end{split}

As
\Theta^{k}
maximizes the concave function
\widetilde{\mathcal{}L}(\Theta|\Theta^{k-1})
, it holds

\widetilde{\mathcal{}L}^{\prime}(\Theta^{k};\Theta-\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1})\leq 0,~\forall~\Theta\in\mathfrak{T}.

(46)

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, this implies that

\sup_{\Theta\in\mathfrak{T}}\frac{{\mathcal{}L}^{\prime}(\Theta^{k};\Theta-\Theta^{k})}{\|\Theta-\Theta^{k}\|}\leq\|{\nabla}{\tt D}(\Theta^{k}|\Theta^{k-1})\|,

(47)

where
\frac{0}{0}=0
.
Combining with (
45
) leads to the conclusion.

{\Theta}

) where

(51)

) into the fixed point condition (

Radiative gravastar with thermal spectrum – Sudden vacuum condensation without gravitational collapse – Preprint: AP-GR-186 Preprint: NITEP 150 Preprint: YITP-22-141 Preprint: RUP-22-24
Ken-ichi Nakao 1,2, Kazumasa Okabayashi 3, Tomohiro Harada 4

Abstract
The gravastar is an exotic compact object proposed as a final product of gravitational collapse of a massive object in order to resolve problems associated with black holes. It is enclosed by a thin crust and the inside of it is occupied by the positive cosmological constant. Recently, the present authors studied quantum particle creation through spherically symmetric gravitational collapse to form a gravastar, and showed that the newly formed gravastar emits thermal radiation with the Gibbons-Hawking temperature of its de Sitter core. In this paper, in order to understand more about the thermal radiation associated with the gravastar formation, we investigate the quantum particle creation in another toy model of the gravastar formation; a star with the hollow inside suddenly becomes a gravastar through gravitational vacuum condensation. We find that the thermal radiation is emitted from the gravastar just formed also in the present model. The thermal radiation from the gravastar just formed comes from the change of the geometry inside the star accompanied by gravitational vacuum condensate.

II
A toy model of a gravastar formation due to gravitational vacuum condensation

As mentioned, we study a toy model of the gravastar formation through sudden gravitational vacuum condensation in

The outside of the star is assumed to be vacuum. By Birkhoff’s theorem, the outside domain is described

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr}{f(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(2)

(3)

The constant

The following inequality should be satisfied so that the timelike Killing vector field in the Schwarzschild domain

We assume that the crust is infinitesimally thin.
The world line of an observer rest on the crust is represented

a_{\rm s}=a(\eta_{\rm s})~~~{\rm and}~~~f_{\rm s}=f(r_{\rm s}).

(6)

Note that

From the first equality of Eq. (

III
Quantum particle creation

As in Paper I, we study the quantum dynamics of the massless scalar field
\phi
in the spherically symmetric
asymptotically flat spacetime by the semi-classical treatment in which the effect of the quantum field on the classical
spacetime geometry is ignored.
By adopting the double null coordinates, the infinitesimal world interval can be written in the following form;

ds^{2}=-h^{2}(u,v)dudv+r^{2}(u,v)d\Omega^{2},

(29)

where
u
and
v
are the retarded and the advanced time coordinates, respectively.
In the case of the Minkowski spacetime,
h=1
and the areal radius
r

agrees with
(v-u)/2

u=v
holds at
r=0

By contrast, in general dynamical cases, the symmetry center
r=0
is not
v=u
but

v=F(u).

(30)

In order to study the quantum effect in this spacetime, we consider the massless free scalar field which is the simplest model but
sufficient for the present purpose. The Lagrangian density is given as

{\cal L}=-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)\partial_{\nu}\phi,

(31)

where
g_{\mu\nu}
,
g^{\mu\nu}
and
g
are the metric tensor, its inverse and its determinant, respectively.
The average of radiation power of the massless scalar field due to the quantum effect caused by the spacetime curvature is estimated
through the expectation value of the stress-energy-momentum tensor

as

\displaystyle P(u)=\oint_{r\rightarrow\infty}\langle 0|(\hat{T}_{uu}-\hat{T}_{vv})|0\rangle r^{2}d\Omega=\frac{1}{48\pi}\kappa^{2}(u),

(32)

where

\displaystyle\kappa(u):=-\frac{\dfrac{d^{2}F(u)}{du^{2}}}{\dfrac{dF(u)}{du}},

(33)

and we have ignored the total derivative term in Eq. (
32
) since it does not contribute the total emission.
It is known that if the adiabatic condition
|d\kappa/du|\ll\kappa^{2}
holds, the spectrum of the radiation is thermal with the temperature
BLSV:2011

k_{\rm B}T=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}.

(34)

These results are derived by invoking the S-wave approximation in which only the contributions of the spherically symmetric mode function
is taken into account

{\cal G}(t,r_{*})
behaves as

{\cal G}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\omega}r}e^{-i\omega v}

(35)

in the past null infinity,
u\rightarrow-\infty
, whereas it behaves as

{\cal G}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\omega}r}e^{-i\omega F(u)}

(36)

in the future null infinity,

in the past null infinity,
\omega_{-}
, is equal to
\omega

whereas that in the future null infinity,
\omega_{+}

\omega_{+}=\dfrac{d[\omega F(u)]}{du}.

(37)

Hence the redshift,

is given as

1+z=\frac{\omega_{-}}{\omega_{+}}=\left(\frac{dF(u)}{du}\right)^{-1}.

(38)

Thus, the definition of
\kappa
given as Eq. (
33

\kappa=\frac{d\ln(1+z)}{du}.

(39)

By estimating the derivative of redshift

of the spherically symmetric mode function

with respect to the retarded time

\kappa
and
the radiation power

through Eq. (
32

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{f_{\rm s}}}{a_{\rm s+}\sqrt{1-\chi_{\rm s+}^{\prime 2}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{\rm s+}}\ln\left(a_{\rm s+}\sqrt{\frac{1+\chi_{\rm s+}^{\prime}}{1-\chi_{\rm s+}^{\prime}}}\right)=\frac{6H^{2}r_{\rm s}\gamma_{+}(\gamma_{+}-1)\sqrt{f_{\rm s}}}{\left[1-H^{2}r_{\rm s}^{2}\gamma_{+}^{4}(2\gamma_{+}-3)^{2}\right]^{3/2}}\varepsilon^{-1}+{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{0}),

(64)

where we have used
a_{\rm s+}=\dfrac{r_{\rm s}}{\chi_{\rm s+}}
to obtain a derivative of
a_{\rm s+}
with respect to
\eta_{\rm s+}
.
This result implies that the mode function associated with the null geodesics of Class II
causes very large radiation power
P\propto\varepsilon^{-2}
, i.e., the burst of the quantum radiation.

V.3
Class III: thermal radiation

The mode function along the null geodesics in Class III also causes the non-vanishing radiation power.
In this case, the null geodesic enters the star with hollow inside and comes out from the gravastar. We have

a_{\rm s-}=1
,
\chi_{\rm s-}=r_{\rm s}
,
\chi_{\rm s-}^{\prime}=0=\chi_{\rm s-}^{\prime\prime}
, whereas the scale factor
a_{\rm s+}
is given by Eq. (
15
),
and then Eq. (
16
) leads to
\chi_{\rm s+}^{\prime}=-Hr_{\rm s}
and
\chi_{\rm s+}^{\prime\prime}=0
. Equation (
57
) leads to

1+z=a_{\rm s+}\sqrt{\frac{1+\chi_{\rm s+}^{\prime}}{1-\chi_{\rm s+}^{\prime}}}=-\frac{1}{H(\eta_{\rm s+}-\eta_{\rm c})}\sqrt{\frac{1-Hr_{\rm s}}{1+Hr_{\rm s}}}.

(65)

Substituting this result into Eq. (
59
), we have

\kappa=\frac{d\ln(1+z)}{du}=H\sqrt{\frac{f_{\rm s}}{1-H^{2}r_{\rm s}^{2}}}=\frac{H}{\alpha},

(66)

where
\alpha
is a positive parameter defined as

\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{1-H^{2}r_{\rm s}^{2}}{f_{\rm s}}}.

(67)

This result implies that
\kappa
is exactly constant,
and hence the adiabatic condition
|d\kappa/du|\ll\kappa^{2}
is trivially satisfied.
The spectrum of the radiation is thermal with the temperature

T=T_{\rm GH}\sqrt{\frac{f_{\rm s}}{1-H^{2}r_{\rm s}^{2}}}=\frac{T_{\rm GH}}{\alpha}.

(68)

where

k_{\rm B}T_{\rm GH}=\frac{H}{2\pi}

(69)

is the Gibbons-Hawking temperature of the de Sitter spacetime with the Hubble constant
H

Gibbons-Hawking
.
Here it is worth noting that
\alpha
is equal to the redshift factor suffered by a null particle moving
from the symmetry center to the static observer at infinity in the static gravastar phase (see Appendix C).
Hence the temperature
T
of the quantum radiation can be regarded as the redshifted Gibbons-Hawking temperature
T_{\rm GH}
.

From Eqs. (
21
) and (
68
), we find the relation between the temperature,
T
, and the sign of the energy per unit area,
\sigma
, of the crust as follows;

\begin{cases}T<T_{\rm GH}&{\rm for}~\sigma>0,\\
T=T_{\rm GH}&{\rm for}~\sigma=0,\\
T>T_{\rm GH}&{\rm for}~\sigma<0.\end{cases}

(70)

It is an intriguing fact that the weak energy condition
\sigma\geq 0

imposes an upper limit
T_{\rm GH}
on the temperature
T
of the quantum radiation.

Let us calculate the duration
\Delta t
of this thermal emission. The first null of Class III comes out from the star at

\eta=\eta_{1}:=-H^{-1}(1-\varepsilon).

(71)

The conformal time
\eta=\eta_{2}
at which the last null of Class III comes out from the star is
the intersection between the outgoing null

\chi=\eta-(-H^{-1}+r_{\rm s})

(72)

and the world line of the surface of the star which is given by Eq. (
16

\eta_{\rm c}=H^{-1}\varepsilon/2
(see Fig.
1

Hence, we have

\eta_{2}=-\frac{H^{-1}-r_{\rm s}\left(1+\varepsilon/2\right)}{1+Hr_{\rm s}}.

(73)

By using Eq. (
28

\displaystyle\Delta t

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{\frac{1-H^{2}r_{\rm s}^{2}}{f_{\rm s}}}\int_{\eta_{1}}^{\eta_{2}}\frac{d\eta}{H\eta-\varepsilon/2}=H^{-1}\sqrt{\frac{1-H^{2}r_{\rm s}^{2}}{f_{\rm s}}}\ln\left|\frac{(1-\varepsilon/2)(1+Hr_{\rm s})}{1+\varepsilon/2-Hr_{\rm s}}\right|

\displaystyle=H^{-1}\alpha\ln\left|\frac{1+Hr_{\rm s}}{1-Hr_{\rm s}}\right|+{\cal O}(\varepsilon).

(74)

Thus, the total energy
E
of the emitted thermal radiation is given as

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{48\pi}\kappa^{2}\Delta t=\frac{H}{48\pi}\sqrt{\frac{f_{\rm s}}{1-H^{2}r_{\rm s}^{2}}}\ln\left|\frac{(1-\varepsilon/2)(1+Hr_{\rm s})}{1+\varepsilon/2-Hr_{\rm s}}\right|

\displaystyle=\dfrac{H}{48\pi\alpha}\ln\left|\dfrac{1+Hr_{\rm s}}{1-Hr_{\rm s}}\right|+{\cal O}(\varepsilon).

(75)

If the weak energy condition
\sigma\geq 0
holds, the parameter
\alpha

\alpha\geq 1

and hence

is bounded above by a value of the order
H

diverges as the temperature
T

From Eq. (
67

H=\dfrac{1}{r_{\rm s}}\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}f_{\rm s}}
and hence, by using
r_{\rm s}=\dfrac{2GM}{1-f_{\rm s}}

is rewritten as

H=\frac{(1-f_{\rm s})\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}f_{\rm s}}}{2GM}=1.1\times 10^{-29}(1-f_{\rm s})\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}f_{\rm s}}\left(\frac{M_{\odot}}{M}\right){\rm J},

(76)

where

is the solar mass.

V.4
Class IV: 2nd burst

The null geodesic of Class IV enters the star in the transition phase and comes out from the gravastar phase.
As in the case of the 1st burst, instead of

This result implies that the mode function propagating along null geodesics of Class IV also causes the radiation power

V.5
Class V: no radiation phase again

The null geodesics of Class V enter and come out from the star in the gravastar phase. If the null geodesic enters the star at

and
\chi_{\rm s-}

Substituting

(87)

From this result and Eq. (

The mode function propagating along the null geodesics of Class V causes no radiation power.

VI
Summary

We studied the quantum radiation in the gravastar formation process through the
vacuum condensation in a spherically symmetric star with constant mass and constant radius.
The star is assumed to be initially composed of a crust of infinitesimally thin width, which encloses
a vacuum domain. In other words, the initial situation is a star with hollow inside.
The inside of the star, i.e., the vacuum domain enclosed by the infinitesimally thin crust
is assumed to suddenly get occupied by the positive cosmological constant, and the star eventually
becomes a gravastar. By virtue of the spherical symmetry, the initial vacuum domain enclosed by the crust is
Minkowskian, whereas the inside of the gravastar as a final product has the de Sitter geometry.

The sudden gravitational vacuum condensation leads two bursts of the particle creation and the thermal radiation
between these bursts even without the event horizon.
Therefore the thermal spectrum does not necessarily imply the existence of hidden information.
In Paper I, we assumed

1-\dfrac{2GM}{r_{\rm s}}=1-H^{2}r_{\rm s}^{2}

(88)

in accordance with the original proposal by Mazur and Mottola, and in this case,
the temperature of the thermal spectrum is equal to the Gibbons-Hawking one.
On the other hand, in case that Eq. (
88
) does not hold, the temperature of the thermal radiation is proportional
to
\sqrt{\dfrac{f_{\rm s}}{1-H^{2}r_{\rm s}^{2}}}
which can be regarded as the redshift effect.
It may be very high in the case of
f_{\rm s}\gg 1-H^{2}r_{\rm s}^{2}

as well as the total radiated energy
E
, while it is bounded above by
T_{\rm GH}
if the weak energy condition
equivalent to
0<f_{\rm s}\leq 1-Hr_{\rm s}^{2}
is satisfied.
Although the high temperature needs the violation of the weak energy condition,
it is not clear whether the weak energy condition holds
in the process of the gravitational vacuum condensation in
accordance with the new physics beyond Einstein theory.

Appendix B
On reasonable energy conditions at the crust of the gravastar

We show the constraint on the radius coming from the reasonable energy conditions.
By using the parameter
\alpha
defined as Eq. (
67
), Eqs. (
21
) and (
22
) are rewritten in the form,

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{f_{\rm s}}}{4\pi Gr_{\rm s}}\left(\alpha-1\right),

(107)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=\frac{2+\alpha+\alpha(1-4\alpha)f_{\rm s}}{16\pi Gr_{\rm s}\alpha\sqrt{f_{\rm s}}},

(108)

and we obtain

\frac{p}{\sigma}=\frac{1}{4\alpha(\alpha-1)}\left[\frac{2+\alpha}{f_{\rm s}}+\alpha(1-4\alpha)\right].

(109)

The weak energy condition requires
\alpha\geq 1
. In this case, we find that the dominant energy condition,

\frac{\alpha+2}{\alpha(8\alpha-5)}\leq f_{\rm s}\leq\frac{\alpha+2}{3\alpha}.

Since the inequality,

has to hold by its definition,
we can see from this equation that the dominant energy condition does not hold for
\alpha=1
.
Hence, the dominant energy condition requires
\alpha>1
, and hereafter we assume this inequality.

From Eqs. (
67
) and (
110
), we have

\frac{\alpha(\alpha+2)}{8\alpha-5}\leq 1-H^{2}r_{\rm s}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{3}\alpha(\alpha+2).

\frac{\alpha(\alpha+2)}{8\alpha-5}<1,

(112)

and this inequality leads to
1<\alpha<5
. The minimum value of the left-most-side of Eq. (
111

\alpha=\alpha_{\rm min}:=\frac{1}{8}\left(5+\sqrt{105}\right).

(113)

Hence we have

1-H^{2}r_{\rm s}^{2}\geq\left.\frac{\alpha(\alpha+2)}{8\alpha-5}\right|_{\alpha=\alpha_{\rm min}}=\frac{13+\sqrt{105}}{32}\simeq 0.726.

(114)

The constraint

Hence, if the dominant energy condition is strictly satisfied, the areal radius,

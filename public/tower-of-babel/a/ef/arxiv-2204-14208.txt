Diophantine triples with the property D ⁡ ( n ) D(n) for distinct n n
Kalyan Chakraborty, Shubham Gupta and Azizul Hoque

Abstract
. We prove that for every integer n n , there exist infinitely many D ⁡ ( n ) D(n) -triples which are also D ⁡ ( t ) D(t) -triples for t ∈ ℤ t\in\mathbb{Z} with n ≠ t n\neq t . We also prove that there are infinitely many triples with the property D ⁡ ( − 1 ) D(-1) in ℤ ⁡ [ i ] \mathbb{Z}[i] which are also D ⁡ ( n ) D(n) -triple in ℤ ⁡ [ i ] \mathbb{Z}[i] for two distinct n n ’s other than n = − 1 n=-1 and these triples are not equivalent to any triple with the property D ⁡ ( 1 ) D(1) .

1.

A set
\{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{m}\}
of
m
distinct non-zero integers is called a Diophantine
m
-tuple with the property
D(n)
for a given integer

a_{i}a_{j}+n
is a perfect square for all
1\leq i<j\leq m
. Note that it is also known as
D(n)
-
m
-tuple or
D(n)
-set and a
D(1)
-set is simply a Diophantine
m
-tuple. The problem of constructing such sets was first studied by Diophantus of Alexandria, who found four rational numbers with this property. First Diophantine quadruple was found by Fermat, viz.
\{1,3,8,120\}
. Later, Baker and Davenport
[
4
]
proved that this quadruple can not be extended to quintuple. In
[
8
]
, Dujella proved that there exist at most finitely many quintuples and confirmed that there is no Diophantine sextuple. Recently, He et. al.
[
18
]
came up with a conclusion that there is no Diophantine quintuple. Analogous problems for
D(-1)
-
m
-set were investigated in
[
3
,
9
,
22
]
.

Let us look at a general concept of
m
-tuple with the property
D(n)
in a commutative ring with unity.
Let
\mathcal{R}
be a commutative ring with unity. Given

,
a set

m
-tuple with the property

if
a_{i}a_{j}+n
is a square in
\mathcal{R}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq m
.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
its ring of integers. In this setup, there are some interesting results for the existence of Diophantine
m
-tuples with the property
D(n)
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with a bound on

. In 2019, Adžaga
[
1
]
confirmed the non-existence of

-tuples with the property

in

[

]
proved that there is no Diophantine
m
-tuple with the property

when
m\geq 37
.

It is interesting to see that

-quadruples are related to the representations of

by the binary quadratic form

. In particular, Dujella
[

proved that a

-quadruple in

(see
[

]
) suggesting that the relation between

-quadruples and differences of two squares is not just a coincidence. This motivated Franušić and Jadrijavić

to conjecture that there exists a quadruple with the property

in a commutative ring

with unity if and only if

, except for finitely many

This conjecture is proven for

(see
[
7
]
), rings of integers of

(for certain

[

,
[

[

]
) and that of

(see

]
). However, Chakraborty et. al.

shown on the other hand that this conjecture does not hold in

proved the existence of infinitely many quintuples with the property

2.
Preliminaries

Let
R
be the ring of integers of a number field
F
. We use the standard notation

to indicate that the expression is a complete square. Assume that
\{a,b,c\}
is triple in

with the property

for a given integer

ab+n=\square,~~~ac+n=\square,~~~bc+n=\square.

(2.1)

This shows that

ab+x=\square,~~~ac+x=\square,~~~bc+x=\square.

It is easy to see that the above system of equations has finitely many integer solutions since there are only finitely many integral points on the elliptic curve

E:y^{2}=(ab+x)(ac+x)(bc+x).

(2.2)

The following proposition helps us to reduce the investigation of integer solutions of the above system of equations to the investigation of the induced elliptic curve (
2.2
).

Proposition 2.1

(
[
20
]
, Theorem 4.2)
.

Let
K
be a field with characteristic not equal to 2 and 3. Let

E(K):y^{2}=(x-p)(x-q)(x-r).

Let

. Then for some
Q\in E(K)
,

if and only if
(x-p),(x-q)
and
(x-r)
all are squares in

.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we need to find points

satisfying

and

-coordinate of

lies in

for some

.

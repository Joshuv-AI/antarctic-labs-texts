Abstract
. We prove hypercontractive inequalities on high dimensional expanders. As in the settings of the p p -biased hypercube, the symmetric group, and the Grassmann scheme, our inequalities are effective for global functions, which are functions that are not significantly affected by a restriction of a small set of coordinates. As applications, we obtain Fourier concentration, small-set expansion, and Kruskal–Katona theorems for high dimensional expanders. Our techniques rely on a new approximate Efron–Stein decomposition for high dimensional link expanders.

X

\epsilon

\epsilon
-link expander) if the

1

X
is a spectral expander
graph whose second-largest eigenvalue is bounded by
\epsilon
. In
recent years, HDX have received much attention in theoretical computer
science

,

, statistical

[

]
,

[

,
and beyond. Notably, very recently the study of HDX led to a breakthrough
in quantum computing, breaking the
\sqrt{n}
distance in quantum
LDPC
[

]
, as well as to a resolution of one
of the most important questions in coding theory, namely, the first
construction of
O(1)
-query asymptotically good locally testable
codes
[

]
.

In this work, we focus on analysis of Boolean functions on high dimensional
expanders, whose systematic study was recently initiated by Dikstein
et al.
[
9
]
. This continues a long line of investigation
of Fourier analysis of Boolean functions on extended domains beyond
the Boolean hypercube, such as the Boolean slice
[

,

,

, the symmetric

,

]
,
and the multi-slice
[

,

]
. The foregoing
extended domains arise naturally throughout theoretical computer science,

.

Hypercontractive inequalities are amongst the most powerful technical
tools in Fourier analysis, yielding a plethora of applications in
algorithms, complexity, learning theory, statistical physics, social
choice, and beyond (see
[
43
]
and references therein).
Loosely speaking, such statements assert that functions of low Fourier
degree are “well behaved” in terms of their distribution around
their mean. Concretely, in the Boolean hypercube, the simplest example
of a hypercontractive inequality is Bonami’s lemma, which states that
for every function
f\colon\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{{R}}
of Fourier
degree at most
d
, it holds that
\|f\|_{4}\leq\sqrt{{3}^{d}}\|f\|_{2}
.

Alas, in the setting of high dimensional expanders, where the domain
is not a product space and the induced measure is biased, general
strong hypercontractivity cannot hold. The heart of the problem is
that some highly local functions, such as dictators (i.e.,
f(x)=x_{i}
),
provide strong counterexamples to hypercontractivity. A similar phenomenon
also occurs in several prominent extended domains, such as the
p
-biased
cube and the Grassmann scheme.

Fortunately, as observed in the setting of the
p
-biased cube
[
36
]
,
all of the aforementioned examples are local, in the sense that a
small number of coordinates can significantly influence the output
of the function. This led to the definition of ‘global’ functions.
For Boolean valued functions, these are functions wherein a small
number of coordinates can change the output of the function only with
a negligible probability. For real valued functions, this is captured
by the
2
-norm remaining roughly the same when restricting O(1)
coordinates of the input. More precisely, consider the setting of
a general product measure. Let
\left(V_{i},\mu_{i}\right)
be probability
spaces, let
V_{S}=\prod_{i\in S}V_{i}
and equip
V_{S}
with the
product measure, which we denote by
\mu_{S}.
Every function
f\in L^{2}\left(V_{\left[k\right]},\mu\right)

is equipped with an orthogonal decomposition
\sum_{S\subseteq\left[n\right]}f^{=S}

known as the Efron–Stein decomposition. The function
f^{=S}
in
the Efron–Stein decomposition plays a similar role to the function

\hat{f}\left(S\right)\chi_{S}
in the Boolean cube. Using that analogy
we write

f^{\leq d}=\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}f^{=S},

and
f
is said to be of degree
d
if
f=f^{\leq d}
. Keevash
et al.
[
37
]
introduced the following notions. The
Laplacians
of
f
are given by

L_{S}\left[f\right]=\sum_{T\supseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|T\right|}f^{=T}.

For
x\in V_{S}
the
derivatives
are given by restricting
the laplacians

D_{S,x}f=L_{S}\left[f\right]\left(x,\cdot\right),

and the
\left(S,x\right)
-influence
of
f
is defined as

I_{S,x}\left[f\right]=\|D_{S,x}\left[f\right]\|_{2}^{2}.

In this setting, a function
f
is
\left(r,\delta\right)
-global

if
\|f\left(x,\cdot\right)\|_{2,\mu_{\left[n\right]\setminus S}}^{2}\leq\delta

for each
\left|S\right|\leq r.
We remark that here, being
\left(r,\delta\right)
-global
for a small
\delta>0
is, in a sense, equivalent to having
I_{S,x}\left[f\right]\leq\delta^{\prime}

for a small
\delta^{\prime}
for all
\left|S\right|\leq r
and all
x
.
In fact,
\delta,\delta^{\prime}
can be taken to be within a factor of
2^{r}

of one another.

In
[
37
]
, it was shown that if
f\in L^{2}\left(V,\mu\right)

is of degree
d
, then the following hypercontractive inequality
holds:

(1.1)

\|f\|_{4}^{4}\leq 1000^{d}\sum_{S}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}I_{S,x}\left[f\right]^{2}.

This allowed them to deduce if a function
f
of degree
d
is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global
,
then

\|f\|_{4}^{4}\leq\delta 8000^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Here when setting
\delta=100\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}^{2}
one gets the
statement
\|f\|_{4}\leq C^{d}\|f\|_{2},
which replicates the behavior
in the Boolean cube. Moreover, the statement is useful even for larger
values of
\delta
.

In this work, we raise the following question.

Does hypercontractivity hold for high dimensional expanders?

1.1.
Main results

We answer the question above in the affirmative. Namely, our main
contribution is a hypercontractive inequality for functions on the

k
-faces of an
\epsilon
-HDX. We denote by
X(k)
the
k
-faces
of a simplicial complex
X
, and denote by
\mu
the uniform measure
on its
k
-faces. We define the influences
I_{S,x}^{\leq d}
and
the degree restriction operator
(\cdot)^{\leq d}
analogously to
their definition on the
p
-biased cube (see Section
4

for precise definition). We then prove the following hypercontractive
statement for high dimensional expanders in the spirit of (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be an
\epsilon
-HDX, and let
f\in L^{2}\left(X(k),\mu\right)
.
We have

\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}\leq 20^{d}\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S\right|}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}I_{S,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}.

In the setting of
\epsilon
-HDX, we say that a function
f
is

\left(d,\delta\right)
-global if for each
\left|S\right|\leq d
,
we have
\|f\left(x,\cdot\right)\|_{L^{2}\left(V_{x},\mu_{x}\right)}\leq\delta.

We show that we can bound the infinity norm of global functions and
obtain the following strong hypercontractive inequality for global
functions on
\epsilon
-HDX.

Corollary 1.2
.

For each
\zeta,d,k>0,
there exists
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}\left(\zeta,k,d\right),\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}\left(\zeta,d\right)
,
such that the following holds. Let
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0},\delta\leq\delta_{0}
,
let
X
be an
\epsilon
-HDX, and let
f\in L^{2}\left(X(k),\mu\right)
.
If
f
is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global, then we have

\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}\leq\zeta\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

We remark that, in fact, we prove our results in a slightly more general
setting, to which we refer as
\epsilon
-product measures. See Section

7
for details.

Applications

As corollaries of our hypercontractive inequality for high dimensional

1.2.1.
Fourier spectrum concentration theorem

Fourier concentration results are widely useful in complexity theory
and learning theory. Our first application is a Fourier concentration
theorem for HDX. Namely, the following theorem shows that global Boolean
functions on
\epsilon
-HDX are concentrated on the high degrees,
in the sense that the
2
-norm of the restriction of a function to
its low-degree coefficients only constitutes a tiny fraction of its
total
2
-norm.

Theorem 1.3
.

For each
\zeta,d,k>0,

there exists
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}\left(\zeta,k,d\right),\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}\left(\zeta,d\right)
,
such that the following holds. Let
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0},\delta\leq\delta_{0}
,
let
X
be an
\epsilon
-HDX, and let
f\colon X(k)\to\left\{0,1\right\}

be
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global. Then

\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\zeta\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

1.2.2.
Small set expansion theorem

Small set expansion is a fundamental property that is prevalent in
combinatorics and complexity theory. In the setting of the
\rho
-noisy
Boolean hypercube, the small set expansion theorem of Kahn, Kalai,
and Linial
[
31
]
gives an upper bound on
\mathrm{{Stab}_{\rho}}(1_{A})=\langle 1_{A},T_{\rho}1_{A}\rangle

for indicators
1_{A}
of small sets
A
. The noise stability
\mathrm{{Stab}_{\rho}}(1_{A})

captures the probability that a random edge
\left(x,y\right)
of
the
\rho
-noisy hypercube has both its endpoints in
A
. Hence,
an inequality of the form
\mathrm{{Stab}_{\rho}}(1_{A})\leq\zeta\|1_{A}\|_{2}^{2}

for an arbitrarily small
\zeta
and sufficiently small
A
implies
that that small sets are expanding in the sense that the random walk
makes you leave them with probability
\geq 1-\zeta
. Our second application
is a small set expansion theorem for global functions on
\epsilon
-HDX,
captured via bounding the natural noise operator in this setting.
Let
\rho\in\left(0,1\right)
be a noise-rate parameter. The noise
operator is given by

\mathrm{T}_{\rho}f\left(x\right):=\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}\rho^{\left|S\right|}\left(1-\rho\right)^{k-\left|S\right|}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mu}\left[f\left(y\right)|y_{S}=x_{S}\right].

In other words,
\mathrm{T}_{\rho}
corresponds to the random walk
that starts with
x
chooses a
\rho
-biased random
S\subseteq\left[k\right]
,
keeps
x_{S}
, and re-randomises
x
given
x_{S}
. Our small set
expansion theorem tells us that if we start with a small subset
A\subseteq X\left(k\right)

and we apply one step of the random walk, then we leave
A
with
probability
0.99
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For each
\zeta,d,k>0,
there exists

\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}\left(\zeta,k,d\right),\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}\left(\zeta,d\right)
,
such that the following holds. Let
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0},\delta\leq\delta_{0}
,
and let
X
be an
\epsilon
-HDX. If
f\colon V_{\left[k\right]}\to\left\{0,1\right\}

is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global, then

\|\mathrm{T}_{\rho}f\|_{2}^{2}\leq\zeta\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

1.2.3.
Kruskal–Katona theorem

Our last application is an analogue of the Kruskal–Katona theorem
in the setting of high dimensional expanders. The Kruskal-Katona theorem
is a fundamental and widely-applied result in extremal combinatorics,
which gives a lower bound on the size of the lower shadow
\partial\left(A\right)

of a
k
-uniform hypergraph
A
on
n
vertices. The
lower
shadow
is defined to be the family of all
(k-1)
-sets that are
contained in an edge of
A
. More generally, if
A\subseteq X\left(k\right)
,
then we similarly let
\partial\left(A\right)
be the family of all

k-1
-faces that are contained in a
k
-face of
A
.

Filmus et al.
[
23
]
used their hypercontractivity
theorem to prove a stability result for the Kruskal–Katona theorem.
We prove a similar stability result for
\epsilon
-HDX.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be an
\epsilon
-HDX, for a sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
.
Let
\delta\leq\left(200d\right)^{-d},
and let
A\subseteq X\left(k-1\right)

be
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global. Then

\mu\left(\partial\left(A\right)\right)\geq\mu\left(A\right)\left(1+\frac{d}{2k}\right).

1.3.
Techniques

Conceptually, one can view the theory of expanders and pseudorandom
graphs in the following perspective: Given a pseudorandom regular
graph
G=\left(V,E\right)
and
\left(x,y\right)\sim E
, the goal
is to show that
x,y
behave similarly to independent random variables

x,y\sim V
, i.e., as an approximation of a product space.

In the theory of high dimensional expanders, we are given a distribution

\mu
on
(k+1)
-tuples by choosing a random
k
-face
\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+1}\right)

of a sparse simplicial complex, and the goal is again to show that
the variables
\{x_{i}\}
approximately behave as though they were
independent. Thus, our main objective is to generalise results from
the product space setting, where the
x_{i}
’s are independent, to
the setting of HDX, where we only have local spectral information
about the links. However, such a generalisation yields significant
challenges.

One of the fundamental tools for studying the product space setting
is the aforementioned Efron–Stein decomposition. Its role in the
analysis of product spaces is that it allows us to easily generalise
techniques from the Boolean cube by replacing the Fourier expression

\hat{f}\left(S\right)\chi_{S}
with the function
f^{=S}.

Our high-level proof strategy is to develop new Efron–Stein decompositions
for HDX. We show that despite the more involved setting, and despite
the fact that we only have mere local spectral information, we can
still obtain similar structural properties as in product spaces. We
now list a few of the challenges that we are facing, which require
fundamentally new ideas and techniques.

Dikstein et al.
[
9
]
gave a decomposition of the
form
f=\sum_{d=0}^{k}f^{=d}
. We provide a new decomposition
\left\{f^{=S}\right\}_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}

such that
f=\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}f^{=S},
and despite
not having orthogonality, we can still show that the inner product

\left\langle f^{=S},f^{=T}\right\rangle
is negligible compared
to
\|f\|_{2}^{2}.
This allows us to generalise the Laplacians,
derivatives and influences, but we have to deal with the following
problems:

•

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\left[k\right]
be a small set. We would
like to say that
g=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}f^{=S}
is supported on

\mathcal{F},
but we have no way of knowing that looking at
\left\{g^{=S}\right\}_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]},

as
g^{=S}
may be nonzero even for
S\notin\mathcal{F}.
This leads
to the problem of how to even define the degree of a function. We
would like to say that
f^{\leq d}:=\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}f^{=S}

is of degree at most
d
, and that
f
is of degree
d
if
f=f^{\leq d}
.
Alas, according to this definition the function
f^{\leq d}
is not
of degree
d
.

•

We can and do define the derivatives
D_{S,x}
to be the restrictions
of the Laplacians. In the product case the derivatives decrease the
degree by
\left|S\right|
, and this is a very desirable property
as our proof goes by induction on
d
. However, this is no longer
true in the HDX setting.

•

We may define the influences by taking
2
-norms of the derivatives.
However, now it is no longer true that having small influences is
equivalent to being global. This leads us to the following problem
which is the source for all of the difficulty.

•

The spectral information tells us that HDX should behave similarly
to product spaces with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm. However, we care
about
L_{4}
information when bounding
\|f\|_{4}
, and we deal
with
L_{\infty}
-hypothesis as the globalness notion is about
all

the restrictions. There is no reason for HDX to behave well with respect
to
L_{4}
and even more so for
L_{\infty}.

At first, the above, and especially the last point, seem as fundamental
barriers to this approach.

Nevertheless, we overcome this barrier by developing an alternative
notion, which we call the
approximate Efron–Stein decomposition
.
Our new notion has the following properties that fix all of the above
problems.

•

If
\left\{f_{S}\right\}_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}
is an approximate
Efron–Stein decomposition, then crucially,
\left\{f_{S}\right\}_{S\in\mathcal{F}}

is an approximate Efron–Stein decomposition for
\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}f^{=S}
.

•

If
f
is approximately of degree
d
, in the sense that
\left\{f_{S}\right\}

is an approximate Efron-Stein decomposition for
f
, then the derivative

D_{S,x}\left[f\right]
may be
L_{4}
-approximated by
D_{S,x}\left[f\right]^{\leq d-\left|S\right|}

.

•

We find a way of proving an inequality of the form

\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}I_{S,x}^{2}\left[f\right]\leq\delta\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}\left[I_{S,x}\right],

without having the traditional hypothesis
\max_{x}I_{S,x}\left[f\right]\leq\delta

at our disposal.

•

We show that we may move freely between different approximate Efron–Stein
decomposition up to a small
L_{4}
-norm error term.

We believe that our approximate Efron–Stein decomposition provides
the desired comfortable platform for analysing functions on HDX in
the same way one would analyze a product space.

See Section
4
for a detailed exposition
of our approximate Efron–Stein decomposition, and see Section
5

for a more detailed proof overview of our main hypercontractivity
results, which build on the aforementioned decomposition.

.

2.
Recalling globalness and hypercontractivity in the product space
setting

We begin by recalling the Efron–Stein decomposition, as well as
derivatives and Laplacians in the setting of general product spaces,
and state the hypercontractivity inequalities for product spaces that
were shown in
[
37
]
. We then give a proof, inspired by
[
18
]
,
of a slightly weaker hypercontractivity inequality that we will later
generalise to approximate product spaces.

2.1.
Efron-Stein decomposition

Let
\left(V_{1},\mu_{1}\right),\ldots,\left(V_{k},\mu_{k}\right)

be a probability space. Let
\mu
be the corresponding product measure

\mu_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mu_{k}
. For a set
S\subseteq\left[k\right]
,
we write
V_{S}=\prod_{i\in S}V_{i},
and we write
\text{$\mu_{S}$ for the product measure }\,\mu_{S}=\bigotimes_{i\in S}\mu_{i}.

The
Efron–Stein decomposition
is a decomposition of
L^{2}\left(V_{\left[k\right]},\mu\right)

into
2^{k}
orthogonal spaces
\left\{W_{S}\right\}_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}.

Every function
f\in L^{2}\left(V_{\left[k\right]},\mu\right)
can
then be decomposed as
f=\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}f^{=S},

where
f^{=S}
is the projection of
f
to
W_{S}.
The Efron–Stein
decomposition is characterised by the orthogonality of
\left\{W_{S}\right\}
,
the fact that
\sum_{S}W_{S}=L^{2}\left(V,\mu\right)
, and the fact
that the space
W_{S}
is composed of functions depending only on

S.

The functions
f^{=S}
also have an explicit formula for
x\in V_{S}
,
where we denote

A_{S}f\left(x\right)=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\left(V_{\overline{S}},\mu_{\overline{S}}\right)}\left[f\left(x,y\right)\right],

where
\bar{{S}}=[k]\setminus S
. We then write

f^{=S}=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus T\right|}A_{T}f.

The function
A_{S}\left[f\right]
then has the following neat Efron–Stein
decomposition

A_{S}\left[f\right]=\sum_{T\subseteq S}f^{=T}.

See
[
43
, Chapter 8]
for more details.

2.2.
Notations

We write
a=b\pm\epsilon
to indicate that
a\in\left(b-\epsilon,b+\epsilon\right).

We use
a\leq O\left(b\right)
to denote that the inequality holds
up to an absolute constant, and
a\leq O_{k}\left(b\right)
to denote
that the inequality holds up to a constant only depending on
k
.

2.3.
Derivatives and Laplacians

Let
\mu=\mu_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mu_{k}
be a product measure.
Let
f\in L^{2}\left(V_{\left[k\right]},\mu\right),

S\subseteq\left[n\right]
.
The
Laplacian
is given by the formula

L_{S}\left[f\right]=\sum_{T\supseteq S}f^{=T}=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|T\right|}A_{\left[k\right]\setminus T}f.

For
S\subseteq\left[n\right]
and
x\in V_{S}
the
derivative

D_{S,x}\in L^{2}\left(V_{\overline{S}},\mu_{\overline{S}}\right)

is defined by

D_{S,x}f=L_{S}\left[f\right]\left(x,\cdot\right).

For convenience, we also write
D_{\varnothing}f=f.
The
\left(S,x\right)
-influence

of
f
is defined as

I_{S,x}\left[f\right]=\|D_{S,x}\left[f\right]\|_{2}^{2}.

This includes the case
S=\varnothing
, where we have
I_{\varnothing}\left[f\right]:=\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

We now state a few facts from
[
37
]
that we generalise. The
following lemma, which appears in
[
37
]
, shows that the notion
of small influences corresponds to small
2
-norms of the restriction
of
f
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
I_{S,x}\left[f\right]\leq\delta
for each set
S
of
size at most
r.
Then
\|f\left(x,\cdot\right)\|_{2,\mu_{\left[n\right]\setminus S}}^{2}\leq\delta 4^{r}

for each
S
and
x\in V_{S}
. Conversely, if
\|f\left(x,\cdot\right)\|_{2,\mu_{\left[n\right]\setminus S}}^{2}\leq\delta

for each
\left|S\right|\leq r
and
x\in V_{S}
, then
I_{S,x}\left[f\right]\leq\delta 4^{r}

for each
S
of size at most
r
and
x\in V_{S}.

For the above reason they gave the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
f
is said to be
\left(r,\delta\right)
-global

if
I_{S,x}\left[f\right]\leq\delta
for each
\left|S\right|\leq r.

The
degree
of a function is the largest
S
, such that
f^{=S}\neq 0
.
The derivatives decrease the degrees for the following reason.

Lemma 2.3
.

D_{S,x}\left[f^{=T}\right]
is
0
unless
S\subseteq T
, and
if
S\subseteq T
, then

D_{S,x}\left[f^{=T}\right]\in W^{T\setminus S}.

Consequently, if
f=\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}f^{=S}
is of degree

d
, then
D_{S,x}\left[f\right]
is of degree
d-\left|S\right|.

2.4.
Hypercontractivity

The following result is by
[
37
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

If
f\in L^{2}\left(V,\mu\right)
is of degree
d
,
then

\|f\|_{4}^{4}\leq 1000^{d}\sum_{S}\mathbb{E}_{x}I_{S,x}\left[f\right]^{2}.

To show the implication of the theorem for global functions they use
the following inequality.

Lemma 2.5
.

\sum_{S}\mathbb{E}_{x}I_{S,x}\left[f\right]\leq 2^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

The right hand side is equal to

\sum_{S}\|L_{S}\left[f\right]\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{S}\sum_{T\supseteq S,\left|T\right|\leq d}\|f^{=T}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2^{d}\sum_{T}\|f^{=T}\|_{2}^{2}=2^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

∎

Combining Theorem
2.4
and Lemma
2.5
,
we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.6
.

If
f
of degree

d
is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global. Then
\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}\leq\delta 2000^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq 1000^{d}\sum_{S\subseteq\left[n\right]}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}\|I_{S,x}\left[f\right]\|_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\delta 1000^{d}\sum_{S}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}\|I_{S,x}\left[f\right]\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta 2000^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

∎

2.5.
An alternative proof of hypercontractivity on product spaces

We give an alternative proof of the following slightly weaker version
of Theorem
2.4
. The proof is inspired by a future work
by Ellis, Kindler, and the second author
[
18
]
, who show that
the same idea works in the Grassmann setting. In this paper we show
that it generalises to HDX as well.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
f\in L^{2}\left(V,\mu\right)

be of degree
d.
Then

\|f\|_{4}^{4}\leq 2\cdot 9^{d}\sum_{\left|T\right|\leq d}\left(9d\right)^{\left|T\right|}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[I_{S,x}\left[f\right]^{2}\right].

2.5.1.
Proof overview.

Before providing the full proof, we first describe the high-level
approach for proving Theorem
2.7
.
The strategy is to first show a lemma that gives the following bound

(2.1)

\|f\|_{4}^{4}\leq C^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{4}+\sum_{S\subseteq\left[n\right]}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S\right|}\|L_{S}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4},

for a constant
C
. Using this lemma, we can give an inductive proof
by first noting that
\|L_{S}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}=\mathbb{E}_{x}\|D_{S,x}\|_{4}^{4}
,
and then applying induction using the fact that
D_{S,x}
is of degree

d-\left|S\right|
. Finally, using the fact that
D_{S,x}D_{T,y}=D_{S\cup T,\left(x,y\right)}
,
we can get our desired hypercontractive statement.

Hence, the key step is to prove the aforementioned lemma. To this
end, we first use the fact that

\mathbb{E}\left[f^{4}\right]=\sum_{S}\|\left(f^{2}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}.

We then expand the summands of
\left(f^{2}\right)^{=S}
as sums
of terms of the form
\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}.
Next,
we note that the nonzero terms either satisfy
T_{1}\cap T_{2}\cap S\neq\varnothing

or satisfy
T_{1}\Delta T_{2}=S
. Terms of the first kind are cancelled
out by
L_{i}\left[f\right]^{4}
for an
i\in T_{1}\cap T_{2}\cap S

on the right hand side of (
2.1
). (The terms

\|L_{S}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}
appear because of over counting,
which we resolve by inclusion exclusion.) Terms of the latter kind
correspond to the situation in the Boolean cube where
f^{=T}=\hat{f}\left(T\right)\chi_{T}

and
\chi_{T}\chi_{S}=\chi_{T\Delta S}.
We then upper bound
\|\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}

by
\|f^{=T_{1}}\|_{2}\|f^{=T_{2}}\|_{2}
. This allows us to translate
the problem of upper bounding the terms of the first kind to the problem
of upper bounding the
4
-norm of a low degree function on the Boolean
cube. Namely, the function

\sum_{\left|T\right|\leq d}\|f^{=T}\|_{2}\chi_{T}.

Finally, we use hypercontractivity to upper bound the
4
-norm by
its
2
-norm, which is equal to the
2
-norm of
f
. This concludes
the proof overview.

2.5.2.
Proof of hypercontractivity on product spaces

We now give a formal proof of Theorem
2.7
.
We shall first need the following key lemma, which admits the inductive
approach.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
f\in L^{2}\left(V,\mu\right)

be of degree
d.
Then

\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{4}^{4}\leq 9{}^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{4}+\sum_{T\neq\varnothing}\left(4d\right)^{\left|T\right|}\|L_{T}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}.

We are now ready to prove the lemma.

Proof of Lemma
2.8
.

By Parseval we have

\|f\|_{4}^{4}=\sum\|\left(f^{2}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}.

We bound each term
\|\left(f^{2}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}
individually.
By expanding and using the linearity of the
\cdot^{=S}
operator
we have

\left(f^{2}\right)^{=S}=\sum_{T_{1},T_{2}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}.

We now divide the pairs
\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)
into three sums.

(1)

We let
I_{1}
be the set of pairs
\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)
such
that
T_{1}\cap T_{2}\cap S\neq\varnothing.
If
i
is in
T_{1}\cap T_{2}\cap S
,
then the summand
\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}
appears
as a summand when expanding
\left(L_{i}\left[f\right]^{2}\right)^{=S}.

This explains the role of the Laplacians in the right hand side.

(2)

We let
I_{2}
be the set of pairs such that
T_{1}\Delta T_{2}=S.

These kind of pairs have a similar behavior to the one in the Boolean
cube. There
f^{=S}=\hat{f}\left(S\right)\chi_{S}
and

f^{=S}f^{=T}=\hat{f}\left(S\right)\hat{f}\left(T\right)\chi_{S\Delta T}.

We show that the contribution from the pairs in
I_{2}
is
\leq C^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

(3)

We let
I_{3}=\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)
such that either
\left(T_{1}\Delta T_{2}\right)\setminus S\neq\varnothing

or
S\setminus\left(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\right)\neq\varnothing
. We show
that in this case
\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}=0.

It is easy to verify that each pair
\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)
belongs
to at least one of the sets
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
. We additionally
have
I_{1}\cap I_{2}=\varnothing.

Upper bounding the contribution from
I_{1}

Let us start by upper bounding the contribution from pairs corresponding
to
I_{1}
. For a nonempty
T\subseteq S
write
I_{1}\left(T\right)

for the pairs
\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)
, such that
T_{1}\cap T_{2}\supseteq T.

Then

\left(L_{T}\left[f\right]^{2}\right)^{=S}=\sum_{\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)\in I_{1}\left(T\right)}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}.

Now
I_{1}=\bigcup_{i\in S}I_{1}\left(i\right),
so as a multiset
inclusion-exclusion shows that we have

I_{1}=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|T\right|-1}\bigcap_{i\in T}I_{1}\left(i\right)=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|T\right|-1}I_{1}\left(T\right).

We therefore have the equality:

\sum_{\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)\in I_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}=\sum_{T\subseteq S,T\neq\varnothing}\left(-1\right)^{\left|T\right|-1}\left(L_{T}\left[f\right]^{2}\right)^{=S}.

By the triangle inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\sum_{\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)\in I_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\left|S\right|}\binom{\left|S\right|}{i}\left(4\left|S\right|\right)^{-i}\right)\left(\sum_{T\subseteq S}\left(4\left|S\right|\right)^{\left|T\right|}\|\left(L_{T}\left[f\right]^{2}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T\subseteq S}\left(4\left|S\right|\right)^{\left|T\right|}\|\left(L_{T}\left[f\right]^{2}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}.

Summing over all
S
we have

\sum_{S}\|\sum_{\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)\in I_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{T}\left(4d\right)^{\left|T\right|}\|L_{T}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}.

Upper bounding the contribution from
I_{2}

We now upper bound the contribution from
I_{2}.
Let
T_{1}\Delta T_{2}=S.

Then for each
S^{\prime}\subsetneq S
, we assert that
A_{S^{\prime}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)=0
.
Let
i\in S\setminus S^{\prime}
. Then
i\in T_{1}\Delta T_{2}.
Assume
without loss of generality that
i\in T_{1}
. Then

A_{S^{\prime}\cup T_{2}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)=f^{=T_{2}}A_{S^{\prime}\cup T_{2}}f^{=T_{1}}=0.

This shows that
A_{S^{\prime}}\left[f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right]=0.
Hence,

\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}=A_{S}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)=\left\langle f^{=T_{1}}\left(x,\cdot\right),f^{=T_{2}}\left(x,\cdot\right)\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mu_{\overline{S}}\right)}.

By Cauchy–Schwarz we have

\displaystyle\|\sum_{\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)\in I_{2}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T_{1}\Delta T_{2}=T_{3}\Delta T_{4}=S}\left\langle\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S},\left(f^{=T_{3}}f^{=T_{4}}\right)^{=S}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T_{1}\Delta T_{2}=T_{3}\Delta T_{4}=S}\left\|\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\right\|_{2}\left\|\left(f^{=T_{3}}f^{=T_{4}}\right)^{=S}\right\|_{2}.

Now, for each
\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)\in I_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}\left\langle f^{=T_{1}}\left(x,\cdot\right),f^{=T_{2}}\left(x,\cdot\right)\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mu_{\overline{S}}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}\left[\|f^{=T_{1}}\left(x,\cdot\right)\|_{L^{2}\left(\mu_{\overline{S}}\right)}^{2}\|f_{x}^{=T_{2}}\|_{L^{2}\left(\mu_{\overline{S}}\right)}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}\|f_{x}^{=T_{1}}\|_{2}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}\|f_{x}^{=T_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|f^{=T_{1}}\|_{2}^{2}\|f^{=T_{2}}\|_{2}^{2},

where in the second equality we used the fact that
\|f^{=T}\left(x,\cdot\right)\|_{L^{2}\left(\mu_{\overline{S}}\right)}^{2}

depends only on
x_{T\cap S}
, so these are independent for
T=T_{1}

and
T=T_{2}.
This establishes

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\left(f^{=T_{3}}f^{=T_{4}}\right)^{=S}\right]\leq\|f^{=T_{1}}\|_{2}\|f^{=T_{2}}\|_{2}\|f^{=T_{3}}\|_{2}\|f^{=T_{4}}\|_{2}

Summing over all
S
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{S}\|\sum_{\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)\in I_{2}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\left(\left\{0,1\right\}^{n},\mu_{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}\left(\sum_{S\subseteq\left[n\right]}\|f^{=S}\|_{2}\chi_{S}\right)^{4}

\displaystyle\leq 9^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[(\sum\|f^{=S}\|_{2}\chi_{S})^{2}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=9^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{4}.

Here the first inequality follows by expanding both terms and the
second is a well known consequence of hypercontractivity in the uniform
cube.

Showing that there is no contribution from
I_{3}

We recall that
I_{3}
consist of the pairs with either
\left(T_{1}\Delta T_{2}\right)\setminus S\neq\varnothing

or
S\setminus\left(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\right).
Then we claim that
\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}=0
.
If
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
does not contain
S
, then

f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}=A_{T_{1}\cup T_{2}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)=\sum_{S^{\prime}\subseteq S}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S^{\prime}}.

The uniqueness of the Efron–Stein decomposition shows that
\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}=0
.
Suppose now that there exists
i\in\left(T_{1}\Delta T_{2}\right)\setminus S.

Without loss of generality
i\in T_{1}.
We then have

A_{\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)=f^{=T_{2}}\cdot A_{\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}f^{=T_{1}}=0.

In particular,
\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}=0
as for
each
S\subseteq\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}
we have

\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\left[A_{\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)\right]^{=S}=0

Combining the contributions from
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
.

The lemma now follows by Cauchy–Schwarz. We have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{4}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S}\|\left(f^{2}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S}\left(2\sum_{\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)\in I_{1}}\|\left(f^{2}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}+2\sum_{\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)\in I_{2}}\|\left(f^{2}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{T}\left(4d\right)^{\left|T\right|}\|L_{T}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}+2\cdot 9^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{4}.

∎
Finally, using Lemma
2.8
, we can derive
Theorem
2.7
as
follows.

Proof of Theorem
2.7
.

The proof is by induction on
d
. Since
D_{T,x}\left[f\right]

is of degree
d-\left|T\right|
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{4}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq 9{}^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{4}+\sum_{T\neq\varnothing}\left(4d\right)^{\left|T\right|}\|L_{T}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}

\displaystyle=9^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{4}+\sum_{T\neq\varnothing}\left(4d\right)^{\left|T\right|}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}\|D_{T,x}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq 9^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{4}+\sum_{T\neq\varnothing}2\cdot 9^{d-\left|T\right|}\left(4d\right)^{\left|T\right|}\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq\left[n\right]\setminus T}\left(8d\right)^{T^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T\cup T^{\prime}}}I_{T\cup T^{\prime},x}^{2}

\displaystyle=9^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{4}+\sum_{T\cap T^{\prime}=\varnothing}2^{\left|T^{\prime}\right|+1}9^{d-\left|T\right|}\left(4d\right)^{\left|T\cup T^{\prime}\right|}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T\cup T^{\prime}}}\|D_{T^{\prime}\cup T,x}\left[f\right]\|_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq 9^{d}\sum_{T\subseteq S}\left(9d\right)^{\left|T\right|}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}I_{T,x}^{2}.

∎

3.
\epsilon
-product spaces and the operators

A_{S,T}

In this section, we present the framework of
\epsilon
-product spaces,
of which high dimensional expanders are a special case. We also define
key operators in this setting and show some basic properties that
they satisfy.

3.1.
Complexes having
\epsilon
-pseudorandom links.

It is useful for us to consider measures on
V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{k}

rather than pure
(k-1)
-dimensional complexes, which can be identified
with subsets
S\subseteq V^{k}.
Instead we identify a set with the
uniform measure over it.

Projected complexes

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{k}.

We say that
\mu
is a, weighted
k
-partite,
(k-1)
-dimensional
complex. Let
S\subseteq\left[k\right]
we write
\mu_{S}
for the
projection of
\mu
on
S
. We write
\mu_{i}
rather than
\mu_{\left\{i\right\}}.

We write
V_{S}
for the support of
\mu_{S}
inside
\prod_{i\in S}V_{i}.

We write
\overline{S}
for the complement of
S
.

Restricted complexes

Let
x\in V_{S}.
We write
\mu_{x}
for the measure on
V_{\overline{S}}

given by

\mu_{x}\left(y\right)=\frac{\mu\left(x,y\right)}{\mu_{S}\left(x\right)}.

We write
V_{x}
for the support of
\mu_{x}.
We refer to
\left(V_{x},\mu_{x}\right)

as the
link
of
\mu
on
x
.

\epsilon
-pseudorandom weighted graphs

Let
V_{1},V_{2}
be finite sets. A measure
\mu
on
V_{1}\times V_{2}

can be thought of as a weighted bipartite graph. We say that
\mu

is
\epsilon
-pseudorandom if for each
f_{1}\colon V_{1}\to\mathbb{R},

f_{2}\colon V_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\sim\mu}\left[f_{1}\left(x_{1}\right)f_{2}\left(x_{2}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{x_{1}\sim\mu_{1}}\left[f_{1}\left(x_{1}\right)\right]\mathbb{E}_{x_{2}\sim\mu_{2}}\left[f_{2}\left(x_{2}\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{x_{1}\sim\mu_{1}}\left[f_{1}\left(x_{1}\right)\right]\mathrm{Var}_{x_{2}\sim\mu_{2}}\left[f_{2}\left(x_{2}\right)\right]}.

We let
A_{12}
be the operator from
L^{2}\left(V_{1},\mu_{1}\right)

to
L^{2}\left(V_{2},\mu_{2}\right)
given by

A_{12}f\left(x\right)=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mu_{x}}\left[f\left(y\right)\right].

We have the following standard lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following are equivalent.

(1)

\mu
is
\epsilon
-pseudorandom

(2)

\|A_{12}-\mathbb{E}\|_{2\to 2}\leq\epsilon.

(3)

The second eigenvalue of
\mathrm{A}_{12}^{*}\mathrm{A}_{12}
is

\leq\epsilon^{2}.

\epsilon
-pseudorandom links

Now let
\mu
on
V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{k}.
We say that
\mu

has

\epsilon
-pseudorandom skeletons
if for each
S
of
size
2
the measure
\mu_{S}
is
\epsilon
-pseudorandom.

We say that
\mu
is
\epsilon
-product
if for each
S\subseteq\left[k\right]

of size
\leq k-2
and each
x\in V_{S}
the link
\mu_{x}
has

\epsilon
-pseudorandom skeletons.

In all that follows we assume that
\mu
is an
\epsilon
-product
measure on
V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{k}.

Inheritance

The definition of
\epsilon
-product makes it easy for inductive
type argument for the following reason.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mu
on
\prod_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
be
\epsilon
-product. Let

S,T\subseteq\left[k\right]
be disjoint. Then for each
x\in V_{S}
,
the probability measure
\left(\mu_{x}\right)_{T}=\left(\mu_{S\cup T}\right)_{x}

is
\epsilon
-product.

Proof.

All the skeletons of links of
\left(\mu_{S\to x}\right)_{T}
are
also skeletons of links of
\mu.

∎

Pseudorandomness as a measure of independence

Let
S,T\subseteq\left[n\right]
. Then we have an operator
A_{S,T}:L^{2}\left(V_{S},\mu_{S}\right)\to L^{2}\left(V_{T},\mu_{T}\right).

The operator is given by

A_{S,T}f\left(y\right)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu}\left[f\left(x_{S}\right)|x_{T}=y\right].

We write
A_{S,T}^{\mu}
to stress that the operator is taken with
respect to
\mu.
We write
A_{S}
for
A_{\left[k\right],S},

the operator given by restricting
S
and taking expectation.

When
S,T
are disjoint we expect
A_{S,T}f
to be close to
\mathbb{E}\left[f\right],

as in the product case
A_{S,T}
is equal to the expectation. In
fact, we do have the following.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mu
be

\epsilon
-product. Let
S,T\subseteq\left[k\right]
be disjoint,
and let
f\in L^{2}\left(V_{S},\mu_{S}\right)
. We have

\|A_{S,T}f-\mathbb{E}\left[f\right]\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left|S\right|\left|T\right|\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}

Proof.

We prove it by induction on
k
. The case where
k=2
is Lemma
3.1
,
so we assume
k>2
. Given a probability space
\left(\Omega,\mu\right)

we write
1^{\bot}
for the subspace of
L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mu\right)

consisting of functions that are orthogonal to the constant function

1.
We write
\tilde{\|}A_{S,T}\tilde{\|}
for the
L^{2}
operator
norm of
A_{S,T}
as an operator from
1^{\bot}
to
1^{\bot}
.
I.e.

\tilde{\|}A_{S,T}\tilde{\|}=\max_{f\in 1^{\bot}}\frac{\|A_{S,T}f\|_{2}}{\|f\|_{2}}.

Our goal is to show that

\tilde{\|}A_{S,T}\tilde{\|}\leq\sqrt{\left|S\right|\left|T\right|}\epsilon.

Discarding the trivial cases

If
T=\varnothing
, then
A_{S,T}=\mathbb{E}
and the result is
trivial. If
S\cup T\neq\left[k\right]
the result follows
by working with the space
\left(V_{S\cup T},\mu_{S\cup T}\right)

rather then
\left(V_{\left[k\right]},\mu_{\left[k\right]}\right).

We also have
\tilde{\|}A_{S,T}\tilde{\|}=\tilde{\|}A_{S,T}^{*}\tilde{\|}

as
A_{S,T}1=1.
As
A_{S,T}^{*}=A_{T,S}
we may assume that
\left|T\right|\leq\left|S\right|.

As
k>2
we may therefore assume that
\left|T\right|\geq 2.

Completing the proof in the case where
\left|T\right|>1

Assume without loss of generality that
1\in T
.

Let
f\in 1^{\bot}
. Using the fact that the equality

\|X\|_{2}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[X\right]^{2}+\|X-\mathbb{E}\left[X\right]\|_{2}^{2}

holds for every random variable
X
we have

\displaystyle\|A_{S,T}f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mu_{T}}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{y}}^{2}\left[f\left(x_{S}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mu_{1}}\|A_{S,T\setminus\left\{1\right\}}^{\mu_{a}}f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mu_{1}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{a}}^{2}f+\|A_{S,T\setminus\left\{1\right\}}^{\mu_{a}}f-\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{a}}f\|_{2}^{2}\right].

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mu_{1}}\left[A_{S,1}f^{2}\left(a\right)+\|A_{S,T\setminus\left\{1\right\}}^{\mu_{a}}f-\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{a}}f\|_{2}^{2}\right]

By induction we may upper bound the right hand side we have

\displaystyle RHS

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mu_{1}}\left[A_{S,1}f^{2}\left(a\right)+\left|S\right|\left|T-1\right|\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}\left(\mu_{a}\right)}^{2}\right].

\displaystyle=\|A_{S,1}f\|_{2}^{2}+\left|S\right|\left|T-1\right|\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

\displaystyle\leq\left|S\right|+\left|S\right|\left|T-1\right|\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|S\right|\left|T\right|\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}

∎

Understanding the operators
A_{S,T}
and their compositions

We now deduce that we have a similar upper bound of the form

\|A_{S,T}-A_{S,S\cap T}\|_{2\to 2}\leq\sqrt{\left|S\right|\left|T\right|}\epsilon.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
S,T\subseteq\left[k\right]
,
and let
f\in L^{2}\left(\mu_{S}\right)
. Then

\|A_{S,T}f-A_{S,S\cap T}f\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left|S\right|\left|T\right|\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

Lemma
3.3
covers the
case
S\cap T=\varnothing.
This shows that the corollary is true
in
\mu_{x}
for each
x\in V_{S\cap T}.
Therefore

\displaystyle\|A_{S,T}f-A_{S,S\cap T}f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S\cap T}}\|A_{S\setminus T,T\setminus S}^{\mu_{x}}f-A_{S\setminus T,\mathrm{\varnothing}}^{\mu_{x}}f\|_{L^{2}\left(\mu_{x}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|S\right|\left|T\right|\epsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}_{x}\|f\|_{L^{2}\left(\mu_{x}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|S\right|\left|T\right|\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

∎

We now show that compositions behave similarly to the product space
setting.

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

\|A_{T_{2}}A_{T_{1}}-A_{T_{1}\cap T_{2}}\|_{2\to 2}\leq\left|T_{1}\right|\left|T_{2}\right|\epsilon.

Proof.

We may assume that
T_{1}\cap T_{2}=\varnothing
. Indeed, if the
lemma holds for
T_{1}\cap T_{2}=\varnothing
then it holds in general.
Indeed, write

\tilde{T_{1}}=T_{1}\setminus T_{2},\tilde{T_{2}}=T_{2}\setminus T_{1},A=\left[k\right]\setminus\left(T_{1}\cap T_{2}\right).

Let
x\in V_{T_{1}\cap T_{2}}
. Then we have

\left(A_{T_{2}}A_{T_{1}}f\right)\left(x,\cdot\right)=\left(A_{\tilde{T}_{2}}^{\mu_{x}}A_{\tilde{T_{1}}}^{\mu_{x}}\right)\left(f\left(x,\cdot\right)\right)

and

A_{T_{1}\cap T_{2}}f\left(x,\cdot\right)=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mu_{x}}\left[f\left(x,y\right)\right].

Therefore once we prove the case
T_{1}\cap T_{2}=\varnothing
it
would imply that for each
x

\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mu_{x}}\left(A_{T_{2}}A_{T_{1}}f\left(x,y\right)-A_{T_{1}\cap T_{2}}f\left(x,y\right)\right)^{2}\leq\left|T_{1}\right|\left|T_{2}\right|\epsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mu_{x}}f\left(x,y\right)^{2}.

The lemma will then follow by taking expectations over
x
.

Let us now settle the case
T_{1}\cap T_{2}=\varnothing
. Write
\mathrm{T}=A_{T_{2}}A_{T_{1}}.

Then

\mathrm{T}=A_{T_{1},T_{2}}A_{T_{1}}.

Write
g=A_{T_{1}}f
. We have
\|g\|_{2}\leq\|f\|_{2}
by Cauchy–Schwarz.
By Lemma
3.3
we have

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{T}f-\mathbb{E}\left[f\right]\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|A_{T_{1},T_{2}}g-\mathbb{E}g\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|T_{1}\right|\left|T_{2}\right|\epsilon^{2}\|g\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|T_{1}\right|\left|T_{2}\right|\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

∎

4.
Efron–Stein decompositions for link
expanders

In this section, we introduce a new approximate Efron–Stein decomposition
for high dimensional expanders. In fact, it is more convenient to
state and prove our results in the more general setting of
\epsilon
-product
spaces, of which high dimensional expanders are a special case. We
proceed to discuss this setting below.

We first define the Efron–Stein decomposition via the usual formula
for it.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}\left(V,\mu\right)
and
S\subseteq\left[n\right].

We write

f^{=S}=\sum_{T\subseteq\left[S\right]}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus T\right|}A_{T}f.

The functions
f^{=S}
are defined in terms of the operators
A_{T}
.

L^{2}
-wise the composition of the operators
\left\{A_{T}\right\}_{T\subseteq\left[k\right]}

behave similarly to the compositions in the product case setting.
We satrt this section by making use of that and showing that many known
facts from the product setting generalize to the
\epsilon
-product
setting up to a small error.

4.1.
L^{2}
-approximations for the Efron–Stein decomposition

Thinking of
\epsilon
as tending to
0
in a much quicker pace
than
\frac{1}{k}
. Our goal is now to show that if
\mu
is
\epsilon
-product,
then we have:

(1)

\left|\|f\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}\|f^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}\right|=o\left(\|f\|_{2}^{2}\right),

(2)

and more generally

\left|\left\langle f,g\right\rangle-\sum_{S}\left\langle f^{=S},g^{=S}\right\rangle\right|=o\left(\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}\right).

One main tool involves the notion of a junta. We say that
g\colon V\to\mathbb{R}

is a
T
-junta
if
g\left(x\right)
depends only on
x_{T}.

Equivalently,
g
is a
T
-junta if
A_{T}g=g.

Our first step towards the proof is a near orthogonality result between

f^{=T}
and
g^{=S}
for
T\neq S.

We start by a Fourier formula that holds exactly, this is unlike most
of the results in this section that only generalize the situation
from the product space setting up to a small error term.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have

A_{S}\left[f\right]=\sum_{T\subseteq S}f^{=T}\left(x\right).

In particular
f=\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}f^{=S}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{T\subseteq S}f^{=T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T}\left(-1\right)^{\left|T\setminus T^{\prime}\right|}A_{T^{\prime}}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq S}A_{T^{\prime}}f\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|T\setminus T^{\prime}\right|}

\displaystyle=A_{S}f,

where the last equality follows from the fact that whenever
T^{\prime}\neq S

and
i\in S\setminus T^{\prime}
the pairs

\left(T,T\Delta\left\{i\right\}\right)

contribute opposing signs to the sum
\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|T\setminus T^{\prime}\right|}.

The ‘in particular’ part follows by taking
S=\left[k\right].

∎

The following lemma holds even without assuming that
\mu
is
\epsilon
-product.

Lemma 4.3
.

We have
\|A_{S,T}\|_{2\to 2}\leq 1

and

\|f^{=S}\|_{2}\leq 2^{\left|S\right|}\|f\|_{2}.

Proof.

The triangle inequality implies that it suffices to prove the former
claim. Now by Cauchy–Schwarz we have

\displaystyle\|A_{S,T}f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}A_{S,T}f\left(x\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\left(\mu_{x}\right)_{T}}f\left(y\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\left(\mu_{x}\right)_{T}}f\left(y\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f\colon V\to\mathbb{R}
,

T
be a set not containing
S
, and
g
be a
T
-junta.
Then

\left\langle f^{=S},g\right\rangle\leq\epsilon\sqrt{\left|S\right|\left|T\right|}2^{\left|S\right|}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

Proof.

As
A_{T}
is the dual to the inclusion operator
L^{2}\left(V_{T}\right)\to L^{2}\left(V_{\left[k\right]}\right)

we have

\left\langle f^{=S},g\right\rangle=\left\langle A_{T}f^{=S},g\right\rangle.

By Cauchy–Schwarz it is sufficient to show that

\|A_{T}f^{=S}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon\left|S\right|\left|T\right|2^{\left|S\right|}\|f\|_{2}.

Now

A_{T}f^{=S}=\sum_{S^{\prime}\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus S^{\prime}\right|}A_{T}A_{S^{\prime}}f.

Roughly speaking, we rely on Lemma
4.2
,
which says that
\|A_{T}A_{S^{\prime}}-A_{T\cap S^{\prime}}\|_{2\to 2}
is small together
with the fact that

(4.1)

\sum_{S^{\prime}\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus S^{\prime}\right|}A_{T\cap S^{\prime}}f=0.

The equality follows by choosing an arbitrary
i\in S\setminus T

and noting that the sets
\left(S^{\prime},S^{\prime}\Delta\left\{i\right\}\right)_{S^{\prime}\subseteq S}

correspond to the same term
A_{\left[k\right],T\cap S^{\prime}}
, while
appearing with opposite signs. This shows that we have

A_{T}f^{=S}=\sum_{S^{\prime}\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus S^{\prime}\right|}\left(A_{T}A_{S^{\prime}}f-A_{T\cap S^{\prime}}f\right).

By Lemma
3.5
we have

\displaystyle\|A_{T}A_{S^{\prime}}f-A_{T\cap S^{\prime}}f\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{\left|T\right|\left|S\right|}\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{\left|S\right|\left|T\right|}\epsilon\|f\|_{2}.

Hence,

\|A_{T}f^{=S}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{\left|S\right|\left|T\right|}2^{\left|S\right|}\epsilon.

∎

Proof of our near orhogonality result

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
T\neq S.
Then
\left\langle f^{=S},g^{=T}\right\rangle\leq 2^{2\left|S\right|+2\left|T\right|}\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

Proof.

The function
g^{=T}
is a
T
-junta. By Lemmas
4.4

and
4.3
we therefore have the following
chain of inequalities if
T
does not contain
S
.

\left\langle f^{=S},g^{=T}\right\rangle\leq\epsilon\sqrt{\left|S\right|\left|T\right|}2^{\left|S\right|}\|f\|_{2}\|g^{=T}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon 2^{2\left|S\right|+2\left|T\right|}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

A similar chain of inequalities holds when
S
does not contain

T.

∎

Parseval holds approximately for the Efron–Stein decomposition

Lemma 4.6
.

We have

\left|\left\langle f,g\right\rangle-\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}\left\langle f^{=S},g^{=S}\right\rangle\right|\leq 2^{4k}\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

Moreover, if
f
is a
T
-junta, then

\left|\left\langle f,g\right\rangle-\sum_{S\subseteq T}\left\langle f^{=S},g^{=S}\right\rangle\right|=2^{4\left|T\right|}\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

Proof.

We have
\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}\left\langle f^{=S},g^{=S}\right\rangle+\sum_{S\neq T}\left\langle f^{=S},g^{=T}\right\rangle.

By corollary
4.5

we have

\sum_{S\neq T}\left\langle f^{=S},g^{=T}\right\rangle\leq 2^{4k}\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

For the ‘moreover’ part note that if
f
is a
T
-junta, then

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\left\langle f,A_{T}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mu_{T}\right)}.

We may then apply the first part of the lemma in
\mu_{T}
noting
that
\left(A_{T}g\right)^{=T^{\prime}}=g^{=T^{\prime}}
for each
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
.
∎

\left(f^{=S}\right)^{=S}
is
L^{2}
-close to
f^{=S}.

In the product space setting we have
\left(f^{=S}\right)^{=T}=\begin{cases}f^{=S}&T=S\\
0&T\neq S\end{cases}.
Here we have the following instead:

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
g=f^{=S}.
Then:

(1)

If
S\neq T
, then

\|g^{=T}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2^{8k}\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}

(2)

\|g^{=S}-g\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2^{10k}\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

We have

g^{=T}=\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T,S^{\prime}\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus S^{\prime}\right|+\left|T\setminus T^{\prime}\right|}A_{T^{\prime}}A_{S^{\prime}}f.

Write

h=\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T,S^{\prime}\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus S^{\prime}\right|+\left|T\setminus T^{\prime}\right|}A_{T^{\prime}\cap S^{\prime}}f.

By Lemma
3.4
we therefore have

\|h-g^{=T}\|_{2}\leq 2^{2k}\max_{T^{\prime},S^{\prime}}\|A_{T^{\prime}}A_{S^{\prime}}-A_{T^{\prime}\cap S^{\prime}}\|_{2\to 2}\|f\|_{2}\leq 2^{4k}\epsilon\|f\|_{2}.

Now we claim that
h=0.
Indeed, assume without loss of generality
that
T
is not contained in
S
and let
i\in T\setminus S.
Then
the terms
A_{T^{\prime}\cap S^{\prime}}
appears with opposing sums for the pairs

T^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime}\Delta\left\{i\right\}
.

(2)-follows by the fact that

\|g^{=S}-g\|_{2}=\left\|\sum_{T\neq S}g^{=T}\right\|_{2}\leq\sum_{T\neq S}\|g^{=T}\|_{2}\leq 2^{5k}\epsilon\|f\|_{2}.

∎

4.2.
Approximate Efron-Stein decomposition

Again think of
\epsilon
as tending to 0 much more quickly than

\frac{1}{k}
. We now define a notion of
\left(\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-approximate
Efron–Stein decomposition. We show that a version of Lemma
4.6

still holds for these approximate Efron–Stein decompositions.

Motivation

One reason that demonstrates our need for an approximate Efron–Stein
decomposition is as follows. Let
f^{\leq d}=\sum_{\left|S\right|<d}f^{=S}
.
Then we do not have

\displaystyle\left(f^{\leq d}\right)^{=S}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}f^{=S}&\left|S\right|\leq d\\
0&\left|S\right|>d\end{cases},

but we would nevertheless like to work with the decomposition
\left\{f^{=S}\right\}_{\left|S\right|\leq d}

as an approximate Efron–Stein decomposition for
f
. We capture
that notion as follows.

Defining the
\left(\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-approximate Efron–Stein
decomposition

Definition 4.8
.

We say that
\left\{f_{S}\right\}_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}

is an
\left(\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-approximate Efron–Stein decomposition
if

(1)

\|f\|_{2}\leq\alpha.

(2)

\|f-\sum_{S}f_{S}\|_{2}<\epsilon^{\prime},

(3)

For each
S
there exists
h_{S}
with
\|h_{S}\|_{2}\leq\alpha

and

\|h_{S}^{=S}-f_{S}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon^{\prime}.

It turns out that we have an approximate Parseval theorem for every
approximate Efron–Stein decomposition.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}>0
.
Suppose that
f
has an
\left(\alpha_{1},\epsilon_{1}\right)
-bounded
approximate Efron–Stein decomposition
\left\{f_{S}\right\}

and
g
has an
\left(\alpha_{2},\epsilon_{2}\right)
-bounded Efron–Stein
decomposition
\left\{g_{S}\right\}.
Then

\left|\left\langle f,g\right\rangle-\sum_{S}\left\langle f_{S},g_{S}\right\rangle\right|\leq 2^{6k}\left(\epsilon_{1}\alpha_{2}+\epsilon_{2}\alpha_{1}+\epsilon\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\right).

Proof.

For each
S\subseteq\left[k\right]
let
\tilde{f}_{S},\tilde{g}_{S}

be with
\|\tilde{f}_{S}\|_{2}\leq\alpha_{1},\|\tilde{g}_{S}\|_{2}\leq\alpha_{2}

\|\tilde{f}{}_{S}^{=S}-f_{S}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon_{1},

and

\|\tilde{g}_{S}^{=S}-g_{S}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon_{2}.

Let

f^{\prime}_{S}=\tilde{f}_{S}^{=S},g^{\prime}_{S}=\tilde{g}_{S}^{=S},

f^{\prime}=\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}f^{\prime}_{S}

and

g^{\prime}=\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}g^{\prime}_{S}.

By Lemma
4.5

we have

\displaystyle\left\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{S}\left\langle f^{\prime}_{S},g^{\prime}_{S}\right\rangle+\sum_{S\neq T\subseteq\left[k\right]}\left\langle f_{S}^{\prime},g_{T}^{\prime}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{S}\left\langle f_{S}^{\prime},g_{S}^{\prime}\right\rangle\pm 2^{6k}\epsilon\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}.

Now by Cauchy–Schwarz

\displaystyle\left\langle f,g\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle f^{\prime},g-g^{\prime}\right\rangle+\left\langle f-f^{\prime},g\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{S}\left\langle f^{\prime}_{S},g^{\prime}_{S}\right\rangle\pm\left(2^{6k}\epsilon\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{2}\|g-g^{\prime}\|_{2}+\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{S}\left\langle f^{\prime}_{S},g^{\prime}_{S}\right\rangle\pm\left(2^{6k}\epsilon\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}+2^{2k}\alpha_{1}\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{1}\alpha_{2}\right),

where the last equality used

\|f^{\prime}\|_{2}\leq\sum\|f^{\prime}_{S}\|_{2}\leq 2^{k+\left|S\right|}\alpha_{1}\leq 2^{2k}\alpha_{1},

which follows from Lemma
4.3
.

To complete the proof we note that we similarly have

\displaystyle\left\langle f^{\prime}_{S},g^{\prime}_{S}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f_{S},g_{S}\right\rangle\pm\|f_{S}\|_{2}\|g_{S}-g_{S}^{\prime}\|_{2}+\|f_{S}^{\prime}-f_{S}\|_{2}\|g_{S}^{\prime}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle f_{S},g_{S}\right\rangle\pm\alpha\epsilon_{2}+2^{k}\epsilon_{1}\alpha_{2}.

∎

The above approximate Efron–Stein decomposition works well when
we care about
L_{2}
-norms. We actually care about closeness in
higher norms specifically
4
-norms. Our strategy when wishing to
upper bound
\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{4}
is to use the inequality

\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{4}^{4}\leq\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\left(\|f\|_{\infty}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\right).

Where we hope that the
L^{2}
-closeness is sufficient to overcome
the loss of using infinity norms. We would therefore like everything
to have a relatively small infinity norm.

Definition 4.10
.

We say that
\left\{f_{S}\right\}
is a
\left(\beta,\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-
bounded
approximate Efron-Stein
decomposition if it is an
\left(\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-approximate
Efron–Stein decomposition and moreover for each
S
:

\|h_{S}^{=S}\|_{\infty},\|f_{S}\|_{\infty},\|f\|_{\infty}

are all
\leq\beta.
Here
h_{S}^{=S}
is as in Definition
4.8
.

We now show that the different Efron–Stein decompositions of a function

f
are all close in
L_{4}.

Lemma 4.11
.

Suppose that
\left\{f_{S}\right\},\left\{f^{\prime}_{S}\right\}
are

\left(\beta,\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-bounded approximate Efron–Stein
decompositions for
f.
Then

(1)

\|f_{S}-f^{\prime}_{S}\|_{2}^{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\alpha^{2}\right),

(2)

\|f_{S}-f_{S}^{\prime}\|_{4}^{4}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{\prime 2}\beta^{2}\right)+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\alpha^{2}\beta^{2}\right),

(3)

\|\sum_{S}\left(f_{S}-f^{\prime}_{S}\right)\|_{4}^{4}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{\prime 2}\beta^{2}\right)+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}\beta^{2}\right),

(4)

and

\|f-\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}f_{S}\|_{4}^{4}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\beta^{2}\right)\left(\alpha^{2}+\|f\|_{2}^{2}\right).

Proof.

(3) is an immediate corollary of (2). (4) also follows immediately
from (3) by setting
f^{\prime}_{S}=f^{=S}
while applying it with
2^{k}\beta

rather than
\beta
. Indeed,
\|f^{=S}\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{k}\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{k}\beta
.
Therefore
\left\{f^{=S}\right\}_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}
is
a
\left(2^{k}\beta,\alpha,0\right)
-approximate Efron–Stein decomposition
for
f
. (2) follows immediately from (1) as we have

\|f_{S}-f^{\prime}_{S}\|_{4}^{4}\leq\|f_{S}-f^{\prime}_{S}\|_{2}^{2}\|f_{S}-f^{\prime}_{S}\|_{\infty}^{2}

and
\|f_{S}-f^{\prime}_{S}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq 4\beta^{2}.

We now prove (1).

Reducing to the case that
f^{\prime}_{S}=f^{=S}

First we assert that we may assume that
f^{\prime}_{S}=f^{=S}
for each

S.
Indeed,
\left\{f^{=S}\right\}
is a
\left(\beta,\alpha,0\right)
-Efron–Stein
decomposition. By the triangle inequality we have

\|f_{S}-f^{\prime}_{S}\|_{2}\leq\|f_{S}-f^{=S}\|_{2}+\|f^{=S}-f^{\prime}_{S}\|_{2},

which implies (by Hólder) that

\|f_{S}-f^{\prime}_{S}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|f_{S}-f^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}+2\|f^{=S}-f^{\prime}_{S}\|_{2}^{2}.

This shows that it is sufficient to prove the theorem when
\left\{f_{S}\right\}=\left\{f^{=S}\right\}

and when
\left\{f^{\prime}_{S}\right\}=\left\{f^{=S}\right\}.
Without
loss of generality we may assume that
f^{\prime}_{S}=f^{=S}.

Reducing to the case that
f_{S}=h_{S}^{=S}

Let
h_{S}
be with
\|h_{S}\|_{2}\leq\alpha
and
\|f_{S}-h_{S}^{=S}\|_{2}<\epsilon^{\prime}.

Setting
\tilde{f}_{S}=h_{S}^{=S}
we obtain by the triangle inequality
that
\left\{\tilde{f}_{S}\right\}_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}

is a
\left(\beta,\alpha,\left(2^{k}+1\right)\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-bounded
approximate Efron–Stein decomposition for
f
. We have

\|f_{S}-f^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|\tilde{f_{S}}-f_{S}\|_{2}^{2}+2\|\tilde{f}_{S}-f^{=S}\|_{2}\leq 2\epsilon^{\prime}+2\|\tilde{f}_{S}-f^{=S}\|_{2}.

Therefore it is sufficient to prove (1) when
f_{S}
is replaced
by
\tilde{f}_{S}
.

Proving the lemma when
f_{S}=h_{S}^{=S}
and
f^{\prime}_{S}=f^{=S}

By Cauchy–Schwarz and Corollary
4.5

we have:

\displaystyle\left\langle f_{S}-f^{=S},f\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\subseteq\left[k\right]}\left\langle f_{S}-f^{=S},f^{=T}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f_{S}-f^{=S},f^{=S}\right\rangle+\sum_{T\neq S}\left\langle f^{=S}-h_{S}^{=S},f^{=T}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f_{S}-f^{=S},f^{=S}\right\rangle+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\alpha^{2}\right).

Again by Corollary
4.5

and Cauchy–Schwarz we have:

\displaystyle\left\langle f_{S}-f^{=S},f\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f_{S}-f^{=S},\sum_{T}f_{T}\right\rangle+\left\langle f_{S}-f^{=S},f-\sum_{T}f_{T}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f_{S}-f^{=S},f_{S}\right\rangle+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\alpha^{2}\right)+\|f_{S}-f^{=S}\|_{2}\epsilon^{\prime}.

Rearranging we obtain,

\displaystyle\|f_{S}-f^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)\left(\|f^{=S}-f_{S}\|_{2}\right)+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\alpha^{2}\right).

This shows that

\|f_{S}-f^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\alpha^{2}\right).

∎

5.
Proof overview

Building on the framework we established in Section 4, we can now
give a proof overview for our hypercontactive inequality on high dimensional
expanders. Recall that in the setting of direct products, we first
prove a key lemma, (Lemma
2.8
) and then
use it to derive the theorem via an inductive argument. We now give
a sketch of how to generalise this approach to the
\epsilon
-product
setting.

5.1.
Generalising Lemma
2.8

Recall that we would like to show a lemma of the form

\|f\|_{4}^{4}\leq C^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{4}+\sum_{S}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S\right|}\|L_{S}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}.

We instead show a similar lemma that holds up to a small error term
of
O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)
:

(5.1)

\frac{1}{2}\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}\leq 9^{d}\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}^{4}+4\sum_{0<\left|T\right|\leq d}\left(4d\right)^{\left|T\right|}\|L_{T}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}.

However, first note that we do not have a useful notion of a low degree
function. Instead we work with

f^{\leq d}=\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}f^{=S}.

In turn, instead of
L_{S}\left[f\right]
we have

L_{S}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]=\sum_{T\supseteq S,\left|T\right|\leq d}f^{=T}.

We show that when expanding

\left(\left(f^{\leq d}\right)^{2}\right)^{=S}=\sum_{T_{1},T_{2}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S},

there are three kinds of terms: (1) terms that vanish in the product
space setting, but here they do not; (2) terms with
T_{1}\cap T_{2}\cap S\neq\varnothing
;
and (3) terms with
T_{1}\Delta T_{2}=S.

Our high-level approach is to show that the same proof as in the setting
of product spaces works up to an error term. We accomplish that by
expressing everything in terms of our operators
\left\{A_{S}\right\}
,
and we then replace equalities that hold in the product space by
L_{2}
-approximation
of the form

\|A_{S}A_{T}-A_{S\cap T}\|_{2\to 2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right).

At first glance, it might appear that this approach would not suffice,
as we eventually would like to upper bound
4
-norms of terms, or

2
-norms of expressions involving the product of two functions such
as
\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{S}.
Nevertheless, we are
able to accomplish that via inequalities of the form

\|f\|_{4}^{4}\leq\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}.

We then use the fact that all our terms are bounded by
O_{k}\left(\|f\|_{\infty}\right)
,
and our
L_{2}
-approximations involve
\epsilon
, and therefore
beat the
O_{k}\left(1\right)
-terms. This allows us to generalise
Lemma
2.8
and prove (
5.1
).

5.2.
Applying induction

After having an inequality of the form

\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}\leq C^{d}\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{S}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S\right|}\|L_{S}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4},

we would like to use a similar idea to the one we used in the product
space setting; that is, restrict
S
to some
x\in V_{S}
, and then
apply induction for the function
L_{S}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\left(x,\cdot\right).

The problem is that the restricted function
L_{S}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\left(x,\cdot\right)

is no longer of degree
d-\left|S\right|
, and hence we can no longer
use induction.

We overcome this problem by using the notion of our approximate Efron–Stein
decompositions. Namely, we show that
L_{S}^{\leq d}
has two different
approximate Efron–Stein decomposition. The first one is

\left\{f^{=T}\right\}_{T\supseteq S,\left|T\right|\leq d},

and the other one replaces
f^{=T}
by the function
f_{T}

\left(x,y\right)\mapsto\left(L_{S}\left[f\right]\left(x,\cdot\right)\right)^{=T\setminus S}\left(y\right).

We then obtain that
\sum_{\left|T\right|\supseteq S,\left|T\right|\leq d}f_{T}\left(x,\cdot\right)

is of the form
D_{S,x}^{\leq d-\left|S\right|}
, which allows us
to use induction similarly as in the product space setting.

After applying induction we get the compositions of two derivatives,
and we are again able to translate them back to expressions of the
form
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}I_{S,x}^{2}
by showing that
D_{S,x}D_{T,y}

and
D_{S\cup T,\left(x,y\right)}
are both approximate Efron–Stein
decompositions of the same expression.

The remaining step is to upper bound the influences. We achieve that
by generalising the inequality

\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}\left[I_{S,x}^{2}\right]\leq\delta\|L_{S}\left[f\right]\|_{2}^{2}

from the product space setting, where crucially, we obtain that without
upper bounding
\|I_{S,x}\|_{\infty}
.

6.
Laplacians, influences, and globalness on
\epsilon
-measures

In this section, we define the notions of laplacians, derivatives
and influences in the setting of
\epsilon
-measures, give bounded
approximated Efron–Stein decompositions related to the Laplacians,
define globalness, and show that it implies small influences.

6.1.
Defining the Laplacians, derivatives and influences

Definition 6.1
.

We define the Laplacians via the formula

L_{i}\left[f\right]=f-A_{\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}f.

Lemma 6.2
.

We have

L_{i}\left[f\right]=\sum_{S\ni i}f^{=S}.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemma
4.2
,
which shows that

A_{\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}\left[f\right]=\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}f^{=S}.

∎

Definition 6.3
.

We define
L_{S}\left[f\right]=\sum_{T\supseteq S}f^{=T}.
Alternatively,

L_{S}\left[f\right]=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|T\right|}A_{\left[k\right]\setminus T}f.

Let
x\in V_{S}.
We let
D_{S,x}=L_{S}\left[f\right]\left(x,\cdot\right)
,
i.e. the function in
L^{2}\left(V_{x},\mu_{x}\right)
obtained by
plugging in
x
in the
S
coordinates. We let

I_{S,x}\left[f\right]=\|D_{S,x}\left[f\right]\|_{L^{2}\left(V_{x},\mu_{x}\right)}.

6.2.
Bounded approximated Efron–Stein decompositions related to the
Laplacians

Lemma 6.4
.

. There exists

C=O_{k}\left(1\right)
, such that
\left\{f^{=T}\right\}_{T\supseteq S}

is a
\left(C\|f\|_{\infty},C\|f\|_{2},0\right)
-bounded approximate
Efron–Stein decomposition for
L_{S}\left[f\right].

Proof.

We have

\|f^{=S}\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{T\subseteq S}\|A_{T}f\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{\left|S\right|}\|f\|_{\infty}

and

\|L_{S}\left[f\right]\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{T\subseteq S}\|A_{\overline{T}}f\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{\left|S\right|}\|f\|_{\infty}.

The other properties are easy to verify.
∎

Let
f\in L^{2}\left(V_{\left[k\right]},\mu_{\left[k\right]}\right)

and let
g_{T}\in L^{2}\left(V_{T},\mu_{T}\right)
be given by

g_{T}\left(x\right)=I_{T,x}\left[f\right].

Then
g_{T}
can be interpreted interms of the Laplacians and the
averaging operators as

g_{T}=A_{T}\left(L_{T}\left[f\right]^{2}\right).

Suppose that
\left\{f_{S}\right\}_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}

is a
\left(\beta,\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-bounded approximate Efron–Stein
decompositions for
f
and set
\widetilde{L_{T}\left[f\right]}=\sum_{S\supseteq T}f_{S}
.
The following lemma essentially shows that the function
A_{T}\left(\widetilde{L_{T}\left[f\right]}^{2}\right)

is a good
L_{2}
-approximation for the function
g_{T}
. This can
be interpreted by saying that the generalised influences could be
computed via any
\left(\beta,\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-bounded approximate
Efron–Stein decomposition for
f
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\left\{f_{S}\right\}

and
\left\{f_{S}^{\prime}\right\}
be
\left(\beta,\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-bounded
Efron-Stein decompositions for
f.
Then

\|A_{T}\left(\sum_{S\supseteq T}f_{S}\right)^{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|A_{T}\left(\sum_{S^{\prime}\supseteq T}f^{\prime}_{S}\right)^{2}\|_{2}^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{\prime 2}\beta^{2}\right)+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\alpha^{2}\beta^{2}\right).

Proof.

By Cauchy–Schwarz we have

\left(\sum_{S\supseteq T}f_{S}\right)^{2}\leq 2\left(\sum_{S\supseteq T}f_{S}^{\prime}\right)^{2}+2\left(\sum_{S\supseteq T}f_{S}^{\prime}-f_{S}\right)^{2}.

Therefore

\|A_{T}\left(\sum_{S\supseteq T}f_{S}\right)^{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|A_{T}\left(\sum_{S^{\prime}\supseteq T}f^{\prime}_{S}\right)^{2}\|_{2}^{2}+2\|A_{T}\left(\sum_{S\supseteq T}f^{\prime}_{S}-f_{S}\right)^{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

Now since
A_{T}
contracts 2-norms (Lemma
4.3
).
We have

2\|A_{T}\left(\sum_{S\supseteq T}f^{\prime}_{S}-f_{S}\right)^{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|\sum_{S\supseteq T}f_{S}^{\prime}-f_{S}\|_{4}^{4}.

Lemma
4.11

now completes the proof.
∎

We now show that Lemma
6.4

is a special case of a more general phenomenon. Whenever
\left\{f_{S}\right\}_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}

is a
\left(\beta,\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-bounded approximate Efron–Stein
decomposition for
f
, we obtain that
\left\{f_{T}\right\}_{T\supseteq S}

is a
\left(\tilde{\beta},\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\epsilon}\right)
-bounded
approximate Efron–Stein decomposition for suitable values of
\tilde{\beta},\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\epsilon}
.
We show the following.

Lemma 6.6
.

There exists
C=O_{k}\left(1\right)
, such that the following holds.
Suppose that
\left\{f_{T}\right\}_{T\subseteq\left[k\right]}

is a
\left(\beta,\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-Approximate Efron–Stein
decomposition for
f
. Then
\left\{f_{T}\right\}_{T\supseteq S}

is a
\left(C\beta,\alpha,C\left(\epsilon^{\prime}+\alpha\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)\right)
-Approximate
Efron–Stein decomposition for
L_{S}\left[f\right].

Proof.

The only requirements that are not automatically inherited from
f

are the upper bounds on
\|L_{S}\left[f\right]\|_{\infty}
, and on

\|L_{S}f-\sum_{T\supseteq S}f_{T}\|_{2}.
The former inequality
follows from the inequality

\|L_{S}\left[f\right]\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{\left|S\right|}\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{k}\beta.

While the latter follows from Lemma
4.11

and the triangle inequality:

\displaystyle\|L_{S}f-\sum_{T\supseteq S}f_{T}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{T\supseteq S}\left(f^{=T}-f_{T}\right)\|_{2}.

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T\supseteq S}\|f^{=T}-f_{T}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)+O_{k}\left(\alpha\sqrt{\epsilon}\right).

∎

6.3.
Low degree functions and truncations

Definition 6.7
.

We define the
low degree part
of
f
by setting

f^{\leq d}=\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}f^{=S}

we define the
low degree Laplacians
of
f
by setting

L_{T}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]=\sum_{S\supseteq T,\left|S\right|\leq d}f^{=T}.

We now show that if
\left\{f_{T}\right\}_{T\subseteq\left[k\right]}

is a
\left(\beta,\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
bounded approxiate Efron–Stein
decomposition for
f
, then we may turn it into an Efron–Stein
decomposition for
f^{\leq d}
and
L_{S}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]

in the obvious way.

Lemma 6.8
.

There exists
C=O_{k}\left(1\right)
, such that the following holds.
Suppose that
\left\{f_{T}\right\}_{T\subseteq\left[k\right]}

is a
\left(\beta,\alpha,\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-Approximate Efron–Stein
decomposition for
f
. Then

(1)

The functions
\left\{f_{T}\right\}_{\left|T\right|\leq d}
are
a
\left(C\beta,\alpha,C\epsilon+C\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-approximate Efron–Stein
decomposition for
f^{\leq d}.

(2)

the functions
\left\{f_{T}\right\}_{\left|T\right|\supseteq S,\left|T\right|\leq d}

are a
\left(C\beta,\alpha,C\epsilon+C\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-approximate
Efron–Stein decomposition for
L_{S}^{\leq d}\left[f\right].

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove (2) as (1) is the special case where
S=\varnothing.

By Lemma
4.11
,
we have

\|f_{T}-f^{=T}\|_{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)+O_{k}\left(\sqrt{\epsilon}\alpha\right).

Hence, by the triangle inequality we have

\displaystyle\|L_{S}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]-\sum_{T\supseteq S,\left|T\right|\leq d}f_{T}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T\supseteq S,\left|T\right|\leq d}\|f^{=T}-f_{T}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{\prime}\right)+O_{k}\left(\sqrt{\epsilon}\alpha\right).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|L_{S}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{T\supseteq S,\left|T\right|\leq d}f^{=T}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{k}\max_{S}\|f^{=S}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{k}\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 4^{k}\beta.

∎

6.4.
Globalness

Unlike the product space setting the two possible definitions of globalness
are not equivalent. It turns out to be more convenient to work with
the notion concerning the restrictions.

Definition 6.9
.

We say that
f
is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global if for each
\left|S\right|\leq d
and each
x\in V_{S}

we have

\|f\left(x,\cdot\right)\|_{L^{2}\left(V_{x},\mu_{x}\right)}\leq\delta.

Claim 6.10
.

If
f
is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global
and
\epsilon
is sufficiently small, then for each
T
of size

\leq d
we have

\|f^{=T}\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{\left|T\right|}\delta.

Proof.

This follows from the triangle inequality once we show that
\|A_{T^{\prime}}f\|_{\infty}\leq\delta

for each
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
. Indeed, for each
x
we have

A_{T^{\prime}}f\left(x\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\left(V_{x},\mu_{x}\right)}f\left(x,\cdot\right)\leq\|f\left(x,\cdot\right)\|_{L^{2}\left(V_{x},\mu_{x}\right)}\leq\delta.

∎

Lemma 6.11
.

Suppose that
f
is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global.
Then
\left\{f^{=S}\right\}_{\left|S\right|\leq d}
is a
\left(k^{d}\delta,\|f\|_{2},0\right)
-bounded
Efron–Stein decomposition for
f^{\leq d}.

Proof.

We have
\|f^{=S}\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{d}\delta
by Claim
6.10
.
We also have

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\sum\|f^{=S}\|_{\infty}\leq k^{d}\delta.

The rest of the conditions hold automatically.
∎

Definition 6.12
.

We say that
f
is of
\left(\beta,\alpha\right)
-degree
d
if

f=\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}f_{S}
and
f_{S}=h_{S}^{=S}
where

\|h_{S}\|_{2}\leq\alpha
,
\|h_{S}^{=S}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta
and

\|f\|_{2}\leq\alpha,\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\beta.

If
f=\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}f_{S}
is of
\left(\beta,\alpha\right)
-degree

d
as above, then
\left\{f_{S}\right\}
is one
\left(\beta,\alpha,0\right)
-bounded
Efron–Stein decomposition for
f.
We now show that in this case
the canonical
\left\{f^{=S}\right\}_{\left|S\right|\leq d}
is
also
\left(\beta^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}\right)
-bounded Efron–Stein
decomposition for the right parameters.

6.5.
Other approximate Efron-Stein decompositions for
L_{T}\left[f\right],L_{T}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]

Definition 6.13
.

We define the low degree derivatives for
T\subseteq\left[k\right]

and
x\in V_{T}

D_{T,x}^{\leq d}\colon L^{2}\left(\mu\right)\to L^{2}\left(V_{x},\mu_{x}\right)

via

D_{T,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]=L_{T}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\left(x,\cdot\right)

The low degree influences for
T\subseteq\left[n\right]
and
x\in V_{T}

are defined by

I_{T,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]=\|L_{T}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\left(x,\cdot\right)\|_{L^{2}\left(V_{x},\mu_{x}\right)}^{2}.

We now move on to the critical lemma for our inductive approach. In
the product space setting our inductive approach relied on the fact
that
D_{T,x}f
is of degree
\leq d-\left|T\right|
whenever
f

is of degree
d.
Here we show that
L_{T}\left[f\right]
has an
alternative
\left(\beta,\alpha,\epsilon\right)
-bounded approximate
Efron–Stein decompositions
\left\{f_{S}\right\}_{S\supseteq T}

that gives rise to a function

\widetilde{L_{T}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]}=\sum_{S\supseteq T,\left|S\right|\leq d}f^{=S}

with the property that for each

\widetilde{L_{T}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]}\left(x,\cdot\right)

is of degree
d-\left|T\right|
.

Lemma 6.14
.

Let
f_{S}\left(x,y\right)=D_{T,x}^{=S\setminus T}[f](y).
Then for each

f
:

(1)

The set
\left\{f_{S}\right\}_{S\supseteq T}
is a
\left(C\|f\|_{\infty},C\|f\|_{2},C\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\right)
-bounded
approximate Efron–Stein decomposition for
L_{T}\left[f\right].

(2)

The set
\left\{f_{S}\right\}_{S\supseteq T,\left|S\right|\leq d}

is a
\left(C\|f\|_{\infty},C\|f\|_{2},C\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\right)
-bounded
approximate Efron–Stein decomposition for
L_{T}^{\leq d}\left[f\right].

(3)

If
T^{\prime}\supseteq T
, then the set
\left\{f_{S}\right\}_{S\supseteq T^{\prime}}

is a
\left(C\|f\|_{\infty},C\|f\|_{2},C\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\right)
-bounded
approximate Efron Stein decomposition for
L_{T^{\prime}}\left[f\right].

(4)

The set
\left\{f_{S}\right\}_{S\supseteq T^{\prime},\left|S\right|\leq d}

is a
\left(C\|f\|_{\infty},C\|f\|_{2},C\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\right)
-bounded
approximate Efron–Stein decomposition for
L_{T^{\prime}}^{\leq d}\left[f\right].

(5)

If
f
is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global. Then
\left\{f_{S}\right\}_{S\supseteq T^{\prime},\left|S\right|\leq d}

is a
\left(C\delta,C\|f\|_{2},C\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\right)
-bounded
approximate Efron–Stein decomposition for
L_{T^{\prime}}^{\leq d}\left[f\right].

Proof.

Due to Lemma
6.8

(1) implies (2)-(4). By Lemma
4.3
all
the operators
A_{I}
contract
\infty
-norms. We therefore have

\|f_{S}\|_{\infty}\leq\max_{x}2^{\left|S\setminus T\right|}\|D_{T,x}f\|_{\infty}=2^{\left|S\setminus T\right|}\|L_{T}f\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{\left|S\right|}\|f\|_{\infty}.

To complete the proof it is sufficient to show that

\|f_{S}-f^{=S}\|_{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\right)

as this will also imply that

\displaystyle\|\sum_{S\supseteq T}f_{S}-L_{T}\left[f\right]\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{S\supseteq T}f_{S}-\sum_{S\supseteq T}f^{=S}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\right).

We have

\displaystyle f_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{S^{\prime}\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus S^{\prime}\right|}A_{S^{\prime}}L_{T}f.

\displaystyle=\sum_{S^{\prime}\subseteq S}\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus S^{\prime}\right|+\left|T^{\prime}\right|}A_{S^{\prime}}A_{[k]\setminus T^{\prime}}f.

Write

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=\sum_{S^{\prime}\subseteq S}\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus S^{\prime}\right|+\left|T^{\prime}\right|}A_{S^{\prime}\setminus T^{\prime}}f

Then by Lemma
3.4
we have

\|f_{S}-h\|_{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}.

We then observe that whenever
S^{\prime}\not\supseteq T
the inner sum corresponding to it
is
0.
In this case there is some
i\in T\setminus S^{\prime}
and

T^{\prime},T^{\prime}\cup\left\{i\right\}
appear with alternating signs and correspond
to the same term
A_{S^{\prime}\setminus T^{\prime}}.
Therefore we have

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\subseteq S^{\prime}\subseteq S}\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus S^{\prime}\right|+\left|T^{\prime}\right|}A_{S^{\prime}\setminus T^{\prime}}f=\sum_{S^{\prime\prime}\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus S^{\prime\prime}\right|}A_{S^{\prime\prime}}f=f^{=S}.

This shows that
\|f_{S}-f^{=S}\|_{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

6.6.
Globalness implies small influences

In the product space setting we had
\|I_{T,x}\|_{\infty}\leq 4^{d}\delta^{2}

and we used it via the inequality

(6.1)

\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}\left[I_{T,x}^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}\left[I_{T,x}\right]4^{d}\delta^{2}=4^{d}\delta^{2}\|L_{T}\left[f\right]\|_{2}^{2}.

See the proof of Corollary
2.6
.
Here we find a convoluted way of proving an analogue of (
6.1
)
without having any upper bound on
\|I_{T,x}\|_{\infty}
at our disposal.

Lemma 6.15
.

Suppose that
f\colon V_{\left[k\right]}\to\mathbb{R}

is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global, and let
\left|T\right|\leq d
.
Then

\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}\left[\left(I_{T,x}\left[f\right]\right)^{2}\right]\leq 2^{d+1}\delta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}I_{T,x}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{4}^{4}\right).

Proof.

Write
g\left(x\right)=I_{T,x}\left[f\right].
Then
g=A_{T}\left[\left(L_{T}\left[f\right]\right)^{2}\right].

We would like to upper bound
\|g\|_{2}^{2}.
We accomplish that
by upper bounding
\|g\|_{2}^{2}
by
\mathbb{E}\left[gg^{\prime\prime}\right]

for a function
g^{\prime\prime}
with a small
\infty
-norm.

By Cauchy–Schwarz we have

L_{T}\left[f\right]^{2}\leq 2^{\left|T\right|}\sum_{T^{\prime}\supseteq\overline{T}}A_{T^{\prime}}\left[f\right]^{2}.

This shows that

(6.2)

g\leq 2^{\left|T\right|}\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T}A_{T}\left[\left(A_{T^{\prime}}f\right)^{2}\right],

on all
x
. Let us denote by
g^{\prime}
the right hand side of
6.2
.
Also let

g^{\prime\prime}=2^{\left|T\right|}\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T}A_{T\cap T^{\prime}}\left[\left(A_{T^{\prime}}f\right)^{2}\right].

Then in the product space setting the functions
g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}
would have
been equal. Here we have an
L^{2}-
approximation between them.

Claim 6.16
.

\|g^{\prime}-g^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{4}^{2}.

Proof.

As
\left(A_{T^{\prime}}f\right)^{2}
is a
T^{\prime}
-junta we have

\left(A_{T^{\prime}}f\right)^{2}=A_{T^{\prime}}\left[\left(A_{T^{\prime}}f\right)^{2}\right].

By Lemma
3.4
we have

\|A_{T}A_{T^{\prime}}-A_{T\cap T^{\prime}}\|_{2\to 2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right).

We therefore have

\displaystyle\|A_{T}\left[\left(A_{T^{\prime}}f\right)^{2}\right]-A_{T\cap T^{\prime}}\left[\left(A_{T^{\prime}}f\right)^{2}\right]\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\|\left(A_{T^{\prime}}f\right)^{2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\|f\|_{4}^{4},

as
A_{T^{\prime}}
contracts
4
-norms. Therefore,

\|g^{\prime}-g^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{4}^{2}.

∎

As
0\leq g\leq g^{\prime}
we have

\mathbb{E}\left[g^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[g^{\prime}g\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[g^{\prime\prime}g\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(g^{\prime}-g^{\prime\prime}\right)g\right].

By Cauchy–Schwarz we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(g^{\prime}-g^{\prime\prime}\right)g\right]\leq\|g^{\prime}-g^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{4}^{2}\|g\|_{2}.

Now either

(6.3)

\mathbb{E}\left[g^{2}\right]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[g^{\prime\prime}g\right]

or

\mathbb{E}\left[g^{2}\right]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\left(g^{\prime}-g^{\prime\prime}\right)g\right]\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{4}^{2}\|g\|_{2}.

In the latter case we have

(6.4)

\|g\|_{2}^{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\|f\|_{4}^{4}.

after rearranging. We can now sum the upper bounds of (
6.3
)
and (
6.4
) corresponding to each of the cases to obtain
the upper bound

\mathbb{E}\left[g^{2}\right]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[g^{\prime\prime}g\right]+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\|f\|_{4}^{4}.

that is true in both cases. The following claim completes the proof.

Claim 6.17
.

\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{d}\delta^{2}.

Proof.

By Cauchy–Schwarz we point-wise have
\left(A_{T^{\prime}}f\right)^{2}\leq A_{T^{\prime}}\left(f^{2}\right).

We therefore have

A_{T\cap T^{\prime}}\left[\left(A_{T^{\prime}}f\right)^{2}\right]\leq A_{T\cap T^{\prime}}A_{T^{\prime}}\left(f^{2}\right)=A_{T\cap T^{\prime}}\left(f^{2}\right)\leq\delta^{2}.

This shows that
\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{d}\delta^{2}.

∎

∎

The same proof works for the truncated influences.

Lemma 6.18
.

Suppose that
f\colon V_{\left[k\right]}\to\mathbb{R}

is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global. Suppose additionally that
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}\left(k\right)
.
Then we have

\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}\left[\left(I_{T,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\right)^{2}\right]\leq 2^{d+4}\delta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}\left[I_{T,x}^{\leq d}\right]+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\right).

Proof.

Write

g_{1}\left(x\right)=\|\left(D_{T,x}\left[f\right]\right)^{\leq d-\left|T\right|}\|_{2}^{2}.

We now proceed with the following steps.

Upper bounding
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}\left[\left(I_{T,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\right)^{2}\right]

in terms of
g_{1}

By Lemma
6.14

the functions

\left\{D_{T,x}\left[f\right]^{=S}\right\}_{\left|S\right|\leq d-\left|T\right|},

is an alternative (
O_{k}\|f\|_{\infty},O_{k}\|f\|_{2},O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\right)
)-bounded
approximate Efron–Stein decomposition for
L_{T}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]
.
Therefore by Lemma
6.5
we
have

(6.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}\left[I_{T,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}\left[g_{1}\left(x\right)^{2}\right]+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

Repeating the proof of Lemma
6.18

Now by Lemma
4.9
we have
g_{1}\left(x\right)\leq 2I_{T,x}

for each
x
, provided that
\epsilon
is sufficiently small. Write

g_{2}\left(x\right)=I_{T,x}\left[f\right].
Similarly to the proof
of Lemma
6.18
we let

g_{2}^{\prime}=2^{\left|T\right|}\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T}A_{T}\left[\left(A_{T^{\prime}}f\right)^{2}\right]

and let

g_{2}^{\prime\prime}=2^{\left|T\right|}\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T}A_{T\cap T^{\prime}}\left[\left(A_{T^{\prime}}f\right)^{2}\right].

By Cauchy–Schwarz we have

\displaystyle\|g_{1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[g_{1}g_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[g_{1}g_{2}^{\prime}\right]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[g_{1}g_{2}^{\prime\prime}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[g_{1}\left(g_{2}^{\prime}-g_{2}^{\prime\prime}\right)\right].

Now either

\|g_{1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 4\mathbb{E}\left[g_{2}^{\prime\prime}g_{1}\right],

which would imply

\|g_{1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 4\mathbb{E}\left[g_{2}^{\prime\prime}g_{1}\right]\leq 2^{d+2}\delta\mathbb{E}\left[g_{1}\right]

by Claim

. This shows that

(6.6)

\|g_{1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2^{d+2}\delta\mathbb{E}\left[g_{1}\right]+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{4}^{4}\right).

Moving back from

to
I_{T,x}^{\leq d}

By Lemmas
6.14

yielding

),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{T}}\left[I_{T,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\|g_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

\displaystyle\leq 2^{d+3}\delta^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[g_{1}\right]+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{4}^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{d+3}\delta^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[g_{1}\right]+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

The lemma now follows by putting everything together.
∎

Proving hypercontractivity for
\epsilon
-product measures

We suggest revisiting Section 2 before reading this section. Our strategy
is the same as in the product case, and we deal with the differences

7.1.
Upper bounding
\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}
by
4
-norms of non-trivial
Laplacians and
\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}^{4}

We now move on to preparing the ground for the proof of our hypercontractive
inequality.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
f
be
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global.
Suppose that
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}\left(k\right)
. Then we have

\frac{1}{2}\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}\leq 9^{d}\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}^{4}+4\sum_{0<\left|T\right|\leq d}\left(4d\right)^{\left|T\right|}\|L_{T}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
g=f^{\leq d}.
By Lemma
4.9
we have

\|g\|_{4}^{4}\leq 2\sum_{S}\|\left(g^{2}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}.

We now upper bound
\|\left(g^{2}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}.
We have

\left(g^{2}\right)^{=S}=\sum_{\left|T_{1}\right|\leq d,\left|T_{2}\right|\leq d}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}.

Let

(1)

I_{1}=\left\{\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right):T_{1}\cap T_{2}\cap S\neq\varnothing\right\}.

(2)

I_{2}=\left(\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right):T_{1}\Delta T_{2}=S\right)

(3)

I_{3}=\left(T_{1}\Delta T_{2}\right)\setminus S\neq\varnothing
or

S\setminus\left(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\right)\neq\varnothing
.

Our first step is to show that the contribution from
I_{3}
is negligible.
This is to be expected as in the product space setting we were able
to show that the contribution from
I_{3}
is 0.

Claim 7.2
.

Let
\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)\in I_{3}
. Then

\|\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\left(T_{1}\Delta T_{2}\right)\setminus S\neq\varnothing.

Then without loss of generality we may assume that there is some
i\in T_{1}\setminus\left(T_{2}\cup S\right).

By Lemma
4.9
we have

\|\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|A_{\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)\|_{2}^{2}.

Now

A_{\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)=\left(A_{\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}\right)\right)f^{=T_{2}}.

By Lemma
4.7
we have

\|A_{\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}f^{=T_{1}}\|_{2}\leq\sum_{T^{\prime}\not\ni i}\|\left(f^{=T_{1}}\right)^{=T^{\prime}}\|_{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}.

This shows that

\displaystyle\|A_{\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}f^{=T_{1}}\|_{2}^{2}\|f^{=T_{2}}\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Suppose now that
S\setminus\left(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\right)\neq\varnothing.

Let
i\in S\setminus\left(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\right).
Then the function

g=f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}
is a
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
-junta. This shows
that
g=A_{T_{1}\cup T_{2}}g.

Hence by Lemma
4.7
and the triangle
inequality we have

\displaystyle\|g^{=S}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|\left(A_{T_{1}\cup T_{2}}g\right)^{=S}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sum_{T\subseteq T_{1}\cup T_{2}}\left(g^{=T}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|g\|_{2}.

It now remains to note that
\|g\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|f^{=T_{1}}\|_{2}\|f^{=T_{2}}\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{2k}\|f\|_{2}\|f\|_{\infty}.

∎

We now move on to our next step of upper bounding the contribution
from the pairs in
I_{1}.

Claim 7.3
.

\sum_{\left(T_{1},T_{2}\right)\in I_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\left(-1\right)^{\left|T\right|+1}\|\left(L_{T}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]^{2}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof is exactly the same as in the product case so we omit it.
∎

It now remains to consider the contribution from
I_{2}
, i.e. the
case
T_{1}\Delta T_{2}=S
. Here just like the product case it is
sufficient to show the following claim

Claim 7.4
.

Let
T_{1}\Delta T_{2}=S
. Then we have

\|\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}\leq 2\|f^{=T_{1}}\|_{2}\|f^{=T_{2}}\|_{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}\|f\|_{\infty},

provided that
\epsilon
is sufficiently small.

Proof.

First let
S^{\prime}\subsetneq S.
As
T_{1}\Delta T_{2}=S
, there exists

i\in\left(T_{1}\Delta T_{2}\right)\setminus S^{\prime}.
Without loss of
generality
i\in T_{1}.
By Lemmas
4.9
,
4.7
,
and
4.2
we have

\displaystyle\|A_{S^{\prime}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|A_{S^{\prime}\cup T_{2}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|A_{S^{\prime}\cup T_{2}}f^{=T_{1}}\|_{2}\|f^{=T_{2}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}\|f\|_{\infty}.

By the triangle inequality this shows that

\displaystyle\|\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{=S}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S^{\prime}}\left(-1\right)^{\left|S\setminus S^{\prime}\right|}A_{S^{\prime}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right).

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{S}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)\|_{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}\|f\|_{\infty}

We now upper bound
\|A_{S}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)\|_{2}.

By Cauchy–Schwarz for
x\in V_{S}
we have

\displaystyle A_{S}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle f^{=T_{1}}\left(x,\cdot\right),f^{=T_{2}}\left(x,\cdot\right)\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(V_{x},\mu_{x}\right)}

This shows that

(7.1)

\displaystyle\|A_{S}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)\|_{2}^{2}

\|f^{=T_{1}}\left(x,\cdot\right)\|_{L^{2}\left(V_{x},\mu_{x}\right)}^{2}=A_{S}\left[\left(f^{=T_{1}}\right)^{2}\right]=A_{S}A_{T_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}\right)^{2}

By Lemma
3.4

\|A_{S}A_{T_{1}}-A_{S\cap T_{1}}\|_{2\to 2}\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle\|A_{S}A_{T_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}\right)^{2}-A_{S\cap T_{1}}\left[\left(f^{=T_{1}}\right)^{2}\right]\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\|f^{=T_{1}}\|_{4}^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

By Cauchy–Schwarz this shows that

RHS of (
7.1
)

\displaystyle=\left\langle A_{S}A_{T_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}\right)^{2},A_{S}\left(f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(V_{S},\mu_{S}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\langle A_{S\cap T_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}\right)^{2},A_{S}\left(f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(V_{S},\mu_{S}\right)}

\displaystyle+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}\|f\|_{\infty}\right)\|A_{S}\left(f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{2}\|_{2}.

As we have

\|A_{S}\left(f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq O_{k}\left(\|f\|_{4}^{4}\right)\leq O_{k}\left(\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|A_{S}\left(f^{=T_{1}}f^{=T_{2}}\right)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle A_{S\cap T_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}\right)^{2},A_{S}\left(f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(V_{S},\mu_{S}\right)}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle A_{S\cap T_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}\right)^{2},A_{S\cap T_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mu\right)}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

Now

A_{S\cap T_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{2}=A_{S\cap T_{1}}A_{T_{2}}\left(f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{2}

and
\|A_{S\cap T_{1}}A_{T_{2}}-\mathbb{E}\|_{2\to 2}\leq\epsilon

by Lemma
3.4
. Therefore we similarly
have

\displaystyle\left\langle A_{S\cap T_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}\right)^{2},A_{S\cap T_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{2}}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mu\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\langle A_{S\cap T_{1}}\left(f^{=T_{1}}\right)^{2},\|f^{=T_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}\right\rangle+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

\displaystyle=\|f^{=T_{1}}\|_{2}^{2}\|f^{=T_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

This completes the proof of the claim.
∎

The rest of the proof is the exactly the same as in the product case
setting.
∎

Now the only thing to remains is to apply the inductive hypothesis.

Theorem 7.5
.

We have
\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}\leq 20^{d}\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S\right|}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}I_{S,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
d
. By Lemma
7.1

we have

(7.2)

\|f{}^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}\leq 2\cdot 9^{d}\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}^{4}+2\cdot\sum_{S\neq\varnothing}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S\right|}\|L_{S}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Write
g_{S,x}\left(y\right)=\left(D_{S,x}\left[f\right]\right)^{\leq d-\left|T\right|}\left(y\right).

Then by Lemma
6.14

and Lemma
4.11

we have:

\mathbb{E}_{x}\|D_{S,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{x}\|g_{S,x}\|_{4}^{4}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}.

By induction, we have

(7.3)

\displaystyle\|g_{S,x}\|_{4}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq 20^{d-\left|S\right|}\sum_{T\cap S=\varnothing,\left|T\right|\leq d-\left|S\right|}\left(4d\right)^{\left|T\right|}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mu_{T}}I_{T,y}^{2}\left[g_{S,x}\right]+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|g_{S,x}\|_{2}^{2}\|g_{S,x}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

By Lemma
6.14

we have
\|g_{S,x}\|_{\infty}=O_{k}\left(\|f\|_{\infty}\right)
.
By Lemmas
6.14
,

4.11
,
and
4.9
we have

(7.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}\|g_{S,x}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}\|D_{S,x}^{\leq d}f\|_{2}^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq O_{k}\left(\|f\|_{2}^{2}\right)

Taking expectations over (
7.3
), and plugging in (
7.4
)
we obtain:

\mathbb{E}_{x}\|g_{S,x}\|_{4}^{4}\leq 2\sum_{T\cap S=\varnothing,\left|T\right|\leq d-\left|S\right|}\left(4d\right)^{\left|T\right|}\mathbb{E}_{\left(x,y\right)\sim\mu_{S\cup T}}I_{T,y}^{2}\left[g_{S,x}\right]+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}.

By Lemmas
6.14

and
4.11

we have

\mathbb{E}_{\left(x,y\right)\sim\mu_{S\cup T}}I_{T,y}^{2}\left[g_{S,x}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mu_{T\cup S}}I_{T\cup S,z}^{2}\left[g\right]+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Hence,

\mathbb{E}_{x}\|g_{S,x}\|_{4}^{4}\leq 20^{d-\left|S\right|}\sum_{S^{\prime}\supseteq S\left|S^{\prime}\right|\leq d}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S^{\prime}\setminus S\right|}\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mu_{S^{\prime}}}\left(I_{S^{\prime},z}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\right)^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}.

This gives

\mathbb{E}_{x}\|D_{S,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\|_{4}^{4}\leq 2\cdot 20^{d-\left|S\right|}\sum_{S^{\prime}\supseteq S\left|S^{\prime}\right|\leq d}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S^{\prime}\setminus S\right|}\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mu_{S^{\prime}}}\left(I_{S^{\prime},z}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\right)^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}

The proof is now completed by plugging this inequality in (
7.2
).
Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 9^{d}\|f\|_{2}^{4}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{0<\left|S\right|\leq d}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S\right|}\cdot 2\cdot 20^{d-\left|S\right|}\sum_{S^{\prime}\supseteq S\left|S^{\prime}\right|\leq d}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S^{\prime}\setminus S\right|}\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mu_{S^{\prime}}}\left(I_{S^{\prime},z}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 20^{d}\sum_{\left|S^{\prime}\right|\leq d}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S\right|}\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mu_{S^{\prime}}}\left(I_{S^{\prime},z}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\right)^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

∎

7.2.
The case where
\|f\|_{\infty}
is large

Here we show a hypercontractive inequality whose error term does not
include the factor
\|f\|_{\infty}.
This may be useful when
\|f\|_{\infty}

is significantly larger than
\delta.

Theorem 7.6
.

Suppose
that
f
is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global, then

\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}\leq 20^{d+1}\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S\right|}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}I_{S,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\delta^{2}\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

By applying Theorem
7.7
with

f^{\leq d}
rather then
f
and using
\|f^{\leq d}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta

we obtain

\|\left(f^{\leq d}\right)^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}\leq 20^{d}\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}\left(4d\right)^{\left|S\right|}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}I_{S,x}^{\leq d}\left[f^{\leq d}\right]^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\delta^{2}\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

The theorem now follows from Lemmas
6.11
,
4.11

and
6.5
.
∎

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}\left(k\right)

be sufficiently small. Suppose that
f
is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global.
Then we have

\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}\leq\left(100d\right)^{d}\delta^{2}\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}^{2}+O_{k}\left(\delta^{2}\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\right).

Proof.

By Theorem
7.6
,
Lemma
6.18
, and
4.9

we have

\displaystyle\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq 20^{d}\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}\left(4d\right)^{d}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{S}}\left(I_{S,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\right)^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 20^{d}\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}\left(8d\right)^{d+2}\delta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[I_{S,x}^{\leq d}\left[f\right]\right]+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 20^{d}\sum_{\left|S\right|\leq d}\left(8d\right)^{d+2}\delta^{2}\sum_{T\supseteq S,\left|T\right|\leq d}\|f^{=T}\|_{2}^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(40d\right)^{d}\delta^{2}\sum_{\left|T\right|\leq d}\|f^{=T}\|_{2}^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(40d\right)^{d}\delta^{2}\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}^{2}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

8.
Applications

In this section, we show our applications of the hypercontractive
inequality on high dimensional expanders, which we have shown in the
previous section. The applications follow in a fairly straightforward
way, and hence we present them with brevity.

8.1.
Global Boolean functions are concentrated on the high degrees.

Fourier concentration results are widely useful in complexity theory
and learning theory. Our first application is a Fourier concentration
theorem for HDX. Namely, the following theorem shows that global Boolean
functions on
\epsilon
-HDX are concentrated on the high degrees,
in the sense that the
2
-norm of the restriction of a function to
its low-degree coefficients only constitutes a tiny fraction of its
total
2
-norm.

Corollary 8.1
.

If
f\colon V_{\left[k\right]}\to\left\{0,1\right\}

is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global and
\epsilon
is sufficiently
small. Then

\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left(O_{k}\left(\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)+\left(200d\right)^{d}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.9
we have

\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}^{2}=\left\langle f^{\leq d},f\right\rangle-O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

We also have by Theorem
7.7

\displaystyle\left\langle f^{\leq d},f\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|f^{\leq d}\|_{4}\|f\|_{\frac{4}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(100d\right)^{d}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}}\|f\|_{\frac{4}{3}}+O_{k}\left(\sqrt{\epsilon}\|f\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\frac{4}{3}}\right).

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(100d\right)^{d}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\|f\|_{2}}\|f\|_{\frac{4}{3}}+O_{k}\left(\sqrt{\epsilon}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(200d\right)^{d}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{2}^{2}+O_{k}\left(\sqrt{\epsilon}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\right)

∎

The Corollary completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

8.2.
Small-set expansion theorem

Small set expansion is a fundamental property that is prevalent in
combinatorics and complexity theory. In the setting of the
\rho
-noisy
Boolean hypercube, the small set expansion theorem gives an upper
bound on
\mathrm{{Stab}_{\rho}}(1_{A})=\langle 1_{A},T_{\rho}1_{A}\rangle=E[1_{A}(x)1_{A}(y)]

for indicators
1_{A}
of small sets
A
, which captures the probability
that a random walk starting at a point
x\in A
remains in
A
,
hence showing that small sets are expanding. Our second application
is a small set expansion theorem for global functions on
\epsilon
-HDX,
captured via bounding the natural noise operator in this setting.

Definition 8.2
.

Let
\rho\in\left(0,1\right).
Given
x\in V_{\left[k\right]}
we
let
N_{\rho}\left(x\right)
be the distribution where
y\sim N_{\rho}\left(x\right)

is chosen by choosing a random set
S
where each
i
is in
S

independently with probability
\rho,
then choosing
z\sim\mu_{x_{S}}

and setting
y=\left(x_{S},z\right).
We then set

\mathrm{T}_{\rho}f\left(x\right)=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim N_{\rho}\left(x\right)}f.

Alternatively we can use the averaging operators to give the following
equivalent definition:

\mathrm{T}_{\rho}:=\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}\rho^{\left|S\right|}\left(1-\rho\right)^{k-\left|S\right|}A_{S}\left[f\right].

We have the following formula for the noise operator, which is similar
to the one in the product space setting.

Claim 8.3
.

We have
\mathrm{T}_{\rho}f=\sum_{S}\rho^{\left|S\right|}f^{=S}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{\rho}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}\rho^{\left|S\right|}\left(1-\rho\right)^{k-\left|S\right|}A_{S}\left[f\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\subseteq\left[k\right]}\rho^{\left|S\right|}\left(1-\rho\right)^{k-\left|S\right|}\sum_{T\subseteq S}f^{=T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\subseteq\left[k\right]}\sum_{S\supseteq T}\rho^{\left|S\right|}\left(1-\rho\right)^{k-\left|S\right|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\subseteq\left[k\right]}\rho^{\left|T\right|}f^{=T}.

∎

Via a standard argument we have the following bound on the noise operator.

Lemma 8.4
.

We have

\|\mathrm{T}_{\rho}f\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|f^{\leq d}\|_{2}^{2}+\left(\rho^{d}+O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

This is immediate from Lemmas
8.3

and
4.9
.
∎

Our small set expansion applications are as follows.

Corollary 8.5

(Small set expansion theorem)
.

If
f\colon V_{\left[k\right]}\to\left\{0,1\right\}
is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global.
Then

\|\mathrm{T}_{\rho}f\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left(\rho^{d}+\left(100d\right)^{d}\delta^{2}+O_{k}\left(\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemma
8.4

and Corollary
8.1
.
∎

8.3.
Kruskal–Katona theorem

Our last application is an analogue of the Kruskal–Katona theorem
in the setting of high dimensional expanders. The Kruskal-Katona theorem
is a fundamental and widely-applied result in algebraic combinatorics,
which gives a lower bound on the size of the lower shadow of a set

A
, denoted
\partial(A)=\{x\colon y\prec x,\;\text{for some}\;y\in A\}
.

We first consider the natural up-down walk in our setting.

Definition 8.6
.

The operator corresponding to up-down random walk is

\mathrm{T}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{\left[k\right]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}\left[f\right]=\sum_{S}\frac{k-\left|S\right|}{k}f^{=S}.

By applying the approximate Parseval inequality (Lemma
4.9
),
we obtain the following claim.

Claim 8.7
.

We have

\left\langle f-\mathrm{T}f,f\right\rangle\geq\frac{d}{k}\|f^{\geq d}\|_{2}^{2}-O_{k}\left(\epsilon\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

By our Fourier concentration theorem, (Corollary
8.1
),
we have the following lower bound on the
2
-norm of the high degree
part of
f
.

Claim 8.8
.

Let
\delta\leq\left(200d\right)^{-2d},
and
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}\left(k\right)

be sufficiently small. If
f\colon V_{\left[k\right]}\to\left\{0,1\right\}

is
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global. Then

\|f^{\geq d}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Combining the above claims we get the following.

Claim 8.9
.

Let
\delta\leq\left(200d\right)^{-2d}.
We have

\left\langle f-\mathrm{T}f,f\right\rangle\geq\frac{d}{2k}\|f\|_{2}^{2}

We are now ready to prove the Kruskal–Katona theorem in the setting
of high dimensional expanders.

Corollary 8.10
.

Let
X
be an
\epsilon
-HDX, for a sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
.
Let
\delta\leq\left(200d\right)^{-d},
and let
A\subseteq X\left(k-1\right)

be
\left(d,\delta\right)
-global. Then

\mu\left(\partial(A)\right)\geq\mu\left(A\right)\left(1+\frac{d}{2k}\right).

Proof.

Let
f=1_{A}.
We have

\displaystyle\left\langle f-\mathrm{T}f,f\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Pr_{\sigma\sim X\left(k-1\right)}\Pr_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\supset\sigma}\left[\tau_{1}\in A,\tau_{2}\notin A\right].

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\left[\sigma\in\partial(A),f\left(\tau_{2}\right)\notin A\right]

\displaystyle=\mu\left(\partial(A)\right)-\mu\left(A\right).

∎

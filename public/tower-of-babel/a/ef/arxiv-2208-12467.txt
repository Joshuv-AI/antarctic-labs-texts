Periodic Clifford symmetry algebras on flux lattices
Yue-Xin Huang, Z. Y. Chen, Xiaolong Feng, Shengyuan A. Yang, Y. X. Zhao

Abstract
Real Clifford algebras play a fundamental role in the eight real Altland-Zirnbauer symmetry classes and the classification tables of topological phases. Here, we present another elegant realization of real Clifford algebras in the d d -dimensional spinless rectangular lattices with π \pi flux per plaquette. Due to the T T -invariant flux configuration, real Clifford algebras are realized as projective symmetry algebras of lattice symmetries. Remarkably, d d mod 8 8 exactly corresponds to the eight Morita equivalence classes of real Clifford algebras with eightfold Bott periodicity, resembling the eight real Altland-Zirnbauer classes. The representation theory of Clifford algebras determines the degree of degeneracy of band structures, both at generic k k points and at high-symmetry points of the Brillouin zone. Particularly, we demonstrate that the large degeneracy at high-symmetry points offers a rich resource for forming novel topological states by various dimerization patterns, including a 3 3 D higher-order semimetal state with double-charged bulk nodal loops and hinge modes, a 4 4 D nodal surface semimetal with 3 3 D surface solid-ball zero modes, and 4 4 D Möbius topological insulators with a eightfold surface nodal point or a fourfold surface nodal ring. Our theory can be experimentally realized in artificial crystals by their engineerable ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} gauge fields and capability to simulate higher dimensional systems.



I
Introduction

Clifford algebra is a mathematical structure that has found applications in diverse subjects such as modern geometry
Atiyah et al. 1964
, quantum field theory, supergravity
Polchinski 1998
, and digital image processing
Rodriguez et al. 2008
. Its subset, the real Clifford algebras (RCAs), recently came into the limelight in
condensed matter physics, when they were revealed to underlie the classification theory of topological band structures
Schnyder et al. 2008
;
Kitaev 2009
;
Zhao and Wang 2013
;
Chiu et al. 2016
;
Zhao et al. 2016
.
Considering the time-reversal (
T
) and particle-hole (
C
) symmetries, there are ten symmetry classes, known as the Altland-Zirnbauer classes
Altland and Zirnbauer 1997
,
eight of which containing at least
T
or
C
symmetry are real and are described by the RCAs
Kitaev 2009
.
The theory of RCA helps to establish the topological classification table
Chiu et al. 2016
, which exhibits an intriguing eightfold periodicity along spatial dimensions, rooted in the famous Bott periodicity of RCAs under the Morita equivalence
Atiyah et al. 1964
;
Morita 1958
.

In this Letter, we present another elegant realization of RCAs by the projective symmetry algebras of simple rectangular lattices with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge fluxes. The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge fields, namely allowing the (real) hopping amplitudes to take positive or negative signs, can be readily engineered in various artificial crystals, such as acoustic/photonic crystals
Lu et al. 2014
;
Yang et al. 2015
;
Mittal et al. 2019
;
Xue et al. 2020
, cold atoms in optical lattices
Jaksch and Zoller 2003
;
Aidelsburger et al. 2011
;
Aidelsburger et al. 2013
, electric-circuit arrays
Imhof et al. 2018
;
Yu et al. 2020
, and mechanical systems
Huber 2016
;
Prodan and Prodan 2009
. The importance of projective symmetry algebras under
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge fields has recently been emphasized
Zhao et al. 2020
;
Zhao et al. 2021
;
Shao et al. 2021
, with fascinating consequences demonstrated in experiment
Xue et al. 2021
;
Li et al. 2021
. Meanwhile, artificial crystals also enable simulations of higher-dimensional states by synthesized dimensions, e.g.,
4
D quantum Hall states has been demonstrated experimentally
Price et al. 2015
;
Lohse et al. 2018
;
Wang et al. 2020a
.

Motivated by these recent experimental progress, we consider spinless rectangular lattices in
d
dimensions, with
\pi
-flux for each plaquette. Their projective symmetry algebras are generated by lattice translations, mirror reflections, and
T
.
We show that RCAs naturally arise as projective algebras of these symmetries in momentum space. It follows that the RCA theory elegantly captures the symmetry characters of the band structures. (i) For a generic
k
-point in the Brillouin zone (BZ),
d=1,2,\cdots,8
exhaust all eight Morita equivalence classes of RCAs, resembling the eight real Altland-Zirnbauer classes. Interestingly, there again emerges an eightfold periodicity: If
d_{1}=d_{2}\mod 8
, they correspond to the same Morita class, such that all dimensions
>8
can be reduced to
d=1,\cdots,8
. (ii) For any high-symmetry point
K^{s,r}
in BZ, with
s
(
r
) the number of
\pi
(
0
) components in its momentum-space coordinate, the little algebra is also characterized by a RCA determined by
r=d-s
.
We show that the irreducible representations (IRREPs) of RCAs determine the band structure degeneracy. Particularly,
large degeneracies are dictated at certain high-symmetry points, which are resources transformable to rich novel topological phases under proper symmetry breaking. We demonstrate concrete examples with
3
D higher-order nodal-loop semimetals,
4
D nodal-surface semimetals, and
4
D Möbius insulators, which can be realized using artificial crystals.

II
Projective symmetry algebra

A RCA
C^{p,q}
is generated by
(p+q)
generators
e_{\mu}
(
\mu=1,\cdots,p+q
) which satisfy

\{e_{\mu},e_{\nu}\}=\eta_{\mu\nu}1,

(1)

where
\eta_{\mu\nu}
is the diagonal matrix with first
p
diagonal elements being
-1
and the other being
1

Below, we show that this algebra is naturally realized by the symmetries of a rectangular lattice in a projective way.

Consider a rectangular lattice in
d

L_{a}
and mirror reflections
M_{a}
with
a=1,2,\cdots,d
. Here,
M_{a}
reverses the
a

x_{a}
by a lattice constant

L_{a}
and
M_{a}
. The inversion symmetry
P
is also generated as
P=\prod_{a}M_{a}
.

To analyze the constraint of the projective symmetry algebra on band structures, we need to represent it in momentum space.
To this end, we first have to specify the BZ by choosing an Abelian subalgebra of the projective algebra (

Here, we choose the one generated by

Hereafter, we use the calligraphy font to denote representation in momentum space. Then,

Preferably, we introduce

Consider a generic

in the BZ. The little algebra, namely the symmetries leaving

VI
Discussion

We have unveiled that all RCA equivalence classes can be elegantly realized in a very simple lattice setup as projective symmetry algebras. The realization exhibits the eightfold Bott periodicity with spatial dimension of the lattice. The IRREPs of RCAs directly determine the degeneracies in the band structures of the lattice. The findings are not only of fundamental interest, but also offers a new route to achieve unprecedented topological phases. For instance, the higher-order nodal-loop semimetal state was initially proposed using a Dirac model
Wang et al. 2020b
, which is difficult to find an experimental realization. Here, via RCAs and dimerization strategy, we achieve this state in a simple rectangular lattice, which can be readily
realized, e.g., in acoustic crystals
Li et al. 2021
;
Xue et al. 2021
. The construction here also provide a general approach to topological phases in higher dimensions, as exemplified by our
4

Derivation of projective algebras

In this appendix, we derive the projective algebras of
\mathsf{L}_{a}
and
\mathsf{M}_{a}
in the main text. On a lattice with
\pi
fluxes, a spatial symmetry
S
may preserve the flux configuration, but may not preserve the gauge connection configuration, namely the configuration of hopping phases. Then, after the transformation of
S
, a gauge transformation
\mathsf{G}_{S}
should be applied in order to restore the original gauge connection configuration. Thus, the
proper
symmetry operator is a combination
Zhao et al. 2020
:

\mathsf{S}=\mathsf{G}_{S}S.

(A1)

Because of the accompanied gauge transformations
\mathsf{G}_{S}
, the original group algebra of spatial symmetries will be modified to be a projective group algebra.

To understand the projective algebra
\{\mathsf{M}_{a},\mathsf{M}_{b}\}=2\delta_{ab}
in the main text, it suffices to consider a single rectangular plaquette with flux
\pi
. As illustrated in Fig.
S1
(c), the spatial mirror operators are given by

M_{x}=\sigma_{1}\otimes\tau_{0},\quad M_{y}=\sigma_{0}\otimes\tau_{1}.

(A2)

After the mirror reflections, the gauge connection configuration is generally changed. Particularly, for the gauge choice in Fig.
S1
(c), the required gauge transformations are given by

\mathsf{G}_{M_{x}}=\sigma_{0}\otimes\tau_{3},\quad\mathsf{G}_{M_{y}}=\sigma_{0}\otimes\tau_{0}.

(A3)

Here,
\mathsf{G}_{M_{y}}
is the identity operator because the gauge choice preserves
M_{y}
.
Then, the proper mirror operators are given by

\mathsf{M}_{x}=\mathsf{G}_{M_{x}}M_{x}=\sigma_{1}\otimes\tau_{3},\quad\mathsf{M}_{y}=M_{y}=\sigma_{0}\otimes\tau_{1}.

(A4)

It is now evident that

\{\mathsf{M}_{x},\mathsf{M}_{y}\}=0,\quad\mathsf{M}_{x}^{2}=\mathsf{M}_{y}^{2}=1.

(A5)

We then repeat the plaquette infinitely along the
x
and
y
directions to form a
2
D rectangular lattice. Manifestly, the gauge connection configuration is invariant under
L_{y}
. Hence,
\mathsf{L}_{y}=L_{y}
. However,
L_{x}
changes the gauge connection configuration, and therefore,
L_{x}
should be accompanied with a gauge transformation
\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}}
, as illustrated in Fig.
S1
(d). It is observed that
\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}}
is odd under
L_{y}
, i.e.,

L_{y}\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}}L_{y}^{-1}=-\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}}.

(A6)

Thus, for

\mathsf{L}_{x}=\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}}L_{x},\quad\mathsf{L}_{y}=L_{y},

(A7)

we have

\{\mathsf{L}_{x},\mathsf{L}_{y}\}=0.

(A8)

On the rectangular lattice, it is obvious that

\mathsf{G}_{M_{x}}=\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}},

(A9)

and therefore,

\mathsf{M}_{x}=\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}}M_{x},\quad\mathsf{M}_{y}=M_{y}.

(A10)

Moreover,
L_{x}
and
M_{x}
both preserve
\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}}
, i.e.,

L_{x}\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}}L_{x}^{-1}=\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}},\quad M_{x}\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}}M_{x}=\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}},

(A11)

But
M_{y}
inverses
\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}}
, i.e.,

M_{y}\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}}M_{y}=-\mathsf{G}_{L_{x}}.

(A12)

Thus, we conclude that

\mathsf{M}_{x}\mathsf{L}_{x}\mathsf{M}_{x}=\mathsf{L}_{x}^{-1},\quad\{\mathsf{M}_{x},\mathsf{L}_{y}\}=0

(A13)

\mathsf{M}_{y}\mathsf{L}_{y}\mathsf{M}_{y}=\mathsf{L}_{y}^{-1},\quad\{\mathsf{M}_{y},\mathsf{L}_{x}\}=0.

(A14)

Lattice models

As mentioned above, due to the AB effect, any two different translation operators anti-commute
with each other in the
d
D system. Typically, we specify the lattice with

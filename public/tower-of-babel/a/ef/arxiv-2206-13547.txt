Precise Low-Temperature Expansions for the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev model
Erick Arguello Cruz and Grigory Tarnopolsky

Abstract
We solve numerically the large N N Dyson-Schwinger equations for the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model utilizing the Legendre polynomial decomposition and reaching 10 − 36 10^{-36} accuracy. Using this we compute the energy of the SYK model at low temperatures T ≪ J T\ll J and obtain its series expansion up to T 7.54 T^{7.54} . While it was suggested that the expansion contains terms T 3.77 T^{3.77} and T 5.68 T^{5.68} , we find that the first non-integer power of temperature is T 6.54 T^{6.54} , which comes from the two point function of the fermion bilinear operator O h 1 = χ ​ ∂ τ 3 χ O_{h_{1}}=\chi\partial_{\tau}^{3}\chi with scaling dimension h 1 ≈ 3.77 h_{1}\approx 3.77 . The coefficient in front of T 6.54 T^{6.54} term agrees well with the prediction of the conformal perturbation theory. We conclude that the conformal perturbation theory appears to work even though the SYK model is not strictly conformal. In memory of Yaroslav Pugai

The SYK model low temperature behavior displays various interesting properties. Particularly in the large

II
The SYK model

The Majorana SYK model is described by the following Hamiltonian with even integer
q

\displaystyle H=\frac{i^{q/2}}{q!}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{q}}J_{i_{1}\dots i_{q}}\chi_{i_{1}}\dots\chi_{i_{q}}\,,

(1)

where
N
Majorana fermions
\chi_{i}
with
i=1,\dots,N
interact via
random couplings
J_{i_{1}\dots i_{q}}
drawn from a Gaussian ensemble with zero mean

\overline{J_{i_{1}\dots i_{q}}}=0
and variance
\overline{J_{i_{1}\dots i_{q}}^{2}}=(q-1)!J^{2}/N^{q-1}
.
The free energy of the SYK model after disorder average is

\displaystyle-\beta F=\overline{\ln Z}=\partial_{n}\overline{Z^{n}}|_{n\to 0}\,,

(2)

where
\beta=1/T
is the inverse temperature.
In
Kitaev:2017awl
;
Gurau:2017xhf
it was explained that for the SYK model up to
1/N^{q-2}
order

\overline{Z^{n}}=(\overline{Z})^{n}
, therefore at leading
N
we
get
-\beta F=\ln\overline{Z}
. Taking disorder average of the partition function
and integrating out the Majorana fermions we obtain a functional integral over two bi-local fields
G
and
\Sigma
:

\displaystyle e^{-\beta F}=\int DG(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})D\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})e^{-I[G,\Sigma]}\,,

(3)

where
G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
has a meaning of imaginary time two-point function of the Majorana fermions

\displaystyle G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=-\frac{1}{N}\langle\textrm{T}\chi_{i}(\tau_{1})\chi_{i}(\tau_{2})\rangle

(4)

and the
(G,\Sigma)
action
I
is
Maldacena:2016hyu
;
Kitaev:2017awl

\displaystyle-\frac{I[G,\Sigma]}{N}=\log\textrm{Pf}(-\sigma-\Sigma)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\Big[\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})-\frac{J^{2}}{q}G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{q}\Big]\,,

(5)

where
\sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=\delta^{\prime}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})
.
In the large
N
limit the leading contribution to the thermodynamic free energy
F
comes from the saddle point configuration
(G_{*},\Sigma_{*})
of the functional
I
, so
\beta F_{*}=I[G_{*},\Sigma_{*}]
. This configuration is a solution of the Dyson-Schwinger (DS) equations

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau_{1}}G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau^{\prime}\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau^{\prime})G(\tau^{\prime},\tau_{2})=\delta(\tau_{12})\,,

\displaystyle\Sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=J^{2}G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{q-1}\,,

(6)

and it has translational symmetry
G_{*}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=G_{*}(\tau_{12})
.
It is well-know that the saddle point solution
for
\beta,\tau_{12}\gg 1/J
can be approximated by the power law form
Sachdev:1992fk
;
2000PhRvL..85..840G
;
Maldacena:2016hyu
;
Kitaev:2017awl

\displaystyle G_{c}(\tau)=-b^{\Delta}\Big(\frac{\pi}{\beta J|\sin\frac{\pi\tau}{\beta}|}\Big)^{2\Delta}\textrm{sgn}(\tau)\,,

(7)

where
\Delta=1/q
and

\displaystyle b=\frac{1}{2\pi}(1-2\Delta)\tan(\pi\Delta)\,.

(8)

This is a consequence of the emergent time reparametrization invariance of
the DS equations in the IR limit.

Since
\beta F_{*}
depends only on
\beta J
, the large
N
energy of the system per particle is
\beta E=J\partial_{J}(\beta F_{*}/N)
and thus we obtain for the energy

\displaystyle E=-\frac{J^{2}}{q}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau G_{*}(\tau)^{q}=-\frac{1}{q}\partial_{\tau}G_{*}(\tau)|_{\tau\to 0^{+}}\,.

(9)

This is a UV quantity and cannot be calculated using the conformal approximation (
7
).
At large
\beta J
, the energy admits a
1/\beta J
expansion, so
we have for the energy per coupling
J

\displaystyle\epsilon(\beta J)

\displaystyle=E/J=c_{0}+\frac{c_{2}}{(\beta J)^{2}}+\frac{c_{3}}{(\beta J)^{3}}+\dots\,,

(10)

which is a function of
\beta J
only.
For the first three terms of the energy it was found that
Maldacena:2016hyu
;
Cotler:2016fpe
;
Jevicki:2016bwu
;
Jevicki:2016ito
;
Kitaev:2017awl

\displaystyle\epsilon(\beta J)=\epsilon_{0}-\frac{\pi^{2}k^{\prime}(2)\alpha_{0}}{3qa(\beta J)^{2}}+\frac{2\pi^{2}k^{\prime}(2)\alpha_{0}^{2}}{3a(\beta J)^{3}}+\dots\,,

(11)

where
a=((q-1)b)^{-1}
and
k(h)
is given in (
13
) and for
q=4
the ground energy is
\epsilon_{0}\approx-0.0406
and
\alpha_{0}\approx 0.2648
, which are determined numerically. The next order terms in the energy expansion are unknown.
It was suggested in
Cotler:2016fpe
;
Kitaev:2017awl
that the next order term should have the form
c_{h_{1}}/(\beta J)^{h_{1}}
, where

(
\approx 3.77
for
q=4
)
is the scaling dimension of the bilinear operator

On the other hand, the conformal perturbation theory, and arguments based on the large
q
expansion in
Tikhanovskaya:2020elb
;
Tikhanovskaya:2020zcw
, imply that this term should be absent. The main goal of this paper is to clarify the low temperature energy expansion of the Majorana SYK model.
For this, we used a high-precision computation of the SYK model Green’s function

1/\beta J
expansion of the two-point function
and the free energy.

III
SYK Effective theory

The effective theory approach to the SYK model
provides a way to compute higher power temperature corrections (higher
1/\beta J
terms) of the SYK free energy
and the two point function using the conformal perturbation theory.
In this approach one assumes that the
(G,\Sigma)
action
I
can be viewed as a Conformal Field Theory (CFT) action, perturbed by an infinite series
of irrelevant bilinear operators
O_{h}(\tau)

\displaystyle I=I_{\textrm{CFT}}+\sum_{h}g_{h}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau O_{h}(\tau)\,,

(12)

with the scaling exponents
h
obtained from the solution of the equation
k(h)=1

with

\displaystyle k(h)=\frac{\Gamma(2\Delta-h)\Gamma(h+2\Delta-1)}{\Gamma(2\Delta-2)\Gamma(2\Delta+1)}\Big(1-\frac{\sin(\pi h)}{\sin(2\pi\Delta)}\Big)\,.

(13)

For any
\Delta
the lowest scaling exponent is
h_{0}=2
, but the higher scaling exponents depend on
q
and
for
q=4
the next three are

\displaystyle h_{1}\approx 3.7735,\quad h_{2}\approx 5.6795,\quad h_{3}\approx 7.6320\,.

(14)

The bilinear operators
O_{h_{k}}
schematically are

\displaystyle O_{h_{k}}(\tau)=\chi_{i}(\tau)\partial_{\tau}^{2k+1}\chi_{i}(\tau)\,.

(15)

Conformal SYK two-point function in (
7
) is obtained using the
I_{\textrm{CFT }}
action

\displaystyle G_{c}(\tau_{12})=-\frac{1}{Z}\int D\chi_{i}\;\frac{1}{N}\chi_{i}(\tau_{1})\chi_{i}(\tau_{2})e^{-I_{\textrm{CFT }}}\,.

(16)

The
1/\beta J
corrections to the two-point function
can be computed using conformal perturbation theory with the action in (
12
).
For the first order correction one uses the three-point function
Gross:2016kjj
;
Gross:2017hcz
;
Gross:2017vhb

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\langle\chi_{i}(\tau_{1})\chi_{i}(\tau_{2})O_{h}(\tau_{3})\rangle=

\displaystyle\;\;=\frac{c_{h}b^{\Delta}\textrm{sgn}(\tau_{12})}{|\frac{\beta J}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi\tau_{12}}{\beta}|^{2\Delta-h}|\frac{\beta J}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi\tau_{13}}{\beta}|^{h}|\frac{\beta J}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi\tau_{23}}{\beta}|^{h}}\,,

where the structure constants

;
Gross:2016kjj

\displaystyle c_{h}^{2}=\frac{a}{k^{\prime}(h)}\frac{(h-1/2)}{\pi\tan(\pi h/2)}\frac{\Gamma(h)^{2}}{\Gamma(2h)}\,.

The second order of the conformal perturbation theory requires the computation of the four-point function and so on

Finally this leads to the following expansion of the exact two-point function

\displaystyle G_{c}(\tau)\bigg(1-\sum_{h}\frac{\alpha_{h}}{(\beta J)^{h-1}}f_{h}(\tau)

\displaystyle-\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}\frac{a_{hh^{\prime}}\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}}{(\beta J)^{h+h^{\prime}-2}}f_{h,h^{\prime}}(\tau)-\dots\bigg)\,,

(19)

where the functions

\displaystyle f_{h}(\tau)=\frac{(2\pi)^{h-1}\Gamma(h)^{2}(A_{h}(e^{\frac{2\pi i\tau}{\beta}})+A_{h}(e^{-\frac{2\pi i\tau}{\beta}}))}{2\sin\frac{\pi h}{2}\Gamma(2h-1)}\,,

(20)

with

Parameters

\displaystyle g_{h}^{2}=\frac{J^{2}k^{\prime}(h)}{a}\frac{(h-1/2)}{\pi\tan(\pi h/2)}\frac{\Gamma(h)^{2}}{\Gamma(2h)}\alpha_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\langle O_{h_{0}}\dots O_{h_{0}}O_{h_{k}}O_{h_{m}}\rangle_{\beta}\propto\delta_{km}\,.

IV
Numerical solution of the Dyson-Schwinger equations

Conventional approach to numerical solution of the SYK Dyson-Schwinger equations (
6
) at finite
\beta J

uses iterations of the equations, starting with the free correlation function and using the Fourier series expansion
Maldacena:2016hyu

\displaystyle G(\tau)=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}G(i\omega_{n})e^{-i\omega_{n}\tau}\,,

(32)

where
\omega_{n}=2\pi(n+1/2)/\beta
are the Matsubara frequencies. In the Matsubara frequency space the first equation in (
6
) is diagonal

\displaystyle(i\omega_{n}-\Sigma(i\omega_{n}))G(i\omega_{n})=1\,.

(33)

Numerically, one keeps only Matsubara modes with
|n|<N_{M}
and because at large frequencies the Green’s function decays as
G(i\omega_{n})\sim 1/(i\omega_{n})
the error of the numerical solution scales as
O(N_{M}^{-1})
. To improve accuracy of this approach one uses the knowledge of the first
p
high-frequency expansion terms of the Green’s function
G(i\omega_{n})\approx\sum_{k=1}^{p}g_{k}/(i\omega_{n})^{k}
, where
g_{1}=1
. This helps to reduce the error to the order
O(N_{M}^{-p-1})

doi:10.1063/5.0003145
;
2002BlumerPhd
;
2007PhDT180C
;
PhysRevB.94.195119
. In the Appendix
B

we discuss this approach to the Majorana SYK model.

In this paper we use the Legendre spectral method
PhysRevB.84.075145
;
doi:10.1063/5.0003145
to solve numerically the Dyson-Schwinger equations (
6
) much more efficiently. Namely
we decompose the Green’s function and the self-energy in the Legendre polynomials
(there are other orthogonal polynomials which can be used
PhysRevB.98.075127
as well as methods
kaye2021discrete
)

\displaystyle G(\tau)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}G_{\ell}L_{\ell}(x(\tau))\,,\;\;\Sigma(\tau)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\Sigma_{\ell}L_{\ell}(x(\tau))\,,

(34)

where
x(\tau)=2\tau/\beta-1
. The Legendre coefficients
G_{\ell}
and
\Sigma_{\ell}
decay exponentially with
\ell

PhysRevB.84.075145

and therefore if we retain only
N_{L}
Legendre coefficients in the expansion (
34
) we expect numerical solution to have exponentially high accuracy of the order
O(e^{-N_{L}})
.

An inherent particle-hole symmetry of the Majorana fermions leads to the symmetry of Green’s function around
\tau=\beta/2
, so
G(\tau)=G(\beta-\tau)
. This implies that all the odd coefficients of the Legendre decomposition must be zero
G_{2k+1}=0
for
k=0,1,2,\dots
.

Using the Legendre polynomial expansion we can find the energy of the SYK model (
10
) as

\displaystyle\epsilon(\beta J)=\frac{1}{2\beta J}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k(2k+1)G_{2k}\,,

(35)

and also the two-point function at
\tau=\beta/2
point:

\displaystyle G(\beta/2)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{4^{k}}\frac{(2k)!}{(k!)^{2}}G_{2k}\,,

(36)

where we used that
L^{\prime}_{\ell}(-1)=(-1)^{\ell+1}\ell(\ell+1)/2
and

L_{2k+1}(0)=0
and

\displaystyle L_{2k}(0)=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{4^{k}}\frac{(2k)!}{(k!)^{2}}\,.

(37)

Details of the numerical solution with the Legendre polynomial expansion
are discussed in the Appendix
A
. In the next section we present our numerical results
and compare them with the theoretical predictions.

We are interested in
1/\beta J
series expansion of the energy
\epsilon(\beta J)
. This is an asymptotic series
and the accuracy of its approximation to
\epsilon(\beta J)
for large enough

is proportional to
(1/\beta J)^{p^{\prime}}
,
where

is the next omitted power in the series expansion up to the power

\displaystyle\epsilon_{p}(\beta J)=c_{0}+c_{2}/(\beta J)^{2}+\dots+c_{p}/(\beta J)^{p}\,,

\displaystyle|\epsilon(\beta J)-\epsilon_{p}(\beta J)|\leq c_{p^{\prime}}/(\beta J)^{p^{\prime}}\,.

(43)

\beta J

c_{p^{\prime}}/(\beta J)^{p^{\prime}}\ll c_{p}/(\beta J)^{p}

\beta J

VI
Discussion

In this article we show numerically that the conformal perturbation theory appears to work for the SYK model even though the SYK model is not strictly
conformal. We found that there are no terms
(\beta J)^{-h_{1}+1}
and
(\beta J)^{-h_{2}+1}
in the

1/\beta J
expansion of the SYK free energy and the first non-integer power term is
p=2h_{1}-2
(
\approx 5.54
for
q=4
). It comes from
the two point function of the bilinear operator
O_{h_{1}}=\chi\partial_{\tau}^{3}\chi
.
It would be interesting to investigate the low temperature expansion of the complex SYK model
Sachdev:2015efa

with non-zero chemical potential
\mu
, where there is a local operator
O_{h}=c^{{\dagger}}\partial^{2}_{\tau}c
with the scaling dimension

h\approx 2.65
which depends slightly on
\mu

Klebanov:2016xxf
;
Gu:2019jub
. We expect that the first non-integer power in the cSYK model free energy should be
p=2h-2
(
\approx 3.3
for
q=4
) and is much lower than in the Majorana SYK
ArguelloCruzTarnopol
.
There are variety of other random and non-random quantum models, where the scaling dimensions of the bilinear operators
depend strongly on the models’ parameters
Kim:2019upg
;
Klebanov:2020kck
;
Milekhin:2021sqd
;
Milekhin:2021cou
;
Tikhanovskaya:2020elb
;
Tikhanovskaya:2020zcw
. In some of these models the scaling dimensions of the lowest operators can be less than
3/2
(and for some parameters can become even
relevant
h<1
, thus breaking the conformal invariance completely). For these type of models the validity
of the conformal perturbation theory approach is of primary importance, since it defines the leading
behavior of the free energy.

Appendix A
Legendre spectral method

In this appendix, following
doi:10.1063/5.0003145
, we briefly describe the Legendre spectral method for solving the Dyson-Schwinger equations.
We would like to solve numerically the Dyson-Schwinger equation

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}G(\tau)+\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau^{\prime}\Sigma(\tau-\tau^{\prime})G(\tau^{\prime})=-\delta(\tau)\,,

(52)

where
\Sigma(\tau)=J^{2}G^{q-1}(\tau)
.
We expand Green’s function
G(\tau)
in the Legendre series

\displaystyle G(\tau)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}G_{\ell}L_{\ell}(x(\tau))\,,

(53)

where
x=2\tau/\beta-1
.
Since the Legendre polynomials
L_{\ell}(x(\tau))
are defined only on the interval
\tau\in[0,\beta]
(
x\in[-1,1]
)
the delta function in (
52
) can be omitted, but its effect must be reinstated in the boundary
condition for the Green’s function
1
1

1

The delta function implies the boundary condition
G(0^{+})-G(0^{-})=-1
. Using the KMS condition
G(-\tau)=-G(\beta-\tau)
we arrive to the boundary condition on the interval
\tau\in[0,\beta]
.

G(0^{+})+G(\beta^{-})=-1
. In terms of the Legendre coefficients this boundary
condition reads

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}((-1)^{\ell}+1)G_{\ell}=-1\,,

(54)

where we used that
L_{\ell}(-x)=(-1)^{\ell}L_{\ell}(x)
and
L_{\ell}(1)=1
.
For the derivative of the Legendre polynomial we find

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}L_{\ell}(x(\tau))=\frac{2}{\beta}\partial_{x}L_{\ell}(x)=\frac{2}{\beta}\sum_{k}D_{k\ell}L_{k}(x)\,,

(55)

where
D_{k\ell}=(2k+1)
if
k+\ell
is odd and
k<\ell
, otherwise
D_{k\ell}=0
. The derivative matrix
D_{k\ell}
for the first several values of
k
and
\ell
starting from
0
is

\displaystyle D_{k\ell}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&1&0&1&0&\ldots\\
0&0&3&0&3&\ldots\\
0&0&0&5&0&\ldots\\
0&0&0&0&7&\ldots\\
0&0&0&0&0&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{array}\right)\,.

So we obtain that

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}G(\tau)=\frac{2}{\beta}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big[\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}D_{k\ell}G_{\ell}\Big]L_{k}(x(\tau))\,.

(62)

Next we write the convolution as the Legendre expansion

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau^{\prime}\Sigma(\tau-\tau^{\prime})G(\tau^{\prime})=\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{\infty}[\Sigma*]_{k\ell}G_{\ell}L_{k}(x(\tau))\,.

(63)

Therefore the Dyson-Schwinger equation (
52
) reads

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\Big(D_{k\ell}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}[\Sigma*]_{k\ell}\Big)G_{\ell}=0

(64)

and it has to be supplemented with the boundary condition (
54
). We notice that since
G(\tau)=G(\tau/\beta,\beta J)
the Legendre coefficients
G_{\ell}
depend only on combination
\beta J
and this is also evident from the equation (
64
).

To find an expression for the convolution matrix
[\Sigma*]_{k\ell}
we write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau^{\prime}\Sigma(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}G_{\ell}L_{\ell}(x^{\prime}(\tau^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{\infty}[\Sigma*]_{k\ell}G_{\ell}L_{k}(x(\tau))\,,

(65)

therefore

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}dx^{\prime}\Sigma(\tau(x)-\tau^{\prime}(x^{\prime}))L_{\ell}(x^{\prime})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}[\Sigma*]_{k\ell}L_{k}(x)\,.

(66)

Now multiplying both sides by the Legendre polynomial and integrating over
x
we obtain

\displaystyle[\Sigma*]_{k\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{2k+1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}dx^{\prime}dx\Sigma(\tau(x)-\tau^{\prime}(x^{\prime}))L_{k}(x)L_{\ell}(x^{\prime})\,,

(67)

where we used orthogonality of the Legendre polynomials

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}dxL_{k}(x)L_{\ell}(x)=\frac{2}{2k+1}\delta_{k\ell}\,.

(68)

It is possible to express
[\Sigma*]_{k\ell}
through the Legendre coefficients
\Sigma_{\ell}
of
\Sigma(\tau)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\Sigma_{\ell}L_{\ell}(\tau(x))
.
The matrix elements of
[\Sigma*]_{k\ell}
can be found recursively
doi:10.1137/140955835

\displaystyle[\Sigma*]_{k,\ell+1}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\ell+1}{2k+3}[\Sigma*]_{k+1,\ell}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\ell+1}{2k-1}[\Sigma*]_{k-1,\ell}+[\Sigma*]_{k,\ell-1}\,.

(69)

The first two columns of the matrix
[\Sigma*]_{k,\ell}
are computed using the relations

\displaystyle[\Sigma*]_{k,0}=2\Big(\frac{\Sigma_{k-1}}{2k-1}-\frac{\Sigma_{k+1}}{2k+3}\Big),\quad k\geq 1

\displaystyle[\Sigma*]_{k,1}=\frac{[\Sigma*]_{k-1,0}}{2k-1}-\frac{[\Sigma*]_{k+1,0}}{2k+3},\quad k\geq 1

(70)

with
[\Sigma*]_{0,0}=-2\Sigma_{1}/3
and
[\Sigma*]_{0,1}=-[\Sigma*]_{1,0}/3
. The recursion relation (
69
) works only for the lower triangular part
of the matrix
[\Sigma*]_{k,\ell}
, i.e. for
k\geq\ell
. To compute elements of the upper triangular part one has to use the transpose relation

\displaystyle[\Sigma*]_{k,\ell}=(-1)^{\ell+k}\frac{2k+1}{2\ell+1}[\Sigma*]_{\ell,k}\,.

(71)

In practice we retain only
N_{L}+1
Legendre coefficients
G_{\ell}
and
\Sigma_{\ell}
with
\ell=0,1,\dots,N_{L}
and to find
G_{\ell}
from given
\Sigma_{\ell}
we
solve the
(N_{L}+1)\times(N_{L}+1)
linear matrix equation composed of
k=0,1,\dots,N_{L}-1
equations (
64
) and
k=N_{L}
equation (
54
).

In order to perform fast transform from the Legendre coefficients
G_{\ell}
to the imaginary time
Green’s function
G(\tau)
we discretize the imaginary time
\tau=\beta(1+x)/2
using the
Legendre-Gauss-Lobatto (LGL) points
x_{i}
with
i=0,1,2,\dots,N
, where
x_{i}
are solutions of the equation

\displaystyle(1-x^{2})L^{\prime}_{N}(x)=0

(72)

and they fill the interval
x\in[-1,1]
with boundary values
x_{0}=-1
and
x_{N}=1
. The main statement for
this particular choice of points
x_{i}
is that the following equation holds for any polynomial
p(x)
of degree less than
2N
:

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{+1}dxp(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\omega_{i}p(x_{i})\,,

(73)

where
\omega_{i}=\frac{2}{N(N+1)}\frac{1}{[L_{N}(x_{i})]^{2}}
are called weights
JieShenSM
. We assume that we expand
G(\tau)
in Legendre series up to the
N_{L}
th Legendre polynomial

\displaystyle G(\tau_{i})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N_{L}}G_{\ell}L_{\ell}(x_{i})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N_{L}}L_{i\ell}G_{\ell}\,,

(74)

with
x_{i}=2\tau_{i}/\beta-1
, therefore the transformation from the Legendre coefficients
G_{\ell}
to the discretized values of the imaginary
time Green’s function
G(\tau_{i})
is a multiplication by the matrix
L_{i\ell}=L_{\ell}(x_{i})
. To transform from
G(\tau)
to
G_{\ell}
we need to compute the integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau G(\tau)L_{\ell}(x(\tau))=\frac{\beta}{2\ell+1}G_{\ell}\,.

(75)

On the other hand if
\ell+N_{L}<2N
we use (
73
) to get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau G(\tau)L_{\ell}(x(\tau))=\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\omega_{i}G(\tau_{i})L_{\ell}(x_{i})\,,

(76)

and therefore we find

\displaystyle G_{\ell}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}S_{\ell i}G(\tau_{i}),\quad S_{\ell i}=\frac{2\ell+1}{2}\omega_{i}L_{\ell}(x_{i})\,.

(77)

In our numerical computations we take
N=N_{L}
. We solve the Dyson-Schwinger equation (
52
)
using iterations with the weighted update
Maldacena:2016hyu
:

\displaystyle G^{(j+1)}_{\ell}=(1-u)G^{(j)}_{\ell}

\displaystyle+u\cdot\textrm{Solution of}\,[\textrm{eq.}(\ref{LegDS})+\textrm{eq.}(\ref{bcond})\;\textrm{with}\;[\Sigma*]_{k\ell}^{(j)}]\,,

(78)

where
u
is the weighting parameter and
[\Sigma*]_{k\ell}^{(j)}
is constructed from
\Sigma_{\ell}^{(j)}=J^{2}S_{\ell i}G^{(j)}(\tau_{i})^{q-1}
. We start with the bare solution
G_{\ell}^{(0)}=-\delta_{\ell,0}/2

and weight
u=1/2
. We monitor the difference
u^{-1}\sum_{i}\Delta\tau_{i}|G^{(j+1)}(\tau_{i})-G^{(j)}(\tau_{i})|^{2}
between successive iterations
and divide
u
by a half if this difference increases. The iteration cycle is depicted in Fig.
4
.

uses the equation

and the lower horizontal arrow
uses the equation (

).

We performed all computations in Wolfram Mathematica and used small computer cluster
to perform computations for different values of
\beta J
in parallel.

Appendix B
Matsubara spectral method

In this appendix we discuss improved numerical method for solving the Dyson-Schwinger equations (
6
) using the standard Matsubara frequency approach.
Since in the Majorana SYK model
G(\beta-\tau)=G(\tau)
Green’s function in the
frequency space is anti-symmetric
G(-i\omega_{n})=-G(i\omega_{n})
and therefore
the high frequency expansions of it and of the self-energy have only odd powers of frequency
with real coefficients

\displaystyle G(i\omega_{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{g_{2k-1}}{(i\omega_{n})^{2k-1}},\;\Sigma(i\omega_{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{s_{2k-1}}{(i\omega_{n})^{2k-1}}

(79)

and
g_{1}=1
. Expanding the r.h.s. of the DS equation

\displaystyle G(i\omega_{n})=(i\omega_{n}-\Sigma(i\omega_{n}))^{-1}

(80)

for large
i\omega_{n}
we find relations
between the coefficients
g_{2k-1}
and

\displaystyle g_{3}=s_{1},\;\;g_{5}=s_{1}^{2}+s_{3},\;\;g_{7}=s_{1}^{3}+2s_{1}s_{3}+s_{5},\;\dots

Denoting an inverse Fourier transform of a given frequency power as

\displaystyle Q_{p}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-i\omega_{n}\tau}}{(i\omega_{n})^{p}}=\mathop{\textrm{res}}_{z=0}\Big(\frac{-e^{z(\beta/2-\tau)}}{2z^{p}\cosh(\frac{\beta z}{2})}\Big),

where the first three functions are

\displaystyle s_{1}=J^{2}/2^{q-2}\,,

and thus

(84)

where we used eqs. (

\displaystyle\Sigma(\tau)=-J^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{2^{q-1}}+\frac{q(q-1)}{2^{q-2}}\epsilon J\tau

\displaystyle\;\;+\Big(\frac{q-1}{2^{2q-2}}+\frac{q^{2}(q-1)(q-2)}{2^{q-2}}\epsilon^{2}\Big)(J\tau)^{2}+\dots\bigg)\,.

(85)

Since we can write

\displaystyle\Sigma(\tau)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{2k-1}Q_{2k-1}(\tau)\,,

and only

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0:2}^{\infty}(\ell-1)\ell(\ell+1)(\ell+2)G_{\ell}=-\frac{(\beta J)^{2}}{2^{q-2}}\,,

Appendix C
Computation of the free energy using Kitaev-Suh resonance theory

We can rewrite the SYK
(G,\Sigma)

\displaystyle-\frac{I}{N}=\log\textrm{Pf}(-\hat{\Sigma})-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\Big[\hat{\Sigma}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{J^{2}}{q}G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{q}\Big]+\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\,,

where we made a replacement

(G_{c}(\tau),\Sigma_{c}(\tau))

The last term in (

The leading correction to the free energy is obtained from the first order perturbation theory

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta\delta F}{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\sigma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\langle G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\rangle

We can not use direct expression for the source function
\sigma(\tau)=\delta^{\prime}(\tau)
in this formula, as it

that the source function
\sigma

15

\tau/\beta

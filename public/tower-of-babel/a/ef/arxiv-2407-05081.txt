Isomer production studied with simultaneous decay curve analysis for alpha-particle induced reactions on natural platinum up to 29 MeV
Naohiko Otuka, Sándor Takács, Masayuki Aikawa, Shuichiro Ebata, Hiromitsu Haba

Abstract
The isomeric ratios of 198 Au, 197 Hg and 195 Hg produced by α \displaystyle\alpha -particle induced reactions on natural platinum were investigated experimentally up to 29 MeV by using the standard stacked foil activation technique and γ \displaystyle\gamma -ray spectrometry. The isomeric ratios of 197 Hg and 195 Hg determined by the conventional activation cross section formula showed strong cooling time dependence. The time dependence was resolved by adjusting the isomeric transition branching ratios for the two isotopes within a simultaneous decay curve analysis framework. Our analysis suggests 94.5 ± \displaystyle\pm 0.7% and 48.9 ± \displaystyle\pm 1.8% as the isomeric transition branching ratios of 197m Hg (24 h) and 195m Hg (42 h), respectively. The isomeric ratios and independent production cross sections of 198 Au, 197 Hg, 195 Hg and some other Hg, Au and Pt isotopes were also measured down to 6 MeV with these corrected isomeric transition branching ratios, and compared with predictions of statistical and pre-equilibrium models by TALYS-2.0 to discuss spin cutoff parameter dependence. We found the measured isomeric ratios are better predicted if we reduce the spin cutoff parameter to half or less from that estimated with the rigid body moment of inertia.

If we denote a set of the emission rates of various
\displaystyle\gamma
lines and a set of the production cross sections of various nuclides by column vectors
\displaystyle\bm{A}=\{A_{i}\}
and
\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}=\{\sigma_{i}\}
,
respectively,
the relationship between the two vectors at a given incident energy may be modelled by the observation equation

\bm{A}=G\bm{\sigma}+\bm{e}

(15)

with the design matrix
\displaystyle G
and a vector
\displaystyle\bm{e}
expressing the residual (fitting deviation).
As Table

\displaystyle\gamma

the vectors
\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}
and
\displaystyle\bm{A}
have 16 and 27 elements,
respectively,
and
\displaystyle G
is a 16
\displaystyle\times

The least-squares solution of the observation equation gives the production cross sections and their covariance
\displaystyle M
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(G^{T}V^{-1}G)^{-1}G^{T}V^{-1}\bm{A}

(16)

\displaystyle\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(G^{T}V^{-1}G)^{-1},

(17)

where
\displaystyle V
is a diagonal matrix with the variances of the measured
\displaystyle\gamma
emission rates as the diagonal elements,
which originates from the uncertainty in the peak area in the present work.

In the present work, we included the IT branching ratios of
197m
Hg and
195m
Hg as additional parameters.
Since the IT branching ratio and metastable state production cross section appear as their product in the observation equation,
adjustment of the IT branching ratios and production cross sections together based on the observation equation is no longer a linear least-squares problem.
In the present work, we adopted the Marquardt-Levenberg algorithm
[
44
,
45
]
implemented in the computer program
gnuplot

[
46
]
to solve the equation including the IT branching ratios as fitting parameters.
The branching ratios were determined by fitting for the first Pt foil irradiated at 29 MeV because many data points are available for this foil thanks to the repeated measurements, and the branching ratios adjusted at 29 MeV were treated as constants in the subsequent analysis of the Pt foils bombarded at lower energies.

Among the nuclides in Table
1
,
we excluded
197

(1) the
197m
Pt 346.5 keV peak area is seen only in one measurement at each of three Pt foils, and
(2) the
197g

Similarly, we set the production cross section to zero for a Pt foil if
(1) the most intense
\displaystyle\gamma
line of the nuclide is absent in any spectrum, or
(2) fitting procedure makes the production cross section negative.

Our preliminary decay curve analysis of the 98 keV
\displaystyle\gamma
emission rate at 29 MeV including
195m

195g

\displaystyle\gamma
emission rate to the production cross sections of these nuclides (i.e., initial guesses became adjusted values but with huge uncertainties), and we excluded them from the fitting procedure for all Pt foils as well.
Note that none of the other intense
\displaystyle\gamma

195m

195g

Additionally, we excluded the

\displaystyle\gamma

Summary

The independent production cross sections and their isomeric ratios for the radionuclides produced by irradiation of natural platinum by
\displaystyle\alpha
-particles up to 29 MeV were measured.
To resolve the observed cooling time dependence of the isomeric ratios of
197
Hg and
195
Hg determined with the conventional activation cross section formula and the decay data in the ENSDF library,
we performed simultaneous decay curve analysis at 29 MeV with the isomeric transition branching ratios of
197
Hg and
195
Hg as well as independent production cross sections as adjustable parameters.
The observed cooling time dependence was resolved by applying the isomeric transition branching ratios adjusted in the decay curve analysis.
We found that omission of the factor
\displaystyle p\lambda_{m}/(\lambda_{m}-\lambda_{g})
in determination of
\displaystyle\sigma_{g}
from the ground state activity may introduce more than 60% overestimation for
198
Au production around 30 MeV.

We also derived the independent production cross sections of various nuclides with the newly determined
197,195
Hg isomeric transition branching ratios at lower incident energies by using the simultaneous decay curve analysis.
The energy dependent
197
Hg isomeric ratio measured by us shows an excellent agreement with those measured by Sudár et al. and Vandenbosch et al.
Overall,
our cross sections are consistent with those measured by Hermanne et al.,
while the cross sections measured by Sagaidak et al. are always systematically higher by a factor of
\displaystyle\sim
3 than the cross sections measured by us though their energy dependence is often similar to ours.

The generalized activation cross section formula Eq. (
40
) shows that one can determine the independent production cross section by subtraction of the precursor decay contribution following the decay chain.
However,
the subtraction procedure adds an uncertainty,
and it is not practical to repeat the subtraction procedure for several generations on the decay chain.
In contrast,
the simultaneous decay curve analysis allows us to determine independent production cross sections of various nuclides by adjustment to the emission rates of all measured
\displaystyle\gamma
lines at once as demonstrated in the present work.
Unlike determination of a cross section with the activation cross section formula,
we can include in simultaneous decay curve analysis a peak area related with several
\displaystyle\gamma
-lines that are unresolved to each other,
and utilize more information from each spectrum.
It is also very easy to repeat analysis by adding or deleting a product nuclide and/or new decay path in the decay chain model.

We studied the spin cutoff parameter dependence of the
198
Au,
197
Hg and
195
Hg isomeric ratios,
and found that model prediction is improved if the spin cutoff parameter is reduced to the half or less from the one estimated with the rigid body moment of inertia.
This result is consistent with analysis of the mass dependence of
\displaystyle\eta

[
59
]
, our global adjustment of
\displaystyle\eta
performed to the experimental isomeric ratios
[
1
]
and another recent global analysis
[
60
]
.

We also briefly discussed a question on the quantity specification in the EXFOR library.
The information in an experimental report is often insufficient for EXFOR compilers to distinguish
\displaystyle\sigma_{g}^{\mathrm{cum}}
and
\displaystyle\sigma_{g}+p\sigma_{m}
,
and the EXFOR coding rule also does not distinguish them.
The present work is motivated by possible improvement of the current way of compilation,
and we plan to investigate this question further.

The initial
\displaystyle\alpha
-particle beam energy used in the present work is not high enough to study the peak regions of the excitation functions,
and we plan an additional experiment covering higher energies.

Appendix A
Simultaneous decay curve analysis and generalized activation cross section formula

The formulae required for the simultaneous decay curve analysis and the generalized activation cross section formula are summarized in this appendix.

A.1
Number of atoms after cooling

If each nuclide on a decay chain 1
\displaystyle\to
2
\displaystyle\to\cdots\to i\to\cdots
has a single decay branch and there is no production process other than decays,
the number of atoms of each nuclide follows

\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\lambda_{i}N_{i}&\mathrm{if\quad}i=1\\[14.22636pt]
\lambda_{i-1}N_{i-1}-\lambda_{i}N_{i}&\mathrm{otherwise}\end{array}\right.,

(18)

where
\displaystyle N_{i}=N_{i}(t)
denotes the number of atoms of the nuclide
\displaystyle i
after cooling time
\displaystyle t
.
Let us assume that only the nuclide
1
presents at
\displaystyle t=0
, namely
\displaystyle N_{1}(0)=N_{1}^{0}
and
\displaystyle N_{i}(0)=0
for
\displaystyle i\geq 2
.
The solution of this differential equation system given by Bateman
[
61
]
is

N_{i}(t)=N_{1}^{0}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}e^{-\lambda_{k}t}

(19)

with

\Lambda_{k}^{j,i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mathrm{if\quad}i=j\\[14.22636pt]
\prod_{l=j}^{i-1}\lambda_{l}\left/\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=j\\
(l\neq k)\end{subarray}}^{i}(\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{k})\right.&\mathrm{otherwise}\\
\end{array}\right..

(20)

Examples of the explicit form of
\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}^{j,i}
are

\begin{array}[]{lll}\Lambda_{1}^{1,2}=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}},&\Lambda_{2}^{1,2}=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}},\\
\Lambda_{2}^{2,3}=\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2}},&\Lambda_{3}^{2,3}=\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}},\\
\Lambda_{1}^{1,3}=\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{1})},&\Lambda_{2}^{1,3}=\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2})},&\Lambda_{3}^{1,3}=\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3})(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3})}.\\
\end{array}

(21)

A.2
Number of atoms at end of bombardment

Let us extend Eq. (
18
) by adding a production term for the nuclide
1
(production cross section
\displaystyle\sigma_{1}
) to

\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}R_{1}-\lambda_{i}N_{i}&\mathrm{if\quad}i=1\\[14.22636pt]
\lambda_{i-1}N_{i-1}-\lambda_{i}N_{i}&\mathrm{otherwise}\end{array}\right.,

(22)

where the reaction rate
\displaystyle R_{1}=\phi n\sigma_{1}
is introduced with the beam flux
\displaystyle\phi
and sample atom areal number density
\displaystyle n
.
The solution of this differential equation is

N_{i}(t)=R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}h_{k}(t)

(23)

with
\displaystyle h_{k}(t)=(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t})/\lambda_{k}
.
For example,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}N_{1}&=&R_{1}h_{1},\\[14.22636pt]
N_{2}&=&R_{1}\left[\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}h_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}h_{2}\right],\\[14.22636pt]
N_{3}&=&R_{1}\left[\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{1})}h_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2})}h_{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3})(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3})}h_{3}\right].\end{array}

(24)

Proof

The number of atoms of the nuclide
\displaystyle i
produced during short interval
\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\tau
is
\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\tau R_{i}
.
According to Eq. (
19
),
the number of the nuclide produced during
\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\tau
and survived after cooling for
\displaystyle\tau
is
[
62
]

\mathrm{d}N_{i}=\mathrm{d}\tau R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}e^{-\lambda_{k}\tau}.

(25)

If the bombardment continues for
\displaystyle t
,

N_{i}=R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,e^{-\lambda_{k}\tau}=R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}\frac{1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t}}{\lambda_{k}}.

(26)

A.3
Number of atoms after bombardment and cooling

The number of atoms of the nuclide
\displaystyle i
produced by bombardment for
\displaystyle t_{b}
at the reaction rate
\displaystyle R_{1}=\phi n\sigma_{1}
followed by cooling for
\displaystyle t
is

N_{i}(t)=R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}g_{k}(t)

(27)

with
\displaystyle g_{k}(t)=(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t}/\lambda_{k}
.
For example,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}N_{1}&=&R_{1}\,g_{1},\\[14.22636pt]
N_{2}&=&R_{1}\left[\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}g_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}g_{2}\right],\\[14.22636pt]
N_{3}&=&R_{1}\left[\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{1})}g_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2})}g_{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3})(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3})}g_{3}\right].\\
\end{array}

(28)

Proof

Considering the relation
\displaystyle g_{k}(0)=h_{k}(t_{b})
,
it is obvious that Eq. (
27
) satisfies the initial condition given by Eq. (
23
) at
\displaystyle t=0
.
Furthermore,
we can demonstrate Eq. (
27
) and

N_{i+1}(t)=R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i+1}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i+1}g_{k}(t)

(29)

satisfy

\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{i+1}}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\lambda_{i+1}N_{i+1}+\lambda_{i}N_{i}

(30)

as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{i+1}}{\mathrm{d}t}+\lambda_{i+1}N_{i+1}-\lambda_{i}N_{i}

(31)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i+1}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i+1}\frac{1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}}}{\lambda_{k}}(-\lambda_{k})e^{-\lambda_{k}t}+\lambda_{i+1}R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i+1}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i+1}\frac{1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}}}{\lambda_{k}}e^{-\lambda_{k}t}-\lambda_{i}R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}\frac{1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}}}{\lambda_{k}}e^{-\lambda_{k}t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i+1}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i+1}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i+1}}{\lambda_{k}}-1\right)(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t}-R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{k}}(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i+1}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i+1}}{\lambda_{k}}-1\right)(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t}-R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{k}}(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t}=0,

where the relation
\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}^{1,i+1}=\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}\lambda_{i}/(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle(k\leq i)
is used.

A.4
Number of decays during measurement after bombardment and cooling

The number of decays of the nuclide
\displaystyle i
during time interval
\displaystyle t
after bombardment for
\displaystyle t_{b}
at the reaction rate
\displaystyle R_{1}
and after cooling for
\displaystyle t_{c}
is

C_{i}(t)=\lambda_{i}R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}\frac{f_{k}(t)}{\lambda_{k}},

(32)

where

\displaystyle f_{k}(t)=(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{c}}(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t})/\lambda_{k}
.
For example,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}C_{1}&=&R_{1}\,f_{1},\\[14.22636pt]
C_{2}&=&R_{1}\left[\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}f_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}f_{2}\right],\\[14.22636pt]
C_{3}&=&R_{1}\left[\frac{\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{1})}f_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2})}f_{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3})(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3})}f_{3}\right].\end{array}

(33)

Proof

According to Eq. (
27
),
the decay rate of the nuclide
\displaystyle i
after bombardment for
\displaystyle t_{b}
and cooling for
\displaystyle t_{c}+\tau
is

-\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\lambda_{i}N_{i}=\lambda_{i}R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}\frac{(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}})e^{-\lambda_{k}(t_{c}+\tau)}}{\lambda_{k}}.

(34)

The number of atoms of the nuclide
\displaystyle i
decaying during the short interval
\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\tau
after measurement for
\displaystyle t_{c}+\tau
is

\mathrm{d}C_{i}=\mathrm{d}\tau\lambda_{i}N_{i}=\mathrm{d}\tau\lambda_{i}R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}\frac{(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}})e^{-\lambda_{k}(t_{c}+\tau)}}{\lambda_{k}}.

(35)

The number of the nuclide decays between
\displaystyle t_{c}
and
\displaystyle t_{c}+t
is

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{i}R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}\frac{1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}}}{\lambda_{k}}e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{c}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,e^{-\lambda_{k}\tau}=\lambda_{i}R_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{1,i}\frac{(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{b}})e^{-\lambda_{k}t_{c}}(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}t})}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}.

(36)

A.5

The equations developed so far can be easily generalized to a case where not only the nuclide
1
but also nuclides
\displaystyle j

\displaystyle(j=2,i)
are produced with reaction rate
\displaystyle R_{j}

and the number of decays of the nuclide
\displaystyle i
during measurement for
\displaystyle t

\displaystyle t_{b}

C_{i}(t)=\lambda_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}R_{j}p_{ji}\sum_{k=j}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{j,i}\frac{f_{k}(t)}{\lambda_{k}},

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle i

A.7
Generalized activation cross section formula

If the nuclides on the decay chain 1
\displaystyle\to
2
\displaystyle\to\cdots\to i
can be produced with production cross sections
\displaystyle\sigma_{j}
(
\displaystyle j=1,i
),
and each nuclide has only one decay precursor,

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}
is related with the number of decays
\displaystyle C_{j}
(
\displaystyle j=1,i
) by

\sigma_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{i}^{\mathrm{cum}}&\mathrm{if\quad}i=1\\
\sigma_{i}^{\mathrm{cum}}-\frac{\lambda_{i}}{f_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\sigma_{j}p_{ji}\sum_{k=j}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{j,i}\frac{f_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}&\mathrm{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

(40)

with

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}^{\mathrm{cum}}=C_{i}/(f_{i}\phi n)
and the decay branching ratio from the nuclide
\displaystyle j
to nuclide
\displaystyle i
denoted by
\displaystyle p_{ji}
.
For example,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sigma_{1}&=&\sigma_{1}^{\mathrm{cum}},\\[14.22636pt]
\sigma_{2}&=&\sigma_{2}^{\mathrm{cum}}-\sigma_{1}p_{12}\left[\frac{f_{1}}{f_{2}}\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\right],\\[14.22636pt]
\sigma_{3}&=&\sigma_{3}^{\mathrm{cum}}-\sigma_{2}p_{23}\left[\frac{f_{2}}{f_{3}}\frac{\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}}\right]\\[14.22636pt]
&-&\sigma_{1}p_{13}\left[\frac{f_{1}}{f_{3}}\frac{\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{1})}\right.+\frac{f_{2}}{f_{3}}\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2})}+\left.\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3})(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3})}\right].\end{array}

(41)

Proof

The formula for
\displaystyle i=1
is obvious from Eq. (
33
).
For
\displaystyle i\geq 2
,
Eq. (
38
) gives

C_{i}=\lambda_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}R_{j}p_{ji}\sum_{k=j}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{j,i}\frac{f_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}=R_{i}f_{i}+\lambda_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}R_{j}p_{ji}\sum_{k=j}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{j,i}\frac{f_{k}}{\lambda_{k}},

(42)

where
\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}^{i,i}=1
is used.
This is solved in terms of
\displaystyle R_{i}
to

R_{i}=\frac{C_{i}}{f_{i}}-\frac{\lambda_{i}}{f_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}R_{j}p_{ji}\sum_{k=j}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{j,i}\frac{f_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}=R_{i}^{\mathrm{cum}}-\frac{\lambda_{i}}{f_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}R_{j}p_{ji}\sum_{k=j}^{i}\Lambda_{k}^{j,i}\frac{f_{k}}{\lambda_{k}},

(43)

where
\displaystyle R_{i}^{\mathrm{cum}}=C_{i}/f_{i}
.
By dividing the both sides of this equation by
\displaystyle\phi n
,
we obtain Eq. (
40
) with
\displaystyle i\geq 2
.

Almost-Sure Stability of the Single Mode Solution of a Noisy Autoparametric Vibration Absorber
Peter H. Baxendale, N. Sri Namachchivaya

Abstract
For a pendulum suspended below a vibrating block with white noise forcing, the solution in which the pendulum remains vertical is called the single mode solution. When this solution becomes unstable there is energy transfer from the block to the pendulum, helping to absorb the vibrations of the block. We study the Lyapunov exponent λ \lambda governing the almost-sure stability of the process linearized along the single mode solution. The linearized equation is excited by a combination of white and colored noise processes, which makes the evaluation of λ \lambda non trivial. For white noise forcing of intensity ε ​ ν \varepsilon\nu we prove λ ⁡ ( ε ) = λ 0 + ε 2 ​ λ 2 + O ⁡ ( ε 4 ) \lambda(\varepsilon)=\lambda_{0}+\varepsilon^{2}\lambda_{2}+O(\varepsilon^{4}) as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 , where λ 0 \lambda_{0} and λ 2 \lambda_{2} are given explicitly in terms of the parameters of the system.



Keywords: autoparametric system, Lyapunov exponent, stability boundary, resonance.

Introduction

Autoparametric systems are vibrating systems that consist of two subsystems. The primary system is an oscillator that is directly excited by some external forcing, and the secondary system is coupled nonlinearly to the oscillator in such a way that it can be at rest while the oscillator is vibrating.

Figure 1:
Block and pendulum

In this paper we consider the two-degree-of-freedom system shown in Figure
1
. The primary system consists of a block of mass
m_{1}
attached by a linear spring of stiffness
k_{1}
and a viscous dashpot with a damping coefficient
c_{1}
. The block is excited directly by (downward) forcing
\Xi(t)
. The secondary system comprises a simple pendulum of length
\ell
attached to the block. The mass of the pendulum bob is
m_{2}
and the pendulum motion is damped by a viscous damper of coefficient
c_{2}
. This system appears in Hatwal, Mallik and Ghosh
[
12
]
. See also
[
4
,
6
]
and a similar model with a non-linear spring and a compound pendulum in
[
32
]
. A closely related system with a vertically mounted cantilever beam with a tip mass appears in Haxton and Barr
[
13
]
.

It is a characteristic property of an autoparametric system that there is a solution involving forced motion of the primary system while the secondary system remains at rest. Here it corresponds to the “single mode” solution in which the pendulum remains directly below the vibrating block at all times. As parameters in the system are varied, it may happen that the single mode solution becomes unstable and the pendulum begins to move. In this case the motion of the primary system causes excitation of the secondary
system, and there is energy transfer from the vibrating block to the pendulum. In effect,
the pendulum acts as a vibration absorber.
Our primary concern is the almost-sure stability analysis of the single mode solution when the forcing function
\Xi(t)
is a multiple of white noise.

In addition to the block and pendulum and block and beam examples mentioned above, autoparametric systems are used to model the dynamics of structural and mechanical systems such as the vibration of an initially deflected shallow arch
[
29
]
, in-plane and out-of-plane motions of suspended cables
[
8
]
, and the pitching and rolling motions of a ship
[
24
,
26
]
. A larger collection of examples is given in the monograph
[
30
]
.

Periodically excited autoparametric systems have been studied extensively; see for example
[
13
,
25
,
12
,
9
,
4
,
6
]
. These are deterministic systems, and the most interesting situations occur when the natural frequencies of the primary (excited mode) and the secondary (unexcited) systems are in 2:1 resonance. Issues of bifurcation theory arise, and numerical computations frequently show chaotic behavior.

Autoparametric systems with stochastic forcing have also been studied. Gaussian and non-Gaussian closure techniques were used in
[
15
,
27
,
14
,
10
,
20
]
to approximate the moments of solutions of the associated Fokker-Planck equation. Sufficient conditions for almost-sure stability of the single mode solution were obtained in
[
1
]
. Stochastic averaging techniques were used in
[
23
]
.

The equations of motion for the block and pendulum system shown in Figure

\begin{split}(m_{1}+m_{2})\ddot{y}(t)+c_{1}\dot{y}(t)+k_{1}y(t)-m_{1}\ell(\ddot{\theta}(t)\sin\theta(t)+{\dot{\theta}}^{2}(t)\cos\theta(t))&=\Xi(t)\\

\big(\eta(t),\dot{\eta}(t)\big)=\big(\bar{\eta}(t),\dot{\bar{\eta}}(t)\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\big(\theta(t),\dot{\theta}(t)\big)=\big(0,0\big),

\lambda:=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\|\left(\varphi(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\right)\|

The novelty of the linear SDE (

and

It is shown in Section

\lambda\leq-\zeta_{2}+\frac{\nu}{2\kappa_{d}}\sqrt{\frac{\chi^{2}+4\zeta_{1}^{2}}{4\zeta_{1}}}+\frac{\nu^{2}}{2\kappa_{d}^{2}}.

(1.7)

The main results in the paper are given in Section

We see that the second line in (

\displaystyle\ddot{\eta}(t)+2\zeta_{1}\dot{\eta}(t)+\chi^{2}\eta(t)

\displaystyle=\nu\dot{W}(t)

\displaystyle\ddot{\varphi}(t)+2\zeta_{2}\dot{\varphi}(t)+\big(\kappa^{2}+2\zeta_{1}\dot{\eta}(t)+\chi^{2}\eta(t)\big)\varphi(t)

\displaystyle=\nu\varphi(t)\dot{W}(t),

(2.7)

Remark 2.3
.

This paper concentrates on the well-posedness and evaluation of
\lambda
. The interpretation of

, and especially of the sign of

9.2
Adjoint expansion

Instead of evaluating the right side of (
9.3
) directly, we will consider the adjoint equation
{\cal A}^{\varepsilon}F^{\varepsilon}=Q^{\varepsilon}-\lambda(\varepsilon)
using the asymptotic expansion method originated by Arnold, Papanicolaou and Wihstutz
[
2
]
.
The integrand
Q^{\varepsilon}
in (
9.3
) can be written
Q^{\varepsilon}=-\zeta_{2}+\varepsilon Q_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}Q_{2}
where

Q_{1}(v,\psi)=\frac{1}{\kappa_{d}}\langle a,v\rangle\sin\psi\cos\psi\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad Q_{2}(v,\psi)=\frac{\|\gamma\|^{2}}{2\kappa_{d}^{2}}\cos^{2}\psi\cos 2\psi.

The diffusion process
\{v(t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}
given by (
3.2
) has generator
{\cal G}
given by

{\cal G}f(v)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(Av)_{j}\frac{\partial f}{\partial v_{j}}(v)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}(BB^{\ast})_{jk}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial v_{j}\partial v_{k}}.

(9.4)

For
j=1,\ldots,d
we have

dv_{j}(t)d\psi(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(Be_{\ell})_{j}\left(\frac{\varepsilon\gamma_{\ell}}{\kappa_{d}}\cos^{2}\psi(t)\right)=\frac{\varepsilon}{\kappa_{d}}\cos^{2}\psi(t)(B\gamma)_{j}.

(9.5)

Therefore the generator
{\cal A}^{\varepsilon}
of the diffusion
\{(v(t),\psi(t)):t\in\mathbb{R}\}
can be written
{\cal A}^{\varepsilon}={\cal A}_{0}+\varepsilon{\cal A}_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}{\cal A}_{2}
where

{\cal A}_{0}={\cal G}-\kappa_{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi}

and

{\cal A}_{1}=\frac{1}{\kappa_{d}}\cos^{2}\psi\left(\langle a,v\rangle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}(B\gamma)_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\psi\partial v_{j}}\right)

and

{\cal A}_{2}=\frac{\|\gamma\|^{2}}{\kappa_{d}^{2}}\left(-\sin\psi\cos^{3}\psi\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi}+\cos^{4}\psi\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\psi^{2}}\right).

Expanding the adjoint equation
{\cal A}^{\varepsilon}F^{\varepsilon}=Q^{\varepsilon}-\lambda(\varepsilon)

as

({\cal A}_{0}+\varepsilon{\cal A}_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}{\cal A}_{2})(\varepsilon F_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}F_{2}+\cdots)=(-\zeta_{2}+\varepsilon Q_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}Q_{2})-(\lambda_{0}+\varepsilon\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}\lambda_{2}+\cdots)

and equating powers of
\varepsilon
we get
\lambda_{0}=-\zeta_{2}
and

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{0}F_{1}

\displaystyle=Q_{1}-\lambda_{1}

(9.6)

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{0}F_{2}+{\cal A}_{1}F_{1}

\displaystyle=Q_{2}-\lambda_{2}

(9.7)

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{0}F_{3}+{\cal A}_{1}F_{2}+{\cal A}_{2}F_{1}

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{3},

(9.8)

and so on. In the next three sections we will solve these equations, finding explicit values for
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{3}
. We will find an explicit formula for
F_{1}
and characterizations of the functions
F_{2}
and
F_{3}
which are sufficiently precise so as to enable a rigorous asymptotic estimate to be made in Section
9.3
.

Remark 9.1
.

Solving the system recursively, all the equations will be of the form

{\cal A}_{0}F(v,\psi)=G(v,\psi)-\lambda

where
G
is given and
\lambda
and
F
are to be found. For an exponentially ergodic Markov process with generator
{\cal L}
, semigroup
\{P_{t}:t\geq 0\}
, and invariant probability
m
, then
P_{t}G\to\int Gdm

exponentially fast as
t\to\infty
. Then taking
\lambda=\int Gdm
and

F=-\int_{0}^{\infty}(P_{t}G-\lambda)dt

(9.9)

solves
{\cal L}F=G-\lambda
. But this is not the case for
{\cal A}_{0}
; for example

but

so that the integral for

does not converge. This causes some extra work, especially when solving the second and third equations. The construction of
D_{n}
in Lemmas
9.1
and
9.2
is related to, but distinctly different from, the construction in (

).

9.2.1
The first equation

We need to find

and

Define

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{0}F_{1}(v,\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{d}}\Re\biggl(ie^{i2\psi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\langle a,e^{tA}Av\rangle-i2\kappa_{d}\langle a,e^{tA}v\rangle\Bigr)e^{-i2\kappa_{d}t}dt\biggr)

\displaystyle=Q_{1}(v,\psi).

(9.11)

Therefore

.
For ease of notation we write

(9.12)

so that

F_{1}(v,\psi)=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{d}}\Re\biggl(ie^{i2\psi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle e^{tA^{\ast}}a,v\rangle e^{-i2\kappa_{d}t}dt\biggr)=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{d}}\Re\Bigl(ie^{i2\psi}\langle b,v\rangle\Bigr).

(9.13)

The second equation

and

Lemma 9.1

Suppose

is bilinear. For

such that

Moreover

E_{0}=-\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}C(e^{tA}Be_{\ell},e^{tA}Be_{\ell})dt=-\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}C(e_{j},e_{k})R_{jk}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}C(v,v)d\mu(v)

where

Proof.

D_{n}
is well defined because the eigenvalues of
A
have negative real parts. We will break down the calculation of

into first and second order derivatives.
We have

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{d}(Av)_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{j}}-\kappa_{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi}\Bigr)\bigl(e^{in\psi}D_{n}(v,v)\bigr)

\displaystyle=e^{in\psi}\Bigl(D_{n}(Av,v)+D_{n}(v,Av)-in\kappa_{d}D_{n}(v,v)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=-e^{in\psi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(C(Ae^{tA}v,e^{tA}v)+C(e^{tA}v,Ae^{tA}v)-in\kappa_{d}C(e^{tA}v,e^{tA}v)\Bigr)e^{-in\kappa_{d}t}dt

\displaystyle=-e^{in\psi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Bigl(C(e^{tA}v,e^{tA}v)e^{-in\kappa_{d}t}\Bigr)dt

\displaystyle=e^{in\psi}C(v,v),

and

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}(BB^{\ast})_{jk}\frac{\partial^{2}D_{n}}{\partial v_{j}\partial v_{k}}(v,v)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}(BB^{\ast})_{jk}\Bigl(D_{n}(e_{j},e_{k})+D_{n}(e_{k},e_{j})\Bigr):=E_{n}

where

and

denote the

th and

th standard unit vectors in

Together we have

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{0}\bigl(e^{in\psi}D_{n}(v,v)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{d}(Av)_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{j}}-\kappa_{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi}\Bigr)\bigl(e^{in\psi}D_{n}(v,v)\bigr)+\frac{e^{in\psi}}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}(BB^{\ast})_{jk}\frac{\partial^{2}D_{n}}{\partial v_{j}\partial v_{k}}(v,v)

\displaystyle=e^{in\psi}C(v,v)+e^{in\psi}E_{n}.

Finally

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}(BB^{\ast})_{jk}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(C(e^{tA}e_{j},e^{tA}e_{k})+C(e^{tA}e_{k},e^{tA}e_{j})\Bigr)dt

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}C(e^{tA}Be_{\ell},e^{tA}Be_{\ell})dt

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}C(e_{j},e_{k})\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl(e^{tA}Be_{\ell}\bigr)_{j}\bigl(e^{tA}Be_{\ell}\bigr)_{k}dt,

and the rest of (
9.18
) follows immediately from (
3.4
).
∎

m^{\varepsilon}
gives

0=\int Q^{\varepsilon}dm^{\varepsilon}+\zeta_{2}-\varepsilon^{2}\lambda_{2}+\varepsilon^{4}\int_{M}({\cal A}_{1}F_{3}+{\cal A}_{2}F_{2})dm^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{5}\int_{M}{\cal A}_{2}F_{3}dm^{\varepsilon}.

(9.26)

Using Khas’minskii’s formula (
9.3
), this gives

\lambda(\varepsilon)=-\zeta_{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon^{4}\int_{M}({\cal A}_{1}F_{3}+{\cal A}_{2}F_{2})dm^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{5}\int_{M}{\cal A}_{2}F_{3}dm^{\varepsilon}.

(9.27)

Finally the growth estimates above on
{\cal A}_{1}F_{3}
and
{\cal A}_{2}F_{2}
and
{\cal A}_{2}F_{3}
together with (
9.25
) imply that the integrals

\int_{M}({\cal A}_{1}F_{3}+{\cal A}_{2}F_{2})dm^{\varepsilon}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\int_{M}{\cal A}_{2}F_{3}dm^{\varepsilon}

are bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon
. At this point we let
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
9.27
) and obtain

\lambda(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{2}\lambda_{2}+O(\varepsilon^{4})

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.1
. ∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
(ii).
From (
9.14
) we see that
{\cal A}_{0}F_{2}
is a finite sum of terms of the form
\Re\left(e^{in\psi}c\right)
or
\Re\left(e^{in\psi}C(v,v)\right)
where the constants
c
and the coefficients in the bilinear mappings
C
are continuous functions of
\kappa_{d}
for
0<\kappa_{d}<\infty
. The construction in Section
9.2.2
implies that the same is true for
F_{2}
, and then also for
{\cal A}_{2}F_{2}
. So given
0<c_{1}<c_{2}<\infty
there exists
K_{1}
such that
|{\cal A}_{2}F_{2}(\psi,v)|\leq K_{1}(1+\|v\|^{2})
whenever
c_{1}\leq\kappa_{d}\leq c_{2}
.

Similarly from (
9.21
) we see that
{\cal A}_{0}F_{3}
is a finite sum of terms of the form
\Re\left(e^{in\psi}D(v)\right)
or
\Re\left(e^{in\psi}C(v,v,v)\right)
where the coefficients in the linear mappings
D
and trilinear mappings
C
are continuous functions of
\kappa_{d}
for
0<\kappa_{d}<\infty
. The construction in Section
9.2.3
implies that the same is true of
F_{2}
. So given
0<c_{1}<c_{2}<\infty
there exists
K_{2}
and
K_{3}
such that
|{\cal A}_{1}F_{3}(\psi,v)|\leq K_{2}(1+\|v\|^{4})
and
|{\cal A}_{2}F_{3}(\psi,v)|\leq K_{3}(1+\|v\|^{3})

.

These estimates can now be used in (

) to give the uniform estimate (

). ∎

9.4
Proof of Proposition
5.1

Taking
a=A^{\ast}\alpha
and
\gamma=B^{\ast}\alpha
in (
5.7
) we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\omega^{2}}\Bigl(\langle A^{\ast}\alpha,\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega)RA^{\ast}\alpha\rangle+\langle A^{\ast}\alpha,\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega)BB^{\ast}\alpha\rangle+\frac{\|B^{\ast}\alpha\|^{2}}{2\pi}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\omega^{2}}\Bigl(\langle\alpha,A\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega)RA^{\ast}\alpha\rangle+\langle\alpha,A\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega)BB^{\ast}\alpha\rangle+\frac{\langle\alpha,BB^{\ast}\alpha\rangle}{2\pi}\Bigr).

The covariance matrix
R
in
3.4
satisfies

AR+RA^{\ast}=-BB^{\ast},

(9.28)

see Gardiner
[
11
, Sect 4.4]
. Substituting for
BB^{\ast}
and noting
\langle\alpha,BB^{\ast}\alpha\rangle=-\langle\alpha,AR\alpha\rangle-\langle\alpha,RA^{\ast}\alpha\rangle=-2\langle\alpha,AR\alpha\rangle
we get

\lambda_{2}(\omega)=\frac{\pi}{\omega^{2}}\Bigl(-\langle\alpha,A\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega)AR\alpha\rangle-\frac{\langle\alpha,AR\alpha\rangle}{\pi}\Bigr).

(9.29)

Integrating by parts twice gives

A\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega)A=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}Ae^{tA}A\cos\omega t\,dt=-\frac{1}{\pi}A-\frac{\omega^{2}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{tA}\cos\omega t\,dt=-\frac{1}{\pi}A-\omega^{2}\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega).

Substituting for
A\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega)A
in (
9.29
) gives

\lambda_{2}(\omega)=\pi\langle\alpha,\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega)R\alpha\rangle.

(9.30)

Let
R(t)
denote the autocovariance matrix for
\{v(t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Then
R(0)
is the covariance matrix
R
of the stationary version, and
R(t)=e^{tA}R
for
t\geq 0
. The autocovariance function for
V
is

R_{V}(t)=\mathbb{E}[V(t)V(0)]=\langle\alpha,R(t)\alpha\rangle=\langle\alpha,e^{tA}R\alpha\rangle

for
t\geq 0
. The power spectral density of
V
is

S_{V}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}R_{V}(t)\cos\omega t\,dt=\langle\alpha,\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega)R\alpha\rangle,

and so

\lambda_{2}(\omega)=\pi\langle\alpha,\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega)R\alpha\rangle=\pi S_{V}(\omega),

as required. ∎

9.5
Proof of Proposition
5.2

We use the stationary version of
v
given by

v(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}BdW(s)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}Be_{\ell}dW_{\ell}(s),

where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
denote the standard basis vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle a,v(u)\rangle\psi_{\delta}(t-u)du

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{u}\left\langle a,e^{(u-s)A}Be_{\ell}\right\rangle dW_{\ell}(s)\right)\psi_{\delta}(t-u)du

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\left\langle a,e^{(u-s)A}Be_{\ell}\right\rangle\,\psi_{\delta}(t-u)du\right)dW_{\ell}(s),

and so

\xi_{\delta}(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\langle a,e^{(u-s)A}Be_{\ell}\rangle\,\psi_{\delta}(t-u)du+\gamma_{\ell}\psi_{\delta}(t-s)\right)dW_{\ell}(s)\equiv\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\xi_{\delta,\ell}(t),

say, Since the
\{W_{\ell}:1\leq\ell\leq m\}
are independent, then the
\{\xi_{\delta,\ell}:1\leq\ell\leq m\}
are independent, and the autocovariance function for
\xi_{\delta}
is the sum of the autocovariance functions for each of the
\xi_{\delta,\ell}
. The computation of each
E[\xi_{\delta,\ell}(t)\xi_{\delta,\ell}(0)]
can be broken down into 4 terms. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\delta}(t)\xi_{\delta}(0)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\delta,\ell}(t)\xi_{\delta,\ell}(0)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\langle a,e^{(u-s)A}Be_{\ell}\rangle\,\psi_{\delta}(t-u)du\right)\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\langle a,e^{(w-s)A}Be_{\ell}\rangle\,\psi_{\delta}(-w)dw\right)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\langle a,e^{(u-s)A}Be_{\ell}\rangle\,\psi_{\delta}(t-u)du\right)\gamma_{\ell}\psi_{\delta}(-s)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\langle a,e^{(u-s)A}Be_{\ell}\rangle\,\psi_{\delta}(-u)du\right)\gamma_{\ell}\psi_{\delta}(t-s)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\gamma_{\ell}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{\delta}(t-s)\psi_{\delta}(-s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{s}^{\infty}\langle a,e^{(u-s)A}BB^{\ast}e^{(w-s)A^{\ast}}a\rangle\psi_{\delta}(t-u)\psi_{\delta}(-w)dudw\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\langle a,e^{(u-s)A}B\gamma\rangle\,\psi_{\delta}(t-u)du\right)\psi_{\delta}(-s)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\langle a,e^{(u-s)A}B\gamma\rangle\,\psi_{\delta}(-u)du\right)\psi_{\delta}(t-s)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|\gamma\|^{2}\psi_{\delta}(t-s)\psi_{\delta}(-s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Bigl(I_{\delta}^{(1)}(s,t)+I_{\delta}^{(2)}(s,t)+I_{\delta}^{(3)}(s,t)+I_{\delta}^{(4)}(s,t)\Bigr)ds,

say. Then

S_{\xi_{\delta}}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\delta}(t)\xi_{\delta}(0)]\cos\omega t\,dt=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(i)}(s,t)\,ds\right)\cos\omega t\,dt.

We consider the limit as
\delta\to 0
for each of the four terms separately.

i=1
:

I_{\delta}^{(1)}(s,t)=\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{s}^{\infty}\left\langle a,e^{(u-s)A}BB^{\ast}e^{(w-s)A^{\ast}}a\right\rangle\psi_{\delta}(t-u)\psi_{\delta}(-w)\,dudw.

Recalling
t\geq 0
we have

I_{\delta}^{(1)}(s,t)\to\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\langle a,e^{(t-s)A}BB^{\ast}e^{-sA^{\ast}}a\rangle&\mbox{ if }s<0\\
0&\mbox{ if }s>0\end{array}\right.

as
\delta\to 0
. Also
I_{\delta}^{(1)}(s,t)=0
if
s>\delta
and

\displaystyle\left|I_{\delta}^{(1)}(s,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{-\delta\leq r_{1},r_{2}\leq\delta}\left|\left\langle a,e^{(t+r_{1}-s)A}BB^{\ast}e^{(r_{2}-s)A^{\ast}}a\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{|r|\leq\delta}\|e^{rA^{\ast}}a\|\right)^{2}\|e^{(t-s)A}BB^{\ast}e^{-sA^{\ast}}\|.

Therefore by the dominated convergence theorem we have

\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(1)}(s,t)\,ds\right)\cos\omega t\,dt\to\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\langle a,e^{(t-s)A}BB^{\ast}e^{-sA^{\ast}}a\rangle\,ds\right)\cos\omega t\,dt

as
\delta\to 0
. Since

\int_{-\infty}^{0}\langle a,e^{(t-s)A}BB^{\ast}e^{-sA^{\ast}}a\rangle\,ds=\left\langle a,e^{tA}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-sA}BB^{\ast}e^{-sA^{\ast}}\,ds\right)a\right\rangle=\langle a,e^{tA}Ra\rangle

where
R
is the covariance matrix for the invariant probability measure for
v(t)
, see (
3.4
), we have

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(1)}(s,t)\,ds\right)\cos\omega t\,dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle a,e^{tA}Ra\rangle\cos\omega t\,dt=\langle a,\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega)Ra\rangle.

i=2
:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(2)}(s,t)\,ds=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\langle a,e^{(u-s)A}B\gamma\rangle\psi(t-u)\,du\right)\psi(-s)\,ds.

For
t>0
we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(2)}(s,t)\,ds\to\langle a,e^{tA}B\gamma\rangle

as
\delta\to 0
.
Also

\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(2)}(s,t)\,ds\right|\leq\sup_{|r|\leq 2\delta}\left|\langle a,e^{(t+r)A}B\gamma\rangle\right|\leq\left(\sup_{|r|\leq 2\delta}\|e^{rA^{\ast}}a\|\right)\|e^{tA}B\gamma\|.

Therefore by the dominated convergence theorem we have

\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(2)}(s,t)\,ds\right)\cos\omega t\,dt\to\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle a,e^{tA}B\gamma\rangle\cos\omega t\,dt=\langle a,\widehat{S}_{A}(\omega)B\gamma\rangle

as
\delta\to 0
.

i=3
:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(3)}(s,t)\,ds=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\langle a,e^{(u-s)A}B\gamma\rangle\,\psi_{\delta}(-u)du\right)\psi_{\delta}(t-s)ds.

Note that
\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(3)}(s,t)\,ds\right|\leq\sup_{r\geq 0}|\langle a,e^{rA}B\gamma\rangle|<\infty
. Also, if
0<2\delta<t
then
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(3)}(s,t)\,ds=0
.
Therefore by the dominated convergence theorem we have

\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(3)}(s,t)\,ds\right)\cos\omega t\,dt\to 0

as
\delta\to 0
.

i=4
:

The substitution
u=s-t
gives

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(4)}(s,t)\,ds=\|\gamma\|^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma_{\delta}(t-s)\psi_{\delta}(-s)ds=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(4)}(s,-t)\,ds,

and so

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(4)}(s,t)\,ds\right)\cos\omega t\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(4)}(s,t)\,ds\right)\cos\omega t\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\gamma\|^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{\delta}(t-s)\psi_{\delta}(-s)\cos\omega t\,dsdt

Notice
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{\delta}(t-s)\psi_{\delta}(-s)ds=0
if
|t|>2\delta
. Also

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{\delta}(t-s)\psi_{\delta}(-s)\,dsdt

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{\delta}(-s)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{\delta}(t-s)\,dt\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{\delta}(-s)\,ds=1.

Therefore

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\gamma\|^{2}}{2\pi}-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(4)}(s,t)\,ds\right)\cos\omega t\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\gamma\|^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{\delta}(t-s)\psi_{\delta}(-s)\bigl(1-\cos\omega t\bigr)\,dsdt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\gamma\|^{2}}{2\pi}\sup_{|t|\leq 2\delta}\big|1-\cos\omega t\big|

and so

\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}I_{\delta}^{(4)}(s,t)\,ds\right)\cos\omega t\,dt\to\frac{\|\gamma\|^{2}}{2\pi}

as
\delta\to 0
.

Together these four limits give (
5.16
), and the rest of Proposition
5.2
follows directly from the definition (
5.7
). ∎

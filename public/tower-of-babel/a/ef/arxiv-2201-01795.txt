Note on the Fekete–Szegö problem for spirallike mappings in Banach spaces
Mark Elin, Fiana Jacobzon

Abstract
. In this note we present a remark on the paper “On the coefficient inequalities for a class of holomorphic mappings associated with spirallike mappings in several complex variables” by Y. Lai and Q. Xu [ 10 ] published recently in the journal Results in Mathematics . We show that one of the theorems in [ 10 ] concerning the finite-dimensional space ℂ n {\mathbb{C}}^{n} is a direct consequence of another one, so it does not need an independent proof. Moreover, we prove that a sharp norm estimate on the Fekete–Szegö functional over spirallike mappings in a general Banach space can be deduced from a result in [ 10 ] .

We mostly preserve the notations used in
[
10
]
to make the reading more convenient. Let
\mathbb{U}
be the open unit disk in the complex plane
{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathbb{B}}
be the open unit ball in a complex Banach space
X
equipped with norm
\|\cdot\|
.

Let
X^{*}
be the dual space of
X
. For each
x\in X\setminus\{0\}
, we define

T(x)=\left\{T_{x}\in X^{*}:\|T_{x}\|=1\text{ and }T_{x}(x)=\|x\|\right\}.

(1)

According to the Hahn-Banach theorem (see, for example, Theorem 3.2 in
[
11
]
),
T(x)
is nonempty. Let
Y
be a Banach space (possibly, different from
X
). Denote by
\mathop{\rm Hol}\nolimits({\mathbb{B}},Y)
the set of all holomorphic mappings from
{\mathbb{B}}
into
Y
. It is well known
that if
f\in\mathop{\rm Hol}\nolimits({\mathbb{B}},Y)
, then

f(y)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}D^{n}f(x)\left[(y-x)^{n}\right]

(2)

for all
y
in some neighborhood of
x\in{\mathbb{B}}
, where
D^{n}f(x):\prod_{k=1}^{n}X\to Y
is the
n^{th}
Fréchet derivative of
f
at
x
and

D^{n}f(x)\left[(y-x)^{n}\right]=D^{n}f(x)[y-x,\ldots,y-x].

(3)

It is well-known that
D^{n}f(x)
is a bounded symmetric
n
-linear operator.

Recall that a holomorphic mapping
f:{\mathbb{B}}\to X
is called biholomorphic if the inverse
f^{-1}
exists and
is holomorphic on the image
f({\mathbb{B}})
. A mapping
f\in\mathop{\rm Hol}\nolimits({\mathbb{B}},X)
is said to be locally biholomorphic
if for each
x\in{\mathbb{B}}
there exists a bounded inverse for the Fréchet derivative
Df(x)
.

Further we will use the following definitions.

Definition 1

(see
[
4
]
)
.

Suppose
|\beta|<\frac{\pi}{2}
. A normalized locally biholomorphic mapping
f:{\mathbb{B}}\to X
is called spirallike of type
\beta
if

\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\left(e^{-i\beta}T_{x}\left(\left(Df(x)\right)^{-1}f(x)\right)\right)>0,\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{B}}\setminus\{0\}\text{ and }\ T_{x}\in T(x).

(4)

Let
\widehat{\mathop{\mathcal{S}}\nolimits}_{\beta}({\mathbb{B}})
be the class of spirallike mappings of type
\beta
on
{\mathbb{B}}
. In the case
X={\mathbb{C}}
,
{\mathbb{B}}={\mathbb{U}}
, we denote
\widehat{\mathop{\mathcal{S}}\nolimits}_{\beta}:=\widehat{\mathop{\mathcal{S}}\nolimits}_{\beta}({\mathbb{U}}).

Moreover, the relation (
4
) is equivalent to
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\left(e^{-i\beta}\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)>0
. Therefore, Definition
1
is the standard one for spirallike functions of type
\beta
on
{\mathbb{U}}
(see, for example,
[
4
]
).

Definition 2

(cf.
[
1
,
12
]
)
.

Suppose
|\beta|<\frac{\pi}{2}
. Let
g:{\mathbb{U}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a biholomorphic function such
that
g(0)=1
,
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits g(z)>0
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and
g_{\beta}(z)=\frac{\cos\!\beta g(z)+\!i\sin\beta}{e^{i\beta}}.
Denote

\widehat{\mathop{\mathcal{M}}\nolimits}_{g}(\beta)\!:=\!\left\{h\in\mathop{\rm Hol}\nolimits({\mathbb{B}},X)\!:\!\frac{\|x\|}{T_{x}(h(x))}\!\in g_{\beta}({\mathbb{U}}),x\in{\mathbb{B}}\!\setminus\{0\},T_{x}\in T(x)\!\right\}\!.

(5)

In the special case when
X={\mathbb{C}}
,
{\mathbb{B}}={\mathbb{U}}
, the class
\widehat{\mathop{\mathcal{M}}\nolimits}_{g}(\beta)
consists of holomorphic functions
h
that satisfy

\frac{z}{h(z)}\!\in g_{\beta}({\mathbb{U}}).

Note that the class
\widehat{\mathop{\mathcal{M}}\nolimits}_{g}(0)
was introduced in
[
3
,
9
]
and studied in
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
.
Furthermore, if one takes
g(z)=\frac{1+z}{1-z}
,
z\in{\mathbb{U}}
, in Definition
2
, then the fact

\left(Df(x)\right)^{-1}f(x)\in\widehat{\mathop{\mathcal{M}}\nolimits}_{g}(\beta)
is equivalent to
f\in\widehat{\mathop{\mathcal{S}}\nolimits}_{\beta}({\mathbb{B}})
.

To proceed, we assume that
g:{\mathbb{U}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies the conditions of Definition
2
and the number
\beta
,
-\frac{\pi}{2}<\beta<\frac{\pi}{2}
, is fixed. Define the function
\psi_{\beta}:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
by

\psi_{\beta}(\lambda):=\cos\beta\cdot\,\max\!\left\{1,\left|(1-2\lambda)\cos\beta g^{\prime}(0)+\frac{e^{i\beta}}{2}\!\cdot\!\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(0)}{g^{\prime}(0)}\right|\!\right\}.

Y. Lai and Q. Xu in
[
10
]
studied so-called mappings of one-dimensional type (that is, such that
F(x)=f(x)x
, where
f\in\mathop{\rm Hol}\nolimits({\mathbb{B}},{\mathbb{C}})
, see, for example,
[
2
]
) and proved the following result:

Theorem 1

(Theorem 3.2 in
[
10
]
)
.

Let
g\in\mathop{\rm Hol}\nolimits({\mathbb{U}},{\mathbb{C}})
satisfy the conditions of Definition
2
,
f\in\mathop{\rm Hol}\nolimits({\mathbb{B}},{\mathbb{C}})
,
f(0)=1
,
F(x)=xf(x)
and suppose that
\left(DF(x)\right)^{-1}F(x)\in\widehat{M_{g}}(\beta)
. Then for every
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
we have

\left|\frac{T_{x}\left(D^{3}F(0)(x^{3})\right)}{3!\|x\|^{3}}\!-\!\lambda\left(\frac{T_{x}\left(D^{2}F(0)(x^{2})\right)}{2!\|x\|^{2}}\right)^{2}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\ \psi_{\beta}(\lambda)|g^{\prime}(0)|,

where
x\in{\mathbb{B}}\setminus\{0\}
and
T_{x}\in T(x).
The above estimate is sharp.

Further, the authors of
[
10
]
prove independently Theorem 3.3 that asserts that in the case where
X={\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and is equipped with the norm
\|x\|:=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\left|x_{j}\right|
, the hypotheses of Theorem
1
imply that inequality (
6
) below holds and is sharp. We claim that the mentioned Theorem 3.3 in
[
10
]
is a direct consequence of Theorem
1
. Moreover, we show that for any Banach space
X
the following assertion holds.

Theorem 2
.

Let
g\in\mathop{\rm Hol}\nolimits({\mathbb{U}},{\mathbb{C}})
satisfy the conditions of Definition
2
, and mapping
F\in\mathop{\rm Hol}\nolimits({\mathbb{B}},X)
be of one-dimensional type such that
F(0)=0
,
DF(0)=I
and
\left(DF(x)\right)^{-1}F(x)\in\widehat{M_{g}}(\beta)
. Then for every
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
,

\left\|\frac{D^{3}F(0)(x^{3})}{3!}\!-\!\lambda\frac{1}{2}D^{2}F(0)\!\!\left(\!x,\frac{D^{2}F(0)(x^{2})}{2!}\!\right)\right\|\!\leq\!\frac{\|x\|^{3}}{2}\psi_{\beta}(\lambda)|g^{\prime}(0)|,

(6)

where
x\in{\mathbb{B}}\setminus\{0\}.
The above estimate is sharp.

Proof.

Since
F
is of one-dimensional type and
F(0)=0,\ DF(0)=I
, it can be represented as
F(x)=xf(x)
, where
f\in H({\mathbb{B}},{\mathbb{C}})
with
f(0)=1.

In order to find the first Fréchet derivative we calculate

\displaystyle F(z+w)-F(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(z+w)(z+w)-f(z)z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f(z+w)-f(z))z+f(z+w)w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Df(z)[w]z+f(z)w+o(\|w\|).

This implies

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}(i)\ \ \ DF(z)[w]=Df(z)[w]z+f(z)w,\\
(ii)\ \ DF(0)[x]=f(0)x,\\
(iii)\ T_{x}(DF(0)(x))=T_{x}(f(0)x)=f(0)\|x\|.\end{array}

Turn now to the second Fréchet derivative:

\displaystyle DF(z+w_{1})[w_{2}]-DF(z)[w_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Df(z+w_{1})[w_{2}](z+w_{1})+f(z+w_{1})w_{2}-Df(z)[w_{2}]z-f(z)w_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(Df(z+w_{1})-Df(z))[w_{2}]z+Df(z+w_{1})[w_{2}]w_{1}+(f(z+w_{1})-f(z))w_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{2}f(z)[w_{1},w_{2}]z+Df(z)[w_{2}]w_{1}+Df(z)[w_{1}]w_{2}+o(\left\|w_{1}\right\|\cdot\left\|w_{2}\right\|).

This leads to

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}(i)\ \ \ D^{2}F(z)[w_{1},w_{2}]=D^{2}f(z)[w_{1},w_{2}]z+Df(z)[w_{2}]w_{1}+Df(z)[w_{1}]w_{2},\\
(ii)\ \ D^{2}F(0)[w_{1},w_{2}]=Df(0)[w_{2}]w_{1}+Df(0)[w_{1}]w_{2},\\
(iii)\ D^{2}F(0)[x^{2}]=2Df(0)[x]x,\\
(iv)\ T_{x}(D^{2}F(0)[x^{2}])=2Df(0)[x]T_{x}(x)=2Df(0)[x]\|x\|.\end{array}

Using these calculations we can similarly find the third Fréchet derivative and get:

D^{3}\!F(0)\left[x^{3}\right]\!=\!3D^{2}\!f(0)\left[x^{2}\right]x\ \text{ and }\ T_{x}(D^{3}\!F(0)[x^{3}])\!=\!3D^{2}\!f(0)[x^{2}]\|x\|.

(16)

We now apply formulae (
Proof.
)(iii) and (
16
) to compute the analog of the Fekete–Szegö functional for
F
:

\displaystyle\frac{D^{3}F(0)[x^{3}]}{3!}-\lambda\frac{1}{2}D^{2}F(0)\left[x,\frac{D^{2}F(0)(x^{2})}{2!}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D^{2}f(0)[x^{2}]}{2!}x-\lambda\frac{1}{2}D^{2}F(0)\left[x,Df(0)(x)x\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D^{2}f(0)[x^{2}]}{2!}x-\lambda\frac{1}{2}\left(Df(0)[Df(0)[x]x]x+Df(0)[x]Df(0)[x]x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{D^{2}f(0)[x^{2}]}{2!}x-\!\lambda Df(0)[x]Df(0)[x]x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\!\frac{D^{2}f(0)[x^{2}]}{2!}-\lambda\left(Df(0)[x]\right)^{2}\!\right)\!x.

Thus this is a mapping of one-dimensional type. Combining formulae (
Proof.
)–(
16
) we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{D^{3}F(0)[x^{3}]}{3!}-\lambda\frac{1}{2}D^{2}F(0)\left[x,\frac{D^{2}F(0)[x^{2}]}{2!}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{D^{2}f(0)[x^{2}]}{2!}-\lambda\left(Df(0)[x]\right)^{2}\right|\cdot\|x\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x\|^{3}\left|\frac{T_{x}\left(D^{3}F(0)[x^{3}]\right)}{3!\|x\|^{3}}-\lambda\left(\frac{T_{x}(D^{2}F(0)[x^{2}])}{2!\|x\|}\right)^{2}\right|.

This equality together with Theorem
1
implies that estimate (
6
) holds and is sharp. ∎

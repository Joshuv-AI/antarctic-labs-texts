A Radiative Neutrino Mass Model in Dark Non-Abelian Gauge Symmetry
Takaaki Nomura, Hiroshi Okada

Abstract
We discuss a model based on dark sector described by non-Abelian S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) D SU(2)_{D} gauge symmetry where we introduce S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L × S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) D SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{D} bi-doublet vector-like leptons to generate active neutrino masses and kinetic mixing between S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) D SU(2)_{D} and U ​ ( 1 ) Y U(1)_{Y} gauge fields at one-loop level. After spontaneous symmetry breaking of S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) D SU(2)_{D} , we have remnant Z 4 Z_{4} symmetry guaranteeing stability of dark matter candidates. We formulate neutrino mass matrix and related lepton flavor violating processes and discus dark matter physics estimating relic density. It is found that our model realize multicomponent dark matter scenario due to the Z 4 Z_{4} symmetry and relic density can be explained by gauge interactions with kinetic mixing effect.

I
Introduction

A mechanism of generating neutrino mass and the existence of dark matter (DM) are important hints to understand physics beyond the standard model(SM).
One of the attractive scenarios is that DM and neutrino mass generation are induced from dark sector described by dark gauge symmetry under which the SM fields are singlet.
Then we expect nature of neutrino mass generation mechanism and DM physics are understand by dark gauge symmetry.
For example, the stability of DM could be understood by remnant of dark gauge symmetry
Krauss:1988zc
;
Ko:2018qxz
and neutrino mass at tree level can be suppressed by such symmetry.

One interesting scenario is non-Abelian dark gauge symmetry such as
SU(2)
which provides us rich structure of dark sector, giving
possibility of vector DM from dark gauge sector and
Z^{\prime}
as mediator at the same time.
In fact, we can find various approaches applying a dark
SU(2)
gauge symmetry in literatures, for examples, a remaining
Z_{2,3,4}

Chiang:2013kqa

Chen:2015nea

Boehm:2014bia
;
Hambye:2008bq
;

U(1)

Baek:2013dwa
;
Khoze:2014woa
;
Daido:2019tbm

U(1)_{h}

Davoudiasl:2013jma

Barman:2017yzr
;

;
Barman:2020ifq

Karam:2015jta

Hall:2019ank

Ghosh:2020ipy

Here one interesting question for non-Abelian dark gauge symmetric case is how we can induce interactions among dark gauge bosons and the SM particles,
since kinetic mixing is not allowed at renormalizable level in contrast to the Abelian gauge symmetric case.
In ref.
Nomura:2021tmi

SU(2)
and the SM
U(1)_{Y}
introducing a field which has both dark
SU(2)
and
U(1)_{Y}

Interestingly when we chose such a field as vector-like leptons, they can also play a role in generating active neutrino mass at loop level
Okada:2013iba
;
Okada:2014qsa
;
Okada:2015vwh

SU(2)

In this work, we discuss a model with non-Abelian
SU(2)_{D}
gauge symmetry in which we introduce
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{D}
bi-doublet vector like leptons.
This bi-doublet leptons can induce mixing among
SU(2)_{D}
and
U(1)_{Y}
gauge fields and play a role to generate active neutrino mass
when we introduce relevant scalar
SU(2)_{D}
multiplets.
It is also found that there is remnant
Z_{4}
symmetry after spontaneous symmetry breaking and stability of DM is guaranteed by this symmetry.
We then formulate active neutrino mass and branching rations (BRs) of lepton flavor violating (LFV) charged lepton decay
\ell_{i}\to\ell_{j}\gamma
in our model.
In addition, relic density of our DM candidates is estimated where DM is more than one component in our scenario.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.II, we introduce our model showing relevant Lagrangian and particle contents.
In Sec.III, we discuss phenomenology of the model such as neutrino mass generation, LFV and DM physics.
Summary and discussion are given in Sec.IV.

II.1
Scalar sector and symmetry breaking

Firstly we consider gauge invariant operators in scalar potential in terms of the components of the scalar multiplets.
Quadratic terms are given by

\displaystyle\chi^{\dagger}\chi=\chi_{1}^{*}\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}^{*}\chi_{2},

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}[\varphi\varphi]=\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{0}^{2}+\varphi_{+}\varphi_{-},

(8)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi=\Phi_{0}^{2}+\Phi_{++}\Phi_{--}+\Phi_{+}\Phi_{-}+\tilde{\Phi}_{++}\tilde{\Phi}_{--}+\tilde{\Phi}_{+}\tilde{\Phi}_{-}.

(9)

Non-trivial terms in the potential are written by

\displaystyle\Phi^{\dagger}\hat{\varphi}\Phi=\sqrt{3}\Phi_{0}\tilde{\Phi}_{+}\varphi_{-}+\sqrt{3}\Phi_{0}\Phi_{-}\varphi_{+}+\sqrt{3}\Phi_{0}^{*}\Phi_{+}\varphi_{-}+\sqrt{3}\Phi_{0}^{*}\tilde{\Phi}_{-}\varphi_{+}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(2\Phi_{++}\Phi_{--}+\Phi_{+}\Phi_{-}-\tilde{\Phi}_{+}\tilde{\Phi}_{-}-2\tilde{\Phi}_{++}\tilde{\Phi}_{--})\varphi_{0}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sqrt{2}\Phi_{--}\Phi_{+}\varphi_{+}+\sqrt{2}\tilde{\Phi}_{--}\tilde{\Phi}_{+}\varphi_{+}+\sqrt{2}\Phi_{++}\Phi_{-}\varphi_{-}+\sqrt{2}\tilde{\Phi}_{++}\tilde{\Phi}_{-}\varphi_{-}

(10)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\dagger}\hat{\varphi}\hat{\varphi}\Phi=\varphi_{0}^{2}(4\Phi_{++}\Phi_{--}+4\tilde{\Phi}_{++}\tilde{\Phi}_{--}+\Phi_{+}\Phi_{-}+\tilde{\Phi}_{+}\tilde{\Phi}_{-})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\varphi_{+}\varphi_{-}(2\Phi_{++}\Phi_{--}+2\tilde{\Phi}_{++}\tilde{\Phi}_{--}+5\Phi_{+}\Phi_{-}+5\tilde{\Phi}_{+}\tilde{\Phi}_{-})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\varphi_{-}^{2}(3\Phi_{+}\tilde{\Phi}_{+}+\sqrt{6}\Phi_{0}\tilde{\Phi}_{++}+\sqrt{6}\Phi_{0}^{*}\Phi_{++})+\varphi_{+}^{2}(3\Phi_{-}\tilde{\Phi}_{-}+\sqrt{6}\Phi^{*}_{0}\tilde{\Phi}_{--}+\sqrt{6}\Phi_{0}\Phi_{--})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sqrt{3}\varphi_{0}\Phi_{0}(\Phi_{-}\varphi_{+}-\tilde{\Phi}_{+}\varphi_{-})+\sqrt{3}\varphi_{0}\Phi^{*}_{0}(\Phi_{+}\varphi_{-}-\tilde{\Phi}_{-}\varphi_{+})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+3\sqrt{2}\varphi_{0}(\Phi_{++}\Phi_{-}\varphi_{-}+\Phi_{--}\Phi_{+}\varphi_{+}-\tilde{\Phi}_{++}\tilde{\Phi}_{-}\varphi_{-}-\tilde{\Phi}_{--}\tilde{\Phi}_{+}\varphi_{+}),

(11)

\displaystyle\chi(i\sigma_{2})\varphi\chi+h.c.=-\sqrt{2}\varphi_{0}\chi_{1}\chi_{2}+\varphi_{-}\chi_{1}\chi_{1}-\varphi_{+}\chi_{2}\chi_{2}+h.c.\,.

\partial V/\partial\phi=0

\phi

(15)

\lambda_{\Phi H}

On the other hand VEVs

and

13

14

II.2
Gauge sector

Here we focus on gauge sector of
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{D}\times U(1)_{Y}
where the Lagrangian is

\mathcal{L}_{G}=-\frac{1}{4}W^{a\mu\nu}W^{a}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{X}^{\alpha\mu\nu}\tilde{X}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu},

(19)

and
W^{a\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu}
are gauge field strength of
SU(2)_{L}
and
U(1)_{Y}
, respectively.
In addition to these terms in Eq. (
19
), a term connecting
SU(2)_{D}
and
U(1)_{B}
is generated by a one-loop diagrams
in which
L^{\prime}
propagates
Nomura:2021tmi
.
We obtain such a term as

\mathcal{L}_{XB}=\sum_{a}\frac{g_{X}g_{B}y_{aa}}{12\pi^{2}M_{L^{\prime}}}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{X}^{\alpha\mu\nu}\varphi^{\alpha}.

(20)

Then after
\varphi
developing its VEV, we obtain kinetic mixing term

\mathcal{L}_{KM}=\sum_{a}\frac{g_{X}g_{B}y_{aa}v_{\varphi}}{12\sqrt{2}\pi^{2}M_{L^{\prime}}}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{X}^{3\mu\nu}\equiv-\frac{1}{2}\sin\chi\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{X}^{3\mu\nu},

(21)

where
\tilde{X}^{3}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\tilde{X}^{3}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\tilde{X}^{3}_{\mu}
.
We thus find magnitude of kinetic mixing parameter
\sin\chi
as

\sin\chi\simeq 1.8\times 10^{-3}\frac{g_{X}}{0.5}\frac{v_{\varphi}}{M_{L^{\prime}}}\sum_{a}y_{aa}.

(22)

In our analysis we consider
\sin\chi<10^{-3}
.
We can diagonalize the kinetic terms for
\tilde{X}^{3}_{\mu}
and
\tilde{B}_{\mu}
by the following transformations:

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{\mu}=B_{\mu}-\tan{\chi}X^{3}_{\mu},

(23)

\displaystyle\tilde{X}^{3}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\cos{\chi}}X^{3}_{\mu}.

(24)

Since the kinetic mixing term is generated at loop level,
\chi
is typically very small, and we take a limit of
{\chi}\ll 1
writing gauge field approximately by

\tilde{B}_{\mu}\simeq B_{\mu}-{\chi}X^{3}_{\mu},\quad\tilde{X}^{3}_{\mu}\simeq X^{3}_{\mu}.

(25)

After quintet and triplet scalar fields develop nonzero VEVs, mass terms for
SU(2)_{D}
gauge fields and SM Z boson field are given such that

\displaystyle L_{M}=\frac{1}{2}m_{Z_{SM}}^{2}\tilde{Z}_{\mu}\tilde{Z}+m_{Z_{SM}}^{2}\chi\sin\theta_{W}\tilde{Z}_{\mu}X^{3\mu}+\frac{1}{2}m_{X^{3}}^{2}X^{3}_{\mu}X^{3\mu}+m_{X^{\pm}}^{2}X^{+}_{\mu}X^{-\mu},

(26)

\displaystyle m_{Z_{SM}}^{2}=\frac{v^{2}}{4}(g^{2}+g_{B}^{2}),\quad m_{X^{3}}^{2}=4g_{X}^{2}v_{\Phi}^{2},\quad m_{X^{\pm}}^{2}=g_{X}^{2}v_{\Phi}^{2}\left(1+\frac{v_{\varphi}^{2}}{v_{\Phi}^{2}}\right),

(27)

where
g
and
g_{B}
are gauge couplings of
SU(2)
and
U(1)_{Y}
,
\tilde{Z}
is
Z
boson field in the SM, and
X_{\mu}^{\pm}=(\tilde{X}_{\mu}^{1}\mp iX_{\mu}^{2})/\sqrt{2}
.
Diagonalizing
\tilde{Z}
and
X^{3}
mass terms, we obtain mass eigenstate and mixing angles as

\displaystyle m_{Z,Z^{\prime}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(m_{X^{3}}^{2}+m_{Z_{SM}}^{2})\mp\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(m_{X^{3}}^{2}-m_{Z_{SM}}^{2})^{2}+4\chi^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}m_{Z_{SM}}^{4}},

(28)

\displaystyle\tan 2\theta_{ZZ^{\prime}}=\frac{2\sin\theta_{W}\chi m_{Z_{SM}}^{2}}{m_{Z_{SM}}^{2}-m_{X^{3}}^{2}},

(29)

where we approximate
m_{Z}\simeq m_{Z_{SM}}
and
m_{Z^{\prime}}\simeq m_{X^{3}}
for tiny
\chi
, and we take
0\leq\theta_{ZZ^{\prime}}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
as our convention.
Here we emphasize that mass relation
m_{Z^{\prime}}\sim 2m_{X^{\pm}}
is obtained when dark gauge boson masses are dominantly induced by quintet VEV,
and
X^{\pm}
annihilation cross section via
Z^{\prime}
is enhanced by resonant effect.

Thus the mass eigenstates
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
are written by

\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
Z^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{ZZ^{\prime}}&-\sin\theta_{ZZ^{\prime}}\\
\sin\theta_{ZZ^{\prime}}&\cos\theta_{ZZ^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{Z}\\
X^{3}\end{pmatrix}.

(30)

We find that
\sin\theta_{ZZ^{\prime}}\lesssim 10^{-4}
for
\chi\leq 10^{-3}
and
m_{X^{\pm}}\geq 100

.

II.3
Mass terms of hidden fermions

In this subsection we discuss mass terms for hidden fermions.

Neutral fermion masses:

After spontaneous symmetry breaking we obtain mass terms of dark neutral fermions such that

\displaystyle L_{M_{N}}=

\displaystyle M_{N}^{ab}(\overline{n_{1}^{a}}n_{1}^{b}+\overline{n_{2}^{a}}n_{2}^{b})+M_{nn}^{ab}(\overline{n^{ca}_{2L}}n^{b}_{1L}+\overline{n^{ca}_{1L}}n^{b}_{2L})+\tilde{M}_{nn}^{ab}(\overline{n^{ca}_{2R}}n^{b}_{1R}+\overline{n^{ca}_{1R}}n^{b}_{2R})

\displaystyle+M_{D_{n}}^{ab}(\overline{n^{a}_{1L}}n^{b}_{1R}-\overline{n^{a}_{2L}}n^{b}_{2R})+M_{nn^{\prime}}^{ab}(\overline{n^{\prime a}_{1R}}n^{b}_{1L}+\overline{n^{\prime a}_{2R}}n^{b}_{2L})+\tilde{M}_{nn^{\prime}}^{ab}(\overline{n^{\prime a}_{1L}}n^{b}_{1R}+\overline{n^{\prime a}_{2L}}n^{b}_{2R})

\displaystyle+M_{L^{\prime}}^{ab}(\overline{n^{\prime a}_{1}}n^{\prime b}_{1}+\overline{n^{\prime a}_{2}}n^{\prime b}_{2}),

(31)

where mass matrices appearing these terms are given by

\displaystyle M_{nn^{\prime}}^{ab}=\frac{g_{R}^{ab}}{\sqrt{2}}v_{H},\quad\tilde{M}_{nn^{\prime}}^{ab}=\frac{g_{L}^{ab}}{\sqrt{2}}v_{H},\quad M_{nn}^{ab}=\frac{y_{N_{L}}^{ab}}{2}v_{\varphi},\quad\tilde{M}_{nn}^{ab}=\frac{y_{N_{R}}^{ab}}{2}v_{\varphi},\quad M_{D_{n}}=\frac{y_{D}^{ab}}{2}v_{\varphi}.

(32)

We thus obtain Majorana mass matrix for dark neutral fermions under the basis of
\Psi_{R}=(n^{\prime}_{1R},n^{\prime}_{2R},n^{\prime c}_{1L},n^{\prime c}_{2L},n_{1R},n_{2R},n^{c}_{1L},n^{c}_{2L})^{T}
such as

M_{R}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&M_{L^{\prime}}^{T}&0&0&0&M^{*}_{nn^{\prime}}&0\\
0&0&0&M_{L^{\prime}}^{T}&0&0&0&M^{*}_{nn^{\prime}}\\
M_{L^{\prime}}&0&0&0&\tilde{M}_{nn^{\prime}}&0&0&0\\
0&M_{L^{\prime}}&0&0&0&\tilde{M}_{nn^{\prime}}&0&0\\
0&0&\tilde{M}^{T}_{nn^{\prime}}&0&0&\tilde{M}_{nn}^{\dagger}&\tilde{M}^{T}_{N}+M^{T}_{D_{n}}&0\\
0&0&0&\tilde{M}^{T}_{nn^{\prime}}&\tilde{M}_{nn}^{\dagger}&0&0&\tilde{M}^{T}_{N}-M^{T}_{D_{n}}\\
M^{\dagger}_{nn^{\prime}}&0&0&0&\tilde{M}_{N}+M_{D_{n}}&0&0&M_{nn}^{\dagger}\\
0&M^{\dagger}_{nn^{\prime}}&0&0&0&\tilde{M}_{N}-M_{D_{n}}&M_{nn}^{\dagger}&0\\
\end{pmatrix},

(33)

where generation indices are omitted and it is
24\times 24

This mass matrix can be diagonalized by
24\times 24

V_{N}
assuming all matrix elements are real, and mass eigenstates are given by

\psi_{R}=V_{N}^{T}\Psi_{R}.

(34)

We write mass eigenvalues as
M_{x}\ (x=1,..,24)
. The orthogonal matrix
V_{N}
and mass
M_{x}
can be numerically obtained.

Charged fermion masses

IV
Summary and discussion

We have discussed a model based on dark
SU(2)_{D}
gauge symmetry in which we introduce

SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{D}
bi-doublet vector like leptons.
The bi-doublets connect dark sector and SM sector through the interaction associated with the SM lepton doublets and
SU(2)_{D}
scalar doublet.
We then obtain active neutrino masses and an interaction realizing kinetic mixing between
SU(2)_{D}
and
U(1)_{Y}
gauge fields at loop level.
Moreover there is remnant
Z_{4}
symmetry after spontaneous breaking of
SU(2)_{D}
in our scenario and the symmetry guarantees the stability of our DM candidates.

We have formulated active neutrino mass matrix and related LFV processes in our model.
Also relic density of our DM candidates is estimated scanning some relevant parameters.
Remarkably we have found multicomponent scenario in some parameter space where we have vector and scalar DM components.
We have shown parameter points realizing observed relic density where vector DM tends to provide smaller relic density due to resonant enhancement of corresponding annihilation cross section.

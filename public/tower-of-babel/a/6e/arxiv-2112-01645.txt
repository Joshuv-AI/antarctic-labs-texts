Winding and intersection of Brownian motions
Isao Sauzedde

Abstract
. We study the set of points 𝒟 n , m \mathcal{D}_{n,m} around which two independent Brownian motions wind at least n n (resp. m m ) times. We prove that its area is asymptotically equivalent, in L p L^{p} and almost surely, to ℓ ⁡ ( ℝ 2 ) 4 ​ π 2 ​ n ​ m \frac{\ell({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}{4\pi^{2}nm} , where ℓ \ell is the intersection measure of the two trajectories. We also prove that the properly scaled Lebesgue measure carried by 𝒟 n , m \mathcal{D}_{n,m} converges almost surely weakly toward ℓ \ell .

Introduction

In
[

[

By a very careful asymptotic analysis of the joint law of winding around two points, he managed to show that

In

by showing that, both in

D^{X}_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi n}+O(n^{-\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}).

It allowed us to proved an almost sure version of the stochastic Green’s formula,

\int X^{1}\,{\rm d}X^{2}\overset{a.s.}{=}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=-n}^{n}kA^{X}_{k}.

The present work was initially motivated by the idea of pushing the asymptotic expansion further. We think that such an expansion should lead for example to a better understanding of magnetic impurities as described in
[
5
]
and
[
4

, and also of the winding field associated with the Brownian loop soup
[

22

should allow to relates this winding field with the multiplicative chaos of the Brownian loop soup
[

.
We also expect that this asymptotic expansion should allow to define stochastic integrals of extremely irregular random
1

[

In fact, the second order term in the asymptotic expansion of

seems to be deeply related to the self-intersections of the Brownian motion, and actually determined by its self-intersection local time. In order to prove such a result, it is needed to first understand the intersection of the large winding sets of different Brownian pieces.

This paper is devoted to the study of the large
n,m

\mathcal{D}^{X,Y}_{n,m}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\theta_{X}(z)\geq n,\theta_{Y}(z)\geq m\},

Our main results are the following. Let

.

Let

We endow the space

2.
Notations and general ideas

In the following, it is always assumed that
m
is a non-decreasing integer-valued function of the integer
n
, and that there exists
c>0
such that
n^{c}<m\leq n
for all positive integer
n
. It is also assumed that
T
is another integer-valued function of
n
, and that
m
and
T
are larger than
2
.

Unless otherwise specified, all the Brownian motions are defined from
[0,1]
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Under
\mathbb{P}_{x,y}
,
X
and
Y
are two independent Brownian motions starting respectively from
x
and
y
. When it is not necessary to specify these starting points, we simply write
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{x,y}
. When we write
\mathbb{E}_{T^{\frac{1}{2}}X_{iT^{-1}},T^{\frac{1}{2}}Y_{jT^{-1}}}[f(\hat{X},\hat{Y})]
,
it should be understood that
\hat{X},\hat{Y}
are two independent Brownian motions, defined from
[0,1]
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, starting respectively from
T^{\frac{1}{2}}X_{iT^{-1}}
and
T^{\frac{1}{2}}Y_{jT^{-1}}
, and such that

(\hat{X}-T^{\frac{1}{2}}X_{iT^{-1}},\hat{Y}-T^{\frac{1}{2}}Y_{jT^{-1}})
is independent from
(X_{iT^{-1}},Y_{jT^{-1}})
.

For
i\in\{1,\dots,T\}
, we define
X^{i}
(resp.
Y^{i}
) as the restriction of
X
(resp.
Y
) to the interval
[(i-1)T^{-1},iT^{-1}]
.
We denote by
\theta_{X}^{i}(z)
the integer winding of
X^{i}
around
z
.
For two generic curves
\tilde{X}
and
\tilde{Y}
, we define the sets

\mathcal{D}^{\tilde{X}}_{n}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\theta_{\tilde{X}}(z)\geq n\},\quad\mathcal{D}^{{\tilde{X}},{\tilde{Y}}}_{n,m}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\theta_{\tilde{X}}(z)\geq n,\theta_{\tilde{Y}}(z)\geq m\}.

We replace the superscripts
X^{i}
and
X^{i},Y^{j}
with the superscripts
i
and
i,j
.

For each of these sets, we replace the curly letter with a straight one to designate its area: for example,
D^{i}_{n}=|\mathcal{D}^{i}_{n}|=|\mathcal{D}^{X^{i}}_{n}|
.

The
2
-dimensional heat kernel is denoted
p_{t}(x,y)=(2\pi t)^{-1}e^{-\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2t}}
, and we write
P_{t}f
for
p_{t}(0,\cdot)*f
.

In Section
3
, we will first show that the quantity

\Sigma_{n,m,T}=nm\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}D^{i,j}_{n,m}

is a good approximation of
nmD_{n,m}
. In Section
4
, we will give an asymptotic estimation of
\Sigma_{n,m,T}
, proving therefore the
L^{2}
convergence of
nmD^{X,Y}_{n,m}
. In Section
5
, we improve the convergence rate. In Section
6
, we extend the convergence to the
L^{p}
and almost sure sense. Finally, Section
7
is devoted to the convergences for the measure

\mu^{X,Y}_{n,m}
.

The idea behind our method, that we used previously in
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
, is that the winding
\theta_{X}(z)
is equal to the sum of all the windings
\theta^{i}_{X}
, plus a piecewise-linear part. When
\theta_{X}(z)
is large, it is in general only one of these pieces
X^{i}
which have a large winding, so that
\theta_{X}(z)
is then roughly equal to
\theta_{X}^{i}
. It follows that the set
\mathcal{D}^{X}_{N}
is roughly equal to the union of the sets
\mathcal{D}^{i}_{N}
, and that these sets are roughly disjoint, so that a kind of central limit theorem occurs.

Following again the ideas introduced in
[
18
]
, we first presume that neither the rate at which we can show
\Sigma_{n,m,T}
to converge, nor the choice of
T
we take, are actually relevant, as soon as the convergence rate is some power of
T
and
T
is some power of
m
. A rather simple procedure ultimately allows to drastically improve this convergent rate.

We arrange the families to have

Let

Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}

\displaystyle[\big(R^{\mathbf{i}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{i}_{d}}_{\mathbf{n}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{n}_{d}}\big)^{p}]=\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{P}_{x_{j}}\big(\forall l\in\{1,\dots,k_{j}\},\forall q\in\{1,\dots,p\},\ \theta_{X_{j}}^{i_{j,l}}(z_{q})\geq n_{j,l}\big)\,{\rm d}\mathfrak{z}

Remark 4.2
.

Many of the proofs we will present have the same structure, which therefore deserve a general explanation. We expend the square into a sum over four indices
i,j,k,l
, and we split this sum in four parts:

\{i=k,j=l\},\ \{i=k\mbox{ or }j=l\},\{i<k,j<l\mbox{ or }i>k,j<l\},

\{i<k,j>l\mbox{ or }i>k,j>l\}.

It will be useful to keep track of how many powers of
T
we should be able to save, and how many of them we need to save. For example, if we consider the sum with all the four indices
i=k,j\neq l
, we need save
3
powers of
T
so that the sum does not diverge, plus some extra power so that it actually goes to zero sufficiently fast. We will save a factor
T^{2}
from scaling. One should come from the fact that

has an extremely large probability to be
0
, unless
|X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}-Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}|
is of the order of
T^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, which happens with probability about
T^{-1}
(or
(i+j)^{-1}
around

).
The fourth one comes from the fact that the correlation between

and
D^{i,l}_{n,m}
decays at least as
|l-j|^{-1}
, which in computations is seen by the apparition of a kernel
p_{(l-j-1)T^{-1}}
which is integrated over a small ball of area about
T^{-1}
.

In some of the proofs, the contribution from the fourth part of the sum is easily shown to be equal to
0
. This, however, is not always the case, and we then have to get this factor
|l-j|^{-1}
, as well as a factor

. The difficulty, in the case

for example, is that the natural thing to do is to disintegrate with respect to the variables

instead of a Brownian motion. Because we are lacking, for Brownian bridges, the nice estimates in the mean that we have for the Brownian motion (see Corollary
4.6
in particular), one must avoid the apparition of such a bridge, or control how far it is from a Brownian motion, which can become tedious.

To be perfectly rigorous, we should also deal separately with the expressions when two indices differs by exactly
1
(e.g.

), or when one of them is equal to
1
(e.g.

). Yet, these cases are always treated identically to the other ones, except for the two following things: some heat kernels such as

or

degenerate, so that they should be interpreted as Dirac measures. The steps that consist in bounding the integral on some balls of such a kernel, by bounding the kernel itself by its maximum, should simply be replaced by the operation of bounding the integral directly by

5.
Implementing the bootstrap

The idea of the bootstrap is that the decomposition

D^{X,Y}_{n,m}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}D^{i,j}_{n,m}+O(n^{-\alpha}m^{-\beta}),

together with the
L^{2}
-asymptotics

D^{X,Y}_{n,m}=\frac{\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}{nm}+O(n^{-1}m^{-1-\epsilon})

and the asymptotic in mean

\mathbb{E}[D^{X,Y}_{n,m}]=\frac{\mathbb{E}[\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})]}{nm}+O(n^{-\gamma}m^{-\delta}),\gamma\geq\alpha,\delta\geq\beta,

should imply the
L^{2}
-asymptotics

D^{X,Y}_{n,m}=\frac{\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}{nm}+O(n^{-\alpha}m^{-\beta}).

Let us first explains shortly this idea for the set
D^{X}_{n}
, because this case is much simpler to deal with. Let us assume that we already know the
L^{2}
bounds

D^{X}_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}D^{i}_{n}+R_{n},\quad R_{n}=O(n^{-\alpha}),

D^{X}_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi n}+O(n^{-\alpha^{\prime}}),

and

\mathbb{E}[D^{X}_{n}]=\frac{1}{2\pi n}+O(n^{-\beta}),

with
\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha\leq\beta
. Then, we can improve the middle bound by applying it to the
D^{i}_{n}
, which are i.i.d. scaled copies of
D_{n}^{X}
,
D_{n}^{i}\overset{(d)}{=}T^{-1}D_{n}
.
We then get

\Var(D^{X}_{n})\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{T}\Var(D^{i}_{n})+2\Var(R_{n})=\frac{2}{T}\Var(D^{X}_{n})+O(n^{-2\alpha})=O(T^{-1}n^{-2\alpha^{\prime}}+n^{-2\alpha}),

so that
D^{X}_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi n}+O(n^{\alpha^{\prime}}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}+n^{-\alpha}+n^{-\beta})
: the worst term
n^{-\alpha^{\prime}}
has been improved.
5
5

5

In this particular case, one can simply put the expression

\frac{2}{T}\Var(D^{X}_{n})
on the other side of the equation, and directly obtain the bound we are searching for without any recursion, but this is a special case.

There are at least two additional difficulties that arises in our situation. First, the extra factor
T
that we gain during the bootstrap came from the independence between the different pieces
D^{i}_{n}
,
D^{j}_{n}
. On the opposite, we do not have independence between
D^{i,j}_{n,m}
and
D^{k,l}_{n,m}
, and we will have to show that the contribution from the covariances becomes small as
|i-k|
and
|j-l|
becomes large. Besides, the scaling relation is much less trivial in our situation: if we consider for example

\sup_{x,y}\mathbb{E}[D^{1,1}_{n,m}D^{1,1}_{n,m}],

this only scales as
T^{-2}
instead of
T^{-4}
, so that this extra scaling factor does not compensate the
T^{4}
factor coming from the sum over the indices
i,j,k,l
. There is indeed an extra
T^{-2}
factors coming from the fact that

D^{i,j}_{n,m}D^{k,l}_{n,m}
is vanishing as soon as
|X_{iT^{-1}}-Y_{jT^{-1}}|
or
|X_{kT^{-1}}-Y_{lT^{-1}}|
is large, but this is less simple to take into account.

\displaystyle\leq CT^{-3+4\epsilon}\frac{1}{(i-1)T^{-1}(j-1)T^{-1}}\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}[(Q^{X,Y}_{n,m})^{2}]+T^{-2}\hskip-8.5359pt\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}:|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\geq T^{\epsilon}}\hskip-8.5359pt\mathbb{E}_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}[(Q^{X,Y}_{n,m})^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}T^{-3+4\epsilon}\frac{1}{(i-1)T^{-1}(j-1)T^{-1}}n^{-\alpha}m^{-\beta}+T^{-r},

with
r>0
arbitrary.

\scriptstyle\diamond
We finally look at the expression
\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[Q^{i,j}_{n,m}Q^{i,l}_{n,m}]
, with
j<l
. Once again, we know that

\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[Q^{i,j}_{n,m}Q^{i,l}_{n,m}\mathbbm{1}_{|Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}-X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}|\geq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\mbox{ or }|Y_{(k-1)T^{-1}}-X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}|\geq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}]\leq T^{-r}.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[

\displaystyle Q^{i,j}_{n,m}Q^{i,l}_{n,m}\mathbbm{1}_{|Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}-X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}\mathbbm{1}_{|Y_{(k-1)T^{-1}}-X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}|\geq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x,y}(|Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}-X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})\sup_{z}\mathbb{P}_{z}(|Y_{(l-j)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[Q^{i,j}_{n,m}Q^{i,l}_{n,m}||Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}-X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon},|Y_{(k-1)T^{-1}}-X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}|\geq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CT^{-2+4\epsilon}}{(i-1)T^{-1}(j-i)T^{-1}}\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[(Q^{i,j}_{n,m})^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CT^{-2+4\epsilon}}{(i-1)T^{-1}(j-i)T^{-1}}n^{-\alpha}m^{-\beta}.

Summing over all the indices
i,j,k,l
, we obtain

\sum_{i,j,k,l}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[Q^{i,j}_{n,m}Q^{k,l}_{n,m}]\leq CT^{-1+\epsilon^{\prime}}n^{-\alpha}m^{-\beta}.

We conclude as in Lemma
4.1
.

∎

5.2.
Elimination of the indices
n,m

Now, we take a different turn than in the previous section. Trying to follow the same path as before would be doomed, because a quantity that would depend only on the
X_{iT^{-1}},Y_{jT^{-1}}
cannot be a sufficiently good approximation of
\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
unless
T
is large, which we don’t want.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\beta>0
, and assume that

\sup_{x,y}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[(nm\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[D^{X,Y}_{n,m}|\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})]-\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))^{2}]=O(m^{-\beta}).

Then, for all
\omega<\frac{1}{2}

\sup_{x,y}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}(nm\mathbb{E}[D^{i,j}_{n,m}|\ell^{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),X_{(i-1)T^{-1}},Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}]-\ell^{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))\Big)^{2}\Big]=O(T^{-\omega}m^{-\beta}+T^{2}m^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}}).

Proof.

We set

E^{i,j}_{n,m}=nm\mathbb{E}[D^{i,j}_{n,m}|\ell^{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),X_{(i-1)T^{-1}},Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}]-\ell^{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),

and

F^{i,j}_{n,m}=E^{i,j}_{n,m}\mathbbm{1}_{|X_{iT^{-1}}-X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}\mathbbm{1}_{|Y_{jT^{-1}}-Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}\mathbbm{1}_{|X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}-Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}.

In this proof,
\sup_{y^{\prime}_{1}}
always means
\sup_{y^{\prime}_{1}\in B(y_{1},T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})}
.

\scriptstyle\diamond
As usual, the case
i<k-1
,
j<l-1
is easily dealt with. Let
Z=(X_{iT^{-1}},Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}})
.
Then,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\big[F^{k,l}_{n,m}\big|Z\big]|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\big(|X_{(k-1)T^{-1}}-Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\big|Z\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}|\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\big[F^{k,l}_{n,m}\big|X_{(k-1)T^{-1}}=x^{\prime},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}=y^{\prime}\big]|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CT^{-1+2\epsilon}}{(k+l-i-j-2)T^{-1}}T^{-1}\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}|\mathbb{E}_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\big[F^{X,Y}_{n,m}]|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}T^{-2+2\epsilon}}{(k+l-i-j-2)T^{-1}}m^{-\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\mbox{using Corollary \ref{coro:werner}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}F^{k,l}_{n,m}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\big[F^{i,j}_{n,m}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\big[F^{k,l}_{n,m}\big|Z\big]\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}T^{-2+2\epsilon}}{(k+l-i-j-2)T^{-1}}m^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[|F^{i,j}_{n,m}|]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}T^{-2+2\epsilon}}{(k+l-i-j-2)T^{-1}}m^{-\frac{1}{2}}T^{-1}\mathbb{P}(|X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}-Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})

\displaystyle\hskip 241.84842pt\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}[(F^{i,j}_{n,m})^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime\prime}T^{-4+4\epsilon}}{(k+l-i-j-2)(i-j-2)T^{-2}}m^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}}.

The sum over
i,j,k,l
is then smaller than

CT^{4\epsilon}m^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}}.

Remark that we cannot get a factor
m^{-1}
because of the absolute value around
F^{i,j}_{n,m}
, and that our bound would not have been sufficient if we would also have absolute values around
F^{k,l}_{n,m}
: keeping one of this term with no absolute value is the requirement that makes the next case difficult to deal with.

\scriptstyle\diamond
We now assume
i<k-1
,
j>l
, but for once we also assume
j-l>T^{\delta}
, for some fixed but arbitrary
\delta>0
.

Let
Z=(X_{iT^{-1}},Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}})
,
W=(Z,Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}})
and
\mathcal{F}=\sigma(Z,(X_{t})_{t<iT^{-1}},(Y_{s})_{s>(j-1)T^{-1}})
.
Remark that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}F^{k,l}_{n,m}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{k,l}_{n,m}|\mathcal{F}]]=\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{k,l}_{n,m}|Z]]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}|Z]\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{k,l}_{n,m}|Z]].

Disintegrating
\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}F^{k,l}_{n,m}]
with respect to
Z=(X_{iT^{-1}},Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}})
, and disintegrating then

\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{k,l}_{n,m}|Z=(x_{1},y_{2})]
with respect to
Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}F^{k,l}_{n,m}]

\displaystyle=\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}}p_{iT^{-1}}(x,x_{1})p_{(j-1)T^{-1}}(y,y_{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}|Z=(x_{1},y_{2})]\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{k,l}_{n,m}|Z=(x_{1},y_{2})]\,{\rm d}x_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{2},

\displaystyle=\int_{H}p_{iT^{-1}}(x,x_{1})p_{(l-1)T^{-1}}(y,y_{1})p_{(j-l)T^{-1}}(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}|Z=(x_{1},y_{2})]\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{k,l}_{n,m}|W=(x_{1},y_{2},y_{1})]\,{\rm d}x_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{2},

where
H\subseteq({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{3}
is the set of triples
(x_{1},y_{1},y_{2})
with
|y_{2}-x_{1}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}
.
Under

\mathbb{P}_{x,y}(\ \cdot\ |W=(x_{1},y_{2},y_{1})),

(X_{s})_{s\geq{iT^{-1}}}
is a Brownian motion started from
x_{1}
, and
(Y_{t})_{t\in[(l-1)T^{-1},(j-1)T^{-1}]}
is a Brownian bridge from
y_{1}
to
y_{2}
of duration
j-l
. When further restricted to

t\in[(l-1)T^{-1},lT^{-1}]
,
its distribution becomes absolutely continuous with respect to the one of the Brownian motion started from
y_{1}
, with Radon-Nikodym derivative given by

\Big(\frac{\mathbb{P}_{y}(\ \cdot\ |W=(x_{1},y_{2},y_{1}))}{\mathbb{P}_{y_{1}}}\Big)_{\big|\sigma((Y_{t})_{t\in[(l-1)T^{-1},lT^{-1}]}}=\frac{p_{(j-l-1)T^{-1}}(Y_{lT^{-1}},y_{2})}{p_{(j-l)T^{-1}}(y_{1},y_{2})}.

For any two measures
\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}
and variable
X
(not necessarily positive),

|\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[X]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[X]|=\Big|\int X(\omega)\big(\frac{\,{\rm d}\mathbb{Q}}{\,{\rm d}\mathbb{P}}(\omega)-1\Big)\,{\rm d}\mathbb{P}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[X^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\Big[\Big(\frac{\,{\rm d}\mathbb{Q}}{\,{\rm d}\mathbb{P}}-1\Big)^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In our case, the second factor reads

A(y_{1},y_{2})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Big(\frac{p_{(j-l-1)T^{-1}}(y,y_{2})}{p_{(j-l)T^{-1}}(y_{1},y_{2})}-1\Big)^{2}p_{T^{-1}}(y_{1},y)\,{\rm d}y.

The most simple way to compute this integral is to develop the square, and to us the fact that
p_{t}(x,y)^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi t}p_{t/2}(x,y)
.
All computations done, we get

A(y_{1},y_{2})=\frac{j-l-1}{j-l+1}p_{(j-l-1)T^{-1}}(y_{1},y_{2})-p_{(j-l)T^{-1}}(y_{1},y_{2}).

We deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}F^{k,l}_{n,m}]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{H}p_{iT^{-1}}(x,x_{1})p_{(l-1)T^{-1}}(y,y_{1})p_{(j-l)T^{-1}}(y_{1},y_{2})\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}|Z=(x_{1},y_{2})]

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{k,l}_{n,m}|X_{iT^{-1}}=x_{1},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}=y_{1}]\,{\rm d}x_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{H}p_{iT^{-1}}(x,x_{1})p_{(l-1)T^{-1}}(y,y_{1})A(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt|\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}|Z=(x_{1},y_{2})]|\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\big[(F^{k,l}_{n,m})^{2}\big|X_{iT^{-1}}=x_{1},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}=y_{1}\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}x_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{2}

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}.

For the first term
I_{1}
, we can integrate back with respect to
y_{2}
, so that

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}}p_{iT^{-1}}(x,x_{1})p_{(l-1)T^{-1}}(y,y_{1})\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}|X_{iT^{-1}}=x_{1},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}=y_{1}]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{k,l}_{n,m}|X_{iT^{-1}}=x_{1},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}=y_{1}]\,{\rm d}x_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[\mathbb{E}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}|X_{iT^{-1}},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}]\mathbb{E}[F^{k,l}_{n,m}|X_{iT^{-1}},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}]]

Now,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}\big[F^{k,l}_{n,m}\big|X_{iT^{-1}},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}\big]|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathbb{E}\big[F^{k,l}_{n,m}\big|X_{(k-1)T^{-1}},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}\big]|\big|X_{iT^{-1}},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}\big]

\displaystyle\leq CT^{-1}m^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbb{E}[|E^{k,l}_{n,m}-F^{k,l}_{n,m}|\big|X_{iT^{-1}},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}\big]|.

Proceeding as we already did several times, we easily show that
\mathbb{E}[|E^{k,l}_{n,m}-F^{k,l}_{n,m}|^{2}]
decays to
0
more quickly than any power of
T
,
and we deduce that

I_{1}\leq(CT^{-1}m^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbb{E}[|E^{k,l}_{n,m}-F^{k,l}_{n,m}|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}})\mathbb{E}[(E^{i,j}_{n,m})^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C^{\prime}T^{-2}m^{-\frac{1}{2}}m^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}.

For the second term
I_{2}
, we split
H
into
H^{\prime}\sqcup H^{\prime\prime}
with

H^{\prime}=\{(x_{1},y_{1},y_{2})\in H:|y_{1}-y_{2}|\leq(j-l)^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}}\},

and we decompose
I_{2}
into
I_{2}^{\prime}+I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
accordingly. We first have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\big[(F^{k,l}_{n,m})^{2}\big|X_{iT^{-1}}=x_{1},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}=y_{1}\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq T^{-1}\mathbb{P}(|X_{(k-1)T^{-1}}-Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\big[(E^{X,Y}_{n,m})^{2}\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

(10)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{T^{-\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}}{(k-j)^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}}m^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}.

On
H^{\prime}
,
A(y_{1},y_{2})
is less than

(11)

\frac{(j-l)^{2}}{(j-l)^{2}-1}\exp(\frac{T^{\delta}}{j-l})-1\leq C\frac{T^{\delta}}{j-l}.

Using (
11
) and (
10
), we obtain

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{T^{-\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}}{(k-j)^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}}m^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\frac{T^{\delta}}{j-l}\int_{H}p_{iT^{-1}}(x,x_{1})p_{(l-1)T^{-1}}(y,y_{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\big[|F^{i,j}_{n,m}|\big|X_{iT^{-1}}=x_{1},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}=y_{1}\big]\,{\rm d}x_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\frac{T^{-\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}}{(k-j)^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}}m^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\frac{T^{\delta}}{j-l}(j-l)T^{-1+\delta}\int_{({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}}p_{iT^{-1}}(x,x_{1})p_{(l-1)T^{-1}}(y,y_{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\big[|F^{i,j}_{n,m}|\big|X_{iT^{-1}}=x_{1},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}=y_{1}\big]\,{\rm d}x_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{1}

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}\frac{T^{-\frac{5}{2}+\epsilon+2\delta}}{(k-j)^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}}m^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\big[|F^{i,j}_{n,m}|\big]

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}\frac{T^{-\frac{5}{2}+\epsilon+2\delta}}{(k-j)^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}}m^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\frac{T^{-2+2\epsilon}}{(i+j)T^{-1}}\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\big[(F^{X,Y}_{n,m})^{2}\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{(3)}\frac{T^{-\frac{9}{2}+3\epsilon+2\delta}}{(k-j)^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}(i+j)T^{-1}}m^{-\beta}.

On
H^{\prime\prime}
, we simply bound
A(y_{1},y_{2})
by

p_{(j-l-1)T^{-1}}(y_{1},y_{2})+p_{(j-l)T^{-1}}(y_{1},y_{2}).

For any
\delta>0
, the integral over
y_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus B(y_{1},(j-l)^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta})
decays more quickly than any power of
T
, and we get

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{T^{-2+2\epsilon}}{(k-j)^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}}m^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\int_{H^{\prime}}p_{iT^{-1}}(x,x_{1})p_{(l-1)T^{-1}}(y,y_{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt(p_{(j-l)T^{-1}}(y_{1},y_{2})+p_{(j-l-1)T^{-1}}(y_{1}-T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(y_{1}-y_{2}),y_{2}))

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\big[|F^{i,j}_{n,m}|\big|X_{iT^{-1}}=x_{1},Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}=y_{1}\big]\,{\rm d}x_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{2}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{-r}.

All together, and using again that
\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[(E^{i,j}_{n,m}-F^{i,j}_{n,m})^{2}]
decays more quickly than any power of
T
, we have

\lx@nobreakspace\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[E^{i,j}_{n,m}E^{k,l}_{n,m}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[F^{i,j}_{n,m}F^{k,l}_{n,m}]+O(T^{-r})\leq+CT^{-2}m^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}}+C\frac{T^{-\frac{9}{2}+3\epsilon+2\delta}}{(k-j)^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}(i+j)T^{-1}}m^{-\beta}.

The sum over all
i<k,j>l+T^{\delta}
is less than

C^{\prime}(T^{2}m^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}}+T^{-\frac{1}{2}+3\epsilon+2\delta}m^{-\beta}).

\scriptstyle\diamond
Of course, we can interchange the roles of
(i,j)
and
(l,k)
, so we can bound identically the sum over
i+T^{\delta}<k
,
j>l
, so that there only remains the about
T^{2+2\delta}
quadruples with
|j-l|<T^{\delta}
,
|k-i|<T^{\delta}
. We now deal with them.

First, disintegrating with respect to
U=(X_{(i-1)T^{-1}},Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}})
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[(E^{i,j}_{n,m})^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x,y}(|X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}-Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[(E^{i,j}_{n,m})^{2}|U=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})]+O(T^{-r})

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{T^{-1+2\epsilon}}{(i-1)T^{-1}(j-1)T^{-1}}T^{-2}\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}[(E^{X,Y}_{n,m})^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\frac{T^{-\frac{5}{2}+2\epsilon}}{(i-1)T^{-1}(j-1)T^{-1}}m^{-\beta}.

From Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we deduce the same bound for

\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[E^{i,j}_{n,m}E^{k,l}_{n,m}]
. The sum over the quadruples
(i,j,k,l)
with
|j-l|<T^{\delta}
,
|k-i|<T^{\delta}
is therefore less than

C^{\prime\prime}\log(T)^{2}T^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\epsilon+2\delta}m^{-\beta}.

\scriptstyle\diamond
It remains to deal with the terms
\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[E^{i,j}_{n,m}E^{i,l}_{n,m}]
. We assume
l<j
.
Set

U=(X_{(i-1)T^{-1}},Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}),\mbox{ and }Z=Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}-Y_{jT^{-1}}.

Remark that for
E^{i,j}_{n,m}E^{i,l}_{n,m}
to be nonzero, it requires both
|X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}-Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}|

and
|X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}-Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}|
to be small, in which case
Z
is small as well.
Then, disintegrating with respect to
U
and
Z
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[E^{i,j}_{n,m}E^{i,l}_{n,m}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x,y}(|X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}-Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})\mathbb{P}_{x,y}(|Z|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[E^{i,j}_{n,m}E^{i,l}_{n,m}|U=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),Z=z]+O(T^{-r})

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{T^{-2+2\epsilon}}{(i+j)T^{-1}(l-j)T^{-1}}T^{-2}\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}[(E^{X,Y}_{n,m})^{2}]+O(T^{-r})

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{T^{-4+2\epsilon}}{(i+j)T^{-1}(l-j)T^{-1}}m^{-\beta}.

The sum of these terms over
i,j,l
is less than

C^{\prime}\log(T)T^{-1+2\epsilon}m^{-\beta},

which concludes the proof.
∎

5.3.
End of the bootstrap

From the two previous lemma
5.1
and
5.2
, we easily deduce the following.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\beta>0
and assume that

\sup_{x,y}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\Big[\Big(nmD^{X,Y}_{n,m}-\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq O(m^{-\beta}).

Then, for all

i\neq k,j\neq l\end{subarray}}^{T}\mathbb{P}_{x,y}(E^{i,j}\geq\frac{Cm^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}{4}T^{-1-\epsilon^{\prime}},E^{k,l}\geq\frac{m^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}{4}T^{-1-\epsilon^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt|X_{(i-1)T^{-1}}-Y_{(j-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon^{\prime\prime}},|X_{(k-1)T^{-1}}-Y_{(l-1)T^{-1}}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j,k,l=1\\
i\neq k,j\neq l\end{subarray}}^{T}2\Big(1+\frac{T^{-1+2\epsilon^{\prime\prime}}}{2(k-i)T^{-1}}\Big)\sup_{x,y}\mathbb{P}_{x,y}(E^{i,j}\geq\frac{Cm^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}{4}T^{-1-\epsilon^{\prime}})\sup_{x,y}\mathbb{P}_{x,y}(E^{k,l}\geq\frac{Cm^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}{4}T^{-1-\epsilon^{\prime}})

(using Sublemma
6.3
)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\log(T)T^{3+2\epsilon^{\prime\prime}}(p^{+}_{n,\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\omega,\frac{C}{4}})^{2}\leq C^{\prime\prime}T^{4}(p_{n,\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\omega,\frac{C}{4}}^{+})^{2}.

Finally, for
q
arbitrary

p^{+}_{n,\epsilon,C}\leq C^{\prime}T^{-q}+T^{2}p^{+}_{n,\epsilon+\frac{\omega}{2},\frac{C}{4}}+C^{\prime}T^{4}(p^{+}_{n,\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\omega,\frac{C}{4}})^{2}.

Setting

r(\epsilon)=\sup\Big\{r:\exists C:\forall n,m,\sup_{x,y}\mathbb{P}_{x,y}(nmD^{x,y}_{n,m}-\frac{\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}{\geq}C^{\prime}m^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})\leq Cm^{-r}\Big\},

which does not depend on
C^{\prime}
, we get

r(\epsilon)\leq\min(\omega q,-2\omega+r(\epsilon+\frac{\omega}{2}),-4\omega+2r(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\omega).

Since
q
is arbitrary,

r(\epsilon)\leq\min(-2\omega+r(\epsilon+\frac{\omega}{2}),-4\omega+2r(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}\omega).

Set
\epsilon_{0}=\inf\{\epsilon:r(\epsilon)=\infty
, and assume
\epsilon_{0}>0
. Let then
\omega=\frac{1}{7}r(\epsilon_{0}/2)
. Remark that we
know
\epsilon_{0}<\infty
(and actually,
\epsilon_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}
) from the first case we treated, and we know
\omega>0
from the second case we treated.

Then, for all
\epsilon\in\big(\max(\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2},\epsilon_{0}-\frac{\omega}{2}),\epsilon_{0}\big)
which is a continuity point of
r
, taking
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}
such that
r(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\omega)>\frac{6}{7}r(x)
,
we have
2r(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\omega)-4\omega>\frac{8}{7}r(x)
, so that

r(\epsilon)\geq r(\epsilon+\omega\epsilon^{\prime})-2\omega
. Yet,
\epsilon+\omega\epsilon^{\prime}>\epsilon_{0}
so that
r(\epsilon)=\infty
. This contradicts the minimality of
\epsilon_{0}
. We can conclude that
r
has no continuity point on
\big(\max(\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2},\epsilon_{0}-\frac{\omega}{2}),\epsilon_{0}\big)
, which is absurd since
r
is monotonic. We can conclude that
\epsilon_{0}=0
.

The probability that
\frac{\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}{4\pi^{2}}-nmD^{X,Y}_{n,m}\geq m^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}
is controlled in an identical way, and that concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

For all
p\in[1,\infty)
, for all

,

\sup_{x,y}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\big[\big|m^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}|(mD^{X,Y}_{n,m}-\ell^{X,Y}_{n,m}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))\big|^{p}]\underset{n^{c}<m<n}{=}O(m^{-r}).

The almost sure asymptotic then follows directly from Lemma 4.1 in

18
]
:

Lemma 6.5
.

Let

be a random sequence which is almost surely decreasing and takes non-negative values. Assume that there exists

,

and

such that, for all

Then, for

\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{N\geq N_{0}}N^{q}|ND_{N}-C|^{p}\big]\underset{N_{0}\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Remark that, in
[

]
,

was thought of as deterministic, but neither the lemma nor the proof use such a condition.

Recall that
m=m(n)
, and that we have fix some

. We apply Lemma

.

7.
Convergence for the joint winding measure

The space of finite measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is endowed
with the
1
-Wasserstein distance

W_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f\,{\rm d}(\mu-\nu):f\mbox{ $1$-Lipschitz},f(0)=0\}.

Remark that the condition
f(0)=0
(or a similar normalisation condition) is necessary to deal with non-probability measures.

7.1.
Convergence in
L^{2}

Our goal in this section is to show that for all
p\geq 1
, the normalised joint winding measure
\mu_{n,m}=nm\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{D}_{n,m}}\,{\rm d}z
converges in
L^{p}
, for the
1
-Wasserstein distance, toward the intersection measure
\ell^{X,Y}
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
\mu_{n,m}^{i,j}=nm\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{D}^{i,j}_{n,m}}\,{\rm d}z
and let
\ell_{i,j}
be te intersection measure of
X_{i}
and
Y_{j}
. Let also
n^{\pm}=n+\pm\sqrt{n}(T+1)
,
m^{\pm}=n\pm\sqrt{n}(T+1)
, and
f^{\pm}
be the positive (resp. negative) part of
f
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,m}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}{\ell}_{X,Y}\Big|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,m}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\mu_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}^{i,j}\Big|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\mu^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}-f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}})nmD^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}\Big)\Big|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\Big(f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}})(nmD^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}-\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))\Big)\Big|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\Big(f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}})\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})-\int f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\ell_{i,j}\Big)\Big|^{p}\Big]\Big).

\scriptstyle\diamond
To control the first term, remark first that the supremum of
|f^{\pm}|
over
B(0,K)
is smaller than
K
, so that the essential supremum of
|f^{\pm}|
over the measures we look at is smaller than
\|X\|_{\infty}
. We deduce that

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,m}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\mu_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}^{i,j}\Big|\leq nm\|X\|_{\infty}R,

with
R
given in Proposition
3.5
.
We get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,m}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\mu_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}^{i,j}\Big|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq n^{p}m^{p}\mathbb{E}\big[\|X\|_{\infty}^{2}R^{p}\big]\leq n^{p}m^{p}\mathbb{E}\big[\|X\|_{\infty}^{4}\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[R^{2p}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\log(T)^{c}T^{3p-2}m^{-p}.

\scriptstyle\diamond
We now look at the second term. For
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\mu^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}-f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}})nmD^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(f^{\pm}(z)-f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}}))\,{\rm d}\mu^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}(z)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|z-X_{iT^{-1}}|\,{\rm d}\mu^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}T^{-\alpha}\mu^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}T^{-\alpha}nmD^{i,j}_{n,m},

so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\Big[\Big|\!\sum_{i,j}\!\big(\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\!f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\mu^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}\!-\!f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}})nm

\displaystyle D^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}\!\big)\!\Big|^{p}\Big]\leq T^{-p\alpha}\mathbb{E}[\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}^{2p}]^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{p}m^{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\!\big(\sum_{i,j}D^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}\big)^{2p}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{-\frac{p}{2}+p\epsilon}n^{p}m^{p}\big(\mathbb{E}[(D_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}^{X,Y})^{2p}]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbb{E}\big[R^{2p}\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\log(n)^{c}T^{-\frac{p}{2}+p\epsilon}\qquad\mbox{(using Lemma \ref{le:globalBound}).}

\scriptstyle\diamond
We now look at the third term. We decomposite it into two parts, depending on
\|X\|_{\infty}
. First,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\|X\|_{\infty}\leq T^{\epsilon}}\Big|\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\Big(f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}})(nmD^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}-

\displaystyle\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))\Big)\Big|^{p}\Big]\leq T^{2p+2\epsilon}\max_{i,j}\mathbb{E}\big[|nmD^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}-\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})|^{p}\big]

\displaystyle\leq T^{p+2\epsilon}\sup_{x,y}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\big[|nmD^{X,Y}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}-\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})|^{p}]

\displaystyle\leq CT^{p+2\epsilon}m^{-p+\epsilon}.

Secondly, for
r
arbitrary large, there exists
C
such that for all
T
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\|X\|_{\infty}\geq T^{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\Big(f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}})(nmD^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}-\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))\Big)\Big|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(\mathbbm{1}_{\|X\|_{\infty}\geq T^{\epsilon}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\Big(f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}})(nmD^{i,j}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}-\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))\Big)\Big|^{2p}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{-r}T^{p}\sup_{x,y}\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\Big[|nmD^{X,Y}_{n^{\pm},m^{\pm}}-\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})|^{2p}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}T^{-r}T^{p}\log(n)^{c}n^{p}m^{p}.

\scriptstyle\diamond
Finally,

\displaystyle|f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}})\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})-\int f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\ell_{i,j}|

\displaystyle\leq\int|f^{\pm}-f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}})|\,{\rm d}\ell_{i,j}

\displaystyle\leq T^{-\alpha}\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),

so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\Big(f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}})\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})-\int f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\ell_{i,j}\Big)\Big|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq T^{-\frac{p}{2}+p\epsilon}\mathbb{E}\Big[\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}^{p}\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\Big)^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle=T^{-\frac{p}{2}+p\epsilon}\mathbb{E}[\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}^{p}\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq T^{-\frac{p}{2}+p\epsilon}\mathbb{E}[\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}^{2p}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}[\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2p}]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

All together, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,m}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}{\ell}_{X,Y}\Big|^{p}\Big]\leq C(\log(T)^{c}T^{3p-2}m^{-p}+T^{-\frac{p}{2}+p\epsilon}+T^{p+2p\epsilon}m^{-p+\epsilon}).

For
T
a small enough power of
m
and
\epsilon
sufficiently small, this goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
, which concludes the proof.
∎

7.2.
Almost sure convergence

Our goal in this section is to show that almost surely,
\mu_{n,m}
converges toward
\ell^{X,Y}
, for the
1
-Wasserstein distance –or equivalently, in the weak sense.

Proof.

We follow the same pattern as in the previous proof. We fix
\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
and some constants
K,K_{\alpha},l,D
, and we work on the intersection of the event
\|X\|_{\infty}\leq K
,
\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\leq K_{\alpha}
,
\ell^{X,Y}\leq l
,
|nmD_{n,m}-\ell^{X,Y}|\leq D
. We assume the function
f
is positive, the negative part is treated similarily.

Let us denote
R=R_{n,m}
the random variable that appears in Proposition
3.5
. Remark that, for all
\epsilon>0
and
p>1
,

\mathbb{P}(nmR_{n,m}\geq m^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[(nmR_{n,m})^{p}]}{m^{-\frac{p}{2}+p\epsilon}}\leq C\log(T)^{c}T^{3p-2}m^{-p\epsilon},

so that this probability decays more quickly than any power of
m
, and therefore more quickly than any power of
n
(recall once gain that
m\geq n^{\epsilon^{\prime}}
for some
\epsilon^{\prime}
). It follows that

\mathbb{P}(\exists n\geq n_{0}:nmR_{n,m}\geq m^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}).

decays more quickly than any power of
n_{0}
, and in particular tends to
0
as
n_{0}\to\infty
. We can therefore fix
n_{0}
and work on the event

\forall n\geq n_{0},nmR_{n,m}\leq m^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}.

Finally, for
\epsilon>0

\mathbb{P}(\exists i,j\in\{,\dots,t\}:|nmD^{i,j}_{n,m}-\ell^{i,j}_{n,m}|\geq T^{-1}m^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})\leq T^{2}\sup_{x,y}\mathbb{P}_{x,y}(|nmD^{X,Y}_{n,m}-\ell^{X,Y}_{n,m}|\geq m^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}).

From Lemma
6.1
, we know the right-hand side decays to
0
more quickly than any power of
m
. We can therefore fix
n_{1}
and work on the event

\forall n\geq n_{1},\forall i,j\in\{1,\dots,T\},|nmD^{i,j}_{n,m}-\ell^{i,j}_{n,m}|\geq T^{-1}m^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}.

In the following, we assume
n\geq n_{0}
and
n\geq n_{1}
.

\scriptstyle\diamond
We deduce

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,m}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f\,{\rm d}\mu_{n,m}^{i,j}\Big|\leq nm\|X\|_{\infty}R_{n,m}\leq Km^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

\scriptstyle\diamond
Besides,

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f\,{\rm d}\mu^{i,j}_{n,m}-f(X_{iT^{-1}})nmD^{i,j}_{n,m}\Big|\leq\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}T^{-\alpha}nmD^{i,j}_{n,m},

so that

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f\,{\rm d}\mu^{i,j}_{n,m}-f(X_{iT^{-1}})nmD^{i,j}_{n,m}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}T^{-\alpha}nm\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}D^{i,j}_{n,m}\leq\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}T^{-\alpha}nm(D_{n^{-},m^{-}}+R)

\displaystyle\leq K_{\alpha}(D+1)T^{-\alpha}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

\scriptstyle\diamond
Besides,

\Big|\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\Big(f(X_{iT^{-1}})(nmD^{i,j}_{n,m}-\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))\Big)\Big|\leq K\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}|nmD^{i,j}_{n,m}-\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})|\leq KTm^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

\scriptstyle\diamond
Finally,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}|f^{\pm}(X_{iT^{-1}})\ell_{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})-\int f^{\pm}\,{\rm d}\ell_{i,j}|\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}T^{-\alpha}\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\ell^{i,j}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})=T^{-\alpha}\|X\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\ell^{X,Y}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0,

which concludes the proof.
∎

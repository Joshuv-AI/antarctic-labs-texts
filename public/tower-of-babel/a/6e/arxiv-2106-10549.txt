Abstract
In 1970, Davis and Knuth introduced the concept of revolving sequences to represent Gaussian integers. Much later, Kawamura and Allen recently generalized this idea to a wider class of revolving sequences that parametrize certain self-similar fractals including the Levy Dragon and Tiling Dragon, which are the unique compact solution of certain families of Iterated Function Systems. In this paper, we build on the work of Kawamura and Allen to include a wider collection of Iterated Function Systems and introduce a new type of revolving sequence which parametrizes a different family of self-similar fractals including the Terdragon.



Introduction

The history of systematic mathematical research on self-similar fractals dates back to 1981, when Hutchinson proved the following celebrated theorem
[
4
]
: For any finite family of similar contractions
\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\dots,\psi_{m}\}
on
\mathrm{I\!R}^{n}
, which is called an iterated function system or IFS, there exists a unique non-empty compact solution
X
of the set equation:

X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\psi_{i}(X).

We call
X
an attractor or a self-similar set for the IFS.

Before Hutchinson, it is worth mentioning that Williams
[
8
]
in 1971 essentially proved that the following set is a self-similar set.

X=\text{closure}\Big(\bigcup_{1\leq i_{1},i_{2},\cdots i_{n}\leq m}\text{Fix}(\psi_{i_{1}}\circ\psi_{i_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{i_{n}})\Big),

where
\text{Fix}(\psi_{i})
is the unique fixed point of
\psi_{i}
.

Around the same time, in 1970, C. Davis and D. E. Knuth
[
1
]
introduced the notion of
revolving representations
of a Gaussian integer: for any
z=x+iy
with
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a revolving sequence
(\delta_{0},\delta_{1},\dots\delta_{n})
such that

z=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\delta_{n-k}(1+i)^{k},

where
\delta_{k}\in\{0,1,-1,i,-i\}
with the restriction that the non-zero values must follow the cyclic pattern from left to right:

\cdots\to 1\to(-i)\to(-1)\to i\to 1\to\cdots.

Notice that a revolving sequence is created by a 90 degree angle of rotation.

Recently, Kawamura and Allen
[
6
]
defined
generalized revolving sequences
, where the 90 degree angle of rotation is replaced with a more general angle
\theta
where
|\theta|=\frac{2\pi q}{p}\leq\pi
. In other words,
\delta_{k}\in\{0,1,e^{i\theta},e^{2i\theta},\cdots,e^{(p-1)i\theta}\}
with the restriction that the non-zero values must follow the cyclic pattern from left to right:

\cdots\to 1\to e^{i\theta}\to e^{2i\theta}\to e^{3i\theta}\to e^{4i\theta}\to\cdots.

Define
W_{\theta}
as the set of all generalized revolving sequences with parameter
\theta
. Kawamura and Allen considered a relationship between
W_{\theta}
and self-similar sets and found new parametrized expressions for certain self-similar sets.

Theorem 0.1

(Kawamura-Allen)
.

Let
X_{1,\alpha,\theta}
be the self-similar set generated by the iterated function system (IFS):

\begin{cases}\psi_{1}(z)=\alpha z,&\\
\psi_{2}(z)=(\alpha e^{i\theta})z+\alpha,\end{cases}

(0.1)

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|\alpha|<1
.

The self-similar set
X_{1,\alpha,\theta}
has the following parametrized expression:

X_{1,\alpha,\theta}=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\alpha^{n}:\delta_{j_{1}}=1,(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots)\in W_{\theta}\right\},

(0.2)

where
j_{1}:=\min\{j:\delta_{j}\not=0\}
.

Notice that each generalized revolving sequence determines a complex number and
X_{1,\alpha,\theta}
is a set of points in the complex plane.
In particular, if
\alpha=(1-i)/2
and
\theta=-\pi/2
,
X_{1,\alpha,\theta}
is a Tiling dragon
[
7
]
. If
\alpha=(1-i)/2
and
\theta=\pi/2
, then
X_{1,\alpha,\theta}
is Lévy’s dragon curve, which is a continuous curve but with positive area
[
5
]
.

Figure 1:
X_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}:\alpha=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i}{2},\theta=-\pi/2
(left) and
\theta=\pi/2
(right).

In addition to these two dragons, there are three other famous dragons: the Heighway dragon, the Twindragon and Terdragon. (See
[
2
]
for more details). Motivated by these three dragons, in this paper we build on the work of Kawamura and Allen to include a wider collection of self-similar sets.

Section 1 gives the parametric expression of certain self-similar sets, including the Heighway dragon and the Twindragon, generated by the IFS:

\begin{cases}\phi_{1}(z)=\alpha z,&\\
\phi_{2}(z)=(\alpha e^{i\theta})z+1.\end{cases}

Interestingly, the parametric expression is given by only changing the initial index in the sum from
n=1
to
n=0
in (
0.2
). This result suggests a natural question: Does changing the constant term in the IFS from
\alpha
to
1
always generate a geometrically similar attractor? Is it always true that this small change does not influence the parametrized expression for certain self-similar sets?
These questions are discussed in Section 2.

In section 3, we finally study the Terdragon, a more challenging self-similar set generated by the following set of three contractions:

\begin{cases}\phi_{1}(z)=\alpha z,&\\
\phi_{2}(z)=(\alpha e^{i\theta})z+\alpha,&\\
\phi_{3}(z)=\alpha z+\beta,\end{cases}

where
\alpha=1/2-(\sqrt{3}/6)i,\beta=1/2+(\sqrt{3}/6)i
and
\theta=2\pi/3
. We introduce a new type of revolving sequence which parametrizes this different family of self-similar sets.

1
The Heighway dragon and Twindragon

Before stating our results, some notation needs to be introduced. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
denote a complex parameter satisfying
|\alpha|<1
. Let
\theta
be an angle with
-\pi<\theta\leq\pi
and a rational multiple of
2\pi
; that is
|\theta|=(2\pi q)/p
where
p\in\mathrm{I\!N},q\in\mathrm{I\!N}_{0}
. Define

\Delta_{\theta}:=\{0,1,e^{i\theta},e^{2i\theta},\cdots e^{(p-1)i\theta}\}.

Definition 1.1
.

A sequence
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots)\in\{0,1,e^{i\theta},e^{2i\theta},\cdots,e^{(p-1)i\theta}\}^{\mathrm{I\!N}}
satisfies the
Generalized Revolving Condition (GRC) if the subsequence obtained after the removal of its zero elements is a (finite or infinite) truncation of the sequence
(e^{ni\theta})
. More precisely, let
(n_{i})
be the sequence of indices
n
such that
\delta_{n}\neq 0
, listed in increasing order. Then
\delta_{n_{i+1}}=e^{i\theta}\delta_{n_{i}}
for each
i
.

The Heighway dragon was first discovered by a physicist J. Heighway. M. Gardner described that the Heighway dragon is the shape that is generated from repeatedly folding a strip of paper in half
[
3
]
. Many of its properties were first investigated by Davis and Knuth. Motivated by the Heighway dragon, Davis and Knuth also constructed another dragon, called the Twindragon
[
1
]
.

Figure 2:
The Heighway dragon and the Twindragon

The Heighway dragon and the Twindragon are both self-similar sets, not generated by (
0.1
), but by a slightly different pair of two similar contractions:

\begin{cases}\phi_{1}(z)=\alpha z,&\\
\phi_{2}(z)=(\alpha e^{i\theta})z+1.\end{cases}

(1.1)

In particular, if
\alpha=(1+i)/2
and
\theta=\pi/2
, then (
1.1
) generates the Heighway dragon. If
\alpha=(1+i)/2
and
\theta=\pi
, then (
1.1
) generates the Twindragon
[
1
]
.

The following questions arise naturally. How can we give a parametrized expression for self-similar sets generated by the IFS (
1.1
)? Does this small change from
\alpha
to
1
give any influence to the relationship with generalized revolving sequences?

Let
H_{1,\alpha,\theta}
be the self-similar set generated by the IFS (
1.1
). Notice that
\psi_{1}(z)=\alpha\phi_{1}(z/\alpha)
and
\psi_{2}(z)=\alpha\phi_{2}(z/\alpha)
in (
0.1
) and (
1.1
). Thus,

X_{1,\alpha,\theta}=\psi_{1}(X_{1,\alpha,\theta})\cup\psi_{2}(X_{1,\alpha,\theta})=\alpha\phi_{1}\left(\frac{X_{1,\alpha,\theta}}{\alpha}\right)\cup\alpha\phi_{2}\left(\frac{X_{1,\alpha,\theta}}{\alpha}\right).

Therefore,

\frac{X_{1,\alpha,\theta}}{\alpha}=\phi_{1}\left(\frac{X_{1,\alpha,\theta}}{\alpha}\right)\cup\phi_{2}\left(\frac{X_{1,\alpha,\theta}}{\alpha}\right).

Since
H_{1,\alpha,\theta}
is a unique non-empty compact solution of the IFS (
1.1
) and
X_{1,\alpha,\theta}
is a closed set, we have

H_{1,\alpha,\theta}=X_{1,\alpha,\theta}/\alpha.

Therefore, the following proposition follows immediately from (
0.2
).

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
H_{1,\alpha,\theta}
be the self-similar set generated by the IFS:

\begin{cases}\phi_{1}(z)=\alpha z,&\\
\phi_{2}(z)=(\alpha e^{i\theta})z+1.\end{cases}

Then
H_{1,\alpha,\theta}
has the following parametrized expression.

H_{1,\alpha,\theta}=\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\alpha^{n}:\delta_{j_{1}}=1,(\delta_{0},\delta_{1},\dots)\in W_{\theta}\right\}=X_{1,\alpha,\theta}/\alpha,

(1.2)

where
j_{1}:=\min\{j:\delta_{j}\not=0\}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Define

\displaystyle H_{\alpha,\theta}:

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\alpha^{n}:(\delta_{0},\delta_{1},\dots)\in W_{\theta}\right\},

\displaystyle X_{\alpha,\theta}:

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\alpha^{n}:(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots)\in W_{\theta}\right\}.

Notice that

H_{\alpha,\theta}=\bigcup_{l=0}^{p-1}(e^{i\theta})^{l}H_{1,\alpha,\theta},\qquad X_{\alpha,\theta}=\bigcup_{l=0}^{p-1}(e^{i\theta})^{l}X_{1,\alpha,\theta}.

Figure
3
also confirms that two slightly different pairs IFSs can generate a geometrically similar attractor. It is interesting to note that the only difference in the parametric expressions of
H_{\alpha,\theta}
and
X_{\alpha,\theta}
is whether the sum begins from
n=0
or
n=1
.

(left) and

(right):
(\alpha,\theta)=((1+i)/2,\pi/2)

2
Structure of attractor and signed revolving sequences

Proposition
1.2
suggests a natural question: Does a small change in the constant term of the second map from
\alpha
to
1
always generate a geometrically similar attractor? Is it always true that this small change does not give any major influence to the parametrized expression for certain self-similar sets?

To begin, we recall another result from
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A sequence
{(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots)\in\Delta_{\theta}^{\mathrm{I\!N}}}
satisfies the Signed Revolving Condition (SRC) if

1.

\delta_{1}
is free to choose,

2.

If
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=\dots=\delta_{k}=0
, then
\delta_{k+1}
is free to choose,

3.

Otherwise,
\delta_{k+1}=0
or

\displaystyle\delta_{k+1}=\begin{cases}(e^{+i\theta})\delta_{j_{0}(k)},&\mbox{if $j_{0}(k)$ is odd},\\
(e^{-i\theta})\delta_{j_{0}(k)},&\mbox{if $j_{0}(k)$ is even},\end{cases}

where
j_{0}(k):=\max\{j\leq k:\delta_{j}\not=0\}
.

Compared to the generalized revolving sequences, which always move in the same direction, we see that the direction of movement of the sequence
(\delta_{n})
depends on its past. Define
W^{\pm}_{\theta}
as the set of all signed revolving sequences with parameter
\theta
.

Theorem 2.2

(Kawamura-Allen)
.

Let
X^{2}_{1,\alpha,\theta}
be the self-similar set generated by the iterated function system:

\begin{cases}\psi_{1}(z)=\alpha\overline{z},&\\
\psi_{2}(z)=(\alpha e^{i\theta})\overline{z}+\alpha,\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|\alpha|<1
.

Then
X_{1,\alpha,\theta}^{2}
has the following parametrized expression:

X_{1,\alpha,\theta}^{2}=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\eta_{j}:\delta_{j_{1}}=1,(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots)\in W^{\pm}_{\theta}\right\},

(2.2)

where
j_{1}:=\min\{j:\delta_{j}\not=0\}
and

\eta_{j}=\begin{cases}\alpha,&\mbox{if $j$ is odd},\\
\overline{\alpha},&\mbox{if $j$ is even}.\end{cases}

Remark 2.3
.

Define

\displaystyle H_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}:

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\eta_{j}:(\delta_{0},\delta_{1},\dots)\in W^{\pm}_{\theta}\right\},

\displaystyle X_{\alpha,\theta}^{2}:

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\eta_{j}:(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots)\in W^{\pm}_{\theta}\right\}.

Surprisingly, computer simulations suggest that changing the starting point from
n=1
to
n=0
generates a geometrically very different attractor; see Figure
4
.

Figure 4:
H^{2}_{\alpha,\theta}
(left) and
X^{2}_{\alpha,\theta}
(right):
(\alpha,\theta)=(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{3}i}{6},\frac{\pi}{6})

Let
X_{1,\overline{\alpha},\theta}^{2}
be the self-similar set generated by

\begin{cases}\psi_{1}(z)=\overline{\alpha}\cdot\overline{z},&\\
\psi_{2}(z)=(\overline{\alpha}e^{i\theta})\overline{z}+\overline{\alpha}.\end{cases}

(2.3)

Notice that Theorem
2.2
applies to all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|\alpha|<1
, so that
X_{1,\overline{\alpha},\theta}^{2}

has the following parametrized expression:

X_{1,\overline{\alpha},\theta}^{2}=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\overline{\eta_{j}}:\delta_{j_{1}}=1,(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots)\in W^{\pm}_{\theta}\right\},

where
j_{1}:=\min\{j:\delta_{j}\not=0\}
and
\eta_{1}=\alpha
and
\eta_{j+1}=\overline{\eta_{j}}
for
j=1,2,\dots
. This leads to the following proposition:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
H_{1,\alpha,\theta}^{2}
be the self-similar set generated by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\phi_{1}(z)=\alpha\overline{z}&\\
\phi_{2}(z)=(\alpha e^{i\theta})\overline{z}+1.\end{cases}

(2.4)

Then
H_{1,\alpha,\theta}^{2}
has the following parametrized expression

H_{1,\alpha,\theta}^{2}=\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\eta_{j}:\delta_{j_{1}}=1,(\delta_{0},\delta_{1},\dots)\in W^{\pm}_{\theta}\right\}=X_{1,\overline{\alpha},\theta}^{2}/\overline{\alpha},

where
j_{1}:=\min\{j:\delta_{j}\not=0\}
and
\eta_{1}=\alpha
and
\eta_{j+1}=\overline{\eta_{j}}
for
j=1,2,\dots
.

Proof.

From (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), notice that
\psi_{1}(z)=\overline{\alpha}\phi_{1}(z/\overline{\alpha})
and
\psi_{2}(z)=\overline{\alpha}\phi_{2}(z/\overline{\alpha})
. Thus, we have

X_{1,\overline{\alpha},\theta}^{2}=\psi_{1}(X_{1,\overline{\alpha},\theta}^{2})\cup\psi_{2}(X_{1,\overline{\alpha},\theta}^{2})=\overline{\alpha}\phi_{1}(X_{1,\overline{\alpha},\theta}^{2}/\overline{\alpha})\cup\overline{\alpha}\phi_{2}(X_{1,\overline{\alpha},\theta}^{2}/\overline{\alpha}).

Since
H_{1,\alpha,\theta}^{2}
is the unique non-empty compact solution of (
2.4
) and
X_{1,\overline{\alpha},\theta}^{2}
is a closed set,

\displaystyle H_{1,\alpha,\theta}^{2}=X_{1,\overline{\alpha},\theta}^{2}/\overline{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\prod_{j=2}^{n}\overline{\eta_{j}}:\delta_{j_{1}}=1,(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots)\in W^{\pm}_{\theta}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\eta_{j}:\delta_{j_{1}}=1,(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots)\in W^{\pm}_{\theta}\right\}.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

It is interesting to notice that changing the constant term in the IFS (
2.1
) from
\alpha
to
1
generates a very different attractor since
H_{1,\alpha,\theta}^{2}\neq X_{1,\alpha,\theta}^{2}/\alpha
but rather
H_{1,\alpha,\theta}^{2}=X_{1,\overline{\alpha},\theta}^{2}/\overline{\alpha}
. However, the only difference in the parametric expressions of
H_{1,\alpha,\theta}^{2}
and
X_{1,\alpha,\theta}^{2}
is whether the sum begins from
n=0
or
n=1
.

3
Terdragon and Ternary Revolving Sequences

Lévy dragon curve, the Heighway dragon and the Twindragon are all generated by two similar contractions; however, the Terdragon is generated by three similar contractions:

\begin{cases}\phi_{1}(z)=\alpha z,&\\
\phi_{2}(z)=(\alpha e^{i\theta})z+\alpha,&\\
\phi_{3}(z)=\alpha z+\beta.\end{cases}

(3.1)

In particular, if
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{3}i}{6},\beta=\bar{\alpha}
and
\theta=\frac{2\pi}{3}
, then the IFS (
3.1
) generates the Terdragon.

A question arises naturally: How can we give a parametrized expression for self-similar sets generated by the IFS (
3.1
)? How does the number of contractions influence the properties of generalized revolving sequences?

Definition 3.1
.

A sequence
{(\delta_{n})\in\Delta_{\theta}^{\mathrm{I\!N}}}
satisfies the Ternary Revolving Condition (TRC) if

1.

\delta_{1}
is free to choose,

2.

If
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=\dots=\delta_{k}=0
then
\delta_{k+1}
is free to choose,

3.

Otherwise,
\delta_{k+1}=0
,
\delta_{k+1}=\delta_{j_{0}(k)}
or
\delta_{k+1}=(e^{i\theta})\delta_{j_{0}(k)}

where
j_{0}(k)=\max\{j\leq k:\delta_{j}\neq 0\}
.

Loosely speaking we can say that a ternary revolving sequence
(\delta_{n})\in\Delta_{\theta}^{\mathrm{I\!N}}
is a sequence with nonzero terms lying on the unit circle and with subsequent terms which either:

1.

Stay in place,

2.

Move forward on the unit circle by angle
\theta
,

3.

Or go to the origin.

Define
W_{\theta}^{3}
to be the set of all ternary revolving sequences with parameter
\theta
:

W_{\theta}^{3}:=\{(\delta_{n})\in\Delta_{\theta}^{\mathbb{N}}:(\delta_{n})\text{ satisfies the TRC}\}.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma:=(\delta_{n})\in W_{\theta}^{3}
. Define the function
b_{\Sigma}:\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow\{0,1\}
as follows:

b_{\Sigma}(n):=\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }\delta_{n}=\delta_{j_{1}(n)}\\
0,\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

where
j_{1}(n):=\min\{j>n:\delta_{j}\neq 0\}
. Fix
b_{\Sigma}(n)=0
, if
\delta_{j}=0

\forall j>n
.

The Binary Static Sequence (BSS) for
(\delta_{n})
is the binary sequence
(b_{n})
such that
b_{n}=b_{\Sigma}(n)
,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
.

For example, consider the following segment of a ternary revolving sequence
(\delta_{n})
with parameter
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
.

\boldsymbol{(\delta_{n}):}\>1\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow 1\longrightarrow i\longrightarrow i\longrightarrow-1\dots

Then the corresponding binary static sequence
(b_{n})
is

\boldsymbol{(b_{n}):}\>1\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow 1\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow\text{?}\dots

Note that because the BSS for
(\delta_{n})
is
future dependent
we cannot know what the last shown element in
(b_{n})
is without knowing what comes next in
(\delta_{n})
.

Loosely speaking,
(b_{n})
tells you when the the sequence
(\delta_{n})
stays in place on the unit circle.
Also note that
b_{\Sigma}(n)=0
for all
n\in\mathrm{I\!N}
such that
\delta_{n}=0
.

For given
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|\alpha|<1
,
|\beta|<1
and
|\theta|=\frac{2\pi q}{p}\leq\pi
, define
T_{\alpha,\beta,\theta}
as follows:

T_{\alpha,\beta,\theta}:=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\alpha^{n}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n}}:(\delta_{n})\in W_{\theta}^{3},(b_{n})\text{ is the BSS for }(\delta_{n})\right\}.

Figure 5:
T_{\alpha,\beta,\theta}:\alpha=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{3}i}{6},\beta=\bar{\alpha}
,
\theta=\frac{2\pi}{3}
(left) and
\theta=\frac{\pi}{3}
(right).

See Figure
5
. Computer simulations suggest that
T_{\alpha,\beta,\theta}
must be a union of three Terdragons as a special case.
Finally, we will prove the following main theorem.

Define a subset of
T_{\alpha,\beta,\theta}
as follows.

T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}:=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\alpha^{n}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n}}:\delta_{j_{0}}=1,(\delta_{n})\in W_{\theta}^{3},(b_{n})\text{ is the BSS for }(\delta_{n})\right\}

where
j_{0}=\min\{j:\delta_{j}\neq 0\}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}
satisfies the set equation

T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}=\Psi_{1}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta})\cup\Psi_{2}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta})\cup\Psi_{3}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}),

where

\begin{cases}\Psi_{1}(z)=\alpha z,\\
\Psi_{2}(z)=(\alpha e^{i\theta})z+\alpha,\\
\Psi_{3}(z)=\alpha z+\beta,\end{cases}

(3.2)

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|\alpha|<1
,
|\beta|<1
and
|\theta|=\frac{2\pi q}{p}\leq\pi
.

Proof.

Let
z\in T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}
. Then
z=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\alpha^{n}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n}}
for some
(\delta_{n})\in W_{\theta}^{3}
and the corresponding BSS
(b_{n})
. Consider the ordered pair of points
(\delta_{n},b_{n})
. Since the first non-zero digit of
\delta_{n}
is
1
, the only possible values for
(\delta_{1},b_{1})
are
(0,0),(1,0)
, and
(1,1)
.

If
(\delta_{1},b_{1})=(0,0)
, set
\delta_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}:=\delta_{n+1}
and
b_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}:=b_{n+1}
for
n=1,2,\dots
. Then
(\delta_{n}^{{}^{\prime}})
satisfies the TRC with its first nonzero digit equal to
1
and
(b_{n}^{{}^{\prime}})
is the BSS for
(\delta_{n}^{{}^{\prime}})
. Thus, we have

z=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n+1}\alpha^{n+1}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n+1}}=\alpha\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha^{n}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}}\in\Psi_{1}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}).

If
(\delta_{1},b_{1})=(1,0)
, set
\delta_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}:=(e^{-i\theta})\delta_{n+1}
and
b_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}:=b_{n+1}
for
n=1,2,\dots
. Since
b_{1}=0
, the next nonzero digit of
\delta_{n}
is
e^{i\theta}
. Then the sequence
(\delta_{n}^{{}^{\prime}})
satisfies the TRC with its first nonzero digit equal to
1
and
(b_{n}^{{}^{\prime}})
is the BSS for
(\delta_{n}^{{}^{\prime}})
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\alpha+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n+1}\alpha^{n+1}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n+1}}=\alpha+\alpha(e^{i\theta})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(e^{-i\theta})\delta_{n+1}\alpha^{n}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\alpha+\alpha(e^{i\theta})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha^{n}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}}\in\Psi_{2}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}).

If
(\delta_{1},b_{1})=(1,1)
, set
\delta_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}:=\delta_{n+1}
and
b_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}:=b_{n+1}
. Since
b_{1}=1
, the next nonzero digit of
\delta_{n}
is
1
. Then the sequence
(\delta_{n}^{{}^{\prime}})
satisfies the TRC with its first nonzero digit equal
1
and
(b_{n}^{{}^{\prime}})
is the BSS for
(\delta_{n}^{{}^{\prime}})
. Thus, we have

z=\beta+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n+1}\alpha^{n+1}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n+1}}=\beta+\alpha\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha^{n}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}}\in\Psi_{3}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}).

Thus,

T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}\subset\Psi_{1}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta})\cup\Psi_{2}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta})\cup\Psi_{3}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}).

The reverse inclusion follows analogously. If
z\in\Psi_{1}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta})
, then

z=\alpha\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\alpha^{n}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n}}

for some
(\delta_{n})\in W_{\theta}^{3}
and the corresponding BSS
(b_{n})
. Set

\delta_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}:=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }j=1\\
\delta_{j-1},&\text{ if }j\geq 2,\end{cases}\qquad b_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}:=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }j=1\\
b_{j-1},&\text{ if }j\geq 2.\end{cases}

Since
(\delta_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})
satisfies the TRC with first nonzero digit equal to
1
and
(b_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})
is the BSS for
(\delta_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})
, we have

z=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\delta_{n-1}\alpha^{n}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n-1}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\delta_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha^{j}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}\in T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}.

If
z\in\Psi_{2}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta})
, then

z=\alpha+(\alpha e^{i\theta})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\alpha^{n}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n}}.

for some
(\delta_{n})\in W_{\theta}^{3}
and the corresponding BSS
(b_{n})
. Set

\delta_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}:=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }j=1\\
e^{i\theta}\delta_{j-1},&\text{ if }j\geq 2\end{cases}\qquad b_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}:=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }j=1\\
b_{j-1},&\text{ if }j\geq 2.\end{cases}

Notice that the sequence
(\delta_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})
satisfies the TRC with the first nonzero digit equal to
1
. Thus,

z=\delta_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha(\beta/\alpha)^{0}+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}(e^{i\theta}\delta_{j-1})\alpha^{j}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{j-1}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\delta_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha^{j}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}\in T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}.

Finally, if
z\in\Psi_{3}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta})
, then

z=\beta+\alpha\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}\alpha^{n}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n}}.

Set

\delta_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}:=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }j=1\\
\delta_{j-1},&\text{ if }j\geq 2\end{cases}\qquad b_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}:=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }j=1\\
b_{j-1},&\text{ if}j\geq 2.\end{cases}

Notice that both the first and second nonzero digits of
(\delta_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})
are
1
and
(\delta_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})
satisfies the TRC.
Since
b_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}=1
,
(b_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})
is the corresponding BSS for
(\delta_{j}^{{}^{\prime}})
. So,

z=\beta+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\delta_{n-1}\alpha^{n}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{n-1}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\delta_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha^{j}(\beta/\alpha)^{b_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}\in T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}.

As a result,

\Psi_{1}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta})\cup\Psi_{2}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta})\cup\Psi_{3}(T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta})\subset T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}.

∎

Corollary 3.4
.

The closure of
T_{1,\alpha,\beta,\theta}
is the self-similar set generated by the iterated function system (
3.2
).

Remark 3.5
.

We do not know if

(b_{n})

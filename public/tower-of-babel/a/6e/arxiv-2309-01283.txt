Square Packings and Rectifiable Doubling Measures
Raanan Schul

Abstract
We prove that for all integers 2 ≤ m ≤ d − 1 2\leq m\leq d-1 , there exist doubling measures on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with full support that are m m -rectifiable and purely ( m − 1 ) (m-1) -unrectifiable in the sense of Federer (i.e. without assuming μ ≪ ℋ m \mu\ll\mathcal{H}^{m} ). The corresponding result for 1-rectifiable measures is originally due to Garnett, Killip, and Schul (2010). Our construction of higher-dimensional Lipschitz images is informed by a simple observation about square packing in the plane: N N axis-parallel squares of side length s s pack inside of a square of side length ⌈ N 1 / 2 ⌉ ​ s \lceil N^{1/2}\rceil s . The approach is robust and when combined with standard metric geometry techniques allows for constructions in complete Ahlfors regular metric spaces. One consequence of the main theorem is that for each m ∈ { 2 , 3 , 4 } m\in\{2,3,4\} and s < m s<m , there exist doubling measures μ \mu on the Heisenberg group ℍ 1 \mathbb{H}^{1} and Lipschitz maps f : E ⊂ ℝ m → ℍ 1 f:E\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1} such that μ ≪ ℋ s − ϵ \mu\ll\mathcal{H}^{s-\epsilon} for all ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 , f ⁡ ( E ) f(E) has Hausdorff dimension s s , and μ ⁡ ( f ⁡ ( E ) ) > 0 \mu(f(E))>0 . ∎

Remark 2.6

(no rounding)

If

Remark 2.7

Corollary 2.8

(Lipschitz maps from

3
Densities, Assouad dimension, and rectifiability

As an initial application of Theorem
2.5
, we extend Martin and Mattila’s
[
MM88
, Theorem 4.1(1)]
on the rectifiability of Hausdorff measures on
s
-sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
when
s<m
(also see
[
BV19
, Theorem C]
) to general measures on complete metric spaces. To build a tree,
[
MM88
]
and
[
BV19
]
each used the Besicovitch covering theorem, which is not available in a general metric space. We are able to avoid reliance on a covering theorem by utilizing
r
-nets, i.e. maximal subsets of
r
-separated points.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on a complete metric space
\mathbb{X}
. For every integer
m\geq 1
and real-valued dimension
s\in[0,m)
, the measure

\mu\hbox{ {\vrule height=6.25963pt}{\leaders{\hrule}{\hskip 5.69046pt}} }\left\{x\in\mathbb{X}:0<\liminf_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{s}}\leq\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{s}}<\infty\right\}

(3.1)

is
m
-rectifiable. In fact, the set described in (
3.1
) is contained in a countable union of images of Lipschitz maps of the form
f:E\subset[0,1]^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}
.

Proof.

For each
n\geq 2
, let
G_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:r^{s}/n\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq nr^{s}\text{ for all }0<r<1/n\}
. Because the set of points with positive lower
s
-density and finite upper
s
-density can be written as
\bigcup_{n=2}^{\infty}G_{n}
, it suffices to fix
G=G_{n}
for some
n\geq 2
and prove that there exists a compact set
E\subset[0,1]^{m}
and a Lipschitz map
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{X}
such that
f(E)\supset G
. The Lipschitz constant of
f
will depend only on
\mu(\mathbb{X})
,
m
,
s
,
n
, and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits G
.

The set
G
is compact.
Since
\mathbb{X}
is complete, it suffices to prove that
G
is closed and totally bounded. To show that
G
is closed, suppose that
x_{1},x_{2},\dots\in G
and
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}x_{k}=x
for some
x\in\mathbb{X}
and let
0<r<1/n
. Since
B(x_{k},r-|x-x_{k}|)\subset B(x,r)\subset B(x_{k},r+|x-x_{k}|)
and
0<r-|x-x_{k}|\leq r+|x-x_{k}|<1/n
when
k
is sufficiently large, we have

\frac{1}{n}r^{s}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}(r-|x-x_{k}|)^{s}\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mu(B(x_{k},r-|x-x_{k}|))\leq\mu(B(x,r))

and

\mu(B(x,r))\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mu(B(x_{k},r+|x-x_{k}|))\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}n(r+|x-x_{k}|)^{s}=nr^{s}.

Hence
x\in G
and the set
G
is closed. To show that
G
is totally bounded, let
0<r<2/n
and let
G_{r}
be a maximal set of points in
G
such that
|x-y|>r
for all distinct
x,y\in G
. The set
G_{r}
is finite, because

\frac{1}{n}\left(r/2\right)^{s}\cdot\#G_{r}\leq\sum_{x\in G_{r}}\mu\left(B\left(x,r/2\right)\right)\leq\mu(\mathbb{X})<\infty.

As
G\subset\bigcup_{x\in G_{r}}B(x,r)
and
\#G_{r}<\infty
for any
0<r<2/n
, it follows that
G
can be covered by finitely many balls of any prescribed radius. That is,
G
is totally bounded.

We build a tree of sets
\mathcal{T}=\bigcup_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}_{l}
with
\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T})=G
.
Let
b\gg 1
be a large number, to be specified below, with
b\rightarrow\infty
as
s\rightarrow m
or
n\rightarrow\infty
. At the top level, assign
\mathcal{T}_{0}=\{G\}
. For each
l\geq 1
, assign
\rho_{l}:=(1/n)b^{-l}
,
\sigma_{l}:=\rho_{l}+\rho_{l+1}+\cdots=[b/(b-1)](1/n)b^{-l}
, and

\mathcal{T}_{l}:=\{G\cap B(x,\sigma_{l}):x\in G_{\rho_{l}}\}.

We define the parental structure as follows. As
\#\mathcal{T}_{0}=1
, the parent of any set in
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is automatically determined. For each
l\geq 2
and
x\in G_{\rho_{l}}
, choose any
x^{\uparrow}\in G_{\rho_{l-1}}
such that
|x-x^{\uparrow}|\leq\rho_{l-1}
; then assign
G\cap B(x,\sigma_{l})^{\uparrow}=G\cap B(x^{\uparrow},\sigma_{l-1})
. Clearly, every set in
\mathcal{T}_{l}
is contained in its parent, since
\rho_{l-1}+\sigma_{l}=\sigma_{l-1}
. Thus,
\mathcal{T}=\bigcup_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}_{l}
is a tree of sets in the sense of Definition
2.1
. Observe that
\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{l}=G
for every
l\geq 0
. It follows that
\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T})=\bigcap_{l=0}^{\infty}\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{l}=G
.

Rectifiability.
For each
l\geq 0
, let
N_{l}=\max_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{l}}\#\mathsf{Child}(F)
and
D_{l}=\max_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{l}}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits F
. Of course,
N_{0}=\#\mathcal{T}_{1}=\#G_{\rho_{1}}\lesssim_{s,n,b}\mu(\mathbb{X})
,
D_{0}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits G
, and
D_{l}\leq 2\sigma_{l}
for all
l\geq 1
. To bound
N_{l}
for
l\geq 1
, let
F=G\cap B(z,\sigma_{l})\in\mathcal{T}_{l}
for some
z\in G_{\rho_{l}}
. Using the pairwise disjointness of
\{B(x,\frac{1}{2}\rho_{l+1}):x\in G_{\rho_{l+1}}\}
and the definition of
G
, we have

\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{2n}b^{-(l+1)}\right)^{s}\#\mathsf{Child}(F)\leq\sum_{\mathsf{Child}(F)}\mu(B(x,\tfrac{1}{2}\rho_{l+1}))\leq\mu(B(z,\sigma_{l}))\leq n\left(\frac{b}{(b-1)n}b^{-l}\right)^{s}.

Hence

N_{l}\leq n^{2}\left(\frac{2b^{2}}{b-1}\right)^{s}=:P_{l}\quad\text{for all }l\geq 1.

Note that, since
n>1
and
b\geq 2
(at the end of the day, much bigger than 2),

\lceil P_{l}^{1/m}\rceil\leq P_{l}^{1/m}+1=n^{2/m}\left(\frac{2b^{2}}{b-1}\right)^{s/m}+1<n^{2/m}(4b)^{s/m}+1<n^{2/m}(5b)^{s/m}=:C.

Thus, recalling that
s<m
, we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}C^{l}\sigma_{l}\leq\frac{b}{n(b-1)}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{n^{2/m}(5b)^{s/m}}{b}\right)^{l}<\infty

provided that
b
is large enough depending only on
m
,
s
, and
n
. Therefore,

\displaystyle S:=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{l}\lceil N_{j}^{1/m}\rceil\right)D_{j}<\lceil N_{0}^{1/m}\rceil\left(D_{0}+2\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}C^{l}\sigma_{l}\right)<\infty.

By Theorem
2.5
, it follows that
G=\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T})\subset f(E)
for some compact set
E\subset[0,1]^{m}
and some
S
-Lipschitz map
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{X}
. Reviewing dependencies, we see that the Lipschitz constant
S\lesssim_{m,s,n}(1+\mu(\mathbb{X})^{1/m})(1+\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits G)
.
∎

In a metric space
\mathbb{X}
, a nonempty set
F\subset\mathbb{X}
is said to be
s
-homogeneous
if there exists
C>1
such that for every bounded set
A\subset F
and for every
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exist
C\delta^{-s}
or fewer sets
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\subset F
with
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits A_{i}\leq\delta\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits A
for all
i
such that
A\subset A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{n}
. That is, bounded subsets of
F
can be covered by a controlled number of uniformly smaller sets. The
Assouad dimension
of
F
(see e.g.
[
Luu98
]
or
[
Fra21
]
) can be defined as

\dim_{A}F:=\inf\{s\geq 0:\text{$F$ is $s$-homogeneous}\}.

(3.2)

It is easy to see that
\dim_{H}F\leq\dim_{A}F=\dim_{A}{\overline{F}}
for all
F\subset\mathbb{X}
. Also, if
\dim_{A}\mathbb{X}<\infty
, then every bounded set in
\mathbb{X}
is totally bounded and the space
\mathbb{X}
is separable. In particular, on any metric space
\mathbb{X}
with
0<\dim_{A}\mathbb{X}<\infty
, we can find a countable dense subset
F
of
\mathbb{X}
and this set has
\dim_{H}F=0<\dim_{A}\mathbb{X}=\dim_{A}F
.

As a second application of the square packing construction, we extend the Lipschitz case of Badger and Vellis’
[
BV19
, Theorem 3.2]
from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to complete metric spaces.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a complete metric space. If
F\subset\mathbb{X}
is nonempty,
m\geq 1
is an integer, and
\dim_{A}F<m
, then there exists a closed set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
and a Lipschitz map
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{X}
such that
f(E)\supset F
. When
F
is bounded, we may take
E\subset[0,1]^{m}
.

Proof.

The proof for bounded sets is even easier than the proof of Theorem
3.1
, as the definition of Assouad dimension is perfectly suited to building uniform trees. The proof for unbounded sets will follow from estimates on the Lipschitz constant in the bounded case and effective use of the Attouch-Wets topology (see e.g.
[
Bee93
]
or
[
BL15
]
).

Suppose that
F\subset\mathbb{X}
is nonempty, bounded, and
\dim_{A}F<m
for some integer
m\geq 1
. Note for later that the closure
\overline{F}
of
F
is compact, since
\dim_{A}\overline{F}=\dim_{A}F<\infty
and
\overline{F}
is bounded imply that
\overline{F}
is totally bounded and
\mathbb{X}
is complete implies that
\overline{F}
is complete. Now, since
\dim_{A}F<m
, there exists
s<m
and
C>1
such that
F
is
s
-homogeneous with associated constant
C>1
. Let
b\gg C
be a large number. We aim to build a tree a sets
\mathcal{T}=\bigcup_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}_{l}
with
\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T})=F
. Assign
\mathcal{T}_{0}:=\{F\}
. For the induction step, suppose that we have defined
\mathcal{T}_{l}
for some
l\geq 0
so that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits Q\leq b^{-l}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits F
for all
Q\in\mathcal{T}_{l}
. To define
\mathcal{T}_{l+1}
, it suffices to define the set of children for each
Q\in\mathcal{T}_{l}
. Given
Q\in\mathcal{T}_{l}
, use
s
-homogeneity of
F
with
\delta=b^{-1}
to find
Cb^{s}
or fewer sets
A_{1},\dots,A_{N}\subset F
such that
Q\subset A_{1}\cup\dots\cup A_{N}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits A_{i}\leq b^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits Q\leq b^{-(l+1)}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits F
for all
i
; then assign
\mathsf{Child}(Q):=\{Q\cap A_{1},\dots,Q\cap A_{N}\}
. This completes the definition of
\mathcal{T}
. For every level
l\geq 1
, we have
F\supset\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{l}\supset\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{l-1}\supset\cdots\supset\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{0}=F
. Thus,
\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T})=\bigcap_{l=0}^{\infty}\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{l}=F
, as desired. From construction, we see that for every
l\geq 0
,

N_{l}:=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{T}_{l}}\#\mathsf{Child}(Q)\leq Cb^{s}\quad\text{and}\quad D_{l}:=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{T}_{l}}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits Q\leq b^{-l}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits F.

Choose
t\in(s,m)
, for concreteness say
t:=(s+m)/2
. Then, as long as
b\gg C
, we can bound
\lceil N_{l}^{1/m}\rceil\leq C^{1/m}b^{s/m}+1\leq(C^{1/m}+1)b^{s/m}\leq b^{t/m}
. Hence

S:=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{i=0}^{j}\lceil N^{1/m}_{i}\rceil\right)D_{j}\leq b^{t/m}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits F\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b^{((t/m)-1)j}\lesssim_{m,s,C}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits F<\infty.

(3.3)

Therefore, by Theorem
2.5
, we can find a compact set
E\subset[0,1]^{m}
and a
L
-Lipschitz map
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{X}
such that
f(E)\supset\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T})=F
, where
L\lesssim_{m,s,C}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits F
.

Suppose that
F\subset\mathbb{X}
is unbounded and
\dim_{A}F<m
. Then we can find
s<m
such that
F
is
s
-homogeneous with associated constant
C>1
. Choose any base point
x_{0}\in F
. Then
F_{n}:=F\cap B(x_{0},n)
is also
s
-homogeneous with associated constant
C
for each
n\geq 1
. Since
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits F_{n}\leq 2n
for each
n\geq 1
, after rescaling the domain of the maps from the bounded case, we can find a constant
L=L(m,s,C)
, compact sets
E_{n}\subset[0,n]^{m}
, and
L
-Lipschitz maps
g_{n}:E_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}
such that
g_{n}(E_{n})\supset F_{n}
for each

. Without loss of generality, replacing each set

by
E_{n}\cap g_{n}^{-1}(\overline{F}_{n})
, we may assume that

g_{n}(E_{n})=\overline{F_{n}}\subset\overline{F}\quad\text{for each }n\geq 1.

At this stage, it is theoretically possible that the domains of
g_{n}
escape to infinity as

. To correct for this, first choose points

such that

; then define sets

and functions
\tilde{g}_{n}:\tilde{E}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}
by setting
\tilde{g}_{n}(x):=g_{n}(x+w_{n})
. For all

, the function
\tilde{g}_{n}
is

-Lipschitz, the image
\tilde{g}_{n}(\tilde{E}_{n})=\overline{F_{n}}\subset\overline{F}
, and we have

and
\tilde{g}_{n}(0)=x_{0}
. In particular, neither the domains
\tilde{E}_{n}
nor the graphs of
\tilde{g}_{n}
escape to infinity as

. Because
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{Y}=\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\overline{F}
are proper metric spaces (i.e. closed balls are compact), we can use the Attouch-Wets analogue of the Blaschke selection theorem
5
5

5

see
[
BL15
, Theorem 2.5]
or combine Theorems 3.1.4, 3.1.7, 5.1.10, 5.2.10, and 5.2.12 in
[

]
and mimic the argument from the proof of Theorem
2.5
above to produce a closed set
\tilde{E}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
and an

4.1
Generalized b-adic cubes

We will use the streamlined construction of metric cubes by Käenmäki, Rajala, and Suomala
[
KRS12
]
. The original application (see
[
KRS12
, Theorem 4.1]
) was to construct doubling measures of arbitrarily small upper packing dimension. Recall that a set
B
in a metric space
\mathbb{X}
is
totally bounded
if
B
can be covered by finitely many balls of radius
r
for every
r>0
. We let
B(x,r)
and
U(x,r)
denote the closed and open balls in
\mathbb{X}
with center
x
and radius
r
, respectively. We emphasize that the following theorem does not require
\mathbb{X}
to be complete. Note that we have replaced the parameter
r\in(0,1/3]
in the original statement of the theorem with
b=r^{-1}\in[3,\infty)
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
KRS12
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a metric space in which every ball is totally bounded and choose an “origin”
o\in\mathbb{X}
and scaling factor
b\in[3,\infty)
. For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a set
\Delta_{k}
of nonempty bounded Borel sets (“cubes”) and a set of points
\{x_{Q}:Q\in\Delta_{k}\}
(“centers”) with the following properties.

1.

partitioning:
\mathbb{X}=\bigcup_{Q\in\Delta_{k}}Q
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
;

2.

nesting:
Q\cap R=\emptyset
or
R\subset Q
for all
Q\in\Delta_{k}
and
R\in\Delta_{m}
when
m\geq k
;

3.

roundness:
U(x_{Q},c_{b}b^{-k})\subset Q\subset B(x_{Q},C_{b}b^{-k})
for all
Q\in\Delta_{k}
, where

c_{b}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{b-1}\quad\text{and}\quad C_{b}=\frac{b}{b-1};

4.

origin: for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, there is
Q\in\Delta_{k}
such that
x_{Q}=o
; and,

5.

inheritance: for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
Q\in\Delta_{k}
, there exists a cube
R\in\Delta_{k+1}
such that
R\subset Q
and
x_{R}=x_{Q}
.

Remark 4.2

(associated notions)
.

We call any family
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
given by Theorem
4.1
a
system of
b
-adic (KRS) cubes
for
\mathbb{X}
with
origin

o
. We call
\Delta_{k}
the
k
-th level
or
generation
of
\Delta:=\bigsqcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Delta_{k}
. The same point set
Q
in
\mathbb{X}
may belong to
\Delta_{k}
for several
k
. For each
Q\in\Delta_{k}
, we call the number
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q:=b^{-k}
the
side length
of
Q
, we call
U_{Q}:=U(x_{Q},c_{b}b^{-k})
the
inner ball
for
Q
, and we call
B_{Q}:=B(x_{Q},C_{b}b^{-k})
the
outer ball
for
Q
. We always have
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits Q\leq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits B_{Q}\leq 2C_{b}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q.
However, in general, there is no lower bound on
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits Q
in terms of
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q
, because
\mathbb{X}
could be bounded and/or disconnected.

For each
Q\in\Delta_{k}
, let
\mathsf{Child}(Q)=\{R\in\Delta_{k+1}:R\subset Q\}
denote the
children
of
Q
in
\Delta
; we call
R\in\mathsf{Child}(Q)
a
child
of
Q
and we call
Q
the
parent
of
R
. More generally, for any
Q\in\Delta_{k}
and
j\geq 0
, let
\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)=\{R\in\Delta_{k+j}:R\subset Q\}
denote the set of
j
-th generation descendants
of
Q
. (Note that
\mathsf{Child}^{0}(Q)=\{Q\}
.)

For every cube
Q\in\Delta
, we let
Q^{\uparrow}
denote the parent of
Q
and let
Q^{\downarrow}
denote the unique cube
R\in\mathsf{Child}(Q)
such that
x_{R}=x_{Q}
. We call
Q^{\downarrow}
the
central child
of
Q
. In order to write down later estimates, we define the
central indicator function

\mathsf{ci}:\Delta\rightarrow\{0,1\}
so that
\mathsf{ci}(Q)=1
if
Q
is the central child of its parent (i.e.
Q=Q^{\uparrow\downarrow}
) and
\mathsf{ci}(Q)=0
otherwise. Finally, for every cube
Q_{0}\in\Delta
and chain of descendants
Q_{1}\in\mathsf{Child}(Q_{0})
, …,
Q_{j}\in\mathsf{Child}(Q_{j-1})
with
j\geq 1
, we define the
central counting function

\mathsf{cc}(Q_{0},Q_{j}):=\#\{1\leq i\leq j:Q_{i}=Q_{i-1}^{\downarrow}\}=\#\{1\leq i\leq j:\mathsf{ci}(Q_{i})=1\}.

(4.1)

Remark 4.3

(exhaustion)
.

Inclusion of the origin in a system of
b
-adic cubes has several consequences. For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
Q^{o}_{k}
denote the unique cube in
\Delta_{k}
whose center is the origin. For any
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
Q^{o}_{k_{0}}\subset Q^{o}_{k_{0}-1}\subset Q^{o}_{k_{0}-2}\subset\cdots
and
\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}Q^{o}_{k_{0}-j}=\mathbb{X}
, because
Q^{o}_{k_{0}-j}\supset U(o,b^{j-k_{0}})
. Therefore, it is possible to exhaust
\mathbb{X}
by cubes in
\Delta
. In particular, every cube
Q\in\Delta
belongs to
Q^{o}_{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Consequently, every pair of cubes in
\Delta
have a common ancestor.

Remark 4.4

(the central child is relatively far away from the boundary)
.

Recall that for any nonempty sets
A
and
B
in
\mathbb{X}
,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(A,B)=\inf_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}|a-b|
. A key property that we will use without further comment is monotonicity:
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(A,B)\geq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(C,D)
whenever
A\subset C
and
B\subset D
. If the scaling factor
b>5
and
Q\in\Delta_{k}
is any cube such that
Q\neq\mathbb{X}
, then

\begin{split}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(Q^{\downarrow},\mathbb{X}\setminus Q)&\geq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(B_{Q^{\downarrow}},\mathbb{X}\setminus U_{Q})\\
&\geq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{radius}\nolimits U_{Q}-\mathop{\mathrm{}}{radius}\nolimits B_{Q^{\downarrow}}=(bc_{b}-C_{b})b^{-(k+1)}\gtrsim_{b}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q^{\downarrow}.\end{split}

(4.2)

Indeed,
bc_{b}-C_{b}=b(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{b-1})>0
if and only if
b>5
.

Example 4.5
.

A modified version of the usual triadic cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
enjoys all of the essential properties of KRS cubes. Let
o=(0,\dots,0)
denote the origin. We initially declare that each cube of the form
Q_{k}^{o}=(-\frac{1}{2}\cdot 3^{k},\frac{1}{2}\cdot 3^{k}]^{d}
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}
is an
origin-based triadic cube
. Further, we declare that any half-open cube that appears after trisecting an origin-based triadic cube into
3^{d}
equal size subcubes is also an origin-based triadic cube. Let
\Delta_{k}
denote all origin-based triadic cubes of side length
3^{-k}
. The center
x_{Q}
of
Q\in\Delta=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Delta_{k}
is the geometric center of the cube. The family
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of origin-based triadic cubes satisfy properties (1)–(5) of Theorem
4.1
with scaling factor
b=3
and with constants
c_{b}
and
C_{b}
replaced by
1/2
and
\sqrt{n}/2
, respectively. Of course,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(Q^{\downarrow},\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Q)\geq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q^{\downarrow}
for all
Q
. One way in which origin-based triadic cubes are superior to the usual variant is that each nonempty bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is contained in an origin-based triadic cube. Another improvement is that any two origin-based triadic cubes have a common ancestor.

Lemma 4.6

(counting cubes I)
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be an Ahlfors
q
-regular metric space. If
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a system of
b
-adic cubes for
\mathbb{X}
with
b\geq 5
, then

b^{jq}\lesssim_{\mathbb{X}}\#\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)\lesssim_{\mathbb{X}}b^{jq}\quad\text{for all $Q\in\Delta$ with $Q\neq\mathbb{X}$ and $j\geq 1$.}

(4.3)

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{X}
is
q
-regular, there exists a measure
\nu
and constants
C,D>0
such that
Cr^{q}\leq\nu(U(x,r))\leq\nu(B(x,r))\leq CDr^{q}
for all
x\in\mathbb{X}
and for all
0<r<\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits X
. Let
Q\in\Delta_{k}
with
Q\neq\mathbb{X}
. Then we may use the lower bound on
\nu(U_{Q})
and on
\nu(U_{R})
for any descendant
R
of
Q
. The upper bounds on
\nu(B_{Q})
and
\nu(B_{R})
are always valid. Let
j\geq 1
. Using
U_{Q}\subset Q\subset\bigcup_{R\in\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)}B_{R}
and
\bigcup_{R\in\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)}U_{R}\subset Q\subset B_{Q}
, as well as the pairwise disjointness of
\{U_{R}:R\in\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)\}
, we have

Cc_{b}^{q}b^{-kq}\leq\#\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)CDC_{b}^{q}b^{-(k+j)q}\text{\ \ and\ \ }\#\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)Cc_{b}^{q}b^{-(k+j)q}\leq CDC_{b}^{q}b^{-kq}.

Rearranging yields
[D(C_{b}/c_{b})^{q}]^{-1}b^{jq}\leq\#\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)\leq D(C_{b}/c_{b})^{q}b^{jq}
. Noting that
C_{b}/c_{b}=2b/(b-3)
yields

\left[(\tfrac{2b}{b-3})^{q}D\right]^{-1}b^{jq}\leq\#\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)\leq\left[(\tfrac{2b}{b-3})^{q}D\right]b^{jq}.

(4.4)

When
b\geq 5
, we have
C_{b}/c_{b}\leq C_{5}/c_{5}=5
. Thus, letting
\nu
range over all possible Ahlfors
q
-regular measures on
\mathbb{X}
, it follows that we may replace
D(C_{b}/c_{b})^{q}
in (
4.4
) with a constant depending on
\mathbb{X}
(including
q
), but not on a choice of
\nu
nor on the choice of a particular system of cubes.
∎

To construct doubling measures using
b
-adic cubes (see §
5
), it will be convenient to have the following variant of Lemma
4.6
.

Lemma 4.7

(counting cubes II)
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be an Ahlfors
q
-regular metric space and let
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a system of
b
-adic cubes for
\mathbb{X}
with
b\geq 47
. For all
Q\in\Delta_{k}
, define

\displaystyle\mathsf{Inner}(Q)

\displaystyle:=\{R\in\mathsf{Child}(Q):R\cap U(x_{Q},\tfrac{1}{2}c_{b}b^{-k})\neq\emptyset\},

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Outer}(Q)

\displaystyle:=\mathsf{Child}(Q)\setminus\mathsf{Inner}(Q)=\{R\in\mathsf{Child}(Q):R\cap U(x_{Q},\tfrac{1}{2}c_{b}b^{-k})=\emptyset\}.

(4.6)

For all
Q\in\Delta
with
Q\neq\mathbb{X}
, we have

b^{q}\lesssim_{\mathbb{X}}\#\mathsf{Inner}(Q)\lesssim_{\mathbb{X}}b^{q}\quad\text{and}\quad\#\mathsf{Outer}(Q)\lesssim_{\mathbb{X}}b^{q}.

(4.7)

If
Q\in\Delta
,
Q\neq\mathbb{X}
,
R\in\mathsf{Child}(Q)
, and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(R,\mathbb{X}\setminus Q)\leq 9\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits R
, then
R\in\mathsf{Outer}(Q)
.

Proof.

Let
Q\in\Delta_{k}
with
Q\neq\mathbb{X}
. We start with the final claim. For all
R\in\mathsf{Child}(Q)
,

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits R\leq\frac{2b}{b-1}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits R<2.1\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits R\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\mathrm{}}{radius}\nolimits U_{Q}=\frac{b-3}{2b-2}b\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits R>22.4\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits R,

since
b\geq 47
. Also,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(\frac{1}{2}U_{Q},\mathbb{X}\setminus Q)\geq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(\frac{1}{2}U_{Q},\mathbb{X}\setminus U_{Q})\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{radius}\nolimits U_{Q}
. Hence

\begin{split}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(\tfrac{1}{2}U_{Q},R)&\geq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(\tfrac{1}{2}U_{Q},\mathbb{X}\setminus Q)-\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits R-\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(R,\mathbb{X}\setminus Q)\\
&>11.2\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits R-2.1\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits R-\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(R,\mathbb{X}\setminus Q)>0\end{split}

and
R\in\mathsf{Outer}(Q)
if
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(R,\mathbb{X}\setminus Q)\leq 9\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits R
.

Let
\nu
,
C
, and
D
be as in the proof of Lemma
4.6
. By definition of
\mathsf{Inner}(Q)
, we have
\frac{1}{2}U_{Q}\subset\bigcup_{R\in\mathsf{Inner}(Q)}B_{R}
. Hence using the Ahlfors regularity inequalities for
\nu
,

C(\tfrac{1}{2}c_{b})^{q}b^{-kq}\leq\#\mathsf{Inner}(Q)CDC_{b}^{q}b^{-(k+1)q}.

Since
2C_{b}/c_{b}=4b/(b-3)<4.3
when
b\geq 47
,

\#\mathsf{Inner}(Q)\geq(4.3D)^{-1}b^{q}.

(4.8)

Letting
\nu
range over all Ahlfors
q
-regular measures on
\mathbb{X}
yields the lower bound on
\#\mathsf{Inner}(Q)
in (
4.7
). Lemma
4.6
gives the upper bounds.
∎

.

The constants

and

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be an Ahlfors
q
-regular metric space with
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits\mathbb{X}\geq 2.1
and let
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a system of
b
-adic cubes for
\mathbb{X}
with
b\geq 47
. If
0<s<q
and
b
is sufficiently large depending only on
\mathbb{X}
(including
q
) and

, then

\#\mathsf{Inner}(Q)\geq b^{s}\quad\text{and}\quad\#\mathsf{Child}(Q)\leq b^{q+1}

(4.9)

for all

.

Proof.

For any

, we have
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits Q\leq 2C_{b}/c_{b}<2.1\leq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits\mathbb{X}

4.2
Dimension of measures

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a metric space. To set conventions, we define the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure

\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)
and
s
-dimensional packing measure

\mathcal{P}^{s}(E)
for all
s\in[0,\infty)
and for all nonempty
E\subset\mathbb{X}
by

\mathcal{H}^{s}(E):=\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\inf\left\{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits E_{i})^{s}:E\subset\textstyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i},\,\forall_{i\geq 1}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits E_{i}\leq\delta\right\}

(4.10)

and

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}^{s}(E)&:=\inf\left\{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}P^{s}(E_{i}):E\subset\textstyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}\right\},\text{ where}\\
P^{s}(E)&:=\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\sup\left\{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(2r_{i})^{s}:\forall_{i\geq 1}x_{i}\in E,r_{i}\in(0,\delta/2],\,\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \left.\forall_{i\neq j}B(x_{i},r_{i})\cap B(x_{j},r_{j})=\emptyset\,\right\}.\end{split}

(4.11)

Then
\dim_{H}E:=\inf\{s\geq 0:\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)=0\}
and
\dim_{P}E:=\inf\{s\geq 0:\mathcal{P}^{s}(E)=0\}
, using the convention that
\inf\emptyset=\infty
. Because a general metric space is not necessarily uniformly perfect
6
6

6

A metric space
\mathbb{X}
is
c
-uniformly perfect if
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits B(x,r)\geq cr
for all
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
r>0
with
B(x,r)\neq\mathbb{X}
. For example, connected metric spaces are
1
-uniformly perfect.
, it is important to adopt the “radial” definition of the packing measure (see
[
Cut95
]
) and we have done so.

The
lower
and
upper Hausdorff dimensions
of a Borel measure
\mu
on a separable metric space
\mathbb{X}
are given respectively by

\displaystyle\underline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\dim_{H}E:\mu(E)>0,\;E\text{ Borel}\},

(4.12)

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\dim_{H}E:\mu(\mathbb{X}\setminus E)=0,\;E\text{ Borel}\}.

(4.13)

The
support
of
\mu
, denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{spt}\nolimits\mu
, is the smallest closed set
F
such that
\mu(\mathbb{X}\setminus F)=0
. Naturally,
\underline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu\leq\overline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu\leq\dim_{H}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{spt}\nolimits\mu
for all
\mu
, but both inequalities can be strict. That is to say, the dimension of a measure is a distinct notion from the dimension of its support. The
lower
and
upper packing dimensions
of
\mu
are defined analogously by substituting the packing dimension of
E
for the Hausdorff dimension of
E
. In general,

\underline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu\leq\underline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu;\quad\overline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu\leq\overline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu;\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu\leq\overline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu.

(4.14)

In the rare situation that
\underline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu=\overline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu=\underline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu=\overline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu=s
, we may say that
\mu
has
exact
dimension
s
. The following well-known formulas for dimensions of measures in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see e.g.
[
Fal97
]
) continue to persist in the metric setting:

Theorem 4.10

(Tamashiro
[
Tam95
, Theorem 1.8]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a separable metric space. If
\mu
is a finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{X}
, then

\displaystyle\underline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu

\displaystyle=\mu{\!-\!}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ess\,inf}_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\left(\liminf_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\log\mu(B(x,r))}{\log r}\right),

(4.15)

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu

\displaystyle=\mu{\!-\!}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ess\,sup}_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\left(\liminf_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\log\mu(B(x,r))}{\log r}\right),

(4.16)

\displaystyle\underline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu

\displaystyle=\mu{\!-\!}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ess\,inf}_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\left(\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\log\mu(B(x,r))}{\log r}\right),

(4.17)

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu

\displaystyle=\mu{\!-\!}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{ess\,sup}_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\left(\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\log\mu(B(x,r))}{\log r}\right).

(4.18)

The quantities
\liminf_{r\downarrow 0}\log\mu(B(x,r))/\log(r)
and
\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\log\mu(B(x,r)/\log(r)

and

of
\mu

On doubling metric spaces, one may replace balls

4.3
Measures with prescribed values

In the remainder of the paper, we will study the properties of certain measures on metric spaces
\mathbb{X}
whose balls are totally bounded, formally built by

1.

choosing a system of
b
-adic cubes
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
on
\mathbb{X}
;

2.

specifying a function
w:\{\overline{Q}:Q\in\Delta_{+}\}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
on the closure of cubes in
\Delta_{+}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}
such that
w(\overline{Q})=\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Child}(Q)}w(\overline{R})
for all
Q\in\Delta_{+}
and extending the definition by assigning
w(\emptyset)=0
; and,

3.

defining
\mu_{w}(E):=\inf\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}w(\overline{Q_{i}}):E\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\overline{Q_{i}}\text{ for some }Q_{1},Q_{2},\cdots\in\Delta_{+}\cup\{\emptyset\}\}
for all
E\subset\mathbb{X}
.

Because the weight
w
is additive over children, we may alternatively write

\mu_{w}(E)=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}w(\overline{Q_{i}}):E\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\overline{Q}_{i}\text{ for some }Q_{1},Q_{2},\cdots\in\Delta_{+}\cup\{\emptyset\},\,\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits Q_{i}\leq\delta\right\}

It easily follows that
\mu_{w}
is a metric outer measure and Borel sets are
\mu_{w}
measurable. Further, because any ball can be covered by a finite number of cubes of side length 1 and the outer measure is defined using outer approximation by closed sets, it follows that
\mu_{w}
is a Radon measure on
\mathbb{X}
, i.e. a locally finite Borel regular outer measure on
\mathbb{X}
. For details, see e.g.
[
Rog98
]
. However, it is an unpleasant reality that the measures
\mu_{w}(Q)
and
\mu_{w}(\overline{Q})
and the weight
w(\overline{Q})
do not need to agree on cubes
Q\in\Delta_{+}
. There are two issues.

Example 4.12
.

Let
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}
and let
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the system of left-open triadic intervals. Define a weight
w
so that
w([0,3^{-k}])=1
for all
k\geq 0
, and
w(\overline{I})=0
for all other triadic intervals of length at most 1. Then
\mu_{w}([0,1])=0
even though
w([0,1])=1
. To see this, simply note that

[0,1]\subset[-1,0]\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}[3^{-k},2\cdot 3^{-k}]\cup[2\cdot 3^{-k},3\cdot 3^{-k}]

and the weight of each closed triadic interval on the right hand side is zero. The difficulty in this example is that
w
is not countably subadditive.

Example 4.13
.

Let
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{Q}
be equipped with the subspace metric from
\mathbb{R}
, which is a doubling metric space. Let
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the system of left-open triadic intervals (in
\mathbb{Q}
). Define a weight
w
so that
w(\mathbb{Q}\cap[n,n+1])=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and on any triadic interval
I=L\cup M\cup R
of side length at most 1,
w(\overline{L})=w(\overline{M})=w(\overline{R})=(1/3)w(\overline{I})
, where
L
,
M
, and
R
are the left, middle, and right triadic children of
I
, respectively. In contrast to the previous example, the weight
w
has the nice property that
w
is centrally doubling insofar as
w(\overline{I^{\downarrow}})=w(\overline{M})\gtrsim w(\overline{I})
for all
I\in\Delta_{+}
. Nevertheless,
\mu_{w}
is the zero measure. Indeed,
\mu_{w}
is nothing other than the restriction of Lebesgue outer measure on
\mathbb{R}
to the power set of
\mathbb{Q}
. Since
\mathbb{Q}
is countable,
\mu_{w}(\mathbb{Q})=0
. The difficulty in this example is that
\mathbb{Q}
is not complete.

The following criterion is sufficient to ensure that
\mu_{w}
takes prescribed values. It gives one possible solution to the technical issue described in
[
KRS12
, Remark 5.1(1)]
.

Lemma 4.14

(extension criterion)
.

Assume that
\mathbb{X}
is a proper metric space (i.e. every closed ball in
\mathbb{X}
is compact) and
b>5
. If there exists a constant
0<p\leq 1
such that
w(\overline{Q^{\downarrow}})\geq pw(\overline{Q})
and
w(\overline{Q})=\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Child}(Q)}w(\overline{R})
for all
Q\in\Delta_{+}
, then
\mu_{w}(\partial Q)=0
and
\mu_{w}(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{int}\nolimits{Q})=\mu_{w}(Q)=\mu_{w}(\overline{Q})=w(\overline{Q})
for all
Q\in\Delta_{+}
.

Proof.

Let
Q\in\Delta_{+}
. If it happens that
Q=\mathbb{X}
, then
\mu_{w}(\partial Q)=\mu_{w}(\emptyset)=0
. Suppose that
Q\neq\mathbb{X}
. Since
b>5
, there exists a constant
\delta=\delta(b)>0
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(P^{\downarrow},\mathbb{X}\setminus P)\geq\delta\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits P^{\downarrow}>0
for all
P\in\Delta
such that
P\neq\mathbb{X}
by Remark
4.4
. Hence
\partial Q
is covered by

\{\overline{R}:R\in\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)\text{ and }\mathsf{cc}(Q,R)=0\},

where
\mathsf{cc}(Q,R)
is given by (
4.1
). Thus, by the hypothesis,
\mu_{w}(\partial Q)\leq(1-p)^{j}w(\overline{Q})
for all
j\geq 1
. It follows that
\mu_{w}(\partial Q)=0
and
\mu_{w}(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{int}\nolimits{Q})=\mu_{w}(Q)=\mu_{w}(\overline{Q})\leq w(\overline{Q})
. To finish, it suffices to show that
w
is countably subadditive, i.e.
w(\overline{Q})\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}w(\overline{Q}_{n})
whenever
\overline{Q}\subset\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{Q}_{n}
for some sequence
Q_{n}\in\Delta_{+}\cup\{\emptyset\}
, because this implies
w(\overline{Q})\leq\mu_{w}(\overline{Q})
.

Suppose that
\overline{Q}\subset\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{Q}_{n}
for some sequence
Q_{n}\in\Delta_{+}\cup\{\emptyset\}
. Fix
\epsilon>0
. By the same reasoning as in the previous paragraph, for each
n\geq 1
, we can choose
j=j(Q_{n})
sufficiently large so that

\displaystyle\mathsf{Aux}(Q_{n}):=\{R\in\mathsf{Child}^{j}(P):\,

\displaystyle P\in\Delta_{+},\;\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits P=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q_{n},\;

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(P,Q_{n})<\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q_{n},\;\mathsf{cc}(P,R)=0\}

satisfies
\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Aux}(Q_{n})}w(\overline{R})\leq 2^{-n}\epsilon
. (The set of all
P\in\Delta_{+}
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits P=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q_{n}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(P,Q)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q_{n}
is finite, because the set
\{U_{P}\}
of associated inner balls are pairwise disjoint, have the same radius
c_{b}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q_{n}
, are contained in
B(x_{Q_{n}},(2C_{b}+1)\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q_{n})
, and balls in
\mathbb{X}
are totally bounded.) For each
n\geq 1
, define the open set

U_{n}:=\bigcup_{x\in Q_{n}}U\left(x,\tfrac{1}{2}\delta b^{-j(Q_{n})}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q_{n}\right).

If
y\in U_{n}\setminus\{\overline{Q}_{n}\}
and
P_{y}\in\Delta_{+}\setminus\{Q_{n}\}
is the cube containing
y
with
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits P_{y}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q_{n}
, then
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(P_{y},Q_{n})\leq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dist}\nolimits(y,Q_{n})<\frac{1}{2}\delta b^{-j}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q_{n}<\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q_{n}
; moreover, if
R_{y}\in\mathsf{Child}^{j(Q_{n})}(P_{y})
is the descendent containing
y
, then
R_{y}\in\mathsf{Aux}(Q_{n})
. For the latter claim, simply note that any
R\in\mathsf{Child}^{j(Q_{n})}(P_{y})
with
\mathsf{cc}(P_{y},R)\geq 1
has

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(R,Q_{n})\geq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(R,\mathbb{X}\setminus P_{y})\geq\delta\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits R=\delta b^{-j(Q_{n})}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q_{n}>2\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dist}\nolimits(y,Q_{n}),

whence
y\not\in R
. Everything considered,
U_{n}
is an open set and
\overline{Q}_{n}\subset U_{n}\subset\overline{Q}_{n}\cup\bigcup\mathsf{Aux}(Q_{n})
. It follows that
\{U_{n}:n\geq 1\}
is an open cover of
\overline{Q}
, since
\{\overline{Q}_{n}:n\geq 1\}
covers
\overline{Q}
, and
\overline{Q}
is compact, since
\mathbb{X}
is proper. Hence, after relabeling, we can assume that
U_{1},\dots,U_{k}
cover
\overline{Q}
for some
k
. In particular,

\mathcal{F}:=\{\overline{Q}_{1},\dots,\overline{Q}_{k}\}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}\{\overline{R}:R\in\mathsf{Aux}(Q_{j})\}

is a finite cover of
\overline{Q}
, with the total weight of auxiliary cubes
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Aux}(Q_{j})}w(\overline{R})\leq\epsilon
. The subfamily
\{S\in\mathcal{F}:S\cap Q\neq\emptyset\}
also covers
\overline{Q}
. Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be any minimal subcover of
\{S\in\mathcal{F}:S\cap Q\neq\emptyset\}
.
Because
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is finite, we can use finite additivity of
w
to obtain

w(\overline{Q})=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}w(S)\leq\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}}w(S)\leq\epsilon+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}w(\overline{Q}_{n}).

Sending
\epsilon\to 0
confirms that
w
is countably subadditive.
∎

Remark 4.15

(how to use this practically)
.

On a proper metric space
\mathbb{X}
, we choose
b>5
and fix a system
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
b
-adic cubes. To define a Radon measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{X}
with prescribed values on
\Delta_{+}
, we may casually

1.

assign some arbitrary finite value
\mu(Q)
for each
Q\in\Delta_{0}
and

2.

describe how to distribute the mass
\mu(Q)
for each
Q\in\Delta_{+}
among its children in any way such that
\mu(Q)=\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Child}(Q)}\mu(R)
and
\mu(Q^{\downarrow})\geq p\,\mu(Q)
with
0<p\leq 1
independent of
Q
.

Then the measure
\mu
exists and is unique. Indeed, to show existence, define a weight
w
such that
w(\overline{Q}):=\mu(Q)
for all
Q\in\Delta_{+}
. Then
\mu_{w}
is a Radon measure with
\mu_{w}(Q)=\mu(Q)
and
\mu_{w}(\partial Q)=0
for all
Q\in\Delta_{+}
by Lemma
4.14
. We then relabel
\mu_{w}
as
\mu
and forget about the weight
w
. For uniqueness, simply note that the values of
\mu
on
\Delta_{+}
determine the values of
\mu
on open sets, and thus, determine the values of
\mu
on arbitrary sets, because
\mu
is Radon.

5
Estimates for quasi-Bernoulli measures

We begin by describing the measures in Theorem
1.1
in a special case. The definition of the measures will rely in part on the following calculation, a simple exercise in calculus. For an introduction to the concept of entropy, we refer the reader to
[
CT06
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every scaling factor
b>1
and integer
N\geq 1
, the entropy function
h_{b,N}:(0,1/N]\rightarrow(0,\infty)
given by

h_{b,N}(\delta):=(N-1)\delta\log_{b}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)+(1-(N-1)\delta)\log_{b}\left(\frac{1}{1-(N-1)\delta}\right)

(5.1)

is differentiable, monotone increasing,
h_{b,N}(0+)=0
, and
h_{b,N}(1/N)=\log_{b}(N)
.

Example 5.2

(Euclidean space)
.

Let
1\leq m\leq d-1
be integers and let
s\in(m-1,m)
. We define a self-similar Bernoulli-type measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
by specifying its values on (origin-based or standard) triadic cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as follows. Let
\delta>0
be a small number, whose exact value depending on
d
and
s
will be given momentarily.

1.

Declare
\mu(Q)=1
for any triadic cube of side length 1.

2.

For any triadic cube
Q
of side length at most 1, declare
\mu(R)=\delta\mu(Q)
for all non-central children
R
of
Q
and declare
\mu(Q^{\downarrow})=(1-(3^{d}-1)\delta)\mu(Q)
.

That is to say, below scale 1,
\mu
is defined by concentrating most of the mass in the center of a cube. The measure
\mu
is a doubling measure (see
[
BLZ23
, §8.2]
or
[
KRS12
, §3]
for sample details) and the Hausdorff and packing dimension of
\mu
is exact (see e.g.
[
Heu07
]
or
[
BP17
, §1.5]
) and is given by the entropy formula

h_{3,3^{d}}(\delta)=(3^{d}-1)\delta\log_{3}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)+(1-(3^{d}-1)\delta)\log_{3}\left(\frac{1}{1-(3^{d}-1)\delta}\right).

(5.2)

To force
\mu
to have dimension
s
, we simply choose
\delta=\delta(d,s)
so that
h_{3,3^{d}}(\delta)=s
. Because
s>m-1
, we immediately see that
\mu
is purely
(m-1)
-unrectifiable. Since
0<s<d
, it can be shown using the law of the iterated logarithm (see e.g.
[
BH10
, Theorem 3.1]
or
[
LW04
, Theorem 1.1]
) that at
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\downarrow 0}\dfrac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{s}}=\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\mu(Q_{k}(x))}{3^{-ks}}=0,\quad\displaystyle\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\dfrac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{s}}=\displaystyle\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\mu(Q_{k}(x))}{3^{-ks}}=\infty,

(5.3)

where
Q_{k}(x)
denotes the triadic cube of side length
3^{-k}
containing
x
. This means that it is impossible to use Theorem
3.1
(or the earlier results of
[
MM88
]
or
[
BV19
]
) to verify that
\mu
is
m
-rectifiable. Nevertheless, using some estimates developed later in this section (inspired by
[
GKS10
]
) together with either Theorem
2.5
or Corollary
2.8
, it can be shown that
\mu
is
m
-rectifiable (see §
6.1
).

Up to technical details, the measures in Theorem
1.1
on an Ahlfors regular metric space are defined analogously to the measures in Example
5.2
. All difficulties stem from imprecise and locally varying counts of metric cubes. Instead of using two weights per cube to define the mass of children, we will need three. See Figure
5.1
.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
b>1
and
L
and
M
are integers such that
0\leq L\leq b^{y}
and
M\geq b^{s}
for some
s,y>0
, then there exists a number
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(b,y,s)
such that for all
0\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
, there exist unique numbers
\beta=\beta(\alpha,b,y,s,L,M)
and
\gamma=\gamma(\alpha,b,y,s,L,M)
such that

L\alpha+(M-1)\beta+\gamma=1

(5.4)

and the entropy function

h_{b,L,M}(\alpha,\beta):=L\alpha\log_{b}(1/\alpha)+(M-1)\beta\log_{b}(1/\beta)+\gamma\log_{b}(1/\gamma)=s.

(5.5)

We may always bound
L\alpha\log_{b}(1/\alpha)\leq\min(1,s)/e
,
L\alpha\leq\min(1,s^{2})/e^{2}
,

\gamma\geq 1-L\alpha-\frac{s-L\alpha\log_{b}(1/\alpha)}{\log_{b}(M-1)}\geq 1-\frac{\min(1,s^{2})}{e^{2}}-\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right)\frac{s}{\log_{b}(M-1)},

(5.6)

\text{and}\qquad\gamma\geq\frac{1-L\alpha}{M}\geq\frac{1}{M}\left(1-\frac{\min(1,s^{2})}{e^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{M}\left(1-\frac{1}{e^{2}}\right).

(5.7)

Moreover, if
2e^{2}\log_{b}(e^{2})\leq(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{e})s
, then

\beta\geq\frac{s}{2(M-1)\log_{b}(M-1)}.

(5.8)

Proof.

Assign
z:=\max(1,y)
and
t:=\min(1,s)
. The function
x\mapsto x\log_{b}(1/x)
is strictly increasing on
(0,1/e]
and takes its maximum value
1/(e\ln(b))
at
x=1/e
. Since

b^{z}\geq z\ln(b)\geq\ln(b)\geq(t/e)e\ln(b),

there is a unique number
a\in(0,1/e]
such that
b^{z}a\log_{b}(1/a)=t/e
. Using the comparison
\ln(x)\leq(2/e)x^{1/2}
for
x>0
, we see that

a=\frac{t\ln(b)}{eb^{z}\ln(1/a)}\geq a^{1/2}\frac{t\ln(b)}{2b^{z}}\quad\text{whence}\quad a\geq\left(\frac{t\ln(b)}{2b^{z}}\right)^{2}.

With partial foresight, we choose

\alpha_{0}:=\min\bigg(\Big(\frac{t\ln(b)}{2b^{z}}\Big)^{2},\frac{1}{b^{s}}\bigg),\qquad\bigg[\text{note that }\alpha_{0}=\Big(\frac{t\ln(b)}{2b^{z}}\Big)^{2}\text{ when }s\leq y,\bigg]

(5.9)

which depends only on
b
,
y
, and
s
. Let
0\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
be given. Using
\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}\leq\min(a,b^{-s})
and the upper bound on
L
, we see that

\begin{array}[]{c}L\alpha s\leq L\alpha\log_{b}(1/\alpha)\leq b^{z}a\log_{b}(1/a)=\min(1,s)/e,\\
\text{and}\quad L\alpha\leq t^{2}\ln(b)^{2}/4b\leq\min(1,s^{2})/e^{2}.\end{array}

(5.10)

To continue, we abbreviate
w:=1-L\alpha\in(0,1]
and consider the function

h(x):=(M-1)x\log_{b}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)+(w-(M-1)x)\log_{b}\left(\frac{1}{w-(M-1)x}\right)\quad\text{on }(0,w/M].

Observe that
h
is differentiable and strictly increasing on
(0,w/M]
,
h(0+)=0
, and

h(w/M)=w\log_{b}(M/w)\geq w\log_{b}(M)\geq(1-L\alpha)s\geq s-L\alpha\log_{b}(1/\alpha)\geq s(1-1/e)

by the lower bound on
M
and (
5.10
). Thus, there exists some unique
\beta=\beta(\alpha,b,y,s,L,M)
such that
h(\beta)=s-L\alpha\log_{b}(1/\alpha)
. Setting
\gamma:=w-(M-1)\beta
, we arrive at the desired conditions (
5.4
) and (
5.5
).

To find lower bounds for
\beta
and
\gamma
, set
\epsilon=(M-1)\beta
and write

\begin{split}s-L\alpha\log_{b}(1/\alpha)&=\epsilon\log_{b}(M-1)+\underbrace{\epsilon\log_{b}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)+(w-\epsilon)\log_{b}\left(\frac{1}{w-\epsilon}\right)}_{I(\epsilon)}\\
&\leq\epsilon\log_{b}(M-1)+w\log_{b}(2/w)\leq\epsilon\log_{b}(M-1)+2e^{2}\log_{b}(e^{2}).\end{split}

(5.11)

To verify the first inequality above, simply check that the function
I(\epsilon)
has a unique critical point at
\epsilon=w/2(M-1)
. To verify the second, check that
w\log_{b}(2/w)
has a unique critical point at
w=2e^{-2}
. Since
I(\epsilon)\geq 0
, the first line in (
5.11
) and (
5.10
) yield:

\gamma=1-L\alpha-\epsilon\geq 1-L\alpha-\frac{s-L\alpha\log_{b}(1/\alpha)}{\log_{b}(M-1)}\geq 1-\frac{\min(1,s^{2})}{e^{2}}-\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right)\frac{s}{\log_{b}(M-1)}.

This confirms (
5.6
). To check (
5.7
), note that
\gamma=w-(M-1)\beta\geq w/M
(since
\beta\leq w/M
) and recall that
w=1-L\alpha\geq 1-t^{2}/e^{2}
. Finally, if
2e^{2}\log_{b}(e^{2})\leq(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{e})s
, then (
5.11
) and (
5.10
) yield (
5.8
):

\beta=\frac{\epsilon}{M-1}\geq\frac{s-L\alpha\log_{b}(1/\alpha)-2e^{2}\log_{b}(e^{2})}{(M-1)\log_{b}(M-1)}\geq\frac{s}{2(M-1)\log_{b}(M-1)}.\qed

Definition 5.4

(quasi-Bernoulli measures)
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a complete Ahlfors
q
-regular metric space with
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits\mathbb{X}\geq 2.1
, let
\nu
be a doubling measure on
\mathbb{X}
, and let
\mathbf{s}=(s_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive numbers (“target dimensions”) such that

s_{*}:=\inf_{k\geq 1}s_{k}>0\quad\text{and}\quad s^{*}:=\sup_{k\geq 1}s_{k}<q.

(5.12)

Let
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a system of
b
-adic cubes for
\mathbb{X}
for some large
b\geq 47
. For all
Q\in\Delta
, assign
L_{Q}:=\#\mathsf{Outer}(Q)
,
M_{Q}:=\#\mathsf{Inner}(Q)
, and
N_{Q}:=\#\mathsf{Child}(Q)
. We require that
b
be large enough depending on at most
\mathbb{X}
and
s^{*}
so that

M_{Q}\geq b^{s^{*}}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{Q}\leq N_{Q}\leq b^{q+1}\quad\text{for all $Q\in\Delta_{+}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}$.}

(5.13)

(See Corollary
4.9
.) Let
0<\alpha\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\min\{s_{*},1\}\ln(b)b^{-(q+1)}\right)^{2}
be a given weight; cf. (
5.9
). For all
k\geq 0
and
Q\in\Delta_{k}
, we may use Lemma
5.3
to define unique weights

\beta_{Q}=\beta(\alpha,b,q+1,s_{k+1},L_{Q},M_{Q})\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma_{Q}=\gamma(\alpha,b,q+1,s_{k+1},L_{Q},M_{Q})

satisfying

1=L_{Q}\alpha+(M_{Q}-1)\beta_{Q}+\gamma_{Q}\quad\text{and}\quad h_{b,L_{Q},M_{Q}}(\alpha,\beta_{Q})=s_{k+1}.

(5.14)

We specify a Radon measure
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
on
\mathbb{X}
by specifying its values on cubes as follows:

1.

Declare
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q):=\nu(Q)
for all
Q\in\Delta_{0}
.

2.

For all
k\geq 0
and
Q\in\Delta_{k}
, declare
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R):=\alpha\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)
for all
R\in\mathsf{Outer}(Q)
, declare
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R):=\beta_{Q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)
for all
R\in\mathsf{Inner}(Q)\setminus\{Q^{\downarrow}\}
, and declare
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{\downarrow}):=\gamma_{Q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)
.

We call
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
a
quasi-Bernoulli measure on

\mathbb{X}

with target dimensions

\mathbf{s}
,
background measure

\nu
, and
outer weight

\alpha
. (Of course,
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
also depends on the choice of
b
and
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
.)

Lemma 5.5

(existence and doubling)
.

For any sequence
\mathbf{s}=(s_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of target dimensions, background measure
\nu
, and outer weight
\alpha
, the quasi-Bernoulli measure
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
exists and

0<\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(B(x,r))<\infty\quad\text{for all $x\in\mathbb{X}$ and $r>0$}.

(5.15)

If
b
is large enough so that
2e^{2}\log_{b}(e^{2})\leq s_{*}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{e})
, then
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
is doubling and

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(B(x,2r))\lesssim_{\nu,\alpha,b,s_{*},q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(B(x,r))\quad\text{for all $x\in\mathbb{X}$ and $r>0$,}

(5.16)

where the dependence on
\nu
is on the doubling constant of
\nu
.

Proof.

Existence and uniqueness.
On the one hand, if
Q\in\Delta_{+}
and
\log_{b}(M_{Q}-1)\geq 2s^{*}
, then
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{\downarrow})=\gamma_{Q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)\geq(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{e^{2}}+\frac{1}{2e})\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)
by (
5.6
). On the other hand, if
\log_{b}(M_{Q}-1)\leq 2s^{*}
, then
M_{Q}\leq 1+b^{2s^{*}}
and
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{\downarrow})=\gamma_{Q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)\gtrsim_{b,s^{*}}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)
by (
5.7
). Therefore, by (
5.14
) and Remark
4.15
, the measure
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
exists and is the unique Radon measure taking the indicated values on
\Delta_{+}
. Because
0<\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)<\infty
for all
Q\in\Delta
, every ball in
\mathbb{X}
contains some cube in
\Delta
, and every ball is contained in some cube in
\Delta
(see Remark
4.3
), the measure
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
has full support (
5.15
).

Doubling on large radii.
On balls with large radii,
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
inherits the doubling property from
\nu
. Let
x\in\mathbb{X}
and suppose that
2C_{b}b^{-k}\leq r<2C_{b}b^{-(k-1)}
for some integer
k\leq 0
. On the one hand, if we let
Q\in\Delta_{k}
be the unique cube of side length
b^{-k}
containing
x
, then

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(B(x,r))\geq\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)=\nu(Q)\geq\nu(U_{Q})\geq\nu(B(x_{Q},\tfrac{1}{2}c_{b}b^{-k})).

On the other hand, let
\mathcal{R}=\{R\in\Delta_{0}:R\cap B(x,2r)\neq\emptyset\}
. For any
R\in\mathcal{R}
and
y\in R
,

|y-x_{Q}|\leq|y-x|+|x-x_{Q}|\leq(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits R+2r)+r\leq 7C_{b}b^{-(k-1)}=(14bC_{b}/c_{b})\tfrac{1}{2}c_{b}b^{-k}.

Hence

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(B(x,2r))\leq\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R)=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\nu(R)\leq\nu(B(x_{Q},(14bC_{b}/c_{b})\tfrac{1}{2}c_{b}b^{-k})).

It follows that

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(B(x,2r))\leq\nu(B(x_{Q},(14bC_{b}/c_{b})\tfrac{1}{2}c_{b}b^{-k}))\lesssim_{\nu,b}\nu(B(x_{Q},\tfrac{1}{2}c_{b}b^{-k}))\leq\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(B(x,r)),

where the implicit constant depends only on the doubling constant for
\nu
and
b
.

b
-adic doubling.
Assume that
2e^{2}\log_{b}(e^{2})\leq s_{*}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{e})
. Then
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
is
b
-adic doubling in the sense that

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R)\gtrsim_{\nu,\alpha,b,s_{*},q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)\quad\text{for all $Q\in\Delta$ and $R\in\mathsf{Child}(Q)$}.

(5.17)

There are two regimes. First, if
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q=b^{-k}
for some
k\leq-1
, then

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R)=\nu(R)\gtrsim_{\nu,b}\nu(Q)=\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)

by an argument similar to the one in the previous paragraph, since
\nu
is doubling. Second, if
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q=b^{-k}
for some
k\geq 0
, then either
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R)=\alpha\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)
or
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R)=\beta_{Q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)
or
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R)=\gamma_{Q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)
, so it suffices to find a common lower bound for all of the weights. In the existence paragraph, we already showed that
\gamma_{Q}\gtrsim_{b,s^{*}}1
. Since
2e^{2}\log_{b}(e^{2})\leq s_{*}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{e})\leq s_{k+1}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{e})
, we also have
\beta_{Q}\gtrsim_{b,s_{*},q}1
by (
5.8
) and (
5.13
). Finally, the weight
\alpha>0
by definition. Therefore,
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
is
b
-adic doubling.

Doubling on small radii.
The key point is that the weights
\alpha
attached to children
R
of
Q\in\Delta_{k}
(
k\geq 0
) that lie near
\partial Q
do not depend on
Q
. This idea is already present in
[
KRS12
, §3]
and
[
BLZ23
, §8]
. We continue to assume that
2e^{2}\log_{b}(e^{2})\leq s_{*}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{e})
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{X}
and suppose that
2C_{b}b^{-k}\leq r<2C_{b}b^{-(k-1)}
for some integer
k\geq 1
. Let
Q
be the unique cube of side length
b^{-k}
containing
x
and let
\mathcal{S}=\{R\in\Delta_{k-1}:R\cap B(x,2r)\neq\emptyset\}
. It is easy to see using the doubling property of
\nu
that
\#\mathcal{S}\lesssim_{\nu,b}1
(cf. proof of Lemma
4.6
). Thus, if we can show that
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R)\lesssim_{\nu,\alpha,b,s_{*},q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(S)
for all
R,S\in\mathcal{S}
, then

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(B(x,2r))\leq\sum_{R\in\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R)\lesssim_{\nu,b,s_{*},q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{\uparrow})\lesssim_{\nu,\alpha,b,s_{*},q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)\leq\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(B(x,r)).

Fix
R,S\in\mathcal{S}
with
R\neq S
. Since
R
and
S
both intersect
B(x,2r)
, we have
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(R,S)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits B(x,2r)\leq 8C_{b}\,b^{-(k-1)}<9\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits R
, since
b\geq 47
implies
C_{b}\leq 47/46
. To proceed, let us assign labels to the ancestors of
R
and
S
: for all
0\leq j\leq k-1
, let
Q^{R}_{j}
and
Q^{S}_{j}
be unique cubes in
\Delta_{j}
containing
R
and
S
, respectively. Note that
Q^{R}_{k-1}=R
and
Q^{S}_{k-1}=S
. Let
i\geq 0
be the least index such that
Q^{R}_{i}\neq Q^{S}_{i}
. If
i=0
, then
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{R}_{0})=\nu(Q^{R}_{0})
,
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{S}_{0})=\nu(Q^{S}_{0})
, and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(Q^{R}_{0},Q^{S}_{0})\leq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(R,S)<9
, so

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{R}_{0})\lesssim_{\nu}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{S}_{0})\lesssim_{\nu}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{R}_{0})

(5.18)

by the doubling of
\nu
. If
i\geq 1
, then
(Q^{R}_{i})^{\uparrow}=Q^{R}_{i-1}=Q^{S}_{i-1}=(Q^{S}_{i})^{\uparrow}
. Hence

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{R}_{i})\leq\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{R}_{i-1})=\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{S}_{i-1})\lesssim_{\nu,\alpha,b,s_{*},q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{S}_{i})

(5.19)

by (
5.17
). The same argument shows
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{S}_{i})\lesssim_{\nu,\alpha,b,s_{*},q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{R}_{i})
. For all
i+1\leq j\leq k-1
, we have
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(Q^{R}_{j},\mathbb{X}\setminus Q^{R}_{j-1})\leq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{gap}\nolimits(R,S)<9\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits R\leq 9\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q^{R}_{j}
, whence
Q^{R}_{j}\in\mathsf{Outer}(Q^{R}_{j-1})
by Lemma
4.7
. The same is true if we swap the role of
R
and
S
. Thus,
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{R}_{j})=\alpha\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{R}_{j-1})
and
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{S}_{j})=\alpha\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{S}_{j-1})
for all
i+1\leq j\leq k-1
. It follows that

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R)=\alpha^{k-1-i}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{R}_{i})\lesssim_{\nu,\alpha,b,s_{*},q}\alpha^{k-1-i}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q^{S}_{i})=\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(S).

(5.20)

Similarly,
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(S)\lesssim_{\nu,b,s_{*},q}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R)
. Therefore,
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
is a doubling measure.
∎

Lemma 5.6

(dimension)
.

If
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
is
b
-adic doubling (e.g. if
2e^{2}\log_{b}(e^{2})\leq s_{*}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{e})
), then

\underline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu_{\mathbf{s}}=\overline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu_{\mathbf{s}}=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j},\quad\underline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu_{\mathbf{s}}=\overline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu_{\mathbf{s}}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j}.

(5.21)

Proof.

To prove (
5.21
), we may fix
Q_{0}\in\Delta_{0}
and show that (see §
4.2
)

\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{k}(x))}{\log(b^{-k})}=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j},\quad\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{k}(x))}{\log(b^{-k})}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j}

at
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
-a.e.
x\in Q_{0}
, where
Q_{k}(x)\in\Delta_{k}
is the cube containing
x
. For convenience, so that we may adopt the probabilistic viewpoint, we temporarily rescale
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
so that
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{0})=1
. Let
\mathbb{E}
denote the expectation with respect to the probability measure
\mathbb{P}:=\mu_{\mathbf{s}}\hbox{ {\vrule height=6.25963pt}{\leaders{\hrule}{\hskip 5.69046pt}} }Q_{0}
. Following the same plan as in
[
Heu07
, §3]
, for each
k\geq 1
we define a random variable
X_{k}
on
Q_{0}
by

X_{k}(x)=-\log_{b}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{k}(x))+\log_{b}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{k-1}(x)).

Note that
|X_{k}(x)|\lesssim_{\mu_{\mathbf{s}}}1
for all
x\in Q_{0}
, since
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
is
b
-adic doubling. The relevance of these random variables for the problem at hand is that

\frac{S_{k}(x)}{k}:=\frac{X_{1}(x)+\cdots+X_{k}(x)}{k}=\frac{\log\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{k}(x))}{\log(b^{-k})}\quad\text{for all }x\in Q_{0},

(5.22)

since
\log_{b}(\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{0}(x))=\log_{b}(\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{0}))=0
by our assumption that
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{0})=1
.

We claim that the random variables
X_{k}
are uncorrelated, i.e.
\mathbb{E}(X_{i}X_{j})=\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\mathbb{E}(X_{j})
for all
i\neq j
. For each
j\geq 1
,
X_{j}
is constant on each cube
R\in\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q_{0})
and the value
X_{j}(R)
that it takes can be computed knowing only type of child that
R
is:

X_{j}(R)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\log_{b}(1/\alpha)&\text{when }R\in\mathsf{Outer}(R^{\uparrow}),\\
\log_{b}(1/\beta_{Q})&\text{when }R\in\mathsf{Inner}(R^{\uparrow})\setminus\{R^{\uparrow\downarrow}\},\\
\log_{b}(1/\gamma_{Q})&\text{when }R=R^{\uparrow\downarrow}.\end{array}\right.

(5.23)

Thus, by (
5.5
) and (
5.14
), we obtain

\mathbb{E}(X_{j})=\!\!\!\sum_{Q\in\mathsf{Child}^{j-1}(Q_{0})}\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Child}(Q)}\!\!\!X_{j}(R)\mu(R)=\!\!\!\sum_{Q\in\mathsf{Child}^{j-1}(Q_{0})}\!\!\!h_{b,L_{Q},M_{Q}}(\alpha,\beta_{Q})\,\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)=s_{j}.

(5.24)

A similar computation shows that
\mathbb{E}(X_{i}X_{j})=s_{i}s_{j}
when
1\leq i<j
. Indeed, since
X_{i}
takes constant values on each cube in
\Delta_{i}
and
X_{j}
takes constant values on each cube in
\Delta_{j}
, and
j>i
, we witness that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}(X_{i}X_{j})&=\sum_{P\in\mathsf{Child}^{i-1}(Q_{0})}\sum_{Q\in\mathsf{Child}(P)}\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Child}^{j-1-i}(Q)}\sum_{S\in\mathsf{Child}(R)}X_{i}(Q)X_{j}(S)\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(S)\\
&=\sum_{P\in\mathsf{Child}^{i-1}(Q_{0})}\sum_{Q\in\mathsf{Child}(P)}X_{i}(Q)\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Child}^{j-1-i}(Q)}h_{b,L_{R},M_{R}}(\alpha,\beta_{R})\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R)\\
&=\sum_{P\in\mathsf{Child}^{i-1}(Q_{0})}\sum_{Q\in\mathsf{Child}(P)}X_{i}(Q)\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q)s_{j}\\
&=s_{i}s_{j}.\end{split}

(5.25)

Because the random variables
X_{k}
are uncorrelated and are uniformly bounded in
L^{2}
(as they are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}
), the strong law of large numbers (see e.g.
[
Chu01
, Theorem 5.1.2]
) ensures that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{S_{k}-\mathbb{E}(S_{k})}{k}=0\quad\text{at $\mu_{\mathbf{s}}$-a.e.~$x\in Q_{0}$}.

(5.26)

Combining (
5.22
), (
5.24
), and (
5.26
), we conclude that at
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
-a.e.
x\in Q_{0}
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{k}(x))}{\log(b^{-k})}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}(X_{j})=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}s_{j}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{k}(x))}{\log(b^{-k})}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}(X_{j})=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}s_{j}.\qed

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a complete Ahlfors
q
-regular metric space. For any four numbers
d_{*}
,
d^{*}
,
D_{*}
,
D^{*}
satisfying
0<d_{*}\leq d^{*}\leq q
,
0<D_{*}\leq D^{*}\leq q
,
d_{*}\leq D_{*}
, and
d^{*}\leq D^{*}
, and
d_{*}=q\Leftrightarrow D_{*}=q
and
d^{*}=q\Leftrightarrow D^{*}=q
, there is a doubling measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{X}
such that

\underline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu=d_{*},\quad\overline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu=d^{*},\quad\underline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu=D_{*},\quad\overline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu=D^{*}.

(5.27)

Proof.

If
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits\mathbb{X}=0
, the conclusion is trivial. Thus, after scaling the metric if necessary, we may suppose that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits\mathbb{X}\geq 2.1
. Because the sum of two doubling measures is again a doubling measure, it suffices to prove that for any
0<d\leq D\leq q
with
d=q\Leftrightarrow D=q
, there exists a doubling measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{X}
such that
\underline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu=\overline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu=d
and
\underline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu=\overline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu=D
. If
d=D=q
, then we may simply take
\mu
to be any Ahlfors
q
-regular measure on
\mathbb{X}
. Otherwise,
0<d\leq D<q
. Choose any sequence
\mathbf{s}=(s_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
taking values in
\{d,D\}
such that
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}k^{-1}(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{k})=d
and
\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}k^{-1}(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{k})=D
. Let
\mu=\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
be a quasi-Bernoulli measure with target dimensions
\mathbf{s}
, built using a system of
b
-adic cubes with scaling factor
b
satisfying
2e^{2}\log_{b}(e^{2})\leq s_{*}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{e})=d(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{e})
. Then
\mu
exists and is doubling by Lemma
5.5
and has Hausdorff dimension
d
and packing dimension
D
by Lemma
5.6
.∎

Because the construction of the quasi-Bernoulli measure
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
demands that we take

as

(see Corollary

), we do not currently know how to build a doubling measure

-regular space such that
\underline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu=\overline{\dim}_{H}\,\mu<q=\underline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu=\overline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu
. Despite this technical obstruction, we cannot think of a compelling reason why such a measure should not exist.

Lemma 5.9

(measure concentration)
.

Let
Q\in\Delta
, let
0<t<1
, and suppose that
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R^{\downarrow})\geq(1-t)\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(R)
for all
R\in\bigcup_{j=0}^{n-1}\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)
, where
\mathsf{Child}^{0}(Q)=\{Q\}
. If
n
and
k
are positive integers and
tn\leq k\leq n
, then

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}\left(\textstyle\bigcup K_{Q}(n,k)\right)\geq\mu_{\mathbf{s}}\left(\textstyle\bigcup K_{Q}(n,k-1)\right)\geq\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{n}{2}\left[\frac{k}{n}-t\right]^{2}\right)\right)\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q).

(5.29)

Proof.

The first inequality in (
5.29
) is trivial and is recorded for the convenience of applying the lemma in §
6
. As to the main matter, we will derive the second inequality in (
5.29
) from Azuma’s inequality, a standard measure concentration estimate for martingales with bounded differences (see e.g.
[
CL06
, top of p. 96]
).
Let
\mathbb{P}
denote the Borel probability measure
(\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q))^{-1}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}\hbox{ {\vrule height=6.25963pt}{\leaders{\hrule}{\hskip 5.69046pt}} }Q
and let
\mathbb{E}
denote its expectation. For each integer
1\leq j\leq n
, define the random variable

Y_{j}:=\sum_{R\in\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)}\mathsf{ci}(R)\chi_{R}=\sum_{S\in\mathsf{Child}^{j-1}(Q)}\chi_{S^{\downarrow}}.

That is,
Y_{j}
is the sum of indicator functions for
j
-th generation descendants of
Q
that are the central child of their parents. By the hypothesis on the measure of central children, we have
1-t\leq\mathbb{E}(Y_{j})\leq 1
for all
j\geq 1
and

Q\setminus\textstyle\bigcup K_{Q}(n,k-1)=\{x\in Q:\sum_{j=1}^{n}Y_{j}(x)\leq n-k\}.

Next, define random variables
X_{0}\equiv 0
and
X_{j}:=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(Y_{i}-\mathbb{E}(Y_{i})\right)
for all
j\geq 1
. Then the sequence
X_{0},X_{1},\cdots
is a martingale with respect to the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{j}
is the
\sigma
-algebra generated
\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)
. Moreover, for all
j\geq 1
,
c_{j}:=\|X_{j}-X_{j-1}\|_{\infty}=\|Y_{j}-\mathbb{E}(Y_{j})\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. Suppose that
k-nt\geq 0
. Then, by Azuma’s inequality,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}Y_{j}\leq n-k\right)&=\mathbb{P}\left(X_{n}\leq n-k-\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(Y_{j})\right)\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}\left(X_{n}-X_{0}\leq-\left[k-nt\right]\right)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Azuma}}}{{\leq}}\exp\left(\frac{-\left[k-nt\right]^{2}}{2\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}^{2}}\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{n}{2}\left[\frac{k}{n}-t\right]^{2}\right).\end{split}

This yields (
5.29
).
∎

Lemma 5.10

(cardinality estimate)
.

Let
Q\in\Delta
and let
n\geq 1
. Suppose that
\hat{N}
is a positive number such that

\max\left\{N_{R}:0\leq i\leq n-1,\;R\in\mathsf{Child}^{i}(Q)\right\}\leq\hat{N},\quad\text{where }N_{R}=\#\mathsf{Child}(R).

(5.30)

If
n
and
k
are positive integers with
k\leq n\hat{N}/(\hat{N}+1)
, then

\#K_{Q}(n,k)\leq\hat{N}^{k}\frac{n^{n}}{k^{k}(n-k)^{n-k}}\leq b^{n\left((k/n)\log_{b}(\hat{N})+\log_{b}(2)\right)}\quad\text{for all $b>1$}.

(5.31)

Proof.

Let
1\leq k\leq n
. Since
K_{Q}(n,k)=\bigcup_{i=n-k}^{n}\{R\in\mathsf{Child}^{n}(Q):\mathsf{cc}(Q,R)=i\}
, for any real-valued
x\geq 1
, we may bound

\#K_{Q}(n,k)\leq\sum_{i=n-k}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\hat{N}^{n-i}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{n}{j}\hat{N}^{j}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\hat{N}^{j}x^{k-j}=x^{k-n}(x+\hat{N})^{n}=:f(x).

The function
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
has a unique critical point

x_{0}=\frac{\hat{N}(n-k)}{k}

and
x_{0}\geq 1
precisely when
k\leq n\hat{N}/(\hat{N}+1)
. Since
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0+}f(x)=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}f(x)=\infty
, the first upper bound on
\#K_{Q}(n,k)
in (
5.31
) is achieved by choosing
x=x_{0}
. The second bound in (
5.31
) follows by expanding
k=(k/n)n
, writing
x=b^{\log_{b}(x)}
, and applying the entropy bound
\epsilon\log_{b}(1/\epsilon)+(1-\epsilon)\log_{b}(1/(1-\epsilon))\leq\log_{b}(2)
for all
0<\epsilon<1
.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

Lemma
5.9
requires a lower bound
k\gtrsim_{t}n
whereas Lemma
5.10
requires an upper bound
k\lesssim_{\hat{N}}n
.

6
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Suppose that
\mathbb{X}
is a complete Ahlfors
q
-regular metric space,
m\geq 1
is an integer with
q>m-1
, and
0<s_{H}\leq s_{P}<q
are real numbers with
m-1<s_{P}<m
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits\mathbb{X}\geq 2.1
. Let
\nu
be any doubling measure on
\mathbb{X}
. Let
b\geq 47
be large and fixed, ultimately depending on at most
q
,
s_{H}
,
s_{P}
, and
\mathbb{X}
. Let
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be any system of
b
-adic cubes for
\mathbb{X}
(see §
4.1
). By Lemma
4.6
and Lemma
4.7
, there exists
C_{\mathbb{X}}>1
such that for all
Q\in\Delta_{+}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}
and
j\geq 1
,

C_{\mathbb{X}}^{-1}b^{jq}\leq\#\mathsf{Child}^{j}(Q)\leq C_{\mathbb{X}}b^{jq}\quad\text{and}\quad\#\mathsf{Inner}(Q)\geq C_{\mathbb{X}}^{-1}b^{q}.

(6.1)

Let
\mathbf{s}=(s_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of numbers taking values in
[s_{H},s_{P}]
such that

\displaystyle s_{*}

\displaystyle=\inf_{k\geq 1}s_{k}=s_{H}=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j},

(6.2)

\displaystyle s^{*}

\displaystyle=\sup_{k\geq 1}s_{k}=s_{P}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j}.

(6.3)

Let
\alpha>0
be small and fixed, ultimately depending on at most
q
,
s_{H}
,
s_{P}
,
b
, and
\mathbb{X}
. Finally, let
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
be the quasi-Bernoulli measure with target dimensions
\mathbf{s}
, background measure
\nu
, and outer weight
\alpha
as in Definition
5.4
. To prove the theorem, we show that (
1.2
)–(
1.7
) hold with
\mu=\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
. A lot of the work has been done already. For large enough
b
, the measure
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
is doubling in the sense of (
1.2
) by Lemma
5.5
and
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
satisfies (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) by Theorem
4.10
, Lemma
5.6
, (
6.2
), and (
6.3
). As we already noted in the introduction,
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
is purely
(m-1)
-unrectifiable in the sense of (
1.5
), because
\underline{\dim}_{P}\,\mu_{\mathbf{s}}=s_{P}>m-1
and Lipschitz images of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{m-1}
have packing dimension at most
m-1
. Finally, we will prove (
1.6
) in §
6.1
and prove (
1.7
) in §
6.2
.

6.1
Rectifiability

We would like to prove that
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
is
m
-rectifiable in the sense of (
1.6
). Since
\Delta_{0}
is countable and covers
\mathbb{X}
, it suffices to fix
Q_{0}\in\Delta_{0}
and prove that
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}\hbox{ {\vrule height=6.25963pt}{\leaders{\hrule}{\hskip 5.69046pt}} }Q_{0}
is
m
-rectifiable. Further, it is enough to fix
0<\theta<1
(close to 1) and find a tree of sets
\mathcal{T}=\bigcup_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}_{l}
such that
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T}))>\theta\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{0})
and
\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T})
lies in the image of some Lipschitz map
f:E\subset[0,1]^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}
.

Definition of
\mathcal{T}
.
For the remainder of §
6.1
, we fix any number
\tau=\tau(q,m,s_{P})>0
satisfying

(1+3\tau)\frac{s_{P}}{m}<1\quad\text{and}\quad a:=(1+2\tau)\frac{s_{P}}{q}\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1).

(6.4)

Write
a=u/v
in reduced form, i.e.
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}
,
v\geq 1
, and
\mathrm{gcd}(u,v)=1
. Let
n_{0}\geq v
be a large integer such that
n_{0}\equiv 0\pmod{v}
. For all
i\geq 1
, we put
n_{i}:=in_{0}
and
k_{i}:=an_{i}
. Since
v
divides
n_{i}
, we get that
k_{i}
is also an integer. We build
\mathcal{T}
using induction. Initialize
\mathcal{T}_{0}=\{Q_{0}\}
. Then, assuming that the level
\mathcal{T}_{j}
of
\mathcal{T}
has been defined for some
j\geq 0
, define the next level
\mathcal{T}_{j+1}
by specifying that

\mathsf{Child}_{\mathcal{T}}(Q)\cap\mathcal{T}_{j+1}=K_{Q}(n_{j+1},k_{j+1})\quad\text{for all }Q\in\mathcal{T}_{j},

(6.5)

with
K_{Q}(n,k)
as in (
5.28
). Note that

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{side}\nolimits Q=b^{-(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j})}=b^{-\frac{1}{2}j(j+1)n_{0}}\quad\text{for any $j\geq 0$ and $Q\in\mathcal{T}_{j}$}.

(6.6)

We show that
\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T})
has significant measure.
In view of (
6.1
), by taking
b
to be large enough depending only on
q
,
C_{\mathbb{X}}
, and
\tau
(hence only on
q
,
C_{\mathbb{X}}
,
m
, and
s_{P}
) and
\alpha>0
to be sufficiently small depending on
q
,
C_{\mathbb{X}}
,
b
, and
\tau
(hence only on
q
,
C_{\mathbb{X}}
,
b
,
m
, and
s_{P}
), we may arrange things so that for all
Q\in\Delta_{+}
,

L_{Q}\alpha+\frac{s_{P}-L_{Q}\alpha\log_{b}(1/\alpha)}{\log_{b}(M_{Q}-1)}\leq C_{\mathbb{X}}b^{q}\alpha+\frac{s_{P}}{q-\log_{b}(2C_{\mathbb{X}})}\leq(1+\tau)\frac{s_{P}}{q}=:t,

(6.7)

where
L_{Q}=\#\mathsf{Outer}(Q)
and
M_{Q}=\#\mathsf{Inner}(Q)
. Since
k_{j+1}/n_{j+1}=a=t+\tau s_{P}/q>t
, from (
5.6
), (
5.29
), and (
6.7
), we conclude that for all
j\geq 0
and all
Q\in\mathcal{T}_{j}
,

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}\left(\textstyle\bigcup K_{Q}(n_{j+1},k_{j+1})\right)\geq\left(1-\exp\left(-n_{j+1}\frac{\tau^{2}s_{P}^{2}}{2q^{2}}\right)\right)\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q).

(6.8)

Thus, by continuity from above,

\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T}))=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{\mathbf{s}}\left(\textstyle\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{j}\right)=\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{0})\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\exp\left(-in_{0}\frac{\tau^{2}s_{P}^{2}}{2q^{2}}\right)\right)>0,

(6.9)

where the infinite product is positive, since
0<c:=\exp(-n_{0}\tau^{2}s_{P}^{2}/(2q^{2}))<1
. As the parameter
n_{0}\rightarrow\infty
, the number
c\rightarrow 0
and the infinite product in (
6.9
) tends to
1
. Therefore, by taking
n_{0}
to be large enough depending only on
q
,
s_{P}
,
\tau
, and
\theta
(hence only on
q
,
m
,
s_{P}
, and
\theta
), we obtain
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T}))\geq\theta\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(Q_{0})
.

We show that
\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T})
is contained in a Lipschitz image of
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Write
\hat{N}:=C_{\mathbb{X}}b^{q}
. Taking
b
to be large enough depending only on
C_{\mathbb{X}}
,
q
,
s_{P}
, and
\tau
(hence only on
C_{\mathbb{X}}
,
q
,
s_{P}
, and
m
) ensures that
a=(1+2\tau)s_{P}/q<\hat{N}/(\hat{N}+1)
. By Lemma
5.10
, for all
j\geq 0
and
Q\in\mathcal{T}_{j}
,

\#\mathsf{Child}_{\mathcal{T}}(Q)=\#K_{Q}(n_{j+1},k_{j+1})\leq b^{(j+1)n_{0}\left((1+2\tau)(s_{P}/q)\log_{b}\hat{N}+\log_{b}(2)\right)}

(6.10)

Note that
\log_{b}(\hat{N})=q+\log_{b}(C_{\mathbb{X}})
. Increasing the scaling factor
b
as necessary (depending only on
q
,
C_{\mathbb{X}}
,
m
, and
s_{P}
), we can arrange for
(1+2\tau)(s_{P}/q)\log_{b}(C_{\mathbb{X}})+\log_{b}(2)\leq\tau s_{P}
. Then

N_{j}=\max_{Q\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}\#\mathsf{Child}_{\mathcal{T}}(Q)\leq b^{(j+1)n_{0}(1+3\tau)s_{P}},\quad D_{j}=\max_{Q\in\mathcal{T}_{j}}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}\nolimits Q\leq 2C_{b}\,b^{-\frac{1}{2}j(j+1)n_{0}}.

(6.11)

Hence

\check{S}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{i=0}^{j}N_{i}^{1/m}\right)D_{j}\leq 2C_{b}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b^{\frac{1}{2}n_{0}\left[(j+1)(j+2)(1+3\tau)(s_{P}/m)-j(j+1)\right]}.

(6.12)

Because
\eta:=1-(1+3\tau)s_{P}/m>0
, see (
6.4
), the expression in square brackets

[\cdots]\leq-\eta j^{2}+O(j)\leq-\frac{1}{2}\eta j^{2}\quad\text{for }j\gg_{\eta}1.

(Here the big-

notation means

for all

.) Thus, we certainly know that
\check{S}<\infty
. We are almost ready to invoke Theorem
2.5
.

To proceed, note that for any
j\geq 0
and any
Q\in\mathcal{T}_{j}
, we can bound

N_{j}\geq\#\mathsf{Child}_{\mathcal{T}}(Q)=\#K_{Q}(n_{j+1},k_{j+1})\geq\#\mathsf{Child}_{\Delta}^{k_{j+1}}(Q)\geq C_{\mathbb{X}}^{-1}b^{(j+1)n_{0}(1+2\tau)s_{P}},

(6.13)

where the final inequality is by (
6.1
). (The penultimate inequality holds, because there is a bijection between
\mathsf{Child}_{\Delta}^{k_{j+1}}(Q)
and the set of all
R\in\mathsf{Child}_{\Delta}^{n_{j+1}}(Q)
such that
\mathsf{ci}(R)=1
and whose
n_{j+1}-k_{j+1}-1
immediate ancestors
P
also satisfy
\mathsf{ci}(P)=1
.) It follows that
C=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}N_{j}^{-1/m}<\infty
, and thus, in addition to
\check{S}<\infty
, we have

S=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{i=0}^{j}\lceil N_{i}^{1/m}\rceil\right)D_{j}\leq e^{C}\check{S}<\infty

(6.14)

by Remark
2.6
. Therefore, by Theorem
2.5
, there exists a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
and a Lipschitz map
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{X}
such that
f(E)\supset\mathsf{Leaves}(\mathcal{T})
. This completes the proof of (
1.6
).

6.2
Doubling measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
vanish on porous sets

To complete the proof of the theorem, all that remains is to verify (
1.7
). The argument is now standard. Assume that
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
1\leq m\leq d-1
. Let
\mu
be any doubling measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with full support. If
g:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bi-Lipschitz embedding, then
\Sigma=g(\mathbb{R}^{m})
is
m
-Ahlfors regular. In particular,
\dim_{A}\Sigma=m\leq d-1
. More generally, suppose that
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is any set with
\dim_{A}\Sigma<d
. By
[
Luu98
, Theorem 5.2]
,
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\dim_{A}\Sigma<d
imply that
\Sigma
is porous in the sense that there exists
0<\epsilon<1
such that

for all
x\in\Sigma
and
r>0
, there exists a ball
B(y,\epsilon r)\subset B(x,r)
such that
B(y,\epsilon r)\cap\Sigma=\emptyset
.

Let
x\in\Sigma
, let
r>0
, and let
B(y,\epsilon r)
be the ball given by the porosity condition. Since
\mu
is doubling and
y
is in the support of
\mu
(simply because
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{spt}\nolimits\mu=\mathbb{R}^{d}
), we have
\mu(B(x,r)\setminus\Sigma)\geq\mu(B(y,\epsilon r))\gtrsim\mu(B(y,2r))\geq\mu(B(x,r))
, where the implicit constant depends only on
\epsilon
and the doubling constant for
\mu
. Thus, on the one hand,

\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\setminus\Sigma)}{\mu(B(x,r))}=0

(6.15)

fails at every
x\in\Sigma
. On the other hand, by the Lebesgue-Besicovitch differentiation theorem for Radon measures (applied to the characteristic function
\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Sigma}
), we know that (
6.15
) holds at
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\Sigma
(see e.g.
[
Mat95
, Corollary 2.4]
). Therefore,
\mu(\Sigma)=0
.

asymptotically hyperbolic manifold with a horospherical boundary
Xiaoxiang Chai

Abstract
. We discuss asymptotically hyperbolic manifold with a noncompact boundary which is close to a horosphere in a certain sense. The model case is a horoball or the complement of a horoball in standard hyperbolic space. We show some geometric formulas.

2.
Background

The Christoffel symbols of the standard hyperbolic metric
b
is given by

(11)

\bar{\Gamma}_{in}^{j}=-(x^{n})^{-1}\delta_{i}^{j},\bar{\Gamma}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}=0,\bar{\Gamma}_{\alpha\beta}^{n}=(x^{n})^{-1}\delta_{\alpha\beta}

for
i
,
j
possibly being
n
ranging from 1 to
n
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
ranges from 2 to
n
. We use this convention later as well.

Consider again the half space model and the slice
\{x_{n}=1\}
, then (with
all quantities evaluated on the slice
\{x_{n}=1\}
)

(12)

\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{n}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\bar{\nabla}_{\alpha}e_{\beta n}+\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}e_{\alpha n}-\bar{\nabla}_{n}e_{\alpha\beta}).

The outward normal is
\eta^{i}=(g^{nn})^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}g^{ni}
and the
second fundamental form is

(13)

A_{\alpha\beta}=-(g^{nn})^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{n}.

Hence the mean curvature is

(14)

H=-h^{\alpha\beta}(g^{nn})^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{n}.

Note that
h^{\alpha\beta}=\delta^{\alpha\beta}-e_{\alpha\beta}+O(\mathrm{e}^{-2\tau r})
and
(g^{nn})^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}=1+\tfrac{1}{2}e_{nn}+O(\mathrm{e}^{-2\tau r})
, we have

(15)

\displaystyle 2(H+n-1)

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2h^{\alpha\beta}(g^{nn})^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{n}

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\alpha}(2\bar{\nabla}_{\alpha}e_{\alpha n}-\bar{\nabla}_{n}e_{\alpha\alpha})-(n-1)e_{nn}+2e_{\alpha\alpha}+O(\mathrm{e}^{-2\tau r}).

Write
\mathbb{U}
in coordinates, we have with
E=\operatorname{tr}_{b}e

\mathbb{U}^{i}=Vg^{ik}\bar{\nabla}^{j}e_{jk}-V\bar{\nabla}^{i}E+E\bar{\nabla}^{i}V-g^{ik}e_{jk}\bar{\nabla}^{k}V.

Along
\mathcal{H}
,

(18)

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}^{i}\bar{\eta}_{i}=

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V\bar{\eta}^{i}\bar{\nabla}^{j}e_{ji}-V\bar{\eta}^{i}\bar{\nabla}_{i}e_{jj}+E\bar{\eta}^{i}\bar{\nabla}_{i}V-\bar{\eta}^{i}e_{ji}\bar{\nabla}^{j}V

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V\bar{\nabla}_{\alpha}e_{\alpha n}-e_{\alpha n}\bar{\nabla}_{\alpha}V-V\bar{\nabla}_{n}e_{\alpha\alpha}+e_{\alpha\alpha}\bar{\nabla}_{n}V+O(\mathrm{e}^{-2\tau r+r}).

So we have that

(21)

\displaystyle 2V(H+n-1)+\mathbb{U}^{i}\bar{\eta}_{i}

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-V\bar{\nabla}_{\alpha}e_{\alpha n}-e_{\alpha n}\bar{\nabla}_{\alpha}V-(n-1)Ve_{nn}+(2V+\bar{\nabla}_{n}V)e_{\alpha\alpha}+O(\mathrm{e}^{-2\tau r+r})

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{\nabla}_{\alpha}(Ve_{\alpha n})-(n-1)Ve_{nn}+(2V+\bar{\nabla}_{n}V)e_{\alpha\alpha}+O(\mathrm{e}^{-2\tau r+r})

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{\alpha}(Ve_{\alpha n})+V\bar{\Gamma}_{\alpha n}^{i}e_{\alpha i}+V\bar{\Gamma}_{\alpha\alpha}^{i}e_{in}-V(n-1)e_{nn}

(25)

\displaystyle+(2V+\bar{\nabla}_{n}V)e_{\alpha\alpha}+O(\mathrm{e}^{-2\tau r+r})

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{\alpha}(Ve_{\alpha n})+O(\mathrm{e}^{-2\tau r+r}).

2.1.
Conformal Killing vectors

Since the metric of the hyperbolic space using the upper half space model is
conformal to standard metric of the Euclidean metric. We investigate

\bar{\nabla}_{i}X^{j}
for such
X
which is a conformal Killing vector with
respect to the Euclidean metric. We use repeatedly that the fact

\bar{\nabla}_{i}X^{j}=\bar{\nabla}^{i}X_{j}

which follows easily from conformality to
\delta
.

The
X=x^{i}\partial_{i}X=x^{i}\partial_{i}
, then

(27)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{i}X^{j}

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{i}x^{j}+x^{k}\Gamma_{ik}^{j}

(29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{i}^{j}+x^{k}\tfrac{1}{2}(x^{n})^{2}(g_{ij,k}+g_{kj,i}-g_{ik,j})

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{i}^{j}+\tfrac{1}{2}(x^{n})^{2}(x^{n}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x^{n}}((x^{n})^{-2})\delta_{ij}+x^{j}g_{jj,i}-x^{i}g_{ii,j})

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}(x^{n})^{2}(x^{j}g_{jj,i}-x^{i}g_{ii,j}).

So
\bar{\nabla}_{i}X^{j}+\bar{\nabla}^{j}X_{i}=0
. We see that vector
x^{i}\partial_{i}
is actually a Killing vector.

Now we consider the the translation vectors
\partial_{i}
. First, obviously

\partial_{i}
for
i\neq n
is obviously a Killing vector field. For
X=\partial_{n}
,

(32)

\bar{\nabla}_{i}X^{j}=X^{k}\Gamma_{ik}^{j}=\Gamma_{in}^{j}=-(x^{n})^{-1}\delta_{i}^{j}.

So

\bar{\nabla}_{i}X^{j}+\bar{\nabla}^{j}X_{i}=-2(x^{n})^{-1}\delta_{i}^{j}

and
\partial_{n}
is a conformal Killing vector. We remark here the recent
article of Jang and Miao
[
JM21
]
uses
\tfrac{1}{x^{n}}

to express the usual mass along horospheres converging to infinity by taking

\rho(\varepsilon)
to grow fast. It might be possible to obtain a formula
similar to Theorem
2
evaluating the their mass expression only
along horospheres by exploiting the special properties of the vector
-\partial_{n}
.

Now we consider the family
X=X^{j}\partial_{j}
with
X^{j}
being

\langle x,a\rangle_{\delta}x^{j}-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{\delta}a^{j}

where
a
is nonzero constant vector in Euclidean space. We see first that

(33)

\partial_{i}X^{j}=a_{i}x^{j}+\langle x,a\rangle_{\delta}\delta_{i}^{j}-x_{i}a^{j}.

We consider separately two cases of values of
i,j
. For
i\neq n
, we have
that

(34)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{i}X^{j}

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{i}X^{j}+X^{k}\Gamma_{ik}^{j}

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{i}X^{j}+X^{n}\Gamma_{in}^{j}

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{i}x^{j}+\langle x,a\rangle\delta_{i}^{j}-x_{i}a^{j}-[\langle x,a\rangle_{\delta}x^{n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{\delta}a^{n}](x^{n})^{-1}\delta_{i}^{j}

(38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{i}x^{j}-x_{i}a^{j}+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{\delta}a^{n}(x^{n})^{-1}\delta_{i}^{j}.

For
i=n
, we have that

(39)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{n}X^{j}

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{n}X^{j}+X^{k}\Gamma_{nk}^{j}

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{n}X^{j}-X^{k}(x^{n})^{-1}\delta_{k}^{j}

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{n}X^{j}-X^{j}(x^{n})^{-1}

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{n}x^{j}+\langle x,a\rangle\delta_{n}^{j}-x_{n}a^{j}-[\langle x,a\rangle_{\delta}x^{j}-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{\delta}a^{j}](x^{n})^{-1}

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{n}x^{j}-x_{n}a^{j}-\tfrac{1}{x^{n}}\langle x,a\rangle_{\delta}x^{j}+\tfrac{1}{2x^{n}}|x|_{\delta}^{2}a^{j}.

And for
j\neq n
,

(45)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}^{j}X_{n}=\bar{\nabla}_{j}X^{n}

(46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{j}X^{n}+X^{k}\Gamma_{jk}^{n}

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{j}X^{n}+X^{\alpha}(x^{n})^{-1}\delta_{\alpha}^{j}

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{j}x^{n}-x_{j}a^{n}+\langle x,a\rangle_{\delta}\tfrac{x^{j}}{x^{n}}-\tfrac{1}{2x^{n}}\langle x,x\rangle_{\delta}a^{j}.

For
j=n
, we have that

(49)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{n}X^{n}

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{n}X^{n}+X^{k}\Gamma_{nk}^{n}

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x,a\rangle_{\delta}-X^{n}(x^{n})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x,a\rangle_{\delta}-[x^{n}\langle x,a\rangle_{\delta}-\tfrac{1}{2}|x|^{2}_{\delta}a^{n}](x^{n})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}|x|^{2}_{\delta}(x^{n})^{-1}a^{n}.

To summarize, we have that

(54)

\bar{\nabla}_{i}X^{j}+\bar{\nabla}^{j}X_{i}=\tfrac{1}{2x^{n}}\langle x,x\rangle_{\delta}a^{n}\delta_{i}^{j}.

We have that
\langle x,\partial_{n}\rangle_{\delta}x-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{\delta}\partial_{n}

\langle x,\partial_{n}\rangle_{\delta}x-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{\delta}\partial_{n}-x

\mathcal{H}
.

(56)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{i}X^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{i}X^{j}+X^{k}\Gamma_{ik}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{i}^{n}\delta_{j}^{k}-\delta_{i}^{k}\delta_{n}^{j}+X^{n}\Gamma_{in}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{i}^{n}\delta_{j}^{k}-\delta_{i}^{k}\delta_{n}^{j}+x^{k}(x^{n})^{-1}\delta_{i}^{j}

\displaystyle x^{k}(x^{n})^{-1}\delta_{i}^{j}.

\bar{\nabla}_{i}X^{j}+\bar{\nabla}_{j}X^{i}=X^{k}\Gamma_{ik}^{j}+X^{k}\Gamma_{jk}^{i}=2\tfrac{x^{k}}{x^{n}}\delta_{i}^{j}.

We have for

,

(62)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{n}X^{j}

(63)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{n}X^{j}+X^{l}\Gamma_{nl}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{k}^{j}-\delta^{k}_{n}\delta_{n}^{j}+X^{\alpha}\Gamma_{n\alpha}^{j}

(65)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{k}^{j}-\delta_{n}^{k}\delta_{n}^{j}+x^{n}\Gamma^{j}_{nk}

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{k}^{j}-\delta_{n}^{k}\delta_{n}^{j}-\delta^{j}_{k}=0.

Similarly,
\bar{\nabla}_{j}X^{n}=0
. And

(67)

\bar{\nabla}_{n}X^{n}=X^{j}\Gamma^{n}_{jn}=X^{n}\Gamma_{nn}^{n}=\tfrac{x^{k}}{x^{n}}.

(68)

\bar{\nabla}_{i}X^{j}+\bar{\nabla}^{j}X_{i}=\tfrac{2x^{k}}{x^{n}}\delta_{i}^{j}

for all

and
j
.

We consider the vectors
Y=x-\partial_{n}
and

(69)

They are conformal Killing vectors along

\displaystyle|Y|_{b}=

\displaystyle\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{x^{n}}(|\hat{x}|+|x^{n}-1|)

(76)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{x^{n}}(|\hat{x}|^{2}+x^{n}+1)

(77)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(\cosh r),

according to (
3
). For
i\neq n
,
j\neq n
,

;

The length of

\displaystyle|Y|^{2}_{b}

(80)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x^{n})^{-2}[(x^{n})^{2}+(x^{k})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\langle x,x\rangle_{\delta}^{2}-\langle x,x\rangle_{\delta}x^{n}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{(x^{n})^{2}}[(x^{k})^{2}+(x^{n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{\delta})^{2}]

(82)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{(x^{n})^{2}}[|\hat{x}|^{2}+2(x^{n})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{\delta}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{(x^{n})^{2}}[|\hat{x}|^{2}+2(x^{n})^{2}+|\hat{x}|^{4}+(x^{n})^{4}].

We see then that
|Y|_{b}=O(\cosh r)
. We write

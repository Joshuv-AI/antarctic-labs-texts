Logarithmic Gysin sequences for regular immersions
Doosung Park

Abstract
. For a regular immersion of schemes Z → X Z\to X and a cohomology theory of fs log schemes, we formulate the logarithmic Gysin sequence using the “logarithmic compactification” ( Bl Z ​ X , E ) (\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E) instead of the open complement X − Z X-Z , where E E is the exceptional divisor. We show that all A 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant cohomology theories produced from motivic spectra and various non A 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant cohomology theories like Nygaard completed prismatic cohomology admit logarithmic Gysin sequences.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{E}
be an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant Nisnevich sheaf of spectra on the category of schemes.
A fundamental theorem of Morel-Voevodsky
[
13
, Theorem 2.23 in §3]
implies that if
Z\to X
is a closed immersion of smooth schemes over a scheme,
then there is a natural fiber sequence

\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Th}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X))\to\mathbb{E}(X)\to\mathbb{E}(X-Z),

which is called the
Gysin sequence
.
Here,
\mathrm{N}_{Z}X
denotes the normal bundle of
Z
in
X
,
and
\mathrm{Th}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X)
denotes its Thom space.
It is important to have all three terms in the Gysin sequence for computational aspects.

If
Z\to X
is a regular immersion of schemes,
then there is no such a Gysin sequence for general
\mathbb{E}
.
If we further assume that
X
and
Z
are regular,
then the existence of a Gysin sequence is a special property for
\mathbb{E}
characterized by Déglise
[
8
, Definition 1.3.2]
in the motivic setting, which is called the
absolute purity
.
The algebraic
K
-theory spectrum satisfies the absolute purity as a consequence of Quillen’s localization sequence for algebraic
K
-theory.
We refer to
[
9
, Example 4.3.13]
for more examples satisfying the absolute purity.

The situation is worse for non
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant cohomology theories,
e.g., Hodge cohomology, topological Hochschild homology, prismatic cohomology, syntomic cohomology, etc.
The above Gysin sequence does not exist even for the smooth case.
The non-properness of the open immersion
X-Z\to X
causes such a failure.

The logarithmic approach helps in this situation.
For effective Cartier divisors
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
on
X
given by invertible sheaves of ideals
\mathcal{I}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{I}_{n}
,
let
(X,D_{1}+\cdots+D_{n})
be the log scheme whose log structure is obtained by the Deligne-Faltings structure
[
17
, §III.1.7]
associated with the inclusions
\mathcal{I}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{I}_{n}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
We consider the
logarithmic compactification

(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E)
of
X-Z
in
X
,
where
E
is the exceptional divisor on the blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X
.
The logarithmic compactification of the interval
\mathbb{A}^{1}
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is
\square:=(\mathbb{P}^{1},\infty)
.
The
logarithmic deformation space
is

\mathrm{D}_{Z}X:=\mathrm{Bl}_{Z\times\{0\}}(X\times\square)-\mathrm{Bl}_{Z\times\{0\}}(X\times\{0\}).

If we use
\mathbb{A}^{1}
instead of
\square
here, then we recover the classical deformation space.
We have the induced diagram of fs log schemes with cartesian squares

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{D}_{Z}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{N}_{Z}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{1\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\square}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{0\},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{0}}

where
i_{0}
(resp.
i_{1}
) is the
0
-section (resp.
1
-section).
Observe also that
\mathrm{D}_{Z}X
contains
Z\times\square
as a strict closed subscheme.
Using these replacements,
we can formulate the logarithmic Gysin sequences as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{E}
be a presheaf on the category of fs log schemes with values in a stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{V}
.
We say that
\mathbb{E}
admits
logarithmic Gysin sequences
if the squares in the induced commutative diagram

(1.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X),E^{D})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X),E^{N})}

are cartesian for every regular immersion of schemes
Z\to X
,
where
E
,
E^{D}
, and
E^{N}
are the exceptional divisors.
In this case,
the induced fiber sequence

\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Th}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X))\to\mathbb{E}(X)\to\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X)

is called the
logarithmic Gysin sequence
,
where

\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Th}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X)):=\cofib(\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X)\to\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X),E^{N})).

By
[
5
, Proposition 7.4.5]
,
there is a natural isomorphism

\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Th}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X))\simeq\cofib(\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X\oplus\mathcal{O}))\to\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X))).

Hence the
Gysin morphism

\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Th}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X))\to\mathbb{E}(X)
can be written purely in terms of schemes.

The author’s joint work with Binda and Østvær
[

proved that

-invariant strict Nisnevich sheaves admit logarithmic Gysin sequences for the smooth case,

Z\to X
is a closed immersion of smooth schemes over a scheme

.
This is a generalization of

-invariant setting.

,
we have an isomorphism

(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E)\times_{\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X}U_{I}\simeq\Spec(B_{I},Q_{I})

that is natural in
I
.

Let
P_{I}
be the submonoid of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\mathbb{Z}e_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}e_{n}
generated by
e_{i}
and
e_{i}-e_{j}
for
i,j\in I
.
We have the natural map
P_{I}\to B_{I}
sending
e_{i}
to
a_{i}
and
e_{i}-e_{j}
to
a_{i}/a_{j}
.
Furthermore,

P_{I}
is a chart of
\Spec(B_{I},Q_{I})
.
Hence we have a natural isomorphism

(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E)\times_{\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X}U_{I}\simeq\Spec(B_{I},P_{I}).

In particular,
the log structure on
(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E)
is fine saturated.

Now, we set
C:=A/(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
and

C_{I}:=C[(t_{i})_{i\in I},(t_{j}/t_{i})_{i\in I,1\leq j\leq n}],

where
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
are indeterminates.
Observe that
C_{I}
is naturally isomorphic to
B_{I}/(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.
The composite map
P_{I}\to C_{I}
sends
e_{i}
to
0
and
e_{i}-e_{j}
to
t_{i}/t_{j}
.
Furthermore,
we have a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{Tub}_{Z}X\times_{\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X}U_{I}\simeq\Spec(C_{I},P_{I}).

This implies that the log structure on
\mathrm{Tub}_{Z}X
is uniquely determined by
Z
and
n
and independent of the choice of
X
.

Note that this claim is only for the affine case.
If
Z
is not affine,
then
\mathrm{Tub}_{Z}X
is not uniquely determined by
Z
and
n
.

Recall the following fundamental result on the theme of logarithmic Gysin sequences:

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{E}
be a presheaf on
\mathrm{lSch}
with values in
\mathcal{V}
.
Then
\mathbb{E}
is a
(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})
-invariant strict Nisnevich sheaf if and only if
\mathbb{E}
is a
\square
-invariant dividing Nisnevich sheaf.
In this case,
for every closed immersion of smooth schemes
Z\to X
over a scheme
S
,
the squares in (
1.1
) are cartesian.

Proof.

For the noetherian case,
this is a direct consequence of
[
5
, Proposition 7.3.1, Theorems 7.5.4, 7.7.4]
.
As in
[
6
, Theorems 3.2.21, 3.5.6, 3.5.7]
,
use the noetherian reduction argument in
[
6
, §3.1]
.
∎

With log cdh descent that is stronger than strict Nisnevich descent,
we have logarithmic Gysin sequences for regular immersions as follows:

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mathbb{E}
be a
(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})
-invariant log cdh sheaf on
\mathrm{lSch}
with values in
\mathcal{V}
.
Then
\mathbb{E}
admits logarithmic Gysin sequences.

Proof.

Let
Z\to X
be a regular immersion of schemes.
We will only focus on showing that the left square of (
1.1
) for
Z\to X
is cartesian since the proof for the right square is similar.
Since
\mathbb{E}
is a strict Nisnevich sheaf,
the question is Zariski local on
X
.
Hence we may assume that
X
is an affine regular scheme
\Spec(A)
and
Z
is given by a regular sequence
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
in
A
.
Consider

\mathrm{Tub}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X):=(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X),E^{D})\times_{\mathrm{Bl}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X)}E^{D},

where
E^{D}
is the exceptional divisor.
The squares

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Tub}_{Z}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X,}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Tub}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X),E^{D})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z\times\square}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{D}_{Z}X,}

are log cdh distinguished squares.
Since
\mathbb{E}
is a log cdh sheaf,
the squares

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Tub}_{Z}X),}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X),E^{D})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(Z\times\square)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Tub}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X))}

are cartesian.
Hence the claim that the left square of (
1.1
) is cartesian is equivalent to the claim that the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(Z\times\square)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Tub}_{Z}X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Tub}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X))}

is cartesian,
which is equivalent to the claim that
\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Tub}_{Z}X)\to\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Tub}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X))
is an isomorphism by
(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})
-invariance.

Example
2.5
shows that the log structure on
\mathrm{Tub}_{Z}X
is uniquely determined by
Z
and
n
.
Similarly,
the log structure on
\mathrm{Tub}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X)
is uniquely determined by
Z
and
n
.
Hence to show that
\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Tub}_{Z}X)\to\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Tub}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X))
is an isomorphism or equivalently the left square of (
1.1
) is cartesian,
we may assume that
Z\to X
is given by the
0
-section
Z\to Z\times\mathbb{A}^{n}
.
This smooth case is done by Theorem
2.6
.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

Let

be a scheme.
For every object
\mathbf{E}
of
\mathrm{SH}(B)

the presheaf
\mathbb{E}
of spectra on
\mathrm{lSch}/B

is a
(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})

Hence it admits logarithmic Gysin sequences.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.7
, it suffices to show the first claim.
Consider the full subcategory
\mathrm{SH}^{\mathrm{ex}}(X)

\mathrm{SH}(X)
in
[

, Definition 2.3.1]
.

\mathrm{SH}^{\mathrm{ex}}(B)=\mathrm{SH}(B)
and
\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+}\in\mathrm{SH}^{\mathrm{ex}}(X)
,
we have

\mathbb{E}(X)\simeq\hom_{\mathrm{SH}^{\mathrm{ex}}(X)}(\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+},p^{*}\mathbf{E}).

[
20
, Corollary 3.6.8]
,
we deduce that
\mathbb{E}
is a log cdh sheaf.

Since
\mathbb{E}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant,
(
ver
-inv) in
[

, Theorem 2.3.5]
implies that
\mathbb{E}
is
(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})
-invariant.
∎

The next result shows that there exists a non

is applicable.

.

For every integer

,

(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})

, it suffices to show the first claim.

,

[
15
, Proposition 5.4(2)]
and
\square
-invariant by Theorem
2.6
,
so it is
(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})
-invariant by
[
5
, Proposition 7.3.1]
.
Hence
R\Gamma_{\mathrm{k\acute{e}t}}(-,\mathbb{Z}/n)
is
(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})
-invariant too.

To show that
R\Gamma_{\mathrm{k\acute{e}t}}(-,\mathbb{Z}/n)
is a log cdh sheaf,
consider the presheaf of
\infty
-categories on
\mathrm{lSch}
assigning the small Kummer étale site
X_{\mathrm{k\acute{e}t}}
for
X\in\mathrm{lSch}
and the functor
f^{*}\colon X_{\mathrm{k\acute{e}t}}\to Y_{\mathrm{k\acute{e}t}}
for a morphism
f\colon Y\to X
in
\mathrm{lSch}
.
Consider a commutative diagram consisting of cartesian squares in
\mathrm{lSch}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

such that the left square is a log cdh distinguished square and

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}}

\mathrm{lSch}
extending
L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\mathrm{THH}
from schemes to fs log schemes.

3.
Logarithmic absolute purity

The purpose of this section is to recall the logarithmic absolute purity property and show that it is equivalent to the absolute purity property under certain conditions.

For an fs log scheme
X
,
let
\partial X
denote the set of points
x
of
X
such that the log structure on
X
is nontrivial at
x
.
We regard
\partial X
as a strict closed subscheme of
X
with the reduced scheme structure.

We say that an fs log scheme
X
is
regular log regular
if its underlying scheme
\underline{X}
is regular and
X
is log regular.

Recall that
B
is a base scheme.

Definition 3.1
.

A presheaf
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathrm{lSch}/B
with values in
\mathcal{V}
satisfies the
logarithmic absolute purity
if for every regular log regular
X\in\mathrm{lSch}/B
,
the induced morphism

\mathbb{E}(X)\to\mathbb{E}(X-\partial X)

is an isomorphism.

Remark 3.2
.

If a presheaf
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathrm{lSch}/B
satisfies
(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})
-invariance and the logarithmic absolute purity,
then
\mathbb{E}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant for regular log regular
X\in\mathrm{lSch}/B
:
The morphism

p^{*}\colon\mathbb{E}(X)\to\mathbb{E}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{1})

induced by the projection
p\colon X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\to X
is an isomorphism.

Proposition 3.3
.

A
(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})
-invariant log cdh sheaf
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathrm{lSch}/B
with values in
\mathcal{V}
satisfies the logarithmic absolute purity if and only if for every codimension
1
regular immersion of regular schemes
W\to Y
,
the induced morphism

\mathbb{E}(Y,W)\to\mathbb{E}(Y-W)

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The only if direction is trivial.
To show the if direction,
for a regular log regular
X\in\mathrm{lSch}/B
,
we need to show that the induced morphism
\mathbb{E}(X)\to\mathbb{E}(X-\partial X)
is an isomorphism.
Working Zariski locally on
X
,
we may assume that
X
has a chart
\mathbb{N}^{n}
for some integer
n\geq 0
by
[
17
, Theorem III.1.11.8(2)]
.
We proceed by induction on
n
.
The claim is trivial if
n=0,1
.
Hence assume
n\geq 2
.

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
be the standard coordinates in
\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
and let
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
be the corresponding effective Cartier divisors on
\underline{X}
.
Then we have
X\simeq(\underline{X},D_{1}+\cdots+D_{n})
.
We set

Z:=(D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{n})\times_{\underline{X}}X,\text{ }X^{\prime}:=(\mathrm{Bl}_{\underline{Z}}\underline{X},E+\widetilde{D}_{1}+\cdots+\widetilde{D}_{n}),

where
\widetilde{D}_{i}
is the strict transform of
D_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
,
and
E
is the exceptional divisor.
For every nonempty subset
I:=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
consider

X_{I}:=(\underline{X},D_{i_{1}}+\cdots+D_{i_{r}}),\text{ }X_{I}^{\prime}:=(\underline{Y},E+\widetilde{D}_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\widetilde{D}_{i_{r}}).

As in the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 3.2.5]
,
we have

\lim_{I}\mathbb{E}(X_{I}^{\prime})\simeq\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{\underline{Z}}\underline{X},E)

using the fact that
\mathbb{E}
is a Zariski sheaf,
where the limit runs over the category associated with the partially ordered set of nonempty subsets of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
We will show below that the induced morphism
\mathbb{E}(X_{I})\to\mathbb{E}(X_{I}^{\prime})
is an isomorphism for every
I
.
Assuming this,
we have

\lim_{I}\mathbb{E}(X_{I})\simeq\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{\underline{Z}}\underline{X},E).

By induction,
we have
\mathbb{E}(X_{I})\simeq\mathbb{E}(X_{I}-\partial X_{I})
if
I\neq\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{\underline{Z}}\underline{X},E)\simeq\mathbb{E}(\underline{X}-\underline{Z})
.
Since
\mathbb{E}
is a Zariski sheaf,
we have

\lim_{I}\mathbb{E}(X_{I}-\partial X_{I})\simeq\mathbb{E}(\underline{X}-\underline{Z}).

Combine the above equations to have
\mathbb{E}(X_{I})\simeq\mathbb{E}(X_{I}-\partial X_{I})
if
I=\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
i.e.,
\mathbb{E}(X)\simeq\mathbb{E}(X-\partial X)
.

It remains to show that
\mathbb{E}(X_{I})\to\mathbb{E}(X_{I}^{\prime})
is an isomorphism for every
I
.
Since
\mathbb{E}
is a log cdh sheaf,
the induced square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(X_{I})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(Z_{I})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(X_{I}^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(Z_{I}^{\prime})}

is cartesian,
where
Z_{I}:=X_{I}\times_{\underline{X}}\underline{Z}
and
Z_{I}^{\prime}:=Z_{I}\times_{X_{I}}X_{I}^{\prime}
.
Hence the claim that
\mathbb{E}(X)\to\mathbb{E}(X^{\prime})
is an isomorphism is equivalent to the claim that
\mathbb{E}(Z_{I})\to\mathbb{E}(Z_{I}^{\prime})
is an isomorphism.

Consider
X_{0}:=\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{N}^{n}}
,
and produce the fs log schemes
X_{0,I}
,
X_{0,I}^{\prime}
,
Z_{0,I}
, and
Z_{0,I}^{\prime}
as
X
has produced
X_{I}^{\prime}
,
Z_{I}
, and
Z_{I}^{\prime}
.
Then we have an isomorphism
Z_{I}^{\prime}\simeq\underline{Z_{I}^{\prime}}\times_{\underline{Z_{0,I}^{\prime}}}Z_{0,I}^{\prime}
.
Hence the log structure on
Z_{I}^{\prime}
is uniquely determined by
Z
,
I
, and
n
,
so we may assume that
X=X_{0}
.
This smooth case is done by
[
19
, Proposition 2.4.3]
since
\mathbb{E}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant.
∎

For a regular immersion
Z\to X
in
\mathrm{lSch}/B
,
consider
\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}X:=\mathrm{D}_{Z}X-\partial\mathrm{D}_{Z}X
,
which is the usual deformation space in
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathbb{E}
a
(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})
-invariant and
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant log cdh sheaf on

with values in

Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

satisfies the logarithmic absolute purity.

(2)

\mathbb{E}
satisfies the absolute purity in the sense of

, Definition 1.3.2]

(3.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(X-Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}X-Z\times\mathbb{A}^{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X-Z)}

in

.

(3)

The squares in (

) are cartesian for every codimension

.

Proof.

Let

be a regular immersion (resp. codimension

regular immersion) in
\mathrm{Sch}/B
.
We know that the squares in (

) are cartesian by Theorem

.

Conversely,
assume (2) (resp. (3)).
Then the squares in the induced commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z\times\square}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}X),E^{D})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X),E^{N})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(X-Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{D}_{Z}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}X-Z\times\mathbb{A}^{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X-Z)}

are cartesian.
Since the

is a closed immersion of smooth schemes over

the morphism

is an isomorphism by

and Theorem

.
It follows that the morphism

is an isomorphism too.
Together with Proposition

∎

Definition 3.5
.

An object
\mathbf{E}\in\mathrm{SH}(B)
satisfies the

if the presheaf on

given by

satisfies the absolute purity for every integer

,
where

is the structure morphism.

.

Fujiwara and Kato

.

[

]
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let

be a regular log regular log scheme over

4.
Hochschild homology

Let
R
be a commutative ring throughout this section.

A log ring (often called pre-log ring) is a map of commutative monoids
M\to A
,
where
A
is a commutative ring whose monoid structure is the multiplication.
A commutative ring
A
can be considered as the log ring
(A,\{1\})
.

For every log
R
-algebra
(A,M)
,
we have Hochschild homology
\mathrm{HH}((A,M)/R)
,
see
[
3
, Definition 5.3]
.
We can globalize this to obtain Hochschild homology
\mathrm{HH}(X/R)
for log scheme
X
over
R
,
see
[
3
, Definition 7.15]
for the noetherian case and
[
6
, Definition 8.3.7, Proposition 8.3.8]
to avoid the noetherian assumption.
We refer to
[
4
, §2.11]
for a natural
S^{1}
-action on
\mathrm{HH}((A,M)/R)
.
From this,
we also have a natural
S^{1}
-action on
\mathrm{HH}(X/R)
too.

For a log ring
(R,P)
,
consider the category
\mathrm{Poly}_{(R,P)}
consisting of the log rings
(R,P)\otimes(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}^{I}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{J}],\mathbb{N}^{J})
for finite sets
I
and
J
.
Let
\mathcal{D}
be an
\infty
-category admitting sifted colimits.
We have an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

(4.1)

\Fun_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{AniLog}_{(R,P)},\mathcal{D})\simeq\Fun(\mathrm{Poly}_{(R,P)},\mathcal{D})

arguing as in
[
1
, Construction 2.1]
,
where
\Fun_{\Sigma}
denotes the
\infty
-category of functors preserving sifted colimits,
and
\mathrm{AniLog}_{(R,P)}
denotes the
\infty
-category of animated log (=pre-log)
(R,P)
-algebras in the sense of
[
3
, §2.2]
.
If
F\colon\mathrm{AniLog}_{(R,P)}\to\mathcal{D}
corresponds to
f\colon\mathrm{Poly}_{(R,P)}\to\mathcal{D}
,
we say that
F
is a
left Kan extension of
f
.

We have the HKR filtration
[
3
, Theorem 5.15]
on
\mathrm{HH}(X/R)
,
which we review as follows.
For
(A,M)\in\mathrm{Poly}_{(R,\{1\})}
,
the HKR filtration on
\mathrm{HH}((A,M)/R)
is the Postnikov filtration.
For general log
R
-algebra
(A,M)
,
we get the HKR filtration on
\mathrm{HH}((A,M)/R)
by left Kan extension.
For general log scheme
X
,
we get the HKR filtration on
\mathrm{HH}(X/R)
by Zariski descent.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(A,M)\to(B,N),(C,L)
be maps of log
R
-algebras,
where
R
is a commutative ring.
Then the induced natural
S^{1}
-equivariant morphism of filtered
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings

\mathrm{HH}((B,N)/R)\otimes_{\mathrm{HH}((A,M)/R)}\mathrm{HH}((C,L)/R)\to\mathrm{HH}((B,N)\otimes_{(A,M)}{L}(C,L)/R)

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Note that the claim is obvious if we are not concerned about the filtrations.

We first treat the special case when
(A,M)=R
.
By left Kan extension,
we may assume
(B,N),(C,L)\in\mathrm{Poly}_{(R,\{1\})}
.
In this case,
the filtrations on
\mathrm{HH}((B,N)/R)
,
\mathrm{HH}((C,L)/R)
, and
\mathrm{HH}((B,N)\otimes_{R}{L}(C,L)/R)
are the Postnikov filtrations.
Hence the claim follows from the fact that the tensor product of two
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings with the Postnikov filtrations is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring with the Postnikov filtration.

For general
(A,M)
,
by left Kan extension,
we may assume
(C,L)\in\mathrm{Poly}_{(A,M)}
, i.e.,

(C,L)=(A,M)\otimes_{R}(R[\mathbb{N}^{I}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{J}],\mathbb{N}^{J})
for some finite sets
I
and
J
.
Due to the above special case,
we have the natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathrm{HH}((B,N)/R)\otimes_{\mathrm{HH}((A,M)/R)}\mathrm{HH}((C,L)/R)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{HH}((B,N)/R)\otimes_{\mathrm{HH}((A,M)/R)}\mathrm{HH}((A,M)/R)\otimes_{R}\mathrm{HH}((R[\mathbb{N}^{I}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{J}],\mathbb{N}^{J})/R)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{HH}((B,N)/R)\otimes_{R}\mathrm{HH}((R[\mathbb{N}^{I}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{J}],\mathbb{N}^{J})/R)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{HH}((B,N)\otimes_{R}(R[\mathbb{N}^{I}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{J}],\mathbb{N}^{J})/R)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{HH}((B,N)\otimes_{(A,M)}{L}(C,L)/R).

This shows the claim.
∎

5.
Prismatic cohomology

Let
\mathrm{lQSyn}
denote the category of log quasi-syntomic log rings in

,
and let
\mathrm{lQRSPerfd}
denote the category of log quasi-regular semiperfectoid log rings in

.
These are the log analogues of the corresponding categories due to Bhatt-Morrow-Scholze in
[

A

is a log formal scheme that is strict étale locally isomorphic to
\mathrm{Spf}(A,M)
for some

We have the BMS filtrations on
\mathrm{THH}((A,M);\mathbb{Z}_{p})

,
\mathrm{TP}((A,M);\mathbb{Z}_{p})

[

, §7]

, §5]
.
For a log quasi-syntomic formal log scheme

,

\mathrm{THH}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
,
\mathrm{TC}^{-}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
,
\mathrm{TP}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, and
\mathrm{TC}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
by strict étale descent.

For a commutative ring
A
(resp. log scheme

),
let

(resp.

) denote its

-completion.
Then

is a
p
-adic formal log scheme.
As noted in
[

,
we have a natural isomorphism

of commutative algebra objects of the

-category of cyclotomic spectra
\mathrm{CycSp}
.
Hence for every log scheme

such that

\mathrm{THH}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

The
double speed Postnikov filtration
is
\mathrm{Fil}_{\geq n}:=\tau_{\geq 2n-1}
for every integer

.

Lemma 5.1
.

For

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}]
is smooth and

there are isomorphisms of complexes

\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}]/\mathbb{Z}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}]\simeq\mathbb{L}_{(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}],\mathbb{N})/\mathbb{Z}}.

Using this,
the argument in
[

, Lemma 5.7]
can be translated into the setting of

.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For
(A,M)\in\mathrm{lQRSPerfd}
,
there are natural

-equivariant isomorphisms of filtered
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings

\displaystyle\mathrm{THH}((A,M)\otimes\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}];\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\mathrm{THH}((A,M);\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes\mathrm{HH}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}]),

\displaystyle\mathrm{THH}((A,M)\otimes(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}],\mathbb{N});\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\mathrm{THH}((A,M);\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes\mathrm{HH}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}],\mathbb{N}).

Proof.

The first isomorphism is in

, (5.3)]
.
We can similarly obtain the second isomorphism using Lemma

∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(R,P)\to(A,M),(B,N)
be maps of log quasi-syntomic log rings.
Then the induced morphism of filtered
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings

\mathrm{THH}((A,M)\otimes{L}_{(R,P)}(B,N);\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\mathrm{THH}((A,M);\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes_{\mathrm{THH}((R,P);\mathbb{Z}_{p})}\mathrm{THH}((B,N);\mathbb{Z}_{p})

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

By left Kan extension,
we reduce to the case when
(B,N)=(R,P)\otimes(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{N}^{i+j}],\mathbb{N}^{j})
for some integers
i,j\geq 0
.
We proceed by induction on
i
and
j
.
The claim is clear if
i=j=0
.

If
i=1
and
j=0
,
then by log quasi-syntomic descent and
[
4
, Theorem 4.22]
,
we reduce to the case when
(A,M),(R,P)\in\mathrm{lQRSPerfd}
.
Apply Lemma
5.2
to
(A,M)
and
(R,P)
to show the desired result.

Assume
i>1
and
j=0
.
Then we have the natural
S^{1}
-equivariant isomorphism of filtered
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings

\displaystyle\mathrm{THH}((A[x_{1},\ldots x_{i}],M);\mathbb{Z}_{p})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{THH}((A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}],M);\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes_{\mathrm{THH}(R;\mathbb{Z}_{p})}\mathrm{THH}(R[x_{i}];\mathbb{Z}_{p})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{THH}((A,M);\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes_{\mathrm{THH}(R;\mathbb{Z}_{p})}\mathrm{THH}(R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}];\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes_{\mathrm{THH}(R;\mathbb{Z}_{p})}\mathrm{THH}(R[x_{i}];\mathbb{Z}_{p})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\mathrm{THH}((A,M);\mathbb{Z}_{p})\otimes_{\mathrm{THH}(R;\mathbb{Z}_{p})}\mathrm{THH}(R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}];\mathbb{Z}_{p}),

where the first and third isomorphisms are due to the case of
i=1
and the second isomorphism is due to the induction hypothesis.

Argue as above for the induction on
j
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
Z\to X
be a regular immersion of noetherian schemes,
and let
E
be the exceptional divisor on
\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X
.
If
X_{p}^{\wedge}
is quasi-syntomic,
then
(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X)_{p}^{\wedge}
is quasi-syntomic, and
(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E)_{p}^{\wedge}
is log quasi-syntomic.

Proof.

Since the question is strict étale local on
X
,
we may assume that
X=\Spec(A)
for some commutative ring
A
and
Z=\Spec(A/(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))
for some regular sequence
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
in
A
such that
A_{p}^{\wedge}
is quasi-syntomic.
Consider the log ring
(B_{I},P_{I})
in Example
2.5
for every nonempty subset
I
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Every
p
-complete noetherian commutative ring has bounded
p^{\infty}
-torsion,
so it suffices to show that
\mathbb{L}_{(B_{I})_{p}^{\wedge}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
and
\mathbb{L}_{((B_{I})_{p}^{\wedge},P_{I})/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
have
p
-complete Tor amplitude in
[-1,0]
.
Since
A_{p}^{\wedge}
is quasi-syntomic,

\mathbb{L}_{A_{p}^{\wedge}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\otimes{L}_{A_{p}^{\wedge}}(B_{I})_{p}^{\wedge}
has
p
-complete Tor amplitude in
[-1,0]
by
[
1
, Lemma 4.5(1)]
.
Due to transitivity sequences for log cotangent complexes,
it suffices to show that
\mathbb{L}_{(B_{I})_{p}^{\wedge}/A_{p}^{\wedge}}
and
\mathbb{L}_{((B_{I})_{p}^{\wedge},P_{I})/A_{p}^{\wedge}}
have
p
-complete Tor amplitude in
[-1,0]
.
This question is closed under derived base change,
so we may assume that
Z\to X
is the
0
-section
\Spec(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{A}^{n}
by Construction
4.2
.
To finish the proof,
observe that
X
,
\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X
, and
(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E)
are log smooth over
\Spec(\mathbb{Z})
in this case.
∎

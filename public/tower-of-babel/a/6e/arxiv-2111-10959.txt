Maximality of moduli spaces of vector bundles on curves
Erwan Brugallé, Florent Schaffhauser

Abstract




Abstract.
We prove that moduli spaces of semistable vector bundles of coprime
rank and degree over a non-singular real projective curve are maximal
real algebraic varieties if and only if the base curve itself is
maximal. This provides a new family of maximal varieties, with members
of arbitrarily large dimension. We prove the result by comparing the
Betti numbers of the real locus to the Hodge numbers of the complex
locus and showing that moduli spaces of vector bundles over a maximal
curve actually satisfy a property which is stronger than maximality
and that we call Hodge-expressivity.
We also give a brief account on
other varieties for which this property was already known.

Keywords.
Maximal real algebraic varieties, Hodge numbers, moduli spaces of vector bundles

Notation

A real algebraic variety is a pair
(X,\tau)
where
X
is a complex
algebraic variety equipped with an antiholomorphic involution

\tau:X\to X
. We denote by

\mathbb{R}X
the set of its real points, that is to say the fixed point set
of
\tau
.
Throughout this note, complex algebraic varieties are endowed with
their Euclidean topology. Except otherwise stated, we consider cohomology with
coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
Given a smooth compact manifold
M
and a
non-singular
projective complex algebraic variety
X
, we denote by

P_{t}(M)
the mod 2 Poincaré polynomial of
M
and by

H_{(x,y)}(X)
the Hodge polynomial of
X
,
i.e.

P_{t}(M)\ =\ \sumop\displaylimits_{i\geqslant 0}b_{i}(M)t^{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad H_{(x,y)}(X)\ =\ \sumop\displaylimits_{i,j\geqslant 0}h^{i,j}(X)x^{i}y^{j}.

1. Introduction

1.1. Maximal and Hodge-expressive real algebraic varieties

Let
X
be a real algebraic variety (not necessarily projective nor smooth). It is a consequence of Smith theory (see for example
[
Man17
, Section
3.3]
) that the mod 2 Betti numbers of
\mathbb{R}X
and
X
satisfy
the following
Smith–Thom inequality:

\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{\dim X}b_{i}(\mathbb{R}X)\ \leqslant\ \sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{2\,\dim X}b_{i}(X).

When equality holds, the real algebraic variety
X
is said to be

maximal
.
Maximal varieties constitute extremal objects in real algebraic
geometry that enjoy special properties. We refer to the book

[
Man17
]
or to the survey

[
DK00
]
for an account on the subject.

To date, not many maximal real algebraic varieties are
known. It is standard that Grassmannians equipped with their standard real structure
(
e.g.
real projective spaces) are maximal, as well as
non-singular (or mildly singular) real toric varieties
[
BFMvH06
,
Fra22
]
.
One also easily constructs maximal real algebraic (hyperelliptic) curves of arbitrary
genus. Furthermore, the Jacobian of a real algebraic curve with
non-empty real part is
maximal if and only if the curve is maximal
[
GH81
]
.
Knowledge starts to be more fragmented in the case of surfaces, we refer the
interested reader to
[
Man17
, Chapter 4]
or

[
DK00
, Section 3]
, as well as to Section
3
. Things are of course
getting worse when increasing the dimension; the knowledge of the
authors essentially reduces to the classifications of maximal cubic
real hypersurfaces of dimension 3 and 4
[
Kra09
,
FK10
]
, and to the
unpublished construction by
Itenberg and Viro, dating back to the 1990’s, of maximal real projective hypersurfaces of arbitrary degree and
dimension.

The goal of this note is to provide a new family of maximal
real algebraic varieties with members of arbitrarily large
dimension: moduli spaces of vector bundles of coprime rank and degree over a maximal
real algebraic curve.

Poincaré polynomials of the complex and real part of these moduli spaces have been computed in

[
AB83
]
and
[
LS13
]
respectively (see also
[
Bai14
]
).
It turns out, however, that equating both sums of Betti numbers
directly is quite intricate (see

[
LS13
]
for the rank
2
case). So, in Theorem

1.2
, we prove a
stronger statement instead: the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
Betti number of
the real part of the moduli space is equal to the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
ascending diagonal sum of the Hodge
diamond of its complex part (see Figure
1
).
This implies maximality since the moduli spaces in question have torsion-free integral
cohomology
[
AB83
]
.

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}&&&&h^{n,n}&&&&\\
&&&\iddots&&\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}&&&\\
&&h^{n,i}&&&&h^{i,n}&&\\
&\iddots&&&&\iddots&&\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}&\\
h^{n,0}&&&&h^{i,n-i}&&&&h^{0,n}\\
&\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}&&\iddots&&&&\iddots&\\
&&h^{i,0}&&&&h^{0,i}&&\\
&&&\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}&&\iddots&&&\\
&&&&h^{0,0}&&&&\end{array}

Figure 1.
Ascending diagonals of the Hodge diamond.

The idea of deducing maximality from this stronger statement
comes from the old empirical observation that, although few maximal
real algebraic varieties are currently known, a seemingly large proportion of
them, that we suggest to call

Hodge-expressive
, in fact satisfy this stronger property.

Definition 1.1
.

A non-singular projective real algebraic variety
X
is called Hodge-expressive
if
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is torsion free and

P_{t}(\mathbb{R}X)\ =\ H_{(t,1)}(X).

Clearly, a Hodge-expressive variety is maximal. As mentioned above, such
varieties already appear in the literature and, in an informal way,
one may say that Hodge-expressive real algebraic varieties constitute
the basic maximal real algebraic varieties. In Section

3
, we provide a brief panorama of known
Hodge-expressive varieties. Usually, Hodge-expressivity of a real
algebraic variety is a consequence of a prior proof of its
maximality. Here, we propose to go the other way round: maximality is
established as a consequence of Hodge-expressivity. From what we know,
this is also the strategy used in the aforementioned unpublished construction by
Itenberg and Viro.

Note that Hodge-expressive varieties satisfy
\chi=\sigma
(in the terminology of
[
Bru22
]
), since

\chi(\mathbb{R}X)\ =\ P_{-1}(\mathbb{R}X)\ =\ H_{(-1,1)}(X)\ =\ \sigma(X),

where the last equality is the Hodge index Theorem.

1.2. Moduli spaces of vector bundles over real algebraic curves

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a non-singular connected projective complex algebraic
curve
of genus
g\geqslant 1
.
Given two integer numbers
r\geqslant 1
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by

\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
the moduli space of semistable holomorphic vector bundles of
rank
r
and degree
d
over
\mathscr{C}
.
When
r
and
d
are coprime, the space

\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
is a non-singular complex projective variety of
dimension
r^{2}(g-1)+1
. For the rest of this discussion, we assume
that
g\geqslant 2
. Using the number-theoretic approach to the cohomology
of moduli spaces of vector bundles developed by Harder and Narasimhan
in
[
HN75
]
, Desale and Ramanan obtained in
[
DR75
]
a
recursive formula for the rational Poincaré polynomial of

\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
. Then, using gauge theory, Atiyah and Bott proved in

[
AB83
]
that the integral cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
is
torsion-free and gave an alternative proof of the recursive formula
by Desale and Ramanan. Later on, Zagier obtained a closed
formula for
P_{t}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d))
in
[
Zag96
]
, and Earl and
Kirwan used a finite-dimensional analogue of the Atiyah–Bott approach to prove a recursive formula
for the Hodge polynomial
H_{(x,y)}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d))
in
[
EK00
]
. A
consequence of these formulas (either recursive or closed) is that
Betti and Hodge numbers of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
depend only on the topological data
g
and
r
(and not
d
).

When
\mathscr{C}
is a non-singular real projective curve, the
anti-holomorphic involution
\tau:\mathscr{C}\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}
induces a
real structure
\mathcal{E}\longmapsto\overline{\tau^{*}\mathcal{E}}
on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
,
turning the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
into a real algebraic variety
as well. In fact, if
r
and
d
are coprime and
\mathscr{C}
has real
points, the real locus of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
consists exactly of
isomorphism classes of geometrically stable real vector bundles of
rank
r
and degree
d
(
cf.

[
Sch12
]
), where geometric stability of a real bundle
(\mathscr{E},\tau)
means that the underlying complex bundle
\mathscr{E}
is stable.
In
[
LS13
]
, Liu and Schaffhauser developed a real analogue of
the Atiyah–Bott approach to obtain a recursive
formula, as well as a closed formula, computing

P_{t}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d))
. As in the complex
situation, the Betti numbers of
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)

are seen
a
posteriori
not to depend on
d

but only on
g,r
and

b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})
. Note that

b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d))=2^{b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})-1}
by
[
Sch12
]
.

Given a line bundle
\Lambda
of degree
d
on
\mathscr{C}
, one may also
consider the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)
of semistable
holomorphic vector bundles of
rank
r
with fixed determinant
\Lambda
. By definition, this space is a fiber of the
determinant map
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})
.
When
r
and
d
are coprime, the space

\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)
is a non-singular complex projective variety of
dimension
(r^{2}-1)(g-1)
, which is real if both
\mathscr{C}
and
\Lambda

are
real. Both polynomials
H_{(x,y)}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda))
and

P_{t}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda))
can be deduced
from those of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
, see Section
2.3
.

The next statement is the main result of this note.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a maximal non-singular real projective curve of genus

g\geqslant 1
. Let
r\geqslant 1
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}
be coprime integers, and let

\Lambda
be a real line bundle of degree
d
on
\mathscr{C}
. Then both
moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)
are
Hodge-expressive.

In particular, these moduli spaces are maximal when
\mathscr{C}
is maximal.
The next proposition shows that the converse is true for the moduli spaces

\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a non-singular real projective curve of genus

g\geqslant 1
and such that
\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C}\neq\emptyset
. Let
r\geqslant 1
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}
be coprime integers. If
\mathscr{C}
is not maximal, then neither is
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
.

Note that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)
is maximal when either
g=1
or
r=1
(provided

\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C}\neq\emptyset
), since in this case it is
reduced to a point.
Whether
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)
is non-maximal when
\mathscr{C}

is non-maximal and
g,r\geqslant 2
remains an
open question.
As noted in
[
LS13
]
, the explicit formulas for the mod
2
Betti
numbers of
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)
quickly become too complex to be
evaluated at
t=1
(and similar remarks apply for the Hodge numbers of

\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)
). It was however checked in
[
LS13
]
, using a
computer, that this is indeed the case up until
r=6
.
As is visible from Section
2.3
, our proof of Proposition

1.3
does not allow us to conclude that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)

is not maximal when
\mathscr{C}
is not maximal and
g,r\geqslant 2
.

1.3. Outline of the paper

Section
2
is devoted to the proof of Theorem

1.2
and Proposition
1.3
.
In Section
3
, we give a panorama of known
maximal and Hodge-expressive real algebraic varieties.

2. Hodge-expressivity for moduli spaces of vector bundles

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
1.3
.
We first treat the particular cases
g=1
and
r=1
in Sections
2.1

and
2.2
respectively.
As a consequence, we prove Proposition
1.3

in Section
2.3
and show that it suffices to prove Theorem

1.2
for
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
. This is achieved in Sections

2.4
and
2.5
.

2.1. The case
g=1

If
\mathscr{C}
is of genus
1
and
\mathrm{gcd}(r,d)=1
, the determinant map

\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})
is an isomorphism of
algebraic varieties
[
Tu93
]
. In particular, one gets an isomorphism

\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\simeq\mathscr{C}

after a point
x_{0}\in\mathscr{C}
has been chosen.
In the case when
\mathscr{C}
is real and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C}
, this isomorphism is
real by
[
BS16
]
, proving Theorem
1.2

and Proposition
1.3

in the case

g=1
.

From now on, we suppose that
g\geqslant 2
.

2.2. The case
r=1

The case of moduli spaces of line bundles over
\mathscr{C}

is well known, since these moduli spaces are the Picard varieties

\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})
. In particular one has

H_{(x,y)}\big(\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\big)\ =\ (1+x)^{g}\,(1+y)^{g}.

On the other hand by
[
GH81
]
, one has

P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\big)\ =\ 2^{b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})-1}P_{t}((\mathbb{S}^{1})^{g})\ =\ 2^{b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})-1}(1+t)^{g}

when
\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C}\neq\emptyset
.
Since
\mathscr{C}
is maximal if and only if
b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})=g+1
,
this proves Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
1.3
in
the case
r=1
.

2.3. Preliminaries

Recall that the groups
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d);\mathbb{Z})
and

H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda);\mathbb{Z})
are torsion-free by
[
AB83
]
. Hence
it is enough to prove the two identities

P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)=H_{(t,1)}\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)\big)=H_{(t,1)}\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)\big).

Moreover when
\mathrm{gcd}(r,d)=1
, it is proved in
[
EK00
]

and in
[
Bai20
]
, respectively,
that

H_{(x,y)}\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\ =\ H_{(x,y)}\big(\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\big)H_{(x,y)}\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)\big)

and

P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\ =\ P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\big)P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)\big).

In view of Section
2.2
, this gives

(2.1)

H_{(t,1)}\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\ =\ 2^{g}\,(1+t)^{g}H_{(t,1)}\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)\big),

and

P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\ =\ 2^{b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})-1}(1+t)^{g}P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)\big).

In particular, applying the Smith–Thom inequality to

\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)
, we get:

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i\geqslant 0}b_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)

\displaystyle\ =\ P_{1}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)

\displaystyle\ =\ 2^{b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})-1+g}P_{1}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)\big)

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ 2^{2g}P_{1}\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)\big)\ =\ \sumop\displaylimits_{i\geqslant 0}b_{i}\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)

and equality can only hold here if
b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})=g+1
, that is to say if

\mathscr{C}
is maximal. This proves Proposition
1.3
. ∎

Next, when
\mathscr{C}
is maximal, we see from (
2.1
) that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
is
Hodge-expressive if and only if
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,\Lambda)
is
Hodge-expressive. To prove Theorem

1.2
, it is therefore sufficient to prove

(2.2)

P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)=H_{(t,1)}\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big).

In order to do so, we will show in the next two sections,
using results from
[
EK00
,
LS13
]
, that the polynomials

H_{(t,1)}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d))
and
P_{t}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d))

satisfy the same recursion relation.

2.4. Hodge numbers of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)

Here we recast the computation from
[
AB83
,
EK00
]
of Poincaré and Hodge
polynomials of the
moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
.
For all
(r,d)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by
Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
the
moduli stack of all vector bundles of rank
r
and degree
d
on

\mathscr{C}
. It contains, as an open substack, the moduli stack of
semistable vector bundles of rank
r
and degree
d
on
\mathscr{C}
, which
we denote by
Bun^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
. If we fix a
C^{\infty}
complex vector bundle
E
of rank
r
and degree
d
over
\mathscr{C}
, there is an isomorphism of stacks

Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\ \simeq\ [\mathcal{A}_{E}/\mathcal{G}_{E}]\,,

where
\mathcal{A}_{E}
is the set of Dolbeault operators (
i.e.
holomorphic structures) on
E
and
\mathcal{G}_{E}
is the automorphism group of
E
(
i.e.
the gauge group). In particular, we can think of the cohomology of the stack
Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
in the sense of
[
Beh05
]
simply as the
\mathcal{G}_{E}
-equivariant cohomology of the affine space
\mathcal{A}_{E}
. By
[
AB83
]
, the integral
cohomology of
Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
is torsion-free. Moreover, its Poincaré
series does not depend on
d
and is given by

P_{t}\big(Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\ =\ \frac{(1+t)^{2g}}{1-t^{2}}\,\prodop\displaylimits_{i=2}^{r}\frac{(1+t^{2i-1})^{2g}}{(1-t^{2i-2})(1-t^{2i})}\,.

By
[
HN75
]
, an algebraic vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
over
\mathscr{C}

admits a unique filtration, called the
Harder–Narasimhan filtration
,

0=\mathscr{E}_{0}\subset\mathscr{E}_{1}\subset\,\cdots\,\subset\mathscr{E}_{\ell}=\mathscr{E}

such that:

•

for all
i\in\{1,\,\ldots\,,\ell\}
, the vector bundle
\mathscr{E}_{i}/\mathscr{E}_{i-1}
is semistable;

•

setting
r_{i}:=\mathrm{rk}(\mathscr{E}_{i}/\mathscr{E}_{i-1})
and
d_{i}:=\mathrm{deg}(\mathscr{E}_{i}/\mathscr{E}_{i-1})
, we have

\frac{d_{1}}{r_{1}}>\,\cdots\,>\frac{d_{l}}{r_{l}}\,.

The topological invariants
(r_{i},d_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant\ell}
of the successive quotients
\mathscr{E}_{i}/\mathscr{E}_{i-1}
constitute the

Harder–Narasimhan type
(or HN type) of the vector bundle

\mathscr{E}
. If
\mathscr{E}
is of rank
r
and degree
d
, then its HN type is subject to the following constraints:

(1)

d_{1}+\,\cdots\,+d_{l}=d
,

(2)

r_{1}+\,\cdots\,+r_{l}=r
,

(3)

\frac{d_{1}}{r_{1}}>\,\cdots\,>\frac{d_{l}}{r_{l}}\,.

By definition, the vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
is semistable if and only if
it is of HN type
(r,d)
, which we denote by
\mu_{\mathrm{ss}}
.
We denote by

I_{r,d}
the set of all possible tuples of integers

\mu=(r_{i},d_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant\ell_{\mu}}
satisfying properties (1), (2), (3) above.
Note that this set is infinite as soon as
r\geqslant 2
. For instance:

I_{2,d}=\left\{(2,d),(1,k,1,d-k)\ |\ k>\frac{d}{2}\right\}.

For all
\mu\in I_{r,d}
, there exists an algebraic substack

Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu)
of
Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
, parameterizing vector bundles of
type
\mu
, with

Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu_{\mathrm{ss}})=Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r,d)
.
The codimension of
Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu)
is finite and, for all

\mu\neq\mu_{\mathrm{ss}}
, it is equal to

d_{\mu}\ :=\ \sumop\displaylimits_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant l(\mu)}\big(r_{j}d_{i}-r_{i}d_{j}+r_{i}r_{j}(g-1)\big).

Moreover,
there is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces

H^{*}\big(Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu);\mathbb{Q}\big)\ \simeq\ \bigotimesop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\ell_{\mu}}H^{*}\big(Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r_{i},d_{i});\mathbb{Q}\big).

In particular,
we have, for all
\mu\in I_{r,d}
,

P_{t}\big(Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu)\big)\ =\ \prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\ell_{\mu}}P_{t}\big(Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r_{i},d_{i})\big)\,.

Finally, the stratification of the moduli stack
Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
by the
substacks
Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu)

is
perfect
in the sense that the associated Gysin long exact
sequence breaks up into short exact sequences
[
AB83
]
. This implies that

P_{t}\big(Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\ =\ \sumop\displaylimits_{\mu\in I_{r,d}}t^{2d_{\mu}}\,P_{t}\big(Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu)\big),

yielding the recursive formula

P_{t}\big(Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r,d)\big)\ =\ P_{t}\big(Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\ -\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu\neq\mu_{\mathrm{ss}}}t^{2d_{\mu}}\,\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\ell_{\mu}}P_{t}\big(Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r_{i},d_{i})\big)\,.

When
r
and
d
are coprime, all semistable vector bundles of rank

r
and degree
d
are stable. Atiyah and Bott have shown that, in
this case, the Poincaré polynomial of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
is
related to the Poincaré series of
Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r,d)

via the identity

P_{t}\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\ =\ (1-t^{2})\,P_{t}\big(Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r,d)\big)\,.

Altogether, setting

Q^{\mathbb{C}}_{t}(r,d)=(1-t^{2})P_{t}\big(Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r,d)\big)
, one obtains the recursive formula

(2.3)

Q^{\mathbb{C}}_{t}(r,d)\ =\ (1+t)^{2g}\prodop\displaylimits_{i=2}^{r}\frac{(1+t^{2i-1})^{2g}}{(1-t^{2i-2})(1-t^{2i})}\ -\negmedspace\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu\in I_{r,d}\setminus\{\mu_{\mathrm{ss}}\}}\frac{t^{2d_{\mu}}}{(1-t^{2})^{\ell_{\mu}-1}}\,\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\ell_{\mu}}Q^{\mathbb{C}}_{t}(r_{i},d_{i})\,,

expressing all polynomials
Q^{\mathbb{C}}_{t}(r,d)

in terms of the initial term

Q^{\mathbb{C}}_{t}(1,d)\ =\ P_{t}\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(1,d)\big)\ =\ P_{t}\big(\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\big)\ =\ (1+t)^{2g}\,.

In
[
EK00
]
, Earl and Kirwan obtained a similar
recursive formula for the Hodge polynomial of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
when
r

and
d
are coprime. Namely, setting
Q_{(x,y)}(r,d)=H_{(x,y)}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d))

and using the construction of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
as a GIT quotient, they proved that

(2.4)

\displaystyle Q_{(x,y)}(r,d)\ =

\displaystyle(1-xy)\,G_{(x,y)}(r,d)\ -\negmedspace\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu\in I_{r,d}\setminus\{\mu_{\mathrm{ss}}\}}\frac{(xy)^{d_{\mu}}}{(1-xy)^{\ell_{\mu}-1}}\,\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\ell_{\mu}}Q_{(x,y)}(r_{i},d_{i})\,,

expressing recursively all polynomials
Q_{(x,y)}(r,d)

in terms of the power series

G_{(x,y)}(r,d):=\frac{(1+x)^{g}\,(1+y)^{g}}{1-xy}\,\prodop\displaylimits_{i=2}^{r}\frac{(1+x^{i-1}y^{i})^{g}\,(1+x^{i}y^{i-1})^{g}}{(1-x^{i-1}y^{i-1})(1-x^{i}y^{i})}

and the initial term

Q_{(x,y)}(1,d)\ =\ (1-xy)\,G_{(x,y)}(1,d)\ =\ (1+x)^{g}(1+y)^{g}\ =\ H_{(x,y)}\big(\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\big)\,.

Plugging
x=y=t
in equation (
2.4
), one finds again the
recursion (
2.3
). And plugging
x=t
and
y=1
in equation (
2.4
), one obtains the following recursion:

(2.5)

\displaystyle Q_{(t,1)}(r,d)\ =

\displaystyle 2^{g}(1+y)^{g}\,\prodop\displaylimits_{i=2}^{r}\frac{(1+t^{i-1})^{g}\,(1+t^{i})^{g}}{(1-t^{i-1})(1-t^{i})}\ -\negmedspace\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu\in I_{r,d}\setminus\{\mu_{\mathrm{ss}}\}}\frac{t^{d_{\mu}}}{(1-t)^{\ell_{\mu}-1}}\,\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\ell_{\mu}}Q_{(t,1)}(r_{i},d_{i})\,,

which will be useful to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Remark 2.1
.

Although this is not how Earl and Kirwan prove their result, the power
series
G_{(x,y)}(r,d)

can be interpreted as the Hodge series of the moduli stack
Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)

[
Tel98
,
BD07
]
.

2.5. Betti numbers of
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)

When the curve
\mathscr{C}
is defined over the reals, so is the moduli stack

Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
.
Here, we recast the computation of the mod
2
Poincaré
polynomial of
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
from
[
LS13
]
.
We denote by
n:=b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})
the number of connected components of

\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C}
,
and we assume that
n>0
. The stack
\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
is a disjoint
union

\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\ =\ \bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{s}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}{R}(r,d,s)

of

2^{n-1}
connected components, each of which is indexed by the
possible real invariants of a real vector bundle
(\mathscr{E},\tau)
of rank

r
and degree
d
, namely the first Stiefel–Whitney
class

s=(s_{1},\,\ldots\,,s_{n})\in(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{n}

of the vector bundle

\mathbb{R}\mathscr{E}
over
\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C}=\sqcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{S}^{1}
, subject to the condition

s_{1}+\,\cdots\,+s_{n}=d\mod{2}
. The substack
Bun_{\mathscr{C}}{R}(r,d,s)
is the
stack of all real vector bundles of rank
r
, degree
d
and real type

s
. Fixing a
C^{\infty}
real vector bundle
(E,\tau)
with these
invariants
(r,d,s)
, we get an isomorphism of
stacks

Bun_{\mathscr{C}}{R}(r,d,s)\ \simeq\ [\mathcal{A}_{E}^{\tau}/\mathcal{G}_{E}^{\tau}]\,,

where
\mathcal{A}^{\tau}\subset\mathcal{A}
is the set of all
\tau
-fixed Dolbeault
operators (for the real structure on
\mathcal{A}_{E}
induced by the real
structure of
E
), and
\mathcal{G}_{E}^{\tau}\subset\mathcal{G}_{E}
is the group of gauge
transformations of
E
that commute with
\tau
. It turns out that the
mod
2
Poincaré series of
Bun_{\mathscr{C}}{R}(r,d,s)
is independent of
d
and

s

[
LS13
]
. As a consequence, the space

\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
has mod 2 Poincaré series

\ P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\ =\ 2^{n-1}\,\frac{(1+t)^{g}}{1-t}\,\prodop\displaylimits_{i=2}^{r}\frac{(1+t^{2i-1})^{g+1-n}\,(1+t^{i-1})^{n-1}\,(1+t^{i})^{n-1}}{(1-t^{i-1})(1-t^{i})}\,.

Because of the uniqueness of the destabilizing sub-bundle, semistability
over
\mathbb{R}
is equivalent to semistability over
\mathbb{C}
and all sub-bundles

(\mathscr{E}_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant\ell}
in the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of a
real algebraic vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
over
\mathscr{C}
are real. So the
substack
Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu)
is defined over
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu)
is the stack of all real vector bundles of HN type
\mu
. Moreover, for all
\mu\in I_{r,d}
,
there is
an isomorphism of
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
-vector spaces

H^{*}\big(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu);\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\big)\ \simeq\ \bigotimesop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\ell_{\mu}}H^{*}\big(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r_{i},d_{i});\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\big).

In particular, we have, for all
\mu\in I_{r,d}
,

P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu)\big)\ =\ \prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\ell_{\mu}}P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r_{i},d_{i})\big)\,.

Finally, the substacks
(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu))_{\mu\in I_{r,d}}
form a
stratification of
\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)
which is perfect over
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
,
so

P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\ =\ \sumop\displaylimits_{\mu\in I_{r,d}}t^{d_{\mu}}\,P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(\mu)\big)

yielding the recursive formula

P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r,d)\big)\ =\ P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\ -\negmedspace\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu\in I_{r,d}\setminus\{\mu_{\mathrm{ss}}\}}t^{d_{\mu}}\,\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\ell_{\mu}}P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r_{i},d_{i})\big).

When
r
and
d
are coprime, one has the identity

P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\ =\ (1-t)\,P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r,d)\big)\,.

Altogether, setting

Q{R}_{t}(n,r,d)=(1-t)P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}Bun_{\mathscr{C}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(r,d)\big)
, one obtains the recursive formula

(2.6)

\displaystyle Q{R}_{t}(n,r,d)\ =

\displaystyle 2^{n-1}\,(1+t)^{g}\,\prodop\displaylimits_{i=2}^{r}\frac{(1+t^{2i-1})^{g+1-n}\,(1+t^{i-1})^{n-1}\,(1+t^{i})^{n-1}}{(1-t^{i-1})(1-t^{i})}

\displaystyle-\negmedspace\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu\neq\mu_{\mathrm{ss}}}\frac{t^{d_{\mu}}}{(1-t)^{\ell_{\mu}-1}}\,\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\ell_{\mu}}Q{R}_{t}(n,r_{i},d_{i}),

expressing all polynomials
Q{R}_{t}(n,r,d)

in terms of the initial term

Q{R}_{t}(n,1,d)\ =\ P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\big)\ =\ 2^{n-1}(1+t)^{g}\,.

Thus it follows
from relations (
2.5
)
and (
2.6
)
that, for all
r
and
d
, one has

Q{R}_{t}(g+1,r,d)\ =\ Q_{t}(r,d).

Since, when
d
and
r
are coprime,

Q{R}_{t}(g+1,r,d)\ =\ P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad Q_{t}(r,d)\ =\ H_{(t,1)}\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}(r,d)\big),

by results from
[
EK00
,
LS13
]
, we see that relation (
2.2
), hence also Theorem
1.2
, is proved. ∎

3. A brief panorama of Hodge-expressive varieties

We list below a few basic examples of Hodge-expressive varieties and
discuss their relevance among maximal real algebraic varieties.

3.1. Handy real algebraic varieties

Real projective spaces,
i.e.

\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{n}
equipped with the
standard complex conjugation, constitute the simplest
projective real algebraic varieties. One checks easily that they are
all Hodge-expressive.

More generally, the standard complex conjugation in
\mathbb{C}
induces a
real structure on the Grassmannian variety
\mathbf{Gr}(d,n)

of
d
-planes in

\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Schubert cells provide a stratification
of
\mathbf{Gr}(d,n)
by real affine spaces
[
MS74
]
, implying
that
for all
i\geqslant 0

b_{2i+1}\big(\mathbf{Gr}(d,n)\big)=0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad b_{i}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathbf{Gr}(d,n)\big)=b_{2i}\big(\mathbf{Gr}(d,n)\big)=h^{i,i}\big(\mathbf{Gr}(d,n)\big).

Hence all Grassmannian varieties are Hodge-expressive.

Toric varieties constitute another generalization of projective
spaces. A toric variety carries a standard real structure inherited
once again from the standard complex conjugation in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, and
all these non-singular real projective toric varieties are Hodge-expressive
by
[
BFMvH06
,
Fra22
]
.

Grassmannian and toric varieties are particular cases of

balanced varieties
,
i.e.
non-singular projective complex
manifold
X
such that
h^{i,j}(X)=0
whenever
i\neq j
.
By definition, such a variety satisfies

P_{t}(\mathbb{C}X)=H_{(t,t)}(X)=H_{(t^{2},1)}(X)
. In particular if
X
is
both balanced and Hodge-expressive, then

P_{t}(\mathbb{C}X)\ =\ P_{t^{2}}(\mathbb{R}X)
.

Hodge-expressivity is preserved by certain elementary operations on real algebraic varieties, for instance taking products or projectivizing a vector bundle over the variety. This follows from the fact that both Hodge and
Betti numbers satisfy the same relations under these elementary operations:
on the one hand, the Hodge
polynomial extends to a motivic invariant from the Grothendieck ring

K_{0}(Var{C})

of complex algebraic varieties to
\mathbb{Z}[x,y]

[
PS08
, Remark 5.56]
, and on the
other hand, the
Künneth formula (or more generally the Leray–Hirsch Theorem,

[
Hat02
, Theorem 4D.1]
) implies that the Poincaré polynomial behaves analogously
in basic situations. More precisely, we observe the following:

•

The product of Hodge-expressive real algebraic varieties
X_{1},X_{2},\,\ldots\,,X_{k}
,
equipped with the product real structure, is Hodge-expressive. Indeed,
one deduces from the multiplicativity of the Hodge polynomial and from
the Künneth formula that

H_{(x,y)}\left(\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}X_{i}\right)=\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}H_{(x,y)}(X_{i}),\quad\mbox{and}\quad P_{t}\left(\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{R}X_{i}\right)=\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}P_{t}(\mathbb{R}X_{i}).

•

Similarly, if
E
is a real algebraic vector bundle of rank
r
over a Hodge-expressive real
algebraic variety
X
, the real projective bundle
\mathbb P(E)

is also Hodge-expressive. By the scissor relations on
K_{0}(Var{C})
, we get that

H_{(x,y)}(\mathbb P(E))=H_{(x,y)}(X)H_{(x,y)}(\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{r-1}).

And since the first Chern class (resp. the first
Stiefel–Whitney class) of the tautological line
bundle on
\mathbb P(E)
(resp.
\mathbb{R}\mathbb P(E)
) restricts to the
generator of
H^{*}(\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{r-1})
(resp.
H^{*}(\mathbb{R}\mathbf{P}^{r-1})
), we deduce from the Leray–Hirsch theorem that

H^{*}(\mathbb P(E);\mathbb{Z})
is torsion-free if
X
is, and that

P_{t}(\mathbb{R}\mathbb P(E))=P_{t}(X)P_{t}(\mathbb{R}\mathbf{P}^{r-1}).

•

We deduce in particular from the last item that the blow-up
\widetilde{X}
of a Hodge-expressive
variety
X
along a
Hodge-expressive subvariety
Y
is Hodge-expressive as well.
Using again the scissor relations on
K_{0}(Var{C})
, we have

H_{(x,y)}(\widetilde{X})=H_{(x,y)}(X)+(x^{r-1}y^{r-1}+x^{r-2}y^{r-2}+\cdots+xy)H_{(x,y)}(Y),

where
r
is the codimension of
Y
in
X
.
The computation of Betti numbers of
\widetilde{X}
from

[
GH78
, Chapter 4, Section 6]
carries over word for word to the real
part. In particular, we obtain that
H^{*}(\widetilde{X};\mathbb{Z})
is
torsion-free if
X
and
Y
are, and that

P_{t}(\mathbb{R}\widetilde{X})=P_{t}(\mathbb{R}X)+(t^{r-1}+t^{r-2}+\cdots+t)P_{t}(\mathbb{R}Y).

One may also consider symmetric powers of real algebraic varieties, or
more generally quotients
X^{k}/\Gamma
of a
k^{\mathrm{th}}
product

X^{k}
by the canonical action of a
subgroup
\Gamma
of the symmetric group
\mathfrak S_{k}
. The product
and the symmetric power
correspond to the two extremal cases
\Gamma=\{\mathrm{Id}\}
and

\Gamma=\mathfrak S_{k}
, respectively. Any real structure on

X
extends canonically to
X^{k}/\Gamma
, and
Franz proved in
[
Fra18
]
that
X^{k}/\Gamma
is
a maximal real algebraic variety as soon as
X
is (note that the
converse does not hold in general, as shown by the second symmetric power
of
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
equipped with the antipodal involution).
It would be interesting to investigate whether

X^{k}/\Gamma
is Hodge-expressive as soon as it is
non-singular and
X
is Hodge-expressive.
When
\Gamma=\mathfrak S_{k}
, the
k^{\mathrm{th}}
symmetric power

X^{[k]}
of
X

is projective and non-singular if and only if
X
is a non-singular
projective curve.
In this case partial results regarding Hodge-expressivity can
be obtained, as we shall see in Section
3.2
below.

3.2. Curves

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a non-singular real projective curve of genus
g
. In
this case the Smith–Thom inequality reduces to
the Harnack–Klein inequality

b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})\ \leqslant\ g+1,

with equality if and only if
\mathscr{C}
is maximal.
Since

H_{(x,y)}(\mathscr{C})=1+g(x+y)+xy,

and

P_{t}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})\ =\ b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})(1+t)\leqslant(g+1)(1+t)\ =\ H_{(t,1)}(\mathscr{C}),

the curve
\mathscr{C}
is Hodge-expressive if and only if it is maximal.

By the Abel–Jacobi Theorem, we have

\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\simeq\mathrm{Jac}(\mathscr{C})
for all
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, so

H_{(x,y)}\big(\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\big)\ =\ (1+x)^{g}\,(1+y)^{g}.

By
[
GH81
]
, if
b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})>0
one has

P_{t}\big(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\big)\ =\ 2^{b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})-1}\,(1+t)^{g}\ \leqslant\ 2^{g}\,(1+t)^{g}\ =\ H_{(t,1)}\big(\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\big).

Since
\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})
has torsion-free integral cohomology, we
deduce that

(3.1)

\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\mbox{ Hodge-expressive}\Longleftrightarrow\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})\mbox{ maximal }\Longleftrightarrow\mathscr{C}\mbox{ maximal}

as soon as
b_{0}(\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})>0
.

Remark 3.1
.

When
\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C}=\emptyset
, one has
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})=\emptyset
if

g
and
d
are both odd
[
GH81
]
. And if
g
is odd and
d
is
even, the Poincaré polynomial of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})
is

2(1+t)^{g}
, so its value at
t=1
is strictly smaller than
2^{2g}
,
unless
g=1
(in which case
\mathrm{Pic}^{2d}(\mathscr{C})
is maximal even
though
\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C}=\emptyset
). Finally, if
g
is even, the Poincaré
polynomial of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(\mathscr{C})
is
(1+t)^{g}
, so its value at

t=1
is strictly smaller than
2^{2g}
, unless
g=0
.

From the utmost right equivalence in (
3.1
) and the discussion in Section
3.1
, one deduces that the
k^{\mathrm{th}}
symmetric power
\mathscr{C}^{[k]}

of
\mathscr{C}
is Hodge-expressive if
\mathscr{C}
is maximal and

k\geqslant 2g-1
, which refines
[
BDM17
, Theorem 3.1]
.
Indeed, the Riemann–Roch and Abel–Jacobi theorems
imply that
\mathscr{C}^{[k]}
can be
expressed as the projectivization of a real algebraic vector bundle
over
Jac(\mathscr{C})
, see
[
ACGH85
, Chapter IV]
.
Furthermore, from the explicit computation of Betti numbers of
\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C}^{[k]}
for

k=2,3
in
[
BDM17
]
, one also sees that
\mathscr{C}^{[k]}
is
Hodge-expressive when
\mathscr{C}
is maximal and
k=2,3
(see for example

[
LS11
]
for Hodge numbers of
\mathscr{C}^{[k]}
).
It would be interesting to investigate whether
\mathscr{C}^{[k]}
is
Hodge-expressive as soon as
\mathscr{C}
is.

Hodge-expressivity may also be used to determine
“elementary embeddings” of real algebraic curves in
real algebraic surfaces.
As an example, let us consider
Harnack curves in the real projective plane. They
were originally
constructed by Harnack in
[
Har76
]
, and constituted the first
family of maximal curves of arbitrary degree in
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{P}^{2}
.
Since then, Harnack curves have also been obtained
as elementary applications of
a large proportion of construction methods of maximal real algebraic curves
(
e.g.

[
IV96
]
). Surprisingly, they also appeared in several
contexts other than pure real algebraic geometry (
e.g.

[
KO06
]
).
To summarize informally, a maximal real algebraic curve in
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a Harnack curve, except if it has a
good reason not to be so.

The topological type of the pair
(\mathbb{R}\mathbf{P}^{2},\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C})
for a Harnack curve

\mathscr{C}
in
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{P}^{2}
is depicted in Figure
2
below.

d=2k

d=2k+1

d
in
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{P}^{2}

Suppose that a Harnack curve
\mathscr{C}
of even degree
2k
in
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{P}^{2}

is given by the real equation
P(x,y,z)=0
, where the real polynomial

P(x,y,z)
is positive on the non-orientable

\mathbb{R}\mathbf{P}^{2}\setminus\mathbb{R}\mathscr{C}
. Then, on the one hand, one easily sees from

X
with

3.3. Surfaces

We refer to
[
DK00
, Section 3]
and
[
Man17
, Chapter 4]
for
more details and references
regarding the various classifications of real algebraic
surfaces discussed in this section.

It follows from the
classifications by Comessati that all maximal real Abelian surfaces are Hodge-expressive. For
examples, the Hodge diamond of an Abelian surface is

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}&&1&&\\
&2&&2&\\
1&&4&&1\\
&2&&2&\\
&&1&&\end{array},

and such a real surface
X
is maximal if and only if
\mathbb{R}X
is the
disjoint union of four tori
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

There exist maximal real algebraic

K3
-surfaces that are not Hodge-expressive. As shown by Kharlamov
[
Kha76
]
, the
three possible topological types for the real part of a
maximal real
K3
-surface are: the disjoint union of a sphere
S^{2}
and a
surface of genus
10
, the disjoint union of five spheres with a
surface of genus
6
, and the disjoint union of nine spheres with a
surface of genus
2
.
Since a
K3
surface has torsion-free integral cohomology and has
the following Hodge diamond

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}&&1&&\\
&0&&0&\\
1&&20&&1\\
&0&&0&\\
&&1&&\end{array},

only the first topological type corresponds to a Hodge-expressive
variety.

Note that there exist maximal real algebraic Enriques surfaces
satisfying

b_{i}(\mathbb{R}X)\ >\ \sumop\displaylimits_{j\geqslant 0}h^{i,j}(X)\quad\forall i\in\{0,1,2\},

see
[
DK00
,
Man17
]
. This shows the necessity of
the torsion-freeness assumption in the implication

\Big(P_{t}(\mathbb{R}X)=H_{(t,1)}(X)\Big)\Longrightarrow X\mbox{ maximal}.

The first family of maximal real algebraic surfaces of arbitrary degree in

\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{3}
was constructed by Viro in
[
Vir79
]
,
generalizing Harnack’s construction.
A non-singular hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{n}
has torsion-free
integral cohomology by the Lefschetz hyperplane section Theorem,
and it turns out that
all
surfaces in Viro’s family are
Hodge-expressive.

3.4. Higher dimension

As mentioned earlier, our knowledge of maximal real
algebraic varieties of dimension at least 3 is quite restricted.
By
[
Kra09
]
, there exists a unique topological type of maximal real
cubic 3-folds in
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{4}
, and one checks that it is Hodge-expressive.
By
[
FK10
]
, there exist three topological types of maximal real
cubic 4-folds in
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{5}
, only one of them being Hodge-expressive.

All maximal real algebraic projective hypersurfaces from
Itenberg and Viro’s unpublished construction are Hodge-expressive.
As mentioned earlier,
this is even a crucial point in their argumentation,
since they
prove that the real algebraic hypersurfaces that they construct are maximal
by showing that they are Hodge-expressive.
Note that the Itenberg–Viro’s construction uses primitive combinatorial
patchworking.
Renaudineau and Shaw recently proved
[
RS18
]

that all maximal real algebraic hypersurfaces of a non-singular compact toric
variety that are obtained by primitive combinatorial patchworking are
Hodge-expressive, thus confirming a long standing conjecture of Itenberg’s.

We are not aware of any general construction of maximal real
hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{n}
that are not Hodge-expressive.

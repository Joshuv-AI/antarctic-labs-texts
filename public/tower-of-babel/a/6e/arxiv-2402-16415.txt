Achievable Rate Optimization for Stacked Intelligent Metasurface-Assisted Holographic MIMO Communications
Anastasios Papazafeiropoulos, Jiancheng An, Pandelis Kourtessis, Tharmalingam Ratnarajah, Symeon Chatzinotas

Abstract
Stacked intelligent metasurfaces (SIM) is a revolutionary technology, which can outperform its single-layer counterparts by performing advanced signal processing relying on wave propagation. In this work, we exploit SIM to enable transmit precoding and receiver combining in holographic multiple-input multiple-output (HMIMO) communications, and we study the achievable rate by formulating a joint optimization problem of the SIM phase shifts at both sides of the transceiver and the covariance matrix of the transmitted signal. Notably, we propose its solution by means of an iterative optimization algorithm that relies on the projected gradient method, and accounts for all optimization parameters simultaneously. We also obtain the step size guaranteeing the convergence of the proposed algorithm. Simulation results provide fundamental insights such the performance improvements compared to the single-RIS counterpart and conventional MIMO system. Remarkably, the proposed algorithm results in the same achievable rate as the alternating optimization (AO) benchmark but with a less number of iterations.

•

We maximise the achievable rate of a multi stream HMIMO system equipped with a SIM at the transmitter and a SIM at the receiver. To this end, we formulate the joint optimization problem of the transmit covariance matrix, and the RIS phase shift values of each surface at the transmitter and the receiver SIMs.

System Model

We consider a SIM-assisted HMIMO, where the transmitter and the receiver have

In particular, the transmitter SIM plays the role of the precoder by sending the information-bearing EM wave through the ether to the receiver SIM, which can combine the received EM to recover the transmitted signal. In other words, precoding and combining take place partially in the wave domain

Problem Formulation

In this work, we aim at maximizing the achievable rate of the SIM-assisted HMIMO wireless communication system. For a given covariance matrix
{\mathbf{Q}}
, assuming Gaussian signaling, the achievable rate can be written as

\displaystyle R=\log_{2}\det\left({\bm{\mathrm{I}}}+\frac{1}{N_{0}}{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{H}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}}\right)(\mathrm{bit/s/Hz}),

(19)

where
{\mathbf{H}}
is perfectly known at both the transmitter and the receiver, and depends on
{\bm{\phi}}^{l},l\in\mathcal{L}
and
{\bm{\psi}}^{k},k\in\mathcal{K}
.

Mathematically, the optimization problem can be formulated as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P})~~

\displaystyle\max_{{\mathbf{Q}},{\bm{\phi}}_{l},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}}\;f({\mathbf{Q}},{\bm{\phi}}_{l},{\bm{\psi}}_{k})=\ln\det\left({\bm{\mathrm{I}}}+\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}{\mathbf{Q}}\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}}\right)

(20a)

\displaystyle~\mathrm{s.t}~~~\;\!\tr(\bQ)\leq P;{\mathbf{Q}}\succeq{\mathbf{0}},

(20b)

\displaystyle\;\quad\;\;\;\;\;\!\!~\!{\mathbf{P}}={\bm{\Phi}}^{L}{\mathbf{W}}^{L}\cdots{\bm{\Phi}}^{2}{\mathbf{W}}^{2}{\bm{\Phi}}^{1}{\mathbf{W}}^{1},

(20c)

\displaystyle\;\quad\;\;\;\;\;\!\!~\!{\mathbf{Z}}={\mathbf{U}}^{1}{\bm{\Psi}}^{1}{\mathbf{U}}^{2}{\bm{\Psi}}^{2}\cdots{\mathbf{U}}^{K}{\bm{\Psi}}^{K},

(20d)

\displaystyle\;\quad\;\;\;\;\;\!\!~\!{\bm{\Phi}}^{l}=\text{diag}(\phi^{l}_{1},\dots,\phi^{l}_{M}),l\in\mathcal{L},

(20e)

\displaystyle\;\quad\;\;\;\;\;\!\!~\!{\bm{\Psi}}^{k}=\text{diag}(\psi^{k}_{1},\dots,\psi^{k}_{N}),k\in\mathcal{K},

(20f)

\displaystyle\;\quad\;\;\;\;\;\!\!~\!|\phi^{l}_{m}|=1,m\in\mathcal{M},l\in\mathcal{L},

(20g)

\displaystyle\;\quad\;\;\;\;\;\!\!~\!|\psi^{k}_{n}|=1,n\in\mathcal{N},k\in\mathcal{K},

(20h)

where we have denoted
\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}={\mathbf{H}}/\sqrt{N_{0}}

III
Achievable Rate Optimization

We observe that problem
(\mathcal{P})
is nonconvex with an objective function being neither concave nor convex with respect to its variables and with non-convex constant modulus constraints. Hence, contrary to conventional MIMO systems, the
water-filling algorithm cannot be used to obtain the maximum achievable rate. Also, previous proposed optimization methods on RIS-assisted systems have relied on the alternating optimization (AO)
[
9
,
19
]
, where the covariance matrix and the RIS phase shifts are optimized separately in an alternating fashion. However, despite the easy implementation of the AO method, its convergence may require many iterations, which increase with the number of RIS elements
[
20
]
. Given that SIM-assisted HMIMO systems suggest a case, where each metasurface has a large number of elements, AO is not recommended. These observations motivate us to propose to apply an efficient projected gradient method similar to
[
42
,
20
]
, where the covariance matrix and the phase shifts at the transmitter and receiver SIMs are optimized simultaneously.

III-A

Proposed Algorithm

According to the proposed approach, we perform a simultaneous optimization of all variables in each iteration instead of optimizing them a single variable at a time. Section
V
will demonstrate the faster convergence compared to the AO method.

We outline the proposed algorithm solving (

f({\mathbf{Q}},{\bm{\phi}}_{l},{\bm{\psi}}_{k})

q=1,2,3

For the description of the proposed algorithm, we make use of the following sets.

Complexity Analysis

Herein, we present the computational complexity of Algorithm (
1

Steps
3-5
of Algorithm
1
determine the complexity of the proposed approach. In particular, the computation of
{\mathbf{H}}={\mathbf{Z}}{\mathbf{G}}{\mathbf{P}}
requires
N_{r}M(N+N_{t})

\nabla_{\mathcal{Q}}f({\mathbf{Q}},{\bm{\phi}}_{l},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}))

. Instead of this, we focus on
{\mathbf{V}}={\mathbf{K}}({\mathbf{Q}},{\bm{\phi}}_{l},{\bm{\psi}}_{k})\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}\times N_{t}}
as provided in (
24
). We can simplify its computation by observing that
{\mathbf{V}}
is the result to the linear system
\left({\bm{\mathrm{I}}}+\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}{\mathbf{Q}}\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}}\right){\mathbf{X}}=\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}
.
For
\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}{\mathbf{Q}}\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}}
, we need
N_{t}^{2}N_{r}
multiplications to form
\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}{\mathbf{Q}}
, and then
(N_{r}^{2}+N_{t})N_{t}/2
multiplications for the multiplication between
\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}{\mathbf{Q}}
and
\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}}
. A Cholesky decomposition leads to the solution of the linear system with a complexity equal to
\mathcal{O}(N_{r}^{3}+N_{r}^{2}N_{t})
. Overall,
{\mathbf{V}}
requires
\mathcal{O}(N_{r}^{3}+N_{r}^{2}N_{t}+\frac{3}{2}N_{t}N_{r}^{2})

\bar{{\mathbf{H}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}}{\mathbf{V}}

N_{t}^{2}N_{r}
multiplications.

\nabla_{{\bm{\psi}}_{k}}f({\mathbf{Q}},{\bm{\phi}}_{l},{\bm{\psi}}_{k})

{\mathbf{C}}_{K}

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a SIM-assisted HMIMO communication system, where the transmitter and the receiver are implemented by SIMs that enhance the wave-based analog precoding and combining. Specifically, we formulated the achievable rate problem, and we proposed an efficient gradient ascent algorithm that optimizes the covariance matrix of the transmitted signal and the SIM phase shifts at both sides of the transceiver simultaneously. Moreover, we obtained a Lipschitz constant guaranteeing the convergence of the proposed iterative algorithm. Numerical results confirmed that the proposed algorithm requires a lower number of iterations compared to the usually used AO approach. Finally, we showed the outperformance of the architecture with respect to its single-RIS counterpart and conventional MIMO system.

\nabla_{{\bm{\psi}}_{k}}f({\mathbf{Q}},{\bm{\phi}}_{l},{\bm{\psi}}_{k})
can be written as

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{{\bm{\psi}}_{k}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{1},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{1},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{1})-\nabla_{{\bm{\psi}}_{k}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{2},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{2},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{K}}({\mathbf{Q}}^{1},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{1},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{1}){\mathbf{C}}_{k,1}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}}-{\mathbf{K}}({\mathbf{Q}}^{2},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{2},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{2}{\mathbf{C}}_{k,2}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{H}}})\|.

(102)

In a similar way, we can upper bound the previous inequality. We omit the details due to limited space. Hence, we conclude the proof.

Proof of Theorem

According to Lemma (

), we can write

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{{\mathbf{Q}}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{1},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{1},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{1})-\nabla_{{\mathbf{Q}}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{2},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{2},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{2})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla_{{\bm{\phi}}_{l}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{1},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{1},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{1})-\nabla_{{\bm{\phi}}_{l}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{2},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{2},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{2})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla_{{\bm{\psi}}_{k}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{1},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{1},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{1})-\nabla_{{\bm{\psi}}_{k}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{2},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{2},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}\|{\mathbf{Q}}^{1}-{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\|^{2}+\Lambda_{{\bm{\phi}}_{l}}^{2}\|{\mathbf{P}}^{1}-{\mathbf{P}}^{2}\|^{2}+\Lambda_{{\bm{\psi}}_{k}}^{2}\|{\mathbf{Z}}^{1}-{\mathbf{Z}}^{2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\|{\mathbf{Z}}^{1}-{\mathbf{Z}}^{2}\|^{2}\big).

(103)

Applying the square root on both sides of (
103
) together with the inequality

\displaystyle 2\|{\mathbf{Q}}^{1}-{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\|\cdot\|{\mathbf{P}}^{1}-{\mathbf{P}}^{2}\|+2\|{\mathbf{Z}}^{1}-{\mathbf{Z}}^{2}\|\cdot\|{\mathbf{P}}^{1}-{\mathbf{P}}^{2}\|

we observe that

Appendix D
Proof of Theorem
2

The point
{\mathbf{Q}}^{n+1}
can be projected onto
\mathcal{Q}
according to the following equation.

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Q}}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{{\mathbf{Q}}\in\mathcal{Q}}\|{\mathbf{Q}}-{\mathbf{Q}}^{n}-\mu_{n}^{1}\nabla_{\mathcal{Q}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{n},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{{\mathbf{Q}}\in\mathcal{Q}}\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{Q}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{n},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n}),{\mathbf{Q}}-{\mathbf{Q}}^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\mu_{n}^{1}}\|{\mathbf{Q}}-{\mathbf{Q}}^{n}\|^{2}.

(105)

In the case of
{\mathbf{Q}}-{\mathbf{Q}}^{n}
, we meet that the objective is
0
, which means that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{Q}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{n},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n}),{\mathbf{Q}}-{\mathbf{Q}}^{n}\rangle-\frac{1}{2\mu_{n}^{1}}\|{\mathbf{Q}}-{\mathbf{Q}}^{n}\|^{2}\geq 0.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{{\bm{\phi}}_{l}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{n},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n}),{\bm{\phi}}_{l}-{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n}\rangle-\frac{1}{2\mu_{n}^{2}}\|{\bm{\phi}}_{l}-{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n}\|^{2}\geq 0,

(107)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{{\bm{\psi}}_{k}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{n},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n}),{\bm{\psi}}_{k}-{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n}\rangle-\frac{1}{2\mu_{n}^{3}}\|{\bm{\psi}}_{k}-{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n}\|^{2}\geq 0.

(108)

Now, applying the following inequality

\displaystyle f({\mathbf{y}})\geq f({\mathbf{y}})+\langle\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}),{\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{x}}\rangle-\frac{\Lambda}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2},

(109)

which holds for any
\Lambda
-smooth function

, we obtain

\displaystyle f({\mathbf{Q}}^{n+1},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n+1},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n+1})\geq f({\mathbf{Q}}^{n},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n})

\displaystyle+\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{Q}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{n},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n}),{\mathbf{Q}}-{\mathbf{Q}}^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\nabla_{{\bm{\phi}}_{l}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{n},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n}),{\bm{\phi}}_{l}-{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\nabla_{{\bm{\psi}}_{k}}f({\mathbf{Q}}^{n},{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n},{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n}),{\bm{\psi}}_{k}-{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{\Lambda}{2}\|{\mathbf{Q}}^{n+1}-{\mathbf{Q}}^{n}\|^{2}-\frac{\Lambda}{2}\|{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n+1}-{\bm{\phi}}_{l}^{n}\|^{2}-\frac{\Lambda}{2}\|{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n+1}-{\bm{\psi}}_{k}^{n}\|^{2}

The trianguline variety, tangent spaces and the Grothendieck-Springer resolution
Seginus Mowlavi

Abstract
. By the work of Breuil-Hellmann-Schraen, we know that the trianguline variety contains crystalline companion points which are parametrised by pairs w ⪯ w sat w\preceq w_{\mathrm{sat}} of permutations where ⪯ \preceq is the Bruhat order. We first define and study a certain combinatorial property of a pair w ′ ⪯ w w^{\prime}\preceq w of permutations in the context of Weyl groups of root systems. We call good pairs the pairs satisfying this property (which is the vast majority of pairs) and bad pairs the other ones. We then give an exact formula for the dimension of the tangent space to the trianguline variety at (generic) crystalline companion points such that w ⪯ w sat w\preceq w_{\mathrm{sat}} is a good pair. The method (due to Breuil-Hellmann-Schraen) is to first compute an analogous dimension for a local model of the trianguline variety built out of Grothendieck’s simultaneous resolution. To achieve this, we prove a conjecture of Breuil-Hellmann-Schraen, describing the intersection of the closure of a Schubert cell with another Schubert cell on this local model (in the context of an arbitrary split reductive group), when this pair of cells is parametrised by a good pair of permutations. We give counter-examples to this conjecture for an infinite family of pairs of cells (associated to bad pairs).



1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
. In their study
[
8
]
of the trianguline variety
X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
of Hellmann
[
12
]
, Breuil-Hellmann-Schraen introduced a variety
X
over
k
associated to a split reductive group
G
over
k
. This variety admits a stratification
X=\coprod_{w\in W}V_{w}
indexed by the Weyl group
W
of
G
, similar to the Schubert decomposition of the flag variety. Denoting by
X_{w}
the Zariski-closure in
X
of the locally closed subvarieties
V_{w}
, the authors of
[
8
]
have linked the dimension of the tangent spaces of
X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
at certain points of interest to the dimension of
T_{X_{w},x}
at particular points
x\in X_{w}\cap V_{w^{\prime}}
for
w,w^{\prime}\in W
such that
w^{\prime}\preceq w
where
\preceq
is the Bruhat order. They also formulate a conjecture describing
X_{w}\cap V_{w^{\prime}}
, which if true allows to compute the dimension of
T_{X_{w},x}
. This conjecture is the object of the present paper.

A special case of a variant of this conjecture was already known by the work of Ginsburg
[
11
]
using the Beilinson-Bernstein correspondence. By applying this special case to certain Levi subgroups of
G
, we are able to expand this result into a proof of many cases of the conjecture.

These cases are characterised by a combinatorial condition on the pair
(w^{\prime},w)
that we call being a “good pair”. We develop the theory of good pairs in the context of Weyl groups of root systems and establish along the way different criteria for good pairs. We also show that the set of good pairs is related to pattern avoidance.

Finally, by spelling out explicit equations defining
V_{w}
as a quasiprojective variety in the case
G=\GL_{n/k}
, we give a family of counter-examples to the conjecture.

We now explain our results in more detail.

Good pairs in a Weyl group

Let
\Phi
be an abstract root system; we write
W\coloneqq W(\Phi)
for its Weyl group. For
w\in W
, let
\Gamma_{w}\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathbb{Z}(w(\alpha)-\alpha)
, let
E_{w}\coloneqq\Gamma_{w}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
and finally let
\Phi_{w}\coloneqq\Phi\cap E_{w}
. We call
\Phi_{w}
the
minimal generating root subsystem of
w
. It has a clear interpretation in terms of reductive groups:

Proposition 1

(Proposition
2.2.2
)
.

Let
(G,T)
be a connected split reductive algebraic group over a field
k
of characteristic 0 whose root system is isomorphic to
\Phi
. Then the centraliser
\left(C_{G}((T^{w})^{\circ}),T\right)
is a connected split reductive group whose root system is isomorphic to
\Phi_{w}
.

Fixing a basis
I
of
\Phi
gives a length function
\lg
and a Bruhat order
\prec
on
W
. The central notion we study in §
2
and use in §
4
is the following:

Definition 2

(Definition
2.3.1
)
.

Let
(w_{1},w_{2})\in W
such that
w_{1}\preceq w_{2}
. If there exist roots
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\in\Phi_{w_{1}w_{2}^{-1}}
which give rise to an ascending sequence
w_{1}\prec w_{1}s_{\alpha_{1}}\prec\ldots\prec w_{1}s_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{r}}=w_{2}
, then
(w_{1},w_{2})
is called a
good pair
; otherwise, it is called a
bad pair
.

In §
2.1
, we establish a few combinatorial results about minimal generating subsystems
\Phi_{w}
. In §
2.3
we prove a criterion for good pairs which is motivated by the fact that, for any
w\in W
, the subsystem
\Phi_{w}
is conjugated to a standard subsystem,
i.e.
a root subsystem
\Phi_{J}\subseteq\Phi
generated by some subset
J\subseteq I
of simple roots. More precisely, for
J\subseteq I
, let
W_{J}
be the subgroup of
W
generated by
\left\{s_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in J\right\}
, and let
W^{J}
be the set of representatives of minimal length for
W_{J}\backslash W
. Then one can write
\Phi_{w}=u^{J}(\Phi_{J})
for some
J\subseteq I
and
u^{J}\in W^{J}
. The criterion is:

Proposition 3

(Proposition
2.3.7
)
.

Let
(w_{1},w_{2})\in W^{2}
. Write
\Phi_{w_{2}w_{1}^{-1}}=u^{J}(\Phi_{J})
for some
J\subseteq I
and
u^{J}\in W^{J}
. Then there exist
v^{J}\in W^{J}
and
w_{J,1},w_{J,2}\in W_{J}
such that

w_{1}=u^{J}w_{J,1}(v^{J})^{-1}\,,\quad w_{2}=u^{J}w_{J,2}(v^{J})^{-1}\,,

and the pair
(w_{1},w_{2})
is good if and only if
w_{J,1}\preceq w_{J,2}
.

We conclude in §
2.4
with an application of this last criterion in the special case where
\Phi
is of type
A_{n-1}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
,
i.e.
W=\mathcal{S}_{n}
is the symmetric group. It is stated in terms of what is called
pattern avoidance
. Here we say that a permutation
w\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
has a pattern
f\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
when there exist
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{m}\leq n
such that the
m
-uples
(w(i_{1}),\ldots,w(i_{m}))
and
(f(1),\ldots,f(m))
are in the same order; see Definition
2.4.8
. This can in fact be stated in the general setting of an abstract root system, and many properties such as smoothness of Schubert varieties have been found to be instances of pattern avoidance; see for example
[
3
, §2, §4]
. We prove that, at least for root systems of type
A
, being a good pair is also an instance of pattern avoidance:

Theorem 4

(Theorem
2.4.10
)
.

Let
w\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
.

(1)

The pair
(w^{\prime},w)
is good for any
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
if and only if
w
avoids the four patterns
[4231]
,
[42513]
,
[35142]
and
[351624]
.

(2)

The pair
(w,w^{\prime\prime})
is good for any
w^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
if and only if
w
avoids the four patterns
[1324]
,
[24153]
,
[31524]
and
[426153]
.

Linking these patterns with those deciding smoothness of Schubert varieties, we obtain that, if
(w,w^{\prime})\in(\mathcal{S}_{n})^{2}
is a bad pair, then the Schubert variety
\overline{BwB/B}
in the flag variety
G/B
is singular (but not necessarily at the point
w^{\prime}B\in\overline{BwB/B}
); see Corollary
2.4.11
.

The conjecture of
[
8
]

Let
G
be a connected split reductive group over
k
,
T
a split maximal torus inside
G
,
B
a Borel subgroup of
G
containing
T
and
U
the unipotent subgroup of
B
. We write
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{t}
,
\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{u}
for their respective Lie algebras, and
W\coloneqq N_{G}(T)/T
for the Weyl group of
(G,T)
. For
w\in W
, we choose a lifting of
w
in
N_{G}(T)
which we still call
w
. We have the Schubert decomposition
G/B=\coprod_{w\in W}BwB/B
of
G/B
into locally closed strata
BwB/B
, where a cell
Bw^{\prime}B/B
intersects the Zariski-closure
\overline{BwB/B}
of
BwB/B
if and only if
w^{\prime}\preceq w
for the Bruhat order on
W
, in which case
Bw^{\prime}B/B\subseteq\overline{BwB/B}
.

Let
G\times^{B}\mathfrak{g}
be the quotient of
G\times\mathfrak{g}
by the right-action of
B
given by
(g,\psi)b=(gb,\Ad(b^{-1})\psi)
. We define the morphisms
q\colon G\times^{B}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and
\widetilde{\pi}\colon G\times^{B}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow G/B
by
q(g,\psi)=\Ad(g)\psi
and
\widetilde{\pi}(g,\psi)=gB
(

is called Grothendieck’s simultaneous resolution of singularities). We then define the algebraic variety
\widetilde{X}\coloneqq q^{-1}(\mathfrak{b})
. For
w\in W
, we consider the locally closed subvariety
\widetilde{V}_{w}\coloneqq\widetilde{X}\cap\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(BwB/B)
of
\widetilde{X}
as well as its Zariski-closure
\widetilde{X}_{w}
in
\widetilde{X}
; they give a cell decomposition of
\widetilde{X}
.

When
L
is another split reductive group over
k
, we add the subscript
L
to
q,\widetilde{\pi},\widetilde{X},\widetilde{V}_{w},\widetilde{X}_{w}
(for
w\in W(L)
) to mean the same definition with the reductive group
L
in place of the reductive group
G
. When nothing is specified, we mean
G
.

Similarly to Schubert cells in
G/B
, the intersection
\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap\widetilde{X}_{w}
is non-empty if and only if
w^{\prime}\preceq w
. The difference is that in this case
\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
is not contained in
\widetilde{X}_{w}
. More precisely, it is not difficult to show that
\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap\widetilde{X}_{w}\subseteq\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})
(
[
8
, Lemma 2.3.4]
) where
\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}
is the closed subvariety of fixed points by
ww^{\prime-1}
and Breuil-Hellmann-Schraen conjecture that this is an equality:

Conjecture 5

(Conj. 2.3.7 of
[
8
]
)
.

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in W
. If
w^{\prime}\preceq w
, then

\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap\widetilde{X}_{w}=\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap q^{-1}\left(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u}\right)\,.

Conjecture
5
first appeared in the arithmetic context of the study of the rigid analytic
trianguline variety

X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
introduced by Hellmann
[
12
]
(here
\bar{r}
is a mod
p
representation of
\Gal(\overline{K}/K)
where
p
is a prime number and
K
a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
). More precisely, Breuil-Hellmann-Schraen proved in
[
8
]
that the varieties
\widetilde{X}_{w}
are local models of
X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
at
crystalline regular generic points
, which allow them to derive a number of consequences about the local geometry of
X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
at these points.

We show in §
5
that Conj.
5
is actually
false
in general! In §
4
, we nonetheless prove it in the case of good pairs:

Theorem 6

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in W
such that
w^{\prime}\preceq w
and
(w^{\prime},w)
is a good pair. Then, for all
t\in\mathfrak{t}
fixed by
ww^{\prime-1}
, we have

(1.1)

\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap q^{-1}(t+\mathfrak{u})\subseteq\widetilde{X}_{w}\,.

Hence Conj.
5
for
(w^{\prime},w)
is true.

Going back to the motivation of
[
8
]
, Theorem
6
has the following application:

Corollary 7

(Theorem
3.3.2
)
.

Let
x\in X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
be a crystalline regular generic point and
T_{X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r}),x}
the tangent space to
X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
at
x
. Let
w_{x},w
be as in
[
8
, §4.1]
(
w_{x}
and
w
are elements of a fixed product of Weyl groups of type
A
) and assume that
(w_{x},w)
is a good pair. Then

(1.2)

\dim_{k(x)}T_{X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r}),x}=\dim X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})-d_{ww_{x}^{-1}}+\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}T_{\overline{(BwB/B)},w_{x}B}-\length(w_{x})

is the residue field of

at

,

In

Secondly, by carefully decomposing the adjoint action of

is the Lie algebra of

(Proposition

)
.

Let

Let

Counter-examples

In the last §
5
, we give explicit equations and inequations for the projective varieties
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{V}_{w}
in the case
G=\GL_{n/k}
. Even though we do not have such an explicit description for the varieties
\widetilde{X}_{w}
, this is enough to find a family of counter-examples to Conj.
5
.

We use that
\widetilde{X}
can be seen as the subvariety
\left\{(gB,\psi)\in G/B\!\times\!\mathfrak{b}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\Ad(g^{-1})\psi\in\mathfrak{b}\right\}
of
G/B\times\mathfrak{b}
, see (
5.5
), which is easier to handle than a quotient variety. Indeed
\mathfrak{b}
is an affine space, and the flag variety
G/B
is very well-understood. Specifically,
G/B
is a subvariety of a product of Grassmannians defined by incidence relations; and there are well-known equations defining the Grassmannian as a subvariety of a projective space
via
the Plücker embedding. Therefore
G/B\times\mathfrak{b}
is well-understood as a subvariety of
\prod_{d=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n})\times\mathbb{A}^{n(n+1)/2}
. Details are spelled out in §
5.1
.

We then work out a set of equations defining
\widetilde{X}
inside
G/B\times\mathfrak{b}
; this is Lemma
5.2.3
. The next step is to use the (in)equations defining each Schubert cell
BwB/B
inside
G/B
in order to find a set
\{F_{i}=0,G_{j}\neq 0\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
of (in)equations defining
\widetilde{V}_{w}
inside
\prod_{d=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n})\times\mathbb{A}^{n(n+1)/2}
. Doing so by brute force is not too difficult; however the challenge is to have enough equations so that, hopefully, the set of equations
\{F_{i}=0\}_{i\in I}
describes the Zariski-closure
\widetilde{X}_{w}
of
\widetilde{V}_{w}
inside
\prod_{d=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n})\times\mathbb{A}^{n(n+1)/2}
. The result we achieve in Proposition
5.2.4
is quite satisfying, as Conj.
5
is equivalent to
\widetilde{X}_{w}
being the closed subset
\{F_{i}=0\}_{i\in I}
; see Proposition
5.3.1
.

On the other hand, through technical simplifications in §
5.3
, we improve on Proposition
5.2.4
by finding unexpected additional equations satisfied on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
. As a consequence, we get for a certain family of couples
(w,w^{\prime})\in(\mathcal{S}_{n})^{2}
, a beautifully simple equation satisfied on
\widetilde{X}_{w}\cap\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
but not on
\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})
, hence the following result:

Theorem 10

(Theorem
5.3.7
, Remark
5.3.8
, Remark
5.3.9
)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 4}
, there exists a bad pair
(w,w^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{S}_{n}
such that

\dim\left(\widetilde{X}_{w}\cap\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\right)<\dim\left(\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})\right)\,.

In particular, Conj.
5
is false.

To our knowledge, the question of deciding whether or not Conj.
5
is false for
all
bad pairs remains open (we know that this holds at least for
\GL_{4}
and
\GL_{5}
). Another area of further investigation is to find accurate estimates for the difference
\dim\big(\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})\big)-\dim\big(\widetilde{X}_{w}\cap\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\big)
.

. We denote by

,

.

\alpha\in X(T)\setminus\{0\}
such that the eigenspace
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\Ad(t)x=\alpha(t)x\;\forall t\in T\right\}
is non-zero. For any root
\alpha\in\Phi
, we have
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=1
(see
[
20
, Thm. 21.11(b)]
). We write
U_{\alpha}\subset G
for the root subgroup of
\alpha
(see
[
20
, 21.10, 21.11]
); its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}
satisfies
\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}(k)=\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.

Next, we write
\Gamma\subseteq X(T)
for the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule generated by
\Phi
, and
E\coloneqq\Gamma\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. We then write
I
for the basis of the root system
\Phi
defined by the Borel
B
and
\Phi_{+}
for the set of positive roots defined by
I
.

We write
W\coloneqq W(\Phi)
for the Weyl group of
\Phi
. Recall that
W(G,T)\coloneqq N_{G}(T)/T
is a finite group with a natural faithful action on
T
and hence on
X(T)
, which makes
W(G,T)
equal to
W
as groups of automorphisms of
X(T)
(see
[
20
, Cor. 21.38]
). We therefore make the identification
W=N_{G}(T)/T
. For
w\in W
, we write
\Ad(w)
for the conjugation morphism on
T
by any
\dot{w}\in N_{G}(T)
which lifts
w
,
i.e.
\Ad(w)t=\dot{w}t\dot{w}^{-1}
for
t\in T
. Then the action of
W
on
\Phi
can be written as
(w\alpha)(t)=\alpha(\Ad(w^{-1}){t})
for
\alpha\in\Phi
,
w\in W
and
t\in T
. Finally,
T^{w}\coloneqq\left\{t\in T\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\Ad(w)t=t\right\}
denotes the subgroup of
T
of invariants under
\Ad(w)
and
(T^{w})^{\circ}
denotes its identity component.

In §
2
(except §
2.2
),
E
is simply defined as a vector space over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\Phi
as an abstract root system in
E
, independently of any split reductive group
G
. In §
5
, we restrict to the case
G=\GL_{n/k}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
; then
T
and
B
are respectively taken to be the subgroups of diagonal and upper triangular matrices.

From §
3
onwards, we deal with algebraic varieties over
k
,
i.e.
reduced separated schemes of finite type over
k
. Morphisms of varieties are then uniquely determined by their value on
{k^{\mathrm{a}}}
-points, and their “image” (more precisely, the surjectivity onto a subvariety of the target) can be checked on closed points (see
[
27
, Ex. 7.4.C]
). Given two varieties
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
equipped with immersions into a third variety
X
, we write

X_{1}\cap X_{2}\coloneqq\left(X_{1}\times_{X}X_{2}\right)^{\mathrm{red}}

when there is no ambiguity. By base change, the natural map
X_{1}\cap X_{2}\longrightarrow X_{1}
is a closed (resp. locally closed) immersion when
X_{2}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow X
is closed (resp. locally closed), and similarly for
X_{1}\cap X_{2}\longrightarrow X_{2}
. In the same way, given a morphism of varieties
f\colon X\longrightarrow Y
and a closed (resp. locally closed) immersion
Y^{\prime}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow Y
, we write

f^{-1}(Y^{\prime})\coloneqq\left(X\times_{Y}Y^{\prime}\right)^{\mathrm{red}}\,,

and the natural map
f^{-1}(Y^{\prime})\longrightarrow X
is a closed (resp. locally closed) immersion.

2.
Some combinatorics in Weyl groups

In this section, we study particular subsystems of a root system
\Phi
associated with an element of the Weyl group
W(\Phi)
. We then define the notions of
good pairs
and
bad pairs
in
W(\Phi)
. Finally, we explicitly describe the case of systems of type
A
.

2.1.
Minimal generating root subsystems

Let
E
be a finite dimensional a vector space over
\mathbb{Q}
and let
\Phi
be a root system inside
E
(see
[
5
, Chapter VI, §1]
). Write the (finite) Weyl group of
\Phi
as
W\subset\End_{\mathbb{Q}}(V)
. For a root
\alpha\in\Phi
, we denote by
s_{\alpha}\in W
its associated reflection.

Definition 2.1.1
.

Let
w\in W
,
\Gamma_{w}
the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of
E
generated by
w(\alpha)-\alpha
for
\alpha\in\Phi
,
E_{w}\coloneqq\Gamma_{w}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}=\im(w-\id_{E})
and
\Phi_{w}\coloneqq\Phi\cap E_{w}
. We call
\Phi_{w}
the
minimal generating (root) subsystem
of
w
. We also define
d_{w}\coloneqq\rk_{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma_{w}=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}E_{w}
.

Being the intersection of
\Phi
with a subspace
E_{w}
of
E
, the set
\Phi_{w}
is a root subsystem in
\Phi
,
i.e.
it is a root system in the subspace of
E
that it generates (see the Corollary to Proposition 4 of
[
5
, Chapter VI, §1.1]
). Note that this latter subspace may be strictly smaller than
E_{w}
; however we will see later in Corollary
2.1.5
that this never occurs. It therefore makes sense to consider the Weyl group
W(\Phi_{w})
of
\Phi_{w}
as a subgroup of
W
, with the convention
W(\emptyset)=\{1\}
.

Proposition 2.1.2
.

Let
w\in W
. Then
w
is in the Weyl group
W(\Phi_{w})
of its minimal generating subsystem.

Proof.

Any root system comes from some connected split reductive group
G
over
\mathbb{Q}
(see for instance the discussion in
[
14
, §33.6]
). Hence the statement follows from Corollary
2.2.3
below.
∎

We equip
E
with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
(\,\cdot\mid\cdot\,)
invariant by
W
(see Proposition 3 of
[
5
, Chapter VI, §1.1]
). Then every root
\alpha\in\Phi
satisfies
(\alpha\mid\alpha)\neq 0
, and
s_{\alpha}(x)=x-2\frac{(x\mid\alpha)}{(\alpha\mid\alpha)}\alpha
for all
x\in E
. For any
x\in E
, we also note
x^{\perp}\coloneqq\ker(\,\cdot\mid x)
the orthogonal subspace of
x
.

Lemma 2.1.3
.

Let
w\in W
and
x\in E
. Then
x\in E_{w}
if and only if
\ker(w-\id_{E})\subseteq x^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Suppose
x\in E_{w}
; take
y\in E
such that
x=w(y)-y
. Then, for all
z\in\ker(w-\id)=\ker(w^{-1}-\id)
, we have

(z\mid x)=(z\mid w(y)-y)=(z\mid w(y))-(z\mid y)=(w^{-1}(z)-z\mid y)=0

hence
z\in x^{\perp}
.

Conversely, suppose that
\ker(w-\id)\subseteq x^{\perp}
. Then the linear form
(\,\cdot\mid x)
factors through a linear form
l
on
E/\ker(w-\id)
. The map
w-\id
also factors through an isomorphism
f\colon E/\ker(w-\id)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}\im(w-\id)
. The linear form
l\circ f^{-1}
on
\im(w-\id)
can be extended arbitrarily to a linear form
\widetilde{l}
on
E
. Since
(\,\cdot\mid\cdot\,)
is non-degenerate, there exists
y\in E
such that
\widetilde{l}=(\,\cdot\mid y)
. Then, for all
z\in E
,

(z\mid x)=(w(z)-z\mid y)=(w(z)\mid y)-(z\mid y)=(z\mid w^{-1}(y)-y)

thus, by non-degeneracy,
x=w^{-1}(y)-y=(w-\id)(-w^{-1}(y))\in E_{w}
.
∎

Proposition 2.1.4
.

Let
w\in W
and
\alpha\in\Phi
. Let
w^{\prime}
be either
s_{\alpha}w
or
ws_{\alpha}
.

(1)

If
\alpha\notin\Phi_{w}
, then
E_{w^{\prime}}=\mathbb{Q}\alpha\oplus E_{w}
. In particular,
d_{w^{\prime}}=d_{w}+1
.

(2)

If
\alpha\in\Phi_{w}
, then
E_{w}=\mathbb{Q}\alpha\oplus E_{w^{\prime}}
. In particular,
d_{w^{\prime}}=d_{w}-1
.

Proof.

Assume that
w^{\prime}=ws_{\alpha}
. Using the decomposition
E=\mathbb{Q}\alpha\oplus\alpha^{\perp}
, one can write

(2.1)

E_{w}=\mathbb{Q}(w(\alpha)-\alpha)+(w-\id)\alpha^{\perp}

and
E_{w}=(w-\id)\alpha^{\perp}
if and only if
\ker(w-\id)\not\subseteq\alpha^{\perp}
. From Lemma
2.1.3
, this happens if and only if
\alpha\notin E_{w}
.
Similarly, because
w^{\prime}(\alpha)=-w(\alpha)
and
w^{\prime}|_{\alpha^{\perp}}=w|_{\alpha^{\perp}}
,

(2.2)

E_{w^{\prime}}=\mathbb{Q}(w(\alpha)+\alpha)+(w-\id)\alpha^{\perp}

and
E_{w^{\prime}}=(w-\id)\alpha^{\perp}
if and only if
\alpha\notin E_{w^{\prime}}
.

Suppose first that
\alpha\notin\Phi_{w}
, or equivalently
\alpha\notin E_{w}
. Then

w(\alpha)-\alpha\in E_{w}=(w-\id)\alpha^{\perp}\subseteq E_{w^{\prime}}\,.

Since
w(\alpha)+\alpha\in E_{w^{\prime}}
, we get
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}(w(\alpha)+\alpha)-\frac{1}{2}(w(\alpha)-\alpha)\in E_{w^{\prime}}
. We also know from (
2.2
) that
E_{w}=(w-\id)\alpha^{\perp}\subseteq E_{w^{\prime}}
and that
\dim{E_{w^{\prime}}}\leq\dim{E_{w}}+1
. Altogether, this yields
E_{w^{\prime}}=\mathbb{Q}\alpha\oplus E_{w}
, so
(1)
is proved.

Now suppose that
\alpha\in\Phi_{w}
, or equivalently
\alpha\in E_{w}
. Take
x\in E
such that
\alpha=w(x)-x
. Then

(w(x)+x\mid\alpha)=(w(x)+x\mid w(x)-x)=(w(x)\mid w(x))-(x\mid x)=0

so
w(x)+x\in\alpha^{\perp}
, hence
x=\frac{1}{2}(-\alpha+w(x)+x)\in-\frac{1}{2}\alpha+\alpha^{\perp}
. Therefore

2\alpha=2(w(x)-x)\in\alpha-w(\alpha)+(w-\id)\alpha^{\perp}

which can be rewritten as
w(\alpha)+\alpha\in(w-\id)\alpha^{\perp}
. From (
2.2
), this means
E_{w^{\prime}}=(w-\id)\alpha^{\perp}
, so
\alpha\notin E_{w^{\prime}}
. As before, this also implies, using (
2.1
), that
E_{w^{\prime}}\subseteq E_{w}
and
\dim{E_{w}}\leq\dim{E_{w^{\prime}}}+1
. We deduce that
E_{w}=\mathbb{Q}\alpha\oplus E_{w^{\prime}}
, so
(2)
is proved.

If
w^{\prime}=s_{\alpha}w
, then one can also write
w^{\prime}=ww^{-1}s_{\alpha}w=ws_{w^{-1}(\alpha)}
. Since
\alpha\in\Phi_{w}
if and only if
w^{-1}(\alpha)\in\Phi_{w}
, and similarly for
\Phi_{w}^{\prime}
, the problem reduces to the previous case.
∎

Let
R\coloneqq\left\{s_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in\Phi\right\}
be the set of reflections of
W
, which generates
W
as a group. We can then view any element
w\in W
as a word on the alphabet
R
(in a non-unique way); we call
length of
w
on
R
the minimal length of such a word, and
reduced decomposition of
w
on
R
any such word of minimal length. The following corollary to Proposition
2.1.4
interprets minimal generating subsystems using reduced decompositions on
R
.

Corollary 2.1.5
.

Let
w\in W
.

(1)

The number
d_{w}
is the length of
w
on the alphabet
R
.

(2)

The subsystem
\Phi_{w}
is the set of roots
\alpha\in\Phi
such that
s_{\alpha}
appears in a reduced decomposition of
w
on the alphabet
R
.

(3)

The subsystem
\Phi_{w}
spans
E_{w}
as a vector space.

Proof.

If
w=1
, then all claims are true because
E_{w}=\im(\id_{E}-\id_{E})=\{0\}
. We now suppose that
w\neq 1
.

Let
w=s_{\alpha_{r}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}
be a decomposition of
w
on
R
, with
\alpha_{i}\in\Phi
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. By Proposition
2.1.4
, we know that
d_{s_{\alpha_{i}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}}\leq d_{s_{\alpha_{i-1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}}+1
for all
1\leq i\leq d
, with equality if and only if
E_{s_{\alpha_{i}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}}=\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{i}\oplus E_{s_{\alpha_{i-1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}}
. An induction on
i
gives
d_{w}=d_{s_{\alpha_{r}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}}\leq r
, with equality if and only if

(2.3)

E_{w}=\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{r}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{1}\,.

Therefore the three claims will be proved if we can show that
\Phi_{w}
is non-empty and that for any
\alpha\in\Phi_{w}
, there is a decomposition of
w
on
R
of length
d_{w}
containing
s_{\alpha}
.

The non-emptiness of
\Phi_{w}
is a consequence of Proposition
2.1.2
. Now take
\alpha\in\Phi_{w}
. The wanted decomposition can be obtained by induction on
d_{w}
: Proposition
2.1.4
implies that
d_{s_{\alpha}w}=d_{w}-1
, and if
s_{\alpha}w=s_{\alpha_{r}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}
is a decomposition of length
r\coloneqq d_{s_{\alpha}w}
, then
w=s_{\alpha}s_{\alpha_{r}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}
is a decomposition of length
d_{w}
.
∎

The following corollary explains the terminology of minimal generating subsystem introduced in Definition
2.1.1
.

Corollary 2.1.6
.

Let
w\in W
and let
E^{\prime}
be a subspace of
E
. Let
\Phi^{\prime}\coloneqq\Phi\cap E^{\prime}
, which is a root system in
E^{\prime}
with Weyl group
W(\Phi^{\prime})\subseteq W
. Then
w\in W(\Phi^{\prime})
if and only if
\Phi_{w}\subseteq\Phi^{\prime}
, if and only if
E_{w}\subseteq E^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The implication
E_{w}\subseteq E^{\prime}\implies\Phi_{w}\subseteq\Phi^{\prime}
is a mere consequence of the definitions, and the implication
\Phi_{w}\subseteq\Phi^{\prime}\implies w\in W(\Phi^{\prime})
is Proposition
2.1.2
.

Now suppose that
w\in W(\Phi^{\prime})
. Let
\Phi^{\prime}_{w}
be the minimal generating subsystem of
w
in
\Phi^{\prime}
and let
E^{\prime}_{w}\coloneqq\im(w|_{E^{\prime}}-\id_{E^{\prime}})\subseteq E^{\prime}\cap E_{w}
. From Corollary
2.1.5
(1)
applied to
\Phi^{\prime}
,
w
has a reduced decomposition on
R^{\prime}\coloneqq\left\{s_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in\Phi^{\prime}\right\}
of length
\dim{E^{\prime}_{w}}
. Since
R^{\prime}\subseteq R
, the same result applied to
\Phi
shows that
\dim{E^{\prime}_{w}}\geq\dim{E_{w}}
. Therefore, the chain of inclusions
E^{\prime}_{w}\subseteq E^{\prime}\cap E_{w}\subseteq E_{w}
is a chain of equalities. In particular,
E^{\prime}\cap E_{w}=E_{w}
; or equivalently
E_{w}\subseteq E^{\prime}
.
∎

Fix
I\subset\Phi
a basis of simple roots of
\Phi
, and write
S\coloneqq\left\{s_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in I\right\}\subseteq W
for the set of simple reflections corresponding to the simple roots. These define a length function
\lg
on
W
. The following result is a generalisation of
[
6
, Lemma 2.7]
where it was proved for root systems
\Phi
of type
A
.

Corollary 2.1.7
.

Let
w\in W
. Then

d_{w}\leq\lg(w)

with equality if and only if
w
is a product of distinct simple reflections.

Proof.

Let
w=s_{\alpha_{\lg(w)}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}
be a reduced decomposition of
w
on
S
, with
\alpha_{i}\in I
for all
1\leq i\leq\lg(w)
. Corollary
2.1.5
(1)
and its proof give
d_{w}\leq\lg(w)
, with equality if and only if
E_{w}=\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{\lg(w)}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{1}
(see (
2.3
)). Therefore, in the equality case, the
\alpha_{i}
must be distinct.

Conversely, suppose that
w
is a product of distinct simple reflections. Write
w=s_{\alpha_{r}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}
with
\alpha_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
being distinct elements of
I
; then
r\geq\lg(w)
by definition. Also, since the elements of
I
are linearly independent, the sum
\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{r}+\ldots+\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{1}
is direct. The proof of Corollary
2.1.5
implies that
r=d_{w}
; hence
d_{w}\geq\lg(w)
, so we have equality.
∎

2.2.
Interpretation for a split reductive group

For
\Phi
a root system and
(G,T)
a connected split reductive group giving rise to
\Phi
, we give an interpretation of the minimal generating subsystem
\Phi_{w}\subseteq\Phi
for
w\in W(\Phi)
in terms of Levi groups. We use the notation of the introduction.

We start by recalling a classical lemma.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
be an integer and
M
be a
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{N}]
-module equipped with a linear action of the group
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
. Then
H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},M)=0
.

Proof.

The
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{N}]
-module
H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},M)
is killed by
N
(see
[
22
, §VII.7, Proposition 6]
), hence it must be
0
.
∎

Proposition 2.2.2
.

For
w\in W
, the centraliser
\left(C_{G}((T^{w})^{\circ}),T\right)
is a connected split reductive group whose root system is the minimal generating subsystem
\Phi_{w}
of
w
.

Proof.

(T^{w})^{\circ}
is a subtorus of
T
(see the discussion in
[
20
, Notation 12.29]
; note that
T^{w}
is always reduced in characteristic
0
), hence
C_{G}((T^{w})^{\circ})
is connected and split reductive by
[
20
, Corollary 17.59]
. Note that it always contains
T
, so
\left(C_{G}((T^{w})^{\circ}),T\right)
is split reductive.

Let
\alpha\in\Phi
be a root of
(G,T)
. The eigenspace
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
has dimension
1
, so
\alpha
is a root of
\left(C_{G}((T^{w})^{\circ}),T\right)
if and only if
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is in the Lie group of
C_{G}((T^{w})^{\circ})
. By
[
20
, Thm. 21.11(c)]
, we thus have

\Phi^{\prime}\coloneqq\Phi\left(C_{G}((T^{w})^{\circ}),T\right)=\left\{\alpha\in\Phi\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha|_{(T^{w})^{\circ}}=1\right\}\,.

Let
N
be the order of
w
in
W
. The morphism of algebraic varieties
T\longrightarrow T,\,t\longmapsto\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\Ad(w^{-k})t
has a connected image (because
T
is connected) included in
T^{w}
. Hence,
\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\Ad(w^{-k})t\in(T^{w})^{\circ}
for all
t\in T
. Then, for
\alpha\in\Phi^{\prime}
and
t\in T
, we have

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w^{k}\alpha\right)(t)=\alpha\left(\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\Ad(w^{-k})t\right)=1

hence
(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w^{k})\alpha=0
. Conversely, if
\alpha\in\ker(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w^{k})
, then for all
t\in(T^{w})^{\circ}
,

\alpha(t^{N})=\alpha\left(\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\Ad(w^{-k})t\right)=1

so, since the group of characters on the torus
(T^{w})^{\circ}
has no torsion, we necessarily have
\alpha(t)=1
. Hence
\alpha|_{(T^{w})^{\circ}}=1
. Therefore
\Phi^{\prime}=\Phi\cap\ker(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}w^{k})
. Since
H^{1}(\langle w\rangle,E)=0
by Lemma
2.2
, we have
\Phi^{\prime}=\Phi\cap\im(w-\id_{E})=\Phi_{w}
.
∎

Corollary 2.2.3
.

Let
w\in W
. Then
w\in W(\Phi_{w})
.

Proof.

Seeing
w
as an element of
N_{G}(T)/T
, we have
w\in C_{G}((T^{w})^{\circ})/T
, so that actually
w\in N_{C_{G}((T^{w})^{\circ})}(T)/T=W(C_{G}((T^{w})^{\circ}),T)=W(\Phi_{w})
where the last equality comes from Proposition
2.2.2
.
∎

2.3.
Pairs of elements in the Weyl group

Fix a basis of simple roots
I\subset\Phi
and write
\Phi_{+}
for the subset of positive roots that it generates. Write
S\coloneqq\left\{s_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in I\right\}\subseteq W
for the set of simple reflections corresponding to the simple roots. They define a length function
\lg
and a Bruhat order
\preceq
on
W
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
(w_{1},w_{2})\in W^{2}
such that
w_{1}\preceq w_{2}
. Let
\Phi_{w_{1}w_{2}^{-1}}\subseteq\Phi
be the minimal generating subsystem of
w_{1}w_{2}^{-1}
. We say that
(w_{1},w_{2})
is a
good pair
if there exists a sequence
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
of roots in
\Phi_{w_{1}w_{2}^{-1}}
such that
w_{2}=s_{\alpha_{r}}s_{\alpha_{r-1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}w_{1}
and that for each
1\leq i\leq r
, the relation
s_{\alpha_{i}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}w_{1}\succ s_{\alpha_{i-1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}}w_{1}
holds. If such a sequence does not exist, we say that
(w_{1},w_{2})
is a
bad pair
.

Remark 2.3.2
.

The set of reflections
R=\left\{s_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in\Phi\right\}
of
W
can be rewritten as
R=\bigcup_{w\in W}wSw^{-1}
. On the other hand, using
(2)
of Corollary
2.1.5
, one sees that the subset
\left\{s_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in\Phi_{w_{1}w_{2}^{-1}}\right\}
of
R
is the set of reflections that appear in some reduced decomposition of
w
on the alphabet
R
. Hence the notion of good pair is intrinsic to the Coxeter system
(W,S)
.

Let
w_{0}\in W
be the maximal element for the Bruhat order. Using that
w\mapsto ww_{0}
and
w\mapsto w_{0}w
are antiautomorphisms of
W
for the Bruhat order, one easily proves the following result.

Proposition 2.3.3
.

Let
(w_{1},w_{2})\in W^{2}
. Then
(w_{1},w_{2})
is a good pair (resp. bad pair) if and only if
(w_{2}w_{0},w_{1}w_{0})
is a good pair (resp. bad pair). Similarly,
(w_{1},w_{2})
is a good pair (resp. bad pair) if and only if
(w_{0}w_{2},w_{0}w_{1})
is a good pair (resp. bad pair).

Proposition 2.3.4
.

Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
with
w_{1}\preceq w_{2}
. Then

d_{w_{2}w_{1}^{-1}}\leq\lg(w_{2})-\lg(w_{1})

Let

The following proposition provides a direct way of seeing if a given pair is good.

Proposition 2.3.7
.

Let
(w_{1},w_{2})\in W^{2}
. Let

be the minimal generating subsystem of

and

and

Take

\Phi_{w_{1}w_{2}^{-1}}=u(\Phi_{J})
. Let

be the representative of
uW_{J}

and

are the same. Let

be the representative for that coset; we then have elements

and

of

such that
w_{1}^{-1}u^{J}=v^{J}w_{J,1}^{-1}
and
w_{2}^{-1}u^{J}=v^{J}w_{J,2}^{-1}
,

w_{1}=u^{J}w_{J,1}(v^{J})^{-1}
,
w_{2}=u^{J}w_{J,2}(v^{J})^{-1}
.

Now assume that
w_{J,1}\preceq w_{J,2}

(see
[
4
, Thm. 2.2.6]
) there is a sequence
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
of roots in

and for each

The inclusion

\bigcup_{1\leq k\leq r}\left\{e_{i}-e_{j}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}i,j\in\Omega_{k}\;,\;i\neq j\right\}=\Phi(C_{\GL_{n}}((T^{w})^{\circ}))=\Phi_{w}

3.
A conjecture on a Schubert-like decomposition

We recall Conjecture 2.3.7 of
[
8
]
, whose study is the main topic of this paper.

3.1.
A scheme related to the Springer resolution

We recall the construction of a reduced scheme
X
associated to the split reductive group
G
and equipped with a stratification similar to the Schubert decomposition.

First recall the Schubert decomposition on the flag variety
G/B
. Let
w\in W
and
\dot{w}\in N_{G}(T)(k)
be any closed point lifting
w
. The left-action of
B
on
G
by multiplication induces an action on
G/B
, write
BwB/B
for the orbit of
\dot{w}B/B
under this action. Then
BwB/B
is a smooth locally closed subvariety of
G/B
called a
Schubert cell
, and we have the decomposition

(3.1)

G/B=\coprod_{w\in W}BwB/B

(see
[
20
, Thm. 21.73]
). The cells
BwB/B
are stable under the left action of
B
, thus their Zariski closure
\overline{BwB/B}
too, and this action is transitive on
BwB/B
. Thus, a closed cell
\overline{BwB/B}
intersects a cell
Bw^{\prime}B/B
if and only if
Bw^{\prime}B/B\subseteq\overline{BwB/B}
, which happens if and only if
w^{\prime}\preceq w
for the Bruhat order (see
[
1
, Thm. 2.11]
). Therefore (
3.1
) gives a good stratification of
G/B
, and we have a more general decomposition into locally closed strata for all
w\in W

\overline{BwB/B}=\coprod_{w^{\prime}\preceq w}Bw^{\prime}B/B\,.

One can get a similar stratification on
G/B\times G/B
: consider the isomorphism

(3.2)

f\colon\begin{array}[]{ccc}G\times^{B}G/B&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}&G/B\times G/B\\
(g_{1},g_{2}B)&\longmapsto&(g_{1}B,g_{1}g_{2}B)\end{array}

where
G\times^{B}G/B
is the quotient of
G\times G/B
by the right
B
-action defined by
(g_{1},g_{2}B)b\coloneqq(g_{1}b,b^{-1}g_{2}B)
(see
[
23
, §3.7]
). For
w\in W
, the right
B
-action leaves
G\times(BwB/B)
invariant, so we can write

U_{w}\coloneqq f(G\times^{B}(BwB/B))\subseteq G/B\times G/B\,.

Equivalently,
U_{w}
is the orbit of
(B/B,\dot{w}B/B)
under the left
G
-action on
G/B\times G/B
given by diagonal left multiplication. Then
G/B\times G/B=\coprod_{w\in W}U_{w}
and for any
w\in W
, the decomposition into locally closed strata

\overline{U_{w}}=\coprod_{w^{\prime}\preceq w}U_{w^{\prime}}

is a consequence of the analogous one in
G/B
.

As in
[
8
]
, let
X
be the following algebraic variety:

X\coloneqq\left\{(g_{1}B,g_{2}B,\psi)\in G/B\times G/B\times\mathfrak{g}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\Ad(g_{1}^{-1})\psi\in\mathfrak{b}\,,\,\Ad(g_{2}^{-1})\psi\in\mathfrak{b}\right\}\,.

Let us write
\pi
for the composition
X\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow G/B\times G/B\times\mathfrak{b}\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow G/B\times G/B
. For
w\in W
, define

V_{w}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(U_{w})\subset X\,,\quad X_{w}\coloneqq\overline{V_{w}}\,.

Note that
V_{w}
is open in
X_{w}
but not in
X
, since it is only locally closed. We also define two morphisms

\kappa_{1}\colon\;\begin{aligned} X\;&\longrightarrow\;\mathfrak{t}\\
(g_{1}B,g_{2}B,\psi)\;&\longmapsto\;\overline{\Ad(g_{1}^{-1})\psi}\end{aligned}\,,\quad\kappa_{2}\colon\;\begin{aligned} X\;&\longrightarrow\;\mathfrak{t}\\
(g_{1}B,g_{2}B,\psi)\;&\longmapsto\;\overline{\Ad(g_{2}^{-1})\psi}\end{aligned}

where
\overline{\Ad(g_{1}^{-1})\psi}
(resp.
\overline{\Ad(g_{2}^{-1})\psi}
) denotes the image of
\Ad(g_{1}^{-1})\psi
(resp.
\Ad(g_{2}^{-1})\psi
) under the projection
\mathfrak{b}\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{u}=\mathfrak{t}
.

We have the left action of
G
on
G/B\times G/B
by diagonal left multiplication, as well as the action of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
by adjunction. This gives a diagonal action on
G/B\times G/B\times\mathfrak{g}
by
g\cdot(g_{1}B,g_{2}B,\psi)=(gg_{1}B,gg_{2}B,\Ad(g)\psi)
. For this action,
X
is stable and
\pi\colon X\longrightarrow G/B\times G/B
is
G
-equivariant. It therefore induces an action on
V_{w}
for any given
w\in W
.

The cell decompositions
\overline{U_{w}}=\coprod_{w^{\prime}\preceq w}U_{w^{\prime}}
for
w\in W
imply that
X_{w}\cap V_{w^{\prime}}=\emptyset
unless
w^{\prime}\preceq w
, and that (set theoretically)

X_{w}=\coprod_{w^{\prime}\preceq w}X_{w}\cap V_{w^{\prime}}\,.

3.2.
Statement of the conjecture

Conjecture 3.2.1

(Conjecture 2.3.7 of
[
8
]
)
.

Let

such that

. Then

as reduced closed subschemes of

.

A consequence of this conjecture is the following proposition, that is mentioned in

at least when

is the element of maximal length in

Proposition 3.2.2
.

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in W
and
x=(\pi(x),0)\in G/B\times G/B\times\mathfrak{b}
be a closed point of

. Let

(resp.

) be the tangent space of

at

(resp. of
\overline{U_{w}}
at

of
X_{w}
at
x
(resp. the residue field
k(\pi(x))
of
\overline{U_{w}}
at
\pi(x)
). If Conjecture
3.2.1
is true for

, then

(3.3)

\dim_{k(x)}T_{X_{w},x}=\dim_{k(\pi(x))}T_{\overline{U_{w}},\pi(x)}+\dim_{k(x)}\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}(k(x))+\length(w^{\prime}w_{0})\,.

Proof.

Since the action of
G
on

is transitive and
\pi
is
G
-equivariant, we can assume
\pi(x)=(B,w^{\prime}B)
(in particular the residue field

is
k
). Consider the composition
X_{w}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow X\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow G/B\times G/B\times\mathfrak{g}
; it factors through a closed immersion
\iota\coloneqq X_{w}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\overline{U_{w}}\times\mathfrak{g}
. Because
\pi(x)=(B,w^{\prime}B)
, the proof of
[

shows that the differential
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{x}\iota\colon T_{X_{w},x}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow T_{\overline{U_{w}},\pi(x)}\oplus T_{\mathfrak{g},0}=T_{\overline{U_{w}},\pi(x)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}(k)
factors through an injection of

(3.4)

\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{x}\iota\colon T_{X_{w},x}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow T_{\overline{U_{w}},\pi(x)}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}(k)\oplus(\mathfrak{u}(k)\cap\Ad(w^{\prime})\mathfrak{u}(k))\,.

Assuming Conjecture
3.2.1
, we wish to prove the surjectivity of this map; comparing dimensions would then give the desired equality (

).

Consider the closed immersion

given by

. Its restriction to

factors through a closed immersion

is the closed immersion

), the first summand of the right-hand side is contained in the image of

.

Now, we know from
[
8

that

is a geometric vector bundle, and the fiber of
\pi(x)
in

is

(3.5)

\pi^{-1}(\pi(x))=(B,w^{\prime}B)\times(\mathfrak{t}\oplus(\mathfrak{u}\cap\Ad(w^{\prime})\mathfrak{u}))\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow U_{w^{\prime}}\times\mathfrak{g}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow G/B\times G/B\times\mathfrak{g}\,.

Take
Y\coloneqq(B,w^{\prime}B)\times(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}\oplus(\mathfrak{u}\cap\Ad(w^{\prime})\mathfrak{u}))

and

Conjecture
3.2.1
of
[
8
]
gives a closed immersion
V_{w^{\prime}}\cap{\kappa_{1}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}})\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow X_{w}

\iota_{2}\coloneqq Y\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow X_{w}

. Therefore, by definition of

).

We now formulate a conjecture equivalent to Conjecture

using a slightly simpler scheme than

. Recall from

(g,\psi)\;&\longmapsto\;\Ad(g)\psi\end{aligned}

where
G\times^{B}\mathfrak{b}
is the quotient of
G\times\mathfrak{b}
by the right
B
-action given by

. There is also a map
\widetilde{\pi}\colon G\times^{B}\mathfrak{b}\longrightarrow G/B

.

Define, for

:

\widetilde{X}\coloneqq q^{-1}(\mathfrak{b})\,,\quad\widetilde{V}_{w}\coloneqq\widetilde{X}\cap{\widetilde{\pi}}^{-1}(BwB/B)\,,\quad\widetilde{X}_{w}\coloneqq\overline{\widetilde{V}_{w}}\,.

As for

, we have the decomposition

for any

Conjecture 3.2.3

(Breuil-Hellmann-Schraen)
.

Let

, then the locally closed immersion
\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\widetilde{X}
factors into a locally closed immersion

\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\widetilde{X}_{w}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\widetilde{X}\,.

Proposition 3.2.4
.

Conjecture

and Conjecture

are equivalent.

Proof.

We have an isomorphism

(3.7)

G\times^{B}\widetilde{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}X,\quad(g_{1},(g_{2},\psi))\longmapsto(g_{1}B,g_{1}g_{2}B,\Ad(g_{1}g_{2})\psi)

where

by

(on the right) and with the composition of

with

and identifies

3.3.
Application to tangent spaces on the trianguline variety

We recall the setting in which the variety
X
was originally introduced by Breuil-Hellmann-Schraen
[
8
]
who were motivated by the study of the
trianguline variety
from
p
-adic Hodge theory.

Let
p
be a prime number and
K,L
be finite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that
\Sigma\coloneqq\Hom_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(K,L)
has
[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
elements. Let
K_{0}
be the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
inside
K
. We write
\mathcal{G}_{K}
for the absolute Galois group of
K
and
k_{L}
for the residual field of
L
. We fix an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and a continuous representation
\bar{r}\colon\mathcal{G}_{K}\longrightarrow\GL_{n}(k_{L})
. We set
G
to be the split reductive group
G\coloneqq\left(\Res_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}G^{\prime}_{K}\right)\times_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}L\simeq\prod_{\tau\in\Sigma}G^{\prime}_{L}
over
L
,
T\coloneqq\prod_{\tau\in\Sigma}T^{\prime}_{L}
and
B\coloneqq\prod_{\tau\in\Sigma}B^{\prime}_{L}
, where
G^{\prime}
is the split reductive group
\GL_{n,\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
T^{\prime}
is the maximal torus in
G^{\prime}
of diagonal matrices and
B^{\prime}
is the Borel subgroup of
G^{\prime}
of upper-triangular matrices. Note that the Weyl group of
G
is
(\mathcal{S}_{n})^{\Sigma}
.

Let
R_{\bar{r}}^{\square}
be the framed deformation ring of
\bar{r}
constructed by Mazur
[
19
, §1.2]
and Kisin
[
16
]
. Taking its formal spectrum
\Spf(R_{\bar{r}}^{\square})
and then applying Berthelot’s rigidification functor
[
2
, §0.2]
gives a rigid analytic space
\mathfrak{X}^{\square}_{\bar{r}}\coloneqq\Spf(R_{\bar{r}}^{\square})^{\mathrm{rig}}
over
L
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{L}\coloneqq\widehat{K^{\times}}\times_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}L
be the rigid analytic space over
L
parametrising the continuous characters of
K^{\times}
. For any
\mathbf{k}=(k_{\tau})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma}
, we write
z^{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathcal{T}_{L}(L)
for the character
z\longmapsto\prod_{\tau\in\Sigma}\tau(z)^{k_{\tau}}
, and
\lvert z\rvert_{K}\in\mathcal{T}_{L}(L)
for the
p
-adic absolute value normalised by
\lvert p\rvert_{K}=p^{-[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}
. A character
\underline{\delta}=(\delta_{i})\in\mathcal{T}_{L}^{n}
is called regular if, for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, the character
\delta_{i}\delta_{j}^{-1}
is not of the form
z^{-\mathbf{k}}
or
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(z)z^{\mathbf{k}}\lvert z\rvert_{K}
for some
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\Sigma}
, where
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is the norm map. We write
\mathcal{T}^{n}_{\mathrm{reg}}\subset\mathcal{T}_{L}^{n}
for the subset of regular characters, which is Zariski-open.

Definition 3.3.1
.

Let
U_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
be the set of points
x=(r,\underline{\delta})\in\mathfrak{X}^{\square}_{\bar{r}}\times\mathcal{T}^{n}_{\mathrm{reg}}
such that the representation
r\colon\mathcal{G}_{K}\longrightarrow\GL_{n}(k(x))
is trianguline in the sense of Colmez with parameter
\underline{\delta}\colon(K^{\times})^{n}\longrightarrow k(x)^{\times}
(see
e.g.
[
8
, Definition 3.3.8]
). The
trianguline variety

X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
is defined as the Zariski-closure of
U_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
in
\mathfrak{X}^{\square}_{\bar{r}}\times\mathcal{T}_{L}^{n}
.

The geometry of
U_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
is well understood:
U_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
is a Zariski-dense and Zariski-open subset of
X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
which is smooth of dimension
n^{2}+[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
; see
[
7
, Thm. 2.6]
. In particular,
X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
is also equidimensional of dimension
n^{2}+[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
.

Now let
x=(r,\underline{\delta})\in X_{\mathrm{tri}}^{\square}(\bar{r})
be a crystalline point,
i.e.
a point such that
r
is a crystalline representation in the sense of Fontaine
[
9

4.1.
Preliminaries: Levi subgroups and the adjoint action

We establish a few technical lemmas about Levi subgroups and the derived adjoint action, which are used in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
J\subseteq I
be a subset of simple roots of
G
, write
\Phi_{J}\subseteq\Phi
for the root subsystem generated by
J
and denote by
T_{J}\coloneqq\left(\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi_{J}}(\ker\alpha)\right)^{\circ}\subseteq T
the largest subtorus of
T
such that
\alpha(T_{J})=1
for all
\alpha\in\Phi_{J}
(or equivalently
\alpha\in J
). It is indeed a torus, as it is a reduced (the characteristic is
0
) and connected diagonalisable group; see the discussion in
[
20
, Notation 12.29]
.

Let
L\coloneqq C_{G}(T_{J})
be the standard Levi subgroup of
G
associated to
J
. According to
[
20
, Proposition 21.90]
,
L
is a connected reductive group with
T
as a maximal torus and root system
\Phi_{J}
. Therefore
L=\left\langle T,U_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in\Phi_{J}\right\rangle
,
i.e.
L
is the subgroup of
G
generated by
T
and the
U_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Phi_{J}
. Write
U_{L}\coloneqq\left\langle U_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in\Phi_{J}\cap\Phi_{+}\right\rangle
for the unipotent radical of
L\cap B
and
\mathfrak{t}_{J}
,
\mathfrak{l}
,
\mathfrak{u}_{L}
for the Lie algebras of
T_{J}
,
L
,
U_{L}
respectively.

For a root
\alpha\colon T\longrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
, consider its differential
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{e}\alpha\colon\mathfrak{t}\longrightarrow k
; we can see it as
\mathrm{Lie}(\alpha)
, where
\mathrm{Lie}
is the Lie algebra functor from algebraic groups over
k
to Lie algebras over
k
. Since
\mathrm{Lie}
commutes with finite limits, and in particular with kernels and fibred products (see
[
20
, 10.14]
), we have

\mathfrak{t}_{J}=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi_{J}}(\ker\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{e}\alpha)\,.

Proposition 4.1.1
.

There is a Zariski-dense open subscheme
\mathfrak{t}_{J}^{\mathrm{gen}}
of the affine scheme
\mathfrak{t}_{J}
such that, for any closed point
t\in\mathfrak{t}_{J}^{\mathrm{gen}}
:

(1)

L=C_{G}(t)^{\circ}\coloneqq\left\{g\in G\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\Ad(g)t=t\right\}^{\circ}
;

(2)

\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(t)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}[x,t]=0\right\}
.

Proof.

For any closed point
t\in\mathfrak{t}
, the centraliser
C_{G}(t)
contains
T
and is normalised by
T
, therefore the same is true of the identity component
C_{G}(t)^{\circ}
since
T
is connected. By
[
20
, 21.66]
, we then have

(4.2)

C_{G}(t)^{\circ}=\left\langle T,U_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in\Phi,\,U_{\alpha}\subseteq C_{G}(t)^{\circ}\right\rangle=\left\langle T,U_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in\Phi,\,U_{\alpha}\subseteq C_{G}(t)\right\rangle

since each
U_{\alpha}
is connected.

Let
\alpha\in\Phi
be a root and
t\in\mathfrak{t}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
be a
{k^{\mathrm{a}}}
-point such that
U_{\alpha}\subseteq C_{G}(t)
. Then the morphism

\phi\colon U_{\alpha}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\,,\quad u\longmapsto\Ad(u)t

is constant, so its differential

\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{e}\colon\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\,,\quad u_{\alpha}\longmapsto\ad(u_{\alpha})t

is zero. But for any
u_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
, we have
\ad(u_{\alpha})t=[u_{\alpha},t]=-\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{e}\alpha(t)u_{\alpha}
. This shows that
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{e}\alpha(t)=0
and hence
t\in\ker\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{e}\alpha
.

Not let
\alpha\in\Phi\setminus\Phi_{J}
. Since
\Phi_{J}
is the root system of
L
, necessarily
U_{\alpha}\not\subseteq L=C_{G}(T_{J})
. Therefore there exist
u_{\alpha}\in U_{\alpha}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
and
t\in T_{J}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
such that
u_{\alpha}t\neq tu_{\alpha}
, which means
u_{\alpha}\neq tu_{\alpha}t^{-1}
. This implies
\alpha(t)\neq 1
(see
[
20
, 21.19]
). Hence
\ker(\alpha)\cap T_{J}\neq T_{J}
. Applying the
\mathrm{Lie}
functor (which preserves finite limits), we get
\ker(\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{e}\alpha)\cap\mathfrak{t}_{J}\neq\mathfrak{t}_{J}
, so
\mathfrak{t}_{J}\setminus(\ker(\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{e}\alpha)\cap\mathfrak{t}_{J})
is non-empty hence dense open in
\mathfrak{t}_{J}
. We thus define

\mathfrak{t}_{J}^{\mathrm{gen}}\coloneqq\mathfrak{t}_{J}\setminus\bigg(\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Phi\setminus\Phi_{J}}\ker(\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{e}\alpha)\cap\mathfrak{t}_{J}\bigg)=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi\setminus\Phi_{J}}\left(\mathfrak{t}_{J}\setminus\left(\ker(\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}_{e}\alpha)\cap\mathfrak{t}_{J}\right)\right)

which is dense open in
\mathfrak{t}_{J}
.

Now, fix a closed point
t\in\mathfrak{t}_{J}^{\mathrm{gen}}
. For any root
\alpha\in\Phi
, the previous discussion shows that
U_{\alpha}\subseteq C_{G}(t)
implies
\alpha\in\Phi_{J}
. By (
4.2
), we have

C_{G}(t)\subseteq\left\langle T,U_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in\Phi_{J}\right\rangle=L\,.

The converse
L=C_{G}(T_{J})\subseteq C_{G}(t)
is true for any
t\in\mathfrak{t}_{J}
, so
(1)
is proved.

Statement
(2)
is a consequence of
(1)
and
[
20
, Proposition 17.76]
.
∎

The following lemma explains the relevance of Proposition

.

Lemma 4.1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}
be a Zariski-dense subscheme. Then the algebraic variety (over

)

\widetilde{V}(w^{\prime},\mathfrak{t}^{\prime})\coloneqq\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{u})

is a dense subvariety of

\widetilde{V}(w^{\prime},\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}})\coloneqq\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})\,.

Proof.

Recall the morphism
\widetilde{\pi}\colon G\times^{B}\mathfrak{b}\longrightarrow G/B
, which restricts to two morphisms

\widetilde{\pi}_{1}\colon q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})\longrightarrow G/B\ ,\quad\widetilde{\pi}_{1}^{\prime}\colon q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{u})\longrightarrow G/B\,.

Then
\widetilde{V}(w^{\prime},\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}})
(resp.
\widetilde{V}(w^{\prime},\mathfrak{t}^{\prime})
) is just the fibre of
\widetilde{\pi}_{1}
(resp.
\widetilde{\pi}_{1}^{\prime}
) above
Bw^{\prime}B/B
. It is thus enough to check density on fibres above closed points of
Bw^{\prime}B/B
: let
gB\in Bw^{\prime}B/B\subset G/B
with
g\in G
, we want to show that
\widetilde{\pi}_{1}^{\prime-1}(gB)
is dense in
\widetilde{\pi}_{1}^{-1}(gB)
. Now the left-action of
B
on
G\times\mathfrak{b}
given by
b(g,\psi)=(bg,\psi)
induces a left-action on
G\times^{B}\mathfrak{b}
. This action stabilises
q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})
and
q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{u})
because
\Ad(b)
stabilises
t+\mathfrak{u}
for any
b\in B({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
and
t\in\mathfrak{t}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
. Moreover, this action induces an action on the set of fibres of
\widetilde{\pi}_{1}
which is continuous in the sense that, for any

and

, we have an isomorphism of algebraic varieties
\widetilde{\pi}_{1}^{-1}(gB)\longrightarrow\widetilde{\pi}_{1}^{-1}(bgB),\,x\longmapsto bx
; and similarly for
\widetilde{\pi}_{1}^{\prime}
. Also, this action on the fibres is obviously transitive on the subset of fibres at points of

.

Therefore it suffices to consider the fibre at

: we want to check that
\widetilde{\pi}_{1}^{\prime-1}(ww^{\prime}B)
is dense in
\widetilde{\pi}_{1}^{-1}(w^{\prime}B)
. Combining the formula (
3.5
) from
[
8

with the isomorphism (
3.7

\widetilde{\pi}_{1}^{\prime-1}(ww^{\prime}B)=\left\{(w^{\prime},\psi)\in G\times^{B}\mathfrak{b}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\psi\in\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}\oplus(\mathfrak{u}\cap\Ad(w^{\prime})\mathfrak{u})\right\}

and

\widetilde{\pi}_{1}^{-1}(ww^{\prime}B)=\left\{(w^{\prime},\psi)\in G\times^{B}\mathfrak{b}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\psi\in\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}\oplus(\mathfrak{u}\cap\Ad(w^{\prime})\mathfrak{u})\right\}\,,

from which we easily deduce the desired density statement.
∎

We introduce other objects associated to
J
. Let
P\coloneqq P_{J}=\left\langle U_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in\Phi_{J}\cup\Phi_{+}\right\rangle
be the parabolic subgroup associated to

and
U_{P}\coloneqq\left\langle U_{\alpha}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in\Phi_{+}\setminus\Phi_{J}\right\rangle
be the unipotent radical of

. Write
W_{J}\coloneqq W(\Phi_{J})\subseteq W
for the Weyl group of
\Phi_{J}
; therefore
W_{J}=N_{L}(T)/T
is also the Weyl group of
L
. Write
W^{J}\subset W
for the set of

such that
ws_{\alpha}\succ w
for all
\alpha\in\Phi_{J}
,
i.e.
w(\alpha)\in\Phi_{+}
for all
\alpha\in\Phi_{J}\cap\Phi_{+}
. The reductive group

is the Levi factor of
P
, and we have the decomposition

(see
[
20
, Thm. 21.91]
). Also recall from §

.

Lemma 4.1.3
.

Let
l\in L
and
w^{J}\in W^{J}
be such that
w^{J}l(w^{J})^{-1}\in B
. Then
l\in L\cap B
.

Proof.

From the Bruhat decomposition in
L
, let
w_{J}\in W_{J}
be such that
l\in(L\cap B)w_{J}(L\cap B)
. The hypothesis
w^{J}\in W^{J}
can be reformulated as
w^{J}(L\cap B)(w^{J})^{-1}\subseteq B
. Thus we have
w^{J}l(w^{J})^{-1}\in Bw^{J}w_{J}(w^{J})^{-1}B
. The Bruhat decomposition in

and our hypothesis then give
w^{J}w_{J}(w^{J})^{-1}=1
,
i.e.
w_{J}=1
, hence
l\in B
.
∎

Lemma 4.1.4
.

Let
t\in\mathfrak{t}_{J}^{\mathrm{gen}}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
be a
{k^{\mathrm{a}}}
-point. Let
w^{J}\in W^{J}
,
u_{P}\in U_{P}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
,

and

be such that
\Ad(w^{J}u_{P}l)(t+x)\in\mathfrak{b}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
. Then
\Ad(l)x\in\mathfrak{u}_{L}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
.

Proof.

Since
U_{P}
is normal in
P
, the action of
L
on
P
by conjugation (resp. on the Lie algebra of
P
by adjunction) keeps
U_{P}
(resp.
\mathfrak{u}_{P}
) stable. Also, the action of

by conjugation on

induces a trivial action on

, hence on

as well. Therefore, setting
u_{P}^{\prime}\coloneqq l^{-1}u_{P}l\in U_{P}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
, we have using

:

y\coloneqq\Ad(u_{P}^{\prime})(t+x)-(t+x)\in\mathfrak{u}_{P}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})\,.

Hence, setting

, we have using
\Ad(l)t=t
:

\Ad(w^{J}u_{P}l)(t+x)=\Ad(w^{J}lu_{P}^{\prime})(t+x)=\Ad(w^{J})(t+\Ad(l)x+z)

which is in
\mathfrak{b}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})=\mathfrak{t}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})

Now we have
\Ad(l)x\in\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_{J}}\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
and

\Ad(w^{J})\Ad(l)x

have disjoint components in the sum

for

, so

\Ad(w^{J})\Ad(l)x\in\mathfrak{u}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})\,.

However,
w^{J}\in W^{J}
sends negative roots in
\Phi_{J}
to negative roots in
\Phi
. Because
\Ad(l)x\in\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_{J}}\mathfrak{u}_{\alpha}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
, this necessarily implies

.
∎

Lemma 4.1.5
.

With the setting of Lemma

w^{J}u_{P}(w^{J})^{-1}\in U({k^{\mathrm{a}}})

Proof.

We know that
\Ad(l)x\in\mathfrak{u}_{L}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})
and that
\Ad(l)t=t
, so replacing

\Ad(w^{J}u_{P})(t+x)\in\mathfrak{b}({k^{\mathrm{a}}})

Partition
\Phi_{+}\setminus(\Phi_{J}\cap\Phi_{+})
into the two sets

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}\setminus(\Phi_{J}\cap\Phi_{+})\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}w^{J}(\alpha)>0\right\}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}\setminus(\Phi_{J}\cap\Phi_{+})\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}w^{J}(\alpha)<0\right\}

and put a total order

(with

) whenever

(recall that by definition

-span of

). Then, for any total order on

, there is an isomorphism of affine schemes

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\left(\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{1}}U_{\alpha}\right)\times\left(\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{2}}U_{\alpha}\right)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}&U_{P}\\

∎

Proposition 4.2.4
.

Let

be a closed point, let

and let

\mu_{t,u^{J},w_{J},v^{J}}\colon\begin{array}[]{ccc}U\times\left(\widetilde{V}_{L,w_{J}}\cap q_{L}^{-1}(\mathfrak{u}_{L})\right)&\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow&\widetilde{V}_{w}\cap q^{-1}\left(\Ad(u^{J})t+\mathfrak{u}\right)\\
\left(u,(l,\psi_{L})\right)&\longmapsto&\left(uu^{J}l(v^{J})^{-1}B,\Ad(v^{J})(t+\psi_{L})\right).\end{array}

Proof.

Remember that the surjectivity of a morphism of algebraic varieties can be checked on closed points (
[
27

). The morphism of algebraic varieties

(4.12)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}U\times q^{-1}(\Ad(u^{J})(t+\mathfrak{u}_{L}))&\longrightarrow&q^{-1}(\Ad(u^{J})t+\mathfrak{u})\\
(u,(g,x))&\longmapsto&(ug,x)\end{array}

4.2.3

and

\begin{array}[]{ccc}U\times\left(\widetilde{V}_{w}\cap q^{-1}\left(\Ad(u^{J})(t+\mathfrak{u}_{L})\right)\right)&\longrightarrow&\widetilde{V}_{w}\cap q^{-1}\left(\Ad(u^{J})t+\mathfrak{u}\right)\\

Composing with the bijective morphism

of Proposition

(2)
yields the desired morphism

∎

Counter-examples for bad pairs when

In §
4
, we proved Conjecture
3.2.1
in the case of good pairs. The present section explores the case of bad pairs for the split reductive group
G=\GL_{n/k}
where
n\geq 1
and
k
is any field of characteristic
0
. From now on
G=\GL_{n/k}
,
B
is the subgroup of upper triangular matrices in
G
,
T
is the subgroup of diagonal matrices in
G
,
U
is the subgroup of upper triangular matrices in
G
whose diagonal coordinates are
1
, and
W=\mathcal{S}_{n}
is the Weyl group of
G
.

5.1.
Grassmannians, the flag variety and Schubert cells

We recall a few well-know facts about the flag variety and its decomposition in Schubert cells from the computational point of view. We also set the notation for the rest of the section.

Definition 5.1.1
.

For
1\leq d\leq n
, the
Grassmannian
over
k
of
d
-planes in the
n
-space is the set of linear subspaces of dimension
d
in
k^{n}
. It is noted
\Gr_{d,n/k}
.

For
V\in\Gr_{d,n/k}
, let
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})
be a basis of
V
. Then
v_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{d}
is a non-zero element of
\bigwedge^{d}k^{n}
. Choosing a different basis would change this alternate product by a multiplicative factor (the determinant of one basis in the other), therefore the line spanned by
v_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{d}
in
\bigwedge^{d}k^{n}
is a well-defined element of
\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n})
. One can also recover
V
from this line by the property that a vector
v
is in
V
if and only if
v\wedge v_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{d}=0
in
\bigwedge^{d+1}k^{n}
.

Definition 5.1.2
.

The
Plücker embedding
is the map

\displaystyle\Gr_{d,n/k}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\;\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n}\Big)

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\longmapsto\;k\cdot(v_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge v_{d})

where
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})
is any linearly independent family in
k^{n}
.

Remark 5.1.3
.

Proposition
5.1.5
below together with the discussion above Definition
5.1.2
show that the Grassmannian is a projective variety and the Plücker embedding a closed immersion.

We write any point
x\in\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n})
as

x=[x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}]_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq n}

using the projective coordinates along the standard basis
(e_{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{i_{d}})_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq n}
of
\bigwedge^{d}k^{n}
, where
(e_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
is the standard basis of
k^{n}
. It is sometimes convenient to use the variables
x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}
for any sequence
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})
of integers between
1
and
n
; they satisfy the relations
x_{i_{\sigma(1)}\ldots i_{\sigma(d)}}=\sgn(\sigma)x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}
for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
and
x_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}=0
whenever there are
1\leq k<l\leq d
such that
i_{k}=i_{l}
.

Definition 5.1.4
.

The
Plücker coordinates
of a point in
\Gr_{d,n/k}
are the projective coordinates
[x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}]_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq n}
of its image under the Plücker embedding.

Let
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})
be a linearly independent family in
k^{n}
, and for any
1\leq j\leq d
write the coordinates of
v_{j}
along the standard basis as
(v_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq n}
. Then the Plücker coordinate
x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}
of the span
\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\rangle
can be computed as the rank
d
minor of the matrix
(v_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq n}
along the rows
i_{1}
,…,
i_{d}
and columns
1,\ldots,d
.

Proposition 5.1.5
.

The Plücker coordinates satisfy the following quadratic relations, called the
Plücker relations in dimension
d
:

(5.1)

\sum_{k=1}^{d+1}(-1)^{k}x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d-1}j_{k}}x_{j_{1}\ldots\widehat{j_{k}}\ldots j_{d+1}}=0

where
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1})
and
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d+1})
are any increasing sequences of indices and the notation
\widehat{j}
indicates that the index
j
is omitted. These relations define precisely the points in
\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n})
that arise from the Grassmannian.

Proof.

See for example
[
17
, Thm. 1]
or
[
10
, §9.1 Lemma 1]
.
∎

In the same vein as Proposition
5.1.5
, one can express the property that a subspace of
k^{n}
is included into another.

Proposition 5.1.6
.

Let
1\leq d<d^{\prime}\leq n
be two integers. The set of pairs
(V,V^{\prime})\in\Gr_{d,n/k}\times\Gr_{d^{\prime},n/k}
such that
V\subset V^{\prime}
is identified with the set of points

\left([x_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}]_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq n},[x^{\prime}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d^{\prime}}}]_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d^{\prime}}\leq n}\right)\in\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n}\Big)\times\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigwedge^{d^{\prime}}k^{n}\Big)

which satisfy both sets of Plücker relations and the following additional quadratic relations:

(5.2)

\sum_{k=1}^{d^{\prime}+1}(-1)^{k}x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d-1}j_{k}}x^{\prime}_{j_{1}\ldots\widehat{j_{k}}\ldots j_{d^{\prime}+1}}=0

where
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d-1})
and
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d^{\prime}+1})
are any increasing sequences of indices. These relations are called the
incidence relations in dimensions
(d,d^{\prime})
.

Proof.

See
[
10
, §9.1 Lemma 2]
.
∎

Definition 5.1.7
.

A
flag
in
k^{n}
is a strictly increasing sequence
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n-1})
of proper subspaces of
k^{n}
. The
flag variety over
k
is the set of flags and it is noted
\flag_{n/k}
.

There is an obvious map
\flag_{n/k}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\prod_{d=1}^{n-1}\Gr_{d,n/k}
which, when composed with the product of the Plücker embeddings, gives the embedding

(5.3)

\flag_{n/k}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\prod_{d=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n}\Big)\,.

This realises the flag variety as the projective subvariety of
\prod_{d=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n})
defined by the Plücker relations and the incidence relations (note that the Segre embedding
[
25
,
Lemma 01WD
]
indeed makes
\prod_{d=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n})
projective).

We identify
\flag_{n/k}
with the algebraic group
G/B
in the standard way. As in the first paragraph of §
3.1
, we have the decomposition
G/B=\coprod_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}BwB/B
. The following is well-known (see for example
[
10
, §10.5 Exercise 11]
):

Proposition 5.1.8
.

The cell
BwB/B
is the set of points in
G/B
that satisfy the following equations on Plücker coordinates, for all
d\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
:

(5.4)

x_{w(1)\ldots w(d)}\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}=0\quad\text{if}\quad\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\}\not\preceq\{w(1),\ldots,w(d)\}

where
\preceq
is the order defined in Definition
2.4.2
.

5.2.
Explicit equations for
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{V}_{w}

We give a description of the algebraic varieties
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{V}_{w}
of §
3.2
as locally closed subvarieties of a projective space defined by explicit equations.

Consider the morphism

\displaystyle G\times\mathfrak{b}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\;G/B\times\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle(g,\psi)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\;(gB,\Ad(g)\psi).

It induces an injection
G\times^{B}\mathfrak{b}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow G/B\times\mathfrak{g}
, which restricts to the isomorphism

(5.5)

\widetilde{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}\left\{(gB,\psi)\in G/B\times\mathfrak{b}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\Ad(g^{-1})\psi\in\mathfrak{b}\right\}

between
\widetilde{X}
and a closed subvariety of
G/B\times\mathfrak{b}
. From now on, we use this isomorphism (
5.5
) as the definition of
\widetilde{X}
. This way, the maps
\widetilde{\pi}\colon\widetilde{X}\longrightarrow G/B
and
q\colon\widetilde{X}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{b}
also introduced in §
3.2
, are the projections on the first and second coordinate respectively. For
w\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
we have the cell

\widetilde{V}_{w}=\left\{(gB,\psi)\in\widetilde{X}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}g\in BwB/B\right\}

and its Zariski-closure
\widetilde{X}_{w}
, which give the cell decomposition
\widetilde{X}=\coprod_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\widetilde{V}_{w}
.

A point
(gB,\psi)\in\widetilde{X}
will be identified by the projective coordinates
x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}
of the flag
gB
seen inside
\prod_{d=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n})
by (
5.3
), and by the affine coordinates
(u_{ij})_{i\leq j}
given by the matrix coefficients of
\psi
. We sometimes write
t_{i}
instead of
u_{ii}
. We view
\psi
as the matrix of an endomorphism
u
of
k^{n}
written relatively to the standard basis (
u
is upper triangular). We view
g
as a flag
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n-1})
, so that
V_{d}
is the span of the first
d
columns of
g
.

Lemma 5.2.1
.

Suppose that
u
is an isomorphism. Then
\Ad(g^{-1})\psi\in\mathfrak{b}
if and only if, for each
1\leq d\leq n-1
, the point
x_{d}=\left(x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}\right)_{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}}\in k^{\binom{n}{d}}
is colinear to

u(x_{d})\coloneqq\left(\sum_{j_{1}<\ldots<j_{d}}\Delta^{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(u)x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}\right)_{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}}\in k^{\binom{n}{d}}

where
\Delta^{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(u)
is the minor of
u
associated to rows
i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}
and columns
j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}
. This amounts to
\binom{n}{d}\left(\binom{n}{d}-1\right)/2
equations.

Proof.

\Ad(g^{-1})\psi
is the matrix of
u
relative to the basis given by the columns of
g
. It is upper triangular if and only if each
V_{d}
is stable by
u
. Since
u
is an isomorphism,
u(V_{d})
is of dimension
d
and we can consider its Plücker coordinates: we specifically want that
u(V_{d})
and
V_{d}
have the same image in
\bigwedge^{d}k^{n}
under the Plücker embedding. The image of
V_{d}
is the line spanned by the vector

x_{d}=\sum_{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}}x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}e_{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{i_{d}}\quad\in\bigwedge^{d}k^{n}

and a quick calculation shows that the image of
u(V_{d})
is the line spanned by

u(x_{d})=\sum_{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}}\left(\sum_{j_{1}<\ldots<j_{d}}\Delta^{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(u)x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}\right)e_{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{i_{d}}\quad\in\bigwedge^{d}k^{n}\,.\qed

Remark 5.2.2
.

Note that, since
u
is upper triangular, we have
\Delta^{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(u)=0
unless
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\}\preceq\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}\}
for the partial order of Definition
2.4.2
. Also note that
\Delta^{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(u)=t_{i_{1}}\ldots t_{i_{d}}
.

Lemma 5.2.3
.

Let
A
be the polynomial ring in the coordinates of
(gB/B,\psi)
, that is
A=\mathbb{Z}[x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}},u_{kl},t_{m}]_{1\leq d<n,\>1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq n,\>1\leq k<l\leq n,\>1\leq m\leq n}
. Let
\lambda
be an indeterminate variable.

(1)

Keep the notation of Lemma
5.2.1
. The condition

(5.6)

x_{d}=\left(x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}\right)_{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}}\quad\text{is colinear to}\\
(u+\lambda\id)(x_{d})=\left(\sum_{j_{1}<\ldots<j_{d}}\Delta^{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(u+\lambda\id)x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}\right)_{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}}

translates into
d\binom{n}{d}\left(\binom{n}{d}-1\right)/2
equations in
A
.

(2)

The collection of all these equations in
A
for
1\leq d\leq n-1
defines
\widetilde{X}
inside
G/B\times\mathfrak{b}
.

Proof.

For all
i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}
and
j_{1}<\ldots<j_{d}
, the degree in
\lambda
of
\Delta^{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(u+\lambda\id)
is at most
d-1
, unless
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d})=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})
where the degree is
d
with leading term
\lambda^{d}
. Hence the degree of
\sum_{j_{1}<\ldots<j_{d}}\Delta^{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(u+\lambda\id)x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}
in
\lambda
is
d
, with leading term
x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}\lambda^{d}
. Therefore the
\binom{n}{d}\left(\binom{n}{d}-1\right)/2
“cross-product” equations in
A[\lambda]
that express the condition (
5.6
) are of degree
d-1
in
\lambda
. This proves
(1)
.

As for
(2)
, the equations defining
\widetilde{X}
are the ones that express the condition
\Ad(g^{-1})\psi\in\mathfrak{b}
. They are equivalent to saying that

\Ad(g^{-1})(\psi+\lambda\id)\in\mathfrak{b}

for all
\lambda\in k
. Enlarging
k
if necessary, we can assume that it is algebraically closed, in particular infinite. Therefore there is a
\lambda\in k
such that
\psi+\lambda\id
is invertible. Applying Lemma
5.2.1
, our condition is equivalent to (
5.6
) for any such
\lambda
. Note that (
5.6
) still holds true for any value of
\lambda
. Indeed, if
(u+\lambda)(V_{d})
is of dimension
<d
, then
(u+\lambda)(\sum x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}e_{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{i_{d}})=0
,
i.e.
the Plücker coordinates of
(u+\lambda)(V_{d})
are zero. In particular their vector is colinear to the vector of the Plücker coordinates of
V_{d}
.

In the end,
\Ad(g^{-1})\psi\in\mathfrak{b}
if and only if (
5.6
) is true for all
\lambda\in k
. As
k
is algebraically closed, the corresponding polynomials in
A[\lambda]
are identically zero.
∎

Proposition 5.2.4
.

Let
w\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
. Fix an integer
1\leq d\leq n-1
, let
A
be the ring
\mathbb{Z}[x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}},u_{kl},t_{m}]_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq n,\>1\leq k<l\leq n,\>1\leq m\leq n}
, let
\lambda
be an indeterminate variable. For a sequence
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq n
, let
P_{w,i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}\in A[\lambda]
be the polynomial

P_{w,i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(\lambda)\coloneqq\sum_{j_{1}<\ldots<j_{d}}\Delta^{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(u+\lambda\id)x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}-\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{w(k)}+\lambda)\right)x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}

and for
0\leq s\leq d-1
, let
P_{w,i_{1}\ldots i_{d},s}\in A
be the unique homogeneous polynomials such that

P_{w,i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(\lambda)=\sum_{s=0}^{d-1}P_{w,i_{1}\ldots i_{d},s}\lambda^{s}\,.

Then, using the Plücker coordinates
(x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}})_{1\leq d<n,\>1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq n}
on
G/B
and the affine coordinates
(u_{kl},t_{m})_{1\leq k<l\leq n,\>1\leq m\leq n}
on
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{u}\oplus\mathfrak{t}
, the subvariety
\widetilde{V}_{w}
of
G/B\times\mathfrak{b}
is defined by:

—

the Plücker relations in dimension
d
, given in Proposition
5.1.5
, for each
1\leq d\leq n-1

—

the incidence relations in dimension
(d,d+1)
, given in Proposition
5.1.6
, for each
1\leq d\leq n-2

—

the equations for
(x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}})_{1\leq d<n,\>1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq n}
to be in the cell
BwB/B
of
G/B
, given in Proposition
5.1.8

—

the equations
P_{w,i_{1}\ldots i_{d},s}=0
for all
1\leq d\leq n-1
,
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq n
and
0\leq s\leq d-1
.

Proof.

Lemma
5.2.3
gives a desciption of
\widetilde{X}
as a subvariety of
G/B\times\mathfrak{b}
defined by
\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}d\binom{n}{d}\left(\binom{n}{d}-1\right)/2
equations (which are not necessarily independent). To find the equations for
\widetilde{V}_{w}
, we add the ones given by Proposition
5.1.8
.

Now,
x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}=0
for
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}\}\not\preceq\{w(1),\ldots,w(d)\}
and
\Delta^{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}_{w(1)\ldots w(d)}(u+\lambda\id)=0
for
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}\}\not\succeq\{w(1),\ldots,w(d)\}
. Hence the
(w(1),\ldots,w(d))
-th term of the vector
(u+\lambda\id)(x)
, as expressed in (
5.6
), is

\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{w(k)}+\lambda)\right)x_{w(1)\ldots w(d)}\,.

Knowing that
x_{w(1)\ldots w(d)}\neq 0
and that
x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}=0
for
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}\}\not\preceq\{w(1),\ldots,w(d)\}
, the colinearity condition (
5.6
) is reduced to the following equations:

\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{w(k)}+\lambda)\right)x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}=\sum_{j_{1}<\ldots<j_{d}}\Delta^{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(u+\lambda\id)x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}

where
i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}
is any strictly increasing sequence of indices. These are the announced equations.
∎

Remark 5.2.5
.

We have also seen that for each
w\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
,
1\leq d\leq n-1
and
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq n
, the polynomial
P_{w,i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}
is a sum of terms

a_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}},\quad\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}\}\succeq\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\}\,,

where
a_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}
is an element of
\mathbb{Z}[u_{kl},t_{m},\lambda]_{1\leq k<l\leq n,1\leq m\leq n}
. Also, the particular “first term coefficient”
a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}
is equal to
\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{i_{k}}+\lambda)-\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{w(k)}+\lambda)
. In particular, for
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\}\succeq\{w(1),\ldots,w(d)\}
the polynomial
P_{w,i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}
is in the ideal generated by the
x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}}
for
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}\}\succ\{w(1),\ldots,w(d)\}
, which are all zero on the cell
BwB/B
of
G/B
by Proposition
5.1.8
. Thus the associated equation is tautological on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
.

5.3.
Taking the Zariski closure

For
w\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
, let
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{w}
denote the subvariety of
\prod_{d=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n})\times\mathbb{A}^{n(n+1)/2}
, with coordinate ring
A\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}[x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}},u_{kl},t_{m}]
, defined by all the equations listed in Proposition
5.2.4
except for the inequations
x_{w(1)\ldots w(d)}\neq 0
for
1\leq d\leq n-1
. It is therefore the subvariety of
G/B\times\mathfrak{b}
that satisfies the equations
P_{w,i_{1}\ldots i_{d},s}=0
for all
1\leq d\leq n-1
,
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq n
and
0\leq s\leq d-1
. In particular, note that
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{w}
contains
\widetilde{V}_{w}
by Proposition
5.2.4
and is Zariski-closed. Therefore
\widetilde{X}_{w}\subseteq\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{w}
.

Proposition 5.3.1
.

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
such that
w^{\prime}\preceq w
. Then, as algebraic varieties over
k
,

\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{w}\cap\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}=\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})\,.

Proof.

Let
(gB/B,\psi)\in G/B\times\mathfrak{b}
be a point in the intersection
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{w}\cap\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
. Its coordinates
(x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{d}},u_{kl},t_{m})
satisfy in particular
P_{w,i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(\lambda)=0
in
k[\lambda]
for any
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\}
and
x_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}=0
if
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\}\not\preceq\{w^{\prime}(1),\ldots,w^{\prime}(d)\}
. Under these latter conditions, almost all the terms in
P_{w,w^{\prime}(1)\ldots w^{\prime}(d)}(\lambda)
become zero, and the equation
P_{w,w^{\prime}(1)\ldots w^{\prime}(d)}(\lambda)=0
becomes

\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{w^{\prime}(k)}+\lambda)-\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{w(k)}+\lambda)\right)x_{w^{\prime}(1)\ldots w^{\prime}(d)}=0

on
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{w}
. Since we also have
x_{w^{\prime}(1)\ldots w^{\prime}(d)}\neq 0
, we then must have
\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{w^{\prime}(k)}+\lambda)=\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{w(k)}+\lambda)
as polynomials in
k[\lambda]
.

Also, we have by definition, for all
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\}
,

P_{w,i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(\lambda)-P_{w^{\prime},i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}(\lambda)=\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{w(k)}+\lambda)-\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{w^{\prime}(k)}+\lambda)

in
A[\lambda]
. As a consequence,
P_{w,i_{1}\ldots i_{d},s}
and
P_{w^{\prime},i_{1}\ldots i_{d},s}
are equal for all
s
and
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\}
on
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{w}
. Therefore
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{w}\cap\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
is the subvariety of
\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
where
\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{w^{\prime}(k)}+\lambda)=\prod_{k=1}^{d}(t_{w(k)}+\lambda)
for all
1\leq d\leq n-1
. This last equality of polynomials in
k[\lambda]
means that the multisets
\{t_{w^{\prime}(1)},\ldots,t_{w^{\prime}(d)}\}
and
\{t_{w(1)},\ldots,t_{w(d)}\}
are equal for all
1\leq d\leq n-1
. A recursion on
d
shows that this amounts to saying
t_{w^{\prime}(k)}=t_{w(k)}
for all
1\leq k\leq d
. For
d=n-1
, this is equivalent to
(gB/B,\psi)\in q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})
.
∎

Replacing
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{w}
by
\widetilde{X}_{w}
in Proposition
5.3.1
would yield Conjecture
3.2.3
. However we shall see in Theorem
5.3.7
that this is not always true.

The strategy to find counterexamples to Conjecture
3.2.3
is to use the inequations
x_{w(1)\ldots w(d)}\neq 0
satisfied on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
to simplify other equations satisfied on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
, by “removing” non-zero factors. We then get an equation that is satisfied on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
, hence on
\widetilde{X}_{w}
as well, but not necessarily on
\tilde{X}^{\prime}_{w}
. This is the object of the following two lemmas. Once this is done, this equation, when considered on
\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
, gives a condition that turns out to be strictly more restrictive that simply being in
q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})
.

Lemma 5.3.2
.

Let
w\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
,
q\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
and
b\in\{w(1),\ldots,w(q+1)\}
; write
\{w(1),\ldots,w(q+1)\}=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q},b\}
. Let
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}\}
be a subset of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
which does not contain
b
. Assume that for any
1\leq l\leq q
such that
j_{l}<b
, we have
j_{l}\in\{w(1),\ldots,w(q+1)\}
. Then, for any
a\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the equation

x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}a}x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}b}=\pm x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}b}x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}a}

is satisfied on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
for a certain choice of sign.

Proof.

Consider the Plücker relation in dimension
q+1
with indices
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}\}
and
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q},a,b\}
(see Proposition
5.1.6
), which is satisfied on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
. Up to signs, it is of the form

(5.7)

\pm x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}a}x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}b}\pm x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}b}x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}a}+\sum_{1\leq l\leq q}\pm x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}k_{l}}x_{j_{1}\ldots\hat{j_{l}}\ldots j_{q}ab}=0\,.

Let
1\leq l\leq q
. If
j_{l}<b
, then our hypothesis implies that
x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}j_{l}}=0
because the indices are redundant. Else we have
j_{l}>b
, so that
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q},j_{l}\}\succ\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q},b\}=\{w(1),\ldots,w(q+1)\}
, hence
x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}j_{l}}=0
on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
by virtue of Proposition
5.1.8
. Eliminating all those null variables in (
5.7
) gives the desired equation.
∎

Lemma 5.3.3
.

Keep the notation and assumption of Lemma
5.3.2
. Assume further that
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q},a\}\preceq\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q},b\}
. Then the equation

(5.8)

\sum_{\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}\}\preceq\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}\}\preceq\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}\}}\pm\Delta^{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}b}_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}b}(u+\lambda\id)x_{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}a}-\left(\prod_{i=1}^{p+1}(t_{w(i)}+\lambda)\right)x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}a}=0

is satisfied on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
for a certain choice of sign.

Proof.

We can assume that the sequences
(i_{l})_{1\leq l\leq q}
and
(j_{l})_{1\leq l\leq q}
are in increasing order. Let
1\leq r\leq q
be such that
j_{r}<b<j_{r+1}
(setting
j_{q+1}=\infty
if necessary). Then
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q},a\}
has either
r
or
r+1
elements smaller than or equal to
b
, depending on wether
a>b
or
a\leq b
. Because
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q},a\}\preceq\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q},b\}
, this implies that
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q},b\}
has at most
r+1
elements smaller than or equal to
b
. However the hypothesis of Lemma
5.3.2
implies that
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r},b\}\subseteq\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q},b\}
. This forces
j_{1}
,…,
j_{r}
to be the
r
smallest elements of
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}\}
, hence
i_{l}=j_{l}
for all
l\leq r
.

In particular, we have
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q},b\}\preceq\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q},b\}
and any set of integers that sits between those two is of the form
\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{q},b\}
with
k_{l}=i_{l}
for
l\leq r
and
k_{l}>b
for
l>r
. We can therefore apply Lemma
5.3.2
replacing
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}\}
by
\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}\}
; this yields, up to sign,

(5.9)

x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}a}x_{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}b}=\pm x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}b}x_{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}a}\,.

Now, keeping the notation of Proposition
5.2.4
, we multiply by
x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}a}
the equation
P_{w,j_{1}\ldots j_{q}b}(\lambda)=0
(in
A[\lambda]
). From the discussion of Remark
5.2.5
, the resulting equation only has terms in
x_{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}b}x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}a}
for
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}\}\preceq\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}\}
. Also, because of Proposition
5.1.8
, the
x_{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}b}
are non-zero on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
only for
\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}\}\preceq\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}\}
. With all these simplifications, we get on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
the equation

\sum_{\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}\}\preceq\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}\}\preceq\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}\}}\Delta^{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}b}_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}b}(u+\lambda\id)x_{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}b}x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}a}\\
-\left(\prod_{i=1}^{q+1}(t_{w(i)}+\lambda)\right)x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}b}x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}a}=0\,.

Using the equations (
5.9
), we get

\sum_{\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}\}\preceq\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}\}\preceq\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}\}}\pm\Delta^{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}b}_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}b}(u+\lambda\id)x_{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}a}x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}b}\\
-\left(\prod_{i=1}^{q+1}(t_{w(i)}+\lambda)\right)x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}a}x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}b}=0

which, after factorisation and simplification by
x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}b}
(the simplification is valid because
x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{q}b}=x_{w(1)\ldots w(q+1)}\neq 0
on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
), gives the equation we wanted.
∎

Proposition 5.3.4
.

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
with
w^{\prime}\preceq w
. Assume that there exist integers
a<b
and
q\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
such that:

—

a\notin\{w(1),\ldots,w(q+1)\}
and
a\in\{w^{\prime}(1),\ldots,w^{\prime}(q+1)\}
,

—

b\in\{w(1),\ldots,w(q+1)\}
and
b\notin\{w^{\prime}(1),\ldots,w^{\prime}(q+1)\}
,

—

for any
i\in\{w^{\prime}(1),\ldots,w^{\prime}(q+1)\}
such that
i\neq a
and
i<b
, we have
i\in\{w(1),\ldots,w(q+1)\}
.

Then the equation
t_{a}=t_{b}
is satisfied on
\widetilde{X}_{w}\cap\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
.

Example 5.3.5
.

Take
n=4
,
w=[4231]
and
w^{\prime}=[1324]
. Then
q=1
and
(a,b)=(1,2)
meets the condition of Proposition
5.3.4
, hence
\widetilde{X}_{w}\cap\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
satisfies
t_{1}=t_{2}
. This is stronger than for
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{w}\cap\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
, which by Proposition
5.3.1
only satisfies
t_{1}=t_{4}
and
t_{2}=t_{3}
(the equations defining
\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}
). One could also take
(a,b)=(3,4)
; this gives the equation
t_{3}=t_{4}
on
\widetilde{X}_{w}\cap\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
, which is equivalent to the already found
t_{1}=t_{2}
, since
t_{1}=t_{4}
and
t_{2}=t_{3}
are true.

Proof.

Write
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}\}=\{w^{\prime}(1),\ldots,w^{\prime}(q+1)\}\setminus\{a\}
; then the hypotheses of Lemma
5.3.2
are satisfied. From Example
2.4.4
applied to
w^{\prime}\preceq w
, we also have
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q},a\}\preceq\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q},b\}
. We can therefore apply Lemma
5.3.3
to get

\sum_{\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}\}\preceq\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}\}\preceq\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}\}}\pm\Delta^{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}b}_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}b}(u+\lambda\id)x_{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}a}-\left(\prod_{i=1}^{p+1}(t_{w(i)}+\lambda)\right)x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}a}=0

which is satisfied on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
, hence on the Zariski closure
\widetilde{X}_{w}
as well.

Now consider this equation on
\widetilde{X}_{w}\cap\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
. From Proposition
5.1.8
, all variables
x_{k_{1}\ldots k_{q}a}
are zero on
\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
for
\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}\}\succ\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{q}\}
, so it reduces to one term:

\left(\Delta^{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}b}_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}b}(u+\lambda\id)-\prod_{i=1}^{p+1}(t_{w(i)}+\lambda)\right)x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}a}=0\,.

Again on
\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
we can simplify by
x_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}a}=x_{w^{\prime}(1)\ldots w^{\prime}(q+1)}\neq 0
. Moreover,
u
being upper triangular gives
\Delta^{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}b}_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}b}(u+\lambda\id)=(t_{b}+\lambda)\prod_{l=1}^{p}(t_{j_{l}}+\lambda)=\frac{t_{b}+\lambda}{t_{a}+\lambda}\prod_{i=1}^{p+1}(t_{w^{\prime}(i)}+\lambda)
, so the equation becomes

\frac{t_{b}+\lambda}{t_{a}+\lambda}\prod_{i=1}^{p+1}(t_{w^{\prime}(i)}+\lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^{p+1}(t_{w(i)}+\lambda)\,.

We also know that
\prod_{i=1}^{p+1}(t_{w^{\prime}(i)}+\lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^{p+1}(t_{w(i)}+\lambda)
from the proof of Proposition
5.3.1
; so we finally get
t_{a}+\lambda=t_{b}+\lambda
, which implies
t_{a}=t_{b}
.
∎

Remark 5.3.6

Theorem 5.3.7
.

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
with
w^{\prime}\preceq w
. Assume that there exist
a
,
b
as in Proposition
5.3.4
such that
a
and
b
are not in the same orbit under
ww^{\prime-1}
. Then Conjecture
3.2.1
is false for the pair
(w,w^{\prime})
, that is:

\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}\cap q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})\not\subseteq\widetilde{X}_{w}\,.

Proof.

Consider any point
x=(x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}},t_{i},u_{ij})\in\prod_{d=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{d}k^{n})\times\mathbb{A}^{n(n+1)/2}
where, for
1\leq d\leq n-1
and
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{d}<n
,
x_{i_{1}\ldots i_{d}}\neq 0
if and only if
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\}=\{w^{\prime}(1),\ldots,w^{\prime}(d)\}
, and where
u_{ij}=0
for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
. It is easily checked that
x\in\widetilde{V}_{w^{\prime}}
, either from the equations of Proposition
5.2.4
, or from the fact that such a point

corresponds to
(gB/B,\psi)\in G/B\times\mathfrak{b}
where
g
is an invertible matrix with zeroes everywhere but in positions
(w^{\prime}(j),j)
for
1\leq j\leq n
, and where
\psi\in\mathfrak{t}
.

When
a
and
b
are not in the same orbit under
ww^{\prime-1}
, it is possible to set
\psi=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}
with
t_{a}\neq t_{b}
. This gives
x\in q^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}^{ww^{\prime-1}}+\mathfrak{u})
, while Proposition
5.3.4
forces

, so

.
∎

Remark 5.3.8
.

(defined in §

) in place of

, which is
[

. Therefore

is a union of irreducible component which have dimension strictly smaller than the dimension of

Remark 5.3.9
.

The easiest example of

meeting the conditions of Theorem

is when there exist

and

such that:

(w(i),w(j),w(k),w(l))=(d,b,c,a)
.

(2)

(w^{\prime}(i),w^{\prime}(j),w^{\prime}(k),w^{\prime}(l))=(a,c,b,d)
.

(3)

for all

,

The smallest such example is when

is the first bad pair for

(see Theorem
2.4.10
). In fact, all cases of interest (
e.g.
when

is a bad pair, see Lemma

meet the conditions of Theorem

.

For

, a few cases arise where neither those conditions nor the variant of Remark
5.3.6
are met, for example when
w=(16)(25)
and

(using the cycles notation for elements of the symmetric group
\mathcal{S}_{6}
). In this specific case, the approach of the proof of Theorem
5.3.7
is not adapted. Indeed, the only equations defining the Schubert cell
BwB/B
in

are
x_{6}\neq 0
,
x_{56}\neq 0
,
x_{356}\neq 0
,
x_{3456}\neq 0
,
x_{23456}\neq 0
and

. Because

is the only zero variable, there are too few Plücker relations and incidence relations that have only two nonzero terms on

(in the same way equation (

) gives Lemma

). Furthermore those few relations cannot be used to get new equations satisfied on
\widetilde{V}_{w}
(and hence
\widetilde{X}_{w}
) that are nontrivial on

(the same way the equation

of Proposition
5.3.4
is), because all Plücker coordinates but

Finally, the following lemma confirms that Theorem

and Theorem

are consistent with each other.

Lemma 5.3.10
.

Let
(w^{\prime},w)\in W
satisfy the conditions of Theorem
5.3.7
. Then it is a bad pair in the sense of §
2
. The same conclusion holds with the conditions of Remark
5.3.6
.

Proof.

If the pair
(w,w^{\prime})
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
5.3.7
, including the one that states that
a
and
b
are not in the same orbit under
ww^{\prime-1}
, then it is a bad pair in the sense of §
2
. Indeed, let
\Omega
the orbit of
b
under
ww^{\prime-1}
. By assumption,

, so the last condition of Proposition
5.3.4
implies that

\left(\Omega\cap w(\{1,\ldots,q+1\})\right)\cap\{1,\ldots,b\}\supsetneq\left(\Omega\cap w^{\prime}(\{1,\ldots,q+1\})\right)\cap\{1,\ldots,b\}

with the inequality stemming from the presence of

in the left-hand side and not in the right-hand side. Therefore
\Omega\cap w(\{1,\ldots,q+1\})\succeq\Omega\cap w^{\prime}(\{1,\ldots,q+1\})
is false (see the second characterisation in Definition
2.4.2
), so the pair
(w,w^{\prime})
is bad according to Proposition
2.4.5
.

In the situation of Remark
5.3.6
, take
\Omega
to be the orbit of
a
under
ww^{\prime-1}
. Again,
b\notin\Omega
, so the condition spelled out in Remark
5.3.6
implies that

\left(\Omega\cap w(\{1,\ldots,q+1\})\right)\cap\{a,\ldots,n\}\subsetneq\left(\Omega\cap w^{\prime}(\{1,\ldots,q+1\})\right)\cap\{a,\ldots,n\}

from which we get
\lvert(\Omega\cap w(\{1,\ldots,q+1\}))\cap\{a,\ldots,n\}\rvert<\lvert(\Omega\cap w^{\prime}(\{1,\ldots,q+1\}))\cap\{a,\ldots,n\}\rvert
, or equivalently
\lvert(\Omega\cap w(\{1,\ldots,q+1\}))\cap\{1,\ldots,a-1\}\rvert>\lvert(\Omega\cap w^{\prime}(\{1,\ldots,q+1\}))\cap\{1,\ldots,a-1\}\rvert

is false and hence

∎

Appendix A
Jordan decomposition and orbits in
\mathfrak{g}

We recall here a few well-known results about Jordan decompositions in Lie algebras. Note that Proposition
A.9
below is needed in §
4.2
. We thank Anne Moreau for its proof, which we couldn’t find precisely in the literature.

In the following,
k
is an algbraically closed field of characteristic
0
(so that
k
-points and closed points on algebraic varieties over
k
are the same). For instance, there is no distinction between viewing a Lie algebra as a
k
-scheme (as we do in §
4
) or as a
k
-vector space.

Definition A.1
.

Let
G
be an algebraic group over
k
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. A closed point
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is called
semisimple
(resp.
nilpotent
) if the following property holds. For any morphism of algebraic groups
\phi\colon G\longrightarrow\GL(V)
inducing
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\phi\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\gl(V)
, where
V
is a finite-dimensional vector space over
k
, the endomorphism
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\phi(x)\in\gl(V)\simeq\End(V)
is diagonalisable (resp. nilpotent).

Theorem A.2

(Jordan decomposition)
.

(1)

Let
G
be an algebraic group over
k
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. For any closed point
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, there is a unique couple
(x_{\mathrm{s}},x_{\mathrm{n}})\in\mathfrak{g}^{2}
with
x_{\mathrm{s}}
semisimple and
x_{\mathrm{n}}
nilpotent such that
[x_{\mathrm{s}},x_{\mathrm{n}}]=0
and
x=x_{\mathrm{s}}+x_{\mathrm{n}}
. We call this the
Jordan decomposition
of
x
.

(2)

The Jordan decomposition is functorial: for any morphism
\phi\colon G\longrightarrow H
of algebraic groups over
k
inducing
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\phi\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
and any closed point
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\phi(x_{\mathrm{s}})=(\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\phi(x))_{\mathrm{s}},\quad\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\phi(x_{\mathrm{n}})=(\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\phi(x))_{\mathrm{n}}\,.

Proof.

The existence of a functorial Jordan decomposition is a combination of 23.6.2, 23.6.3 and 23.6.4 of
[
26
]
. The uniqueness property in
(1)
is given by the fact that for any algebraic group
G
, there exists a finite-dimensional vector space
V
with a morphism of algebraic groups
\phi\colon G\longrightarrow\GL(V)
such that
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\phi\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\gl(V)
is injective (see 22.1.5 and 24.4.1 of
loc. cit.
).
∎

Proposition A.3
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
k
, let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
and
\mathfrak{z}
be the centre of
\mathfrak{g}
. There is a decomposition of
k
-vector spaces

(A.1)

\mathfrak{g}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\oplus\mathfrak{z}

and for any closed point
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

x
is semisimple (resp. nilpotent).

(ii)

\ad(x)\in\gl(\mathfrak{g})
is semisimple (resp.
\ad(x)\in\gl(\mathfrak{g})
is nilpotent and the component of
x
in
\mathfrak{z}
in (
A.1
) is
0
).

(iii)

For any finite-dimensional representation
\sigma\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\gl(V)
, the endomorphism
\sigma(x)\in\gl(V)\simeq\End(V)
is diagonalisable (resp. nilpotent).

Proof.

The decomposition (
A.1
) is 27.2.2 and 20.5.5 of
[
26
]
. Note that
\mathfrak{z}=0
if and only if
G
is semisimple (see 20.1.2 and 20.5.4 of
loc. cit.
). The implication
(iii)
\implies
(i)
is a consequence of the definitions. We now prove
(i)
\implies
(ii)
. Since
\ad\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\gl(\mathfrak{g})
is the differential of
\Ad\colon G\longrightarrow\GL(\mathfrak{g})
(see 23.5.5 of
loc. cit.
), we only need to prove the following statement (the rest follows from the definitions): if
x\in\mathfrak{g}
decomposes as
x=y+z
via (
A.1
) and
x
is nilpotent, then
z=0
. Since
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is the Lie algebra of the derived group
(G,G)
(see 27.2.3 of
loc. cit.
), we can consider the Jordan decomposition
y=y_{\mathrm{s}}+y_{\mathrm{n}}
of
y
in
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Applying Theorem
A.2
(2)
to the inclusion
(G,G)\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow G
, we see that
y_{\mathrm{s}}
(resp.
y_{\mathrm{n}}
) is semisimple (resp. nilpotent) in
\mathfrak{g}
. As
z
is semisimple in
\mathfrak{g}
(see 27.2.2(ii) of
loc. cit.
) and commutes with everything, we see that
y_{\mathrm{s}}+z
is semisimple and
x=(y_{\mathrm{s}}+z)+y_{\mathrm{n}}
is the Jordan decomposition of
x
. By uniqueness,
y_{\mathrm{s}}+z=0
hence
z=0
. This finishes the proof of
(i)
\implies
(ii)
. Finally,
(ii)
\implies
(iii)
follows from 20.5.7 and 20.5.8 of
loc. cit.
(note that their definition of semisimple and nilpotent elements differs from ours).
∎

Lemma A.4
.

Let
G
be a semisimple algebraic group over
k
, with semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
be Cartan subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then there exists a closed point
g\in G
such that
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}=\Ad_{G}(g)\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

We proceed exactly as in
[
26
, 29.2.3(ii)]
. We first recall the notation of 19.1.1 of
loc. cit
. For a closed point
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, we write

\mathfrak{g}^{0}(x)\coloneqq\cap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\ker((\ad{x})^{n})

and we say that
x
is
generic
if
\dim{\mathfrak{g}^{0}(x)}
(which is the multiplicity of
0
as a root of the characteristic polynomial of
\ad(x)
) is minimal among all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. Call
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{gen}}
the set of generic elements; it is a Zariski-open subset of
\mathfrak{g}
since the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial are a polynomial expression in
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. By 29.2.3(i) of
loc. cit.
a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
is Cartan if and only if it is of the form
\mathfrak{g}^{0}(x)
for some
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{gen}}
. Hence choose
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{gen}}
such that
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}^{0}(x)
. Consider the morphism

\theta\colon\;\begin{aligned} G\times\mathfrak{h}\;&\longrightarrow\;\mathfrak{g}\\
(g,y)\;&\longmapsto\;\Ad(g)y\end{aligned}

whose differential at the point
(e,x)\in G\times\mathfrak{h}
(
e
is the neutral element in
G
) is

\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\theta_{(e,x)}\colon\;\begin{aligned} \mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{h}\;&\longrightarrow\;\mathfrak{g}\\
(z,y)\;&\longmapsto\;[z,x]+y\end{aligned}

(see 29.1.4(i) and 23.5.5 of
loc. cit.
). By definition,
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}^{0}(x)
is a subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
stable by
\ad(x)
, and
\ad(x)
induces an automorphism on
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{0}(x)=\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
. Therefore, as in 29.2.1 of
loc. cit.
,
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\theta_{(e,x)}
is surjective, so
\theta
is dominant by 16.5.7 of
loc. cit.
, and from 15.4.2 of
loc. cit.
, the image
\Ad(G)\mathfrak{h}
contains an open dense subset of
\mathfrak{g}
. Since the same can be said of
\Ad(G)\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
, we have in
\mathfrak{g}

\Ad(G)\mathfrak{h}\cap\Ad(G)\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{gen}}\neq\emptyset\,.

Therefore there is
t\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{gen}}
and
g,g^{\prime}\in G
such that
\Ad(g)t\in\mathfrak{h}
and
\Ad(g^{\prime})t\in\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
. Since
\Ad(g)t
and
\Ad(g^{\prime})t
are generic as well, 19.8.5 of
loc. cit.
gives

\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}^{0}(\Ad(g)t)\,,\quad\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{g}^{0}(\Ad(g^{\prime})t)

hence
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}=\Ad(g^{\prime}g^{-1})\mathfrak{h}
.
∎

For any vector space
V
over any field, write
V^{*}
for its dual,
S(V)
for the symmetric algebra of
V
and
S^{n}(V)
for the graded piece of

of degree
n
(where

). Recall that if

is finite dimensional,

can be interpreted as the algebra of polynomial functions on

.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Lie algebra, the action of
\mathfrak{g}

. We can then consider the algebra
S(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\mathfrak{g}}
of invariants under this action. Similarly, given a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, the Weyl group
W
of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
acts on
\mathfrak{h}
(see
[

), so we can consider

Theorem A.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Lie algebra over

,
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of

and

be the Weyl group of the root system associated to

(1)

The restriction morphism

.

(2)

For

, the vector space

is spanned by functions of the form
x\longmapsto\tr(\sigma(x)^{n})
, where
\sigma
is a finite-dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{g}
over

.

Proof.

This is
[
26
, 31.2.6]
.

Proposition A.7
.

Let
G
be a semisimple algebraic group over
k
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, let
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
be semisimple elements. Assume that
\tr(\sigma(x)^{n})=\tr(\sigma(y)^{n})
for all finite-dimensional representation
\sigma
of
\mathfrak{g}
and all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Then
x
and
y
are in the same orbit for the action of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
by adjunction.

Proof.

We know from
[
26
, 20.6.3(ii)]
that
x
and
y
are each contained in a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. According to Lemma
A.4
, we can assume that
x
and
y
lie in the same Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
. Let
W
be the Weyl group associated to the root system of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
. It follows from Theorem
A.5
and Lemma
A.6
that
x
and
y
are in the same orbit for the action of
W
on
\mathfrak{h}
.

There is a maximal torus
T
of
G
such that
\mathfrak{h}
is the Lie algebra of

(see 29.2.5 of
loc. cit.
and
[
14
, 26.2.A]
). The action of the Weyl group

on

induces, through differentiation, an action of

; this identifies

with

is isomorphic to

, and the corresponding action of

.
∎

Lemma A.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Lie algebra over
k
, let
\sigma\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\gl(V)
be a finite-dimensional representation and let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(1)

Let
x\in\mathfrak{g}
be a closed point with Jordan decomposition
x=x_{\mathrm{s}}+x_{\mathrm{n}}
. Then
\tr(\sigma(x)^{n})=\tr(\sigma(x_{\mathrm{s}})^{n})
.

(2)

Let
\mathfrak{b}
be a Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, let
x,t\in\mathfrak{b}
be closed points such that
x-t
is nilpotent. Then
\tr(\sigma(x)^{n})=\tr(\sigma(t)^{n})
.

Proof.

The assertion
(1)
is
[
26
, 34.2.3]
. As for
(2)
, note that by semisimplicity
\mathfrak{g}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, so
\sigma(\mathfrak{b})
is a solvable subalgebra of
\slfrak(V)=[\gl(V),\gl(V)]
. Hence
\sigma(\mathfrak{b})
is contained in a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
of
\slfrak(V)
. Using 29.4.7 of
loc. cit.
, one can find a basis of

such that
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
is the set of upper triangular matrices in
\slfrak(V)
. Since

is nilpotent, by Proposition
A.3
the matrix
\sigma(x)-\sigma(t)
is nilpotent and in
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
hence is strictly upper triangular. Therefore
\sigma(x)
and
\sigma(t)
are upper triangular with the same diagonal in
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
. Thus so are
\sigma(x)^{n}
and
\sigma(t)^{n}
, which therefore have the same trace on
V
.
∎

Proposition A.9
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
k
with a maximal torus
T
and a Borel subgroup
B
containing
T
, let
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{t}
and
\mathfrak{b}
be their respective Lie algebra. Let
x\in\mathfrak{b}
be a closed point with Jordan decomposition
x=x_{\mathrm{s}}+x_{\mathrm{n}}
.

(1)

The components
x_{\mathrm{s}}
and
x_{\mathrm{n}}
are in
\mathfrak{b}
.

(2)

Let
t\in\mathfrak{t}
and
u\in\mathfrak{b}
such that
u
is nilpotent and
x=t+u\ \in\mathfrak{b}
. Then there exists
g\in(G,G)
such that
x_{\mathrm{s}}=\Ad_{G}(g)t
.

Proof.

The inclusion
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is the differential of the inclusion
B\subset G
, hence by Theorem

(2)
, the Jordan decomposition of

viewed as an element of
\mathfrak{b}
is the same as that of
x
viewed as an element of
\mathfrak{g}
. This proves
(1)
.

For
(2)
, the decomposition (
A.1
) gives
x=y+z
with
y\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and
z\in\mathfrak{z}
. Proposition
A.3
gives
u\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, hence
t=s+z
for some semisimple element

. Since
\mathfrak{z}
is the Lie algebra of the center

of
G
(see
[
26
, 24.4.2(ii)]
) and

(see
[
14

), we have
\mathfrak{z}\subset\mathfrak{t}\subset\mathfrak{b}
, hence

and

, thus
y,s\in\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathfrak{b}\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
.

Note that
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{z}
since
\mathfrak{z}\subset\mathfrak{b}
. Let
\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\prime}
be a solvable Lie subalgebra of

containing
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{1}\coloneqq\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\mathfrak{b}_{1}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{b}
and it is solvable since

\mathfrak{b}_{1}=\mathfrak{b}
, and
\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}

\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
is a Borel subalgebra of

. Consequently, since

, we can apply Lemma
A.8
and Proposition
A.7
to the semisimple group

and the elements
y
,
s
of its Lie algebra

(see
[

, 27.2.3]
). This gives

From the proof of Proposition

, we know that

. Since the conjugation by

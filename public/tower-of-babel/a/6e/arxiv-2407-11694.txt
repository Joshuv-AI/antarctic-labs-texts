1.
Introduction

Let
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
denote the space of cusp forms of weight
k\geq 2
, level
N\geq 1
, and character
\chi
. Here,
\chi
is a Dirichlet character modulo
N
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. For
m\geq 1
coprime to
N
, we will denote the
m
-th Hecke operator on
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
by
T_{m}(N,k,\chi)
. (Throughout this entire we will only consider
m
coprime to
N
.) When the character
\chi
is trivial, we will drop
\chi
and simply write
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
and
T_{m}(N,k)
, respectively.

The space of cusp forms has a decomposition
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=S_{k}^{\old}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)\oplus S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
; see Cohen and Stromberg
[
4
, Proposition 13.3.2]
. The subspaces in this decomposition are orthogonal complements with respect to the Petersson inner product, and further, they are stable under the Hecke operator
T_{m}(N,k,\chi)
.
We write
T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)
for the restriction of
T_{m}(N,k,\chi)
to the new subspace
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
.

Let
d=\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
and
n=\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
. We write the characteristic polynomials of
T_{m}(N,k,\chi)
and
T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)
as

(1.1)

\displaystyle T_{m}(N,k,\chi)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}a_{i}(m,N,k,\chi)x^{d-i},\quad\text{and}

(1.2)

\displaystyle T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}a_{i}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)x^{n-i},

respectively. Here, we refer to
a_{i}(m,N,k,\chi)
and
a_{i}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
as the
i
-th coefficient of the Hecke polynomials for
T_{m}(N,k,\chi)
and
T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)
, respectively. Again, if
\chi
is trivial, we will drop it from the notation. Observe that the first coefficients
a_{1}(m,N,k,\chi)
and
a_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
are the traces of
T_{m}(N,k,\chi)
and
T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)
, respectively.

In
[
15
]
, Rouse conjectured that the traces of Hecke operators (that is
a_{1}(m,N,k)
) are nonvanishing for non-square
m
,
N
coprime to
m
, and even
k\geq 16
or
k=12
. This is a generalization of Lehmer’s conjecture
[
9
]
on the nonvanishing of Ramanujan’s
\tau
function. The work of Clayton et al.
[
3
]
and Ross and Xue
[
14
]
studied a related question: the nonvanishing of the second coefficients
a_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)
.
In this paper, we study the the nonvanishing of the second coefficients
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
on the new subspace. This new subspace
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
deserves separate attention; it is generated by newforms, and hence understanding the Hecke operators on
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
will reveal useful information about newforms.
The works
[
3
]
and
[
14
]
showed that the second coefficient is more predictable than the trace in a certain sense. In this paper, we provide more evidence in this aspect. In particular, we show nonvanishing results for
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
; however, the analogs of these results have not yet been established for
a_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
.

Our first main result concerns the nonvanishing of
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
, and furthermore the sign of
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
.
Observe that when
\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)<2
, the Hecke polynomial for
T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)
has degree
<2
, and hence
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
trivially vanishes.
In the first part of Theorem
1.1
, we do not need to exclude the case when
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
trivially vanishes, because
\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))<2
for only finitely many pairs
(N,k)

[
12
, Theorem 1.3]
.
However, we do need to exclude the case where
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
trivially vanishes, because as discussed in Section
6
,
\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=0
for an infinite family of triples
(N,k,\chi)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m\geq 1
be fixed. Consider
N\geq 1
coprime to
m
and
k\geq 2
even. Then for all but finitely many pairs
(N,k)
,

a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)\text{ is }\begin{cases}\text{negative}&\text{when }m\text{ is not a perfect square,}\\
\text{positive}&\text{when }m\text{ is a perfect square.}\end{cases}

Furthermore, consider
N\geq 1
coprime to
m
,
k\geq 2
, and
\chi
a Dirichlet character modulo
N
where
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. Then
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
nontrivially vanishes for only finitely many triples
(N,k,\chi)
.

The approach we use here is similar to that adopted in
[
3
]
and
[
14
]
. We first express
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
in terms of traces of various Hecke operators. Then for fixed
m
, the Eichler-Selberg trace formula is applied to determine the asymptotic behavior of
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
with respect to
N
and
k
.

For our second main result, we compute explicit bounds for each of the terms in the Eichler-Selberg trace formula and use these bounds to determine the exceptional pairs for the cases of
m=2
and
m=4
with trivial character.
Here, we give the pairs
(N,k)
for which
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
nontrivially vanishes. (The pairs
(N,k)
for which
\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))<2
and hence
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
trivially vanishes can be found in Ross
[
12
, Tables 6.2, 6.3]
.)

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider
N\geq 1
odd and
k\geq 2
even. Then
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(2,N,k)
nontrivially vanishes precisely for
(N,k)\in\{(37,2),\,(57,2)\}
, and
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(4,N,k)
nontrivially vanishes precisely for
(N,k)\in\{(43,2),(57,2),(75,2),(205,2)\}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we express
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
in terms of traces of Hecke operators and state the Eichler-Selberg trace formula for the new subspace. Then in Section
3
, we bound each of the terms appearing in the Eichler-Selberg trace formula for
\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)
. In Section
4
, we prove the trivial character case of Theorem
1.1
using the bounds obtained in Section
3
. In Section
5
, we prove Theorem
1.2
, determining the complete list of pairs
(N,k)
for which
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(2,N,k)
and
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(4,N,k)
vanish. In Section
6
, we prove the general character case of Theorem
1.1
.
Finally, in Section
7
, we discuss these results, giving some additional motivation and surveying potential future work.

2.
The Second Coefficient Formula

In the manner of
[
3
, Proposition 2.1]
and
[
14
, Lemma 2.1]
, we develop a formula for
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
in terms of traces of Hecke operators.

Lemma 2.1
.

For convenience, let
T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}
denote
T_{m}^{{\textnormal{new}}}(N,k,\chi)
. Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle a_{2}^{{\textnormal{new}}}(m,N,k,\chi)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\Tr T_{m}^{{\textnormal{new}}}\right)^{2}-\sum_{d\mid m}\chi(d)d^{k-1}\Tr T_{m^{2}/d^{2}}^{{\textnormal{new}}}\right].

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}
. Then by the definition of the characteristic polynomial and the Hecke operator composition formula
[
4
, Theorem 10.2.9]
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}-\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\lambda_{i}^{2}\right]

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}})^{2}-\Tr(T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}})^{2}\right]

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}})^{2}-\sum_{d\mid m}\chi(d)d^{k-1}\Tr T_{m^{2}/d^{2}}^{\textnormal{new}}\right],

as desired.
∎

We now state the Eichler-Selberg trace formula.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
7
, pp. 370-371]
,
[
4
, Theorem 12.4.11]
)
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
N\geq 1
,
k\geq 2
, and
\chi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
.
Then

(2.6)

\Tr T_{m}(N,k,\chi)=A_{1}(m,N,k,\chi)-A_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)-A_{3}(m,N,k,\chi)+A_{4}(m,N,k,\chi)

where

(2.7)

\displaystyle A_{1}(m,N,k,\chi)

\displaystyle=\chi(\sqrt{m})\frac{k-1}{12}\psi(N)m^{k/2-1},

(2.8)

\displaystyle A_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t^{2}<4m}U_{k-1}(t,m)\sum_{n}h_{w}\left(\frac{t^{2}-4m}{n^{2}}\right)\mu_{t,n,m}(N),

(2.9)

\displaystyle A_{3}(m,N,k,\chi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d|m}\min(d,m/d)^{k-1}\sum_{\tau}\phi(\gcd(\tau,N/\tau))\chi(y_{\tau}),

(2.10)

\displaystyle A_{4}(m,N,k,\chi)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c|m\\
(N,m/c)=1\end{subarray}}c&\text{if $k=2$ and $\chi=\chi_{0}$},\\
0&\text{if $k>2$ or $\chi\neq\chi_{0}$}.\end{cases}

Here, we have the following notation.

•

\chi(\sqrt{m})
is interpreted as
0

•

\psi(N)=\left[\,{\mathrm{SL}_{2}}(\ZZ)\,:\,\Gamma_{0}(N)\,\right]=N\prod_{p|N}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right).

•

The outer summation for

runs over all

such that

. Note that the terms corresponding to

•

•

•

•

•

•

•

•

•

Lemma 2.3

(
[
4
, Theorem 13.5.7 for
\gcd(m,N)=1

Let
m\geq 1,N\geq 1

,

\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. Recall that
{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
denotes the conductor of

\beta
be the multiplicative function defined on prime powers

3.
Bounding the
A_{i}^{{\textnormal{new}}}(m,N,k)

In this section, we write each
A_{i}(m,N,k)
term from the Eichler-Selberg trace formula as a linear combination of multiplicative functions
f(N)
. This will allow us to rewrite (
2.16
) as a linear combination of Dirichlet convolutions of the form
\beta*f
. We then use these convolutions to give explicit bounds for each of the
A_{i}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
terms from (
2.15
). We also give the asymptotic behavior of these terms. This asymptotic behavior will be stated using big-
O
notation with respect to
N
and
k
.

The Dirichlet convolution

(3.1)

\displaystyle\beta*f(N)=\sum_{M\mid N}\beta\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)\cdot f(M)

can be computed by the following formula.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f
be a multiplicative function (not identically zero) and
\beta*f
denote the Dirichlet convolution of
\beta
with
f
. Then
\beta*f
is the multiplicative function defined on prime powers by

(\beta*f)(p^{r})=\begin{cases}f(p)-2,&\text{if }r=1,\\
f(p^{r})-2f(p^{r-1})+f(p^{r-2})&\text{if }r\geq 2.\end{cases}

This formula follows directly from the definition of
\beta
given in Lemma
2.3
.

3.1.
Bounding
A_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)

Recall that

(3.2)

A_{1}(m,N,k)=\chi_{0}(\sqrt{m})\frac{k-1}{12}\psi(N)m^{k/2-1},

where

(3.3)

\psi(N)=N\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right).

Observe that as a function of
N
,
A_{1}(m,N,k)
is a multiple of the multiplicative function
\psi(N)
. We now use Lemma
3.1
to give a lower bound on the convolution
\beta*\psi
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\psi^{\textnormal{new}}:=\beta*\psi
.
Then

(3.4)

\displaystyle\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(N)\geq\frac{N}{\pi_{1}(N)},

where

(3.5)

\pi_{1}(N)=\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1+\frac{p+1}{p^{2}-p-1}\right).

Proof.

Let
p\mid N
be prime. Applying Lemma
3.1
to
\psi^{\textnormal{new}}
yields

(3.6)

\displaystyle\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(p)

\displaystyle=\psi(p)-2=p-1,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(p^{2})

\displaystyle=\psi(p^{2})-2\psi(p)+1=p^{2}-p-1

(3.8)

\displaystyle\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(p^{r})

\displaystyle=\psi(p^{r})-2\psi(p^{r-1})+\psi(p^{r-2})

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\left(p^{r}-2p^{r-1}+p^{r-2}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)

(3.10)

\displaystyle=p^{r}-p^{r-1}-p^{r-2}+p^{r-3}\qquad\text{for }r\geq 3.

Observe that in each of these three cases,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(p^{r})

\displaystyle\geq p^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)=\frac{p^{r}}{\left(\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-p-1}\right)}=\frac{p^{r}}{\left(1+\frac{p+1}{p^{2}-p-1}\right)},

verifying the desired result.
∎

We can then employ (
3.2
) to write
A_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
as

(3.12)

\displaystyle A_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{M\mid N}\beta\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)\cdot A_{1}(m,M,k)

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\chi_{0}(\sqrt{m})\frac{k-1}{12}\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(N)m^{k/2-1}.

3.2.
Bounding
A_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)

Next, recall that

(3.14)

A_{2}(m,N,k)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t^{2}<4m}U_{k-1}(t,m)\sum_{n}h_{w}\left(\frac{t^{2}-4m}{n^{2}}\right)\mu_{t,n,m}(N).

Observe that
A_{2}(m,N,k)
is a linear combination of the multiplicative functions
\mu_{t,n,m}
.
We can then use Lemma
3.1
to bound the convolution
\beta*\mu_{t,n,m}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
m\geq 1
and
t,n
be as in (
2.8
). Define
\mu^{\textnormal{new}}_{t,n,m}:=\beta*\mu_{t,n,m}
. Then for
N

m
,

\left\lvert\mu^{\textnormal{new}}_{t,n,m}(N)\right\lvert\leq 2^{\omega(N)}\psi(n)\,2^{\omega(4m-t^{2})}\sqrt{4m-t^{2}}.

Here,
\omega(N)
denotes the number of distinct prime divisors of
N
.

Proof.

Recall from (
2.8
) that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\mu_{t,n,m}(N)=\frac{\psi(N)}{\psi(N/\gcd(N,n))}\nu(N)\qquad\text{with}\qquad\nu(N)=\mathop{\mathchoice{{\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}\kern-2.80556pt}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\mod N\end{subarray}}}}{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\mod N\end{subarray}}}{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\mod N\end{subarray}}}{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\mod N\end{subarray}}}}\nolimits 1,

where the sum for
\nu(N)
ranges over all
c\in(\ZZ/N\ZZ)^{\times}
that lift to a solution of the polynomial
x^{2}-tx+m\equiv 0\mod N\cdot\gcd(N,n)
.

We now prove the desired bounds for
\mu^{\textnormal{new}}_{t,n,m}
. Let
D:=t^{2}-4m
be the discriminant of
x^{2}-tx+m
. We will show that for each prime

,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mu^{\textnormal{new}}_{t,n,m}(p^{r})\right\lvert

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}2&\text{if }p\nmid m,\ p\nmid D,\\
4p^{v_{p}(D)/2}\psi(p^{v_{p}(n)})&\text{if }p\nmid m,\ p\mid D.\end{cases}

First, consider the case when
p\nmid m,\ p\nmid D
. Since
n^{2}\mid D
, we also have
p\nmid n
, and so
\gcd(p^{r},n)=1
. This means that
\frac{\psi(p^{r})}{\psi(p^{r}/\gcd(p^{r},n))}=\frac{\psi(p^{r})}{\psi(p^{r})}=1
. Additionally, observe that since
p\nmid m
, every solution to
x^{2}-tx+m\equiv 0\mod p^{r}
will necessarily be a unit modulo
p^{r}
.
Thus,
\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{r})=\nu(p^{r})
is precisely the number of solutions to the equation
x^{2}-tx+m\equiv 0\mod p^{r}
. And since
p\nmid D
, we have by Hensel’s Lemma
[
8
, Chapter 2, Section 2, Proposition 2]
that
\nu(p^{r})=\nu(p)
for all
r\geq 1
. Additionally, we have
\nu(p)\leq 2
since
x^{2}-tx+m
is quadratic.

Combining these observations, we obtain by Lemma
3.1
,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mu^{\textnormal{new}}_{t,n,m}(p)\right\lvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\mu_{t,n,m}(p)-2\right\lvert=\left\lvert\nu(p)-2\right\lvert\leq 2,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mu^{\textnormal{new}}_{t,n,m}(p^{2})\right\lvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{2})-2\mu_{t,n,m}(p)+1\right\lvert=\left\lvert\nu(p)-2\nu(p)+1\right\lvert\leq 1,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mu^{\textnormal{new}}_{t,n,m}(p^{r})\right\lvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{r})-2\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{r-1})+\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{r-2})\right\lvert=\left\lvert\nu(p)-2\nu(p)+\nu(p)\right\lvert=0\quad\text{for }r\geq 3.

This verifies the first case of (
3.16
).

Next, consider the case of
p\nmid m,\ p\mid D
. Then

(3.20)

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(p^{r})}{\psi(p^{r}/\gcd(p^{r},n))}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\psi(p^{r})}{\psi(1)}&\text{if }r\leq v_{p}(n)\\
\frac{\psi(p^{r})}{\psi(p^{r-v_{p}(n)})}&\text{if }r>v_{p}(n)\end{cases}

(3.21)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\psi(p^{r})\qquad\ \ \,&\text{if }r\leq v_{p}(n)\\
p^{v_{p}(n)}\qquad\ \ \,&\text{if }r>v_{p}(n)\end{cases}

(3.22)

\displaystyle\leq\psi(p^{v_{p}(n)}).

Also, observe that
\nu(p^{r})
will be bounded by the number of solutions to
x^{2}-tx+m\equiv 0\mod p^{r}
. We have from Huxley
[
6
, Page 194]
that the equation
x^{2}-tx+m\equiv 0\mod p^{r}
has at most
2p^{v_{p}(D)/2}
solutions. Thus

(3.23)

\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{r})=\frac{\psi(p^{r})}{\psi(p^{r}/\gcd(p^{r},n))}\nu(N)\leq\psi(p^{v_{p}(n)})\cdot 2p^{v_{p}(D)/2}.

This yields

(3.24)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mu^{\textnormal{new}}_{t,n,m}(p^{r})\right\lvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{r})-2\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{r-1})+\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{r-2})\right\lvert

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left(\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{r})+\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{r-2}),\ 2\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{r-1})\right)

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\psi(p^{v_{p}(n)})\cdot 2p^{v_{p}(D)/2},

where we interpret
\mu_{t,n,m}(p^{r-2})
here as
0
if
r=1
. This verifies the second case of (
3.16
).

Then from (
3.16
), for
N
coprime to
m
,

\mu^{\textnormal{new}}_{t,n,m}(N)\leq 2^{\omega(N)}2^{\omega(D)}\psi(n)\sqrt{\left\lvert D\right\lvert},

as desired.
∎

We can then use this lemma to determine the asymptotic behavior of
A_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
. Keep in mind that throughout this entire paper, all big-O notation is with respect to
N
and
k
(
m
is just a fixed constant).

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
m\geq 1
be fixed, and consider
N\geq 1
coprime to
m
and
k\geq 2
even.
Then

A_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=O(m^{k/2}2^{\omega(N)}).

Proof.

From (
2.8
) and (
2.16
),

(3.27)

\displaystyle A_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{M\mid N}\beta\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)\sum_{t^{2}<4m}\sum_{n}U_{k-1}(t,m)h_{w}\left(\frac{t^{2}-4m}{n^{2}}\right)\mu_{t,n,m}(M)

(3.28)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t^{2}<4m}\sum_{n}U_{k-1}(t,m)h_{w}\left(\frac{t^{2}-4m}{n^{2}}\right)\mu_{t,n,m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N).

Using the facts that
\left\lvert\rho\right\lvert=\sqrt{m}
and
\left\lvert\rho-\bar{\rho}\right\lvert=\sqrt{4m-t^{2}}
, where
\rho,\bar{\rho}
are the roots of
x^{2}-tx+m=0
, we have by the definition of
U_{k-1}
from Lemma
2.2
that

(3.29)

\left\lvert U_{k-1}(t,m)\right\lvert=\left\lvert\frac{\rho^{k-1}-\bar{\rho}^{k-1}}{\rho-\bar{\rho}}\right\lvert\leq\frac{\left\lvert\rho^{k-1}\right\lvert+\left\lvert\bar{\rho}^{k-1}\right\lvert}{\left\lvert\rho-\bar{\rho}\right\lvert}=\frac{2m^{(k-1)/2}}{\sqrt{4m-t^{2}}}.

So by Lemma
3.3
,

(3.30)

|U_{k-1}(t,m)\cdot\mu_{t,n,m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N)|\leq 2m^{(k-1)/2}\cdot 2^{\omega(N)}\cdot\psi(n)\cdot 2^{\omega(4m-t^{2})}.

Thus,

(3.31)

\displaystyle|A_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t^{2}<4m}\sum_{n}|U_{k-1}(t,m)|\cdot h_{w}\left(\frac{t^{2}-4m}{n^{2}}\right)|\mu_{t,n,m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N)|

(3.32)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t^{2}<4m}\sum_{n}h_{w}\left(\frac{t^{2}-4m}{n^{2}}\right)2m^{(k-1)/2}\cdot 2^{\omega(N)}\cdot\psi(n)\cdot 2^{\omega(4m-t^{2})}

(3.33)

\displaystyle=m^{(k-1)/2}2^{\omega(N)}\sum_{t^{2}<4m}2^{\omega(4m-t^{2})}\sum_{n}h_{w}\left(\frac{t^{2}-4m}{n^{2}}\right)\psi(n)

(3.34)

\displaystyle=O\left(m^{k/2}2^{\omega(N)}\right),

as desired.
∎

3.3.
Bounding
A_{3}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)

Next, recall that

(3.35)

A_{3}(m,N,k)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d|m}\min(d,m/d)^{k-1}\sum_{\tau}\phi(\gcd(\tau,N/\tau)),

where the inner summation runs over
\tau\mid N
such that
\gcd(\tau,N/\tau)\mid(d-m/d)
.
Now denote this sum as

(3.36)

\Sigma_{m,d}(N):=\sum_{\tau}\phi(\gcd(\tau,N/\tau)).

Observe that
A_{3}(m,N,k)
is a linear combination of the
\Sigma_{m,d}
. We now show that these
\Sigma_{m,d}
are multiplicative and use Lemma
3.1
to bound the convolution
\beta*\Sigma_{m,d}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
m,d\geq 1
with
d\mid m
,
h:=|d-\frac{m}{d}|
, and
\Sigma_{m,d}(N)
be defined according to (
3.36
). Then
\Sigma_{m,d}
is multiplicative. Furthermore, define
\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}:=\beta*\Sigma_{m,d}
. Then
\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}
is bounded by

(3.37)

|\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(N)|\leq\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\pi_{2}(N)^{2}}&\text{if }h=0,\\
h\cdot 4^{\omega(h)}&\text{if }h\neq 0.\\
\end{cases}

Here,

\pi_{2}(N)
is the multiplicative function defined as

(3.38)

\displaystyle\pi_{2}(N):=\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1+\frac{1}{p-1}\right).

Proof.

Let
L
and
M
be coprime. Note that if
\tau\mid L
and
\rho\mid M
, then
\gcd(\tau,L/\tau)
and
\gcd(\rho,M/\rho)
are coprime, and moreover
\gcd(\tau\rho,LM/\tau\rho)=\gcd(\tau,L/\tau)\cdot\gcd(\rho,M/\rho)
. Thus,

(3.39)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m,d}(L)\Sigma_{m,d}(M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\mid L\\
(\tau,L/\tau)\mid h\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\mid M\\
(\rho,M/\rho)\mid h\end{subarray}}\phi(\gcd(\tau,L/\tau))\phi(\gcd(\rho,M/\rho))

(3.40)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\mid L,\ \rho\mid M\\
(\tau,L/\tau)\mid h,\ (\rho,L/\rho)\mid h\end{subarray}}\phi(\gcd(\tau,L/\tau)\gcd(\rho,L/\rho))

(3.41)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\rho\mid LM\\
(\tau\rho,LM/\tau\rho)\mid h\end{subarray}}\phi(\gcd(\tau\rho,LM/\tau\rho))

(3.42)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{m,d}(LM).

This proves that
\Sigma_{m,d}
is multiplicative. We can then define
\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}:=\beta*\Sigma_{m,d}
. We divide the remaining proof into the case of
h=0
and the case of
h\neq 0
.

(1) First, suppose
h=0
.
Then

(3.43)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\mid p^{r}\\
(\tau,\,p^{r}/\tau)\mid 0\end{subarray}}\phi(\gcd(\tau,p^{r}/\tau))

(3.44)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq s\leq r}\phi(\gcd(p^{s},p^{r-s}))

(3.45)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2\cdot\sum_{0\leq s\leq r/2}\phi(p^{s})-\phi(p^{r/2})&\text{if }r\text{ is even},\\
2\cdot\sum_{0\leq s\leq(r-1)/2}\phi(p^{s})&\text{if }r\text{ is odd},\\
\end{cases}

(3.46)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2p^{r/2}-\phi(p^{r/2})&\text{if }r\text{ is even},\\
2p^{(r-1)/2}&\text{if }r\text{ is odd}.\\
\end{cases}

For the last step, we used the well-known formula
\sum_{d\mid N}\phi(d)=N
.

We can now compute
\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(p^{r})
explicitly. By
Lemma
3.1

and (
3.46
),

(3.47)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(p)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{m,d}(p)-2=2-2=0,

(3.48)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(p^{2})

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{2})-2\Sigma_{m,d}(p)+1

(3.49)

\displaystyle=2p-\phi(p)-2\cdot 2+1

(3.50)

\displaystyle=p-2.

For
r\geq 3
odd,

(3.51)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(p^{r})

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r})-2\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r-1})+\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r-2})

(3.52)

\displaystyle=2p^{(r-1)/2}-2\left(2p^{(r-1)/2}-\phi(p^{(r-1)/2})\right)+2p^{(r-3)/2}

(3.53)

\displaystyle=2p^{(r-1)/2}-2\left(p^{(r-1)/2}+p^{(r-3)/2}\right)+2p^{(r-3)/2}

(3.54)

\displaystyle=0.

For
r\geq 3
even,

(3.55)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(p^{r})

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r})-2\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r-1})+\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r-2})

(3.56)

\displaystyle=2p^{r/2}-p^{r/2-1}(p-1)-2\cdot 2p^{r/2-1}+2p^{r/2-1}-p^{r/2-2}(p-1)

(3.57)

\displaystyle=p^{r/2-2}\left(2p^{2}-p^{2}+p-4p+2p-p+1\right)

(3.58)

\displaystyle=p^{r/2}\left(\frac{p^{2}-2p+1}{p^{2}}\right)

(3.59)

\displaystyle=p^{r/2}\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{2}.

To summarize, when
h=0
,

(3.60)

\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(p^{r})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }r\text{ is odd},\\
p-2&\text{if }r=2,\\
p^{r/2}\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{2}&\text{if }r\geq 4\text{ is even.}\\
\end{cases}\\

Observe that in each of these cases,

(3.61)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(p^{r})

\displaystyle\leq p^{r/2}\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{2}=\frac{p^{r/2}}{\left(1+\frac{1}{p-1}\right)^{2}},

which yields

(3.62)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(N)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\pi_{2}(N)^{2}},

as desired.

(2) Next, consider the case of
h\neq 0
. For
p\nmid h
and
r\geq 1
,

(3.63)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau}\phi(\gcd(\tau,p^{r}/\tau))

(3.64)

\displaystyle=2\cdot\phi(\gcd(1,p^{r}))

(3.65)

\displaystyle=2,

so by Lemma
3.1
,

(3.66)

\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(p^{r})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }r=1,\\
-1&\text{if }r=2,\\
0&\text{if }r\geq 3.\\
\end{cases}

For
p\mid h
,

(3.67)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau}\phi(\gcd(\tau,p^{r}/\tau))

(3.68)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s\leq r\\
\gcd(p^{s},p^{r-s})\mid h\end{subarray}}\phi(\gcd(p^{s},p^{r-s}))

(3.69)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{0\leq s\leq v_{p}(h)}\phi(p^{s})

(3.70)

\displaystyle=2p^{v_{p}(h)}.

Then by Lemma
3.1
,

(3.71)

\displaystyle|\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(p^{r})|

\displaystyle=|\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r})-2\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r-1})+\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r-2})|

(3.72)

\displaystyle\leq\max(\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r})+\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r-2}),\ 2\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r-1}))

(3.73)

\displaystyle\leq 2p^{v_{p}(h)}+2p^{v_{p}(h)}

(3.74)

\displaystyle=4p^{v_{p}(h)},

where we interpret
\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{r-2})
here as 0 if
r=1
. It immediately follows from (
3.66
) and (
3.74
) that when
h\neq 0
and
N
is coprime to
m
,

(3.75)

\displaystyle|\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(N)|

\displaystyle\leq h\cdot 4^{\omega(h)},

as desired.
∎

We now use this lemma to determine the asymptotic behavior of
A_{3}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
m\geq 1
be fixed, and consider
N\geq 1
coprime to
m
, and
k\geq 2
even. Then

(3.76)

A_{3}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=\begin{cases}O\left(\frac{m^{k/2}\sqrt{N}}{\pi_{2}(N)^{2}}\right)&\text{if }m\text{ is a perfect square,}\\
O\left(m^{k/2}\right)&\text{if }m\text{ is not a perfect square.}\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
\min(d,m/d)^{k-1}\leq m^{(k-1)/2}
,

(3.77)

\displaystyle|A_{3}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)|

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\frac{1}{2}\sum_{M\mid N}\beta\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)\sum_{d|m}\min(d,m/d)^{k-1}\Sigma_{m,d}(M)\right\lvert

(3.78)

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d|m}\min(d,m/d)^{k-1}\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(N)\right\lvert

(3.79)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}m^{(k-1)/2}\sum_{d|m}\left\lvert\Sigma_{m,d}^{\textnormal{new}}(N)\right\lvert.

The desired result then follows immediately from Lemma
3.5
(since
h=0
only for
d=\sqrt{m}
, which requires
m
to be a perfect square).
∎

3.4.
Bounding
A_{4}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)

For
t\geq 0
, we use the notation
\sigma_{t}(m)=\sum_{d\mid m}d^{t}
. Then since
N
is coprime to
m
,

(3.80)

A_{4}(m,N,k)=\begin{cases}\sigma_{1}(m)&\text{if $k=2$}\\
0&\text{if $k>2$}.\\
\end{cases}\\

Observe that
A_{4}(m,N,k)
is a multiple of the constant multiplicative function
\mathbf{1}(N)=1
. The following Lemma then follows immediately from Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Define the multiplicative function
\mathbf{1}^{\textnormal{new}}:=\beta*\mathbf{1}
. Then
\left\lvert\mathbf{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(N)\right\lvert\leq 1
.

This lemma then yields

(3.81)

|A_{4}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)|\leq\sigma_{1}(m)=O(1).

(Remember that we are using big-O notation with respect to
N
and
k
.)

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
for trivial character

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
for trivial character. First, we define the functions
\theta_{i}(N)
which will be used to express the error terms for certain trace estimates.

Lemma 4.1
.

Define

(4.1)

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(N)\pi_{2}(N)^{2}},\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\theta_{2}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{4^{\omega(N)}}{\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(N)},

\displaystyle\theta_{3}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\omega(N)}}{\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(N)},\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\theta_{4}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(N)}.

Then each
\theta_{i}(N)\longrightarrow 0
as
N\longrightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

Recall that
\pi_{1}(N)=\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1+\frac{p+1}{p^{2}-p-1}\right)
and
\pi_{2}(N)=\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1+\frac{1}{p-1}\right)
. Now, observe that
\frac{1}{3}\pi_{1}(N),\pi_{2}(N)\leq 2^{\omega(N)}=O(N^{\varepsilon})
for any
\varepsilon>0

[
5
, Sections 18.1, 22.13]
.
Thus since
\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(N)\geq\frac{N}{\pi_{1}(N)}
by Lemma
3.2
, we have that
\theta_{1}(N)=O(N^{-1/2+\varepsilon})\longrightarrow 0
as
N\longrightarrow\infty
, and for
i\in\{2,3,4\}
,
\theta_{i}(N)=O(N^{-1+\varepsilon})\longrightarrow 0
as
N\longrightarrow\infty
.
∎

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
for trivial character will be divided into two cases: when
m
is not a perfect square, and when
m
is a perfect square. First, we present two lemmas which estimate
\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
m
fixed not be a perfect square, and consider
N\geq 1
coprime to
m
and
k\geq 2
even. Then

\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k)=O(2^{\omega(N)}m^{k/2}).

Proof.

We consider each of the
A_{i}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
terms from (
2.15
) separately. First, since
m
is not a perfect square,
A_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=0
.
Next, by Corollary
3.4
,

A_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=O\left(m^{k/2}2^{\omega(N)}\right).

By Corollary
3.6
, since
m
is not a perfect square,

A_{3}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=O\left(m^{k/2}\right).

And from (
3.81
),
A_{4}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=O(1)
. Thus,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k)

\displaystyle=A_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)-A_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)-A_{3}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)+A_{4}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)

(4.3)

\displaystyle=O\left(m^{k/2}2^{\omega(N)}\right)+O\left(m^{k/2}\right)+O(1)

(4.4)

\displaystyle=O\left(m^{k/2}2^{\omega(N)}\right),

completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
m
fixed be a perfect square, and consider
N\geq 1
coprime to
m
and
k\geq 2
even. Then

\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k)=\frac{k-1}{12}\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(N)m^{k/2-1}+O\left(\frac{m^{k/2}\sqrt{N}}{\pi_{2}(N)^{2}}\right).

Proof.

First, by (
3.13
),

A_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=\chi_{0}(\sqrt{m})\frac{k-1}{12}\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(N)m^{k/2-1}=\frac{k-1}{12}\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(N)m^{k/2-1}.

Next, as in Lemma
4.2
, we have the bounds
A_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=O\left(2^{\omega(N)}m^{k/2}\right)
and

A_{4}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=O(1)
. Additionally, by Corollary
3.6
,

A_{3}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=O\left(\frac{m^{k/2}\sqrt{N}}{\pi_{2}(N)^{2}}\right)

since
m
is a perfect square.

Thus by the trace formula,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k)

\displaystyle=A_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)-A_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)-A_{3}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)+A_{4}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{k-1}{12}\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(N)m^{k/2-1}+O\left(2^{\omega(N)}m^{k/2}\right)+O\left(\frac{m^{k/2}\sqrt{N}}{\pi_{2}(N)^{2}}\right)+O(1)

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{k-1}{12}\psi^{\textnormal{new}}(N)m^{k/2-1}+O\left(\frac{m^{k/2}\sqrt{N}}{\pi_{2}(N)^{2}}\right),

completing the proof.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.1
for trivial character in two separate cases. Proposition
4.4
addresses the case when
m
is not a perfect square, and Proposition
4.5
addresses the case when
m
is a perfect square.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
m
be fixed and not a perfect square, and consider
N\geq 1
coprime to
m
and
k\geq 2
even. Then
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)<0
for all but finitely many pairs
(N,k)
.

Proof.

Recall from Lemma
2.1
that

(4.8)

\displaystyle a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}\right)^{2}-\sum_{d\mid m}d^{k-1}\Tr T_{m^{2}/d^{2}}^{\textnormal{new}}\right].

Since
m^{2}/d^{2}
is a perfect square, we can employ Lemma
4.3
on the
T^{\textnormal{new}}_{m^{2}/d^{2}}
terms in (
4.8
) and obtain

(4.9)

6.
Extending to the General Character Case

Now, we would like to extend our results to the case of general character.
But from
[
12
, Proposition 6.1]
,
\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=0
for the infinite family of triples
(N,k,\chi)
where
2\mid{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
and
2\parallel N/{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)
.
This means that
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
trivially vanishes for infinitely many
(N,k,\chi)
.
However, if we only consider nontrivial vanishing of
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
, then we are able to extend our result. In particular, for any given
m
, consider
N\geq 1
coprime to
m
,
k\geq 2
, and
\chi
a Dirichlet character modulo
N
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. Then we show that
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)
nontrivially vanishes for only finitely many triples
(N,k,\chi)
, proving Theorem
1.1
for general character.

Recall the Eichler-Selberg trace formula from Lemma
2.2
,

(6.1)

\Tr T_{m}(N,k,\chi)=A_{1}(m,N,k,\chi)-A_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)-A_{3}(m,N,k,\chi)+A_{4}(m,N,k,\chi).

Additionally, recall from Lemma
2.3
that the trace of
T_{m}^{{\textnormal{new}}}(N,k,\chi)
is given by

(6.2)

\Tr T_{m}^{{\textnormal{new}}}(N,k,\chi)=\sum_{\mathfrak{f}(\chi)\mid M\mid N}\beta\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)\cdot\Tr T_{m}(M,k,\chi).

The main difference between the general character case and the trivial character case is that this summation no longer takes the form of a Dirichlet convolution. This means that in particular, we can no longer easily write
\Tr T_{m}^{{\textnormal{new}}}(N,k,\chi)
as a linear combination of convolutions of the form
\beta*f
. However, we can still bound each of the terms in (
6.2
) separately. This rougher bound will suffice for our purposes.

Just like in the trivial character case, we define

(6.3)

A_{i}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi):=\sum_{{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)\mid M\mid N}\beta\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)\cdot A_{i}(m,M,k,\chi).

For a positive integer
g
, we also define

(6.4)

\psi_{g}^{\textnormal{new}}(N):=\sum_{g\mid M\mid N}\beta\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)\cdot\psi(M),

so that

(6.5)

A_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k,\chi)=\chi(\sqrt{m})\frac{k-1}{12}m^{k/2-1}\psi_{{\mathfrak{f}}(\chi)}^{\textnormal{new}}(N).

Then in a manner similar to Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
, we can determine the asymptotic behavior of
\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
m
fixed not be a perfect square, and consider

m
,

, and

\displaystyle\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k,\chi)=O\left(m^{k/2}4^{\omega(N)}\sigma_{0}(N)\right).

First,
A_{1}(m,N,k,\chi)=0

is not a perfect square.

Second, we show that
A_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)=O(m^{k/2}2^{\omega(N)})

notation.
Also, by (

(6.8)

\displaystyle A_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t^{2}<4m}\sum_{n}U_{k-1}(t,m)h_{w}\left(\frac{t^{2}-4m}{n^{2}}\right)\mu_{t,n,m}(N)

Third, we show that

\displaystyle A_{3}(m,N,k,\chi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d|m}\min(d,m/d)^{k-1}\mspace{-40mu}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\mid N\\

(6.12)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\mid N\\

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}O\left(2^{\omega(N)}\right)&\text{if }d-m/d\neq 0,\\

\displaystyle A_{3}(m,N,k,\chi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d|m}\min(d,m/d)^{k-1}\mspace{-40mu}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\mid N\\

Fourth, we observe from (

Finally, we determine the asymptotics of the

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\Tr T_{m}^{{\textnormal{new}}}\right)^{2}-\sum_{d\mid m}\chi(d)d^{k-1}\Tr T_{m^{2}/d^{2}}^{{\textnormal{new}}}\right]

\psi_{g}^{\textnormal{new}}(N)\geq\frac{N}{\pi_{3}(N)}

7.
Discussion

In this section, we discuss some motivation for the study of
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
, as well as potential future work.

As noted previously, we observe that the second coefficients
a_{2}(m,N,k)
and
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
seem to be easier to study than the first coefficients
a_{1}(m,N,k)
and
a_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
. This is due in a large part to the fact that we can estimate the growth of
a_{2}(m,N,k)
and
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
(e.g. (
4.13
), (
4.20
)), but
a_{1}(m,N,k)=\Tr T_{m}(N,k)
and
a_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k)
do not seem to follow any sort of asymptotic behavior.
This observation has several potential applications.
Traces of Hecke operators have been used in the past to prove several interesting results about modular forms, and in certain of these scenarios, the second coefficient can be used instead of the trace to obtain stronger results. For example, in
[
18
]
, Vilardi and Xue used the non-repetition of
\Tr T_{2}(1,k)
in order to prove that, assuming Maeda’s conjecture, Hecke eigenforms can be distinguished by their
2
nd Fourier coefficient. More recently, Clayton et. al.
[
2
]
instead studied the non-repetition of
a_{2}(m,N,k)
. They were able to use these non-repetition results for
a_{2}(4,1,k)
in order to prove that, assuming Maeda’s conjecure, Hecke eigenforms can also be distinguished by their
4
th Fourier coefficient. For any given
m\geq 2
, they also provided a general strategy to prove the same result for the
m
-th Fourier coefficient.

Additionally, recall that the classical Lehmer conjecture predicts the non-vanishing of
\tau(m)=\Tr T_{m}(1,12)
. In order to generalize this conjecture to higher levels and weights, Rouse proposed the “generalized Lehmer conjecture”
[
15
, Conjecture 1.5]
, predicting that for any non-square
m
,
\Tr T_{m}(N,k)\neq 0
for all
N
coprime to
m
and even
k\geq 12
,
k\neq 14
.
We note that since we also have
\tau(m)=\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(1,12)
, one could also attempt to generalize the Lehmer conjecture to newspaces of higher level and weight.
However, this generalization turns out to not be true; we were able to find several families of
m,N,k
for which
\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k)
vanishes
[
13
]
. In future work, we plan to investigate various cases in which this occurs. At a minimum, we propose the following conjecture, based on our numerical computations.

Conjecture 7.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
be a fixed non-square. Then there exist
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
coprime to
m
such that
\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N_{1},k)=0
for all even
k\geq 2
and
\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N_{2},k)\neq 0
for all even
k\geq 2
.

Like the generalized Lehmer conjecture from Rouse, determining precisely when
\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k)
vanishes seems to be a rather difficult problem. However, as the content of this paper shows, we are actually able to obtain results if we consider the second coefficient
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
instead of the first coefficient
a_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
. (And in fact we can even generalize slightly to general character.) One of our goals is that further study of these other coefficients
a_{j}(m,N,k)
and
a_{j}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
will lead to ideas and strategies to answer questions about the behavior of the first coefficient
a_{1}(m,N,k)=\Tr T_{m}(N,k)
and
a_{1}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)=\Tr T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k)
. Similarly, we are also interested in answering questions about the behavior of the last coefficient
a_{\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))}(m,N,k)
and
a_{\dim S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
.
This is just the determinant of
T_{m}(N,k)
and
T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k)
, so any results concerning the behavior of this last coefficient would be very valuable in understanding the Hecke operators in general.
Much more work and investigation remains to be completed in this direction.

Now, the results of
[
14
]
show that the second coefficient
a_{2}(m,N,k)
(over
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
) is biased to be positive for square
m
and negative for non-square
m
. Moreover, this paper establishes that the same bias holds when one restricts to the newspace
S_{k}^{\textnormal{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
. One might then ask if the second coefficient exhibits a similar behavior for other restrictions. For example, these two results would lead one to intuitively expect the same result on the old space
S_{k}^{\text{old}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
. However, this needs to be proven and made more precise. For another example, one could also ask the same question for the restrictions to the subspaces determined by Atkin-Lehner sign pattern
S_{k}^{{\textnormal{new}},\varepsilon_{M}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))

[
10
]
.

Lastly, we note that the second coefficient is closely related to the second moment, and hence the quadratic mean, of the eigenvalues. Using the techniques developed to study
a_{2}(m,N,k)
and
a_{2}^{\textnormal{new}}(m,N,k)
, we were also able to determine the average size of the eigenvalues of the Hecke operators (measured via the quadratic mean)
[
1
]
. Using a similar strategy, further work can be done to similarly compute the average size of the eigenvalues over
S_{k}^{{\textnormal{new}},\varepsilon_{M}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
and other subspaces. Additionally, further investigation could be done to compute other moments of the eigenvalues of the Hecke operators
T_{m}(N,k)
and
T_{m}^{\textnormal{new}}(N,k)
.

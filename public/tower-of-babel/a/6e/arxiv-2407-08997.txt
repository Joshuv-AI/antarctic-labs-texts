Asymptotic Expansions for Semilinear Waves on Asymptotically Flat Spacetimes
Sam Looi, Haoren Xiong

Abstract
. We establish precise asymptotic expansions for solutions to semilinear wave equations with power-type nonlinearities on asymptotically flat spacetimes. Our analysis focuses on two key cases: cubic nonlinearities and higher-order power nonlinearities. For cubic nonlinearities of the form a ⁡ ( t , x ) ​ ϕ 3 a(t,x)\,\phi^{3} , we prove asymptotic expansions for the solution globally in the spacetime. In the special case of compact spatial regions, solutions exhibit the asymptotic behavior ϕ ⁡ ( t , x ) = c ​ t − 2 + 𝒪 ⁡ ( t − 3 + ) \phi(t,x)=c\,t^{-2}+\mathcal{O}(t^{-3+}) . For higher-order nonlinearities a ⁡ ( t , x ) ​ ϕ p a(t,x)\,\phi^{p} with p ≥ 4 p\geq 4 , we prove the solution satisfies ϕ ⁡ ( t , x ) = d ​ t − 3 + 𝒪 ⁡ ( t − 4 + ) \phi(t,x)=d\,t^{-3}+\mathcal{O}(t^{-4+}) , thereby extending the classical Price’s law (a late-time tail postulated in 1972) to nonlinear settings in a precise fashion. These results sharpen previous decay estimates for nonlinear waves. We develop a radiation field expansion and a low-energy resolvent expansion adapted to conormal asymptotic inputs, extending Hintz’s approach for linear waves to the semilinear setting. Our methods connect geometric microlocal analysis (b-calculus) with classical physical-space techniques, providing a convenient tool for analyzing asymptotic behavior of nonlinear waves.



1.
Introduction

We study late-time asymptotics for semilinear wave equations on stationary, asymptotically flat spacetimes. The goal is to go beyond decay rates and obtain asymptotic expansions, including an explicit description of the leading tail and its coefficient. The analysis distinguishes the cubic case from higher powers, and it yields both spatially compact asymptotics and a global description on the resolved spacetime.

The geometric hypotheses are tailored to stationary black hole exteriors. The class of metrics in Definition
2.3
encodes the long-range
r^{-1}
correction to Minkowski determined by the mass parameter
m
; for Kerr one uses a minor variant, see
[
43
, Section 4.1]
. We also assume spectral admissibility (Definition
3.1
), namely mode stability and high-energy resolvent control. These assumptions hold for subextremal Kerr exteriors: mode stability is established in
[
118
,
96

, and the required resolvent bounds follow from microlocal estimates at the normally hyperbolic trapped set
[

,
29
,
30
]
together with radial point estimates at the horizons and at spatial infinity
[

Quantitative information about late-time tails in the exterior, in particular polynomial lower bounds along the event horizon, are a key input in the spherically symmetric strong cosmic censorship analysis: when propagated into the black hole interior, they lead to instability at the Cauchy horizon; see
[

Let the coordinate
t_{*}
be a globally defined time function that interpolates smoothly between the standard asymptotically flat time coordinate far from the black hole and a regular time coordinate near the event horizon. Specifically,
t_{*}
can be chosen such that

t_{*}\approx\begin{cases}t+r_{*}&\text{near the event horizon }r=2m,\\
t-r_{*}&\text{for large }r,\end{cases}

where
r_{*}=r+2m\log(r-2m)
is the tortoise coordinate. This coordinate is regular across the entire exterior spacetime while asymptotically matching standard coordinates. We remark that the gradient of
t_{*}
is everywhere timelike, so each level set
\Sigma_{t_{*}}
is spacelike.

We study the following initial value problem:

Problem 1.1

(Initial Value Problem)
.

Let
(M,g)

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\Box_{g}\phi=a(t_{*},x)\phi^{p},&\text{on }M,\\
\phi(0,x)=\phi_{0}(x),&\text{on }\Sigma_{0},\\
\partial_{t_{*}}\phi(0,x)=\phi_{1}(x),&\text{on }\Sigma_{0},\end{cases}

where
\Box_{g}
is the wave operator associated with the metric

,

is an integer, and
\Sigma_{0}=\{t_{*}=0\}
is the initial Cauchy hypersurface. The coefficient

is a symbol of order

Our main results can be summarized as follows: The main asymptotic regimes are different for
p=3
and for
p\geq 4
.

(1)

(Cubic case.) For
\Box_{g}\phi=a(t_{*},x)\phi^{3}
, we prove that
\phi
admits a full asymptotic description on the resolved spacetime and, in particular, that on any fixed compact set
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
one has

\phi(t_{*},x)=2c_{0}\,t_{*}^{-2}+\mathcal{O}(t_{*}^{-3+}),

with
c_{0}
given explicitly in terms of the initial data, the geometry, and the first radiation field. We prove complete asymptotics in the entire spacetime.

(2)

(Higher powers.) For
\Box_{g}\phi=b(t_{*},x)\phi^{p}
with
p\geq 4
, we show that on any compact set
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

\phi(t_{*},x)=d\,t_{*}^{-3}+\mathcal{O}(t_{*}^{-4+}),

where
d
is an explicit constant determined by the background metric, the nonlinearity, and radiation field data. This matches the
t_{*}^{-3}
Price tail on compact sets, while accounting for the nonlinear contribution to the coefficient.

Theorem
1.4
contains details about this constant. This result extends the classical linear Price’s law to nonlinearities of the above form.

Our method has two main components. First, we derive higher-order radiation field expansions at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
with conormal remainders, using available pointwise decay estimates for semilinear waves. Second, we extend low-energy resolvent expansions to inputs with these conormal asymptotics. The low-frequency structure of the resolvent produces the logarithmic terms that control the leading late-time tail and yields formulas for the leading coefficients.

We separate the asymptotic analysis from the existence theory. Throughout, we assume the forward solution exists globally in
t_{*}
. This holds in standard settings (e.g. defocusing nonlinearities, or small-data focusing problems). For convenience we recall in Appendix
A
the global existence results and sharp pointwise decay estimates from the literature that motivate the a priori bounds used in the main arguments.

Theorem 1.4

(Asymptotics for higher-order nonlinearities)

Assume that a global solution

Remark 1.5
.

We note that the leading order term
2d_{X}(\widehat{f}_{0})t_{*}^{-3}
vanishes trivially when restricted to the Minkowskian spacetime,
\Box\phi=b\phi^{p}

The proofs rely on a careful analysis of the radiation fields associated with the solutions and the Fourier transform to study the low-frequency behavior of the resolvent operator. The key ingredients include the derivation of high-order radiation field expansions, which allow us to identify the leading-order terms in the asymptotic behavior of the solutions, and the resolvent expansion with more general inputs than in
[
43
, Theorem 3.1]
, see Theorem
6.1
. We remark that these results sharpen previous decay estimates by
[

,
62
,

]
into precise asymptotics with explicit leading-order terms.

The present work builds on the linear analysis in
[
43
]
and on decay estimates for semilinear problems in
[

,
62
,
109
]
(though
[

]
also studied quasilinear problems). The additional ingredients needed for the nonlinear asymptotics are the following:

(1)

We show that the nonlinear forcing term
a(t_{*},x)\phi^{p}
admits a partial expansion near
\rho=0
with a conormal remainder. This uses higher-order radiation field expansions derived from pointwise decay bounds.

(2)

We extend the low-energy resolvent expansion of
[
43
]
to such conormal inputs. This yields the singular low-frequency terms responsible for the leading tail and provides computable formulas for the leading coefficients in the expansions.

(3)

We combine the resolvent expansion with the radiation field analysis to obtain complete leading-order asymptotics on
M_{+}
and, on compact sets, the
t_{*}^{-2}
tail for
p=3
and the
t_{*}^{-3}
tail for
p\geq 4
.

These novelties give insight into the analysis of more general nonlinear wave equations. While this paper focuses on power-type nonlinearities as a canonical example, the core of our methodology is robust: the systematic expansion of the low-energy resolvent and the derivation of high-order radiation fields using b-calculus. We expect that these techniques can be adapted to treat a broader class of nonlinear problems, such as those with derivative nonlinearities or more complex algebraic structures.

Potential future extensions:

(1)

Genericity of non-vanishing leading coefficient.
For the cubic nonlinearity, the explicit formula (
4.22
) for the coefficient
c_{0}
of the leading
t_{*}^{-2}
term along
\mathcal{K}^{+}
and
I^{+}
expresses
c_{0}
as a nonlinear functional of the radiation field
\phi_{\mathrm{rad},1}
and of the angular coefficients
c_{2},d_{1}
in the spatial asymptotics. Since
\phi_{\mathrm{rad},1}
is itself determined by the Cauchy data on
\Sigma
, a natural open problem is to understand genericity of the condition
c_{0}\neq 0
at the level of initial data, for instance by showing that in the class of small asymptotically flat data considered here the subset of data whose associated solution has
c_{0}\neq 0
is open and dense (or at least residual) in the corresponding topology.

(2)

Relation to strong cosmic censorship (SCC).
We work on stationary, asymptotically flat exteriors, impose Cauchy data supported away from the event horizon, and derive complete late-time asymptotics in this exterior region. We do not study horizon crossing or interior evolution, and we do not address the inextendibility statements required by strong cosmic censorship, which are potential extensions of our results. Nevertheless, exterior tails and quantitative decay along the event horizon are standard inputs in modern SCC analyses: they provide lower and upper bounds for horizon fluxes which, once propagated across the horizon, drive blow-up/instability at the Cauchy horizon in symmetry-reduced models; see the works
[
69
,
70
,
72
,
19
]
. In that sense, the present paper provides a refined exterior ingredient for a model problem in nonlinear wave equations that is relevant to SCC, as the explicit tail coefficients (such as
c_{0}
and
d_{X}(\widehat{f}_{0})
) and the detailed asymptotics at
\mathcal{K}^{+}
,
I^{+}
, and
\mathscr{I}^{+}
give more than just decay rates – they give a precise profile of the exterior field.

(3)

More general nonlinearities than the power-type.
In this paper we restrict to power-type nonlinearities
a(t_{*},x)\phi^{p}
with
p\geq 3
. The arguments only use that the nonlinearity is a fixed polynomial in
\phi
with
p\geq 3
and that the coefficients are sufficiently regular and slowly varying in
t_{*}
. It is natural to ask whether analogous asymptotics hold for semilinear equations

\Box_{g}\phi=\mathcal{N}(t_{*},x,\phi),

where
\mathcal{N}
is smooth in
(t_{*},x)
, at least
C^{\infty}
(or analytic) in
\phi
near
\phi=0
, and satisfies
\mathcal{N}(t_{*},x,0)=\partial_{\phi}\mathcal{N}(t_{*},x,0)=0
. The structure of the same proofs in this paper suggests that, in such a setting, the leading late-time behaviour on compact sets should again be governed by the first non-vanishing term in the Taylor expansion of
\mathcal{N}
in
\phi
(cubic versus
p\geq 4
), but we do not pursue this extension here. A further direction, also outside the scope of the present work, would be to allow dependence on derivatives of
\phi
, or even quasilinear problems in which the metric depends on the solution. This would require a further detailed calculation of the radiation fields and its interplay with the nonlinear structure. We also remark that our approach still applies while assuming weaker pointwise decay estimates of the solution, which is therefore expected to treat more general nonlinear wave equations.

1.2.
Organization

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the necessary geometric and analytic preliminaries. Section 3 is devoted to the study of the mapping properties of the resolvent operator. In Section 4, we derive the asymptotic expansions for the cubic nonlinearity in compact spatial sets before deriving global asymptotic expansions in Section 5. In Section 6, we prove long time asymptotic expansions in compact spatial sets for equations with the higher-order power nonlinearities. Appendix A presents pointwise decay estimates for solutions of power-nonlinearity wave equations on curved spacetimes. These results establish the hypotheses required for the theorems in the main text, at least in various special cases.

The compactified spatial manifold

X:=\overline{\mathbb{R}^{3}}=(\mathbb{R}^{3}\sqcup([0,\infty)_{\rho}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}))/\sim

is the radial compactification of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where the equivalence relation
\sim
identifies points
r\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
for
r>0
,
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
with
(\rho,\omega)
,
\rho=r^{-1}
.

It follows that the smooth functions on
X
are those functions in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
which in the region
\{r>1\}
are smooth in
\rho=r^{-1}
and the spherical variables. Near the boundary
\partial X\cong{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
, we shall work in the collar neighborhood
[0,\epsilon)_{\rho}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
,
\epsilon>0
. Throughout this paper, we use
\mathbb{S}^{2}
to denote the boundary of the spatial manifold
X
. Thus
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\partial X)
denotes the space of smooth functions on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. We primarily use
\mathbb{S}^{2}
notation, though
\partial X
can be substituted where
\mathbb{S}^{2}
appears.

We recall the definition of a scattering tangent bundle in order to introduce our assumptions on the metric.

Definition 2.2

(Scattering tangent bundle)
.

The scattering tangent bundle
{}^{\textrm{sc}}TX
is the unique vector bundle over
X
characterized as follows:

(1)

Its smooth sections over
X^{\circ}
are smooth vector fields
V
of the form

V=a\partial_{r}+r^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{3}b_{j}\Omega_{j}

in
(1,\infty)_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, where
a,b_{j}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
.
Here,
\{\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3}\}\subset\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
are the rotation vector fields that span
T_{p}\mathbb{S}^{2}
at each
p\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

(2)

In local coordinates
(\theta,\varphi)
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, a section
V
takes the form

V=a\partial_{r}+r^{-1}\tilde{b}_{1}\partial_{\theta}+r^{-1}\tilde{b}_{2}\partial_{\varphi}

where
\tilde{b}_{1},\tilde{b}_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
.

We make the same assumptions on the metric as in
[
43
]
:

Definition 2.3
.

A smooth Lorentzian metric
g
on
\mathbb{R}_{t_{*}}\times X^{\circ}
is called
stationary and asymptotically flat
(with mass
m\in\mathbb{R}
) if
\partial_{t_{*}}
is a Killing vector field, and if

(1)

dt_{*}
is everywhere future timelike, namely,
g^{00}<0
;

(2)

the coefficients of the dual metric

g^{-1}=g^{00}\partial_{t_{*}}^{2}+2\partial_{t_{*}}\otimes_{s}g^{0X}+g^{XX}

satisfy the following conditions, with
\rho=r^{-1}
,

\displaystyle g^{00}

\displaystyle\in\rho^{2}\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X),

\displaystyle g^{0X}

\displaystyle\in-\partial_{r}+\rho^{2}\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X;{}^{\textrm{sc}}TX),

\displaystyle g^{XX}

\displaystyle\in(1-2m\rho)\partial_{r}^{2}+\rho^{2}g_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}^{-1}+\rho^{2}\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X;S^{2}\,{}^{\textrm{sc}}TX).

Here
g_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}=d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2}
is the standard metric on
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
.

The concept of b-vector fields or b-differential operators is essential in our analysis, and we recall the following definition from
[
43
]
:

Definition 2.4
.

The space
\mathcal{V}_{\rm{b}}(X)
of

on

consists of all smooth vector fields
\Gamma_{\text{b}}
on

that are tangent to
\partial X\cong{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
. This means that
\Gamma_{\text{b}}=a\rho\partial_{\rho}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}b_{j}\Omega_{j}
in
(1,\infty)_{r}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
, with
a,b_{j}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)

For

let
\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{b}}^{m}(X)
denote the space of b-differential operators of order at most
m
. Explicitly,

(2.1)

\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{b}}^{m}(X)=\left\{\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(x)V_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots V_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}:a_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X),V_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{b}(X)\right\},

where
\mathcal{V}_{b}(X)
is the space of b-vector fields on
X
, and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})
is a multi-index with
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}\leq m
.

For a metric
g
given in Definition
2.3
, we can rewrite its corresponding wave operator:

\Box_{g}=-|g|^{-1/2}\partial_{\mu}(|g|^{1/2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu})

using b-differential operators as follows:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
g
be a stationary and asymptotically flat metric (see Definition
2.3
). Let
\Box
denote
\Box_{g}
. Then

\Box=\widehat{\Box}(0)-g^{00}\partial_{t_{*}}^{2}-2\rho\partial_{t_{*}}Q
with
\widehat{\Box}(0)\in\rho^{2}\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{b}}^{2}(X)
and
Q\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{b}}^{1}(X)
.
Near
\partial X=\{\rho=0\}
,

(2.2)

Q=Q_{0}+\rho^{2}\widetilde{Q},\quad Q_{0}=\rho\partial_{\rho}-1,\quad\widetilde{Q}\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{b}}^{1}(X);

(2.3)

\rho^{-2}\widehat{\Box}(0)=L_{0}+\rho L_{1}+\rho^{2}L_{2},\quad L_{2}\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{b}}^{2}(X);

(2.4)

L_{0}=-(\rho\partial_{\rho})^{2}+\rho\partial_{\rho}+\Delta_{\omega},

(2.5)

L_{1}=2m(\rho\partial_{\rho})^{2},

where
\Delta_{\omega}=-(\sin\theta)^{-1}\partial_{\theta}\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}-(\sin\theta)^{-2}\partial_{\varphi}^{2}
is the positive and spherical Laplacian.

We omit the proof here and refer the reader to
[
43
, Lemma 2.7]
.

We furnish the compactified space

with a volume density
|dg_{X}|
, which is related to the spacetime volume density
|dg|
by the equation
|dg|=|dg_{X}||dt_{*}|
. Here,
|dg|
is the natural volume density associated with the metric

. We define the corresponding

. This setting allows us to introduce the b-Sobolev spaces.

Definition 2.6
.

On the spatial manifold
X
, we introduce the following function spaces:

(1)

The
b-Sobolev spaces

H_{\text{b}}^{s}(X)
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
are defined as follows:

•

For
s=0
, set
H_{\text{b}}^{0}(X)=L^{2}(X)
.

•

For positive integers
s
, define
H_{\text{b}}^{s}(X)
as the set of all
u\in L^{2}(X)
such that
Au\in L^{2}(X)
for every
A\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{b}}^{s}(X)
.

We equip
H_{\text{b}}^{s}(X)
with the Hilbert space norm

\|u\|_{H_{\text{b}}^{s}(X)}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\sum_{k}\|A_{jk}u\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2},

where
\{A_{jk}\}
is a finite set of operators spanning
\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{b}}^{j}(X)
over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
.

•

For non-integer
s>0
, define
H_{\text{b}}^{s}(X)
by interpolation between the integer cases.

•

For
s<0
, define
H_{\text{b}}^{s}(X)
as the dual space of
H_{\text{b}}^{-s}(X)
.

(2)

For a small parameter
h>0
, we define the
semiclassical b-Sobolev spaces

H_{\text{b},h}^{s}(X)
. These share the same underlying vector space as
H_{\text{b}}^{s}(X)
but are equipped with the modified norm

\|u\|_{H_{\text{b},h}^{s}(X)}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\sum_{k}\|h^{j}A_{jk}u\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}.

This norm scales each b-derivative by a factor of
h
.

(3)

The
weighted b-Sobolev spaces

H_{\text{b}}^{s,\ell}(X)
and
H_{\text{b},h}^{s,\ell}(X)
for
s,\ell\in\mathbb{R}
are defined as

H_{\text{b}}^{s,\ell}(X)=\rho^{\ell}H_{\text{b}}^{s}(X),\quad H_{\text{b},h}^{s,\ell}(X)=\rho^{\ell}H_{\text{b},h}^{s}(X),

with respective norms

\|u\|_{H_{\text{b}}^{s,\ell}(X)}=\|\rho^{-\ell}u\|_{H_{\text{b}}^{s}(X)},\quad\|u\|_{H_{\text{b},h}^{s,\ell}(X)}=\|\rho^{-\ell}u\|_{H_{\text{b},h}^{s}(X)}.

Remark 2.7
.

The operators
A_{jk}
in the definition of the
H_{\text{b}}^{s}(X)
norm can be specifically chosen as all up to
j
-fold compositions of
\partial_{x^{i}}
and
x^{i}\partial_{x^{j}}
for
i,j=1,2,3
, since these vector fields span
\mathcal{V}_{b}(X)
over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
.

We next introduce the notion of conormal spaces, which is closely related to the b-Sobolev spaces and is essential in our future analysis.

Definition 2.8
.

The space of conormal functions of order
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is:

\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X):=\{u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X):|(r\partial_{r})^{j}\Omega^{\beta}u|\leq C_{j,\beta}r^{-\alpha},\quad\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}\text{ and multi-index }\beta\},

The following proposition establishes inclusions between b-Sobolev spaces and conormal spaces. While this connection is discussed in
[
43
]
, we provide a more detailed proof for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 2.9
.

We define

H_{\text{b}}^{s,\ell-}(X):=\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}H_{\text{b}}^{s,\ell-\epsilon}(X),\quad\mathcal{A}^{\alpha-}(X):=\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}\mathcal{A}^{\alpha-\epsilon}(X).

Then we have

(2.6)

H_{\text{b}}^{\infty,\ell}(X)\subset\mathcal{A}^{\ell+3/2}(X)\subset H_{\text{b}}^{\infty,\ell-}(X);

Proof.

Noticing that
|dg_{X}|
is comparable to
r^{2}dr|dg_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|
and that
\rho\partial_{\rho}=-\partial_{s}
if
\rho=e^{-s}
, we observe that
u\in H_{\text{b}}^{\infty,\ell}(X)
implies
\partial_{s}^{k}\partial_{\theta,\varphi}^{\beta}(\rho^{-\ell}u)\in L^{2}(X;r^{2}dr|dg_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|)
,
\forall k,\beta
. Since
r^{2}\,dr=r^{3}\,ds
, it follows that in the variable
s
we have

\rho^{-\ell-\frac{3}{2}}u\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{s}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2})\implies\partial_{s}^{k}\partial_{\theta,\varphi}^{\beta}(\rho^{-\ell-\frac{3}{2}}u)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{s}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}),\quad\forall k,\beta.

Returning to the variable
r
or
\rho
, we obtain
u\in\mathcal{A}^{\ell+3/2}(X)
provided that
u\in H_{\text{b}}^{\infty,\ell}(X)
. Similarly, one can justify the other inclusion
\mathcal{A}^{\ell+3/2}(X)\subset H_{\text{b}}^{\infty,\ell-}(X)
in (
2.6
).
∎

For future reference, we prove here two results to illustrate the connection between b-differential equations and conormal spaces in a simple setting:

Proposition 2.10

(Gain-of-decay)
.

Let
1<\beta<\alpha
and
u\in\mathcal{A}^{\beta}(X)
. If
(\rho\partial_{\rho}-1)u=f\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)
, then
u\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)
. I.e., if
u
solves
(\rho\partial_{\rho}-1)u\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)
, then
u
gains decay.

Proof.

Passing to the variable
s=-\log\rho
as in the proof of Proposition
2.9
, we shall work in the space
(0,\infty)_{s}\times{\mathbb{S}}_{\omega}^{2}
replacing the collar region
\{0<\rho<1\}
. Using
\rho\partial_{\rho}=-\partial_{s}
and suppressing the spherical variable
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
for simplicity, we get

(2.7)

(\partial_{s}+1)u=-f,\quad 0<s<\infty

We note that
f\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)
implies
g:=e^{\alpha s}f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\rm b}((0,\infty)_{s}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2})
, the space of smooth functions with all derivatives bounded; similarly,
e^{\beta s}u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\rm b}((0,\infty)_{s}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2})
thus
u=\mathcal{O}(e^{-\beta s})
. We can therefore solve (
2.7
) and obtain

u(s)=e^{-s}\int_{s}^{\infty}e^{t}f(t)\,dt,\quad 0<s<\infty.

To prove
u\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)
, it suffices to show
e^{\alpha s}u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\rm b}((0,\infty)_{s}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2})
. We first estimate

\begin{split}|e^{\alpha s}u(s)|=e^{(\alpha-1)s}\left|\int_{s}^{\infty}e^{t}f(t)\,dt\right|&=e^{(\alpha-1)s}\left|\int_{s}^{\infty}e^{-(\alpha-1)t}g(t)\,dt\right|\\
&\leq\|g\|_{C^{0}}\int_{s}^{\infty}e^{(\alpha-1)(s-t)}dt=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\|g\|_{C^{0}}.\end{split}

To bound the derivatives, we observe by (
2.7
) that

\partial_{s}(e^{\alpha s}u)=e^{\alpha s}(\partial_{s}+\alpha)u=e^{\alpha s}((\alpha-1)u-f)=(\alpha-1)e^{\alpha s}u-g,

we can then iterate and conclude that
e^{\alpha s}u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\rm b}((0,\infty)_{s}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2})
, thus
u\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)
.
∎

The next proposition shows that in the case
\beta<1<\alpha
, we will obtain a
\rho^{1}
leading order term when we intend to get better decay of
u
than
\mathcal{A}^{\beta}
, using the equation
(\rho\partial_{\rho}-1)u\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)
. This is due to
\rho^{1}
being an indicial solution of the regular singular operator
\rho\partial_{\rho}-1
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
0<\beta<1<\alpha
and
u\in\mathcal{A}^{\beta}(X)
. Suppose
(\rho\partial_{\rho}-1)u\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)
. Then in the collar neighborhood
[0,1)_{\rho}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
of
\partial X
, there exists
g\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})
such that

u=\rho g(\omega)+\widetilde{u},\quad\omega\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2},\quad\text{with}\ \widetilde{u}\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X).

Proof.

Let
\chi=\chi(\rho)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,\infty))
be identically
1
for
0\leq\rho\leq 1/2
and identically
0
for
\rho\geq 1
, then we have

(2.8)

(\rho\partial_{\rho}-1)(\chi u)=\rho\chi^{\prime}u+\chi(\rho\partial_{\rho}-1)u=:h\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X).

We use a b-operator argument similar to
[
43
, Lemma 2.23]
. To this end, we recall the Mellin transform in
\rho
, defined by

\mathcal{M}(f)(\xi):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{-i\xi}f(\rho)\frac{d\rho}{\rho},

where we suppressed the spherical variables. The equation (
2.8
) implies

(2.9)

(i\xi-1)\mathcal{M}(\chi u)(\xi)=\mathcal{M}(h)(\xi).

Here we used
\mathcal{M}(\rho\partial_{\rho}f)(\xi)=i\xi\mathcal{M}(f)(\xi)
. We note that
h\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)
implies that
\mathcal{M}(h)(\xi)
is holomorphic in
\mathrm{Im}\,\xi>-\alpha
and satisfies the estimates

(2.10)

\|\mathcal{M}(h)(\xi)\|_{C^{k}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})}\leq C_{kN\delta}\langle\xi\rangle^{-N},\quad-\alpha+\delta<\mathrm{Im}\,\xi<0,

for any
k,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta>0
. Since
\chi u\in\mathcal{A}^{\beta}(X)
, the estimate (
2.10
) holds for
\mathcal{M}(\chi u)(\xi)
but with
\alpha
replaced by
\beta
. Using the inverse Mellin transform and (
2.9
) we get

(\chi u)(\rho)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathrm{Im}\,\xi=-\beta+\epsilon}\rho^{i\xi}\frac{\mathcal{M}(h)(\xi)}{i\xi-1}\,d\xi.

We now shift the contour of integration through the simple pole
\xi=-i
to
\mathrm{Im}\,\xi=-\alpha+\epsilon
, with estimate (
2.10
) justifying the infinite ends, the residue theorem gives

\chi u=\rho\mathcal{M}(h)(-i)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathrm{Im}\,\xi=-\alpha+\epsilon}\rho^{i\xi}\frac{\mathcal{M}(h)(\xi)}{i\xi-1}\,d\xi.

We can therefore infer from the estimate (
2.10
) that

\widetilde{u}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathrm{Im}\,\xi=-\alpha+\epsilon}\rho^{i\xi}\frac{\mathcal{M}(h)(\xi)}{i\xi-1}\,d\xi\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha-\epsilon}(X),\quad\forall\epsilon>0,

namely
\widetilde{u}\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha-}(X)
. Our desired expansion for
u
follows by setting
g:=\mathcal{M}(h)(-i)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{-2}f\,d\rho\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})
and noting that
(\rho\partial_{\rho}-1)\widetilde{u}=(\rho\partial_{\rho}-1)u\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)
implies
\widetilde{u}\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)
by Proposition
2.10
.
∎

3.
Mapping properties of the spectral wave operator

In this section we review the mapping properties of the specral family associated to the wave operator
\Box_{g}
, following closely
[
43
, Section 2]
.

We note that in this paper we shall use the Fourier transform defined by

\mathcal{F}\phi(\sigma):=\widehat{\phi}(\sigma):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\sigma t_{*}}\phi(t_{*})dt_{*},

which is not the usual convention. As a consequence, the inverse Fourier transform is

(\mathcal{F}^{-1}f)(t_{*}):=\check{f}(t_{*})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\sigma t_{*}}f(\sigma)d\sigma.

The spectral family of
\Box\equiv\Box_{g}
is the following conjugated wave operator:

(3.1)

\widehat{\Box}(\sigma):=e^{i\sigma t_{*}}\Box e^{-i\sigma t_{*}}=2i\sigma\rho Q+\widehat{\Box}(0)+\sigma^{2}g^{00}\in\rho\rm{Diff}^{2}_{\rm{b}}.

We see that
\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)\colon\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)\to\mathcal{A}^{1+\alpha}(X)
for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
, and that at
\sigma=0
,

\widehat{\Box}(0)\colon\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)\to\mathcal{A}^{2+\alpha}(X).

In the present paper, we consider only the following class of “spectrally admissible” metrics defined below.

Definition 3.1
.

We call the metric
g

spectrally admissible
if the following hold:

(1)

(Mode stability)
The nullspace of the operator
\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)
defined on
\mathcal{A}^{1}(X)
is trivial for all complex numbers
\sigma
with
\mathrm{Im}\,\sigma\geq 0
.

(2)

There exists a real number
\delta
such that for
s\in\mathbb{R},\ell<-1/2,s+\ell>-1/2
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that the estimate

(3.2)

\|u\|_{H^{s,\ell}_{\textrm{b},|\sigma|^{-1}}(X)}\leq C|\sigma|^{-1+\delta}\|\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)u\|_{H^{s,\ell+1}_{\textrm{b},|\sigma|^{-1}}(X)}

holds for
0\leq\mathrm{Im}\,\sigma<1,|\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma|\geq C,
and for all
u
for which the norms on both sides are finite.

We now turn our attention to the behavior of the resolvent
\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}
, both for
\sigma
farther away from and close to 0. Our analysis builds on results of
[
43
]
and
[
114
,
115
]
.

3.1.
Resolvent regularity at nonzero frequencies

We first consider the case where
|\sigma|>R_{0}>0
for some
R_{0}
:

Proposition 3.2

(Resolvent estimates for non-zero frequencies, Theorem 1.1 from
[
114
]
)
.

Let
0<R_{0}<R_{1}
be fixed. For
s,\ell\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\ell<-\frac{1}{2}
and
s+\ell>-\frac{1}{2}
, the Mode stability assumption implies the following estimate:

(3.3)

\|u\|_{{H}_{b}^{s,\ell}(X)}\leq C\|\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)u\|_{{H}_{b}^{s,\ell+1}(X)},

where
\mathrm{Im}\,\sigma\geq 0
,
R_{0}<|\sigma|<R_{1}
, and
C
depends on
R_{0},R_{1},s,\ell
.

Building on this result, we can establish regularity properties of the resolvent:

Lemma 3.3

(Regularity of the resolvent)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then:

1. The operator
\partial_{\sigma}^{m}\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}:H_{b}^{s,\ell+1}(X)\to H_{b}^{s-m,\ell}(X)
is bounded for
\sigma,s,\ell
as in Proposition
3.2
.

2. For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\ell<-\frac{1}{2}
,
s+\ell>-\frac{1}{2}
,
\mathrm{Im}\,\sigma\in[0,1)
, and
|\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma|\geq C>0
, the operator

\partial_{\sigma}^{m}\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}:H_{b,|\sigma|^{-1}}^{s,\ell+1}(X)\to|\sigma|^{-1+(m+1)\delta}H_{b,|\sigma|^{-1}}^{s-m,\ell}(X)

is uniformly bounded.

Proof.

The full proof can be found in
[
43
, Lemma 2.10]
. The key idea is to use the factorization
\partial_{\sigma}\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}=-\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}\circ\partial_{\sigma}\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)\circ\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1},

along with the fact that
\partial_{\sigma}\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)\in\rho\text{Diff}_{b}^{1}+\sigma\rho^{2}\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
.
∎

Next, we consider the low energy behavior of
\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}
, which is critical for our asymptotic analysis:

Theorem 3.4

(Low energy resolvent estimate, Theorem 1.1 from
[
115
]
)
.

Assume Mode stability for
\sigma=0
. Let
s,\ell,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
\ell<-\frac{1}{2}
,
s+\ell>-\frac{1}{2}
, and
\ell-\nu\in(-\frac{3}{2},-\frac{1}{2})
. Then for
\mathrm{Im}\,\sigma\geq 0
and
|\sigma|\leq\sigma_{0}\ll 1
, we have

\|(\rho+|\sigma|)^{\nu}u\|_{H_{b}^{s,\ell}(X)}\leq C\|(\rho+|\sigma|)^{\nu-1}\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)u\|_{H_{b}^{s,\ell+1}(X)}.

As a consequence of the embedding estimate mentioned in (
2.6
), we have

(3.4)

\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\colon\mathcal{A}^{2+\alpha}(X)\to\mathcal{A}^{\alpha-}(X),\quad\alpha\in(0,1).

In order to capture the output of the resolvent
\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}
precisely near
\rho=\sigma=0

[

Definition 3.5

(Resolved space)
.

For positive frequencies, we define the
resolved space

X^{+}_{\text{res}}
as the blow-up

X^{+}_{\text{res}}:=[X\times[0,1)_{\sigma};\partial X\times\{0\}].

Here,
\beta:X^{+}_{\text{res}}\to[0,1)\times X

X^{+}_{\text{res}}
has three boundary hypersurfaces:

•

The
transition face

tf
: This is the new front face created by the blow-up.

•

The

bf

[0,1)\times\partial X
, given by
\overline{\beta^{-1}((0,1)\times\partial X)}
.

•

The

zf

\{0\}\times X

\overline{\beta^{-1}(\{0\}\times X^{\circ})}

This resolved space helps us analyze the resolvent’s behavior near
\rho=\sigma=0

In
\rho<1

\rho_{\mathrm{bf}}:=\frac{\rho}{\rho+\sigma},\quad\rho_{\mathrm{tf}}:=\rho+\sigma,\quad\rho_{\mathrm{zf}}:=\frac{\sigma}{\rho+\sigma}

Near
{\mathrm{bf}}

(\widehat{\rho},\sigma)
where
\widehat{\rho}=(\sigma r)^{-1}
. Near
{\mathrm{zf}}
, we use
(\rho,\widehat{r})
where
\widehat{r}=\sigma r
. The transition face
{\mathrm{tf}}
represents the transition between regimes
\sigma r\lesssim 1
and
\sigma r\gtrsim 1
.

For functions on the resolved space
X^{+}_{\text{res}}
(which includes both radial and
\sigma
asymptotics), we use spaces of the form
\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(X^{+}_{\text{res}})
, where
\alpha
,
\beta
,

describe the decay rates at the b-face, transition face, and zero face respectively. More specifically

\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(X^{+}_{\rm res})=\rho_{\mathrm{bf}}^{\alpha}\rho_{\mathrm{tf}}^{\beta}\rho_{\mathrm{zf}}^{\gamma}\mathcal{A}^{0,0,0}(X^{+}_{\rm res}).

When
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=0
this denotes the set of locally bounded functions that remain locally bounded after applying any finite number of vector fields tangent to all boundary hypersurfaces of
X^{+}_{\rm res}
.

Definition 3.6

(Partial expansions)
.

An
index set
is a subset
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathbb{C}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
such that the number of
(z,k)\in\mathcal{E}
with
\mathrm{Re}\,z<C
is finite for any fixed
C\in\mathbb{R}
, and so that
(z,k)\in\mathcal{E}
implies
(z+1,k)\in\mathcal{E}
and, if
k\geq 1
,
(z,k-1)\in\mathcal{E}
. For example, a typical index set is

(z_{0},k_{0}):=\{(z,k)\in\mathbb{C}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\colon k\leq k_{0},\ z-z_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\}.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an index set and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. We define the space
\mathcal{A}^{(\mathcal{E},\alpha)}(X)
as the set of all functions
u
smooth in the interior of
X
which, near the boundary
\partial X\cong{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
, admit a partial expansion of the form:

u-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(z,k)\in\mathcal{E}\\
\mathrm{Re}\,z<\alpha\end{subarray}}u_{z,k}(\omega)\rho^{z}(\log\rho)^{k}\in\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}(X)

with
u_{z,k}(\omega)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})
. For instance, we have

\mathcal{A}^{((1,0),2)}(X)=+\rho\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})+\mathcal{A}^{2}(X)
and
\mathcal{A}^{((0,1),1-)}(X)=\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})+(\log\rho)\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})+\mathcal{A}^{1-}(X)
.

Partially polyhomogeneous spaces like
\mathcal{A}^{\alpha_{\mathrm{bf}},\alpha_{\mathrm{tf}},(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{zf}},\alpha_{\mathrm{zf}})}(X^{+}_{\rm res})
have partial expansions only at the boundary hypersurfaces at which an index set is given.

Proposition 3.7
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})

(3.7)

\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}(\rho^{2}\tilde{f})\in\tilde{u}_{\rm mod}(\sigma/\rho)+\tilde{u}_{0}(x)+\mathcal{A}^{1-,1-,1-}(X_{\rm res}^{+}),

with

being real-valued. In fact, we have
\tilde{u}_{0}\in\mathcal{A}^{((1,0),2-)}(X;\mathbb{R})

Proof.

In view of Lemma
2.5
, (
3.1
) and (
3.6
), we have

\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)-\sigma^{2}\widetilde{\Box}=\rho^{3}L_{1}+\rho^{4}L_{2}+2i\sigma\rho^{3}\widetilde{Q}+\sigma^{2}g^{00}.

where we recall from our notation (
2.2

\widetilde{Q}\in\mathrm{Diff}_{\mathrm{b}}^{1}(X).
We shall now compute this operator applied to

. We first note that

eliminates the singular term

and the

\rho^{3}L_{1}\tilde{u}_{\rm mod}\in\rho^{3}\mathcal{A}^{1-,0,1-}(X_{\rm res}^{+})\subset\mathcal{A}^{4-,3,1-}(X_{\rm res}^{+})

In view of our definition of

, which in turn is a consequence of the definition of the metrics we consider,
Definition

2.3
, we see that in local coordinates

\begin{split}L_{2}=&a_{1}(\rho\partial_{\rho})^{2}+a_{2}\rho\partial_{\rho}\partial_{\theta}+a_{3}\rho\partial_{\rho}\partial_{\varphi}+b_{1}\partial_{\theta}^{2}+b_{2}\partial_{\theta}\partial_{\varphi}+b_{3}\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\\

does not contain zeroth-order terms, thus

eliminates the

term in the expansion of
\tilde{u}_{\rm mod}
near zf, while
L_{2}(-\log(\sigma/\rho))=c_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X;\mathbb{R})
is real-valued. Therefore,

\sigma\rho^{3}=\rho_{\mathrm{bf}}^{3}\rho_{\mathrm{tf}}^{4}\rho_{\mathrm{zf}}

We recall from

3.9

\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}:\mathcal{A}^{3-,3,1-}(X_{\rm{res}}^{+})\to\mathcal{A}^{1-,1-,1-}(X_{\rm{res}}^{+})

3.7

\tilde{u}_{0}:=-\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\tilde{e}_{0}
is real-valued since
\tilde{e}_{0}
is real-valued. More precisely, arguing as in
[
43

or following the proof of Theorem

\tilde{u}_{0}=-\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\tilde{e}_{0}\in\tilde{c}\rho+\mathcal{A}^{2-}(X)\subset\mathcal{A}^{((1,0),2-)}(X).

We convert the initial value problem to a forcing problem by introducing a cutoff function. The forcing problem allows us to decompose its solution into low frequency, high frequency, and intermediate frequency parts. Each part is then analyzed separately using Fourier analysis and resolvent expansion/estimates. By combining the asymptotic expansion for each part, we derive the full asymptotic expansion.

4.1.
Preliminaries for the proof of Theorem

Recalling the decomposition of

Recalling the mapping property

To conclude, we have obtained an expansion for frequencies
\sigma>0
,

(4.50)

\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}\widehat{f}_{0}=u_{\rm{sing}}(\sigma)+u_{\rm{reg}}(\sigma),

(4.51)

(4.52)

The leading term of

, with

\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\widehat{f}_{0}

\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\tilde{f_{1}}

To prove Theorem
4.4
, we restrict our attention to spatially compact sets
K\Subset X^{\circ}
i.e. where
\rho>c>0
for some constant

. This will allow us to take all terms in the resolvent expansion (
4.50
)–(
4.52
) and replace the conormal space
\mathcal{A}^{1-}(X)
with
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X^{\circ})
. We note that for real
\sigma
,

(4.53)

\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}g=\overline{\widehat{\Box}(-\sigma)^{-1}\overline{g}}.

It follows that the smooth terms of
u_{\rm{reg}}
in (
4.52
) for
\sigma>0
and
\sigma<0
can be glued together as

\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\widehat{f}_{0}+i\sigma\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\tilde{f_{1}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}((-1,1)_{\sigma};\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X^{\circ})),

since

\overline{\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\widehat{f}_{0}+i(-\sigma)\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\tilde{f_{1}}}=\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\widehat{f}_{0}+i\sigma\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\tilde{f_{1}},

for which we recalled that
\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\widehat{f}_{0}
and
\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}\tilde{f_{1}}
are both real-valued. Arguing as in
[
43
, Theorem 3.4]
to glue the pieces
u_{\rm{sing}}
for
\sigma>0
and
\sigma<0
together, we conclude that

(4.54)

\begin{split}\widehat{\phi_{0}}(\sigma)&=\psi(\sigma)\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}\widehat{f}_{0}\in-2ic_{0}\psi(\sigma)\sigma\log(\sigma+i0)\\
&+\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}((-1,1)_{\sigma};\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X^{\circ}))+\sum_{\pm}\mathcal{A}^{2-}(\pm[0,1)_{\sigma};\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X^{\circ})).\end{split}

We now compute
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\psi(\sigma)\sigma\log(\sigma+i0))(t_{*})
. Writing
\log(\sigma+i0)=\log|\sigma|+i\pi H(-\sigma),

where
H
is the Heaviside function, we get, through a straightforward computation,

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\log(\sigma+i0))(t_{*})&=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{p.v.}\frac{1}{t_{*}}+\mathrm{f.p.}\frac{1}{|t_{*}|}\right)+\left(\frac{i\pi}{2}-\gamma\right)\delta(t_{*})\\
&=-\mathrm{f.p.}\frac{1}{(t_{*})_{+}}+\left(\frac{i\pi}{2}-\gamma\right)\delta(t_{*}).\end{split}

Here
\mathrm{p.v.}
denotes the principal value,
\mathrm{f.p.}
denotes the finite part distribution;
(t_{*})_{+}=\max(t_{*},0)
;
\gamma
is the Euler’s constant. Then

(4.55)

\begin{split}F(t_{*}):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\sigma\log(\sigma+i0))(t_{*})&=i\partial_{t_{*}}\left(\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\log(\sigma+i0))(t_{*})\right)\\
&=-i\left(\mathrm{f.p.}\frac{1}{(t_{*})_{+}}\right)^{\prime}-\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+i\gamma\right)\delta^{\prime}(t_{*}).\end{split}

Noting that
F(t_{*})\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}_{t_{*}})
, we can therefore note that
\text{sing supp}\,F=\{0\}
. Moreover, letting
\chi(t_{*})\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{t_{*}})
be identically equal to
0
when
t_{*}\leq 1/2
and identically equal to

1
when
t_{*}\geq 1
, we have, in view of (
4.55
),

(\chi F)(t_{*})=it_{*}^{-2}\chi(t_{*});\quad\text{supp\,}(1-\chi)F\subset[0,1]_{t_{*}}.

Using the convolution theorem,
\mathcal{F}(u\ast v)=\mathcal{F}u\mathcal{F}v
, we can write

\begin{split}{}&\quad\ \mathcal{F}^{-1}(\psi(\sigma)\sigma\log(\sigma+i0))=F\ast\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi=\chi F\ast\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi+(1-\chi)F\ast\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi.\end{split}

Note that
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\sigma})
implies
\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{t_{*}})
, and that
(1-\chi)F
is a distribution of compact support. It follows that

(1-\chi)F\ast\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{t_{*}}).

We assert that

\chi F-\chi F\ast\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{t_{*}}).

This is equivalent to claim that
(1-\psi)\widehat{\chi F}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{\sigma})
, i.e. for any
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C_{km}
such that

(4.56)

\left|\sigma^{m}\partial_{\sigma}^{k}\big((1-\psi(\sigma))\widehat{\chi F}(\sigma)\big)\right|\leq C_{km}.

Recalling that
(\chi F)(t_{*})=\chi(t_{*})it_{*}^{-2}
, through integration by parts, we have

\sigma\widehat{\chi F}(\sigma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t_{*}}(e^{i\sigma t_{*}})\chi(t_{*})t_{*}^{-2}dt_{*}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\sigma t_{*}}\partial_{t_{*}}(\chi(t_{*})t_{*}^{-2})dt_{*}.

It follows that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sigma^{k}\widehat{\chi F}(\sigma)=i^{k+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\sigma t_{*}}\partial_{t_{*}}^{k}(\chi(t_{*})t_{*}^{-2})dt_{*}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\sigma});

here we used the fact that
\partial_{t_{*}}^{k}(\chi(t_{*})t_{*}^{-2})=\mathcal{O}(|t_{*}|^{-2-k})
. Since

t_{*}^{l}\partial_{t_{*}}^{k}(\chi(t_{*})t_{*}^{-2})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{t_{*}}),\quad l\leq k,

we can therefore write

\partial_{\sigma}^{l}\big(\sigma^{k}\widehat{\chi F}(\sigma)\big)=i^{k+l+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\sigma t_{*}}t_{*}^{l}\partial_{t_{*}}^{k}(\chi(t_{*})t_{*}^{-2})dt_{*}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\sigma}).

We recall that
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\sigma})
is identically equal to
1
near
\sigma=0
. Thus, we can define
\sigma^{-k}(1-\psi(\sigma))\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{\sigma})
(with all derivatives bounded). The product rule gives

(4.57)

\partial_{\sigma}^{k}\big((1-\psi(\sigma))\widehat{\chi F}(\sigma)\big)=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\partial_{\sigma}^{k-l}\left(\frac{1-\psi(\sigma)}{\sigma^{k}}\right)\partial_{\sigma}^{l}\big(\sigma^{k}\widehat{\chi F}(\sigma)\big).

Using integration by parts as above, we can derive that for
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
l\leq k
,

(4.58)

\sigma^{m}\partial_{\sigma}^{l}\big(\sigma^{k}\widehat{\chi F}(\sigma)\big)=i^{k+l+m+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\sigma t_{*}}\partial_{t_{*}}^{m}\big(t_{*}^{l}\partial_{t_{*}}^{k}(\chi(t_{*})t_{*}^{-2})\big)dt_{*}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\sigma}).

Combining (
4.57
) and (
4.58
), we obtain (
4.56
). Thus
(1-\psi)\widehat{\chi F}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{\sigma})
. We conclude from the above arguments that

\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\psi(\sigma)\sigma\log(\sigma+i0))-i\chi(t_{*})t_{*}^{-2}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{t_{*}});

thus, the contribution to
\phi_{0}
from the most singular term is
2c_{0}t_{*}^{-2}
.
More generally, we have

\mathcal{F}_{t_{*}\to\sigma}^{-1}(\sigma^{k}\log(\sigma+i0))=d_{k}t_{*}^{-k-1},\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\quad d_{k}=e^{-i\pi k/2}(-1)^{k}k!.

We also note that the inverse Fourier transform of the smooth term (namely the
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}((-1,1)_{\sigma};\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X^{\circ}))
part) lies in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{t_{*}})
. In view of
[
43
, Lemma 3.6]
, either of the conormal terms, which belongs to

or

t_{*}^{-3+}
together with all their

and

derivatives. Hence, we conclude that for

,

(4.59)

|\partial_{t_{*}}^{j}\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\phi_{0}-2c_{0}t_{*}^{-2})|\leq C_{j\beta K}t_{*}^{-3-j+},\quad\forall\,j\in\mathbb{N},\ \beta\in\mathbb{N}^{3}.

which thus completes the proof of Theorem

is independent of the choice of the cutoff

6.
Price’s Law for semi-linear wave equations

In this section we consider semilinear wave equations with nonlinearities of the form

,

, of which power-type nonlinearities
\pm\phi^{p}
are a special case, and prove Theorem

.
We adapt the methods from the cubic case to higher-power nonlinearities and use a refined resolvent expansion that allows us to handle the modified asymptotic behavior. In particular, we carefully analyze how the structure of the nonlinearity affects the leading-order terms. There is a transition at
p=4
between “low power” and “high power” behavior. The proof shows how the nonlinearity modifies the classical Price’s law and also provides a systematic method for computing the explicit constant in the leading-order term, a level of precision not attainable by all approaches.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
. Let
c(\omega),\,d(\omega)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})
satisfy
\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}c(\omega)\,d\omega=0
, and let
\tilde{f}(x)\in\mathcal{A}^{4+\alpha}(X)
. For positive frequencies
\sigma>0
, the resolvent
\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}
acting on

f=c(\omega)\rho^{3}+d(\omega)\rho^{4}+\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{A}^{((3,0),4+\alpha)}(X)

is then of the form:
\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}f=u_{\rm sing}(\sigma)+u_{\rm reg}(\sigma)
,

\displaystyle u_{\rm sing}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\in\sigma^{2}\mathcal{A}^{1-,((0,0),1-),((0,1),1-)}(X^{+}_{\rm res}),

\displaystyle u_{\rm reg}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1)_{\sigma};\mathcal{A}^{1-}(X))+\mathcal{A}^{\alpha-,2+\alpha-,((2,0),2+\alpha-)}(X^{+}_{\rm res}),

where the leading term of
\sigma^{-2}u_{\rm sing}(\sigma)
at
{\rm zf}
is
-d_{X}(f)\log(\sigma/\rho)
. The constant
d_{X}(f)
is given by the following formula, where
\tilde{c}(\omega)
is defined in (
6.26
):

(6.23)

d_{X}(f)=\frac{m}{\pi}\int_{X}\big(f-\widehat{\Box}(0)(\tilde{c}(\omega)\rho)\big)\,|dg_{X}|-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}d(\omega)\,d\omega.

Proof.

We follow the outline of the proof of
[
43
, Theorem 3.1]
but present a more detailed analysis due to the appearance of the terms
c(\omega)\rho^{3}
and
d(\omega)\rho^{4}
in the expansion of
f
.

We have
a priori

u:=\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}f\in\mathcal{A}^{1-}(X)
; then, in view of (
2.3
),

L_{0}u=\rho^{-2}f-(\rho L_{1}+\rho^{2}L_{2})u=c(\omega)\rho+d(\omega)\rho^{2}+\rho^{-2}\tilde{f}-(\rho L_{1}+\rho^{2}L_{2})u\in c(\omega)\rho+\mathcal{A}^{2-}.

To solve away the term
c(\omega)\rho
, we expand
c(\omega)
into spherical harmonics by writing

(6.24)

c(\omega)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}(\omega),\quad c_{k}(\omega)\in\mathcal{Y}_{k},

where
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
is the
k
-th eigenspace, so that
\Delta_{\omega}|_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}=k(k+1)
. We note that there is no
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
–component in
c(\omega)
since
\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}c(\omega)\,d\omega=0
, as
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
consists of constant functions. Recalling that
L_{0}=-(\rho\partial_{\rho})^{2}+\rho\partial_{\rho}+\Delta_{\omega}
, we compute

(6.25)

L_{0}(\rho c_{k}(\omega))=k(k+1)\rho c_{k}(\omega),\quad k\geq 0.

We can then find
\tilde{c}(\omega)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})
, solving
L_{0}(\tilde{c}(\omega)\rho)=c(\omega)\rho
and defined by

(6.26)

\tilde{c}(\omega)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\tilde{c}_{k}(\omega),\quad\tilde{c}_{k}(\omega):=\frac{c_{k}(\omega)}{k(k+1)},\quad\text{with $c_{k}(\omega)$'s given in \eqref{Expand c(omega) Sec 6}}.

It follows that
L_{0}(u-\tilde{c}(\omega)\rho)\in\mathcal{A}^{2-}(X)
and
\widehat{\Box}(0)(u-\tilde{c}(\omega)\rho)=f-\widehat{\Box}(0)(\tilde{c}(\omega)\rho)\in\mathcal{A}^{4}(X)
. Following the proof of
[
43
, Lemma 3.2]
, we therefore obtain

u-\tilde{c}(\omega)\rho=c_{(0)}\rho+\tilde{u}_{0},\quad\tilde{u}_{0}\in\mathcal{A}^{2-},\quad c_{(0)}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{X}\big(f-\widehat{\Box}(0)(\tilde{c}(\omega)\rho)\big)\,|dg_{X}|.

Next, we expand
\tilde{u}_{0}
. Noting that
L_{0}\rho=0
, we get

\begin{split}L_{0}\tilde{u}_{0}=L_{0}(u-\tilde{c}(\omega))&=d(\omega)\rho^{2}+\rho^{-2}\tilde{f}-\rho L_{1}((c_{(0)}+\tilde{c}(\omega))\rho)-\rho L_{1}\tilde{u}_{0}-\rho^{2}L^{2}u\\
&\in(d(\omega)-2mc_{(0)}-2m\tilde{c}(\omega))\rho^{2}+\mathcal{A}^{2+\alpha}.\end{split}

By direct computation, we have for any
Y_{k}(\omega)\in\mathcal{Y}_{k}
:

L_{0}(\rho^{2}Y_{k}(\omega))=(k(k+1)-2)\rho^{2}Y_{k}(\omega),\quad k\geq 0;\qquad{\rm and}\ L_{0}((\rho^{2}\log\rho)Y_{1}(\omega))=-3\rho^{2}Y_{1}(\omega).

Expanding
d(\omega)=d_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}(\omega)
into spherical harmonics, with
d_{0}\in\mathcal{Y}_{0}
being a constant and
d_{k}(\omega)\in\mathcal{Y}_{k}
, we can obtain from the computations above that

L_{0}\left(\tilde{u}_{0}+\frac{d_{1}(\omega)-2m\tilde{c}_{1}(\omega)}{3}\rho^{2}\log\rho-\big(mc_{(0)}-\frac{d_{0}}{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{d_{k}(\omega)-2m\tilde{c}_{k}(\omega)}{k(k+1)-2}\big)\rho^{2}\right)\in\mathcal{A}^{2+\alpha}.

Using the Mellin transform as in the proof of Proposition
2.11
, we get

\tilde{u}_{0}+\frac{d_{1}(\omega)-2m\tilde{c}_{1}(\omega)}{3}\rho^{2}\log\rho-\big(mc_{(0)}-\frac{d_{0}}{2}+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{d_{k}(\omega)-2m\tilde{c}_{k}(\omega)}{k(k+1)-2}\big)\rho^{2}=\rho^{2}Y_{1}+\tilde{u}

for some
Y_{1}\in\mathcal{Y}_{1}
and some
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{A}^{2+\alpha-}(X)
.
We then conclude and write

\begin{gathered}u_{0}:=\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}f=(c_{(0)}+\tilde{c}(\omega))\rho-\frac{d_{1}(\omega)-2m\tilde{c}_{1}(\omega)}{3}\rho^{2}\log\rho+(\tilde{d}_{0}+\tilde{d}(\omega))\rho^{2}+\tilde{u},\\
\text{with}\quad\tilde{d}_{0}:=mc_{(0)}-\frac{1}{2}d_{0},\quad\tilde{d}(\omega):=Y_{1}(\omega)+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{d_{k}(\omega)-2m\tilde{c}_{k}(\omega)}{k(k+1)-2}\in\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{Y}_{k}\end{gathered}

As in (
4.48
), we next compute

(6.27)

f_{1}(\sigma):=-\sigma^{-1}(\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)-\widehat{\Box}(0))u_{0}=(-2i\rho(Q_{0}+\rho^{2}\widetilde{Q})-g^{00}\sigma)u_{0}.

Noting that
Q_{0}\rho=0
,
Q_{0}(\rho^{2}\log\rho)=\rho^{2}\log\rho+\rho^{2}
, and
Q_{0}(\rho^{2})=\rho^{2}
, we obtain

(6.28)

f_{1}(\sigma)=\frac{2i}{3}(d_{1}(\omega)-2m\tilde{c}_{1}(\omega))\rho^{3}\log\rho-2i(\tilde{d}_{0}-\frac{d_{1}(\omega)}{3}+\frac{2m\tilde{c}_{1}(\omega)}{3}+\tilde{d}(\omega))\rho^{3}+\tilde{f}_{1}(\sigma),

where

.

4.44
), with
f_{1}(\sigma)
defined in (
6.27
); we have

(6.29)

\begin{split}\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}f&=u_{0}+\sigma\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)^{-1}f_{1}(\sigma)\\

We therefore expand

u_{1}:=\widehat{\Box}(0)^{-1}f_{1}(0)\in\mathcal{A}^{1-}(X).

In view of (
6.28
), we have

\begin{split}L_{0}u_{1}&=\rho^{-2}f_{1}(0)-(\rho L_{1}+\rho^{2}L_{2})u_{1}\\
&\in\frac{2i}{3}(d_{1}(\omega)-2m\tilde{c}_{1}(\omega))\rho\log\rho-2i\bigl(\tilde{d}_{0}-\frac{d_{1}(\omega)}{3}+\frac{2m\tilde{c}_{1}(\omega)}{3}+\tilde{d}(\omega)\bigr)\rho+\mathcal{A}^{1+\alpha-}(X).\end{split}

L_{0}(Y_{1}(\omega)(\rho\log\rho+\dfrac{\rho}{2}))=2Y_{1}(\omega)\rho\log\rho,\quad L_{0}(\rho\log\rho)=-\rho,

\begin{gathered}f_{2}(\sigma):=-\sigma^{-1}\bigl(\widehat{\Box}(\sigma)-\widehat{\Box}(0)\bigr)u_{1}=(-2i\rho(Q_{0}+\rho^{2}\widetilde{Q})-g^{00}\sigma)u_{1}=f_{2,0}+\tilde{f}_{2}(\sigma),\\

Appendix A
Global existence and pointwise decay theorem from previous work for the power-type nonlinearities that are cubic and higher order

The sharp decay estimates used as an assumption in this paper for power-type nonlinearities on asymptotically flat spacetimes have been established under specific smoothness and decay conditions on the initial data. Additionally, there are assumptions regarding the asymptotic flatness of the coefficients of the operator. We first state the result for asymptotically Minkowski spacetimes, followed by another result for black hole spacetimes.

We assume the existence of a global solution throughout this work. Suppose we are in the defocusing, constant-coefficient case
a\equiv 1,b\equiv 1
. Then in stationary spacetimes
g=g(x)
, the global existence of solutions for defocusing power-type nonlinearities with large initial data can be established by leveraging a combination of local-in-time Strichartz estimates and the conservation of the energy associated to (
1.1
) and (
6.1
). These tools and estimates are well-known. In the energy critical case where
p=5
, global existence for the initial value problem with large initial data can be proven by performing an estimate in the backward cone (domain of dependence) with an apex at a fixed time slice. Specifically, if
\|u\|_{L^{6}_{x}(\text{slices})}\to 0
as one approaches the apex along constant-time slices within the cone, then global existence follows. Global existence is known for focusing
a\equiv-1,b\equiv-1
power-type nonlinearities with sufficiently small initial data. In the variable-coefficient case with
a(t_{*},x)
and
b(t_{*},x)
, global existence considerations would be related to the sign properties of these functions.

Sharpening a gap theorem: nonnegative Ricci and small curvature concentration
Adam Martens

Abstract
. We sharpen a gap theorem of Chan & Lee [ 4 ] for nonnegative Ricci curvature manifolds that have positive asymptotic volume ratio and small enough scale-invariant integral curvature (so-called “curvature concentration”), by showing that the curvature concentration need only depend linearly on the asymptotic volume ratio. We prove the result by exhibiting a long-time Ricci flow solution with faster than 1 / t 1/t curvature decay, which allows us to shift the limiting contradiction argument to time infinity and thus obtain an explicit bound on the size of the gap.



1.
Introduction

The Ricci flow, introduced by Hamilton
[
13
]
, deforms an initial Riemannian metric
g(0)=g_{0}
on a
n
-dimensional manifold
M^{n}
according to the equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(t)=-2\text{Rc}_{g(t)}.

A central aspect of Ricci flow analysis is the tendency for the flow to roughly preserve the geometry of almost Euclidean regions, despite the manifold’s global geometry being possibly not well understood. This is the so-called “pseudolocality” of the Ricci flow. Although the sense in which a region is “almost Euclidean” may differ, the general arguments of pseudolocality theorems often follow this outline:

1.

Construct a sequence of Ricci flows which are becoming more “locally Euclidean” but for which some undesirable behaviour occurs (often of the form of large curvature).

2.

Normalize the metrics to focus on the local region where the undesirable behavior occurs.

3.

Analyze the sequence of normalized metrics as they approach a limiting space, wherein a contradiction is apparent.

We will refer to this method of proof (where a contradiction is derived in the limiting space) as a “limiting contradiction argument”. A notable aspect of such arguments is that they often involve constants that are not explicitly computable due to the nature of the contradiction appearing in the limit space instead of at a specific moment along the sequence.

One of the reasons why pseudolocality theorems are important is that they relay geometric and topological information in the local region. In particular, if the sense in which a manifold
M
is “almost Euclidean” is invariant under scaling and is satisfied everywhere on
M
, then one can apply the result to a sequence of blow-downs of the initial metric, thus providing information about the manifolds global geometric structure. Consider for example the following notable result of Chan & Lee.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
4
, Theorem 1.1]

For any
n\geq 4
and
v>0
there exists some
\varepsilon
, depending on
n
and
v
, such that if
(M^{n},g)
is complete with nonnegative Ricci curvature
\text{Rc}_{g}\geq 0
, asymptotic volume ratio satisfying

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x_{0},r)}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\geq v,

and globally small scale-invariant integral curvature (curvature concentration)

(1)

\int_{M}|\text{Rm}|_{g}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g}\leq\varepsilon(n,v),

then
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to
({\mathbb{R}}^{n},g_{E})
.

The proof given in
[
4
]
is a limiting contradiction argument wherein they fix some
v>0
and then assume there is a sequence of nonnegative Ricci curved
(M_{i}^{n},g_{i})
, each with asymptotic volume ratio at least
v
and integral curvatures satisfying (
1
) with
\varepsilon_{i}\to 0
, which results in a contradiction in the limit. This raises two questions: 1. For any fixed
v>0
, precisely how small does
\varepsilon(v)
need to be to lie within the desired gap? 2. Given two distinct
v_{1},v_{2}>0
, how does
\varepsilon(v_{1})
relate to
\varepsilon(v_{2})
? The purpose of this paper is to provide an answer to both of these questions. We prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
n\geq 4
, there exists a dimensional constant
\varepsilon(n)\geq(10n)^{-10n^{3}}
such that the following holds. Let
(M^{n},g)
be any complete Riemannian manifold (not necessarily bounded curvature) with nonnegative Ricci curvature
\text{Rc}_{g}\geq 0
. Suppose that
(M^{n},g)
has positive asymptotic volume ratio

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x_{0},r)}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\geq v>0,

and curvature concentration satisfying

(2)

\int_{M}|\text{Rm}|_{g}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g}\leq\varepsilon(n)v.

Then
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to
({\mathbb{R}}^{n},g_{E})
.

Even though Theorem
1.2
is stated for dimensions
n\geq 4

[
6

and Colding’s Volume Continuity Theorem
[

that for any odd

({\mathbb{R}}^{n},g_{E})
. When

is even, the picture is less clear. For instance, we have the example of the Eguchi-Hanson metric which is a nonflat 4-dimensional Ricci flat manifold with
\operatorname{AVR}\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
\|\text{Rm}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{2}}}<\infty
. For the dimensions

which defines the “size of the gap” for manifolds with at least the given asymptotic volume ratio, i.e.,
f_{n}(v)
is the supremum of all
\rho>0
for which

\left\{(M^{n},g)\text{ with }\text{Rc}_{g}\geq 0,\;\operatorname{AVR}(g)\geq v\text{ and }\int_{M}|\text{Rm}|_{g}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g}\leq\rho\right\}=\{({\mathbb{R}}^{n},g_{E})\},

where both of the sets of Riemannian manifolds above is understood in the sense of equivalent classes modulo isomorphism. The general behaviour of

is not currently well understood. For instance, it is clear that

is nondecreasing, but it is not known (to the author’s knowledge) if we can even say that
\lim_{v\to 0^{+}}f_{n}(v)=0
. In light of Theorem
1.2
however, we have the following natural question.

Question 1.3
.

Is the linear relationship between the required smallness of the curvature concentration and the asymptotic volume ratio
v
in Theorem
1.2
optimal? In other words, is it true that

\lim_{v\to 0^{+}}\frac{f_{n}(v)}{v}<\infty?

Some evidence towards an affirmative answer to this question is the relationship between between the curvature concentration and an

-Sobolev inequality via Hölder’s inequality:

\int_{M}|\text{Rm}|u^{2}\leq\left(\int_{M}|\text{Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{M}u^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\lesssim v^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{M}u^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}.

This coefficient of

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
from Theorem
1.4

Here we provide a brief proof of our main result Theorem
1.2
from our auxiliary long-time Ricci flow existence result, Theorem
1.4
. The method of proof will be familiar to experts; due to the scale-invariant properties of the hypotheses of Theorem
1.4
, we may apply Hamilton’s Compactness Theorem to a sequence of blow-down flows to extract a converging subsequence on
(0,1]
. Then the faster than
1/t
curvature decay and volume estimates together imply that this limiting flow must be isometric to the flat Euclidean flow on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. The rigidity of the original metric can subsequently be concluded using distance distortion of Ricci flows. Here are the details.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
(assuming Theorem
1.4
).

Let
g(t)
,
t\in[0,\infty)
be the long-time Ricci flow emanating from
g
given by Theorem
1.4
. Now take any sequence of times
t_{i}\to\infty
, and define the sequence of parabolically rescaled flows
g_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{t_{i}}g(t_{i}t)
, each defined on
[0,1]
. Note that the curvature bound (
ii
) and the injectivity radius bound (
iii
) survive this rescaling, and so by Hamilton’s Compactness Theorem
[
14
]
and a diagonal argument, the
(M,g_{i}(t),x_{0})
subconverges (in the pointed Cheeger-Gromov sense) to some limiting complete flow
(\widetilde{M},\tilde{g}(t),x_{\infty})
,
t\in(0,1]
which, by nature of the faster than
1/t
curvature decay (
v
), must satisfy

|\text{Rm}|_{\tilde{g}(t)}\equiv 0\;\text{ for each }t\in(0,1].

Furthermore, each
\tilde{g}(t)
must have a positive asymptotic volume ratio because (
iv
) passes to the limit as well. Therefore, for each
t\in(0,1]
, we must have that
(\widetilde{M},\tilde{g}(t))
is isometric to
({\mathbb{R}}^{n},g_{E})
. Thus if we fix any
\delta>0
and
t\in(0,1]
small enough (depending on
\delta
and
n
), we can say that for all sufficiently large
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there holds

\displaystyle\text{Vol}_{g_{i}(0)}B_{g_{i}(0)}(x_{0},1)

\displaystyle\geq\text{Vol}_{g_{i}(t)}B_{g_{i}(0)}(x_{0},1)

\displaystyle\geq\text{Vol}_{g_{i}(t)}B_{g_{i}(t)}(x_{0},1-\beta\sqrt{t})

\displaystyle\geq(1-\delta)\omega_{n}(1-\beta\sqrt{t})^{n}

\displaystyle\geq(1-2\delta)\omega_{n}

where we have used the nonnegative scalar curvature of the flow (
i
) in the first inequality, the Shrinking Balls Lemma
[
19
, Corollary 3.3]
in the second (here
\beta=\beta(n)
is a dimensional constant), the convergence
g_{i}(t)\to g_{E}
in the third, and our smallness condition on
t
in the fourth. Scaling back tells us that

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x_{0},r)}{r^{n}}\geq(1-2\delta)\omega_{n}.

Since
\delta>0
was arbitrary, we can use our assumption of
\text{Rc}_{g}\geq 0
again to in fact see that

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x_{0},r)}{r^{n}}=\omega_{n}.

Therefore
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to
({\mathbb{R}}^{n},g_{E})
by the rigidity statement in the Bishop-Gromov Volume Comparison Theorem.
∎

3.
Relationship between Sobolev quantities

In this section, we will investigate the relationship between three different notions of “Sobolev control” in manifolds which enjoy lower Ricci bounds: volume ratios,
L^{2}
-Sobolev inequality, and control on the
\bar{\nu}
-functional. We will see that when
\text{Rc}_{g}\geq 0
, all three of these notions of Sobolev control are equivalent (up to dimensional constants).

For the sake of brevity, we introduce the following notation. For a connected
\Omega\subset(M,g)
, we will write

\operatorname{Sob}(\Omega)\leq[A,B]

to mean that the
L^{2}
-Sobolev inequality

\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq A\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{g}u|^{2}_{g}+Bu^{2}\,dV_{g}\right)

holds for all
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
with
\text{supp}\,u\subset\subset\Omega
. We may also write
\operatorname{Sob}(\Omega,g)
to emphasize which metric is being used.

The fact that an
L^{2}
-Sobolev inequality implies control on volume ratios is well-known, but since we care about the exact relationship between these quantities, we will provide a short proof. The following is only a slight modification of
[
22
, Theorem 6.1]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Volume from Sobolev)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
,
n\geq 3
be a (possibly incomplete) Riemannian manifold with
B_{g}(x,L)\subset\subset M
for some
L>0
. Assume that
\operatorname{Sob}(B_{g}(x,L))\leq[A,B]
for some
A>0
,
B\geq 0
. Then whenever
r\leq L
, there holds

\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r)\geq\left(\frac{1}{A(2^{n+3}+2BL^{2})}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}r^{n}.

Proof.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that there exists
(M^{n},g,x)
,
L>0
,
A>0
,
B\geq 0
for which the hypotheses hold, and some
r\in(0,L]
such that

\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r)<\left(\frac{1}{A(2^{n+3}+2BL^{2})}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}r^{n}=:\delta r^{n}.

Set
\tilde{g}=r^{-2}g
so that
\text{Vol}_{\tilde{g}}B_{\tilde{g}}(x,1)<\delta
. Then by the assumed Sobolev inequality for the original metric
g
and the fact that
r\leq L
, we obtain
\operatorname{Sob}(B_{\tilde{g}}(x,1))\leq[A,BL^{2}]
. Now by Hölder’s inequality,
\text{Vol}_{\tilde{g}}B_{\tilde{g}}(x,1)<\delta
, and the definition of
\delta
, we have

ABL^{2}\int_{B_{\tilde{g}}(x,1)}u^{2}\,dV_{\tilde{g}}\leq ABL^{2}\delta^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{g}}(x,1)}|u|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dV_{\tilde{g}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{g}}(x,1)}|u|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dV_{\tilde{g}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}.

Therefore this term can be absorbed into the LHS of the Sobolev inequality for
\tilde{g}
to give
\operatorname{Sob}(B_{\tilde{g}}(x,1))\leq[2A,0]
. The rest of the proof then is verbatim to the latter half of the proof of
[
22
, Theorem 6.1]
.
∎

Now we recall the definition of local entropy formulated by Wang
[
20
]
: Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
, and
\Omega\subset M
a connected open subset with smooth boundary and define

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(\Omega):=\left\{u\bigg|u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega),\;\;u\geq 0,\;\;\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dV_{g}=1\right\},

\displaystyle\overline{W}(\Omega,g,u,\tau):=\int_{\Omega}4\tau|\nabla^{g}u|_{g}^{2}-u^{2}\log u^{2}\,dV_{g}-\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi\tau)-n,

\displaystyle W(\Omega,g,u,\tau):=\int_{\Omega}\tau(4|\nabla^{g}u|_{g}^{2}+{\mathcal{R}}_{g}u^{2})-u^{2}\log u^{2}\,dV_{g}-\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi\tau)-n,

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}(\Omega,g,\tau):=\inf_{u\in\mathscr{S}(\Omega)}\overline{W}(\Omega,g,u,\tau),\;\;\;\mu(\Omega,g,\tau):=\inf_{u\in\mathscr{S}(\Omega)}W(\Omega,g,u,\tau),

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}(\Omega,g,\tau):=\inf_{s\in(0,\tau]}\bar{\mu}(\Omega,g,s),\;\;\;\nu(\Omega,g,\tau):=\inf_{s\in(0,\tau]}\mu(\Omega,g,s),

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}(\Omega,g):=\inf_{s\in(0,\infty)}\bar{\mu}(\Omega,g,s),\;\;\;\nu(\Omega,g):=\inf_{s\in(0,\infty)}\mu(\Omega,g,s).

In the above (and throughout the paper),
{\mathcal{R}}_{g}
denotes the scalar curvature of
g
. Although these definitions are given for domains
\Omega
with
smooth
boundaries, we will often take the domain to be some geodesic ball within

[

The following shows the implication of

[

]
,

]

Lemma 3.2

(
\bar{\nu}
-functional from Sobolev)
.

For any
n\geq 3
, there exists a dimensional constant
C(n)>0
such that the following holds. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a (possibly incomplete) Riemannian manifold with
\Omega\subset\subset M
and
\operatorname{Sob}(\Omega)\leq[A,\frac{a}{\tau}]
for some
A\geq 1
,
a\geq 0
,
\tau>0
. Then
\bar{\nu}(\Omega,g,\tau)\geq-\frac{n}{2}\log(C(n)A)-4a
. If in addition
\int_{\Omega}|\text{Rm}|_{g}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g}\leq\left(\frac{2}{nA}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}
, or more generally
\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{R}}_{g}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g}\leq\left(\frac{2}{A}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}
, then
\nu(\Omega,g,\tau)\geq-\frac{n}{2}\log(C(n)A)-4a
.

Proof.

Just as in the proof of
[
5

, Jensen’s inequality and the fact that
\log x\leq bx-1-\log b
(for any
b>0
) implies

(4)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\log u^{2}\,dV_{g}&\leq\frac{n}{2}\log\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{g}u|^{2}_{g}+\frac{a}{\tau}u^{2}\,dV_{g}\right)+\frac{n}{2}\log A\\
&\leq 4s\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{g}u|^{2}_{g}+\frac{a}{\tau}u^{2}\,dV_{g}\right)+\frac{n}{2}\log A-1-\log\left(\frac{8s}{n}\right).\end{split}

This shows that
\bar{\nu}(\Omega,g,\tau)\geq-\frac{n}{2}\log(C_{1}(n)A)-4a
since
\frac{4sa}{\tau}\leq 4a
when
s\leq\tau
. For the other implication, we apply Hölder’s and our Sobolev inequality

2s\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{R}}_{g}u^{2}\,dV_{g}\geq-4s\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{g}u|^{2}_{g}+\frac{a}{\tau}u^{2}\,dV_{g}\right).

Putting this back into (
4
) gives

\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\log u^{2}\,dV_{g}\leq 8s\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{g}u|^{2}_{g}+\frac{{\mathcal{R}}_{g}}{4}u^{2}+\frac{a}{\tau}u^{2}\,dV_{g}\right)+\frac{n}{2}\log A-1-\log\left(\frac{8s}{n}\right).

Now relabel
\tilde{s}=2s
and restrict
\tilde{s}\leq\tau
to obtain
\nu(\Omega,g,\tau)\geq-\frac{n}{2}\log(2C_{1}(n)A)-4a
. For simplicity, we will assume
C(n)=2C_{1}(n)
from which both implications clearly follow.
∎

It is also well-known that rough control on the
\bar{\nu}
-functional on a set
\Omega
is sufficient to obtain a Sobolev inequality on
\Omega
. We include a precise statement for our ease of reference.

Lemma 3.3

(Sobolev from
\bar{\nu}
-functional)
.

For any
n\geq 3
, there exists a dimensional constant
C(n)>0
such that the following holds. Suppose
(M^{n},g)
, is a (possibly incomplete) Riemannian manifold,
\Omega\subset\subset M
is connected, and
A,\tau>0
.

(1)

If
\bar{\nu}(\Omega,g,\tau)\geq-\frac{n}{2}\log A
, then
\operatorname{Sob}(\Omega)\leq[C(n)A,\frac{1}{\tau}]
.

(2)

If
\nu(\Omega,g,\tau)\geq-\frac{n}{2}\log A
and
\int_{\Omega}|\text{Rm}|_{g}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g}\leq\left(\frac{2}{nC(n)A}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}
, or more generally
\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{R}}_{g}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g}\leq\left(\frac{1}{C(n)A}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}
, then
\operatorname{Sob}(\Omega)\leq[2C(n)A,\frac{1}{\tau}]
.

Proof.

We will only discuss the proof of part (
2
) since part (
1
) is simpler. Fix some
\lambda>0
, a compact
K\subset\subset\Omega
, and observe that the condition on the
\nu
-functional implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\log u^{2}\,dV_{g}

\displaystyle\leq s\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{g}u|_{g}^{2}+\frac{{\mathcal{R}}_{g}}{4}u^{2}\,dV_{g}\right)-\frac{n}{2}\log s+\frac{n}{2}\log A-\frac{n}{2}\log(\pi)-n

\displaystyle\leq s\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{g}u|_{g}^{2}+\frac{{\mathcal{R}}_{g}^{+}}{4}u^{2}+\lambda u^{2}\,dV_{g}\right)-\frac{n}{2}\log s+\frac{n}{2}\log A-\frac{n}{2}\log(\pi)-n

for all
s\in(0,4\tau]
and
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(K)
with
\int_{K}u^{2}\,dV_{g}=1
. Now by
[
22
, Theorem 5.5]
, we may conclude a Sobolev inequality

(5)

\left(\int_{K}u^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq C(n,A)\left(\int_{K}|\nabla^{g}u|^{2}_{g}+\frac{{\mathcal{R}}_{g}^{+}}{4}u^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}u^{2}+\lambda u^{2}\,dV_{g}\right)

for all
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(K)
. Even though the results in
[
22
]
are stated for closed manifolds, equivalent statements hold for compact manifolds with boundary (such as our
K\subset\subset\Omega
). Now since
K
is compact, we may send
\lambda\to 0
. The fact that the constant
C(n,A)
is of the precise form
C(n)A
follows by tracking through the constants in
[
22
, Theorems 5.3-5.5]
as was first noticed by Chen
[
9
]
(see also
[
5
, Appendix]
). So it remains to deal with the remaining scalar curvature term. For this, we apply Hölder’s inequality

\frac{C(n)A}{4}\int_{K}{\mathcal{R}}_{g}^{+}u^{2}\,dV_{g}\leq\frac{C(n)A}{4}\left(\int_{K}|{\mathcal{R}}_{g}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{K}u^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{K}u^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}.

So we can absorb this term into the LHS of (
5
) to give
\operatorname{Sob}(K)\leq[2C(n)A,\frac{1}{\tau}]
. The result follows by taking an exhaustion of compact
K_{1}\subset K_{2}\subset\dots\subset\Omega
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

In our application, we will have some Ricci flow
g(t)
,
t\in[0,T]
, an initial Sobolov inequality
\operatorname{Sob}(\Omega,g(0))\leq[A,0]
, and some assumed smallness of
\int|\text{Rm}|_{g(0)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(0)}
. Lemma
3.2
then will give us
\nu
-functional control
\nu(\Omega,g(0),\tau+T)\geq-\frac{n}{2}\log(C_{1}(n)A)
, which - in our application where our flows enjoy
c/t
curvature control - is preserved under the Ricci flow to give
\nu(\Omega,g(t),\tau)\geq-\frac{n}{2}\log(C_{1}(n)A)
. Then Lemma
3.3
will return us to a Sobolev inequality for
g(t)
:
\operatorname{Sob}(\Omega,g(t))\leq[2C_{2}(n)C_{1}(n)A,\frac{1}{\tau}]
. It follows from
[
5
, Proof of Theorem 3.4 and Appendix]
that
C_{1}(n)C_{2}(n)\leq 250n
. We will use this estimate in obtaining a bound for our constant
\varepsilon(n)
.

In the presence of a (possibly negative) lower Ricci curvature bound
\text{Rc}_{g}\geq-K
, Wang
[
20
, Theorem 3.6]
proved that one can obtain local control on the
\bar{\nu}
-functional depending on the volume ratio:

(6)

\bar{\nu}(B_{g}(x,r),g,r^{2})\geq-10n^{2}(1+Kr)+\log\left(\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r)}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\right)^{n+1}.

Here
\omega_{n}
is the volume of an
n
-dimensional Euclidean unit ball. In particular, when
\text{Rc}_{g}\geq 0
, we can obtain global control on the
\bar{\nu}
-functional (and subsequently a global Sobolev inequality) if we know that
all the volume ratios
of
(M,g)
are uniformly bounded from below. Such a manifold is said to have
positive asymptotic volume ratio
, which we will denote by

\operatorname{AVR}(g):=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x_{0},r)}{\omega^{n}r^{n}}.

It is well-known that when
\text{Rc}_{g}\geq 0
,
\operatorname{AVR}(g)
does not depend on the choice of fixed point
x_{0}
, is a well-defined number in
[0,1]
, and that
\operatorname{AVR}(g)=1
if and only if
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to
({\mathbb{R}}^{n},g_{E})
where
g_{E}
is the Euclidean metric.

Now suppose that
(M,g)
were some nonnegative Ricci curvature manifold with a known volume bound

\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,1)\geq v>0.

Then by (
6
) we may obtain
\bar{\nu}(B_{g}(x,1),g,1)\geq-C+\log(v^{\frac{n+1}{n}})
, which in turn gives
\operatorname{Sob}(B_{g}(x,1))\leq[C^{\prime}v^{-\frac{2(n+1)}{n}},1]
by Lemma
3.3
. Now if we return to a bound on the volume via Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,1)\geq cv^{n+1},

which is a much worse volume estimate than we started with as
v\to 0
. Therefore one step along this process is not sharp with respect to
v
. It turns out that this bluntness occurs in going from volume to
\bar{\nu}
-functional in (
6
), and is rectified in the following lemma. Put another way, Lemma
3.5
completes the circle of equivalences between volume ratios, Sobolev inequality, and control on the
\bar{\nu}
-functional (up to dimensional constants).

Lemma 3.5

(Sobolev from volume)
.

For any
n\geq 3
, there exists a dimensional constant
C(n)>0
such that the following holds. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold (not assumed bounded curvature) such that
\text{Rc}_{g}\geq 0
and
\operatorname{AVR}(g)>0
. If for some
r>0
and
v>0
there holds

(7)

\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r)}{r^{n}}=v,

then
\operatorname{Sob}(B_{g}(x,r))\leq[C(n)v^{-\frac{2}{n}},0]
.

Remark 3.6
.

The constant
v
in the volume ratio hypothesis (
7
) is permitted to be much larger than the value of
\omega_{n}\cdot\operatorname{AVR}(g)
. In fact, the assumption that
\operatorname{AVR}(g)>0
in Lemma
3.5
is likely superfluous because the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
(which we will be modifying) is local in nature until the end when they let
r\to\infty
(instead we will take a determined value of
r
). Regardless, the stated version will be sufficient for our purposes.

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

First note that by volume comparison, we have

\frac{v}{2^{n}}=\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r)}{(2r)^{n}}\leq\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,2r)}{(2r)^{n}}\leq\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r)}{r^{n}}=v.

Therefore, it suffices to prove the lemma with
v
replaced by

v_{0}:=\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,2r)}{(2r)^{n}},

at the expense of replacing the dimensional constant
C(n)
with (at worst)
2^{n}C(n)
.

Fix some smooth nonnegative function
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{g}(x,r))
with
u\equiv 0
on
\partial B_{g}(x,r)
. Since the hypotheses and conclusion of the lemma are invariant under scaling, we may assume that

(8)

\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}|\nabla^{g}u|_{g}\,dV_{g}=n\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dV_{g}.

If this seems like a somewhat mysterious condition, we refer the reader to the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
, which we will be modifying (note that the coefficient of
n
in (
8
) is only there to match the convention in
[
1
]
). We start with a couple elementary claims.

Claim 3.7
.

\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r)\leq(2r)^{n}(1-(\frac{1}{7})^{n})v_{0}

Proof of Claim.

Let
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M
be a geodesic ray with
\gamma(0)=x
. Setting
y=\gamma(3r/2)
, we therefore have

B_{g}(y,r/2)\subset B_{g}(x,2r)\setminus B_{g}(x,r).

Then by volume comparison and the fact that
B_{g}(y,\frac{7}{2}r)\supset B_{g}(x,2r)
, we can see that

\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(y,r/2)}{(r/2)^{n}}\geq\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(y,7r/2)}{(7r/2)^{n}}\geq\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,2r)}{(7r/2)^{n}}\geq\frac{\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,2r)}{(2r)^{n}}(4/7)^{n}=v_{0}(4/7)^{n}.

Then we obtain the upper bound

\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r)\leq\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,2r)-\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(y,r/2)\leq(2r)^{n}v_{0}-(2r/7)^{n}v_{0}=(2r)^{n}(1-(1/7)^{n})v_{0}.

∎

Claim 3.8
.

There exists dimensional constants
\delta(n)>0
,
C_{1}(n)\geq 1
such that for any measurable nonnegative function
f:B_{g}(x,r)\to{\mathbb{R}}
, there holds

\frac{1}{(2r)^{n}}\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}(1+f(x))^{n}\,dV_{g}\leq(1-\delta)v_{0}+\frac{C_{1}}{(2r)^{n}}\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}f(x)^{n}\,dV_{g}.

Proof of Claim.

Applying the Binomial Theorem gives

\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}(1+f(x))^{n}\,dV_{g}\leq\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}f(x)^{k}\,dV_{g}.

For the
1\leq k\leq n-1
terms, we write
p_{k}=\frac{n}{k}
,
q_{k}=\frac{n}{n-k}
and apply Hölder’s then Young’s inequalities

\displaystyle\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}f(x)^{k}\,dV_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}f(x)^{n}\,dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}(\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r))^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{k}^{q_{k}}}{q_{k}}\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}^{p_{k}}p_{k}}\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}f(x)^{n}\,dV_{g}.

We may choose the
\varepsilon_{k}
small enough so that

(1-(1/7)^{n})\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}{n\choose k}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}^{q_{k}}}{q_{k}}\right)\leq 1-\frac{1}{2}(1/7)^{n}.

Therefore, by applying Claim
3.7
, we can see that with
\delta(n)=\frac{1}{2}(1/7)^{n}
and

C_{1}(n)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}{n\choose k}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}^{p_{k}}p_{k}},

the claim holds.
∎

Now we recall some of the details from
[
1
]
. The following is taken out of the proof of their main theorem (specifically right up until the end of their Corollary 2.6 and the subsequent few lines), which we state as an independent result for clarity.

Proposition 3.9

(Proof of
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Suppose that
(M,g)
satisfies
\text{Rc}_{g}\geq 0
and
\operatorname{AVR}(g)>0
. Let
D\subset M
be some compact connected region with smooth boundary and
u\in C^{\infty}(D)
is nonnegative with
u\equiv 0
on
\partial D
and

\int_{D}|\nabla^{g}u|\,dV_{g}=n\int_{D}u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dV_{g}.

Then for all
\rho>0
, there holds

\text{Vol}_{g}\{y\in M:d_{g}(x,y)<\rho\;\text{ for all }x\in D\}\leq\int_{D}\left(1+\rho\,u^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\right)^{n}\,dV_{g}.

We apply Proposition
3.9
with
2
2

2

The smooth boundary condition can be accomplished by padding
B_{g}(x,r)
slightly by taking
B_{g}(x,r)\subset D_{\varepsilon}
, where
D_{\varepsilon}
has smooth boundary and is contained in the
\varepsilon

(see
[
20

), then take

D=B_{g}(x,r)
and

\text{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,2r)=\text{Vol}_{g}\{y\in M:d_{g}(p,y)<3r\;\text{ for all }p\in B_{g}(x,r)\}\leq\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}\left(1+3r\,u^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\right)^{n}\,dV_{g}.

Now divide both sides by

and apply Claim

with

to obtain

Rearranging and writing

Therefore, by applying our scaling condition (

\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}|\nabla^{g}u|_{g}\,dV_{g}=n\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dV_{g}\geq nv_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}C_{2}^{-\frac{1}{n}}\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Thus we obtain an

-Sobolev inequality for the given function
u

r

u\equiv 0
on
\partial B_{g}(x,r)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}u^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}\left(u^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(nv_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}C_{2}^{-\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{-2}\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}|\nabla^{g}u^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}}|_{g}\,dV_{g}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(nv_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}C_{2}^{-\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{-1}\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}\right)^{2}\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}u^{\frac{n}{n-2}}|\nabla^{g}u|_{g}\,dV_{g}\right)^{2}

-Sobolev inequality by rearranging the above and approximation. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

4.
Evolution of distances and curvature concentration along the Ricci flow

In this section, we first give two results which describe how the distance function behaves under the Ricci flow. In particular, these results will allow us to conclude that the radius of geodesic balls stay uniformly controlled both forward and backward in time. The first is the well-known “Shrinking Balls Lemma”
[
19
, Corollary 3.3]
which controlls the rate at which distances can contract.

Lemma 4.1

(Shrinking Balls
[
19
]
)
.

For any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\beta(n)=8\sqrt{2/3}(n-1)\geq 1
, the following holds. Suppose
(M^{n},g(t))
,
t\in[0,T]
is a (not necessarily complete)
n
-dimensional Ricci flow such that
B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r)\subset\subset M
and
|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}\leq c_{0}/t
on
B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r)\cap B_{g(t)}(x_{0},r-\beta\sqrt{c_{0}t})
for each
t\in(0,T]
and some
c_{0}>0
. Then for any
t\in[0,T]
, there holds

B_{g(t)}(x_{0},r-\beta\sqrt{c_{0}t})\subset B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r).

Obtaining control in the other direction (i.e., the rate at which distances can expand) is historically more challening; If it is known that the flow enjoys a lower Ricci bound
for all times
, then Simon & Topping have proved an analogous “Expanding Balls Lemma”
[
19
, Lemma 3.1]
. The issue is however that lower Ricci curvature bounds are not respected under Ricci flow when
n\geq 4
. Therefore it is desirable to replace the lower Ricci bound assumption with a lower
scalar curvature
bound (which is preserved) along with some volume estimates (see
[
15
, Lemma 3.7]
and
[
18
, Lemma 2.2]
for such results of this flavour). The improvement that we offer here is uniform control on the rate of the expansion depending only on the quotient of volume ratios, albeit for a short amount of time. Importantly, the time interval does
not
depend on the given volume bounds.

Lemma 4.2

(Expanding Balls)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\alpha,\sigma>0
, there exists
\widehat{T}(n,\alpha,\sigma)>0
such that the following holds. Let
(M^{n},g(t))
,
t\in[0,T]
be a complete bounded curvature Ricci flow such that for some
x_{0}\in M
,
b_{0}>0
, and
c_{0}>0
there holds:

(a)

\inf_{x\in M}{\mathcal{R}}_{g(0)}(x)\geq-\sigma
,

(b)

\sup_{x\in M}|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}(x)\leq\alpha t^{-1}
for all
t\in(0,T]
,

(c)

\text{Vol}_{g(0)}B_{g(0)}(x_{0},2)\leq b_{0}
, and

(d)

\text{Vol}_{g(t)}B_{g(t)}(x,r)\geq c_{0}r^{n}
whenever
x\in B_{g(0)}(x_{0},1)
,
r\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, and
t\in(0,T]
.

Then for all
t\in[0,T\land\widehat{T}]
, we have
B_{g(0)}(x_{0},1)\subset B_{g(t)}(x_{0},2^{n+2}b_{0}c_{0}^{-1})
.

Proof.

We begin by setting our maximal time interval:

\widehat{T}=\min\left\{\frac{\log 2}{\sigma},\frac{1}{4\beta^{2}\alpha}\right\}.

Here
\beta=\beta(n)
is from Lemma
4.1
. Fix
\tau\in[0,T\land\widehat{T}]
and define

R=\inf\{r>0:B_{g(0)}(x_{0},1)\subset B_{g(\tau)}(x_{0},r)\}.

We will show that
R\leq 2^{n+2}b_{0}c_{0}^{-1}
which will prove the result. By definition, there exists
y\in M
such that
d_{g(0)}(x_{0},y)=1
and
d_{g(\tau)}(x_{0},y)=R
. Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
be a minimizing unit-speed
g(0)
-geodesic with
\gamma(0)=x_{0}
and
\gamma(1)=y
. Notice that because
\widehat{T}\leq\frac{1}{4\beta^{2}\alpha}
, we have that
B_{g(\tau)}(x,1/2)\subset B_{g(0)}(x,1)
for all
x\in M
by Lemma
4.1
. Thus by taking
z_{1}=x_{0}
, we can inductively choose some set of points
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k}\subset B_{g(0)}(x_{0},1)
such that
\{B_{g(\tau)}(z_{j},\frac{1}{2})\}_{j=1}^{k}
are mutually disjoint and

\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{k}B_{g(\tau)}(z_{j},1/2)\subset B_{g(0)}(x_{0},2)\;\;\text{ and }\;\;B_{g(0)}(x_{0},1)\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}B_{g(\tau)}(z_{j},1).

Then since
\gamma\subset\overline{B_{g(0)}(x_{0},1)}
, we can obtain a lower bound on
d_{g(\tau)}(x_{0},y)
by estimating the length of
\gamma
with respect to the metric
g(\tau)
as

(10)

R=d_{g(\tau)}(x_{0},y)\leq\text{Length}_{g(\tau)}(\gamma)\leq 2k.

By our assumptions on the bounds of volumes and the assumed disjointness of
\{B_{g(\tau)}(z_{j},\frac{1}{2})\}_{j=1}^{k}
, we can obtain an upper bound on
k
:

(11)

\begin{split}kc_{0}(1/2)^{n}&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{Vol}_{g(\tau)}B_{g(\tau)}(z_{j},1/2)\\
&\leq\text{Vol}_{g(\tau)}(B_{g(0)}(x_{0},2))\\
&\leq 2\text{Vol}_{g(0)}(B_{g(0)}(x_{0},2))\leq 2b_{0}.\end{split}

In the third inequality, we have used the evolution of the volume form
\partial_{t}dV_{g(t)}=-{\mathcal{R}}_{g(t)}dV_{g(t)}
, the lower scalar bound
{\mathcal{R}}_{g(0)}\geq-\sigma
(which is preserved along complete bounded curvature flows), and the condition on the existence time
\sigma\widehat{T}\leq\log 2
. By (
10
) and (
11
), we have the desired bound
R\leq 2^{n+2}b_{0}c_{0}^{-1}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Because local lower bounds on the scalar curvature
{\mathcal{R}}_{g(0)}
are semi-preserved along all Ricci flows with
\alpha/t
curvature control
[
8
]
, it is clear that Lemma
4.2
can be localized (i.e., for some compact region contained within a possibly incomplete and/or unbounded curvature flow), but the stated version will suffice for our purposes.

We end this section by giving a lemma demonstrating how the local curvature concentration evolves under the Ricci flow. The proof is essentially that of
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
, but we provide a brief sketch to demonstrate explicit estimates on the coefficients.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
(M,g(t))
,
n\geq 3
,
t\in[0,T]
is a complete (possibly unbounded curvature) Ricci flow and
\phi:M\times[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a smooth nonnegative function with compact support. If
\square\phi\leq 0
(here
\square=\partial_{t}-\Delta^{g(t)}
is the heat operator), then for any
\beta>0
there holds

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\phi^{2}

\displaystyle(|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{2}+\beta)^{\frac{n}{4}}\,dV_{g(t)}\leq-\frac{1}{3}\int|\nabla(\phi(|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{2}+\beta)^{\frac{n}{8}})|^{2}\,dV_{g(t)}

\displaystyle\;\;\;+10n\int|\nabla^{g(t)}\phi|_{g(t)}^{2}(|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{2}+\beta)^{\frac{n}{4}}\,dV_{g(t)}+5n\int\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{2}+\beta)^{\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}}\,dV_{g(t)}.

Proof.

In this proof, we will denote
\alpha=\frac{n}{4}
and we will not write the metric
g(t)
in any terms except for the volume form
dV_{t}
. We begin by differentiating under the integral, integrating by parts (since
\text{supp}\,\phi
is compact), using the evolution of volume
\partial_{t}\,dV_{t}=-{\mathcal{R}}\,dV_{t}
, and
|{\mathcal{R}}|\leq n|\text{Rm}|
(see for instance
[
17
, Proposition 7.28]
) which gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha}\,dV_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\int\square\left(\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha}\right)\,dV_{t}+n\int\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\,dV_{t}.

For the
\square
term, we calculate

\displaystyle\int\square

\displaystyle\left(\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha}\right)\,dV_{t}=\int 2\phi\square\phi(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha}\,dV_{t}-\int 2|\nabla\phi|^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha}\,dV_{t}

\displaystyle+\int\alpha\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha-1}\square|\text{Rm}|^{2}\,dV_{t}-\int 4\alpha(\alpha-1)\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha-2}|\text{Rm}|^{2}|\nabla|\text{Rm}||^{2}\,dV_{t}

\displaystyle-\int 8\alpha\phi\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla|\text{Rm}|\rangle|\text{Rm}|(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha-1}\,dV_{t}=\textbf{I}+\textbf{II}+\textbf{III}+\textbf{IV}+\textbf{V}.

Clearly
\textbf{II}\leq 0
and since
\square\phi\leq 0
, we have
\textbf{I}\leq 0
as well. Also, by the evolution of the curvature tensor and Kato’s inequality
|\nabla|T||\leq|\nabla T|
, we have

III

\displaystyle\leq-2\alpha\int\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha-2}|\text{Rm}|^{2}|\nabla|\text{Rm}||^{2}\,dV_{t}+16\alpha\int\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\,dV_{t}=\textbf{IIIa}+\textbf{IIIb}.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequality gives

V

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\alpha}{\varepsilon}\int|\nabla\phi|^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha}\,dV_{t}+4\alpha\varepsilon\int\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha-2}|\text{Rm}|^{2}|\nabla|\text{Rm}||^{2}\,dV_{t}=\textbf{Va}+\textbf{Vb}.

Now gather up the terms
IV
,
IIIa
,
Vb
, and set
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{8}
so that the coefficient is
c(\alpha):=\frac{3}{2}\alpha+4\alpha(\alpha-1)
, which satisfies
-c(\alpha)\in[\frac{2}{3}\alpha^{2},4\alpha^{2}]
. Then using Cauchy-Schwarz again gives

\displaystyle c(\alpha)

\displaystyle\int\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha-2}|\text{Rm}|^{2}|\nabla|\text{Rm}||^{2}\,dV_{t}=c(\alpha)\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\int\phi^{2}|\nabla(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}|^{2}\,dV_{t}

\displaystyle\leq c(\alpha)\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla\phi(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}|^{2}\,dV_{t}-\int|\nabla\phi|^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha}\,dV_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{3}\int|\nabla\phi(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}|^{2}\,dV_{t}+4\int|\nabla\phi|^{2}(|\text{Rm}|^{2}+\beta)^{\alpha}\,dV_{t}.

The result follows by gathering like terms and simplifying.
∎

5.
A bounded curvature Ricci flow existence result

As part of the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we will require a short-time Ricci flow existence result for bounded curvature metrics with the properties that the flow enjoys
1/t
curvature control and preservation of the local curvature concentration (see Theorem
5.2
). This result is similar to
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
, which in turn is only a slight generalization of
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
. Because of these similarities, we may omit some of the details in the proof of Theorem
5.2
when appropriate and refer the reader to those works. Before stating that however, we first prove an elementary lemma which states that the local curvature concentration can only grow at most at some uniform rate under bounded curvature flows.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))
,
t\in[0,T]
,
n\geq 3
be a complete bounded curvature Ricci flow such that

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{B_{g(0)}(x,r_{1})}|\text{Rm}|_{g(0)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(0)}\leq\sigma

for some
r_{1}>0
,
\sigma>0
. Then for any
r_{2}<r_{1}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\eta>0
such that for each
t\in[0,\eta]
, there holds

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{B_{g(t)}(x,r_{2})}|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(t)}\leq\sigma+\varepsilon.

Proof.

For the sake of brevity, we write
K=\sup|\text{Rm}(x,t)|+1
where the supremum is taken over all
(x,t)\in M\times[0,T]
. In what follows, we will write
C
to be some constant possibly depending on
n,r_{1},r_{2},\sigma,K
which may vary line-by-line. Let
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be some smooth decreasing cutoff function such that
\psi\equiv 1
on
(-\infty,0]
,
\psi\equiv 0
on
[1,\infty)
and
\psi^{\prime}\geq-2
. Now for any
x\in M
, let
\phi_{x}:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
be defined as

\phi_{x}(y)=\psi\left(\frac{2}{r_{1}-r_{2}}d_{g(0)}(x,y)-\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{r_{1}-r_{2}}\right).

Then
\text{supp}\,\phi_{x}\subset B_{g(0)}(x,r_{1})
,
\phi_{x}\equiv 1
on
B_{g(0)}(x,\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{2})
, and
|\nabla^{g(0)}\phi_{x}|^{2}_{g(0)}\leq C
. By the fact that the flow has bounded curvature, we may assume that
\eta>0
is small enough so that for all
(x,t)\in M\times[0,\eta]
, we have

\begin{cases}&B_{g(t)}(x,r_{2})\subset B_{g(0)}(x,\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{2}),\\
&|\nabla^{g(t)}\phi_{x}|^{2}_{g(t)}\leq 2|\nabla^{g(0)}\phi_{x}|^{2}_{g(0)},\text{ and }\\
&\text{Vol}_{g(t)}B_{g(0)}(x,r_{1})\leq C.\end{cases}

Now for any
0<\beta\leq K
, we apply
[
5
, Lemma 4.1]
to see that

\frac{d}{dt}\int\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{2}+\beta)^{\frac{n}{4}}\,dV_{g(t)}\leq C\int\phi^{2}(|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{2}+\beta)^{\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}}\,dV_{g(t)}+C\int|\nabla^{g(t)}\phi|^{2}_{g(t)}(|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{2}+\beta)^{\frac{n}{4}}\,dV_{g(t)}\leq C.

Integrating in time then sending
\beta\to 0
, we can apply our properties of
\phi
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{g(t)}(x_{0},r_{2})}|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\int\phi^{2}|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(t)}\leq\int\phi^{2}|\text{Rm}|_{g(0)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(0)}+Ct

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{1})}|\text{Rm}|_{g(0)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(0)}+Ct.

The result follows by restricting
\eta\leq\varepsilon/C
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

For all
n\geq 3
,
\lambda\geq 1
,
v_{0}>0
, there exists
C(n)\geq 1
,
c(n)>0
,
\iota(n,\lambda,v_{0})
,
\delta(n,\lambda)>0
,
\Lambda(n,\lambda)>1
, and
\widetilde{T}(n,\lambda,v_{0})>0
such that the following holds. Suppose that
(M^{n},g(t))
,
t\in[0,T]
is a complete bounded curvature Ricci flow and the initial metric
g(0)
satisfies the following for all
x\in M
:

(a)

\text{Rc}_{g(0)}(x)\geq-1
;

(b)

\text{Vol}_{g(0)}B_{g(0)}(x,2r)\leq 2^{2n+1}\cdot\text{Vol}_{g(0)}B_{g(0)}(x,r)\leq 2^{2n+2}\omega_{n}r^{n}
for all
r\leq 4
; and

(c)

\int_{B_{g(0)}(x,4)}|\text{Rm}|_{g(0)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(0)}\leq\delta(n,\lambda)v_{0}
.

Moreover for each
x\in M
and
r\in(0,1]
, assume that

v_{xr}:=\frac{\text{Vol}_{g(0)}B_{g(0)}(x,r)}{r^{n}}\geq v_{0}

and that

(12)

\operatorname{Sob}(B_{g(0)}(x,4r),g(0))\leq\left[\lambda v_{xr}^{-\frac{2}{n}},0\right].

Then the flow
g(t)
satisfies each of the following estimates uniformly for all
(x,t)\in M\times[0,\widetilde{T}\land T]
:

(i)

{\mathcal{R}}_{g(t)}(x)\geq-n
;

(ii)

|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}(x)\leq\frac{1}{t}
;

(iii)

\operatorname{inj}_{g(t)}(x)\geq\iota(n,\lambda,v_{0})\sqrt{t}
;

(iv)

\text{Vol}_{g(t)}B_{g(t)}(x,r)\geq c(n)\lambda^{-\frac{n}{2}}v_{0}\,r^{n}
for all
r\in(0,1]
;

(v)

\int_{B_{g(t)}(x,1)}|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(t)}\leq\Lambda(n,\lambda)\delta(n,\lambda)v_{0}
; and

(vi)

\operatorname{Sob}(B_{g(t)}(x,r),g(t))\leq\left[C(n)\lambda v_{xr}^{-\frac{2}{n}},1\right]
for all
r\in(0,1]
.

In particular, the flow exists up until time
\widetilde{T}
.

Remark 5.3
.

The lower Ricci bound assumption (
a
) can be weakened to a lower bound on the scalar curvature
{\mathcal{R}}_{g(0)}\geq-1
in the precense of the volume doubling assumption (
b
), at the expense of possibly shrinking
\delta(n,\lambda)
. We choose to state the theorem like this because it is more natural in our setting, and because this stronger hypothesis allows us to substitute a limiting contradiction argument for the Moser Iteration argument (see Step 2 in the proof).

Proof.

Note that since lower scalar curvature bounds are preserved by complete bounded curvature flows, conclusion (
i
) is trivially satisfied (we have included it in the theorem statement for clarity later). We begin the proof of the other properties by fixing our constants. First, we set

(13)

\widetilde{T}:=\min\left\{T_{1},C_{4}^{-1},\frac{1}{16}\beta^{-2},\frac{1}{4}C_{3}^{-1},\frac{\log 2}{10},\frac{r_{0}^{4}}{10^{4}},r_{0}^{2}\widehat{T}\right\}.

Here
T_{1}=T_{1}(n,\lambda,v_{0})>0
is some positive time coming from Step 2 of the proof,
\beta(n)
is from the Shrinking Balls Lemma
4.1
,
\widehat{T}=\widehat{T}(n,1,1)
is from the Expanding Balls Lemma
4.2
, and
c_{1}(n)
,
C_{3}(n)
,
C_{4}(n,\lambda)
,
r_{0}(n,\lambda)
are constants that will be defined within the proof. Also, we define

(14)

\delta(n,\lambda)=\Lambda(n,\lambda)^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{10^{7}n^{3}\lambda}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}},

where
\Lambda(n,\lambda)
is defined in (
29
). We will see in Step 1 of the proof that we may take

C(n)=1000n\;\;\text{ and }\;\;c(n)=(2^{n+4}\cdot 1000n)^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

Finally, recall that condition (
iii
) follows from the conclusions (
ii
) and (
iv
) by
[
7
]
, so we will assume that the
\iota(n,\lambda,v_{0})>0
is the constant given there.

Now we may proceed with the proof. Let
T^{\prime}
be the maximal time in the interval
[0,T\land\widetilde{T}]
such that for all
(x,t)\in M\times[0,T^{\prime}]
, there holds

(15)

\int_{B_{g(t)}(x,1)}|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(t)}\leq\Lambda(n,\lambda)\delta(n,\lambda)v_{0}.

Note that because
(M,g(t))
,
t\in[0,T]
is a bounded curvature flow which satisfies (
c
), Lemma
5.1
guarantees that
T^{\prime}>0
. In advance however, it appears as though
T^{\prime}
depends on the curvature bound of the flow
g(t)
, but we will show that this is not the case.

Our proof is broken down into three steps: (1) Show we have a uniform local Sobolev inequality (i.e., conclusion (
vi
)) along the flow on the interval
[0,T^{\prime}]
assuming the time interval is possibly shortened so that a global
\alpha/t
curvature bound holds, which in particular implies conclusion (
iv
); (2) Show that the assumption of the global
\alpha/t
curvature bound assumption in Step 1 is implied as long as
T^{\prime}\leq\widetilde{T}
(this gives conclusions (
ii
) and (
iii
)); (3) Derive a contradiction in the event that
T^{\prime}<T\land\widetilde{T}
.

Step 1 (Sobolev inequality)

Fix some
r_{0}(n,\lambda)\in(0,1)
to be defined later (in hindsight, we could take
r_{0}=[2^{3n+6}(2^{n+4}1000n\lambda)^{\frac{n}{2}}]^{-1}
). Since
(M,g(t))
,
t\in[0,T]
is a bounded curvature flow, we may assume that there is some
T_{1}>0
such that

(16)

|\text{Rm}|_{g(t)}\leq\frac{3r_{0}/10}{t}\;\;\text{ for all }t\in(0,T_{1}].

In this step of the proof, we will restrict to the interval
[0,T_{1}\land T^{\prime}]
. We will show in Step 2 that it is necessarily true that
T_{1}
can be taken to depend only on the variables
n,\lambda,v_{0}
.

Fix some
x\in M
and
r\in[r_{0},1]
. By our smallness condition on
\delta(n,\lambda)
in (
14
) and our assumptions (
12
) and (
c
), as well as the fact that
v_{x,r}\geq v_{0}
, we may apply Lemma
3.2
(with
a=0
) to obtain

\nu(B_{g(0)}(x,4r),g(0),2)\geq-\frac{n}{2}\log(C_{1}(n)\lambda v_{x,r}^{-\frac{2}{n}}).

Now by our curvature assumption (
16
), we apply the “almost-monotonicity” of this local functional along the Ricci flow
[
10
, Lemma 2.5]
(with the variables there being
A=\frac{3r}{10},B=\frac{1}{4}
,
D=0
,
\tau_{1}=1
) to see that for each
y\in B_{g(0)}(x,r)
and
t\in[0,T_{1}\land T^{\prime}]
there holds

\displaystyle\nu(B_{g(t)}(y,r),g(t),1)

\displaystyle\geq\nu(B_{g(0)}(y,3r),g(0),2)-\left(\frac{2}{10(3r/10)^{2}(1/4)^{2}}+e^{-1}\right)(e^{\frac{T^{\prime}}{10(3r/10)^{2}(1/4)^{2}}}-1)

\displaystyle\geq\nu(B_{g(0)}(x,4r),g(0),2)-\frac{n}{2}\log 2\geq-\frac{n}{2}\log(2C_{1}(n)\lambda v_{x,r}^{-\frac{2}{n}}).

Note that in the second inequality, we have used the fact that
T^{\prime}\leq\widetilde{T}\leq\frac{r_{0}^{4}}{10^{4}}
so that

\left(\frac{2}{10(3r/10)^{2}(1/4)^{2}}+e^{-1}\right)(e^{\frac{T^{\prime}}{10(3r/10)^{2}(1/4)^{2}}}-1)\leq\frac{100}{r^{2}}(e^{\frac{50T^{\prime}}{r^{2}}}-1)\leq 10^{4}r_{0}^{-4}\widetilde{T}\leq 1<\frac{n}{2}\log 2.

Since our smallness condition on
\delta
in (
14
) in particular implies that

\delta(n,\lambda)\leq\Lambda(n,\lambda)^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{250n\lambda}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}},

then by Lemma
3.3
and Remark
3.4
, we obtain (with
C_{2}(n)=1000n
) the uniform Sobolev inequality

(17)

\operatorname{Sob}(B_{g(t)}(y,r),g(t))\leq[C_{2}(n)\lambda v_{x,r}^{-\frac{2}{n}},1]

whenever
y\in B_{g(0)}(x,r)
, which gives conclusion (
vi
). Note that by Lemma
3.1
, we can also say that

(18)

\text{Vol}_{g(t)}B_{g(t)}(y,\rho)\geq c_{1}(n)\lambda^{-\frac{n}{2}}v_{x,r}\rho^{n}\;\;\text{ whenever }\;y\in B_{g(0)}(x,r),\;\rho\in(0,r],\;t\in[0,T_{1}\land T^{\prime}].

This gives conclusion (
iv
) since
v_{x,r}\geq v_{0}
. Furthermore, note that
c_{1}(n)\geq(2^{n+4}\cdot C_{2}(n))^{-\frac{n}{2}}
. We also point out that although the work in this step was valid for any
r\in[r_{0},1]
, we will only need to apply it with
r=r_{0}
and
r=1
. This volume ratio estimate, together with the assumed curvature bound on
(0,T_{1}\land T^{\prime}]
imply (by
[
7
]
) the injectivity radius bound

(19)

\operatorname{inj}_{g(t)}(x)\geq\iota(n,\lambda,v_{0})\sqrt{t}\;\text{ for all }x\in M,\;t\in(0,T_{1}\land T^{\prime}].

This gives conclusion (
iii
).

Step 2 (Curvature bound)

In this step of the proof, we will show that the flow
g(t)
satisfies the curvature bound (
16
) as long as (
15
) holds on the time interval
[0,T^{\prime}]
, assuming that
T^{\prime}\leq T_{1}
where
T_{1}
depends only on
n,\lambda,v_{0}
. Just like the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
, we could do this by applying Moser’s iteration, which is well-known to experts but is a somewhat technical argument, especially in our setting where we must prove that the smallness of
\int|\text{Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}}
need only depend linearly on
v
. Instead, we will argue by contradiction by employing a limiting contradiction argument with respect to the existence time. Thus we may assume that for some
n\geq 3
,
\lambda\geq 1
,
v_{0}>0
, there exists a sequence of times
T_{i}\to 0
and associated bounded curvature Ricci flows
(M_{i}^{n},g_{i}(t))
,
t\in[0,T_{i}]
each satisfying the hypotheses of the theorem and such that

\int_{B_{g_{i}(t)}(x,1)}|\text{Rm}|_{g_{i}(t)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g_{i}(t)}\leq\Lambda\delta v_{0}

for all
(x,t)\in M_{i}\times[0,T_{i}]
, but which do not enjoy a
\alpha/t
curvature bound on the interval
[0,T_{i}]
, where
\alpha=3r_{0}/10
. Since each
(M_{i},g_{i}(t))
,
t\in[0,T_{i}]
is assumed to be a bounded curvature flow, we may assume that there is some
first time

t_{i}\in[0,T_{i}]
which fails our desired bound. In other words we assume that

|\text{Rm}|_{g_{i}(t_{i})}(x_{i})=\frac{\alpha}{t_{i}}

for some
x_{i}\in M_{i}
and that

|\text{Rm}|_{g_{i}(t)}\leq\frac{\alpha}{t}\;\text{ on }M_{i}\times(0,t_{i}].

Moreover by Step 1, we know that the Sobolev inequality (
17
) and volume ratio bound (
18
) hold with
r=1
,
\rho=\sqrt{t}
,
v_{x,1}=v_{0}
. Recall this in turn gave the injectivity radius estimate (
19
) for each
g_{i}(t)
on
(0,t_{i}]
.

Now parabolically rescale the
g_{i}(t)
so that
t_{i}\nearrow 1
, which is to say we define the sequence of complete flows
\tilde{g}_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{t_{i}}g_{i}(t_{i}t)
, each defined on
[0,1]
, which satisfy

(20)

\begin{cases}&\text{Rc}_{\tilde{g}_{i}(0)}\geq-t_{i},\;\;{\mathcal{R}}_{\tilde{g}_{i}(t)}\geq-nt_{i},\\
&|\text{Rm}|_{\tilde{g}_{i}(1)}(x_{i})=\alpha,\\
&|\text{Rm}|_{\tilde{g}_{i}(t)}\leq\frac{\alpha}{t}\;\text{ for all }t\in(0,1],\\
&\int_{\Omega_{i}}|\text{Rm}|_{\tilde{g}_{i}(1)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{\tilde{g}_{i}(1)}\leq\Lambda\delta v_{0},\\
&\operatorname{inj}_{\tilde{g}_{i}(1)}(x_{i})\geq\iota(n,\lambda,v_{0}),\text{ and }\\
&\operatorname{Sob}(\Omega_{i},\tilde{g}(1))\leq[C_{2}(n)\lambda v_{0}^{-\frac{2}{n}},t_{i}].\end{cases}

Here we have denoted
\Omega_{i}=B_{\tilde{g}(1)}(x,t_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}})
. Therefore we may apply Hamilton’s Compactness Theorem
[
14
]
and a diagonal argument to say that
(M_{i},\tilde{g}_{i}(t),x_{i})
subconverges (in the pointed Cheeger-Gromov sense) to some complete Ricci flow
(N,g_{\infty}(t),x_{\infty})
,
t\in(0,1]
, which by virtue of the properties (
20
), must satisfy

\begin{cases}&{\mathcal{R}}_{g_{\infty}(t)}\geq 0\;\text{ for all }t\in(0,1],\\
&|\text{Rm}|_{g_{\infty}(1)}(x_{\infty})=\alpha,\\
&\sup_{x\in N}|\text{Rm}|_{g_{\infty}(t)}(x)\leq\frac{\alpha}{t}\;\text{ for all }t\in(0,1],\\
&\int_{N}|\text{Rm}|_{g_{\infty}(1)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g_{\infty}(1)}\leq\Lambda\delta v_{0},\;\text{ and }\\
&\operatorname{Sob}(N,g_{\infty}(1))\leq[C_{2}(n)\lambda v_{0}^{-\frac{2}{n}},0].\end{cases}

Because
C_{2}(n)=1000n
, our requirement on
\delta(n,\lambda)
in (
14

exhausting
M
, and metrics
g_{j}
on
U_{j}
such that each
(U_{j},g_{j})
is compete with bounded curvature and for any compact
\Omega\subset M
,
g_{j}|_{\Omega}=g|_{\Omega}
for all
j
large enough. Moreover each of the following conditions holds uniformly for every
x\in U_{j}
:

(i)

\text{Rc}_{g_{j}}(x)\geq-1
;

(ii)

\text{Vol}_{g_{j}}B_{g_{j}}(x,2r)\leq 2^{2n+1}\text{Vol}_{g_{j}}B_{g_{j}}(x,r)\leq 2^{2n+2}\omega_{n}r^{n}
for each
r\leq 4
;

(iii)

\int_{B_{g_{j}}(x,4)}|\text{Rm}|_{g_{j}}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g_{j}}\leq 4\alpha
;

(iv)

\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{j}}B_{g_{j}}(x,r)}{r^{n}}\geq\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}v
for each
r\leq 4
; and

(v)

\operatorname{Sob}(B_{g_{j}}(x,r),g_{j})\leq\left[C(n)\left(\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{j}}B_{g_{j}}(x,r)}{r^{n}}\right)^{-\frac{2}{n}},0\right]
for each
r\leq 4
.

Proof.

By the scale invariance of the hypotheses and each of the conclusions save (
i
), we may prove the theorem with
g_{0}=\frac{1}{32}g
which allows us to only require that conditions (
ii
), (
iii
), (
iv
), (
v
) only hold for scales
r\leq 1/2
(or larger). Clearly Lemma
6.2
allows us to apply Theorem
6.1
, which gives the existence of the
(U_{j},g_{j})
. Moreover, properties (
i
), (
ii
), (
iii
) follow immediately from Theorem
6.1
as well because
\text{Rc}_{g_{0}}\geq 0
and volume comparison implies

\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{0}}B_{g_{0}}(x,2r)}{(2r)^{n}}\leq\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{0}}B_{g_{0}}(x,r)}{r^{n}}\leq\omega_{n}

for all
r>0
. Now fix some
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
x\in U_{j}
,
r\in(0,1]
and write
\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{j}}B_{g_{j}}(x,r)}{r^{n}}=v_{xjr}
. By the locally equivalent condition (
32
) and the fact that
\operatorname{AVR}(g_{0})\geq v
, we obtain

v_{xjr}\geq c_{x,j}^{n}\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{0}}B_{g_{j}}(x,r)}{r^{n}}\geq c_{x,j}^{n}\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{0}}B_{g_{0}}(x,C_{x,j}^{-1}r)}{r^{n}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{0}}B_{g_{0}}(x,C_{x,j}^{-1}r)}{(C_{x,j}^{-1}r)^{n}}\geq\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}v,

which proves (
iv
). Now it remains to show (
v
). Just as above, we may obtain an upper volume ratio bound:

v_{xjr}\leq C_{x,j}^{n}\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{0}}B_{g_{j}}(x,r)}{r^{n}}\leq C_{x,j}^{n}\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{0}}B_{g_{0}}(x,c_{x,j}^{-1}r)}{r^{n}}\leq 2\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{0}}B_{g_{0}}(x,c_{x,j}^{-1}r)}{(c_{x,j}^{-1}r)^{n}}.

Because
\text{Rc}_{g_{0}}\geq 0
and
C_{x,j}^{-1}r\leq c_{x,j}^{-1}r
, we may once again use volume comparison to say that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{0}}B_{g_{0}}(x,c_{x,j}^{-1}r)}{(c_{x,j}^{-1}r)^{n}}\leq v_{xjr}\leq 2\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{0}}B_{g_{0}}(x,c_{x,j}^{-1}r)}{(c_{x,j}^{-1}r)^{n}}\;\iff\;\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{0}}B_{g_{0}}(x,c_{x,j}^{-1}r)}{(c_{x,j}^{-1}r)^{n}}\in\left[\frac{1}{2}v_{xjr},2v_{xjr}\right].

Now by Lemma
3.5
, we obtain the Sobolev inequality

\operatorname{Sob}(B_{g_{0}}(x,rc_{x,j}^{-1}),g_{0})\leq\left[C_{1}(n)\left(\frac{v_{xjr}}{2}\right)^{-\frac{2}{n}},0\right]

for some dimensional constant
C_{1}(n)\geq 1
. Since
B_{g_{j}}(x,r)\subset B_{g_{0}}(x,rc_{x,j}^{-1})
, we may again apply the locally equivalent condition (
32
) to obtain Sobolev inequality for
g_{j}
:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{g_{j}}(x,r)}|u|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dV_{g_{j}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{C_{x,j}}{c_{x,j}}\right)^{n-2}C_{1}(n)\left(\frac{v_{xjr}}{2}\right)^{-\frac{2}{n}}\int_{B_{g_{j}}(x,r)}|\nabla^{g_{j}}u|^{2}_{g_{j}}\,dV_{g_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{1}(n)v_{xjr}^{-\frac{2}{n}}\int_{B_{g_{j}}(x,r)}|\nabla^{g_{j}}u|^{2}_{g_{j}}\,dV_{g_{j}}

for all
u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(B_{g_{j}}(x,r))
. This gives (
v
) and completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Given
n\geq 4
and
v\in(0,1]
, let
(M^{n},g)
satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
1.2
with this choice of
n,v
and fix some
x_{0}\in M
. For any
R\geq 1
, denote
g_{R}=R^{-2}g
, which still satisfies
\operatorname{AVR}(g_{R})\geq v
and

\int_{M}|\text{Rm}|_{g_{R}}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g_{R}}\leq\varepsilon(n)v.

Then by Proposition
6.3
, we obtain a sequence of sets
U_{R,j}\subset M
exhausting
M
and complete bounded curvature metrics
g_{R,j}
on
U_{R,j}
which satisfy
g_{R,j}=g_{R}
on
B_{g_{R}}(x_{0},1)
. Moreover each of the following conditions holds uniformly for every
x\in U_{R,j}
:

(i)

\text{Rc}_{g_{R,j}}(x)\geq-1
;

(ii)

\text{Vol}_{g_{R,j}}B_{g_{R,j}}(x,2r)\leq 2^{2n+1}\text{Vol}_{g_{R,j}}B_{g_{R,j}}(x,r)\leq 2^{2n+2}\omega_{n}r^{n}
for each
r\leq 4
;

(iii)

\int_{B_{g_{R,j}}(x,4)}|\text{Rm}|_{g_{R,j}}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g_{R,j}}\leq 4\varepsilon(n)v
;

(iv)

\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{R,j}}B_{g_{R,j}}(x,r)}{r^{n}}\geq\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}v
for each
r\in(0,1]
; and

(v)

\operatorname{Sob}(B_{g_{j}}(x,r),g_{j})\leq\left[C_{1}(n)\left(\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{j}}B_{g_{j}}(x,r)}{r^{n}}\right)^{-\frac{2}{n}},0\right]
for each
r\in(0,4]
.

Writing
v_{0}=\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}v
, we replace condition (
iv
) with

v_{xRjr}:=\frac{\text{Vol}_{g_{R,j}}B_{g_{R,j}}(x,r)}{r^{n}}\geq v_{0}\;\text{ for each }r\in(0,1],

and replace condition (
v
) with

\operatorname{Sob}(B_{g_{j}}(x,4r),g_{j})\leq\left[4^{2}C_{1}(n)v_{xRjr}^{-\frac{2}{n}},0\right]\;\text{ for each }r\in(0,1].

If we write
\lambda=4^{2}C_{1}(n)
and define

(33)

\varepsilon(n)=\frac{\delta(n,\lambda)\omega_{n}}{8},

then we may apply Theorem
5.2
to each metric
g_{R,j}
. Taking the diagonal scale normalized sequence
g_{j}=j^{2}g_{j,j}
, the result is a sequence of flows
g_{j}(t)
,

such that
g_{j}(0)=g
on
B_{g}(x_{0},j)
and for each

, all of the following are satisfied for every

:

(i)

{\mathcal{R}}_{g_{j}(t)}(x)\geq-nj^{-2}
;

(ii)

|\text{Rm}|_{g_{j}(t)}(x)\leq\frac{1}{t}
;

(iii)

\operatorname{inj}_{g_{j}(t)}(x)\geq\iota(n,v)\sqrt{t}

(iv)

\text{Vol}_{g_{j}(t)}B_{g_{j}(t)}(x,r)\geq c_{1}(n)v\,r^{n}

(v)

\int_{B_{g_{j}(t)}(x,j)}|\text{Rm}|_{g_{j}(t)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g_{j}(t)}\leq\Lambda(n,\lambda)\delta(n,\lambda)v_{0}

\operatorname{Sob}(B_{g_{j}(t)}(x,j),g_{j}(t))\leq[C_{2}(n)\lambda v_{0}^{-\frac{2}{n}},\frac{1}{j^{2}}]

Then just as in the proof of

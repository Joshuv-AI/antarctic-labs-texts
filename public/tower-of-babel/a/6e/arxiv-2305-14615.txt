Abstract
. In this paper, we establish the boundary regularity results for viscosity solutions of fully nonlinear degenerate/singular parabolic equations of the form u t − x n γ ​ F ​ ( D 2 ​ u , x , t ) = f , u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)=f, where γ < 1 \gamma<1 . These equations are motivated by the porous media type equations. We show the boundary C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} -regularity of functions in their solutions class and the boundary C 2 , α C^{2,\alpha} -regularity of solutions. As an application, we derive the global regularity results and the solvability of the Cauchy-Dirichlet problems.

Introduction

In this paper, we study regularity results for viscosity solutions of the following fully nonlinear degenerate/singular parabolic equations

(1.1)

u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)=f,

where
\gamma<1
and
f
is a bounded continuous function. Here the fully nonlinear operator F is uniformly parabolic with certain structural conditions (the hypotheses on F will be precisely stated in
Section

2
).

The fully nonlinear parabolic equations (
1.1
) are motivated by the porous media type equations

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-F(D^{2}u^{m})&=0&&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty)\\
u&=u_{0}&&\text{in }\Omega\times\{t=0\}\\
u&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times[0,\infty),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth bounded domain and
u_{0}>0
in
\Omega
. By setting
v=u^{m}
, the Cauchy-Dirichlet problems (
1.2
) can be transformed into the following form

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} v_{t}-v^{\gamma}\widetilde{F}(D^{2}v)&=0&&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty)\\
v&=u_{0}^{m}&&\text{in }\Omega\times\{t=0\}\\
v&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times[0,\infty),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\gamma=1-1/m\in(-\infty,1)
and
\widetilde{F}(M)=mF(M)
; we refer to
[
9
, Section 5]
. In their work
[
5
,
8
]
, it is demonstrated that (
1.1
) can be derived by applying the hodograph transform to (
1.3
). Moreover, they investigate the regularity results for solutions and free boundary of the porous medium equation by analyzing the regularity for solutions of (
1.1
). Besides the porous medium equation, degenerate/singular equations on flat boundaries appear in various nonlinear problems with applications; for example, Monge–Ampère equations, Gauss curvature flow, extension problems related to non-local equations, and mathematical finance, etc; see e.g.
[
2
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

Our main results are to prove the boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity and the boundary
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity for viscosity solutions. Unlike the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity, the boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity does not require the regularity of the operator in the variables
x
and
t
, so it can be proved for functions in the solutions class
S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
suggested in
[
3
]
. In detail, the solutions class
S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
is the set of functions that satisfy the following inequalities

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)&\geq-\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\\
u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)&\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{in $Q_{1}^{+}$},

where
\gamma<1
,
f\in C(Q_{1}^{+})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})
, and
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}
are the Pucci’s extremal operators. (The operators
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}
will be precisely defined in
Definition

2.6
.)

A standard method to establish the boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for functions in the solutions class of uniformly parabolic equations has been presented; see
[
16
,
22
]
for details. This method utilizes Lipschitz estimate and Hopf principle to sandwich the solution
u
between the two linear functions and applies it repeatedly through scaling to find a linear function that approximates
u
. Moreover, the boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity has been investigated for uniformly elliptic equations by means of this method, as discussed in
[
15
,
20
]
. On the other hand, the interior regularity for functions in the solutions class is at most
C^{\alpha}
in general; we refer to
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. In fact, since the approximation lemma is used in the proof for the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity, the regularity of
F
in the variables
x
and
t
is required, so solutions for the given
F
rather than functions in solutions class is considered; we refer to
[
3
,
22
]
. Nonetheless, there is an interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity result for functions in the solutions class under stringent conditions. In
[
14
]
, they proved that for a given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists
\delta>0
depending only on
n
and
\alpha
such that if
\Lambda\leq(1+\delta)\lambda
, then functions in the solutions class becomes
C^{1,\alpha}
at the interior point.

Our first main theorem is concerned with the boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for viscosity solutions
u
of (
1.4
). The constant
\overline{\alpha}\in(0,1)
used in describing theorems throughout this paper is a universal constant derived from
Lemma

3.4
.

Theorem 1.1

(Boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
with
\alpha\leq 1-\gamma
and
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}
. Suppose that
f\in C(Q_{1}^{+})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})
,
g\in C^{1,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
, and
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
satisfy (
1.4
) with
u=g
on
\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}
. Then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
, i.e., there exists a linear function
L
such that

\displaystyle|u(x,t)-L(x)|\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})})(|x-x_{0}|+|t-t_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{1+\alpha}

for all
(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{+}\cap Q_{1}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})
and

|Du(x_{0},t_{0})|\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}),

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
\alpha
.

The following remark shows that solutions of (
1.4
) cannot be more regular than
C^{1,1-\gamma}
when
0<\gamma<1
.

Remark 1.2
.

Let
0<\gamma<1
. For
\varepsilon\in(0,\gamma]
, consider the equations
u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u=x_{n}^{\gamma-\varepsilon}
which has a solution of the form

u(x,t)=1-\frac{x_{n}^{2-\varepsilon}}{(2-\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon)}.

Since
|Du|=x_{n}^{1-\varepsilon}/|1-\varepsilon|
, the solutions
u
is
C^{1,1-\varepsilon}
on the flat boundary
\{x_{n}=0\}
. This example shows that even for a Hölder continuous
f
and the Laplace operator, we cannot expect in general the solution of (
1.1
) to be more regular than
C^{1,1-\gamma}
on
\{x_{n}=0\}
when
0<\gamma<1
.

Combining
Theorem

1.1
and the known
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity results (see
[
16
, Theorem 1.8]
,
[
22
, Theorem 1.3]
) gives the following global
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity. For this purpose,
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate near the boundary is required, and this result can be found in
[
13
, Lemma 3.10]
.

Corollary 1.3

(Global
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity)
.

Let
\alpha_{1}\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
with
\alpha_{1}\leq 1-\gamma
, and let
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded domain with
(0,0)\in\Omega
and
\partial_{p}\Omega^{+}\in C^{1,\alpha_{1}}
. Assume that
f\in C(\Omega^{+})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega^{+})
,
g\in C^{1,\alpha_{1}}(\partial_{p}\Omega^{+})
,
F
satisfies
(F1)
and
(F5)
, and
u\in C(\overline{\Omega^{+}})
is a viscosity solution of

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)&=f&&\text{in }\Omega^{+}\\
u&=g&&\text{on }\partial_{p}\Omega^{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then there exists a constant
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{1}]
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
, and
\gamma
such that
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega^{+}})
with the uniform estimate

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega^{+}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{+})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\partial_{p}\Omega^{+})}),

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
,
\alpha
,
C_{\theta}
, and
\|\partial_{p}\Omega^{+}\|_{C^{1,\alpha_{1}}}
.

Our second main theorems are concerned with the boundary
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity of viscosity solutions
u
of (
1.1
). Unlike the boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity, the
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity of solutions depends on the regularity of the operator
F
and forcing term
f
. This study investigated how the conditions of
f
relate to the
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity for solutions of (
1.1
) depending on the value of
\gamma
.
In order to obtain the heuristic idea, let’s first consider the degenerate elliptic equation
x_{n}^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x)=f
for
\gamma\in(0,1)
. This equation can be understood as a uniformly elliptic equation
F(D^{2}u,x)=x_{n}^{-\gamma}f
, so if
F(\cdot,x)
is
C^{\alpha}
at
x=0
and
f
is
C^{\alpha+\gamma}
at
x=0
, we have
u
is
C^{2,\alpha}
at
x=0
. In fact, it can be seen that the conditions of
f
obtained heuristically for elliptic equations are expandable for (
1.1
) through this study.

Recall that the optimal regularity for solutions of (
1.5
) was at most
C^{1,1-\gamma}
when
0<\gamma<1
; see
Remark

1.2
. When
f\equiv 0
in (
1.5
), we can expect higher regularity for (
1.5
) under the assumptions of certain structure hypotheses (the hypotheses will be precisely stated in
Section

2.3
). Actually, since
x_{n}^{\gamma}F(O_{n},x,t)
plays the role of
f
, it is the same as considering
f
where
x_{n}^{-\gamma}f
is
C^{\alpha}
at
(0,0)
.

Remark 1.4
.

Consider the equation
u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta u=0
which has a solution of the form

u(x,t)=l(x)+tx_{n}+\frac{x_{n}^{3-\gamma}}{(3-\gamma)(2-\gamma)},

where
l(x)
is a linear function. Since
\|D^{2}u\|=x_{n}^{1-\gamma}
, the solutions
u
is
C^{2,1-\gamma}
on the flat boundary
\{x_{n}=0\}
. This example shows that we cannot expect in general solutions of

(1.6)

u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)=0

to be more regular than
C^{2,1-\gamma}
on
\{x_{n}=0\}
when
0<\gamma<1
. Also,
u_{t}(x^{\prime},0,t)=0
is an essential condition of the existence of solutions
u\in C^{2}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
of (
1.6
). Indeed, if
u\in C^{1,1}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
then
|F(D^{2}u,x,t)|\leq C
on
\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}
. It follows that

|u_{t}(x,t)|=x_{n}^{\gamma}|F(D^{2}u,x,t)|\leq Cx_{n}^{\gamma}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}},

when
0<\gamma<1
.

We now introduce the second main theorem for the degenerate equations.

Theorem 1.5

(Boundary
C^{2,\alpha}
-estimate for
0<\gamma<1
)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
with
\alpha\leq 1-\gamma
,
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}
, and let
F
satisfies
(F1)
,
(F2)
, and
(F3)
. Suppose that
g\in C^{2,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
with
g_{t}(x_{0},t_{0})=0
, and
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
is a viscosity solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)&=0&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u&=g&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
, i.e., there exists a polynomial
P
with
\deg P\leq 2
such that

\displaystyle|u(x,t)-P(x)|\leq CN(|x-x_{0}|+|t-t_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{2+\alpha}

for all
(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{+}\cap Q_{1}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})
,
F(D^{2}P,x_{0},t_{0})=0
,
P(x^{\prime},0)\equiv P_{g}(x^{\prime},0)
, and

u_{t}(x_{0},t_{0})=0,\quad|Du(x_{0},t_{0})|+\|D^{2}u(x_{0},t_{0})\|\leq CN,

where the constant
N
is given by

N\coloneqq(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}+[\beta^{2}]_{C^{\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}+|F(O_{n},x_{0},t_{0})|)

and
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
,
\alpha
, and
[\beta^{1}]_{C^{\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}
.

To prove the
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity of solutions to uniformly parabolic equations, it suffices to have
f\in C^{\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
, which is a less stringent condition than the condition in
Theorem

1.5
for degenerate equations. From the following theorem, we know that the
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity of solutions to singular equations can be achieved with lower regularity than
f\in C^{\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
, even if it is discontinuous. The following result is our third main theorem for the singular equations.

Theorem 1.6

(Boundary
C^{2,\alpha}
-estimate for
\gamma\leq 0
)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
,
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}
, and let
F
satisfies
(F1)
,
(F2)
, and
(F3)
. Suppose that

(1.7)

|f(x,t)-f(x_{0},t_{0})|\leq K(|x-x_{0}|+|t-t_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{\alpha+\gamma}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}},

g\in C^{2,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
, and
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
is a viscosity solution of

(1.8)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)&=f&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u&=g&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then
u\in C^{2,\alpha^{*}}(x_{0},t_{0})
, i.e., there exists a polynomial
P
with
\deg P\leq 2
such that

\displaystyle|u(x,t)-P(x,t)|\leq CN(|x-x_{0}|+|t-t_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{2+\alpha^{*}}

for all
(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{+}\cap Q_{1}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})
,
F(D^{2}P,x_{0},t_{0})=0
,
\partial_{t}P=\partial_{t}P_{g}
,
P(x^{\prime},0,t)\equiv P_{g}(x^{\prime},0,t)
, and

|u_{t}(x_{0},t_{0})|+|Du(x_{0},t_{0})|+\|D^{2}u(x_{0},t_{0})\|\leq CN,

where the constant
N
is given by

N\coloneqq(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}+[\beta^{2}]_{C^{\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}+|F(O_{n},x_{0},t_{0})|+K),

a^{*}\coloneqq\min\{\alpha,-\gamma\}
, and
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
,
\alpha
, and
[\beta^{1}]_{C^{\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}
.

Finally, such boundary
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity can be applied to describe the solvability of the Cauchy-Dirichlet problems (
1.5
) and the short time existence of solutions for (
1.3
); see
[
5
, Theorem]
and
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
for the short time existence.

Theorem 1.7

(The solvability of the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem for
0<\gamma<1
)
.

Let
\alpha_{1}\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
with
\alpha_{1}\leq 1-\gamma
and let
\Omega_{T}^{+}
be a bounded domain with
(0,0)\in\Omega
and
\partial\Omega^{+}\in C^{2,\alpha_{1}}
. Suppose that
F
satisfies
(F1)
,
(F2)
, and
(F3)
. If
g\in C^{2,\alpha_{1}}(\overline{\Omega_{T}^{+}})
satisfy the compatibility conditions

\left\{\begin{aligned} g_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}F(D^{2}g,x,t)&=0&&\text{on }\partial_{c}\Omega_{T}^{+}\\

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)&=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{T}^{+}\\

Similar to the one described above, the solvability of the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem for singular equations can be derived. It is also worth emphasizing that while (
1.7
) provides a sufficient condition for boundary
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity, it does not guarantee interior
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity.

Theorem 1.8

(The solvability of the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem for
\gamma\leq 0
)
.

Let
\alpha_{1}\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
and let
\Omega_{T}^{+}
be a bounded domain with
(0,0)\in\Omega
and
\partial\Omega^{+}\in C^{2,\alpha_{1}}
. Suppose that
F
satisfies
(F1)
,
(F2)
, and
(F4)
. If
f\in H^{\alpha_{1}}(\overline{\Omega_{T}^{+}})
and

satisfy the compatibility conditions

then the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)&=f&&\text{in }\Omega_{T}^{+}\\

is uniquely solvable in
C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega_{T}^{+}})
for some
\alpha\in(0,\alpha^{*}]
.

When we investigate the boundary regularity for solutions of (
1.1
), the essential challenge arises from the fact that the fully nonlinear operator
F
strongly depends on the term
x_{n}^{\gamma}
. It causes the ellipticity constants to be zero or infinity near the boundary and makes it difficult to construct a barrier function using the fundamental solution of the heat equation. In the case of
F(D^{2}u,x,t)=\Delta u
, estimates of
Du
,
u_{ii}\,(1\leq i<n)
, and
u_{t}
can be obtained using Bernstein’s technique; we refer to
[
8
]
. They obtain an estimate of
x_{n}^{\gamma}u_{nn}
using these estimates and the equation that
u
satisfies. For smooth and concave operator
F
, which is a natural extension of
\Delta
, the estimate of
u_{in}\,(1\leq i<n)
is required. However, since the
u_{n}
is not a function in the same solutions class, it is difficult to obtain the estimate of
u_{in}\,(1\leq i<n)
using Bernstein’s technique. In contrast, our approach can obtain estimates of these derivatives even under the general
F
. Also, our study accurately captures the variations of assumptions regarding the existence of a classical solution based on the value range of
\gamma
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we summarize some basic notations and gather definitions and known regularity results used throughout the paper.

Since the standard scaling used in the uniformly parabolic equations cannot be applied to (
1.1
), it is necessary to define a cylinder and Hölder space accordingly.

For a point
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
r>0
, we denote the intrinsic cylinder and upper cylinder as

\displaystyle Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-x_{0}|<r\}\times(t_{0}-r^{2-\gamma},t_{0}],

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})\cap\{x_{n}>0\}.

For convenience, we denote
Q_{r}=Q_{r}(0,0)
and
Q_{r}^{+}=Q_{r}^{+}(0,0)
.

Now let’s define a more general domain. For a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we denote the upper domain and parabolic boundary as

\displaystyle\Omega^{+}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(x,t)\in\Omega:x_{n}>0\},

\displaystyle\partial_{p}\Omega

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(x,t)\in\partial\Omega:Q_{r}(x,t)\cap\Omega^{c}\text{ is nonempty for all }r>0\}.

In the special case
\Omega_{T}^{+}\coloneqq\Omega^{+}\times(0,T]
for a bounded domain
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the bottom, corner, and side as

\displaystyle\partial_{b}\Omega^{+}_{T}\coloneqq\Omega^{+}\times\{t=0\},\quad\partial_{c}\Omega^{+}_{T}\coloneqq\partial\Omega^{+}\times\{t=0\},\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{s}\Omega^{+}_{T}\coloneqq\partial\Omega^{+}\times(0,T).

2.1.
Hölder Spaces

Defines the Hölder continuity of functions and domains used in the description of the theorem. Note that unlike commonly used Hölder continuity, it is Hölder continuity suitable for scaling in (
1.1
).

Definition 2.1

(Hölder spaces)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an open set and
\alpha\in(0,1)
with
\alpha\leq 1-\gamma
.

(i)

u\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
means that there exists
C>0
such that

|u(x,t)-u(y,s)|\leq C(|x-y|+|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }(x,t),(y,s)\in\Omega.

In other words,
u
is
\frac{\alpha}{2-\gamma}
-Hölder continuous in
t
and
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in
x
.

(ii)

u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
means that
u
is
\frac{1+\alpha}{2-\gamma}
-Hölder continuous in
t
and
Du
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in
x
.

(iii)

u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
means that
u_{t}
is
\frac{\alpha+\gamma}{2-\gamma}
-Hölder continuous in
t
and
D^{2}u
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in
x
.

(iv)

We denote
H^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\coloneqq C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
and
H^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\coloneqq C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
when
\gamma=0
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded set and
f:A\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. We say that
f
is
C^{k,\alpha}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})\in A
(denoted by
f\in C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
), if there exist constant
C>0
,
r>0
, and a polynomial
P_{f}(x,t)
with
\deg P_{f}\leq k
such that

(2.1)

|f(x,t)-P_{f}(x,t)|\leq C(|x-x_{0}|+|t-t_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{k+\alpha}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in A\cap Q_{r}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0}).

We define

\displaystyle[f]_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\{C>0\mid\eqref{cka_f}\text{ holds with }P_{f}(x,t)\text{ and }C\},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[f]_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|D^{i}P_{f}(x_{0},t_{0})\|.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded domain. We say that
\partial_{p}\Omega
is
C^{k,\alpha}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\partial_{p}\Omega
(denoted by
\partial_{p}\Omega\in C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
), if there exist constant
C>0
,
r>0
, a new coordinate system
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},t\}
, and a polynomial
P(x^{\prime},t)
with
\deg P\leq k
,
P(0^{\prime},0)=0
, and
DP(0^{\prime},0)=0
such that
(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
in this coordinate system,

(2.2)

\displaystyle Q_{\rho}\cap\{(x^{\prime},x_{n},t)\mid x_{n}>P(x^{\prime},t)+C(|x^{\prime}|+|t|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{k+\alpha}\}\subset Q_{r}\cap\Omega,

\displaystyle Q_{\rho}\cap\{(x^{\prime},x_{n},t)\mid x_{n}<P(x^{\prime},t)-C(|x^{\prime}|+|t|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{k+\alpha}\}\subset Q_{r}\cap\Omega^{c}.

We define

\displaystyle[\partial_{p}\Omega]_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\{C>0\mid\eqref{cka_dom}\text{ holds with }P(x^{\prime},t)\text{ and }C\},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{p}\Omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[\partial_{p}\Omega]_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}+\sum_{i=2}^{k}\|D^{i}P(0^{\prime},0)\|.

Moreover, if
\partial_{p}\Omega\in C^{k,\alpha}(x,t)
for all
(x,t)\in\partial_{p}\Omega
, we define

\|\partial_{p}\Omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}\coloneqq\sup_{(x,t)\in\partial_{p}\Omega}\|\partial_{p}\Omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(x,t)}.

2.2.
Viscosity Solutions

We now introduce the concept of solutions of fully nonlinear parabolic equations:

(2.3)

u_{t}-F(D^{2}u,Du,x,t)=0,

where we assume that the fully nonlinear operator
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and satisfies the following condition:

M\leq N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F(M,p,x,t)\leq F(N,p,x,t).

Definition 2.4

(Test functions)
.

Let
u
be a continuous function in
\Omega
. The function
\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is called
test function
if it is
C^{1}
with respect to
t
and
C^{2}
with respect to
x
.

(i)

We say that the test function
\varphi
touches
u
from above at
(x,t)
if there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
(x,t)
such that

u\leq\varphi\quad\mbox{in }U\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad u(x,t)=\varphi(x,t).

(ii)

We say that the test function
\varphi
touches
u
from below at
(x,t)
if there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
(x,t)
such that

u\geq\varphi\quad\mbox{in }U\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad u(x,t)=\varphi(x,t).

Definition 2.5

(Viscosity solutions)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a fully nonlinear parabolic operator.

(i)

Let
u
be a upper semicontinuous function in
\Omega
.
u
is called a
viscosity subsoution
of (
2.3
) when the following condition holds: if for any
(x,t)\in\Omega
and any test function
\varphi
touching
u
from above at
(x,t)
, then

\varphi_{t}(x,t)-F(D^{2}\varphi(x,t),D\varphi(x,t),x,t)\leq f(x,t).

(ii)

Let
u
be a lower semicontinuous function in
\Omega
.
u
is called a
viscosity supersoution
of (
2.3
) when the following condition holds: if for any
(x,t)\in\Omega
and any test function
\varphi
touching
u
from below at
(x,t)
, then

\varphi_{t}(x,t)-F(D^{2}\varphi(x,t),D\varphi(x,t),x,t)\geq f(x,t).

We introduce the Pucci’s extremal operators to define the solutions class.

Definition 2.6

(Pucci’s extremal operators)
.

Given elliptictity constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
and
\mathcal{S}^{n}\coloneqq\{M:\text{$M$ is an $n\times n$ real symmetric matrix\lx@text@rbrace}
, we define
Pucci’s extremal operators
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(M)\coloneqq\sup_{\lambda I\leq A\leq\Lambda I}\text{tr}(AM)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(M)\coloneqq\inf_{\lambda I\leq A\leq\Lambda I}\text{tr}(AM).

Definition 2.7

(Solutions classes)
.

Let
f
be a continuous bounded function in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
. We define several solutions classes as follows:

\displaystyle\underline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{u\in C(\Omega)\mid u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)\leq f\ \text{in the viscosity sense}\},

\displaystyle\overline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{u\in C(\Omega)\mid u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)\geq f\ \text{in the viscosity sense}\},

\displaystyle S(\lambda,\Lambda,f)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\underline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)\cap\overline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,f),

\displaystyle S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\underline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})\cap\overline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,-\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}).

We call the functions in
S(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
solutions. Clearly, we have
S(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
is contained in
S^{\ast}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
and
S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,0)=S(\lambda,\Lambda,0)
.

Remark 2.8
.

If
u\in C(\Omega)
is a viscosity solution of (
1.1
) with ellipticity constants
\lambda
and
\Lambda
, then we know that
u\in S(\lambda,\Lambda,f(x,t)+x_{n}^{\gamma}F(O_{n},x,t))
in
\Omega
.

2.3.
Structure Hypotheses on the operator
F

We assume that the fully nonlinear operator
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\times\overline{\Omega^{+}}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies some of the following conditions:

(F1)

F
is uniformly parabolic; that is, there exist constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
such that

\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(M-N)\leq F(M,x,t)-F(N,x,t)\leq\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(M-N)

for all
M,N\in\mathcal{S}^{n},\,(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega^{+}}
.

(F2)

F
is concave or convex.

(F3)

Let
\alpha\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
and
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\overline{\Omega^{+}}
. There exist a constant
r_{0}>0
and two nonnegative functions
\beta^{1},\beta^{2}\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega^{+}})
such that
\beta^{1}(x_{0},t_{0})=0
,
\beta^{2}(x_{0},t_{0})=0
, and

|F(M,x,t)-F(M,x_{0},t_{0})|\leq\beta^{1}(x,t)\|M\|+\beta^{2}(x,t)

for all
M\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
,
(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega^{+}}\cap\overline{Q_{r_{0}}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})}
.

(F4)

Let
\alpha\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
and
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\overline{\Omega^{+}}
. There exist a constant
r_{0}>0
and two nonnegative functions
\beta^{1},\beta^{2}\in H^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega^{+}})
such that
\beta^{1}(x_{0},t_{0})=0
,
\beta^{2}(x_{0},t_{0})=0
, and

|F(M,x,t)-F(M,x_{0},t_{0})|\leq\beta^{1}(x,t)\|M\|+\beta^{2}(x,t)

for all
M\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
,
(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega^{+}}\cap\overline{Q_{r_{0}}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})}
.

(F5)

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega
. There exist constants
r_{0}>0
,
C_{\theta}>0
and two nonnegative functions
\theta
satisfying
\theta(x_{0},t_{0})=0
and

\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{r}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})|}\int_{Q_{r}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})}|\theta(x,t)|^{n+1}\,dx\,dt\right)^{1/(n+1)}\leq C_{\theta}r^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }r\leq r_{0}

such that

|F(M,x,t)-F(M,x_{0},t_{0})|\leq\theta(x,t)(\|M\|+1)

for all
M\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
,
(x,t)\in\Omega^{+}\cap Q_{r_{0}}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})
.

Remark 2.9
.

Below are some comments regarding the structural hypotheses on
F
.

(i)

(F1)
,
(F2)
, and
(F3)
are conditions for boundary
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity.

(ii)

(F2)
can be reduced to the condition on
F
such that (
1.1
) has a solution
u\in C_{\textnormal{loc}}^{2}(\Omega^{+})
.

(iii)

Regarding pointwise regularity, it suffices to have
\beta^{1},\beta^{2}\in C^{\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
in
(F3)
.

(iv)

(F4)
is a condition for global
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity when
\gamma<0
.

(v)

(F5)
is a condition for global
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity.

(vi)

(F5)
is satisfied if
F(\cdot,x)\in C^{\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
.

2.4.
Known Regularity Results

Here we gather some known regularity results that we will need later on.

2.4.1.
Interior Harnack Inequality

For a given
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, the function
u\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
in
\Omega\subset\joinrel\subset\{(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{+}:x_{n}\geq\varepsilon\}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\varepsilon^{\gamma}\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)&\geq-\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\\
u_{t}-\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\varepsilon^{\gamma}\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)&\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{in }\Omega

when
0<\gamma<1
and

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\varepsilon^{\gamma}\Lambda}(D^{2}u)&\geq-\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\\
u_{t}-\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\varepsilon^{\gamma}\Lambda}(D^{2}u)&\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{in }\Omega

when
\gamma\leq 0
. In other words, in a domain sufficiently far away from the boundary
\{(x,t)\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}:x_{n}=0\}
,
u
belongs to the solutions class for certain uniformly parabolic equations, and hence we obtain the following Interior Harnack Inequality.

Theorem 2.10

(Interior Harnack inequality,
[
21
, Theorem 4.18]
)
.

Let
u\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
be a nonnegative function in
Q_{1}^{+}
. Suppose that
\Omega\coloneqq B_{r}(a)\times(-3r^{2-\gamma},0]
is compactly contained in
Q_{1}^{+}
and
f\in C(Q_{1}^{+})\cap L^{n+1}(Q_{1}^{+})
. Then

\sup_{B_{r}(a)\times(-3r^{2-\gamma},-2r^{2-\gamma}]}u\leq C\left(\inf_{B_{r}(a)\times(-r^{2-\gamma},0]}u+\|f\|_{L^{n+1}(Q_{1}^{+})}\right),

where
C
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
d\coloneqq\textnormal{dist}\,(\Omega,\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+})
.

2.4.2.
Comparison Principle

We are interested in the comparison principle of (
1.1
).
[
4
, Theorem 8.2]
provides a comparison principle for many kinds of fully nonlinear operators, but (
1.1
) does not satisfy
[
4
, Condition (3.14)]
. To demonstrate the comparison principle of (
1.1
), we introduce semijets and theorems suggested in
[
4
, Section 8]
.

Definition 2.11

(Parabolic semijets)
.

Let
u
be a function defined in
Q_{1}^{+}
and let
(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{+}
.

(i)

A
parabolic superjet

\mathscr{P}^{2,+}u(x,t)
consists of
(a,p,M)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}^{n}
which satisfy

\displaystyle u(y,s)

\displaystyle\leq u(x,t)+a(s-t)+p\cdot(y-x)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}(y-x)^{T}M(y-x)+o(|s-t|+|z-x|^{2})\quad\text{as $(y,s)\to(x,t)$}.

Similarly, we can define a
parabolic subjet

\mathscr{P}^{2,-}u(x,t)
. It immediately follows that

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}^{2,-}u(x,t)=-\mathscr{P}^{2,+}(-u)(x,t).

(ii)

A
limiting superjet

\overline{\mathscr{P}}^{2,+}u(x,t)
consists of
(a,p,M)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}^{n}
with the following statement holds: there exists a sequence
\{(x_{n},t_{n},a_{n},p_{n},M_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
(a_{n},p_{n},M_{n})\in\mathscr{P}^{2,+}u(x_{n},t_{n})
and

\text{$(x_{n},t_{n},u(x_{n},t_{n}),a_{n},p_{n},M_{n})\to(x,t,u(x,t),a,p,M)$}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

We define a
parabolic subjet

\overline{\mathscr{P}}^{2,-}u(x,t)
in a similar way.

Theorem 2.12

(Jensen Ishii’s Lemma,
[
4
, Theorem 8.3]
,
[
1
, Lemma 2.3.23]
)
.

Let
U
and
V
be two open sets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
I
an open interval of
\mathbb{R}
. Consider also a viscosity subsolution
u
of (
2.3
) in
U\times I
and a viscosity supersolution
v
of (
2.3
) in
V\times I
. Suppose that

(2.4)

w(x,y,t)\coloneqq u(x,t)-v(y,t)-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}|x-y|^{2}

has a local maximum at
(x_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})\in U\times V\times I
. Then there exists
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
M,N\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
such that
(a,p,M)\in\overline{\mathscr{P}}^{2,+}u(x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})
,
(a,p,N)\in\overline{\mathscr{P}}^{2,-}v(y_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})
, and

-\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}&O_{n}\\
O_{n}&I_{n}\end{pmatrix}\leq\begin{pmatrix}M&O_{n}\\
O_{n}&-N\end{pmatrix}\leq\frac{3}{\varepsilon}\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}&-I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&I_{n}\end{pmatrix},

where
p\coloneqq\varepsilon^{-1}(x_{\varepsilon}-y_{\varepsilon})
,
I_{n}
and
O_{n}
are the indentity matrix and zero matrix, respectively.

Lemma 2.13

(
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
,
[
1
, Lemma 2.3.19]
)
.

Let
u
be a upper semicontinuous in
\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}
and
v
be a lower semicontinuous in
\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}
. Assume that

\mu_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\sup_{(x,t),(y,s)\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}}\left(u(x,t)-v(y,s)-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}|x-y|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}|t-s|^{2}\right)<\infty

for small
\varepsilon>0
and
\{(x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon},s_{\varepsilon})\}
satisfies that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\mu_{\varepsilon}-u(x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})+v(y_{\varepsilon},s_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}|x_{\varepsilon}-y_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}|t_{\varepsilon}-s_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)=0.

Then the following statements hold:

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\varepsilon}^{-1}|x_{\varepsilon}-y_{\varepsilon}|^{2}=0
and
\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\varepsilon}^{-1}|t_{\varepsilon}-s_{\varepsilon}|^{2}=0
,

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mu_{\varepsilon}=u(\overline{x},\overline{t})-v(\overline{x},\overline{t})=\sup_{Q_{1}^{+}}(u-v)
whenever
(\overline{x},\overline{t})\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}
is a limit point of
\{(x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})\}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

We are now ready to prove the comparison principle for (

).

Theorem 2.14

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
and
v\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
be a viscosity subsolution and viscosity supersolution of (
1.1
) in
Q_{1}^{+}
, respectively. If
u\leq v
on
\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}
, then
u\leq v
in
Q_{1}^{+}
.

Proof.

We first observe that for
\delta>0
,
\tilde{u}=u+\delta/t
is also viscosity subsolution of (
1.1
). Since
u\leq v
in
Q_{1}^{+}
follows from
\tilde{u}\leq v
in
Q_{1}^{+}
and letting
\delta\to 0
, we may assume that

u_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)\leq f-\delta\quad\text{in the viscosity sense}

and
u\to-\infty
uniformly on
\overline{\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}:|x_{i}|\leq 1\,(1\leq i\leq n)\}}
as
t\to 0
.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that
m\coloneqq u(x_{0},t_{0})-v(x_{0},t_{0})=\sup_{Q_{1}^{+}}(u-v)>0
for some
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{1}^{+}
. Since
u
is bounded above,
w
defined as in (
2.4
) has the maximum
\mu_{\varepsilon}
at
(x_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})
. Furthermore,
\{(x_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon})\}
is a bounded sequence, there exists a subsequence
\{(x_{\varepsilon_{j}},y_{\varepsilon_{j}})\}
such that
x_{\varepsilon_{j}}\to\overline{x}
as
j\to\infty
and
y_{\varepsilon_{j}}\to\overline{y}
as
j\to\infty
. If
t_{\varepsilon}=-1
, by
Lemma

2.13
, we have
\overline{x}=\overline{y}
and

\displaystyle 0<m\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}\mu_{\varepsilon_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon_{j}\to 0}\sup_{(x,-1),(y,-1)\in Q_{1}^{+}}\left(u(x,-1)-v(y,-1)-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{j}}|x-y|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=u(\overline{x},-1)-y(\overline{x},-1)\leq\sup_{\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}}(u-v)\leq 0

which is not possible. Similarly, we can see that
(x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon}),(y_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})\in Q_{1}^{+}
if
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small. Thus we can apply
Theorem

2.12
to the point
(x_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})
, there exists
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
M,N\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
such that
(a,p,M)\in\overline{\mathscr{P}}^{2,+}u(x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})
,
(a,p,N)\in\overline{\mathscr{P}}^{2,-}v(y_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})
, and

M\leq N
, where
p\coloneqq\varepsilon^{-1}(x_{\varepsilon}-y_{\varepsilon})
. These imply that

a-x_{\varepsilon n}^{\gamma}F(M,x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})\leq f(x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})-\delta\quad\text{and}\quad a-y_{\varepsilon n}^{\gamma}F(N,x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})\geq f(y_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon}),

where
x_{\varepsilon n}
is the
n
-th coordinate of
x_{\varepsilon}
. Since
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-1}|x_{\varepsilon}-y_{\varepsilon}|^{2}=0
and
M\leq N
, we have

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle\leq f(x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})-f(y_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})+x_{\varepsilon n}^{\gamma}F(M,x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})-y_{\varepsilon n}^{\gamma}F(N,x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq f(x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})-f(y_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})+(x_{\varepsilon n}^{\gamma}-y_{\varepsilon n}^{\gamma})F(N,x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})\to 0\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0

which leads to a contradiction.
∎

Note that

,

is a viscosity subsolution of (

) in
Q_{1}^{+}
, and
u_{*}
is a viscosity supersolution of (
2.5
) in
Q_{1}^{+}
. Since
u^{*}
is viscosity subsolution of (
2.5
) in
Q_{1}^{+}
and
u_{*}
is viscosity supersolution of (
2.5
) in
Q_{1}^{+}
, by the comparison principle [
Theorem

2.14
], we have
u^{*}\leq u_{*}
in
Q_{1}^{+}
. Furthermore,
u_{*}\leq u\leq u^{*}
by definition of the semicontinuous envelopes, we have
u=u_{*}=u^{*}
and hene
u
is a viscosity solution of (
2.5
). The uniqueness of the solution is obtained directly by the comparison principle.
∎

3.
Boundary Hölder Gradient Estimates

In this section, we prove the boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for functions in
S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
. We first prove Lipschitz estimate and Hopf principle. These are the key lemmas for proving the boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for functions in
S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
.

3.1.
Lipschitz Estimate and Hopf Principle

Lipschitz estimation and Hopf principle are mainly concerned with the construction of barrier functions. However, because of term
x_{n}^{\gamma}
, the construction of the barrier function using the fundamental solution of the heat equation is not suitable. That is, it is necessary to construct a barrier function considering the influence of
x_{n}^{\gamma}
and consider a suitable domain for it.

To simplify the notation in the proof, we denote
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Lemma 3.1

(Lipschitz estimate)
.

Let
f\in C(Q_{1}^{+})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})
. Assume that
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} u&\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u&=0&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then

|u(x,t)|\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})})|x|\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
, and
\gamma
.

Proof.

For any
\delta>0
, replacing
u
by

\frac{u}{\delta+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}}

and letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
, we may assume that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1.

Let
\eta(x)=1-|x+e_{n}|^{-\beta}
for the constant
\beta>\max\left\{2,(n-1)\Lambda/\lambda-1\right\}
and consider following functions

\tilde{u}=(t+1)u\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad v=2M\eta-Mx_{n}^{2-\gamma}.

Then, it can be seen that
\tilde{u}\leq v
on
\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}
for sufficiently large
M>0
.

We claim that
\tilde{u}
and
v
are a viscosity subsolution and supersolution respectively of the following equation:

w_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}w)=2\quad\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}.

For any
(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{+}
, let
\tilde{\varphi}
be a test function that touching
\tilde{u}
from above at
(x,t)
. Then the test function
\varphi\coloneqq\tilde{\varphi}/(t+1)
touches
u
from above at
(x,t)
. Since
u\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
in
Q_{1}^{+}
, we know that

\varphi_{t}(x,t)-x_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}\varphi(x,t))\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}

and hence we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(x,t)-x_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}\left(D^{2}\tilde{\varphi}(x,t)\right)\leq(t+1)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+u(x,t)\leq 2.

On the other hand, we can see that

\displaystyle D^{2}\eta(x)

\displaystyle\sim\beta|x+e_{n}|^{-\beta-2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}-(1+\beta)&\textbf{0}\\
\hline\cr\textbf{0}&I_{n-1}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle D^{2}x_{n}^{2-\gamma}

\displaystyle=(2-\gamma)(1-\gamma)x_{n}^{-\gamma}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}O_{n-1}&\textbf{0}\\
\hline\cr\textbf{0}&1\end{array}\right)

by rotational symmetry, where
I_{n-1}
and
O_{n-1}
are the indentity matrix and zero matrix. Then, we have

\displaystyle v_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}v)

\displaystyle=-Mx_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(2D^{2}\eta-D^{2}x_{n}^{2-\gamma})

\displaystyle\geq-2Mx_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}\eta)+Mx_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D^{2}x_{n}^{2-\gamma})

\displaystyle=2\beta Mx_{n}^{\gamma}|x+e_{n}|^{-\beta-2}\big((1+\beta)\lambda-(n-1)\Lambda\big)+\lambda M(2-\gamma)(1-\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq 2

for sufficiently large
M>0
. By the comparison principle [
Theorem

2.14
], we have

(t+1)u\leq 2M\eta-Mx_{n}^{2-\gamma}\quad\mbox{in }Q_{1}^{+}.

Therefore for
0<r<1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t+1}\big(2M\eta-Mx_{n}^{2-\gamma}\big)\leq\frac{2M}{1-r^{2-\gamma}}\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{\beta}}\right)\leq\frac{2\beta M}{1-r^{2-\gamma}}|x|

for all
(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{r}^{+}}
. Similarly, we can obtain the lower bound by considering the following functions

\hat{u}=-(t+1)u\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad v=2M\eta-Mx_{n}^{2-\gamma}.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

Assume that
u\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
in
Q_{1}^{+}
and let
v(x,t)=u(x-y,t)
be a translation in direction
y\perp e_{n}
. Then
v\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,\tilde{f})
in
Q_{r}^{+}
for some
r\in(0,1)
, where
\tilde{f}(x,t)\coloneqq f(x-y,t)
. We can apply
Lemma

3.1
to the function
v
and hence we have

|u(x,t)|\leq C\big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\big)x_{n}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{r/2}^{+}}.

Lemma 3.3

(Hopf principle)
.

Let
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
be a nonnegative function satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} u&\in S(\lambda,\Lambda,0)&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u&=0&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then

(3.1)

u(x,t)\geq Cu(e_{n}/2,-2/4^{2-\gamma})x_{n}\quad\mbox{for all }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}},

where
C
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
, and
\gamma
.

Proof.

By the interior Harnack inequality [
Theorem

2.10
], we have

u(e_{n}/2,-2/4^{2-\gamma})\leq\sup_{B_{1/4}(e_{n}/2)\times(-3/4^{2-\gamma},-2/4^{2-\gamma}]}u\leq C_{0}^{-1}\inf_{B_{1/4}(e_{n}/2)\times(-1/4^{2-\gamma},0]}u,

where
C_{0}>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
, and
\gamma
. Then it immediately follows that

u\geq C_{0}u(e_{n}/2,-2/4^{2-\gamma})\quad\mbox{on }\overline{B_{1/4}(e_{n}/2)\times(-1/4^{2-\gamma},0]}.

First, let us consider the case of singular equations, i.e.,
\gamma<0
. In this case, consider functions
v,\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
given by

v(x,t)\coloneqq C_{0}u(e_{n}/2,-2/4^{2-\gamma})\frac{\varphi(x,t)-e^{-\beta/4}}{e^{-\beta/16}-e^{-\beta/4}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi(x,t)\coloneqq e^{-\beta(|x-e_{n}/2|^{2}-4^{1-\gamma}t)},

where
\Omega=\{(x,t):1/4<|x-e_{n}/2|<\sqrt{4^{1-\gamma}t+1/4},\,t<0\}
. Since the parabolic boundary is

\displaystyle\partial_{p}\Omega

\displaystyle=S_{1}\cup S_{2},

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}:|x-e_{n}/2|=\sqrt{4^{1-\gamma}t+1/4},\,t<0\},

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}:|x-e_{n}/2|=1/4,\,t<0\},

we have
u\geq v
on
\partial_{p}\Omega
.

For any matrix
A=(a^{ij})\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
with
\lambda I\leq A\leq\Lambda I
and sufficiently large
\beta>0
, we have

\displaystyle(x_{n}^{-\gamma}\varphi_{t}-a^{ij}\varphi_{ij})/\varphi

\displaystyle\leq 4^{1-\gamma}\beta-\frac{1}{4}\lambda\beta^{2}+2n\Lambda\beta<0\quad\text{in }\Omega

and hence

\varphi_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D^{2}\varphi)=\sup_{\lambda I\leq A\leq\Lambda I}(\varphi_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}a^{ij}\varphi_{ij})\leq 0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega.

This implies that

v_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D^{2}v)\leq 0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega.

By the comparison principle [
Theorem

2.14
], we have

u(0^{\prime},x_{n},0)\geq v(0^{\prime},x_{n},0)\geq Cu(e_{n}/2,-2/4^{2-\gamma})x_{n}\quad\text{for }0<x_{n}<1/4.

For the same reason as
Remark

3.2
, we know that (
3.1
) holds by considering the translation in the
x^{\prime}
and
t
directions.

Next, let us consider the case of degenerate equations, i.e.,
0<\gamma<1
. Consider functions
w:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle w(x,t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq C_{0}u(e_{n}/2,-2/4^{2-\gamma})e^{\beta(\delta-2\cdot 4^{2-\gamma})/4^{3-\gamma}}\frac{e^{-\beta(|x-e_{n}/2|^{2}-\delta tx_{n}-1/4)}-e^{-\beta(4^{2-\gamma}-\delta)tx_{n}}}{e^{-\beta/16}-e^{-\beta/4}},

where
\Sigma=\{(x,t):1/4<|x-e_{n}/2|<\sqrt{4^{2-\gamma}tx_{n}+1/4},\,t<0\}
. Since the parabolic boundary is

\displaystyle\partial_{p}\Sigma

\displaystyle=T_{1}\cup T_{2},

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{X\in\overline{\Sigma}:|x-e_{n}/2|=\sqrt{4^{2-\gamma}tx_{n}+1/4},\,t<0\},

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{X\in\overline{\Sigma}:|x-e_{n}/2|=1/4,\,t<0\},

we have
u\geq w
on
\partial_{p}\Sigma
.
Note that for any
(x,t)\in\Sigma
, the following inequalities hold

\frac{1}{4}<|x-e_{n}/2|<\frac{1}{2},\quad-\frac{1}{4^{2-\gamma}}<t\leq 0\quad\text{and}\quad-tx_{n}<\frac{1}{4^{3-\gamma}}.

Let
\xi(x,t)\coloneqq e^{-\beta(|x-e_{n}/2|^{2}-\delta tx_{n}-1/4)}
. Then for
\delta<\frac{\lambda}{20\Lambda}
and
\beta>\frac{30(\delta+2n\Lambda)}{\lambda}
, we have

\displaystyle(x_{n}^{-\gamma}\xi_{t}-a^{ij}\xi_{ij})/\xi

\displaystyle\leq\beta\delta-4\beta^{2}\lambda|x-e_{n}/2|^{2}+2n\beta\Lambda+4\beta^{2}\delta\Lambda|x-e_{n}/2||t|-\beta^{2}\delta^{2}\lambda t^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\beta\delta-\frac{1}{4}\beta^{2}\lambda+2n\beta\Lambda+2\beta^{2}\delta\Lambda

\displaystyle\leq\beta\delta-\frac{1}{5}\beta^{2}\lambda+2n\beta\Lambda\leq-\frac{1}{6}\beta^{2}\lambda\quad\text{in }\Sigma.

It follows that

(3.2)

x_{n}^{-\gamma}\xi_{t}-a^{ij}\xi_{ij}\leq-\frac{1}{6}\beta^{2}\lambda e^{-\beta(|x-e_{n}/2|^{2}-\delta tx_{n}-1/4)}\quad\text{in }\Sigma.

Let
\psi(x,t)\coloneqq-e^{-\beta(4^{2-\gamma}-\delta)tx_{n}}
. Then for
\beta>4^{2-\gamma}/\Lambda
, we have

\displaystyle x_{n}^{-\gamma}\psi_{t}-a^{ij}\psi_{ij}

\displaystyle=\beta(4^{2-\gamma}-\delta)x_{n}^{1-\gamma}e^{-\beta(4^{2-\gamma}-\delta)tx_{n}}+a^{nn}\beta^{2}(4^{2-\gamma}-\delta)^{2}t^{2}e^{-\beta(4^{2-\gamma}-\delta)tx_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq(4^{2-\gamma}\beta+\beta^{2}\Lambda)e^{-\beta(4^{2-\gamma}-\delta)tx_{n}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq 2\beta^{2}\Lambda e^{-\beta(16-\delta)tx_{n}}\quad\text{in }\Sigma.

Combining (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) gives

\displaystyle x_{n}^{-\gamma}w_{t}-a^{ij}w_{ij}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C_{0}u(e_{n}/2,-2/4^{2-\gamma})e^{\beta(\delta-2\cdot 4^{2-\gamma})/4^{3-\gamma}}}{e^{-\beta/16}-e^{-\beta/4}}\beta^{2}\Big(2\Lambda e^{-\beta(4^{2-\gamma}-\delta)tx_{n}}-\frac{1}{6}\lambda e^{-\beta(|x-e_{n}/2|^{2}-\delta tx_{n}-1/4)}\Big)

in
\Sigma
. Since
|x-e_{n}/2|<\sqrt{4^{2-\gamma}tx_{n}+1/4}
, we can see that

-\beta(4^{2-\gamma}-\delta)tx_{n}<-\beta(|x-e_{n}/2|^{2}-\delta tx_{n}-1/4)

Then for sufficiently large
\beta>0
, we have
w_{t}-x_{n}^{\gamma}a^{ij}w_{ij}\leq 0
in
\Sigma
. Thus, we obtain the desired conclusion as in the case of singular equations.
∎

3.2.
Boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate for functions in
S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
.

We will first apply the iteration procedure to the simplified problems and use the compactness argument in the general case.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} u&\in S(\lambda,\Lambda,0)&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u&=0&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then there exists
\overline{\alpha}\in(0,1)
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
, and
\gamma
such that
u\in C^{1,\overline{\alpha}}(0,0)
, i.e., there exists a constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that

|u(x,t)-ax_{n}|\leq C_{1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}(|x|+|t|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{\overline{\alpha}}x_{n}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}}

and
|a|\leq C_{1}
, where
C_{1}>1
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
\overline{\alpha}
.

Proof.

For any
\delta>0
, replacing
u
by
u/(\delta+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})})
and letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
, we may assume that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1
. We will construct an increasing sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
and a decreasing sequence
\{b_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
such that for any
k\geq 1
, we have

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &a_{k}x_{n}\leq u(x,t)\leq b_{k}x_{n}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{2^{-k}}^{+}}\\
&0\leq b_{k}-a_{k}\leq\mu 2^{-k\overline{\alpha}},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mu>0
and
\overline{\alpha}\in(0,1)
are constants which will be determined later.

We argue by induction. The first step in this construction is obtained by Lipschitz estimate. By
Lemma

3.1
, we have

-\overline{C}x_{n}\leq u(x,t)\leq\overline{C}x_{n}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}},

where
\overline{C}>0
is determined in
Lemma

3.1
. In this case, we can take
a_{0}=-2\overline{C}
,
a_{1}=-\overline{C}
,
b_{0}=2\overline{C}
,
b_{1}=\overline{C}
, and
\mu=2^{\overline{\alpha}+1}\overline{C}
, then (
3.4
) holds for
k=1
.

Suppose that (
3.4
) holds for
k\geq 1
. We claim that (
3.4
) also holds for
(k+1)
. Let
r_{k}=2^{-k}
. Based on the induction assumption, there are two possible cases:

\text{either}\quad u(r_{k}e_{n}/2,-2r_{k}^{2-\gamma}/4^{2-\gamma})\geq\dfrac{r_{k}}{4}(a_{k}+b_{k})\quad\text{or}\quad u(r_{k}e_{n}/2,-2r_{k}^{2-\gamma}/4^{2-\gamma})<\dfrac{r_{k}}{4}(a_{k}+b_{k}).

Let us first assume the former. Then the function

w(x,t)=\frac{u(r_{k}x,r_{k}^{2-\gamma}t)-a_{k}r_{k}x_{n}}{r_{k}^{1+\overline{\alpha}}}\in[0,\mu]

satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} w&\in S(\lambda,\Lambda,0)&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
w&=0&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that if
b_{k}-a_{k}\leq\mu r_{k+1}^{\overline{\alpha}}
, then we take
a_{k+1}=a_{k}
and
b_{k+1}=b_{k}
. So we can assume that
b_{k}-a_{k}>\mu r_{k+1}^{\overline{\alpha}}
. By the Hopf principle [
Lemma

3.3
], we have

w(x,t)\geq\widetilde{C}w(e_{n}/2,-2/4^{2-\gamma})x_{n}\geq\frac{1}{4r_{k}^{\overline{\alpha}}}\widetilde{C}(b_{k}-a_{k})x_{n}>2^{-\overline{\alpha}-2}\widetilde{C}\mu x_{n}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}},

where
\widetilde{C}>0
is determined in
Lemma

3.3
. It follows that

u(x,t)\geq\Big(a_{k}+2^{-\overline{\alpha}-2}\widetilde{C}\mu r_{k}^{\overline{\alpha}}\Big)x_{n}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{r_{k+1}}^{+}}.

We now take

a_{k+1}=a_{k}+2^{-\overline{\alpha}-2}\widetilde{C}\mu r_{k}^{\overline{\alpha}}\quad\text{and}\quad b_{k+1}=b_{k},

and the constant
\overline{\alpha}\in(0,1)
is chosen such that
2^{\overline{\alpha}}\leq 1+\tilde{C}/4
. Then we have

\displaystyle b_{k+1}-a_{k+1}=b_{k}-a_{k}-2^{-\overline{\alpha}-2}\widetilde{C}\mu r_{k}^{\overline{\alpha}}\leq(1-2^{-\overline{\alpha}-2}\widetilde{C})\mu r_{k}^{\overline{\alpha}}\leq\mu r_{k+1}^{\overline{\alpha}}.

Thus, (
3.4
) holds for
(k+1)
. Similarly, we can obtain the iterative step in the case of
u(r_{k}e_{n}/2,-2r_{k}^{2-\gamma}/4^{2-\gamma})<r_{k}(a_{k}+b_{k})/4
.

Finally, this construction ensures that there exists a constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{k}=a=\lim_{k\to\infty}b_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad|a|\leq 2\overline{C}.

Furthermore, for any
(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}}
, we can choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
r_{k+1}<\max\{|x|,|t|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}}\}\leq r_{k}
, and hence we have

u(x,t)-ax_{n}\leq(b_{k}-a)x_{n}\leq 2^{\overline{\alpha}}\mu r_{k+1}^{\overline{\alpha}}x_{n}\leq C(|x|+|t|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{\overline{\alpha}}x_{n}.

Similarly, we can obtain the lower bound.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\overline{\alpha}\in(0,1)
and
C_{1}>1
be as in
Lemma

3.4
. For any
\alpha\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
, there exists
\delta>0
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
\alpha
such that if
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} u&\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u&=g&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}\end{aligned}\right.

with
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1
,
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq\delta
, and
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\})}\leq\delta
, then there exists a constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\|u-ax_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta}^{+})}\leq\eta^{1+\alpha}

and
|a|\leq C_{1}
, where
\eta>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
, and
\alpha
.

Proof.

We prove by contradiction; we suppose the conclusion does not hold. In other words, there exist sequences
u_{k}
,
f_{k}
, and
g_{k}
such that
u_{k}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{k}&\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f_{k})&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u_{k}&=g_{k}&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\},\end{aligned}\right.

\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1
,
\|f_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1/k
, and
\|g_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\})}\leq 1/k
. Moreover, for any constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
|a|\leq C_{1}
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|u_{k}-ax_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta}^{+})}>\eta^{1+\alpha},

where
C_{1}>1
and
\eta\in(0,1)
will be determined later.

We first note that
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})
and by the interior Hölder estimate (
[
21
, Theorem 4.19]
),
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is equicontinuous in compact sets of
Q_{1}^{+}
. Thus, by Arzela-Ascoli theorem, there exist a subsequence
\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and a limit function
\overline{u}\in C(Q_{1}^{+})
such that
u_{k_{j}}\to\overline{u}
uniformly in compact sets of
Q_{1}^{+}
as
j\to\infty
. Moreover, since
\|f_{k_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
and
\|g_{k_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\})}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
, the stability theorem yields that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \overline{u}&\in S(\lambda,\Lambda,0)&&\text{in }Q_{3/4}^{+}\\
\overline{u}&=0&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{3/4}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\},\end{aligned}\right.

Then, by
Lemma

3.4
, there exist
\overline{\alpha}\in(0,1)
and
C_{1}>1
such that

\displaystyle|\overline{u}(x,t)-\overline{a}x_{n}|\leq C_{1}(|x|+|t|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{1+\overline{\alpha}}\quad\text{for all $(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}}$}

and
|\overline{a}|\leq C_{1}
. For any
\alpha\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
, we take
\eta
small enough so that
2^{1+\overline{\alpha}}\eta^{\overline{\alpha}-\alpha}C_{1}<1/2
. Then we observe that

\displaystyle\|\overline{u}-\overline{a}x_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta}^{+})}\leq 2^{1+\overline{\alpha}}\eta^{1+\overline{\alpha}}C_{1}<\eta^{1+\alpha}/2.

On the other hand, by letting
k_{j}\to\infty
in (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\|\overline{u}-\overline{a}x_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta}^{+})}\geq\eta^{1+\alpha},

which leads to the contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\overline{\alpha}\in(0,1)
,
C_{1}>1
,
\delta>0
, and
\eta>0
be as in
Lemma

3.5
. Suppose that
\alpha\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
with
\alpha\leq 1-\gamma
and
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} u&\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u&=g&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}.\end{aligned}\right.

with
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1
,
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq\delta
, and

|g(x,t)|\leq\frac{\delta}{2^{1+\alpha}}(|x|+|t|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{1+\alpha}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}.

Then there exists a sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k=-1}^{\infty}

such that for all
k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\|u-a_{k}x_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta^{k}}^{+})}\leq\eta^{k(1+\alpha)}\quad\text{and}\quad|a_{k}-a_{k-1}|\leq C_{1}\eta^{(k-1)\alpha}.

Proof.

We argue by induction. For
k=0

, the conditions immediately hold. Suppose that the conclusion holds for

. We claim that the conclusion also holds for
(k+1)

For this purpose, let

,

,

, and

\displaystyle v(y,s)\coloneqq\frac{u(x,t)-a_{k}x_{n}}{r^{1+\alpha}}.

Then

\left\{\begin{aligned} v&\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,\widetilde{f})&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
v&=\widetilde{g}&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{y_{n}=0\},\end{aligned}\right.

where

\widetilde{f}(y,s)\coloneqq r^{1-\alpha-\gamma}f(x,t)\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{g}(y,s)\coloneqq\frac{g(x,t)-a_{k}x_{n}}{r^{1+\alpha}}.

Then it immediately follows that
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1
,
\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq\delta
, and

\displaystyle|\widetilde{g}(y,s)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r^{1+\alpha}}\cdot\frac{\delta}{2^{1+\alpha}}\cdot 2^{1+\alpha}r^{1+\alpha}=\delta\quad\text{for all }(y,s)\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{y_{n}=0\}.

Thus, by applying
Lemma

3.5
to

, there exists a constant
\widetilde{a}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|v-\widetilde{a}y_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta}^{+})}\leq\eta^{1+\alpha}

and

(3.8)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{a}|\leq C_{1}.

We now let

. Then (
3.8
) implies that

\displaystyle|a_{k+1}-a_{k}|\leq C_{1}\eta^{k\alpha}.

Finally, (
3.7
) shows that

\displaystyle|u(x,t)-a_{k+1}x_{n}|

\displaystyle=|u(x,t)-a_{k}x_{n}-r^{\alpha}\widetilde{a}x_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq r^{1+\alpha}|v(x/r,t/r^{2-\gamma})-\widetilde{a}(x_{n}/r)|

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{(k+1)(1+\alpha)}

for all
(x,t)\in Q_{\eta^{k+1}}^{+}
, as desired.
∎

We are now ready to prove the first main theorem,
Theorem

1.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
. We claim that the assumptions in

3.6
hold after the appropriate reduction argument. Since

Then
\overline{u}\coloneqq u-L_{g}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} \overline{u}&\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)&&\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
\overline{u}&=\overline{g}&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\overline{g}\coloneqq g-L_{g}
satisfies

For
K\coloneqq\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}/\delta+2^{1+\alpha}\|\overline{g}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(0,0)}/\delta+1
, by considering

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(x,t)\coloneqq\overline{u}(x,t)/K,\quad\widetilde{f}(x,t)\coloneqq f(x,t)/K,\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{g}(x,t)\coloneqq\overline{g}(x,t)/K,

we may assume that

,

, and

Thus, we can apply
Lemma

3.6
for
u
to find a sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k=-1}^{\infty}
satisfying (
3.6
). Indeed, by standard argument, we obtain limit
a
such that
a_{k}\to a
satisfying

\displaystyle|a_{k}-a|\leq C\eta^{k\alpha}.

Finally, for any

, there exists

such that

. Then we conclude that

\displaystyle|u(x,t)-ax_{n}|\leq|u(x,t)-a_{j}x_{n}|+|a_{j}-a||x_{n}|\leq C(|x|+|t|^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}})^{1+\alpha},

which implies that

is

at

.
∎

4.

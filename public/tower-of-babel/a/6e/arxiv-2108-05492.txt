Some Results on k k -Critical P 5 P_{5} -Free Graphs
Qingqiong Cai, Jan Goedgebeur, Shenwei Huang

Abstract
A graph G G is k k -vertex-critical if G G has chromatic number k k but every proper induced subgraph of G G has chromatic number less than k k . The study of k k -vertex-critical graphs for graph classes is an important topic in algorithmic graph theory because if the number of such graphs that are in a given hereditary graph class is finite, then there is a polynomial-time algorithm to decide if a graph in the class is ( k − 1 ) (k-1) -colorable. In this paper, we prove that for every fixed integer k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , there are only finitely many k k -vertex-critical ( P 5 P_{5} ,gem)-free graphs and ( P 5 , P 3 + P 2 ¯ ) (P_{5},\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}) -free graphs. To prove the results we use a known structure theorem for ( P 5 P_{5} ,gem)-free graphs combined with properties of k k -vertex-critical graphs. Moreover, we characterize all k k -vertex-critical ( P 5 P_{5} ,gem)-free graphs and ( P 5 , P 3 + P 2 ¯ ) (P_{5},\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}) -free graphs for k ∈ { 4 , 5 } k\in\{4,5\} using a computer generation algorithm.



Keywords.
Graph coloring;
k
-critical graphs; forbidden induced subgraphs; computer generation algorithm.

t_{j}\in T_{J}
are adjacent,
then
t_{i},t_{j},v_{j},v_{k}
induces a
P_{4}
. So
T_{I}
and
T_{J}
are anticomplete.

This proves that for each
I\subseteq K
,
T_{I}
is homogeneous with respect to
V(G)-A_{7}
.
Moreover,
\omega(T_{I})<\omega
. By the inductive hypothesis, it follows that
|T_{I}|\leq 2^{2(\omega-1)^{2}}
.
So

|H|=\sum_{I\subseteq K}|T_{I}|+|K|\leq 2^{2(\omega-1)^{2}}2^{\omega}+\omega.

Thus,
|X|\leq 2^{2(\omega-1)^{2}}2^{\omega}+\omega+\omega^{2}=2^{2\omega^{2}-3\omega+2}+(\omega+\omega^{2})
.
Since
\omega+\omega^{2}\leq 2^{3\omega-2}
, it follows that
|X|\leq 2^{2\omega^{2}}
.
This proves the claim.
∎

Applying
Claim 1
to
A_{6}
, we have that
|A_{6}|\leq 2^{2\omega^{2}(A_{6})}\leq 2^{2k^{2}}
. Finally, we bound
A_{7}
.

Claim 2
.

|A_{7}|\leq k2^{|A_{6}|}
.

Proof.

Recall that each component of
A_{7}
is a clique and so has size at most
k
.
We now group the components by size and show that each group has at most
2^{|A_{6}|}
components.
Suppose not. Then there are two components
C
and
C^{\prime}
of
A_{7}
having the same size
i
with

N(C)=N(C^{\prime})
, which contradicts
Lemma 2
. This proves the claim.
∎

Therefore,
|G|=\sum_{i=1}^{7}|A_{i}|\leq 5k+|A_{6}|+|A_{7}|
, which is a function of
k
.
∎

3.2
\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}
-Free Graphs

Theorem 5
.

For every fixed integer
k\geq 1
, there are finitely many
k
-vertex-critical (
P_{5}
,
\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}
)-free graphs.

Proof.

Let
G
be a
k
-vertex-critical (
P_{5}
,
\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}
)-free graph. We show that
|G|
is bounded by a function of
k
.
If
G
contains a
K_{k}
, then
G
is isomorphic to
K_{k}
and thus
|G|=k
.
So assume that
G
is
K_{k}
-free. Since
G
is imperfect,
G
contains an induced
C_{5}
by
Theorem 3
.
Let
Q=v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5}
be an induced
C_{5}
. For each
1\leq i\leq 5
, we define

\begin{split}Z&=\{v\in V\setminus Q:N_{Q}(v)=\emptyset\},\\
C_{i}&=\{v\in V\setminus Q:N_{Q}(v)=\{v_{i-1},v_{i+1}\}\},\\
Y_{i}&=\{v\in V\setminus Q:N_{Q}(v)=\{v_{i-2},v_{i},v_{i+2}\}\},\\
T_{i}&=\{v\in V\setminus Q:N_{Q}(v)=\{v_{i-1},v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\},\\
F_{i}&=\{v\in V\setminus Q:N_{Q}(v)=V(Q)\setminus\{v_{i}\}\},\\
U&=\{v\in V\setminus Q:N_{Q}(v)=V(Q)\}.\\
\end{split}

Let
C=\cup_{1\leq i\leq 5}C_{i}
,
Y=\cup_{1\leq i\leq 5}Y_{i}
,
T=\cup_{1\leq i\leq 5}T_{i}
and
F=\cup_{1\leq i\leq 5}F_{i}
.
We choose
Q
so that
|Y|
is maximum.

Claim 3
.

V(G)=V(Q)\cup Z\cup C\cup Y\cup T\cup F\cup U
.

Proof.

Suppose that the claim is false. Then there is a vertex
u
such that
\{v_{i}\}\subseteq N(u)\subseteq\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}
for some

1\leq i\leq 5
. Then
u,v_{i},v_{i+4},v_{i+3},v_{i+2}
induces a
P_{5}
.
∎

We first bound
U
.

Claim 4
.

For each
1\leq i\leq 5
,
Y_{i}\cup U
and
F_{i}\cup U
are cliques.

Proof.

Suppose that
Y_{i}\cup U
contains two nonadjacent vertices
x
and
y
, then
\{x,y,v_{i-2},v_{i},v_{i+2}\}
induces a
\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}
. The proof for
F_{i}\cup U
is completely analogous.
∎

By
Claim 4
, it follows that
|U|<k
.

Claim 5
.

For each
1\leq i\leq 5
,
C_{i}
,
Y_{i}
and
F_{i}
are independent sets.

Proof.

Note that for each vertex
x\in C\cup Y\cup F
, there is an index
i
such that
x
is not adjacent to
v_{i}
but adjacent to
both
v_{i+1}
and
v_{i-1}
. If
C_{i}
contains two adjacent vertices
x
and
y
, then let
j
be such that

x,y
are not adjacent to
v_{j}
but adjacent to both
v_{j-1}
and
v_{j+1}
. Then
\{x,y,v_{j-1},v_{j},v_{j+1}\}
induces a
\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}
.
The proof for
Y_{i}
and
F_{i}
is completely analogous.
∎

By
Claim 4
and
Claim 5
,
|Y_{i}|\leq 1
and
|F_{i}|\leq 1
. So
|Y|\leq 5
and
|F|\leq 5
.
Next we bound
Z
.

Claim 6
.

Z
is anticomplete to
C\cup T
.

Proof.

Let
z\in Z
. If
z
has a neighbor
x\in C_{i}\cup T_{i}
, then
z,x,v_{i+1},v_{i+2},v_{i+3}
induces a
P_{5}
.
∎

Claim 7
.

Each vertex in
Y\cup F
is either complete or anticomplete to each component of
Z
.

Proof.

Let
v\in Y\cup F
be that
v
is adjacent to
a
but not adjacent to
b
with
ab\in E(Z)
.
Note that there is an index
i
such that
v,v_{i},v_{i+1}
is an induced
P_{3}
.
Then
b,a,v,v_{i},v_{i+1}
induces a
P_{5}
.
∎

Claim 8
.

Z
is an independent set.

Proof.

Suppose not. Let
K
be a component of
Z
containing an edge
ab
. By
Claim 6
,
N(K)\subseteq Y\cup F\cup U
.
Since
G
has no clique cutset, it follows from
Claim 7
and
Claim 4
that there are two nonadjacent
vertices
x
and
y
in
Y\cup F
that are complete to
K
. Let
v_{i}\in V(Q)
be a common neighbor of
x
and
y
.
Then
\{a,b,x,y,v_{i}\}
induces a
\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}
.
∎

By
Claim 6
,
N(z)\subseteq Y\cup F\cup U
for each
z\in Z
. Note that
|Y\cup F\cup U|\leq k+10
and so
there are
2^{10+k}
possible neighborhoods for vertices in
Z
.
By
Lemma 2
and the pigeonhole principle,
|Z|\leq 2^{10+k}
.

Next we bound
C
.

Claim 9
.

C_{i}
and
C_{i+1}
are complete to each other.

Proof.

Let
c_{3}\in C_{3}
and
c_{4}\in C_{4}
. If
c_{3}c_{4}\notin E
, then
c_{4},v_{5},v_{1},v_{2},c_{3}
induces a
P_{5}
.
∎

Claim 10
.

C_{i}
is complete to
T_{i}
.

Proof.

By symmetry, we prove for
i=1
. If
c_{1}\in C_{1}
and
t_{1}\in T_{1}
are not adjacent, then
\{c_{1},t_{1},v_{5},v_{1},v_{2}\}
induces a
\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}
.
∎

Claim 11
.

C_{i}
is anticomplete to
T_{i+1}
and
T_{i-1}
.

Proof.

If
c_{1}\in C_{1}
is adjacent to
t_{2}\in T_{2}
, then
\{v_{5},c_{1},v_{1},v_{2},t_{2}\}
induces a
\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}
.
The case for
T_{5}
is similar.
∎

Claim 12
.

No vertex in
T_{i+2}
or
T_{i-2}
can be mixed on
C_{i}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
t_{3}\in T_{3}
is mixed on
C_{1}
. Then there are vertices
c_{1},c^{\prime}_{1}\in C_{1}
with
t_{3}c_{1}\in E
and
t_{3}c^{\prime}_{1}\notin E
.
Then
c^{\prime}_{1},v_{5},c_{1},t_{3},v_{3}
induces a
P_{5}
.
The case for
T_{4}
is similar.
∎

Claim 13
.

For each
1\leq i\leq 5
,
|C_{i}|\leq 2^{|Y\cup F\cup U|}
.

Proof.

Suppose not. Then by the pigeonhole principle, there are two vertices
c_{1},c_{2}\in C_{1}
such that
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
have the same neighbors in
Y\cup F\cup U
. By
Claim 6
and
Claim 9
-
Claim 12
, it follows that
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
have the
same neighbors in
Z\cup T\cup C_{1}\cup C_{2}\cup C_{5}
. Since
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are not comparable, there exist
d_{1}\in N(c_{1})\setminus N(c_{2})

and
d_{2}\in N(c_{2})\setminus N(c_{1})
. So
d_{1},d_{2}\in C_{3}\cup C_{4}
.
If
d_{1},d_{2}\in C_{3}
, then
d_{1},c_{1},v_{5},c_{2},d_{2}
induces a
P_{5}
.
So we may assume that
d_{1}\in C_{4}
and
d_{2}\in C_{3}
.

Consider the induced five-cycle
Q^{\prime}=Q-\{v_{1}\}\cup\{c_{1}\}
.
Let
Y^{\prime}
be the set of vertices in
V(G)\setminus V(Q^{\prime})
whose neighborhood on
Q^{\prime}
induces a
P_{2}+P_{1}
.
Note that
d_{1}\in Y^{\prime}\setminus Y
. By the choice of
Q
, there exists a vertex
y\in Y\setminus Y^{\prime}
.
Clearly,
y\in Y_{1}\cup Y_{3}\cup Y_{4}
and
yc_{1}\notin E
. If
y\in Y_{1}
, then
c_{1},v_{5},v_{1},y,v_{3}
induces a
P_{5}
.
So
y\in Y_{3}\cup Y_{4}
. By symmetry, we may assume that
y\in Y_{4}
.
Note that
yc_{2}\notin E
since otherwise
\{y,c_{2},v_{1},v_{2},v_{5}\}
induces a
\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}
,
yd_{1}\in E
since otherwise
y,v_{1},v_{5},d_{1},v_{3}
induces a
P_{5}
, and that

yd_{2}\notin E
since otherwise
\{y,d_{1},d_{2},v_{2},v_{3}\}
induces a
\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}
.
But now
c_{2},d_{2},d_{1},y,v_{1}
induces a
P_{5}
, a contradiction.
∎

By
Claim 13
,
|C|\leq 5\times 2^{k+10}
. Finally, we bound
T
.

Claim 14
.

For each
1\leq i\leq 5
,

is
P_{2}+P_{1}
-free and thus induces a complete multipartite graph.

Proof.

If

contains three vertices

such that

and

induce a disjoint union of an edge and a vertex,
then
\{x,y,z,v_{i-1},v_{i+1}\}
induces a
\overline{P_{3}+P_{2}}
. It is well-known that a graph is a
complete multipartite graph if and only if it is
P_{2}+P_{1}
-free.
∎

Claim 15
.

For each
1\leq i\leq 5
,
T_{i}
and
T_{i+1}
are complete.

Proof.

If
t_{1}\in T_{1}
and
t_{2}\in T_{2}
are not adjacent, then
t_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5},t_{1}
induces a
P_{5}
.
∎

Claim 16
.

|T_{i}|\leq k2^{|Z\cup C\cup Y\cup F\cup U|}
.

Proof.

By
Claim 14
,
T_{1}
is a complete
r
-partite graph
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
for some

where

and
X_{j}
are complete for all

, then by the pigeonhole principle there are two nonadjacent vertices
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
that have the same neighbors in

. Since
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are not comparable, there are vertices
y_{1}\in N(x_{1})\setminus N(x_{2})
and
y_{2}\in N(x_{2})\setminus N(x_{1})
. By
Claim 15
,
y_{1},y_{2}\in T_{3}\cup T_{4}
. Then
v_{4},y_{1},x_{1},v_{1},x_{2}

induces a
P_{5}
.
∎

By
Claim 16
,
|T|
is bounded by a function of

and so is
|G|
. This completes the proof.
∎

1.
Introduction

In this paper we deal with solvability of a system having the form

(1.1)

-\left[\phi(u^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}=\nabla_{u}F(t,u)\quad\mbox{in }[0,T],

associated with the potential multivalued boundary condition

(1.2)

\left(\phi\left(u^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi\left(u^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)\in\partial j(u(0),u(T)),

under the following hypotheses:

\begin{array}[]{clrc}(H_{\Phi})&\phi\,\,\mbox{\sl is a homeomorphism from }B_{a}\,\,\mbox{\sl onto }\mathbb{R}^{N},\,\,\mbox{\sl such that }\phi(0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}})=0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}},\,\phi=\nabla\Phi,\,\,\mbox{\sl with }\\
&\Phi:\overline{B}_{a}\to(-\infty,0]\,\,\mbox{\sl of class }C^{1}\,\mbox{\sl on }B_{a},\,\,\mbox{\sl continuous and strictly convex on }\overline{B}_{a};\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{clrc}(H_{F})&F\!=\!F(t,u)\!:\![0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\,\,\mbox{\sl is continuous, }\nabla_{u}F\,\,\mbox{\sl exists and is continuous on the set}\\
&[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{\sl and }F(\,\cdot\,,0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}})=0;\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{clrc}(H_{j})&j:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]\,\,\mbox{\sl is proper, convex, lower semicontinuous, }j(0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}})=0\\
&\mbox{\sl and }0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\in\partial j(0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}).\end{array}

Here, for
0<r<\infty
,
B_{r}
denotes the open ball of radius
r
centered at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
– considered with the Euclidean norm
|\cdot|,
and
\partial j
stands for the subdifferential of
j
in the sense of convex analysis
[
31
]
. As
a
in (
H_{\Phi}
) is finite, according to an already usual terminology, the
\phi
-Laplacian operator
u\mapsto\left[\phi(u^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}
is called ”singular”.
It is worth to notice that assumption of strict convexity of
\Phi
in hypothesis
(H_{\Phi})
ensures that
\phi
is strictly monotone on
B_{a}
while, by the lower semicontinuity assumption on the proper convex function

in (
H_{j}
), the multivalued operator
\partial j:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is maximal monotone
[
14
,
31
]
.

The prototype of singular
\phi
-Laplacians is the relativistic operator

\displaystyle u\mapsto{\mathcal{R}}u:=\left[\frac{u^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1-|u^{\prime}|^{2}}}\right]^{\prime},

which is engendered by

\phi(y)=\frac{y}{\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}}\quad(y\in B_{1})

and has the potential
\Phi:\overline{B}_{1}\to(-\infty,0]
,
\Phi(y)=-\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}
. The operator
{\mathcal{R}}
occurs in the dynamics of special relativity
[
11
,
27
]
. So, for
N=3
, it is involved in
equations describing the motion of some mechanical systems when the Newtonian acceleration is replaced by
the relativistic one (with the velocity of light normalized to
1
). Also, a natural extension of
{\mathcal{R}}
appears as being the so-called
p
-relativistic operator

\displaystyle u\mapsto{\mathcal{R}}_{p}u:=\left[\frac{|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime}}{(1-|u^{\prime}|^{p})^{1-1/p}}\right]^{\prime}\qquad(p>1)

which is another relevant example of singular
\phi
-Laplacian having the structure required by hypothesis
(H_{\Phi})

[
20
,
24
]
.

By

1.1
)-(

\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|<a

, such that

,

and which satisfies (

Due to the boundedness of the derivative of a solution, it is noteworthy that its values at the end points

and

D_{\sigma}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,|x-y|<\sigma\},

and

)-(

(1.3)

\left|u(T)-u(0)\right|\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|dt<Ta,

clearly one has that

(1.4)

\left(u(0),u(T)\right)\in D_{Ta}\cap D\left(\partial j\right).

Besides the topological techniques – such as Leray-Schauder degree, fixed point index or lower and upper solutions, the variational approach proved to be very effective in treatment of this class of problems. Thus, starting from a formulation in terms of variational inequality due to H. Brezis and J. Mawhin

-functionals developed by A. Szulkin
[

enabled the treatment of various systems, among others concerning Lorentz force equation
[

[

]
.

On the other hand, the boundary condition (

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we consider an auxiliary problem in which the system is simpler than (

) but it is subject to a more general boundary condition – in the sense that

1.1
)-(
1.2
) in the frame of the critical point theory for convex, lower semicontinuous perturbations of

-functionals. Here the existence and uniqueness result from the previous section plays a key role in proving that any critical point of the energy functional is actually a solution of problem (

)-(
1.2

which are periodic (Corollary

2.
An auxiliary existence and uniqueness result

On
\mathbb{R}^{N}
we consider the usual inner product
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle
engendering the norm
|\cdot|
. The notation
\|\cdot\|
stands for the norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
corresponding to the
inner product
\langle\!\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle\!\rangle
introduced in the previous section.
By
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
we mean the uniform norm on
C:=C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{N})

and the usual norm on

will be denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
(
1\leq p\leq\infty
). The Sobolev space
W^{1,\infty}:=W^{1,\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{N})
is considered as being endowed with the norm
\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}}=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Let

be fixed and
\gamma:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to 2^{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be a monotone operator. We consider the problem

[formula]

-\left[\phi(u^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}+u=h(t),\qquad\left(\phi\left(u^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi\left(u^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)\in\gamma(u(0),u(T)).

The notion of

for the problem
[{\mathcal{P}}_{\gamma}(h)]
is perfectly similar to that of the solution for (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) from the previous section, with the difference that instead of
\partial j
is
\gamma
. Notice that if

is a solution of
[{\mathcal{P}}_{\gamma}(h)]
then necessarily
(u(0),u(T))\in D_{Ta}\cap D\left(\gamma\right).

Proposition 2.1
.

Problem
[{\mathcal{P}}_{\gamma}(h)]
has at most one solution.

Proof.

If
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are solutions of
[{\mathcal{P}}_{\gamma}(h)]
, from the monotonicity of
\gamma
it follows

\omega(u_{1},u_{2}):=\langle\phi(u_{1}^{\prime})(0)-\phi(u_{2}^{\prime})(0)\,|\,u_{1}(0)-u_{2}(0)\rangle-\langle\phi(u_{1}^{\prime})(T)-\phi(u_{2}^{\prime})(T)\,|\,u_{1}(T)-u_{2}(T)\rangle

=\!\langle\!\langle\left(\phi\left(u_{1}^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi\left(u_{1}^{\prime}\right)(T)\right),\left(\phi\left(u_{2}^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi\left(u_{2}^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)|\left(u_{1}(0),u_{1}(T)\right)-\left(u_{2}(0),u_{2}(T)\right)\rangle\!\rangle\geq 0.

Then, multiplying the equality

-\left[\phi(u_{1}^{\prime})-\phi(u_{2}^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}+u_{1}-u_{2}=0

by
(u_{1}-u_{2})
and integrating by parts over
[0,T]
, we get

\omega(u_{1},u_{2})+\int_{0}^{T}\langle\phi(u_{1}^{\prime})-\phi(u_{2}^{\prime})\,|\,u_{1}^{\prime}-u_{2}^{\prime}\rangle+\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0,

which implies
u_{1}=u_{2}
, by the monotonicity of
\phi
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

The mapping
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\ni(x,y)\mapsto(\overline{u}_{x,y}(0),\overline{u}_{x,y}(T))\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
is continuous.

Proof.

Let
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Integrating

(2.2)

-\left[\phi(\overline{u}_{x,y}^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}+\overline{u}_{x,y}=h(t)

over
[0,T]
we obtain

(2.3)

\int_{0}^{T}\overline{u}_{x,y}(t)dt=y-x+\int_{0}^{T}h.

Also, from

\phi(\overline{u}_{x,y}^{\prime})(s)=x+\int_{0}^{s}\left(\overline{u}_{x,y}-h\right)d\tau\qquad(s\in[0,T])

we deduce

\overline{u}_{x,y}(t)=\overline{u}_{x,y}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{-1}\left(x+\int_{0}^{s}(\overline{u}_{x,y}-h)d\tau\right)ds\qquad(t\in[0,T])

and, combining this with (
2.3
), it follows

(2.4)

\overline{u}_{x,y}(0)=\frac{1}{T}\left[y-x+\int_{0}^{T}h-\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{-1}\left(x+\int_{0}^{s}(\overline{u}_{x,y}-h)d\tau\right)ds\right]dt\right]

Similarly, we derive

(2.5)

\overline{u}_{x,y}(T)=\frac{1}{T}\left[y-x+\int_{0}^{T}h-\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\phi^{-1}\left(y+\int_{T}^{s}(\overline{u}_{x,y}-h)d\tau\right)ds\right]dt\right]

Now, let
(x_{n},y_{n})\rightarrow(x,y)
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, as
n\to\infty
. From (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) and the boundedness of the range of
\phi^{-1}
we infer that the sequences
\{\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}(0)\}

and
\{\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}(T)\}
are bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Using the monotonicity of
\phi
and integration by parts formula, we have the estimate

0=-\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\left[\phi(\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}^{\prime})-\phi(\overline{u}_{x,y}^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}\,|\,\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}-\overline{u}_{x,y}\right\rangle+\|\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}-\overline{u}_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\geq\left\langle x_{n}-x\,|\,\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}(0)-\overline{u}_{x,y}(0)\right\rangle-\left\langle y_{n}-y\,|\,\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}(T)-\overline{u}_{x,y}(T)\right\rangle+\|\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}-\overline{u}_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

which gives

\|\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}-\overline{u}_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\langle y_{n}-y\,|\,\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}(T)-\overline{u}_{x,y}(T)\right\rangle-\left\langle x_{n}-x\,|\,\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}(0)-\overline{u}_{x,y}(0)\right\rangle,

implying that

in

and hence in

, as

.

To complete the proof, we show that

\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}(0)\rightarrow\overline{u}_{x,y}(0),\quad\mbox{as }n\to\infty;

similar argument for

. In this respect, since for any

,

we have

\phi^{-1}\left(x_{n}+\int_{0}^{s}(\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}-h)d\tau\right)\to\phi^{-1}\left(x+\int_{0}^{s}(\overline{u}_{x,y}-h)d\tau\right),\quad\mbox{as }n\to\infty,

It follows

\beta_{n}:=\int_{0}^{T}\left|\phi^{-1}\left(x_{n}+\int_{0}^{s}(\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}-h)d\tau\right)-\phi^{-1}\left(x+\int_{0}^{s}(\overline{u}_{x,y}-h)d\tau\right)\right|ds\to 0,\quad\mbox{as }n\to\infty.

Then, using (
2.4
), it is straightforward to see that

\left|\overline{u}_{x_{n},y_{n}}(0)-\overline{u}_{x,y}(0)\right|\leq\frac{1}{T}\left[|y_{n}-y|+|x_{n}-x|+T\beta_{n}\right],

which clearly implies (
2.6
).
∎

Now, define
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
by setting

D(\theta):=D_{Ta},\qquad\theta(x,y):=\left(-\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})(0),\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})(T)\right)\quad\left((x,y)\in D_{Ta}\right).

Proposition 2.6
.

The operator
\theta
is maximal monotone and coercive.

Proof.

To prove the maximal monotonicity of
\theta
, according to
[
14
, Proposition 2.2]
, we have to show that: (i)
\theta
is monotone and (ii)
\theta+i_{d}:D_{Ta}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
is surjective (here,
i_{d}
stands for the identity mapping on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
).

(i) Let
(x,y),\,(\xi,\eta)\in D_{Ta}
. The monotonicity inequality

\langle\!\langle\theta(x,y)-\theta(\xi,\eta)|(x,y)-(\xi,\eta)\rangle\!\rangle\geq 0

means

(2.7)

\beta:=-\langle\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})(0)-\phi(u_{\xi,\eta}^{\prime})(0)|x-\xi\rangle+\langle\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})(T)-\phi(u_{\xi,\eta}^{\prime})(T)|y-\eta\rangle\geq 0.

To see that this is true, we integrate by parts and make use of the boundary conditions (
u_{x,y}(0)=x,\,u_{x,y}(T)=y,\,u_{\xi,\eta}(0)=\xi,\,u_{\xi,\eta}(T)=\eta
) in

0=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\left[\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})-\phi(u_{\xi,\eta}^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}|\,u_{x,y}-u_{\xi,\eta}\rangle-\|u_{x,y}-u_{\xi,\eta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

obtaining

0=\beta-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})-\phi(u_{\xi,\eta}^{\prime})|\,(u_{x,y}^{\prime}-u_{\xi,\eta}^{\prime}\rangle)-\|u_{x,y}-u_{\xi,\eta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

which, on account of the monotonicity of
\phi
, clearly implies (
2.7
).

(ii) For an arbitrary given and fixed
(\xi,\eta)\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, we have to show that there is some
(x,y)=(x_{\xi},y_{\eta})\in D_{Ta}
such that

(2.8)

\left(-\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})(0),\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})(T)\right)=\left(\xi-x,\eta-y\right).

In this view, we proceed by a fixed point argument, as follows. Let the operator
\Lambda=\Lambda_{\xi,\eta}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
be defined by

\Lambda(x,y)=\left(\overline{u}_{x-\xi,\eta-y}(0),\overline{u}_{x-\xi,\eta-y}(T)\right)\quad\left((x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

Suppose that we have already proved that there exists a fixed point
(x,y)
of
\Lambda
. This means

(2.9)

\left(\overline{u}_{x-\xi,\eta-y}(0),\overline{u}_{x-\xi,\eta-y}(T)\right)=(x,y),

which reads: the solution
\overline{u}_{x-\xi,\eta-y}
of the Neumann problem
({\mathcal{N}}_{x-\xi,\eta-y})
satisfies the Dirichlet boundary conditions

(2.10)

\overline{u}_{x-\xi,\eta-y}(0)=x,\quad\overline{u}_{x-\xi,\eta-y}(T)=y.

Thus,
(x,y)\in D_{Ta}
by Proposition
2.3

(i)
and
\overline{u}_{x-\xi,\eta-y}=u_{x,y}
which implies (
2.8
).

Therefore, it remains to prove that
\Lambda
has a fixed point. We apply the a priori estimate method. By Proposition
2.5
it is immediate that
\Lambda
is continuous and hence compact on the finite dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
According to Schaefer’s theorem (see e.g.
[
28
, Corollary 4.4.12]
), it suffices to show that there is a constant
c>0
such that

(2.11)

|x|+|y|\leq c

for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying
(x,y)=\mu\,\Lambda(x,y)
with some
\mu\in(0,1].

So, let
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
be such that

(x,y)=\mu\left(\overline{u}_{x-\xi,\eta-y}(0),\overline{u}_{x-\xi,\eta-y}(T)\right)

with
\mu\in(0,1].
From (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) we have

(2.12)

\left(\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{1}{T}\right)x+\frac{1}{T}\,y=Q_{1}(x,y)\ \mbox{ and }\ \frac{1}{T}\,x+\left(\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{1}{T}\right)y=Q_{2}(x,y)

where

Q_{1}(x,y):=\frac{1}{T}\left[\xi+\eta+\int_{0}^{T}h-\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{-1}\left(x-\xi+\int_{0}^{s}(\overline{u}_{x-\xi,\eta-y}-h)d\tau\right)ds\right]dt\right],

Q_{2}(x,y):=\frac{1}{T}\left[\xi+\eta+\int_{0}^{T}h-\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\phi^{-1}\left(\eta-y+\int_{T}^{s}(\overline{u}_{x-\xi,\eta-y}-h)d\tau\right)ds\right]dt\right].

Solving (
2.12
) we obtain

(2.13)

x=\frac{\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{1}{T}\right)Q_{1}(x,y)-\frac{1}{T}Q_{2}(x,y)}{\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{1}{T}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{T}\right)^{2}},\quad y=\frac{\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{1}{T}\right)Q_{2}(x,y)-\frac{1}{T}Q_{1}(x,y)}{\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{1}{T}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{T}\right)^{2}}.

Then, using that

\max\left\{\left|Q_{1}(x,y)\right|\mbox{, }\left|Q_{2}(x,y)\right|\right\}\leq\frac{1}{T}\left[\left|\xi\right|+\left|\eta\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{T}h\right|+T^{2}a\right]=:\overline{Q}=\overline{Q}(\xi,\eta),

together with the assumption
\mu\in(0,1]
and (
2.13
), it is straightforward to see that (
2.11
) holds true with
c=2\,\overline{Q}
and the proof of the maximal monotonicity of
\theta
is complete.

We show that
\theta
is coercive, i.e.,

(2.14)

\lim_{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{crlc}&\|(x,y)\|\to+\infty\\
&(x,y)\in D_{Ta}\end{array}}\!\!{\frac{\langle\!\langle\theta(x,y)|(x,y)\rangle\!\rangle}{\|(x,y)\|}=+\infty}.

Let
(x,y)\in D_{Ta}
. Integrating by parts and using the boundary conditions (
u_{x,y}(0)=x
,
u_{x,y}(T)=y
) in

-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\left[\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}|\,u_{x,y}\rangle+\|u_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\langle h|\,u_{x,y}\rangle

we obtain

-\langle\!\langle\theta(x,y)|(x,y)\rangle\!\rangle+\int_{0}^{T}\langle\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})|\,u_{x,y}^{\prime}\rangle+\|u_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\langle h|\,u_{x,y}\rangle,

which gives

(2.15)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\theta(x,y)|(x,y)\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|u_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle h|\,u_{x,y}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|u_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|u_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}-\sqrt{T}\|h\|_{\infty}\right).

Let
t_{0}\in[0,T]
be such that

\int_{0}^{T}|u_{x,y}(t)|dt=T|u_{x,y}(t_{0})|.

From

|u_{x,y}(t_{0})|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\|u_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}

and

u_{x,y}(t)=u_{x,y}(t_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}u_{x,y}^{\prime}(s)ds

it follows

(2.16)

|u_{x,y}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\|u_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}+Ta\qquad(t\in[0,T]).

As
x=u_{x,y}(0)
,
y=u_{x,y}(T)
, this yields

\max\left\{|x|,\,|y|\right\}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\|u_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}+Ta

and, hence

\|(x,y)\|=\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}\leq\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\|u_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}+Ta\right),

or

\|u_{x,y}\|_{L^{2}}\geq\sqrt{T}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|(x,y)\|-Ta\right)

This together with (
2.15
) shows that, for
(x,y)\in D_{Ta}
with
\|(x,y)\|>\sqrt{2}(Ta+\|h\|_{\infty})
, it holds

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\theta(x,y)|(x,y)\rangle\!\rangle\geq T\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|(x,y)\|-Ta\right)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|(x,y)\|-Ta-\|h\|_{\infty}\right),

which clearly implies (
2.14
) and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

(
i
) Observe that by the reasoning in the proof of inequality (
2.16
) we can actually infer that any
v\in W^{1,\infty}
with
\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq a

satisfies

(2.17)

|v(t)|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}+Ta\qquad(t\in[0,T]).

(
ii
) It is worth comparing the proof of the maximal monotonicity of
\theta
in our singular case with that in the classical case. More precisely, if for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we denote by
q_{x,y}
the unique solution of the Dirichlet problem

-q^{\prime\prime}+q=h(t)\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\qquad q(0)=x,\,q(T)=y

and we set
\theta_{0}(x,y):=(-q_{x,y}^{\prime}(0),q_{x,y}^{\prime}(T))
, then it can be shown (see the proof of Proposition 3.1 from
[
25
]
) that
\theta_{0}
is monotone and continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Thus, since
D(\theta_{0})=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, it will follow from
[
14
, Proposition 2.4]
that it is maximal monotone. By comparison, in our case - of the operator
\theta
, it occurs the obstruction of the fact that
D(\theta)=D_{Ta}\subsetneqq\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
and hence the above argument can not be invoked. This is why to overcome this difficulty we have been led to provide a completely different proof.

Now, we can state the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.8
.

If
\gamma
is maximal monotone and
0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\in\gamma(0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}})
, then problem
[{\mathcal{P}}_{\gamma}(h)]
has an unique solution
u_{h}
, for any
h\in C
.

Proof.

The uniqueness part is ensured by Proposition
2.1
. The prove the existence, let
\Gamma:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to 2^{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be defined by

D(\Gamma):=D(\gamma)\cap D_{Ta},\qquad\Gamma(x,y):=\gamma(x,y)+\theta(x,y)\quad\left((x,y)\in D(\gamma)\cap D_{Ta}\right).

Since
\theta
is maximal monotone (Proposition
2.6
) and
D(\gamma)\cap\mbox{int}D(\theta)=D(\gamma)\cap D_{Ta}\ni 0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, from
[
14
, Corollaire 2.7]
we have that

\Gamma
is maximal monotone.

We
claim
that
\Gamma:D(\Gamma)\to 2^{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is surjective.

If
D(\Gamma)
is bounded this follows by
[
14
, Corollaire 2.2]
. If this is not the case, we show that
\Gamma
is coercive, in the sense that

(2.18)

\lim_{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{crlc}&\|(x,y)\|\to+\infty\\
&(x,y)\in D(\Gamma)\end{array}}\!\!{\frac{\displaystyle\inf\left\{\langle\!\langle(\xi,\eta)|(x,y)\rangle\!\rangle\,:\,(\xi,\eta)\in\Gamma(x,y)\right\}}{\|(x,y)\|}=+\infty}.

According to
[
14
, Corollaire 2.4]
(also, see
[
33
, Corollary 32.35]
), this will ensure the surjectivity of
\Gamma
, as claimed.

So, let
(x,y)\in D(\Gamma)
and
(\xi,\eta)\in\Gamma(x,y)
. To estimate
\langle\!\langle(\xi,\eta)|(x,y)\rangle\!\rangle
, let
(\zeta,\nu)\in\gamma(x,y)
be with

(\xi,\eta)=(\zeta,\nu)+\theta(x,y).
Using that
\gamma
is monotone and
0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\in\gamma(0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}})
, we obtain

\langle\!\langle(\xi,\eta)|(x,y)\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle(\zeta,\nu)-0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(x,y)-0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rangle\!\rangle+\langle\!\langle\theta(x,y)|(x,y)\rangle\!\rangle\geq\langle\!\langle\theta(x,y)|(x,y)\rangle\!\rangle.

This implies

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\langle\!\langle(\xi,\eta)|(x,y)\rangle\!\rangle\,:\,(\xi,\eta)\in\Gamma(x,y)\right\}\geq\langle\!\langle\theta(x,y)|(x,y)\rangle\!\rangle

which, together with the coercivity of
\theta
(see Proposition
2.6
), yields (
2.18
).

Now, since
\Gamma
is surjective, we have that there exists
(x,y)\in D(\gamma)\cap D_{Ta}
such that

0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\in\gamma(x,y)+\left(-\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})(0),\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})(T)\right),

which means

\left(\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})(0),-\phi(u_{x,y}^{\prime})(T)\right)\in\gamma(u_{x,y}(0),u_{x,y}(T))

and the proof is accomplished by taking
u_{h}=u_{x,y}
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

On account of Remark
2.4
it is easily seen that in the particular case
N=1
Theorem
2.8
holds true under the following hypothesis weaker than
(H_{\Phi})
: ”
\phi:(-a,a)\to\mathbb{R}
is an increasing homeomorphism”.

3.
A variational approach

Under hypothesis
(H_{j})
, Theorem
2.8
will be employed with
\gamma=\partial j
. Thus, for
h\in C
, we consider the problem

[formula]

-\left[\phi(u^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}+u=h(t),\qquad\left(\phi\left(u^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi\left(u^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)\in\partial j(u(0),u(T)).

Since
\partial j
is maximal monotone and
0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\in\partial j(0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}})
, from Theorem
2.8
this problem has an unique
solution
u_{h}
.

Setting

{\mathcal{K}}:=\left\{v\in W^{1,\infty}\,:\,\|v^{\prime}\|_{{}_{L^{\infty}}}\leq a\right\},

we define
\Psi:C\to(-\infty,+\infty]
by

\Psi(v)=\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left[\Phi(v^{\prime})-\Phi(0)\right],&\hbox{if $v\in{\mathcal{K}}$;}\\
\\
+\infty,&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then, following exactly the outline and the arguments from the proof of Lemma 1 and
Step I
in the proof of Proposition 1 from
[
10
]
(also, see
[
16
, Lemma 4.1]
), we get that
the convex set
{\mathcal{K}}
is closed and
\Psi
is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous. We also introduce the function
J:C\to(-\infty,+\infty]
given by

J(v)=j(v(0),v(T))\qquad(v\in C).

Since
j
is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous, it is immediate that the same hold true for
J
.
Notice that

(3.1)

\begin{array}[]{rl}D(\Psi+J)=D(\Psi)\cap D(J)=&\left\{v\in{\mathcal{K}}\,:\,(v(0),v(T))\in D(j)\right\}\\
=&\left\{v\in{\mathcal{K}}\,:\,(v(0),v(T))\in D(j)\cap\overline{D}_{Ta}\right\},\end{array}

u_{h}\in D(\Psi+J)
and
(u_{h}(0),u_{h}(T))\in{D}_{Ta}\cap D\left(\partial j\right)
(see (
1.4
)). Also, as
\Psi
is bounded from below and
J\geq 0
, it is clear that
\Psi+J
is bounded from below on
C
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The solution
u_{h}
of problem
[{\mathcal{P}}_{\partial j}(h)]
is the unique solution in
D(\Psi+J)
of the variational inequality

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left[\Phi(v^{\prime})-\Phi(u^{\prime})\right]+J(v)-J(u)+\int_{0}^{T}\langle u-h\,|\,v-u\rangle\geq 0,\quad\mbox{ for all }v\in D(\Psi+J)

and the unique minimum point of
E:C\to(-\infty,+\infty]
defined by

E(v)=\Psi(v)+J(v)+\frac{1}{2}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{0}^{T}\langle h\,|\,v\rangle\qquad(v\in C).

Proof.

First, using the elementary inequality

\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}\geq\langle x\,|\,y-x\rangle\qquad(x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}),

it is straightforward to see that any solution of (
3.2
) is a minimum point of
E
. Therefore, it suffices to prove that
u_{h}
solves (
3.2
) and that it is
the unique minimum point of
E
on
C
.

Let
v\in{\mathcal{K}}
be with
(v(0),v(T))\in D(j)
and
v\neq u_{h}
be arbitrarily chosen.
Using that, for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
it holds

j(x,y)-j(u_{h}(0),u_{h}(T))\geq\langle\phi(u_{h}^{\prime}(0)|x-u_{h}(0)\rangle-\langle\phi(u_{h}^{\prime}(T)|y-u_{h}(T)\rangle,

we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle A&:=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left[\Phi(v^{\prime})-\Phi(u_{h}^{\prime})\right]+J(v)-J(u_{h})\\
&\geq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\phi(u_{h}^{\prime})|v^{\prime}-u_{h}^{\prime}\rangle+\langle\phi(u_{h}^{\prime}(0))|v(0)-u_{h}(0)\rangle-\langle\phi(u_{h}^{\prime}(T))|v(T)-u_{h}(T)\rangle.\end{array}

On the other hand, as
u_{h}
solves the equation in
[{\mathcal{P}}_{\partial j}(h)]
, we have

-\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\left[\phi(u_{h}^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}|v-u_{h}\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{T}\langle u_{h}|v-u_{h}\rangle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle h|v-u_{h}\rangle

and integration by parts formula gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\langle\phi(u_{h}^{\prime})|v^{\prime}-u_{h}^{\prime}\rangle+\langle\phi(u_{h}^{\prime}(0))|v(0)-u_{h}(0)\rangle-\langle\phi(u_{h}^{\prime}(T))|v(T)-u_{h}(T)\rangle

=\!\!\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\langle h-u_{h}|v-u_{h}\rangle

Thus, we obtain

(3.3)

A\geq\int_{0}^{T}\langle h-u_{h}|\,v-u_{h}\rangle

and this implies that
u_{h}
is a solution of (
3.2
).
It remains to check that

E(v)>E(u_{h}).

From

E(v)-E(u_{h})=A+\frac{1}{2}\left(\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)-\int_{0}^{T}\langle h|v-u_{h}\rangle

and (
3.3
) it follows

E(v)-E(u_{h})\geq\frac{1}{2}\|v-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}>0

and the proof is complete.
∎

Further, having in view hypothesis
(H_{F})
, we introduce
{\mathcal{F}}:C\to\mathbb{R}
by setting

{\mathcal{F}}(v):=-\int_{0}^{T}F(t,v)dt\qquad(v\in C).

A standard reasoning (also see
[
19
, Remark 2.7]
) shows that
{\mathcal{F}}
is of class
C^{1}
on
C
and its derivative is given by

\langle{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla_{u}F(t,u)|v\rangle dt\qquad(u,v\in C).

Now,
the energy functional associated to problem
(
1.1
)-(
1.2
) will be the mapping
{\mathcal{E}}:C\to(-\infty,+\infty]
defined by

(3.4)

{\mathcal{E}}:=\Psi+J+{\mathcal{F}}.

This fits the structure required by Szulkin’s critical point theory
[
32
]
. Accordingly, an element
u\in D(\Psi+J)
is called
a critical point of

{\mathcal{E}}
in (
3.4
) if it satisfies

(3.5)

(\Psi+J)(v)-(\Psi+J)(u)+\langle{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}(u),v-u\rangle\geq 0,\quad\mbox{ for all }v\in D(\Psi+J).

Also, a sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset D(\Psi+J)
will be called a
(PS)-sequence
if
{\mathcal{E}}(u_{n})\to c\in\mathbb{R}
and

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left[\Phi(v^{\prime})-\Phi(u_{n}^{\prime})\right]+J(v)-J(u_{n})+\langle{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}(u_{n}),v-u_{n}\rangle

\geq-\varepsilon_{n}\|v-u_{n}\|_{\infty},\quad\mbox{ for all }v\in D(\Psi+J),

where
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
. The functional
{\mathcal{E}}
is said to satisfy the
(PS)-condition
if any (PS)-sequence has a convergent subsequence in
C
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Any critical point of
{\mathcal{E}}
is a solution of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Proof.

Let
u\in D(\Psi+J)
be a critical point of
{\mathcal{E}}
. By virtue of (
3.5
) it holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\left[\Phi(v^{\prime})-\Phi(u^{\prime})\right]+J(v)-J(u)+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\langle u-[u+\nabla_{u}F(t,u)]\,|v-u\rangle\geq 0,\quad\mbox{for all }v\in D(\Psi+J)

which is a variational inequality of type (
3.2
) with
h=u+\nabla_{u}F(t,u)
. From Proposition
3.1
we have that
u
solves
[{\mathcal{P}}_{\partial j}(u+\nabla_{u}F(t,u))]
, which actually means that it is a solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).
∎

4.
Minimum energy and saddle-point solutions

We introduce the first eigenvalue-like constant

(4.1)

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}(a,j):=\inf\left\{\frac{\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\,:\,v\in D(\Psi+J),\;v\not\equiv 0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\right\}.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
\lambda_{1}>0
then the following are true:

(i) for any
v\in D(\Psi+J)
one has

(4.2)

|v(t)|\leq a\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}+T\right)\qquad(t\in[0,T]);

(ii)
{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies the (PS)-condition;

(iii)
{\mathcal{E}}
is bounded from below and attains its infimum at some
u\in D(\Psi+J)
, which is a critical
point of
{\mathcal{E}}
and hence, a solution of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Proof.

(i)
Since
v\in{\mathcal{K}}
, it holds
\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\leq a\sqrt{T}
and by (
4.1
) we have
\|v\|_{L^{2}}\leq a\sqrt{T/\lambda_{1}}
.
Then (
4.2
) follows from (
2.17
).

(ii)
From (
4.2
) we infer that any
v\in D(\Psi+J)
satisfies

\|v\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq a\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}+T+1\right),

hence
D(\Psi+J)
is bounded in
W^{1,\infty}
, and therefore relatively compact in
C
– by the compactness of the embedding
W^{1,\infty}\hookrightarrow C
. This clearly implies that

{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies the (PS)-condition.

(iii)
As
F
is continuous on the compact set

has a critical point and then the conclusion will follow by Theorem
3.2
. With this aim, let us split
C=\mathbb{R}^{N}\oplus\widetilde{C}
, where
\widetilde{C}=\{u\in C\,:\,\overline{u}=0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\}.
Since for
u=x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, one has
J(x)=0
, from (
4.20
) it follows

(4.21)

{\mathcal{E}}(x)=-\int_{0}^{T}F(t,x)\,dt\to-\infty,\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty.

Since
\nabla_{u}F
is bounded, proceeding exactly as in the proof of Corollary

4.9
, we obtain the existence of a constant
k_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
with

{\mathcal{E}}(u)\geq k_{1}-\int_{0}^{T}F(t,\overline{u})\,dt\qquad(u\in D(\Psi+J)),

showing that

{\mathcal{E}}(u)\geq k_{1}-\int_{0}^{T}F(t,0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}})\,dt=:k_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\qquad(u\in D(\Psi+J)\cap\widetilde{C}).

From (
4.21
) there are constants
\rho>0
and
\alpha_{1}<k_{2}
so that

(4.22)

{\mathcal{E}}|_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,|x|=\rho\}}\leq\alpha_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathcal{E}}|_{\widetilde{C}}\geq k_{2}.

It remains to check that
{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies the (PS)-condition. Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset D(\Psi+J)
be a (PS)-sequence. First, observe that again by the boundedness of
\nabla_{u}F
we can infer (see (
4.18
)) that the sequence

\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\left[F(t,u_{n})-F(t,\overline{u}_{n})\right]\,dt\right\}

is bounded. Next, since
\{{\mathcal{E}}(u_{n})\}
and
\{J(u_{n})\}
are also bounded, from the writing

{\mathcal{E}}(u_{n})=\int_{0}^{T}\left[\Phi(u_{n}^{\prime})-\Phi(0)\right]+J(u_{n})-\int_{0}^{T}F(t,\overline{u}_{n})\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\left[F(t,u_{n})-F(t,\overline{u}_{n})\right]\,dt

it follows that there is a constant
k_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(4.23)

\int_{0}^{T}F(t,\overline{u}_{n})\,dt\leq k_{3}\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Then, by (
4.20
) the sequence
\{\overline{u}_{n}\}
is bounded and Proposition
4.11
ensures that
\{u_{n}\}
has a convergent subsequence in
C
. Consequently,
{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies the (PS)-condition and
the proof is complete.

∎

Theorem 4.15
.

If either

(4.24)

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}F(t,x)<\min\Phi-\Phi(0)\quad\mbox{ uniformly with }t\in[0,T],

or
j|_{d_{N}^{1}}\equiv 0

)-(

Proof.

If (
4.24
) holds true, then there are numbers
\varepsilon>0<\rho_{\varepsilon}
so that if
|x|>\rho_{\varepsilon}

F(t,x)<\min\Phi-\Phi(0)-\varepsilon\quad\mbox{ for all }t\in[0,T].

If
u\in D(\Psi)
is with
|\overline{u}|>\rho_{\varepsilon}+Ta\sqrt{N}
, from (
4.4
) it follows
|u(t)|>\rho_{\varepsilon}
and, hence

F(t,u(t))<\min\Phi-\Phi(0)-\varepsilon\quad\mbox{ for all }t\in[0,T].

Then, using the estimation (recall,

):

{\mathcal{E}}(u)\geq T\left(\min\Phi-\Phi(0)\right)-\int_{0}^{T}F(t,u(t))dt>T\varepsilon

and
{\mathcal{E}}(0)=0

4.4

\delta=\rho_{\varepsilon}+Ta\sqrt{N}.

For the remaining case, observe that (
4.25
) implies (
4.20

4.12

{\mathcal{E}}
has the geometry required by Saddle Point Theorem (see (
4.22

To see that
{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies (PS)-condition, let

\{|\overline{u}_{n}|\}

4.11

5.
Appendix

We claim that if
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
is such that
|z_{1}-z_{2}|=\sigma
, then

[formula]

N_{{\overline{D}}_{\sigma}}(z)=\left\{s(z_{1}-z_{2},z_{2}-z_{1})\,:\,s\geq 0\right\}.

Indeed, first observe that if
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\overline{D}}_{\sigma}
, then

\langle\!\langle(z_{1}-z_{2},z_{2}-z_{1})|x-z\rangle\!\rangle=\langle z_{1}-z_{2}|x_{1}-z_{1}\rangle+\langle z_{2}-z_{1}|x_{2}-z_{2}\rangle\leq 0,

means

\langle z_{1}-z_{2}|x_{1}-z_{1}-(x_{2}-z_{2})\rangle\leq 0,

or

\langle z_{1}-z_{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\leq\langle z_{1}-z_{2}|z_{1}-z_{2}\rangle=\sigma^{2},

which clearly is true, because

\langle z_{1}-z_{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\leq|z_{1}-z_{2}|\,|x_{1}-x_{2}|\leq\sigma^{2}.

Thus, we have

\langle\!\langle s(z_{1}-z_{2},z_{2}-z_{1})|x-z\rangle\!\rangle\leq 0\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in{\overline{D}}_{\sigma}\mbox{ and }s\geq 0,

meaning that
N_{{\overline{D}}_{\sigma}}(z)\supset\left\{s(z_{1}-z_{2},z_{2}-z_{1})\,:\,s\geq 0\right\}
and it remains to show the reverse inclusion. In this respect, since if
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in N_{{\overline{D}}_{\sigma}}(z)
, then

\langle\xi_{1}|x_{1}-z_{1}\rangle+\langle\xi_{2}|x_{2}-z_{2}\rangle=\langle\!\langle(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|x-z\rangle\!\rangle\leq 0\quad\mbox{ for all }x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\overline{D}}_{\sigma}

and taking
x_{j}=z_{j}+y
(
j=1,2
), it holds
\langle\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|y\rangle\leq 0
for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we obtain that necessarily
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=(\xi,-\xi)
with some
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying

[formula]

\langle\xi|x_{1}-z_{1}-x_{2}+z_{2}\rangle\leq 0\quad\mbox{ for all }x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\overline{D}}_{\sigma}.

If
\xi\neq 0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, taking
x_{1}=\xi|z_{1}-z_{2}|/|\xi|
and
x_{2}=0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
in
(A.2)
, we get

|\xi|\,|z_{1}-z_{2}|\leq\langle\xi|z_{1}-z_{2}\rangle,

which implies that equality holds in Cauchy-Schwartz inequality (i.e.
|\langle\xi|z_{1}-z_{2}\rangle|=|\xi|\,|z_{1}-z_{2}|
).
But this means that
\xi=\tau(z_{1}-z_{2})
with some
\tau\neq 0
and, using again
(A.2)
with
x_{1}=x_{2}=0_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, we infer that
\tau>0
. The proof of
(A.1)
is accomplished.

Partial Dyck path interpretation for three sequences in the Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences
Helmut Prodinger

Abstract
. Descents of odd length in Dyck paths are discussed, taking care of some variations. The approach is based on generating functions and the kernel method and augments relations about them from the Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences, that were pointed out by David Callan [ 1 ] .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
05A15

1.
Introduction

A Dyck path consists of (a sequence of) up-steps and down-steps of one unit each, such that it never goes below the
x
-axis and ends at the
x
-axis.
There are numerous entries of this popular concept in
[
3
]
. If the path does not end on the
x
-axis but can be completed to a Dyck path, we
speak about a partial Dyck path or the prefix of a Dyck path. A
descent
is a maximal sequence of contiguous down-steps; its length is measured in
terms of down-steps. We follow Callan
[
1
]
and interpret the sequences A101785, A113337, and A143017 in
[
3
]
; however we extend the analysis by considering partial versions of such paths. As a bonus, we discuss a further sequence of related interest.

The subclasses of Dyck paths are:

•

All descents have odd length,

•

All descents have odd length, but the last descent has even length,

•

All descents have odd length, but the last descent can have even or odd length.

Since we do not consider the empty path (of length zero), it always makes sense to speak about the “last descent.”

Our bonus is about descents of odd length if they do not end on the
x
-axis, but any descent reaching the
x
-axis can be even resp. odd (not only the final one).

2.
All descents have odd length

We use an (infinite) graph (automaton) to control the odd length(s) of the descents:

Figure 1.
Three layers of states, labelled
f,g,h
, in that order. The state
g_{0}
is responsible for allowed Dyck paths.

The recursions for the generating functions
f_{i}=f_{i}(z)
,
g_{i}=g_{i}(z)
,
h_{i}=h_{i}(z)
, can be read off from the graph in Fig.
1
, by considering the last step made;

\displaystyle f_{i+1}

\displaystyle=zf_{i}+zg_{i},\ i\geq 0,\ f_{0}=1,

\displaystyle g_{i}

\displaystyle=zf_{i+1}+zh_{i+1},\ i\geq 0,

\displaystyle h_{i}

\displaystyle=zg_{i+1},\ i\geq 0.

So the coefficient
[z^{n}]f_{i}
is the number of partial Dyck path under consideration that end in the
i
-th state of the first layer, and similarly for the other two.
Summing up the recursions we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}f_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}zf_{i}+\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}zg_{i},

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}g_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}zf_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}zh_{i+1},

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}h_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}zg_{i+1}

and with

F(u)=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}f_{i},\quad G(u)=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}g_{i},\quad H(u)=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}h_{i},

furthermore

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=1+uzF(u)+uzG(u),

\displaystyle uG(u)

\displaystyle=zF(u)-z+zH(z)-zh_{0},

\displaystyle uH(u)

\displaystyle=zG(u)-zg_{0}.

This system can be solved:

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{uz(uzh_{0}+z^{2}g_{0}-u^{2}+z^{2})}{u^{3}z+u^{2}z^{2}-u^{2}-z^{3}u+z^{2}},

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{u(-u^{2}z-u^{2}zh_{0}+uh_{0}-uz^{2}g_{0}+zg_{0})}{u^{3}z+u^{2}z^{2}-u^{2}-z^{3}u+z^{2}},

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{u(-g_{0}zu^{2}+g_{0}u-z^{2}h_{0}u-z^{2}g_{0}u-z^{2}u+zh_{0})}{u^{3}z+u^{2}z^{2}-u^{2}-z^{3}u+z^{2}}.

Plugging in
u=0
does not help, but the (joint) denominator may be factored:

u^{3}z+u^{2}z^{2}-u^{2}-z^{3}u+z^{2}=z(u-v_{1})(u-v_{2})(u-v_{3}).

Two of these factors are ‘bad,’ as the have no combinatorial relevance and, more importantly, no power series expansion around
(u,z)=(0,0)
.
This procedure is commonly known as the
kernel method
, see e.g.
[
2
]
. Thus,

(u-v_{2})(u-v_{3})
may be divided out from both, numerator and denominator, yielding

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{u}{u-v_{1}},

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{-z(1+h_{0})}{u-v_{1}},

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{-zg_{0}}{u-v_{1}}

and thus

g_{0}=\frac{zv_{1}}{-z^{2}+v_{1}^{2}},\quad h_{0}=\frac{z^{2}}{-z^{2}+v_{1}^{2}}=-\frac{v_{1}}{z}-1+\frac{1}{z^{2}}.

The relevant solution can be expanded by a Computer algebra system like Maple:

v_{1}=\frac{1}{z}-z-z^{5}-2z^{7}-4z^{9}-10z^{11}-26z^{13}-68z^{15}-183z^{17}-504z^{19}-1408z^{21}-\dots

As predicted in OEIS (and automatically established by Computer algebra)

g_{0}=\frac{z^{2}(1+g_{0})}{1-z^{4}(1+g_{0})^{2}}

and thus we get the algebraic equation (with
z^{2}=Z
)

-{Z}^{2}g_{0}^{3}-2{Z}^{2}g_{0}^{2}+{g_{0}}-{Z}^{2}{g_{0}}-Z{g_{0}}-Z=0.

Note that

g_{0}=Z+Z^{2}+2Z^{3}+5Z^{4}+12Z^{5}+30Z^{6}+79Z^{7}+213Z^{8}+584Z^{9}+1628Z^{10}+4600Z^{11}+\dots,

which is sequence A101785 in
[
3
]
.

Reading off the coefficient of
u^{j}
in
F(u)
,
G(u)
,
H(u)
gives us explicit expressions for
f_{j}
,
g_{j}
,
h_{j}
and thus all types of
partial
Dyck paths under consideration. We have
f_{0}=1
and for
j\geq 1

[u^{j}]F(u)=-[u^{j}]\frac{u}{u-v_{1}}=[u^{j-1}]\frac{1}{v_{1}(1-u/v_{1})}=v_{1}^{-j}.

Further,

[u^{j}]G(u)=[u^{j}]\frac{-z(1+h_{0})}{u-v_{1}}=[u^{j}]\frac{z(1+h_{0})}{v_{1}(1-u/v_{1})}=\frac{z(1+h_{0})}{v_{1}^{j+1}};

finally

[u^{j}]H(u)=[u^{j}]\frac{-zg_{0}}{u-v_{1}}=[u^{j}]\frac{zg_{0}}{v_{1}(1-u/v_{1})}=\frac{zg_{0}}{v_{1}^{j+1}}.

3.
All descents have odd length, but the last descent has even length

For this instance we don’t need new computations, as it is covered by
h_{0}
from the previous section:

h_{0}=Z^{2}+2Z^{3}+4Z^{4}+10Z^{5}+26Z^{6}+68Z^{7}+183Z^{8}+504Z^{9}+1408Z^{10}+3982Z^{11}+\dots

This is sequence A113337.

4.
All descents have odd length, but the last descent can have even or odd length

Again, no new computation is necessary, as we just have to consider
g_{0}+h_{0}
;

Z+2Z^{2}+4Z^{3}+9Z^{4}+22Z^{5}+56Z^{6}+147Z^{7}+396Z^{8}+1088Z^{9}+3036Z^{10}+8582Z^{11}+\dots,

which is sequence A143017.

5.
Bonus problem: descents of odd length when they do not touch the
x
-axis, otherwise no restriction

In this instance, we draw again (Figure
2
) a graph and notice the anomalies in the beginning (related to returns to the
x
-axis).
We use the same letters as before, but now with a slightly different meaning. As a first step, it is beneficial to ignore
f_{0}
,
g_{0}
,
h_{0}

for the time being, which leads us to Figure
3
.

Figure 2.
Three layers of states, labelled
f,g,h
, in that order.

Figure 3.
Three layers of states, labelled
f,g,h
, in that order.

It is beneficial to consider first the following generating functions,

F(u)=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i-1}f_{i},\quad G(u)=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i-1}g_{i},\quad H(u)=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i-1}h_{i}

following the recursions

\displaystyle f_{i+1}

\displaystyle=zf_{i}+zg_{i},\ i\geq 1,

\displaystyle g_{i}

\displaystyle=zf_{i+1}+zh_{i+1},\ i\geq 1,

\displaystyle h_{i}

\displaystyle=zg_{i+1},\ i\geq 1.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}f_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zf_{i}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zg_{i},

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}g_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zf_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zh_{i+1},

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}h_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zg_{i+1}

and

\displaystyle F(u)-f_{1}

\displaystyle=uzF(u)+uzG(u),

\displaystyle uG(u)

\displaystyle=zF(u)-zf_{1}+zH(u)-zh_{1},

\displaystyle uH(u)

\displaystyle=zG(u)-zg_{1}.

The system is almost the same as in previous sections except for the unspecified quantity
f_{1}
which will be fixed later;

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=f_{1}+\frac{uz(-f_{1}u^{2}+zh_{1}u+z^{2}g_{1}+z^{2}f_{1})}{u^{3}z+u^{2}z^{2}-u^{2}-z^{3}u+z^{2}},

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{z(uh_{1}+zg_{1}-u^{2}zf_{1}-u^{2}zh_{1}-uz^{2}g_{1})}{u^{3}z+u^{2}z^{2}-u^{2}-z^{3}u+z^{2}},

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{z(zh_{1}-uz^{2}f_{1}-uz^{2}h_{1}-uz^{2}g_{1}+g_{1}u-g_{1}u^{2}z)}{u^{3}z+u^{2}z^{2}-u^{2}-z^{3}u+z^{2}}.

The denominator is the same as before, and dividing out
(u-v_{2})(u-v_{3})
leads to

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=f_{1}-\frac{uf_{1}}{u-v_{1}},

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{-z(f_{1}+h_{1})}{u-v_{1}},

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{-zg_{1}}{u-v_{1}}.

Consequently, setting
u=0
,

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{zf_{1}v_{1}}{-z^{2}+v_{1}^{2}},\quad h_{1}=\frac{z^{2}f_{1}}{-z^{2}+v_{1}^{2}}.

Now, bringing the initial layers into the game, we have

f_{1}=z+zg_{0},\quad g_{0}=z(f_{1}+g_{1}+h_{1}),

and therefore the quantity of interest

g_{0}=\frac{z^{2}v_{1}}{v_{1}(1-z^{2})-z}.

The series expansion is

g_{0}=Z+2Z^{2}+5Z^{3}+13Z^{4}+35Z^{5}+97Z^{6}+274Z^{7}+785Z^{8}+2275Z^{9}+6655Z^{10}+19618Z^{11}+\dots

The series
g_{0}
satisfies a cubic equation, and all functions
[u^{j}]F(u)
,
[u^{j}]G(u)
,
[u^{j}]H(u)
could be computed as well.

Obligatory hypergraphs
Christian Reiher

Abstract
. Erdős and Hajnal proved that every graph of uncountable chromatic number contains arbitrarily large finite, complete, bipartite graphs. We extend this result to hypergraphs.

1.
Introduction

For an integer
k\geq 2
a
k
-uniform hypergraph
is a pair
H=(V,E)
consisting
of a (finite or infinite) set of
vertices

V
and a
set
E\subseteq V^{(k)}=\{e\subseteq V\colon|e|=k\}
of
k
-element subsets
of
V
, called the
edges
of
H
.
The
chromatic number
of a hypergraph
H=(V,E)
, denoted by
\chi(H)
, is the
least cardinal
\kappa
for which there exists a colouring
f\colon V\longrightarrow\kappa
such
that no edge of
H
is monochromatic with respect to
f
. A finite hypergraph
F
is
said to be
obligatory
if every hypergraph
H
with
\chi(H)\geq\aleph_{1}
has
a subhypergraph isomorphic to
F
.
Erdős and Hajnal
[
3
]
*Corollary 5.6 and Theorem 7.4 obtained the
following characterisation of obligatory graphs.

Theorem 1.1

(Erdős and Hajnal)
.

A finite graph is obligatory if and only if it is bipartite. ∎

For general hypergraphs no comparable result is known, and no plausible conjecture
has ever been proposed. The earliest contribution to this area we are aware
of is due to Erdős, Hajnal, and Rothschild
[
2
]
, who showed that
hypergraphs with two edges intersecting in two or more vertices are non-obligatory.
A systemstic study of the
3
-uniform case was initiated by
Erdős, Galvin, and Hajnal
[
1
]
, and later continued by
Komjáth
[
5
,
6
,
4
]
. Many results from
[
5
]
can be
extended in a straightforward manner to
k
-uniform hypergraphs. For instance,
every obligatory
k
-uniform hypergraph is
k
-partite, and the class of obligatory,

k
-uniform hypergraphs is closed under taking disjoint unions and one-point
amalgamations. In particular, every finite forest is obligatory, i.e., every finite
hypergraph
F
whose set of edges admits an enumeration
E(F)=\{e_{0},\dots,e_{n-1}\}

such that
\big|e_{i}\cap\bigcup_{j<i}e_{j}\big|\leq 1
holds for every positive
i<n
.
To the best of our knowledge, however, it has never been shown that for any
k\geq 3

there exists an obligatory,
k
-uniform hypergraph which fails to be a forest.

The goal of this article is to describe a natural class of obligatory hypergraphs
that generalise bipartite graphs. Given an arbitrary graph
F
and an integer
k

the
expansion

F^{(k)}
is defined to be the
k
-uniform hypergraph derived
from
F
by adding
k-2
new vertices to every edge; thus it has
|V(F)|+(k-2)|E(F)|

vertices and there is a bijection
\varphi\colon E(F)\longrightarrow E(F^{(k)})
such that

e\subseteq\varphi(e)
and
e\cap e^{\prime}=\varphi(e)\cap\varphi(e^{\prime})
hold for all distinct

e,e^{\prime}\in E(F)
. Hypergraph expansions were introduced about twenty years ago by
Mubayi in his work on the hypergraph Turán problem
[
7
]
.

In the result that follows,

Theorem 1.2

For all integers

In particular, for every even

\ell

2.
The proof

We shall exploit the following submultiplicativity property of the chromatic number,
which was observed for finite graphs by Zykov
[
9
]
* Teorema 2.
His product argument establishes the general case as well.

Fact 2.1
.

Let
H=(V,E)
be a hypergraph. If
E=\bigcup_{i\in I}E_{i}
, then

\chi(H)\leq\prod_{i\in I}\chi(V,E_{i})\,.\qed

Often we use the following consequence.

Corollary 2.2
.

If
H=(V,E)
denotes a hypergraph of uncountable chromatic number and

E^{\prime}\subseteq E
satisfies
\chi(V,E^{\prime})\leq\aleph_{0}
, then

\chi(V,E\smallsetminus E^{\prime})\geq\aleph_{1}
. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Arguing indirectly, we fix the smallest integer
k
such that for some positive
integer
n
the expansion
K^{(k)}_{n,n}
is non-obligatory. Theorem
1.1

tells us that
k\geq 3
. Let
\kappa
be the least
cardinal such that there exists a
K^{(k)}_{n,n}
-free,
k
-uniform hypergraph

H=(\kappa,E)
whose chromatic number is uncountable.

Set
t=kn^{2}+1
.
By a
delta system
we shall mean a subset
D\subseteq E
of size
|D|=t

such that setting
d=\bigcap D
we have
e\cap e^{\prime}=d
for all distinct
e,e^{\prime}\in D
;
the set
d
is also called the
root
of the delta system
D
.
For every
\ell\in[2,k)
we define
H^{(\ell)}=(\kappa,E^{(\ell)})
to be the

\ell

\ell
-element sets which are roots
of such delta systems.

Claim 2.3
.

For every
\ell\in[2,k)
we have
\chi(H^{(\ell)})\leq\aleph_{0}
.

Proof.

Assume contrariwise that
\chi(H^{(\ell)})\geq\aleph_{1}
holds for
some
\ell\in[2,k)
. Due to the minimality of
k
the expansion

K^{(\ell)}_{n,n}
is obligatory and, hence, there is a copy
of
K^{(\ell)}_{n,n}
in
H^{(\ell)}
. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{n^{2}}
be
the edges of such a copy. We want to extend these edges
recursively to edges
e_{i}\supseteq f_{i}
of
H
such that
e_{1},\dots,e_{n^{2}}

form a copy of
K^{(k)}_{n,n}
.

Suppose that for some positive
i\leq n^{2}
the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{i-1}

have already been selected. Recall that
f_{i}
is the root of a delta system.
Since the set

g_{i}=(e_{1}\cup\dots\cup e_{i-1})\cup(f_{i}\cup\dots\cup f_{n^{2}})

satisfies
|g_{i}|\leq kn^{2}<t
, this yields an edge
e_{i}\in E
such
that
f_{i}\subseteq e_{i}
and
g_{i}\cap e_{i}=f_{i}
. This completes the
selection of
e_{1},\dots,e_{n^{2}}\in E
.

Whenever
1\leq i<j\leq n^{2}
we have
e_{i}\subseteq g_{j}
and
f_{j}\subseteq g_{i}
,
wherefore

e_{i}\cap e_{j}=e_{i}\cap g_{j}\cap e_{j}=e_{i}\cap f_{j}=e_{i}\cap g_{i}\cap e_{j}=f_{i}\cap f_{j}\,.

Thus the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{n^{2}}
form indeed a copy
of
K^{(k)}_{n,n}
in
H
, which is absurd.
∎

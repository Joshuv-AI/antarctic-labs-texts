Jets of foliations and b k b^{k} -algebroids
Francis Bischoff, Álvaro del Pino and Aldo Witte

Abstract
. In this article, we introduce and study singular foliations of b k b^{k} type. These singular foliations formalize the properties of vector fields that are tangent to order k k along a submanifold W ⊂ M W\subset M . Our first result is a classification of these foliations, relating them to geometric structures defined in a formal neighborhood of the submanifold, such as jets of distributions that are involutive up to order k − 1 k-1 . When W W is a hypersurface, singular foliations of b k b^{k} type are Lie algebroids. In this particular case, they are generalizations of the b k b^{k} -tangent bundles introduced by Scott. Indeed, they are always locally isomorphic to b k b^{k} -tangent bundles, but globally such an isomorphism is obstructed by a holonomy invariant. Our second main result is a Riemann-Hilbert-style classification of singular foliations of b k b^{k} type in terms of holonomy representations. In this paper, we study singular foliations of b k b^{k} type from several different perspectives. In particular: (1) We study the problem of extending a k k th-order foliation to a ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -th order foliation and prove that this is obstructed by a characteristic class. (2) When W W is a hypersurface, we give a detailed study of algebroid differential forms and extend Scott’s calculation of the cohomology. (3) We study algebroid symplectic forms in terms of the geometric structures induced on W W . In particular, we find that there is a close relationship between the above obstruction class for extensions and the symplectic variation of the symplectic foliation induced on W W .

Introduction

Let
D

[

The

-tangent bundle is a central example in the theory of Lie algebroids, due both to its simplicity and its usefulness in studying singular geometric structures in a wide variety of settings; see for example

,

,

,

]

Lie algebroids can be seen as particularly nice examples of singular foliations. Singular foliations appear in many different situations, such as the orbits of Lie groups, the symplectic foliation underlying a Poisson manifold, and within geometric mechanics. The study of singular foliations has received renewed attention within differential geometry following the seminal work of Androulidakis and Skandalis

A crucial class of Lie algebroids for us are the

In the literature, the

1.1.
Hypersurface Lie algebroids

In order to understand
b^{k+1}
-tangent bundles better, we abstract their properties:

1.1
.

Fix a manifold
M^{n}
of dimension
n
and a hypersurface
D\subset M
. A Lie algebroid
\rho:{\mathcal{A}}^{n}\rightarrow M^{n}
of rank

is of

D
and the components of the complement

This definition can equivalently be stated as the condition that
\rho
is an isomorphism on the complement of
D
and has rank
n-1
over
D
. Given
\rho
, we can consider its determinant
\det(\rho)
as a section of
\Gamma(\det(A^{*})\otimes\det(TM))
. The hypersurface assumption implies that
\det(\rho)
vanishes precisely along
D
.

1.2
.

A hypersurface Lie algebroid
A\rightarrow M
is of
b^{k+1}
-type
if
\det(\rho)
vanishes to order
k+1<\infty
along
D
.

Do note that, more generally, we could consider algebroids that have a different order of vanishing in each component of
D
. However, we will henceforth focus on the case in which
D
is connected, since all interesting behaviours are already present in this setting.

It is immediate that
b^{k+1}
-tangent bundles are Lie algebroids of
b^{k+1}
-type. The question that kick-started the present paper is the converse:
Is every Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type a
b^{k+1}
-tangent bundle?

Locally
, the answer is
yes
:

1.3
.

Let
A\rightarrow M
be a Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type. Then for any
x\in D
there are coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
around
D
such that

(1.1)

\Gamma({\mathcal{A}})\simeq\langle x_{1}^{k+1}\partial_{x_{1}},\partial_{x_{2}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}}\rangle.

But
globally
, the answer turns out to be
no
. The goal of this article is to formalise these claims. We dedicate the rest of the introduction to stating and discussing our main results.

1.2.
k
-th order foliations

To understand Lie algebroids of
b^{k+1}
-type we have to introduce the notion of
k
-th order foliation.
These can be thought of as germs of distributions along
D
, which are
involutive up to order
k
. We will show that:

1.4
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between Lie algebroids of
b^{k+1}
-type and
k
-th order foliations.

The Lie algebroids of Scott fit into this framework by letting the
k
-th order foliation

be induced by the foliation of level sets of a defining function around

. However, general

-th order foliations might have holonomy around

b^{k+1}
-type from being globally isomorphic to
b^{k+1}
-tangent bundles.

k
-th order foliations are interesting objects in their own right, and there is no reason to restrict ourselves to jets along a hypersurface
D

W\subset M
. This forces us to leave the realm of Lie algebroids and work with the following more general family of singular foliations:

1.5
.

Fix a manifold
M
and a submanifold
W
. A singular foliation
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathfrak{X}^{1}(M)
is of
type
b^{k+1}
if its leaves are the components of
W
and the components of the complement and, moreover, around every point of
W
,
\mathcal{F}
is isomorphic to the following local model

\langle x^{I}\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,x^{I}\partial_{x_{l}},\partial_{x_{l+1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}}\rangle,

with
x^{I}=x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{l}^{i_{l}}
with
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{l})
running over all multi-indices of length
k+1
.

Then:

Proposition
3.23
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between singular foliations of
b^{k+1}
-type and
k
-th order foliations.

The correspondence goes roughly as follows. Given a
k
-th order foliation
\sigma
along a submanifold
W
, we can consider a germ of distribution
\xi
along
W
representing it. To it, we can associate the module of vector fields which are tangent to it up to order
k
:

{\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(M,\xi)=\mathcal{I}_{W}^{k+1}\mathfrak{X}(M)+\Gamma(\xi),

where
\mathcal{I}_{W}^{k+1}
is the ideal of functions vanishing to order
k+1
along
W
and

denotes the
C^{\infty}(M)
-span of the union. This module is a singular foliation and locally of the form described above.

We will see that there are several equivalent ways of describing
k
-th order foliations, each with their own merits. The first comes from adopting the algebraic viewpoint and considering a
k
-th order neighbourhood
\mathcal{N}^{k}_{W}
of
W
. One may think of this as a ringed space whose structure sheaf of functions consists of
k
-jets of polynomials in the normal directions of
W
. One may then make sense of
foliations
on
{\rm Spec}(\mathcal{N}^{k}_{W})
.

The second alternative approach comes from the following observation: the singular foliation has a preferred local model and can thus, locally, be described by a defining function. These local defining functions can be combined into a
subsheaf of invariant functions

\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{N}^{k}_{W}
.

The final alternative approach comes from observing that, although a singular foliation of
type
b^{k+1}
is not naturally a Lie algebroid, there is nevertheless a natural Lie algebroid that one can define once a choice of tubular neighbourhood has been fixed. This is given by the
k
-th order Atiyah algebroid of the normal bundle
\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
, defined to be the algebroid of
k
-jets of projectable vector fields on the total space of
\nu_{W}
which are tangent to the zero section. It turns out that a singular foliation
\mathcal{F}
of type
b^{k+1}
is completely encoded by the intersection
\mathcal{F}\cap\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
, which is in turn equivalent to a Lie algebroid morphism
\sigma:TW\to\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
.

The first main result of this article is to show that all these approaches are equivalent:

Theorem
3.29
and Proposition
4.3
.

Let
M^{n}
be a manifold together with a connected embedded submanifold
W^{n-l}
of codimension
l
. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between:

(1)

Singular foliations of type
b^{k+1}
,
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

(2)

k
-th order foliations
\sigma\in\Hol^{k}_{W}(\operatorname{Gr}(TM,l))
.

(3)

Foliations on the
k
-th order neighbrouhood of
W
,
F\subset\Der(\mathcal{N}_{W}^{k})
.

(4)

Subsheaves of invariant functions
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{N}^{k}
.

(5)

And, after fixing a tubular neighbourhood embedding, flat connections
\sigma:TW\rightarrow\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
.

1.3.
Holonomy, Frobenius and Riemann-Hilbert

We will give a precise notion of
holonomy
for
k
-th order foliations, using the Lie algebroid
\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
of
k
-jets of vector fields along
W
. We view a
k
-th order foliation as a Lie algebroid morphism
\sigma:TW\rightarrow\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
. By invoking Lie’s second theorem for Lie algebroid morphisms we obtain a groupoid morphism
\Sigma:\Pi_{1}(W)\rightarrow\mathcal{G}({\operatorname{Fr}}^{k}(\nu_{W}))
. By restricting
\Sigma
to
x\in W
, and choosing a framing for
\nu_{W,x}
we obtain a representation

{\operatorname{hol}}_{\sigma,x}:\pi_{1}(W,x)\rightarrow J^{k}\operatorname{Diff}(\mathbb{R}^{l},0)

which is the holonomy of
\sigma
. Here
J^{k}\operatorname{Diff}(\mathbb{R}^{l},0)
denotes the group of
k
-jets at 0 of diffeomorphisms of
\mathbb{R}^{l}
, which fix the origin.

Observe that
\At^{1}(\nu_{W})=\At(\nu_{W})
, the Atiyah algebroid of the normal bundle of
W
. Hence a
1
-th order foliation is simply a flat linear connection on
\nu_{W}
. In this case, the holonomy as defined above is precisely the holonomy of the connection.

The classical Riemann-Hilbert correspondence states that every linear representation of
\pi_{1}(W,x)
corresponds to a flat vector bundle. We will show a higher order version of this result:

Theorem
5.4
.

The association
\sigma\mapsto{\operatorname{hol}}_{\sigma,x}
induces an equivalence between:

•

k
-th order foliations, up to isomorphism

•

conjugacy classes of representions, i.e. points of
{\rm Hom}(\pi_{1}(W,x),J^{k}\operatorname{Diff}(\mathbb{R}^{l},0))/J^{k}\operatorname{Diff}(\mathbb{R}^{l},0)

In this theorem, two
k
-th order foliations
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
are isomorphic if there exists a germ of diffeomorphism
\varphi:\nu_{W}\rightarrow\nu_{W}
which is the identity on
W
and satisfies
\varphi^{*}\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
.

The existence of a holonomy representation for
k
-th order foliations also implies the following:

Proposition
4.5
.

Let
W
be simply connected, and
\sigma
a
k
-th order foliation. Then, there exists a germ of foliation
\mathcal{F}
around
W
with
J^{k}\mathcal{F}=\sigma
.

In particular, this allows us to show that every
k
-th order foliation is locally represented by an actual foliation. Using this we immediately obtain a Frobenius theorem result for
k
-th order foliations (Theorem
4.6
).

1.4.
The extension problem

Although every
k
-th order foliation around a symply connected submanifold is representable by an actual foliation, this is not true in general. In Section
6
we study the following question:

1.1
.

Let
\sigma
be a
k
-jet of a foliation. When does there exist a
(k+1)
-th order foliation
\tilde{\sigma}
with the same
k
-jet as
\sigma
?

We will solve this problem by making use of the theory of Lie algebroid extensions. We will show that:

Proposition
6.5
.

A
k
-th order foliation
\sigma
may be extended to a
(k+1)
-th order foliation if and only if a certain characteristic class
c(\sigma)\in H^{2}(D;\nu_{W}^{-k})
vanishes.

Secondly we will make use of the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence to show:

Theorem
6.7
.

A
k
-th order foliation
\sigma
may be extended to a
(k+1)
-th order foliation if and only if a certain class in
H^{2}(\pi_{1}(D,x);J^{k}\operatorname{Diff}(\mathbb{R}^{l},0))
vanishes.

The two approaches will be linked using the Van-Est isomorphism and Morita equivalence of the integrating groupoids.

1.5.
Maurer-Cartan equation

A
k
-th order foliation always induces a
1
-th order foliation, and consequently a flat connection
\nabla
on
\nu_{W}
. One way of thinking about the
k
-th order foliation is as a deformation of this flat connection. This can be made precise as follows:

Proposition
4.9
.

Let
E\rightarrow W
be a vector bundle with flat linear connection
\nabla
. Then,
k
-th order foliations correspond to tuples
(\eta_{0},\ldots,\eta_{k-1})
with
\eta_{i}\in\Omega^{1}(W;\Sym^{i}E^{*}\otimes\operatorname{End}(E))
satisfying the Maurer-Cartan equation

d^{\nabla}\eta_{r}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=r}[\eta_{i},\eta_{j}]

Here
\Sym^{i}E^{*}\otimes\operatorname{End}(E)\subset\At^{k}(E)
is identified with the vertical vector fields of degree
i
. The corresponding bracket is the one induced from the bracket on
\At^{k}(E)
.

1.6.
Cohomology and symplectic structures

We now revert back to the case where
W=D
is codimension one. We can use the description of a
k
-th order foliation as a Maurer-Cartan element to endow
\Omega^{\bullet-1}(D;\bigoplus_{i=0}^{k}\nu_{D}^{i})
with a differential. This allows us to compute the cohomology of Lie algebroids of
b^{k+1}
-type:

Corollary
7.8
.

Let
A
be a Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type. Then there exists an isomorphism

H^{\bullet}(A)\simeq H^{\bullet}(M)\oplus H^{\bullet-1}(D;\bigoplus_{i=0}^{k}\nu_{D}^{i}),

where the cohomology of the right-most entry is obtained from a particular
TD
connection on
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{k}\nu_{D}^{i}
induced by the
k
-th order foliation.

As a special case, this recovers Scott’s calculation of the cohomology of
b^{k+1}
-tangent bundles. Indeed, in this case the normal bundle
\nu_{D}
is trivial, and the right hand side reduced to a direct sum of
k+1
copies of
H^{\bullet-1}(D)
. For general Lie algebroids this cohomology is much more intricate. We show in Proposition
7.11
that there exists a spectral sequence computing this cohomology whose
E_{1}
-page is given by the cohomology of the local systems
\nu_{D}^{i}
induced by the flat connection on the normal bundle. Furthermore, in Proposition
7.10
we give a detailed description of the structure of
(\Omega_{A}^{\bullet},d)
as a commutative differential graded algebra in the case that
M
is a tubular neighbourhood of
D
. Interestingly, as we explain in Section
7.6
, this algebra structure is controlled by a universal cdga related to the Lie algebra of
k
-jets of vector fields on
\mathbb{R}
.

1.7.
Symplectic structures

We can now use the description of the Lie algebroid cohomology to study symplectic structures on Lie algebroids of
b^{k+1}
-type. We will provide ample examples of such structures, and present a normal form result around
D
. A
b^{k+1}
-symplectic structure induces a symplectic foliation on
D
. In contrast to the case of
b^{k+1}
-tangent bundles, this symplectic foliation can have non-trivial symplectic variation.

Proposition
2.8
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{A}})
be a
b^{k+1}
-symplectic structure. Then the symplectic foliation on
D
has symplectic variation given by (the restriction of) the extension class
c(\sigma)\in H^{2}(D;\nu_{D}^{-k})
from Proposition

.

Structure of the article

Section

is a completely self-contained section, summarizing the results of this article applicable to the theory of Lie algebroids and providing concrete examples. It is aimed at the reader interested in the applications to the study of singular symplectic structures. Indeed, in this section we describe the geometric structures induced by an algebroid symplectic form on the submanifold

and prove normal form theorems.

In Section

we set the stage by fixing the terminology regarding holonomic jets of subbundles. This will be used to define the notion of a

-th order neighbourhoods, sheaves of invariant functions and show that all these objects are equivalent to singular foliations of type

.

In Section

-jets of vector fields. We will use this to identify

In Section

There is an unfortunate clash of terminology between the theory of

-tangent bundles and jets of vector fields. Informally, Scott states in

]
that the

-tangent bundle consists of “vector fields

This is however inconsistent with the point of view of jets: A vector field

and let
\At^{k}(L)
be the extended Atiyah algebroid

\At^{k}(L)=\At(L)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k-1}L^{-i}.

This contains the subalgebroid
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{k-1}L^{-i}
, which is a bundle of Lie algebras whose bracket is given by

[-,-]:L^{-i}\times L^{-j}\rightarrow L^{-i-j},\quad[s,t]=(j-i)s\otimes t.

2.1
.

A
b^{k+1}
-algebroid
A
on the total space of
L
is equivalent to the data of a bracket preserving splitting
\nabla+\sigma:TD\to\At^{k}(L)
of the anchor map of
\At^{k}(L)
. This decomposes into the data of

(1)

A bracket preserving splitting
\nabla:TD\to\At(L)
corresponding to a flat connection on
L
.

(2)

An element
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\eta_{i}\in\Omega_{D}^{1}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k-1}L^{-i})
satisfying the following Maurer-Cartan equation:

(2.2)

d^{\nabla}\eta_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=r}(j-i)\eta_{i}\wedge\eta_{j}=0,

for
r=1,...,k-1
.

This result will be proven in Proposition
4.9
. In particular, we will see that a
b^{k+1}
-algebroid for
k\geq 1
always induces a linear connection on the normal bundle to
D
. In terms of the above proposition this is the connection
\nabla
.

Given a Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type
A_{k}
, we see that we can truncate
(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k-1})
to a Maurer-Cartan element
(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{i-1})
. Therefore, we see that we obtain a sequence of Lie algebroids of
b^{i+1}
-type:

(2.3)

A_{k}\rightarrow A_{k-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow A_{0}=T(-\log D).

In fact, we will see below in Proposition
6.6
that the Lie algebroid
A_{k}
may be described as a canonical elementary modification of
A_{k-1}
at
W
.

The sequence of Lie algebroids leads us to the following problem:

2.1
The extension problem.

Given a Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type
A_{k}\rightarrow M
, does there exist a Lie algebroid of
b^{k+2}
-type
A_{k+1}\rightarrow M
for which the induced Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type is
A_{k}
?

When this is the case we will say that
A_{k+1}

extends

A_{k}
. In terms of the
\eta_{i}
, we see that the extension problem boils down to finding
\eta_{k}\in\Omega^{1}(D;L^{-k})
such that Equation
2.2
is satisfied. Finding this may be addressed purely in cohomological terms:

2.2
.

Let
A_{k}\rightarrow M
be a Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type, with corresponding
(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{i-1})
. Then there exists a Lie algebroid of type
b^{k+2}
extending it if and only if the cohomology class

c=\left[\sum_{i<k/2}(k-2i)\eta_{i}\wedge\eta_{k-i}\right]\in H^{2}(D;L^{-k})

vanishes.

This cohomology class will be studied in detail in Section
6
. In particular, we will see that it coincides with the extension class of the sequence

0\to L^{-k}\to A_{k}|_{D}\to TD\to 0.

2.3
.

Let
S
be a genus
g
Riemann surface. Its de Rham cohomology ring is given by

H^{\bullet}(S)\cong\mathbb{R}[\alpha_{i},\beta_{i},\omega;\ i=1,...,g]/(\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}=\beta_{i}\beta_{j}=0,\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}=\delta_{ij}\omega),

where the degrees are
|\alpha_{i}|=|\beta_{i}|=1
and
|\omega|=2
. Choose representatives for the degree
1
classes. Abusing notation, we denote these closed
1
-forms by
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
. Consider the trivial line bundle
L=S\times\mathbb{R}
with trivial connection. Let
t
be a coordinate on
\mathbb{R}
. By Proposition
2.1
, a
b^{4}
-algebroid can be specified by the data of two closed
1
-forms:

\eta_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{g}(x_{i}\alpha_{i}+y_{i}\beta_{i}),\qquad\eta_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{g}(w_{i}\alpha_{i}+z_{i}\beta_{i}),

where
(x_{i},y_{i},w_{i},z_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{4g}
. The associated
b^{4}
-algebroid
A_{3}
is the subsheaf generated by the following vector fields on the total space of
L
:

X+\eta_{1}(X)t^{2}\partial_{t}+\eta_{2}(X)t^{3}\partial_{t},\qquad t^{4}\partial_{t},

where
X\in\mathfrak{X}(S)
are vector fields on
S
which can be lifted to horizontal vector fields on
L=S\times\mathbb{R}
. This algebroid can be extended to a
b^{5}
-algebroid if and only if the cohomology class
c
from Theorem
2.2
vanishes. This cohomology class is given by

c(x_{i},y_{i},w_{i},z_{i})=\eta_{1}\wedge\eta_{2}=\sum_{i,j}(x_{i}\alpha_{i}+y_{i}\beta_{i})(w_{j}\alpha_{j}+z_{j}\beta_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{g}(x_{i}z_{i}-y_{i}w_{i})\omega.

2.4
.

Let
H(\mathbb{R})
be the Heisenberg group, which is the subgroup of real valued
3\times 3
upper-triangular matrices with
1
’s on the diagonal. Let
H(\mathbb{Z})
be the subgroup with integer entries. Let
X=H(\mathbb{R})/H(\mathbb{Z})
, which is a compact
3
-dimensional manifold. Writing an upper triangular matrix as

\begin{pmatrix}1&x&z\\
0&1&y\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}

we get local coordinates
(x,y,z)
on
X
. The
1
-forms
a=dx,b=dy
and
c=dz-ydx
are well-defined on
X
and generate the cohomology. Furthermore,
dc=a\wedge b
. See Example
7.5.1
for more details on the cohomology. Let
L=X\times\mathbb{R}
be the trivial line bundle and equip it with the trivial flat connection. By Proposition
2.1
, a
b^{5}
-algebroid
A_{4}
on
L
can be specified by the data of three
1
-forms which satisfy the Maurer-Cartan equation. Such a solution is provided by

\eta_{1}=a,\qquad\eta_{2}=b,\qquad\eta_{3}=-c.

The cohomology class of Theorem
2.2
is given by
-2a\wedge c
, which is non-trivial in cohomology. Therefore
A_{4}
cannot be extended to a
b^{6}
-algebroid. Let
t
be a linear coordinate on
\mathbb{R}
, so that
(x,y,z,t)
define coordinates on
X
. Then
A_{4}
has a global basis given in coordinates by

X_{1}=\partial_{x}+y\partial_{z}+t^{2}\partial_{t},\ \ X_{2}=\partial_{y}+t^{3}\partial_{t},\ \ X_{3}=\partial_{z}-t^{4}\partial_{t},\ \ X_{4}=t^{5}\partial_{t}.

2.3.
Cohomology

Let
A
be a
b^{k+1}
-algebroid on the total space of a line bundle
L\rightarrow M
, and let
(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k-1})
be the corresponding Maurer-Cartan element. Define
S_{k}(L)=\oplus_{i=0}^{k}L^{i}
. In Section
7
we will see that we may endow
S_{k}(L)
with a natural flat
TD
-connection induced by the
b^{k+1}
-algebroid. In terms of the Maurer-Cartan element the action of this connection on a section
t_{r}\in\Gamma(L^{r})
is given by:

(2.4)

d(t_{r})=d^{\nabla}t_{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}(i-r)\eta_{i}\otimes t_{r},

where
\eta_{i}\otimes t_{r}\in\Omega_{D}^{1}(L^{r-i})
.

Let
\Omega^{\bullet-1}(D;S_{k}(L))
be the cochain complex endowed with the differential induced by this connection:

Lemma
7.6
.

Let
A\rightarrow L
be a Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type over a line bundle
L\rightarrow D
. Then there exists
\tau_{i}\in\Omega^{1}(A;L^{-i})
, for
0\leq i\leq k
, such that the following morphism is an isomorphism cochain complexes

\tau:\Omega^{\bullet}_{L}\oplus\Omega^{\bullet-1}_{D}(S_{k}(L))\to\Omega^{\bullet}(A),\qquad(\beta,\sum_{r=0}^{k}\phi_{r}\otimes u^{(r)})\mapsto\beta+\sum_{r=0}^{k}\phi_{r}\wedge\tau_{r}(u^{(r)}).

We will also sometimes write
(\phi_{r}\otimes u^{(r)})\wedge\tau_{r}
for
\phi_{r}\wedge\tau_{r}(u^{(r)})
.

This isomorphism
\tau
may also be upgraded to an isomorphism of algebras by introducing an algebra structure on
\Omega_{D}^{\bullet-1}(S_{k}(L))
, which is the content of Proposition
7.10
. In Section
7.5.1
, an example of this algebra for a
b^{5}
-algebroid is computed in detail.

We will see that we can use Lemma
7.6
to compute the cohomology of Lie algebroids of
b^{k+1}
-type on general manifolds:

Proposition
7.14
.

Let
A\rightarrow M
be a Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type. Then there exist isomorphisms

H^{i}(A)\cong H^{i}(M)\oplus H^{i-1}(D,S_{k}(L)),

for all
i
.

2.4.
b^{k+1}
-symplectic structures

Now that we understand the cohomology of the Lie algebroids of
b^{k+1}
-type, we can study symplectic structures on them. The first step is to find out what structure is induced on the hypersurface
D
. As our study is completely local around
D
, we will make use of a tubular neighbourhood embedding and assume that
M=L=\nu_{D}
for the remainder of this section.

Using the isomorphism
\tau
in Lemma
7.6
we immediately obtain that a
b^{k+1}
-symplectic form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(A)
decomposes into a smooth part
\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{L}
and a singular part
\alpha\in\Omega_{D}^{1}\otimes(L^{0}\oplus...\oplus L^{k})
. Explicitly:

2.5
.

Let
A\rightarrow L
be a Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type. Then the data of a closed two-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(A)
is equivalent to the data:

•

\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{L}
, with
d\beta=0
,

•

\alpha\in\Omega_{D}^{1}\otimes(L^{0}\oplus...\oplus L^{k})
, satisfying

d^{\nabla}\alpha_{p}=p\sum_{r=p+1}^{k}\eta_{r-p}\wedge\alpha_{r},

for all
0\leq p\leq k
. This is an iterative system of equations starting with

d^{\nabla}\alpha_{k}=0,\ \ d^{\nabla}\alpha_{k-1}=(k-1)\eta_{1}\wedge\alpha_{k},\ \ d^{\nabla}\alpha_{k-2}=(k-2)\big(\eta_{1}\wedge\alpha_{k-1}+\eta_{2}\wedge\alpha_{k}\big),\ \ ...

Now let
\omega
be a closed algebroid
2
-form. In order for
\omega
to be non-degenerate in a neighbourhood of the zero section
D
, it suffices to check that the restriction
\omega|_{D}
to
A|_{D}
is non-degenerate. Using
\tau
we can decompose
A|_{D}\cong TD\oplus L^{-k}
as a vector bundle. The restriction of forms then is described by the following lemma from Section
7
.

Lemma
7.16
.

Under the identifications
\Omega^{\bullet}_{A}\cong\Omega^{\bullet}_{L}\oplus\Omega^{\bullet-1}_{D}(S_{k}(L))
and
\Omega^{\bullet}_{A|_{D}}\cong\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}\oplus\Omega^{\bullet-1}_{D}(L^{k})
, the restriction of forms
\Omega_{A}^{\bullet}\to\Omega_{A|_{D}}^{\bullet}
is given by the following chain map

\Omega^{\bullet}_{L}\oplus\Omega^{\bullet-1}_{D}(S_{k}(L))\to\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}\oplus\Omega^{\bullet-1}_{D}(L^{k}),\qquad(\beta,\sum_{r=0}^{k}\alpha_{r})\mapsto(\beta|_{D}+\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{r}\wedge\eta_{r},\alpha_{k}).

We can now describe non-degeneracy of a
2
-form.

2.6
.

Let
\omega=(\beta,\sum_{r=0}^{k}\alpha_{r})\in\Omega^{2}_{L}\oplus\Omega^{1}_{D}(S_{k}(L))
be an algebroid
2
form. It’s restriction to
D
is given by
\omega|_{D}=(\gamma,\alpha_{k})\in\Omega^{2}_{D}\oplus\Omega^{1}_{D}(L^{k})
, where

\gamma=\beta|_{D}+\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{r}\wedge\eta_{r}.

Then
\omega
is non-degenerate in a neighbourhood of
D
if and only if
\alpha_{k}
is nowhere vanishing and
\gamma
restricts to a non-degenerate
2
-form on the kernel of
\alpha_{k}
.

Proof.

It suffices to determine the conditions for non-degeneracy of
\omega|_{D}
. This in turn is equivalent to
(\omega|_{D})^{n}
being nowhere vanishing. Writing
\omega|_{D}=\gamma+\alpha_{k}
, and noting that the ranks of
\gamma
and
\alpha_{k}
are respectively bounded above by
2n-2
and
2
, we see that

(\omega|_{D})^{n}=n\alpha_{k}\wedge\gamma^{n-1}.

From this the two conditions follow.
∎

Let
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(A)
be a symplectic form. We now analyze the geometric structures induced on
D
from the restriction
\omega|_{D}=(\gamma,\alpha_{k})
. Starting with
\alpha_{k}\in\Omega_{D}^{1}(L^{k})
, we note first from Lemma
2.6
that the kernel
\mathcal{F}=\ker(\alpha_{k})\subset TD
defines a corank
1
distribution on
D
. By Lemma
2.5
,
\alpha_{k}
is closed and hence
\mathcal{F}
defines a foliation. In fact, more is true.

2.7
.

Let
A\to L
be a
b^{k+1}
-algebroid on the total space of a line bundle over
D
and let
\nabla
be the induced flat connection on
L
. Let
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(A)
be a symplectic form and let
\omega|_{D}=(\gamma,\alpha_{k})
be its restriction to
D
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\ker(\alpha_{k})\subset TD
be the induced foliation on
D
and let
\nu_{\mathcal{F}}
be the normal bundle of
\mathcal{F}
, which is equipped with the flat Bott connection
\nabla^{Bott}
. Then
\alpha_{k}
defines a flat isomorphism

\alpha_{k}:(\nu_{\mathcal{F}},\nabla^{Bott})\to(L^{k},\nabla^{k})|_{\mathcal{F}}.

In particular, the foliation
\mathcal{F}
has trivial variation:
\mathrm{var}(\mathcal{F})=0
(see Definition
B.4
).

Proof.

By definition of
\alpha_{k}
, it defines an isomorphism of line bundles
\nu_{F}\to L^{k}
, so we must check that it relates the two connections, meaning that for all sections
X\in\mathcal{F}
,
s\in\nu_{F}
, we have

(2.5)

\alpha_{k}(\nabla^{\rm Bott}_{X}(s))=\nabla_{X}(\alpha_{k}(s)).

Let
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(D)
be a vector field extending
s
, meaning that
p(s)=Y
, where
p:TD\to\nu_{\mathcal{F}}
. By definition of the Bott connection

\nabla^{\rm Bott}_{X}(s)=p([X,Y]).

Furthermore, since
X
is tangent to the foliation,
\alpha_{k}(X)=0
. Hence, Equation
2.5
may be rearranged as follows:

d^{\nabla}(\alpha_{k})(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}(\alpha_{k}(Y))-\nabla_{Y}(\alpha_{k}(X))-\alpha_{k}([X,Y])=0,

and this follows because
\alpha_{k}
is closed. Finally,
\mathcal{F}
has trivial variation by Proposition
B.6
.
∎

Next, we consider the restriction of
\gamma
to the foliation, denoted
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}=\gamma|_{\mathcal{F}}
. The next result shows, in particular, that
(\mathcal{F},\omega_{\mathcal{F}})
defines a symplectic foliation. This is not surprising. Indeed,
\rho(\omega^{-1})\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}(L)
is a Poisson structure and
(\mathcal{F},\omega_{\mathcal{F}})
is part of its induced symplectic foliation. What is more surprising is that its symplectic variation is nontrivial and is determined by the extension class of
A|_{D}
.

2.8
.

Let
A\to L
be a
b^{k+1}
-algebroid on the total space of a line bundle over
D
and let
c\in H^{2}(D,L^{-k})
be its extension class, as in Theorem
2.2
. Let
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(A)
be a symplectic form, let
\omega|_{D}=(\gamma,\alpha_{k})
be its restriction to
D
, and let
\mathcal{F}
be the induced foliation on
D
from Proposition
2.7
. Then

\omega_{\mathcal{F}}=\gamma|_{\mathcal{F}}\in\Omega^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}

is a foliated symplectic form whose symplectic variation is given by the restriction of
c
:

\mathrm{var}(\omega_{\mathcal{F}})=-c|_{\mathcal{F}}\in H^{2}_{D}(\nu^{-1}_{F}).

Proof.

First, we decompose
\omega=(\beta,\sum_{r=0}^{k}\alpha_{r})\in\Omega^{2}_{L}\oplus\Omega^{1}_{D}(S_{k}(L))
. Both
\beta
and
\sum_{r=0}^{k}\alpha_{r}
are individually closed. By Lemma
2.5
we have
d^{\nabla}\alpha_{k}=0
. Hence, the closure can be written as

0=d\alpha_{k}+d(\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(i-k)\eta_{i}\wedge\alpha_{k}+d(\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{r}).

This is now an equation in
\Omega^{1}_{D}(S_{k-1}(L))
. Applying the restriction to the
D
map
\mathcal{R}
from Lemma
7.16
this gives us

0=-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(i-k)\eta_{i}\wedge\eta_{k-i}\wedge\alpha_{k}+d\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{r}\wedge\eta_{r}.

Using the fact that
\beta
is closed and the expression for
c
from Theorem
2.2
, this rearranges to the equation
d\gamma=-c\wedge\alpha_{k}
. This implies in particular that
d_{\mathcal{F}}\omega_{\mathcal{F}}=0
. Hence, since it is non-degenerate by Lemma
2.6
, it defines a foliated symplectic form. Furthermore, since
\gamma
is an extension of
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
to
\Omega_{D}^{2}
, it can be used to compute the symplectic variation. Indeed, this is nothing but a connecting homomorphism and is given by

\mathrm{var}(\omega_{\mathcal{F}})=\frac{d\gamma}{\alpha_{k}}=-c|_{F}.

∎

2.5.
Normal form theorem around the hypersurface

Using the already abundant theory of normal forms for Lie algebroid symplectic forms we can readily obtain a normal form theorem for
b^{k+1}
-symplectic forms in terms of the data described in Lemma
2.5
.

2.9
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(A)
be a
b^{k}
-symplectic form. Then there exists a tubular neighbourhood
L
of
D
, on which
\omega
is symplectomorphic to

\omega^{\prime}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}\wedge\tau_{i}+p^{*}\iota^{*}\beta,

where the
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta
are as in Lemma
2.5
,
p:L\to D
is the projection, and
\iota:D\to L
is the inclusion of the zero section.

Proof.

By the discussion above we have that
\omega|_{D}=\omega^{\prime}|_{D}
. Therefore, by choosing the tubular neighbourhood small enough, we can ensure that
\omega^{\prime}
is non-degenerate. Next note that by construction
\omega-\omega^{\prime}
extends smoothly to zero over
D
. Therefore we are in the setting to use Theorem 4.61 from
[
Kla18
]
. The desired result then follows immediately.
∎

2.6.
The dual of the Lie algebroid

Recall that there exists a Poisson structure on the dual of any Lie algebroid. This Poisson structure is itself Lie algebroid symplectic for the right choice of Lie algebroid:

Let
A\rightarrow M
be a Lie algebroid, and write
\pi:A^{*}\rightarrow M
for the dual. Then
A^{*}
admits a Poisson structure
\pi_{A^{*}}
defined by the brackets:

\{s_{1},s_{2}\}_{A^{*}}=[s_{1},s_{2}],\quad\{s,f\}_{A^{*}}=\rho_{A}(s)(f),

where we identify
s_{1}\in\Gamma(A)
with fibre-wise linear functions on
{\rm tot}(A^{*})
. It can be shown that this is in fact a Lie algebroid symplectic structure:

2.10
[
dLMM04
,
Smi22
]
.

Let
\lambda_{\rm can}\in\Omega^{1}(\pi^{!}A)
be the canonical one-form defined by

\lambda_{\rm can}(\alpha)(v)=\alpha(d_{A}\pi(v)),

for all
\alpha\in A^{*}
and
v\in(\pi^{!}A)_{v}
. Then
\omega_{\rm can}:=-d\lambda_{\rm can}
is an
\pi^{!}A
-symplectic form, which induces
\pi_{A^{*}}
.

Therefore, whenever one is given a Lie algebroid one immediately obtains Lie algebroid symplectic structures on its dual. We will now see that the pull-back Lie algebroid of a Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type is again of
b^{k+1}
-type:

2.11
.

Let
A\rightarrow M
be a Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type corresponding to a
k
-th order foliation
\sigma
. Then
\pi^{!}A\rightarrow A^{*}
is a Lie algebroid of
b^{k+1}
-type corresponding to the
k
-th order foliation
\pi^{*}\sigma
.

Therefore, we immediately obtain a large class of examples of symplectic structures on Lie algebroids of
b^{k+1}
-type.

2.7.
Some explicit examples

In this section we give some explicit examples of
b^{k}
-symplectic forms.

2.7.1.
Genus
g
surface

Let
S
be a genus
g
surface. In Example
2.3
we constructed
b^{4}
-algebroids on the total space of the trivial line bundle
L=S\times\mathbb{R}
. Now let
D=S\times\mathbb{R}
, with coordinate
u
on the factor
\mathbb{R}
. Consider the trivial line bundle
L^{\prime}=D\times\mathbb{R}
with fibre coordinate
t
. The total space inherits a family of
b^{4}
-algebroid
A_{3}
depending on
4g
-parameters
(x_{i},y_{i},w_{i},z_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{4g}
. Indeed, let
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
be the two closed
1
-forms from Example
2.3
, now pulled back to
D
. We recall their definitions here:

\eta_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{g}(x_{i}\alpha_{i}+y_{i}\beta_{i}),\qquad\eta_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{g}(w_{i}\alpha_{i}+z_{i}\beta_{i}).

These determine
b^{4}
-algebroids
A_{3}
just as in Example
2.3
. Recall that the extension class of the
A_{3}
is given by the formula
c=\sum_{i=1}^{g}(x_{i}z_{i}-y_{i}w_{i})\omega
, where
\omega\in H^{2}(D)\cong\mathbb{R}
is the generator.

We now construct a symplectic form. First, let
\beta\in\Omega^{2}_{S}
be a symplectic representative of
\omega
, pulled back to a form on
L^{\prime}
. Define

\alpha_{1}=-u\eta_{2},\qquad\alpha_{2}=-2u\eta_{1},\qquad\alpha_{3}=du.

By Lemma
2.5
these define the data of a closed algebroid
2
-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(A_{3})
.

Restricting
\omega
to
D
we get
(\alpha_{3}=du,\gamma)
, where
\gamma
is given by

\gamma=\beta-u\eta_{1}\wedge\eta_{2}=\beta-uc.

The
1
-form
\alpha_{3}
defines a foliation whose leaves are given by the surface
S
. The restriction of
\gamma
to this foliation is
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}=\beta-uc
which is non-degenerate for small values of
u
. Let
D_{\epsilon}=S\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
. By Lemma
2.6
, we therefore obtain a symplectic form on a neighbourhood of
D_{\epsilon}
for some value
\epsilon>0
. Furthermore, the symplectic variation is given by
-c\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\cong H^{2}_{S}(C^{\infty}(-\epsilon,\epsilon))
.

Writing the symplectic form out in coordinates we get

\omega=\beta+(du-ut^{2}\eta_{2}-2ut\eta_{1})\wedge\frac{dt}{t^{4}}+(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\eta_{1}+\frac{1}{t}\eta_{2})\wedge du.

2.7.2.
Heisenberg group

We continue studying the quotient
X=H(\mathbb{R})/H(\mathbb{Z})
of the Heisenberg group from Example
2.4
. In terms of the coordinates introduced there, the Lie algebroid
A_{4}
has dual basis

e^{1}=dx,e^{2}=dy,e^{3}=dz-ydx,e^{4}=\frac{dt}{t^{5}}-\frac{dx}{t^{3}}(1+t^{2}y)-\frac{dy}{t^{2}}+\frac{dz}{t}

By Proposition
2.11
we get that the induced Poisson structure on
Y={\rm tot}(A_{4}^{*})
is again a symplectic structure of
b^{5}
-type. We now explicitly describe this structure. We have that
Y\simeq D\times\mathbb{R}^{4}
, on which we have global coordinates
(x,y,z,t,\alpha,\beta,\eta,\gamma)
. The tautologial form is then given by

\lambda=\alpha e^{1}+\beta e^{2}+\eta e^{3}+\gamma e^{4},

The corresponding symplectic form is then given by

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=d\alpha\wedge e^{1}+d\beta\wedge e^{2}+d\eta\wedge e^{3}+-\eta e^{1}\wedge e^{2}+d\gamma\wedge e^{4}

\displaystyle-\gamma\left(3te^{4}\wedge e^{1}+2t^{2}e^{4}\wedge e^{2}+t^{3}e^{4}\wedge e^{3}-2e^{1}\wedge e^{3}-te^{2}\wedge e^{3}\right)

Along
t=0
we get a foliation defined by
d\gamma=0
, and a foliated symplectic form

\omega=d\alpha\wedge e^{1}+d\beta\wedge e^{2}-\eta e^{1}\wedge e^{2}+d\eta\wedge e^{3}-2\gamma e^{1}\wedge e^{3}.

Hence this form has symplectic variation
-2e^{1}\wedge e^{3}
, which is indeed the extension class as computed in Example
2.4
, which is consistent with Proposition
2.8
.

3.
k
-th order foliations

We begin (Subsection
3.1
) by recalling some elementary facts about bundles, subbundles, their sheaves of sections, and their bundles of jets. We do this in order to set notation. We then specialise this discussion to the case of the tangent bundle (Subsection
3.2
), focusing on the notion of involutivity. This allows us to introduce the Lie algebroids, and some more general singular foliations, to be studied in the paper. Along the way we discuss various equivalent ways of thinking about these objects.

3.1.
Bundles

Let
M
be a smooth manifold, and let
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
denote its structure sheaf of smooth functions. Fix a non-negative integer
k
. Given a fibre bundle
E\rightarrow M
, we write
\Gamma(E)
for its sheaf of sections and
J^{k}(E)
for its bundle of
k
-jets of sections. When
E
is a trivial fibre bundle with fibre
F
we simply write
J^{k}(M,F)
for the
k
-jets of
F
-valued functions.

When
E=M\times\mathbb{R}
is the trivial bundle, we have that
J^{k}(M,\mathbb{R})
moreover inherits the structure of commutative
\mathbb{R}
-algebra. It is convenient to recall that the fibre
J^{k}_{p}(M,\mathbb{R})
is the stalk at
p\in M
of the skyscraper sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}/{\mathcal{I}}^{k+1}_{p}
; here
{\mathcal{I}}^{k}_{p}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
is the ideal of functions that vanish to order at least
k
at
p
.

More generally, consider a fibre bundle
E\rightarrow M
. Its space of sections
\Gamma(E)
is an
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
-module. Moreover,
J^{k}_{p}(E)
is the stalk at
p
of
\Gamma(E)/({\mathcal{I}}^{k+1}_{p}\Gamma(E))
. Putting these facts together we deduce that
J^{k}(E)
is a bundle of
J^{k}(M,\mathbb{R})
-modules.

3.1.1.
Jets along submanifolds

Fix a submanifold
i:W\to M
. Write
{\mathcal{I}}_{W}^{k}
for the sheaf of functions vanishing to order
k
along
W
.

3.1
.

The
k
-th order neighbourhood of
W
is defined as:

{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}:=i^{-1}\dfrac{{\mathcal{O}}_{M}}{{\mathcal{I}}_{W}^{k+1}},

i.e. the sheaf of
k
-jets of functions along
W
.

In algebraic geometry it is customary to think of
W
in terms of its sheaf of functions
{\mathcal{O}}_{W}
. The restriction map
{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{W}
then allows us to think of
{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}
as a thickening of
W
, capable of seeing information around
W
up to
k
-jets.

More generally, consider a fibre bundle bundle
E\rightarrow M
. Recall that a section of
J^{k}(E)|_{W}\rightarrow W
is said to be
holonomic
if its projection to
J^{k}(E|_{W})
is the
k
-jet of a section of
E|_{W}
.

3.2
.

We write
\Hol_{W}^{k}(E)
for the sheaf of holonomic sections of
J^{k}(E)|_{W}
. Its elements are said to be
k
-jets of sections
of
E
along
W
.

We have maps
\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Hol_{W}^{k}(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E|_{W})
which we denote by
j^{k}_{W}
and
|_{W}
, respectively.

The following useful lemma says that holonomic sections have germ representatives:

3.3
.

Let
E\rightarrow M
be a fibre bundle. Fix a section
\sigma\in\Hol_{W}^{k}(E)
. Then there is a germ of section
s\in\Gamma(E)
such that
j^{k}_{W}s=\sigma
.

Proof.

Note that

\Hol_{W}^{k}(E)=i^{-1}\dfrac{\Gamma(E)}{{\mathcal{I}}_{W}^{k+1}\Gamma(E)},

hence the statement holds locally. Therefore, by applying a partition of unity we may prove the desired result.
∎

We can embrace the algebraic viewpoint and study
E
via its sheaf of sections
\Gamma(E)
. This motivates us to write

E|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}:=\Hol_{W}^{k}(E),

which is readily seen to be a locally free sheaf of
{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}
-modules.

3.1.2.
Jets of vector subbundles

Consider now a vector bundle
E\rightarrow M
. We will now study vector subbundles
R\subset E
, which we view as sections of the Grassmannian bundle
\operatorname{Gr}(E,l)
, with
l
the rank. Recall that there is a correspondence between subbundles
R\subset E
and locally free subsheaves
\Gamma(R)\subset\Gamma(E)
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
-modules. Our goal now is to translate this to the jet setting.

By exactness of taking jets, the exact sequence of vector bundles

0\rightarrow R\rightarrow E\rightarrow E/R\rightarrow 0

translates into a exact sequence of
J^{k}(M,\mathbb{R})
-modules:

0\rightarrow J^{k}(R)\rightarrow J^{k}(E)\rightarrow J^{k}(E/R)\rightarrow 0.

An element
\sigma\in J^{k}(E)
is
tangent
to
R
if it is in the kernel of the second map (or equivalently, in the image of the first). A section
s:M\to E
is
k
-tangent
to
R
along
W
if the jets
j^{k}_{W}s
take values in
J^{k}(R)
.

3.4
.

We write
{\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(W,R)
for the sheaf of sections that are
k
-tangent to
R
along
W
.

The following structure theorem provides a rather explicit description of
{\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(W,R)
:

3.5
.

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(W,R)={\mathcal{I}}_{W}^{k+1}\Gamma(E)+\Gamma(R).

Here the symbol
+
denotes the
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
-span of the union.

Proof.

We write
\pi
for the quotient map
E\rightarrow E/R
. First we observe that
{\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(W,R)
contains both
{\mathcal{I}}_{W}^{k+1}\Gamma(E)
and
\Gamma(R)
. Indeed, sections
X
in
\Gamma(R)
satisfy
j^{k}\pi\circ j^{k}(X)=0
everywhere, not just along
W
. Similarly, a section
Y
in
{\mathcal{I}}_{W}^{k+1}\Gamma(E)
satisfies
j^{r}Y|_{W}=0
, so in particular
j^{k}\pi\circ j^{k}(Y)|_{W}=0
.

For the other inclusion observe first that we can work locally, since we are arguing with sheaves. Fix a complement
C\subset E
for
R
. Then, any
X\in{\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(W,R)
can be uniquely written as
X=Y+Z
, where
Y\in\Gamma(R)
and
Z\in\Gamma(C)
. It follows that
Z
is also in
{\operatorname{Tan}}^{r}(W,R)
. We can then observe that there are canonical isomorphisms of
J^{k}(M,\mathbb{R})
-modules

J^{k}(E)\cong J^{k}(R)\oplus J^{k}(C)

and
j^{k}Z
takes values in the term
J^{k}(C)
. However,
Z
being in
{\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(W,R)
implies that
j^{k}Z|_{W}
takes values in
J^{k}(R)
. The conclusion is that
j^{k}Z|_{W}
is zero, proving that
Z\in{\mathcal{I}}_{W}^{k+1}\Gamma(E)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

The following characterises jets of subbundles in terms of the
k
-tangency condition:

3.6
.

Let
R
and
R^{\prime}
be subbundles of
E
of rank
l
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

j^{k}_{W}R
and
j^{k}_{W}R^{\prime}
coincide as sections of the Grassmanian bundle, that is
j^{k}_{W}R=j^{k}_{W}R^{\prime}\in\Hol_{W}^{k}(\operatorname{Gr}(E,l))

(2)

j^{k}_{W}R
and
j^{k}_{W}R^{\prime}
coincide as subsheaves of
\Hol_{W}^{k}(E)
.

(3)

{\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(W,R)={\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(W,R^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Choose a local frame
f
for
R
around
p
. We can provide a chart in
\operatorname{Gr}(E,l)
around
R
as follows: Given any
R^{\prime}
which is
0
-tangent to
R
at
p
, then near
p

R^{\prime}
is graphical over
R
. That is, for all
q
near
p
there exists a linear map
A_{q}\operatorname{Hom}(R_{q},R_{q}^{\perp})
such that
R^{\prime}
has as frame
q\mapsto f+A_{g}(f)
. In these terms, conditions
1
to
3
are all seen to be equivalent to the vanishing of the
k
-jet of
q\mapsto A_{q}
at
p
.
∎

The lemma allows us to discuss jets of subbundles of
E
along a submanifold
W
.

3.7
.

A
k
-jet of subbundle of
E
along
W
is an element of
\Hol_{W}^{k}(\operatorname{Gr}(E,l))
. The integer
l
is called the rank of the subbundle.

3.8
.

A subbundle of
E|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}
is a locally free
{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}
-submodule of
E|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}
. The rank of the subbundle is its rank as a module.

Given a
k
-jet of subbundle, we can associate to it a subbundle of
E|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}
by taking sections:

3.9
.

An element
\sigma\in\Hol_{W}^{k}(\operatorname{Gr}(E,l))
uniquely defines a subbundle
\Gamma(\sigma)\subset E|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}
.

Proof.

We first argue locally. Find a subbundle
R\subset E
representing
\sigma
. Then
R|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}\subset E|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}
is a subbundle of
E|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}
. According to Lemma
3.6
, this subbundle is independent of our choice of
R
. To argue globally, we observe that the choices of
R
for differing charts are compatible, thanks again to Lemma
3.6
.
∎

3.10
.

The assignment
\sigma\mapsto\Gamma(\sigma)
is a bijection between:

•

k
-jets of subbundles of
E
along
W
.

•

Subbundles of
E|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}
.

Proof.

We will provide an inverse. We work locally at first. Given a subbundle
S
of
E|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}
, we use the locally free condition to produce a basis of
S
. We then pick a tuple of
l
-linearly independent sections of
E
, close to
W
, representing said basis. The tuple spans a subbundle
R\subset E
in the vicinity of
W
. All such choices are
k
-tangent to each other along
W
. It follows from Lemma
3.6
that they are compatible and define a holonomic section of
\Hol_{W}^{k}(\operatorname{Gr}(E,l))
. The choices of tuple across charts are compatible, due to Lemma
3.6
, yielding a global element of
\Hol_{W}^{k}(\operatorname{Gr}(E,l))
.
∎

3.2.
Distributions

All the results and discussion in Subsection
3.1
apply to the concrete case of
TM\rightarrow M
and to its subbundles, the tangent distributions
\xi\subset TM
. The new ingredient in this case is the Lie bracket. Henceforth, we will assume that
\operatorname{rank}(\xi)={\rm codim}(W)=l
.

3.2.1.
The Lie bracket in
TM|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}

The Lie bracket

[-,-]:\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(TM)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM)

is a bidifferential operator of order one. It therefore defines an antisymmetric bundle map

J^{k}(TM)\times J^{k}(TM)\rightarrow J^{k-1}(TM)

that is compatible with the
J^{k}(M,\mathbb{R})
-module structure on
J^{k}(TM)
via the Leibniz identity: i.e.
[fX,Y]=f[X,Y]+df(Y)X
holds for every
f\in J^{k}(M,\mathbb{R})
and every pair
X,Y\in J^{k}(TM)
, all based at the same point.

Due to the loss of a derivative, we do not have a Lie algebra structure on
J^{k}(TM)
. To remedy this we will restrict ourselves to
{\mathcal{A}}_{W}\subset\Gamma(TM)
: the subsheaf of vector fields tangent to
W
. Then
k
-jets of vector fields correspond precisely to the derivations of
{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}
.

3.11
.

The sheaf
i^{-1}({\mathcal{A}}_{W})/i^{-1}({\mathcal{I}}_{W}^{k+1}\Gamma(TM))
of
k
-jets of vector fields tangent to
W
is the sheaf of derivations
\Der({\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k})
of
{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}
.

3.12
.

The Lie bracket of vector fields provides
\Der({\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k})
with the structure of a sheaf of Lie-Rinehart algebras over
{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}
. That is:

•

It is a sheaf of
{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}
-modules.

•

\Der({\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k})
acts on
{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}
by derivations.

•

The Lie bracket in
{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}
satisfies the Leibniz rule with respect to
{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}
.

Proof.

Do note that the resulting Lie bracket is precisely the one inherited from being derivations.
∎

We may now consider

3.13
.

A distribution
\xi\subset TM|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}
on the
k
-th order neighbourhood
{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}
is
involutive
if:

•

It is tangent to
W
, or in other words, it is a subsheaf of
\Der({\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k})
.

•

It is closed under Lie bracket.

We will refer to
\xi
as a
foliation on the
k
-th order neighbourhood of
W
.

3.2.2.
Jets of distributions

We may now translate the involutivity condition to the corresponding
k
-jet of distribution:

3.14
.

We say that a
k
-jet of distribution
\sigma\in\Hol^{k}_{W}(\operatorname{Gr}(TM,l))
is
involutive at
p\in W
if for any germ of distribution
\xi
around
p
with
\xi_{p}\in T_{p}M
with
j^{k}_{p}\xi=\sigma_{p}
we have

•

\xi
is tangent to
W

•

j^{k}_{p}[X,Y]\in\sigma_{p}
, for all
X,Y\in\xi_{p}
.

We say that a
k
-jet distribution is
involutive
when it is involutive for all
p\in W
.

We will also refer to an involutive
k
-jet of distribution as a
k
-th order foliation
.

3.15
.

A
k
-jet of distribution
\sigma\in\Hol_{W}^{k}(\operatorname{Gr}(TM,l))
is involutive if and only if the corresponding distribution
\Gamma(\sigma)\subset E|_{{\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k}}
is involutive.

Proof.

First observe that each local section
X
of
\Der({\mathcal{N}}_{W}^{k})
defines a
k
-jet of vector field
X_{p}
at each
p\in W
. Moreover,
X
takes values in
\Gamma(\sigma)
if and only if
X_{p}
is
k
-tangent to
\sigma
.

Suppose then that
\sigma
is involutive and consider two local sections
X,Y
of
\Gamma(\sigma)
. The jets
X_{p}
and
Y_{p}
at
p
are
k
-tangent to
\sigma
so, by involutivity, the
(k-1)
-jet
j^{k}_{p}[X,Y]
is again in
\sigma_{p}
. Reasoning similarly we get that
\Gamma(\sigma)
being involutive implies that
\sigma
is involutive.
∎

3.16
.

We say that a distribution
\xi\in TM
is
involutive up to order
k
at
W
if
\xi
is tangent to
W
and
J^{k}_{W}[\xi,\xi]\subset J^{k}_{W}\xi
.

By Lemma
3.3
, we have that any holonomic section
\sigma\in\Hol^{k}_{W}(\operatorname{Gr}(TM,l))
can be represented by a germ of distribution
\xi\in TM
around
W
. The following now follows immediately:

3.17
.

A
k
-jet of distribution
\sigma\in\Hol^{k}_{W}(\operatorname{Gr}(TM,l))
is involutive if and only if there exists a germ
\xi\subset TM
around
W
which is involutive up to order
k
with
J_{W}^{k}\xi=\sigma
.

3.3.
The associated singular foliation

We now study the module
{\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(M,\xi)
introduced in Lemma
3.5
for tangent distributions:

3.18
.

Let
\sigma\in\Hol^{k}(\operatorname{Gr}(TM,l))
be
k
-th order foliation, and let
\xi
be a germ of distribution extending it. We define
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)\subset\mathfrak{X}(M)
as

{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)={\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(W,\xi).

3.19
.

Let
\sigma\in\Hol^{k}(\operatorname{Gr}(TM,l))
be
k
-th order foliation, then
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
is a singular foliation with leaves (the connected components) of
M\backslash W
and
W
. Moreover
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
does not depend on the choice of extending distribution
\xi
.

Proof.

These facts follow readily from the explicit description of
{\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(W,\xi)
as in Lemma
3.5
.
∎

We may also express
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
in terms of the foliation on the
k
-th order neighbourhood
{\mathcal{N}}^{k}_{W}
.

3.20
.

Let
F\subset\Der({\mathcal{N}}^{k}_{W})
be a foliation, then we define a singular foliation
{\mathcal{A}}_{F}\subset\mathfrak{X}(M)
by

{\mathcal{A}}_{F}=p_{k}^{-1}(i_{*}F),

where
i:W\hookrightarrow M
is the inclusion and
p_{k}:T(-\log W)\rightarrow\iota_{*}\Der({\mathcal{N}}^{k}_{W})
the projection map.

From this description it is clear that when
F
and
\sigma\in\Hol^{k}(\operatorname{Gr}(TM,l))
are related via Lemma
3.10
, then
{\mathcal{A}}_{F}={\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
.

In general,
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
is just a singular foliation. We can characterise precisely when it is a Lie algebroid:

3.21
.

Let
\sigma\in\Hol^{k}(\operatorname{Gr}(TM,l))
be a
k
-th order foliation. Then
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
is locally free, and consequently the sections of some Lie algebroid
A(\sigma)
, if and only if
W
is codimension-one.

Proof.

This again follows readily from the explicit description of
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
in terms of Lemma
3.5
.
∎

We may characterize precisely what properties the induced singular foliation has.

3.22
.

Let
M^{n}
be a manifold with embedded submanifold
W^{n-l}
. Then a singular foliation
{\mathcal{A}}
is said to be of
b^{k+1}
-type
if there exists local coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
with
W=\{x_{1}=\ldots=x_{l}\}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
given by:

\langle x^{I}\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,x^{I}\partial_{x_{l}},\partial_{x_{l+1}},\ldots,\partial{x_{k}}\rangle,

with
I
running over all multi-indices in
1,\ldots,l
of length
k+1
.

3.23
.

There is an equivalence between
k
-th order foliations and singular foliations of
b^{k+1}
-type. Explicitly:

•

If
\sigma\in\Hol^{k}(\operatorname{Gr}(TM,l))
is a
k
-th order foliation, then
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
is a singular foliation of type
b^{k+1}
.

•

If
{\mathcal{A}}
is of
b^{k+1}
-type, then
\sigma=J^{k}_{W}{\mathcal{A}}
is a
k
-th order foliation.

Proof.

The proof of this result depends on a Frobenius theorem for
k
-jets of foliations, whose proof we postpone till Theorem
4.6
. When
\sigma
is a
k
-th order foliation, it follows directly from the explicit description of
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
from Lemma
3.5
and this Frobenius theorem that
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
is of
b^{k+1}
-type. Conversely
{\mathcal{A}}
being of type
k
immediately implies that
J^{k}_{W}{\mathcal{A}}
is a
k
-th order foliation.
∎

3.0
.

There is no a priori reason to restrict ourselves to the case where
{\rm codim}(W)=\operatorname{rank}(\xi)
, the associated singular foliation
{\operatorname{Tan}}^{k}(M,\xi)
is still well-defined. However, it is no longer of
b^{k+1}
-type. We will address singular foliations of this form in upcoming work.

3.4.
Subsheaves of invariant functions

Given a foliation
F
on
\mathcal{N}_{W}^{k}
, we may define the following subsheaf
\mathcal{C}_{F}\subset\mathcal{N}^{k}_{W}
of functions

\mathcal{C}_{F}=\{f\in\mathcal{N}_{W}^{k}:V(f)=0\text{ for all }V\in F\}.

To characterize the properties of
\mathcal{C}_{F}
recall that there are maps
\mathcal{N}^{k}_{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{W}^{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z}
, and that we have a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\nu_{Z}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{W}^{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z}\rightarrow 0.

3.24
.

We say that
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{l})
with
f_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{W}^{k}
is a
local defining function
for
W^{n-l}\subset M^{n}
if its projection to
\mathcal{O}_{W}
is zero, and the corresponding sections
df_{i}\in\nu_{Z}^{*}
are linearly independent.

3.25
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{F}
be the sheaf of invariant functions of a foliation
F
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{F}
is locally generated over
\mathbb{R}
by a local defining function. More precisely, for every
x\in D
, there is a neighbourhood
x\in U\subset M
and a local defining function
s\in\mathcal{C}_{F}(U)
such that we have a surjection of sheaves of algebras

\mathbb{R}_{U}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}]\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{F}(U),\quad f\mapsto f\circ s.

Furthermore, this map induces an isomorphism of sheaves
\mathbb{R}_{U}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}]/\mathcal{I}_{k}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{F}(U)
, where
\mathcal{I}_{k+1}
is the ideal of polynomials vanishing to at least order
k+1
.

Proof.

The proof of this result depends on a Frobenius theorem for
k
-jets of foliations, whose proof we postpone till Theorem
4.6
. By this result, we have that there exists local coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
such that
F=\langle\partial_{x_{l+1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}}\rangle
and
\mathcal{N}^{k}_{W}|_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{U}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}]/(z^{I})
, with
I
running over all multi-indices of length
k+1
. Therefore, we naturally obtain an isomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{C}_{F}(U)\simeq\mathbb{R}_{U}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}]/(z^{I})
. The function
s=\varphi^{-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l})
provides the desired defining function.
∎

We may use this to abstractly characterize the sheaf of invariant functions:

3.26
.

Let
W^{n-l}
be an embedded connected submanifold of
M^{n}
. A
sheaf of invariant functions
on
\mathcal{N}^{k}_{W}
is

•

a locally constant sheaf
\mathcal{C}_{F}
of
\mathbb{R}
-algebras which is locally isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}_{U}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}]/\mathcal{I}_{k+1}
, and

•

a morphism of sheaves of
\mathbb{R}
-algebras
\mathcal{C}_{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{k}
which sends a set of local generators of
\mathcal{C}_{F}
to local defining functions in
\mathcal{N}^{k}_{W}
.

Using this definition we may now recover the foliation:

3.27
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a sheaf of invariant functions on
\mathcal{N}^{k}_{W}
. Then the subsheaf of derivations

4.
Atiyah algebroid and holonomy

In this section we will define a principal bundle consisting of
k
-jets of diffeomorphisms, and it’s associated Atiyah algebroid (Subsection
4.1
). We will see that
k
-th order foliations can be viewed as flat connections on this principle bundle (Subsection

). We will use this point of view to define the holonomy for a
k
-th order foliation (Subsection

4.1.
Principal bundle of
k
-jets

Let
\pi:E^{l}\to W
be a vector bundle. Let
\mathrm{Diff}_{\pi}(\mathbb{R}^{l},E)

\mathbb{R}^{l}
to

with the property that

gets sent to the
0

\mathbb{R}^{l}
gets sent completely into a fibre of

\mathrm{Diff}_{\pi}(\mathbb{R}^{l},E)\to W

We want to modify this construction by passing to

-jets. We first define the group required for this:

4.1

We let

k
-jet of diffeomorphisms fixing the origin.

We may describe

as automorphisms of
k
-jets of functions:

G_{k,l}=\Aut(\mathbb{R}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}]/\mathcal{I}_{k}),

We can now truncate

k
-jets instead

This is a right principal

-bundle over
W
.

Note that
{\operatorname{Fr}}^{1}(E)
is precisely the frame bundle
{\rm Fr}(E)
. The Atiyah algebroid of

is precisely the Atiyah algebroid of

{\operatorname{Fr}}^{k}(E)

Let

•

•

Proof.

In order to understand

k
-jets of) diffeomorphisms

with the properties that

•

\phi
covers a diffeomorphism

of the base,

•

\phi
preserves the zero section and
\phi|_{W}=g

The infinitesimal version is precisely the desired description.
∎

It follows from this that
\At^{k}(E)
is naturally a subsheaf of
\Der(\mathcal{N}_{W}^{k})

4.2.
Lie algebroid splittings

Given a vector bundle

We view the

4.3.

We may now define the holonomy of a jet of foliation as follows. Just as with usual foliations, the definition of holonomy depends on several choices. We therefore first describe out set-up in some detail:

4.4.
Frobenius theorem

We will prove that

-th order foliation might not come from an actual foliation we cannot refer to the standard Frobenius theorem. Instead we will prove:

4.5

Let

be a k-th order foliation and let
\Sigma
be its holonomy. When

\sigma=J^{k}_{W}\varphi^{*}\mathcal{F}

Proof.

By choosing a tubular neighbourhood we may assume that we are working on the normal bundle

. As

-jet of diffeomorphism

\varphi:\nu_{W}\rightarrow W\times\nu_{W,x},v\mapsto\varphi_{x,y}^{-1}(v).

As we are working on a vector bundle, we may represent this

\psi:\nu_{W}\rightarrow W\times\nu_{W,x}
. For this diffeomorphism it holds that

is the

Consequently, we can immediately deduce the following local form result for

-th order foliations:

Given a foliation

\{\partial_{x_{l+1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}}\}.

4.5.
Jets of foliations as Maurer-Cartan elements

For the remainder of this section, suppose we have chosen an embedded normal bundle so that we may assume we are working on a vector bundle

If we have two flat splittings

Proof.

4.8

We have that

Under this isomorphism the dgla structure induced by

[s_{i}\otimes A,t_{j}\otimes B]=j(s_{i}\cdot A(t_{j}))\otimes B+i(t_{j}\cdot B(s_{i}))\otimes A+(s_{i}\cdot t_{j})\otimes[A,B],

5.
The Riemann-Hilbert correspondence

The Riemann-Hilbert correspondence is the name given to various results relating flat connections to representations of the fundamental group. In this section we will prove some results with this flavour for
k
-th order foliations. This will reduce the classification of
k
-th order foliations, up to isomorphism, to a representation theoretical problem (Theorems
5.2
and
5.4
).

5.1.
Setup

Our Riemann-Hilbert statements come in two flavours, which we call parametrised and unparametrised. Both involve jets of foliations
\sigma
in a manifold
E
along a submanifold
W
. The ambient
E
will always be assumed to be a small thickening of
W
. Where
W
plays the role of a leaf of
\sigma
. In the parametrised case we additionally endow
E
with the structure of a fibre bundle over
W
. With this structure in place, we may see the holonomy as a flat structure on the principal
G_{k,l}
-bundle
{\operatorname{Fr}}^{k}(E)
.

5.1.1.
The parametrised setting

5.0
.

Observe that the data of a
0
-th order foliation is vacuous. Because of this, we assume
k>0
for the remainder of the section.

Recall the definition of
J^{k}{\operatorname{Fol}}_{*}(W,x,l)
from Subsection
4.3
.

5.1
.

The
parametrised Riemann-Hilbert map
is defined as:

\displaystyle{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}:J^{k}{\operatorname{Fol}}_{*}(W,x,l)

\displaystyle\rightarrow{\operatorname{Hom}}(\pi_{1}(W,x),G_{k,l})

\displaystyle(E,\phi,\sigma)

\displaystyle\mapsto([\gamma]\mapsto\phi^{-1}\circ{\operatorname{hol}}^{\sigma}([\gamma])\circ\phi).

Then, the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence for
k
-th order foliations, in the parametrised setting, reads:

5.2
.

The map
{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}
defines a bijection

\dfrac{J^{k}{\operatorname{Fol}}_{*}(W,x,l)}{\text{isomorphism}}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Hom}}(\pi_{1}(W,x),G_{k,l}).

Recall that
W
is assumed to be connected. We prove Theorem
5.2
in Subsections
5.2
and
5.3
below. We will address surjectivity first, then injectivity.

5.1.2.
The unparametrised setting

The starting data in the unparametrised case is still the tuple
(W,x,l,k)
. The class under study is now

, whose elements are pairs
(E,\sigma)
with

, along

, and

a

-th order foliation in

-th order foliations to one another.

Instead of the representations, we consider the

i.e. the quotient of the representation variety by the action of

by conjugation. Then:

5.3

The

\displaystyle{\operatorname{RH}}:J^{k}{\operatorname{Fol}}(W,x,l)

\displaystyle\rightarrow{\operatorname{Char}}(\pi_{1}(W,x),G_{k,l})

\displaystyle(E,\sigma)

\displaystyle\mapsto{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}(E,\phi,\sigma)/G_{k,l}.

More precisely: Given

we endow

with an auxiliary microbundle structure and we choose some germ of parametrisation

. We will prove in Subsection

that

does not depend on these auxiliary choices and is thus well-defined. Then, the (unparametrised) Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, again for

5.4

The map
{\operatorname{RH}}
defines a bijection

\dfrac{J^{k}{\operatorname{Fol}}(W,x,l)}{\text{isomorphism}}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Char}}(\pi_{1}(W,x),G_{k,l}).

Surjectivity will be established in Subsection

, and it will follow immediately from the surjectivity of

.

5.2.
Surjectivity of the (parametrised) Riemann-Hilbert map

We begin by proving the surjectivity of the map
{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}
. This will immediately imply the surjectivity of the map
{\operatorname{RH}}
and the surjectivity part of Theorems
5.2
and Theorem

(i.e. half of the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, both parametrised and unparametrised).

The proof amounts to showing that any representation in
{\operatorname{Hom}}(\pi_{1}(W,x),G_{k,l})
can be realised as the holonomy of some
k
-th order foliation. This is a two-step process:

•

Given a representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(W,x)\rightarrow G_{k,l}
, we associate to it a flat principal

.

•

To each principal
G_{k,l}
-bundle

we associate a vector bundle
\pi:E\rightarrow W
so that

.

It will then follow that the flat structure on

defines a

-th order foliation on

whose holonomy, seen from the fibre

, is precisely

5.2.1.
First step

The first step of the argument uses the well-known construction of a flat principal

-representation. We recall this construction, as we need to refer to it later:

5.5
.

Let
\rho:\pi_{1}(W,x)\rightarrow G
be a representation. Then

is a principal

-bundle over

. Moreover, it comes equipped with a representation of the fundamental groupoid

satisfying
{\operatorname{hol}}_{x}=\rho
.

Proof.

P
is a principal bundle by construction, so we just need to construct
{\operatorname{hol}}
. Recall the identification

\Pi_{1}(W)=\dfrac{\widetilde{W}\times\widetilde{W}}{\pi_{1}(W,x)}.

We can then write
[y,z]
for an element in
\Pi_{1}(W)
and
[y,a]
for an element in

; here

and

. It follows that
{\operatorname{hol}}
can be uniquely defined from

by setting
{\operatorname{hol}}([y,z])=([y,a]\mapsto[z,a])
. Suppose then that

is the class of the constant loop at

and

. Then
{\operatorname{hol}}([y,z])([y,a])=[z,a]=[y,\rho(z)(a)]

∎

Later on we will also need the fact that there is preferred identification between

is the constant loop at

5.2.2.
Second step

The second part of the argument, showing that every principal

-bundle is in fact the bundle of jets of fibrewise parametrisations of some vector bundle, is slightly more involved. To establish it, we note that it is enough if we prove that this is the case for the universal
G_{k,l}
-bundle

, since all others are obtained from it by pullback.

We first introduce a model for
EG_{k,l}
that is particularly suited to our arguments:

5.6
.

The space

\{j^{k}_{0}f\,\mid\,f:(\mathbb{R}^{l},0)\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{\infty},0)\text{ is an embedding with image in a linear subspace of dimension $l$}\}

is contractible and has a free
G_{k,l}
-action given by reparametrisation in the source. In particular, it is a model for
EG_{k,l}
.

Moreover, its quotient by
G_{k,l}
is the grassmannian of
l
-planes
\operatorname{Gr}(\mathbb{R}^{\infty},l)
, which is then a model for
BG_{k,l}
.

Proof.

Let us write
E
and
B
for the models of
EG_{k,l}
and

under consideration. Observe that an element
j^{k}_{0}f\in E
defines a linear subspace

; concretely,
A
is the image of the differential

. Since
f^{-1}:(A,0)\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{l},0)
is well-defined as a diffeomorphism germ, postcomposition by
j^{k}_{0}f^{-1}
defines a bijective correspondence between the
G_{k,l}
-orbit of
j^{k}_{0}f
and
G_{k,l}
itself. This proves that the action is free. Moreover, it shows that the quotient is

, the grassmannian of
l

It remains to prove contractibility. This follows immediately from the contractibility of the space of embeddings
\mathbb{R}^{l}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which in turn follows from the Whitney embedding theorem. A more down-to-earth argument goes instead as follows: We can define a deformation retraction from
E
onto its subspace
E\operatorname{GL}_{l}
using the linear interpolation

f_{s}=s.d_{0}f+(1-s)f,

for each embedding
f
, which provides an analogous interpolation at the level of
k
-jets. This homotopy is fibered over
B
. Since
E\operatorname{GL}_{l}
is contractible,
E
is contractible as well.
∎

It follows immediately from the proposition that:

5.7
.

Let
\pi:\gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{Gr}(\mathbb{R}^{\infty},l)
be the tautological vector bundle. Then:

EG_{k,l}=J^{k}\operatorname{Diff}_{\pi}(\mathbb{R}^{l},\gamma).

Moreover, since any principal
G_{k,l}
-bundle is isomorphic to the pullback of the universal one and we can use the classifying map to pull back
\gamma
:

5.8
.

Every principal
G_{k,l}
-bundle is of the form
J^{k}\operatorname{Diff}_{\pi}(\mathbb{R}^{l},E)
for some vector bundle
\pi:E\rightarrow W
.

5.2.3.
End of the surjectivity argument

Let
\rho:\pi_{1}(W,x)\rightarrow G_{k,l}
be a representation. According to Lemma
5.5
, we can use
\rho
to construct a representation
{\operatorname{hol}}:\Pi_{1}(W)\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}(P)
into the gauge groupoid of some principal
G_{k,l}
-bundle
P\rightarrow W
. Moreover, we can assume that
\rho
is precisely the restriction of
{\operatorname{hol}}
to
x
.

By Corollary
5.8
,
P
is isomorphic to
J^{k}\operatorname{Diff}_{\pi}(\mathbb{R}^{l},E)
, with
\pi:E\rightarrow W
a vector bundle of rank
l
. We fix one such isomorphism
\Phi
. As we observed after Lemma
5.5
, there is a preferred element
[[c_{x}],\textbf{{I}}]
in the fibre
P_{x}
. The isomorphism
\Phi
maps it to a preferred jet of embedding
j^{k}_{0}\phi\in J^{k}\operatorname{Diff}_{\pi}(\mathbb{R}^{l},E_{x})
.

According to Proposition
4.3
, the differential of
{\operatorname{hol}}
defines a Lie algebroid splitting
\sigma:TW\rightarrow\At(P)
. It follows that
\sigma
defines a
k
-th order foliation whose holonomy at
x
, once we conjugate with the parametrisation
j^{k}_{0}\phi
, is given by
\rho
. This means that
{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}(E,\phi,\sigma)=\rho
, concluding the proof.
∎

5.3.
Injectivity of the Riemann-Hilbert map

We will now address the injectivity half of Theorems
5.2
and
5.4
. A particular case of this argument, for simply-connected domains, appeared already in our proof of Frobenius’ Theorem
4.5
.

5.3.1.
The parametrised case

Let
(E,\phi,\sigma)
and
(E^{\prime},\phi^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
be two elements in
J^{k}{\operatorname{Fol}}_{*}(W,x,l)
with the same image
\rho:\pi_{1}(W,x)\rightarrow G_{k,l}
under
{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}
. We write
{\operatorname{hol}}:\Pi_{1}(W)\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}(P)
and
{\operatorname{hol}}^{\prime}:\Pi_{1}(W)\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}(P^{\prime})
for their holonomies.

We now define a
k
-jet of fibrewise diffeomorphism
j^{k}\Psi
from
E
to
E^{\prime}
, fixing the zero section
W
. We do this in a fibrewise manner, at each point
y\in W
. We use the formula:

j^{k}_{y}\Psi:={\operatorname{hol}}([\gamma])\circ j^{k}\phi^{\prime}\circ j^{k}\phi^{-1}\circ{\operatorname{hol}}(\bar{[\gamma]}),

where
\gamma
is some path from
x
to
y
. This expression is independent of the choice of
\gamma
, thanks to the assumption

j^{k}\phi^{-1}\circ{\operatorname{hol}}_{x}\circ j^{k}\phi=j^{k}(\phi^{\prime})^{-1}\circ{\operatorname{hol}}_{x}^{\prime}\circ j^{k}\phi^{\prime}.

By construction,
j^{k}\Psi
identifies
\sigma
with
\sigma^{\prime}
and intertwines
j^{k}\phi
with
j^{k}\phi^{\prime}
.

The proof concludes by choosing a germ of fibrewise diffeomorphism
\Psi:(E,W)\rightarrow(E^{\prime},W)
extending
j^{k}\Psi
. This follows from the fact that each
j^{k}_{y}\Psi
can be extended to a fibered-over-
W
map from
E
to
E^{\prime}
, parametrically in
y
. It is a fibrewise embedding sufficiently close to the zero section. This concludes the injectivity argument and therefore the proof of Theorem
5.2
.
∎

5.3.2.
The unparametrised Riemann-Hilbert map is well-defined

Suppose
(E,\phi,\sigma)
and
(E^{\prime},\phi^{\prime},\sigma)
are two elements in
J^{k}{\operatorname{Fol}}_{*}(W,x,l)
with the same underlying element in
J^{k}{\operatorname{Fol}}(W,x,l)
. In particular, this means that
E
and
E^{\prime}
define the same germ of manifold
A
along
W
, but the microbundle projections may differ.

We argue in two steps. First, consider
(E,\phi,\sigma)
and
(E,\phi^{\prime},\sigma)
. As
{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}(E,\phi^{\prime},\sigma)
differs from
{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}(E,\phi,\sigma)
by conjugation with
j^{k}(\phi^{-1}\circ\phi^{\prime})\in G_{k,l}
it follows that the two define the same element in the character variety
{\operatorname{Char}}(\pi_{1}(W,x),G_{k,l})
, as claimed.
It remains to show that
{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}
does not depend on the choice of microbundle structure. To do this, we need the following result (see for instance
[
Cer61
]
):

5.9
.

Let
W\subset M
be an embedded submanifold, and
A\subset M
a germ of manifold around
W
. The space of microbundle structures on
A
is contractible.

Proof.

The claim follows if we prove that it is non-empty and convex. The fact that it is non-empty follows from the existence of tubular neighbourhoods, hence we only need to address convexity. Fix an auxiliary Riemannian metric on
W
. Given two microbundle structures
\pi:A\to W
and
\pi^{\prime}:A\to W
and a sufficiently small neighbourhood of
W\subset A
, it holds that
\pi(a)
takes values in a geodesic ball of
\pi^{\prime}(a)
, for all
a
. I.e. there is a unique geodesic connecting the two. This allows us to uniquely define a linear interpolation between
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
. Sufficiently close to
W
this interpolation is through submersions which, in an even smaller neighbourhood, are microbundle structures.
∎

As there exists a homotopy of microbundle structures between
E
and
E^{\prime}
we may endow
A\times[0,1]
with this microbundle structure. We then consider the triple
(A\times[0,1],\phi^{\prime},\sigma\times[0,1])\in J^{k}{\operatorname{Fol}}_{*}(W\times[0,1],x,l)
. We can apply to it the map
{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}
, which lands in
{\operatorname{Hom}}(\pi_{1}(W\times[0,1],x),G_{k,l})
. The latter can be identified with
{\operatorname{Hom}}(\pi_{1}(W,x),G_{k,l})
, using that
W\equiv W\times\{0\}
is a deformation retract of
W\times[0,1]
. From this we deduce that

{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}(A\times[0,1],\phi^{\prime},\sigma\times[0,1])={\operatorname{RH}}_{*}(E^{\prime},\phi^{\prime},\sigma).

Consider then the germ of diffeomorphism

\phi^{\prime\prime}={\operatorname{hol}}^{\sigma}(\gamma)\circ\phi^{\prime}:(\mathbb{R}^{l},0)\rightarrow(A_{(x,1)},(x,1)),

where
\gamma
is the path
s\mapsto(x,s)
. It follows that:

{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}(A\times[0,1],\phi^{\prime},\sigma\times[0,1])={\operatorname{RH}}_{*}(E,\phi^{\prime\prime},\sigma),

as claimed. This shows that
{\operatorname{RH}}
is well-defined.
∎

5.3.3.
Injectivity in the unparametrised case

We now prove injectivity in Theorem
5.4
. Assume that
{\operatorname{RH}}(E,\sigma)={\operatorname{RH}}(E^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
. This means that we can choose auxiliary parametrisations
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
and auxiliary microbundle structures on
E
and
E^{\prime}
so that
{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}(E,\phi,\sigma)
and
{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}(E^{\prime},\phi^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
differ by an element
h
in
G_{k,l}
. It follows that
{\operatorname{RH}}_{*}(E,\phi,\sigma)={\operatorname{RH}}_{*}(E^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\circ h,\sigma^{\prime})
, so we obtain an isomorphism between the two, by Theorem
5.2
. This is in particular an isomorphism between
(E,\sigma)
and
(E^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
. The proof of Theorem
5.4
is complete.
∎

6.
Extending to higher order

One question of fundamental importance in this paper is whether a
k
-th order foliation can be extended to a
k+1
jet of foliation. That is, given a
k
-th order foliation
\sigma
does there exists a
(k+1)
-th order foliation
\tilde{\sigma}
with the same
k
-jet as
\sigma
?

In this section we will provide two cohomology classes which are the precise obstruction to extending a
k
-th order foliation. The first class is obtained from Lie algebroid extensions (Subsection
6.1
). The second class is a cohomology class in the group cohomology of the fundamental
\pi_{1}(W,x)
(Subsection
6.2
. Using integrating groupoids and Morita invariance and we will see that these two chomology maps are send to each other via the Van-Est map (Subsection
6.3
).

6.1.
Lie algebroid extensions

As the entire discussion in this section is local around
W
, we fix a tubular neighbourhood embedding
\mathcal{U}\rightarrow M
. With this choice in place, we can interchange between
k
-th order foliations and splittings
\sigma:TW\rightarrow\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
at will.

Consider a
k
-th order foliation
\sigma\in\Hol_{W}(\operatorname{Gr}(TM,l))
around
W\subset M
, and the associated singular foliation
\mathcal{A}(\sigma)
. Although
\mathcal{A}(\sigma)
can be represented by a Lie algebroid only when
W
has codimension-one, the restriction to
W
is always a Lie algebroid:

6.1
.

Let
\sigma:TW\rightarrow\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
be a
k
-th order foliation. Then:

\iota^{-1}_{W}{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)\simeq\Gamma(\At^{k+1}(\nu_{W})\oplus_{\At^{k}(\nu_{W})}TW),

with fibre-sum taken along
\sigma
.

Proof.

Sections of the right-hand side consist of
(k+1)
-jets of vector fields with the property that the
k
-jet is contained in the image of the splitting
\sigma:TW\rightarrow\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
. These are exactly the
(k+1)
-jets of vector fields
k
-tangent to
\sigma
at
W
, which is exactly
\iota^{-1}_{W}\mathcal{A}(\sigma)
.
∎

For the purpose of extending a
k
-th order foliation
\sigma
to a
(k+1)
-jet, we will only need to work with the Lie algebroid
\At^{k+1}(\nu_{W})\oplus_{\At^{k}(\nu_{W})}TW
rather then the full singular foliation. This Lie algebroid sits in the short exact sequence:

6.2
.

Let
\sigma:TW\rightarrow\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
be a
k
-th order foliation. Then there is a short exact sequence of Lie algebroids:

(6.1)

0\rightarrow\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W})\rightarrow\At^{k+1}(\nu_{W})\oplus_{\At^{k}(\nu_{W})}TW\rightarrow TW\rightarrow 0.

Proof.

We only need to proof that
\ker\rho
is as described. The kernel of
\rho
is generated precisely by the
(k+1)
-polynomial vector fields, which can in turn be identified with
\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W})
.
∎

Note that for
k\geq 1
the sub Lie algebroid
\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W})
is in fact Abelian. Consequently, by Proposition
A.1
we obtain a canonical
TW
-representation
\nabla^{1}
on
\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W})
. But the underlying
1
-th order foliation also determines a splitting
\sigma_{1}:TW\rightarrow\At_{1}(\nu_{W})
which corresponds to a linear connection on
\nu_{W}
. These connections are related:

6.3
.

Given a
k
-th order foliation with
k\geq 1
, let
(\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W}),\nabla^{1})
be the connection induced by (
6.1
) and let
(\nu_{W},\nabla^{2})
be the connection induced by the corresponding splitting
\sigma:TW\rightarrow\At(P_{1})
. Then the induced connection
\nabla^{2,\rm ind}
on
\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W})
coincides with
\nabla^{1}
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\sigma}:TW\rightarrow\At^{k+1}(\nu_{W})\oplus_{\At^{k}(\nu_{W})}TW
be any splitting of Equation (
6.1
). Then
\nabla^{1}_{X}(V)=[\tilde{\sigma}(X),V]
for all
X\in\Gamma(TW)
and
V\in\Gamma((\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W}))
. However, as
\At^{k+1}(\nu_{W})\oplus_{\At^{k}(\nu_{W})}TW
consist of
(k+1)
-jets of vector fields
[\tilde{\sigma}(X),V]=[\sigma_{1}(X),V]
. As this is precisely
\nabla_{X}^{2,\rm ind}(V)
this finishes the proof.
∎

Therefore, in what follows we may use either description of the connection on
\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W})
to endow
\Omega^{\bullet}(W;\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W}))
with a differential. We will also simply right
\nabla
for the induced flat connection on
\nu_{W}
, and all it’s tensor powers.

6.4
.

Let
\sigma:TW\rightarrow\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
be a
k
-th order foliation with
k\geq 1
. The
extension class
of
\sigma
,
c(\sigma)\in H^{2}(W;\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W}))
is defined to be the extension class of (
6.1
). That is
c(\sigma)=[\Omega]
with

\Omega(X_{1},X_{2})=[\tilde{\sigma}(X_{1}),\tilde{\sigma}(X_{2})]-\tilde{\sigma}([X_{1},X_{2}]),

for any splitting
\tilde{\sigma}
of (
6.1
) and all
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathfrak{X}(W)
.

6.0
.

As we are working with
\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
we are making use of a tubular neighbourhood embedding, however
c(\sigma)
is independent of this. Indeed, if we are to replace
\At^{k+1}(\nu_{W})\oplus_{\At^{k}(\nu_{W})}TW
in Equation
6.1
with
\iota^{-1}_{W}\mathcal{A}(\sigma)
, we see that the extension class of this sequence, which is simply
c(\sigma)
is independent of the choice of tubular neighbourhood embedding.

6.0
.

For
k=0
, a splitting of Equation (
6.1
) is precisely the data of a linear connection
\nabla
on
\nu_{W}
. The induced connection on
\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W})
obtained from Proposition
A.1
is precisely the connection induced by the linear connection. In this case, the definition of the extension class above depends on the choice of splitting. Indeed, in this case the extension class is precisely the curvature of
\nabla
.

The extension class provides the precise obstruction of lifting a
k
-th order foliation to a
(k+1)
-th order foliation.

6.5
.

A
k
-th order foliation
\sigma:TW\rightarrow\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
may be extended to a
(k+1)
-jet if and only if the extension class
c(\sigma)\in H^{2}(W;\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W}))
of (
6.1
) vanishes.

Proof.

A bracket-preserving splitting of (
6.1
) is precisely a splitting
\tilde{\sigma}:TW\rightarrow\At^{k+1}(\nu_{W})
with the property that the induced map to
\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
is
\sigma
. As the extension class measures precisely the whether such a bracket-preserving splitting exists, the conclusion is immediate.
∎

6.1.1.
Elementary modification

We now shortly specify to the case where
W=D
is of codimension-one.

Given a
k
-th order foliation
\sigma_{k}:TD\rightarrow\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
, consider the associated Lie algebroids
A(\sigma_{k})
and
A(\sigma_{k-1})
. As by Proposition
6.1

A(\sigma_{k})=\At^{k}(\nu_{W})\oplus_{\At^{k-1}(\nu_{W})}TD
the splitting
\sigma_{k}
induced in particular a bracket preserving splitting of

0\rightarrow(\nu_{D}^{*})^{k-1}\rightarrow A(\sigma_{k-1})_{k-1}|_{D}\rightarrow TD\rightarrow 0,

which we again denote by
\sigma_{k}
. This realizes
TD
as a Lie subalgebroid
\sigma_{k}(TD)\subset A(\sigma_{k-1})|_{D}
allowing us to proof the following:

6.6
.

Let
\sigma_{k}:TW\rightarrow\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
denote a
k
-th order foliation. Then we can describe
A(\sigma_{k})
as the
elementary modification
:

(6.2)

A(\sigma_{k})=[A(\sigma_{k-1}):\sigma(TW)]:=\{X\in A(\sigma_{k-1}):X|_{W}\in\Gamma(\sigma(W))\}.

Proof.

A local computation will suffice. Therefore we may assume that we have coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
such that
D=\{x_{1}=0\}
and
\sigma
is given by the level sets of
x_{1}
. The associated splitting will simply be given by
{\sigma}(TD)=\{\partial_{x_{2}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}}\}
. If
X
is a section of
A(\sigma_{k-1})
, then
X|_{D}=f|_{Z}x_{1}^{k}\partial_{x_{1}}+\cdots
. This is in
\sigma(TD)
if and only if
f
vanishes along
D
, and hence
X
is in fact in
A(\sigma_{k})
.
∎

6.2.
Group extensions

Another approach in studying the extension problem is by first applying the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence. Therefore, we will first describe the group
G_{k,l}
in a bit more detail in this section.

We again consider the group
G_{k,l}=J^{k}_{0}\operatorname{Diff}(\mathbb{R}^{l},0)
. Note that we have natural homomorphisms
\phi_{k}:G_{k,l}\rightarrow G_{k-1,l}
which forget the
k
-th order part. The composition of these maps to
G_{1,l}=\operatorname{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{l})
will be denoted by

a:G_{k,l}\to G_{1,l}=\operatorname{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{l}),

We have a short exact sequence of groups:

(6.3)

0\rightarrow\Sym^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{l,*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{l})\rightarrow G_{k+1,l}\rightarrow G_{k,l}\rightarrow 0,

where
\Sym^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{l,*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{l})
is equipped with addition.
Consequently, we obtain a
G_{k,l}
-module structure on
\Sym^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{l,*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{l})
. Explicitly, this module structure is given by

\psi\ast b=a(\psi)^{k}b,\quad\psi\in G_{k,l},b\in\Sym^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{l,*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{l}).

The extension class of (
6.3
) is the cohomology class

[G_{k+1,l}]=H^{2}(G_{k,l},\Sym^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{l,*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{l})).

By Theorem
5.4
we have that
k
-th order foliations correspond to representations of the fundamental group in
G_{k,l}
. Hence suppose that we have such a representation

\psi:\pi_{1}(W,d)\to G_{k,l}.

The extension problem is equivalent to finding a representation
\tilde{\psi}:\pi_{1}(W,d)\to G_{k+1,l}
with the property that
\phi_{k+1}(\tilde{\psi})=\psi
. This can be fully classified in terms of
[G_{k+1,l}]
and
\psi
as follows:

6.7
.

Let
\sigma:TW\rightarrow\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
be a
k
-th order foliation, with corresponding representation
\psi:\pi_{1}(W,d)\to G_{k,l}
. Then
\sigma
may be extended to a
(k+1)
-th order foliation if and only if

\psi^{*}[G_{k+1,l}]\in H^{2}(\pi_{1}(W,x),\Sym^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{l,*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{l}))

vanishes.

Proof.

The problem of lifting this to a representation into
G_{k+1,l}
is equivalent to splitting the sequence

(6.4)

1\to\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{l})\to G_{k+1,l}\times_{G_{k,l}}\pi_{1}(W,d)\to\pi_{1}(W,d)\to 1.

The extension class of this sequence is precisely
\psi^{*}[G_{k+1,l}]
which finishes the proof.
∎

Note that here
\Sym^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{l,*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{l})
becomes equipped with the module structure induced by

a\circ\psi:\pi_{1}(W,d)\to GL(\mathbb{R}^{l}).

Geometrically, this representation corresponds to
\nu_{W}
equipped with a a flat connection.

6.3.
Groupoid extensions

We have shown that both extension classes
\psi^{*}[G_{k+1,l}]\in H^{2}(\pi_{1}(W,d),\Sym^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{l,*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{l}))
and
c\in H^{2}(W;\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W}))
are the precise obstruction for extending a
k
-th order foliation to a
(k+1)
-th order foliation. We will now show that the two may in fact be related via passing to the associated integrating groupoids.

Consider a
k
-th order foliation
\sigma:TW\rightarrow\At^{k}(\nu_{W})
and the associated holonomy
\Psi:\Pi^{1}(W)\to\mathcal{G}({\operatorname{Fr}}^{k}(\nu_{W}))
. From the destription of
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)|_{W}
in Proposition
6.1
we immediately obtain:

6.8
.

The source-simply connected integration of the Lie algebroid defining
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)|_{W}
is given by

\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}(P_{k+1})\times_{\Psi}\Pi(W).

Therefore we obtain a short exact sequence of groupoids:

(6.5)

1\to\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W})\to\mathcal{G}\to\Pi(W)\to 1.

This sequence of groupoids in turn determines an extension class
[\mathcal{G}]\in H^{2}(\Pi_{1}(W);\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W}))
.
We may consider the Van-Est map

VE:H^{2}(\Pi_{1}(W);\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W}))\rightarrow H^{2}(W;\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W})).

By the proof of Theorem 5 in
[
Cra03
]
we have that
VE([\mathcal{G}])=c(\sigma_{k})\in H^{2}(W;\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W}))
.

As the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is transitive it is Morita equivalent to its restriction to a point
x\in W
, which is the isotropy group

\mathcal{G}_{x}=G_{k+1,l}\times_{G_{k,l}}\pi_{1}(W,x).

The corresponding sequence of groups has precisely extension class
\psi^{*}[G_{k+1,l}]\in H^{2}(\pi_{1}(W,x),\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W,x}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W,x}))
so we conclude:

6.9
.

Let
[\mathcal{G}]
be the extension class of (
6.5
), and
\psi^{*}[G_{k+1,l}]\in H^{2}(\Pi(W),\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W}))
the class form Theorem
6.7
. Then under the isomorphism induced by Morita equivalence

H^{2}(\pi_{1}(W,x),\mathbb{R})\cong H^{2}(\Pi(W),\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W})),

\psi^{*}[G_{k+1,l}]
is send to
[\mathcal{G}]
. We conclude that the Van-Est map pre-composed with Morita equivalence identifies
\psi^{*}[G_{k+1,l}]
with
c(\sigma)\in H^{2}(W;\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W}))

An immediate corollary of the fact that
c(\sigma)
is in the image of the Van-Est map is that:

6.10
.

The extension class
c\in H^{2}(W;\Sym^{k}(\nu_{W}^{*})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\nu_{W}))
is aspherical, that is:

\int_{S^{2}}c(\sigma)=0,

for any embedding of
S^{2}
in
W
.

Note that this also follows from Proposition
4.5
: Indeed, as
S^{2}
is simply connected and the extension class is functional we find that the the extension class vanishes when pulled back to
S^{2}
.

7.
Lie algebroid cohomology

In this section we specialise to the case of
W=D
a submanifold of codimension
1
. As we saw in Proposition
3.21
, the singular foliation
{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
, associated to a
k
-th order foliation
\sigma
, arises as the space of sections of a Lie algebroid
A(\sigma)
. In this section we will compute the cohomology of this Lie algebroid. We will first compute this cohomology in the case where the Lie algebroid is defined on the total space of a line bundle
L\to D
(Subsection
7.3
), to afterwards globalize this computation to general manifolds (Subsection
7.7
).

7.1.
The anti-canonical representation

Let
\pi:L\to D
be a line bundle over
D
. Note that
\At^{k}(L)
can be described as:

\At^{k}(L)=\At(L)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k-1}L^{-i}.

The kernel of the anchor map is
\ad({\operatorname{Fr}}^{k}(L))
, the
k
-jets of vertical vector fields that vanish along
D
. Concretely:

\ad({\operatorname{Fr}}^{k}(L))=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{k-1}L^{-i}.

The Lie bracket between vertical vector fields is given by

[-,-]:\Gamma(L^{-i})\times\Gamma(L^{-j})\to\Gamma(L^{-i-j}),\qquad[t,s]=(i-j)t\otimes s.

The algebroid
\At(L)
has a canonical representation on
L
which we denote
\nabla^{0}
. This induces a representation on all tensor powers, which we will also denote
\nabla^{0}
. The Lie bracket between
\At(L)
and
L^{-i}\subset\ad({\operatorname{Fr}}^{k}(L))
is given by these connections:

[-,-]:\Gamma(\At(L))\times\Gamma(L^{-i})\to\Gamma(L^{-i}),\qquad[X,t]=\nabla^{0}_{X}(t).

Since
\At^{k}(L)
consists of
k
-jets of vector fields, it naturally acts on the
k^{th}
order neighbourhood of
D
in
L
. This is given by

\mathcal{N}_{k}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{k}L^{-i}

and is naturally a sheaf of algebras over
D
, obtained as a quotient of the tensor algebra of
L^{-1}
.

7.1
.

The natural
\At^{k}(L)
representation on
\mathcal{N}_{k}
extends the canonical
At(L)
-representation
\nabla^{0}
on the tensor powers of
L^{-1}
. This action is given by derivations of the algebra. Explicitely, the subbundle
L^{-i}\subset\ad({\operatorname{Fr}}^{k}(L))
acts via the following formula:

\nabla^{0}:\Gamma(L^{-i})\times\Gamma(L^{-j})\to\Gamma(L^{-i-j}),\qquad\nabla^{0}_{t}(s)=jt\otimes s.

Now let

S_{k}(L)=\mathcal{N}_{k}^{*}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{k}L^{i},

which is equipped with the dual
\At^{k}(L)
-flat connection, denoted
\nabla^{AC}
. We refer to this as the
anti-canonical representation
. We can also decompose this connection into its components. The action of
t\in L^{-i}
on
v\in L^{j}
vanishes unless
i\leq j
, in which case it is given by

\nabla^{AC}_{t}(v)=(i-j)t\otimes v\in L^{j-i}.

7.2.
Maurer-Cartan element

Now let
\sigma:TD\rightarrow\At^{k}(L)
be a
k
-th order foliation. This corresponds to a flat
TD
-connection
\nabla
on
L
as well as the data of
1
-forms
(0,\eta_{1},...,\eta_{k-1})\in\Omega^{1}(D;\oplus_{i=0}^{k-1}L^{-i})
satisfying the Maurer-Cartan equation. The anti-canonical connection pulls back to define a flat
TD
-connection
\sigma^{*}(\nabla^{AC})
on
S_{k}(L)
. This turns
\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}(S_{k}(L))
into an
\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}
-dg-module. The differential of a section
t_{r}\in\Gamma(L^{r})
is given by

(7.1)

d(t_{r})=d^{\nabla}t_{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}(i-r)\eta_{i}\otimes t_{r},

where
\eta_{i}\otimes t_{r}\in\Omega_{D}^{1}(L^{r-i})
.

7.3.
Algebroid differential forms

Now consider the
b^{k+1}
-algebroid
A(\sigma)
on the total space of
L
which is associated to
\sigma
. Its sections are generated by the vector fields

\sigma(X)=\nabla_{X}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\eta_{i}(X),

for
X\in\mathfrak{X}(D)
, as well as the vertical vector fields coming from
L^{-k}
. Here,
\nabla_{X}
is a linear vector field on
L
and
\eta_{i}(X)\in L^{-i}=L^{-i-1}\otimes L
is a vector field of the form

f(x)z^{i+1}\partial_{z},

where
z
is a vertical coordinate. The sections of
L^{-k}=L^{-k-1}\otimes L
correspond to vector fields of the form

z^{k+1}\partial_{z}.

Now we describe the complex of algebroid differential forms for
A(\sigma)
. First, we use the linear
TD
-connection
\nabla
to describe the differential forms on
L
. The linear connection defines a direct sum decomposition

TL\cong\pi^{*}(TD)\oplus\pi^{*}(L).

A pair
(X,s)
defines the vector field
\nabla_{X}+s
. Dualizing, we get

T^{*}L\cong\pi^{*}(T^{*}D)\oplus\pi^{*}(L^{-1}).

Given a form
\beta\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}
, we abuse notation and let
\beta
denote the pullback
\pi^{*}(\beta)\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{L}
. In this way, viewing
L^{-i}
as a subset of the functions on
L\setminus D
, the sections of
\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}\otimes L^{-i}
may be viewed as differential forms on
L\setminus D
.

Given a section
s\in L^{-1}
, denote the corresponding vertical
1
-form by
s_{\vertrm}\in\Omega^{1}_{L}
. More generally, we can factor
L^{-i}=L^{-i+1}\otimes L^{-1}
. Hence, for
s\in L^{-i}
, we write
s_{\vertrm}\in\Omega^{1}_{L}
when we view the first factor as a function on
L\setminus D
and the second factor as a vertical
1
-form.

7.2
.

Let
s\in L^{-i}
, viewed as a function on
L\setminus D
. Then

ds=\nabla(s)+is_{\vertrm},

where
\nabla(s)\in\Omega^{1}_{D}\otimes L^{-i}
.

7.3
.

Let
s_{\vertrm}\in L^{-i}=L^{-i+1}\otimes L^{-1}
be a vertical
1
-form. Then

d(s_{\vertrm})=(\nabla(s))_{\vertrm}\in\Omega^{1}_{D}\otimes L^{i}.

We now use the data of
\sigma
to construct algebroid forms.

7.4
.

Given a section
s\in L^{r}
, for
0\leq r\leq k
, the following expression defines an algebroid
1
-form with a pole of order
r+1
along
D
:

\tau_{r}(s)=s_{\vertrm}-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\eta_{i}\otimes s\in\Omega^{1}(A(\sigma)).

In other words, we may view
\tau_{r}\in\Omega^{1}(A(\sigma))\otimes\pi^{*}L^{-r}
.

7.0
.

Note that although
\tau_{r}(s)
has a pole when viewed as a differential form on
L
, when viewed as an algebroid
1
-form for
A(\sigma)
it is perfectly smooth.

7.0
.

Let
z
be a local flat section of
L^{-1}
, which also serves as a vertical coordinate on
L
. We may locally write
\eta_{i}=a_{i}z^{i}
, for
a_{i}\in\Omega_{D}^{1}
. Then

\tau_{r}(z^{-r})=\frac{dz}{z^{r+1}}-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\frac{a_{i}}{z^{r-i}}.\hfill

Now we can describe the dual space
A(\sigma)^{*}
.

7.5
.

Let
\beta_{1},...,\beta_{n}
be a local basis of
T^{*}D
and let
s\in L^{k}
be a non-vanishing section. Then a local basis of
A(\sigma)^{*}
is given by the forms
\beta_{i}
along with
\tau_{k}(s)
.

Proof.

Let
X_{i}
be a basis of vector fields on
D
which is dual to the forms
\beta_{i}
and let
t=s^{-1}\in L^{-k}
. Then
\sigma(X_{i})
along with
t
, viewed as a vertical vector field, form a local basis of
A(\sigma)
. The above one forms are the dual basis.
∎

Using the local basis of forms provided by Lemma
7.5
, we can write an arbitrary form
\omega\in\Omega^{\bullet}(A(\sigma))
as follows:

\omega=\alpha\wedge\tau_{k}(s)+\beta,

where both
\alpha
and
\beta
are smooth ‘horizontal’ forms (i.e. generated by the
\beta_{i}
). We can expand the form
\alpha
as

\alpha=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+...+\alpha_{k}+\tilde{\alpha}_{k+1},

where
\alpha_{i}\in\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}\otimes L^{-i}
and
\tilde{\alpha}_{k+1}
vanishes to order at least
k+1
along
D
. Hence
\tilde{\alpha}_{k+1}\wedge\tau_{k}(s)
is a smooth form and so

\omega=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}\wedge\tau_{k}(s)+\gamma,

with
\gamma=\tilde{\alpha}_{k+1}\wedge\tau_{k}(s)+\beta
a smooth form on
L
. Now let
t\in L^{-1}
be a local basis, so that
\alpha_{i}=\phi_{i}\otimes t^{i}
, with
\phi_{i}\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}
. Then

\alpha_{i}\wedge\tau_{k}(s)=\phi_{i}\wedge\tau_{r}(t^{i}s)-\phi_{i}\wedge\sum_{j=r}^{k-1}\eta_{j}\otimes(t^{i}s),

where
r=k-i
and the second summand is smooth. Hence,

\omega=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\phi_{i}\wedge\tau_{k-i}(t^{i}s)+\gamma^{\prime},

where
\gamma^{\prime}
is a smooth form on
L
.

7.6
.

The following morphism is an isomorphism of vector spaces

\tau:\Omega^{\bullet}_{L}\oplus\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k}\Omega^{\bullet-1}_{D}(L^{r})\to\Omega^{\bullet}(A(\sigma)),\qquad(\beta,\sum_{r=0}^{k}\phi_{r}\otimes u^{(r)})\mapsto\beta+\sum_{r=0}^{k}\phi_{r}\wedge\tau_{r}(u^{(r)}).

Proof.

Showing that
\tau
is injective is straightforward. We have shown above that
\tau
is surjective locally on
D
. From this we get global surjectivity using a partition of unity.
∎

Observe that the second summand
\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k}\Omega^{\bullet-1}_{D}(L^{r})
is nothing by
\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(S_{k}(L))[-1]
, the graded vector space underlying the anticanonical dg module of Section
7.2
with the degrees shifted by
1
. Then, the map
\tau
may be upgraded to an isomorphism of cochain complexes:

7.7
.

The morphism
\tau:\Omega_{L}^{\bullet}\oplus\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(S_{k}(L))[-1]\to\Omega^{\bullet}(A(\sigma))
is a chain map.

Proof.

It suffices to check that
d\tau_{r}(u)=\tau(du)
for
u\in L^{r}
. Starting from the right hand side, with the expression for
du
given by Equation
7.1
, we get

\displaystyle\tau(du)

\displaystyle=\tau_{r}(d^{\nabla}u)+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}(i-r)\tau_{r-i}(\eta_{i}\otimes u)

\displaystyle=(d^{\nabla}u+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}(i-r)\eta_{i}\otimes u)_{\vertrm}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\eta_{i}\wedge d^{\nabla}u-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\sum_{j=1}^{r-i-1}(i-r)\eta_{i}\wedge\eta_{j}\otimes u

\displaystyle=(d^{\nabla}u+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}(i-r)\eta_{i}\otimes u)_{\vertrm}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\eta_{i}\wedge d^{\nabla}u-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s=1}^{r-1}\sum_{i+j=s}(i-j)\eta_{i}\wedge\eta_{j}\otimes u

\displaystyle=(d^{\nabla}u+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}(i-r)\eta_{i}\otimes u)_{\vertrm}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\eta_{i}\wedge d^{\nabla}u-\sum_{s=1}^{r-1}d^{\nabla}\eta_{s}\otimes u

\displaystyle=(d^{\nabla}u+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}(i-r)\eta_{i}\otimes u)_{\vertrm}-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}d^{\nabla}(\eta_{i}\otimes u)

\displaystyle=d\tau_{r}(u).

In the fourth equality we have used the Maurer-Cartan equation and in the last equation we have used Lemmas
7.2
and
7.3
.
∎

7.8
.

There is a canonical isomorphism

H^{\bullet}(A(\sigma))\cong H^{\bullet}(D)\oplus H^{\bullet-1}(D,S_{k}(L)).

We now prove a technical lemma.

7.9
.

Let
\alpha=\sum_{r=0}^{k}\alpha_{r}\in\Omega_{D}^{i-1}(S_{k}(L))
be a closed element. Let
h\in L^{-2}
be a metric and let
\gamma\in\Omega_{D}^{1}
be the closed
1
-form such that
\nabla(h)=-2\gamma\otimes h
. Then

\tau(\alpha)|_{L\setminus D}=\alpha_{0}\wedge\gamma+d\eta,

where
\eta=(-1)^{i}(\sum_{s=1}^{k}s^{-1}\alpha_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\log(h)\alpha_{0})
is a horizontal form with singularities along
D
.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\tau(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{k}\tau_{r}(\alpha_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{k}\alpha_{r,\vertrm}+(-1)^{i}\sum_{r=0}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\eta_{j}\wedge\alpha_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{k}\alpha_{s,\vertrm}+(-1)^{i}\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}\sum_{r=s+1}^{k}\eta_{r-s}\wedge\alpha_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{k}\alpha_{s,\vertrm}+(-1)^{i}\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{-1}d^{\nabla}\alpha_{s}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0,\vertrm}+\sum_{s=1}^{k}s^{-1}(-1)^{i}d\alpha_{s}.

In the fourth equality we have used Proposition
4.9
and in the last equality we have used Lemma
7.2
to show that

(-1)^{i}d\alpha_{s}=s\alpha_{s,\vertrm}+(-1)^{i}d^{\nabla}\alpha_{s}.

Now
\alpha_{0}\in\Omega_{D}^{i-1}
and so
\alpha_{0,\vertrm}=\alpha_{0}\wedge\epsilon
, where
\epsilon=1_{\vertrm}
, viewing
1\in L^{1}\otimes L^{-1}
. This can be constructed from the metric
h
as
\epsilon=\frac{h_{\vertrm}}{h}
. Now using Lemma
7.2
we compute

d\log(h)=\frac{\nabla(h)+2h_{\vertrm}}{h}=-2\gamma+2\epsilon.

Hence
\epsilon=\gamma+\frac{1}{2}d\log h
, and so

\alpha_{0,\vertrm}=\alpha_{0}\wedge\gamma-\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{i}d(\log h\alpha_{0}).\qed

Notice that
\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}\oplus\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(S_{k}(L))[-1]
is a quasi-isomorphic subcomplex of
\Omega^{\bullet}(A(\sigma))
.

7.10
.

The (isomorphic) image of
\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}\oplus\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(S_{k}(L))[-1]
under
\tau
is a quasi-isomorphic subalgebra of
\Omega^{\bullet}(A(\sigma))
. With respect to this algebra structure,
\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}
is a subalgebra with its usual structure and
\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(S_{k}(L))[-1]
is an ideal. Hence,
\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(S_{k}(L))[-1]
is an
\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}
-cdga. The dg-module structure is the usual one, and the product is given by the following formula:

t_{s}\ast t_{r}=\sum_{i=s}^{r-1}\eta_{i}\otimes t_{s}\otimes t_{r}.

where
s<r
and
t_{r}\in L^{r}
and
t_{s}\in L^{s}
.

Proof.

Consider two elements
(\beta_{1},\sum_{r=0}^{k}\phi_{r}\otimes u_{r})
and
(\beta_{2},\sum_{s=0}^{k}\psi_{s}\otimes v_{s})
. Computing their product, we get

\displaystyle\tau(\beta_{1},\sum_{r=0}^{k}\phi_{r}\otimes u_{r})\wedge\tau(\beta_{2},\sum_{s=0}^{k}\psi_{s}\otimes v_{s})

\displaystyle=(\beta_{1}+\sum_{r=0}^{k}\phi_{r}\wedge\tau_{r}(u_{r}))\wedge(\beta_{2}+\sum_{s=0}^{k}\psi_{s}\wedge\tau_{s}(v_{s}))

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}\wedge\beta_{2}+\sum_{s=0}^{k}\beta_{1}\wedge\psi_{s}\wedge\tau_{s}(v_{s})+(-1)^{|\beta_{2}|}\sum_{r=0}^{k}\phi_{r}\wedge\beta_{2}\wedge\tau_{r}(u_{r})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{r,s=0}^{k}(-1)^{|\psi_{s}|}\phi_{r}\wedge\psi_{s}\wedge\tau_{r}(u_{r})\wedge\tau_{s}(v_{s})

\displaystyle=\tau(\beta_{1}\wedge\beta_{2},\sum_{s=0}^{k}\beta_{1}\wedge\psi_{s}\otimes v_{s}+(-1)^{|\beta_{2}|}\sum_{r=0}^{k}\phi_{r}\wedge\beta_{2}\otimes u_{r})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{r,s=0}^{k}(-1)^{|\psi_{s}|}\phi_{r}\wedge\psi_{s}\wedge\tau_{r}(u_{r})\wedge\tau_{s}(v_{s}).

It thus remains to compute the formula for
\tau_{s}(v_{s})\wedge\tau_{r}(u_{r})
. We can assume that
s<r
(otherwise, just flip the order at the cost of a sign). Computing this we get

\displaystyle\tau_{s}(v_{s})\wedge\tau_{r}(u_{r})

\displaystyle=(v_{s,\vertrm}-\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\eta_{i}\otimes v_{s})\wedge(u_{r,\vertrm}-\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\eta_{j}\otimes u_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\eta_{j}\wedge(v_{s}\otimes u_{r})_{\vertrm}-\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\eta_{i}\wedge(v_{s}\otimes u_{r})_{\vertrm}+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\eta_{i}\wedge\eta_{j}\otimes(v_{s}\otimes u_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=s}^{r-1}\eta_{j}\wedge(v_{s}\otimes u_{r})_{\vertrm}-\sum_{j=s}^{r-1}\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\eta_{j}\wedge\eta_{i}\otimes(v_{s}\otimes u_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=s}^{r-1}\eta_{j}\wedge((v_{s}\otimes u_{r})_{\vertrm}-\sum_{i=1}^{s+r-j-1}\eta_{i}\otimes(v_{s}\otimes u_{r}))+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq s,\\
i+j\leq r+s-1\end{subarray}}\eta_{j}\wedge\eta_{i}\otimes(v_{s}\otimes u_{r})

\displaystyle=\tau(\sum_{j=s}^{r-1}\eta_{j}\otimes v_{s}\otimes u_{r}).

Here we have used
\eta_{j}\otimes v_{s}\otimes u_{r}\in\Omega_{D}^{1}\otimes L^{s+r-j}
and the fact that symmetric sums of
\eta_{j}\wedge\eta_{i}
vanish.
∎

7.4.
A spectral sequence

Note that
\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}(S_{k-1}(L))[-1]\subset\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}(S_{k}(L))[-1]
is a subcomplex for all
k
. This gives rise to the following bounded decreasing filtration
F^{\bullet}
on
\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}(S_{k}(L))[-1]
:

F^{p}\big(\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}(S_{k}(L))[-1]\big)=\begin{cases}\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}(S_{k}(L))[-1]&p\leq-k\\
\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}(S_{-p}(L))[-1]&-k\leq p\leq 0\\
0&p>0\end{cases}

7.11
.

The filtration
F^{\bullet}
gives rise to a spectral sequence
E
which converges to the cohomology
H^{\bullet-1}_{D}(S_{k}(L))
. The
E_{1}
page is given by the cohomology of the local systems
L^{i}
:

E_{1}^{p,q}=H^{p+q-1}(L^{-p}),\qquad p=0,...,-k.

Proof.

Because the filtration is bounded, the spectral sequence converges to the cohomology. The
E_{0}
page of the spectral sequence is given by the associated graded of the complex. Hence, for
p=0,...,-k
we have

E_{0}^{p,q}=\frac{F^{p}\big(\Omega_{D}^{p+q}(S_{k}(L))[-1]\big)}{F^{p+1}\big(\Omega_{D}^{p+q}(S_{k}(L))[-1]\big)}\cong\Omega_{D}^{p+q-1}(L^{-p}),

and
E_{0}^{p,q}=0
for all other values of
p
. The differential on the complex is given by Equation
7.1
. The portion that survives after taking the associated graded is given by
d^{\nabla}
, the connection for the local systems
L^{i}
. Hence, the
E_{1}
page is given by

E_{1}^{p,q}=H_{D}^{p+q-1}(L^{-p})

for
p=0,...,-k
and
E_{1}^{p,q}=0
for all other values of
p
. ∎

7.5.
The algebra in the case of the trivial line bundle

We study the special case where
L
is the trivial bundle with trivial local system. Let
t_{r}\in L^{r}
be the unit section. Hence
t_{r}\otimes t_{s}\cong t_{r+s}
. Now we prove a technical lemma that will be useful in computing examples.

Let
(C^{\bullet},d)\subset(\Omega^{\bullet}_{D},d)
be a quasi-isomorphic sub-cdga. Assume that the Maurer-Cartan elements have the form
\eta_{i}=\alpha_{i}\otimes t_{-i}
for
\alpha_{i}\in C^{1}
. Define

C^{\bullet}(\sigma)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{k}(C^{\bullet}\otimes t_{i}[-1]),

where we now view
t_{i}
to have degree
+1
. It is straightforward to check that
C^{\bullet}(\sigma)
is a sub-cdga of
\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(S_{k}(L))[-1]
.

7.12
.

The cdga
C^{\bullet}(\sigma)
is a quasi-isomorphic subalgebra of
\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(S_{k}(L))[-1]
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the inclusion, denoted
i
, is a quasi-isomorphism. Note first that the filtration
F^{\bullet}
induces a filtration on
C^{\bullet}(\sigma)
and hence the inclusion induces a morphism between the associated spectral sequences. The map on the
E_{1}
-page is given by

i:H^{p+q-1}(C^{\bullet})\to H^{p+q-1}_{D}.

This is an isomorphism because we have assumed that
C^{\bullet}
is a quasi-isomorphic subcomplex of
\Omega_{D}^{\bullet}
. Hence, by the Eilenberg-Moore Comparison theorem
[
Wei94
]
,
i:C^{\bullet}(\sigma)\to\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(S_{k}(L))[-1]
is a quasi-isomorphism.
∎

7.5.1.
An Example

We continue with the example of Heisenberg group from Example
2.4
. So recall that
H(\mathbb{R})
denotes the Heisenberg group,
H(\mathbb{Z})
the subgroup consisting of matrices with integer entries and
X=H(\mathbb{R})/H(\mathbb{Z})
the quotient. Let
a=dx
,
b=dy
and
c=dz-ydx
. These descend to
1
-forms in
\Omega^{1}(X)
and satisfy
da=db=0
and
dc=a\wedge b
. Let

C=\mathbb{R}[a,b,c]

be the induced subalgebra of
\Omega^{\bullet}(X)
. Then by the Gysin sequence,
C
is quasi-isomorphic to
\Omega^{\bullet}(X)
. The cohomology is given by

H^{\bullet}(C)=\langle 1\rangle\oplus\langle a,b\rangle\oplus\langle ac,bc\rangle\oplus\langle abc\rangle.

Choose Maurer-Cartan elements given by

\eta_{1}=a\otimes t_{-1},\qquad\eta_{2}=b\otimes t_{-2},\qquad\eta_{3}=-c\otimes t_{-3}.

This gives rise to the cdga

C(\sigma)=\mathbb{R}[a,b,c]\langle t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\rangle,

with differentials

dt_{0}=0,\ \ dt_{1}=0,\ \ dt_{2}=-at_{1},\ \ dt_{3}=-2at_{2}-bt_{1},\ \ dt_{4}=-3at_{3}-2bt_{2}+ct_{1},

and products

\displaystyle t_{0}t_{1}

\displaystyle=0,\ \ t_{0}t_{2}=at_{1},\ \ t_{0}t_{3}=at_{2}+bt_{1},\ \ t_{0}t_{4}=at_{3}+bt_{2}-ct_{1},\ \ t_{1}t_{2}=at_{2},

\displaystyle t_{1}t_{3}

\displaystyle=at_{3}+bt_{2},\ \ t_{1}t_{4}=at_{4}+bt_{3}-ct_{2},\ \ t_{2}t_{3}=bt_{3},\ \ t_{2}t_{4}=bt_{4}-ct_{3},\ \ t_{3}t_{4}=-ct_{4}.

This algebra is quasi-isomorphic to
\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}(S_{4}(L))[-1]
by Lemma
7.12
. Using the spectral sequence, we compute the cohomology algebra. It is given by

H^{\bullet}(C(\sigma))=H^{\bullet}(C)t_{0}\bigoplus\big(\langle t_{1}\rangle\oplus\langle bt_{1},bt_{2}+ct_{1},at_{4}+\frac{1}{2}bt_{3}-ct_{2}\rangle\oplus\langle bct_{1},act_{3}+bct_{2},act_{4}+bct_{3}\rangle\oplus\langle abct_{4}\rangle\big).

Computing the products of this algebra we find that only two of them are non-vanishing. They are given by

t_{1}\ast(at_{4}+\frac{1}{2}bt_{3}-ct_{2})=\frac{1}{6}bct_{1},\qquad(at_{4}+\frac{1}{2}bt_{3}-ct_{2})^{2}=abct_{4}.

The dimensions of the cohomology of the associated
b^{5}
-algebroid are given by

\dim(H^{i}(A(\sigma)))=1,\ \ 4,\ \ 7,\ \ 6,\ \ 2.

7.6.
Universal algebra for foliations

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{k}=Lie(G_{k,1})
, which has basis
z\partial_{z},z^{2}\partial_{z},...,z^{k}\partial_{z}
. Let
x_{0},x_{1},...,x_{k-1}
be the dual basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{k}^{*}
. Then the Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra of this Lie algebra is given by

CE(\mathfrak{g}_{k})=\mathbb{R}[x_{0},...,x_{k-1}],

where each
x_{r}
has degree

Group and Lie algebroid extensions

In this section we recall some general theory for the extension of groups, Lie algebroids and Lie groupoids. For a detailed account on Lie algebroid extensions see
[

A.1.
Group extensions

We consider group extensions of the form

1\rightarrow A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow 1,

with
A
Abelian. Let
\sigma:C\rightarrow B
be any set-theoretic splitting, then we may define a
C
-module structure:

c\cdot a:=\sigma(c)\cdot a\cdot\sigma(c)^{-1}

This structure does not depend on the choice of splitting as
A
is abelian. Consequently, one may consider the complex of group-cochains with values in
A
:
\Omega^{k}(C;A)
.

Given any splitting
\sigma:C\rightarrow B
we may consider

C_{\sigma}(c_{1},c_{2})=\sigma(c_{1}c_{2})^{-1}\sigma(c_{1})\sigma(c_{2}).

It then follows that using
\sigma
,
B
is isomorphic to
C\ltimes_{C_{\sigma}}A
which is
C\times A
as a set and has group structure given by

(c_{1},a_{1})\cdot(c_{2},a_{2})=(c_{1}\cdot c_{2},c_{1}^{-1}\cdot a_{1}\cdot a_{2}\cdot(c_{1}c_{2})^{-1}\cdot C_{\sigma}(c_{1},c_{2})).

The cohomology class
[C_{\sigma}]\in H^{2}(C;A)
is independent on the choice of splitting, and vanishes precisely when the extension is split, that is
B
is isomorphic to
A\rtimes C
as groups.

A.2.
Lie algebroid extensions

The following result is well-known, but for completeness-sake we will still provide a proof:

A.1
.

Suppose that we have a short exact sequence of Lie algebroids:

0\rightarrow B\rightarrow A\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0,

with
B
contained in
\ker\rho(A)
. Then:

(1)

Any vector bundle splitting
\sigma:C\rightarrow A
induces a
C
-connection on
B
, given by
\nabla_{c}(b):=[\sigma(c),b]
.

(2)

If
\sigma
is a bracket preserving splitting
\Omega^{\bullet}(C;B)
obtains the structure of a dgla. If
\tilde{\sigma}
is any other bracket preserving splitting then
\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}\in{\rm MC}(\Omega^{\bullet}(C,B))
.

(3)

When
B
is abelian, the representation
\nabla
does not depend on the choice of splitting
\sigma
.

Proof.

1) We have that

\nabla_{c}(fb)=[\sigma(c),fb]=f[\sigma(c),b]+\mathcal{L}_{\rho_{A}(\sigma(c))}(b)=f[\sigma(c),b]+\mathcal{L}_{\rho_{C}(c)}(b),

and

\nabla_{fc}(b)=[f\sigma(c),b]=f[\sigma(c),b]-\mathcal{L}_{\rho_{B}(b)}(f)c=f[\sigma(c),b],

as
B
has trivial anchor.

2) We have to show that

d^{\nabla}(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})+\frac{1}{2}[\sigma-\tilde{\sigma},\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}]=0.

We have

\displaystyle d^{\nabla}(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X}(\sigma(Y)-\tilde{\sigma}(Y))-\nabla_{Y}(\sigma(X)-\tilde{\sigma}(Y))-\sigma([X,Y])+\tilde{\sigma}([X,Y])

\displaystyle=[\sigma(X),\sigma(Y)-\tilde{\sigma}(Y)]-[\sigma(Y),\sigma(X)-\tilde{\sigma}(X)]-\sigma([X,Y])+\tilde{\sigma}([X,Y])

\displaystyle=[\sigma(X),\sigma(Y)]-[\sigma(X),\tilde{\sigma}(Y)]-[\sigma(Y),\sigma(X)]+[\sigma(Y),\tilde{\sigma}(X)]-\sigma([X,Y])+\tilde{\sigma}([X,Y])

\displaystyle=C_{\sigma}(X,Y)+[\sigma(X),\sigma(Y)]-[\sigma(X),\tilde{\sigma}(Y)]+[\sigma(Y),\tilde{\sigma}(X)]+\tilde{\sigma}([X,Y]),

whereas

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\sigma-\tilde{\sigma},\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}]

\displaystyle=[\sigma(X)-\tilde{\sigma}(X),\sigma(Y)-\tilde{\sigma}(Y)]

\displaystyle=[\sigma(X),\sigma(Y)]-[\sigma(X),\tilde{\sigma}(Y)]-[\tilde{\sigma}(X),\sigma(Y)]+[\tilde{\sigma}(X),\tilde{\sigma}(Y)].

From this we conclude that

d^{\nabla}(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})-\frac{1}{2}[\sigma-\tilde{\sigma},\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}]=C_{\sigma}+C_{\tilde{\sigma}}.

3)
As
\sigma(c)-\tilde{\sigma}(c)
lies in
B
as the sequence is exact it follows that
[\sigma(c)-\tilde{\sigma}(c),b]=0
for all
b\in B
. Consequently
\nabla_{c}(b)
does not depend on the choice of splitting.
∎

Given a short exact sequence of Lie algebroids, and a vector bundle splitting

Now let
F
be a foliation with trivial variation. Let
\theta\in\Omega_{X}^{1}(\nu)
be the defining
1
-form and let
\nabla
be a flat connection such that
d^{\nabla}\theta=0
. Now let
\alpha\in H^{0}_{F}(\nu^{*})
. Namely,
\alpha\in\nu^{*}\subset\Omega_{X}^{1}
such that
d\alpha=0
. Then
\alpha\wedge\theta=0
and hence
d^{\tilde{\nabla}}(\theta)=0
, where
\tilde{\nabla}=\nabla+\alpha
.

Let
t_{\alpha}\in\nu^{*}
be the section
\alpha
but viewed as a section of the line bundle rather than a
1
-form. Then

\alpha=\langle t_{\alpha},\theta\rangle,

and

0=d\alpha=\langle d^{\nabla}t_{\alpha},\theta\rangle+\langle t_{\alpha},d^{\nabla}\theta\rangle=\langle d^{\nabla}t_{\alpha},\theta\rangle.

This means that
d^{\nabla}t_{\alpha}\in\nu^{*}\otimes\nu^{*}
. Hence,

d^{\nabla}t_{\alpha}=\langle u_{\alpha},\theta\rangle,

with
u_{\alpha}\in\nu^{-2}
.

Now suppose that
[\theta]=0\in H^{1}_{X}(\nu,\nabla)
. This means that there is some section
s\in\nu
such that

\nabla(s)=\theta.

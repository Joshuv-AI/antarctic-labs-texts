Introduction

Kac and Wakimoto introduced the notion of admissible highest weight representations for affine Lie (super)algebras to study the modular invariant representations of affine Lie (super)algebras in
[
17
]
,
then they classified the admissible weights for all affine Lie algebras in
[
18
]
.
Admissible modules also studied in the context of vertex operator algebras, in
[
3
]
,
Adamović and Milas proved the simple affine vertex operator algebra

L_{\widehat{sl_{2}}}(\mathcal{l},0)
at admissible level is rational in the category
\mathcal{O}
,
and conjectured this holds for simple affine vertex operator algebras
L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{l},0)

associated to any simple finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
This conjecture has been proved by Arakawa in
[
1

However, simple affine vertex operator algebras
L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{l},0)

at admissible level
\mathcal{l}
may not be rational if
\mathcal{l}
is not a positive integer.
In
[
7
]
, Dong, Li and Mason showed that
L_{\widehat{sl_{2}}}(\mathcal{l},0)
is a
rational
\mathbb{Q}
-graded vertex operator algebra
under a new Virasoro element and that the irreducible modules are exactly the admissible modules for
\widehat{sl_{2}}
.
Furthermore, these results has been proved by Lin for

L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{l},0)
associated to any simple finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
in
[
23
]
.
For superalgebra case,
Wood classified the irreducible relaxed highest-weight modules for the simple affine vertex operator superalgebra
L_{\widehat{osp(1|2)}}(\mathcal{l},0)
at admissible level,
and also proved that
L_{\widehat{osp(1|2)}}(\mathcal{l},0)
is rational in the category
\mathcal{O}
in
[
25
]
.
Gorelik and Serganova later
showed the rationality in the category
\mathcal{O}
for

L_{\widehat{osp(1|2n)}}(\mathcal{l},0)
in
[
13
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}={osp}(1|2)=\mbox{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{h,e,f,x,y\}
,
where
\{h,e,f,x,y\}
is the standard Chevalley basis of
\mathfrak{g}
.
In our paper we study the simple affine vertex operator superalgebra

\mathcal{l}
.
Let

This also obtained in

2.1
Vertex operator superalgebras and their modules

Let
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}

the elements in
V_{\bar{0}}
(resp.
V_{\bar{1}}

even
(resp.

).
For any

with

|v|=i
.
First we recall the definitions of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vertex operator superalgebra
and their various module categories following

20

Definition 2.1
.

A vertex superalgebra is a quadruple
(V,\textbf{1},D,Y)
,
where
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
is a vector superspace,

D
is an endomorphism of
V
,
1
is a specified even vector called the vacuum vector of
V
,
and
Y
is a linear map

\displaystyle Y(\cdot,z):

\displaystyle V\rightarrow(\mbox{End}V)[[z,z^{-1}]]

\displaystyle a\mapsto Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}z^{-n-1}~~(a_{n}\in\mbox{End}V)

such that

(1) For any
a,b\in V
,
a_{n}b=0
for
n
sufficiently large;

(2)
[D,Y(a,z)]=Y(D(a),z)=\frac{d}{dz}Y(a,z)
for any
a\in V
;

(3)
Y(\textbf{1},z)=\mbox{Id}_{V}
;

(4)
Y(a,z)\textbf{1}\in(\mbox{End}V)[[z]]
and
\mbox{lim}_{z\rightarrow 0}Y(a,z)\textbf{1}=a
for any
a\in V
;

(5) For
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-homogeneous elements
a,b\in V
, the following Jacobi identity holds:

\displaystyle z_{0}^{-1}\delta(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}})Y(a,z_{1})Y(b,z_{2})-(-1)^{|a||b|}z_{0}^{-1}\delta(\frac{z_{2}-z_{1}}{-z_{0}})Y(b,z_{2})Y(a,z_{1})

\displaystyle=z_{2}^{-1}\delta(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}})Y(Y(a,z_{0})b,z_{2}).

A vertex superalgebra
V
is called a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vertex operator superalgebra
if there is an even vector
\omega
called the Virasoro element of
V
such that

(6) Set
Y(\omega,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L(n)z^{-n-2}
, for any
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

[L(m),L(n)]=(m-n)L(m+n)+\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}\delta_{m+n,0}c,

where
c\in\mathbb{C}
is called the central charge;

(7)
L(-1)=D
;

(8)
V
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded such that
V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{(n)}
,

L(0)\mid_{V_{(n)}}=n\mbox{Id}_{V_{(n)}}
,
\mbox{dim}~V_{(n)}<\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and

V_{(n)}=0
for
n
sufficiently small. For
v\in V_{(n)}
,
the conformal weight of
v
is defined to be
\mbox{wt}~v=n
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
V
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vertex operator superalgebra.
A weak
V
-module is a triple
(M,d,Y_{M})
where

M=M_{\bar{0}}\oplus M_{\bar{1}}
is a vector supersapce,

d
is an endomorphism of
M

and
Y_{M}
is a linear map

\displaystyle Y_{M}(\cdot,z):

\displaystyle V\rightarrow(\mbox{End}M)[[z,z^{-1}]]

\displaystyle a\mapsto Y_{M}(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}z^{-n-1}~~(a_{n}\in\mbox{End}M)

such that

(1) For any
a\in V,u\in M
,
a_{n}u=0
for
n
sufficiently large;

(2)
[d,Y_{M}(a,z)]=Y_{M}(D(a),z)=\frac{d}{dz}Y_{M}(a,z)
for any
a\in V
;

(3)
Y_{M}(\textbf{1},z)=\mbox{Id}_{M}
;

(4) For
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-homogeneous elements
a,b\in V
, the following Jacobi identity holds:

\displaystyle z_{0}^{-1}\delta(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}})Y_{M}(a,z_{1})Y_{M}(b,z_{2})-(-1)^{|a||b|}z_{0}^{-1}\delta(\frac{z_{2}-z_{1}}{-z_{0}})Y_{M}(b,z_{2})Y_{M}(a,z_{1})

\displaystyle=z_{2}^{-1}\delta(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}})Y_{M}(Y(a,z_{0})b,z_{2}).

A weak
V
-module
M
is called an admissible module (
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-graded weak module)
if
M
has a
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-gradation
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}M(n)
such that

a_{m}M(n)\subseteq M(\mbox{wt}~a+n-m-1)

for any
\mathbb{Z}
-homogeneous element
a\in V,m\in\mathbb{Z},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

A weak
V
-module
M
is called an ordinary module
if
M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}M_{\lambda}
, where
M_{\lambda}=\{w\in M\mid L(0)w=\lambda w\}
such that

\mbox{dim}~M_{\lambda}<\infty
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and

M_{\lambda}=0
for the real part of
\lambda
sufficiently small.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
V
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vertex operator superalgebra and
(W_{i},Y_{i}),(W_{j},Y_{j})
,
(W_{k},Y_{k})
be three weak

-modules.
An intertwining operator of type

W_{j}~~W_{k}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}(\cdot,z):

\displaystyle W_{j}\rightarrow(\mbox{Hom}(W_{k},W_{i}))\{z\}

\displaystyle w\mapsto\mathcal{Y}(w,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Q}}w_{n}z^{-n-1}~~(w_{n}\in\mbox{Hom}(W_{k},W_{i}))

such that for any

,

(1)

(2)

(3) for
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-homogeneous elements

\displaystyle z_{0}^{-1}\delta(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}})Y_{i}(v,z_{1})\mathcal{Y}(w^{j},z_{2})w^{k}-(-1)^{|v||w^{j}|}z_{0}^{-1}\delta(\frac{z_{2}-z_{1}}{-z_{0}})\mathcal{Y}(w^{j},z_{2})Y_{k}(v,z_{1})w^{k}

\displaystyle=z_{2}^{-1}\delta(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}})\mathcal{Y}(Y_{j}(v,z_{0})w^{j},z_{2})w^{k}.

For any irreducible weak
V
-modules

,
the space of all intertwining operators of type
\begin{pmatrix}W_{i}\\
W_{j}~~W_{k}\end{pmatrix}

2.2
Admissible modules of
\widetilde{osp(1|2)}

Let
\mathfrak{g}={osp}(1|2)
be the finite dimensional simple Lie superalgebra with the basis
\{h,e,f,x,y\}

such that the even part
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mbox{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{h,e,f\}\cong sl_{2}
and
the odd part
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mbox{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{x,y\}
.
The anticommutation relations are given by

\displaystyle[e,f]=h,~[h,e]=2e,~[h,f]=-2f,

\displaystyle[h,x]=x,~[e,x]=0,~[f,x]=-y,

\displaystyle[h,y]=-y,~[e,y]=-x,~[f,y]=0,

\displaystyle\{x,x\}=2e,\{x,y\}=h,\{y,y\}=-2f.

The invariant nondegenerate even supersymmetric bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathfrak{g}
such that non-trivial products are given by

(e,f)=(f,e)=1,(h,h)=2,(x,y)=-(y,x)=2.

Let
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\oplus\mathbb{C}k
be the affine Lie superalgebra of
osp(1|2)

with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-gradation

|a\otimes t^{n}|=|a|~(a\in\mathfrak{g}),~|k|=0,

and anticommutation relations

[a\otimes t^{n},b\otimes t^{m}]=[a,b]\otimes t^{m+n}+m\delta_{m+n,0}(a,b)k~(a,b\in\mathfrak{g},m,n\in\mathbb{Z}),~[k,\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}]=0.

We identify
\mathfrak{g}
with
\mathfrak{g}\otimes t^{0}
and
set
a(n)=a\otimes t^{n}
for
a\in\mathfrak{g}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
for convenience.
Define subalgebras

\displaystyle N_{+}=\mathbb{C}e\oplus\mathbb{C}x\oplus\mathfrak{g}\otimes t\mathbb{C}[t],

(2.1)

\displaystyle N_{-}=\mathbb{C}f\oplus\mathbb{C}y\oplus\mathfrak{g}\otimes t^{-1}\mathbb{C}[t^{-1}],

\displaystyle B=N_{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}h\oplus\mathbb{C}k,~P=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t]\oplus\mathbb{C}k.

Let
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}=\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathbb{C}d
be the extended affine Lie superalgebra with

|d|=0
and

[d,a\otimes t^{n}]=na\otimes t^{n}~(a\in\mathfrak{g},n\in\mathbb{Z}),~[d,k]=0.

Let
H=\mathbb{C}h\oplus\mathbb{C}k\oplus\mathbb{C}d
be the Cartan subalgebra of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
.
For any
\lambda\in H^{*}
,
denote by
M(\lambda)
(resp.
L(\lambda)
) the Verma (resp. the irreducible highest weight)
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
-module.
It is clear that
L(\lambda)
is an irreducible
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-module,
denote by
L(\mathcal{l},\mathcal{j})
the
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-module

, where
\mathcal{l}=\langle\lambda,k\rangle,\mathcal{j}=\langle\lambda,h\rangle
.
Conversely, for any restricted

-module

,

to a

-module by letting

act on

as
-L(0)
.
In this paper we shall consider any restricted

-module as a

-module in this way.

Let
\mathcal{l}\in\mathbb{C}
and

be a
\mathbb{C}h
-module,

can be regarded as a

-module by setting

acting trivially and

,

If

Then

Similarly we can define the generalized Verma

for any
\mathfrak{g}
-module

which can be extend to a

acting trivially and

\mathcal{l}
.
Note that if

-module,

Proposition 2.5

[

. For any

Remark 2.6

Let

\displaystyle m,m^{\prime},s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq m\leq p-1,1\leq m^{\prime}\leq p-1,0\leq s^{\prime}\leq s\leq q-1,

We will show that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{l}}
is semisimple. Let

\Omega(M)=\{v\in M\mid(\mathfrak{g}\otimes t\mathbb{C}[t]).v=0\}

for a weak

-module

.
We can prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let

be a weak
L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{l},0)
-module belonging to

, then

has a highest weight vector.

Proof.

Similar to the Proposition 3.6 of
[
23
]
or the Theorem 3.7 of
[

, we have
\Omega(M)\neq 0
.
Since

is an object of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{l}}
,
we have

and

act locally nilpotent on

Let

Since

is a nilpotent Lie superalgebra,

Since

.
Furthermore,
let

be the weak

-submodule generated by

.
Then

is isomorphic to a quotient module of

as
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-module.
From Lemma
3.1
,
L(\mathcal{l},U)
is isomorphic to some quotient module of

as
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-module,
then

is a quotient of

as weak

-module.

is a weak
L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{l},0)
-module.
From Theorem

, we have

then

semisimply acts on

is a highest weight vector.
∎

We now classify simple objects in the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{l}}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Any irreducible weak

-module belonging to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{l}}
is of the form

with admissible weight
\mathcal{j}
.
Moreover, up to isomorphism, there are finitely many irreducible weak

-modules belonging to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{l}}

Proof.

Let
M
be an irreducible weak
L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{l},0)
-module belonging to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{l}}
,
then by Proposition
3.7
,
M
contains a highest weight vector
w
.
Since

is an irreducible weak
L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{l},0)
-module,
we have

is an irreducible weak

-module,
then

is an irreducible
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-module generated by

Therefore

is an irreducible highest weight module of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}

,

is of the form

L(\mathcal{l},\mathcal{j})
for some admissible weight
\mathcal{j}
.
∎

Then we can prove the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{l}}
is semisimple.

Theorem 3.9
.

The category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{l}}
is semisimple.
Moreover,
any object
M
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{l}}
is a direct sum of
L(\mathcal{l},\mathcal{j})
with admissible weight
\mathcal{j}
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a weak
L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{l},0)

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{l}}
.
From Proposition
3.7
,
M
contains a highest weight vector

Let

be the weak
L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{l},0)

-module.
Hence

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{l}}

From Theorem

, it is completely reducible and

for admissible weights
\mathcal{j}
.
But the highest weight subspace of

is one dimensional,
then

\mathcal{j}^{\prime}

Let

be the sum of irreducible weak

,

Let

,
the submodule of

generated by

and

with admissible weight

∎

Proposition 3.10

Any ordinary

Let

For any

Proposition 3.11
.

Let

\mathfrak{g}
-module,

Let

be an irreducible ordinary

is of the form

with admissible weight

.

\mathfrak{g}

Let

\displaystyle[L(m),h_{n}]=-nh_{m+n}-\frac{m^{2}+m}{2}\delta_{m+n,0}\kappa_{1}\mbox{Id}_{V},

(3.7)

Let

For any homogeneous element

Let

.
The following theorem were established in

Theorem 4.1
.

(a)
The subspace

is a two-sided ideal of

(b)

-module

is an
A(V)
-module with

-graded weak

-module

(d)

.
Similar to the Theorem 2.11 in

Theorem 4.3

(a)
The quotient space

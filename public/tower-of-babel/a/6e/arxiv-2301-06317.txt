Four types of variant Euler harmonic sums Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author
Necdet Batir and Junesang Choi ∗

Abstract
. We aim to investigate the four types of variant Euler harmonic sums. Also, as corollaries, we provide particular examples of our core findings, some of whose further instances are evaluated in terms of basic and well-known functions as well as certain mathematical constants. We explore relevant linkages between our results and those of other previously established studies. An examination of a specific case of one result shows a relationship to series involving zeta functions, which is also a popular area of research.

Introduction

Certain required functions, numbers and notations are recalled and given. The gamma function
\Gamma
is given by

\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\,t^{z-1}e^{-t}\,dt\quad(\Re(z)>0).

(1.1)

The Beta function
B(\mu,\,\nu)
is given as follows (see, e.g.,
[
22

B(\mu,\,\nu)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{1}\,t^{\mu-1}(1-t)^{\nu-1}\,dt\quad(\Re(\mu)>0,\,\,\Re(\nu)>0)\\
&\frac{\Gamma(\mu)\,\Gamma(\nu)}{\Gamma(\mu+\nu)}\hskip 65.44133pt\left(\mu,\,\nu\in\mathbb{C}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leqslant 0}\right).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

The psi (or digamma) function
\psi
is defined by

\psi(z):=\frac{d}{dz}\log\Gamma(z)=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(z)}{\Gamma(z)}\quad\left(z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant 0}\right).

(1.3)

The polygamma function
\psi^{\left(k\right)}(z)
is defined by

\displaystyle\psi^{\left(k\right)}(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}\{\psi(z)\}=\left(-1\right)^{k+1}k!\,\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(r+z\right)^{k+1}}

(1.4)

\displaystyle=\left(-1\right)^{k+1}k!\,\zeta(k+1,z)\quad\left(k\in\mathbb{N},\,\,z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant 0}\right),

where
\psi^{\left(0\right)}(z)=\psi(z)
, and
\zeta(s,z)
is the generalized (or Hurwitz) zeta function defined by

\zeta(s,z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{(j+z)^{s}}\quad\left(\Re(s)>1,\,z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant 0}\right),

(1.5)

and
\zeta(s,1)=:\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function.
It has the recurrence

\psi^{\left(k\right)}(z+1)=\psi^{\left(k\right)}(z)+\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k!}{z^{k+1}}\quad\left(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\right).

(1.6)

Here and in the following, let
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,

, and
\mathbb{N}
denote the sets of complex numbers, real numbers, positive real numbers, integers, and positive integers, respectively. Also let
\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant\ell}
and

denote the sets of integers greater than or equal to
\ell
and less than or equal to
\ell

For more properties and formulas of the above functions, one may refer to

The generalized harmonic numbers
H_{n}^{(s)}
of order

are defined by

H_{n}^{(s)}:=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\quad\left(n\in\mathbb{N},\,s\in\mathbb{C}\right),

(1.7)

where
H_{n}^{(1)}=:H_{n}

(n\in\mathbb{N})
are harmonic numbers, and
H_{0}^{(s)}=0=H_{0}
.
Here and elsewhere, an empty sum is assumed to be nil. The following relations are recalled:

H_{n}=\gamma+\psi(n+1)\quad\left(n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\right),

(1.8)

where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant (see, e.g.,
[

, Section 1.2]
);

H_{n}^{(m+1)}=\zeta(m+1)+\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\psi^{(m)}(n+1)\quad\left(m\in\mathbb{N},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\right)

(1.9)

(see, e.g.,
[

Equations (

[
21

H_{\eta}^{(m)}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma+\psi(\eta+1)&(m=1),\\
\zeta(m)+\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}\,\psi^{(m-1)}(\eta+1)&(m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 2}).\end{array}\right.

(1.10)

A generalized binomial coefficient
\binom{s}{t}
(
s,\,t\in\mathbb{C}
) is defined, in terms of the gamma functions, by

\binom{s}{t}:=\frac{\Gamma(s+1)}{\Gamma(t+1)\Gamma(s-t+1)}\quad(s,\,t\in\mathbb{C}).

(1.11)

Euler found the following identity in 1775, and it has a lengthy history (see, e.g.,

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k}}{(k+1)^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k}}{k^{2}}=\zeta(3).

(1.13)

or, equivalently,

2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k}}{k^{m}}=(m+2)\,\zeta(m+1)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-2}\,\zeta(k+1)\zeta(m-k)\quad\left(m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 2}\right).

During his interaction with Goldbach starting in 1742, Euler initiated a series of investigations for the

\mathtt{S}(p,q):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{H_{n}^{(p)}}{n^{q}}\quad\left(p\in\mathbb{N},\,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 2}\right).

The

Put

(1.17)

where the quantity

is called the weight, the quantity
\ell
is the degree.
In partitions, repeating summands are represented by powers for brevity, for instance,

\mathtt{S}(1^{2},2^{3},7;q)=\mathtt{S}(1,1,2,2,2,7;q)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{H_{n}^{2}\,\big\{H_{n}^{(2)}\big\}^{3}\,H_{n}^{(7)}}{n^{q}}.

The main purpose of this paper is to explore the following variants of the Euler harmonic sums: For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and

p\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant-1}

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k}}{(n+k+1)^{m}\binom{n+k}{k}},\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k}^{2}-H_{k}^{(2)}}{(n+k+1)^{m}\binom{n+k}{k}},

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k}}{k(p+n+k)^{m}\binom{n+k}{k}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k}^{2}-H_{k}^{(2)}}{k(p+n+k)^{m}\binom{n+k}{k}}.

In addition, as corollaries, we present specific cases of our primary discoveries, some of whose further particular instances are assessed in terms of elementary and well-known functions as well as certain mathematical constants. We discuss pertinent connections between our findings and those of other known ones. Investigation of a particular instance of one result reveals a connection to series involving zeta functions, which is also an interesting and useful research topic.

3.
Main results

This section establishes our main findings.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant-1}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k}}{(n+k+1)^{m+1}\binom{n+k}{k}}

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k^{m}}\binom{n}{k}\left(H_{n-k}^{2}+H_{n-k}^{(2)}-H_{n}^{(2)}-H_{n}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{(-1)^{m+n}}{2\,m!}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\left\{\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial z^{m}}\frac{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(z)}{\Gamma(z+x)}\right\}\bigg|_{\begin{subarray}{l}x=n\\
z=1\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(-1)^{m+n}\,H_{n}}{m!}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left\{\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial{z}^{m}}\frac{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(z)}{\Gamma(z+x)}\right\}\bigg|_{\begin{subarray}{l}x=n\\

Let

(3.2)

Also

We first show that the series

Note that

\displaystyle=O\left(x^{k}\right)\left\{O\left(\frac{k}{x}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right\}

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant 0}
,
x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant-1}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{x}{k}=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial s^{m}}\frac{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(x+s+1)}\bigg|_{s=p}.

(3.27)

Proof.

Using (
1.1
), one may find

\frac{1}{t^{m+1}}=\frac{1}{m!}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{m}e^{-t\,u}\,du\quad\left(m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0},\,\,\Re(t)>0\right).

(3.28)

Employing (
3.28
), one can obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{x}{k}=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\binom{x}{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{m}e^{-(p+k)u}\,du.

Here and in the following,
\Re(p)>0
is assumed.
Interchanging the order of integration and summation, one may get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{x}{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\,u^{m}e^{-pu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\binom{x}{k}e^{-ku}du

(3.29)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m!}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{m}\,e^{-pu}(1-e^{-u})^{x}\,du.

In order to verify the above term-by-term integration, let

g_{k}(u):=u^{m}\,e^{-pu}\,(-1)^{k}\,\binom{x}{k}\,e^{-ku}\quad\left(u\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\right).

Note that

\left|\binom{x}{k}\right|\leqslant\frac{|x|(|x|+1)\cdots(|x|+k-1)}{k!}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(|x|)}\frac{\Gamma(k+|x|)}{\Gamma(k+1)}.

Holding
x
fixed and using the asymptotic expansion of ratio of gamma functions (see, e.g.,
[
22
, p. 7]
),
we may find

\left|\binom{x}{k}\right|=O\left(k^{|x|-1}\right)\quad(k\rightarrow\infty).

That is, there exist
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 2}
such that

\left|\binom{x}{k}\right|\leqslant M\,k^{|x|-1}\,\,\,\text{ for all}\,\,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant N},

in particular,

\left|\binom{x}{k}\right|\leqslant M\,\,\,\text{ for all}\,\,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant N}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,|x|<1.

Thus we may find that, for
|x|<1
and
\Re(p)>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}\,\left|g_{k}(u)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant M\,u^{m}\,e^{-\Re(p)\,u}\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}\,e^{-ku}

\displaystyle=M\,u^{m}\,e^{-\Re(p)\,u}\,\frac{e^{-Nu}}{1-e^{-u}}

\displaystyle=M\,u^{m}\,e^{-\Re(p)\,u}\,\frac{e^{-(N-1)u}}{e^{u}-1}.

Since
e^{u}-1\geqslant u
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we obtain

\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}\,\left|g_{k}(u)\right|\leqslant M\,u^{m-1}\,e^{-(\Re(p)+N-1)u}

and, with the aid of (
3.28
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}\,\left|g_{k}(u)\right|\,du

\displaystyle\leqslant M\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\,u^{m-1}\,e^{-(\Re(p)+N-1)u}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{M\,(m-1)!}{(\Re(p)+N-1)^{m}}<\infty.

Now, by employing the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem (see, e.g.,
[
13
, p. 53]
),
(
3.29
) may be justified.

Making the change of variable
1-e^{-u}=y
, we derive that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{x}{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\int_{0}^{1}y^{x}(1-y)^{p-1}\log^{m}(1-y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\int_{0}^{1}y^{x}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial s^{m}}(1-y)^{s-1}\big|_{s=p}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial s^{m}}\,B(x+1,s)\big|_{s=p},

where
B(x+1,s)
is the Beta function in (
1.2
).

We thus proved the following identity:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{x}{k}=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial s^{m}}\frac{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(x+s+1)}\bigg|_{s=p}

(3.30)

\displaystyle\left(\Re(p)>0,\,\,|x|<1,\,\,m\in\mathbb{N}\right).

One can observe that both sides of (
3.30
) are analytic functions of both variables
p
and
x

in the wider domains
p\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant 0}
and
x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant-1}
.
Finally, by the principle of analytic continuation, the desired identity (
3.27
) can hold true for the given domains.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant 0}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(H_{k}-2H_{2k}\right)\binom{2k}{k}}{4^{k}(p+k)^{m+1}}

(3.31)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,(-1)^{m}}{m!}\frac{d^{m}}{ds^{m}}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+\tfrac{1}{2})}\left\{\psi\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)-\psi\left(s+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\right\}\Big|_{s=p}.

Proof.

We may get (
3.31
) by differentiating both sides of (
3.27
), with respect to
x
, and then setting
x=-\frac{1}{2}

in the resultant identity, and using several formulas for gamma and
\psi
functions such as

\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-k\right)=\sqrt{\pi}\,(-1)^{k}\,\frac{2^{2k}\,k!}{(2k)!}\quad\left(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\right),

(3.32)

\psi\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)-\psi\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-k\right)=H_{k}-2\,H_{2k}\quad\left(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\right)

(3.33)

and

\psi\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-k\right)=\psi\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+k\right)\quad\left(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\right).

(3.34)

∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant 0}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
, and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{k(p+n+k)^{m+1}\binom{n+k}{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{n}{k}\big(H_{n}-H_{n-k}\big)

(3.35)

\displaystyle-\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial s^{m}}\frac{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(x+s+1)}\bigg|_{\begin{subarray}{l}x=n\\
s=p\end{subarray}}.

Proof.

Denote both sides of (
3.27
) by
Q(x)
. Then, differentiating the left member of (
3.27
), with respect to
x
, gives

Q^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{x}{k}(\psi(x+1)-\psi(x-k+1)),

which, upon setting
x=n
, yields

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}(n)

\displaystyle=\psi(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{n}{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{n}{k}\psi(n-k+1).

(3.36)

Split the second sum in (
3.36
) into two parts as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{n}{k}\psi(n-k+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{n}{k}(H_{n-k}-\gamma)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{n}{k}\psi(n-k+1).

Letting
k-n-1=k^{\prime}
and then dropping the prime on
k
, and using (
2.9
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{n}{k}\psi(n-k+1)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=(-1)^{n+1}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+n+k+1)^{m+1}}\frac{n!}{(n+k+1)!}\frac{\psi(-k)}{\Gamma(-k)}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(p+n+k+1)^{m+1}(n+k+1)\binom{n+k}{k}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{n}{k}\psi(n-k+1)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(p+k)^{m+1}}\binom{n}{k}(H_{n-k}-\gamma)

(3.37)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(p+n+k+1)^{m+1}(n+k+1)\binom{n+k}{k}}.

Finally, substituting (
3.37
) for the second sum (
3.36
), and differentiating the right member of (
3.27
), with respect to
x
,
setting
x=n
, and matching the two expressions, we may obtain the desired result (
3.35
).
∎

3.35

Theorem 3.9
.

Proof.

3.1

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant 0}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k-1}}{k(p+k)^{m+1}}=\frac{1}{2}\Big\{(\gamma+\psi(p+1))^{2}+\zeta(2)-\psi^{\prime}(p+1)\Big\}

(3.40)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\,\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell!\,p^{m-\ell+1}}\Big\{2\gamma\,\psi^{(\ell)}(p+1)-\psi^{(\ell+1)}(p+1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}\,\psi^{(j)}(p+1)\psi^{(\ell-j)}(p+1)\Big\}.

Proof.

Setting
n=0
in (
3.39
) offers

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k-1}}{k(p+k)^{m+1}}=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{2\,m!}\left[\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial s^{m}}\bigg\{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\frac{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(x+s+1)}\bigg\}\bigg|_{x=0}\right]\bigg|_{s=p}.

\Gamma(s)\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\frac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\Gamma(x+s+1)}\bigg|_{x=0}=\frac{h(s)}{s},

Then one may get the desired identity (
3.40
).
∎

\displaystyle-\gamma^{3}-3\gamma\,\zeta(2)-2\zeta(3)-3\{\gamma^{2}+\zeta(2)\}\,\psi(z+1)

This section demonstrates certain particular instances of our main findings

Example 1.
From (
3.21
), one may find that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k}^{2}-H_{k}^{(2)}}{k^{m+1}}=\mathtt{S}(1^{2};m+1)-\mathtt{S}(2,m+1)\quad(m\in\mathbb{N}),

(4.1)

which is a combination of a nonlinear harmonic sum and a linear harmonic sum and can be evaluated in terms of
Riemann zeta functions for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
. The simple one is

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k}^{2}-H_{k}^{(2)}}{k^{2}}=\mathtt{S}(1^{2};2)-\mathtt{S}(2,2)=\frac{5}{2}\,\zeta(4).

(4.2)

As noted in the paragraph between (
1.15
) and (
1.16
),
the linear Euler sum
\mathtt{S}(2,m+1)

is determined in terms of Riemann zeta functions
only when
m=1
,
m=5
, and
m
is even. So is
\mathtt{S}(1^{2};m+1)
for
m=1
,
m=5
, and
m
is even.
For example,

\mathtt{S}(2,2)=\frac{7}{4}\,\zeta(4)

(4.3)

and

\mathtt{S}(1^{2};2)=\frac{17}{4}\,\zeta(4).

(4.4)

The identity in (

) was observed by E. Au-Yeung.
The
\mathtt{S}(1^{2};m+1)
for
m=1
,
m=5
, and
m
is even
was evaluated in terms of Riemann zeta functions by Borwein et al.
[

]

who used the Eulerian beta integral in (

) and, also, by Flajolet and Salvy
[
11
]

who applied residue calculus to
\psi
expansions such as (
2.4
) and (
2.6
).

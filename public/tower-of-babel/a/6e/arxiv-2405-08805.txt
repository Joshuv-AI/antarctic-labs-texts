Special potentials for relativistic Laplacians I: Fractional Rollnik-class
Giacomo Ascione, Atsuhide Ishida and József Lőrinczi

Abstract
. We propose a counterpart of the classical Rollnik-class of potentials for fractional and massive relativistic Laplacians, and describe this space in terms of appropriate Riesz potentials. These definitions rely on precise resolvent estimates, which we present in detail. We obtain these classes for the operators with fractional exponent α = 1 \alpha=1 in low dimensions, making use of tools of Γ \Gamma -convergence. We show that Coulomb-type potentials are elements of fractional Rollnik-class up to but not including the critical singularity of the Hardy potential. In a second part of the paper we derive detailed results on the self-adjointness and spectral properties of relativistic Schrödinger operators obtained under perturbations by fractional Rollnik potentials. We also define an extended fractional Rollnik-class which is the maximal space for the Hilbert-Schmidt property of the related Birman-Schwinger operators. Key-words : fractional and relativistic Laplacians, Riesz potential, resolvent estimates, Γ \Gamma -convergence, Coulomb potentials, subcriticality 2010 MS Classification : Primary 47D08, 60G51; Secondary 47D03, 47G20



1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to define and explore a relativistic counterpart of the classical
Rollnik-class of potentials, which plays a fundamental role in mathematical quantum theory.
We call this fractional Rollnik-class, and also set ourselves the task to describe the
properties of relativistic Schrödinger operators obtained as perturbations of the (free)
relativistic operators by this class of potentials. Since both the massive and massless cases
involve singular integral operators, in our approach the Riesz potential will play a role
to a far-reaching extent. In particular, we will make use of it to a detailed description
of Coulomb-type potentials belonging to fractional Rollnik-class and investigate their
boundaries verging on the critical Hardy potential.

In the paper
[
29
]
Rollnik identified the class of potentials

(1.1)

{\mathcal{R}}=\left\{V:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}:\,\|V\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{R}}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2}}dxdy<\infty\right\},

which later turned out to play a special role in the classical theory of Schrödinger operators on
the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. He noticed that for an eigenvalue
\lambda<0
generated by a negative
potential
V
, the eigenvalue equation
H\varphi:=(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta+V)\varphi=\lambda\varphi

can equivalently be written as
K_{\lambda}\psi:=\sqrt{-V}(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}\sqrt{-V}\psi=\psi
, with
\psi=\sqrt{-V}\varphi
, so the original Schrödinger operator
H
has
a eigenvalue
\lambda
exactly when the related operator
K_{\lambda}
has eigenvalue 1.
Thereby he appears to have anticipated by a five years (if we count by the publication dates) what is
now known as the Birman-Schwinger principle.
In fact, Rollnik studied mostly the case
\lambda=0
and required his operator
K_{0}
(in his notation

W(0)
) to be compact, that he satisfied by turning its kernel square-integrable, which results in
(
1.1
).

The integral condition (
1.1
) has appeared in subsequent work by Schwinger
[
33
]
, Scadron,
Weinberg and Wright
[
32
]
, Coester
[
9
]
, and in the series of papers by Grossmann and Wu

[
19
]
. A later development is Simon’s mathematical work in the paper
[
34
]
and the monograph

[
35
]
, in which he made a comprehensive analysis of Rollnik potentials in the non-relativistic case.
Some more recent related works include
[
12
,
28
,
38
]
.

Our goal here is to construct and analyse a counterpart of Rollnik-class for the massive and massless
relativistic Laplacians

L_{m,\alpha}=(-\Delta+m^{2/\alpha})^{\alpha/2}-m,\quad 0<\alpha<2,\;m\geq 0,

on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
d\geq 1
, and study relativistic Schrödinger operators obtained as perturbations of

L_{m,\alpha}
by these potentials. For the massless case
m=0
the operator
L_{m,\alpha}
reduces to the
fractional Laplacian with exponent
\frac{\alpha}{2}
, and for
\alpha=1
and
m>0
it is the physical
relativistic operator widely used in mathematical physics. For the purposes of understanding fractional
Rollnik-class it will be useful to keep the full range
\alpha\in(0,2)
in order to appreciate how our
results (qualitatively) depend on the details of the operators of different fractional exponent. Also,
exponents
\alpha
different from 1 relate with further models in physics, see
[
21
]
. Our guiding
principle is to keep to the original idea that the Birman-Schwinger operators associated with the non-local
Schrödinger operators should have the Hilbert-Schmidt property, however, for the rest we will
have to trod a new path due to qualitative differences between the Laplacian and the fractional operators
requiring very different techniques.

In the following we summarise our approach and the main results of this paper.

(1) Our first task is to find an appropriate concept of a fractional Rollnik-class holding to this principle
and investigate its structure. The construction will depend on precise estimates of the resolvent kernels of
the operators
L_{m,\alpha}
. Making use of estimate (
2.14
) and assuming

(1.2)

\alpha<d<2\alpha,

we introduce the fractional Rollnik-class

\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}=\left\{V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\,\|V\|^{2}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2(d-\alpha)}}dxdy<\infty\right\}

in the massless case and

\mathcal{R}^{m}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}\cap\mathcal{R}

in the massive case (see Definition
3.1
). This definition is consistent with the classical case for
it reproduces
\mathcal{R}
for
d=3
and
\alpha=2
(see also Corollary
3.14
below). Observing that
the Fourier transform of the Riesz potential
\mathcal{I}_{\beta}
of functions on Lizorkin space can be computed (Theorem

2.2
), we show that the fractional Rollnik norm of
V
can be expressed in the form

(1.3)

\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}=c(d,\alpha)\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}|V|\right\|_{2}

where
c(d,\alpha)
is a constant and the left-hand side above is indeed a norm (Theorem
3.4
),
turning
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
into a Banach space (Theorem
3.6
). Furthermore, as it will be seen,
fractional Rollnik-class contains a variety of
L^{p}
subspaces (Propositions
3.11
-
3.13
).

To get a closer idea of fractional Rollnik potentials, we consider several specific cases (Section 3.3). In
particular, we consider potentials having Coulomb-type singularities and by using (
1.3
) and formula
(
3.16
) allowing to compute Riesz potentials of radial functions, we show that
V(x)=|x|^{-\beta_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<1\}}+|x|^{-\beta_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\geq 1\}}
belongs to
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
if and only if

\beta_{1}<\alpha
and
\beta_{2}\in(\alpha,d)
(see Propositions
3.20
-
3.23
and Corollary

3.25
). Since for the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
the critical potential is the
Hardy potential
V(x)=|x|^{-\alpha}
, we see that the Coulomb-type fractional Rollnik-class potentials are
subcritical with possible singularities arbitrarily approaching but not attaining the critical exponent. Also,
we show (Theorem
3.19
) that the Hardy exponent marks the boundary to Coulomb-potentials in fractional
Rollnik class, and
V(x)=|x|^{-\alpha}(-\log|x|)^{-\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<1/2\}}
, with
\alpha<d
, belongs to
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
exactly when
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
(Proposition
3.21
). Apart from
Coulomb singularities, we also discuss potentials with compact support and show that their fractional Rollnik norm
increases under symmetric rearrangement and it can be bounded by the fractional perimeter of the supporting set
(Section 3.3.1), and asymmetric potentials whose non-radial symmetry deficit we can bound by the
L^{1}
-norm of
the difference between the potential and a family of rotationally symmetric potentials (Section 3.3.2).

Using another key resolvent estimate (see (
2.16
)-(
2.17
) and
(
2.18
)-(
2.20
) below) we also introduce extended fractional Rollnik-classes

{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}_{d,\alpha}
and
{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}^{m}_{d,\alpha}
(Definition
3.26
),
containing
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
as a proper subset (Proposition
3.27
and Remark
3.30
below).
We will discuss the role of the extended fractional Rollnik-class further below.

The restriction (
1.2
) has the implication that in case
d\geq 2\alpha
no non-trivial continuous potential
exists in
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
(Theorem
3.9
). As frequently with fractional Laplacians, the

\alpha=1
case is difficult. In this case there exists no fractional Rollnik-class potential in any dimension
directly, which is an essential difference from the classical situation. However, mathematically this case remains
very interesting and from a physics point of view it
is satisfying that in low dimensions we can make sense of it by further developing Definition
3.1
. In

d=1
we cover it by the extended fractional Rollnik-class, and for
d=2
we obtain it as a limiting case. Proposition

3.10
shows, however, that the required limit is not straightforward. As it turns out, with a sequence

(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
accumulating at
\alpha=1
, the corresponding Schrödinger operators with

\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha_{n}}
-potentials have a limit in
\Gamma
-convergence sense (see Theorem
5.3
), and
we arrive to define it by
\mathcal{R}_{d,1}^{p}:=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow 1}\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
with
p>1
.

(2) Our interest next is to study the properties of relativistic Schrödinger
operators of the form
H_{m,\alpha}=L_{m,\alpha}+V
with
V
in
\mathcal{R}^{m}_{d,\alpha}
or
{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}^{m}_{d,\alpha}
, for
m\geq 0
. First we consider the self-adjointness of
H_{m,\alpha}
. To obtain this it
suffices to have the operator norm of
K^{m}_{\lambda}=|V|^{1/2}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}|V|^{1/2}
vanish as
\lambda\to\infty
(Lemma
4.1
). To ensure this, we show that
K^{m}_{\lambda}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator at
least for a large enough
\lambda
. In particular, we show that for potentials in
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
the operator

H_{m,\alpha}
is self-adjoint (Theorem
4.3
). Also, we show that if a potential is in the extended
fractional Rollnik-class
{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}_{d,\alpha}
, then the related
K^{m}_{\lambda}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt
operator, and conversely, if
K^{m}_{\lambda}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator for a potential, then the potential is in

{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}_{d,\alpha}
(Theorem
4.4
), thus the extended fractional Rollnik-class is
the maximal space for the Hilbert-Schmidt property to hold.

Next we study the spectrum of
H_{m,\alpha}
. Since fractional Rollnik potentials are small in an aggregate sense
(without being subject to any specific fall-off rate), a perturbation by such potentials does not change the essential
spectrum of
L_{m,\alpha}
(see Theorem
4.7
). However, contrary to the classical case, we can not make use of
Fourier methods and develop an other argument to show this. In case
H_{m,\alpha}
has a non-empty discrete spectrum,
we can estimate the number of negative eigenvalues (Theorem
4.8
); since this number is controlled above by the
fractional Rollnik norm, the discrete spectrum is finite in both the massless and massive cases. We obtain similar
results for
{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}_{d,\alpha}
-class potentials (Theorems
4.9
-
4.10
),
where now the control is made by the quantity
[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}}
, which defines the extended class. By
using the extended Birman-Schwinger principle, we can further derive conditions under which no bound states exist and
zero (the spectral edge) is not an embedded eigenvalue. This occurs when then fractional Rollnik norm is small enough
(Theorem
4.14
).

(3) The main estimates on the resolvent
R^{m}_{\lambda}=(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}
,
\lambda>0
, (uniform in
\lambda
,
pointwise upper and lower for large
\lambda
and for
\lambda=0
both for the massive and massless cases) on which our
constructions are built are stated in the expressions (
2.14
)-(
2.15
),
(
2.16
)-(
2.17
) and (
2.19
)-(
2.20
). Detailed proofs will be presented in
the Appendix. We note that these results have an independent interest, in areas such as analytic and probabilistic
potential theory.

Finally, we point out that in the classical theory of Schrödinger operators there are several competing spaces of
potentials suiting different purposes. For instance, Kerman-Sawyer and Stummel-Schechter classes can be used to obtain
improved conditions on self-adjointness, and Kato-class is a natural space for Feynman-Kac representations, which yield
detailed results also on ground state properties. We note that the fractional Rollnik-class introduced in this paper has
an overlap with (relativistic/fractional) Kato-class, however, neither space contains the other (see Proposition
3.17

and its consequences below). Thus the detailed results that we obtain under the Rollnik integral condition not just on
self-adjointness, but also on the spectral properties of such relativistic Schrödinger operators cannot be fully covered
under the conditions of relativistic Kato-class. In forthcoming papers, we will further study the relativistic counterparts
of the above mentioned potential spaces.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Massive and massless relativistic Laplacians

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
m\geq 0
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and consider the family of functions
\Psi_{m,\alpha}(u)=(u+m^{2/\alpha})^{\alpha/2}-m
for every
u\geq 0
. Using these functions, we define the operators

\displaystyle L_{m,\alpha}\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\Psi_{m,\alpha}(-\Delta)=(-\Delta+m^{2/\alpha})^{\alpha/2}-m\qquad\mbox{if $m>0$}

\displaystyle L_{0,\alpha}\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\Psi_{0,\alpha}(-\Delta)=(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}\hskip 85.35826pt\mbox{if $m=0$}

and generically combine the notation into just
L_{m,\alpha}
,
m\geq 0
, whenever suitable. These operators can be defined
in several different ways. We define them through the Fourier multipliers

\widehat{(L_{m,\alpha}f)}(y)=\Psi_{m,\alpha}(|y|^{2})\widehat{f}(y),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;f\in\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}),

with domain

\Dom(L_{m,\alpha})=\Big\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\Psi_{m,\alpha}(|\cdot|^{2})\widehat{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\Big\},\quad m\geq 0.

In the
m=0
case we have more specifically
\Dom(L_{0,\alpha})=H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e., the fractional Sobolev space of
order
0<\alpha<2
.

For
f\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have the expression

L_{m,\alpha}f(x)=-\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{|y-x|>\varepsilon}\left(f(y)-f(x)\right)\nu_{m,\alpha}(dy)

with the Lévy jump measures as follows. For
m>0
we have (see, for instance,
[
7
, Equation
(2.2)
]
)

(2.1)

\nu_{m,\alpha}(dx)=j_{m,\alpha}(|x|)dx=\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha-d}{2}}m^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2\alpha}}\alpha}{\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\frac{K_{(d+\alpha)/2}(m^{1/\alpha}|x|)}{|x|^{(d+\alpha)/2}}\,dx,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\},

and for
m=0
we have (see, for instance,
[
3
, Equation (1.22)]
)

(2.2)

\nu_{0,\alpha}(dx)=j_{0,\alpha}(|x|)dx=\frac{2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\frac{d+\alpha}{2})}{\pi^{d/2}|\Gamma(-\frac{\alpha}{2})|}\frac{dx}{|x|^{d+\alpha}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

Here

K_{\rho}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{\rho}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\rho-1}e^{-t-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}}dt,\quad\rho>0,

is the standard modified Bessel function of the second (sometimes also called third) kind. Below we will make use of the
asymptotic formulae (see
[
1
, Ch. 9, eqs. 9.6.6, 9.7.2]
)

(2.3)

K_{\rho}(z)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}\,e^{-z}\quad\mbox{as $z\to\infty$}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad K_{\rho}(z)\sim\frac{\Gamma(\rho)}{2}\left(\frac{2}{z}\right)^{\rho}\quad\mbox{as $z\downarrow 0$}.

A consequence of the second expression is that
j_{m,\alpha}\to j_{0,\alpha}
as
m\downarrow 0
, thus the operator
L_{0,\alpha}

is the massless limit of
L_{m,\alpha}
.

The operators
L_{m,\alpha}
,
m\geq 0
, are positive with spectrum
\Spec L_{m,\alpha}=\Spec_{\rm ess}L_{m,\alpha}=[0,\infty)
, and self-adjoint with core
C^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for every
0<\alpha<2
and
m\geq 0
. In case
m>0
, the operator
L_{m,\alpha}
is called
relativistic fractional Laplacian with exponent
\frac{\alpha}{2}
and
rest mass
m
, while for
m=0
it is the
fractional Laplacian with exponent
\frac{\alpha}{2}
, but for simplicity
we will refer to them as the massive and massless relativistic Laplacians, respectively.

Below we will use the following generic notations.
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norms will be denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{p}
and
L^{p}(E)
,
E\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, norms by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(E)}
. A ball centred in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with radius
r>0
will be denoted by

B_{r}(x)
, and simply
B_{r}
when
x=0
. We write
\omega_{d}
for the Lebesgue measure of
B_{1}
and
\sigma_{d}
for the
surface measure of
\partial B_{1}
, so that
\sigma_{d}=d\omega_{d}
. When convenient, we will also use the notations

a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\}
and
a\vee b=\max\{a,b\}
.

2.2.
Lizorkin space and Riesz potential

Consider the negative Laplacian
-\Delta
and for
\beta\in(0,d)
let

\gamma_{d}(\beta)=\frac{2^{\beta}\pi^{d/2}\Gamma\big(\frac{\beta}{2}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{d-\beta}{2}\big)}.

The Riesz potential of order
0<\beta<d
of a measurable function
f
is defined as

\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f(x)=(-\Delta)^{-\beta/2}f(x)=\frac{1}{\gamma_{d}(\beta)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{d-\beta}}dy=(k_{\beta}\ast f)(x).

The requirement
\beta>0
is necessary in order to guarantee that the Riesz kernel
k_{\beta}
is at least

L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It is clear that the integral defining
\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f
is absolutely convergent
almost everywhere if and only if
f
is taken from

(2.4)

{\mathfrak{X}}_{\beta}^{d}:=\Big\{f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}:\mbox{$f$ is measurable and}\,\int_{|x|<1}|f(x)|dx+\int_{|x|\geq 1}\frac{|f(x)|}{|x|^{d-\beta}}dx<\infty\Big\}.

If
f
belongs to the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of rapidly decreasing functions, its Riesz potential

\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f
is well-defined since
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset{\mathfrak{X}}_{\beta}^{d}
. However, it is
not guaranteed that
\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. To ensure this, we need to consider
f
in
a suitable subspace
of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, where
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
, be the set of all multi-indices and for all

\mathbf{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
denote by
D^{\mathbf{j}}h
the partial derivative of
h
taken
j_{i}
-times with respect
to the
x_{i}
variable, for all
i=1,\dots,d
. Following the lines of
[
31
, Ch. 2.2]
, we introduce the
spaces

\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\ D^{\mathbf{j}}h(0)=0,\ \forall\mathbf{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\right\}

and

\mathcal{FS}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\ h=\mathcal{F}[g],\ g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}\right\},

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes Fourier transform. The set
\mathcal{FS}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called Lizorkin space. Since
\mathcal{F}

maps
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into itself, it is clear that
\mathcal{FS}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In particular, it is not
difficult to check that
\mathcal{FS}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is formed by all (complex-valued) functions in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

that are orthogonal to all polynomials. We will make use of the following denseness result, which was shown in

[
31
, Th. 2.7]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The Lizorkin space
\mathcal{FS}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
.

For our purposes below the following connection between Lizorkin space and the Riesz potential will be important, for
details we refer to
[
31
, Lem. 2.15, Th. 2.16]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\beta\in(0,d)
. If
h\in\mathcal{FS}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
\mathcal{I}_{\beta}h\in\mathcal{F}\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{I}_{\beta}h](z)=|z|^{-\beta}\mathcal{F}[h](z).

In the special case of radially symmetric functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
written in the form
f(x)=g(|x|)

with some
g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
, it has been shown in
[
37
, Th. 1]
that the Riesz potential

\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f
is radially symmetric and for
d\geq 2
the expression

(2.5)

\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f(x)=\frac{1}{\gamma_{d}(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\beta-1}F_{\beta}\left(\frac{|x|}{r}\right)g(r)dr

holds, where

(2.6)

F_{\beta}(r)=\sigma_{d-1}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-t^{2})^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}{(1+r^{2}-2rt)^{\frac{d-\beta}{2}}}\,dt,\quad r>0.

The asymptotic behaviour of the function
F_{\beta}
is known. First, it is continuous for
r\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
,
and for
\beta>1
it is continuous also in
r=1
with value

F_{\beta}(1)=\sigma_{d-1}2^{\beta-1}B\left(\frac{d-1}{2},\frac{\beta-1}{2}\right),

where
B
is the Beta-function. Also, if
\beta=1
, then

\lim_{r\to 1}\frac{F_{\beta}(r)}{-\sigma_{d-1}\log(|r-1|)}=1

while if
\beta\in(0,1)
, then

\lim_{r\to 1}\frac{2F_{\beta}(r)}{\sigma_{d-1}B\left(\frac{d-1}{2},\frac{1-\beta}{2}\right)|r-1|^{\beta-1}}=1.

For its behaviour at zero we have

(2.7)

F_{\beta}(r)=\sigma_{d}+O(r),\ \mbox{ as }r\to 0

while at infinity

(2.8)

F_{\beta}(r)=\sigma_{d}r^{\beta-d}+O(r^{\beta-d-1}),\ \mbox{ as }r\to\infty.

We note that in
[
31
, Eq. (2.10)]
the same formula has been obtained in terms of hypergeometric functions.
However, the above representation is preferable for our purposes since usually the analytic extension of the
hypergeometric function is considered on the cut complex plane
\mathbb{C}\setminus(1,\infty)
. This is due to
the fact that
z=1
and
z=\infty
are branch points of this function.

The
d=1
case is not covered by (
2.6
), however, we can derive a similar type of representation. Let

f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be even. First observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f(-x)=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}(\beta)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(y)}{|x+y|^{1-\beta}}\,dy=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}(\beta)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{1-\beta}}\,dy=\mathcal{I}_{\beta}(x),

thus
\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f
is also even. Next, notice that for
x>0

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}(\beta)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{1-\beta}}\,dy=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}(\beta)}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{1-\beta}}\,dy+\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{1-\beta}}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}(\beta)}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{1-\beta}}\,dy+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)}{|x+y|^{1-\beta}}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\beta-1}f(y)\left(\frac{1}{\left|\frac{x}{y}-1\right|^{1-\beta}}+\frac{1}{\left|\frac{x}{y}+1\right|^{1-\beta}}\right)\,dy.

This means that (
2.5
) holds for
d=1
with

F_{\beta}(r)=\frac{1}{\left|r-1\right|^{1-\beta}}+\frac{1}{\left|r+1\right|^{1-\beta}}.

For its the asymptotic behaviour, we still have (
2.7
)-(
2.8
), while

\lim_{r\to 1}\frac{F_{\beta}(r)}{|r-1|^{\beta-1}}=1.

Below we will also make use of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, which gives conditions under which
\mathcal{I}_{\beta}
is a
bounded operator between specific
L^{p}
spaces. Let
\beta\in(0,d)
,
p_{\beta}:=\frac{dp}{dp-\beta}
be the
Sobolev exponent, and
p>1
. Then

(2.9)

\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f\right\|_{p_{\beta}}\leq C_{p,\beta}\left\|f\right\|_{p}

holds. A direct consequence is the inequality

(2.10)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(x)||g(x)|}{|x-y|^{\delta}}\,dx\,dy\leq C_{p,\delta,d}\left\|f\right\|_{p}\left\|g\right\|_{q},

where
\delta\in(0,d)
,
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=2-\frac{\delta}{d}
.

2.3.
Resolvent estimates

For every operator
L_{m,\alpha}
,
m\geq 0
, introduced in Section 2.1 above, the one-parameter operator semigroup

\{e^{-tL_{m,\alpha}}:t\geq 0\}
can be defined by functional calculus, each of whose elements is an integral operator
for
t>0
. The integral kernel
p(t,x,y)=e^{-tL_{m,\alpha}}(x,y)
,
t>0
, which we call heat kernel as in the literature,
satisfies the property
p(t,x,y)=p(t,x-y,0)
and, for fixed
t>0
,
p(t,\cdot,0)
is rotationally symmetric. Throughout the paper we
use the simpler conventional notation
p_{t}(x)
. It is well-known that
for
m=0
the semigroup is transient whenever
\alpha<d
, and for
m>0
whenever
d\geq 3
, while in the complementary cases
the semigroups are recurrent. In our considerations below we will occasionally have to make a distinction between transient and
recurrent cases. Results relying on the
0
-resolvent operator will apply only in the transient case, since otherwise

0
-resolvents are not well-defined. On the other hand, results relying on
\lambda
-resolvents with
\lambda>0
can be
stated also in the recurrent case.

The following heat kernel estimates are known, for a proof see
[
3
]
,
[
6
, Th. 1.2]
,
[
7
, Th. 4.1]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

(1)

Let
p_{t}(x)
be the integral kernel of the operator
e^{-tL_{0,\alpha}}
. Then

(2.11)

p_{t}(x)\,\,\asymp\,\,t|x|^{-d-\alpha}\wedge t^{-d/\alpha}

holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
.

(2)

Let
p^{m}_{t}(x)
be the integral kernel of the operator
e^{-tL_{m,\alpha}}
,
m>0
. Then

(2.12)

\displaystyle p^{m}_{t}(x)\asymp\begin{cases}\ t^{-d/\alpha}\wedge t|x|^{-(d+\alpha)/2}\kappa_{m}(|x|)&{\rm if}\quad 0<t\leq\frac{1}{m}\\
\\
\ m^{d/\alpha-d/2}t^{-d/2}e^{-c\left(m^{1/\alpha}|x|\wedge m^{2/\alpha-1}|x|^{2}/t\right)}&{\rm if}\quad t>\frac{1}{m}\end{cases}

hold with
c>0
and

(2.13)

\kappa_{m}(r)=m^{(d+\alpha)/(2\alpha)}K_{(d+\alpha)/2}(m^{1/\alpha}r).

For every
\lambda>0
, let
R^{m}_{\lambda}=(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}
,
m\geq 0
, be the resolvent of
the relativistic operator, with integral kernel
R^{m}_{\lambda}(x-y)
. We have then

R^{m}_{\lambda}(x-y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}p^{m}_{t}(x-y)dt,\quad m\geq 0,

and similarly to the heat kernel write just
R^{m}_{\lambda}(x)
. For the massless case we drop the superscripts
for all these objects.

Our results below will largely depend on detailed resolvent estimates for the fractional and relativistic Laplace
operators. We will need uniform (in
\lambda
), and pointwise upper and lower estimates on the resolvent kernel

R^{m}_{\lambda}
separately for large
\lambda
and for
\lambda=0
, both for the fractional (
m=0
) and the
relativistic (
m>0)
Laplacians. These results stand also on their own, and can be applied in independent
directions such as potential theory.

We have the following uniform resolvent kernel estimates in the transient massless (
d>\alpha
) and transient
massive (
d>2
) cases. For every
\lambda\geq 0

(2.14)

\displaystyle R^{m}_{\lambda}(x)\leq\begin{cases}C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{\alpha-d}&\mbox{if \, $m=0$}\\
&\\
C_{d,\alpha}\big(|x|^{\alpha-d}+m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}{|x|^{2-d}}\big)&\mbox{if \, $m>0$}\end{cases}

holds with appropriate constant
C_{d,\alpha}>0
. In fact, for
\lambda=0
the expression

R_{0}(x)=C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{\alpha-d},

holds
[
23
, Prop. 4.293]
, while for the massive case we have

(2.15)

R^{m}_{0}(x)\asymp C_{d,\alpha,m}\left(|x|^{\alpha-d}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}+|x|^{2-d}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|>1\}}\right).

Throughout the paper we will also make use of the non-uniform estimate

(2.16)

\displaystyle R^{m}_{\lambda}(x)\leq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}H^{0}_{d,\alpha}(|x|),

which holds for every
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 1
,
m\geq 0
and
\lambda>0
with

(2.17)

H_{d,\alpha}^{0}(r):=\begin{cases}r^{-(d-\alpha)}\mathbf{1}_{\{r\leq 1\}}+2^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\right)r^{-(d+\alpha)}\mathbf{1}_{\{r>1\}}&\mbox{if \, $\alpha<d$}\\
&\\
\log\left(1+\frac{1}{r}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{r\leq 1\}}+2^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\right)r^{-2}\mathbf{1}_{\{r>1\}}&\mbox{if \, $\alpha=d=1$}\\
&\\
\mathbf{1}_{\{r\leq 1\}}+2^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\right)r^{-(1+\alpha)}\mathbf{1}_{\{r>1\}}&\mbox{if \, $\alpha>d=1$}.\end{cases}

Again, for
m=0
we actually have

(2.18)

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(x)\asymp C_{d,\alpha,\lambda}H^{0}_{d,\alpha}(|x|).

In the massive case we do not have this equivalence. However, for
m>0
and
\lambda>m
, we prove
that

(2.19)

\displaystyle R^{m}_{\lambda}(x)\asymp C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}H^{m}_{d,\alpha}(|x|),

where

(2.20)

H^{m}_{d,\alpha}(r):=\begin{cases}r^{-(d-\alpha)}\mathbf{1}_{\{r\leq 1\}}+r^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(r)\mathbf{1}_{\{r>0\}}&\mbox{if \, $\alpha<d$}\\
&\\
\log\left(1+\frac{1}{r}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{r\leq 1\}}+r^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(r)\mathbf{1}_{\{r>0\}}&\mbox{if \, $\alpha=d=1$}\\
&\\
\mathbf{1}_{\{r\leq 1\}}+r^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(r)\mathbf{1}_{\{r>0\}}&\mbox{if \, $\alpha>d=1$}.\end{cases}

We note that

(2.21)

\displaystyle R^{m}_{\lambda}(x)\geq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}H^{m}_{d,\alpha}(|x|)

holds in fact for all
m,\lambda>0
. It is also worth recalling that in (
2.16
) the constant satisfies

(2.22)

C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}\sim C_{d,\alpha}\lambda^{-2}\left(1+e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}\right)\quad\mbox{ as }\quad\lambda\downarrow 0.

The proof of these resolvent estimates is given in the Appendix.

3.
Fractional Rollnik class

3.1.
Definitions and basic properties

3.1.1.
Fractional Rollnik norm

Making use of (
2.14
) we introduce the following class of potentials for the operators
L_{m,\alpha}
,
m\geq 0
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\alpha<2
, and assume that

(3.1)

0<\alpha<d<2\alpha.

(1)

Let
m=0
. We call

\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}=\left\{V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\,\|V\|^{2}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2(d-\alpha)}}dxdy<\infty\right\}

fractional Rollnik-class of order
\alpha
for the massless Laplacian
L_{0,\alpha}
.

(2)

Let
d=3
and
m>0
. We define the
fractional Rollnik-class of order
\alpha
for the massive
Laplacian
L_{m,\alpha}
by
\mathcal{R}^{m}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}\cap\mathcal{R}
, where

\mathcal{R}
is the classical Rollnik class.

It is seen that the study of the massive case reduces to the study of the massless case. First therefore
we focus on the class
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
and as we progress we will consider extensions, which
will also accommodate cases of recurrent semigroups.

We begin by showing that the expression
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
used in the definition of

\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
is indeed a norm and that
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
is a Banach space with
respect to this norm. Before that, we note the following two properties which will be used below.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have that
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
and

\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{(2r(K))^{2d-2\alpha}}\left\|V\right\|_{L^{1}(K)}^{2},

where
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is any compact set and
r(K)=\inf\{r\geq 0:\ K\subset B_{r}\}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, it will be sufficient to show this for every ball
B_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then we have

\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}\geq\int_{B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}|V(x)||V(y)||x-y|^{2\alpha-2d}\,dx\,dy\geq(2r)^{2\alpha-2d}\left\|V\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{r})}^{2}.

∎

A straightforward but useful consequence of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality is the following inclusion
property.

Proposition 3.3
.

L^{d/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuously embedded in
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, i.e.,

\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}\leq C_{d,\alpha}\left\|V\right\|_{d/\alpha}.

Proof.

It is immediate by (
2.10
) with
\sigma=2(d-\alpha)
and
p=q=\frac{d}{\alpha}
.
∎

Apart from
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
, we can also define the quantity

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|V\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)\overline{V(y)}|x-y|^{2\alpha-2d}\,dx\,dy

on
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, where we allow
V
to be possibly complex-valued. Next we prove that both
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}

and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
are norms on
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
; we take inspiration from a strategy proposed in

[
24
, Sect. 4]
for
d=1
only, with substantial modifications. Recall the factor
\gamma_{d}
from (
2.2
).

Theorem 3.4
.

The functionals
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}

are norms on
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. Furthermore, for all
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
we have

(3.2)

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|V\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}=\gamma_{d}(2\alpha-d)\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}V\right\|_{2}^{2}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}=\gamma_{d}(2\alpha-d)\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}|V|\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof will proceed through several steps.

Step 1:

First we show (
3.2
), starting with assuming that
V\in\mathcal{F}\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Clearly, by the definition of
Lizorkin space, we know that
V\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and then
V\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{d/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which in turn
implies
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. Furthermore, also
\mathcal{I}_{2\alpha-d}V\in\mathcal{F}\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
Theorem
2.2
. Hence, by an application of Fubini’s theorem and Plancherel’s identity we obtain

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|V\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{d}(2\alpha-d)\left(V,\mathcal{I}_{2\alpha-d}V\right)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{d}(2\alpha-d)\left(\mathcal{F}[V],|\cdot|^{d-2\alpha}\mathcal{F}[V]\right)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{d}(2\alpha-d)\left(|\cdot|^{\frac{d}{2}-\alpha}\mathcal{F}[V],|\cdot|^{\frac{d}{2}-\alpha}\mathcal{F}[V]\right)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\gamma_{d}(2\alpha-d)\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}V\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Step 2:

Next assume that
V\in L^{d/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
\mathcal{F}\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
L^{d/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by Theorem
2.1
,
we may choose a sequence
(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{F}\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
V_{n}\to V\in L^{d/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
V_{n}-V\in L^{d/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by Proposition
3.3

we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V_{n}(x)V_{n}(y)|x-y|^{2\alpha-2d}dx\,dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)V(y)|x-y|^{2\alpha-2d}dx\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V_{n}(x)-V(x)||V_{n}(y)||x-y|^{2\alpha-2d}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V(x)||V_{n}(y)-V(y)||x-y|^{2\alpha-2d}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{d,\alpha}\left\|V_{n}-V\right\|_{d/\alpha}\left(\left\|V_{n}\right\|_{{d/\alpha}}+\left\|V\right\|_{{d/\alpha}}\right)\to 0\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$}.

On the other hand, by the triangle inequality and (
2.9
),

\left|\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}V_{n}\right\|_{2}-\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}V\right\|_{2}\right|\leq\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}(V_{n}-V)\right\|_{2}\leq C_{d,\alpha}\left\|V_{n}-V\right\|_{d/\alpha}\to 0.

This proves the first equality in (
3.2
) for
V\in L^{d/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The second equality follows by applying the
first on
|V|\in L^{d/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Step 3:

To complete this part of the proof, consider now any
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
\eta_{M}
be a

C^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
function such
that
\eta_{M}(x)=1
for every
x\in B_{M}
,
\eta_{M}(x)\in[0,1]
and
{\rm supp}(\eta_{M})\subset B_{M+1}
. Then
V_{M}:=\min\{|V|,M\}\eta_{M}\in L^{{d/\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and (
3.2
) holds for this function. Next, since

V_{M}\leq V_{M+1}
for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
, by the monotone convergence theorem we have

\lim_{M\to\infty}\left\|V_{M}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}=\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\lim_{M\to\infty}\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}V_{M}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}|V|\right\|_{2}^{2}.

This proves the second equality in (
3.2
). To prove the first, notice that we have in fact shown
that if
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, then
\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}|V|\right\|_{2}<\infty
. Hence by setting
V_{M}=\max\{\min\{V,M\},-M\}\eta_{M}
, we can use the dominated convergence theorem to obtain

\lim_{M\to\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|V_{M}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|V\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\lim_{M\to\infty}\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}V_{M}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}V\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Step 4:

Finally we show the norm property.
Identity (
3.2
) guarantees that for all
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, both
\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}

and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|V\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
are non-negative. Furthermore, note that directly by the definition of the
Riesz potential, the facts
V_{1},V_{2}\geq 0
and
V_{1}\leq V_{2}
, imply
\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}V_{1}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}V_{2}\right\|_{2}
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\left\|V_{1}+V_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\gamma(2\alpha-d)}\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}|V_{1}+V_{2}|\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{\gamma(2\alpha-d)}\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}|V_{1}|+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}|V_{2}|\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\gamma(2\alpha-d)}\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}|V_{1}|\right\|_{2}+\sqrt{\gamma(2\alpha-d)}\left\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}|V_{2}|\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|V_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}+\left\|V_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}.

The fact that
\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}=0
implies
V=0
a.e. follows by injectivity of the Riesz potential

\mathcal{I}_{2\alpha-d}
. The same argument can be repeated for
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
, which proves that
both
are norms.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Notice that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
is induced by the scalar product

(V_{1},V_{2})_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V_{1}(x)\overline{V_{2}(y)}|x-y|^{2\alpha-2d}\,dx\,dy.

We can prove the following completeness result with respect to the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

The fractional Rollnik class
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
is
a Banach space.

Proof.

Let
(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. By Lemma
3.2
it is a Cauchy sequence also in

L^{1}(B_{m})
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By a simple diagonal argument we know that there exists a subsequence

(V_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a function
V
such that
V_{n_{k}}(x)\to V(x)
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By Fatou’s
lemma we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V(x)-V_{n_{k}}(x)||V(y)-V_{n_{k}}(y)||x-y|^{2\alpha-2d}\,dx\,dy\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\left\|V_{n_{j}}-V_{n_{k}}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since
(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, there exists a label
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such
that
\left\|V_{n_{j}}-V_{n_{k}}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}<\varepsilon
holds for all
j,k\geq k_{0}
. Taking the limit inferior, this
means that for all
k\geq k_{0}

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V(x)-V_{n_{k}}(x)||V(y)-V_{n_{k}}(y)||x-y|^{2\alpha-2d}\,dx\,dy\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\left\|V_{n_{j}}-V_{n_{k}}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}<\varepsilon^{2}.

This proves that
V-V_{n_{k}}\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, for all
k\geq k_{0}
, and thus also
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. Furthermore,
for all
k\geq k_{0}
we have

\left\|V-V_{n_{k}}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}<\varepsilon
.
Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we get
V_{n_{k}}\to V
in
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. This is sufficient to conclude that
V_{n}\to V
in
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

It is natural to ask whether
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
is a Hilbert space when equipped with the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
. Adapting an argument in
[
24
]
originally only for
d=1
, we see that this is not the case.

Theorem 3.7
.

The fractional Rollnik class
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
is not complete with respect to the norm

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
.

Proof.

Assume, to the contrary, that
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
is complete with respect to
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
.
Consider any function
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\phi\not\in\Dom((-\Delta)^{(2\alpha-d)/4})
. Since

\mathcal{F}\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a sequence
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{F}\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\phi_{n}\to\phi
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Define the sequence of functions

f_{n}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[|\cdot|^{\frac{d}{2}-\alpha}\mathcal{F}[\phi_{n}]]\,\in\,\mathcal{F}\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\subset\,\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}.

Since
\phi_{n}\to\phi
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, also
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}f_{n}\to\phi
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, hence

(\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By the first equality in (
3.2
),
this implies that
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
(\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha},{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}})
. Since
by assumption this space is complete, there exists a function
f
such that
f_{n}\to f
in

(\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha},{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}})
. Again by (
3.2
), we know that

\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}f_{n}\to\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}f
. However,
this is impossible since then we would have
\phi=\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}f\in\Dom((-\Delta)^{(2\alpha-d)/4})
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

We note that if
V_{1},V_{2}
are two potentials such that
|V_{1}(x)|\leq|V_{2}(x)|
a.e. and
V_{2}\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, then
clearly also
V_{1}\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. Furthermore, by Lemma
3.2
, it is clear that if
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
,
then for all
R>0

(3.3)

\left\|V\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}\leq 2^{d-\alpha}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}R^{d-\alpha}.

This means that fractional Rollnik-class potentials tend to concentrate their mass at infinity.

3.1.2.
Restrictions on the fractional exponent

Next we turn to discussing the role of condition (
3.1
). A straightforward calculation shows that

|x|^{-2(d-\alpha)}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
only when
\alpha<2d
. This implies that if
d\geq 2\alpha
there
exist no continuous potentials
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
apart from
V\equiv 0
. In fact, we can prove the following
more general property.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
k:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a function such that for all
\delta>0

(3.4)

\int_{0}^{\delta}r^{d-1}k(r)=\infty.

Suppose that
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function such that

(3.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V(x)||V(y)|k(|x-y|)dxdy<\infty.

Then for any choice of
\varepsilon,r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
there is no ball
B_{r}(x)
such that
|V(y)|>\varepsilon
for a.e.
y\in B_{r}(x)
. In particular, if
V
is continuous, then
V\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Let
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be satisfying (
3.5
) and assume, to the contrary, that there exist

\varepsilon^{\ast},r^{\ast}>0
and
x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|V(y)|>\varepsilon^{\ast}
for all
y\in B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V(x)||V(y)|k(|x-y|)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}|V(x)||V(y)|k(|x-y|)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon^{2}\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}k(|x-y|)\,dx\,dy,

which implies
\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}k(|x-y|)\,dx\,dy<\infty
.
However, by (
3.4
) we also have

\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}k(|x-y|)\,dx\,dy\geq\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}\int_{B_{r^{\ast}-|x-x^{\ast}|}(x)}k(|x-y|)\,dx\,dy=\infty,

which is impossible.
∎

In fact, a fractional Rollnik-class cannot be obtained even in the limit
\alpha\uparrow\frac{d}{2}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
d=1,2
and suppose
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function such that
\liminf\limits_{\alpha\downarrow\frac{d}{2}}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}<\infty
. Then for any choice of
\varepsilon,r>0

and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
there is no ball
B_{r}(x)
such that
|V(y)|>\varepsilon
for a.e.
y\in B_{r}(x)
.
In particular, if
V
is continuous, then
V\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Let
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\liminf\limits_{\alpha\downarrow\frac{d}{2}}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}<\infty
and assume, to the contrary, that there exists
\varepsilon^{*},r^{*}
and
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

|V(y)|>\varepsilon^{*}
for almost every
y\in B_{r^{*}}(x^{*})
. Let also
\alpha_{k}
be such that
\alpha_{k}\downarrow\frac{d}{2}
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha_{k}}}=\liminf\limits_{\alpha\downarrow\frac{d}{2}}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha_{k}}}<\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Observe that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}

(3.6)

\infty>\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V(x)||V(y)||x-y|^{2\alpha_{k}-2d}dxdy\geq\varepsilon^{2}\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}|x-y|^{2\alpha_{k}-2d}dxdy.

On the other hand,

\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}|x-y|^{2\alpha_{k}-2d}dxdy\geq\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}\int_{B_{r^{\ast}-|x-x^{\ast}|}(x)}|x-y|^{2\alpha_{k}-2d}dydx.

We can evaluate the inner integral for
x\in B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})
as

\int_{B_{r^{\ast}-|x-x^{\ast}|}(x)}|x-y|^{2\alpha_{k}-2d}dy=\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{r^{\ast}-|x-x^{\ast}|}\rho^{2\alpha_{k}-d-1}d\rho=\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2\alpha_{k}-d}(r^{\ast}-|x-x^{\ast}|)^{2\alpha_{k}-d}

and thus

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}\int_{B_{r^{\ast}-|x-x^{\ast}|}(x)}|x-y|^{2\alpha_{k}-2d}dydx

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{d}^{2}}{2\alpha_{k}-d}\int_{0}^{r^{\ast}}(r^{\ast}-\rho)^{2\alpha_{k}-d}\rho^{d-1}d\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{d}^{2}}{2\alpha_{k}-d}(r^{\ast})^{2\alpha_{k}}B(2\alpha_{k}-d+1;d),

where
B(x,y)
is the Beta function. Taking the limit as
k\to\infty
we get

\int_{B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})}\int_{B_{r^{\ast}-|x-x^{\ast}|}(x)}|x-y|^{2\alpha_{k}-2d}dydx=\infty,

which is in contradiction with (
3.6
).
∎

Clearly, by (
3.1
) we have the possible ranges
D_{\alpha}
of
\alpha
as follows:

D_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}(\frac{1}{2},1)&\mbox{if \, $d=1$}\\
(1,2)&\mbox{if \, $d=2$}\\
(\frac{3}{2},2)&\mbox{if \, $d=3$}\\
\emptyset&\mbox{if \, $d\geq 4$}.\end{cases}

This just excludes the case
\alpha=1
, which will be dealt with below. In Section
3.4

we introduce an extended fractional Rollnik-class which is subject only to the constraint
d<2\alpha
, in
particular, the case
\alpha=1
is covered by it in the
d=1
case. In
d=2
we will obtain it in Section

5
below as a limiting case. As Proposition
3.10
suggests, this limit is not
straightforward and we arrive at defining

\mathcal{R}_{d,1}^{p}:=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\cap\,\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow 1}\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha},\quad p>1.

For later use below, we define more generally for
\alpha=\frac{d}{2}
,
d=1,2,3
, the class

(3.7)

\mathcal{R}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}^{p}:=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\cap\,\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow\frac{d}{2}}\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha},\quad p>1,

where the limit inferior means
\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow\frac{d}{2}}\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}=\cup_{\beta\in\left(\frac{d}{2},d\right)}\cap_{\alpha\in\left(\frac{d}{2},\beta\right)}\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. The
intersection with the
L^{p}
spaces in the definition will be justified by Theorem
5.3
below.

3.2.
Comparison properties

We have already seen in Proposition
3.3
that
L^{d/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. A corollary to this result is the following.

Proposition 3.11
.

If
p\leq\frac{d}{\alpha}\leq q
, then
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
.

Proof.

By interpolation it is immediate that
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{d/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
.
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

If
\alpha>\frac{d}{2}
, then
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
and the estimate

(3.8)

\|V\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}\leq\left(\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2(d-\alpha)}\right)^{(d-\alpha)/d}\sqrt{\frac{d}{2\alpha-d}}\,\|V\|_{1}^{(2\alpha-d)/d}\|V\|_{2}^{2(d-\alpha)/d}

holds, where
\sigma_{d}
is the surface area of the
d
-dimensional unit sphere.

Proof.

Let
V\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\delta>0
. We compute

\iint_{|x-y|\geq\delta}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2(d-\alpha)}}dxdy\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{2(d-\alpha)}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|V(x)||V(y)|dxdy=\frac{\|V\|_{1}^{2}}{\delta^{2(d-\alpha)}}.

By the Schwarz inequality, we also have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\iint_{|x-y|<\delta}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2(d-\alpha)}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\iint_{|x-y|<\delta}\frac{|V(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2(d-\alpha)}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|V\|_{2}^{2}\int_{|x|<\delta}\frac{dx}{|x|^{2(d-\alpha)}}=\frac{\sigma_{d}\delta^{2\alpha-d}}{2\alpha-d}\|V\|_{2}^{2},

and thus

\|V\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}\leq\frac{\|V\|_{1}^{2}}{\delta^{2(d-\alpha)}}+\frac{\sigma_{d}\delta^{2\alpha-d}}{2\alpha-d}\|V\|_{2}^{2}.

Optimizing over
\delta
by the choice

\delta=\left(\frac{(2(d-\alpha)\|V\|_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{d}\|V\|_{2}^{2}}\right)^{1/d}

leads to (
3.8
).
∎

Proposition 3.13
.

The following inclusions hold:

(1)

Let
\alpha\leq\alpha_{1}\leq d
. Then
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha_{1}}
.

(2)

Let
\alpha_{2}\leq\alpha
and
\frac{d}{\alpha_{2}}<p\wedge q
for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Then

\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha_{2}}
. In
particular,
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha_{2}}
.

(3)

Let
\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{2}
. Then
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha_{1}}\cap\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha_{2}}\subset\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
.

Proof.

(1) Let
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. As in Proposition
3.12
, we compute

\iint_{|x-y|\geq 1}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2d-2\alpha_{1}}}dxdy\leq\|V\|_{1}^{2}.

We clearly have

\iint_{|x-y|<1}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2d-2\alpha_{1}}}dxdy\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2d-2\alpha}}dxdy=\|V\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}

using that
2d-2\alpha_{1}\leq 2d-2\alpha
.

(2)
Let
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We have

\iint_{|x-y|\geq 1}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2d-2\alpha_{2}}}dxdy\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2d-2\alpha}}dxdy=\|V\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}

since
2d-2\alpha_{2}\geq 2d-2\alpha
. On the other hand, as in (
3.9
), we compute

\iint_{|x-y|<1}\frac{|V(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{pd-p\alpha_{2}}}dxdy\leq\frac{\sigma_{d}}{p\alpha_{2}-pd+d}\|V\|_{p}^{p}

noting that
p\alpha_{2}-pd+d>0
is equivalent to
d/\alpha_{2}<q
. Therefore, by the Hölder inequality
we obtain

\iint_{|x-y|<1}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2d-2\alpha_{2}}}dxdy\leq\left(\frac{\sigma_{d}}{p\alpha_{2}-pd+d}\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{\sigma_{d}}{q\alpha_{2}-qd+d}\right)^{1/q}\|V\|_{p}\|V\|_{q}.

(3) We have

\displaystyle\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\iint_{|x-y|<1}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2d-2\alpha}}dx\,dy+\iint_{|x-y|\geq 1}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2d-2\alpha}}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{|x-y|<1}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2d-2\alpha_{1}}}dx\,dy+\iint_{|x-y|\geq 1}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2d-2\alpha_{2}}}dx\,dy\leq\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha_{1}}}^{2}+\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha_{2}}}^{2}.

∎

By extension of the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
to
\alpha=2
we consider the space

\mathcal{R}_{3,2}
which coincides with the classical Rollnik-class given by (
1.1
). A
consequence of part (3) in Proposition
3.13
and the dominated convergence theorem is the following
result.

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
d=3
and
V\in\mathcal{R}_{3,\alpha_{0}}\cap\mathcal{R}_{3,2}
for some
\alpha_{0}\in\left(\frac{3}{2},2\right)
. Then
the function
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},2]\mapsto\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}
is continuous.

Remark 3.15
.

We see that for
\mathcal{R}_{d,2}
the only possibility permitted by (
3.1
) is
d=3
and

\mathcal{R}_{3,2}=\mathcal{R}
. Corollary
3.14
shows, moreover, continuity as
\alpha\uparrow 2
.
Also, for
d=3
and
\alpha=2
, estimate (
3.8
) gives

\|V\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\sqrt{3}(2\pi)^{1/3}\|V\|_{1}^{1/3}\|V\|_{2}^{2/3},

which coincides with the standard norm estimate for the classical Rollnik-class.

Next we compare the fractional Rollnik-class with the domain of the Riesz potential. Recall the definition in
(
2.4
).

Proposition 3.16
.

The following properties hold.

(1)

\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}\subset{\mathfrak{X}}^{d}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}
.

(2)

If
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
satisfies
|\{V(x)=0\}|=0
, then
V\in{\mathfrak{X}}^{d}_{2\alpha-d}
.

Proof.

To prove (1), just observe that by (
3.2
) it follows that if
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, then

\mathcal{I}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}|V|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, in particular it is a.e. finite. Hence
|V|\in{\mathfrak{X}}^{d}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}
and then also
V\in{\mathfrak{X}}^{d}_{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}
. Next
consider (2) and define the measure
\mu_{V}(dx)=|V(x)|dx
. Since
|\{V(x)=0\}|=0
, the measure
\mu_{V}
is
equivalent to Lebesgue measure. Since
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{I}_{2\alpha-d}|V|(x)\mu_{V}(dx)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V(x)|\mathcal{I}_{2\alpha-d}|V|(x)dx<\infty,

hence
\mathcal{I}_{2\alpha-d}|V|
is
\mu_{V}
-a.e. finite. Since
\mu_{V}
and Lebesgue measure are equivalent,

\mathcal{I}_{2\alpha-d}|V|
is a.e. finite and then
|V|\in{\mathfrak{X}}^{d}_{2\alpha-d}
, which in turn implies

V\in{\mathfrak{X}}^{d}_{2\alpha-d}
.
∎

Finally, we discuss the relationship between fractional Rollnik-class and relativistic Kato-class by borrowing
some results from Section 3.3.4 below. Recall that a potential
V
is in relativistic Kato-class

\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha}
if

(3.10)

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|x-y|<\delta}R_{1}^{m}(x-y)|V(y)|dy=0,

and
V
is said to be relativistic Kato-decomposable if
V_{-}\in\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha}
and
V_{+}\mathbf{1}_{K}\in\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha}
for all compact sets
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
V^{+},V^{-}
denote positive and negative
parts, respectively, see
[
23
, Def. 4.280]
. Using the resolvent estimates, we can provide an alternative
formulation of (
3.10
).

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,2)
, and
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a potential. For any
\delta>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
define

{}_{\delta}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}V(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|<\delta}\frac{|V(y)|}{|x-y|^{d-\alpha}}dy&\alpha<d\\
\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|<\delta}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right)|V(y)|dy&\alpha=d=1\\
\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|<\delta}|V(y)|dy&\alpha>d=1.\end{cases}

Then
V\in\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha}
holds if and only if
\lim\limits_{\delta\downarrow 0}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{}_{\delta}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}V(x)=0
.

Proof.

The statement easily follows by (
2.19
), which implies that there exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha,m}

such that for all
\delta<1
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\frac{1}{C_{d,\alpha,m}}{}_{\delta}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}V(x)\leq\int_{|x-y|<\delta}R_{1}^{m}(x-y)V(y)dy\leq C_{d,\alpha,m}{}_{\delta}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}V(x).

∎

Notice that, up to a constant, for
\alpha<d
the expression
{}_{\delta}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}
is a truncated Riesz potential.
Furthermore, we note that the above result extends
[
23
, Prop. 4.295]
to the massive case, also proving the converse
implication. It is seen that the condition defining relativistic Kato-class is independent of the mass
m
.

Using the above characterization we can check that if
\alpha<d
, the Coulomb-type potential
V(x)=|x|^{-\beta}
,

\beta<\alpha
, belongs to
\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha}
(see
[
23
, Ex. 4.296]
). As a consequence, also the local Coulomb-type
potential
V(x)=|x|^{-\beta}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<1\}}\in\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha}
whenever
\beta<\alpha
. In Proposition

3.20
below we show that this potential is also in fractional Rollnik-class, which then implies that

\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha}\cap\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}\not=\emptyset
. On the other hand, the outer Coulomb-type potential
V(x)=|x|^{-\beta}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\geq 1\}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha}
for all
\beta>0
, while it belongs
to
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
if and only if
\beta>\alpha
, see Proposition
3.23
below. Hence
\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}\not=\emptyset
. On the other hand, taking a critical local Coulomb-type
potential with logarithmic correction
V(x)=|x|^{-\alpha}(-\log|x|)^{-\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<1/2\}}
,
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, in Proposition
3.21
below we show that
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, while

{}_{\delta}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}V(0)=\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{\delta}r^{-1}(-\log r)^{-\gamma}dr=\infty\quad\mbox{for all $\delta<\frac{1}{2}$},

hence
V\not\in\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha}
. This shows that
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha}\not=\emptyset
. Furthermore,
it is clear that
V(x)=|x|^{-\alpha}(-\log|x|)^{-\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<1/2\}}
is not even Kato-decomposable. Hence
it is seen that the fractional Rollnik-class and the relativistic Kato-class overlap, but neither contains the other.

3.3.
Specific cases

3.3.1.
Potentials with compact support

Let
V_{K}=-\mathbf{1}_{K}
for a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then clearly
V_{K}\in L^{d/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. We compare the Rollnik norm of
V_{K}
with the norm of
V_{B^{K}}
, where
B^{K}
is
a ball centred in the origin having the same volume as
K
. Recall the fractional perimeter of index
s\in(0,1)
of a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

\Per_{s}(E)=\frac{s2^{s-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+s}{2}\right)}{\pi^{d/2}\Gamma\left(1-\frac{s}{2}\right)}\int_{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus E}\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{d+s}}.

A sufficient condition for a set
E
to have a finite fractional perimeter of order
s
is that its indicator
function
\mathbf{1}_{E}
belongs to the fractional Sobolev space
W^{\frac{s}{p},p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In particular,
E

has finite fractional perimeter if it has finite classical perimeter
\Per(E)
. The inequality

(3.11)

\Per_{s}(E)\geq\Per_{s}(B^{E}),\ s\in(0,1]

is well-known. Indeed, the case
s=1
is the classical isoperimetric inequality, while for
s\in(0,1)
it can
be obtained as a consequence of
[
15
, Th. 4.1]
. For quantitative versions of (
3.11
) we
refer to
[
17
,
16
]
.

A straightforward consequence of the Riesz rearrangement inequality
[
27
,
5
]
gives

(3.12)

\left\|V_{K}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}\leq\left\|V_{B^{K}}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}},\quad 0<\alpha<d<2\alpha.

Combining the two inequalities above, we get for any compact set
K
with finite
s
-perimeter

\left\|V_{K}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}\leq\left\|V_{B^{K}}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}=\frac{\left\|V_{B^{K}}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}}{\Per_{s}(B^{K})}\Per_{s}(B^{K})\leq\frac{\left\|V_{B^{K}}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}}{\Per_{s}(B^{K})}\Per_{s}(K),

where the constant
\left\|V_{B^{K}}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}/\Per_{s}(B^{K})
is sharp in the sense that all the
inequalities become equalities if
K=B^{K}
. The constant can be exactly evaluated. Indeed, by
[
13
, Prop. 2.3]

we have

\Per_{s}(B^{K})=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{|K|}{\omega_{d}}\right)^{\frac{d-s}{d}}\frac{\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{d+s}{2}\right)\right)^{2}}{2\pi\Gamma\left(1-\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d-s}{2}+1\right)}\sigma_{d}&s\in(0,1)\\
\left(\frac{|K|}{\omega_{d}}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{d}}\sigma_{d}&s=1.\end{cases}

On the other hand, by using
[
13
, Prop. 7.5]
we obtain

\left\|V_{B^{K}}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}=\left(\frac{|K|}{\omega_{d}}\right)^{\frac{d+\alpha}{d}}\frac{\sigma_{d}}{\alpha(d+\alpha)}\mu_{2\alpha-d},

where

\mu_{2\alpha-d}=\begin{cases}2^{1+2\alpha-d}\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+2\alpha-d}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}&\alpha\not=\frac{d+1}{2}\\
\frac{16\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)}&\alpha=\frac{d+1}{2}.\end{cases}

3.3.2.
Asymmetric fractional Rollnik potentials

By (
3.12
) symmetric rearrangement increases the fractional Rollnik norm, and since the kernel in the
Rollnik integral is spherically symmetric, the inequality reduces to equality only for spherically symmetric
potentials. We can use the fractional Rollnik norm to estimate the symmetry deficit of a non-radial
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
potential from a rotationally symmetric potential. We discuss this by comparing with a spherical
potential well.

Let
V\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
V\leq 0
a.e. Clearly,
V\in L^{d/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
.
Denote by
B^{V}
the ball centered in the origin with volume
\left\|V\right\|_{1}/\left\|V\right\|_{\infty}
, and consider the
family of potential wells
V_{B,a}:=-\left\|V\right\|_{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{B^{V}+a}
, for
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. As a direct consequence
of
[
14
, Th. 4]
we get

\left\|V_{B,0}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}-\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}\geq\frac{C_{d,\alpha}}{2}\left(\frac{\left\|V\right\|_{\infty}}{\left\|V\right\|_{1}}\right)^{\frac{2(d-\alpha)}{d}}\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|V-V_{B,a}\right\|_{1}^{2},

i.e., the difference of the fractional Rollnik norms of
V
and the spherical potential well
V_{B}
controls from
above an
L^{1}
distance of
V
from the family of spherical potential wells with the same
L^{\infty}
and
L^{1}
norms.

3.3.3.
Rotationally symmetric potentials

Consider a generic potential
V
and denote by
V^{\ast}
the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
|V|
. Then the
following property holds.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
such that
\alpha<d<2\alpha
. If
V^{\ast}\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, then

V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
and

(3.13)

\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}\leq\left\|V^{\ast}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}.

Equality holds if and only if there exists a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with
|{\rm det}(A)|=1
and a vector

a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(3.14)

|V(x)|=V^{\ast}(Ax+a).

Furthermore, if
V
is continuous, then equality holds if and only if there exists a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with

|{\rm det}(A)|=1
and a vector
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(3.15)

\mbox{either}\quad V(x)=V^{\ast}(Ax+a),\quad\mbox{ or }\quad V(x)=-V^{\ast}(Ax+a).

Proof.

The inequality (
3.13
) follows by a direct application of Riesz rearrangement inequality
[
27
]
. If equality
holds in (
3.13
), then we are under the assumptions of
[
22
, Lem. 3]
and (
3.14
) holds. If,
moreover,
V
is continuous, then
V^{\ast}(x)=0
for some
x
with
|x|=R
implies
V^{\ast}(x)=0
for all

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|x|\geq R
. Thus either
V^{\ast}(Ax+a)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, or there exists a bounded open set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
V^{\ast}(Ax+a)=0
exactly when
x\not\in K^{c}
. By (
3.14
) this means that
V

cannot change sign and so (
3.15
) holds.
∎

We focus now on the case when
V
is a non-negative radially symmetric potential, with a non-increasing profile (or,
equivalently, on
V^{\ast}
for any potential
V
). Let this profile be
v:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
such that
v(t)=V(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|x|=t
. Making use of (
3.3
) we show a bound on
v
near the origin.

Theorem 3.19
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
with
\alpha<d<2\alpha
. Suppose that
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
is non-negative,
radially symmetric, with non-increasing profile
v
. Then there exists
t_{0}>0
such that

v(t)<\frac{2^{d-\alpha}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}}{\omega_{d}}t^{-\alpha},\quad t\in(0,t_{0}).

Proof.

Consider the function

U(r)=\int_{0}^{r}t^{d-1}v(t)dt\leq\frac{2^{d-\alpha}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}}{\sigma_{d}}r^{d-\alpha},

where the bound follows by (
3.3
). Assume, to the contrary, that there exists a sequence
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

t_{n}\downarrow 0
and

v(t_{n})\geq\frac{2^{d-\alpha}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}}{\omega_{d}}t_{n}^{-\alpha}
. Then we have

U(t_{0})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{t_{n+1}}^{t_{n}}t^{d-1}v(t)dt\geq\frac{2^{d-\alpha}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}}{\sigma_{d}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t_{n}^{-\alpha}(t_{n}^{d}-t_{n+1}^{d})=\frac{2^{d-\alpha}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}}{\sigma_{d}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(t_{n}^{d-\alpha}-t_{n}^{-\alpha}t_{n+1}^{d}).

Since
t_{n}>t_{n+1}
and thus
t_{n}^{-\alpha}<t_{n+1}^{-\alpha}
, in the end we have

U(t_{0})>\frac{2^{d-\alpha}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}}{\sigma_{d}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(t_{n}^{d-\alpha}-t_{n+1}^{d-\alpha})=\frac{2^{d-\alpha}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}}{\sigma_{d}}t_{0}^{d-\alpha},

which is impossible.
∎

3.3.4.
Coulomb-type potentials

We give an alternative expression of the fractional Rollnik norm for radial potentials,
which will be useful below. Let
V(x)=v(|x|)
with a function
v:\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
. Then, by making use of
(
2.5
) we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}&=\gamma_{d}(2\alpha-d)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)\mathcal{I}_{2\alpha-d}V(x)dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(|x|)\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2\alpha-d-1}F_{2\alpha-d}\left(\frac{|x|}{r}\right)v(r)dr\,dx\\
&=\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}r^{2\alpha-d-1}v(s)F_{2\alpha-d}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)v(r)dr\,ds.\end{split}

Proposition 3.20
.

The potential
V(x)=|x|^{-\beta}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<1\}}
belongs to
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
if and only
if
\beta<\alpha
.

Proof.

By (
3.16
) we get

\displaystyle\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}s^{d-1-\beta}r^{2\alpha-d-1-\beta}F_{2\alpha-d}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)ds\,dr

\displaystyle=\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1/r}z^{d-\beta-1}r^{2(\alpha-\beta)-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)dz\,dr

\displaystyle=\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}z^{d-\beta-1}r^{2(\alpha-\beta)-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)dz\,dr+\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{1}^{1/r^{2}}z^{d-\beta-1}r^{2(\alpha-\beta)-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)dz\,dr

\displaystyle=\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}z^{d-\beta-1}r^{2(\alpha-\beta)-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)dr\,dz+\sigma_{d}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1/z}z^{d-\beta-1}r^{2(\alpha-\beta)-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)dr\,dz

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2(\alpha-\beta)}\int_{0}^{1}z^{d-\beta-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)dz+\frac{\sigma_{d}}{2(\alpha-\beta)}\int_{1}^{\infty}z^{d-2\alpha+\beta-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)dz,

where integration with respect to
r
was possible since
\beta<\alpha
, while otherwise the integral is
divergent. The first integral is finite since
z^{d-\beta-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)\sim\sigma_{d}z^{d-\beta-1}

as
z\to 0
, which is integrable near zero, while
F_{2\alpha-d}
is always integrable near
1
. For the
second integral observe that
z^{d-2\alpha+\beta-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)\sim\sigma_{d}z^{\beta-1-d}
as
z\to\infty
, which is integrable at infinity since
\beta<d
.
∎

In case
\beta=\alpha
, we can make a finer analysis including logarithmic corrections.

Proposition 3.21
.

The potential
V(x)=|x|^{-\alpha}(-\log|x|)^{-\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<1/2\}}
,
\alpha<d
, belongs to

\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
if and only if
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

By (
3.16
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{1/2}\int_{0}^{1/2}s^{d-1-\alpha}r^{\alpha-d-1}(-\log s)^{-\gamma}(-\log r)^{-\gamma}F_{2\alpha-d}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)ds\,dr

\displaystyle=\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{1/2}\int_{0}^{1/2r}z^{d-\alpha-1}(-\log z-\log r)^{-\gamma}r^{-1}(-\log r)^{-\gamma}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)dz\,dr

\displaystyle=\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{1/2}\int_{0}^{1}z^{d-\alpha-1}(-\log z-\log r)^{-\gamma}r^{-1}(-\log r)^{-\gamma}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)dz\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{1/2}\int_{1}^{1/2r}z^{d-\alpha-1}(-\log z-\log r)^{-\gamma}r^{-1}(-\log r)^{-\gamma}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)dz\,dr

\displaystyle=\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1/2}z^{d-\alpha-1}(-\log z-\log r)^{-\gamma}r^{-1}(-\log r)^{-\gamma}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)dr\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad+\sigma_{d}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1/2z}z^{d-\alpha-1}(-\log z-\log r)^{-\gamma}r^{-1}(-\log r)^{-\gamma}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)dr\,dz

\displaystyle=\sigma_{d}(I_{1}+I_{2}).

If
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
, then

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}z^{d-\alpha-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)\int_{\log 2}^{\infty}x^{-\gamma}\left(x-\log z\right)^{-\gamma}dx\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}z^{d-\alpha-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)\int_{\log 2-\log z}^{\infty}(x+\log z)^{-\gamma}x^{-\gamma}dx\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}z^{d-\alpha-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)(-\log z)^{-2\gamma}\int_{\log 2-\log z}^{\infty}(1-\frac{-\log z}{x})^{-\gamma}\left(\frac{x}{-\log z}\right)^{-2\gamma}dx\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}z^{d-\alpha-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)(-\log z)^{1-2\gamma}\int_{0}^{\frac{-\log z}{\log 2-\log z}}(1-x)^{-\gamma}x^{-2\gamma-2}dx\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}z^{d-\alpha-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)(-\log z)^{1-2\gamma}\int_{0}^{\frac{-\log z}{\log 2-\log z}}B\left(\frac{-\log z}{\log 2-\log z};2\gamma-1,1-\gamma\right)\,dz,

with the incomplete Beta-function
B(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)
. In the limit
z\downarrow 0
we have

B\left(\frac{-\log z}{\log 2-\log z};2\gamma-1,1-\gamma\right)\to B(2\gamma-1,1-\gamma),

hence

z^{d-\alpha-1}\log^{1-2\gamma}(z)F_{2\alpha-d}(z)B\left(\frac{-\log z}{\log 2-\log z};2\gamma-1,1-\gamma\right)\sim B(1-\gamma,2\gamma-1)\sigma_{d}z^{d-\alpha-1}\log^{1-2\gamma}(z),

where the right hand side is integrable in a neighbourhood of zero. As to the behaviour at
1
, recall that the
incomplete Beta-function satisfies

B\left(z;2\gamma-1,1-\gamma\right)\sim\frac{z^{2\gamma-1}}{2\gamma-1}\quad\mbox{ as $z\downarrow 0$}.

Indeed, by
[
18
, eq. (8.391)]
, we have

B\left(z;2\gamma-1,1-\gamma\right)=\frac{z^{2\gamma-1}}{2\gamma-1}{}_{2}F_{1}(2\gamma-1,\gamma;2\gamma;z),

where
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c,z)
is Gauss’ hypergeometric function, so it is clear that

\lim_{z\to 0^{+}}\frac{(2\gamma-1)B\left(z;2\gamma-1,1-\gamma\right)}{z^{2\gamma-1}}={}_{2}F_{1}(2\gamma-1,\gamma;2\gamma;0)=1.

Hence we get, in general,

z^{d-\alpha-1}\log^{1-2\gamma}(z)F_{2\alpha-d}(z)B\left(\frac{-\log z}{\log 2-\log z};2\gamma-1,1-\gamma\right)\sim\frac{F_{2\alpha-d}(z)}{2\gamma-1}\quad\mbox{ as $z\downarrow 1$},

where the right-hand side is integrable near
1
. This proves that
I_{1}<\infty
. Next we estimate
I_{2}
and write

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}z^{d-\alpha-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)\int_{\log(2z)}^{\infty}x^{-\gamma}(x-\log z)^{-\gamma}dx\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}z^{d-\alpha-1}\log^{1-2\gamma}(z)F_{2\alpha-d}(z)\int_{0}^{\log z/(\log 2+\log z)}x^{2\gamma-2}\left(1-x\right)^{-\gamma}dx\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}z^{d-\alpha-1}\log^{1-2\gamma}(z)F_{2\alpha-d}(z)B\left(\frac{\log z}{\log 2+\log z};2\gamma-1,1-\gamma\right),

with the incomplete Beta-function
B(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)
. We have

B\left(\frac{\log z}{\log 2+\log z};2\gamma-1,1-\gamma\right)\to B(1-\gamma,2\gamma-1)

as
z\to\infty
, hence

z^{d-\alpha-1}\log^{1-2\gamma}(z)F_{2\alpha-d}(z)B\left(\frac{\log z}{\log 2+\log z};2\gamma-1,1-\gamma\right)\sim B(1-\gamma,2\gamma-1)\sigma_{d}z^{\alpha-d-1}\log^{1-2\gamma}(z),

which is integrable near infinity since
\alpha<d
. Again, to handle the behaviour at
1
, note that

z^{d-\alpha-1}\log^{1-2\gamma}(z)F_{2\alpha-d}(z)B\left(\frac{\log z}{\log 2+\log z};2\gamma-1,1-\gamma\right)\sim\frac{F_{2\alpha-d}(z)}{2\gamma-1}\quad\mbox{ as $z\downarrow 1$},

where the right-hand side is integrable near
1
.

Finally, consider the case
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Then

I_{1}\geq\sigma_{d}\int_{0}^{1}z^{d-\alpha-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(-\log z+x)^{2\gamma}}dx\,dz=\infty

due to
2\gamma\leq 1
.
∎

Remark 3.22

Notice that for

Proposition 3.23

\infty
, observe that

z^{d-\alpha-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)\log^{1-2\gamma}(z)B\left(\frac{-\log z}{\log 2-\log z};2\gamma-1,1-\gamma\right)\sim\sigma_{d}z^{\alpha-d-1}\log^{1-2\gamma}(z)B(2\gamma-1,\gamma-1)\mbox{ as $z\uparrow\infty$},

which is integrable near
\infty
since
\alpha<d
. This proves that
I_{2}<\infty
.

For the case
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2}
, note that

I_{2}\geq\int_{1}^{\infty}z^{d-\alpha-1}F_{2\alpha-d}(z)\int_{1}^{\infty}(x+\log z)^{-2\gamma}dx\,dz=\infty.

∎

Corollary 3.25
.

The potential
V(x)=|x|^{-\beta_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<1\}}+|x|^{-\beta_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\geq 1\}}
belongs to

\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
if and only if
\beta_{1}<\alpha
and
\beta_{2}\in(\alpha,d)
.

Proof.

By Propositions
3.20
and
3.23
it follows that if
\beta_{1}<\alpha
and
\beta_{2}\in(\alpha,d)
,
then
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. Conversely, if
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, then it follows by Remark
3.8
that
both
V_{1}(x)=|x|^{-\beta_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|<1\}}
and
V_{2}(x)=|x|^{-\beta_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\geq 1\}}
belong
to
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, which implies
\beta_{1}<\alpha
and
\beta_{2}\in(\alpha,d)
again by Propositions
3.20

and
3.23
.
∎

3.4.
Extended fractional Rollnik-class

Next we introduce another class of potentials related to the fractional Rollnik-class, constructed on the resolvent
estimates (
2.17
) and (
2.20
). This class will be justified by the behaviours we will discuss in Section
4 below.

Definition 3.26
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\geq 0
, and assume that
d<2\alpha
. We call

\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{m}_{d,\alpha}:=\left\{V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}:[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H^{m}_{d,\alpha}(|x-y|)^{2}|V(x)||V(y)|dx\,dy<\infty\right\}

extended fractional Rollnik-class. If
m=0
, we omit the superscript.

First we settle the relationship between the fractional Rollnik-classes and their extensions.

Proposition 3.27
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that
d<2\alpha
.

(1)

If
d>\alpha
, we have
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}
. In particular, there exists a constant

C_{d,\alpha}>0
such that

[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}}\leq C_{d,\alpha}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}

for every
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
.

(2)

For every
m>0
we have
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{m}_{d,\alpha}
. In particular,
there exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha}>0
such that

[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{m}_{d,\alpha}}\leq C_{d,\alpha}[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}}

for every
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
.

Proof.

(1) easily follows since
r\geq 1
trivially implies
r^{-2(d+\alpha)}\leq r^{-2(d-\alpha)}
, and thus

\displaystyle[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\iint_{|x-y|\leq 1}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2(d-\alpha)}}dx\,dy+2^{d+\alpha-2}\Gamma^{2}\Big(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\Big)\iint_{|x-y|\geq 1}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2(d+\alpha)}}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{|x-y|\leq 1}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2(d-\alpha)}}dx\,dy+2^{d+\alpha-2}\Gamma^{2}\Big(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\Big)\iint_{|x-y|\geq 1}\frac{|V(x)||V(y)|}{|x-y|^{2(d-\alpha)}}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,\alpha}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}.

To obtain (2) note that by (
6.6
) there exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha}>0
such that
H^{m}_{d,\alpha}(r)\leq C_{d,\alpha}H_{d,\alpha}(r)
, for all
r>0
.
∎

The above definitions yield a consistent behaviour in the massless limit.

Proposition 3.28
.

Let
V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}
. Then

\lim_{m\downarrow 0}[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{m}_{d,\alpha}}=[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}}
.

Proof.

By Remark
6.15
in the Appendix we have
\lim_{m\downarrow 0}H_{d,\alpha}^{m}(r)=H_{d,\alpha}(r)
, and then the claim follows by the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

Concerning the relationship between
L^{p}
-spaces and
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}
, we can prove the following
continuous embedding property.

Proposition 3.29
.

Let
m\geq 0
. Then
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}
. In particular,

(3.17)

[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}}\leq\left\|H_{d,\alpha}^{m}\right\|_{2}\left\|V\right\|_{2}.

Proof.

Note that if
d<2\alpha
then
H_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|\cdot|)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
m\geq 0
and thus (
3.17
)
follows by a direct application of Young’s convolution inequality.
∎

Remark 3.30
.

Proposition
3.29
guarantees that for
\beta>\frac{d}{2}
the potential
V(x)=|x|^{-\beta}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\geq 1\}}
belongs to
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}
for all

. However, since

, we can take
\beta\in\left(\frac{d}{2},\alpha\right)
such that

due to Proposition
3.23
, and thus

.

Similarly to (
3.7
) we define
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}^{m,p}
for some
p>1
through
of a limiting argument by

\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}^{m,p}:=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\cap\,\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow\frac{d}{2}}\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}.

By Proposition
3.29
, for
p=2
we have
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}^{m,2}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Furthermore, Proposition
3.27

4.
Perturbations by fractional Rollnik class potentials

4.1.
Self-adjointness

In what follows our main interest is to study the properties of fractional Schrödinger operators for
m\geq 0

H=L_{m,\alpha}+V

with fractional Rollnik-class potentials
V
. First we show that
H
can be defined as a self-adjoint linear
operator in the form sense for
V\in{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}
.

Define

K^{m}_{\lambda}=|V|^{1/2}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}|V|^{1/2},

which is an integral operator with kernel

K^{m}_{\lambda}(x-y)=\sqrt{|V(x)|}R^{m}_{\lambda}(x-y)\sqrt{|V(y)|}.

First we give a criterion for self-adjointness of the operator
H
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
m\geq 0
and assume that
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left\|K_{\lambda}^{m}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=0
. Then
H
is
self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the form sense.

Proof.

By the assumption we have directly that

\|\sqrt{|V|}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2})}\rightarrow 0

as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
. Define the function

\widetilde{V}^{1/2}(x)=\begin{cases}V(x)/\sqrt{|V(x)|}&\mbox{if}\ V(x)\not=0,\\
0&\mbox{if}\ V(x)=0.\end{cases}

Then
|\widetilde{V}^{1/2}|=\sqrt{|V|}
and
\tilde{V}^{1/2}\sqrt{|V|}=V
hold. For
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we
compute

\displaystyle\vskip-85.35826pt\|(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}V(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}\phi\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\tilde{V}^{1/2}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}V(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}\phi,\sqrt{|V|}(\lambda+(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2})^{-1/2}\phi)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|K^{m}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2})}\|\sqrt{|V|}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2})}^{2}\|\phi\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus we obtain

(4.1)

\|(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}V(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2})}\rightarrow 0

as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
. An application of the KLMN
theorem
[
25
, Th. X.17]
completes the proof.
∎

Using Lemma
4.1
it remains to show that under specific assumptions on
V
it follows that

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left\|K_{\lambda}^{m}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=0
. This can be guaranteed if for a sufficiently
large
\lambda_{0}>0
the operator
K^{m}_{\lambda_{0}}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that
K^{m}_{\lambda_{0}}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator. Then

K^{m}_{\lambda}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator for all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
and

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left\|K_{\lambda}^{m}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left\|K_{\lambda}^{m}\right\|_{\rm HS}=0,

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\rm HS}
is the Hilbert-Schmidt norm.

Proof.

Assume that
K^{m}_{\lambda_{0}}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator and let
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
. Then we have

R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}p^{m}_{t}(x)\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{0}t}p^{m}_{t}(x)\,dt=R_{\lambda_{0}}^{m}(x).

Furthermore, by the dominated convergence theorem, it is clear that
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}R_{\lambda}(x)=0
.
We get

\displaystyle\left\|K_{\lambda}^{m}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|V(x)|\,R_{\lambda}^{m}(x-y)^{2}\,|V(y)|dx\,dy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|V(x)|\,R_{\lambda_{0}}^{m}(x-y)^{2}\,|V(y)|dx\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|K_{\lambda_{0}}^{m}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}<\infty.

Again by dominated convergence, it follows that

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left\|K_{\lambda}^{m}\right\|_{\rm HS}=0
.
∎

Once this established, our goal is determine conditions under which
K_{\lambda}^{m}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator
for some
\lambda>0
. First consider the case of the fractional Rollnik-class.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 1
,
m\geq 0
and assume that
\alpha<d<2\alpha
. If
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
,
then
K^{m}_{\lambda}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator for every
\lambda>0
. Furthermore, there exists a constant

C_{d,\alpha}>0
such that

\left\|K^{m}_{\lambda}\right\|_{\rm HS}\leq\sqrt{C_{d,\alpha}}\Big(1+\frac{me^{-\lambda/m}}{\lambda}\Big)\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}.

(The bracket equals 1 if
m=0
.) In particular, if
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
, then
H
is a self-adjoint operator in the form sense.

Proof.

By Lemmas
6.1
and
6.10
, it is straightforward that

(4.2)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}K^{m}_{\lambda}(x-y)^{2}dxdy=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}R^{m}_{\lambda}(x-y)^{2}|V(x)||V(y)|dxdy\leq C_{d,\alpha}^{2}\left(1+\frac{me^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}}{\lambda}\right)^{2}\|V\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}

for every
\lambda>0
and
m\geq 0
. Lemma
4.1
then implies that
H
is self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the form sense.
∎

We can, however, provide a finer argument to characterize the class of potentials
V
such that
K_{\lambda}^{m}
is a
Hilbert-Schmidt operator, at least for
\lambda>m
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 1
,
m\geq 0
and
\lambda>0
and assume that
d<2\alpha
.
Then the following properties hold:

(1)

If
V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}
, then
K_{\lambda}^{m}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator and
there exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}
, independent of
V
, such that

\left\|K_{\lambda}^{m}\right\|_{\rm HS}\leq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}}.

(2)

If
K_{\lambda}^{m}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator, then
V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}
and
there exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}
, independent of
V
, such that

[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}}\leq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}\left\|K_{\lambda}^{m}\right\|_{\rm HS}.

(3)

If
\lambda>m
, then
K_{\lambda}^{m}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator if and only if
V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}
, and there exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}>1
independent
of
V
such that

\frac{1}{C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}}[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}}\leq\left\|K_{\lambda}^{m}\right\|_{\rm HS}\leq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}}.

Proof.

To obtain (1), let
V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}
. Then by (
2.16
)
we have

\left\|K_{\lambda}^{m}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|V(x)|\,R^{m}_{\lambda}(x-y)^{2}\,|V(y)|dx\,dy\\
\leq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|V(x)|\,H_{d,\alpha}^{0}(|x-y|)^{2}\,|V(y)|dx\,dy=C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}.

Next we show (2). Assume that
K_{\lambda}^{m}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator. By (
2.21
)
we then have

[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}}^{2}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|V(x)|\,H_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x-y|)^{2}\,|V(y)|dx\,dy\\
\leq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|V(x)|\,R^{m}_{\lambda}(x-y)^{2}\,|V(y)|dx\,dy=C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}\left\|K_{\lambda}^{m}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}.

Finally, to show (3) note that one implication has already been shown in (2). If we assume further that

\lambda>m
and
V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}
, then by (
2.19
)
we get

\left\|K_{\lambda}^{m}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|V(x)|\,R^{m}_{\lambda}(x-y)^{2}\,|V(y)|dx\,dy\\
\leq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|V(x)|\,H_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x-y|)^{2}\,V(y)|dx\,dy=C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}[V]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{m}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}.

∎

Remark 4.5
.

We note that by the Kato-Rellich theorem the relativistic Schrödinger operators with Coulomb potentials

|x|^{-\gamma}
,
\gamma>0
, are self-adjoint if
\gamma<\frac{d}{2}
for
d\leq 3
by
[
20
, Prop. 1.5]
.
On the other hand, a combination of Theorem
4.4
and Corollary
3.25
improve
this to
\gamma<\alpha
.

Finally we note that if
d\geq 2\alpha
, then
K^{m}_{\lambda}
cannot be a Hilbert-Schmidt operator for continuous
potentials
V
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 1
,
\lambda>0
,
m\geq 0
and assume
d\geq 2\alpha
. If there exist

\varepsilon^{\ast},r^{\ast}>0
and
x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|V(y)|>\varepsilon^{\ast}
for all
y\in B_{r^{\ast}}(x^{\ast})
,
then
K_{\lambda}^{m}
is not a Hilbert-Schmidt operator. In particular, if
V
is continuous, then
K_{\lambda}^{m}
is not a
Hilbert-Schmidt operator, unless
V\equiv 0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.17
we see that the function
r\in[0,\infty)\mapsto R_{\lambda}^{m}(r\mathbf{e}_{1})^{2}\in[0,\infty)
satisfies (
3.4
), since clearly
H^{m}_{d,\alpha}
does not belong to
L^{2}(B_{\delta})

for any ball
B_{\delta}
centred in
0
. The statement follows then by Theorem
3.9
.
∎

4.2.
Spectral properties

4.2.1.
Essential spectrum

In this section we discuss the spectrum of non-local Schrödinger operators with fractional Rollnik-class potentials.
In the classical case, for
V\in\mathcal{R}
Fourier transform gives that
{\Spec}_{\rm ess}(-\Delta+V)={\Spec}_{\rm ess}(-\Delta)
without any further requirements
[
35
]
. However, if
(d,\alpha)\not=(3,2)
, it
appears to be difficult to construct a Fourier theory of
\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. Therefore we need other ways to
prove the stability of the essential spectrum.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 1
,
m\geq 0
, and assume that
d<2\alpha
. If the potential

V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{m}_{d,\alpha}
, then

{\Spec}_{\rm ess}H={\Spec}_{\rm ess}L_{m,\alpha}=[0,\infty)

holds for
m\geq 0
.

Proof.

Due to (
4.1
) we may choose
\lambda\gg 1
such that

(4.3)

\|G_{m,\lambda}\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2})}<1,

where we wrote

G_{m,\lambda}=(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}V(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}.

We first prove the following Tiktopoulos-type formula (
[
35
, Lemmas II.10 and II.11]
),

(4.4)

(\lambda+H)^{-1}=(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}(1+G_{m,\lambda})^{-1}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}.

Since
H
is defined as the self-adjoint operator in the form sense on
\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})
, we have to consider the duality argument. We regard the operator
H
and
V
as the map from
\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})
to its dual. Indeed
, we can define
H:\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})\rightarrow\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})^{*}
by
\phi\mapsto f_{H\phi}
with
f_{H\phi}(\psi)=(H\phi,\psi)
for
\phi,\psi\in\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})
. The resolvent is also defined as the map from
\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})^{*}
to
\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})
in the following sense. For any
f\in\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})^{*}
, there exists a unique
\tilde{\phi}\in\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})

such that
f=f_{\tilde{\phi}}=(\cdot,\tilde{\phi})
by the Riesz representation theorem. Putting
\phi=(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{1/2}\tilde{\phi}
, we define
(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}:\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})^{*}\rightarrow\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})
by
f\mapsto(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}\phi
. By these definitions, we have

\displaystyle(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}(\lambda+H)(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{N}(-1)^{j}G_{m,\lambda}^{j}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}

(4.5)

\displaystyle=(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}+(-1)^{N}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}G_{m,\lambda}^{N+1}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}

as the map from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Denoting the right-hand-sided of (
4.4
) by
\tilde{R}_{m,\lambda}
, and taking the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
in (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}(\lambda+H)\tilde{R}_{m,\lambda}=(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}

in the operator norm sense. For
\lambda\gg 1
,
(\lambda+H)^{-1}
exist by (
4.1
). Once we write

\displaystyle(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}(\lambda+H)\tilde{R}_{m,\lambda}=(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}(\lambda+H)(\lambda+H)^{-1},

we obtain (
4.4
) by injectivity of
\lambda+H
and
(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}
.
We next define

\tilde{K}_{m,\lambda}=\tilde{V}^{1/2}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}|V|^{1/2}.

The following strategy is based on
[
35
, Theorem II.34]
. As in Lemma 4.2, we have
\|\tilde{K}_{m,\lambda}\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2})}<1
for
\lambda\gg 1
. By the duality argument again, we have

\displaystyle(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}|V|^{1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{N}(-1)^{j+1}\tilde{K}_{m,\lambda}^{j}\tilde{V}^{1/2}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}

(4.6)

\displaystyle=(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{N+1}(-1)^{j}G_{m,\lambda}^{j}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1/2}-(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}

for
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
in (
4.6
), we have

-(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}|V|^{1/2}(1+\tilde{K}_{m,\lambda})^{-1}\tilde{V}^{1/2}(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}=(\lambda+H)^{-1}-(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}

in the operator-norm sense. This says that
(\lambda+H)^{-1}-(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}
is compact because
\tilde{K}_{m,\lambda}
is compact and the operator-norm limit of a sequence of compact operators is compact. The Weyl theorem then completes the proof.
∎

4.2.2.
Discrete spectrum

Next we consider the discrete spectrum. First we provide a bound on the number of negative eigenvalues, which in
order to keep explicit, we only consider the case
\alpha<d
and
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 1
,
m\geq 0
and assume that
\alpha<d<2\alpha
. Let the potential

V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. Denote by
V_{-}\geq 0
its negative part and by
N_{\lambda_{0}}(V)
the number of the eigenvalues of
H
that are less than
-\lambda_{0}
with
\lambda_{0}>0
. Then

(4.7)

N_{\lambda_{0}}(V)\leq C_{d,\alpha}^{2}\left(1+\frac{me^{-\lambda_{0}/m}}{\lambda_{0}}\right)^{2}\|V_{-}\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}

holds (the quantity in the brackets is set to
1
and is independent of
\lambda_{0}
if
m=0
), where
C_{d,\alpha}>0
is same constant as in Lemma 6.10. In particular, if
m=0
and we denote the number of the negative eigenvalues of
H
by
N(V)
, then

(4.8)

N(V)\leq C_{d,\alpha}^{2}\|V_{-}\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}

holds, that is, the discrete spectrum of
H
is finite for the massless case.

Proof.

The argument is based on the Birman-Schwinger principle, for details see
[
23
, Section 4.9.7]
and
[
26
, Theorem XIII.10]
. By the limit (
4.1
), we find that
H
is bounded below. To apply the min-max principle
[
26
, Theorem XIII.1]
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(H)=\sup_{\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n-1}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\Dom{H},\|\phi\|=1\\
\phi\in\langle\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n-1}\rangle^{\perp}\end{subarray}}(H\phi,\phi)

where
\langle\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n-1}\rangle^{\perp}
means
\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):(\psi,\phi_{j})=0\ \mbox{for}\ 0\leq j\leq n-1\}
if
n\geq 2
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if
n=1
. By the min-max principle, we know that either
\mu_{n}(H)
denotes the
n
th negative eigenvalue of
H
counting multiplicity or it coincides with
\inf\Spec_{\rm ess}(H)=0
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
V\leq 0
. Indeed
L_{m,\alpha}-V_{-}\leq H
, thus the number of negative eigenvalues of
H
is not large than the one of
L_{m,\alpha}-V_{-}
.

Let
H(\gamma)=L_{m,\alpha}+\gamma V
with
\gamma>0
. By Theorem
4.7
, we also have
{\Spec}_{\rm ess}(H(\gamma))=[0,\infty)
. If
-\lambda\in{\Spec}_{\rm p}(H(\gamma))
, there exists an eigenfunction
\phi\in\Dom(L_{m,\alpha}^{1/2})
such that
H(\gamma)\phi=-\lambda\phi
. Passing through the duality argument, we have

(\lambda+L_{m,\alpha})^{-1}(-V)\phi=\frac{1}{\gamma}\phi.

This implies that
1/\gamma\in{\Spec}_{\rm p}(K_{\lambda}^{m})
and
(-V)^{1/2}\phi
is the corresponding eigenfunction. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, We write
\mu_{n}(\gamma)
=
\mu_{n}(H(\gamma))
for simplicity. For
\lambda_{0}>0
, we can write

N_{\lambda_{0}}(V)=\#\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\mu_{n}(1)<-\lambda_{0}\right\}.

Note that
\mu_{n}(\gamma)
is monotone decreasing and continuous in
\gamma
by
[
26
, Lemma XIII.3.C]
. For every
1\leq n\leq N_{\lambda_{0}}(V)
, the relations
\mu_{n}(0)=0
and
\mu_{n}(1)<-\lambda_{0}
imply that there exists a
0<\gamma_{n}<1
such that
\mu_{n}(\gamma_{n})=-\lambda_{0}
. We thus compute

(4.9)

N_{\lambda_{0}}(V)=\#\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\mu_{n}(\gamma_{n})=-\lambda_{0}\right\}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\lambda_{0}}(V)}\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}^{2}}\leq\sum_{\nu\in{\Spec}_{\rm p}(K_{\lambda_{0}})}\nu^{2}=\|K^{m}_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{\rm HS}^{2}.

By Theorem
4.3

and (
4.9
) we obtain (
4.7
). If
m=0
, we have (
4.8
) from (
4.7
) with
m=0
and
N(V)=\sup_{\lambda_{0}>0}N_{\lambda_{0}}(V)
.
∎

By the same proof, with the only difference being the employement of Item
(1)
in Theorem
4.4
in place of
4.3
, we can show the following result for extended fractional Rollnik class.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 1
,
m\geq 0
and assume that
d<2\alpha
. Let the potential

V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}
. Denote by
V_{-}

its negative part and by
N_{\lambda_{0}}(V)
the number of the eigenvalues of
H
that are less than
-\lambda_{0}
for
m\geq 0
. Then

N_{\lambda_{0}}(V)\leq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda_{0},m}^{2}[V_{-}]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}

holds, where
C_{d,\alpha,\lambda_{0},m}>0
is the same constant as in (
2.16
).

Notice that the constant
C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}
in (
2.16
)
diverges as
\lambda\to 0
even if
m=0
by (
2.22
). Hence, we are not able to provide an exact estimate on the number of negative eigenvalues. Nevertheless, Theorem
4.9
provides a lower bound on the discrete spectrum: if
C_{d,\alpha,\lambda_{0},m}^{2}[V_{-}]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}<1
then
N_{\lambda_{0}}(V)=0
. This implies that the discrete spectrum should be contained in the interval
[-\overline{\lambda}(V),0]
, where

\overline{\lambda}(V)=\inf\left\{\lambda_{0}>0:\ C_{d,\alpha,\lambda_{0},m}^{2}[V_{-}]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}\geq 1\right\}.

In case
m>0
, we cannot directly provide a bound on the number of negative eigenvalues. This is due to the fact that we cannot guarantee that
K_{\lambda}^{m}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator for any
\lambda>0
. Nevertheless, the previous results allowed us to find a lower bound on the discrete spectrum, provided that either
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
or
V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}
. In case
V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}
, we can still provide a bound on the number of eigenvalues that are less then
-m
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 1
,
m>0
and assume that
d<2\alpha
. Let
V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha}^{m}
.
Denote by
V_{-}
its negative part and by
N_{m}(V)
the number of
eigenvalues of
H
, counting multiplicity, that are less then
-m
. Then there exists
\lambda_{0}>m
such that

N_{m}(V)\leq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda_{0},m}^{2}[V_{-}]_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{m}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}

holds, where
C_{d,\alpha,\lambda_{0},m}>0
is the same constant as in (
2.19
).

Proof.

Arguing exactly as in Theorem
4.8
, we use the min-max principle to characterize the negative eigenvalues
\mu_{n}(H)
of
H
. Furthermore, the fact that
L_{m,\alpha}-V_{-}\leq H
still guarantees that the number of eigenvalues of
H
that are less then
-m
do not increase if we use
V_{-}
in place of
V
. Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume that
V\leq 0
. Define

H(\gamma)=L_{m,\alpha}+\gamma V
with
\gamma>0
, so that, as shown in the proof of Theorem
4.8
, if
-\lambda\in{\rm Spec}_{p}(H(\gamma))
then
1/\gamma\in\Spec_{p}(K_{\lambda})
. Since
\gamma V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{m}_{d,\alpha}
, by Theorem
4.7
we have
\Spec_{\rm ess}(H(\gamma))=[0,\infty)
. Now denote, for simplicity,
\mu_{n}(\gamma)=:\mu_{n}(H(\gamma))
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and notice that these are monotone decreasing and continuous functions of
\gamma
. Now, using the notation in Theorem
4.8
, if
N_{m}(V)=0
the statement clearly follows.
In the contrary case
N_{m}(V)>0
and notice that

N_{m}(V):=\max\{n\geq 1:\ \mu_{n}(1)<-m\},

since
\mu_{n}(1)
is a non-decreasing sequence. Clearly,
N_{m}(V)<\infty
. Indeed, if this is not the case, then
\mu^{\star}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mu_{n}(1)
is an accumulation point of the discrete spectrum of
H
, that is absurd since
\mu^{\star}\leq-m<0
and thus
\mu^{\star}\not\in Spec_{\rm ess}(H)
. Furthermore, notice that
\mu_{N_{m}(V)}(1)<-m
, hence there exists
\lambda_{0}>m
such that
\mu_{N_{m}(V)}(1)<-\lambda_{0}
. In particular, for all
n\leq N_{m}(V)
it holds
\mu_{n}(1)<-\lambda_{0}
. At the same time
\mu_{n}(0)=0
and
\mu_{n}(\gamma)
is continuous and non-decreasing in
\gamma
, thus there exists
\gamma_{n}\in(0,1)
such that
\mu_{n}(\gamma_{n})=-\lambda_{0}
. Let
(\gamma_{n})_{n=1,\dots,N_{m}(V)}\in(0,1)^{N_{m}(V)}
be the sequence of such
\gamma_{n}
. In particular,
1/\gamma_{n}\in\Spec_{\rm p}(K_{\lambda_{0}}^{m})
. On the other hand, if
n>N_{m}(V)
, then
\mu_{n}(1)\geq-m>-\lambda_{0}
and thus
\mu_{n}(\gamma)\not=-\lambda_{0}
for any
\gamma\in[0,1]
. We have then

N_{m}(V)=\#\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\ \mu_{n+n_{\star}}(\gamma_{n+n_{\star}})=-\lambda_{0}\}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N_{m}(V)}\frac{1}{\gamma_{n+n_{\star}}^{2}}\leq\sum_{\nu\in\Spec_{\rm p}(K_{\lambda_{0}}^{m})}\nu^{2}=\left\|K_{\lambda_{0}}^{m}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}.

Since
\lambda_{0}>m
, by part (3) of Theorem
4.4
it follows that
K_{\lambda_{0}}^{m}
is a
Hilbert-Schmidt operator, and the same result then completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

Notice that under the assumptions of Theorem
4.9
, if we know that
0\in{\rm Spec}_{\rm ess}(H)
is not an accumulation point of
{\rm Spec}_{\rm d}(H)
, then
N(V)
is finite and there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that

(4.10)

N(V)\leq C^{2}_{d,\alpha,\lambda_{0},m}[V_{-}]_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}.

Indeed, in such a case, arguing as in Theorem
4.10
, we have that

N(V)=\max\{n\geq 0:\ \mu_{n}(1)<0\},

where the latter is clearly a maximum since otherwise
N(V)=+\infty
and
0
would be an accumulation point of
{\rm Spec}_{\rm d}(H)
. Then there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that
\mu_{N(V)}(1)<-\lambda_{0}
, hence
N(V)=N_{\lambda_{0}}(V)
and (
4.10
) follows by Theorem
4.9
.

4.2.3.
Non-existence of negative and zero eigenvalues

It is known, see
[
36
]
,
[
23
, Th. 4.308]
,
that whenever the semigroup
\{e^{-tL_{m,\alpha}}:t\geq 0\}
is recurrent, the operator
H=L_{m,\alpha}+V
has at least one bound state whenever
V
is a non-positive potential. However, when

\{e^{-tL_{m,\alpha}}:t\geq 0\}
is transient, a bound state exists only when the potential is strong enough
in a specific sense. By making use of the extended Birman-Schwinger principle, we can show that for too small
Rollnik-norm there exist no bound states and as a bonus we can also rule out embedded eigenvalues at zero. We
will use the following result, for a proof see
[
23
, Cor. 4.150, Cor. 4.151, Prop. 4.309]
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\alpha<d
. Suppose that
V\leq 0
is form-compact with respect to
L_{m,\alpha}
and the operator norm

\|K^{m}_{0}\|<1
. Then
H
has no non-positive eigenvalues, in particular, it has no bound states at negative
eigenvalues and zero is not an embedded eigenvalue.

In what follows, we derive suitable upper estimates on the norm of
K_{0}^{m}
which will permit to use this observation.
We will separately consider the cases
m=0
and
m>0
, which will require different treatment.

First we note that the map
\lambda\in[0,\infty)\mapsto\left\|K^{m}_{\lambda}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
is monotone and
continuous.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
m\geq 0
and suppose that
K^{m}_{\lambda_{0}}\in\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for some
\lambda_{0}>0
. Then
K^{m}_{\lambda}\in\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and
\left\|K^{m}_{\lambda}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq\left\|K^{m}_{\lambda_{0}}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
. Furthermore, if
\left\|K^{m}_{\lambda_{0}}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}<\infty
,
then
\lim_{\lambda\downarrow\lambda_{0}}\left\|K^{m}_{\lambda}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=\left\|K^{m}_{\lambda_{0}}\right\|_{{\mathscr{B}}(L^{2})}
.

Proof.

Since
K_{\lambda}
is positivity preserving, we have

(4.11)

\left\|K_{\lambda}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\\
f\geq 0,\ f\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\|K_{\lambda}f\right\|_{2}}{\left\|f\right\|_{2}}.

Indeed, choosing
C
to be the right-hand side of (
4.11
), for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with positive and negative
parts
f^{+},f^{-}
respectively, we get

\left\|K_{\lambda}f\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{\left\|K_{\lambda}f^{+}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|K_{\lambda}f^{-}\right\|_{2}^{2}}\leq C\sqrt{\left\|f^{+}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|f^{-}\right\|_{2}^{2}}=C\left\|f\right\|_{2}.

Once this is established, the statement clearly follows by the fact that
0\leq R_{\lambda}(x)\leq R_{\lambda_{0}}(x)
.
By the monotone convergence theorem, the same relation guarantees that
\left\|K_{\lambda}f\right\|_{2}\to\left\|K_{\lambda_{0}}f\right\|_{2}

for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
f\geq 0
, and so
\lim_{\lambda\to\lambda_{0}}\left\|K_{\lambda}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq\left\|K_{\lambda_{0}}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
. To prove that in fact equality holds, for every
\varepsilon>0
consider

f_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
f_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
such that

\left|\frac{\left\|K_{\lambda_{0}}f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}}-\left\|K_{\lambda_{0}}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\right|<\varepsilon.

There also exists
\delta>0
such that if
0<\lambda-\lambda_{0}<\delta
then

\left|\frac{\left\|K_{\lambda}f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}}-\frac{\left\|K_{\lambda_{0}}f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}}\right|<\varepsilon.

Then for all
\lambda_{0}<\lambda<\lambda_{0}+\delta
it follows that

\left|\frac{\left\|K_{\lambda}f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}}-\left\|K_{\lambda_{0}}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\right|<2\varepsilon,

which in turn implies

\left\|K_{\lambda}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\geq\frac{\left\|K_{\lambda}f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|f_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}}\geq\left\|K_{\lambda_{0}}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}-2\varepsilon.

Taking
\varepsilon\to 0
the claim follows.
∎

Now we focus on estimating
\left\|K_{0}^{m}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
. In fact, we will provide a necessary and sufficient
condition for
K_{0}^{m}
to be a Hilbert-Schmidt operator.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
with
0<\alpha<d<2\alpha
and
m\geq 0
.

(1)

If
m=0
, then
K_{0}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator if and only if
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. Furthermore, there exists
a constant
C_{d,\alpha}>0
such that

(4.12)

\left\|K_{0}\right\|_{\rm HS}=C_{d,\alpha}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}

for all
V\in\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}
. In particular, whenever
V\leq 0
such that

(4.13)

\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}<\frac{1}{C_{d,\alpha}},

the operator
H=L_{0,\alpha}+V
has no bound states at
negative eigenvalues and zero is not an embedded eigenvalue.

(2)

If
m>0
, let
d=3
. Then
K_{0}^{m}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator if and only if
V\in\mathcal{R}_{3,\alpha}\cap\mathcal{R}
and there exist two constants
C^{1}_{\alpha},C^{2}_{\alpha,m}>0
such that

(4.14)

C^{2}_{\alpha,m}\sqrt{\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{3,\alpha}}^{2}+\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}}\leq\left\|K_{0}^{m}\right\|_{\rm HS}\leq C^{1}_{\alpha}\sqrt{\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{3,\alpha}}^{2}+m^{\frac{4}{\alpha}-2}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}}

for all
V\in\mathcal{R}_{3,\alpha}\cap\mathcal{R}

In particular, whenever
V\leq 0
is such that

\sqrt{\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{3,\alpha}}^{2}+m^{\frac{4}{\alpha}-2}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}}<\frac{1}{C_{\alpha}},

the operator
H=L_{m,\alpha}+V
has no bound states at
negative eigenvalues and zero is not an embedded eigenvalue.

Proof.

To prove (1) observe that by (
2.3
)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}K_{0}(x-y)^{2}dxdy=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}R_{0}(x-y)^{2}|V(x)||V(y)|dxdy=C_{d,\alpha}^{2}\|V\|_{\mathcal{R}_{d,\alpha}}^{2}.

Note that (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) directly imply that
V
is form-compact with respect to

L_{0,\alpha}
with relative bound strictly less than one, thus by Theorem
4.12
the operator
H=L_{0,\alpha}+V
has no bound states at negative eigenvalues and zero is not an embedded eigenvalue.
To get (2) first we show that if
V\in\mathcal{R}_{3,\alpha}\cap\mathcal{R}
, then
K_{0}^{m}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt
operator and that the second inequality in (
4.14
) holds. By Lemma
6.7

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}K^{m}_{0}(x-y)^{2}dxdy=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}R^{m}_{0}(x-y)^{2}|V(x)||V(y)|dxdy

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|x-y|^{-(6-2\alpha)}|V(x)||V(y)|dxdy+m^{\frac{4}{\alpha}-2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|x-y|^{-2}|V(x)||V(y)|dxdy\right)

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}(\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{3,\alpha}}^{2}+m^{\frac{4}{\alpha}-2}\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}).

If
V\in\mathcal{R}_{3,\alpha}\cap\mathcal{R}
, we restate Lemma
6.8
for any
\alpha\in(0,2)
,

m>0
and
d\geq 3
as follows

R_{0}^{m}(x)\geq C_{d,\alpha,m}\max\{|x|^{-(d-\alpha)},|x|^{-(d-2)}\}\geq\frac{C_{d,\alpha,m}}{2}(|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}+|x|^{-(d-2)}).

Hence, for
d=3
we get

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|K^{m}_{0}(x-y)|^{2}dxdy=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}R^{m}_{0}(x-y)^{2}|V(x)||V(y)|dxdy

\displaystyle\geq C_{\alpha,m}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|x-y|^{-(6-2\alpha)}|V(x)||V(y)|dxdy+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|x-y|^{-2}|V(x)||V(y)|dxdy\right)

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha,m}(\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}_{3,\alpha}}^{2}+\left\|V\right\|_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}).

The remaining part of the claim follows in the same way as in part (1).
∎

5.
The case
\alpha=1

In this section we study the
\alpha\to 1
limit and, more generally, consider
\alpha\to\frac{d}{2}
for

d=1,2,3
, using the spaces defined in (
3.7
). As seen in Proposition
3.10
,
this limit taken directly does not lead to a sensible set of potentials. Here we show, however, that the limit
of Schrödinger operators with Rollnik-class potentials in
\Gamma
-convergence sense behaves much better,
and as a bonus we even obtain self-adjointness of the limit operator.

Recall the Lévy jump measures (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) and let

[u]_{m,\alpha}^{2}:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{2}j_{m,\alpha}(|h|)dxdh

be a Gagliardo-type seminorm on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and rewrite fractional Sobolev space

H^{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\Big\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):[u]_{0,\alpha}<\infty\Big\}

equivalently in its terms. For a given potential
V
, we define the quadratic form associated to the
operator formally written as
H=L_{m,\alpha}+V
by

\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha}^{V}[u]:=[u]_{m,\alpha}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)u^{2}(x)dx,\quad u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We denote
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha}^{V})=H^{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(|V|)
and set

\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha}^{V}[u]=\infty
if
u\not\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha}^{V})
. We will show self-adjointness
of the limit Schrödinger operator obtained on varying
\alpha
by means of the
\Gamma
-convergence of
the associated quadratic forms.

First we recall the concept of
\Gamma
-convergence.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a metric space,
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of functionals
F_{n}:\mathscr{M}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, and
F:\mathscr{M}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be another functional. We say that the sequence
is
\Gamma
-convergent
to
F
as
n\to\infty
, denoted
F=\Gamma-\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}
, if

(1)

for all sequences
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathscr{M}
convergent to
x\in\mathscr{M}
the property
F(x)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(x_{n})
holds

(2)

for all
x\in\mathscr{M}
there exists a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\to x
in
\mathscr{M}
such that
F(x)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(x_{n})
.

Furthermore, if
\mathscr{M}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we say that
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
Mosco-convergent
to
F
as
n\to\infty
, denoted
F={\rm M}-\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}
, if property (2) above holds and

(1_{w})

for all sequences
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
weakly convergent to
x\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
it follows that

F(x)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(x_{n})
.

It is clear that if
F={\rm M}-\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}
, then also
F=\Gamma-\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}

in the strong topology of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We will make use of the following decomposition, obtained in
[
30
, Lem. 2]
, see also
[
2
, Prop. 2.7]
.
For every
m>0
,
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
d\geq 1
there exists a function
\Sigma_{m,\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
the property that

(5.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Sigma_{m,\alpha}(|x|)dx=m,

such that

(5.2)

j_{0,\alpha}(r)=j_{m,\alpha}(r)+\Sigma_{m,\alpha}(r).

This leads to the following domination property.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
m>0
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
. Then

(5.3)

[u]^{2}_{m,\alpha}\leq[u]^{2}_{0,\alpha}\leq[u]_{m,\alpha}^{2}+2m\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}

for every
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In particular, if
u\in H^{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
[u]_{m,\alpha}<\infty

for all
m>0
. Conversely, if
[u]_{m,\alpha}<\infty
for some
m>0
, then
u\in H^{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

The first bound in (
5.3
) is a straightforward consequence of (
5.2
) and the fact that

\sigma_{m,\alpha}
is positive. For the second we have

\displaystyle[u]^{2}_{0,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{2}j_{0,\alpha}(|h|)dxdh

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{2}j_{m,\alpha}(|h|)dxdh+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{2}\Sigma_{m,\alpha}(|h|)dxdh

\displaystyle=:[u]^{2}_{m,\alpha}+I.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|u(y)|^{2}+|u(x)|^{2})\Sigma_{m,\alpha}(|x-y|)dxdy

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(y)|^{2}\Sigma_{m,\alpha}(|x-y|)dxdy=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(y)|^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sigma_{m,\alpha}(|x-y|)dx\right)dy=2m\left\|u\right\|_{2},

where we used (
5.1
).
∎

Now we can prove our result on
\Gamma
-convergence yielding self-adjointness.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
d=1,2,3
,
m\geq 0
, and consider a potential
V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{m,p}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}
for
some
p\geq 4
such that
V_{-}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the Schrödinger operator
H=L_{m,\frac{d}{2}}+V

is self-adjoint
in the form sense
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}^{m,p}
there exists a sequence
(\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\left(\frac{d}{2},2\right)
such that
\alpha_{k}\downarrow\frac{d}{2}
and
V\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d,\alpha_{k}}^{m}
. Consider the sequence of Schrödinger operators
H_{k}:=L_{\alpha_{k},m}+V
.
By Section
4.1

H_{k}
is self-adjoint for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence we can consider the
lower-semicontinuous (in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) quadratic forms
\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha_{k}}^{V}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m,\frac{d}{2}}^{V}[u]:=[u]_{m,\frac{d}{2}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)u^{2}(x)dx,\quad u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We show that
\mathcal{A}_{m,\frac{d}{2}}^{V}=\Gamma-\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha_{k}}^{V}
. First note that

[\cdot]_{m,\frac{d}{2}}={\rm M}-\lim_{k\to\infty}[\cdot]_{m,\alpha_{k}}
by
[
8
, Lem. 3.2]
. We
proceed in two steps.

Step 1.
We show that condition (1) in Definition
5.1
holds. Let
u_{k}\to u
in

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In case that
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha_{k}}^{V}[u_{k}]=\infty
, the statement is trivial,
thus we assume that
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha_{k}}^{V}[u_{k}]<\infty
. With no loss of generality we
assume that the sequence
u_{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle(1)\,\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha_{k}}^{V}[u_{k}]=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha_{k}}^{V}[u_{k}],

\displaystyle(2)\,\liminf_{k\to\infty}\,[u_{k}]_{m,\alpha_{k}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}[u_{k}]_{m,\alpha_{k}},

\displaystyle(3)\,\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V(x)+C_{V})|u_{k}(x)|^{2}dx=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V(x)+C_{V})|u_{k}(x)|^{2}dx,

\displaystyle(4)\,\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}(x)\to u(x)\ \mbox{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
C_{V}:=\left\|V_{-}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Note also that

\lim_{k\to\infty}C_{V}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{k}(x)|^{2}dx=C_{V}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)|^{2}dx.

Then we have

(5.4)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha_{k}}^{V}[u_{k}]=\lim_{k\to\infty}[u_{k}]_{m,\alpha_{k}}+\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V(x)+C_{V})|u_{k}(x)|^{2}dx-C_{V}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)|^{2}dx.

By Mosco-convergence of
[\cdot]_{m,\alpha_{k}}
to
[\cdot]_{m,\frac{d}{2}}
it follows that

\lim_{k\to\infty}[u_{k}]_{m,\alpha_{k}}\geq[u]_{m,\frac{d}{2}}.

Furthermore, since
(V(x)+C_{V})|u_{k}(x)|^{2}\geq 0
, Fatou’s Lemma gives

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V(x)+C_{V})|u_{k}(x)|^{2}dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V(x)+C_{V})|u(x)|^{2}dx.

Hence, by (
5.4
) we get

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha_{k}}^{V}[u_{k}]

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}[u_{k}]_{m,\alpha_{k}}+\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V(x)+C_{V})|u_{k}(x)|^{2}dx-C_{V}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq[u]_{m,\alpha_{k}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V(x)+C_{V})|u(x)|^{2}dx-C_{V}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)|^{2}dx=\mathcal{A}_{m,\frac{d}{2}}^{V}[u].

Step 2.

Next we show that condition (2) in Definition
5.1
also holds. Let
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
If
u\not\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{m,\frac{d}{2}}^{V})
, then the statement is trivial with the sequence

u_{k}=u
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. If
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{m,\frac{d}{2}}^{V})
, then consider the sequence

(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
u_{k}\to u
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
[u]_{m,\frac{d}{2}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}[u_{k}]_{m,\alpha_{k}}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
u_{k}(x)\to u(x)
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and that there exists a constant
M_{1}
(possibly dependent on
m
,

d
and
u
) such that
\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}+[u_{k}]_{m,\alpha_{k}}\leq M_{1}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By
(
5.3
) we have
[u_{k}]_{0,\alpha_{k}}^{2}\leq M_{2}
for some constant
M_{2}
. Thus we obtain

\displaystyle[u_{k}]_{0,\frac{d}{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{d2^{\frac{d}{2}-3}}{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{k}(x+h)-u_{k}(x)|^{2}|h|^{-\frac{3d}{2}}dhdx

\displaystyle=\frac{d2^{\frac{d}{2}-1}}{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{B_{1}}|u_{k}(x+h)-u_{k}(x)|^{2}|h|^{-\frac{3d}{2}}dhdx

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{d2^{\frac{d}{2}-1}}{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{B_{1}^{c}}|u_{k}(x+h)-u_{k}(x)|^{2}|h|^{-\frac{3d}{2}}dhdx=I_{1}^{k}+I_{2}^{k}.

For the first integral we have

I_{1}^{k}\leq\frac{d2^{\frac{d}{2}-1}}{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{B_{1}}|u_{k}(x+h)-u_{k}(x)|^{2}|h|^{-d-\alpha_{k}}dhdx\leq C_{0}[u_{k}]_{0,\alpha_{k}}^{2},

where

C_{0}:=\max_{\alpha\in\left[\frac{d}{2},\alpha_{1}\right]}\frac{d2^{\frac{d}{2}-\alpha+1}\Gamma\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}{\alpha_{k}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\right)}.

For the second,

I_{2}^{k}\leq\frac{\sigma_{d}2^{\frac{d}{2}+1}}{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
, independent of
k
, such that

[u_{k}]_{0,\frac{d}{2}}\leq C_{1}([u_{k}]_{0,\alpha_{k}}+\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{2})\leq M_{3},

where
M_{3}>0
is a suitable constant. By the Sobolev inequality (see
[
11
, Th. 6.5]
)
we have

\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{q}\leq M_{4}(q)

for every
q\in\left[2,\frac{8}{3}\right]
, where
M_{4}(q)>0
is a suitable constant.
Note that
\frac{8}{3}
is the critical Sobolev exponent in
H^{\frac{d}{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This
further implies that

\left\||u_{k}|^{2}\right\|_{q}\leq M_{5}(q)

for a suitable constant
M_{5}(q)
, for all
q\in\left[1,\frac{4}{3}\right]
. Hence

\left\||u_{k}|^{2}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq M_{5}(p^{\prime})
due to
p\geq 4
. Without loss of generality, we
can then assume that
|u_{k}|^{2}\rightharpoonup|u|^{2}
weakly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)|u_{k}(x)|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)|u(x)|^{2}dx

and so

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha_{k}}^{V}[u_{k}]=\lim_{k\to\infty}[u_{k}]_{m,\alpha_{k}}+\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)|u_{k}(x)|^{2}dx=[u]_{m,\frac{d}{2}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)|u(x)|^{2}dx=\mathcal{A}_{m,\frac{d}{2}}^{V}[u].

Hence
\mathcal{A}^{V}_{m,\frac{d}{2}}=\Gamma-\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{m,\alpha_{k}}^{V}
. By
[
4
, Prop. 1.28]
this
implies that
\mathcal{A}_{m,\frac{d}{2}}^{V}
is lower semi-continuous. However,
\mathcal{A}_{m,\frac{d}{2}}
is the quadratic
form associated with the operator
H
, hence by
[
10
, Th. 12.13]
the operator
H
is self-adjoint in the form sense.
∎

In this section we prove the resolvent estimates stated in Section
2.3
. We treat the massless
(
m=0

6.1.
Massless case

6.1.1.
Proof of estimate (
2.14
) in the
m=0
case

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
d\geq 1
. There exists
C_{d,\alpha}>0
such that for all
\lambda>0
and

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}

R_{\lambda}(x)\;\leq\;C_{d,\alpha}\begin{cases}|x|^{\alpha-d},&\alpha<d,\\[10.0pt]
\log\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda|x|}\right)+1,&\alpha=d=1,\\[10.0pt]
|x|^{\alpha-1}\wedge 1+\lambda^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}},&\alpha>d=1.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if
\alpha<d
, then the statement holds also for
\lambda=0
.

Proof.

From (2.11) it follows that there exists a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

(6.1)

p_{t}(x)\leq c_{1}t|x|^{-d-\alpha}\;\;\mbox{if $t<|x|^{\alpha}$}\quad\mbox{and}\quad p_{t}(x)\leq c_{1}t^{-d/\alpha}\;\;\mbox{if $t>|x|^{\alpha}$}.

We write

R_{\lambda}(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{|x|^{\alpha}}+\int_{|x|^{\alpha}}^{\infty}\right)e^{-\lambda t}p_{t}(x)dt=I_{1}+I_{2}.

We first estimate
I_{1}
as

I_{1}\leq c_{1}|x|^{-d-\alpha}\int_{0}^{|x|^{\alpha}}te^{-\lambda t}dt\leq c_{1}|x|^{-d-\alpha}\int_{0}^{|x|^{\alpha}}tdt=\frac{c_{1}}{2}|x|^{-d+\alpha}

if
\alpha\leq d
. If
\alpha>d=1
and
|x|\geq 1
, by
(\lambda t)^{1-1/\alpha}e^{-\lambda t}\leq(1-1/\alpha)^{1-1/\alpha}e^{-1+1/\alpha}
, we have

I_{1}\leq c_{1}\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha e}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}|x|^{-1-\alpha}\lambda^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{|x|^{\alpha}}t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}dt=c_{1}\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha e}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}\lambda^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}.

In estimating
I_{2}
we have three cases to consider. If
\alpha<d
, we have

I_{2}\leq c_{1}\int_{|x|^{\alpha}}^{\infty}t^{-d/\alpha}e^{-\lambda t}dt\leq c_{1}\int_{|x|^{\alpha}}^{\infty}t^{-d/\alpha}dt=\frac{c_{1}}{\frac{d}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{-d+\alpha}.

Note that the same estimate holds for
\lambda=0
in this case. If
d=1
and
\alpha>d
, we get

I_{2}\leq c_{1}\int_{|x|^{\alpha}}^{\infty}t^{-1/\alpha}e^{-\lambda t}dt\leq c_{1}\lambda^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1/\alpha}e^{-t}dt=c_{1}\lambda^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\Gamma\Big(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\Big).

In case
\alpha=d=1
we use the exponential integral function

E_{1}(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-t}dt,\ x>0

and recall the following bracketing property (see
[
1
, Ch. 5, eq. 5.1.20]
)

(6.2)

\frac{e^{-x}}{2}\log\Big(1+\frac{2}{x}\Big)\leq E_{1}(x)\leq e^{-x}\log\Big(1+\frac{1}{x}\Big).

Hence we obtain

I_{2}\leq c_{1}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-\lambda t}dt\leq c_{1}\int_{\lambda|x|}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-t}dt=c_{1}E_{1}(\lambda|x|)\leq c_{1}\log\Big(1+\frac{1}{\lambda|x|}\Big).

∎

6.1.2.
Proof of estimate (
2.18
)

Below we will make use of the inequality

(6.3)

s^{p}e^{-s}\leq p^{p}e^{-p},

for
s,p\geq 0
. Beside Proposition
6.1
, we can also prove a further estimate that is sharper for large values of
|x|
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
d\geq 1
. Then there exists a positive constant
C_{d,\alpha}
such that
for all
\lambda>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

R_{\lambda}(x)\leq\frac{C_{d,\alpha}}{\lambda^{2}|x|^{d+\alpha}}.

Proof.

We give another estimate of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
in the proof of Lemma
6.1
. Concerning
I_{1}
, we have

I_{1}\leq c_{1}|x|^{-d-\alpha}\int_{0}^{|x|^{\alpha}}te^{-\lambda t}dt=c_{1}|x|^{-d-\alpha}\left(-|x|^{\alpha}\frac{e^{-\lambda|x|^{\alpha}}}{\lambda}-\frac{e^{-\lambda|x|^{\alpha}}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\lambda^{2}|x|^{d+\alpha}}

Next, observe that

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq c_{1}\int_{|x|^{\alpha}}^{\infty}t^{-d/\alpha}e^{-\lambda t}dt=c_{2}\lambda^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-1}\int_{\lambda|x|^{\alpha}}^{\infty}s^{-d/\alpha}e^{-s}ds.

By (
6.3
), we clearly have

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq\frac{2c_{1}}{e}\lambda^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-1}\int_{\lambda|x|^{\alpha}}^{\infty}s^{-d/\alpha-1}e^{-\frac{s}{2}}ds\leq\frac{2c_{1}}{e}e^{-\frac{\lambda|x|^{\alpha}}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-1}\int_{\lambda|x|^{\alpha}}^{\infty}s^{-d/\alpha-1}ds\\
&=\frac{2\alpha c_{1}}{de}e^{-\frac{\lambda|x|^{\alpha}}{2}}\lambda^{-1}|x|^{-d}\leq\frac{4\alpha c_{1}}{d\lambda^{2}e^{2}|x|^{d+\alpha}}.\end{split}

∎

Combining Proposition
6.1
and Lemma
6.2
we get the following non-uniform upper bound.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
d\geq 1
. Then for all
\lambda>0
there exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha,\lambda}>0

such that

R_{\lambda}(x)\leq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda}H_{d,\alpha}^{0}(|x|),

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, where
H_{d,\alpha}^{0}
is defined in (
2.18
).

Proposition
6.3
corresponds to the upper bound in (
2.18
). We only have to prove the lower bound.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
d\geq 1
. Then for all
\lambda>0
there exists
C_{d,\alpha,\lambda}>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}

R_{\lambda}(x)\geq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda}H_{d,\alpha}^{0}(|x|),

where
H_{d,\alpha}^{0}
is defined in (
2.17
).

Proof.

First we give an estimate for
|x|>1
. By (
2.11
) there exists a constant
c_{2}
such that

(6.4)

p_{t}(x)\geq c_{2}t|x|^{-d-\alpha}\;\;\mbox{if $t<|x|^{\alpha}$}\quad\mbox{and}\quad p_{t}(x)\geq c_{2}t^{-d/\alpha}\;\;\mbox{if $t>|x|^{\alpha}$}.

Using (
6.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{|x|^{\alpha}}e^{-\lambda t}p_{t}(x)dt\geq c_{2}|x|^{-d-\alpha}\int_{0}^{|x|^{\alpha}}te^{-\lambda t}dt

\displaystyle\geq c_{2}|x|^{-d-\alpha}\int_{0}^{|x|^{\alpha}}te^{-\lambda t}dt\geq\frac{c_{2}}{e^{\lambda}\lambda^{2}}|x|^{-d-\alpha}\left(e^{\lambda}-1-\lambda\right).

Now let
|x|\leq 1
and assume
\alpha<d
. Then we have, again by (
6.1
),

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle\geq c_{2}|x|^{-d-\alpha}\int_{0}^{|x|^{\alpha}}te^{-\lambda t}dt\geq c_{2}|x|^{-d-\alpha}e^{-\lambda}\int_{0}^{|x|^{\alpha}}tdt=\frac{c_{2}}{2}e^{-\lambda}|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}.

If
\alpha=d=1
we get instead

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle\geq c_{2}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-\lambda t}=c_{2}E_{1}(\lambda|x|)\geq\frac{c_{2}}{2}e^{-\lambda}\log\left(1+\frac{2}{\lambda|x|}\right)\geq C_{1,1,\lambda}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{|x|}\right),

where we also used (
6.2
). Finally, for

∎

6.2.
Massive case

6.2.1.
The function
\kappa_{m}

Next we turn to the massive case. First we state some basic properties of the function
\kappa_{m}
defined in
(
2.13
).

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
d\geq 1
.

(1)

We have

(6.5)

\lim_{m\downarrow 0}\kappa_{m}(r)=2^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\right)r^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}.

(2)

There exists a positive constant
C_{1,d,\alpha}
such that

(6.6)

\kappa_{m}(r)\leq C_{1,d,\alpha}r^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}.

(3)

There exists a positive constant
C_{2,d,\alpha}
such that

(6.7)

\kappa_{m}(r)\geq C_{2,d,\alpha}r^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}

for
r\leq 1
.

Proof.

Part (1) directly follows by the second asymptotic relation in (
2.3
). Consider part (2) and (3). We note that the modified Bessel function of the second kind
K_{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}(r)
is positive, bounded and does not have any zero points for
r>0
. Using (
2.3
) again, we have

(6.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\uparrow\infty}\sqrt{r}e^{r}K_{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}(r)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}},

(6.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\downarrow 0}r^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}K_{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}(r)=2^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\right).

Hence (
6.6
) and (
6.7
) hold for
r\leq 1
by (
6.9
). If
r\geq 1
,

c_{1,d,\alpha}:=\sup_{r\geq 1}\sqrt{r}e^{r}K_{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}(r)

is finite by (
6.8
). Thus we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{m}(r)\leq c_{1,d,\alpha}m^{\frac{d+\alpha-1}{2\alpha}}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}r}=c_{1,d,\alpha}r^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\left(m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}r\right)^{\frac{d+\alpha-1}{2}}e^{-m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}r}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1,d,\alpha}r^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\left(\frac{d+\alpha+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{d+\alpha+1}{2}}e^{-\frac{d+\alpha+1}{2}}.

∎

Since the function

\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(r)=\frac{r^{\alpha/2}}{\kappa_{m}(r)^{\alpha/(d+\alpha)}},\quad r>0

will play a prominent role in what follows, we show some of its elementary properties.

Lemma 6.6
.

The following hold:

(1)

For
r\downarrow 0
we have

\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(r)\sim\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha}{d+\alpha}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\Gamma^{\frac{\alpha}{d+\alpha}}\left(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\right)}r^{\alpha}.

As a consequence, for all
R>0
there exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha,m,R}
such that for all
r\in(0,R]

(6.10)

\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(r)\leq C_{d,\alpha,m,R}r^{\alpha}.

(2)

As
r\uparrow\infty
we have

\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(r)\sim\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha}{2(d+\alpha)}}}{m^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2(d+\alpha)}}\pi^{\frac{\alpha}{2(d+\alpha)}}}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2(d+\alpha)}}e^{\frac{\alpha}{d+\alpha}m^{1/\alpha}r}.

(3)

The function
\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(r)
is strictly increasing.

(4)

For every fixed
r>0
we have

\lim_{m\downarrow 0}\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(r)=\frac{2^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\Gamma\Big(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\Big)^{d/(d+\alpha)}}r^{\alpha}.

(5)

There exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha}
such that for all
r>0

(6.11)

\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(r)\geq C_{d,\alpha}r^{\alpha}.

Proof.

Using (
2.3
), properties (1)-(2) are straightforward. (3) is also immediate since
\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(r)
is
the product of the two strictly increasing positive functions
r\in(0,\infty)\mapsto m^{-1/2}r^{\alpha/2}
and
r\in(0,\infty)\mapsto\big(K_{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}(m^{1/\alpha}r)\big)^{-\alpha/(d+\alpha)}
. Finally, (4) and (5) are
consequences of (
6.5
) and (
6.6
), respectively.
∎

6.2.2.
Proof of estimate (
2.14
) in the
m>0
case

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 3
. There exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha}>0
such that for all
m>0
,
\lambda\geq 0

and

R^{m}_{\lambda}(x)\leq C_{d,\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{d-\alpha}}+\frac{m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}}{|x|^{d-2}}\right).

Proof.

While in the massless case we split
R_{\lambda}(x)
into two integrals, here we have to deal with four different
integrals depending on the range of
|x|
. Indeed, by (
2.12
) it follows for
m>0
that there exists
a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

(6.12)

\displaystyle p^{m}_{t}(x)\leq\begin{cases}c_{1}t|x|^{-(d+\alpha)/2}\kappa_{m}(|x|)&\mbox{if $t<\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\wedge\frac{1}{m}$}\\
\\
c_{1}t^{-d/\alpha}&\mbox{if $\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)<t\leq\frac{1}{m}$}\\
\\
c_{1}m^{d/\alpha-d/2}t^{-d/2}e^{-c(m^{1/\alpha}|x|)}&\mbox{if $\frac{1}{m}<t<m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}|x|$}\\
\\
c_{1}m^{d/\alpha-d/2}t^{-d/2}e^{-c(m^{2/\alpha-1}|x|^{2}/t)}&\mbox{if $m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}|x|\vee\frac{1}{m}\leq t$}.\end{cases}

According to this split, we define

\displaystyle I_{1}^{m}:=\int_{0}^{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\wedge\frac{1}{m}}e^{-\lambda t}p_{t}^{m}(x)dt,\qquad I_{2}^{m}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}^{1/m}e^{-\lambda t}p_{t}^{m}(x)dt&\mbox{if $\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)<\frac{1}{m}$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle I_{3}^{m}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{1/m}^{m^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}|x|}e^{-\lambda t}p_{t}^{m}(x)dt&\mbox{if $|x|^{\alpha}>\frac{1}{m}$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad I_{4}^{m}:=\int_{m^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}|x|\vee\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}p_{t}^{m}(x)dt,

so that

R_{\lambda}(x)=I_{1}^{m}+I_{2}^{m}+I_{3}^{m}+I_{4}^{m}.

We will use (
6.12
) to estimate each integral from above. To estimate
I_{1}^{m}
we need to distinguish two
cases. If
\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(|x|)\geq\frac{1}{m}
, we have

I_{1}^{m}\leq c_{1}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{m}}te^{-\lambda t}dt\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2m^{2}}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|).

However, since
\frac{1}{m^{2}}\leq\left(\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(|x|)\right)^{2}
, we get

I_{1}^{m}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2}|x|^{-\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}\kappa^{\frac{d-\alpha}{d+\alpha}}_{m}(|x|)\leq C_{d,\alpha}\frac{c_{1}}{2}|x|^{-(d-\alpha)},

where we also used (
6.6
). In case
\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(|x|)<\frac{1}{m}
, we get

I_{1}^{m}\leq c_{1}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\int_{0}^{\Phi_{m}^{d,\alpha}(|x|)}te^{-\lambda t}dt\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)(\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(|x|))^{2}\leq C_{d,\alpha}\frac{c_{1}}{2}|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}.

We only need to estimate
I_{2}^{m}
in case
\Phi^{m}_{d,\alpha}(|x|)<\frac{1}{m}
. Since
d\geq 3>\alpha
, we have

I_{2}^{m}\leq c_{1}\int_{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}^{\frac{1}{m}}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}e^{-\lambda t}dt\leq c_{1}\int_{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}dt=\frac{\alpha c_{1}}{d-\alpha}\left(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\right)^{-\frac{d-\alpha}{\alpha}}\leq C_{d,\alpha}\frac{\alpha c_{1}}{d-\alpha}|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}.

Next we estimate
I_{3}^{m}
. We only need to consider the case
|x|^{\alpha}\geq\frac{1}{m}
giving

\displaystyle I_{3}^{m}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{m^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}|x|}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}dt\leq c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}dt=\frac{2c_{1}}{d-2}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-1}e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|}

\displaystyle=\frac{2c_{1}}{(d-2)c^{d-\alpha}|x|^{d-\alpha}}\left(cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|\right)^{d-\alpha}e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|}\leq\frac{2c_{1}(d-\alpha)^{d-\alpha}}{(d-2)e^{d-\alpha}c^{d-\alpha}|x|^{d-\alpha}}.

Finally, consider
I_{4}^{m}
. With
|x|^{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{m}
we obtain

\displaystyle I_{4}^{m}\leq c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt\leq c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt.

The change of variable
s=cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t
gives

\displaystyle I_{4}^{m}\leq\frac{c_{1}m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}}{c^{\frac{d-2}{2}}|x|^{d-2}}\int_{cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}|x|^{2}}^{\infty}s^{\frac{d}{2}-2}e^{-s}ds\leq\frac{c_{1}m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}}{c^{\frac{d-2}{2}}|x|^{d-2}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{d}{2}-1\Big).

∎

6.2.3.
Proof of estimate (
2.15
)

Since

|x|^{\alpha-d}+m^{2/\alpha-1}|x|^{2-d}\leq 2(1\vee m^{2/\alpha-1})(|x|^{\alpha-d}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}+|x|^{2-d}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|>1\}}),

the above also shows the upper bound in (
2.15
). The lower bound is shown in the next statement.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
d\geq 3
. Then, for all
m>0
there exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha,m}>0
such that for all

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}

R_{0}^{m}(x)\geq C_{d,\alpha,m}(|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}+|x|^{-(d-2)}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|>1\}}).

Proof.

Now we use the lower bounds in (
2.12
). Thus there exists a constant
c_{2}>0
such that

(6.13)

\displaystyle p^{m}_{t}(x)\geq\begin{cases}c_{2}t|x|^{-(d+\alpha)/2}\kappa_{m}(|x|)&\mbox{if $t<\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\wedge\frac{1}{m}$}\\
\\
c_{2}t^{-d/\alpha}&\mbox{if $\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)<t\leq\frac{1}{m}$}\\
\\
c_{2}m^{d/\alpha-d/2}t^{-d/2}e^{-c(m^{1/\alpha}|x|)}&\mbox{if $\frac{1}{m}<t<m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}|x|$}\\
\\
c_{2}m^{d/\alpha-d/2}t^{-d/2}e^{-c(m^{2/\alpha-1}|x|^{2}/t)}&\mbox{if $m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}|x|\vee\frac{1}{m}\leq t$}.\end{cases}

Consider the case
|x|>1
and let first
|x|^{\alpha}>\frac{1}{m}
. We obtain

I_{3}^{m}\geq c_{2}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{m^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}|x|}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}dt\geq c_{2}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{m^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}|x|}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt,

since for
t\leq m^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}|x|
we clearly have
e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|}\geq e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}
. Hence for all
|x|>1
we get

R_{0}^{m}(x)\geq I_{3}^{m}+I_{4}^{m}\geq c_{2}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt.

The substitution
s=\frac{cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}}{t}
then gives

R_{0}^{m}(x)\geq c_{2}m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}c^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}|x|^{-(d-2)}\int_{0}^{cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}|x|^{2}}s^{\frac{d}{2}-2}e^{-s}ds\geq c_{2}m^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}c^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}|x|^{-(d-2)}\int_{0}^{cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}s^{\frac{d}{2}-2}e^{-s}ds

where the integral at the right-hand side is finite since
d\geq 3
. Next consider
|x|\leq 1
. If

\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\leq\frac{1}{m}
, then

\displaystyle R_{0}^{m}(x)\geq I_{1}^{m}\geq c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\int_{0}^{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}t\,dt=\frac{c_{2}}{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)^{2}\geq C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{-(d-\alpha)},

where we used (
6.11
) and the fact that
\kappa_{m}(|x|)\geq C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}

since
|x|\leq 1
. If
\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)>\frac{1}{m}
, we have

\displaystyle R_{0}^{m}(x)

\displaystyle\geq I_{1}^{m}\geq c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{m}}t\,dt=\frac{c_{2}}{2m^{2}}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{2}}{2m^{2}(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(1))^{2}}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|))^{2}\geq c_{d,\alpha,m}|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}.

∎

Remark 6.9
.

Notice that, up to a multiplicative constant, the term
|x|^{-(d-2)}
represents the
0
-resolvent kernel of the
Laplacian. Indeed, we could say that the
0
-potential
R_{0}^{m}
interpolates between the massless case
R_{0}(x)=C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}
and the classical Newtonian case (i.e.
\alpha=2
) that we denote here by

R_{0}^{\rm N}(x)=C_{d}|x|^{-(d-2)}
. To observe this, we introduce a constant
c_{\ell}
and write
c_{\ell}^{2}m
in
place of
m
; this constant is physically motivated and it stands for the speed of light (which was set to
1

in units used in mathematical physics). Taking
m\downarrow 0
and using
[
8
, Lem. 3.2]
it follows that

[\cdot]_{0,\alpha}={\rm M}-\lim_{m\to 0}[\cdot]_{c_{\ell}^{2}m,\alpha}
, which implies by an application of

[
8
, Prop. 2.1]
the strong resolvent convergence of the operators
-L_{c_{\ell}^{2}m,\alpha}
to

-L_{0,\alpha}
as
m\downarrow 0
. On the other hand, if we consider the classical limit
c_{\ell}\to\infty
,
we obtain again by
[
8
, Lem. 3.2]
that
[\cdot]_{0,2}={\rm M}-\lim_{c_{\ell}\to\infty}[\cdot]_{c_{\ell}^{2}m,\alpha}
, where
[u]_{0,2}=\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}
for all
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence

L_{c_{\ell}^{2}m,\alpha}
converges to
-\Delta
in strong resolvent sense.

6.2.4.
Proof of estimate (
2.16
)

We start by the following preliminary estimate.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 1
and
m>0
. There exists
C_{d,\alpha}>0
such that

R^{m}_{\lambda}(x)\leq C_{d,\alpha}\begin{cases}\Big(1+\frac{me^{-\lambda/m}}{\lambda}\Big)\frac{1}{|x|^{d-\alpha}}&\alpha<d\\
\\
\log\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda|x|}\right)+1+\frac{m^{1/\alpha}}{\lambda}e^{-\lambda/m}&\alpha=d=1\\
\\
|x|^{\alpha-1}\wedge 1+\lambda^{-\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}+\frac{m^{1/\alpha}}{\lambda}e^{-\lambda/m}&\alpha>d=1\end{cases}

holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\lambda>0
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
6.7
, we have

I_{1}^{m}\leq C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}

if
\alpha\leq d
. If
\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\geq{\frac{1}{m}}
and
\alpha>d=1
, by (
6.5
), there exists a small
m_{0}>0
such that

(6.14)

\kappa_{m}(r)\geq 2^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}-2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\right)r^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}

for
0<m<m_{0}
. We thus have

I_{1}^{m}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2}|x|^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}}(|x|)\leq C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{\alpha-1}

for
|x|<1
or
0<m<m_{0}
by (
6.7
) and (
6.14
) noting
(1-\alpha)/(1+d)<0
. In the case were
|x|\geq 1
and
m\geq m_{0}
, since
2-(1+1/\alpha)=1-1/\alpha>0
and

\frac{1}{m^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\leq\left(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\right)^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}},

we estimate

\displaystyle I_{1}^{m}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2m^{2}}|x|^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2m_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}|x|^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\left(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\right)^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}=\frac{c_{1}}{2m_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}.

If
\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)<{\frac{1}{m}}
and
\alpha>d=1
, we have
I_{1}^{m}\leq C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{\alpha-1}
for
|x|<1
or
0<m<m_{0}
in the same way above. For
|x|\geq 1
and
m\geq m_{0}
, we also have

\displaystyle I_{1}^{m}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2}|x|^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2m_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}|x|^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\left(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\right)^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}=\frac{c_{1}}{2m_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}

since

\left(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}<\frac{1}{m^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}.

If
d>\alpha
, we obtain

I_{2}^{m}\leq C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}.

If
d=\alpha=1
, by making use of (
6.2
) it follows that

\displaystyle I_{2}^{m}\leq c_{1}\int_{\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|)}^{\frac{1}{m}}t^{-1}e^{-\lambda t}dt

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\int_{\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|)}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-\lambda t}dt=c_{1}\int_{\lambda\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|)}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-t}dt

\displaystyle=c_{1}E_{1}(\lambda\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|))\leq c_{1}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{C_{d,\alpha}\lambda|x|}\right)\leq C_{d,\alpha}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda|x|}\right).

Finally, if
\alpha>d=1
, then

I_{2}^{m}\leq c_{1}\int_{\Phi_{1,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}^{\frac{1}{m}}t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}e^{-\lambda t}dt\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}e^{-\lambda t}dt=c_{1}\lambda^{-\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\right).

Next we estimate
I_{3}^{m}
. It is necessary to do this only if
|x|^{\alpha}>\frac{1}{m}
. In this case by
(
6.12
) we get

(6.15)

I_{3}^{m}\leq c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{m^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}|x|}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}dt\leq\frac{c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}}}{\lambda}e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|-\frac{\lambda}{m}}.

It is clear that if
\alpha\geq d=1

I_{3}^{m}\leq C_{1,\alpha}\frac{m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{\lambda}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}.

For
\alpha<d
, by (
6.3
) we have

I_{3}^{m}\leq\frac{c_{1}m}{\lambda c^{d-\alpha}|x|^{d-\alpha}}\left(cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|\right)^{d-\alpha}e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}\leq\frac{c_{1}m(d-\alpha)^{d-\alpha}}{\lambda c^{d-\alpha}e^{d-\alpha}|x|^{d-\alpha}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}.

Finally, for
I_{4}^{m}
we have two cases. If
|x|^{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{m}
, then for
d>\alpha
we obtain

\displaystyle I_{4}^{m}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}m^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{c^{\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}|x|^{d-\alpha}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left(cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t\right)^{\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}m^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{e^{\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}c^{\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}|x|^{d-\alpha}}\left(\frac{d-\alpha}{2}\right)^{\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}dt\leq\frac{c_{2}me^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}}{e^{\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}c^{\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}\lambda|x|^{d-\alpha}}\left(\frac{d-\alpha}{2}\right)^{\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}.

For
\alpha\geq d=1
we estimate like

\displaystyle I_{4}^{m}\leq c_{1}m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt\leq c_{1}\frac{m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{\lambda}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}.

In case
|x|^{\alpha}>\frac{1}{m}
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{4}^{m}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{m^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}|x|}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt\leq c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt

and then we proceed as in the previous case.
∎

The following estimate is sharper for large values of
|x|
.

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 1
and
m>0
. Then there exists
C_{d,\alpha}>0
such that for all

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and

we have

R^{m}_{\lambda}(x)\leq C_{d,\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}}{m\lambda}+\frac{e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{|x|^{d+\alpha}}.

Proof.

We estimate
I^{m}_{j}
,
j=1,2,3,4
, in the proof of Lemma
6.10
in different ways. First consider
I_{1}^{m}
. By (
6.6
) we get

I_{1}^{m}\leq c_{1}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\int_{0}^{\min\left\{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|),\frac{1}{m}\right\}}te^{-\lambda t}dt\leq c_{1}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\int_{0}^{\infty}te^{-\lambda t}dt\leq\frac{C_{d,\alpha}}{\lambda^{2}|x|^{d+\alpha}}.

Next consider
I_{2}^{m}
. We need to obtain an estimate only for the case
\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)<\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle I_{2}^{m}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\int_{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}^{\frac{1}{m}}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}e^{-\lambda t}dt\leq\frac{2c_{1}}{\lambda e}\int_{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}-1}e^{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}}dt\leq\frac{2\alpha c_{1}}{d\lambda e}e^{-\frac{\lambda\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}{2}}(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|))^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\alpha c_{1}}{d\lambda^{2}e}\left(\frac{\lambda\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}{2}\right)e^{-\frac{\lambda\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}{2}}(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|))^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}-1}\leq\frac{4\alpha c_{1}}{d\lambda^{2}e^{2}}(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|))^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{\alpha}}\leq\frac{C_{d,\alpha}}{\lambda^{2}|x|^{d+\alpha}},

where we also used (
6.11
). Next we need to control
I_{3}^{m}
. We only need to consider the case
|x|^{\alpha}>\frac{1}{m}
.
Starting from (
6.15
) and using (
6.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle I_{3}^{m}\leq\frac{c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}}}{\lambda}e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|-\frac{\lambda}{m}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1}}{\lambda c^{d+\alpha}m|x|^{d+\alpha}}\left(cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|\right)^{d+\alpha}e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|-\frac{\lambda}{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(d+\alpha)^{d+\alpha}c_{1}}{\lambda e^{d+\alpha}c^{d+\alpha}m|x|^{d+\alpha}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}.

Finally, we consider
I_{4}^{m}
. If
|x|^{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{m}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{4}^{m}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}m^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}}{c^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}|x|^{d+\alpha}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left(cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t\right)^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}m^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}}{e^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}c^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}|x|^{d+\alpha}}\left(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\right)^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{e^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}c^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}|x|^{d+\alpha}}\left(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\right)^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\left(\frac{e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}}{m\lambda}+\frac{\alpha e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}}{2\lambda^{2}}\right)

where we computed

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}dt=\frac{e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}}{\lambda m^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}+\frac{\alpha}{2\lambda}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}dt\leq\frac{e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}}{\lambda m^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}+\frac{\alpha}{2\lambda m^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}dt

integrating by parts and noting
\alpha/2-1<0
. If

\displaystyle I_{4}^{m}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{m^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}|x|}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt\leq c_{1}m^{\frac{d}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt

and we proceed as in the previous case.

for all

H^{0}_{d,\alpha}
is defined in (
2.17
).

This shows (
2.16
) for
m>0
.

6.2.5.
Proof of estimate (
2.19
)

To provide the upper bound we improve the bound given in Lemma
6.11
for large values of
\lambda
.

Lemma 6.13
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 1
and
m>0
. Then there exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha}>0
such that for
all
\lambda>m
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}

R^{m}_{\lambda}(x)\leq C_{d,\alpha}\frac{|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)}{(\lambda-m)^{2}}.

Proof.

By
[
7
, eq. (2.16)]
there exists a constant
c_{1}
dependent on
\alpha
and
d
such that for
all
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}

p_{t}(x)\leq c_{1}te^{mt}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)

If
\lambda>m
, we have

R^{m}_{\lambda}(x)\leq c_{1}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\int_{0}^{\infty}te^{-(\lambda-m)t}dt\leq c_{1}\frac{|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)}{(\lambda-m)^{2}}.

∎

Combining the previous estimate with Lemma
6.10
gives the following result.

Proposition 6.14
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
d\geq 1
. Then for all
m>0
and
\lambda>m
, there exists a constant

C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}

R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)\leq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}H^{m}_{d,\alpha}(|x|)

where
H^{m}_{d,\alpha}
is defined in (
2.20
)

Remark 6.15
.

We note that (
6.5
) gives
\lim_{m\downarrow 0}H^{m}_{d,\alpha}(r)=H^{0}_{d,\alpha}(r)
.

Remark 6.16
.

We note that in the classical case (
\alpha=2
, while the value of
m
is not influent in such a case) for
d\geq 2
the resolvent kernel is given by

(6.16)

R_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{\lambda^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}|x|^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}K_{\frac{d-2}{2}}(\sqrt{\lambda}|x|),

as shown in
[
23
, Equation (4.2.20)]
. For
d\geq 3
, we can control

R_{\lambda}(x)\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x)\,dt=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-1\right)}{4\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2}},

where
p_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{d}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}
is the classical heat kernel. This corresponds to Proposition
6.1
in case
2=\alpha<d
. However, notice that this upper bound is not sharp, since for
\sqrt{\lambda}|x|\geq 1
we have, by
[
23
, Lemma 4.97]
,

R_{\lambda}(x)\leq C_{d}\frac{\lambda^{\frac{d-3}{4}}}{|x|^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}.

Thus the behaviour of
R_{\lambda}
for large values of
|x|
is analogous to the one presented in Proposition
6.14
, where we recall that for
m>0
and
|x|\geq 1
we have

|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\leq C_{d,\alpha,m}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha+1}{2}}e^{-m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|}.

Concerning the case
\alpha=d=2
, we recall that, by
[
1
, Ch. 9, eqs. 9.6.8, 9.7.2]
,

(6.17)

K_{0}(z)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}\,e^{-z}\quad\mbox{as $z\to\infty$}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad K_{0}(z)\sim-\log(z)\quad\mbox{as $z\downarrow 0$}.

Furthermore, by
[
23
, Lemmas 4.97, 4.98]
, for
\lambda>0
,

(6.18)

R_{\lambda}(x)\leq C_{d}\left(\log\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\sqrt{\lambda}|x|\leq 1}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}\mathbf{1}_{\sqrt{\lambda}|x|>1}\right).

Once we note that for
\sqrt{\lambda}|x|>1
it holds

\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}\leq e^{-1}

then we get

(6.19)

R_{\lambda}(x)\leq C_{d}\left(\log\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}\right)+1\right).

The latter corresponds to the case
\alpha=d
in Proposition
6.1
. Again, for fixed
\lambda>0
, (
6.18
) is sharper than (
6.19
) and it is analogous to the case
\alpha=d
in Proposition
6.14
. Finally, in case
d=1
, by
[
23
, Equation (4.2.18)]
, we have the explicit expression

(6.20)

R_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.

that clearly satisfies

R_{\lambda}(x)\leq(|x|\wedge 1+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}),

that is the case
\alpha>d
in Proposition
6.1
. Finally, it is clear by (
6.20
) that for fixed
\lambda>0
we have an exponential decay as
|x|\to\infty
, analogously to the case
\alpha>d
in Proposition
6.14
.

This proves the upper bound in (
2.19
). Finally, we prove the lower bound in
(
2.19
), together with (
2.21
).

Lemma 6.17
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
d\geq 1
. Then for all
\lambda>0
and
m>0
there exists a constant

C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}>0
such that

R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)\geq C_{d,\alpha,\lambda,m}H^{m}_{d,\alpha}(|x|),

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, where
H^{m}_{d,\alpha}
is defined in (
2.20
).

Proof.

Throughout this proof we will use the lower bounds provided in (
6.13
). First we give
an estimate for
|x|>1
. We need to distinguish among two cases. If
\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\geq\frac{1}{m}
, we have

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{m}}e^{-\lambda t}p_{t}^{m}(x)dt\geq c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{m}}te^{-\lambda t}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)}{\lambda^{2}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}-\frac{\lambda}{m}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}\right)\geq\frac{c_{2}}{2}m^{-2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)e^{-\lambda/m}.

If
\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\leq\frac{1}{m}
, we have

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}e^{-\lambda t}p_{t}^{m}(x)dt\geq c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\int_{0}^{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}te^{-\lambda t}dt

\displaystyle\geq c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\int_{0}^{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(1)}te^{-\lambda t}dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)}{\lambda^{2}}\left(1-e^{-\lambda\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(1)}-\lambda\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(1)e^{-\lambda\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(1)}\right),

where we used that
\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}
is strictly increasing. Next let
|x|\leq 1
and consider the following
cases. If
d>\alpha
and
\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\leq\frac{1}{m}
, we get

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}e^{-\lambda t}p_{t}^{m}(x)dt\geq c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\int_{0}^{\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}te^{-\lambda t}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)}{\lambda^{2}}e^{-\lambda\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}\left(e^{\lambda\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}-1-\lambda\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)\right)

\displaystyle\geq c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|))^{2}e^{-\lambda\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)}.

Now observe that for
|x|\leq 1
the second asymptotic relation in (
2.3
) implies that there
exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha}
such that
\kappa_{m}(|x|)\geq C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}
. Hence,
by also using (
6.11
) and the fact that
\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}
is increasing, we obtain

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)\geq C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}e^{-\lambda\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(1)}.

Next, if
d>\alpha
and
\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|)>\frac{1}{m}
, then we have

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{m}}e^{-\lambda t}p_{t}^{m}(x)dt\geq c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{m}}te^{-\lambda t}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)}{\lambda^{2}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}\left(e^{\frac{\lambda}{m}}-1-\frac{\lambda}{m}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)}{m^{2}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}\geq\frac{c_{2}|x|^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{m}(|x|)(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|x|))^{2}}{(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(1))^{2}m^{2}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}\geq C_{d,\alpha}\frac{|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}}{(\Phi_{d,\alpha}^{m}(|1|))^{2}m^{2}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{m}}.

Next assume that
d=\alpha=1
and let
\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|)\leq\frac{1}{m}
. Then we start from

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)\geq I_{2}^{m}+I_{3}^{m}+I_{4}^{m}

and we first search for some lower bounds of
I_{3}^{m}
and
I_{4}^{m}
. Again, we have to distinguish two cases.
If
|x|\leq\frac{1}{m}
, then
I_{3}^{m}=0
and we have

I_{4}^{m}\geq c_{2}m^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-cm|x|^{2}/t}dt.

Now observe that, for fixed
|x|
, the function
f(r)=r^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-cm|x|^{2}r}
is decreasing, hence, for

t>\frac{1}{m}
and
|x|\leq 1
, we have

(6.21)

t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-cm|x|^{2}/t}\geq m^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-cm^{2}|x|^{2}}t^{-1}\geq m^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-cm^{2}}t^{-1}.

Thus

I_{4}^{m}\geq c_{2}e^{-cm^{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-\lambda t}dt.

If
|x|>\frac{1}{m}
, we have

I_{3}^{m}\geq c_{2}m^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-cm|x|}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{|x|}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\lambda t}dt\geq e^{-cm}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{|x|}t^{-1}e^{-\lambda t}dt.

Furthermore

I_{4}^{m}\geq c_{2}m^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt\geq c_{2}e^{-cm^{2}}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-\lambda t}dt,

where we used again (
6.21
), since
t>|x|>\frac{1}{m}
and
|x|\leq 1
. In general, we have

I_{3}^{m}+I_{4}^{m}\geq C_{1,1,m}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-\lambda t}dt

and then

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)\geq I_{2}^{m}+I_{3}^{m}+I_{4}^{m}

\displaystyle\geq C_{1,1,m}\int_{\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|)}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-\lambda t}dt=C_{1,1,m}E_{1}(\lambda\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{1,1,m}e^{-\lambda\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|)}}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{2}{\lambda\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|)}\right),

where we used (
6.2
). Since
|x|\leq 1
, we can use (
6.10
) to finally obtain

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)\geq C_{1,1,m,\lambda}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{|x|}\right).

Now consider the case
d=\alpha=1
and
\Phi^{m}_{1,1}(|x|)>\frac{1}{m}
. Arguing as before, we have

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)

\displaystyle\geq I_{3}^{m}+I_{4}^{m}\geq C_{1,1,m}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-\lambda t}dt\geq C_{1,1,m}\int_{\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|)}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-\lambda t}dt

\displaystyle=C_{1,1,m}E_{1}(\lambda\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|))\geq\frac{C_{1,1,m}e^{-\lambda\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|)}}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{2}{\lambda\Phi_{1,1}^{m}(|x|)}\right)\geq C_{1,1,m,\lambda}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{|x|}\right).

Finally, let
\alpha>d=1
and make the split

R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)\geq I_{3}^{m}+I_{4}^{m}.

If
|x|\leq\frac{1}{m}
, then
I_{3}^{m}=0
and

I_{4}^{m}\geq c_{2}m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt.

Since
f_{\alpha}(r)=r^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}r}
is decreasing, for
t>\frac{1}{m}
and
|x|\leq 1
we get

(6.22)

t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}|x|^{2}/t}dt\geq m^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}

and then

I_{4}^{m}\geq c_{2}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}dt.

If
|x|>\frac{1}{m}
, we have

I_{3}^{m}\geq c_{2}m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|x|}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{m^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}|x|}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\lambda t}dt\geq e^{-cm^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{m^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}|x|}t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}e^{-\lambda t}dt

and

I_{4}^{m}\geq c_{2}e^{-cm^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}\int_{m^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}|x|}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}dt,

by (
6.22
) since
t>m^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}|x|>\frac{1}{m}
. Thus we finally obtain

R_{\lambda}^{m}(x)\geq I_{3}^{m}+I_{4}^{m}\geq C_{1,\alpha,\lambda,m}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}dt=C_{1,\alpha,\lambda,m}\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha},\frac{\lambda}{m}\right).

∎

Remark 6.18
.

We can actually get similar same lower bounds as in Lemma
6.17
in the classical case. Indeed, if
\alpha=2
and
d\geq 3
the asymptotic behaviour in (
2.3
) tells us that

R_{\lambda}(x)\geq C_{d}\left(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}|x|^{-(d-2)}\mathbf{1}_{\{\sqrt{\lambda}|x|\leq 1\}}+\lambda^{\frac{d-5}{4}}|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}\mathbf{1}_{\{\sqrt{\lambda}|x|>1\}}\right),

while for
d=2
we get from (
6.17
)

R_{\lambda}(x)\geq C\left(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\sqrt{\lambda}|x|\leq 1\}}+\lambda^{\frac{d-5}{4}}|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}\mathbf{1}_{\{\sqrt{\lambda}|x|>1\}}\right).

In case
d=1
, we have from (
6.20
)

R_{\lambda}(x)\geq C(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\sqrt{\lambda}|x|\leq 1\}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}|x|}\mathbf{1}_{\{\sqrt{\lambda}|x|>1\}}).

This provides a corresponding lower bound to the classical resolvent estimate in Remark
6.16
.

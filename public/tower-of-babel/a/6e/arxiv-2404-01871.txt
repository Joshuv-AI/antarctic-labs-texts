On the reduction of Linear Parameter-Varying State-Space models
E. Javier Olucha 1, Bogoljub Terzin 1, Amritam Das 1, Roland Tóth 1,2

Abstract
This paper presents an overview and comparative study of the state of the art in state-order reduction (SOR) and scheduling dimension reduction (SDR) for linear parameter-varying (LPV) state-space (SS) models, comparing and benchmarking their capabilities, limitations and performance. The use case chosen for these studies is an interconnected network of nonlinear coupled mass spring damper systems with three different configurations, where some spring coefficients are described by arbitrary user-defined static nonlinear functions. For SOR, the following methods are compared: linear time-invariant (LTI), LPV and LFR-based balanced reductions, moment matching (MM) and parameter-varying oblique projection. For SDR, the following methods are compared: principal component analysis (PCA), trajectory PCA, Kernel PCA and LTI balanced truncation, autoencoders and deep neural network. The comparison reveals the most suitable reduction methods for the different benchmark configurations, from which we provide use case SOR and SDR guidelines that can be used to choose the best reduction method for a given LPV-SS model.

I
Introduction

Recently, the
linear parameter-varying
(LPV) framework has been receiving a significant amount of attention for analysis and controller synthesis of
nonlinear
(NL) and
time-varying
(TV) systems. Beyond the realm of theoretical studies, this framework is widely applicable in diverse industrial context, as shown in
[
1
]
. Although most methods for analysis and controller synthesis of LPV models have been developed, one of the major bottlenecks of the framework is the transformation of a NL or TV model into an LPV one. This operation, commonly referred to as
embedding
, is generally executed executed either by hand or by means of heuristic methods, which depends on the expertise of the user and often can lead to undesirably complex LPV models. While some procedures to automatize the LPV embedding have been recently developed
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
, they can still yield LPV representations with a considerable model order. The complexity of LPV models becomes a problem when used for analysis and synthesis, as the available powerful computational tools rapidly scale in computational and memory load with the scheduling dimension and the model order, easily reaching hardware limitations. Hence, from a practical viewpoint, it is crucial to reduce the complexity of LPV models, where the complexity depends on the number of state variables, the number of scheduling variables, and the dependency of the coefficients in the representation of the scheduling variables. Moreover, the embedding operation typically introduces conservatism caused by the interdependence of the variations expressed by the scheduling variables. This aggregated conservatism affects the achievable performance of the synthesized controllers, therefore another objective of the LPV model reduction is to reduce this conservatism.

To address the complexity reduction problem in the LPV case, several reduction methods have been introduced. On the reduction of the number of state variables,
[
7
]
introduces a balanced reduction for
linear time-invariant
(LTI) models. A proper formulation under the scheduling variation is developed in
[
8
]
, which relies on finding static controllability and observability gramians for the LPV model. In
[
9
]
, a balanced reduction for
linear fractional representation
(LFR) models is presented. From an alternative perspective,
[
10
]
proposes a moment matching method which approximates the input-output behaviour of affine LPV models. At last,
[
11
]
develops an extension of the Petrov-Galerkin approximation with a parameter-varying oblique projection.

On the reduction of the number of scheduling variables, a
principal component analysis
(PCA) approach based on data from the scheduling trajectories is proposed in
[
12
]
. This method is enhanced in
[
5
]
, proposing a reduction based on the variation of the state space matrices. Another PCA approach is detailed in
[
13
]
, where a kernel enables a nonlinear reduction mapping, followed by an optimization step to obtain the reduced LPV state-space matrices. Furthermore, the balanced reduction from
[
7
]
can be used to reduce the scheduling dimension by interchanging the roles of the state and latent variables. Finally, the capabilities of
neural networks
(NN) are explored in
[
14
]
, proposing an
autoencoder
(AE) NN to obtain a nonlinear reduction map, followed by an optimization step similar to
[
13
]
. Based on this concept,
[
15
]
proposes the use of a
deep neural network
(DNN), able to directly determine the reduced LPV state-space matrices together with the scheduling reduction.

While there is an extensive number of procedures to tackle the LPV reduction problem, there is no overview present about the available methods. Furthermore, there are no comparative studies or reliable analysis of the strengths and weaknesses of the available methods, and it is difficult for the user to make the appropriate choices in practice. Therefore, as a contribution of this paper, we provide an overview and a comparison among the existing methods over different benchmarks that cover a broad range of use cases, and we determine the most suitable procedures based on different performance metrics.

The paper is structured as follows, in Section
II
, a mathematical problem definition of the LPV reduction problem is given. An overview of the existing LPV model reduction methods is given in Section
III
. Section
IV
describes the different benchmarks designed to compare the reduction methods. In Section
V
, the performance comparison of the reduction methods is given. Finally, Section
VI
draws the conclusions on the given results.

II
Problem definition

II-A

LPV state-space embedding

Consider a nonlinear system defined by the state-space representation:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{x}(t)&=f(x(t),u(t)),\\
y(t)&=h(x(t),u(t)),\end{split}

(1)

where
x(t):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
is a state associated with (
1
), while
u(t):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}
is the input, and
y(t):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{y}}
is the output of the system. The functions
f:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
and
h:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{y}}
are assumed to be Lipschitz continuous. Embedding of the NL system (
1
) in an LPV representation corresponds to constructing
[
16
]
:

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=A(p(t))x(t)+B(p(t))u(t),

\displaystyle y(t)=C(p(t))x(t)+D(p(t))u(t),

(2a)

\displaystyle\eta(x(t),u(t))=p(t),

(2b)

where
p(t)\in\mathbb{P}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{p}}
is the
scheduling variable
, and
\eta
is the
scheduling map
that describes the internal dependence of scheduling variables on state and input values.

However, different problems arise regarding the complexity of the model. First, the obtained LPV model might be of too high order for the utilization objective (e.g. control design). Additionally, the LPV embedding introduces conservatism by intentionally hiding the dependency of the
p(t)
on
x(t)
and
u(t)
, which should be reduced to get an effective representation of (
1
). At last, regarding the complexity of the scheduling dependency, the embedding of (
2a
) can easily result in polynomial or rational dependency on
p
, which is undesirable in control synthesis. Hence it is desirable to reduce the complexity of the dependence preferably to affine, while preserving the representation capability of (
2a
).

II-B

State order reduction problem

The
state order reduction
(SOR) problem for LPV models consists of finding a
reduced-order
(RO) LPV model approximation of (
2a
) as:

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{x}}(t)=\hat{A}(p(t))\hat{x}(t)+\hat{B}(p(t))u(t)

\displaystyle\hat{y}(t)=\hat{C}(p(t))\hat{x}(t)+\hat{D}(p(t))u(t),

(3a)

\displaystyle\eta(\hat{x}(t),u(t))=\hat{p}(t),

(3b)

where the reduced state vector
\hat{x}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{x}}\ll\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
.

II-C

Scheduling dimension reduction problem

After the embedding procedure, the scheduling map
\eta
can be high dimensional containing many nonlinear functions that are dependent on the same elements of
x
and
u
. Therefore, there may exist a large dependency between the scheduling variables, which contributes to the conservatism of the LPV model. This can dramatically affect the capability of LPV synthesis to find a stabilizing controller with acceptable performance
[
15
]
. The
scheduling dimension reduction
(SDR) problem can be defined as follows:
given an LPV embedding (
2a
) of the NL system (
1
) and a set of nominal scheduling-variable trajectories of (
2a
), denoted by
\mathcal{D}
that corresponds to the typical behavior of (
1
), find an LPV embedding in a form:

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{x}}(t)=\bar{A}(\phi(t))\bar{x}(t)+\bar{B}(\phi(t))u(t),

\displaystyle\bar{y}(t)=\bar{C}(\phi(t))\bar{x}(t)+\bar{D}(\phi(t))u(t),

(4a)

\displaystyle\mu(p(t))=\phi(t),

(4b)

where
\phi(t)\in\Phi\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{\phi}}
is the reduced scheduling variable with
n_{\phi}\ll n_{p}
such that (
4a
) approximates (
2a
) for all trajectories
p\in\mathcal{D}
, and the set
\mathcal{D}
introduced for (
4a
) is less conservative than (
2a
). Here, approximation is considered in the sense that
\phi=\mu\circ\eta
, with
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{n_{p}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{\phi}}
, where

\bar{M}(\phi)=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{A}(\phi)&\bar{B}(\phi)\\
\bar{C}(\phi)&\bar{D}(\phi)\end{bmatrix}\approx M(p).

(5)

If possible, also find the inverse mapping

\mu^{-1}(\phi(t))=\bar{p}(t),\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\bar{p}(t)\approx p(t).

(6)

II-D

Performance metrics

We consider several performance metrics to assess the capabilities of the reduction methods from different viewpoints. First, we consider the
computation time
(CPU) required to execute a reduction algorithm.

Next, let
\mathrm{NRMSE_{(\bullet)}}
denote the
Normalized Root-Mean-Square Error
of the simulated RO-LPV model w.r.t. the FO-LPV model, given as

\displaystyle{\mathrm{NRMSE_{SOR}}}=\frac{\lVert{y}-\hat{y}\rVert}{\lVert y-y_{\mathrm{mean}}\rVert}\cdot 100,

(7a)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{NRMSE_{SDR}}}=\frac{\lVert{y}-\bar{y}\rVert}{\lVert y-y_{\mathrm{mean}}\rVert}\cdot 100,

(7b)

where
\{y(kT_{\mathrm{s}})\}_{k=0}^{N}
,
\{\hat{y}(kT_{\mathrm{s}})\}_{k=0}^{N}
and
\{\bar{y}(kT_{\mathrm{s}})\}_{k=0}^{N}
are the respective output signals from (
2a
), (
3a
), and (
4a
) sampled at time instances
t=kT_{\mathrm{s}}
, and
y_{\text{mean}}
is the mean of of the sampled sequence
y
.

Now, let
\lambda_{{2},(\bullet)}
and
\lambda_{\infty,(\bullet)}
denote the local
\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
norm of the approximation error, given as

\displaystyle\lambda_{{2},\mathrm{SOR}}^{(i)}=\lVert G_{p_{\ast}^{(i)}}-\hat{G}_{p_{\ast}^{(i)}}\rVert_{2},~\lambda_{\infty,\mathrm{SOR}}^{(i)}=\lVert G_{p_{\ast}^{(i)}}-\hat{G}_{p_{\ast}^{(i)}}\rVert_{\infty},

(8)

\displaystyle\lambda_{{2},\mathrm{SDR}}^{(i)}=\lVert G_{p_{\ast}^{(i)}}-\bar{G}_{p_{\ast}^{(i)}}\rVert_{2},~\lambda_{\infty,\mathrm{SDR}}^{(i)}=\lVert G_{p_{\ast}^{(i)}}-\bar{G}_{p_{\ast}^{(i)}}\rVert_{\infty},

(9)

where
G_{p_{\ast}^{(i)}}
,
\hat{G}_{p_{\ast}^{(i)}}
and
\bar{G}_{p_{\ast}^{(i)}}
are the respective local frequency response functions of (
2a
), (
3a
) and (
4a
) for a frozen (constant)
p_{*}^{(i)}
. We denote
\mathcal{P}
(different from
\mathbb{P}
) as a grid that contains
n_{g}
scheduling values
\{p_{*}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{n_{g}}\in\mathbb{P}
.

III
Overview of the reduction techniques

III-A

Preliminaries

Consider the LPV embedding (
2a
) of the NL system (
1
) with the scheduling map (
2b
) is given. An LPV-LFR form is obtained by introducing the latent variables
w_{\Delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{w}}
and
z_{\Delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{z}}
, and disconnecting the LTI part of the model from the scheduling variables:

\begin{bmatrix}\dot{x}(t)\\
z_{\Delta}(t)\\
y(t)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A&B_{d}&B_{u}\\
C_{d}&D_{dd}&D_{du}\\
C_{y}&D_{yd}&D_{yu}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x(t)\\
w_{\Delta}(t)\\
u(t)\end{bmatrix},

(10a)

together with the feedback path represented by:

w_{\Delta}(t)=\Delta(p(t))z_{\Delta}(t),

(10b)

where
\Delta:\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{w}\times n_{z}}
is the matrix function containing repeated instances of the scheduling variables.

III-B

State-order reduction techniques

III-B
1
LTI balanced reduction (LTIBR)

One of the earliest SOR methods developed in
[
7
]
for stable LTI models, is developed on the basis of considering only those states that are easy to reach (low control cost) and easy to observe (high observation energy), quantified by the controllability and observability gramians. For LPV models, it is applied by reducing (
10a
), in which the latent channels
z_{\Delta}
and
w_{\Delta}
are seen as additional output and input signals respectively. The underlying LTI model is transformed to an equivalent SS model using a balancing transformation, and thenceforth truncated to an approximated model that contains only the chosen number of the states.

III-B
2
LPV balred (LPVBR)

The first proper formulation of balanced reduction for LPV models where the scheduling dependence was taken into account has been developed in
[
8
]
for quadratically stable LPV models. This method relies on obtaining static (independent of
p
) controllability and observability gramians
P
and
Q
, by solving the following
linear matrix inequalities
(LMIs) constrained optimization problem on a gridded
p\in\mathcal{P}
:

\displaystyle\min_{P,Q}\quad

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}(PQ)

(11)

\displaystyle\vskip-3.0pt\mathrm{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle A(p)P+PA^{\top}(p)+B(p)B^{\top}(p)\prec 0,

\displaystyle A^{\top}(p)Q+QA(p)+C^{\top}(p)C(p)\prec 0,

\displaystyle\forall p\in\mathcal{P}.

The gramians are used to find a balancing transformation which is applied to (
2a
), and truncated to obtain (
3a
) with the chosen number of states.

III-B
3
Moment matching (MM)

The moment matching method is formulated for LPV-SS models with affine static dependence on the scheduling variable in
[
10
]
. This method preserves the first
N
impulse response coefficients, and it is computed by finding the
N
-partial reachability and
N
-partial observability spaces, and transforming the matrix functions in (
2a
) accordingly. This method can also be used to obtain a minimal realization of LPV-SS models.

III-B
4
Parameter-Varying Oblique Projections (PVOP)

In the oblique projections method
[
11
]
, the ideas of balanced reduction and generalized controllability and observability gramians are used and extended to find a reduced order model. Since static gramians restrict the search space, this alternative employs local gramian approximations on a gridded space of scheduling variables, which is later interpolated.

III-B
5
LFR-based balred (LFRBR)

A model reduction method for LPV-LFR and uncertain models is developed in
[
9
]
, and relies on reducing (
10a
) extended with the latent variables. The extended LTI model, defined as

\begin{bmatrix}y_{1}\\
y\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{A}&\tilde{B}\\
\tilde{C}&\tilde{D}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}\\
u\end{bmatrix},

(12)

where

\tilde{A}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{0}&B_{d}\\
C_{z}&D_{dd}\end{bmatrix},\tilde{B}=\begin{bmatrix}B_{u}\\
D_{du}\end{bmatrix},\tilde{C}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{y}&D_{yd}\end{bmatrix}
,
\tilde{D}=D_{yu}
,
y_{1}=\operatorname{col}(\dot{x},z_{\Delta})
, and
u_{1}=\operatorname{col}(x,w_{\Delta})
, is required to be controllable and observable. Structured gramians for (
12
) are obtained, used to find a balancing transformation, that can be used to truncate the dimensions of
\tilde{A}
. The method can be extended to handle unstable models by using a right-coprime factorization of (
2a
), see
[
9
]
.

III-C

Scheduling reduction techniques

First, we define relevant notation related to the SDR methods. The nominal scheduling trajectories are obtained by simulation, sampled at time instances
t=kT_{s},\,k=0,\dots,N-1
, and collected in

\Gamma=\mathcal{N}\left(\begin{bmatrix}p(0)&\ldots&p((N-1)T_{s})\end{bmatrix}\right)~\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{p}\times N}

(13)

and
\mathcal{D}=\{p(kT_{s})\}_{k=0}^{N-1}
, where
\mathcal{N}
is an affine centering and normalization function. Moreover, the matrices of (
2a
) can be joined as:

L(p)=\begin{bmatrix}A(p(t))&B(p(t))\\
C(p(t))&D(p(t))\end{bmatrix},

(14)

and the data can be collected as the vectorized values of matrix
L(p)
across the nominal trajectory

\Gamma_{m}=\mathcal{N}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\overrightarrow{L}(p(0))&\ldots&\overrightarrow{L}(p((N-1)T_{s})\end{bmatrix}\right).

(15)

III-C
1
Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

The standard PCA approach, proposed in
[
12
]
, is one of the earliest SDR methods, and it applies
singular value decomposition
(SVD) on
\Gamma
(
13
) to find the principal components of the scheduling trajectories and projects them to a subspace of lower order. Using the
economic SVD
:

\displaystyle\Gamma=\begin{bmatrix}U_{s}&U_{r}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\Sigma_{s}&0\\
0&\Sigma_{r}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}V_{s}^{\top}\\
V_{r}^{\top}\end{bmatrix},

(16)

where
U_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{p}\times n_{\phi}},V_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n_{\phi}}
are unitary and
\Sigma_{s}\succ 0\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\phi}\times n_{\phi}}
, containing the highest
n_{\phi}
singular values of
\Gamma
. The reduced scheduling variable (
4b
) is given by
\phi(t)=U_{s}^{\top}p(t)
, and the inverse mapping (
6
) is obtained by
\bar{p}(t)=U_{s}\phi(t)
. The matrices of (
4a
) are then constructed using the same projection matrix
U_{s}
.

III-C
2
Trajectory PCA (TPCA)

This approach, developed in
[
5
]
, finds a reduced order LPV-SS model of an affine LPV-SS model with minimal overbounding. This method applies a PCA to the state-space matrices over the course of
\Gamma_{m}
(
15
). The advantage over the standard PCA method is that it captures not only the scheduling variables, but also all the variations of the state-space matrices.

III-C
3
Kernel PCA (KPCA)

The KPCA method
[
13
]
extends the PCA method by allowing a nonlinear mapping of the scheduling map. In the KPCA method, the data is mapped with a kernel function to a higher dimensional feature space, on which a PCA is applied. Choosing a kernel function
k:\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
results in the gram matrix
K_{ij}=(\Theta(p(i))^{\top}\Theta(p(j)))=k(p(i),p(j)
,
where
K\in R^{N\times N}
and
\Theta
denotes the nonlinear mapping. However, this method requires an additional optimization step to obtain the state-space matrices in (
4a
).

III-C
4
SDR balred (SDRBR)

The LTI balancing truncation method in §
III-B1
can be used for SDR by interchanging the roles of the states and scheduling variables of (
10a
),-(
10b
). The alternate representation
connects the time-shift operator between the latent variables
w_{\Delta}
and
z_{\Delta}
, resulting in the following LTI-SS model:

\displaystyle\dot{z}

\displaystyle=D_{dd}\hat{z}+\begin{bmatrix}C_{d}&D_{du}\end{bmatrix}\tilde{u},

(17a)

\displaystyle\tilde{y}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}B_{d}\\
D_{yd}\end{bmatrix}\hat{z}+\begin{bmatrix}A&B_{u}\\
C_{y}&D_{yu}\end{bmatrix}\tilde{u},

(17b)

with
\tilde{u}=\operatorname{col}(x,u)
, and
\tilde{y}=\operatorname{col}(\dot{x},y)
. The balanced truncation is performed on (
17
) to reduce the dimension of the state
z
, therefore reducing the
\Delta
block. In LPV-LFR forms,
\Delta
contains repeated instances of the scheduling variables in the diagonal, hence its reduction means reducing the multiplicity of the scheduling variables and the scheduling dimension.

III-C
5
Autoencoders (AE)

The AE method
[
14
]
makes use of an AE NN, which is trained with (
13
) to construct the nonlinear mappings (
4b
) and (
6
). This is an advantage over KPCA in §
III-C3
since both
\mu
and
\mu^{-1}
are co-synthesized. The AE NN consists of an encoder layer mapping
p
to
\phi
and a decoder layer mapping
\phi
to
\bar{p}
. However, as in the KPCA method, a separate optimization procedure is required to obtain the reduced order matrices in (
4a
).

III-C
6
Deep neural network (DNN)

The DNN approach
[
15
]
provides a method that allows a better approximation of more complex scheduling maps. This method, like the AE method, uses an NN to construct the mapping
\phi=\mu(p)
, with the benefit that the output of the network is a vectorized form
\hat{M}(p)
(
5
) so that the reduced order matrices are directly constructed, avoiding the optimization procedure.

subject to the restrictions
\mathbb{U}_{\mathrm{out}}\cap(\mathbb{U}\setminus\mathbb{U}_{\mathrm{in}})\neq\emptyset
and
\mathbb{U}_{\mathrm{out}}\cap(\mathbb{U}\setminus\mathbb{U}_{\mathrm{train}})\neq\emptyset

VI
Conclusions

In this paper the performance of different SOR and SDR methods are analysed using three affine LPV-SS benchmarks. Concerning the SOR, it has been found that
Moment Matching
is the only method able to show extrapolation capabilities on all the three benchmarks, and the only one capable of reducing unstable and/or non-minimal SS representations. However, other reduction methods are able to obtain a higher performance depending of specific model properties. Regarding the SDR, only
SDR balred
is able to show extrapolation capabilities for the three benchmarks, but the performance of
Trajectory PCA
or
DNN
methods is better. As the analysis results show that the suitability of the reduction methods depends on the model to be reduced, some reduction guidelines are presented based on the model properties and reduction objectives. In addition, the presented guidelines can serve as a strategy for an iterative state order and scheduling dimension reduction for large order systems. For future research, the possibility of developing a joint state order and scheduling dimension reduction needs to be investigated.

Kröger’s type upper bounds for Dirichlet eigenvalues of the fractional Laplacian


Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to provide an upper bound for the increasing sequence of eigenvalues { λ s , i ​ ( Ω ) } i \{\lambda_{s,i}(\Omega)\}_{i} to the Dirichlet problem ( − Δ ) s ​ u = λ ​ u in ​ Ω , u = 0 in ℝ N ∖ Ω , (-\Delta)^{s}u=\lambda u\ \ {\rm in}\ \,\Omega,\quad\quad u=0\ \ {\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega, where ( − Δ ) s (-\Delta)^{s} is the fractional Laplacian operator defined in the principle value sense, Ω \Omega is a bounded domain in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} with N ≥ 1 N\geq 1 . We were able to establish an upper bound of the sum of the eigenvalues. This important result is obtained by a subtle computation of Rayleight quotient for specific functions. Our method is inspired with Kröger’s one in [ 22 ] .

Introduction and main results

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the integer
N\geq 1
. The main purpose of this paper is to study
the upper bounds of eigenvalues of the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(-\Delta)^{s}u=\lambda u&{\rm in}\quad\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{(-\Delta)^{s}}u=0&{\rm{in}}\ \quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the fractional laplacian defined in the following sense (principle value):

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=c_{N,s}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy

(1.2)

with

c_{N,s}=2^{2s}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}s\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1-s)}
and

\Gamma
being the Gamma function, see e.g.
[
32
]
.
Recall that, for
s\in(0,1)
, the fractional Laplacian of a function
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
can also be defined by:

\mathcal{F}((-\Delta)^{s}u)(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{u}(\xi)\qquad\text{for all $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

Here and in the sequel both
\mathcal{F}
and
\widehat{\cdot}
denote the Fourier transform.

During the last years, there has been a renewed and increasing
interest in the study of linear and nonlinear integral operators,
especially for the fractional Laplacian.
This was motivated by numerous applications, which necessitated a significant progress in the theory of linear and nonlinear
partial differential equations, see basic properties
[
29
]
, regularities
[
2
,
31
]
,
Liouville property
[
3
]
, general nonlocal operator
[
6
]
,
fractional Pohozaev identity
[
32
]
, singularities
[
4
,
5
]
, uniqueness
[
16
]
, fractional variational setting
[
11
,
17
,
20
,
33
]
and the references therein.

To analyze the fractional Dirichlet eigenvalues, we denote
{\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
the space of all measurable functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
with
u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy<+\infty.

We shall see that
{\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space with inner product

\mathcal{E}_{s}(u,w)=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y)){(w(x)-w(y))}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy

and the induced norm
\|u\|_{s}=\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{s}(u,u)}
. A function
u\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
will then be called an eigenfunction of (
1.1
) corresponding to the eigenvalue

\lambda
if

{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(u,w)=\lambda\int_{\Omega}uw\,dx\qquad\text{for all $w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)$.}

Here if necessary, the above inner product is replaced by
{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(u,w)=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\bar{w}\,dx
for complex functions
u,\,w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
. It is known that problem (
1.1
) admits a sequence of real eigenvalues

0<\lambda_{s,1}(\Omega)<\lambda_{s,2}(\Omega)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{s,i}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{s,i+1}(\Omega)\leq\cdots

and corresponding eigenfunctions
\phi_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds:

(a)

\lambda_{s,i}(\Omega)=\min\{{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(u,u):\,u\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0,i}(\Omega),\,\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1\}
, where

{\mathbb{H}}_{1}(\Omega):={\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbb{H}}_{0,i}(\Omega):=\{u\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)\>:\>\text{$\int_{\Omega}u\phi_{i}\,dx=0$ for $i=1,\dots,i-1$}\}\quad\text{for $i>1$;}

(b)

\{\phi_{i}\>:\>i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
;

(c)

\phi_{1}
is strictly positive in
\Omega
. Moreover,
\lambda_{s,1}(\Omega)
is simple, i.e., if
u\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies (
1.1
) in weak sense with
\lambda=\lambda_{s,1}(\Omega)
, then
u=t\phi_{1}
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(d)

\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{s,i}(\Omega)=+\infty
.

In the classical setting
(s=1)
, the asymptotic behavior of eigenvalues attracted the attention of mathematicians since 1912. Indeed, in [30], he was able to show that the
k
-th eigenvalue
\mu_{k}(\Omega)
of Dirichlet problem with
s=1
, i.e. the Laplacian, has the asymptotic behavior

\lambda_{1,k}(\Omega)\sim C_{N}(k|\Omega|)^{\frac{2}{N}}
as
k\to+\infty
, where
C_{N}=(2\pi)^{2}|B_{1}|^{-\frac{2}{N}}
. Later, Pólya
[
30
]
(in 1960) proved that

\lambda_{1,k}(\Omega)\geq C(k/|\Omega|)^{\frac{2}{N}}

(1.3)

holds for
C=C_{N}
and any tiling Domain
D
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, (his proof also works in dimension
N\geq 3
).
He also conjectured that (
1.3
) holds with
C=C_{N}
for any bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Lieb
[
25
]
proved (
1.3
) with a positive constant
C
for general bounded domain and Li-Yau
[
24
]
improved
the constant
C=\frac{N}{N+2}C_{N}
. With this famous constant, (
1.3
) is now called Brezis-Lieb-Yau inequality. It has played a crucial role in the study of linear elliptic operators
[
10
,
15
,
25
,
22
,
28
,
9
]
. The upper bounds of Dirichlet eigenvalues are derived by Kröger in
[
22
]
by calculating the Rayleigh quotient by using a sequence of functions approaching the characterized function of
\Omega
. We also refer to Yang’s upper bounds of the Dirichlet’s eigenvalues in
[
9
,
8
]
in the following way:

\lambda_{1,k}(\Omega)\leq c(N,k)k^{\frac{2}{N}}\lambda_{1,1}(\Omega)\quad\text{ for some $c(N,k)>0$}.

For the fractional laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
, the Wely’s estimate was shown in
[
12
]
and the lower bounds of the Dirichlet’s eigenvalues were formulated in
[
19
,
35
]
in the following

\lambda_{s,k}(\Omega)\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2s}N}{N+2s}(|B_{1}||\Omega|)^{-\frac{2s}{N}}k^{\frac{2s}{N}}.

(1.4)

In particular the lower bounds for Klein-Gordon operators
\sqrt{-\Delta+m^{2}}
are obtained in
[
19
]
.
For the upper bounds of fractional Dirichlet eigenvalues, Yang type inequality has been obtained in
[
7
]
:

\lambda_{s,k}(\Omega)\leq c(N,k)k^{\frac{2s}{N}}\lambda_{s,1}(\Omega)\quad\text{ for some $c(N,k)>0$ and some $s\in(0,1)$}.

However, this type of inequality heavily depends on a very precise estimates of
\lambda_{s,1}(\Omega)
. For a more detailed account about that, the reader can refer to
[
17
]
.

Despite the importance and the numerous relevant applications of the establishment of an upper bound for the eigenvalues for (
1.1
),
the literature remained silent until very recently the survey
[
17
]
.
The nonlocal aspect makes this problem very complicated.
Additionally, Caffarelli and Silvestre extension does not help in this case.
Therefore, all techniques developed to address the bounds of eigenvalues for (
1.1
) when
s=1
, do not extend to the fractional setting.

The main objective of this work is to provide an upper bound for the sum of eigenvalues of (
1.1
). The main result of this paper is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that for some
R>0
,

B_{R}\subset\Omega\subset B_{2R}

and there exists
c_{0}>0
such that

|\Omega_{t}|\leq c_{0}R^{N-1},

(1.5)

where
\Omega_{t}=\{x\in\Omega:\,\rho(x)={\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)=t\}.

Let
\{\lambda_{s,i}(\Omega)\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the increasing sequence of eigenvalues of problem (
1.1
). Then there exists
c_{1}>0
independent of
k
such that for
k\in\mathbb{N}

\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{s,i}(\Omega)\leq\frac{(2\pi)^{2s}N}{N+2s}(|B_{1}||\Omega|)^{-\frac{2s}{N}}k^{1+\frac{2s}{N}}+c_{1}k^{1+\frac{s}{N}}.

Compared with the lower bound (
1.4
), our upper bound in Theorem
1.1
provides an sharp main term

\frac{(2\pi)^{2s}N}{N+2s}(|B_{1}||\Omega|)^{-\frac{2s}{N}}k^{1+\frac{2s}{N}}
.
Our proof is inspired by the method of Kröger in
[
22
]
. The major difficulty is to do estimates for

(-\Delta)^{s}(w_{\sigma}(x)e^{ix\cdot z})
, where

w_{\sigma}(x)=\eta_{0}(\sigma^{-1}\rho(x)),\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Here
\eta_{0}
is a
C^{2}
increasing function such that

\eta_{0}(t)=1\ \ {\rm if}\ \,t\geq 1,\qquad\eta_{0}(t)=0\ \ {\rm if}\ \,t\leq 0.

Indeed, we have the following decomposition:

(-\Delta)^{s}(w_{\sigma}(x)e^{ix\cdot z})=w_{\sigma}(x)(-\Delta)^{s}e^{ix\cdot z}+e^{ix\cdot z}(-\Delta)^{s}w_{\sigma}(x)+{\mathcal{L}}^{s}_{z}w_{\sigma},

where

{\mathcal{L}}^{s}_{z}w_{\sigma}=c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(w_{\sigma}(x)-w_{\sigma}(\tilde{x}))(e^{i\tilde{x}\cdot z}-e^{ix\cdot z})}{|x-\tilde{x}|^{N+2s}}d\tilde{x}.

The dominating term is
w(x)(-\Delta)^{s}e^{ix\cdot z}
. For the latter, we obtain the following identity:

(-\Delta)^{s}e^{ix\cdot z}=|z|^{2s}e^{ix\cdot z},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

for any given
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Together with the lower bound of the sum of eigenvalues, we can obtain the limit as following

Corollary 1.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1

we have that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}k^{-1-\frac{2s}{N}}\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{s,i}(\Omega)=\frac{(2\pi)^{2s}N}{N+2s}(|B_{1}||\Omega|)^{-\frac{2s}{N}}.

Throughout this paper,
e
denotes the Euler number,
\rho(x)={\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
B_{r}(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an open ball of radius
r
centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and we put
B_{r}:=B_{r}(0)
for
r>0
. The rest of this paper is organized as following: Section 2 is devoted
to the normalization of the constant
d_{{}_{N}}(s)
.
In Section 3, we provide the proofs of our results.

2
Preliminary

For an integer
m\geq 2
, we denote

E_{m}(m+2s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m-2}}{(1+t^{2})^{\frac{m+2s}{2}}}dt,

then we have that

\displaystyle E_{2}(2+2s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+t^{2})^{1+s}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{(1+t)^{1+s}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}B(\frac{1}{2},s+\frac{1}{2})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(1+s)},

E_{3}(3+2s)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+t^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}+s}}d(1+t^{2})=\frac{1}{1+2s}

and for
m\geq 4

E_{m}(m+2s)=\frac{m-3}{m+2s-2}E_{m-2}(m+2s-2),

where
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Beta function.
Direct computation implies that for
n\geq 4
even,

\displaystyle E_{m}(m+2s)

\displaystyle=E_{2}(2+2s)\cdot\frac{1}{2+2s}\cdots\frac{m-3}{m-2+2s}

(2.1)

and for
n\geq 5
odd

\displaystyle E_{m}(m+2s)

\displaystyle=E_{3}(3+2s)\cdot\frac{2}{3+2s}\cdots\frac{m-3}{m-2+2s}=\frac{1}{1+2s}\cdot\frac{2}{3+2s}\cdots\frac{m-3}{m-2+2s}.

(2.2)

We remark that for
n\geq 4
and
s=\frac{1}{2}
,
we have that

E_{m}(m+1)=E_{2}(3)\cdot\frac{1}{3}\cdots\frac{m-3}{m-1}=\frac{1}{m-1},\quad m{\rm\ is\ even}

and

E_{m}(m+1)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{4}\cdots\frac{m-3}{m-1}=\frac{1}{m-1},\quad m{\rm\ is\ odd}.

For
N\geq 2
, denote

\displaystyle b_{{}_{N}}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{N-1}{2})}E_{N}(N+2s)

(2.3)

and
b_{{}_{N}}(s)\equiv 1
for
N=1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
b_{{}_{N}}(s)
be defined in (
2.3
), then for
N\geq 2

b_{{}_{N}}(s)=1.

Proof.

It is obvious for
N=2,3
.

When
N\geq 5
is odd, in view of (
2.2
), we obtain that

\displaystyle b_{{}_{N}}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\frac{N+2s}{2}-1}{\frac{N-1}{2}-1}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2}-1)}{\Gamma(\frac{N-1}{2}-1)}E_{N-2}(N+2s-2)\frac{N-3}{N+2s-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2}-1)}{\Gamma(\frac{N-1}{2}-1)}E_{N-2}(N+2s-2)

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{3+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1)}E_{3}(3+2s)

\displaystyle=1,

where we used
E_{3}(3+2s)=\frac{1}{1+2s}
.

When
N\geq 4
is oven, in view of (
2.1
), we obtain that

\displaystyle b_{{}_{N}}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\frac{N+2s}{2}-1}{\frac{N-1}{2}-1}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2}-1)}{\Gamma(\frac{N-1}{2}-1)}E_{N-2}(N+2s-2)\frac{N-3}{N+2s-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2}-1)}{\Gamma(\frac{N-1}{2}-1)}E_{N-2}(N+2s-2)

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(1+s)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}E_{2}(2+2s)

\displaystyle=1.

This completes the proof.

\Box

3
Upper bounds

The following lemma plays an important role in our proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
, denote

v_{z}(x)=e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

then

(-\Delta)^{s}v_{z}(x)=|z|^{2s}v_{z}(x),\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(3.1)

Proof.
Without loss of generality, we only need to
calculate (
3.1
) with
z=te_{1}
, where
t>0
and
e_{1}=(1,0,\cdots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For this,
we write

v_{t}(x)=v_{t}(x_{1})=e^{{\rm i}tx_{1}},\quad x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}.

Note that for
N\geq 2

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}v_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{N,s}{\rm p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{v_{t}(x)-v_{t}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{N,s}}{c_{1,s}}\Big(c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v_{t}(x_{1})-v_{t}(y_{1})}{|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{1+2s}}dy_{1}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{1}{(|y^{\prime}|^{2}+1)^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1+2s}{2})\Gamma(\frac{N-1}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{N-2}}{(t^{2}+1)^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}dt\,(-\Delta)^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}v_{t}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}v_{t}(x_{1}),

where we used lemma
2.1
,

\omega_{{}_{N-2}}=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{N-1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{N-1}{2})}

and

\frac{c_{N,s}}{c_{1,s}}=\pi^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1+2s}{2})}.

Now we claim that

(-\Delta)^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}v_{t}(x_{1})=t^{2s}v_{t}(x_{1}),\quad\forall\,x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.2)

Indeed, observe that
-\Delta v_{t}=t^{2}v_{t}
in
\mathbb{R}
and then

(|\xi_{1}|^{2}-t^{2})\hat{v_{t}}={\mathcal{F}}\left(-\Delta v_{t}-t^{2}v_{t}\right)=0,

which implies that

{\rm supp}(\hat{v_{t}})\subset\{\pm t\}.

Thus, we have that

{\mathcal{F}}\left((-\Delta)^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}v_{t}-t^{2s}v_{t}\right)(\xi_{1})=(|\xi_{1}|^{2s}-t^{2s})\hat{v_{t}}(\xi_{1})=0

and

(-\Delta)^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}v_{t}=t^{2s}v_{t}\quad{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}.

Now we can conclude that

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}v_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=(-\Delta)^{s}_{\mathbb{R}}v_{t}=t^{2s}v_{t}(x),\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

This completes the proof.
\Box

Let
\eta_{0}
be a
C^{2}
increasing function such that
\|\eta_{0}\|_{C^{2}},\,\|\eta_{0}\|_{C^{2}}\leq 2

\eta_{0}(t)=1\ \ {\rm if}\ \,t\geq 1,\qquad\eta_{0}(t)=0\ \ {\rm if}\ \,t\leq 0.

For
\sigma>0
, denote

w_{\sigma}(x)=\eta_{0}(\sigma^{-1}\rho(x)),\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(3.3)

Observe that
w_{\sigma}\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s}(\Omega)
and

w_{\sigma}\to 1\quad{\rm in}\ \,\Omega\ \ {\rm as}\ \ \sigma\to 0^{+}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
B_{R}\subset\Omega\subset B_{2R}
,
then

|(-\Delta)^{s}w_{\sigma}(x)|\leq 2c_{N,s}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}\sigma^{-2s}\quad{\rm for}\ \,x\in\Omega.

Proof.

For
x\in\Omega
, we have that

\displaystyle|2w_{\sigma}(x)-w_{\sigma}(x+\zeta)-w_{\sigma}(x-\zeta)|

\displaystyle\leq\min\{2,\|w_{\sigma}\|_{C^{2}}|\zeta|^{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq\min\{2,\sigma^{-2}\|\eta_{0}\|_{C^{2}}|\zeta|^{2}\}

We use an equivalent definition

\displaystyle\frac{2}{c_{N,s}}|(-\Delta)^{s}w_{\sigma}(x)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{2w_{\sigma}(x)-w_{\sigma}(x+\zeta)-w_{\sigma}(x-\zeta)}{|\zeta|^{N+2s}}d\zeta\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\min\{2,\sigma^{-2}\|\eta_{0}\|_{C^{2}}|\zeta|^{2}\}}{|\zeta|^{N+2s}}d\zeta

\displaystyle\leq 2\sigma^{-2}\int_{B_{\sigma}}\frac{|\zeta|^{2}}{|\zeta|^{N+2s}}d\zeta+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\sigma}}\frac{2}{|\zeta|^{N+2s}}d\zeta

\displaystyle\leq 4\omega_{{}_{N-1}}\sigma^{-2s},

where
\|\eta_{0}\|_{C^{2}}\leq 2
.
This completes the proof.
\Box

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
B_{R}\subset\Omega\subset B_{2R}
and

{\mathcal{L}}^{s}_{z}w_{\sigma}(x)=c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(w_{\sigma}(x)-w_{\sigma}(\tilde{x}))(e^{i\tilde{x}\cdot z}-e^{ix\cdot z})}{|x-\tilde{x}|^{N+2s}}d\tilde{x}.

Then we have that for
x\in\Omega

(i)
for
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{N,s}}|{\mathcal{L}}^{s}_{z}w_{\sigma}(x)|\leq\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{1-s}\sigma^{-1}|z|^{2s-1}+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{2s-1}|z|^{2s-1}+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{2s}R^{-2s};

(ii)
for
s=\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{N,s}}|{\mathcal{L}}^{s}_{z}w_{\sigma}(x)|\leq\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{1-s}\sigma^{-1}+\omega_{{}_{N-1}}(\log|z|+\log(4R))+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{2s}R^{-1};

(iii)
for
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{N,s}}|{\mathcal{L}}^{s}_{z}w_{\sigma}(x)|\leq\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{1-s}\sigma^{-1}|z|^{2s-1}+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{1-2s}(4R)^{1-2s}+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{2s}R^{-2s}.

Proof.

Note that

|e^{i\tilde{x}\cdot z}-e^{ix\cdot z}|\leq\min\{2,|z||\tilde{x}-x|\}

and

|w_{\sigma}(x)-w_{\sigma}(\tilde{x})|\leq\frac{2}{\sigma}|x-\tilde{x}|,\quad|\tilde{x}|<3R.

For
x\in\Omega
and
|z|>1
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{N,s}}|{\mathcal{L}}^{s}_{z}w_{\sigma}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|w_{\sigma}(x)-w_{\sigma}(\tilde{x})|\,|e^{i\tilde{x}\cdot z}-e^{ix\cdot z}|}{|x-\tilde{x}|^{N+2s}}d\tilde{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{4R}}\frac{2\sigma^{-1}|x-\tilde{x}|\,\min\{2,|z||\tilde{x}-x|\}}{|x-\tilde{x}|^{N+2s}}d\tilde{x}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{4R}}\frac{2}{|x-\tilde{x}|^{N+2s}}d\tilde{x}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sigma^{-1}|z|\int_{B_{\frac{1}{|z|}}(x)}|x-\tilde{x}|^{2-N-2s}d\tilde{x}+4\sigma^{-1}\int_{B_{4R}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{|z|}}(x)}|x-\tilde{x}|^{1-N-2s}d\tilde{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{2}{|\tilde{x}|^{N+2s}}d\tilde{x},

where

2\sigma^{-1}|z|\int_{B_{\frac{1}{|z|}}(x)}|x-\tilde{x}|^{2-N-2s}d\tilde{x}\leq\frac{\sigma^{-1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{1-s}|z|^{2s-1},

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{2}{|\tilde{x}|^{N+2s}}d\tilde{x}\leq\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{2s}R^{-2s}

and

4\sigma^{-1}\int_{B_{4R}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{|z|}}(x)}|x-\tilde{x}|^{1-N-2s}d\tilde{x}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\sigma^{-1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{2s-1}|z|^{2s-1}&{\rm if}\ \,s\in(\frac{1}{2},1),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\sigma^{-1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}(\log|z|+\log(4R))&{\rm{if}}\ \,s=\frac{1}{2},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\frac{\sigma^{-1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{1-2s}(4R)^{1-2s}&{\rm{if}}\ \,s\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).\end{array}\right.

This completes the proof.
\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Denote

\Phi_{k}(x,y)=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(y)

and

\hat{\Phi}_{k}(z,y)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{k}(x,y)e^{ix\cdot z}dx,

here
\hat{\Phi}_{k}
is the Fourier transform with respect to
x
.

Denote

v_{\sigma}(x,z)=w_{\sigma}(x)e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z}.

Note that the projection of
v_{\sigma}
onto the subspace of
L^{2}(\Omega)
spanned by
\phi_{i}
can be written in terms of the Fourier transform
\eta_{\sigma}\Phi_{k}
of
w_{\sigma}
with respect to the
x
-variable:

\int_{\Omega}v_{\sigma}(x,z)\Phi_{k}(x,y)dx=(2\pi)^{N/2}{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(w_{\sigma}\Phi_{k})(z,y).

Denote

v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)=v_{\sigma}(z,y)-(2\pi)^{N/2}{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(w_{\sigma}\Phi_{k})(z,y)

and the Rayleigh-Ritz formula shows that

\lambda_{s,k+1}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)|^{2}dy\leq\int_{\Omega}|v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)(-\Delta)^{\alpha}_{y}v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)|dy

for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\sigma>0
.
Thus, we can conclude that

\lambda_{s,k+1}(\Omega)\leq\inf_{\sigma>0}\frac{\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}|v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)(-\Delta)^{\alpha}_{y}v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)|dydz}{\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}|v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)|^{2}dydz}.

An elementary calulation yields that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}|v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)|^{2}dydz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}|v_{\sigma}(z,y)|^{2}dydz-(2\pi)^{N}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}\sum^{k}_{i=1}|{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(w_{\sigma}\phi_{i})(z)|^{2}\phi_{i}(y)^{2}dydz

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}}{N}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy-(2\pi)^{N}\sum^{k}_{i=1}\int_{B_{r}}|{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(w_{\sigma}\phi_{i})(z)|^{2}dz,

where
r\geq\sigma^{-1}
.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}|v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)(-\Delta)^{\alpha}_{y}v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)|

\displaystyle dydz=\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}|v_{\sigma}(z,y)(-\Delta)^{\alpha}_{y}v_{\sigma}(z,y)|dydz

\displaystyle-(2\pi)^{N}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}\sum^{k}_{i=1}|{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(w_{\sigma}\Phi_{k})(z,y)(-\Delta)^{\alpha}_{y}{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(w_{\sigma}\Phi_{k})(z,y)|dydz,

where

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}\sum^{k}_{i=1}|{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(w_{\sigma}\Phi_{k})(z,y)(-\Delta)^{\alpha}_{y}{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(w_{\sigma}\Phi_{k})(z,y)|dydz=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(\Omega)\int_{B_{r}}|{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(w_{\sigma}\phi_{i})(z)|^{2}dz

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}|v_{\sigma}(z,y)(-\Delta)^{\alpha}_{y}v_{\sigma}(z,y)|dydz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}|w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)|(-\Delta)^{\alpha}_{y}e^{{\rm i}y\dot{z}}|dydz

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}|w_{\sigma}(y)(-\Delta)^{\alpha}_{y}w_{\sigma}(y)|dydz+\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\Omega}|{\mathcal{L}}^{s}_{z}w_{\sigma}(y)|dydz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N+2s}r^{N+2s}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}}{N}\frac{2c_{N,s}}{\sigma^{2s}}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}(y)dy

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N+2s-1}}{\sigma(N+2s-1)(1-s)}|\Omega|+\frac{1}{\sigma}\phi_{s}(r,R)|\Omega|+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}}{2\sigma sN}R^{-2s}|\Omega|

\displaystyle:=\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N+2s}r^{N+2s}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy+\Psi_{s}(r,\sigma)

with

\phi_{s}(r,R)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N+2s-1}}{(2s-1)(N+2s-1)}&{\rm if}\quad s\in(\frac{1}{2},1),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}(\log r+\log(4R))}{N}&{\rm{if}}\ \,s=\frac{1}{2},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}}{N(1-2s)}(4R)^{1-2s}&{\rm{if}}\ \,s\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

Observe that Parseval’s identity implies that

\int_{B_{r}}|{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(w_{\sigma}\phi_{i})(z)|^{2}dz\leq\int_{\Omega}|(w_{\sigma}\phi_{i})|^{2}dx\leq 1.

and if
\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N+2s}r^{N+2s}\geq\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}}{N}

and
\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}}{N}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy>(2\pi)^{N}k
, we have that

\displaystyle\lambda_{s,k+1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N+2s}r^{N+2s}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy+\Psi_{s}(r,\sigma)-(2\pi)^{N}\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(\Omega)\int_{B_{r}}|{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(w_{\sigma}\phi_{i})(z)|^{2}dz}{\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}}{N}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy-(2\pi)^{N}\sum^{k}_{i=1}\int_{B_{r}}|{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(w_{\sigma}\phi_{i})(z)|^{2}dz}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N+2s}r^{N+2s}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy+\Psi_{s}(r,\sigma)-(2\pi)^{N}\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(\Omega)}{\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}}{N}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy-(2\pi)^{N}k}.

(3.5)

Therefore, choosing
\sigma=r^{-\frac{s}{2}}
, we have that

\displaystyle\Psi_{s}(r,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}}{N}\frac{2c_{N,s}}{\sigma^{2s}}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}(y)dy+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N+2s-1}}{\sigma(N+2s-1)(1-s)}|\Omega|+\frac{1}{\sigma}\phi_{s}(r,R)|\Omega|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}}{\sigma(2sN)}R^{-2s}|\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{N,s}\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}(y)dy\,r^{N+s^{2}}+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}|\Omega|}{(N+2s-1)(1-s)}r^{N+\frac{3s}{2}-1}

\displaystyle\quad+r^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi_{s}(r,R)|\Omega|+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N+\frac{s}{2}}}{2sN}R^{-2s}|\Omega|,

now choosing
r>1
large such that

\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}}{N}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy=(2\pi)^{N}(k+1),

we derive that

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{s,i}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-N}\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N+2s}r^{N+2s}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy+(2\pi)^{-N}\Psi_{s}(r,\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{N+2s}(2\pi)^{2s}(\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N})^{-\frac{2s}{N}}(\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy)^{-\frac{2s}{N}}(k+1)^{1+\frac{2s}{N}}+(2\pi)^{-N}\Psi_{s}(r,\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{N+2s}(2\pi)^{2s}|B_{1}|^{-\frac{2s}{N}}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2s}{N}}\Big(1+\frac{c}{k}\Big)(k+1)^{1+\frac{2s}{N}}+(2\pi)^{-N}\Psi_{s}(r,\sigma),

where
\sigma\sim k^{-\frac{s}{N}}
, and

(\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy)^{-\frac{2s}{N}}\leq|\Omega|^{-\frac{2s}{N}}(1+\frac{c}{k})

by the assumption of
|\Omega_{t}|\leq c_{0}R^{N-1}
, using (
1.5
), we have that

\displaystyle\Psi_{s}(r,\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\Big(k^{1+\frac{s^{2}}{N}}+k^{1+\frac{3s-2}{2N}}+k^{1+\frac{s}{2N}}+k^{1+\frac{1}{N}\max\{2s-1,0\}+\frac{s}{2N}}\log k\Big)

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}k^{1+\frac{s}{N}}.

where
c_{2},\,c_{3}>0
could be chosen independently of
k
.

In conclusion, we have that

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{s,i}(\Omega)\leq\frac{N(2\pi)^{2s}}{N+2s}(|B_{1}||\Omega|)^{-\frac{2s}{N}}k^{1+\frac{2s}{N}}+c_{0}k^{1+\frac{s}{N}}.

(3.6)

This completes the proof.
\Box

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.
From
[
35
, Corollary 2.2]
, using the Berezin-Li-Yau method, a lower bound could be derived as following

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{s,i}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{N(2\pi)^{2s}}{N+2s}(|B_{1}||\Omega|)^{-\frac{2s}{N}}k^{1+\frac{2s}{N}}.

which, combining with (
3.6
), implies

\lim_{k\to+\infty}k^{-1-\frac{2s}{N}}\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{s,i}(\Omega)=\frac{(2\pi)^{2s}N}{N+2s}(|B_{1}||\Omega|)^{-\frac{2s}{N}}.

We complete the proof.
\Box

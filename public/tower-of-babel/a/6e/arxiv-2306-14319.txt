Bulk-preventing actions for SU(N) gauge theories
Tobias Rindlisbacher, Kari Rummukainen, Ahmed Salami

Abstract
Lattice gauge field theories may suffer from unphysical ”bulk” phase transitions at strong lattice gauge coupling. We introduce a one-parameter family of lattice OPEN SU ⁡ ( N CLOSE ) \operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right) gauge actions which, when used in combination with an HMC update algorithm, prevents the appearance of the bulk phase transition. We briefly discuss the (presumed) mechanism behind the prevention of the bulk transition and present test results for different OPEN SU ⁡ ( N CLOSE ) \operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right) gauge groups.



I
Introduction

In asymptotically free gauge theories on the lattice the continuum limit is obtained when the bare lattice gauge coupling vanishes. In practice lattice simulations are always done at finite lattice spacing, and as long as the coupling constant is sufficiently small we can analytically extrapolate the results to the continuum limit. The range of lattice spacings (or coupling constants) is often limited by the emergence of an unphysical “bulk phase” at strong lattice gauge coupling, which prevents the analytical connection to the continuum phase from this region. The value of the lattice coupling where the transition to the bulk phase happens depends on the lattice gauge group, the choice of the gauge action and the matter content.

The problem of the bulk phase transition becomes acute in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
gauge theories with large number of colors and in models with large number of fermion degrees of freedom. In pure gauge
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
theories with the standard Wilson plaquette action the bulk transition is a rapid cross-over if
N\leq 4
but becomes an increasingly strong first order transition for
N\geq 5

Lucini:2005vg
. Adding fermionic degrees of freedom slows down the evolution of the coupling constant (i.e. the magnitude of the
\beta
-function is smaller) and also increase the effective lattice gauge coupling
Hasenfratz:1993az
. Depending on the physical case of interest, these effects require one to use strong bare coupling.
This happens especially in infrared (near-)conformal models, where the coupling runs very slowly, for example in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2}}\right)
with large numbers of fundamental fermions
Bursa:2010xn
;
Karavirta:2011zg
;
Leino:2019qwk
;
Rantaharju:2021iro
;
Rindlisbacher:2021hhh
,
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2}}\right)
with adjoint fermions
Hietanen:2008mr
;
Bursa:2009we
;
Rantaharju:2015cne
,
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(3}}\right)
with large Nf
LSD:2014nmn
;
Hasenfratz:2022zsa
;
LatticeStrongDynamics:2018hun
, and
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(4}}\right)
with fermions in the antisymmetric representation
DeGrand:2014cea
.

In this work we present a local lattice gauge action, defined on elementary plaquettes, which efficiently removes the transition to the bulk phase. Our approach is related to the ”dislocation prevention” method by DeGrand
et al.

DeGrand:2014rwa
. For
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)
and
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2}}\right)
gauge groups also the topological gauge actions that restict the plaquette magnitude
Bornyakov:1991gq
;
Akerlund:2015zha
;
Nogradi:2018ivi
are to some extent related. The latter stops being the case for
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
gauge groups with
N>2
, as the plaquette eigenvalues are then no longer determined by the trace of the plaquette. We note that our approach removes merely the bulk phase but does not restrict in any way gauge-topology fluctuations.

Following Wilson’s prescription, the lattice discretization of a
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
gauge theory is obtained by promoting the Lie algebra valued continuum gauge field,

A_{\mu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\prime}}}\right)=\sum_{a}A^{a}_{\mu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\prime}}}\right)\,T^{a}\,\in\,\mathfrak{su}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)\ ,

(1)

with
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{}}\right.T^{a}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.}}\right\}_{a=1,\ldots,N^{2}-1}
being a basis of
\mathfrak{su}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
, to Lie group valued link variables,

U_{\mu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\,=\,\mathcal{P}\operatorname{e}^{\mathrm{i}\,\int_{ax}^{a\,(x+\widehat{\mu})}\mathrm{d}{x^{\prime}}\,A_{\mu}(x^{\prime})}\,\in\,\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)\ ,

(2)

which can be interpreted as the gauge-parallel transporters along the link between a site
x
and a neighboring site
x+\widehat{\mu}
. The leading
\mathcal{P}
on the right-hand side of (
2
) indicate that path-ordering should be applied when evaluating the exponential of the line-integral. The relation between the coordinate
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
in (
1
) and the coordinate
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}
in (
2
) is given by
x^{\prime}=a\,x
, where
a
is the lattice spacing, and
\widehat{\mu}
refers to the unit-vector in
\mu
-direction. Parallel transporters over longer distances are then expressed as products of consecutive link variables and a lattice gauge action can be defined in terms of link variables by requiring that in the limit
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(a\to 0}}\right)
the lattice gauge action converges to the continuum gauge action,

S_{G}=\frac{1}{2\,g_{0}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x^{\prime}\operatorname{tr}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\prime}}}\right)F_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\prime}}}\right)}}\right)\ .

(3)

Wilson proposed the gauge action
Wilson:1974sk

S_{G,W}=\frac{\beta}{N}\sum\limits_{x}\sum\limits_{\mu<\nu}\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\big.\mathbbm{1}-U_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\big.\bigr)\ ,

(4)

which, as is well known, satisfies the above condition and is here
written in terms of the inverse bare gauge coupling
\beta=2\,N/g_{0}^{2}
and the plaquette variables

U_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)=U_{\mu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\,U_{\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x+\widehat{\mu}}}\right)\,U^{\dagger}_{\mu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x+\widehat{\nu}}}\right)\,U^{\dagger}_{\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\ .

(5)

The gauge action (
4
) and improved versions of it
Luscher:1984xn
are the most commonly used in Monte Carlo studies of
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
lattice gauge theories. They are, however, not unique and might not be the best choice for the study of lattice gauge theories at strong coupling, as they allow the gauge system to enter the bulk phase. Despite its name, the bulk phase is not necessarily a proper phase, but simply a region in parameter space of the lattice theory where lattice artifacts dominate in ensemble averages. As a consequence the relation between lattice and continuum results becomes very complicated or can even be lost completely if bulk and continuum phase are separated by a first order transition.

II
Avoiding the lattice bulk phase

In this section we propose a characterization of ”bulk configurations” in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
lattice gauge systems, which allows for the definition of a family of lattice gauge actions, that separate such bulk configurations from regular ones by an infinite potential barrier, while still yielding the same naive continuum limit as Wilson’s plaquette gauge action. In combination with a hybrid Monte Carlo (HMC) update algorithm, the new gauge actions prevent the gauge system from entering a bulk phase.

II.1
Motivation in
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)

In the
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)

II.2
Situation in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)

Compact Lie groups like
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
carry a Riemannian metric
g
which is invariant under left and right translation. As a consequence, the well known bi-invariant Haar measure for group integration exists, which is given by the volume form of the invariant metric, normalized so that that the group volume is
1
. The fact that
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
has a Riemannian metric also means that we can at each point
U\in\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
define a tangent space
\mathrm{T}_{U}\!\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
and the so called exponential map
\exp_{U}\,:\,\mathrm{T}_{U}\!\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)\to\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
that maps vectors
\omega\in\mathrm{T}_{U}\!\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
from the tangent space at point
U
to points
V=\exp_{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\omega}}\right)
in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
. The graph of the function
\gamma_{U,\omega}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t}}\right)=\exp_{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\omega\,t}}\right)
,
t\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[}}\right.0,1\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.}}\right]
then describes a geodesic segment in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
that start in the point
U
in the direction specified by
\omega
and has geodesic length
|\omega|_{g\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U}}\right)}
.

The exponential map
\exp_{U}\,:\,\mathrm{T}_{U}\!\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)\to\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
is bijective only in a neighborhood
\Omega_{U}\subset\mathrm{T}_{U}\!\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
around the origin of
\mathrm{T}_{U}\!\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
. The boundary
\partial\Omega_{U}
of the region
\Omega_{U}\subset\mathrm{T}_{U}\!\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
over which the exponential map
\exp_{U}
is bijective is called the cut locus of
U
in
\mathrm{T}_{U}\!\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
and its image
\exp_{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\partial\Omega_{U}}}\right)
is, correspondingly, the cut locus of
U
in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
.

At the point
U=\mathbbm{1}\in\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
the tangent space to
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
corresponds to the usual Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)=\mathrm{T}_{\mathbbm{1}}\!\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
and the exponential map applied to some
\omega\in\mathfrak{su}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
can be identified with the usual matrix exponential,

\exp_{\mathbbm{1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\omega}}\right)=\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{i}\,\omega^{a}\,T^{a}}}\right)\ ,

(10)

with
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{}}\right.T_{a}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.}}\right\}_{a=1,\ldots,N^{2}-1}
being a hermitian basis of
\mathfrak{su}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
.

The domain
\Omega_{\mathbbm{1}}\subset\mathfrak{su}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)=\mathrm{T}_{\mathbbm{1}}\!\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
over which
\exp_{\mathbbm{1}}
is bijective is given by the set of hermitian matrices
\omega\in\mathfrak{su}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
whose eigenvalues
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{}}\right.\phi_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.}}\right\}_{i=1,\ldots,N}
satisfy
rossmann2002lie
:

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\phi_{i}}}\right|<\pi\quad\forall\,i\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{}}\right.1,\ldots,N\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.}}\right\}\ .

(11)

The image
\exp_{\mathbbm{1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\partial\Omega_{\mathbbm{1}}}}\right)\subset\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
, i.e. the cut locus of
\mathbbm{1}
in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
, is therefore given by the set of matrices
V\in\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
for which at least one eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}=\operatorname{e}^{\mathrm{i}\,\phi_{i}}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
is equal to
-1
.

To illustrate this, we pick a
\omega\in\mathfrak{su}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
and write it as

\omega=U_{\omega}D_{\omega}U_{\omega}^{\dagger}\ ,

(12)

with
U_{\omega}\in\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
and
D_{\omega}=\operatorname{diag}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{N}}}\right)
being a diagonal matrix containing the eigenvalues of
\omega
. The image
V=\exp_{\mathbbm{1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\omega}}\right)
of
\omega
under the exponential map
\exp_{\mathbbm{1}}
is then,

V=U_{\omega}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{i}\,D_{\omega}}}\right)U_{\omega}^{\dagger}=U_{\omega}D_{V}U_{\omega}^{\dagger}\ ,

(13)

with
D_{V}=\operatorname{diag}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N}}}\right)
being the diagonal matrix of eigenvalues of
V
, given by
\lambda_{i}=\operatorname{e}^{\mathrm{i}\,\phi_{i}}
,
i\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{}}\right.1,\ldots,N\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.}}\right\}
. As long as
-\pi<\phi_{i}<\pi

\forall i\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{}}\right.1,\ldots,N\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.}}\right\}
, the exponential map is a bijection as the inverse map can be defined using,

\phi_{i}=-\mathrm{i}\,\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\lambda_{i}}}\right)\quad,\quad i=1,\ldots,N\ ,

(14)

with
\log
being the principal value complex logarithm. But, if
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\phi_{i}}}\right|\geq\pi
for any
i\in{1,\ldots,N}
, then (
14
) breaks down.

We can now generalize the criterion we found in the previous section for the production of bulk configurations in
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)
lattice gauge theory to the
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
case. In the
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)
case we argued, that if the phase (
8
) of any plaquette variable
U_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)
in the system crosses continuously the
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\pi,\pi}}\right]
-interval boundary (plaquette wrappping of type
(b)
), then a bulk configuration is produced. Such a continuous crossing of the
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\pi,\pi}}\right]
-interval boundary by the plaquette phase implies of course that the corresponding plaquette variable
U_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)
passes through the value
-1
, which is the cut locus for the exponential map from the Lie algebra
\mathrm{u}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)=\mathrm{T}_{1}\!\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)=\mathbb{R}
to the group manifold
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)
. If a plaquette variable
U_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)
approaches and smoothly crosses the cut locus value
-1
the resulting lattice gauge field described by the link variables can no longer be properly mapped on a corresponding Lie algebra valued gauge field that is relevant for continuum physics. The same applies in the case of
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
lattice gauge theory if a
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
plaquette variable approaches and smoothly cross the
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
cut locus. The resulting configuration cannot be mapped on a corresponding Lie algebra valued gauge configuration that is relevant for continuum physics, which qualifies it as a bulk configuration.

In order to prevent a
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
gauge system form entering a bulk phase at strong coupling, the plaquette variables must not continuously cross the cut locus. As discussed below (
11
), this means that the eigenvalues of the plaquette variables must not continuously cross the value
-1
.

The question of how such a restriction affects the creation of magnetic monopoles in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
gauge theory is addressed in Appendix
A.2
.

II.3
Bulk-preventing action

To prevent plaquettes from having eigenvalues close to
-1

S_{G,b}\,=\,\frac{2\,\gamma}{n\,N}\sum\limits_{x}\sum\limits_{\mu<\nu}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\big.\bigl(\big.\Omega^{\dagger}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\Omega_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\big.\bigr)^{-n}-\mathbbm{1}\big.\bigr)\ ,

-{s^{4}}\frac{1}{12}\begin{cases}\bigl(\big.\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\big.F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\big.\bigr)/2\big.\bigr)^{2}&\text{if}\ N<4\\
\ \,\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\big.\bigl(\big.F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\big.\bigr)^{2}\big.\bigr)/2&\text{if}\ N\geq 4\end{cases}\\
+\mathcal{O}\big(s^{6}\big)\ ,

(17)

and, correspondingly, for the local plaquette action contributing to
S_{G,b}
from (
15
):

\frac{2}{n}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\big.\bigl(\big.\Omega^{\dagger}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\Omega_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\big.\bigr)^{-n}-\mathbbm{1}\big.\bigr)={s^{2}}\operatorname{tr}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)}}\right)/2\\
+{s^{4}}\frac{1+3\,n}{24}\begin{cases}\bigl(\big.\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\big.F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\big.\bigr)/2\big.\bigr)^{2}&\text{if}\ N<4\\
\ \,\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\big.\bigl(\big.F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\big.\bigr)^{2}\big.\bigr)/2&\text{if}\ N\geq 4\end{cases}\\
+\mathcal{O}\big({s^{6}}\big)\ .

(18)

In the limit
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(a\to 0}}\right)
, one has
s\sim a^{2}
,
F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\sim F_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)
, and we see that the two local actions have the same leading term
\sim a^{4}
, namely:

a^{4}\,\operatorname{tr}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)F_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)}}\right)/2\ .

(19)

The actions (
15
) introduce an infinite potential barrier between bulk and continuum configurations. This is sufficient to ensure that, if we start a simulation from a cold configuration (all link variables equal to the identity) and use a hybrid Monte Carlo (HMC) algorithm to update the gauge system, no bulk-configurations will be produced.

One could infer that our bulk-preventing setup results in a non-ergodic update algorithm. However, one should keep in mind that the part of the configuration space that is not sampled is irrelevant for the continuum limit of the theory. The algorithm prevents ensemble averages of the lattice system from being contaminated (or even dominated) by bulk-configurations, which should allow one to extract continuum physics also at stronger coupling. The same effect could be achieved by defining a modified measure, which gives zero weight to bulk configurations. However, this would be difficult to implement as the latter are hard to identify once they are created. The use of an action (
15
) in combination with an HMC algorithm is a proxy to achieve the same effect but in a simpler and more economic way. An expression for the gauge force, required for the HMC, is presented in Appendix
B
.

Let us note that for
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)
and
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2}}\right)
, the actions in (
15
) alone would have a similar effect as the topological actions discussed in
Brower:1982ak
;
Bornyakov:1991gq
;
Akerlund:2015zha
;
Nogradi:2018ivi
: the larger the inverse bare coupling
\gamma
, the stronger the plaquette values are repelled from
-1
resp.
-\mathbbm{1}
. For
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
with
N>2
the effect of (
15
) is, however, different from the one of the topological action discussed in
Nogradi:2018ivi
, as for
N>2
the trace of the plaquette does no-longer completely determine the plaquette eigenvalues. An action which can have a similar effect as our actions (
15
) has been given in
Brandt:2019ukx
. We also note that based on the observation that the bulk transition of the Wilson gauge action (
4
) is sensitive to the addition of an adjoint plaquette term
Bhanot:1981eb
, it has in
Hasenfratz:2011xn
been demonstrated that for
N=3
the bulk phase can be avoided by adding an appropriately scaled negative adjoint plaquette term to the standard Wilson gauge action
Hasenbusch:2004yq
;
Hasenfratz:2011xn
.

While for us it is desirable that the actions (
15
) do not prevent topology from fluctuating, there have also been attempts to find actions which keep the topology fixed
Bietenholz:2005rd
.

IV
Conclusions

We have identified a mechanism which appears to be relevant for the formation of unphysical ”bulk” configurations and the corresponding occurrence of a ”bulk transition” in simulations of lattice
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
gauge theories using Wilson’s plaquette gauge action. We proposed a one-parameter family of alternative gauge actions, which possess the same continuum limit as the Wilson plaquette gauge action but which, when used in combination with an HMC update algorithm, prevent bulk-configurations from being created.

We tested our bulk-preventing simulation framework for pure gauge
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2}}\right)
, pure gauge
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(5}}\right)
, and for
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(3}}\right)
with
N_{f}=4
mass-degenerate Wilson-clover fermion flavors with hopping parameter
\kappa=0.1358
, and which are coupled to the gauge field via 2-step stout smeared link variables. We found that in all three cases, the bulk-preventing action (
15
) with
n=2
removes the bulk transition and reproduces at sufficiently weak coupling the same results as the Wilson plaquette action.

In the case of the fermionic
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(3}}\right)
theory, the Wilson gauge action could not be used to study the physical finite temperature phase transition on
N_{t}=6
lattices at small quark masses. This is due to the fact that the bulk transition prevents the system from simultaneously reaching the physical transition region and small quark masses.
On the other hand, with the bulk-preventing action (
15
) the bulk transition is absent and
m_{q}
can be made arbitrarily small.
It is also worth noting that the bulk-preventing actions do not seem to hinder any processes required for topology fluctuations.

II.1
, are related to the creation of magnetic Dirac monopoles as defined in
DeGrand:1980eq
. The situation with monopoles in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
lattice gauge theory will be addressed in the second part of this appendix.

A.1
Situation in
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)

Dirac monopoles in
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)
lattice gauge theory, as described in
DeGrand:1980eq
, live on the dual lattice and are defined by a non-zero net physical magnetic flux through the boundary of a spatial cube. If this flux is non-zero for a given cube, then this cube contains a Dirac monopole. With our conventions, the corresponding operator can be written as:

M\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\epsilon_{4\sigma\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\arg\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x+\widehat{\sigma}}}\right)}}\right)-\arg\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)}}\right)}}\right)\ ,

(20)

which is the lattice analogue of the continuum expression,
M\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)=J_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(m}}\right)}^{0}
, with

J_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(m}}\right)}^{\rho}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)=\epsilon^{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\partial_{\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\ ,

(21)

i.e.
M\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)=\operatorname{div}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(B\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)}}\right)
. If one defines integer plaquette variables
n_{x,\mu\nu}\,\in\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{}}\right.-2,-1,0,1,2\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.}}\right\}
, so that

\arg\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)}}\right)=\Theta_{x,\mu\nu}-2\,\pi\,n_{x,\mu\nu}\ ,

(22)

with
\Theta_{x,\mu\nu}
from (
8
), then
n_{x,\mu\nu}
counts the amount of Dirac flux passing through the (oriented) plaquette on which
U_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)
is defined. The magnetic monopole operator (
20
) can then also be written as
DeGrand:1980eq

M\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)=\epsilon_{4\sigma\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(n_{x+\widehat{\sigma},\mu\nu}-n_{x,\mu\nu}}}\right)\ .

(23)

From (
20
) or (
23
) we see, that in order to produce or destroy a magnetic monopole, one has to change the net Dirac flux through the boundary of a spatial cube. Considering the plaquette wrapping types
(a)
and
(b)
introduced in Sec.
II.1
, a change of the net Dirac flux through the boundary of a spatial cube can only be achieved with plaquette wrappings of type
(b)
. Fig.
7
illustrates how a type
(b)
plaquette wrapping of a spatial plaquette produces in the two adjacent spatial cubes a pair of magnetic monopoles of opposite charge. The monopoles serve, respectively, as source and sink for the Dirac flux passing through the wrapped plaquette.

Figure 7:
If a single spatial plaquette (blue) picks up a non-zero Dirac flux (red dashed arrow), the two spatial cubes adjacent to the plaquette get, respectively, occupied by a monopole (black sphere) and an anti-monopole (white sphere).

Figure 8:
If the phase of the displayed spatial link (long black arrow) wraps around the
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\pi,\pi}}\right]
-interval, this implies that the phases of all plaquettes that contain this link also wrap around the
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\pi,\pi}}\right]
-interval. For the spatial plaquettes (left-hand panel) this means that they pick up a Dirac flux passing through them, which is such that a closed magnetic flux loop (red dotted arrow loop) is formed that winds around the phase-wrapped link (long black arrow). For the temporal plaquettes (right-hand panel), the phase-wrapping does not correspond to the production of Dirac flux as temporal plaquettes are associated with the electric and not the magnetic field.

With plaquette wrappings of type
(a)
on the other hand, only closed Dirac strings can be produced and therefore no monopole/anti-monopole pairs are created. The reason for this is, that plaquette wrappings of type
(a)
are caused by a link variable wrapping around the
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\pi,\pi}}\right]
-interval. Such a link wrapping will, however, never affect just a single plaquette but all plaquettes that contain the given link. If this link is a spatial one, it is contained in spatial as well as in temporal plaquettes. For the spatial plaquettes the wrapping creates the aforementioned Dirac flux that forms a closed loop winding around the shared link, illustrated in the left-hand panel of Fig.
8
.
For the temporal plaquettes, sketched in the right-hand panel of Fig.
8
, the wrapping does not give rise to the production of Dirac flux as temporal plaquettes are associated to the electric and not the magnetic field. For the same reason, no Dirac flux is produced if the wrapping link is a temporal one, as this would affect only temporal plaquettes.

A.2
Situation in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)

In non-abelian
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
gauge theory magnetic monopoles can be defined in different ways
Goddard:1977da
;
Brandt:1980em
;
Weinberg:1983bf
;
Hollands:1998np
and can be divided into two categories: singular monopoles which require a Dirac string, and non-singular monopoles which possess a Dirac string only in certain gauges. On the lattice singular monopoles can be implemented in terms of net
\mathrm{Z}_{N}
Dirac flux through the boundary of spatial cubes
Ukawa:1979yv
;
Brower:1981rz
;
deForcrand:2005zg
, with the Dirac flux,
\sim\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2\,\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,n_{x,\mu\nu}/N}}\right)
,
n_{x,\mu\nu}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{}}\right.0,\ldots,N-1\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.}}\right\}
, associated with a plaquette variable
U_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)
, being defined so that in the factorization

U_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)=\operatorname{e}^{2\,\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,n_{x,\mu\nu}/N}\,\tilde{U}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)

(24)

one has

\arg(\operatorname{tr}(\tilde{U}_{\mu\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)))\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\pi/N,\pi/N}}\right]\ .

(25)

The possible values for the first factor on the right-hand side of (
24
) correspond to the center of
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
:

\mathrm{Z}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)}}\right)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{}}\right.\mathbbm{1}\,\operatorname{e}^{2\,\pi\,\mathrm{i}\,n/N}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.}}\right\}_{n\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{}}\right.0,\ldots,N-1\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.}}\right\}}\cong\mathrm{Z}_{N}\ .

(26)

As the above discussed Dirac monopoles in
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)
lattice gauge theory, also the so defined
\mathrm{Z}_{N}
monopoles decouple in the (naive) continuum limit
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\beta\to\infty}}\right)
of the
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
lattice gauge theory. Preventing the formation of
\mathrm{Z}_{N}
monopoles in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
lattice gauge theory (with WG action) has for
N=2
been reported to push the bulk phase to stronger coupling
Brower:1981rz
. Preventing not only
\mathrm{Z}_{N}
monopoles but also the formation of
\mathrm{Z}_{N}
Dirac strings from being formed removes the bulk phase completely
Brower:1981rz
;
Brower:1982ak
;
Bornyakov:1991gq
. The continuum limit of the model remains, however, unaffected by these modifications
Fingberg:1994ut
.

To make contact with our discussion in Sec.
II.2
about the relation between the production of bulk configurations in
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
lattice gauge theory and having
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
plaquettes continuously crossing the cut locus of
\mathbbm{1}
on
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
, we note that the non-trivial
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
center elements listed in (
26
) are all located beyond this cut locus for
N>2
(resp. on the cut locus for
N=2
). Therefore, by preventing the continuous cut locus crossing of
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
plaquettes, the production of
\mathrm{Z}_{N}
Dirac flux though individual plaquettes is not possible, which should also prevent the production of
\mathrm{Z}_{N}
monopole/anti-monopole pairs. This is the analogue of the type
(b)
plaquette wrapping from Sec.
II.1
, which is responsible for the monopole/anti-monopole pair production in
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)
lattice gauge theory, as described in Fig.
7
.

We note that this does not imply that the use of our bulk-preventing actions (
15
) in combination with HMC updates would prevent the production of
\mathrm{Z}_{N}
monopoles in general; only the production of cutoff scale monopoles is exlucded. Extended
\mathrm{Z}_{N}
monpoles with ”thick” strings
Mack:1978rq
;
Mack:1978kr
;
Brower:1981um
attached should still be possible.

Also, the production of non-singular
\operatorname{SU}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N}}\right)
monopoles
tHooft:1974kcl
;
Polyakov:1974ek
is not prevented by our bulk-preventing setup. Non-singular monopoles could on the lattice be located by gauge fixing the system to maximal abelian gauge (MAG)
Bonati:2013bga
and then performing abelian projection (AP) to the Cartan subgroup
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)^{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N-1}}\right)}

tHooft:1981bkw
. In each of the
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N-1}}\right)

\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)
factors of
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)^{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N-1}}\right)}
one can then look for Dirac monopoles with the method from
DeGrand:1980eq
, discussed in the first part of this appendix. It is only in this maximum abelian gauge that non-singular monopoles show up as Dirac monopoles of the projected
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)^{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N-1}}\right)}
subgroup; fixing to another gauge before projecting to
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)^{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N-1}}\right)}
results in fewer monopoles being found
Bonati:2010bb
in the
\operatorname{U}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)
subgroup factors. We have not tried to use the MAG-AP method to monitor the monopole densities directly in our HMC simulations with the BP actions from (
15
). However, there is no reason to expect that the BP setup would prevent continuum-relevant monopoles from being produced. Since finite temperature instantons (calorons) can be described as composite objects, consisting of multiple monopoles
Lee:1998bb
, the bottom-left panels of Figs.
3
,
4
, which illustrate that topology fluctuations are unhindered by the bulk-prevention, indicate that also the caloron constituent monopoles can fluctuate.

\rho

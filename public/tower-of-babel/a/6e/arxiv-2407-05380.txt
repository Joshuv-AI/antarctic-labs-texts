Pseudorandomness of primes at large scales
Sun-Kai Leung

Abstract
. Assuming a q q -variant of the prime k k -tuple conjecture uniformly, we compute mixed moments of the number of primes in disjoint short intervals and progressions, respectively. This involves estimating the mean of singular series along products of lattices, which is of independent interest. As a consequence, we establish the convergence of both sequences of suitably normalized primes to a standard Poisson point process.

1.
Introduction

The study of prime numbers dates back over 2000 years to the era of Euclid and Eratosthenes. While significant progress has been made during these two millennia, the exact distribution of primes remains a mystery. One of the biggest challenges is that, despite being a deterministic sequence of natural numbers, primes exhibit highly pseudorandom behavior, apart from some obvious “local obstructions” (see
[
Tao08
, p. 194]
), such as the absence of consecutive primes other than the pair
\{2,3\}
due to parity. Therefore, natural questions arise: to what extent are primes pseudorandom at appropriate larger scales? Can such pseudorandomness be rigorously justified?

In this paper, we provide one possible answer that suitably normalized primes in short intervals and short progressions, respectively, are located on the positive real number line
(0,\infty)
as if they are randomly positioned points in the sense of a
Poisson point process
.

Such a point process models the random and independent occurrence of events. Due to its Poissonian property and complete independence, a Poisson point process is often referred to as a purely random process. Thanks to the Kolmogorov extension theorem, it is completely characterized by the finite-dimensional distribution, i.e., the joint probability distributions for the number of events in all finite collections of disjoint intervals (see
[
DV03
, p. 19]
for instance). This leads to the following convenient definition.

Definition 1.1

(Standard Poisson point process)
.

A point process
\xi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{X_{n}}
(or
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
by abuse of notation) on
(0,\infty)
is called a
standard Poisson point process
if
for any integer
r\geqslant 1
and collection of disjoint intervals
\{I_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
in
(0,\infty),
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\xi(I_{i})=k_{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant r\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}e^{-|I_{i}|}\frac{|I_{i}|^{k_{i}}}{{k_{i}}!},

where
\xi(A):=\#\{n\geqslant 1\,:\,X_{n}\in A\}
is the number of points in the subset
A\subseteq(0,\infty)
.

To be more precise, we shall establish the convergence of sequences of suitably normalized primes to a standard Poisson point process conditionally. For our purpose, we first recall the definition of convergence in distribution of point processes (see
[
Kal17
, pp. 3–4, 109]
and
[
Kal17
, Theorem 4.11]
for instance), and propose a
q
-variant of the prime
k
-tuple conjecture, which is essentially
[
Leu24
, Conjecture 1.1]
without the power-saving error term, again inspired by the modified Cramér model (see
[
Gra95
, pp. 23–24]
).

Definition 1.2

(Convergence in distribution)
.

Let
\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots
be point processes on
(0,\infty).
Then we say
\xi_{n}
converges to

\xi_{n}\xrightarrow{vd}\xi
as random measures. Equivalently, for any integer

and any collection of disjoint

Conjecture 1.1

(
q

k
-tuple conjecture (HL
[

.

Given integers

let

\mathcal{H}\subseteq[0,H]
be a set consisting of

Then as

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}}1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h)=(1+o_{k,H}(1))\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H};q)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant N\\

where

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-k}\prod_{p\nmid q}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left(1-\frac{v_{p}(\mathcal{H})}{p}\right).

Note that
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H};q)=\left(\varphi(q)/q\right)^{-1}\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}),
where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}):=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left(1-\frac{v_{p}(\mathcal{H})}{p}\right)

is the classical singular series.

Assuming HL[1] with sufficient uniformity, Gallagher
[
Gal76
]
showed that the prime count in short intervals has a Poissonian limiting distribution. More precisely, if
\lambda>0,
then for any integer
k\geqslant 0,
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\#\{N<n\leqslant 2N:\pi(n+H)-\pi(n)=k\}=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!},

where
H=\lambda\log N
, i.e., we have the convergence in distribution to a Poissonian random variable with parameter
\lambda

\displaystyle\pi(n+H)-\pi(n)\xrightarrow{d}Poisson(\lambda).

For longer intervals, assuming HL[1] with a power-saving error term, Montgomery and Soundararajan
[
MS04
]
showed that the prime count in short intervals has a Gaussian limiting distribution (see
[
GR07
, pp. 59–73]

Inspired by their work, assuming HL[
q
] with a power-saving error term, the author
[

]

\lambda>0,
then for any integer

we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\left|\left\{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\,:\,\pi(N;q,a)=k\right\}\right|=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!},

where
N=\lambda\varphi(q)\log q
, i.e., we have the convergence in distribution to a Poissonian random variable with parameter
\lambda

\displaystyle\pi(N;q,a)\xrightarrow{d}Poisson(\lambda).

2.
Main results

Analogous to
[
Gal76
]
,
[
MS04
]
and
[
Leu24
]
, to establish the convergence in distribution of point processes, we compute mixed moments of the number of primes in short intervals and short progressions respectively. To state our main results, let us denote
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{I}):=\sup\cup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}I_{i}-\inf\cup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}I_{i}
as the diameter of the collection of disjoint intervals
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
in
(0,\infty),
and

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}
as the
Stirling number of the second kind
, i.e., the number of ways to partition a set of
k

j
non-empty subsets.

Theorem 2.1
.

Given an integer
r\geqslant 1,
let
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
be a collection of disjoint intervals in
(0,\infty).
Suppose HL[1] holds for
1\leqslant H\leqslant\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{I})
uniformly. Then for all integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geqslant 0,
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{N<n\leqslant 2N}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\#\left\{p>n\,:\,\frac{p-n}{\log N}\in I_{i}\right\}^{k_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Bigg(\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{k_{i}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}|I_{i}|^{j_{i}}\Bigg).

Theorem 2.2
.

Given an integer
r\geqslant 1,
let
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
be a collection of disjoint intervals in
(0,\infty).
Suppose HL[q] holds for
1\leqslant H\leqslant\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{I})\varphi(q)\log q
uniformly. Then for all integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geqslant 0,
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\#\left\{p\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\,:\,\frac{p}{\varphi(q)\log q}\in I_{i}\right\}^{k_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Bigg(\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{k_{i}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}|I_{i}|^{j_{i}}\Bigg).

Remark 2.1
.

It is, in fact, easier and considered more natural to compute factorial moments rather than ordinary moments in this context. However, we opt for the latter to ensure compatibility with the existing literature.

Applying the method of moments, by Definitions
1.1
and
1.2
, the convergence of both sequences of suitably normalized primes in short intervals and short progressions to a standard Poisson point process is an immediate consequence.

Corollary 2.1
.

Given an integer
r\geqslant 1,
let
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
be a collection of disjoint intervals in
(0,\infty).
Suppose HL[1] holds for
1\leqslant H\leqslant\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{I})
uniformly. Then for all integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geqslant 0,
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\#\left\{N<n\leqslant 2N\,:\,\#\left\{p>n\,:\,\frac{p-n}{\log N}\in I_{i}\right\}=k_{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant r\right\}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}e^{-|I_{i}|}\frac{|I_{i}|^{k_{i}}}{k_{i}!},

i.e., the point process
\{\frac{p_{i}(n)-n}{\log N}\}_{i=1}^{\infty},
for
N<n\leqslant 2N
chosen uniformly at random, converges in distribution to the standard Poisson point process as
N\to\infty,
where
p_{i}
denotes the
i
-th least prime after
n
. In particular, by taking
r=1
and
I_{1}=(0,\lambda],
we recover
[
Gal76
, Theorem 1]
.

Remark 2.2
.

On the “frequency side”, one formulation of the GUE hypothesis states that the point process

\{\frac{\log T}{2\pi}(\gamma_{n}-t)\}
, for
T<t\leqslant 2T
chosen uniformly at random, converges in distribution to the sine-kernel process, where
\gamma_{n}
is the
n
-th (counted with potential multiplicities) least positive ordinate of a nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function

(see
[

]

Corollary 2.2
.

Given an integer
r\geqslant 1,
let
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
be a collection of disjoint intervals in
(0,\infty).
Suppose HL[

] holds for
1\leqslant H\leqslant\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{I})\varphi(q)\log q

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\#\left\{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\,:\,\#\left\{p\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\,:\,\frac{p}{\varphi(q)\log q}\in I_{i}\right\}=k_{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant r\right\}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}e^{-|I_{i}|}\frac{|I_{i}|^{k_{i}}}{k_{i}!},

i.e., the point process

3.
Estimates of singular series

As observed in
[
Gal76
]
, while primes exhibit “local” correlations, leading to those singular series defined in Conjecture
1.1
, such correlations diminish “globally” as the classical singular series is
1
on average over hypercubes. Similarly, the following (unconditional) proposition, which is a “multidimensional” version of
[
Leu24
, Lemma 6.1]
but without an explicit error term, lies at the crux of the mixed moment computation.

Proposition 3.1
.

Given integers
q,r\geqslant 1,
let
\{J_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
be a collection of disjoint intervals in
(0,\infty)
with
\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}|J_{i}|/q\to\infty
as
\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}|J_{i}|\to\infty.
Then for any
(b,q)=1
and
l_{1},\ldots,l_{r}\geqslant 1,
we have

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{l_{i}}^{(i)}\in J_{i}\text{ distinct}\\
h_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv h_{l_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H};q)=(1+o_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{r}}(1))\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{|J_{i}|}{\varphi(q)}\right)^{l_{i}}

as
\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}|J_{i}|\to\infty,
where
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{l_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r},
i.e., the singular series
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H};q)
is, on average,
(\varphi(q)/q)^{-(l_{1}+\cdots+l_{r})}
along a product of lattices of ranks
l_{1},\ldots,l_{r}.

Proof.

We shall follow the proof in
[
Gal76
]
(see
[
For16
]
for a simplification). Let
p\nmid q
be a prime. Then we define

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left(1-\frac{v_{p}(\mathcal{H})}{p}\right)=:1+a(p,v_{p}(\mathcal{H})),

and

\displaystyle a(d;\mathcal{H}):=\prod_{p\mid d}a(p,v_{p}(\mathcal{H}))

for any square-free integer
d
coprime to
q.
It follows from the definition that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H};q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1\\
(d,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\mu^{2}(d)a(d;\mathcal{H}),

which can be verified to be absolutely convergent, so that

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{l_{i}}^{(i)}\in J_{i}\text{ distinct}\\
h_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv h_{l_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H};q)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-|\bm{l}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1\\
(d,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\mu^{2}(d)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{l_{i}}^{(i)}\in J_{i}\text{ distinct}\\
h_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv h_{l_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}a(d;\mathcal{H})

(3.1)

\displaystyle=:\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\right)^{-|\bm{l}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1\\
(d,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\mu^{2}(d)S^{\prime}(d),

where
|\bm{l}|:=l_{1}+\cdots+l_{r}.
Arguing as in
[
Gal76
]
, one can
show that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d>D\\
(d,q)=1\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(d)S^{\prime}(d)\ll_{\bm{l},\epsilon}(D|J|)^{\epsilon}\frac{|J|}{q^{r}D},

where
J:=\prod_{i=1}^{r}J_{i}.

From now on, we always assume
1\leqslant d\leqslant D.
Interchanging the order of summation, the sum
S^{\prime}(d)
becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{v}=(v_{p})_{p\mid d}}\prod_{p|d}a(p,v_{p})\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{l_{i}}^{(i)}\in J_{i}\text{ distinct}\\
h_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv h_{l_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\\
(v_{p}(\mathcal{H}))_{p\mid d}=\bm{v}\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}1=:S(d)-R(d),

where
\bm{v}=(v_{p})_{p\mid d}\in\mathbb{N}^{w(d)}
with

\displaystyle S(d):=\sum_{\bm{v}=(v_{p})_{p\mid d}}\prod_{p|d}a(p,v_{p})\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{l_{i}}^{(i)}\in J_{i}\\
h_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv h_{l_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\\
(v_{p}(\mathcal{H}))_{p\mid d}=\bm{v}\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}1,

and
R(d)
is the contribution of all non-distinct terms to
S(d)
, which is

\displaystyle\ll_{r}\sum_{\bm{v}=(v_{p})_{p\mid d}}\prod_{p|d}|a(p,v_{p})|\left(\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}\frac{|J_{i}|}{q}\right)^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{|J_{i}|}{q}\right)^{l_{i}}.

Applying the Chinese remainder theorem, the inner sum of
S(d)
is

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{|J_{i}|}{qd}+O(1)\right)^{l_{i}}\prod_{p\mid d}{p\choose v_{p}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{|\boldsymbol{l}|}{v_p}v_{p}!

\displaystyle=\left(1+O_{r}\left(\left(\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}\frac{|J_{i}|}{qd}\right)^{-1}\right)\right)\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{|J_{i}|}{qd}\right)^{l_{i}}\prod_{p\mid d}{p\choose v_{p}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{|\boldsymbol{l}|}{v_p}v_{p}!,

so that

\displaystyle S^{\prime}(d)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{|J_{i}|}{qd}\right)^{l_{i}}A(d)+

\displaystyle O_{r}\left(\left(\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}\frac{|J_{i}|}{qd}\right)^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{|J_{i}|}{qd}\right)^{l_{i}}B(d)\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle O_{r}\left(\left(\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}\frac{|J_{i}|}{q}\right)^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{|J_{i}|}{q}\right)^{l_{i}}C(d)\right),

where

\displaystyle A(d):=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{v}}\prod_{p\mid d}a(p,v_{p}){p\choose v_{p}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{|\boldsymbol{l}|}{v_p}v_{p}!=\prod_{p\mid d}\sum_{v_{p}=1}^{p}a(p,v_{p}){p\choose v_{p}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{|\boldsymbol{l}|}{v_p}v_{p}!,

\displaystyle B(d):=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{v}}\prod_{p\mid d}|a(p,v_{p})|{p\choose v_{p}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{|\boldsymbol{l}|}{v_p}v_{p}!=\prod_{p\mid d}\sum_{v_{p}=1}^{p}|a(p,v_{p})|{p\choose v_{p}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{|\boldsymbol{l}|}{v_p}v_{p}!

and

\displaystyle C(d):=\sum_{\bm{v}}\prod_{p\mid d}|a(p,v_{p})|=\prod_{p\mid d}\sum_{v_{p}=1}^{p}|a(p,v_{p})|.

Arguing as in

\displaystyle\ll_{\epsilon}D^{1+\epsilon}\left(\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}\frac{|J_{i}|}{q}\right)^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{|J_{i}|}{q}\right)^{l_{i}}.

Finally, by definition the

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p-1)^{|\bm{l}|}}\left((p^{|\bm{l}|}-(p-1)^{|\bm{l}|})\sum_{v=1}^{p}{p\choose v}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{|\boldsymbol{l}|}{v}v!-p^{|\bm{l}|-1}\sum_{v=1}^{p}v{p\choose v}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{|\boldsymbol{l}|}{v}v!\right).

We also require an upper bound for individual (classical) singular series.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given integers
k,q\geqslant 1,
let
H\geqslant 2q
be an integer and
\mathcal{H}\subseteq[0,H]
be a set consisting of
k
distinct multiples of
q
. Then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})\ll_{k}\left(\log\frac{H}{q}\right)^{k-1}.

Proof.

By definition, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant H/q\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left(1-\frac{v_{p}(\mathcal{H})}{p}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>H/q\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left(1-\frac{v_{p}(\mathcal{H})}{p}\right).

Since the set
\mathcal{H}
consists of distinct multiples of
q
, we must have
v_{p}(\mathcal{H})=k
provided that
p>H/q
and
p\nmid q,
so that the last product is

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>H/q\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}\left(1-\frac{v_{p}(\mathcal{H})}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>H/q\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\left(1+O_{k}\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=1+O_{k}\bigg(\left(\frac{H}{q}\log\frac{H}{q}\right)^{-1}\bigg).

On the other hand, since the first product of (
3.5
) is
\ll_{k}\log^{k-1}(H/q)
using Mertens’ estimates, the lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem

To lighten the notation, we adopt the convention
\widetilde{x}:=\frac{x}{\varphi(q)\log q}.
Without loss of generality, we assume
I_{i}=(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}]
with
0<\alpha_{1}<\beta_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{r}<\beta_{r}
and
k_{i}\geqslant 1
for
i=1,\ldots,r.
Then, adapting the proof of
[

, Theorem 1.2]
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\#\left\{p\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\,:\,\frac{p}{\varphi(q)\log q}\in I_{i}\right\}^{k_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{p_{1}^{(i)}},\ldots,\widetilde{p_{k_{i}}^{(i)}}\in I_{i}\\
p_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv p_{k_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{k_{i}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}j_{i}!\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{p_{1}^{(i)}}<\ldots<\widetilde{p_{j_{i}}^{(i)}}\in I_{i}\\
p_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv p_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}1.

Making the change of variables
p_{1}^{(i)}=n_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,p_{j_{i}}^{(i)}=n_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}
for
i=1,\ldots,r,
this becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{k_{i}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}j_{i}!\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=d_{1}^{(i)}<\cdots<d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\\
d_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{i}\,:\,\widetilde{n_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)}},\ldots,\widetilde{n_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}}\in I_{i}\\
n_{i}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}1_{\mathbb{P}}(n_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)})\cdots 1_{\mathbb{P}}(n_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\underset{1\leqslant j_{i}\leqslant k_{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant r}{\sum\cdots\sum}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}j_{i}!\right)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=d_{1}^{(i)}<\cdots<d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\\
d_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{i}\,:\,\widetilde{n_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)}},\ldots,\widetilde{n_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}}\in I_{i}\\
n_{i}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\prod_{i=1}^{r}1_{\mathbb{P}}(n_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)})\cdots 1_{\mathbb{P}}(n_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}

\displaystyle\underset{1\leqslant j_{i}\leqslant k_{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant r}{\sum\cdots\sum}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}j_{i}!\right)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=d_{1}^{(i)}<\cdots<d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\\
d_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{i}\,:\,\widetilde{n_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)}},\ldots,\widetilde{n_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}}\in I_{i}\\
n_{1}\equiv\cdots\equiv n_{k}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\\
(n_{1}\cdots n_{k},q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\prod_{i=1}^{r}1_{\mathbb{P}}(n_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)})\cdots 1_{\mathbb{P}}(n_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}).

Making another change of variables
n_{i}=n+h_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r,
this becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\underset{1\leqslant j_{i}\leqslant k_{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant r}{\sum\cdots\sum}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}j_{i}!\right)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=d_{1}^{(i)}<\cdots<d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\\
d_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=h_{1}<\cdots<h_{r}\\
h_{1}\equiv\cdots\equiv h_{r}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}

(4.1)

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}n\,:\,\widetilde{n+h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)}},\ldots,\widetilde{n+h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}}\in I_{i}\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum}\prod_{i=1}^{r}1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)})\cdots 1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}).

Since
\widetilde{n+h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)}},\ldots,\widetilde{n+h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}}\in I_{i}

for
i=1,\ldots,r
if and only if

\displaystyle\widetilde{n}\in\left(\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}(\alpha_{i}-\widetilde{h_{i}}),\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r}(\beta_{i}-\widetilde{h_{i}}-\widetilde{d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}})\right]=:I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}},

where
\bm{\alpha}:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}),\ldots,\bm{h}:=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}),
the expression (
4.1
) is
\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2},
where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}:=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\underset{1\leqslant j_{i}\leqslant k_{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant r}{\sum\cdots\sum}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}j_{i}!\right)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=d_{1}^{(i)}<\cdots<d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\\
d_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\,\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=h_{1}<\cdots<h_{r}\\
h_{1}\equiv\cdots\equiv h_{r}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\\
|I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}|>\widetilde{q}\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{n}\in I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum}\prod_{i=1}^{r}1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)})\cdots 1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)})

and
\Sigma_{2}
is the same sum but with the condition
|I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}|>\widetilde{q}
replaced by
|I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}|\leqslant\widetilde{q}.

Let us deal with
\Sigma_{2}
first. Since
\widetilde{n}\in I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}
and
|I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}|\leqslant\widetilde{q},
by a simple application of the Selberg sieve (see
[
IK04
, Theorem 6.7]
for instance), the second line of (
4.2
) is

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\widetilde{n}\in I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)})\cdots 1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)})

\displaystyle\ll_{\bm{k}}\mathfrak{S}(\{h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r})\times\frac{q}{(\log q)^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{r}}},

where
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
. Therefore, it follows from Lemma
3.1
that the sum
\Sigma_{2}
is

\displaystyle\ll_{\bm{k}}\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\times\max_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}}\frac{1}{(\log q)^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{r}}}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=\widetilde{d_{1}^{(i)}}<\cdots<\widetilde{d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}}<\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}\\
d_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=\widetilde{h_{1}}<\cdots<\widetilde{h_{r}}<\beta_{r}-\alpha_{1}\\
h_{1}\equiv\cdots\equiv h_{r}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\\
|I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}|\leqslant\widetilde{q}\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\mathfrak{S}(\{h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r})

\displaystyle\ll_{\bm{k},\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\left(\log\frac{\varphi(q)\log q}{q}\right)^{k-1}\max_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}}\frac{1}{(\log q)^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{r}}}

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=\widetilde{d_{1}^{(i)}}<\cdots<\widetilde{d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}}<\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}\\
d_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=\widetilde{h_{1}}<\cdots<\widetilde{h_{r}}<\beta_{r}-\alpha_{1}\\
h_{1}\equiv\cdots\equiv h_{r}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}1

\displaystyle\ll_{\bm{k},\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\left(\log\frac{\varphi(q)\log q}{q}\right)^{k-1}\max_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}}\frac{1}{(\log q)^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{r}}}\times(\log q)^{(j_{1}-1)+\cdots+(j_{r}-1)+(r-1)}

\displaystyle\ll_{\bm{k},\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}\frac{q}{\varphi(q)\log q}\left(\log\frac{\varphi(q)\log q}{q}\right)^{|\bm{k}|-1},

where
|\bm{k}|:=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r},
which is negligible.

It remains to estimate
\Sigma_{1},
which can be split into two sums
\Sigma_{1,1}
and
\Sigma_{1,2},
where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1,1}:=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\underset{1\leqslant j_{i}\leqslant k_{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant r}{\sum\cdots\sum}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}j_{i}!\right)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=d_{1}^{(i)}<\cdots<d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\\
d_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\,\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=h_{1}<\cdots<h_{r}\\
h_{1}\equiv\cdots\equiv h_{r}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\\
|I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}|>\widetilde{q}\\
\{h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r}\text{ admissible}\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{n}\in I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)})\cdots 1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)})

and
\Sigma_{1,2}
is the sum of the remaining terms, i.e., the set
\{h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r}
is non-admissible.

Suppose
\{h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r}
is non-admissible. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{n}\in I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)})\cdots 1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)})\leqslant|\bm{k}|,

so that the sum
\Sigma_{1,2}
is

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\underset{1\leqslant j_{i}\leqslant k_{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant r}{\sum\cdots\sum}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}j_{i}!\right)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=\widetilde{d_{1}^{(i)}}<\cdots<\widetilde{d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}}<\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}\\
d_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\,\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=\widetilde{h_{1}}<\cdots<\widetilde{h_{r}}<\beta_{r}-\alpha_{1}\\
h_{1}\equiv\cdots\equiv h_{r}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}|\bm{k}|

\displaystyle\ll_{\bm{k},\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\left(\frac{\varphi(q)\log q}{q}\right)^{|\bm{k}|-1},

which is again negligible.

Finally, suppose
\{h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r}
is admissible. Then assuming HL[
q
] uniformly for
0\leqslant h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\dots,h_{i}+d_{k_{i}}^{(i)}\leqslant(\beta_{r}-\alpha_{1})\varphi(q)\log q
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{n}\in I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)})\cdots 1_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+o_{\bm{k},\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(1))\mathfrak{S}(\{h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r};q)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{n}\in I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\log(n+h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)})\cdots\log(n+h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)})\right)^{-1},

so that the sum
\Sigma_{1,1}
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\underset{1\leqslant j_{i}\leqslant k_{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant r}{\sum\cdots\sum}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}j_{i}!\right)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=d_{1}^{(i)}<\cdots<d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\\
d_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\,\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}0=h_{1}<\cdots<h_{r}\\
h_{1}\equiv\cdots\equiv h_{r}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\\
|I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}|>\widetilde{q}\\
\{h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r}\text{ admissible}\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}

\displaystyle\frac{1+o_{\bm{k},\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(1)}{(\log q)^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{r}}}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}n\,:\,\widetilde{n+h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)}},\ldots,\widetilde{n+h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}}\in I_{i}\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\mathfrak{S}(\{h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r};q).

Since the singular series
\mathfrak{S}(\{h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r};q)
vanishes provided that the set
\{h_{i}+d_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,h_{i}+d_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r}
is non-admissible, the condition on the admissibility can be disregarded. Also, the error term induced by dropping the condition
|I_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};\bm{d},\bm{h}}|>\widetilde{q}
is negligible. Unravelling all the changes of variables made, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1+o_{\bm{k},\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(1)}{\varphi(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{k_{i}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}\frac{j_{i}!}{(\log q)^{j_{i}}}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{n_{1}^{(i)}}<\ldots<\widetilde{n_{j_{i}}^{(i)}}\in I_{i}\\
n_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\cdots\equiv n_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\cdots\sum}\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{N};q),

where
\mathcal{N}:=\{n_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,n_{j_{i}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r}.
Applying Proposition
3.1
, this is

\displaystyle\frac{1+o_{\bm{k},\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(1)}{\varphi(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{k_{i}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}\frac{1}{(\log q)^{j_{i}}}\times\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{|I_{i}|\varphi(q)\log q}{\varphi(q)}\right)^{j_{i}}

\displaystyle=(1+o_{\bm{k},\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(1))\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{k_{i}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k_i}{j_i}|I_{i}|^{j_{i}}\right)

and the theorem follows.

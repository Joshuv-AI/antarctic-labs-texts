Derivation of the Weizsäcker Density Functional from Probability Theory
Connor Dolan

Abstract
We demonstrate that the Weizsäcker potential is an exact term in the universal functional in density functional theory (DFT) for the ground state of a system with N N electrons. This proof uses no approximations or physical arguments, and follows from the form of kinetic energy of the ground state and probability theory. We also examine the form of the other terms in the kinetic energy.



I
Universal Functional

The Hohenberg-Kohn theorems state that the ground state of
N
electrons in a potential is determined uniquely by the electron density
Hohenberg and Kohn 1964
.
This implies the existence of a universal functional that accounts for the kinetic energy and electron-electron potential energies.

F_{N}[n]=T_{N}[n]+V_{ee,N}[n]

(1)

Where
T_{N}[n]
is the kinetic energy and
V_{ee,N}[n]
is the electron-electron interaction energy.

The existence of the universal functional implies that any term in
F_{N}[n]
found using first principles must
be an exact term in the the universal functional.

II
Kinetic Energy

The wavefunction
\Psi
for a system on
N
electrons is given by:

\Psi(r_{1},...,r_{N})=\sqrt{\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N})}e^{i\phi(r_{1},...,r_{N})}

(2)

Where
\rho
is the joint probability,
\phi
is the phase, and
r_{n}
is the position of the
n
-th electron.

The kinetic energy is given by
Sakurai and Napolitano 1994
:

\begin{split}T&=\int d^{3}r_{1}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\Psi^{*}(r_{1},...,r_{N})\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\nabla^{2}_{i}\right)\Psi(r_{1},...,r_{N})\\
&=-\frac{N}{2}\int d^{3}r_{1}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\Psi^{*}(r_{1},...,r_{N})\nabla^{2}_{1}\Psi(r_{1},...,r_{N})\\
&=\frac{N}{2}\int d^{3}r_{1}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N})\left({\left|\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N}))\right|}^{2}+{\left|\nabla_{1}\phi(r_{1},...,r_{N})\right|}^{2}\right)\\
\end{split}

(3)

Where we have used the fact that the wavefunction is antisymmetric to change variables for the Laplacian of the position of each electron to operate only
on
r_{1}
N times.

No other terms in the electron-electron interactions or the potential depend on the phase
\phi
. If we wish to find the ground state,
we minimize the energy, and the minimum of the phase dependent term in our kinetic energy is zero and occurs when the phase is a constant.

The kinetic energy for the ground state is then:

T=\frac{N}{8}\int d^{3}r_{1}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N}){\left|\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N}))\right|}^{2}

(4)

III
The Weizsäcker Functional

The joint probability distribution
\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N})
can be rewritten as a single electron probability distribution and a conditional probability:

\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N})=\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1})\rho(r_{1})

(5)

This gives us:

\begin{split}&\log(\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N}))=\log(\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1}))+\log(\rho(r_{1}))\\
\implies&\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N}))=\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1}))+\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{1}))\\
\end{split}

(6)

The kinetic energy is then:

\begin{split}T&=\frac{N}{8}\int d^{3}r_{1}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N})\Bigl({\left|\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{1}))\right|}^{2}\\
&+2\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{1}))\cdot\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1}))+{\left|\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1}))\right|}^{2}\Bigr)\\
\end{split}

(7)

Let us denote the three terms in the order they are shown as
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
and
T_{3}
so that
the kinetic energy is
T=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}
. The first term can be simplified:

\begin{split}T_{1}&=\frac{N}{8}\int d^{3}r_{1}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N})\left|\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{1}))\right|^{2}\\
&=\frac{N}{8}\int d^{3}r_{1}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1})\rho(r_{1})\left|\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{1}))\right|^{2}\\
&=\frac{N}{8}\int d^{3}r_{1}\rho(r_{1})\left|\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{1}))\right|^{2}\\
&=\frac{N}{8}\int d^{3}r\rho(r)\left|\nabla\log(\rho(r))\right|^{2}\\
&=\frac{N}{8}\int d^{3}r\frac{\left|\nabla\rho(r)\right|^{2}}{\rho(r)}\\
\end{split}

(8)

Where the conditional probability
\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1})
factors out and integrates to
1
, and we relabel
r_{1}
as
r
.

The density is equal to the number of electrons times the probability distribution of one electron,
n(r)=N\rho(r)
, so we obtain
the kinetic energy term:

T_{1}=\frac{1}{8}\int d^{3}r\frac{\left|\nabla n(r)\right|^{2}}{n(r)}\\

(9)

Which is the Weizsäcker functional
Weizsacker 1935
.

This means the Weizsäcker functional is an exact term in the universal density functional.

IV
Other Kinetic Energy Terms

We now examine the other terms in (
7
).
The second term
T_{2}
:

\begin{split}T_{2}&=\frac{N}{4}\int d^{3}r_{1}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N})\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{1}))\cdot\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1}))\\
&=\frac{N}{4}\int d^{3}r_{1}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1})\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{1}))\cdot\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1}))\\
&=\frac{N}{4}\int d^{3}r_{1}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\nabla_{1}\rho(r_{1})\cdot\nabla_{1}\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1})\\
&=\frac{N}{4}\int d^{3}r_{1}\nabla_{1}\rho(r_{1})\cdot\nabla_{1}\left(\int d^{3}r_{2}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1})\right)\\
&=\frac{N}{4}\int d^{3}r_{1}\nabla_{1}\rho(r_{1})\cdot\nabla_{1}1=0\\
\end{split}

(10)

T_{2}
vanishes.
T_{3}
gives us:

\begin{split}T_{3}&=\frac{N}{8}\int d^{3}r_{1}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\rho(r_{1},...,r_{N}){\left|\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1}))\right|}^{2}\\
&=\frac{N}{8}\int d^{3}r_{1}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1})\rho(r_{1}){\left|\nabla_{1}\log(\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r_{1}))\right|}^{2}\\
&=\int d^{3}r_{1}\rho(r_{1})T(r_{1})=\int d^{3}r\rho(r)T(r)\\
\end{split}

(11)

Where we relabeled
r_{1}
to
r
and defined a kinetic energy function given by:

T(r)=\frac{N}{8}\int d^{3}r_{2}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r){\left|\nabla\log(\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r))\right|}^{2}\\

(12)

The information metric
Caticha 2006
for the conditional probability distribution of all but one electron positions on that of the position of one electron,
is given by:

g_{ij}(r)=\int d^{3}r_{2}...\int d^{3}r_{N}\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r)\nabla_{i}\log(\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r))\nabla_{j}\log(\rho(r_{2},...,r_{N}|r))

(13)

We then see that the kinetic energy function
T(r)
is proportional to the trace of the information metric:

T(r)=\frac{N}{8}\sum_{i}g_{ii}(r)

(14)

So that
T_{3}
is given as:

T_{3}=\frac{N}{8}\int d^{3}r\rho(r)\sum_{i}g_{ii}(r)=\frac{1}{8}\int d^{3}rn(r)\sum_{i}g_{ii}(r)

(15)

This result substantiates previous work that finds the kinetic energy is proportional to the information metric
Ipek and Caticha 2016
,
and explorations of the relationship between Density Functional Theory and information physics more generally
Yousefi 2021
.

2.
Preliminaries

In the context of this paper, let
\mathcal{M}
be a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra on a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, equipped with a faithful and normal state denoted as
\varphi
. We shall briefly recall the definition of Haagerup noncommutative
L^{p}
-spaces associated with
\mathcal{M}
.

Let
\{\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be the one-parameter modular automorphism group of
\mathcal{M}
associated with
\varphi
and denote the crossed product of
\mathcal{M}
by this group as
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\sigma^{\varphi}}\mathbb{R}
. We consider the canonical embedding
\pi(M)
of
\mathcal{M}
into
\mathcal{N}
and identify
\mathcal{M}
with
\pi(\mathcal{M})
within
\mathcal{N}
. There is a dual action
\{\hat{\sigma}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathbb{R}
on
\mathcal{N}
. Then, we can express
\mathcal{M}
as the set of elements in
\mathcal{N}
that remain invariant under this dual action:

\mathcal{M}=\{x\in\mathcal{N}:\;\hat{\sigma}_{t}(x)=x,\;\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Recall that
{\mathcal{N}}
is semi-finite (cf.
[
26
]
), and therefore, there exists a normal, semi-finite, and faithful trace denoted as
\tau
defined on
\mathcal{N}
that satisfies the equation

\tau\circ\hat{\sigma}_{t}=e^{-t}\tau,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

Now, by denoting
L_{0}(\mathcal{N})
as the set of
\tau
-measurable operators affiliated with
\mathcal{N}
, we can define the Haagerup noncommutative
L^{p}
-spaces, where
0<p\leq\infty
, as follows

L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)=\{x\in L_{0}({\mathcal{N}},\tau):\;\hat{\sigma}_{t}(x)=e^{-\frac{t}{p}}x,\;\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

For the space
L^{1}({\mathcal{M}})
, it possesses a canonical trace functional denoted as
tr
, which is used to define a norm
\|x\|_{p}=tr(|x|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}
on
L^{p}({\mathcal{M}})
. The topology on
L^{p}({\mathcal{M}})
induced by this norm coincides with the relative topology of convergence in measure inherited by
L^{p}({\mathcal{M}})
from
L_{0}({\mathcal{N}})
. For
0<p<\infty
and any element
x
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
, we have the relationship

\mu_{t}(x)=t^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|x\|_{p},\qquad t>0,

(2.1)

where
\mu_{t}(\cdot)
is relative to
({\mathcal{N}},\tau)
(see
[
11
, Lemma 4.8]
).
Additionally, it is worth noting that

L^{\infty}({\mathcal{M}},\varphi)={\mathcal{M}}.

Finally, according to
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
, for any element
x\in\mathcal{M}
, we have that

\|x\|_{\infty}=\mu_{t}(x)\qquad\mbox{for all}\;0<t\leq 1.

Recall that the dual weight
\hat{\varphi}
of
\varphi
has a Radon-Nikodym derivative
D
with respect to
\tau
(cf.
[
26
]
). This
D
is an invertible positive self-adjoint operator affiliated with
\mathcal{N}
and satisfies the following relationships

\hat{\varphi}(x)=\tau(Dx),\quad x\in{\mathcal{N}}_{+},

\varphi(x)=tr(Dx),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{M}

and the modular automorphism group
\{\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is given
by

\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)=D^{it}xD^{-it},\quad x\in\mathcal{M},\;t\in\mathbb{R}.

It is a well-known fact that
L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is, up to isometry, independent of the choice of
\varphi
. Therefore, we will use the notation
L^{p}(\mathcal{M})
to denote the abstract Haagerup noncommutative
L^{p}
-space, i.e.,
L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
.

For
0<p\leq\infty
and a subset

, we denote by

the closed linear span of

within
L^{p}(\mathcal{M})
(relative to the w*-topology for

). For
0<p<\infty
and

, it can be obtained that

L^{p}(\mathcal{M})=[D^{\frac{1-\eta}{p}}{\mathcal{M}}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{p}

( refer to
[
19
, Lemma 1.1]
for details).

Next, consider the interval

(2.2)

Moreover, another useful representation in terms of the distribution function is given by

Now, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose

is a w*-closed subalgebra of

. We refer to

(i)

\mathcal{M}
,

(ii)

\mathcal{E}(xy)=\mathcal{E}(x)\mathcal{E}(y),\;\forall\;x,y\in\mathcal{A}
,

(iii)

\mathcal{A}\cap J(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{D}
,

We also refer to
\mathcal{D}
as the diagonal of
\mathcal{A}
.

It’s noteworthy that Arveson
[
1
]
does not assume subdiagonal subalgebras to be w*-weakly closed. However, since the weak* closure of a subalgebra that is subdiagonal subalgebra with respect to
\mathcal{E}

(as pointed out in Remark 2.1.2 in

18

\sigma
-finite, we

is equivalent to

(cf.
[

]

Henceforth, in the rest of this paper,

consistently denotes a normal faithful state that satisfies

.

is considered a maximal subdiagonal subalgebra of

with

\mathcal{E}
(or to

Additionally, we define

According to

\mathcal{A}=\{x\in\mathcal{M}:\;\mathcal{E}(yxz)=0,\;\forall y\in\mathcal{A},\;\forall z\in\mathcal{A}_{0}\}.

Using

of

with respect to

\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}({\mathcal{A}})={\mathcal{A}},\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}

(refer to

Throughout this paper, we will consistently denote

as a maximal subdiagonal subalgebra within

\mathcal{E}

Definition 2.3
.

For
0<p<\infty
, we define the Haagerup
noncommutative
H^{p}
-space that

\displaystyle H^{p}(\mathcal{A})=[\mathcal{A}D^{\frac{1}{p}}]_{p},\quad H^{p}_{0}(\mathcal{A})=[\mathcal{A}_{0}D^{\frac{1}{p}}]_{p}.

Let

. By
[
18

, we know that

H^{p}(\mathcal{A})=[D^{\frac{1-\eta}{p}}{\mathcal{A}}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{p},\quad H^{p}_{0}(\mathcal{A})=[D^{\frac{1-\eta}{p}}{\mathcal{A}}_{0}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{p}.

(2.5)

It is known that

L^{p}(\mathcal{D})=[D^{\frac{1-\eta}{p}}\mathcal{D}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{p},\quad\forall p\in(0,\infty),\;\forall\eta\in[1,0].

(2.6)

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
0<p<1,\;0\leq\eta\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle H^{p}(\mathcal{A})=[D^{\frac{1-\eta}{p}}{\mathcal{A}}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{p},\quad H^{p}_{0}(\mathcal{A})=[D^{\frac{1-\eta}{p}}{\mathcal{A}}_{0}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{p}.

Proof.

First assume that
\frac{1}{2}\leq p<1
. If
0<\eta\leq\frac{1}{2}
, by (
2.5
), we have that

\displaystyle[{\mathcal{A}}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{\frac{p}{\eta}}=[D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}{\mathcal{A}}]_{\frac{p}{\eta}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle H^{p}({\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[{\mathcal{A}}D^{\frac{1}{p}}]_{p}=[[{\mathcal{A}}D^{\frac{1}{2p}}]_{2p}D^{\frac{1}{2p}}]_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[D^{\frac{1}{2p}}{\mathcal{A}}]_{2p}D^{\frac{1}{2p}}]_{p}=[D^{\frac{1}{2p}}{\mathcal{A}}D^{\frac{1}{2p}}]_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[D^{\frac{1}{2p}}[D^{\frac{1}{2p}}{\mathcal{A}}]_{2p}]_{p}=[D^{\frac{1}{p}}{\mathcal{A}}]_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[D^{\frac{1-\eta}{p}}[D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}{\mathcal{A}}]_{\frac{p}{\eta}}]_{p}=[D^{\frac{1-\eta}{p}}[{\mathcal{A}}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{\frac{p}{\eta}}]_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[D^{\frac{1-\eta}{p}}{\mathcal{A}}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{p}.

If
\frac{1}{2}<\eta<1
, then
0<1-\eta<\frac{1}{2}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle H^{p}({\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[{\mathcal{A}}D^{\frac{1}{p}}]_{p}=[[{\mathcal{A}}D^{\frac{1-\eta}{2p}}]_{\frac{p}{1-\eta}}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[D^{\frac{1-\eta}{p}}{\mathcal{A}}]_{\frac{p}{1-\eta}}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{p}=[D^{\frac{1-\eta}{p}}{\mathcal{A}}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{p}.

In the case

, we use the result of the first case and similar method to get that

H^{p}(\mathcal{A})=[D^{\frac{1-\eta}{p}}{\mathcal{A}}D^{\frac{\eta}{p}}]_{p},\qquad\mbox{for each}\;0<\eta\leq 1.

Iterating
this procedure, we obtain the desired result.

The second result follows analogously.

Proposition 3.2

Proof.

[

\displaystyle g(z)=f(z)-(f(\theta)-i_{p_{1}}^{\eta}(x))=\sum_{k=1}^{n}g_{k}(z)i_{p_{1}}^{\eta}(y_{k}),

where

Therefore, there exist an natural number

and a sequence

such that for each

\displaystyle\|y_{k}-a_{k}\|_{p_{1}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k+1}C_{k}},

\displaystyle h(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}g_{k}(z)a_{k},\qquad l(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}g_{k}(z)(y_{k}-a_{k}).

whence

\displaystyle(\mbox{respectively,}\;\|f(\theta)\|_{p_{\theta}}^{p_{\theta}}\leq 1+O(\varepsilon^{p_{\theta}})).

\displaystyle\|i_{p_{\theta}}^{\eta}(x)\|_{\theta}\leq C\|i_{p_{\theta}}^{\eta}(x)\|_{p_{\theta}}.

Consider

and a sequence
\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
as described in
2.6
. Given that

[

[
2

2.6

and

\|a\|_{\theta}\leq C\|a\|_{p_{\theta}},\qquad\forall a\in H^{p_{\theta}}({\mathcal{A}}_{n})

with

Applying (2) of Theorem

The monotonicity of

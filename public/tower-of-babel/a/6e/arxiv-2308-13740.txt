Some New Results on Gaussian Product Inequalities
Qian-Qian Zhou, Han Zhao, Ze-Chun Hu, Renming Song

Abstract
The long-standing Gaussian product inequality (GPI) conjecture states that, for any centered ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} -valued Gaussian random vector ( X 1 , … , X n ) (X_{1},\dots,X_{n}) and any positive reals α 1 , … , α n \alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n} , 𝐄 ⁡ [ ∏ j = 1 n | X j | α j ] ≥ ∏ j = 1 n 𝐄 ⁡ [ | X j | α j ] {\bf E}[\prod_{j=1}^{n}|X_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}]\geq\prod_{j=1}^{n}{\bf E}[|X_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}] . In this paper, we present some related inequalities for centered ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} -valued Gaussian random vector ( X 1 , … , X n ) (X_{1},\dots,X_{n}) when { α 1 , … , α n } \{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\} contains both positive and negative numbers.



MSC:
Primary 60E15; secondary 62H12

Keywords:
Gaussian product inequality, Opposite
Gaussian product inequality, Gaussian hypergeometric function.

1
Introduction and main results

The Gaussian product inequality (GPI) conjecture can be traced back to
[
2
]
. An earlier form of this conjecture states
that for any centered
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Gaussian random vector
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}^{2m}\right]\geq\prod_{j=1}^{n}{\bf E}\left[X_{j}^{2m}\right],\ m\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.1)

Li and Wei
[
13
]
proposed the following general version of the GPI conjecture:
for any centered
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Gaussian random vector
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
and any positive reals
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n}|X_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\right]\geq\prod_{j=1}^{n}{\bf E}\left[|X_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\right].

(1.2)

The GPI is known to imply the real polarization conjecture in functional analysis and is related to the U-conjecture,
see
[
14
]
.

The GPI conjecture is still open. However, some special cases have been proved. Frenkel
[
5
]
proved, using
an algebraic method,
(
1.2
)
for
\alpha_{j}=2
,
j=1,\dots,n
, i.e., for any centered
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Gaussian random vector
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}^{2}\right]\geq\prod_{j=1}^{n}{\bf E}\left[X_{j}^{2}\right].

(1.3)

Malicet et al.
[
14
]
, among many other things, gave an analytic proof to (
1.3
).

It follows from
[
11
, Corollary 1.1, Theorem 3.1 and Remark 1.4]
that the GPI (
1.2
) holds for
n=2
, i.e.,
for any centered
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued Gaussian random vector
(X_{1},X_{2})
and any
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0
,

{\bf E}[|X_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}|X_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}}]\geq{\bf E}|X_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}]{\bf E}|X_{2}|^{\alpha_{1}}].

(1.4)

For the case
n=3
, Lan et al.
[
12
]
proved the following result using
hypergeometric functions:
for any
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and any centered
\mathbb{R}^{3}
-valued Gaussian random vector
(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[X_{1}^{2m_{1}}X_{2}^{2m_{2}}X_{3}^{2m_{2}}\right]\geq{\bf E}\left[X_{1}^{2m_{1}}\right]{\bf E}\left[X_{2}^{2m_{2}}\right]{\bf E}\left[X_{3}^{2m_{2}}\right].

(1.5)

Russell and Sun
[
20
]
proved that for any
m_{2},m_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
and any centered
\mathbb{R}^{3}
-valued Gaussian random vector
(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2m_{2}}X_{3}^{2m_{3}}\right]\geq{\bf E}\left[X_{1}^{2}\right]{\bf E}\left[X_{2}^{2m_{2}}\right]{\bf E}\left[X_{3}^{2m_{3}}\right].

(1.6)

Herry et al.
[
8
]
proved that
for any
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
and any centered
\mathbb{R}^{3}
-valued Gaussian random vector
(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[X_{1}^{2m_{1}}X_{2}^{2m_{2}}X_{3}^{2m_{3}}\right]\geq{\bf E}\left[X_{1}^{2m_{1}}\right]{\bf E}\left[X_{2}^{2m_{2}}\right]{\bf E}\left[X_{3}^{2m_{3}}\right].

(1.7)

Genest and Ouimet
[
6
]
proved that if there exists a matrix
C\in[0,+\infty)^{n\times n}
such that
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})=(Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n})C
in law, where
(Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n})
is
a standard
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Gaussian random vector, then

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}^{2m_{j}}\right]\geq\mathbf{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}X_{j}^{2m_{j}}\right]\mathbf{E}\left[\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}X_{j}^{2m_{j}}\right],\ m_{j}\in\mathbb{N},j=1,\ldots,n,\ \forall 1\leq k\leq n-1.

(1.8)

Russell and Sun
[
17
]
proved, among other things, that (
1.8
) holds if all the correlation coefficients of
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
are nonnegative.
Edelmann et al.
[
4
]
extended (
1.8
) to the
multi-variate gamma distributions. For other related work,
we refer to Genest and Ouiment
[
7
]
, and Russell and Sun
[
19
]
.

Wei
[
21
]
proved the following version of GPI: If
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
is a centered
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Gaussian random vector, then for any
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in(-1,0)
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n}|X_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\right]\geq{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}|X_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\right]{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}|X_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\right],\ \forall 1\leq k\leq n-1.

(1.9)

Russell and Sun
[
18
]
proved the following opposite GPI:
If
(X_{1},X_{2})
is a centered
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued Gaussian random vector, then for any
\alpha_{1}\in(-1,0)
and
\alpha_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}[|X_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}|X_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}}]\leq{\bf E}[|X_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}]{\bf E}[|X_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}}].

(1.10)

In
[
10
]
, several quantitative versions of the Gaussian product inequality for
n=2
were given.

In this paper,
we will prove several inequalities related to the GPI (
1.2
) when
n\geq 3
and
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}

contains both positive and negative numbers. Our main results are as follows.

Theorem 1.1

Let
\textbf{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
be
a centered
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Gaussian random vector with
correlation coefficients
\{\rho_{ij}|1\leq i<j\leq n\}
.
Then for any
\alpha_{1}\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}X_{i}^{2}\right]\geq\prod_{i=2}^{n}(1-\rho_{1i}^{2}){\bf E}[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}]\prod_{i=2}^{n}{\bf E}[X_{i}^{2}].

(1.11)

Remark 1.2

(i) By letting
\rho_{1i}=0
in (
1.11
) for any
i=2,\ldots,n
, we know that (
1.11
) implies (
1.3
).

(ii) For
n=2
, by (
1.10
) and (
1.11
), we know that for any real numbers
-1<\alpha_{1}<0,

\alpha_{2}>0
,

(1-\rho_{12}^{2}){\bf E}[|X_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}]{\bf E}[|X_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}}]\leq{\bf E}[|X_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}|X_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}}]\leq{\bf E}[|X_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}]{\bf E}[|X_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}}].

Theorem 1.3

Let
\textbf{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
be
a centered
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Gaussian random vector.
Then for any
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}\in(0,1)
and
\alpha_{n}\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}{\cdots}{|X_{n-1}|}^{-\alpha_{n-1}}{|X_{n}|}^{\alpha_{n}}\right]\leq{\bf E}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}{|X_{k}|}^{-\alpha_{k}}\right]{\bf E}[{|X_{n}|}^{\alpha_{n}}].

(1.12)

Remark 1.4

(i)
By taking
n=2
in (
1.12
), we get (
1.10
).

(ii)
If the covariance matrix
\Sigma
of
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
can be written as
MM^{\prime}
, where
M
is a lower triangular matrix and
M^{\prime}
stands for the transpose of
M
, then combining (
1.12
) and Wei
[
21
, Theorem 3.1]
, we get that
for any
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}\in(0,1)
and
\alpha_{n}\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}{\cdots}{|X_{n-1}|}^{-\alpha_{n-1}}{|X_{n}|}^{\alpha_{n}}\right]\leq\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{m_{j,j}}\right)^{\alpha_{j}}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}{\bf E}[|X_{j}|^{-\alpha_{j}}]{\bf E}[|X_{n}|].

(1.13)

Proposition 1.5

Let
\textbf{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{4})
be a centered
\mathbb{R}^{4}
-valued Gaussian random vector with correlation coefficients
\{\rho_{ij}|1\leq i<j\leq 4\}
.
Then for any
\alpha_{1}\in(0,1)
and
any positive even integers
\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}{|X_{3}|}^{\alpha_{3}}{|X_{4}|}^{\alpha_{4}}\right]\geq\prod_{i=2}^{4}(1-\rho_{1i}^{2})^{\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2}}{\bf E}[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}]\prod_{i=2}^{4}{\bf E}[{|X_{i}|}^{\alpha_{i}}].

(1.14)

By letting
\rho_{1i}=0
in (
1.14
) for any

i=2,3,4,
we easily get (
1.7
).

Proposition 1.6

Let
\textbf{X}=(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})
be a centered
\mathbb{R}^{3}
-valued Gaussian random vector with correlation coefficients
\{\rho_{ij}|1\leq i<j\leq 3\}
.
Then for any
\alpha_{1}\in(0,1)
and
\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\prod_{i=2}^{3}(1-\rho_{1i}^{2})^{\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2}}{\bf E}[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}]{\bf E}[{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]{\bf E}[{|X_{3}|}^{\alpha_{3}}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}{|X_{3}|}^{\alpha_{3}}\right]

(1.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textbf{C}_{\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}}{\bf E}[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}]{\bf E}[{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]{\bf E}[{|X_{3}|}^{\alpha_{3}}],\quad\quad

where

\textbf{C}_{\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha_{3}}{2})}
.

Proposition 1.7

Let
\textbf{X}=(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})
be a centered
\mathbb{R}^{3}
-valued Gaussian random vector with covariance matrix
\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&a&b\\

b&c&1\end{pmatrix}
. Then for any
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(0,1)
and
\alpha_{3}\in(0,\infty)

\displaystyle\frac{\det(\Sigma)}{1-a^{2}}{\bf E}[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}]{\bf E}[{|X_{2}|}^{-\alpha_{2}}]{\bf E}[{|X_{3}|}^{\alpha_{3}}]\leq{\bf E}[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}{|X_{2}|}^{-\alpha_{2}}{|X_{3}|}^{\alpha_{3}}]\leq{\bf E}[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}{|X_{2}|}^{-\alpha_{2}}]{\bf E}[{|X_{3}|}^{\alpha_{3}}].

(1.16)

In this section, we give the

2.1
Preliminaries

Lemma 2.1

(Bernstein
[
3
, Fact 2.8.16]
)
Let
A
be an
m\times m
invertible matrix,
B\in R^{m\times n}
,
C\in R^{n\times m}
,
D\in R^{n\times n}
, and
(D-CA^{-1}B)
be an
n\times n
invertible matrix. Then

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}A^{-1}+A^{-1}B(D-CA^{-1}B)^{-1}CA^{-1}&-A^{-1}B(D-CA^{-1}B)^{-1}\\
-(D-CA^{-1}B)^{-1}CA^{-1}&(D-CA^{-1}B)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma 2.2

(Bernstein
[
3
, Fact 2.8.17]
)
Let
A
be an
m\times m
invertible matrix,
B\in R^{m\times n}
,
C\in R^{n\times m}
,
D\in R^{n\times n}
, and
(A-BD^{-1}C)
be an
m\times m
invertible matrix. Then

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}(A-BD^{-1}C)^{-1}&-(A-BD^{-1}C)^{-1}BD^{-1}\\
-D^{-1}C(A-BD^{-1}C)^{-1}&D^{-1}+D^{-1}C(A-BD^{-1}C)^{-1}BD^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma 2.3

Suppose that
\Sigma=(\sigma_{ij})_{n\times n}=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{1}&\textbf{t}\\
\textbf{t}^{{}^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}

is a positive definite

n\times n
matrix with all diagonal elements equal to 1, where

\Sigma_{1}=(\sigma_{ij})_{(n-1)\times(n-1)}

is an
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix and

\textbf{t}=(\sigma_{1n},\dots,\sigma_{n-1\ n})^{{}^{\prime}}
,
then
\Sigma_{1}-\textbf{t}\textbf{t}^{{}^{\prime}}
is also a positive definite matrix.

Proof.

We prove this lemma by induction.
When
n=2
, we have

\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{1}&\sigma_{12}\\
\sigma_{21}&1\end{pmatrix}
,
where
\Sigma_{1}=1
and

\sigma_{12}=\sigma_{21}
. Thus
\Sigma_{1}-tt^{{}^{\prime}}=1-\sigma_{12}^{2}\textgreater 0

by the positive definiteness of
\Sigma
.
Thus, the result holds for
n=2.

Suppose that the result holds for
n-1(n\geq 3)
: If
\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{1}&\textbf{t}_{n-2}\\
\textbf{t}_{n-2}^{{}^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}_{(n-1)\times(n-1)}

is a positive definite
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix with all diagonal elements equal to 1,
where
\Sigma_{1}=(\sigma_{ij})_{(n-2)\times(n-2)}

is an
(n-2)\times(n-2)
matrix and

\textbf{t}_{n-2}=(\sigma_{1\,n-1},\dots,\sigma_{n-2\,n-1})^{{}^{\prime}}
,
then
{\Sigma_{1}}-\textbf{t}_{n-2}\textbf{t}_{n-2}^{{}^{\prime}}
is also a positive definite matrix.
We will prove that the result holds for
n
. Assuming that
\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{1}&\textbf{t}_{n-1}\\
\textbf{t}_{n-1}^{{}^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}_{n\times n}
is a
positive definite
n\times n
matrix with all diagonal elements equal to 1, where

\Sigma_{1}=(\sigma_{ij})_{(n-1)\times(n-1)}

is an
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix and

\textbf{t}_{n-1}=(\sigma_{1n},\dots,\sigma_{n-1\ n})^{{}^{\prime}}
.
It follows from
[
3
, Fact 2.14.3]
that
\det({\Sigma_{1}}-\textbf{t}_{n-1}\textbf{t}_{n-1}^{{}^{\prime}})=\det(\Sigma)\textgreater 0
.
Thus
we only need to prove that the
(n-2)
-th order principal submatrix of
({\Sigma_{1}}-\textbf{t}_{n-1}\textbf{t}_{n-1}^{{}^{\prime}})
is positively definite.

Note that

\displaystyle{\Sigma_{1}}-\textbf{t}_{n-1}\textbf{t}_{n-1}^{{}^{\prime}}=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}&\widetilde{{\bf t}}_{n-2}\\
\widetilde{{\bf t}}^{{}^{\prime}}_{n-2}&1\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\textbf{t}}_{n-2}\\
\sigma_{n-1\ n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\textbf{t}}_{n-2}^{{}^{\prime}}&\sigma_{n\ n-1}\end{pmatrix},

where
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}={(\sigma_{ij})}_{(n-2)\times(n-2)}
,

\widetilde{{\bf t}}_{n-2}=(\sigma_{1\ n-1},\ldots,\sigma_{n-2\ n-1})^{{}^{\prime}}
,
\hat{\textbf{t}}_{n-2}=(\sigma_{1n},\dots,\sigma_{n-2\ n})^{{}^{\prime}}
.
So the
(n-2)
-th order principal submatrix of
({\Sigma_{1}}-\textbf{t}_{n-1}\textbf{t}_{n-1}^{{}^{\prime}})
is

({\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}}-\hat{\textbf{t}}_{n-2}\hat{\textbf{t}}_{n-2}^{{}^{\prime}})
.
Note that
\begin{pmatrix}{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}}&\hat{\textbf{t}}_{n-2}\\
\hat{\textbf{t}}_{n-2}^{{}^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}
is the
(n-1)
-th order principal submatrix of
\Sigma
.
Since
\Sigma
is positive definite,
\begin{pmatrix}{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}}&\hat{\textbf{t}}_{n-2}\\
\hat{\textbf{t}}_{n-2}^{{}^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}
is also a positive definite matrix.
Now using the induction hypothesis, we get that
({\widetilde{\Sigma}_{1}}-\hat{\textbf{t}}_{n-2}\hat{\textbf{t}}_{n-2}^{{}^{\prime}})
is a positive definite matrix. The proof is complete.

2.2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Without loss of generality, we can assume that

X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are all standard Gaussian random variables.
Then, the desired assertion reduces to

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}X_{i}^{2}\right]\geq\prod_{i=2}^{n}(1-\rho_{1i}^{2}){\bf E}[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}].

(2.1)

Without loss of generality, we can assume that the covariance matrix
\Sigma
is
invertible and thus positive definite.
Otherwise, we can take the covariance matrix to be
\frac{1}{1+1/m}(\Sigma+\frac{1}{m}I_{n})
and then let
m\to\infty
. Hereafter,
I_{n}
stands for the
n
-dimensional identity matrix.

For any
t>0
, let
T
be the
n\times n
diagonal matrix
\mbox{diag}(t^{\frac{2}{\alpha_{1}}},0,\dots,0)
. Since
\Sigma
is positive definite, we know that
\Sigma^{-1}
is also positive definite and so

\Sigma^{-1}+2T
is also positive definite. Let
(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})
be a centered
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Gaussian random vector with covariance matrix
(\Sigma^{-1}+2T)^{-1}
and let

\displaystyle\hat{f}(x_{2},\dots,x_{n}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\textbf{x},(\Sigma^{-1}+2T)\textbf{x}\right\rangle)}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{\det((\Sigma^{-1}+2T)^{-1})}}dx_{1},

where
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
.

By the definition of the Gamma function and a change of variables, we have

\displaystyle{|u|}^{-\alpha}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-t^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}u^{2}\right)dt,\ \forall\alpha>0.

(2.2)

By (
2.2
), Fubini’s theorem and (
1.3
), we have

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[{|X_{1}|}^{-\alpha_{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}X_{i}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\bf E}\left[\exp\left(-t^{\frac{2}{\alpha_{1}}}X^{2}_{1}\right)\prod^{n}_{i=2}X_{i}^{2}\right]dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}x_{i}^{2}\exp(-\left\langle\textbf{x},T\textbf{x}\right\rangle)\frac{\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\textbf{x},\Sigma^{-1}\textbf{x}\right\rangle)}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{\det(\Sigma)}}d\textbf{x}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}x_{i}^{2}\frac{\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\textbf{x},(\Sigma^{-1}+2T)\textbf{x}\right\rangle)}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{\det((\Sigma^{-1}+2T)^{-1})}}d\textbf{x}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\det((\Sigma^{-1}+2T)^{-1})}}{\sqrt{\det(\Sigma)}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}[\det(I_{n}+2T\Sigma)]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}x_{i}^{2}\frac{\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\textbf{x},(\Sigma^{-1}+2T)\textbf{x}\right\rangle)}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{\det((\Sigma^{-1}+2T)^{-1})}}d\textbf{x}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}[\det(I_{n}+2T\Sigma)]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}x_{i}^{2}\hat{f}(x_{2},\dots,x_{n})dx_{2}\dots dx_{n}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1+2t^{\frac{2}{\alpha_{1}}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf E}\left[\prod_{i=2}^{n}Y_{i}^{2}\right]dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1+2t^{\frac{2}{\alpha_{1}}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}{\bf E}[Y_{i}^{2}]dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1+2t^{\frac{2}{\alpha_{1}}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}{\rm Var}(Y_{i})dt.

(2.3)

The covariance matrix
(\Sigma^{-1}+2T)^{-1}
of
(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})
can be written as
\begin{pmatrix}a&\mathbf{b}\\
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}&D\end{pmatrix}
, where
D

is an
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix and
\prod_{i=2}^{n}{\rm Var}(Y_{i})
is the product of the diagonal elements of the matrix
D.

We rewrite the matrix
\Sigma
as
\begin{pmatrix}1&{\bf t}\\
{\bf t}^{{}^{\prime}}&\Sigma_{1}\end{pmatrix}

with
\Sigma_{1}
being an
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix and
{\bf t}=(\rho_{12},\dots,\rho_{1n})
.
By the property of determinants of block matrices,
we have
\det(\Sigma)=\det(\Sigma_{1}-{\bf t}^{{}^{\prime}}{\bf t})>0
, which implies that
\Sigma_{1}-{\bf t}^{{}^{\prime}}{\bf t}
is invertible. Then, by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\Sigma^{-1}+2T=\begin{pmatrix}1+{\bf t}(\Sigma_{1}-{\bf t}^{{}^{\prime}}{\bf t})^{-1}{\bf t}^{{}^{\prime}}+2t^{\frac{2}{\alpha_{1}}}&-{\bf t}(\Sigma_{1}-{\bf t}^{{}^{\prime}}{\bf t})^{-1}\\
-(\Sigma_{1}-{\bf t}^{{}^{\prime}}{\bf t})^{-1}{\bf t}^{{}^{\prime}}&(\Sigma_{1}-{\bf t}^{{}^{\prime}}{\bf t})^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

(2.4)

Since
\Sigma^{-1}+2T
is positive definite, by (
2.4
) and Lemma
2.2

\displaystyle D

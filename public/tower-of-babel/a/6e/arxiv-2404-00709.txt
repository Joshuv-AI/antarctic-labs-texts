The Gaussian free-field as a stream function: continuum version of the scale-by-scale homogenization result
Peter Morfe, Felix Otto, Christian Wagner

Abstract
. This note is about a drift-diffusion process X X with a time-independent, divergence-free drift b b , where b b is a smooth Gaussian field that decorrelates over large scales. In two space dimensions, this just fails to fall into the standard theory of stochastic homogenization, and leads to a borderline super-diffusive behavior. In a previous paper by Chatzigeorgiou, Morfe, Otto, and Wang (2022), precise asymptotics of the annealed second moments of X X were derived by characterizing the asymptotics of the effective diffusivity λ L \lambda_{L} in terms of an artificially introduced large-scale cut-off L L . The latter was carried out by a scale-by-scale homogenization, and implemented by monitoring the corrector ϕ L \phi_{L} for geometrically increasing cut-off scales L + = M ​ L L^{+}=ML . In fact, proxies ( ϕ ~ L , σ ~ L ) (\tilde{\phi}_{L},\tilde{\sigma}_{L}) for the corrector and flux corrector were introduced incrementally and the residuum f L f_{L} estimated. In this short supplementary note, we reproduce the arguments of the above paper in the continuum setting of M ↓ 1 M\downarrow 1 . This has the advantage that the definition of the proxies ( ϕ ~ L , σ ~ L ) (\tilde{\phi}_{L},\tilde{\sigma}_{L}) becomes more transparent – it is given by a simple Itô SDE with ln ⁡ L \ln L acting as a time variable. It also has the advantage that the residuum f L f_{L} , which is a martingale, can be efficiently and precisely estimated by Itô calculus. This relies on the characterization of the quadratic variation of the (infinite-dimensional) Gaussian driver.

a=\frac{\exp(\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(x-1))}{x}\int_{1}^{x}dy\,(\frac{x}{y})^{\frac{3}{2}}\exp(\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(y-x))\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\exp(\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(x-1))}{x}.

We now argue that (
30
) holds.
Since
\tilde{\phi}
is a martingale we have

\displaystyle d\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\phi}|^{2}=\mathbb{E}[d\tilde{\phi}\cdot d\tilde{\phi}].

Inserting (
3

(32)

\displaystyle d\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\phi}|^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}[d\phi\cdot d\phi]+\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\tilde{\phi}^{i}\tilde{\phi}^{j}[\partial_{i}d\phi\cdot\partial_{j}d\phi].

Turning to the quadratic variation of the drivers, we have by isotropy

\displaystyle 2\tilde{\lambda}^{2}[\partial_{i}d\phi\cdot\partial_{j}d\phi]=\delta_{ij}\tilde{\lambda}^{2}[{\rm tr}\nabla d\phi(\nabla d\phi)^{*}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{wr18})}}{{=}}\delta_{ij}ds.

By the same argument that implied (

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}^{2}[d\phi\cdot d\phi]=L^{2}\tilde{\lambda}^{2}[{\rm tr}\nabla d\phi(\nabla d\phi)^{*}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{wr18})}}{{=}}L^{2}ds.

Inserting these two identities into (
32

25
)
gives (

Step 2: Evolution of
\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\sigma}|^{2}
.
We claim that

(35)

d\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\sigma}|^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\sigma}|^{2}\frac{ds}{2\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}+2\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\tilde{\sigma}\cdot J\tilde{\phi}\frac{ds}{\tilde{\lambda}}+\varepsilon^{2}L^{2}ds+\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}|\phi|^{2}ds,

which upon integration becomes

\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\sigma}|^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}L^{2}.

Indeed, using Young’s inequality
and (
31

d\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\sigma}|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\sigma}|^{2}\frac{ds}{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}L^{2}ds,

x\frac{d}{dx}\frac{b}{x}=\frac{db}{dx}-\frac{b}{x}\lesssim\exp(\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(x-1)),

b\lesssim\exp(\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(x-1))\int_{1}^{x}dy\,\frac{x}{y}\exp(\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(y-x)).

Now comes the argument for (

d|\tilde{\sigma}|^{2}=2\tilde{\sigma}\cdot d\tilde{\sigma}+[d\tilde{\sigma}\cdot d\tilde{\sigma}]

d\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\sigma}|^{2}=2\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\tilde{\sigma}\cdot J\tilde{\phi}\frac{ds}{\tilde{\lambda}}+\mathbb{E}[d\tilde{\sigma}\cdot d\tilde{\sigma}].

Let us observe that a parity argument similar to (
13

[d\tilde{\sigma}\cdot d\tilde{\sigma}]=\varepsilon^{2}\tilde{\sigma}^{i}\tilde{\sigma}^{j}[\partial_{i}d\phi\cdot\partial_{j}d\phi]+2\varepsilon^{2}\tilde{\sigma}^{i}\tilde{\phi}\cdot[d\psi\,\partial_{i}d\phi]+\varepsilon^{2}[(d\sigma+d\psi\,\tilde{\phi})\cdot(d\sigma+d\psi\,\tilde{\phi})].

Using (

[d\tilde{\sigma}\cdot d\tilde{\sigma}]=\varepsilon^{2}|\tilde{\sigma}|^{2}\frac{ds}{2\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}+\varepsilon^{2}\tilde{\sigma}\cdot J\tilde{\phi}\frac{ds}{\tilde{\lambda}}+\varepsilon^{2}[d\sigma\cdot d\sigma]+2\varepsilon^{2}\tilde{\phi}\cdot[d\psi\,d\sigma]+\varepsilon^{2}|\tilde{\phi}|^{2}[d\psi\,d\psi].

Since
\tilde{\phi}
has vanishing expectation, the mixed term
\tilde{\phi}\cdot[d\psi\,d\sigma]

d\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\sigma}|^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\sigma}|^{2}\frac{ds}{2\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}+2\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\tilde{\sigma}\cdot J\tilde{\phi}\frac{ds}{\tilde{\lambda}}+\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}[d\sigma\cdot d\sigma]+\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\phi}|^{2}ds.

\varepsilon^{2}[d\sigma\cdot d\sigma]=\varepsilon^{2}L^{2}ds,

We now give the argument for (

In view of (

[\tr\nabla d\sigma(\nabla d\sigma)^{*}]=2[d\psi\,d\psi]-\tilde{\lambda}^{2}[\tr\nabla d\phi(\nabla d\phi)^{*}]=ds

Step 3: Evolution of
\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\phi}|^{4}

We claim that

which integrates to

\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\phi}|^{4}\lesssim\varepsilon^{4}\frac{L^{4}}{\tilde{\lambda}^{4}}.

The integration follows along the lines of (

).

Itô’s formula implies

d|\tilde{\phi}|^{2}=2\tilde{\phi}\cdot d\tilde{\phi}+[\tilde{\phi}\cdot\tilde{\phi}],

which we combine with (

Since the covariation of
\phi

\displaystyle d|\tilde{\phi}|^{4}

\displaystyle=\text{martingale}+\varepsilon^{2}|\tilde{\phi}|^{4}\frac{ds}{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}+2\varepsilon^{2}|\tilde{\phi}|^{2}\frac{L^{2}ds}{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}

\displaystyle+4\varepsilon^{2}[(\tilde{\phi}\cdot d\phi)(\tilde{\phi}\cdot d\phi)]+4\varepsilon^{2}[(\tilde{\phi}^{i}\tilde{\phi}\cdot\partial_{i}d\phi)(\tilde{\phi}^{j}\tilde{\phi}\cdot\partial_{j}d\phi)],

\displaystyle d\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\phi}|^{4}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\phi}|^{4}\frac{ds}{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}+2\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}|\tilde{\phi}|^{2}\frac{L^{2}ds}{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}

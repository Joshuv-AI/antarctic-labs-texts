Deepest nodes in marked ordered trees
Helmut Prodinger

Abstract
. A variation of ordered trees, where each rightmost edge might be marked or not, if it does not lead to an endnode, is investigated. These marked ordered trees were introduced by E. Deutsch et al. to model skew Dyck paths. We study the number of deepest nodes in such trees. Explicit generating functions are established and the average number of deepest nodes, which approaches 5 3 \frac{5}{3} when the number of nodes gets large. This is to be compared to standard ordered trees where the average number of deepest nodes approaches 2 2 .

3.
Continuing with exact analysis

First, note that
R|_{t=0}=\dfrac{-v^{2}-3v-1}{-v^{2}-3v-1}=1
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{p_{h}(z,t)-p_{h}(z,0)}{z(1+v)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-Rvq^{h-2}}{1-Rv^{2}q^{h-2}}-\frac{1-vq^{h-2}}{1-v^{2}q^{h-2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-Rvq^{h-2})(1-v^{2}q^{h-2})-(1-vq^{h-2})(1-Rv^{2}q^{h-2})}{(1-Rv^{2}q^{h-2})(1-v^{2}q^{h-2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-v)v(1-R)q^{h-2}}{(1-Rv^{2}q^{h-2})(1-v^{2}q^{h-2})}

\displaystyle=(1-v)v\bigg[\frac{q^{h-2}}{1-v^{2}q^{h-2}}-\frac{Rq^{h-2}}{1-Rv^{2}q^{h-2}}\bigg],

or

\displaystyle p_{h}(z,t)-p_{h}(z,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{z(1-v^{2})}{v}\bigg[\frac{v^{2}q^{h-2}}{1-v^{2}q^{h-2}}-\frac{Rv^{2}q^{h-2}}{1-Rv^{2}q^{h-2}}\bigg].

Summing,

\displaystyle\sum_{h\geq 1}\Big(p_{h+1}(z,t)-p_{h+1}(z,0)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{z(1-v^{2})}{v}\sum_{h\geq 1}\bigg[\frac{v^{2}q^{h-1}}{1-v^{2}q^{h-1}}-\frac{Rv^{2}q^{h-1}}{1-Rv^{2}q^{h-1}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{z(1-v^{2})}{v}\sum_{h\geq 1}\bigg[\frac{\delta q^{h}}{1-\delta q^{h}}-\frac{R\delta q^{h}}{1-R\delta q^{h}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{z(1-v^{2})}{v}\sum_{k\geq 1}\bigg[\frac{\delta^{k}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}-\frac{R^{k}\delta^{k}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{z(1-v^{2})}{v}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\delta^{k}q^{k}(1-R^{k})}{1-q^{k}}

with

\delta=\frac{v(2v+1)}{v+2}.

Using the binomial theorem,

R^{k}-1=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k}{i}{\bigg(\frac{v-1}{v}\frac{t(1+v)z}{1-t(1+v)z}\bigg)}^{i}.

Putting things together,

\displaystyle G(z,t)-zt

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\geq 1}\Big(p_{h+1}(z,t)-p_{h+1}(z,0)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{z(v^{2}-1)}{v}\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}\binom{k}{i}{\bigg(\frac{v-1}{v}\frac{t(1+v)z}{1-t(1+v)z}\bigg)}^{i}\frac{\delta^{k}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}.

The generating function is now fully explicit.

.

The generating function

refers to the number of marked ordered trees with

G(z,t)=zt+\frac{z(v^{2}-1)}{v}\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}\binom{k}{i}{\bigg(\frac{v-1}{v}\frac{t(1+v)z}{1-t(1+v)z}\bigg)}^{i}\frac{\delta^{k}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}},

with
z=\dfrac{v}{1+3v+v^{2}}
,
q=\dfrac{v(v+2)}{2v+1}
, and
\delta=\dfrac{v(2v+1)}{v+2}
.∎

4.
Asymptotics

For the following, we refer to
[
4
]
and use a hybrid approach, first the Mellin transform, to establish to local behaviour, and then singularity analysis to switch to
the behaviour of the coefficients. The book
[
6
]
is of course also relevant here.

The goal is to find the behaviour of

\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{k\delta^{k}q^{2k}}{1-q^{k}}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{kv^{2k}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}=\sum_{k,j\geq 1}kv^{2k}q^{jk}

as
z\to\frac{1}{5}
, or
v\to 1
. First, we start with the simpler sum

\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{kq^{3k}}{1-q^{k}}

and discuss later that the difference is negligible. We set
q=e^{-w}
. Then we deal with

\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{ke^{-3kw}}{1-e^{-kw}}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{j\geq 3}ke^{-kjw}.

The Mellin transform of this is then
\Gamma(s)\zeta(s-1)\bigl(\zeta(s)-1-\frac{1}{2^{s}}\bigr)
, and the next step is to find the residues of

\Gamma(s)\zeta(s-1)(\zeta(s)-1-\frac{1}{2^{s}})w^{-s}
left to the line
\Re s=\frac{3}{2}
, say. We compute these residues at
s=1

and
s=0
(with a computer), with the (cumulative) result
-\frac{1}{2w}+\frac{5}{24}
. But
w=-\log(q)
, and we expand

\frac{{(1-v^{2})}^{2}}{(v+2)v(2v+1)}\Big(\frac{1}{2\log(q)}+\frac{5}{24}\Big)

around
v=1
, with the result

-\frac{1}{3}(1-v)-\frac{2}{27}(1-v)^{2}+\cdots.

One could from this translate to an expansion about
1-5z
, but it is not necessary, since the generating function of the
marked ordered trees, established to be
z(1+v)
, which we need for normalization, is
\sim\frac{2}{5}-\frac{1}{5}(1-v)+\cdots
, and the quotient
(-\frac{1}{3})/(-\frac{1}{5})=\frac{5}{3}
is the average number of deepest nodes (leading term), when all trees of size
n
are considered to be equally likely and
n
gets large.

as

The Modular Stone-von Neumann Theorem
Lucas Hall, Leonard Huang, John Quigg

Abstract
. In this paper, we use the tools of nonabelian duality to formulate and prove a far-reaching generalization of the Stone-von Neumann Theorem to modular representations of actions and coactions of locally compact groups on elementary C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras. This greatly extends the Covariant Stone-von Neumann Theorem for Actions of Abelian Groups recently proven by L. Ismert and the second author. Our approach is based on a new result about Hilbert C ∗ C^{\ast} -modules that is simple to state yet is widely applicable and can be used to streamline many previous arguments, so it represents an improvement — in terms of both efficiency and generality — in a long line of results in this area of mathematical physics that goes back to J. von Neumann’s proof of the classical Stone-von Neumann Theorem.

Introduction

In a groundbreaking 1925 paper (
[
9
]
), W. Heisenberg introduced the very first version of quantum mechanics, known as “matrix mechanics”. According to matrix mechanics, classical observables (continuous real-valued functions on a phase space describing observable properties of classical particles) were to be replaced by quantum observables (self-adjoint unbounded operators on a Hilbert space describing observable properties of quantum particles), and all quantum phenomena could be mathematically explained by the noncommutativity of certain pairs of quantum observables, as expressed by the Heisenberg Commutation Relation (HCR):

\left[A,B\right]\coloneqq AB-BA=i\hbar\cdot\mathbf{1}.

A well-known example of a pair of quantum observables that satisfies the HCR is
\left(X,P\right)
, where
X
and
P
are the position and momentum operators on the Hilbert space
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, defined by

\displaystyle\left(X\psi\right)\left(x\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq x\psi(x),

\displaystyle\left(P\psi\right)\left(x\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-i\hbar\psi^{\prime}\left(x\right).

Note that
X
and
P
are not bounded operators on
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
. Rather, they are unbounded operators on
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, where we may take the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

(for example)
to be a suitable dense common domain.

In 1926, E. Schrödinger published his famous equation that formed the cornerstone of a second version of quantum mechanics, known as “wave mechanics” (
[
30
]
). As matrix mechanics and wave mechanics made the same experimental predictions, a mathematical equivalence between them was immediately suspected. Schrödinger attempted to prove this equivalence in
[
31
]
, but his proof failed to meet the standards of mathematical rigor due to its inability to address technical domain issues surrounding unbounded operators.

To overcome the challenges faced by Schrödinger, one may take any pair
\left(A,B\right)
of self-adjoint unbounded operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
that satisfies the HCR and use Stone’s Theorem to exponentiate them, thus obtaining (strongly continuous) one-parameter unitary groups
U
and
V
on
\mathcal{H}
:

U=(e^{ixA})_{x\in\mathbb{R}},\qquad V=(e^{iyB})_{y\in\mathbb{R}}.

It can then be shown that the pair
\left(U,V\right)
satisfies the so-called “Weyl Commutation Relation (WCR)” on
\mathcal{H}
:

\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}:\quad V_{y}U_{x}=e^{ixy}U_{x}V_{y}.

The Stone-von Neumann Theorem — formulated by M. Stone in
[
33
]
and rigorously demonstrated by J. von Neumann in
[
22
]
— states that
\left(U,V\right)
can be completely described in terms of the pair
\left(\lambda,\operatorname{Mod}\right)
, where
\lambda
and
\operatorname{Mod}
are unitary groups on
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
obtained by exponentiating (via Stone’s Theorem) the pair
\left(X,P\right)
. These are called, respectively, the “left regular representation” and the “phase modulation” of
\mathbb{R}
. The theorem rigorously proves the equivalence between matrix mechanics and wave mechanics, and is a fundamental result in the mathematical theory of nonrelativistic quantum mechanics.

The following is a rather general version of the Stone-von Neumann Theorem.

Theorem 0.1

(Stone-von Neumann Theorem
[
22
]
)
.

Let
\left(U,V\right)
be a pair of unitary representations of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
that satisfies the WCR, i.e,

\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\quad V_{y}U_{x}=e^{i\left\langle x,y\right\rangle}U_{x}V_{y}.

Let
\lambda
and
\operatorname{Mod}
be the unitary representations of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
on the Hilbert space
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
defined by

\lambda_{x}\left(f\right)\coloneqq f\left(\cdot-x\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{Mod}_{y}\left(f\right)\coloneqq e^{i\left\langle\cdot,y\right\rangle}f.

Then
\left(\lambda,\operatorname{Mod}\right)
satisfies the WCR on
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.
Moreover,
there exist a set
I
and a unitary map
W:\mathcal{H}\to\bigoplus_{i\in I}L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

WU_{x}W^{-1}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\lambda_{x}\quad\text{and}\quad WV_{y}W^{-1}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\operatorname{Mod}_{y}.

The Stone-von Neumann Theorem thus completely classifies — up to unitary equivalence — all pairs of unitary Hilbert-space representations of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that satisfy the WCR.

In
[
19
]
, G. Mackey noted that as
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is its own Pontryagin dual, the WCR could be formulated for locally compact abelian groups in general. He then proceeded to formulate and establish the Stone-von Neumann Theorem for the class of all separable locally compact abelian groups, where the separability hypothesis was required by the measure-theoretic machinery of his proof. Shortly after, in
[
18
]
, L.H. Loomis was able to remove this superfluous hypothesis with a new proof that did not rely on measure theory. Finally, in
[
28
]
, M. Rieffel found a mostly algebraic proof containing rudiments of the concept of Morita equivalence for
C^{\ast}
-algebras, which he went on to develop more fully in
[
29
]
. It is believed by most operator algebraists that the proper way to view the Mackey-Stone-von Neumann Theorem is as a Morita-equivalence result.

To give an efficient formulation of the Mackey-Stone-von Neumann Theorem, let us define Heisenberg and Schrödinger representations.

Definition 0.2

(
[
19
,
28
]
)
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group. Then a

G
-representation
is defined as a triple
\left(\mathcal{H},U,V\right)
with the following properties:

(1)

\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space.

(2)

\mathcal{H}
.

(3)

is a unitary representation of
\widehat{G}
on

.

(4)

A Heisenberg

-representation

is said to be

if and only if there is a unitary map

such that for all

-representations.

Definition 0.3

(
[
19
,
28
]
)
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group. Then the
Schrödinger

-representation
is the triple

(L^{2}\left(G\right),\lambda,\operatorname{Mod}),

where
\lambda
and
\operatorname{Mod}
are, respectively, the unitary representations of

and
\widehat{G}
on the Hilbert space

defined by:

\lambda_{x}\left(f\right)\coloneqq f(x^{-1}\cdot)\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{Mod}_{\varphi}\left(f\right)\coloneqq\varphi f.

Theorem 0.4

(
[
19
,
28
]
)
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group. Then the following statements are true:

(1)

The Schrödinger
G
-representation is a Heisenberg
G
-representation.

(2)

Let
I
be a set and
\left(\mathcal{H}_{i},U_{i},V_{i}\right)_{i\in I}
an

-representations. Then the direct sum

\bigoplus_{i\in I}\left(\mathcal{H}_{i},U_{i},V_{i}\right)\coloneqq\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{H}_{i},\bigoplus_{i\in I}U_{i},\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}\right)

-representation.

Theorem 0.5

(Mackey-Stone-von Neumann Theorem
[
19
,
28
]
)
.

Let

-representation.

Several generalizations of the Mackey-Stone-von Neumann Theorem have appeared in the literature over time. They deal with nonregular group representations (
[
4
]
) or nonabelian duality (
[
11
,
23
]
). As the theory of Hilbert
C^{\ast}
-modules was already highly developed, it seemed natural to expect that the theorem could also be generalized to the setting of Hilbert
C^{\ast}
-modules, but heuristic arguments against the feasibility of such a generalization were made in
[
1
]
, owing to the lack of a spectral decomposition theorem for Hilbert
C^{\ast}
-modules.

In
[
10
]
, L. Ismert and the second author proposed an alternative way of viewing the Mackey-Stone-von Neumann Theorem. Their main idea was that the theorem should be viewed as a special case of a far more general result about the uniqueness of representations of abelian
C^{\ast}
-dynamical systems on Hilbert
C^{\ast}
-modules. For a
C^{\ast}
-dynamical system
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
with
A
an elementary
C^{\ast}
-algebra (i.e., isomorphic to the compacts on some Hilbert space) and
G
abelian, they were able to completely classify, up to unitary equivalence, all quadruples
\left(X,\pi,U,V\right)
with the following properties:

(1)

X
is a Hilbert
A
-module.

(2)

\left(X,\pi,U\right)
is a covariant representation of
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
.

(3)

\left(X,\pi,V\right)
is a covariant representation of
(A,\widehat{G},\iota)
, where
\iota
denotes the trivial action of
\widehat{G}
on
A
.

(4)

\left(U,V\right)
satisfies the WCR on
X
.

In the present paper, we further promote the paradigm shift in
[
10
]
, streamlining many of their arguments to present what may reasonably be thought of as an abstract Stone-von Neumann Theorem, which we state in terms of Hilbert modules over elementary
C^{\ast}
-algebras. Notably, this abstract characterization makes no mention of groups but largely takes on the flavor of the von Neumann Uniqueness Theorem (cf.
[
34
]
*Theorem 4.29). This abstract characterization offers more flexibility, allowing us to consider dynamical systems involving arbitrary locally compact groups, successfully removing the abelian hypothesis through the tools of nonabelian duality.

We provide a brief overview of nonabelian duality in §
1
as preparation for the parallel sections §
3
and §
4
involving the Covariant Stone von-Neumann Theorem for Actions and Coactions respectively.

.

(Abstract Modular Stone-von Neumann Theorem)
.

is elementary and

3.
The Covariant Stone-von Neumann Theorem for Actions

The Covariant Stone-von Neumann Theorem for Actions of Abelian Groups formulated and proven in
[
10
]
generalizes the Mackey-Stone-von Neumann Theorem by replacing Hilbert spaces by Hilbert
C^{\ast}
-modules as the basis of representations. However, the theorem is more than just a statement about representations of a locally compact abelian group
G
and its dual
\widehat{G}
on a Hilbert
C^{\ast}
-module, as the WCR alone suggests. As a Hilbert
C^{\ast}
-module is a right module
X
over a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
A
, one can introduce a strongly continuous action
\alpha
of
G
on
A
and attempt to classify “representations” of the
C^{\ast}
-dynamical system
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
on
X
, assuming one knows precisely what these “representations” are. The next definition seeks to realize this assumption.

Definition 3.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
be a
C^{\ast}
-dynamical system with
G
abelian. Then an
abelian Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation
is defined as a quadruple
\left(X,\pi,U,V\right)
with the following properties:

(1)

X
is a Hilbert
A
-module.

(2)

\left(X,\pi,U\right)
is a covariant representation of
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
.

(3)

\left(X,\pi,V\right)
is a covariant representation of
(A,\widehat{G},\iota)
, where
\iota
denotes the trivial action of
\widehat{G}
on
A
.

(4)

\left(U,V\right)
satisfies the WCR on
X
: For all
x\in G
and
\varphi\in\widehat{G}
,

V_{\varphi}U_{x}=\varphi\left(x\right)U_{x}V_{\varphi}.

An abelian Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation
\left(X,\pi,U,V\right)
is said to be
equivalent
to another one
\left(X^{\prime},\pi^{\prime},U^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right)
if and only if there is a unitary map
W:X\to X^{\prime}
such that for all
a\in A
,
x\in G
, and
\varphi\in\widehat{G}
,

W\pi\left(a\right)W^{-1}=\pi^{\prime}\left(a\right),\quad WU_{x}W^{-1}=U^{\prime}_{x},\quad\text{and}\quad WV_{\varphi}W^{-1}=V^{\prime}_{\varphi},

in which case we write
\left(X,\pi,U,V\right)\simeq\left(X^{\prime},\pi^{\prime},U^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right)
. Note that
\simeq
is an equivalence relation on the class of all abelian Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representations.

Note that in the definition above, in addition to the WCR, there are two commutation relations coming from the covariant representations in Properties (
2
) and (
3
). These two relations are novel features of the Covariant Stone-von Neumann Theorem for Actions of Abelian Groups that do not arise in the Mackey-Stone-von Neumann Theorem.

Definition 3.2

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
be a
C^{\ast}
-dynamical system with
G
abelian. Then the
abelian Schrödinger
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation
is the quadruple

(A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right),\left(\operatorname{id}\otimes M\right)\circ\widetilde{\alpha},1\otimes\lambda,1\otimes\operatorname{Mod}),

where
(A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right),\left(\operatorname{id}\otimes M\right)\circ\widetilde{\alpha},1\otimes\lambda)
is the regular covariant representation of
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
(defined in the introduction) and
\operatorname{Mod}
is the phase modulation of
G
, as in Definition
0.3
.

The following theorem is reminiscent of Theorem
0.4
.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
be a
C^{\ast}
-dynamical system with
G
abelian. Then the following statements are true:

(1)

The abelian Schrödinger
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation is an abelian Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation.

(2)

Let
I
be a set and
\left(X_{i},\pi_{i},U_{i},V_{i}\right)_{i\in I}
an
I

\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representations. Then the direct sum

\bigoplus_{i\in I}\left(X_{i},\pi_{i},U_{i},V_{i}\right)_{i\in I}\coloneqq\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}X_{i},\bigoplus_{i\in I}\pi_{i},\bigoplus_{i\in I}U_{i},\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}\right)

is also an abelian Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation.

The Covariant Stone-von Neumann Theorem for Actions of Abelian Groups can now be stated concisely as follows.

Theorem 3.4

(Covariant Stone-von Neumann Theorem for Actions of Abelian Groups
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
be a
C^{\ast}
-dynamical system with
A
an elementary
C^{\ast}
-algebra and
G
abelian. Then every abelian Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation is a multiple of the abelian Schrödinger
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation.

In order to generalize the Covariant Stone-von Neumann Theorem for Actions of Abelian Groups to include actions of nonabelian groups, we must first reformulate the WCR in nonabelian terms.

Let
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
be a
C^{\ast}
-dynamical system with
G
abelian, and let the following data be given:

•

A Hilbert
A
-module
X
.

•

A unitary representation
U
of
G
on
X
.

•

A unitary representation
V
of
\widehat{G}
on
X
.

•

A representation
\pi
of
A
by adjointable operators on
X
.

Let
\widetilde{V}
denote the integrated form of
V
, which is a representation of
C^{\ast}(\widehat{G})
on
X
. Letting
\mathcal{F}:C^{\ast}(\widehat{G})\to C_{0}\left(G\right)
denote the
\widehat{G}
-Fourier transform, we get a representation
\mu\coloneqq\widetilde{V}\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}
of
C_{0}\left(G\right)
on

. It is a standard consequence of properties of Fourier transforms that
\left(U,V\right)
satisfies the WCR if and only if
\left(X,\mu,U\right)
is a covariant representation of
\left(C_{0}\left(G\right),G,\textnormal{lt}\right)
(see
[
34
]
*Lemma 4.28, for example).

This reformulation of the WCR does not appeal to the abelian nature of

and can thus be applied even to nonabelian groups. Moreover,
\left(X,\pi,V\right)
is a covariant representation of
(\widehat{G},A,\iota)
if and only if
\pi
and
\mu
commute.

These observations motivate the following alternative definition of a modular representation of a

-dynamical system, which makes sense even if the underlying group is not abelian.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
be a
C^{\ast}
-dynamical system. Then a
Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation
is a quadruple
\left(X,\pi,U,\mu\right)
with the following properties:

(1)

X
is a Hilbert
A
-module.

(2)

\left(X,\pi,U\right)
is a covariant representation of
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
.

(3)

\left(X,\mu,U\right)
is a covariant representation of
\left(C_{0}\left(G\right),G,\textnormal{lt}\right)
.

(4)

\pi
and
\mu
commute.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
be a
C^{\ast}
-dynamical system, and let
\widehat{\alpha}
denote the dual coaction of
G
on
A\rtimes_{\alpha}G
. Then

\left(X,\pi,U,\mu\right)\mapsto\left(X,\pi\times U,\mu\right)

gives a bijection from the Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representations to the covariant representations of
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G,G,\widehat{\alpha}\right)
. Consequently,

\left(X,\pi,U,\mu\right)\mapsto\left(X,\left(\pi\times U\right)\times\mu\right)

gives a bijection from the Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representations to the nondegenerate representations of
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\right)\rtimes_{\widehat{\alpha}}G
.

Proof.

By
[
7
]
*Proposition A.63 and the discussion preceding it (see also
[
26
]
*Examples 2.9),
\left(X,\pi,U,\mu\right)
is a Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation if and only if
\left(X,\pi\times U,\mu\right)
is a covariant representation of
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G,G,\widehat{\alpha}\right)
. Since the latter is equivalent to

\left(X,\left(\pi\times U\right)\times\mu\right)
being a nondegenerate representation of
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\right)\rtimes_{\widehat{\alpha}}G
by definition, we are done.
∎

Definition 3.7
.

A Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation
\left(X,\pi,U,\mu\right)
is said to be
equivalent
to another one
\left(X^{\prime},\pi^{\prime},U^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}\right)
if and only if there is a unitary map
W:X\to X^{\prime}
such that for all
a\in A
,
x\in G
, and
f\in C_{0}\left(G\right)
,

W\pi\left(a\right)W^{-1}=\pi^{\prime}\left(a\right),\quad WU_{x}W^{-1}=U^{\prime}_{x},\quad\text{and}\quad W\mu\left(f\right)W^{-1}=\mu^{\prime}\left(f\right),

in which case we write
\left(X,\pi,U,\mu\right)\simeq\left(X^{\prime},\pi^{\prime},U^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}\right)
.

Note that
\simeq
is an equivalence relation on the class of all Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representations. Heisenberg modular representations are equivalent if and only if the associated representations of the double crossed product are equivalent.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
be a
C^{\ast}
-dynamical system. Then the
Schrödinger
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation
is the quadruple

(A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right),\left(\operatorname{id}\otimes M\right)\circ\widetilde{\alpha},1\otimes\lambda,1\otimes M).

Note:
If
G
is abelian, then
M=\mu_{\operatorname{Mod}}
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
be a
C^{\ast}
-dynamical system. Then the following statements are true:

(1)

The Schrödinger
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation is a Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation.

(2)

Let
I
be a set and
\left(X_{i},\pi_{i},U_{i},\mu_{i}\right)_{i\in I}
an
I
-indexed family of Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representations. Then the direct sum

\bigoplus_{i\in I}\left(X_{i},\pi_{i},U_{i},\mu_{i}\right)\coloneqq\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}X_{i},\bigoplus_{i\in I}\pi_{i},\bigoplus_{i\in I}U_{i},\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mu_{i}\right)

is also a Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation.

Proof.

Statement (1) is true by virtue of the following facts:

•

The regular covariant representation of
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
is precisely

(A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right),\left(\operatorname{id}\otimes M\right)\circ\widetilde{\alpha},1\otimes\lambda).

•

\left(L^{2}\left(G\right),M,\lambda\right)
is a covariant representation of
\left(C_{0}\left(G\right),G,\textnormal{lt}\right)
.

•

\left(\operatorname{id}\otimes M\right)\circ\widetilde{\alpha}
commutes with
1\otimes M
as
C_{0}\left(G\right)
is commutative.

Statement (2) follows routinely from the definitions.
∎

By Lemma
3.6
and Proposition
3.9
, the direct sum of Heisenberg modular representations is associated to the direct sum of their associated representations of the double crossed product. In particular, a Heisenberg modular representation
\left(X,\pi,U,\mu\right)
is a multiple of another one
\left(X^{\prime},\pi^{\prime},U^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}\right)
if and only if the associated representation
\left(\pi\times U\right)\times\mu
of the double crossed product is a multiple of
\left(\pi^{\prime}\times U^{\prime}\right)\times\mu^{\prime}
.

Proposition 3.10

(Covariant Stone-von Neumann Theorem for Actions)
.

Let
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
be a
C^{\ast}
-dynamical system with
A
an elementary
C^{\ast}
-algebra. Then every Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation is a multiple of the Schrödinger
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation.

Proof.

Let
\left(X,\pi,U,\mu\right)
be a Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation. Imai-Takai Duality says that the integrated form of the Schrödinger
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation

(A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right),\left(\operatorname{id}\otimes M\right)\circ\widetilde{\alpha},1\otimes\lambda,1\otimes M)

is a
C^{\ast}
-isomorphism from
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\right)\rtimes_{\widehat{\alpha}}G
to
A\otimes\mathcal{K}\left(L^{2}\left(G\right)\right)
. Using this
C^{\ast}
-isomorphism and the identification

\mathcal{K}(A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right))\cong A\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}\left(G\right)),

the
\mathcal{K}\left(A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right)\right)
-
A
imprimitivity bimodule
A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right)
becomes an
\left(\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\right)\rtimes_{\widehat{\alpha}}G\right)
-
A
imprimitivity bimodule, and
\phi_{A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right)}
becomes the integrated form of the Schrödinger
\left(A,G,\alpha\right)
-modular representation. The Covariant Stone-von Neumann Theorem for Actions then follows from the paragraph preceding this proposition.
∎

4.
The Covariant Stone-von Neumann Theorem for Coactions

In this section, we apply the Abstract Modular Stone-von Neumann Theorem again to crossed-product duality, this time starting with a coaction rather than an action. The development is parallel to that in Section
3
, and we significantly streamline the presentation.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a coaction
\left(A,G,\delta\right)
, a
Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\delta\right)
-modular representation
is a quadruple
\left(X,\pi,\mu,U\right)
with the following properties:

•

\left(X,\pi,\mu\right)
is a covariant representation of
\left(A,G,\delta\right)
.

•

\left(X,\mu,U\right)
is a covariant representation of
\left(C_{0}\left(G\right),G,\textnormal{rt}\right)
.

•

\pi
and
U
commute.

Lemma 4.2
.

With the above notation, the assignments
\left(X,\pi,\mu,U\right)\mapsto\left(X,\pi\times\mu,U\right)
and
\left(X,\pi,\mu,U\right)\mapsto\left(X,\left(\pi\times\mu\right)\times U\right)
give bijections from the Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\delta\right)
-modular representations to the covariant representations of
(A\rtimes_{\delta}G,G,\widehat{\delta})
and the nondegenerate representations of
\left(A\rtimes_{\delta}G\right)\rtimes_{\widehat{\delta}}G
, respectively.

It seems difficult to find the above statement in the literature, but it is an easy application of standard techniques, and for completeness, we include an outline of the proof below.

Proof.

For the first part, it suffices to note that

•

if
\left(X,\pi,\mu,U\right)
is a Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\delta\right)
-module representation, then
\left(X,\pi\times\mu,U\right)
is a covariant representation of
(A\rtimes_{\delta}G,G,\widehat{\delta})
, and this can be quickly checked separately on the generators
\pi\left(a\right)
and
\mu\left(f\right)
for
a\in A
and
f\in C_{0}\left(G\right)
, while, on the other hand,

•

if
\left(X,\sigma,U\right)
is a covariant representation of
(A\rtimes_{\delta}G,G,\widehat{\delta})
, then there is a unique covariant representation
\left(X,\pi,\mu\right)
of
\left(A,G,\delta\right)
such that
\sigma=\pi\times\mu
, and, moreover, covariance of
\left(X,\sigma,U\right)
applied to the generators
\pi\left(a\right)
and
\mu\left(f\right)
shows that
\left(X,\mu,U\right)
is covariant for
\left(C_{0}\left(G\right),G,\textnormal{rt}\right)
and that
\pi
commutes with
U
.

Then the second part follows immediately since, by definition, the covariant representations of the action
\widehat{\delta}
are in bijective correspondence with the nondegenerate representations of the crossed product by
\widehat{\delta}
.
∎

Definition 4.3
.

The
Schrödinger
\left(A,G,\delta\right)
-modular representation
is

(A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right),\left(\operatorname{id}\otimes\lambda\right)\circ\delta,1\otimes M,1\otimes\rho),

where
\rho
is the right regular representation of
G
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The Schrödinger
\left(A,G,\delta\right)
-modular representation is a Heisenberg modular representation.

Proof.

This is true because of the following facts:

•

\left(A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right),\left(\operatorname{id}\otimes\lambda\right)\circ\delta,1\otimes M\right)
is the regular covariant representation of
\left(A,G,\delta\right)
.

•

\left(A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right),M,\rho\right)
is a covariant representation of
\left(C_{0}\left(G\right),G,\textnormal{rt}\right)
.

•

\left(\operatorname{id}\otimes\lambda\right)\circ\delta
commutes with
1\otimes\rho
since
\lambda
and
\rho
commute. ∎

Just as we did for actions, we say that a Heisenberg modular representation is a multiple of another one if and only if it is equivalent to a direct sum of copies of it.

Theorem 4.5

(Covariant Stone-von Neumann Theorem for Coactions)
.

If
\delta
is a maximal coaction of
G
on an elementary
C^{\ast}
-algebra
A
, then every Heisenberg
\left(A,G,\delta\right)
-modular representation is a multiple of the Schrödinger
\left(A,G,\delta\right)
-modular representation.

Proof.

The Katayama Duality Theorem says that if the coaction
\delta
is maximal, then the integrated form of the Schrödinger modular representation is a
C^{\ast}
-isomorphism of
\left(A\rtimes_{\delta}G\right)\rtimes_{\widehat{\delta}}G
onto
A\otimes\mathcal{K}
. Using this
C^{\ast}
-isomorphism, the
\left(A\otimes\mathcal{K}\right)
-
A
imprimitivity bimodule
A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right)
becomes an
\left(\left(A\rtimes_{\delta}G\right)\rtimes_{\widehat{\delta}}G\right)
-
A
imprimitivity bimodule, and
\phi_{A\otimes L^{2}\left(G\right)}
is the integrated form of the Schrödinger
\left(A,G,\delta\right)
-modular representation. Now the result follows from Proposition
2.5
.
∎

.

Studies of beauty suppression via nonprompt \PDz mesons in PbPb collisions at \sqrtsNN = 5.02 ​ \TeV \sqrtsNN=5.02\TeV


Abstract
The transverse momentum spectra of \PDz mesons from \cPqb hadron decays are measured at midrapidity ( \abs ​ y < 1 \abs{y}<1 ) in \Pp \Pp and PbPb collisions at a nucleon–nucleon center of mass energy of 5.02 \TeV with the CMS detector at the LHC. The \PDz mesons from \cPqb hadron decays are distinguished from prompt \PDz mesons by their decay topologies. In PbPb collisions, the \PB → \PDz \PB\to\PDz yield is found to be suppressed in the measured \pt range from 2 to 100 \GeVc as compared to \Pp \Pp collisions. The suppression is weaker than that of prompt \PDz mesons and charged hadrons for \pt around 10 \GeVc . While theoretical calculations incorporating partonic energy loss in the quark-gluon plasma can successfully describe the measured \PB → \PDz \PB\to\PDz suppression at higher \pt , the data show an indication of larger suppression than the model predictions in the range of 2 < \pt < 5 ​ \GeVc 2<\pt<5\GeVc .

Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) predicts the existence of a quark-gluon plasma (QGP) phase, consisting of deconfined quarks and gluons, at extremely high temperatures and/or densities
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Experiments at the BNL RHIC and the CERN LHC indicate that a strongly coupled QGP is created in relativistic heavy ion collisions at nucleon–nucleon center-of-mass energies
\sqrtsNN
from 200
\GeV
to several
\TeV

[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
. Heavy quarks (charm and beauty) produced in heavy ion collisions are valuable probes for studying the properties of this deconfined medium. They are mostly produced in primary hard QCD scatterings at an early stage of the collision.
During their propagation through the QGP, heavy quarks lose energy via radiative and collisional interactions with the medium constituents, with the 2 processes dominating at high and low transverse momentum (
\pt
), respectively.
Parton energy loss can be studied using the nuclear modification factor (
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
), which is defined as the ratio of the particle yield in nucleus–nucleus (AA) to that in proton–proton (pp) collisions, normalized by the number of binary nucleon–nucleon collisions (
N_{\mathrm{coll}}
)
[
9
]
. Precise measurements of
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
for particles containing light, charm, and beauty quarks over a wide
\pt
range can test the predicted flavor (parton mass) and energy dependence of the parton energy loss in the QGP
[
10
]
. This can provide both important tests of QCD at extreme densities and temperatures, and constraints on theoretical models describing the system evolution in heavy ion collisions.

Charm suppression in heavy ion collisions was reported by RHIC and LHC experiments
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
.
For beauty production, the CMS Collaboration measured
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
for nonprompt
\PJGy
mesons (coming from decays of
\cPqb
hadrons) and for fully reconstructed
\PBpm
mesons
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. A suppression by a factor of about two was observed in both channels for
\pt>6\GeVc
at mid-rapidity.
At the same time, the
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
of nonprompt
\PJGy
mesons in the
\pt
range of 6.5–30
\GeVc
was found to be larger than the
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
of prompt D mesons in the 8–16
\GeVc
\pt
region for central events, which is in line with a mass ordering of quark energy loss
[
10
]
.
An indication of less suppression of nonprompt
\PJGy
mesons is seen at forward rapidity (
1.8<\abs{y}<2.4
), at low
\pt
, down to 3
\GeVc
.
Extending measurements of charm and beauty suppression to a broader
\pt
coverage should provide improved discrimination between the radiative and collisional parton energy loss mechanisms, leading to better constraints on theoretical predictions.

In this letter, we report a study of beauty production and in-medium energy loss performed by measuring nonprompt
\PDz

\pt

\Pp

The

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6
\unit

\unit
T. Within the solenoid volume are a silicon pixel and strip tracker, a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter, and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter, each composed of a barrel and two endcap sections.
The silicon tracker measures charged particles within the pseudorapidity range
\abs{\eta}<2.5

For nonisolated particles of

[

A detailed description of the CMS experiment can be found in Ref.

The

Each track is required to pass a high purity selection based on a multi-variate analysis of track quality variables
[

For each pair of selected tracks, two

\PDz

In order to reduce the combinatorial background and prompt

\PDz

a minimum probability that the two tracks come from a common decay vertex,
a minimum distance between the decay vertex and the PV divided by its uncertainty,
and minimum distances of closest approach (DCA) to the PV for the pion and kaon tracks divided by their uncertainties.
The selection is optimized using simulated signal samples complemented by background events from mass sidebands in the data. Dedicated optimizations are performed for different

Figure

\PDz

Here

In order to obtain the
\PB\to\PDz

\PDz

\PDz

The prompt

\PDz

To assess systematic effects on the two-component fit arising from potential differences between the resolution in data and simulation, the widths of the simulated DCA distributions are varied by a floating scale factor.
The best simulated DCA width scale factor to match the data is determined by minimizing the
\chi^{2}
of the two-component fit. It is found to be in the range of

\pt

The
\PB\to\PDz
differential cross section with
\abs{y}<1
in
\Pp
\Pp
collisions is calculated with the following equation:

\left.\frac{\rd\sigma^{\PB\to\PDz}_{{\Pp}{\Pp}}}{\rd\pt}\right|_{\abs{y}<1}=\frac{1}{2\mathcal{L}\Delta\pt\mathcal{B}}\left.\frac{N^{\PB\to\PDz+\PaDz}_{{\Pp}{\Pp}}}{\alpha\epsilon}\right|_{\abs{y}<1}.

(1)

Here
N^{{\PB\to\PDz+\PaDz}}_{{\Pp}{\Pp}}
are the nonprompt
\PDz
and
\PaDz
meson yields extracted in each
\pt
interval;
\mathcal{L}
is the integrated luminosity for the corresponding trigger;
\Delta\pt
is the width of the
\pt
interval;
\mathcal{B}
is the decay branching fraction; and
\alpha\epsilon
represents the product of acceptance and efficiency.
The factor 1/2 accounts for the fact that the yields were measured for
\PDz
plus
\PaDz
, but the cross section is for either
\PDz
or
\PaDz
production.

The
\PB\to\PDz
yield with
\abs{y}<1
in
PbPb
collisions is calculated similarly, and normalized by the nuclear overlap function
T_{\mathrm{AA}}=N_{\text{coll}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{NN}}^{\text{inelastic}}=5.61\mbinv

[
24
]
calculated with the Glauber model
[
9
]
, to facilitate the comparison with the
\Pp
\Pp
spectrum, as:

\frac{1}{T_{\mathrm{AA}}}\left.\frac{\rd N^{\PB\to\PDz}_{\text{PbPb}}}{\rd\pt}\right|_{\abs{y}<1}=\frac{1}{T_{\mathrm{AA}}}\frac{1}{2N_{\text{events}}\Delta\pt\mathcal{B}}\left.\frac{N^{\PB\to\PDz+\PaDz}_{\text{PbPb}}}{\alpha\epsilon}\right|_{\abs{y}<1},

(2)

where the number of sampled inelastic collision events
N_{\text{events}}
replaces the integrated luminosity
\mathcal{L}
.

The nuclear modification factor is defined as

R_{\mathrm{AA}}=\frac{1}{T_{\mathrm{AA}}}\frac{\rd N^{\PB\to\PDz}_{\text{PbPb}}}{\rd\pt}/\frac{\rd\sigma^{\PB\to\PDz}_{{\Pp}{\Pp}}}{\rd\pt}.

(3)

In the calculation of

The following systematic uncertainties are evaluated separately in different
\pt

The systematic uncertainty due to the signal extraction from the invariant mass fit (3.2–5.3%) is evaluated by varying the function used to fit the background, and by comparing the default double-Gaussian signal yield with that obtained with a different method, in which the integral of a third-order polynomial function describing the background and the
\PK

\PGp
swapped pairs in the signal invariant mass region is subtracted from the number of candidate counts.
The uncertainty due to the signal extraction with the sideband subtraction method (1.4–8.6%) is obtained by comparing the
\PDz
meson yield from the sideband method with the yield from the invariant mass fit, both obtained within the
\PDz
DCA range where the nonprompt
\PDz
component dominates.
The systematic uncertainty associated with the separation of prompt
\PDz

\PDz
mesons from
\cPqb
hadron decays (4.2–30.4%) comes from two sources. The first part, which is due to the data–simulation difference in the
\PDz
DCA shapes, is estimated by comparing the default
\PB\to\PDz

\PDz
DCA distributions according to the statistical uncertainties in each individual bin, and repeating the two-component fit 1000 times.
The systematic uncertainty in the tracking efficiency is 4% for a single track
[
32
]

The systematic uncertainty in the selection efficiency due to the geometrical criteria (6.9–11.6%) is evaluated by varying the selection variables.
The systematic uncertainty in the

\PDz

The systematic uncertainty in the acceptance and efficiency due to the simulated

The systematic uncertainty in the acceptance and efficiency due to the simulated

\PB

In summary, this letter presents the transverse momentum spectra of
\PDz
mesons from
\cPqb
hadron decays measured in
\Pp
\Pp
and
PbPb
collisions at a center-of-mass energy
\sqrtsNN
= 5.02
\TeV
per nucleon pair with the CMS detector at the LHC. The
\PDz
mesons from
\cPqb
hadron decays are distinguished from the prompt
\PDz
mesons by the distance of closest approach of the
\PDz
path relative to the primary vertex.
The measured spectrum in
\Pp
\Pp
collisions is close to the upper limit of a Fixed-Order Next-to-Leading Logarithm perturbative quantum chromodynamics calculation.
In
PbPb
collisions, the
\PB\to\PDz
yield is suppressed in the measured transverse momentum (
\pt
) range from 2 to 100
\GeVc
. The
\PB\to\PDz
nuclear modification factor
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
is higher than for prompt
\PDz
mesons and charged hadrons around 10
\GeVc
, which is in line with a quark mass ordering of suppression. Compared to theoretical predictions, the measured
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
is consistent with some models at higher
\pt
, but shows a hint of stronger suppression than all of the available models at low
\pt
. This could indicate a stronger energy loss of
\cPqb
quarks in the quark-gluon plasma than predicted at low
\pt
, or could reflect an enhanced
\cPqb
baryon production due to quark coalescence in
PbPb
collisions.

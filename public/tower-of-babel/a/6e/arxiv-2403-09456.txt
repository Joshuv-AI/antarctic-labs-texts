Edge-apexing in hereditary classes of graphs
Jagdeep Singh, Vaidy Sivaraman

Abstract
. A class 𝒢 \mathcal{G} of graphs is called hereditary if it is closed under taking induced subgraphs. We denote by 𝒢 epex \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{epex}} the class of graphs that are at most one edge away from being in 𝒢 \mathcal{G} . We note that 𝒢 epex \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{epex}} is hereditary and prove that if a hereditary class 𝒢 \mathcal{G} has finitely many forbidden induced subgraphs, then so does 𝒢 epex \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{epex}} . The hereditary class of cographs consists of all graphs G G that can be generated from K 1 K_{1} using complementation and disjoint union. Cographs are precisely the graphs that do not have the 4 4 -vertex path as an induced subgraph. For the class of edge-apex cographs our main result bounds the order of such forbidden induced subgraphs by 8 and finds all of them by computer search.

Introduction

We consider finite, simple, undirected graphs. An

For a hereditary class

For an edge

The

is the class of graphs

is in

, or

such that

is in
\mathcal{G}
. It is easy to see that if

is hereditary, then so is
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{epex}}
. We prove that if
\mathcal{G}
has a finite set of forbidden induced subgraphs, then so does
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{epex}}
, and we apply this to the class of cographs.

A

is a graph that can be generated from the single-vertex graph

using the operations of complementation and disjoint union. A graph

is an

if
G
is a cograph, or

has an edge

is a cograph. In Theorem

we characterize edge-apex cographs by their forbidden induced subgraphs. Cographs are also called

Theorem 1.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

A graph

on four vertices as an induced subgraph.

For a graph

is. We prove the following.

Theorem 1.2

Let

Its proof is in Section 2. In Section 3, we show that the number of vertices of a forbidden induced subgraph for the class of edge-apex cographs is at most eight. Following is the precise statement.

Proposition 2.2

Let

. Then

Since

, the graph

contains a forbidden induced subgraph

. Observe that for every edge

so we have a subset

of

. If there is a vertex

, a contradiction. Therefore,

Observe that

contains both the endpoints of the edge

induces a forbidden induced subgraph for

in

The bound can be slightly improved if a forbidden induced subgraph for

Proof.

First we consider the possibility that

and

are edge-disjoint. In this case, we

. Suppose not and let
x
be a vertex in

. Then

Let

. Observe that, if there is a vertex

, then

is not in

, a contradiction. Therefore,

.

For

, if

does not contain the endpoints of

. Since

∎

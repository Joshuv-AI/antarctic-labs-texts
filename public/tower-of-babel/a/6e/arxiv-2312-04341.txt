Circular Nets with Spherical Parameter Lines and Terminating Laplace Sequences
Alexander I. Bobenko, Alexander Y. Fairley

Abstract
The focus is on circular nets with one or two families of spherical parameter lines, which are treated in Möbius geometry. These circular nets provide a discretisation of surfaces with one or two families of spherical curvature lines. The special cases of planar, circular and linear parameter lines are also investigated. A Lie-geometric discretisation in terms of principal contact element nets is also presented. Its properties are analogous to the classical properties of surfaces with one or two families of spherical curvature lines. Circular nets with two families of spherical parameter lines have geometric properties that are related to Darboux cyclides. Circular nets with one or two families of spherical parameter lines are examples of Q-nets with terminating Laplace sequences. More generally, this article considers Q-nets that are inscribed in quadrics and that have terminating Laplace sequences.

In the context of discrete differential geometry, discrete conjugate-line parametrised surfaces are defined as Q-nets, which are maps

A classical topic in differential geometry is the study of surfaces with one or two families of curvature lines that are spherical, planar, circular or linear. The foregoing surfaces were studied mostly in the nineteenth century in the setting of local differential geometry

Curvature-line parametrisations are a subclass of conjugate-line parametrisations. The latter admit Laplace transformations, which can be iterated. The iterations determine a so-called Laplace sequence. Generically, the Laplace sequence is infinite. In this paper, we introduce and study discrete curvature-line parametrisations with spherical parameter lines. Although our main focus is on circular nets in Möbius geometry, we also consider general Q-nets in projective geometry as well as principal contact element nets in Lie geometry. Up to now, this problem has not been investigated in detail although some examples appeared in the literature. Our main motivation is to find a structure-preserving discretisation of surfaces with spherical curvature lines. One can pass to a continuum limit by refining the mesh size. This general picture of discrete differential geometry looks very natural. There is a common belief that the smooth theory can be obtained from the corresponding discrete theory. The corresponding
C^{\infty}
convergence of general circular nets to curvature line parametrised surfaces was proven in
[
5
]
. A method to generate the curvature-line parametrisation of a given surface from circular nets on the surface was developed in
[
10
]
. However, one should not underestimate possible difficulties and ambiguities concerning connections of the discrete and smooth theories. An example of such a problem is an alternation phenomenon which is discussed in Section
5
. We demonstrate that although the natural discretisation via circular nets with spherical parameter lines comprises a nice consistent theory, it is slightly too general to be seen as the perfect discretisation model.

As indicated in
[
8
,
9
,
45
]
, there are three natural discretisations of curvature-line parametrisations: circular nets, conical nets and principal contact element nets. They correspond to Möbius-, Laguerre- and Lie-geometric descriptions of curvature-line parametrisations. The most general one is the Lie-geometric description, which is also applicable to the case of spherical curvature lines.

Curvature-line parametrised surfaces with spherical curvature lines belong to Lie geometry. If one applies a Lie transformation to such a surface, one obtains again a curvature-line parametrised surface with spherical curvature lines. The corresponding discretisation is given in Section
5
in terms of principal contact element nets. A Lie-geometric approach is used to exhibit the aforementioned alternation phenomenon. Once the alternation phenomenon is resolved, the discrete theory fits perfectly to the smooth theory and clarifies the classical properties of surfaces with spherical curvature lines.

Genericity assumption:
In the spirit of local differential geometry, this article assumes implicitly in all statements and proofs that
all the data are generic
. This means that the geometric structure of the nets under consideration is invariant under all small perturbations that preserve the constraints of the data. For example, Q-nets are maps
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
, with the constraint that the points
P_{i,j},P_{i+1,j},P_{i+1,j+1},P_{i,j+1}
are coplanar. The genericity assumption implies that the points
P_{i,j},P_{i+1,j},P_{i+1,j+1},P_{i,j+1}
are distinct and that no three of them are collinear. The genericity assumption also implies that the lines
P_{i,j}\vee P_{i,j+1}
,
P_{i+1,j}\vee P_{i+1,j+1}
, and
P_{i+2,j}\vee P_{i+2,j+1}
are not concurrent. More generally, the genericity assumption ensures that generic Q-nets have well-defined Laplace sequences.

2
Terminating Laplace Sequences

Let
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
be a Q-net. This means that
\forall(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the points
P_{i,j},P_{i+1,j},P_{i+1,j+i},P_{i,j+1}
are coplanar. The intersection points

\displaystyle A_{i,j}:=(P_{i,j}\vee P_{i+1,j})\cap(P_{i,j+1}\vee P_{i+1,j+1})

\displaystyle B_{i,j}:=(P_{i,j}\vee P_{i,j+1})\cap(P_{i+1,j}\vee P_{i+1,j+1})

are known as the
Laplace points
of the planar quad
\Box(P_{i,j},P_{i+1,j},P_{i+1,j+1},P_{i,j+1})
.

The
Laplace transforms

\mathcal{L}_{A}P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
of
P
are defined such that
\mathcal{L}_{A}P(i,j):=A_{i,j}
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}P(i,j):=B_{i,j}
. The
Laplace transformations
are
\mathcal{L}_{A}
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}
such that
P\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{A}P
and
P\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{B}P
, respectively. It is straightforward to check that the Laplace transforms
\mathcal{L}_{A}P
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}P
are Q-nets. Thus, Laplace transformations can be iterated. Let
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{d}
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{d}
denote the
d
-times iterated Laplace transformations. An example of Laplace transforms is shown in Figure
2
.

Figure 2
:
A patch of Q-net
P
and its Laplace transforms
\mathcal{L}_{A}P
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}P
. The Laplace transforms
\mathcal{L}^{2}_{A}P
and
\mathcal{L}^{2}_{B}P
are also shown.

It is straightforward to check that the Laplace transformations
\mathcal{L}_{A}
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}
are mutually inverse up to an index shift:

\mathcal{L}_{A}\circ\mathcal{L}_{B}P(i,j)=\mathcal{L}_{B}\circ\mathcal{L}_{A}P(i,j)=P(i+1,j+1).

Thus, the iterated Laplace transforms can be arranged into the following
Laplace sequence
.

\displaystyle\ldots\leftarrow\mathcal{L}_{A}^{3}P\leftarrow\mathcal{L}_{A}^{2}P\leftarrow\mathcal{L}_{A}P\leftarrow P\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{B}P\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{B}^{2}P\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{B}^{3}P\rightarrow\ldots

Generically, the Laplace sequence is infinite in both directions. However, there are interesting cases where the sequence terminates in one or both directions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
be a Q-net and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We call

•

\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P

Goursat degenerate
if
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(i,j)
is independent of
i
.

•

\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P

Laplace degenerate
if
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(i,j)
is independent of
j
.

•

\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P

Goursat degenerate
if
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P(i,j)
is independent of
j
.

•

\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P

Laplace degenerate
if
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P(i,j)
is independent of
i
.

The case of Laplace degenerate
\mathcal{L}_{A}P
or
\mathcal{L}_{B}P
was studied in
[
30
]
.

Definition
2.1
has a counterpart in the smooth theory. The attributions to Goursat and Laplace are compatible with the attributions that are made in
[
11
,
32
]
.

If
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P
is either Goursat degenerate or Laplace degenerate, then
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P
degenerates to a discrete curve
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
, and the Laplace sequence terminates after
m
steps. If
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P
is both Goursat and Laplace degenerate, then
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P
degenerates to a single point.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
be a Q-net, and
m<n
. Generically,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P
is Goursat degenerate if and only if the parameter lines
\{P_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
are contained in
m
-dimensional projective subspaces of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
.

Proof.

Due to the genericity assumption, the claim follows by counting dimensions of projective subspaces. Let
H_{j}
be the
m
-dimensional space containing the points
\{P_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
. The points
\{\mathcal{L}_{A}P(i,j)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
lie in
H_{j}\cap H_{j+1}
. Generically,
H_{j}\cap H_{j+1}
is
(m-1)
-dimensional because
P
is a Q-net. By the same reasoning, for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
d\leq m
, the points
\{\mathcal{L}_{A}^{d}(i,j)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
are contained in a
(m-d)
-dimensional space. In particular, the points
\{\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}(i,j)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
coincide.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
be a Q-net, and
m<n
. Generically,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P
is Laplace degenerate if and only if

\cap_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{i,j}\vee P_{i+1,j}\vee\ldots\vee P_{i+m,j}
is a point for each
i
.

Proof.

For all
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
P_{i,j}\vee P_{i+1,j}\vee\ldots\vee P_{i+m,j}
is generically
m
-dimensional. Because
P
is a Q-net, the intersection
(P_{i,j}\vee P_{i+1,j}\vee\ldots\vee P_{i+m,j})\cap(P_{i,j+1}\vee P_{i+1,j+1}\vee\ldots\vee P_{i+m,j+1})
is
(m-1)
-dimensional, and
\cap_{l\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}}P_{i,j+l}\vee P_{i+1,j+l}\vee\ldots\vee P_{i+m,j+l}
is a point. The point coincides with
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(i,j)
since the latter is contained in
\cap_{l\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}}P_{i,j+l}\vee P_{i+1,j+l}\vee\ldots\vee P_{i+m,j+l}
. So,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(i,j)
is independent of
j
if and only if
\cap_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{i,j}\vee P_{i+1,j}\vee\ldots\vee P_{i+m,j}
is a point for each
i
.
∎

Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
have counterparts in the smooth theory
[

]
. For instance, the case
m=2
of Proposition

can be interpreted in terms of osculating planes.

Recall,
l:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\{\text{lines in }\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}\}
is a discrete line congruence if and only if any two neighbouring lines intersect.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
l:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\{\text{lines in }\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}\}
be a discrete line congruence, where
n\geq 3
. Define the
Laplace transforms

\mathcal{L}_{A}l:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\{\text{lines in }\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}\}
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}l:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\{\text{lines in }\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}\}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{A}l(i,j)

\displaystyle:=(l_{i,j}\vee l_{i+1,j})\cap(l_{i,j+1}\vee l_{i+1,j+1}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{B}l(i,j)

\displaystyle:=(l_{i,j}\vee l_{i,j+1})\cap(l_{i+1,j}\vee l_{i+1,j+1}).

It is straightforward to check that the Laplace transforms
\mathcal{L}_{A}l
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}l
are generically discrete line congruences. Consequently, the Laplace transformations
\mathcal{L}_{A}
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}
of discrete line congruences can be iterated. Let
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{d}
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{d}
denote the
d
-times iterated Laplace transformations, where
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

Analogously to the Laplace sequences of Q-nets, discrete line congruences also determine Laplace sequences. Goursat degenerations and Laplace degenerations are defined for discrete line congruences analogously to Definition
2.1
.

Q-Nets that are Inscribed in Quadrics

Lemmas

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
O:=\Box(O_{1,1},O_{2,1},O_{2,2},O_{1,2})
and
P:=\Box(P_{1,1},P_{2,1},P_{2,2},P_{1,2})
be two planar quads in
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{A}O
,
\mathcal{L}_{B}O
,
\mathcal{L}_{A}P
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}P
be their Laplace points. (See Figure

in

imply the sixth one.

\displaystyle O_{1,2}\perp P_{1,2},\ O_{1,1}\perp P_{1,1},\ O_{2,1}\perp P_{2,1},O_{2,2}\perp P_{2,2},\ \mathcal{L}_{B}O\perp\mathcal{L}_{A}P,\ \mathcal{L}_{A}O\perp\mathcal{L}_{B}P.

\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}
that satisfy the six conjugacy conditions in Lemma
3.1

Proof.

Denote by
[x]\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
a point with representative vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Let
O_{1,2}=[u]
,
O_{1,1}=[v]
,
O_{2,1}=[w]
. Choose the representative vectors

and

so that
O_{2,2}=[u+v+w]
. Then,
\mathcal{L}_{A}O=[v+w]
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}O=[u+v]
. Analogously,
P_{1,2}=[x]
,
P_{1,1}=[y]
,
P_{2,1}=[z]
,
P_{2,2}=[x+y+z]
. Then,
\mathcal{L}_{A}P=[y+z]
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}P=[x+y]
. Let

be a symmetric bilinear form representing the quadric

. The six conditions in Lemma

are equivalent to the following equations.

\displaystyle\varphi(u,x)=0,\ \varphi(v,y)=0,\ \varphi(w,z)=0,

Lemma 3.2
.

Let three of the vertices of a planar quad

Analogously to the proof of Lemma

,
P_{1,1}=[v]
,
P_{2,1}=[w]

.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
P:[m]\times[m]\to\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
be a Q-net, where
n\geq m\geq 1
. For each
i\in[m]
, let
V_{i}:=\mathrm{join}\{P_{i,j}\}_{j\in[m]}
. For each
j\in[m]
, let
H_{j}:=\mathrm{join}\{P_{i,j}\}_{i\in[m]}
. Suppose that the points
\{P_{i,j}\mid(i,j)\in[m]\times[m]\text{ s.t.\ }(i,j)\neq(m,m)\}
are contained in a non-degenerate quadric
\mathcal{Q}
. Generically, the spaces

and

are points. The point
P_{m,m}
is contained in
\mathcal{Q}
if and only if the points
X
and
Y
are conjugate with respect to
\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

A point
P
is contained in the quadric
\mathcal{Q}
if and only if
P
is self-conjugate relative to
\mathcal{Q}
. This proves the case
m=1
. The case
m=2
is Lemma
3.2
. For the remainder of this proof, let

. The genericity assumption ensures that
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{d}P,\mathcal{L}_{B}^{d}P:[m-d]\times[m-d]\to\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
are well defined Q-nets for all
d\leq m-1
. Then,
\mathcal{L}^{m-1}_{A}P
and
\mathcal{L}^{m-1}_{B}P
are points such that
\mathcal{L}^{m-1}_{A}P\in X:=\cap_{j\in[m]}H_{j}
and
\mathcal{L}^{m-1}_{B}P\in Y:=\cap_{i\in[m]}V_{i}
. Generically, the spaces
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in[m]}
and
\{H_{j}\}_{j\in[m]}
are
(m-1)
-dimensional.
V_{i}\vee V_{i+1}
is
m
-dimensional because
P
is a Q-net and
V_{i}\neq V_{i+1}
since
\mathcal{Q}
is non-degenerate. Equivalently,
V_{i}\cap V_{i+1}
is
(m-2)
-dimensional. It follows that
Y:=\cap_{i\in[m]}V_{i}
is
0
-dimensional. Similarly,
X:=\cap_{j\in[m]}H_{j}
is
0
-dimensional. Therefore,
X
equals
\mathcal{L}^{m-1}_{A}P
and
Y
equals
\mathcal{L}^{m-1}_{B}P
. Suppose that the points
\{P_{i,j}:(i,j)\in[m]\times[m],(i,j)\neq(m,m)\}
are contained in a quadric
\mathcal{Q}
. By Theorem
1.1
,
P_{m,m}\in\mathcal{Q}
if and only if
\mathcal{L}^{m-1}_{A}P\perp\mathcal{L}^{m-1}_{B}P

and

are conjugate relative to
\mathcal{Q}
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
be a Q-net that is inscribed in a quadric, and all lines
P_{i,j}\vee P_{i+1,j}
are not isotropic.
Suppose that
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P
is Goursat degenerate for some
m
, and
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{d}P
is well defined for all
d\leq m
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P
is Laplace degenerate.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{d}P
is well defined for all
d\leq m
and
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P
is Goursat degenerate. Then, for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, the point
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(j):=\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(i,j)
is well defined because it is independent of
i
. By Theorem
1.1
, for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(j)
is conjugate to the points
\{\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P(i,j)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
. We will prove that this is in fact a unique point by showing that
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P(i,j)=\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P(i+1,j)
. Indeed, suppose that
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P(i,j)\neq\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P(i+1,j)
. Then, the line
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P(i,j)\vee\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P(i+1,j)
is conjugate to
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(j)
. Notice that the line
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P(i,j)\vee\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P(i+1,j)
coincides with the line
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m-1}P(i+1,j)\vee\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m-1}P(i+1,j+1)
. If
P_{i+m+1,j+m}
is replaced by any point in the line
P_{i+m,j+m}\vee P_{i+m+1,j+m}
, then the Laplace point
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(i,j)
does not change, whereas the Laplace point
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m}P(i+1,j)
does change but it remains in the line
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m-1}P(i+1,j)\vee\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m-1}P(i+1,j+1)
which is conjugate to
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(j)
. Therefore, by Theorem
1.1
, any point in the line
P_{i+m,j+m}\vee P_{i+m+1,j+m}
is contained in
\mathcal{Q}
. This contradicts the assumption that
P_{i+m,j+m}\vee P_{i+m+1,j+m}
is not an isotropic line.
∎

The assumption of Theorem

that the lines

is not an isotropic line of
\mathcal{Q}
. For example, suppose that

of signature

and

, the points

are contained in an isotropic line of

. However,

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
be a Q-net that is inscribed in a non-degenerate quadric.
Suppose that
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P
is Laplace degenerate for some
m
, and
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{d}P
is well defined for all
d\leq m+n-1
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+n-1}P
is Goursat degenerate.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{d}P
is well defined for all
d\leq m+n-1
. Let
\mathcal{L}^{m}_{A}P
be Laplace degenerate. Then, for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, the point
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(i):=\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(i,j)
is well defined, i.e. it is independent of
j
. By Theorem

,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P(i)
is conjugate to the points

. Thus, these points are contained in the
(n-1)
-dimensional polar hyperplane

. Generically,

is

-dimensional. Equivalently, the intersection

-dimensional. This implies,

\{\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+n-1}P(i,j)\}
are independent of

.
∎

. However,
V_{0}\cap\mathcal{V}
cannot be a quadric because it contains
U_{0}\cup W_{0}
, which is a degenerate quadric in
V_{0}
, and it also contains

. Therefore,
V_{0}
must be an isotropic subspace of
\mathcal{V}
. Further,
V_{1}
must also be an isotropic subspace of
\mathcal{V}
. Indeed,
U_{1}
and
W_{1}
are isotropic subspaces of
\mathcal{V}
and
V_{0}\cap V_{1}
is also isotropic because
V_{0}
is isotropic. However,
V_{0}\cap V_{1}
is not contained in the degenerate quadric
U_{1}\cup W_{1}\subset V_{1}
. So,
V_{1}\cap\mathcal{V}
is not a quadric but an isotropic subspace of
\mathcal{V}
. Iteratively, all
V_{i}
are isotropic subspaces of
\mathcal{V}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.8
.

By applying Lemma
3.11
to the restriction of
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2m}
to
\mathbb{Z}\times[m+1]
, there exists a quadric
\mathcal{V}
such that the projective subspaces
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
are isotropic subspaces of
\mathcal{V}
. By applying Lemma
3.11
to the restriction of
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2m}
to
[m+1]\times\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a quadric
\mathcal{H}
such that the projective subspaces
\{H_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
are isotropic subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
. Non-degenerate quadrics with
m
-dimensional isotropic spaces can only exist in
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
where
n\geq 2m+1
. The quadrics
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{H}
must be degenerate because they are contained in
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2m}
. Let
(p,q,r)
be the signature of
\mathcal{V}
. Looking for a contradiction, suppose that
r\geq 3
. Then,
\mathcal{V}
has at least a
2
-dimensional subspace of singular points. By the construction of
\mathcal{V}
, which is explained in the proof of Lemma
3.11
,
\mathcal{V}
is in the pencil of quadrics spanned by
\mathcal{Q}
and a degenerate quadric, say
\mathcal{D}
, that consists of a pair of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2m}
. The locus of singular points of
\mathcal{D}
is
(2m-2)
-dimensional, which intersects the locus of singular points of
\mathcal{V}
in at least one point. Such an intersection point must also be a singular point of
\mathcal{Q}
because
\mathcal{Q}
is in the pencil of quadrics spanned by
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{D}
. This contradicts the assumption that
\mathcal{Q}
is non-degenerate. Therefore,
r\leq 2
. The generators of
\mathcal{V}
are
(min\{p,q\}-1+r)
-dimensional. Because
\mathcal{V}
contains
m
-dimensional isotropic spaces,
\mathcal{V}
has signature
(m,m,1)
. Similarly,
\mathcal{H}
also has signature
(m,m,1)
.

To prove the claim about the systems of generators, observe that
V_{i}\cap V_{i+1}
is
(m-1)
-dimensional. By intersecting with a generic hyperplane
E
, the dimension of
V_{i}\cap V_{i+1}\cap E
is
m-2
which has the same parity as
m
. Thus, by the explanation at Proposition
3.7
, we have two systems of generators
\{V_{2i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{V_{2i+1}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

Generically, the apex of
\mathcal{H}
is not contained in
\mathcal{Q}
. Then, there is a unique quadric
\mathcal{R}
that contains the apex of
\mathcal{H}
and that is a member of the pencil of quadrics spanned by
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{Q}
. We will show that
\mathcal{R}
equals
\mathcal{H}
. The
m
-dimensional spaces
H_{j}
are not isotropic subspaces of
\mathcal{Q}
because
\mathcal{Q}
is non-degenerate. So,
H_{j}\cap\mathcal{Q}
is a quadric that contains the points
\{P_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Generically,
H_{j}\cap\mathcal{Q}
is the unique quadric in
H_{j}
that contains these points. So,
\mathcal{R}
contains the quadric
H_{j}\cap\mathcal{Q}
because
\mathcal{R}
contains the points
\{P_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
that are in the base locus of the pencil of quadrics spanned by
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{V}
. However,
\mathcal{R}
also contains the apex of
\mathcal{H}
which is not contained in
\mathcal{Q}
. So,
H_{j}\cap\mathcal{R}
cannot be a quadric. Instead,
H_{j}
is an isotropic subspace of
\mathcal{R}
. The quadrics
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{H}
are equal because they have the same generators
H_{j}
.
∎

4
Circular Nets with Spherical Parameter Lines

Circular nets in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
provide a Möbius-geometric discretisation of curvature-line parametrisations. See
[
9
,
10
,
5
,
17
]
.

A map
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a circular net if the vertices
P_{i,j}
,
P_{i+1,j}
,
P_{i+1,j+1}
,
P_{i,j+1}
lie on circles, i.e.
\Box_{i,j}:=\Box(P_{i,j},P_{i+1,j},P_{i+1,j+i},P_{i,j+1})
are circular quads. The points
\{P_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{P_{i,j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
build two families of parameter lines. If
n=3
, then the parameter lines are interpreted as discrete curvature lines.

Throughout this Section
4
, the lift of a circular net
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the Möbius quadric
\mathcal{M}^{n}:=\{[x]\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}\mid x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\ldots+x_{n+1}^{2}-x_{n+2}^{2}=0\}
is denoted
M:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{M}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}
. The lift is a Q-net that is inscribed in the Möbius quadric such that
\sigma(M_{i,j})=P_{i,j}
, where
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}-N\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is the central projection with centre
N
that is defined in Appendix
A
. Here
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is the projective closure of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. See Appendix
A
for a brief introduction to Möbius geometry.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a circular net and let
M:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{M}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}
be its lift. A parameter line
\{P_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
m
-spherical
or
m
-planar
if its vertices are contained in an
m
-dimensional sphere or
m
-dimensional plane in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively. Equivalently, the points
\{M_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the lift
M:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{M}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}
to the Möbius quadric are contained in a
(m+1)
-dimensional projective subspace of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}
.

A parameter line
\{P_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
m
-planar when the corresponding
(m+1)
-dimensional projective subspace of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}
contains the point
N
.

Some of the proofs in this Section
4
repeatedly use the same arguments. To avoid excessive repetition, some of them are omitted.

4.1
One Family Spherical or Circular

The following proposition is a direct corollary of Proposition
2.2
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
M:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{M}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}
be a circular net, and
m<n
. Generically, the parameter lines
\{M_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
are
m
-spherical if and only if
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m+1}M
is Goursat degenerate.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
M:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{M}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}
be a circular net, where
n\geq 2
. Suppose that the parameter lines
\{(M_{i,j})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
are
m
-spherical, where
m<n
. Generically,
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M
is Laplace degenerate. For each
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, let
\mathcal{S}_{i,j}
be the
m
-dimensional sphere that contains the points
M_{i,j},\ldots,M_{i,j+m+1}
. For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
m
-spheres
\{\mathcal{S}_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
have a common orthogonal hypersphere.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m+1}M
is generically Goursat degenerate. Generically, by Theorem
3.4
,
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M
is Laplace degenerate. For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, the point
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M(j):=\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M(i,j)
is well defined because it is independent of
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. For each
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, let
S_{i,j}
be the
(m+1)
-dimensional projective subspace that contains the points
M_{i,j},\ldots,M_{i,j+m+1}
. Then,
S_{i,j}\cap\mathcal{M}^{n}=\mathcal{S}_{i,j}
. For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, the spaces
\{S_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
contain the point
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M(j)
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M(j)^{\perp}
be the polar hyperplane of
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M(j)
relative to
\mathcal{M}^{n}
. The (possibly imaginary) hypersphere
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M(j)^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{M}^{n}
is orthogonal to the
m
-dimensional spheres
\{\mathcal{S}_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
∎

Let
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a circular net with spherical parameter lines
\{(P_{i,j})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
M:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{M}^{3}
be its lift. By the case
m=2
of Proposition
4.2
,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{3}M
is Goursat degenerate. For each
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
d\in[3]
,
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{A}^{d}M(i,j))=\mathcal{L}_{A}^{d}P(i,j)
. So,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{3}P
is Goursat degenerate. This agrees with the smooth theory of surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with one family of spherical curvature lines
[
26
]
. By the case
m=2
of Proposition
4.3
,
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{3}M
is Laplace degenerate. So,
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{3}P
is Laplace degenerate. This differs to the smooth theory. Along any of the spherical curvature lines of a smooth surface with one family of spherical curvature lines, the osculating planes of the non-spherical curvature lines are generically concurrent
[
42
]
. Then, the case
m=2
of Proposition
2.3
implies that the circular net
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
with one family of spherical parameter lines
\{(P_{i,j})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
would better mimic a smooth surface with one family of spherical curvature lines if
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{2}P
were Laplace degenerate. Let
\nu_{i,j}
be the discrete normal through the centre of the circle containing
P_{i,j}
,
P_{i+1,j}
,
P_{i+1,j+1}
,
P_{i,j+1}
and that is orthogonal to the plane of the circle. It can readily be shown that the planes
\{\nu_{i,j}\vee\nu_{i,j+1}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
are concurrent at the centre of the sphere through the points
\{P_{i,j+1}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
. This feature is comparable to the condition that the osculating planes are concurrent.

In Section
5.2
, an alternation phenomenon is exhibited that is used to define an improved discretisation of surfaces with spherical curvature lines.

Let
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a circular net with circular parameter lines
\{(P_{i,j})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
. By the case
m=1
of Proposition
4.2
,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{2}M
is Goursat degenerate. For each
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
d\in[2]
,
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{A}^{d}M(i,j))=\mathcal{L}_{A}^{d}P(i,j)
. So,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{2}P
is Goursat degenerate. This agrees with the smooth theory of surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with one family of circular curvature lines
[
26
]
, which are classical surfaces that are known as
channel surfaces
. By the case
m=1
of Proposition
4.3
,
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{2}M
is Laplace degenerate. So,
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{2}P
is Laplace degenerate. This differs to the smooth theory. Joachimsthal’s theorem ensures that the tangent planes of a channel surface along any circular curvature line are the tangent planes of a cone (or cylinder) of revolution. The tangent lines of the non-circular curvature lines are rulings of the foregoing cone. Therefore, any channel surface has a Laplace transform that is Laplace degenerate. It consists of the locus of the apices of the cones of revolution that are tangent to the channel surface along the circular curvature lines. Therefore, the circular net
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
with one family of circular parameter lines
\{(P_{i,j})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
would better mimic a channel surface if
\mathcal{L}_{B}P
were Laplace degenerate. The condition that
\mathcal{L}_{B}P
is Laplace degenerate is present in the Lie-geometric discretisation of channel surfaces in
[
28
]
. Let
\nu_{i,j}
be the discrete normal through the centre of the circle containing
P_{i,j}
,
P_{i+1,j}
,
P_{i+1,j+1}
,
P_{i,j+1}
and that is orthogonal to the plane of the circle. It can readily be shown that the planes
\{\nu_{i,j}\vee\nu_{i,j+1}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
are concurrent at the line through the centre of the circle containing the points
\{P_{i,j+1}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
and that is orthogonal to the plane of the circle.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a circular net, where
n\geq 2
. For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, suppose that the points
\{P_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
are contained in an
m
-dimensional plane
\mathcal{H}_{j}
, where
m<n
. Generically,
\mathcal{L}^{m}_{A}P
is Goursat degenerate and
\mathcal{L}^{m+1}_{B}P
is Laplace degenerate. The discrete curve
\mathcal{L}^{m+1}_{B}P
is contained in the hyperplane at infinity of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, the lines
\{\mathcal{L}^{m}_{B}P(i,j)\vee\mathcal{L}^{m}_{B}P(i,j+1)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
are parallel.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m}P
is Goursat degenerate. Let
M:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{M}^{n}
be the lift of
P
. For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
H_{j}:=\mathrm{join}\{M_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
(m+1)
-dimensional projective subspace of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}
that contains the point
N
. By Proposition
2.3
,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m+1}M
is Laplace degenerate. By Proposition
2.2
,
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m+1}M
is Goursat degenerate. So,
N=\mathcal{L}_{A}^{m+1}M(i,j)
for all
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. By Theorem
3.4
,
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M
is Laplace degenerate. So,
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M(j):=\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M(i,j)
is well defined. By Theorem
1.1
,
N
and
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M(j)
are conjugate for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then, the points
\{\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M(j)\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
are contained in the polar hyperplane
N^{\perp}
. For each
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}M(i,j))=\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}P(i,j)
. The polar hyperplane
N^{\perp}
projects under
\sigma
to the hyperplane at infinity of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}P
is Laplace degenerate and the points
\{\mathcal{L}_{B}^{m+1}P(j)\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
are contained in the hyperplane at infinity of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.
∎

5
Principal Contact Element Nets with Spherical Parameter Lines

5.1
Lie Geometry and Principal Contact Element Nets

We start with some standard facts about the Lie geometry of oriented hyperspheres in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. See
[
15
,
3
,
9
]
for more information on Lie geometry.

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n+1},e_{n+2},e_{n+3}
be an orthonormal basis in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,2}
:

\displaystyle\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle=0,\ i\neq j,\langle e_{i},e_{i}\rangle=1,\ i=1,\ldots,n+1,\langle e_{n+2},e_{n+2}\rangle=\langle e_{n+3},e_{n+3}\rangle=-1.

The norm of a vector
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+3})=\sum_{i=1}^{n+3}x_{i}e_{i}
is given by

\langle x,x\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}^{2}-x_{n+2}^{2}-x_{n+3}^{2}.

It is convenient to introduce two isotropic vectors

e_{\infty}=\frac{1}{2}(e_{n+2}+e_{n+1}),\quad e_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(e_{n+2}-e_{n+1}).

(5)

The
(n+1)
-dimensional
Lie quadric

\mathcal{L}^{n+1}:=\{[x]=[x_{1}\ldots,x_{n+3}]\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+2}\mid\langle x,x\rangle=0\}

parametrises the set of non-empty oriented hyperspheres, oriented hyperplanes and points in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Models of the above elements in
\mathcal{L}^{n+1}
are as follows.

•

Oriented hypersphere with center
c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
signed radius
r\in\mathbb{R}
:

\hat{S}=[c+e_{0}+(|c|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}-r^{2})e_{\infty}+re_{n+3}].

(6)

•

Oriented hyperplane
\langle v,x\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=d
with

|v|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=1
and
d\in\mathbb{R}
:

\hat{E}=[v+0\cdot e_{0}+2de_{\infty}+e_{n+3}].

(7)

•

Point
P\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\hat{P}=[P+e_{0}+|P|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}e_{\infty}+0\cdot e_{n+3}].

(8)

The Möbius quadric
\mathcal{M}^{n}:=\{[x]\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}\mid x_{1}^{2}+\ldots+x_{n+1}^{2}-x_{n+2}^{2}=0\}
is identified with
\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\cap[e_{n+3}]^{\perp}
where
[e_{n+3}]^{\perp}
is the polar hyperplane relative to
\mathcal{L}^{n+1}
of the point
[e_{n+3}]
. Points of the
point complex

\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\cap[e_{n+3}]^{\perp}
are identified with spheres of radius zero, which are treated as points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The point
[e_{\infty}]
is interpreted as the point at infinity of the one-point compactification of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Points of the
hyperplane complex

\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\cap[e_{\infty}]^{\perp}
, where
[e_{\infty}]^{\perp}
is the polar hyperplane relative to
\mathcal{L}^{n+1}
of the point
[e_{\infty}]
, are identified with oriented hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The Lie quadric provides a double cover of the points in the exterior of
\mathcal{M}^{n}
. In Möbius geometry, the exterior of
\mathcal{M}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}
parametrises the set of non-oriented hyperspheres and hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
S
be a point in the exterior of the Möbius quadric
\mathcal{M}^{n},
which is identified with the exterior of
\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\cap[e_{n+3}]^{\perp}
. Then, the line
S\vee[e_{n+3}]
intersects the Lie quadric at two real points, which are interpreted as the two orientations of the hypersphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
corresponding to the point
S
.

Two oriented hyperspheres are in
oriented contact
if and only if the corresponding points
\hat{S}_{1}
and
\hat{S}_{2}
in
\mathcal{L}^{n+1}
are conjugate relative to
\mathcal{L}^{n+1}
. Any two hyperspheres in oriented contact determine a
contact element
, i.e. their point of contact and their
common tangent hyperplane. Then, the line
\hat{S}_{1}\vee\hat{S}_{2}
is
isotropic
, i.e. lies entirely in
\mathcal{L}^{n+1}
. An isotropic line
l
contains exactly one point
l\cap[e_{n+3}]^{\perp}
with vanishing
e_{n+3}
-component (the common point
P
of contact of all the oriented hyperspheres in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
corresponding to the points of
l
), and, exactly one point
l\cap[e_{\infty}]^{\perp}
with vanishing
e_{0}
-component (the common tangent oriented hyperplane
E
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of all the hyperspheres). We denote the space of isotropic lines by

\mathcal{L}^{n+1}_{0}=\{\text{space of isotropic lines}\}=\{\text{space of contact elements}\}.

Principal contact element nets provide a discretisation of curvature-line parametrisations of surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in terms of points and also tangent planes. They were introduced in
[
8
]
to provide a Lie-geometric discretisation of curvature-line parametrisations, see also
[
9
]
for more information on principal contact element nets.

Let

l:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{L}^{4}_{0}

be a
principal contact element net
. This means that
l
is a
discrete line congruence
, i.e. any two neighboring lines intersect. By intersecting a principal contact element net by two hyperplanes one obtains two Q-nets. The first one

\hat{P}:=l\cap[e_{6}]^{\perp}:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{M}^{3}=\mathcal{L}^{4}\cap[e_{6}]^{\perp}

is a Q-net in the Möbius quadric. Via stereographic projection, it determines a circular net
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The second one

\hat{E}:=l\cap[e_{\infty}]^{\perp}:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{L}^{4}\cap[e_{\infty}]^{\perp}

is a
Q
-net in the plane complex. Let
E_{i,j}
be the oriented plane in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
corresponding to
\hat{E}_{i,j}
. Then,
E:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\{\text{oriented planes in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\}
is a conical net. The oriented planes
E_{i,j}
,
E_{i+1,j}
,
E_{i+1,j+1}
,
E_{i,j+1}
are tangent to a cone of revolution. See
[
35
,
43
]
for more information concerning conical nets and their applications in architectural geometry. Points of the circular net lie in the planes of the conical net, i.e.
P_{i,j}\in E_{i,j}
.

In this elementary interpretation, a principal contact element net can be identified with a pair of maps
(P,E)
where
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a circular net and
E:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\{\text{oriented planes in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\}
is a conical net such that
P_{i,j}\in E_{i,j}
. A contact element
(P_{i,j},E_{i,j})
has oriented contact with an oriented sphere
\mathcal{S}
if
P_{i,j}\in\mathcal{S}
and
E_{i,j}
is an oriented tangent plane of
\mathcal{S}
. The defining property of a principal contact element net
(P,E)
is that any two neighbouring contact elements have oriented contact with an oriented sphere.

As explained in
[
9
,
45
]
any circular net
P:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
can be extended to a principal contact element net
(P,E)
in a
2
-parameter family of ways. There is a
2
-parameter family of possible choices for the oriented plane
E_{0,0}
containing
P_{0,0}
. The conical net
E:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\{\text{oriented planes in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\}
is uniquely determined by the choice of
E_{0,0}
. For instance, the plane
E_{1,0}
is determined as the reflection of
E_{0,0}
about the plane that is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining
P_{0,0}
and
P_{1,0}
. Similarly, any conical net
E:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\{\text{oriented planes in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\}
has a
2
-parameter family of extensions to a principal contact element net
(P,E)
.

Proposition 5.3

Here we give a brief overview of Möbius geometry and Darboux cyclides. For instance, see

Möbius geometry is a subgeometry of Lie geometry, with points distinguishable among all hyperspheres as those of radius zero. As it was explained in Section
5.1
, it is a restriction of Lie geometry to the polar hyperplane
[e_{n+3}]^{\perp}
of a time-like vector
e_{n+3}
. Thus one can use the Lie-geometric description and just omit the
e_{n+3}
-component, passing to the Lorentz space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1,2}
. The resulting objects are points of the
(n+1)
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}:=\mathrm{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1})
. We continue to use notations of Section
5.1
in the context of Möbius geometry.

The
n
-dimensional
Möbius quadric
is given by

\mathcal{M}^{n}:=\{[x]=[x_{1}\ldots,x_{n+2}]\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}\mid\langle x,x\rangle=0\},

where

\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}y_{i}-x_{n+2}y_{n+2}.

is the Lorentz product of two vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
.
We will use the basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n},e_{o},e_{\infty}
(see (
5
)), and denote the corresponding coordinates of the vectors
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
as follows:

x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}e_{i}+x_{0}e_{0}+x_{\infty}e_{\infty},\quad x_{0}=x_{n+2}-x_{n+1},\ x_{\infty}=x_{n+2}+x_{n+1}.

Without loss of generality two arbitrary points on the Möbius quadric can be normalized as
[e_{\infty}]
and
[e_{0}]
. Let us consider the tangent hyperplanes to
\mathcal{M}^{n}
at these points as well as their intersection plane:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\infty}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{[x]\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}\mid x_{0}=0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{0}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{[x]\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}\mid x_{\infty}=0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{H}^{0}=\{[x]\in\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n+1}\mid x_{0}=x_{\infty}=0\}.

Elements of Möbius geometry of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\cup\infty
are modelled in the space

of homogeneous coordinates as follows:

•

Theorem A.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{Q}\cap\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be a general complex Darboux cyclide. There are
n+2
different ways to generate the Darboux cyclide
\mathcal{D}
as the envelope of a
(n-1)
-dimensional family of hyperspheres in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
that are twice tangent to
\mathcal{D}
. For each generation
i
, the hyperspheres have a common orthogonal hypersphere
\mathcal{S}_{i}
. The
n+2
common orthogonal hyperspheres
\mathcal{S}_{i}
are pairwise orthogonal. Consider the Darboux cyclide
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{D})
in
\overline{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
. For each generation of
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{D})
, the centres of the generating spheres are contained in a quadric hypersurface of
\overline{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
. These
n+2
quadric hypersurfaces are confocal.

Proof.

There are
n+2
degenerate quadric hypersurfaces in the pencil spanned by
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\mathcal{K}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{K}_{n+2}
be the degenerate quadrics. Let
A_{i}
be the point that is the apex of
\mathcal{K}_{i}
. Any tangent hyperplane of
\mathcal{K}_{i}
intersects
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
in a hypersphere of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. The tangent hyperplane is tangent to
\mathcal{K}_{i}
along the points of an isotropic line that passes through the apex
A_{i}
. The isotropic line intersects
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
in two points. The hypersphere is tangent to
\mathcal{D}
at the two foregoing points. The cone
\mathcal{K}_{i}
has a
(n-1)
-dimensional family of tangent hyperplanes. They intersect
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
in a
(n-1)
-dimensional family of hyperspheres that are orthogonal to the hypersphere
A_{i}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
, where
A_{i}^{\perp}
is the polar hyperplane of
A_{i}
relative to
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Consider an arbitrary plane that contains two of the points
A_{i},\ldots,A_{n+2}
. Generically, the plane intersects
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{Q}\cap\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
in four points. The four points determine a planar quad such that its two Laplace points are two of the points
A_{i}
. By Lemma
3.2
, the two Laplace points are conjugate relative to
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Therefore, any two of the points in
A_{i}
must be conjugate relative to
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Equivalently, the spheres
\mathcal{S}_{i}=A_{i}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
are pairwise orthogonal.

Let
\mathcal{K}^{\perp}_{i}
be the polar quadric of
\mathcal{K}_{i}
relative to
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Each
\mathcal{K}^{\perp}_{i}
is a
(n-1)
-dimensional non-degenerate quadric that is contained in the hyperplane
A_{i}^{\perp}
. By Lemma
A.1
, the centres of the hyperspheres in
\overline{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
of the
n+2
different generations of
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{D})
are exactly the points of the quadrics
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}^{\perp}_{i})
.

Let
N_{\mathbb{C}}^{\perp}
be the tangent hyperplane of
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
at the point
N_{\mathbb{C}}
. The intersection
N_{\mathbb{C}}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{D}
projects under
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}
to the quadric
\mathscr{C}^{n-2}
. The quadrics
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}^{\perp}_{i})
are confocal quadrics because they belong to the dual of a pencil of quadrics such that
\mathscr{C}^{n-2}
also belongs to the dual pencil. To see this, consider an arbitrary point in
\mathscr{C}^{n-2}
. It can be described as the projection
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(P)
of some point
P\in N_{\mathbb{C}}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{D}
. Let
P^{\perp}
be the polar hyperplane relative to
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. The point
P
is contained in all of the quadrics
\mathcal{K}_{i}
. Dually,
P^{\perp}
is tangent to all of the quadrics
\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\perp}
. The hyperplane
P^{\perp}
intersects
\overline{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
in a
(n-1)
-dimensional projective subspace that is tangent to
\mathscr{C}^{n-2}
at the point where the line
P\vee N_{\mathbb{C}}
intersects
\mathscr{C}^{n-2}
. Each quadric
\mathcal{K}^{\perp}_{i}
is tangent to the hyperplane
P^{\perp}
. So, each projected quadric
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}^{\perp}_{i})
is tangent to the foregoing
(n-1)
-dimensional projective subspace that is tangent to
\mathscr{C}^{n-2}
. By Definition
A.3
, this means that the
n+2
quadrics are confocal.

Bregman Circumcenters: Monotonicity and Forward Weak Convergence
Hui Ouyang

Abstract
Recently, we systematically studied the basic theory of Bregman circumcenters in another paper. In this work, we aim to apply Bregman circumcenters to optimization algorithms. Here, we propose the forward Bregman monotonicity which is a generalization of the powerful Fejér monotonicity and show a weak convergence result of the forward Bregman monotone sequence. We also naturally introduce the Bregman circumcenter mappings associated with a finite set of operators. Then we provide sufficient conditions for the sequence of iterations of the forward Bregman circumcenter mapping to be forward Bregman monotone. Furthermore, we prove that the sequence of iterations of the forward Bregman circumcenter mapping weakly converges to a point in the intersection of the fixed point sets of relevant operators, which reduces to the known weak convergence result of the circumcentered method under the Euclidean distance. In addition, particular examples are provided to illustrate the Bregman isometry and Browder’s demiclosedness principle, and our convergence result.

Forward Bregman monotonicity

]

Definition 3.1
.

A sequence
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{H}
is
forward Bregman monotone with respect to a set

if
C\cap\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f\neq\varnothing

\displaystyle(\forall c\in C\cap\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f)~(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad\operatorname{D}_{f}(x_{k+1},c)\leq\operatorname{D}_{f}(x_{k},c).

Fact 3.2
.

Suppose that
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
with
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f\neq\varnothing
, that
f
is Gâteaux differentiable on
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f
, and that
f
is essentially strictly convex. Then

is strictly convex on
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f
, which is equivalent to

\displaystyle(\forall x\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f)(\forall y\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f)\quad x\neq y\Rightarrow\left\langle x-y,\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\right\rangle>0.

Proof.

Because
f
is convex, by
[
7
, Propositions 3.45(ii), 8.2 and 16.27]
,
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f
is a convex subset of
\operatorname{dom}\partial f
. Then employ
Definition

Item

(ii)
to see that
f
is strictly convex on
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f
.
Hence, the last equivalence is obtained by applying
[
7

to the function

∎

Theorem 3.3

Suppose that
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})

(i)

\left(\forall c\in C\cap\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f\right)

\left(\operatorname{D}_{f}(x_{k},c)\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is decreasing, nonnegative and convergent.

(ii)

Let

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\langle\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(z),x_{k}\right\rangle

(iii)

(iv)

Suppose that

is essentially strictly convex. Then

Proof.

Item

(i)
: This is clear from

.

Item

(ii)
: According to

\displaystyle\operatorname{D}_{f}(x_{k},y)-\operatorname{D}_{f}(x_{k},z)=f(x_{k})-f(y)-\left\langle\nabla f(y),x_{k}-y\right\rangle-\Big(f(x_{k})-f(z)-\left\langle\nabla f(z),x_{k}-z\right\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla f(z)-\nabla f(y),x_{k}\right\rangle=\operatorname{D}_{f}(x_{k},y)-\operatorname{D}_{f}(x_{k},z)+f(y)-f(z)-\left\langle\nabla f(y),y\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla f(z),z\right\rangle.

: Let

Then based on

Item

:
“
\Rightarrow
”
: This is trivial.

“
\Leftarrow
”

Because the boundedness of

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla f(\bar{x})-\nabla f(\hat{x}),x_{k_{t}}\right\rangle\to\left\langle\nabla f(\bar{x})-\nabla f(\hat{x}),\hat{x}\right\rangle.

4
Bregman circumcenter methods

As we mentioned in the introduction, projection methods based on Bregman distances have broad applications and some circumcentered methods accelerate the method of cyclic projections for finding the best approximation point onto, or a feasibility point in, the intersection of finitely many closed convex sets.
In this section, we introduce backward and forward Bregman (pseudo-)circumcenter mappings and methods. We shall also investigate the convergence of the forward Bregman circumcenter method.

Throughout this section, suppose that
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
with
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f\neq\varnothing
and that
f
is Gâteaux differentiable on
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f
. Set
\operatorname{I}:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
.

Bregman circumcenter mappings

Henceforth, for every
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\text{denote by }\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D})\text{ the set of all nonempty subsets of }\mathcal{D}\text{ containing finitely many elements}.

Suppose that
K\in\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{dom}f)
. Set

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{E}_{f}(K):=\{q\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f~:~(\forall x\in K)~\operatorname{D}_{f}(x,q)\text{ is identical}\}.

Suppose additionally
K\in\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f)
. Denote by

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{E}_{f}(K):=\{p\in\operatorname{dom}f~:~(\forall y\in K)~\operatorname{D}_{f}(p,y)\text{ is identical}\}.

Definition 4.1
.

[
14
, Definition 3.1]

Let
K\in\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f)
.

(i)

Define the
backward Bregman circumcenter operator
\overleftarrow{CC{}}
w.r.t.
f
as

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{CC{}}:\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f)\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}:K\mapsto\operatorname{aff}\,(K)\cap\overleftarrow{E}_{f}(K).

(ii)

Define the
backward Bregman pseudo-circumcenter operator
\overleftarrow{CC{}}^{ps}
w.r.t.
f
as

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{CC{}}^{ps}:\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f)\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}:K\mapsto\operatorname{aff}\,(\nabla f(K))\cap\overleftarrow{E}_{f}(K).

In particular, for every
K\in\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f)
, we call the element in
\overleftarrow{CC{}}(K)
and
\overleftarrow{CC{}}^{ps}(K)

Backward Bregman circumcenter and Backward Bregman pseudo-circumcenter of
K
, respectively.

Definition 4.2
.

[
14
, Definition 4.1]

Let
K\in\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{dom}f)
.

(i)

Define the
forward Bregman circumcenter operator w.r.t.
f
as

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{CC{}}:\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{dom}f)\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}:K\mapsto\operatorname{aff}\,(K)\cap\overrightarrow{E}_{f}(K).

(ii)

Define the
forward Bregman pseudo-circumcenter operator w.r.t.

as

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{CC{}}^{ps}:\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{dom}f)\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}:K\mapsto\left(\nabla f^{*}\left(\operatorname{aff}\,K\right)\right)\cap\overrightarrow{E}_{f}(K).

In particular, for every
K\in\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{dom}f)
, we call the element in
\overrightarrow{CC{}}(K)
and
\overrightarrow{CC{}}^{ps}(K)

forward Bregman circumcenter and forward Bregman pseudo-circumcenter of
K
, respectively.

Suppose that
\operatorname{D}_{f}
is symmetric, that is,
\operatorname{dom}f=\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f
and
(\forall\{x,y\}\subseteq\operatorname{dom}f)

\operatorname{D}_{f}(x,y)=\operatorname{D}_{f}(y,x)

, then

and hence, the notions backward Bregman circumcenter, backward Bregman pseudo-circumcenter, forward Bregman circumcenter and forward Bregman pseudo-circumcenter are all the same and reduce to the circumcenter defined in
[

under the Euclidean distance.

From now on, for every set-valued operator

, if

is a singleton for some

, we sometimes by slight abuse of notation allow

to stand for its unique element. The intended meaning should be clear from the context.

Bregman isometry and Browder’s Demiclosedness Principle

In this subsection, we generalize the traditional isometry and Browder’s Demiclosedness Principle from the Euclidean distance to general Bregman distances and
investigate the Bregman isometry and Browder’s Demiclosedness Principle, which play critical roles in the main result in this section. The Bregman isometry and Browder’s Demiclosedness Principle are interesting in their own right.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a nonempty subset of
\mathcal{H}

\left(Tx,Ty\right)\in\operatorname{dom}f\times\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f

\displaystyle\left(\forall\{x,y\}\subseteq\operatorname{dom}f\times\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f\right)~\operatorname{D}_{f}\left(Tx,Ty\right)=\operatorname{D}_{f}(x,y).

In view of the definition, it is trivial that given an arbitrary function

satisfying the statements of

2.1
, the identity operator

is Bregman isometric w.r.t.

Notice that if

(

Remark 4.13
.

We uphold assumptions in
Theorem

4.12
and have a closer look at the condition
Equation

4.9
. As we mentioned before, in the classical Euclidean distance, the condition
Equation

4.9
is immediate from the triangle inequality of the Euclidean distance. Because generally Bregman distances do not obey the triangle inequality,
Equation

4.9
is no longer trivial if
f\neq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
. We explain below that, even if the classical triangle inequality may not hold in a general Bregman distance, there is no need to be pessimistic.

Let
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be in
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f
. According to
Definition

4.4
and
Equation

4.2
,

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})(\forall i\in\operatorname{I})\quad\operatorname{D}_{f}(T_{i}u_{k},\overrightarrow{CC{}}_{\mathcal{S}}u_{k})=\operatorname{D}_{f}(u_{k},\overrightarrow{CC{}}_{\mathcal{S}}u_{k}),

which yields that
Equation

4.9
is equivalent to the following statement

\displaystyle\operatorname{D}_{f}(u_{k},\overrightarrow{CC{}}_{\mathcal{S}}u_{k})\to 0\Rightarrow(\forall i\in\operatorname{I})\operatorname{D}_{f}(u_{k},T_{i}u_{k})\to 0.

Combine this with
Definition

2.1
to ensure that
Equation

4.9
holds if and only if

\displaystyle f\left(u_{k}\right)-f\left(\overrightarrow{CC{}}_{\mathcal{S}}u_{k}\right)-\left\langle\nabla f\left(\overrightarrow{CC{}}_{\mathcal{S}}u_{k}\right),u_{k}-\overrightarrow{CC{}}_{\mathcal{S}}u_{k}\right\rangle\to 0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle(\forall i\in\operatorname{I})f\left(u_{k}\right)-f\left(T_{i}u_{k}\right)-\left\langle\nabla f\left(T_{i}u_{k}\right),u_{k}-T_{i}u_{k}\right\rangle\to 0.

Therefore, we observe that
Equation

4.9
depends on not only the function
f
but also the set
\mathcal{S}:=\{\operatorname{Id},T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}\}
. For example,

(i)

if
\mathcal{S}=\{\operatorname{Id}\}
, then
Equation

4.9
holds for an arbitrary function
f
satisfying the statements of
Definition

2.1
;

(ii)

if there exists a particular constant
\rho\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
such that
(\forall i\in\operatorname{I})

(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})

\operatorname{D}_{f}(u_{k},T_{i}u_{k})\leq\rho\operatorname{D}_{f}(u_{k},\overrightarrow{CC{}}_{\mathcal{S}}u_{k})
, then
Equation

4.9
is also satisfied.

Notice that if we can find a function
f\neq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{2}
such that
(\forall\{x,y,z\}\subseteq\mathcal{H})

D_{f}(x,y)\leq D_{f}(x,z)+D_{f}(z,y)
, then we obtain
Equation

4.9
immediately. (We believe such functions actually have a wide range of applications in various areas.)
Based on our explanations in
Remark

4.13
, even if we cannot find such beautiful functions, there is a large probability that there exist some special functions
f
together with appropriate sets
\mathcal{S}:=\{\operatorname{Id},T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}\}
(the set might be dependent on the corresponding
f
) such that
Equation

4.9
is satisfied.

Given a function
f
and a set
\mathcal{S}
, there may be infinitely many sequences
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}f
satisfying
\operatorname{D}_{f}(u_{k},\overrightarrow{CC{}}_{\mathcal{S}}u_{k})\to 0
and
(\forall i\in\operatorname{I})

\operatorname{D}_{f}(T_{i}u_{k},\overrightarrow{CC{}}_{\mathcal{S}}u_{k})\to 0
, so the workload of verifying
Equation

4.9
might be really large if we don’t use tricks like
Remark

4.13

(ii)
or some better ones. The following result demonstrates that to show the weak convergence of forward Bregman circumcenter methods,
the condition
Equation

4.9
might be unnecessary.

x_{k_{j}}{\;\operatorname{\rightharpoonup}\;}\bar{x}
and
(\forall i\in\operatorname{I})

\operatorname{D}_{f}(x_{k_{j}},T_{i}x_{k_{j}})\to 0

T_{i}
is
f
-demiclosed to necessitate that
\bar{x}\in\cap_{i\in\operatorname{I}}\operatorname{Fix}T_{i}
.

Altogether, the proof is done.

Example 4.16
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Define
f:x\mapsto\sum^{n}_{i=1}x_{i}\ln(x_{i})+(1-x_{i})\ln(1-x_{i})
, with
\operatorname{dom}f=\left[0,1\right]^{n}
, which is the Fermi-Dirac entropy.
Let
\Lambda
be a subset of
\operatorname{J}:=\{1,\ldots,n\}

Define

by
\left(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

(\forall i\in\operatorname{J}\smallsetminus\Lambda)

and
(\forall i\in\Lambda)

y_{i}=1-x_{i}
. Set
\mathcal{S}:=\{\operatorname{Id},T\}
.
Let
x:=(x_{i})_{i\in\operatorname{J}}\in\left[0,1\right]^{n}
. Denote by
\Phi:=\{i\in\operatorname{J}~:~x_{i}\neq\frac{1}{2}\}
.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

\overrightarrow{E}_{f}(\mathcal{S}(x))=\left\{(p_{i})_{i\in\operatorname{J}}\in\left]0,1\right[^{n}~:~0=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}(2x_{i}-1)\ln\left(\frac{p_{i}}{1-p_{i}}\right)\right\}
.

(ii)

\overrightarrow{CC{}}_{\mathcal{S}}(x)=\left\{(p_{i})_{i\in\operatorname{J}}\in\Big(\left]0,1\right[^{n}\cap\operatorname{aff}\,\{x,1-x\}\Big)~:~0=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}(2x_{i}-1)\ln\left(\frac{p_{i}}{1-p_{i}}\right)\right\}

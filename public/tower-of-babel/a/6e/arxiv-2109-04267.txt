Realizations of the formal double Eisenstein space
Henrik Bachmann, Ulf Kühn, Nils Matthes

Abstract
. We introduce the formal double Eisenstein space ℰ k \mathcal{E}_{k} , which is a generalization of the formal double zeta space 𝒟 k \mathcal{D}_{k} of Gangl–Kaneko–Zagier, and prove analogues of the sum formula and parity result for formal double Eisenstein series. We show that ℚ \mathbb{Q} -linear maps ℰ k → A \mathcal{E}_{k}\rightarrow A , for some ℚ \mathbb{Q} -algebra A A , can be constructed from formal Laurent series (with coefficients in A A ) that satisfy the Fay identity. As the prototypical example, we define the Kronecker realization ρ 𝔎 : ℰ k → ℚ ⁡ [ [ q ] ] \rho^{\mathfrak{K}}:\mathcal{E}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}[[q]] , which lifts Gangl–Kaneko–Zagier’s Bernoulli realization ρ B : 𝒟 k → ℚ \rho^{B}:\mathcal{D}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q} , and whose image consists of quasimodular forms for the full modular group. As an application to the theory of modular forms, we obtain a purely combinatorial proof of Ramanujan’s differential equations for classical Eisenstein series.

1.
Introduction

Double zeta values
are real numbers, defined for integers
k_{1}\geq 2
,
k_{2}\geq 1
, by

\zeta(k_{1},k_{2})=\sum_{m>n>0}\frac{1}{m^{k_{1}}n^{k_{2}}}\,.

The integer
k:=k_{1}+k_{2}
is the
weight
of
\zeta(k_{1},k_{2})
.
As was first observed by Euler, double zeta values arise naturally when multiplying the single zeta values
\zeta(k)=\sum_{n>0}n^{-k}
, for
k\geq 2
. More precisely, one has the identities

(1.1)

\displaystyle\zeta(k_{1})\zeta(k_{2})

\displaystyle=\zeta(k_{1},k_{2})+\zeta(k_{2},k_{1})+\zeta(k_{1}+k_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}\left(\binom{j-1}{k_{1}-1}+\binom{j-1}{k_{2}-1}\right)\zeta(j,k_{1}+k_{2}-j)\,,

valid for all
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 2
, which are referred to as the
double shuffle relations
.

A natural question to ask is which other relations among double zeta values can be deduced from (
1.1
). In order to address this question, Gangl–Kaneko–Zagier introduced the (weight
k
)
formal double zeta space

\mathcal{D}_{k}
, which is
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly spanned by formal symbols
Z_{k_{1},k_{2}}
,
P_{k_{1},k_{2}}
,
Z_{k}
(with
k=k_{1}+k_{2}
) subject to the double shuffle relations. Several other identities among double zeta values then hold in
\mathcal{D}_{k}
; for example, Euler’s result (every double zeta value of odd weight can be written in terms of single zeta values and products thereof) is true in
\mathcal{D}_{k}
.
1
1

1

On the other hand, it is also proved in
[
GKZ
]
that double zeta values satisfy ‘exotic’ relations, which do not hold in
\mathcal{D}_{k}
, and which come from cusp forms for the full modular group.

Solutions to the double shuffle equations in a general
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
A
correspond to
\mathbb{Q}
-linear maps
\rho_{k}:\mathcal{D}_{k}\rightarrow A
, called
realizations
(of
\mathcal{D}_{k}
). The archetypal example is the
Euler realization

\rho^{E}_{k}:\mathcal{D}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that maps the generators of
\mathcal{D}_{k}
to (double) zeta values in the natural way.
2
2

2

Some care must be taken in defining
\rho^{E}_{k}
for
Z_{k_{1},k_{2}}
,
P_{k_{1},k_{2}}
with
k_{1}=1
. For simplicity of the exposition, we ignore this issue in the introduction.
Another example is the
Bernoulli realization

\rho^{B}_{k}:\mathcal{D}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
, which sends the generator
Z_{k}
to
-B_{k}/2k!
and the
Z_{k_{1},k_{2}}
to certain linear combinations of products of Bernoulli numbers. The Bernoulli realization is of arithmetic interest as it gives an explicit construction of a rational associator in depth two, a problem raised by Drinfeld,
[
D
]
.
3
3

3

More recently, Brown gave an explicit construction of a rational associator
(\tau^{(d)})_{1\leq d\leq 3}
up to and including depth three,
[
Br
]
. It differs from Gangl–Kaneko–Zagier’s starting in weight
8
(the smallest weight for which there exists a double zeta value that, conjecturally, cannot be written as a product of single zeta values).

From the perspective of (quasi-)modular forms, Bernoulli numbers arise naturally as constant terms in the Fourier expansion of the classical Eisenstein series

\displaystyle G_{k}(q)=-\frac{B_{k}}{2k!}+\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{k-1}q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}\,.

Therefore, one may wonder whether there exists a realization with values in the algebra of quasimodular forms, which gives back the Bernoulli realization upon taking constant terms. In this paper, we address this problem by introducing the
formal double Eisenstein space

\mathcal{E}_{k}
, which is equipped with a canonical
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
\pi_{k}:\mathcal{E}_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{k}
. We construct a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
\rho^{\mathfrak{K}}_{k}:\mathcal{E}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}[G_{2},G_{4},G_{6}]
, called the
Kronecker realization
, which fits into a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}[G_{2},G_{4},G_{6}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}\,,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{\mathfrak{K}}_{k}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{k}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q\,\mapsto\,0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{B}_{k}}

where the right-hand vertical arrow maps
G_{k}
to the constant term in its Fourier expansion.
4
4

4

The Kronecker realization should not be confused with the Eisenstein realization
\rho^{Eis}_{k}:\mathcal{D}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}[[q]]
constructed in
[
GKZ
]
; indeed, the image of the latter consists of double Eisenstein series
G_{k_{1},k_{2}}(q)
, which are in general not (quasi)modular forms. A lift of the Eisenstein realization to
\mathcal{E}_{k}
was constructed in
[
B2
]
.
The Kronecker realization is constructed from its namesake, the
Kronecker function

\mathfrak{K}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-X-mY}q^{m}}{1-q^{m}e^{-X}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{Y+mX}q^{m}}{1-q^{m}e^{Y}}\,,

which plays a central role in the study of elliptic functions (cf.
[
W
, p.73]
). For our purposes, its crucial property is the
Fay identity
,
[
BL
,
Mu
]
,

\mathfrak{K}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1}\mathfrak{K}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_2}{Y_2}+\mathfrak{K}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{-X_2}{Y_1-Y_2}\mathfrak{K}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1+X_2}{Y_1}+\mathfrak{K}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{-X_1-X_2}{-Y_2}\mathfrak{K}_{q}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1}{Y_1-Y_2}=0\,,

familiar from the theory of theta functions and which, in this case, goes back essentially to Riemann (cf.
[
Z
, Proposition 5]
). More generally, we prove that every solution to the Fay identity can be used to construct a realization of the formal double Eisenstein space.

The formal double Eisenstein space
\mathcal{E}_{k}
shares many similarities with the formal double zeta space
\mathcal{D}_{k}
. For example, we prove a sum formula (analogous to
[
GKZ
, Theorem 1]
) and also a parity result for
\mathcal{E}_{k}
. Moreover, the projection
\pi_{k}:\mathcal{E}_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{k}
admits a canonical, explicit, splitting
\sigma_{k}:\mathcal{D}_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{k}
. Using these two maps, every realization
\rho:\mathcal{E}_{k}\rightarrow A
induces one of
\mathcal{D}_{k}
via pullback:
\rho\circ\sigma_{k}:\mathcal{D}_{k}\rightarrow A
; and vice versa with
\pi_{k}
in place of
\sigma_{k}
. This establishes a close connection between the two spaces and its various realizations.
As in the work of Gangl–Kaneko–Zagier, a key technical role is played by the modular group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and its action on polynomials. More precisely, Gangl–Kaneko–Zagier consider the standard right action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbb{Q}[Y_{1},Y_{2}]
, whereas we consider a lift of this action to
\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},X_{2},Y_{1},Y_{2}]
which, on the
X
-variables, is the standard action conjugated by the element
S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. We remark that, with completely different terminology, this modified action also plays a role in Ecalle’s mould theory, where the Fay identity appears under the name
tripartite equation
,
[
E
, §3.2]
.

Finally, we mention that double zeta values are depth two versions of multiple zeta values and that the double shuffle relations naturally extend to multiple zeta values. In upcoming work
[
BIM
]
, we will generalize the results of this paper by introducing the algebra of
formal multiple Eisenstein series
. One can define a subalgebra of
formal quasimodular forms
of this algebra for which many relations among modular forms can be proved on a purely formal level. Some results obtained in this work were announced in
[
BBK
]
(with slightly different notation).

2.
Formal double Eisenstein space

Motivated by the relations (
1.1
), Gangl, Kaneko and Zagier,
[
GKZ
]
, defined the
formal double zeta space
, for an integer
k\geq 1
, to be the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{k}=\mathchoice{\text{\raise 4.30554pt\hbox{$\big\langle Z_{k},Z_{k_{1},k_{2}},P_{k_{1},k_{2}}\mid k_{1}+k_{2}=k,k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1\big\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}$}\Big/\lower 4.30554pt\hbox{$\eqref{eq:dzrel}$}}}{\big\langle Z_{k},Z_{k_{1},k_{2}},P_{k_{1},k_{2}}\mid k_{1}+k_{2}=k,k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1\big\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}\,/\,\eqref{eq:dzrel}}{\big\langle Z_{k},Z_{k_{1},k_{2}},P_{k_{1},k_{2}}\mid k_{1}+k_{2}=k,k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1\big\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}\,/\,\eqref{eq:dzrel}}{\big\langle Z_{k},Z_{k_{1},k_{2}},P_{k_{1},k_{2}}\mid k_{1}+k_{2}=k,k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1\big\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}\,/\,\eqref{eq:dzrel}}\,,

which is spanned by formal symbols
Z_{k}
,
Z_{k_{1},k_{2}}
, and
P_{k_{1},k_{2}}
, satisfying the relations

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{k_{1},k_{2}}&=Z_{k_{1},k_{2}}+Z_{k_{2},k_{1}}+Z_{k_{1}+k_{2}}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}\left(\binom{j-1}{k_{1}-1}+\binom{j-1}{k_{2}-1}\right)Z_{j,k_{1}+k_{2}-j}\,.\end{split}

The following definition generalizes the formal double zeta space.

.

.

For all

\displaystyle\pi\left(\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,X_2}{Y_1,Y_2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{z}(X_{1},X_{2})+\mathfrak{z}(Y_{1}+Y_{2},Y_{1})\,.

Applying
\pi
to the relations (
2.2
), we obtain a relation for
\mathcal{D}_{k}
by using (
2.1
).
∎

In the other direction, we have the following proposition.

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}:Z_{k_{1},k_{2}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,k_2}{0,0}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{k_{2},1}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1}{1}-\delta_{k_{1},1}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_2}{1}+\delta_{k_{1},2}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_2+1}{1}\right)\,,

Proof.

As before, the content of this proposition is that

By Proposition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{k}}

Proposition 2.7
.

For all
k\geq 1
, the following gives a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
\partial_{k}:\mathcal{E}_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{k+2}

\displaystyle\partial_{k}:G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k}{d}

\displaystyle\longmapsto kG\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k+1}{d+1}\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{k}:G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,k_2}{d_1,d_2}

\displaystyle\longmapsto k_{1}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1+1,k_2}{d_1+1,d_2}+k_{2}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,k_2+1}{d_1,d_2+1}\,.

Proof.

This can be checked on the level of coefficients by applying
\partial_{k}
to (
2.2

\partial
the map on generating series which is given on the coefficients of the degree

\displaystyle\partial\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}=\frac{d}{dX}\frac{d}{dY}\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X}{Y}\,,\qquad\partial\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,X_2}{Y_1,Y_2}=\left(\frac{d}{dX_{1}}\frac{d}{dY_{1}}+\frac{d}{dX_{2}}\frac{d}{dY_{2}}\right)\mathfrak{G}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{X_1,X_2}{Y_1,Y_2}\,.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dX_{1}}\frac{d}{dY_{1}}+\frac{d}{dX_{2}}\frac{d}{dY_{2}}\right)=\left(\frac{d}{d(X_{1}+X_{2})}\frac{d}{dY_{2}}+\frac{d}{dX_{1}}\frac{d}{d(Y_{1}-Y_{2})}\right)\,.

Lemma 3.1

For

(ii)

In

Now denote by

the space of

with rational coefficients,
[

]
. It is graded for the weight,

Proposition 3.7
.

For every
K\geq 1
, the Kronecker function gives rise to a realization
\rho^{\mathfrak{K}}_{K}:\mathcal{E}_{K}\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{K}
, the
Kronecker realization
, which is given by

\displaystyle\rho^{\mathfrak{K}}_{K}:G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k}{d}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\frac{(k-d-1)!}{(k-1)!}\left(q\frac{d}{dq}\right)^{d}G_{k-d},\qquad(k>d\geq 0,k+d=K\text{ even})\,,

and for
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1
, with

even, by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho^{\mathfrak{K}}_{K}:P\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,k_2}{0,0}&\longmapsto\,G_{k_{1}}G_{k_{2}}\,,\\
\rho^{\mathfrak{K}}_{K}:G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,k_2}{0,0}&\longmapsto\,\frac{1}{3}G_{k_{1}}G_{k_{2}}+\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}}}{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+l_{2}=k_{1}+k_{2}\\

&-\frac{1}{12}\left(5+3(-1)^{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}{k_{1}-1}-(-1)^{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}{k_{1}}\right)G_{k_{1}+k_{2}}\\

where

, for

, and

.

Proof.

This follows by using
\mathfrak{K}_{q}
in Corollary
3.4
together with Lemma
3.5
and then considering the coefficients of the resulting power series. That the image of this realization is precisely the space

(see, for example,

∎

In particular, we can also consider just the constant terms in the Kronecker function and obtain a realization of

Proposition 3.8
.

For every

, the following diagram commutes
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{k}}

Denote by

4.
Families of relations in
\mathcal{E}_{k}

In this section, we describe some consequences of the defining relation (
2.2
) of the formal double Eisenstein space. Applying one of the various realizations discussed before then gives relations among either Eisenstein series and their derivatives, Bernoulli numbers, or zeta values.
Firstly we have the following analogue of the sum formula,
[
GKZ
, Theorem 1]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

(Sum formula) For
k\geq 2
and
d\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}=k\\
d_{1}+d_{2}=d\\
(k_{1},d_{2})\neq(1,0)\end{subarray}}(-1)^{d_{2}}\,\binom{d}{d_{2}}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,k_2}{d_1,d_2}=G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k}{d}-\frac{1}{d+1}G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k-1}{d+1}\,,

where the sum runs over all
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1
and all
d_{1},d_{2}\geq 0
.

Proof.

This follows by considering the coefficient of
X^{k-2}Y^{d}
in (
2.3
) after setting
X_{1}=X,X_{2}=0,Y_{1}=0
and
Y_{2}=Y
.
∎

Similarly to
[
GKZ
, Theorem 1]
, one can also give odd and even variants of the above sum formula, which we will omit here. A more interesting family of relations can be obtained from the following lemma, which generalizes
[
B3
, Lemma 4.5]
, and which we state again by using the group actions on the generating series
\mathfrak{P}
,
\mathfrak{G}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{G}_{2}
of the elements in
\mathcal{E}_{k}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
A=\epsilon U\epsilon
we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{2}\mid(1-\sigma)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{P}\mid\!(1-\delta)(1+A-SA^{2})-(\mathfrak{R}^{\ast}_{\mathfrak{G}_{1}}+\mathfrak{R}^{\shuffle}_{\mathfrak{G}_{1}}\mid\!T^{-1}\epsilon)\mid\!(1+A+A^{2})\,.

Proof.

First, notice that
A=\epsilon U\epsilon=T\epsilon T^{-1}\epsilon=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&1\end{pmatrix}
and that we have
A^{3}=\sigma
. Now recall that by (
2.3
)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{G}_{2}{\mid\!(1+\epsilon)}+\mathfrak{R}_{\mathfrak{G}_{1}}^{\ast}=\mathfrak{G}_{2}{\mid T(1+\epsilon)}+\mathfrak{R}_{\mathfrak{G}_{1}}^{\shuffle}\,,

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{2}\mid\!\epsilon

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{G}_{2}+\mathfrak{P}-\mathfrak{R}_{\mathfrak{G}_{1}}^{\ast}\,,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{2}\mid\!T\epsilon T^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{G}_{2}+\mathfrak{P}\mid\!T^{-1}-\mathfrak{R}_{\mathfrak{G}_{1}}^{\shuffle}\mid\!T^{-1}\,.

This implies

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{2}\mid\!A

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{G}_{2}\mid\!(T\epsilon T^{-1})\epsilon=\left(-\mathfrak{G}_{2}+\mathfrak{P}\mid\!T^{-1}-\mathfrak{R}_{\mathfrak{G}_{1}}^{\shuffle}\mid\!T^{-1}\right)\mid\!\epsilon

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{G}_{2}+\underbrace{\mathfrak{P}\mid\!(T^{-1}\epsilon-1)+\mathfrak{R}_{\mathfrak{G}_{1}}^{\ast}-\mathfrak{R}^{\shuffle}_{\mathfrak{G}_{1}}\mid(T^{-1}\epsilon)}_{=:\mathfrak{K}}\,.

Iterating this identity two more times yields

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{2}\mid\!A^{3}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{G}_{2}+\mathfrak{K}\mid\!(1+A+A^{2})\,.

By direct calculation, one can check that the action of
(T^{-1}\epsilon-1)(1+A+A^{2})
and
-(1-\delta)(1+A-SA^{2})
is the same on
\mathfrak{P}
, since
\mathfrak{P}\mid\epsilon=\mathfrak{P}
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

(Parity) For
K\geq 3
odd, every
G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{k_1,k_2}{d_1,d_2}
with
k_{1}+k_{2}+d_{1}+d_{2}=K
can be written as a linear combination of
G\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{-}{-}
and
P\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{}{\,-\,,\,-\,}{\,-\,,\,-\,}
.

Proof.

Due to the action of
(1-\sigma)
in Lemma
4.2
, the even weight terms on the left-hand side vanish, and the odd weight terms just get multiplied by
2
. This gives an explicit representation of the coefficients in
\mathfrak{G}_{2}
as a linear combination of the coefficients in
\mathfrak{G}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{P}
.
∎

Applying
\pi_{k}
and then the Euler realization mentioned in the introduction, Theorem
4.3
implies in particular Euler’s result that every double zeta value of odd weight can be written as a linear combination of products of single zeta values.
An explicit formula which gives the special case of Theorem
4.3
can be found in
[

, Theorem 4.6]
, where the corresponding statement for
\mathcal{D}_{k}
is given. Applying

then gives the

The next theorem gives the even weight case of Lemma
4.2
. Again we merely state a special case by giving the formula coming from the coefficients of

For all

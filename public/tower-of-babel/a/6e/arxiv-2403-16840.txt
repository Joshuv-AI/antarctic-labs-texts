High entropy measures on the space of lattices with escape of mass
Taehyeong Kim

Abstract
. For any diagonal element a a with two eigenvalues, we construct a sequence of a a -invariant probability measures on the space of unimodular lattices with high entropy but converging to the zero measure. This extends the result of Kadyrov [Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems, 32 (1) (2012)].

1.
Introduction

Let
m,n
be positive integers. Consider the homogeneous space
X_{m+n}=\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathbb{Z})
, which can be identified with the space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
. We consider the diagonal flow

a_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{t/m}I_{m}&\\
&e^{-t/n}I_{n}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},

which naturally acts on
X_{m+n}
by left multiplication. We let
a=a_{1}
be the time-one map for the diagonal flow.

The following non-escape of mass result was proved in
[
KKLM17
]
using
Margulis functions
which originated in
[
EMM98
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
KKLM17
]

For any
h>0
and any sequence
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of
a
-invariant probability measures on
X_{m+n}
with
h_{\mu_{k}}(a)\geq h
, any weak
∗
limit
\mu
of the sequence satisfies

\mu(X_{m+n})\geq h-(m+n-1).

A similar statement was first proved in
[
ELMV12
]
for the
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
case. Since then, generalizations were considered in
[
EKP15
,
Mor22
]
for other rank
1
cases, as well as in
[
EK12
,
Mor
]
for higher rank cases. See also
[
DKMS
]
for “almost” invariant measures.

Note that the maximal entropy for the transformation
a
on
X_{m+n}
is
m+n
(see e.g.
[
EL10
]
) and Theorem
1.1
is nontrivial for
h\in(m+n-1,m+n]
. Kadyrov, Kleinbock, Lindenstrauss, and Margulis
[
KKLM17
]
conjectured that Theorem
1.1
is sharp in the sense that for any
h\in[m+n-1,m+n]
there should exist a sequence of probability invariant measures
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
with
\lim_{k\to\infty}h_{\mu_{k}}(a)=h
such that the limit measure
\mu
satisfies
\mu(X_{m+n})=h-(m+n-1)
. This was proved in
[
Kad12
]
when
\min(m,n)=1
.

In this article, we prove that the conjecture is true when
(m,n)\neq(1,1)
, hence combining with
[
Kad12
]
it is true for every
m
and
n
.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a sequence of
a
-invariant probability measures
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
on
X_{m+n}
with
\lim_{k\to\infty}h_{\mu_{k}}(a)=m+n-1
such that any weak
∗
limit measure is the zero measure.

Corollary 1.3
.

For any
h\in[m+n-1,m+n]
, there exists a sequence of
a
-invariant probability measures
(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
on
X_{m+n}
with
\lim_{k\to\infty}h_{\nu_{k}}(a)=h
such that any weak
∗
limit
\nu
satisfies
\nu(X_{m+n})=h-(m+n-1)
.

Structure of the paper.

In Section
2
, using variational principle developed in
[
DFSU24
]
, we estimate the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension for the set of matrices such that the diagonal orbit of the corresponding lattice stays in a certain compact region in
X_{m+n}
near the cusp.
In Section
3
, we construct an
a
-invariant probability measure on
X_{m+n}
with high entropy supported on that compact region near the cusp.
In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
.

2.
Dimension estimates using variational principle

In this section, we will estimate the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension for the set of matrices such that the diagonal orbit of the corresponding lattice stays in a certain compact region in
X_{m+n}
near the cusp. We make use of the variational principle in the parametric geometry of numbers developed in
[
DFSU24
]
.

For given positive integers
m
and
n
, denote
d=m+n
.
For each
j=1,\dots,d
, let
\lambda_{j}(\Lambda)
denote the
j
th successive minimum of a lattice
\Lambda
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e. the infimum of
\lambda
such that the set
\{\mathbf{r}\in\Lambda:\|\mathbf{r}\|\leq\lambda\}
contains
j
linearly independent vectors.
Given a matrix
A\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{R})
, we define the
successive minima function

\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{h}_{A}=(h_{1},\dots,h_{d}):[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the matrix
A
by

h_{i}(t)=\log\lambda_{i}(a_{t}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d}),\quad\text{where}\quad u_{A}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{m}&A\\
&I_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

We use the following notation:

[a,b]_{\mathbb{Z}}=[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{and}\quad(a,b]_{\mathbb{Z}}=(a,b]\cap\mathbb{Z}.

Now we recall the definition of templates in
[
DFSU24
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Template)
.

An
m\times n

template
is a piecewise linear map
\mathbf{f}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the following properties:

(1)

f_{1}\leq\cdots\leq f_{d}.

(2)

-\frac{1}{n}\leq f_{i}^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{m}
for all
i
.

(3)

For all
j=0,\dots,d
and for every interval
I
such that
f_{j}<f_{j+1}
on
I
, the function

\sum_{0<i\leq j}f_{i}
is convex and piecewise linear on
I
with slopes in the set

\left\{\frac{L_{+}}{m}-\frac{L_{-}}{n}:L_{+}\in[0,m]_{\mathbb{Z}},L_{-}\in[0,n]_{\mathbb{Z}},L_{+}+L_{-}=j\right\}.

As a convention, we use
f_{0}=-\infty
and
f_{d+1}=+\infty
.

Definition 2.2

(Contraction rate of a template)
.

Let
\mathbf{f}
be a template and
I
be an open interval on which
\mathbf{f}
is linear.
An
interval of equality
for
\mathbf{f}
on
I
is an interval
(p,q]_{\mathbb{Z}}
with
0\leq p<q\leq d
such that

f_{p}<f_{p+1}=\cdots=f_{q}<f_{q+1}\ \text{on}\ I.

For
0\leq p<q\leq d
with an interval of equality
(p,q]_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we let
M_{\pm}(p,q)
be the unique integers such that

M_{+}+M_{-}=q-p\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=p+1}^{q}f_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{M_{+}}{m}-\frac{M_{-}}{n}\text{ on }I.

Note that
M_{\pm}\geq 0
by (
2
) of Definition
2.1
. We also let

S_{+}=\bigcup_{(p,q]_{\mathbb{Z}}}(p,p+M_{+}(p,q)]_{\mathbb{Z}}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{-}=\bigcup_{(p,q]_{\mathbb{Z}}}(p+M_{+}(p,q),q]_{\mathbb{Z}}

where the unions are taken over all intervals of equality for
\mathbf{f}
on
I
. Define

\begin{split}\delta(\mathbf{f},I)&=\#\{(i_{+},i_{-})\in S_{+}\times S_{-}:i_{+}<i_{-}\};\\
\underline{\delta}(\mathbf{f})&=\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\delta(\mathbf{f},t)dt,\end{split}

where
\delta(\mathbf{f},t)
is the piecewise constant function with value
\delta(\mathbf{f},I)
on
I
.
The value
\underline{\delta}(\mathbf{f})
is called the
lower average contraction rate
of
\mathbf{f}
.

The following
[
DFSU24
, Theorem 4.7]
is a uniform version of the variational principle.

Theorem 2.3

(Uniform variational principle)
.

For all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C>0
such that for every template
\mathbf{f}
,

\dim_{H}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{f},C))\geq\underline{\delta}(\mathbf{f})-\epsilon,

where

\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{f},C)=\{A\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{R}):\|\mathbf{h}_{A}-\mathbf{f}\|\leq C\}.

In
[
DFSU24
, Section 9]
, the following special template was mainly used to estimate the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension of singular matrices. We also need that template for our purpose.

Definition 2.4

(Standard template)
.

Fix
0\leq t_{k}<t_{k+1}
and
\epsilon_{k},\epsilon_{k+1}\geq 0
and let
\Delta t=\Delta t_{k}=t_{k+1}-t_{k}
and
\Delta\epsilon=\Delta\epsilon_{k}=\epsilon_{k+1}-\epsilon_{k}
. Assume that
[
DFSU24
, Equations (9.1)–(9.3)]
hold.
The
standard template
defined by the two points
(t_{k},-\epsilon_{k})
and
(t_{k+1},\epsilon_{k+1})
is the partial template
\mathbf{f}:[t_{k},t_{k+1}]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined as follows:

•

Let
g_{1},g_{2}:[t_{k},t_{k+1}]\to\mathbb{R}
be piecewise linear functions such that
g_{i}(t_{j})=-\epsilon_{j}
, and
g_{i}
has two intervals of linearity: one on which
g_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{m}
and another on which
g_{i}^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{n}
. For
i=1
the latter interval comes first while for
i=2
the former interval comes first. Finally, let
g_{3}=\cdots=g_{d}
be chosen so that
g_{1}+\cdots+g_{d}=0
.

•

For each
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
let
\mathbf{f}(t)=\mathbf{g}(t)
if
g_{2}(t)\leq g_{3}(t)
; otherwise let
f_{1}(t)=g_{1}(t)
and let
f_{2}(t)=\cdots=f_{d}(t)
be chosen so that
f_{1}+\cdots+f_{d}=0
.

Denote the standard template defined by
(t_{k},-\epsilon_{k})
and
(t_{k+1},-\epsilon_{k+1})
by
\mathbf{s}[(t_{k},-\epsilon_{k}),(t_{k+1},-\epsilon_{k+1})]
.

Remark 2.5
.

As explained in
[
DFSU24
, Definition 9.1 and Lemma 9.2]
,
[
DFSU24
, Equations (9.1)–(9.3)]
is necessary to ensure the existence of the standard template and that it is indeed a balanced partial template.

We will choose
t_{k}=kt
and
t_{k+1}=(k+1)t
for some large
t>0
, and large
\epsilon_{k}=\epsilon_{k+1}>0
, hence
\Delta\epsilon_{k}=0
. Note that if
\Delta t_{k}=t
is large enough compared to
\epsilon_{k}
,
[
DFSU24
, Equations (9.1)–(9.3)]
hold unless
(m,n)=(1,1)
. If
(m,n)=(1,1)
, it follows from
[
DFSU24
, Equations (9.2) and (9.3)]
that
\epsilon_{k}=\epsilon_{k+1}=0
. Since we want to choose
\epsilon_{k}=\epsilon_{k+1}>0
large, we need to assume
(m,n)\neq(1,1)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose that
(m,n)\neq(1,1)
.
For any
\epsilon>0
, there are constants
\rho_{\epsilon},\eta_{\epsilon}>0
and
t_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\dim_{H}\{A\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{R}):\rho_{\epsilon}\leq\lambda_{1}(a_{t}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d})\leq\eta_{\epsilon}\text{ for all }t\geq t_{\epsilon}\}\geq mn-\frac{mn}{m+n}-\epsilon.

Moreover,
\rho_{\epsilon},\eta_{\epsilon}
converge to
0
as
\epsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

We will use the following notation: given any template
\mathbf{g}
and interval
[T_{1},T_{2}]
, we denote

\Delta(\mathbf{g},[T_{1},T_{2}])=\frac{1}{T_{2}-T_{1}}\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\delta(\mathbf{g},t)dt.

Fix
\epsilon>0
and let
C_{\epsilon}>0
be as in the statement of Theorem
2.3
. We may assume that
C_{\epsilon}\to\infty
as
\epsilon\to 0
by replacing
C_{\epsilon}
with
\max(C_{\epsilon},1/\epsilon)
.
Fix large
t>0
to be determined later. Define the template
\mathbf{f}
by

\mathbf{f}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{s}[(0,0),(t,-2C_{\epsilon})]&\text{ on }[0,t]\\
\mathbf{s}[(kt,-2C_{\epsilon}),((k+1)t,-2C_{\epsilon})]&\text{ on }[kt,(k+1)t]\text{ for all }k\geq 1.\end{cases}

Note that if
t>0
is large enough, then
[
DFSU24
, Equations (9.1)–(9.3)]
hold for all the above standard templates since
(m,n)\neq(1,1)
.

Following
[
DFSU24
, Section 9]
, we have that for each
k\geq 1

\begin{split}\Delta(\mathbf{f},[kt,(k+1)t])&=\Delta(\mathbf{s}[(kt,-2C_{\epsilon}),((k+1)t,-2C_{\epsilon})],[kt,(k+1)t])\\
&=\Delta\left(\mathbf{s}\left[\left(0,-\frac{2C_{\epsilon}}{t}\right),\left(1,-\frac{2C_{\epsilon}}{t}\right)\right],[0,1]\right)\\
&=\Delta(\mathbf{s}[(0,0),(1,0)],[0,1])-O\left(\frac{C_{\epsilon}}{t}\right)\\
&=mn-\frac{mn}{m+n}-O\left(\frac{C_{\epsilon}}{t}\right).\end{split}

By Theorem
2.3
,
\dim_{H}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{f},C_{\epsilon}))\geq mn-\frac{mn}{m+n}-\epsilon-O\left(\frac{C_{\epsilon}}{t}\right)
.
Take
t=t_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
large so that
\dim_{H}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{f},C_{\epsilon}))\geq mn-\frac{mn}{m+n}-2\epsilon
.

Fix any
A\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{f},C_{\epsilon})
and let
\mathbf{h}_{A}=(h_{1},\dots,h_{d})
. Observe that

h_{1}(t)=f_{1}(t)-(f_{1}(t)-h_{1}(t))\leq-2C_{\epsilon}+C_{\epsilon}=-C_{\epsilon}

for all
t\geq t_{\epsilon}
. Note that
\mathbf{f}
is a bounded template. Setting
D_{\epsilon}=\min_{t}(-f_{1}(t))
,

h_{1}(t)=f_{1}(t)-(f_{1}(t)-h_{1}(t))\geq-D_{\epsilon}-C_{\epsilon}

for all
t\geq t_{\epsilon}
.
Hence, it follows that

\begin{split}\dim_{H}\{A\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{R}):-D_{\epsilon}-C_{\epsilon}\leq\log\lambda_{1}(a_{t}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d})&\leq-C_{\epsilon},\forall t\geq t_{\epsilon}\}\\
&\geq mn-\frac{mn}{m+n}-2\epsilon.\end{split}

Since
-D_{\epsilon}-C_{\epsilon}
and
-C_{\epsilon}\to-\infty
as
\epsilon\to 0
, taking
\rho_{\epsilon}=e^{-D_{\epsilon/2}-C_{\epsilon/2}}
and
\eta_{\epsilon}=e^{-C_{\epsilon/2}}
, we can conclude Proposition
2.6
.
∎

3.
Construction of high entropy measures

In this section, we will construct an
a
-invariant probability measure on
X_{m+n}
with high entropy supported on a certain compact region near the cusp. We basically follow the strategy as in
[
LSS19
,
KKL
,
KLP23
]
to construct invariant measures and make use of Proposition
2.6
to bound the entropy.

Let
d=m+n
as in Section
2
. Denote
G=\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
,
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
, and
X=G/\Gamma
. Let
d_{G}
be a right invariant metric on
G
and let
d_{X}
be the metric on
X
induced by
d_{G}
.
Denote by
d_{\infty}
the metric on
G
induced by the supremum norm on
M_{d,d}(\mathbb{R})
.
Since
d_{G}
and
d_{\infty}
are locally bi-Lipschitz (see e.g.
[
EW11
, Lemma 9.12]
), there are constants
0<r_{0}<1
and
C_{0}\geq 1
such that if
d_{G}(g,id)<r_{0}
or
d_{\infty}(g,id)<r_{0}
, then

(3.1)

{\frac{1}{C_{0}}d_{\infty}(g,id)\leq d_{G}(g,id)\leq C_{0}d_{\infty}(g,id).}

We refer the reader to
[
ELW
, Chapters 1 and 2]
for definitions and properties of entropies. In particular, for a countable partition
\mathcal{P}
of
X
and a probability measure
\mu
on
X
, the entropy
H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P})
is defined by

H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P})=-\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mu(P)\log\mu(P)\in[0,\infty]

where
0\log 0
is defined to be
0
. The (dynamical) entropy
h_{\mu}(a)
of the transformation
a
on
X
is defined by

h_{\mu}(a)=\sup_{\mathcal{P}:H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P})<\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}H_{\mu}\left(\bigvee_{k=0}^{N-1}a^{-k}\mathcal{P}\right),

where
\bigvee_{k=0}^{N-1}a^{-k}\mathcal{P}
denotes the join of the preimages
a^{-k}\mathcal{P}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
(m,n)\neq(1,1)
. Fix
\epsilon>0
and let
\rho_{\epsilon},\eta_{\epsilon}
be as in Proposition
2.6
. Then there exists an
a
-invariant probability measure
\mu
on
X
such that

(1)

\operatorname{Supp}\mu\subset\{x\in X:\rho_{\epsilon}\leq\lambda_{1}(x)\leq\eta_{\epsilon}\}
;

(2)

h_{\mu}(a)\geq m+n-1-\frac{m+n}{mn}\epsilon
.

Remark 3.2
.

The set
\{x\in X:\rho_{\epsilon}\leq\lambda_{1}(x)\leq\eta_{\epsilon}\}
is a compact subset of
X
by Mahler’s compactness criterion.

Proof.

Given
\epsilon>0
, let
\rho_{\epsilon},\eta_{\epsilon}>0
and
t_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
be as in Proposition
2.6
. Denote

E(\epsilon)=\{A\in\mathbb{T}^{mn}:\eta_{\epsilon}\leq\lambda_{1}(a_{t}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d})\leq\rho_{\epsilon}\text{ for all }t\geq t_{\epsilon}\},

where
\mathbb{T}^{mn}=\mathbb{R}^{mn}/\mathbb{Z}^{mn}
with the metric
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{T}^{mn}
induced by the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{mn}=M_{m,n}(\mathbb{R})
.
For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
S_{N}
be a maximal
e^{-(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})N}
-separated subset of
E(\epsilon)
. By Proposition
2.6
, we have

(3.2)

{\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log|S_{N}|}{(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})N}\geq\dim_{H}E(\epsilon)\geq mn-\frac{mn}{m+n}-\epsilon,}

Define the measures
\nu_{N}
and
\mu_{N}
by

\nu_{N}=\frac{1}{|S_{N}|}\sum_{A\in S_{N}}\delta_{u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}a^{k}_{\ast}\nu_{N}.

Extracting a subsequences if necessary, we may assume that the sequence
(\mu_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weak
∗
to some measure
\mu
on
X
. Note that
\mu
could not be a probability measure since
X
is non-compact.

First, observe that
\mu
is clearly
a
-invariant since
a_{\ast}\mu_{N}-\mu_{N}
goes to the zero measure. Next, we claim that
\mu
is a probability measure on
X
and

\operatorname{Supp}\mu\subset\{x\in X:\rho_{\epsilon}\leq\lambda_{1}(x)\leq\eta_{\epsilon}\}.

Indeed, writing
X(\epsilon)=\{x\in X:\rho_{\epsilon}\leq\lambda_{1}(x)\leq\eta_{\epsilon}\}
,
it follows that

\mu_{N}(X\smallsetminus X(\epsilon))=\frac{1}{N|S_{N}|}\sum_{A\in S_{N}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\delta_{a^{k}u_{A}\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(X\smallsetminus X(\epsilon))\leq\frac{t_{\epsilon}}{N}.

By taking
N\to\infty
, we have
\mu(X\smallsetminus X(\epsilon))=0
and the claim follows since
X(\epsilon)
is compact in
X
.

Finally, we claim that

{h_{\mu}(a)\geq m+n-1-\frac{m+n}{mn}\epsilon.}

In order to prove the claim, we fix small
r>0
which will be determined in Lemma
3.3
and consider a finite partition
\mathcal{P}
of
X
satisfying

•

\mathcal{P}
contains an atom
P_{\infty}
such that
X\smallsetminus P_{\infty}
contains
X(\epsilon)
and has compact closure;

•

For any
P\in\mathcal{P}\smallsetminus\{P_{\infty}\}
,
\diam P<r
;

•

For any
P\in\mathcal{P}
, the boundary of
P
is
\mu
-null, i.e.
\mu(\partial P)=0
.

We can build such
\mathcal{P}
following the procedure in the proof of
[
LSS19
, Proposition 2.3]
.
For any
q\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{P}^{(q)}=\bigvee_{k=0}^{q-1}a^{-k}\mathcal{P}
. Write the Euclidean division of large enough
N-1
by
q
as

N-1=qN^{\prime}+s\text{ with }s\in\{0,\dots,q-1\}.

By subadditivity of entropy of the partition it follows that for each
p\in\{0,\dots,q-1\}

H_{\nu_{N}}(\mathcal{P}^{(N)})\leq H_{a^{p}\nu_{N}}(\mathcal{P}^{(q)})+H_{a^{p+q}\nu_{N}}(\mathcal{P}^{(q)})+\cdots+H_{a^{p+qN^{\prime}}\nu_{N}}(\mathcal{P}^{(q)})+2q\log|\mathcal{P}|.

Summing those inequalities for
p=0,\dots,q-1
, and using concavity of entropy of the measure, it follows that

qH_{\nu_{N}}(\mathcal{P}^{(N)})\leq\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}H_{a^{k}\nu_{N}}(\mathcal{P}^{(q)})+2q^{2}\log|\mathcal{P}|\leq NH_{\mu_{N}}(\mathcal{P}^{(q)})+2q^{2}\log|\mathcal{P}|.

Therefore, we have

(3.3)

{\frac{1}{q}H_{\mu_{N}}(\mathcal{P}^{(q)})\geq\frac{1}{N}H_{\nu_{N}}(\mathcal{P}^{(N)})-\frac{2q\log|\mathcal{P}|}{N}.}

Now we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

There is small enough
r>0
such that for any non-empty atom
P
of
\mathcal{P}^{(N)}
, there is at most one element
A\in S_{N}
such that
u_{A}\Gamma\in P
. In particular,
r
is independent of
N
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Recall that the constants
0<r_{0}<1
and
C_{0}\geq 1
are given in the beginning of Section
3
.
Take
r>0
small enough so that
r<\min\{r_{0},r_{0}C_{0}^{-1}e^{-(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})t_{\epsilon}}\}
and
C_{0}^{2}re^{(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})t_{\epsilon}}
is less than the injectivity radius of
X\smallsetminus P_{\infty}
.

Suppose that there are two distinct elements
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
in
S_{N}
such that
u_{A_{1}}\Gamma,u_{A_{2}}\Gamma\in P
, that is,
a^{k}u_{A_{1}}\Gamma,a^{k}u_{A_{2}}\Gamma
are contained in the same atom of
\mathcal{P}
for each
k=0,\dots,N-1
. Since
A_{1},A_{2}\in E(\epsilon)
,
a^{k}u_{A_{1}}\Gamma,a^{k}u_{A_{2}}\Gamma\notin P_{\infty}
for all
k\geq t_{\epsilon}
, hence

d_{X}(a^{k}u_{A_{1}}\Gamma,a^{k}u_{A_{2}}\Gamma)<r\quad\text{ for all }t_{\epsilon}\leq k\leq N-1.

We start with the case
k=0
. Since
u_{A_{1}}\Gamma,u_{A_{2}}\Gamma\notin P_{\infty}
, it follows that
d_{X}(u_{A_{1}}\Gamma,u_{A_{2}}\Gamma)<r
. Since the map
A\to u_{A}\Gamma
is injective on
\mathbb{T}^{mn}
, it follows from the right invariance of
d_{G}
that

d_{G}(u_{A_{1}-A_{2}},id)=d_{G}(u_{A_{1}},u_{A_{2}})=d_{X}(u_{A_{1}}\Gamma,u_{A_{2}}\Gamma)<r.

Since
r<r_{0}
, by (
3.1
), we have

\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|=d_{\infty}(u_{A_{1}-A_{2}},id)\leq C_{0}d_{G}(u_{A_{1}-A_{2}},id)<C_{0}r.

Now consider the case
k=t_{\epsilon}
. Observe that

d_{\infty}(a^{t_{\epsilon}}u_{A_{1}-A_{2}}a^{-t_{\epsilon}},id)=e^{(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})t_{\epsilon}}\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|<e^{(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})t_{\epsilon}}C_{0}r<r_{0}.

Hence using the right invariance of
d_{G}
and (
3.1
), we have

\begin{split}d_{G}(a^{t_{\epsilon}}u_{A_{1}},a^{t_{\epsilon}}u_{A_{2}})=d_{G}(a^{t_{\epsilon}}u_{A_{1}-A_{2}}a^{-t_{\epsilon}},id)&\leq C_{0}e^{(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})t_{\epsilon}}\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|\\
&<C_{0}^{2}re^{(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})t_{\epsilon}}.\end{split}

Since the last line is less than the injectivity radius of
X\smallsetminus P_{\infty}
, we have

e^{(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})t_{\epsilon}}\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|\leq C_{0}d_{G}(a^{t_{\epsilon}}u_{A_{1}},a^{t_{\epsilon}}u_{A_{2}})=C_{0}d_{X}(a^{t_{\epsilon}}u_{A_{1}}\Gamma,a^{t_{\epsilon}}u_{A_{2}}\Gamma)<C_{0}r,

hence
\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|<C_{0}re^{-(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})t_{\epsilon}}
.

In the case
k=t_{\epsilon}+1
, since

d_{\infty}(a^{t_{\epsilon}+1}u_{A_{1}-A_{2}}a^{-(t_{\epsilon}+1)},id)=e^{(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})(t_{\epsilon}+1)}\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|<e^{(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})}C_{0}r<r_{0},

it follows from the right invariance of
d_{G}
and (
3.1
) that

d_{G}(a^{t_{\epsilon}+1}u_{A_{1}},a^{t_{\epsilon}+1}u_{A_{2}})\leq C_{0}e^{(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})(t_{\epsilon}+1)}\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|<C_{0}^{2}re^{(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})},

which is less than the injectivity radius of
X\smallsetminus P_{\infty}
, hence

e^{(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})(t_{\epsilon}+1)}\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|\leq C_{0}d_{X}(a^{t_{\epsilon}+1}u_{A_{1}}\Gamma,a^{t_{\epsilon}+1}u_{A_{2}}\Gamma)<C_{0}r,

that is,
\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|<C_{0}re^{-(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})(t_{\epsilon}+1)}
.

Following this procedure until
k=N-1
, we have

\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|<C_{0}re^{-(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})(N-1)}<e^{-(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})N}.

This is a contradiction to the fact that
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are
e^{-(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n})N}
-separated.
∎

It follows from (
3.3
) and Lemma
3.3
that

\frac{1}{q}H_{\mu_{N}}(\mathcal{P}^{(q)})\geq\frac{\log|S_{N}|}{N}-\frac{2q\log|\mathcal{P}|}{N}.

Since the boundary of the atoms of
\mathcal{P}
, hence of
\mathcal{P}^{(q)}
, is of zero
\mu
-measure, taking
N\to\infty
and using (
3.2
), we have

\frac{1}{q}H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P}^{(q)})\geq m+n-1-\frac{m+n}{mn}\epsilon.

Therefore, we have

h_{\mu}(a)\geq m+n-1-\frac{m+n}{mn}\epsilon.

∎

4.
Proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

When
(m,n)=(1,1)
, it follows from
[
Kad12
, Theorem 1.2]
. Assume
(m,n)\neq(1,1)
. Using Proposition
3.1
with
\epsilon=1/k
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain a sequence of
a
-invariant probability measures
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
on
X
such that
\operatorname{Supp}\mu_{k}\subset\{x\in X:\rho_{1/k}\leq\lambda_{1}(x)\leq\eta_{1/k}\}
and
h_{\mu_{k}}(a)\geq m+n-1-\frac{m+n}{mnk}
. Hence, it follows that
\liminf_{k\to\infty}h_{\mu_{k}}(a)\geq m+n-1
and any weak
∗
limit measure is the zero measure since
\rho_{1/k},\eta_{1/k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.

On the other hand, if
\limsup_{k\to\infty}h_{\mu_{k}}(a)>m+n-1
, it follows from Theorem
1.1
that there is a subsequence of
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
such that any weak
∗
limit measure along this subsequence cannot be the zero measure, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\lim_{k\to\infty}h_{\mu_{k}}(a)=m+n-1
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Let
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be as in Theorem
1.2
. Denote by
m_{X}
the Haar probability measure on
X
.
Note that
h_{m_{X}}(a)=m+n
(see e.g.
[
EL10
]
). Given
h\in[m+n-1,m+n]
,
define

\nu_{k}=(h-(m+n-1))m_{X}+(m+n-h)\mu_{k}.

It follows from
[
ELW
, Theorem 2.33]
that

h_{\nu_{k}}(a)=(h-(m+n-1))h_{m_{X}}(a)+(m+n-h)h_{\mu_{k}}(a),

hence
\lim_{k\to\infty}h_{\nu_{k}}(a)=h
. Since the weak
∗
limit measure of
(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is
(h-(m+n-1))m_{X}
, this completes the proof.
∎

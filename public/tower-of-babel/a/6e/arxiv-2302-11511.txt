Recombination of B c B_{c} Mesons in Ultra-Relativistic Heavy-Ion Collisions
Biaogang Wu, Zhanduo Tang, Min He, Ralf Rapp

Abstract
High-energy heavy-ion collisions have been suggested as a favorable environment for the production of B c B_{c} mesons, due to a much larger abundance of charm and bottom quarks compared to elementary reactions. Motivated by recent CMS data for B c + B_{c}^{+} production in Pb-Pb(5.02 TeV) collisions at the LHC, we deploy a previously developed transport approach for charmonia and bottomonia to evaluate the kinetics of B c B_{c} mesons throughout the fireball formed in these reactions. The main inputs to our approach are two transport parameters: the B c B_{c} ’s reaction rate and equilibrium limit. Both quantities are determined by previous calculations via a combination of charm and bottom sectors. In-medium binding energies of B c B_{c} mesons are calculated from a thermodynamic T T -matrix with a lattice-QCD constrained potential, and figure in their inelastic reaction rates. Temperature-dependent equilibrium limits include charm- and bottom-quark fugacities based on their initial production. We compute the centrality dependence of inclusive B c B_{c} production and transverse-momentum ( p T p_{T} ) spectra using two different recombination models, instantaneous coalescence and resonance recombination. The main uncertainty in the resulting nuclear modification factors, R AA R_{\rm AA} , is currently associated with the B c B_{c} cross section in elementary p ​ p pp collisions, caused by the uncertainty in the branching ratio for the B c − → J / ψ ​ μ − ​ ν ¯ B_{c}^{-}\to J/\psi\mu^{-}\bar{\nu} decay. Our results indicate a large enhancement of the R AA R_{\rm AA} at low p T p_{T} , with significant regeneration contributions up to p T ≃ p_{T}\simeq 20 GeV. Comparisons to CMS data are carried out but firm conclusions will require a more accurate value of the branching ratio, or alternative channels to measure the B c B_{c} production in p ​ p pp collisions.



I
Introduction

Measurements of charmonia in ultra-relativistic heavy-ion collisions (URHICs) at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) have demonstrated the importance of charm-quark recombination processes
in the strongly interacting fireball formed in these reactions
Andronic et al. 2016

;

;

;

c
) and anticharm (

) quarks in the fireball. In the bottomonium sector, this
evidence is less pronounced, although transport calculations predict a non-negligible component of regeneration in an overall suppressed
R_{\rm AA}
of
\Upsilon
mesons in Pb-Pb collisions
Du et al. 2017
;
Yao and Müller 2018
.
The ratio of bottomonium over total bottom production in

collisions of typically a few permille is much smaller than the
\approx

It is therefore of great interest to study bound states of bottom (

) and

quarks,

mesons.
For the ground state,

This suggests that

In a broader context,

Originally discovered in

Pioneering data by the CMS collaboration

Earlier theoretical studies of

More recently, an instantaneous coalescence model (ICM)

calculate the yield of

’s

In the present paper, we employ a kinetic rate equation
Grandchamp et al. 2004
;
Zhao and Rapp 2011
;
Du et al. 2017
;
Wu et al. 2021
to compute the time evolution of
B_{c}

The in-medium binding energies are determined from thermodynamic

With the resulting reaction rates and equilibrium limits, we calculate the centrality dependence of inclusive

In the context of the CMS data, good control over the

Since the aforementioned

II
B_{c}
Spectral Functions in the QGP

When utilizing quarkonia as a probe of the QGP, their in-medium spectral properties play a key role in determining transport parameters that are required to compute observables suitable for comparison with the experiment. While this program has been widely carried out for charmonia and bottomonia, we are not aware of microscopic calculations of in-medium spectral functions of
B_{c}
mesons to date. Toward this end, we employ a thermodynamic
T
-matrix approach along the lines of previous investigations
Mannarelli and Rapp 2005
;
Cabrera and Rapp 2007
;
Riek and Rapp 2010
;
Liu and Rapp 2018
.
It is based on a temperature-dependent two-body potential and solved
self-consistently for the resummed Dyson-Schwinger equations of the in-medium one- and two-parton correlation functions in the QGP, schematically written as

\displaystyle T_{Q\bar{Q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{Q\bar{Q}}+\int dkV_{Q\bar{Q}}D_{Q}(k)D_{\bar{Q}}(p-k)T_{Q\bar{Q}}

(1)

\displaystyle D_{Q}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/[k_{0}-\omega_{1,k}-\Sigma_{1}(k)]

(2)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{Q}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dpT_{Qi}D_{i}(p)f_{i}\ ,

(3)

where
T_{Q\bar{Q}}
denotes the quarkonium
T
-matrix,
D_{Q,i}
single-parton propagators for either heavy quarks (
Q
) or thermal partons (
i=q,\bar{q},g
), and
f_{i}
the pertinent thermal-parton distribution function.
The input potential is taken of Cornell type with in-medium screened color-Coulomb and string interactions, which in the color-singlet amounts to the ansatz

V(r;T)=-\frac{4}{3}\alpha_{s}[\frac{e^{-m_{d}r}}{r}+m_{d}-\frac{\sigma}{m_{s}}[e^{-m_{s}r-\left(c_{b}m_{s}r\right)^{2}}-1]\ .

(4)

A Fourier transform into momentum space is carried out, followed by a partial-wave expansion of the
T
-matrix equation. We adopt the parameters of Ref.
Liu and Rapp 2018
, where the coupling constant and string tension are fixed at
\alpha_{s}=0.27
and
\sigma=0.225

\textup{GeV}^{2}

reproduce lQCD data for the HQ free energy in vacuum.
The finite-temperature screening masses,

m_{s}=\left(c_{s}m_{d}^{2}\sigma/\alpha_{s}\right)^{1/4}
, where
m_{d}(T)

are parameters of the in-medium potential, while

controls the string-breaking distance. Together with the bare masses for the light thermal partons, they are used to fit the selfconsistent
T
-matrix results to finite-temperature lQCD data for the HQ free energy, euclidean quarkonium correlator
ratios, and the equation of state of the QGP. Here, we focus on a solution referred to as a strongly coupled scenario (SCS)
Liu and Rapp 2018
.
Compared to the solution of a weakly coupled scenario (WCS), the SCS is preferred by yielding transport parameters
Liu and Rapp 2016
that are close to the
ones extracted from phenomenological studies based on hydrodynamics and HQ transport models
He et al. 2022a
.

III
Kinetic Approach

The kinetic rate equation for the number,
N_{B_{c}}
, of a specific
B_{c}
state is given by
Grandchamp et al. 2004
;
Zhao and Rapp 2010

\frac{dN_{B_{c}}(\tau)}{d\tau}=-\Gamma_{B_{c}}(T(\tau))\left[N_{B_{c}}(\tau)-N_{B_{c}}^{\rm eq}(T(\tau))\right]\ .

(7)

In the present work, we will consider
B_{c}\left(1S\right)
and
B_{c}\left(1P\right)
states.
The rate equation requires two transport parameters, the equilibrium limit,
N_{B_{c}}^{\rm eq}
,
and the reaction rate,
\Gamma_{B_{c}}
.
The equilibrium limit is calculated from the statistical model, taking the form

N_{B_{c}}^{\rm eq}(T)=V_{\rm FB}d_{B_{c}}\gamma_{c}\gamma_{\bar{b}}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\exp(-E_{k}/T)\ ,

(8)

where
E_{k}=\sqrt{k^{2}+m_{B_{c}}^{2}}
, and
V_{\rm FB}
is the time-dependent volume of the expanding fireball.
We neglect the spin-induced
1/m_{Q}
corrections in this work. Therefore, using standard spectroscopic notation,
{}^{2S+1}{L}_{J}
, the 4
S
-wave states

{}^{1}{S}_{0}
and
{}^{3}{S}_{1}
are degenerate, and so are the 12
P
-wave states
{}^{3}{P}_{0}
,
{}^{1}{P}_{1}
,
{}^{3}{P}_{1}
and
{}^{3}{P}_{2}

(the individual degeneracy of each state is given by a factor of
2J+1
).
The equilibrium limits critically depend on the fugacity factors
\gamma_{c}
and
\gamma_{\bar{b}}
, which have been computed in our earlier works

;
Du et al. 2017
assuming conservation of

and

N_{Q\bar{Q}}=\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{Q}n_{\rm{op}}V_{\rm{FB}}\frac{I_{1}(\gamma_{Q}n_{\rm{op}}V_{\rm{FB}})}{I_{0}(\gamma_{Q}n_{\rm{op}}V_{\rm{FB}})}+\gamma_{Q}^{2}n_{\rm{hid}}V_{\rm{FB}}\ ,

(9)

where

) and hidden (

VI
Conclusions

We have investigated the production of
B_{c}
mesons in heavy-ion collisions using a thermal-rate equation approach. We have first calculated
B_{c}
spectral functions in the QGP from a thermodynamic
T
-matrix and used them to extract the
pertinent binding energies. The latter have been implemented into the evaluation of dissociation rates from inelastic scattering of thermal partons off the
b
and
c
quarks in the
B_{c}
mesons. We have also constructed the
B_{c}
equilibrium limits through a combination of
b
- and
c
-quark fugacities and included effects of incomplete HQ thermalization. We have solved the rate equations for
1S
and
1P
states in Pb-Pb(5.02 TeV) collisions and computed the centrality dependence of inclusive
B_{c}^{\pm}
yields. With
\approx
100% feeddown from excited states below the open HF threshold, the latter make up
\approx
50% of the inclusive yield of the pseudoscalar
B_{c}\left(1S\right)
meson. Large regeneration contributions cause a markedly rising
R_{\rm AA}
with centrality, reaching values of up to
\approx
4-6 in central collisions. We have then calculated
p_{T}
spectra of the
B_{c}
using two
different recombination models (ICM and RRM with and without SMCs). The spectra of
c
and
b
quarks used in this calculation were generated from relativistic Langevin transport simulation, and the pertinent
p_{T}
spectra from regeneration have been normalized to the yields from the rate equation. The inclusive
p_{T}
-dependent
R_{\rm AA}
for the
B_{c}
is dominated by regeneration contributions for
p_{T}\lesssim
10-15 GeV in semi-/central collisions, reaching values up to around 10 at low
p_{T}
. The primordial yield dominates in peripheral (central) collisions for
p_{T}\gtrsim 10(20)
GeV. The results for the
R_{\rm AA}
are rather sensitive to the
B_{c}
production cross section in

pp
collisions. A more precise measurement of this quantity, and of the production systematics of
B_{c}
’s in heavy-ion collisions (with a potentially spectacular enhancement at low
p_{T}
), will provide unprecedented insights into their in-medium properties and a valuable complement to, and interface of, the charmonium and bottomonium sectors.

Three-body scattering and quantization conditions from S S matrix unitarity Preprint: JLAB-THY-22-3664
Andrew W. Jackura

Abstract
Two methodologies have been presented in the literature which connect relativistic three-particle scattering amplitudes with lattice QCD spectra – the “relativistic effective field theory” approach and the “finite-volume unitarity” method. While both methods have been shown to be equivalent in various works, it has not been shown how to arrive at the relativistic effective field theory results directly from S S matrix unitarity. In this work, we provide a simple proof of the relativistic effective field theory form of the scattering equations directly from unitarity. Motivated by the finite-volume unitarity approach, we then postulate a set of quantization conditions which relate the finite-volume energy spectra to the K K matrices which drive the short-distance physics in the scattering equations, obtaining all previously known results for three identical particles. This work also presents new relations which provide a new pathway to generalize the results to arbitrary systems.

Following this procedure, we generate new relations for the scattering integral equations and quantization conditions, which may prove useful for numerical applications

III.1
Unitarity relations

Following the procedure discussed in Refs.
Mai et al. 2017
;
Jackura et al. 2019a

1

11

\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}
scattering. Therefore, the unitarity relation for the amplitude
\mathcal{T}^{(u,u)}
is given by

\displaystyle\im\mathcal{T}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{k^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{T}^{(u,u)\,{\dagger}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\rho(\sigma_{k^{\prime}})\,\,\mathcal{T}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle+\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{T}^{(u,u)\,{\dagger}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\Delta(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{T}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,,

(13)

which is an infinite dimensional matrix equation in angular momentum space where the
\ell
and
m_{\ell}
indices are suppressed. For convenience, we present the derivation of Eq. (
III.1
) is given in Appendix
A
. Two kinematic functions are introduced in Eq. (
III.1
), the first being the usual two-body phase space
\rho
, given by

\displaystyle\rho_{\ell^{\prime}m_{\ell^{\prime}},\ell m_{\ell}}(\sigma_{k})

\displaystyle=\delta_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\delta_{m_{\ell^{\prime}}m_{\ell}}\,\frac{\xi q_{k}^{\star}}{8\pi\sqrt{\sigma_{k}}}\,\Theta(q_{k}^{\star})\,,

(14)

where
\xi=1/2!
is the symmetry factor for two identical particles. The Heaviside function
\Theta(q_{k}^{\star})
enforces that the intermediate state exists when the pair CM energy allows for on-shell pair production, which induces a square root branch cut in the
\sigma_{k}
-plane when
\sqrt{\sigma_{k}}\geq 2m

III.1
) the
direct
term since the spectator remains unchanged in the intermediate state. The second kinematic function is the single particle exchange pole,
\Delta

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ell^{\prime}m_{\ell^{\prime}},\ell m_{\ell}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})=\pi\,

\displaystyle\delta\left((P-k-p)^{2}-m^{2}\right)\,

\displaystyle\times 4\pi Y_{\ell^{\prime}m_{\ell^{\prime}}}^{*}(\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{k}}}_{p}^{\star})Y_{\ell m_{\ell}}(\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{p}}}_{k}^{\star})\Big\rvert_{(P-k-p)^{2}=m^{2}}\,\Theta\left(q_{p}^{\star}\right)\Theta\left(q_{k}^{\star}\right)\,,

(15)

which characterizes the singularity associated with exchanging an on-shell particle between pairs.

13

The phases of the spherical harmonics cancel each other in the unitarity condition as it sums over all possible intermediate states. p

III.2
On-shell scattering equations

Nonlinear constraints such as Eq. (
III.1
) are solved by linear equations that are driven by a kernel, known as a
K
matrix, which is a real function within the restricted kinematic region which we consider and represents the short-distance dynamics of the scattering process which are not fixed by the unitarity condition. As an example, we first review the well-known on-shell representation for the
\mathbf{2}\to\mathbf{2}
amplitude that satisfies Eq. (
16
). We make an
ansatz
for the on-shell amplitude in the linear form

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k})=\mathcal{K}_{2}(\sigma_{k})-\mathcal{K}_{2}(\sigma_{k})\,\mathcal{I}(\sigma_{k})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k})\,,

(19)

where
\mathcal{K}_{2}
is the
\mathbf{2}\to\mathbf{2}
partial wave
K
matrix, which is an unknown real function in the elastic kinematic domain, and
\mathcal{I}
is a kinematic function which must fulfill the constraint

\displaystyle\im\mathcal{I}(\sigma_{k})=-\rho(\sigma_{k})

in order to satisfy Eq. (
16
). It is straightforward to show that Eq. (
19
) satisfies unitarity (
16
). The
K
matrix inherits the angular momentum structure of
\mathcal{M}_{2}
, reducing Eq. (
19
) to a single algebraic relation. Subject to these restrictions, one can easily verify that Eq. (
19
) satisfies Eq. (
16
), and therefore a solution for a given particular
\mathcal{K}_{2}
constitutes an on-shell representation for the
\mathbf{2}\to\mathbf{2}
scattering amplitude valid for the kinematic domain
(2m)^{2}\leq\sigma_{k}\leq(\sqrt{s}-m)^{2}
.

On-shell representations such as Eq. (
19

scheme dependence
since the choice of
\mathcal{I}
, and hence the
K
matrix, is not unique. For any
\mathcal{I}
satisfying the requisite
\im\mathcal{I}=-\rho
, the function
\mathcal{K}_{2}
must compensate the difference in order to ensure
\mathcal{M}_{2}
is the physical scattering amplitude. Choosing an alternative
\mathcal{I}^{\,\prime}
results in an associated
\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\prime}
, which can be related to
\mathcal{K}
through the invariance of
\mathcal{M}_{2}
as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\,\prime}}^{-1}={\mathcal{K}_{2}}^{-1}+\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{I}^{\,\prime}\,.

Note that the relation between
K
matrices preserves that condition that
\mathcal{K}_{2}
is real in the restricted kinematic since we require
\im\mathcal{I}=\im\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
. We will use two different choices of
\mathcal{I}
in this work. The first one is trivially related to the simple phase space factor Eq. (
14
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\ell^{\prime}m_{\ell^{\prime}},\ell m_{\ell}}(\sigma_{k})=-i\delta_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\delta_{m_{\ell^{\prime}}m_{\ell}}\,\frac{\xi\,q_{k}^{\star}}{8\pi\sqrt{\sigma_{k}}}\,,

(20)

With an associated
K
matrix
\mathcal{K}_{2}
. Note that this term does not depend on any explicit parameter that indicates a scheme dependence, however it is still a choice that we can make which fixes the on-shell form for
\mathcal{K}_{2}
. The second choice is given by an integral form

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\ell^{\prime}m_{\ell^{\prime}},\ell m_{\ell}}^{\,\prime}(\sigma_{k})

\displaystyle=\int^{\Lambda}\!\frac{\textrm{d}\sigma^{\prime}}{\pi}\,\frac{\rho_{\ell^{\prime}m_{\ell^{\prime}},\ell m_{\ell}}(\sigma^{\prime})}{\sigma_{k}-\sigma^{\prime}+i\epsilon}\left(\,\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^{\prime}-4m^{2}}{\sigma_{k}-4m^{2}}}\,\right)^{\ell^{\prime}+\ell}\,,

(21)

with the associated
K

\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\prime}
. This is the usual Chew-Mandelstam dispersive form
Chew and Mandelstam 1960
including barrier factors to ensure the kinematic singularity induced by the partial wave projection is removed. It is understood that
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, and the dispersive integral runs from
4m^{2}\leq\sigma^{\prime}<\infty
, with
\Lambda
indicating the need for regularization of the divergent behavior.
14
14

14

In phenomenological analyses, it is usual to perform subtractions to tame the UV divergence.
We do not make any specific choice for UV regulation here as any choice which respects the kinematic requirements is valid for our arguments. This shows an explicit scheme dependence in
\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\,\prime}
, as it must depend on the regularization
\Lambda
in order to ensure that
\mathcal{M}_{2}
is regularization independent. By inspection, both Eqs. (
20
) and (
21
) have
\im\mathcal{I}=\im\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=-\rho
above the two-body threshold, and the relation between the two
K

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\,\prime}}^{-1}={\mathcal{K}_{2}}^{-1}-\re\mathcal{I}^{\,\prime}\,.

The advantage of Eq. (
21

20

\sigma_{k}=0

\mathcal{I}

Briceno et al. 2018b

IV
Quantization conditions

We now construct the quantization conditions associated with the on-shell scattering equations. The scattering equations provide the basis for the structure of the finite-volume counterparts to the scattering amplitudes. The system is put into a cubic volume with a side length
L
and subject to periodic boundary conditions. Consequently, rotational invariance is broken and all momentum are quantized according to

\displaystyle\mathbf{k}=\frac{2\pi}{L}\,\mathbf{n}\,,

where
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbbm{Z}^{3}
. Therefore, all momentum integrals are replaced by sums by the rule,

\displaystyle\int\!\frac{\textrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\longrightarrow\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in(2\pi/L)\mathbbm{Z}^{3}}\,.

Likewise, the Dirac delta functions will be replaced by the Kronecker equivalent,

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{k})\longrightarrow L^{3}\delta_{pk}\,.

Since the system is confined in a box, the only singularities of the finite-volume amplitudes are poles on the real energy axis, known as Lüscher poles, which correspond to the spectrum of the system. The quantization condition allows us to connect the finite-volume spectrum of three particles in a finite spatial volume to the infinite volume scattering quantities governed by the on-shell relations.

Since Lorentz invariance is broken, the resulting finite-volume amplitude will depend not only on the invariant mass of the two particles, but also the direction of the total momentum of the system. Total angular momentum is no longer conserved in a finite-volume, and therefore is it not a suitable quantum number to label for the two particle state, requiring the need to subduce the system to an irreducible representation of the octahedral group or one of its little groups for moving frames. We will not discuss the subduction to the cubic irreps, and instead refer to Refs.
Thomas et al. 2012
;
Moore and Fleming 2006
for the discussion.

The finite-volume scaling for short-distance objects will be such that they are equivalent to the infinite-volume quantity up to some correction which is exponenitally suppressed in the volume. For example, the finite-volume hadron mass
M_{L}
,
M_{L}=M+\mathcal{O}(e^{-mL})
where
m
is the lightest mass scale of the system, e.g. the pion in QCD
Luscher 1986a
. Similar arguments hold for all connected
K
matrices. Therefore, we can ignore the volume correction for large enough box sizes and simply replace all short-distance finite-volume objects with their infinite-volume counterparts. This argument holds because any such object can be represented by an analytic function in the kinematic region of study since all the relevant on-shell physics has been isolated
Kim et al. 2005
. Since the functions are analytic, any integral representation will not contain singular behavior in the integrand
f
, and we can freely replace discrete sums with continuous integrals up to the exponentially suppressed behavior according to the Poisson summation formula,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\,f(\mathbf{k})=\int\frac{\textrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,f(\mathbf{k})+\mathcal{O}(e^{-mL})\,.

IV.1
Two particle quantization condition

First we establish the quantization condition for the two-particle subsystem. Let us fix the spectator momentum
\mathbf{k}
, and work with the
\mathbf{2}\to\mathbf{2}

\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k})

\mathcal{M}_{2,L}

P_{k}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2,L}(P_{k})=\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\,\prime}(\sigma_{k})-\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\,\prime}(\sigma_{k})\,\mathcal{I}_{L}^{\,\prime}(P_{k})\,\mathcal{M}_{2,L}(P_{k})\,,

(23)

where we have used the relation
\mathcal{K}_{2,L}^{\,\prime}(P_{k})=\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\,\prime}(\sigma_{k})

(24)

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi}{2}\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{\mathbf{a}}^{\Lambda}\left(\frac{a^{\star}}{q_{k}^{\star}}\right)^{\ell^{\prime}}\frac{4\pi\,Y_{\ell^{\prime}m_{\ell^{\prime}}}^{*}(\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{a}}}^{\star})Y_{\ell m_{\ell}}(\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{a}}}^{\star})}{2\omega_{a}(q_{k}^{\star\,2}-a^{\star\,2})}\left(\frac{a^{\star}}{q_{k}^{\star}}\right)^{\ell}\,,

IV.2
Three-particle quantization condition

We now construct the three-particle quantization condition. The most direct way is to use the on-shell equation for the
T
matrix element, Eq. (
2
). In transitioning to the finite-volume, the finite-volume amplitudes in general will become dense matrices in both angular momentum (
\ell,m_{\ell}
) and the spectator momentum spaces (
k

(k\ell m_{\ell})
basis. The spectator delta function
\delta(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle\delta(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\to 2\omega_{k}L^{3}\,\delta_{pk}=[\,2\omega L^{3}\,]_{pk}\,.

The
[2\omega L^{3}]

(\ell,m_{\ell})
space. Integration measures over the spectator momentum then transition as

\displaystyle\int_{k}=\int\!\frac{\textrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}\,2\omega_{k}}\longrightarrow\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\,\frac{1}{2\omega_{k}L^{3}}\,.

We denote the finite-volume
T

\mathcal{T}_{L}^{(u,u)}(P)
, which is a matrix in
(k\ell m_{\ell})
space, and a function of the total energy-momentum
P=(E,\mathbf{P})
. From Eq. (
2

\mathcal{T}_{L}^{(u,u)}(P)
satisfies the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{L}^{(u,u)}(P)=\mathcal{K}_{3,L}^{(u,u)}(P)-\mathcal{K}_{3,L}^{(u,u)}(P)\cdot\frac{1}{2\omega L^{3}}\cdot\Gamma_{L}(P)\cdot\frac{1}{2\omega L^{3}}\cdot\mathcal{T}_{L}^{(u,u)}(P)

(29)

where we have introduced the
\cdot

k\ell m_{\ell}
) space. We also use the notation that any object with an
L

L
dependence through the quantized momentum,
\mathbf{k}=2\pi\,\mathbf{n}/L

K

L

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{3,L}^{(u,u)}(P)=2\omega L^{3}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{2}+\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(P)\,,

where
\mathcal{K}_{2}
is the infinite-volume two-body
K
matrix interpreted now as a diagonal matrix in (

) space where the
k

\sigma_{k}=(P-k)^{2}
, that is

\displaystyle[\,\mathcal{K}_{2}\,]_{p\ell^{\prime}m_{\ell^{\prime}},k\ell m_{\ell}}=\delta_{pk}\delta_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\delta_{m_{\ell^{\prime}}m_{\ell}}\,\mathcal{K}_{2,\ell}(\sigma_{k})\,,

and the infinite-volume connected three-body
K

\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(P)
is a dense matrix in (
k\ell m_{\ell}
) space where the elements in momentum space are determined by
\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})
with
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}=2\pi\,\mathbf{n}/L
. We keep
\mathcal{K}_{2}
an implicit function of
P
for convenience. Following Ref.
Blanton and Sharpe 2020a
we define a convenient matrix

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{2,L}\equiv 2\omega L^{3}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{2}

because the combination appears frequently.

The remaining finite-volume function
\Gamma_{L}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{L}(P)=2\omega L^{3}\cdot H\cdot F_{2,L}+G_{L}(P)\,,

(30)

which is found by replacing
\mathcal{I}
with
F_{2,L}
and taking
H
as a diagonal matrix in
k
space with elements
H(\sigma_{k})
. The second function is the finite-volume counterpart to
\mathcal{G}
, which is identical to the infinite-volume since
\mathcal{G}
contains only simple poles which carry over to the finite-volume, i.e.
G_{L}=\mathcal{G}
. The matrix elements of
G_{L}
in momentum space are given by
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})
where
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}
are evaluated for quantized momenta. Like
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{2,L}
, these function frequently appear with multiplicative factors of
[2\omega L^{3}]^{-1}
. We therefore conveniently define the functions

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Gamma}_{L}(P)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2\omega L^{3}}\cdot\Gamma_{L}(P)\cdot\frac{1}{2\omega L^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle=\widetilde{F}_{2,L}+\widetilde{G}_{L}(P)\,,

where we have defined
\widetilde{F}_{2,L}
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{2,L}\equiv\frac{1}{2\omega L^{3}}\cdot H\cdot F_{2,L}\,,

and
\widetilde{G}_{L}
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{L}(P)\equiv\frac{1}{2\omega L^{3}}\cdot G_{L}(P)\cdot\frac{1}{2\omega L^{3}}\,.

The Lüscher poles of
\mathcal{T}_{L}^{(u,u)}
occur whenever

\displaystyle\det\Big[\,1+\mathcal{K}_{3,L}^{(u,u)}(P)\cdot\widetilde{\Gamma}_{L}(P)\,\Big]_{E=E_{\mathfrak{n}}}=0\,,

(31)

or equivalently the result shown in Eq. (
7

\displaystyle\det\Big[\,1+\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{2,L}+\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(P)\right)\cdot\left(\widetilde{F}_{2,L}+\widetilde{G}_{L}(P)\right)\,\Big]_{E=E_{\mathfrak{n}}}=0\,.

The quantization condition is a determinant over
(k\ell m_{\ell})

Blanton and Sharpe 2020a
. If one can determine the spectrum

of three particles in a box, say from lattice QCD simulations, then Eq. (

\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}

K

\mathbf{2}\to\mathbf{2}

K

The
K
matrices can parameterized by some analytic function which are fitted to the spectra, and then in turn can be fed into the scattering equations such as Eq. (
2
) or Eq. (
II
) to determine the infinite-volume
\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}
scattering amplitude. Both the two and three particle quantization conditions involve infinite-dimensional matrices in angular momentum space, while the cutoff functions truncate the spectator momentum space for the three body conditions. the barrier suppression of partial wave amplitudes allow us to truncate the determinant by focusing on a low-energy region and neglecting waves which have negligible. In each case this leads to systematic effects in determining
\mathcal{K}_{2}
and
\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}

\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}

III.1
) and Eq. (
III.1
) are not diagonal in
(\ell m_{\ell})
space. This gives another set of systematic errors which are similar to the ones usually found in isobar truncations used in phenomenological analyses of three-body decays
Mikhasenko et al. 2020
;
Albaladejo et al. 2020
;
Albaladejo et al. 2021
. A possible course of action is to include enough waves to saturate the system in some low-energy domain with the relevant physical channels, and treat the breaking of unitarity as a systematic error in the determination of the
\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}
amplitudes.

This concludes the main results, where we have used
S
matrix unitarity to construct both the infinite-volume scattering equations in terms of on-shell quantities and some unknown short-distance object, and the finite-volume quantization conditions which relate these short-distance objects to the two and three particle spectra. In the following section, we present an alternative derivation which is closely related to the approach taken by Ref.
Mikhasenko et al. 2019
, and connect the symmetric
\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}

Hansen and Sharpe 2015

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})=-\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\,\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k})-\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\int_{k^{\prime}}\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{D}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,,

(33)

which is completely controlled by two-body physics and is represented in Fig.

.

\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}
amplitude

We can remove the ladder contribution from the integral equation for
\mathcal{M}_{3}^{(u,u)}
by defining the divergence free amplitude
\mathcal{M}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\equiv\mathcal{M}_{3}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})-\mathcal{D}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,.

(34)

Inserting this into Eq. (
V
) and using the ladder equation (
33
), we find an integral equation for
\mathcal{M}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\int_{k^{\prime}}\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{M}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle+\left[\,1-\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\,\mathcal{I}(\sigma_{p})\,\right]\int_{k^{\prime}}\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{R}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle-\left[\,1-\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\,\mathcal{I}(\sigma_{p})\,\right]\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\Gamma(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{M}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,,

where
\mathcal{R}^{(u,u)}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\delta(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})-\int_{k^{\prime}}\Gamma(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\Big[\,\delta(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k^{\prime}})+\mathcal{D}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,\Big]\,.

(35)

We interpret
\mathcal{R}^{(u,u)}
as an initial state rescattering function, which contains three distinct topological structures of three interacting particles in the initial state before they succumb to any short-distance three-body forces. The first term of Eq. (
35
) represents the case where the initial state does not interact, the second indicating two of the particles scatter either directly or with an initial exchange, and the third with pair-wise exchanges. Figure
8
shows the different on-shell topologies which dress the initial state.

We can define a similar rescattering function for the final state, denoted by
\mathcal{L}^{(u,u)}
, which we define as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\delta(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})-\int_{k^{\prime}}\Big[\,\delta(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k^{\prime}})+\mathcal{D}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\Big]\,\Gamma(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,,

(36)

and has a similar interpretation as the initial state rescattering function. The functions
\mathcal{R}^{(u,u)}
and
\mathcal{L}^{(u,u)}
are closely related to those presented in Ref.
Hansen and Sharpe 2015
, which we will show in the following section.

The introduction of the initial and final state rescattering functions motivates us to define an amputated divergence-free amplitude
\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}
, which is free of rescatterings in both the initial and final state,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\equiv\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{L}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{R}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,,

(37)

Substituting this relation into the above integral equation, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\left[\,1+\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\,\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\right]\,\mathcal{L}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\left[\,1-\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\,\mathcal{I}(\sigma_{p})\,\right]\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle-\left[\,1-\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\,\mathcal{I}(\sigma_{p})\,\right]\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{q^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\Gamma(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{L}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{q}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,.

Combining the equations of
\mathcal{L}^{(u,u)}
,
\mathcal{D}^{(u,u)}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
, we find the identity

\displaystyle\int_{k^{\prime}}\left[\,1+\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\,\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\right]\,\mathcal{L}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\left[\,1-\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\,\mathcal{I}(\sigma_{p})\,\right]\delta(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,.

Therefore, all terms of the above integral equation have a common
[1-\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\,\mathcal{I}(\sigma_{p})]
factor on the final state. Removing this factor, we arrive at the integral equation for
\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})=\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})-\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{q^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\Gamma(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{L}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{q}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,.

(38)

The structure of Eq. (
38
) similar to the original one for the
T
matrix element Eq. (
2
), except explicit kinematic divergences are removed. This includes initial and final state rescatterings, which also removes the disconnected structure of
\mathcal{M}_{2}
implicitly since it is part of the rescattering functions. Equation (
38
) also shows explicit intermediate state interactions with
\Gamma\cdot\mathcal{L}^{(u,u)}
. We can interpret
\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}
as the

\displaystyle\im\mathcal{M}_{3}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{M}_{3}^{(u,u)\,{\dagger}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\Phi(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{M}_{3}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle+\int_{p^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{M}_{3}^{(u,u)\,{\dagger}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\Phi(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k})

Eden et al. 1966

We can now remove the single exchange pole from
\mathcal{M}_{3}^{(u,u)}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{3,0}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\equiv\mathcal{M}_{3}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})-\mathcal{D}_{0}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,.

We substitute Eq. (

\displaystyle\im\mathcal{D}_{0}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{2}^{{\dagger}}(\sigma_{p})\,\rho(\sigma_{p})\,\mathcal{D}_{0}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})+\mathcal{M}_{2}^{{\dagger}}(\sigma_{p})\,\Delta(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k})

\displaystyle+\mathcal{D}_{0}^{(u,u)\,{\dagger}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,\rho(\sigma_{k})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k})\,,

(45)

where Eq. (

\displaystyle\im\mathcal{M}_{3,0}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{M}_{3,0}^{(u,u)\,{\dagger}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\Phi(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{M}_{3,0}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle+\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{M}_{3,0}^{(u,u)\,{\dagger}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\Phi(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\Big[\,\delta(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k})+\mathcal{D}_{0}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,\Big]

\displaystyle+\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}\,\Big[\,\delta(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}^{{\dagger}}(\sigma_{p})+\mathcal{D}_{0}^{(u,u)\,{\dagger}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\Big]\,\Phi(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{M}_{3,0}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})

The kinematic singularity associated with the single particle exchange is absent, however we have introduced explicit singularities from two particle exchanges in the last three terms. The procedure of removing isolated kinematic divergences is repeated, this time for the two particle exchange amplitude

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})=\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\,\int_{k^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}(k^{\prime})\,\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k})\,.

which does not contain

,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{3,1}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{M}_{3,0}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})-\mathcal{D}_{1}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,,

and inserting into Eq. (

to generate the

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})=-\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\widetilde{\rho}(\sigma_{p^{\prime}})\,\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,.

After applying these operations on all occurrences of
\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}
in

K

\mathcal{M}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{3,\textrm{df}}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\equiv\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,,

(65)

where
\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}
is a symmetric amputated amplitude satisfying the linear integral equation driven by the symmetric
K

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})=\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})-\int_{p^{\prime}}\int_{k^{\prime}}\,\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,\widetilde{\rho}(\sigma_{p^{\prime}})\,\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,.

(66)

Equation (

provided the cutoff functions are chosen to be the same. We can combine Eq. (

), (

), and (

) into a single integral equation similar to its asymmetric form (
II
). Alternatively, we can arrive at the same relation by applying the symmetry operations to Eq. (
II

\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}
amplitude satisfies the integral equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{3}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{9}\,\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})-\mathcal{K}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\,\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k})-\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,\widetilde{\rho}(\sigma_{k})\,\mathcal{M}_{2}(\sigma_{k})\,

\displaystyle\qquad-\mathcal{K}_{2}(\sigma_{p})\,\int_{k^{\prime}}\,\Gamma(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{M}_{3}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})-\frac{1}{3}\int_{k^{\prime}}\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{df},3}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\widetilde{\rho}(\sigma_{k^{\prime}})\,\mathcal{M}_{3}^{(u,u)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\,,

where

directly given
\mathcal{K}_{2}
and

As a final remark, the finite-volume equivalents can be found by following the same arguments in the beginning of Sec.

\displaystyle\det\Big[\,1+\mathcal{K}_{3,\textrm{df}}(P)\cdot F_{3,L}(P)\,\Big]_{E=E_{\mathfrak{n}}}=0\,,

which agrees with the original quantization condition presented in Ref.
Hansen and Sharpe 2014
.

VI
Conclusions

We have presented a general way to derive relativistic
\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}
scattering integral equations and finite volume quantization conditions using
S
matrix unitarity. The method uses the fact that singularities from on-shell physics is responsible for the dominant finite-volume effects and
S
matrix unitarity constrains all physical singularities in the kinematic region of interest. The method is non-perturbative, and considers only on-shell amplitudes, avoiding the need to connect these quantities to quantum fields. This approach differs from previous FVU studies
Mai et al. 2017
;
Mai and Doring 2017
by considering the
T
matrix elements directly, which give a simpler path toward both the scattering equations and the finite-volume quantization conditions.

While the methods were shown to be equivalent
Jackura et al. 2019b
;
Blanton and Sharpe 2020b
, this work provides a direct connection between the RFT and FVU approaches. We found that the TOPT version of the RFT approach is most directly related to the FVU method, which was noted in Ref.
Blanton and Sharpe 2020a
in their derivation of the quantization conditions. The connection manifest itself by examining the on-shell representation of the
T
matrix elements, not the fully connected amplitude, and embedding the system in a confined volume. Making this connection more explicit allowed us to find a new expression for the
\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}

T
matrix integral equation shown in Eq. (
2
), as well as gave new physical insight the
\mathcal{T}_{\textrm{df}}
object which can be interpreted as the kinematically singular free
T
matrix element which was shown in Sec.
V
. We were able to recover the quantization conditions of Ref.
Blanton and Sharpe 2020a
directly, shown in Eq. (
7
). By using various identities and manipulations, we were able to transform the simple on-shell form for the
T
matrix element and the resulting quantization conditions to all previously derived results for three identical particles, including new versions for the asymmetric three-body
K
matrix given in Eq. (
II
) for the amplitude and Eq. (
8
) for the associated quantization condition.

Our approach here is rather efficient in generating the scattering equations and quantization conditions, requiring only that unitarity condition for the
T
matrix element and the kinematic functions which characterize the on-shell propagation of intermediate state particles. The results can easily be generated to particles of different species, coupled two and three channel systems, and particles with arbitrary spin once the state space is fully defined. Moreover, we find this direction promising for extending the framework to higher number of particles, e.g. four particle systems. Combined with the recent efficient version of the all-orders approach using TOPT
Blanton and Sharpe 2020a
, this framework provides a unified approach to linking lattice QCD observables to few-body phenomena.

Appendix B
Proof of unitarity for scattering equations

We show how the various
ansatz
for the
\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}
scattering equations satisfy the unitarity relations. The approach presented here is more efficient than those presented in Refs.
Jackura et al. 2019a
;
Briceño et al. 2019c
. It is instructive to illustrate the method by demonstrating that the simpler
\mathbf{2}\to\mathbf{2}
on-shell representation satisfies Eq. (
16

19

\mathcal{K}_{2}^{{\dagger}}=\mathcal{K}_{2}
in the physical region to find

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{2}=\mathcal{M}_{2}^{{\dagger}}+\mathcal{M}_{2}^{{\dagger}}\,\mathcal{I}^{{\dagger}}\,\mathcal{K}_{2}\,.

(74)

The, we substitute Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}=\mathcal{K}_{2}-\mathcal{M}_{2}^{{\dagger}}\,\mathcal{I}\,\mathcal{M}_{2}-\mathcal{M}^{{\dagger}}\,\mathcal{I}^{{\dagger}}\,\mathcal{K}_{2}\,\mathcal{I}\,\mathcal{M}_{2}\,.

Finally, we take the Hermitian conjugate of Eq. (
75

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}-\mathcal{M}_{2}^{{\dagger}}=-\mathcal{M}_{2}^{{\dagger}}\,\left[\,\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{I}^{{\dagger}}\,\right]\,\mathcal{M}_{2}\,,

This appendix serves to summarize important properties of the asymmetric amplitudes, which have been detailed in Refs.

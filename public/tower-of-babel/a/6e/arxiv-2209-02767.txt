Abstract
. Leroux has proved that unreachability in Petri nets can be witnessed by a Presburger separator, i.e. if a marking 𝒎 src \bm{m}_{\text{src}} cannot reach a marking 𝒎 tgt \bm{m}_{\text{tgt}} , then there is a formula φ \varphi of Presburger arithmetic such that: φ ⁡ ( 𝒎 src ) \varphi(\bm{m}_{\text{src}}) holds; φ \varphi is forward invariant, i.e., φ ⁡ ( 𝒎 ) \varphi(\bm{m}) and 𝒎 → 𝒎 ′ \bm{m}\rightarrow\bm{m}^{\prime} imply φ ⁡ ( 𝒎 ′ CLOSE \varphi(\bm{m}^{\prime} ); and ¬ φ ⁡ ( 𝒎 tgt ) \neg\varphi(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}) holds. While these separators could be used as explanations and as formal certificates of unreachability, this has not yet been the case due to their worst-case size, which is at least Ackermannian, and the complexity of checking that a formula is a separator, which is at least exponential (in the formula size). We show that, in continuous Petri nets, these two problems can be overcome. We introduce locally closed separators, and prove that: (a) unreachability can be witnessed by a locally closed separator computable in polynomial time; (b) checking whether a formula is a locally closed separator is in NC (so, simpler than unreachability, which is P-complete). We further consider the more general problem of (existential) set-to-set reachability, where two sets of markings are given as convex polytopes. We show that, while our approach does not extend directly, we can efficiently certify unreachability via an altered Petri net.

1.
Introduction

Petri nets form a widespread formalism of concurrency with several
applications ranging from the verification of concurrent programs to
the analysis of chemical systems. The reachability problem — which
asks whether a marking
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
can reach
another marking
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
— is fundamental as a plethora of problems,
such as verifying safety properties, reduce to it
(e.g.
[
GS92
,
FMW+17
,
BMTZ21
]
).

Leroux has shown that unreachability in Petri nets can be witnessed by a Presburger
separator
, i.e., if a marking
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
cannot reach a marking
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
, then there exists a formula
\varphi
of Presburger arithmetic such that:
\varphi(\bm{m}_{\text{src}})
holds;
\varphi
is forward invariant, i.e.,
\varphi(\bm{m})
and
\bm{m}\rightarrow\bm{m}^{\prime}
imply
\varphi(\bm{m}^{\prime}
); and
\varphi(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
does not hold
[
Ler12
]
. Intuitively,
\varphi
“separates”
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
from the set of markings reachable from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
. Leroux’s result leads to a very simple algorithm to decide the Petri net reachability problem, consisting of two semi-algorithms; the first one explores the markings reachable from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
, and halts if and when it hits
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
, while the second enumerates formulas from Presburger arithmetic, and halts if and when it hits a separator.

Separators can be used as
explanations
and as formal
certificates
. Verifying a safety property can be reduced to proving that a target marking (or set of markings) is not reachable from a source marking, and a separator is an invariant of the system that
explains
why the property holds. Further, if a reachability tool produces separators, then the user can check that the properties of a separator indeed hold, and so trust the result even if they do not trust the tool (e.g., because it has not been verified, or is executed on a remote faster machine). Yet, in order to be useful as explanations and certificates, separators have to satisfy two requirements: (1) they should not be too large, and (2) checking that a formula is a separator should have low complexity, and, in particular, strictly lower complexity than deciding reachability. This does not hold, at least in the worst-case, for the separators of
[
Ler12
]
: In the worst case, the separator has a size at least Ackermannian in the Petri net size (a consequence of the fact that the reachability problem is Ackermann-complete
[
LS19
,
Ler21
,
CO21
]
) and the complexity of the check is at least exponential.

In this paper, we show that, unlike the above,
continuous
Petri nets do have separators satisfying properties (1) and (2). Continuous Petri nets are a relaxation of the standard Petri net model, called
discrete
in the following, in which transitions are allowed to fire “fluidly”: instead of firing once, consuming
i_{p}
tokens from each input place
p
and adding
o_{q}
tokens to each output place
q
, a transition can fire
\alpha
times for any nonnegative real number
\alpha
, consuming and adding
\alpha\cdot i_{p}
and
\alpha\cdot o_{q}
tokens, respectively. Continuous Petri nets are interesting in their own right
[
DA10
]
, and moreover as an overapproximation of the discrete model. In particular, if
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
is not reachable from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
under the continuous semantics, then it is also not under the discrete one. As reachability in continuous Petri nets is P-complete
[
FH15
]
, and so drastically more tractable than discrete reachability, this approximation is used in many tools for the verification of discrete Petri nets, VAS, or multiset rewriting systems (e.g.
[
BFHH17
,
BEH+20
,
EHJM20
]
).

It is easy to see that unreachability in continuous Petri nets can be witnessed by separators expressible in linear arithmetic (the first-order theory of the reals with addition and order). Indeed, Blondin
et al
. show in
[
BFHH17
]
that the continuous reachability relation is expressible by an existential formula
\textit{reach}(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime})
of linear arithmetic, from which we can obtain a separator for any pair of unreachable markings. Namely, for all markings
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
and
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
, if
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
is not reachable from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
, then the formula
\textit{sep}_{\bm{m}_{\text{src}}}(\bm{m})\coloneqq\neg\textit{reach}(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m})
is a separator. Further,
\textit{reach}(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime})
has only linear size. However, these separators do not satisfy property (2). Indeed, while the reachability problem for continuous Petri nets is P-complete
[
FH15
]
, checking if a formula of linear arithmetic is a separator is coNP-hard, even for quantifier-free formulas in disjunctive normal form, a very small fragment. So, the separators arising from
[
BFHH17
]
cannot be directly used as certificates.

In this paper, we overcome this problem. We identify a class of
locally closed separators
, satisfying the following properties: unreachability can always be witnessed by locally closed separators; locally closed separators can be constructed in polynomial time; and checking whether a formula is a locally closed separator is computationally strictly
1
1

1

Assuming
\text{P}\neq\text{NC}
.
easier than deciding unreachability. Let us examine the last claim in more detail. While the reachability problem for continuous Petri nets is decidable in polynomial time, it is still time consuming for larger models, which can have tens of thousands of nodes. Indeed, for a Petri net with
n
places and
m
transitions, the algorithm of
[
FH15
]
requires to solve
\mathcal{O}(m^{2})
linear programming problems in
n
variables, each of them with up to
m
constraints. Moreover, since the problem is P-complete, it is unlikely that a parallel computer can significantly improve performance. We prove that, on the contrary, checking if a formula is a locally closed separator is in NC rather than P-complete, and so efficiently parallelizable. Further, the checking algorithm only requires to solve linear programming problems in
a single
variable.

We further consider “set-to-set reachability” where one must
determine whether there exists a marking
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\in A
that can reach
some marking
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\in B
. This naturally generalizes the case where

A=\{\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\}
and
B=\{\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\}
. We focus on the case where sets

A
and
B
are convex polytopes, as it can be solved in polynomial
time for continuous Petri nets
[
BH17
]
. We prove that,
unfortunately, we cannot validate locally closed separators in NC for
set-to-set reachability. Nonetheless, we show that we can efficiently
construct and validate locally closed separators (respectively in P
and NC), at the cost of working with an altered Petri net that encodes

A
and
B
.

2

3

[

, and derives a characterization of

4

[

]
is coNP-hard, and introduces

5

Section

Linear arithmetic and Farkas’ lemma.

An
atomic proposition
is a linear inequality of the form

\bm{a}\bm{x}\leq b
or
\bm{a}\bm{x}<b
, where
b
and the
components of
\bm{a}
are over
\mathbb{R}
. Such a proposition is

homogeneous
if
b=0
. A
linear formula
is a
first-order formula over atomic propositions with variables ranging
over
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(the classical definition uses
\mathbb{R}
, but in our context
variables will encode markings.) The
solutions
of a linear
formula
\varphi
, denoted
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
, are the assignments to the
free variables of
\varphi
that satisfy
\varphi
. A linear formula
is
homogeneous
if all of its atomic propositions are
homogeneous. For every formula
\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y})
where

\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
have the same arity, we write
\varphi^{\mathsf{T}}

to denote the formula that syntactically swaps
\bm{x}
and

\bm{y}
, so that
\llbracket\varphi^{\mathsf{T}}\rrbracket=\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathsf{T}}
. Throughout the paper, we will use Farkas’ lemma,
a fundamental result of linear arithmetic that rephrases the absence
of solution to a system into the existence of one for another
system. The lemma has many variants, of which we give just one (e.g. see
[
GM07
, Proposition 6.4.3(iii)]
):

Lemma 1

(Farkas’ lemma)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The
formula
\mathbf{A}\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
has no solution iff

\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}=\bm{0}\land\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}<0\land\bm{y}\geq\bm{0}
has a solution.

We give some geometric intuition for this lemma. We consider only the case
n=m
. Let
\bm{y}
be a vector satisfying
\bm{y}\geq\bm{0}
(that is,
\bm{y}
belongs to the positive orthant) and
\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}<0
. The hyperplane
H
perpendicular to
\bm{y}
divides the space into two half-spaces. Since
\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}<0
, the vectors
\bm{y}
and
\bm{b}
lie in opposite half-spaces; we say that
H

separates

\bm{b}
from
\bm{y}
. Further, since
\bm{y}
belongs to the positive orthant, the complete positive orthant lies in the same half-space as
\bm{y}
. In other words,
H
separates
\bm{b}
not only from
\bm{y}
, but from the positive orthant.

We argue that
\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}=\bm{0}
holds for some
\bm{y}
satisfying
\bm{y}\geq\bm{0}
and
\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}<0
iff
\mathbf{A}\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
has no solution. It is the case that
\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}=\bm{0}
holds for a vector
\bm{y}
iff every column vector of
\mathbf{A}
is perpendicular to
\bm{y}
, and so iff every column vector of
\mathbf{A}
lies in
H
. Since
H
separates
\bm{y}
and
\bm{b}
, and
\mathbf{A}\bm{x}
is a linear combination of the columns of
\mathbf{A}
for every vector
\bm{x}
, every column vector of
\mathbf{A}
lies in
H
iff
\bm{b}-\mathbf{A}\bm{x}
lies on the same half-space as
\bm{b}
for every
\bm{x}
. Since
H
separates
\bm{b}
from the positive orthant, this is the case iff
\bm{b}-\mathbf{A}\bm{x}
is not in the positive orthant for any
\bm{x}
, and so iff
\mathbf{A}\bm{x}\leq\bm{b}
has no solution.

2.1.
Separators and bi-separators

Let us fix a Petri net
\mathcal{N}=(P,T,F)
and two markings

\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}
.

{defi}

A
separator
for
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
is a linear formula
\varphi

over
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}
such that:

(1)

\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
;

(2)

\varphi
is
forward invariant
, i.e.,
\bm{m}\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
and
\bm{m}\xrightarrow{}\bm{m^{\prime}}
implies
\bm{m}^{\prime}\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
; and

(3)

\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\notin\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
.

It follows immediately from the definition that if there is a
separator
\varphi
for
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
, then
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\not\xrightarrow{}^{*}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
. Thus, in order to show that
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\not\xrightarrow{}^{*}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
in

\mathcal{N}
, we can either give a separator for
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})

w.r.t.
\mathcal{N}
, or a separator for
(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}},\bm{m}_{\text{src}})

w.r.t.
\mathcal{N}^{\mathsf{T}}
. Let us call them
forward
and

backward
separators. Loosely speaking, a forward separator
shows that
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
is not among the markings reachable from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
,
and a backward separator shows that
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
is not among the markings
backward-reachable from
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
. Bi-separators are formulas from which
we can easily obtain forward and backward separators. The symmetry
w.r.t. forward and backward reachability make them easier to handle.

{defi}

A linear formula
\varphi
over
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}
is

•

forward invariant
if
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
and
\bm{m}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}\bm{m}^{\prime\prime}
imply
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
;

•

backward invariant
if
(\bm{m}^{\prime},\bm{m}^{\prime\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
and
\bm{m}\xrightarrow{}\bm{m}^{\prime}
imply
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
; and

•

bi-invariant
if it is forward and backward invariant.

A
bi-separator
for
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
is a bi-invariant linear
formula
\varphi
such that
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{src}})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
,

(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
and
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})\notin\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
.

The following proposition shows how to obtain separators from
bi-separators.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\varphi
be a bi-separator for
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
. The following
holds:

•

\mathmakebox[27pt][l]{\psi(\bm{m})}\coloneqq\varphi(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m})
is a separator for
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
in
\mathcal{N}
;

•

\mathmakebox[27pt][l]{\psi^{\prime}(\bm{m})}\coloneqq\varphi(\bm{m},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
is a separator for
(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}},\bm{m}_{\text{src}})
in
\mathcal{N}^{\mathsf{T}}
.

Proof 2.1
.

It suffices to prove the first statement, the second is
symmetric. We have
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
and
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\notin\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
as
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{src}})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
and
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})\notin\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
.

It remains to show that
\psi
is forward invariant. Let
\bm{m}\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
and
\bm{m}\xrightarrow{\alpha t}\bm{m}^{\prime}
. Since

(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
and
\varphi
is forward
invariant, it is the case that
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}^{\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
. Hence,
\bm{m}^{\prime}\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
as desired.

3.
A characterization of unreachability

Given a Petri net
\mathcal{N}=(P,T,F)
, a set of transitions
U\subseteq T
, and two markings

\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}
, we write
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\xhookrightarrow{}^{U}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
to denote
that
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\xrightarrow{\sigma}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
for some sequence
\sigma
with support
U
.
In words,
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\xrightarrow{\sigma}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
denotes that
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
can be reached from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
by firing

all
transitions of
U
and
only
transitions of
U
. We say that
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
is
U
-reachable

from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}

In
[
FH15
]
, Fraca and Haddad gave the following characterization
of
U
-reachability in continuous Petri nets:

{thmC}

[
[
FH15
]
]
Let
\mathcal{N}=(P,T,F)
be a Petri net, let
U\subseteq T
, and let

\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}
. It is the case that
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\xhookrightarrow{}^{U}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
iff the following
conditions hold:

(1)

some vector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{T}
with support
U
satisfies

\bm{m}_{\text{src}}+\mathbf{F}\bm{x}=\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
,

(2)

some firing sequence
\sigma
with support
U
is enabled at

\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
, and

(3)

some firing sequence
\tau
with support
U
is
backward-enabled at
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
.

Furthermore, these conditions can be checked in polynomial time.

Theorem
3
provides a witness of
U
-reachability in the shape of a vector
\bm{x}
and two firing sequences

\sigma
and
\tau
. In this section, we find a witness of
U
-unreachability, i.e., a witness showing that
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\not\xhookrightarrow{}^{U}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
.

Consider the logical form of Theorem
3
:

\begin{gathered}\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\xhookrightarrow{}^{U}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\\
\iff(\exists\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{T}\colon A_{1}(\bm{x}))\land(\exists\sigma\in T^{*}\colon A_{2}(\sigma))\land(\exists\tau\in T^{*}\colon A_{3}(\tau)),\end{gathered}

(1)

where
A_{1}(\bm{x}),A_{2}(\sigma),A_{3}(\tau)
denote that
\bm{x}
has support
U
and satisfies

\bm{m}_{\text{src}}+\mathbf{F}\bm{x}=\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
, that
\sigma
has support
U
and is enabled at

\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
, and that
\tau
has support
U
and is
backward-enabled at
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
, respectively. The theorem is logically equivalent to

\begin{gathered}\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\not\xhookrightarrow{}^{U}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\\
\iff(\forall\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{T}\colon\neg A_{1}(\bm{x}))\lor(\forall\sigma\in T^{*}\colon\neg A_{2}(\sigma))\lor(\forall\tau\in T^{*}\colon\neg A_{3}(\tau)).\end{gathered}

(2)

To obtain a witness of
U
-unreachability, we
proceed in two steps:

•

In Section
3.1
, we define predicates
B_{2}(Q)
and
B_{3}(R)
, where
Q,R\subseteq P
, satisfying

\begin{gathered}(\forall\sigma\in T^{*}\colon\neg A_{2}(\sigma))\Longleftrightarrow(\exists Q\subseteq P\colon B_{2}(Q)),\\
(\forall\tau\in T^{*}\colon\neg A_{3}(\tau))\Longleftrightarrow(\exists R\subseteq P\colon B_{3}(R)).\\
\end{gathered}

(3)

•

In Section
3.2
, we define a predicate
B_{1}(\bm{y})
, where
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}
, satisfying

\begin{gathered}(\forall\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{T}\colon\neg A_{1}(\bm{x}))\Longrightarrow(\exists\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}\colon B_{1}(\bm{y}))\Longrightarrow\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\not\xhookrightarrow{}^{U}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}.\end{gathered}

(4)

Here, a remark is in order. Observe that we do not claim that
“
\forall\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{T}\colon\neg A_{1}(\bm{x})
” is
equivalent to “
\exists\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}\colon B_{1}(\bm{y})
.”
Rather, we claim that the former implies the latter, and the latter
implies
U
-unreachability.

Altogether, applying propositional logic to (
2
), (
3
), and (
4
) we obtain:

\begin{gathered}\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\not\xhookrightarrow{}^{U}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\\
\iff(\exists\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}\colon B_{1}(\bm{y}))\vee(\exists Q\subseteq P\colon B_{2}(Q))\vee(\exists R\subseteq P\colon B_{3}(R)).\end{gathered}

(5)

This shows that
U
-unreachability is witnessed either by a vector
\bm{y}
, a set
Q
, or a set
R
.

3.1.
The predicates
B_{2}(Q)
and
B_{3}(R)

The predicates
B_{2}(Q)
and
B_{3}(R)
were already implicitly defined in
[
FH15
]
in terms of sets of places called
siphons

and
traps
. Given a set of places
X
, let

{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}{X}
(resp.
{{X}^{\bullet}}
) be the set of transitions
t
such that

\mathbf{F}_{+}(p,t)>0
(resp.
\mathbf{F}_{-}(p,t)>0
) for some
p\in X
. A
siphon
of
\mathcal{N}
is a subset
Q
of places such that

{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}{Q}\subseteq{{Q}^{\bullet}}
. A
trap
is a subset
R
of places
such that
{{R}^{\bullet}}\subseteq{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}{R}
. Informally, empty siphons
remain empty, and marked traps remain marked. Formally, if
\bm{m}\xrightarrow{}\bm{m}^{\prime}
, then
\bm{m}(Q)=0
implies
\bm{m}^{\prime}(Q)=0
,
and
\bm{m}(R)>0
implies
\bm{m}^{\prime}(R)>0
. Fraca and Haddad proved in
[
FH15
]
:

{propC}

[
[
FH15
]
]
Let
\mathcal{N}=(P,T,F)
be a Petri net, let
U\subseteq T
, and let

\bm{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}
. The following statements hold:

•

No firing sequence with support
U
is enabled at
\bm{m}

iff there exists a siphon
Q
of
\mathcal{N}_{U}
such that
{{Q}^{\bullet}}\neq\emptyset
and
\bm{m}(Q)=0
;

•

No firing sequence with support
U
is backward-enabled at

\bm{m}
iff there exists a trap
R
of
\mathcal{N}_{U}
such that
{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}{R}\neq\emptyset
and
\bm{m}(R)=0
.

The if-direction of
the proposition is easy to prove. A siphon
Q
of
\mathcal{N}_{U}
satisfies

{{Q}^{\bullet}}\subseteq U
. Since
Q
is empty at
\bm{m}
, if we only
fire transitions from
U
then
Q
remains empty, and so no transition
of
{{Q}^{\bullet}}
ever becomes enabled. So, transitions of
{{Q}^{\bullet}}
can
only fire after transitions that do not belong to
U
have fired
first. But no such firing sequence has support
U
, and we are
done. The case of traps is analogous. For the only-if direction, we
refer the reader to
[
FH15
]
.

By Proposition
3.1
, the predicates

\displaystyle B_{2}(Q)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\text{$Q$ is a siphon of $\mathcal{N}_{U}$ such that ${{Q}^{\bullet}}\neq\emptyset$ and $\bm{m}_{\text{src}}(Q)=0$},

\displaystyle B_{3}(R)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\text{$R$ is a trap of $\mathcal{N}_{U}$ such that ${\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}{R}\neq\emptyset$ and $\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}(R)=0$},

satisfy (
3
).

3.2.
The predicate
B_{1}(\mathbf{y})

In order to define the predicate
B_{1}(\bm{y})
, we need to
introduce
exclusion functions
.

{defi}

Let
\mathcal{N}=(P,T,F)
be a Petri net, let
\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}

and let
S\subseteq S^{\prime}\subseteq T
. An
exclusion function
for
(S,S^{\prime})
is a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(1)

for all
t\in S^{\prime}
, if
\bm{m}\xrightarrow{t}\bm{m}^{\prime}
then
f(\bm{m})\leq f(\bm{m}^{\prime})
; and

(2)

either
f(\bm{m}_{\text{src}})>f(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
, or
f(\bm{m}_{\text{src}})=f(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
and
there exists
t\in S
such that if
\bm{m}\xrightarrow{t}\bm{m}^{\prime}

then
f(\bm{m})<f(\bm{m}^{\prime})
.

An
exclusion function for
S
is an exclusion function for
(S,S)
. An exclusion function
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}\to\mathbb{R}
is
linear
if there exists a vector
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{P}
such that
f(\bm{m})=\sum_{p\in P}\bm{y}(p)\cdot\bm{m}(p)
for every
\bm{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}
. Abusing language, we speak of the linear exclusion function
\bm{y}
.

We define:

B_{1}(\bm{y})\coloneqq\text{$\bm{y}$ is a linear exclusion function for $U$}.

We can easily show the second implication of (
4
), namely that an exclusion function for
U
is a witness of
U
-unreachabilty:

Lemma 3
.

If there exists a (linear) exclusion function for
U\subseteq T
, then
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\not\xhookrightarrow{}^{U}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
.

Proof 3.1
.

Let
f
be an exclusion function for
U
.
Call
f(\bm{m})
the
value
of
\bm{m}
. By the definition of an exclusion function,
either
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
has lower value than
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
and no transition of
U

decreases the value, or
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
and
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
have the same value, no
transition of
U
decreases the value, and at least one transition of
U
increases it. So, it
is impossible to reach
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
by firing
all
and

only
the transitions of
U
.

The first implication of (
4
) is shown in the forthcoming Proposition
5
.
We first need a technical lemma, one of the many consequences of Farkas’ lemma. Recall that, given
{\sim}\in\{\leq,=,\geq\}
, the notation
\bm{x}\sim_{U}\bm{y}
stands for
“
\bm{x}(t)\sim\bm{y}(t)
for all
t\in U
”.

Lemma 4
.

The system
\exists\bm{x}\geq\bm{0}:\mathbf{A}\bm{x}=\bm{b}\land U\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\bm{x})\subseteq V
has no solution iff
this system has a solution:
\exists\bm{y}:\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}\geq_{V}\bm{0}\land\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}\leq 0\land\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}<\sum_{t\in U}(\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y})(t)
.

Rather than giving a proof (which appears in the appendix), let us give
some geometric intuition. Consider the case in which
\emptyset=U\subseteq V=T
. In this case,
U\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\bm{x})\subseteq V
holds vacuously, and we can replace “
\geq_{V}
” by “
\geq
”
and “
\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}<\sum_{t\in U}(\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y})(t)
” by “
\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}<0
”. The conditions can now be geometrically interpreted as follows:

(1)

\exists\bm{x}\geq\bm{0}:\mathbf{A}\bm{x}=\bm{b}
. The vector
\bm{b}
lies in the cone spanned by the columns of
\mathbf{A}
.

(2)

\exists\bm{y}:\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}\geq\bm{0}\land\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}<0
. There exists a hyperplane
H
(the hyperplane perpendicular to
\bm{y}
) such that all columns of

\mathbf{A}
lie on the same side of the hyperplane as
\bm{y}
(because
\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}\geq\bm{0}
),
while
\bm{b}
lies on the opposite side (because
\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}<0
).

Clearly, the two conditions are incompatible. The conditions of the general case
U\subseteq V
are similar, but restricted to the columns of
\mathbf{A}

for which
\bm{x}
is positive: (1) now states that
\bm{b}
lies in the cone spanned by these columns, and (2) that some hyperplane separates
these columns from
\bm{b}
.

We are now ready to prove the first implication of (
4
), namely

(\forall\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{T}\colon\neg A_{1}(\bm{x}))\Longrightarrow(\exists\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}\colon\neg B_{1}(\bm{y})).

Recall that
A_{1}(\bm{x})
means that
\bm{x}
has support
U
and satisfies
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}+\mathbf{F}\bm{x}=\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
, and

B_{1}(\bm{y})
means that
\bm{y}
is a linear exclusion function for
U
. We prove a slightly more general result; the first implication
of (
4
) is the special case
S=U=S^{\prime}
:

Proposition 5
.

Let
\mathcal{N}=(P,T,F)
be a Petri net, let
\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}
, and let
S\subseteq S^{\prime}\subseteq T
. If no vector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{T}
satisfies
S\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\bm{x})\subseteq S^{\prime}
and

\bm{m}_{\text{src}}+\mathbf{F}\bm{x}=\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
, then there exists a linear
exclusion function for
(S,S^{\prime})
.

Proof 3.2
.

Assume no such
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{T}
exists. Let

\bm{b}\coloneqq\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}-\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
. By
Lemma
4
, there exists
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{P}

such that:
\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}\geq_{S^{\prime}}\bm{0}\land\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}\leq 0\land\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}<\sum_{s\in S}(\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y})_{s}
. We show that

f(\bm{k})\coloneqq\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{k}
is a linear exclusion
function for
(S,S^{\prime})
.

(1)

We have

f(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})-f(\bm{m}_{\text{src}})=\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}-\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{m}_{\text{src}}=\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}-\bm{m}_{\text{src}})=\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{b}=\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}\leq 0,

and hence
f(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})\leq f(\bm{m}_{\text{src}})
.

(2)

Let
\bm{m}\xrightarrow{\lambda s}\bm{m}^{\prime}
with
s\in S^{\prime}
and

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We have
\bm{m}^{\prime}=\bm{m}+\lambda\mathbf{F}\bm{e}_{s}
. Thus:

f(\bm{m}^{\prime})=\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{m}^{\prime}=\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{m}+\lambda(\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{F})\bm{e}_{s}=\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{m}+\lambda(\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y})^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{e}_{s}\geq\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{m}=f(\bm{m})
,
where the inequality follows from
\lambda>0
,

\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}\geq_{S^{\prime}}\bm{0}
and

s\in S^{\prime}
.

(3)

Recall that
\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}\leq 0
and
\sum_{s\in S}(\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y})_{s}>\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}
. If the latter sum equals zero, then
\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}<0
, and hence we are done since
f(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})-f(\bm{m}_{\text{src}})=\bm{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}<0
.

Otherwise, we have
\sum_{s\in S}(\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y})_{s}>0
since
S\subseteq S^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}\geq_{S^{\prime}}\bm{0}
. Therefore, there exists a transition
s\in S

such that
(\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y})_{s}>0
. Let
\bm{m}\xrightarrow{s}\bm{m}^{\prime}
. We have
\bm{m}^{\prime}=\bm{m}+\lambda\mathbf{F}\bm{e}_{s}
for some
\lambda>0
. Thus,

f(\bm{m}^{\prime})=\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{m}+\lambda(\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y})^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{e}_{s}>\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{m}=f(\bm{m})
, where the inequality holds by
\lambda>0
and

(\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y})_{s}>0
.

Putting together Theorem
3
with Proposition
3.1
, and Proposition
5
,
we obtain the following characterization of unreachability:

Proposition 6
.

Let
\mathcal{N}=(P,T,F)
be a Petri net, let
U\subseteq T
, and

\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}
. It is the case that

\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\not\xhookrightarrow{}^{U}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
iff

(1)

there exists a linear exclusion function for
U
, or

(2)

there exists a siphon
Q
of
\mathcal{N}_{U}
such that

{{Q}^{\bullet}}\neq\emptyset
and
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}(Q)=0
, or

(3)

there exists a trap
R
of
\mathcal{N}_{U}
such that
{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}{R}\neq\emptyset
and
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}(R)=0
.

This proposition shows that we can produce a
witness of unreachability for a given support either as an exclusion function, a siphon, or a
trap. The following example shows how to use this to show that a marking
cannot be reached from another one.

{exa}

Consider the Petri net of Figure
1
, but with
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\coloneqq\{p_{1}\mapsto 0,p_{2}\mapsto 0,p_{3}\mapsto 1,p_{4}\mapsto 0\}
as target. We prove
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\not\xrightarrow{}^{*}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
. For the sake
of contradiction, assume
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\xhookrightarrow{}^{U}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
for some
U\subseteq T
. We proceed in several steps:

•

Claim:
t_{4}\notin U
. The function
f(\bm{m})\coloneqq\bm{m}(p_{4})
is an exclusion function for
T
. Indeed, since no
transition decreases the number of tokens of
p_{4}
,

\bm{m}\xrightarrow{t}\bm{m}^{\prime}
implies
f(\bm{m})\leq f(\bm{m}^{\prime})

for every transition
t\in T
. Furthermore,
f(\bm{m}_{\text{src}})=0=f(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
, and, since
t_{4}
adds tokens to
p_{4}
,
\bm{m}\xrightarrow{t_{4}}\bm{m}^{\prime}
implies
f(\bm{m})<f(\bm{m}^{\prime})
. It
follows that no firing sequence from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
to
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
can fire

t_{4}
. Therefore,
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
is reachable from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
in
\mathcal{N}
iff it is reachable from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
in

\mathcal{N}_{T\setminus\{t_{4}\}}
.

•

Claim:
t_{2}\notin U
. The set
Q\coloneqq\{p_{4}\}
is a
siphon of
\mathcal{N}_{T\setminus\{t_{4}\}}
(but not of
\mathcal{N}
). Since

\bm{m}_{\text{src}}(Q)=0
, it is impossible to use transitions of
\mathcal{N}_{T\setminus\{t_{4}\}}
that consume from
Q
, i.e. transitions of

{{Q}^{\bullet}}=\{t_{2}\}
. So
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
is reachable from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
in
\mathcal{N}
iff it is reachable from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
in

\mathcal{N}_{T\setminus\{t_{2},t_{4}\}}

•

Claim:
t_{1},t_{3}\notin U
. The set
R\coloneqq\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
is a trap of
\mathcal{N}_{T\setminus\{t_{2},t_{4}\}}
(but not of

\mathcal{N}_{T\setminus\{t_{4}\}}
). Since
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}(R)=0
, it is
impossible to reach
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
using transitions of
\mathcal{N}_{T\setminus\{t_{2},t_{4}\}}
that produce in

, i.e. transitions of

{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}{R}=\{t_{1},t_{3}\}
.

By the claims,
\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
is reachable from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
in
\mathcal{N}
iff it is reachable from
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}
in
\mathcal{N}_{\emptyset}
. But this can only happen

, which is not the case.

In the next section, we transform this collection of witnesses of unreachability into one single separator that can be used as
certificate.

4.
Separators as certificates

Let
\mathcal{N}=(P,T,F)
be a Petri net and let
\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{P}
be markings of
\mathcal{N}
. From
[
BFHH17
]
, one can easily
show that if
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\not\xrightarrow{}^{*}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
, then there is a separator
for
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
. Indeed,
[
BFHH17
, Prop. 3.2]
shows that there
exists an existential formula
\psi
of linear arithmetic such that

\bm{m}\xrightarrow{}^{*}\bm{m}^{\prime}
iff
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime})\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
. Thus, the formula
\varphi(\bm{m})\coloneqq\psi(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m})
is a separator.

However,
\varphi
is not adequate as a
certificate
of
unreachability. Indeed, checking a certificate for

\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\not\xrightarrow{}^{*}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
should have smaller complexity than
deciding whether
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\xrightarrow{}^{*}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
. This is not the case for
existential linear formulas, because
\bm{m}_{\text{src}}\xrightarrow{}^{*}\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}}
can be
decided in polynomial time, but checking that an existential linear
formula is a separator is coNP-hard.

Proposition 7
.

The problem of determining whether an existential linear formula

\varphi
is a separator for
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
is coNP-hard, even if

\varphi
is a quantifier-free formula in DNF and homogeneous (i.e.,
each atomic proposition
\bm{a}\bm{x}\sim b
is such that
b=0
.)

Proof 4.1
.

We give a reduction from the problem of determining whether a DNF
boolean formula
\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
is a tautology. Since

\psi
is in DNF, we can test whether it is satisfiable in

4.1.
Locally closed bi-separators

The most difficult part of checking that a formula
\varphi
is a
bi-separator consists of checking that it is forward and backward
invariant. Let us focus on forward invariance, backward invariance
being symmetric.

Recall the definition: for all markings
\bm{m},\bm{m^{\prime}},\bm{m^{\prime\prime}}

and every transition
t
: if
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket

and
\bm{m}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\alpha t}\bm{m}^{\prime\prime}
then
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
. Assume now that
\varphi
is in DNF, i.e., a
disjunction of clauses
\varphi=\varphi_{1}\lor\cdots\lor\varphi_{n}
. The forward invariance check can be decomposed into
n

smaller checks, one for each
i\in[1..n]
, of the form: if

(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
, then
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
. However, in general the check
cannot
be
decomposed into
local
checks of the form: there exists
j\in[1..m]
such that
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
implies

(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi_{j}\rrbracket
. Indeed, while this
property is sufficient for forward invariance, it is not
necessary. Intuitively, locally closed bi-separators are separators
where invariance can be established by local checks.

For the formal definition, we need to introduce some notations.
Given a transition
t
and atomic propositions
\psi,\psi^{\prime}
, we say
that
\psi

t
-implies

\psi^{\prime}
, written
\psi\leadsto_{t}\psi^{\prime}
, if
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime})\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
and
\bm{m}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\alpha t}\bm{m}^{\prime\prime}
implies
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime\prime})\in\llbracket\psi^{\prime}\rrbracket
. We further say that a clause
\psi=\psi_{1}\land\cdots\land\psi_{m}

t
-implies
a clause
\psi^{\prime}=\psi_{1}^{\prime}\land\cdots\land\psi_{n}^{\prime}
, written
\psi\leadsto_{t}\psi^{\prime}
, if for every

j\in[1..n]
, there exists
i\in[1..m]
such that
\psi_{i}\leadsto_{t}\psi_{j}^{\prime}
.

{defi}

A linear formula
\varphi
is
locally closed
w.r.t.
\mathcal{N}=(P,T,F)
if:

•

\varphi=\varphi_{1}\lor\cdots\lor\varphi_{n}
is
quantifier-free, in DNF and homogeneous,

•

for every
t\in T
and every
i\in[1..n]
, there exists
j\in[1..n]
s.t.
\varphi_{i}\leadsto_{t}\varphi_{j}
,

•

for every
t\in T^{\mathsf{T}}
and every
i\in[1..n]
, there
exists
j\in[1..n]
s.t.
\varphi_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}\leadsto_{t}\varphi_{j}^{\mathsf{T}}
.

Note that the definition is semantic. We make the straightforward but
crucial observation that:

Proposition 9
.

Locally closed formulas are bi-invariant.

Proof 4.2
.

Let
\varphi=\varphi_{1}\lor\cdots\lor\varphi_{n}
be a locally
closed formula. We only consider the forward case; the other case is
symmetric. Let
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
and
\bm{m}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\alpha t}\bm{m}^{\prime\prime}
. Let
i\in[1..n]
be such that

(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
. Since
\varphi
is
locally closed, there exists
j\in[1..n]
such that
\varphi_{i}\leadsto_{t}\varphi_{j}
. For every atomic proposition
\psi^{\prime}
of

\varphi_{j}
, there exists an atomic proposition
\psi
of

\varphi_{i}
such that
\psi\leadsto_{t}\psi^{\prime}
. Since each atomic
proposition of
\varphi_{i}
is satisfied by
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime})
, we
obtain
(\bm{m},\bm{m}^{\prime\prime})\in\llbracket\varphi_{j}\rrbracket
.

Proposition
9
justifies the following
definition:

{defi}

A
locally closed bi-separator
for
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})
is a
locally closed formula
\varphi
s.t.
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{src}})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
,
(\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
and
(\bm{m}_{\text{src}},\bm{m}_{\text{tgt}})\notin\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
.

Indeed, by Proposition
9
, a locally closed
bi-separator is a bi-separator, as the bi-invariance condition of
Definition
2.1
follows from local closedness.

8.
Conclusion

We have shown that continuous Petri nets admit locally closed bi-separators that can be efficiently computed. These separators are succinct and very efficiently checkable certificates of unreachability. In particular, checking that a linear formula is a locally closed bi-separator is in NC, and only requires to solve linear inequations in one variable over the nonnegative reals. While this does not directly hold for the more general of set-to-set reachability, we have shown that it can be extended to an altered Petri net (in the case of convex polytopes).

Verification tools that have not been formally verified, or rely (as is usually the case) on external packages for linear arithmetic, can apply our results to provide certificates for their output. Further, our separators can be used as explanations of why a certain marking is unreachable. Obtaining minimal explanations is an interesting research avenue.

From a logical point of view, separators are very closely related to interpolants for linear arithmetic, which are widely used in formal verification to refine abstractions in the CEGAR approach
[
BZM08
,
RS10
,
SPDA14
,
ABKS15
]
. We intend to explore whether they can constitute the basis of a CEGAR approach for the verification of continuous Petri nets.

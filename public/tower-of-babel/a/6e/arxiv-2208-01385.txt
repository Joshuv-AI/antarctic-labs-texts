Abstract
We derive a fast and optimal algorithm for solving practical weighted max-min SINR problems in cell-free massive MIMO networks. For the first time, the optimization problem jointly covers long-term power control and distributed beamforming design under imperfect cooperation. In particular, we consider user-centric clusters of access points cooperating on the basis of possibly limited channel state information sharing. Our optimal algorithm merges powerful power control tools based on interference calculus with the recently developed team theoretic framework for distributed beamforming design. In addition, we propose a variation that shows faster convergence in practice.



I
Introduction

Cell-free massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) is one of the key research avenues for future generation sub-
6
GHz wireless access networks. Its main focus is the study of simple and scalable access-point (AP) cooperation architectures in ultra-dense networks, able to offer uniformly good service in a cost-effective manner
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

To achieve this ambitious goal, cell-free massive MIMO has evolved from related standardized technologies, such as 4G and 5G coordinated multi-point (CoMP), by introducing flexible cooperation regimes with lower information exchange and joint processing requirements. A first innovation is the adoption of
user-centric
designs, where multiple network components are grouped into possibly overlapping cooperation clusters, each of them allocated to a single user equipment (UE), without relying on the notion of a cell
[
2
,
3
]
. A second crucial aspect is the optimal distribution of signal processing tasks between remote control units and APs such that information exchange is minimized. For instance, in the original cell-free massive MIMO idea
[
1
]
, all short-term array processing tasks, such as channel estimation and beamforming, are performed by the APs on the basis of only
local
channel measurements. Then, joint encoding/decoding and resource allocation tasks such as power control are performed at a remote processing unit by only assuming knowledge of long-term statistical information. Many variations of this cooperation regime are currently subject of active research
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

Against this background, this study considers the important open problem of joint optimal long-term power control and distributed beamforming design under imperfect channel state information (CSI) sharing across the APs. To relate the present study with existing approaches, we recall that iterative algorithms for long-term power control under fixed beamforming design have been extensively studied in both the cellular and cell-free massive MIMO literature (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
4
,
8
,
9
]
). In contrast, the problem of optimal distributed beamforming design without power control has been solved only recently in
[
10
,
11
]
using elements of
team
decision theory, a rigorous control theoretical framework for decentralized decision making under asymmetry of information. However, none of these previous studies have jointly optimized beamformers and transmit powers. The closest approaches to the problem at hand date back to the studies on joint beamforming design and power control in
static
channels (see, e.g.,
[
12
,
13
]
).
Although conceptually similar, these methods only apply to centralized or purely statistical beamforming. Furthermore, the centralized case typically considers rather impractical short-term optimization, i.e., for each channel state realization.

Unlike previous studies, we target a certain class of weighted max-min SINR problems where, for the first time, joint optimal long-term power control and distributed beamforming design is performed.
By tuning the problem weights, the corresponding solutions attain all positive boundary points of the achievable rate region predicted by the popular
use-and-then-forget
(UatF) ergodic capacity inner bound
[
8
]
, which is particularly suitable for distributed cell-free operations
[
4
]
. We propose two fast algorithms: for the first algorithm, global optimality is theoretically demonstrated; for the second algorithm, convergence to the global optimum is illustrated via simulations. On top of handling distributed cell-free operations, the proposed algorithms may also offer a more practical alternative to the available approaches for centralized operations.

II
Model and problem statement

II-A

Capacity of distributed cell-free uplink networks

Let us consider the uplink of a cell-free network composed of
L
APs indexed by
\mathcal{L}:=\{1,\ldots,L\}
, each of them equipped with
N
antennas, and
K
single-antenna UEs indexed by
\mathcal{K}:=\{1,\ldots,K\}
. By assuming conventional flat-fading synchronous channel models
[
16
]
, we focus on the ergodic capacity in the classical Shannon sense, which is approximated with the popular
use-and-then-forget
(UatF) inner bound
[
8
]
. In more detail, we define
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})

R_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}):=\log_{2}(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})),

\mathrm{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}):=\resizebox{15600900}{}{$\dfrac{p_{k}|\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}]|^{2}}{p_{k}\mathsf{V}(\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k})+\underset{j\neq k}{\sum}p_{j}\mathsf{E}[|\mathbbm{h}_{j}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}|^{2}]+\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{v}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}]}$},

where
\bm{p}:=[p_{1},\ldots,p_{K}]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}
is a deterministic vector of transmit powers,
\mathbbm{h}_{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{NL}
is a random channel vector modeling the fading state between UE
k
and all APs, and
\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{NL}
is a random beamforming vector, which is applied by the APs to process jointly the signal of UE
k
. We assume that
\mathbbm{v}_{k}\neq\bm{0}
almost surely (a.s.), so
\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{v}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}]\neq 0
and the SINR is well defined. We stress that
random
beamforming here simply refers to the fact that beamformers may adapt to fading realizations. The achievable rate region is given by

\mathcal{R}:=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\|\bm{p}\|_{\infty}\leq P\\
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\\
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{v}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}]\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\bigcup}\left\{\bm{r}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}\middle|\begin{array}[]{c}r_{1}\leq R_{1}(\mathbbm{v}_{1},\bm{p})\\
\vdots\\
r_{K}\leq R_{K}(\mathbbm{v}_{K},\bm{p})\end{array}\right\},

where
0<P<\infty
denotes the per-UE power budget, and where the set
\mathcal{V}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{NL}
denotes an
information
constraint, defined in the following section, modelling
distributed
cell-free operations such as in
[
2
]
. The region
\mathcal{R}
is especially relevant if codewords can span many realizations of a stationary and ergodic fading process, and it relies on the following widely-used information theoretical simplifications
[
8
,
17
]
:

(i)

Correlation across different fading realizations is neglected in the encoding and decoding phase, although it can be exploited in the channel estimation phase (the overhead of this operation is left unspecified here);

(ii)

CSI is neglected in the decoding phase, but it is exploited for coherent receive beamforming, i.e., to generate the soft estimates feeded as an input to the channel decoder;

(iii)

Treating-interference-as-noise
(TIN) is considered in the decoding phase. More precisely, the message of UE
k
is transmitted using simple coding techniques for the memoryless point-to-point Gaussian channel, while interference is mitigated by only using spatial processing.

Remark 1
.

Alternative rate regions based on TIN with various forms of CSI at the decoder are often considered (see
[
9
,
17
,
18
]
and references therein). These regions may provide less pessimistic capacity approximations, but optimization problems become challenging because of the expectation outside the logarithm in the resulting rate expressions. To deal with this issue, most studies perform short-term optimization, i.e., an optimization problem is solved for each fading realization. However, this approach is hardly practical in cell-free networks because of the overhead required to exchange instantaneous CSI among many geographically distant network elements. In contrast, the proposed algorithms based on
\mathcal{R}
are readily implementable in distributed cell-free networks such as the ones envisioned in
[
4
]
. In these networks, short-term array processing is confined to the APs, while joint decoding and optimization (e.g., power control) takes place at a remote processing unit based on long-term statistical information.

II-B

Information constraints

In distributed cell-free operation, APs joint processing capabilities are impaired. One of the major impairment is that the APs must compute their portion of the beamformers on the basis of imperfect CSI sharing, or even based on only
local
CSI
[
1
]
. However, to date, most studies on cell-free massive MIMO do not formally model this type of impairment, so, strictly speaking, they do not perform truly distributed beamforming optimization. To address this limitation, as in
[
10
]
, we model constraints related to imperfect CSI sharing with the following set:

\mathcal{V}_{k}=\mathcal{H}_{1}^{N}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{H}_{L}^{N},

(1)

where
\mathcal{H}_{l}^{N}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{N}
denotes the set of
N
-tuples of
\Sigma_{l}
-measurable functions
\Omega\to\mathbbmss{C}
satisfying
(\forall\mathbbm{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{l}^{N})

\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{h}\|_{2}^{2}]<\infty
, and where
\Sigma_{l}\subseteq\Sigma
is the sub-
\sigma
-algebra induced by the available CSI at the
l
th AP, also called the
information subfield
of AP
l

[
19
]
. Furthermore, another practical limitation is that only a subset of APs are used for decoding the message of a given UE. More specifically, scalable cell-free networks are expected to implement some type of user-centric rule
[
3
]
, which allocates to each UE a cluster of serving APs. As shown in
[
11
]
, this practical limitation can be modeled by setting

in (
1

\{\bm{0}_{N}~(\mathrm{a.s.})\}
if AP

is not serving UE
k
. Considering both impairments, we remark that
\mathcal{V}_{k}
is a linear

of
\mathcal{H}^{NL}
.

II-C

Weighted max-min SINR optimization

The objective of the proposed algorithms is to optimize jointly the distributed beamformers and the transmit powers, with the intent to obtain rate tuples on the boundary
\partial\mathcal{R}

(see Definition

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}\\

\displaystyle\min_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\|\bm{p}\|_{\infty}\leq P

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}

Remark 2
.

A popular alternative to weighted max-min optimization is

(WSR) optimization (see, e.g.,
[

]

III
Optimal iterative solution

In this section we present a fast iterative algorithm that provably converges to a solution to Problem (
2
), provided that a solution exists and that it attains a positive optimum. As we prove later, existence and positivity of a solution is guaranteed by a simple condition on the fading and CSI distribution. Specifically, it is guaranteed if
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathcal{V}_{k}^{\prime}:=\{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}~|~\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}]\neq 0\}
is nonempty, which holds for most fading and CSI distributions.

Remark 3
.

In particular,
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
does not hold if the APs have no CSI and, in addition, the channels have zero mean. In this case,
(\forall\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k})~\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}]=\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{h}_{k}]^{\mathsf{H}}\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{v}_{k}]=0
holds. This is one of the main drawbacks of the UatF bounding technique, which requires either sufficient CSI
or
a strong channel mean to produce non-trivial capacity inner bounds.

III-A

Power control using interference calculus

As a preliminary step, we first revisit known results for fairly general power control problems of the type

\underset{\|\bm{p}\|\leq P}{\text{maximize}}\min_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}^{-1}u_{k}(\bm{p}),

(3)

III-C

Team MMSE beamforming

The following proposition states that optimal distributed beamformers under fixed power control, i.e., beamformers that attain the supremum in (
6
) for a given
\bm{p}
, can be obtained by solving a minimum mean squared error (MMSE) problem under information constraints. For convenience, we define the random channel matrix
\mathbbm{H}:=[\mathbbm{h}_{1},\ldots,\mathbbm{h}_{K}]

Proposition 4
.

For given
k\in\mathcal{K}
and
\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}
, consider the optimization problem

\underset{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\emph{minimize}}~\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}),

(7)

where we define
(\forall\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{NL})

\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}):=\mathsf{E}\left[\|\mathrm{diag}(\bm{p})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbbm{H}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}-\bm{e}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\mathsf{E}\left[\|\mathbbm{v}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\right].

Problem (
7
) has a unique solution
\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, then
u_{k}(\bm{p})=\mathrm{SINR}_{k}\left(\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{\star},\bm{p}\right)
holds.

Proof.

Existence and uniqueness of the solution follows from
[
10

. For the second statement, we observe that

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\\
\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{v}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}]\neq 0\end{subarray}}\inf_{\beta\in\mathbbmss{C}}\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\beta\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\\
\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{v}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}]\neq 0\end{subarray}}1-\dfrac{p_{k}|\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}]|^{2}}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}}p_{j}\mathsf{E}[|\mathbbm{h}_{j}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}|^{2}]+\mathsf{E}\left[\|\mathbbm{v}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\\
\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{v}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}]\neq 0\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})}=\dfrac{1}{1+u_{k}(\bm{p})}\overset{(c)}{<}1,

where
(a)
follows from
(\forall\beta\in\mathbbmss{C})(\forall\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k})

\beta\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
,
(b)
follows from standard minimization of scalar quadratic forms as in the proof of the UatF bound (see, e.g.,
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
), and
(c)
follows from
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
and Proposition
3
(i). If
\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{\star}\neq\bm{0}
a.s., the identity
u_{k}(\bm{p})=\mathrm{SINR}_{k}\left(\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{\star},\bm{p}\right)
follows readily from the above chain of equalities. The proof is concluded by observing that
\inf_{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})<1
implies
\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{\star}\neq\bm{0}
a.s..
∎

The solution to (
7
), which can be interpreted as the best distributed approximation of a regularized zero-forcing beamformer, can be obtained via the recently developed
team
MMSE beamforming framework given by
[
10
]
. Although presented in the context of downlink beamforming with perfect message sharing, the method in
[
10
]
directly applies to the uplink case and to user-centric clustering
[
11
]
. This method states that the solution to (
7

quantized

To keep the proposed algorithms general, in the following section we do not specify the setup (i.e.,
\mathbbm{H}

III-D

Proposed algorithms

We now have all the elements for deriving an algorithmic solution to Problem (
2
), i.e., to perform joint optimal long-term power control and distributed beamforming design. Specifically, we propose to solve Problem (
2
) via the fixed-point iterations in (
5
), as summarized below:

\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}

repeat

for

k\in\mathcal{K}

do

\mathbbm{v}_{k}\leftarrow\arg\min_{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})

end

for

\bm{t}\leftarrow\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\omega_{1}p_{1}}{\mathrm{SINR}_{1}(\mathbbm{v}_{1},\bm{p})}&\ldots&\frac{\omega_{K}p_{K}}{\mathrm{SINR}_{K}(\mathbbm{v}_{K},\bm{p})}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}

\bm{p}\leftarrow\frac{P}{\|\bm{t}\|_{\infty}}\bm{t}

until
no significant progress is observed.

Asymptotic convergence to an optimal solution is guaranteed by Proposition
2
, Proposition
3
, and Proposition
4
. Clearly, the above algorithm relies on solving Problem (
7
) at each iteration, which, as already mentioned, can be done following
[
10
,
11
]
. Note that
\mathbbm{v}_{k}
is a
function
of the CSI, and not a deterministic vector. In practice, as we will see in the numerical examples, storing
\mathbbm{v}_{k}
during the algorithm execution means storing its long-term parameters (e.g., the regularization factor in a regularized channel inversion block).

An intuitive variant of the first algorithm proposed above alternates between distributed beamforming optimization and power control, and it is summarized below:

\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}

repeat

for

k\in\mathcal{K}

do

\mathbbm{v}_{k}\leftarrow\arg\min_{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})

end

for

\bm{p}\leftarrow\arg\max_{\|\bm{p}\|_{\infty}\leq P}\min_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})

until
no significant progress is observed.

In addition to solving Problem (
7
), this algorithm requires a solution to
\max_{\|\bm{p}\|_{\infty}\leq P}\min_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})
for given beamformers
(\mathbbm{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbbm{v}_{K})
, which is obtained in closed form in
[
25
]
. The advantage is that this power control step maximizes the improvement in utility. The disadvantage is that performing this power control step is more complex than performing a fixed point iteration. However, this increase in complexity is in practice negligible when compared to the complexity of solving Problem (
7
). Convergence properties of this algorithm will be studied in a future work, and they are here only tested using numerical experiments.

IV-B

Local CSI and path loss based user-centric clustering

We solve Problem (
2
) by focusing on the following simple user-centric distributed information structure with no short-term CSI sharing. We assume that each UE
k
is served only by its
Q=4
strongest APs, i.e., by the subset of APs indexed by

(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\hat{\mathbbm{h}}_{l,k}:=\begin{cases}\mathbbm{h}_{l,k}&\text{if }l\in\mathcal{L}_{k},\\
\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{h}_{l,k}]&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This model mirrors the fully distributed implementation proposed in
[
2
]
. For simplicity, we neglect estimation noise, and we focus on a model where small-scale fading coefficients are either perfectly known at some APs or completely unknown. Under this model, the optimal solution
[\mathbbm{v}^{\mathsf{H}}_{1,k},\ldots,\mathbbm{v}^{\mathsf{H}}_{L,k}]:=\mathbbm{v}^{\mathsf{H}}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
to Problem (

(l\in\mathcal{L})(k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathbbm{v}_{l,k}=\mathbbm{V}_{l}\bm{c}_{l,k},

where:

•

\mathbbm{V}_{l}:=\left(\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{p})\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}+\bm{\Psi}_{l}\right)^{-1}\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{p})^{\frac{1}{2}}

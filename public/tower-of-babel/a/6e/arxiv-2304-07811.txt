Abstract
. We study spectral subspaces of the Sturm-Liouville operator f ↦ − ( p ​ f ′ ) ′ f\mapsto-(pf^{\prime})^{\prime} on ℝ \mathbb{R} , where p p is a positive, piecewise constant function. Functions in these subspaces can be thought of as having a local bandwidth determined by 1 / p 1/\sqrt{p} . Using the spectral theory of Sturm-Liouville operators, we make the reproducing kernel of these spectral subspaces more explicit and compute it completely in certain cases. As a contribution to sampling theory, we then prove necessary density conditions for sampling and interpolation in these subspaces and determine the critical density that separates sets of stable sampling from sets of interpolation.

1.
Introduction

In
[
9
]
we proposed a new notion of variable bandwidth that
connects the theory of Sturm-Liouville operators on
\mathbb{R}
with
notions from signal processing. Roughly speaking, bandwidth is the
largest frequency
\Omega
in a signal (or function)
f
on the real line, so
that
f
can be represented as
f(x)=\int_{-\Omega}^{\Omega}\hat{f}(\lambda)e^{i\lambda x}\,d\lambda
via the inverse Fourier
transform. Intuitively it makes sense to speak of a
maximum frequency near a time
x
or, in other words, of variable
bandwidth. However, a precise mathematical formulation of variable
bandwidth seems difficult. The engineering literature offers several
informal definitions
[
3
,
5
,
11
,
19
]
, but so far none of them has become
accepted. The mathematical approach in

[
1
,
2
]
only yields a class of new norms on Sobolev spaces
and lacks the fundamental features of bandwidth. The attempt of Kempf
and Martin
[
14
]
uses self-adjoint extensions of a
differential operator for an algorithmic approach to variable
bandwidth.

Our idea in
[
9
]
was to define variable bandwidth by means of
the spectral subspaces of a Sturm-Liouville operator on
\mathbb{R}
which is closely related to the work of Pesenson and Zayed on abstract Paley-Wiener spaces (
[
12
,
15
]
, see also
[
18
]
). Precisely, let
p>0
be a strictly positive, sufficiently smooth
function on
\mathbb{R}
and consider the Sturm-Liouville operator
A_{p}f=-(pf^{\prime})^{\prime}
on a suitable domain in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
so that
A_{p}
is a
positive self-adjoint operator. The spectral theorem asserts the
existence of a projection-valued measure
\Lambda\mapsto\chi_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
. In
[
9
]
we argued that the range of such an orthogonal
projection is a good model for variable bandwidth and defined the
Paley-Wiener space (in engineering language this is a space of bandlimited
functions) as

PW_{[0,\Omega]}\left(A_{p}\right)=\chi_{[0,\Omega]}\left(A_{p}\right)L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\,.

For a general Borel set
\Lambda\subset[0,\infty)
of finite
measure, we write
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})=\chi_{\Lambda}(A_{p})L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

for the corresponding spectral subspace.

Taking
p\equiv 1
and
A_{p}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}
, we see that the
Fourier transform of
A_{p}f
is

\widehat{A_{p}f}(\xi)=\xi^{2}\hat{f}(\xi)
. Thus spectral values

\xi^{2}\in[0,\Omega]
correspond to frequencies
\xi\in[-\sqrt{\Omega},\sqrt{\Omega}]
. Consequently,
PW_{[0,\Omega]}(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\supp\hat{f}\subseteq[-\sqrt{\Omega},\sqrt{\Omega}]\}
and coincides with the classical space of bandlimited functions with
maximum frequency
\sqrt{\Omega}
.
1
1

1

Since we deal with a second order differential
operator, the largest frequency
\sqrt{\Omega}
corresponds to the largest spectral value

\Omega
. Since we use a spectral definition of variable bandwidth,
we prefer the largest eigenvalue as the parameter in the
definition.

In
[
9
]
we proved several general theorems about the general
Paley-Wiener spaces
PW_{[0,\Omega]}\left(A_{p}\right)
to support the
interpretation of variable bandwidth. Among these were a general,
almost optimal sampling theorem for the reconstruction of a function in

PW_{[0,\Omega]}\left(A_{p}\right)
from its samples
\{f(x):x\in X\}
on a discrete set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and a necessary density
condition for sampling. These results indicate that a function in

PW_{[0,\Omega]}\left(A_{p}\right)
behaves like a function with maximum
frequency
(\Omega/p(x))^{1/2}
near
x
. Thus the function
p

defining the Sturm-Liouville operator parametrizes the local
bandwidth.

Despite the success of the theory and an explicit iterative
reconstruction algorithm, an intended numerical implementation faces
some immediate and serious difficulties. Even the most naive numerical
treatment requires some knowledge of the reproducing kernel, that is,
of those functions in
PW_{[0,\Omega]}\left(A_{p}\right)
that determine
the point evaluations
f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\overline{k_{x}(y)}\,dy=\langle f,k_{x}\rangle
. Since
PW_{[0,\Omega]}\left(A_{p}\right)
is a reproducing kernel
Hilbert space
[
9
, Prop. 3.3]
these functions do exist. But what
exactly is this reproducing kernel?

Any deeper investigation of the Paley-Wiener spaces and any attempt
of a numerical implementation of a sampling reconstruction must use
some knowledge about the reproducing kernel. In a sense the situation
is similar to the theory of spaces of analytic functions (Bergman
spaces), where some of the deepest results hinge on delicate properties of
the reproducing kernel (see
[
7
]
for a celebrated example).

Although the spectral projections are
natural objects associated to a Sturm-Liouville operator,
surprisingly little explicit knowledge seems to be available. A
general formula can be derived from spectral theory as follows.
Let
\Phi(\lambda,\cdot)=(\Phi^{+}(\lambda,\cdot),\Phi^{-}(\lambda,\cdot))

be a suitable fundamental system of solutions of
A_{p}f=-(pf^{\prime})^{\prime}=\lambda f

on
\mathbb{R}
that depends continuously on
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. If

\Lambda\subseteq[0,\infty)
has finite Lebesgue measure, then there
exists a
2\times 2
positive matrix-valued Borel measure
\mu
constructed
from
\Phi(\lambda,\cdot)
(see, e.g.,
[
21
, Sec. 14]
for details), such that the
reproducing kernel of
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
is given by

(1.1)

k_{\Lambda}(x,y)=k_{x}(y)=\int_{\Lambda}\overline{\Phi(\lambda,x)}\cdot\Phi(\lambda,y)\,d\mu(\lambda)=\langle\Phi(\cdot,y),\Phi(\cdot,x)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Lambda,\mu)}\,.

The inner product in
L^{2}([0,\Omega],\mu)
is

\displaystyle\langle F,G\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mu)}=\int_{\Lambda}F(\lambda)\cdot\overline{G(\lambda)}\,d\mu(x)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{2}\int_{\Lambda}F_{j}(\lambda)\overline{G_{k}(\lambda)}\,d\mu_{jk}(\lambda)

where the
\mu_{jk}
’s are the component measures of
\mu
. See
[
6
, Sec. XIII.5]

for more details on matrix measures.

In this classical formula the kernel still depends on the knowledge of
a fundamental solution and is far from explicit.

In this paper we study the Sturm-Liouville operator
A_{p}f=-(pf^{\prime})^{\prime}

for a piecewise constant, positive function
p
and make the reproducing kernel
k

as explicit as possible. Let
t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{n}
be the position
of the jumps of
p
and set
t_{0}=-\infty
and
t_{n+1}=+\infty
. Then the step function

(1.2)

p(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}p_{j}\chi_{(t_{j},t_{j+1}]}(x)

is our model to parametrize variable bandwidth. Indeed, this is the
most natural assumption, since it can be shown that for such a
parametrizing function the restriction of
f\in PW_{[0,\Omega]}\left(A_{p}\right)
to an interval

I_{j}=[t_{j},t_{j+1})
is equal to the restriction of a classical
bandlimited function with bandwidth
(\Omega/p_{j})^{1/2}
. This is
plausible because the operator
A_{p}
restricted to
L^{2}(I_{j})
is just

-p_{j}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}
with eigenfunctions
e^{\pm i\lambda x}

corresponding to the eigenvalues
p_{j}\lambda^{2}
. Consequently a
spectral value
p_{j}\lambda^{2}\leq\Omega
corresponds to a
frequency
|\lambda|\leq(\Omega/p_{j})^{1/2}
, cf.
[
9
, Prop. 3.5]
.

For this natural class of parametrizing functions we can describe the
reproducing kernel in a more explicit fashion than in
(
1.1
). Let
\Phi(\lambda,\cdot)=(\Phi^{+}(\lambda,\cdot),\Phi^{-}(\lambda,\cdot))

be a fundamental system of solutions of
A_{p}f=-(pf^{\prime})^{\prime}=\lambda f

that depends analytically on
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
. Then the reproducing kernel of
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
and
p
be a
piecewise constant, positive function as in (
1.2
). Then
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})

is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with a kernel of the form

\displaystyle k_{\Lambda}(x,y)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\chi_{(t_{j},t_{j+1}]}(x)\chi_{(t_{k},t_{k+1}]}(y)\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{\vartheta_{jk}(u)}{\kappa(u)}\,du,\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

All functions
\vartheta_{jk}
and
\kappa
depend only on the
parameters

\{p_{j}:j=1,\dots,n\}
and the jumps
\{t_{j}\}
of
p
. Furthermore,
each function
\vartheta_{jk}
and
\kappa
are almost periodic polynomials
with real coefficients.

For the spectrum
\Lambda=[0,\Omega]
and
n=2
, i.e.,
p
has two jumps and partitions
\mathbb{R}
into three
intervals of constant local bandwidth,
\kappa
can be explicitly
computed to be (set
q_{k}=p_{k}^{-1/2}
,
k=0,1
)

(1.3)

\kappa(u)=\tfrac{1}{16q_{0}^{2}}\left[\left(1+\tfrac{q_{0}}{q_{1}}\right)^{2}\left(1+\tfrac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}\right)^{2}+\left(1-\tfrac{q_{0}}{q_{1}}\right)^{2}\left(1-\tfrac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}\right)^{2}\right]+\tfrac{1}{8q_{0}^{2}}\left(1-\tfrac{q_{0}^{2}}{q_{1}^{2}}\right)\left(1-\tfrac{q_{1}^{2}}{q_{2}^{2}}\right)\,\cos 2q_{1}Tu\,.

The kernel
k
is then obtained by evaluating parameter integrals of
the form

\displaystyle J(s)=\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\frac{e^{isu}}{C+K\cos\zeta u}\,du,\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

This integral seems to be a new type of special function. The kernel
can then be written explicitly by means of this function
J
. This
will be done in Theorem
4.4
.

In the second part of the paper we derive necessary density conditions for sampling and
interpolation in
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
in the style of Landau

[
13
]
for the case of a piecewise constant
parametrizing function
p
. As
in
[
9
, Sec. 6]
, we consider the positive measure

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mu_{p}([a,b])=\int_{a}^{b}\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{p(x)}}\,,

which is determined
by the parametrizing function
p
. We also recall the following notions.
We say
X
is a
set of stable sampling
for
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})

if there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{x\in X}|f(x)|^{2}\leq C_{2}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{ for all }f\in PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})\,.

Furthermore,
X
is a

set of interpolation
for
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
if for every

c\in\ell^{2}(X)
there exists
f\in PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
such that

f(x)=c_{x}
for all
x\in X
. Using a modified form of the upper
and lower Beurling densities via
\mu_{p}
, we derive the following
version of the density theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Density theorem in
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
)
.

Let
p
be a piecewise constant function and
\Omega>0
.

(i)

If
X
is a set of stable sampling for
PW_{[0,\Omega]}(A_{p})
, then

\displaystyle D_{p}^{-}(X)=\liminf_{r\to\infty}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\#(X\cap B_{r}(x))}{\mu_{p}(B_{r}(x))}\geq\frac{\Omega^{1/2}}{\pi}.

(ii)

If
X
is a set of interpolation for
PW_{[0,\Omega]}(A_{p})
, then

\displaystyle D_{p}^{+}(X)=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\#(X\cap B_{r}(x))}{\mu_{p}(B_{r}(x))}\leq\frac{\Omega^{1/2}}{\pi}.

Although the density theorem does not make any reference to
the reproducing kernel, Theorem
1.1
is substantial for
its proof. In the proof of Theorem
1.2
we will apply a
general density theorem for sampling and interpolation in
reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces
[
8
]
. This statement
identifies an averaged trace of the reproducing kernel as the
critical density. Based on the semi-explicit formula of
Theorem
1.1
we will show that the averaged trace equals

\sqrt{\Omega}/\pi
(see Theorem
5.9
).

Theorem
1.2
holds for much more general spectra
\Lambda
. In fact, if
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
has finite Lebesgue measure, then
the necessary condition for sampling is

D_{p}^{-}(X)\geq\tfrac{1}{\pi}\big|\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x^{2}\in\Lambda\}\big|\,,

where
|I|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of a set
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.

Finally, the formulation of the necessary densities is identical to the main
result in
[
9
, Thm. 6.2, 6.3]
. However, the assumptions on
the parametrizing function
p
are radically different. In
[
9
]

we assumed
p
to be smooth, at least
C^{2}
, and then transformed the
Sturm-Liouville operator into an equivalent Schrödinger operator and
applied scattering theory. In the multivariate version in
[
10
]

we even assumed
p\in C^{\infty}
and used the regularity theory of
elliptic partial differential equations to derive information about
the reproducing kernel. For the case of a singular function
p
all
these tools fail dramatically; our main tool is the simple form of the
operator
A_{p}
and the ensuing more explicit knowledge about the
reproducing kernel.

2.
Spectral Theory of Sturm-Liouville Operators

This section, adapted from
[
9
]
, is a brief
review of the relevant spectral theory of singular Sturm-Liouville
differential expressions on
\mathbb{R}
in divergence form. Given a positive
function
p
on
\mathbb{R}
, we define the
differential expression
\tau_{p}
by
\tau_{p}f=-\left(pf^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
p
is a piecewise constant function as defined in (
1.2
),
then
\tau_{p}
with domain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(A_{p})

\displaystyle=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):f,pf^{\prime}\in AC_{loc}(\mathbb{R})\textrm{ and }\tau_{p}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\},

\displaystyle A_{p}f

\displaystyle=\tau_{p}f,f\in\mathcal{D}(A_{p})

defines the self-adjoint operator
A_{p}
on
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.
The spectrum of
A_{p}
is purely absolutely continuous and consists
of the positive semiaxis:
\sigma\left(A_{p}\right)=\sigma_{ac}\left(A_{p}\right)=[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

The assertions concerning self-adjointness are standard, see, e.g.,

[
17
,
21
,
6
]
.
The result on the purely absolutely continuous spectrum can be inferred
form
[
16
]
.

We also have the following spectral representation of
A_{p}
(see

[
9
]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

If
\Phi(\lambda,x)=\big(\phi_{1}(\lambda,x),\phi_{2}(\lambda,x)\big)

is a fundamental system of solutions of
(\tau-\lambda)\phi=0
that
depends continuously on
\lambda
, then there exists a
2\times 2

matrix measure
\rho
, such that the operator

(2.1)

\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R},d\rho);\quad\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}f(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,\overline{\Phi(\lambda,x)}dx

is unitary and diagonalizes
A
, i.e.,

\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}A\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}^{-1}G(\lambda)=\lambda G(\lambda)

for all
G\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},d\rho)
. The inverse has the form

{\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}^{-1}}G(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(\lambda)\cdot{\Phi(\lambda,x)}\,d\rho(\lambda)\,.

for
G\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},d\rho)
.

If
g
is a bounded Borel function on
\mathbb{R}
, then for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

(2.2)

g(A)f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}}g(\lambda)\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}f(\lambda)\cdot{\Phi(\lambda,x)}\,d\rho(\lambda)\,.

All integrals
\int_{\mathbb{R}}
have to be understood as
\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\to-\infty\\
b\to\infty\end{subarray}}\int_{a}^{b}
with convergence in
L^{2}
.

\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}
is called the
spectral transform
(or
also a spectral representation of
A
).

Remark
.

It is always possible to choose a fundamental system of solutions

\Phi(\lambda,\cdot)
that depends continuously (even analytically)
on
\lambda

[
21
]
. The spectral measure
can (and will be) then be constructed explicitly from the knowledge
of such a set of fundamental solutions
(A-z)\phi=0
, see
[
6
,
17
,
20
,
21
]

.

In particular, the
spectral projection

\chi_{\Lambda}(A_{p}):L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

corresponding to a Borel set
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
is given
by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\chi_{\Lambda}(A_{p})f=\int_{\Lambda}\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}f(\lambda)\cdot\Phi(\lambda,\cdot)\,d\mu(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}^{-1}(\chi_{\Lambda}\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}f)

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. With this projection operator we define
the main object for our approach to variable bandwidth.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
A_{p}
as defined in Proposition
2.1

and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
be of finite measure. The Paley-Wiener
space, denoted
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
,
is the range of
\chi_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
, i.e.,

PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})=\chi_{\Lambda}(A_{p})(L^{2}(\mathbb{R})).

Equivalently, a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
belongs to
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})

if
f=\chi_{\Lambda}(A_{p})f
.

Some elementary properties of functions of variable bandwidth can
be found in
[
9
, Sec. 3]
. As argued in
[
9
]
, this
definition is one possibility to give meaning to the notion of
variable bandwidth.

3.
Paley Wiener Space with Piecewise Constant Parametrization

We now present the fundamental results on
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})

for
p
piecewise constant as in (
1.2
).We will derive a formula for the spectral measure and discuss a strategy how to compute the reproducing kernel.

3.1.
Fundamental solutions of
(\tau_{p}-\lambda)f=0

In order to derive the spectral representation
\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}
,
we choose a fundamental system
\Phi(z,\cdot)=(\Phi^{+}(z,\cdot),\Phi^{-}(z,\cdot))

of
(\tau_{p}-z)f=0
of (classical) solutions that depends analytically on
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}

and such that one solution lies right and the other lies left in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
(a function
f

lies right
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

if
f\in L^{2}(c,\infty)
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Similarly,
f

lies
left
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
if
f\in L^{2}(-\infty,c)
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
). These additional integrability conditions are in preparation
for the derivation of the spectral measure.

The strategy is as follows. Since
p
is constant

p(x)=p_{k}
on the interval
I_{k}=[t_{k},t_{k+1})
, the eigenvalue equation

A_{p}f=zf
becomes
(-p_{k}D^{2}-z)f=0
on
I_{k}
, and therefore
every eigenfunction of
A_{p}
restricted to
I_{k}
possesses an
elementary solution by exponentials. To obtain an eigenfunction for

A_{p}
on all of
\mathbb{R}
, we have to glue together these local
solutions, which is a construction similar to that of
splines. In the following we use once and for all the notation

(3.1)

q_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{k}}}\,,

as this is the precise local frequency of the eigenfunctions.
We also use the principal square root of
z
defined as follows: if
z=re^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
with
r>0
and
-\pi<\theta<\pi,
then
\sqrt{z}=\sqrt{r}e^{i\theta/2}
. In particular,
\textrm{Im}z
and
\textrm{Im}\sqrt{z}
have the same sign.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p
be a piecewise constant function. Then there
exist analytic functions
a_{l}^{+},b_{l}^{+},a_{j}^{-},b_{j}^{-}

from
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
to
\mathbb{C}
such that,
for every
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
, the functions

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Phi^{+}(z,x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a_{l}^{+}(z)e^{iq_{l}\sqrt{z}x}+b_{l}^{+}(z)e^{-iq_{l}\sqrt{z}x},&x\in I_{l},0\leq l\leq n-1,\\
e^{iq_{n}\sqrt{z}x},&x\in I_{n},\end{cases}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Phi^{-}(z,x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e^{-iq_{0}\sqrt{z}x},&x\in I_{0},\\
a_{j}^{-}(z)e^{iq_{j}\sqrt{z}x}+b_{j}^{-}(z)e^{-iq_{j}\sqrt{z}x},&x\in I_{j},1\leq j\leq n\end{cases}

are solutions of
(\tau_{p}-z)f=0
that are analytic in
z
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
.

If
\textrm{Im}z>0
, then
\Phi(z,\cdot)=\big(\Phi^{+}(z,\cdot),\phi^{-}(z,\cdot)\big)
is a fundamental system
of
(\tau_{p}-z)f=0
such that
\Phi^{+}(z,\cdot)
lies right and

\Phi^{-}(z,\cdot)
lies left in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and

Similarly, if
\textrm{Im}z<0
, then
\overline{\Phi(\overline{z},\cdot)}

is a fundamental system of
(\tau_{p}-z)f=0
such that
\overline{\Phi^{+}(\overline{z},\cdot)}

lies right and
\overline{\Phi^{-}(\overline{z},\cdot)}
lies left
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Setting

(3.4)

L_{k}(z)=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}\left(1+\frac{q_{k}}{q_{k-1}}\right)e^{it_{k}(q_{k-1}-q_{k})\sqrt{z}}&\left(1-\frac{q_{k}}{q_{k-1}}\right)e^{-it_{k}(q_{k-1}+q_{k})\sqrt{z}}\\
\left(1-\frac{q_{k}}{q_{k-1}}\right)e^{it_{k}(q_{k-1}+q_{k})\sqrt{z}}&\left(1+\frac{q_{k}}{q_{k-1}}\right)e^{-it_{k}(q_{k-1}-q_{k})\sqrt{z}}\end{bmatrix}\,,

the coefficients
a^{\pm},b^{\pm}
satisfy the recursion relations

\begin{bmatrix}a_{k}^{\pm}(z)\\
b_{k}^{\pm}(z)\end{bmatrix}=L_{k}(z)\begin{bmatrix}a_{k-1}^{\pm}(z)\\
b_{k-1}^{\pm}(z)\end{bmatrix}\,.

As a consequence the coefficients
a^{\pm},b^{\pm}
are almost
periodic trigonometric polynomials of the variable
\sqrt{z}
.

Proof.

Fix
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
. On the interval
I_{k}=[t_{k},t_{k+1})
the parametrizing function
p
is constants and we need to
solve

(A_{p}-z)f(x)=(-p_{k}\frac{d^{2}f}{dx^{2}}-zf)(x)=0\qquad\for x\in I_{k}\,.

It is clear that these local solutions
\varphi_{k}(z,\cdot)

take the form

\displaystyle\varphi_{k}(z,x)=a_{k}(z)e^{iq_{k}\sqrt{z}x}+b_{k}(z)e^{-iq_{k}\sqrt{z}x},\quad x\in I_{k}

for some choice of constants
a_{k}(z),b_{k}(z)\in\mathbb{C},0\leq k\leq n.

We need to choose these constants in such a way that the function

\varphi(z,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\varphi_{k}(z,x)\chi_{I_{k}}(x)

satisfies

\varphi(z,\cdot),p\varphi^{\prime}(z,\cdot)\in AC_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
. These
conditions
imply that for each
k
,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\varphi_{k-1}(z,t_{k})\\
\frac{1}{q_{k-1}^{2}}\varphi_{k-1}^{\prime}(z,t_{k})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\varphi(z,t_{k})\\
p(t_{k})\varphi^{\prime}(z,t_{k})\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\downarrow t_{k}}\begin{bmatrix}\varphi(z,x)\\
p(x)\varphi^{\prime}(z,x)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\varphi_{k}(z,t_{k})\\
\frac{1}{q_{k}^{2}}\varphi_{k}^{\prime}(z,t_{k})\end{bmatrix}\,.

Upon substituting the local solutions, this yields

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}e^{iq_{k-1}\sqrt{z}t_{k}}&e^{iq_{k-1}\sqrt{z}t_{k}}\\
i\frac{\sqrt{z}}{q_{k-1}}e^{iq_{k-1}\sqrt{z}t_{k}}&-i\frac{\sqrt{z}}{q_{k-1}}e^{iq_{k-1}\sqrt{z}t_{k}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a_{k-1}(z)\\
b_{k-1}(z)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}e^{iq_{k}\sqrt{z}t_{k}}&e^{iq_{k}\sqrt{z}t_{k}}\\
i\frac{\sqrt{z}}{q_{k}}e^{iq_{k}\sqrt{z}t_{k}}&-i\frac{\sqrt{z}}{q_{k}}e^{iq_{k}\sqrt{z}t_{k}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a_{k}(z)\\
b_{k}(z)\end{bmatrix}.

With the notation for the matrices
L_{k}(z)
from (
3.4
),

\varphi(z,\cdot)
is a solution of

(\tau_{p}-z)f=0
if and only if the relations

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}a_{k}(z)\\
b_{k}(z)\end{bmatrix}=L_{k}(z)\begin{bmatrix}a_{k-1}(z)\\
b_{k-1}(z)\end{bmatrix}

hold for all
k
. In particular, taking
a_{0}^{-}(z)=0
,
b_{0}^{-}(z)=1

and recursively generating the remaining coefficients via

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}a_{j}^{-}(z)\\
b_{j}^{-}(z)\end{bmatrix}=L_{j}(z)\begin{bmatrix}a_{j-1}^{-}(z)\\
b_{j-1}^{-}(z)\end{bmatrix},\quad 1\leq j\leq n,

we generate the solution
\Phi^{-}(z,\cdot)
of
(\tau_{p}-z)f=0,z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]

as claimed in (
3.3
). Analogously, we generate
\Phi^{+}
in (
3.2
)
using

(3.6)

\displaystyle\qquad R_{k}(z)=L_{k}^{-1}(z)=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}\left(1+\frac{q_{k-1}}{q_{k}}\right)e^{-it_{k}(q_{k-1}-q_{k})\sqrt{z}}&\left(1-\frac{q_{k-1}}{q_{k}}\right)e^{-it_{k}(q_{k-1}+q_{k})\sqrt{z}}\\
\left(1-\frac{q_{k-1}}{q_{k}}\right)e^{it_{k}(q_{k-1}+q_{k})\sqrt{z}}&\left(1+\frac{q_{k-1}}{q_{k}}\right)e^{it_{k}(q_{k-1}-q_{k})\sqrt{z}}\end{bmatrix},

the initial values

(3.7)

a_{n}^{+}(z)=1,b_{n}^{+}(z)=0

and the recursions

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}a_{l}^{+}(z)\\
b_{l}^{+}(z)\end{bmatrix}=R_{l+1}(z)\begin{bmatrix}a_{l+1}^{+}(z)\\
b_{l+1}^{+}(z)\end{bmatrix},\quad 0\leq l\leq n-1\,.

The analyticity of
a_{k}^{\pm}
,
b_{k}^{\pm},
and consequently
of
\Phi^{+}
and
\Phi^{-}
in
z
follows from the analyticity
of
z\mapsto e^{\pm iq_{k}\sqrt{z}x}
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Finally, if
\textrm{Im}z>0
then
\textrm{Im}\sqrt{z}>0
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle|e^{\pm iq\sqrt{z}x}|=e^{\mp q\textrm{Im}\sqrt{z}x},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},q>0,z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0].

Thus,
\Phi^{-}(z,x)=e^{-iq_{0}\sqrt{z}x}\in L^{2}(I_{0})
and

\Phi^{+}(z,x)=e^{iq_{n}\sqrt{z}x}\in L^{2}(I_{n})
. This means that
\Phi^{-}(z,\cdot)
lies left and
\Phi^{+}(z,\cdot)
lies
right in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The case
\textrm{Im}z<0
for
\overline{\Phi^{+}(\overline{z},\cdot)}

and
\overline{\Phi^{-}(\overline{z},\cdot)}
is proved analogously.

We call the analytic functions
a_{k}^{\pm}
and
b_{k}^{\pm}
the

connection coefficients
of
\Phi^{+}
and
\Phi^{-}
. From
the iterative computations in (
3.5
) and (
3.8
) we see
that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}a_{l}^{+}(z)\\
b_{l}^{+}(z)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=R_{l+1}(z)R_{l+2}(z)\cdots R_{n}(z)\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\end{bmatrix},\quad 0\leq l\leq n-1

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}a_{j}^{-}(z)\\
b_{j}^{-}(z)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=L_{j}(z)L_{j-1}(z)\cdots L_{1}(z)\begin{bmatrix}0\\
1\end{bmatrix},\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

These coefficients are used to continuously glue together the local
solutions of
(\tau_{p}-z)f=0
on each
I_{k}
to form two global
solutions
\Phi^{+}(z,\cdot)
,
\Phi^{-}(z,\cdot)
that lie right
and lie left in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, respectively. Moreover, the above formulas
show that the connection coefficients are almost periodic polynomials
in
\sqrt{z}
with real coefficients.

Remark 3.2
.

Hidden in the recursion relations (
3.9
) is a group
structure. To see this, define for
1\leq k\leq n
the quantities

\gamma_{k}=(\tfrac{q_{k-1}}{q_{k}})^{1/2},\quad\eta_{k}=t_{k}(q_{k-1}-q_{k})\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\theta_{k}=t_{k}(q_{k-1}+q_{k}).

We can then express
L_{k}(z)
as

\displaystyle L_{k}(z)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{k}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{k}^{-1}}{2}e^{i\eta_{k}\sqrt{z}}&\frac{\gamma_{k}-\gamma_{k}^{-1}}{2}e^{-i\theta_{k}\sqrt{z}}\\
\frac{\gamma_{k}-\gamma_{k}^{-1}}{2}e^{i\theta_{k}\sqrt{z}}&\frac{\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{k}^{-1}}{2}e^{-i\eta_{k}\sqrt{z}}\end{bmatrix},\quad\det L_{k}(z)=\gamma_{k}^{-2}

and (
3.6
) as

\displaystyle R_{k}(z)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{k}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{k}^{-1}}{2}e^{-i\eta_{k}\sqrt{z}}&-\frac{\gamma_{k}-\gamma_{k}^{-1}}{2}e^{-i\theta_{k}\sqrt{z}}\\
-\frac{\gamma_{k}-\gamma_{k}^{-1}}{2}e^{i\theta_{k}\sqrt{z}}&\frac{\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{k}^{-1}}{2}e^{i\eta_{k}\sqrt{z}}\end{bmatrix},\quad\det R_{k}(z)=\gamma_{k}^{2}.

For
z\in(0,\infty)
, the matrices
\gamma_{k}L_{k}(z)
and
\gamma_{k}^{-1}R_{k}(z)

are elements of the Lie group

\displaystyle SU(1,1)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}\overline{a}&b\\
\overline{b}&a\end{bmatrix}:a,b\in\mathbb{C}\textrm{ and }|a|^{2}-|b|^{2}=1\right\}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}L_{j}(z)\cdot\ldots\cdot\gamma_{1}L_{1}(z)=\left(\prod_{k=1}^{j}\gamma_{k}\right)\begin{bmatrix}\overline{b_{j}^{-}(z)}&a_{j}^{-}(z)\\
\overline{a_{j}^{-}(z)}&b_{j}^{-}(z)\end{bmatrix}

and therefore

(3.11)

\displaystyle L_{j}(z)\cdot\ldots\cdot L_{1}(z)=\begin{bmatrix}\overline{b_{j}^{-}(z)}&a_{j}^{-}(z)\\
\overline{a_{j}^{-}(z)}&b_{j}^{-}(z)\end{bmatrix},\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

By the same token, it can be shown that

(3.12)

\displaystyle R_{l+1}(z)\cdot\ldots\cdot R_{n}(z)=\begin{bmatrix}a_{l}^{+}(z)&\overline{b_{l}^{+}(z)}\\
b_{l}^{+}(z)&\overline{a_{l}^{+}(z)}\end{bmatrix},\quad 0\leq l\leq n-1.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Phi=(\Phi^{+},\Phi^{-})
be defined as in Theorem
3.1
.
Then
\Phi
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By definition of
\Phi
, we see that for
0\leq k\leq n
and
(\lambda,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times I_{k}
,

\displaystyle|\Phi^{\pm}(\lambda,x)|\leq|a_{k}^{\pm}(\lambda)|+|b_{k}^{\pm}(\lambda)|=\left\|\begin{bmatrix}a_{k}^{+}(\lambda)&b_{k}^{+}(\lambda)\end{bmatrix}^{T}\right\|_{1}.

Let the matrix norm subordinate to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{1}
be denoted
by the same symbol. From (
3.9
) we have that for
(\lambda,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle|\Phi^{+}(\lambda,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{0\leq k\leq n}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}a_{k}^{+}(\lambda)&b_{k}^{+}(\lambda)\end{bmatrix}^{T}\right\|_{1}\leq\max\left\{1,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\left\|R_{k}R_{k-1}\cdots R_{n}\right\|_{1}\right\}\,.

From (
3.6
), we obtain for
1\leq k\leq n
the estimates

\displaystyle\left\|R_{k}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\left|1+\tfrac{q_{k-1}}{q_{k}}\right|+\left|1-\tfrac{q_{k-1}}{q_{k}}\right|\right)\leq 1+\tfrac{q_{k-1}}{q_{k}}

The assertion now follows from the submultiplicativity of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}

. The estimate for
\Phi^{-}(\lambda,x)
follows the same lines.

Next, we derive several fundamental identities
for the connection coefficients. These expressions will follow from
properties of a (modified) Wronskian determinant.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{k}^{\pm},b_{k}^{\pm},0\leq k\leq n
,
be the connection coefficients of
\Phi=(\Phi^{+},\Phi^{-})
. Then
the following identities hold.

(i) If
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
, then, for all
1\leq k\leq n-1

(3.13)

\displaystyle\frac{a_{0}^{+}(z)}{q_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{k}}\left(a_{k}^{+}(z)b_{k}^{-}(z)-a_{k}^{-}(z)b_{k}^{+}(z)\right)=\frac{b_{n}^{-}(z)}{q_{n}}

(ii) If
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, then for all
0\leq k\leq n
,

(3.14)

\displaystyle q_{0}(|b_{k}^{-}(\lambda)|^{2}-|a_{k}^{-}(\lambda)|^{2})

\displaystyle=q_{n}(|a_{k}^{+}(\lambda)|^{2}-|b_{k}^{+}(\lambda)|^{2})=q_{k}\,.

Consequently,

(3.15)

\frac{|a_{k}^{+}(\lambda)|^{2}}{q_{0}}+\frac{|a_{k}^{-}(\lambda)|^{2}}{q_{n}}=\frac{|b_{k}^{+}(\lambda)|^{2}}{q_{0}}+\frac{|b_{k}^{-}(\lambda)|^{2}}{q_{n}}.

(iii) If
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, then for all
1\leq k\leq n-1

(3.16)

\displaystyle\frac{b_{0}^{+}(\lambda)}{q_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{k}}\left(b_{k}^{+}(\lambda)\overline{b_{k}^{-}(\lambda)}-a_{k}^{+}(\lambda)\overline{a_{k}^{-}(\lambda)}\right)=-\frac{\overline{a_{n}^{-}(\lambda)}}{q_{n}}\,.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle W_{p}(u,v)=\begin{vmatrix}u&v\\
pu^{\prime}&pv^{\prime}\end{vmatrix}=u(pv^{\prime})-v(pu^{\prime})

be the modified Wronskian determinant of a pair of solutions
u,v

of
(A_{p}-z)f=0
and recall the important fact that the Wronskian of solutions
of the differential equation is a constant and independent of the
variable
x
(see, e.g.,
[
17
,
21
]
).
We apply this fact to the components of
\Phi^{+}
and
\Phi^{-}
on each interval
I_{k}
.

(i) Let
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
. Then for
x\in I_{k},0\leq k\leq n

and

\displaystyle\Phi^{\pm}(z,x)=a_{k}^{\pm}(z)e^{iq_{k}\sqrt{z}x}+b_{k}^{\pm}(z)e^{-iq_{k}\sqrt{z}x},

we compute directly that

(3.17)

\displaystyle W_{p}\left(\Phi^{+}(z,x),\Phi^{-}(z,x)\right)=

\displaystyle=a_{k}^{+}(z)b_{k}^{-}(z)W\left(e^{iq_{k}\sqrt{z}x},e^{-iq_{k}\sqrt{z}x}\right)+b_{k}^{+}(z)a_{k}^{-}(z)W\left(e^{-iq_{k}\sqrt{z}x},e^{iq_{k}\sqrt{z}x}\right)

(3.18)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{2i\sqrt{z}}{q_{k}}\left(a_{k}^{+}(z)b_{k}^{-}(z)-a_{k}^{-}(z)b_{k}^{+}(z)\right).

On the unbounded intervals
I_{0}
and
I_{n}
, (
3.18
)
reduces to

(3.19)

\displaystyle W_{p}\left(\Phi^{+}(z,x),\Phi^{-}(z,x)\right)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{2i\sqrt{z}}{q_{0}}a_{0}^{+}(z)\quad(k=0:a_{0}^{-}(z)=0,b_{0}^{-}(z)=1)

(3.20)

\displaystyle W_{p}\left(\Phi^{+}(z,x),\Phi^{-}(z,x)\right)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{2i\sqrt{z}}{q_{n}}b_{n}^{-}(z)\quad(k=n:a_{n}^{+}(z)=1,b_{n}^{+}(z)=0).

The equality of all three expressions yields (
3.13
) for all

z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0].

(ii) Given
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, we can infer from
p
being real-valued
that
(\Phi^{-}(\lambda,\cdot),\overline{\Phi^{-}(\lambda,\cdot)})

and
(\Phi^{+}(\lambda,\cdot),\overline{\Phi^{+}(\lambda,\cdot)})

are also pairs of solutions of
(\tau_{p}-\lambda)f=0
. Identities
(
3.14
) are derived analogously from the Wronskian of the respective
pairs.

(iii) Given
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, identity (
3.16
) follows
from computing the Wronskian of the pair
(\Phi^{+}(\lambda,\cdot),\overline{\Phi^{-}(\lambda,\cdot)})

of solutions of
(\tau_{p}-\lambda)f=0
on each interval
I_{k}
.

Remark
.

An alternative proof of (ii) is possible by using the underlying group
structure explained in Remark
3.2
.
Let
z=\lambda\in(0,\infty)
. If
k=0
, then
a_{0}^{-}(\lambda)=0
,

b_{0}^{-}(\lambda)=1
and (
3.14
) trivially holds. Now, suppose

1\leq k\leq n
. By taking the determinant of both sides of (
3.11
)
and with
j=k
, we get

\displaystyle|b_{k}^{-}(\lambda)|^{2}-|a_{k}^{-}(\lambda)|^{2}=\gamma_{k}^{-2}\cdot\ldots\cdot\gamma_{1}^{-2}=\tfrac{q_{k}}{q_{0}},

proving (
3.14
). The other identity is proved similarly.

An immediate consequence of identities (
3.13
) and (
3.15
)
is the following.

Corollary 3.5
.

With the notation of Lemma
3.4
and for

\lambda\in(0,\infty)
we have

\frac{|b_{0}^{+}(\lambda)|^{2}}{q_{0}^{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{0}q_{n}}=\frac{|a_{0}^{+}(\lambda)|^{2}}{q_{0}^{2}}=\frac{|b_{n}^{-}(\lambda)|^{2}}{q_{n}^{2}}=\frac{1}{q_{0}q_{n}}+\frac{|a_{n}^{-}(\lambda)|^{2}}{q_{n}^{2}}.

3.2.
The spectral measure

We are now ready to discuss some of the spectral properties of
A_{p}

and derive a formula for the spectral measure
\mu
of
A_{p}
.
We will need the following expressions. An application of
[
20
, Thm. 7.8]

to the fundamental system
\Phi
shows that for
z\in\rho(A_{p})=\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(A_{p}),

the resolvent
R_{z}(A_{p})=(A_{p}-z)^{-1}
can be expressed as integral
operator

\displaystyle R_{z}(A_{p})g(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}r_{z}(x,y)g(y)\,dy,\quad g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

with the

resolvent kernel
r_{z}(x,y)
defined as

(3.21)

\displaystyle r_{z}(x,y)=\dfrac{1}{W_{p}(\Phi^{+}(z,\cdot),\Phi^{-}(z,\cdot))}\begin{cases}\Phi^{+}(z,x)\Phi^{-}(z,y),&y\leq x,\\
\Phi^{-}(z,x)\Phi^{+}(z,y),&y>x\,.\end{cases}

Following Weidmann
[
21
, Sec. 14]
we can find
2\times 2
matrices
m^{\pm}
with entries analytic
in
\rho(A_{p})
such that

(3.22)

\displaystyle r_{z}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\overline{\Phi(\overline{z},x)}\cdot m^{+}(z)\Phi(z,y),&y\leq x\\
\overline{\Phi(\overline{z},x)}\cdot m^{-}(z)\Phi(z,y),&y>x.\end{cases}

For any bounded interval
(\gamma,\lambda]
the spectral measure

\mu
is given by the Weyl-Titchmarsh Formula (cf.
[
6
, Thm. XIII.5.18]
,

[
20
, Thm. 9.4]
,
[
21
, Thm. 14.5]
):

(3.23)

\displaystyle\mu((\gamma,\lambda])=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\gamma+\delta}^{\lambda+\delta}\left(m^{+}(t+i\epsilon)-m^{+}(t-i\epsilon)\right)\,dt.

As
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
on
\mathbb{R}
this equation can also be written as

d\mu\left(\lambda\right)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\left(m^{+}(\lambda+i\epsilon)-m^{+}(\lambda-i\epsilon)\right)\,.

The following theorem describes the spectral measure of
A_{p}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
p
a step function with

n
jumps. Let
\Phi=(\Phi^{+},\Phi^{-})
be the fundamental system
as constructed in Theorem

3.1
with connection coefficients
a_{k}^{\pm},b_{k}^{\pm}
,

0\leq k\leq n
. Define

(3.24)

\kappa(\sqrt{\lambda})=\frac{|a_{0}^{+}(\lambda)|^{2}}{q_{0}^{2}}=\frac{|b_{n}^{-}(\lambda)|^{2}}{q_{n}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{q_{0}q_{n}}\,.

Then the spectral measure of
A_{p}
is a
2\times 2

positive matrix measure
\mu
given by

(3.25)

\displaystyle d\mu(\lambda)=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\kappa(\sqrt{\lambda})}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{q_{0}}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{q_{n}}\end{bmatrix}\dfrac{d\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\,.

The spectral transform

(3.26)

\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow L^{2}([0,\infty),d\mu),\quad\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}f(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\Phi(\lambda,x)}\,dx

yields a spectral representation of
A_{p}
. The inverse
\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}^{-1}

takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}^{-1}G(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}G(\lambda)\cdot\Phi(\lambda,x)\,d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\frac{1}{q_{0}}G_{1}(\lambda)\Phi^{+}(\lambda,x)+\frac{1}{q_{n}}G_{2}(\lambda)\Phi^{-}(\lambda,x)}{\kappa(\sqrt{\lambda})}\,\dfrac{d\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda}}

for all
G=(G_{1},G_{2})\in L^{2}([0,\infty),d\mu)
.

Proof.

The result is a direct consequence of Proposition
2.2
.
Since
\Phi
is given explicitly in Theorem
3.1
, we can proceed one step
further to compute
\mu
.

We follow the lines of
[
21
]
. Assume that
\textrm{Im}z>0
.
By Theorem
3.1
,
\Phi(z,\cdot)
is a fundamental system
of
(\tau_{p}-z)f=0
, so that
\Phi^{+}(z,\cdot)
lies right and

\Phi^{-}(z,\cdot)
lies left in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, respectively. By
(
3.19
) and (
3.21
),

(3.27)

\displaystyle r_{z}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{iq_{0}}{2a_{0}^{+}(z)\sqrt{z}}\begin{cases}\Phi^{+}(z,x)\Phi^{-}(z,y),&y\leq x,\\
\Phi^{-}(z,x)\Phi^{+}(z,y),&y>x.\end{cases}

On the other hand, since
\textrm{Im}\overline{z}<0
,
\overline{\Phi(z,\cdot)}

is a fundamental system of
(\tau_{p}-\overline{z})f=0
where
\overline{\Phi^{+}(z,\cdot)}

lies right and
\overline{\Phi^{-}(z,\cdot)}
lies left in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
respectively. Therefore

(3.28)

\displaystyle r_{\overline{z}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{-iq_{n}}{2\overline{b_{n}^{-}(z)\sqrt{z}}}\begin{cases}\overline{\Phi^{+}(z,x)}\overline{\Phi^{-}(z,y)},&y\leq x\\
\overline{\Phi^{-}(z,x)}\overline{\Phi^{+}(z,y)},&y>x.\end{cases}

To apply the Weyl-Titchmarsh formula we need to compute the
2\times 2

matrices
m^{\pm}
in (
3.22
). This amounts to expressing

\Phi^{\pm}(z,x)
by
\overline{\Phi(\overline{z},x)}
.

Since
m^{\pm}(z)
in (
3.22
) does not depend on
x,y
, we may
choose
x,y
in the unbounded
intervals. This choice makes the calculations
simpler.

For
x\in I_{n}
we get

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi^{+}(\overline{z},x)}

\displaystyle=e^{-iq_{n}\sqrt{z}x},\quad\overline{\Phi^{-}(\overline{z},x)}=\overline{a_{n}^{-}(\overline{z})}e^{-iq_{n}\sqrt{\overline{z}}x}+\overline{b_{n}^{-}(\overline{z})}e^{iq_{n}\sqrt{\overline{z}}x}

and consequently,

\displaystyle\Phi^{+}(z,x)

\displaystyle=e^{iq_{n}\sqrt{z}x}

(3.29)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\overline{b_{n}^{-}(\overline{z})}}\overline{\Phi^{-}(\overline{z},x)}-\dfrac{\overline{a_{n}^{-}(\overline{z})}}{\overline{b_{n}^{-}(\overline{z})}}\overline{\Phi^{+}(\overline{z},x)},\quad x\in I_{n}.

Assuming
y\leq x
and substituting (
3.29
) to (
3.27
) for Im
z>0
yields

(3.30)

\displaystyle r_{z}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{iq_{0}}{2a_{0}^{+}(z)\overline{b_{n}^{-}(\overline{z})}\sqrt{z}}\left\{\overline{\Phi^{-}(\overline{z},x)}-\overline{a_{n}^{-}(\overline{z})}\ \overline{\Phi^{+}(\overline{z},x)}\right\}\Phi^{-}(z,y)\,;

If we compare this to (
3.22
) we obtain

m^{+}(z)=\dfrac{iq_{0}}{2a_{0}^{+}(z)\overline{b_{n}^{-}(\overline{z})}\sqrt{z}}\begin{bmatrix}0&-\overline{a_{n}^{-}(\overline{z})}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\,.

The analogous calculation for
x,y\in I_{0},y\leq x,

yields

m^{+}(\overline{z})=\dfrac{-iq_{n}}{2a_{0}^{+}(\overline{z})\overline{b_{n}^{-}(z)}\sqrt{\overline{z}}}\begin{bmatrix}1&-b_{0}^{+}(\overline{z})\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}.

Hence, for
\textrm{Im}z>0
we have

(3.31)

m^{+}(z)-m^{+}(\overline{z})=\begin{bmatrix}\dfrac{iq_{n}}{2a_{0}^{+}(\overline{z})\overline{b_{n}^{-}(z)}\sqrt{\overline{z}}}&\dfrac{-iq_{0}\overline{a_{n}^{-}(\overline{z})}}{2a_{0}^{+}(z)\overline{b_{n}^{-}(\overline{z})}\sqrt{z}}-\dfrac{iq_{n}b_{0}^{+}(\overline{z})}{2a_{0}^{+}(\overline{z})\overline{b_{n}^{-}(z)}\sqrt{\overline{z}}}\\
0&\dfrac{iq_{0}}{2a_{0}^{+}(z)\overline{b_{n}^{-}(\overline{z})}\sqrt{z}}\end{bmatrix}.

As
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
on
[0,\infty)
, by (
3.23
) the matrix
\mathcal{M}
of
densities of
\mu
has entries

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{jl}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\left(m_{jk}^{+}(\lambda+i\epsilon)-m_{jk}^{+}(\lambda-i\epsilon)\right).

Applying (
3.13
), (
3.16
) and Corollary
3.5

to (
3.31
) with
z=\lambda+i\epsilon
,
\epsilon\downarrow 0

gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{11}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{q_{n}}{4\pi\sqrt{\lambda}a_{0}^{+}(\lambda)\overline{b_{n}^{-}(\lambda)}}=\dfrac{q_{0}}{4\pi\sqrt{\lambda}|a_{0}^{+}(\lambda)|^{2}}=\frac{1}{4\pi q_{0}\kappa\left(\sqrt{\lambda}\right)\sqrt{\lambda}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{22}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{0}}{q_{n}}\mathcal{M}_{11}(\lambda),

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{12}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{q_{0}q_{n}\left(\tfrac{1}{q_{n}}\overline{a_{n}^{-}(\lambda)}+\tfrac{1}{q_{0}}b_{0}^{+}(\lambda)\right)}{4\pi a_{0}^{+}(\lambda)\overline{b_{n}^{-}(\lambda)}\sqrt{\lambda}}=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{21}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=0.

By definition of
\kappa
and by Corollary
3.5
,
\kappa(\sqrt{\lambda})\geq\tfrac{1}{q_{0}q_{n}}>0

for all

and

0&\frac{1}{q_{n}}\end{bmatrix}\dfrac{d\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.

The formulas for the spectral transform and its inverse are stated in Theorem

.

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi\kappa(u)}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{q_{0}}&0\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}^{-1}G(x)

, and constants

3.3.
The reproducing kernel of
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})

Next we investigate the reproducing kernel of the Paley-Wiener space

PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
. With the spectral measure of
A_{p}
in place, the general
properties of
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
follow exactly as for the classical Paley-Wiener
space.
Recall that by definition,
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})=\chi_{\Lambda}(A_{p})(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
be a Borel set
of finite measure and
p
a piecewise constant function. Define
\Lambda^{1/2}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}:\lambda^{2}\in\Lambda\}

and

(3.33)

\vartheta(u,x,y)=\frac{1}{q_{0}}\overline{\Phi^{+}(u^{2},x)}\Phi^{+}(u^{2},y)+\frac{1}{q_{n}}\overline{\Phi^{-}(u^{2},x)}\Phi^{-}(u^{2},y).

Then
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space
with kernel

(3.34)

\displaystyle k_{\Lambda}(x,y)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{\vartheta(u,x,y)}{\kappa(u)}\,du,\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

We first claim that
\{\|\Phi(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda,d\mu)}\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}

is bounded. Indeed, Theorem
3.6
implies that for each

x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda,d\mu)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\frac{\frac{1}{q_{0}}|\Phi^{+}(u^{2},x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{q_{n}}|\Phi^{-}(u^{2},x)|^{2}}{\kappa(u)}\,du

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\essupp_{u\in\Lambda^{1/2}}|\Phi^{\pm}(u^{2},x)|^{2}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\frac{q_{0}+q_{n}}{q_{0}q_{n}\kappa(u)}\,du.

By (
3.25
)
\left(q_{0}q_{n}\kappa\left(\sqrt{\lambda}\right)\right)^{-1}\leq 1
,
and Lemma
3.3
asserts the uniform boundedness of
\Phi^{\pm}

, so

\displaystyle\|\Phi(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda,d\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\essupp_{u\in\Lambda^{1/2}}|\Phi^{\pm}(u^{2},x)|\left(\frac{q_{0}+q_{n}}{2\pi}|\Lambda^{1/2}|\right)^{1/2}=C<\infty.

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Then by the claim and by the unitarity of

\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Lambda}\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}f(\lambda)\cdot\Phi(\lambda,x)\,d\mu(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda,d\mu)}\|\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}f\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda,d\mu)}

\displaystyle=|\Phi(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda,d\mu)}\|f\|_{2}\leq C\|f\|_{2}\,,

and the integral makes sense for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
and for all
f\in PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle f(x)=\chi_{\Lambda}(A_{p})f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Lambda}\mathcal{F}_{A_{p}}f(\lambda)\cdot\Phi(\lambda,x)\,d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\overline{\Phi(\lambda,y)}\cdot\Phi(\lambda,x)\,dy\,d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\int_{\Lambda}\overline{\Phi(\lambda,y)}\cdot\Phi(\lambda,x)\,d\mu(\lambda)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\overline{k_{\Lambda}(x,y)}\,dy.

Consequently the
evaluation map
f\mapsto f(x)
,
f\in PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
is bounded,
in other words,
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space.
Formula (
3.34
) now follows by substituting the expressions
in Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.6
to (
1.1
).
See also
[
6
, Thm. XIII.5.24]
).

3.4.
Computation of the reproducing kernel

In view of eventual numerical implementations, it may be helpful to
make the structure of
k_{\Lambda}
even more explicit. By
Theorem
3.8

k_{\Lambda}
depends the fundamental
solutions
\Phi^{\pm}
.

Fix
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. Since the connection
coefficients
a^{\pm}(u^{2})
and
b^{\pm}(u^{2})
are almost periodic
polynomials in
u
, there exist a positive integer
m(x,y)
and
real numbers
\alpha_{k}(x,y),\beta_{k}(x,y),

1\leq k\leq m(x,y)

such that

(3.35)

\displaystyle\vartheta(u,x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{m(x,y)}\alpha_{k}(x,y)e^{i\beta_{k}(x,y)u}.

Note that for
0\leq j,l\leq n
fixed and
x\in I_{j},y\in I_{l}
, the coefficients
\alpha_{k}(x,y)
depend only on
j

and
l
, and are thus constant on
I_{j}\times I_{l}
. We can
therefore write

\vartheta(u,x,y)=\sum_{j,l=1}^{n}\vartheta_{jl}(u)\chi_{I_{j}}(x)\chi_{I_{l}}(y)

with almost periodic functions
\vartheta_{jl}
. This is the
formulation of Theorem
1.1
of the introduction.

Furthermore, by substituting (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) in the definition of
\vartheta
, we see that the exponents
\beta_{k}(x,y)
,
x\in I_{j}
,

y\in I_{l}
are of the form

(3.36)

\displaystyle\beta_{k}(x,y)=c_{k}^{(j,l)}\pm q_{j}x\pm q_{l}y,

where the scalars
c_{k}^{(j,l)}\in\mathbb{R}
depend on the jump positions
\{t_{r}\}_{r=1}^{n}

and the local parameters
\{q_{r}\}_{r=0}^{n}
.

Since
\kappa
is bounded below on
(0,\infty)
and
\Lambda
has
finite Lebesgue measure, the integral

(3.37)

\displaystyle J(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\frac{e^{isu}}{\kappa(u)}\,du,\quad s\in\mathbb{R}

is a well-defined function whose Fourier transform is supported in

\overline{\Lambda^{1/2}}
. By (
3.34
) and (
3.35
),
we can now write the reproducing kernel
k_{\Lambda}
as

(3.38)

\displaystyle k_{\Lambda}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{m(x,y)}\alpha_{k}(x,y)\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\frac{e^{i\beta_{k}(x,y)u}}{\kappa(u)}\,du=\sum_{k=1}^{m(x,y)}\alpha_{k}(x,y)J(\beta_{k}(x,y)).

The function
J
is the Fourier transform of
\kappa
occuring in the
spectral measure and usually has to be computed numerically or by
means of the series expansion (see the next section).

The case

We consider a step function

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p_{0},p_{1},p_{2},T>0
and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}

be of finite measure and set
q_{k}=p_{k}^{-1/2},k=0,1,2

(4.1)

\displaystyle p(x)

\displaystyle=p_{0}\chi_{(-\infty,-T/2]}(x)+p_{1}\chi_{(-T/2,T/2]}(x)+p_{2}\chi_{[(T/2,\infty)}(x)\,,

(4.2)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{16q_{0}^{2}}\left[\left(1+\tfrac{q_{0}}{q_{1}}\right)^{2}\left(1+\tfrac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}\right)^{2}+\left(1-\tfrac{q_{0}}{q_{1}}\right)^{2}\left(1-\tfrac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}\right)^{2}\right],

(4.3)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{8q_{0}^{2}}\left(1-\tfrac{q_{0}^{2}}{q_{1}^{2}}\right)\left(1-\tfrac{q_{1}^{2}}{q_{2}^{2}}\right),

(4.4)

\displaystyle\zeta

\displaystyle=2q_{1}T\,.

Then the density in the spectral measure in (
3.24
) is
\kappa(u)=C+K\cos\zeta u
and the associated integral is

J(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{e^{isu}}{C+K\cos\zeta u}\,du,\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, the fundamental system
\Phi(u^{2},\cdot)=(\Phi^{+}(u^{2},\cdot),\Phi^{-}(u^{2},\cdot)),u\in(0,\infty)

is of the form

\displaystyle\Phi^{+}(u^{2},x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a_{0}^{+}(u^{2})e^{iq_{0}xu}+b_{0}^{+}(u^{2})e^{-iq_{0}xu},&x\in(-\infty,-\frac{T}{2}]\\
a_{1}^{+}(u^{2})e^{iq_{1}xu}+b_{1}^{+}(u^{2})e^{-iq_{1}xu},&x\in(-\frac{T}{2},\frac{T}{2}]\\
e^{iq_{2}xu},&x\in(\frac{T}{2},\infty)\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Phi^{-}(u^{2},x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e^{-iq_{0}xu},&x\in(-\infty,-\frac{T}{2}]\\
a_{1}^{-}(u^{2})e^{iq_{1}xu}+b_{1}^{-}(u^{2})e^{-iq_{1}xu},&x\in(-\frac{T}{2},\frac{T}{2}]\\
a_{2}^{-}(u^{2})e^{iq_{2}xu}+b_{2}^{-}(u^{2})e^{-iq_{2}xu},&x\in(\frac{T}{2},\infty).\end{cases}

Applying (
3.9
) for
l=2
yields

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}a_{0}^{+}(u^{2})\\
b_{0}^{+}(u^{2})\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4}\begin{bmatrix}\left(1+\frac{q_{0}}{q_{1}}\right)\left(1+\frac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}\right)e^{i\frac{T}{2}\left(q_{0}-2q_{1}+q_{2}\right)u}+\left(1-\frac{q_{0}}{q_{1}}\right)\left(1-\frac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}\right)e^{i\frac{T}{2}\left(q_{0}+2q_{1}+q_{2}\right)u}\\
\left(1-\frac{q_{0}}{q_{1}}\right)\left(1+\frac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}\right)e^{i\frac{T}{2}\left(-q_{0}-2q_{1}+q_{2}\right)u}+\left(1+\frac{q_{0}}{q_{1}}\right)\left(1-\frac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}\right)e^{i\frac{T}{2}\left(-q_{0}+2q_{1}+q_{2}\right)u}\end{bmatrix}

Since
|Ae^{i\alpha}+Be^{i\beta}|^{2}=A^{2}+B^{2}+2AB\cos(\alpha-\beta)
, we obtain the claimed formula for
\kappa
from the above
as

\displaystyle\kappa(u)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{|a_{0}^{+}(u^{2})|^{2}}{q_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{16q_{0}^{2}}\left[(1+\tfrac{q_{0}}{q_{1}}\big)^{2}\big(1+\tfrac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}\big)^{2}+\big(1-\tfrac{q_{0}}{q_{1}}\big)^{2}\big(1-\tfrac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}\big)^{2}\right]\ +\tfrac{1}{8q_{0}^{2}}\big(1-\tfrac{q_{0}^{2}}{q_{1}^{2}}\big)\big(1-\tfrac{q_{1}^{2}}{q_{2}^{2}}\big)\cos(2Tq_{1}u)

\displaystyle=C+K\cos\zeta u\,,

with the constants
C
and
K
from (
4.2
) and
(
4.3
). Consequently,

(4.5)

\displaystyle J(s)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\frac{e^{isu}}{C+K\cos\zeta u}\,du

for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

To the best of our knowledge,
J
does not belong to any known class of
special functions. If the spectrum
\Lambda
is an interval, we can
expand
J
into a series
in terms of the cardinal sine function
\mathrm{sinc}\,x=\frac{\sin x}{x}
. Its partial sums
converge to
J
uniformly on
\mathbb{R}
and at a geometric rate.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
R=\frac{K}{C}
and
\Lambda=[0,\Omega]
. Then for
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle J(s)=\dfrac{\Omega^{1/2}}{2C\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\left(-\frac{R}{2}\right)^{m}\binom{m}{l}e^{i\tfrac{\Omega^{1/2}}{2}(s+(m-2l)\zeta)}\mathrm{sinc}\left(\tfrac{\Omega^{1/2}}{2}(s+(m-2l)\zeta)\right).

Moreover, the
M
-th partial sum
J_{M}
of
J
satisfies the error estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}|J(s)-J_{M}(s)|\leq\dfrac{\Omega^{1/2}}{2C\pi}\dfrac{|R|^{M+1}}{1-|R|}.

Note that by the definition of
K
and
C
we have
|R|=|K|/C<1
so that the integrand of
J
does not have any
singularities. Therefore the error estimate implies uniform
convergence of the partial
sums and this rate depends only on
R
, which in turn depends only on
the parameters of the local bandwidths
p
.

Proof.

By assumption we have
|R|<1
. The expression for
J(s)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
can be expanded in a geometric series.

(4.6)

\displaystyle\qquad J(s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi C}\int_{0}^{\Omega^{1/2}}\dfrac{e^{isu}}{1-\left(-R\cos\zeta u\right)}\,du=\dfrac{1}{2\pi C}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(-R\right)^{m}\int_{0}^{\Omega^{1/2}}e^{isu}\cos^{m}\zeta u\,du\,.

The interchange of the above integral and sum follows from Weierstrass

M
-test. For
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, define the bandlimited function

F_{m}(s)=\int_{0}^{\Omega^{1/2}}e^{isu}\cos^{m}\zeta u\,du,\quad s\in\mathbb{R}

so that
J=\frac{1}{2\pi C}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(-R\right)^{m}F_{m}
.
To compute
F_{m}
, we write cosine using complex exponentials:

\displaystyle F_{m}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\Omega^{1/2}}e^{isu}\cdot\left(\dfrac{e^{i\zeta u}+e^{-i\zeta u}}{2}\right)^{m}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\int_{0}^{\Omega^{1/2}}e^{i(s+(m-2l)\zeta)u}\,du

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{1/2}}{2^{m}}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}e^{i\tfrac{\Omega^{1/2}}{2}(s+(m-2l)\zeta)}\mathrm{sinc}\left(\tfrac{\Omega^{1/2}}{2}(s+(m-2l)\zeta)\right).

Substituting (
4.7
) to (
4.6
) gives the desired expansion.
Now, since
|F_{m}(s)|\leq\Omega^{1/2}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
for
M\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we observe that

\displaystyle|J(s)-J_{M}(s)|=\frac{1}{2\pi C}\left|\sum_{m=M+1}^{\infty}(-R)^{m}F_{m}(s)\right|\leq\frac{\Omega^{1/2}}{2\pi C}\sum_{m=M+1}^{\infty}|R|^{m}=\frac{\Omega^{1/2}}{2\pi C}\frac{|R|^{M+1}}{1-|R|}.

Taking the supremum over all
s\in\mathbb{R}
completes the proof.

5.
A Density Theorem for Functions of Variable Bandwidth

In this section, we derive necessary density conditions
for sampling and interpolation in
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
with piecewise
constant
p
using a general theory about sampling in reproducing
kernel Hilbert spaces from
[
8
]
.

5.1.
Sampling in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces

We first summarize the
definition of density and the necessary density conditions in
reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces. For complete details see
[
8
]
.

For a Borel measure
\mu
we define the the upper and lower Beurling
densities of a separable set
X
as the quantities

\displaystyle D_{\mu}^{+}(X)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\#(X\cap B_{r}(x))}{\mu(B_{r}(x))},

\displaystyle D_{\mu}^{-}(X)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{r\to\infty}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\#(X\cap B_{r}(x))}{\mu(B_{r}(x))}

The relevant measure for sampling in a reproducing kernel Hilbert
space with kernel
k

is
d\mu\left(x\right)=k\left(x,x\right)dx
. The dimension-free
Beurling densities
D_{0}^{\pm}(X)
are defined with respect to the
measure
d\mu\left(x\right)=k\left(x,x\right)dx
:

D_{0}^{+}(X)=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\#(X\cap B_{r}(x))}{\int_{B_{r}(x)}k(x,x)dx},\quad D_{0}^{-}(X)=\liminf_{r\to\infty}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\#(X\cap B_{r}(x))}{\int_{B_{r}(x)}k(x,x)dx}

To derive the desired density theorems in
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
with
piecewise constant
p
, we will use the following special case of

[
8
, Thm. 2.2]
. Note the crucial role of the reproducing
kernel in the assumptions.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume
k
is the reproducing kernel for a subspace
\mathcal{H}\subset L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},dx\right)

and satisfies the following conditions:

(i) Boundedness of diagonal: There exist constants

C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}\leq k(x,x)\leq C_{2}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}.

(ii) Weak localization property: For every
\epsilon>0
,
there exists
r(\epsilon)>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B_{r(\epsilon)}(x)}|k(x,y)|^{2}\,dy<\epsilon^{2}.

(iii) Homogeneous approximation property: Assume that a
subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}
satisfies a Bessel inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}|f(x)|^{2}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
. Then for every
\epsilon>0
there exists

r(\epsilon)>0
such that

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{\underset{|x-y|>r(\epsilon)}{x\in X}}|k(x,y)|^{2}<\epsilon^{2}.

Under these assumptions the following density conditions hold in
\mathcal{H}
.

(A) If
X
is a set of stable sampling for
\mathcal{H}
, then
D_{0}^{-}(X)\geq 1.

(B) If
X
is a set of interpolation for
\mathcal{H}
, then
D_{0}^{+}(X)\leq 1.

Instead of using the measure
k(x,x)\,dx
, this result can be written in terms of any measure
\mu

equivalent
2
2

2

in the sense that
d\mu=h\,dx
for some measurable function
h
with
0<c\leq h(x)<C
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and some constants
c
and
C
.
to the Lebesgue measure. For this define the upper and
lower averaged traces of
k
with respect to
\mu
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr_{\mu}^{+}}=

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{\mu(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)}k(y,y)\,dy,

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr_{\mu}^{-}}=

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\to\infty}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{\mu(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)}k(y,y)\,dy.

Then the following reformulation of Theorem
5.1
holds
[
10
, Lemma 6.6]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
d\mu=h\,dx
be equivalent to the Lebesgue
measure. Then
D_{0}^{-}(X)\geq 1

holds, if and only if
D_{\mu}^{-}\left(X\right)\geq\mathrm{tr_{\mu}^{-}}
.
Similarly
D_{0}^{+}\left(X\right)\leq 1
, if and only if
D_{\mu}^{+}\left(X\right)\leq\mathrm{tr_{\mu}^{+}}
.

Proof.

The inequality
D_{0}^{-}(X)\geq 1
means that for all
\varepsilon>0

there is an
r_{\epsilon}>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and all
r>r_{\varepsilon}

(5.1)

\#\left(X\cap B_{r}\left(x\right)\right)\geq\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\int_{B_{r}\left(x\right)}k\left(y,y\right)dy\,,

or equivalently,

\frac{\#\left(X\cap B_{r}\left(x\right)\right)}{\mu\left(B_{r}\left(x\right)\right)}\geq\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\frac{1}{\mu\left(B_{r}\left(x\right)\right)}\int_{B_{r}\left(x\right)}k\left(y,y\right)dy\,.

Written in terms of the Beurling density, this is

D_{\mu}^{-}\left(X\right)\geq\liminf_{r\to\infty}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\int_{B_{r}\left(x\right)}k\left(y,y\right)dy}{\mu(B_{r}(x))}=\mathrm{tr}_{\mu}^{-}(k)\,.

The converse is obtained by reading the argument backwards.

5.2.
Necessary density conditions in
PW_{[0,\Omega]}\left(A_{p}\right)

For the Paley-Wiener space
PW_{[0,\Omega]}\left(A_{p}\right)
we define the positive measure

\mu=\mu_{p}
by

\displaystyle\mu(I)=\mu_{p}(I)=\int_{I}\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{p(x)}}\,.

Since
p
is positive and bounded away from zero,
\mu_{p}
is equivalent
to Lebesgue measure.

In the case
\mu=\mu_{p}
we write
D_{p}^{\pm},\trace_{p}^{\pm}
instead
of
D_{\mu_{p}}^{\pm}\left(X\right)
etc. Our aim is to prove the
following theorem for the spectral set
\Lambda
being an interval.

Theorem 5.3

(Density theorem in
PW_{[0,\Omega]}(A_{p})
)
.

Let
\Lambda=[0,\Omega]
and
p
a piecewise constant function.

(i)

If
X
is a set of stable sampling for
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
, then

D_{p}^{-}(X)\geq\frac{|\Lambda^{1/2}|}{\pi}.

(ii)

If
X
is a set of interpolation for
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
, then

D_{p}^{+}(X)\leq\frac{|\Lambda^{1/2}|}{\pi}.

The result is similar to
[
9

but with notable
differences in the proofs. In particular, since
p
is not smooth, not
even continuous, we cannot use a Liouville transform to change
A_{p}

into an equivalent Schrödinger operator and apply scattering theory
(as was done in
[
9
]
).

The plan of the proof is to verify the conditions of Theorem
5.1

for the reproducing kernel of
PW(A_{p})
.
We will work our way through a series of Lemmas that verify the conditions
of Theorem
5.1
and then
obtain the value for the averaged trace of the kernel.

5.3.
Fine properties of the reproducing kernel of
PW_{[0,\Omega]}\left(A_{p}\right)

We first verify that the properties of Theorem
5.1
are
satisfied for the reproducing kernel of
PW_{[0,\Omega]}\left(A_{p}\right)
.

Lemma 5.4

(Boundedness of the diagonal)
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
be a set of finite measure,

p
a piecewise constant function and
k_{\Lambda}
the reproducing
kernel for
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
. Then there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0

such that

C_{1}\leq k_{\Lambda}(x,x)\leq C_{2}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The uniform boundedness of
k_{\Lambda}
(hence the existence of

C_{2}
) follows from the uniform boundedness of the fundamental
solutions of
A_{p}
by Lemma

3.3
and the formula for the reproducing kernel
k_{\Lambda}
in Theorem

3.8
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle k_{\Lambda}(x,x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{\frac{1}{q_{0}}|\Phi^{+}(u^{2},x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{q_{n}}|\Phi^{-}(u^{2},x)|^{2}}{\kappa(u)}\,du

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{2\pi q_{0}}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{|\Phi^{+}(u^{2},x)|^{2}}{\kappa(u)}\,du

\displaystyle\geq\min_{0\leq j\leq n}\inf_{x\in I_{j}}\dfrac{1}{2\pi q_{0}}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{|a_{j}^{+}(u^{2})e^{iq_{j}xu}+b_{j}^{+}(u^{2})e^{-iq_{j}xu}|^{2}}{\kappa(u)}\,du

\displaystyle\geq\min_{0\leq j\leq n}\dfrac{1}{2\pi q_{0}}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{\left||a_{j}^{+}(u^{2})|-|b_{j}^{+}(u^{2})|\right|^{2}}{\kappa(u)}\,du=C_{1}.

Since
|a_{j}^{+}(u^{2})|-|b_{j}^{+}(u^{2})|=\frac{q_{j}}{q_{n}}

by (
3.14
) and
\kappa
is bounded, we conclude that
C_{1}>0
.

Next we show that the kernel
k_{\Lambda}
exhibits off-diagonal
decay. Here we need that
\Lambda
is an interval.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\Lambda=[0,\Omega]
for some
\Omega>0
,

p
a piecewise constant function and
k_{\Lambda}
the reproducing
kernel for
PW_{[0,\Omega]}(A_{p})
. Then for some constant
C>0

(5.2)

\displaystyle|k_{\Lambda}(x,y)|\leq\dfrac{C}{1+|x-y|}\qquad\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}\,.

Proof.

Let
p
be a piecewise constant function for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Recall
the expression (
3.38
) for the reproducing kernel

\displaystyle k_{\Lambda}(x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{m(x,y)}\alpha_{k}(x,y)J(\beta_{k}(x,y)),\quad m(x,y)\in\mathbb{N},\alpha_{k}(x,y),\beta_{k}(x,y)\in\mathbb{R},1\leq k\leq m(x,y)

with
J(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\Omega^{1/2}}\frac{e^{isu}}{\kappa(u)}\,du

and
\beta_{k}(x,y)=c_{k}^{(jl)}\pm q_{j}x\pm q_{l}y
for

x\in I_{j},y\in I_{l}
.

In a first step we show that
J(s)=\mathcal{O}(s^{-1})
for
\left|s\right|
large enough.
Indeed, as
\kappa

is (infinitely) differentiable on
(0,\infty)
with bounded derivatives
and
0<\frac{1}{\kappa(u)}\leq q_{0}q_{n}
for all
u\in(0,\infty)
,
integration by parts yields

(5.3)

\displaystyle J(s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi is}\left(\dfrac{e^{is\Omega^{1/2}}}{\kappa(\Omega^{1/2})}-\dfrac{1}{\kappa(0^{+})}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2\pi is}\int_{0}^{\Omega^{1/2}}\frac{\kappa^{\prime}(u)}{\kappa(u)^{2}}e^{isu}

\displaystyle|J(s)|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{2\pi|s|}\left\{\dfrac{1}{|\kappa(\Omega^{1/2})|}+\dfrac{1}{|\kappa(0^{+})|}+\int_{0}^{\Omega^{1/2}}\dfrac{\left|\kappa^{\prime}(u)\right|}{\kappa(u)^{2}}\,du\right\},

where
\kappa(0^{+})=\lim_{u\downarrow 0}\kappa(u)
. Therefore for
all
s\neq 0

\displaystyle|J(s)|\leq\frac{q_{0}q_{n}}{2\pi|s|}\left(2+q_{0}q_{n}\Omega^{1/2}\sup_{0\leq u\leq\Omega^{1/2}}|\kappa^{\prime}(u)|\right).

In the second step, we verify that for all
k
there exist for some

(5.4)

|\beta_{k}(x,y)|\geq{N_{k}}(1+|x-y|)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
|x-y|>r_{k}
. We proceed as follows: let
a>0
such that the knots
\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}

of

are contained in
[-a,a]
. Consider cases where
x
and

y
take values on the intervals

(-\infty,-a)\subset I_{0},\quad[-a,a]\supset I_{1}\cup\ldots I_{n-1},\quad(a,\infty)\subset I_{n}

with
|x-y|
large.

•

Suppose
x
and
y
belong to the same unbounded interval, say without
loss of generality
x,y<-a

\beta_{k}(x,y)=c_{k}\pm q_{0}(x\pm y)

As
|x\pm y|\geq|x-y|

)

sufficiently large, i.e.,

•

Likewise the case
|x|\geq a

(
5.4

•

The remaining case is

and
y>a

and

\displaystyle\vartheta(u,x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{0}}\overline{\Phi^{+}(u^{2},x)}\Phi^{+}(u^{2},y)+\frac{1}{q_{n}}\overline{\Phi^{-}(u^{2},x)}\Phi^{-}(u^{2},y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{0}}\Big(\overline{a_{0}^{+}(u^{2})}e^{-iq_{0}x}+\overline{b_{0}^{+}(u^{2})}e^{iq_{0}x}\Big)e^{iq_{n}yu}+\frac{1}{q_{n}}e^{iq_{0}xu}\Big(a_{n}^{-}(u^{2})e^{iq_{n}yu}+b_{n}^{-}(u^{2})e^{-iq_{n}yu}\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\overline{b_{0}^{+}(u^{2})}}{q_{0}}+\frac{a_{n}^{-}(u^{2})}{q_{n}}\Big)e^{i(q_{0}x+q_{n}y)u}+\frac{\overline{a_{0}^{+}(u^{2})}}{q_{0}}e^{-i(q_{0}x-q_{n}y)u}+\frac{a_{0}^{+}(u^{2})}{q_{0}}e^{i(q_{0}x-q_{n}y)u}

This implies that there are no exponentials containing

To summarize, the decay estimate on

Lemma 5.6

(Weak localization)
.

Let
\Lambda=[0,\Omega]
for
some
\Omega>0
,
p
be a piecewise constant function and

the reproducing kernel for

. Then for every

such that

(5.5)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{|x-y|>r(\epsilon)}|k_{\Lambda}(x,y)|^{2}\,dy<\epsilon^{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma

\displaystyle|k_{\Lambda}(x,y)|\leq\dfrac{C}{1+|x-y|}.

Lemma 5.7

(Homogeneous approximation property)
.

\Lambda=[0,\Omega]

for some
\Omega>0
,

a piecewise constant function and

(5.7)

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{\underset{|x-y|>r(\epsilon)}{x\in X}}|k_{\Lambda}(x,y)|^{2}<\epsilon^{2}.

Proof.

Fix
y\in\mathbb{R}
. By Lemma
5.5
, there exists
r,C>0

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{\underset{|x-y|>r}{x\in X}}|k_{\Lambda}(x,y)|^{2}\leq\sum_{\underset{|x-y|>r}{x\in X}}\dfrac{C^{2}}{1+|x-y|^{2}}.

As
X
is relatively separated, the right hand side
can be made smaller than a given
\epsilon>0
for
r
large enough.

Lemmas
5.6
and
5.7
hold for arbitrary spectral sets
\Lambda

of finite measure, although the off-diagonal decay of
k_{\Lambda}
in
(
5.2
) no
longer holds.
See
[
4
, Lems. C.1.2, C.2.3]
for the treatment of the general case.

5.4.
The averaged trace of the reproducing kernel

Next we compute the averaged trace of
k
required for the abstract
density theorem (Lemma
5.2
). For these arguments

\Lambda
may be an arbitrary set of finite measure.

To rewrite the diagonal of the reproducing kernel, we
define the auxiliary functions

\displaystyle h_{j}^{(1)}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{|a_{j}^{+}(u^{2})|^{2}}{q_{0}}+\frac{|a_{j}^{-}(u^{2})|^{2}}{q_{n}}

\displaystyle h_{j}^{(2)}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{a_{j}^{+}(u^{2})}b_{j}^{+}(u^{2})}{q_{0}}+\frac{\overline{a_{j}^{-}(u^{2})}b_{j}^{-}(u^{2})}{q_{n}}\,

for
0\leq j\leq n

and
u\in(0,\infty)
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
be of finite measure,

p
a step function with
n
jumps and
k_{\Lambda}
the reproducing kernel for
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
.
Then

(5.8)

\displaystyle k_{\Lambda}(y,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{h_{j}^{(1)}(u)}{\kappa(u)}\,du+\dfrac{1}{\pi}\emph{Re}\mathcal{F}\big(\dfrac{h_{j}^{(2)}}{\kappa}\cdot\chi_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\big)(2q_{j}y)\Big)\chi_{(t_{j},t_{j+1}]}(y)\,.

Proof.

We evaluate the diagonal of
k_{\Lambda}
using Proposition
3.8
.
Fix
y\in I_{j}
for some
0\leq j\leq n
. Using the formula (
3.34
)
for
k_{\Lambda}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\vartheta(u,y,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{0}}|\Phi^{+}(u^{2},y)|^{2}+\frac{1}{q_{n}}|\Phi^{-}(u^{2},y)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{0}}|a_{j}^{+}(u^{2})e^{iuq_{j}y}+b_{j}^{+}(u^{2})e^{-iuq_{j}y}|^{2}+\frac{1}{q_{n}}|a_{j}^{-}(u^{2})e^{iuq_{j}y}+b_{j}^{-}(u^{2})e^{-iuq_{j}y}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{|a_{j}^{+}(u^{2})|^{2}}{q_{0}}+\frac{|b_{j}^{+}(u^{2})|^{2}}{q_{0}}\right)+\left(\frac{|a_{j}^{-}(u^{2})|^{2}}{q_{n}}+\frac{|b_{j}^{-}(u^{2})|^{2}}{q_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle+2\textrm{Re}\left[\left(\frac{\overline{a_{j}^{+}(u^{2})}b_{j}^{+}(u^{2})}{q_{0}}+\frac{\overline{a_{j}^{-}(u^{2})}b_{j}^{-}(u^{2})}{q_{n}}\right)e^{-2iuq_{j}y}\right]

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{|a_{j}^{+}(u^{2})|^{2}}{q_{0}}+\frac{|a_{j}^{-}(u^{2})|^{2}}{q_{n}}\right)+2\textrm{Re}\left[\left(\frac{\overline{a_{j}^{+}(u^{2})}b_{j}^{+}(u^{2})}{q_{0}}+\frac{\overline{a_{j}^{-}(u^{2})}b_{j}^{-}(u^{2})}{q_{n}}\right)e^{-2iuq_{j}y}\right]\,,

where in the last identity we have used (
3.15
).
Then

\displaystyle k_{\Lambda}(y,y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{\vartheta(u,y,y)}{\kappa(u)}\,du

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{h_{j}^{(1)}(u)}{\kappa(u)}\,du+\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{\textrm{Re}(h_{j}^{(2)}(u)e^{-2iq_{j}yu})}{\kappa(u)}\,du

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{h_{j}^{(1)}(u)}{\kappa(u)}\,du+\dfrac{1}{\pi}\textrm{Re}\,\mathcal{F}\left(\dfrac{h_{j}^{(2)}}{\kappa}\cdot\chi_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\right)(2q_{j}y)

as claimed.

Note that the first term
\int h^{(1)}_{j}
depends only on the
interval
I_{j}
, but not on
y\in I_{j}
.

In the following theorem we compute the averaged trace of the kernel
k
. We recall that the
notation
f\lesssim g
means that there exists

C\geq 0
such that
f\leq Cg
.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
be of finite measure,

p
a piecewise constant function and
k_{\Lambda}
the reproducing
kernel of
PW_{\Lambda}(A_{p})
. Suppose
I
is a large interval
such that
I\not\subset[t_{1},t_{n}]
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(I)}\int_{I}k_{\Lambda}(y,y)\,dy-\dfrac{|\Lambda^{1/2}|}{\pi}\right|\lesssim\mu_{p}(I)^{-1/2}\,.

In particular,

\emph{tr}^{-}=\liminf_{r\to\infty}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)}k_{\Lambda}(y,y)\,dy=\frac{|\Lambda^{1/2}|}{\pi}\,,

and likewise
\emph{tr}^{+}=\frac{|\Lambda^{1/2}|}{\pi}
.

Proof.

Let
p
be a piecewise constant function with
n
jumps at
\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
. We may assume that
the interval
I=[\alpha,\beta]\supseteq[t_{1},t_{n}]
. The two other
cases are easier to handle. Since
\frac{1}{\sqrt{p(x)}}\chi_{(t_{j},t_{j+1}]}(x)=q_{j}
for
x\in(t_{j},t_{j+1}]
, we obtain

(5.9)

\displaystyle\mu_{p}(I)

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p(x)}}\,dx=\left[q_{0}(t_{1}-\alpha)+q_{n}(\beta-t_{n})\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}q_{j}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})\,,

and
\mu_{p}(I)\geq(\beta-\alpha)\min_{j}q_{j}
. We first observe that

\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(I)}\int_{I}k_{\Lambda}(y,y)\,dy=\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(I)}\int_{I\setminus[t_{1},t_{n}]}k_{\Lambda}(y,y)\,dy+\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(I)}\int_{[t_{1},t_{n}]}k_{\Lambda}(y,y)\,dy\,.

Since the second term in this some is of the form
C\mu_{p}(I)^{-1}

we can focus on the first term. We use the representation of the diagonal
of
k_{\Lambda}
obtained in Lemma
5.8
and consider first
the expression

T_{1}=\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(I)\pi}\Big(\int_{\alpha{}_{\alpha}}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{h_{0}^{(1)}(u)}{\kappa(u)}\,du\,dy+\int_{t_{n}}^{\beta}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{h_{0}^{(1)}(u)}{\kappa(u)}\,du\,dy\Big)\,.

Using the initial conditions
a_{0}^{-}=b_{n}^{+}=0
,
a_{n}^{+}=b_{0}^{-}=1

and the definition of
\kappa
in (
3.25
), we obtain

h_{0}^{(1)}(u)=\frac{|a_{0}^{+}(u^{2})|^{2}}{q_{0}}=q_{0}\kappa(u),\quad h_{n}^{(1)}(u)=\frac{|b_{n}^{-}(u^{2})|^{2}}{q_{n}}=q_{n}\kappa(u)\,,

and consequently

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{|\Lambda^{1/2}|}{\mu_{p}(I)\pi}\left(q_{0}(t_{1}-\alpha)+q_{n}(\beta-t_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{|\Lambda^{1/2}|}{\mu_{p}(I)\pi}\Big(\mu_{p}(I)-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}q_{j}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})\Big)=\dfrac{|\Lambda^{1/2}|}{\pi}+\mathcal{O}\big(\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(I)}\big).

This leads to the estimate

(5.10)

\displaystyle\left|T_{1}-\dfrac{|\Lambda^{1/2}|}{\pi}\right|\lesssim\mu_{p}(I)^{-1}\lesssim\mu_{p}(I)^{-1/2}.

For the second term, we make a similar computation. Let

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(I)\pi}\int_{I\setminus[t_{1},t_{n}]}\textrm{Re}\mathcal{F}\Big(\dfrac{h_{j}^{(2)}}{\kappa}\cdot\chi_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\Big)(2q_{j}y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(I)\pi}\textrm{Re}\left(\int_{\alpha}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{h_{0}^{(2)}(u)e^{-2iq_{0}yu}}{\kappa(u)}\,du\,dy+\int_{t_{n}}^{\beta}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{h_{n}^{(2)}(u)e^{-2iq_{n}yu}}{\kappa(u)}\,du\,dy\right)\,.

Interchanging the order of integration and evaluating the
integral over
y
first, we have

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(I)\pi}\textrm{Re}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{h_{0}^{(2)}(u)e^{-2iq_{0}(t_{1}+\alpha)u}}{\kappa(u)}\cdot\dfrac{\sin(q_{0}(t_{1}-\alpha)u)}{q_{0}u}\,du

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(I)\pi}\textrm{Re}\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{h_{n}^{(2)}(u)e^{-2iq_{n}(\beta+t_{n})u}}{\kappa(u)}\cdot\dfrac{\sin(q_{n}(\beta-t_{n})u)}{q_{n}u}\,du.

With the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, this can be estimated as

\displaystyle\left|T_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(I)\pi}\left(C_{0}\left(\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{\sin^{2}(q_{0}(t_{1}-\alpha)u)}{(q_{0}u)^{2}}du\right)^{1/2}+C_{n}\left(\int_{\Lambda^{1/2}}\dfrac{\sin^{2}(q_{n}(\beta-t_{n})u)}{(q_{n}u)^{2}}du\right)^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{C_{0}}{\mu_{p}(I)\pi}\left(\frac{t_{1}-\alpha}{q_{0}}\int_{q_{0}(t_{1}-\alpha)\Lambda^{1/2}}\!\mathrm{sinc}^{2}\omega\,d\omega\right)^{1/2}+\dfrac{C_{n}}{\mu_{p}(I)\pi}\left(\frac{\beta-t_{n}}{q_{n}}\int_{q_{n}(\beta-t_{n})\Lambda^{1/2}}\!\mathrm{sinc}^{2}\omega\,d\omega\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C}{\mu_{p}(I)\pi}\mu_{p}(I)^{1/2}\leq C^{\prime}\mu_{p}(I)^{-1/2}\,.

Putting these estimates together we obtain

(5.11)

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{1}{\mu_{p}(I)}\int_{I}k_{\Lambda}(y,y)\,dy-\dfrac{|\Lambda^{1/2}|}{\pi}\right|=\left|T_{1}+T_{2}-\dfrac{|\Lambda^{1/2}|}{\pi}\right|\leq C\mu_{p}(I)^{-1/2}\,.

5.5.
Proof of Theorem
5.3

Proof.

Theorem
5.3
now follows from the general density theorem
as stated in Theorem
5.1
. In Lemmas
5.4
—
5.7
we
have verified that the conditions on the reproducing kernel are
satisfied, and in Theorem
5.9
we have computed the critical
density given by the averaged trace of the kernel.

Remark
.

5.3

If the spectral set is not an interval
[0,\Omega]

5.5
no longer holds, and therefore the proofs of Lemmas

5.6
and
5.7
do not work for general spectral
sets. Nevertheless the conclusions of
5.6
and
5.7
still
hold whenever
\Lambda
has finite measure. The proofs are more
delicate and the interested reader is referred to
[
4
]
.

Maximal Inequality Associated to Doubling Condition for State Preserving Actions
Panchugopal Bikram, Diptesh Saha

Abstract
. In this article, we prove maximal inequality and ergodic theorems for state preserving actions on von Neumann algebra by an amenable, locally compact, second countable group equipped with the metric satisfying the doubling condition. The key idea is to use Hardy–Littlewood maximal inequality, a version of the transference principle and certain norm estimates of differences between ergodic averages and martingales.

Primary 46L53, 46L55; Secondary 37A55, 46L40.

1.
Introduction

In this article, we aim to study non-commutative maximal ergodic inequalities and pointwise ergodic theorems for certain group actions. The origin of ergodic theorems on classical measure spaces traces back to the 1930s due to Birkhoff and von Neumann. The subject has evolved a lot since then. The non-commutative analogue of the ergodic theorems first appeared in the works of Lance (
[
Lan76
]
) in 1976. In (
[
Lan76
]
), the author proved a pointwise ergodic theorem for the average of a single automorphism preserving a faithful, normal state in von Neumann algebra. These results are then generalised by Kummerer, Conze, Dang-Ngoc and many others (cf.
[
K7̈8
]
,
[
CDN78
]
and references therein).
Eventually, it became evident that the standard approach to establishing an ergodic theorem is to show a maximum inequality and the accompanying Banach principle.
However, establishing maximal inequality is one of the hardest and most delicate part in the non-commutative setting.

In the seminal works
[
Yea77
]
and
[
Yea80
]
, Yeadon obtained maximal ergodic theorems for actions of positive sub-tracial, sub-unital maps on the preduals of semifinite von Neumann algebras and proved associated pointwise ergodic theorems. Later on, Junge and Xu (
[
JX07
]
)) extended Yeadon’s maximal inequalities to prove Dunford-Schwartz maximal inequalities in the non-commutative
L^{p}
-spaces using interpolation techniques.

On the other hand, various results are obtained regarding the ergodic theorems for actions of various groups on classical measure spaces (cf.
[
AAB+10
]
,
[
Cal53
]
). Among them,
[
Lin01
]
is particularly important for our purpose. He proved pointwise ergodic theorems for tempered Følner sequences, which is considered as the generalisation of Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem for actions of second countable, amenable groups. In the non-commutative realm, the first significant breakthrough is made by Hong, Liao and Wang in
[
HLW21
]
. In this article, the authors obtained several maximal inequalities on non-commutative
L^{p}
-spaces for the actions of locally compact group metric measure spaces satisfying doubling conditions and compactly generated groups of polynomial growth and used them to prove associated pointwise ergodic theorems. Furthermore, these results are extended to the general setting of amenable groups in
[
CW22
]
. It is worth mentioning here that both these articles deal with actions preserving a faithful, normal, semifinite trace. However, in
[
JX07
]
, the authors studied maximal inequalities for ergodic averages in Haagerup’s
L^{p}
-spaces (
1<p<\infty
) associated to state preserving actions of the group
\mathbb{Z}
and semigroup
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. However, the maximal inequality for state preserving actions in the endpoint case (
p=1
) was not studied until
[
BS23b
]
,
[
BS23a
]
.

In
[
BS23b
]
, the authors studied maximal inequalities and pointwise ergodic theorems on non-commutative
L^{1}
-spaces for locally compact polynomial growth group actions preserving a faithful, normal state on a von Neumann algebra. To obtain the aforesaid maximal inequality, we basically used an inequality obtained in
[
HLW21
, Proposition 4.8]
to dominate the associated ergodic averages for groups by an ergodic average of Markov operator.
In
[
BS23a
]
, the authors have established similar maximal inequality for the action of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{+}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
by using a similar method. In this setup, obtaining maximal inequality for actions of general amenable groups is known to be challenging in the literature, and it requires delicate analysis. However, in this article, we obtain maximal inequalities for actions of group metric measure spaces satisfying the doubling condition using suitable analogues of Calderon’s transference principle and maximal inequality for Martingales.

2.1.
Non-commutative
L^{1}
-spaces

Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful, normal, semifinite (f.n.s.) trace

X:\mathcal{D}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}

X\eta\mathcal{M}

X\eta\mathcal{M}

\tau

\epsilon>0

\tau(1-e)<\epsilon
and
e\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(X)
and one defines the set

\displaystyle L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau):=\{X\eta\mathcal{M}:X\text{ is }\tau\text{-measurable}\}.

Notice that

is a
*

]

There are several topologies on

. Two of them, which are relevant to this article, are discussed in the following. The measure topology is given by the neighbourhood system
\{X+\mathcal{N}(\epsilon,\delta):X\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau),\epsilon,\delta>0\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\epsilon,\delta):=\{X\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\exists~e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})\text{ such that }\tau(1-e)<\delta,\text{ and }\left\|eXe\right\|<\epsilon\}.

Clearly, a net

in

\epsilon,\delta>0
there exists
i_{0}\in I
such that for all

there exists

with
\tau(1-e_{i})<\delta

\displaystyle\left\|e_{i}(X_{i}-X)e_{i}\right\|<\epsilon.

Note that it follows from
[

is complete with respect to the measure topology. Moreover,

becomes a metrizable Hausdorff space with respect to this topology in which
\mathcal{M}
is dense. On the other hand, we have the following definition for bilateral almost uniform topology.

Definition 2.1
.

A net
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
in
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
converges in bilateral almost uniform (b.a.u.) topology to
X\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
if for all
\epsilon>0
there exists

with
\tau(1-e)<\delta
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{i}\left\|e(X_{i}-X)e\right\|=0.

We have the following result from
[
Lit24
, Theorem 2.2]
, which is important to our purpose.

Theorem 2.2
.

The space
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is complete with respect to b.a.u. topology.

Let us also observe the following.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}

\{Y_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}

The trace
\tau

M_{+}
can be extended to

\displaystyle\tau(X):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda d\tau(e_{\lambda}),

\displaystyle L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau):=\{X\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\tau(\left|X\right|)<\infty\},

3.
Weak type maximal inequality

In this section, we will prove a weak type maximal inequalities for certain predual actions. Let
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be a non-commutative dynamical system. In
[
BS23b
]

In this section, we prove the maximal inequality when the group
G
is equipped with a metric

(in this case,

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra with a f.n positive linear functional

on
\mathcal{M}

(1)

The associated dual map

For simplicity, we introduce the following notations. For

(1)

[m]:=\{1,2,\cdots,m\}\subset\mathbb{N}

(2)

[-m\mathrel{{.}\,{.}}\penalty m]:=\{-m,-(m-1),\cdots,0,\cdots m-1,m\}\subset\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\kappa(\underline{x})-\kappa(\underline{x}^{\prime})\geq 0

4.
Pointwise ergodic theorems

In this section, we will study pointwise convergence of specific ergodic averages. With the weak type maximal inequality in our disposal (cf. §
3
), now we need to prove an appropriate Banach principle and pointwise convergence of elements from a dense subset.

Throughout this section,
\mathcal{M}
is assumed to be a von Neumann algebra with a f.n. tracial state
\tau
and
(G,d,\mu)
is a group metric measure space satisfying doubling condition. We further assume that
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
is a non-commutative dynamical system such that there exists a f.n. state
\rho
on
\mathcal{M}
such that
\rho\circ\alpha_{s}=\rho
for all
s\in G
.

Before we proceed to the main results of this section, let us recall the following structural property of
(G,d,\mu)
. For proof of this fact, we refer to
[
Tes07

(also cf.
[
HLW21

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a lacunary sequence
(r_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}

(i)

2^{i}\leq r_{i}<2^{i+1}
and

(ii)

\frac{\mu(V(x,r_{i})\setminus V(x,r_{i}-(3/2)^{i}))}{\mu(V(x,r_{i}))}\leq C^{\prime}(3/4)^{i}
for all
x\in G
,

where the constant
C^{\prime}
depends only on the doubling condition.

As we have
\rho\circ\alpha_{s}=\rho
for all
s\in G

u_{s}
on the
GNS
space

\displaystyle u_{s}(x\Omega_{\rho})=\alpha_{s}(x)\Omega_{\rho},

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(r_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the lacunary sequence as in Lemma
4.1
. Then the averages

\displaystyle A_{r_{i}}x:=\frac{1}{\mu(V_{r_{i}})}\int_{V_{r_{i}}}u_{s}(x)d\mu(s),~x\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho)

converges in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\rho}
.

Proof.

Since
(L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho),G,u)
forms a non-commutative dynamical system , we observe that

\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho):=F_{2}\oplus F_{2}^{\perp},

where

\displaystyle F_{2}:=\{x\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho):u_{s}(x)=x~\forall s\in G\}

\displaystyle F_{2}^{\perp}:=\overline{span}\{y-u_{s}y:y\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho),~s\in G\}.

Therefore, it is enough to check the convergence of
A_{r_{i}}x
for
x=y-u_{s}y
where
y\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho),~s\in G
. Moreover, note that

\displaystyle A_{r_{i}}x=A_{r_{i}}^{1}y-A_{r_{i}}^{2}y,

where

\displaystyle A_{r_{i}}^{1}y:=\frac{1}{\mu(V_{r_{i}})}\int_{V_{r_{i}}\setminus(V_{r_{i}}\cap V_{r_{i}}s)}u_{s}(y)d\mu(s),

\displaystyle A_{r_{i}}^{2}y:=\frac{1}{\mu(V_{r_{i}})}\int_{V_{r_{i}}s\setminus(V_{r_{i}}\cap V_{r_{i}}s)}u_{s}(y)d\mu(s).

Now since
(3/2)^{i}\rightarrow\infty
as
i\rightarrow\infty
, we can choose
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(3/2)^{i}\geq d(e,s)=\left|s\right|
. Moreover,
V_{r_{i}}\setminus(V_{r_{i}}\cap V_{r_{i}}s)\subseteq V_{r_{i}}\setminus V_{r_{i}-(3/2)^{i}}
and
V_{r_{i}}s\setminus(V_{r_{i}}\cap V_{r_{i}}s)\subseteq V_{r_{i}}s\setminus V_{r_{i}-(3/2)^{i}}s
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|A_{r_{i}}^{1}y\right\|\leq\frac{\mu(V_{r_{i}}\setminus V_{r_{i}-(3/2)^{i}})}{\mu(V_{r_{i}})}\left\|y\right\|\leq C(3/4)^{i}\left\|y\right\|,~\text{and }

\displaystyle\left\|A_{r_{i}}^{2}y\right\|\leq\frac{\mu(V_{r_{i}}s\setminus V_{r_{i}-(3/2)^{i}}s)}{\mu(V_{r_{i}}s)}\left\|y\right\|\leq C(3/4)^{i}\left\|y\right\|.

Therefore,
\left\|A_{r_{i}}x\right\|
converges to
0

∎

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(V_{r_{n}})}\frac{1}{\mu(V_{r_{k}})}\int_{V_{r_{k}}}\int_{V_{r_{n}}}\Big(\gamma_{s}(a)-\gamma_{st}(a)\Big)d\mu(s)d\mu(t)\quad\text{ (using Fubini)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(V_{r_{k}})}\int_{V_{r_{k}}}\frac{1}{\mu(V_{r_{n}})}\Big(\int_{V_{r_{n}}}\gamma_{s}(a)d\mu(s)-\int_{V_{r_{n}}t}\gamma_{s}(a)d\mu(s)\Big)d\mu(t).

Now,

\displaystyle\int_{V_{r_{n}}}\gamma_{s}(a)d\mu(s)-\int_{V_{r_{n}}t}\gamma_{s}(a)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{V_{r_{n}}\setminus V_{r_{n}}\cap V_{r_{n}}t}\gamma_{s}(a)d\mu(s)-\int_{V_{r_{n}}t\setminus V_{r_{n}}\cap V_{r_{n}}t}\gamma_{s}(a)d\mu(s).

Now since
(3/2)^{n}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we can choose
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(3/2)^{n}\geq d(e,t)=\left|t\right|
. Moreover,
V_{r_{n}}\setminus(V_{r_{n}}\cap V_{r_{n}}t)\subseteq V_{r_{n}}\setminus V_{r_{n}-(3/2)^{n}}
and
V_{r_{n}}t\setminus(V_{r_{n}}\cap V_{r_{n}}t)\subseteq V_{r_{n}}t\setminus V_{r_{n}-(3/2)^{n}}t
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\mu(V_{r_{n}})}\int_{V_{r_{n}}\setminus V_{r_{n}}\cap V_{r_{n}}t}\gamma_{s}(a)d\mu(s)\right\|\leq\frac{\mu(V_{r_{n}}\setminus V_{r_{n}-(3/2)^{n}})}{\mu(V_{r_{n}})}\left\|a\right\|\leq C^{\prime}(3/4)^{n}\left\|a\right\|,~\text{and }

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\mu(V_{r_{n}})}\int_{V_{r_{n}}t\setminus V_{r_{n}}\cap V_{r_{n}}t}\gamma_{s}(a)d\mu(s)\right\|\leq\frac{\mu(V_{r_{n}}t\setminus V_{r_{n}-(3/2)^{n}}t)}{\mu(V_{r_{n}}t)}\left\|a\right\|\leq C^{\prime}(3/4)^{n}\left\|a\right\|.

Finally, we obtain

which proves the result.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha,\rho)
be a kernel and
X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Then there exist
\bar{X}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
such that
A_{n}(X)
converges b.a.u. to
\bar{X}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from Theorem
4.13
and Remark
4.10
.
∎

5.
Stochastic Ergodic Theorem

This section combines our previous results to prove a stochastic ergodic theorem. Throughout this section, as before, we assume that
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a von Neumann algebra with a f.n. tracial state
\tau
and we consider
L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Further, we also take
G
as an amenable group metric measure space satisfying the doubling condition.
Then, we consider a covariant system
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
.
As earlier, for
X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, we consider the following average

\displaystyle A_{n}(X):=A_{r_{n}}(X):=\frac{1}{\mu(V_{r_{n}})}\int_{V_{r_{n}}}\alpha^{*}_{s}(\nu)d\mu(s)=\frac{1}{\mu(V_{r_{n}})}\int_{V_{r_{n}}}\hat{\alpha}_{s^{-1}}(X)d\mu(s).

Then using the previous results, we established the stochstic ergodic theorem for the average
A_{n}(\cdot)
. First, we recall the following theorem from
[
BS23b
]
.

Theorem 5.1

(Neveu Decomposition)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be a covariant system and
\tau
be a f.n. tracial state on
\mathcal{M}
. Then there exist two projections
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that
e_{1}+e_{2}=1
and
e_{1}e_{2}=0
such that

(i)

there exists a
G
-invariant normal state
\rho
on
\mathcal{M}
with support
s(\rho)=e_{1}
and

(ii)

there exists an operator
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
with support
s(x_{0})=e_{2}
such that
\frac{1}{\mu(V_{r_{n}})}\int_{V_{r_{n}}}\alpha_{s}(x_{0})d\mu(s)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0
in
\left\|\cdot\right\|
.

Further,
s(\rho)
and
s(x_{0})
are unique.

Now, we collect the following basic facts from
[
BS23b
]
.

(1)

\alpha_{g}(e_{i})=e_{i}
for all
g\in G
and
i=1,2
.

(2)

The restriction of
\alpha
to the reduced von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}_{e_{i}}
(
=e_{i}\mathcal{M}e_{i}
) induces an action by automorphisms, and we denote this induced action by the same notation
\alpha
. Thus,
(\mathcal{M}_{e_{i}},G,\alpha)
for
i=1,2
becomes a non-commutative dynamical system.

(3)

For
i=1,2
, we also note that for all
g\in G
, the predual transformation
\hat{\alpha_{g}}
defined on
L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha_{g}}(e_{i}Xe_{i})=e_{i}\hat{\alpha_{g}}(X)e_{i}\text{ for all }X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\text{ and for all }g\in G.

As a consequence, the ergodic averages satisfy

\displaystyle e_{i}A_{n}(X)e_{i}=A_{n}(e_{i}Xe_{i})\text{ for all }X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau),n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }i=1,2.

We write
\tau_{e_{i}}=\frac{1}{\tau(e_{i})}\tau|_{e_{i}\mathcal{M}e_{i}}
for
i=1,2
. Now, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be a covariant system and

\tau
be a f.n. tracial state on
\mathcal{M}
. Consider the projections
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
as mentioned above. Then, we have the following results.

(i)

For all
B\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M}_{e_{1}},\tau_{e_{1}})
, there exists
\bar{B}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M}_{e_{1}},\tau_{e_{1}})
such that
A_{n}(B)
converges b.a.u to
\bar{B}
. Moreover,
A_{n}(B)
converges in measure to
\bar{B}
.

(ii)

For all
B\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M}_{e_{2}},\tau_{e_{2}})
,
A_{n}(B)
converges to
0
in measure.

Moreover, for
X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, there exists
Z\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
such that
A_{n}(X)
converges to
Z
in measure and we have
e_{1}Ze_{1}=Z
and
e_{2}Ze_{2}=0
.

Proof.

Once we have the Neveu decomposition (Theorem
5.1
) and Theorem
4.14
, the proof follows verbatim as
[
BS23b
, see §5]
.
∎

Measurements of rate coefficients of CN + \text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} , HCN + \text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} and HNC + \text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} collisions with H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} at cryogenic temperatures
Petr Dohnal, Pavol Jusko, Miguel Jiménez-Redondo, Paola Caselli

Abstract
The experimental determination of the reaction rate coefficients for production and destruction of HCN + \text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} and HNC + \text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} in collisions with H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} is presented. A variable temperature 22 pole radio frequency ion trap was used to study the reactions in the temperature range of 17 – 250 K. The obtained rate coefficients for the reaction of CN + \text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} and of HCN + \text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} with H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} are close to the collisional (Langevin) value, whereas that for the reaction of HNC + \text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} with H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} is quickly decreasing with increasing temperature. The product branching ratios for the reaction of CN + \text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} with H 2 \text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} are also reported and show a notable decrease of HNC + \text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} product with respect to HCN + \text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} product with increasing temperature. These measurements have consequences for current astrochemical models of cyanide chemistry, in particular for the HCNH + \text{HCNH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} cation.

Methods

II.1
Experimental setup

The experimental setup is described in detail in
Jusko, Jiménez-Redondo, and Caselli 2023
, only a short overview will be given here.
The ions are produced in the storage ion source (SIS)
Gerlich 1992
, mass selected by passing through the
first quadrupole mass filter and then refocused on the trap axis using an electrostatic bender.
The 22 pole radio frequency ion trap is positioned on a cold head (RDK-101E, Sumitomo) enabling operation down to
4\;\rm{K}
. The pressure
is measured by a Bayard-Alpert type ionisation gauge calibrated by a capacitance manometer CMR 375 from Pfeiffer. After a set
storage time, the trap is opened and the ions exiting the trap are mass selected by the second quadrupole mass
spectrometer and detected in a Daly type conversion detector.
The trapped ions are cooled in collisions with helium gas that is introduced for a few milliseconds into the trap volume
at the beginning of every trapping cycle by a custom piezo valve.
This measurement sequence is repeated, usually at
1\;\text{Hz}

repetition rate, until an adequate signal for the set experimental condition is acquired.

II.3
Data analysis

By integrating the chemical rate equations for the ion number densities over the trap volume, balance equations
describing time evolutions of the number of trapped ions
n_{\mathrm{i}}
are obtained. As an example, for the
reaction of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
with
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
the corresponding balance equation can be written as

\frac{\mathrm{d}n_{\mathrm{HCN^{+}}}}{\mathrm{d}t}=-k^{\mathrm{H_{2}}}_{\mathrm{HCN^{+}}}[{}\mathrm{H}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}]n_{\mathrm{HCN^{+}}},

(1jn)

where
k^{\mathrm{H_{2}}}_{\mathrm{HCN^{+}}}
is the binary rate coefficient for the reaction of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
with
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

and
[{}\mathrm{H}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}]
is the
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
number density.
When evaluating the time dependencies of the number of ions in the trap, the quantities that are obtained by
fitting the set of appropriate balance equations to the measured data are reaction rates, i. e., the reaction rate
for reaction (
1jn
) is
k^{\mathrm{H_{2}}}_{\mathrm{HCN^{+}}}[{}\mathrm{H}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}]
. The reaction rate coefficient
is then determined from the slope of the dependence of the reaction rate on the number density of the
corresponding reactant at the given temperature (for a detailed description of the fitting procedure see
ref.
Jusko, Jiménez-Redondo, and Caselli 2023

IV
Conclusion

The formation and destruction of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
isomers in reactions with
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
were studied in the temperature
range of
17-250\;\text{K}
. The values of the reaction rate coefficients for reactions of
\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
with
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
are
constant in the studied temperature range and close to the collisional Langevin reaction rate coefficient. The reaction
of
\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
with
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
produces predominantly
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
at 250 K, but below 100 K,
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
is favoured. The obtained
values of the reaction rate coefficient for the reaction of
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
with
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
decrease with increasing temperature.
As current astrophysical databases such as KIDA
Wakelam et al. 2012
contain rate coefficients for these key reactions that were obtained at 300 K, in some cases with vibrationally excited ions, we believe that our results will help to improve models of cyanide chemistry in the interstellar medium.

The isomer specific results in this work were achieved solely using chemical probing. This technique is fully dependent
on the availability of chemicals with the right energy levels with respect to the isomers being studied. Even then,
the process is rather tedious, since several reactants have to be used in sequence (e. g. isomerisation/ reactivity/
probing) with tight control of the real number density inside the trap, especially while lowering the temperature
towards the neutral gas freeze out. While we have demonstrated that rf multipole ion traps are a suitable tool for
isomer specific studies, the application of novel techniques and schemes based on direct discrimination of ions with different
kinetic energy (e. g. the reaction of of
\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
with
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
releases two equal
m/z
products with two distinctive total energies),
as described in
Jusko, Jiménez-Redondo, and Caselli 2023
,
or indirectly, based on the transfer of the internal excitation to translation energy in a collision with a neutral, as described
in the “leak out” method
Schmid et al. 2022
, shall be developed.

Abstract
. We consider commutative C ∗ C^{*} -algebras of Toeplitz operators in the weighted Bergman space on the unit ball in ℂ 𝐧 . \mathbb{C}^{{\mathbf{n}}}. For the algebras of elliptic type we find a new representation, namely as the algebra of operators which are functions of certain collections of commuting unbounded self-adjoint operators in the Bergman space. 1.
Introduction

An important topic in the analysis of Toeplitz operators in Bergman spaces is the description of commutative
C^{*}
- and Banach algebras generated by such operators. Such description was performed in the book
[
16
]
and the series of papers, see e.g.
[
7
,
8
,
11
]
, for weighted Bergman spaces on the unit disk and on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}
in
\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{n}}
. Typical results in this direction relate commutative algebras with certain groups of conformal transformations (biholomorphisms) of
\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}
,
{\mathbf{n}}\geq 1
, and the symbols of Toeplitz operators that generate commutative
C^{*}
-algebra, should be invariant with respect to these transformations.

Recently, in the paper
[
13
]
, a different approach to the construction of commutative algebras was initiated. Namely, for a self-adjoint operator
V
in the Bergman space on the unit disk
\mathbb{B}^{1}
, the algebra of functions of this operator is commutative (in the sense corresponding to the size of the algebra of functions under consideration). If the operator
V
commutes with a group of isometries of the Bergman space, generated by a one-parametric group of conformal mappings of the disk, the algebra of functions of this operator contains the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by Toeplitz operators, whose symbols are invariant under the above one-parametric group of conformal mappings. Such correspondence gives a better insight to the structure of commutative algebras of operators, extends the known classes of symbols generating bounded Toeplitz operators and, in particular, provides a clear explanation for the property of a Toeplitz operator to be compact.

In the present paper we extend the approach developed in
[
13
]
to the study of commutative algebras of Toeplitz operators in the Bergman space on the ball in
\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{n}}.
The passage to the multi-dimensional case requires some additional considerations. The most important is the one that the treatment of the classical commutative algebras of Toeplitz operators requires, generally, not the usual spectral theory of self-adjoint operators but the multi-variable spectral theory for collections
{\mathbf{V}}=(V_{1},\dots,V_{m})
of commuting self-adjoint operators.

Following the pattern of the one-dimensional case, it was shown in
[
11
]
that for each maximal Abelian subgroup (MASG) of the biholomorphisms of the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
, the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by Toeplitz operators whose symbols are invariant under the action of this subgroup, is commutative. Recall
[
11
]
, that there are five types of model MASGs on
\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
(or its unbounded realization, the Siegel domain
\mathbb{D}_{{\mathbf{n}}}
):

Quasi-elliptic
group of biholomorphisms of the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
, isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with the group action

t:\ z=(z_{1},...,z_{\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}\longmapsto tz=(t_{1}z_{1},...,t_{\mathbf{n}}z_{\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}},

for each
t=(t_{1},...,t_{\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{n}}
.

Quasi-parabolic
group of biholomorphisms of the Siegel domain
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{n}}
, isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{{\mathbf{n}}-1}\times\mathbb{R}
with the group action

(t,h):\ (z^{\prime},z_{\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{n}}\longmapsto(tz^{\prime},z_{\mathbf{n}}+h)\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{n}},

for each
(t,h)\in\mathbb{T}^{{\mathbf{n}}-1}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Quasi-hyperbolic
group of biholomorphisms of the Siegel domain
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{n}}
, isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{{\mathbf{n}}-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with the group action

(t,r):\ (z^{\prime},z_{\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{n}}\longmapsto(r^{1/2}tz^{\prime},rz_{\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{n}},

for each
(t,r)\in\mathbb{T}^{{\mathbf{n}}-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

Nilpotent
group of biholomorphisms of the Siegel domain
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{n}}
, isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{{\mathbf{n}}-1}\times\mathbb{R}

with the group action

(b,h):\ (z^{\prime},z_{\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{n}}\mapsto(z^{\prime}+b,z_{n}+h+2iz^{\prime}\cdot b+i|b|^{2})\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{n}},

for each
(b,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{{\mathbf{n}}-1}\times\mathbb{R}
;

Quasi-nilpotent
group of biholomorphisms of the Siegel domain
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{n}}
, isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{{\mathbf{k}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{k}}-1}\times\mathbb{R}
,
0<{\mathbf{k}}<{\mathbf{n}}-1
,
with the group action

(t,b,h):\ (z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime},z_{\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{n}}\longmapsto\\
(tz^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime}+b,z_{\mathbf{n}}+h+2iz^{\prime\prime}\cdot b+i|b|^{2}))\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{n}},

for each
(t,b,h)\in\mathbb{T}^{{\mathbf{k}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{k}}-1}\times\mathbb{R}
.

The latter, quasi-nilpotent, type of groups involves the integer parameter
{\mathbf{k}}
, thus giving in total
{\mathbf{n}}+2
model groups for the
{\mathbf{n}}
-dimensional ball
\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
.

In the present paper we give a detailed characterization of commutative
C^{*}
-algebras generated by Toeplitz operators related to the
quasi-elliptic group
(and of ambient commutative von Neumann algebras) as algebras of functions of the proper systems of commuting self-adjoint operators. The largest among the considered algebras is the algebra generated by Toeplitz operators with symbols being invariant under the smallest quasi-elliptic group
\mathbb{T}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
, namely the diagonal subgroup of
\mathbf{U}({\mathbf{n}})
(the latter, as usual, denotes the group of all
{\mathbf{n}}\times{\mathbf{n}}
unitary matrices). Here the collection of operators
{\mathbf{V}}
consists of
{\mathbf{n}}
entries. Toeplitz operators with symbols invariant with respect to larger subgroups of
\mathbf{U}({\mathbf{n}})
, the ones consisting of block-diagonal matrices, generate commutative algebras coinciding with algebras of functions of smaller collections of commuting operators. Such symbols are called
quasi-radial
.

We consider also Toeplitz operators with radial symbols, the ones invariant with the respect to the group
\mathbf{U}({\mathbf{n}})
itself. It turns out that this algebra is isomorphic to the algebra of functions of one single operator
{\mathbf{V}}=(V_{1})
. Generally, the quantity of self-adjoint operators present in the functional calculus representation of the algebra generated by Toeplitz operators equals the number of diagonal blocks in the subgroup of
\mathbf{U}({\mathbf{n}}).

Finally, we consider from the spectral point of view certain commutative algebras of Toeplitz operators, which are proper subalgebras of the previous ones. Symbols of such Toeplitz operators depend only on a part of variables, or possess some similar degeneracy. Such symbols and the corresponding Toeplitz operators were discussed, in particular, in
[
1
,
4
,
10
]
. Contrary to the above, for each such algebra there is no subgroup of
\mathbf{U}({\mathbf{n}})
for which the symbols of the generating Toeplitz operators are exactly those which are invariant under the action of this group. Nevertheless, a spectral representation of such algebras can be found.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Spectral Theorem

For the Readers’ convenience and in order to fix the notation, we recall some basic facts related to the Spectral Theorem for a finite collection of commuting self-adjoint operators. For proofs and more details see, e.g.,
[
5
, Chapter 6, Section 5]
,
[
14
, Chapter 1]
, and
[
15
, Chapter 5]
.

As well known, for each, generally speaking unbounded, self-adjoint operator
V
in a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H},
there exists its spectral measure
E(\cdot)
, the projection-valued
\sigma
-additive function defined on Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}
, orthogonal in the sense
E(\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2})=E(\Delta_{1})E(\Delta_{2})
, and such that
E(\varnothing)=0
,
E(\mathbb{R})={\mathbf{I}}
. Associated with
E(\Delta),
is the resolution of identity,
{\mathbf{E}}(\eta)=E(-\infty,\eta].
The operator
V
admits the Stieltjes integral representation

V=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta d{\mathbf{E}}(\eta),

understood in the strong sense, with the domain

\mathcal{D}(V)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}:\ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta^{2}d\langle{\mathbf{E}}(\eta)f,f\rangle<\infty\right\}.

In the functional calculus associated with
V
, for a Borel complex-valued function
\psi(\eta)
on
\mathbb{R},
the operator
\psi(V)
is defined as

\psi(V)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(\eta)d{\mathbf{E}}(\eta),

(2.1)

again understood in the strong sense, with the domain

{\mathcal{D}}(\psi(V))=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}:\ \int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi(\eta)|^{2}d\langle{\mathbf{E}}(\eta)f,f\rangle<\infty\right\}.

Such operators
\psi(V)
are normal, self-adjoint for a real-valued function
\psi
; they are bounded for
E
-essentially bounded functions
\psi
and the mapping

Op_{E}:\,\psi\mapsto\psi(V)

(2.2)

is an isometric isomorphism of the commutative
C^{*}
algebra of
E
-essentially bounded Borel function to a commutative algebra of bounded operators.

For unbounded,
E
-almost everywhere finite functions
\psi,
the normal operators
\psi(V)
commute in the resolvent sense, as long as they possess at least one regular point.

Suppose now that a finite collection of (possibly, unbounded) self-adjoint operators
{\mathbf{V}}=(V_{1}
,
V_{2}
, …,
V_{m})
(an
m
-tuple) in the same separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is given, and let
E_{1}(\cdot)
,
E_{2}(\cdot)
, …,
E_{m}(\cdot)
be their spectral measures.
We say that the operators
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
, …,
V_{m}
(strongly) commute if their spectral measures commute, i.e., for any Borel sets
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}
and each
j^{\prime}\neq j
,
E_{j}(\Delta_{1})E_{j^{\prime}}(\Delta_{2})=E_{j^{\prime}}(\Delta_{2})E_{j}(\Delta_{1})
.

Theorem 2.1

(
Spectral Theorem)
.

For each
m
-tuple
{\mathbf{V}}=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\}
of pairwise strongly commuting self-adjoint operators in a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, there exists a unique
joint spectral measure

E(\cdot)
on the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, such that for the corresponding resolution of identity,

{\mathbf{E}}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\equiv E\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}(-\infty,\eta_{j}]\right),\quad\boldsymbol{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{m}),

the following Stieltjes representation holds:

V_{j}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\eta_{j}d{\mathbf{E}}(\boldsymbol{\eta}),\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

with the integral understood in the strong sense.

The spectral measure
E(\cdot)
can be constructed in the following way.
For a rectangle
\Delta=[a_{1},b_{1}]\times\ldots\times[a_{m},b_{m}]
, we set
E(\Delta)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}E_{j}([a_{j},b_{j}])
and then extend it to all Borel sets on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
in the standard way.

We recall also that the support of the spectral measure
E(\cdot)
(the smallest closed set of full measure) is called the
joint spectrum

of the
m
-tuple
{\mathbf{V}}=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\}
and is denoted by

\sigma({\mathbf{V}})\equiv\sigma(V_{1},\ldots,V_{m})\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
; here

\sigma({\mathbf{V}})=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\sigma(V_{j}).

(2.3)

Theorem 2.2

(
Functional Calculus)
.

Given an
E
-measurable function
\psi
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the operator

\displaystyle\psi({\mathbf{V}})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\psi(V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\psi(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m})\,d{\mathbf{E}}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m})

(2.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma({\mathbf{V}})}\psi(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m})\,d{\mathbf{E}}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m})

is well defined and
normal
on its domain

\mathcal{D}_{\psi}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|\psi(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m})|^{2}\,d\langle{\mathbf{E}}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m})f,f\rangle<\infty\right\}.

The operator
\psi(V_{1},\ldots,V_{m})
is bounded, and thus defined on the whole
\mathcal{H}
, if and only if the function
\psi
is
E
-essentially bounded.
The functional calculus mapping

Op_{E}:\psi\mapsto\psi(V_{1},\ldots,V_{m})\equiv\psi({\mathbf{V}})

is an isometric isomorphism of the
C^{*}
-algebra of
E
-essentially bounded complex functions onto a commutative
*
- algebra of of bounded normal operators in
\mathcal{H}.

2.2.
The Bergman space on the ball

Our considerations deal with objects in the complex space
\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{n}}
for a certain
{\mathbf{n}}.
Let
\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}=\{z=(z_{1},...,z_{\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{n}}:\ |z|^{2}\equiv|z_{1}|^{2}+...+|z_{\mathbf{n}}|^{2}<1\}
be the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{n}}
; for any
k\leq{\mathbf{n}},
for a ball
\mathbb{B}^{k}\subset\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{n}},
we denote by
\tau(\mathbb{B}^{k})\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}\subset\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{n}}
the base of the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{k}
, considered as a Reinhard domain, i.e.,

\tau(\mathbb{B}^{k})=\{(r_{1},...,r_{k})=(|z_{1}|,...,|z_{k}|)\,:\ r^{2}=r_{1}^{2}+...+r_{k}^{2}\in[0,1)\}.

Given a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},...,\alpha_{\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+}
we will use the standard notation,

\displaystyle|\alpha|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+...+\alpha_{\mathbf{n}},

\displaystyle\alpha!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}!\,\alpha_{2}!\,\cdots\,\alpha_{\mathbf{n}}!,

\displaystyle z^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\,z_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\,\cdots\,z_{\mathbf{n}}^{\alpha_{\mathbf{n}}},

\displaystyle z_{l}=x_{l}+iy_{l},\,l=1,2,...,{\mathbf{n}}.

Denote by
d{\mathcal{V}}=dx_{1}dy_{1}...dx_{\mathbf{n}}dy_{\mathbf{n}}
the standard Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{n}}
; for
\lambda>-1
, we introduce the probability weighted measure on
\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}},

dv_{\lambda}(z)=c_{\lambda}\,(1-|z|^{2})^{\lambda}\,d{\mathcal{V}}(z),\quad\mathrm{where}\quad c_{\lambda}=\frac{\Gamma({\mathbf{n}}+\lambda+1)}{\pi^{\mathbf{n}}\,\Gamma(\lambda+1)},\ \ \ \lambda>-1.

The basic object of our considerations is the weighted Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathbf{n}}_{\lambda}\equiv L_{2}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}},dv_{\lambda})
and its subspace, the weighted Bergman space
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
which consists of all analytic functions in
{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathbf{n}}_{\lambda}
. By
\mathbb{P}
we denote the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\lambda}
.

Further on, in notations, the superscript
2
will be skipped since our considerations concern only the Hilbert space theory.

Recall that the standard orthonormal basis of the Bergman space
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})
consists of the monomials

e_{\alpha}(z):=e_{\alpha,({\mathbf{n}})}(z)\equiv\omega_{\lambda,\alpha,({\mathbf{n}})}z^{\alpha},\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathbf{n}}.

(2.5)

where

\omega_{\lambda,\alpha,({\mathbf{n}})}=\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma({\mathbf{n}}+|\alpha|+\lambda+1)}{\alpha!\Gamma({\mathbf{n}}+\lambda+1)}}.

Note that the normalization factor
\omega_{\lambda,\alpha,({\mathbf{n}})}
in (
2.5
) depends on the dimension
{\mathbf{n}}
, so the function
z^{\alpha}
having
\alpha_{l}=0
for
l>k
with some
k
,
1\leq k<{\mathbf{n}}
, produces an element
e_{\alpha,(k)}(z)
in the basis of the space
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{k})
, but with the normalization factor
\omega_{\lambda,\alpha,(k)},
different from the one in (
2.5
). If some value
k
different from
{\mathbf{n}}
comes into consideration, the subscript
(k)
will be present in the notation
e_{\alpha,(k)}
, otherwise,
k={\mathbf{n}}
is assumed. In such sections where the weight exponent
\lambda
is fixed, we may omit it in the notation as well.

Thus, the space
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})={\mathcal{A}}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
consists of analytic functions
f(z)=f(z_{1},\dots,z_{\mathbf{n}})
for which the Fourier coefficients

f_{\alpha}=\langle f,e_{\alpha}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})}

(2.6)

satisfy
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+}}|f_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty.

2.3.
Partitions and Isometry Groups

Let
\mathbb{k}=(k_{1},...,k_{m})
be a
tuple
of positive integers:
k_{1}+...+k_{m}={\mathbf{n}}
.
Given the partition
\mathbb{k}=(k_{1},...,k_{m})
, we arrange the coordinates of
z\in\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}
in
m
groups, each one of which has
k_{j}
,
j=1,...,m
, entries and introduce the notation

z_{(1)}=(z_{1,1},...,z_{1,k_{1}})\in\mathbb{C}^{k_{1}},\ z_{(2)}=(z_{2,1},...,z_{2,k_{2}})\in\mathbb{C}^{k_{2}},\ ...,\ z_{(m)}=(z_{m,1},...,z_{m,k_{m}})\in\mathbb{C}^{k_{m}},

with a proper re-numbering of co-ordinates, so that

\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}\ni z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{\mathbf{n}})=(z_{(1)},z_{(2)},\ldots,z_{(m)}).

With a group
z_{(j)}
fixed, the
complementing
group of variables will be denoted by

\widetilde{z_{(j)}}=(z_{(1)},\ldots,z_{(j-1)},z_{(j-1)},\ldots,z_{(m)}),

(2.7)

so, with an obvious permutations of variables (which is always assumed performed),
z=(z_{(j)},\widetilde{z_{(j)}}).
In the extreme case
\mathbb{k}=(1,1,\dots,1),

\widetilde{z_{j}}=\widetilde{z_{(j)}}.

(Note the subtlety in notation: the subscript
j
(as in
z_{j}
,
\widetilde{z_{j}}
,
V_{j}
, etc.) is used for denoting a single complex variable or some related object, while the subscript
(j)
is used to denote a tuple of variables and related objects, as in
z_{(j)},\widetilde{z_{(j)}}
etc.)

With a partition
\mathbb{k}=(k_{1},...,k_{m})
we associate the subgroup
{\mathbf{H}}={\mathbf{H}}_{\mathbb{k}}\subset{\mathbf{U}}({\mathbf{n}})

{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathbb{k}}=\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k}):=\mathbf{U}(k_{1})\times\ldots\times\mathbf{U}(k_{m}),

realized as a block diagonal subgroup of
\mathbf{U}({\mathbf{n}})
.

Among such subgroups, the smallest one and the only commutative one
is the group
{\mathbf{H}}_{\min}
corresponding to the partition
\mathbb{k}_{\min}=(1,1,\dots,1)
; this group is the
{\mathbf{n}}
-dimensional torus
{\mathbf{H}}_{\min}=\mathbb{T}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
realised as the group of diagonal unitary matrices. The largest of such subgroups is
{\mathbf{U}}({\mathbf{n}})
itself. It corresponds to the trivial partition
\mathbb{k}=({\mathbf{n}}).
All other partitions generate groups partially ordered by inclusion.

With each partition
\mathbb{k}
we associate the class of (first, bounded) symbols
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbb{k})
invariant under the action of the group
{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathbb{k}}.
It stands to reason that the classes
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbb{k})
are ordered in the inverse way to the groups
{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathbb{k}}
. As we will see, Toeplitz operators with symbols in
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbb{k})
generate a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra. In the sections to follow we associate with each subgroup
{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathbb{k}}
a collection of commuting unbounded self-adjoint operators
{\mathbf{V}}={\mathbf{V}}(\mathbb{k})
so that the (von Neumann) algebra of all bounded measurable functions of the operators
{\mathbf{V}}
is commutative and contains the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by Toeplitz operators with symbols in
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbb{k})
.
It turns out that the smaller is the group
{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathbb{k}},
the more operators in the system
{\mathbf{V}}
are needed. In the extreme cases
{\mathbf{H}}={\mathbf{U}}({\mathbf{n}}),
respectively,
{\mathbf{H}}=\mathbb{T}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
, the system
{\mathbf{V}}
consists of one, respectively,
{\mathbf{n}}
operators.

3.
Toeplitz operators with separately radial symbols

We consider first the subgroup
{\mathbf{H}}_{\min}=\mathbb{T}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
represented as the group of diagonal unitary matrices in a certain fixed co-ordinate system
{\mathbf{z}}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{{\mathbf{n}}})
in
\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}.
This group, the smallest one, corresponds to the maximal partition,
\mathbb{k}_{\max}=(1,\dots,1).
Symbols in the class
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbb{k}_{\max})
are invariant with respect to the action of
\mathbb{T}^{{\mathbf{n}}};
these are functions
a(r_{1},\dots,r_{\mathbf{n}}),
where
r_{j}=|z_{j}|,

j=1,\dots,{\mathbf{n}}.

Following our convention, for

\widetilde{z_{j}}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{j-1},z_{j+1},\dots z_{{\mathbf{n}}}),

z_{j}
taken exactly to the power
\ell,

f(z)=z_{j}^{\ell}h(\widetilde{z_{j}}).
After being closed in the norm of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})
, this structure is modified in the following way. We calculate the norm in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})
of a function

.
We have

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})}=c_{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}}|z_{j}^{\ell}|^{2}|h(\widetilde{z_{j}})|^{2}(1-|z_{j}|^{2}-|\widetilde{z_{j}}|^{2})^{\lambda}d{\mathcal{V}}(z)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=c_{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}-1}}|h(\widetilde{z_{j}})|^{2}d{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{z_{j}})\int_{|z_{j}|<(1-|\widetilde{z_{j}}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}|z_{j}|^{2\ell}(1-|z_{j}|^{2}-|\widetilde{z_{j}}|^{2})^{\lambda}d{\mathcal{V}}(z_{j}).

We set
|\widetilde{z_{j}}|=\widetilde{r_{j}}
and calculate the
z_{j}
-integral in (
3.4
), making use of

\displaystyle\int_{|z_{j}|<(1-\widetilde{r_{j}}^{2}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}}|z_{j}|^{2\ell}(1-|z_{j}|^{2}-|\widetilde{z_{j}}|^{2})^{\lambda}d{\mathcal{V}}(z_{j})

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{0}^{(1-\widetilde{r_{j}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}r_{j}^{2\ell+1}(1-r_{j}^{2}-\widetilde{r_{j}}^{2})^{\lambda}dr_{j}

\displaystyle=\pi\int_{0}^{1-\widetilde{r_{j}}^{2}}\rho_{j}^{\ell}(1-\rho_{j}-\widetilde{r_{j}}^{2})^{\lambda}d\rho_{j}

\displaystyle=\pi(1-|\widetilde{r_{j}}^{2}|)^{\ell+\lambda+1}\frac{\Gamma(\ell+1)\Gamma(\lambda+1)}{\Gamma(\ell+\lambda+2)}.

Thus, the square of the norm of

equals

(3.6)

with

c_{\lambda,\ell}=({\mathbf{n}}+\lambda)\frac{\Gamma(\ell+1)\Gamma(\lambda+1)}{\Gamma(\ell+\lambda+2)}

(3.7)

As a result of these calculations we can see that the space
H_{\ell}^{(j)}
defined in (
3.3
) consists of functions of the form
f(z)=z_{j}^{\ell}h(\widetilde{z_{j}}),
with
h
belonging to the Bergman space
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda+\ell+1}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}-1})
. Moreover, the norms of
f
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})
and of
h
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda+\ell+1}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}-1})
are equivalent.

It follows from the definition that
H_{\mathbf{s}}=\bigcap_{j=1}^{{\mathbf{n}}}H_{s_{j}}^{(j)}
for
{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+}.

We introduce the orthogonal projections,
{\mathbf{P}}_{{\mathbf{s}}}:{\mathcal{H}}\longrightarrow H_{{\mathbf{s}}}
(a rank one projection) and
{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}^{(j)}:{\mathcal{H}}\longrightarrow H_{\ell}^{(j)},
so that
{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathbf{s}}=\prod_{j=1}^{{\mathbf{n}}}{\mathbf{P}}_{s_{j}}^{(j)}.

For each
j,\ell,
the monomials
e_{\alpha}
with
\alpha_{j}=\ell
form an orthonormal basis in
H_{\ell}^{(j)}
. Therefore the projection
{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}^{(j)}
can be written as

({\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}^{(j)}f)(z_{j},\widetilde{z_{j}})=\sum_{\alpha_{j}=\ell}\langle f,e_{\alpha}\rangle e_{\alpha}(z).

(3.8)

With this notation, the Bergman space
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
splits into the orthogonal sum in two ways:

{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})=\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{{\mathbf{n}}}_{+}}H_{{\mathbf{s}}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mbox{for each}\,j=1,\dots,{\mathbf{n}},\qquad{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})=\bigoplus_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}H_{\ell}^{(j)}.

(3.9)

We can see that in the first decomposition, we split the Bergman space into the direct sum of one-dimensional subspaces, in the second one each subspace in the splitting consists of functions having a fixed homogeneity order in the variable
z_{j}
(and analytic in all remaining variables.)

For
f
being a basic monomial,
f(z)=e_{\alpha}(z_{j},\widetilde{z_{j}})
, the projection
{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}^{(j)}
acts, according to (
3.8
),
as the identity operator in
\widetilde{z_{j}}
variables and as an integral operator in
z_{j}
variable. By continuity, this description extends to the whole
H^{(j)}_{\ell}.
Therefore, for
j^{\prime}\neq j
and any
\ell^{\prime},\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
the projections
{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}^{(j)}
and
{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{(j^{\prime})}
commute; taken in any order, their composition is the projection onto the space of functions of the form
z_{j}^{\ell}z_{j^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}h(\widetilde{z_{j,j^{\prime}}}),
where
\widetilde{z_{j,j^{\prime}}}
is the collection of variables complementary to
z_{j},z_{j^{\prime}}
and
h
is a function in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda+\ell+\ell^{\prime}+2}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}-2}).

Now we introduce the
rotation operator
with respect to the variable
z_{j},

V_{j}=z_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}=\frac{1}{\imath}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}},

(3.10)

in the trigonometrical representation
z_{j}=|z_{j}|e^{\imath\theta_{j}}.

This operator is self-adjoint being considered on the domain

{\mathcal{D}}(V_{j})=\left\{f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{{\mathbf{n}}}_{+}}f_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}):\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{{\mathbf{n}}}_{+}}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}|f_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

For a fixed
j
, each element in the space
H_{\ell}^{(j)}
is an eigenfunction of
V_{j}
with eigenvalue
\ell
. The second orthogonal sum decomposition in (
3.9
) implies that finite linear combinations of elements in all
H_{\ell}^{(j)}
are dense in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
.

For any fixed
j,
each function of the form
z_{j}^{\ell}h(\widetilde{z_{(j)}}),

h\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda+\ell+1}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}-1}),
is an eigenfunction of
V_{j}
with eigenvalue
\ell;
linear combinations of all such functions are dense in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
. This means that the spectrum of
V_{j}
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
consists of all nonnegative integer points
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with the corresponding spectral subspace
H_{\ell}^{(j)};
each integer point is an eigenvalue of infinite multiplicity.

It follows that the spectral measures of the self-adjoint operators
V_{j},

j\leq{\mathbf{n}}
commute, with
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
being the joint spectrum. Therefore, the joint spectral measure
E
is supported on the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+}
, while the set
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{n}}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+}
has zero
E
-measure. The corresponding partition of unity is

{\mathbf{E}}(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{\mathbf{n}})=\prod_{j=1}^{\mathbf{n}}\left(\sum_{s_{j}\leq\eta_{j}}{\mathbf{P}}_{s_{j}}^{(j)}\right).

The general spectral theory for systems of commuting operators, presented in Sect.2, applies to
{\mathbf{V}}=(V_{1},\dots,V_{{\mathbf{n}}})
.

Therefore, the Functional Calculus produces

Proposition 3.1
.

Given an
E
-measurable function
\psi(\boldsymbol{\eta})
on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{n}}
, the operator

\psi({\mathbf{V}})\equiv\psi(V_{1},\ldots,V_{\mathbf{n}}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{n}}}\psi(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{\mathbf{n}})\,d{\mathbf{E}}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{\mathbf{n}})

is well defined and normal on its domain

\mathcal{D}_{\psi}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{n}}}|\psi(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{\mathbf{n}})|^{2}\,d\langle{\mathbf{E}}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{\mathbf{n}})f,f\rangle<\infty\right\}.

Moreover the operator
\psi(V_{1},\ldots,V_{\mathbf{n}})
is bounded, and thus defined on the whole
\mathcal{H}
, if and only if the function
\psi
is
E
-essentially bounded.

Representation (
2.4
) implies that

\psi(\mathbf{V})\equiv\psi(V_{1},\ldots,V_{\mathbf{n}})=\int_{\sigma(\mathbf{V})}\psi(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n})\,d{\mathbf{E}}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{\mathbf{n}})=\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+}}\psi({\mathbf{s}}){\mathbf{P}}_{\mathbf{s}}.

Corollary 3.2
.

The set of all operators
\{\psi(\mathbf{V})\}
, defined by (the classes of equivalence) of
E
-measurable essentially bounded functions

, and the mapping

Op_{E}:\,\boldsymbol{\psi}\ \longmapsto\psi(\mathbf{V})=\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+}}\psi({\mathbf{s}})P_{\mathbf{s}}.

defines the isomorphism

Op_{E}:\,\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+})\ \longrightarrow\ \mathcal{R}

(3.11)

of the
C^{*}
-algebras.

Note here that although the operator
V_{j}
acts upon the basis elements in the same way in weighted spaces

for all values of

the domain of

depends on

therefore, the self-adjoint operators

as well as their spectral projections depend on

although this dependence might be not reflected in the notation.

Returning now to Toeplitz operators with separately radial symbols, we see that by (

), the Toeplitz operator

with such

where the scalars

). Thus, by Corollary

,

{\mathbf{T}}_{a}=\psi_{a}(V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{\mathbf{n}}),

where

\boldsymbol{\psi}_{a}=\{\psi_{a}({\mathbf{s}})\}_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+}}

.
In other words

{\mathbf{T}}_{a}=\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+}}\psi_{a}({\mathbf{s}}){\mathbf{P}}_{{\mathbf{s}}}=\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+}}\gamma_{a}({\mathbf{s}}){\mathbf{P}}_{{\mathbf{s}}}.

(3.12)

Theorem 3.3
.

Denote by
{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbb{k}_{\max})
the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by Toeplitz operators with bounded separately radial symbols. Then
{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbb{k}_{\max})
is contained in the image
{\mathcal{R}}
in the mapping (

). In other words, every operator in

is a certain function of the system of commuting operators

Similar to the one-dimensional case,
{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbb{k}_{\max})
does not exhaust
{\mathcal{R}},
this means that there exist functions
\psi
such
that the operator
\psi({\mathbf{V}})
does not belong to the

algebra

, although

is bounded. On the other hand, a Toeplitz operator with an unbounded separately radial symbol

can belong to

Corresponding examples can be constructed on the base of reasoning in
[

]
. Note also that Theorem

implies a compactness condition for Toeplitz operators of the type under consideration. Namely, sice all points of spectrum of

are eigenvalues of finite multiplicity placed at the nods of a lattice, an operator

is compact iff

as

For a Toeplitz operator, such behavior of

4.
Toeplitz operators with radial symbols

The radial symbols correspond to the smallest partition
\mathbb{k}_{min}=({\mathbf{n}})
consisting of only one element. The corresponding isometry group is
{\mathbf{U}}({\mathbf{n}})
. Although this group is not commutative, Toeplitz operators
{\mathbf{T}}_{a}
with symbols in
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbb{k}_{\min})
, commute, see
[
16
]
. Such symbols, invariant with respect to
{\mathbf{U}}({\mathbf{n}})
, depend only on the radius
r=(\sum r_{j}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and therefore they are called ’radial’. We denote by
\mathfrak{A}_{\hbox{\footnotesize{\rm rad}\,}}
the
C^{*}
algebra generated by Toeplitz operators with bounded symbols in
\mathfrak{S}(\mathbb{k}_{\min})
. An extensive description of properties of operators with radial symbols can be found in
[
2
]
. We use these facts to perform the spectral analysis of such operators, following the lines of our paper
[
13
]
, where the case of the Bergman space on the disk
({\mathbf{n}}=1)
was considered. We will see that, similarly to the case of the disk, the spectral representation of the algebra
\mathfrak{A}
uses only one operator
V
.

We define the differential operator (the rotation operator)

V=z_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}+\dots+z_{{\mathbf{n}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{\mathbf{n}}}=\frac{1}{\imath}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{1}}+\dots+\frac{1}{\imath}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{\mathbf{n}}},

(4.1)

in the trigonometric representation
z_{j}=|z_{j}|e^{\imath\theta_{j}}
.
This operator is self-adjoint in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}),
being defined on

{\mathcal{D}}(V)=\left\{f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{{\mathbf{n}}}}f_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}):\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{{\mathbf{n}}}}|\alpha|^{2}|f_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

(4.2)

The operator
V
is diagonal in the basis
\{e_{\alpha}\}
and it acts on an element
e_{\alpha}
in the basis as
Ve_{\alpha}=|\alpha|e_{\alpha}.
Therefore, each nonnegative integer
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is an eigenvalue of
V,
and the multiplicity of
\ell
equals the number of multiindices
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathbf{n}}
satisfying
|\alpha|=\ell
. As well known, this number equals
{\mathbf{d}}_{\ell}=\binom{\ell+{\mathbf{n}}-1}{{\mathbf{n}}-1}.

We denote by
\sc\mbox{L}\hskip 1.0pt_{\ell}
the eigenspace of
V
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\ell
, so
\dim\sc\mbox{L}\hskip 1.0pt_{\ell}={\mathbf{d}}_{\ell}.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}
be the orthogonal (in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
) projection onto
\sc\mbox{L}\hskip 1.0pt_{\ell}.
It can be expressed as

{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}f=\sum_{|\alpha|=\ell}\langle f,e_{\alpha}\rangle e_{\alpha},

(4.3)

where
\langle.,.\rangle
denotes the scalar product in the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}).
By (
4.3
),
{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}
is an integral operator

({\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}f)(z)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}}f(\zeta){\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}(z,\zeta)dv_{\lambda}(\zeta)

with the (degenerate) integral kernel

{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}(z,\zeta)=\sum_{|\alpha|=\ell}e_{\alpha}(z)\overline{e_{\alpha}(\zeta)}=\sum_{|\alpha|=\ell}{\mathbf{p}}_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\bar{\zeta}^{\alpha}

(4.4)

where
{\mathbf{p}}_{\alpha}\equiv{\mathbf{p}}_{\alpha,({\mathbf{n}})}=\frac{1}{\alpha!}\frac{\Gamma({\mathbf{n}}+\ell+\lambda+1)}{\Gamma({\mathbf{n}}+\lambda+1)},

in accordance with (
2.5
).
Since

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=\ell}\frac{1}{\alpha!}z^{\alpha}\bar{\zeta}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\ell!}\sum_{|\alpha|=\ell}\frac{\ell!}{\alpha!}z^{\alpha}\bar{\zeta}^{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell!}\sum_{|\alpha|=\ell}\frac{\ell!}{\alpha!}\prod_{1\leq j\leq{\mathbf{n}}}(z_{j}\bar{\zeta_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}=\frac{1}{\ell!}\langle z,\bar{\zeta}\rangle^{\ell},

(
4.4
) gives

{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}(z,\zeta)=\frac{1}{\ell!}\frac{\Gamma({\mathbf{n}}+\ell+\lambda+1)}{\Gamma({\mathbf{n}}+\lambda+1)}\langle z,\bar{\zeta}\rangle^{\ell}.

(4.5)

The spectral measure for
V
is given by

E_{V}(\Delta)=\sum_{\ell\in\Delta}{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell},

(4.6)

with the corresponding resolution of identity

{\mathbf{E}}_{V}(\eta)=\sum_{\ell\leq\eta}{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}.

(4.7)

Thus, for any
f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
,
the measure
\mu_{f}(\Delta)=\langle E_{V}(\Delta)f,f\rangle
is supported at integer points.

Further on, for an
E_{V}
-measurable, almost everywhere finite complex-valued function
\psi(\eta),

\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{1},
the operator
\psi(V)
is defined as

\psi(V)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\psi(\ell){\mathbf{P}}_{\ell},

(4.8)

with the natural domain

{\mathcal{D}}(\psi(V))=\{f=\sum f_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}):\sum_{\ell}|\psi(\ell)|^{2}\sum_{|\alpha|=\ell}|f_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty\}.

On this domain, the operator
\psi(V)
is normal; if
\psi
has real values at all integer points,
\psi(V)
is self-adjoint; it is bounded iff
\psi
is a bounded function on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Similarly to the case
{\mathbf{n}}=1
considered in
[
13
]
, the operator
\psi(V)
is compact iff
\psi(\ell)\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty.
The explanation of this property is just a little bit more intricate than for
{\mathbf{n}}=1
, since the projections
{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}
are not one-dimensional any more and their rank is not uniformly bounded. Namely, for any given
\epsilon>0
, let
\Delta(\epsilon)
be the set of those
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
where
|\psi(\ell)|>\epsilon.
This set is finite, moreover the spectral projection
E_{V}(\Delta(\epsilon))
is finite-dimensional (here we use the fact that all eigenspaces of
V
are finite-dimensional). Thus the operator
\psi_{\epsilon}(V)=\sum_{\ell\in\Delta(\epsilon)}\psi(\ell){\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}
has finite rank, in particular, it is compact. On the other hand, for
\psi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\eta)=\psi(\eta)-\psi_{\epsilon}(\eta)
, we have
\|\psi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(V)\|\leq\epsilon,
since
|\psi(\eta)|\leq\epsilon
for
\eta\notin\Delta(\epsilon).
So,
\psi(V)
is
\epsilon
-approximated in the operator norm by a compact operator and therefore it is compact itself. The converse implication is also justified rather easily.

For different functions
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
the operators
\psi_{1}(V),\psi_{2}(V)
commute: in the strict sense, for bounded
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},
or in the resolvent sense for more general functions, as in
[
13
]
.

Being applied to the function
\psi=\boldsymbol{1}
,
\boldsymbol{1}(\ell)=1
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},
(
4.8
) gives
\boldsymbol{1}(V)|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}}=\mathbf{Id}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}{\mathbf{n}})},
the identity operator in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}).
On the other hand, if
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
and
f
is orthogonal to all functions in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
, we have
\boldsymbol{1}(V)f=0
since each
{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}f
equals zero. Therefore,
\boldsymbol{1}(V)
is nothing else but the Bergman projection
\mathbb{P}\equiv\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}:{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}).

Let
a(r)=a(|z|)
be a radial symbol on the ball
\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}.
Suppose that
a(r)\in L_{\infty}(0,1).
Following
[
13
]
, we establish a relation between radial Toeplitz operators in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
and functions of the rotation operator
V.

The Toeplitz operator
{\mathbf{T}}_{a}
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
with symbol
a\in L_{\infty}
acts as

{\mathbf{T}}_{a}f=\mathbb{P}af.

(4.9)

In particular, for
f=e_{\alpha},
(
4.9
) gives

({\mathbf{T}}_{a}e_{\alpha})(z)=(\mathbb{P}ae_{\alpha})(z)=\sum_{\ell}{\mathbf{P}}_{\ell}(a(|\zeta|)e_{\alpha}(\zeta))(z).

(4.10)

Now we recall that the multiplication by a radial symbol retains the orthogonality of the basic functions
e_{\alpha}.

Therefore, the operator
{\mathbf{T}}_{a}
is diagonal in the basis
\{e_{\alpha}\}.
As well known (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
), in each eigenspace
\sc\mbox{L}\hskip 1.0pt_{\ell},
the elements on the diagonal of
{\mathbf{T}}_{a}
depend only on
\ell,
in particular, they are equal. It follows that the operator
{\mathbf{T}}_{a}
has block structure with respect to the eigenspaces
\sc\mbox{L}\hskip 1.0pt_{\ell},
with each block of dimension
{\mathbf{d}}_{\ell}
having the form
\gamma_{\ell,a}\mathbf{Id}_{\ell},
this latter symbol denoting the identity operator in
\sc\mbox{L}\hskip 1.0pt_{\ell}.
This is exactly the structure we described above for functions of the operator
V.
Namely, with the radial symbol
a(r),

we associate the function
\psi_{a}

\psi_{a}(\ell)=\gamma_{\ell,a},\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(4.11)

Thus we obtain the description of a class of radial Toeplitz operators.

Theorem 4.1
.

For a radial symbol
a,
the Toeplitz operator
{\mathbf{T}}_{a}
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
is a function of the operator
V
,

{\mathbf{T}}_{a}=\psi_{a}(V).

The operator
{\mathbf{T}}_{a}
is bounded iff the function
\psi_{a}
is bounded on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
;
{\mathbf{T}}_{a}
is compact iff
\psi_{a}(\ell)\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty.

It follows from the expression for
\gamma_{\ell,a}
that for a bounded radial symbol
a(r)
, the function
\psi_{a}
is bounded and therefore the operator
{\mathbf{T}}_{a}
is bounded. The relation
a\Leftrightarrow\psi_{a}
can be extended to a wide class of unbounded symbols
a.
Since the expression for
\psi_{a}
for the ball
\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}
is formally the same as for the one-dimensional complex disk
\mathbb{B}^{1}
(up to a re-numeration), the results in
[
13
]
carry over to the multidimensional case without changes. We describe them here briefly.

On the one hand, bounded Toeplitz operators can be defined for symbols considerably more general than the bounded ones. First, as in
[
13
]
, quite a wide class of symbols with compact support is admissible. Any
a\in L_{1}(0,1)
that vanish in some neighborhood of the endpoint
1
generates a bounded function
\psi_{a}.

Moreover, the same is correct for
a
being a distribution with compact support in
[0,1).
Although the reasoning in
[
13
]
cannot be carried over directly to the multi-dimensional case (some complications in dealing with the point
0
arise), the corresponding reasoning in
[
12
]
works, namely by means of considering the symbol
a
as a distribution in
\sc\mbox{E}\hskip 1.0pt^{\prime}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}),
invariant with respect to rotations of the ball. A class of symbols with support touching the point
1
can be considered as well, including the ones unbounded in any neighborhood of
1
but fast oscillating as
r\to 1
.

On the other hand, it is important to keep in mind that not for all bounded functions
\psi(\ell)
the operator
\psi(V),
even a bounded one, is a radial Toeplitz operator with a bounded radial symbol
a.
The necessary and sufficient condition for this can be derived from
[
7
]
. Namely, the sequence
\psi(\ell)
must be slowly oscillating, see
[
13
]
.

The condition for the compactness of Toeplitz operators, expressed in terms of the function
\psi_{a}
, is given above: it requires that
\psi_{a}(\ell)\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty.
In terms of the symbol
a,
this condition involves a certain (in some mean sense) vanishing of
a(r)
as
r\to 1.

5.
Toeplitz operators with quasi-radial symbols

Now we pass to the algebras of Toeplitz operators with symbols of more complicated structure, namely the ones being radial in separate groups of variables. Each class of symbols is invariant with respect to a certain isometry group of the ball
\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}
described by a partition
\mathbb{k},
as in Section
2.3
. The classes considered in two previous sections serve as extreme cases.

Further on, in this Section, the partition
\mathbb{k}=(k_{1},...,k_{m})
is assumed fixed. A measurable function
a=a(z)
,
z\in\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}
, will be called
\mathbb{k}
-quasi-radial
if it depends only on
r_{(1)}
, …,
r_{(m)}
, where

r_{(j)}=|z_{(j)}|=\sqrt{|z_{j,1}|^{2}+\ldots+|z_{j,k_{j}}|^{2}},\qquad j=1,\ldots,m.

(5.1)

With this definition, such symbols can vary from
separately radial
,
a=a(|z_{1}|,\ldots,|z_{\mathbf{n}}|)
, if
{\mathbf{k}}=(1,...,1)
, to
radial
,
a=a(|z|)
, if
\mathbb{k}=({\mathbf{n}});
these extreme cases have been considered in the previous sections.

Recall, see
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
, that the Toeplitz operator
{\mathbf{T}}_{a}
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})
, with
\mathbb{k}
-quasi-radial symbol
a=a(r_{(1)},\ldots,r_{(m)})
, acts on the basis elements
e_{\alpha}(z)
as

{\mathbf{T}}_{a}\,e_{\alpha}=\gamma_{a,\mathbb{k},\lambda}(\alpha)\,e_{\alpha},\ \ \ \ \alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+},

(5.2)

where

\displaystyle\gamma_{a,\mathbb{k},\lambda}(\alpha)=\gamma_{a,\mathbb{k},\lambda}(|\alpha_{(1)}|,...,|\alpha_{(m)}|)

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{2^{m}\,\Gamma({\mathbf{n}}+|\alpha|+\lambda+1)}{\Gamma(\lambda+1)\prod_{j=1}^{m}(k_{j}-1+|\alpha_{(j)}|)!}\int_{\tau(\mathbb{B}^{m})}a(r_{(1)},...,r_{(m)})(1-|r|^{2})^{\lambda}\prod^{m}_{j=1}r_{\!(j)}^{2|\alpha_{(j)}|+2k_{j}-1}dr_{\!(j)}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\Gamma({\mathbf{n}}+|\alpha|+\lambda+1)}{\Gamma(\lambda+1)\prod_{j=1}^{m}(k_{j}-1+|\alpha_{(j)}|)!}\int_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{m}}a(\sqrt{\rho_{1}},...,\sqrt{\rho_{m}})(1-(\rho_{1}+\ldots+\rho_{m}))^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\times\ \prod^{m}_{j=1}\rho_{j}^{|\alpha_{(j)}|+k_{j}-1}d\rho_{j},

and
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{m}=\{\rho\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}:\rho_{1}+\ldots+\rho_{m}<1\}
denotes the standard simplex,
\rho_{j}=r_{(j)}^{2}
.

We will denote by
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{k})
the
C^{*}
-algebra, generated by Toeplitz operators with
\mathbb{k}
-quasi-radial symbols.

Given a multi-index
{\mathbf{s}}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}
, we define (see e.g.
[
3
, Formula (3.5)]
) the finite dimensional subspace
H_{\mathbf{s}}
in
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})
as

H_{\mathbf{s}}=\mathrm{span}\,\{e_{\alpha}:\ |\alpha_{(j)}|=s_{j},\quad j=1,\ldots,m\}.

This subspace consists of functions which are, for each
j=1,\dots,m,
homogeneous polynomials of order
s_{j}
in the group of variables
z_{(j)}
.
Then, given
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, for any fixed
j,
we define also (see e.g.
[
3
, Formula (3.10)]
) the
infinite dimensional
subspace
H^{(j)}_{\ell}
in
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})
as

H^{(j)}_{\ell}=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\,\{e_{\alpha}:\ |\alpha_{(j)}|=\ell\}.

The latter space is the closure in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})
of the space of polynomials in
z
which are order
\ell
polynomials with respect to the group of variables
z_{(j)}.
With this definition,

H_{\mathbf{s}}=\bigcap_{j=1,\ldots,m}H^{(j)}_{s_{j}}
.
We introduce the corresponding orthogonal projections

{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathbf{s}}:\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})\longrightarrow H_{\mathbf{s}}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad{\mathbf{P}}^{(j)}_{\ell}:\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})\longrightarrow H^{(j)}_{\ell},\quad\mathrm{with}\quad{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathbf{s}}=\prod_{j=1,\ldots,m}{\mathbf{P}}^{(j)}_{s_{j}}.

In this way, the Bergman space
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})
splits into the orthogonal sum in two ways:

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})=\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}}H_{\mathbf{s}}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})=\bigoplus_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}H^{(j)}_{\ell}.

(5.4)

We see that in the second case, each summand consists of functions having the fixed homogeneity order in variables in the group
z_{(j)}
and analytic in the remaining variables.

Further, (
5.2
) implies a decomposition of the Toeplitz operator:

{\mathbf{T}}_{a}=\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}}\gamma_{a,\mathbb{k},\lambda}({\mathbf{s}}){\mathbf{P}}_{\mathbf{s}},

(5.5)

understood in the sense of the strong convergence.

For each
j=1,\ldots,m
, we introduce the rotation operator along the group of variables
z_{(j)}
:

V_{(j)}=z_{j,1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j,1}}+\ldots+z_{j,k_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j,k_{j}}}=\frac{1}{\imath}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j,1}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{\imath}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j,k_{j}}},\quad\mathrm{where}\ \ z_{j,\ell}=|z_{j,\ell}|e^{\imath\theta_{j,\ell}},

(5.6)

which is self-adjoint being defined on

\mathcal{D}(V_{(j)})=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}),f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathbf{n}}}f_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}:\ \sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathbf{n}}}|\alpha_{(j)}|^{2}|f_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

Note that on this domain, the operator
V_{(j)}
admits the representation

V_{(j)}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\ell{\mathbf{P}}^{(j)}_{\ell},

(5.7)

understood in the sense of the strong convergence.

For each
j
the operator
V_{(j)}
acts only upon variables
z_{l}
entering in
z_{(j)}
and is the identity operator in all other variables. Therefore, these operators commute.
For each fixed
j,
the operator
V_{(j)}
has spectral structure similar to the one we described in Sect.
4
for a ball, with the only difference that this ball has dimension
k_{j}
instead of
{\mathbf{n}}.
Therefore,
the spectrum of
V_{(j)}
consists of isolated points
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Namely, the spectral subspace of
V_{(j)}
, corresponding to the eigenvalue
\ell
is spanned by the eigenfunctions of the form

f_{\ell}^{(j)}(z)=z_{(j)}^{\alpha_{(j)}}h(\widetilde{z_{(j)}})

(5.8)

with some multiindex
\alpha_{(j)}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k_{j}},

|\alpha_{(j)}|=\ell
and an analytic function
h
of the complementing variables
\widetilde{z_{(j)}}.

The calculation, similar to the one in (
3.5
) shows that the function
f_{\ell}^{(j)}(z)
of the form (
5.8
) belongs to the Bergman space
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
if and only if the function
h(\widetilde{z_{(j)}})
of the complementing variables belongs to the Bergman space
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda+\ell+k_{j}}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}-k_{j}})
in the ball of a smaller dimension
{\mathbf{n}}-k_{j}
, with equivalence of norms.

Finite linear combinations of elements in different subspaces
H^{(j)}_{\ell}
are dense in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})
, therefore there are no other points of spectrum of
V_{(j)}
. Each point
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is thus an eigenvalue of
V_{(j)}
of
infinite multiplicity
, and
{\mathbf{P}}^{(j)}_{\ell}
is the spectral projection of
V_{(j)}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

The Spectral Theorem representation of the operator
V_{(j)}
is therefore

V_{(j)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta_{j}d{\mathbf{E}}_{j}(\eta_{j}),

where the resolution of identity
{\mathbf{E}}_{j}(\eta_{j})\equiv E_{j}((-\infty,\eta_{j}])
is given by

{\mathbf{E}}_{j}(\eta_{j})=\sum_{\ell\leq\eta}{\mathbf{P}}^{(j)}_{\ell}.

Having done this for all
j=1,2,...,m
, we construct the joint spectral measure on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
using
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
, …,
E_{m}
, as described in Sect. 2. The measure
E
is supported on the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+}
, while the set
\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+}
has zero
E
-measure. Note that, for each
j=1,2,...,m
, we have also

V_{(j)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\eta_{j}d{\mathbf{E}}(\eta_{1},...,\eta_{m}),

where
{\mathbf{E}}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
is the resolution of identity corresponding to the measure
E
,

{\mathbf{E}}(\boldsymbol{\eta})=\prod_{j}{\mathbf{E}}_{j}(\eta_{j}).

The Functional Calculus implies now

Proposition 5.1
.

Given an
E
-measurable almost everywhere finite function
\psi
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the operator

\psi({\mathbf{V}})=\psi(V_{(1)},\ldots,V_{(m)}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\psi(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m})\,d{\mathbf{E}}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m})

is well defined and normal on its domain

\mathcal{D}_{\psi}=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|\psi(\boldsymbol{\eta})|^{2}\,d\langle{\mathbf{E}}(\boldsymbol{\eta})f,f\rangle<\infty\right\},\qquad\boldsymbol{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{m}).

Moreover the operator
\psi({\mathbf{V}})
is bounded, and thus defined on the whole
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})
, if and only if the function
\psi
is
E
-essentially bounded.

Representation (
2.4
) implies that

\psi(\mathbf{V})\equiv\psi(V_{(1)},\ldots,V_{(m)})=\int_{\sigma(\mathbf{V})}\psi(\boldsymbol{\eta})\,d{\mathbf{E}}(\boldsymbol{\eta})=\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+}}\psi({\mathbf{s}}){\mathbf{P}}_{\mathbf{s}}.

Corollary 5.2
.

The set of all operators
\{\psi(\mathbf{V})\}
, defined by (the classes of equivalence) of
E
-measurable essentially bounded functions
\psi
with
\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\psi}}=\{\psi({\mathbf{s}})\}_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+}}\in\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+})
constitutes the algebra
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{k})
of bounded mutually commuting operators in
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}})
, and the mapping

Op_{E}:\,\boldsymbol{\psi}\ \longmapsto\psi(\mathbf{V})=\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+}}\psi({\mathbf{s}}){\mathbf{P}}_{\mathbf{s}}.

defines the isomorphism

Op_{E}:\,\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+})\ \longrightarrow\ \mathcal{R}(\mathbb{k})\,\subset{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}))

of commutative
C^{*}
-algebras.

We return now to Toeplitz operators with
\mathbb{k}
-quasi-radial symbols. By (
5.5
), a Toeplitz operator
{\mathbf{T}}_{a}
, with
\mathbb{k}
-quasi-radial symbol
a=a(r_{(1)},r_{(2)},\ldots,r_{(m)})
, admits the representation

{\mathbf{T}}_{a}=\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}}\gamma_{a,\mathbb{k},\lambda}({\mathbf{s}}){\mathbf{P}}_{\mathbf{s}},

where the coefficients
\gamma_{a,\mathbb{k},\lambda}({\mathbf{s}})
are given by (
5.3
). Thus, by Corollary
5.2
,

{\mathbf{T}}_{a}=\psi_{a}(V_{(1)},V_{(2)},\ldots,V_{(m)}),

(5.9)

where
\psi_{a}
belongs to the equivalence class of essentially bounded
E
-measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, defined by the sequence
\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{a}=\{\psi_{a}({\mathbf{s}})\}_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+}}
, with
\psi_{a}({\mathbf{s}})=\gamma_{a,\mathbb{k},\lambda}({\mathbf{s}})
.

It seems to be useful to enrich the situation by adding some important properties and characteristics of the objects under study in terms of the representation theory.

Our considerations are based upon
[
9
]
, where all definitions, details, and proofs can be found.

Recall, that a unitary representation
\pi_{\lambda}:\mathbf{U}({\mathbf{n}})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))
of the group
\mathbf{U}({\mathbf{n}})
of all
n\times n
unitary matrices on the weighted Bergman space
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})
is given by

\pi_{\lambda}(U)(f)=f\circ U^{-1},\,U\in{\mathbf{U}}({\mathbf{n}}).

For a closed subgroup
{\mathbf{H}}
of
\mathbf{U}({\mathbf{n}})
, we denote by
\pi_{\lambda}|_{\mathbf{H}}
the restriction of the representation
\pi_{\lambda}
to the group
{\mathbf{H}}
. Then, an operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))
intertwines the restriction
\pi_{\lambda}|_{\mathbf{H}}
, equivalently
T
is
{\mathbf{H}}
-equivariant, if

T\pi_{\lambda}(U)=\pi_{\lambda}(U)T,\quad\mathrm{for\ all}\quad U\in{\mathbf{H}}.

We will denote by
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{H}}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))
the algebra of all such intertwining, or
{\mathbf{H}}
-equivariant, operators. It is well known that
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{H}}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))
is a von Neumann algebra.

For our partition
\mathbb{k}=(k_{1},...,k_{m})
, let

{\mathbf{H}}=\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k}):=\mathbf{U}(k_{1})\times\ldots\times\mathbf{U}(k_{m}),

realized as a block diagonal subgroup of
\mathbf{U}({\mathbf{n}})
.

Note that
[
9
, Proposition 4.5]
demonstrates the importance of the first orthogonal sum decomposition in (
5.4
). We give an equivalent formulation of this proposition, in which the summands are defined in terms of basis elements of
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))
, while the original formulation defines them in terms of certain polynomials in
z
.

Proposition 5.3

(
[
9
, Proposition 4.5]
)
.

Given a partition
\mathbb{k}=(k_{1},...,k_{m})
, the isotypic decomposition of the restriction
\pi_{\lambda}|_{\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k})}
is

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}})=\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}}H_{\mathbf{s}}.

(5.10)

Furthermore, this isotypic decomposition is multiplicity-free.

This means that the restriction
\pi_{\lambda}|_{\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k})}
onto each summand
{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathbf{s}}
is irreducible, and each two summands in the above orthogonal sum decomposition are non-isomorphic irreducible
\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k})
-modules. Then by the Schur Lemma, each
\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k})
-equivariant operator preserves isotypic components in (
5.10
), acting on each
{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathbf{s}}
as the multiplication by a scalar operator. This means that each operator

T\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k})}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))
must have the form

T=\sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}}\gamma({\mathbf{s}}){\mathbf{P}}_{\mathbf{s}},

where, recall,
{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathbf{s}}:\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))\rightarrow{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathbf{s}}
is the orthogonal projection.

An equivalent reformulation of
[
9
, Proposition 4.9]
says now

Proposition 5.4

(
[
9
, Proposition 4.9]
)
.

Given a partition
\mathbb{k}=(k_{1},...,k_{m})
, the mapping

\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})\ \longrightarrow\ \mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k})}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))

defined by

\{\gamma({\mathbf{s}})\}_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}}\ \longmapsto\ \sum_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}}\gamma({\mathbf{s}}){\mathbf{P}}_{\mathbf{s}}

is the isomorphism of the
C^{*}
-algebras which are von Neumann algebras at the same time.

The results of Corollary
5.2
and Proposition
5.4
lead now to the following statement.

Proposition 5.5
.

The
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{k})=Op_{E}(\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+}))
coincides with the von Neumann algebra
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k})}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))
of all bounded
\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k})
-equivariant operators. Each operator
T\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k})}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))
is thus a certain function of the commuting operators
V_{1},\ V_{2},\ \ldots,\ V_{m}
,

T=\psi(V_{(1)},V_{(2)},\ldots,V_{(m)}),

where
\psi
belongs to the equivalence class of essentially bounded
E
-measurable functions, defined by the sequence
\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\psi}}=\{\psi({\mathbf{s}})\}_{{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+}}
, with

\psi({\mathbf{s}})=\frac{\mathrm{tr}(T|_{H_{{\mathbf{s}}}})}{\mathrm{dim}\,H_{{\mathbf{s}}}}.

Now instead of a fixed partition
\mathbb{k}=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{m})
, we consider the finite set
\mathbb{K}
of all possible partitions of
{\mathbf{n}}
into positive integers. We introduce the partial order on
\mathbb{K}
as

\mathbb{k}^{\prime}=(k^{\prime}_{1},k^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,k^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}})\ \preccurlyeq\ \mathbb{k}^{\prime\prime}=(k^{\prime\prime}_{1},k^{\prime\prime}_{2},\ldots,k^{\prime\prime}_{m^{\prime\prime}})

if and only if for each
j=1,2,\ldots,m^{\prime}
,
k^{\prime}_{j}
is a sum, after a re-numeration, of certain consecutive elements of the partition
\mathbb{k}^{\prime\prime}
,

k^{\prime}_{j}=k^{\prime\prime}_{p_{j}}+k^{\prime\prime}_{p_{j}+1}+\ldots+k^{\prime\prime}_{p_{j}+q_{j}-1},

or if and only if for each
j=1,2,\ldots,m
, after a reordering,

z^{\prime}_{(j)}=(z^{\prime\prime}_{p_{j}},z^{\prime\prime}_{p_{j}+1},\ldots,z^{\prime\prime}_{p_{j}+q_{j}-1}),

equivalently if and only if
\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k}^{\prime\prime})\subseteq\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k}^{\prime})
.

With respect to this partial order,
\mathbb{k}=({\mathbf{n}})
and
\mathbb{k}=(1,1,\ldots,1)
are the minimal and the maximal elements in
\mathbb{K}
, respectively. Furthermore, this order implies the order by inclusion of the von Neumann algebras
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k})}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))
of the sets of bounded
\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k})
-equivariant operators:

\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k}^{\prime})}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))\ \subseteq\ \mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k}^{\prime\prime})}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))\quad\mathrm{if\ and\ only\ if}\quad\mathbb{k}^{\prime}\ \preccurlyeq\ \mathbb{k}^{\prime\prime}.

Note that the subalgebras
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{k})
of
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{U}(\mathbb{k})}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{B}^{\mathbf{n}}))
, generated by Toeplitz operators with bounded symbols, maintain the same order,

\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{k}^{\prime})\ \subseteq\ \mathcal{T}(\mathbb{k}^{\prime\prime})\quad\mathrm{if\ and\ only\ if}\quad\mathbb{k}^{\prime}\ \preccurlyeq\ \mathbb{k}^{\prime\prime}.

6.
Degenerate elliptic commutative
C^{*}
-algebras

In the preceding sections we have demonstrated that the size of the set of commuting operators involved in the spectral representation of Toeplitz operators with a certain class of symbols depends on the size of this set of symbols: the larger is the algebra of symbols, the more operators are needed for the spectral representation. In this last section we discuss two examples demonstrating to what extent this rule applies to classes of symbols in a more general setting.

The symbols to be consider are characterized by their (essential) dependence only on a part of variables
z_{(j)}
(or only on a non-complete set of their combinations), therefore, we call them
degenerate elliptic
. Such symbols and the corresponding Toeplitz operators were studied, in particular, in
[
1
,
4
]
, see also
[
10
, Section 6]
, where they are present under the name
\beta
-quasi-elliptic symbols.

As before, we rearrange (via an appropriate biholomorphism of
\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
) the coordinates of
z=(z_{1},...,z_{\mathbf{n}})\in\mathbb{B}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
in the most convenient order for the further description. Thus, without loss of generality, we will use the following setup.

Let, as Section
5
,
\mathbb{k}=(k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{m})
be a partition of
{\mathbf{n}}
, with correspondingly
z=(z_{(1)},\dots,z_{(m-1)},z_{(m)})
. We denote by
\mathbb{k}^{\prime}
the partition
\mathbb{k}^{\prime}=(k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{m^{\prime}}),

m^{\prime}=m-1
of the integer
{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{n}}-k_{m}.
The symbols under consideration depend on the first
m^{\prime}
radii
r_{(j)}=z_{(j)},

j=1,\dots,m-1,
where
r^{\prime}=(r_{(1)},\dots,r_{(m-1)}).
The dependence on
r^{\prime\prime}\equiv r_{(m)}=|z_{(m)}|
is realized, in our first example, via the presence of the weight,

a_{w}(z)=a_{w}(r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime})=a\left(\frac{r_{(1)}}{\sqrt{1-|r^{\prime\prime}|^{2}}},\ldots,\frac{r_{(m^{\prime})}}{\sqrt{1-|r^{\prime\prime}|^{2}}}\right).

(6.1)

Here, although all variables are present, the symbol is, actually, a function of
m^{\prime}
variables via the superposition.

In the second example, the symbols are independent of
z_{(m)}:

a(z)=a(r_{(1)},\dots,r_{(m-1)})=a(r^{\prime}).

(6.2)

Of course, both classes of symbols belong to the set of
\mathbb{k}
-quasi-radial ones, so the general spectral representation involving
m
operators
V_{(j)}
is valid. Since each of these classes is considerably smaller than the algebra of
\mathbb{k}
-quasi-radial symbols, one might expect that the spectral representation uses fewer operators.

6.1.
Weighted
\mathbb{k}^{\prime}
-quasi-radial symbols

We consider the algebra
\mathfrak{S}_{w}(\mathbb{k}^{\prime})
of all bounded symbols
a_{w}(z)
in (
6.1
) and call them
weighted
\mathbb{k}^{\prime}
-quasi-radial symbols
.

Since each weighted
\mathbb{k}^{\prime}
-quasi-radial symbol is quasi-radial for our
\mathbb{k}=(\mathbb{k}^{\prime},k_{m})
, the corresponding Toeplitz operator is diagonal,

{\mathbf{T}}_{a_{w}}e_{\alpha}(z)=\gamma_{a_{w},\mathbb{k}^{\prime},\lambda}e_{\alpha}(z),

where, by (
5.3
), for a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{(1)},\dots,\alpha_{(m-1)},\alpha^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{a_{w},\mathbb{k}^{\prime},\lambda}(\alpha)=\frac{2^{m}\,\Gamma({\mathbf{n}}+|\alpha|+\lambda+1)}{\Gamma(\lambda+1)\prod_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}(k_{j}-1+|\alpha_{(j)}|)!(k_{m}-1+|\alpha^{\prime\prime}|)!}

\displaystyle\times\ \int_{\tau(\mathbb{B}^{m})}a_{w}(r_{(1)},...,r_{(m^{\prime})},|r^{\prime\prime}|)(1-|r|^{2})^{\lambda}\prod^{m-1}_{j=1}{r_{(j)}}^{2|\alpha_{(j)}|+2k_{j}-1}dr_{(j)}{r^{\prime\prime}}^{2|\alpha^{\prime\prime}+2k_{m}-1}dr^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{2^{m}\,\Gamma({\mathbf{n}}+|\alpha|+\lambda+1)}{\Gamma(\lambda+1)\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(k_{j}-1+|\alpha_{(j)}|)!(k_{m}-1+|\alpha^{\prime\prime})!}\int_{0}^{1}{r^{\prime\prime}}^{2|\alpha^{\prime\prime}|+2k_{m}-1}dr^{\prime\prime}\,\cdot\,I,

with

I=\int_{|r^{\prime}|^{2}<1-|r^{\prime\prime}|^{2}}a_{w}(r_{(1)},...,r_{(m-1)},|r^{\prime\prime}|)(1-|r^{\prime}|^{2}-|r^{\prime\prime}|^{2})^{\lambda}\prod^{m-1}_{j=1}{r_{(j)}}^{2|\alpha_{(j)}|+2k_{j}-1}dr_{(j)}.

Making the change of variables
r_{(j)}=\sqrt{1-|r^{\prime\prime}|^{2}}u_{j}
,
|u|=\sqrt{u_{1}^{2}+...+u_{m-1}^{2}}
,
\upsilon_{j}=u_{j}^{2}
we have

\displaystyle I=\int_{\tau(\mathbb{B}^{m-1})}a(u_{(1)},...,u_{(m-1)})(1-|r^{\prime\prime}|^{2})^{\lambda+|\alpha^{\prime}|+{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}}(1-|u|^{2})^{\lambda}\prod^{m-1}_{j=1}{u_{j}}^{2|\alpha_{(j)}|+2k_{j}-1}du_{j}

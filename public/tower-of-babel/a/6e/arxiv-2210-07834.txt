Abstract




I contribute this paper on anonymity to honor the birthday of John Crossley. I am not only John’s friend but also his grandson in the academic sense, as my doctoral supervisor was Peter Aczel, a student of John’s. Of course, it is my hope that this attempt to find the logic of anonymity amuses John.

Suppose we have data about some individuals and in the data the values of some attributes are publicly known while the values of some other attributes are sensitive personal data that should be protected from becoming public.
Anonymity
in this context means the impossibility to derive the values of the sensitive attributes from the values of the publicly known attributes, and this impossibility should be true of each individual.

Anonymity has gained notoriety in modern times as data about our actions and choices accumulates in the internet partly unbeknownst to us and partly by our own choice. Usually people wish some data about themselves were private while some other date may be public or is even wanted to be public for publicity reasons. There are different criteria which characterize the degree of anonymity of data. Given data can also be anonymized by different techniques in order to increase its degree of anonymity.

In this paper we take a very simple “atomic” degree of anonymity as our starting place. We axiomatize these atoms
and propose the investigation of first order logic based on these atoms. Considering the vast literature and the huge importance of anonymity our investigation may seem quite modest. However, it is about the
logic
of anonymity, not about how to secure, create or break anonymity.

Definition 1

(Anonymity atom)
.

A team

\forall s\in X\exists s^{\prime}\in X(s(x)=s^{\prime}(x)\wedge s(y)\neq s^{\prime}(y)).

The intuition of

X\restriction x
and still keep the anonymity of
X\restriction y
as there is

But note that the independence atom

Thus ‘anonymity logic’ is a sublogic on inclusion logic. In fact, the two are equivalent

Theorem 3
.

The following are equivalent for any set
\Sigma\cup\{x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muy\}
of anonymity atoms:

1.

\Sigma\vdash x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muy
.

2.

M\models_{X}\Sigma
implies
M\models_{X}x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muy
for every model
M
and team
X
.

Proof.

Suppose first
\Sigma\vdash x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muy
and
M\models_{X}\Sigma
. All the rules (A1)-(A4) preserve truth. Hence
M\models_{X}x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muy
.
Suppose then
\Sigma\nvdash x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muy
. By the validity of (A3) we may assume
x\cap y=\emptyset
. By the validity of (A4) we may assume
y\neq\emptyset
. Let
x=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
and
y=\langle y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\rangle
.
Let
W
be the set of all variables occurring in
\Sigma\cup\{x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muy\}
. Let
M
be a model with at least three elements
0,1,2
and

\begin{array}[]{lcl}X&=&\{s\in\{0,1,2\}^{W}:\bigvee_{1\leq i\leq n}(s(x_{i})\neq 0)\vee\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq m}(s(y_{i})=0)\}\end{array}

First of all,
M\not\models_{X}x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muy
for if
M\models_{X}x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muy
and we let
s(w)=0
for all
w\in W
, then there would be
s^{\prime}\in X
such that
s^{\prime}(x_{i})=s(x_{i})=0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
s^{\prime}(y_{i})\neq s(y_{i})=0
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, a contradiction.
We are left with proving
M\models_{X}\Sigma
. To see this, let
{u\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muv}\in\Sigma
. We may assume, w.l.o.g.,
u\cap v=\emptyset
. Let
s\in X
. We shall construct
s^{\prime}\in X
such that
s^{\prime}(u)=s(u)
and
s^{\prime}(v)\neq s(v)
.

Let us assume first:

{x\not\subseteq u.}

Since
x\cap(W\setminus u)\neq\emptyset
, there is some
x_{i}\notin u
. Either
x_{i}\in v
or
x_{i}\notin v
.

Case 1.1:

x_{i}\in v
, i.e.
x_{i}=v_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Since there are three possible values for
s^{\prime}(v_{j})
, we can make sure both
s^{\prime}(v_{j})\neq s(v_{j})
and
s^{\prime}(v_{j})\neq 0
. Then
s^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
, since
x_{i}=v_{j}
. Thus
s^{\prime}\in X
, whichever way we otherwise define
s^{\prime}
. By construction
s^{\prime}(v)\neq s(v)
. Since
u\cap v=\emptyset
, we are free to let
s^{\prime}(u)=s(u)
, and we are done.

Case 1.2:

x_{i}\notin v
. Let
s^{\prime}(u)=s(u)
and let
s^{\prime}(v)
be such that
s^{\prime}(v)\neq s(v)
.
Since now
x_{i}\notin u\cup v
, we can let
s^{\prime}(x_{i})\neq 0
, and this guarantees
s^{\prime}\in X
. We are done again.

Let us then assume

{x\subseteq u.}

Since
s\in X
, we know that
s(x)
is not all zero or
s(y)
is all zero.

Case 2.1:

s(x)
is not all zero. We let
s^{\prime}(u)=s(u)
. Since now
s^{\prime}(x)
is not all zero, we have
s^{\prime}\in X
whatever
s^{\prime}
is otherwise. So we choose
s^{\prime}(v)\neq s(v)
in an arbitrary way, and we are done.
So let us assume

Case 2.2:

s(y)
is all zero. If
v\subseteq y
, then
{u\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muv}\in\Sigma
implies

x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muy\in\Sigma
, by Axiom (A2), contrary to our assumption. So we may pick
v_{i}\in v\setminus y
. Let
s^{\prime}(v_{i})\neq s(v_{i})
. Now we know
s^{\prime}(v)\neq s(v)
. Since
u\cap v=\emptyset
, we can let
s^{\prime}(u)=s(u)
. Since
v_{i}\notin y
we can let
s^{\prime}(y)=s(y)=0
. Now
s^{\prime}\in X
, whichever way
s^{\prime}
is otherwise defined.

We may combine

Example 4
.

Suppose we collect some data about individuals and we need to publish this data together with the addresses of the individuals. Some individuals may, for privacy reasons, not want their address to be associated with other data about them. For this purpose we allow any individual to declare their address private by setting
{\tt publicity}={\tt private}
. A team satisfies, according to the semantics of implication given in
[
6
]
:

{\tt publicity}={\tt private}\to{\tt data}\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0mu{\tt address},

iff the anonymity constraint
{\tt data}\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0mu{\tt address}
holds (at least) in the part of the team that satisfies
{\tt publicity}={\tt private}
.

Example 5
.

We continue the previous example. A team satisfies

\exists x(\phi(x)\wedge({\tt data}\ x)\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0mu{\tt address}),

iff (possibly multiple) values can be assigned to
x
so that the new values of
x
satisfy
\phi(x)
and, in addition, the anonymity constraint
({\tt data}\ x)\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0mu{\tt address}
(still) holds. In other words, data about the individuals can be updated with data about the attributes
x
without violating the modest anonymity provided by the
\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0mu
-atom.

Our anonymity atom provides the most basic form of anonymity, not sufficient for practical applications. Therefore we strengthen the atom to
k
-anonymity atom. The bigger the
k
, the more anonymity it provides. The idea is that an attribute
y
is
k
-anonymous with respect to some other attributes
x
if any data about
x
in the team is associated with at least
k
values for
y
. Intuitively, my name does not reveal me if there are
k
different persons with the same name as me, and
k
is relatively large. In September 9, 2022, there were 4703 people alive with the last name Väänänen in Finland. Thus I can remain relatively anonymous in Finland even if I reveal that my last name is Väänänen. At the same moment of time there were only 7 people in Finland’s population information system with the last name Crossley, providing much less anonymity if the last name was revealed.

Definition 6

(
k
-anonymity atom)
.

A team
X
satisfies “publishing
x
keeps
y

k
-anonymous” in a model
M
, in symbols

M\models_{X}x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0muy
, if

\forall s\in X(|\{s^{\prime}(y):s^{\prime}\in X\wedge s(x)=s^{\prime}(x)\}|\geq k).

The atom
x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0muy
is called simple, if
|y|=1
.

Note that the criterion is different from

\forall s\in X(|\{s\in X:s(x)=s^{\prime}(x)\wedge s(y)\neq s^{\prime}(y)|\geq k)

but equivalent to

\forall s\in X\exists s^{\prime}_{1}\ldots\exists s^{\prime}_{k}\in X\bigwedge_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(s(x)=s^{\prime}_{i}(x)\wedge s^{\prime}_{i}(y)\neq s^{\prime}_{j}(y)).

x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{1}\mkern 2.0muy
is always true and
x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{2}\mkern 2.0muy
is equivalent to
x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern 2.0muy
.

Definition 7
.

The axioms of
k
-anonymity (for varying
k
) are

A1

xy\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0muzu
implies
yx\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0muzu
and
xy\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0muuz
. (Permutation)

A2

xy\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0muz
implies
x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{l}\mkern 2.0muzu

A3

If

, then
xy\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0muz
. (Cancellation)

A4

If
x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0mu
, then
\bot
.

A5

If
x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k_{1}}\mkern 2.0muy_{1}
,
xy_{1}\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k_{2}}\mkern 2.0muy_{2},\ldots,xy_{1}...y_{n-1}\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k_{n}}\mkern 2.0muy_{n}
, then

x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot k_{n}}\mkern 2.0muy_{1}y_{2}\ldots y_{n}.

From the axioms (or rules) of Definition
7
we get a notion

We conjecture that the above axioms are complete in the sense that

.

The following are equivalent for any set
\Sigma\cup\{x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0muy\}
of simple

\Sigma\vdash x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0muy
.

M\models_{X}\Sigma

M\models_{X}x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0muy

.

Proof.

Suppose first
\Sigma\vdash x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0muy
and
M\models_{X}\Sigma
. All the rules (A1)-(A4) preserve truth. Hence
M\models_{X}x\mkern 2.0mu\bm{\Upsilon}\mkern-3.0mu_{k}\mkern 2.0muy

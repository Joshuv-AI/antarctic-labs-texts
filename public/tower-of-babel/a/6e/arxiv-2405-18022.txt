Syzygies of algebraic varieties through symmetric products of algebraic curves
Jinhyung Park \orcidID 0000-0002-9710-9728

Abstract
This is a survey paper on recent work on syzygies of algebraic varieties. We discuss the gonality conjecture on weight-one syzygies of algebraic curves, syzygies of secant varieties of algebraic curves, syzygies of tangent developable surfaces and Green’s conjecture on syzygies of canonical curves, and asymptotic syzygies of algebraic varieties. All results considered in this paper were proven using the geometry of symmetric products of algebraic curves.

Introduction

Throughout the paper, we work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbf{k}
of arbitrary characteristic.
In the late 1960s, Mumford
[
36
]
proved that if
X
is a smooth projective variety and
L
is a sufficiently positive line bundle, then
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(X,L)=\mathbf{P}^{r}
is projectively normal and
I_{X|\mathbf{P}^{r}}
is generated by quadrics. In the early 1980s, Green realized that the classical results on equations defining algebraic varieties should be seen as the first cases of a much more general picture involving higher syzygies. After his pioneering work
[
28
,
29
]
on Koszul cohomology, there has been a considerable amount of interest in syzygies of algebraic varieties. In the early 2000s, Voisin
[
49
,
50
]
resolved Green’s conjecture for general canonical curves using the Hilbert scheme interpretation of Koszul cohomology. Voisin’s method has led to a great deal of progress on syzygies of algebraic varieties over the past two decades. Some of the recent developments were surveyed in
[
16
]
and
[
24
]
. We will discuss further advances centered around the author’s work since those papers.

Let
X
be a projective scheme of dimension
n
, and
L
be a globally generated line bundle on
X
with
r:=h^{0}(X,L)-1
. We usually assume that
L
is sufficiently positive; in particular,
L
is very ample, so it gives rise to an embedding
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(X,L)=\mathbf{P}^{r}
. Let
B
be a coherent sheaf on
X
with
H^{0}(X,B\otimes L^{-m})=0
for
m>0
. By Hilbert syzygy theorem, the finitely generated graded section module

R=R(X,B;L):=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}H^{0}(X,B\otimes L^{m})

over the polynomial ring
S:=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}S^{m}H^{0}(X,L)
has a minimal free resolution

0\longleftarrow R\longleftarrow E_{0}\longleftarrow E_{1}\longleftarrow\cdots\longleftarrow E_{r}\longleftarrow 0,

where

E_{p}=\bigoplus_{q\in\mathbf{Z}}\underbrace{K_{p,q}(X,B;L)}_{\emph{Koszulcohomology}}\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}S(-p-q).

Here
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)
is the cohomology of the Koszul-type complex

\wedge^{p+1}H^{0}(L)\otimes H^{0}(B\otimes L^{q+1})\!\rightarrow\!\wedge^{p}H^{0}(L)\otimes H^{0}(B\otimes L^{q})\!\rightarrow\!\wedge^{p-1}H^{0}(L)\otimes H^{0}(B\otimes L^{q-1}),

and it can be regarded as the space of
p
-th syzygies of weight
q
. Denote by

\kappa_{p,q}=\kappa_{p,q}(X,B;L):=\dim K_{p,q}(X,B;L)

the
graded Betti numbers
of
R=R(X,B;L)
. The
Betti table
of
R
is the following:

0

1

2

~\cdots~

0

\kappa_{0,0}

\kappa_{1,0}

\kappa_{2,0}

~\cdots~

1

\kappa_{0,1}

\kappa_{1,1}

\kappa_{2,1}

~\cdots~

2

\kappa_{0,2}

\kappa_{1,2}

\kappa_{2,2}

~\cdots~

\vdots

\vdots

\vdots

\vdots

The width of the Betti table of
R
is the
projective dimension

\operatorname{pd}(R)
. By Auslander–Buchsbaum formula,
\operatorname{pd}(R)=r-\operatorname{depth}(R)
. The height of the Betti table of
R
is the
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity

\operatorname{reg}(R)
(see
(
35
, Subsection 1.8)
).

Our main concern is the overall shape of the Betti table of
R(X,B;L)
when
L
is sufficiently positive. Specifically, we investigate from a geometric perspective when
\kappa_{p,q}(X,B;L)
are vanishing and what their values are if they are not vanishing. In this paper, we always assume that
H^{i}(X,B\otimes L^{m})=0
for
i>0
and
m>0
. Then
\operatorname{reg}(R)\leq n+1
, so
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=0
for
q\geq n+2
. It is well-known that

K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=H^{q-1}(X,\wedge^{p+q-1}M_{L}\otimes B\otimes L)~~\text{ for $p\geq 0$ and $q\geq 2$},

where
M_{L}
is the kernel bundle of the evaluation map
H^{0}(X,L)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\to L
. If
H^{i}(X,B\otimes L^{m})=0
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
m\in\mathbf{Z}
(i.e.,
R(X,B;L)
is Cohen–Macaulay), then
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=H^{q}(X,\wedge^{p+q}M_{L}\otimes B)
and
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=K_{r-p-n,n+1-q}(X,\omega_{X}\otimes B^{-1};L)^{\vee}
for
p\geq 0
and

. The latter can be easily checked using Serre duality.
For more details, we refer to
(
3
, Chapter 2)
,
(
14
, Section 3)
,
(
41
, Section 2)
.

For a moment, suppose that
B=\mathscr{O}_{X}
. We put
R(X;L):=R(X,\mathscr{O}_{X};L)
and
K_{p,q}(X;L):=K_{p,q}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X};L)
. We know
K_{0,0}(X;L)=\mathbf{k}
and
K_{p,0}(X;L)=0
for
p\geq 1
. For integers
d\geq 2
and
m\geq 0
, we say that
L
satisfies
N_{d,m}
-property
(when
d=2
, we simply say
N_{m}
-property
) if
K_{p,q}(X;L)=0
for
0\leq p\leq m
and
q\geq d
. This notion was introduced in
[
21
]
based on
[
30
,
31
]
(our definition is slightly different but the same when
X\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is arithmetically normal, i.e.,
R=S/I_{X|\mathbf{P}^{r}}
). As
K_{0,1}(X;L)=0
, we see that
R=S/I_{X|\mathbf{P}^{r}}
if and only if
L
satisfies
N_{0}
-property. In this case,
\kappa_{1,d}(X;L)
is the number of minimal generators of
I_{X|\mathbf{P}^{r}}
of degree
d+1
, so
L
satisfies
N_{1}
-property if and only if
I_{X|\mathbf{P}^{r}}
is generated by quadrics.

Generalizing classical results of Castelnuovo, Mattuck, Mumford, Saint-Donat, Fujita on equations defining algebraic curves, Green
(
28
, Theorem 4.a.1)
proved that if
C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g
and
\deg L\geq 2g+1+p
, then
L
satisfies
N_{p}
-property. Green–Lazarsfeld
(
31
, Theorem 1.2)
then showed that
K_{p+1,2}(C;L)\neq 0
when
\deg L=2g+1+p
and
H^{0}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\neq 0
. Thus we obtain the following:

Theorem 1.1
(
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
)

If
\deg L\geq 2g+1
and
H^{0}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\neq 0
, then

K_{p,2}(C;L)\neq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~r-g\leq p\leq r-1.

The vanishing and nonvanishing of
K_{p,1}(C;L)
is predicted by Green–Lazarsfeld’s gonality conjecture
(
30
, Conjecture 3.7)
. This was verified by Ein–Lazarsfeld
[
15
]
when
\deg L\gg 0
. Recently, Niu–Park
[
37
]
showed the following effective gonality theorem establishing a sharp effective bound on
L
to satisfies the gonality conjecture.

Theorem 1.2
(
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
)

If
\deg L\geq 2g+\gon(C)
, then

K_{p,1}(C;L)\neq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~1\leq p\leq r-\gon(C).

Here
\gon(C)
is the
gonality
of
C
, the minimal degree of a branch covering
C\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
. As Castryck
[
8
]
observed that the gonality conjecture may not hold for a plane curve when
\deg L=2g+\gon(C)-1
, this theorem is the best possible and thus gives a complete answer to the effective gonality problem. By Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, if
\deg L\geq 3g
, then the Betti table of
R(C;L)
is the following:

\begin{array}[]{c|ccccccccccc}&~0&~1&~2&~\cdots&r-g-1&r-g&\cdots&r-\gon(X)&r+1-\gon(X)&\cdots&r-1\\
\hline\cr~0&1&-&-&\cdots&-&-&\cdots&-&-&\cdots&-\\
~1&-&*&*&\cdots&*&*&\cdots&*&-&\cdots&-\\
~2&-&-&-&\cdots&-&*&\cdots&*&*&\cdots&*\end{array}

Here “
*
” indicates nonzero and “
-
” indicates zero. The number of nonzero entries in the last row is
g
, and the number of last zero entries in the row above is
\gon(C)-1
. This reveals the surprising connection between the intrinsic geometric properties of the algebraic curve
C
and the extrinsic algebraic properties of the embedded curve
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
. The syzygies of algebraic curves will be discussed in Section
2
.

The
tangent developable surface
of
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is the union of tangent lines to
C
in
\mathbf{P}^{r}
. There has been a lot of interest in the syzygies of the tangent developable surface of a rational normal curve of degree

because they are the same to the syzygies of general canonical curves of genus
g
. This was independently observed by O’Grady and Buchweitz–Schreyer in 1980s. After 35 years, Aprodu–Farkas–Papadima–Raicu–Weyman
[
4

finally proved the following:

Theorem 1.5
(
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0

or
\geq(g+2)/2
)

If

is the tangent developable surface of a rational normal curve of degree

, then
\mathscr{O}_{T}(1)
satisfies
N_{\lfloor(g-3)/2\rfloor}
-property.

This implies generic Green’s conjecture
(
28

, which asserts that the canonical line bundle of a general curve of genus
g
satisfies
N_{\lfloor(g-3)/2\rfloor}
-property. Recently, it was realized by Park
[

that
T
is a Weil divisor linearly equivalent to
-K_{\Sigma}

\Sigma
is the first secant variety of the rational normal curve of degree
g

1.5
. Furthermore, it was also shown that if
C
is a smooth projective curve of genus

and
L
is a line bundle on
C
with
\deg L\geq 4g+3
, then the tangent developable surface

of

in
\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,L)
is arithmetically normal (
(

, Theorem 1.3)
). The syzygies of tangent developable surfaces and Green’s conjecture will be discussed in Section
4
.

Green’s
(2g+1+p)
-theorem has stimulated further work on
N_{p}
-property, and several analogous statements for higher dimensional algebraic varieties have been established. On the other hand, in 2001, Ottaviani–Paoletti
[
38
]
called attention to the failure of
N_{m}
-property. Putting
r_{d}:=h^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{2},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{2}}(d))-1
, they proved that

K_{p,2}(\mathbf{P}^{2};\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{2}}(d))\neq 0~~\text{ for $3d-2\leq p\leq r_{d}-3$}.

As
r_{d}\approx d^{2}/2
, this shows
N_{m}
-property for
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{2}}(d)
describes only a small fraction of the syzygies of the
d
-th Veronese embedding of
\mathbf{P}^{2}
. It is an interesting problem to describe the overall asymptotic behaviors of
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)
as the positivity of
L
grows. To set the stage, assume that
X
is a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
and
B
is a line bundle on
X
, and let
L_{d}:=\mathscr{O}_{X}(dA+P)
for an integer
d\gg 0
and
r_{d}:=h^{0}(X,L_{d})-1
, where
A
is an ample divisor on
X
and
P
is an arbitrary divisor on
X
. By
(
15
, Proposition 5.1 and Corollary 5.2)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}K_{p,0}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0~\Longleftrightarrow~0\leq p\leq h^{0}(X,B)-1;\\
K_{p,n+1}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0~\Longleftrightarrow~r_{d}-n-h^{0}(X,\omega_{X}\otimes B^{-1})+1\leq p\leq r_{d}-n.\end{array}

(1)

The main issue is then to study vanishing and nonvanishing of
K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})
for
1\leq q\leq n
. Ein–Lazarsfeld
[
15
]
and Park
[
39
,
41
]
established the following:

Theorem 1.6
(
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})\geq 0
)

For each
1\leq q\leq n
, there exist functions
c_{q}(d)
and
c_{q}^{\prime}(d)
depending on
q,d
and
X,B,A,P
with

c_{q}(d)=\Theta(d^{q-1})~~\text{ and }~~c_{q}^{\prime}(d)=\begin{cases}\Theta(d^{n-q})&\text{if $H^{q-1}(X,B)=0$ or $q=1$}\\
q-1&\text{if $H^{q-1}(X,B)\neq 0$ and $q\geq 2$}\end{cases}

such that if
d
is sufficiently large, then

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~c_{q}(d)\leq p\leq r_{d}-c_{q}^{\prime}(d).

For a nonnegative function
f(d)
, we write
f(d)=\Theta(d^{k})
if there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that
C_{1}d^{k}\leq f(d)\leq C_{2}d^{k}
for any sufficiently large positive integer
d
(for convention, we write
f(d)=\Theta(1)
even when
f(d)=0
for all
d\gg 0
). A widely-accepted intuition derived from the case of curves was that syzygies become simpler as the positivity of the embedding line bundle is increasing. However, as was noticed by Ein–Lazarsfeld
[
15
]
, this intuition was misleading since Theorem
1.6
says that almost all possibly nonzero syzygies are actually nonzero. Instead, one may say that the syzygies have a surprisingly uniform asymptotic behavior as the positivity of the embedding line bundle is increasing. Although Theorem
1.6
is for higher dimensional algebraic varieties, the geometry of symmetric products of algebraic curves plays a crucial role in an important step of the proof. The asymptotic syzygies of algebraic varieties will be discussed in Section
5
.

2
Gonality conjecture on weight-one syzygies of algebraic curves

In this section, we assume that
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
.
Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 0
, and
B,L
be line bundles on
C
. We begin with the preparation for the necessary techniques relating
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)
to vector bundles on symmetric products of
C
. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
15
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
37
]
.

For an integer
m\geq 0
, the
m
-th symmetric product
C_{m}
of
C
parameterizes effective divisors of degree
m
on
C
. It is well-known that
C_{m}
is a smooth projective variety of dimension
m
. For integers
m,n\geq 1
, there is an addition map

\sigma_{m,n}\colon C_{m}\times C_{n}\longrightarrow C_{m+n},~~(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\longmapsto\xi+\xi^{\prime},

which is a finite morphism.
The incidence subvariety

D_{m,n}=\{(\xi,\eta)\in C_{m}\times C_{n}\mid\operatorname{Supp}(\xi)\cap\operatorname{Supp}(\eta)\neq\emptyset\}\subseteq C_{m}\times C_{n}

is an effective divisor on
C_{m}\times C_{n}
. If
n=1
, then
D_{m,1}=C_{m-1}\times C
(the map is given by
(\xi,x)\mapsto(\xi-x,x)
) and
\sigma_{m-1,1}=\pr_{1}|_{D_{m,1}}\colon C_{m-1}\times C\to C_{m}
is the universal family over
C_{m}
, where
\pr_{1}\colon C_{m}\times C\to C_{m}
is the projection. Let

E_{m,L}:=\sigma_{m-1,1,*}(\mathscr{O}_{C_{m-1}}\boxtimes L)

be the
tautological bundle
, which is a vector bundle of rank
m
on
C_{m}
with
H^{0}(C_{m},E_{m,L})=H^{0}(C,L)
. Put
N_{m,L}:=\det(E_{m,L})
. If we write
N_{m,\mathscr{O}_{C}}=\mathscr{O}_{C_{m}}(-\delta_{m})
, then
\sigma_{m-1,1}^{*}\delta_{m}=\pr_{1}^{*}\delta_{m-1}+D_{m-1,1}\subseteq C_{m-1}\times C
. Note that
\Omega_{C_{m}}^{1}=E_{m,\omega_{C}}
and
\omega_{C_{m}}=N_{m,\omega_{C}}
. Now, consider the group action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
on the
m
-th ordinary product
C^{m}
of
C
and the quotient map
q_{m}\colon C^{m}\to C_{m}
. There is a line bundle
S_{m,L}
on
C_{m}
which is the
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
-invariant descent of

L^{\boxtimes m}:=\underbrace{L\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes L}_{\text{$m$ times}}.

Note that
q_{m}^{*}S_{m,L}=L^{\boxtimes m}
and
N_{m,L}=S_{m,L}(-\delta_{m})
. By
(
19
, Lemma 3.7)
(see also
(
2
, Lemma 2.4)
), we have

\begin{array}[]{l}H^{i}(C_{m},N_{m,L})=\wedge^{m-i}H^{0}(C,L)\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,L);\\
H^{i}(C_{m},S_{m,L})=S^{m-i}H^{0}(C,L)\otimes\wedge^{i}H^{1}(C,L).\end{array}

Notice that the fiber of
E_{p+1,B}
over
\xi\in C_{p+1}
is
H^{0}(\xi,B|_{\xi})
. Thus
B
is
p
-very ample (i.e., the restriction map
H^{0}(C,B)\to H^{0}(\xi,B|_{\xi})
is surjective for every
\xi\in C_{p+1}
) if and only if
\operatorname{ev}_{p+1,B}
is surjective if and only if
E_{p+1,B}
is globally generated. From now on, suppose that
B
is
p
-very ample and
L
is globally generated. Let
M_{p+1,B}
be the kernel bundle of the evaluation map
H^{0}(C,B)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\to E_{p+1,B}
. The key observation of Voisin (see e.g.,
(
15
, Lemma 1.1)
) is that
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)
is the cokernel of the multiplication map

H^{0}(C,B)\otimes H^{0}(C_{p+1},N_{p+1,L})\longrightarrow H^{0}(C_{p+1},E_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L}).

Thus
H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})=0
implies
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)=0
, and the converse also holds when
H^{1}(C,L)=0
.

We now turn to the gonality conjecture of Green–Lazarsfeld
(
30
, Conjecture 3.7)
asserting that
K_{p,1}(C;L)=0
for
p\geq r-\gon(C)+1
when
\deg L\gg 0
.
By the duality theorem (
(
28
, Theorem 2.c.1)
),

K_{p,1}(C;L)=K_{r(L)-p-1,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)^{\vee},

where
r(L):=h^{0}(C,L)-1
. It is well-known that
\gon(C)-2\geq p
if and only if
\omega_{C}
is
p
-very ample. Thus the gonality conjecture can be restated as if
\omega_{C}
is
p
-very ample, then
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=0
. Niu–Park
(
37
, Theorem 1.3)
established a general effective vanishing theorem for weight-one syzygies of algebraic curves.

Theorem 2.1

Suppose that
B
is
p
-very ample. If

h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\leq r(B)-p-1,

and
q^{*}:=2k+2-q
are introduced by duality theorem (
(
28
, Theorem 2.c.1)
).
By
(
10
, Lemma 3.3)
, it is equivalent to

H^{q^{*}+1}(C_{p^{*}+q^{*}}\times C_{k+2},(N_{p^{*}+q^{*},L}\boxtimes S_{k+2,\omega_{C}})(-D_{p^{*}+q^{*},k+2}))=0.

Let
\pr_{1}\colon C_{p^{*}+q^{*}}\times C_{k+2}\to C_{p^{*}+q^{*}}
be the projection map. By the Leray spectral sequence for
\pr_{1}
, it is enough to check that

H^{i}(C_{p^{*}+q^{*}},R^{q^{*}+1-i}\pr_{1,*}(N_{p^{*}+q^{*},L}\boxtimes S_{k+2,\omega_{C}})(-D_{p^{*}+q^{*},k+2}))=0~~\text{ for $0\leq i\leq q^{*}+1$}.

When
i>0
, the cohomology vanishing follows from Fujita–Serre vanishing since
N_{p^{*}+q^{*},L}
is sufficiently positive. When
i=0
, we consider the fiber of
R^{q^{*}+1}\pr_{1,*}(N_{p^{*}+q^{*},L}\boxtimes S_{k+2,\omega_{C}})(-D_{p^{*}+q^{*},k+2})
over
\xi\in C_{p^{*}+q^{*}}
, which is

H^{q^{*}+1}(C_{k+2},S_{k+2,\omega_{C}(-\xi)})=S^{k+1-q^{*}}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}(-\xi))\otimes\wedge^{q^{*}+1}H^{1}(C,\omega_{C}(-\xi)).

Now, the gonality sequence condition
p\geq e-\gamma^{2k+2-q}(C)+1
can be restated as
\gamma^{q^{*}}(C)\geq p^{*}+1
, which gives
h^{1}(C,\omega_{C}(-\xi))\leq q^{*}
in view of the positivity of
\omega_{C}
. Thus we obtain

R^{q^{*}+1}\pr_{1,*}(N_{p^{*}+q^{*},L}\boxtimes S_{k+2,\omega_{C}})(-D_{p^{*}+q^{*},k+2})=0,

which implies the desired cohomology vanishing for
i=0
.

For the nonvanishing part, it suffices to show that the map

\begin{array}[]{l}H^{q-1}(\wedge^{p+q-1}M_{\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(1)}\otimes\mathscr{I}_{\Sigma_{k-1}|\Sigma_{k}}(1))\longrightarrow H^{q}(\wedge^{p+q-1}M_{\mathscr{O}_{\Sigma_{k+1}}(1)}\otimes\mathscr{I}_{\Sigma_{k}|\Sigma_{k+1}}(1))\end{array}

is nonzero. We can reduce to showing that the map

\begin{array}[]{l}R^{q^{*}+1}\pr_{1,*}(N_{p^{*}+q^{*},L}\boxtimes S_{k+2,\omega_{C}})(-D_{p^{*}+q^{*},k+2})\\
\longrightarrow R^{q^{*}}\pr_{1,*}(N_{p^{*}+q^{*},L}\boxtimes S_{k+1,\omega_{C}})(-D_{p^{*}+q^{*},k+1})\otimes H^{1}(C,\omega_{C})\end{array}

is nonzero. By the gonality sequence condition
p\leq e-\gamma^{2k+2-q}(C)
, which can be rephrased as
\gamma^{q^{*}}(C)\leq p^{*}
, we can find an effective divisor
\xi\in C_{p^{*}+q^{*}}
with
h^{0}(C,\omega_{C}(-\xi))=g-p^{*}
and
h^{1}(C,\omega_{C}(-\xi))=q^{*}+1
. The above map over
\xi\in C_{p^{*}+q^{*}}
is
\operatorname{id}_{S^{k+1-q^{*}}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}(-\xi))}\otimes\delta
, where
\delta
is the Koszul-like map

\wedge^{q^{*}+1}H^{1}(C,\omega_{C}(-\xi))\longrightarrow\wedge^{q^{*}}H^{1}(C,\omega_{C}(-\xi))\otimes H^{1}(C,\omega_{C}).

Since
\delta
is clearly nonzero, it follows that the above map is nonzero.

In view of the effective gonality theorem (Theorem
1.2
), it is a natural problem to establish an effective lower bound for
\deg L
such that the conclusion of Theorem
1.4
holds. Some partial answers and related results are discussed in
(
10
, Section 5)
.

4
Syzygies of tangent developable surfaces and generic Green’s conjecture

For a moment, we assume that
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
. Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 3
. If
\Cliff(C)\geq 1
(i.e.,
C
is nonhyperelliptic), then
\omega_{C}
is very ample and M. Noether’s theorem says that
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})=\mathbf{P}^{g-1}
is projectively normal. Petri’s theorem states that if
\Cliff(C)\geq 2
, then
I_{C|\mathbf{P}^{g-1}}
is generated by quadrics. In the 1980s, Green
(
28
, Conjecture 5.1)
formulated a very famous conjecture predicting that
\omega_{C}
satisfies
N_{(\Cliff(C)-1)}
-property. By the duality theorem (
(
28
, Theorem 2.c.1)
), this completely determines the shape of the Betti table of
R(C;\omega_{C})
. This conjecture is still widely open. However, in the 2000s, Voisin
[
49
,
50
]
resolved Green’s conjecture for general curves. For this purpose, it suffices to exhibit one curve of each genus for which the assertion holds. If
S\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g}
is a K3 surface of degree
2g-2
, then its general hyperplane section is a canonical curve
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g-1}
and
K_{p,2}(S;\mathscr{O}_{S}(1))=K_{p,2}(C;\omega_{C})
. Voisin used the geometry of the Hilbert schemes of K3 surfaces for generic Green’s conjecture. Recently, Kemeny
[
32
]
gave a simpler proof of Voisin’s theorem for even genus case and a streamlined version of her arguments for odd genus case.

Prior to Voisin’s work, in 1980s, O’Grady and Buchweitz–Schreyer independently observed that one could use the tangent developable surface
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g}
of a rational normal curve of degree
g
to solve Green’s conjecture for general curves of genus
g
. Note that
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g}
is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay and it is a degeneration of a K3 surface. A general hyperplane section of
T
in
\mathbf{P}^{g}
is a canonically embedded
g
-cuspidal rational curve
\overline{C}\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g-1}
of degree
2g-2
, which is a degeneration of a general canonical curve
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g-1}
with
\Cliff(C)=\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor
. By the upper semicontinuity of graded Betti numbers,
K_{p,2}(\overline{C};\mathscr{O}_{\overline{C}}(1))=K_{p,2}(T;\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0
implies
K_{p,2}(C;\omega_{C})=0
. For generic Green’s conjecture, it suffices to prove that

K_{p,2}(T;\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq\lfloor(g-3)/2\rfloor$}.

This was finally established by Aprodu–Farkas–Papadima–Raicu–Weyman
[
4
]
when
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
or
\geq(g+2)/2
(see Theorem
1.5
). This important result gives not only an alternative proof of generic Green’s conjecture but also an extension to positive characteristic. This circle of ideas is surveyed in
[
17
]
.

We explain the proof of Theorem
1.5
given in
[
4
]
. We have a short exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathscr{O}_{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\nu_{*}\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{T}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\omega_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0,}

where
\nu\colon\widetilde{T}=\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}\to T
is a resolution of singularities. It is easy to see that

K_{p,2}(T;\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=\operatorname{coker}\big(K_{p,1}(T,\nu_{*}\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{T}};\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))\xrightarrow{~\gamma~}K_{p,1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\omega_{\mathbf{P}^{1}};\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(g))\big).

Let
U:=H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(1))
,
V:=D^{p+2}U
,
W:=D^{2p+2}U
, and
q:=g-p-3
. Here
D^{m}U
is the
m
-th divided power of
U
(see e.g.,
(
4
, Section 3)
,
(
40
, Section 2)
). The authors of
[
4
]
devoted considerable effort to show that
\gamma
arises as the composition

S^{q}V\otimes W\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}_{S^{q}V}\otimes\Delta}S^{q}V\otimes\wedge^{2}V\xrightarrow{~\delta~}\ker(S^{q+1}V\otimes V\xrightarrow{~\delta~}S^{q+2}V),

(5)

where
\Delta
is the co-Wahl map and
\delta
is the Koszul differential. To achieve this, they established an explicit characteristic-free Hermite reciprocity for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-representations, and they carried out complicated algebraic computations. Now,
K_{p,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))
is the degree
q
piece of the
Koszul module

W_{q}(V,W)
, which is defined to be the homology of a complex

S^{q}V\otimes W\xrightarrow{~\gamma~}S^{q+1}V\otimes V\xrightarrow{~\delta~}S^{q+2}V.

Then it is enough to prove that

W_{q}(V,W)=0~~\text{ for $q\geq p$}.

(6)

The vanishing result (
6
) was established in the previous work of the authors of
[
4
]
in characteristic zero and extended to positive characteristics in
[
4
]
.

Park
[
40
]
gave a geometric approach to proving Theorem
1.5
utilizing the first secant variety
\Sigma:=\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},L)
of a rational normal curve
C
of degree
g
, where
L:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(g)
. We remark that Theorem
3.1
holds in positive characteristics when
C=\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Note that
Q:=\sigma(D_{1,1})
is a smooth conic in
\mathbf{P}^{2}
, where
\sigma:=\sigma_{1,1}\colon\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}\to\mathbf{P}^{2}
is the universal family over
(\mathbf{P}^{1})_{2}=\mathbf{P}^{2}
. Let
\widetilde{T}:=\mathbf{P}(E|_{Q})
, where
E:=E_{2,L}
. Then
\widetilde{T}
is linearly equivalent to
K_{B}+Z
, where
B:=B^{1}(L)
and
Z:=Z_{0}=\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}
, and
\widetilde{T}=\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}
when
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
or
\geq(g+2)/2
. Then
\beta_{1}(\widetilde{T})=T
is the tangent developable surface of
v_{g}(\mathbf{P}^{1})\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},L)=\mathbf{P}^{g}
, and
\beta_{1}|_{\widetilde{T}}
coincides with the map
\nu
. One can easily check that
T
is a Weil divisor on
\Sigma
linearly equivalent to
-K_{\Sigma}
. Let
H:=H_{1}
be the tautological divisor on
B=\mathbf{P}(E)
. We can realize (
5
) as maps induced in cohomology of vector bundles on
B
:

H^{1}(\widetilde{T},\wedge^{p+2}M_{H}|_{\widetilde{T}})\xrightarrow{~\alpha~}H^{2}(B,\wedge^{p+2}M_{H}\otimes\omega_{B}(Z))\xrightarrow{~\delta~}H^{2}(Z,\omega_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}\boxtimes\wedge^{p+2}M_{L}\otimes\omega_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}).

It is easy to check that
\alpha=\operatorname{id}_{S^{q}V}\otimes\Delta
, where
\Delta
is the dual of the restriction map
H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{2},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{2}}(p+1))\rightarrow H^{0}(Q,\mathscr{O}_{Q}(p+1))
. Put
M_{E}:=M_{2,L}
. The map
\delta
is naturally factored as

\textstyle{H^{2}(\wedge^{p+2}M_{H}\otimes\omega_{B}(Z))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{id}_{S^{q}V}\otimes\iota}
\textstyle{H^{2}(\sigma^{*}\wedge^{p+2}M_{E}\otimes(\omega_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}\boxtimes\omega_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{m\otimes\operatorname{id}_{V}}
\textstyle{H^{2}(\omega_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}\boxtimes\wedge^{p+2}M_{L}\otimes\omega_{\mathbf{P}^{1}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S^{q}V\otimes\wedge^{2}V}
\textstyle{S^{q}V\otimes V\otimes V}
\textstyle{S^{q+1}V\otimes V,}

where
\iota
is the canonical injection and
m
is the multiplication map identified with

H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{p+2}\times\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{p+2}}(q)\boxtimes\omega_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-p-2))\longrightarrow H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{p+2}\times\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{p+2}}(q+1)\boxtimes\omega_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}).

This gives a quick verification of the descriptions of the maps in (
5
). Next, regarding
V=H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{p+2},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{p+2}}(1))
, we can give a direct proof of (
6
) as follows.
We put
M_{V}:=M_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{p+2}}(1)}
. Then (
5
) can be identified with

W\otimes H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{p+2},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{p+2}}(q))\longrightarrow\wedge^{2}V\otimes H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{p+2},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{p+2}}(q))\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{p+2},M_{V}(q+1)).

We can show that
W\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{p+2}}\to M_{V}(1)
is surjective and its kernel is
(p+1)
-regular. This implies (
6
). For this last part, we use the characteristic assumption
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
or
\geq(g+2)/2
.
It is worth noting that the characteristic assumption cannot be improved. Indeed, if
2\leq\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})\leq(g+1)/2
, then
K_{\lfloor(g-3)/2\rfloor,2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))\neq 0
(see
(
4
, Remark 5.17)
).

Concerning the characteristic of the base field, Raicu–Sam
(
43
, Theorem 1.5)
obtained a sharp result: Green’s conjecture holds for general curves of genus
g
when
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
or
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})\geq\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor
. This confirms a conjecture of Eisenbud–Schreyer.
It has long been known that generic Green’s conjecture would follow from the canonical ribbon conjecture. A
canonical ribbon
is a certain double structure on a rational normal curve, and it can be realized as a hyperplane section of a
K3 carpet

X=X(a,b)\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{a+b+1}
for integers
b\geq a\geq 1
, which is a unique double structure on a rational normal surface scroll
S(a,b)=\mathbf{P}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(a)\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(b))\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{a+b+1}
such that
\omega_{X}=\mathscr{O}_{X}
and
h^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})=0
. Then
X
is a degeneration of a K3 surface of degree
2(a+b)
, and its hyperplane section (a canonical ribbon) is a degeneration of a general canonical curve
C
of genus
a+b+1
with
\Cliff(C)=a
. By extending algebraic arguments of
[
4
]
, Raicu–Sam
(
43
, Theorem 1.1)
proved that
\mathscr{O}_{X}(1)
satisfies
N_{(a-1)}
-property when
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
or
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})\geq a
. For Eisenbud–Schreyer’s conjecture, we only need to consider the case
a=\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor
and
b=\lfloor g/2\rfloor
. In this case, we take a general double line
\ell\in|Q|=|\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{2}}(2)|
. Then
\beta_{1}(\mathbf{P}(E|_{\ell}))=X(\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor,\lfloor g/2\rfloor)\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g}
is a K3 carpet. Along the same way as the geometric proof of Theorem
1.5
, we can obtain a simple geometric proof of Raicu–Sam’s result
(
43
, Theorem 1.1)
.

Theorem 4.1
(
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0

or
\geq\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor
and
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})\neq 2
)

Consider a K3 carpet
X=X(\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor,\lfloor g/2\rfloor)\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{g}
with
g\geq 3
. Then
\mathscr{O}_{X}(1)
satisfies
N_{\lfloor(g-3)/2\rfloor}
-property.

It is quite natural to study syzygies of tangent developable surfaces of arbitrary smooth projective curves of genus
g\geq 1
. As a first step, Park
(
40
, Theorem 1.3)
showed the arithmetic normality of tangent developable surfaces.

Theorem 4.2
(
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
)

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 1
, and
L
be a line bundle on
C
with
\deg L\geq 4g+3
. Consider the tangent developable surface
T
of
C
embedded in
\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,L)=\mathbf{P}^{r}
. Then
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is arithmetically normal but not arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay, and
H^{i}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T}(m))=0
for
i>0,m>0
but
H^{1}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T})\neq 0,H^{2}(T,\mathscr{O}_{T})\neq 0
.

To prove the theorem, regarding
T
as a Weil divisor on the first secant variety of
C
in
\mathbf{P}^{r}
, we transfer the problem to cohomology vanishing of vector bundles on the second symmetric (or ordinary) product of
C
. The most difficult part is to check the
2
-normality of
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
, which is turned out to be equivalent to the cohomology vanishing
H^{1}(C\times C,(L\boxtimes L)(-3D_{1,1}))=0
. This was previously established by Bertram–Ein–Lazarsfeld
[
6
]
when
\deg L\geq 4g+3
. The degree condition in Theorem
4.2
is optimal. If
\deg L=4g+2
, then
T\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is arithmetically normal if and only if
C
is neither elliptic nor hyperelliptic (see
(
40
, Remark 4.4)
). Some relevant conjectures and examples on syzygies of tangent developable surfaces are presented in
(
40
, Section 4)
. For example, it was conjectured that if
g=1
, then
K_{p,1}(T;\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0
for
p\geq\lfloor(\deg L-3)/2\rfloor
and
K_{p,2}(T;\mathscr{O}_{T}(1))=0
for
0\leq p\leq\lfloor(\deg L-7)/3\rfloor
. In this case,
T
is a degeneration of an abelian surface, so this conjecture may have some applications to syzygies of general abelian surfaces.

5
Asymptotic syzygies of algebraic varieties

In this section, we assume that
\operatorname{char}(\mathbf{k})\geq 0
. Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional smooth projective variety,
B
be a line bundle on
X
, and
L_{d}:=\mathscr{O}_{X}(dA+P)
for an integer
d\gg 0
, where
A
is an ample divisor and
P
is an arbitrary divisor on
X
. Put
R_{d}:=R(X,B;L_{d})
and
r_{d}:=h^{0}(X,L_{d})-1
. Assume that
d\gg 0
. Then
r_{d}=\Theta(d^{n})
, and
H^{i}(X,B\otimes L^{m})=0
for
i>0
and
m>0
. Thus
\operatorname{reg}(R_{d})\leq n+1
, so
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=0
for
q\geq n+2
.
We want to determine vanishing and nonvanishing of
K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})
for
0\leq q\leq n+1
. By (
1
) in the introduction, we only need to focus on the cases of
1\leq q\leq n
. Motivated by the nonvanishing results of
[
21
,
38
]
, Ein–Lazarsfeld
(
15
, Theorem 4.1)
established the following:

Theorem 5.1
(Asymptotic Nonvanishing Theorem)

For each
1\leq q\leq n
, there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that if
d
is sufficiently large, then

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0~~\text{ for $C_{1}d^{q-1}\leq p\leq r_{d}-C_{2}d^{n-1}$}.

The influential paper
[
15
]
opens the door to research on the asymptotic behaviors of
K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})
for
d
increasing. It is natural to ask whether
K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})=0
for the values of
p
outside the range in Theorem
5.1
. Based on Raicu’s result
(
42
, Appendix)
, Park
(
39
, Theorem 1.1)
proved the following theorem, which was previously conjectured by Ein–Lazarsfeld
(
15
, Conjecture 7.1)
.

Theorem 5.2
(Asymptotic Vanishing Theorem)

For each
1\leq q\leq n
, there is a constant
C_{3}>0
such that if
d
is sufficiently large, then

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0~~\text{ for $p\leq C_{3}d^{q-1}$}.

Park
[
41
]
subsequently realized that the asymptotic vanishing theorem leads to a quick proof of the more precise version of the asymptotic nonvanishing theorem, that is Theorem
1.6
: for each
1\leq q\leq n
, there are functions
c_{q}(d)
and
c_{q}^{\prime}(d)
with

c_{q}(d)=\Theta(d^{q-1})~~\text{ and }~~c_{q}^{\prime}(d)=\begin{cases}\Theta(d^{n-q})&\text{if $H^{q-1}(X,B)=0$ or $q=1$}\\
q-1&\text{if $H^{q-1}(X,B)\neq 0$ and $q\geq 2$}\end{cases}

(7)

such that if
d
is sufficiently large, then

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~c_{q}(d)\leq p\leq r_{d}-c_{q}^{\prime}(d).

It is natural to wonder whether
c_{q}(d)
and
c_{q}^{\prime}(d)
are polynomials in
d
when
d\gg 0
(see
(
41
, Question 5.5)
). It seems very challenging even for
X=\mathbf{P}^{n}
(cf.
(
16
, Conjecture 2.3)
). However, the answer is known for
q=1
by Yang
(
51
, Theorem 1)
. Furthermore, Ein–Lazarsfeld–Yang
(
18
, Theorem A)
proved that if
B
is
p
-jet very ample, then
c_{1}(d)\geq p+1
, and Agostini
(
2
, Theorem A)
proved that if
B
is not
p
-very ample, then
c_{1}(d)\leq p+1
. These results are already highly nontrivial and in fact a higher dimensional generalization of the gonality conjecture. We refer to
(
41
, Section 5)
for more discussion on related open problems.

Following
[
39
,
41
]
, we give an outline of the proof of Theorem
1.6
. First, we prove the asymptotic vanishing theorem for products of projective spaces (see
(
39
, Subsection 3.2)
). Let
Y:=\mathbf{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{P}^{n_{k-1}}
and
X:=Y\times\mathbf{P}^{n_{k}}
. Set

\begin{array}[]{l}B_{Y}:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n_{1}}}(b_{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n_{k-1}}}(b_{k-1}),~L_{Y}:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n_{1}}}(d_{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n_{k-1}}}(d_{k-1});\\
B:=B_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n_{k}}}(b_{k}),~L:=L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n_{k}}}(d_{k}).\end{array}

For simplicity, we put
n:=n_{k}
. Fix
2\leq q\leq n
. Assuming
d:=\min\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\}\gg 0
and
p\leq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k}!)(d^{q-1}+bd^{q-2})=\Theta(d^{q-1})
, we show that

K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=H^{q-1}\big(Y\times\mathbf{P}^{n},\wedge^{p+q-1}M_{L}\otimes((L_{Y}\otimes B_{Y})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n}}(d_{k}+b_{k}))\big)=0.

Regarding
\mathbf{P}^{n}=(\mathbf{P}^{1})_{n}
, let
\sigma:=\operatorname{id}_{Y}\times\sigma_{n-1,1}
, where
\sigma_{n-1,1}\colon\mathbf{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}\to\mathbf{P}^{n}
is the universal family. It suffices to show that

H^{q-1}\big(Y\times\mathbf{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1},\wedge^{p+q-1}\sigma^{*}M_{L}\otimes((L_{Y}\otimes B_{Y})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k}+b_{k})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d_{k}+b_{k}+n-1))\big)=0.

To this end, using the geometry of symmetric products of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
, Park
(
39
, Subsection 3.1)
constructed a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\bigoplus\mathscr{O}_{Y\times\mathbf{P}^{n-1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-n)\longrightarrow\sigma^{*}M_{L}\longrightarrow M_{L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k})}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d_{k})\longrightarrow 0.

Considering the filtration for
\wedge^{p+q-1}\sigma^{*}M_{L}
, we can reduce the problem to

H^{q-1}\big(Y\times\mathbf{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1},(\wedge^{i}M_{L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k})}\otimes((L_{Y}\otimes B_{Y})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k}+b_{k})))\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(a_{i})\big)=0

for
i\leq p+q-1
, where
a_{i}:=i(d_{k}+n)+d_{k}+b_{k}+2n-qn-1-pn
.
By the Künneth formula, it is equivalent to

\begin{array}[]{l}(i)~H^{q-1-j}\big(Y\times\mathbf{P}^{n-1},\wedge^{i}M_{L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k})}\otimes((L_{Y}\otimes B_{Y})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k}+b_{k}))\big)=0~\text{ or }\\
(ii)~H^{j}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(a_{i}))=0~~\text{ for $0\leq i\leq p+q-1$ and $j=0,1$}.\end{array}

Assume
3\leq q\leq n
. We proceed by induction on
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}
. When
j=0
,
(i)
holds for
i\leq p+q-1
by induction. When
j=1
,
(i)
holds for
i\leq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k-1}!(n_{k}-1)!)(d^{q-2}+bd^{q-3})+q-2=\Theta(d^{q-2})
by induction, and
(ii)
holds for
i\geq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k-1}!(n_{k}-1)!)(d^{q-2}+bd^{q-3})+q-1
since
a_{i}\geq-1
. When
q=2
, the argument is slightly different but easier (see
(
39
, Subsection 3.2)
).

Next, we deduce Theorem
5.2
for general case from the same assertion for products of three projective spaces proven in the previous paragraph. This reduction argument was done by Raicu
(
42
, Appendix)
(see also
(
39
, Subsection 3.3)
) shortly after Ein–Lazarsfeld’s asymptotic vanishing conjecture
(
15
, Conjecture 7.1)
was raised.
We can choose suitable positive integers
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
depending only on
X,A,P
and positive integers
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}
grow linearly in
d
in such a way that if

\begin{array}[]{l}A_{1}:=\mathscr{O}_{X}(a_{1}A),~A_{2}:=\mathscr{O}_{X}(a_{2}A+P),~A_{3}:=\mathscr{O}_{X}(a_{3}A-P),\\
\mathbf{P}^{n_{1}}:=\mathbf{P}H^{0}(X,A_{1}),~\mathbf{P}^{n_{2}}:=\mathbf{P}H^{0}(X,A_{2}),~\mathbf{P}^{n_{3}}:=\mathbf{P}H^{0}(X,A_{3}),\\
Y:=\mathbf{P}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbf{P}^{n_{2}}\times\mathbf{P}^{n_{3}},~L_{Y}:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n_{1}}}(d_{1})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n_{2}}}(d_{2})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n_{3}}}(d_{3}),\end{array}

then
L_{d}=A_{1}^{d_{1}}\otimes A_{2}^{d_{2}}\otimes A_{3}^{d_{3}}
and there is a commutative diagram

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbf{P}^{r}:=\mathbf{P}H^{0}(X,L_{d})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
linear subspace
\textstyle{\mathbf{P}H^{0}(Y,L_{Y})=:\mathbf{P}^{N}.}

We may regard
B
as a coherent sheaf on
Y,\mathbf{P}^{r},\mathbf{P}^{N}
.
Since

\begin{array}[]{l}\text{(syzygies of $B$ on $\mathbf{P}^{N}$)}=\text{(syzygies of $B$ on $\mathbf{P}^{r}$)}\otimes\text{(Koszul complex of linear forms)},\end{array}

it follows that

\min\{p\mid K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0\}=\min\{p\mid K_{p,q}(Y,B;L_{Y})\neq 0\}.

By considering the minimal free resolution of
B
on
Y
with
\mathbf{Z}^{3}
-grading, we can deduce Theorem
5.2
(the case of
q=1
is trivial) from the result proven in the previous paragraph. Actually this argument shows that Theorem
5.2
holds when
X
is a possibly singular projective variety and
B
is an arbitrary coherent sheaf on
X
.

Finally, we prove Theorem
1.6
using the asymptotic vanishing theorem (Theorem
5.2
). As in
[
15
]
, the argument proceeds by induction on
n=\dim X
. Let
H
be a suitably positive very ample line bundle on
X
, and choose a general member
\overline{X}\in|H|
. Put

\overline{L}_{d}:=L_{d}|_{\overline{X}},~~\overline{B}:=B|_{\overline{X}},~~\overline{H}:=H|_{\overline{X}},~~V_{d}^{\prime}:=H^{0}(X,L_{d}\otimes H^{-1}).

We have noncanonical splitting

\begin{array}[]{l}K_{p,q}(X,\overline{B};L_{d})=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{p}\wedge^{j}V_{d}^{\prime}\otimes K_{p-j,q}(\overline{X},\overline{B};\overline{L}_{d});\\
K_{p,q}(X,\overline{B}\otimes\overline{H};L_{d})=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{p}\wedge^{j}V_{d}^{\prime}\otimes K_{p-j,q}(\overline{X},\overline{B}\otimes\overline{H};\overline{L}_{d}).\end{array}

There are exact sequences

\begin{array}[]{l}K_{p+1,q-1}(X,B\otimes H;L_{d})\rightarrow K_{p+1,q-1}(X,\overline{B}\otimes\overline{H};L_{d})\xrightarrow{\theta_{p,q}}K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})\rightarrow K_{p,q}(X,B\otimes H;L_{d});\\[3.0pt]
K_{p,q}(X,B\otimes H^{-1};L_{d})\rightarrow K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})\xrightarrow{\theta_{p,q}^{\prime}}K_{p,q}(X,\overline{B};L_{d})\rightarrow K_{p-1,q+1}(X,B\otimes H^{-1};L_{d}).\end{array}

When
q=1
or
2
, the map
\theta_{p,q}
should be slightly modified (see
(
41
, Section 3)
). By Theorem
5.2
and induction on
n
, we can easily get the estimation of
c_{q}(d)
and
c_{q}^{\prime}(d)
in (
7
). Furthermore,
\theta_{c_{q}(d),q}
and
\theta_{r_{d}-c_{q}^{\prime}(d),q}
are nonzero maps. Thus there are

\begin{array}[]{l}\text{$\alpha\in K_{c_{q}(d)+1-j_{0},q-1}(\overline{X},\overline{B}\otimes\overline{H};\overline{L}_{d})$ for some $0\leq j_{0}\leq c_{q}(d)+1$ lifted to $K_{c_{q}(d),q}(X,B;L_{d})$;}\\
\text{ $\beta\in K_{r_{d}-c_{q}^{\prime}(d)-j_{0}^{\prime},q}(\overline{X},\overline{B};\overline{L}_{d})$ for some $r_{d}^{\prime}-c_{q}^{\prime}(d)\leq j_{0}^{\prime}\leq r_{d}^{\prime}$ lifted to $K_{r_{d}-c_{q}^{\prime}(d),q}(X,B;L_{d})$.}\end{array}

Then we can lift
\alpha
and
\beta
to
K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})
for
c_{q}(d)\leq p\leq r_{d}-\Theta(d^{n-1})
and
\Theta(d^{n-1})\leq p\leq r_{d}-c_{q}^{\prime}(d)
, respectively (see
(
41
, Theorem 3.1)
for more details on lifting syzygies from hypersurfaces). This gives the desired nonvanishing for
K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})
as stated in Theorem
1.6
.

We close this paper by recalling open problems on the quantitative behavior of the nonzero graded Betti numbers
\kappa_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})
as
d\to\infty
. Ein–Erman–Lazarsfeld
(
13
, Conjecture B)
(resp. Park
(
41
, Conjecture 5.7)
) conjectured that the Betti numbers
\kappa_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})
are normally distributed (resp. form a unimodal sequence) for each
1\leq q\leq n
. It is also expected that the Betti numbers
\kappa_{p,0}(X,B;L_{d})
are increasing and the Betti numbers
\kappa_{p,n+1}(X,B;L_{d})
are decreasing (see
(
41
, Remark 5.9)
). These have been verified when
n=1
(see
(
13
, Proposition A)
and
(
41
, Proposition 5.8)
) but they are widely open when
n\geq 2
. It seems that these problems are already very challenging even for
X=\mathbf{P}^{n}
. However, from the perspective of Boij–Söderberg theory
[
7
,
22
]
, Ein–Erman–Lazarsfeld
(
13
, Corollary D)
proved that each row of the “random” Betti table is normally distributed.

A symplectic formula of generalized Casson invariants
Shaoyun Bai

Abstract
. Suppose Y Y is an integer homology 3-sphere, Taubes [ 28 ] proved that the number of irreducible critical orbits of the perturbed Chern-Simons functional on Y Y , counted with signs, is equal to the algebraic intersection number of two character varieties associated with Heegaard splittings when the structure group is SU ⁡ ( 2 ) \SU(2) . Taubes’ result established a relationship between gauge theory and the Casson invariant. This article proves an analogous identification result for SU ⁡ ( n ) \SU(n) generalized Casson invariants. As a special case, we show that the SU ⁡ ( 3 ) \SU(3) Casson invariant of Boden-Herald [ 6 ] can be equivalently calculated by taking an appropriate intersection number of Lagrangian submanifolds.



1.
Introduction

In 1985, Casson introduced an invariant for integer homology 3-spheres (see
[
2
,
21
]
) by considering the
\SU(2)
–representations of the fundamental group. Suppose
Y
is an integer homology 3-sphere with a Heegaard splitting
Y=H_{1}\cup_{\Sigma}H_{2}
, then the inclusions of
\Sigma
in
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
induce embeddings of the
\SU(2)
–character varieties of
\pi_{1}(H_{1})
and
\pi_{1}(H_{2})
into the
\SU(2)
–character variety of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
. The character variety of
Y
is given by the intersection of the character varieties of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
as a set. After taking a sufficiently small generic perturbation on the embeddings, one obtains a well-defined intersection number of the character varieties of
\pi_{1}(H_{1})
and
\pi_{1}(H_{2})
. The intersection number is always even (see, for example,
[
26
, Corollary 17.6]
), and the Casson invariant is defined to be half of this intersection number.

On the other hand, let
P
be the trivial
\SU(2)
bundle over
Y
with a fixed trivialization, and let

\mathcal{C}(Y)
be the affine space of smooth connections on
P
. Let
\theta
be the flat connection associated with the given trivialization of
P
. For
B\in\mathcal{C}(Y)
, suppose
B=\theta+b
where
b\in\Omega^{1}(Y,\mathfrak{su}(2))
, then the
Chern-Simons functional

\CS:\mathcal{C}(Y)\to\mathbb{R}

is defined by

\CS(B)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{Y}\Tr\left\lparen db\wedge b+\frac{2}{3}b\wedge b\wedge b\right\rparen.

After fixing a Riemannian metric on
Y
, the formal gradient of
\CS
at
B\in\mathcal{C}(Y)
is equal to
*F_{B}
, where
*
is the Hodge star operator of the given metric; therefore, the critical points of
\CS
are given by flat connections, and the set of critical orbits of
\CS
has a one-to-one correspondence with the conjugacy classes of
\SU(2)
–representations of
\pi_{1}(Y)
. Taubes
[
28
]
proved that after adding a generic perturbation to the Chern-Simons functional, the signed count of its critical orbits is equal to two times the Casson invariant.

A natural question about the Casson invariant is whether it can be extended to more general groups and more general 3-manifolds. This question has been studied intensively since the introduction of the theory, and many constructions are given using either intersection of character varieties arisen from Heegaard splittings or the Chern-Simons functionals. The Heegaard-splitting approach has been studied by Boyer-Nicas
[
7
]
, Walker
[
29
]
, Cappell-Lee-Miller
[
8
]
, and Curtis
[
11
]
.
More recently, Abouzaid-Manolescu
[
1
]
studied the intersection of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
character varieties arisen from Heegaard splittings and obtained a sheaf-theoretic 3-manifold invariant. On the gauge theory side, Boden-Herald
[
6
]
defined an
\SU(3)
Casson invariant for integer homology spheres by introducing real-valued correction terms on the reducible critical orbits. Variations of the
\SU(3)
Casson invariant have also been defined by Boden-Herald-Kirk
[
5
]
and Cappell-Lee-Miller
[
10
]
. Together with Zhang, the author
[
3
]
generalized the construction of Boden-Herald and defined perturbative
\SU(n)
–Casson invariants for integer homology spheres. The construction of
[
3
]
is reviewed in Section
2
.

This article studies the relationship of the gauge-theoretic generalization of Casson invariants in
[
3
]
with Heegaard splittings. We obtain an identification result analogous to Taubes’ theorem
[
28
]
: the quantities entering into the definition of the
\SU(n)
–Casson invariant could all be expressed in terms of quantities constructed using finite-dimensional symplectic geometry. As a special case, we show that the
\SU(3)
Casson invariant of Boden-Herald
[
6
]
is equal to an appropriate equivariant intersection number of character varieties associated with Heegaard splittings.

Now we describe the contents of this paper in more detail. Let
Y=H_{1}\cup_{\Sigma}H_{2}
be a closed, oriented 3-manifold with a Heegaard splitting, and let
G
be a compact Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
In general, the

–character variety of

have singularities. Their appearance put forth difficulties on constructing perturbations and studying intersection theory from the differential-geometric approach. To get around this difficulty, we make use of the
extended moduli space of flat connections

on

introduced by Jeffrey
[
17

and Huebschmann
[
16

to study an equivalent question using equivariant geometry, where

be the Riemann surface obtained from

whose moment map reduction

–character variety of

We are then interested in studying the

and

Theorem 1.1

The equivariant Maslov index

After a further identification between the symplectic areas and Chern-Simons invariants coming into the definitions of

Let

\displaystyle=\sum_{[p]\in(\Phi_{H}(L_{1})\cap L_{2})^{irr}}(-1)^{\mu(D(p))}

\displaystyle-\sum_{[p]\in(\Phi_{H}(L_{1})\cap L_{2})^{red}}(-1)^{\mu_{t}(D(p))}(\mu_{n}(D(p))-\frac{\omega(D(\hat{p}))}{2\pi^{2}}+1).

The reader might have realized that this is parallel to the definition of Casson-Walker invariant

We hope that the symplectic approach used in this paper could help understand structural aspects of the generalized Casson invariants. Indeed, in

2.1.
Some linear algebra

Let

\mathcal{R}(G)\otimes\mathbb{R}

Definition 2.1

Define
\mathcal{R}_{G}

(H,V,\rho)

\rho:H\to\Hom(V,V)

H^{\prime}=gHg^{-1},

\rho^{\prime}(ghg^{-1})=\varphi\circ\rho(h)\circ\varphi^{-1}.

Given a closed subgroup

, let
\mathcal{R}_{G}([H])
be the subset of
\mathcal{R}_{G}
consisting of elements represented by representations of
H
. Any
\sigma\in\mathcal{R}_{G}

Definition 2.2
.

Let
H
be a closed subgroup of
G
. Define

i^{H}_{G}:\mathcal{R}_{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{G}

be the tautological map by identifying subgroups of
H
as subgroups of
G
.

Given a compact Lie group
H
, if

is an irreducible
\mathbb{R}
-linear

–representation, recall that

is said to be of type
\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{H}
, if
\hom_{H}(V,V)\cong\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{H}
respectively.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose
V
is an orthogonal irreducible
H
–representation of type
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}\}
. Given
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, define

to be the dimension of self-adjoint maps on

intertwining with the

d_{V}(r)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}r(r+1)&\text{if $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}$,}\\
r^{2}&\text{if $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}$,}\\
2r^{2}-r&\text{if $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{H}$}.\end{cases}

Definition 2.4
.

Given
\sigma\in\mathcal{R}_{G}
with representative given by
(H,V,\rho)
such that

is an orthogonal

Define the

This definition is independent of the choice of the representative of the equivalence class

. According to Schur’s lemma,

is equal to the dimension of

, of the representation

. The following lemma is
[

.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
\sigma\in\mathcal{R}_{G}([H])
is represented by the triple
(H,V,\rho)

by the construction above. We will study the equivariant intersection theory of a pair of such Lagrangians and define some version of equivariant intersection number which calculates the generalized Casson invariants.

4.
Equivariant transversality of Lagrangian submanifolds

This section develops an equivariant transversality result for Lagrangian submanifolds inside symplectic manifolds equipped with Lie group actions, and defines the notion
equivariant Maslov index
which will be used in the calculation of the generalized Casson invariants.

Suppose
(M,\omega)
is a (not necessarily closed) smooth symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
, where
\omega
is the symplectic form . Let
G
be a connected, compact Lie group that acts on
(M,\omega)
such that
\forall g\in G
,
g^{*}\omega=\omega
. Let
L_{1},L_{2}
be two oriented Lagrangian submanifolds in
M
which are invariant under the
G
–action.

For

, we use
\Orb(p)
to denote the orbit of

under the action of

. Note that
\Orb(p)
could be identified with
G/\Stab(p)
, where
\Stab(p)
is the stabilizer group of
p
under the
G
–action. In particular,
\Orb(p)
is a smooth manifold. A subset
X
of
M
is called

–invariant

; and similarly, a subbundle

of
TM|_{X}
where

is

–invariant is called

–invariant
if

.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
intersect

at p, if for

, we have

.
L_{1}
and

are said to intersect

if they intersect
non-degenerately
at each of their intersection point.

Remark 4.2
.

Using the terminology in
[
24
]
, the above definition is equivalent to saying that

and
L_{2}

have clean intersection along
\Orb(p)
for each

. Note that if

and

are compact and intersect non-degenerately, they only intersect along finitely many

–orbits.

4.1.
Transversality

Suppose

are as above, we prove that

and

–equivariant Hamiltonian perturbation on

We start by fixing some notations from symplectic geometry. Let

Definition 4.3
.

Let

Take

Given any

Let

Recall that for every manifold

We need the following equivariant local version of the Lagrangian neighborhood theorem:

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose

is a
G
–invariant Lagrangian submanifold of
(M,\omega)
, and suppose
\Orb(p)\subset L
.
Let

be a
G
–invariant Lagrangian subbundle that is transverse to
TL|_{\Orb(p)}

G
–invariant open subset

, then there is a

–equivariant symplectomorphism
\varphi
from an open neighborhood of the zero section of

to

, such that

(1)

\varphi
equals the identity map on

(2)

the tangent map of
\varphi
on

sends the fibers of
T^{*}S
to the fibers of
E
on
\Orb(p)
.

Proof.

Because

are transverse to each other fiberwisely as Lagrangian subbundles of

, the map
\beta_{E}:E\rightarrow T^{*}L|_{\Orb(p)}

TM|_{\Orb(p)}\cong E\oplus TL|_{\Orb(p)}\cong T^{*}L|_{\Orb(p)}\oplus TL|_{\Orb(p)},

we can construct a fiberwise

–invariant almost complex structure
J_{p}
on

Extend

to a

–equivariant compatible almost complex structure

be the compatible Riemannian metric. After choosing sufficiently small

[

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
S
be a
G
–manifold and suppose that

is a sufficiently small

–invariant neighborhood of

–invariant Lagrangian

which is transverse to the Lagrangian foliation on

-equivariant
1

.

Proof.

Because

is transverse to the Lagrangian foliation on

induced by cotangent fibers, it is locally represented by the graph of some
1
–form
\theta_{L}
over

\theta_{L}
is closed.

Using the slice theorem, without loss of generality we can assume that

where

is a contractile

is 0. Using the de Rham complex of

is exact.
∎

Now we are ready to establish the equivariant transversality result. Let
A\subset M
be a compact subset such that

is included in the interior of

\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset\mathcal{H}
be a sufficiently small open ball centered at zero, such that for every

.

Suppose

(1)

p\in\Phi_{H_{s}}(L_{1})\cap L_{2}
,

(2)

The linear space

as a
\Stab(p)
–representation represents
\sigma
.

Proposition 4.6
.

The space
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
is a Banach manifold. The projection of
M_{\sigma}
to
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, written as

, is Fredholm with index
-d(\sigma)

Proof.

Let
(H_{s},p)\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
. Let
Q_{p}\subset TM|_{\Orb(p)}
be a
G
–invariant Lagrangian subbundle such that

is transverse to
T\Phi_{H_{s}}(L_{1})|_{\Orb(p)}
and
TL_{2}|_{\Orb(p)}
along

. Let
B_{2}
be a small

–invariant open neighborhood of

in

G
–invariant open neighborhood

of

(1)

There exists a
G
–equivariant symplectomorphism to a neighborhood of the zero section of
T^{*}B_{2}
,

(2)

is tangent to

at

under the above diffeomorphism.

After further shrinking

and

if necessary, by Lemma

is the graph of the differential of a
G
–invariant function on

of

to

such that for each

The map

onto the subspace

For each

from

Corollary 4.7
.

Let

and

be two

such that their intersection is contained in a compact subset of

be a small neighborhood of

in

.Then for a generic perturbation

Proof.

Using Proposition

is a

is a meager subset of

∎

Now we show that for a generic

Choose

,

(2)

(3)

The spaces
\big(T_{p_{i}}\Phi_{H_{s}}(L_{1})\cap T_{p_{i}}(L_{2})\big)/T_{p_{i}}\Orb(p_{i})
as

.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}

. The following statement follows from the arguments in the proof of Proposition

Corollary 4.9
.

4.2.
An equivariant Maslov index

In this subsection, we give the definition of equivariant Maslov index which will appear in the index formula later on.

We recall the definition of the classical Maslov index. Let
(V_{0},\omega_{0})
be a symplectic vector space. For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that
a<b
, let
\mathcal{P}_{[a,b]}(V_{0})
be the space of continuous and piecewise smooth maps

q:[a,b]\rightarrow\{\textrm{paris of Lagrangian subspaces in }V_{0}\}.

Then there exists a map

\mu_{V_{0}}:\mathcal{P}_{[a,b]}(V_{0})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

which is called the
Maslov index
uniquely characterized by the following properties (see
[
9
]
):

(1)

(Affine scale invariance) Suppose
k>0
and
l\geq 0
and let
\Gamma:[a,b]\rightarrow[ka+l,kb+l]
be the affine linear map given by
t\mapsto kt+l
. Then for
q\in\mathcal{P}_{[ka+l,kb+l]}(V_{0})
, we have

\mu_{V_{0}}(q)=\mu_{V_{0}}(q\circ\Gamma);

(2)

(Homotopy invariance) Given
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{[a,b]}(V_{0})
such that
q_{0}(a)=q_{1}(a)
and
q_{0}(b)=q_{1}(b)
, if there is a homotopy between
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
in
\mathcal{P}_{[a,b]}(V_{0})
relative to end points, then

\mu_{V_{0}}(q_{0})=\mu_{V_{0}}(q_{1});

(3)

(Additivity under concatenation of paths) If
a<c<b
and
q\in\mathcal{P}_{[a,b]}(V_{0})
, then the following additive property is satisfied:

\mu_{V_{0}}(q)=\mu_{V_{0}}(q|_{[a,c]})+\mu_{V_{0}}(q|_{[c,b]});

(4)

(Symplectic additivity) Let
(V_{0}^{\prime},\omega_{0}^{\prime})
be another symplectic vector space and suppose
q\in\mathcal{P}_{[a,b]}(V_{0}),q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{[a,b]}(V_{0}^{\prime})
. Then

\mu_{V_{0}\oplus V_{0}^{\prime}}(q\oplus q^{\prime})=\mu_{V_{0}}(q)+\mu_{V_{0}^{\prime}}(q^{\prime});

(5)

(Independence of symplectic framing) Suppose
\phi_{t}:[a,b]\rightarrow\Sp(V_{0})
is a
1
–parameter family of symplectic matrices. Denote by
q(t)=(L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t))
for some
q\in\mathcal{P}_{[a,b]}(V_{0})
and define
\phi_{*}(q)(t)=(\phi_{t}(L_{1}(t)),\phi_{t}(L_{2}(t)))
. Then

\mu_{V_{0}}(\phi_{*}(q)(t))=\mu_{V_{0}}(q(t));

(6)

(Normalization) Equip
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x,y}\cong\mathbb{C}_{z=x+iy}
with the symplectic form
dx\wedge dy=\frac{i}{2}dz\wedge d\overline{z}
. Consider the path of Lagrangians
p(t)
in
\mathcal{P}_{[-\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{\pi}{4}]}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
given by

t\mapsto(\mathbb{R}\{1\},\mathbb{R}\{e^{it}\}).

Then

\mu_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(p|_{[-\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{\pi}{4}]})=1,\mbox{ }\mu_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(p|_{[-\frac{\pi}{4},0]})=0,\mbox{ }\mu_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(p|_{[-\frac{\pi}{4},0]})=1.

There are many ways to construct the map
\mu_{V_{0}}
explicitly. For our purpose later, we use the characterization of
\mu_{V_{0}}
as the spectral flow of certain self-adjoint operators, see
[
9
, Section 7]
. Let
J_{0}\in GL(V_{0})
be a complex structure which is compatible with
\omega_{0}
. Given
L_{1},L_{2}\subset V_{0}
which are linear Lagrangian subspaces, let
L^{2}_{1}([0,1];L_{1},L_{2})
be the completion of smooth maps
f:[0,1]\rightarrow V
with
f(0)\in L_{1},f(1)\in L_{2}
under
L^{2}_{1}
–norm induced by the metric
\omega(\cdot,J_{0}\cdot)
. Let
L^{2}([0,1];V_{0})
be the space of
L^{2}
–maps from
[0,1]
to
V_{0}
. Then the operator

D_{L_{1},L_{2}}:L^{2}_{1}([0,1];L_{1},L_{2})\rightarrow L^{2}([0,1];V_{0})

f\mapsto-J_{0}\frac{df}{dt}

is a real self-adjoint unbounded Fredholm operator with discrete spectrum. The kernel of
D_{L_{1},L_{2}}
is the same as constant functions taking value in
L_{1}\cap L_{2}
.

If we take a path of pairs of Lagrangian subspaces
q(t)=(L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t))_{t\in[a,b]}
in
V_{0}
, we have a family of self-adjoint operators
\{D_{L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t)}\}_{a\leq t\leq b}
. Let
\epsilon>0
be small enough so that
D_{L_{1}(a),L_{2}(a)}
and
D_{L_{1}(b),L_{2}(b)}
do not have any other eigenvalues between
-\epsilon
and
\epsilon
except for
0
.

Proposition 4.10
.

The spectral flow of
{
D_{L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t)}-\epsilon\id\}_{a\leq t\leq b}
is independent of the choice of
J_{0}
and equal to
\mu_{V_{0}}(p).
∎

Now suppose
V_{0}
is a
H
–representation where
H
is a connected compact Lie group, and the
H
–action preserves the symplectic form
\omega_{0}
. Let

V_{0}\cong(V_{0}^{1})^{\oplus a_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus(V_{0}^{m})^{\oplus a_{m}}

be the isotypic decomposition of
V_{0}
. Then each isotypic piece inherits a symplectic structure and the above decomposition is compatible with the symplectic structure.
If
q(t)=(L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t))_{t\in[a,b]}
is a path such that both
L_{1}(t)
and
L_{2}(t)
are preserved under the
H
–action, then

(V_{0}^{1})^{\oplus a_{1}}\cap L_{i}(t)\oplus\cdots\oplus(V_{0}^{m})^{\oplus a_{m}}\cap L_{i}(t)

gives the isotypic decomposition of
L_{i}(t)
for
i=1,2
and
t\in[a,b]
. Write
q_{k}(t)
as the induced path on the isotypic piece
(V_{0}^{k})^{\oplus a_{k}}
for
1\leq k\leq m
.

Definition 4.11
.

The
equivariant Maslov index

\mu^{H}(q)
of the path
q(t)
as above is the element

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\dim_{\mathbb{R}}V_{i}}\cdot\mu_{(V_{0}^{i})^{\oplus a_{i}}}(q_{i})\cdot[V_{i}]\in\mathcal{R}(H).

(4.1)

Remark 4.12
.

The above quantity is well-defined because the
V_{i}
–isotypic piece of
L_{1}(t)\cap L_{2}(t)
would change the Maslov index by a multiple of
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}V_{i}
. Alternatively, we can define
\mu^{H}(q)
using the equivariant spectral flow of the
H
–equivariant operator
-J_{0}\frac{d}{dt}
where
J_{0}
is an
H
–equivariant complex structure on
V_{0}
compatible with

Example 4.14
.

Suppose

is an orthogonal representation of
G
, and suppose
(M,\omega)
is given by the cotangent bundle of

. Let

be a smooth

–invariant function on

with finitely many critical orbits. Furthermore, we assume that
f
is a
G
–Morse function, i.e. the Hessian of

is non-degenerate on
T_{p}V/T_{p}\Orb(p)
if
p
is a critical point of
f
. Let
L_{1}
be the graph of the zero section of
M=T^{*}V
, let
L_{2}
be the graph of
df
. Then it is easy to see that
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
intersect non-degenerately. Let
p\in V
be critical point of
f
such that
H\subset\Stab(p)
. Let

V/T\Orb(p)=(V_{1})^{\oplus a_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus(V_{m})^{\oplus a_{m}}

be the isotypic decomposition of
V
as an
H
–representation. Then for each
1\leq i\leq m
, the Hessian of
f
induces an invertible self-adjoint
H
–equivariant linear map

(V_{i})^{\oplus a_{i}}\mapsto(V_{i})^{\oplus a_{i}}

whose negative eigenspace has dimension denoted by
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}V_{i}\cdot\ind(p,V_{i})
. Define

\ind_{H}(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ind(p,V_{i})\cdot[V_{i}]\in\mathcal{R}(H).

Now consider that we have a pair of critical points
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
of

which do not lie on the same

–orbit and they are both fixed under the action of

. Then we can find a path

in

which is invariant under the

–action connecting
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
. Note that the time-

Hamiltonian flow of the function

is given by

(v,v^{\prime})\rightarrow(v,v^{\prime}+t\cdot df),

where we extend

to a smooth function over

which is constant along each fiber. The trace of the image of

for

defines a map

which has a well-defined Maslov index. It could be shown that

which is a classical computation of Lagrangian Floer homology of cotangent bundles.

Example
4.14
shows that in this simple case, the non-degeneracy of critical points of a

–invariant smooth function is equivalent to the non-degeneracy of intersection between a closely-related pair of Lagrangians. Moreover, the equivariant Maslov index could be computed as a relative equivariant Morse index. We extend these basic correspondences in the next section to infinite dimensions which eventually allow us to interpret the generalized Casson invariants using finite-dimensional symplectic constructions.

5.
The gauge-symplectic correspondence

This section proves the most important technical results of this paper. Given a three manifold
Y
equipped with a Heegaard splitting
Y=H_{1}\cup_{\Sigma}H_{2}
, we show that there is a bijection between gauge equivalence classes of perturbed flat
G
–connections on
Y
and intersection
G
–orbits of Lagrangians in
\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\Sigma^{\prime})
induced by
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
after applying a compatible
G
–equivariant Hamiltonian isotopy. Moreover, the non-degeneracy condition from gauge-theoretic context is shown to be equivalent to the non-degeneracy condition of Lagrangian intersections. We then prove several technical results which eventually reduce the computation of spectral flows on
Y
to the computation of Maslov indices on
\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathfrak{g}}(\Sigma^{\prime})
.

Definition 5.6
.

Let

\Lambda_{\pm 1}(t)

D(t)\begin{pmatrix}a\\
\phi\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}*\dot{a}+d_{A_{s,t}}\phi+d_{A_{s,t}}^{*}\psi-*dX_{s}(A_{s,t})(a)\\
-*\dot{\psi}+d^{*}_{A_{s,t}}a\\
*\dot{\phi}+d_{A_{s,t}}a\end{pmatrix},

where the dot represents taking derivative along the

Remark 5.7
.

The operator

could be written as
\tilde{J}_{\Sigma}(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+S_{A_{s,t}}-dX_{s}(A_{s,t}))
. Using the Lagrangian boundary conditions

defines a self-adjoint operator such that the inclusion map from its domain to the background Hilbert space is a compact operator. Meanwhile,

on the region

As a consequence, we obtain a

. Note that all the discussions above are compatible with the
\Stab(B_{t})
–action, therefore the
H
–equivariant spectral flow

of the family

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\{K_{B_{t},f_{\bf q}}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
and
\{D(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
be as above. Then

Sf_{H}(K_{B_{t},f_{\bf q}})=Sf_{H}(D(t)).

is based on adapting the arguments in
[

]
. The idea is to modify the path of self-adjoint operators to a preferred form to simplify the calculation of the spectral flow.

We start by reducing the calculation of the equivariant spectral flow to the calculation of the ordinary spectral flow. Identifying

with the space of smooth sections of the vector bundle

, for any connection

by the fiberwise adjoint

\mathfrak{g}

as above. Suppose the adjoint action of

on

decomposes

into isotypic pieces

we define the vector bundle

and the vector bundle

where

Let

Correspondingly, we have

\Hom_{H}(V_{i},L_{k}^{2}(\Omega^{0}([-1,1]\times\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g})\oplus L_{k}^{2}(\Omega^{1}([-1,1]\times\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}))\cong L^{2}_{k}(E_{i}|_{[-1,1]\times\Sigma})

and the spaces over the Riemann surface

The symplectic form

As a result, the

for all

. According to Definition

, we have

Lemma 5.9
.

Sf_{H}(K_{B_{t},f_{\bf q}})=Sf_{H}(D(t))
if and only if
Sf(K^{i}_{B_{t},f_{\bf q}})=Sf(D^{i}(t))
for any

. ∎

From now on, we will focus on the operators

and

for a fixed

Definition 5.10
.

An operator

is said to satisfy

if for any

such that

where

is a non-empty open set, we have

.

Lemma 5.11
.

For any
t\in[0,1]
, the operator
K^{i}_{B_{t},f_{\bf q}}
satisfies unique continuation.

Proof.

Let
v\in\ker(K^{i}_{B_{t},f_{\bf q}})
and
v|_{U}=0
where
U
is as above. The operator
K^{i}_{B_{t},f_{\bf q}}|_{[1,2]\times\Sigma\cup H_{2}}
comes from the twisted signature operator therefore is a Dirac operator. By the unique continuation property of Dirac operators, we know that
v
is identically
0
over
[1,2]\times\Sigma\cup H_{2}
. Over the region
[-1,1]\times\Sigma
, the element

defines a curve

in

satisfying the ODE

\tilde{J}_{\Sigma}(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+S_{A_{s,t}}-dX_{s}(A_{s,t}))v(s)=0.

Here we use the same notation to represent the induced operators on
L^{2}(E_{i}|_{[-1,1]\times\Sigma})
.
Since the solution is uniquely determined by the initial value and

, we know that
v|_{[-1,1]\times\Sigma}=0
. The vanishing of
v
over

comes again from the unique continuation property of the twisted signature operator on
H_{1}\cup[-2,-1]\times\Sigma
and the fact that we have already shown that

. Therefore the lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 5.12
.

The operator
K^{i}_{B_{1},f_{\bf q}}-K^{i}_{B_{0},f_{\bf q}}
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}_{k}(E_{i})
.

Proof.

By definition,
K^{i}_{B_{1},f_{\bf q}}-K^{i}_{B_{0},f_{\bf q}}
is the sum of a
0
–th order differential operator depending on
B_{0},B_{1}
and the induced operator on
L^{2}_{k}(E_{i})
given by the difference

DV_{f_{\bf q}}(B_{1})-DV_{f_{\bf q}}(B_{0}).

Use the definition and
[
18
, Proposition 3.5]
, these
2
terms are both bounded by
C(\|B_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{k}}+\|B_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{k}})
where
C>0
is some constant depending on

, so the lemma is proved.
∎

Recall that for a family of self-adjoint operators
\{D_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
, its
resonance set

Z(D_{t})
consists of
t\in[0,1]
such that
\ker(D_{t})\neq\{0\}
. Suppose
t_{0}\in Z(D_{t})
and let
P_{t_{0}}
be the orthogonal projection onto
\ker(D_{t_{0}})
. Then the
resonance matrix

R(D_{t_{0}})
at

is defined to be the map
P_{t_{0}}\dot{D}_{t_{0}}:\ker(D_{t_{0}})\rightarrow\ker(D_{t_{0}})
.

Definition 5.13
.

A family of self-adjoint operators
\{D_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
is said to be
positive
if the resonance matrices are all positive definite. It is said to be
negative
if the family
\{-D_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
is positive.

Using Kato’s selection theorem, if the family
\{D_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
is positive or negative, the set

is discrete.

Lemma 5.14
.

The
1
–parameter family
\{K^{i}_{B_{t},f_{\bf q}}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
defined over
L^{2}_{1}(E_{i})\subset L^{2}(E_{i})
is homotopic relative end points to the concatenation of a positive
1
–parameter family and a negative
1
–parameter family such that each operator in these
2
–families satisfies unique continuation.

Proof.

Using Lemma
5.12
, we can choose some
C>0
such that the norm of
K^{i}_{B_{1},f_{\bf q}}-K^{i}_{B_{0},f_{\bf q}}
over
L^{2}(E_{i})
is bounded above by
C-1
. Consider the families

K^{i}_{+}(t)=K^{i}_{B_{0},f_{\bf q}}+tC\cdot\id

K^{i}_{-}(t)=K^{i}_{B_{0},f_{\bf q}}+C\cdot\id+t(K^{i}_{B_{1},f_{\bf q}}-K^{i}_{B_{0},f_{\bf q}}-C\cdot\id)

for
0\leq t\leq 1
. It is easy to see that
K^{i}_{+}(t)
is positive and
K^{i}_{-}(t)
is negative. The affine homotopy between
\{K^{i}_{B_{t},f_{\bf q}}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
and the concatenation of these
2
families gives the desired homotopy. For the unique continuation property, one proceeds exactly the same as in the proof of Lemma
5.11
by noticing that
K^{i}_{\pm}(t)
could be described by Dirac operators on
H_{1}\cup[-2,-1]\times\Sigma\cup[1,2]\times\Sigma\cup H_{2}
and the kernel of
K^{i}_{\pm}(t)
consists of solutions to an ODE on
[-1,1]\times\Sigma
.
∎

Given a positive or negative family
\{D_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
, we wish to perturb it further so that for any
t_{0}\in Z(D_{t})
, the kernel of
D_{t_{0}}
becomes
1
–dimensional. This is the transversality result established in
[
23
, Proposition 3.6]
. The unique continuation condition stated above guarantees that the proof of
[
23
, Proposition 3.6]
works without change to give the following statement.

Lemma 5.15
.

For the two families
\{K^{i}_{\pm}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
constructed in Lemma
5.14
, one can find

\alpha_{\pm}:[0,1]\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\Hom(E_{i},E_{i}))

which are arbitrarily small such that the following holds:

(1)

\alpha_{\pm}(t)
is supported away from
[-2,2]\times\Sigma
;

(2)

The family
K^{i}_{+}(t)+\alpha_{+}(t)
(resp.

(resp.

(resp.

Proof of Theorem
5.8
.

Let us fix
\alpha_{\pm}(t)
as in Lemma
5.15
and denote
\tilde{K}^{i}_{\pm}(t)=K^{i}_{\pm}(t)+\alpha_{\pm}(t)
. By construction,
\{\tilde{K}^{i}_{\pm}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
defines operators

\tilde{K}^{i}_{\pm}(t):L^{2}_{1}(E_{i})\rightarrow L^{2}(E_{i})

for all
0\leq t\leq 1
which are Dirac operators when restricted to
H_{1}\cup[-2,-1]\times\Sigma\cup[1,2]\times\Sigma\cup H_{2}
and they are cylindrical over
[-2,-1]\times\Sigma\cup[1,2]\times\Sigma
. Therefore, they define Lagrangian subspaces

\tilde{\Lambda}^{i}_{-1}(t)_{\pm}\subset L^{2}(E_{i}|_{\{0\}\times\Sigma})

consisting of restriction of elements in
L^{2}_{1/2}(E_{i}|_{H_{1}\cup[-2,-1]\times\Sigma})
lying in

\ker(\tilde{K}^{i}_{\pm}(t)|_{H_{1}\cup[-2,-1]\times\Sigma}).

Similarly one can construct

\tilde{\Lambda}^{i}_{1}(t)_{\pm}\subset L^{2}(E_{i}|_{\{0\}\times\Sigma})

by considering the restriction to
[1,2]\times\Sigma\cup H_{2}
. We can then construct the operators

\tilde{D}^{i}_{\pm}(t):L_{1}^{2}(E_{i}|_{[-1,1]\times\Sigma};\tilde{\Lambda}^{i}_{-1}(t)_{\pm},\tilde{\Lambda}^{i}_{1}(t)_{\pm})\rightarrow L^{2}(E_{i}|_{[-1,1]\times\Sigma})

similar to the construction of
D(t)
, which define self-adjoint operators using the Lagrangian boundary conditions.

Making
\alpha_{\pm}(t)
smaller if necessary, the spectral flow of the family
\{K_{B_{t},f_{\bf q}}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
is the same as the sum of the spectral flows of
\{\tilde{K}^{i}_{+}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
and
\{\tilde{K}^{i}_{-}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
. Similarly, by concatenating the Lagrangian boundary conditions, it is easy to see that the spectral flow of
\{D^{i}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
is equal to the sum of the spectral flows of
\{\tilde{D}^{i}_{+}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
and
\{\tilde{D}^{i}_{-}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
. Using Lemma
5.9
, it suffices to show that

Sf(\tilde{K}^{i}_{+}(t))=Sf(\tilde{D}^{i}_{+}(t))\mbox{ and }Sf(\tilde{K}^{i}_{-}(t))=Sf(\tilde{D}^{i}_{-}(t)).

We prove the plus version of the above statement and the negative version holds using the same argument. From the definition, we see that

Z(\tilde{K}^{i}_{+}(t))=Z(\tilde{D}^{i}_{+}(t))

therefore it suffices to show that the resonance matrices of
\{\tilde{K}^{i}_{+}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
and
\{\tilde{D}^{i}_{+}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
have the same sign at any
t_{0}\in Z(\tilde{K}^{i}_{+}(t))=Z(\tilde{D}^{i}_{+}(t))
. In other words, we just need to show that the resonance matrix of
\tilde{D}^{i}_{+}(t)
at
t=t_{0}
is positive definite. Let
v\in\ker(\tilde{D}^{i}_{+}(t_{0}))-\{0\}
. Note that
v
could also be viewed as an element of
\ker(\tilde{K}^{i}_{+}(t_{0})).
Let
\chi:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative smooth function supported in
(0,1)
which is strictly positive over
[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}]
. Then
\chi
defines a function on
[0,1]\times\Sigma\subset Y
by composing it with the projection to the
s
–coordinate. This further extends to a smooth function over
Y
in the obvious way. Then for
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, the family

\{\tilde{K}^{i}_{+}(t_{0})+(t-t_{0})\chi\cdot\id\}_{t_{0}-\epsilon\leq t\leq t_{0}+\epsilon}

(5.10)

is
C^{0}
–close to
\{\tilde{K}^{i}_{+}(t)\}_{t_{0}-\epsilon\leq t\leq t_{0}+\epsilon}
and the affine homotopy between them preserves the spectral flow. This is because
\langle v,\chi v\rangle_{L^{2}}>0
and the kernel is
1
–dimensional: if
\langle v,\chi v\rangle_{L^{2}}=0
, the restriction of
v
to the time slice
\{\frac{1}{2}\}\times\Sigma
is
0
thus it vanishes on
[-1,1]\times\Sigma
by uniqueness of solutions to ODE therefore vanishes on whole
Y
by unique continuation of Dirac operators. Accordingly, we can construct the induced affine homotopy from
\{\tilde{D}^{i}_{+}(t)\}_{t_{0}-\epsilon\leq t\leq t_{0}+\epsilon}
to

\{\tilde{D}^{i}_{+}(t_{0})+(t-t_{0})\chi\cdot\id\}_{t_{0}-\epsilon\leq t\leq t_{0}+\epsilon}

(5.11)

preserving the spectral flow. Now notice that because
\chi
is supported in the region
[0,1]\times\Sigma
and the kernels of the new families at time

are all spanned by

, we see that the resonance matrices of these
2
families (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) have the same value at time

. This finishes the proof.
∎

5.3.
An adiabatic limit result

Let
\{A_{s,t}\}_{-1\leq s\leq 1,0\leq t\leq 1}\subset A_{F}(\Sigma)
be a smooth family of flat connections on
\Sigma
. Viewing
\Sigma
as the boundary of
H_{1}\cup[-2,-1]\times\Sigma
and
[1,2]\times\Sigma\cup H_{2}

extends to a flat connection

on
H_{1}\cup[-2,-1]\times\Sigma
and
A_{1,t}
extends to a flat connection
B_{1,t}
on
[1,2]\times\Sigma\cup H_{2}
for all

\{\Lambda_{\pm 1}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
be the same as in the previous subsection. Note that we do not require that

or

comes from the restriction of a

–perturbed flat connection on

Given

, let
\mathcal{H}_{A_{s,t}}
be the kernel of the twisted de Rham operator

In other words,
\mathcal{H}_{A_{s,t}}
is given by the total space of twisted harmonic forms on
\Sigma
with respect to the flat connection
A_{s,t}
. This is a symplectic vector space using the restriction of the symplectic form
\tilde{\omega}_{\Sigma}
. Let

\pi_{A_{s,t}}:L^{2}(\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\Omega^{0}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\Omega^{2}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{A_{s,t}}

be the

Define

\mathcal{L}_{\pm 1}(t):=\mathcal{H}_{A_{\pm 1,t}}\cap\Lambda_{\pm 1}(t),\mbox{ for }0\leq t\leq 1.

Lemma 5.16
.

\mathcal{L}_{i}(t)
is a Lagrangian subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{A_{i,t}}
for

.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathcal{L}_{i}(t)
is isotropic. So we just need to prove that
\dim\mathcal{L}_{i}(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}\dim\mathcal{H}_{A_{i,t}}
. Notice that
(0,\ker d_{A_{i,t}},0)\subset\mathcal{L}_{i}
because this comes from the Lie algebra of the stabilizer group and
\Stab(B_{i,t})=\Stab(A_{i,t})
.

Let
m=\dim\ker d_{B_{-1,t}}
. Recall that
h
is the genus of the Riemann surface
\Sigma
. Fix a base point
p_{0}
on
\Sigma\cong\partial H_{1}
, let
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{h}
be
h

that generates the fundamental group of
H_{1}
. Let
\hat{\mathcal{C}}
be the space of smooth connections
B
on
H_{1}\cup[-2,-1]\times\Sigma
such that
B(\partial_{s})=0
on the boundary. Define

\hol:\hat{\mathcal{C}}\to G^{h}

to be the map given by holonomies along
\gamma_{i}
.
Let
U=(-\epsilon,\epsilon)^{h}
, let
\Phi:U\to\hat{\mathcal{C}}
be a map such that
\Phi(0)
equals
B_{-1,t}
, and that
\ima d(\hol\circ\Phi)
is surjective at zero. By construction,
\ima d\Phi(0)\subset\Omega^{1}(H_{1}\cup[-2,-1]\times\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}
is a

, there is a unique

*v=0
on
\{-1\}\times\Sigma

\ima d\Phi(0)

\ima d\Phi(0)
under the map

is Lagrangian. The same argument works to prove

∎

By definition,
\dim\mathcal{H}_{A_{s,t}}
remains to be the same for all
(s,t)\in[-1,1]\times[0,1]
. For a given

, consider the spaces

consisting of elements

whose value at each

lies in
\mathcal{H}_{A_{s,t}}
. Then we can define a self-adjoint operator

D_{0}(t):L_{1}^{2}([-1,1];\mathcal{H}_{A_{s,t}};\mathcal{L}_{-1}(t),\mathcal{L}_{1}(t))\rightarrow L^{2}([-1,1];\mathcal{H}_{A_{s,t}})

(5.12)

given by

D_{0}(t)\begin{pmatrix}a\\
\phi\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}=\pi_{A_{s,t}}\begin{pmatrix}*\dot{a}-*dX_{s}(A_{s,t})(a)\\
-*\dot{\psi}\\
*\dot{\phi}\end{pmatrix}.

By construction, the family
\{D_{0}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
has a well-defined
H
–equivariant spectral flow

. The following is another comparison result of spectral flows.

Theorem 5.17
.

Let
\{D(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
be the family constructed in Section
5.2
and let
\{D_{0}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
be the family constructed above. Then

Sf_{H}(D(t))=Sf_{H}(D_{0}(t)).

The proof of Theorem
5.17
is exactly the same as the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 6.1]
. We will sketch a proof for completeness. Because we have used connections in temporal gauge, the covariant differentiation
\nabla_{s}
in
[
14
]
is replaced by the ordinary differentiation
\partial_{s}
. Note that the calculation of equivariant spectral flow could be reduced to the calculation of ordinary spectral flow as in Section
5.2
so the
H
–equivariance will be suppressed in this subsection.

The idea is to use the
1
–parameter family of operators

D_{\epsilon}(t):L_{1}^{2}([-1,1]\times\Sigma;\Lambda_{-1}(t),\Lambda_{1}(t))\rightarrow L^{2}([-1,1];\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\Omega^{0}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\Omega^{2}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g})

given by the formula

D_{\epsilon}(t)\begin{pmatrix}a\\
\phi\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}*\dot{a}+d_{A_{s,t}}\phi+d_{A_{s,t}}^{*}\psi-*dX_{s}(A_{s,t})(a)\\
-*\dot{\psi}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}d^{*}_{A_{s,t}}a\\
*\dot{\phi}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}d_{A_{s,t}}a\end{pmatrix}

depending on the parameter
\epsilon>0
, so that the spectral flow of
\{D(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}=\{D_{1}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
could be reduced to the spectral flow of
\{D_{0}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
by passing to the adiabatic limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Fix
t\in[0,1]
. Introduce norms

\|(a,\phi,\psi)\|_{0,\epsilon}^{2}=\|a\|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\|\phi\|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\|\psi\|^{2}

on the space
L^{2}([-1,1];\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\Omega^{0}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\Omega^{2}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g})
and

\displaystyle\|(a,\phi,\psi)\|_{1,\epsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|a\|^{2}+\|d_{A_{s,t}}a\|^{2}+\|d_{A_{s,t}}*a\|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\|\dot{a}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon^{2}\|d_{A_{s,t}}\phi\|^{2}+\epsilon^{4}\|\dot{\phi}\|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\|d_{A_{s,t}}^{*}\psi\|^{2}+\epsilon^{4}\|\dot{\psi}\|^{2}

on
L^{2}_{1}([-1,1];\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\Omega^{0}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\Omega^{2}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g})
using the product metric on
[-1,1]\times\Sigma
.

Sketch of proof of Theorem
5.17
.

The proof breaks into five steps.

Step 1-Elliptic estimate:
There is an
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\epsilon
such that

\|u-\pi_{A_{s,t}}(u)\|_{1,\epsilon}\leq C\epsilon(\|D_{\epsilon}(t)u\|_{0,\epsilon}+\|\pi_{A_{s,t}}(u)\|_{L^{2}})

(5.13)

for
u\in L_{1}^{2}([-1,1]\times\Sigma;\Lambda_{-1}(t),\Lambda_{1}(t))
.

This is
[
14
, Lemma 7.3]
. Note that although our
3
–manifold
[-1,1]\times\Sigma
has boundary, the Lagrangian boundary condition guarantees that the integration by parts formula entering the proof works without change. Meanwhile, our projection map
\pi_{A_{s,t}}
involves the
H^{0}
and
H^{2}
part so the reducible connections would not affect the estimate.

Step 2-Convergence of resolvent set:
For every
C_{0}>0
there exists constants
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that the following holds for all
t\in[0,1]
and
|\lambda|\leq C_{0}
. If

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{0}\|D_{0}(t)u_{0}-\lambda u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

for all
u_{0}\in L_{1}^{2}([-1,1];\mathcal{H}_{A_{s,t}};\mathcal{L}_{-1}(t),\mathcal{L}_{1}(t)),

then

\|u\|_{0,\epsilon}\leq C\|D_{\epsilon}(t)u-\lambda u\|_{0,\epsilon}

for
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
and
u\in L_{1}^{2}([-1,1]\times\Sigma;\Lambda_{-1}(t),\Lambda_{1}(t))
.

This is
[
14
, Lemma 7.4]
. Note that the proof is based on a direct computation and an application of equation (
5.13
) therefore it works in our situation without change.

Step 3-Refined convergence of operators:
Define

R_{0}=\{(t,\lambda)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{C}\big|\lambda\mbox{ is not an eigenvalue of }D_{0}(t)\}

to be the resolvent set of the family
\{D_{0}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
. Denote by
R_{\epsilon}
is resolvent set of the family
\{D_{\epsilon}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
. Then for every compact subset
K\subset R_{0}
, there exists a constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
K\subset R_{\epsilon}
for
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
and

\|\pi_{A_{s,t}}((\lambda\cdot\id-D_{\epsilon}(t))^{-1}v)-(\lambda\cdot\id-D_{0}(t))^{-1}\pi_{A_{s,t}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon\|v\|_{0,\epsilon}

(5.14)

for
(t,\lambda)\in K
and
v\in L^{2}([-1,1];\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\Omega^{0}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\Omega^{2}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g})
.

This is
[
14
, Lemma 7.5]
.

Step 4-Multiplicity estimate:
For all
C_{0}>0
there exists a constant
k_{0}>0
such that the following holds for all
t\in[0,1]
and
0<\epsilon^{2}+\delta^{2}<k_{0}
. If
\lambda_{0}
is an eigenvalue of
D_{0}(t)
of multiplicity
m_{0}
and

u_{0}\perp\ker(\lambda_{0}\cdot\id-D_{0}(t))\Longrightarrow\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{0}\|\lambda_{0}u_{0}-D_{0}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

for all
u\in L_{1}^{2}([-1,1];\mathcal{H}_{A_{s,t}};\mathcal{L}_{-1}(t),\mathcal{L}_{1}(t))
, then the multiplicity of all eigenvalues of
\lambda
of
D_{\epsilon}(t)
with
|\lambda-\lambda_{0}|\leq\delta
does not exceed
m_{0}
.

This follows from
[
14
, Lemma 7.6]
.

Step 5-Concluding the proof.
By Kato’s selection theorem, we can find
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
which is arbitrarily close to
0
such that the resonance set of the family of the operators
\{D_{0}(t)-\delta\cdot\id\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
, written as
Z(D_{0}(t)-\delta\cdot\id)
is finite and contained in
(0,1)
. Moreover, we can guarantee that the resonance matrices of this family is non-degenerate. Use
\sigma(D)
to represent the spectrum of
D
. Let
Z(D_{0}(t)-\delta\cdot\id)=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{N}\}
. Recall that
R(D_{0}(t_{i})-\delta\cdot\id)
for
i=1,\dots,N
is the resonance matrix. Define

m_{i}:=\mbox{sign}(R(D_{0}(t_{i})-\delta\cdot\id)),i=1,\dots,N

to be the signatures of the resonance matrices. Then

Sf(\{D_{0}(t)\}_{0\leq t\leq 1})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}

and
m_{i}
could be computed as follows. Choose
\kappa>0
such that
\lambda=\delta
is the only eigenvalue of
D_{0}(t_{i})
in the interval
[\delta-\kappa,\delta+\kappa]
. Now choose
\tau>0
such that
\delta\pm\kappa\notin\sigma(D_{0}(t))
for all
t\in[t_{i}-\tau,t_{i}+\tau]
. Then

\displaystyle m_{i}

\displaystyle=\#\{\lambda\in\sigma(D_{0}(t_{i}-\tau))\big|\delta-\kappa<\lambda<\delta\}

\displaystyle\quad-\#\{\lambda\in\sigma(D_{0}(t_{i}+\tau))\big|\delta-\kappa<\lambda<\delta\}.

By
Step 2
, there exists a constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
\delta\notin\sigma(D_{\epsilon}(t))
for
t=t_{i}-\tau\mbox{ or }t_{i}+\tau)
and
\delta\pm\kappa\notin\sigma(D_{\epsilon}(t))
for all
t\in(t_{i}-\tau,t_{i}+\tau)
as long as
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
. Using
Step 3
,
Step 4
and the spectral projection operators, one can show that

\displaystyle m_{i}

\displaystyle=\#\{\lambda\in\sigma(D_{\epsilon}(t_{i}-\tau))\big|\delta-\kappa<\lambda<\delta\}

\displaystyle\quad-\#\{\lambda\in\sigma(D_{\epsilon}(t_{i}+\tau))\big|\delta-\kappa<\lambda<\delta\}.

Therefore we conclude that

Sf(D_{\epsilon}(t))=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}=Sf(D_{0}(t))

for
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small. By homotopy invariance,
Sf(D_{\epsilon}(t))
is independent of
\epsilon>0
. This concludes the proof. For the full details of the proof, we refer the readers to
[
14
, Section 7]
.
∎

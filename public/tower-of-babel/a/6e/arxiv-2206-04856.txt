Abstract
. This paper purposes to characterize Noetherian local rings ( R , 𝔪 ) (R,\mathfrak{m}) such that the Chern numbers of certain 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} -primary ideals in R R bounded above or range among only finitely many values. Consequently, we characterize the Gorensteinness, Cohen-Macaulayness, generalized Cohen-Macaulayness of local rings in terms of the behavior of its Chern numbers.

2.1.
Hilbert coefficients

It is well-known that there are integers
\{\mathrm{e}_{i}(I,M)\}_{i=0}^{s}
, called the
Hilbert coefficients
of
M
with respect to
I
such that

\displaystyle\ell_{R}(M/{I^{n+1}}M)={\mathrm{e}}_{0}(I,M)\binom{n+s}{s}-{\mathrm{e}}_{1}(I,M)\binom{n+s-1}{s-1}+\ldots+(-1)^{s}{\mathrm{e}}_{s}(I,M).

for large integer
n
. In particular, the leading coefficient
\mathrm{e}_{0}(I)
is said to be
the multiplicity
of
M
with respect to
I
and
\mathrm{e}_{1}(I)
, which Vasconselos (
[
28
]
) refers to as the
Chern number
of
M
with respect to
I
. For inductive arguments that used in this paper we need the following results.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
20
, 22.6]
)
.

For every superficial element
x\in I
, we have

e_{j}(I,M/xM)=\begin{cases}e_{j}(I,M)&\text{if $0\leq j\leq s-2$,}\\
e_{s-1}(I,M)+(-1)^{s}\ell_{R}(0:_{M}x)&\text{if $0\leq j=s-1$}.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.2

(
[
1
, Lemma 3.4]
)
.

Let
N
be a submodule of
M
with
\dim N=t<s
. Then

e_{j}(I,M)=\begin{cases}e_{j}(I,M/N)&\text{if $0\leq j\leq s-t-1$,}\\
e_{s-t}(I,M/N)+(-1)^{s-t}e_{0}(I,N)&\text{if }j=s-t.\end{cases}

2.2.
Index of reducibility

Recall that a proper submodule
N
of
M
is called

can not be written as an intersection of two strict larger submodules of

of

of

[

For an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal

\mathrm{ir}_{M}(I):=\mathrm{ir}_{M}(IM)=\ell_{R}([IM:_{M}\mathfrak{m}]/IM).

Moreover,
there exists the integers

such that

\mathrm{ir}_{M}(I^{n+1})=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}f_{i}(I,M)\binom{n+d-1-i}{d-1-i}.

for all large
n
(
[
2
, Lemma 4.2]
).
The leading coefficient

is called

of

with respect to

.

A system
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}
of parameters of
M
is called a
C
-system of parameters
of
M
if
x_{s}\in\mathfrak{b}(M)^{3}
and
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{b}(M/(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{s})M)^{3}
for all
i=s-1,\ldots,1
. A parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of

is called

-parameter ideal
if it is generated by a

-system of parameters of

(
[

, Definition 2.15]
). Notice that,

-systems of parameters of

always exist, provided

is a homomorphic image of a Cohen-Macaulay local ring (see
[

]
) and
a
C
-system of parameters forms a

-sequence. Moreover, the index of reducibility of

-parameter ideals

of

Furthermore, we have the following results which are useful in this paper.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

Proof.

i

[

.

iii

After applying the functor

Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{R}(M)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell((0):_{M/\mathfrak{q}M}\mathfrak{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell((0):_{M/W+\mathfrak{q}M}\mathfrak{m})+\ell((0):_{W}\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{N}_{R}(M/W)+r_{0}(M),

Let

3.
Characterizations of Cohen-Macaulay rings

The purpose of this section is to present characterizations of Cohen-Macaulay rings in terms of
its Chern numbers, irreducible multiplicities, stable value and the Cohen-Macaulay type. As corollaries, we obtained the
characterizations of a Gorenstein ring in terms of its Chern numbers, irreducible multiplicity and stable value. We begin with the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
M
is unmixed of dimension
d\geq 2
. Suppose that

\mathcal{N}(M)\leq f_{0}(\mathfrak{q},M),

for some
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
M
. Then
M
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

We use induction on the dimension
s
of
M
. In the case
\dim M=2
, then
M
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay because of Lemma
2.4
. We get by the Lemma
2.6
that

r_{1}(M)+r_{2}(M)=f_{0}(\mathfrak{q},M)\geq\mathcal{N}(M)=r_{2}(M)+2r_{1}(M).

Thus,
r_{1}(M)=0
and therefore
M
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Suppose that
\dim M\geq 3
and that our assertion holds true for
\dim M-1
. Since
M
is unmixed,
x_{1}
is
M
-regular whence
H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(M)=(0):_{M}x_{1}=(0)
. Let
A=M/x_{1}M
and
\overline{\mathfrak{q}}=(x_{2},\ldots,x_{s})
.
Then by Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.3
ii), we have
\mathrm{Ass}A\subseteq\mathrm{Assh}A\cup\{\mathfrak{m}\}
and
x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
is a
C
-system of parameters of
A
. Thus
B:=A/H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(A)
is unmixed and
x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
is also a
C
-system of parameters of
B
.
It follows from Lemma
2.3
that we have

\mathcal{N}(B)\leq\mathcal{N}(A)=\mathcal{N}(M).

On the other hand, we have

f_{0}(\mathfrak{q},M)\leq f_{0}(\overline{\mathfrak{q}},A)\leq f_{0}(\overline{\mathfrak{q}},B),

because of Lemma 2.1 and 2.2 in
[
24
]
. Consequently, we get that
\mathcal{N}(B)\leq f_{0}(\overline{\mathfrak{q}},B)
.
By the hypothesis of induction,
B
is Cohen-Macaulay. Thus
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A)=0
for all
1\leq i<d
.

Now it follows from the following sequence

0\to M\xrightarrow{x_{1}}M\to A\to 0

that we have the long exact sequence

\displaystyle\ldots\to H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\xrightarrow{x_{1}}

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)

\displaystyle\to H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A)\to 0\ldots

\displaystyle\ldots\to H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\xrightarrow{x_{1}}

\displaystyle H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)

\displaystyle\to H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A)\to 0\ldots

Then we have
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0
for all
2\leq i\leq d-1
and
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=x_{1}H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
. Thus
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0

because
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
is a finite generated
R
-module. Hence
M
is Cohen-Macaulay, as required.
∎

For each integer
n\geq 1
, we denote by
{\underline{x}}^{n}
the sequence

. Let

be the

For each

that
I_{S}^{[n]}=I_{R}^{[n]}

Moreover, it follows Lemma
2.6
that

e_{d-u}(I_{S}^{[n]},S)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }u\leq d-2,\\
r_{d}(S)&\text{ if }u=d-1.\end{cases}

which is independent of the choice of
n
.
On the other hand, we have

e_{0}(I_{R}^{[n]},\mathfrak{u})=e_{0}(\mathfrak{q}^{[n]},\mathfrak{u})=e_{0}((x_{1}^{n},\ldots,x_{u}^{n}),\mathfrak{u})=n^{u}e_{0}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{u})\geq n^{u}.

Thus, by Lemma
2.2
, we get that

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-u}e_{d-u}(I_{R}^{[n]},R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-u}e_{d-u}(I_{R}^{[n]},S)+e_{0}(I_{R}^{[n]},\mathfrak{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-u}e_{d-u}(I_{S}^{[n]},S)+n^{u}e_{0}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{u})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n^{u}+r_{d}(S),

which is in contradiction with the finiteness of
\Xi_{d-u}(R)
. Hence
u=0
, and this complete the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Assume that
\dim R\geq 2
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

R
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay.

(ii)

The sets
\Xi_{i}(R)
are finite for all
i=1,2,\ldots,d
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
follows from Lemma
2.6
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. We put
S=R/\mathfrak{u}
and
u=\dim\mathfrak{u}
. Assume that
u>0
. Since
\Xi_{1}(R)
is finite, it follows from Theorem
4.5
that
S
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay.
Then there exists a
C
-system
\underline{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
of parameters of
R
such that

i)

(\underline{x})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{t}
,

ii)

(x_{u+1},x_{u+2},\ldots,x_{d})\mathfrak{u}=0
,

iii)

\underline{x}
is a
C
-system of parameters of
S
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}^{[n]}=(x_{1}^{n},\ldots,x_{d}^{n})R
,
I_{R}^{[n]}=\mathfrak{q}^{[n]}:_{R}\mathfrak{m}
and
I_{S}^{[n]}=\mathfrak{q}^{[n]}S:_{S}\mathfrak{m}S
for all
n\geq 0
. Since
S
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay, we get by Lemma
4.3
that

I_{S}^{[n]}=I_{R}^{[n]}
.
Moreover, it follows Lemma
2.6
that

e_{d-u}(I_{S}^{[n]},S)=(-1)^{d-u}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{u}\binom{u-1}{j-1}\ell_{R}(H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(S))-\sum_{j=1}^{u+1}r_{j}(S)\right),

which is independent of the integer
n
.
On the other hand, we have

e_{0}(I_{R}^{[n]},\mathfrak{u})=e_{0}(\mathfrak{q}^{[n]},\mathfrak{u})=e_{0}((x_{1}^{n},\ldots,x_{u}^{n}),\mathfrak{u})=n^{u}e_{0}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{u})\geq n^{u}.

Thus, by Lemma
2.2
, we get that

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-u}e_{d-u}(I_{R}^{[n]},R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-u}e_{d-u}(I_{R}^{[n]},S)+e_{0}(I_{R}^{[n]},\mathfrak{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-u}e_{d-u}(I_{S}^{[n]},S)+n^{u}e_{0}(\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{u}),

which is in contradiction with the finiteness of
\Xi_{d-u}(R)
and
\Xi_{d-u}(S)
. Hence
u=0
, as required.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

In
[
17
]
, A. Koura and N. Taniguchi proved that the Chern numbers of
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals in
R
range among only finitely many values if and only if
\dim R=1
and
R/H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(R)
is analytically unramified.

Let us note the following example of non-generated Cohen-Macaulay local rings

with
\left|\Xi_{1}(R)\right|=1
. Moreover, this example also shows that the statements
(1),(4)
and
(5)
in Theorem
3.6
will not be equivalent, if the unmixed condition is removed from the hypothesis.

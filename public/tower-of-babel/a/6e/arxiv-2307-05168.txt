Total mutual-visibility in Hamming graphs
Csilla Bujtás, Sandi Klavžar, Jing Tian

Abstract
If G G is a graph and X ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) X\subseteq V(G) , then X X is a total mutual-visibility set if every pair of vertices x x and y y of G G admits a shortest x , y x,y -path P P with V ⁡ ( P ) ∩ X ⊆ { x , y } V(P)\cap X\subseteq\{x,y\} . The cardinality of a largest total mutual-visibility set of G G is the total mutual-visibility number μ t ​ ( G ) \mu_{\rm t}(G) of G G . In this paper the total mutual-visibility number is studied on Hamming graphs, that is, Cartesian products of complete graphs. Different equivalent formulations for the problem are derived. The values μ t ​ ( K n 1 ​ □ ​ K n 2 ​ □ ​ K n 3 ) \mu_{\rm t}(K_{n_{1}}\,\square\,K_{n_{2}}\,\square\,K_{n_{3}}) are determined. It is proved that μ t ( K n 1 □ ⋯ □ K n r ) = 𝒪 ( N r − 2 ) \mu_{\rm t}(K_{n_{1}}\,\square\,\cdots\,\square\,K_{n_{r}})={\cal O}(N^{r-2}) , where N = n 1 + ⋯ + n r N=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r} , and that μ t ​ ( K s □ , r ) = Θ ⁡ ( s r − 2 ) \mu_{\rm t}(K_{s}^{\,\square\,,r})=\Theta(s^{r-2}) for every r ≥ 3 r\geq 3 , where K s □ , r K_{s}^{\,\square\,,r} denotes the Cartesian product of r r copies of K s K_{s} . Introduction

The mutual-visibility sets were introduced by Di Stefano in
[
7

motivated by mutual visibility in distributed computing and social networks. Although the motivation came from theoretical computer science, it is a graph theory concept. It needs to be said that the term mutual-visibility is also used in other contexts, for instance in robotics, where the mutual visibility problem asks for a distributed algorithm that reposition robots to a configuration where they all can see each other, cf.
[
1
]
. Some related research can be found in
[
3
,
6
,
12

. The graph theoretic mutual-visibility was further investigated in
[

,

, where the latter paper naturally raised the need to introduce the total mutual-visibility which was in turn investigated in
[

,

]
.

A graph

is a

if

is the Cartesian product of complete graphs. In particular, complete graphs are Hamming graphs. In
[

, where

[

,

]
. On the other hand, it was proved in

that

. In
[

the authors proposed a challenging problem to determine the total mutual-visibility number of Hamming graphs with at least three factors. They provided a total mutual-visibility set of

of cardinality

In the light of what has just been said, in this paper we focus on the total mutual-visibility in Hamming graphs. In the next section we give equivalent formulations of the problem which later serve as a tool for proof. Our first main result which we prove in Section

Theorem 1

If

\mut(K_{n_{1}}\,\square\,K_{n_{2}}\,\square\,K_{n_{3}})=\left.\begin{cases}N-4;&n_{3}=2,\\
N-5;&n_{3}=3,\\
N-6;&n_{3}\geq 4\,.\end{cases}\right.

Note that if

In Section

Theorem 2

If

, and

(i)

\mut(K_{n_{1}}\,\square\,\cdots\,\square\,K_{n_{r}})\leq\frac{6}{r!}\,N^{r-2}

(ii)

In the subsequent section, we strengthen the result for balanced Hamming graphs by establishing the exact magnitude for their total mutual-visibility number. The result

Theorem 3

If

In the last section we reformulate our problem as a Turán-type question and accordingly restate Theorems

-

Equivalent formulations of the problem

In this section we prove two equivalent formulations of the total mutual-visibility problem in Hamming graphs. First we prove that total mutual-visibility sets in Hamming graphs are precisely the vertex sets such that no pair of vertices is at distance
2
. Then we reformulate this fact in terms of clique systems in complete multipartite graphs.

We say that a
4
-cycle of a Cartesian product graph
G
is a
Cartesian square
if it is not contained in a single layer of
G
. This definition also applies to Cartesian product of more than two factors. More precisely, let
G=G_{1}\,\square\,\cdots\,\square\,G_{r}
,
r\geq 2
. Then the vertices
u,u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime},u^{\prime\prime\prime}\in V(G)
form a Cartesian square if there exist
i,j\in[r]
,
i<j
, such that

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},u_{i},u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{j-1},u_{j},u_{j+1},\ldots,u_{r})\,,

\displaystyle u^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},u_{i},u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{j-1},u_{j}^{\prime},u_{j+1},\ldots,u_{r})\,,

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},u_{i}^{\prime},u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{j-1},u_{j}^{\prime},u_{j+1},\ldots,u_{r})\,,

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},u_{i}^{\prime},u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{j-1},u_{j},u_{j+1},\ldots,u_{r})\,.

The following results were stated in
[
11
, Lemma 5.8]
for two factors, the proof for more factors is analogous. That is, we just need to infer that a Cartesian square is a convex subgraph of a Cartesian product graph.

Lemma 4

Let
G=H_{1}\,\square\,\cdots\,\square\,H_{r}
,
r\geq 2
. If
X
is a total mutual-visibility set of
G
and

is a Cartesian square of
G
, then

contains no diametral pair of vertices of

.

If a Cartesian square of a Cartesian product graph

fulfils the condition of Lemma

for a set

, then we say that the cycle is
X
-suitable
. For the proof of our first characterization, we need the following result.

Proposition 5

If
G
is a Hamming graph,
u,v\in V(G)
, and
d_{G}(u,v)=t
, then the subgraph induced by

is isomorphic to the
t

.

Our first equivalent formulation of the total mutual-visibility problem in Hamming graphs now reads as follows.

Theorem 6

If
G
is a Hamming graph and

, then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

X
is a total mutual-visibility set of
G
.

(ii)

2\notin\{d_{G}(u,v):\ u,v\in X\}
.

(iii)

Each Cartesian square of
G
is
X
-suitable.

Proof.
Let
G=K_{n_{1}}\,\square\,\cdots\,\square\,K_{n_{r}}
, where
n_{i}\geq 2
for

, and
r\geq 1
. If

, then

is a complete graph which contains no Cartesian square, every subset of

forms a total mutual-visibility set, and
\diam(G)=1
. Hence the three statements are equivalent for

and we may assume in the rest that
r\geq 2
.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: Suppose on the contrary that there exist vertices
u,v\in X
with
d_{G}(u,v)=2
. Then
u
and
v
lie in a convex
C_{4}
, but then the other two vertices of this convex

are not

-visible, a contradiction.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
: If
2\notin\{d_{G}(u,v):\ u,v\in X\}
, then clearly each Cartesian square contains at most two vertices of
X
, and if so, these two vertices are adjacent, hence each Cartesian square is
X
-suitable.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
:
Let
X\subseteq V(G)
and assume that each Cartesian square of
G
is
X
-suitable. We need to show that each two vertices

are
X
-visible and proceed by induction on

. If

, the assertion is clear. Suppose now that

and that each pair of vertices at distance at most

-visible. By Proposition

induces a
t
-cube

. Let

be the neighbors of

and

lies in a Cartesian square of

. Since each Cartesian square of

is

-suitable, we infer that at most one of the vertices

and

lies in

. Assume without loss of generality that

. By the induction hypothesis,

and

are

-visible which in turn implies that

and

are also

.

\square

To get another reformulation of the total mutual-visibility problem on Hamming graphs, we consider the complete multipartite graph
K_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}}

, such that no two cliques from the family intersect in an

Set

Let

(1)

To each vertex

(2)

We claim that

-clique.

To prove the reverse assignment, we proceed in the reverse order as above. That is, we start with a family of

-cliques

such that no two cliques from the set intersect in an

-clique. Then we use their enumeration as in (

1
).

\square

Proof of Theorem

Theorem

asserts that for every integer

\mut(K_{s}^{\,\square\,,r})=\Theta(s^{r-2})
. For
r\geq 3
, Theorem
2
(ii)
directly implies
\mut(K_{s}^{\,\square\,,r})={\cal O}(s^{r-2}).
For the lower bound, we give a probabilistic proof based on a similar idea as the proof in
[
2
, Section 4]
for a famous hypergraph Turán-problem of Brown, Erdős, and Sós.

Let
r\geq 3
and
H=K_{s}^{\,\square\,,r}
. From
H
, we choose each vertex with probability
p=\frac{2}{r(r-1)s^{2}}
, independently of the decisions made for other vertices. This way, we obtain a set
S\subseteq V(H)
. The expected value of the size of
S
is

E(|S|)=s^{r}p=\frac{2}{r(r-1)}s^{r-2}\,.

We say that a set
\{u,v\}
of two vertices from
S
is a
bad pair
in
S
, if
d_{H}(u,v)=2
. Let
B
denote the set of all bad pairs that are present in
S
. To get the pairs of vertices at distance
2
apart in
H
, we may first choose the two entries where they differ, fix the coordinates for these two entries appropriately, and fix the remaining coordinates arbitrarily. As the vertices of
S
were selected randomly with probability
p
, we may estimate the size of
B
in the following way:

\displaystyle E(|B|)

\displaystyle={r\choose 2}\frac{s^{2}(s-1)^{2}}{2}s^{r-2}\,p^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r(r-1)s^{4}}{4}\,\frac{2}{r(r-1)s^{2}}\,s^{r-2}\,p

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,s^{r}\,p=\frac{1}{2}\,E(|S|).

For a set
S\subseteq V(H)
, we remove one vertex from each bad pair. The obtained set
S^{*}
contains no bad pairs, that is,
S^{*}
is a total mutual-visibility set in
H
. Moreover, we have

E(|S^{*}|)\geq E(|S|)-E(|B|)\geq\frac{1}{2}\,E(|S|)=\frac{s^{r-2}}{r(r-1)}.

There exists at least one set
S
that results in a total mutual visibility set
X
with
|X|\geq E(|S^{*}|)
after removing one vertex from each bad pair. We may therefore infer

\mut(K_{s}^{\,\square\,,r})\geq\frac{1}{r(r-1)}\,s^{r-2}.

Together with the upper bound, this implies
\mut(K_{s}^{\,\square\,,r})=\Theta(s^{r-2})
.

In this section, we show that our main results can be reformulated in the language of Turán-type problems on hypergraphs.

A
hypergraph

H=(V,E)
is a set system over the vertex set
V

e\in E
is a nonempty subset of
V
. A hypergraph
H^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is a
subhypergraph
of
H=(V,E)
if both
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
and
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
hold. A hypergraph
H
is
r
-uniform
if every
e\in E
contains exactly
r
vertices. Note that
r
-uniform hypergraphs are often called
r
-graphs
. Then,
2
-uniform hypergraphs correspond to simple graphs. The
complete
r
-partite
r
-graph
{\cal K}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{r}}^{(r)}
is the
r
-uniform hypergraph on the vertex set
V=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{r}
, where the partite classes
V_{1},\dots,V_{r}
are pairwise disjoint and
|V_{i}|=n_{i}
holds for every
i\in[r]
, and moreover, the edge set is defined as

E=\{e\subseteq V:|e\cap V_{i}|=1\mbox{ for every }i\in[r]\}
. Thus,
{\cal K}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{r}}^{(r)}
contains
\Pi_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}
edges.

The basic example for a hypergraph Turán problem takes an
n
-element vertex set
V
and asks for the maximum number of edges in a

-uniform hypergraph

that contains no subhypergraph isomorphic to a given (
r
-uniform) hypergraph

. This maximum number is denoted by
{\rm ex}_{r}(n,F)
. Remark that most of the Turán-type hypergraph problems considered are notoriously hard. Even the tight asymptotics or the exact order of magnitude for

may be hard to identify as

For more details on the subject we refer the reader to the book
[
8
]
and the survey
[
10
]
.

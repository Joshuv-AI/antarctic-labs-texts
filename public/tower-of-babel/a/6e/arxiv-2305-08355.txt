Asymptotic analysis of thin structures with point dependent energy growth
Michela Eleuteri

Abstract
3 ​ d − 2 ​ d 3d-2d dimensional reduction for hyperelastic thin films modeled through energies with point dependent growth, assuming that the sample is clamped on the lateral boundary, is performed in the framework of Γ \Gamma -convergence. Integral representation results, with a more regular lagrangian related to the original energy density, are provided for the lower dimensional limiting energy, in different contexts.

Introduction

In solid mechanics, the equilibrium state of a body can be described by an energy minimization problem, over a suitable class of fields, of an integral functional of the type

\int_{{\Omega}}f(x,\nabla v(x))dx,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded open set,
v
is the deformation or the displacement, defined in a suitable function space
X(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, e.g.
W^{1,1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the hyperelastic energy density, assumed as a Carathéodory function satisfying suitable growth conditions.

In many applications, for instance to deal with multiphase materials, finer and finer heterogeneities, shape optimization, thin structures, phase transitions, etc. other parameters may come into play and the equilibrium configurations arise as solutions of the following
\varepsilon
- parameterized problems

(1.1)

\displaystyle\min_{v\in X(\varepsilon)(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})}{\mathcal{F}}_{\varepsilon}(v):=\min_{v\in X(\varepsilon)(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f(\varepsilon)(x,\nabla v(x))dx

where
\{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\}
may represent a
\varepsilon
- dependent family of bounded open subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
X(\varepsilon)(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a suitable functional space, possible encoding the anisotropy of the model, expressed through a point-dependent integrability condition on the deformation gradients.
Hence one is interested in detecting, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, the asymptotic behaviour of such configurations.

Two main types of questions are addressed in this paper, one dealing with the detection of sufficient conditions on
X(\varepsilon)
, that, under special structure conditions on
f(\varepsilon),
ensure existence of solutions of the problem (
1.1
), when the domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is a thin domain of the form

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\omega\times(-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2),

with
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
a bounded, connected, open set with Lipschitz boundary,
the other one concerning the asymptotic behaviour of problems (
1.1
), under more general assumptions on
f(\varepsilon)
. In the latter case, by means of a suitable variational convergence, we derive a limiting problem defined on
\omega
with a structure analogous to the original one, which admits solutions.

This approach rigorously justifies the fact that, in the applications, when dealing with
very thin structures, it is convenient to work with a lower dimensional model. We refer to the pioneering papers
[
1
]
and
[
44
]
where this approach has been formally obtained, in the framework of
\Gamma
-convergence, in the hyperelastic setting for strings and membranes, in the classical functional setting of Sobolev spaces.

The main novelty of this manuscript is that the energy density
f(\varepsilon)
depends on the parameter
\varepsilon
also through its growth which involves a so called
variable growth exponent

p_{\varepsilon}=p_{\varepsilon}(x)
or better, a family of variable growth exponents converging to a fixed one in some suitable topology. This leads to non-trivial mathematical questions, because it appears to be challenging the idea to control the dependence of the parameter
\varepsilon
both in the set and in the energy density.

In this context it is convenient to rephrase the problem (
1.1
) on a fixed domain

(1.3)

\Omega:=\Omega_{1}=\omega\times(-1/2,1/2)

through a re-scaling in the transverse direction
x_{3}
, thus obtaining the equivalent minimization problem

(1.4)

\displaystyle\min_{u\in X_{\varepsilon}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\min_{u\in X_{\varepsilon}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})}\int_{\Omega}f_{\varepsilon}\left(x,\nabla u(x)\right)dx,

where
f_{\varepsilon},

X_{\varepsilon}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and
u
have been obtained by
f(\varepsilon)
,
X(\varepsilon)(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
d=3
and

, respectively, via the change of variables which maps
x_{3}
from
(-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2)
into
(-1/2,1/2)
.
In the first case, we consider
p_{\varepsilon}:\Omega\rightarrow(1,+\infty)
,
X_{\varepsilon}(\omega;{R}^{3})=W^{1,p_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
f_{\varepsilon}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of the following type

f_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi):=W^{p_{\varepsilon}(x)}(x,\xi),\quad\hbox{or }\quad f_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi):=a_{\varepsilon}(x)W^{p_{\varepsilon}(x)}(\xi),

with suitable functions

W
and
a_{\varepsilon}
(which are made precise in the sequel). In particular we provide lower semicontinuity
with respect to the (strong

and

1.1

In the second case, we deal with the very dimensional reduction problem where the function

in (
1.4

thin structure,
is of the form

(1.5)

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi):=f(x_{\alpha},\varepsilon x_{3},p_{\varepsilon}(x),\xi_{\alpha},\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\xi_{3}),

for every

Here

We aim now to present the main results of the paper. To this purpose, referring to
[
23
]
for a comprehensive treatment of the topic,
we recall that a (Lebesgue) measurable function
p:\Omega\rightarrow[1,+\infty]
, playing the role of an exponent, is called a

and we denote by
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
the class of variable exponents on
\Omega
. Moreover,
\mathcal{P}_{b}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{P}^{log}(\Omega)
are the subsets of
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
whose elements satisfy the
boundedness condition

(1.7)

\displaystyle 1<{p^{-}}:=\mathop{\rm ess\>inf}_{\Omega}p(x)\leq\mathop{\rm ess\>sup}_{\Omega}p(x)=:{p^{+}}<+\infty,

and the so called

log
-Hölder continuity property
:

(1.8)

\displaystyle\exists\gamma>0:|p(x)-p(y)|\leq\frac{\gamma}{|\log|x-y||}\hbox{ for every }x,y\in\Omega,0<|x-y|<\frac{1}{2},

respectively. Finally we set
\mathcal{P}^{log}_{b}(\Omega):=\mathcal{P}^{log}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{P}_{b}(\Omega)
(according to Definitions
2.1
and
2.5
).
Condition (
1.8
) has been first introduced by V.V. Zhikov in
[
50
]
; the failure of this condition is a possible cause of discontinuity of minimizers, while, by assuming it, it is possible to prove higher integrability of minimizers, which is the first step towards further regularity. From that moment onwards, a large number of papers devoted to the mathematical analysis of energy functionals involving variable exponents appeared in several and different directions, motivated by the fact that such types of energies describe models (also non variational) coming from Mathematical Physics that are built using a variable growth exponent. For instance, we refer to the recent contribution
[
3
]
, where this theory is employed in the study of elastic problems, to
[
21
]
and
[
47
]
, where the variable exponent growth condition is considered in the framework of free discontinuity problems, i.e. for materials allowing for fractures and damage,
to
[
36
]
, where a variable exponent model for image restoration has been studied, from the existence point of view and the
\Gamma-
convergence one, in the case that the exponent attains the critical value one, to
[
27
]
where power-law approximation of supremal functionals has been studied once more by means of the
\Gamma-
convergence tool.

Thus, we are in position to state our main results for the first class of problems. To this end we recall that
a Borel measurable function

or

[

In this section we collect some basic results concerning variable exponent Lebesgue spaces. For more details we refer to the monograph

Let

Definition 2.1
.

For any (Lebesgue) measurable function
p:\Omega\rightarrow[1,+\infty]

we define

p^{-}:=\displaystyle\mathop{\rm ess\>inf}_{x\in\Omega}p(x)\qquad\qquad p^{+}:=\displaystyle\mathop{\rm ess\>sup}_{x\in\Omega}p(x).

Such function

a bounded variable exponent.

We denote by

the class of variable exponents and with

the class of variable exponents satisfying (

).

In the sequel we consider the case
p^{+}<+\infty
. In this case, the variable exponent Lebesgue space
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
can be defined as

L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\Omega\rightarrow{R}\,\,\,\textnormal{measurable such that}\,\,\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx<+\infty\right\}.

Let us note that in the case
p^{+}=+\infty
the space above defined may even fail to be a vector space (see
[
17
]
Section 2)
and a different definition of the variable Lebesgue spaces has been given in order to preserve the vectorial structure of the space (we refer to
[
23
]
, Definition 3.2.1).
In addition, if

then

is a Banach space endowed with the

\|u\|_{p(\cdot)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\,\,\,\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{u(x)}{\lambda}\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\leq\,1\right\}

(see Theorem 3.2.7 in
[
23
]
). Moreover, if

, the space

is separable and
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\hbox{ is dense in }L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
, while, if

is reflexive and uniformly convex (see
Theorem 3.4.12, Theorem 3.4.7, Theorem 3.4.9 and Theorem 3.4.12 in
[
23
]
). For any variable exponent
p
, we define

by setting

\frac{1}{p(x)}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}(x)}=1,

with the convention that, if
p(x)=+\infty
then
p^{\prime}(x)=1
. The function
p^{\prime}
is called
the dual variable exponent of

.

The following result holds (for more details, see
[
23
, Lemma 3.2.20]
in the case

Theorem 2.2
.

(Hölder’s inequality)

Let

be measurable exponents such that

\frac{1}{s(x)}=\frac{1}{p(x)}+\frac{1}{q(x)}\qquad\hbox{for }\mathcal{L}^{N}\hbox{-a.e. }x\in\Omega.

In particular, in the case

\left|\int_{\Omega}f\,g\,dx\right|\leq 2\|f\|_{p(\cdot)}\,\|g\|_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}\ .

We introduce the
modular
of the space

which is the mapping

Thanks to Lemma 3.2.4 in

\displaystyle\|u\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq 1\Longleftrightarrow\rho_{p(\cdot)}(u)\leq 1

By Lemma 3.2.5 in

Moroever, from (

\displaystyle\|u\|_{p(\cdot)}>1\Longrightarrow\|u\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}\leq\,\rho_{p(\cdot)}(u)\leq\,\|u\|^{p^{+}}_{p(\cdot)}\hbox{ and }\|u\|_{p(\cdot)}<1\Longrightarrow\|u\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\leq\,\rho_{p(\cdot)}(u)\leq\,\|u\|^{p^{-}}_{p(\cdot)}.

Finally, by

In view of introducing some important results concerning variable exponents Sobolev spaces, in this section we recall some useful remarks concerning the trace operator.

\displaystyle=||D(\varphi_{j}u)||_{L^{p^{-}_{j}-\delta_{j}}(\Omega\cap\tilde{B}_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(||\varphi_{j}Du||_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega\cap\tilde{B}_{j})}+||uD\varphi_{j}||_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega\cap\tilde{B}_{j})})

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}||u||_{W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}

i.e.

(2.13)

W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)\ni u\longmapsto\varphi_{j}u\in W^{1,p^{-}_{j}(\cdot)-\delta_{j}}(\Omega)\hbox{ holds and is continuous }\forall j\in F.

By combining (
2.13
), (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), it easily follows that
the embedding
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is compact.

Finally, it is easy to show that
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega;{R}^{d})
is complete with respect to the norm
|||\cdot|||
. Since

|||\cdot|||\leq C||\cdot||_{W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, by a classical result of Functional Analysis, we get that the two norms are equivalent on
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega;{R}^{d})
.

We conclude this subsection by recalling the following theorem which is of crucial importance for the results contained in Section
4
. It is a consequence of
[
23
, Theorem 11.2.7]
and properties of sets with Lipschitz boundary in
[
23
, Section 9.5]
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary and let
p\in\mathcal{P}_{b}^{log}(\Omega)
. If

u\in W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
then

u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}_{0}(\Omega)
.

-convergence

For an introduction to
\Gamma
-convergence, we refer to

]
.
We recall the sequential characterization of the
\Gamma
-limit when

is a metric space.

Proposition 2.10

(
[

]
Proposition 8.1)
.

Let

be a metric space and let

for every

.
Then
\{\varphi_{k}\}

\Gamma
-converges to

(

converging

(ii)

We recall that the

.

We say that a family

3
Some lower semicontinuity results

In this section we provide some lower semicontinuity results for different classes of integral functionals of the type

(3.1)

F(u,p)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,p(x),\nabla u(x))\,dx

where the variable
p
may stand for a variable growth exponent and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a open set.
We discuss the two cases: convexity and quasiconvexity of
\varphi(x,p,\cdot),
in order to point out the different sets of assumptions.

Before proceeding, we recall the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

A function
\varphi:O\times{R}^{m}\times{R}^{s}\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
is said to be a normal integrand if

-

\varphi
is
{\cal L}^{N}(O)\otimes{\cal B}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{s})
-measurable;

-

\varphi(x,\cdot,\cdot)
is lower semicontinuous for
{\mathcal{L}}^{N}
- a.e.
x\in O
.

Given a closed set
M\subseteq{R}^{m}
, a function
\varphi:O\times M\times{R}^{s}\rightarrow{R}
is said to be a Carathéodory integrand if

-

\varphi(\cdot,z,\xi)
is
\mathcal{L}^{N}(O)
-measurable for every
(z,\xi)\in M\times{R}^{m}
;

-

\varphi(x,\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous for
{\cal L}^{N}
a.e.
x\in O
.

3.1
The convex case

If the function
\varphi
in (
3.1
) is convex in the last variable, then the functional
F
is sequentially lower semicontinuous along sequences
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,1}(\Omega,{R}^{d})
and
p_{k}\rightarrow p_{0}
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, in view of the following result, shown by De Giorgi and Ioffe (see
[
6
, Theorem 5.8]
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
O
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and let

\varphi:O\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times{R}^{s}\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
be a normal integrand such that the map
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{s}\rightarrow\varphi(x,z,\xi)
is convex for
{\cal L}^{N}
- a.e.
x\in O
and every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
\{z_{k}\}\subset L^{1}(O,{R}^{m})
and
\{U_{k}\}\subset L^{1}(O;\mathbb{R}^{s})
. If
z_{k}\rightarrow z_{0}
in
L^{1}(O;{R}^{m})
and
U_{k}\rightharpoonup U_{0}
in
L^{1}(O;\mathbb{R}^{s})
then it holds

\int_{O}\varphi(x,z_{0}(x),U_{0}(x))dx\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{O}\varphi(x,z_{k}(x),U_{k}(x))dx.

As an application of Theorem
3.2
with
m=1
and
s=d\times N
, we give the following result, that is crucial in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\{p_{k}\}\subset\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\cap L^{1}(\Omega)
and
\{v_{k}\}\subset W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If
p_{k}\rightarrow p_{0}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
and
v_{k}\rightharpoonup v
in
W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

(3.2)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v(x)|^{p_{0}(x)}\,dx\leq\,\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{k}(x)|^{p_{k}(x)}\,dx.

In particular, if
\{v_{k}\}
satisfies the equiboundedness assumption (
1.10
), then
\nabla v\in L^{p_{0}(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}).
Finally, if
\{v_{k}\}
satisfies (
1.10
),
\Omega
is a Lipschitz bounded open set,
p_{0}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and
v\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
v\in W^{1,p_{0}(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

In order to show (
3.2
), it is sufficient to apply Theorem
3.2
on a sequence of open sets
\{O_{n}\}
such that
O_{n}\Subset\Omega
and
\cup_{n}O_{n}=\Omega
, with
\varphi:\Omega\times{R}^{d\times N}\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
given by

\varphi(x,z,U)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|U|^{z}&\hbox{ if }z\in[1,+\infty)\\
+\infty&\hbox{ otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and

z_{k}=p_{k},\quad U_{k}=\nabla u_{k}.

For every
n\in{{N}}
, we obtain that

\int_{O_{n}}|\nabla v(x)|^{p_{0}(x)}\,dx\leq\,\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{O_{n}}|\nabla v_{k}(x)|^{p_{k}(x)}\,dx\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{k}(x)|^{p_{k}(x)}dx

and (
3.2
) follows by sending
n\rightarrow+\infty.

Finally, the additional assumption (
1.10
) combined with inequality (
3.2
) implies that
\nabla v\in L^{p_{0}(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}).
The last part of the theorem follows by Proposition
2.8
.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
\{p_{k}\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{b}(\Omega)\cap L^{1}(\Omega)
be such that, for some
k_{0}\in{{N}}
, it holds

(3.3)

1<\inf_{k\geq k_{0}}p_{k}^{-}.

(1)

If
\{v_{k}\}\subset W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is equibounded in the sense of (
1.10
) and
v_{k}\rightharpoonup v
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
then
v_{k}\rightharpoonup v
in
W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Indeed, exploiting (
3.3
), we can take
1<r<\inf_{k\geq k_{0}}p_{k}^{-}
. Hence, by (
2.4
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{k\geq k_{0}}\|\nabla v_{k}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(\mathcal{L}^{N}(\Omega)+1)\sup_{k\geq k_{0}}\|\nabla v_{k}\|_{L^{p_{k}(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(\mathcal{L}^{N}(\Omega)+1)\sup_{k\geq k_{0}}\left\{\big(\rho_{p_{k}(\cdot)}(\nabla v_{k})\big)^{\frac{1}{p_{k}^{-}}},\big(\rho_{p_{k}(\cdot)}(\nabla v_{k})\big)^{\frac{1}{p_{k}^{+}}}\right\}<+\infty.

Since
r>1
, we obtain that there exists a not relabeled subsequence
\{v_{k}\}
such that
\nabla v_{k}\rightharpoonup V
in
L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
.
This easily implies that
V=\nabla v
and that
v_{k}\rightharpoonup v
in
W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This fact is used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

(2)

Let
\varphi:\Omega\times{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}\rightarrow{R}
be a
\mathcal{L}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{B}({R}\times{R}^{d\times N})
-measurable function satisfying the growth condition

(3.4)

\varphi(x,p,\xi)\geq\,b(x)+C|\xi|^{p}\,\quad\hbox{ for }{\mathcal{L}}^{N}\hbox{-}\hbox{a.e. }x\in\Omega,\,\forall\xi\in{R}^{d\times N},\forall p\geq 1,

where
C>0
and
b\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.
Assume that
p_{k}\rightarrow p_{0}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
with
p_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\cap L^{1}(\Omega)
and let
\{v_{k}\}\subset W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that

(3.5)

\sup_{k}F(v_{k},p_{k})<+\infty,

where
F
is the functional defined by (
3.1
). If
v_{k}\rightharpoonup v
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})

then
v_{k}\rightharpoonup v
in
W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\nabla v\in L^{p_{0}{(\cdot)}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}).
Indeed, (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) imply (
1.10
) and then it is sufficient to use the Part
(1)
of this remark, combined with Corollary
3.3
.

In particular, the coercivity assumption (
3.4
) is satisfied by integrands of the form

\varphi(x,p,\xi)=W^{p}(x,\xi)
with
p\geq 1
and

W(x,\xi)\geq b(x)+C|\xi|>0\quad\hbox{ for }\mathcal{L}^{N}\hbox{-}\hbox{a.e. }x\in\Omega,\,\forall\xi\in{R}^{d\times N},

where
C>0
and
b(x)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

3.2
The quasiconvex case

The following special case of
[
45
, Theorem 4.12]
holds (see also
[
4
, Theorem 7.4]
).
It is important in order to show Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
For the reader’s convenience, after the statement, we provide an outline of the main steps necessary to deduce this application from the results contained in
[
45
]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
p_{0}\in C(\Omega)
be such that
p_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{b}^{log}(A)
for every
A\Subset\Omega
.
Let
\varphi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a function such that

-

\varphi(\cdot,\xi)
is
\mathcal{L}^{N}(\Omega)
-measurable for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
;

-

\varphi(x,\cdot)
is quasiconvex for
\mathcal{L}^{N}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
;

-

there exists
C>1
and
b(x)\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
with
b(x)\geq\,0
such that

0\leq\,\varphi(x,\xi)\leq\,b(x)+C|\xi|^{p_{0}(x)}.

Then, for every sequence
\{v_{k}\}\subset W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
v_{k}\rightarrow v
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and such that

(3.6)

\sup_{k}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{k}(x)|^{p_{0}(x)}\,dx<+\infty,

we have that
v\in W^{1,{p_{0}(\cdot)}}_{loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
|\nabla v|^{{p_{0}(\cdot)}}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and

(3.7)

\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,\nabla v(x))\,dx\leq\,\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))\,dx.

Sketch of the proof.

We observe that, by applying Remark
3.4
(1) with
p_{k}\equiv p_{0}
, we have that the sequence
\{v_{k}\}
weakly converges to
v
in
W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega;{R}^{d})
. Hence, by applying Corollary
3.3
, taking into account (
3.6
), we get that
v\in W^{1,p_{0}(\cdot)}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
|\nabla v|^{p_{0}(\cdot)}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Hence, the lower semicontinuity result given by (
3.7
) follows as an application of
[
45
, Theorem 4.12]

recalling that, for every
A\Subset\Omega
,

•

if
p_{0}\in C(A)
and
p_{0}>1
on
A
, then the function
\psi(x,\xi)=|\xi|^{p_{0}(x)}
enjoys the
Sobolev type property
and
the Rellich type property
on
A
required by
[
45
, Theorem 4.12]
(see
[

, and
[
17

);

•

if
p_{0}\in\mathcal{P}^{log}(A)
then
\psi
verifies
the maximal property
on

, see
[

. Hence
\psi
satisfies also the

on

, see
[

.

Hence,
[

, Theorem 4.12]
entails that
for every
A\Subset\Omega

\int_{A}\varphi(x,\nabla v(x))\,dx\leq\,\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{A}\varphi(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))\,dx\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))\,dx.

By passing to the sup on

, we get the desired conclusion (
3.7
).

By using the arguments in
[
24
, Lemma 4.1]
(see also
[
43
, Lemma 5.1]
), we show the following key result.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
an open set with finite Lebesgue measure. Let
W:\Omega\times{R}^{d\times N}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a Borel function. Let
a_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
a_{0}\geq 0

\mathcal{L}^{N}
-a.e. in
\Omega
.
Let
p_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{b}(\Omega)
and let
\{p_{k}\}\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
p_{k}\rightarrow p_{0}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Then, for every
0<\delta<\min\{1/2,p_{0}^{-}-1\}
, there exists
k_{0}\in{{N}}
such that,
for every
k\geq k_{0}
and for every
U\in L^{1}(\Omega,{R}^{d\times N})
, it holds

(3.8)

\int_{\Omega}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{0}(x)-{\delta}}(x,U(x))dx\leq 2{\delta}\mathcal{L}^{N}(\Omega)+(2{\delta})^{-2{\delta}}||a_{0}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,U(x))dx.

Proof.
Let
0<\delta<\min\{1/2,p_{0}^{-}-1\}
. Thanks to the uniform convergence of
\{p_{k}\}
to
p_{0}
, there exists
k_{0}\in{{N}}
and a negligible measurable set
N\subseteq\Omega
such that

1<p_{0}(x)-{\delta}<p_{k}(x)<p_{0}(x)+{\delta}\quad\hbox{ for every }x\in\ \Omega\setminus N,

for every
k\geq k_{0}
. Hence, for every
x\in\Omega\setminus N
and for every
U\in L^{1}(\Omega,{R}^{d\times N})
, if
2{\delta}<W(x,U(x))<1
, then

(2{\delta})^{2{\delta}}<(2{\delta})^{p_{k}(x)-p_{0}(x)+{\delta}}<W^{p_{k}(x)-p_{0}(x)+{\delta}}(x,U(x))<1

that implies

1<W^{p_{0}(x)-p_{k}(x)-{\delta}}(x,U(x))<(2{\delta})^{-2{\delta}},

while, if
W(x,U(x))\leq 2\delta
, then

W^{p_{0}(x)-\delta}(x,U(x))\leq(2\delta)^{p_{0}(x)-\delta}<2\delta,

being
2\delta<1
and
p_{0}(x)-\delta>1.

Hence, for every
k\geq k_{0}
and for every
U\in L^{1}(\Omega,{R}^{d\times N})
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{0}(x)-{\delta}}(x,U(x))dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{W(x,U(x))\leq 2{\delta}\}}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{0}(x)-{\delta}}(x,U(x))dx+\int_{\{2{\delta}<W(x,U(x))<1\}}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{0}(x)-{\delta}}(x,U(x))dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\{W(x,U(x))\geq 1\}}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{0}(x)-{\delta}}(x,U(x))dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\delta||a_{0}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\mathcal{L}^{N}(\Omega)+(2{\delta})^{-2{\delta}}\bigg(\int_{\{2{\delta}<W(x,U(x))<1\}}W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,U(x))dx+\int_{\{W(x,U(x))\geq 1\}}W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,U(x))dx\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\delta||a_{0}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\mathcal{L}^{N}(\Omega)+(2{\delta})^{-2{\delta}}\int_{\Omega}W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,U(x))dx.

which gives the desired conclusion.

Now, we are in position to show the following general result which allows us to deduce Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
as immediate corollaries.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\Omega
,
W
,
p_{0}
,
\{p_{k}\}
,
v
,
\{v_{k}\}
be as in Theorem
1.1
and let
a_{0}
,
\{a_{k}\}
be as in Theorem
1.2
. Then
v
satisfies (
1.11
) and

\int_{\Omega}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{0}(x)}(x,\nabla v(x))dx\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}a_{k}(x)W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx.

Proof.
First of all, we note that, for every
A\Subset\Omega
, since
\inf_{A}p_{0}>1
and
p_{k}
uniformly converges to
p_{0}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we have that there exists
k_{0}=k_{0}(A)\in{{N}}
such that

\inf_{k\geq k_{0}}\mathop{\rm ess\>inf}_{A}p_{k}(x)>1.

In view of Remark
3.4
(1), this implies that
v_{k}\rightharpoonup v
in
W^{1,1}(A;{R}^{d})
for every
A\Subset\Omega
. By applying Corollary
3.3
in view of (
1.10
), we get that
v\in W^{1,p_{0}(\cdot)}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\nabla v\in L^{p_{0}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
.
Now we claim that for every
A\Subset\Omega
it holds

(3.9)

\int_{A}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{0}(x)}(x,\nabla v(x))dx\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{A}a_{k}(x)W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx.

Hence, by passing to the sup in (
3.9
) with respect to
A\Subset\Omega
, we get the desired conclusion.

In order to show (
3.9
), for fixed
A\Subset\Omega
, we note that

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{A}a_{k}(x)W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\int_{A}(a_{k}(x)-a_{0}(x))W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx+\int_{A}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\left(-||a_{k}-a_{0}||_{L^{\infty}(A)}\int_{A}W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx+\int_{A}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx\right).

On the other hand, (
1.10
) and (
1.12
) ensure

\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{A}W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx<+\infty

and, since
a_{k}\rightarrow a_{0}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, this implies

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}||a_{k}-a_{0}||_{L^{\infty}(A)}\int_{A}W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx=0.

Hence

(3.10)

\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{A}a_{k}(x)W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx\geq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{A}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx.

Since
\{p_{k}\}
uniformly converges to
p_{0}
on
A
and
p_{0}>1
on
A
, by applying Lemma
3.6
, there exists
k_{0}=k_{0}(A)\in{{N}}
such that, for every
k\geq k_{0}
, it holds

(3.11)

\int_{A}W^{p_{0}(x)-{\delta}}(x,U(x))dx\leq 2{\delta}\mathcal{L}^{N}(A)+(2{\delta})^{-2{\delta}}\int_{A}W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,U(x))dx\qquad\forall\,U\in L^{1}(A,{R}^{d\times N}).

By applying (
3.11
) with
U=\nabla v_{k}
and then passing to the limit when
k\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain that

\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{A}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{0}(x)-\delta}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx\leq(2\delta)^{-2\delta}\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx+2\delta||a||_{L^{\infty}(A)}\mathcal{L}^{N}(A).

Taking into account the growth condition (
1.12
), we can apply Theorem
3.5
with the variable exponent
p_{0}(\cdot)-\delta\in P_{b}^{log}(A)
to obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{A}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{0}(x)-\delta}(x,\nabla v(x))dx\leq(2\delta)^{-2\delta}\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{A}a_{0}(x)W^{p_{k}(x)}(x,\nabla v_{k}(x))dx+2\delta||a_{0}||_{L^{\infty}(A)}\mathcal{L}^{N}(A).

By sending

F(u)\leq\int_{\omega}f(x_{\alpha},0,p_{0}(x_{\alpha}),\nabla_{\alpha}u(x_{\alpha}),w_{0}(x_{\alpha}))dx_{\alpha}=\,\int_{\omega}f_{0}(x_{\alpha},0,p_{0}(x_{\alpha}),\nabla u(x_{\alpha}))\,dx_{\alpha}=\mathcal{F}(u).

- a.e.

4.3
The nonconvex case

This subsection is devoted to the proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
.
With this aim, we recall a relaxation result which extends, in the case of variable exponents, to integral functionals with trace constraints on the boundary the more general results proven in
[
45
]
in the uncostrained case.
For the readers’ convenience we state it in our framework, which deals with functionals with growth of the type
|\xi|^{p(x)}
, referring to
[
48
, Theorem 5.1]
for the result stated in full generality.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
U
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with Lipschitz boundary, let
p\in\mathcal{P}_{b}(U)
and consider a Carathéodory integrand
\varphi:U\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying the growth condition

(4.14)

C_{1}+C_{2}|\xi|^{p(x)}\leq\varphi(x,\xi)\leq C_{3}+C_{4}|\xi|^{p(x)},

for a.e.
x\in U
, every
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
,
with
0<C_{2}<C_{4}
.
Let
J:L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
be the functional defined by

J(u):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,\nabla u(x))dx&\hbox{ if }\varphi(\cdot,\nabla u(\cdot))\in L^{1}(U),\\
\\
+\infty&\hbox{ otherwise in }L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d}).\end{array}\right.

Assume that
u_{0}\in W^{1,p^{-}}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is such that
J(u_{0})<\infty
. If there exists a sequence
u_{k}\in W^{1,p^{-}}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C^{1}(U_{k};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where

U_{k}:=\{x\in U:{\rm dist}(x,\partial U)>1/k\},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

such that
u_{k}\lfloor_{\partial U}=u_{0}\lfloor_{\partial U}
,

u_{k}\rightarrow u_{0}
in
W^{1,p^{-}}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
J(u_{k})\rightarrow J(u_{0})
, as
k\rightarrow+\infty
, then there exists a sequence
\tilde{u}_{k}\in W^{1,p^{-}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{1,\infty}(U_{k};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\tilde{u}_{k}\lfloor_{\partial U}=u_{0}\lfloor_{\partial U}
,
\tilde{u}_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
W^{1,p^{-}}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})

and

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}J(\tilde{u}_{k})=\int_{U}Q\varphi(x,\nabla u_{0}(x))dx,

where
Q\varphi
denotes the quasiconvex envelope of
\varphi(x,\cdot)
.

Now, exploiting the same techniques of Theorem
1.1
, we are in position to show the lower bound inequality.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\{\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\}
and
\mathcal{I}
be the functionals defined by (
1.19
) and (
1.22
), respectively.
Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.4
,

\mathcal{I}(u)\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{\varepsilon_{k}}),

for every
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\ni u_{\varepsilon_{k}}\rightarrow u
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
as
{k\rightarrow+\infty}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{\varepsilon_{k}})<+\infty
.
By exploiting the assumption
p_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{b}^{log}(\omega)
, Proposition
4.1
ensures that
u
can be identified with a function in
W^{1,p_{0}(\cdot)}_{0}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
u_{\varepsilon_{k}}\rightarrow u
in
L^{p_{0}(\cdot)}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
u\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,p^{-}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).
Taking into account that
p_{0}^{-}>1
, let
0<\delta<\min\{1/2,p_{0}^{-}-1\}
and let
k_{0}\in{{N}}
be such that

1<p_{0}(x)-\delta<p_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x)<p_{0}(x)+\delta\quad\hbox{ for $\mathcal{L}^{3}$ \hbox{-} a.e. }x\in\Omega

Hence, by passing to the liminf as
{k\rightarrow+\infty}
and by applying the very definition of
W_{0}
and
Q(W_{0}^{p_{0}(\cdot)})
, we get that

(4.15)

\displaystyle 2\delta|\Omega|+(2\delta)^{-2\delta}\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}W^{p_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x)}(x_{\alpha},\nabla_{\alpha}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x),\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\nabla_{3}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x))dx\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})-\delta}(x_{\alpha},\nabla_{\alpha}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x),\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\nabla_{3}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x))dx\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}W_{0}^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})-\delta}(x_{\alpha},\nabla_{\alpha}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x))dx\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}Q(W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})-\delta}_{0})(x_{\alpha},\nabla_{\alpha}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x))dx.

Then, by Theorem
3.5

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}Q(W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})-\delta}_{0})(x_{\alpha},\nabla_{\alpha}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x))dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}Q(W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})-\delta}_{0})(x_{\alpha},\nabla_{\alpha}u(x_{\alpha}))dx_{\alpha}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}Q(W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})-\delta}_{0})(x_{\alpha},\nabla_{\alpha}u(x_{\alpha}))dx_{\alpha}.

Hence, by combining (
4.15
) and the above inequality, as

, we obtain

\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}W^{p_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x)}(x_{\alpha},\nabla_{\alpha}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x),\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\nabla_{3}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x))dx\geq\liminf_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\omega}Q(W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})-\delta}_{0})(x_{\alpha},\nabla_{\alpha}u(x_{\alpha}))dx_{\alpha}.

Now we claim that,

(4.16)

|W_{0}(x_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha})-W_{0}(x_{\alpha},\eta_{\alpha})|\leq w(x_{\alpha},|\xi_{\alpha}-\eta_{\alpha}|)\hbox{ for every }\xi_{\alpha},\eta_{\alpha}\in{R}^{3\times 2},\hbox{ for }\mathcal{L}^{2}\hbox{-a.e. }x_{\alpha}\in\omega.

Indeed, let
N\subseteq\omega
be such that (
1.20
), (
1.9
) and (

) hold for every

and for every

Hence, thanks to the coercivity assumption (

), for a.e.

Therefore, for every

\displaystyle W_{0}(x_{\alpha},\eta_{\alpha})\leq W(x_{\alpha},\eta_{\alpha},\bar{\xi}_{3})\leq w(x_{\alpha},|(\xi_{\alpha},\bar{\xi}_{3})-(\eta_{\alpha},\bar{\xi}_{3}|)+W(x_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha},\bar{\xi}_{3})=

\displaystyle w(x_{\alpha},|\xi_{\alpha}-\eta_{\alpha},|)+W(x_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha},\bar{\xi}_{3})\leq w(x_{\alpha},|\xi_{\alpha}-\eta_{\alpha}|)+W_{0}(x_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha}).

By changing the role of

-a.e.

satisfies the assumptions of
[

and can be represented as

Q(W_{0}^{q})(x_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha})=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{2}(D)}\int_{D}W_{0}^{q}(x_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha}+\nabla_{\alpha}\psi(y_{\alpha}))dy_{\alpha}:\psi\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D;\mathbb{R}^{3})\right\}

Hence, by using

-a.e.
x_{\alpha}\in\omega
and for every

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}Q(W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})-\delta}_{0})(x_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha})=Q(W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})}_{0})(x_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha})

and, by using Fatou’s Lemma, we finally get

\liminf_{\varepsilon_{k}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}W^{p_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x)}(x_{\alpha},\nabla_{\alpha}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x),\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\nabla_{3}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x))dx\geq\int_{\omega}Q(W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})}_{0})(x_{\alpha},\nabla_{\alpha}u(x_{\alpha}))dx_{\alpha}.

Now we are in position to prove the upper bound inequality for Theorem

.

u\in W^{1,p_{0}(\cdot)}_{0}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
, which in view of the definition of
\mathcal{J}
in (
1.27
) concludes the proof.

Proposition 4.12
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.5
,
it results that

\Gamma(L^{1})\hbox{-}\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{J}_{{{\varepsilon_{k}}}}\leq\mathcal{J},

where
\{\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\}
and
\mathcal{J}
are the functionals defined by (
1.23
) and (

), respectively.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can restrict to consider the case when
\mathcal{J}(u)<+\infty
.

Taking into account (
1.26
), up to some minor modifications, we can reason as in the first part of Proposition
4.5
and obtain that

(4.24)

I(u)\leq\,\inf_{w\in L^{p_{0}(\cdot)}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})}\int_{\omega}a_{0}(x_{\alpha})W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})}(\nabla u(x_{\alpha}),w(x_{\alpha}))\,dx_{\alpha}\qquad\forall u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

where

I:=\Gamma(L^{p_{0}(\cdot)})\hbox{-}{\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}}\mathcal{J}_{{{\varepsilon_{k}}}}.

Following the same argument in
[
44
, page 558]
,
there exists a measurable function
w_{0}=w_{0}(x_{\alpha})
such that

W_{0}(\nabla u(x_{\alpha}))=\inf_{\xi_{3}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}[W(\nabla u(x_{\alpha}),\xi_{3})]=W(\nabla u(x_{\alpha}),w_{0}(x_{\alpha}))\qquad\mbox{ for }\mathcal{L}^{2}-\hbox{ a.e. }x_{\alpha}\in\omega.

In particular, by using (
4.18
), we deduce that

\displaystyle a_{0}(x_{\alpha})W_{0}^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})}(\nabla_{\alpha}u(x_{\alpha}))=a_{0}(x_{\alpha})W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})}(\nabla_{\alpha}u(x_{\alpha}),w_{0}(x_{\alpha}))=\inf_{\xi_{3}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}[a_{0}(x_{\alpha})W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})}(\nabla_{\alpha}u(x_{\alpha}),\xi_{3})].

Moreover, thanks to (
1.24
), such a function
w_{0}
belongs to
L^{p_{0}(\cdot)}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Hence, by using (
4.24
), we get that

I(u)\leq\mathcal{J}_{1}(u)\qquad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

where
\mathcal{J}_{1}:L^{1}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
is the functional defined by

{\mathcal{J}}_{1}(u):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\omega}a_{0}(x_{\alpha})W^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})}_{0}(\nabla_{\alpha}u(x_{\alpha}))\,dx_{\alpha}&\hbox{ if }u\in W^{1,p_{0}(\cdot)}_{0}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\\
\\
+\infty&\hbox{ otherwise in }L^{1}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).\end{array}\right.

Since
a_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\omega)
and thanks to the growth condition

W_{0}(\xi_{\alpha})\leq C_{2}(|\xi_{\alpha}|+1)\hbox{ for every }\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 2},

inherited by (
1.12
),
{\mathcal{J}}_{1}
results strongly continuous on
W^{1,p_{0}(\cdot)}_{0}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

By using the density of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
in
W^{1,p_{0}(\cdot)}_{0}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
(see Part (1) in Remark
2.7
) and the lower semicontinuity of
I
with respect to the strong convergence in
W^{1,p_{0}(\cdot)}_{0}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
we obtain that

(4.25)

I(u)\leq\,\mathcal{J}_{1}(u)\quad\forall u\in L^{1}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Now, by reasoning as in the proof of Proposition (
4.10
), we can show that that there exists a sequence
\tilde{u}_{{\varepsilon_{k}}}\in W^{1,p_{0}(\cdot)}_{0}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
\tilde{u}_{{\varepsilon_{k}}}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{p_{0}(\cdot)}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

and

(4.26)

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{J}_{1}(\tilde{u}_{{\varepsilon_{k}}})=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\omega}a_{0}(x_{\alpha})W_{0}^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})}(x_{\alpha},\nabla\tilde{u}_{{\varepsilon_{k}}}(x_{\alpha}))dx_{\alpha}=\int_{\omega}Q(a_{0}(x_{\alpha})W_{0}^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})})(x_{\alpha},\nabla u(x_{\alpha}))dx_{\alpha}.

Since
I
is lower semicontinuous with respect to the
L^{p_{0}(\cdot)}
-convergence and
Q\left(a_{0}(x_{\alpha})W_{0}^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})}(x_{\alpha},\cdot)\right)=a_{0}(x_{\alpha})Q\left(W_{0}^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})}(x_{\alpha},\cdot)\right),
for
\mathcal{L}^{2}
-a.e.
x_{\alpha}\in\omega
, (
4.26
) combined with (
4.25
) implies

I(u)\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}I(\tilde{u}_{{\varepsilon_{k}}})\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{J}_{1}(\tilde{u}_{{\varepsilon_{k}}})=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}J(\tilde{u}_{{\varepsilon_{k}}})=\int_{\omega}Q(W_{0}^{p_{0}(x_{\alpha})})(x_{\alpha},\nabla\tilde{u}(x_{\alpha}))dx_{\alpha}=\mathcal{I}(u).

and the proof is over.
